Modified scattering of small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with a trapping potential
Léo Bigorgne, Anibal Velozo Ruiz, Renato Velozo Ruiz

Abstract
. In this paper, we study small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the simplest external potential, for which unstable trapping holds for the associated Hamiltonian flow. First, we provide a new proof of global existence for small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the trapping potential − | x | 2 2 \frac{-|x|^{2}}{2} in dimension two. We exploit the uniform hyperbolicity of the Hamiltonian flow, by making use of the commuting vector fields contained in the stable and unstable invariant distributions of phase space for the linearized system. In contrast with the proof in [ VRVR23 ] , we do not use modified vector field techniques. Moreover, we obtain small data modified scattering for this non-linear system. We show that the distribution function converges to a new regular distribution function along modifications to the characteristics of the linearized problem. We define the linearly growing corrections to the characteristic system, in terms of a precise effective asymptotic force field. We make use of the scattering state to obtain the late-time asymptotic behavior of the spatial density. Finally, we prove that the distribution function (up to normalization) converges weakly to a Dirac mass on the unstable manifold of the origin.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of collisionless many-particle systems on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
. We consider many-particle systems described statistically by a distribution function satisfying a collisionless non-linear model arising in kinetic theory. More precisely, we investigate the asymptotic properties of small data solutions
f(t,x,v)
to the
Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
; given by

(1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+x\cdot\nabla_{v}f-\mu\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho(f),\\
\rho(f)(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v,\\
f(t=0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\end{cases}

where
t\in[0,\infty)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
,
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
, and
\mu\in\{1,-1\}
is a fixed constant. The interaction between the particles of the system is
attractive
when
\mu=1
, or
repulsive
when
\mu=-1
. The nonlinearity in this kinetic PDE system arises from the mean field generated by the many-particle system. The Vlasov–Poisson system with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, describes a collisionless many-particle system for which the trajectories described by its particles are set by the mean field generated by the system, and the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. We call
\nabla_{x}\phi
the
force field
, and
\rho(f)
the
spatial density
.

The Vlasov–Poisson system is a non-linear PDE system whose dynamics have been extensively studied in the scientific literature. The first well-posedness results for this PDE system (without external potential) were obtained by Okabe and Ukai
[
OU78
]
, who proved global well-posedness in dimension two and local well-posedness in dimension three. Seminal independent works by Pfaffelmoser
[
Pfa92
]
and Lions–Perthame
[
LP91
]
proved
global well-posedness
for the Vlasov–Poisson system (without external potential) in dimension three. See also Schaeffer’s proof
[
Sch91
]
. These global well-posedness results can be adapted to incorporate an external potential
\Phi(x)
, as long as
\nabla_{x}\Phi
has Lipschitz regularity (see the introduction of
[
GHK12
]
). Although the well-posedness properties of the Vlasov–Poisson system have been settled in
[
Pfa92
,
LP91
,
Sch91
]
, the description of the non-linear dynamics of the solutions to this PDE system for arbitrary finite energy data is not yet fully understood.

The class of small data solutions for the Vlasov–Poisson system has been studied in great detail in the literature. The first small data asymptotic stability result for the Vlasov–Poisson system was obtained by Bardos and Degond
[
BD85
]
, who studied the evolution in time of solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system for compactly supported initial data using the method of characteristics. Later in time, this small data global existence result was improved by Hwang, Rendall and Velásquez
[
HRV11
]
, who proved optimal decay in time for higher order derivatives of the spatial density for compactly supported data, using again the method of characteristics. Subsequently, the stability of the vacuum solution for the Vlasov–Poisson system in
[
BD85

was revisited by Smulevici
[
Smu16
]
, who proved small data global existence based upon energy estimates using the vector field method. As a result, Smulevici
[
Smu16
]
obtained boundedness in time of a suitable energy norm, and optimal space and time decay estimates for the spatial density. We emphasize the novel
modified vector field technique
introduced in
[
Smu16
]
, in order to address the small data global existence for the Vlasov–Poisson system in dimension three. Later Duan
[
Dua22

simplified the functional framework and the proof of the stability of vacuum for the Vlasov–Poisson system in
[
Smu16

[
Wan23

Moreover, there have been several works concerned on the
scattering properties of the distribution function
for small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{v}

Pan22

More recently, the first author has established small data modified scattering for the

The motivation behind considering small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential

In this paper, we prove small data modified scattering for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
in dimension two. Firstly, we provide a new proof of small data global existence for solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
in dimension two. In contrast with the proof of
[
VRVR23
, Theorem 1.2]
, we do not use modified vector field techniques to establish small data global existence. Later, we obtain small data modified scattering for this non-linear system. We show that the distribution function converges to a new highly regular distribution function along modifications to the characteristics of the linearized problem. We define the linearly growing corrections to the characteristic system in terms of a precise effective asymptotic force field. The regularity of the scattering state is proven by introducing novel asymptotic modified vector fields, whose components depend on the effective asymptotic force field for the characteristic system. Later, we make use of the scattering state to obtain the late-time asymptotic behavior of the spatial density. Finally, we prove that the distribution function (up to normalization) converges weakly to a Dirac mass on the unstable manifold of the origin. The mass of the corresponding Dirac mass is explicitly identified in terms of the scattering state.

We investigate this model with the hope to offer new insights to study asymptotic stability results for dispersive collisionless systems for which the associated characteristic system is hyperbolic. This dispersive behavior holds locally for 1D Hamiltonian flows arising from potentials with a global maximum in a neighborhood of the associated hyperbolic fixed point. An important example of dispersive collisionless systems for which the associated Hamiltonian flow is hyperbolic is given by collisionless systems in the exterior of black hole backgrounds which admit a
normally hyperbolic trapped set
. We have in mind massless collisionless systems on the exterior of black holes spacetimes, as for example, the subextremal family of Kerr black holes. See
[
WZ11
,
Dya15
]
for more details.

1.1.
A first glance to the main results

In this manuscript, we investigate the non-linear dynamics of small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
in dimension two, given by

(2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+x\cdot\nabla_{v}f-\mu\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho(f),\\
\rho(f)(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v,\\
f(t=0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\end{cases}

where
t\in[0,\infty)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
,
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
, and
\mu\in\{1,-1\}
is a fixed constant.

The local well-posedness theory for this PDE system is standard (see for instance
[
HK19
, Section 3]
). We study the evolution in time of small initial distribution functions
f_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to[0,\infty)
, in a space of functions defined by a higher order weighted
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{N,M}:=\sum_{|\beta|+|\kappa|\leq N}\sup_{(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\langle x-v\rangle^{M+|\beta|}\langle x+v\rangle^{M+|\kappa|}|(\partial_{x}-\partial_{v})^{\beta}(\partial_{x}+\partial_{v})^{\kappa}f_{0}|,

where
N,M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle
is the standard Japanese bracket. The differential operators considered in this norm are obtained as compositions of vector fields in a class
{\lambda}
of commuting vector fields for the linearized system, at time
t=0
. Similarly, the weights considered in the norm above are conserved quantities along the characteristic flow of the linearized system, at time
t=0
. See Section
2
for further details concerning the commuting vector fields and the weights for the linearized problem.

In the following, we denote by
(X_{{\mathscr{L}}},V_{{\mathscr{L}}})
to the components of the characteristics to the linearized problem, given by

(X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v)):=(x\cosh t+v\sinh t,x\sinh t+v\cosh t).

For a distribution function satisfying the linear Vlasov equation with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we have that
f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))=f_{0}(x,v)
, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. In contrast, the expression
f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))
does not converge as
t\to\infty
, for small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
in dimension two, unless the initial data is identically zero.

We introduce the coordinate system
(s,u)
in phase space, defined by

s^{i}:=\frac{x^{i}-v^{i}}{2},\qquad u^{i}:=\frac{x^{i}+v^{i}}{2},

which is more suitable to capture the hyperbolicity of the linearized system. We will frequently use this identification without explicit reference. In the following, we write a distribution function in the hyperbolic coordinate system
(s,u)
by

\bar{f}(t,s,u):=f(t,x,v).

Similarly, in the rest of the paper, the notation
A\lesssim B
is repetitively used to specify that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, where
C
depends only on the corresponding order of regularity, or other fixed constants.

Theorem 1.1

(Small data modified scattering for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|}{2}
)
.

Every solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from smooth and small initial data is global in time. Moreover, the following properties hold.

(a)

Let
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. The normalized stable average of
f
along
\{u=\bar{u}\}
converges to a regular function
Q_{\infty}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s-Q_{\infty}(\bar{u})\Big|\lesssim\dfrac{(1+t)^{4}}{e^{2t}}.

(b)

Let
\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the solution to
\Delta_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}=Q_{\infty}
. The force field has a regular self-similar asymptotic profile
u\mapsto\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
, in the sense that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}(u)|\lesssim\langle e^{-t}s\rangle\dfrac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{t}}.

(c)

We have modified scattering for the distribution function, in the sense that there exists a regular distribution
f_{\infty}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to[0,\infty)
, such that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\quad|f(t,X_{\mathscr{C}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,x,v))-f_{\infty}(x,v)|\lesssim\dfrac{(1+t)^{16}}{e^{t}},

where the components
(X_{{\mathscr{C}}},V_{{\mathscr{C}}})
of the modified characteristics are defined as

\displaystyle X_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,x,v):=x\cosh t+v\sinh t+\dfrac{\mu t}{2e^{t}}\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}(u),

\displaystyle V_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,x,v):=x\sinh t+v\cosh t-\dfrac{\mu t}{2e^{t}}\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}(u).

Remark 1.1.1
.

(a)

The proof of Theorem
1.1
fits into the general framework of vector field methods for dispersive collisionless kinetic equations. In order to show small data modified scattering for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we follow the strategy outlined by
[
Big22b
]
. We observe that the self-similar asymptotic profile
\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
of the force field allows us to write an explicit correction to the characteristic system. The self-similar asymptotic profile
\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
is defined as the field induced by the
asymptotic Poisson equation

\Delta_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}=Q_{\infty}
, where the normalized stable average
Q_{\infty}
plays the role of density on the unstable leaves of phase space. See Subsection
5.2
for further details.

(b)

We exploit the uniform hyperbolicity of the non-linear Hamiltonian flow by making use of the commuting vector fields contained in the stable and unstable invariant distributions of phase space for the linearized system. In contrast with the proof of
[
VRVR23
, Theorem 1.2]
, we do not use modified vector field techniques to show small data global existence. Nonetheles, the regularity of the scattering state is proven by introducing novel
asymptotic modified vector fields
, whose components depend on the effective asymptotic force field for the characteristic system. The modifications to the commuting vector fields for the linearized system
grow linearly in time
. This contrast with previous results
[
CK16
,
IPWW22
,
Pan22
,
Big22b
,
PBA23
]
concerning small data modified scattering for collisionless kinetic equations, where the modifications
grow logarithmically in time
.

The first part of Theorem
1.1
consists in proving small data global existence for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. For this purpose, we prove exponential decay in time of velocity averages. Previously in
[
VRVR23
]
, the second two authors proved that
|\rho(f)|\lesssim e^{-2t}
for small data solutions of this non-linear system. As part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we establish the late time asymptotic behavior of the spatial density.

Theorem 1.2

(Late-time asymptotic of the spatial density)
.

For every solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from smooth and small initial data. Then, the corresponding spatial density has a self-similar asymptotic profile, in the sense that

\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x},\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\rho(f)(t,x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}\Big(s,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\frac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{2t}}.

Moreover, the spatial density satisfies

\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x},\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\rho(f)(t,x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim(1+|x|)\frac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{t}}.

Remark 1.1.2
.

(a)

As we commented before, we use the hyperbolicity of the Hamiltonian flow, by making use of the vector fields contained in the stable and unstable invariant distributions of phase space for the linearized system. As a result, we obtain optimal exponential decay in time for the induced spatial density. The rate of exponential decay for the spatial density coincides with the
sum of all positive Lyapunov exponents
of the Hamiltonian flow. Moreover, we obtain a self-similar asymptotic profile
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}s
of the spatial density in terms of the scattering state. We observe that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}(t,s,0)\mathrm{d}s
is a conservation law along the stable manifold of the origin for the linearized system. As a result, the limit at infinity of the corresponding conserved quantities for the linearized system describes the late time asymptotic behavior of the spatial density.

(b)

We observe that the self-similar asymptotic profile
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}s
of the spatial density is defined by integrating the scattering state on the stable manifold of the origin. In terms of the initial data, this quantity corresponds to integrating the initial distribution function on the trapped set at time
\{t=0\}
. The second two authors have obtained an explicit teleological construction of the trapped set in terms of the evolution in time of the force field (see
[
VRVR23
, Theorem 1.3]
for further details).

The decay in time of the spatial density holds due to the concentration in time of the support of the distribution function on the unstable manifold of the origin. Motivated by this fact, we capture the concentration of the support of the distribution function in the unstable manifold with a suitable weak convergence statement.

Theorem 1.3

(Concentration of the distribution in the unstable manifold)
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{x,v}
be a compactly supported test function. Then, for every solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from smooth and small initial data, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\delta_{s=0}(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

In other words, the distribution function
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u)
converges weakly to
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
.

Remark 1.1.3
.

We observe that the mass of the corresponding Dirac measure is explicitly identified, as the mass of the stable manifold of the origin in terms of the scattering state. The mass of the Dirac measure is equal to the mass of the trapped set in terms of the initial distribution function. In the core of the paper, we prove a more general weak convergence statement for
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
, for a fixed
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. We show that
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
converges weakly to the Dirac mass
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
. We observe that the masses
\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s
of these Dirac measures are explicitly identified, as the masses along the leaves
\{u=\bar{u}\}
in terms of the scattering state. Note that the mass of the stable leaves
\{u=\bar{u}\}
defines the self-similar asymptotic profile of the spatial density.

For every sufficiently regular solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we consider the
Hamiltonian energy of the system
, given by

(3)

\mathcal{H}[f]:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v-\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

The Hamiltonian energy of a solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
is
conserved in time
. This quantity has a key role in the Hamiltonian structure of this PDE system. We conclude this subsection with an explicit characterization of the Hamiltonian energy of the system in terms of the scattering state, in the class of small data solutions studied in this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f
be a solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from small data. Then, the Hamiltonian energy of the system is equal to the asymptotic one induced by the scattering state. In other words, we have

\mathcal{H}[f(t)]=\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f_{\infty}(x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v-\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}|^{2}(u)\mathrm{d}u.

Remark 1.1.4
.

(a)

The asymptotic Hamiltonian energy
\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]
of the system in terms of the scattering state has a contribution coming from the self-similar asymptotic profile
\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]
. The contribution of this asymptotic profile does not appear in the corresponding formula for the asymptotic Hamiltonian energy
\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]
in terms of the scattering state for the standard Vlasov–Poisson system on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{v}
.

(b)

Later in the paper, we also obtain an explicit characterization of the total mass of the system
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
in terms of the scattering state, in the class of small data solutions studied in this paper. We show that
\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
is equal to the total mass
\|f_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
induced by the scattering state
f_{\infty}
.

We refine the decay estimates, by proving that the spatial density and the force field have self-similar asymptotic profiles. This profiles allow us to define the modified trajectories along which the distribution function converges. We prove small data modified scattering for the distribution function.

•

Section
6
.

We obtain the late-time asymptotic behavior of the spatial density. We prove that the distribution function (up to normalization) converges weakly to a Dirac mass on the unstable manifold of the origin. We also capture the hyperbolicity of the system with a more general weak convergence statement. Finally, we relate the Hamiltonian energy of the system to the corresponding asymptotic Hamiltonian energy of the scattering state.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the class of commuting vector fields used to study dispersion estimates for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. This is motivated by the dynamics defined by the characteristics to the linearized system. Furthermore, we prove some useful lemmata which are going to be applied in the following sections.

2.1.
The Vlasov equation with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}

In this subsection, we study the dynamics of the linearization of the non-linear Vlasov–Poisson system with the trapping potential (
2
) with respect to its vacuum solution, which is given by the
Vlasov equation with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
taking the form

(4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+x\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
f(t=0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\end{cases}

where
f_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to[0,\infty)
is a sufficiently regular initial data. We emphasize that this linear Vlasov equation is a transport equation along the Hamiltonian flow given by

(5)

\dfrac{dx^{i}}{dt}=v^{i},\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v^{i}}{dt}=x^{i},

defined by the Hamiltonian system
({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},H)
in terms of the Hamiltonian

H(x,v):=\dfrac{1}{2}(v^{1})^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}(v^{2})^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}(x^{1})^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}(x^{2})^{2}.

The Hamiltonian system
({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},H)
is
completely integrable in the sense of Liouville
due to the two independent conserved quantities in involution

H^{i}(x,v):=\dfrac{1}{2}(v^{i})^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}(x^{i})^{2},

where
i\in\{1,2\}
, whose sum yields the total Hamiltonian
H
. The flow map of the Vlasov system is given by the explicit formula

(X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))=(x\cosh t+v\sinh t,x\sinh t+v\cosh t).

In particular, we can write an explicit solution to the Vlasov equation (
4
) using the formula

f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t,x,v)).

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
f_{0}
be an initial data for the Vlasov equation (
4
). Then, the corresponding solution
f
to the Vlasov equation is given by

(6)

f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(x\cosh t-v\sinh t,v\cosh t-x\sinh t).

2.3.
Weights preserved along the linear flow

The set
\mathbf{k}
of weight functions composed by

z_{i}^{+}:=e^{t}\Big(\frac{x^{i}-v^{i}}{2}\Big),\qquad z_{i}^{-}:=e^{-t}\Big(\frac{x^{i}+v^{i}}{2}\Big),

where
i\in\{1,2\}
, are conserved along the characteristics
(t,x,v)\mapsto(X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))
of the linear Vlasov equation with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. As a result, the weight functions are solutions to the linear Vlasov equation, in other words,
\forall z\in\mathbf{k}
we have
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(z)=0
. If
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(g)=0,
then the same property is satisfied by
zg
, so weighted Sobolev norms of
g
are conserved for solutions to the linear Vlasov equation. In our nonlinear setting these norms will grow polynomially in time and will then provide useful decay properties for the Vlasov field. For convenience, we define

\mathbf{z}:=\Big(1+\sum_{z\in\mathbf{k}}z^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which by construction satisfies
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(\mathbf{z})=0
, and
\mathbf{z}\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
Z\in\lambda
,
z\in\mathbf{k}
, and
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, we have either
Z(z)\in\mathbf{k}\cup\{0,1\}
or
-Z(z)\in\mathbf{k}\cup\{0,1\}
. Moreover, we have

(7)

|Z(\mathbf{z}^{a})|\lesssim|a|\mathbf{z}^{a}.

Proof.

If
Z=U_{i}
, then, we have

U_{i}(z_{j}^{+})=0,\qquad U_{i}(z_{j}^{-})=\delta_{ij},

for every
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. If
Z=S_{i}
, then, we have

S_{i}(z_{j}^{+})=\delta_{ij},\qquad S_{i}(z_{j}^{-})=0,

for every
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. If
Z=R_{12}
, then, we have

R_{12}(z_{1}^{+})=-z_{2}^{+},\quad R_{12}(z_{2}^{+})=z_{1}^{+},\quad R_{12}(z_{1}^{-})=-z_{2}^{-},\quad R_{12}(z_{2}^{-})=z_{1}^{-}.

If
Z=L
, then, we have

L(z_{j}^{+})=z_{j}^{+},\qquad L(z_{j}^{-})=z_{j}^{-},

for every
j\in\{1,2\}
. The estimate (
7
) follows directly by using the previous identities.
∎

Motivated by the fact that any regular solution to the linear Vlasov equation
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(h)=0
is constant along the flow lines, that is
h(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))=h(0,x,v)
, it will sometimes be useful to work with
g(t,x,v):=f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))
, in particular when studying the asymptotic properties of
\rho(f)(t,x)
and its derivatives. The following result suggests that
g
enjoys strong decay and that none of its derivatives grow exponentially in time.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to[0,\infty)
be a sufficiently regular distribution function and
g(t,x,v):=f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))
. Then, we have

\langle x,v\rangle^{a}|g(t,x,v)|\lesssim|\mathbf{z}^{a}f|(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v)),

and,

|(\nabla_{x}\pm\nabla_{v})g|(t,x,v)\leq\sum_{Z\in\lambda}|Zf|(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v)).

Proof.

Since weights in
\mathbf{k}
are preserved by the linear Vlasov equation,

\mathbf{z}^{2}(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v))=\mathbf{z}^{2}(0,x,v)=1+\frac{1}{4}(|x-v|^{2}+|x+v|^{2})=1+\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}+|v|^{2}),

and thus the first inequality holds. The second inequality follows by noting that

(\partial_{x^{i}}\pm\partial_{v^{i}})g(t,x,v)=e^{\pm t}(\partial_{x^{i}}\pm\partial_{v^{i}})f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t,x,v)).

∎

Remark 2.3.1
.

There is an explicit correspondence between the set of stable and unstable vector fields, and the weights in
\mathbf{k}
. More precisely, we have
U_{i}=\{z_{i}^{+},\cdot\},
and
S_{i}=\{z_{i}^{-},\cdot\}
, where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is the Poisson bracket of the standard symplectic structure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{n}
.

2.4.
Macroscopic, microscopic, and unstable differential operators

Let
(Z^{i})_{i}
be an arbitrary ordering of the microscopic vector fields contained in
\lambda
. In the following, we use a multi-index notation for the microscopic differential operators of order
|\alpha|
given by the composition

Z^{\alpha}:=Z^{\alpha_{1}}Z^{\alpha_{2}}\dots Z^{\alpha_{n}},

for every multi-index
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. We denote by
\lambda^{|\alpha|}
the family of microscopic differential operators obtained as a composition of
|\alpha|
vector fields in
\lambda
.

Furthermore, we can uniquely associate a macroscopic differential operator to any microscopic differential operator
Z^{\alpha}\in\lambda^{|\alpha|}
, by replacing every microscopic vector field
Z
by the corresponding macroscopic vector field
Z_{x}
, so that

U_{i,x}=e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}},\qquad S_{i,x}=e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}},\qquad L_{x}=x^{1}\partial_{x^{1}}+x^{2}\partial_{x^{2}},\qquad R_{12,x}=x^{1}\partial_{x^{2}}-x^{2}\partial_{x^{1}}.

By a small abuse of notation, we denote also by
Z^{\alpha}
to the associated macroscopic differential operator to an arbitrary microscopic differential operator
Z^{\alpha}
. We denote by
\Lambda^{|\alpha|}
to the family of macroscopic differential operators of order
|\alpha|
obtained as a composition of
|\alpha|
vector fields in
\Lambda
.

Let us now consider a microscopic vector field
Z^{\alpha}

without
stable vector fields. In this case, we can uniquely associate an unstable differential operator to any microscopic differential operator
Z^{\alpha}\in\lambda^{|\alpha|}
by replacing every microscopic vector field
Z
by the corresponding unstable vector field
Z_{u}
. By a small abuse of notation, we denote by
Z_{u}^{\alpha}
the associated unstable differential operator to an arbitrary microscopic differential operator
Z^{\alpha}
. We denote by
\lambda_{u}^{|\alpha|}
to the family of unstable differential operators of order
|\alpha|
obtained as a composition of
|\alpha|
unstable vector fields in
\lambda_{u}
.

Finally, we denote by
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
a standard macroscopic differential operator

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}:=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x^{1}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x^{2}},

for every multi-index
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
.

The following two results can be found in
[
VRVR23
, Lemma 2.3.1, Lemma 2.3.3]
.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
\Omega\in\{\lambda,\Lambda\}
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two multi-indices. Then, the commutator between
Z^{\alpha}\in\Omega^{|\alpha|}
and
Z^{\beta}\in\Omega^{|\beta|}
is given by

[Z^{\alpha},Z^{\beta}]=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq|\alpha|+|\beta|-1}\sum_{Z^{\gamma}\in\Omega^{|\gamma|}}C^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}Z^{\gamma},

for some constant coefficients
C^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 2.4.2
.

For any multi-index
\alpha
, we have

(8)

(e^{t}+|x|)^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}\sum_{Z^{\beta}\in\Lambda^{|\beta|}}C_{\beta}Z^{\beta},

for some uniformly bounded functions
C_{\beta}
.

In the later result we have used the following key identity (see
[
Smu16
, Lemma 2.5]
),

|x|^{2}\partial_{x^{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{i}R_{ij,x}+x^{j}L_{x}.

We conclude this subsection by relating the macroscopic and microscopic differential operators
Z^{\alpha}
in terms of their action on
Z^{\alpha}\rho(f)
and
\rho(Z^{\alpha}f)
.

Lemma 2.4.3
.

Let
f
be a sufficiently regular distribution function and let
\alpha
be a multi-index. Then, there exist constant coefficients
C^{\alpha}_{\beta}
such that

(9)

Z^{\alpha}\rho(f)=\rho(Z^{\alpha}f)+\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1}C^{\alpha}_{\beta}\rho(Z^{\beta}f),

where the vector fields in the left hand side are macroscopic, whereas the ones in the right hand side are microscopic.

2.5.
The commuted equations

Let us denote the non-linear transport operator applied to the distribution function in the Vlasov–Poisson system with the external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
by

{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}:=\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+x\cdot\nabla_{v}-\mu\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},

where the force field
\nabla_{x}\phi
is defined through the Poisson equation
\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho(f)
. In order to identify hierarchies in the commuted equations, we introduce the following notation.

Definition 2.5.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a multi-index and
Z^{\alpha}\in\lambda^{|\alpha|}
or
Z^{\alpha}\in\Lambda^{|\alpha|}
. We denote by
\alpha_{u}
(respectively
\alpha_{s}
) the number of unstable (respectively stable) vector fields composing
Z^{\alpha}
. Then,
|\alpha|=\alpha_{u}+\alpha_{s}
and, for instance, if
Z^{\alpha}=U_{1}S_{2}LR_{12}
, we have
\alpha_{u}=3
and
\alpha_{s}=1
.

By straightforward computations, one obtains the first order commutation formula.

Lemma 2.5.1
.

Let
Z\in\lambda
and define
c_{Z}:=-2
if
Z=L
and
c_{Z}:=0
otherwise. Then,

[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z]=\mu\nabla_{x}\big(Z\phi+c_{Z}\phi\big)\cdot\nabla_{v}.

Iterating the above, we obtain the higher order case.

Lemma 2.5.2
.

There exist constant coefficients
C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(10)

[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\alpha}]=\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1}\,\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}Z^{\beta},

where the vector fields
Z^{\alpha}\in\lambda^{|\alpha|}
,
Z^{\gamma}\in\Lambda^{|\gamma|}
, and
Z^{\beta}\in\lambda^{|\beta|}
. Moreover, we have that either
\beta_{u}<\alpha_{u}
, or
\beta_{u}=\alpha_{u}
and
\gamma_{s}\geq 1
.

As obtained in
[
VRVR23
, Lemma 2.4.2]
, we have the next result for the Poisson equation.

Lemma 2.5.3
.

Let
f
be a sufficiently regular distribution function, and let
\phi
be the
solution to the Poisson equation
\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho(f)
. Then, for any multi-index
\alpha
the function
Z^{\alpha}\phi
satisfies the equation

(11)

\Delta_{x}Z^{\alpha}\phi=\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}C^{\alpha}_{\beta}\rho(Z^{\beta}f),

for some constant coefficients

.

Remark 2.5.1
.

If

does not contain the scaling vector field, then

2.6.
Conservation laws

For every sufficiently regular solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we define
the total mass of the system
, given by

(12)

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v,

and
the Hamiltonian energy of the system
, given by

(13)

\mathcal{H}[f]:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v-\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

By standard arguments in collisionless systems, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 2.6.1
.

Let
f
be a regular classical solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. Then, the total mass and the Hamiltonian energy of the system, are both conserved in time. In other words, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
we have

\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}},\qquad\mathcal{H}[f(t)]=\mathcal{H}[f_{0}].

The Hamiltonian energy (
13
) is a central quantity in the Hamiltonian structure of the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
.

3.
The main results

We recall the hyperbolic coordinate system
(s,u)
in phase space, defined by

s^{i}:=\frac{x^{i}-v^{i}}{2},\qquad u^{i}:=\frac{x^{i}+v^{i}}{2},

which is more suitable to capture the hyperbolicity of the linearized system. We observe that
x^{i}=s^{i}+u^{i},
and
v^{i}=u^{i}-s^{i}.
The coordinate system
(s,u)
induces the following vector fields in phase space

\partial_{s^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}}-\partial_{v^{i}},\qquad\partial_{u^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}}+\partial_{v^{i}}.

We observe that
\partial_{x^{i}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{s^{i}}+\partial_{u^{i}})
, and
\partial_{v^{i}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{u^{i}}-\partial_{s^{i}}).
We write the distribution function in
(s,u)
coordinates by

\bar{f}(t,s,u):=f(t,x,v).

Furthermore, we write the derivatives of the distribution function by
Z^{\alpha}\bar{f}=\overline{Z^{\alpha}f}
, where we abuse of notation by writing
Z^{\alpha}
in terms of the hyperbolic vector fields
\{\partial_{u^{i}},\partial_{s^{i}}\}
. We will frequently use these identifications without further references.

3.1.
Precise statements of the main results

We are now ready to provide a full and detailed version of the main results of the article. First, we prove the global existence of small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with external potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. We further provide pointwise estimates for derivatives of the distribution function, and exponential decay in time of the force field. These properties will be needed later to investigate the scattering properties of these small data solutions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
, and
f_{0}
be an initial data of class
C^{N}
for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. Consider further
\epsilon>0
, a constants
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and assume that

(14)

\sum_{|\beta|+|\kappa|\leq N}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{M+|\beta|}\langle u\rangle^{M+|\kappa|}|\partial_{s}^{\beta}\partial_{u}^{\kappa}\bar{f}_{0}|(s,u)\leq\epsilon.

If
M\geq 6
, there exists
0<\sigma\leq 1/4
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then the unique solution
f
arising from this initial data is global in time. Moreover, the following properties hold.

(a)

The force field and its derivatives
\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi
decay exponentially in time. For every
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\forall|\gamma|\leq N-1,\qquad

\displaystyle|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon,

\displaystyle\forall|\gamma|=N,\qquad

\displaystyle|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{\sigma t}.

(b)

The following
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
estimates hold for the derivatives of the distribution function. For every
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|\leq N-1,\qquad

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{z}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon(1+t)^{M+N-1},

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|=N,\qquad

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{z}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon(1+t)^{M}e^{\sigma t},

\displaystyle\forall|\kappa|\leq N-1,\qquad

\displaystyle|e^{-t|\kappa|}(\partial_{x}-\partial_{v})^{\kappa}f|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon.

(c)

The spatial density and its derivatives
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
decay exponentially in time. For every
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|<N,\qquad

\displaystyle e^{2t}|\rho(Z^{\beta}f)|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon,

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|=N,\qquad

\displaystyle e^{2t}|\rho(Z^{\beta}f)|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{\sigma t}.

We now proceed to state the main scattering result of the article. Given a function
Q:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. In the following, we denote the unique solution
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
to the Poisson equation
\Delta_{u}\phi=Q
by
\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f
be a smooth solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from initial data satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. Then, the following properties are satisfied.

(a)

The normalized stable averages of
Z^{\beta}f
along
\{u=u_{0}\}
converge to a function
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u})
of class
C^{N-1-|\beta|}
. For every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, we have

\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad\Big|\langle u\rangle^{M-3}\Big(e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s-Q^{\beta}_{\infty}(u)\Big)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\dfrac{(1+t)^{N+2}}{e^{2t}},

where

can be computed explicitly in terms of
\partial^{\kappa}_{u}Q_{\infty}
for

.

(b)

The spatial density

\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v-Q^{\beta}_{\infty}\Big(\dfrac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\dfrac{(1+t)^{N+5}}{e^{2t}}.

(c)

The force field and its derivatives

have a self-similar asymptotic profile. For every

\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad\Big|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}](u)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\,\langle e^{-t}s\rangle\,\dfrac{(1+t)^{N+5}}{e^{t}}.

(d)

If
N\geq 3
, the distribution function
\bar{f}
has modified scattering to a distribution
\bar{f}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}_{s,u}
of class
C^{N-3}
. For any
|\kappa|+|\beta|\leq N-3
, we have

\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\square\quad\qquad\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad\Big|\langle s\rangle^{M-1}\langle u\rangle^{M}\big(\partial_{u}^{\kappa}\partial_{s}^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}},u)-\partial_{u}^{\kappa}\partial_{s}^{\beta}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\big)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\dfrac{(1+t)^{3N+M+1}}{e^{t}},

where the component
S_{{\mathscr{C}}}
of the modified stable characteristics is defined as

\displaystyle S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u):=e^{-t}\Big(s+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}](u)\Big).

(e)

The asymptotic modified vector fields of the unstable vector fields
U_{i}
, the rotation
R_{ij}
, and the scaling
L
, given respectively by

\displaystyle U_{i}^{\mathrm{mod}}

\displaystyle:=U_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[\partial_{u^{i}}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k},

\displaystyle R_{ij}^{\mathrm{mod}}

\displaystyle:=R_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[R_{ij,u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k},

\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{mod}}

\displaystyle:=L+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[L_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k}-2\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k},

verify the improved estimates

\|U_{i}^{\mathrm{mod}}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\|R_{ij}^{\mathrm{mod}}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\|L^{\mathrm{mod}}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\epsilon.

Remark 3.1.1
.

The statements
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
hold true as well for any
|\beta|=N-1
, but with the weaker rate of convergence
\langle t\rangle^{N+6}e^{-(2-\sigma)t}
. Similarly,
\bar{f}_{\infty}
is in fact of class
C^{N-2}
.

Remark 3.1.2
.

We consider asymptotic modified vector fields to prove regularity of the scattering state with respect to the unstable variable
u
. The modification to the commuting vector fields for the linearized system grows linearly in time. Compare the asymptotic modified vector fields
U_{i}^{\mathrm{mod}}
,
R_{ij}^{\mathrm{mod}}
, and
L^{\mathrm{mod}}
, with the modified vector fields previously considered in
[
VRVR23
, Subsection 5.1]
to show small data global existence. The corrections in the asymptotic modified vector fields
U_{i}^{\mathrm{mod}}
,
R_{ij}^{\mathrm{mod}}
, and
L^{\mathrm{mod}}
, are identified dynamically in terms of the asymptotic behavior of the force field and its derivatives.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
M\geq 6
. Let
f\in C^{N}
be a solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from small data. Then, the corresponding spatial density has a self-similar asymptotic profile, in the sense that

\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x},\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\rho(f)(t,x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}\Big(s,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{2t}}.

Moreover, the spatial density satisfies

\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x},\qquad\Big|e^{2t}\rho(f)(t,x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon(1+|x|)\frac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{t}}.

Next, we capture the concentration of the support of the distribution function in the unstable manifold with a suitable weak convergence statement. We also capture the hyperbolicity of the non-linear system with a more general weak convergence statement for
\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
for a fixed
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{s,u}
be a compactly supported test function. Let
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. Let
f
be a small data solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
. Then, the distribution
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u)
converges weakly to
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
. In other words, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\delta_{s=0}(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

Moreover, the distribution
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
converges weakly to
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
. In other words, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\delta_{s=0}(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

Finally, we show an explicit characterization of the total mass and the Hamiltonian energy of the system in terms of the scattering state, in the class of small data solutions studied in this paper.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f
be a solution to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from small data. Then, the total mass and the Hamiltonian energy of the system are equal to the asymptotic ones induced by the scattering state. In other words, we have

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}=\|f_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}},

and

\mathcal{H}[f(t)]=\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f_{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v-\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]|^{2}\mathrm{d}u.

4.
Global existence of small data solutions

In this section we prove Theorem
3.1
, which states the global existence of small data solutions with respect to the weighted
L_{x,v}^{\infty}
norm in (
14
). For this purpose, we will prove decay in time of the spatial density and its derivatives via a bootstrap argument. Parts
(b)
and
(a)
in Theorem
3.1
are proved along with the proof of the bootstrap argument. The estimates obtained in this section are crucial inputs in the proof of the modified scattering theorem obtained in Section
5
.

4.1.
The bootstrap argument

Let
N\geq 2
and
M\geq 6
. Let us consider an initial data
f_{0}
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
. By a standard local well-posedness argument, there exists a unique maximal solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
arising from this data. Let
T_{\mathrm{max}}\in(0,\infty]
be the maximal time such that the solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system is defined on
[0,T_{\mathrm{max}})
. By continuity, there exists a largest time
T\in[0,T_{\mathrm{max}}]
, and a constant
C_{\mathrm{boot}}>0
such that the following bootstrap assumption holds:

BA1

For every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
and every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, we have

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\leq\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{boot}}\epsilon}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}.

BA2

For every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
and every
|\beta|=N
, we have

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{boot}}\epsilon\,e^{\sigma t}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}},

where
0<\sigma\leq 1/4
is a fixed constant.

We will improve these estimates when
\epsilon>0
is small enough, for a constant
C_{\mathrm{boot}}>0
chosen sufficiently large.

Structure of the proof of small data global existence

(a)

First, we prove decay estimates in time for the force field and its derivatives
\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi
. We consider non-linear modifications of the weights
z_{i}^{+}
, which are defined to be preserved by the non-linear Vlasov equation. We prove that these
modified weights
grow at most linearly in time.

(b)

We prove that for every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, a weighted
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
norm of
Z^{\beta}f
grows at most polynomially in time. At the top order
|\beta|=N
, we will merely be able to close the estimates with an
e^{\sigma t}
growth. Next, we obtain uniform boundedness in time of normalized weighted stable averages of
Z^{\beta}f
for every
|\beta|\leq N-1
. These estimates allow us to prove exponential decay in time for velocity averages
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
for
|\beta|\leq N-1
and improve the bootstrap assumptions (
BA1
)-(
BA2
).

4.2.
Pointwise decay estimates for the force field

We start slowly with an elementary calculus lemma.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

There exists a uniform constant
C>0
, such that for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|(1+|x+y|)^{2}}\leq C.

Proof.

First, we observe that in the region
\{|y|\leq 1\}
, we have

\int_{|y|\leq 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|(1+|x+y|)^{2}}\leq\int_{r=0}^{1}\int_{\theta=0}^{2\pi}\frac{r\,\mathrm{d}r}{r}\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 2\pi.

We deal with the remaining region
\{|y|>1\}
, by applying Hölder inequality from where we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|(1+|x+y|)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{|y|\geq 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{3}}\bigg|^{\frac{1}{3}}\,\bigg|\int_{|y|\geq 1}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{(1+|x+y|)^{3}}\bigg|^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{z}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{(1+|z|)^{3}}<+\infty.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.2.1
, we obtain decay in time for the integral term

(15)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{|y|(e^{t}+|x-y|)^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=\dfrac{1}{e^{t}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y^{\prime}}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{|y^{\prime}|(1+|y^{\prime}-\frac{x}{e^{t}}|)^{2}}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{t}},

by using the change of variables
y=e^{t}y^{\prime}
. We use the estimate (
15
) to prove decay for the gradient
\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi
.

Proposition 4.2.2
.

For every
|\gamma|\leq N-1
and every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
, we have

|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\dfrac{\epsilon}{e^{(1+2\gamma_{s})t}}.

For the top order derivatives
|\gamma|=N
, there holds

|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\dfrac{\epsilon}{e^{(1-\sigma+2\gamma_{s})t}}.

Proof.

Combining the commuted Poisson equation in Lemma
2.5.3
with the relation between the macroscopic and microscopic vector fields established in Lemma
2.4.3
, we obtain

\Delta_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi=\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq|\gamma|}C^{\gamma}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho(Z^{\gamma^{\prime}}f),

for some constants
C^{\gamma}_{\gamma^{\prime}}>0
. We use the Green function for the Poisson equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
to write the solution of the commuted Poisson equation as

Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)=\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq|\gamma|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}CC_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\log|y|\rho(Z^{\gamma^{\prime}}f)(t,x-y)\mathrm{d}y,

whose gradient can be estimated directly by

(16)

|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq|\gamma|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{|y|}|\rho(Z^{\gamma^{\prime}}f)|(t,x-y)\mathrm{d}y.

Hence, the solution of the commuted Poisson equation satisfies that for every
|\gamma|\leq N-1
, we have

|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|(e^{t}+|x-y|)^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\epsilon}{e^{t}},

where we have used the bootstrap assumption (
BA1
) and the estimate (
15
). We get similarly from the bootstrap assumption (
BA2
) and (
15
) that

\forall\,|\gamma|=N,\qquad|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-(1-\sigma)t}.

The improved estimate in terms of
\beta_{s}
follows directly by rewritting the stable vector fields in
Z^{\gamma}
in terms of the corresponding unstable vector fields,
S_{k}=e^{-2t}U_{k}
.
∎

4.3.
Key lemma for the
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
bounds of the distribution

In this subsection, we prepare the ground to prove
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
bounds for the distribution function based on the method of characteristics. For this purpose, we prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
a\in[0,\infty)
and
g:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution function such that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(g)|(t,x,v)\leq C_{g}(1+t)^{a},

for some constant
C_{g}>0
. Then, there exists
C>0
depending only on
a
, such that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|g(t,x,v)|\leq\|g(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{CC_{g}}{a+1}(1+t)^{a+1}.

Moreover, if a distribution function
h:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
verifies, for some constants
C_{h}>0
and
\delta>0
, that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(h)|(t,x,v)\leq C_{h}e^{-t\delta},

then, there exists
C>0
such that

\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|h(t,x,v)|\leq C\|h(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+CC_{h}\delta^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}
and
h_{0}
be functions defined as the solutions to

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(g_{0})

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{a},\qquad

\displaystyle g_{0}(0,x,v)=0,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(h_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{-t\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle h_{0}(0,x,v)=0.

By Duhamel’s formula, for every
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
we have

(17)

|g|\leq\|g(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+C_{g}|g_{0}|,\qquad|h|\leq\|h(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+C_{h}|h_{0}|.

Fix a point
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
. Let us denote by
(X(s),V(s))
the characteristic flow associated to the transport operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}
such that

\dfrac{d}{\mathrm{d}s}X(s)=V(s),\qquad\dfrac{d}{\mathrm{d}s}V(s)=X(s)-\mu\nabla_{x}\phi(s,X(s)),

where
X(t)=x
and
V(t)=v
. Using the method of the characteristics, we have

\displaystyle g_{0}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{a}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{a+1}(1+t)^{a+1},

\displaystyle h_{0}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-s\delta}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{\delta}.

The lemma follows by using these estimates in (
17
).
∎

4.4.
The modified weights

Since we expect
e^{t}(x-v)
to grow as
t
along the nonlinear flow, we will rather work with the following modification of this weight.

Definition 4.4.1
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi^{1},\varphi^{2}):[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the unique solution to

\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(\varphi^{i})=-\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(e^{t}(x^{i}-v^{i})),\qquad\varphi(0,x,v)=0.

We define the
modified weight function

\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}
as

\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v):=\langle e^{t}(x-v)+\varphi(t,x,v)\rangle.

One important property of the weight
\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}
is that it is, by definition, constant along the nonlinear flow. In order to exploit this property, we need to prove that it does not deviate too much from the weight
e^{t}(x-v)
, which is preserved by the linear flow.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

We have
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}})=0
. Moreover, the correction
\varphi
satisfies the estimates

\forall\,(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad|\varphi|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon(1+t),\qquad|\nabla_{v}\varphi|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{t}.

Proof.

The first property is straightforward, since we have defined
\varphi
so that
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}})=0
. Next, we have

\left|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(e^{t}(x^{i}-v^{i}))\right|\leq e^{t}|\nabla_{x}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon,

for
i\in\{1,2\}
. We then get that
|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(\varphi)|\lesssim\epsilon
on
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
, which implies, according to Lemma
4.3.1
, the estimate for
\varphi
. In order to conclude the proof, it suffices to prove, as
2\partial_{v^{i}}=e^{-t}U_{i}-e^{t}S_{i}
, that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(18)

\displaystyle\forall\,(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle|S_{1}\varphi|(t,x,v)+|S_{2}\varphi|(t,x,v)\leq 2C\,\epsilon,

(19)

\displaystyle|U_{1}\varphi|(t,x,v)+|U_{2}\varphi|(t,x,v)\leq 2C\,\epsilon\,(1+t).

By continuity, there exists a maximal time
0<T_{\mathrm{boot}}\leq T
such that (
18
)-(
19
) holds on
[0,T_{\mathrm{boot}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
. Let us prove by a bootstrap argument that
T_{\mathrm{boot}}=T
. Consider
Z\in\{S_{1},\,S_{2},\,U_{1},\,U_{2}\}
and apply the commutation formula of Lemma
2.5.1
. We get

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(Z\varphi)

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z]+Z{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(\varphi)=\mu\nabla_{x}Z\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi-\mu e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z\phi

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 2}\mu\frac{e^{-t}}{2}\partial_{x^{i}}Z\phi\,U_{i}\varphi-\mu\frac{e^{t}}{2}\partial_{x^{i}}Z\phi\,S_{i}\varphi-\mu e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z\phi.

We then deduce from the pointwise decay estimates of Proposition
4.2.2
as well as from the bootstrap assumptions for the derivatives of
\varphi
that, for all
(t,x,v)\in[0,T_{\mathrm{boot}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
and any
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(S_{i}\varphi)\right|(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon e^{-2t}\left(C\epsilon(1+t)e^{-2t}+C\epsilon+1\right),

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(U_{i}\varphi)\right|(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\big(C\epsilon(1+t)e^{-2t}+C\epsilon+1\big).

Hence, if
C
is chosen large enough and if
\epsilon
is small enough, we have

\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(S_{i}\varphi)\right|(t,x,v)\leq C\epsilon\,e^{-2t},\qquad\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(U_{i}\varphi)\right|(t,x,v)\leq C\epsilon.

Using that
\varphi
initially vanishes and Lemma
4.3.1
, we improve (
18
)-(
19
) on
[0,T_{\mathrm{boot}})
, implying that
T_{\mathrm{boot}}=T
as well the stated estimate for
\nabla_{v}\varphi
.
∎

4.5.
Pointwise estimates for the distribution and its derivatives

We are now able to prove upper bounds for the weighted derivatives
\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f
. We recall that for a multi-index
\beta
, the number of stable and unstable vector fields composing
Z^{\beta}
are denoted by
\beta_{s}
and
\beta_{u}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.5.1
.

If
\epsilon
is small enough, then, for every
(t,x,v)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f\big|(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon(1+t)^{\beta_{u}},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if}\ |\beta|\leq N-1,

(21)

\displaystyle\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f\big|(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon e^{\sigma t},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if}\ |\beta|=N.

Proof.

There exists a maximal time
T_{0}\leq T
such that (
20
) holds on
[0,T_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
. By the initial data assumptions the estimate (
20
) holds when
t=0
, and then
T_{0}>0
. In the following, we prove that (
20
) holds with
\epsilon+C\epsilon^{2}
instead of
\epsilon
, where
C>0
is a large constant. We improve the bootstrap assumption by using the method of characteristics through Lemma
4.3.1
. For this purpose, we estimate, for every
|\beta|\leq N
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f\right)

\displaystyle=M{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle\right)\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M-1}{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f

(22)

\displaystyle\quad+\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(Z^{\beta}f),

where we used
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}})=0
. We start by dealing with the first term on the right hand side of (
22
). We recall that
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(e^{-t}(x+v))=0
, so that

\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle\right)\right|=|\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{v}\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle|\leq e^{-t}|\nabla_{x}\phi|(t,x).

In view of the decay estimate for the force field given by Proposition
4.2.2
and the bootstrap assumption (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle\right)\right|\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M-1}|{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f|(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-2t}\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M-1}|{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f|(t,x,v)

(23)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\epsilon^{2}(1+t)^{\beta_{u}}e^{-2t}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}e^{-t}.

Next, we estimate the second term on the right hand side of (
22
). By the commutation formula in Lemma
2.5.2
and
2\nabla_{v}=e^{-t}U+e^{t}S
, we have

\displaystyle\big|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\big(Z^{\beta}f\big)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq|\beta|-1}\,\sum_{|\gamma|+|\alpha|\leq|\beta|}\,\sum_{\alpha_{u}\leq\beta_{u}}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}Z^{\alpha}f|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq|\beta|-1}\,\sum_{|\gamma|+|\alpha|\leq|\beta|}\,\sum_{\alpha_{u}\leq\beta_{u}}e^{-t}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot UZ^{\alpha}f|+e^{t}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot SZ^{\alpha}f|,

where the extra conditition
\alpha_{u}=\beta_{u}
implies
\gamma_{s}\geq 1
holds. The first term on the right hand side of the previous inequality are the easiest to handle, since they carry the factor
e^{-t}
. According to Proposition
4.2.2
, we have
|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-t/2}
, so that

\big|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\big(Z^{\beta}f\big)\big|\lesssim\sum_{|\kappa|\leq N}\epsilon\,e^{-\frac{3t}{2}}|Z^{\kappa}f|+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq|\beta|-1}\,\sum_{|\gamma|+|\alpha|\leq|\beta|}\,\sum_{\alpha_{u}\leq\beta_{u}}e^{t}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot SZ^{\alpha}f|.

Fix multi-indices
\alpha
and
\gamma
satisfying
|\alpha|\leq|\beta|-1
,
|\alpha|+|\gamma|\leq|\beta|
,
\alpha_{u}\leq\beta_{u}
and
\gamma_{s}\geq 1
if
\alpha_{u}=\beta_{u}
. Note that
Z^{\xi}=S_{i}Z^{\alpha}
and
Z^{\alpha}
contain the same number of unstable vector fields, we have
|\xi|=|\alpha|+1
and
\xi_{u}=\alpha_{u}
.

•

If
|\gamma|\leq N-1
, then
|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-t-2\gamma_{s}t}
according to the pointwise decay estimates given by Proposition
4.2.2
. By exploiting the extra condition on
\alpha_{u}
,
\beta_{u}
and
\gamma_{s}
, we get

e^{t}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot SZ^{\alpha}f|\lesssim\epsilon\,\sup_{|\xi|=|\alpha|+1,\;\xi_{u}<\beta_{u}}|Z^{\xi}f|+\epsilon e^{-2t}\,\sup_{|\xi|=|\alpha|+1,\;\xi_{u}=\beta_{u}}|Z^{\xi}f|.

•

Otherwise
|\gamma|=N
, so
|\alpha|=0
and we merely have
|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-(1-\sigma)t}
. Hence,

e^{t}|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\cdot SZ^{\alpha}f|\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{\sigma t}|Sf|.

Let us focus now on the case
|\beta|\leq N-1
. Since
|\gamma|=N
cannot occur, we have

\big|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\big(Z^{\beta}f\big)\big|\lesssim\sum_{|\kappa|\leq N}\epsilon\,e^{-\frac{3}{2}t}|Z^{\kappa}f|+\sum_{|\xi|\leq N}\,\sum_{\xi_{u}<\beta_{u}}\epsilon|Z^{\xi}f|,

where, by convention, the second term vanish if
\beta_{u}=0
. Using now the bootstrap assumption (
20
), we get

\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}\big|{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(Z^{\beta}f)\big|(t,x,v)\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\epsilon^{2}e^{-t}&\mbox{if $\beta_{u}=0$, }\\
\epsilon^{2}(1+t)^{\beta_{u}-1}&\mbox{if $\beta_{u}\geq 1$}.\end{array}\right.

Combining (
22
)-(
23
) and the last estimate, it gives

\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f\right)\right|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\epsilon^{2}e^{-t}&\mbox{if $\beta_{u}=0$, }\\
\epsilon^{2}(1+t)^{\beta_{u}-1}&\mbox{if $\beta_{u}\geq 1$}.\end{array}\right.

Then, we deduce by Lemma
4.3.1
that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
such that, for any
|\beta|\leq N-1
,

\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f|(t,x,v)\lesssim\|\langle x+v\rangle^{M}\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+C\epsilon^{2}(1+t)^{\beta_{u}}

for every
(t,x,v)\in[0,T_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
. We improve the bootstrap assumption by setting
\epsilon
small enough so that
C\epsilon<1
. It remains us to deal with the case
|\beta|=N
. We have

\big|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\big(Z^{\beta}f\big)\big|\lesssim\epsilon e^{\sigma t}|Sf|+\sum_{|\kappa|\leq N}\epsilon\,e^{-\frac{3}{2}t}|Z^{\kappa}f|+\sum_{|\xi|\leq N}\,\sum_{\xi_{u}<\beta_{u}}\epsilon|Z^{\xi}f|.

We then get from (
22
), (
23
) and the bootstrap assumptions (
20
)–(
21
),

\left|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M}{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\beta}f\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}e^{\sigma t}+\epsilon^{2}e^{-\frac{3}{2}t+\sigma t}+\epsilon^{2}e^{\sigma t}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}e^{\sigma t}.

Applying once again Lemma
4.3.1
, one can improve (
21
) if
\epsilon
is small enough.
∎

4.6.
Uniform boundedness of normalized stable averages

Let
\beta
be a multi-index. We proceed to show uniform boundedness in time of the
normalized stable averages
, defined by

(24)

Q^{\beta}(t,u):=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s.

For this purpose, we begin studying the transport equation satisfied by the normalized stable average
Q^{\beta}(t,e^{-t}u)=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s
.

Proposition 4.6.1
.

Let
|\beta|\leq N-1
. Then, for every
(t,u)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\Big|(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{u})\Big[e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big]\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\dfrac{\,(1+t)^{3}}{e^{2t}}\sup_{|\kappa|\leq|\beta|+1}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\big|\mathbf{z}^{3}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\big|.

Proof.

Fix
(t,u)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
and
|\beta|\leq N-1
. Integrating the commutation formula of Proposition
2.5.2
for
Z^{\beta}f
and performing integration by parts in
s
, we have,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big[e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s\Big]

\displaystyle=2e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s+e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\partial_{t}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}u\cdot\nabla_{u}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s+\mu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}e^{2t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu\sum_{|\delta|+|\gamma|\leq|\beta|}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq|\beta|-1}C^{\beta}_{\gamma\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}e^{2t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\delta}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}Z^{\gamma}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s.

Decomposing the vector fields
2\partial_{v^{i}}=\partial_{u^{i}}-\partial_{s^{i}}
and using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{u})\Big[e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big]\Big|\lesssim\sum_{|\delta|+|\gamma|\leq|\beta|}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}e^{2t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\delta}\phi\cdot\big(\nabla_{u}Z^{\gamma}\bar{f}-\nabla_{s}Z^{\gamma}\bar{f}\big)\mathrm{d}s\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\delta|+|\gamma|\leq|\beta|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\big|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\delta}\phi\cdot e^{t}\nabla_{u}Z^{\gamma}\bar{f}|+|(e^{t}\nabla_{x})^{2}Z^{\delta}\phi\cdot Z^{\gamma}\bar{f}\big|\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle s.

Next, we use the time decay of the force field and the linear weight
\langle e^{t}s\rangle^{3}
to obtain

\Big|(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{u})\Big[e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big]\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\sup_{|\kappa|\leq|\beta|+1}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}|\langle e^{t}s\rangle^{3}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\langle e^{t}s\rangle^{3}}.

Finally, we obtain the result by making use of

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\langle e^{t}s\rangle^{3}}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{(1+|e^{t}s|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}=\frac{1}{e^{2t}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\frac{2}{e^{2t}}

as well as
\langle e^{t}s\rangle\leq 2(1+t)\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}
, which is implied by Lemma
4.4.1
and
2s=x-v
.
∎

In particular, we obtain uniform boundedness of the normalized stable averages (
24
) of the distribution function in a weighted
L^{\infty}_{u}
space. We recall that
e^{-t}(x+v)=2e^{-t}u
.

Corollary 4.6.2
.

For every
(t,u)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}
and every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, we have

\Big|\langle e^{-t}u\rangle^{M}\,e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon.

Proof.

Applying a variant of Lemma
4.3.1
for the transport operator
\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{u}
combined with Proposition
4.6.1
, we have that for every
|\beta|\leq N-1
and every
(t,u)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\langle e^{-t}u\rangle^{M}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle\tau\rangle^{3}}{e^{2\tau}}\sup_{|\kappa|\leq N}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\langle e^{-\tau}u\rangle^{M}\big|\mathbf{z}^{3}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}(\tau,s,u)\big|\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle\tau\rangle^{3}e^{\sigma\tau}}{e^{2\tau}}d\tau\lesssim\epsilon,

where we have used Proposition
4.5.1
to bound the integrand.
∎

Thus, we obtain control in time of the normalized stable average
Q^{\beta}(t,u)
, by integrating the transport equation satisfied by
Q^{\beta}(t,e^{-t}u)=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s
and applying Proposition
4.5.1
.

Proposition 4.6.3
.

Let
|\beta|\leq N-1
. Then, for every
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
and every
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle u\rangle^{M}\Big|e^{2t_{2}}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t_{2},s,e^{t_{2}}u)\mathrm{d}s-e^{2t_{1}}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t_{1},s,e^{t_{1}}u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\!\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\dfrac{\langle\tau\rangle^{4+|\beta|}}{e^{2\tau}}\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad

\displaystyle|\beta|\leq N-2,

\displaystyle\langle u\rangle^{M}\Big|e^{2t_{2}}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t_{2},s,e^{t_{2}}u)\mathrm{d}s-e^{2t_{1}}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t_{1},s,e^{t_{1}}u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\!\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{e^{(2-\sigma)\tau}},\quad

\displaystyle|\beta|=N-1.

As a result, or by a direct application of Corollary
4.6.2
, we obtain the desired uniform boundedness in time of the normalized stable averages
Q^{\beta}(t,u)
.

Corollary 4.6.4
.

Let
|\beta|\leq N-1
. Then, for every
(t,u)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon.

4.7.
Pointwise decay estimates for velocity averages

In this subsection, we prove that the decay rate of
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
for
|\beta|\leq N-1
coincides with the one of the linearized system. In particular, we improve the bootstrap assumption (
BA1
). The starting point consists in performing the change of variables
y=\frac{1}{2}e^{t}(x-v)
.

Lemma 4.7.1
.

Let
g:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution. Then, for every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
, we have

e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}g(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\mathrm{d}v=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big)\mathrm{d}y.

This change of variables is motivated by the linearized problem. Every solution to the Vlasov equation with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
is transported along the lines of corresponding characteristic flow, so
h(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))=h(0,x,v)
. The previous lemma, applied to
g(t,x,v)=h(0,x,v)
, shows that
|\rho(h)|(t,x)\lesssim e^{-2t}
. Next, we control
\rho(|Z^{\beta}f|)
for every
|\beta|\leq N
, which has a slower decay rate than in the linear case.

Lemma 4.7.2
.

Let
g:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular function. Then, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}|g|(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\lesssim\frac{1}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}\sup_{(y,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{5}\,g\big|(t,y,v)+\langle e^{-t}(y+v)\rangle^{5}|g|(t,y,v).

Proof.

We start by writing

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}|g(t,x,v)|\mathrm{d}v\leq\sup_{(y,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{3}\,g\big|(t,y,v)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\langle e^{t}(x-v)+\varphi(t,x,v)\rangle^{3}}.

The change of variables
y=e^{t}(x-v)
yields

e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}|g(t,x,v)|\mathrm{d}v\lesssim\sup_{(y,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{3}\,g\big|(t,y,v)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\langle y+\varphi(t,x,x-e^{-t}y)\rangle^{3}}.

Let
\phi:y\mapsto y+\varphi(t,x,x-e^{-t}y)
and
\psi=\mathrm{Id}-\phi
. In order to perform the change of variables
w=\phi(y)
, we will prove that
\frac{1}{2}\leq|\det(\mathrm{d}\phi(y))|\leq 2
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. This property is satisfied because

\forall\,(t,x,y)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y},\qquad|\mathrm{d}\psi(y)|\leq e^{-t}|\nabla_{v}\varphi|(t,x,x-e^{-t}y)\lesssim\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2},

which holds provided that
\epsilon
is small enough by Lemma
4.4.1
. We then deduce

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\langle y+\varphi(t,x,x-e^{-t}y)\rangle^{3}}\leq 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{w}}\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\langle w\rangle^{3}}\leq 4.

We have then proved the estimate

(25)

\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x},\qquad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}|g|(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\lesssim e^{-2t}\sup_{(y,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{3}\,g\big|(t,y,v).

In order to obtain the spatial decay and conclude the proof, remark that, in view of Lemma
4.4.1
,

\displaystyle|x|

\displaystyle=\left|e^{t}\big[e^{-t}(x+v)\big]+e^{-t}\big[e^{t}(x-v)\big]\right|\lesssim e^{t}\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle+e^{-t}\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v)+\epsilon\,(1+t)e^{-t}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{t}\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle+\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v)

and apply (
25
) to
\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{2}g
as well as
\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{2}g
.
∎

We are now able to improve the bootstrap assumption (
BA1
) if
C_{\mathrm{boot}}
is chosen large enough. Applying Lemma
4.7.2
to
g(t,x,v)=\langle e^{-t}(x-v)\rangle^{M-5}\mathbf{z}^{M-5}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)
and then Proposition
4.5.1
, we get the following estimates.

Corollary 4.7.3
.

For any
|\beta|\leq N
and for every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\langle e^{-t}(x-v)\rangle^{M-5}\big|\mathbf{z}^{M-5}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\beta}f\big|(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\lesssim\dfrac{\epsilon\,e^{\sigma t}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}.

Our next goal will be to remove the
e^{\sigma t}
loss in the estimate for
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
in Corollary
4.7.3
. We will not be able to improve the estimate of
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
for top order derivatives since our analysis relies on the following lemma, which requires a loss of one derivative. We remark that spatial decay will only be exploited in the next Section
5
.

Lemma 4.7.4
.

Let
g:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution. Then, for every
t\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}g(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\mathrm{d}v~-

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\big(\langle s\rangle+\langle u\rangle\big)^{6}|\nabla_{u}\bar{g}(t,s,u)|.

Proof.

By the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big)~-~

\displaystyle\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\frac{|y|}{e^{2t}}\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}|\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\tau\frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big).

Consider now
u_{\tau}:=e^{-t}(x-\tau e^{-t}y)
and remark that

(26)

\forall\,\tau\in[0,1],\qquad 2\langle u_{\tau}\rangle+2\langle y\rangle\geq 2\langle e^{-t}x\rangle.

Indeed, if
\tau e^{-t}|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
we have
2\langle u_{\tau}\rangle\geq\langle e^{-t}x\rangle
. Otherwise, there holds
2\langle y\rangle\geq\langle e^{t}x\rangle
. We then deduce that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\frac{y}{e^{t}}

\displaystyle\Big)\Big)\mathrm{d}y-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{2t}\langle e^{-t}x\rangle^{2}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\big(\langle s\rangle+\langle u\rangle\big)^{2}\langle s\rangle^{4}|\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|(t,s,u)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\langle y\rangle^{3}}.

It remains to apply Lemma
4.7.1
and to use
e^{t}\langle e^{-t}x\rangle\gtrsim e^{t}+|x|
.
∎

Finally, we are able to prove optimal time decay of the velocity averages
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
for every
|\beta|\leq N-1
. The following proposition improves the bootstrap assumption (
BA1
) if the constant
C_{\mathrm{boot}}
is chosen large enough.

Proposition 4.7.5
.

For every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, the decay of the spatial density
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
is optimal. There exists
C>0
such that for every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}
, we have

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\leq\frac{C\epsilon}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}.

Proof.

Consider
g(t,x,v)=Z^{\beta}f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))
. Applying Lemma
2.3.2
and Proposition
4.5.1
to
g
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\big(\langle s\rangle+\langle u\rangle)^{6}|\partial_{u}\bar{g}|(t,s,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\sum_{|\kappa|\leq 1}\big(\langle e^{t}s\rangle+\langle e^{-t}u\rangle)^{6}\big|Z^{\kappa}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\big|(t,s,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\epsilon\,\langle t\rangle^{N+5}&\mbox{if $|\beta|\leq N-2$,}\\
\epsilon(1+t)^{6}e^{\sigma t}&\mbox{if $|\beta|=N-1$},\end{array}\right.

since we have
M\geq 6
and
\langle e^{t}s\rangle\lesssim(1+t)\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,s,u)
. Finally, applying Lemma
4.7.4
to the distribution
g
, we have

e^{2t}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\lesssim\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|+\epsilon\dfrac{(1+t)^{6}e^{\sigma t}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}},

for every
t\geq 0
. It remains to bound by
\epsilon\langle e^{-t}x\rangle^{-2}
the first term on the right hand side. For this purpose, we apply Corollary
4.6.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\Big(t,\frac{y}{e^{t}},x\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|=\Big|\langle e^{-t}x\rangle^{-2}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{w}^{2}}\langle e^{-t}x\rangle^{2}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,w,x)\mathrm{d}w\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\langle e^{-t}x\rangle^{-2}.

∎

4.8.
Improved estimates for derivatives of velocity averages

As in the linear case, spatial derivatives of velocity averages
\partial_{x}\rho(f)
enjoy stronger decay properties.

Proposition 4.8.1
.

For every
|\beta|\leq N-1
, every
i\in\{1,2\}
, and every
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}
, we have

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{e^{\sigma t}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{3}}.

Proof.

Writing the velocity averages in terms of the commuting vector fields, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{t}+|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\beta}f+|x|\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\beta}f\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{t}+|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}U_{i}Z^{\beta}f\mathrm{d}v+\dfrac{1}{e^{t}+|x|}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\frac{x^{j}}{|x|}R_{ij}Z^{\beta}f+\frac{x^{i}}{|x|}LZ^{\beta}f\mathrm{d}v.

Finally, we use Corollary
4.7.3
to obtain

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{t}+|x|}\sum_{|\kappa|\leq 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}|Z^{\kappa}Z^{\beta}f|\mathrm{d}v\lesssim\epsilon\frac{e^{\sigma t}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{3}}.

∎

5.
Modified scattering for the distribution function

In this section, we obtain the modified scattering properties of the distribution function and its derivatives. For this purpose, we determine the self-similar profile of the spatial density
\rho(f)
, the self-similar profile of the force field
\nabla_{x}\phi
, and we define the modified trajectories along which
f
converges to a new distribution function. In Section
4
, we obtained that the distribution function
f
is global in time. As a result, all the statements proved in the previous sections hold true for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. During this section, we use several times the estimates obtained in Section
4
.

5.1.
Convergence of normalized stable averages

In this subsection, we study the convergence in time of the
normalized stable averages
, given by

Q^{\beta}(t,u)=e^{2t}\int Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s.

We show that the normalized stable averages
Q^{\beta}(t,u)
converge to regular real-valued functions
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}(u)
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. Moreover, we will prove that the profile
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}(u)
dictates the late-time asymptotic behavior of the normalized spatial density
e^{2t}\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
(see Proposition
5.1.2
below for more details).

The analysis performed in the previous section shows that the distribution function
f
is global in time. As a result, the control in time of the normalized stable averages in Proposition
4.6.3
holds for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. Thus, we obtain the existence of the profiles
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}(u)
.

Proposition 5.1.1
.

Let
|\beta|\leq N-2
. There exists a continuous function
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}\in L^{\infty}_{u}\cap L^{1}_{u}
such that for every
(t,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

(29)

\Big|\langle u\rangle^{M}\Big(Q_{\infty}^{\beta}(u)-e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}f(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big|\leq\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{N+2}}{e^{2t}}.

Proof.

Since Proposition
4.6.3
is in fact verified for all
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<+\infty
, there exists
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}\in L^{\infty}_{u}
such that (
29
) holds. As
M>2
, we have
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}\in L^{1}_{u}
as well.
∎

Remark 5.1.1
.

Performing the change of variables
(x,v)\mapsto(s,u)
, the total mass of the system can be written as

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}=2\|\bar{f}\|_{L^{1}_{s,u}}=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

We observe that the function
Q_{\infty}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to[0,\infty)
determines the normalized total mass
Q_{\infty}(u_{0})
of the system in the stable leaves
\{u=u_{0}\}
.

We are now able to show that the asymptotic behavior of the spatial density
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
is described by a self similar asymptotic profile in terms of the normalized stable average
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}
.

Proposition 5.1.2
.

For every
|\beta|\leq N-2
, the spatial density
\rho(Z^{\beta}f)
satisfies

\Big|e^{2t}\rho(Z^{\beta}f)(t,x)-Q_{\infty}^{\beta}\Big(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{N+5}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
|\beta|\leq N-2
. Applying Lemma
4.7.4
and the estimate (
4.7
) to
g(t,x,v):=Z^{\beta}f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))
, we obtain

(30)

\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}Z^{\beta}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v-e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\Big(t,y,\Big(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)e^{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\dfrac{\langle t\rangle^{N+5}}{(e^{t}+|x|)^{2}}.

Furthermore, we obtain from Proposition
5.1.1
that

\Big|Q^{\beta}_{\infty}(u)-e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\beta}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{\langle t\rangle^{N+2}}{e^{2t}\langle u\rangle^{2}},

as
M-3\geq 2
. The result follows from (
30
) and the last estimate applied for
u=e^{-t}x
.
∎

5.2.
Self-similar asymptotic profile of the force field

We fix, for all this section, a sufficiently regular function
Q_{\infty}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to[0,\infty)
. Motivated by Proposition
5.1.2
, we define the
asymptotic potential

\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
as the unique solution to the
asymptotic Poisson equation
given by

(31)

\Delta_{u}\,\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]=Q_{\infty}.

We show the dependence of the asymptotic potential
\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
on the RHS of the asymptotic Poisson equation by
\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]
.

Let us recall the set of vector fields
\lambda_{u}:=\{\partial_{u^{i}},L_{u},R^{u}_{ij,u}\}
introduced in Subsection
2.2
. We consider an ordering on
\lambda_{u}
, which is compatible with the one on
\lambda
and we denote by
Z_{u}^{\beta}
the vector field
Z_{u}^{\beta_{1}}\dots Z^{\beta_{p}}_{u}
, where
|\beta|=p
.

Before commuting the asymptotic Poisson equation, we prove that
Q_{\infty}
is differentiable and we relate its derivatives to
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

For any
|\beta|\leq N-1
the function
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}
is of class
C^{N-1-|\beta|}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u})
. Moreover, the derivatives of
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}
can be obtained by iterating the following relations.

(a)

if
\beta_{s}\geq 1
, so that
Z^{\beta}
is composed by at least one stable vector field, we have
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}=0
;

(b)

if
Z^{\beta}=U_{i}Z^{\kappa}
, we have
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}=\partial_{u^{i}}Q^{\kappa}_{\infty}
;

(c)

if
Z^{\beta}=LZ^{\kappa}
, we have
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}=L_{u}Q^{\kappa}_{\infty}-2Q^{\kappa}_{\infty};

(d)

if
Z^{\beta}=R_{12}Z^{\kappa}
, we have
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}=R_{12,u}Q^{\kappa}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

First, we assume that
\beta_{s}\geq 1
. Since
[Z,S_{i}]
either vanishes or it is another stable vector field, then, it suffices to consider the case when
Z^{\beta}=S_{i}Z^{\xi}
. In this case, we can integrate by parts to show that
\int Z^{\beta}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s=0
, so
Q^{\beta}_{\infty}=0
. Next, for every
(t,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\partial_{u^{i}}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}e^{t}\partial_{u^{i}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}U_{i}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s.

According to Proposition
5.1.1
, the right hand side converges to
Q_{\infty}^{\beta}
in
L^{\infty}_{u}
as
t\to\infty
. If
Z^{\beta}=LZ^{\kappa}
, it is enough to notice

e^{2t}\int LZ^{\kappa}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s=e^{2t}\big(u^{1}\partial_{u^{1}}+u^{2}\partial_{u^{2}}\big)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s-2e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s,

where we used that
L=u^{1}\partial_{u^{1}}+u^{2}\partial_{u^{2}}+s^{1}\partial_{s^{1}}+s^{2}\partial_{s^{2}}
. It remains to apply the Proposition
5.1.1
. Finally, it is enough to notice

e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}R_{12}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s=e^{2t}\big(u^{1}\partial_{u^{2}}-u^{2}\partial_{u^{1}}\big)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}Z^{\kappa}\bar{f}\mathrm{d}s,

where we used that
R_{12}=u^{1}\partial_{u^{2}}-u^{2}\partial_{u^{1}}+s^{1}\partial_{s^{2}}-s^{2}\partial_{s^{1}}
, and to apply Proposition
5.1.1
.
∎

We have the following commutation relations.

Proposition 5.2.2
.

There holds, for any
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{u}\,\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]=\partial_{u^{i}}Q_{\infty},

\displaystyle\qquad\Delta_{u}\,R_{12,u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]=R_{12,u}Q_{\infty},

\displaystyle\Delta_{u}\,L_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]

\displaystyle=L_{u}Q_{\infty}+2Q_{\infty}.

In particular, for any
Z_{u}\in\lambda_{u}
we have

Z_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]=\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Z_{u}Q_{\infty}]+2\delta^{Z_{u}}_{L_{u}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}].

More generally, for any multi-index
\beta
, there exist
C^{\beta}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(32)

\Delta_{u}\,Z_{u}^{\beta}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq|\beta|}C^{\beta}_{\alpha}Z_{u}^{\alpha}Q_{\infty},\qquad Z_{u}^{\beta}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq|\beta|}C^{\beta}_{\alpha}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z_{u}^{\alpha}Q_{\infty}\big].

Proof.

One simply has to iterate the commutation relations

[\Delta_{u},\partial_{u^{i}}]=[\Delta_{u},R^{u}_{12}]=0,\qquad[\Delta_{u},L_{u}]=2\Delta_{u}.

∎

Remark 5.2.1
.

We observe that the coefficients
C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}
in the commuted asymptotic Poisson equation (
32
), are equal to the coefficients
C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}
in the commuted Poisson equation (
11
). In the following, this property is key in order to obtain the self-similar asymptotic profile of the commuted force field
\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi
.

We are now able to show that the asymptotic behavior of the force field
\nabla_{x}\phi
is described by a self similar asymptotic profile in terms of the asymptotic potential
\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}
.

Proposition 5.2.3
.

Let
|\gamma|\leq N-2
with
\gamma_{s}=0
. Then, for every
Z^{\beta}\in\lambda^{|\beta|}
, we have

\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2},\qquad\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\left(t,x\right)-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon\,\langle t\rangle^{N+5}e^{-t}.

Proof.

Let
Z^{\gamma}\in\lambda^{|\beta|}
and assume for simplicity that
Z^{\gamma}
does not contain the vector field
L
. Then, by the commutation formula of Propositions
2.5.3
and
5.2.2
,

\Delta Z^{\gamma}\phi=\rho\big(Z^{\gamma}f\big),\qquad\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Z_{u}^{\gamma}Q_{\infty}]=Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}=Q^{\gamma}_{\infty}.

Now, by a change of variables, we have

e^{t}\nabla_{x}\Phi\left(t,x\right)=\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}]\left(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\right),\qquad\Delta\Phi(t,x)=\frac{1}{e^{2t}}Q_{\infty}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\right).

Consequently,

\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\left(t,x\right)-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\right)\right|=e^{t}\left|\nabla_{x}\big(Z^{\gamma}\phi-\Phi\big)\right|\left(t,x\right).

To prove the result, it suffices to control
\nabla_{x}\Psi
, where

\Delta\Psi(t,x)=\rho\big(Z^{\gamma}f\big)-\frac{1}{e^{2t}}Q_{\infty}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{e^{t}}\right).

The proposition follows by using Proposition
5.1.2
and the integral estimate (
15
).
∎

Let us recall that
2u=x+v
and
2s=x-v
. We investigate the convergence of
e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\left(t,e^{t}u+e^{-t}s\right)
along the linear spatial characteristics given by
t\mapsto(t,e^{t}u+e^{-t}s)
.

Proposition 5.2.4
.

For all
(t,s,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}
and every
|\gamma|\leq N-2
, we have

\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\left(t,e^{t}u+e^{-t}s\right)-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(u\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon\,\langle e^{-t}s\rangle\,(1+t)^{N+5}e^{-t}.

Proof.

Fix
(t,s,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}
and apply the mean value theorem. We obtain

\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\left(t,e^{t}u+e^{-t}s\right)-e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,e^{t}u)\right|\leq|s|\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left|\nabla^{2}Z^{\gamma}\phi\right|(t,y).

Use then the decay of the force field
|\nabla^{2}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon e^{-3t}
and Proposition
5.2.3
.
∎

Proposition
5.2.4
allows us to deduce the following corollary, which it will be useful when we will consider improved commutators.

Corollary 5.2.5
.

For all
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
and every
|\gamma|\leq N-2
, we have, with
2u=x+v
,

\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\left(t,x\right)-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(e^{-t}u\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon\,(1+t)^{N+6}e^{-t}\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v).

Proof.

We write
x=e^{t}e^{-t}u-e^{-t}e^{t}s
, where
2u=x+v
and
2s=x-v
, and we apply Proposition
5.2.4
, with a slight abuse of notations, to
(e^{t}s,e^{-t}u)
. It remains to apply Lemma
4.4.1
, which implies
\langle s\rangle\leq\langle e^{t}(x-v)\rangle\lesssim(1+t)\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v).

∎

We deduce from the Proposition
5.2.4
a uniform bound on
\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]
.

Proposition 5.2.6
.

For every
|\gamma|\leq N-2
, we have
\|\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}}\lesssim\epsilon
.

Proof.

This is implied by the estimate
|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,e^{t}u)|\lesssim\epsilon
obtained in Proposition
4.2.2
.
∎

For later use, we prove that the structure of the asymptotic force field is preserved by differentiation.

Corollary 5.2.7
.

For every
|\gamma|\leq N-2
, we set

\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},i}(t,x,v):=e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\Big(\frac{x+v}{2}\Big).

For every
|\gamma|\leq N-3
, there holds

(33)

\displaystyle U_{j}(\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},i})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{U_{j}Z^{\gamma},i},\qquad L(\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},i})=\Delta_{LZ^{\gamma},i}-\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},i},

(34)

\displaystyle R_{12}(\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},i})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{R_{12}Z^{\gamma},i}-\delta^{1}_{i}\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},2}+\delta^{2}_{i}\Delta_{Z^{\gamma},1}.

Proof.

First, we compute the derivatives

\displaystyle U_{j}(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x))

\displaystyle=e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}U_{i}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x),

\displaystyle L(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x))

\displaystyle=e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}LZ^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x).

For the angular term, we compute

R_{12}(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x))=e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}R_{12}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\delta_{i}^{1}e^{t}\partial_{x^{2}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)+\delta_{i}^{2}e^{t}\partial_{x^{1}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x).

∎

5.3.
Convergence of the distribution along the modified characteristics

Motivated by
[
VRVR23
, Section 6]
and by Proposition
5.3.1
, we modify the linear stable characteristics
t\mapsto e^{-t}s
as follows.

Definition 5.3.1
.

Let
(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. We define the
modified stable characteristic

S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(\cdot,s,u)\!:t\mapsto e^{-t}(s+{\mathscr{C}}(t,u))
to be the trajectory given by

\displaystyle S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u):=e^{-t}\Big(s+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big](u)\Big).

We also define the
correction of the stable characteristic
as
{\mathscr{C}}(t,u)=\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big](u)
. Observe that the components
{\mathscr{C}}^{i}(t,u)
of the correction term
{\mathscr{C}}(t,u)
verify

\forall t\in[0,\infty),\qquad|{\mathscr{C}}^{i}|(t,u)\lesssim(1+t)|\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]|\lesssim\epsilon(1+t).

We now need to adapt the result of Proposition
5.2.4
for the modified trajectories.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

For every
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}
and every
|\gamma|\leq N-2
, we have

\left|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\big(t,e^{t}u+S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u)\big)-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(u\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{N+6}}{e^{t}}\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v).

Proof.

It suffices to apply Proposition
5.2.4
to
(e^{t}S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u),u)
and to use that

\langle S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u)\rangle\lesssim\langle e^{-t}s\rangle+\langle e^{-t}{\mathscr{C}}(t,u)\rangle\lesssim\langle e^{-t}s\rangle\lesssim(1+t)\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,x,v),

where, in the last step, we applied Lemma
4.4.1
.
∎

We now estimate the time derivative of a distribution function
g(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)
evaluated along the modified characteristics.

Proposition 5.3.2
.

Let
g:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to[0,\infty)
be a sufficiently regular distribution function, and set
h(t,s,u):=g(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)
. Then, for all
(t,s,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
we have,

|\partial_{t}h(t,s,u)|\lesssim|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(g)|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)+\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{N+6}}{e^{t}}\sum_{Z\in\lambda}|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}Zg|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}},e^{t}u).

Proof.

We have, for all
(t,s,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}h(t,s,u)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|(\partial_{t}g+u\cdot\nabla_{u}g-s\cdot\nabla_{s}g-\frac{\mu}{2}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{u}g+\frac{\mu}{2}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{s}g)(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Big|(\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{u}g+(\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]-e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi)\cdot e^{-t}\nabla_{s}g)(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(g)|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)+e^{-2t}|e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot e^{t}\nabla_{u}g|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big|e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi-\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]\big||e^{-t}\nabla_{s}g|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

Finally, we use Proposition
5.3.1
to obtain

|\partial_{t}h(t,s,u)|\lesssim|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(g)|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)+\epsilon(1+t)^{N+6}e^{-t}\sum_{Z\in\lambda}|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}Zg|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}},e^{t}u).

∎

By applying this result to the Vlasov field
\bar{f}
, we obtain the existence of a distribution
\bar{f}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}_{s,u}
such that
f(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}},u)\to\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)
as
t\to\infty
. Applying this argument to
\partial_{s^{i}}\bar{f}
, we deduce that
\bar{f}_{\infty}
is
C^{1}
with respect to the stable variable
s
if
N\geq 3
. Obtaining the regularity with respect to the unstable variable
u
requires a more thorough analysis.

5.4.
Modified commutators

We assume, for the rest of this article, that
N\geq 3
. Let
Z\in\lambda\setminus\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
be a vector field. Contrary to the case of the stable vector fields, the error term
[\mathbf{T}_{\phi},Z]f
does not decay sufficiently fast in order to prove a convergence result for
Zf
, even along the modified characteristics. Recall from Lemma
2.5.1
that

(35)

[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z]=\mu\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{x^{k}}(Z\phi+c_{Z}\phi)\partial_{v^{k}},

where
c_{Z}=-2
if
Z=L
, otherwise,
c_{Z}=0
. Rewriting
\partial_{v^{j}}
in terms of the stable and unstable vector fields, and estimating the force field through Lemma
4.2.2
, we have

(36)

\Big|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}(Zf)+\frac{\mu}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}e^{t}\partial_{x^{k}}(Z\phi+c_{Z}\phi)e^{-t}\partial_{s^{k}}\Big|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon}{e^{2t}}\sum_{Z\in\lambda}|Zf|.

In view of Proposition
4.5.1
, the right hand side is bounded by
\epsilon(1+t)e^{-2t}
and then it belongs to
L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}
. On the other hand, if
\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[ZQ_{\infty}\big]
does not vanish, the decay rate of
|e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z\phi(t,x)|
along the particle trajectories is not time integrable. For this reason, we modify the linear commutator
Z
in a similar fashion than the modification made for the characteristic flow. Motivated by Corollary
5.2.5
and (
36
), we introduce the following set of modified vector fields.

Definition 5.4.1
.

For every
Z\in\lambda\setminus\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
, we define the
asymptotic modified vector fields

Z^{\mathrm{mod}}
as

\displaystyle Z^{\mathrm{mod}}:=Z+\frac{1}{2}\mu t\sum_{k=1}^{2}\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k}+c_{Z}\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)S_{k}.

We also define the
correction coefficients of the asymptotic modified vector fields
as

{\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}(t,u)=\frac{1}{2}\mu t\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\big(e^{-t}u\big)+c_{Z}\frac{1}{2}\mu t\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]\big(e^{-t}u\big),

so that
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}=Z+{\mathscr{C}}^{1}_{Z}(t,u)S_{1}+{\mathscr{C}}^{2}_{Z}(t,u)S_{2}
.

We have the improved commutation relations.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

Let
Z\in\lambda\setminus\{S_{1},\,S_{2}\}
. Then, for every
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
we have

\displaystyle 2[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod}}]

\displaystyle=-\mu\left(e^{t}\nabla_{x}Z\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\right)\cdot S+\mu e^{-t}\nabla_{x}Z\phi(t,x)\cdot U

\displaystyle\quad-c_{Z}\mu\left(e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\right)\cdot S+c_{Z}\mu e^{-t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)\cdot U

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\!\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2}\!{\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}(t,u)\nabla_{x}S_{k}\phi(t,x)\cdot\big(e^{-t}U-e^{t}S\big)-e^{-t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)\cdot U\big({\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\big)(t,u)S_{k}.

Proof.

In view of the commutation relation (
35
),

\displaystyle[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod}}]

\displaystyle=[{\mathbf{T}}_{F},Z]+\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2}{\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\,[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},S_{k}]+{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left({\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\right)S_{k}

\displaystyle=\mu\nabla_{x}Z\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}+c_{Z}\mu\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}+\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2}\mu{\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\nabla_{x}S_{k}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}+{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left({\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\right)S_{k}.

Then, we write
2\nabla_{v}=e^{-t}U-e^{t}S
and we compute, using
{\mathbf{T}}_{0}(e^{-t}u)=0
,

{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\left({\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\mu\partial_{u^{k}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[(Z^{u}+c_{Z})Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)-\mu\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}{\mathscr{C}}^{k}_{Z}(t,u).

∎

We now state the higher order commutation formula. For this, it will be convenient to denote by
P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
to any quantity of the form

\prod_{1\leq i\leq p}Z^{\gamma^{i}}\big({\mathscr{C}}^{k_{i}}_{Z^{\ell_{i}}}\big),\quad p\!\in\!\mathbb{N},\;\;\,0\leq q\leq N-3,\;\;\,k_{i}\!\in\!\{1,2\},\;\;\,1\leq\ell_{i}\leq 4,\;\;\,\sum_{1\leq i\leq p}|\gamma^{i}|=q,\;\;\,\gamma^{i}_{u}=|\gamma^{i}|.

The last condition means that
Z^{\gamma^{i}}
is only composed by unstable vector fields
U_{i}
,
L
and
R_{12}
. If
p=0
, then, we set the convention that
P_{0,q}({\mathscr{C}})=1
. Note that Proposition
5.2.2
implies

\displaystyle\big|P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\big|(t,u)

\displaystyle=\Big|\prod_{1\leq i\leq p}\partial_{u^{1}}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}Z_{u}^{\gamma^{i}}\big({\mathscr{C}}^{k_{i}}_{Z^{\ell_{i}}}\big)\Big|(t,u)\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|\leq q+1}\big|(1+t)\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big](u)\big|^{p}

(37)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{p}(1+t)^{p}.

Note further that the functions
P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
can be used in order to express a differential operator
Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}
, for
|\beta|\leq N-2
, in terms of differential operators
Z^{\kappa}
. We have, as
S_{k}P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})=0
, that

(38)

Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}=\sum_{q+|\kappa|\leq|\beta|,}\sum_{p\leq|\beta|,\,q\leq|\beta|-1}D_{\kappa,p,q}^{\beta}\,P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})Z^{\kappa},\qquad D^{\beta}_{\kappa,p,q}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Because of regularity issues on the coefficients
{\mathscr{C}}_{Z}
, we are only able to commute the Vlasov equation by
Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}
where
|\beta|\leq N-3
.

Proposition 5.4.2
.

Let
Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}\in\lambda_{\mathrm{mod}}^{|\beta|}
with
|\beta|\leq N-3
. Then, we can write the commutator
[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}]
as a linear combination of the following two types of terms,

\displaystyle\bullet\;\,P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\left(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\right)Z^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\bullet\;\,P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)Z^{\kappa},

where

|\gamma|+|\kappa|\leq|\beta|+1,\qquad|\gamma|,\,|\kappa|\leq|\beta|,\qquad p\leq|\beta|,\qquad q\leq|\beta|,\qquad i\in\{1,2\}.

Proof.

For the case
|\beta|=1
, apply Proposition
5.4.1
and note, in view of
S_{k}\phi=e^{-2t}U_{k}\phi
,

\nabla_{x}S_{k}\phi(t,x)\cdot e^{t}S=e^{-t}\nabla U_{k}\phi(t,x)\cdot S.

Let
1\leq n\leq N-4
such that the result holds for all multi-indices
|\beta|\leq n
. Fix then
|\beta|=n
,
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}\in\lambda_{\mathrm{mod}}
and write

(39)

[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}]=[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod}}]Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}+Z^{\mathrm{mod}}[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}].

We will make use several times of the following properties. For any
Z\in\lambda
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\leq N-4
,
ZP_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
can be written as a linear combination of terms of the form
P_{p,q+1}({\mathscr{C}})
.

Combining the first order commutation formula with (
38
), which allows us to rewrite
Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}
in terms of the linear commutators as well as
P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
, and this last property, one gets that the first term on the right hand side of (
39
) has the expected form.

Next, by the induction hypothesis, we can write
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}[{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi},Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}]
as a linear combination of terms of the form
\mathcal{T}_{1}[Z^{\mathrm{mod}}]
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}[Z^{\mathrm{mod}}]
, where, for a vector field
X
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}[X]

\displaystyle:=X\Big[P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\left(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\right)Z^{\kappa}\Big],

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}[X]

\displaystyle:=X\Big[P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)Z^{\kappa}\Big],

and
|\gamma|+|\kappa|\leq|\beta|+1
,
|\gamma|\leq|\beta|
,
|\kappa|\leq|\beta|
,
p\leq|\beta|
,
q\leq|\beta|+1
as well as
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
X=S_{k}
, for
k\in\{1,2\}
, then, using
S_{k}(u)=S_{k}(t)=0
, we get

\mathcal{T}_{1}[S_{k}]=e^{t}S_{k}\big(\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)\big)Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\left(e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\right)S_{k}Z^{\kappa}.

Clearly, the second term on the right hand side has the expected form. For the first one, use that
e^{t}S_{k}\psi=e^{-t}U_{k}\psi
for any function
\psi(t,x)
and then
[U_{k},\partial_{x^{i}}]=0
. For
\mathcal{T}_{2}[S_{k}]
, using the same arguments, we get

\mathcal{T}_{2}[S_{k}]=P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}S_{k}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)S_{k}Z^{\kappa},

which concludes the case
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}=S_{k}
. Otherwise
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}\in\lambda_{\mathrm{mod}}\setminus\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
, so that

\mathcal{T}_{k}\big[Z^{\mathrm{mod}}\big]=\mathcal{T}_{k}\big[Z\big]+{\mathscr{C}}^{1}_{Z}\mathcal{T}_{k}\big[S_{1}\big]+{\mathscr{C}}^{2}_{Z}\mathcal{T}_{k}\big[S_{2}\big],\qquad k\in\{1,2\}.

In the view of the analysis of the case
Z^{\mathrm{mod}}=S_{k}
, the last two terms on the right hand side has the expected form. For the first one, we have

\mathcal{T}_{1}\big[Z\big]=Z\big(P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\big)\Delta^{i}_{Z^{\gamma}}Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})Z\big(\Delta^{i}_{Z^{\gamma}}\big)Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\Delta^{i}_{Z^{\gamma}}ZZ^{\kappa}.

The first and the last terms on the right hand side have the required form. The same holds true for the second one according to Corollary
5.2.7
. Finally,

\mathcal{T}_{2}\big[Z\big]=Z\big(P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})\big)e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})Z\big(e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi\big)Z^{\kappa}+P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi ZZ^{\kappa}

and since
[e^{-t}\partial_{x^{i}},Z]\in\{0,\pm e^{-t}\partial_{x^{1}},\pm e^{-t}\partial_{x^{2}}\}
, all the terms on the right hand side have the expected form.
∎

We now control the two type of error terms in Proposition
5.4.2
. As a result, we prove a uniform boundedness statement for the derivatives
Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}f
.

Proposition 5.4.3
.

For any
|\beta|\leq N-3
, we have

\forall\,(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad\big|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}\big(Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}f\big)\big|(t,x,v)\lesssim\epsilon\frac{\langle t\rangle^{2N+4}}{e^{t}}\sum_{|\kappa|\leq|\beta|}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\kappa}f\big|(t,x,v).

Proof.

Fix
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}
. Combining Proposition
5.4.2
with the bound (
37
) on
P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
, we obtain that
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(Z^{\mathrm{mod}}f)
is bounded by

\langle t\rangle^{N-2}\!\sum_{|\gamma|,\,|\kappa|\leq N-3}\!\Big[\Big|e^{t}\partial_{x^{i}}Z^{\gamma}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Z^{\gamma}_{u}Q_{\infty}\big]\!\left(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\right)\!\Big|+\frac{1}{e^{t}}\big|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi\big|(t,x)\Big]\big|Z^{\kappa}f\big|(t,x,v).

It remains to estimate the force field through Proposition
4.2.2
, so that
|\nabla_{x}Z^{\gamma}\phi|(t,x)\lesssim\epsilon e^{-t}
, and to apply Corollary
5.2.5
.
∎

We state the next corollary for completeness, even if we will not need it in order to prove that
\bar{f}_{\infty}\in C^{N-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s})
.

Corollary 5.4.4
.

For any
|\beta|\leq N-3
, there holds

\forall\,(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v},\qquad\big(\langle e^{-t}(x+v)\rangle^{M-1}+\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M-1}(t,x,v)\big)\big|Z^{\mathrm{mod},\beta}f\big|(t,x,v)\leq 2\epsilon.

Corollary
5.4.4
is a direct consequence of Proposition
5.4.3
, the property
{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}})=0
, the bound
|{\mathbf{T}}_{\phi}(e^{-t}(x+v))|\lesssim\epsilon\,e^{-2t}
obtained in (
23
), and the
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
estimates in Proposition
4.5.1
.

5.5.
Regularity of the scattering state

In order to prove that
\bar{f}_{\infty}
is differentiable with respect to
u
, we need to compute the first order derivatives of the correction terms in the modified characteristics. We will bound their higher order derivatives in the following.

Lemma 5.5.1
.

Let
i,k\in\{1,2\}
. Then, for every
(t,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\partial_{u^{k}}{\mathscr{C}}^{i}(t,u)=e^{-t}{\mathscr{C}}^{i}_{U_{k}}(t,e^{t}u).

Proof.

We have

\partial_{u^{k}}{\mathscr{C}}^{i}(t,u)=\partial_{u^{k}}\Big(\frac{\mu}{2e^{t}}t\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[Q_{\infty}\big](u)\Big)=\frac{\mu}{2e^{t}}t\partial_{u^{i}}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}\big[\partial_{u^{k}}Q_{\infty}\big](u)=e^{-t}{\mathscr{C}}^{i}_{U_{k}}(t,e^{t}u).

∎

Before arriving to the main result of the paper, we obtain a useful lemma to estimate the derivatives of the profile
f(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)
.

Lemma 5.5.2
.

Let
h\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u},{\mathbb{R}})
. Then, for any
\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{s^{i}}\big[h(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\big]

\displaystyle=\big[S_{i}h\big](t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u),

\displaystyle\partial_{u^{i}}\big[h(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\big]

\displaystyle=\big[U_{i}^{\mathrm{mod}}h\big](t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

Proof.

For the first identity, simply use
\partial_{s^{i}}S^{j}_{{\mathscr{C}}}=e^{-t}\delta^{j}_{i}
as well as
e^{-t}\partial_{s^{i}}=S_{i}
. Next, since
\partial_{u^{i}}S^{j}_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,u)=\partial_{u^{i}}{\mathscr{C}}^{j}(t,u)=e^{-t}{\mathscr{C}}^{j}_{U_{i}}(t,e^{t}u)
according to Lemma
5.5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{u^{i}}\big[h(t,S{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\big]=

\displaystyle e^{-t}{\mathscr{C}}_{U_{i}}(t,e^{t}u)\cdot\big[\nabla_{s}h\big](t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)

\displaystyle+e^{t}\big[\partial_{u^{i}}h\big](t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

It remains to use
e^{-t}\partial_{s^{j}}=S_{j}
as well as
e^{t}\partial_{u^{j}}=U_{j}
.
∎

We are now able to prove the main modified scattering result of the paper.

Proposition 5.5.3
.

There exists a distribution function
\bar{f}_{\infty}\in C^{N-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u})
such that for every
|\kappa|+|\xi|\leq N-3
and all
(t,s,u)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\langle u\rangle^{M}\,\langle s\rangle^{M-1}\Big|\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}\big(\bar{f}(t,S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)\big)-\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{3N+M+1}}{e^{t}}.

In particular, as
M\geq 6
, we have
\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}\bar{f}_{\infty}\in L^{1}_{s,u}
.

Proof.

Let
h(t,s,u):=\bar{f}(t,S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)
. Iterating Lemma
5.5.2
, we have

\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}h(t,s,u)=\big[U^{\mathrm{mod},\,\xi}S^{\kappa}\bar{f}\big](t,S_{\mathscr{C}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

We then get, by applying Proposition
5.3.2
to
\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}h
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}h|(t,s,u)\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\big|\mathbf{T}_{\phi}\big(U^{\mathrm{mod},\,\xi}S^{\kappa}\bar{f}\big)\big|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)

\displaystyle+\epsilon(1+t)^{N+6}e^{-t}\sum_{Z\in\lambda}|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}ZU^{\mathrm{mod},\,\xi}S^{\kappa}\bar{f}|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

The first term is controlled in Proposition
5.4.3
. In order to bound the second term on the right hand side, we express
U^{\mathrm{mod},\,\xi}
in terms of
Z^{\alpha}
and
P_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})
through (
38
). Since
|ZP_{p,q}({\mathscr{C}})|\lesssim(1+t)^{p}
by (
37
), we get

|\partial_{t}\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}h|(t,s,u)\lesssim\epsilon(1+t)^{2N+4}e^{-t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-2}|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}Z^{\alpha}\bar{f}|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u).

Note now that Proposition
5.2.6
and Lemma
4.4.1
provide

\displaystyle|s|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}|S_{{\mathscr{C}}}|(t,s,u)+e^{-t}|{\mathscr{C}}|(t,s,u)\lesssim e^{-t}|S_{{\mathscr{C}}}|(t,s,u)+\epsilon,

\displaystyle e^{-t}|s^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,e^{-t}s^{\prime},u^{\prime})+|\overline{\varphi}|(t,e^{-t}s^{\prime},u^{\prime})\leq 2(1+t)\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,e^{-t}s^{\prime},u^{\prime}).

We then deduce that

\displaystyle\langle u\rangle^{M}\,\langle s\rangle^{M-1}|\partial_{t}\partial_{u}^{\xi}\partial_{s}^{\kappa}h|(t,s,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\langle t\rangle^{2N+M+3}e^{-t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-2}\langle u\rangle^{M}\big|\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{M}Z^{\alpha}\bar{f}\big|(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\langle t\rangle^{3N+M+1}e^{-t},

where, in the last step, we apply the
L^{\infty}
estimates of Proposition
4.5.1
. It implies that
(s,u)\mapsto\langle u\rangle^{M}\,\langle s\rangle^{M-1}h(t,s,u)
converges in
C^{N-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s})
, as
t\to+\infty
, and that the stated rate of convergence holds.
∎

6.
Asymptotic properties in terms of the scattering state

In this section, we obtain several asymptotic properties of small data solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
in terms of the scattering state. For this purpose, we revise the estimates of the normalized stable averages and the velocity averages, to obtain Theorem
3.3
. We finish the paper with the proof of Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.5

6.1.
Asymptotics of normalized stable averages

In this subsection, we revise the convergence of the normalized stable averages obtained in Proposition
5.1.1
. For this purpose, we use the modified scattering of the distribution function.

Proposition 6.1.1
.

For every
(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}s-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\frac{(1+t)^{N+5}}{e^{t}}.

In other words, the normalized stable average
Q(t,u)
converges to
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s
.

Proof.

Performing the change of variables
s\mapsto y(s):=e^{t}s-\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}](u)
, we have

e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,y,u),e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}y.

We then deduce that

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s,u\rangle^{3}|\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)|,

which, in view of
M\geq 6
and Proposition
5.5.3
, implies
Q_{\infty}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}s
. It remains to apply Proposition
5.1.2
.
∎

In particular, we obtain the following corollary when
u=0
in Proposition
6.1.1
.

Corollary 6.1.2
.

For every
(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
, we have

\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\langle u\rangle\frac{(1+t)^{N+5}}{e^{t}}.

Proof.

After performing the change of variables
s\mapsto y(s):=e^{t}s-\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}](\frac{u}{e^{t}})
and the mean value theorem, the proof is identical to the one of Lemma
6.1.1

∎

6.2.
Asymptotics of velocity averages

In this subsection, we revise the decay estimate of the spatial density performed in Proposition
4.7.5
. We use the asymptotics derived in Corollary
6.1.2
for the normalized stable averages in order to obtain the precise late-asymptotic behavior of the spatial density

Proposition 6.2.1
.

For every
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}
, the spatial density satisfies

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\Big|\lesssim\epsilon(1+|x|)\frac{(1+t)^{7}}{e^{t}}.

In other words, the normalized spatial density
e^{2t}\rho(f)
converges to the constant
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s
.

Proof.

Consider
g(t,x,v)=f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))
. Applying Lemma
2.3.2
and Proposition
4.5.1
to
g
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\big(\langle s\rangle+\langle u\rangle)^{6}|\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|(t,s,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\big(\langle e^{t}s\rangle+\langle e^{-t}u\rangle)^{6}\big|Z\bar{f}\big|(t,s,u)

(40)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon(1+t)^{7},

since we have
M\geq 6
and
\langle e^{t}s\rangle\lesssim(1+t)\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{mod}}(t,s,u)
. Applying Lemma
4.7.4
to the distribution
g
and using Lemma
2.3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{f}\Big(t,\frac{y}{e^{t}},x\Big)\mathrm{d}y=e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,x)\mathrm{d}s.

Finally, we conclude using Corollary
6.1.2
.
∎

6.3.
Weak convergence I: Concentration in the unstable manifold

In this subsection, we show that
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t)
converges weakly to the Dirac mass
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{x,v}
be a compactly supported test function. The starting point consists in performing the change of variables
y=\frac{1}{2}e^{t}(x-v)
in the integral
\int g(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\varphi(x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
g:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution, and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{x,v}
be a compactly supported test function. Then, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
we have

e^{2t}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}g(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\varphi(x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x\ -\ \frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big)\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{2e^{t}},x\ -\ \frac{y}{2e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Next, we apply the mean value theorem to obtain a technical lemma that will be used to capture the weak convergence property of
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t)
.

Lemma 6.3.2
.

Let
g:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution. Then, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}g(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\varphi(x,v)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x~-

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}}\bar{\varphi}(0,x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{4}(|\nabla_{s,u}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{g}|+|\bar{\varphi}||\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|).

Proof.

Using the change of variables
y=\frac{1}{2}e^{t}(x-v)
and applying the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{2e^{t}},x-\frac{y}{2e^{t}}\Big)-\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\bar{\varphi}(0,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}|s|(|\nabla_{s,u}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{g}|+|\nabla_{u}\bar{g}||\bar{\varphi}|).

Then, the difference

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(x-\frac{y}{e^{t}}\Big)\Big)\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{2e^{t}},x-\frac{y}{2e^{t}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{x}{e^{t}}\Big)\bar{\varphi}(0,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\Big|

satisfies the corresponding time decay estimate.
∎

We are now able to prove the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 6.3.3
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{x,v}
be a compactly supported test function. Then, the distribution
f(t)
satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\delta_{s=0}(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

In other words, the distribution
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t)
converges weakly to
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,0)\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
.

Proof.

Applying the previous lemma to the distribution
g(t,x,v):=f(t,X_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),V_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{2}}f(t,x,v)\varphi(x,v)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x~-

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}}\bar{\varphi}(0,x)e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{w}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,w,x)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{4}(|\nabla_{s,u}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{f}|+|\nabla_{u}\bar{f}||\bar{\varphi}|).

We conclude by using the dominated convergence theorem and Corollary
6.1.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}\bar{\varphi}(0,x)e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{w}}\bar{f}(t,w,x)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}\bar{\varphi}(0,x)\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{w}^{2}}\bar{f}_{\infty}(w,0)\mathrm{d}w.

∎

6.4.
Weak convergence II: Hyperbolicity of the Hamiltonian flow

Let
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. In this subsection, we show that
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
converges weakly to the Dirac mass
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
. The starting point consists in performing the change of variables
y=e^{t}s
in the integral
\int\bar{g}(t,S_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),U_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\bar{\varphi}(s,u-e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u
, where
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{s,u}
is a compactly supported test function.

Lemma 6.4.1
.

Let
\bar{g}:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a regular distribution, and let
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{s,u}
be a compactly supported test function. Then, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
we have

e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}}\bar{g}(t,S_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),U_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\bar{\varphi}(s,u-e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{u}{e^{t}}+\bar{u}\Big)\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{e^{t}},u\Big)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u.

Next, we apply the mean value theorem to obtain a technical lemma that will be used to capture the weak convergence property of
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
.

Lemma 6.4.2
.

Let
\bar{g}:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sufficiently regular distribution. Then, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}}\bar{g}(t,S_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t),U_{{\mathscr{L}}}(-t))\bar{\varphi}(s,u-e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u~-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}}\bar{\varphi}(0,u)\mathrm{d}u\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{g}(t,y,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{4}(|\nabla_{s,u}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{g}|+|\bar{\varphi}||\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|).

Proof.

Using the change of variables in Lemma
6.4.1
and applying the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{u}{e^{t}}+\bar{u}\Big)

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{e^{t}},u\Big)-\bar{g}(t,y,\bar{u})\bar{\varphi}(0,u)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{4}(|\nabla_{s}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{g}|+|\bar{\varphi}||\nabla_{u}\bar{g}|).

Then, the difference

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{g}\Big(t,y,\frac{u}{e^{t}}+\bar{u}\Big)\bar{\varphi}\Big(\frac{y}{e^{t}},u\Big)-\bar{g}(t,y,\bar{u})\bar{\varphi}(0,u)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\Big|

satisfies the corresponding time decay estimate.
∎

We are now able to prove the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 6.4.3
.

Let
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{s,u}
be a compactly supported test function, and let
\bar{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}
. Then, the distribution
\bar{f}(t)
satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2t}\int_{s,u}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\bar{\varphi}(s,u-e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\int_{s}\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\int_{s,u}\bar{\varphi}(s,u)\delta_{s=0}(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

In other words, the distribution
e^{2t}\bar{f}(t,s,u+\bar{u}e^{t})
converges weakly to
(\int\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s)\delta_{s=0}(s)
.

Proof.

Applying the previous lemma to the distribution
\bar{g}(t,s,u):=\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t),U_{{\mathscr{L}}}(t))
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\bar{\varphi}(s,u-e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u~-

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{u}^{2}}\bar{\varphi}(0,u)\mathrm{d}u\cdot e^{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}^{2}}\bar{f}(t,s,e^{t}\bar{u})\mathrm{d}s\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s\rangle^{4}(|\nabla_{s}\bar{\varphi}||\bar{f}|+|Z\bar{f}||\bar{\varphi}|).

We conclude by using the dominated convergence theorem and Corollary
6.1.2
.
∎

6.5.
Conservation laws of the system

In this subsection, we show an explicit characterization of the total mass and the Hamiltonian energy of the system in terms of the scattering state. In other words, we relate the Hamiltonian energy and the total mass of the initial data
f_{0}
, to the asymptotic Hamiltonian energy and the asymptotic total mass in terms of the scattering state
f_{\infty}
, respectively.

Proposition 6.5.1
.

For every regular small data solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we have

\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f_{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v-\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]|^{2}\mathrm{d}u=\mathcal{H}[f_{0}].

Note that the asymptotic Hamiltonian energy
\mathcal{H}[f_{\infty}]
is finite, due to the estimates shown in the previous sections. Proposition
6.5.1
follows by the conservation in time of the Hamiltonian energy, and the following two lemmata.

Lemma 6.5.2
.

There holds

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v=-8\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}(u\cdot s)\bar{f}_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

Moving to the hyperbolic variables
(s,u)
and performing the change of variables

(s,u)\mapsto(y,w):=\Big(e^{t}s-\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]\Big(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\Big),\frac{u}{e^{t}}\Big),

we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}(|v|^{2}-|x|^{2})f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=-8\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{s}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}e^{-t}u\cdot e^{t}s\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=-8\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{w}}(e^{t}w)\cdot S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,y,w)\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,y,w),e^{t}w)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}w,

where we note that
|(e^{t}w)\cdot S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,y,w)|\lesssim|w|(|s|+\epsilon(1+t))
. We then deduce that

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}(u\cdot s)(\bar{f}(t,s,u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u))\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\Big|\leq\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s,u\rangle^{4}|\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)|,

which, in view of
M\geq 6
and Proposition
5.5.3
, implies the result.

∎

Lemma 6.5.3
.

There holds

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}|\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]|^{2}(u)\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

Performing the change of variables
x\mapsto u=e^{-t}x
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}|e^{t}\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}(t,e^{t}u)\mathrm{d}u.

We then conclude by applying the dominated convergence theorem and Proposition
5.2.4
.
∎

We finish this subsection with the explicit characterization of the total mass of the system in terms of the scattering state.

Proposition 6.5.4
.

For every regular small data solution
f
to the Vlasov–Poisson system with the potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, we have

\|f_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

Moving to the hyperbolic variables
(s,u)
, and performing the change of variables

(s,u)\mapsto(y,w):=\Big(e^{t}s-\frac{1}{2}\mu t\nabla_{u}\phi_{\mathrm{asymp}}[Q_{\infty}]\Big(\frac{u}{e^{t}}\Big),\frac{u}{e^{t}}\Big),

we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{v}}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{y}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{w}}\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,y,w),e^{t}w)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}w.

We then deduce that

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\bar{f}(t,s,u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\Big|\leq\sup_{(s,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{s}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{u}}\langle s,u\rangle^{3}|\bar{f}(t,S_{{\mathscr{C}}}(t,s,u),e^{t}u)-\bar{f}_{\infty}(s,u)|,

which, in view of
M\geq 6
and Proposition
5.5.3
, implies the result.
∎

Remark 6.5.1
.

Observe that the total mass
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
of the system can also be written as
2\|Q_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{u}}
, since the total mass is equal to twice the total mass of the scattering state by Proposition
6.5.4
.

Ambiguity Function Shaping in FMCW Automotive Radar Thanks: * First two authors have equal contributions.
Zahra Esmaeilbeig ∗1⋄ , Arindam Bose ∗2† , Mojtaba Soltanalian 3⋄

Abstract
Frequency-modulated continuous wave (FMCW) radar with inter-chirp coding produces high side-lobes in the Doppler and range dimensions of the radar’s ambiguity function. The high side-lobes may cause miss-detection due to masking between targets that are at similar range and have large received power difference, as is often the case in automotive scenarios. In this paper, we develop a novel code optimization method that attenuates the side-lobes of the radar’s ambiguity function. In particular, we introduce a framework for designing radar transmit sequences by shaping the radar Ambiguity Function (AF) to a desired structure. The proposed approach suppresses the average amplitude of the AF of the transmitted signal in regions of interest by efficiently tackling a longstanding optimization problem. The optimization criterion is quartic in nature with respect to the radar transmit code. A cyclic iterative algorithm is introduced that recasts the quartic problem as a unimodular quadratic problem (UQP) which can be tackled using power-method-like iterations (PMLI). Our numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in designing sequences with desired AF which is of great interest to the future generations of automotive radar sensors.



I
Introduction

In radar signal processing, the range-Doppler response of the transmitted waveform also known as the ambiguity function plays a critical role, as it governs the Doppler and range resolutions of the system and regulates the interference power from unwanted returns at the output of the matched filter to the target signature. To put it another way, the radar designer is faced with the problem of choosing signal waveforms that yield desirable ambiguity functions. What is considered desirable, of course, depends on the operational use of
the radar. While ambiguity function shaping is widely studied in the literature, the topic remains unexplored in the context of automotive radar
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we study the ambiguity function shaping in frequency-modulated continuous wave (FMCW) automotive radar. Shaping radar ambiguity functions has long been considered difficult from a pure design or computational perspective due to the fact that the two-dimensional nature of the ambiguity function implies the number of design constraints would grow much faster than the design variables and that the design objective (to be optimized) has a quartic nature
[
2
]
. In
[
2
]
, a method based on maximum block improvement is devised to tackle the quartic objective in the ambiguity function shaping problem. In a more recent work in
[
3
]
, an algorithm based on accelerated iterative sequential optimization is proposed to minimize the weighted integrated sidelobes level (WISL) over desired range-Doppler bins of interest. A similar problem is solved by successive application of majorization minimization (MM) and projected gradient descent algorithm (PGD) in
[
4
]
. This method can be inefficient due to the approximation error in the MM step. Similar problem is addressed by convex relaxation in
[
5
]
.

In this paper, inspired by the algorithm proposed in
[
6
,
7
]
for mutual interference mitigation, we devise a low-complexity algorithm based on power-method-like iterations to minimize the ambiguity function in the range-Doppler bins corresponding to echoes from clutters in the environment.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we formulate the ambiguity function shaping problem for FMCW radar. In section
III
, we propose our algorithm for designing a radar code with the desired ambiguity function. We evaluate our method via numerical experiments in section
IV
and conclude the paper in section
V
.

II
Problem Formulation

We start by considering an FMCW automotive radar system whose frequency is swept linearly over a bandwidth

B
in a time duration
T_{c}
. The transmit signal with an intra-pulse code length
N
can be represented as

s(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}u(t-nT_{c}),\;0\leq t\leq T_{c}

(1)

where
\mathbf{x}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is the slow-time sequence and the chirp is

u(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{c}}}\exp(\mathrm{j}(2\pi f_{c}t+\pi Kt^{2}))\mathrm{rect}\left(\frac{t}{T_{c}}\right),

(2)

where
K=\frac{B}{T_{c}}
is the chirp rate, and

\mathrm{rect}\left(t\right)=\begin{cases}1&0\leq t\leq 1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3)

In order to keep constant transmit power over the
N
chirps, we constrain the code sequence to be unimodular i.e.
|x_{n}|=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N

[
8
,
9
]
.

The ambiguity function is defined as
[
10
]

\displaystyle\chi(\tau,\nu)

(4)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(t)s^{*}(t-\tau)\exp{(-\mathrm{j}2\pi\nu(t-\tau))}\;dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}{x_{n}u(t-nT_{c})}\right)\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N}{x_{m}^{*}u^{*}(t-mT_{c}-\tau)}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 129.49915pt\ignorespaces\cdot\exp{(-\mathrm{j}2\pi\nu(t-\tau))}\;dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{m}^{*}\left(\int_{0}^{T}{u(t-nT_{c})u^{*}(t-mT_{c}-\tau)e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\nu(t-\tau)}dt}\right)x_{n}.

where
\tau
is the time delay and
\nu
is the Doppler frequency shift.
With an aim to discretize the AF in (
4

for

and

\displaystyle\chi[k,p]

\displaystyle\triangleq\chi(kT_{c},\dfrac{p}{NT_{c}})

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{j}\pi\frac{p}{N}}\text{sinc}\left(\pi\dfrac{p}{N}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{N}{x_{n}x_{n-k}^{*}e^{-\mathrm{j}\pi(n-k)p/N}}.

(5)

where

We assume the target under study is moving with low speed i.e.

\displaystyle r[k,p]\triangleq\sum_{n=1}^{N}{x_{n}x_{n-k}^{*}e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{(n-k)p}{N}}}.

(6)

for

and
p=-\frac{N}{2},\cdots,\frac{N}{2}-1

for odd

.
In the next section, we will primarily be focused on designing the sequence
\{x_{n}\}^{N}_{n=1}
so as to minimize the sidelobes of the discrete-AF in a certain region.

III
Proposed Method

The goal herein is to suppress the energy of the discrete-AF in a region of interest defined by the index sets
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{P}
for delay and Doppler shift, respectively, by minimizing the criterion:

\displaystyle C=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left|r[k,p]\right|^{2}.

(7)

In particular, the AF shaping problem that we are interested in is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}:\penalty\ \underset{\mathbf{x}}{\textrm{minimize}}

\displaystyle\quad C

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\penalty\ \text{is unimodular}.

(8)

Note that the discrete-AF
r[k,p]
can be reformulated as

\displaystyle r[k,p]=\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{D}_{p}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{x},

(9)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{p}

\displaystyle=\text{Diag}\left(\left[e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{p}{N}},\cdots,e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{(N-1)p}{N}},e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi\frac{Np}{N}}\right]\right),

(10)

and

\mathbf{J}_{k}=\mathbf{J}_{-k}^{\mathrm{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{0}&\mathbf{I}_{N-k}\\
\mathbf{I}_{k}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}

(11)

is the shift matrix that performs the shifting of the vector being multiplied by
k
lags.
Therefore the problem in (
7
) can be recast as,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{D}_{p}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{x}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}\mathbf{x}|^{2}

(12)

where
\mathbf{A}_{k,p}=\mathbf{D}_{p}\mathbf{J}_{k}
.
Interestingly, as one can observe,
C
is quartic with respect to
\mathbf{x}
making
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in (
III
) a non-convex problem.
In order to recast the problem in a quadratic form, let

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{k,p}+\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{\mathrm{H}}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{k,p}-\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{\mathrm{H}})

(13)

and note that

1.

Matrices
\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}
and
\mathrm{j}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}
are Hermitian and skew-Hermitian matrices, respectively
[
11
]
.

2.

For any generic vector
\mathbf{z}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}\mathbf{z}

(14)

where

\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}\mathbf{z}\in\mathds{R}\qquad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{j}\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}\mathbf{z}\in\mathds{R}.

(15)

In particular, it follows from (
15
) that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}\mathbf{z}|^{2}=|\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}\mathbf{z}|^{2}+|\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathrm{j}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}\mathbf{z}|^{2}.

(16)

Hence we can write,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,p}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}\mathbf{x}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,p}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}j\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}\mathbf{x}|^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,p}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}+\zeta\mathbf{I}_{N})\mathbf{x}-\zeta N|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{j}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}+\zeta\mathbf{I}_{N})\mathbf{x}-\zeta N|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,p}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{r}\mathbf{x}-\zeta N|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{i}\mathbf{x}-\zeta N|^{2}

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{r}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}+\zeta\mathbf{I}_{N},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{i}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{j}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}+\zeta\mathbf{I}_{N}

(19)

and
\zeta\in\mathds{R}
is chosen such that

\displaystyle\zeta>-\min\left(\bigcup_{k,p}\left\{\gamma_{\min}\left(\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{r}\right),\gamma_{\min}\left(\mathrm{j}\mathbf{A}_{k,p}^{i}\right)\right\}\right).

(20)

The modification in (
18
)-(
19
) is known as diagonal loading and it ensures the positive definiteness of

\{\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{r}\}
and

\{\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{i}\}
. The objective (
17
) is still quartic w.r.t.
\mathbf{x}
. In order to make it quadratic we resort to the equivalence properties of Hermitian square roots.

.

For the positive definite matrix

III-A

Optimization w.r.t.
\mathbf{x}

The objective function in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is recast as

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}^{H}\left(\sum_{k,p}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{r}+\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{i}\right)\right)\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ -2\sqrt{\zeta N}\operatorname{Re}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{H}\sum_{k,p}(\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{r})^{H/2}\mathbf{u}_{k,p}^{r}\right\}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ -2\sqrt{\zeta N}\operatorname{Re}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{H}\sum_{k,p}(\mathbf{\tilde{A}}_{k,p}^{i})^{H/2}\mathbf{u}_{k,p}^{i}\right\}+\text{const.}

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{x}+2\operatorname{Re}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle\underset{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{\textrm{minimize}}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \bar{\mathbf{x}}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{x}}\bar{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ |x_{n}|=1,\penalty\ \penalty\ n=1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ |x_{n}|=1,\penalty\ \penalty\ n=1,\cdots,N,

V
Summary

In this paper, we addressed the unimodular radar code design for FMCW radar ambiguity function shaping. We devised the radar codes by minimizing a criterion obtained from the absolute value of the ambiguity function in the regions of interest and we addressed the quartic optimization problem using the PMLI iterations. Numerical experiments were conducted to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed method in shaping the ambiguity function.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context and motivation

Hilbert’s tenth problem inquires whether there exists a universal algorithm to determine the solvability of any given Diophantine equation over the integers
\mathbb{Z}
. This problem was resolved negatively by Matijasevich
[
21
]
, who demonstrated that no such algorithm exists. Extending this inquiry to number fields, we can ask whether a procedure can be devised to decide the solvability of Diophantine equations over the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a number field
K
.

It is established that if the ring of rational integers
\mathbb{Z}
can be described as a Diophantine set within
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then the analogue of Hilbert’s tenth problem for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
also has a negative solution. The Denef-Lipshitz conjecture posits that
\mathbb{Z}
is indeed a Diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
for any number field
K
. This conjecture has been validated in several specific instances:

•

When
K
is totally real
[
7
]
.

•

When
K
is a quadratic extension of a totally real number field
[
6
]
.

•

When
K
has exactly one complex place
[
34
]
,
[
25
]
.

•

When
K/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian
[
33
]
.

It is natural to seek to prove the conjecture for families of number fields for which the above results do not apply. Mazur and Rubin
[
22
]
introduced the notion of
diophantine stability
for a variety defined over a number field. By studying this notion for abelian varieties, they were able to prove that Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for certain large families of number fields. Garcia-Fritz and Pasten
[
9
]
confirmed the Denef–Lipshitz conjecture for fields of the form
\mathbb{Q}(p^{1/3},\sqrt{-q})
, with
p
and
q
being primes from specific sets characterized by positive Tchebotarev density. Recently, Kundu, Lei, and Sprung
[
19
]
extended these results, proving the conjecture for a broader family of number fields
\mathbb{Q}(p^{1/3},\sqrt{Dq})
, where
D
ranges over a finite set of integers, and
p
and
q
are primes within explicit sets with positive Tchebotarev density.

The number fields examined in these studies are all of degree less than 6. This raises the question of whether the conjecture can be proven for families of non-abelian number fields with unbounded degrees. Partial progress has been made by the second named author
[
29
]
. Consider a number field
K
and its cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, with
K_{n}
as the intermediate extension of degree
p^{n}
, where
p
is an odd prime. If an elliptic curve
E/K
satisfies certain local conditions at
p
, then
\mathbb{Z}
is a Diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}
for all
n
. Starting from an imaginary quadratic field
K
and examining the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, similar results were obtained by the second named author and Weston for the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K

[
28
]
. This utilized the theory of congruences between Galois representations, and the techniques of Greenberg and Vatsal
[
10
]
, who study the effect of such congruences in Iwasawa theory. This leads to explicit examples, for instance, it is shown that in all finite layers of the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})
, Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer.

1.3.
Organization and methodology

Including the introduction, the article consists of five sections. Section
2
is dedicated to establishing preliminary notions and setting up relevant notation. In this section, we discuss the algebraic structure of Selmer groups over infinite towers of number fields. Moreover, we discuss basic notions related to Hilbert’s tenth problem and the notion of an integrally diophantine extension. A criterion of Shlapentokh (Theorem
2.6
) relates the stability of ranks of elliptic curves to Hilbert’s tenth problem. Given an extension of number fields

, if there is an elliptic curve
E_{/K}
such that

, then,

is an integrally diophantine extension. This is the main tool that we shall use in establishing our results in subsequent sections. Iwasawa theory is used to give explicit conditions for the rank of an elliptic curve to remain constant in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. In section
3
, we discuss the role of the Euler characteristic in Iwasawa theory and its relevance to the study of ranks of elliptic curves. We discuss an explicit formula due to Perrin-Riou and Schneider which is used to calculate Iwasawa invariants of Selmer groups. In section
4
, we prove the main results of this article, namely Theorems
4.12
,
4.13
and
4.14
. Given an imaginary quadratic field

and an odd prime

, we give explicit conditions for there to exist an elliptic curve

with positive rank, such that the rank remains constant in many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

. This construction relies on understanding the structure of the Selmer group over the

-extension of

. Explicit examples are provided at the end of this section, cf. Examples

and
4.16
. In section

4.12

4.13
and
4.14
in families of imaginary quadratic fields. The strategy used here is motivated (in part) by the method of Gajek-Leonard et al.

1.4.
Outlook

Our results illustrate that Iwasawa theory can be employed as a novel tool to establish new cases of the Denef-Lipschitz conjecture. When combined with arithmetic statistics, these techniques give unconditional positive-density families for
p=3
and conditional positive-density families in the Kriz–Li setting described above. The Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves was introduced by Mazur
[
23
]
, whose original motivation was to study the growth of ranks of elliptic curves in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers. We consider a broader application of Iwasawa theory which is in keeping with its traditional scope. One may seek to explore more generalized contexts, specifically those involving
p
-adic Lie extensions of dimension
d>1
. Harris
[
13
]
provided general upper bounds for the asymptotic growth of the rank of the Mordell-Weil group of an abelian variety in a tower of number fields contained within a
p
-adic Lie extension. These upper bounds are crucial as they give a framework for understanding how the rank behaves as we move up through the layers of the extension. Despite this progress, the precise order of growth in Mordell-Weil ranks remains an open question and is not fully understood, as highlighted in
[
14
, Question following Proposition 4.7]
.

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\mathrm{cyc}})\simeq\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}})\oplus\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E^{(K)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}).

are natural numbers (possibly
0
),
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
f_{j}(T)
are distinguished polynomials. The Iwasawa
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants are then defined as follows:

\mu_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\deg f_{j}.

If
s=0
(respectively,
t=0
), the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}
(respectively,
\sum_{j=1}^{t}\deg f_{j}
) is taken to be
0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The module
M
is cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module and
\mu_{p}(M)=0
.

(2)

The module
M
is cofinitely generated as a

Moreover, if the above (equivalent) conditions are satisfied, then,

M\simeq\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)^{\lambda_{p}(M)}\oplus A,

-module.

Proof.

The result is an easy consequence of the structure isomorphism (

∎

It then follows from results of Kato

]

that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})

.

2.2.
Hilbert’s tenth problem for number rings

Let
A
be a commutative ring with an identity element, and consider
A^{n}
, which is the free
A
-module of rank
n
. This module consists of tuples
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
where each
a_{i}
is an element of
A
. Suppose
m
and
n
are positive integers. For
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in A^{m}
, we denote by
(a,b)\in A^{n+m}
the concatenated tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
. Given a finite collection of polynomials
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
, we define the set

\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{b\in A^{m}\mid F_{i}(a,b)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,k\}.

Definition 2.3
.

A subset
S
of
A^{n}
is called a
diophantine subset
of
A^{n}
if there exists an integer
m\geq 1
and a collection of polynomials

F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\in A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]

such that
S
consists of all
a\in A^{n}
for which the set
\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})
is nonempty.

Definition 2.4
.

An extension of number fields
L/K
is said to be
integrally diophantine
if the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a diophantine subset of the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Let
L^{\prime}
,
L
, and
K
be number fields such that

L^{\prime}\supseteq L\supseteq K.

We assume that both
L^{\prime}/L
and
L/K
are integrally diophantine extensions. It is a basic fact in the theory of diophantine sets and fields that, under these conditions, the composite extension
L^{\prime}/K
is also integrally diophantine. This assertion follows from
[
35
, Theorem 2.1.15]
. In fact,
[
35
]
provides a comprehensive treatment of Hilbert’s tenth problem for rings of integers in number fields.

In the context of integrally diophantine extensions, we recall a conjecture proposed by Denef and Lipshitz
[
6
]
.

Conjecture 2.5

(Denef-Lipshitz)
.

For any number field
L
, the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
is integrally diophantine.

This conjecture posits that for any number field
L
, the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Z}
is a diophantine subset of the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. The veracity of this conjecture would imply a significant expansion of the negative resolution of Hilbert’s tenth problem.

The renowned result by Matijasevich, building on work by Davis, Putnam, and Robinson, demonstrated that Hilbert’s tenth problem, which asks whether there is an algorithm to determine the solvability of arbitrary diophantine equations over
\mathbb{Z}
, has a negative answer. Specifically, there is no such algorithm. If Conjecture
2.5
holds for a number field
L
, it would imply that Hilbert’s tenth problem also has a negative answer for the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. In other words, there would be no algorithm to determine the solvability of diophantine equations over
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. In addition to the cases discussed in the introduction, there have been significant advancements in establishing new criteria for the validity of Conjecture
2.5
. Notable contributions have been made by Poonen
[
26
]
, Cornelissen, Pheidas, and Zahidi
[
5
]
, and Shlapentokh
[
36
]
. These researchers have introduced criteria linked to the stability of the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic curves over number fields.

One particularly influential result is Shlapentokh’s criterion, which relates diophantine stability for elliptic curves with positive rank to Hilbert’s tenth problem for rings of integers in number fields:

Theorem 2.6

(Shlapentokh)
.

Let
L/K
be an extension of number fields, and suppose there exists an elliptic curve
E
defined over
K
such that
\operatorname{rank}E(L)=\operatorname{rank}E(K)>0
. Then,
L/K
is integrally diophantine. Consequently, if Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then Hilbert’s tenth problem also has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

This theorem demonstrates that the existence of an elliptic curve
E
over
K
with equal positive rank over both
K
and
L
ensures that the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. This result provides a powerful tool for transferring the negative solution of Hilbert’s tenth problem from
\mathcal{O}_{K}
to
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, thereby expanding the range of number fields for which the problem is known to have no algorithmic solution.

p

3.1.
Mazur’s control theorem

There is a natural map

\alpha_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(n)})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\Gamma_{F_{\operatorname{cyc}},n}},

which fits into the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(n)})}

(Mazur)
.

The maps

We now present a criterion for

If

Proof.

For the proof, see
[

Theorem 3.3

(Kato–Rohrlich)
.

Assume

\mathbb{Q}

. Then,
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})

3.2.
Generalities on the Euler characteristic

In this section, let
\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Let
M
be a
\Lambda
-module that is cofinitely generated and cotorsion. We focus on two derived modules: the module of invariants
H^{0}(\Gamma,M)=M^{\Gamma}
and the module of co-invariants
H^{1}(\Gamma,M)=M_{\Gamma}=M/TM
. There is a natural map between these modules:

\phi_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\Gamma}\rightarrow M_{\Gamma},

sending an element
x\in M^{\Gamma}
to
x\mod{TM}
in
M_{\Gamma}
. Since
M
is cofinitely generated over
\Lambda
, both
M^{\Gamma}
and
M_{\Gamma}
are cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Additionally, since
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
has cohomological dimension 1,
H^{i}(\Gamma,\cdot)=0
for
i\geq 2
. For such a module
M
, the coranks of
M^{\Gamma}
and
M_{\Gamma}
as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules are equal:

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M_{\Gamma},

cf.
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
. A key consequence of this lemma is that
M^{\Gamma}
is finite if and only if
M_{\Gamma}
is finite.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
M
be a
\Lambda
-module that is cofinitely generated and cotorsion. The
Euler characteristic
of
M
is well-defined if
M^{\Gamma}
(and equivalently
M_{\Gamma}
) is finite. In this case, the
Euler characteristic
of
M
is defined as:

\chi(\Gamma,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(H^{i}(\Gamma,M)\right)^{(-1)^{i}}=\left(\frac{\#M^{\Gamma}}{\#M_{\Gamma}}\right).

There is indeed a generalization of the above notion when
M^{\Gamma}
is infinite which we shall treat in the next subsection.
We define the characteristic series of
M
as follows.

Definition 3.5
.

Consider the numbers
n_{i}
and polynomials
f_{j}
in (
2.1
). Then the characteristic series
f_{M}(T)
is defined by:

f_{M}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i}p^{n_{i}}\times\prod_{j}f_{j}(T),

and can be expressed as:

f_{M}(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\dots+a_{\lambda}T^{\lambda}.

Proposition 3.6
.

For a
\Lambda
-module
M
that is cofinitely generated and cotorsion, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is well-defined.

(2)

The constant term
a_{0}
of the characteristic series
f_{M}(T)
is non-zero.

Furthermore, if these conditions are satisfied, then
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is an integer, and it holds that:

a_{0}\sim\chi(\Gamma,M),

where
a\sim b
means
a=ub
for some unit
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

Proof.

For a proof of the result, see
[
27
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module

with a well-defined Euler characteristic, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mu_{p}(M)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(M)=0
,

(2)

is not divisible by

,

(3)

\chi(\Gamma,M)=1
.

Proof.

This result is
[
27
, Lemma 4.5]
.
∎

3.3.
The truncated Euler characteristic

When the cohomology groups
H^{i}(\Gamma,M)
are not finite, a generalized version called the truncated Euler characteristic, denoted
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
, is used. Given that
H^{1}(\Gamma,M)
is isomorphic to the group of coinvariants
H_{0}(\Gamma,M)=M_{\Gamma}
, there exists a natural map

\Phi_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\Gamma}\rightarrow M_{\Gamma}

which sends
x\in M^{\Gamma}
to its residue class in
M_{\Gamma}
. The truncated Euler characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
is defined when both the kernel and the cokernel of
\Phi_{M}
are finite. It is given by the formula

\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\operatorname{ker}(\Phi_{M})}{\#\operatorname{cok}(\Phi_{M})}.

It can be shown that if the classical Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is defined, then
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
is also defined, and in fact,

\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)=\chi(\Gamma,M).

Let
r_{M}
denote the order of vanishing of
f_{M}(T)
at
T=0
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, recall that
a\sim b
if there exists a unit
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that
a=bu
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module. Assume that the kernel and cokernel of
\Phi_{M}
are finite. Then,

(1)

r_{M}=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M^{\Gamma})=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M_{\Gamma})
.

(2)

a_{r_{M}}\neq 0
.

(3)

a_{r_{M}}\sim\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
.

Proof.

For the proof, see
[
37
, Lemma 2.11]
.
∎

In particular, the classical Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is defined if and only if
r_{M}=0
. Next, we give a criterion for the truncated Euler characteristic to be defined.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module. Let
f_{1}(T),\dots,f_{t}(T)
be distinguished polynomials in (
2.1
). Assume that
T^{2}\nmid f_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,t
. Then the truncated Euler characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
is well defined. In particular,
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)
is defined when
r_{M}\leq 1
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from the proof of
[
37
, Lemma 2.11]
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module for which
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M^{\Gamma})=1
. Then, one has that
r_{M}=1
. Moreover, the truncated Euler characteristic is defined and

\chi_{t}(\Gamma,M)\sim a_{1}.

Proof.

The result follows from Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
.
∎

3.4.
A formula for the truncated Euler characteristic

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
F
. Assume that
E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes of
F
that lie above
p
. We set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F)
. Assume that the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated and cotorsion as a
\Lambda_{\operatorname{cyc}}
-module. Note that this is indeed true when
F=\mathbb{Q}
, or if
F
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)
is
1
. Consider the natural map

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\Gamma}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})_{\Gamma}

sending an element
x\in\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\Gamma}
to its residue class
\Phi(x)=\bar{x}\in\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})_{\Gamma}
. It follows from Mazur’s control theorem that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})^{\Gamma_{\operatorname{cyc}}}=1
.
The truncated Euler characteristic
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E[p^{\infty}])
is defined as the following quotient

\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E[p^{\infty}])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\operatorname{ker}\Phi}{\#\operatorname{cok}\Phi}.

Note that by Lemma
3.10
,
\operatorname{ker}\Phi
and
\operatorname{cok}\Phi
are finite, and thus the truncated Euler characteristic is well defined.

Let
f(T)
represent the characteristic element of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})
. Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the order of vanishing of
f(T)
at
T=0
, and express
f(T)
as
T^{r}\cdot g(T)
. According to Lemma
3.10
,
r
is equal to 1. Note that
g(0)\neq 0
. The
p
-adic regulator of
E
over
F
, denoted as
\operatorname{Reg}_{p}(E/F)
, is defined as the determinant of the
p
-adic height pairing (see
[
31
]
). It is conjectured that the
p
-adic regulator is non-zero (see
[
32
]
). If
r_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{rank}E(F)
, let
\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{-r_{E}}\operatorname{Reg}_{p}(E/F)
denote the normalized regulator. Thus
\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)=\operatorname{Reg}_{p}(E/F)/p
in the rank-one situation considered above, while in rank zero we use the usual empty-determinant convention
\operatorname{Reg}_{p}(E/F)=\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)=1
. Given a prime
v
of
K
, denote by
c_{v}(E/F)
the Tamagawa number of
E
over
F_{v}
. Set
\kappa_{v}
to denote the residue field at
v
. Suppose
E
has good reduction at
v
, set
\widetilde{E}(\kappa_{v})
to be the group of
\kappa_{v}
-rational points of the reduction of a regular model of
E
at the prime
v
.

The following result gives the formula for the truncated Euler characteristic of the
p
-primary Selmer group when it is defined.
In the CM case, this was proven by B. Perrin-Riou (see
[
24
]
) and in the general case by P. Schneider (see
[
32
]
).

Theorem 3.11

(Perrin–Riou and Schneider)
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve over a number field
F
and let
p
be an odd prime. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated and cotorsion over
\Lambda_{\operatorname{cyc}}
, and
\operatorname{rank}E(F)\in\{0,1\}
;

(2)

E(F)[p]=0
;

(3)

the
p
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite;

(4)

the
p
-adic height pairing on

is nondegenerate.

Then

\chi_{t}\bigl(\Gamma,\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\bigr)\sim\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)\,\#\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]\,\prod_{v\nmid p}c_{v}(E/F)\left(\prod_{v\mid p}\#\widetilde{E}(\kappa_{v})[p^{\infty}]\right)^{2}.

Here
\sim
denotes equality up to multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. When
\operatorname{rank}E(F)=0
, the truncated Euler characteristic in this formula is the classical Euler characteristic. When
\operatorname{rank}E(F)=1
, it is the rank-one truncated Euler characteristic defined above.

The following result gives a relationship between the Iwasawa invariants and the (truncated) Euler characteristic.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve satisfying the conditions of the above theorem. Assume in addition that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)=1
. Then with respect to notation above, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The Iwasawa invariants of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})
are given by

\mu\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})\right)=0\text{ and }\lambda\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})\right)=1;

(2)

\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E[p^{\infty}])=1
;

(3)

the following are all satisfied:

•

p\nmid\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)
,

•

\#\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]=1
,

•

p\nmid c_{v}(E/F)
for all primes
v\nmid p
of
F
,

•

and
\widetilde{E}(\kappa_{v})[p^{\infty}]=0
for all primes
v|p
of
F
.

Proof.

Recall that
f(T)=Tg(T)
. Thus,
\mu=0
and
\lambda=1
if and only if
g(T)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. On the other hand,
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E[p^{\infty}])\sim g(0)
(cf. Lemma
3.8
, part (3)). Thus,
\mu=0
and
\lambda=1
if and only if
\chi_{t}(\Gamma,E[p^{\infty}])=1
. This proves that (1) and (2) are equivalent. The equivalence of (2) and (3) follows from Theorem
3.11
.
∎

Corollary 3.13

(Rank-zero criterion)
.

Let
E/F
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.11
, and assume that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)=0
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mu\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})\bigr)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda\bigl(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})\bigr)=0;

(2)

\chi\bigl(\Gamma,\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})\bigr)=1;

(3)

\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]=0
,
p\nmid c_{v}(E/F)
for every
v\nmid p
, and
\widetilde{E}(\kappa_{v})[p^{\infty}]=0
for every
v\mid p
.

Proof.

Since
\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite, the Selmer exact sequence shows that
E(F)
has rank zero. Mazur’s control theorem therefore makes the
\Gamma
-invariants of
\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F_{\operatorname{cyc}})
finite, so its classical Euler characteristic is defined. The equivalence of (1) and (2) is Lemma
3.7
. Theorem
3.11
, together with
\mathcal{R}_{p}(E/F)=1
in rank zero, gives the equivalence of (2) and (3).
∎

4.
Hilbert’s tenth problem in towers of number fields

In this section, we apply ideas from Iwasawa theory to prove Hilbert’s tenth problem in the finite layers of various
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of interest. Our results require that there exist elliptic curves for which certain additional conditions are satisfied. We shall present examples to illustrate our results. The main results of this section are Theorems
4.12
,
4.13
and
4.14
. In the next section, we verify these conditions unconditionally for a positive-density family when

. For the families obtained from Kriz–Li, the required vanishing of the
p
-primary Tate–Shafarevich group of the rank-zero twists is retained as an explicit density hypothesis.

As in the previous section, fix an elliptic curve

(2)

Let

Consider as before the power series
\omega_{a,b}
(but this time we do not assume that

,

We first consider the case where

, we can assume that

is coprime to

as well. As in the proof of Theorem

\omega_{a,b}(g(Y),Y)=0
and we find that

is divisible by

-b/a
is a

\begin{split}&h_{a,b}(Y)=F(g(Y),Y)=a_{0,0}+(-a_{1,0}b/a+a_{0,1})Y+O(Y^{2});\\
&h(Y)=F(0,Y)=a_{0,0}+a_{0,1}Y+O(Y^{2}).\end{split}

As
\mu_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})=1

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve,
p
an odd prime and
K
be an imaginary quadratic field. Assume that the following conditions hold

(1)

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})=1

(2)

and

(3)

\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{p})[p]=0
and
\widetilde{E}^{(K)}(\mathbb{F}_{p})[p]=0

(4)

;

(5)

the normalized
p
-adic regulator of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
is a
p
-adic unit;

(6)

\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]=0
and
\Sh(E^{(K)}/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]=0
.

Let
(a_{0},b_{0})
be a representative for the line excluded in Theorem
4.13
.
Then, for all
(a,b)\not\equiv(a_{0},b_{0})\pmod{p}
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{a,b})
is a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda_{a,b}
-module,

(2)

\mu_{p}(E/K_{a,b})=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E/K_{a,b})=1
.

(3)

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the Conjecture
2.5
is satisfied for
K_{a,b}^{(n)}
. In particular, Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
K_{a,b}^{(n)}
.

Proof.

Note that the Selmer groups
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
and
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E^{(K)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
are cofinitely generated and cotorsion over
\Lambda_{\operatorname{cyc}}
. The kernel of the reduction map

E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

consists of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-points on the formal group of
E
at
p
, and is in particular torsion free. Condition (3) thus implies in particular that
E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})[p]=0
and
E^{(K)}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})[p]=0
. Thus, we find that
E(\mathbb{Q})[p]=0
and
E^{(K)}(\mathbb{Q})[p]=0

We apply the two Euler-characteristic criteria separately. For
E/\mathbb{Q}
, condition (1) is the rank-one Selmer-corank hypothesis, while conditions (2)–(6) give all the remaining hypotheses of Corollary
3.12
. Hence

\mu_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=1.

For

, condition (1) gives Selmer corank zero. Its

-adic height pairing is therefore vacuously nondegenerate, and conditions (2), (3), (4), and (6) give the other hypotheses of the rank-zero criterion, Corollary
3.13
. Consequently,

\mu_{p}(E^{(K)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{p}(E^{(K)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0.

From the discussion in the above lines, it follows that

\mu_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0\text{ and }\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})=1.

The assertions thus follow from Theorem
4.13
.
∎

5.
Density results

In this section, we prove the unconditional

(1)

;

(2)

(3)

the prime

These conditions imply that

and moreover, the Iwasawa invariants are given by

3.12
. As

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be the number of distinct prime factors dividing

. Let

\mathfrak{d}(S)=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}

We begin by considering the case when

, where the

are the prime factors of

. As all the primes dividing

are split in

, we see that

is coprime to

, that proposition gives a proportion at least

\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{r+m}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid N\\
\ell\neq 3\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{\ell}}{\ell+1}

of negative fundamental discriminants for which
E^{(d)}
has analytic rank zero. If
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction at
3
, the corresponding lower bound for negative rank-zero twists is

\frac{1}{2^{r+m+2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid N\\
\ell\neq 3\end{subarray}}\frac{q_{\ell}}{\ell+1}.

Together with the additional condition at
p
, these are precisely the density bounds stated above. Denote the resulting family by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and put
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
. Since
E^{(d)}
has analytic rank zero, the theorem of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin implies that
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})
has rank zero and
\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
is finite. Consequently,

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=0.

Moreover,
\psi_{d}(p)=1
means that
p
splits in
K
. Hence
d
is a square in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
, so
E
and
E^{(d)}
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Thus
E^{(d)}
has good ordinary reduction at
p
and is non-anomalous at
p
.

It remains to check the Tamagawa condition. If
\ell\mid N
and
\ell\neq 3
, the local conditions in
[
17
, Theorem 9.5]
ensure that
E^{(d)}
has non-split multiplicative reduction at
\ell
, and hence its Tamagawa number is at most
2
. At a prime dividing
d
but not
N
, the twist has additive reduction and its Tamagawa number is at most
4
. At
3
, if
3\mid N
, the possibilities occurring in
[
17
, Proposition 9.7]
give either the original multiplicative Tamagawa factor, a non-split multiplicative factor of order at most
2
, or an additive factor of order at most
4
. Since
p>3
and the original Tamagawa product is prime to
p
, it follows that

p\nmid\prod_{\ell\neq p}c_{\ell}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}).

Thus the pair
(E,E^{(d)})
satisfies conditions (1)–(5) of Theorem
4.14
. If
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{\Sh}
, the fixed-curve hypothesis in (1) above and the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{\Sh}
give condition (6) as well. Theorem
4.14
therefore proves (
1.1
) for every such
d
. The final density assertion follows immediately from the definition of
\eta_{E,p}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}})
.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

The explicit expression in Theorem
5.7
is the density of the underlying Kriz–Li family. The lower density of the subfamily for which (
1.1
) is proved is obtained by multiplying it by
\eta_{E,p}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}})
.

Example 5.9
.

Consider the curve with Cremona label 209a1 and
p=5
. The rank-one Euler-characteristic criterion verifies
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[5^{\infty}]=0
from the corresponding Iwasawa and local data. Thus
(E,p)
satisfies the conditions of the above theorem. The positive-density conclusion for this example remains conditional on
\eta_{E,5}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}})>0
.

5.2.
Density results for
p=3

In this section, we fix the prime
p=3
and an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with conductor
N_{E}
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

E
admits a
3
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
,

(2)

E
has good ordinary reduction at
3
,

(3)

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}_{3^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})=1
,

(4)

\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{3})[3]=0
;

(5)

3\nmid\prod_{\ell\neq 3}c_{\ell}(E/\mathbb{Q})
;

(6)

the normalized
3
-adic regulator of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
is a
3
-adic unit;

(7)

\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[3^{\infty}]=0
.

We wish to apply the results of Bhargava-Klagsburn-Oliver-Schnidman
[
2
]
to prove that there is a positive density of negative squarefree numbers
d
for which that following conditions hold for
E^{(d)}

(1)

\operatorname{Sel}_{3^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=0
;

(2)

E^{(d)}
has good ordinary reduction at
3
;

(3)

3
is non-anomalous for
E^{(d)}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{E}^{(d)}(\mathbb{F}_{3})[3]=0
;

(4)

3\nmid\prod_{\ell\neq 3}c_{\ell}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
.

Let
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow\widehat{E}
be a
3
-isogeny defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that this induces a
3
-isogeny
\phi_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{(d)}\rightarrow\widehat{E}^{(d)}
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\ell
be a prime, define the local Selmer ratio as follows

c_{\ell}(\phi_{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\dfrac{\left|\widehat{E}^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})/\phi\left(E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\right)\right|}{|E^{(d)}[\phi](\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})|}.

The
global Selmer ratio
is defined as the product of all local Selmer ratios

c(\phi_{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\ell}c_{\ell}(\phi_{d}).

We recall some of the results of
[
2
]
. A subset
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
is said to be determined by local conditions if it can be expressed as
\Sigma=\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}\cap\prod_{\ell}\Sigma_{\ell}
, where each
\Sigma_{\ell}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{*2}
representing a local condition. If
\Sigma_{\ell}=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{*2}
for all but a finite number of places
\ell
, we then say that
\Sigma
is defined by finitely many local conditions. The height of an element
d\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
is defined as
H(d)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|d|
, and for
X>0
, set
\Sigma(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{d\in\Sigma\mid|d|<X\}
.
Define, for each
m\geq 0
, the subset

T_{m}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{d\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}\mid|t(\phi_{d})|=m\},

where
t(\phi_{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{ord}_{3}\,c(\phi_{d})
, and let
\mu(T_{m}(\phi))
denote the density of
T_{m}(\phi)
within
\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
. The set
T_{m}(\phi)
is defined by local conditions at the primes
\ell
that divide
6N\infty
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
admitting a
3
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to\widehat{E}
. Let
\Sigma
be a subset of
T_{0}(\phi)
defined by finitely many local conditions. Then the proportion of twists
E^{(d)}
for which
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E^{(d)})=0
is at least
\frac{1}{2}\mu(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

The above result follows from
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
and its proof.
∎

We recall results in
loc. cit.
, which provide a description for the local Selmer ratios
c_{\ell}(\phi)
. We start with
\ell=\infty
.

Proposition 5.12
.

With respect to notation above, we have that

c_{\infty}(\phi_{d})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}&E^{(d)}[\phi](\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z};\\
1&E^{(d)}[\phi](\mathbb{R})=0.\end{cases}

Proposition 5.13
.

For
\ell\notin\{3,\infty\}
we have that
c_{\ell}(\phi_{d})=c_{\ell}(\widehat{E}^{(d)})/c_{\ell}(E^{(d)})
.

Proof.

This result is
[
2
, Corollary 10.3]
.
∎

We now come to the main application of this section.

Theorem 5.14
.

There is unconditionally a positive density of negative squarefree integers such that
(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}),3)
satisfies (
1.1
).

Proof.

The strategy is to show that there is an explicit elliptic curve with
3
-isogeny
\phi
for which the set
T_{0}(\phi)
has positive density. The values of
d
shall range in a certain subset of
T_{0}(\phi)
subject to conditions at
3
and
\infty
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be the elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+216x-54
(LMFDB label
1216.o3
) and set
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=3
. There is a cyclic
3
-isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow\widehat{E}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The Iwasawa invariants are given by

\mu_{3}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{3}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=1.

Since
E
has analytic rank
1
, the Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin theorem implies that
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})
is finite. The normalized
3
-adic regulator is a unit, so the
3
-adic height pairing is nondegenerate; moreover, the non-anomalous condition implies
E(\mathbb{Q})[3]=0
. Corollary
3.12
therefore applies, and the displayed Iwasawa invariants imply that

\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[3^{\infty}]=0.

We find that the twist
E^{(-2)}
(LMFDB label
304.f3
) has
\mu_{3}(E^{(-2)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
and
\lambda_{3}(E^{(-2)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0
. The Tamagawa product for all curves in the isogeny class
304.f
is
1
. It follows from Proposition
5.13
that
c_{\ell}(\phi_{-2})=1
for
\ell\notin\{3,\infty\}
. We find that
c(\phi_{-2})=c_{3}(\phi_{-2})c_{\infty}(\phi_{-2})
. According to Proposition
5.12
,
c_{\infty}(\phi_{-2})\in\{1,1/3\}
. On the other hand, it follows from
[
2
, Theorem 10.5]
that
c_{3}(\phi_{-2})\in\{1,3\}
. Therefore,
c(\phi_{-2})\in\{1/3,1,3\}
. On the other hand, it follows from
[
1
, Prop. 42(ii)]
that

t(\phi_{-2})\equiv\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E^{(-2)}/\mathbb{Q})=0\pmod{2}.

Thus we deduce that
c(\phi_{-2})=1
, i.e.,
-2\in T_{0}(\phi)
.

We now introduce local conditions at
3
and
\infty
. Consider the subset
T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
of
T_{0}(\phi)
consisting of those
d
for which
d\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
d<0
. The set
T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
is nonempty since
-2\in T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
. The set
T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
is defined by finitely many congruence conditions (cf.
[
2
]
) and is nonempty. This implies that
T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
has positive density. Set

\mathcal{D}_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{d\in T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Sel}_{3}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=0\}.

By Theorem
5.11
,
\mathcal{D}_{3}
has positive density. For every
d\in\mathcal{D}_{3}
, the Kummer exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})/3E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}_{3}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})[3]\longrightarrow 0

shows that
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})
has rank zero and that
\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})[3]=0
. Since every nonzero
3
-primary torsion group contains an element of order
3
, it follows that
\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})[3^{\infty}]=0
, and consequently
\mathrm{Sel}_{3^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=0
.

For
d\in T_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
, the prime
3
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
because
d\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. Since
\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{3})[3]=0
, we have
\widetilde{E}^{(d)}(\mathbb{F}_{3})[3]=0
. For every prime
\ell\neq 3
, the Kodaira type of
E^{(d)}
is
I_{0}
,
I_{0}^{*}
,
I_{1}
, or
I_{1}^{*}
(cf.
[
2
, Table 3]
); in particular,

3\nmid\prod_{\ell\neq 3}c_{\ell}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}).

Moreover,
E
and
E^{(d)}
have the same reduction type at
3
, so
E^{(d)}
has good ordinary reduction there. Thus all six hypotheses of Theorem
4.14
hold for every
d\in\mathcal{D}_{3}
, and

\mu_{3}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})_{\operatorname{cyc}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{3}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})_{\operatorname{cyc}})=1.

Theorems
4.12
and
4.13
therefore show that
(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}),3)
satisfies (
1.1
) for every
d\in\mathcal{D}_{3}
. This proves the assertion unconditionally.
∎

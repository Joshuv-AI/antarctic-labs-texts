Hölder regularity for mixed local and nonlocal p p -Laplace parabolic equations Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Bin Shang and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We give a unified proof of Hölder regularity of weak solutions for mixed local and nonlocal p p -Laplace type parabolic equations with the full range of exponents 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . Our proof is based on the expansion of positivity together with the energy estimate and De Giorgi type lemma.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the regularity results for the following mixed parabolic problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta_{p}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0\quad\text{in }E_{T},\quad 1<p<\infty,

(1.1)

where
E_{T}:=E\times(0,T)
with
T>0
and
E
being an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The local
p
-Laplace operator is given by

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u:=-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

The nonlocal
p
-Laplace operator is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy,

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{P.V.}
denotes the Cauchy principle value. The kernel
K
satisfies that
K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)
and

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\leq K(x,y,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

(1.3)

with
\Lambda\geq 1
and
0<s<1
for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in(0,T)
. This kind of problems, which were extensively applied in plasma physics
[
4
]
and biology
[
19
]
, arise from the mixture of classical random walk and Lévy flight.

Before giving our main contribution, let us review some known results. In the local setting, the Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the usual
p
-Laplace equation

\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0

(1.4)

was proved by DiBenedetto
[
13
]
for
p>2
and by Chen-DiBenedetto
[
9
,
10
]
for
1<p<2
. A comprehensive treatment of this issue goes back to the monograph
[
14
]
in which the intrinsic scaling method plays a central role. Roughly speaking, the main idea of this method is to make the solutions of equation (
1.4
) behave like the solutions of heat equation over the intrinsic parabolic cylinders. It is worth mentioning that a more simple approach so-called expansion of positivity was introduced by DiBenedetto-Gianazza in
[
16
]
and by DiBenedetto-Gianazza-Vespri in the monograph
[
17
]
to access the theory of regularity for quasilinear parabolic equations. Based on expansion of positivity, Liao
[
26
]
derived the Hölder continuity of locally bounded solutions to parabolic equations with structures modeled after the parabolic
p
-Laplace equation and the porous medium equation. We refer the readers to
[
1
,
5
,
15
,
29
,
28
,
31
]
and references therein for more related results.

For the nonlocal
p
-Laplace equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0,

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{L}
is defined as in (
1.2
), Strömqvist
[
30
]
showed the existence and boundedness of weak solutions for the case
p>2
with the kernel
K(x,y,t)
satisfying (
1.3
). In view of iterated discrete differentiation of the equation in the spirit of Moser’s technique, Hölder estimates with specific exponents of weak solutions in the case
p>2

was established by Brasco-Lindgren-Strömqvist in
[
6
]
. Regarding the inhomogeneous case of (
1.5
), Ding-Zhang-Zhou
[
18
]
proved the local boundedness (
1<p<\infty
) and Hölder regularity (
2<p<\infty
) for the local weak solutions by using the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration. Very recently, the Hölder regularity of weak solutions of (
1.5
) in the full range
1<p<\infty
was investigated by Liao
[
27
]
without using any logarithmic estimate and any comparison principle. At the same time, Adimurthi-Prasad-Tewary
[
2
]
obtained the same result by employing the expansion of positivity and provided a new form of isoperimetric inequality. More results can be found in
[
7
,
8
,
25
,
21
]
.

In recent years, the mixed local and nonlocal problems have attracted increasing interest. For the special scenario

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=0,

(1.6)

Foondun
[
22
]
obtained the Harnack inequality and Hölder continuity by probabilistic methods, see also
[
11
]
for an another argument. Some qualitative and quantitative properties such as the maximum principle, interior Sobolev regularity and boundary regularity were developed by Biagi-Dipierro-Valdinoci-Vecchi in
[
3
]
, where the authors utilized difference quotient and constructed barriers appropriately. Concerning the nonlinear framework of (
1.6
)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u=0,

Garain-Kinnunen
[
23
]
first studied the regularity properties of weak solutions incorporating local boundedness, Hölder continuity and Harnack estimates along with lower semicontinuity of weak supersolutions. When it comes to the parabolic analogue of (
1.6
), Garain-Kinnunen
[
24
]
worked with sign changing supersolution and investigated the Harnack inequality with a tail term. A priori Hölder estimate, parabolic Harnack principle and heat kernel estimates were established in
[
12
]
. Furthermore, Fang-Shang-Zhang
[
20
]
proved the local boundedness and Hölder continuity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) for
1<p<\infty
and
2<p<\infty
, respectively. We remark that, for the technical reason, it is necessary to impose the assumption that
p>2
in order to obtain the Logarithmic type estimate. As a consequence, this restriction prevents an extension of Hölder continuity in
[
18
,
20
]
to the subquadratic case
1<p<2
.

The present paper is a continuation of our previous work
[
20
]
to derive the Hölder continuity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) for the subquadratic case that
1<p<2
. Motivated by the ideas developed in a series of papers
[
16
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
, we will adopt the methods of expansion of positivity with strong geometric characteristics to obtain the regularity results of weak solutions in the full range of exponents
1<p<\infty
. We employ the Caccioppoli type inequality, De Giorgi’s iteration without using Logarithmic type estimates and exponential change of variables. To overcome the difficulties arising from the nonlocal operator, the tail term (see (
1.7
) below) and the information of solutions will appear via an either-or form. We will estimate the tail term carefully so that it can be controlled by the local oscillation, which allows us to utilize the measure information and then to get the reduction of oscillation. Notice that our results also hold for mixed local and nonlocal
p
-Laplace parabolic equations with more general structure conditions.

Before stating the notations of weak solutions to (
1.1
), we introduce the tail space

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{q}}{1+|x|^{N+\alpha}}\,dx<+\infty\right\},\quad q>0\text{ and }\alpha>0.

Define the nonlocal tail by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(v;x_{o},R;t_{o}-S,t_{o}):=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{o}-S<t<t_{o}}\left(R^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{o})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.7)

If
x_{o}=0
, we denote tail as
\mathrm{Tail}(v;R;t_{o}-S,t_{o})
.
It is obvious that
\mathrm{Tail}(v;x_{o},R;t_{o}-S,t_{o})
is well-defined for any
v\in L^{\infty}(t_{o}-S,t_{o};L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

Next, we present the definition of weak solutions to (
1.1
) as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u
is a weak subsolution (super-) to equation (
1.1
) if
u\in L^{p}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;W_{\rm{loc}}^{1,p}(E))\cap C_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L_{\rm{loc}}^{2}(E))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for any closed interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq(0,T)
and any compact subset
K\subset E
, there holds that

\displaystyle\int_{K}u(x,t_{2})\zeta(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{K}u(x,t_{1})\zeta(x,t_{1})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{K}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\zeta(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{K}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u,\zeta,t)\,dt\leq(\geq)0,

(1.8)

for every nonnegative test function
\zeta\in L^{p}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(K))\cap W^{1,2}_{\rm{loc}}(0,T;L^{2}(K))
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\zeta,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle\Big[|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))

\displaystyle\times(\zeta(x,t)-\zeta(y,t))K(x,y,t)\Big]\,dxdy.

A function
u
is a local weak solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
u
is a local weak subsolution and supersolution.

We now are in position to state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that
u
is a locally bounded, local weak solution to equation (
1.1
) in
E_{T}
. Then
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
E_{T}
. More precisely, there exist constants
\gamma>1
and
\beta\in(0,1)
depending a prior on
N,p,s,\Lambda
such that for any
0<\rho<R<\widetilde{R}\leq 1
, the cylinders
(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\rho}(\omega^{2-p}):=B_{\rho}(x_{o})\times(t_{o}-\omega^{2-p}\rho^{p},t_{o})\subset(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\widetilde{R}}:=B_{\widetilde{R}}\times(t_{o}-\widetilde{R}^{p},t_{o})
are included in
E_{T}
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,osc}_{(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\rho}(\omega^{2-p})}u\leq\gamma\omega\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\beta},

where

\displaystyle\omega=2\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\widetilde{R}}}|u|+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{o},\widetilde{R};t_{o}-\widetilde{R}^{p},t_{o}).

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.2
, we assume that the local weak solution
u
is locally bounded. In fact, the local boundedness of weak solutions has been proved in our previous paper
[
20
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
. We also stress that Theorem
1.2
still holds for more general jumping kernel
K(x,y,t)
such as

\displaystyle K(x,y,t):=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}p}}\textbf{{\rm 1}}_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}\textbf{{\rm 1}}_{\{|x-y|>1\}},

where
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we fix basic notations and present several auxiliary lemmas. Section
3
is devoted to establishing some lemmas which are crucial to obtain the Hölder regularity. We will give the proof of Theorem
1.2
with
1<p\leq 2
in Section
4
. Finally, the other case
p>2
of Theorem
1.2
will be proved in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce some notations and then give several useful lemmas.

2.1.
Notation

For convenience, we give some notations to be used later. Let
B_{\rho}(y)
be the open ball centered at
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with radius
\rho>0
. For fixed
(x_{o},t_{o})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define the forward and backward parabolic cylinders as

(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\rho}^{+}(\theta)=B_{\rho}(x_{o})\times\left(t_{o},t_{o}+\theta\rho^{p}\right]

and

(x_{o},t_{o})+Q_{\rho}^{-}(\theta)=B_{\rho}(x_{o})\times\left(t_{o}-\theta\rho^{p},t_{o}\right].

For simplicity, we will omit
(x_{o},t_{o})
from the definitions above. We also omit
\theta
when
\theta=1
. In addition, define the backward parabolic cylinders

(x_{o},t_{o})+Q(R,S)=B_{R}(x_{o})\times\left(t_{o}-S,t_{o}\right].

Set

\displaystyle d\mu=d\mu(x,y,t)=K(x,y,t)\,dxdy.

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\displaystyle(u-k)_{+}=\max\{u-k,0\}\quad\textmd{and}\quad(u-k)_{-}=\max\{-(u-k),0\}.

Throughout the paper, we always assume that
0<\rho<R<\widetilde{R}\leq 1
.

2.2.
Auxiliary lemmas

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 2.3,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then for every

\displaystyle u\in L^{p}\left(t_{1},t_{2};W^{1,p}(B_{\rho})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(B_{\rho})\right),

it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\rho^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}},

where
C>0
only depends on
p
and
N
.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 4.1,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\{Y_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq Kb^{j}Y_{j}^{1+\delta},\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots

for some constants
K
,
b>1
and
\delta>0
. If

\displaystyle Y_{o}\leq K^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}b^{-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}},

then we have
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}Y_{j}=0
.

3.
crucial results

In this section, several preliminary results will be discussed. We first present the following Caccioppoli type estimate for weak subsolutions of (
1.1
) and the supersolution case is similar.

Proposition 3.1

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)
.

Assume that
p>1
and
u
is a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
). Let cylinders
Q(R,S)\subset E_{T}
. For any piecewise smooth cutoff functions
\zeta(x,t)\in[0,1]
vanishing on
\partial B_{\rho}
and
\zeta(x,t)=0
for
t<t_{o}-S
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\,\sup}_{t_{o}-s<t<t_{o}}\int_{B_{R}}w_{+}^{2}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dx-\int_{B_{R}}w_{+}^{2}\zeta^{p}(x,t_{o}-S)\,dx+\iint_{Q(R,S)}|\nabla w_{+}(x,t)|^{p}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{o}-S}^{t_{o}}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\min\{\zeta(x,t),\zeta(y,t)\}^{p}\frac{|w_{+}(x,t)-w_{+}(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdyd\tau

\displaystyle+\iint_{Q(R,S)}w_{+}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\int_{B_{R}}\frac{w_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dydxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\iint_{Q(R,S)}w_{+}^{p}(x,t)|\nabla\zeta(x,t)|^{p}+w_{+}^{2}(x,t)|\partial_{t}\zeta^{p}|\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{t_{o}-S}^{t_{o}}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\max\{w_{+}(x,t),w_{+}(y,t)\}^{p}\frac{|\zeta(x,t)-\zeta(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdyd\tau

\displaystyle+\gamma\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\,\sup}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{o}-S<t<t_{o}}}{{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot,t)}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\iint_{Q(R,S)}w_{+}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dxd\tau,

(3.1)

where
w_{+}=(u-k)_{+}
with
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>0
depends only on
N,p,s,\Lambda
.

Remark 3.2
.

The Caccioppoli inequality above can be obtained by using the method of Lemma 3.1 in our previous paper
[
20
]
. However, we follow the estimates (2.1)–(2.2) in
[
27
]
here to get the additional term

\iint_{Q(R,S)}w_{+}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\int_{B_{R}}\frac{w_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dydxd\tau.

From now on, we always denote
\mathcal{Q}=B_{R}(x_{o})\times(T_{1},T_{2}]\subset E_{T}
. The number
\mu^{\pm}
and
\omega
satisfy

\displaystyle\mu^{+}\geq\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{\mathcal{Q}}u,\quad\mu^{-}\leq\operatorname*{ess\,\inf}_{\mathcal{Q}}u,\quad\omega\geq\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}.

We now prove a De Giorgi type lemma for weak supersolutions. Notice that the tail will appear in terms of either-or in order to control the nonlocal terms.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
u
is a locally bounded, local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) in
E_{T}
. For parameters
\delta,\xi\in(0,1)
, let
\theta=\delta(\xi\omega)^{2-p}
and
Q_{\rho}^{-}(\theta)\subset\mathcal{Q}
. There exists a constant
\nu\in(0,1)
depending on
N,p,s,\Lambda
and
\delta
such that if

\displaystyle\left|[u-\mu_{-}\leq\xi\omega]\cap Q_{\rho}^{-}(\theta)\right|\leq\nu|Q_{\rho}^{-}(\theta)|,

(3.2)

then either

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}\left((u-\mu^{-})_{-};x_{o},R;T_{1},T_{2}\right)>\xi\omega,

or

\displaystyle u-\mu^{-}\geq\frac{1}{2}\xi\omega\quad\text{a.e. in }Q_{\frac{1}{2}\rho}^{-}(\theta).

Proof.

We can assume
(x_{o},t_{o})=(0,0)
and
\mu^{-}=0
. Let

\displaystyle\rho_{j}=\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2^{j}},\quad\tilde{\rho}_{j}=\frac{\rho_{j}+\rho_{j+1}}{2},\quad B_{j}=B_{\rho_{j}},\quad\widetilde{B}_{j}=B_{\tilde{\rho}_{j}},\quad j=0,1,2\ldots.

Denote the cylinders

\displaystyle Q_{j}=B_{j}\times\left(-\theta\rho_{j}^{p},0\right],\quad\widetilde{Q}_{j}=\widetilde{B}_{j}\times\left(-\theta\tilde{\rho}_{j}^{p},0\right],\quad j=0,1,2\ldots.

Let the level

\displaystyle k_{j}=\frac{\xi\omega}{2}+\frac{\xi\omega}{2^{j+1}}.

We apply the energy estimate (
3.1
) for function
(u-k_{j})_{-}
over the cylinder
Q_{j}
. Choose the cutoff function
\zeta(x,t)=\zeta_{1}(x)\zeta_{2}(t)
in
Q_{j}
satisfying that

\displaystyle 0\leq\zeta_{1}(x)\leq 1,\quad|\nabla\zeta_{1}(x)|\leq\frac{2^{j+1}}{\rho},\quad\zeta_{1}(x)=1\text{ in }B_{j+1},\quad\zeta_{1}(x)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\widetilde{B}_{j}

and

\displaystyle 0\leq\zeta_{2}(t)\leq 1,\quad 0\leq\zeta_{2,t}\leq\frac{2^{p(j+1)}}{\theta\rho^{p}},\quad\zeta_{2}(t)=1\text{ for }t\geq-\theta\rho_{j+1}^{p},\quad\zeta_{2}(t)=0\text{ for }t<-\theta\tilde{\rho}_{j}^{p}.

Then it yields that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{-\theta\rho^{p}_{j}<t<0}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dx+\iint_{Q_{j}}|\nabla(u-k_{j})_{-}\zeta(x,t)|^{p}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{-\theta\rho_{j}^{p}}^{0}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}|(u-k_{j})_{-}\zeta(x,t)-(u-k_{j})_{-}\zeta(y,t)|^{p}\,d\mu d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\rho^{p}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{p}\,dxd\tau+\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\theta\rho^{p}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\int_{-\theta\rho_{j}^{p}}^{0}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(\max\{(u-k_{j})_{-}(x,t),(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)\})^{p}|\zeta(x,t)-\zeta(y,t)|^{p}\,d\mu d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\,\sup}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\theta\rho_{j}^{p}<t<0}}{{(x,t)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\zeta}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{j}}\frac{(u-k_{j})_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

Using the definition of
k_{j}
, we estimate
I_{1}
as

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\rho^{p}}\iint_{Q_{j}}\left(u-k_{j}\right)_{-}^{p}\chi_{\{u(x,t)<k_{j}\}}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\rho^{p}}(\xi\omega)^{p}\left|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}\right|.

For the terms
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, it holds that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\theta\rho^{p}}(\xi\omega)^{2}\left|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}\right|

and

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq\gamma\frac{2^{jp}}{\rho^{p}}(\xi\omega)^{p}\left|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}\right|.

For every
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\zeta_{1}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{j}
, notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x-y|}=\frac{1}{|y|}\frac{|x-(x-y)|}{|x-y|}\leq\frac{1+2^{j+3}}{|y|}\leq\frac{2^{j+4}}{|y|}.

If we enforce

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}\left(u_{-};R;T_{1},T_{2}\right)\leq\xi\omega,

the term
I_{4}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma 2^{j(N+sp)}\xi\omega|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|\left(\gamma 2^{spj}\frac{(\xi\omega)^{p-1}}{\rho^{sp}}+\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\,\sup}_{-\theta\rho_{j}^{p}<t<0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma 2^{j(N+sp)}\frac{\xi\omega}{\rho^{p}}|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|\left(\gamma 2^{spj}(\xi\omega)^{p-1}+\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{p}[\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};R;T_{1},T_{2})]^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma 2^{j(N+2sp)}\frac{(\xi\omega)^{p}}{\rho^{p}}|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|.

Combining the estimates
I_{1}
–
I_{4}
and discarding the positive term on the left-hand side yields that

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{-\theta\rho^{p}_{j+1}<t<0}\int_{B_{j+1}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}\,dx+\iint_{Q_{j+1}}|\nabla(u-k_{j})_{-}|^{p}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\frac{2^{j(N+2sp+p)}}{\delta\rho^{p}}(\xi\omega)^{p}|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|.

According to the Sobolev inequality in Lemma
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\quad\iint_{Q_{j+1}}[\left(u-k_{j}\right)_{-}]^{p\frac{N+2}{N}}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\iint_{Q_{j+1}}\left|\nabla[(u-k_{j})_{-}]\right|^{p}\,dxd\tau\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{-\theta\rho^{p}_{j}<t<0}\int_{B_{j+1}}[(u-k_{j})_{-}]^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left[\frac{2^{j(N+2sp+p)}}{\delta\rho^{p}}(\xi\omega)^{p}\right]^{\frac{N+p}{N}}|[u<k_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|^{\frac{N+p}{N}}.

On the other hand,

A new generalization of Browder’s degree
Mohammad Niksirat

Abstract
. A new generalization of the Browder’s degree for the mappings of the type ( S ) + (S)_{+} is presented. The main idea is rooted in the observation that the Browder’s degree remains unchanged for the mappings of the form A : Y → X ∗ A:Y\to X^{*} , where Y Y is a reflexive uniformly convex Banach space continuously embedded in the Banach space X X . The advantage of the suggested degree lies in the simplicity it provides for the calculations of degree associated to nonlinear operators. An application from the theory of phase transition in liquid crystals is presented for which the suggested degree has been successfully applied.

X
, the continuous pairing between
X^{*}

X

X

\langle,\rangle

A:X\rightarrow X^{\ast}
is called

(S)_{+}

(u_{n})\subset X
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
, the
inequality

(1.1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0,

imply
u_{n}\to u
; see e.g.
[
6
,
1
]
. It is well known ( see for example
[

]

(1.2)

A[u]:=\sum_{\alpha\leq m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(x,u,\dots,D^{m}u),

from
X=W_{0}^{m,p}(\Omega)
to

is
(S)_{+}
where the pairing
\langle A[u],\phi\rangle
is defined by the relation

(1.3)

\langle A[u],\phi\rangle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x,u,\dots,D^{m}u)D^{\alpha}\phi.

The existence and the multiplicity problems of the quasi-linear elliptic equations in divergence form can be studied by the degree of the operator
A
at zero.

A degree theory, keeping all classical properties of a topological degree, for the bounded demi-continuous
(S)_{+}
maps in Hilbert spaces is developed by I. Skrypnik
[
15
]
. F. Browder generalized the degree to the mapping in uniformly convex reflexive Banach spaces
[
6
,
5
]
. Browder’s construction is based on the direct generalization of the classical Brouwer degree through the Galerkin type approximation of
(S)_{+}
mappings. An alternative construction through generalizing the Leray-Schauder degree has been

[
2

]
. A new generalization of the Browder’s degree theory from the Nagumo degree is reported by A. Kartsatos and D. Kerr

.

A turning point in the application of degree theory is made by Y.Y. Li
[
11
]
, where the author uses the Fitzpatrick’s degree of quasi-linear Fredholm maps to define a degree for fully nonlinear second order elliptic equations. A remarkable progress is reported by I. Skrypnik
[

, showing that every fully nonlinear uniformly
elliptic equation (satisfying some growth rate conditions) is represented by an operator equation involving
(S)_{+}
mapping. This formulation opens up a way to study the well-posedness problem of such equations by topological degree argument.

Nevertheless, in all constructions of degree, some type of continuity (and in the weakest case, the demi-continuity) is required and can not be relaxed. A map
A:X\to X^{*}
is called demi-continuous if the strong convergence
x_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{\to}}x
implies the weak convergence
Ax_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X^{*}}}{{\rightharpoonup}}Ax
. However, this assumption fails for some real applications. Here we give an example from the phase transition in liquid crystals. It is shown that (see
[
13
,
12
]
), the stationary solution of the Doi-Onsager equation

(1.4)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\Delta_{r}f+{\rm div}(f\nabla_{r}U(f)),

for the interaction potential function
U(r)

(1.5)

U(f)(r)=\lambda\int_{S^{2}}|r\times r^{\prime}|f(r^{\prime})d\sigma(r^{\prime}),

and the probability density function
f(r)
of the directions of the rod-like molecules

(1.6)

f(r)=\left(\int_{S^{2}}e^{-U(f)}d\sigma\right)^{-1}e^{-U(f)(r)},

reduces to the fixed point problem
u-\lambda\Gamma[u]=0
, where

(1.7)

\Gamma[u](r):=\left(\int_{S^{2}}e^{-u(r)}\right)^{-1}\int_{S^{2}}\left(|r\times r^{\prime}|-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)e^{-u(r^{\prime})}d\sigma(r^{\prime}).

The natural function space for the problem is

(1.8)

H_{0}(S^{2})=\{u\in L^{2}(S^{2}),u(-r)=u(r),\int_{S^{2}}{u(r)d\sigma(r)}=0\}.

It is simply seen that
\Gamma
fails to be demi-continuous in any open neighbourhood of
0\in H_{0}(S^{2})
. Fix
r\in S^{2}
, and let
u_{n}
be the following sequence

(1.9)

u_{n}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log(2\pi(1-\cos(1/n)))&\cos^{-1}(r.\bar{r})\in\left(0,\frac{1}{n}\right)\\
0&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Obviously,
u_{n}\xrightarrow{H_{0}(S^{2})}0
, while

\lim_{n}\Gamma[u_{n}]=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{2\pi(1-\cos(1/n))}\int_{0}^{1/n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\gamma)d\sigma\neq G(0)=0.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Niksirat
[
13
]
)
.

Fix
\lambda
and let
\Omega_{\lambda}
be the following set

\Omega_{\lambda}=\{u\in H_{0}(S^{2}),|u(r)|\leq\lambda\}.

The map
\Gamma:\Omega_{\lambda}\subset H_{0}(S^{2})\to H_{0}(S^{2})
is continuous and compact.

By the above theorem, the map
\Gamma:W^{k,2}(S^{2})\cap H_{0}(S^{2})\to H_{0}(S^{2})
for

is continuous due to the compact embedding of
W^{k,2}
into the space of continuous functions. For the problem defined on the unit circle, the author
[

\mathbb{I}-\lambda G:W^{k,2}(S^{1})\cap H_{0}(S^{1})\to\left(W^{k,2}(S^{1})\cap H_{0}(S^{1})\right)^{*}.

For higher dimensional problems, the calculations are intricate and lengthy. In this paper, we construct a new degree theory for the mapping

where
X
is a
Banach space,
Y

X

(S)_{+}
mappings.

2.
Definition of the degree

Let
A:Y\to X^{*}
be a map where
X
is a Banach spaces and
Y
is a separable reflexive Banach space continuously embedded
in
X
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
Y
is a reflexive separable locally uniformly convex space due to the Kadec-Klee theorem (see
[
9
]
Theorem 8.1).
On the other hand, if
Y
is a separable locally uniformly Banach space, the Browder-Ton embedding theorem, see
[
4
,
3
]
, implies the existence of a Hilbert spacee
H
such that the embedding
j:H\hookrightarrow Y
is dense and compact. Choosing a basis
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots\}
for
H
, we can define the set
\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots\}
where
y_{k}=j(h_{k})
, and accordingly, the filtration
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots\}
for
Y
where
Y_{n}={\rm span}\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
A:Y\to X^{*}
, the finite rank approximation
A_{n}:Y\rightarrow Y_{n}
is defined by the relation

(2.1)

A_{n}(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle A[u],i(y_{k})\rangle y_{k},

where
\langle,\rangle
denotes the continuous pairing between
X^{\ast}
and

X
and
i:Y\to X
denotes the continuous embedding of
Y
into

X
. We should notice that the above approximation is completely different from the finite rank approximation of the map
i^{*}A:Y\to Y^{*}
.

The inner product
(,)
in
Y_{n}
is uniquely defined by the relation
(y_{i},y_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. It is simply verified that
A_{n}
and
A
coincide on
Y_{n}
in the following sense

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle A[u],i(v)\rangle=(A_{n}(u),v),\hskip 11.38092pt\forall v\in Y_{n}.

Let
W
be a subspace of
X
. We write
A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W}}{{=}}0
if
\langle A[u],i(v)\rangle=0
for all
v\in W
. In sequel, we assume that
\Omega\subset Y
is an open and bounded set, and
A:\Omega\to X^{*}
is bounded, demi-continuous and
(S)_{+}
in the following sense:

•

A
is bounded if
A[\Omega]
is bounded in
X^{\ast}
.

•

A
is demi-continuous if
u_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{\to}}u
, then
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X^{*}}}{{\rightharpoonup}}A[u]
. In our setting, the latter weak convergence reads

\langle A[u_{n}],i(y)\rangle\rightarrow\langle A[u],i(y)\rangle
for all
y\in Y
. Note that this notion is weaker than the
demi-continuity of a map from
X
to
X^{*}
.

•

A
is
(S)_{+}
if for every sequence
(u_{n})\subset Y
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
, the inequality

(2.3)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],i(u_{n}-u)\rangle\leq 0,

imply
u_{n}\rightarrow u
.

We further assume that
A
satisfies the following condition
(H)
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle(H):\{u\in Y;A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{=}}0\}=\{u\in Y;A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
D\subset\bar{\Omega}
be a closed set. If there exists a
sequence
(u_{n})\subset D\cap Y_{n}
such that
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
,
then the equation
A[u]=0\in X^{*}
is solvable in
D
. In
particular, if
u_{n}\in\partial\Omega
and
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
then
there is
u\in\partial\Omega
such that
A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})\subset D
be a sequence and
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
. Since
D
is bounded, there exists
u\in Y
and a subsequence of
(u_{n})

(that we still denote it by
u_{n}
) such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
. Take an arbitrary
sequence
(\zeta_{n})
,
\zeta_{n}\in Y_{n}
such that
\zeta_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{\longrightarrow}}u
. By the relation
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
and the boundedness property of
A
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],i(u_{n}-u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],i(\zeta_{n}-u)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|A[u_{n}]\|_{X^{*}}\|\zeta_{n}-u\|_{Y}=0.

Since
A
is of class
(S)_{+}
on
\Omega
, the inequality (
2.5
) and the closedness of
D
imply
u_{n}\to u\in D
.
By the demi-continuity of
A
on
Y
we conclude
A[u_{n}]\rightharpoonup A[u]
. Now let
y\in Y
be arbitrary. Take a sequence
(\xi_{n})
,
\xi_{n}\in Y_{n}
such that
\xi_{n}\xrightarrow{Y}y
. We have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\langle A[u],i(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],i(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle A[u_{n}],i(y-\xi_{n})\rangle

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|A[u_{n}]\|_{X^{*}}\|y-\xi_{n}\|_{Y}=0.

Replacing
y
by
-y
implies
\langle A[u_{n}],i(y)\rangle\geq 0
and thus
A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{=}}0
. Finally the condition
(H)
implies
A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0
.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Assume that
0\not\in A[\partial\Omega]
. The index of
A
in
\Omega
at
0\in X^{\ast}
is defined by the relation

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\rm ind}(A,\Omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\deg_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{n},0),

where
\Omega_{n}=\Omega\cap Y_{n}
and
\deg_{B}
denotes the Brouwer degree.

Remark 2.4
.

The index of
A
is different from the Browder degree of the map

A:Y\rightarrow Y^{*}
even if
Y
is a subspace of

X
. For example, let
X
be a uniformly convex Banach space
X=Y\oplus\{u\}
and
J:X\to X^{*}
the duality map. Consider the map

A:Y\to X^{*}
,
\langle A[x],y+tu\rangle=\langle J(x),y\rangle+t
. It is simply seen that the Browder degree of the map
A:Y\to Y^{*}
equals

1
but the index of the map
A:Y\to X^{*}
is
0
by the definition (
2.3
). We first justify the definition (
2.3
).

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
0\not\in A[\partial\Omega]
. The Brouwer degree of
A_{n}
in

\Omega_{n}
is stable, that is, there exists
N_{0}>0
such that for
n\geq N_{0}
we have

(2.9)

\deg_{B}(A_{n-1},\Omega_{n-1},0)=\deg_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{n},0).

Proof.

We first show that there is
N_{0}>0
such that
0\notin A_{n}(\partial\Omega_{n})
for all
n\geq N_{0}
. Assuming the contrary, let there exist a sequence

u_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{n}
such that
A[u_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
.
As
\Omega
is open in
Y
, we have

u_{n}\subseteq\partial\Omega
. An argument similar to the one employed in the proof of the lemma (
2.2
), implies the
existence of
u\in\partial\Omega
such that
A[u]=0
, a contradiction!
Hence, there is
N_{0}>0
such that the Brouwer degree of
A_{n}
in
\Omega_{n}

is well defined at
0
for
n\geq N_{0}
. Consider the map
B_{n}:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow Y_{n}
defined by

(2.10)

\displaystyle B_{n}(u):=(A_{n-1}(u),Pr_{n}(u)),

where
Pr_{n}(u)
denotes the
n
-th component of
u
in
Y_{n}
. Apparently, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\deg_{B}(A_{n-1},\Omega_{n-1},0)=\deg_{B}(B_{n},\Omega_{n},0).

Note that the proof ends once we show that for
n
large enough there holds

\displaystyle\deg_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{n},0)=\deg_{B}(B_{n},\Omega_{n},0).

Consider the following convex homotopy
h(t)
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle h_{n}(t)=(1-t)A_{n}+tB_{n}.

It suffices to show that
h_{n}(t)(u)\neq 0
for all
u\in\partial\Omega_{n}
and for all

t\in[0,1]
when
n
is sufficiently large.
Assume the contrary. Then there exists
z_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{n}
and
t_{n}\in[0,1]
such that
h(t_{n})(z_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
. By the definition of finite rank approximation (
2.1
),
we can write
A_{n}(z_{n})
as

A_{n}(z_{n})=\left(A_{n-1}(z_{n}),\langle A[z_{n}],i(y_{n})\rangle y_{n}\right),

where
\{y_{1},y_{2},..\}
is a frame for
Y
. Now, the relation
h(t_{n})(z_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
implies that

A[z_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n-1}}}{{=}}0
and

(2.13)

(1-t_{n})\langle A[z_{n}],i(y_{n})\rangle y_{n}+t_{n}Pr_{n}(z_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0.

But
A[z_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n-1}}}{{=}}0
implies
A_{n}(z_{n})=r_{n}y_{n}
for
some
r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. This together with (
2.13
) gives

r_{n}=\frac{-t_{n}}{1-t_{n}}z_{n,n},
where
z_{n,n}
is the component of
z_{n}
along
y_{n}
, that is,
Pr_{n}(z_{n})=z_{n,n}y_{n}
. Therefore, we have

(2.14)

\langle A[z_{n}],i(z_{n})\rangle=(A_{n}(z_{n}),z_{n})=-\frac{t_{n}}{1-t_{n}}z_{n,n}^{2}\leq 0.

Since
\partial\Omega
is bounded and
(z_{n})\in\partial\Omega
, there is a subsequence
(z_{n_{k}})
such that

z_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z\in Y
. To keep notations simple, we denote this subsequence by
(z_{n})
. Let
\zeta_{n}\in Y_{n-1}
be a sequence such that
\zeta_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{\to}}z
. We have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\langle A[z_{n}],i(z_{n}-z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle A[z_{n}],i(z_{n}-\zeta_{n})\rangle+\langle A[z_{n}],i(\zeta_{n}-z)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle A[z_{n}],i(z_{n})\rangle+\|A[z_{n}]\|\|\zeta_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A[z_{n}]\|\|\zeta_{n}-z\|.

The last equality follows from (
2.14
). Therefore

(2.16)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\langle A[z_{n}],i(z_{n}-z)\rangle\leq 0,

and since
A
is
(S)_{+}
, we conclude
z_{n}\to z\in\partial\Omega
. Finally, following the lemma (
2.2
), we reach
A[z]=0
,
a contradiction!

3.
Properties of the invariant

We show that the topological invariant defined in the definition (
2.3
) satisfies the classical
properties of a topological degree. First we identify the admissible class of
homotopy.

Definition 3.1

(Admissible homotopy)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
Y
. A one parameter family of maps
h:[0,1]\times\Omega\to X^{*}
is called an admissible homotopy if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

h:[0,1]\times\Omega\rightarrow X^{\ast}
is bounded and
demi-continuous,

(2)

0\not\in h([0,1]\times\partial\Omega)
,

(3)

for every sequence
t_{n}\rightarrow t
,
t_{n}\in[0,1]
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
for
u_{n}\in\bar{\Omega}
, the inequality

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle h(t_{n})(u_{n}),i(u_{n}-u)\rangle\leq 0,

implies
u_{n}\to u
,

(4)

for all
t\in[0,1]
we have

H(t):\{z\in Y;h(t)(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{=}}0\}=\{z\in Y;h(t)(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0\}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A:\Omega\rightarrow X^{\ast}
be a bounded,
demi-continuous,
(S)_{+}
mapping that satisfies the condition
(H)
and furthermore,

0\not\in A[\partial\Omega]
. The index of
A:\Omega\subset Y\to X^{*}
given in the definition(
2.3
) has the following properties:

(1)

the equation
A[u]=0
is solvable in
\Omega
if
{\rm ind}(A,\Omega)\neq 0
,

(2)

if
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
where
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are disjoint open sets
then

{\rm ind}(A,\Omega)={\rm ind}(A,\Omega_{1})+{\rm ind}(A,\Omega_{2}),

(3)

If
h:[0,1]\times\Omega\rightarrow X^{\ast}
is an admissible
homotopy then
{\rm ind}(h(t),\Omega)
is independent of
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

To prove (1), let us assume
{\rm ind}(A,\Omega)=1
. Therefore, there is
N
such that
{\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{n},0)=1
for all
n\geq N
. By the properties of Brouwer degree, the equation
A_{n}(u)=0\in Y_{n}
is solvable in
\Omega_{n}
. Let
(u_{n})\subset\overline{\Omega}_{n}
be a sequence such that
A_{n}(u_{n})=0
. Now lemma (
2.2
) implies the existence of some

u\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
such that
A[u]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0
. Since
0\not\in A[\partial\Omega]
, we
conclude
u\in\Omega
. The second property also follows directly from the domain decomposition of the Brouwer degree. In fact, if
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
then for sufficiently large
n
we have

{\rm ind}(A,\Omega)={\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{1,n}\cup\Omega_{2,n},0),

where
\Omega_{1,n}=\Omega_{1}\cap Y_{n}
and
\Omega_{2,n}=\Omega_{2}\cap Y_{n}
. By the domain decomposition of the Brouwer degree, we have

{\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{1,n}\cup\Omega_{2,n},0)={\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{1,n},0)+{\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{2,n},0).

Now the claim is proved due to the relation

{\rm deg}_{B}(A_{n},\Omega_{k,n},0)={\rm ind}(A,\Omega_{k}),k=1,2.

To prove the homotopy invariance property of the defined index, assume

(3.1)

{\rm ind}(h(t),\Omega)\neq{\rm ind}(h(s),\Omega),

for some
s,t\in[0,1]
. Let
n
be so large such that the following relations hold

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\rm ind}(h(t),\Omega)=\deg_{B}(h_{n}(t),\Omega_{n},0),

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\rm ind}(h(s),\Omega)=\deg_{B}(h_{n}(s),\Omega_{n},0),

Therefore, there exist
\tau_{n}\in[s,t]
and
\zeta_{n}\in\partial\Omega

such that
h(\tau_{n})(\zeta_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y_{n}}}{{=}}0
. Since
(\zeta_{n})
is
bounded, by an argument similar to the proof of the lemma (
2.2
), we
derive the inequality

(3.4)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle h(\tau_{n})(\zeta_{n}),i(\zeta_{n}-\zeta)\rangle\leq 0,

for some
\zeta\in Y
. The condition
(3)
in the
definition (
3.1
) implies
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta\in\partial\Omega
. Therefore,
h(\tau)(\zeta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Y}}{{=}}0
for some
\tau
, a limit point of the sequence

(\tau_{n})\subset[s,t]
. The condition
H(t)

implies
h(\tau)(\zeta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle X}}{{=}}0
which contradicts
the condition
(2)
of the definition (
3.1
). Therefore there exist
N_{0}>0
such
that

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\rm ind}(h_{n}(t),\Omega)={\rm ind}(h_{n}(s),\Omega),\forall n\geq N_{0}.

and thus the index is independent of the homotopies in the class of admissible homotopy defined in the definition (
3.1
).
∎

4.
The stationary solutions of Doi-Onsager equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Here we study the one dimensional Doi-Onsager equation, and employ the suggested degree in this article. As we have shown earlier in
[
12
]
, this problem is reduced to the fixed point problem

(4.1)

A[u]:=u-\lambda\Gamma[u]

where

(4.2)

\Gamma[u]=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-u(\theta)}d⁣\theta\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})e^{-u(\theta^{\prime})}d⁣\theta^{\prime}.

The kernel
\hat{K}
is assumed to be in
W^{1,\infty}[0,2\pi])
having the following expansion

(4.3)

\hat{K}(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{k_{n}\cos(2n\theta)},

where
k_{n}<0
for all
n
and they satisfy the condition

k_{1}<k_{2}<k_{3}<\cdots.

It is obviously seen that the Onsager’s kernel
\hat{K}(\theta)=|\sin(\theta)|-\frac{2}{\pi}
satisfies the above assumptions. Let
Y
be the space

Y=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}([0,2\pi]),u(\theta)=u(\pi+\theta)a.e.,u(\theta)=u(2\pi-\theta)a.e.,\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\theta)d⁣\theta=0\right\}.

It is seen that
A:Y\to Y
is a continuous map in the class of
(S)_{+}
mappings. In order to simplify calculations for the degree argument, we employ the constructed degree above and study the map
A:Y\to X

X
is the Hilbert space

X=\left\{u\in L^{2}([0,2\pi]),u(\theta)=u(\pi+\theta)a.e.,u(\theta)=u(2\pi-\theta)a.e.,\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(\theta)d⁣\theta=0\right\}.

Theorem 4.1

(Niksirat
[
12
]
)
.

Assume that
\hat{K}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,2\pi])
. Then
\Gamma:Y\to Y
is continuous and compact.

The following proposition guarantees that all the solutions of
A[u]=0
are in the space
Y
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
u\in X
and
A[u]=0
, then
u\in Y
.

Proof.

We have

(4.4)

u(\theta)=\lambda\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-u(\theta)}d⁣\theta\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})e^{-u(\theta^{\prime})}d\theta^{\prime}.

We have

(4.5)

||\frac{d}{d\theta}u||^{2}=\lambda^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-u(\theta)}d⁣\theta\right)^{-2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d}{d\theta}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})e^{-u(\theta^{\prime})}d\theta^{\prime}\right]^{2}d\theta.

Since
\hat{K}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,2\pi])
, then

(4.6)

||\frac{d}{d\theta}u||\leq 2\pi\lambda||\frac{d}{d\theta}\hat{K}||_{\infty}<\infty

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For the map
A:Y\to Y^{*}
, let the finite rank approximation
\tilde{A}_{n}:Y\to Y_{n}
be defined by the relation

(4.7)

\tilde{A}_{n}(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\langle A[u],y_{k}\rangle_{Y}y_{k}},

and let
\Omega\subset Y
be an open bounded subset. If
0\not\in A(\partial\Omega)
, then we have the following relation

(4.8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\deg(\tilde{A}_{n},\Omega_{n},0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\deg(A_{n},\Omega_{n},0),

where
A_{n}
is the finite rank approximation of
A:Y\to X
defined in Def.(
2.1
), and
\Omega_{n}:=\Omega\cap Y_{n}
.

Proof.

Otherwise there is a sequence
z_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{n}
, and a sequence
t_{n}\in(0,1)
such that

t_{n}\tilde{A}_{n}(z_{n})+(1-t_{n})A_{n}(z_{n})=0.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, there is a subsequence (we still denote as
z_{n}
) such that
z_{n}\rightharpoonup z
.
For any
y\in Y_{n}
we have

\langle A[z_{n}],y\rangle_{Y}=-\frac{1-t_{n}}{t_{n}}\langle A[z_{n}],y\rangle_{X}.

Since
Y\hookrightarrow C([0,2\pi])
is a compact embedding, then there
is a subsequence (we still denote as
z_{n}
) such that
z_{n}\xrightarrow{X}z
,
and thus

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[z_{n}],y\rangle_{Y}=-\frac{1-\tau}{\tau}\langle A[z],y\rangle_{X},

for some
\tau\in[0,1]
. Let
\zeta_{n}\in Y_{n}
such that
\zeta_{n}\xrightarrow{Y}z
, and thus

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[z_{n}],z_{n}-z\rangle_{Y}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[z_{n}],z_{n}-\zeta_{n}\rangle_{Y}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-\tau}{\tau}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[z_{n}],z_{n}-z\rangle_{X}\leq 0.

On the other hand,
\Gamma
is a compact mapping and therefore

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A[z_{n}],z_{n}-z\rangle_{Y}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle z_{n},z_{n}-z\rangle_{Y}\leq 0,

that implies
z_{n}\xrightarrow{Y}z\in\partial\Omega
.
But for
y_{n}=\cos(2n\theta)
we have

\langle A[z],y_{n}\rangle_{Y}=(1+4n^{2})\langle A[z],y_{n}\rangle_{X}

that holds only if
A[z]=0
and thus
A[z]=0
. Since
z\in\partial\Omega
, this is a contradiction.
∎

Now we state the main result of the solution of the equation
A[u]=0
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The equation
u-\lambda\Gamma[u]=0
has the unique trivial solution
u\equiv 0
for

0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}:=||\hat{K}||_{\infty}^{-1},

and two solutions bifurcate from the trivial solution at
\lambda_{n}:=-\frac{2}{k_{n}}
for
n\geq 1
. The trivial solution is stable for
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, and is unstable for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
.

For the proof of theorem, we need the following Grüss type of inequality that the reader can see
[
8
]
for a proof.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that
d\mu
is a probability measure and
f,g\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
, then

\left|\int_{\Omega}fd\mu\int gd\mu-\int fgd\mu\right|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\lambda<\|\hat{K}\|_{\infty}^{-1}
, then all solutions
to the equation
A[u]=0
are isolated.

Proof.

According to the Fredholm alternative theorem it is enough to show that
\ker(Id-\lambda D\Gamma)[u]=\{0\}
. We calculate the Jacobian matrix
D\Gamma
. A straightforward
calculation gives the following formula for the Gâteaux derivative

(4.9)

D\Gamma[u](v)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}v(\theta)d\mu_{u}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})d\mu_{u}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})v(\theta^{\prime})d\mu_{u},

where
d\mu_{u}
denotes the probability measure

d\mu(\theta)=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-u(\theta)}d\theta\right)^{-1}e^{-u(\theta)}.

Assume that
v=\lambda D\Gamma[u](v)
. By lemma (
4.5
), we
obtain

|v|=\lambda\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}v(\theta)d\mu_{u}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})d\mu_{u}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})v(\theta^{\prime})d\mu_{u}\right|\leq\lambda\|\hat{K}\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{\infty},

and thus
v=0
.
∎

(of theorem
4.4
)
The apriori estimate of the set containing the solution of
A[u]=0
is

(4.10)

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda\|\hat{K}\|_{\infty},

and thus there is an open set
\Omega\subset Y
such that for each
\lambda
,
there is no solution to
A[u]=0
on
\Omega^{c}
. Therefore, by the homotopy
invariance property of degree for the convex homotopy
h_{t}=Id-t\lambda\Gamma
, we have

\deg({Id}-\lambda\Gamma,\Omega,0)=\deg({Id},\Omega,0)=1.

Now, we calculate the Jacobian matrix of the map
\Gamma
in the basis

\left\{\phi_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cos(2n\theta)\right\}
, that is,
a_{nm}(u):=\langle D\Gamma[u](\phi_{n}),\phi_{m}\rangle
. According to (
4.9
), we derive

a_{nm}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi_{n}(\theta)d\mu\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\phi_{m}(\theta)d\mu d\theta-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{K}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\phi_{n}(\theta^{\prime})\phi_{m}(\theta)d\mu d\theta,

and thus by Lemma (
4.5
) we obtain
|a_{nm}|\leq|k_{m}|
.
On the other hand, since the solutions for
\lambda<\|\hat{K}\|_{\infty}^{-1}

4.6
), the index of any

{\rm ind}(u,0)={\rm sign}\det({Id}-\lambda(a_{nm}))=1,

|a_{nm}|<|k_{m}|

\lambda<\|\hat{K}\|_{\infty}^{-1}
. Since the trivial solution

A

\det({Id}-\lambda A_{0})

\lambda_{n}=2k_{n}^{-1}

F(\lambda,u):=u-\lambda D\Gamma[0](u)+G(\lambda,u)=0,

. It is simply seen

Id-\lambda D\Gamma[0]

Introduction

The possible torsion groups of an elliptic curve over the rational numbers are known by Mazur’s theorem
[
Maz77
]
. These groups are

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}_{n},&1\leq n\leq 12,\ n\neq 11\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2n},&1\leq n\leq 4\end{array}

The work of Mazur was generalized by Kenku, Momose and Kamienny to quadratic fields.

Theorem

(Kamienny
[

]

[

Let K be a quadratic number field and
E/K
an elliptic curve. Then

is isomorphic to one of the followings:

(2)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}_{n},&1\leq n\leq 18,\ n\neq 17\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2n},&1\leq n\leq 6\\
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{3n},&1\leq n\leq 2\\
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{4}&.\end{array}

Over cubic fields, the exact analog of Mazur’s theorem has been established last year
[
DEvH+21
]
. Although it is not known which groups can appear as torsion groups of elliptic curves over quartic number fields, Jeon, Kim and Park
[
JKP06
]
determined which groups can appear for infinitely many non-isomorphic curves.
It was also shown by Derickx, Kamienny, Stein and Stoll
[

]
that

is the largest prime dividing

over any quartic field

This work is a first step towards classifying the growth of torsion in quadratic extensions of base fields larger than

Proposition 2.2

Suppose

The next lemma plays a key role in the proof of the main theorem. It shows that the growth of odd-torsion can be understood with torsion arising over base field.

Lemma 1
.

(
[
GJT14
]
,Cor 4)
If n is an odd positive integer we have

E(K(\sqrt{d}))[n]\simeq E(K)[n]\oplus E^{d}(K)[n]

An immediate corollary of the combination of these results follows.

Corollary 1
.

Let E be an elliptic curve defined over a quadratic number field
K
with trivial 2-torsion. Then
E(K(\sqrt{d}))_{\text{tor}}
is of odd order for all non-squares
d\in K
and with only finitely many exceptions,

E(K(\sqrt{d}))_{\text{tor}}=E(K)_{\text{tor}}

Proof.

Suppose
E/K
and
d
satisfy the hypothesis. Since
E(K)_{\text{tor}}\subseteq E(K(\sqrt{d}))_{\text{tor}},
let us assume
E(K)_{\text{tor}}\subsetneq E(K(\sqrt{d}))_{\text{tor}}.
Note that the claim where
E(K(\sqrt{d}))[2]=\mathbb{Z}_{1}
follows immediately from the observation in Remark
1
. By Lemma
1
,
E(K(\sqrt{d}))_{\text{tor}}
is strictly larger than
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
only if
E^{d}(K)[n]
is non-trivial for some odd number
n.
From Proposition
2.2
, we know that there is only a finite number of quadratic twists of
E
with a non-trivial odd torsion, which therefore yields finitely many quadratic extensions of
K
in which
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
grows as claimed.
∎

Remark 2
.

Corollary
1
can in fact be generalized to any given elliptic curve over a quadratic field. See Corollary 1 in
[

]
.

Corollary
1
particularly shows that the list of odd-order torsion groups given in Sarma and Saika’s classification must also appear as torsion group over a quadratic extension of base field. Thus a large number of the torsion structures that we search for are known.

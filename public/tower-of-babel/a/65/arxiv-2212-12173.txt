Production of massive W ± W^{\pm} bosons and fermion-antifermion pairs from vacuum in the de Sitter Universe Thanks: cosmin.crucean@e-uvt.ro, amalia.fodor97@e-uvt.ro
Cosmin Crucean, Amalia Dariana Fodor

Abstract
In this paper we study the charged electro-weak interactions in the de Sitter geometry. We develop the reduction formalism for the Proca field with the help of the solutions for the interacting fields. Perturbation theory is used for obtaining the definition of the transition amplitudes in the first order. We apply our formalism to the study of spontaneous vacuum emission of fermions, anti-fermions and W ± W^{\pm} bosons in the expanding de Sitter universe. Our results are generalizations of the Weinberg-Salam electro-weak theory to curved space-time, in the case of W ± W^{\pm} boson interaction with leptons. The probability and transition amplitude are found to be a quantities which depend on the Hubble parameter. Our analytical and graphical results prove that such perturbative processes are possible only for the large expansion conditions of the early Universe. The total probability and rate of transition are obtained for the case of large expansion and we use dimensional regularization and Pauli-Villars regularization for the momenta integrals. In the end we recover the Minkowski limit, where our probabilities vanish, thus confirming the well known fact that spontaneous vacuum emission of W ± W^{\pm} bosons and fermions is forbidden in flat space-time due to energy-momentum conservation.

W^{-}

The charged massive bosons

Subsequently these bosons decay into more light and stable particles, such as electrons and neutrinos. It is also well established that, in the very early stages of universe, the temperatures and densities were high enough to allow these bosons to exist in large numbers at thermal equilibrium

The perturbative approach allows one to obtain the dependence of the transition rates on the Hubble parameter and explore the Minkowski limit, where processes which involve the spontaneous emission of particles from vacuum are forbidden. In this paper we explore the vacuum generation of massive
W^{\pm}
bosons due to space-time expansion in the de Sitter geometry.

In the limiting case of large expansion, the ratios between particle masses and expansion factor become very small and it is possible to write

Taking into account that the neutrino polarization can take only the value
\sigma=-\frac{1}{2}
, the final result for the amplitude reads as:

\displaystyle A_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2P}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})}{(p+p^{\prime}+P)}sgn(\sigma^{\prime})\,\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p}\,)\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1).

The probability per unit volume takes the form:

\displaystyle P(\lambda=\pm 1)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\lambda\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{g^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})}{P(p+p^{\prime}+P)^{2}}\,|\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p}\,)\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)|^{2}.

We set up both the neutrino and the positron to be emitted in the direction of the third axis, such that the angle between the momenta vectors is

\vec{p}=p\,\vec{e}_{3}\,,\,\vec{p}\,^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\,\vec{e}_{3}

0,\,\pm 1
. The momentum of the
W^{-}
boson is considered to also be on the third axis such that
\vec{P}=-P\,\vec{e}_{3}
. Since the momenta are on fixed directions, the helicity bispinors are very simple and no longer depend on the momenta. This will facilitate our computations of the momenta integrals:

\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{p}\,)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix};\eta_{\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{p}\,)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-1\end{pmatrix};\eta_{-\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{p}\,)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\

(122)

In this particular case the bispinor and polarization vector summation gives:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\sigma^{\prime}}|\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p}\,)\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\sigma^{\prime}}\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p}\,)\sigma^{i}{\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1){\epsilon}^{*}_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\sigma^{j}\xi_{-\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{p}\,),

which reduces to unity if we use the above equations for spinors and the following relations:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon_{i}^{*}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\,\lambda)\epsilon_{j}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\,\lambda)=\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})=I_{2\times 2}.

(125)

We denote the momenta integrals by

and solve the integral with regards to

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}P\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\,\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})}{(2\pi)^{3}P(p^{\prime}+p+P)^{2}}

(126)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}p}{4(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{1}{(p^{\prime}+p)^{3}},

keeping in mind that
|\vec{p}\,^{\prime}+\vec{p}|=p^{\prime}+p
, because the particles move in the same direction and the momenta are aligned with
\vec{e}_{3}

Because our remaining integrals are divergent, we make use of dimensional regularization, a method used for studying the properties of propagators in the de Sitter geometry
[

,

The

[

,

\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}(p^{\prime}+p)^{3}}\rightarrow I(D)=\int\frac{d^{D}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{D}(p^{\prime}+p)^{3}},

(127)

Considering the case when the variable is changed as
p^{\prime}=py
, the above integral changes into:

\displaystyle I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}p^{D-3}}{(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy\,y^{D-1}}{(1+y)^{3}}.

(128)

This is just the integral of the Beta Euler function, defined bellow
[
22

as:

B(\alpha,\gamma)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\gamma)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\frac{y^{\alpha-1}}{(1+y)^{\alpha+\gamma}}.

(129)

which for
\alpha=D,\,\beta=3-D
becomes:

I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}p^{D-3}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{\Gamma(D)\Gamma(3-D)}{\Gamma(3)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}.

(130)

The above result contains ultraviolet divergences for
D\geq 3
and infrared divergences for
D\leq 0
. These divergences are contained in the poles of the gamma Euler functions. The poles of the gamma functions can be extracted by introducing an arbitrary mass parameter denoted by
\mu
, and an arbitrary coupling dimensionless constant denoted by
\gamma=\lambda\mu^{D-3}=\lambda\mu^{-\varepsilon}
.

We can then write the result of integral (

) as a function of
\gamma
and
\varepsilon
for

I(D)_{r}=\lambda\int\frac{d^{D}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{1}{(p^{\prime}+p)^{3}}=\frac{\gamma}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{p^{2}}4\pi\right)^{\epsilon/2}\frac{\Gamma(3-\epsilon)\Gamma(\epsilon-1)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3-\epsilon}{2}\right)}.

(131)

The series expansion in terms of
\epsilon
of the last two factors gives:

\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}4\pi\right)^{\epsilon/2}=1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{p^{2}}4\pi\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(132)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(3-\epsilon)\Gamma(\epsilon)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3-\epsilon}{2}\right)}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\epsilon}+\frac{2(\psi(3/2)-3)}{\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon\,(5\pi^{2}+48-36\psi(3/2)+6\psi^{2}(3/2))}{12\sqrt{\pi}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(133)

where
\psi(a)
are the digamma Euler functions defined in Appendix.

Collecting the above expansions we obtain the final result for the regularized integral in the case of small momenta

\displaystyle I_{r}(D)=\frac{\gamma}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\biggl\{\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\epsilon}+\frac{2\left(\psi(3/2)+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)-3\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,\left[\frac{5\pi^{2}+48-36\psi(3/2)+6\psi^{2}(3/2)}{12\sqrt{\pi}}+\frac{(\psi(3/2)-3)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\biggl\}.

The result is finite for arbitrary small values of
\epsilon
, but becomes infinite for
\epsilon=0
. In order to replace the term corresponding to the
1/\epsilon
divergence, we will solve the integral with respect to
p^{\prime}
by using the Pauli-Villars regularization method
[
33
]
. At the end of the computations we will find the exact substitute for the term proportional with
1/\epsilon
from the previous expression.

Let us write the integral with
p\sim M_{W}

\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}}=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp^{\prime}\,p^{\prime 2}}{(2\pi)^{3}(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}},

(135)

and expand the integrand

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}}+\frac{a}{(p^{\prime}+\Lambda)^{3}}

(136)

\Lambda
is a bosonic mass parameter and

will be determined as function of

The integrand can be rewritten in terms of a common denominator:

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\prime 3}+\Lambda^{3}+3p^{\prime}\Lambda^{2}+3p^{\prime 2}\Lambda+a(p^{\prime 3}+M_{W}^{3}+3M_{W}p^{\prime 2}+3M_{W}^{2}p^{\prime})}{(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}(p^{\prime}+\Lambda)^{3}}.

(137)

The ultra-violet divergences can be removed from the integral by demanding that terms proportional with
\,p^{\prime 3}
vanish. This gives rise to the following condition:

\displaystyle 1+a=0,

(138)

And we obtain
a=-1
, which cancel the terms in
p^{\prime 3}
.

The final form of the integral is:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\frac{p^{\prime 2}(\Lambda^{3}-M_{W}^{3}+3p^{\prime}\Lambda^{2}+3p^{\prime 2}\Lambda-3p^{\prime}M_{W}^{2}-3p^{\prime 2}M_{W})}{(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{3}(p^{\prime}+\Lambda)^{3}}=\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M_{W}}\right).

Then the final result of our integral obtained with the Pauli-Villars method
[
33
]
reads

\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}(p^{\prime}+M_{W})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M_{W}}\right).

(140)

By comparing equations (
8
) and (

\frac{1}{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M_{W}}\right).

(141)

The above result proves that the divergences manifest as logarithmic functions of
\Lambda
. The last integral that needs to be solved is the one with respect to the momentum of the neutrino.

We must specify that this first order process with three particles in the final state is not encountered in the Minkowski field theory and for that reason the regularization of the third momenta integral is hard to accomplish by using the known regularization methods. For this reason we will make a cutoff of the integral up to the mass of the
W
boson such that the upper limit of the
p

M_{W}
. This is equivalent to the computation of total probability for a fixed value of the neutrino moment.

Our analysis is important since the regularization of the probability helps us understand the nature of the divergences that appear in our exact perturbative calculations and could help one develop a complete renormalized theory by introducing the necessary counter-terms in the lagrangian density. In order to do that, however, further study of particle production processes which are forbidden in Minkowski space-time is required. It may also be possible that new regularization methods will be needed for the regularization of these types of integrals, since in the Minkowski theory we do not encounter such processes.

Collecting our prior results, we can write down the total probability in the case of large expansion:

\displaystyle P_{tot}=\frac{g^{2}\gamma M_{W}^{3}}{96\pi^{2}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M_{W}}\right)+2\left(\psi(3/2)+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)-3\right)

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,\left[\frac{5\pi^{2}+48-36\psi(3/2)+6\psi^{2}(3/2)}{12}+(\psi(3/2)-3)\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\biggl\}.

The above total probability is finite for small values of
\epsilon
, as well as in the case of
\epsilon=0
.

\displaystyle P_{tot}=\frac{g^{2}\gamma M_{W}^{3}}{48\pi^{2}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M_{W}}\right)+\psi(3/2)+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}\right)-3\biggl\}.

(143)

In this section we have shown that some of the infrared and ultraviolet divergences can be removed from the momenta integrals by applying a combination of methods used in the flat space-time theory. Still, in order to achieve a complete renormalizable theory, one needs to write down the renormalized amplitudes by adding the necessary counter-terms to the lagrangian density.

The transition rate for
\lambda=\pm 1

The probability of the particle production process suggests that this type of boson emission was highly likely for
\omega\gg M_{W}
, however one must find measurable quantities to validate such a process. This leads to the problem of computing the transition rate, i.e. the probability per unit time.

In flat space-time, energy-momentum conservation allows us to find a transition rate which depends on the volume
V
and interaction time
T
. But energy and momentum are not simultaneously conserved in de Sitter space-time, and the amplitude is also time-dependant.

We can see this better when writing the amplitude as such:

\displaystyle A_{i\rightarrow f}=-\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}}e^{-\pi k/2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)\sigma^{\prime}\cdot

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}dzze^{-ipz}\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{-}}^{(2)}(p^{\prime}z)\mathcal{K}_{-iK}(iPz)\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1).

(144)

The previous expression for amplitude can be rewritten as

A_{i\rightarrow f}=M_{i\rightarrow f}I_{i\rightarrow f}\delta^{3}(\vec{p_{f}}-\vec{p_{i}}),

(145)

where
I_{i\rightarrow f}
represents the time-dependent integral:

I_{i\rightarrow f}=e^{-\pi k/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{i\rightarrow f}dz,

(146)

The integrand in (
146
) is given by :

K_{i\rightarrow f}=z\,e^{-ipz}\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{-}}^{(2)}(p^{\prime}z)\mathcal{K}_{-iK}(Pz),

(147)

while

\displaystyle M_{i\rightarrow f}=-\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)\sigma^{\prime}\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1).

The transition rate with respect to the conformal time is defined by
[
58
]
:

R_{i\rightarrow f}=\lim_{t_{c}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2V}\frac{d}{dt_{c}}|A_{if}|^{2}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e^{\omega t}}{2V}\frac{d}{dt}|A_{if}|^{2}.

(149)

This expression can be brought to the form:

R_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p_{f}}-\vec{p_{i}})|M_{i\rightarrow f}|^{2}|I_{i\rightarrow f}|\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|e^{\omega t}K_{i\rightarrow f}|.

(150)

In our case we must take into account the summations after
\sigma^{\prime},\lambda
since
\sigma=-1/2
. We also specify that the limit in equation (
150
) will be taken up to a sufficiently large time after the interaction, denoted by
t_{\infty}
.

With these things in mind, the formula of the transition rate in our case is:

R_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\lambda}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p_{f}}-\vec{p_{i}})|M_{i\rightarrow f}|^{2}|I_{i\rightarrow f}|\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}|e^{\omega t}K_{i\rightarrow f}|.

(151)

Because we are working in the limit
t\rightarrow t_{\infty}
, the Hankel functions may be approximated using the formula for small arguments
[
22
]
:

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{(2)}(z)\approx i\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\nu}\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{\pi},

(152)

which works for
z\rightarrow 0
.

Thus the first Hankel function becomes:

\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{2}-ik}^{(2)}\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}\right)\simeq i\left(\frac{2}{\frac{p^{\prime}}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-ik}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-ik)}{\pi}.

(153)

The transition rate is relevant when
\omega\gg M_{W}
and
\omega\gg m_{e}
, meaning that we can further approximate the index of the modified Bessel function
\mathcal{K}_{-iK}
:

-iK=-i\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{2}.

(154)

Consequently, the behaviour of the modified Bessel function for small arguments is:

\mathcal{K}_{-iK}\left(\frac{P}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}\right)\simeq\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{\sqrt{2i\frac{P}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}}}.

(155)

This leads to the limit:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}|e^{\omega t}K_{i\rightarrow f}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}\bigg|i\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\omega}e^{-ip\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\omega}}\left(\frac{2}{\frac{p^{\prime}}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-ik}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-ik)}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{\sqrt{2i\frac{P}{\omega}e^{-\omega t}}}\bigg|

(156)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{Pp^{\prime}\cosh(\pi k)}},

where we have used
[

]
:

\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-ik\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+ik\right)=\frac{\pi}{\cosh(\pi k)}.

(157)

By substituting the limit of the temporal integral in the transition rate formula (
150

R_{i\rightarrow f}=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\lambda}\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}pp^{\prime}}{32(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})|\xi_{-1/2}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)|^{2}\frac{|I_{i\rightarrow f}|}{\sqrt{Pp^{\prime}\cosh(\pi k)}},

(158)

where

.

The explicit form of

is:

I_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{e^{-i\pi/4}e^{-\pi k/2}}{i\sqrt{p}\cosh(\pi k)}(T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}+T_{4}),

(159)

where

are defined in equations (

The final result for the transition rate in the general case is:

R_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}\sqrt{pp^{\prime}}}{32(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})\frac{e^{-\pi k/2}}{\cosh(\pi k)}\sqrt{\frac{(T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}+T_{4})(T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}+T_{4})^{*}}{P\cosh(\pi k)}}.

and

:

R_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{pp^{\prime}}e^{-\pi k/2}}{\cosh(\pi k)}\sqrt{\frac{(T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}+T_{4})(T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}+T_{4})^{*}}{P\cosh(\pi k)}}.

(161)

9.1
Total rate in the limit of large expansion

In this section we will discuss the problem of the total transition rate in the limit of large expansion, where the functions that define the amplitude and transition rate from the previous section become simpler and depend only on the momenta of the emitted particles.

The first task is to establish the expression of the transition rate in the limit
\frac{m_{e}}{\omega}=\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}=0
. In order to do this we first need to write the amplitude in this limit:

\displaystyle A_{i\rightarrow f}=-\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}dzze^{-ipz}\mathcal{H}_{1/2}^{(2)}(p^{\prime}z)\mathcal{K}_{1/2}(iPz)\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p}\,)\sigma^{i}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}}){\epsilon}^{*}_{i}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)

\displaystyle=M_{i\rightarrow f}I_{i\rightarrow f}\delta^{3}(\vec{p_{f}}-\vec{p_{i}}),

(162)

where
M_{i\rightarrow f}
is defined in equation (
9
).

The temporal integral is solvable for the Bessel and Hankel functions of index
1/2
:

I_{i\rightarrow f}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{i}{\sqrt{ip^{\prime}P}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}dz\,e^{-i(p+p^{\prime}+P)z-\varepsilon z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ip^{\prime}P}(p+p^{\prime}+P)}.

(163)

The integrand is of the form
K_{i\rightarrow f}=e^{-i(p+p^{\prime}+P)z}
, while the limit that needs to be done for computing the rate reads:

\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}|e^{\omega t}K_{i\rightarrow f}|=\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{ip^{\prime}P}}e^{-i(p+p^{\prime}+P)\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\omega}}\bigg|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}P}},

(164)

which is precisely the limit obtained directly from equation (
156
) for

.

Then the rate in the limit
\frac{m_{e}}{\omega}=\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}=0
can be written in final form as:

\displaystyle R=\frac{g^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})\frac{1}{P(p+p^{\prime}+P)},

(165)

where the results of the
\sigma^{\prime},\lambda
summations were included.

The total rate of transition can be obtained by integrating over all possible values of the momenta:

R_{vac\rightarrow W^{-}\nu e^{+}}=\int d^{3}p\,d^{3}p^{\prime}\,d^{3}P\,R_{i\rightarrow f}.

(166)

We assume that the particles are emitted in the same direction as in the case of the total probability, with the momenta of the neutrino and positron being oriented in the direction of the positive third axis, while the boson momentum is oriented in the direction of the negative third axis.

We solve the
P
integral and obtain:

\displaystyle I=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}P\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{p}+\vec{p^{\prime}}+\vec{P})}{(2\pi)^{3}P(p+p^{\prime}+P)}=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{3}(p^{\prime}+p)^{2}}.

The
p^{\prime}
integral is divergent, therefore we have to make use of dimensional regularization
[
29

Let us recall the results from the Minkowski electro-weak theory. In the Minkowski field theory, in order to compute the transition rate for the

Then is seems that the finite results for total transition rates can be obtained for the moment in particular cases.

10
Concluding remarks

The present study focuses on the study of the interaction between charged
W^{\pm}
bosons and fermions in the de Sitter geometry by using perturbative methods. The equations of interaction between massive charged vector fields and fermions are established and their solutions are written with the help of the usual method of the Green functions. With this approach one can introduce the
IN/OUT
fields and develop the formalism of reduction for the Proca field in the de Sitter geometry. The definition of the transition amplitudes in the first order of perturbation theory is also established. This is a powerful tool for the study of the phenomenon of particle production in the early stages of the universe.

As an application of our formalism, we have studied the problem of generating the triplet
W^{-}
boson, positron and neutrino from the de Sitter vacuum. We established the transition amplitudes for both the transversal and longitudinal modes and defined the transition probabilities per unit volume. From our analytical and graphical results we prove that that the phenomenon of massive boson production is present during the inflation period. From our general results one recovers the Minkowski limit, where the amplitude and probability of this process vanish. We also study the interesting limiting case where
m_{e}/\omega=M_{W}/\omega=0
, which corresponds to large values of the expansion parameter
\omega
. Under these limiting conditions we obtain the total probability and rate of transition by using dimensional regularization and Pauli-Villars regularization for computing the momenta integrals. We conclude that the process of spontaneous particle emission from vacuum is possible only for a large expansion factor, which corresponds to the early universe period.

For further analysis, the study of all possible processes which generate charged
W^{\pm}
bosons would be of interest, since all possible transitions in the first order of perturbation theory have nonvanishing amplitudes for the large expansion conditions of the early universe. Our results aim to open the way for the study of electro-weak interactions in curved backgrounds and for applying perturbative methods to the understanding of mechanisms which generate massive bosons in the early universe.

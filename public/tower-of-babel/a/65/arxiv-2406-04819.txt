Magnetism of NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} : From finite temperatures to ground state
Weizhen Zhuo, Zheng Zhang, Mingtai Xie, Anmin Zhang, Jianting Ji, Feng Jin, Qingming Zhang

Abstract
Rare-earth chalcogenide compounds ARECh 2 \text{ARECh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} (A = alkali or monovalent metal, RE = rare earth, Ch = O, S, Se, Te) are a large family of quantum spin liquid (QSL) candidate materials. NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is a representative member of the family. Several key issues on NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , particularly how to determine the highly anisotropic spin Hamiltonian and describe the magnetism at finite temperatures and the ground state, remain to be addressed. In this paper, we conducted an in-depth and comprehensive study on the magnetism of NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} from finite temperatures to the ground state. Firstly, we successfully detected three crystalline electric field (CEF) excitation energy levels using low-temperature Raman scattering technique. Combining them with the CEF theory and magnetization data, we worked out the CEF parameters, CEF energy levels, and CEF wavefunctions. We further determined a characteristic temperature of ∼ \sim 40 K, above which the magnetism is dominated by CEF excitations while below which the spin-exchange interactions play a main role. The characteristic temperature has been confirmed by the temperature-dependent electron spin resonance (ESR) linewidth. Low-temperature ESR experiments on the dilute magnetic doped crystal of NaYb 0.1 ​ Lu 0.9 ​ S 2 \text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} further helped us to determine the accurate g g -factor. Next, we quantitatively obtained the spin-exchange interactions in the spin Hamiltonian by consistently simulating the magnetization and specific heat data. Finally, the above studies allow us to explore the ground state magnetism of NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} by using the density matrix renormalization group. We combined numerical calculations and experimental results to demonstrate that the ground state of NaYbS 2 \text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is a Dirac-like QSL.

Introduction

The quantum spin liquid (QSL) is a novel state that the spins exhibit a high degree of entanglement. Unlike conventional magnets, these entangled spins do not form long-range magnetic order even down to zero temperature. This behavior arises due to the influence of strong quantum fluctuations
[

,

Initial research efforts on QSL candidate materials primarily centered on transition metal compounds.

[

.
Organic materials forming triangular lattice are also promising QSL candidates, such as
\text{$\upkappa$}\text{-}\text{(}\text{BEDT}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{TTF}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{CN}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{EtMe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}\text{[}\text{Pd}\text{(}\text{dmit}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{]}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Thermal conductivity experiments show that
\text{$\upkappa$}\text{-}\text{(}\text{BEDT}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{TTF}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{CN}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has non-zero thermal conductivity even as the temperature approaches 0 K. This observation suggests the presence of itinerant spinon excitations within the material.

In recent years, rare earth materials with triangular lattice have attracted great attention.
Firstly, the triangular lattice inherently possesses a geometric frustration effect, which provides a key structure foundation for realizing QSL.
Secondly, rare earth ions have strong spin-orbit coupling (SOC).
Based on the Jackeli-Khaliullin scenario
[
16
,
17

, anisotropic spin-exchange interactions can be produced through strong SOC.
More importantly, rare earth ions with an odd number of 4f electrons are protected by time reversal symmetry, namely Kramers doublets.
The Kramers doublets are robust and less affected by lattice distortions or other structural defects.

\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

Experimental data indicates that
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
exhibits strong magnetic anisotropy.
Crucially, the energy gap between the crystalline electric field (CEF) ground state and the first excited state in
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
exceeds 30 meV
[
19
]
, allowing the doubly degenerate CEF ground state to be mapped to an effective spin-1/2 model.
A large amount of experimental evidence, including INS
[
23
,
24
]
, muon spin relaxation (
\mu

[
25

, and thermodynamic measurements, consistently support the ground state of
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
as a gapless QSL state.
Therefore,
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is considered as a typical QSL material.
However, the previously discussed materials all exhibit limitations to some extent.
For example, there are serious problems of ion vacancies and substitutions in transition metal magnetic materials, which is related to the valence change of transition metals.
In
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, there is a problem of
Mg

\text{ARECh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(A = alkali or monovalent metal, RE = rare earth, Ch = O, S, Se, Te) has been discovered

.
Within this family, the sub-family
\text{NaYbCh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(Ch = O, S, Se, Te) is particularly noteworthy, and has the following advantages.
Firstly, compared to
\text{YbMgGaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, the sub-family eliminates the Mg/Ga disorder while maintaining the perfect triangular lattice.
Secondly, through solid-phase reactions, high-quality polycrystalline or single-crystal samples can be grown

Thirdly, this sub-family of materials has excellent two-dimensional properties, and can be achieved with few or single layers through simple mechanical exfoliation.
Fourth, the regulation of magnetic ions or coordination ions can be easily achieved by doping or replacement, which provides a good platform for in-depth study of the evolution mechanism of QSL.

\text{NaYbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a typical member of this sub-family. A series of experimental results, including magnetization, thermodynamic measurements, INS, and

\text{NaYbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

However, the lack of single crystal samples of this material hinders a deeper study and understanding of its ground state magnetism.

\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is the most extensively studied in the sub-family, an important reason being that centimeter-size single crystals can be obtained through solid-phase reactions.
The INS experiments observed features of a spinon Fermi surface in
\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
[
35
]
, which has attracted widespread attention in the field of frustrated magnetism.
Adding to its intrigue, the energy gap from the valence band to the conduction band of
\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is about 1.9 eV
[
27
]
, and metallization and even superconductivity can be achieved by applying high pressure
[
38
,
39
]
.
This has advanced one step further in revealing the electronic pairing mechanism of QSL and high-temperature superconductivity.
There are also many experimental reports on
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
[
31
,
40
,
41
,
37
]
. However, some key information about
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is not fully clear yet.
Firstly, three CEF energy levels of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
have not been fully determined
[
31
]
. A quantitative description of the CEF is lacking in
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
Secondly, despite some experimental investigation into the

-factor of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
[
41

, further refinement remains necessary. This is particularly crucial considering the potential influence of the effective field arising from spin-exchange interactions on the
g
-factor.
Another more critical piece of information is the lack of determination of the spin-exchange interactions in the low-energy Hamiltonian.
These interactions are very important for studying the ground state magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
There is also a lack of corresponding numerical calculations for the magnetism of the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
Therefore,
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
deserves further and comprehensive study.

In this paper, we conducted an in-depth study on the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
from finite temperatures to the ground state. Firstly, we detected the two optical phonon peaks and three CEF excitation energy levels of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
based on polarized Raman spectroscopy and low-temperature Raman scattering. For the three CEF excitations, they are at 207 cm
-1
(
\sim
25.6 meV), 263 cm
-1
(
\sim
32.6 meV), and 314 cm
-1
(
\sim
38.9 meV), respectively.
Secondly, we obtained the CEF parameters, excitation energy levels, and wave functions by fitting the temperature-dependent magnetization (
M/H-T
) data of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
under a magnetic field of 0.1 T. In the fitting process, we also confirmed that the characteristic temperature of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is 40 K.
Above the characteristic temperature, the CEF excitations play important roles in magnetism. Below the temperature, the spin-exchange interaction dominates.
ESR measurements on the dilute doped sample of
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

g
-factor values of 3.14 in the ab-plane and 0.86 along the c-axis.
We also explained the influence of the effective field contributed by the spin-exchange interaction on the
g
-factor based on the mean-field (MF) theory.
Next, by fitting
M/H-T
data under a magnetic field of 14 T below the characteristic temperature, we obtained the diagonal spin-exchange interactions
J_{\pm}
and
J_{zz}
to be 3.52 K and
-
0.87 K, respectively. The off-diagonal spin-exchange interactions
J_{\pm\pm}
and
J_{z\pm}
were determined to be
-
0.18 K and 2.66 K, respectively, by fitting the specific heat data in the low temperature range.
The most important thing is that we studied the ground state magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
We built the ground state phase diagram using the density matrix renormlization group (DMRG) method. We found that spin-exchange interactions of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
are located in the QSL region of the phase diagram and the
S\left(\vec{q}\right)
in reciprocal space exhibits a broadened peak at the high-symmetry K point.
These calculations preliminarily reveal that the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a Dirac-like QSL state, consistent with the neutron reflections and INS experiment. It also lays the foundation for further studying the potential topological excitations of the
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
ground state.

3
Effective magnetic Hamiltonian

The electron configuration of 4
f
orbital in
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
is
4f^{13}
.
Following the Hund’s rules, there emerges a spectral term
{}^{2}I_{7/2}
featuring an 8-fold degeneracy and another spectral term
{}^{2}I_{5/2}
with a 10-fold degeneracy after SOC.
The energy gap between the two spectral terms is approximately 1 eV
[
45
]
, suggesting that transitions between SOC energy levels can be neglected even at room temperatures.
Consequently, the spectral term
{}^{2}I_{7/2}
denotes the SOC ground state configuration.
Considering the shielding effect of the
6s
and
5p
orbitals on the
4f
electrons, the CEF environment formed by surrounding
S^{2-}
ligands with
D_{3d}
point group symmetry plays a crucial role in
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
In this CEF environment,
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
ions with an odd number of
4f
electrons split into four pairs of doubly degenerate Kramers states, protected by time-reversal symmetry.
The INS experiments on
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
observed the first CEF excitation at approximately 23 meV (
\sim
270 K)
[
31
]
.
This implies that excitation effects of the CEF cannot be ignored when the temperature is comparable to the excitation energy.
In the limit of temperatures significantly lower than the first CEF energy level, the influence of excitations becomes negligible.
This allows for the construction of an effective spin-1/2 model capturing the essential physics. Within this model, spin-exchange interactions, correlations, and quantum fluctuations take center stage.

Based on the understanding of the
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
in
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
described above, we can construct a magnetic effective Hamiltonian to describe the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
[
33
,
34
]
share the same triangular lattice formed by
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
ions due to their isostructural crystal structure.
Therefore, the magnetic effective Hamiltonian used to describe the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
within the temperature range we studied is represented as follows
[
46
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{CEF}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{spin}-\text{ spin }}+\hat{H}_{\text{Zeeman }}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sum_{m,n}B_{m}^{n}\hat{O}_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\left[J_{zz}S_{i}^{z}S_{j}^{z}+J_{\pm}\left(S_{i}^{+}S_{j}^{-}+S_{i}^{-}S_{j}^{+}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+J_{\pm\pm}\left(\gamma_{ij}S_{i}^{+}S_{j}^{+}+\gamma_{ij}^{*}S_{i}^{-}S_{j}^{-}\right)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{iJ_{z\pm}}{2}\left(\gamma_{ij}S_{i}^{+}S_{j}^{z}-\gamma_{ij}^{*}S_{i}^{-}S_{j}^{z}+\langle i\longleftrightarrow j\rangle\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\mu_{0}\mu_{B}\sum_{i}\left[g_{ab}\left(h_{x}S_{i}^{x}+h_{y}S_{i}^{y}\right)+g_{c}h_{c}S_{i}^{z}\right],

(1)

where
B_{m}^{n}
denotes the CEF parameters and
O_{m}^{n}
symbolizes the Steven operator, which is constructed based on the angular momentum
\hat{J}
after the SOC.
S_{i}^{\alpha}(\alpha=x,y,z)
is the effective spin-1/2 operators at site
i
and
S_{i}^{\pm}=S_{i}^{x}\pm iS_{i}^{y}
are the non-Hermitian ladder operators. The nearest neighbor (NN) anisotropic spin-exchange interactions are denoted by
J_{\pm}
,
J_{zz}
,
J_{\pm\pm}
, and
J_{z\pm}
. The phase factor
\gamma_{ij}
is taken as 1,
\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}}
along the three bonds
\vec{a}_{1},\vec{a}_{2}
and
\vec{a}_{3}
. The Land
é

g
-factors in the ab-plane and along the c-axis are represented by
g_{ab}
and
g_{c}
, respectively.

It is noteworthy that the CEF part and the anisotropic spin-exchange interactions part in the magnetic effective Hamiltonian do not exist in the same Hilbert space.
The CEF description is particularly effective for capturing the high-temperature magnetism dominated by CEF excitations. Conversely, the spin-exchange interaction model is suitable for describing low-energy spin excitations within the CEF ground state.
Therefore, the analysis of magnetization and thermodynamic data needs to be discussed separately in two parts.
In the following, we would apply the above strategy to study the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
from finite temperatures to the ground state.

4
CEF and Raman spectra

For the study of CEF excitations in rare-earth materials, INS offers a powerful capability. This technique can directly probe CEF energy levels that are independent of momentum transfer
\vec{Q}
.
Besides, Raman scattering provides another valuable tool for investigating CEF excitations. Compared to INS, Raman scattering offers the advantage of more convenient laboratory-based measurements of CEF excitations.
In
fig.

1
(b), we show the Raman spectrum of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at 2 K.
From the spectrum, we can clearly observe five excitation peaks in the range of 100 to 350 cm
-1
.
Among these peaks, we can divide them into two parts: one part corresponds to the phonon peaks related to optical branch lattice vibrations, and the other part corresponds to the CEF peaks associated with CEF excitations.
By employing both symmetry analysis and polarization scattering experiments, we can determine the vibrational modes associated with the observed phonon peaks.
With the spatial symmetry of
R\overline{3}m
, Raman scattering can detect two phonon modes in
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, namely
A_{g}
and
E_{g}
modes.
The Raman scattering tensors of
A_{g}
and
E_{g}
modes tell us that the
A_{g}
mode is visible only in the parallel polarization (XX) configuration, while the
E_{g}
mode can be observed in both XX and cross polarization (XY) configurations.
The polarized Raman spectrum of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at 300 K are shown in
fig.

1
(a). The two modes can be clearly identified and consistent with our symmetry analysis.

5
Magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at finite temperatures

In the previous section, we conducted detailed investigations on the CEF excitations associated with
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
based on the low-temperature Raman scattering experiment, accurately determining the three CEF excitation energy levels of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
This lays foundations for studying the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at finite temperatures.
In this section, we present a discussion on the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at finite temperatures, including the effect of CEF excitations and the influence of low-energy spin-exchange interactions on magnetism.

We first focus on the contribution of CEF excitations to magnetism.
At high temperature ranges, thermal fluctuations primarily influence magnetization through a series of CEF excitation levels within individual ions.
When the temperature is comparable to the CEF excitation energy levels, the measured magnetization data are mainly contributed by the CEF excitations.
Based on the CEF energy levels obtained from Raman scattering of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and our measured
M/H-T
data, we can simulate the
M/H-T
data by employing magnetization calculation formulas
[
20
,
47
]
.
We used a nonlinear least squares method to fit the contributions of CEF to
M/H-T
in the ab-plane and along the c-axis. The CEF parameters obtained from the point charge model were used as initial values, and three CEF levels were set as constraints.
The experimental data of
M/H-T
and the simulation results based on the CEF theory are shown in
fig.

1
(c) and
fig.

1
(d).
A clear discrepancy arises between the simulation results and experimental data below 40 K both in the ab-plane and along the c-axis.
This indicates that the magnetism is mainly contributed by CEF excitations above 40 K. In contrast, the magnetism is predominantly governed by the anisotropic spin-exchange interactions below 40 K.
Therefore, we can define 40 K as the characteristic temperature of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
Analyzing the temperature-dependent ESR linewidths can also help us determine the characteristic temperature associated with CEF excitations.

fig.

2
(d) and
fig.

2
(h) show the temperature-dependent of the linewidth for
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
samples, respectively. Both figures show that the ESR linewidth rapidly broadens with increasing temperature above 40 K.
This is consistent with the results we obtained by simulating the
M/H-T
data.
Furthermore, by fitting the ESR linewidth above 40 K with the following formula, we can also estimate the energy gap from the CEF ground state to the first excited state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

\mu_{0}\Delta H\simeq\frac{1}{\mathrm{exp}\left(\Delta E/T\right)-1}

(2)

where
\Delta H
is the full width at half maximum (FWHM),
\Delta E
is the energy gap, and
T
is the temperature.
The fitting results are shown as the blue solid lines in
fig.

2
(d) and
fig.

2
(h), respectively. The
\Delta E
obtained by fitting estimation is 263 K (
\sim
22.67 meV), which is close to the CEF first excitation energy level measured by Raman scattering.
The characteristic temperature of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is obviously higher than that of
\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(25 K)
[
34
]
.
The primary reason is that
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
has higher CEF excitation energy levels compared to
\text{NaYbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which fundamentally stems from the stronger electronegativity of the
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
anion.

In addition, we also obtained the CEF parameters, excitation energy levels, and corresponding wave functions during the non-linear fitting process, as shown in
table

1
.
Based on the fitted CEF parameters, the three CEF excitation energy levels obtained through diagonalization calculations are 24.45, 32.29, and 39.62 meV, which are very close to our Raman scattering results. This further demonstrates the reliability of the CEF-related information obtained from our fitting and calculations.

In the limit of temperatures significantly lower than the first CEF excitation energy, the doubly degenerate CEF ground state can be mapped onto an effective spin-1/2 model.
When considering the influence of the magnetic field on the magnetic moment, two additional key parameters arise: the Land
é

g
-factors in the ab-plane and along the c-axis.
The
g
-factor not only reflects the magnetic anisotropy but also serves as a bridge connecting the CEF ground state and the effective spin-1/2 model.
Therefore, accurately determining the
g
-factors of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is important.
The methods to determine
g
-factors mainly include two approaches: one method is to obtain the
g
-factors from the saturation magnetization by applying a magnetic field at low temperature, and the other is to directly obtain them from the resonance magnetic field in the ESR spectrum.
Both method have their own advantages and disadvantages.
For the saturation magnetization method, there is no need to consider the influence of effective internal field caused by spin-exchange interactions on the
g
-factors
[
48
]
. However, if the interaction between spins is strong or exhibits significant paramagnetism, the magnetic moment may require a very high magnetic field to saturate.
For the ESR method, the
g
-factor can be measured by a weak resonant magnetic field, but it is easily affected by the internal field.
This leads to the
g
-factor measured by ESR to deviate from the true value.
If the
g
-factor of magnetic ions is small, it can also be influenced by spin-exchange interactions, causing the observed ESR linewidth to exceed the range of the spectrometer, and thereby limiting the ability to fully capture the ESR resonance spectrum.
This problem exists in the ESR spectrum along the c-axis of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, as shown in
fig.

2
(b).
Therefore, we propose to determine the accurate
g
-factors by measuring the ESR spectrum of a dilute magnetic doped sample.
In
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, spin-exchange interactions are almost nonexistent, thus the
g
-factors obtained from ESR measurements are not affected by spin-exchange interactions.
In
fig.

2
(e) and
fig.

2
(f), we respectively present the temperature-dependent ESR resonance spectra of
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the ab-plane and along the c-axis from 1.8 K to 50 K.
In dilute magnetic doping samples, issues such as imperfect sample homogeneity and magnetic ion vacancies are inevitably introduced.
The ESR is highly sensitive to these issues, resulting in some subtle features in
fig.

2
(e).
Based on Lorentzian fitting, we determine the
g
-factors corresponding to the resonance field as
g_{ab}=3.14
and
g_{c}=0.86
.
By substituting the determined
g_{ab}
and
g_{c}
into the angle-dependent
g
-factor formula (see Supporting Information S4), we can simulate the experimental measurement results of the
g
-factor at different angles.

To further check the accuracy of our calculations, we also calculated the
g

[
33
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}g_{ab-\text{plane}}=g_{j}\left|\left\langle\psi_{\pm}\left|\hat{J}^{\pm}\right|\psi_{\mp}\right\rangle\right|,\\
g_{c-\text{axis}}=2g_{j}\left|\left\langle\psi_{\pm}\left|\hat{J}^{z}\right|\psi_{\pm}\right\rangle\right|,\end{gathered}

where
g_{j}=8/7
and
|\psi_{\pm}\rangle
are the Land
é

g
-factor of free
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
and the CEF ground state.
We can obtain the
g_{ab}\sim 3.13
in the ab-plane and
g_{c}\sim 0.88
along the c-axis.
This is highly consistent with the results obtained from the ESR spectrum of
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. On the one hand, it demonstrates the reliability of the
g
-factors obtained from the dilute magnetic doped samples. On the other hand, it validates the accuracy of the CEF parameters obtained through fitting the
M/H-T

We can compare the
g
-factors of
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with those of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. We found that the

\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is significantly larger than that of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
along the c-axis.
The main reasons for this are as follows: In the ab-plane, the spin-exchange interaction

The ferromagnetic spin-exchange interaction
J_{zz}

g_{c}

Based on the MF Hamiltonian along the c-axis, we can estimate the magnitude of the internal field.
The MF Hamiltonian along the c-axis is represented as follows:

H_{MF,c-axis}=-g_{c}\mu_{0}\mu_{B}\left(h_{c}-\frac{6J_{zz}m_{c}}{\mu_{B}^{2}g_{c}^{2}}\right)\sum_{i}S_{i}^{z},

The relationship between

, resonance frequency, and resonance magnetic field, ignoring spin-exchange interaction

g_{c}=\frac{h\nu}{\mu_{0}\mu_{B}h_{c}},

(6)

where
h
is planck’s constant and
\nu
is resonance frequency.
Taking into account the effect of spin-exchange interaction
J_{zz}
on the

g_{c}^{\prime}=\frac{h\nu}{\mu_{0}\mu_{B}\left(h_{c}-\frac{6J_{zz}m_{c}}{\mu_{B}^{2}g_{c}^{2}}\right)}.

(7)

\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

J_{zz}
as

h_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{6J_{zz}m_{c}}{\mu_{B}^{2}g_{c}^{2}}=\left(1-\frac{g_{c}}{g_{c}^{\prime}}\right)h_{c}=-\text{0.14 T}

(8)

The negative sign indicates that the exchange interaction
J_{zz}
along the c-axis is ferromagnetic type, aligning with

We also use the Debye formula to fit the specific heat data of

from 1.8 to 20 K (see Supporting Information S7).
Next, we can extract off-diagonal spin interactions from the magnetic specific heat data by selecting appropriate numerical calculation method.
We employ the FD to fit the magnetic specific heat. The method can preserve off-diagonal spin-exchange interactions.

The off-diagonal spin-exchange interactions
J_{\pm}
and

are determined to be

With all parameters in the anisotropic spin-exchange interaction Hamiltonian now determined, we are poised for a comprehensive exploration of

6
Ground state magnetism

In this section, we employed the DMRG method to investigate the ground state magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
The PBC was employed to get a accurate matrix product state (MPS). After benchmarking by selecting clusters of different sizes, we finally determined that there are no significant size effects for the
L_{x}

\times

L_{y}
= 12
\times
12 cluster.
In order to determine the location of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the phase diagram, we first calculated the ground state phase diagram.
We set
J_{zz}
as a dimensionless parameter of 1 and fixed the dimensionless parameters
J_{\pm}/J_{zz}
=
-
4.05.
The parameter range for
J_{\pm\pm}/J_{zz}
was chosen to be from
-
2 to 2, and the parameter range for
J_{z\pm}/J_{zz}
is from 0 to 6.
Eventually, we obtained the two-dimensional ground state phase diagram.

fig.

5
(a) and
fig.

5
(b) respectively display the ground state phase diagrams scaled by ground state energy and bipartite entanglement entropy.
The phase boundaries marked by white dashed lines in the ground state phase diagram are determined by bipartite entanglement entropy, the arrangement of ground state magnetic moments, and the static spin structure factor
\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\right\rangle
.
Overall, the ground state phase diagram is in good agreement with existing results from the classical Monte Carlo simulations
[
46
,
49
]
, exact diagonalization
[
49
,
50
]
, and DMRG calculations
[
49
,
51
,
52
]
.
We can divide the phase diagram into four regions: stripe-x, stripe-yz, AFM-
120^{\circ}
, and QSL phase.

fig.

5

fig.

5

fig.

5

120^{\circ}
, stripe-x, and stripe-yz, respectively.
For the stripe-x phase, the magnetic moments on each point are arranged in opposite directions along the edge of the triangle lattice.
The
S\left(\vec{q}\right)
in reciprocal space shows sharp peak located at the high-symmetry point
M
in the Brillouin zone (see
fig.

5
(d)).
For the stripe-yz phase, similar to the stripe-x phase, the magnetic moments are arranged in opposite directions along the perpendicular direction of the triangular lattice.
The peaks of
S\left(\vec{q}\right)
are also located at the
M
points (see
fig.

5
(g)).
The bipartite entanglement entropy can provide us with more information. Compared to the QSL state and the AFM-
120^{\circ}
phase, the entanglement entropy of the two stripe phases is close to zero.
This implies that the quantum fluctuations of these two stripe phases are weak.
The NN spin correlation
\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\right\rangle
also exhibits strong periodicity in the two stripe phases (see Supporting Information S10). Each spin lattice point forms a negative (blue), negative, and positive (red) correlation structure with the NN spin lattice points along the
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, and
a_{3}
directions, respectively.
AFM-
120^{\circ}
is not a trivial quantum state, even though the magnetic moments form a long-range periodic arrangement in the ab-plane.
From the view of entanglement entropy, the bipartite entanglement of the AFM-
120^{\circ}
phase is not low, indicating the presence of strong quantum fluctuations within this regime.
The NN spin correlation
\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\right\rangle
also exhibits some interesting properties (see Supporting Information S10).
The bonds with stronger correlations form a periodic hexagonal structure, while the spin site located at the center of the hexagonal structure has weak correlations with the surrounding NN spin sites.
This means that these central spins can generate confined spinon excitations with strong fluctuations. It is possible to realize a deconfined ”liquid” phase by introducing next-nearest-neighbor or non-diagonal interactions.
More interestingly, AFM-
120^{\circ}
phase can simultaneously break the translational symmetry of the triangular lattice and the U(1) spin rotation symmetry within a certain range of spin-exchange interactions
[
53
,
54
,
55
,
56
]
.
The breaking of these two symmetries allows the spin system to keep strong quantum fluctuations, which is called a spin supersolid. Both spin wave excitations and continuum excitations can be observed in INS spectra.

Compared to ordered phases, we are more interested in the QSL phase located in the central region of the ground state diagram.
The phase has the strongest entanglement entropy in the phase diagram, which means that the spin system has strong quantum fluctuations due to anisotropic spin-exchange interactions.
The red star marks the position of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the phase diagram which is located within the QSL phase region.
A series of related experiments, including neutron diffraction, INS, and
\mu
SR, have confirmed that the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a quantum spin liquid state.
We attempted to understand the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
from numerical calculations. We plotted the arrangement of magnetic moments in real space and the
S\left(\vec{q}\right)
in reciprocal space calculated by DMRG, as shown in
fig.

From the arrangement of magnetic moments, we found that the magnetic structure cannot be described by a single magnetic propagation vector, thus exhibiting the characteristics of spin disorder.
Such a magnetic configuration is more like a distorted AFM-
120^{\circ}
phase, which also indicates that off-diagonal spin-exchange interactions play a key role in the formation of spin disorder.
More importantly,
S\left(\vec{q}\right)
exhibits a broadened peak at the high-symmetry K point. One characteristic of Dirac-like spin liquids is the presence of weak peaks at the K points.
[

.
The NN spin correlation
\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\right\rangle

To better illustrate the series of magnetic field-induced phase transitions in

For INS, we can distinguish a cone feature at

From the perspective of spin dynamics, the ground state of

All of these numerical calculations and experimental results point to the ground state of

The consistency of experimental and computational results also demonstrates the validity of our chosen low-energy spin Hamiltonian model and the fitted exchange interactions.

7
Summary

In summary, we conducted a detailed investigation of the magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
from finite temperatures to ground state.
Firstly, we conducted a Raman scattering on
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
using Raman spectroscopy techniques.
Through polarized Raman scattering, we identified two phonon excitations with Raman activity, namely the
E_{g}
and
A_{1g}
modes.
More importantly, we clearly detected three CEF excitation energy levels of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at 2 K, which are at 207
\mathrm{cm^{-1}}
(
\sim
25.6 meV), 263
\mathrm{cm^{-1}}
(
\sim
32.6 meV), and 314
\mathrm{cm^{-1}}
(
\sim
38.9 meV).

Next, we performed relevant calculations on the contribution of CEF excitations to the magnetization. By fitting the
M/H-T
data of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
under a magnetic field of 0.1 T, we can extract the CEF parameters of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
Based on the CEF parameters, we can obtain the excitation energy levels and wave functions by diagonalizing the CEF Hamiltonian. These energy levels are highly consistent with the results of Raman scattering, further verifying the reliability of these parameters we obtained from the fitting.
At the same time, we determined the characteristic temperature of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
to be 40 K. Above the temperature, the CEF excitations play a key role in magnetism, and below the temperature, the spin-exchange interaction dominates.
This is consistent with the results obtained from the analysis of the ESR linewidths of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
We also studied the
g
-factors in the effective spin-1/2 model of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Through ESR measurements of a dilute magnetic doped sample of
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we determined the accurate
g
-factors in the ab-plane and along the c-axis to be 3.14 and 0.86, respectively.
Through the MF theory, we can well explain why there are differences in the
g
-factors of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{NaYb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.1}}}\text{Lu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.9}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
along the c-axis.

Before analyzing the ground state magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we first obtained the spin-exchange interactions in the low-energy spin Hamiltonian through MF theory and FD method.
By fitting the
M/H-T
data below the characteristic temperature, we can obtain the diagonal spin-exchange interactions
J_{\pm}
and
J_{zz}
to be 3.52 K and
-
0.87 K, respectively.
For the off-diagonal spin-exchange interactions,
J_{\pm\pm}
and
J_{z\pm}
, we can obtain them to be
-
0.18 K and 2.66 K, respectively, by fitting the specific heat data in the low temperature range using the FD method.
We employed the DMRG method to investigate the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
The calculation results directly point to the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
being spin disordered.
By comparing with the existing experimental results, we can further confirm that the ground state of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a Dirac-like spin liquid.
This lays the foundation for us to further study the topological properties of the ground state magnetism of
\text{NaYbS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

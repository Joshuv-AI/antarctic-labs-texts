Introduction

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices whose degree sequence is
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
. For a real
p\geq 1
, we denote the
degree power sum
of
G
by
D_{p}(G):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{p}
. This parameter, which is also known as the
general zeroth-order Randić index
, is well-studied in chemical graph theory, see the comprehensive survey
[
2
]
. Caro and Yuster
[
5
]
initiated a Turán-type problem of degree power sum: determining the function
D_{p}(n,H):=\max\{D_{p}(G):\text{$G$ is an $n$-vertex $H$-free graph}\}
. Since then, a lot of research on this Turán-type problem has appeared, see
[
5
,
3
,
9
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
6
]
.

In this note, we focus on this Turán-type problem when the forbidden graph is a path. Caro and Yuster
[
5
]
determined
D_{p}(n,P_{k})
for any integers
k\geq 2
,
p\geq 2
and sufficiently large
n
, and characterized the corresponding extremal graphs. Define
W_{n,k,s}
to be a graph on
n
vertices, in which its vertex set can be partitioned into three subsets
X,Y,Z
such that
|X|=s
,
|Y|=k-2s
,
|Z|=n-(k-s)
, and the edge set consists of all possible edges between
X
and
Z
and all edges in
X\cup Y
.

Theorem 1

(
[
5
]
)
.

For two integers
k\geq 4
and
p\geq 2
, there exists a positive integer
n_{0}=n_{0}(k)
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, the following holds. If
G
is an
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph with maximum degree power sum
D_{p}(G)
, then
G
is
W_{n,k-1,t}
where
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
.

Note that Caro and Yuster obtained the above result for integers
p\geq 2
. A natural question arising from Theorem
1
is to determine, for fixed real
p
, how large
n_{0}(k)
should be as a function of
k
. Caro and Yuster mentioned that “a close examination of the proof of Theorem 1.2 shows that the value of
n_{0}(k)
in the statement of the theorem is
O(k^{2})
” (quoted from
[
5
]
). In this note, by a different method, we prove that
n_{0}(k)
can be as small as
10k
. And our result extends Theorem
1
to any real
p\geq 2
. We remark that the bound is tight up to a constant factor. Note that
K_{k-1}
, rather than
W_{k-1,k-1,t}
where
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
, attains the maximum degree power sum among all
(k-1)
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graphs. This yields
n_{0}(k)\geq k
.

Theorem 2
.

For an integer
k\geq 4
and real
p\geq 2
, let
G
be an
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph with the maximum degree power sum
D_{p}(G)
. If
n\geq 10k
, then
G
is
W_{n,k-1,t}
where
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
.

Let us outline the main ideas behind the proof of Theorem
2
. Given an
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph
G
with the largest degree power sum, we will first show that there are a few components. This will allow us to use structural information to guarantee the existence of a large component
G_{i}
. Next, we will show that if there is any other component
G_{j}
, then we can use
G_{i}
to “absorb”
G_{j}
to get a larger degree power sum which contradicts the maximality of the degree power sum of
G
.

Lemma 1

W_{n,k-1,1}

Lemma 2

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
and let
p\geq 2
. The following holds for all

. If
G
is a connected
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph with maximum degree power sum
D_{p}(G)

G

W_{n,k-1,t}

The following lemma is useful in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 3
.

Let
a\geq b>0
and
p\geq 2
. Then
(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+pa^{p-1}b+b^{p}
. In particular,
(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+(p+1)b^{p}
.

Proof.

We first show that
(x+1)^{p}\geq x^{p}+px^{p-1}+1
when
x\geq 1
. Let
f(x)=(x+1)^{p}-x^{p}-px^{p-1}-1
and so
f(1)=2^{p}-p-2\geq 0
. Taking derivative of
f(x)
, we get
f^{\prime}(x)=p\left((x+1)^{p-1}-x^{p-1}-(p-1)x^{p-2}\right)
. Note that
f^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
due to convexity of
x^{p-1}
. Hence,
f(x)\geq 0
when
x\geq 1
as desired.
Then

(a+b)^{p}=b^{p}\left(\frac{a}{b}+1\right)^{p}\geq b^{p}\left(\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{p}+p\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{p-1}+1\right)=a^{p}+pa^{p-1}b+b^{p}\geq a^{p}+(p+1)b^{p}.

This completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph which attains the maximum degree power sum
D_{p}(G)
where
p\geq 2
. Let
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
and let
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{\ell}
be all components of
G
. Let
n_{i}=|G_{i}|
for
i\in[\ell]
. By Lemma
1
, we can immediately get the following claim.

Claim 1
.

For any component
G_{i}
, where
1\leq i\leq\ell
, if
n_{i}\leq k-1
, then
G_{i}
is a clique; if
n_{i}\geq k
, then
G_{i}\in\{W_{n_{i},k-1,1},W_{n_{i},k-1,t}\}
.

Proof.

When
n_{i}\leq k-1
, then any
n_{i}
-vertex graph is
P_{k}
-free. Hence,
K_{n_{i}}
would attain the largest degree power sum among all
n_{i}
-vertex graphs. When
n_{i}\geq k
, by Lemma
1
, we have
G_{i}\in\{W_{n_{i},k-1,1},W_{n_{i},k-1,t}\}
.

In the rest of this proof, we say that a component

is a
Type-A
component if
G_{i}=W_{n_{i},k-1,1}
, and is a
Type-B
component if
G_{i}=W_{n_{i},k-1,t}
where
t=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor-1
.

Claim 2
.

For any two components
G_{1}

and
G_{2}
of order
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq k-1
, we have
n_{1}+n_{2}\geq k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq k-1
.
Then
G_{i}
must be a clique for
i=1,2
. Note that
D_{p}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})=n_{1}(n_{1}-1)^{p}+n_{2}(n_{2}-1)^{p}
. If we replace
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
with
K_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
and denote the new graph by
G^{\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime}
is still
P_{k}
-free since
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq k-1
. And
D_{p}(K_{n_{1}+n_{2}})=(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)^{p}
.
Note that
D_{p}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})=n_{1}(n_{1}-1)^{p}+n_{2}(n_{2}-1)^{p}\leq(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)((n_{1}-1)^{p}+(n_{2}-1)^{p})<D_{p}(K_{n_{1}+n_{2}})
, as there holds the inequality
x^{p}+y^{p}<(x+y+1)^{p}
where
x\geq 0,y\geq 0
and
p\geq 2
. Now, we proved
G^{\prime}
has a larger degree power sum than
G
, a contradiction. This proves the claim.

Claim 3
.

There exists at most one component of order at most
k-2
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist two components, say
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, of order
n_{1}\leq k-2
and
n_{2}\leq k-2
, respectively. By Claim

,
n_{1}+n_{2}\geq k
. Let
n^{\prime}_{1}=k-1
and
n^{\prime}_{2}=n_{1}+n_{2}-k+1

n^{\prime}_{2}\leq k-3
, then

contains no

. Recall that

by convexity argument. This means that if we replace
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
with
K_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{2}}
, then we get an
n
-vertex
P_{k}
-free graph with a larger degree power sum, a contradiction.

There exist at most five components of order
k-1

Suppose to the contrary that there exist six components, say

Giusy Monzillo giusy.monzillo@famnit.upr.si UP FAMNIT University of Primorska Glagoljǎska 8 6000 Koper, Slovenia, Tim Penttila tim.penttila@adelaide.edu.au School of Mathematical Sciences The University of Adelaide Adelaide, South Australia 5005 Australia, Alessandro Siciliano ∗ alessandro.siciliano@unibas.it Dipartimento di Matematica, Informatica ed Economia Università degli Studi della Basilicata Viale dell’Ateneo Lucano 10 85100 Potenza, Italy

Abstract
Brown et al. provide a representation of a spread of the Tits quadrangle T 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) T_{2}(\mathscr{O}) , 𝒪 \mathscr{O} an oval of PG ⁡ ( 2 , q ) {\rm PG}(2,q) , q q even, in terms of a certain family of q q ovals of PG ⁡ ( 2 , q ) {\rm PG}(2,q) . By combining this representation with the Vandendriessche classification of hyperovals in PG ⁡ ( 2 , 64 ) {\rm PG}(2,64) and the classification of flocks of the quadratic cone in PG ⁡ ( 3 , 64 ) {\rm PG}(3,64) , recently given by the authors, in this paper, we classify all the spreads of T 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) T_{2}(\mathscr{O}) , 𝒪 \mathscr{O} an oval of PG ⁡ ( 2 , 64 ) {\rm PG}(2,64) , up to equivalence. These complete the classification of spreads of T 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) T_{2}(\mathscr{O}) for q ≤ 64 q\leq 64 .



Keywords: generalized quadrangle, spread, oval, flock

Introduction

The recent classification of hyperovals of
{\rm PG}(2,64)

[
28
]
has provided an opportunity to extend classification results for related structures to order 64. The first of these was inversive planes
[
20
]
, which correspond to fans of ovals. The second of these was flocks (and the related elation generalized quadrangles)
[
11
]
, which correspond to herds of ovals. This paper gives a third: spreads of Tits quadrangles, which correspond to generalized fans of ovals.

This paper is thus a sequel to
[
5
]
, which classified spreads of Tits quadrangles of order at most 32. We take the opportunity to clarify some issues of when isomorphic spreads of Tits quadrangles correspond to isomorphic generalized fans of ovals, left open in
[
4
]
, by introducing the concept of a posy of ovals (and of equivalence of posies) in Section
2
. We also correct an error in a line of a table in
[
5
]
.

All spreads of Tits quadrangles of order 64 were previously known – no new spreads were discovered in the course of this classification.
Of the nineteen Tits quadrangles of order 64, fifteen admit a unique spread. Each of the remaining four, which are already known to be proper subquadrangles of a generalized quadrangle with the same number of lines on a point, has a spread on every point of the oval on which it is constructed; all these spreads are subtended. In addition, these Tits quadrangles are actually the
only
subquadrangles of a generalized quadrangle with the same number of lines on a point; the Tits quadrangle arising from the pointed conic is not among the latter, as is also the case for orders 16 and 32.

A (finite)

and

(i)

Each point is incident with
t+1
lines, and two distinct points are incident with at most one line.

(ii)

Each line is incident with
s+1
points, and two distinct lines are incident with at most one point.

(iii)

If

such that
x\,{\rm I}\,M\,{\rm I}\,y\,{\rm I}\,L

The integers

(s,t)

. A

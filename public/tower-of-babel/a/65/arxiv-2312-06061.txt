Loops with Universal and Semi-Universal Flexibility
Riley Britten, Michael Kinyon, Kenneth Kunen, J. D. Phillips

Abstract
. We study loops which are universal (that is, isotopically invariant) with respect to the property of flexibility ( x ​ y ⋅ x = x ⋅ y ​ x xy\cdot x=x\cdot yx ). We also weaken this to semi-universality, that is, loops in which every left and right isotope is flexible, but not necessarily every isotope. One of our main results is that universally flexible, inverse property loops are Moufang loops. On the other hand, semi-universally flexible, inverse property loops are diassociative. We also examine the relationship between universally flexible loops and middle Bol loops. The paper concludes with some open problems.

Ken Kunen passed away in 2020. It is difficult to overstate the influence that he had on our work. It was thanks to him
that both MK and JDP have focused their research careers on the use of automated deduction tools in quasigroup theory and
other parts of algebra. After mulling it over for a long time, we decided that the best tribute we could give to him
was to finally submit the paper for publication, leaving Problem

6.
Diassociativity

To prove that a loop is diassociative, it is natural to introduce the following definition:

Definition 6.1
.

Given elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in a loop,
let
A(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
abbreviate the statement that

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

associates
, in the sense that all associated
products of the elements in that order are equal.

The number of such products is the Catalan number
C_{n-1}=\frac{(2n-2)!}{n!(n-1)!}
.
For example, when
n=4
,
C_{4-1}=5
, and
A(w,x,y,z)
iff

w\cdot(x\cdot yz)=w\cdot(xy\cdot z)=wx\cdot yz=(w\cdot xy)\cdot z=(wx\cdot y)\cdot z\ \ .

Definition 6.2
.

In an IP loop, let
H(n)
abbreviate the statement that for all
a,b
in the loop,

A(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
holds for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{1,a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

In any IP loop
Q
:

•

H(n)\to H(m)
whenever
m\leq n
.

•

Q
is diassociative iff
H(n)
holds for all
n
.

•

H(3)
holds iff
Q
is flexible and alternative.

Moufang’s original proof
[
8
,
9
]
of what we now call Moufang’s Theorem was inductive.
Moufang loops satisfy a number of additional properties which allow for a proof of
Moufang’s Theorem without an explict induction; this is the approach taken in
[
3
]
.
More recently, Drápal found a simpler proof in the spirit of Moufang’s original proof
[
4
]
.

Our proof of diassociativity will prove
H(n)
by induction on
n
. Definition
6.5
below introduces
a class of loops in which one can step up easily from
H(n)
to
H(n+1)
.
This class includes all SUF IP loops, and hence all Moufang loops.

Definition 6.4
.

If
X
is a subset of an IP loop
Q
, then
X

strongly associates

iff
A(x,y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X\cup X^{-1}
.
Let
\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)
abbreviate the statement that
\{x,y,z\}
strongly associates.

In Moufang loops,
A(x,y,z)
is equivalent to
SA(x,y,z)
.

The notation
\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)
does not imply that
x,y,z
are distinct.
If
Q
is also alternative and flexible, then every
\{a,b\}
strongly associates, and the assertion
\mathit{SA}(a,b,c)

is naturally written as
6\cdot 8=48
equations, since there are
6
permutatons
of
\{a,b,c\}
and then
2^{3}=8
choices of which of
a,b,c
to invert.
However, by IP, these
48
come in
24
equivalent pairs,
since
A(x,y,z)\leftrightarrow A(z^{-1},y^{-1},x^{-1})
;
for example,
A(a,b^{-1},c^{-1})
is equivalent to
A(c,b,a^{-1})
.

Definition 6.5
.

A
stepping-up loop
is an alternative, flexible IP loop
Q
satisfying the implication

\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)\to\mathit{SA}(x,y,yz)
.

Note that the property
\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)
is invariant under permutations of
x,y,z
and under replacing any of
x,y,z
by its inverse.
Thus in a stepping-up loop, if
\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)
holds, then also
\mathit{SA}(x^{-1},y^{-1},y^{-1}z^{-1})
,
\mathit{SA}(x,y,zy)
,
\mathit{SA}(x,xy,z)
,

\mathit{SA}(x,x^{-1}y,z)
, etc.

Stepping up loops form a quasivariety because the implication
\mathit{SA}(x,y,z)\to\mathit{SA}(x,y,yz)
can be written as a collection of
quasi-identities. Moufang loops are stepping up; this is a consequence of Moufang’s Theorem or can be shown directly.
There exist some Steiner loops which are stepping up, but not all of them are.

Lemma 6.6
.

Every stepping-up loop is diassociative.

Proof.

We shall prove by induction that
H(n)
holds for all
n
.
We already have
H(3)
by the IP,
Flex
,
LAlt
and
RAlt
.
Now, we fix
n\geq 3
and assume
H(n)
; we shall prove
H(n+1)
,
so fix
a,b
, and fix
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{1,a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}
;
we shall prove
A(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
Note that
H(n)
implies

\mathit{SA}(x_{1}\cdots x_{j},\;y_{1}\cdots y_{k},\;z_{1}\cdots z_{\ell})

whenever
j+k+\ell\leq n
and all

x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},\allowbreak z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\in\{1,a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}
.

In view of
H(n)
, there are
n

C_{n}

for

.

P_{0}=P_{1}
and
P_{n-2}=P_{n-1}

P_{0}=P_{1}

x_{0}=a

a\cdot(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})=(a_{0}x_{1})\cdot(x_{2}\cdots x_{n})

This is clear from ALT+IP if

,
so Without loss of generality,
x_{1}=b
. Then the result follows from

\mathit{SA}(a,b,\;x_{2}\cdots x_{n})
, which in turn follows from

\mathit{SA}(a,b,1)
and the stepping-up property.

Now, we prove by induction on
\ell
that
P_{\ell}=P_{0}
,
so we fix
\ell
, assume that
P_{0}=\cdots=P_{\ell-1}
,
and we prove
P_{\ell}=P_{0}
. We may assume that
2\leq\ell\leq n-2
.
Without loss of generality,
x_{\ell}=a
. We first attempt to prove

P_{\ell}=P_{\ell-1}
; that is,

(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1}a)\cdot(x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})=(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1})\cdot(ax_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})\ \ .

There are three cases.

Case 1: at least one of
x_{0},x_{\ell-1},x_{\ell+1},x_{n}
is
1
;
then just apply
H(n)
.

Case 2: at least one of
x_{0},x_{\ell-1},x_{\ell+1},x_{n}
is

a
or
a^{-1}
; then we can verify

\mathit{SA}(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1},\;a,\;x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})
;
for example, if
x_{\ell+1}=a^{-1}
, we have

\mathit{SA}(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1},\;a,\;x_{\ell+2}\cdots x_{n})
from

and then
\mathit{SA}(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1},\;a,\;x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})

Case 3: assume that

x_{0},x_{\ell-1},x_{\ell+1},x_{n}\in\{b,b^{-1}\}
.
Now, we prove instead
P_{\ell}=P_{0}
. Without loss of generality,
x_{0}=b
, so we must prove

(bx_{1}\cdots x_{\ell})\cdot(x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})=b\cdot(x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})\ \ .

But now we use
\mathit{SA}(b,\;x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell},\;x_{\ell+1}\cdots x_{n})
,
which follows
(using
x_{\ell+1}\in\{b,b^{-1}\}
)
from
\mathit{SA}(b,\;x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell},\;x_{\ell+2}\cdots x_{n})

and this in turn follows from
H(n)
.

7.
Open Problems

The main open problem here is the following:

Problem 7.1
.

Does there exist an SUF IP loop which is not a Moufang loop?

We have spent a lot of effort over a period of many years on both sides of this question. Using
Prover9
,
we know various cases in which SUF IP loops are Moufang. For example, every commutative SUF IP loop is Moufang, as is
every nilpotent SUF IP loop of nilpotency class
2
. We are not particularly motivated to present the
proofs of these claims, because they are long “equation-bashing” proofs and not very enlightening.
None of the proofs we have in special cases seem to give any insight into the general question.

In the negative direction, naive
Mace4
searches up to order 20 have been unsuccessful.
As part of their PhD dissertation work
[
2
]
, one of us (RB) attacked the problem using central extensions,
the same methodology used to construct Example
5.3
.
They checked central extensions of the Moufang loops of orders 16, 32, 64 and 81 by cyclic groups of various
orders; see
[
2
]
for details. Unfortunately, none of those cases were successful.

Despite all this, it is still our opinion that proper (i.e. nonMoufang) SUF IP loops exist.

By Theorem
2.2
, an affirmative answer to Problem
7.1
will also be an affirmative answer to the following.

Problem 7.2
.

Does there exist an SUF loop which is not a UF loop?

Here we have not searched as extensively as for Problem
7.1
.

We have mentioned the property in loops that the inner mappings
R(x)L(x)^{-1}
are all automorphisms; this property
implies flexibility.

Problem 7.3
.

Does there exist a loop satisfying the aforementioned property universally, but which does not satisfy the AAIP?

Example
5.3
also suggests the following problems.

Problem 7.4
.

Does there exist a UF loop which does not satisfy the semi-automorphic inverse property
(xyx)^{-1}=x^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}
?

Problem 7.5
.

Does there exist a commutative UF loop with the AAIP which is not a middle Bol loop?

On MSRD codes, h-designs and disjoint maximum scattered linear sets
Paolo Santonastaso, John Sheekey

Abstract
. In this paper we study geometric aspects of codes in the sum-rank metric. We establish the geometric description of generalised weights, and analyse the Delsarte and geometric dual operations. We establish a correspondence between maximum sum-rank distance codes and h h -designs, extending the well-known correspondence between MDS codes and arcs in projective spaces and between MRD codes and h h -scatttered subspaces. We use the geometric setting to construct new h h -designs and new MSRD codes via new families of pairwise disjoint maximum scattered linear sets.

1.
Introduction

The interplay between coding theory and finite geometry has a long and rich history. From the classical connections between linear codes in the Hamming metric and (multi)sets of points in a projective space, through more recent connections between linear codes in the rank metric and linear sets in a projective space, these correspondences have lead to many interesting results, techniques, and research questions. Natural notions in one of these settings lead to new questions in the other, and in many cases questions that appear difficult to address in one setting become more tractable in the other.

In this paper we consider codes in the
sum-rank metric
, a metric that can be seen as a generalisation of both the Hamming and rank metrics, and their geometric counterparts. The sum-rank metric has attracted sustained interest in recent years, due to applications in information theory, including multishot network coding, distributed storage systems and multi-antenna wireless communication
[
36
]
. Codes in the sum-rank metric can be viewed as spaces of block-diagonal matrices, and so this allows for a broad range of cases of possible interest by varying the number and sizes of the blocks; however, most research focuses on cases with additional structure where they more closely resemble the classical cases of the Hamming and rank-metrics, while still generalising enough to give rise to a rich source of interesting mathematical questions and sufficient applicability.

The correspondence between linear codes in the Hamming metric and sets of points in a projective space (sometimes referred to as
projective systems
) is well-known and is a key tool in classical coding theory. More recently, a correspondence between codes in the rank-metric (with additional linearity assumptions) and geometric objects analogous to projective systems has been established, leading to new constructions, techniques, and furthering our understanding of the structure of these codes.

With certain assumptions on the parameters and linearity of a sum-rank metric code, the geometric counterparts of optimal codes are
(optimal) subspace designs
and further analogues of projective systems. These are sets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space with restricted intersection properties with respect to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces, and were first introduced by Guruswami and Xing in
[
15
]
for the purposes of studying rank-metric codes. The connection with sum-rank metric codes was established in
[
38
]
, which leads to a host of natural questions regarding the existence and structure of these objects.

In this paper we determine the geometric counterpart of the coding-theoretic notion of
generalised weights
for the sum-rank metric in terms of subspace designs, and study geometric characterisations of the systems corresponding to
MSRD codes
. We study the implications of the coding-theoretic
Delsarte dual
on subspace designs, and in the opposite direction we consider the implications of geometric duality for codes. We then use the geometric setting to construct new subspace designs, and hence new MSRD codes. In particular we show the existence of large sets of of pairwise disjoint
maximum scattered linear sets
in many projective spaces.

1.2.
Layout of paper

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we recall the basic definitions and preliminary results on sum-rank metric codes and subspace designs. We establish the necessary tools and correspondences between the settings, and prove some basic properties which will be used in later sections. In Section 3 we further develop the geometric setting, in particular with regard to
h
-designs, generalised weights, and geometric duality. Section 4 contains the main new constructions of this paper; in particular we construct new examples of optimal maximum
1
-designs for many new parameters. In Section 5 we show how these objects give rise to two-intersecting sets, strongly regular graphs, and two-weight codes in the Hamming metric. 2.
Sum-rank-metric codes and their geometric description

2.1.
Preliminaries

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rank of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is defined as the quantity

\rk_{q}(v)\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

For an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, (sometimes we also write
(x_{1}\mid\ldots\mid x_{t})
), the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-sum-rank weight of
x
is defined as

\ww(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}).

A
(vector) sum-rank metric code

\C
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-sum-rank distance on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
defined as

\dd(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}-y_{i}),

for any
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
. The minimum (sum-rank) distance of a code
\C
is denoted
d(\C)
, that is,

d(\C):=\min\{\dd(x,y)\,:\,x,y\in\C,x\neq y\}=\min\{\ww(x)\,:\,x\in\C,x\neq 0\}.

From now on, we write that
\C
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code, if
\C
is a sum-rank metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
having
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-dimension
k
and minimum (sum-rank) distance
d
, or simply an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code if the minimum distance is not relevant/known.

For an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, we denote by
W_{r}(\C)
the number of codewords having
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-sum-rank weight
r
. For vector sum-rank metric codes, a Singleton-like bound holds.

Theorem 2.1

(see
[
8
, Theorem III.2]

[
33
, Proposition 16]
)
.

Let
\C
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
j
and
\delta
be the unique integers satisfying

d-1=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\min\{m,n_{i}\}+\delta\mbox{ and }0\leq\delta\leq\min\{m,n_{j}\}-1.

Then

\lvert\C\rvert\leq q^{m\sum_{i=j}^{t}n_{i}-\max\{m,n_{j}\}\delta}.

In particular,

\begin{cases}k\leq N-d+1&\mbox{ if }m\geq n_{1};\\
mk\leq n(tm-d+1)&\mbox{ if }n\coloneqq n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}\geq m.\end{cases}

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called
Maximum Sum-Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MSRD code
) if its parameters attains the bound of Theorem
2.1
.

Let consider the symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}

(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\cdot y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}

induced by the standard inner product on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
The dual of an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
is defined as

\C^{\perp}=\left\{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\cdot y_{i}=0\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1}\mid\ldots\mid x_{t})\in\C\right\}.

Consequentely, the dual of a
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is a
[\mathbf{n},N-k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}
. Moreover, the MSRD property is invariant under dualization.

Theorem 2.2

(see
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
34
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
\C
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
Then,

d(\C^{\perp})\leq\begin{cases}N-d+2,&\mbox{ if }m\geq n_{1},\\
tm-d+2,&\mbox{ if }n\coloneqq n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}\geq m,\end{cases}

and the equality holds when
\C
is an MSRD code. In particular, if
\C
is an MSRD code then
\C^{\perp}
is an MSRD code as well.

Now, we recall the definition of equivalence between sum-rank metric codes. We will use the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the whole ambient space in order to define the equivalence of sum-rank metric codes. The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank distance are classified in
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
. The case
\mathbf{n}=(n,\ldots,n)
was already proved in
[
35
, Theorem 2]
.

In this way, we say that two
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are
equivalent
if there is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry mapping
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. More precisely,
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are equivalent if and only if there exist an element
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t})\in\GL(n_{1},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\ldots\times\GL(n_{t},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, an element
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*})^{t}
and a permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
such that

\C_{2}=\left\{(a_{1}x_{\pi(1)}A_{1}\mid\ldots\mid a_{t}x_{\pi(t)}A_{t})\colon(x_{1}\mid\ldots\mid x_{t})\in\C_{1}\right\}.

Finally, we recall the notion of degeneracy of a sum-rank metric code.
Let
\C
be a
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. A generator matrix
G
for
\C
is naturally partitioned as
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})
, with
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
. In this way,
\C
is said to be
sum-rank nondegenerate
if for any generator matrix
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})
, with
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
, the columns of each
G_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear independent. This property can be characterize as follows.

Proposition 2.3

(see
[
38
, Proposition 2.13]
)
.

Let
\C
be a
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
k<N
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\C
is sum-rank nondegenerate.

(2)

\dd(\C^{\perp})\geq 2
.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes by
\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

The interested reader is referred to
[
33
,
34
,
37
,
41
]
for a more detailed description of this setting.

2.2.
Geometry and Delsarte dual of a system

Recently in
[
38
]
, a geometric characterization of sum-rank metric codes has been provided.
This is very useful to the aim of studying metric properties of sum-rank metric codes. We will indeed use this geometric characterization later with the principal goal of constructing optimal codes in this metric.
Let start by introducing the systems, that are the geometric counterpart of sum-rank metric codes.

Definition 2.4
.

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system

\mathcal{U}
is an ordered set
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
, where
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n_{i}
, for any
i\in[t]
, such that

\langle\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
d
is defined as

d\coloneqq N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\,:\,\mathcal{H}\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

We say that two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
(\mathcal{V}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{V}_{t})
are
equivalent
if there exists a matrix
A\in\GL(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
, an element
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*})^{t}
and a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, such that for every
i\in[t]

\mathcal{U}_{i}\cdot A=a_{i}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(i)}.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems by
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Moreover, we recall this fundamental result.

Theorem 2.5

(see
[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with generator matrix
G=(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})
.
Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for
i\in[t]
. Then, for every
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
i\in[t]
we have

\ww(vG)=N-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap v^{\perp}).

In particular,

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\,:\,\mathcal{H}\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

We are ready to describe the 1-to-1 correspondence between equivalence classes of sum-rank nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems.

Let
[\C]\in\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
where
\C
is a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
be a generator matrix for
{\mathcal{C}}
and consider for each
i\in[t]
,
\mathcal{U}_{i}
to be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
. Define
\Psi([\C])
as the equivalence class of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
[\mathcal{U}]
, where
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
. In this case
\mathcal{U}
is also called a
system associated with

{\mathcal{C}}
. Viceversa, let
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]\in\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
. For every
i\in[t]
, fix an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. Define
G_{i}
as the matrix whose columns are
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
and let
\Phi([(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})])
be the equivalence class of the sum-rank metric code generated by
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
.

Taking into account the notion of equivalence for
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes and
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system and Theorem
2.5
, the following maps

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q},

are defined.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
38
, Theorem 3.7]
)
.

\Psi
and
\Phi
are well-defined and are the inverse of each other.

Remark 2.7

(see
[
38
, Remark 3.9]
)
.

Note that when
t=1
, the notion of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system coincides with the notion of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system introduced in
[

]
; see also
[

]
. In the case where

, this notion coincides with the notion of projective systems. Consequently, the above geometric characterization extend in the sum-rank metric the well-known one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of Hamming-metric nondegenerate codes and equivalence classes of projective systems; see
[
51
, Theorem 1.1.6]
and the one-to-one correspondence between classes of nondegenerate rank-metric codes and equivalence classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-systems, estabilished in
[
48
,
45
]
, see also
[
1
]
.

Finally, we recall a duality introduced in
[
46
]
for
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems by using their connection with sum-rank metric codes.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system and suppose that

. Let
[{\mathcal{C}}]=\Phi([\mathcal{U}])
. Since

, by Proposition

, the dual codes of the codes in
[{\mathcal{C}}]
are nondegenerate and taking into account that two sum-rank metric codes

and

are equivalent if and only if their dual codes

[{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}]
of

. We call

the

of

and any

a

of
\mathcal{U}
.

2.3.
Matrix sum-rank metric codes and weight distributions of MSRD codes

We can deal with sum-rank metric codes also in a matrix setting.
We consider the direct sum of
t
matrix spaces

\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}):=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}\times m}.

We define the
sum-rank weight
of an element
X=(X_{1}|\ldots|X_{t})\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
as

w(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}(X_{i}).

Consequently, the
sum-rank distance
is defined as

d(X,Y):=w(X-Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{rk}(X_{i}-Y_{i}),

for any
X=(X_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert X_{t})
,
Y=(Y_{1}\lvert\ldots\lvert Y_{t})\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, with
X_{i},Y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}\times m}
.

A
(matrix) sum-rank metric code

\mathrm{C}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
endowed with the sum-rank distance and its minimum sum-rank distance is defined as

d(\mathrm{C})=\min\{w(X):X\in\mathrm{C},X\neq 0\}.

The matrix and vector settings for the sum-rank metric described above are related as following.
Let
\Gamma_{r}=(\gamma_{1}^{(r)},\ldots,\gamma_{m}^{(r)})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, for each
r\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
and define
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
. For an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, define the element
\Gamma(x)=(\Gamma_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{t}(x_{t}))\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where

(x_{r})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\Gamma_{r}(x_{r}))_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{(r)},\qquad\mbox{ for each }i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{r}\}.

Equivalently, the
r
-th block of
\Gamma(x)
is the matrix expansion of the vector
x_{r}
with respect to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma_{r}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.
As already stated in
[
38
, Theorem 2.7]
, the map

(1)

\Gamma:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\longrightarrow\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isometry between the metric spaces
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\mathrm{d})
and
(\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}),d)
. Also in the matrix framework, we can deal with optimal codes. Indeed, for a sum-rank metric code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
having dimension
k
and with minimum distance
d
, a Singleton-like bound holds and if its parameters attains the bound in Theorem
2.1
, it is called
Maximum Sum-Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MSRD code
) (see
[
8
, Theorem III.2]
). Clearly, if
\C
is an MSRD
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code then
\Gamma(\C)
is an MSRD code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, as well.

Remark 2.8
.

Actually, we can consider a more general ambient space for the matrix setting, i.e. the direct sum of
t
matrix spaces
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}\times m_{i}}
in which not all the
m_{i}
’s are necessarily equal. But for our aim, we will restrict the study to
(\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q}),d)
.

For more details on the matrix setting and its connection with the vector setting, see
[
8
]
and
[
37
]
.

2.4.
Linearized and Twisted linearized Reed-solomon codes

The first construction of MSRD codes was introduced in
[
33
]
and it is known as
linearized Reed-Solomon codes
. These codes are the analogue in the sum-rank metric of Gabidulin codes in the rank metric and the Reed-Solomon codes in the Hamming metric. See also
[
37
]
, for a description of these codes in a skew polynomial setting.

In order to describe such codes, we recall the notion of skew polynomials, which were originally presented in the seminal paper by Ore
[
40
]
, in a
more general setting.

Let
\sigma
be a generator of
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
The ring of skew polynomials
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]
is a set of formal polynomials

\left\{f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}\,:\,f_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}},d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\right\}

equipped with the ordinary addition

\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}x^{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}g_{i}x^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}(f_{i}+g_{i})x^{i}

and the multiplication rule

x\cdot a=\sigma(a)\cdot x,\mbox{ for any }a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}},

extended to polynomials by associativity and distributivity.

We say that the degree of a nonzero skew polynomial
\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}
is
d
if
f_{d}\neq 0
and we assume that the zero polynomials has degree
-\infty
. For an integer
v
, we denote by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<v}
the set of skew polynomials of degree smaller than
v
.

We recall the
generalized operator evaluation
introduced in
[
25
]
.
Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Define the map

\begin{array}[]{rccl}f(\cdot)_{\mu}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\\
&\beta&\longmapsto&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\sigma^{i}(\beta)\mathrm{N}_{i}(\mu),\end{array}

where
\mathrm{N}_{i}(\mu)\coloneqq\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^{j}(\mu)
.

For
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, we can define the multi-point evaluation map as

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathrm{ev}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\\
&f&\longmapsto&(f(\beta_{1})_{\mu_{1}},\ldots,f(\beta_{n})_{\mu_{1}}\lvert\cdots\lvert f(\beta_{1})_{\mu_{t}},\ldots,f(\beta_{n})_{\mu_{t}}).\end{array}

The properties of the map
\mathrm{ev}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
have been studied in
[
33
]
and they lead to the construction of the the following class of MSRD codes.

Definition 2.9

(see
[
33
, Definition 31]
)
.

Let
n\leq m
,
t\leq q-1
and
1\leq k\leq tn
. Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, such that the elements of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and assume that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{i})\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{j})
, if
i\neq j
.

The
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code

\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\{\mathrm{ev}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}(f)\,:\,f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<k}\},

is called
linearized Reed-Solomon code
.

In
[
33
, Theorem 4]
, it has been shown that the linearized Reed-Solomon codes
\mathcal{C}_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
are MSRD codes.

Recently, in
[
37
]
a new family of MSRD codes has been introduced.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
n\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq tn
. Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, such that the elements of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and let
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{i})=\lambda_{i}
and suppose
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, if
i\neq j
. Let
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
with the property that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\eta)(-1)^{km}\notin G
, with
G
the multiplicative subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
generated by
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\}
.
The
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code

\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\eta)=\{\mathrm{ev}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}(f)\,:\,f(x)=f_{0}+\ldots+f_{k-1}x^{k-1}+\eta f_{0}X^{k},f_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\},

is called
twisted linearized Reed-Solomon code
.

The twisted linearized Reed-Solomon code can be considered as an extension in the sum-rank metric of twisted Gabidulin code in the rank metric
[
47
,
28
]
and the twisted Reed-Solomon code in the Hamming metric
[
5
]
. In
[
37
, Theorem 6.3]
, it has been shown that such codes are MSRD codes. Note that when
\eta=0
, these codes coincide with the linearized Reed-Solomon codes, i.e.
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}(0)=\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.

2.5.
Weight distribution of MSRD codes

In
[
8
]
the weight distribution of MSRD codes is also determined, as we will describe in the following. Assume that
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
and let
m^{\prime}=\max\{m,n\}
and
n^{\prime}=\min\{m,n\}
. For
i=\{1,\ldots,t\}
, let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be the product lattice, where
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is the lattice of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n^{\prime}}
. This means that an element of
\mathcal{L}
is of the form
\mathbf{W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})
, where
W_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n^{\prime}}
, for each
i
and define
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathbf{W})=(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W_{1}),\ldots,\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W_{t}))
. The rank function on
\mathcal{L}
is defined by
rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W_{i})
, for all
\mathbf{W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})\in\mathcal{L}
. Moreover, we can define the partial order
\leq
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{t}
by

\mathbf{v}\leq\mathbf{u}\Longleftrightarrow v_{i}\leq u_{i},\mbox{for each }i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}

for any
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}),\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{t}
. Moreover, define

f_{\ell}(\mathbf{u})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\leq\mathbf{u}\\
\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{u_{i}-v_{i}}q^{\binom{u_{i}-v_{i}}{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{i}\\
v_{i}\end{matrix}\right]_{q},

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{t})
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}v_{i}
if
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})
.

For a sum-rank metric code
\mathrm{C}
in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, let denote by
W_{r}(\mathrm{C})
the number of codewords having sum-rank weight
r
.

Theorem 2.11

(see
[
8
, Theorem VI.5, Remark VI.11.]
)
.

Using the above notation, if
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
having minimum distance
d
, we have

W_{r}(\mathrm{C})=\sum_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{L},rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W})=r}\sum_{\ell=d}^{r}(q^{m^{\prime}(\ell-d+1)}-1)f_{\ell}(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathbf{W})).

By the above theorem, we note that all MSRD codes with the same parameters have the same sum-rank distribution. For this reason, we will also denote
W_{r}(\mathrm{C})
by

(2)

W_{r}(n^{\prime},m^{\prime},t):=\sum_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{L},rk_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W})=r}\sum_{\ell=d}^{r}(q^{m^{\prime}(\ell-d+1)}-1)f_{\ell}(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathbf{W})),

if
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, with
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
and
m^{\prime}=\max\{m,n\}
,
n^{\prime}=\min\{m,n\}
.

We conclude this section with some results that will be useful in the next section. In particular we prove that every MSRD sum-rank metric code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
with
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
, having minimum distance
d
and
t\leq q-1
, has at least one codeword of weight
j
, for any
d\leq j\leq N
. We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code as in Definition
2.9
, with
d=tn-k+1
. Then for each
k\leq tn
and any
r\in\{d,\ldots,tn\}
, there exists an element
x\in\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
such that

\ww(x)=r
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. If
k=1
, all the elements in
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
have sum-rank weight
tn
. So assume that the statement holds for
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. Since
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}\subseteq\C_{k+1,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
, there exists at least one element of sum-rank weight
r
in
\C_{k+1,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
, for each
r\in\{tn-k+1,\ldots,tn-1,tn\}
. On the other side,
\C_{k+1,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is an MSRD code that implies that there exist at least one of its element having sum-rank weight
tn-k
. Hence, the statement holds also for
\C_{k+1,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
that concludes our proof.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Assume that
n:=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
,
t\leq q-1
and
m\geq n
. Let
\mathrm{C}
be an MSRD code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
having minimum distance
d
, with
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
. For any
0\leq i\leq tn-d
, there always exists at least one codeword
X\in\mathrm{C}
having sum-rank weight
d+i
, i.e.

W_{r}(n,m,t)>0,

for each
d\leq r\leq tn
.

Proof.

Let consider the
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
as in Definition
2.9
. Let
\mathrm{C}_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\Gamma(\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}})
, where
\Gamma
is as in (
1
). Since
\C_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is an MSRD code then
\mathrm{C}_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is an MSRD code in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},m,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
as well. By Theorem
2.11
, we know that all MSRD codes with the same parameters have the same sum-rank distribution. This allow us to look only to
\mathrm{C}_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. Then by Lemma
2.12
, we get the assertion.
∎

For the vectorial setting, we have the following.

Corollary 2.14
.

Assume that
n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}
and
t\leq q-1
. Let
\C
be an MSRD
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. For any
0\leq i\leq t\min\{m,n\}-d
, we have
W_{d+i}(\C)>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{C}=\Gamma(\C)
, where
\Gamma
is as in (
1
). Since
\C
is an MSRD code then also
\mathrm{C}
is an MSRD code as well. Moreover,

W_{r}(\C)=W_{r}(\mathrm{C})=W_{r}(n^{\prime},m^{\prime},t),

for each
r
, where
m^{\prime}=\max\{m,n\}
,
n^{\prime}=\min\{m,n\}

2.13
, we have that

∎

3.
MSRD codes and subspace designs

In this section, we will use the geometric point of view described in the above section to give a characterization of the generalized weights of a sum-rank metric code via the associated system. Consequently, we will use relations on generalized sum-rank weights to estabilish
a correspondence between MSRD codes and systems having special properties of intersection with respect to subspaces of fixed dimension, i.e.
s
-designs.

3.1.
Subspace designs

The notion of subspace design was first given by Guruswami and Xing in
[
15
]
. Subspace designs with good parameters were very useful in order to construct the first explicit families of rank metric codes and subspace codes which are efficiently list-decodable, see also
[
14
]
. Moreover, these objects were later used also to obtain
algebraic-geometric codes efficiently list-decodable
[
17
]
and to give explicit constant degree dimension expanders over large fields
[
16
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

An ordered set
\mathcal{U}=({\mathcal{U}}_{1},{\mathcal{U}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
, where
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V=V(k,q^{m})
, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, is called a
(non-degenerate)
(h,r)
-subspace design
in
V
if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\langle{\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V
and

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{i}\cap W)\leq r,

for every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W\subseteq V
of dimension
h
.

Note that, when
t=1
, the notion of
(s,A)
-subspace designs coincide with that of
(s,A)
-evasive subspaces. Evasive subspaces can be considered as the
q
-analogues of evasive sets originally introduced in
[
44
]
by Pudlák and Rödl in order to
construct explicit Ramsey graphs. Such subpaces with good parameters were also later constructed and studied in
[
12
]
and
[
3
]
and applied in several contexts, see e.g.
[
16
,
15
]
.

In the next, we recall some main properties of a subspace design.

Proposition 3.2

(see
[
46
, Proposition 1.3, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

If
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
is an
(h,r)
-subspace design in

, then

(1)

r\geq h
;

(2)

({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
is also an
(h-h^{\prime},r-h^{\prime})
-subspace design for any
h^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}
.

When
r
is the smallest possible, i.e.
r=h
, an
(h,h)
-subspace design in
V
is also called to be an
h
-
design
in
V
. These objects were introduced and studied in
[
46
]
.

When
V=V(k,q^{m})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
{\mathcal{U}}=({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
is
(h,r)
-subspace design (or
h
-design) in
V

(h,r)
-subspace design (or
h
-design)

In the case that

Scattered subspaces were first introduced by Blokhuis and Lavrauw in
[

]
for

[

]
and by Sheekey and Van de Voorde in

Remark 3.3
.

By Proposition

Moreover, gluing together subspace designs we are able to get subspace designs in a bigger space as following.

(see

)

Let

(see

3.2.
Generalized weights

Let
\C
be an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code and
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times N}
be an its generator matrix, with
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
. Let denote by
(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})_{j}
the
j
-th column of
(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
. The elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
can be naturally seen as long vectors in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
. In this way, by considering
\C
also as a subset in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
and we can endow it with the Hamming metric.

We recall that the
r
-th generalized Hamming weight
of
\C
is defined as

(3)

\begin{array}[]{rl}d_{r}^{H}(\C)&:=\min\left\{N-\left\lvert\{j:(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\}\right\rvert\colon\mathcal{H}\mbox{ is a }(k-r)\mbox{-dimensional }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}\\
&=N-\max\left\{\left\lvert\{j:(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\}\right\rvert\colon\mathcal{H}\mbox{ is a }(k-r)\mbox{-dimensional }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\},\end{array}

for each
r\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, see

[
51
, Theorem 1.1.14]
.

The notion of generalized weight for sum-rank metric was introduced in
[
34
]
.
Since the sum-rank metric extends both the Hamming and rank metrics, we can also see that generalized sum-rank weights specialize to
generalized Hamming weights
[
18
,
29
,
52
]
and generalized rank weights
[
20
,
39
]
when
n_{i}=1

and
t=1
, respectively.
In
[
34
, Section 4]
, several characterization of generalized sum-rank weight are given, and in this paper we will assume as definition one of these characterization
[
34
, Proposition 4]
that is more useful for our aims.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\C
be a
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. For each
r\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, the non negative integer

d_{r}^{srk}(\C):=\min\{d^{H}_{r}(\C\cdot A)\colon A=diag(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}),A_{i}\in\GL(n_{i},q),\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,t\}

is called the
r
-th generalized sum-rank weight
of
\C
.

Using the geometric description of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
, we are able to give a geometric characterization of generalized sum-rank weights as following.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})\in\Psi([\C])
. It holds that

\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm{srk}}_{r}(\C)=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\colon\mathcal{H}\mbox{ is a }(k-r)\mbox{-dimensional }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
G=(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})
be a generator matrix for
\C
such that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
is
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. If
A=diag(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}),
with
A_{i}\in\GL(n_{i},q),\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,t
, then a generator matrix for
\C\cdot A
is the matrix
(G_{1}A_{1}|\ldots|G_{t}A_{t})
. So, using (
3
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm{srk}}_{r}(\C)=&N-\max\{\lvert\{j:(G_{1}A_{1}|\ldots|G_{t}A_{t})_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\}\rvert\colon A_{i}\in\GL(n_{i},q),\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\mbox{and }\mathcal{H}\mbox{ is a }(k-r)\mbox{-dimensional }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\}\\
=&N-\max\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}\lvert\{j:(G_{i}A_{i})_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\}\rvert\colon A_{i}\in\GL(n_{i},q),\right.\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\left.\mbox{and }\mathcal{H}\mbox{ is a }(k-r)\mbox{-dimensional }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}\\
\end{array}

By noting that the
G_{i}A_{i}
’s , when
A_{i}
runs in
\GL(n_{i},q)
, give all the matrices whose columns is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, we get the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

When
t=1
, the above characterization of generalized sum-rank weights corresponds exactly to the one proved in
[
45
]
given for generalized rank weights.

Finally, we recall important properties of generalized sum-rank weights that we will use later. We start by giving the monotonicity property of generalized sum-rank weights.

Proposition 3.10

(see
[
34
, Lemma 4]
)
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then

d=d_{1}^{srk}(\C)<d_{2}^{srk}(\C)<\ldots<d_{k}^{srk}(\C)=N.

We also recall the Wei’s duality theorem for sum-rank metric codes that establish a relation between generalized sum-rank weight of a codes and of its dual.

Theorem 3.11

(see
[
34
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then it holds

\{1,2,\ldots,N\}=\{d_{i}^{srk}(\C)\colon 1\leq i\leq k\}\cup\{N+1-d_{i}^{srk}(\C^{\perp})\colon 1\leq i\leq N-k\},

where the union is disjoint and

. In particular, the generalized sum-rank weights of
\C
uniquely determine those of
\C^{\perp}
.

3.3.
Connection with MSRD codes

From Theorem
2.5
, we can rephrase the MSRD property of a code as geometric properties of the system.

Corollary 3.12

(see
[
38
, Corollary 3.10]
,
[
46
, Theorem 6.10]
)
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}])
.
\mathcal{C}
is an MSRD code with minimum distance

d=\begin{cases}N-k+1&\mbox{if }m\geq n_{1}\\
tm-\frac{mk}{n}+1&\mbox{ if }m<n=n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}\end{cases}

3.4.
Geometric dual of subspace design and sum-rank metric codes

We can recall also another notion of dual of a subspace design, i.e. the geometric dual subspace design.
For a nondegenerate reflexive sesquilinear form
\sigma\colon V\times V\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
on
V(k,q^{m})
we can consider

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sigma^{\prime}:&V\times V&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
&(x,y)&\longmapsto&\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\sigma(x,y)).\end{array}

It results that
\sigma^{\prime}
is a nondegenerate reflexive sesquilinear form on
V(k,q^{m})=V(km,q)
seen as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space of dimension

. This allows to consider
\perp
and
\perp^{\prime}
as the orthogonal complement maps defined by
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, respectively. Moreover, it can be proved that for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
V
holds
W^{\perp^{\prime}}=W^{\perp}
.
This leads to the following property. For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
W
of
V
and an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

of

(5)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap W^{\perp})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W).

For more details see
[
50
, section 7]
and
[
42
, Property 2.6]
.

Let consider an ordered set
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in

.
The ordered set

will be called the

(w.r.t.

Furthermore, note that the dual subspaces of
({\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\perp^{\prime}},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t}^{\perp^{\prime}})
coincide with the ordered set
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
.

From Equation (
5
), one can get the following result.

Theorem 3.21

(see
[
46
, Theorem 7.1]
)
.

Suppose that
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
is an
(h,r)
-subspace design in

({\mathcal{U}}_{1}^{\perp^{\prime}},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t}^{\perp^{\prime}})

Note that if

4.1.
Scattered linear sets.

First, we recall the projective version of the system, i.e. linear sets.
Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space. By
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
we mean the projective space defined by
V
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. For
{\mathcal{U}}
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
V
of dimension
n
, the set of points

L_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\{\langle{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}:{u}\in{\mathcal{U}}\setminus\{{0}\}\}\subseteq\Lambda

is called an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-
linear set of rank
n
. Note that the rank of a linear set
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}

is defined by the underlying subspace
{\mathcal{U}}
that defines it. So, we have to keep in mind both the set of points defined by
U
and the subspace
{\mathcal{U}}
.

The
weight
of a subspace
\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})\subseteq\Lambda
in
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is defined as

w_{L_{U}}(\mathcal{S})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}\cap W).

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
of rank
n
we have that

|L_{{\mathcal{U}}}|\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}.

When
|L_{{\mathcal{U}}}|
satisfies the equality,
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is called
1-scattered
(or simply
scattered
), or equivalently, if all the points of
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}
have weight one, i.e.

w_{L_{{\mathcal{U}}}}(P)\leq 1,

for every point
P\in\Lambda
.
Note that
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is scattered if and only if
{\mathcal{U}}
is
(1,1)_{q}
-evasive subspace in
V
, that is a
1
-scattered subspace without the assumption that it spans the entire space.
Finally, a canonical subgeometry is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{{\mathcal{U}}}
of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
having rank
k
and such that
\langle{\mathcal{U}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V
.
In particular, canonical subgeometries are scattered linear sets of rank

\mu_{i}f_{s,a,b}(x)^{q^{2m}}y+\mu_{j}f_{s,a,b}(y)x^{q^{2m}}-\mu_{j}f_{s,a,b}(y)^{q^{2m}}x-\mu_{i}f_{s,a,b}(x)y^{q^{2m}}=(xy^{q^{2m}}-x^{q^{2m}}y)B.

Recalling that
f_{s,a,b}(x)=ax^{q^{s}}+b{x^{q^{2m+s}}}
, (
12
) reads like

G(w):=\mu_{i}\mu_{j}(b^{q^{2m}+1}w^{q^{2m+s}}-b^{q^{2m}}aw^{q^{m+s}}+a^{q^{2m}+1}w^{q^{s}})+Aw=0,

where
w=xy^{q^{2m}}-x^{q^{2m}}y
.
Arguing as in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 2.7]
for the case
\mu_{i}=\mu_{j}=1
, we get that
w=0
.
This implies that
x=\alpha y
, for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Putting
x=\alpha y
and
f_{s,a,b}(x)=ax^{q^{s}}+b^{x^{q^{2m+s}}}
in (
13
), and observing that
f_{s,a,b}(\alpha y)=\alpha^{q^{s}}f_{s,a,b}(y)
, we have

(\alpha^{q^{s}}\mu_{i}-\alpha\mu_{j})(f_{s,a,b}(y)^{q^{2m}}y-f_{s,a,b}(y)y^{q^{2m}})=0.

If
f_{s,a,b}(y)^{q^{2m}}y-f_{s,a,b}(y)y^{q^{2m}}=0
, we get that
f_{s,a,b}(y)/y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
\alpha^{q^{s}}\mu_{i}-\alpha\mu_{j}=0
, i.e.
\alpha^{q^{s}-1}=\mu_{j}/\mu_{i}
and so again

1=N_{q^{m}/q}(\alpha^{q^{s}-1})=N_{q^{m}/q}\left(\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{i}}\right),

a contradiction, to the fact that the
\mu_{i}
’s have pairwise distinct norm.
∎

Finite
presemifields
are division algebras with a finite number of elements in which multiplication is not assumed to be associative. If a presemifield contains a multiplicative identity, then
it is called a
semifield
.
It is possible to identify the elements of a

-dimensional semifield over

with the elements of the
field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. We refer to
[
22
]
, for details on semifields; in particular the operations of isotopy and Knuth-equivalence, which preserve the property of having no non-trivial zero divisors. Two multiplications
S
and
S^{\prime}
are

if there exist invertible additive maps
f,g,h
such that
S^{\prime}(x,y)=f(S(g(x),h(y))
. The Knuth operations arise from the following action of the symmetric group
S_{3}
. Given an element
\pi\in S_{3}
and a multiplication
S(x,y)
, the multiplication
S^{\pi}(x,y)
is defined as one satisfying

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{6}/q}(S^{\pi}(x_{1},x_{2})x_{3})=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{6}/q}(S(x_{\pi(1)},x_{\pi(2)})x_{\pi(3)}).

Two multiplications which are equivalent under a combination of these actions are said to be
Knuth equivalent
.

We start with a useful lemma. Let us denote

U_{a}=\{ax^{q^{3}}+x\omega\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{6}}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{12}}.

Lemma 4.13
.

The linear sets
L_{U_{a}}
and
L_{U_{b}}
are disjoint if and only if there are no solutions to the system
S(x,y)=T(x,y)=0
,
xy\neq 0
, where

\displaystyle S(x,y)

\displaystyle:=(xy^{q^{4}}-x^{q^{4}}y)A-(a^{q^{4}}bx^{q}y^{q^{3}}-ab^{q^{4}}x^{q^{3}}y^{q})

\displaystyle T(x,y)

\displaystyle:=(xy^{q^{4}}-x^{q^{4}}y)B-(a^{q^{4}}x^{q}y-ax^{q^{3}}y^{q^{4}})-(bx^{q^{4}}y^{q^{3}}-b^{q^{4}}xy^{q}).

Proof.

The proof is completely analogous to the proof of Theorem
4.8
up to equations (
8
) and (
9
).
∎

Theorem 4.14
.

There exists a set of
2(q-1)
pairwise disjoint maximum scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{4})
for any

odd with
q\equiv~1~\hbox{{\rm mod}\,}3
.

Proof.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{6}}
be such that
N_{q^{6}/q^{3}}(a)\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and
b
such that
a^{q^{3}}b=\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
. We claim that
L_{U_{a}}
and
L_{U_{b}}
are disjoint. Let
S(x,y)
be as in Lemma
4.13
. First we suppose that
\mu^{q+1}\neq A^{q+1}
, and show that there are no non-trivial solutions to
S(x,y)=0
, and hence
L_{U_{a}}
and
L_{U_{b}}
are disjoint.

Note that
S(x,y)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-bilinear map, and so we are claiming that
S(x,y)
defines the multiplication of a (pre)semifield.
A straightforward calculation verifies that

S^{(23)}(x,y)=A(x^{q^{2}}y^{q^{2}}-x^{q^{4}}y)-\mu(a^{q-1}x^{q^{4}}y^{q^{3}}-a^{q^{5}-1}x^{q^{2}}y^{q^{5}}),

Thus
S(x,y)
is Knuth-equivalent to

S_{1}(x,y):=S^{(23)}(x^{q^{4}},y)=(Ay+\mu a^{q^{3}-q^{4}}y^{q^{3}})^{q^{2}}x-(Ay+\mu a^{q-1}y^{q^{3}})x^{q^{2}},

which defines a presemifield if and only if

(Ay+\mu a^{q^{3}-q^{4}}y^{q^{3}})^{q^{4}+q^{2}+1}-(Ay+\mu a^{q-1}y^{q^{3}})^{q^{4}+q^{2}+1}\neq 0\quad\forall~y\neq 0.

This condition holds if and only if the multiplication

\displaystyle S_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=(Ay+\mu a^{q^{3}-q^{4}}y^{q^{3}})x-(Ay+\mu a^{q-1}y^{q^{3}})x^{q^{2}}

\displaystyle=A(x-x^{q^{2}})y+\mu(a^{q^{3}-q^{4}}x-a^{q-1}x^{q^{2}})y^{q^{3}}

defines a presemifield. This multiplication is in turn isotopic to

\displaystyle S_{3}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{\mu^{-1}a^{q^{4}-q^{3}}}{1-a^{(q^{3}+1)(q-1)}}\right)S_{2}(y,x-\mu A^{-1}a^{q-1}x^{q^{3}})

\displaystyle=(\alpha y+\beta y^{q^{2}})x+yx^{q^{3}},

where

\alpha=\frac{a^{q^{4}-q^{3}}(A^{q+1}-\mu^{q+1})}{\mu A^{q}(1-a^{(q^{3}+1)(q-1)})};\quad\beta=\frac{a^{q^{4}-q^{3}}(\mu^{q+1}a^{(q^{3}+1)(q-1)}-A^{q+1})}{\mu A^{q}(1-a^{(q^{3}+1)(q-1)})}.

This is an algebra of the form
\mathbb{S}(\alpha,0,\beta,0,q^{2})
defined in
[
32
]
and studied further in
[
21

is odd and

Note that, if

(14)

By the constructions in Subsection

, we also get two-intersection sets, strongly regular graphs and two-weight linear codes with parameters described in the above corollaries for any
t\leq 2(q-1)
,
m=4
and

, as well as some sporadic larger values of
t
for some small

,

,
m=4
,

, we obtain an
\mathrm{srg}(4096,1890,874,870)

6.
Constructions and existence of MSRD code and
h
-design

In this section, we will explore the consequences of the previous sections for the existence of
h
-design and MSRD codes.

Considering a system associated to a Linerized Reed-Solomon code and Twisted Linearized Reed-Solomon code as already proved in
[
46
,
38
]
, we get the following construction of
(k-1)
-designs.

Theorem 6.1

(see
[
46
, Theorem 5.2]

[
38
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and suppose that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{i})\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{j})
if
i\neq j
. Let
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
with the property that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\eta)(-1)^{km}\notin G
, with
G
the multiplicative subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
generated by
\{\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{t})\}
. Let
S
be an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. If
k\leq tn
then
the ordered set of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
, where

{\mathcal{U}}_{i}=\{(x+\eta\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{k}(\mu_{i})\sigma^{k}(x),\sigma(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{1}(\mu_{i}),\sigma^{2}(x)N_{\sigma}^{2}(\mu_{i}),\ldots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{k-1}(\mu_{i}))\colon x\in S\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

is
(k-1)
-design
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
-system, with
\mathbf{n}=(n,\ldots,n)
. If
n=m
, then it is a maximum
(k-1)
-design.

Remark 6.2
.

Note that condition
(-1)^{km}\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\eta)\notin G
in Theorem
6.1
leads to a restriction on
t
. As a consequence, we can construct subspace designs with
\eta\neq 0
if
t
is not larger of the size of biggest proper subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, that is
(q-1)/r^{\prime}
, where
r^{\prime}
is the smallest prime diving
q-1
.
If
G={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, the only possibly choice for
\eta
is
\eta=0
and in this case we can go until
t=q-1
, see also
[
37
, Proposition 6.8]
.

It is possible construct a
(k-1)
-design having a number of blocks greater than
q-1
in the following way.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and suppose that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{i})\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\mu_{j})
if
i\neq j
. Let
S
be an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and
S_{j_{i},i}\neq\{0\}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
S
such that

S=S_{1,i}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{j_{i},i},

for each
i
.
If
k\leq tn
then
the ordered set of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
({\mathcal{U}}_{1,1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{j_{1},1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{1,t},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{j_{t},t})
, where

{\mathcal{U}}_{l,i}=\{(x,\sigma(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{1}(\mu_{i}),\sigma^{2}(x)N_{\sigma}^{2}(\mu_{i}),\ldots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{k-1}(\mu_{i}))\colon x\in S_{l,i}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

is
(k-1)
-design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

Proof.

Consider

{\mathcal{U}}_{i}=\{(x,\sigma(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{1}(\mu_{i}),\sigma^{2}(x)N_{\sigma}^{2}(\mu_{i}),\ldots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)\mathrm{N}_{\sigma}^{k-1}(\mu_{i}))\colon x\in S\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}.

By Theorem
6.1
, we know that
({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
is a
(k-1)
-design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Since

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{1,i}\cap H)+\ldots+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{j_{i},i}\cap H)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{i}\cap H),

for each
i
and any hyperplane
H
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, it follows that also the
U_{l,i}^{\prime}s
form a
(k-1)
-design in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Finally, since

\langle{\mathcal{U}}_{1,1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{j_{t},t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\langle{\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k},

we have the assertion.
∎

Since when
m\geq n_{i}
, optimal subspace designs correspond to
(k-1)
-design (cf. Corollary
3.17
) using Proposition
6.3
, we get the following result.

Corollary 6.4
.

There exists an MSRD
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
m\geq n_{i}
, for any
k<\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}\leq(q-1)m
.

Recalling that the Delsarte dual system
{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp}
of a system
{\mathcal{U}}
is the system associated to a code
\C\in\Phi([{\mathcal{U}}])
. So from Theorem
3.16
we immediately get the following geometric property on
{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp}
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system with
d\geq 2
. Then
\mathcal{U}
is an
(h,r)
-subspace design if an only if an its Delsarte dual
[\mathbf{n},N-k]_{q^{m}/q}
system
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
is an
(N-k-r+h-1,N-r-2)
-design.

Another consequence of Theorem
3.16
, is that a Delsarte dual of a system that is a maximum
h
-design is also an optimal
h^{\prime}
-design as well.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be a maximum
h
-design
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system, with
n=\frac{mk}{h+1}
. Then any Delsarte dual
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
of
\mathcal{U}
is an optimal
(tm-h-2)
-design
[\mathbf{n},N-k]_{q^{m}/q}
system.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
is a maximum
h
-design
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system, by Theorem
3.16
any code
\C\in\Psi([\mathcal{U}])
is an MSRD
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code and so any
\C^{\perp}
is an MSRD
[\mathbf{n},N-k]_{q^{m}/q}
code as well. Therefore, by applying Theorem
3.16
to
\C^{\perp}
, we have that any Delsarte dual
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
of
\mathcal{U}
is an optimal
h^{\prime}
-design
[\mathbf{n},N-k]_{q^{m}/q}
system, with
h^{\prime}
such that
\frac{mk}{h+1}=\frac{m(N-k)}{h^{\prime}+1}
and hence the assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 6.7

(see
[
46
, Theorem 5.8]
)
.

If
h+1
divides
k
,

and

, then there exists a maximum

-design
\left[\left(\frac{mk}{h+1},\ldots,\frac{mk}{h+1}\right),k\right]_{q^{m}/q}

This existence result was the only known construction for

. Hence the following theorem provides new examples.

Theorem 6.8
.

For
m\geq 4
even,
k\geq 3
odd and

, there exist a optimal
(tm-3)
-design
\left[(\frac{km}{2},\ldots,\frac{km}{2}),\frac{k(tm-2)}{2}\right]_{q^{m}/q}

6.7
.

Proof.

For
m\geq 4
even and
k\geq 3
odd, by Theorem
4.11
, there exists
{\mathcal{U}}
a maximum

-design
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system. Let consider a Delsarte dual system
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
of
{\mathcal{U}}
. By Corollary
6.6
, for

, we get that
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
is a maximum
(tm-3)
-design. Moreover, since
tm-2
does not divide

then this system cannot be obtained by by Theorem

∎

From Theorem
4.11
and Theorem
3.16

Theorem 6.9
.

For any positive integer

, such that

[(mk/2,\ldots,mk/2),k]_{q^{m}/q}

Theorem 6.10

For any positive integer

Problem 6.11

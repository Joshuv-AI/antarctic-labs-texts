Uniform Stability for a Spatially-Discrete, Subdiffusive Fokker–Planck Equation Thanks: The support of KFUPM through the project No. SB191003 is gratefully acknowledged.
William McLean, Kassem Mustapha

Abstract
We prove stability estimates for the spatially discrete, Galerkin solution of a fractional Fokker–Planck equation, improving on previous results in several respects. Our main goal is to establish that the stability constants are bounded uniformly in the fractional diffusion exponent α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in(0,1] . In addition, we account for the presence of an inhomogeneous term and show a stability estimate for the gradient of the Galerkin solution. As a by-product, the proofs of error bounds for a standard finite element approximation are simplified.

Introduction

We consider the stability of semidiscrete Galerkin methods for the
time-fractional Fokker–Planck
equation
[
1
,
2
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\nabla\cdot\bigl(\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u-\vec{F}\,\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}u\bigr)

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{for $\vec{x}\in\Omega$ and $0<t\leq T$,}

(1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u_{0}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle\text{for $\vec{x}\in\Omega$ when $t=0$,}

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{for $\vec{x}\in\partial\Omega$ and $0<t\leq T$.}

Here,
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
), the
fractional exponent satisfies
0<\alpha\leq 1
and the fractional time
derivative is understood in the Riemann–Liouville sense:

\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}=\partial_{t}\,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}
where the fractional
integration operator
\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}
is defined as usual
in (
9
) below. The diffusivity
\kappa\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
is
assumed independent of time, positive and bounded below:

\kappa(\vec{x})\geq\kappa_{\min}>0
for
\vec{x}\in\Omega
.
The forcing vector
\vec{F}
may depend both on
\vec{x}
and
t
, and we assume
that
\vec{F}
,
\partial_{t}\vec{F}
,
\nabla\cdot\vec{F}
and

\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}\vec{F}
are bounded on
\Omega\times[0,T]
. Note
that if
\alpha=1
then
\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\phi=\phi
so the governing
equation in (
1
) reduces to a classical Fokker–Planck equation.

If
\vec{F}
is independent of
t
, then by applying
\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}
to
both sides of the governing equation we find that (
1
) is
equivalent to

{}^{\mathrm{C}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\nabla\cdot\bigl(\kappa\nabla u-\vec{F}u\bigr)=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}g,

(2)

where
{}^{\mathrm{C}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}u
is
the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
. In this form, numerous
authors have studied the numerical solution of the problem, mostly for a 1D
spatial domain
\Omega=(0,L)
and with
g\equiv 0
. For instance,
Deng
[
4
]
considered the method of lines, Jiang and
Xu
[
8
]
proposed a finite volume method, Yang et
al.
[
21
]
a spectral collocation method, and Duong and
Jin
[
5
]
a Wasserstein gradient flow formulation.

For both continuous and discrete solutions to fractional PDEs, it is natural to
expect stability constants to remain bounded as
\alpha\to 1
if the limiting
classical problem is stable. In applications, the value of
\alpha
is
typically estimated from measurements, and this process might be treated as
an inverse problem. It would then be desirable that simulations of the
forward problem are uniformly stable in
\alpha
, particularly if the diffusion
turns out to be classical (
\alpha=1
) or only slightly subdiffusive (
\alpha

close to
1
). Perhaps for these reasons, interest in the question of

\alpha
-uniform stability and convergence seems to be growing. Note that
growth in the stability constant as
\alpha\to 0
is of less concern, since
very small values of
\alpha
have not been observed in real physical systems.

In the special case
\vec{F}\equiv\vec{0}
of fractional diffusion,
Chen and Stynes
[
3
]
showed that as
\alpha
tends to
1

the solution of (
2
) tends to the solution of the classical
diffusion problem, uniformly in
x
and
t
. They also discussed several
examples of numerical schemes for which the error analysis leads to constants
that remain bounded as
\alpha\to 1
(said to be
\alpha
-robust bounds), as
well as several for which the constants blow up (
\alpha
-nonrobust bounds).
Recent examples in the former category include Jin et
al.
[
9
, see Remark 4]
, Huang et
al.
[
6
, see Section 5]
and
Mustapha
[
17
, Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.5]
.

We work with the weak solution
u:(0,T]\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1
)
characterized by

\langle u^{\prime},v\rangle+\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-\langle\vec{F}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}u,\nabla v\rangle=\langle g,v\rangle\quad\text{for $v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$}

(3)

and
0<t\leq T
, with
u(0)=u_{0}
, where
u^{\prime}=\partial_{t}u
,

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}uv
and

\langle\vec{u},\vec{v}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}
.
Strictly speaking, to allow minimal assumptions on the regularity of the data

u_{0}
and
g
, we define the solution
u
by requiring that it satisfy the
time-integrated equation

\langle u,v\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\kappa\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle-\bigl\langle\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u,\nabla v\bigr\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle,

(4)

where

(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\vec{F}\,\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\phi\bigr)(s)\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad f(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds.

(5)

In previous work, we have established that this problem is well-posed

[
12
,
16
]
.

For a fixed, finite dimensional subspace
\mathbb{X}\subseteq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
the semidiscrete Galerkin solution
u_{\mathbb{X}}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{X}
is given by

\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\rangle+\bigl\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\kappa\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\langle f_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\rangle\quad\text{for $\chi\in\mathbb{X}$},

(6)

with
f_{\mathbb{X}}(t)=u_{0\mathbb{X}}+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds
and with

u_{\mathbb{X}}(0)=u_{0\mathbb{X}}\in\mathbb{X}
a suitable approximation to
u_{0}
.
Previously, we studied this problem in the particular case
when
\mathbb{X}=S_{h}
is a space of continuous, piecewise-linear finite element
functions corresponding to a conforming triangulation of
\Omega
with maximum
element size
h>0
. We showed that the Galerkin finite element
solution
u_{h}(t)
is stable in the norm of
L_{2}(\Omega)
when
g(t)\equiv 0
,
satisfying the bound
[
10
, Theorem 4.5]

\|u_{h}(t)\|\leq C_{\alpha}\|u_{0h}\|\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$ and $0<\alpha<1$,}

(7)

where
u_{h}(0)=u_{0h}\in S_{h}
approximates
u_{0}
. The method of proof relied on
estimates for fractional integrals
[
10
, Lemmas 3.2–3.4]

involving powers of
(1-\alpha)^{-1}
, leading to a stability
constant
C_{\alpha}
that blows up as
\alpha\to 1
. However, in the limiting
case
\alpha=1
the semidiscrete finite element method is easily seen to be
stable
[
10
, Remark 4.7]
, that is,
(
7
) holds with
C_{1}<\infty
. In the absence of
forcing, that is, in the simple case
\vec{F}\equiv\vec{0}
of fractional
diffusion, the stability constant equals one:
\|u_{h}(t)\|\leq\|u_{0h}\|

for
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

Our primary aim in what follows is to improve the results of our earlier
paper
[
10
]
via a new analysis
of (
6
) that yields a uniform stability constant
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
, as well as allowing a non-zero source term
g
. Recently,
Huang et al.
[
6
]
have addressed the same question using a
different analysis that requires
1/2<\alpha\leq 1
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
with a stability constant that blows up as
\alpha\to 1/2
.

Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a generic constant that may depend of
T
,

\Omega
,
\kappa
and
\vec{F}
. Dependence on any other parameters will be
shown explicitly, and in particular, we write
C_{\alpha}
to show that the
constant may also depend on
\alpha
.
After citing some technical lemmas in
Section

2
, we
present the stability proof in
Section

3
, stating our main
result as
Theorem

3.4
. Using similar arguments,

Section

4
establishes an estimate for the gradient of
u_{\mathbb{X}}

in
Theorem

4.4
. Combining these results gives, for

0\leq t\leq T
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|+t^{\alpha/2}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)+C\biggl(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|sg(s)\|^{2}\,ds\biggr)^{1/2}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq C\biggl(\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|+t^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds\biggr).

(Here, the second bound follows from the first by

Lemma

3.5
with
\eta=1
.) At the end of

Section

4
, in
Remark

4.5
, we note that
the exact solution
u
has the same uniform stability property as the
semidiscrete Galerkin approximation
u_{\mathbb{X}}
, that is, (
8
)
holds with
u
and
u_{0}
replacing
u_{\mathbb{X}}
and
u_{0\mathbb{X}}
. Also, in

Remark

4.6
, we discuss briefly the implications of

).
Section

5
applies our new
stability analysis to the piecewise linear Galerkin finite element

, showing in
Theorem

that

\|u_{h}-u\|=O(t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2}h^{2})
and

\|\nabla u_{h}-\nabla u\|=O(t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2}h)
. These error bounds rely on

. Finally,

considers the time

2
Preliminaries

This brief section introduces notations and gathers together results
from the literature that we will use in our subsequent analysis.
Denote the fractional integral operator of order
\mu>0
by

(\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\mu}(t-s)\phi(s)\,ds\quad\text{for $t>0$, where $\omega_{\mu}(t)=\frac{t^{\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)}$,}

(9)

with
\mathcal{I}^{0}\phi=\phi
, and observe that

(\mathcal{I}^{1}\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\,ds
. If we denote the Laplace
transform of
\phi
by
\hat{\phi}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}\phi(t)\,dt
then

(\widehat{\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi})(z)=\hat{\omega}_{\mu}(z)\hat{\phi}(z)
and

\hat{\omega}_{\mu}(z)=z^{-\mu}
. Hence, assuming
\phi
is real-valued with (say)
compact support in
[0,\infty)
, we find that

\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\phi,\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi\bigr\rangle\,ds=\frac{\pi\mu/2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{-\mu}\|\hat{\phi}(iy)\|^{2}\,dy\geq 0\quad\text{if $0<\mu<1$.}

(10)

Our analysis relies on
properties of
\mathcal{I}^{\mu}
stated in the next three lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
0\leq\mu\leq\nu\leq 1
, then for
t>0
and

\phi\in L_{2}\bigl((0,t),L_{2}(\Omega)\bigr)
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|(\mathcal{I}^{\nu}\phi)(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2t^{2(\nu-\mu)}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi)(s)\|^{2}\,ds.

Proof.

See Le et al.
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
0<\mu\leq 1
, then for
t>0
and
\phi\in L_{2}\bigl((0,t),L_{2}(\Omega)\bigr)
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|(\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi)(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\mu}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\langle\phi(q),(\mathcal{I}^{\mu}\phi)(q)\bigr\rangle\,dq.

Proof.

See McLean et al.
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0\leq\mu<\nu\leq 1
. If
\phi:[0,T]\to L_{2}(\Omega)
is continuous with

\phi(0)=0
, and if its restriction to
(0,T]
is differentiable
with
\|\phi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ct^{-\mu}
for
0<t\leq T
, then

\|\phi(t)\|^{2}\leq 2\omega_{2-\nu}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\phi^{\prime}(s),(\mathcal{I}^{\nu}\phi^{\prime})(s)\bigr\rangle\,ds.

Proof.

See McLean et al.
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
.
∎

We will also require the following fractional Gronwall inequality involving
the Mittag-Leffler
function
E_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}/\Gamma(1+n\mu)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu>0
and assume that
a
and
b
are non-negative and non-decreasing
functions on the interval
[0,T]
. If
y:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is an integrable
function satisfying

0\leq y(t)\leq a(t)+b(t)\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\mu}(t-s)y(s)\,ds\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$,}

then

y(t)\leq a(t)E_{\mu}\bigl(b(t)\,t^{\mu}\bigr)\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$.}

Proof.

See Dixon and McKee
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
and
Ye, Gao and Ding
[
22
, Corollary 2]
.
∎

We recall the definition of the linear operator
\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
in (
5
),
and introduce two other linear operators
\mathcal{M}
and
\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
, defined by

(\mathcal{M}\phi)(t)=t\,\phi(t)\quad\quad\text{and}\qquad\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\phi=\bigl(\mathcal{M}\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi\bigr)^{\prime}.

With the help of the identity

\mathcal{M}\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}-\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}=\alpha\mathcal{I}^{\alpha+1}

(11)

and using our assumption that
\vec{F}
and
\vec{F}^{\prime}
are bounded, one can show
the following technical estimates.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mu>0
. If
\phi:[0,T]\to L_{2}(\Omega)
is continuous, and if its
restriction to
(0,T]
is differentiable with
\|\phi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ct^{\mu-1}

for
0<t\leq T
, then

\int_{0}^{t}\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}\,ds

and

\int_{0}^{t}\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\phi\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\phi)^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

The estimate for
\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
was proved by Le et
al.
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
, who also proved the bound for

\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
(denoted there by
B_{3}
) but with the extra term
term
C\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\phi\|^{2}\,ds
. However, we
may omit this extra term because it is bounded by

Ct^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}\,ds
, as one sees from

Lemma

2.1
and (
11
).
∎

3
Stability analysis

To simplify the error analysis of
Section

5
we will include an
additional term on the right-hand side of (
6
) and
study the stability of
u_{\mathbb{X}}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{X}
satisfying

\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\langle f_{1},\chi\rangle+\langle\vec{f}_{2},\nabla\chi\rangle\quad\text{for $\chi\in\mathbb{X}$,}

(12)

with
u_{\mathbb{X}}(0)=u_{0\mathbb{X}}
; the semidiscrete Galerkin solution is then given by the
special case
f_{1}=f_{\mathbb{X}}
and
\vec{f}_{2}=\vec{0}
. Our goal is to bound

\|u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|
pointwise in
t
, and for this purpose our overall strategy is to
apply
Lemma

2.3
with
\phi=\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}}
, which in turn
requires an estimate for
\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\,ds
. The technical details are worked out in

Lemmas

3.1
and
3.2
below, and the stability
estimate itself is then obtained in
Lemma

3.3

for (
12
), and in
Theorem

3.4
for the
original semidiscrete Galerkin problem (
6
).

Our method of proof begins by multiplying both sides of (
12
)
by
t
and using the identity (
11
) to obtain

\langle\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})+\alpha\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha+1}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}-\mathcal{M}\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\langle\mathcal{M}f_{1},\chi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2},\nabla\chi\rangle.

Differentiating this equation with respect to time then yields

\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\chi\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})+\alpha\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}-\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle\\
=\langle(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime},\chi\rangle+\langle(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime},\nabla\chi\rangle.

(13)

Lemma 3.1
.

For
i\in\{0,1\}
and
0\leq t\leq T
, the solution of (
12
)
satisfies

\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\bigl\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}^{i}u_{\mathbb{X}})\bigr\|^{2}+t^{\alpha}\bigl\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\nabla\mathcal{M}^{i}u_{\mathbb{X}})\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\leq Ct^{2(\alpha+i)}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+t^{-\alpha}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Choose
\chi=(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)
in (
12
) so that

\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle+\kappa_{\min}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\leq\bigl\langle\vec{f}_{2}+\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr\rangle+\langle f_{1},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle\\
\leq\kappa_{\min}^{-1}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\min}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|f_{1}\|\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|.

After cancelling
\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\min}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}
,
integrating in time and applying
Lemma

2.5
, we deduce that

\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\min}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\\
\leq C_{0}t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\kappa_{\min}^{-1}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

(14)

where
C_{0}
is a fixed constant depending on
T
,
\kappa_{\min}

and
\vec{F}
. Apply
\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}
to both sides of (
12
)
and choose
\chi=\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)
to obtain, for any
\eta>0
,

\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}),\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{f}_{2}+\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}}),\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}f_{1},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle\\
\leq\eta\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}f_{1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}.

Simplifying, integrating in time, and noting

\int_{0}^{t}\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}),\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle\,ds\geq 0
by (
10
),
we observe that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}f_{1}\|^{2}\,ds+2\eta\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds.

By
Lemmas

2.1
and
2.5
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq Ct^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds,

and so, again with the help of
Lemma

2.1
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+2\eta\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\eta t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\\
+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds.

(15)

However, by (
14
),

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq\frac{2C_{0}}{\kappa_{\min}}\,t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

and by
Lemma

2.2
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\langle u_{\mathbb{X}}(q),(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}})(q)\bigr\rangle\,dq\,ds\,.

(16)

Inserting these two estimates in the right-hand side of (
15
)
and choosing
\eta=4C_{0}t^{-\alpha}/\kappa_{\min}
yields

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr\rangle\,dq\,ds.

(17)

Let

y(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\langle u_{\mathbb{X}},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\rangle+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds
,
and deduce from (
14
) and (
17
) that

y(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)y(s)\,ds.

(18)

It follows by
Lemma

2.4
that, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

y(t)\leq CE_{\alpha}\bigl(Ct^{\alpha}\bigr)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

(19)

Together, (
16
) and (
19
) imply

\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+t^{\alpha}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)y(s)\,ds,

and using (
19
) a second time gives

\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)y(s)\,ds\leq C\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\,ds\biggr)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

completing the proof for the case
i=0
.

The identity (
11
) implies that

\|(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)\|^{2}\leq 2t^{2}\|(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)\|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\|(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha+1}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)\|^{2}

so, using
Lemma

2.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2t^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds+4\alpha^{2}t^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 6t^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 6t^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds,

so the case
i=1
follows from the already proven case
i=0
.
∎

The next lemma makes use of the identity

(\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\phi)(t)=(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi)^{\prime}(t)=\phi(0)\omega_{\alpha}(t)+(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime})(t).

(20)

Lemma 3.2
.

For
0\leq t\leq T
, the solution of (
12
) satisfies

\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\\
\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Rewriting (
13
) as

\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\chi\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}),\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}+\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}-\alpha\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle+\langle(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime},\chi\rangle,

(21)

we note that the first term on the right is bounded by

\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\min}\|\nabla\chi\|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\kappa_{\min}^{-1}\bigl(\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr).

Choose
\chi=\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)=\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})\bigr)^{\prime}(t)
and note that,
since
(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})(0)=0
, the identity (
20
)
implies
\chi=\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}
so

\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\min}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\\
\leq\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|\,\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|+C\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+C\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+C\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}.

Thus, by
Lemma

2.5
,

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(t^{\alpha}\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad{}+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C_{0}t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds.

The second integral on the right is bounded
by
Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+t^{-\alpha}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds
via

Lemma

3.1
, giving

y(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+C_{0}t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds.

(22)

Now apply
\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}
to both sides
of (
21
), again with

\chi=\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})(t)=\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}(t)
, to conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr),\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|+\|\mathcal{I}^{2\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|\bigr)\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}.

After cancelling the last term on the right we have, for any
\eta>0
,

\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+2\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr),\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\\
\leq 3\eta\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{2}\|+\|\mathcal{I}^{2\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)+\eta^{-1}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Since the integral over
(0,t)
of the second term on the left is non-negative,
it follows using
Lemma

2.1
that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 6\eta t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\\
+\eta^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds+2t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds.

By
Lemmas

2.5
and
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+t^{-\alpha}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad{}+C\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds+C\bigl(t^{\alpha}+t^{2\alpha}\bigr)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+t^{-\alpha}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

and consequently,

C_{0}t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\eta t^{\alpha}\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+C\eta t^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\eta t^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds+\frac{C_{0}t^{-\alpha}}{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds.

Choosing
\eta=2C_{0}t^{-\alpha}
, we see from (

) that

y(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds.

The desired estimate follows after applying

2.2
to bound

Lemma

2.4
.
∎

.

2.3
, with

, to conclude that

.

The semidiscrete Galerkin solution, defined by (

),

\|u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|\leq C\biggl(\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)+C\biggl(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|sg(s)\|^{2}\,ds\biggr)^{1/2}

for

, where the stability constant
C
depends on
T
,
\Omega
,

\kappa
and
\vec{F}
, but not on

or the subspace
\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

Apply

3.3
with

and

,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f_{\mathbb{X}}(s)\|^{2}\leq\biggl(\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)^{2}

Lemma 3.5
.

For
0<t\leq T
and
0<\eta\leq 1
,

\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|sg(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq(1+\eta^{-1})t^{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\eta}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}s^{-(1-\eta)/2}s^{(1-\eta)/2}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}s^{\eta-1}\,ds\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\eta}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds=\frac{t^{\eta}}{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\eta}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds,

and furthermore,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|sg(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}s\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq t^{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\eta}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds.

∎

A strategy similar to the one used in

pointwise in

: we once again apply

Lemma

2.3
, this time with

. The key
result is stated as
Lemma

4.3
for the generalized
problem (
12
), and as

4.4
for the

). The proofs

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu>0
. If
\phi:[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega)
is continuous, and if its
restriction to
(0,T]
is differentiable with

\|\phi^{\prime}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq Ct^{\mu-1}
for
0<t\leq T
, then

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi)\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla\phi\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds

and

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\phi)\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\phi)^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla\phi)^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Integration by parts gives

(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi)(t)=\vec{F}\,(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi)(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\vec{F}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\,ds
, so

\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\phi)=(\nabla\cdot\vec{F})(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi)+\vec{F}\cdot(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla\phi)-\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl((\nabla\cdot\vec{F})^{\prime}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi)+\vec{F}^{\prime}\cdot(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla\phi)\Bigr)\,ds,

implying the first estimate. Furthermore,

\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\phi=\vec{F}^{\prime}\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\phi+\alpha\mathcal{I}^{\alpha+1}\phi\bigr)+\vec{F}\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\phi)^{\prime}+\alpha\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\bigr)-\mathcal{I}^{1}\bigl(\vec{F}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi\bigr)-\mathcal{M}\vec{F}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\phi,

which implies the second estimate.
∎

The next result builds on the estimates of
Lemmas

3.1
and
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The solution of (
12
) satisfies, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr)\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\\
+C\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Using the first Green identity, we deduce from (
21
) that

\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\chi\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}-(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}-\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}}),\chi\bigr\rangle\\
-\alpha\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle,

and from (
12
) that

\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle f_{1}-\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})-\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}-u_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\bigr\rangle
, so

\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\chi\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle\kappa(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle f_{3}+\alpha\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})-\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}})+\alpha u_{\mathbb{X}},\chi\bigr\rangle\\
\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\chi\|^{2}+2\bigl(\|f_{3}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}+\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr),

where
f_{3}=(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}-\alpha f_{1}-(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}+\alpha\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}
. Choose
\chi=(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}
, cancel the
term
\tfrac{1}{2}\|\chi\|^{2}
, and integrate in time to obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr)\,ds\leq CJ(t)+C\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{3}\|^{2}\,ds

(23)

where, by
Lemma

4.1
,

J(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds.

If we let
y(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\,ds
then, by
Lemma

2.2
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)y(s)\,ds\leq 2\omega_{\alpha+1}(t)\max_{0\leq s\leq t}y(s),

and so, using
Lemmas

3.1
and
3.2
,

J(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Since

\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{3}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 4\int_{0}^{t}(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2})\,ds,

the desired estimate now follows from (
23
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
0<t\leq T
,

t^{\alpha}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Since
t^{\alpha}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}=t^{\alpha-2}\|\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}
,

Lemmas

2.3
and
4.2
imply that

t^{\alpha}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\omega_{2-\alpha}(t)}{t^{2-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime},(\mathcal{M}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\,ds\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\\
+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds,

and it suffices to estimate
\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds
. Choose

\chi=u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)
in (
12
) and use the first Green identity to deduce
that

\displaystyle\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\bigl\langle\kappa\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle f_{1}-\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}-\nabla\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}},u_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr).

After cancelling
\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}
, integrating in time and using
(
10
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 3\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+3\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\,ds,

and by
Lemmas

4.1
and
3.1
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\cdot(\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}u_{\mathbb{X}})\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq Ct^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+t^{-\alpha}\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

which completes the proof.
∎

.

The semidiscrete Galerkin solution, defined by (

t^{\alpha/2}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|\leq C\biggl(\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|\,ds\biggr)+C\biggl(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|sg(s)\|^{2}\,ds\biggr)^{1/2}

for

, where the constant

depends on

,
\Omega
,
\kappa
and

\vec{F}
, but not on

or the subspace
\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

Choose

and

in

Remark 4.5

The uniform stability estimate (

Remark 4.6
.

By introducing a flux vector
\vec{Q}u=-\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u+\vec{F}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}u
we can write the fractional Fokker–Planck
equation (
1
) as a conservation law:

\partial_{t}u+\nabla\cdot\vec{Q}u=g
. It is then natural to consider a zero-flux
boundary condition,

\vec{n}\cdot\vec{Q}u=0\quad\text{for $\vec{x}\in\partial\Omega$ and $0<t\leq T$,}

(24)

where
\vec{n}
denotes the outward unit normal to
\Omega
. (Notice that this
boundary condition is non-local in time.) In this case, the weak
solution
u:(0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega)
is again characterized
by (
3
), and hence satisfies (
4
), but with
the test functions
v
now taken from the larger space
H^{1}(\Omega)
. We can
then choose a finite dimensional subspace
\mathbb{X}\subseteq H^{1}(\Omega)
and
again define the Galerkin solution
u_{\mathbb{X}}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{X}

by (
6
). The analysis of

Section

3
goes through with no change, and in
particular
u_{\mathbb{X}}
is again stable in
L_{2}(\Omega)
. However, the first step in
the proof of
Lemma

4.2
fails because boundary terms are
introduced if one integrates by parts in space, so our analysis no longer
yields a bound for
t^{\alpha/2}\|\nabla u_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|
.

5
Error estimates

We now decompose the error in the semidiscrete Galerkin solution as

u_{\mathbb{X}}-u=\theta_{\mathbb{X}}-\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\quad\text{where}\quad\theta_{\mathbb{X}}=u_{\mathbb{X}}-R_{\mathbb{X}}u\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\mathbb{X}}=u-R_{\mathbb{X}}u,

and where
R_{\mathbb{X}}
denotes the Ritz projector for the (stationary) elliptic problem

-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla v)+v=g\quad\text{in $\Omega$, with $v=0$ on~$\partial\Omega$.}

(25)

Thus,
R_{\mathbb{X}}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{X}
satisfies

\langle\kappa\nabla R_{\mathbb{X}}v,\nabla\chi\rangle+\langle R_{\mathbb{X}}v,\chi\rangle=\langle\kappa\nabla v,\nabla\chi\rangle+\langle v,\chi\rangle\quad\text{for $v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$ and $\chi\in\mathbb{X}$,}

(26)

or in other words,
R_{\mathbb{X}}:v\mapsto v_{\mathbb{X}}
where

v_{\mathbb{X}}\in\mathbb{X}
is the Galerkin solution of the elliptic
problem (
25
). Note that, by including the lower-order
term
v
, the Ritz projector
R_{\mathbb{X}}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{X}
would also be
well-defined for the zero-flux boundary condition (
24
).

It follows from (
4
), (
6
) and
(
26
) that
\theta_{\mathbb{X}}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{X}
satisfies

\langle\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t),\chi\rangle+\bigl\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\kappa\nabla\theta_{\mathbb{X}})-\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\theta_{\mathbb{X}},\nabla\chi\bigr\rangle=\langle f_{1},\chi\rangle+\langle\vec{f}_{2},\nabla\chi\rangle\quad\text{for $\chi\in\mathbb{X}$,}

(27)

where

f_{1}=(u_{0\mathbb{X}}-P_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0})+(\rho_{\mathbb{X}}-\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}),\qquad\vec{f}_{2}=-\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\rho_{\mathbb{X}},

(28)

and
P_{\mathbb{X}}:L_{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{X}
is the orthoprojector
given by
\langle P_{\mathbb{X}}v,\chi\rangle=\langle v,\chi\rangle

for
v\in L_{2}(\Omega)
and
\chi\in\mathbb{X}
. If
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
so
that
R_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}
exists, then
\langle f_{1},\chi\rangle=\langle\tilde{f}_{1},\chi\rangle
where

\tilde{f}_{1}=(u_{0\mathbb{X}}-R_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0})+\bigl(\rho_{\mathbb{X}}-\rho_{\mathbb{X}}(0)\bigr)-\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}.

(29)

We estimate
\theta_{\mathbb{X}}
in terms of
\rho_{\mathbb{X}}
and the error in the discrete
initial data
u_{0\mathbb{X}}
, as follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
0<t\leq T
,

\|\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq C\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}-P_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

Noting that (
27
) has the same form as (
12
), with

\theta_{\mathbb{X}}
playing the role of
u_{\mathbb{X}}
, we may apply

Lemma

3.3
and conclude that

\|\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|f_{1}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+\frac{C}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\vec{f}_{2}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Since
f_{1}^{\prime}=\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}-\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}
, we find with the help
of
Lemma

2.1
that

\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|f_{1}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq C\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}-P_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\partial_{s}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Using the identity (
11
) and noting that

(\mathcal{M}\rho_{\mathbb{X}})(0)=0
,

\displaystyle s\partial_{s}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}

\displaystyle=s\partial_{s}\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}=\partial_{s}(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}})-\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}=\partial_{s}\bigl(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}+\alpha\mathcal{I}^{\alpha+1}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)-\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\rho_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}+(\alpha-1)\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}=\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\bigl(\mathcal{M}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}+\alpha\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr),

so by
Lemma

2.1
,

\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\|\partial_{s}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds\leq 2t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\|s\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}+\alpha\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr\|^{2}\,ds\leq 4t^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds,

and hence

\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|f_{1}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq C\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}-P_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}\|^{2}\\
+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

(30)

Recalling (
5
), we have

f_{2}^{\prime}(t)=-(\vec{F}\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}})(t)
and therefore
by
Lemma

2.5
,

\frac{C}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\vec{f}_{2}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq\frac{C}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds+\frac{C}{t^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\|\partial_{s}^{1-\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}\,ds,

which is bounded by the second term on the right-hand side
of (
30
).
∎

Two similar bounds hold for
\nabla\theta_{\mathbb{X}}
, but now
involving also
\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}
and
\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
0<t\leq T
,

t^{\alpha}\|\nabla\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq C\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}-P_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+Ct^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

If
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then we also have the alternative bound

t^{\alpha}\|\nabla\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}\leq C\bigl\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}-R_{\mathbb{X}}u_{0}\bigr\|^{2}+\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}-\rho_{X}(0)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\\
+Ct^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{X}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds.

Proof.

With
f_{1}
and
\vec{f}_{2}
given by (
28
), we apply

Lemma

4.3
to (
27
) and bound

t^{\alpha}\|\nabla\theta_{\mathbb{X}}(t)\|^{2}
by

\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}f_{1})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds.

The terms in
f_{1}
can be bounded as in (
30
), and since

\vec{f}_{2}=-\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}
and
(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}=-\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}
we see from

Lemma

2.5
followed by
Lemma

4.1
and then

Lemma

2.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(

\displaystyle\|\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2}\|^{2}+\|(\mathcal{M}\nabla\cdot\vec{f}_{2})^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\rho_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}})^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}\|^{2}\Bigr)\,ds,

which completes the proof of the first bound. If

then
we can replace
f_{1}
with
\tilde{f}_{1}
from (

), and since

to (

) (with

∎

To obtain more explicit error bounds we will use the regularity properties
stated in the next theorem. The seminorm
|\cdot|_{r}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
in
the (fractional-order) Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{r}(\Omega)
is defined in the usual
way
[
20
]
via the Dirichlet eigenfunctions of the Laplacian
on
\Omega
, and this spatial domain is assumed convex to ensure

H^{2}
-regularity for the elliptic problem. The proof relies on
results
[
15
, Lemma 2,Theorems 11–13]
involving constants that
blow up as
\alpha\to 1
. Nevertheless, the estimates
(
31
)–(
33
) hold in the limiting case
\alpha=1
,
when the problem reduces to the classical Fokker–Planck PDE; see
Thomée
[
20
, Lemmas 3.2 and 4.4]
for a proof if
M=0
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
\Omega
is convex,
0<\alpha<1
,
0\leq r\leq 2
and
\eta>0
. If

u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{r}(\Omega)
and if
g:(0,T]\to L_{2}(\Omega)
is continuously
differentiable with
\|g(t)\|+t\|g^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Mt^{\eta-1}
,
then the weak solution of (
1
) satisfies, for
0<t\leq T
,

\|u(t)\|_{1}\leq C_{\alpha,\eta}\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{r}\,t^{-\alpha(1-r)/2}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha/2}\bigr)\quad\text{if $r\leq 1$,}

(31)

and

t^{-\alpha/2}\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{1}\leq C_{\alpha,\eta}\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{r}\,t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha}\bigr)\quad\text{if $r\geq 1$,}

(32)

and

t^{1-\alpha/2}\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{1}+\|u(t)\|_{2}+t\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{2}\leq C_{\alpha,\eta}\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{r}\,t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha}\bigr).

(33)

Proof.

We showed
[
15
, Theorem 11]
that

\|u(t)\|_{1}\leq C_{\alpha,\eta}\bigl(\|u_{0}\|t^{-\alpha/2}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha/2}\bigr)

and
[
15
, Theorem 12]
that

\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{1}+t\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{1}\leq C_{\alpha,\eta}\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha/2}\bigr).

Hence,
\|u(t)\|_{1}\leq C\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+Mt^{\eta-\alpha/2}\bigr)
and
(
31
) follows by interpolation. The
estimates (
32
) and (
33
) were proved
already
[
15
, Theorems 12 and 13]
.
∎

Now consider the concrete example in which
\mathbb{X}=S_{h}
is the usual
continuous piecewise-linear finite element space for a triangulation
of
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with maximum element diameter
h
, and
use a subscript
h
instead of
\mathbb{X}
, writing
u_{h}
,
\theta_{h}
,

\rho_{h}
etc. The error in the Ritz projection satisfies

\|\rho_{h}(t)\|+h\|\nabla\rho_{h}(t)\|\leq Ch^{r}|u(t)|_{r}\quad\text{for $r\in\{1,2\}$,}

(34)

allowing us to prove the following error bounds for
u_{h}
and
\nabla u_{h}
.
Notice that if
0<\alpha<1/2
, then the restriction
\alpha(2-r)<1
is satisfied
for all
r\in[0,2]
, but if
1/2\leq\alpha<1
(and hence

0\leq 2-\alpha^{-1}<1
) then we are limited to
r\in(2-\alpha^{-1},2]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
0\leq r\leq 2
with
\alpha(2-r)<1
, and assume that the assumptions of

Theorem

5.3
are satisfied with
\eta\geq\alpha r/2
. Then, the
semidiscrete finite element solution
u_{h}
satisfies the error bound

\|u_{h}(t)-u(t)\|\leq C\|u_{0h}-P_{h}u_{0}\|+C_{\alpha}h^{2}\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha(2-r)}}\,(\|u_{0}\|_{r}+M),

and the gradient of
u_{h}
satisfies

\|\nabla u_{h}(t)-\nabla u(t)\|\leq Ct^{-\alpha/2}\|u_{0h}-Q_{r,h}u_{0}\|+C_{\alpha}h\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)/2})}{\sqrt{1-\alpha(2-r)}}\,(\|u_{0}\|_{r}+M),

where
Q_{r,h}
is either
P_{h}
if
r\leq 1
, or else
R_{h}
if
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

For brevity, let
K_{r}=(\|u_{0}\|_{r}+M)^{2}
. Using (
34
) followed
by (
33
), we have

\|\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\leq Ch^{4}\bigl(|u(s)|_{2}^{2}+s^{2}|u^{\prime}(s)|_{2}^{2}\bigr)\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{4}s^{-\alpha(2-r)},

so, because of the assumption
\alpha(2-r)<1
,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{4}\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)}}{1-\alpha(2-r)}.

Since
\|u_{h}-u\|\leq\|\theta_{h}\|+\|\rho_{h}\|
and

\|\rho_{h}(t)\|^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}t^{-\alpha(2-r)}h^{4}
, the error bound for
u_{h}

follows by
Lemma

5.1
.

To estimate the error in
\nabla u_{h}
, we apply (
34
) and
(
31
) to obtain

\|\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\bigl(|u(s)|_{1}^{2}+s^{2}|u^{\prime}(s)|_{1}^{2}\bigr)\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}s^{-\alpha(1-r)}\quad\text{if $r\leq 1$,}

and (
33
) to obtain

\|\nabla\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\bigl(|u(s)|_{2}^{2}+s^{2}|u^{\prime}(s)|_{2}^{2}\bigr)\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}s^{-\alpha(2-r)},

so

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq t^{\alpha}\,C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)})}{1-\alpha(1-r)}\quad\text{if $r\leq 1$,}

and

t^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\nabla\rho_{h}\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{h}^{\prime}\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}K_{r}t^{\alpha r}h^{2}}{1-\alpha(2-r)}=t^{2\alpha}\,C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)}}{1-\alpha(2-r)}.

(35)

Since

\|\nabla u_{h}(t)-\nabla u(t)\|\leq\|\nabla\theta_{h}(t)\|+\|\nabla\rho_{h}(t)\|
,
the first estimate of
Lemma

5.2
implies that
the error bound for
\nabla u_{h}
holds for the case
r\leq 1
.

If
r\geq 1
, then we see using (
31
)–(
34
) that

\|\rho_{h}(s)-\rho_{h}(0)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\bigl(\|u(s)-u(0)\|_{1}^{2}+s^{2}\|u^{\prime}(s)\|_{1}^{2}\bigr)\leq s^{\alpha}C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}s^{-\alpha(2-r)}

and
\|\rho_{h}(s)\|^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\|u(s)\|_{1}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}K_{1}h^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}
, so

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|\rho_{h}-\rho_{h}(0)\|^{2}+s^{2}\|\rho_{h}^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\bigr)\,ds+t^{2\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{h}\|^{2}\,ds\leq t^{\alpha}C_{\alpha}K_{r}h^{2}\,\frac{t^{-\alpha(2-r)}}{1-\alpha(2-r)}.

Hence, using the second estimate of
Lemma

5.2

and (

), the error bound for
\nabla u_{h}
follows also for
the case

.
∎

6
Discontinuous Galerkin time stepping when
\vec{F}\equiv\vec{0}

We briefly consider a fully-discrete scheme for the fractional diffusion
equation, that is, for the problem (
1
) in the
case
\vec{F}\equiv\vec{0}
. For time levels
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
we denote
the
n
th time interval by
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n})
and the
n
th step size
by
k_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
. We choose an integer
p_{n}\geq 0
for each time
interval
I_{n}
, and define the vector space
\mathcal{W}
consisting of all
functions
X:\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}\to\mathbb{X}
such that the
restriction
X|_{I_{n}}
is a polynomial in
t
of degree at most
p_{n}
with
coefficients in
\mathbb{X}
. For any
X\in\mathcal{W}
, write

X^{n}_{+}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}X(t_{n}+\epsilon),\qquad X^{n}_{-}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}X(t_{n}-\epsilon),\qquad\llbracket X\rrbracket^{n}=X^{n}_{+}-X^{n}_{-},

then the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) solution
U\in\mathcal{W}
is defined by
requiring that
[
18
]

\bigl\langle\llbracket U\rrbracket^{n},X^{n-1}_{+}\bigr\rangle+\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}U,X\rangle\,dt+\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla X\rangle\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}\langle g,X\rangle\,dt

(36)

for
X\in\mathcal{W}
and
1\leq n\leq N
, with
U^{0}_{-}=u_{0\mathbb{X}}
(so that

\llbracket U\rrbracket^{0}=U^{0}_{+}-u_{0\mathbb{X}}
). To state a stability estimate for this scheme,
let
C_{\Omega}
denote the constant arising in the Poincaré inequality
for
\Omega
,

\|v\|^{2}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\nabla v\|^{2}\quad\text{for $v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$,}

(37)

and define
\Psi:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Psi(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\pi^{1-\alpha}}\,\frac{(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)^{1-\alpha}}\,\frac{1}{\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi\alpha)}\quad\text{for $0<\alpha<1$.}

Notice that
\Psi(1)=\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\Psi(\alpha)=1
but

\Psi(\alpha)\sim 8\pi^{-2}\alpha^{-1}
blows up as
\alpha\to 0
. We will use
the inequality
[
13
, Theorem A.1]

\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}v,v\bigr\rangle\,dt\geq\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{\Psi(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}\|v\|^{2}\,dt.

(38)

Theorem 6.1
.

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then the DG solution of the fractional diffusion problem
satisfies

\|U^{n}_{-}\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\|\llbracket U\rrbracket^{j}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla U\bigr\rangle\,dt\leq\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+\frac{C_{\Omega}\Psi(\alpha)}{\kappa_{\min}t_{n}^{1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\|g(t)\|^{2}\,dt

for
1\leq n\leq N
.

Proof.

Let
B(U,X)
denote the bilinear form

\langle U^{0}_{+},X^{0}_{+}\rangle+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\langle\llbracket U\rrbracket^{n},X^{n}_{+}\rangle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}U,X\rangle\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla X\bigr\rangle\,dt,

and observe that the time-stepping equations (
36
) are equivalent to

B(U,X)=\langle u_{0\mathbb{X}},X^{0}_{+}\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,X\rangle\,dt\quad\text{for $X\in\mathcal{W}$.}

Taking
X=U
, we find by arguing as in the proof of
Mustapha
[
18
, Theorem 1]
that

B(U,U)=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\|U^{0}_{+}\|^{2}+\|U^{N}_{-}\|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|\llbracket U\rrbracket^{n}\|^{2}\biggr)+\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla X\bigr\rangle\,dt,

and so

\|U^{0}_{+}\|^{2}+\|U^{N}_{-}\|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|\llbracket U\rrbracket^{n}\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla U\bigr\rangle\,dt\\
=2\langle u_{0\mathbb{X}},U^{0}_{+}\rangle+2\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,U\rangle\,dt.

For any constant
M>0
,

2\langle u_{0\mathbb{X}},U^{0}_{+}\rangle+2\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,U\rangle\,dt\leq\|U^{0}_{+}\|^{2}+\|u_{0\mathbb{X}}\|^{2}+M\int_{0}^{T}\|g\|^{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{T}\|U\|^{2}\,dt,

and using (
37
) and (
38
),

\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{T}\|U\|^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{M}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla U\|^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}\Psi(\alpha)}{M\kappa_{\min}T^{1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla U,\nabla U\bigr\rangle\,dt.

Choosing
M=C_{\Omega}\Psi(\alpha)/(\kappa_{\min}T^{1-\alpha})
implies the
estimate in the case
n=N
, which completes the proof since
T=t_{N}
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

If
p_{n}=0
then we have
\|U(t)\|\leq\|U^{n}_{-}\|
for
t\in I_{N}
, and likewise if

p_{n}=1
then
\|U(t)\|\leq\max(\|U^{n}_{-}\|,\|U^{n-1}_{+}\|)\leq\|U^{n}_{-}\|+\|U^{n-1}_{-}\|+\|\llbracket U\rrbracket^{n-1}\|
for
t\in I_{n}
. Thus, for the piecewise
constant
[
14
]
and
piecewise linear
[
19
]
DG schemes we can prove stability
in
L_{\infty}(L_{2})
, uniformly for
\alpha
bounded away from zero.

Remark 6.3
.

For the solution
u
of the continuous fractional diffusion problem we have the
analogous stability property

\|u(t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u,\nabla u\bigr\rangle\,dt\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{C_{\Omega}\Psi(\alpha)}{\kappa_{\min}t^{1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,ds\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$.}

The proof follows the same lines as above, except that now

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}u,u\rangle\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha}\kappa\nabla u,\nabla u\bigr\rangle\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,u\rangle\,dt

and

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}u,u\rangle\,dt=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|u(T)\|^{2}-\|u_{0}\|^{2}\bigr).

Remark 6.4
.

Le et al.
[
11
]
proved stability and convergence of the
DG scheme with general
\vec{F}
, but only for the lowest-order (
p_{n}=0
) case
and with no spatial discretization. Although the constants are bounded
as
\alpha\to 1
, they blow up as
\alpha\to 1/2
and thus the fractional exponent
is restricted to the range
1/2<\alpha\leq 1
. Huang et al.
[
6
]

proved similar results for a slightly modified scheme.

A geometric proof of Duflo’s theorem
Ioan Stanciu

Abstract
Let R R be a commutative ring: we explain the Beilinson-Bernstein localisation mechanism for sheaves of homogeneous twisted differential operators defined over a smooth, separated, locally of finite type R R -scheme. As an application, we give a new proof of Duflo’s theorem characterising the primitive ideals of the enveloping algebra of a complex semisimple Lie algebra.



1
Introduction

A classical problem in ring theory is the characterisation of the primitive spectrum. In this paper, we focus on the primitive spectrum of a semisimple Lie algebra.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic 0 and
G
be a connected, simply-connected, split semisimple, smooth affine algebraic group over
K
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
. Fix
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
a Cartan decomposition. In a seminal paper
[
9
]
, the authors define the category
\mathcal{O}
of representations for the algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
. The building blocks are given by Verma modules
M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{g})\underset{U(\mathfrak{b})}{\otimes}{}K_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
;
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
. These are highest weight modules with unique maximal submodule
N(\lambda)
and unique simple quotient
L(\lambda)
. Moreover, this category
\mathcal{O}
is Artinian and the set of simple objects is characterised exactly by
L(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. An excellent exposition of category
\mathcal{O}
can be found in
[
18
]
.

The importance of category
\mathcal{O}
in the representation theory of the ring
U(\mathfrak{g})
can be seen in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Duflo’s Theorem)
.

[
13
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
I
be a primitive/prime ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g})
with
K
-rational infinitesimal central character. Then

I=\Ann(L(\lambda))\text{ for some }\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}.

Duflo’s original statement requires the ground field to be
\mathbb{C}
. In their paper
[
10
]
, Bernstein and Gelfand extend this result for algebraically closed fields of characteristic
0
which in turn can be extended to the generality stated by a base change argument. Another purely algebraic proof of Duflo’s theorem can be found in
[
20
]
and for a categorical proof, see
[
15
]
. One should note that if
K
is algebraically closed, all primitive ideals have
K
-rational central character, so the theorem gives a full classification of the primitive spectrum.

Statement of the main results

The proofs in the literature use deep knowledge about the structure of category
\mathcal{O}
and Harish-Chandra bimodules. The Beilinson-Bernstein localisation theorem allows for the study of representation theory of
U(\mathfrak{g})
using the geometry of
\mathcal{D}
-modules. Along with the BB localisation, the heart of the argument lies in the following informal description: "
G
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-modules on the double flag variety correspond to
B
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-modules on the flag variety".

For
\lambda
dominant, we let
\chi_{\lambda}:Z(U(\mathfrak{g}))\to K
denote the corresponding central character and
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}:=U(\mathfrak{g})/\ker_{\chi_{\lambda}}U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 1.2

(Lemma
7.5
- Corollary
7.7
)
.

The function

\mathcal{F}:\{\text{two-sided ideals in }U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}\}\to\{\text{submodules of }M(\lambda)\},\qquad\mathcal{F}(I)=IM(\lambda)

is injective.

This was Dixmier’s initial idea of proving Duflo’s theorem. We give a new proof of this proposition using geometric tools. This approach has been sketched in
[
11
]
in the case
\lambda=0
. Our approach uses the language of
\lambda
-twisted differential operators on the flag variety and works for all
\lambda
dominant. As a corollary, we obtain Duflo’s theorem:

Corollary 1.3

(Corollary
7.1
)
.

Let
\lambda:\mathfrak{h}\to K
be a dominant weight and
I
a prime ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
. Then:

I=\Ann(L(\mu))\text{ for some }\mu:\mathfrak{h}\to K.

Strategy of the proof

A secondary goal of the paper is to construct a good framework that we will use to prove an affinoid version of Duflo’s theorem in
[

]

Structure of the paper

The paper is organised as follows: in Section

Proof.

Proof of Proposition

4.3
Equivariance of two-sided ideals in classical enveloping algebras

In this subsection we prove that any two-sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a
G
-equivariant
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})
-module (
U(\mathfrak{g})-U(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodule) when the base ring is a field of characteristic 0. Here we view
G
via its isomorphism with the diagonal subgroup of
G\times G
. The group
G
acts on the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
via the Adjoint action-denoted
\Ad
.

Let
{\tau}
denote the principal anti-automorphism of
U(\mathfrak{g})
induced by
x\mapsto-x
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. To simplify the notation, we will use
x^{\tau}
to denote
\tau(x)
. We have an action of
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})\cong U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
(by
[
24
, III.2.2)]
) on
U(\mathfrak{g})
via

(x\otimes y)\cdot a=yax^{\tau}\text{ for all }x,a,y\in U(\mathfrak{g}).

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
R
is a field of characteristic 0. Let
I
be a two-sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Then
I\in\Mod(U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}),G)
.

Proof.

First, since
R
is a field of characteristic
0
, we have by
[
12
, Proposition 2.4.17]
that
I
is invariant under the Adjoint action, so
I
is a
G
-module. By construction, it is clear
I
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
-module under the action defined above. We then have that for all
g\in G
,
x,y\in U(\mathfrak{g}),u\in I
that

\begin{split}\Ad(g)\cdot((x\otimes y)u)&=\Ad(g)(xuy^{\tau})\\
&=\Ad(g)y\Ad(g)u\Ad(g)x^{\tau}\\
&=\Ad(g)y\Ad(g)u(\Ad(g)x)^{\tau}\\
&=(\Ad(g)x\otimes\Ad(g)y)\cdot\Ad(g)u.\end{split}

(14)

The derivative of the
\Ad
action is the
\ad
-action of the Lie Algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\Lie(G)
embeds into
U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
via
x\to x\otimes 1+1\otimes x
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the two actions coincide. Therefore, we have proven all the conditions for
I
to be a
G
-equivariant
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})
-module.
∎

We now specialise to two-sided ideals in
U(\mathfrak{g})
with a given central character. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we let
\chi_{\lambda}:Z(\mathfrak{g})\to R
the associated central character. Let
m_{\lambda}=\ker(\chi_{\lambda})
and
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}=U(\mathfrak{g})/m_{\lambda}U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Consider the centre
C:=Z(U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}))=Z(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})
. Since,
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})\cong U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
and since
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a free
R
-module one obtains
C\cong Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes Z(\mathfrak{g})
. Any central character of
C
is determined by a pair
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
, where
\theta_{i}:Z(\mathfrak{g})\to R
. Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and let
\theta_{1}=\ker_{\chi_{\lambda}}
and
\theta_{2}=\ker_{\chi_{\mu}},
so that we obtain:

\ker(\chi_{\lambda},\chi_{\mu})=m_{\lambda}\otimes Z(\mathfrak{g})+Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes m_{\mu}.

Therefore we obtain:

\begin{split}U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda,\mu}&\cong U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})/U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})(m_{\lambda}\otimes Z(\mathfrak{g})+Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes m_{\mu})\\
&\cong U(\mathfrak{g})/U(\mathfrak{g})m_{\lambda}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})/U(\mathfrak{g})m_{\mu}\\
&\cong U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})^{\mu}.\end{split}

(15)

Recall that if
I
is a two-sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})
and
R
a field of characteristic 0, we proved in Proposition
4.4
that
I\in(U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}),G)
. Furthermore, if

is a two-sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
, we can view it as a
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
-bimodule, so a module over the ring
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda^{\op}}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
. Further, we have by
[
7
, Lemma 5.4-Equation 5.5]
that
\tau
induces an isomorphism
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda^{\op}}\cong U(\mathfrak{g})^{-w_{o}\lambda}
; recall that
w_{o}
denotes the longest element of
W
. Therefore, using equation (
15
), we deduce that a
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
-bimodule is the same as
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})^{-w_{o}\lambda,\lambda}
-module. In particular, we obtain:

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume
R
is a field of characteristic 0. Let
I
be a two-sided ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
. Then
I\in\Mod(U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})^{-w_{o}\lambda,\lambda},G)
.

4.4
Equivariance of two-sided ideals in deformed enveloping algebras

Throughout this subsection only, we will assume that

is a Noetherian local ring of characteristic 0.

Let

a regular element and consider the

Lemma 4.6

Let

-ideal in

and

. Then

. In other words,

on

Since

. Since

is an ideal in

. The claim follows since

is an

-ideal.
∎

For

, we let

and

be the root homomorphism and root vector corresponding to

Corollary 4.7

Let

be an

-ideal in

; in other words

is closed under the Adjoint action.

Proof.

We have by
[

that for all

and

:

x_{\alpha}(s)\cdot a=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ad(se_{\alpha})^{m}}{m!}(a),

(16)

and there exists

such that

. In particular, combining Lemma

with equation (

), we obtain

. To finish, we have that

is a local ring, so by

the Chevalley group

is generated by elements of the form

.
∎

Recall that
τ
denote the principal anti-automorphism of

induced by

for all

. We have by combining

and the PBW theorem that

. We get an action of

on

(x\otimes y)\cdot a=yax^{\tau}\text{ for all }x,a,y\in U(r\mathfrak{g}).

.

Let

be an

-ideal in

. Then

.

We have by the Corollary above that

is a

is a

-module under the action defined above. We then have by the same arguments as in Proposition

that for all

&=[i_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)D-Di_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)]\otimes m+D\otimes\psi m\\
&=i_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)D\otimes m-Di_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)\otimes m+D\otimes\psi m\\
&=i_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)D\otimes m-D\otimes\psi m+D\otimes\psi m\\
&=i_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi)D\otimes m\\
&=\psi\cdot(D\otimes m).\end{split}

(19)

Thus the lemma is proved.

Recall that by equation (

Let

\begin{split}i_{l}^{*}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}&\cong(\id\times i)\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}\\

Proof of theorem
6.5
.

Let
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{J}:\Coh(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu},G)\to\Mod(U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda},B)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{M}):&=\Gamma(X,i_{l}^{\#}\mathcal{M}),\\
\mathscr{J}(\mathcal{M}):&=T(\mu)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\mu}}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{M}).\end{split}

(30)

The theorem follows if we can prove that
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathscr{J}(\mathcal{M})
. First, we extend the functors
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{J}
to the category
\WCoh(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu},G)
; this is the category of coherent
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}
-modules that are equivariant
\mathcal{O}
-modules together with

-equivariant morphisms. The reason to do this is that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}\in\WCoh(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu},G)
, but
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}\notin\Coh(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu},G)
.

Since

as
\mathcal{O}
-modules,

is an equivalence of Abelian categories and
\lambda
is dominant, we obtain by Theorem
5.20
that
\mathscr{F}
is right exact. Furthermore since
\lambda
and

are dominant weights, by the same theorem applied on
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}
-modules,

is also right exact.

Next, let us construct a map from

\mathcal{N}=i_{l_{*}}i_{l}^{*}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}
and consider the space
\mathcal{N}\underset{\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}}{\otimes}{}\mathcal{M}
. By construction, this is isomorphic to
i_{l_{*}}i_{l}^{*}\mathcal{M}

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{M})&=\Gamma(X,i_{l}^{\#}\mathcal{M})\\
&=\Gamma(X,i_{l}^{*}\mathcal{M})\\
&\cong\Gamma(X\times X,i_{l_{*}}i_{l}^{*}\mathcal{M})\\
&\cong\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{N}\underset{\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu}}{\otimes}{}\mathcal{M}).\end{split}

(31)

Therefore, it is enough to construct a map from

\mathscr{J}(\mathcal{M})\cong\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{N})\underset{\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu})}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{M})

\mathcal{B}:=\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{N})\underset{\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu})}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{M})

We have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}&=\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{N})\underset{\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu})}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{M})\\

\begin{split}\mathscr{J}(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu})&=T(\mu)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\mu}}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\mu})\\

We retain the notations from
6.1
, recall that
R=K
is a field of characteristic
0

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\lambda:\mathfrak{h}\to K
be a dominant weight and let
I
be a primitive/prime ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}

I=\Ann(L(\mu))\text{ for some $\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}$}.

For the rest of this section, fix

a dominant weight and let

\lambda

will also be dominant. Recall further that

Consider the functor

\mathscr{F}(M):=\Gamma(X,i_{l}^{\#}\circ\Loc^{\lambda^{*},\lambda}(M)).

The functor

is exact and
\mathscr{F}(M)\cong T(\lambda)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}}{\otimes}{}M
as

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}:=\Loc^{\lambda^{*},\lambda}(M)
; we have by Corollary
5.21
that
\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{M})\cong M
and by Theorem
5.20
that
\mathcal{M}\in\Coh(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda^{*},\lambda},G)
, so the second claim follows from Theorem
6.5
. Consider a short exact sequence of finitely generated
G
-equivariant
U(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda^{*},\lambda}
-modules:

We let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P}
be the localisations of

and

respectively and let
\mathcal{K}=\ker(\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M})

Since

is an equivalence of Abelian categories by Theorem

,

is in particular exact. Furthermore, by Theorem

we obtain an exact sequence

\begin{split}0&\to T(\lambda)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}}{\otimes}{}\Gamma(X\times X,\mathcal{K})\to T(\lambda)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}}{\otimes}{}N\to\\
&\to T(\lambda)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}}{\otimes}{}M\to T(\lambda)\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}}{\otimes}{}P\to 0.\end{split}

(34)

The claim follows since

by definition of

.

By Proposition

Lemma 7.5

Let

Let

The map

\phi:T(\lambda)\to M(\lambda),\qquad\phi(k\otimes x)=\sigma(x)\otimes k,\quad k\in K_{\lambda},x\in U(\mathfrak{g})

Since
\sigma
swaps
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and fixes
\mathfrak{h}

\phi
is well-defined. Furthermore, as
\sigma
is a

Finally, we have for

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}(\sigma(J))}

We now have all the ingredients to prove Duflo’s Theorem; the idea of the proof was first given by Dixmier in

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Let
I
be a prime ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
. Then, by Corollary
7.7
, we have
I=\Ann(M(\lambda)/IM(\lambda))
. Since
M(\lambda)/IM(\lambda)
is quotient of
M(\lambda)
, we have by Proposition
4.10
that there exists a finite composition series

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\ldots\subset M_{n}=M(\lambda)/IM(\lambda).

Let
I_{i}:=\Ann(M_{i}/M_{i-1})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\begin{split}I_{1}I_{2}\ldots I_{n}(M(\lambda)/IM(\lambda))&=I_{1}I_{2}\ldots I_{n}M_{n}\\
&=I_{1}I_{2}\ldots I_{n-1}M_{n-1}\\
&=\ldots\\
&=0,\end{split}

(36)

so
I_{1}I_{2}\ldots I_{n}\subset I
. Since the ideal
I
is prime, there exists
1\leq j\leq n
such that
I_{j}\subset I
. On the other hand, by construction
I\subset I_{j}
. Thus, we obtain that
I=I_{j}=\Ann(M_{j}/M_{j-1})
. The claim follows since by Proposition
4.10
,
M_{j}/M_{j-1}\cong L(\mu)
for some
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}.

Since any primitive ideal is in particular prime, we obtain that any primitive ideal in
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}
is the annihilator of some
L(\mu)
.
∎

Cosmological Reconstruction and Λ \Lambda CDM Universe in f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) Gravity
Gaurav N. Gadbail, Avik De, P.K. Sahoo

Abstract
Symmetric Teleparallel Gravity allows for the reformulation of gravity in the form of nonmetricity by vanishing the contorsion term in the generic affine connection. Our focus is on investigating a recently proposed extension of this theory in which the Lagrangian has the form f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) by incorporating the boundary term C C . In this work, we first use a reconstruction approach in f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) gravity that might admit the Λ \Lambda CDM expansion history. Furthermore, we perform a novel approach for cosmological reconstruction of f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) gravity in terms of e-folding, and it shows how any FLRW cosmology can arise from a specific f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) gravity. A variety of instances are provided using this approach in which f ⁡ ( Q , C ) f(Q,C) gravity is reconstructed to yield the well-known cosmic evolution: Λ \Lambda CDM era, acceleration/deceleration era which is equivalent to the presence of phantom and non-phantom matter, late-time acceleration with the crossing of phantom-divide line and transient phantom era.

As we know, the Levi-Civita connection
\mathring{\Gamma}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
is the unique affine connection which satisfies both the metric-comptaibility and torsion-free conditions. We relax this restriction and instead assume a torsion-free and curvature-free affine connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}

to develop the symmetric teleparallel geometry. The torsionless environment makes the affine connection symmetric in its lower indices, hence the term ‘symmetric’. The incompatibility of this affine connection with the metric is characterised by the non-metricity tensor

Q_{\lambda\mu\nu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\nabla_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\lambda\mu}g_{\beta\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\lambda\nu}g_{\beta\mu}\neq 0\,.

(2)

We can always express

\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathring{\Gamma}^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}+L^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\,.

(3)

It follows that,

L^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(Q^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}-Q_{\mu}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\nu}-Q_{\nu}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\mu})\,.

(4)

We can construct two different types of non-metricity vectors,

Q_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g^{\nu\lambda}Q_{\mu\nu\lambda}=Q_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\nu}\,,\qquad\tilde{Q}_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g^{\nu\lambda}Q_{\nu\mu\lambda}=Q_{\nu\mu}{}^{\nu}\,.

Likewise, we write

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=L_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\nu}\,,\qquad\tilde{L}_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=L_{\nu\mu}{}^{\nu}\,.

(5)

The superpotential (or the non-metricity conjugate) tensor
P^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}
is given by

P^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\left(-2L^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}+Q^{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}-\tilde{Q}^{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}-\delta^{\lambda}{}_{(\mu}Q_{\nu)}\right)\,.

(6)

Finally, the non-metricity scalar
Q
is defined as

Q=Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,.

(7)

From the torsion-free and curvature-free constraints
one can further easily obtain the following relations:

\displaystyle\mathring{R}_{\mu\nu}+\mathring{\nabla}_{\alpha}L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}-\mathring{\nabla}_{\nu}\tilde{L}_{\mu}+\tilde{L}_{\alpha}L^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}-L_{\alpha\beta\nu}L^{\beta\alpha}{}_{\mu}=0\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\mathring{R}+\mathring{\nabla}_{\alpha}(L^{\alpha}-\tilde{L}^{\alpha})-Q=0\,.

(9)

As
Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}=L^{\alpha}-\tilde{L}^{\alpha}
,
from the preceding relation, one also defines the boundary term as

\displaystyle C=\mathring{R}-Q

\displaystyle=-\mathring{\nabla}_{\alpha}(Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha})

(10)

+(P_{\mu\alpha\beta}Q_{\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta}-2P_{\alpha\beta\nu}Q^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\mu})f_{Q}\\

III
FLRW cosmology in
f(Q,C)
gravity

The “cosmological principle” states that on a large enough scale our
Universe is homogenous and isotropic, that is, it is the same at every point
and in every direction. Based on this, the most reasonable and theoretically
as well as observationally supported model of the present Universe is the spatially
flat Friedmann-Lemaitree-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime given by the line
element in Cartesian coordinates

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)[dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}],

(19)

where
a(t)
is said to be the scale factor of the Universe, and its first time derivative is given by the Hubble parameter
H(t)=\frac{\dot{a}}{a(t)}
. Here
\dot{()}
indicates a derivative with respect to cosmic time
t
. We proceed with the vanishing affine connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}=0
and compute the followings as required

\displaystyle\mathring{R}=

\displaystyle 6(2H^{2}+\dot{H}),\quad Q=-6H^{2},\quad C=6(3H^{2}+\dot{H}).

(20)

With these data, we derive the Friedmann-like equations as

\displaystyle\kappa\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{f}{2}+6H^{2}f_{Q}-(9H^{2}+3\dot{H})f_{C}+3H\dot{f_{C}}

(21)

\displaystyle\kappa p=

\displaystyle-\frac{f}{2}-(6H^{2}+2\dot{H})f_{Q}-2H\dot{f}_{Q}+(9H^{2}+3\dot{H})f_{C}-\ddot{f_{C}}.

(22)

We know that for a flat FLRW universe, the boundary term

Now, we can rewrite the boundary term

Now, the Hubble parameter and Its derivative in terms of boundary term

H(C)=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{0}}{3a(C)^{3}}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}},

(30)

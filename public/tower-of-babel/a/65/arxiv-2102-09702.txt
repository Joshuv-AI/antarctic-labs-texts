Multiplicity and asymptotics of standing waves for the energy critical half-wave


Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the multiplicity and asymptotics of standing waves with prescribed mass ∫ ℝ N u 2 = a 2 \int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}{{u}^{2}}=a^{2} to the energy critical half-wave − Δ ​ u = λ ​ u + μ ​ | u | q − 2 ​ u + | u | 2 ∗ − 2 ​ u , u ∈ H 1 / 2 ​ ( ℝ N ) , \sqrt{-\Delta}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,\ \ u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}), (0.1) where N ≥ 2 N\!\geq\!2 , a > 0 a\!>\!0 , q ∈ ( 2 , 2 + 2 N ) q\!\in\!\big(2,2+\frac{2}{N}\big) , 2 ∗ = 2 ​ N N − 1 2^{*}\!=\!\frac{2N}{N-1} and λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\!\in\!\mathbb{R} appears as a Lagrange multiplier. We show that ( 0.1 ) admits a ground state u a u_{a} and an excited state v a v_{a} , which are characterised by a local minimizer and a mountain-pass critical point of the corresponding energy functional. Several asymptotic properties of { u a } \{u_{a}\} , { v a } \{v_{a}\} are obtained and it is worth pointing out that we get a precise description of { u a } \{u_{a}\} as a → 0 + a\!\to\!0^{+} without needing any uniqueness condition on the related limit problem. The main contribution of this paper is to extend the main results in J. Bellazzini et al. [Math. Ann. 371 (2018), 707-740] from energy subcritical to energy critical case. Furthermore, these results can be extended to the general fractional nonlinear Schrödinger equation with Sobolev critical exponent, which generalize the work of H. J. Luo-Z. T. Zhang [Calc. Var. 1.
Introduction and Main Result

The nonlinear half-wave equations

i\partial_{t}\psi=\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi-f(|\psi|)\psi\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.1)

are well-known and widely studied by scholars, see
[
12
,
3
,
2
,
14
,
17
]
and references therein. The operator
\sqrt{-\Delta}
is defined by

\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|\mathcal{F}\psi),\ \ \forall\psi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\mathcal{F}\psi
is the Fourier transform of
\psi
, the space
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+1}}<+\infty\big\}.

Physically, the motivation to the study of (
1.1
) ranges from turbulence phenomena, wave propagation, continuum limits of long-range lattice system, and models for gravitational collapse in astrophysics (see e. g.
[
8
,
18
]
).
Equation (
1.1
) with power-type nonlinearities has been studied in
[
3
]
. Concerning the local/global well-posedness of solutions to (
1.1
), please refer to
[
2
,
14
]
. The dynamical properties of blow-up solutions have been investigated in e.g.
[
9
,
17
]
.

Taking
f(t)=\mu t^{q-2}+t^{p-2}
with
2<q,p\leq\frac{2N}{N-1}
, if
u
satisfies the following equation

\sqrt{-\Delta}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ \ u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.2)

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)

solves equation (
1.1
). We say that
u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a weak solution to (
1.2
) if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\sqrt{\!-\!\Delta}u\varphi\!-\!\lambda u{\varphi}]\!-\!\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u\right|^{q\!-\!2}u{\varphi}\!-\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi\!=0,~~~~\forall\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and
u\in H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
is a
normalized solution
to (
1.2
) if there exist a real number
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a function
u\in H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
weakly solving (
1.2
) together with the normalization condition
||u||_{2}^{2}=a^{2}
.

Recently, normalized solutions to elliptic PDEs and systems attract much attention of researchers e.g.
[
15
,
16
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
20
]
. In
[
26
]
, N. Soave considered the existence of normalized ground states to the following energy (Sobolev) critical Schrödinger equation

-\Delta u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,\ \ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.3)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
2<q<\frac{2N}{N-2}
. The whole study of
[
26
]
can be considered as a counterpart of the Brézis-Nirenberg problem in the context of normalized solutions. If
\mu>0
and
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, it was proved in
[
26
]
and
[
15
]
that for small mass, there is a ground state to (
1.3
), which corresponds to the local minimizer of the associated energy functional constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere via different methods, respectively. In this case, N. Soave
[
26
]
raised an open question on how to obtain the second solution of (
1.3
) since the associated energy functional constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere admits a convex-concave geometry. It is worth pointing out that, L. Jeanjean and T. T. Le
[
16
]
solved this open question if
N\geq 4
. Also for small mass, Jeanjean et al. in
[
16
]
proved the existence of a second solution to (
1.3
), which is an excited state corresponding to the mountain-pass critical point of the associated energy functional.

H. J. Luo and Z. T. Zhang
[
19
]
extended the existence and nonexistence results of N. Soave
[
25
]
to fractional Schrödinger equation

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ u\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.4)

where
0<\sigma<1
,
N\geq 2
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
2<q<p<\frac{2N}{N-2\sigma}
.
In addition, they proved a uniqueness result for the case
\mu=0
and

2<p<2+\frac{4\sigma}{N}
.
If
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}
,
N\geq 2
,
\mu=0
and
2+\frac{2}{N}<p<\frac{2N}{N-1}
, Bellazzini et al. in
[
3
]
considered a minimization problem for (
1.4
) under the normalization constraint on the Pohozaev manifold. Moreover, they presented a different proof of the existence of ground states and characterized the dynamics near the ground states. In preparing this paper, we notice that in the very recent work
[
29
]
, the authors extended the results of
[
26
]
to the fractional Laplacian operator and studied (
1.4
) with
p=\frac{2N}{N-2\sigma}
, they also obtained a ground state to (
1.4
) for small mass.

Motivated by the above works, in this paper, we consider the existence and asymptotic properties of solutions to the energy critical half-wave

\sqrt{-\Delta}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,\ \ u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.5)

under the constraint

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}=a^{2},

(1.6)

where

\mu>0,~~q\!\in\!\big(2,2+\frac{2}{N}\big),~~2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-1}
.
Notice that
|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
is energy critical as
2^{*}
is the Sobolev critical exponent. A natural approach to find solutions of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) is to search critical points of the energy functional

\displaystyle F_{\mu}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\sqrt{-\Delta}u-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{q}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|u|^{2^{*}}

on the
L^{2}
-sphere

S(a):=\Big\{u\in H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}):{||u||}_{2}^{2}=a^{2}\Big\}.

Then the parameter
\lambda
appear as Lagrange multipliers.

By the
L^{2}
-norm preserving dilations
t\star u(x)=t^{\frac{N}{2}}u(tx)
with
t\!>\!0
, it is easy to know that

\bar{q}:=2+\frac{2}{N}
is the
L^{2}
-critical exponent of (
1.5
).
We say that
\tilde{u}
is a ground state of (
1.5
) on
S(a)
if

d\left.F_{\mu}\right|_{S(a)}(\tilde{u})=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ and }\hskip 9.24994ptF_{\mu}(\tilde{u})=\inf\big\{F_{\mu}(u):~~~~d\left.F_{\mu}\right|_{S(a)}(u)=0,\ \text{and}\ u\in S(a)\big\}.

As
2<q<\bar{q}<2^{*}
, we have
\inf\limits_{u\in S(a)}F_{\mu}(u)=-\infty
. In spirit of Jeanjean
[
15
]
, we see that the presence of the mass subcritical term
\mu|u|^{q-2}u
created a convex-concave geometry of
F_{\mu}|_{S(a)}
if
a>0
is small. In view of this, it is natural to introduce a suitable local minimization problem

m(a):=\inf_{u\in V(a)}F_{\mu}(u)<0\leq\inf_{u\in\partial V(a)}F_{\mu}(u),

(1.7)

where

V(a):=\{u\in S(a):\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}<\rho_{0}\},~~\partial V(a):=\{u\in S(a):\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}=\rho_{0}\},

see Section 3 for details. Up to a normalizing constant, we write
\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}
as

\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\sqrt{-\Delta}u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+1}}.

Our main results are as follows. We first consider the existence and asymptotic behaviour of ground states to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
). Let
Q
be the unique positive radial ground state of
\sqrt{-\Delta}Q+Q-|Q|^{q-2}Q=0
, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\!\geq\!2
,
\mu>0
and
q\in\big(2,2+\frac{2}{N}\big)
, then there exists a
a_{*}\!=\!a_{*}(\mu)\!>\!0
such that, for any
a\in(0,a_{*}]
,
m(a)
is achieved by some
u_{a}\in V(a)
and
u_{a}
is a ground state of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
). Furthermore, we have

(1)

For any fixed
a>0
,
u_{a}\in S(a)
exists for any
\mu>0
sufficiently small and

\|u_{a}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\to 0,\ \ F_{\mu}(u_{a})\to 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\ \mu\to 0;

(2)

For any fixed
\mu>0
, letting
a\to 0
, then for any ground state
u_{a}\in V(a)
, we have

\frac{m(a)}{a^{\frac{2q(1-\gamma_{q})}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}}\to-K_{N,q},\ \ \frac{\lambda_{a}}{a^{\frac{2(q-2)}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}}\to-\frac{2q(1-\gamma_{q})}{2-q\gamma_{q}}K_{N,q},\ \ \frac{\|u_{a}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}}{a^{\frac{2q(1-\gamma_{q})}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}}\to\frac{2q\gamma_{q}}{2-q\gamma_{q}}K_{N,q}.

In addition, we have

\frac{1}{\alpha}u_{a}\big(\frac{x}{\beta}\big)\to Q\ \ \text{in}\ H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \ \text{as}\ a\to 0^{+},

where
\alpha\!=\!\frac{a\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\|Q\|_{2}}
,
\beta\!=\!\Big[\frac{\mu a^{q-2}}{\|Q\|^{q-2}_{2}}\Big]^{\frac{2}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}
,
K_{N,q}\!=\!\frac{(2-q\gamma_{q})\gamma_{q}}{2q(1-\gamma_{q})}\cdot\|Q\|^{\frac{2(2-q)}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}_{2}\mu^{\frac{2}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}\!>\!0
and
\gamma_{q}\!=\!\frac{N(q-2)}{q}
.

Next, we study the existence and asymptotic behaviour of a second solution of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
). Denote

\mathcal{S}=\inf_{u\in{\dot{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}}{||u||_{2^{*}}^{2}},

refer to Section 2 for details (see also
[
22
]
). We have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\!\geq\!2
,
q\in\big(2,2+\frac{2}{N}\big)
,
\mu>0
and
a_{*}=a_{*}(\mu)>0
given by Theorem
1.1
. Then, for any
a\in(0,a_{*}]
, there exists a second solution
v_{a}\in S(a)
to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) such that

0<F_{\mu}(v_{a})<m(a)+\frac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}.

Furthermore, we have

(1)

For any fixed
\mu>0
, it holds that

\|v_{a}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\to\mathcal{S}^{N},~~~~F_{\mu}(v_{a})\to\frac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\hskip 9.24994pta\to 0;

(2)

For any fixed
a>0
,
v_{a}\in S(a)
exists for any
\mu>0
sufficiently small and

\|v_{a}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\to\mathcal{S}^{N},~~~~F_{\mu}(v_{a})\to\dfrac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\hskip 9.24994pt\mu\to 0.

Denote the set of ground states to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) by
\mathcal{M}_{a}
. If
u
solves (
1.5
) with some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
satisfies the time-dependent equation

i\partial_{t}\psi-\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi+|\psi|^{2^{*}-2}\psi=0,\ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.8)

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
\mu\geq 0
,
q\!\in\!\big(2,2+\frac{2}{N}\big)
,
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-1}
. Suppose the local well posed-ness of solutions to (
1.8
) holds for
\mu>0
(it holds for the case
\mu=0
, see Theorem 1.3 in
[
2
]
), then we can consider the stability of
\mathcal{M}_{a}
. The set
\mathcal{M}_{a}
is said to be
stable
under the Cauchy flow of (
1.8
) if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
\psi_{0}\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

\text{dist}_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}(\psi_{0},\mathcal{M}_{a})<\delta,

then the solution
\psi(t,\cdot)
of (
1.8
) with
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}
satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\text{dist}_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big(\psi(t,\cdot),\mathcal{M}_{a}\big)<\varepsilon,

where
T
is the maximal existence time for
\psi(t,\cdot)
. We have

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
2<q<2+\frac{2}{N}
,
\mu>0
and
a_{*}=a_{*}(\mu)>0
be given in Theorem
1.1
. Assume that the local well posed-ness of solutions to (
1.8
) holds. Then, for any
a\in(0,a_{*}]
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{a}
is compact, up to translation, and it is stable.

Remark 1.1.

In Theorem
1.1
, the ground state can be taken by a real valued, positive, radially symmetric decreasing function and the mass threshold value
a_{*}=a_{*}(\mu)>0
is explicit, see (
3.2
). Moreover,
a_{*}>0
can be taken arbitrary large by taking
\mu>0
small enough. Under the local well posed-ness of (
1.8
), Theorem
1.3
indicates that a small
L^{2}
-subcritical term leads to a stabilization of standing waves corresponding to (
1.8
), see e.g.
[
2
,
7
]
.
Our results complete the works of
[
2
,
3
]
and extend the results of Jeanjean et al.
[
15
,
16
]
, which studied nonlinear Schrödinger equations with combined nonlinearities, to half-wave. We also extend the results of H. J. Luo and Z. T. Zhang
[
19
]
to Sobolev critical case. The works of
[
2
]
focused on half wave in purely mass-supercritical and energy subcritical setting, and showed that the positive ground states are unique up to space shift and phase multiplication.
Therefore, our results indicate that the mass subcritical term enriches the set of solutions to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).

Furthermore, it is worth pointing out that the precise description of the local minimizers
\{u_{a}\}
is new in
L^{2}
-constraint variational problems. Usually, proof of such a precise description depends on a uniqueness of positive solutions to the corresponding limit problem, see
[
13
]
. However, this uniqueness result for following limit problem

\sqrt{-\Delta}u+u=|u|^{q-2}u\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}

is still unknown.
We develop another way to derive such precise description of
\{u_{a}\}
, the main ingredients are more accurate estimates of the ground state energy and Lagrange multiplier together with a mass minimality of the ground state solution, see Lemma
3.7
for details.

In the proof of Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
, the main difficulty lies in that the term
|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
is mass super-critical and energy critical. Although we have
\inf_{u\in S(a)}F_{\mu}(u)=-\infty
, the mass subcritical term
\mu|u|^{q-2}u
created a convex-concave geometry of
F_{\mu}|_{S(a)}
if
a>0
is small. So it is reasonable to study a local minimization problem

m(a):=\inf_{u\in V(a)}F_{\mu}(u)<0\leq\inf_{u\in\partial V(a)}F_{\mu}(u),

where
V(a)\!:=\!\{u\in S(a):\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\!<\!\rho_{0}\}
and
\partial V(a)\!:=\!\{u\in S(a):\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\!=\!\rho_{0}\}
. We use
m(a)\!<\!0
to exclude the vanishing of any minimizing sequences for
m(a)
. Moreover, a scaling argument gives the subadditivity of
m(a)
:

m(a)\leq m(\alpha)+m(\sqrt{a^{2}-\alpha^{2}}),~~\forall\alpha\in(0,a),

which is used to rule out the dichotomy of any minimizing sequences for
m(a)
. Therefore,
m(a)
is attained by some
u_{a}\!\in\!V(a)
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we follow some ideas from
[
21
]
. The main ingredient is the refined upper bound of
m(a)
(see Section 3), i.e.

m(a)<-K_{N,q}a^{\frac{2q(1-\gamma_{q})}{2-q\gamma_{q}}}<0.

This refinement needs to keep the testing functions staying in the admissible set
V(a)
. We overcome this difficulty by utilising the properties of the unique positive radial ground state
Q
of

\sqrt{-\Delta}u+u=|u|^{q-2}u\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.9)

where
2<q<2+\frac{2}{N}
. Noticing that the uniqueness of positive solutions to (
1.9
) is still unknown, so it brings new difficulties in deriving the precise description of the local minimizers
\{u_{a}\}
. Fortunately, by the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

||u||_{q}\leq C_{opt}(N,q)\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{\gamma_{q}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{q}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall u\in{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{opt}(N,q)}=\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1/2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{\gamma_{q}}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\|u\|^{1-\gamma_{q}}_{2}}{\|u\|_{q}}=\Big[\frac{\gamma_{q}}{1-\gamma_{q}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}}(1-\gamma_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\|Q\|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}_{2},

we conclude that every ground state of (
1.9
) has the least
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm among all nontrivial solutions to (
1.9
). This is enough to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, see Section 3.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we follow the approach of
[
16
]
. Since
\inf_{u\in S(a)}F_{\mu}(u)=-\infty
, we can obtain a bounded Palais-Smale sequence by using the Pohozaev constraint approach (see
[
25
,
26
]
). However, it is very difficult to prove the compactness of a Palais-Smale sequence at positive energy level. Recall that, N. Soave
[
26
]
raised an open question on how to obtain the second solution of the Sobolev critical equation (
1.3
) since the associated energy functional constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere admits a convex-concave geometry. In
[
16
]
, L. Jeanjean and T. T. Le observed that a better energy estimate may contribute to the understanding of this open question and they succeeded in solving this question if
N\geq 4
.
Motivated by
[
16
]
, we drive a better energy estimate on the associated mountain pass level (see Proposition
4.9
), i.e.

0<M^{0}(a)=M(a)<m(a)+\frac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}.

As
m(a)\!<\!0
, the revised upper bound
m(a)+\frac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}
is better than the well-known energy threshold
\frac{\mathcal{S}^{N}}{2N}
. This is enough to guarantee the compactness of Palais-Smale sequence at the mountain pass level
M(a)>0
.

Remark 1.2.

With slightly modifications, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
in this paper can be easily extended to the general fractional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.4
) with
0<\sigma<1
,
\mu>0
,
N>2\sigma
,
p=2_{\sigma}^{\ast}=\frac{2N}{N-2\sigma}
and
2<q<2+\frac{4\sigma}{N}
. Notice that when extending Theorem
1.2
, if we follow the approach of
[
16
]
to (
1.4
), in our compactness argument, the energy threshold of such compactness
m_{\sigma}(a)+\frac{\sigma}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N}
can be carry out only for dimensions
N\geq 4\sigma
, see Proposition
4.9
, where
m_{\sigma}(a)
is the ground state energy relating to (
1.4
). Turning to directly use the radial superposition of a ground state obtained in Theorem
1.1
and the Aubin-Talenti bubble as the test function in estimating mountain pass energy threshold, then Theorem
1.2
can be extended to
{(-\Delta)}^{\sigma}
for
0<\sigma<1
in all dimensions
N>2\sigma
. These are motivated by a very recently work
[
27
]
.

This paper is organized as follows, in Section 2, we give some preliminary results. The proof of Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
are given in Section 3, Section 4 respectively.

Notations:

The Hilbert space
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big\},

with the inner product and norm given respectively by

(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv,\hskip 9.24994pt\|u\|:=\Big(\big\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\big\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

\big\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\big\|_{2}^{2}:=\frac{C_{N,\sigma}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}
and
C_{N,\sigma}
is some positive normalization constant. The space
{\dot{H}}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the norm

||u||_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}u|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The dual space of
X
is denoted by
X^{\prime}

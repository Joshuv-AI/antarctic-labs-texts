In particular, we consider the cases A = C ⁡ ( X ) A=C(X) for X X a compact Hausdorff space and A = C ∞ ​ ( X ) A=C^{\infty}(X) for X X a compact smooth manifold. 1.
Introduction

All algebras are assumed to be commutative and all dg-algebras are assumed to be graded-commutative.
To an algebra
A
over a field
k
one associates a dg-algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}_{A|k}
with
\Omega^{0}_{A|k}=A
, called the de Rham dg-algebra, in a standard way (see Subsection
2.4
or
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign 3
]
).
Our main focus will be the cases of
\mathbb{R}
-algebras
A=C(X)
for a compact Hausdorff space
X
,
A=C^{\infty}(M)
for a smooth manifold
M
(possibly with boundary) and
A=\mathrm{PPol}_{K}(|K|)
, the algebra of piecewise polynomial functions on a polyhedron
K
.
Here all functions are assumed to be real-valued, but our results will hold for complex-valued functions also.
We study the cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{A|k})
.

There is a canonical morphism of dg-algebras
\pi:\Omega^{\bullet}_{C^{\infty}(M)|\mathbb{R}}\to\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
, where
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
is the dg-algebra of smooth differential
\mathbb{R}
-forms on
M
(see Subsection
4.2
).
The morphism
\pi
is the identity in degree
0
.
It is not an isomorphism.
For example, the equality
df(t)=(\partial f/\partial t)dt
holds in
\Omega^{1}_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})|\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
f(t)
is an algebraic function of
t
(
[
19
, Corollary of Proposition
1
]
).
Moreover, if
M
is of dimension
\geq 1
, then the cardinality of any set of generators for
\Omega^{1}_{C^{\infty}(M)|\mathbb{R}}
as a
C^{\infty}(M)
-module is at least that of
\mathbb{R}
(
[
8
, Corollary
15
]
).
The map on cohomology, induced by
\pi
, is not an isomorphism either, see
[
19
]
for the proof that the closed form
dt/(1+t^{2})
is not exact in
\Omega^{\bullet}_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})|\mathbb{R}}
.
On the other hand, consider the algebra
A
of regular functions on a smooth affine variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
.
It is a subalgebra of
C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{C})
with inclusion
i:A\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{C})
.
Consider the composition

\Omega^{\bullet}_{A|\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\Omega_{i}}\Omega^{\bullet}_{C^{\infty}(V)|\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathbb{C}}}\Omega^{\bullet}(V,\mathbb{C}),

where
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
is the analogue of
\pi
over
\mathbb{C}
.
Grothendieck’s comparison theorem states that the induced map
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{A|\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}(V,\mathbb{C}))
is an isomorphism (
[
10
, Theorem
1^{\prime}
]
).

\mathcal{F}
on a compact Hausdorff space

.
This map is natural with respect to morphisms of ringed spaces (see Proposition

We are mostly interested in the case

.

In the paper we only consider algebraic structures, however one can consider topological algebras.
Using projective tensor products, one can define the dg-algebra

.

Follows from Lemmas

Proof.

Follows from Lemma

Lemma 18

The map

Proof.

Proposition 20

(Stokes’ formula)

For every

\mathfrak{J}_{n}(d\eta)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\mathfrak{J}_{n-1}(\partial_{i}\eta).

We have

\mathfrak{J}_{n}(d\eta)=\left<d\eta,\mathrm{id}_{\Delta^{n}}\right>=\left<\eta,\partial\,\mathrm{id}_{\Delta^{n}}\right>=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\left<\eta,\gamma_{i}\right>=

=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\left<\partial_{i}\eta,\mathrm{id}_{\Delta^{n-1}}\right>=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\mathfrak{J}_{n-1}(\partial_{i}\eta).

The third equality follows from Proposition

9.1.
Polyhedra and rectilinear maps

A

K
is a finite set of affine simplices in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

(1)

for any
a\in K
and any face

we have

;

(2)

if

then

is either a common face of
a
and
b
or empty.

A subset

of

that is also a polyhedron is called a
subpolyhedron
of
K
.
Define the space
|K|\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
as the union of all simplices of
K
.

We say that a function

is a
rectilinear map
if for any

there exists

such that
f(a)\subset b
and
f
maps
a
to
b
affinely.
We call two rectilinear maps

adjacent
if for every simplex

the set

is contained in a simplex of
K^{\prime}
.

We say that a polyhedron
P
is a
minor
of a polyhedron

if
|P|\subset|K|
and
|P|\hookrightarrow|K|
is a rectilinear map (in other words, for any
a\in P
there is
b\in K
such that
a\subset b
).
We call a minor
P
of
K
a
subdivision
if
|P|=|K|
.

We call a polyhedron

a
star
with the center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
if
\{x\}
is a vertex of
S
and each maximal by inclusion simplex of
S
has
\{x\}
as a vertex.
Take a polyhedron

with

, then a minor
S
of
K
is called a
star neighborhood
of

if
S
is a star with center
x
and

.

For a polyhedron

consider the algebra

of functions
\varphi:|K|\to\mathbb{R}
such that for each simplex

\varphi|_{a}
is a polynomial.
This algebra was considered, for example, in
[

]
.
The algebra

is contravariant with respect to rectilinear maps, in particular, if

is an inclusion of a minor we have the restriction homomorphism

Lemma 59
.

Suppose
k
is of characteristic
0
.
Suppose
F/k
is a field extension and
L/F
is a finite field extension.
Then the map

\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{i}):\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{L|k})

induced by the inclusion
i:F\hookrightarrow L
is injective.

Proof.

There is a morphism of complexes
\Sigma:\Omega^{\bullet}_{L|k}\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k}
with the following properties (see
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign 16
]
):

(1)

if we consider
\Omega^{\bullet}_{L|k}
as a
\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k}
-module, then
\Sigma
is
\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k}
-linear;

(2)

the restriction of
\Sigma
to the elements of degree
0
coincides with the trace
\sigma:L\to F
.

Therefore, the composition

\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k}\xrightarrow{\Omega_{i}}\Omega^{\bullet}_{L|k}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\Omega^{\bullet}_{F|k}

is the multiplication by
\sigma(1)=[L:F]
.
As
k
is of characteristic zero the map
\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{i})
is an injection.
∎

Lemma 60
.

Let
K/k
be a field extension, where
K
is an infinite field, and
K(\Gamma)/K
, where
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{l}\}
, be a purely transcendental extension.
Then the map

\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{i}):\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K|k})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K(\Gamma)|k}),

induced by the inclusion
i:K\hookrightarrow K(\Gamma)
, is injective.

Proof.

The field
K(\Gamma)
is the colimit over finite sets
S\subset K[\Gamma]-\{0\}
of the localizations of
K[\Gamma]
:

K(\Gamma)\cong\underset{S\subset K[\Gamma]-\{0\}}{\underset{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{lim\,}}}K[\Gamma][S^{-1}].

Therefore, it is enough to prove that the map

\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{i_{S}}):\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K|k})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K[\Gamma][S^{-1}]|k})

induced by the inclusion
i_{S}:K\hookrightarrow K[\Gamma][S^{-1}]
is injective for each
S
.

There exists a point
(\bar{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\bar{\gamma}_{l})\in K^{l}
which is not a zero of any element of
S
.
Consider the homomorphism
K[\Gamma]\to K
,
\gamma_{j}\mapsto\bar{\gamma}_{j}
.
By the universal property of localization it can be extended to a homomorphism
\tau:K[\Gamma][S^{-1}]\to K
such that
\tau|_{K}
is the identity.
Therefore, the composition

\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K|k})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{i_{S}})}\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K[\Gamma][S^{-1}]|k})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}(\Omega_{\tau})}\mathrm{H}^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{K|k})

is the identity map, which suffices.
∎

Lemma 61
.

Suppose the characteristic of
k
is zero.
The equation

(10)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})F_{i}=c

where
c\in k
and
c\neq 0
, has no solutions in rational functions
F_{i}\in k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
.
For
n=0
the claim is obvious.
Take
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that, for each
i
,
x_{1}
is not a factor in the denominators of
x_{1}^{l}F_{i}
.
Multiply the equation (
10
) by
x_{1}^{l}
and apply
\left(\partial/\partial x_{1}\right)^{l}
to both sides of the equation.
We get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\partial/\partial x_{1}\right)^{l}(x_{1}^{l}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})F_{i})=l!c.

Notice that the operators
x(\partial/\partial x)
and
\left(\partial/\partial x\right)^{l}x^{l}
commute.
That can be easily checked by induction.
Hence, the operators
x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})
and
\left(\partial/\partial x_{j}\right)^{l}x^{l}_{j}
commute for any
i
and
j
.
We get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})\left(\partial/\partial x_{1}\right)^{l}(x_{1}^{l}F_{i})=l!c.

Put
G_{i}:=\left(\partial/\partial x_{1}\right)^{l}(x_{1}^{l}F_{i})
.
We obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})G_{i}=l!c.

But none of
G_{i}
has
x_{1}
as a factor in the denominator so we can substitute
x_{1}:=0
in the above equation and obtain

\sum_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})(G_{i}|_{x_{1}=0})=l!c.

By the induction hypothesis this equation has no solutions in rational functions.
∎

Lemma 62
.

Suppose the characteristic of
k
is zero.
The closed form

\omega=\dfrac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\dfrac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}\in\Omega^{n}_{k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|k}

is not exact.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
\eta\in\Omega^{n-1}_{k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|k}
such that
d\eta=\omega
.
Write
\eta
as

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}

where
F_{i}\in k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Then

d\eta=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}(\partial/\partial x_{i})F_{i}\right]dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}.

The equality
d\eta=\omega
then takes the form

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}(\partial/\partial x_{i})F_{i}\right]dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}=\dfrac{1}{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}.

The vector
dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
of the vector space
\Omega^{n}_{k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|k}
over the field
k(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is not zero
[
17
, p.
201
]
.
Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}(\partial/\partial x_{i})F_{i}=\dfrac{1}{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}.

Put
G_{i}:=(-1)^{i+1}x_{1}\dots\hat{x}_{i}\dots x_{n}F_{i}
.
Then the above equation takes the form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(\partial/\partial x_{i})G_{i}=1.

By Lemma
61
this equation has no solutions in rational functions.
∎

Theorem 63
.

Suppose
k
is of characteristic
0
.
Take

a
k
-algebra and a set of invertible elements

in

By Lemma
7
it is enough to consider

By Lemma

there is a prime ideal

such that the elements

are still algebraically independent in

Consider the field of fractions

which is finitely generated over

The elements

are algebraically independent in

Take a finite transcendental basis
\hat{\Gamma}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{l}\}
of

over

The field

is a finite extension of

By Lemma

and Lemma

the composition

The image of

\omega_{0}=\dfrac{da_{1}}{a_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\dfrac{da_{n}}{a_{n}}\in\Omega^{n}_{k(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})|k},

.

be a topological space and

.

For the second part, we prove that
\Psi_{A}([\omega])=0
.
Denote by
i:A\hookrightarrow C(X)
the inclusion.
Consider the algebra
E\subset C(\mathbb{R})
generated as an algebra by the functions
e^{\lambda t}\in C(\mathbb{R})
.
We have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

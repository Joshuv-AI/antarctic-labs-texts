Minimal Axiomatization of Boolean Algebras
Eugene Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we present a new axiomatic system that is a minimal axiomatization of Boolean algebras. Furthermore, the symmetric difference is shown to be algebraically analogous to the modular difference of two numbers. Finally, a new method to prove equivalent relations in a Boolean algebra is also given. Key Word: Boolean algebra; Modular difference; Equivalence method MSC Class: 03G05; 03G25

Introduction

In 1933, Huntington showed that a Boolean algebra can be axiomatized by three axioms (
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
) as follows:

H1.

\qquad a+b\,=\,b+a

H2.

\qquad\left(a+b\right)+c\,=\,a+\left(b+c\right)

H3.

\qquad\left(a^{\prime}+b\right)^{\prime}+\left(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}\,=\,a

However, this claim is incorrect because there are also three definitions assumed in the above Huntington system as
\,ab=\left(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)^{\prime},\,1=a+a^{\prime}
and
\,0=\left(a+a^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}
(definition 4.7, 4.12 and 4.13 in
[
4
]
), which should be considered as axioms. This is because the above three definitions are provable in an axiomatic system in which they are not axioms (such as a standard Boolean algebra). Thus the Huntington system actually assumes six axioms and should be seven in total including an axiom for the difference operator (
a-b=ab^{\prime}
).

Since
\,ab=a-b^{\prime}
and
\,a+b=\left(a^{\prime}-b\right)^{\prime}
, a Boolean algebra (with the difference operator) can be expressed in terms of the difference only which is known as a difference algebra. In 1914, Bernstein presented the first version of difference algebras (
[
1
]
and
[
6
, p100]
) as:

B1.

\qquad a-\left(b-1\right)\,=\,a

B2.

\qquad(a-a)-c\,=\,\left(b-b\right)-d

B3.

\qquad a-b=b-a\,\,\Longrightarrow\,a=b

B4.

\qquad(a-b)-\left(c-d\right)\,=\,\left(\left(\left(a-b\right)-d^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\left(c^{\prime}-a^{\prime}\right)-b\right)\right)^{\prime}

In 1920, Taylor improved the above Bernstein system (
[
7
]
and
[
6
, p100]
) as:

T1.

\qquad a-\left(b-b\right)\,=\,a

T2.

\qquad a-b^{\prime}\,=\,b-a^{\prime}

T3.

\qquad a-\left(b-c\right)\,=\,\left(\left(a-b\right)^{\prime}-\left(a-c^{\prime}\right)\right)^{\prime}

However, both systems are problematic because a good axiomatic system should not contain reducible axioms.
1
1

1

As a result, both were published in the University of California Publications and so less well-known.
For examples, in the Bernstein system, B1 should be
\,a-0=a
and B2 is actually
0=0
; in the Taylor system, T1 should be
\,a-0=a
. In both systems,
a-a=0
is a definition that should be an axiom.

In the first part of this paper, we will introduce a new axiomatic system with only three (solid) axioms that is a minimal axiomatization of difference algebras and overcomes the problems of previous models. Furthermore, with two additional axioms for addition and multiplication, it becomes a minimal axiomatization of Boolean algebras, reducing the seven axioms of the Huntington system by two. Our model is complete itself and thus can realize Huntington’s claim that a Boolean algebra can be axiomatized by three axioms in another sense.

In the second part of this paper, we will show that the symmetric difference in a Boolean algebra is algebraically analogous to the modular (absolute) difference of two numbers. This analogy is better than the well-known Boolean sum in the algebraic sense. Furthermore, we will present a new method that can prove equivalent relations of a Boolean algebra in a single step and easily find new relations.

b-a\leq c\,
do not imply
\,a+b\leq c
. As the result, the symmetric difference in
\Im
is algebraically closer to the modular difference of numbers than the Boolean sum, and so is called the modular difference.

3.2
A New Equivalence Method

In this section, we will present a new way to prove equivalent relations in a Boolean algebra.

Definition 3.4

Suppose
\phi
is a relation in
\Im
. Then
f_{\phi}
is an
equation
of
\phi
iff
\,\phi\Leftrightarrow f_{\phi}=0
.

Axiom 3.5

Suppose
\phi
and
\varphi
are relations in
\Im
with
f_{\phi}
and
f_{\varphi}
being their equations.

(i)

f_{\phi\wedge\varphi}\,=f_{\phi}+f_{\varphi}

(ii)

f_{\phi\vee\varphi}\,=f_{\phi}\times f_{\varphi}

Theorem 3.6

Suppose
\,\phi
and
\varphi
are relations in
\Im
with
f_{\phi}
and
f_{\varphi}
being their equations.

f_{\phi}=f_{\varphi}\,\Longrightarrow\,\left(\phi\Longleftrightarrow\varphi\right)

Proof.

By III’

f_{\phi}=f_{\varphi}\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,\left(\left(f_{\phi}=0\,\Longrightarrow f_{\varphi}=0\right)\,\wedge\,\left(f_{\varphi}=0\,\Longrightarrow f_{\phi}=0\right)\right)

So it follows by definition
3.4
.

Remark 3.7

Theorem
3.6
can prove equivalent relations in a single step.

Corollary 3.8

(i)

f_{a\leq b}\,=\,a-b

(ii)

f_{a=0\,\wedge\,b=0}\,=\,a+b

(iii)

f_{a=b}\,=\,\left|a-b\right|

Proof.

(i) By definition
3.4
and
2.5
.

(ii) By axiom
3.5
(i).

(iii) By proposition
2.33
(ii) and definition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.9

(i)

a\leq b\,\Longleftrightarrow\,a=a\cdot b

(ii)

a\leq c\,\wedge\,b\leq c\,\Longleftrightarrow\,a+b\leq c

(iii)

\left(a+b=a\cdot b\right)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,a=b

(iv)

\left(a+c=b+c\,\wedge\,a\cdot c=b\cdot c\right)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,a=b

(v)

\left|a-b\right|=\left|a-c\right|\,\Longleftrightarrow\,b=c

(vi)

a=\left|b-c\right|\,\Longleftrightarrow\,b=\left|a-c\right|

Proof.

We only prove (v). The rest are straightforward.

(v) By corollary
3.8
(iii) and lemma
3.2

\displaystyle\left|\left|a-b\right|-\left|a-c\right|\right|

\displaystyle\,\,=\,\,\left|\left|\left|b-a\right|-a\right|-c\right|

\displaystyle\,\,=\,\,\left|\left|b-\left|a-a\right|\right|-c\right|

\displaystyle\,\,=\,\,\left|\left|b-0\right|-c\right|

\displaystyle\,\,=\,\,\left|b-c\right|

So (v) follows by theorem
3.6
.

Theorem
3.6
can also help to find new equivalent relations. Let’s take a look at the following examples.

Problem 3.10

Suppose
\,\phi\Longleftrightarrow\left(a\cdot c\leq b\cdot d\,\wedge\,a\leq b+c\,\wedge\,c\leq a+d\right)
. Find an equivalent relation of
\phi
.

Solution.

By corollary
3.8

\displaystyle f_{\phi}

\displaystyle\>=\>\left(ac-bd\right)+\left(a-\left(b+c\right)\right)+\left(c-\left(a+d\right)\right)

\displaystyle\>=\>ab^{\prime}c+acd^{\prime}+ab^{\prime}c^{\prime}+a^{\prime}cd^{\prime}

\displaystyle\>=\>\left(a-b\right)+\left(c-d\right)

So by theorem
3.6
,
\ \phi\Longleftrightarrow\left(a\leq b\,\wedge\,c\leq d\right)
.

Problem 3.11

Suppose
\,\varphi\Longleftrightarrow\left(\left(a+b\right)\cdot c\,=\,c-a\cdot b\right)
. Find an equivalent relation of
\varphi
.

Solution.

By corollary
3.8

\displaystyle f_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\>=\>|(ac+bc)-(c-ab)|

\displaystyle\>=\>\left(\left(ac+bc\right)-\left(a^{\prime}c+b^{\prime}c\right)\right)+\left(\left(a^{\prime}c+b^{\prime}c\right)-\left(ac+bc\right)\right)

\displaystyle\>=\>abc+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c

So by theorem
3.6
,
\varphi\Longleftrightarrow\left(c\leq a+b\,\wedge\,c\leq a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)
.

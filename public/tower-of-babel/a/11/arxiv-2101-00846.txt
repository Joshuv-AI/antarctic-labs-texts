Compatible systems of ℓ \ell -adic sheaves
Quentin Guignard

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of compatibility for families ( ℱ ℓ ) ℓ (\mathcal{F}_{\ell})_{\ell} of bounded constructible ℓ \ell -adic complexes of étale sheaves on schemes. For schemes of finite type over a field, this notion is preserved by the usual six functors. We prove that the compatibility of a family is preserved by the nearby cycles functor and by the linearized ε \varepsilon -factors from [ Gu19-2 ] . We establish independence of ℓ \ell for the characteristic cycles and characteristic ε \varepsilon -cycles of compatible families.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

In
[
Ill10
, 5.4]
, Illusie formulates the problem of associating to any reasonable scheme
X
a triangulated category

M(X,\mathbb{Q})\subseteq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\end{subarray}}D_{c}^{b}\left(X\left[\frac{1}{\ell}\right],\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\right),

of families of étale
\ell
-adic complexes on
X
, satisfying the six functors formalism, i.e. stability by
f^{*},f^{!},Rf_{*},Rf_{!},RHom,\otimes^{L}
, and satisfying properties of uniform constructibility similar to those proved in
[
Ill10
, 2.5-2.7]
. For example, for any family
(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})_{\ell}
in
M(X,\mathbb{Q})
, we would like to ask for the existence of a dense open subset
U
of
X
such that for each
\ell
, the cohomology sheaves of
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
are lisse on
U\left[\frac{1}{\ell}\right]
.

1.2.

More generally, if
E
is a number field, one can similarly ask for triangulated categories
M(X,E)
in
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\end{subarray}}D_{c}^{b}\left(X\left[\ell_{\lambda}^{-1}\right],E_{\lambda}\right)
satisfying the same stabilities and uniform constructibility properties, where
\lambda
runs over all non archimedean places of
E
and where
\ell_{\lambda}
is the residual characteristic of a place
\lambda
.

1.3.

We consider in this text the smallest collection of triangulated categories
M(X,E)
as above, satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)

for any object
\mathcal{F}
of
M(X,E)
, the Tate twist
\mathcal{F}(1)
is in
M(X,E)
as well;

(2)

for any object
\mathcal{F}
in
D_{c}^{b}(X,E)
, the family
(\mathcal{F}\otimes_{E}E_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is in
M(X,E)
;

(3)

for any separated morphism of finite presentation
f:X\rightarrow Y
, the functor
Rf_{!}
sends
M(X,E)
to
M(Y,E)
.

We refer to
2.4
,
2.20
,
4.3
for precise definitions. We note that these categories contain most complexes of interest in the study of exponential sums over finite fields. Indeed, sheaves of Kümmer or Artin-Schreier type are already in
D_{c}^{b}(X,E)
for a suitable number field
E
, hence belong to
M(X,E)
.

1.4.

We prove that these triangulated categories
M(X,E)
have the following properties:

(1)

uniform constructibility holds for families in
M(X,E)
(cf.
2.14
);

(2)

stability by
Rf_{!},f^{*},\otimes
(cf.
2.7
,
2.21
);

(3)

stability by the vanishing and nearby cycles functors (cf.
3.9
,
3.11
);

(4)

for schemes of finite type over a field, stability by
Rf_{*},f^{!},RHom
(cf.
4.2
,
4.6
,
4.7
).

We refer to the indicated sections for precise statements. We more generally construct triangulated categories
M(X,Q,E)
of
Q
-equivariant
E
-linear coefficients, for a profinite group
Q
acting admissibly on
X
. This additional generality is useful in certain applications, but is also needed to state and prove the stability by the nearby cycles functor. We should note that the tools used to prove the latter stability, including the equivariant version of de Jong’s semistable alteration theorem, were developped in Vidal’s seminal article
[
Vi04
]
in order to generalize the results from
[
Ill81
]
.

1.5.

For a scheme
X
of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
, families
(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
in
M(X,E)
are
E
-compatible in the classical sense, i.e. for any closed point
x
of
X
, the local
L
-function

\det(1-T\mathrm{Frob}_{x}|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\overline{x}}),

is a rational function with coefficients in
E
, which is independent of
\lambda
(cf.
6.2
). For schemes of finite type over a finite field, this notion of compatibility is already known to be preserved by the six functors
f^{*},f^{!},Rf_{*},Rf_{!},RHom,\otimes^{L}
, by results of Gabber, cf. Fujiwara’s account of his proofs in
[
Fu02
]
. Similar results in the context of schemes of finite type over a local field have been obtained by Zheng
[
Zh09
]
. The corresponding stabilities of
M(X,E)
can be considered as a refinement of these results:
E
-compatibility is a property of virtual sheaves, while belonging to
M(X,E)
is a property of the actual complexes.

Similarly, the aforementioned stabilities of
M(X,E)
, together with the
E
-compatibility of families in
M(X,E)
or
M(X,Q,E)
, form a refinement of various
\ell
-independence results, such as those of Ochiai
[
Oc99
, Th. B]
, Vidal
[
Vi04
, Prop. 4.2]
, or Kato
[
Ka19
, Prop. 5.2]
.

(Cor.

.

(Prop.

1.12.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the triangulated categories

, or rather their equivariant versions

, from which

is deduced by localization and inversion of the Tate twist, and we prove their stability by the functors

. We then prove in Section

are preserved by the nearby and vanishing cycles functors, by using Gabber-Vidal’s equivariant version of de Jong’s semistable alteration theorem. The latter result is used in Section

for schemes of finite type over a field. We then prove Theorem

, or rather an equivariant version thereof, in Section

and prove Theorems

and

in Section

.

2.
Compatible systems

For any scheme
X
and any integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
X[\frac{1}{n}]
the fiber product
X\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{n}])
. Throughout this section, we fix a number field
E
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{E}
.

2.1.

A right action of a profinite group
Q
on a scheme
X
is said to be
admissible
if for any point
x
of
X
, there exists an affine open subset
U
of
X
containing
x
, such that
Q
stabilizes
U
and acts continuously on the discrete ring
\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X})
. We may also refer to a scheme endowed with an admissible right action of
Q
as a
Q
-scheme
.

2.2.

Let
X
be a scheme endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be a noetherian ring. A
Q
-equivariant étale sheaf
of
\Lambda
-modules on
X
is an étale sheaf of
\Lambda
-modules
\mathcal{F}
on
X
endowed with a collection
(\sigma(q))_{q\in Q}
of isomorphisms
\mathcal{F}\rightarrow q_{*}\mathcal{F}
such that:

(1)

we have
(q_{2*}\sigma(q_{1}))\sigma(q_{2})=\sigma(q_{1}q_{2})
for any
q_{1},q_{2}
in
Q
;

(2)

for any
Q
-equivariant étale morphism
U\rightarrow X
, with
U
quasi-compact, the left action of
Q
on the discrete set
\mathcal{F}(U)
is continuous.

We denote by
\mathrm{Sh}(X,Q,\Lambda)
the corresponding category, by
D(X,Q,\Lambda)
its the derived category, by
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\Lambda)
the corresponding category of bounded complexes with constructible cohomology, and by
D_{ctf}^{b}(X,Q,\Lambda)
the subcategory of complexes locally of finite Tor-amplitude.

2.3.

Let
\Lambda
be a noetherian ring of finite global dimension. For any integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\Lambda_{n}
the ring
\Lambda/n\Lambda
. Let
\widehat{\Lambda}
be the projective limit of
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Let
X
be a scheme endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
. A
Q
-equivariant
\widehat{\Lambda}
-system
on
X
is an object
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of the category

\prod_{n\geq 1}D_{ctf}^{b}\left(X[\frac{1}{n}],Q,\Lambda_{n}\right),

with isomorphisms
\theta_{n,m}:\mathcal{F}_{m}\otimes^{L}_{\Lambda_{m}}\Lambda_{n}\rightarrow(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{|X[\frac{1}{m}]}
for
n
dividing
m
, such that
\theta_{l,n}\theta_{n,m}=\theta_{l,m}
. The
Q
-equivariant
\widehat{\Lambda}
-systems on
X
form a triangulated category, which we abusively denote by
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\Lambda})
.

2.4.

Let
X
be a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
. We define a category
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
as the smallest triangulated subcategory of
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
(cf.
2.3
) which contains all
Q
-equivariant
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
-systems of the form

(Ra_{!}(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))_{n\geq 1},

where
a:Z\rightarrow X
is a separated morphism of finite presentation and
\mathcal{F}
is in
D_{c}^{b}(Z,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
(cf.
2.2
).

2.5.

Let
X
be a scheme endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
. The category
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
of
effective
Q
-equivariant
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-systems
on
X
is the full subcategory of
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{E}})
(cf.
2.3
) consisting of
Q
-equivariant
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
-systems
\mathcal{F}
on
X
with the following property: for any point
x
of
X
, there exists a
Q
-invariant affine open subscheme
U
such that
\mathcal{F}_{|U}=(\mathcal{F}_{n|U})_{n}
belongs to
\widetilde{M}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
(cf.
2.4
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
. Then the inclusion functor

\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})\rightarrow M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E}),

is an equivalence of categories.

We first remark that if
j:U\rightarrow X
is the immersion of a
Q
-invariant quasicompact open subscheme of
X
, then the functor
j_{!}:\mathcal{F}\mapsto(j_{!}\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
from
D_{c}^{b}(U,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
sends
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(U,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
to
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
. Indeed, if
a:Z\rightarrow U
is a separated morphism of finite presentation and if
\mathcal{F}
is an object of
D_{c}^{b}(Z,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
, then we have

j_{!}(Ra_{!}(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))_{n}\simeq(R(aj)_{!}(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))_{n},

and
aj:U\rightarrow X
is a separated
Q
-equivariant morphism of finite presentation.

Let us now consider an object
\mathcal{F}
of
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
. Since
X
is quasicompact, there exists a finite cover
(U_{r})_{r\in R}
by
Q
-invariant quasicompact open subschemes of
X
such that
\mathcal{F}_{|U_{r}}
belongs to
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{r},Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
for each
r
in
R
. We prove by induction on the cardinality of
R
that
\mathcal{F}
belongs to
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
, the case where
R
has at most one element being trivial. We denote by
j_{U}:U\rightarrow X
the open immersion associated to an open subset
U
of
X
. Let
r_{0}
be an element of
R
, and let us consider
U=U_{r_{0}}
and
V=\bigcup_{r\neq r_{0}}U_{r}
. Then we have a distinguished triangle

(j_{U\cap V})_{!}j_{U\cap V}^{*}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow(j_{U})_{!}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{F}\oplus(j_{V})_{!}j_{V}^{*}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{[1]}.

The objects
(j_{U})_{!}j_{U}^{*}\mathcal{F},(j_{V})_{!}j_{V}^{*}\mathcal{F}
and
(j_{U\cap V})_{!}j_{U\cap V}^{*}\mathcal{F}
of
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
belong to
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
by induction and by the first part of the proof, hence the conclusion.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
Q
be a profinite group, and let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a
Q
-equivariant morphism of schemes.

(1)

The functor
f^{*}:\mathcal{F}\mapsto(f^{*}\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
from
D_{c}^{b}(Y,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
sends
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(Y,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
.

(2)

If
f
is separated of finite presentation, then the functor
Rf_{!}:\mathcal{F}\mapsto(Rf_{!}\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
from
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(Y,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
sends
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(Y,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
.

For the first part it is enough to prove that for any affine
Q
-invariant open subschemes
U
and
V
of
X
and
Y
respectively, with
f(U)
contained in
V
, then the functor
f^{*}
from
D_{c}^{b}(V,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(U,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
sends
\widetilde{M}(V,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
to
\widetilde{M}(U,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
. This follows from the following remark: if
a:Z\rightarrow V
is a separated morphism of finite presentation and if
\mathcal{F}
is an object of
D_{c}^{b}(Z,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
, then by the proper base change theorem we have

f^{*}(Ra_{!}(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))_{n}\simeq(Ra^{\prime}_{!}((f^{\prime})^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes^{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))_{n},

where
a^{\prime}:Z\times_{V}U\rightarrow U
is the base change of
a
along
f:U\rightarrow V
, hence is a separated
Q
-equivariant morphism of finite presentation, and
f^{\prime}:Z\times_{V}U\rightarrow Z
is the first projection.

We now prove
(2)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an object of
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})
. Let
V
be any affine
Q
-invariant open subscheme of
Y
. Then
(Rf_{!}\mathcal{F})_{|V}
is isomorphic to
R(f_{|U})_{!}\mathcal{F}_{|U}
, where
U=f^{-1}(V)
. Since
f
is quasicompact and quasiseparated, we obtain that
U
is quasicompact and quasiseparated. By Proposition
2.6
, the object
\mathcal{F}_{|U}
of
D_{c}^{b}(U,Q,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
belongs to
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(U,Q,\mathcal{O}_{E})

3.1.

Let

S/Q

-trait

3.2.

Let
S
be a
Q
-trait, cf.

, such that the kernel of the homomorphism

is finite. Let
j:\eta\rightarrow S
and be the generic point of

and let

. Let
S_{0}
be the quotient trait

, with generic point
\eta_{0}
and special point

. Then
\eta
is a separable closure of
\eta_{0}
, the
Q
-trait

is the normalization of
S_{0}
in
\eta

\eta\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}\rightarrow\eta_{0}
, with

to

We thus have
Q=Q^{\prime}\times_{\mathrm{Gal}(\eta^{\prime}/\eta_{0})}\mathrm{Gal}(\eta/\eta_{0})
, hence an exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(\eta/\eta^{\prime})\rightarrow Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}\rightarrow 1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a ring. For any

-equivariant morphism

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

be a profinite group, let

be a
Q
-trait, cf.
3.1
, with generic point
\eta
and closed point

. Let
X
be a finitely presented

-equivariant
S
-scheme. Then the functor
R\Psi_{X/S}
from
D_{c}^{b}(X_{\eta},Q,\widehat{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(X_{s},Q,\widehat{E})
sends
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{\eta},Q,E)
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
.

We follow closely the proof of
[
Ka19
, 5.2]
. By Proposition
2.24
, it is enough to prove that objects of the form

R\Gamma^{I}(V\otimes_{E}Ra_{*}E),

are sent by
R\Psi_{X/S}
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
, where
a:Z\rightarrow X
is a proper
Q^{\prime}
-equivariant separated morphism of finite presentation, for some extension
Q^{\prime}
of

by a finite group

, and where

is a finite dimensional continuous

-representation of

.

Let
\overline{a}:\overline{Z}\rightarrow X
be a proper finitely presented

-equivariant morphism whose pullback along

coincides with

. Let
\overline{a}_{s}:\overline{Z}_{s}\rightarrow X_{s}
be the corresponding morphism on the special fibers. Then the proper base change theorem yields

R\Psi_{X/S}R\Gamma^{I}(V\otimes_{E}Ra_{*}E)\simeq R\Gamma^{I}(V\otimes_{E}R(\overline{a}_{s})_{*}R\Psi_{\overline{Z}/S}E),

hence Theorem

-equivariant

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
Q
be a profinite group. Let
S
be a
Q
-trait and let

be a finitely presented
Q
-equivariant
S
-scheme. Then the object
R\Psi_{X/S}E
of

We prove Lemma
3.10
by induction on the dimension

of
X_{\eta}
, the case

being trivial. We can assume (and we do) that
Q
acts faithfully on the pair
(X,S)
, so that the kernel of the homomorphism
Q\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(S)^{\mathrm{op}}
is finite. By Proposition
3.6
, we can assume (and we henceforth do) that
S
is
Q
-excellent. Let
\eta\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}\rightarrow\eta_{0}
be as in
3.2
. Let
S^{\prime}
be the normalization of
S_{0}
in
\eta^{\prime}
. We can assume (and we do) that
X
is of the form
X^{\prime}\times_{S^{\prime}}S
for some finitely presented
Q^{\prime}
-equivariant
S^{\prime}
-scheme
X^{\prime}
. Let
s^{\prime}
be the closed point of
S^{\prime}
. We need to prove that the object
R\Psi_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}E
of

(1)

Let
\tilde{\eta}\rightarrow\eta
be a finite field extension and let
\tilde{S}
be the normalization of

in
\tilde{\eta}
. Since

is separably closed, this extension is purely inseparable, and there is a unique admissible action of

on
\tilde{\eta}
such that
\tilde{\eta}\rightarrow\eta
is
Q
-equivariant. By enlarging
\eta^{\prime}
if necessary, we can assume that
\tilde{\eta}
is given by
\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}\times_{\eta^{\prime}}\eta

\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}

be the normalization of

in

[
SGA

⁤
4

1
2

(R\Psi_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E})_{|X_{s}[\frac{1}{n}]}\simeq(R\Psi_{X^{\prime}_{\tilde{S}^{\prime}}/\tilde{S}^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E})_{|X_{s}[\frac{1}{n}]}.

Consequently, the conclusion of Lemma
3.10
holds for the
Q
-equivariant morphism

if and only if it holds for the
Q
-equivariant morphism
X_{\tilde{S}}\rightarrow\tilde{S}

(2)

By
(1)
and by enlarging
\eta^{\prime}
if necessary, we can assume (and we do) that the irreducible components of

are geometrically integral over
\eta^{\prime}

be the normalization of

in

and

its inverse image by

Since the generic fibers of

(3)

By
(2)
, we can assume (and we do) that

is normal and that we have a finite decomposition

X^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{j\in J}Y^{(j)}

into disjoint open subsets, where

in

is the natural closed immersion. By Proposition

, if the conclusion of Lemma

holds for the

-equivariant morphism

for each

as well.

(4)

By
(3)
, we can assume (and we do) that
X^{\prime}
is normal and that

is geometrically integral. By

, we can assume (and we do) that

acts faithfully on

. By

. The profinite group

Let

be a

-invariant dense open subscheme, with complement

(5)

, we can assume (and we do) that

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
S
be a henselian trait, with closed point
s
and generic point
\eta
, endowed with an admissible right action of a profinite group
Q
, such that
\eta
is unramified over
\eta_{0}
. Let
X
be a
Q
-equivariant
S
-scheme of finite type and let
j:X_{\eta}\rightarrow X
be the canonical open immersion. Then the functor
Rj_{*}
from
D_{c}^{b}(X_{\eta},Q,\widehat{E})
to
D_{c}^{b}(X,Q,\widehat{E})
sends
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{\eta},Q,E)
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,E)
.

We keep the notation from
3.11
. Let
i:X_{s}\rightarrow X
be the canonical closed immersion. For any object
\mathcal{F}
of
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{\eta},Q,E)
, we have a distinguished triangle

j_{!}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow Rj_{*}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}Rj_{*}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{[1]},

and
j_{!}\mathcal{F}
is in
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X,Q,E)
. We now prove that
i^{*}Rj_{*}\mathcal{F}
is in
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
. Let
I_{\eta}
be the interia subgroup in
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\eta}/\eta)
. Then the quotient
\overline{Q}/I_{\eta}
coincides with
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{s}/s_{0})\times_{\mathrm{Gal}(s/s_{0})}Q
. Let us consider the composition

D_{c}^{b}(X_{\overline{s}},\overline{Q},\widehat{E})\xrightarrow{R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}}D_{c}^{b}(X_{\overline{s}},\overline{Q}/I_{\eta},\widehat{E})\simeq D_{c}^{b}(X_{s},Q,\widehat{E}),

which we still denote by

. Then we have

i^{*}Rj_{*}\mathcal{F}\simeq R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}(R\Psi_{X/S}\mathcal{F}).

By
3.11
, it is therefore sufficient to prove that
R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}
sends
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{\overline{s}},\overline{Q},E)
to
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
. Let us consider a
\overline{Q}
-equivariant separated morphism
a:Z\rightarrow X_{\overline{s}}
of finite presentation, and an object
\mathcal{G}
of
D_{c}^{b}(Z,\overline{Q},\mathcal{O}_{E})
. Let us prove that
R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}(Ra_{!}\mathcal{G})
is in
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
. There exists an open subgroup
I^{\prime}
of
I_{\eta}
, normal in
\overline{Q}
, which acts trivially on
(Z,\mathcal{F})
. Then there exists a
Q
-equivariant extension
\eta^{\prime}\rightarrow\eta
such that
I_{\eta^{\prime}}=I^{\prime}
. We have

R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}(Ra_{!}\mathcal{G})\simeq R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}/I_{\eta^{\prime}}}R\Gamma^{I_{\eta^{\prime}}}(Ra_{!}\mathcal{G}),

and
R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}/I_{\eta^{\prime}}}
has the desired property by Proposition
2.23
. By replacing
\eta
with
\eta^{\prime}
if necessary, we can assume (and we henceforth do) that

acts trivially on
(Z,\mathcal{F})
.

Let
p
be the characteristic exponent of
s
, and let
P
be the unique pro-
p
-Sylow subgroup of
I_{\eta}
. Then for any integer

, we have

R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}}(Ra_{!}(\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}^{L}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E}))\simeq R\Gamma^{I_{\eta}/P}(Ra_{!}(\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}^{L}\mathcal{O}_{E}/n\mathcal{O}_{E})),

is an exact functor on prime to

I_{\eta}/P
is canonically isomorphic to the procyclic group
\prod_{\ell\neq p}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
, where the Tate twist reflects the conjugation action of
\overline{Q}/I_{\eta}
. For any object
\mathcal{H}
of

Since

is stable by negative Tate twists, by shifts and by direct sums, we deduce that

is in
M^{\mathrm{eff}}(X_{s},Q,E)
, hence the conclusion.

Proposition 4.2

The following result, which asserts that

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
Q
be a profinite group. Let

be the spectrum of a finite field of characteristic

, endowed with the trivial action of

. Let
\overline{s}
be a separable closure of

, and let
\mathrm{Frob}_{s}
be the geometric Frobenius in

. Let
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n}
be an object of

(cf.

(1)

Let

be an element of
Q
. Then there exists a unique element
\mathrm{Tr}(q|\mathcal{F}_{\overline{s}})
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}\left[p^{-1}\right]
such that for any element

\mathbb{L}

coincides with the image of

(2)

There exists a unique rational function

in

such that for any
\lambda

\mathbb{L}

\det(1-T\mathrm{Frob}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{\overline{s}})
is a quotient of polynomials in

with invertible leading coefficients.

We follow closely the proof of
[
DL76
, 3.3]
. Since these properties are stable by Tate twist, we can assume that
\mathcal{F}
is effective. We can further assume (and we do) that
\mathcal{F}
is of the form
Ra_{!}\mathcal{F}
, for some
Q
-equivariant separated morphism
a:Z\rightarrow s
of finite type and some object
\mathcal{G}
of
D_{c}^{b}(Z,Q,)
. For each
\lambda
in
\mathbb{L}
, we fix an algebraic closure
\overline{E}_{\lambda}
of
E_{\lambda}
. For any element

of
\overline{E}_{\lambda}
, let us set

\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q)=\mathrm{Tr}(q|R\Gamma(Z_{\overline{s}},\mathcal{G}\otimes_{E}\overline{E}_{\lambda})^{\mathrm{Frob}_{s}\sim c}),

where
\sim c
indicates the generalized eigenspace for the eigenvalue

. For each integer

, we have

\sum_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda}^{\times}}\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q)c^{n}=\mathrm{Tr}(q\mathrm{Frob}_{s}^{n}|R\Gamma(Z_{\overline{s}},\mathcal{G}\otimes_{E}\overline{E}_{\lambda})).

If

is the unique extension of degree

of
s
in
\overline{s}
, then the automorphism

of

is the geometric Frobenius automorphism of some

-form of

, and the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula therefore gives

\mathrm{Tr}(q\mathrm{Frob}_{s}^{n}|R\Gamma(Z_{\overline{s}},\mathcal{G}\otimes_{E}\overline{E}_{\lambda}))=\sum_{z\in Z(\overline{s})^{q\mathrm{Frob}_{s}^{n}=1}}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{z}|\mathcal{G}_{\overline{z}}).

In particular, this an element of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Thus, for any
E
-linear field isomorphism
\iota:\overline{E}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\overline{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, we have

\sum_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\times}}\alpha_{c,\lambda^{\prime}}(q)c^{n}=\iota\left(\sum_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda}^{\times}}\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q)c^{n}\right)=\sum_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda}^{\times}}\iota(\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q))\iota(c)^{n},

and consequently
\iota(\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q))=\alpha_{\iota(c),\lambda^{\prime}}(q)
by linear independence of the maps
(n\mapsto c^{n})_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\times}}

\mathrm{Tr}(q|R\Gamma(Z_{\overline{s}},\mathcal{G}\otimes_{E}\overline{E}_{\lambda}))=\sum_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda}^{\times}}\alpha_{c,\lambda}(q),

\lambda

\iota\det(1-T\mathrm{Frob}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\overline{s}})=\iota\prod_{c\in\overline{E}_{\lambda}^{\times}}(1-Tc)^{\alpha_{c,\lambda}(1)}=\det(1-T\mathrm{Frob}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda^{\prime},\overline{s}}).

Thus the rational function
\det(1-T\mathrm{Frob}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\overline{s}})
is

-invariant, hence belongs to

, and is independent of

in
\mathbb{L}
.

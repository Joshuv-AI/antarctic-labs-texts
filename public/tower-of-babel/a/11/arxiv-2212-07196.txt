The Principal Symbol Map for Lagrangian Distributions with Complex Phase
Gisel Mattar Marriaga

Abstract
Fourier integral operators with complex phase function are an important tool in the analysis of partial differential equations. A rigorous study of these operators can be found in [ 5 ] . There, the authors give a rough description of the principal symbol map of a Lagrangian distribution after transverse composition. The present paper provides an alternative construction of the principal symbol map, which allows us to compute the principal symbol after clean composition.

This paper studies Lagrangian distributions with complex phase functions. These are distributions

\displaystyle\int e^{i\phi(x,\theta)}a(x,\theta)\ d\theta,\quad(x,\theta)\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

4

In

Definition 4.1
.

A complex-valued function
\phi(x,\theta)

The number

is called the excess of

It is possible to organize the variables so that we can write
\theta=(\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{e}

\left\{d(\partial\phi/\partial\theta^{\prime}_{j})\right\}
are the ones satisfying the definition. As usual, we denote by

\left\{\left(\tilde{x},\partial_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{x},\tilde{\theta})\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus 0\colon(\tilde{x},\tilde{\theta})\in C_{\tilde{\phi}}\right\},

for some almost analytic extension

of

to a complex extension of

One can easily verify that

is a positive Lagrangian manifold of real dimension

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}(\tilde{x},\tilde{\xi})=\tilde{x}\tilde{\xi}-g(\tilde{\xi}),\quad(\tilde{x},\tilde{\xi})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0)

Denoting by

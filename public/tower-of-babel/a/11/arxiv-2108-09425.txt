Energy equality in the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations allowing vacuum
Yulin Ye, Yanqing Wang and Wei Wei

Abstract
It is well-known that a Leray-Hopf weak solution in L 4 ​ ( 0 , T , L 4 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega)) for the incompressible Navier-Stokes system is persistence of energy due to Lions [ 19 ] . In this paper, it is shown that Lions’s condition for energy balance is also valid for the weak solutions of the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations allowing vacuum under suitable integrability conditions on the density and its derivative. This allows us to establish various sufficient conditions implying energy equality for the compressible flow as well as the non-homogenous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. This is an improvement of corresponding results obtained by Yu in [ 32 , Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal., 225 (2017)] , and our criterion via the gradient of the velocity partially answers a question posed by Liang in [ 18 , Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh Sect. A (2020)] .

1,&\tau+\alpha\leq t\leq t_{0},\\
\frac{t_{0}-t}{\alpha},&t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\alpha,\\
0,&t_{0}+\alpha\leq t.\end{array}\right.

(3.22)

Then substituting this test function into (
3.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\alpha}\int

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+\alpha}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)

(3.23)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}+\alpha}\int\phi_{\tau}\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)=0.

Taking
\alpha\rightarrow 0
and using the fact that
\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)
is continuous with respect to
t
and the Lebesgue point Theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle-\int

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)(\tau)dx+\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)(t_{0})dx

(3.24)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)dxds=0.

Finally, letting
\tau\rightarrow 0
, using the continuity of
\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)
, (
3.17
) and (
3.20
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)(t_{0})dx+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int

\displaystyle\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)dxds

(3.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}v_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\right)dx.

Then we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Next, with the help of Theorem
1.3
,
we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(1)
Choose
p=q=4
in (
1.13
), we obtain Lions type energy
conservation condition.

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho<c<\infty,\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\in L^{4}(L^{4}),

(3.26)

\displaystyle v\in L^{4}(L^{4}),\nabla v\in L^{2}(L^{2})\ and\ v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

(2)
With the help of interpolation, we show (
1.10
) can be reduced to (
1.9
). Indeed, taking advantage of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on bounded domains, Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we know that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{3(4-q)}{2(6-q)}}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{q}{2(6-q)}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\right)^{\frac{3(4-q)}{2(6-q)}}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{q}{2(6-q)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{3(4-q)}{2(6-q)}}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{q}{2(6-q)}}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\right).

From the the result just proved, we obtain energy equality via (
1.9
).
We finish the proof of this case.

(3) Taking
p=4,q=6
in (
1.13
), we immediately get the desired result.

(4) We move to the proof of the last case.
Firstly, we temporarily assume that the following fact is valid,

\int_{\Omega}vdx\in L^{p}(0,T).

(3.28)

It follows from the triangle inequality and Poincaré-Sobolev inequality that, for
q<d
,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(L^{\frac{dq}{d-q}})}\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(L^{\frac{dq}{d-q}})}+\Big\|\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(L^{\frac{dq}{d-q}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+C\Big\|\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

From the result case (2) in just proved, it is enough to
prove the quality (
3.28
) we have assumed.
The Hölder inequality and classical Poincaré inequality ensure that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\rho\left(v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\right)dx\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}\Big\|v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{q}}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q}}.

Using the Hölder’s inequality once again and the upper bound of the density, we find

\int\rho vdx\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.30)

In view of the triangle inequality, (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), we infer that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big|=

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|}{\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}dx}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\right)\rho dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}dx}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\Big[\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\right)-v\Big]\rho dx\Big|+\frac{C}{\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}dx}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\rho vdx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q}}+C\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}}.

which means

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+C\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}.

Thus, it follows from the triangle inequality and Poincaré-Sobolev inequality that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|v-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+\Big\|\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+C\Big\|\int_{\Omega}vdy\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}\right),

which means
v\in L^{p}(D^{1,q})
implies
v\in L^{p}(W^{1,q})
.
which implies

v\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ q<d.

In summary, we have shown

\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{r}=1+\frac{3}{d},\ \frac{3d}{d+3}<r\leq\frac{4d}{d+4},

turns out

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1,3<q\leq 4.

At this stage, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

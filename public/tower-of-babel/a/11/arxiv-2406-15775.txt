On well-posedness for parabolic Cauchy problems of Lions type with rough initial data
Pascal Auscher, Hedong Hou

Abstract
. We establish a complete picture for well-posedness of parabolic Cauchy problems with time-independent, uniformly elliptic, bounded measurable complex coefficients. We exhibit a range of p p for which tempered distributions in homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces H ˙ s , p {\dot{H}}^{s,p} with regularity index s ∈ ( − 1 , 1 ) s\in(-1,1) are initial data. Source terms of Lions’ type lie in weighted tent spaces, and weak solutions are built with their gradients in weighted tent spaces as well. A similar result can be achieved for initial data in homogeneous Besov spaces B ˙ p , p s {\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p} .

1.
Introduction

The goal of this work is to present a complete picture for existence and uniqueness of weak solutions (see Definition
4.1
) to the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div_{x}A(x)\nabla_{x}u=f+\Div_{x}F,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases},

using tent spaces introduced by
[
18
]
and their weighted generalizations (see Section
2.2
for definition). We assume the coefficient matrix
A\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{n};\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}}))
is
uniformly elliptic
, that is, there are
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}>0
so that for a.e.
x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and any
\xi,\eta\in{\mathds{C}}^{n}
,

(1.2)

\Re(\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle)\geq\Lambda_{0}|\xi|^{2},\quad|\langle A(x)\xi,\eta\rangle|\leq\Lambda_{1}|\xi||\eta|.

By the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
, we require that
u(t)
tends to
u_{0}
as
t\to 0
in distributional sense,
i.e.
, in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.

We refer the reader to the introduction of
[
10
]
for motivation to include tools from harmonic analysis such as tent spaces when the initial data are in
L^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
and the coefficients are not regular. There, well-posedness is established for weak solutions
u
to the equation without source terms (even in the non-autonomous case) in a class defined by the non-tangential maximal Kenig–Pipher norm, as well as estimates of
\nabla u
in tent spaces
T^{p}_{0}
(with limited but explicit range of
p
). Uniqueness in the latter class is obtained in
[
37
]
. It is worth pointing out that
L^{p}
is not a trace space, with trace defined in relation to (real) interpolation theory, in contrast to (homogeneous) Besov spaces. So already, the above results do not belong to the realm of maximal regularity using the notion of mild solutions.

Source terms in tent spaces have been considered in nonlinear PDE’s, see
[
27
,
4
,
20
]
, and in deterministic estimates used for stochastic PDE’s, see
[
14
,
32
]
. More recently,
[
11
]
shows existence of weak solutions with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{0}
when
F\in T^{p}_{0}
,
f=0
and
u_{0}=0
. For source terms
f
(and
F=0
), our prior work
[
6
]
obtains well-posedness and maximal regularity of
u
itself in a weighted tent space, the weight being an expression of regularity (see below). In this case, however, the initial data must be zero.

A subsequent forthcoming work of the second author will treat the non-autonomous case.

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, we treat initial data in spaces which are not necessarily trace spaces and carry regularity information by control of the gradient of weak solutions in a
weighted
tent space, where the weight is a power of the time variable. Indeed, we shall show that our approach gives access to initial data in homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces defined by Littlewood–Paley decomposition (see Section
2.1
). The regularity range is between
-1
and 1. Our method also applies for initial data in homogeneous Besov spaces.

The prototypical example illustrating this is the heat equation.

1.1.1.
Heat equation

Our first result gives a flavor for the homogeneous Cauchy problem (By this, we mean having an initial data but null source terms), and we also provide representation of heat solutions by studying their traces.

Theorem 1.1

(Heat equation and weighted tent spaces)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
,
0<p\leq\infty
, and
g\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
.

(i)

(Weighted tent-space estimates)

Suppose
s<1
. If
g\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, then the function
(t,x)\mapsto\nabla e^{t\Delta}g(x)
belongs to
T^{p}_{s/2}
with

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}g\|_{T^{p}_{s/2}}\eqsim\|g\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

(ii)

(Representation of heat solutions)

Let
u
be a distributional solution to the heat equation on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}
. Suppose
s>-1
and
\frac{n}{n+s+1}\leq p\leq\infty
. Then there exists a unique
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
so that
u(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
for all
t>0
. Moreover,

(1)

If
s\geq 1
and
\frac{n}{n+s-1}\leq p\leq\infty
, then
u
is a constant.

(2)

If
-1<s<1
and
\frac{n}{n+s+1}\leq p\leq\infty
, then there exist
g\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
and
c\in{\mathds{C}}
such that
u_{0}=g+c
, so
u(t)=e^{t\Delta}g+c
for all
t>0
.

The first point
(i)
shows a precise correspondence between Sobolev regularity of the initial data and the choice of the power weight. The second point
(ii)
shows the regularity range
s<1
is essentially sharp.

When
s=0
, the first point is reminiscent to the work of Fefferman–Stein
[
21
]
on defining Hardy spaces by so-called
conical square functions
when translated to parabolic setting. But their point was to take extensions not related to any kind of equations to obtain an intrinsic definition of Hardy spaces. Here, we have to restrict ourselves to extensions related to the heat equation. It is also reminiscent to Littlewood–Paley theory using rather vertical square functions. The reader can refer to
[
36
, §4.1]
for more on this topic.

Combining these two points yields in particular well-posedness of the homogeneous Cauchy problem in the following strong form.

Corollary 1.2

(Isomorphism)
.

Let
-1<s<1
and
\frac{n}{n+s+1}\leq p\leq\infty
. The map
g\mapsto(e^{t\Delta}g)_{t>0}
is a bijection from
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
onto the space of distributional solutions
u
to the heat equation with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}
, and

\|g\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}.

1.1.2.
Critical exponents for the parabolic problem

To treat the more general parabolic problems (
1.1
), we introduce the operator
L

L:=-\Div(A\nabla)

on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
with domain

D(L):=\{f\in W^{1,2}({\mathds{R}}^{n}):\Div(A\nabla f)\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})\}.

By the theory of maximal-accretive operators,
-L
generates a bounded analytic semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. Our construction of weak solutions is based on an appropriate extension of the solution map
{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
, which is initially defined from
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
to
L^{2}_{\loc}((0,\infty);W^{1,2}_{\loc}({\mathds{R}}^{n}))
by the semigroup as

(1.3)

{\mathcal{E}}_{L}(u_{0})(t,x):=(e^{-tL}u_{0})(x).

The
L^{p}
-theory of the semigroup is ruled by four critical numbers, which are introduced in
[
12
, Proposition 3.15]
for
1<p<\infty
, and later extended to
p>\frac{n}{n+1}
in
[
3
, §6]
to include Hardy spaces
H^{p}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. These numbers are

•

p_{\pm}(L)\in[\frac{n}{n+1},\infty]
such that
(p_{-}(L),p_{+}(L))
is the largest open set (an interval) of exponents
p
for which the semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly bounded on
L^{p}
when
p>1
and on
H^{p}
when
p\leq 1
;

•

q_{\pm}(L)\in[\frac{n}{n+1},\infty]
such that
(q_{-}(L),q_{+}(L))
is the largest open set (an interval) of exponents
p
for which the family
(t^{1/2}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
is uniformly bounded on
L^{p}
when
p>1
and on
H^{p}
when
p\leq 1
.

It is known that
p_{-}(L)=q_{-}(L)<\frac{2n}{n+2}
,
q_{+}(L)>2
, and
p_{+}(L)\geq\frac{nq_{+}(L)}{n-q_{+}(L)}
(by convention
p_{+}(L)=\infty
if
q_{+}(L)\geq n
). The strict inequalities are best possible. We also have the duality relation
p_{+}(L^{*})=\max\{p_{-}(L),1\}^{\prime}
, where
p^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p\in[1,\infty]
. For the negative Laplacian,
p_{-}(-\Delta)=q_{-}(-\Delta)=\frac{n}{n+1}
and
p_{+}(-\Delta)=q_{+}(-\Delta)=\infty
.

We extend the critical numbers to our
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
-setting. For
-1\leq s\leq 1
, define
p_{\pm}(s,L)
as

(1.4)

\frac{1}{p_{-}(s,L)}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p_{-}(L)}+\frac{s}{n}&\text{ if }0\leq s\leq 1\\
\frac{1+s}{p_{-}(L)}-\frac{s}{q_{+}(L^{\ast})^{\prime}}&\text{ if }-1\leq s\leq 0\end{cases},

and

(1.5)

p_{+}(s,L):=\max\{p_{-}(-s,L^{*}),1\}^{\prime}.

Notice that
p_{\pm}(0,L)=p_{\pm}(L)
,
p_{-}(-1,L)=q_{+}(L^{\ast})^{\prime}\in[1,2)
, and that
p_{+}(1,L)=q_{+}(L)\in(2,\infty]
. In particular,
p_{-}(s,-\Delta)=\frac{n}{n+s+1}
and
p_{+}(s,-\Delta)=\infty
.

We also introduce several other numbers which will parametrize our results. For convenience, we use a parameter
\beta
whose relation to the regularity exponent
s
is given by

\boxed{s=2\beta+1}

with
\beta>-1
and no upper restriction. Define

(1.6)

\beta(L):=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p_{-}(L)}-1\right)\geq-1.

Note that
\beta(L)\geq-1/2
if and only if
p_{-}(L)\geq 1
. For
\beta>-1
, we introduce the numbers
p_{L}(\beta)\in(0,2)
given by

(1.7)

p_{L}(\beta):=\frac{np_{-}(L)}{n+(2\beta+1)p_{-}(L)},

and also
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)\in(0,2)
so that:

(i)

When
p_{-}(L)\geq 1
, it is given by

(1.8)

{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta):=\begin{cases}p_{L}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta\geq-1/2\\
p_{-}(2\beta+1,L)&\text{ if }-1<\beta<-1/2\end{cases}.

(ii)

When
p_{-}(L)<1
, it is given by

(1.9)

{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta):=\begin{cases}p_{L}(\beta)&\text{ if }\beta\geq\beta(L)\\
\frac{(\beta(L)+1)q_{+}(L^{\ast})}{(\beta(L)+1)q_{+}(L^{\ast})+\beta-\beta(L)}&\text{ if }-1<\beta<\beta(L)\end{cases}.

Remark that
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta(L))=1
and
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(-1)=q_{+}(L^{\ast})^{\prime}=p_{-}(-1,L)
. For the negative Laplacian, as
p_{-}(-\Delta)=\frac{n}{n+1}
, we have

{\widetilde{p}}_{-\Delta}(\beta)=p_{-\Delta}(\beta)=\frac{n}{n+2\beta+2},\quad\forall\beta>-1.

Observe that for any

,

{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)\geq p_{L}(\beta)\geq\frac{n}{n+2\beta+2}.

2.
Function spaces

We begin with introducing the spaces for the initial data and next, the spaces for the solutions. Denote by
{\mathscr{S}}
the space of Schwartz functions on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and by
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
the space of tempered distributions. For any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, write
{\widehat{f}}
or
{\mathcal{F}}(f)
for the Fourier transform of
f
.

2.1.
Homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces

Our homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces can be roughly regarded as “realizations” of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
. We follow the formulation in
[
33
, §2.4.3]
, which was originated from an observation of J. Peetre
[
31
, p.56]
, see also
[
17
,
30
]
.

Denote by
C
the annulus
\{\xi\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}:2^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{2}\}
and by
2^{j}C
the annulus
\{\xi\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}:2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+2}\}
for any
j\in{\mathds{Z}}
. Let
\chi
be in
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
so that
\supp(\chi)\subset C
and for any
\xi\neq 0
,

\sum_{j\in{\mathds{Z}}}\chi(2^{-j}\xi)=1.

Let
\Delta_{j}
be the
j
-th
Littlewood–Paley operator
associated with
\chi
, given by

(2.1)

\Delta_{j}f:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi(2^{-j}\cdot){\mathcal{F}}(f)),\quad\forall f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}.

Suppose
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,p}
be the collection of sequences of measurable functions
(f_{j})_{j\in{\mathds{Z}}}
on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
so that

(2.2)

\|(f_{j})\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,p}}:=\left\|\left(\sum_{j}|2^{js}f_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

It is clear that
f_{j}\in L^{p}
for any
j\in{\mathds{Z}}
, whenever
(f_{j})\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,p}
.

Let
{\mathscr{P}}
be the space of polynomials
{\mathds{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
{\mathscr{P}}_{0}:=\{0\}
, and
{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
be the subspace of
{\mathscr{P}}
consisting of polynomials of degree less than
m
for
m\geq 1
. For
p\neq\infty
, the
Triebel–Lizorkin space

{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}
consists of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
for which

\|f\|_{{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}}:=\left\|(\Delta_{j}f)\right\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,p}}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
, the space
{\dot{F}}^{s}_{\infty,2}
consists of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
so that there exists
(f_{j})\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,\infty}
satisfying that
f=\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f_{j}
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
. The norm is given by

\|f\|_{{\dot{F}}^{s}_{\infty,2}}:=\inf\|(f_{j})\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,\infty}},

where the infimum is taken among all
(f_{j})\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{s,\infty}
so that
\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f_{j}=f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
.

Define
\nu(s,p):=\max\{0,[s-\frac{n}{p}]+1\}
. For any
f\in{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}
, the Littlewood–Paley series
\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f
converges in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
. Moreover, it induces an isometric embedding
\iota:{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}\to{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
given by

\iota(f):=\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f.

Definition 2.1

(Homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. The
homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev space

{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
consists of
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
whose class in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,p)}
belongs to
\iota({\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2})
. The (quasi-)semi-norm of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
is given by

\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}:=\|[f]\|_{{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}},

where
[f]
denotes the class of
f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
.

Note that for
s=0
, up to equivalent (quasi-)norms,
{\dot{H}}^{0,p}
agrees with the Hardy space
H^{p}
if
p\leq 1
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
if
1<p<\infty
, and
\bmo
if
p=\infty
. It follows by classical results of Littlewood–Paley theory, see
e.g.
,
[
22
, Theorem 1.3.8]
and
[
35
, §5.2.4]
. Moreover, for any
s\in{\mathds{R}}
,
{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}/{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu(s,\infty)}
is isomorphic to
\bmo^{s}
introduced by Strichartz
[
34
]
.

The reader can refer to
[
3
, §2.5]
and the references there for basic properties of the spaces
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, for instance, duality, complex interpolation, lifting property, and Sobolev embedding. In particular, let us mention a dense subspace of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
. Denote by
{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
the subspace of
{\mathscr{S}}
consisting of
\phi\in{\mathscr{S}}
so that for any multi-index
\alpha
,
\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(0)=0
. It is a dense subspace of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
if
p<\infty
, or weak*-dense if
p=\infty
.

2.2.
Tent spaces and slice spaces

We adapt the original definition of (unweighted) tent spaces in
[
18
]
to our parabolic setting. For any
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
p\in(0,\infty)
, the
(parabolic) tent space

T^{p}_{\beta}
consists of (possibly
{\mathds{C}}^{n}
-valued) measurable functions
F
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/2})}|t^{-\beta}F(t,y)|^{2}\,dtdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
0<p\leq 1
, the space
T^{\infty}_{\beta,([p,1])}
consists of measurable functions
F
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which

\|F\|_{T^{\infty}_{\beta,([p,1])}}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|^{[p,1]}}\left(\int_{0}^{r(B)^{2}}\fint_{B}|t^{-\beta}F(t,y)|^{2}\,dtdy\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
of
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
. We also write
T^{\infty}_{\beta}
for
T^{\infty}_{\beta,(0)}
. Recall that
[p,1]
is defined in Section
1.3
by
\frac{1}{p}-1
.

The reader can refer to
[
6
, §3.1]
for a short summary of basic properties of tent spaces, such as duality and a dense class. Here we also recall two properties:

(i)

(Interpolation)
Let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in{\mathds{R}}
,
0<p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. If
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
and
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
, then the complex interpolation space
[T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}
(in the sense of Kalton–Mitrea method, see
[
25
]
) identifies with
T^{p}_{\beta}
. See
[
9
, Theorem 2.12]
.

(ii)

(Embedding) Let
\beta_{0}>\beta_{1}
and
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
. Suppose

2\beta_{0}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}=2\beta_{1}-\frac{n}{p_{1}}.

Then
T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}
embeds into
T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}
. See
[
9
, Theorem 2.19]
.

We shall also use another family of spaces studied in
[
8
, §3]
, called slice spaces. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\delta>0
. The
(parabolic) slice space

E^{p}_{\delta}
consists of measurable (possibly
{\mathds{C}}^{n}
-valued) functions
f
on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}:=\left\|\left(\fint_{B(\cdot,\delta^{1/2})}|f(y)|^{2}\,dy\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

We also define the space
E^{1,p}_{\delta}
as the collection of
L^{2}_{\loc}
-functions
g
on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
so that
\nabla g
(in the sense of distributions) belongs to
E^{p}_{\delta}
, equipped with the semi-norm

\|g\|_{E^{1,p}_{\delta}}:=\|\nabla g\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}.

Also define
E^{-1,p}_{\delta}:=\{g\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n}):g=\Div(G)\text{ for some }G\in E^{p}_{\delta}\}
with the norm

\|g\|_{E^{-1,p}_{\delta}}:=\inf\{\|G\|_{E^{p}_{\delta}}:G\in E^{p}_{\delta},\penalty\ g=\Div G\}.

The
L^{2}
-inner product on
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
realizes
E^{p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
(resp.
E^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
) as the dual of
E^{p}_{\delta}
(resp.
E^{1,p}_{\delta}
) when
1\leq p<\infty
.

3.
Heat equation

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem of the heat equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0&\quad\text{ in }{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})\\
u(0)=f&\end{cases}.

Recall that any distributional solution to the heat equation on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
is in fact smooth by hypoellipticity, see for instance
[
23
, §4.4]
.

3.1.
Heat extension and homogeneous Hardy–Sobolev spaces

For any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, the function
(t,x)\mapsto(e^{t\Delta}f)(x)
belongs to
C^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})\cap C([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})
. We call it the
heat extension
of
f
, denoted by
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
.

Proposition 3.1

(Heat extension on
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. Then
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}
is a bounded and continuous map from
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
to
C_{0}([0,\infty);{\dot{H}}^{s,p})\cap C^{\infty}((0,\infty);{\dot{H}}^{s,p})
with the estimate

(3.1)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)(t)\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

Here, for
p=\infty
, the space
{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}
is equipped with the weak*-topology against elements of
{\dot{H}}^{-s,1}
.

Proof.

First consider the case
0<p<\infty
. Let
(\Delta_{j})
be the Littlewood–Paley operator defined in (
2.1
). It is well-known that for any
s\in{\mathds{R}}
, there are implicit constants such that for any
f\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
,

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathds{Z}}}|2^{js}\Delta_{j}(e^{t\Delta}f)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathds{Z}}}|2^{js}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}.

By density, this inequality extends to all
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, so we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)(t)\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

Moreover, one can find that
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}
is a bounded map from
{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
to
C_{0}([0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty})\cap\penalty C^{\infty}((0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty})
. Hence, by density, it is bounded from
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
to
C_{0}([0,\infty);{\dot{H}}^{s,p})\cap C^{\infty}((0,\infty);{\dot{H}}^{s,p})
.

For
p=\infty
, we proceed by weak*-duality to obtain boundedness and regularity. This completes the proof.
∎

We now provide an intermediate result describing when the heat extension of a tempered distribution belongs to a weighted tent space in terms of its Hardy–Sobolev regularity. To the best of our knowledge, such a precise result and the next ones below do not appear in the literature.

Theorem 3.2

(Characterization of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
via heat extension)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
.

(i)

Suppose
s<0
. If
f\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}

(
in particular
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime})
, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
belongs to
T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}
with the estimate

(3.2)

\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

(ii)

Suppose
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
belongs to
T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}
.

(1)

If
s\geq 0
and
\frac{n}{n+s}\leq p\leq\infty
, then
f=0
.

(2)

If
s<0
, then
f
belongs to
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
with the estimate

\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}.

Consequently, when
s<0
, a tempered distribution
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
lies in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
lies in
T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}
, with the equivalence of norms as

\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}.

Remark 3.3
.

The restriction on
s
in
(i)
is as in Triebel–Lizorkin theory. For
(ii)
, whenever
s\in{\mathds{R}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\in T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}
implies that there is a representative in the class of
f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
that belongs to
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
. But for
s\geq 0
and
p<\frac{n}{n+s}
, we do not know any better conclusion.

In the sequel, for a function
F(t,x)
, we also write
\|F(t)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}
for
\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}
to emphasize the time variable.

Proof.

Let us start from some general facts on relations of Hardy spaces and tent spaces. Let
\psi
be in
C^{\infty}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
satisfying that

(a)

\psi
is of mean zero,
i.e.
,
\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\psi=0
;

(b)

there exists some
\varepsilon>0
so that for any multi-index
\alpha
,

(3.3)

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi(x)|\lesssim\left(1+|x|\right)^{-n-\varepsilon-|\alpha|}.

Set
\psi_{t}(x):=t^{-n/2}\psi(t^{-1/2}x)
. One can deduce from classical arguments that the map
Q_{\psi}
defined by

Q_{\psi}(f)(t,x):=(\psi_{t}\ast f)(x)

is bounded from
{\dot{H}}^{0,p}
to
T^{p}_{1/2}
, for
0<p\leq\infty
, or equivalently, from
H^{p}
to
T^{p}_{1/2}
when
0<p\leq 1
, from
L^{p}
to
T^{p}_{1/2}
when
1<p<\infty
, and from
\bmo
to
T^{\infty}_{1/2}
.

Indeed, for
p=2
, it is direct from Fourier transform as

\|Q_{\psi}f\|_{T^{2}_{1/2}}^{2}\eqsim\|Q_{\psi}f\|_{L^{2}_{1/2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}^{2}\eqsim\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widehat{\psi}(t^{1/2}\xi)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)|{\widehat{f}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\eqsim\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

For
p=\infty
, it holds by a well-known Carleson measure argument, see for instance,
[
22
, Theorem 3.3.8(c)]
. For
0<p\leq 1
, it follows from atomic decomposition of tent spaces (see
[
18
, Proposition 5]
), using decay and regularity of the kernel
t^{-1/2}\psi(t^{-1/2}(x-y))
given by (
3.3
) and the moment conditions of
H^{p}
-atoms. Details are left to the reader. The rest follows by interpolation.

Moreover, let
\phi\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
. For
0<p<\infty
, we infer from
[
18
, Theorem 6]
(adapted to the parabolic scaling) that for any
F\in T^{p}_{1/2}
, the integral

S_{\phi}(F):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{t}\ast F(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}

converges in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, and hence induces a bounded operator from
T^{p}_{1/2}
to
L^{p}
if
1<p<\infty
and to
H^{p}
if
0<p\leq 1
. For
p=\infty
,
S_{\phi}(F)
is defined in
\bmo
by testing against
h\in H^{1}
using

\langle S_{\phi}(F),h\rangle:=\iint_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}F(t,x)\,\overline{Q_{\widetilde{\phi}}h(t,x)}\,\frac{dtdx}{t},

where
\widetilde{\phi}(x):=\overline{\phi(-x)}
. Since
\widetilde{\phi}
also satisfies the conditions
(a)
and
(b)
, by duality, one can easily get
S_{\phi}
is bounded from
T^{\infty}_{1/2}
to
\bmo
.

Then we proceed to prove
(i)
, assuming
s<0
. For
0<p<\infty
, we use density. Pick
f\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
and define
g:=(-\Delta)^{-s/2}f
, so we have for any
t>0
,

{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)(t)=e^{t\Delta}f=(-\Delta)^{s/2}e^{t\Delta}g=t^{s/2}(-t\Delta)^{-s/2}e^{t\Delta}g=t^{s/2}\psi_{t}\ast g,

where
\psi
denotes the kernel of
(-\Delta)^{-s/2}e^{\Delta}
. One can easily verify that
\psi
satisfies the conditions
(a)
and
(b)
with
\varepsilon=-s>0
. Then we get

\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}=\|Q_{\psi}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{1/2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{{\dot{H}}^{0,p}}=\|(-\Delta)^{-s/2}f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{0,p}}\eqsim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

Since
{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
is dense in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, this inequality extends to all
f\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
.

For
p=\infty
, we use weak*-density. For any
f\in{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}
, pick
(f_{k})
as a sequence in
{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
that converges weakly* to
f
in
{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}
, hence in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, since
{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}
embeds into
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
when
s<0
. Using the above computation, we have

\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f_{k})\|_{T^{\infty}_{(s+1)/2}}\lesssim\|f_{k}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}}.

In particular,
({\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f_{k}))
is bounded in
T^{\infty}_{(s+1)/2}
. Let
F
be a weak*-accumulation point of
({\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f_{k}))
in
T^{\infty}_{(s+1)/2}
. Let
G\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Then

\iint_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}(e^{t\Delta}f_{k})(x){\overline{G}}(t,x)\,{dtdx}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle f_{k},e^{t\Delta}G(t)\rangle\,dt,

where the pairing is in the sense of tempered distributions and Schwartz functions. As
t\mapsto e^{t\Delta}G(t)\in C((0,\infty);{\mathscr{S}})
and the integral is supported on a compact subset of
(0,\infty)
, we obtain at the limit

\iint_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}F(t,x){\overline{G}}(t,x)\,{dtdx}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle f,e^{t\Delta}G(t)\rangle\,dt.

So
F={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
and
\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{\infty}_{(s+1)/2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,\infty}}
. This finishes the proof of
(i)
.

Next, we prove
(ii)
and begin with
(ii)(2)
. Let
s<0
and
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
with
F(t,x):=(t^{-s/2}e^{t\Delta}f)(x)\in T^{p}_{1/2}
. Pick
\phi\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\infty}
with the non-degeneracy condition

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\phi}(r\xi)|r\xi|^{-s}e^{-|r\xi|^{2}}\frac{dr}{r}=1,\quad\forall\xi\neq 0.

We know from the above discussion that
g:=S_{\phi}(F)
lies in
{\dot{H}}^{0,p}
. Define
\psi:=(-\Delta)^{s/2}\phi
. Computing in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
and using (
3.4
), we have

(-\Delta)^{s/2}g=S_{\psi}(e^{t\Delta}f)=f\quad\text{ in }{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}.

Thus, there is a representative
{\widetilde{f}}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
in the class of
f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
which also lies in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
. By
(i)
, we know that
e^{t\Delta}{\widetilde{f}}
lies in
T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}
, thus, so does
e^{t\Delta}({\widetilde{f}}-f)
. As
{\widetilde{f}}-f
is a polynomial,
e^{t\Delta}({\widetilde{f}}-f)(x)=P(t,x)
is also a polynomial, and it is easy to see that
P(t,x)=0
from
\|P\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}<\infty
. Finally, as
e^{t\Delta}({\widetilde{f}}-f)\to{\widetilde{f}}-f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
when
t\to 0
, it follows that
{\widetilde{f}}=f
. This proves
(ii)(2)
.

To establish
(ii)(1)
, we divide the discussion into two cases.

Case 1:
(s,p)\neq(0,\infty)

For
0<a<1
, define

I(a):=\fint_{a}^{2a}\langle e^{t\Delta}f,\phi\rangle dt.

We claim that
I(a)
tends to 0 as
a\to 0
. Meanwhile,
\langle e^{t\Delta}f,\phi\rangle
tends to
\langle f,\phi\rangle
as
t\to 0
, so we get
f=0
. Let us verify the claim. For
N\geq 0
, denote by
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
the semi-norm on
{\mathscr{S}}
defined by

(3.5)

{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi):=\sup_{|\alpha|+|\gamma|\leq N}\sup_{x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\gamma}\phi(x)|.

For
1<p\leq\infty
, pick
N>n/p^{\prime}
so that

\displaystyle\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}\phi\|_{T^{p^{\prime}}_{-(s+1)/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\frac{s}{2}+1}\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\langle x\rangle^{-Np^{\prime}}\Big(\sup_{y\in B(x,(2a)^{1/2})}\langle y\rangle^{N}|\phi(y)|\Big)^{p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\frac{s}{2}+1}{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi),

where
\langle x\rangle
is the Japanese bracket,
i.e.
,
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
. We get

\displaystyle I(a)

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{-1}\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}\phi\|_{T^{p^{\prime}}_{-(s+1)/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\frac{s}{2}}{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi)\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}.

When
s>0
, it follows that
I(a)
tends to 0 if
a\to 0
as
\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}\leq\|e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}
. When
s=0
but
p\neq\infty
, it also holds as
\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}}
tends to 0 if
a\to 0
.

For
\frac{n}{n+s}\leq p\leq 1
,
i.e.
,
s-n[p,1]\geq 0
, note that

\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}\phi\|_{T^{\infty}_{-({(s+1)}/{2}),([p,1])}}\lesssim a{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\phi),

so the claim follows as

I(a)\lesssim a^{-1}\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{{(s+1)}/{2}}}\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}\phi\|_{T^{\infty}_{-({(s+1)}/{2}),([p,1])}}\lesssim_{\phi}\|\mathds{1}_{(a,2a)}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{p}_{(s+1)/2}},

which also tends to 0 when
a\to 0
.

Case 2:
s=0
and
p=\infty

For any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, pick a ball
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
containing
\supp(\phi)
. Note that

\int_{0}^{r(B)^{2}}|\langle e^{t\Delta}f,\phi\rangle|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\|e^{t\Delta}f\|_{T^{\infty}_{1/2}}^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\phi)^{2}|B|^{2}<\infty.

The fact that
\langle e^{t\Delta}f,\phi\rangle
tends to
\langle f,\phi\rangle
if
t\to 0
forces
f=0
for the integral to converge, so
f=0
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Replacing the heat extension by its gradient gives us the following

Corollary 3.4

(Characterization of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
via gradient of heat extension)
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
.

(i)

Suppose
s<1
and
f\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
. Then
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
lies in
T^{p}_{s/2}
with

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{s/2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}.

(ii)

Suppose
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
with
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\in T^{p}_{s/2}
.

(1)

If
s\geq 1
and
\frac{n}{n+s-1}\leq p\leq\infty
, then
f
is a constant.

(2)

If
s<1
, then there exists some constant
c\in{\mathds{C}}
so that
f-c\in{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
with

\|f-c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{s/2}}.

Consequently, when
s<1
, a tempered distribution
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
belongs to
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
if and only if
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)
lies in
T^{p}_{s/2}
, with the equivalence of norms as

\inf_{c\in{\mathds{C}}}\|f-c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{s/2}}.

Proof.

A tempered distribution
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
lies in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
if and only if
\nabla f\in{\dot{H}}^{s-1,p}
, with the equivalence
\inf_{c\in{\mathds{C}}}\|f-c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}\eqsim\|\nabla f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s-1,p}}
. So the corollary follows from applying Theorem
3.2
to
\nabla f
instead of
f
and using the above equivalence.
∎

In fact, working modulo constants, one can equip
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}
with the “norm modulo constants” given by
\inf_{c\in{\mathds{C}}}\|f-c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}
. When
\nu(s,p)\geq 0
, constants are contained in
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, so
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}+{\mathds{C}}={\dot{H}}^{s,p}
, and for any
c\in{\mathds{C}}
,
\|f-c\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}=\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s,p}}
.

3.2.
Representation of heat solutions

We prove here Theorem
1.1
. In Corollary
3.4
, we started from an element of
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
and described in which range of exponents this is equivalent to the gradient of its heat extension being in a weighted tent space. The question is now whether all heat solutions are of this form. We prove this is true in some (different) range of exponents, by establishing a trace result followed by a representation via heat semigroup.

Proposition 3.5

(Representation of heat solutions)
.

Let
s>-1
and
\frac{n}{n+s+1}\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
u
be a distributional solution to the heat equation on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}
. Then there exists a unique
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
so that
u(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
for all
t>0
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that we work in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
, and not just modulo polynomials. This induces restrictions on
s
and
p
in the proof.

Let us start the proof by a lemma on the growth of local
L^{2}
-norms of functions with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
s\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. Let
u
be in
L^{2}_{\loc}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}
. Then for
0<a<b<\infty
and
R>1
,

(3.6)

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}\lesssim_{a,b,p,s}R^{3n+2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((a,b)\times B(0,1))}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Poincaré’s inequality yields

\int_{B(0,R)}\bigg|u-\fint_{B(0,R)}u\bigg|^{2}\lesssim R^{2}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Here and in the sequel, unspecified measures in the integral are unweighted Lebesgue measure. One can also easily verify that

\bigg|\fint_{B(0,R)}u-\fint_{B(0,1)}u\bigg|\lesssim R\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}\bigg|u-\fint_{B(0,R)}u\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}\bigg|\fint_{B(0,R)}u-\fint_{B(0,1)}u\bigg|^{2}+R^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\fint_{B(0,1)}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{2n+2}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}+R^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,1)}|u|^{2}.

Then (
3.6
) follows from the claim that for any
R>1
,

(3.7)

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\lesssim_{a,b,p,s}R^{n}\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}^{2}.

Let us verify the claim. For
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
[
6
, Lemma 4.9]
implies

\left\|\left(\int_{a}^{b}\fint_{B(\cdot,b^{1/2})}|\nabla u(t,y)|^{2}dtdy\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim_{a,b,s}\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}.

Denote by
r
the Hölder conjugate of
p/2
, and (
3.7
) follows from

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(0,R+b^{1/2})}dx\int_{a}^{b}\fint_{B(x,b^{1/2})}|\nabla u(t,y)|^{2}dtdy

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,p,s}R^{\frac{n}{r}}\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}^{2}.

For
0<p\leq 2
, (
3.7
) follows by
[
6
, Lemma 4.8]
as

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\lesssim_{a,b,p,s}\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

The conclusion holds from applying
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let us verify the size condition and the uniform control condition there. Recall that
u
is smooth. Lemma
3.7
implies that for
0<a<b<\infty
, there exists
\gamma\in(0,1/4)
so that for any
R>0
,

\left(\int_{a}^{b}\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{a,b,\gamma}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma R^{2}}{b-a}\right),

which is exactly the size condition. For the uniform control condition, we show that there exists
N>0
so that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
,

(3.8)

\sup_{0<t<1/2}|\langle u(t),\phi\rangle|\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}\right),

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi)
is defined in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. To this end, fix
0<t<1/2<1<t^{\prime}<2
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. Note that

\int_{1}^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|u||\phi|\leq\int_{1}^{2}\int_{|x|<1}|u||\phi|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{2^{k-1}\leq|x|<2^{k}}|u||\phi|.

Denote by
I_{k}
the
k
-th term for
k\geq 0
. For
k=0
, we have

I_{0}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\phi).

For
k\geq 1
, using (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{|x|<2^{k}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{2^{k-1}\leq|x|<2^{k}}|\phi|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(2n+1)}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}\right)\sup_{2^{k-1}\leq|x|<2^{k}}|\phi(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k}{\mathcal{P}}_{N_{1}}(\phi)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}\right)

for some
N_{1}>0
. We hence obtain

\int_{1}^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|u||\phi|\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{N_{1}}(\phi)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}\right).

Next, we claim that there exists
N_{2}>0
so that

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u||\nabla\phi|\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{N_{2}}(\phi)\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}.

Then (
3.8
) follows by using the mean-value inequality in time and the equation for
u
. Indeed, pick
N>\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, and we get

\displaystyle|\langle u(t),\phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{1}^{2}|\langle u(t^{\prime}),\phi\rangle|\,dt^{\prime}+\fint_{1}^{2}|\langle u(t^{\prime}),\phi\rangle-\langle u(t),\phi\rangle|\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{1}^{2}|\langle u(t^{\prime}),\phi\rangle|\,dt^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u||\nabla\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(\phi)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{{s}/{2}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}((1,2)\times B(0,1))}\right)

as desired.

Let us verify (
3.9
). We use duality of tent spaces. Let
s>-1
. For
1<p\leq\infty
, pick
N_{2}>n/p^{\prime}+1
and we get

\displaystyle\|\mathds{1}_{(0,2)}\nabla\phi\|_{T^{p^{\prime}}_{-{s}/{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bigg(\int_{0}^{2}t^{s}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\bigg(\sup_{y\in B(x,2)}|\nabla\phi(y)|\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\langle x\rangle^{-(N_{2}-1)p^{\prime}}\bigg(\sup_{y\in B(x,2)}\langle y\rangle^{N_{2}-1}|\nabla\phi(y)|\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}dx\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{N_{2}}(\phi).

For
\frac{n}{n+s+1}\leq p\leq 1
,
i.e.
,
s+1-n[p,1]\geq 0
, we have

\|\mathds{1}_{(0,2)}\nabla\phi\|_{T^{\infty}_{-{s}/{2},([p,1])}}\lesssim\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq{\mathcal{P}}_{N_{2}}(\phi).

This proves (
3.9
) and hence completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assertion
(i)
has been shown in Corollary
3.4
. For
(ii)
, the existence of

comes from Proposition

. Corollary

and Proposition

and

4.
Basic facts about weak solutions

The goal of the rest of the paper is to study the Cauchy problems

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=\Div F\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases},

where
A
satisfies (
1.2
). In this section, we review the definition of weak solutions, prove
a priori
energy inequalities, and recall elements of the
L^{2}
-theory. Denote by
W^{1,2}
the inhomogeneous Sobolev space with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{1,2}}:=\|f\|_{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{2}
.

Definition 4.1

(Weak solutions)
.

Let
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
,
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
, and
Q:=(a,b)\times\Omega
. Let
f
and
F
be in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(Q)
. A function
u\in L^{2}_{\loc}((a,b);W^{1,2}_{\loc}(\Omega))
is called a
weak solution
to the equation

\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F

with
source term

f+\Div F
,
if for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(Q)
,

(4.2)

-\iint_{Q}u\penalty\ {\partial_{t}\phi}+\iint_{Q}(A\nabla u)\cdot{\nabla\phi}=(f,{\phi})-(F,{\nabla\phi}).

The pairs on the right-hand side are understood as pairings of distributions and test functions on
Q
. We say
u
is a
global weak solution
if (
4.2
) holds for
Q={\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
.

Let
a=0
and
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. By the
initial condition

u(0)=u_{0}
, we further impose that the weak solution
u(t)
converges to
u_{0}
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
as
t\to 0
.

Similarly, there is a corresponding definition of weak solutions for the dual backward equation
-\partial_{t}u-\Div(A^{*}\nabla u)=f+\Div F
.

4.1.
Energy inequalities

Let us recall without proof a form of the classical Caccioppoli’s inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
,
B\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
be a ball, and
f
,
F
be in
L^{2}((a,b)\times 2B)
. Let
u\in L^{2}((a,b);W^{1,2}(2B))
be a weak solution to the equation
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F
in
(a,b)\times 2B
. Then
u
lies in
C([a,b];L^{2}(B))
with

\displaystyle\|u(b)\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r(B)^{2}}+\frac{1}{b-a}\right)\int_{a}^{b}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+r(B)^{2}\int_{a}^{b}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds+\int_{a}^{b}\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds.

Moreover, for any
c\in(a,b)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}(B)}^{2}

\displaystyle\,ds\lesssim\frac{1}{c-a}\left(1+\frac{b-a}{r(B)^{2}}\right)\int_{a}^{b}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{r(B)^{2}(b-a)}{c-a}\int_{a}^{b}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds+\frac{b-a}{c-a}\int_{a}^{b}\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(2B)}^{2}\,ds.

The implicit constants are independent of
a
,
b
,
c
, and
B
.

There is also a corresponding version for weak solutions to the backward equation. In the sequel, we refer to “Caccioppoli’s inequality” in both cases.

For any
(t,x)\in{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
, the set
W(t,x):=(t,2t)\times B(x,t^{1/2})
is called a
(parabolic) Whitney cube
at
(t,x)
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
0<p\leq\infty
. For any global weak solution
u
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=f+\Div F
, the a priori energy inequality holds as

(4.3)

\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}+\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

The same inequality occurs for global weak solutions to the backward equation.

Proof.

When
p<\infty
, using Caccioppoli’s inequality on local Whitney cubes and the change of aperture property of tent-space norms, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}

\displaystyle\eqsim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2\beta-1}dt\fint_{t/2}^{t}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/2})}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2\beta-2}dt\fint_{t/4}^{t}\fint_{B(x,2t^{1/2})}|u|^{2}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2\beta-1}dt\fint_{t/4}^{t}\fint_{B(x,2t^{1/2})}|F|^{2}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2\beta}dt\fint_{t/4}^{t}\fint_{B(x,2t^{1/2})}|f|^{2}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|F\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1/2}}+\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\beta}}.

When
p=\infty
, we take a covering of the Carleson boxes
(0,{r(B)^{2}})\times B
by local Whitney cubes and apply Caccioppoli’s inequality on each, noting that the enlarged local Whitney cubes are contained in
(0,16r(B)^{2})\times 4B
with bounded overlapping. Detailed verification is left to the reader.
∎

4.2.
L^{2}
-theory

In this section, we summarize the
L^{2}
-theory as a starting point. The weak solutions are given by Duhamel’s formula and semigroup theory, see Section
1.1.2
for the definition of operators
L
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
. We also define the
Lions’ operator

{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}:L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})\to L^{2}_{\loc}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
by the
L^{2}
-valued Bochner integrals (where
L^{2}
denotes
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
)

(4.4)

{\mathcal{R}}_{1/2}^{L}(F)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)L}\Div F(s)\,ds,\quad\forall t>0.

Proposition 4.4

(
L^{2}
-theory)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
F\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Then there exists a unique global weak solution
u
to the Cauchy problem (
4.1
) with
\nabla u\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Moreover,

(i)

u
belongs to
C_{0}([0,\infty);L^{2})
with the estimate

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|F\|_{L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})}.

(ii)

(Duhamel’s formula)

u={\mathcal{E}}_{L}(u_{0})+{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}(F)
.

Existence originates from the work of J.-L. Lions
[
28
, Théorème II.3.1]
, and uniqueness in this larger (likely the largest) class is established in
[
10
, Theorem 3.11]
. The reader can refer to
[
11
, Theorem 2.2]
for a detailed survey of different proofs of this theorem.

This also allows us to obtain several equivalent expressions of these operators, using different interpretations of the equation. This will come into play when studying their extensions. Denote by
{\mathds{I}}
the identity matrix in
\mat_{n}({\mathds{C}})
. Define the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{L}:L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})\to L^{2}_{\loc}((0,\infty);L^{2})
by the
L^{2}
-valued Bochner integrals

(4.5)

{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{L}(f)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)L}f(s)\,ds,\quad\forall t>0.

Corollary 4.5

(Explicit formulae)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
F\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
.

(i)

Define
{\widetilde{F}}:=(A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}(F)+F
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
. Then

(4.6)

{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}(F)={\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}({\widetilde{F}})=\Div{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{-\Delta}({\widetilde{F}})\quad\text{ in }{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}).

(ii)

It holds that

(4.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{L}(u_{0})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0})+{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0}))

(4.8)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0})-{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{L}(u_{0})).

Proof.

First consider
(i)
. Proposition
4.4
says that
u:={\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{1/2}(F)
is a global weak solution to the Cauchy problem

(L)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=\Div F\\
u(0)=0\end{cases},

with
\nabla u\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. We hence infer that
{\widetilde{F}}
lies in
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, and then
{\widetilde{u}}:={\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}({\widetilde{F}})
is a global weak solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\widetilde{u}}-\Delta{\widetilde{u}}=\Div{\widetilde{F}}=\Div((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla u)+\Div F\\
{\widetilde{u}}(0)=0\end{cases}

with
\nabla{\widetilde{u}}\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
. Therefore,
w:=u-{\widetilde{u}}
is a global weak solution to the heat equation with null source term and null initial data. Since
\nabla w\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, we deduce from uniqueness in Proposition
4.4
for the heat equation that
w=0
, proving the first equality in (
4.6
). For the last, as
{\widetilde{F}}\in L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+})
, for a.e.
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}({\widetilde{F}})(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\Div{\widetilde{F}}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\Div\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}{\widetilde{F}}(s)\,ds=\Div{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{-\Delta}({\widetilde{F}})(t)

in the sense of distributions in
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and
(i)
follows.

Next, we consider
(ii)
. Proposition
4.4
asserts that
v:={\mathcal{E}}_{L}(u_{0})
is the unique global solution to the homogeneous Cauchy problem

(HC)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\Div(A\nabla v)=0\\
v(0)=u_{0}\end{cases},

with

To prove it, we use (
5.6

\phi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})

to

and we estimate each term separately. For the first term, we use Hölder continuity. For the second, we write it as

satisfies the similar estimate with an extra factor

Let

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}r^{2}}\int_{B}|\chi^{\prime}(s)||\nabla{\widetilde{v}}_{t}(s,y)|^{2}s^{2\beta+2}\,dsdy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}r^{2}}\int_{B}\chi(s)|\nabla{\widetilde{v}}_{t}(s,y)||\nabla\partial_{s}{\widetilde{v}}_{t}(s,y)|s^{2\beta+2}\,dsdy=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

The crucial point is the embedding theorem for weighted tent spaces, recalled in Section

\displaystyle\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(v_{0})\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla v)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(v_{0})\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}+\|\nabla v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}}.

This shows
(a)
and the proof is complete.
∎

8.
Uniqueness and representation

In this section, we prove uniqueness of weak solutions to the homogeneous Cauchy problem and Lions’ equation as asserted in Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
. We also provide the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

8.1.
Uniqueness

The main theorem on uniqueness is as follows.

Theorem 8.1

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\beta>-1
and
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
. Let
u
be a global weak solution to the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0,\\
u(0)=0\end{cases},

with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
. Then
u=0
.

Remark 8.2
.

Let us mention that
[
6
, Theorem 4.3]
proves uniqueness for
\beta>-1/2
and
p_{L}^{\,\flat}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
(where
p_{L}^{\,\flat}(\beta)
is defined in (
1.10
) and equals
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)
when
p_{-}(L)\geq 1
), requiring that
u
lies in
T^{p}_{\beta+1}
(but with no initial data needed). However, recall that Corollary
4.3
asserts the inequality
\|\nabla v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}}\lesssim\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}
holds for any global weak solution
v
to the equation
\partial_{t}v-\Div(A\nabla v)=0
. Therefore, we improve the class of uniqueness in this range of
\beta
with more values of
p
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Let
u
be a global weak solution to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
and null initial data. Thanks to Lemma
6.1
, we may assume

p>\max\{{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta),1\}
in what follows. Let us begin by some important common facts.

First, as
p>{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)\geq\frac{n}{n+2\beta+2}
and
u(0)=0
, Proposition
6.2
yields

(8.1)

u={\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla u).

Hence, Theorem
5.1
for
{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}
applies:
u\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}}
and
u(s)
tends to 0 as
s\to 0
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
and in various finer topology depending on
\beta
and
p
.

Second, Lemma
3.7
gives local
L^{2}
-norm estimates of
u
allowing us to invoke
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
to get an interior representation of
u
as follows: For
0<s<t<\infty
and
h\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
, it holds that

(8.2)

\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}u(t,x){\overline{h}}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}u(s,x)\overline{e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h}(x)dx.

Now, fix
t>0
and
h\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. We wish to let
s\to 0
, yielding
u(t)=0
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
. To take advantage of the different modes of convergence established in Theorem
5.1

(d)
, we split the argument in two cases.

Case 1:
\beta>-1/2

As
p>\max\{{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta),1\}\geq p_{L}^{\,\flat}(\beta)
and
u\in T^{p}_{\beta+1}
, we can use
[
6
, Theorem 4.3]
to conclude
u=0
(the proof there uses (
8.2
) as well).

Case 2:
-1<\beta\leq-1/2

We begin with
p\geq 2
and then
\max\{{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta),1\}<p\leq 2
, which is further split into two sub-cases.

Case 2(a):
2\leq p\leq\infty

Pick
\delta>0
. We use the slice spaces
E^{-1,p}_{\delta}
and
E^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
introduced in Section
2.2
. Recall that the
L^{2}({\mathds{R}}^{n})
-inner product realizes
E^{-1,p}_{\delta}
as the dual of
E^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
. The formula (
8.1
) and Theorem
5.1

(d)
for
{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}
imply

\lim_{s\to 0}u(s)=0\quad\text{ in }E^{-1,p}_{\delta}.

Moreover, we claim that

(8.3)

\lim_{s\to 0}e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h=e^{-tL^{\ast}}h\quad\text{ in }E^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\delta}.

Then taking limit of the right-hand integral in (
8.2
) yields
u(t)=0
.

To prove (
8.3
), pick
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
R>\delta>0
so that
\supp(h)\subset B:=B(0,R)
and for any
|x|>R
,
D(x):=\dist(B(x,\delta),\supp(h))\geq\lambda|x|
. If
|x|\leq R
, we have

\|\mathds{1}_{B(x,\delta)}\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}}h\|_{2}\leq\|\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}}h\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-1/2}\|h\|_{2}.

If
|x|>R
, the exponential
L^{2}-L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates of
(t^{1/2}\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}})
yield there exists
\gamma>0
so that

\|\mathds{1}_{B(x,\delta)}\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}}h\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\gamma D(x)^{2}}{t}}\|h\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\gamma\lambda^{2}|x|^{2}}{t}}\|h\|_{2}.

We hence get

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t/2}\|\mathds{1}_{B(x,\delta)}\nabla e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathds{1}_{B}(x)+\mathds{1}_{B^{c}}(x)e^{-\frac{\gamma\lambda^{2}|x|^{2}}{t}})\|h\|_{2}.

Note that the function on the right-hand side lies in
L^{p^{\prime}}
, so
(\nabla e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h)_{0\leq s\leq t/2}
form a uniformly bounded set in
E^{p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
. Moreover, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and continuity of
(\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}})
on
L^{2}
imply that
\nabla e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h
converges to
\nabla e^{-tL^{\ast}}h
in
E^{p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
as
s\to 0
. Hence equivalently,
e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h
converges to
e^{-tL^{\ast}}h
in
E^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\delta}
as
s\to 0
. This proves (
8.3
).

Case 2(b):
p_{-}(2\beta+1,L)<p<2

Recall that we also assume
p>1
, and we know that
{\widetilde{p}}_{-\Delta}(\beta)\leq p_{-}(2\beta+1,L)
, so in particular, we have

\max\{{\widetilde{p}}_{-\Delta}(\beta),1\}\leq\max\{p_{-}(2\beta+1,L),1\}<p<2.

Using the formula (
8.1
) and Theorem
5.1

(d)
for
{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}
, we get

\lim_{s\to 0}u(s)=0\quad\text{ in }{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}.

On the other hand, as
h\in{\dot{H}}^{-(2\beta+1),p^{\prime}}\cap L^{2}
and

2<p^{\prime}<(\max\{p_{-}(2\beta+1,L),1\})^{\prime}=p_{+}(-(2\beta+1),L^{\ast}),

we infer from Theorem
7.1

(d)
that

\lim_{s\to 0}e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h=\lim_{s\to 0}{\mathcal{E}}_{L^{\ast}}(h)(t-s)=e^{-tL^{\ast}}h\quad\text{ in }{\dot{H}}^{-(2\beta+1),p^{\prime}}.

Realizing the right-hand integral of (
8.2
) in the sense of duality for
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
and
{\dot{H}}^{-(2\beta+1),p^{\prime}}
, we obtain that it tends to 0 as
s\to 0
.

Case 2(c):
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)<p\leq p_{-}(2\beta+1,L)

This case only occurs when
p_{-}(L)<1
. Theorem
5.1

(d)
still gives us a limit
u(s)\to 0
in
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
but now we do not know whether
e^{-(t-s)L^{\ast}}h
tends to
e^{-tL^{\ast}}h
in the dual space. However, the extra embedding in Proposition
7.4

(ii)
allows us to enhance the convergence of
u(s)
.

Lemma 8.3

(Null limit of
u(s)
in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
)
.

In the range determined by this case, for all
t>0
,
u(t)
lies in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
with a uniform bound. Moreover,

\lim_{t\to 0}u(t)=0\quad\text{ in }{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}.

The proof is presented right after. Admitting this lemma, let us show
u=0
. Since
u(s)\in{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
for all
s>0
, we have
u(s)\in L^{2}
and we get from (
8.2
) that
u(t)=e^{-(t-s)L}u(s)
for
t\geq s
. By Lemma
7.5
, we get

\sup_{t\geq s}\|u(t)\|_{{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}}\lesssim\|u(s)\|_{{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}},

with a uniform bound in
s
. As the right-hand side tends to
0
as
s\to 0
, we conclude that
\sup_{t>0}\|u(t)\|_{{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}}=0
, so
u=0
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
8.3
.

The proof is divided into 4 steps.

Step 1: Regularity of
u

We prove
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{p}\cap L^{2})
. Since
1<p<2
, as proved in
[
6
, (4.19)]
, for a.e.
s>0
, we have
u(s)\in L^{p}
with

\|u(s)\|_{p}\lesssim s^{\beta+1/2}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

For such an
s
,
L^{p}
-boundedeness of
(e^{-tL})
yields
e^{-(t-s)L}u(s)\in L^{p}
for all
t\geq s
, and it follows from (
8.2
) that it equals to
u(t)
. Thus we obtain
u(t)=e^{-(t-s)L}u(s)
for any
t\geq s>0
. Applying analyticity of
(e^{-tL})
on
L^{p}
, we get
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{p})
. As
e^{-tL}
also maps
L^{p}
to
L^{2}
for any
t>0
, we have
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{2})
as desired.

Step 2: A key estimate

For any
k\geq 1
, we show that

(8.4)

\|t^{k}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)(t)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}\lesssim_{k}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

We prove it for
k=1
, and iteration concludes the argument. Observe that by Step 1, we have that for any
t>0
and
\tau>0
,

(8.5)

(\partial_{t}u)(t+\tau)=\partial_{\tau}(u(t+\tau))=\partial_{\tau}(e^{-\tau L}u(t))=-Le^{-\tau L}u(t).

In particular, pick
\tau=t
, we get
(\partial_{t}u)(2t)=-Le^{-tL}u(t)
, and hence,

\displaystyle\|t\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\eqsim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,(2s)^{1/2})}|s^{-(\beta+1)}s(\partial_{t}u)(2s,y)|^{2}\,dsdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,(2s)^{1/2})}|s^{-(\beta+1)}(sLe^{-sL}u(s))(y)|^{2}\,dsdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}.

Write
B:=B(x,(2s)^{1/2})
,
C_{0}:=2B
, and
C_{j}:=2^{j+1}B\setminus 2^{j}B
for
j\geq 1
. The
L^{2}-L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates of
(sLe^{-sL})
yield that

\|\mathds{1}_{B}sLe^{-sL}u(s)\|_{2}\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\mathds{1}_{B}sLe^{-sL}\mathds{1}_{C_{j}}u(s)\|_{2}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{-c2^{2j}}\|\mathds{1}_{C_{j}}u(s)\|_{2},

where
c>0
is independent of
s
. Applying this on the above computation, we get

\displaystyle\|t\partial_{t}u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{-c2^{2j}}\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{C_{j}}s^{-\frac{n}{2}}|s^{-(\beta+1)}u(s,y)|^{2}\,dsdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j\frac{n}{2}}e^{-c2^{2j}}\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,2^{j+1}(2s)^{1/2})}|s^{-(\beta+1)}u(s,y)|^{2}\,dsdy\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{p})}e^{-c2^{2j}}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

The factor
2^{j\frac{n}{p}}
in the third inequality comes from change of angle for tent space norms, see
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Step 3: Boundedness of
u(t)
in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
.

Now we prove that for any
t>0
,
u(t)
belongs to
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
with

(8.6)

\sup_{t>0}\|u(t)\|_{{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}}\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

By Step 1,
u(t)
lies in
L^{2}
for any
t>0
, so we just need to show the norm estimate. To this end, pick an integer
k\geq 1
with
(k-\beta-1)p-\frac{n}{2}>0
. From
[
3
, §8.2]
,
\psi(z):=z^{k}e^{-z}
is a valid admissible function for
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
. So we use (
8.4
), (
8.5
) in Step 2, and the identity
\partial_{\tau}^{k}\big(u(t+\tau)\big)=(\partial_{t}^{k}u)(t+\tau)
to deduce that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}}

\displaystyle\quad\eqsim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,\tau^{1/2})}|\tau^{-(\beta+1)}\left((\tau L)^{k}e^{-\tau L}u(t)\right)(y)|^{2}\,dyd\tau\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad\eqsim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{B(x,\tau^{1/2})}|\tau^{-(\beta+1)}\tau^{k}\partial_{\tau}^{k}\big(u(t+\tau,y)\big)|^{2}\,dyd\tau\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad\eqsim\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,(\sigma-t)^{1/2})}(\sigma-t)^{(k-\beta-1)p-\frac{n}{2}}|(\partial_{t}^{k}u)(\sigma,y)|^{2}\,dyd\sigma\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|\sigma^{k}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)(\sigma)\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}}.

The first inequality with implicit constant independent of
t
uses
(k-\beta-1)p-\frac{n}{2}>0
. This proves (
8.6
).

Step 4: Limit at
t=0
.

We finish by showing that
u(t)
tends to 0 in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
as
t\to 0
. Suppose
(k-\beta-1)p-\frac{n}{2}>0
. Remark that examination of the argument in Step 2 allows time truncation. More precisely, if
0<t\leq\delta
, then one finds

\|\mathds{1}_{\{\tau<2\delta\}}\,\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t+\tau))\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1-k}}\lesssim\|\mathds{1}_{\{\tau<\delta\}}\,u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}},

where the implicit constant does not depend on
t,\delta
. Using Newton–Leibniz formula for
L^{2}
-valued functions, we get

\tau^{k}\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t+\tau))-\tau^{k}\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t^{\prime}+\tau))=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\tau^{k+1}\partial_{\tau}^{k+1}(u(h+\tau))\,dh.

Also, following the argument of Step 2 with the help of Minkowski’s integral inequality, we find for
t,t^{\prime}\in(0,\delta]
,

\|\mathds{1}_{\{\tau<2\delta\}}\,(\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t+\tau))-\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t+\tau)))\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1-k}}\lesssim\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{\delta}\|u\|_{T^{p}_{\beta+1}},

again with implicit constant independent of
t,t^{\prime},\delta
. This implies that
((\tau,x)\mapsto\partial_{\tau}^{k}(u(t+\tau,x)))_{t>0}
is Cauchy in
T^{p}_{\beta+1-k}
when
t\to 0
. We deduce as in Step 3 that
(u(t))_{t>0}
is Cauchy in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
when
t\to 0
. As mentioned in the first paragraph of Case 2(c),
u(t)
tends to 0 in
{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
. The conditions on
\beta,p
allow us to apply Proposition
7.4

(ii)
, so we conclude that the limit of
u(t)
exists and must be zero in
{\mathds{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}_{L}
. This completes the proof.
∎

8.2.
Representation

Let us demonstrate Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
u
be a global weak solution to
\partial_{t}u-\Div(A\nabla u)=0
with
\nabla u\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
. As
p>{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)\geq\frac{n}{n+2\beta+2}
, Proposition
6.2
asserts that there exists
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
so that

u={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0})+{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla u).

In case
(i)
, as
\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{n}{n+2\beta}\leq p\leq\infty
, Proposition
6.2

(i)
says
u_{0}
is a constant. Hence,
w:=u-{\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0})
is a global weak solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w-\Div(A\nabla w)=0,\\
w(0)=0\end{cases}

with
\nabla w\in T^{p}_{\beta+{1}/{2}}
. As
{\widetilde{p}}_{L}(\beta)<p\leq\infty
, we invoke Theorem
8.1
to get
w=0
, so
u={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(u_{0})
is a constant as desired.

In case
(ii)
, Proposition
6.2

(ii)
shows that there exist
g\in{\dot{H}}^{2\beta+1,p}
and
c\in{\mathds{C}}
so that
u_{0}=g+c
. Then we apply Theorem
7.1
to
g
to get

u={\mathcal{E}}_{-\Delta}(g)+{\mathcal{R}}^{-\Delta}_{1/2}((A-{\mathds{I}})\nabla u)+c={\mathcal{E}}_{L}(g)+c.

This completes the proof.
∎

9.
Results for homogeneous Besov spaces

One can also study the case where the initial data are taken in homogeneous Besov spaces
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}
. The definition of
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}
can be analogously adapted from Definition
2.1
, as “realization” of homogeneous Besov spaces defined on
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}/{\mathscr{P}}
. In fact, one can also take the definition of
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}
as in
[
15
, Definition 2.15]
, which does not contain polynomials for any
s
and
p
. See
[
15
, Remark 2.26]
for more detailed discussion.

The counterparts of tent spaces are
Z
-spaces, introduced by
[
16
]
(with a different notation). For any
p\in(0,\infty)
and
\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
, the
(parabolic)
Z
-space

Z^{p}_{\beta}
consists of measurable functions
F
on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
for which the (quasi-)norm

\|F\|_{Z^{p}_{\beta}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/2})}|s^{-\beta}F(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\right)^{p/2}\frac{dt}{t}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
, we set

\|F\|_{Z^{\infty}_{\beta}}:=\sup_{t>0,x\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}\fint_{B(x,t^{1/2})}|s^{-\beta}F(s,y)|^{2}dsdy\right)^{1/2}.

The relation between
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}
(resp.
Z^{p}_{\beta}
) and
{\dot{H}}^{s,p}
(resp.
T^{p}_{\beta}
) is given by real interpolation, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.30]
and
[
3
, §2.6]
. Let
0<p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
s_{0},s_{1},\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in{\mathds{R}}
with
s_{0}\neq s_{1}
and
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}
. Define
s,\beta\in{\mathds{R}}
and
p\in(0,\infty]
so that
s:=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
,
\beta:=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
, and
\frac{1}{p}:=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
. Then we have

(9.1)

({\dot{H}}^{s_{0},p_{0}},{\dot{H}}^{s_{1},p_{1}})_{\theta,p}={\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p},\quad(T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},T^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}=Z^{p}_{\beta}.

Moreover, it has been shown in
[
2
, Theorem 2.34]
that

(9.2)

T^{p_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow Z^{p_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\hookrightarrow T^{p_{2}}_{\beta_{2}},

if
0<p_{0}<p_{1}<p_{2}\leq\infty
and
2\beta_{0}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}=2\beta_{1}-\frac{n}{p_{1}}=2\beta_{2}-\frac{n}{p_{2}}
.

Let us first consider a special case for
\beta=0
(or
s=1
) and
p=\infty
. Recall that we say a distribution
g
belongs to the homogeneous Sobolev space
{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
if
\nabla g
is bounded, and
\|g\|_{{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}}:=\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}
. Rademacher’s theorem asserts
{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
coincides with the set of Lipschitz continuous functions (up to almost everywhere equality).

Proposition 9.1
.

(i)

Let
g\in{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
be a Lipschitz function. Then the function
(t,x)\mapsto\nabla e^{t\Delta}g(x)
belongs to
Z^{\infty}_{1/2}
with

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}g\|_{Z^{\infty}_{1/2}}\eqsim\|g\|_{{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}}.

(ii)

Let
u
be a distributional solution to the heat equation on
{\mathds{R}}^{1+n}_{+}
with
\nabla u\in Z^{\infty}_{1/2}
. Then there exists
u_{0}\in{\dot{W}}^{1,\infty}
such that
u(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
for all
t>0
.

Proof.

Observe that the space
Z^{\infty}_{1/2}
coincides with the parabolic version of the Kenig–Pipher space
X^{\infty}
introduced by
[
26
]
. Then we invoke
[
10
, Theorem 5.4]
to get
(i)
as

in

Asymptotic behaviour of integer programming and the v -function of a graded filtration
Antonino Ficarra, Emanuele Sgroi

Abstract
. The v -function of a graded filtration ℐ = { I [ k ] } k ≥ 0 \mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0} is introduced. Under the assumption that ℐ \mathcal{I} is Noetherian, we prove that the v -function v ​ ( I [ k ] ) \text{v}(I_{[k]}) is an eventually quasi-linear function. This result applies to several situations, including ordinary powers, and integral closures of ordinary powers, among others. As another application, we investigate the asymptotic behaviour of certain integer programming problems. Finally, we present the Macaulay2 package VNumber .

1.
The
\operatorname{v}
-function of a graded filtration

Let
R
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded domain, and let
{\mathfrak{m}}=\bigoplus_{k>0}R_{k}
. Suppose that
R/{\mathfrak{m}}\cong R_{0}
is an infinite field. A
graded filtration
of

is a family
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}
of homogeneous ideals of
R
satisfying:

(i)

I_{[0]}=R
,

(ii)

I_{[k+1]}\subseteq I_{[k]}
for all
k\geq 0
,

(iii)

and
I_{[k]}I_{[\ell]}\subseteq I_{[k+\ell]}
for all

We use the notation

to not confuse the ideal
I_{[k]}
with the

of a homogeneous ideal

We say that
\mathcal{I}

\mathcal{R(I)}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I_{[k]}

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a Noetherian graded filtration of

. Then, there exists an integer

such that

\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[ck+j]})=\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[c(k+1)+j]})\ \ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0,

for all
j=0,\dots,c-1
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R(I)}
is a graded Noetherian ring, there exists
c>0
such that the Veronese subalgebra

\mathcal{R(I)}^{(c)}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I_{[ck]}

is standard graded (see
[
30
]
or
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
). For each integer

\mathcal{R(I)}_{(j)}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I_{[ck+j]}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R(I)}^{(c)}
-module. Hence
[

(which is the module version of
[

\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[ck+j]})=\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[c(k+1)+j]}),

for all
k\gg 0
and all

.

∎

We denote by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
the set of those primes

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[k]})
for infinitely many

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[ck+j]})

.

Remark 1.2

If the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R(I)}

A numerical function

(mod

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}

\operatorname{v}(I_{[k]})
is a quasi-linear function in

In order to prove the theorem, we need some auxiliary lemmata. We begin with the following result of Conca
[
10

. Let
M

Lemma 1.4
.

Let

be a graded ideal, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \alpha\Big(\frac{(I:\mathfrak{p})}{I:(\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}^{\infty})}\Big).

Here

Lemma 1.5

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}

I_{[c(k+1)+j]}:I_{[c]}\ =\ I_{[ck+j]}

This result is a consequence of the following more general statement.

Proposition 1.6

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be either a Noetherian local ring or a finitely generated graded

I^{k+1}M:_{M}I\ =\ I^{k}M

Proof.

The result is probably well-known to the experts, but since we could not find a precise reference in the literature, we provide a short proof. We prove it in the case that

2.
When
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
?

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a Noetherian graded filtration of
R
. Given
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, when do we have that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
? The next theorem answers this question. For the proof of this result, we recall some basic facts.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be a Noetherian local ring,
I\subset R
be an ideal, and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, all minimal system of generators of
I
have the same lenght, namely
\mu(I)=\dim_{K}(I/\mathfrak{m}I)
. Let
\mu(I)=m
,
{\bf f}:f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
be a minimal system of generators of
I
, and let
H_{i}({\bf f};M)
be the
i
th Koszul homology of
{\bf f}
with respect to
M
. Then
[
9
, Proposition 1.6.21]
implies that if
{\bf g}:g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
is another minimal system of generators of
I
, we have
H_{i}({\bf f};M)\cong H_{i}({\bf g};M)
for all
i
. In other words, the Koszul homology modules do not depend upon the choice of a minimal system of generators of
I
. Therefore, we write
H_{i}(I;M)
to denote
H_{i}({\bf f};M)
, where
{\bf f}
can be any minimal system of generators of
I
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the assumptions and notation of Theorem
1.3
, for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
.

(b)

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[k]})
for infinitely many
k
.

(c)

The Krull dimension
\dim H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
is positive.

Proof.

That (a) and (b) are equivalent is by definition of the set
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
.

Before proving that (b) and (c) are equivalent, we notice the following fact. By localizing at
\mathfrak{p}
, we see that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[k]})
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Since
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is again a Noetherian ring, then
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}})
if and only if there exists
f\in R_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:f)=\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This implies that
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})/(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
. Conversely, if
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})/(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
and
g+(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a non-zero element belonging to this module, then
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:g)
is a proper ideal that contains
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the unique maximal ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we see that
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:g)=\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and so
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Summarizing,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[k]})
if and only if
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})/(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
.

Now we prove that (b) and (c) are equivalent. By the previous fact,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{[k]})
for infinitely many
k
if and only if
((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})/(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
for infinitely many
k
.

Notice that
\mathcal{R(I)}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Noetherian graded ring. Indeed, localization preserves the Noetherian property. Whereas,
\mathcal{R(I)}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is graded, because applying localization to the property (iii) of
\mathcal{I}
yields
(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}(I_{[\ell]})_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq(I_{[k+\ell]})_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
k,\ell\geq 0
. Hence, the Koszul homology
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a graded
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module whose
k
th graded component is
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}})=((I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})/(I_{[k]})_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus (b) holds if and only infinitely many graded components of
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}};\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
are non-zero. But this is clearly equivalent to condition (c).

∎

In the case of the ordinary powers of monomial ideals of a standard graded polynomial ring with coefficients over a field, this result was firstly noted by Bayati, Herzog and Rinaldo
[
2
]
. Furthermore, in such a situation one can replace ordinary localization with monomial localization. See
[
2
, Section 2]
for the details.

3.
Integer Programming and the
\operatorname{v}
-number

In this section, we show how the computation of the
\operatorname{v}
-function of certain graded filtrations of monomial ideals is related to integer programming problems.

Let
k
be an integer and let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be integral vectors. We set
k{\bf a}=(ka_{1},\dots,ka_{n})
and
{\bf a+b}=(a_{1}+b_{1},\dots,a_{n}+b_{n})
. The modulus of
{\bf a}
is defined as
|{\bf a}|=a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}
. We denote by
{\bf a}[i]=a_{i}
the
i
th component of
{\bf a}
. Let
{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}
be the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. That is,
{\bf e}_{i}[j]=1
for
i=j
and
{\bf e}_{i}[j]=0
if
i\neq j
. We denote by
{\bf 0}
the vector
(0,0,\dots,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Let
{\bf A}=\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{m}\}
and
{\bf B}=\{{\bf b}_{1},\dots,{\bf b}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}\}
be two finite collections of integral vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Let
k\geq 1
. We define
\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A})
to be the following set of lattice points of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
:

\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A})=\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{m})=\big\{\!\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}{\bf a}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}{\bf e}_{i}\ :\ k_{i},h_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}\geq k\big\}.

For any
k\geq 1
, we consider the following integer program:

P_{{\bf A,B},k}\ :\ \ \begin{cases}\textup{minimize}&{\bf c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\\
\textup{subject to}&{\bf c}+{\bf d}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A})\ \textup{if and only if}\ {\bf d}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}({\bf B}).\end{cases}

We say that a solution
{\bf c}
of
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
, if it exists, is an
optimal solution
if its module
|{\bf c}|
is minimum among all solutions of the problem.

Let
K
be a field and
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. For a non-empty subset
A
of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, let
I({\bf A})\subset S
be the monomial ideal generated by
{\bf x^{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
,
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\bf A}
. Then
I({\bf A})^{k}=I(\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A}))
for all
k\geq 1
. Notice that
I({\bf A})\neq S
if and only if
{\bf 0}\notin{\bf A}
.

By Brodmann
[
6
]
, if
I\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal, the sets
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
stabilize, that is:
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\gg 0
. We denote by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
the common sets
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for
k\gg 0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\bf A},{\bf B}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
be non-empty finite subsets such that
{\bf 0}\notin{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}\subseteq\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}\}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The integer program
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
has a solution for all
k\gg 0
.

(b)

I({\bf B})=(x_{i}:{\bf e}_{i}\in B)\in\operatorname{Ass}(I({\bf A})^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

(c)

The Krull dimension of the module
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
is positive, where
\mathcal{I}=\{I({\bf A})^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{p}=I({\bf B})
.

Furthermore, if any of the equivalent conditions hold, then for all
k\gg 0
the modulus of the optimal solution of
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
is a linear function of the form
ak+b
, where
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are suitable integers. In particular,
a\in\{|{\bf a}|\ :\ {\bf a}\in{\bf A}\}
.

Proof.

Fix a field
K
and an integer
k\geq 1
. Then, there exists a solution
{\bf c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
of the integer program
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
if and only if we have
{\bf x^{c}}{\bf x^{d}}\in I(\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A}))=I({\bf A})^{k}
only when
{\bf x^{d}}\in I({\bf B})
. That is, there exists a solution
{\bf c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
of the integer program
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
if and only if
(I({\bf A})^{k}:{\bf x^{c}})=I({\bf B})
. Thus, condition (a) is equivalent to condition (b). Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I({\bf A})^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. By Theorem
2.1
, (b) is equivalent to the fact that
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
is non-zero for infinitely many
k
, where
\mathfrak{p}=I({\bf B})
. This is equivalent to condition (c).

Finally, suppose that (a) or (b) or (c) holds. For
k\gg 0
the modulus
|{\bf c}|
of the optimal solution
{\bf c}
of
P_{{\bf A,B},k}
is the
\operatorname{v}_{I({\bf B})}
-number
\operatorname{v}_{I({\bf B})}(I({\bf A})^{k})
. By Theorem
1.3
, (see also
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
)
\operatorname{v}_{I({\bf B}))}(I({\bf A})^{k})=ak+b
for all
k\gg 0
. By
[
10
, Remark 1.3]
, (see also
[
15
, Proposition 3.2]
) we have
a\in\{\deg({\bf x^{a}}):{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I({\bf A}))\}\subseteq\{|{\bf a}|\ :\ {\bf a}\in{\bf A}\}
, as desired.

∎

As before, let
{\bf A}=\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{m}\}
and
{\bf B}=\{{\bf b}_{1},\dots,{\bf b}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}\}
be two finite collections of integral vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Let
k\geq 1
. We define
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}({\bf A})
to be the set of lattice points of the convex hull in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the set
\mathcal{P}({\bf A})
. That is, we set
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}({\bf A})\ =\ \textup{conv}(\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A}))\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.
By
[
22
, Corollary 1.4.3]
,
I(\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}({\bf A}))
is equal to the integral closure of
\overline{I(\mathcal{P}_{k}({\bf A}))}
. Hence
I(\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}({\bf A}))=\overline{I({\bf A})^{k}}
for all
k\geq 1
.

For any
{\bf A}=\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{m}\}
,
{\bf B}=\{{\bf b}_{1},\dots,{\bf b}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}\}
and
k\geq 1
, we consider the following integer program:

\overline{P}_{{\bf A,B},k}\ :\ \ \begin{cases}\textup{minimize}&{\bf c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\\
\textup{subject to}&{\bf c}+{\bf d}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}({\bf A})\ \textup{if and only if}\ {\bf d}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}({\bf B}).\end{cases}

It is well-known that
\mathcal{I}=\{\overline{I^{k}}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a Noetherian graded filtration, if
I\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal. By Brodmann
[
6
]
, or
[
29
]
, the sets
\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I^{k}})
stabilize: that is

\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I^{k}})=\operatorname{Ass}(\overline{I^{k+1}})

for all
k\gg 0
. We set

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(\{\overline{I^{k}}\}_{k\geq 0})=\overline{\textup{Ass}}^{\infty}(I).

As in Theorem
3.1
one can show that

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\bf A},{\bf B}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
be non-empty finite subsets such that
{\bf 0}\notin{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}\subseteq\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}\}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The integer program
\overline{P}_{{\bf A,B},k}
has a solution for all
k\gg 0
.

(b)

I({\bf B})=(x_{i}:{\bf e}_{i}\in B)\in\overline{\operatorname{Ass}}^{\infty}(I({\bf A}))
.

(c)

The Krull dimension of the module
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})_{\mathfrak{p}})
is positive, where
\mathcal{I}=\{\overline{I({\bf A})^{k}}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{p}=I({\bf B})
.

Furthermore, if any of the equivalent conditions hold, then for all
k\gg 0
the modulus of the optimal solution of
\overline{P}_{{\bf A,B},k}
is a quasi-linear function.

5.
Open questions

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a Noetherian graded filtration of
R
.

Question 5.1
.

Is it true that
\operatorname{v}(I_{[k]})<\operatorname{reg}(I_{[k]})
for all
k\gg 0
?

Problem 5.2
.

Find an example of non Noetherian graded filtration
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{[k]}\}_{k\geq 0}
of
R
whose
\operatorname{v}
-function
\operatorname{v}(I_{[k]})
is not an eventually quasi-linear function in
k
. Does the limit
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{v}(I_{[k]})/k
exists? If so, what it is equal to?

This problem was recently addressed in
[
33
]
where an example of a non Noetherian graded filtration
\mathcal{I}
whose
\operatorname{v}
-function
\operatorname{v}(I_{[k]})
is not an eventually quasi-linear function is provided.

Suppose now that
R
is the standard graded polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
over an infinite field
K
. Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal, and let
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be the
I
-adic filtration. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. While we know that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
are eventually linear functions
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, their initial behaviour is quite mysterious. In particular, we do not know if they can have any given number of strict local maxima. Due to experimental evidence, we ask the following

Question 5.3
.

a

(a)

Is it true that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})<\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k+1})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
and all
k\geq 1
?

(b)

Is it true that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})<\operatorname{v}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
?

In
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
we proved that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{v}(I^{k})/k=\alpha(I)
. Thus
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for all
k\gg 0
and a certain integer
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
, and each
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
is an eventually linear function in
k
, there exists at least one prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})/k=\alpha(I)
. In view of this observation and some experimental evidence, we expect that

Conjecture 5.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal. For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Max}^{\infty}(I)
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})}{k}\ =\ \alpha(I).

Assume that
I\subset S
is a proper monomial ideal. By
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
for all
k\gg 0
,
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=ak+b
where
a=\alpha(I)\geq 1
and
b\geq-1
. By
[
12
, Theorem 5.5]
,
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})=ck+d
where
c\geq 1
and
d\geq 0
. In view of Question
5.1
we ask the following

Question 5.5
.

Let
a,b,c,d
integers such that
a\geq 1
,
b\geq-1
,
c\geq 1
,
d\geq 0
and
ak+b\leq ck+d
for all
k\gg 0
. Can we find a monomial ideal
I
in some polynomial ring
S
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=ak+d
and
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})=ck+d
for all
k\gg 0
.

In
[
5
]
, the authors introduced the
\operatorname{v}
-stability index
of a graded ideal
I\subset S
as

\textup{vstab}(I)\ =\ \min\{t\ :\ \operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b,\ \textit{for all}\ k\geq t,\ \textit{and some}\ b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. We define the
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-stability index
of
I\subset S
as the integer

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\textup{-stab}(I)\ =\ \min\{t\ :\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=ak+b,\ \textit{for all}\ k\geq t,\ \textit{and some}\ a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Recall that by
\textup{astab}(I)
one denotes the integer

\textup{astab}(I)\ =\ \min\{t\ :\ \operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I),\ \textit{for all}\ k\geq t\}.

Whereas, for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
we define the integer

\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \min\{t\ :\ \mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k}),\ \textit{for all}\ k\geq t\}.

Notice that
\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\textup{astab}(I)
and
\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\textup{-stab}(I)
. On the other hand,

\textup{vstab}(I)\ \leq\ \max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\textup{-stab}(I)

For instance, for any edge ideal

having linear resolution,
\textup{vstab}(I(G))=1
. Indeed, by

. However, in most cases

. For a simple example, consider

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}\!\textup{-stab}(I(G))=2
.

This example also shows that

can be smaller than

\textup{astab}(I)=1
but
\textup{vstab}(I)=2
. Thus, there is no comparison between the stability indices
\textup{astab}(I)
and
\textup{vstab}(I)
.

Again, let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. By Brodmann
[
7
]
,
\operatorname{depth}I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}I^{k+1}
for all
k\gg 0
. We denote by
\textup{dstab}(I)
the least integer
t
such that
\operatorname{depth}I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}I^{k+1}
for all
k\geq t
. Finally, let
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})=ck+d
for all
k\gg 0
. We denote by
\textup{rstab}(I)
the least integer
t
such that
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})=ck+d
for all
k\geq t
.

Question 5.6
.

Given positive integer
a,d,r,v\geq 1
, can we find a graded ideal
I
in some polynomial ring
S
such that

\textup{astab}(I)=a,\ \ \ \textup{dstab}(I)=d,\ \ \ \textup{rstab}(I)=r,\ \ \ \textup{vstab}(I)=v?

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. In
[
25
]
, Le Tuan Hoa bounded the index
\textup{astab}(I)
of
I
using exquisite techniques from combinatorics and asymptotic linearity of integer programming. For similar recent results, see the papers
[
24
,
26
]
.

Problem 5.7
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Using integer programming techniques, determine good upper bounds for the
\operatorname{v}
-stability index
\textup{vstab}(I)
of
I
.

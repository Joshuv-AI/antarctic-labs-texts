A classification of planes intersecting the Veronese surface over finite fields of even order
Nour Alnajjarine, Michel Lavrauw

Abstract
In this paper we contribute towards the classification of partially symmetric tensors in 𝔽 q 3 ⊗ S 2 ​ 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}\otimes S^{2}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3} , q q even, by classifying planes which intersect the Veronese surface 𝒱 ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) in at least one point, under the action of K ≤ PGL ⁡ ( 6 , q ) K\leq\mathrm{PGL}(6,q) , K ≅ PGL ⁡ ( 3 , q ) K\cong\mathrm{PGL}(3,q) , stabilising the Veronese surface. We also determine a complete set of geometric and combinatorial invariants for each of the orbits. Keywords: Veronese Surface, Tensors, Ranks, Nets of Conics, Finite Fields.

Introduction

Denote by
W=S^{n}\mathbb{F}^{m}
the subspace of symmetric tensors in the
n
-fold tensor product space
V=\mathbb{F}^{m}\otimes...\otimes\mathbb{F}^{m}
defined over a field
\mathbb{F}
.
Decomposing tensors is a notoriously difficult problem, and even more so is classifying tensors up to (natural) equivalence.

A tensor
A\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}
has a first contraction space
C_{1}(A)\leq\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}
, and
A
is called
partially symmetric
if
C_{1}(A)\leq S^{2}\mathbb{F}^{3}
, in other words
A\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes S^{2}\mathbb{F}^{3}
.
In this paper we classify certain types of partially symmetric tensors in
\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes S^{2}\mathbb{F}^{3}
over finite fields
\mathbb{F}
of even order. The original motivation for studying 3-fold tensor products over finite fields comes from the theory of semifields and non-Desarguesian projective planes, see
[
17
]
.
The geometric approach which is used in this paper was originated in previous work by the second author and J. Sheekey
[
21
]
, in which tensors in
\mathbb{F}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}
were classified, and by the second author and T. Popiel
[
18
]
, in which it was determined which line-orbits in
\mathrm{PG}(\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3})
, arising from this classification, have a symmetric representation. Although a complete classification of tensors in
\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}
seems, at this point in time, out of reach, some progress has been made by reversing the previous approach, i.e. by starting with the study of partially symmetric representations of tensors in
\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Classifying such tensors is equivalent to classifying orbits of subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
under the group
K\cong\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
leaving the Veronese surface
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
invariant. The action of
K
can be obtained by lifting the natural action of
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
on
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
through the Veronese map from
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
to
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
(see e.g. Harris
[
11
, Lecture 10]
). It is known that
K
is the setwise stabiliser of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in
\mathrm{PGL}(6,q)
unless
q=2
.
If
q=2
, then
\mathrm{PGL}(3,2)
is strictly contained in the setwise stabiliser of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
equivalent to
\text{Sym}_{7}
.

For all
q
, points, lines, solids and hyperplanes are completely classified in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
. Indeed,
K
-orbits of points and hyperplanes are easily determined (see Section
2
), lines were classified in
[
18
]
, and solids in
[
1
]
. Moreover, planes in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
intersecting the Veronese surface
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in at least one point,
q
odd, are classified in
[
19
,
20
]
.

Therefore, to complete the
classification of subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
, we need to classify planes which are disjoint from
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for all
q
and planes meeting
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for
q
even. In this paper, we investigate the latter classification by considering cubic curves associated with planes in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
. In
particular, we compute for each (type of) plane
\pi\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
its point-orbit distribution represented by the
4
-tuple
[r_{1},r_{2n},r_{2s},r_{3}]
, where
r_{i}
is the number of rank-
i
points in
\pi
for
i\in\{1,3\}
,
r_{2n}
is the number of rank-
2
points in
\pi
meeting the nucleus plane
\mathcal{N}
and
r_{2s}
is the number of the remaining rank-
2
points in
\pi
. In general, we distinguish between orbits using point-orbit distributions, line-orbit distributions and inflexion points defined in Section
2
. While some of the arguments used in our classification come from the classification of planes meeting
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
q
odd
[
19
]
, a unified proof for all
q
seems impossible due to the fundamental differences in the geometry of the Veronese surface in even characteristic.

Historically, the study of subspaces in the space of the Veronese surface began more than a century ago, through the study of linear systems of conics, which can also be interpreted as the classification of
K
-orbits of subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(5,\mathbb{F})
.
More precisely, classifying solids, planes and lines of
\mathrm{PG}(5,\mathbb{F})
correspond to classifying
pencils
,
nets
and

of conics, namely
1
-,
2
- and
3
-dimensional systems, respectively. This problem was completely solved over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
by Jordan and Wall who classified pencils and nets in
[
15
,
16
,
22
]

[
2
]

For finite fields, the narrative is more complicated. For pencils of conics, first studied by Dickson for
q
odd, and later by Campbell for
q
even, we refer to
[

]
and
[

]

Nets over finite fields of odd order, were studied in 1914 by Wilson
[

]
. For

[
7
]
. While both works definitely have their merits non of these gives a complete classification of nets of conics. For the odd characteristic case, we refer to
[

[

already pointed out an error in Campbell’s work, by providing a construction of nets with

[

[

[

[

[

[

Preliminaries

In this section, we review some definitions and theory needed in our proofs, most of which can be found in
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
18
]
.
We also refer the reader to Section 4.6 in
[
13
]
for an overview of the interesting properties of the Veronese surface over finite fields.
The
Veronese surface

\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a 2-dimensional algebraic variety in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
defined as the image of the Veronese map

\nu:\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\rightarrow\mathrm{PG}(5,q)\quad\text{given by}\quad(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{0}^{2},u_{0}u_{1},u_{0}u_{2},u_{1}^{2},u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}^{2}).

Alternatively,
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the set of points
(u_{00},u_{01},u_{02},u_{11},u_{12},u_{22})\in\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
for which the rank of the symmetric matrix defined by
[u_{ij}]
is
1
.
We use the same notation as in
[
19
,
20
]
, where a point
P=(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4},y_{5})
of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
is represented by a symmetric
3\times 3
matrix

M_{P}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{0}&y_{1}&y_{2}\\
y_{1}&y_{3}&y_{4}\\
y_{2}&y_{4}&y_{5}\end{bmatrix},

and in which a dot represents a zero.
This representation can be extended to any subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
.
For example, the plane spanned by the first three points of the standard frame of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
is represented by

\begin{bmatrix}x&y&z\\
y&\cdot&\cdot\\
z&\cdot&\cdot\end{bmatrix}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}x&y&z\\
y&0&0\\
z&0&0\end{bmatrix}:(x,y,z)\in\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\right\}.

(1)

The
rank
of a point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
is defined as the rank of its associated matrix
M_{P}
.
The points of rank
1
are precisely those belonging to
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Setting the determinant of the matrix representation of a plane
\pi
in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
equal to zero, defines a cubic curve
\mathscr{C}(\pi)
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
. The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on
\mathscr{C}(\pi)
correspond to points of rank at most
2
in
\pi
.
Throughout the paper, the indeterminates of the coordinate rings of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
and
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
are denoted by
(X,Y,Z)
and
(Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{5})
respectively, and
\mathcal{Z}(f)
denotes the zero locus of a form
f
.

Notation 2.1
.

We denote by
C_{k}
,
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
and
E_{q}
, a cyclic group of order
k
, a symmetric group on
k
letters and, an elementary abelian group of order
q
, respectively. Moreover,
A\rtimes B
is a semidirect product with normal subgroup
A
and subgroup
B
.

2.1

Quadratic and cubic equations

Over finite fields of characteristic 2, solutions of quadratic and cubic equations depend on the trace map
\operatorname{Tr}~:~\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
defined by

\operatorname{Tr}(x)=x+x^{2}+x^{2^{2}}+...+x^{2^{h-1}}.

We will make frequent use of the following well-known lemma, see e.g.
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The polynomial
f(X)=\alpha X^{2}+\beta X+\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}[X]
with
\alpha\neq 0
has exactly one root in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
if and only if
\beta=0
, two distinct roots in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
if and only if
\beta\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Tr}(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\beta^{2}})=0
, and no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
otherwise.

Remark 2.1
.

Solutions to a cubic equation
c(X)=0
defined by

c(X)=X^{3}+a_{1}X^{2}+a_{2}X+a_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}[X]

can be retrieved by solving a cubic equation of the form

g(\theta)=\theta^{3}+b\theta+a=0.

(2)

For instance, if
a_{2}\neq a_{1}^{2}
, we can apply the substitution

X=(a_{2}+a_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta+a_{1},

to obtain the cubic polynomial
g(\theta)=\theta^{3}+\theta+a
and

a=\frac{a_{3}+a_{2}a_{1}}{(a_{2}+a_{1}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Notice that, as the product of the three roots of
g(\theta)
is equal to
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
, it follows that
g(\theta)
is either irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
, has all its roots in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
, or exactly one root in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{h}}
.

For cubic equations, we will make use of the next theorem, from
[
4
]
, which gives the number of solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
q=2^{h}>2
, of
g(\theta)=\theta^{3}+\theta+a=0
in terms of a condition on the constant term
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is called
admissible
if

a=\frac{v+v^{-1}}{(1+v+v^{-1})^{3}}

for some
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
q=2^{h}>2
and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. The cubic equation
\theta^{3}+\theta+a=0
has

•

three solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
q\neq 4
,
\operatorname{Tr}(a^{-1})=\operatorname{Tr}(1)
and
a
is admissible,

•

a unique solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\operatorname{Tr}(a^{-1})\neq\operatorname{Tr}(1)
,

•

no solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\operatorname{Tr}(a^{-1})=\operatorname{Tr}(1)
and
a
is not admissible.

2.2

The Veronese surface in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)

The Veronese surface
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
contains
q^{2}+q+1
conics, defined as the image of lines in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
under the Veronese map
\nu
, where any two points
P
,
Q
of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
lie on a unique such conic given by

\mathcal{C}(P,Q):=\nu(\langle\nu^{-1}(P),\nu^{-1}(Q)\rangle).

Since conics of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
correspond to lines of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
via
\nu
, any two of these conics have a unique point in common. Also, the conics of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
correspond to the hyperplane sections of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a repeated line, then the corresponding hyperplane of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
meets
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in a conic, if
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of real lines, then the corresponding hyperplane meets
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in two conics, if
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of conjugate imaginary lines, then the corresponding hyperplane meets
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in a point, if
\mathcal{C}
is a non-singular conic, then the corresponding hyperplane meets
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in a
normal rational curve
. For
q\neq 2
, planes of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
which meet
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in a conic are called
conic planes
.

If
q
is even, then all tangent lines to a conic
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are concurrent, meeting at the
nucleus
of
\mathcal{C}
.
The set of all nuclei of conics in
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
coincides with the set of points of a plane in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
known as the
nucleus plane
of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
which is defined as the subspace
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{Z}(Y_{0},Y_{3},Y_{5})
of

\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
. In the matrix representation, points contained in the nucleus plane correspond to symmetric
3\times 3
matrices with zeros on the main diagonal.
Every rank-
2
point
R
of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
defines a unique conic
\mathcal{C}(R)
in
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
If
R
lies on the secant
\langle P,Q\rangle
with
P,Q\in\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
then
\mathcal{C}(R)=\mathcal{C}(P,Q)
.
If
R
is contained in the nucleus plane, then
\mathcal{C}(R)
is the unique conic contained in
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with nucleus
R
.

Tangent lines of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are tangent lines to the conics in
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Since
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a non-singular 2-dimensional variety, all tangent lines of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
at a point
P\in\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are contained in
the tangent plane of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
at
P
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a pencil of lines in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
,
q
even, and denote by
\nu(\mathcal{L})
the set of corresponding conics on
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
under the Veronese map. The set of nuclei of the conics in
\nu(\mathcal{L})
is a set of
q+1
collinear points in the nucleus plane of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

By transitivity, it suffices to compute the nuclei of the pencil
\mathcal{L}
of lines through the point with coordinates
(1,0,0)
. The nuclei of the conics in
\nu(\mathcal{L})
are the
q+1
points of the line
\mathcal{Z}(Y_{0},Y_{3},Y_{4},Y_{5})
in the nucleus plane of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
∎

2.3

The group action

We are interested in the action on subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
of the group
K\leqslant\mathrm{PGL}(6,q)
defined as the lift of
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
through the Veronese map
\nu
.
Explicitly, if
\phi_{A}\in\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
is represented by the matrix
A\in\mathrm{GL}(3,q)
then we define the corresponding projectivity
\alpha(\phi_{A})\in\mathrm{PGL}(6,q)
through its action on the points of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
by

\alpha(\phi_{A}):P\mapsto Q\quad\text{where}\quad M_{Q}=AM_{P}A^{T},

where
M_{Q}
and
M_{P}
are the matrix representations of
Q
and
P
. Then
K:=\alpha(\mathrm{PGL}(3,q))
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
and leaves
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
invariant.

Remark 2.2
.

If
q>2
then
K\cong\mathrm{PGL}(3,q)
is the
full
setwise stabiliser of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in
\mathrm{PGL}(6,q)
.
If
q=2
then the full setwise stabiliser of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is
\text{Sym}_{7}
.

2.4

Points, lines and hyperplanes of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)

The rank distribution of a subspace
W
of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
is the

-tuple
[r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}]
, where

is the number of rank-
i
points in

. Clearly, rank-distributions are

-invariants. The next definition introduces stronger

-invariants, first introduced in
[

]
and more formally in
[

]
, which play a fundamental role in classifying subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{m}
denote a chosen ordering of the distinct
K
-orbits of
r
-spaces in
\mathrm{PG}(n,q)
.
The

4

Comparison with the
q
odd case

Over finite fields of odd characteristic, there exists a polarity of
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
that maps the set of conic planes of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
onto the set of tangent planes of
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 4.25]
). This allows the correspondence between rank-1 nets of conics in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
, namely, nets with at least one double line, and planes in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
meeting
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in at least one point,
q
odd. This correspondence fails over finite fields of characteristic
2
. For instance, let
\pi_{6}
be the representative of
\Sigma_{6}
defined in Table
1
. Then,
\pi_{6}
meets
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in a unique point, however its associated net of conics
\mathcal{N}_{6}
defined by

\alpha XY+\beta XZ+\gamma(Y^{2}+cYZ+Z^{2})=0

(22)

has, by Lemma
2.3
,
q+1
pairs of real lines defined by the pencil

\mathcal{Z}(XY,XZ),

and a unique pair of conjugate imaginary lines given by

\mathcal{Z}(Y^{2}+cYZ+Z^{2}).

Therefore, the hyperplane-orbit distribution of
\pi_{6}
is
[0,q+1,1,q^{2}-1]
, and
\Sigma_{6}
has no double lines. In other words,
\mathcal{N}_{6}
is not a rank-1 net of conics.

Corollary 4.1
.

Rank-1 nets of conics in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
do not correspond to planes having at least one rank-1 point in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
for
q
even.

5

Conclusion

In this paper, we classified and characterized planes in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
intersecting the Veronese surface
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in at least one point under the action of the group stabilising
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
q
even. This complements the work of the second author, T. Popiel and J. Sheekey
[
19
]
in which such planes were classified for
q
odd. Our results contributes towards completing the classification of nets of conics in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
,
q
even. In future work, we hope to classify planes in
\mathrm{PG}(5,q)
which are disjoint from
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Such a classification would complete the longstanding open problem of classifying nets of conics over finite fields.

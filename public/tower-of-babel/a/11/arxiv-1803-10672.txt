Greatest Lower Bounds on Ricci Curvature for Fano T T -manifolds of Complexity 1 1
Jacob Cable

Abstract
In this short note we determine the greatest lower bounds on Ricci curvature for all Fano T T -manifolds of complexity one, generalizing the result of Chi Li. Our method of proof is based on the work of Datar and Székelyhidi, using the description of complexity one special test configurations given by Ilten and Süß.

Introduction

A fundamental problem in Kähler geometry is finding Kähler-Einstein metrics. The cases of
c_{1}(X)<0
and
c_{1}(X)=0
have been settled by Aubin
[
1
]
and Yau
[
18
]
, and so all that remains is the case of Fano manifolds, where
-K_{X}
is ample. Fix a Kähler form
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
. The problem is then to find a Kähler form
\omega^{\prime}
in the same class such that

\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\omega^{\prime})=\omega^{\prime}.

A powerful technique for dealing with this equation is the continuity method, where for
t\in[0,1]
one considers solutions
\omega_{t}
to the continuity path:

\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\omega_{t})=t\omega_{t}+(1-t)\omega.

From
[
18
]
we always have a solution for
t=0
. However Tian
[
16
]
showed that for some
t
sufficiently close to
1
there may not be a solution for certain Fano manifolds. In this case it is natural to ask for the supremum of permissible
t
, which turns out to be independent of the choice of
\omega
. This invariant was first discussed, although not explicitly defined, by Tian in
[
15
]
. It was first explicitely defined by Rubenstein in
[
11
]
and was further studied by Szekelyhidi in
[
14
]
. Tian’s invariant is defined as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold with
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
. Define:

R(X):=\sup(t\in[0,1]:\exists\ \omega_{t}\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)\ \mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\omega_{t})=t\omega_{t}+(1-t)\omega).

In
[
11
]
Rubenstein showed relation between
R(X)
and Tian’s alpha invariant
\alpha(X)
, and in
[
12
]
conjectured that
R(X)
characterizes the
K
-semistability of
X
. This conjecture was later verified by Li in
[
8
]
.

In
[
7
]
Li determined a simple formula for
R(X_{\Delta})
, where
X_{\Delta}
is the polarized toric Fano manifold determined by a reflexive lattice polytope
\Delta
. This result was later recovered in
[
4
]
, by Datar and Székelyhidi, using notions of
G
-equivariant
K
-stability. Using this same method we obtain an effective formula for manifolds with a torus action of complexity one, in terms of the combinatorial data of its divisorial polytope. Previously
R(X)
has been calculated for group compactifications by Delcroix
[
5
]
and for homogeneous toric bundles by Yao
[
17
]
.

Let
X
be a complexity one Fano
T
-variety, that is a Fano variety admitting an effective action of an algebraic torus with maximal orbits of codimension one. We have mutually dual character and cocharacter lattices
M=\text{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*}),\ N=\text{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)
respectively. Similar to toric varieties there exists a combinatorial description for
X
. Let
\mu:X\to M_{\mathbb{R}}
be the moment map for the torus action. Its image is a lattice polytope
\Box
. The push-forward of the Liouville measure under
\mu
is known as the Duistermaat-Heckman measure on
\Box
, which we denote
\nu
.

By the criterion for

-stability in
[
6
]
we know that if the weighted barycenter
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)
coincides with the origin then
X
is
K
-stable and so
R(X)=1
. Moreover it is shown that there are finitely many normal equivariant toric degenerations of
X
and that the corresponding lattice polytopes
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{m}\subseteq M^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}:=M_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}
may be described using the combinatorial data for
X
.

Suppose now
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)\neq 0
. Let
q
be the intersection of the ray generated by
-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)
with
\partial\Box

. Let

be the point of intersection of
\partial\Delta_{i}
with the ray generated by
-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Delta_{i})
, where
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Delta_{i})
is the barycenter of
\Delta_{i}
. This is well defined since
\pi(\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Delta_{i}))=\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)
, where
\pi
is the projection to
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
F_{i}
be the face of
\Delta_{i}
in which
q_{i}
lies, and let
S
be the set of indices

for which all outer normals to
F_{i}
lie in
H
. We may now state our result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a complexity one Fano
T
-variety as above. If
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)=0
then
R(X)=1
. Otherwise we have:

R(X)=\min\ \left\{\frac{|q|}{|q-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)|}\ \right\}\cup\ \left\{\frac{|q_{i}|}{|q_{i}-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Delta_{i})|}\right\}_{i\in S}.

G-equivariant K-stability

Let
X
be a Fano manifold with the action of a complex reductive group
G
of automorphisms containing a maximal torus
T
. Fix a
T
-invariant Kähler form
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
induced by the Fano condition. Recall that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
of the maximal compact torus in
T
may be identified with
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
G
-equivariant test configuration for
(X,G)
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant flat family
\mathcal{X}
over the affine line equipped with a relatively ample equivariant
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}
such that

1.

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
\lambda
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
lifts the standard action on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
;

2.

The general fiber is isomorphic to
X
and
\mathcal{L}
is the relative anti-canonical bundle of
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

3.

The action of
G
extends to
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
and commutes with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
\lambda
.

A test configuration with
\mathcal{X}\cong X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
is called a product configuration. If such an isomorphism exists and is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant then we call the test configuration trivial. Finally a test configuration with normal special fiber is called special.

In this note we work with
G=T
being a maximal torus in
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X)
. We then have an induced
T^{\prime}=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the special fiber. We follow the conventions in
[
2
]
for moment maps and Hamiltonian functions. There is a canonical lift of
T^{\prime}
-action to
-K_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
, inducing a canonical choice of moment map
\mu:\mathcal{X}_{0}\to M_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
. The restriction of
\lambda
to
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is generated by the imaginary part of a
T^{\prime}
-invariant vector field
w
, and by an abuse of notation we also write
w\in N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
for the corresponding one-parameter subgroup. The moment map
\mu
then specifies a Hamiltonian function
\theta_{w}:=\langle\mu,w\rangle:\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The twisted Donaldson-Futaki character of a special test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is given by:

\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits_{t}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})(w)=\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})(w)+\frac{(1-t)}{V}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\max_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\theta_{w}-\theta_{w})\ \omega^{n}.

where
V=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\omega^{n}
is the volume of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and
\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})(w)=\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\theta_{w}\omega^{n}
is the classical Donaldson-Futaki invariant of the configuration, in the form given in
[
2
, Lemma 3.4]
.

We say the pair
(X,t)
is
G
-equivariantly
K
-semistable if
\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits_{t}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq 0
for all
G
-equivariant special configurations
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
. We then have:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
4
, Proposition 10]
)
.

Let
X
be a polarized Fano manifold, with Kähler form
\omega
. Let
t\in[0,1]
. Then
(X,t)
is
G
-equivariantly
K
-semistable only if for all
s<t
there exists
\omega_{s}\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)
such that
\mathop{\rm Ric}\nolimits(\omega_{s})=s\omega_{s}+(1-s)\omega
.

Remark 2.4
.

It follows from this that:

\displaystyle R(X)=\inf_{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})}(\sup(t|\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits_{t}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq 0)),

where
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
varies over all special test configurations for
(X,L)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
w\in\text{int}(\tau^{\vee})\backslash(\mathbb{R}^{+}n)
. For
s\in[0,1]
set
w(s):=sn+(1-s)w
. As
n\in\tau^{\vee}
we may consider
p(s):=p_{w(s)}
. For
0\leq s^{\prime}<s\leq 1
we then have:

\frac{|p(s)|}{|p(s)-b|}<\frac{|p(s^{\prime})|}{|p(s^{\prime})-b|}.

Proof 3.2
.

Without loss of generality we may assume
s^{\prime}=0
. For
s\in[0,1]
the points
p(s),q,b
are collinear, so
|p(s)|=|p(s)-q|+|q|
and
|p(s)-b|=|p(s)-q|+|q|+|b|
. Therefore:

\frac{|p(s)|}{|p(s)-b|}=\frac{|p(s)-q|+|q|}{|p(s)-q|+|q|+|b|}.

Hence it is enough for
|p(s)-q|<|p(0)-q|
whenever
s>0
. Since
q\neq 0
is fixed this is equivalent to:

\frac{|p(s)-q|}{|q|}<\frac{|p(0)-q|}{|q|}.

For each
s\in[0,1]
choose
a(s)\in\partial P
such that
w(s)\in\mathcal{N}(a(s))
. Write
a=a(0)
for convenience. We then have:

\frac{|p(s)-q|}{|q|}=\frac{\langle a(s)-q,w\rangle}{\langle q,w\rangle}.

Note
n\in\mathcal{N}(q)
. Now
\langle a(s)-q,n\rangle\leq 0
and
\langle a(s)-q,w\rangle\leq\langle a-q,w\rangle
. Clearly we have
\langle q,n\rangle>0
. Then:

\displaystyle\frac{\langle a(s)-q,w(s)\rangle}{\langle q,w(s)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{s\langle a(s)-q,n\rangle+(1-s)\langle a(s)-q,w\rangle}{s\langle q,n\rangle+(1-s)\langle q,w\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-s)\langle a-q,w\rangle}{s\langle q,n\rangle+(1-s)\langle q,w\rangle}

\displaystyle<\frac{\langle a-q,w\rangle}{\langle q,w\rangle}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

F_{b}(w,t):=t\langle b,w\rangle+(1-t)\max_{x\in P}\langle x,w\rangle

\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ \forall_{w\in W}\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)=\frac{|q|}{|q-b|}.

\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ \forall_{w\in H}\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)=\frac{|\tilde{q}|}{|\tilde{q}-\pi(b)|},

(2)

Proof 3.4
.

Note that:

\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ \forall_{w\in W}\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)=\inf_{w\in W}\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0).

Moreover
\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)=1>F_{b}(t,n)
for

, so without loss of generality we may assume

\displaystyle\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\max_{x\in P}\langle x,w\rangle}{\max_{x\in P}\langle x,w\rangle-\langle b,w\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle a,w\rangle}{\langle a,w\rangle-\langle b,w\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle p_{w},w\rangle}{\langle p_{w},w\rangle-\langle b,w\rangle}=\frac{|p_{w}|}{|p_{w}-b|}.

Hence:

\sup(t\in[0,1]\ |\ \forall_{w\in W}\ F_{b}(t,w)\geq 0)=\inf_{w\in W}\frac{|p_{w}|}{|p_{w}-b|}.

Now for

Applying (1) to the polytope

Proof of Theorem 1.1

Let
T
be an algebraic torus. We recall the definition of a divisorial polytope, which is used to generalize the polytope description of a polarized toric variety to the complexity one case. We will be able to calculate
R(X)
for our varieties using this combinatorial data.

Definition 4.1
.

A divisorial polytope function
\Psi
on a lattice polytope
\Box\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
:

\Psi:\Box\to\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}^{1},\ u\mapsto\Sigma_{y\in\mathbb{P}^{1}}\Psi_{y}(u)\cdot\{y\},

such that:

•

For
y\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
the function
\Psi_{y}:\Box\to\mathbb{R}
is the minimum of finitely many affine functions, and
\Psi_{y}\equiv 0
for all but finitely many
y\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

•

Each
\Psi_{y}
takes integral values at the vertices of the polyhedral decomposition its regions of affine linearity induce on
\Box
.

•

\deg\Psi(u)>-2
for
u\in\text{int}(\Box)
;

A divisorial polytope is said to be Fano if additionally we have that:

•

The origin is an interior lattice point of
\Box
.

•

The affine linear pieces of each
\Psi_{y}
are of the form
u\mapsto\frac{\langle v,u\rangle-\beta+1}{\beta}
for some primitive lattice element
v\in N
;

•

Every facet
F
of
\Box
with
(\deg\circ\Psi_{|F})\neq-2
has lattice distance
1
from the origin.

Let
\Psi
be a divisorial polytope. We may construct a complexity one polarized
T
-variety from the graded ring
S
given by:

S_{k}:=\bigoplus_{u\in\Box\cap\frac{1}{k}M}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(\lfloor k\cdot(\Psi(u)+D)\rfloor),

where
D
is some integral divisor of degree
2
. We may also recover a divisorial polytope
\Psi
from any polarized complexity one
T
-variety
(X,L)
such that
H^{0}(X,L^{k})=S_{k}
. Moreover Fano divisorial polytopes correspond to Fano
T
-varieties
(X,-K_{X})
. Thus all Fano complexity one
T
-varieties may be described in this way. For more details of this construction and the correspondence see
[
13
]
or
[
6
]
.

A Fano divisorial polytope fixes a distinguished representative of
-K_{X}
and specifies a linearisation of the action of
T
to
(X,-K_{X})
. By the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.21]
it is seen that the linearisation coincides with the canonical linearisation of
(X,-K_{X})
, and so the moment map specified by the divisorial polytope, with image
\Box
, coincides with the moment specified by the canonical linearisation.

We now recall some basic terminology for divisorial polytopes. The push-forward of the measure induced by
\omega
is known as the Duistermaat-Heckman measure, independent of the choice of
\omega
and which we denote by
\nu
. Denote the standard measure on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
by
\eta
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Psi
be a divisorial polytope.

•

The degree of
\Psi
is the map
\deg\Psi:\Box\to\mathbb{R}
given by
u\mapsto\deg(\Psi(u))
.

•

The barycenter of
\Psi
is
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi)\in\Box
, such that for all
v\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
:

\langle\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi),v\rangle=\int_{\Box}v\cdot\deg\Psi\ d\eta=\int_{\Box}vd\nu.

Note by the second equality we see
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi)=\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box).

•

The volume of
\Psi
is defined to be:

\mathop{\rm vol}\nolimits\Psi=\int_{\Box}\deg\Psi\ d\eta=\int_{\Box}d\nu.

From here on let
(X,-K_{X})
be the polarized complexity one Fano
T
-manifold given by a fixed divisorial polytope
\Psi:\Box\to\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with
\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)\neq 0
. We will calculate
R(X)
by considering first the product configurations and then the non-product ones.

4.1
Product Configurations

If
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is a product configuration then we have
\mathcal{X}_{0}\cong X
. Assuming
X
is non-toric, the maximality of
T
in
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(X)
ensures that the restriction of
\lambda
to
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is a one parameter subgroup of
T
, given by a choice of
w\in N
.
We then have:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits_{t}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})(w)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})(w)+\frac{(1-t)}{V}\int_{X}(\max\theta_{w}-\theta_{w})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi),w\rangle+\frac{(1-t)}{\mathop{\rm vol}\nolimits\Psi}\int_{\Box}\max_{x\in\Box}\langle x,w\rangle-\langle\cdot,w\rangle d\eta

\displaystyle=t\langle\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi),w\rangle+(1-t)\max_{x\in\Box}\langle x,w\rangle.

Let
q\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the point of intersection of the ray generated by
-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits(\Psi)
with
\partial\Box
. Applying (1) we then obtain:

\sup(t|\mathop{\rm DF}\nolimits_{t}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq 0)=\frac{|q|}{|q-\mathop{\rm bc}\nolimits_{\nu}(\Box)|}.

4.2
Non-Product Configurations

We recall, from
[
6
]
, the description of special fibers of non-product special configurations. Let
X
be a Fano
T
-variety of complexity
1
, corresponding to the Fano divisorial polytope
\Psi
. The toric central fibers of non-product special test configurations were explicitely described in
[
6
]
. Namely there exists some
y\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with at most one of
\Psi_{z}
having non-integral slope at any
u\in\Box
for
z\neq y
, such that
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is the toric variety corresponding to the following polytope:

\Delta_{y}:=\Big\{(u,r)\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}\;\Big|\;u\in\Box,\;-1-\sum_{z\neq y}\Psi_{z}(u)\leq r\leq 1+\Psi_{y}(u)\Big\}.

The induced
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is given by the one-parameter subgroup of

T^{\prime}=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
corresponding to
v^{\prime}=(-mv,m)\in N\times\mathbb{Z}
, for some
v\in N
. In fact it turns out, from
[
6

, it is enough to consider those configurations with

.

Proposition 4.3

.

Proof 4.7

(of Theorem 1.1)
.

With Remark 2 in mind, observe that a special test configuration must either be product or non-product. Any non-product configurations
\Delta_{i}
with
\sigma_{i}\cap H\neq\emptyset
have their contribution to the infimum already accounted for and we may exclude them. The result follows.

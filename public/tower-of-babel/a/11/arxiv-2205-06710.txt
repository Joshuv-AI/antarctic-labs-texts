Linesearch Newton-CG methods for convex optimization with noise
S. Bellavia, E. Fabrizi, B. Morini

Abstract
This paper studies the numerical solution of strictly convex unconstrained optimization problems by linesearch Newton-CG methods. We focus on methods employing inexact evaluations of the objective function and inexact and possibly random gradient and Hessian estimates. The derivative estimates are not required to satisfy suitable accuracy requirements at each iteration but with sufficiently high probability. Concerning the evaluation of the objective function we first assume that the noise in the objective function evaluations is bounded in absolute value. Then, we analyze the case where the error satisfies prescribed dynamic accuracy requirements. We provide for both cases a complexity analysis and derive expected iteration complexity bounds. We finally focus on the specific case of finite-sum minimization which is typical of machine learning applications. Key words: Newton-CG, evaluation complexity, inexact function and derivatives, probabilistic analysis, finite-sum optimization, subsampling.

2.1
Assumptions

We introduce the assumptions on the problem (
1

Assumption 2.2
.

(smoothness and strong convexity of

)
The function

is twice continuously differentiable and there exist some
\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{1}>0
such that the Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}f(x)
satisfy

\lambda_{1}I\preceq\nabla^{2}f(x)\preceq\lambda_{n}I,\;\;\;\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

As a consequence,

f(x)\geq f(y)\nabla f(y)^{T}(x-y)+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is Lipschitz-continuous with constant

, i.e.,

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq\lambda_{n}\|x-y\|\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Further, letting

be the unique minimizer of the function

, for all

we have

\lambda_{1}\|x-x^{*}\|\leq\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq\lambda_{n}\|x-x^{*}\|,

(14)

and

\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq f(x)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}.

(15)

see
[
26
, Theorem 2.10]
.

As for approximated evaluations of the objective function, we consider
two possible cases. The first one is such that

is approximated with a value

and the corresponding error is not controllable but

Assumption 2.3
.

(boundness of noise in

There is a positive scalar

The second case concerns a controllable error between

and
\tilde{f}(x)
at the current iteration

and at the trial iteration

.

Assumption 2.4
.

(controllable noise in

For all

\displaystyle|f(x_{k})-\tilde{f}(x_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\theta t_{k}s_{k}^{T}g_{k},

\displaystyle|f(x_{k}+t_{k}s_{k})-\tilde{f}(x_{k}+t_{k}s_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\theta t_{k}s_{k}^{T}g_{k}.

(17)

Bounded noise on

In this section we present and analyze an Inexact Newton method with line-search where

is reduced. Our procedure belongs to the framework given in Section 4.2 of

-th iteration of Algorithm

Step 1.

.

Step 2.

and compute

).

If

satisfies condition (

,

,

]

generates a stochastic process.
Given

Concerning the well definiteness of the linesearch strategy (

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.5
and

hold. Suppose that iteration

is true
and consider any realization of Algorithm
3
.
Then the iteration is successful whenever

Proof.

Let
k
be an arbitrary iteration.
Assumptions
2.2
and (

) imply, using the standard arguments for functions with bounded Hessians,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k}+t_{k}s_{k})-\varepsilon_{f}\leq f(x_{k}+t_{k}s_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{k})+\int_{0}^{1}[\nabla f(x_{k}+yt_{k}s_{k})]^{T}(t_{k}s_{k})dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{k})+\int_{0}^{1}t_{k}\left([\nabla f(x_{k}+yt_{k}s_{k})]^{T}s_{k}\pm\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x_{k})+\int_{0}^{1}t_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k}+yt_{k}s_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k})]^{T}s_{k}dy+t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{k})+\int_{0}^{1}t_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k}+yt_{k}s_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|s_{k}\|dy+t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x_{k})+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k})+\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}.

Since iteration

is true by assumption then

using Lemma

we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k}+t_{k}s_{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k})+2\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}s_{k}\pm t_{k}g_{k}^{T}s_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k})+2\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})-g_{k}]^{T}s_{k}+t_{k}g_{k}^{T}s_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k})+2\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}^{2}\frac{\eta_{k}}{\kappa_{1}}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}g_{k}^{T}s_{k}.

Then, the linesearch condition (

) is clearly enforced whenever

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{k})+2\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}^{2}\frac{\eta_{k}}{\kappa_{1}}\|s_{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}g_{k}^{T}s_{k}\leq\tilde{f}(x_{k})+ct_{k}s_{k}^{T}g_{k}+2\varepsilon_{f}

. Then, since

3.1
Complexity analysis of the stochastic process

In this section we carry out the convergence analysis of Algorithm

. To this end
we provide a bound on the expected number of
iterations that the algorithm takes before it achieves a desired level of accuracy in the optimality gap

Such a number of iteration is defined formally below.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be the global minimizer of

. Given some

The number of iterations

\sigma({\mathbbm{1}}\{N_{\epsilon}>k\})\subset\mathcal{F}_{k-1}

Following the notation introduced in Section

, be the random variable with realization

and consider

Further, we let

be an upper bound for

for any

. We denote with

.

A theoretical framework for analyzing a generic line search with noise has been developed

[

]
. Under a suitable set of conditions, it provides the expected value for

.
We state a result from
[

]
and will exploit it for our algorithm.

.

Suppose that Assumptions

,
2.3
,
2.5
,

Let

a realization of

in (

) and suppose that there exist a constant

, a nondecreasing function

, such that for any realization of Algorithm

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{k}+s_{k}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-H_{k}^{-1}g_{k}+H_{k}^{-1}r_{k}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|H_{k}^{-1}[\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})-\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})+H_{k}(x_{k}-x^{*})-g_{k}\pm\nabla f(x_{k})+r_{k}]\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|H_{k}^{-1}\|(\|\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|+

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})-H_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})\|+\|g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|+\|r_{k}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\Big(\|\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|+

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})-H_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})\|+\|g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|+\|r_{k}\|\Big).

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})-H_{k})(x_{k}-x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})-H_{k}\|\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|,

Then, since

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\left[\frac{L_{H}}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+C\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|+\frac{2\lambda_{n}\eta_{k}}{1-\bar{\eta}}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|\right]

.

The evaluations of

can be made using subsampling, that means picking randomly and uniformly chosen subsets of indexes
{\cal N}_{g,k}
and
{\cal N}_{H,k}
from

and
define

g_{k}=\frac{1}{|{\cal N}_{g,k}|}\sum_{i\in{\cal N}_{g,k}}\nabla f_{i}(x_{k}),\quad\text{ and }\quad H_{k}=\frac{1}{|{\cal N}_{H,k}|}\sum_{i\in{\cal N}_{H,k}}\nabla^{2}f_{i}(x_{k}).

(37)

If we want
g_{k}
and
H_{k}
to be probabilistically sufficiently accurate as in Definition
2.5
and in Assumption
4.5
respectively, the sample sizes
|{\cal N}_{g,k}|
and
|{\cal N}_{H,k}|
can be determined by using the operator-Bernstein inequality introduced in

are
(1-\delta_{g})
and

\displaystyle|{\cal N}_{g,k}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\left\{N,\frac{4\kappa_{f,g}(x_{k})}{\gamma_{g,k}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{f,g}(x_{k})}{\gamma_{g,k}}+\frac{1}{3}\right)\log\left(\frac{n+1}{\delta_{g}}\right)\right\},

(38)

\displaystyle|{\cal N}_{H,k}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\left\{N,\frac{4\kappa_{f,H}(x_{k})}{C\eta_{k}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{f,H}(x_{k})}{C\eta_{k}}+\frac{1}{3}\right)\log\left(\frac{2n}{\delta_{H}}\right)\right\},

(39)

where

is an approximation of the required gradient accuracy, namely
\gamma_{g,k}\approx~t_{k}\eta_{k}\|G_{k}\|

and under the assumption that, for any

and
\kappa_{f,H}

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{f,g}(x),

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\|\nabla^{2}f_{i}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{f,H}(x).

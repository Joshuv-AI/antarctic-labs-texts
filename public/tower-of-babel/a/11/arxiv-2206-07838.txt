Generic root counts and flatness in tropical geometry
Paul Alexander Helminck, Yue Ren

Abstract
. We use tropical and non-archimedean geometry to study generic root counts of families of polynomial equations. These families are given as morphisms of schemes X → Y X\to{Y} that factor through a closed embedding into a relative torus over a parameter space Y Y . We prove a generalization of Bernstein’s theorem for these morphisms, showing that the root count of a single well-behaved tropical fiber spreads to an open dense subset of Y Y . We use this to express the generic root count of a wide class of square systems in terms of the matroidal degree of an explicit variety. This in particular gives tropical formulas for the birational intersection indices and volumes of Newton-Okounkov bodies from [ KK10 , KK12 ] , and the generic root counts of the steady-state equations of chemical reaction networks. An important role in these theorems is played by the notion of tropical flatness, which allows us to infer generic properties of X → Y X\to{Y} from a single tropical fiber. We show that the tropical analogue of the generic flatness theorem holds, in the sense that X → Y X\to{{Y}} is tropically flat over an open dense subset of the Berkovich analytification of Y Y .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the problem of determining the generic number of solutions of parametrized polynomial systems using tropical and non-archimedean geometry.
These systems arise naturally in many areas, describing for instance the
27
lines on a smooth cubic surface, the dynamics of the Wnt signaling pathway
[
GHRS16
]
, the motion of the Gough-Steward platform
[
SW05
]
, and the central configurations in Newton’s
N
-body problem
[
HM06
]
.
One of the leading techniques in solving these systems numerically is homotopy continuation, which is capable of computing a single root in average polynomial time
[
Lai17
]
.
If however all roots are required, then finding a good upper bound on the total number of roots poses a major problem.

The archetypal example of a generic root count of a family of polynomial systems is
Bézout’s theorem
, which states that
n
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
in
n
variables of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
generically have
\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
solutions. Here, the family consists of all
n
-tuples
(f_{1},...,f_{n})
of polynomials of degrees
(d_{1},...,d_{n})
and being generic means that the number of solutions is attained on a Zariski open dense subset of the family.
Another example is the
Bernstein-Kushnirenko theorem

[
Kou76
,
Ber76
]
, where the family consists of systems with fixed monomial supports and the generic root count is the mixed volume of their Newton polytopes.
A third prominent example can be found in the works of Kaveh and Khovanskii
[
KK10
,
KK12
]
, who consider family of systems with fixed polynomial supports. Their generic root count is the birational intersection index, and in special cases it is the volume of the associated
Newton-Okounkov body
.

The goal of this paper is to develop a new approach for determining generic root counts using tropical geometry.
We establish the notion of
tropical flatness
and use it to extend the Bernstein-Kushnirenko theorem to families of properly intersecting schemes. Herein the mixed volume is naturally replaced by the tropical intersection product, and the initial system criterion
[
Ber76
, Theorem B]
is rephrased in terms of tropical intersections. This allows us to introduce a method for constructing good re-embeddings of square polynomial systems, and we show how it can be applied to derive a formula for the number of steady states of chemical reaction networks
[
Dic16
]
, and the birational intersection indices and volumes of the aforementioned Newton-Okounkov bodies from
[
KK10
,
KK12
]
.

1.1.
Outline and main results of this paper

In
Section

2

X\to{Y}
, where
Y
is the parameter space and

is the base space.
The parameter space

will be integral of finite type over a non-archimedean field
K
, and given a generically finite morphism

, we define its

\ell_{X/Y}
to be the length of the fiber of
X\to{Y}
over the generic point
\eta
of
Y
, see
Section

2.2
.

From
Section

3

will denote a relative
n
-dimensional torus over

, and we will assume that

factors through a closed embedding

. This naturally gives rise to the notion of a

over a point

in the Berkovich analytification

of

.
As fiberwise tropicalizations need not vary continuously, we introduce the notion of tropical flatness. We say
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat over

if, locally and after a valued field extension, there exist polynomials

such that:

(1)

(
Non-degeneracy
) The coefficients of
f_{i}
do not vanish around
P
.

(2)

(
Tropical basis
) The
f_{i}
form a tropical basis of
X
around
P
.

We then prove the tropical analogue of the generic flatness theorem:

Theorem
3.22
.

Let
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
be an integral scheme of finite type over a non-archimedean field
K
.
Then the tropically flat locus of a closed embedding of
Y
-schemes
X\to{T_{Y}}

contains an open dense subset of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Theorem

3.22
in particular implies that if
K
is non-trivially valued and algebraically closed, then
Y({K})
contains a dense open subset over which
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat. To prove this theorem, we study tropical bases of
X
over points in
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
that give valuations of the function field
K(Y)
. For small enough neighborhoods of these points, we have that tropical bases continue to give tropical bases. The density in
Theorem

3.22
then follows from an approximation argument.

If
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat over a point, then many properties of the corresponding fiber of
X\to Y
spread to an open dense subset of the parameter space

, thus becoming generic properties.
In
Section

4
, we investigate one instance of this phenomenon, and show that the tropical intersection number over a tropically flat point is the generic root count:

Theorem
4.5
.

Let
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
be an integral scheme that is of finite type over a non-archimedean field
K
and let
p\colon X\to{Y}
be an affine morphism of finite type.
Suppose that the following hold:

(1)

The morphism
p
factors through a closed embedding
X\to{T_{Y}}
of
Y
-schemes, where
T_{Y}
is an
n
-dimensional torus over
Y
.

(2)

The closed subscheme
X\to{T_{Y}}
is an intersection of closed subschemes

X_{i}\to{T_{Y}}
,
i=1,\dots,r
, that are
generically Cohen-Macaulay and pure of relative dimension
k_{i}
over
Y
with

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n-k_{i})=n.

(3)

There is a
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
over which the
X_{i}\to T_{Y}
are tropically flat and the intersection
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{trop}(X_{i,P})
is bounded.

Then

is generically finite and the generic root count

is the tropical intersection number

.

Theorem

4.5
can be regarded as a generalization of Bernstein’s theorem to families of properly intersecting schemes. Here the mixed volume in
[
Ber76
, Theorem A]
is replaced by a tropical intersection number and the initial system criterion of
[
Ber76
, Theorem B]
is phrased in terms of tropical intersections. The concept of tropical flatness is crucial in

, as otherwise the tropical intersection numbers would be able to vary from fiber to fiber.

To facilitate the use of
Theorem

4.5
we introduce the notions of

and

. We say that an embedding

is torus-equivariant if there is an action
T_{Y}\times_{Y}{Y}\to{Y}
such that
\lambda\cdot{X_{P}}=X_{\lambda\cdot{}P}
for every point
(\lambda,P)
of
T_{Y}\times_{Y}{Y}
, see
Section

4.2
for more details.
We furthermore say that the families
X_{i}\to{Y}
from
Theorem

4.5
are algebraically independent if there exist
X^{\prime}_{i}\to Y_{i}
with
\prod_{K}{Y_{i}}=Y
such that
X^{\prime}_{i}\times_{Y_{i}}Y=X_{i}
. In
Proposition

4.19
, we show that torus-equivariant and algebraically independent families satisfy the conditions of
Theorem

4.5
. In a sense, if a family is torus-equivariant then we can move the tropical fibers to obtain a stable intersection. This concept furthermore allows us to eliminate intersections in loci where the fibers
X_{i,P}
are not Cohen-Macaulay.

From
Section

5
on, we focus on square systems. The moduli of these square systems are captured by a finite graph which we call the degeneracy graph of
X\to{Y}
, see
Section

5.1
.
In
Section

5.2
, we propose a re-embedding of
X\to Y
that splits
X
into a linear part
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}\to Y
and a nonlinear part
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin}}\to Y
.
We say that
X\to{Y}
is

if
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}
is torus-equivariant, and
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}\to{Y}
and
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin}}\to{Y}
are algebraically independent and of complementary codimension.
For tropically modifiable

, we obtain the following combinatorial formulas for their generic root counts.

Theorem
5.11
.

Let
X\to Y
be tropically modifiable. Then there exists a dense open subset
U\subset{T_{Y}}
such that
X\cap{U}\to{Y}
is generically finite. The generic root count is given by

(1)

\ell_{X\cap U/Y}=\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{lin}},P})\cdot\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})

for explicit schemes
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}\to Y
and
\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}}}\to Y
and
P
in an open dense subset of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
.

The open subset
U
in
Theorem

can moreover be chosen to be the maximal one with

\ell_{X\cap U/Y}=\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{lin}},P})\cdot\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
. We call this open subset the maximal compatible subset of the modification.
It arises from intrinsic obstructions similar to those in the works of Kaveh and Khovanskii
[
KK10
,
KK12
]
. We describe the maximal compatible subset

and provide sufficient criteria for

.

As the right hand side of Equation (

) depends only on the matroid of
\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{lin}},P}
, we refer to it as a

of
\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P}
. If

is a tropical complete intersection, then the generic root count

is expressible as a weighted sum of mixed volumes using cotransversal matroid decompositions. More generally, we can use a result by Jensen and Yu

, Corollary 5.2]
, based on a result by McMullen

, to write

In
Section

6
, we consider two classes of linearly parametrized square polynomial systems: systems with
vertical dependencies
inspired by steady state equations of chemical reaction networks
[
Dic16
]
, and systems with
horizontal dependencies
inspired by polynomials with fixed polynomial support
[
KK10
,
KK12
]
. We show that the method from
Section

5
is applicable to these systems. This in particular gives a formula for the birational intersection indices
[L_{1},...,L_{n}]
from
[
KK10
]
for

subspaces of the function field of a torus in terms of tropical intersection numbers. Using
[
KK12
, Theorem 4.9]
, we then also obtain a formula for the volumes of Newton-Okounkov bodies in terms of tropical intersection numbers.

These special cases provide simplifications of the tropical intersection numbers from
Section

in two ways. Namely, for systems with vertical dependencies, we find that the
\mathrm{trop}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
are tropical complete intersections, whereas for systems with horzontal dependencies the
\mathrm{trop}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{lin}},P})
are tropical complete intersections. We demonstrate our technique in two examples: the stationary equations of the Kuramoto model
[

]
and Duffing oscillators
[
BMMT22
]
.

1.2.
Connections to existing literature

A landmark result in the subject of counting roots of polynomial equations is Bernstein’s theorem
[
Ber76
,
Kou76
]
, which establishes the mixed volume as an upper bound for the number of roots of a square polynomial system. An algorithmic milestone in this area was the introduction of the technique of polyhedral homotopy continuation
[
HS95
]
. More recently, Kaveh and Khovanskii introduced the notion of Newton-Okounkov bodies
[
KK12
]
, which in particular gives the root count for specialized systems associated to a single linear subspace.
Our work is similar in purpose, but different in approach.
To find generic root counts, we use techniques from non-archimedean and tropical geometry.
Our approach allows us to treat overdetermined systems that come with a suitable decomposition, as well as systems with more general dependencies between coefficients.
In particular, in
Section

6.1
we discuss systems which arise as steady state equations of chemical reaction networks, and in
Section

6.2
we discuss systems as considered by
[
KK12
]
. We show that our techniques can be applied to both types of system to give generic root counts as tropical intersection products.

Our work builds on two fundamental papers
[
OP13
]
and
[
OR13
]
by Osserman, Payne and Rabinoff, which relate tropical intersection theory to algebraic intersection theory. Our contribution with respect to these papers is that we are able to identify a precise context in which these results can be propagated to open dense subsets of arbitrary integral parameter spaces. The key ingredient is tropical flatness, which gives very precise control over the local variation of fiberwise tropicalizations.
We furthermore obtain a generic tropical flatness theorem, showing that the condition imposed here is satisfied for most points in the parameter space.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Berkovich spaces

All schemes in this paper are Noetherian unless mentioned otherwise. The residue field of a point
P\in{X}
is denoted by
k(P)
. For a commutative unitary ring
A
, we denote a polynomial ring in
n
variables over
A
by
A[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
, and a Laurent polynomial ring in
n
variables over
A
by
A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
.
Let
K
be a non-archimedean field with absolute value
|\cdot{}|
. We allow the absolute value to be trivial. The valuation associated to
(K,|\cdot{}|)
is
x\mapsto-\mathrm{log}|x|
, where
\mathrm{log}(\cdot{})
is the natural logarithm.

Let
X
be a scheme that is locally of finite type over a non-archimedean field
K
. The Berkovich analytification

of

is the set of pairs
(P,k(P)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}})
, where
P\in{X}
and
k(P)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}}
is an absolute value extending the absolute value on
K
.
Equivalently, we can view points of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
as equivalence classes of
L
-valued points of
X
, where
L
is a valued field extension of
K
. If
p\colon X\to{Y}
is a morphism of schemes, then we denote its analytification by
p^{\mathrm{an}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
. There is a natural forgetful map
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to{X}
that sends
Q=(P,k(P)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}})
to
P
. We say that
Q
is rational if the induced map
K\to{k(\pi(Q))}
is an isomorphism. If
X=\mathrm{Spec}(B)
, then
X^{\mathrm{an}}
coincides with the usual definition of multiplicative seminorms
B\to{\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}}}
that restrict to the given norm on
K
.
If
f\in{B}
and
P\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
, then we write
f(P)
for the image of
f
under
A\to{k(\pi(P))}
. Similarly, we write
|f(P)|\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}}
for the image of
f
under
B\to{k(\pi(P))}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}}
. The topology on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is generated by

\mathbb{B}(f,r_{1},r_{2})=\{P:r_{1}<|f(P)|<r_{2}\}

for
r_{1}\geq{0}
and
r_{2}>0
. If
X
is given the Zariski topology and
X^{\mathrm{an}}
the topology above, then
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to{X}
is continuous. For more background information on Berkovich spaces and tropicalizations, we recommend
[
BPR16
]
,
[
Gub13
]
and
[
Tem15
]
.

Remark 2.1

Let
X=\mathrm{Spec}(B)
be of finite type over a non-archimedean field
K
and let
P\in{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
with field of definition
L=k(\pi(P))
. The point

induces a canonical point

by the following procedure. We represent

as a ring homomorphism

. Then

is given by the natural ring homomorphism
B_{L}=L[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})\to{L}
. If

is a valued field extension different from

, then

in general does not induce a canonical point of

. In this case we simply choose a point

lying over

. This exists by
[

, Lemma 2.3]
applied to

. Here

is the completion of

with respect to the given valuation. If

is rational, then there is only one point
P_{M}
lying over
P
for any valued field extension

.

2.2.
Generic properties

In this section, we define generic root counts, generically Cohen-Macaulay morphisms and generically pure morphisms. Determining generic root counts will be main focus of the paper; the other two are important conditions required to find generic root counts.

Let
p\colon X\to Y
be locally of finite type with
Y
integral. If
p
is generically finite, then there are finitely many points lying over the generic point
\eta
of
Y
. By
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 02NG
]
, we then have that the generic fiber
X_{\eta}
is a finite

-scheme, where
k(\eta)
is the residue field of
\eta
. This is associated to a finite

-algebra, whose

-dimension we call the length or degree of

.

Definition 2.2

The
generic root count
of

is the length of the
k(\eta)
-scheme
X_{\eta}
. We denote it by
\ell_{X/Y}
. If
X\to{Y}
is not generically finite, then we set
\ell_{X/Y}=\infty
.

Example 2.3

Suppose
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is integral and

is affine with

. Then

\ell_{X/Y}=\mathrm{dim}_{K(A)}(B\otimes_{A}K(A)),

where
K(A)
is the function field of

.

Remark 2.4

By our assumptions,
X
is Noetherian and thus
p:X\to{Y}
is quasi-separated and quasi-compact. If

is generically finite, then there is an open affine
U\subset{Y}
such that

is finite
[
Stacks22

Lemma 02NW

.
For every

over

\ell_{X/Y}
.

We say that a morphism of schemes

is generically Cohen-Macaulay if there is an open dense

such that

is Cohen-Macaulay. We can rephrase this in terms of generic fibers as follows.

Lemma 2.5

Let
Y
be integral and let
p\colon X\to{Y}
a morphism of finite type. Suppose that the generic fiber
X_{\eta}
is Cohen-Macaulay. Then there is an open dense subset
U\subset{Y}
such that
p^{-1}(U)\to{U}
is a Cohen-Macaulay morphism.

Proof.

We can assume by generic flatness that
p
is flat. Consider the open subset
W
from
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 045U
]
. Its complement

is closed and the fiber of
Z\to{Y}
over the generic point is empty by assumption. This gives an open set

containing

p^{-1}(U_{\eta})\cap{Z}=\emptyset
by
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 02NE
]
. The induced morphism
p^{-1}(U_{\eta})\to{U_{\eta}}
is automatically Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

Definition 2.6

Let
p\colon X\to{Y}
be a morphism of finite type. We say that

is

such that the irreducible components of the fibers of
f
over

.

Let

]

]

]

over

. We can furthermore assume by shrinking

Y
-schemes
\phi\colon X\rightarrow T_{Y}
, and we denote the ideal of the embedding by
I\subseteq A[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
. For any Zariski open
V
in
Y
, we write
A_{V}
for the corresponding coordinate ring and
I_{V}
for the corresponding ideal in
A_{V}[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
.

Let
Q\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
.
We write
I_{Q}
for the image of
I\otimes_{A}k(\pi(Q))
in
k(\pi(Q))[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
. The corresponding scheme gives the fiber of
V(I)
over
\pi(Q)
. For
f\in{A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]}
and
\mathcal{G}\subset A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
, we similarly write
f_{Q}
and
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
for the images of
f
and
\mathcal{G}
under
A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]\to k(\pi(Q))[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
.

Definition 2.10

A
decomposition
of the closed embedding
\phi
is a finite set of ideals
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\subseteq A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
such that
I=\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{i}
.
Writing
X_{i}=\mathrm{Spec}(A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]/I_{i})
for the corresponding closed subschemes, we then have an isomorphism

X\simeq{\prod_{T_{Y}}X_{i}},

where the right-hand side is the fiber product over the embeddings
\phi_{i}\colon X_{i}\to{}T_{Y}
.

We say a decomposition is
square
if
k=n
and
I_{i}=(f_{i})
for
f_{i}\in A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
.
We will assume that the
f_{i}
are chosen so that
V(f_{i})
is relatively and generically pure of codimension one.
We also refer to these as
square polynomial systems
.
The
mixed volume
of the decomposition is the normalized mixed volume of the Newton polytopes of the
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
. We denote it by
\mathrm{MV}(f_{1},...,f_{n})
.

Remark 2.11

For more general decompositions, we can view the tropical intersection number of a general fiber as the analogue of the mixed volume, see
Proposition

4.19
.

2.4.
Universal families

We now define universal families of polynomial systems with a fixed monomial support. Square systems are studied in more detail in Sections
5
and
6
.

Definition 2.12

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
[k]=\{1,...,k\}
. A
fixed monomial support
is a finite subset
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times[k]
such that
S\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\{i\})\neq\emptyset
for every
i\in{S}
. We write
S_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for the projection of
S\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\{i\}})
onto
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and we will generally assume that
|S_{i}|>1
. The
parameter ring
associated to a fixed monomial support
S
is the ring
A=K[c_{i,\alpha}|(\alpha,i)\in S]
generated by variables
c_{i,\alpha}
with
(\alpha,i)\in S
.

Consider the (Laurent) polynomials

f_{i}=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{i}}c_{i,\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in A[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}].

These are the universal polynomials with fixed monomial support
S
.
We set
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]/(f_{1},...,f_{k}))
and
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
.
If
k=n
, we call
X\to{Y}
the
universal family
of square polynomial systems over
K
with monomial support
S
.

We will see in
Section

4
that the universal family
\mathrm{Spec}(B)\to\mathrm{Spec}(A)
of square polynomial systems with a fixed monomial support

is generically finite and that its generic root count is the mixed volume of the Newton polytopes of the
f_{i}
. Similar statements hold for the universal families of square systems with fixed polynomial supports, see
Section

5.2
.

3.
Fiberwise tropicalizations and generic flatness

In this section, we study fiberwise tropicalizations for morphisms
p\colon X\to{{Y}}
that factor through a closed embedding into a torus.
We introduce the notion of tropical flatness, as these fibers need not vary continuously.
For families that are tropically flat over a point, we show that the fiberwise tropicalizations are locally constant after a non-archimedean base change. We then show that closed embeddings
X\to{T_{Y}}
are generically tropically flat, see
Section

3.2
.

3.1.
Fiberwise tropicalizations and flatness

Let
T_{K,n}
be an
n
-dimensional torus over a non-archimedean field. Recall that there is a natural tropicalization map
T^{\mathrm{an}}_{K,n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
BPR16
]
and
[
Gub13
]
. For a scheme
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
of finite type over
K
, we similarly define a
relative
tropicalization map

\mathrm{trop}\colon T_{Y}^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

as follows.
For any
P\in{T_{Y}^{\mathrm{an}}}
with ring homomorphism
\psi\colon A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]\to{k(\pi(P))}
, we set

\mathrm{trop}(P)=(-\log|\psi(x_{1})|,...,-\log|\psi(x_{n})|).

We use this to define fiberwise tropicalizations of closed subschemes of
T_{Y}
.

Definition 3.1

Let
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
. We define the
fiberwise tropicalization
of
\phi\colon X\to{T_{Y}}
over
P
to be

\mathrm{trop}(X_{P})\coloneqq\mathrm{trop}\big(\phi^{\mathrm{an}}((p^{\mathrm{an}})^{-1}(P))\big).

Given a decomposition
\phi_{i}\colon X_{i}\to{T_{Y}}
and corresponding
p_{i}\colon X_{i}\to Y
, the
fiberwise tropical prevariety
over
P
is
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{trop}(X_{i,P})
.

Remark 3.2

If we represent a point
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
as a morphism
A\to{k(\pi(P))}
together with a valuation on
k(P)=k(\pi(P))
, then the fiberwise tropicalization of the torus over
P
can be identified with the tropicalization of the Berkovich spectrum of
A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]\otimes_{A}{k(P)}=k(P)[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
. The fiberwise tropicalization of a closed subscheme
X\to{T_{Y}}
is then the tropicalization of the corresponding ideal in
k(P)[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
. In particular, we find that the fiberwise tropicalization of a closed subscheme has the usual structure of a tropical variety in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

General tropical fibers can behave quite erratically, so we impose the following continuity conditions.

Definition 3.3

A
K
-rational
local tropical basis
for
X\to{T_{Y}}
around
P
is an open neighborhood
U
of
P
, a Zariski open
V\subset{Y}
with
\pi(U)\subset{V}
, and generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
of
I_{V}
satisfying:

(1)

(Non-degeneracy)
c_{i,\alpha}(Q)\neq{0}
for all
Q\in{U}
, where
f_{i}=\sum c_{i,\alpha}x^{\alpha}
and
c_{i,\alpha}\in{A_{V}}
.

(2)

(Tropical basis)
f_{1,Q},\dots,f_{k,Q}
form a tropical basis of
I_{Q}
for every
Q\in{U}
, i.e.,
\trop(X_{Q})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\trop(V(f_{i})_{Q})
.

A local tropical basis at
P
is a valued field extension
K\to{L}
, a point
P_{L}
lying over
P
, and an
L
-rational local tropical basis at
P_{L}
for
X_{L}\to{T_{Y,L}}
.

Lemma 3.4

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
be a
K
-rational local tropical basis at
P
and let
K\to{L}
be a valued field extension. Then
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
are an
L
-rational local tropical basis at any
P_{L}
mapping to
P
.

Proof.

The morphism
X_{L}^{\mathrm{an}}\to{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
is continuous, so the neighborhood
U
of
P
gives an open neighborhood
U_{L}
of
P_{L}
. The
f_{i}
then still form a tropical basis on
U_{L}
since tropicalizations are invariant under field extensions by
[
MS15
, Theorem 3.2.4]
. ∎

Definition 3.5

We say a closed embedding
X\to{T_{Y}}
is
tropically flat
over
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
if it admits a local tropical basis at
P
. The tropically flat locus is the set of all
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
such that
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat at
P
.

Example 3.6

If
X=V(f)
for some
f\in{A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]}
, then the fiberwise tropicalizations of
f=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
,
c_{\alpha}\in A
, near any rational
P
outside
\bigcup_{\alpha}V(c_{\alpha})
are locally constant, as we will see in
Lemma

3.9
. This in particular implies that
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat outside
\bigcup_{\alpha}V(c_{\alpha})
.

In general it is difficult to show that a given
X\to{T_{Y}}
is not tropically flat at a point
P
if
P
is outside the non-degenerate locus. We refer the reader to

Example

4.7
for an example of an embedding that is not tropically flat over a non-degenerate
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
.

We will see in
Section

4
that if
X\to{T_{Y}}
is tropically flat at
P
, then many properties of the fiberwise tropicalization
\trop(X_{P})
become generic properties of
p\colon X\to{Y}
.
To prove this, we will need an important fact about tropically flat embeddings, namely that they are locally constant after a non-archimedean base change.

Lemma 3.7

Let
g\in{A}
and
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
with
r=|g(P)|\neq{0}
.
There is a non-empty open neighborhood
U
of
P_{L}
in
Y^{\mathrm{an}}_{L}
and an element
c\in{L}
with
|c|=r
such that

|g(Q)-c(Q)|<r

for all
Q
in
U
.

Proof.

Take any element
c\in{L}
such that
g(P)=c
and consider the open ball
U_{L}=\mathbb{B}(g-c,r)=\{Q\in{Y_{L}^{\mathrm{an}}}:|(g-c)(Q)|<r\}
. For any
P_{L}\in{Y_{L}^{\mathrm{an}}}
mapping to
P
, we then have
P_{L}\in{U_{L}}
since
g(P_{L})=c
.
The inequality in the lemma is then satisfied by definition. Note that such a point
P_{L}
exists by
Remark

2.1
.
∎

Remark 3.8

By applying the non-archimedean triangle inequality to
g
as in
Lemma

3.7
, we find that
g
is locally constant after a base change to
L
. Without the base change, this is false: tropicalizations will generally not be locally constant. Take for instance a complete non-trivially valued algebraically closed field
K
and the affine line
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(K[x])
over
K
. The function
f=x
is then not locally constant on
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
. Indeed,
-\mathrm{log}|x|
is linear with absolute slope
1
on the line segment emanating from
x=0
. It will however be locally constant after a base change.

Lemma 3.9

Let
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
be a rational point and let
f=\sum c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
be a non-constant polynomial in
A[x_{1}^{\pm},...,x_{n}^{\pm}]
. If
c_{\alpha}(P)\neq{0}
for all
c_{\alpha}
, then the fiberwise tropicalizations of
V(f)
are constant on a non-empty open neighborhood of
P
.

Proof.

We apply
Lemma

3.7
to every
c_{\alpha}
to find a set of open neighborhoods
U_{\alpha}
of
P
. By the non-archimedean triangle inequality, we have that
|c_{\alpha}(Q)|
is constant on
U_{\alpha}
. We thus see that the tropicalizations of
f
are constant over the neighborhood
U=\bigcap{U_{\alpha}}
of
P
.
∎

3.2.
Generic tropical flatness theorem

In this section, we prove that a closed embedding
X\to{T_{Y}}
is generically tropically flat if
Y
is integral. The main idea is to use Gröbner bases over valued fields to show that a tropical basis over a generic valuation
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
gives a local tropical basis for an open subset of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
.
By an approximation argument, we then show that the union of these open subsets is dense in
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Assumption 3.10

Throughout this section, except in
Theorem

3.22
, we assume that
K
is algebraically closed, non-trivially valued, and complete.

We start with two important results that allow us to approximate the behavior of points in
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
using rational points.

Lemma 3.11

Let
K
be as in
3.10
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
denote the image of the absolute value
|\cdot|\colon K\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq{0}}
.
Let
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\in{A}
and
P\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
be such that
|c_{i}(P)|=r_{i}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
. Then there is a rational point
Q
of
Y
such that
|c_{i}(Q)|=r_{i}
. If
V
is a strictly
K
-affinoid subdomain of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
and
P\in{V}
, then the same holds for a
K
-rational point of
V
.

Proof.

We write
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}
for the value group of
K
, so that
\Gamma=\{-\mathrm{log}(r):r\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\}
.
We extend the
c_{i}
to a generating set of
A
. This gives a closed embedding

\phi\colon Y\to\mathbb{A}^{m}.

Let
v\in(\Gamma\cup\{\infty\})^{m}
be the tropicalization of
\phi^{\mathrm{an}}(P)
. This lies in the tropicalization of
\phi(Y)
, so by
[
Gub13
, Proposition 3.8]
we can find a
K
-rational point of
\phi(Y)
that tropicalizes to
v
. This also gives a
K
-rational point
Q
of
Y
and it has the desired properties by construction.

If
V
is a strictly
K
-affinoid subdomain of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
, then
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.15]
shows that the
K
-rational points are dense in
V
. The embedding above gives
V
as a closed subset of
\mathbb{A}^{m,\mathrm{an}}
. The arguments in
[
Gub13
, Proposition 3.8]
can now be repeated to obtain the desired statement.
∎

Corollary 3.12

Let
K
be as in
3.10
and let
P
,
c_{i}
,
r_{i}
,
Q
be as in
Lemma

3.11
. Suppose
r_{i}\neq{0}
. Then there is a non-empty open neighborhood
U
of
Q
with
|c_{i}(Q^{\prime})|=r_{i}
for all
Q^{\prime}\in{U}
. The same holds for a strictly
K
-affinoid subdomain of
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Proof.

This follows from
Lemma

3.7
.
∎

Definition 3.13

A
(generic) valuation
on
Y
is a point
P\in Y^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
\pi(P)
is the generic point of
Y
.

For points
Q\in{Y^{\mathrm{an}}}
close to a valuation
P
in the sense of
Corollary

3.12
, we will show that the fiberwise tropicalizations
\mathrm{trop}(V(I)_{P})
and
\mathrm{trop}(V(I)_{Q})
coincide.
In particular, this implies that a tropical basis of
\mathrm{trop}(V(I)_{P})
gives a local tropical basis around
Q
. We first recall several concepts from
[
MS15
, Section 2.4]
.

Definition 3.14

Let
Q\in Y^{\mathrm{an}}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A finite set
G_{Q}\subseteq I_{Q}
is called a
Gröbner basis
with respect to

, if
G_{Q}
generates
I_{Q}
and
\{\initial_{w}(g):g\in G_{Q}\}
generates
\initial_{w}(I_{Q})
.
We define the
Gröbner polyhedron
of
I_{Q}
around

to be
C_{w}(I_{Q})=\mathrm{cl}(\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\initial_{w^{\prime}}(I_{Q})=\initial_{w}(I_{Q})\})
, where
\mathrm{cl}(\cdot)
denotes the closure with respect to the Euclidean topology. We denote the
Gröbner complex
of
I_{Q}
by
\Sigma(I_{Q})=\{C_{w}(I_{Q}):w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
. If
I_{Q}
is homogeneous, then
\Sigma(I_{Q})
is a finite polyhedral complex and
\mathrm{trop}(V(I_{Q}))
is the support of a subcomplex of
\Sigma(I_{Q})
.

Lemma 3.15

Let
K
be as in
3.10
and let
I
be a homogeneous ideal. Let
P\in Y^{\mathrm{an}}
be a valuation, and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
a weight vector. Then there is a Zariski-open

, a
\mathcal{G}\subseteq A_{V}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, a rational point
Q\in V^{\mathrm{an}}
, and an open neighborhood
U\subset{V^{\mathrm{an}}}
of
Q
such that

(1)

\mathcal{G}_{P}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{P}
with respect to
w
,

(2)

\mathcal{G}_{Q^{\prime}}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{Q^{\prime}}
with respect to
w
for all

,

(3)

the monomial supports of
\mathcal{G}_{Q^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{P}
are equal for all
Q^{\prime}\in U
,

(4)

the valuations of the coefficients of
G_{Q^{\prime}}
and

coincide for all
Q^{\prime}\in U
.

Proof.

Fix a monomial ordering

and let

be a Gröbner basis of

with respect to

as computed by
[
CM19

where

[

. The polynomials

∎

Lemma 3.16

Let
K
be as in
3.10
and let

be a valuation. Then there is an open neighborhood

We will show that

Note that, by the proof of

For the upcoming proofs, we will assume that

Lemma 3.17

Let
K
be as in
3.10
. Let

-affinoid neighborhood

Since tropicalizations commute with homogenizations, we may assume that

of

with respect to

\mathcal{G}_{P}

\mathcal{G}\subset{A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}
.
Consider a strictly
K
-affinoid neighborhood

of

with canonical reduction

as in the statement of the lemma.
By assumption, the intersection of the initial degeneration at

over

as follows. By scaling the generators of

of

at

is the special fiber of

, see
[
Gub13
, Definition 5.1]
. Consider the coefficients

of the

at

are non-empty.
We consider a basic open

4.
Tropical intersections and generic root counts

We now use the material from the previous two sections to show how generic properties of morphisms of schemes can be detected using tropical geometry. We will see that many properties of a single tropical fiber over a tropically flat point propagate to an open dense subset of the parameter space. In
Section

4.1
, we prove
Theorem

4.5
, which expresses the generic root count as a tropical intersection product. This also gives a standalone proof of Bernstein’s theorem, see
Corollary

4.11
. In
Section

4.2
we study torus-equivariant and algebraically independent systems, and we prove
Proposition

4.19
. Finally, we discuss extensions of the results given here to analytic families of polynomial equations.

4.1.
Generic root counts as tropical intersection numbers

In this section, we show that generic root counts can be expressed in terms of tropical intersection numbers, provided that we have a tropically flat proper intersection. This extends Bernstein’s theorem to possibly overdetermined families of polynomial equations with non-trivial relations among the coefficients.
An important tool in proving this is the following lemma.

4.2.
Torus-equivariant systems and generic root counts

In this section, we introduce the notion of torus-equivariance, which gives a natural condition under which
Theorem

4.5
holds. It will be used in Sections
5
and
6
to obtain generic root counts for certain classes of systems.

The relative torus is a group scheme with group law

T_{Y}\times{T_{Y}}\to{T_{Y}}.

Let
L
be a field over
K
and let
\lambda\in{T_{Y}(L)}
be an
L
-valued point. We denote the image of
\lambda
in
Y(L)
by
P
. The fiber of
T_{Y}
over
P
gives a torus
T_{P}
defined over
L
and by multiplying by
\lambda
, we obtain an isomorphism
\lambda\colon T_{P}\to{T_{P}}
.
Let
\phi\colon X\to{T_{Y}}
be a closed immersion. The fiber of
\phi\colon X\to{T_{Y}}
over
P
gives a closed immersion
\phi_{P}\colon X_{P}\to{T_{P}}
. We denote the image of
X_{P}
under
\lambda\circ\phi_{P}
by
\lambda\cdot{X_{P}}
.
We use the same notation for points
\lambda\in{T^{\mathrm{an}}_{Y}}
. If we have an action

T_{Y}\times_{Y}Y\to{Y}
,
then we denote
L
-valued points of
T_{Y}\times_{Y}Y
by
(\lambda,P)
. The image of such a point under the group action is denoted by
\lambda\cdot P
.

Definition 4.13

We say that
\phi\colon X\to{T_{Y}}
is
torus-equivariant
if there exists an action
T_{Y}\times_{Y}{Y}\to{Y}
such that for every field
L
and
(\lambda,P)\in{(T_{Y}\times_{Y}Y)}(L)
, we have
X_{\lambda\cdot{}P}=\lambda\cdot{X_{P}}
. Here the tori over two
L
-valued points of
Y
are canonically identified.

Example 4.14

Consider the parameter ring
A=K[a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}]
with polynomial system defined by
f=a_{0}x+a_{1}xy+a_{2}y
. We define an action of
T_{Y}=\mathrm{Spec}(A[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}])
on
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
by the Hopf-algebra maps

\displaystyle a_{0}\mapsto{x^{-1}a_{0}},\qquad a_{1}\mapsto{x^{-1}y^{-1}a_{1}},\qquad a_{2}\mapsto{y^{-1}a_{2}}.

Here we used the identification
A[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}]\otimes_{A}A=A[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}]
. It is easy to see that
A\to{A[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}]/(f)}
is a torus-equivariant system with respect to this action. In a sense, if the monomials in a polynomial contain free variables, then these can be used to define a torus-equivariant system.

Remark 4.15

We will need the torus-equivariance to eliminate the non-Cohen-Macaulay loci in

4.19
. For systems that are automatically Cohen-Macaulay everywhere (e.g., square systems), we can make do with the weaker assumption of the tropicalizations being torus-equivariant, i.e., if for every

and every

mapping to

, there is a

Remark 4.22

5.1.
The degeneracy graph of a square system

Let
p\colon X\to{Y}
be a generically finite square polynomial system given by polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
in
A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. We start with a basic lemma on the quasi-finite locus
\text{QF}(p)\subset{Y}
for the morphism
p\colon X\to{Y}
.

Lemma 5.1

\text{QF}(p)
is open.

Proof.

Since
p:X\to Y
is square, it is flat at every point
x
lying over
y\in{\text{QF}(p)}
by
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 00ST
]
and
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 00SW
]
. The inverse image of
\text{QF}(p)
in
X
is thus in the open flat locus
U
. Consider the restriction
p_{U}
of
p
to
U
, which is a flat morphism. By
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 02NM
]
, the locus of relative dimension zero of

is open in
U
.
The image of this locus under
p_{U}
is exactly
\text{QF}(p)
. Since flat morphisms are open, we conclude that
\text{QF}(p)
is open.
∎

For every

with prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{y}
, we have that the induced system

\mathrm{Spec}(B/\mathfrak{p}_{y}B)\to\mathrm{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{y})

is generically finite. We denote the generic root count of this system by
\ell_{X/Y,y}
. If
y
is the generic point
\eta
of
Y
, this gives the generic root count of

. We now show that root counts decrease under specialization, so that
\ell_{X/Y,y}\leq\ell_{X/Y}
for every

.

Lemma 5.2

Let
y\in{\text{QF}(p)}
with prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{y}
and induced system
p_{y}\colon\mathrm{Spec}(B/\mathfrak{p}_{y}B)\to\mathrm{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{y})
. Then
\ell_{X/Y,y}\leq{\ell_{X/Y}}
.

Proof.

Let
D(h)
be an open dense subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
over which the root count is
\ell_{X/Y}
. By
[
Gub13
, Proposition 4.14]
, the reduction map
\pi\colon D(h)^{\mathrm{an}}\cap{Y^{\mathrm{an},0}}\to{Y}
is surjective. We take a point
Q
of
Y
over which the root count is
\ell_{X/Y,y}
. By the above, we can then find a valuation ring
R
with residue field
k
and a point
P\in{Y(R)}
such that

lies in

and

. Here

is the composition of
P\colon\mathrm{Spec}(R)\to{Y}
and
\mathrm{Spec}(K)\to\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, and

is the composition of
P
and
\mathrm{Spec}(k)\to\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. We can assume that
R
is Henselian.

We consider the base change
R\to{B\otimes_{A}R=:M}
. This is quasi-finite by construction. It is moreover flat by the considerations in
Lemma

5.1
.
Since
R
is Henselian, we can write

M=M_{\mathrm{fin}}\times{M_{\mathrm{nfin}}},

where
M_{\mathrm{fin}}
is finite and
M_{\mathrm{nfin}}\otimes_{R}{k}=(0)
. Moreover,
M_{\mathrm{fin}}
is flat over

, so it is free. We have
\ell_{X/Y}=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\eta})=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\mathrm{fin},\eta})+\mathrm{rank}(M_{\mathrm{nfin},\eta})
and
\mathrm{rank}(M_{s})=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\mathrm{fin},s})
. But
\ell_{X/Y,y}=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\mathrm{fin},s})=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\mathrm{fin},\eta})

∎

Remark 5.3

In general, if a system does not admit a square decomposition, then root counts need not decrease under specialization. We give two examples of this phenomenon. Consider the scheme
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y^{2}-x^{3}-x^{2}))
with normalization
X\to{Y}
. Its generic root count is one, however its root count over the maximal ideal
(x,y)
is two. This can be easily seen using the presentation
X=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]/(y^{2}-x^{3}-x^{2},xz-y,z^{2}-x-1))

For our second example, consider the scheme
X_{t}=V(f_{1},\dots,f_{6})
over

given by

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=(x-t)(z-t),

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=yz,

\displaystyle f_{5}

\displaystyle=x-z,

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=(x-t)w,

\displaystyle f_{4}

\displaystyle=yw,

\displaystyle f_{6}

\displaystyle=y-w.

The generic root count of

is

, but the root count of the fiber

over

is

. We thus see that generic root counts can increase under specialization. Note that in both cases, we necessarily have that the families are not flat, since otherwise the proof in
Lemma

5.2
would have gone through verbatim.

By
Lemma

5.2
and
[
Stacks22
,
Lemma 07RY
]
, there are reduced closed subschemes

\emptyset=Z_{-1}\subset{}Z_{0}\subset{Z_{1}}\subset{...}\subset{Z_{\ell_{X/Y}}}=\text{QF}(p)

such that the locus of rank

is
Z_{i}\backslash{Z_{i-1}}
.
Every

has finitely many generic points which we denote by

. These in turn correspond to minimal prime ideals

. By abuse of notation, we write

for points

in

is a specialization of

.

Definition 5.4

The

of

and edges

Remark 5.5

Consider the universal family

of square polynomial systems with fixed monomial supports

5.2.

Let

Assumption 5.10

Note that without passing to

or

We would now like to extend the open subset from

Suppose that

It now follows that there is a largest open subset

6.
Applications to systems with linear dependencies

In this section, we explore square systems with linear dependencies between the coefficients of their polynomials. We will focus on two special types of dependencies which we call vertical and horizontal dependencies. The first is inspired by the steady-state equations of chemical reaction networks
[
Dic16
]
, and the second is inspired by equations with fixed polynomial supports
[
KK12
]
. For each of these, either
\widehat{X}_{\text{lin}}
or
\widehat{X}_{\text{nlin}}
is a tropical complete intersection, so that the tropical intersection product from
Section

5.2
simplifies.

Assumption 6.1

Throughout this section, the parameter space
Y
will be the
m
-dimensional affine space
\mathbb{A}^{m}=\Spec(K[a_{1},\dots,a_{m}])
and the coefficients of the polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in K[a_{1},\dots,a_{m}][x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
will be linear and homogeneous in
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
.

6.1.
Square systems with vertical parameter dependencies

In this section, we consider a class of parametrized polynomial systems inspired by the steady-state equations of chemical reaction networks
[
Dic16
]
.

Definition 6.2

Let
x^{\alpha_{1}},\dots,x^{\alpha_{m}}
be the monomials of
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
.
We say
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
have

, if there is a decomposition
A=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}
of the parameter ring

, such that each

is of the form

In other words, the Macaulay matrix
(p_{i,j})\in A^{n\times m}
of
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
has algebraic dependencies along its columns, but not rows.

Definition 6.3

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
have vertical parameter dependencies. Choosing

in
Definition

5.6
we obtain:

\displaystyle\widehat{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A[x_{i}^{\pm},w_{j}^{\pm}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m],

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{i,j}w_{j}\qquad\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq w_{j}-x^{\alpha_{j}}\qquad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m.

By
Lemma

5.9
, the resulting
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}=V(\langle\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{n}\rangle)
is torus-equivariant. Note moreover that
\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
is a fixed linear subspace independent of
P
. We will refer to the resulting
\widehat{X}\rightarrow Y
as the
modification
for vertical dependencies.

For vertical dependencies, one exceptionally nice property of

is that it is a tropical complete intersection, i.e., it arises as a stable intersection

\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})=\trop(V(\hat{h}_{1})_{P})\cap_{{\mathrm{st}}}\dots\cap_{{\mathrm{st}}}\trop(V(\hat{h}_{m})_{P}).

This means that the intersection product with
\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
equals the intersection product with all the

:

Proposition 6.4

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
have vertical parameter dependencies. Then, for
P
as in

5.21
, the generic root count of

is

\mathrm{trop}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{lin}},P})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{trop}(V(\hat{h}_{j})_{P}).

Proof.

Indeed,
Corollary

5.19
shows that the maximal compatible subset is
T_{Y}
, so that
\ell_{X/Y}=\ell_{\widehat{X}/Y}
. ∎

Proposition

6.4
allows us to compute the generic root count via mixed volumes, eliminating the need to compute any set-theoretic intersections of tropical hypersurfaces:

Remark 6.5

Let
M
denote the matroid of
\mathrm{trop}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
in
Proposition

6.4
. If
M
is

, then its Bergman fan is a tropical complete intersection, so that

for linear polynomials
\hat{\ell}_{i}
. Consequently, the generic root count coincides with the mixed volume
\MV(\hat{\ell}_{1},\dots,\hat{\ell}_{n},\hat{h}_{1},\dots,\hat{h}_{m})
. If

is not cotransversal, then it can be expressed as a signed linear combination of cotransversal matroids, see
[
Ham17
]
.
Consequently, the generic root count coincides with a signed linear combination of mixed volumes.

We conclude this section with an example that showcases how our techniques can be applied to steady-state equations of reaction networks.

Example 6.6

Consider the following reaction network:

\small X_{i-1}+E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\xrightleftharpoons{\hskip 19.91692pt}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle a_{1,i}$}\vss}}}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-7.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle d_{1,i}$}\vss}}}Y_{1,i}\mathrel{\mathop{\xrightarrow{\hskip 19.91692pt}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle k_{1,i}$}\vss}}}X_{i}+E\hskip 14.22636ptX_{i}+F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\xrightleftharpoons{\hskip 19.91692pt}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle a_{2,i}$}\vss}}}}\limits_{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-7.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle d_{2,i}$}\vss}}}Y_{2,i}\mathrel{\mathop{\xrightarrow{\hskip 19.91692pt}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle k_{2,i}$}\vss}}}X_{i-1}+F\hskip 14.22636pti=1,...,n.

It describes the standard model of distributive sequential phosphorylation on
n
sites, see for example
[
FRW20
, Section 2]
. Under the assumption of mass-action kinematics, the corresponding evolution equations are given by

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}\coloneqq-a_{1,i+1}x_{i}x_{E}-a_{2,i}x_{i}x_{F}+d_{1,i+1}y_{1,i+1}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+d_{2,i}y_{2,i}+k_{1,i}y_{1,i}+k_{2,i+1}y_{2,i+1},

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle=0,\dots,n,

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{1,i}

\displaystyle=f_{1,i}\coloneqq a_{1,i}x_{i-1}x_{E}-(d_{1,i}+k_{1,i})y_{1,i},

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{2,i}

\displaystyle=f_{2,i}\coloneqq a_{2,i}x_{i}x_{F}-(d_{2,i}+k_{2,i})y_{2,i},

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{E}

\displaystyle=f_{E}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}-a_{1,i}x_{i-1}x_{E}+(d_{1,i}+k_{1,i})y_{1,i},

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{F}

\displaystyle=f_{F}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}-a_{2,i}x_{i}x_{F}+(d_{2,i}+k_{2,i})y_{2,i},

where
a_{2,0}=d_{2,0}=k_{1,0}=0
and
a_{1,n+1}=d_{1,n+1}=k_{2,n+1}=0
. The solutions of the evolution equations describe a three-dimensional set of steady states. In order to obtain finitely many solutions, observe that the following quantities are conserved:

\displaystyle E_{tot}

\displaystyle=g_{E}\coloneqq x_{E}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{1,i}-c_{E},

\displaystyle F_{tot}

\displaystyle=g_{F}\coloneqq x_{F}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{2,i}-c_{F},

\displaystyle X_{tot}

\displaystyle=g_{X}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{1,i}+y_{2,i})-c_{F}

To make the system square, we can omit a suitable subset of the evolution equations depending on the conservation equations. In the system above, we may omit
f_{0},f_{E},f_{F}
and consider the system consisting of
f_{i},f_{1,i},f_{2,i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
g_{E},g_{F},g_{X}
.

If the conservation equations
g_{E}
,
g_{F}
and
g_{X}
were generic, in the sense that each of its monomials come with a unique parameter as in the evolution equations, then the modifications in

6.3
would be immediately applicable, so that the generic root count is given by the tropical intersection number in
Proposition

6.4
.

To address non-generic conservation equations, a slight adaptation of the modification and decomposition in

6.3
suffices: Let
\hat{f}_{i},\hat{f}_{1,i},\hat{f}_{2,i}
and
\hat{h}_{j}
be defined as in

6.3
. This means that the
\hat{f}_{i},\hat{f}_{1,i},\hat{f}_{2,i}
are linear and that there is an
\hat{h}_{j}
per vertex in the reaction network. Set

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq V(\hat{f}_{i},\hat{f}_{1,i},\hat{f}_{2,i}\mid i=1,\dots,n),

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\text{con}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq V(g_{E},g_{F},g_{X}),

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq V(\hat{h}_{j}\mid j=1,\dots,m).

One can show that

and

intersect transversally, and that

is equivariant and of complementary dimension. Hence the conditions in

are satisfied, so that

\ell_{X/Y}=\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin},P})\cdot\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin},P})\cdot\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{con},P})

for

in an open dense subset of

. We thus see that the generic root count remains expressible as a tropical intersection product between a tropical linear space

and a classical linear space

.

6.2.
Square systems with horizontal parameter dependencies

In this section, we consider a class of parametrized square systems inspired by the work of Kaveh and Khovanskii
[
KK12
]
.

Definition 6.7

Let
x^{\alpha_{1}},\dots,x^{\alpha_{s}}
be the monomials of
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
.
We say
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
have

, if there is a decomposition of the parameter ring

, such that each

is of the form

f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}p_{i,j}\cdot x^{\alpha_{j}}\qquad\text{with }p_{i,j}\in A_{i}.

In other words, the Macaulay matrix
(p_{i,j})\in A^{n\times s}
of

has algebraic dependencies along its rows, but not columns.
Note that we can also write
f_{i}
as

f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}\cdot q_{j}\qquad\text{with }a_{i,j}\in A_{i}.

where the non-zero

are algebraically independent.

Definition 6.8

Let

have horizontal parameter dependencies. Choosing the

in
Definition

5.6
to be the
q_{i,j}
of
Definition

, we obtain:

\displaystyle\widehat{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A[x_{i}^{\pm},w_{j}^{\pm}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m],

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}w_{j}\qquad\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq w_{j}-q_{j}\qquad\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m.

As before by
Lemma

5.9
, the resulting
\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}=V(\langle\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{n}\rangle)
is torus-equivariant and
\trop(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})
is independent of
P
.
We will refer to the resulting
\widehat{X}\rightarrow Y
as the
modification
for horizontal dependencies.

Recall that, for vertical dependencies,
\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}})
can be a general tropical linear space while
\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin}})
is a tropical complete intersection. In a reversal of roles, for horizontal dependencies,
\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{lin}})
is a tropical complete intersection while
\trop(\widehat{X}_{\mathrm{nlin}})
can be a general tropical variety:

Proposition 6.9

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
have horizontal parameter dependencies and let

be the maximal compatible subset. Then, for

as in
Lemma

5.21
, the generic root count of
X_{U}\rightarrow Y
is

\mathrm{trop}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})\cdot\prod\mathrm{trop}(V(\widehat{f}_{i})_{P}).

Remark 6.10

(Comparison to the works of Kaveh and Khovanskii)

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
n
-dimensional irreducible variety over the complex numbers, and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}(\mathcal{X})
be linear subspaces of the function field.
In
[
KK10
]
, Kaveh and Khovanskii define the
birational intersection index

[L_{1},...,L_{n}]
, which records the generic number of solutions to the system given by
h_{1},...,h_{n}
, where
h_{i}
are generic elements of
L_{i}
, outside a suitable support of the
L_{i}
.
In
[
KK12
]
, a suitable higher-rank valuation
\mathbb{C}(X)\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is used to attach a convex body
\Delta_{L}
, the
Newton-Okounkov body
, to any subspace
L
. In
[
KK12
, Theorem 4.9]
it is then proved that

[L,...,L]=\dfrac{n!\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\Phi_{L})}{\mathrm{ind}(A_{L})}\mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{L}),

where
\Phi_{L}
is the Kodaira map and
\mathrm{ind}(A_{L})
is the index of a certain subgroup, see
[
KK10
,
KK12
]
for more details.
If the Kodaira map is a birational isomorphism, then both
\mathrm{deg}(\Phi_{L})
and
\mathrm{ind}(A_{L})
are equal to
1
, so that the formula reduces to
[L,...,L]=n!\mathrm{vol}(\Delta_{L})
.

In comparison, our paper always assumes that the ambient variety
\mathcal{X}
is a torus, though it may be over any field
K
. Let
q_{i,1},\dots,q_{i,k_{i}}
be a basis of
L_{i}
and consider

f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}}a_{i,j}q_{i,j}\qquad\text{for }i=1,\dots,n.

These give a square system
X\to{Y}
with horizontal parameter dependencies.
The open subset used in
[
KK12
, Definition 4.5 (1)]
is a subset of the maximal compatible subset. Namely, if we write
Z_{i}=\bigcap_{j:p_{i,j}\neq{0}}V(q_{j})
, then
Z_{\mathbf{L}}
from
[
KK12
, Section 4.2]
is
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}
. Its complement is then easily seen to be a union of open subsets
U
as in
Corollary

5.18
.

In
[
KK12
, Definition 4.5 (2)]
, it is furthermore required that the solutions are non-degenerate in the sense that the generic fiber of the morphism
X\to{Y}
is étale. This will generally not be the case if the characteristic of the base field
K
is positive.
For example, the parametrized polynomial
f=a_{1}+a_{2}x^{p}
over the ring
A=\mathbb{F}_{p}[a_{1},a_{2}]
has generic root count
p
, but the number of generic solutions where the morphism is étale is zero. If the characteristic of the base field
K
is zero, then the proof of
[
KK10
, Proposition 5.7]
implies that
X_{U}\to{Y}
is generically étale for the maximal compatible subset
U
, from which we obtain

[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]=\deg_{M}(\widehat{X}_{{\mathrm{nlin}},P})=\ell_{X_{U}/Y}

using
Corollary

5.23
and our earlier observation on the open subset used in the definition of
[L_{1},...,L_{n}]
.

We determine the polyhedral complex

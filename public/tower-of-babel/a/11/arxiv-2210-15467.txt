An alternative proof for the irreducibility of the p p -th cyclotomic polynomial
Tom Moshaiov

Abstract




Let
p
be a prime number. As a standard application of the irreducibility
criterion of Eisenstein, it is well known that the
p
-th cyclotomic
polynomial
\Phi_{p}(t)=1+t+\dots+t^{p-1}
is the minimal polynomial
of
e^{2\pi i/p}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. This note provides an alternative proof,
utilizing determinants to prove a lemma due to Kronecker.

Claim.
Let
p
be a prime number,
a_{0},\dots,a_{p-1}

be indeterminates, and
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{p-1}\\
a_{p-1}&a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{1}&\cdots&a_{p-1}&a_{0}\end{array}\right)
be their circulant matrix. Then
\det A\equiv a_{0}^{p}+a_{1}^{p}+\dots+a_{p-1}^{p}\mod p
.

Lemma (Kronecker).
Let
p
be a prime number. If

{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}}a_{j}e^{2\pi ij/p}=0
with
a_{0},\dots,a_{p-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
then
p\mid{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}}a_{j}
. Equivalently,
if
f(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
vanishes at some primitive
p
-th root of unity,
then
p\mid f(1)
.

Theorem (Gauss).
Let
p
be a prime number. Then
\Phi_{p}(t)=1+t+\dots+t^{p-1}

is the minimal polynomial of
e^{2\pi i/p}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Equivalently,
if
{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}}a_{j}e^{2\pi ij/p}=0

with
a_{0},\dots,a_{p-1}\in\mathbb{Q}
then
a_{0}=a_{1}\dots=a_{p-1}
.

Proof of claim.
Given a permutation
\sigma=(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{p-1})

of
\mathbb{Z}/p
, we let
T.\sigma=(\sigma_{p-1}+1,\sigma_{0}+1,\sigma_{1}+1,\dots)
,
(additions mod
p
). Geometrically,
T
translates the matrix rook
arrangement
\{(i,\sigma_{i})\}_{i=0}^{p-1}
a step down and to the
right, in cyclic fashion. We observe that
T^{p}=\text{id}
, and that the
fixed points of
T
are the cyclic permutations
(i,i+1,\dots)
,
which produce the products
a_{i}^{p}
. The remaining permutations
are partitioned into
p
-cycles
\sigma,T\sigma,\dots,T^{p-1}\sigma

all of which produce the same matrix product. We may assume
p
is
odd, so that
(i,i+1,\dots)
is even. It remains to show that
\text{sgn}(\sigma)=\text{sgn}(T.\sigma)
.
And indeed, by cyclically rotating
\sigma
, we may assume that
\sigma_{p-1}=p-1
.
Then
\text{sgn}(\sigma)
and
\text{sgn}(T.\sigma)
both count the parity of
the number of order inversions in
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{p-2}
.

Proof of Lemma.
Write
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}
. The identity
implies
\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{p-1}\\
a_{p-1}&a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{1}&\cdots&a_{p-1}&a_{0}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\zeta\\
\vdots\\
\zeta^{p-1}\end{array}\right)=0
.

The above claim and Fermat’s little theorem yield the desired

{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}}a_{j}\equiv{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}}a_{j}^{p}\equiv\det(a_{j-i})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/p}=0\mod p
.

Proof of theorem.
The complex roots of
\Phi_{p}(t)
are
precisely the primitive
p
-th roots of unity. We cannot have

\Phi_{p}(t)=f(t)g(t)
for some non-constant
f(t),g(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
,
since
p^{2}\mid f(1)g(1)=\Phi_{p}(1)=p
would be implied by the
above lemma. Thus
\Phi_{p}(t)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}[t]
and
by Gauss’s lemma over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
as well. It follows
that
\Phi_{p}(t)
is the minimal polynomial of
e^{2\pi i/p}
over

\mathbb{Q}
, as we wanted to show.

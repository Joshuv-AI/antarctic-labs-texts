Rigidity of Euler Products
Shin-ya Koyama & Nobushige Kurokawa

Abstract
We report a simple rigidity theorem for certain Euler products.



Key Words: Euler products; Zeta functions

AMS Subject Classifications: 11M06, 11M41

Introduction

For purely imaginary numbers
a,

b
,
c\in i{\mathbb{R}}
we study the meromorphy of the associated Euler product

Z^{abc}(s)=\prod_{p:\text{ prime }}(1-(p^{a}+p^{b})p^{-s}+p^{c-2s})^{-1}

in the family of Euler products

\mathfrak{Z}^{ab}=\{Z^{abc}(s)\ |\ c\in i{\mathbb{R}}\}.

This family
\mathfrak{Z}^{ab}
contains

\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b)=Z^{ab(a+b)}(s),

which is a meromorphic function in all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}

(with a functional equation under
s\longleftrightarrow 1+a+b-s
).
We prove the converse:

Theorem A
.

If
Z^{abc}(s)
is meromorphic in all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we have
a+b=c
and

Z^{abc}(s)=\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b).

This shows rigidity of the Euler product
\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b)
in the family

\mathfrak{Z}^{ab}
concerning the meromorphy on the entire
{\mathbb{C}}
.

The next result gives a detailed meromorphy for
a+b\neq c
.

Theorem B
.

If
a+b\neq c
, then
Z^{abc}(s)
has an analytic continuation to
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
as a meromorphic function with the natural boundary

\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
.
More precisely, each point on
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
is a limit point of poles of

Z^{abc}(s)
in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
.

We notice generalizations in §4 in the text.
Our theorems follow from results of Kurokawa
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
extending results of Estermann
[
1
]
.

We remark that our result characterizes
\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b)
by the meromorphy in all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}

only in contrast to usual “converse theorems”
originated by Hamburger
[
2
]
and Hecke
[
3
]
where the functional equation and the attached automorphic
form are important;
\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b)
corresponds to a Maass wave form studied by Maass
[
7
]
.

1
Euler datum

We use the triple
E=(P,\ {\mathbb{R}},\ \alpha)
, where
P
is the set of all prime numbers,

{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the real numbers, and
\alpha
is the map

\alpha:\ P\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by
\alpha(p)=\log p
.
Such a triple is a simple example of Euler datum studied in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
;
generalized Euler data are treated there with general “primes”
P

and general topological groups
G
instead of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
R({\mathbb{R}})
be the virtual character ring of
{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

R({\mathbb{R}})=\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in i{\mathbb{R}}}m(a)\chi_{a}\ \right|\ m(a)\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ m(a)=0\text{ except for finitely many }a\right\},

where
\chi_{a}
is a (continuous) unitary character

\chi_{a}:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to U(1)
given by
\chi_{a}(x)=e^{ax}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

For a polynomial

H(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}h_{m}T^{m}\in 1+TR({\mathbb{R}})[T]

we denote by
L(s,E,H)
the Euler product

L(s,E,H)=\prod_{p\in P}H_{\alpha(p)}(p^{-s})^{-1},

where

H_{x}(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}h_{m}(x)T^{m}\in 1+T{\mathbb{C}}[T].

For example, let
a
,
b
,
c\in i{\mathbb{R}}
, then the polynomial

H^{abc}(T)=1-(\chi_{a}+\chi_{b})T+\chi_{c}T^{2}

in
1+TR({\mathbb{R}})[T]
gives the Euler product

L(s,E,H^{abc})=\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-(p^{a}+p^{b})p^{-s}+p^{c-2s}\right)^{-1}

since

\displaystyle H^{abc}_{\alpha(p)}

\displaystyle=1-(\chi_{a}(\log p)+\chi_{b}(\log p))p^{-s}+\chi_{c}(\log p)p^{-2s}

\displaystyle=1-(p^{a}+p^{b})p^{-s}+p^{c-2s}.

2
Unitariness and meromorphy

Let
E=(P,\ {\mathbb{R}},\ \alpha)
as in §1 and take a polynomial
H(T)
in
1+TR({\mathbb{R}})[T]
of degree
n
.
We say that
H(T)
is
unitary
when there exist functions
\theta_{j};\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

H_{x}(T)=(1-e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}T)\cdots(1-e^{i\theta_{n}(x)}T)

for all
x
.

The main theorem proved in
[
5
]
gives in this particular situation the following result.

Theorem 1
.

(1)

If
H(T)
is unitary, then
L(s,E,H)
is meromorphic in all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

If
H(T)
is not unitary, then
L(s,E,H)
is meromorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
with the natural boundary.
Moreover, each point on
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
is a limit point of poles of
L(s,E,H)
in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
.

This theorem was proved in
[
5
]
(p.45, §8, Theorem1) since our

E=(P,\ {\mathbb{R}},\ \alpha)
is nothing but
\overline{E_{0}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})}
there.

3
Proof of rigidity

After looking Theorem 1 recalled in §2 we see that Theorems A and B in Introduction
are both derived from the following result:

Theorem 2
.

Let
a
,
b
,
c\in i{\mathbb{R}}
and

H^{abc}(T)=1-(\chi_{a}+\chi_{b})T+\chi_{c}T^{2}\in 1+TR({\mathbb{R}})[T].

Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

a+b=c

(2)

H(T)
is unitary.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2):
From
a+b=c
we get

\displaystyle H^{abc}(T)

\displaystyle=1-(\chi_{a}+\chi_{b})T+\chi_{a}\chi_{b}T^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\chi_{a}T)(1-\chi_{b}T).

This gives

\displaystyle H_{x}^{abc}(T)

\displaystyle=(1-\chi_{a}(x)T)(1-\chi_{b}(x)T)

\displaystyle=(1-e^{ax}T)(1-e^{bx}T)

for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence
H^{abc}(T)
is unitary, by

|e^{ax}|=|e^{bx}|=1.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1):
Assume that
H^{abc}(T)
is unitary, and set

H_{x}^{abc}(T)=(1-e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}T)(1-e^{i\theta_{2}(x)}T)

with
\theta_{j}:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Then comparing with

H_{x}^{abc}(T)=1-(e^{ax}+e^{bx})T+e^{cx}T^{2}

we obtain

\displaystyle e^{ax}+e^{bx}

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}+e^{i\theta_{2}(x)},

(3.1)

\displaystyle e^{cx}

\displaystyle=e^{i(\theta_{1}(x)+\theta_{2}(x))}.

(3.2)

Note that the complex conjugation of (3.1) gives

e^{-ax}+e^{-bx}=e^{-i\theta_{1}(x)}+e^{-i\theta_{2}(x)}.

Since

e^{-ax}+e^{-bx}=e^{-(a+b)x}(e^{ax}+e^{bx})

and

e^{-i\theta_{1}(x)}+e^{-i\theta_{2}(x)}=e^{-i(\theta_{1}(x)+\theta_{2}(x))}(e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}+e^{i\theta_{2}(x)})

we obtain the equality

e^{-(a+b)x}(e^{ax}+e^{bx})=e^{-cx}(e^{ax}+e^{bx})

by using (3.1) and (3.2).

Hence we get

(e^{(a+b-c)x}-1)(e^{ax}+e^{bx})=0

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Especially

\frac{e^{(a+b-c)x}-1}{x}(e^{ax}+e^{bx})=0

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus letting
x\to 0
we obtain the desired equality

a+b-c=0.

∎

4
Generalizations

From the proof above it would be easy to see that we have generalizations of Theorems A and B
by using results of
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Hence we notice simple results only.

(1)
Dedekind case.

Let
\zeta_{F}(s)
be the Dedekind zeta function of a finite extension field
F
of the rational number field
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
a,

b
,
c\in i{\mathbb{R}}
and

Z_{F}^{abc}(s)=\prod_{P\in\mathrm{Specm}(O_{F})}(1-(N(P)^{a}+N(P)^{b})N(P)^{-s}+N(P)^{c-2s})^{-1},

where
P
runs over the set
\mathrm{Specm}(O_{F})
of maximal ideals of the integer ring
O_{F}
of
F
.
Then we have exactly the same Theorems A and B chracterizing

\zeta_{F}(s-a)\zeta_{F}(s-b)
among
Z_{F}^{abc}(s)
by using Theorem 1 of
[
5
, §8]

for
\overline{E_{0}(F/F)}
.

(2)
Selberg case

Let
\zeta_{M}(s)
be the Selberg (or Ruelle) zeta function

\zeta_{M}(s)=\prod_{P\in\mathrm{Prim}(M)}(1-N(P)^{-s})^{-1}

of a compact Riemann surface
M
of genus
g\geq 2
, where
\mathrm{Prim}(M)
denotes the prime geodesics on
M

with
N(P)=\exp(\mathrm{length}(P))
.
Let
a
,
b
,
c\in i{\mathbb{R}}
and

Z_{M}^{abc}(s)=\prod_{P\in\mathrm{Prim}(M)}(1-(N(P)^{a}+N(P)^{b})N(P)^{-s}+N(P)^{c-2s})^{-1}.

Then we have the same Theorems A and B characterizing

\zeta_{M}(s-a)\zeta_{M}(s-b)
among
Z_{M}^{abc}(s)
by using Theorem 9 of
[
6
, p.232]
.

(3)
More parameters

It is possible to generalize the situation with more parameters (or representations).
For example, let
a
,
b
,
c
,
d\in i{\mathbb{R}}
and

Z^{abcd}(s)=\prod_{p:\text{ prime }}(1-(p^{a}+p^{b}+p^{c})p^{-s}+(p^{a+b}+p^{b+c}+p^{c+a})p^{-2s}-p^{d-3s})^{-1}.

Then we have the following result by a similar proof:

Z^{abcd}(s)
is meromorphic in all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
iff
a+b+c=d
.
This result characterizes

\zeta(s-a)\zeta(s-b)\zeta(s-c)
among
Z^{abcd}(s)
.

Moreover, we have the following Theorem C generalizing Theorems A and B.
This characterizes
\zeta(s-a_{1})\cdots\zeta(s-a_{n})
for
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in i{\mathbb{R}}
with
n\geq 2
.

Theorem C
.

For
n\geq 2
and
a_{1},...,a_{n},b\in i{\mathbb{R}}
, let

Z(s)=\prod_{p:\ \text{prime}}\left((1-p^{a_{1}-s})\cdots(1-p^{a_{n}-s})+(-1)^{n}(p^{b}-p^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}})p^{-ns}\right)^{-1}.

Then
Z(s)
has an analytic continuation to all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
as a meromorphic function if and only if

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=b
that is
Z(s)=\zeta(s-a_{1})\cdots\zeta(s-a_{n})
.
When
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\neq b
, it holds that
Z(s)
is meromorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
with the natural boundary
\operatorname{Re}(s)=0
.

Proof.

The method is quite similar to the case of
n=2
treated in the proofs of Theorems A and B.

We define

H(T)\in 1+TR({\mathbb{R}})[T]

by

\displaystyle H(T)

\displaystyle=(1-\chi_{a_{1}}T)\cdots(1-\chi_{a_{n}}T)+(-1)^{n}(\chi_{b}-\chi_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}})T^{n}

\displaystyle=1-(\chi_{a_{1}}+\cdots+\chi_{a_{n}})T+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\chi_{b}T^{n}.

Then it is sufficient to show the equivalence of

(1)
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=b
,

and

(2)
H(T)
is unitary.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2):
If
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=b
, then
H(T)=(1-\chi_{a_{1}}T)\cdots(1-\chi_{a_{n}}T)
, which is unitary.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1):
Suppose that
H(T)
is unitary. Then, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle H_{x}(T)

\displaystyle=(1-e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}T)\cdots(1-e^{i\theta_{n}(x)}T)

\displaystyle=1-(e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}+\cdots+e^{i\theta_{n}(x)})T+\cdots+(-1)^{n}e^{i(\theta_{1}(x)+\cdots+\theta_{n}(x))}T^{n}

with
\theta_{j}:\ {\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. By comparing with

H_{x}(T)=1-(e^{a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{a_{n}x})T+\cdots+(-1)^{n}e^{bx}T^{n}

we obtain the following identities for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

\displaystyle e^{a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{a_{n}x}=e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}+\cdots+e^{i\theta_{n}(x)},

(4. 1)

\displaystyle e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1})x}+\cdots+e^{(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n})x}

(4.
n-1
)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=e^{i(\theta_{1}(x)+\cdots+\theta_{n-1}(x))}+\cdots+e^{i(\theta_{2}(x)+\cdots+\theta_{n}(x))},

\displaystyle e^{bx}=e^{i(\theta_{1}(x)+\cdots+\theta_{n}(x))},

(4.
n
)

where
(4.\,k)
indicates the coefficients of
T^{k}
in both sides for
k=1,\,n-1,\,n
.

Now, the complex conjugate of (4. 1) gives

\displaystyle e^{-a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{-a_{n}x}=e^{-i\theta_{1}(x)}+\cdots+e^{-i\theta_{n}(x)}.

(
\alpha
)

Dividing (4.
n-1
) by (4.
n
) we get

e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}-b)x}+\cdots+e^{(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}-b)x}=e^{-i\theta_{1}(x)}+\cdots+e^{-i\theta_{n}(x)}

that is

\displaystyle e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}-b)x}(e^{-a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{-a_{n}x})=e^{-i\theta_{1}(x)}+\cdots+e^{-i\theta_{n}(x)}.

(
\beta
)

Then
(\beta)-(\alpha)
implies

(e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}-b)x}-1)(e^{-a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{-a_{n}x})=0

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence we obtain

\frac{e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}-b)x}-1}{x}(e^{-a_{1}x}+\cdots+e^{-a_{n}x})=0

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}.
Thus, letting
x\to 0
we have
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=b
.
∎

Abstract.
The quaternion offset linear canonical transform (QOLCT) which is time-shifted and frequency-modulated version of the quaternion linear canonical
transform (QLCT) provides a
more general framework of most existing signal
processing tools. For the generalized QOLCT, the classical Heisenberg’s and Lieb’s uncertainty principles have been studied recently. In this paper, we first define the short-time quaternion offset linear canonical transform (ST-QOLCT) and drive its
relationship with the quaternion Fourier transform
(QFT). The crux of the paper lies in the generalization of several well known uncertainty principles for the ST-QOLCT, including Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle, Hardy’s uncertainty principle, Beurling’s uncertainty principle,
and
Logarithmic uncertainty principle.

Keywords: Quaternion Fourier transform ; Quaternion offset linear canonical transform; Short-time quaternion offset linear canonical transform(ST-QOLCT) ; Uncertainty principle.

1.
Introduction

The linear canonical transform (LCT) with four parameters
(a,b,c,d)
has been generalized to a six parameter transform
(a,b,c,d,u_{0},w_{0})
known as offset linear canonical transform (OLCT). Due to the time shifting
u_{0}
and frequency modulation parameters, the OLCT has gained more flexibility over classical LCT. Hence has found wide applications in image and signal processing. The quaternion offset linear canonical transform (QOLCT) which is time-shifted and frequency-modulated version of the quaternion linear canonical transform (QLCT) provides a more general framework of most existing signal processing tools. For more details we refer to
[
6
,
11
,
12
]
and references therein.

Because of its wide applications in signal analysis, image processing and optics the quaternion offset linear canonical transform(QOLCT) has attained much universality in recent years. However, the QOLCT is inadequate for localizing the QOLCT-frequency of non-transient signals, as such, it is indispensable to introduce an eccentric localized transform coined as the short-time quaternion offset linear canonical transform(ST-QOLCT), which can effectively reveal the local QOLCT-frequency content of such signals. The ST-QOLCT enjoys high resolution, provides local Information and eliminates cross terms. The chrip signals can be better analysed through ST-QOLCT. We refer to
[

,
13
,
16
]
for more details.

With the passage of time researchers further extended the uncertainty principle to different types of new uncertainty principles associated with the Fourier Transform, for instance Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, Logarithmic uncertainty principle,
Hardy’s uncertainty principle and Beurling’s uncertainty principle. Later these uncertainty principles were extended to Quaternion domain. In

Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic facts on the quaternion algebra and the QFT, which will be needed throughout the paper.

2.1.
Quaternion algebra

In 1834 W. R. Hamilton introduced quaternion algebra by extension of the complex number to an associative non-commutative 4D algebra. Denoted by
\mathbb{H}
in his honor where every element of
\mathbb{H}
has a Cartesian form given by

(2.1)

\mathbb{H}=\left\{q|q:=[q]_{0}+{i}[q]_{1}+{j}[q]_{2}+{k}[q]_{3},[q]_{i}\in\mathbb{R},i=0,1,2,3\right\}

where
{i},{j},{k}
are imaginary units obeying Hamilton’s multiplication rules:

(2.2)

{i}^{2}={j}^{2}={k}^{2}=-1,\\

(2.3)

{i}{j}=-{j}{i}={k},\,{j}{k}=-{k}{j}={i},\,{k}{i}=-{i}{k}={j}.

Let
[q]_{0}
and
q={i}[q]_{1}+{j}[q]_{2}+{k}[q]_{3}
denote the real scalar part and the vector part of quaternion number
q=[q]_{0}+\mathbf{i}[q]_{1}+{j}[q]_{2}+{k}[q]_{3}
, respectively. Then, from
[
2
]
, the
real scalar part has a cyclic multiplication symmetry

(2.4)

[pql]_{0}=[qlp]_{0}=[lpq]_{0},\qquad\forall q,p,l\in\mathbb{H},

the conjugate of a quaternion
q
is defined by
\overline{q}=[q]_{0}-{i}[q]_{1}-{j}[q]_{2}-{k}[q]_{3}
, and the norm of
q\in\mathbb{H}
defined as

(2.5)

\left|q\right|=\sqrt{q\bar{q}}=\sqrt{[q]_{0}^{2}+[q]_{1}^{2}+[q]_{2}^{2}+[q]_{3}^{2}}{.}

It is easy to verify that

(2.6)

\overline{pq}=\overline{q}\overline{p},\qquad|qp|=|q||p|,\quad\forall q,p\in\mathbb{H}.

In this paper, we will study the quaternion-valued signal
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to\mathbb{H}
,
f
which can be expressed as
f=f_{0}+if_{1}+jf_{2}+kf_{3},
with
f_{m}\penalty\ :{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to\ {\mathbb{R}}\ for\ m=0,1,2,3.

The quaternion inner product for quaternion valued signals
f,g\ :{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ \to{\mathbb{H}}
, as follows:

(2.7)

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{f\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\overline{g\left(\mathbf{x}\right)}}d\mathbf{x}

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2}),

f(\mathbf{x})=f(x_{1},x_{2}),

\mathbf{x}=dx_{1}dx_{2},
and so on.

Hence, the natural norm is given by

(2.8)

{\left|f\right|}_{2}=\sqrt{<f,f>}={(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{{\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|}^{2}}d\mathbf{x})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the quaternion module
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\ {\mathbb{H}})
, is given by

(2.9)

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\ {\mathbb{H}})=\{f:{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ \to{\mathbb{H}},\ {\left|f\right|}_{2}<\infty}\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
, then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds[25]

(2.10)

\left|\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\right|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}^{2}.

If and only if
f=ig
for some quaternionic parameter
i\in\mathbb{H}
, the equality holds.

2.2.
The two-sided QFT

The QFT belongs to the family of Clifford Fourier transformations.

It is a generalization of the classical Fourier transform (CFT)
[
3
]
.Some useful properties, and theorems of this transform are
generalizations of the corresponding properties and theorems of the classical Fourier transform with some
modifications.
There are three different types of QFT, the left-sided QFT , the right-sided QFT, and two-sided QFT
[
4
]
. In this paper our focus shall be on two-sided QFT. So from here on by QFT we mean two-sided quaternion Fourier transform.
Let us begin with definition of the two-sided QFT and provide some properties used in the sequel.

Definition 2.1
.

(Two-sided QFT.)

For
f\in L^{1}\left({\mathbb{R}^{2}},{\mathbb{H}}\right)
the two-sided QFT with respect to unit quaternions
i;j
is given by

(2.11)

{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}[f](\mathbf{w})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{e^{-i{{w}}_{1}x_{1}}}\ f(\mathbf{x})\ e^{-j{{w}}_{2}x_{2}}dt,\penalty\ \penalty\ where\penalty\ \mathbf{x,w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

We define the modulus of
\mathcal{F}[f]^{i,j}
as follows :

(2.12)

{\left|{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}[f]\right|}\ :=\sqrt{\sum^{m=3}_{m=0}{{\left|{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f_{m}\right]\right|}^{2}}}.

Furthermore, we define a new
L^{2}
-norm of
{\mathcal{F}[f]}
as follows :

(2.13)

{\left\|{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}[f]\right\|}_{2}:=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{{\left|{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f\right](y)\right|}^{2}dy}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Dilation property)

Let
k_{1},k_{2}
be a positive scalar constants, we have

(2.14)

{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f(x_{1},x_{2})\right]\left(\frac{w_{1}}{k_{1}},\frac{w_{2}}{k_{1}}\right)=k_{1}k_{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f(k_{1}x_{1},k_{2}x_{2})\right]\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right).

we can also write it as

(2.15)

{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f(\mathbf{x})\right]\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{k}}\right)=\mathbf{k}{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f(\mathbf{kx})\right]\left(\mathbf{w}\right).

Lemma 2.3
.

(QFT Plancherel)

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{H}})
, then

(2.16)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{{\left|{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}\left[f\right]\left(\mathbf{w}\right)\right|}^{2}}d\mathbf{w}=4{\pi}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{{\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|}^{2}}d\mathbf{x}.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Inverse QFT)

If
f\in L^{1}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{H}}\right),and\ {\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}[f]\in L^{1}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{H}}\right)
, then the two-sided QFT is an invertible transform and its inverse is given
by

(2.17)

f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{{(2\pi)}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{e^{iw_{1}x_{1}}}{\mathcal{F}}^{i,j}[f(\mathbf{x})](\mathbf{w})e^{jw_{2}x_{2}}d\mathbf{w}.

2.3.
Quaternion offset linear canonical transform (QOLCT)

The quaternion linear canonical transform(QLCT) is a generalization of the linear canonical transform(LCT) firstly defined by Kou, et al.
[
5
,
6
]
. Later in
[
Hitzer2
]
Hitzer.E generalized the definitions of Kou, etl to introduce two-sided QLCT. In this paper, we mainly focus on the two-sided QLCT.

Definition 2.2
.

(Quaternion Linear Canonical Transform.)

Let

A_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{s}&b_{s}\\
c_{s}&d_{s}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix parameter satisfying
{\rm det}(A_{s})=1
, for
s=1,2
. The two-sided QLCT of signal
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
is defined by

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}[f](\mathbf{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})f(\mathbf{x})K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rm{d}\mathbf{x},

where
\mathbf{w}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is regarded as the QLCT domain, and the kernel signals
K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})
,
K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})
are respectively given by

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1}):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi ib_{1}}}e^{{i}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{2b_{1}}x_{1}^{2}-\frac{x_{1}\omega_{1}}{b_{1}}+\frac{d_{1}}{2b_{1}}\omega_{1}^{2}\right)},&b_{1}\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d_{1}}e^{{i}\frac{c_{1}d_{1}}{2}\omega_{1}^{2}}\delta(x_{1}-d_{1}w_{1}),&b_{1}=0\end{cases}\end{split}

and

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2}):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi jb_{2}}}e^{{j}\left(\frac{a_{2}}{2b_{2}}x_{2}^{2}-\frac{x_{2}\omega_{2}}{b_{2}}+\frac{d_{2}}{2b_{2}}\omega_{2}^{2}\right)},&b_{2}\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d_{2}}e^{{j}\frac{c_{2}d_{2}}{2}\omega_{2}^{2}}\delta(x_{2}-d_{2}w_{2}),&b_{2}=0\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\delta(x)
representing the Dirac function.

Here we note that for
b_{s}=0,s=1,2
the QLCT of a signal boils down to chirp multiplication operations, and it is of no particular interest for our objective in this work. So without loss of generality, we set
b_{s}\neq 0
in rest of paper.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
, then the inversion of the QLCT of
f
is given by

(2.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\mathbf{x})&=\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{-1}[\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}[f]](\mathbf{x})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{-{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\left\{f\right\}(\mathbf{w})K_{A_{2}}^{-{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rm{d}\mathbf{w}.\end{split}

We now generalize the definitions of
[
8
,
9
]
as follows:

Definition 2.3
.

(QOLCT.)

Let
A_{s}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{s}&b_{s}&|&p_{s}\\
c_{s}&d_{s}&|&q_{s}\\
\end{array}\right]
, be a matrix parameter such that
a_{s}
,
b_{s}
,
c_{s}
,
d_{s}
,
p_{s}
,
q_{s}\in\mathbb{R},\quad b_{s}\neq 0
and
a_{s}d_{s}-b_{s}c_{s}=1,
for
s=1,2.
The two-sided quaternion offset linear canonical transform of any quaternion valued function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
, is given by

(2.22)

\mathcal{O}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{i,j}\big[f(\mathbf{x})\big](\mathbf{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{i}(x_{1},w_{1})\,f(\mathbf{x})\,K_{A_{2}}^{j}(x_{2},w_{2})d\mathbf{x}

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2}),\,w=(w_{1},w_{2})
and the kernel signals
K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})
and
K_{A_{2}}^{j}(x_{2},w_{2})
are respectively given by

(2.23)

K_{A_{1}}^{{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{1}{i}}}\,e^{\frac{{i}}{2b_{1}}\big[a_{1}x_{1}^{2}-2x_{1}(w_{1}-p_{1})-2w_{1}(d_{1}p_{1}-b_{1}q_{1})+d_{1}(w_{1}^{2}+p_{1}^{2})\big]},b_{1}\neq 0\,

(2.24)

K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},w_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{2}{j}}}\,e^{\frac{{j}}{2b_{2}}\big[a_{2}x_{2}^{2}-2x_{2}(w_{2}-p_{2})-2w_{2}(d_{2}p_{2}-b_{2}q_{2})+d_{2}(w_{2}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\big]},\,b_{2}\neq 0\,

Note:
The left-sided and right-sided QOLCT can be defined correspondingly by placing the two above kernels both on the left or on the right, respectively.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
then the inversion of two-sided QOLCT is given by

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{K_{A_{1}}^{-i}(x_{1},w_{1})}\,\mathcal{O}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{i,j}\big[f\big](\mathbf{w})\,{K_{A_{2}}^{-j}(x_{2},w_{2})}\,d\mathbf{w}.

Lemma 2.7
.

(Plancherel for QOLCT) Every two dimensional quaternion valued function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
and its two-sided QOLCT are related to the Plancherel identity in the following way:

(2.25)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{O}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{i,j}\big[f\big]\big\|_{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{2}.\,

3.
Short-time Quaternion Offset Linear Canonical Transform(ST-QOLCT)

In this section, we shall formally introduce the notion of the two-sided Short-time quaternion offset linear canonical transform (ST-QOLCT) then establish some properties of the proposed transform.

Definition 3.1
.

(ST-QOLCT.)
Let
A_{s}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{s}&b_{s}&|&p_{s}\\
c_{s}&d_{s}&|&q_{s}\\
\end{array}\right]
, be a matrix parameter such that
a_{s}
,
b_{s}
,
c_{s}
,
d_{s}
,
p_{s}
,
q_{s}\in\mathbb{R},\quad b_{s}\neq 0
and
a_{s}d_{s}-b_{s}c_{s}=1,
for
s=1,2.
The two-sided short-time quaternion offset linear canonical transform of any quaternion valued function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
, with respect window function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
is given by

(3.1)

\mathcal{S}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\phi,A_{1},A_{2}}\big[f\big](\mathbf{w,u})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{\bf{i}}(x_{1},w_{1})\,f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}\,K_{A_{2}}^{{j}}(x_{2},w_{2})dt

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2}),\,\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2}),\,\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
and the quaternion kernels
K_{A_{1}}^{i}(x_{1},w_{1})
and
K_{A_{2}}^{j}(x_{2},w_{2})
are given by equation 2.23 and 2.24 respectively.

Note:

3.1

\end{array}\right]
,

•

Quaternion short-time fractional Fourier transform when

\end{array}\right]
,

•

Quaternion short-time Fourier transform when
A_{s}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&|&0\\

\end{array}\right]

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, first we establish a relation between two-sided QFT and two-sided ST-QOLCT. Second, we established some basic properties of the two-sided ST-QOLCT including the Moyal’s formula which are proved in
[
1
]
. These
results are very important for their applications in digital signal and image processing. Finally, the uncertainty

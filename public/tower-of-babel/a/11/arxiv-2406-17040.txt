Lepton Flavor-Violating Higgs Decays Mediated by Ultralight Gauge Boson


Abstract
We present an analysis of the lepton-flavor violating decay of the Higgs boson mediated by an ultralight gauge boson, χ \chi . Our analysis matches a model generating the lepton flavor-violating interaction ℓ ¯ i ​ ℓ j ​ χ \bar{\ell}_{i}\ell_{j}\chi at tree level with an effective field theory, safeguarding a physical massless χ \chi -boson limit of the observables. By utilizing the upper bounds on H → ℓ i ​ ℓ ¯ j H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j} decays from CMS and ATLAS Collaborations, we establish an indirect upper limit on the nonstandard decay H → ℓ i ​ ℓ ¯ j ​ χ H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}\chi . The analysis encompasses various observables such as the lepton energy spectrum, Dalitz plot distribution, and Lepton Charge and Forward-Backward Asymmetries.

While extensive efforts are underway to measure the decay rates within SM-favored channels precisely, there is a corresponding need for commensurate attention to be directed toward those subdominant or absent channels within the SM. Especially intriguing are scenarios in which new physics phenomena may emerge, potentially surpassing the expectations set forth by the SM.

The SM assumption that left-handed neutrinos are massless implies conserving the lepton family number. However, neutrino oscillation experiments have unequivocally demonstrated that lepton flavor conservation is not a fundamental symmetry of nature

Even so, cLFV processes are heavily suppressed by the GIM mechanism, making them unobservable in current experiments. Nevertheless, the potential detection of cLFV would provide an enticing avenue for exploring physics Beyond the Standard Model (BSM).

The kinetic and the Yukawa Lagrangian are formulated as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm kin}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}i(\overline{L}_{j}\not{D}L_{j}+\overline{e}_{R_{j}}\not{D}e_{R_{j}})+\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}(D_{\mu}\phi_{jk})^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\phi_{jk})\;,

\displaystyle-{\cal L}_{\rm Yuk}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}y_{jk}\overline{L}_{j}\phi_{jk}e_{R_{k}}+{\rm h.c.}\,,

(1)

where
D_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative, given by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}+igW_{\mu}^{a}T_{a}+ig^{\prime}YB_{\mu}+ig_{\chi}q\chi_{\mu}~~{\rm for~the}~SU(2)_{L}~{\rm doublets}\;,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}+ig^{\prime}YB_{\mu}+ig_{\chi}q\chi_{\mu}~~{\rm for~the}~SU(2)_{L}~{\rm singlets}\;,

(2)

being
g
,
g^{\prime}
and
g_{\chi}
the coupling constants of
SU(2)_{L}
,
U(1)_{Y}
and
U(1)_{\chi}
, respectively.

The LFV vertex arises from recasting the kinetic Lagrangian in terms of the mass eigenstates

\displaystyle-{\cal L}\supset\overline{e_{R}}ig_{e\mu}^{RR}\gamma^{\rho}\chi_{\rho}\mu_{R}+\overline{e_{L}}ig_{e\mu}^{LL}\gamma^{\rho}\chi_{\rho}\mu_{L}+{\rm h.c.}\;,

(3)

with

\displaystyle g_{e\mu}^{RR}

\displaystyle=g_{\chi}(q_{e_{1}}-q_{e_{2}})\sin\theta_{R}\cos\theta_{R}\;,

\displaystyle g_{e\mu}^{LL}

\displaystyle=g_{\chi}(q_{L_{1}}-q_{L_{2}})\sin\theta_{L}\cos\theta_{L}\;,

(4)

where it can be deduced that the mixing angles
\theta_{L}\,,~\theta_{R}
and the masses of the particles can be written as a function of
y_{jk}
,
v_{jk}
and
q_{\phi_{jk}}
, see Appendix
A
. Evidently, when the
U(1)_{\chi}
charges are generation-independent, lepton flavor is exactly conserved within the model. Likewise, if the interaction eigenstates coincide with the mass eigenstates, tree-level flavor-changing interactions are absent (see Eq. (
22
)). This alignment of eigenstates can occur under specific conditions, such as particular configurations of the vacuum expectation values of the fields
\phi_{ij}
, for example, when
v_{ii}=0
but
v_{ij}\neq 0
. This mechanism holds not only for two generations but can also be extended to three generations
[
31
]
.

We can match this model with an Effective Field Theory (EFT), which offers the advantage of greater generality and fewer constraints. Within the framework of general EFT, the interaction responsible for lepton flavor violation can be described by the effective Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}\supset F_{1}\bar{e}\gamma_{\alpha}\chi^{\alpha}\mu+G_{1}\bar{e}\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\chi^{\alpha}\mu+{\rm h.c.}\,,

(5)

where compared to the Lagrangian in Eq. (
3
), we match the effective couplings,
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, to correspond with the parameters of the model

F_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{e\mu}^{RR}+g_{e\mu}^{LL}\right)\,,\quad G_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{e\mu}^{RR}-g_{e\mu}^{LL}\right)\,.

(6)

For simplicity, we assume for the moment that the Yukawa couplings satisfy
y_{22}>>y_{11}>>y_{12},y_{21}
and all
v_{jk}=v
, so
q_{\phi_{jk}}=Q
. The relevant parameters of the tree-level model after the spontaneous breaking of the gauge symmetries are:

\displaystyle m_{\mu}^{2}\simeq y_{22}^{2}v^{2}\,,\quad m_{e}^{2}\simeq y_{11}^{2}v^{2}\,\quad m_{\chi}^{2}\simeq 4g_{\chi}^{2}Q^{2}v^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\sin{2\theta_{L}}\simeq-2\frac{y_{12}}{y_{22}}\,,\quad\sin{2\theta_{R}}\simeq-2\frac{y_{21}}{y_{22}}\,.

(7)

In this work, we focus on the scenario where
\chi
is (ultra)light, specifically
m_{\chi}<m_{\mu}
(with
m_{\chi}\ll m_{\mu}
in the ultralight case). The effective parameters can be constrained to ensure the correct muon mass
m_{\mu}\sim 105
MeV by setting appropriate limits on the Yukawa couplings,
U(1)_{\chi}
-charges, and the gauge coupling
g_{\chi}
, along with the vacuum expectation values of the scalar doublets
\phi_{ij}
. To achieve a light
\chi
-boson, one may select a small
g_{\chi}
, small
q_{\phi_{ij}}
, or both, leading to an ultralight
\chi
-boson while preserving the observed lepton mass spectrum.

Based on the assumptions in Eqs. (
2
), it can be found that
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle F_{1}=\frac{1}{4Q}\left[\left(q_{e_{2}}-q_{e_{1}}\right)y_{21}+\left(q_{L_{1}}-q_{L_{2}}\right)y_{12}\right]\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\mu}}\equiv c^{v}\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\mu}}\,,

\displaystyle G_{1}=\frac{1}{4Q}\left[\left(q_{e_{2}}-q_{e_{1}}\right)y_{21}-\left(q_{L_{1}}-q_{L_{2}}\right)y_{12}\right]]\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\mu}}\equiv c^{a}\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\mu}}\,.

(8)

Expanding to three generations, the model that induces LFV transitions at the tree level is included in the low-energy effective Lagrangian with monopole operators, as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}=f_{ij}\bar{\ell}_{i}\gamma^{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\ell_{j}+g_{ij}\bar{\ell}_{i}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\chi_{\alpha}\ell_{j}+{\rm h.c.},

(9)

where
\ell_{i}\,,\ell_{j}=e\,,\mu\,,\tau
and
f_{ij}\,,~g_{ij}
are the effective couplings in the three generations case. Taking into account the matching with the parameters of the model in Eqs. (
2
), we can simplify and define:

f_{ij}=c^{v}_{ij}\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\ell_{i}^{j}}}~~{\rm and}~~g_{ij}=c^{a}_{ij}\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{\ell_{i}^{j}}}\,,

(10)

where
m_{\ell_{i}^{j}}
, represents the mass of the highest-generation lepton between
\ell_{i}
and
\ell_{j}
, and
c^{v}_{ij}
and
c^{a}_{ij}
are dimensionless independent coefficients.

In
[
30
]
, a model inducing LFV interaction at the one-loop level is also proposed. Although our study focuses on LFV interactions at the tree level, it is noteworthy that the one-loop level model introduces both monopole and dipole operators in an effective Lagrangian.

3
LFV Higgs decays

In the Standard Model, the flavor-conserving interaction
\bar{\ell}_{i}\ell_{i}H

9

\ell_{i}=\ell_{j}

\ell_{i}\neq\ell_{j}

H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}
at the loop level mediated by
\chi

H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}\chi
at the tree level. The forthcoming two subsections will present the analytical and phenomenological outcomes of these decay processes.

4
Conclusions

Lepton Flavor Violation is highly suppressed in the SM extended with right-handed neutrinos, but it may manifest at measurable rates in several well-motivated BSM models. The discovery of the Higgs boson at the LHC has introduced a new avenue for exploring LFV in the decays of this scalar boson.

In this study, we explored the influence of an ultralight gauge boson,
\chi
, in mediating cLFV transitions. Our analysis incorporates a model that generates the lepton flavor-violating interaction
\bar{\ell}_{i}\ell_{j}\chi
at the tree level into an effective field theory, ensuring the
\chi
-boson mass remains well-defined as it approaches zero. We examined the LFV Higgs decay phenomenology under both on-shell and off-shell
\chi
conditions. The results indicate that these processes exhibit minimal dependence on the
\chi
-boson mass, due to the established conditions for the observables to remain finite in the massless limit.

However, the Lepton Charge and Forward-Backward Asymmetries, display sensitivity to
m_{\chi}
. These asymmetries indirectly reveal information about the range of the
\chi
-boson mass as a function of the lepton energy or the angle
\theta_{\ell_{i}\ell_{j}}
. This sensitivity provides a potential method for constraining the mass of the
\chi
-boson in experimental observations.

Utilizing the upper bounds and measurements of Higgs decays into leptons,
H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}
from CMS and ATLAS Collaborations, we derived indirect upper limits on the decays
H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}\chi
. These constraints provide critical insights into the potential signatures of LFV in Higgs decays mediated by an ultralight gauge boson. Collider experiments with enhanced sensitivity to LFV Higgs decay could potentially uncover the presence of an ultralight gauge boson.

Appendix B
Appendix: Counterterms for the Amplitude of
H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}

The amplitude generated by the triangle diagram for the decay
H\to\ell_{i}\bar{\ell}_{j}
includes UV-divergent terms, necessitating the introduction of counterterms to absorb these divergences. The one-loop amplitudes and the corresponding form factors are initially expressed in terms of Passarino-Veltman functions (see Eq. (
3.1
)). For numerical calculations, however, we work with the renormalized amplitude, where the divergences are canceled through counterterms. The associated counterterm Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\rm CT}}=C_{f_{ij}}\bar{\ell}_{i}\ell_{j}H+C_{g_{ij}}\bar{\ell}_{i}\gamma_{5}\ell_{j}H+{\rm h.c.}\,,

(25)

where the coefficients of the scalar and pseudoscalar operators are specified as:

\displaystyle C_{f_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\ell_{k}}}{2m_{\chi}^{2}v\bar{\epsilon}_{{\rm uv}}}\left(M_{H}^{2}-6m_{\ell_{k}}^{2}+6m_{\chi}^{2}\right)(f_{ik}f_{jk}-g_{ik}g_{jk})\,,

\displaystyle C_{g_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\ell_{k}}}{2m_{\chi}^{2}v\bar{\epsilon}_{{\rm uv}}}\left(M_{H}^{2}-6m_{\ell_{k}}^{2}+6m_{\chi}^{2}\right)(f_{ik}g_{jk}-f_{jk}g_{ik})\,,

(26)

with the effective couplings
f_{ij}
and
g_{ij}
defined in Eq. (
10
), and
\frac{1}{\bar{\epsilon}_{{\rm uv}}}\equiv\frac{1}{\epsilon_{{\rm uv}}}-\gamma_{E}+\ln 4\pi
. The amplitude is renormalized using the
\overline{\rm MS}
-scheme, ensuring that only finite contributions remain in the final calculation.

It is important to observe that the counterterm Lagrangian in Eq. (
25
) comprises two distinct contributions: the first term corresponds to a scalar operator, while the second term represents a pseudoscalar operator. The pseudoscalar term does not contribute to the amplitude in the flavor-conserving scenario. This indicates that quantum corrections primarily generate the scalar operator, similar to the behavior observed in the Standard Model.

Automorphic functions as the trace of Frobenius
D. Arinkin, D. Gaitsgory, D. Kazhdan, S. Raskin, N. Rozenblyum, Y. Varshavsky

Abstract
. We prove that the trace of the Frobenius endofunctor of the category of automorphic sheaves with nilpotent singular support is isomorphic to the space of unramified automorphic functions, settling a conjecture from [ AGKRRV1 ] . More generally, we show that traces of Frobenius-Hecke functors produce shtuka cohomologies.



Introduction

0.1.
Overview

This work is part of a series, following
[
AGKRRV1
]
and
[
AGKRRV2
]
,
attempting to understand the (unramified, function field)
arithmetic Langlands conjectures via geometric and categorical techniques.
We begin with an overview of the problems considered here.

0.1.1.

Some of the most striking applications of geometric representation theory pass through
the sheaves-functions dictionary of Grothendieck–Deligne.

Recall the setting: one has an algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, assumed to be defined
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and an
\ell
-adic Weil sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
. For a rational point

y\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, one takes the trace of Frobenius on the stalk

y^{*}({\mathcal{F}})
to obtain an element of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
; this defines a function

\operatorname{funct}({\mathcal{F}}):{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\to\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
.

One finds that many functions of interest in harmonic analysis over
finite fields arise by this procedure, and that the perspective offered by sheaf
theory provides deep insights into function theory.

For example, this is the case in the theory of automorphic functions
(for function fields), whose realizations via
\ell
-adic sheaves exhibit
explicit constructions of Langlands’s conjectures.

0.1.2.

In this paper, we establish a categorical analogue of
the sheaves-functions dictionary: instead of passing from
sheaves to functions, we pass from categories (of sheaves)
to vector spaces (of functions).

As in the previous setting, we decategorify using trace of Frobenius.
However, whereas before we considered the trace of a Frobenius endomorphism
of a vector space (and thus produced a scalar), we now consider the trace
of a Frobenius endofunctor of a category (and thus produce a vector space).

Unlike the usual Grothendieck–Deligne paradigm, where one may take a general
algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
, our results are specialized to spherical automorphic
functions. The results of this paper establish conjectures from
[
AGKRRV1
]
,
which specify a relation between the category of automorphic sheaves and the
vector space of automorphic functions via the trace of Frobenius.

0.1.3.

We give a precise formulation of our main results below.
However, by way of motivation, we highlight the following application,
which illustrates how sheaf-theoretic considerations provide insights
into the classical theory of automorphic functions.

In
[
AGKRRV1
]
, we introduced a version of the geometric Langlands
conjecture suitable for
\ell
-adic sheaves. From this conjecture, combined
with the
Trace Conjecture
, proved in this paper,
we deduced that the space of compactly supported spherical automorphic functions
(denoted
\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
in the body of this text)
can be described as

(0.1)

\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\simeq\Gamma(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arthm}}_{\check{G}}(X),\omega_{\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arthm}}_{\check{G}}(X)}).

In the above formula,
X
is the smooth projective curve corresponding to our choice of
function field and

\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arthm}}_{\check{G}}(X)
is the algebraic stack over

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
, defined in
[
AGKRRV1
]
,
parametrizing (unramified) Langlands parameters.
1

1

In the everywhere unramified function field setting, this conjecture provides an interesting

Formulation of the main result

Let

0.2.2.
Categorical trace

Throughout this paper, all DG categories are enriched
over the field
{\mathsf{e}}
. In particular,
\operatorname{Vect}=\operatorname{Vect}_{\mathsf{e}}

(the DG category of chain complexes of
{\mathsf{e}}
-vector spaces), and so on.

We remind that the category
\operatorname{DGCat}
of presentable DG categories
is equipped with a canonical
symmetric monoidal structure

Lurie tensor product
.
On general grounds, this means one may speak about categorical duals and traces
as follows.

If
{\mathbf{C}}\in\operatorname{DGCat}
is dualizable, there is another DG category

{\mathbf{C}}^{\vee}
equipped with canonical unit and counit maps

\operatorname{u}_{\mathbf{C}}:\operatorname{Vect}\to{\mathbf{C}}\otimes{\mathbf{C}}^{\vee}

and

\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf{C}}:{\mathbf{C}}\otimes{\mathbf{C}}^{\vee}\to\operatorname{Vect}.

For an endofunctor
\Phi:{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
of
{\mathbf{C}}
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})\in\operatorname{Vect}
defined as

\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}}):=\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf{C}}\big((\Phi\otimes\operatorname{Id})(\operatorname{u}_{\mathbf{C}})\big).

We refer to
[
GKRV
]
for further discussion.

0.2.3.
Categories of sheaves

We consider the category
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
of

Precisely,
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is the category of ind-constructible

As in
[

(0.2)

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

The categories
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
and
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
have favorable

compactly generated and therefore dualizable in
\operatorname{DGCat}

Moreover, if we assume
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 14.1.8]

the embedding

0.2.4.

Pushforward with respect to the geometric Frobenius endomorphism (see Sect.

defines an auto-equivalence

Hence, it makes sense to consider the categorical trace

0.2.5.

At this point we can state one of three main results of this paper (it appears as Corollary

\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\simeq\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})).

0.3.
Insertion of Hecke functors

So far, we have not mentioned Langlands duality. Remarkably,
it plays an essential role in the proof of Theorem
0.2.6
.

In fact, our proof of Theorem
0.2.6

constructs isomorphisms between two families
of objects, which are, roughly speaking, indexed by Hecke functors.
As we explain below, this amounts to a proof of
[

, Conjecture 22.5.7]

referred to as the

of
loc. cit.

Moreover, our method of proof relies in an essential way on consideration

Hecke functors simultaneously.

We presently explain what these objects are, and what our results about them assert.

0.3.1.

Let

We let

0.3.2.

Precisely, given

0.3.3.

0.3.5.

On the one hand, we have a functor

\operatorname{Sht}^{\operatorname{Tr}}({\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}})=\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})).

.

To make the above more explicit, we describe the corresponding functors

true, in general, that
\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\mathcal{Y}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))
is isomorphic to

\operatorname{Funct}_{c}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
.

We now take
{\mathcal{Y}}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, but instead of all of
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, we take

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

A key observation is that if we restrict the pairing (
0.7
), the resulting pairing

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{Vect}

is
miraculously
the counit of a self-duality. This is the content of the main result of

[
AGKRRV2
]
, stated therein as Theorem 3.2.2.

Remark 0.4.5
.

The usage of the word “miraculous” in the above phrase is not accidental:

In
[
AGKRRV2
, Theorem 3.3.3]
, we show that the self-duality of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

induced by (
0.7
) is related to the Verdier self-duality of Remark

0.4.3
via the
miraculous functor
, denoted
\operatorname{Mir}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}
.

0.4.6.

The self-duality of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, induced by (
0.7
) paves a way
to computing traces of endofunctors of this category.

However, in order to do so, we need one more ingredient: we need to have an explicit
description of the unit of this self-duality. Another key takeaway from the paper
[
AGKRRV2
]

is such a description:

There exists a particular object

{\mathsf{R}}\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}

(termed
Beilinson’s spectral projector
, see Sect.
0.5.2
below) such that when we apply it along the left (or right) factor
of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
to
\operatorname{ps-u}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}
, the resulting object belongs to the essential
image of the functor

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\overset{\boxtimes}{\hookrightarrow}\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

and is the unit of the above self-duality of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

0.5.
Outline of the proof

We now give an overview of the proof of Theorem
0.3.10
.
It requires a few additional objects and some relations between them.

0.5.1.

First, we recall the (non-algebraic) derived stack

\operatorname{LocSys}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{restr}}(X)

over
{\mathsf{e}}

[
AGKRRV1
]
, the

We have a naturally defined symmetric monoidal localization functor

see Sect.
2.3
.

0.5.2.

We use
Beilinson’s spectral projector
,
{\mathsf{R}}\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
, see Sect.
0.4.6
.

It has the following property
vis-a-vis

see Theorem

, which is a restatement

, Theorem 12.7.4]
.

0.5.3.

Using the result of
[
Xue2
]
mentioned above, we show in Corollary

that the functor
\operatorname{Sht}
factors canonically as a composition

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}\overset{\operatorname{Loc}}{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}(X))\overset{\operatorname{Sht}_{\operatorname{Loc}}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Vect}

for a certain functor

.

0.5.4.

At this point, the assertion of Theorem
0.3.10
follows easily from the properties
of
{\mathsf{R}}
mentioned above:

The property of
{\mathsf{R}}
mentioned in Sect.
0.4.6

this follows by essentially rerunning the calculation in Sect.

Applying Sect.

0.6.
Relation to the classical sheaves-function dictionary

The Trace Conjecture as stated in
[
AGKRRV1
]
is somewhat stronger
than Theorem
0.2.6
stated above, in that it specifies a particular morphism
from
\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))
to
\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))

that is supposed to be an isomorphism, see
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 22.3.7(b) and Remark 22.3.9]
.

In this subsection we describe the results of the present paper in this direction.

0.6.1.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a compact object of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, equipped with a structure of

weak Weil equivariance
, i.e., a morphism

\alpha:{\mathcal{F}}\to({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*}({\mathcal{F}}),

which is equivalent to the datum of a map

\alpha^{\prime}:({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{F}}.

By functoriality of the categorical trace, we can attach to the pair
({\mathcal{F}},\alpha)
its class

\operatorname{cl}({\mathcal{F}},\alpha)\in\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}({\mathcal{Y}})).

Hence, applying Theorem
0.2.6
, we can further attach to it an element of
\operatorname{Funct}_{c}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
.

We wish to say that this function equals the function attached to
({\mathcal{F}},\alpha^{\prime})
by the classical
sheaves-function correspondence, i.e., by taking pointwise traces of the Frobenius on them stalks
of
{\mathcal{F}}
.

However, here is an issue: we do not know that
{\mathcal{F}}
is constructible as a sheaf on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.
So, the above pointwise trace operation is not a priori well-defined, as the stalks in question may
be inifinite-dimensional.

0.6.2.

To overcome to issue mentioned above,
for the duration of this subsection we will assume
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 14.1.8]
. This
conjecture has several equivalent formulations:

•

The category
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is generated by objects that are compact in the ambient
category
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
;

•

Compact objects of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
are compact as objects of
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
;

•

Compact objects of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
are constructible as objects of
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
;

•

Compact objects of
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
are bounded below in the natural t-structure on

\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

An analog of
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 14.1.8]
is known when our ground field
k
has characteristic
0
,
and
\operatorname{Shv}(-)
is either the category of ind-holonomic D-modules or the category of ind-constructible Betti
sheaves (see
[
AGKRRV1
, Theorems 16.4.3 and 16.4.10]
). Furthermore, we believe that we can prove it
when
G=GL_{n}
.

To summarize, this conjecture is a rather plausible statement.

0.6.3.

Assuming
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 14.1.8]
, we obtain that the embedding (
0.2
)
admits a continuous right adjoint.

Hence, by functoriality of categorical trace, we obtain a map

(0.8)

\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\to\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})).

0.6.4.

For any quasi-compact algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
and defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
there is a canonical

local term
map (see
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. 22.2]
),

(0.9)

\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to\operatorname{Funct}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})),

where
\operatorname{Funct}(-)
stands for the (classical) vector space
{\mathsf{e}}
-valued functions.

In addition, in
loc. cit
., we extended this construction to non-quasi-compact
stacks (such as
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
). In this setting, the above map takes the form

\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to\operatorname{Funct}_{c}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})),

where
\operatorname{Funct}_{c}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\subset\operatorname{Funct}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
is the subspace
of compactly supported
{\mathsf{e}}
-valued functions.

Remark 0.6.5
.

By functoriality of traces, any (possibly lax) Weil sheaf

({\mathcal{F}}\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})^{c},\alpha:{\mathcal{F}}\to({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}({\mathcal{F}}))

on
{\mathcal{Y}}
defines an element

\operatorname{cl}({\mathcal{F}},\alpha)\in\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})).

According to

, Sect. 22.2]
, the value of the map

\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}
on
\operatorname{cl}({\mathcal{F}},\alpha)
produces the corresponding Grothendieck–Deligne
function, denoted
\operatorname{funct}({\mathcal{F}})
.

0.6.6.

By the above, we obtain a map

(0.10)

\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\overset{\text{\eqref{e:forget Nilp intro}}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\overset{\operatorname{LT}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}}{\to}\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})),

where we note that the vector space
\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
is the space of
compactly supported unramified automorphic functions.

0.6.7.

We can now state our third main (it appears as Theorem
5.2.3
in the main body of the paper):

Main Theorem 0.6.8
.

The map (
0.10
) equals the
isomorphism
of Theorem
0.2.6

Corollary 0.6.9
.

The map (
0.10
) is an
isomorphism.

Remark 0.6.10
.

Informally, one can view Corollary
0.6.9
as saying that “there are enough
weak Weil sheaves on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
with nilpotent singular support
to recover all automorphic functions, and any relations
between the automorphic functions defined by such sheaves
have categorical origins.”

Of course, the fact that we are considering sheaves with nilpotent singular support
is crucial here. If we did not have the singular support condition, we would obviously
have enough sheaves to recover all functions. However, the relations imposed by sheaves
would not match the relations on functions.

Remark 0.6.11
.

The phrase in quotation makes in the previous remark would be a correct assertion if the phrase
inside the quotation marks was understood in the derived sense.

More precisely, for a (compactly generated) category
{\mathbf{C}}
with an endofunctor
\Phi
,
the trace object
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})\in\operatorname{Vect}
is computed as the geometric realization of a
certain canonically defined simplicial object of
\operatorname{Vect}
; let us denote it
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})^{\bullet}
.

In particular, we have a map
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})^{0}\to\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})
(here the superscript
0

denotes the space of
0
-simplices), and hence a map

H^{0}(\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})^{0})\to H^{0}(\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}})).

Now, the image of the latter map is the span of the classes

\operatorname{cl}({\mathbf{c}},\alpha),\quad{\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathbf{C}}^{c},\alpha:{\mathbf{c}}\to\Phi({\mathbf{c}}).

However, it is not true, in general, that
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi,{\mathbf{C}}))
is spanned by the above
classes: higher cohomologies of higher simplices can also contribute.

1.
The Hecke action

In this section we will recall the pattern of Hecke acton of the category
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})
on

\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, and some related formalism. The section contains no original material.

1.1.
Hecke functors

1.1.1.

In this paper, by the phenomenon of Hecke action we will understand a system of functors, defined for every finite set
I
and every
algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Z}}
(thought of as a stack of parameters):

(1.1)

\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}).

For a fixed
V\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}
, we will denote by
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)
the resulting functor

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}).

When
{\mathcal{Z}}=\operatorname{pt}
, we will simply write
{\mathsf{H}}
(resp.,
{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)
).

1.1.2.

The Hecke functions (
1.1
) are associative in the following sense: we have a natural commutative diagram
of functors

\begin{CD}{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{mult}}>{}>{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\\
@V{\operatorname{Id}\otimes(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}})}V{}V\\
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})&&@V{}V{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}}V\\
@V{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}}V{}V\\
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}\times X^{I})@>{(\operatorname{Id}\times\Delta)^{!}}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}),\end{CD}

where

\operatorname{mult}:\operatorname{Rep}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Rep}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\to\operatorname{Rep}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}

is the tensor product functor and

\Delta_{X^{I}}:X^{I}\to X^{I}\times X^{I}
is the diagonal embedding. The data of associativity of (
1.1
) comes additionally with higher coherence for higher powers of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}
.

We can rephrase this as saying that the category
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})
is a module category for the monoidal category
\operatorname{Rep}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}
,
and the action is
\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})
-linear (i.e. it is a module category for the monoidal category
\operatorname{Rep}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})
),
where the symmetric monoidal structure on
\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})
is given by
\overset{!}{\otimes}
.

1.1.3.

The functors (
1.1
) are naturally compatible with maps between finite sets. Namely, for a map

\psi:I\to J
, we have a data of commutativity for the diagram

(1.2)

\begin{CD}{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\\
@V{\operatorname{Id}\otimes\operatorname{mult}^{\psi}}V{}V@V{}V{(\operatorname{Id}\times\Delta_{\psi})^{!}}V\\
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes J}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{J})\end{CD}

where

\operatorname{mult}^{\psi}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\to{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes J}

is the functor given by the symmetric monoidal structure on
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})
, and

\Delta_{\psi}:X^{J}\to X^{I}

the diagonal map defined by
\psi
.

The above data of commutativity are endowed with a homotopy coherent system of compatibilities for
compositions of maps of finite sets.

Moreover, this data is compatible with the associativity described in Sect.
1.1.2
. Namely,
the functor

(\operatorname{Id}\times\Delta_{\psi})^{!}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{J})

is a functor of
\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})\otimes{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}
-module categories.

1.1.4.

A feature of the functors
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)
is that they are functors that are both

defined and codefined by kernels
; see Sect.
0.8.6
for what this means. In practical
terms, this implies that for a map
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{Z}}_{2}
between algebraic stacks, we have a datum
of commutativity for the diagrams

\begin{CD}\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}),\end{CD}

where the vertical arrows are given by either
\blacktriangle
- or !- pushforwards, and also for the diagrams

\begin{CD}\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}_{2}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}),\end{CD}

where the vertical arrows are given by either !- or *- pullbacks.

Moreover, this datum of commutativity is functorial in
V\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}
, and is compatible with the datum
of commutativity of the diagrams (
1.2
).

1.2.
The ULA property of the Hecke action

1.2.1.

Another key feature of the functors
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}
is that for
{\mathcal{F}}\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})^{\operatorname{constr}}
and

V\in({\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I})^{c}
, the object

(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}})(V,{\mathcal{F}})\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})

is ULA with respect to the projection

{\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}\to X^{I}.

1.2.2.

Let us denote by
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l}
the functor

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})

defined as

(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l})(V,{\mathcal{F}}):=(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}})(V,{\mathcal{F}})\overset{!}{\otimes}p_{3}^{!}(\underline{{\mathsf{e}}}_{X^{I}}).

Note that for a fixed
I
, the difference between
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}
and
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l}
amounts to a cohomological
shift by
2|I|
since
\underline{{\mathsf{e}}}_{X^{I}}\simeq\omega_{X^{I}}[-2|I|]
.

1.2.3.

The ULA property of the objects
(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}})(V,{\mathcal{F}})
implies that we have canonical isomorphisms

(1.3)

(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}})(V,{\mathcal{F}})\overset{!}{\otimes}p_{3}^{!}({\mathcal{M}})\simeq(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l})(V,{\mathcal{F}})\overset{*}{\otimes}p_{3}^{*}({\mathcal{M}}),\quad{\mathcal{M}}\in\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I}).

Furthermore, for a map of finite sets
\psi:I\to J
, we have a data of commutativity for the diagram

(1.4)

\begin{CD}{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l}}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\\
@V{\operatorname{mult}^{\psi}\otimes\operatorname{Id}}V{}V@V{}V{(\operatorname{Id}\times\Delta_{\psi})^{*}}V\\
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes J}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G})@>{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l}}>{}>\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{J}),\end{CD}

endowed with a homotopy coherent system of compatibilities for
compositions of maps of finite sets.

1.2.4.

Thus, we can regard the functors
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}^{l}(V,-)
also as defined and codefined by kernels, and they
have the formal properties parallel to those of the functors
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\boxtimes{\mathsf{H}}(V,-)
.

1.3.
Hecke action on
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

1.3.1.

One of the main actor in this paper is the full subcategory

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

The following result, essentially due to
[
NY
]
, describes the behavior of this subcategory under the Hecke functors
(this is stated as
[
AGKRRV1
, Theorem 14.2.4]
):

Theorem 1.3.2
.

The Hecke functor
{\mathsf{H}}
for

sends the full subcategory

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

to the full subcategory

\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}\times\{0\}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X)\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X).

1.3.3.

Recall also (see
[
AGKRRV1
, Theorem F.9.7 combined with Corollary E.4.7]
) that for any algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
and a conical half-dimensional
Zariski-closed subset
{\mathcal{N}}\subset T^{*}({\mathcal{Y}})

\operatorname{Shv}_{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{Y}})\otimes\operatorname{QLisse}(X)\to\operatorname{Shv}_{{\mathcal{N}}\times\{0\}}({\mathcal{Y}}\times X)

Thus, from Theorem
1.3.2
we obtain:

Corollary 1.3.4
.

The Hecke functor
{\mathsf{H}}
for
I=\{*\}
sends the full subcategory

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

to the full subcategory

\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\otimes\operatorname{QLisse}(X)\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X).

Iterating, from Corollary
1.3.4

Corollary 1.3.5

The Hecke functors
{\mathsf{H}}

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\otimes\operatorname{QLisse}(X)^{\otimes I}\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I}).

1.3.6.

Note that for a scheme or stack

1.4.
The category
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}

We will now introduce a device that allows us to express the Hecke action
on
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
in terms of a single monoidal category, the
Ran version
of

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})
, denoted
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
.

We refer the reader to
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. 11]
for a detailed discussion of this construction.

1.4.1.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a symmetric monoidal DG category.
We define a new symmetric monoidal DG category
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
by
the following construction.

Let
\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
be the category of

twisted arrows
on
\operatorname{fSet}
, see
[
GKRV
, Sect. 1.2.2]
.

The category
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
is the colimit over
\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
of the functor

(1.11)

\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})\to\operatorname{DGCat}

that sends

(I\to J)\mapsto{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J}).

Here for a map

(1.12)

\begin{CD}I_{1}@>{}>{}>J_{1}\\
@V{\phi_{I}}V{}V@A{}A{\phi_{J}}A\\
I_{2}@>{}>{}>J_{2},\end{CD}

in
\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
,
the corresponding functor

{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{1}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{1}})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{2}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{2}})

is given by the tensor product functor along the fibers of
\phi_{I}

(1.13)

\operatorname{mult}^{\phi_{I}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{1}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{2}}

and the functor

(1.14)

(\Delta_{\phi_{J}})_{!}:\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{1}})\to\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{2}}),

where
\Delta_{\phi_{J}}:X^{J_{2}}\to X^{J_{1}}
is the diagonal map induced by
\phi_{J}
.

1.4.2.

The functor (
1.11
) is naturally right-lax symmetric monoidal. Therefore, the colimit
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}

carries a natural symmetric monoidal structure. Explicitly, this symmetric monoidal structure can be described
as follows.
For

V_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{1}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{1}})\text{ and }V_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I_{2}}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{2}}),

the tensor product of their images in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
is the image of the object

(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})\otimes({\mathcal{M}}_{1}\boxtimes{\mathcal{M}}_{2})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes(I_{1}\sqcup I_{2})}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}}).

We will denote the resulting monoidal operation on
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
by

{\mathcal{V}}_{1},{\mathcal{V}}_{2}\mapsto{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\star{\mathcal{V}}_{2}.

We denote the unit object by
{\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}}
. It arises
from
({\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Id}}}:\emptyset\to\emptyset)\in\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
and the
corresponding map

\operatorname{Vect}={\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes\emptyset}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{\emptyset})\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}.

1.4.3.

Let
(\psi:I\to J)\in\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
be given.

We denote by

\operatorname{ins}_{\psi}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J})\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}

the corresponding functor.

In the important special case
\psi=\operatorname{Id}_{I}:I\to I
, we
use the notation
\operatorname{ins}_{I}
in place of
\operatorname{ins}_{\operatorname{Id}_{I}}
.

1.4.4.

We will apply the above discussion to the case
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})
.

We denote the resulting (symmetric monoidal) category by
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
.

1.5.
A Ran version of the Hecke action

1.5.1.

We now claim that the datum of the functors (
1.1
) together with the compatibilities
(
1.2
) allow to define an action of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}

\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\times X^{I})\overset{(p_{1,2})_{*}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Z}}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

1.5.5.

By Theorem

1.6.
The dual category of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}

For what follows we will need to recall some constructions pertaining to duality on
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
.

We refer the reader to
[
AGKRRV1
, Sects. 11.3 and 11.4]
for a detailed discussion.

1.6.1.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a general symmetric monoidal DG category.

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is dualizable, and that for every
(I\overset{\psi}{\to}J)\in\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})
, the functor

\operatorname{mult}^{\psi}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes J}

({\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes J})^{\vee}\to({\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I})^{\vee}

In this case, one shows that the category
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
is dualizable (see, e.g.,
[
GR
, Chapter 1, Proposition 6.3.4]
).

1.6.2.

The dual category
({\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}})^{\vee}
is the category of continuous functors
{\mathcal{C}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}\to\operatorname{Vect}
, and hence it can be described as

(1.17)

\underset{(I\overset{\psi}{\to}J)\in\operatorname{TwArr}(\operatorname{fSet})^{op}}{\operatorname{lim}}\,({\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes I}\otimes\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J}))^{\vee},

where the limit is formed using the functors dual to the ones used in the formation of the colimit in Sect.
1.4.1
.

Using the Verdier self-duality on
\operatorname{Shv}(X^{J})
, we can rewrite

(1.18)

2.1.

2.1.1.

For a test affine (derived) scheme

The main results of this paper will be based on considering the (symmetric monoidal)

4.

In this section we will prove the main result of this paper,

can be obtained as the (categorical) trace of Frobenius on the category of automorphic sheaves
with nilpotent singular support.

4.1.
Traces of Frobenius-Hecke operators

4.1.1.

Recall that the
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
-action on
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
preserves
its subcategory
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

Therefore, we obtain a functor

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}\to{\mathbf{Maps}}_{\operatorname{DGCat}}(\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}),\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))

sending
{\mathcal{V}}
to the functor
{\mathcal{V}}\star-
.

Note also that the subcategory
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is preserved by
the endofunctor
({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*}
, see
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. 22.3.3]
.

We define a functor

\operatorname{Sht}^{\operatorname{Tr}}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}\to\operatorname{Vect}

as the functor

{\mathcal{V}}\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}\big({\mathsf{H}}_{\mathcal{V}}\ \circ({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}),\big).

In other words, we compose
{\mathsf{H}}_{\mathcal{V}}
with pushforward along
the geometric Frobenius
{\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

and form the trace (as an endofunctor of the (dualizable) DG category

\operatorname{Shv}_{{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
).

Our main theorem asserts:

Theorem 4.1.2
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of functors

\operatorname{Ev}(V)\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}(X))\otimes\operatorname{QLisse}(X)^{\otimes I}.

By construction, the corresponding objects

(\iota^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Id})(\operatorname{Ev}(V))\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X))\otimes\operatorname{QLisse}(X)^{\otimes I}

carry a structure of equivariance with respect to the partial Frobenius endomorphisms acting along
X^{I}
.

By (
4.4
) and the projection formula, we have

(4.5)

\operatorname{Sht}^{\operatorname{Tr}}_{I}(V)\simeq(\Gamma_{!}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X),-)\otimes\operatorname{Id})\left((\iota^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Id})(\operatorname{Ev}(V))\otimes\operatorname{Drinf}^{\operatorname{Tr},\operatorname{arithm}})\right).

This interpretation shows that the the objects

\operatorname{Sht}^{\operatorname{Tr}}_{I}(V)\in\operatorname{QLisse}(X)^{\otimes I}\subset\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})

carry a natural structure of equivariance with respect to the partial Frobenius endomorphisms on
X^{I}
.

Remark 4.5.4
.

Recall that
\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X)
is actually a quasi-compact algebraic stack, see Remark
3.4.1
. This implies that
in formula (
4.5
), we have

\Gamma_{!}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X),-)\simeq\Gamma(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X),-).

4.5.5.

Having proved Theorem
4.1.2
, and hence Corollary
4.1.6
, we obtain that the objects

\operatorname{Sht}_{I}(V)\in\operatorname{Shv}(X^{I})

also carry a natural structure of equivariance with respect to the partial Frobenius endomorphisms on
X^{I}
.

However, it is not difficult to show that this structure identifies with the structure of partial Frobenius equivariance
on shtukas, defined in
[
VLaf
]
and
[
Xue1
]
.
The matching between the two is essentially
[
GKRV
, Lemma 4.5.4]
.

4.5.6.

Now, given an object
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}(X))
, the datum required to exhibit it as

\iota_{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{arithm}})

for some
{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{arithm}}\in\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X)
is equivalent to a
compatible
collection of
data of partial Frobenius equivariance on the associated functors

{\mathcal{F}}_{I}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\to\operatorname{QLisse}(X)^{\otimes I},\quad V\mapsto(\Gamma_{!}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}(X),-)\otimes\operatorname{Id})(\operatorname{Ev}(V)\otimes{\mathcal{F}}).

4.5.7.

As was mentioned in Sect.
4.5.5
, the structure of partial Frobenius equivariance on the functors
\operatorname{Sht}_{I}

is a priori defined (in
[
VLaf
]
and
[
Xue1
]
), thereby producing an object

\operatorname{Drinf}^{\operatorname{arithm}}\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{arithm}}_{\check{G}}(X)).

Thus, the assertion in Sect.
4.5.5
can interpreted as an isomorphism

\operatorname{Drinf}^{\operatorname{arithm}}\simeq\operatorname{Drinf}^{\operatorname{Tr},\operatorname{arithm}},

which induces the isomorphism

\operatorname{Drinf}\simeq\operatorname{Drinf}^{\operatorname{Tr}}

of Theorem
4.1.2
by applying
\iota_{*}
.

4.5.8.

Furthermore, as was mentioned in Remark
3.4.3
, the corresponding structure of partial Frobenius equivariance can
be constructed also on the functors

\operatorname{Sht}_{I,D}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\otimes I}\to\operatorname{QLisse}(X-D)^{\otimes I}

that encode the cohomology of shtukas with level structure, thereby producing an object

\operatorname{Drinf}_{D}^{\operatorname{arithm}}\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}(X-D)).

What we do not have at the moment is the interpretation of this object
\operatorname{Drinf}_{D}^{\operatorname{arithm}}

as enhanced categorical trace (nor of the functors
\operatorname{Sht}_{I,D}
as just categorical traces).

5.
Local terms

5.1.
A hypothesis

In order to simplify the exposition, for the duration of this section, we will assume
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 14.1.8]
.
This conjecture says that the category
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is generated by objects that are compact as objects
of the ambient category
\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

Since we know that
\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated as a DG category (see
[
AGKRRV1
, Theorem 16.1.1]
),
this conjecture is equivalent to the statement that the forgetful functor

\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

preserves compactness, or, equivalently, that it admits a continuous right adjoint.

5.2.
Formulation of the problem

5.2.1.

In this section we will construct four maps

\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{naive}},\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{true}},\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Serre}},\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Sht}}:\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}))\to\operatorname{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))

that we refer to as
local term
morphisms.

At this point, we have only encountered the last of these four morphisms:
by definition,
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Sht}}
is the isomorphism of Corollary
4.1.4
.

5.2.2.

Assuming the construction of the other three morphisms,
we can state the main result of this section and the next:

Theorem 5.2.3
.

The four morphisms
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{naive}}
,

\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{true}}
,
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Serre}}
, and
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Sht}}
are equal.

Remark 5.2.4
.

By Corollary
4.1.4
, the source and target of
each local term map is in
\operatorname{Vect}^{\heartsuit}
, so
equality (as opposed to homotopy) is the relevant notion.

5.2.5.

As
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{Sht}}
is an isomorphism, from Theorem
5.2.3
we deduce:

Corollary 5.2.6
.

Each of the four local term morphisms in an isomorphism.

In the case of
\operatorname{LT}^{\operatorname{naive}}
(see below),
this corollary recovers the Trace Conjecture as
formulated in
[
AGKRRV1
, Conjecture 22.3.7]
.

We now proceed to the construction of the local term morphisms.

5.3.
Naive and true local terms

In what follows, we fix an algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
, which plays the role of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.
We will add certain additional assumptions to
{\mathcal{Y}}
as we proceed.

The material in this subsection closely follows
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. 22.1-22.2]
, to which we
refer the reader for more detail.

5.3.1.
Naive local term

Any algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
has the property that
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})
is compactly generated
and every compact object is
!
-extended from some quasi-compact open
in
{\mathcal{Y}}
(see
[
AGKRRV1
, Sect. F.1.1]

Suppose
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is given.
In this case, the
functor

preserves compact objects and intertwines

({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}
with the identity functor (by (

Therefore, functoriality of traces yields a map

\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Id},\operatorname{Vect})={\mathsf{e}}.

In the quasi-compact case, this yields a map

\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\operatorname{naive}}:\operatorname{Tr}(({\operatorname{Frob}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to\operatorname{Funct}({\mathcal{Y}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})).

Remark 5.3.2

By definition, this construction satisfies the compatibility referenced
in Remark

5.3.3.
True local term

Suppose first that
{\mathcal{Y}}

{\mathcal{Y}}

As in

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})\otimes\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}})\overset{\boxtimes}{\hookrightarrow}\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})

We have (see

5.3.6.

Let now

5.3.7.
The case of

However, the identification between

Remark 5.3.8
.

Let us explain the practical implication of the equality

, stated in Theorem

.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an an object of

equipped with a

(5.3)

To such a pair

we can attach an element of

i.e., a compactly supported automorphic function.

Now, the content of Theorem

is that the above element of

equals the function attached to
{\mathcal{F}}
, viewed as a weak Weil sheaf via

correspondence, i.e., by taking pointwise traces of the Frobenius.

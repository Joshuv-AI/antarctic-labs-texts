Stable minimal hypersurfaces in 𝐑 4 \mathbf{R}^{4}
Otis Chodosh, Chao Li

Abstract
. We prove that a complete, two-sided, stable minimal immersed hypersurface in 𝐑 4 \mathbf{R}^{4} is flat.



1.
Introduction

A complete, two-sided, immersed minimal hypersurface
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is
stable
if

(1)

\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}f^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}

for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
. We prove here the following result.

Theorem 1
.

A complete, connected, two-sided, stable minimal immersion
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
is a flat
\mathbf{R}^{3}\subset\mathbf{R}^{4}
.

This resolves a well-known conjecture of Schoen (cf.
[
19
, Conjecture 2.12]
). The corresponding result for
M^{2}\to\mathbf{R}^{3}
was proven by Fischer-Colbrie–Schoen, do Carmo–Peng, and Pogorelov
[
26
,
23
,
43
]
in 1979. Theorem
1
(and higher dimensional analogues) has been established under natural cubic volume growth assumptions by Schoen–Simon–Yau
[
44
]
(see also
[
52
,
47
]
). Furthermore, in the special case that
M^{n}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a minimal graph (implying (
1
) and volume growth bounds) flatness of
M
is known as the
Bernstein problem
, see
[
27
,
22
,
3
,
52
,
6
]
. Several authors have studied Theorem
1
under some extra hypothesis, see e.g.,
[
48
,
8
,
5
,
51
,
12
,
39
,
36
,
42
,
56
]
. We also note here some recent papers
[
7
,
24
]
concerning stability in related contexts.

It is well-known (cf.
[
58
, Lecture 3]
) that a result along the lines of Theorem
1
yields curvature estimates for minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
.

Theorem 2
.

There exists
C<\infty
such that if
M^{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{4}
is a two-sided, stable minimal immersion, then

|A_{M}(p)|d_{M}(p,\partial M)\leq C.

More generally, we recall that a minimal immersion
M^{3}\to(N^{4},g)
is stable if

\int_{M}(|A_{M}|^{2}+\Ric_{N}(\nu,\nu))f^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}

for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M\setminus\partial M)
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
(N^{4},g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold. There exists
C=C(N,g)
such that if
M^{3}\rightarrow N^{4}
is a two-sided, stable minimal immersion, then

|A_{M}(p)|\min\{1,d_{M}(p,\partial M)\}\leq C.

We have recently generalized Theorem
3
to hold in (non-compact) ambient
(N^{4},g)
with bounded sectional curvature in a joint work with Stryker
[
15
, Corollary 2.5]
, which resolves
[
19
, Conjecture 2.13]
.

Theorems
2
and
3
are the four-dimensional analogue of the well-known curvature estimate of Schoen
[
46
]
for minimal surfaces in three-dimensions. Note that by the work of Schoen–Simon–Yau
[
44
]
, such an estimate was previously known to hold where
C
depended on an upper bound for volume of
M^{3}
in small balls.

Remark 4
.

There have been several interesting developments since the first version of this paper was posted. The authors have discovered
[
14
]
a new proof of Theorem
1
that can be localized to obtain (interior) volume estimates (in the spirit of Pogorelov
[
43
]
; cf.
[
17
]
). This new proof is related to the study of
uniformly positive
scalar curvature, while the current paper is related to the study of
non-negative
scalar curvature. Subsequently, Catino–Mastrolia–Roncoroni have discovered
[
28
]
a completely different proof of Theorem
1
, related to the study of Bakry–Émery–Ricci curvature. Interestingly, the dimension restriction
n+1=4
enters each proof in a different way.

A slight modification of the proof of Theorem
1
yields a structure theorem for finite index minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
, analogous to the well-known results of Gulliver, Fischer–Colbrie, and Osserman
[
25
,
29
,
41
]
. Recall that a complete, two-sided, immersed minimal hypersurface
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
has
finite Morse index
if

\ind(M):=\sup\left\{\dim V:V\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(M),Q(f,f)<0\textrm{ for all }f\in V\setminus\{0\}\right\}<\infty,

where
Q(f,f)=\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}-\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}f^{2}
.

Theorem 5
.

A complete, two-sided, minimal immersion
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
has finite Morse index if and only if it has finite total curvature
\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{3}<\infty
.

We remark that Tysk
[
57
]
proved the same statement for a complete, two-sided, minimal immersion
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
(for
3\leq n\leq 6
) under the assumption that
M
has Euclidean volume growth.

Theorem
5
has strong consequences on the structure of
M
near infinity. We recall the following definition.

Definition 6

(
[
50
, Section 2]
)
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
,
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a complete minimal immersion. An end
E
of
M
is
regular at infinity
if it is the graph of a function
w
over a hyperplane
\Pi
with the asymptotics

w(x)=b+a|x|^{2-n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}x_{j}|x|^{-n}+O\left(|x|^{-n}\right),

for some constants
a,b,c_{j}
, where
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
are the coordinates in
\Pi
.

By
[
4
,
50
,
51
,
57
]
(see also
[
31
, Appendix A]
), if
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a minimal immersion with finite total curvature, then each end of
M
is regular at infinity. Moreover, by
[
33
]
a two-sided minimal immersion with finite Morse index has finitely many ends. Combined with Theorem
5
, this yields the following result.

Corollary 7
.

Suppose
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
is a complete, two-sided, minimal immersion with finite Morse index. Then
M
has finitely many ends, each of which is regular at infinity. In particular,
M
has cubic volume growth, i.e.

\sup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Vol\left(B_{r}(0)\cap M\right)}{r^{3}}<\infty.

In fact, by
[
31
]
, we can bound the volume growth rate linearly in terms of the Morse index

\sup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Vol\left(B_{r}(0)\cap M\right)}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}}\leq 6\left(\ind(M)+1\right).

These results should be relevant to the study of minimal hypersurfaces with bounded index in a four-manifold (cf.
[
13
,
53
]
).

Remark 8
.

We also note that Theorems
1
and
2
hold if (

(1-\delta_{s})\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}f^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}

(cf.

1.1.
Idea of the proof of Theorem
1

Consider a non-flat, complete, two-sided stable minimal hypersurface
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
. It turns out to be no loss of generality to assume that
M
is simply connected and of bounded curvature.

It is well-known (see, e.g.
[
32
]
) that
M
admits a positive Green’s function of the Laplacian operator denoted here by
u
. (In other words,
M
is non-parabolic.) The central quantity considered in the proof of Theorem
1
is

F(t):=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}

where
\Sigma_{t}=u^{-1}(t)
. (Quantities of this type have been considered in several contexts including
[
40
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
37
,
2
,
1
]
.) A simple computation shows that
F(t)=4\pi t^{2}
when
M=\mathbf{R}^{3}
.

For general
M
, we can view any estimate of
F(t)
as
t\to 0
as certain kind of control on the growth rate of
M
near infinity. It is straightforward to show that
F(t)
satisfies the
a priori
estimate
F(t)=O(t)
as
t\to 0
(see Lemma
15
). An important observation is that if we can upgrade this to the sharp estimate
F(t)=O(t^{2})
, then we can conclude that
M
is flat.

At a heuristic level, if we were to pretend that
u\sim r^{-1}
and
|\nabla u|\sim r^{-2}
(this holds on
\mathbf{R}^{3}
), we would have
F(t)\sim t^{4}|\partial B_{t^{-1}}|=r^{-4}|\partial B_{r}|
. Thus
F(t)=O(t^{2})
can be thought of as the estimate
|\partial B_{r}|=O(r^{2})
which would suffice to show
M
is flat by work of Schoen–Simon–Yau
[
44
]
. In fact, a closely related argument actually works: we can consider
f=\varphi\circ u
in the
L^{3}
-version of the Schoen–Simon–Yau estimates (see Proposition
27
) and use the co-area formula combined with a log-cutoff near the pole and the end to see that
F(t)=O(t^{2})
implies that
M
is flat.

It thus remains to explain how to show
F(t)=O(t^{2})
.
Our strategy is to find two (competing) estimates relating
F(t)
and the quantity

\mathcal{A}(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|A_{M}|^{2}.

One can view
\mathcal{A}(t)
as measuring the bending of
M
near infinity in a certain sense.

Our first estimate follows by extending a recent

2.
The Green’s function on a stable minimal hypersurface

It is well-known (cf.
[
32
, Theorem 10.1]
) that when
n>2
a stable minimal hypersurface in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is non-parabolic (i.e., admits a positive Green’s function). We recall this fact below as well as establishing several useful facts about the behavior of the level sets of such a Green’s function.

For
n\geq 3
, recall that the Euclidean Green’s function on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
(at a point
p
) is
c_{n}|x-p|^{2-n}
, for
c_{n}^{-1}=(n-2)\omega_{n-1}
(so
c_{3}^{-1}=4\pi
); here,
\omega_{n-1}
denotes the
(n-1)
-volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 9
.

For
n>2
, suppose that
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a complete, connected, simply connected, two-sided, stable minimal immersion with uniformly bounded curvature
|A_{M}|\leq K
. Then, for
p\in M
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}_{\textnormal{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
with the following properties:

(1)

u
is harmonic,
\Delta u=0
, on
M\setminus\{p\}
,

(2)

u>0
on
M\setminus\{p\}
and
\inf u=0
,

(3)

u(x)=c_{n}(1+o(1))d_{M}(x,p)^{2-n}
,
|\nabla u|(x)=(n-2)c_{n}(1+o(1))d_{M}(x,p)^{1-n}
, as well as
\left\langle\nabla|\nabla u|,\tfrac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right\rangle=(n-1)(n-2)c_{n}(1+o(1))d_{M}(x,p)^{-n}
as
x\to p
,

(4)

if
K

p

\int_{M\setminus K}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
,

(5)

u(x)\to 0
as
d_{M}(x,p)\to\infty

(6)

for all
s\in(0,\infty)

\Omega_{s}:=\{u\geq s\}\cup\{p\}

if

is a regular value of

\partial\Omega_{s}=u^{-1}(s):=\Sigma_{s}
is a closed connected hypersurface in
M
.

Proof.

If
M
is a flat
\mathbf{R}^{n}

u

\mathbf{R}^{n}
. As such, we assume below that
M
is not flat.

We construct a sequence of approximations to

\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\dots\subset M

\Omega_{i}

p\in\Omega_{i}

u_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\bar{\Omega}_{i}\setminus\{p\})

(1’)

\Omega_{i}\setminus\{p\}

(2’)

on

(3’)

u_{i}(x)=c_{n}(1+o(1))d_{M}(x,p)^{2-n}

This is simply the statement of existence of a Green’s function on a compact manifold with boundary.

\Omega_{1}

\tilde{u}
is harmonic,
\tilde{u}(\tilde{x})=a
and
\tilde{u}\leq a
. Thus, the maximum principle implies that
\tilde{u}\equiv a
. On the other hand, (
4
) implies that
\tilde{u}\equiv 0
. This is a contradiction, completing the proof of property (5).

Properties (3) and (5) imply property (6).

We now consider property (7). For
s
a regular value,
\Omega_{s}=\{u\geq s\}
is a compact set with smooth boundary
\Sigma_{s}=u^{-1}(s)
a closed (regular) hypersurface.

Claim
.

For
s\in(s_{0},\infty)
a regular value of
u
,
M\setminus\Omega_{s}
has exactly one component and it is unbounded.

Proof of the claim.

Assume that
M\setminus\Omega_{s}
has a bounded component
\Gamma
. Note that
0<u<s
on
\Gamma
and
u=s
on
\partial\Gamma
. Thus,
u
attains its minimum on the interior of
\Gamma
. This is a contradiction since
u
is not constant. This shows that
M\setminus\Omega_{s}
is disconnected then

has at least two ends. This would contradict the one-ended result of Cao–Shen–Zhu (cf. Proposition

).
∎

To finish the proof of (7), if
\Sigma_{s}
were disconnected, then by the claim we can construct a loop with non-trivial algebraic intersection with one of the components of
\Sigma_{s}
(concatenate a path connecting two of the components of

with one outside of

.
∎

2.1.
Integration over level sets of the Green’s function

Given

a complete, non-compact, connected, simply connected two-sided stable minimal immersion with uniformly bounded curvature. For

constructed in Proposition

. Set
\mathcal{R}
the regular values of

and
\mathcal{S}
the singular values of

. Recall that we have defined

. Below we will use this notation even when

is a singular value.

Lemma 10
.

The set of regular values
\mathcal{R}
is open and dense in
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Proposition
9
implies that
u:M\setminus\{p\}\to(0,\infty)
is proper. This easily is seen to imply openness of
\mathcal{R}
. Density follows from Sard’s theorem as usual.
∎

For any

we set

\Sigma_{s}^{*}=\{x\in M:u(x)=s,|\nabla u|(x)>0\}.

Note that
\Sigma_{s}^{*}
is a smooth (possibly incomplete) hypersurface in

. By Proposition

,
\dim_{\textrm{Haus}}(\Sigma_{s}\setminus\Sigma_{s}^{*})\leq n-2
. In particular, we see that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{s}^{*})<\infty
and for any function

,

\int fd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lfloor{\Sigma_{s}}=\int_{\Sigma_{s}^{*}}f

where the right hand side is taken with respect to the induced Riemannian volume form on
\Sigma_{s}^{*}
.

Lemma 11
.

For
f\in C^{0}_{\textnormal{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
, the function

is continuous.

Proof.

Cover
\Sigma_{s}\setminus\Sigma_{s}^{*}
by balls
B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
with
\sum_{i}r_{i}^{n-2+\eta}<\varepsilon
and
r_{i}\leq 1
. It is clear that

\delta\mapsto\int_{\Sigma_{s+\delta}\setminus\cup_{i}B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})}f

is continuous at
\delta=0
. Furthermore, by Proposition

, we have

\int_{\Sigma_{s+\delta}\cap\cup_{i}B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})}f\leq C\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))\leq C\sum_{i}r_{i}^{n-1}\leq C\varepsilon,

where the constant

is independent of

∎

We now consider the continuous functions

(5)

\displaystyle F(s):=\int_{\Sigma_{s}^{*}}|\nabla u|^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s):=\int_{\Sigma_{s}^{*}}|A_{M}|^{2},

defined for

.

Lemma 12
.

The function
F(s)
is locally Lipschitz on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

For a fixed compact subset

, consider
s<t
regular values of
u
with

. Below we will allow the Lipschitz constant to depend on
K
.

Consider the region
\Omega_{s,t}=u^{-1}([s,t])
. Observe that
\Omega_{s,t}
is a compact region with smooth boundary
\Sigma_{s}\cup\Sigma_{t}
. Write
\eta
for the outwards pointing unit normal to
\Omega_{s,t}
and note that
\eta=\tfrac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
along
\Sigma_{s}
and
\eta=-\tfrac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
. We have

\displaystyle F(s)-F(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{s,t}}\left\langle|\nabla u|\nabla u,\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{s,t}}\left\langle(|\nabla u|^{2}+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u,\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{s,t}}\Div\left((|\nabla u|^{2}+\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{s,t}}-\tfrac{1}{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},\nabla u\right\rangle.

Note that

using the Kato inequality. From this, we find

using the co-area formula (where

). Using Proposition

, to bound the volume of

, the assertion follows (the Lipchitz estimate for

∎

3.
Stern’s Bochner formula and stability

Recently, Stern has discovered
[
54
]
that one can combine the Bochner formula with the Schoen–Yau rearrangement
[
49
]
of the Gauss equation to relate the scalar curvature of a three-manifold to the behavior of a harmonic function on that manifold. In this section we consider this idea in the context of the stability operator for a stable minimal
3
-dimensional hypersurface (cf.
[
48
]
).

We consider
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
a complete, connected, simply connected, two-sided, stable minimal immersion with uniformly bounded curvature. For
p\in M
fixed, consider the Greens’ function
u\in C^{\infty}_{\textnormal{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
constructed in Proposition
9
. Recall the definition of
F(s),\mathcal{A}(s)
in (
5
) and (
6
). By Lemma
11
,
F(s),\mathcal{A}(s)
are continuous on
(0,\infty)
.

Proposition 13
.

For any function
\varphi\in C^{0,1}_{c}((0,\infty))
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s)^{2}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s)^{2}\,ds+\frac{4}{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}F(s)\,ds.

Proof.

Consider the following regularization of the square root of the gradient of
u

e_{\delta}=(|\nabla u|^{2}+\delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Note that
e_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
and

\nabla e_{\delta}=\tfrac{1}{4}e_{\delta}^{-3}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2},

as well as

\Delta e_{\delta}=\tfrac{1}{4}e_{\delta}^{-3}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{16}e_{\delta}^{-7}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}.

Applying the Bochner formula, we find

\displaystyle\Delta e_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-3}(|D^{2}u|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u))-\tfrac{3}{16}e_{\delta}^{-7}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-3}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{8}e_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)).

We now consider
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M\setminus\{p\})
and then take
f=e_{\delta}\psi
in the stability inequality (
1
) yielding

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}e_{\delta}^{2}\psi^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla e_{\delta}|^{2}f^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla e_{\delta}^{2},\nabla\psi^{2}\right\rangle+e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla e_{\delta}|^{2}f^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\psi^{2}\Delta e_{\delta}^{2}+e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-e_{\delta}\psi^{2}\Delta e_{\delta}+e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{8}e_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u))\psi^{2}+e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}.

Rearranging this yields

\displaystyle\int_{M}(|A_{M}|^{2}e_{\delta}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{8}e_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)))\psi^{2}\leq\int_{M}e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}.

Note that
\Ric_{M}\geq-|A_{M}|^{2}
by the Gauss equations. Furthermore, the improved Kato inequality yields

\tfrac{3}{8}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}\leq|\nabla u|^{2}|D^{2}u|^{2}\leq e_{\delta}^{4}|D^{2}u|^{2}.

From this, we find that

|A_{M}|^{2}e_{\delta}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{8}e_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u))\geq 0.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be an open subset of
(0,\infty)
containing all singular values of
u
, and
\mathfrak{A}=(0,\infty)\setminus\mathfrak{B}
. We thus find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{A}}(|A_{M}|^{2}e_{\delta}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{\delta}^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{8}e_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)))\psi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathfrak{A}}e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\int_{\mathfrak{B}}e_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}

We can send
\delta\to 0
and apply Fatou’s lemma to find

\displaystyle\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{A})}(|A_{M}|^{2}|\nabla u|+\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{-1}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)))\psi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{A})}|\nabla u||\nabla\psi|^{2}+\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}|\nabla u||\nabla\psi|^{2}

By the co-area formula, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}(|A_{M}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)))\psi^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)ds+\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}|\nabla u||\nabla\psi|^{2}.

For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
, take
\psi=\varphi(u)
to yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|A_{M}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u))\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)ds+\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{2}|\nabla u|^{3}

If
s\in\mathfrak{A}
, note that
\nu=\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
is a unit normal to
\Sigma_{s}
. We now follow the ideas used in
[
54
, Theorem 1]
. The traced Gauss equations yield

2\Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)=R_{M}-2K_{\Sigma_{s}}-|A_{\Sigma_{s}}|^{2}+H_{\Sigma_{s}}^{2}.

Similarly, using the Gauss equations for
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
we have
R_{M}=-|A_{M}|^{2}
. The scalar second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{s}
satisfies

A_{\Sigma_{s}}=|\nabla u|^{-1}D^{2}u|_{\Sigma_{s}}

so

|\nabla u|^{2}|A_{\Sigma_{s}}|^{2}=|D^{2}u|^{2}-2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}+D^{2}u(\nu,\nu)^{2}

and (because
u
is harmonic)

|\nabla u|^{2}H_{\Sigma_{s}}^{2}=D^{2}u(\nu,\nu)^{2}.

Thus,

\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}|A_{M}|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}K_{\Sigma_{s}}-\tfrac{1}{2}|D^{2}u|^{2}+|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

along
\Sigma_{s}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\tfrac{3}{4}|A_{M}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}|\nabla u|^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\tfrac{1}{2}K_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)ds+\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)ds+\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{2}|\nabla u|^{3}

By (6) in Proposition
9
, for
s\in\mathcal{R}
,
\Sigma_{s}
is connected, so
\int_{\Sigma_{s}}K_{\Sigma_{s}}\leq 4\pi
(by Gauss–Bonnet). Using this, the Kato inequality, and the definition of
F(s),\mathcal{A}(s)
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi(s)^{2}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi(s)^{2}\,ds+\frac{4}{3}\int_{\mathfrak{A}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}F(s)\,ds+\frac{4}{3}\int_{u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{2}|\nabla u|^{3}

Since
\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{2}|\nabla u|^{3}
is uniformly bounded, we can send
|\mathfrak{B}\cap\supp\varphi|\to 0
and conclude

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s)^{2}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s)^{2}\,ds+\frac{4}{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}F(s)\,ds.

A standard approximation argument for
\varphi
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 14
.

By using the improved Kato inequality it is easy to generalize the previous argument to prove that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s)^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\tfrac{3}{4}|A_{M}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{12}|A_{\Sigma_{s}}|^{2}-K_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}F(s)\,ds,

in

. However, we will not need this expression in the sequel.

We thus see that the behavior of

near

and

determines the potential test functions

that can be used in the inequality from Proposition

. The behavior of

.

It holds that

.

The bound as

Corollary 16
.

We have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell\searrow 0}\int_{\ell}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+t^{2}\int_{t}^{1}s^{-2}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds\leq O(t)+\frac{4}{3}\int_{t}^{1}t^{2}s^{-4}F(s)\,ds

as

.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\ell<t
, we consider the following function

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\begin{cases}0&s\in(0,\varepsilon\ell)\\
1-\tfrac{\log s-\log\ell}{\log\varepsilon}&s\in[\varepsilon\ell,\ell)\\

ts^{-1}&s\in[t,1)\\

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\,ds=O(t).

Using

as

from Lemma

, we can further estimate

\int_{0}^{\ell}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)^{2}F(s)\,ds=O(|\log\varepsilon|^{-2})\int_{\varepsilon\ell}^{\ell}s^{-1}\,ds=O(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}).

As such, taking

in Proposition

4.

Munteanu–Wang have recently established
[

a sharp monotonicity formula for the quantity

Second, we have to regularize their argument to account for our relatively weak

as

We continue to consider

in (

) and (
6

,

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be an open subset of
(0,\infty)
containing all singular values of

\mathfrak{A}=(0,\infty)\setminus\mathfrak{B}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{-\alpha}\left\langle\nabla j_{\delta},\nu\right\rangle-j_{\delta}\left\langle\nabla u^{-\alpha},\nu\right\rangle-\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}u^{-\alpha}\left\langle\nabla j_{\delta},\nu\right\rangle-j_{\delta}\left\langle\nabla u^{-\alpha},\nu\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{(t,\tau)\cap\mathfrak{A}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}u^{-\alpha}|\nabla u|^{-1}j_{\delta}^{-1}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}j_{\delta}^{-2}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{(t,\tau)\cap\mathfrak{A}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}u^{-\alpha}|\nabla u|^{-1}j_{\delta}^{-1}\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(t,\tau)\cap\mathfrak{A}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\alpha(\alpha+1)u^{-\alpha-2}j_{\delta}|\nabla u|\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{t,\tau}\cap u^{-1}(\mathfrak{B})}\alpha(\alpha+1)u^{-\alpha-2}j_{\delta}|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{-\alpha}j_{\delta}^{-1}\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)

Because
\alpha(\alpha+1)u^{-\alpha-2}j_{\delta}|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{-\alpha}j_{\delta}^{-1}\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t,\tau})
as
\delta\to 0
, we can send
\delta\to 0
and then
|\mathcal{B}|\to 0
to find

\displaystyle(t^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(t)+\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}F(t))-(\tau^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(\tau)+\alpha\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau))

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{t}^{\tau}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}u^{-\alpha}|\nabla u|^{-2}(|D^{2}u|^{2}-|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{\tau}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}u^{-\alpha}|\nabla u|^{-2}\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\alpha(\alpha+1)u^{-\alpha-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)ds.

Using Stern’s rearrangement
[
54
]
of the Bochner terms as in the proof of Proposition
13
we can write (along
\Sigma_{s}
,
s\in\mathcal{R}
)

|\nabla u|^{-2}\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|A_{M}|^{2}-K_{\Sigma_{s}}+|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{-2}|D^{2}u|^{2}

so (also using that
u
is constant along its level sets)

\displaystyle\left(t^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(t)+\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}F(t)\right)-\left(\tau^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(\tau)+\alpha\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|A_{M}|^{2}\right)+\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}K_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|D^{2}u|^{2}\right)ds+\int_{t}^{\tau}\alpha(\alpha+1)s^{-\alpha-2}F(s)ds.

By Gauss–Bonnet and Proposition
9
,

\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}K_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)ds\leq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi(t^{1-\alpha}-\tau^{1-\alpha}).

Furthermore, using the improved Kato inequalty, as well as the Cauchy–Schwarz and Hölder inequalities on (
8
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|D^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla u|^{-2}\left\langle\nabla|\nabla u|,\nu\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{3}{2}F(s)^{-1}F^{\prime}(s)^{2}

for
s\in\mathcal{R}
. Putting this together we find

\displaystyle\left(t^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(t)+\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}F(t)\right)-\left(\tau^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(\tau)+\alpha\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi(t^{1-\alpha}-\tau^{1-\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}(-\tfrac{3}{4}s^{-\alpha}F(s)^{-1}F^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\alpha(\alpha+1)s^{-\alpha-2}F(s))ds

Cauchy–Schwarz yields

2s^{-1}F^{\prime}(s)F(s)\leq\lambda^{-1}F^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\lambda s^{-2}F(s)^{2},

(where
\lambda=\lambda(\alpha)
as defined in (
7
)). Rearranging, we find

-\tfrac{3}{4}s^{-\alpha}F(s)^{-1}F^{\prime}(s)^{2}\leq-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda\tau^{-\alpha-1}F^{\prime}(s)+\tfrac{3}{4}\lambda^{2}s^{-\alpha-2}F(s).

Using this above we find

\displaystyle\left(t^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(t)+\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}F(t)\right)-\left(\tau^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(\tau)+\alpha\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi(t^{1-\alpha}-\tau^{1-\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}(-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda s^{-\alpha-1}F^{\prime}(s)+(\tfrac{3}{4}\lambda^{2}+\alpha(\alpha+1))s^{-\alpha-2}F(s))\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi(t^{1-\alpha}-\tau^{1-\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}(\tfrac{3}{4}\lambda^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda(\alpha+1)+\alpha(\alpha+1))s^{-\alpha-2}F(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau)+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda t^{-\alpha-1}F(t),

where we integrated by parts in the last step. Observe that

\tfrac{3}{4}\lambda^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda(\alpha+1)+\alpha(\alpha+1)=0

by (
7
), so we can rearrange this to read

\displaystyle\left(t^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(t)+(\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda)t^{-\alpha-1}F(t)\right)-\left(\tau^{-\alpha}F^{\prime}(\tau)+(\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda)\tau^{-\alpha-1}F(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi(t^{1-\alpha}-\tau^{1-\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi t^{1-\alpha}.

We thus find

\displaystyle\left(t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}F(t)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}4\pi t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds

where
C=C(\tau)
is bounded uniformly for
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0},2)
. By Lemma
12
we can integrate this on
(\ell,t)
for
\ell,t\in\mathcal{R}
,
\ell<t<\tau
, yielding

\displaystyle t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}F(t)

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}F(\ell)+\frac{C}{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+2)}4\pi t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\ell}^{t}\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\sigma^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,dsd\sigma

(where we have dropped several negative terms evaluated at
\ell
, cf. Lemma
17
). We apply Fubini’s theorem to write

\displaystyle\int_{\ell}^{t}\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\sigma^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,dsd\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\ell}^{t}\int_{\ell}^{s}\sigma^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,d\sigma ds+\int_{t}^{\tau}\int_{\ell}^{t}\sigma^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,d\sigma ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\int_{\ell}^{t}s^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds.

By Lemma
17
,
s^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\leq 1
for
s\in(0,1)
. Because
t<1
we can thus estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\ell}^{t}\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\sigma^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,dsd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\int_{\ell}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds.

On the other hand, by Lemmas
15
and
17
we have that

\ell^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}F(\ell)=O(\ell^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1})=o(1)

as
\ell\to 0
. Thus, we can pass to the limit as
\ell\searrow 0
to obtain

\displaystyle t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda}F(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+2)}4\pi t^{\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2(2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1)}\liminf_{\ell\searrow 0}\int_{\ell}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2(2\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+1)}t^{2\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+1}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-\alpha}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds.

Because
\alpha\mapsto\lambda(\alpha)
is continuous at

and

we can then send
\alpha\nearrow 2
to find

\displaystyle t^{-1}F(t)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2}+4\pi t+\frac{1}{4}\liminf_{\ell\searrow 0}\int_{\ell}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{4}t^{2}\int_{t}^{\tau}s^{-2}\mathcal{A}(s)\,ds

Because

, this yields the assertion.

Proof of Theorems

,
2
, and

Theorem
1
follows from Theorem
2
and the fact that there are no compact minimal surfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. To prove Theorems
2
and
3
, we briefly recall a standard point-picking argument (cf.
[
58
, Lecture 3]
) as follows: if Theorem
2
(or Theorem
3
) was not true, then we could find a sequence of two-sided, stable minimal immersed hypersurfaces
M_{i}
in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
(or in
(N^{4},g)
) and
p_{i}\in M_{i}
such that

|A_{M_{i}}(p_{i})|d_{M_{i}}(p_{i},\partial M_{i})=R_{i}\to\infty.

Here the distance is intrinsic on
M_{i}
. By considering an appropriate subset of
M_{i}
we can assume that
M_{i}
is compact and smooth up to its boundary. This allows us to assume that
p_{i}
maximizes
|A_{i}(x)|d_{M}(x,\partial M_{i})
. By translating and rescaling (we still denote the surfaces by
M_{i}
), we can ensure that
p_{i}=0
and
|A_{i}(0)|=1
. Then, for any
r<R_{i}
and
x\in M_{i}
with
d_{M_{i}}(0,x)\leq r
we find

|A_{M_{i}}(x)|\leq\frac{R_{i}}{d_{M_{i}}(x,\partial M_{i})}\leq\frac{R_{i}}{R_{i}-r},

and thus for each
r>0
,

\sup_{d_{M_{i}}(x,0)\leq r}|A_{M_{i}}(x)|\leq\frac{R_{i}}{R_{i}-r}\to 1.

Therefore
M_{i}
subsequentially converges smoothly to a complete, two-sided, stable minimal immersion
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
with
|A_{M}(0)|=1
and
|A_{M}(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\in M
(this last condition was not assumed
a priori
in the statement of Theorem
1
).

To show that such an immersion does not exist, we first pass to the universal cover to arrange that assume that
M
is simply connected (two-sided stability passes to any covering space by
[
26
, Theorem 1]
). We can construct the Green’s function
u\in C^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(M\setminus\{p\})
as in Proposition
9
and conclude that

F(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}

satisfies
F(t)\leq Ct^{2}
for all
t\in(0,\infty)
by Lemma
15
and Corollary
20
. Consider
f=\varphi\circ u
for
\varphi\in C^{0,1}_{c}((0,\infty))
in Proposition
27
and apply the co-area formula to write

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{3}\varphi(u)^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla(\varphi\circ u)|^{3}

\displaystyle=C\int_{M}\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{3}|\nabla u|^{3}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{3}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{3}F(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{3}s^{2}\,ds.

For
\rho\gg 0
choose

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}0&t\in[0,\rho^{-2})\\
2+\tfrac{\log t}{\log\rho}&t\in[\rho^{-2},\rho^{-1})\\

We find

\displaystyle\int_{\{\rho^{-1}\leq u\leq\rho\}}|A_{M}|^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\rho^{-2}}^{\rho^{-1}}\frac{s^{2}}{s^{3}|\log\rho|^{3}}\,ds+C\int_{\rho}^{\rho^{2}}\frac{s^{2}}{s^{3}|\log\rho|^{3}}\,ds=O(|\log\rho|^{-2}).

Letting

, we find that
A_{M}\equiv 0
. This completes the proof.

Appendix D
The Schoen–Simon–Yau

Schoen–Simon–Yau have obtained
L^{p}
estimates for the second fundamental form of a stable minimal hypersurface
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
in
[
44
]
. The following
L^{3}
estimate follows from their arguments in a straightforward manner but we could not find it in the literature (the estimate from
[
44
]
is only stated for

) so we include a proof.

Proposition 27
.

For
n<8
, if
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
a complete, connected, two-sided, stable minimal immersion then there is
C=C(n)
so that

\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{3}f^{3}\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{3}.

for any
f\in C^{0,1}_{0}(M)
.

Proof.

Set

a_{\delta}=(|A_{M}|^{2}+\delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

For
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
we can take
a_{\delta}f
in the stability inequality to find

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}a_{\delta}^{2}f^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\nabla a_{\delta}|^{2}f^{2}+f\left\langle\nabla a_{\delta}^{2},\nabla f\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{16}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|^{2}f^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}fa_{\delta}^{-2}\left\langle\nabla|A_{M}|^{2},\nabla f\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}

Multiplying Simons identity
[
52
]

|\nabla A_{M}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta|A_{M}|^{2}+|A_{M}|^{4}

by
a_{\delta}^{-2}f^{2}
and integrating by parts we find

\displaystyle\int_{M}a_{\delta}^{-2}|\nabla A_{M}|^{2}f^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{2}a_{\delta}^{-2}f^{2}\Delta|A_{M}|^{2}+a_{\delta}^{-2}|A_{M}|^{4}f^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{4}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|^{2}f^{2}-a_{\delta}^{-2}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{-2}|A_{M}|^{4}f^{2}

so

\displaystyle\int_{M}a_{\delta}^{-2}(|\nabla A_{M}|^{2}f^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}a_{\delta}^{-4}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|^{2}f^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-a_{\delta}^{-2}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{-2}|A_{M}|^{4}f^{2}.

Note that the improved Kato inequality reads

\tfrac{1}{4}(1+\tfrac{2}{n})|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|\leq|A_{M}|^{2}|\nabla A_{M}|^{2}\leq a_{\delta}^{4}|\nabla A_{M}|^{2}

so we can rearrange this into

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{2n}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|f^{2}\leq\int_{M}-a_{\delta}^{-2}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{-2}|A_{M}|^{4}f^{2}.

Because
a_{\delta}^{-2}\leq|A_{M}|^{-1}
we find

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{2n}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|f^{2}\leq\int_{M}-a_{\delta}^{-2}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}\right\rangle+|A_{M}|^{3}f^{2},

so we can combine this with stability to write

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tfrac{8-n}{16n}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|f^{2}\leq\int_{M}-\tfrac{1}{2}a_{\delta}^{-2}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}\right\rangle+a_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}

Because
n<8
we can use Cauchy–Schwartz to find
C=C(n)
so that

(11)

\int_{M}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|f^{2}\leq C\int_{M}a_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}

On the other hand, we can use Cauchy–Schwartz on (
10
) to find

(12)

\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{2}a_{\delta}^{2}f^{2}\leq\int_{M}\tfrac{17}{16}a_{\delta}^{-6}|\nabla|A_{M}|^{2}|^{2}f^{2}+\tfrac{17}{16}a_{\delta}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}.

Combining (
11
) and (
12
) and taking the limit as
\delta\to 0
(using the dominated convergence theorem) we find

\int_{M}|A_{M}|^{3}f^{2}\leq C\int_{M}|A_{M}||\nabla f|^{2}.

A standard argument shows that this holds for
f\in C^{0,1}_{c}(M)
. We can then replace
f
by
f^{\frac{3}{2}}
and using Hölder’s inequality to conclude the proof.
∎

An extension to almost stable minimal hypersurfaces

Theorem 28
.

\delta_{s}

Long Memory of Max-Stable Time Series as Phase Transition: Asymptotic Behaviour of Tail Dependence Estimators
Marco Oesting, Albert Rapp ??

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a simple estimator for tail dependence coefficients of a max-stable time series and show its asymptotic normality under a mild condition. The novelty of our result is that this condition does not involve mixing properties that are common in the literature. More importantly, our condition is linked to the transition between long and short range dependence (LRD/SRD) for max-stable time series. This is based on a recently proposed notion of LRD in the sense of indicators of excursion sets which is meaningfully defined for infinite-variance time series. In particular, we show that asymptotic normality with standard rate of convergence and a function of the sum of tail coefficients as asymptotic variance holds if and only if the max-stable time series is SRD.

Introduction

In extreme value analysis, one is typically interested in events which are extreme in the sense that a high threshold
u
is exceeded, i.e. events of the form
X_{0}>u
where
X_{0}
is part of a time series
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Although
\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)=0
, one studies the behaviour as
u\to\infty
because the limiting probability

\chi_{h}:=\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{h}>u\mid X_{0}>u)\in[0,1]

might be positive, provided that it exists.
Assuming that the limit indeed exists,
\chi_{h}
denotes the limiting probability that, conditional on an extreme event at time
t=0
, another extreme event occurs within
h\in\mathbb{Z}
time steps.
If
\chi_{h}>0
, the random variables
X_{0}
and
X_{h}
are called asymptotically dependent, while they are called asymptotically independent if
\chi_{h}=0
.
For a longer discussion on modelling asymptotic dependence using tail dependence coefficients such as
\chi_{h}
let us refer to Chapter 9.5 in
[
1
]
.

If
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a max-stable time series, it is well-known that
\chi_{h}
always exists and is of the form
\chi_{h}=2-\theta_{h}
,
h\in\mathbb{Z},
where
\theta_{h}\in[1,2]
denotes the corresponding pairwise extremal coefficient. The latter coefficient was first defined in
[
11
]
and is an important characteristic quantity in extreme value statistics.
In the max-stable case, it has been shown that
\theta_{h}=2
, or, equivalently,
\chi_{h}=0
if and only if
X_{0}
and
X_{h}
are fully independent.
Thus, components of a max-stable vector are either asymptotically dependent or fully independent and the pairwise extremal coefficients can be interpreted as a measure for the strength of dependence.
Hence, the pairwise extremal coefficient has gained a lot of attention and has been thoroughly studied in the literature, e.g. in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we will consider the case that
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a stationary max-stable time series with
\alpha
-Fréchet, i.e. heavy-tailed, marginal distributions and focus on the estimation of limits of conditional exceedance probabilities of the form
\mathbb{P}(X_{t_{1}}>u,\ldots,X_{t_{N}}>u\mid X_{0}>u)
as
u\to\infty
where
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is well-defined as the existence of the limit

\displaystyle\chi_{T}:

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u\text{ for all }t\in T\mid X_{0}>u)

(1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u\text{ for all }t\in T)}{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)}\in[0,1],

(2)

is ensured for any set
T=\{0,t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\}
by
Lemma

11
in the appendix.

In practice, estimation has to be based on a single realization of the time series up to time
n
, i.e. on dependent observations
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
.
A straight-forward estimator can be constructed by replacing the desired probabilities in (
1
) with relative frequencies, i.e. a natural estimator of
\chi_{T}
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{T,n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,n}(u_{n})}{\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})}:=\frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\}}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{0}\}}={}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\}}{\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{0}\}},

(3)

where
\mathbf{X}_{t+T}=\big(X_{t+s}\big)_{s\in T}
, for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence such that
u_{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{t_{N}})>0\,\Big|\,x_{0}>1\Big\},

(4)

\displaystyle C_{T}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{t_{N}})>0\,\Big|\,x_{i}>1\text{ for all }i\in T\Big\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{t_{N}+1}.

(5)

Note that we need
u=u_{n}
to depend on
n
with
u_{n}\to\infty
in order to get a good approximation of the limiting quantity
\chi_{T}
.
Furthermore, our estimator uses observations up until time point
n+t_{N}
rather than just
n
.
Asymptotically, the use of
t_{N}
more observations has no influence but eases notation.
Unfortunately, the larger
u_{n}
gets, the less data can be effectively used for our estimation since we do not count those data that are less than
u_{n}
.
Thus, the effective size of the sample that we use is only
\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\}
that is, on average,
n\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u_{n})
.
To make sure that the effective sample size grows to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
, a natural assumption is
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u_{n})=\infty
.

The main result of our paper is that we prove asymptotic normality of our estimator (
3
) in
6
.
The novelty of this result is that
6
characterizes the dependence of the underlying time series in terms of the weak assumption
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\chi_{t}=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
which is equivalent to the finiteness of the asymptotic variance of the estimator.
This assumption is weaker than common assumptions in terms of strong mixing properties involving
\alpha
-mixing coefficients.

More importantly, our condition
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
opens a connection to the phenomenon of long range dependence (LRD).
While this phenomenon has seen increasing relevance in other fields, there are only few results relating to LRD of max-stable time series.
Clearly, this is the case because LRD was previously investigated mainly for finite-variance processes in terms of auto-covariance functions.
However, there have been recent investigations into LRD for infinite-variance processes.
For example,
[
7
]
defines LRD in terms of the covariance of indicators of excursion sets, cf.
Definition

1
.
The advantage of this definition is that it allows to investigate LRD for infinite-variance processes because the covariance of indicators is always well-defined as the indicator functions are bounded.
Furthermore,
[
8
]
have recently shown that max-stable time series are LRD in the sense of
Definition

1
iff
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty
.
Hence, our condition
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
relates to the SRD case.

In addition, our condition marks the point of a phase transition.
Recently, Chapter 9 of
[
9
]
describes the phenomenon of LRD as a phase transition after which the convergence rate in a limit theorem changes magnitude.
In this paper, we show that the limiting variance of our estimator’s denominator undergoes such a phase transition as
X
becomes LRD.
Hence, our paper takes the first steps to investigate LRD in an extreme value statistics setting.
Furthermore, we round off our paper by discussing ways to extend our results into the realm of LRD even further.

Our paper is structured as follows.

Section

2
reviews well-known facts about max-stable time series that we need in order to prove our main result.

Section

3
investigates the limiting behavior of
\Var\big(\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})\big)
and
\Var\big(\,\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})\big)
for arbitrary integer sequences
b_{n}\rightarrow\infty
under the SRD assumption
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
.
In
Section

4
, we prove
5
that shows the asymptotic normality of
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
in the SRD-case.
This result is then used to prove our main result.

Section

5
proves the aforementioned phase transition and discusses conjectures on extending
6
to the LRD case.
Finally, auxillary lemmas and the proof of
5
can be found in
Section

A
.

2
Max-Stable Time Series

Let us review properties of max-stable time series and their extremal coefficients.
These properties are crucial to prove asymptotic normality of the estimators
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
which is a key step to prove our main result,
6
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a stationary max-stable time series with
\alpha
-Fréchet margins.
Then, by the spectral representation according to
[
6
]
,
one can write

X_{t}=\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{i}^{-\alpha}W_{t}^{(i)},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\Gamma_{1}<\Gamma_{2}<\ldots
are the arrival times of a unit rate Poisson process on
(0,\infty)
and, independently of this Poisson process,
W^{(1)},W^{(2)},\ldots
are independent copies of some stochastic process
W=(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
\mathbb{E}(W_{t}^{\alpha})=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A well-known consequence of this is that for any finite index set
T\subset\mathbb{Z}
, one obtains

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x_{t}\text{ for all }t\in T)=\exp\left(-\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{t\in T}\left(\frac{W_{t}}{x_{t}}\right)^{\alpha}\right\}\right),\quad(x_{t})_{t\in T}\in(0,\infty)^{|T|}.

(6)

It can be seen that the joint c.d.f. of
(X_{t})_{t\in T}
can be used to define a measure
\mu_{T}
on
E_{T}=[0,\infty)^{|T|}\setminus\{0\}
, the so-called exponent measure, via

\mu_{T}([0,x]^{c})=-\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x_{t}\text{ for all }t\in T)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{t\in T}\left(\frac{W_{t}}{x_{t}}\right)^{\alpha}\right\}

for
x=(x_{t})_{t\in T}\in(0,\infty)^{|T|}
.
By construction, the exponent measure
\mu_{T}
satisfies
\mu_{T}(A)<\infty
for any measurable set
A\subset E_{T}
bounded away from
0\in\mathbb{R}^{|T|}
and is homogeneous of order
-\alpha
, i.e.
\mu_{T}(cA)=c^{-\alpha}\mu_{T}(A)
for all
c>0
and the same sets
A
as before. Using this definition, Equation (
6
) implies that

\lim_{u\to\infty}u^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}((X_{t})_{t\in T}\in[0,ux]^{c})=\mu_{T}([0,x]^{c}),\quad x=(x_{t})_{t\in T}\in(0,\infty)^{|T|},

or, more generally,

\lim_{u\to\infty}u^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}((X_{t})_{t\in T}\in uA)=\mu(A)

for any measurable set
A\subset E_{T}
bounded away from
0\in\mathbb{R}^{|T|}
and satisfying
\mu(\partial A)=0
. In particular, this also ensures the existence of the limit

\chi_{T}=\frac{\mu_{\{0\}\cup T}((1,\infty)\times\ldots\times(1,\infty))}{\mu_{\{0\}}((1,\infty))}=\mu_{\{0\}\cup T}((1,\infty)\times\ldots\times(1,\infty)),

where we used that
\mu_{\{0\}}([1,\infty))=\lim_{u\to\infty}u^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)=1
according to the first part of Lemma
11
.

A dependence measure that is closely related to
\chi_{T}
is the extremal coefficient

\theta_{T}=\mu_{T}([0,1]^{c})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\max\nolimits_{t\in T}W_{t}^{\alpha}\right\}\in\big[1,\ |T|\,\big]

such that

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x\text{ for all }t\in T)=\exp\left(-\theta_{T}x^{-\alpha}\right),\quad x>0.

In the case that
T=\{0,h\}
with
h\neq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle\chi_{T}={}

\displaystyle\mu_{T}((1,\infty)\times(1,\infty))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu_{T}((1,\infty)\times[0,\infty)])+\mu_{T}([0,\infty)\times(1,\infty))-\mu_{T}([0,1]^{c})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu_{\{0\}}((1,\infty))+\mu_{\{h\}}((1,\infty))-\mu_{T}([0,1]^{c})=2-\theta_{T}=:2-\theta_{h}.

Here, we denoted
\theta_{T}=\theta_{h}
for
T=\{0,h\}
.
For more general finite index sets
T
, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{T}={}

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u\ \forall\ t\in T\setminus\{0\},X_{0}>u)}{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)}-\underbrace{\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u\ \forall\ t\in T\setminus\{0\})}_{\to 0}\bigg]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in T\setminus\{0\}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u\})}{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset\{0\}\cup(T\setminus\{0\})\\
0\in C,C\cap T\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\left(1+\theta_{C\cap(T\setminus\{0\})}-\theta_{C}\right).

Let us note that the last equality follows from
Lemma

11
with
A=\{0\}
and
B=T\setminus\{0\}
.

Finally, let us review the notion of LRD that was discussed in the introduction.
This notion is defined in terms of the covariance of indicators of excursion sets.

Definition 1

(
[
7
]
)
.

A real-valued stationary stochastic process
X=\{X(t),\,t\in T\}
where
T
is an unbounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
is short range dependent (SRD) if

\displaystyle\int_{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X(0)>u\},\mathds{1}\{X(t)>v\})\Big|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}v)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

(7)

for any finite measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
. Otherwise, i.e. if there exists a finite measure
\mu
such that the integral in inequality (
7
) is infinite,
X
is long range dependent.
For stochastic processes in discrete time, the integral
\int_{T}\,\mathrm{d}t
should be replaced by the summation
\sum_{t\in T}
.

Notice that this definition is defined even for infinite-variance processes.
Hence, it is also applicable to the max-stable time series we are investigating in this paper.
In fact, due to
[
8
]
it is known that a max-stable time series is LRD in the sense of
Definition

1
iff
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty
.
Furthermore, it is worth pointing out that this definition is invariant under strictly monotonic transformations.
This is desirable in an extreme value setting because it means that this notion of LRD does not depend on whether we use
\alpha
-Fréchet, Gumbel or Weibull margins.

3
Variance behavior

Let us consider the asymptotic variance behavior of
\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,n}(u_{n})
.
For our purposes it is suitable to replace
n
by a more flexible sequence
b_{n}
that fulfills
b_{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We will denote the corresponding estimators by
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
, respectively.
For the estimator
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
, we will see that the typical rate of convergence
\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}
holds if and only if the time series is short range dependent in the sense of
Definition

1
, i.e.
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a max-stable stationary time series
with
\alpha
-Fréchet margins and extremal coefficients
(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, for any real sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
and any integer sequence
b_{n}\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}p(u_{n})\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}-1\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=1+2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t}).

(8)

In particular, the variance is finite if and only if
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
.

Proof.

Using the stationarity, we calculate that

\displaystyle\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\sum_{j=1}^{b_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{b_{n}}\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{j}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{k}>u_{n}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Var(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}+\frac{2}{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}(b_{n}-t)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Var(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}+\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})

First Case:

\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty

In this case, we can analyze the two terms in

\displaystyle\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\Var(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}+\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\}).

separately.

Using that
\Var(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\})=\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u_{n})-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u_{n})^{2}=p(u_{n})-p(u_{n})^{2}
, the first term is equal to
1-p(u_{n})
which converges to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

For the second term, we notice that each summand is bounded by

|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})|\leq{}u_{n}^{-\alpha}(2-\theta_{t}),

due to the the second part of
Lemma

12
.
From the first part of
Lemma

12
, we know that
u_{n}^{-\alpha}/p(u_{n})
is bounded and by assumption we know that
(2-\theta_{t})
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, is summable.
Therefore, we can apply dominated convergence and obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})-p(u_{n})\big)=2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t}),

where we used that
\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})\to 2-\theta_{t}
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\left(\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}-1\right)\right)=1+2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t}).

Second Case:

\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty

We can use the fact
\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})\geq 0
as the max-stable time series
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is positively associated. Thus, for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
and sufficiently large
n
, we have

\displaystyle\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(u_{n})}\Var(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\})+\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\}).

Using the same calculations as above (here dominated convergence does not need to be applied as the sum consists of a fixed number of summands), we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)\geq 1+2\sum_{t=1}^{k}(2-\theta_{t}).

As this holds for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we finally get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)\geq 1+2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty.

∎

Next, let us consider the numerator of our estimator (
3
).
To do so, denote

\displaystyle\kappa_{t,T}=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(u^{-1}(\mathbf{X}_{0+T},\ \mathbf{X}_{t+T})\in C_{T}^{2})}{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)},\quad t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(9)

where
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a stationary max-stable time series and
C_{T}
is defined in (
5
).
These quantities always exist as they can be expressed in terms of the exponent measure of the underlying time series.

Proposition 3
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a max-stable stationary time series
with
\alpha
-Fréchet margins and pairwise extremal coefficients
(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Assume that
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
and choose a real sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
as well as an integer sequence
b_{n}\to\infty
.
Then, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)=\chi_{T}+2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t,T},

where
\kappa_{t,T}
is defined as in (
9
).

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{n}}\frac{1}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{s=0}^{b_{n}}\sum_{t=0}^{b_{n}}\Cov(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{s+T}\in C_{T}\},\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(u_{n})}\Var(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C_{T}\})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}(b_{n}-t)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C_{T}\},\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(u_{n})}\Var(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C_{T}\})

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C_{T}\},\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\})

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{p(u_{n})}\sum_{t=k+1}^{b_{n}}\left(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\right)\Cov(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C_{T}\},\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T}\})

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sum of the first and the second term converges to

\chi_{T}+2\sum_{t=1}^{k}\kappa_{t,T}

(details similar to the proof of Proposition

From

for some constant

.
As this bound goes to zero as

, taking the limit superior as
k\to\infty
gives the desired result.
∎

Remark 4
.

(a)

Clearly,
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t,T}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})
.
Hence, under the assumptions of

(b)

If

sufficiently fast, we have that the bias of

is asymptotically negligible.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}p(u_{n})\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}-\chi_{T}\right)^{2}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right).

(10)

This follows from
\chi_{T}=\chi_{\{0\},\,T\setminus\{0\}}
and
Lemma

12
(
b
) which implies that

\displaystyle\bigg|\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg]-\chi_{\{0\},\,T\setminus\{0\}}\bigg)\bigg|\leq\sqrt{b_{n}p(u_{n})^{3}}\bigg(1+K\frac{u_{n}^{-2\alpha}}{p(u_{n})^{2}}\bigg)

for some constant

.
The claim now follows from

11

(c)

From combining Prop.
2
and
3

\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})/p(u_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{}1\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})/p(u_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{}\chi_{T}

As we’ve concluded in the last section, the properly normalized numerator and denominator of our estimator (

In this section, we want to show that they are also asymptotically normally distributed.

Let

and

are the limiting variances as defined in Propositions

and

Proof.

For legibility this proof has been moved to
Section

A

∎

Finally, let us combine the results from

Theorem 6

Under the assumptions of

Remark 7
.

The novelty of this result is the weak assumption

Typically, asymptotic normality is proven under the assumption of strong mixing properties of the time series.
For example,

and

Proof.

First, let us show the following multivariate CLT:

for some constant

By the same assumption on the block length

Now, the CLT (

5
.

Next, let us show that

\displaystyle\sqrt{np(u_{n})}\bigg(\widehat{\chi}_{T,n}-\frac{\mathbb{P}(u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,F^{\prime}\Sigma F),

(12)

\displaystyle\sqrt{np(u_{n})}\bigg(\widehat{\chi}_{T,n}-\frac{\mathbb{P}(u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C_{T})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{np(u_{n})}\frac{p(u_{n})}{\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg(\frac{\big[\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,n}(u_{n})-P_{n}(C_{T})\big]p(u_{n})-\big[\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})-p(u_{n})\big]P_{n}(C_{T})}{p(u_{n})^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\sqrt{np(u_{n})}\frac{p(u_{n})}{\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg(1,\,\ -\frac{P_{n}(C_{T})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg)\frac{1}{p(u_{n})}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,n}(u_{n})-P_{n}(C_{T})\\

\displaystyle\frac{p(u_{n})}{\widehat{p}_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg(1,\,\ -\frac{P_{n}(C_{T})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg)\sqrt{\frac{n}{p(u_{n})}}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,n}(u_{n})-P_{n}(C_{T})\\

5
Long Range Dependence as Phase Transition

We have seen in the previous sections that our main results, Theorems
5
and
6
, hold if
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
.
Interestingly, this exact condition is indicative of a max-stable time series being long or short range dependent in the sense of
Definition

1
.
More precisely,
[
8
, Theorem 4.3]
shows that a max-stable time series is LRD in the sense of
Definition

1
iff
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty
.

In this section, we show that the limiting behavior of
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
undergoes a phase transition as the underlying time series becomes LRD.
Moreover, we close this section with an additional discussion of conjectures on how to extend our results even further.

2
shows that the asymptotic variance of the numerator is infinite if
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})=\infty
and the typical normalization
\sqrt{np(u_{n})}
is used.
This begs the question whether a different normalization of the denominator can yield a finite limiting variance in the case where
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{t})<\infty
.
And this is indeed the case.

Proposition 8
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a max-stable stationary time series with
\alpha
-Fréchet margins and pairwise extremal coefficients
(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Furthermore, assume that
2-\theta_{t}=Ct^{-\delta}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, for some constants
C>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Then, for any real sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
and any integer sequence
b_{n}\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}^{\delta}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)=\frac{2C}{(1-\delta)(2-\delta)}

(13)

Proof.

Since
\delta<1
, the first and second term converge to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Due to
Lemma

12
, we know for
t>0
that

\displaystyle(2-\theta_{t})\bigg(1-\frac{5u_{n}^{-2\alpha}}{2p(u_{n})}\bigg)\leq\frac{\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}\leq(2-\theta_{t})\bigg(1+\frac{5u_{n}^{-2\alpha}}{2p(u_{n})}\bigg)

By
Lemma

12
we know that
u_{n}^{-2\alpha}/p(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Var\Big(\sqrt{n^{\delta}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}2b_{n}^{\delta-1}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\bigg(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\bigg)\frac{\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{0}>u_{n}\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u_{n}\})}{p(u_{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}2b_{n}^{\delta-1}\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\bigg(1-\frac{t}{b_{n}}\bigg)(2-\theta_{t})

\displaystyle=2C\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\frac{1}{b_{n}}\bigg(\frac{t}{b_{n}}\bigg)^{-\delta}-\sum_{t=1}^{b_{n}-1}\frac{1}{b_{n}}\bigg(\frac{t}{b_{n}}\bigg)^{-\delta+1}\bigg]

\displaystyle=2C\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\delta}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}x^{1-\delta}\mathrm{d}x\bigg]=\frac{2C}{(1-\delta)(2-\delta)}

∎

Remark 9
.

Clearly, the convergence rate of the variance of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
is bounded by the convergence rate of the variance of
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
.
Hence, there exists a constant
C\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Var\left(\sqrt{b_{n}^{\delta}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\right)=C.

Thus, the properly normalized variance of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
is bounded by a finite constant, possibly zero.

As for our ratio estimator
\widehat{\chi}_{T,b_{n}}
, we conjecture that its limiting variance uses the same rate of convergence as the denominator.
This could possibly shown with a Delta-method argument if assumptions are appropriately chosen such that remainder terms of the resulting Taylor expansion are negligible.

Conjecture 10
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}

.

and

.

Finally, in light of

,

A
Proof of

In this section, we are going to prove
5
.
To do so, let us first show the following two lemmas.

Lemma 11
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a max-stable stationary time series
with
\alpha
-Fréchet margins and extremal coefficients
\theta_{T}
,
T\subset\mathbb{Z}
finite.
Then, it holds that

(a)

\lim_{u\to\infty}u^{-\alpha}/\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)=1
.

(b)

For every pair of disjoint finite subsets
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}
, the limit

\displaystyle\chi_{A,B}:=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})}{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)}

exists and is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{A,B}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right).

Proof.

1.

Follows directly from
\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)=1-\exp(-u^{-\alpha})
and a Taylor expansion of the function
x\mapsto\exp(-x)
around
0
.

2.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we obtain

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u,\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u,\,\forall t\in B\})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{r}>u\,\forall r\in A\cup B)-\mathbb{P}(X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A)\mathbb{P}(X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in A)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Big(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{r}\leq u\text{ for some }r\in A\cup B)\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\leq u\text{ for some }s\in A)\Big)\Big(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq u_{n}\text{ for some }t\in B)\Big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq C^{\prime}\subset A\cup B}(-1)^{|C^{\prime}|}\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(C^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\bigg(1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq A^{\prime}\subset A}(-1)^{|A^{\prime}|}\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(A^{\prime})\bigg)\bigg(1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq B^{\prime}\subset B}(-1)^{|B^{\prime}|}\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(B^{\prime}))\bigg),

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u,\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u,\,\forall t\in B\})

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,\\
C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\Big[(-1)^{|C|}\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(C)-(-1)^{|C\cap A|+|C\cap B|}\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(C\cap A)\mathbb{P}_{X,u}(C\cap B)\Big]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,\\
C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\Big(\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap A})\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap B})\Big).

(14)

By applying the Taylor expansion of the function
x\mapsto\exp(-x)
around

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u,\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u,\,\forall t\in B\})

The following lemma will give us two crucial auxiliary results to prove asymptotic normality.
The first result bounds the covariance of indicators.

Lemma 12
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}

\alpha
-Fréchet margins and extremal coefficients

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})|

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\chi_{A,B}|

\displaystyle u^{-2\alpha}\left(|A|+|B|+\frac{1}{2}\right)2^{|A|+|B|-2}\sum_{s\in A}\sum_{t\in B}(2-\theta_{|s-t|})

We begin to prove the first part. Equation (
14

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u,\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u,\,\forall t\in B\})|

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\Big[\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap A})\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap B})\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\Big|\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap A})\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap B})\Big|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C}}^{u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B})}\exp(-x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C}}^{u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B})}1\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}u^{-\alpha}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right).

Now, we notice that, for arbitrary sets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, we have that, by Equation (15) in
[

]
,

\displaystyle 0\leq{}

\displaystyle\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}+\theta_{A_{3}}-\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}+\theta_{A_{3}}-\left(\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}+\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{3}}+\theta_{A_{2}\cup A_{3}}-\theta_{A_{1}}-\theta_{A_{2}}-\theta_{A_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(\theta_{A_{1}}+\theta_{A_{3}}-\theta_{A_{1}\cup A_{3}})+(\theta_{A_{2}}+\theta_{A_{3}}-\theta_{A_{2}\cup A_{3}}).

Applying this bound iteratively to sets of the type
A_{1}=\{s\}

for

\theta_{\{t\}}=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we eventually obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in A\cap C}\sum_{t\in B\cap C}(\theta_{\{s\}}+\theta_{\{t\}}-\theta_{\{s,t\}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in A\cap C}\sum_{t\in B\cap C}(2-\theta_{|s-t|}).

(16)

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})|

\displaystyle\leq{}u^{-\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{s\in A\cap C}\sum_{t\in B\cap C}(2-\theta_{|s-t|}).

The assertion follows from the fact that, for each
(s,t)\in A\times B
, there exist

2^{|A|-1}
subsets of
A
that contain
s
and
2^{|B|-1}
subsets of
B
that contain
t
.

Next, let us prove the second part.
Plugging in Equation (
14
) and the expression for
\chi_{A,B}

from the second part of
Lemma

11
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\chi_{A,B}|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\Big[\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap A})\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C\cap B})\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u_{n})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|C|}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\Big|\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)\Big|.

(17)

From
x-x^{2}/2\leq 1-e^{-x}\leq x
for all
x>0
, it follows that
u^{-\alpha}-u^{-2\alpha}/2\leq\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\leq u^{-\alpha}
.
Consequently, we can bound

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\chi_{A,B}|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\max\Big\{|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-(u^{-\alpha}-u^{-2\alpha}/2)\chi_{A,B}|,

\displaystyle\qquad|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-u^{-\alpha}\chi_{A,B}|\Big\}

Then, applying analogous calculations as in (
17
) to both parts of the maximum yields

\displaystyle|\Cov(\mathds{1}\{X_{s}>u\,\forall s\in A\},\mathds{1}\{X_{t}>u\,\forall t\in B\})-\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>u)\chi_{A,B}|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,\\
C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\Big|\exp(-u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C})-\exp(-u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}))-u^{-\alpha}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)u^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C}}^{u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B})}(1-e^{-x})\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}}{2}u^{-2\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\bigg[\int_{u^{-\alpha}\theta_{C}}^{u^{-\alpha}(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B})}x\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}}{2}u^{-2\alpha}\bigg]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{-2\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left[(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B})^{2}-\theta_{C}^{2}+\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)\right]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{-2\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left[(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}+\theta_{C}+1)\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle u^{-2\alpha}\left(|A|+|B|+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset A\cup B\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,C\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left(\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}\right),

where we used
\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C}=\theta_{C\cap A}+\theta_{C\cap B}-\theta_{C\cap(A\cup B)}\geq 0
by inequality (
15
) and
\theta_{M}\leq|M|
for all finite sets
M\subset\mathbb{Z}
.
The assertion follows by employing Equation (
16
) analogously to the proof of the first part of the lemma.
∎

This proof follows the same large/small block argument as given in the proof of Theorem 3.2 in
[
5
]
.
We prove only the limit theorem for
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})
.
The result for
\widehat{p}_{b_{n}}(u_{n})
follows analogously.
Let us start by denoting
C:=C_{T}
,
p_{0}:=\mathbb{P}(u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{0+T}\in C)
and

\displaystyle Y_{n,t}:=\big(np(u_{n})\big)^{-1/2}\Big(\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C\}-p_{0}\Big),\quad t=1,\ldots,n.

Now, we want to consider
k_{n}
large blocks of size
b_{n,1}
. To this end, choose
b_{n,1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{n}=n/b_{n,1}
is a positive integer and
b_{n,1}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, for technical reasons that will become clear in the last part of this proof, we assume that
b_{n,1}^{2}/(np(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
. Note that this can always be achieved by letting
b_{n,1}
grow sufficiently slowly. In addition to
b_{n,1}
we choose small block sizes
b_{n,2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
b_{n,2}\to\infty
, but
b_{n,2}/b_{n,1}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Using these block sizes we can define blocks

\displaystyle I_{n,i}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(i-1)b_{n,1}+1,\ldots,ib_{n,1}\big\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{n,i}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(i-1)b_{n,1}+b_{n,2}+1,\ldots,ib_{n,1}\big\}

\displaystyle J_{n,i}

\displaystyle:=I_{n,i}\setminus\widetilde{I}_{n,i}=\big\{(i-1)b_{n,1}+1,\ldots,(i-1)b_{n,1}+b_{n,2}\big\}

for
i=1,\ldots,k_{n}
.
For any index sex
B\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
we write
S_{n}(B)=\sum_{t\in B}Y_{n,t}
.

Next, let us show that the small blocks
J_{n,i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k_{n}
, are asymptotically negligible, i.e.

\displaystyle\Var\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}S_{n}(J_{n,i})\Big)\rightarrow 0

(18)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\Var\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}S_{n}(J_{n,i})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k_{n}\Var(S_{n}(J_{n,1}))+2\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\Cov\big(S_{n}(J_{n,i}),S_{n}(J_{n,j})\big)

\displaystyle=:P_{1}+P_{2}.

Thus, let us show that both
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
converge to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
By
3
and
b_{n,2}/b_{n,1}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, it follows that that

\displaystyle P_{1}=\frac{b_{n,2}}{b_{n,1}}\Var\bigg(\sqrt{b_{n,2}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n,2}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg)\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Furthermore, we get

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\Cov\big(S_{n}(J_{n,i}),S_{n}(J_{n,j})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{np(u_{n})}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\sum_{s\in J_{n,i}}\sum_{t\in J_{n,j}}\Cov\big(\mathds{1}\big\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{s+T}\in C\big\},\mathds{1}\big\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C\big\}\big)

As the index set
T
is bounded and the block distance
b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}
between two subsequent small blocks tends to infinity as
n\to\infty
, the index set
s+T
and
t+T
in the sum above are all disjoint for sufficiently large
n
.
Hence, we can apply the first part of
Lemma

11
.
Counting the pairwise distances between elements of the blocks
J_{n,i}
and
J_{n,j}
we get that there is a constant
K>0
such that

\displaystyle P_{2}\leq 2K\frac{u_{n}^{-\alpha}}{p(u_{n})}\frac{b_{n,2}}{b_{n,1}}\sum_{h=b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}^{n}(2-\theta_{h})\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
since
\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}(2-\theta_{h})<\infty
.
Consequently, we have proven (
18
) and the limiting distribution of
S_{n}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,i})
are equal if the limit exists.

Now, let us consider iid. copies
\widetilde{S_{n}}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})
,
l=1,\ldots,k_{n}
, of
S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,1})
.
Using a telescoping series we get for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
that

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{l=1}^{k_{n}}e^{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{l=1}^{k_{n}}e^{it\widetilde{S}_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}\bigg]\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\prod_{s=1}^{l-1}e^{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})}\bigg)\bigg(e^{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}-e^{it\widetilde{S}_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{s=l+1}^{k_{n}}e^{it\widetilde{S}_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\bigg(\prod_{s=1}^{l-1}e^{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})}\bigg)\bigg(e^{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}-e^{it\widetilde{S}_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\bigg|\Cov\bigg(\exp\bigg\{it\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\bigg\},\exp\Big\{itS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big\}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\bigg[\Big|\Cov\bigg(\cos\Big(t\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\Big),\cos\Big(tS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big)\bigg)\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|\Cov\bigg(\cos\Big(t\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\Big),\sin\Big(tS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big)\bigg)\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|\Cov\bigg(\sin\Big(t\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\Big),\cos\Big(tS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big)\bigg)\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|\Cov\bigg(\sin\Big(t\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\Big),\sin\Big(tS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big)\bigg)\Big|\bigg]

(19)

Next, let us bound each of those covariances.
To do so, let us rewrite

\displaystyle\Cov\bigg(\cos\Big(t\sum_{s=1}^{l-1}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,s})\Big),\cos\Big(tS_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Cov\bigg(f\Big\{\frac{t}{\sqrt{np(u_{n})}}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C\},\ t\in\bigcup_{s=1}^{l-1}\widetilde{I}_{n,s}\Big\},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\widetilde{f}\Big\{\frac{t}{\sqrt{np(u_{n})}}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C\},\ t\in\widetilde{I}_{n,l}\Big\}\bigg)

(20)

where
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{u})=\cos\big(\sum_{k=1}^{u}x_{k}-A_{1}\big)
,
\widetilde{f}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{v})=\cos\big(\sum_{k=1}^{v}x_{k}-A_{2}\big)
for some constants
A_{1},A_{2}>0
.
Clearly,
f
and
\widetilde{f}
are bounded Lipschitz functions with Lipschitz constants
\Lip(f),\Lip(\widetilde{f})\leq 1
.
Furthermore, we know that the sequence
\big\{X_{t},t\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
is associated as a max-stable process.
Now, it is easy to show that
\big\{\mathbf{X}_{t+T},t\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
is associated as well by applying the definition of association.
Furthermore, it follows from
[
2
, Thm. 1.1.18(d)]
that

\big\{\frac{t}{\sqrt{np(u_{n})}}\mathds{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{t+T}\in C\},t\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}

is associated.
Thus, we can apply Theorem 1.5.3 from
[
2
]
and bound covariance (
20
) by

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{np(u_{n})}\sum_{k_{1}\in\bigcup_{s=1}^{l-1}\widetilde{I}_{n,s}}\sum_{k_{2}\in\widetilde{I}_{n,l}}\bigg|\Cov\Big(\mathds{1}\big\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{k_{1}+T}\in C\big\},\mathds{1}\big\{u_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{k_{2}+T}\in C\big\}\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2Kt^{2}\frac{u_{n}^{-\alpha}}{p(u_{n})}\frac{b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}(2-\theta_{kb_{n,1}+b_{n,2}+j}),

where
K
is a positive constant.
Notice that we have used
Lemma

12
here and counted the pairwise distances again.
Thus, we can bound the sum of covariances (
19
) via

(
19
)

\displaystyle\leq 8Kt^{2}\frac{u_{n}^{-\alpha}}{p(u_{n})}\frac{b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}(2-\theta_{kb_{n,1}+b_{n,2}+j})

\displaystyle\leq 8Kt^{2}\frac{u_{n}^{-\alpha}}{p(u_{n})}\frac{b_{n,1}-b_{n,2}}{n}k_{n}\sum_{j=b_{n,2}}^{n}(2-\theta_{j})

\displaystyle=8Kt^{2}\frac{u_{n}^{-\alpha}}{p(u_{n})}\bigg(1-\frac{b_{n,2}}{b_{n,1}}\bigg)\sum_{j=b_{n,2}}^{n}(2-\theta_{j})\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore,
\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}S_{n}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})
and
\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\widetilde{S_{n}}(\widetilde{I}_{n,l})
have the same limiting distributions (provided that the limit exists).

Now, let
\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{n,l})
,
l=1,\ldots,k_{n}
be iid copies of
S_{n}(I_{n,1})
.
By the same arguments as before it suffices to show

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,l})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
First, notice that the limiting variance is correct by
3
since

\displaystyle\Var\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{k_{n}}\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,l})\bigg)=k_{n}\Var\big(\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})\big)=\Var\bigg(\sqrt{b_{n,1}p(u_{n})}\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{T,b_{n}}(u_{n})}{p(u_{n})}\bigg).

Thus, we need to check the Lindeberg condition of the CLT for triangular arrays of iid. mean-zero random variables, i.e. we need to verify

\displaystyle k_{n}\mathbb{E}\Big[\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})^{2}\,\mathds{1}\big\{|\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})|>\varepsilon\big\}\Big]\rightarrow 0,\qquad(n\rightarrow\infty),

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Using Chebyshev’s inequality and the fact that
|\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})|\leq b_{n,1}/\sqrt{np(u_{n})}
, we get

\displaystyle k_{n}\mathbb{E}\Big[\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})^{2}\,\mathds{1}\big\{|\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})|>\varepsilon\big\}\Big]\leq\frac{b_{n,1}^{2}}{np(u_{n})}\frac{k_{n}\Var\big(\widetilde{S_{n}}(I_{n,1})\big)}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Since we have assumed that

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the RHS converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
This completes the proof. ∎

Chiral limit of twisted trilayer graphene
Simon Becker, Tristan Humbert, Jens Wittsten, Mengxuan Yang

Abstract
. We initiate the mathematical study of the Bistritzer-MacDonald Hamiltonian for twisted trilayer graphene in the chiral limit (and beyond). We develop a spectral theoretic approach to investigate the presence of flat bands under specific magic parameters. This allows us to derive trace formulae that show that the tunnelling parameters that lead to flat bands are nowhere continuous as functions of the twisting angles.

Twisted trilayer graphene (TTG) is a material formed by stacking three sheets of graphene, with slight relative twisting angles
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
between layers one and two, and two and three, respectively. This stacking arrangement gives rise to a captivating interference pattern known as a moiré pattern (see Figure
2
). The electron tunnelling described by this pattern leads to significant modifications in the electronic properties of the material, such as flat bands at certain magic parameters, see Figure
1
.

Each layer of graphene is composed of carbon atoms arranged in a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice, featuring two distinct atom types, A and B, per fundamental domain. At sites where neighboring layers (top and middle or middle and bottom) align, inter-layer interactions between atoms of the same type, referred to as AA sites, occur. Additionally, tunnelling interactions between atoms of different types take place at AB and BA sites, where atoms of type A are stacked over atoms of type B, and vice versa.

The exploration of different graphene layer configurations, including twisted trilayer graphene, expands on the research conducted on twisted bilayer graphene
[
TKV19
,
BEWZ21
,
BEWZ22
,
BHZ1-22
,
BHZ2-22
,
BHZ23
]
. These multilayer systems offer increased tunability due to a larger set of parameters
[
KKAJ19
,
LVK22
]
. We also want to mention recent progress on a helical version of twisted trilayer graphene
[
Dev23
,
GMM23a
,
GMM23b
]
. The study of the continuum or Bistritzer-MacDonald (BM) model for twisted trilayer graphene exhibits, unlike the BM model for bilayer graphene
[
BM11
]
, commensurable and incommensurable twisting angles (see Assumption
1
). Thus, TTG serves as an example for a whole range of materials where commensurability matters. The initial theoretical analysis of twisted trilayer graphene revealed a similar phenomenon of electronic bands flattening at various
magic
angles. This breakthrough subsequently led to experimental observations of correlated phenomena
[

In this paper we study the BM model of twisted trilayer graphene described by the Hamiltonian

H(\alpha,\tilde{\alpha})=\begin{pmatrix}W(\tilde{\alpha})&D(\alpha)^{*}\\
D(\alpha)&W(\tilde{\alpha})\end{pmatrix}

(1.1)

\begin{split}D(\alpha)&=\begin{pmatrix}2D_{\bar{z}}&\alpha_{12}U(pz)&0\\

The Floquet-Bloch decomposition of

\Spec_{L^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}H(\alpha,\widetilde{\alpha})=\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}}\Spec_{L^{2}_{0}}H_{k}(\alpha,\widetilde{\alpha})

For each

\begin{gathered}\Spec_{L^{2}_{0}}H_{k}(\alpha)=\{E_{\pm j}(k,\alpha)\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\quad E_{-j}(\alpha,k)=-E_{j}(\alpha,k),\\

(1.10)

We refer to the image of

Having these three protected states, we can define their Wronskian

W(\alpha):=\det[\varphi,\psi,\rho],

Theorem 1

(Flat bands & Wronskian)
.

The parameter
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is magic if and only if the Wronskian vanishes:

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\iff W(\alpha)=0.

Following
[
BEWZ22
]
and
[
BHZ1-22
]
, we will use an alternative characterisation, for
\alpha_{12}\neq 0
, in terms of the compact and non-normal operator which we call the
Birman-Schwinger operator
defined by

B_{k}(\tfrac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}):=R(k)U(-pz)R(k)U(pz)+\Big(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}\Big)^{2}(R(k)U(p\tfrac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}z)R(k)U(-p\tfrac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}z))

(1.15)

with
R(k)=(2D_{\bar{z}}+k)^{-1}
. The set of magic parameters is then characterised by

Theorem 2

(Characterisation via the Birman-Schwinger operator)
.

The parameter
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus(\{0\}\times{\mathbb{C}})
is magic if and only if
\frac{1}{\alpha_{12}^{2}}\in\Spec(B_{k}(\tfrac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}))
for
k\notin\Gamma^{*}.

The above characterisation is explicit up to fixing the hopping ratio
\tfrac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}},
i.e., the set of all magic parameters is a union over all possible ratios
\tfrac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}.
In practice, one may just assume that
\alpha_{12}=\alpha_{23}
for standard TTG.
The Birman-Schwinger operator

is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, which implies that for
\ell\geq 2
all traces
\tr(B_{k}^{2\ell})
are well-defined and moreover

\tr(B_{k}^{\ell})=\sum_{\alpha_{12}\in\mathcal{A}(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}})}\frac{1}{\alpha_{12}^{2\ell}},\quad\ell\geq 2

where we denoted by
\mathcal{A}(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}})
the set of magic parameters for the fixed hopping ratio
\alpha_{23}/\alpha_{12}
(see §
3.2
for a precise definition).
We can therefore try to understand the structure of the set of magic parameters
\mathcal{A}(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}})
by understanding the traces of the operator

. Similarly to what was done in
[
BHZ1-22
]
, we provide, in Theorem
6
, semi-explicit formulas for these traces. These expressions are enough to prove the following result.

Theorem 3
.

For the potential given in (
2.4
) with finite Fourier expansion with coefficients in
c_{n,m}\in\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
and with the additional symmetry

\overline{U(\bar{z})}=U(z)

, we have for any

that
\tr(B_{k}^{\ell})=q_{\ell}\pi/\sqrt{3}
, where

. As a consequence, if the set of magic parameters
\mathcal{A}(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}})
is non-empty, it is infinite.

We will actually prove statements for more general potentials in Theorems
7
and Theorem
8
.

We then investigate the continuity of magic parameters. Our main result shows that the set of magic parameters is maximally discontinuous when changing continuously the twisting angles
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}

In particular, the trace
\mathcal{S}_{4}(r,\zeta_{1}^{(n)},\zeta_{2}^{(n)})

2.
The twisted trilayer graphene Hamiltonian

2.1.
Derivation

We start with the continuum model for twisted trilayer graphene with coupling parameters
\beta,\tilde{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,

\begin{split}&\mathscr{H}(\beta,\tilde{\beta})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{D}&T_{12}(\zeta_{1}z)&0\\
T_{12}(\zeta_{1}z)^{*}&H_{D}&T_{23}(\zeta_{2}z)\\
0&T_{23}(\zeta_{2}z)^{*}&H_{D}\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }T_{ij}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\beta}_{ij}V(z)&(\beta_{ij}U(-z))^{*}\\
\beta_{ij}U(z)&\tilde{\beta}_{ij}V(z)\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Here,
H_{D}
is a Dirac operator
H_{D}=D_{x_{1}}\sigma_{1}+D_{x_{2}}\sigma_{2}
, and
\mathscr{H}
acts on a wavefunction

\psi=(\psi_{\uparrow,A},\psi_{\uparrow,B},\psi_{\rightarrow,A},\psi_{\rightarrow,B},\psi_{\downarrow,A},\psi_{\downarrow,B})

with lattice
\uparrow,\rightarrow,\downarrow
(upper, middle, lower) and sublattice index
A,B

Writing this operator in the basis in which

Proposition 3.3

We have

\Spec D(\alpha)=\Spec D(-\alpha)=\Spec D(\bar{\alpha})\quad\text{ and hence }\quad\mathcal{A}=-\mathcal{A}=\overline{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

Conjugating

3.3.
Theta function argument

To construct the entire flat band from a single Bloch function with a zero, one can use the structure of
D(\alpha)
and employ theta functions for this construction.
To this end, we let
\theta
be the Jacobi theta function

\theta(\zeta):=\theta_{1}(\zeta|\omega):=-\theta_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(\zeta|\omega)
given by

\begin{gathered}\theta(\zeta)=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(\pi i(n+\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\omega+2\pi i(n+\tfrac{1}{2})(\zeta+\tfrac{1}{2})),\ \ \ \theta(-\zeta)=-\theta(\zeta),\\
\theta(\zeta+m)=(-1)^{m}\theta(\zeta),\ \ \theta(\zeta+n\omega)=(-1)^{n}e^{-\pi in^{2}\omega-2\pi i\zeta n}\theta(\zeta),\end{gathered}

so that
\theta
has simple zeros at
{\mathbb{Z}}+\omega{\mathbb{Z}}
(and no other zeros) – see
[
Mu83
]
.

We now define

F_{k}(z):=e^{-\frac{i}{2}(\bar{\omega}z+\bar{z})k}\frac{\theta(\frac{3z}{4\pi i\omega}+\frac{k}{\sqrt{3}\omega})}{\theta\Big(\frac{3z}{4\pi i\omega}\Big)}.

A computation shows that
F_{k}(z+a)=F_{k}(z)
for
a\in\Gamma_{3}
.
We also define

G_{k}(z):=e^{\frac{i}{2}(\bar{k}-k\bar{\omega})z}\frac{\theta(\frac{3z}{4\pi i\omega}+\frac{k}{\sqrt{3}\omega})}{\theta\Big(\frac{3z}{4\pi i\omega}\Big)}

satisfying
G_{k}(z+a)=e^{i\langle a,k\rangle}G_{k}(z)
for
a\in\Gamma_{3}
. Note that in particular we have
\tau(k)F_{k}(z)=G_{k}(z)
, where
\tau
is defined in equation (
2.6
). If
u\in L^{2}_{k^{\prime}}
is a solution to
D(\alpha)u=0
with
u(z_{*})=0
(cf. Lemma
8.1
), then for any
k\in{\mathbb{C}}
we can construct

(D(\alpha)+k)(F_{k}(z-z_{*})u(z))=0\text{ and }D(\alpha)(G_{k}(z-z_{*})u(z))=0.

(3.8)

In particular, multiplication by
F_{k}
leaves
L^{2}_{k^{\prime}}
invariant and multiplication by
G_{k}
maps
L^{2}_{k^{\prime}}
to
L^{2}_{k^{\prime}+k}.

Remark 4
.

We shall refer to equation (

) as the

3.4.
Flat bands via Wronskians

Here we prove Theorem
1
, which gives an alternative characterisation of magic parameters
\mathcal{A}
as the zero set of the Wronskian
W(\alpha)
in (
1.14
) of the protected states.

We devote the rest of this section to proving the theorem. First note that the Wronskian
W(\alpha)
is constant in
z
, as it is periodic and satisfies equation
D_{\bar{z}}W=0
.
Thus we can consider the Wronskian
W(\alpha)
at distinguished points which are invariant under rotation by
\omega
modulo
\Gamma_{3}
:

z_{S}:=\tfrac{1}{3}(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})=\tfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{9},\quad\gamma_{j}=\tfrac{4\pi i}{3}\omega^{j}\in\Gamma_{3},\ \ j=1,2.

In fact,
\omega(\pm z_{S})=\pm z_{S}\mp\gamma_{2}
shows that
\{0,\pm z_{S}\}
is fixed under rotation by
\omega
as a set of elements in
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}
, and we will refer to them as

.
These are precisely the points in
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}
corresponding to the distinguished points
\mathcal{K}=\{-ir:r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}
given in (
2.8
). Indeed,
\Gamma_{3}^{*}=\sqrt{3}(\omega{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\omega^{2}{\mathbb{Z}})
, so
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}
is obtained from
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}
through multiplication by
3\sqrt{3}/(4\pi i)
. Hence,
\pm z_{S}
corresponds to

\pm z_{S}\cdot\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi i}=\mp i\in{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}.

Now we consider
u\in L^{2}_{r,0}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
, then

\begin{split}u(\pm z_{S})&=\mathscr{C}u(\pm z_{S})=u(\pm\omega z_{S})=u(\pm z_{S}\mp\gamma_{2})\\
&=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{\pm p},1,\omega^{\mp q})\mathscr{L}_{\mp\gamma_{2}}u(\pm z_{S})=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{\mp r\pm p},\omega^{\mp r},\omega^{\mp r\mp q})u(\pm z_{S}).\end{split}

(3.9)

In Case
I
,

(1)

if
p\equiv-q
, where we have
\varphi,\rho\in\ker_{L^{2}_{p,0}}D(\alpha)
,
\psi\in\ker_{L^{2}_{0,0}}D(\alpha)

\varphi_{2}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \rho_{2}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \psi_{1}(\pm z_{S})=\psi_{3}(\pm z_{S})=0,

which yields

W(\alpha)=\psi_{2}(\varphi_{1}\rho_{3}-\varphi_{3}\rho_{1})(\pm z_{S});

(2)

if
q\equiv 0
, where we have
\varphi\in\ker_{L^{2}_{p,0}}D(\alpha)
,
\psi,\rho\in\ker_{L^{2}_{0,0}}D(\alpha)
, we obtain

\varphi_{2}(\pm z_{S})=\varphi_{3}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \psi_{1}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \rho_{1}(\pm z_{S})=0,

which yields

W(\alpha)=\varphi_{1}(\psi_{2}\rho_{3}-\psi_{3}\rho_{2})(\pm z_{S}).

In Case
II
, we obtain

\varphi_{2}(\pm z_{S})=\varphi_{3}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \ \psi_{1}(\pm z_{S})=\psi_{3}(\pm z_{S})=0,\ \ \rho_{1}(\pm z_{S})=\rho_{2}(\pm z_{S})=0,

This leads to the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

In the notation of equation (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), we have

W(\alpha)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha\in\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

For Case
I
, we only discuss the subcase

as the other subcase is similar. Assume

. Then if
\psi(\pm z_{S})=0
, we can apply the theta function argument (cf. equation (
3.8
)) to

to conclude that

. Otherwise if
\varphi_{1}\rho_{3}-\varphi_{3}\rho_{1}
vanishes at
\pm z_{S}

vanishes at
\pm z_{S}
for some

not both equal to zero. Applying the theta function argument (
3.8
) to

we find that

.

For Case
II
, we have

W(\pm z_{S})=\varphi_{1}(\pm z_{S})\psi_{2}(\pm z_{S})\rho_{3}(\pm z_{S}).

Therefore,

implies that one of
\varphi,\psi,\rho
vanishes at

and one of
\varphi,\psi,\rho
vanishes at

∎

The kernel of

4.
Trace formulæ

In this section, we use the Birman-Schwinger operator
B_{k}
, defined in equation (
1.15
), to analyse the set of magic parameters
\mathcal{A}(\alpha_{23}/\alpha_{12})
for a fixed hopping ratio. More precisely, using Theorem
2
, we see that the traces of

are actually equal to the sums of powers of magic parameters.
Following
[
BHZ1-22
, Theorem 4]
, our goal is to provide a semi-explicit formula for

\tr(B_{k}^{\ell})=\sum_{\alpha_{12}\in\mathcal{A}(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}})}\frac{1}{\alpha_{12}^{2\ell}},\quad\ell\geq 2.

We will prove the following result, which is a slight variation of
[
BHZ1-22
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
B_{k}:L^{2}_{0}\to L^{2}_{0}
be a meromorphic family of Hilbert-Schmidt operators defined for
k\notin\Gamma^{*}
. We suppose that
B_{k}
satisfies the three properties stated in Lemma
4.1
. Then one has, for any
\ell\geq 2
,

\begin{split}\tr(B_{k}^{\ell})&=\frac{2i\pi\omega}{3\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\left[\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Res}\big(\langle B_{k}^{\ell}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}},\sqrt{3}(m\omega^{2}-n\omega)\big)\right],\end{split}

(4.1)

where all sums are finite sums.

Proof.

The only difference compared to the study of twisted bilayer graphene
[
BHZ1-22
, Theo.4]
is that the set of forbidden values for the parameter
k
is
\Gamma^{*}
instead of
(3\Gamma^{*}+i)\cup(3\Gamma^{*}-i)
. This causes no harm to the proof as we can, by the residue theorem, translate the contour integral, used in the proof, by a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
without changing the final value.
∎

Remark 5
.

Using the conjugation (
3.7
), we see that the trace, for the operator
B_{k}
defined in (
1.15
), is the same on any space
L^{2}_{ir},
where

. The trace on the whole

space is then
9
times the result of (

), see equation (

) and the discussion thereafter. Most of the results derived from these trace formulas will only use algebraic properties of the traces and will thus not be affected by the possible rescaling of (

).

The proof now boils down to proving the following lemma, which is a slight adaptation of
[
BHZ1-22
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
e_{k}(z)
be as in (
2.2
) for
k\in\Gamma^{*}
.
Consider a potential
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}/\Gamma;\mathbb{C})
satisfying the first two symmetries of (
1.4
) with a finite number of non-zero Fourier mode in its decomposition (
2.4
). For
\ell\geq 2
, one has:

•

The trace is constant in
k
,

\tr(B_{k}^{\ell})=\tau_{\ell}\text{ independent of }k\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma^{*}.

•

The function
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma^{*}\ni k\mapsto\langle B_{k}^{\ell}e_{m},e_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}}
is a finite sum of rational fractions on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
with degree equal to
-2\ell
and with
(
a finite number of
)
poles contained in
\Gamma^{*}
.

•

For any
\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}
and for any
k\notin\Gamma^{*}
, we have

\langle B_{k}^{\ell}e_{3\gamma+i},e_{3\gamma+i}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle B_{k-3\gamma}^{\ell}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

The first point is a consequence of the fact that the spectrum of
B_{k}
doesn’t depend on
k
, which follows from Theorem
2
.
For the last two points, we prove by induction that
k\mapsto B_{k}^{\ell}e_{3\gamma+i}
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}
, is of the form

B_{k}^{\ell}e_{3\gamma+i}=\sum_{\nu\in F}R_{\nu+3\gamma}(k)e_{\nu+3\gamma},

(4.2)

where
F\subset\Gamma^{*}
is a finite set and
R_{\nu}(k)
is a sum of rational fractions of degree
-2\ell
with poles located on
\Gamma^{*}
. Moreover, we will prove that one has the relation
R_{\nu+3\gamma}(k)=R_{\nu}(k-3\gamma).

The result is clear for
\ell=0
. Suppose the result is true for
\ell
, and let’s prove it holds for
\ell+1
.
The main observation is that multiplication by
U(\pm z)
acts as a shift on the Fourier basis. The multiplication by
U(-z)
sends
e_{\nu}
to a linear combination of
e_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\Gamma^{*}
. Then applying
(D(0)-k)^{-1}
multiplies the coefficient of
e_{\ell}
by
(\ell-k)^{-1}
. Multiplying by
U(z)
gives back a linear combination of
e_{\nu}
with
\nu\in\Gamma^{*}
. Finally, applying
(D(0)-k)^{-1}
multiplies the coefficient of
e_{\nu}
by
(\nu-k)^{-1}
. This means that, using the induction hypothesis (

p(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})+\tilde{p}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})=(p-\tilde{p})(1,1).

This is a non-zero vector on the line
A
. If one of the last two couples is of type
A
, the so is the last one, so this case is included in the previous one. We can thus suppose that
(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})
is of another type, say
B

p\neq\tilde{p}
and
(1,1)+(-2,1)+(1,-2)=0
, this implies that
p-\tilde{p}=-p=-\tilde{p}
which implies

and gives a contradiction. Thus, there are no additional tuple if
p\neq\tilde{p}
.

•

The last case also does not produce any other tuples in the case
p\neq\tilde{p}
.

We know from the previous three points that we only need to consider the residue for

\displaystyle\pi_{1}

\displaystyle=[((-1,-1),p),((1,1),p),((1,1),\tilde{p}),((-1,-1),\tilde{p})],

\displaystyle\pi_{2}

\displaystyle=[((-1,-1),p),((1,1),p),((-2,1),\tilde{p}),((2,-1),\tilde{p})],

\displaystyle\pi_{3}

\displaystyle=[((-1,-1),p),((1,1),p),((1,-2),\tilde{p}),((-1,2),\tilde{p})].

A straightforward computation of the residues then gives

\tr(B_{k}^{2})(p,\alpha_{23}/\alpha_{12},\zeta_{2}/\zeta_{1})=\frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}p^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(\zeta_{2}/\zeta_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}\right)^{4}+\frac{3}{1-\zeta_{2}/\zeta_{1}+(\zeta_{2}/\zeta_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}\right)^{2}+1\right).

The case
p=\tilde{p}
:

The condition becomes

(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})+(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})+(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})+(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})=0,

where we have

-(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1}),(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}),-(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})\in\{(1,1),(-2,1),(1,-2)\}.

The only solutions are given by

(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})=-(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}),\,\,(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})=-(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2}),

or by

(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})=-(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1}),\,\,(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})=-(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}).

We can compute the residues, using again the three remarks, which gives the formula

\tr(B_{k}^{2})(p,\alpha_{23}/\alpha_{12},1)=\left(1-\left(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{23}}{\alpha_{12}}\right)^{4}\right)\frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}p^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Continuity of bands & discontinuity of magic parameters

In Proposition
6.1
we show the Hölder 1/2-continuity of the spectrum of the Hamiltonian in Hausdorff-distance. This implies that the bands do not change dramatically when changing the twisting angles. This is in contrast to Theorem
4
, which says that the coupling coefficients giving rise to flat bands are rather ill-behaved when changing the twisting angles.

Proposition 6.1
.

The map
(\beta,\tilde{\beta},\zeta)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\mapsto\Spec_{L^{2}({\mathbb{C}};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta))
is locally Hölder 1/2 continuous in Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Let
L>0
to be fixed later on. If
d(\lambda,\Spec_{L^{2}({\mathbb{C}};{\mathbb{C}}^{4})}(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)))<\varepsilon
then there is
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
normalized such that
\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|
. We then define
\eta_{L}(z):=\chi(z/L)
with cut-off function
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}};[0,1])
such that
\chi(z)=1
for
|z|_{\infty}\leq 1/2.

We then set
\eta_{j,L}(z):=\eta_{L}(z-j)
for
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}
and define
\alpha^{\uparrow}_{L}:=\sup_{z}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}|\eta_{j,L}(z)|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(L^{2})
and
\alpha_{L}^{\downarrow}:=\inf_{z}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}|\eta_{j,L}(z)|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(L^{2})
with
\alpha^{\uparrow}_{L}/\alpha_{L}^{\downarrow}=\mathcal{O}(1).

Thus, we have that

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\eta_{j,L}(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}&\leq\alpha^{\uparrow}_{L}\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\lesssim\alpha_{L}^{\uparrow}\varepsilon^{2}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}.\end{split}

Let
\delta>0
, we then have that using
\|u+v\|^{2}\leq(1+\delta)\|v\|^{2}+(1+\delta^{-1})\|w\|^{2}

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\leq(1+\delta^{-1})\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}+(1+\delta)\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|[\eta_{j,L},\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)]\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}.

Now,
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|[\eta_{j,L},\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)]\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\lesssim\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}.

This implies that

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\lesssim\Big((1+\delta^{-1})\varepsilon^{2}+\tfrac{1+\delta}{L^{2}}\Big)\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}.

Since this summed inequality holds, there is in particular some
j
such that

\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\zeta)-\lambda)\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|\lesssim\sqrt{(1+\delta^{-1})\varepsilon^{2}+\tfrac{(1+\delta)}{L^{2}}}\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|.

This implies that for new parameters
\beta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}

\|(\mathcal{H}(\beta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})-\lambda)\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|\lesssim\Big(\sqrt{(1+\delta^{-1})\varepsilon^{2}+\tfrac{1+\delta}{L^{2}}}+(|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|+\|U\|_{C^{1}}|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}|L)\Big)\|\eta_{j,L}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|.

Choosing
L=(|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|+\|U\|_{C^{1}}|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|)^{-1/2},
the result follows.
∎

•

but a splitting of representations is not possible. Indeed, one can only infer that if

{w}_{k},\widetilde{w}_{k}\in L^{2}_{j,k}

\langle w_{k},\widetilde{w}_{k}\rangle=1
, and

(2\lambda_{0}D_{\bar{z}}-W){w}_{k}=0,\ \ \ (2\bar{\lambda}_{0}D_{z}-W^{*})R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{k}=0.

We can split an eigenvalue with eigenvectors
w_{k}
, if we can find

V
such that (see (
7.5
) for the notation)

\begin{gathered}\langle Vw_{0},R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\neq\langle Vw_{2},R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}.\end{gathered}

If for all (analytic)
V
the terms were equal it would follow that

(w_{0})_{\nu}\overline{(R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{0})}_{\ell}=(w_{2})_{\nu}\overline{(R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2})}_{\ell}.

The left hand side vanishes at both
\pm z_{S}
and therefore so does the right-hand side for every
\nu,\ell.

If one component
(w_{2})_{\nu}
of
w_{2}
does not vanish at
z_{S}
, then
R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2}(z_{S})=0
and vice versa.
We conclude that
w_{2}
vanishes at either
z_{S}
or
-z_{S}
and if not, then
R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2}
vanishes at both.

If
w_{2}
or
R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2}
vanishes at both
\pm z_{S}
, then that function has
5
zeros, and we have split the eigenvalue as indicated in (
7.6
). As the theta function argument would then allow us to construct
5
flat bands, this would contradict the assumption that
\ker_{L^{2}_{ip}}(D(\alpha))
is three-dimensional.

Thus, we assume that neither of the two has a zero at both
\pm z_{S}
. We shall focus here on
w_{2}
having a zero at
z_{S}
or
-z_{S}
(but not both) as the case for
R^{*}\widetilde{w}_{2}
is completely analogous. By the symmetry argument at the beginning of this proof, the entire vector
w_{2}
then vanishes at
\pm z_{S}
to second order.

If
w_{2}
vanishes at either
\pm z_{S}
to second order, we can define

v_{1}(z)=w_{2}(z)\wp(z\pm z_{S},\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw^{2})\in\ker_{L^{2}_{-p,1}}(D(\alpha)).

This function vanishes at
0
to second order and at
\mp z_{S}
to first order. Finally,

v_{0}(z)=v_{1}(z)\wp(z,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw^{2})\in\ker_{L^{2}_{-p,0}}(D(\alpha))

vanishes to first order at
\pm z_{S}
and to second order at
\mp z_{S}.

∎

We find from Theorem
12
that the flat bands in
\ker(D(\alpha)+k)
of multiplicity
m
are the vector spaces
V_{m}
given for
w_{i}\in\ker_{L^{2}_{0,i}}(D(\alpha))
by

\begin{split}V_{1}(k)&=\{\zeta F_{k}(z)w_{2}(z);\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}\}\\
V_{2}(k)&=\{\zeta_{1}F_{k_{1}}(z+z_{S})w_{0}(z)+\zeta_{2}F_{k_{1}}(z-z_{S})w_{0}(z);\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}\}\text{ and }\\
V_{3}(k)&=\{\zeta_{1}F_{k_{1}}(z-z_{S})w_{0}(z)+\zeta_{2}F_{k_{2}}(z)w_{1}(z)+\zeta_{3}F_{k_{3}}(z)w_{2}(z);\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\in{\mathbb{C}}\}.\end{split}

We can now finish

Proof of Theorem
12
.

We restrict us to explaining the second part of the proof, as the first part is even simpler since it does not require the splitting of representations in Lemma
7.2
. We have simplicity of the spectrum of
T_{0}
on

L^{2}_{ip}
, modulo the necessary multiplicity of coupled spectrum in
L^{2}_{-p,0},L^{2}_{-p,1}
. Using then Lemma
7.2
(strictly speaking its proof)
and Lemma
7.3
, we conclude
that for every
r>0
, the set

\mathscr{V}_{r}:=\{V:R(W+V)\text{ satisfies the conclusion of Lemma }\ref{l:split}\text{ in }{\mathbb{C}}\setminus D(0,r)\}

is open and dense. We then obtain
\mathscr{V}_{0}
by taking the intersection of

\mathscr{V}_{1/n}
.
∎

8.
Band touching for simple magic parameters

Throughout this section, we shall assume without loss of generality that
p\notin 3\mathbb{Z}
(see Remark
1
). We recall that
\alpha
is a simple magic parameter if
E_{1}(k,\alpha)=0
for any
k
but
E_{2}(k_{0},\alpha)\neq 0
for some
k_{0}
. This leads naturally to the question of band touching: namely, does there exist
K_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}
such that
E_{2}(K_{0},\alpha)=0
? It turns out this is impossible for twisted bilayer graphene: it was shown in
[
BHZ23
, Theorem 4]
that there was a spectral gap for a simple or doubly-degenerate magic angle. By this, we mean that the first non-zero band doesn’t touch the flat bands at any point. Interestingly, in the case of twisted trilayer graphene, the first two bands always touch. Nevertheless, if we suppose that the magic parameter
\alpha
is simple, the point where the two bands touch is unique. This is the statement of the next theorem and is the main result of this section.

Theorem 13
.

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
simple, there exists a unique
K_{0}\in\{-ip,0,iq\}
such that

\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+K_{0})\geq 2,\quad\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)=1

for any
k\notin K_{0}+\Gamma_{3}^{*}
. In other words, the first two bands touch at only one point
K_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}^{*}
. Moreover:

•

For Case
I
, we have
K_{0}=-ip
if
p=-q
and
K_{0}=0
if
q=0
, according to Proposition
8.5
.

•

For Case
II
, we have
K_{0}\equiv-i(m-1)
where the integer
m
is characterized in Proposition
8.6
.

We will split the proofs between Case
I
and
II
, see the end of the section for the proofs of Propositions
8.5
and
8.6
. The main technique is the theta function argument, explained in Section
3.3
. To use it properly, we first need to study the zeroes of eigenfunctions for a simple magic parameter.

8.1.
Vanishing of eigenfunctions for simple magic parameters

We start with a simple lemma characterizing the zeros of zero modes of the Hamiltonian:

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose that
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{2})

and that
(D(\alpha)+k)w=0
for some
k
and
that
w(z_{0})=0
. Then

w(z)=(z-z_{0})w_{0}(z)
, where
w_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

Since the solution to the elliptic equation is (real) analytic, it suffices to show that
(2D_{\bar{z}})^{\ell}w(z_{0})=0
for
all
\ell
. Then, writing

(2D_{\bar{z}})^{\ell}w(z)=(2D_{\bar{z}})^{\ell-1}\left[\left(U-k\right)w\right](z),

the claim follows by induction on
\ell
.
∎

Using the previous lemma, we find the order of vanishing of elements in
L^{2}_{r,\ell}
for
\ell=0,1,2
:

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
u\in\ker_{L^{2}_{r,\ell}}(D(\alpha))
for some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
.

(1)

If
\ell=2
, then
u(z)=zw(z)
for some
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

(2)

If
\ell=1
, then
u(z)=z^{2}w(z)
for some
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

(3)

If
\ell=0
, and
u(0)=0
, then
u(z)=z^{3}w(z)
some
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

Proof.

For the first case, since
u(\omega z)=\bar{\omega}^{2}u(z)=\omega u(z)
we find
u(0)=0
. The result then follows from Lemma
8.1
.

For the second case, we also have
u(0)=0
since
u(\omega z)=\bar{\omega}u(z)
. Since by Lemma
8.1
, we have
u(z)=zu_{1}(z)
and
D(\alpha)u=0
implies
D(\alpha)u_{1}=0
. Using the identity

\bar{\omega}(zu_{1}(z))=\bar{\omega}u(z)=u(\omega z)=(\omega z)u_{1}(\omega z),

we obtain
u_{1}(\omega z)=\omega u_{1}(z)
. This implies
u_{1}(0)=0
. As
D(\alpha)(zu_{1})=zD(\alpha)u_{1}
, applying Lemma
8.1
again yields
u_{1}(z)=zu_{2}(z)
for some
u_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{3})
.

The third case follows from an analogous iteration but with one more step than the proof of the second case.
∎

We record another useful lemma.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
u\in\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha)
for some
k\in\{0,-ip,iq\}
, i.e., at points in
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma^{*}_{3}
where there exists protected states. Then
u(0)=0
implies
\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha)\geq 2
.

Proof.

Note that
L^{2}_{k}=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{2}L^{2}_{{r,\ell}}
for
r=ik\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
. Let
u\in\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha)
with
u(0)=0
. Note that there exists a protected state
\varphi\in\ker_{L^{2}_{r,0}}D(\alpha)
as
k\in\{0,-ip,iq\}
. If
u,\varphi
are linearly independent, the lemma is obviously true.

Otherwise
u=c\varphi\in L^{2}_{r,0}
with
u(0)=0
, so by Lemma
8.2
we obtain
u=z^{3}w(z)
for some
w(z)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. This means that

\tilde{u}(z)=\wp^{\prime}(z,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw^{2})u(z)\in L^{2}_{r,0},\ \ \hat{u}(z)=\wp(z,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi iw^{2})u(z)\in L^{2}_{r,2}

satisfies
\tilde{u},\hat{u}\in\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha)
, where
\wp(z)
as before is the
\Gamma_{3}
-periodic Weierstrass
p
-function with a double pole at zero and
\wp^{\prime}(z)
denotes its derivative. This implies that
\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha)\geq 3
in this case.
∎

We give a simple but useful lemma, especially in constructing flat bands and dealing with multiplicity. This reduces the multiplicity of flat bands to the number of zeroes of eigenfunctions.

Lemma 8.4
.

For
\{z_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, consider
\{F_{k}(z-z_{i}):1\leq i\leq N\}
. If there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma_{3}^{*}
such that
\{F_{k_{0}}(z-z_{i}):1\leq i\leq N\}
is linearly dependent, then there exist
i\neq j
such that
z_{i}-z_{j}\in\Gamma_{3}
.

Proof.

Note that
F_{k}(z)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3})
. By
[
BHZ2-22
]
,
F_{k}(z)
gives Green kernel of
2D_{\bar{z}}+k
with

(2D_{\bar{z}}+k)F_{k}(z)=c(k)\delta_{0}(z),\ \ c(k)=2\pi i\theta(z(k))/\theta^{\prime}(0)\neq 0\text{ when }k\notin\Gamma_{3}^{*}.

If there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma_{3}^{*}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}F_{k_{0}}(z-z_{i})=0
for some non-trivial
\{c_{i}\}
, applying
2D_{\bar{z}}+k
yields

\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}c(k_{0})\delta_{0}(z-z_{i})=0,

which may only be true if there exist
i\neq j
such that
z_{i}=z_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}
.
∎

8.2.
Proof of theorem
13

We first show that the second band always touches the first one. It will be convenient to split the proof between Case
I
and Case
II
, beginning with Case
I
. We will use the various lemmas proved in the previous subsection, and we will also get some additional information on the vanishing point of the eigenfunctions. Our focus on Case
I
is no coincidence as in Case
II
the location of the zero is not uniquely determined, see Figure
9
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
be simple and assume Case
I
.

(1)

If
-q\equiv p

\mathrm{mod}\ 3
, then the unique protected state
\psi(z)\in\ker_{L^{2}_{0,0}}D(\alpha)
has a simple zero at
-pz_{S}+\Gamma_{3}
; there exists
u\in E=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi,\rho\}\setminus\{0\}
such that
u(z)
has a simple zero at
pz_{S}+\Gamma_{3}
, while any
v\in E
linearly independent of
u
has no zero.

(2)

If
q\equiv 0

\mathrm{mod}\ 3
, then the protected state
\varphi(z)\in\ker_{L^{2}_{p,0}}D(\alpha)
only vanishes simply at
pz_{S}+\Gamma_{3}
; there exists
u\in E=\mathrm{span}\{\psi,\rho\}\setminus\{0\}
such that
u
only vanishes simply at
-pz_{S}+\Gamma_{3}
, while any
v\in E
linearly independent of
u
has no zero.

In both cases
u
is unique (up to a rescaling).

Proof.

We only show the first case; the second case is essentially the same. By the symmetry under
\mathscr{L}_{a},a\in\Gamma_{3}
, we only consider points in
{\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{3}
. Recall that by the proof of Proposition
3.5
, there exists
u(z),u^{\prime}(z)\in E
such that at least one of
\psi(z),u(z)
vanishes at
z_{S}
and at least one of
\psi(z),u^{\prime}(z)
vanishes at
-z_{S}
.

Recall that by the argument provided in
[
BHZ2-22
, Theorem 3]
we know that if
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
is simple, then no eigenstate in
\ker_{L^{2}_{r,\ell}}D(\alpha)
with
r,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
can vanish at
z_{0}\notin\{\pm z_{S},0\}.

We also recall that none of the above eigenstates can vanish at
0
, leaving only
\pm z_{S}
as possible zeros. Indeed, assume
u\in L^{2}_{r,0}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
and
u(0)=0
. By Lemma
8.2
,
u(z)=z^{3}w(z)
for some
w\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}};{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
.
Using the theta function argument we construct

\{F_{k+ir}(z-z_{S})\wp(z)\tau(ir)u(z),F_{k+ir}(z+z_{S})\wp(z)\tau(ir)u(z)\}\subset\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)

where
\wp
is the Weierstrass
p
-function with
\Gamma_{3}
-periodicity, which has a double pole at zero and simple zeroes at
\pm z_{S}
. The function
\tau(ir)
above has the mapping property
L^{2}_{-ir}\to L^{2}_{0}
by equation (
2.6
). By Lemma
8.4
,
\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)\geq 2
, which gives a contradiction to
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
being simple.

Now we show that
\psi
cannot vanish at
pz_{S}
. Assume
\psi(pz_{S})=0
. Write
\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})^{T}
. Since
\psi\in L^{2}_{0,0}
, we have

\psi_{1}(z+a)=\bar{\omega}^{p(a_{1}+a_{2})}\psi_{1}(z),\ \psi_{2}(z+a)=\psi_{2}(z),\ \psi_{3}(z+a)={\omega}^{q(a_{1}+a_{2})}\psi_{3}(z).

Define
v(z):=\psi(z+pz_{S})
. Then
v(0)=0
, and

\begin{split}\mathscr{C}v(z)&=v(\omega z)=\psi(\omega z+pz_{S})=\psi(z+\bar{\omega}pz_{S})\\
&=\psi(z+pz_{S}+p\gamma_{1})=v(z+p\gamma_{1})=(\bar{\omega}^{p^{2}}v_{1},v_{2},\omega^{pq}v_{3})^{T}.\end{split}

As in the proof of Lemma
8.2
, this implies that
v(z)=z^{2}w(z)
for some
w\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}})
, i.e.,
\psi(z)=(z-pz_{S})^{2}w(z-pz_{S})
has a double zero at
pz_{S}
. Using the argument in the previous step we conclude that
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
has at least multiplicity two, which is a contradiction.

Therefore,
\psi
can only vanish at
-pz_{S}
, and only of order one. Indeed, if it has a zero of multiplicity two, the theta function argument would imply that
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
has at least multiplicity two, which is again a contradiction. The same argument shows that there exists
u(z)\in E=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi(z),\rho(z)\}
such that
u(z)
only vanishes at
pz_{S}
of order one.

It remains to show that any
v\in E
linearly independent from
u
has no zero. As
E\subset L^{2}_{p,0}
, we can show as before that
v(z)
can at most admit a simple zero only at
pz_{S}
, as otherwise we get a multiplicity two
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
using the theta function argument. This is in fact also impossible, as otherwise we have

\{F_{k+ip}(z-pz_{S})\tau(ip)u(z),F_{k+ip}(z-pz_{S})\tau(ip)v(z)\}\subset\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)

and thus
\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)\geq 2
, which contradicts the fact that
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
is simple.
∎

We now turn to Case
II
. The fact that the two bands touch then essentially follows from lemma
8.3
. The point
K_{0}=-i(m-1)
at which the bands touch is characterized by which eigenfunction vanishes at the stacking point
-z_{S}
. This integer
m
is not made explicit and numerical evidence suggest that it depends on the magic parameter
\alpha
, see Figure
9
.

Proposition 8.6
.

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
simple, then there exists
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
for which we can find
u\in L^{2}_{m,0}
among
\{\varphi,\psi,\rho\}
with
u(-z_{S})=0
and
u(-z_{S})\neq 0
. Moreover, we have

\dim\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)-i(m-1))\geq 2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
, for
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
we have at least one of the protected states
\varphi,\psi,\rho
vanishes at
z_{S}
and at least one of
\varphi,\psi,\rho
vanishes at
-z_{S}
.

We first note that no protected state can vanish at
\pm z_{S}
simultaneously. Indeed, if there exists a single protected state, say
\psi\in L^{2}_{0,0}
(construction for other protected states are similar), that vanishes at
\pm z_{S}
simultaneously, then, using the theta function argument, we can for all

construct

\displaystyle v_{1,k}=F_{k}(z-z_{S})\psi(z),\ v_{2,k}=F_{k}(z+z_{S})\psi(z)\in\ker_{L^{2}_{0}}(D(\alpha)+k)

such that

by Lemma

, which contradicts the assumption that
\alpha
is simple. Therefore, one of
\{\varphi,\psi,\rho\}
has to vanish at
z_{S}
and another one has to vanish at
-z_{S}
. This proves the first part of the result.

Assume
u\in\{\varphi,\psi,\rho\}
with
u\in L^{2}_{m,0}
and
u(-z_{S})=0
. Using the theta function argument in the form of
G_{k}(z)
(cf. equation (

)), we define

u_{k}(z):={G}_{k+im}(z+z_{S})u(z)\in\ker_{L^{2}_{k}}D(\alpha),\ \forall k\in{\mathbb{C}}.

In particular, for

we have by
[
BHZ2-22
, Lemma 3.2]
that

\displaystyle u_{-i(m-1)}\in\ker_{L^{2}_{m-1,2}}D(\alpha),\ \ u_{-i(m-1)}(0)=0.

By Proposition

we have
\ker_{L^{2}_{m-1,0}}D(\alpha)\neq\{0\}
, so there is a

with

. From the definition of

in (

v\in L^{2}_{k},\ \ D(\alpha)v=0\implies\tau(-k)v\in L^{2}_{0},\ \ (D(\alpha)+k)v=0.

Applying this for

we obtain the second part of the result.
∎

To further study the structure of flat bands of the Hamiltonian (

. For our use, we recall that given a vector bundle

, there is a notion of connection that is naturally defined if the bundle is holomorphic and equipped with a hermitian connection: the
Chern connection

D_{C}:C^{\infty}(X;E)\to C^{\infty}(X;E\otimes T^{*}X)

\pi^{-1}(U)\simeq U\times\mathbb{C}^{n}

\langle\zeta,\zeta\rangle_{k}=\langle G(k)\zeta,\zeta\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}G_{ij}(k)\zeta_{i}\bar{\zeta}_{j}\ \ \zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\ \ k\in U.

(9.1)

We see that if
\{u_{1}(k),\ldots,u_{n}(k)\}\subset\mathscr{H}
is a basis of
E_{k}
for
k\in U
,
and
U\ni k\to u_{j}(k)
are holomorphic, then
G(k)
is the Gramian matrix:

G(k):=\left(\langle u_{i}(k),u_{j}(k)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

\eta_{C}(k):=G(k)^{-1}\partial_{k}G(k)\,dk\in C^{\infty}(U,\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes(T^{*}U)^{1,0}).\end{gathered}

. We then see that
\tilde{F}_{i}
also has a simple pole at
z_{i}
which gives

\forall j,i\in\{1,2,3\},\quad\tilde{F}_{i}u_{0}(z_{j})=0\ \iff\ i\neq j.

(9.11)

We can now fix the first variable to be equal to
z_{1}
and thus, using (
9.11
) and the determinant expression of the wedge product,

(\tilde{F}_{2}u_{0}\wedge\tilde{F}_{3}u_{0}\wedge u_{0})(z_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=0,\quad\forall x_{2},x_{3}.

But we can now compute the left hand side of (
9.10
) by expanding on the first line to get

(\tilde{F}_{1}u_{0}\wedge(\tilde{F}_{2}u_{0}-\tilde{F}_{3}u_{0})\wedge u_{0})(z_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\underbrace{\tilde{F}_{1}u_{0}(z_{1})}_{\neq 0}\times((\tilde{F}_{2}u_{0}-\tilde{F}_{3}u_{0})\wedge u_{0})(x_{2},x_{3}).

This would then imply that
(\tilde{F}_{2}u_{0}-\tilde{F}_{3}u_{0})\wedge u_{0}=0
, which is impossible by

, (9.25)]
.
∎

10.
Exponential squeezing of bands

Here we study exponential squeezing of bands in the limit of small twisting angles.
We shall assume that
\zeta_{1}=p\theta
for fixed
p
as
\theta\to 0^{+}
, and that we have a constant angle ratio
\zeta_{2}/\zeta_{1}\equiv r
.
We shall consider the Floquet Hamiltonian
H_{k}(\alpha)
in (

) defined for a general potential satisfying the symmetries

(10.1)

and

c_{(m-n+1)(-n)}=\omega c_{nm},\qquad c_{(-m)(n-m-1)}=\omega^{2}c_{nm},\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that each orbit

(n,m)\to(m-n+1,-n)\to(-m,n-m-1)\to(n,m)

in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is closed, which is in agreement with the fact that
\omega^{3}=1
.
Define an equivalence relation
\sim
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by the condition that
(n,m)\sim(n^{\prime},m^{\prime})
if
(n,m)
and
(n^{\prime},m^{\prime})
are in the same orbit, and let
S
be the set of equivalence classes. Then

U(z)=\sum_{[(n,m)]\in S}g_{[(n,m)]}(z),

where

\displaystyle g_{[(n,m)]}(z)

\displaystyle=c_{nm}f_{nm}(z)+c_{(m-n+1)(-n)}f_{(m-n+1)(-n)}(z)+c_{(-m)(n-m-1)}f_{(-m)(n-m-1)}(z)

\displaystyle=c_{nm}\Big(e^{\frac{i}{2}(-\sqrt{3}(n+m)\re z+(2-3(n-m))\im z)}+\omega e^{\frac{i}{2}(-\sqrt{3}(m-2n+1)\re z+(2-3(m+1))\im z)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\omega^{2}e^{\frac{i}{2}(-\sqrt{3}(n-2m-1)\re z+(2-3(1-n))\im z)}\Big).

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}g_{[(n,m)]}(0)

\displaystyle=3c_{nm}\partial_{z}f_{nm}(0)=\tfrac{3i}{4}c_{nm}[-\sqrt{3}(n+m)-i(2-3(n-m))],

(10.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{z}}g_{[(n,m)]}(0)

\displaystyle=0.

(This does not depend on the choice of representative in
[(n,m)]
.)
We shall require that

|c_{nm}|\leq c_{0}e^{-c_{1}(|n|+|m|)},

(10.3)

for some constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
, which is equivalent
to real analyticity of
U
. We also make the generic non-degeneracy assumption that

0\neq\re(\partial_{z}U(0))=\sum_{[(n,m)]\in S}\re(\tfrac{3i}{4}c_{nm}[-\sqrt{3}(n+m)-i(2-3(n-m))]).

(10.4)

This is verified by the standard potential
U_{0}
in (
1.6
), since
\partial_{z}U_{0}(0)=3/2
.
For such potentials we have the following result.

Theorem 15
.

Let

H_{k}(\alpha)
be given by
(
1.9
) with

(

\{a,b\}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{\xi_{j}}a\partial_{x_{j}}b-\partial_{\xi_{j}}b\partial_{x_{j}}a=\partial_{\zeta}a\partial_{z}b-\partial_{\zeta}b\partial_{z}a+\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}a\partial_{\bar{z}}b-\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}b\partial_{\bar{z}}a.

for some

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle:=rU(pz)U(prz)(2hD_{\bar{z}}-hk)\left((rU(prz))^{-1}(2hD_{\bar{z}}-hk)\left(U(pz)^{-1}(2hD_{\bar{z}}-hk)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-r^{2}U(prz)U(-prz)(2hD_{\bar{z}})-U(pz)U(prz)\left((2hD_{\bar{z}}-hk)(U(-pz)U(prz)^{-1}\bullet\right),

The semiclassical

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{det}(p(z,\bar{z},\bar{\zeta}))=2\bar{\zeta}(4\bar{\zeta}^{2}-V(z,\bar{z})),

Appendix A
Anti-chiral limit

The Hamiltonian in the anti-chiral limit
\alpha=0
can be written, by conjugating with the unitary matrix
\mathbf{V}=(e_{1},e_{4},e_{3},e_{5},e_{2},e_{6})
, in the off-diagonal form

H_{\operatorname{ac}}=\mathbf{V}H(\tilde{\alpha})\mathbf{V}^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&D_{\operatorname{ac}}\\
D_{\operatorname{ac}}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }D_{\operatorname{ac}}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\alpha}_{12}V(pz)&2D_{z}&0\\
2D_{\bar{z}}&(\tilde{\alpha}_{12}V(pz))^{*}&\tilde{\alpha}_{23}V(p\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}z)\\
(\tilde{\alpha}_{23}V(p\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}z))^{*}&0&2D_{z}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 16
.

The anti-chiral Hamiltonian
H_{\operatorname{ac}}
does not have any flat bands at energy zero.

Proof.

We partition the off-diagonal part of the Bloch-Floquet transformed Hamiltonian

D_{\operatorname{ac},k}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}(k)&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}(k)\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }A_{11}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\alpha}_{12}V(pz)&2D_{z}+\bar{k}\\
2D_{\bar{z}}+k&(\tilde{\alpha}_{12}V(pz))^{*}\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }A_{22}(k)=2D_{z}+\bar{k}.

In particular, for
k=k_{1}+ik_{2}
we have

A_{11}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{2}(D_{j}-k_{j})\sigma_{j}+\tilde{\alpha}_{12}(\Real(V(pz))\operatorname{id}+i\Imag(V(pz))\sigma_{3}).

To show that there does not exist a flat band, it suffices to show that
Q_{k}
is invertible for some
k.

If
k\notin\Gamma^{*}
then
A_{22}(k)^{-1}
exists and by the block matrix inversion formula

\begin{split}D_{\operatorname{ac},k}^{-1}&=\operatorname{diag}((A_{11}(k)-A_{12}A_{22}(k)^{-1}A_{21})^{-1},(A_{22}(k)-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}A_{12})^{-1})\times\\
&\quad\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{id}&-A_{12}A_{22}(k)^{-1}\\
-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}&\operatorname{id}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\operatorname{diag}(A_{11}(k)^{-1},(A_{22}(k)-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}A_{12})^{-1})\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{id}&-A_{12}A_{22}(k)^{-1}\\
-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}&\operatorname{id}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

The goal of the proof is to show that all terms on the right-hand side are well-defined for suitable
k
, which shows that
Q_{k}
is invertible and thus the anti-chiral Hamiltonian does not exhibit a flat band at energy zero. The invertibility of
A_{11}(k)
coincides with the expression found in the twisted bilayer case and thus one can see that
A_{11}(k)
is invertible using
[
BEWZ21
]
. Thus it suffices to show the invertibility of
A_{22}(k)-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}A_{12}
.

To do so we recall that for
k=k_{1}+ik_{2}
we have
A_{11}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{2}(D_{j}-k_{j})\sigma_{j}+\tilde{\alpha}_{12}(\Real(V(z))+i\Imag(V(z))\sigma_{3})
such that
in terms of
H_{S}=(D_{1}-k_{1})^{2}+(D_{2}-k_{2})^{2}
, we find

A_{11}(k)(A_{11}(k)-\tilde{\alpha}_{12}\Real(V(z,\bar{z})))=(\operatorname{id}+WH_{S}(k)^{-1})H_{S}(k),

for some
W\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}).

We conclude that

A_{11}(k)^{-1}=(A_{11}(k)-\tilde{\alpha}_{12}\Real(V(z,\bar{z})))H_{S}(k)^{-1}(\operatorname{id}+WH_{S}(k)^{-1}).

By complexifying
k_{1}=\mu_{1}+i\mu_{2}
with
\mu_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}

\|(A_{11}(k)-\tilde{\alpha}_{12}\Real(V(z,\bar{z})))H_{S}(k)^{-1}\|\lesssim\langle\mu_{2}\rangle^{-1}

we find that
\|A_{11}(k)^{-1}\|\lesssim\langle\mu_{2}\rangle^{-1}
which when
\mu_{2}
is taken large enough implies that also

A_{22}(k)-A_{21}A_{11}(k)^{-1}A_{12}
is invertible, since
A_{22}(k)
is normal and invertible for
\bar{k}\notin\Gamma^{*}.
This completes the argument.
∎

Abstract
. We investigate in this work families ( u ε ) ε > 0 (u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0} of sign-changing blowing-up solutions of asymptotically critical stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equations of the following type: Δ g ​ u ε + h ε ​ u ε = | u ε | p ε − 2 ​ u ε \Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-2}u_{\varepsilon} in a closed manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) , where h ε h_{\varepsilon} converges in C 1 ​ ( M ) C^{1}(M) . Assuming that ( u ε ) ε > 0 (u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0} blows-up as a single sign-changing bubble , we obtain necessary conditions for blow-up that constrain the localisation of blow-up points and exhibit a strong interaction between h h , the geometry of ( M , g ) (M,g) and the bubble itself. These conditions are new and are a consequence of the sign-changing nature of u ε u_{\varepsilon} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the main results

Let
(M^{n},g),n\geq 3
be a smooth, connected and closed manifold, where closed means compact without boundary. We study in this paper sign-changing solutions
u\in C^{2}(M)
of the equation

(1.1)

\Delta_{g}u+hu=|u|^{p-2}u\hbox{ in }M

where
\Delta_{g}=-\text{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
h\in C^{1}(M)
and
2<p\leq 2^{\star}
with
2^{\star}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
. When
h\equiv\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
, where
S_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
,
p=2^{\star}
and
u>0
, (
1.1
) is the celebrated Yamabe equation. We let
H^{1}(M)
be the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
for
u\mapsto\|u\|_{H^{1}}:=\|u\|_{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2}
. It has been known since the seminal work of Struwe
[
36
]
that families of solutions to (
1.1
) that are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(M)
may develop concentration phenomena in the form of “bubbles” (see also Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
10
]
for the case of a compact Riemannian manifold). These “bubbles” correspond to a loss of compactness of the solutions, and understanding the conditions in which they may appear and their mutual interactions has been the subject of many works in the last decades.

Despite the abundance of contributions for positive solutions of (
1.1
), the literature for sign-changing solutions is less developed. In the present work we take a step in this direction and study sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
) that blow-up as
a single sign-changing bubble
. In the sequel, we fix
r_{g}\in(0,i_{g}(M))
where
i_{g}(M)>0
is the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
. Throughout this paper
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
will denote a cutoff function such that
\chi(t)=1
if
|t|\leq r_{g}/2
and
\chi(t)=0
if
|t|\geq r_{g}
.
We say that a family
(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
of functions in
H^{1}(M)
is a family of bubbles if there exists a non-zero solution
V
to

(1.2)

\Delta_{\xi}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the norm
u\mapsto\|\nabla u\|_{2}
,
and families of points
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
in
M
and of positive real numbers
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
such that

B_{\varepsilon}(x)-\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(\cdot)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\to 0\text{ in }H^{1}(M)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Here
\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}
is the exponential chart at
x_{\varepsilon}
and
\xi
is the Euclidean metric, so that
\Delta_{\xi}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
. We will say that such a family
(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
and of radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V
, and
V
itself will sometimes be referred to as the bubble. A more precise definition is given in Definition
3.1
and properties of such families are investigated in Section
3
below. All the solutions considered in this paper are allowed to change sign unless mentioned otherwise.

Our main results establish a necessary condition for the existence of blowing-up solutions of (
1.1
) that blow-up with a single sign-changing bubble. We introduce one last notation as follows: if
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a family of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
, of radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V
,
we define

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B

\displaystyle:=\frac{4n}{3(n-2)^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx\right)^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{i\alpha j\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}\partial_{ij}^{2}V\left(\frac{n-2}{2}V+x^{l}\partial_{l}V\right)\,dx

where
x_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
. We first state a result when
n\geq 5
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g),n\geq 5
be a smooth, connected and closed manifold, let
h,(h_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in C^{1}(M)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\varepsilon}=h
in
C^{1}(M)
and
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive. Let
(p_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in(2,2^{\star}]
be such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}p_{\varepsilon}=2^{\star}
. Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
be a family of solutions of

(1.3)

\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-2}u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }M.

We assume that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
satisfies

(1.4)

u_{\varepsilon}=cB_{\varepsilon}+o(1)\hbox{ in }H^{1}(M)

for some
c>0
, where
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a bubble centered at
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in M
and with radius
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in(0,+\infty)
and modeled on
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as in Definition
3.1
below.

Then
c=1
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{\star}-p_{\varepsilon}}=1
, the following limit exists:

\Lambda:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\geq 0,

and
h
satisfies:

(1.5)

h(x_{0})-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}(x_{0})=\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4n}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{2}dx}\Big(\Lambda-\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B\Big),

where we have let
x_{0}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
.

The new term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
appearing in (
1.5
) is well-defined when
n\geq 5
and we prove in Section
3
that it does not depend on the representative chosen for the family
(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
appearing in (
1.4
) (see Proposition
3.1
below).

In dimensions
3
and
4
we prove the following analogue of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g),n=3,4
be a smooth, connected and closed manifold, let
h,(h_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1},(p_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in(2,2^{\star}]
be as in the statement of Theorem
1.1
. Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
be a family of solutions of (
1.3
) satisfying again (
1.4
) for some
c>0
, where
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a bubble centered at
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in M
and with radius
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in(0,+\infty)
and modeled on
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as in Definition
3.1
below.

Then
c=1
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{\star}-p_{\varepsilon}}=1
, the following limits exist:

\Lambda:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{6-p_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}&n=3\\
&\frac{4-p_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}}&n=4\end{aligned}\right\}\geq 0,

and
h
satisfies

(1.6)

\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|V|^{2}V\,dx\right)^{2}}{\omega_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|V|^{4}\,dx}\left(h(x_{0})-\frac{1}{6}S_{g}(x_{0})\right)=\Lambda\hbox{ when }n=4

(1.7)

-6\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|V|^{4}V\,dx\right)^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|V|^{6}\,dx}m_{h}(x_{0})=\Lambda\hbox{ when }n=3,

where as before we have let
x_{0}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
. In (
1.6
)
\omega_{3}
is the area of the
3
-sphere and in (
1.7
)
m_{h}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
is the mass of the Green’s function of
\Delta_{g}+h
at
x_{0}\in M
(see Definition
3.3
below).

A few comments on Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are in order. Assumption (
1.4
) implies that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
blows-up as a single, possibly sign-changing bubble. In other words, (
1.4
) is a Struwe decomposition with a single bubbling profile. No assumption is made on the energy of
V
(except that it is finite) and
V
is not assumed to be non-degenerate in the sense of Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
13
]
. Assumption (
1.4
) should be regarded, insofar as sign-changing solutions are considered, as the analogue of the classical notion of isolated and simple blow-up point for positive solutions introduced in Schoen
[
35
]
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are not new for
positive
solutions. It has been known since Obata
[
26
]
and Struwe
[
36
]
that when
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a family of
positive
solutions of (
1.3
) the bubble
V
is positive and radial and given by (
2.2
) below. In this case, and when
n\geq 5
, the additional term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
in (
1.5
) vanishes by the symmetries of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}
(see Proposition
3.3
below), and condition (
1.5
) becomes

h(x_{0})-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}(x_{0})=C(n)\Lambda\geq 0,\quad\hbox{ with }\Lambda:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{2^{\star}-p_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

for some dimensional constant
C(n)>0
. When
n=3,4
, (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) show that

\displaystyle h(x_{0})-\frac{1}{6}S_{g}(x_{0})=C(4)\Lambda\hbox{ when }n=4\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{h}(x_{0})=-C(3)\Lambda\hbox{ when }n=3,

where
\Lambda
is as in the statement of the Theorem. When
p_{\varepsilon}=2^{\star}
for all
\varepsilon
, and for positive finite-energy solutions, these conditions have been known since the work of Li-Zhu
[
19
]
and Druet
[
11
,
12
]
.

If
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a blowing-up family of
sign-changing
solutions of (
1.3
) with a single bubbling profile as in (
1.4
), however, the bubble
V
is sign-changing in general and (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) display new phenomena. When
n\geq 5
the additional term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
cannot be expected to vanish
a priori
. We construct indeed in Section
3
examples of manifolds
(M,g)
and of solutions
V
of (
1.2
) such that
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B\neq 0
(see Proposition
3.4
below). The term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
is thus a striking new feature of sign-changing blow-up that does not appear for positive solutions. When
n\geq 5
, condition (
1.5
) is entirely new and highlights a strong interaction between
h
, the geometry of
(M,g)
and the limiting bubble
V
itself. When
n=3,4
, (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) involve
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{*}-2}Vdx
. But if
V
is a sign-changing solution of (
1.2
) for
n\geq 3
, the latter integral may vanish if
V
decays strongly at infinity (see (
2.9
) below). When this is the case conditions (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) indicate that, in dimensions
3
and
4
, the interaction between
B_{\varepsilon}
and the geometry of
(M,g)
takes place at a higher-order than it did for positive solutions.

1.2.
Consequences of Theorem
1.1
on the blow-up picture of (
1.3
)

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
shed new light on the
stability
of the set of sign-changing solutions of (
1.3
). We say that equation (
1.1
), with
p=2^{*}
, is stable if for every family
(h_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
converging to
h
, every energy-bounded family of solutions
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
of (
1.3
) converges in
C^{2}(M)
, up to a subsequence. Druet
[
11
]
proved that, for
positive
solutions, stability holds provided
h\neq\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
everywhere in
M
(with a caveat in dimensions
3
and
6
, see
[
11
]
). This result was generalised to the case of sign-changing solutions, when
n\geq 7
and when
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat, in Premoselli-Vétois
[
30
]
. When
(M,g)
is not locally conformally flat, and at least when
n\geq 5
, however, (
1.5
) shows that necessary conditions for a one bubble blow-up involve
h
, the geometry of
(M,g)
and the possible limiting bubble
V
itself. The set of sign-changing solutions of (
1.2
) is still poorly understood and no classification result is known, but examples of large-energy sequences of solutions have been constructed by Del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia
[
7
]
, Ding
[
9
]
, Medina-Musso
[
23
]
, Medina-Musso-Wei
[
22
]
. The wealth of solutions of (
1.2
) is the main obstacle to understanding the term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
in (
1.5
). It seems therefore highly unlikely, even in the single-bubble case, that stability for sign-changing solutions of (
1.3
) can be enforced solely by a global assumption on
h
, as was the case for positive solutions.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, however, allow us to rule out limiting bubbles
V
that may appear in the blow-up in some cases. We start with an example in dimensions
3
and
4
. If
V
is a solution of (
1.2
) it satisfies

V(x)=\frac{\lambda(V)}{|x|^{n-2}}+O\left(|x|^{1-n}\right)\quad\text{ as }|x|\to+\infty,

where
(n-2)\omega_{n-1}\lambda(V)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}-2}Vdx
(see (
2.4
) and (
2.9
) below). Bubbles decaying at infinity as
|x|^{2-n}
satisfy
\lambda(V)>0
: this is the case for the positive bubbles given by (
2.2
) and for the bubbles of
[
7
]
. An immediate corollary of Theorem
1.2
is as follows:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
n=3,4
, be a smooth, closed, connected Riemannian manifold and
h,(h_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in C^{1}(M)
be such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\varepsilon}=h
in
C^{1}(M)
and
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive. Let
(p_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in(2,2^{\star}]
be such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}p_{\varepsilon}=2^{\star}
. Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
be a family of solutions of (
1.3
) satisfying (
1.4
), where
B_{\varepsilon}
is a family of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
, of radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V
. Assume that

•

the mass of
\Delta_{g}+h
is positive at every point of
M
if
n=3

•

h<\frac{1}{6}S_{g}
everywhere in
M
if
n=4
.

Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{*}-2}Vdx=0
. In particular,
V(x)=O(|x|^{1-n})
as
|x|\to+\infty
and
V
is neither positive nor one of the bubbles of
[
7
]
.

In dimensions
n\geq 5
(
1.5
) does not provide any information on the decay of
V
at infinity, but we can still rule out the existence of symmetric bubbles in some cases. We recall that Ding
[
9
]
constructed a family of solutions of (

.

Let
(M^{n},g)=(\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q},g_{p}\times g_{q})
, where
g_{p}
and

\mathbb{S}^{p}

. Let
h,(h_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in C^{1}(M)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{\varepsilon}=h

(p_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in(2,2^{\star}]

Since

,

]

[

]
, Micheletti-Pistoia-Vétois
[

]

]

[

,
33
]
. Deng-Musso-Wei
[

]
have considered an innovative construction of a family of sign-changing solutions

to the (

and

that blows-up like a single bubble as in (

). We discuss similar constructions in Section

.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce a few properties of sign-changing solutions of (
1.2
) and highlight the main differences with respect to the positive case. Section

2.
Finite-energy nodal solutions of the Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

We let
\Sigma
be the set of non-zero finite-energy solutions of (
1.2
):

(2.1)

\Sigma=\big\{V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\{0\},\quad\Delta_{\xi}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V\big\}.

Regularity theory for critical equations (see eg Trudinger
[
37
]
) together with standard elliptic theory, shows that there exists
\alpha_{n}>0
such that
V\in C^{3,\alpha_{n}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
V\in\Sigma
. If
V\in\Sigma
is positive it is equal by Obata
[
26
]
, up to translations and rescalings, to

(2.2)

B_{0}^{+}(x)=\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck
[
6
]
this result remains true for all positive solutions of (
1.2
), without the assumption that they belong to
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note also that, up to dilation,
B_{0}^{+}
is the only non-zero
radial
solution of (
1.2
) which belongs to
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by a simple application of Pohozaev’s identity.

If
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define its Kelvin transform as

(2.3)

V^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2}}V\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

It is well-known (see for instance Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
) that
V\mapsto V^{*}
defines an isometry of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and of
L^{2^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that

\Delta_{\xi}V^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+2}}\Delta_{\xi}V\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, provided
V
is of class
C^{2}
. As a consequence we have
V^{*}\in\Sigma
whenever
V\in\Sigma
(see again
[
13
]
) and the regularity theory for (
1.2
) shows that
V^{*}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
V\in\Sigma
. The following result provides a precise description of the behavior at infinity of the elements in
\Sigma
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V\in\Sigma
. There exist
\lambda(V)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha(V)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the following asymptotic expansion holds as
|x|\to+\infty
and can be differentiated:

(2.4)

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(V)}{|x|^{n-2}}+\frac{\langle\alpha(V),x\rangle}{|x|^{n}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}\right).

When we say that expansion (
2.4
) can be differentiated we mean that the following holds as
|x|\to+\infty
: for any
1\leq i\leq n
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}V(x)

\displaystyle=-(n-2)\lambda(V)\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{n}}+\frac{\alpha(V)_{i}|x|^{2}-n\langle\alpha(V),x\rangle x_{i}}{|x|^{n+2}}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{n+1}}\right).

Proof.

Let
V^{*}
be, as before, the Kelvin transform of
V
. It is of class
C^{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we can consider its Taylor expansion at
0
: there exist
\lambda(V)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha(V)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, as
y\to 0
, and for
1\leq i\leq n
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle V^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\lambda(V)+\langle\alpha(V),y\rangle+O(|y|^{2})

\displaystyle\partial_{i}V^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\alpha(V)_{i}+O(|y|)

hold. Since for any
x\neq 0
we have
V(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2}}V^{*}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)
, (
2.4
) follows from (
2.6
). To prove (
2.5
) we write that by the chain rule, for
x\neq 0
,

(2.7)

\partial_{i}V(x)=-(n-2)\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{n}}V^{*}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2}}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{|x|^{2}}-2\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{4}}\right)\partial_{j}V^{*}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)

holds, and we conclude using again (
2.6
).
∎

As a consequence of (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), there exists
C=C(n,V)
such that

(2.8)

|V(x)|+(1+|x|)|\nabla V(x)|+(1+|x|)^{2}|\nabla^{2}V(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{2-n}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This can also be shown by the conformal invariance of (
1.2
), see e.g. Premoselli
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
. As shown in Premoselli
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have

(2.9)

\lambda(V)=\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}-2}Vdx\quad\hbox{ for all }V\in\Sigma.

If
B_{0}^{+}
is as in (
2.2
) we have
\lambda(B_{0}^{+})=(n(n-2))^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
. The solutions constructed in
[
7
]
also satisfy
\lambda(V)\neq 0
. As proven in Lemma
2.1
,
\lambda(V)=V^{*}(0)
. As a consequence, examples where
\lambda(V)=0
are easily obtained by Kelvin-transforming sign-changing solutions of
\Sigma
at a point where they vanish.

Remark 2.2
.

An accurate inspection of the proof of Lemma
2.1
shows that we can state a slightly more precise result. Let
V\in\Sigma
.

•

Assume that
n=3,4
. Then there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a non-zero homogeneous polynomial
P
of degree
k
such that

V(x)=\frac{P(x)}{|x|^{n-2+2k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{n-1+k}}\right)\quad\text{ as }|x|\to+\infty.

•

Assume that
n=5
. Then there exists
k\in\{0,\dots,4\}
and a homogeneous polynomial
P
of degree
k
such that

V(x)=\frac{P(x)}{|x|^{3+2k}}+\left\{\begin{aligned} &O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{4+k}}\right)&\text{ if }k\leq 3\\
&O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{7+\frac{1}{3}}}\right)&\text{ if }k=4\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ as }|x|\to+\infty.

•

Assume finally that
n\geq 6
. Let
0<\alpha<1
if
n=6
and
0<\alpha\leq\frac{4}{n-2}
if
n\geq 7
. Then there exists
k\in\{0,\dots,3\}
and a homogeneous polynomial
P
of degree
k
such that
V
satisfies

V(x)=\frac{P(x)}{|x|^{n-2+2k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2+k+\alpha}}\right)\quad\text{ as }|x|\to+\infty.

All these expansions can be differentiated. In dimensions
n=3,4
,
P
is the first non-zero homogeneous polynomial in the Taylor expansion of
V^{*}
at
0
, which always exists by classical finite continuation results (see e.g. Aronszajn
[
2
]
) since
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\{0\}
. In dimensions
n\geq 5
,
P
may vanish. Examples of solutions in any dimension
n\geq 3
for which the degree of
P
is larger or equal than
2
are still unknown and the question of their existence was raised in Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
13
]
.

3.
Riemannian preliminaries

3.1.
Sign-changing families of bubbles in
M

We define the notion of Riemannian bubble that we will consider in this work:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in M
and
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be families of points in
M
and positive real numbers, with
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We let
x_{0}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
. Let
V\in\Sigma
be fixed and let
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of functions in
H^{1}(M)
. We say that
B
is a
family of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V

if there exists a family of charts
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\geq 0}
such that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:B_{g}(x_{0},r_{g})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is normal at
x_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\varphi_{0}
in
C^{k}
for all
k
and such that

(3.1)

B_{\varepsilon}-\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\to 0\quad\text{ in }H^{1}(M)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Here
\Sigma
is as in (
2.1
). As the following simple result shows, Definition
3.1
is independent of the choice of the local chart up to an isometry. In the sequel, we let
O(n)
be the group of isometries of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in M
and
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be families of points in
M
and positive real numbers, with
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and let
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{B}=(\bar{B}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be families of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and respectively modeled on
V,\bar{V}\in\Sigma
as in Definition (
3.1
). Then

B_{\varepsilon}-\bar{B}_{\varepsilon}\to 0\text{ in }H^{1}(M)\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\quad\iff\quad\bar{V}=V\circ O

for some orthogonal mapping
O\in O(n)
.

Families of bubbles
(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
and
(\bar{B}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and satisfying
B_{\varepsilon}-\bar{B}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
H^{1}(M)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
will be called
equivalent
families.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{\varepsilon},\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}:B_{g}(x_{0},r_{g})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be normal coordinate charts for
g
at
x_{\varepsilon}
associated to
B
and
\bar{B}
as in definition
3.1
. If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

(3.2)

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})+x)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})+O_{\varepsilon}x+O(|x|^{2})\quad\text{ as }x\to 0,

where we have let
O_{\varepsilon}=D\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\circ\big(D\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\big)^{-1}
. Since
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
are normal charts at
x_{\varepsilon}
,
O_{\varepsilon}\in O(n)
. Straightforward computations using (
2.8
) and (
3.2
) show that

(3.3)

\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\Bigg[\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{O_{\varepsilon}\left(\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\right)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\Bigg]\to 0

in
H^{1}(M)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since
B_{\varepsilon}-\bar{B}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
H^{1}(M)
by assumption we obtain

\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\Bigg[\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{O_{\varepsilon}\left(\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\right)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}\bar{V}\left(\frac{\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\Bigg]\to 0

in
H^{1}(M)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. A simple scaling argument then shows that
V\circ O=\bar{V}
where
O:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}O_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) show that, up to composing
V
with an euclidean isometry, we can always assume that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is an exponential chart at
x_{\varepsilon}
in Definition
3.1
. Choose, for any
p\in B_{g}(x_{0},r_{g})
, an identification of
T_{p}M
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This defines an exponential map at
p
, that we will denote by
\exp_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to M
in all of this paper. Mimicking the arguments in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we similarly have, for
V\in\Sigma
,

\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\Bigg[\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}\big(V\circ O\big)\left(\frac{\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(\cdot)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\Bigg]\to 0

for some
O\in O(n)
. We have thus shown that
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a
family of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V\in\Sigma
if and only if

(3.4)

B_{\varepsilon}-\chi(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{\varepsilon}))\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}\big(V\circ O\big)\left(\frac{\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(\cdot)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\to 0\quad\text{ in }H^{1}(M)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for some
O\in O(n)
. Any two exponential charts at
x_{\varepsilon}
are related by an isometry of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A consequence of (
3.4
) is that Definition
3.1
is independent of the choice of the exponential chart at
x_{\varepsilon}
up to composing
V
with an isometry. The classical example of a family of bubbles is the so-called family of standard bubbles:

(3.5)

\displaystyle B_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=\chi(d_{g}(x,x_{\varepsilon}))B_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x),\hbox{ where }

\displaystyle B_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{d_{g}(x,x_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{n(n-2)}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}=\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}B_{0}^{+}\left(\frac{\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(x)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right),

where
B_{0}^{+}
is given by (
2.2
). This example is peculiar: since
B_{0}^{+}
is radially symmetrical (as a consequence of positivity), any exponential chart at
x_{\varepsilon}
yields the same family of bubbles, and the function
B_{\varepsilon}^{+}
thus obtained is well-defined on the whole of
M
. Naively composing with an exponential chart does not, however, allow to globally pull-back a sign-changing element
V\in\Sigma
in
M
without losing regularity at the cut-locus, hence the need for Definition
3.1
.

The following proposition is straightforward:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of bubble as in Definition
3.1
. Then

•

There exists
C>0
such that
\|B_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C
for all
\varepsilon>0

•

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|B_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=0

•

B_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
H^{1}(M)
as
\varepsilon\to 0

•

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\nabla B_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla V|^{2}\,dx
.

3.2.
The product
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B

We now investigate the properties of the term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
appearing in the Statement of Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 3.2
.

Assume that
n\geq 5
. Let
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of bubbles centered at
x_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and modeled on
V\in\Sigma
as in Definition
3.1
.
We define

(3.6)

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B

\displaystyle:=\frac{4n}{3(n-2)^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx\right)^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{i\alpha j\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}\partial_{ij}^{2}V\left(\frac{n-2}{2}V+x^{l}\partial_{l}V\right)\,dx

where
x_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
and where the coordinates of the Weyl tensor are taken with respect to the chart
\exp_{x_{0}}
.

It is intended in (
3.6
) that repeated indices are summed over. That the second integral in (
3.6
) is finite follows from (
2.8
) since
n\geq 5
. Note that the term
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
depends on
x_{0}
since the family
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}>0
does. We recall the symmetries of the Weyl tensor of
g
at any point
x\in M
:

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{ijk\ell}=-\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{jik\ell}=-\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{ij\ell k}=\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{k\ell ij}

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{iji\ell}=0\text{ and }\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{ijk\ell}+\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{jki\ell}+\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x)_{kij\ell}=0

for any
i,j,k,\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, where repeated indices are summed over. As a consequence of these symmetries, simple integration by parts (see for instance Hebey-Vaugon
[
15
]
or Lemma 3.2 in Mesmar-Robert
[
24
]
) show that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B=\frac{4n}{3(n-2)^{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx\right)^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{i\alpha j\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}\partial_{i}V\partial_{j}Vdx.

Again the integral on the right is finite when
n\geq 5
by (
2.8
). A consequence of (
3.7
) and of the tensorial nature of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
is that
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
does not change if
V
is replaced by
V\circ O
for some
O\in O(n)
, provided the coordinates of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
are now computed in the chart
O\circ\exp_{x_{0}}^{-1}
. Hence
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
does not depend on the choice of the exponential chart at
x_{0}
and only depends on the equivalence class of families of bubbles at
x_{0}
in the sense of Proposition
3.1
.

We describe two simple cases where
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
vanishes globally:

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
n\geq 5
and let
B
be a family of bubbles centered at
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in M
, with radius
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and modeled on
V\in\Sigma
, and
x_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
. Then
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B=0
in the following (non-exhaustive) situations:

•

\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})=0

•

V
is radial (and hence
V=B_{0}^{+}
given by (
2.2
)).

Proof.

By (
3.6
) the first point is trivial. If
V=B_{0}^{+}
is radial, the second point follows from the radiality of
B_{0}^{+}
, from the equivalent expression (
3.7
) and from the total antisymmetry of the Weyl tensor.
∎

The next two results shows that
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B
cannot be expected to vanish in general:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{S}^{p},g_{p})
and
(\mathbb{S}^{q},g_{q})
be the unit spheres of dimensions
p,q\geq 2
endowed with their round metrics. Assume that
n=p+q\geq 5
and that
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\{0\}
solves (
1.2
) and is invariant under the action of
O(p)\times O(q)
but not under the action of
O(n)
. Let
M=\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q}
endowed with the metric
g=g_{p}\otimes g_{q}
. Let
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of bubbles in
(M,g)
modeled on
V
as in Definition
3.1
, centered at
(x_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in M
and with radius
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
x_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}x_{\varepsilon}
. Then
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B<0
.

Proof.

We let
M:=\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q}
where
p,q\geq 2
and
p+q=n\geq 5
and
g:=g_{p}\otimes g_{q}
be the product metric of the round metrics
g_{p}
and
g_{q}
respectively on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
and
\mathbb{S}^{q}
. We follow the geometric notations of Besse
[
4
]
and Hebey
[
16
]
. The Riemann tensors for each metric are

\hbox{Rm}_{g_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{p}\odot g_{p}\hbox{ and }\hbox{Rm}_{g_{q}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{q}\odot g_{q},

where
\odot
is the Kulkarni-Nomizu product, given in coordinates by
(T\odot S)_{ijkl}=T_{ik}S_{jl}-T_{il}S_{jk}+T_{jl}S_{ik}-T_{jk}S_{il}
. Standard results on product manifolds give that, after the necessary projections,
\hbox{Rm}_{g}=\frac{1}{2}g_{p}\odot g_{p}+\frac{1}{2}g_{q}\odot g_{q}
,
\hbox{Ric}_{g}=(p-1)g_{p}+(q-1)g_{q}
and
S_{g}=p(p-1)+q(q-1)
. The Weyl tensor of
g
is defined as

\hbox{Weyl}_{g}=\hbox{Rm}_{g}-\frac{1}{n-2}\hbox{Ric}_{g}\odot g+\frac{S_{g}}{2(n-1)(n-2)}g\odot g.

If
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{p},x_{0}^{q})\in\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q}
we compute the coordinates of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
in the chart obtained from the exponential chart on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
at
x_{0}^{p}
and the exponential chart on
\mathbb{S}^{q}
at
x_{0}^{q}
: this yields a normal chart on
\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q}
. The Latin letters
i,j,k...
refer to coordinates in
\mathbb{S}^{p}
, the Greek letter
\alpha,\beta,...
refer to
\mathbb{S}^{q}
. The previous observations show that, at any point around
x_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=C_{1}(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk})

\displaystyle\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=C_{2}(g_{\alpha\gamma}g_{\beta\delta}-g_{\alpha\delta}g_{\beta\gamma})

\displaystyle\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{i\alpha j\beta}

\displaystyle=-C_{3}g_{ij}g_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{ij\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=0,

with
C_{1}=\frac{2q(q-1)}{(n-1)(n-2)},C_{2}=\frac{2p(p-1)}{(n-1)(n-2)}
and
C_{3}=\frac{2(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-1)(n-2)}
. The mixed terms
\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{ijk\alpha}
and
\big(\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\big)_{i\alpha\beta\gamma}
are null, and the other terms are obtained via the symmetries of the Weyl tensor. We denote the coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
(X,Y)
, where
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Let
V\in\Sigma
be
O(p)\times O(q)
invariant and let
C_{n,V}=\frac{4n}{3(n-2)^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx\right)^{-1}
. Using (
3.7
) and the explicit expression of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
we have

\displaystyle C_{n,V}^{-1}\hbox{Weyl}_{g}

\displaystyle\otimes B=C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[|X|^{2}|\nabla_{1}V|^{2}-(X,\nabla_{1}V)^{2}\right]\,dXdY

\displaystyle+C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[|Y|^{2}|\nabla_{2}V|^{2}-(Y,\nabla_{2}V)^{2}\right]\,dXdY

\displaystyle-C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[|X|^{2}|\nabla_{2}V|^{2}+|Y|^{2}|\nabla_{1}V|^{2}-2(X,\nabla_{1}V)(Y,\nabla_{2}V)\Big]\,dX\,dY

where
\nabla_{1}V
and
\nabla_{2}V
denote respectively the gradient of
V
with respect to
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
The
O(p)\times O(q)
invariance of
V
implies that
V(X,Y)=V(|X|,|Y|)
, and as a consequence we have
\nabla_{1}V(X,Y)=\partial_{1}V(X,Y)\frac{X}{|X|}
and
\nabla_{2}V(X,Y)=\partial_{2}V(X,Y)\frac{Y}{|Y|}
where
\partial_{1}V
and
\partial_{2}V
are the partial derivatives of
(r_{1},r_{2})\mapsto V(r_{1},r_{2})
. We obtain in the end

\displaystyle C_{n,V}^{-1}\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B

\displaystyle=-C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[|X|^{2}(\partial_{2}V)^{2}+|Y|^{2}(\partial_{1}V)^{2}-2|X||Y|\partial_{1}V\partial_{2}V\Big]\,dX\,dY

\displaystyle=-C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(|X|\partial_{2}V-|Y|\partial_{1}V\Big)^{2}\,dX\,dY\leq 0.

If
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B=0
, then
r_{2}\partial_{1}V-r_{1}\partial_{2}V
for all
r_{1}=|X|>0
and
r_{2}=|Y|>0
. This shows that
V=V(r)
, for
r^{2}=r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}
when
r_{1},r_{2}>0
, and this equality extends continuously to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
. We then get that
V
is radially symmetrical, contradicting our assumption. Therefore
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B<0
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

There are manifolds
(M,g)
of dimension
n\geq 5
and families of bubbles
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
as in Definition
3.1
such that
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}\otimes B<0
.

Proof.

We choose
p,q\geq 2
such that
n:=p+q\geq 5
. As before we denote by
g_{n}
the round metric in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. It follows from the result of Ding
[
9
]
that for all
L>0
, there exists
\tilde{V}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
that solves
\Delta_{g_{n}}\tilde{V}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\tilde{V}=|\tilde{V}|^{2^{*}-2}\tilde{V}
, satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\tilde{V}|^{2^{*}}\,dv_{g_{n}}\geq L
and is
O(p)\times O(q+1)
-invariant, that is
\tilde{V}(x,y)=\tilde{V}(|x|,|y|)
for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{q+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let
\pi_{N}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash\{N\}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the stereographic projection from the North pole. Let, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

V(x):=\left(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\tilde{V}\big(\pi_{N}^{-1}(x)\big).

Then
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves
\Delta_{\xi}V=|V|^{2^{\star}-2}V
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfies

-invariant since by Obata

26

finite-energy radial solutions of (

given by (

and

be the product metric of the round metrics on

and
\mathbb{S}^{q}

be any family of bubbles in

3.3.
The mass of the Green’s function in 3D

We conclude this section by recalling the definition of the mass when
n=3
, following Li-Zhu
[
19
]
:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed, connected
3-
dimensional Riemannian manifold. Let
h\in C^{1}(M)
be such that
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive. Let
G_{h}
be the Green’s function for
\Delta_{g}+h
. Then for all
x_{0}\in M
, there exists
m_{h}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
, denoted as the
mass
, such that

G_{h}(x,x_{0})=\frac{1}{4\pi d_{g}(x,x_{0})}+m_{h}(x_{0})+o(1)\hbox{ as }x\to x_{0}.

When
n=3
,
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive and
x_{0}\in M
, It follows from the construction of the Green’s function (see Aubin
[
3
]
, Robert
[
34
]
) that there exists
\beta_{x_{0}}\in C^{0}(M)\cap H_{2}^{p}(M)
for all
p<3
such that

(3.8)

G_{h}(x,x_{0})=\frac{\chi(x)}{4\pi d_{g}(x,x_{0})}+\beta_{x_{0}}(x)\hbox{ for all }x\in M\backslash\{x_{0}\},

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}(M)
is a cutoff function with
\chi(x)=1
in
B_{g}(x_{0},\frac{r_{g}}{2})
and
\chi(x)=0
in
M\backslash B_{g}(x_{0},r_{g})
. We have
(\Delta_{g}+h)\beta_{x_{0}}=-(\Delta_{g}+h)((4\pi)^{-1}\chi d_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})^{-1})=O(d_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})^{-1})
and standard elliptic theory then shows that

(3.9)

|\beta_{x_{0}}(x)|\leq C\,,\,|\nabla\beta_{x_{0}}(x)|\leq C\big(1+|\ln d_{g}(x_{0},x)|\big)\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{g}\left(x_{0},\frac{r_{g}}{2}\right)\backslash\{0\}.

It now follows from Definition
3.3
that

m_{h}(x_{0}):=\beta_{x_{0}}(x_{0}).

4.
A priori pointwise estimates for families of bubbles

4.1.
Statement of the results

In this section we will assume, as in the introduction, that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
is a family of possibly sign-changing solutions of (
1.3

u_{\varepsilon}=cB_{\varepsilon}+o(1)\quad\text{ in }H^{1}(M),

, where

Since

(see Robert
[
34
]

in

\varepsilon\to 0
and
\Delta_{g}+h

\Big|d_{g}(x,y)^{n-2}G_{h_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}\Big|\leq Cd_{g}(x,y)

(x,y)\in M\times M\backslash D(M)
and for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, where
D(M)=\{(x,x),x\in M\}
and where
\omega_{n-1}
is the area of the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Define, for
x\in M
,

F_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},x)=(n-2)\omega_{n-1}d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)^{n-2}G_{h_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon},x).

We define, for any
x\in M
and any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
:

(4.2)

\displaystyle B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\right)F_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\chi\left(d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\right)\right)(n-2)\omega_{n-1}\lambda(V)\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}G_{h_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon},x),

where
\lambda(V)
is as in (
2.9
) and for
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with
\chi\equiv 1
in
[0,\frac{i_{g}(M)}{4}]
and
\chi\equiv 0
in
[\frac{i_{g}(M)}{2},+\infty)
, and where
i_{g}(M)
is the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
. By (
4.1
) and properties of the Green’s function (see
[
34
]
) we have

F_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},x)=1+O\big(d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla_{x}F_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},x)|=O(1)\quad\text{ for }x\in M.

It is therefore easily seen with (
3.4
) that

B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}=B_{\varepsilon}+o(1)\quad\text{ in }H^{1}(M),

so that
(B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
is a family of bubbles which is equivalent to
(B_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
in the sense of Proposition
3.1
. The original assumption (
1.4
) implies that
u_{\varepsilon}
still satisfies

(4.3)

u_{\varepsilon}=cB^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}+o(1)\quad\text{ in }H^{1}(M)

for some
c>0
. We will thus, in the subsequent analysis, work with the family
(B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
. If
(y_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is a family of points in
M
satisfying
\mu_{\varepsilon}<<d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{i_{g}(M)}{4}
we also have, by (
2.4
), (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),

(4.4)

\displaystyle B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}(y_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=F_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(y_{\varepsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=(n-2)\omega_{n-1}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}G_{h_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\left(\lambda(V)+O\left(\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})}\right)\right).

The function

in

4.3

M

given by (

). The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 4.1

Let
(M^{n},g),n\geq 3

be a family of

\Delta_{g}+h

(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}

Then

in (

) and there exists a family

\left|\left|\frac{u_{\varepsilon}-B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}}{B_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\sigma_{\varepsilon}

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{p_{\varepsilon}-2}(1-2\alpha)}d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)^{-\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{p_{\varepsilon}-2}}+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\alpha})d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)^{-\frac{4\alpha}{p_{\varepsilon}-2}}\right)

holds.

Proof of (

).

Let

. For

we let

and let
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\alpha}
be fixed. We claim that there exists

such that, for any

(4.23)

\left|\left|\frac{u_{\varepsilon}}{\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(M\backslash B_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon}))}\leq C

4.23

, and up to replacing

by

\frac{u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}{\Phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\alpha}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}\to+\infty

as

\frac{\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}{u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}\geq\frac{\Delta_{g}\Phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\alpha}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}{\Phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\alpha}(y_{\varepsilon_{k}})}.

as

as

) and the choice of

large enough, and shows that (

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,2\delta)}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{(z_{\varepsilon}-y)_{i}}{|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{n}}\left(|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-2}-|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-2}\right)\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}dy.

Let us estimate the last term. Using the control (
4.6
) , we get that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B(0,2\delta)}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{(z_{\varepsilon}-y)_{i}}{|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{n}}\left(|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-2}-|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-2}\right)\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,2\delta)}|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{1-n}\left||\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}-1\right|\cdot|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-1}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,2\delta)\cap\{|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|<\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\}}|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{1-n}\left||\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}-1\right|\cdot|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-1}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,2\delta)\cap\{|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|\geq\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\}}|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{1-n}\left||\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}-1\right|\cdot|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-1}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}(p_{\varepsilon}-1)}+\int_{B(0,2\delta)\cap\{|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|\geq\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\}}|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{1-n}\left||\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}-1\right|\cdot|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-1}dy

The control (
2.8
) and (
4.6
) yield

\{|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|\geq\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\}\Rightarrow\left||\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-p_{\varepsilon}}-1\right|=o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, so that we get

\displaystyle\partial_{i}w_{\varepsilon}(z_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{(z_{\varepsilon}-y)_{i}}{|z_{\varepsilon}-y|^{n}}|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}-2}\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}dy+o\Bigg(\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(\mu_{\varepsilon}+|z_{\varepsilon}|)^{n-1}}\Bigg)+O(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}})

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}(z_{\varepsilon})+o\Bigg(\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(\mu_{\varepsilon}+|z_{\varepsilon}|)^{n-1}}\Bigg)+O(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}),

where the last equality follows from a representation formula for (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, since
V\in\Sigma
. This proves (
4.43
). Estimate (
4.42
) now follows from (

), (
4.43
), (
4.44
) and the explicit expression of

in (

).

Remark 4.2
.

We assumed for simplicity here that
\Delta_{g}+h
is coercive. If
\Delta_{g}+h
had a kernel and, more generally, if
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}
was a finite-energy family of solutions of (

) (without the one-bubble assumption (

)), an analogue of Proposition
4.1
would still hold. We refer for instance to Premoselli
[
31
]
where a generalisation of Proposition

was proven when

for all
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, in the general multi-bubble case and when

is allowed to have a kernel. The proof of
[
31
]
allows to deal with any multi-bubble configuration and would still adapt to the asymptotically critical setting

,
p_{\varepsilon}\to 2^{\star}
as a careful inspection of the proof shows. In the one-bubble case (

) that we consider here, the proof of Proposition

that we gave in this section is shorter and more direct.

5.

and

with
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\to 0

\displaystyle\big|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla B^{\mu_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}+d_{g}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\right)^{k}|\nabla^{k}u_{\varepsilon}(x)|

hold, where

and

We first recall the classical conformal normal coordinates result of Lee-Parker
[

]
. It states the existence of a positive function

such that, letting

for any

we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{y}(x)

\displaystyle=1+O\left(d_{g_{y}}(y,x)^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{g_{y}}(x)

\displaystyle=O\left(d_{g_{y}}(y,x)\right)

\displaystyle S_{g_{y}}

\displaystyle=O\left(d_{g_{y}}(y,x)^{2}\right)\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\sqrt{|g_{y}|}(x)

\displaystyle=1+O\left(d_{g_{y}}(y,x)\right)^{N}

as
d_{g_{y}}(y,x)\to 0
, for a fixed
N
large enough, where
\text{Ric}_{g_{y}}
denotes the Ricci tensor of
g_{y}
and where it is understood that

is computed with respect to the exponential map of
g_{y}
at
y
. For
\varepsilon>0
we let
g_{x_{\varepsilon}}=\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
and we let
\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{g_{x_{\varepsilon}}}
be the exponential chart of
g_{x_{\varepsilon}}
at
x_{\varepsilon}
. Let
\delta>0
be such that
2\delta<\inf_{\xi\in M}i_{g_{\xi}}(M)
. We define
g_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\varepsilon}
in
B(0,2\delta)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

(5.3)

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\left(\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{g_{x_{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{*}g_{x_{\varepsilon}}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{\varepsilon}}{\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}}\circ\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{g_{x_{\varepsilon}}}.

First, (
5.2
) shows that, for any fixed
x\in M
, we have

(5.4)

d_{g_{x}}(x,y)=d_{g}(x,y)+O\left(d_{g}(x,y)^{3}\right)\text{ as }d_{g}(x,y)\to 0.

The latter with (
2.8
), (
4.2
) and (
5.1
) implies that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Big|v_{\varepsilon}(x)-\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\varepsilon}\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)+C\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}},

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\varepsilon}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(\mu_{\varepsilon}+|x|)^{n-1}}+C\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}+d_{g_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon},x)\right)^{k}|\nabla^{k}v_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)\quad\text{ for }k=1,2

for any
x\in B(0,2\delta)
, where
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
and where we have let

\tilde{B}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)=\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\right).

Moreover, it follows from (
4.9
) that

(5.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}=V\hbox{ in }C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\hbox{ where }\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}v_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon}x).

Using (
1.3
) and the conformal invariance property of the conformal Laplacian it is easily seen that

satisfies

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}v_{\varepsilon}+A_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})+\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}-2}v_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }B(0,2\delta),

where

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}\circ\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{g_{x_{\varepsilon}}}
,

denotes the Euclidean metric and where we have let

,

(5.8)

\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\Bigg[\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{2-2^{\star}}\left(h_{\varepsilon}-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}\right)\Bigg]\left(\exp_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{g_{x_{\varepsilon}}}(x)\right)

and

(5.9)

A_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=\left(\Delta_{g_{\varepsilon}}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}.

Integrating (
5.7
) against
\langle x,\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}
in

with respect to the Euclidean metric yields the following Pohozaev identity (see Marques

):

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|_{\xi}^{2}+\delta(\partial_{\nu}v\varepsilon)^{2}+\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}}\delta|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B(0,\delta)}(p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star})x^{k}\partial_{k}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{p_{\varepsilon}-1-2^{\star}}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}dx

\displaystyle+n\left(\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{2^{\star}}\right)\int_{B(0,\delta)}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)A_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}dx.

In all the computations of this section it is intended that all the terms involving

disappear in the case where
p_{\varepsilon}=2^{\star}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. We now estimate all the integrals appearing in (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}(p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star})x^{k}\partial_{k}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-1-2^{\star}}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}dx

\displaystyle+n\left(\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{2^{\star}}\right)\int_{B(0,\delta)}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4n}(2^{\star}-p_{\varepsilon})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{\star}}dx+o\left(|p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}|\right)

and that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|_{\xi}^{2}+\delta(\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p_{\varepsilon}-2^{\star}}\delta|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=O(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{n-2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}o(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n\geq 5\\
O(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n=4\\
\end{array}\right.

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. A simple integration by parts shows that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\delta\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}d\sigma-\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}x^{k}\partial_{k}\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}\right)v_{\varepsilon}^{2}dx,

so that with (
5.5
), we get that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}

\displaystyle\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}x^{k}\partial_{k}\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}\right)v_{\varepsilon}^{2}dx+\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}o(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n\geq 5\\

\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=-\int_{B(0,\delta)}\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}dx+\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}o(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n\geq 5\\
O(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n=4\\
\end{array}\right.

Let now

be a smooth function. We have, for any

and by (

),

\Delta_{g_{\varepsilon}}w-\Delta_{\xi}w=-\left(g_{\varepsilon}^{ij}-\delta^{ij}\right)\partial^{2}_{ij}w-\partial_{i}(g_{\varepsilon})^{ij}\partial_{j}w+O\left(|x|^{N-1}|\nabla w|_{\xi}\right)

where repeated indices are summed over. By (

and the symmetries of the Riemann tensor show that for any

By (

, where

. Using again Cartan’s expansion of

\Delta_{g_{\varepsilon}}w-\Delta_{\xi}w=\frac{1}{3}\hbox{Weyl}_{g_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon})_{ipqj}x^{p}x^{q}\partial^{2}_{ij}w+O\left(|x|^{3}|\nabla^{2}w|\right)+O\left(|x|^{2}|\nabla w|\right).

Applying the latter to

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)A_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\hbox{Weyl}_{g_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon})_{ipqj}\int_{B(0,\delta)}x^{p}x^{q}\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)dx

\displaystyle+\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}o(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})&\hbox{ if }n\geq 5\\

\end{array}\right.

where again repeated indices are summed over. We now separate between the cases

\displaystyle\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{ipqj}

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\delta)}x^{p}x^{q}\partial_{i}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}v_{\varepsilon}\,dx

\displaystyle=O(R^{4}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})+\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{ipqj}\int_{B(0,R^{-1})\backslash B(0,R\mu_{\varepsilon})}x^{p}x^{q}\partial_{i}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}v_{\varepsilon}dx\,

\displaystyle=4\lambda(V)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})_{ipqj}\int_{B(0,R^{-1})\backslash B(0,R\mu_{\varepsilon})}x^{p}x^{q}\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{4}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{4}}\,dx

\displaystyle+O(R^{4}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})+O\Big(\varepsilon_{R}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\Big)

\displaystyle=O(R^{4}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2})+O\Big(\varepsilon_{R}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\Big)

where as before
\lim_{R\to+\infty}\varepsilon_{R}=0
and where the last term involving
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
vanishes by antisymmetry of
\hbox{Weyl}_{g}(x_{0})
. Going back to (
5.16
) with (
5.21
) we have proven that

(5.22)

\int_{B(0,\delta)}\left(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-2}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\right)A_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})dx\\
=o\Big(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\Big)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Remark that by (
2.9
) we have
2\omega_{3}\lambda(V)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|V|^{2}Vdx
. With this observation, using (
4.35
) and plugging (
5.11
), (
5.12
), (
5.14
), (
5.20
) and (
5.22
) in (
5.10
), we get (
1.6
), which proves Theorem
1.2
for
n=4
.

5.3.
The case

We are left with the case

. Using (

) and (

) the Pohozaev identity (

) writes as

(5.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|_{\xi}^{2}+\delta(\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{p_{\varepsilon}-6}\delta|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}(6-p_{\varepsilon})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{6}dx+o\left(|p_{\varepsilon}-6|\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,\delta)}\big(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\big)\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx

with

\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\left(\Delta_{g_{\varepsilon}}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)v_{\varepsilon}+\Big(\frac{1}{8}S_{g_{\varepsilon}}+\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}\Big)v_{\varepsilon},

where
\hat{h}_{\varepsilon}
is given by (

) and where

is defined in (

). First, by using (

) together with (

|\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}+|x|}\quad\text{ for }x\in B(0,\delta).

As a consequence, straightforward computations show that

(5.24)

\left|\int_{B(0,\delta)}\big(x^{k}\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\big)\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\right|=O\big(\delta\mu_{\varepsilon}\big)

as

. Independently, (

(5.25)

\left|\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{p_{\varepsilon}-6}\delta|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{\varepsilon}}d\sigma\right|=o(\mu_{\varepsilon})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We now compute the remaining boundary term. It follows from (

), (

), (

), (

(5.26)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\lambda\hat{G}(x)\quad\text{ in }C^{2}_{loc}(B_{2\delta}(0)\backslash\{0\}),

where we have let

(5.27)

\lambda:=4\pi\lambda(V)

and

\hat{G}(x):=\frac{G_{h}(x_{0},\hbox{exp}_{x_{0}}^{g_{x_{0}}}(x))}{\Lambda_{x_{0}}(\hbox{exp}_{x_{0}}^{g_{x_{0}}}(x))}\quad\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{2\delta}(0)\backslash\{0\}.

Here we recall that
\lambda(V)
is the first term in the expansion at infinity of

(see (

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|_{\xi}^{2}+\delta(\partial_{\nu}v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\int_{\partial B(0,\delta)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{G}\partial_{\nu}\hat{G}-\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla\hat{G}|_{\xi}^{2}+\delta(\partial_{\nu}\hat{G})^{2}\right)d\sigma.

6.
A non-blowup situation

In this last Section we show how Theorem
1.1
can be used to rule out the existence of one-bubble blowing-up solutions of equations like:

(6.1)

\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}+hu_{\varepsilon}=|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{\star}-2-\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}

at a given point, in some cases. In
[
8
]
, Deng-Musso-Wei have proposed a creative approach to construct families of sign-changing solutions
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
to the equation (
6.1
) that blow-up like a single bubble as in (
1.4
). The bubble that the authors choose in
[
8
]
is one of the symmetric bubbles constructed in
[
7
]
. In their arguments, Deng-Musso-Wei have introduced the function

\varphi_{\ell}:=h-c_{n}\Big(1+\frac{n-4}{3n}\ell\Big)S_{g},

where
\ell
is a parameter associated to the familie of bubbles in
[
7
]
. The function
\varphi_{\ell}
plays a crucial role in the results of
[
8
]
. We show below how, under some conditions, it is possible to use
\varphi_{\ell}
to rule out one-bubble blow-up.

We let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed, connected Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 5
that is locally conformally flat and has constant scalar curvature equal to
-1
. This is for instance satisfied if
(M,g)
is a closed hyperbolic manifold. We define

c_{n}=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\hbox{ and we let }1<t<1+\frac{n-4}{3n}.

Let
\xi_{0}\in M
be any point.

Step
6
.1:
We claim that there exists
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(6.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h(\xi_{0})=-tc_{n}\\
&\nabla h(\xi_{0})=0\\
&\nabla^{2}h(x_{0})\text{ is positive-definite }\\
&\Delta_{g}+h\text{ is coercive in }H^{1}(M).\end{aligned}\right.

Under (
6.2
),
\xi_{0}
is in particular a local strict minimum point of
h
.

We prove the Claim. Let for instance
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a cutoff function with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=1
in
B(0,1)
and
\chi(x)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B(0,2)
. Let, for
0<\delta<\frac{i_{g}(M)}{2}
and
x\in M
,

h_{\delta}(x)=1-\chi\Big(\frac{d_{g}(\xi_{0},x)}{\delta}\Big)+\chi\Big(\frac{d_{g}(\xi_{0},x)}{\delta}\Big)\Big(-tc_{n}+d_{g}(\xi_{0},x)^{2}\Big).

We claim that
h_{\delta}
satisfies (
6.2
) for
\delta
small enough (but fixed). Indeed, in
B_{g}(\xi_{0},\delta)
, we have
h_{\delta}(x)=-tc_{n}+d_{g}(\xi_{0},x)^{2}
, and straightforward computations show that

\|h_{\delta}-1\|_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}(M)}\leq C\delta^{2},

so that
\Delta_{g}+h_{\delta}
is coercive for
\delta
small enough. This proves the claim.

Step
6
.2:
We now let
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfy (
6.2
). For
\ell\geq 1
and
\xi\in M
we define

\displaystyle\varphi_{\ell}(\xi)

\displaystyle=h(\xi)-c_{n}\Big(1+\frac{n-4}{3n}\ell\Big)S_{g}(\xi)=h(\xi)+c_{n}\Big(1+\frac{n-4}{3n}\ell\Big),

We claim that

(6.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{g}+h\hbox{ is coercive};\\
\varphi_{\ell}(\xi_{0})>0\hbox{ for all }l\geq 1;\\
\xi_{0}\hbox{ is a $C^{1}-$stable critical point of }\varphi_{\ell}\hbox{ \emph{uniformly in }}\ell\geq 1.\end{array}\right\}

We prove the claim. We have that

\varphi_{\ell}(\xi_{0})=c_{n}\Big(1+\frac{n-4}{3n}\ell-t\Big)\geq c_{n}\Big(1+\frac{n-4}{3n}-t\Big)>0

for any
\ell\geq 1
. It is easily seen with (
6.2
) that, for any
\ell\geq 1
,
\xi_{0}
is a critical point of
\varphi_{\ell}
satisfying
\varphi_{\ell}(\xi_{0})>0

and
\varphi_{\ell}(\xi_{0})\to+\infty
as
\ell\to+\infty
. Since
\xi_{0}
has non-zero degree, it is a
C^{1}
-stable critical point of
h
(following the terminology of
[
20
]
). That is, there is
r_{0}>0
that satisfies the following: for any
0<r\leq r_{0}
there exists
\alpha>0
such that any
\tilde{h}\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)})
satisfying
\|\tilde{h}-h\|_{C^{1}(\overline{B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)})}\leq\alpha
has at least one critical point in
B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)
. By definition of
\varphi_{\ell}
we observe that
\xi_{0}
then remains a
C^{1}
-stable critical point of
\varphi_{\ell}

uniformly in
\ell\geq 1
:
that is, for any
0<r\leq r_{0}
and
\alpha
as above, and for any
\ell\geq 1
, any function
\tilde{\varphi}\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)})
satisfying
\|\tilde{\varphi}-\varphi_{\ell}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)})}\leq\delta
has at least one critical point in
B_{g}(\xi_{0},r)
. This proves (
6.3
).

Step
6
.3:
We claim that there is no family
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\in C^{2}(M)
of solutions of (
6.1
) such that

(6.4)

u_{\varepsilon}=B_{\varepsilon}+o(1)\hbox{ in }H^{1}(M),

where
B=(B_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a bubble centered at
(\xi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\in M
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\xi_{\varepsilon}=\xi_{0}
as in Definition
3.1
.

We prove the claim by contradiction. Since
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat, then the Weyl tensor vanishes. It then follows from Theorem
1.1
of the present paper that if there is blow-up like in (
6.4
), then equation (
1.5
) in particular tells us that

(6.5)

h(\xi_{0})+c_{n}=h(\xi_{0})-c_{n}S_{g}(\xi_{0})\geq 0.

But this is an obvious contradiction with (
6.2
) since
h(\xi_{0})=-tc_{n}
and
t>1
. This proves the claim.

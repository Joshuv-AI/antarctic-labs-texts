Boundary quotient C*-algebras of semigroups Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 46L08, 46L05, 47L55, 20M32 Thanks: Key words and phrases: Semigroup algebras, partial crossed product, coactions, boundary quotient, C*-envelope.
Evgenios T.A. Kakariadis, Elias G. Katsoulis, Marcelo Laca, Xin Li

Abstract
. We study two classes of operator algebras associated with a unital subsemigroup P P of a discrete group G G : one related to universal structures, and one related to co-universal structures. First we provide connections between universal C*-algebras that arise variously from isometric representations of P P that reflect the space 𝒥 \mathcal{J} of constructible right ideals, from associated Fell bundles, and from induced partial actions. This includes connections of appropriate quotients with the strong covariance relations in the sense of Sehnem. We then pass to the reduced representation C λ ∗ ​ ( P ) \mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P) and we consider the boundary quotient ∂ C λ ∗ ​ ( P ) \partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P) related to the minimal boundary space. We show that ∂ C λ ∗ ​ ( P ) \partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P) is co-universal in two different classes: (a) with respect to the equivariant constructible isometric representations of P P ; and (b) with respect to the equivariant C*-covers of the reduced nonselfadjoint semigroup algebra 𝒜 ⁡ ( P ) \mathcal{A}(P) . If P P is an Ore semigroup, or if G G acts topologically freely on the minimal boundary space, then ∂ C λ ∗ ​ ( P ) \partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P) coincides with the usual C*-envelope C env ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 ⁡ ( P ) ) \mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{env}}(\mathcal{A}(P)) in the sense of Arveson. This covers total orders, finite type and right-angled Artin monoids, the Thompson monoid, multiplicative semigroups of nonzero algebraic integers, and the a ​ x + b ax+b -semigroups over integral domains that are not a field. In particular, we show that P P is an Ore semigroup if and only if there exists a canonical ∗ * -isomorphism from ∂ C λ ∗ ​ ( P ) \partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P) , or from C env ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 ⁡ ( P ) ) \mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{env}}(\mathcal{A}(P)) , onto C λ ∗ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G) . If any of the above holds, then 𝒜 ⁡ ( P ) \mathcal{A}(P) is shown to be hyperrigid.



1.
Introduction

The use of C*-constructs has been central in the study of geometric and topological objects such as semigroups, graphs, dynamical systems etc.
It goes as far back as the work of Murray and von Neumann in the 1930’s and 1940’s, and has been a continuous source of inspiration for further developments.
On one hand one obtains a universal object that covers appropriate representations of the object by Hilbertian operators.
On the other hand one can ask for the minimal quotient that still carries a faithful copy of the original data, through a boundary space.
Similar questions appear in the nonselfadjoint context through the seminal work of Arveson
[
1
]
and the notion of the Šilov boundary.
In a recent work of the authors with Dor-On
[
12
]
it has been shown that C*-boundaries and Arveson’s Šilov boundary interconnect in a rather solid way in the case of product systems.
In this work we continue to investigate boundary quotients in both the C*- and the nonselfadjoint context.
This is part of a bigger programme that aims to elucidate the relations between boundary operator algebras.

Here we examine several operator algebras associated with a unital subsemigroup
P
of a group
G
, always assuming that
P
generates
G
.
This type of operator algebras have been under thorough investigation by many authors and connections have been established with C*-algebras of more general constructs.
The principal examples come from abelian semigroups, starting with
\mathbb{N}
in the seminal work of Coburn
[
4
]
, and more general totally ordered groups by Douglas
[
14
]
and Murphy
[
29
]
.
Nica
[
30
]
introduced quasi-lattice ordered semigroups and established the study of their semigroup C*-algebras as universal models of the left regular representation on
\ell^{2}(P)
.
Further motivating examples come from geometric group theory
[
5
,
6
,
23
]
or have a number-theoretic origin
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
and the references therein.

A major motivation for working with semigroups is that their operator algebras lie in the intersection of several categories of current interest, such as partial dynamical systems, product systems and Fell bundles.
The different realizations bring in existing results from each category for studying their structure.
Conversely, semigroup algebras can act as a medium for cross-pollination of techniques between their wider supercategories, as well as a testing ground for new structural theorems.
As examples we mention the realization: by partial crossed products for computing the K-theory
[
8
]
, for computing the KMS-states
[
7
]
, or for deducing simplicity
[
27
]
; and as product systems for nuclearity results
[
20
,
25
]
.

The representations of
P
that we consider are more than just by isometries as the universal isometric C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
may not be carrying much structure; for example
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
fails to be even nuclear.
The point of inspiration is the left regular representation
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
and in this paper we consider isometric representations of
P
that reflect various aspects of the structure of the constructible right ideals as they are manifested in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The study is carried mainly in five directions:

(i)

universal/maximal constructs;

(ii)

reduced representations;

(iii)

boundary quotients;

(iv)

co-universal C*-algebras; and

(v)

Šilov boundaries.

An important aspect in (i)–(iv) has been the existence of a coaction by the group
G
that induces a topological grading.
In the past, connections with (v) had been established for abelian lattices where the coaction is well understood as an action of the compact dual
[
11
,
13
]
.
Three natural questions that arise in this context:

Qn. 1.

What is the connection between universal semigroup constructions?

Qn. 2.

What is the connection between the induced semigroup boundary quotients?

Qn. 3.

Is there a co-universal semigroup C*-algebra and what is its form?

Here we answer these questions guided by two beacons (see Figure
1
for a summary).
First we establish links between different classes of representations of
P
and the universal C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
of the constructible representations.
Secondly we identify the boundary quotient C*-algebra
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
as the co-universal object in both the C*-algebraic and the nonselfajoint algebraic approaches.
Below we provide a detailed discussion of these results.

1.1.
On universal C*-algebras

Our starting point is the universal C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
with respect to contructible semigroup representations in the sense of
[
25
]
.
This is an equivariant quotient of the universal isometric C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
, that takes into account the space
{\mathcal{J}}
of the constructible right ideals in
P
, and thus sits closer to the structure of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
A second important variant is the boundary quotient
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
, given by the strongly covariant representations of
P
as induced by Sehnem’s work on product systems
[
35
]
.
This universal C*-algebra models the isometric representations that lift automatically to faithful representations on the fixed point algebra.

3.6
we show that strongly covariant representations are constructible, providing the vertical arrow in the following commutative diagram:

Universal/maximal constructs are easier to work with, as they enjoy exactness with respect to induced ideals.
We use this type of arguments for identifying
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
with C*-algebras associated with Fell bundles and partial actions.
On one hand we consider the induced Fell bundles
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
, and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\operatorname{sc}}
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
.
On the other hand we use the fixed point algebra
D_{s}(P)
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
and the minimal
G
-invariant subspace
\partial\Omega_{P}
of its spectrum to realize them as partial crossed products (see Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.14
).
Namely we have

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\simeq D_{s}(P)\rtimes G\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}_{\operatorname{sc}})\simeq C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes G.

In passing, we show that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
for the inverse semigroup

{\mathcal{I}}_{V}:=\{V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\mid p_{i},q_{i}\in P;n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

of
V
-words in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
(see Theorem
3.2
), which improves a previous result of Li
[
26
]
by removing the Toeplitz condition from
P
.
By using a result of Norling
[
31
]
we deduce that
{\mathcal{J}}
is independent if and only if
D_{s}(P)\simeq D_{\lambda}(P)
canonically (see Corollary
3.3
).
These results address
(Qn. 1)
.
In general
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})
differs from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The algrebraic relations that define the Fell bundle induced in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
are completely described by Laca and Sehnem in their recent work
[
22
]
.
Even though we do not rely here on
[
22
]
, it has motivated us in the final stages of this research to free the setup from the independence condition on
{\mathcal{J}}
and work at the general level of the constructible representations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
.

1.2.
On reduced C*-algebras

In
[
27
]
it has been established that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
coincides with the partial crossed product of a partial action of
G
on the diagonal algebra
D_{\lambda}(P)
.
It coincides with the reduced C*-algebra of
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
if and only if
{\mathcal{J}}
is independent.
We have three further reduced boundary quotients arising from:

(a)

the partial crossed product picture, i.e.,
C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G
;

(b)

the strong covariance Fell bundle, i.e.,
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{\operatorname{sc}})
; and

(c)

the strong covariance relations in the left regular representation, i.e.,
q_{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P))
.

Items (a) and (b) are automatically
*
-isomorphic due to the realization of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
as
C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G
.
Under exactness we can pass the results from the universal C*-algebras between items (a) and (c) down to the reduced case.
Indeed exactness induces a normal co-action on
q_{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P))
from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
, and thus we can use the fixed-point-algebra property of
q_{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P))
inherited from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
(see Theorem
3.10
).
This settles
(Qn. 2)
and provides the upper-left part of Figure
1
.

1.3.
On co-universality

We next turn our attention to
(Qn. 3)
.
By
[
10
, Lemma 5.7.10]

\partial\Omega_{P}
embeds as the smallest, closed,
G
-invariant, nonempty subset of the spectrum of
D_{s}(P)
.
Consequently in Theorem
4.2
we deduce that the quotient map

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)

factors through any non-trivial equivariant representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
, i.e.,
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is co-universal for the non-trivial equivariant constructible representations of
P
.

On the other hand we connect
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
to a Šilov boundary.
The natural candidate for a nonselfadjoint algebra is the closed algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
generated by the image of
P
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The normal coaction
\overline{\delta}
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
descends to
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
giving rise to the cosystem
({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
, and in Theorem
4.4
we establish that

\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta}).

We further explore conditions under which
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
is
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))
.
This is automatic for abelian semigroups, however it is unknown if in general
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))
admits automatically a coaction of
G
.
By using the simplicity criteria of
[
27
]
we find that this is the case when
P
is an Ore semigroup or when
G
acts topologically freely on
\partial\Omega_{P}
.
In particular, if
P
is an Ore semigroup then the C*-envelope is the usual
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
Surprisingly this is also a defining property for a semigroup
P\subseteq G
to be Ore.
We thus derive the right-lower part of Figure
1
.

These diagrams expand on the theory of Ore semigroups in amenable groups.
In this case the scheme collapses into two distinct classes of
*
-isomorphic C*-algebras: (a) the Fock type ones

\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\simeq D_{s}(P)\rtimes_{r}G\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq D_{\lambda}(P)\rtimes_{r}G;

and (b) their boundary quotients

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)

\displaystyle\simeq q_{\operatorname{sc}}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P))\simeq\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P):=C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G),

which have the co-universal property with respect to
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Šilov boundary

The reader may refer to
[
33
]
for the general theory of nonselfadjoint operator algebras and dilations of their representations, which we will avoid repeating here in full length.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an operator algebra, which in this paper means a closed subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
for a Hilbert space
H
.
We say that
(C,\iota)
is a
C*-cover
of
{\mathcal{A}}
if
\iota\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to C
is a completely isometric representation with
C=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\iota({\mathcal{A}}))
.
The
C*-envelope

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is a C*-cover
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}),\iota)
with the following co-universal property:
if
(C^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
is a C*-cover of
{\mathcal{A}}
then there exists a (necessarily unique)
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon C^{\prime}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}})
such that
\Phi(\iota^{\prime}(a))=\iota(a)
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
Arveson defined the C*-envelope in
[
1
]
and computed it for a variety of operator algebras, predicting its existence in general.
Ten years later Hamana
[
19
]
confirmed Arveson’s prediction by proving the existence of injective envelopes for the unital case.
The C*-envelope is the C*-algebra generated in the injective envelope of
{\mathcal{A}}
once this is endowed with the Choi-Effros C*-structure.

Dritschel and McCullough
[
15
]
provided an alternative proof based on maximal dilations for the unital case.
A
dilation
of a representation
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a representation
\phi^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H^{\prime})
such that
H\subseteq H^{\prime}
and
\phi(a)=P_{H}\phi^{\prime}(a)|_{H}
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
A completely contractive map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is called
maximal
if every dilation
\phi^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H^{\prime})
is trivial, i.e.,
P_{H}\phi^{\prime}(a)=\phi(a)=\phi^{\prime}(a)|_{H}
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
It follows that the C*-envelope is the C*-algebra generated by a maximal completely isometric representation.
It does not hold in general that if
\pi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}})\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a
*
-representation then it is the unique contractive completely positive (ccp) extension of
\pi|_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.
The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is called
hyperrigid
if this is the case for any representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}})
.

The basic examples of C*-envelopes arise in the context of uniform algebras: the C*-envelope of a uniform algebra is formed by the continuous functions on its Šilov boundary.
The unconditional existence of the C*-envelope provides a non-commutative analogue of this result.
Consider
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
.
An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\lhd\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
is called a
boundary ideal
if the quotient map
q_{{\mathcal{I}}}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})/{\mathcal{I}}
restricts to a completely isometric map on
{\mathcal{A}}
.
The
Šilov ideal

{\mathcal{I}}_{s}
is by definition the boundary ideal that contains all boundary ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
The existence of the C*-envelope implies the existence of the Šilov ideal; in particular it follows that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}})
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})/{\mathcal{I}}_{s}
.

2.2.
Coactions on operator algebras

We denote the minimal tensor product by
\otimes
.
We will need some elements about coactions on C*algebras as well as some results from
[
12
]
about coactions on operator algebras.

For a discrete group
G
we write
u_{g}
for the unitary generator associated with
g\in G
in the full group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
.
We write
\lambda_{g}
for the generators of the left regular representation
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
Recall that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
admits a faithful
*
-homomorphism

\Delta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G);u_{g}\mapsto u_{g}\otimes u_{g}.

On the other hand
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
admits a faithful
*
-homomorphism

\Delta_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G);\lambda_{g}\mapsto\lambda_{g}\otimes\lambda_{g}.

Definition 2.1
.

[

Let

be an operator algebra.
A

is a completely isometric representation

is norm-dense in

satisfies the coaction identity

(\delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)})\delta=({\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\otimes\Delta)\delta.

If, in addition, the map

is injective then the coaction

is called

.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is an operator algebra and

is a coaction on
{\mathcal{A}}
, then we will refer to the triple
({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
as a

.
A map

between two cosystems
({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
and

is said to be

\delta^{\prime}\phi=(\phi\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\delta
.

If
({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
is a cosystem then

for all

is a homomorphism.

[

A coaction

In particular the definition of the coaction here extends that of a full coaction on a C*-algebra by Quigg
[

]
.

Remark 2.3
.

[

Suppose that

.
Due to Fell’s absorption principle,
\delta_{\lambda}
promotes to a normal coaction
\delta
of
G
on
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\delta_{\lambda}=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\delta
.

.

by a direct computation that uses the C*-envelope of the ambient operator algebra.
In order to state the result explicitly we need to make some preliminary remarks and establish the notation.
Suppose

is a cosystem, let

be the C*-envelope of

, and recall that the spatial tensor product of completely isometric maps is completely isometric.
Then the representation of

\textstyle{{\mathcal{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

The following theorem summarizes fundamental results about existence and representations of C*-envelopes for cosystems.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
12
, Theorem 3.8 and Corollary 3.10]

Let
({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
be a cosystem and let

be the inclusion map.
Then

(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta),\iota,\delta_{\operatorname{\textup{env}}})\simeq(\mathrm{C}^{*}(i(a_{g})\otimes u_{g}\mid g\in G),(i\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\delta,{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Delta).

If in addition

is normal on
{\mathcal{A}}
then
\delta_{\operatorname{\textup{env}}}
is normal on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
.

Moreover if
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)\to B
is a
*
-homomorphism that is completely isometric on
{\mathcal{A}}
then it is faithful on the fixed point algebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}},G,\delta)
.

Let us close this section with some remarks on topological gradings from
[

,

.
Recall that a

\{{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of a C*-algebra

consists of linearly independent subspaces that span a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{B}}
and are compatible with the group

, i.e.,
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{*}={\mathcal{B}}_{g^{-1}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}\cdot{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{gh}
.
By
[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
the linear independence condition can be substituted by the existence of a conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}
.
The maximal C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{B}})
of
{\mathcal{B}}
is defined as universal with respect to the representations of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
The reduced C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})
of
{\mathcal{B}}
is defined by the left regular representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{B}})
.
If
\Phi
is a representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
then the range of
\Phi
has a natural topological grading
\Phi({\mathcal{B}})=\{\Phi({\mathcal{B}}_{g})\}_{g\in G}
.
By
[
18

there are equivariant
*
-homomorphisms making the following diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\max}(\Phi({\mathcal{B}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Phi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Phi({\mathcal{B}}))}

commutative.
A topological grading defines a
Fell bundle
and once a representation of a Fell bundle is established the two notions are the same.
In a loose sense a Fell bundle
{\mathcal{B}}
over a discrete group

is a collection of Banach spaces

, often called

, that obey to canonical algebraic properties and the C*-norm properties; see

So we will alternate between these two notions.
Spectral subspaces of coactions on C*-algebras are an important source of topological gradings.

Let

.

Let

be a coaction of

3.
Semigroup algebras

In this section we present the concrete and the universal C*-algebras that are related to semigroups, partial crossed products and product systems.
We provide the identification of several universal and reduced C*-algebras that arise in this context.

3.1.
The reduced semigroup algebra

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a discrete group
G
.
We write

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\mid p\in P)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}(P):=\overline{\operatorname{alg}}\{V_{p}\mid p\in P\}

for the C*-algebra and the operator algebra, respectively, generated by the left-creation operators

V_{p}\colon\ell^{2}(P)\to\ell^{2}(P);\delta_{s}\mapsto\delta_{ps}.

Let
U\colon\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)
be the unitary operator determined by

U(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{g})=\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{sg}\text{ for all }s\in P,g\in G,

and let
\lambda_{g}
be the canonical unitary corresponding to
g\in G
in the left regular representation of
G
.
A routine calculation shows that

(V_{p}\otimes\lambda_{p})U=U(V_{p}\otimes I)\text{ for all }p\in P.

Thus the
*
-homomorphism
\overline{\delta}_{\lambda}
obtained by composition

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq\phantom{ooooooo}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes I\mid p\in P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ad}_{U}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}(V_{p}\otimes\lambda_{p}\mid p\in P)}

is faithful and satisfies the coaction identity.
We also note that

\displaystyle[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)]_{g}

\displaystyle:=\{a\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\mid\overline{\delta}_{\lambda}(a)=a\otimes\lambda_{g}\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\operatorname{span}\{V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\mid p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=g;n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};p_{i},q_{i}\in P\},

and thus by construction these fibers are norm-dense in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
The reverse inclusion, and hence equality, follows by applying
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes E_{g}
, where
E_{g}
is the
g

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
In particular by restricting to
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
we get that

[{\mathcal{A}}(P)]_{p}=\mathbb{C}\cdot V_{p}\text{ for all }p\in P\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{A}}(P)]_{g}=(0)\text{ for all }g\notin P.

Let
\overline{\delta}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
be the normal coaction induced by
\overline{\delta}_{\lambda}
.
The induced faithful conditional expectation

is implemented by compressing
({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\lambda)\overline{\delta}
to the

-entry and thus

E_{\lambda}(V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{p_{n}})=\begin{cases}V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}&\text{if }p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In
[
25
]
it has been established that

E_{\lambda}(V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}})=\sum_{s\in P}Q_{s}(V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}))Q_{s},

where

is the projection on
\mathbb{C}\cdot\delta_{s}
.
In other words,
E_{\lambda}
is the faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
given by compressing to the diagonal.
We will write

for the fixed point algebra
[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)]_{e}
of
\overline{\delta}
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.

We will require some additional facts from
[
25
]
.
For a set

and

we write

(3.1)

pX:=\{px\mid x\in X\}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{-1}X:=\{y\in P\mid py\in X\}.

Note here that by definition

.
We write
{\mathcal{J}}
for the smallest family of right ideals of

containing

and

that is closed under left multiplication and taking pre-images under left multiplication (as in the sense above), i.e.,

It is important to notice that a constructible right ideal

.

{\mathcal{J}}
is
independent
when the following holds:

for all
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}_{n}\in{\mathcal{J}}
with
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}={\mathbf{x}}
there is an
i_{0}
such that
{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}}
.

It then follows that
{\mathcal{J}}
is independent if and only if
\{E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\}_{{\mathbf{x}}\in F}
is a basis for
B_{F}

[
10
, Corollary 5.6.29]
.

3.2.
Universal semigroup C*-algebras

The
isometric semigroup C*-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
is the universal C*-algebra generated by isometries
\{{\mathfrak{v}}_{p}\mid p\in P\}
satisfying
{\mathfrak{v}}_{p}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q}={\mathfrak{v}}_{pq}
.
Universality implies that there exists a coaction of
G
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
determined by
{\mathfrak{v}}_{p}\mapsto{\mathfrak{v}}_{p}\otimes u_{p}
and having fixed point algebra

[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{{\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{1}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{1}}\cdots{\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{n}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{n}}\mid p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e\}.

The
full semigroup C*-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{full}}(P)
of
P
is the universal C*-algebra generated by isometries
\{\mathcal{V}_{p}\mid p\in P\}
and projections
\{\mathcal{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
satisfying the relations:

I.

\mathcal{V}_{pq}=\mathcal{V}_{p}\mathcal{V}_{q}
and
\mathcal{V}_{p}\mathcal{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mathcal{V}_{p}^{*}=\mathcal{E}_{p{\mathbf{x}}}
;

II.

\mathcal{E}_{P}=I
,
\mathcal{E}_{\emptyset}=0
; and
\mathcal{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{{\mathbf{y}}}=\mathcal{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}}
.

Introduced in
[
25
]
,
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{full}}(P)
offers a model of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
where the projections
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}
corresponding to the constructible ideals are regenerated in the obvious way, i.e.,

{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}={\mathcal{V}}_{p_{1}}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}_{q_{1}}\cdots{\mathcal{V}}_{p_{n}}^{*}\\
V_{q_{n}}V_{q_{n}}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}_{p_{n}}\cdots{\mathcal{V}}_{q_{1}}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}_{p_{1}}\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P.

The full semigroup C*-algebra admits a coaction of
G
whose fixed point algebra
contains
the commutative C*-algebra

D_{\textup{full}}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}).

In
[
25
]
it is shown that the canonical
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{full}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is faithful on
D_{\textup{full}}(P)
if and only if
{\mathcal{J}}
is independent.

The
constructible semigroup C*-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
of
P
introduced in
[
25
, Definition 3.2]
is the universal C*-algebra generated by isometries
\{v_{p}\mid p\in P\}
and projections
\{e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
satisfying the relations:

I.

v_{pq}=v_{p}v_{q}
;

II.

e_{\emptyset}=0
;

III
G
.

if
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}
satisfy
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
then

v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}=e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P.

It follows by
[
25
, Lemma 3.3]
that the family
\{v_{p},e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid p\in P,{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
satisfies also the relations

e_{P}=1,\,v_{p}e_{{\mathbf{x}}}v_{p}^{*}=e_{p{\mathbf{x}}},\,\text{and}\,e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\cdot e_{{\mathbf{y}}}=e_{{\mathbf{x}}\cap{\mathbf{y}}}.

Therefore
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
is a quotient of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{full}}(P)
.
Universality implies that there exists a coaction of
G
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
that induces a conditional expectation

E\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}\mid p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e\}.

Hence the projections
e_{{\mathbf{x}}}
have dense linear span
[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]_{e}
.
We will write

D_{s}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}(e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}})=[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]_{e}.

In particular the fixed point algebra
[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]_{e}
is the inductive limit of the (finite dimensional and thus closed) C*-subalgebras

{\mathcal{B}}_{F}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in F\}\textup{ for finite $\cap$-closed }F\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}.

If
{\mathcal{J}}
is independent then the canonical
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is faithful on
D_{s}(P)
, by
[
25
, Corollary 2.27]
.
We will prove the converse of that, by using a result of Norling
[
31
]
.

Towards this end we need to make a connection with inverse semigroups.
Recall that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is an inverse semigroup then the reduced C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{S}})
is the C*-algebra generated by the operators

\lambda(s)\colon\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\})\to\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}}\setminus\{0\}),

determined by

\lambda(s)\delta_{x}=\begin{cases}\delta_{sx}&\text{ if }s^{*}s\geq xx^{*},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

There is also a universal C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
generated by a universal representation
\{u_{s}:s\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
by partial isometries.
The fastest way to obtain an inverse semigroup from our
P
is to use its left regular representation
V
and define

{\mathcal{I}}_{V}:=\{V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}V_{q_{n}}^{*}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};p_{i},q_{i}\in P,1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
is an inverse semigroup (of partial isometries on
\ell^{2}(P)
), so we have two C*-algebras

\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})=\mathrm{C}^{*}(u(V)\mid V\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\lambda(V)\mid V\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V}).

It was shown in
[
26
]
that under the assumption that
P
satisfies the Toeplitz condition from
[
27
, Definition 5.8.1]
, there is a canonical isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
.
We remove this assumption next.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup in a group
G
and let
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
be the induced inverse semigroup in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Then there exists a canonical
*
-isomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V});v_{p}\mapsto u(V_{p})

that restricts to an isomorphism of
D_{s}(P)
to the C*-subalgebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
generated by the semilattice of idempotents in
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
.

Proof.
.

Since the universal representation
u
of
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
is multiplicative and unital, the composition

p\in P\mapsto V_{p}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V}\mapsto u(V_{p})\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})

is clearly an isometric representation of
P
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
.
Recall now that if
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\cdots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e_{G}
and
{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P
, then the relation

E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}=V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}},

holds in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Thus
E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
and the composition map

{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\mapsto E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V}\mapsto u(E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]})\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})

satisfies

u(E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]})=u(V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}})=u(V_{p_{1}})^{*}u(V_{q_{1}})\cdots u(V_{p_{n}})^{*}u(V_{q_{n}}).

Thus the families
\{u(V_{p})\mid p\in P\}
and
\{u(E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]})\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
satisfy the relations defining
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
, and by the universal property there is a
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
mapping
v_{p}
to
u(V_{p})
.

For the other direction we want to show that the assignment

(3.2)

V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{V}\mapsto v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)

is a well-defined representation of
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
by partial isometries.
To this end let us set

V:=V_{p_{1}}^{*}V_{q_{1}}\cdots V_{p_{n}}^{*}V_{q_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad W:=V_{r_{1}}^{*}V_{s_{1}}\cdots V_{r_{m}}^{*}V_{s_{m}}

and suppose that
V=W
.
Hence we also have that
VV^{*}=WW^{*}=WV^{*}=VW^{*}
,
and so

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P=s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\dots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P=:{\mathbf{x}}.

For
V\neq 0
we have that
{\mathbf{x}}\neq\emptyset
, and in particular we get that

VW^{*}\delta_{t}=VV^{*}\delta_{t}=E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\delta_{t}=\delta_{t}\text{ for all }t\in{\mathbf{x}}.

For any such
t\in{\mathbf{x}}
we deduce that

q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\cdots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}t=t,

which yields
q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\cdots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}=e_{G}
.
Consequently we obtain another description for
{\mathbf{x}}
by using the left regular representation.
That is, the relation

E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}=VV^{*}=VW^{*}=E_{[q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\cdots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P]}

yields

{\mathbf{x}}=q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\cdots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}s_{m}^{-1}r_{m}\cdots s_{1}^{-1}r_{1}P.

Likewise we obtain similar expressions for
{\mathbf{x}}
corresponding to the equations
E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}=WV^{*}=WW^{*}
.
Let us set

v:=v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad w:=v_{r_{1}}^{*}v_{s_{1}}\cdots v_{r_{m}}^{*}v_{s_{m}}.

Then by the properties of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
and the descriptions for
{\mathbf{x}}
obtained above we get

e_{{\mathbf{x}}}=vv^{*}=ww^{*}=wv^{*}=vw^{*}.

This shows that
v
and
w
are partial isometries.
By considering the dual equalities

V^{*}V=W^{*}W=V^{*}W=W^{*}V,

we have the symmetrical

v^{*}v=w^{*}w=v^{*}w=w^{*}v.

Since this implies

v=v(v^{*}v)=v(w^{*}w)=(vw^{*})w=(ww^{*})w=w,

the map given in (
3.2
) is well defined and determines a
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
.
Choosing
p_{1}=e_{G}
and
q_{1}=p
shows that this epimorphism is indeed the inverse of the
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
constructed above, and the proof of the isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
is complete.

Finally, notice that by Proposition
3.1
, the semilattice
E
of idempotents in
{\mathcal{I}}_{V}
coincides with the set of characteristic functions of constructible right ideals viewed as operators on
\ell^{2}(P)
.
Therefore
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V})
is the image of
D_{s}(P)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
in the isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

(cf.
[
31
, Theorem 3.22]
and
[
25
, Corollary 2.27]
)
Let

\textstyle{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{full}}(P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

of canonical

Then the following are equivalent:

is independent.

(ii)

.

∎

3.3.
Sehnem’s strong covariance relations

Working at the generality of product systems, Sehnem
[
35
]
has provided a quotient of the Toeplitz C*-algebra with the following properties: (a) it admits an injective copy of the coefficient algebra; and (b) every equivariant
*
-representation of the quotient that is injective on the coefficient algebra is automatically faithful on the fixed point algebra.
Let us review her construction for a unital semigroup
P
in a group
G
.
This corresponds to the product system
X
over
P
such that every fiber
X_{p}
is
\mathbb{C}
.
For a finite set
F\subseteq G
set

K_{F}:=\bigcap_{g\in F}gP.

For
r\in P
and
g\in F
define

I_{r^{-1}K_{\{r,g\}}}:=\begin{cases}(0)&\text{if }K_{\{r,g\}}\neq\emptyset\text{ and }r\notin K_{\{r,g\}},\\
\mathbb{C}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}:=\bigcap_{g\in F}I_{r^{-1}K_{\{r,g\}}}.

Let the spaces

X_{F}:=\left\langle\delta_{r}\in\ell^{2}(P)\mid I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}\neq(0)\right\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad X_{F}^{+}:=\bigoplus_{g\in G}X_{gF}\subseteq\ell^{2}(P)\otimes\ell^{2}(G).

For every
p\in P
define the operator

V_{F,p}\colon X_{F}^{+}\to X_{F}^{+};X_{F}\ni\delta_{r}\mapsto\delta_{pr}\in X_{pF}.

It is well-defined as
I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}=I_{(pr)^{-1}(pr\vee pF)}
for all
r\in P
, and
I_{r^{-1}(r\vee F)}=I_{(s^{-1}r)^{-1}(s^{-1}r\vee s^{-1}F)}
for all
r\in sP
.
It follows that
V_{F,p}
is an isometry and thus we obtain a
*
-representation

\Phi_{F}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}(X_{F}^{+});{\mathfrak{v}}_{p}\mapsto V_{F,p}.

For the projections

Q_{g,F}\colon X_{F}^{+}\to X_{gF}

we get that

V_{F,p}Q_{g,F}=Q_{pg,F}V_{F,p}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{F,p}^{*}Q_{g,F}=Q_{p^{-1}g,F}V_{F,p}^{*}.

Therefore
Q_{e,F}
is reducing for
\Phi_{F}([\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e})
.
For
f\in[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}
define

\left\|f\right\|_{F}:=\left\|Q_{e,F}\Phi_{F}(f)Q_{e,F}\right\|=\left\|\Phi_{F}(f)Q_{e,F}\right\|=\left\|Q_{e,F}\Phi_{F}(f)\right\|.

When we specialize the definition of strongly covariant representations from
[
35
]
to the case of
semigroup algebras, which are obtained from product systems with one-dimensional fibers, we get the following.

Definition 3.4
.

(cf.
[
35
, Definition 3.2]
)
Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup of a group
G
.
An isometric representation of
P
is called
strongly covariant
if it vanishes on the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{e}\lhd[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}
given by

{\mathcal{I}}_{e}:=\{f\in[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}\mid\lim_{F}\left\|f\right\|_{F}=0\},

where the limit is taken with respect to the partial order induced by inclusion on finite sets
F
of
G
.
We write
{\mathcal{I}}_{\infty}
for the ideal of
strong covariance relations
generated by
{\mathcal{I}}_{e}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
the universal C*-algebra with respect to the strong covariant representations of
X
.

Since
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
is a quotient of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
by an induced ideal, it inherits a faithful coaction
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(G)
.
Moreover since
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
is a unital
*
-representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)
, we get that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)\neq\{0\}
.
An important point of Sehnem’s theory is that the image
v_{p}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
of each
p\in P
is an isometry, and thus non-zero.
The next observation follows from
[
35
, Theorem 3.10]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
.
Then a unital
*
-representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
that admits a coaction of
G
is automatically faithful on
[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)]_{e}
.

Proof.
.

The conclusion follows by the property (C3) of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
by
[
35
, Theorem 3.10]
.
We note here that the coefficient algebra of the product system in this setting is
\mathbb{C}
, and that trivially it embedded in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
.
∎

We see that every
\Phi_{F}
is actually a restriction (and thus a sub-representation) of the “reduced” coaction of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Since
Q_{e,F}
is reducing for
\Phi_{F}([\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e})
we obtain the maps

(3.3)

\textstyle{[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]_{e}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda}
\textstyle{[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)]_{e}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi:=\bigoplus\limits_{\textup{fin }F\subseteq G}\Phi_{F}|_{X_{F}}}
\textstyle{\prod\limits_{\text{fin }F\subseteq G}{\mathcal{B}}(X_{F})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{\operatorname{qd}}}
\textstyle{{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\prod\limits_{\text{fin }F\subseteq G}{\mathcal{B}}(X_{F})$}\left/\,\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$c_{0}({\mathcal{B}}(X_{F})\mid{\text{fin }F\subseteq G})$}\right.}.}

Thus by definition it follows that
{\mathcal{I}}_{\infty}=\left\langle\ker(q_{\operatorname{qd}}\Phi\lambda\rho)\cap[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)]_{e}\right\rangle
.
Therefore every
e
-graded relation in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
passes to the strong covariance algebra.
In particular we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
P
be a unital subsemigroup in a group
G
.
Then the canonical
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{iso}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
factors through
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
.

Proof.
.

For convenience let us set
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P):=\mathrm{C}^{*}(\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p}\mid p\in P)
.
It is clear that the family
\{\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p}\mid p\in P\}
is an isometric representation of
P
.
We have to show that there is a family of projections

\{\dot{{\mathfrak{e}}}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)

which, along with the family of isometries
\{\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p}\mid p\in P\}
, satisfies the
e
-graded axioms of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
.
Recall that the projections in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
can be recreated from the isometries, i.e.,

e_{{\mathbf{x}}}=v_{p_{1}}^{*}v_{q_{1}}\cdots v_{p_{n}}^{*}v_{q_{n}}v_{q_{n}}^{*}v_{p_{n}}\cdots v_{q_{1}}^{*}v_{p_{1}}\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P.

We are going to use this as a model.
Fix
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{m},q_{m}\in P
and set

{\mathfrak{f}}:={\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{1}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{1}}\cdots{\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{n}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{n}}{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{n}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{n}}\cdots{\mathfrak{v}}_{q_{1}}^{*}{\mathfrak{v}}_{p_{1}}.

Clearly
{\mathfrak{f}}
is selfadjoint.
Using the diagram (
3.3
) preceding the statement we see that

\lambda\rho({\mathfrak{f}})=E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}\quad\text{for}\quad{\mathbf{x}}=p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P.

As projections in C*-algebras are defined by
*
-algebraic relations, by strong covariance we then derive that the element

\dot{{\mathfrak{f}}}:=\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p_{1}}^{*}\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{q_{1}}\cdots\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p_{n}}^{*}\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{q_{n}}\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{q_{n}}^{*}\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p_{n}}\cdots\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{q_{1}}^{*}\dot{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{p_{1}}

is a projection in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P)
.
Indeed we have that
{\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{e}
since

q_{\operatorname{qd}}\Phi\lambda\rho({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathfrak{f}}^{2})=q_{\operatorname{qd}}\Phi(E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]})-q_{\operatorname{qd}}\Phi(E_{[{\mathbf{x}}]}^{2})=0,

and so
\dot{{\mathfrak{f}}}=\dot{{\mathfrak{f}}}^{2}
.

Next let
r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{n},s_{n}
such that

{\mathbf{x}}:=p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{m}^{-1}q_{m}P=r_{1}^{-1}s_{1}\dots r_{n}^{-1}s_{n}P

and set

3.4.
Fell bundles

If

Definition 3.9

Let

Let

be a unital semigroup in a group

.
Then

\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{I}}_{V}),\quad\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{I}}_{V}),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}}_{\operatorname{sc}})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{sc}}(P),

by canonical

.

{\mathcal{J}}
is independent if and only if
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)

.

We have already seen in Theorem

that

3.5.
The partial crossed product realization

A alternative description of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
as a reduced partial crossed product has been given in
[

.
Let us recall some notation and facts about partial product systems from
[
18
,
28
]
.

Suppose that a group
G
with identity

acts on a topological space

by a partial action
\theta
in the sense that:

(i)

there is a collection
\{\Omega_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of open subsets of
X
such that
\Omega_{e}=X
;

(ii)

there is a collection
\{\theta_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of homeomorphisms
\theta_{g}\colon\Omega_{g^{-1}}\to\Omega_{g}

\theta_{e}={\operatorname{id}}_{X}

(iii)

for all

\theta_{g_{2}}(\Omega_{(g_{1}g_{2})^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{g_{2}^{-1}})=\Omega_{g_{1}^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{g_{2}}

\theta_{g_{1}g_{2}}(x)=\theta_{g_{1}}\circ\theta_{g_{2}}(x)
for all

.

Then the reduced crossed product algebra
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G
is defined in the following way.
On
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(X)
let the twisted representation of
C_{0}(\Omega_{g})
given by

\mu(f)\delta_{h}\otimes\xi=\delta_{h}\otimes M_{h}(f)\xi,

where

(M_{h}(f)\xi)(x)=\begin{cases}f(\theta_{h}(x))\xi(x)&\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{h^{-1}},\\
0&\text{ if }x\notin\Omega_{h^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, let
E_{g}
be the projection on
\overline{\mu(C_{0}(\Omega_{g^{-1}}))(\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(X))}
so that

E_{g}(\delta_{h}\otimes\delta_{x})=\begin{cases}\delta_{h}\otimes\delta_{x}&\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{(gh)^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{h^{-1}},\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and for

\dot{u}_{g}:=(\lambda_{g}\otimes I_{\ell^{2}(X)})\cdot E_{g}.

Then the
reduced crossed product
of the partial action is defined by

C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\mu(f_{g})\dot{u}_{g}\mid f\in C_{0}(\Omega_{g}),g\in G\},

and it follows that it is a C*-algebra.
It is known that the unitary operator

U\colon\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(X)\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(X)\otimes\ell^{2}(G);\delta_{h}\otimes\delta_{x}\otimes\delta_{h^{\prime}}\mapsto\delta_{h}\otimes\delta_{x}\otimes\delta_{hh^{\prime}}

induces a “reduced” coaction
\delta_{\lambda}

\textstyle{C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G)\otimes I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ad}_{U}}
\textstyle{(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G)\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G),}

and thus a normal coaction
\delta
of
G
on
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{r}G
.

Recall that there is also the
universal partial crossed product
, denoted by
C_{0}(X)\rtimes G
, which is the universal C*-algebra subject to
\ell^{1}
-bounded representations of the algebra of monomials

where

(f_{1}\delta_{g_{1}})\cdot(f_{2}\delta_{g_{2}}):=\theta_{g_{1}}(\theta_{g_{1}^{-1}}(f_{1})f_{2})\delta_{g_{1}g_{2}}\text{ for all }f_{i}\in C_{0}(\Omega_{g_{i}}),g_{i}\in G.

In this case one can define a partial action on

\theta_{g}^{\prime}:=\theta_{g}|_{\Omega_{g}^{\prime}}

If the

in the sense that it sends generators to their restriction on

Conversely, quotients of

4.

We will show that

carries two types of co-universality.
The first one is in the C*-context with respect to the representations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)
, while the second one
is in the nonselfadjoint context, with respect to contractive representations of the tensor algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
that are compatible with the canonical coaction of

We introduce some terminology to make this precise.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G

An isometric semigroup representation

\partial_{G}[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]

for every

-equivariant constructible isometric representation

there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}(T)\to\partial_{G}[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]
.

We now prove the first co-universal property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
.
Then

\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\partial_{G}[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)].

Proof.
.

Let
T

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)\simeq D_{s}(P)\rtimes G
.
Let

be the associated Fell bundle on

.
Since

is non-trivial, the isometry

is non-zero for every

, so the Fell bundle

is non-zero and thus
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{T}})
is non-zero.
By
[
18
, Proposition 21.3]
there is a canonical
*
-epimorphism

D_{s}(P)\rtimes_{r}G\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{s})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{T}}).

By Proposition
3.11
we have that
[\mathrm{C}^{*}(T)]_{e}=C(Y)
for some
G
-invariant closed subspace of the spectrum of
D_{s}(P)
.
Therefore, due to the existence of the faithful conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{T}})
we can write

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{T}})\simeq C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G.

By
[
10
, Lemma 5.7.10]
we have the inclusion
\partial\Omega\hookrightarrow Y
.
We deduce that

\mathrm{C}^{*}(T)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{T}})\simeq C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G\to C(\partial\Omega)\rtimes_{r}G,

and the proof is complete.
∎

The next aim is to show that
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is the C*-envelope of the cosystem
({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
.
First we show that it is a boundary quotient of
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
, and hence the terminology makes sense both in the selfadjoint and the nonselfadjoint context.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
and
Y
be a closed
G
-invariant subspace of
\Omega_{P}
.
Then the canonical embedding

{\mathcal{A}}(P)\hookrightarrow C(\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G\to C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G

is unital completely isometric.
Thus
C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G
becomes a (normal) C*-cover for
({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
.

Proof.
.

Let us denote by
\iota\colon{\mathcal{A}}(P)\to C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G
the canonical completely contractive embedding, where
C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G
acts on
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(Y)
.
By construction

\iota(V_{p})=(\lambda_{p}\otimes I)\cdot E_{p}\text{ for all }p\in P,

where
E_{p}
is the projection on

E_{p}(\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\ell^{2}(Y))=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum^{\oplus}}_{h\in G}\mathbb{C}\delta_{h}\otimes\ell^{2}(\Omega_{(ph)^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{h^{-1}}\cap Y).

Notice that for
r\in P
we have that
I=V_{r}^{*}V_{r}\in D_{r^{-1}}
and so
\Omega_{r^{-1}}=\Omega_{P}
.
Hence

\Omega_{(ph)^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{h^{-1}}\cap Y=Y\text{ for all }h\in P.

Thus, if
Q_{P}\colon\ell^{2}(G)\to\ell^{2}(P)
is the canonical projection we see that

E_{p}\cdot(Q_{P}\otimes I)=(Q_{P}\otimes I)\cdot E_{p}=(Q_{P}\otimes I)

for all
p\in P
.
Therefore

\iota(V_{p})\cdot(Q_{P}\otimes I)=(\lambda_{p}\otimes I)\cdot(Q_{P}\otimes I)=V_{p}\otimes I=(Q_{P}\otimes I)\cdot(\lambda_{p}\otimes I)\cdot(Q_{P}\otimes I),

and consequently

\iota(a)\cdot(Q_{P}\otimes I)=a\otimes I\text{ for all }a\in{\mathcal{A}}(P).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
[a_{ij}]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}(P)
.
Then we have that

\|[a_{ij}]\|\geq\|[\iota(a_{ij})]\|\geq\|[\iota(a_{ij})]\cdot(I_{n}\otimes Q_{P}\otimes I)\|=\|[a_{ij}\otimes I]\|=\|[a_{ij}]\|,

giving that the map
{\operatorname{id}}_{n}\otimes\iota
is isometric.
As this holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
it follows that
\iota
is completely isometric.

Finally recall that
C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G
admits a normal coaction
\delta^{\prime}
of
G
.
By definition we have that

(\iota\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\overline{\delta}(V_{p})=\dot{u}_{p}\otimes u_{p}=((\lambda_{p}\otimes I)\cdot E_{p})\otimes u_{p}=\delta^{\prime}\iota(V_{p})

for all
p\in P
, and the proof is complete.
∎

We can now prove the second co-universal result for
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
.
Then

\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta}).

Proof.
.

By Theorem
2.6
, normality of the coaction on
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
implements a normal coaction on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
.
Since any
G
-equivariant quotient of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
implements a partial action by Proposition
3.11
, faithfulness of the conditional expectation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
yields

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})\simeq C(Y)\rtimes_{r}G\quad\text{for}\quad C(Y):=[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})]_{e}.

On the other hand by Proposition
4.3
we have that
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is a C*-cover for
({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
, and so there exists a canonical
*
-epimorphism

\Phi\colon\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)=C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})

that intertwines the conditional expectations.
Hence
\Phi
restricts to a surjection from
C(\partial\Omega_{P})
onto
C(Y)
.
Minimality of
\partial\Omega_{P}
yields
\partial\Omega_{P}=Y
, and thus
\Phi
is faithful.
∎

Recall that the action of
G
on a partial system
(\{\Omega_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\theta_{g}\}_{g\in G})
is called
topologically free
if for every
e\neq g\in G
the set
\{\omega\in\Omega_{g^{-1}}\mid\theta_{g}(\omega)\neq\omega\}
is dense in
\Omega_{g^{-1}}
.
Next we see that under a topological freeness assumption we have that the Šilov boundary of the cosystem is the usual Šilov boundary of
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
.
If the partial action of
G
on
\partial\Omega_{P}
is topologically free then

\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P)).

Proof.
.

Clearly any C*-cover of the cosystem is a C*-cover for the algebra.
Thus by Theorem
4.4
and the definition of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))
we have a canonical
*
-epimorphism

\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P)).

By
[
27
, Corollary 3.22]
we get that
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is a simple C*-algebra and thus the
*
-epimorphism is faithful.
∎

Let us now consider the Ore semigroup case.
Recall that
P
is a
left-Ore semigroup
if it is cancellative and left-reversible, in the sense that it satisfies
pP\cap qP\neq\emptyset
for all
p,q\in P
.
A semigroup
P
is left-Ore if and only if it embeds in a group
G
in such a way that
G=PP^{-1}
. Equivalently, the set
PP^{-1}
of formal right quotients forms a group.
See
[
16
,
32
]
.
Modulo an easy translation from right to left reversibility, Laca
[
21
, Theorem 1.2]
shows that a left-Ore semigroup has the following (universal) extension property for semigroup homomorphisms into groups: every semigroup homomorphism
P\to\mathcal{G}
into a group
{\mathcal{G}}
has a unique extension to a group homomorphism
G=PP^{-1}\to{\mathcal{G}}
.
See also
[
11
, Theorem 2.2.4]
for an alternative proof that uses a construction of
G
by direct limits.
When
P
is an Ore semigroup then
\partial\Omega_{P}
is a singleton, hence
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\{\text{pt}\}\rtimes_{r}G=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
; see the comments following
[
10
, Definition 5.7.9]
.
We will see that if
P
is left-Ore and
G=PP^{-1}
is its enveloping semigroup, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.
In fact, we will prove that this is a characteristic property of Ore semigroups.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
P
be a unital semigroup in a group
G
that it generates.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

P
is an Ore semigroup (in which case necessarily
G=PP^{-1}
).

(ii)

The map
V_{p}\mapsto\lambda_{p}
extends to a completely isometric map
{\mathcal{A}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
.

(iii)

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
by a canonical
*
-homomorphism that fixes
P
.

(iv)

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
by a canonical
*
-homomorphism that fixes
P
.

If any of the above holds then
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
is hyperrigid.

Proof.
.

We will show [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii)], and that item (ii) implies hyperrigidity of
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]:
If
P
is an Ore semigroup then by construction
\partial\Omega_{P}=\{\text{pt}\}
is a singleton; see the comments following
[
10
, Definition 5.7.9]
.
Thus
\{\text{pt}\}\rtimes_{r}G=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
and the canonical embedding

{\mathcal{A}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G);V_{p}\mapsto\lambda_{p}

is completely isometric by Proposition
4.3
.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)]:
Suppose there is a completely isometric map
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
with
\Phi(V_{p})=\lambda_{p}
for all
p\in P
.
Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
is a C*-cover for
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
.
Since contractive dilations of unitaries are trivial we get that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
is the C*-envelope.
For the same reason
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
is hyperrigid.

Thus
\Phi
extends uniquely to a
*
-epimorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
which we denote by the same symbol.
Let
p,q\in P
.
Since

\Phi(V_{p}V_{p}^{*}V_{q}V_{q}^{*})=\lambda_{p}\lambda_{p}^{*}\lambda_{q}\lambda_{q}^{*}=I,

we have that
V_{p}V_{p}^{*}V_{q}V_{q}^{*}\neq 0
.
It follows that
pP\cap qP\neq\emptyset
, and thus
P
satisfies the Ore property.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)]:
Assuming item (ii) we have that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
is a C*-cover of the cosystem
({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})
, and thus there is a canonical
*
-epimorphism

\{\text{pt}\}\rtimes_{r}G=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P),G,\overline{\delta})

that fixes
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
and intertwines the faithful conditional expectations.
As the fixed point algebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
is trivially
\mathbb{C}
and the
*
-epimorphism is non-zero, we have that the
*
-epimorphism is faithful.

[(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)]:
Assuming item (iii) we have that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)
is a C*-cover for
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
by unitaries, and thus (as above) we deduce that it is its C*-envelope.

[(iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]:
Assuming item (iv) we have trivially that the map

{\mathcal{A}}(P)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G);V_{p}\mapsto\lambda_{p}

is completely isometric, and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

We can combine Theorem
4.2
with Theorem
4.6
to deduce that any item of Theorem
4.6
is equivalent to having an equivariant
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G)\simeq\partial_{G}[\mathrm{C}^{*}_{s}(P)]
.

Remark 4.8
.

It is interesting to note that Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
4.6
(iv) give the same conclusion
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))
in opposite situations; the former applies to semigroups with a boundary spectrum that is large enough to support a topologically free partial action of
G
, while the latter applies when the boundary spectrum is a singleton so the boundary action is as far from topologically free as possible.
Combined, they cover the following classes of examples:

(i)

abelian submonoids of groups, in particular,
multiplicative semigroups of nonzero algebraic integers (obviously Ore);

(ii)

total orders in groups (obviously Ore);

(iii)

Artin monoids in Artin groups of finite type (Ore by
[
2
]
);

(iv)

quasi-lattice orders with trivial core (where the action is topologically free by
[
6
, Proposition 5.5]
), in particular, the subclass consisting of Artin monoids in right-angled Artin groups having no direct
\mathbb{Z}
factor,
[
6
, Corollary 5.7]
;

(v)

the Thompson monoid
F^{+}
in the Thompson group
F
(Ore, in fact, lattice ordered);

(vi)

the
ax+b
-semigroup
R\rtimes R^{\times}
over an integral domain that is not a field (where the action is topologically free by combining
[
24
, Corollary 8]
with
[
10
, page 243]
, or by
[
22
, Corollary 8.4 and Proposition 6.18]
).

In fact only simplicity of the boundary quotient
\partial\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(P)
is required in the proof of Theorem
4.5
to deduce that it coincides
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{A}}(P))
.
A characterization of simplicity for general ring C*-algebras has been identified in
[
24
, Corollary 8]
.

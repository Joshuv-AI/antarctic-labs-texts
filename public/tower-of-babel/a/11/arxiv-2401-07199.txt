Introduction and main results

This paper studies the existence of multi-bump solutions for the following nonlinear magnetic
Schrödinger equation with logarithmic nonlinearity

-(\nabla+iA(x))^{2}u+\lambda V(x)u=|u|^{q-2}u+u\log|u|^{2},\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}),

where the magnetic potential

is the nonnegative continuous function.

Logarithmic Schrödinger equation has wide applications in quantum mechanics, quantum optics, nuclear physics, transport and diffusion phenomena, open quantum system, Bose-Einstein condensations, see

&v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

In present paper, our goal is to find the existence of standing wave solutions for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation.

The idea of this paper is derived from the following nonlinear Schrödinger equations with deepening potential well

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v+(\lambda V(y)+P(y))v=v^{p},&y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&v(y)>0,&y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

Preliminary

In this section, we outline the variational framework for problem (

For any

The space

Since

\left|\nabla_{A}u(x)\right|\geq|\nabla|u(x)||.

(2.1)

Let

Obviously,

According to the fact there exist functions

F_{2}(t)-F_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}|t|^{2}\log|t|^{2},\ \forall t\in\mathbb{C},

and

F_{2}(s)-F_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\phi_{R}dx+\frac{C}{R}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{2}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\lambda_{n}V(x)+1\right)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\phi_{R}dx+\frac{C}{R}+o_{n}(1).

Hence, fixing
\xi>0
and passing to the limit, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0)}\left(\left|\nabla_{A}u_{n}\right|^{2}+\left(\lambda_{n}V(x)+1\right)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\right)dx\leqslant\frac{C}{R}<\xi

for some
R
sufficiently large.
Note that
G_{2}^{\prime}
has a subcritical growth, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u_{n}\right)\overline{w}dx\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u\right)\overline{w}dx\right),\quad\forall w\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u_{n}\right)\overline{u_{n}}dx

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u\right)\overline{u}dx\

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}\left(x,u_{n}\right)dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}\left(x,u\right)dx.

Now, recalling that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),w\rangle=0
for all
w\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{2}\leqslant K,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce that

\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla_{A}u\overline{\nabla_{A}\omega}+u\overline{\omega})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\overline{\omega}dx\right)=\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u\right)\overline{\omega}dx\right),

so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla_{A}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\overline{u}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u\right)\overline{u}dx.

Together this equality and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}\rangle=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\left|\nabla_{A}u_{n}\right|^{2}+\left(\lambda_{n}V(x)+1\right)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)\overline{u_{n}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,u_{n}\right)\overline{u_{n}}dx+o_{n}(1)

leads to

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\left|\nabla_{A}u_{n}\right|^{2}+\left(\lambda_{n}V(x)+1\right)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)\overline{u_{n}}dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla_{A}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\overline{u}dx,

up to subsequence if necessary, we have

u_{n}\rightarrow u\quad\text{ in }H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0

and

F_{1}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)\overline{u_{n}}\rightarrow F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\overline{u}\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since
F_{1}
is convex, even and
F(0)=0

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\overline{t}\geq F_{1}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence, using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, it holds

F_{1}\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow F_{1}(u)\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Due to

\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla_{A}u_{n}-\nabla_{A}u\right|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}dx+\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}dx,

it follows that

\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}^{2}\rightarrow 0,

which implies (i) holds. The proofs of (ii) and (iii) are similar to that of Lemma
3.5
, so we omit it.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume
\left(u_{\lambda,R}\right)
be a family of nontrivial solutions of (
3.1
) satisfying
\left(I_{\lambda,R}\left(u_{\lambda,R}\right)\right)
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
for any
\lambda>0
and
R>0
large enough. Then there exists
K>0
that does not depend on
\lambda>0
and
R>0
, and
R^{*}>0
such that

\left\|u_{\lambda,R}\right\|_{\infty,R}\leq K,\quad\forall\lambda>0,\ R\geqslant R^{*}.

Proof.

Consider
\lambda>0,L>0
and
\beta>1
, let

\displaystyle|u_{L,\lambda}|:=\begin{cases}|u_{\lambda,R}|,&\text{ if }|u_{\lambda,R}|\leq L,\\
L,&\text{ if }|u_{\lambda,R}|\geq L,\end{cases}

\displaystyle|z_{L,\lambda}|=|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}|u_{\lambda,R}|\text{ and }\omega_{L,\lambda}=|u_{\lambda,R}||u_{L,\lambda}|^{\beta-1}.

Since
\left(u_{\lambda,R}\right)
is a nontrivial solution to (
3.1
), it follows that

-(\nabla+iA(x))^{2}u_{\lambda,R}+(\lambda V(x)+1)u_{\lambda,R}=g_{2}(x,u_{\lambda,R})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda,R}).

By using Kato’s inequality

\Delta\left|u_{\lambda,R}\right|\geq\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\overline{u_{\lambda,R}}}{\left|u_{\lambda,R}\right|}(\nabla+iA(x))^{2}u_{\lambda,R}\right),

we obtain that

-\Delta\left|u_{\lambda,R}\right|-(\lambda V(x)+1)\left|u_{\lambda,R}\right|\leq g_{2}(x,|u_{\lambda,R}|)-F_{1}^{\prime}(|u_{\lambda,R}|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Now, choosing
z_{L,\lambda}
as a test function, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}\left|\nabla|u_{\lambda,R}|\right|^{2}dx+2(\beta-1)\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2\beta-3}|u_{\lambda,R}|\nabla|u_{\lambda,R}|\nabla|u_{L,\lambda}|dx

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}(0)}(\lambda V(x)+1)|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}\left|u_{\lambda,R}\right|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R}(0)}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(|u_{\lambda,R}|\right)|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}|u_{\lambda,R}|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}G_{2}^{\prime}\left(x,|u_{\lambda,R}|\right)|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}|u_{\lambda,R}|dx.

According to the definition of
G_{2}
,

G_{2}^{\prime}(x,t)\leqslant F_{2}^{\prime}(t)\leqslant Ct^{p-1},\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\ p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right).

(3.11)

Hence, by using (
3.10
) and (
3.11

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\left|\nabla\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2}+\left|\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda,R}|^{p}|u_{L,\lambda}|^{2(\beta-1)}dx

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda,R}|^{p-2}|\omega_{L,\lambda}|^{2}dx.

By Hölder inequality, it is easy to see that

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda,R}|^{p-2}|\omega_{L,\lambda}|^{2}dx\leq C\beta^{2}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda,R}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\omega_{L,\lambda}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

(3.13)

Moreover, it follows from Sobolev inequality that

\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq C\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\left|\nabla\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2}+\left|\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2}\right)dx.

(3.14)

Combining (
3.12
)-(
3.14

\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\omega_{L,\lambda}\right|^{2^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant C\beta^{2}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda}|^{p\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Regarding the use of the Fatou’s lemma for variable
L
, we know that

\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|u_{\lambda}\right|^{2^{*}\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq C\beta^{2}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda}|^{p\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}},

so

\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|u_{\lambda}\right|^{2^{*}\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}\beta}}\leq C^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\beta^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{\lambda}|^{p\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}.

(3.15)

Since
\left(I_{\lambda,R}\left(u_{\lambda,R}\right)\right)
is bounded and
\left(u_{\lambda,R}\right)
is a solution of (
3.1
), similar to the Lemma
3.2

satisfying

for
\lambda>0
and
R>0
large enough. Fixing any sequences
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
and
R_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, it is easy to see that
\left(u_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}\right)
satisfies the hypotheses from Lemma
3.6

u_{\lambda_{n},R_{n}}\rightarrow u
in
H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

On a Class of Integrals related to Wallis Integrals
Andie Burazin, Veselin Jungić, Miroslav Lovrić

Abstract
In this article we provide a motivation for studying integrals ∫ 0 π 2 x ​ cos m ⁡ x ​ 𝑑 x \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x\cos^{m}x~dx , m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} . We demonstrate several properties of this class of integrals and show that it is closely related to the class of Wallis integrals, ∫ 0 π 2 cos m ⁡ x ​ 𝑑 x \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{m}x~dx , m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} .



1
Introduction

In the theory of sound waves, a sawtooth wave is described as a wave that ramps upwards
and then sharply drops; see Figure 1 (left).
The problem we study here stems from
an investigation of
a modification of such a wave,
in which the ramp (rise) is circular, and the amplitudes are limited
by a specific line; see Figure 1 (right).

Figure 1:

A sawtooth wave (left) and a modified sawtooth wave (right).

To construct this wave, we start
with a right triangle
\Delta A_{0}B_{0}C

whose hypotenuse
\overline{A_{0}C}
is of length 1, and the angle

\angle CA_{0}B_{0}=x,
where
0<x<\pi/2.

For

i=1,2,3,\ldots

we construct,
recursively,
a sequence of points

A_{i}\in\overline{A_{0}C}
and
B_{i}\in\overline{B_{0}C}
so that

|\overline{A_{i-1}A_{i}}|=|\overline{A_{i-1}B_{i-1}}|

and

\overline{A_{i}B_{i}}\perp\overline{B_{0}C}.

Among other quantities we studied, we looked into the areas of the circular sectors (Figure 1 (right), shaded).
It is a straightforward exercise to
show that the area of the
i
-th circular sector
A_{i}B_{i}A_{i+1}
is

A_{i}(x)={1\over 2}\,x\cos^{2}x(1-\cos x)^{2i}.

To get a better feel about the function
x\mapsto A_{i}(x)
, for
x\in(0,\pi/2)
, we look at
its
average value

\tilde{A_{i}}={1\over\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{2}x(1-\cos x)^{2i}\,dx.

After expanding the integrand using Newton’s binomial formula

\displaystyle\tilde{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{2}x\sum_{m=0}^{2i}{2i\choose m}(-\cos x)^{m}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\sum_{m=0}^{2i}(-1)^{m}{2i\choose m}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{m+2}x\,dx

(1)

we are left
with a linear combination of integrals
of the form

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx

where
k\geq 2.

Our original idea (discussed here as well)
was to prove that (
1
) can be written in the form

\tilde{A_{i}}=\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}\pi+{\gamma_{i}\over\pi}

where

\alpha_{i},

\beta_{i},
and
\gamma_{i}
are rational numbers.

Problem.

What are the values of the integrals
I_{k}
? What are their properties?

It turns out that we can say quite a lot, especially when
k
is an even number.
(Although in this paper
we focus on even powers
of the cosine, we will make a few comments about odd powers
as well.)

Wallis integrals

2
Calculating
I_{k}

First, we derive a useful iterative formula for the definite integrals
I_{k}.

We start with the expression

\left(\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\right)^{\prime}=k\cos^{k}x-(k-1)\cos^{k-2}x,

multiply it by
x,
and rearrange terms:

x\cos^{k}x={1\over k}x\left(\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\right)^{\prime}+{k-1\over k}x\cos^{k-2}x

Integrating both sides, we obtain

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx={1\over k}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\left(\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\right)^{\prime}\,dx+{k-1\over k}I_{k-2}

To evaluate the remaining integral,
we use integration by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\left(\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\right)^{\prime}\,dx

\displaystyle=\Bigl.\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\Bigr|_{0}^{\pi/2}-\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos^{k-1}x\,dx

\displaystyle=0+\left.{1\over k}\cos^{k}x\right|_{0}^{\pi/2}=-{1\over k}.

Therefore, when
k\geq 2,

I_{k}={k-1\over k}I_{k-2}-{1\over k^{2}}

(3)

Note that the well-known recurrence relation for
Wallis integrals,

W_{k}={k-1\over k}W_{k-2},

for
k\geq 2,

may be obtained in a similar way.

Recurrence (
3
), together with

I_{0}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\,dx={1\over 8}\pi^{2}

and

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos x\,dx=\Bigl.x\sin x+\cos x\Bigr|_{0}^{\pi/2}=-1+{1\over 2}\pi

gives

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{2}x\,dx={1\over 2}I_{0}-{1\over 4}=-{1\over 4}+{1\over 16}\pi^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{3}x\,dx={2\over 3}I_{1}-{1\over 9}=-{7\over 9}+{1\over 3}\pi

(5)

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{4}x\,dx={3\over 4}I_{2}-{1\over 16}=-{1\over 4}+{3\over 64}\pi^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{5}x\,dx={4\over 5}I_{3}-{1\over 25}=-{149\over 225}+{4\over 15}\pi

and so on.
In general,
from (
3
) and the values of
I_{0}
and
I_{1}

we conclude that, for an even number
k\geq 0,

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx=a_{k}\pi^{2}+b_{k}

(7)

where
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are rational numbers, and

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx=c_{k}\pi+d_{k}

(8)

for and odd number
k\geq 1,
where
c_{k}
and
d_{k}
are rational numbers.

The recurrence relation (
3
) allows us to calculate
a_{k},

b_{k},

c_{k}
and
d_{k}.

Using induction, we obtain, for
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 2n,

I_{k}=I_{k-2n}\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}{k-2j-1\over k-2j}-\left({1\over k^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}{1\over(k-2i)^{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}{k-2j-1\over k-2j}\right)

(9)

adopting the convention (and using it, from now on) that
\sum_{i=1}^{0}
is zero.

When
k=2n,
formula (
9
) gives

I_{2n}=I_{0}\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}{2n-2j-1\over 2n-2j}-\left({1\over 4n^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}{1\over(2n-2i)^{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}{2n-2j-1\over 2n-2j}\right)

(10)

with
n\geq 1.

For instance, using (
10
) with
n=1
we obtain

I_{2}=I_{0}\cdot{1\over 2}-{1\over 4}={1\over 16}\pi^{2}-{1\over 4}

which agrees, of course, with the value we computed earlier.

Simplifying the first product in (
10
), we obtain

\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}{2n-2j-1\over 2n-2j}={2n-1\over 2n}\cdot{2n-3\over 2n-2}\cdot{2n-5\over 2n-4}\cdot\cdots\cdot{1\over 2}={(2n-1)!!\over(2n)!!}

and therefore (recall that
I_{0}=\pi^{2}/8
)

a_{2n}={1\over 8}{(2n-1)!!\over(2n)!!}={1\over 4\pi}W_{2n}

(11)

for
n\geq 1.

The second product in formula (
10
) for
I_{2n}
can be simplified in a similar way:
expand the product, and multiply and divide by
(2(n-i)-1)!!
and
(2(n-i))!!

to obtain

\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}{2n-2j-1\over 2n-2j}={(2n-1)!!\,(2(n-i))!!\over(2n)!!(2(n-i)-1)!!}

and thus, when
n\geq 1,

\displaystyle b_{2n}

\displaystyle=-\left({1\over 4n^{2}}+{(2n-1)!!\over 4(2n)!!}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}{1\over(n-i)^{2}}{\,(2(n-i))!!\over(2(n-i)-1)!!}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left({1\over 4n^{2}}+{(2n-1)!!\over 4(2n)!!}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}\right)

\displaystyle=-{(2n-1)!!\over 4(2n)!!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}

Because
I_{0}=\pi^{2}/8,
it follows that
b_{0}=0.

Thus, we have shown that,
for
k=2n\geq 2,

\displaystyle I_{2n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{2n}x\,dx=a_{2n}\pi^{2}+b_{2n}

\displaystyle={1\over 8}{(2n-1)!!\over(2n)!!}\pi^{2}-{(2n-1)!!\over 4\cdot(2n)!!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}

\displaystyle={\pi\over 4}W_{2n}-{1\over 2\pi}W_{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}W_{2n}\left(\pi-{2\over\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}\right)

(12)

The fraction involving double factorials can be simplified:

{(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}={2^{j}\cdot j(j-1)(j-2)\cdot\cdots\cdot 1\over(2j-1)(2j-3)(2j-5)\cdot\cdots\cdot 1}\cdot{(2j)!!\over(2j)!!}={2^{2j}\,j!\,j!\over(2j)!}={2^{2j}\over{2j\choose j}}

The formula for
I_{k}
when
k
is an odd number is derived similarly.
As we focus on even values of
k,

we will not demonstrate it here.

3
Investigating
I_{k}

We now
relate definite integrals

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx\quad\hbox{and}\quad J_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}2x\,dx.

(Note that
I_{0}=J_{0}=\pi^{2}/8.
)

Using the substitution
u=2x,

J_{k}
can be written as

J_{k}={1\over 4}\int_{0}^{\pi}u\cos^{k}u\,du={1\over 4}\left(I_{k}+\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi}u\cos^{k}u\,du\right)

Now substitute
u=\pi-x
to obtain

\displaystyle J_{k}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}\left(I_{k}+\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\pi-x)(-1)^{k}\cos^{k}x\,dx\right)

\displaystyle={1\over 4}\left(I_{k}-(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx+(-1)^{k}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}x\,dx\right)

Thus,

\displaystyle J_{k}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}\left(I_{k}+(-1)^{k+1}I_{k}+(-1)^{k}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}x\,dx\right)

\displaystyle={1\over 4}\Bigl(I_{k}+(-1)^{k+1}I_{k}+(-1)^{k}\pi W_{k}\Bigr).

(13)

In particular, when
k\geq 2
is even, using (
13
), we obtain

J_{k}={\pi\over 4}W_{k}={(k-1)!!\over k!!}\,{\pi^{2}\over 8}

(14)

and, of course,

J_{0}={\pi^{2}/8}.

Thus, when
k\geq 2
is even, we write (
12
) as

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx=J_{k}-{(k-1)!!\over 4\cdot k!!}\sum_{j=1}^{k/2}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}

(15)

For instance
(we know that
I_{0}=J_{0}={\pi^{2}/8}
)
using (
15
) and (
14
),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=J_{2}-{1\over 4}={1\over 16}\pi^{2}-{1\over 4}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=J_{4}-{1\over 4}={3\over 64}\pi^{2}-{1\over 4}

\displaystyle I_{6}

\displaystyle=J_{6}-{17\over 72}={5\over 128}\pi^{2}-{17\over 72}

\displaystyle I_{8}

\displaystyle=J_{8}-{2\over 9}={35\over 1024}\pi^{2}-{2\over 9}

and so on.

By using formula (
14
), we can rewrite (
15
) as:

I_{k}=J_{k}-{1\over 4}\cdot{8J_{k}\over\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k/2}{1\over j^{2}}{\,(2j)!!\over(2j-1)!!}=J_{k}\left(1-{2\over\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k/2}{2^{2j}\over j^{2}{2j\choose j}}\right)

Note: from (
14
) it follows that, due to symmetry,

J_{k}={\pi\over 4}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}x\,dx={\pi\over 4}\cdot{1\over 4}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{k}x\,dx={\pi^{2}\over 8}\cdot{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{k}x\,dx

i.e.,
J_{k}
is equal to the average value of
f(x)=\cos^{k}x
on
[0,2\pi]

multiplied by
\pi^{2}/8.

We mention that when
k
is odd, (
13
) gives a relation between
I_{k}
and
J_{k}
in that case:

J_{k}={1\over 4}\left(2I_{k}-\pi W_{k}\right)={1\over 2}I_{k}-{(k-1)!!\over k!!}\,{\pi\over 4}

(16)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{4}x\,dx={1\over 2^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x(1+\cos 2x)^{2}\,dx={1\over 4}(J_{0}+2J_{1}+J_{2})

5
Summary

In our exploration of a particular sawtooth wave we came across the family of definite integrals

I_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}x\,dx,
for
k\geq 0.

It turns out that these integrals, related to Wallis integrals
W_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{k}x\,dx,

have intriguing properties, some of which we present in this paper. Using an iterative formula
we compute
I_{k},
and then investigate a relationship between
I_{k}
and the family

J_{k}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos^{k}2x\,dx,
for
k\geq 0.

Simplifying one identity, we came across the expression
{2n\choose n}

which is related to Catalan numbers.
We discover two interesting power series expansions involving
J_{k}
and Wallis integrals.
In our view, this math-rich material could be incorporated into an advanced Calculus course,
or, broken-down into steps, given as a problem-solving activity.

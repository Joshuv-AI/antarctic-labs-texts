On Tempered Discrete and Lévy White Noises
Julien Fageot

Abstract
We study the growth properties of the family of i.i.d. random sequences, also known as discrete white noises, and of their continuous-domain generalization, the family of Lévy white noises. More precisely, we characterize the members of both families which are tempered— i.e. , whose asymptotic growth is dominated by some polynomial—in terms of their moment properties. We recover the characterization of tempered Lévy white noises obtained by Robert Dalang and Thomas Humeau and provide a new proof of there fundamental result. Our approach is based on a fruitful connection between the discrete and continuous-domain white noises.

Introduction

1.1
Lévy White Noises as Generalized Random Processes

The Gaussian white noise is a random process whose sample paths are too rough to be described as a pointwise random function.
For this reason, it has been considered as a random element in the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of tempered generalized functions, for instance in quantum physics
[
37
]
, stochastic partial differential equations
[
42
]
, and white noise analysis
[
18
,
36
]
.

This construction of the Gaussian white noise relies on the theory of
generalized random processes
,initiated independently in the 50’s by Israel Moiseevich Gel’fand
[
15
]
and Kiyoshi Itô
[
21
]
.
This framework is the stochastic counterpart of the theory of generalized functions of Laurent Schwartz
[
35
]
and has been extensively detailed in the monographs
[
16
,
13
,
22
]
; see also
[
4
]
for a recent introduction.
The key idea is to endow generalized function spaces with random structures compatible with their topology. This allows to define random elements in the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, but also over the larger space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of (not necessarily tempered) generalized functions.
The later is used by Gel’fand and Vilenkin to introduce the complete family of Lévy white noises
[
16
, Chapter 3]
. Those random processes share the properties of the Gaussian white noise with the exception of Gaussianity in the same way Lévy processes generalize the Brownian motion
[
3
,
33
]
.

Lévy white noises are therefore constructed as random elements in the space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, while the Gaussian white noise is known to be tempered,
i.e.
located in the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. It is then natural to ask whether a given Lévy white noise is tempered or not.

1.2
The Temperedness of Lévy White Noise: Related Works

Tempered Lévy white noises have several advantages. For instance, as random elements of
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, there Fourier transform is well-defined and they can therefore be used for stochastic partial differential equations SPDEs. Many works dealing with Lévy white noise therefore identify sufficient conditions so that the temperedness is achieved.
The authors of
[
25
]
consider Lévy white noise with finite first moment, while finite variance Lévy white noises are used for SPDEs in
[
28
,
29
]
.

In
[
8
]
, Arash Amini, Michael Unser, and the author of this article have shown that a Lévy white noise is tempered as soon as it possesses a finite absolute moment of order
\epsilon>0
arbitrarily small, what they called the
\epsilon
-condition
.
They conjectured that this sufficient condition is also necessary. This was further proved by Robert Dalang and Thomas Humeau in
[
6
]
. More precisely, these authors proved the following result, exposed in Theorem
0
, which also reveals that Lévy white noises are almost surely not tempered when the
\epsilon
-condition does not occur. There result therefore fully characterize the temperedness of Lévy white noise from simple moments properties.

Theorem 0

(Dalang and Humeau, Theorem 2.5 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
W
be a Lévy white noise on
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. If
\mathbb{E}[|\langle W,\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]}\rangle|^{\epsilon}]<\infty
1
1

1

The random variable
X_{\varphi}=\langle W,\varphi\rangle
is a priori well-defined for
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
, which is not the case for
\varphi=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]}
. However, one can extend the definition of
X_{\varphi}
to test functions that are compactly supported and bounded for any Lévy white noise, as can easily be deduced from
[
12
]
. This is implicit in Theorem
0
.
for some
\epsilon>0
, then
W\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
almost surely. Otherwise,
W\notin{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
almost surely.

Understanding the sample path properties of random processes is a natural question. The growth properties of Lévy processes has been considered in
[
30
]
; see
[
33
, Section 48]
for extensive discussions and additional references on this matter. The control of the growth of Lévy processes has also been considered by authors studying the regularity of Lévy processes and Lévy white noises, measured in terms of weighted Besov spaces
[
34
,
1
,
9
]
. Those weighted Besov spaces being subspaces of
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, the temperedness of the considered random processes is established. However, these works do not prove the necessity of the
\epsilon
-condition.

1.3
Contributions and Outline

Our goal in this paper is to revisit the characterization of tempered Lévy white noise of Dalang and Humeau. In particular, we provide a new proof of Theorem
0
. The exposition is self-contained and highlights the connection between Lévy white noises and families of i.i.d. random sequences (
i.e.
, discrete white noises). The paper is organized as follows.

•

We first focus on discrete white noises in Section
2
. Our main result is exposed in Theorem
1
: We characterize the weighted
\ell_{\infty}
-sequence spaces on which an i.i.d. random sequence belongs in terms of its moment properties. In particular, we demonstrate that the
\epsilon
-condition is equivalent to the temperedness of
discrete
white noises. Even if this result is not surprising, it has never been observed to the best of our knowledge.

•

We then consider the case of (continuous-domain) Lévy white noises in Section
3.2
. Our main result is Theorem
2
, where the temperedness of Lévy white noise is characterized from different perspectives.
It covers Theorem
0
, together with known characterizations of the temperedness in terms characteristic functional, characteristic exponent, and the Lévy measure of the underlying white noise.
The novelty of our result is the connection between a Lévy white noise and the random sequences
(\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and results in a new proof of Theorem
0
.

2
Discrete White Noises

2.1
Sequence Spaces

We denote by
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
the space of all real sequences.
This notation is somehow unusual and is made in analogy with the space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of generalized functions (see Section
3.1
).
The space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
is the dual of the space
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
of sequences that have finitely many nonzero elements. Any sequence
u\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
indeed defines a linear form over the space
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
via the relation

\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}u[n]v[n]

(1)

for any
v\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note that the sum in (
1
) is actually finite.
One can show that
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
is the
topological
dual of
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
if we endow the later with the locally convex topology corresponding to the following notion of convergence. The sequences
v_{k}=(v_{k}[n])_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
k\geq 1
, converge to
0
if they have a common support
K\subset\mathbb{Z}
outside of which they vanish and if the finite-dimensional vectors
(v_{k}[n])_{n\in K}
converge to
0
.

The space
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
of bounded sequences
u=(u[n])_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|u[n]|<\infty
is a Banach space for the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
.
More generally, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\ell_{\infty,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})
be the weighted Banach space for the norm

u\mapsto\lVert u\rVert_{\infty,\alpha}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{\alpha}|u[n]|<\infty.

(2)

When
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}
, we have the topological embedding
\ell_{\infty,\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\ell_{\infty,\alpha_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
.

The space of rapidly decaying sequence is denoted by
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
. It corresponds to the sequences
u=(u[n])_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that
|n|^{\alpha}|u[n]|\rightarrow_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}0
for any
\alpha\geq 0
. It is a Fréchet space for the family of norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty,\alpha}
with
\alpha\geq 0
and we have the projective limit

{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigcap_{\alpha\geq 0}\ell_{\infty,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}).

(3)

The topological dual of
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
is the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
of tempered sequences,
i.e.
, the space of sequences which are bounded by a polynomial. It is the inductive limit

{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigcup_{\alpha\geq 0}\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}).

(4)

The following simple characterization of tempered sequences will be useful in Section
3
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
u\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
seen as a linear functional over
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
according to (
1
). Then,
u\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
if and only if
u:{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous for the topology induced by
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
. This is equivalent to the fact that

\langle u,v_{k}\rangle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

(5)

for any sequences
v_{k}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
k\geq 1
, such that
\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha}\rightarrow 0
when
k\rightarrow\infty
for any
\alpha\geq 0
.

Proof.

By definition,
u\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
defines a continuous and linear form over
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
as in (
1
) and its restriction over
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
for the topology induced by
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
is clearly continuous. Conversely, any
u\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
v\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto\langle u,v\rangle
is continuous for the topology of
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
and can be uniquely extended as a linear and continuous form over
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
(using that
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
is dense in
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
) and the extension is therefore in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
by definition of the dual. This extension corresponds to the sequence
u
itself, which is therefore tempered.
∎

2.2
Random Sequences and Discrete White Noises

Throughout this paper, we fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathscr{P})
.
A random sequence is a random element in the space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. More precisely, it is a mapping
X:\mathbb{Z}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
X(n,\omega)=X_{n}(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}
for any
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
X_{n}
is a real random variable. For any
\omega\in\Omega
, we have that
X(\omega)=(X_{n}(\omega))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The law of
X
is characterized by the quantities

\mathscr{P}_{X}(\{u\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})|\langle u,v\rangle\leq a\})=\mathscr{P}(\langle X,v\rangle\leq a)

(6)

for any
v\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Thereafter, it will be important to characterize the random sequences
X
that are
tempered
, meaning that
X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely.

Definition 1
.

A
discrete white noise
is a random sequence
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables
X_{n}
.

The law of a discrete white noise
X
is fully characterized by the law of
X_{0}
. For any sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we indeed have that

\mathscr{P}(\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\ X_{n}\leq a_{n})=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{P}(X_{n}\leq a_{n})=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{P}(X_{0}\leq a_{n}),

(7)

where the infinite products are converging in
[0,1]
(as any infinite product of terms in
[0,1]
).
A discrete white noise is
a priori
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
. In the next section, we provide characterizations of discrete white noises to be localized in the sequence spaces
\ell_{\infty,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})
and
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
.

2.3
Characterization of Tempered Discrete White Noises

The goal of this section is to study the growth properties of discrete white noises. More precisely, we shall prove the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a discrete white noise. Then, we have the equivalence

X\in\ell_{\infty,-1/p}(\mathbb{Z})\text{ a.s. }\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{p}]<\infty.

(8)

It this condition is not fulfilled, then
X\notin\ell_{\infty,-1/p}(\mathbb{Z})
a.s. We also have the equivalence

X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})\text{ a.s. }\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\ \epsilon>0,\ \mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{\epsilon}]<\infty.

(9)

If this condition is not fulfilled, then
X\notin{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
a.s.

Our proof is based on Proposition
2
, which establishes
(0-1)
laws for discrete white noises.

Proposition 2
.

For any discrete white noise
X
and any
\alpha>0
, we have that

\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))\in\{0,1\}

(10)

and
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))=1
if and only if

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}|\geq n^{\alpha})<\infty.

(11)

Moreover,
\mathscr{P}(X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}))\in\{0,1\}
, the value being
1
if and only if there exists
\alpha\geq 0
such that (
11
) holds.

Remark.
The characterization (
11
) in Proposition
2
is false for
\alpha=0
.
In this case, we have that
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}))=1
if and only if the common law
\mathscr{P}_{X_{0}}
of the
X_{n}
has a finite support.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

For
x\geq 0
, we set
F(x)=\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}|\geq x)
. The set
\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})
satisfies

\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigcup_{C>0}\left\{u=(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}),\ |u_{n}||n|^{\alpha}\leq C\right\},

(12)

where the union over
C>0
is increasing when
C
increases. Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{C\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{P}(\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\ |X_{n}||n|^{-\alpha}\leq C)=\lim_{C\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}||n|^{-\alpha}\leq C)

\displaystyle=\lim_{C\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha})\right)=\lim_{C\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\log\left(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha})\right)\right),

(13)

where the infinite product converges in
[0,1]
and the infinite sum in
[-\infty,0]
.

If
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence such that
0\leq u_{n}<1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
when
|n|\rightarrow\infty
, then
-\log(1-u_{n})\geq 0
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{n}<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}-\log(1-u_{n})<\infty.

(14)

This follows from the fact that
-\log(1-u_{n})=\log(1+\frac{u_{n}}{1-u_{n}})\sim\frac{u_{n}}{1-u_{n}}\sim u_{n}
when
|n|\rightarrow\infty
.
We apply this relation to
u_{n}=F(C|n|^{\alpha})
and distinguish two cases.

Assume first that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(C|n|^{\alpha})=\infty
for any
C>0
. Then, (
14
) implies that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\log\left(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha})\right)=-\infty

(15)

for any
C>0
and therefore, due to (
2.3
),
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))=\exp(-\infty)=0
.

Assume now that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(C_{0}|n|^{\alpha})=\infty
.
Then, we have that

-\log(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha}))\underset{C\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\log(1-F(\infty))=-\log(1-0)=0.

(16)

Moreover, for
C\geq C_{0}
, using that
F
is decreasing,

-\log(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha}))\leq-\log(1-F(C_{0}|n|^{\alpha})),

(17)

the right term being summable (using (
14
))
and independent from
C\geq C_{0}
. We can therefore apply the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to deduce that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}-\log(1-F(C|n|^{\alpha}))\underset{C\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(18)

Finally, this implies with (
2.3
) that
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))=\exp(0)=1
.

We have shown that
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))
can only take the value
0
and
1
. Moreover, the value
1
is taken if and only if there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(C_{0}|n|^{\alpha})=\infty
holds. However, this condition is then true for any
C_{0}
. To see this, we simply observe that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(C_{0}|n|^{\alpha})<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(C_{0}|x|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(19)

Indeed,
F
is decreasing and therefore, for any
0\leq n\leq x\leq n+1
,
F(C_{0}(n+1)^{\alpha})\leq F(C_{0}x^{\alpha})\leq F(C_{0}n^{\alpha})
(with similar relations for
x\leq 0
), which easily implies (
19
). Moreover, a change of variable
x\leftarrow C_{0}^{1/\alpha}x
gives that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(C_{0}|x|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}x=C_{0}^{-1/\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(|x|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}x
, hence the finiteness does not depend on
C_{0}>0
. Finally, (
11
) follows by choosing
C_{0}=1
.

For
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
, we remark that the spaces
\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})
are growning when
\alpha\geq 0
increases, hence

{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigcup_{\alpha\geq 0}\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}).

(20)

This implies that

\mathscr{P}(X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}))=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})).

(21)

Assume that there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that (
11
) holds. Then,
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha_{0}}(\mathbb{Z}))=1

and since
\ell_{\infty,-\alpha_{0}}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})
,
we also have that
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))=1
for any
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
,
hence
\mathscr{P}(X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})=1
due to (
21
).
On the contrary, assume that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}|\geq n^{\alpha})=\infty
for any
\alpha
.
This implies that
\mathscr{P}(X\in\ell_{\infty,-\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}))=0
for every
\alpha\geq 0
and therefore
\mathscr{P}(X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}))=0
, again due to (
21
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We first observe that the
(0-1)
law in Proposition
2
implies that
X\notin\ell_{\infty,-1/p}(\mathbb{Z})
a.s. as soon as the condition
X\in\ell_{\infty,-1/p}(\mathbb{Z})
is not satisfied (the same holds for the sequence space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
). This covers the last part of Theorem
1
.

Using the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
2
, we moreover have that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}|\geq n^{1/p})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(n^{1/p})<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x^{1/p})\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(22)

Denoting by
\mathscr{P}_{|X_{0}|}
the probability law of
|X_{0}|
, we also remark that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x^{1/p})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{t\geq x^{1/p}}\mathscr{P}_{|X_{0}|}(\mathrm{d}t)\mathrm{d}x\ =\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{t^{p}\geq x}\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathscr{P}_{|X_{0}|}(\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p}\mathscr{P}_{|X_{0}|}(\mathrm{d}t)=\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{p}].

(23)

Finally, this shows that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}(|X_{0}|\geq n^{1/p})<\infty
if and only if
\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{p}]<\infty
and Theorem
1
follows from the criterion of Proposition
2
.
∎

We illustrate Theorem
1
with some examples. Thereafter,
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a discrete white noise.

•

Assume that the random variables
X_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, are symmetric-

S)
[

]
. This means that the common characteristic function of the

is

where

and

. The case

corresponds to the Gaussian law, for which all moments are finite. Hence,

for any

in this case.

Non-Gaussian S

\alpha<2
, we actually have that
\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{p}]<\infty
if and only if

[

, Property 1.2.16]
. Hence, Theorem

implies that, for

,

(24)

almost surely for any

. In particular,
X\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely. Moreover, the fact that
\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{\alpha}]=\infty
implies that

•

As for the Gaussian case, random variables whose moments are all finite are such that
X\in\ell_{\infty,-\epsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely for any
\epsilon>0
. As such, they only slightly differ from a bounded sequence, since
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|X_{n}|}{1+|n|^{\delta}}<\infty
almost surely for
\delta
arbitrarily small.

•

Assume that the
X_{n}
have a common probability density function given by
f(x)=\frac{c}{(|x|+1)\log^{2}(|x|+2)}
where

is such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
. Note that

is indeed integrable as a Bertrand integral. However, we have for any

that

\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}|^{p}]=c\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|x|^{p}}{(|x|+1)\log^{2}(|x|+2)}\mathrm{d}x=\infty

due to the fact the integrand behaves asymptotically like

and is therefore non-integrable for

. Hence,

3
Lévy White Noises

3.1
Spaces of Generalized Functions

We briefly introduce the relevant function spaces that will be used thereafter. More details can be found, for instance, in
[
14
]
. We refer the interested reader to
[
38
]
for a comprehensive exposition on the topological structures of the proposed function spaces.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of compactly supported and infinitely smooth functions. It is equipped with its natural inductive topology as the union of the spaces
{\mathscr{D}}([-n,n])
of infinitely smooth functions whose support is included in
[-n,n]
for
n\geq 1
. In particular, a family of functions
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
converges to
0
for this topology if there exists
n\geq 1
such that
\mathrm{Supp}(\varphi_{k})\subset[-n,n]
for every
k\geq 1
and
\varphi_{k}
converges uniformly to
0
on
[-n,n]
together with all its derivatives.

The topological dual
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of generalized functions. A locally integrable function
f
defines a linear and continuous functional over
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
via the relation

f:\varphi\mapsto\langle f,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)f(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(26)

The framework of generalized functions allows to define functions with no pointwise interpretation, such as the Dirac impulse
\delta
and all its derivatives. We will define Lévy white noise are random elements in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
in Section
3.2
.

The Schwartz space of rapidly decaying and infinitely smooth functions is denoted by
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
. A function
f
is in
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
if it vanishes faster than any polynomial together with all its derivatives.
Then,
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
is a Fréchet space for the family of semi-norms

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty,\alpha,\beta}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x|^{\alpha}|\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi(x)|

(27)

where
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, a sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of functions in
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
converges to
0
if
\lVert\varphi_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha,\beta}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for any
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.

The topological dual of
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
is the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of tempered generalized functions. It is a subspace of
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and is informally made of generalized functions that are slowly growing,
i.e.
growing slower than some polynomial.

3.2
Lévy White Noises as Generalized Random Processes

A generalized random process is a random element of the space
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of generalized functions.
The theory of generalized random processes was initiated independently by Gel’fand
[
15
]
and Itô
[
21
]
, it offers a broad framework for defining random processes, including some with no pointwise interpretation such as the Lévy white noises. Thereafter, we mostly rely on the expositions in
[
16
]
and
[
22
]
.

A
generalized random process

S
is a collection of random variable
(\langle S,\varphi\rangle)_{\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})}
such that,

•

Linearity:
For any
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
\langle S,a\varphi+b\psi\rangle=a\langle S,\varphi\rangle+b\langle S,\psi\rangle
almost surely.

•

Continuity:
For any sequence
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of test functions
\varphi_{k}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi
in
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
when
k\rightarrow\infty
, the random variables
\langle S,\varphi_{k}\rangle
converges in probability towards
\langle S,\varphi\rangle
.

A generalized random process is therefore a linear and continuous functional
S:{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L_{0}(\Omega)
where
L_{0}(\Omega)
is the space of real random variables endowed with the convergence in probability
[
22
]
.
We associate to
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
its
cylindrical
\sigma
-field
,
i.e.
the
\sigma
-field generated by the sets

\{f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),\ \langle f,\varphi\rangle<a\}

(28)

where
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
The probability law of
S
is then the probability measure
\mathscr{P}_{S}
on
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathscr{P}_{S}(\{f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),\ \langle f,\varphi\rangle<a\})=\mathscr{P}(\langle S,\varphi\rangle<a)
.

Definition 2
.

The
characteristic functional

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}:{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of a generalized random process
S
is defined for
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
by

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{S}(\varphi)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle S,\varphi\rangle}\right]=\int_{{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle f,\varphi\rangle}\mathscr{P}_{S}(\mathrm{d}f).

(29)

The characteristic functional of
S
is the (infinite dimensional) Fourier transform of its probability law
\mathscr{P}_{S}
.
It generalizes the characteristic function of random variables to the case of generalized random processes.
In particular, two generalized random processes have the same law if and only if they share the same characteristic functional.

Definition 3
.

We say that
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a
characteristic exponent
if it can be written as

\Psi(\xi)=\mathrm{i}\mu\xi-\frac{\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi t}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi t\mathbbm{1}_{|t|\geq 1})\nu(\mathrm{d}t),

(30)

with
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
, and
\nu
a Lévy–measure,
i.e.
, a Radon measure over
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\inf(1,t^{2})\nu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty.

(31)

A random variable
X
is
infinitely divisible
if it can be decomposed as a sum
X=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{N}
of
N
i.i.d. random variables for any
N\geq 1
. Infinitely divisible random variables are closely related to continuous-domain random processes. The Lévy–Khintchine theorem
[
33
, Theorem 8.1]
ensures that a random variable
X
is infinitely divisible if and only if its characteristic function can be written as
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{X}(\xi)=\exp(\Psi(\xi))
with
\Psi
a characteristic exponent defined in Definition
3
.
Following
[
16
, Chapter III]
, we now define Lévy white noise from their characteristic functional and exponent.

Definition 4
.

A
Lévy white noise
is a generalized random process
W
in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
whose characteristic functional is given by

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi)=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi(\varphi(x))\mathrm{d}x\right),

(32)

where
\Psi
is a characteristic exponent, called by extension the
characteristic exponent
of
W
.
If moreover
W
is almost surely in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, then
W
is a
tempered Lévy white noise
.

The fact that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}
specifies the characteristic functional of some generalized random process in
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
is proved in
[
16
, Theorem 5, Section III.4.3]
. It relies on the Bochner–Minlos theorem, which characterizes the characteristic functionals as the continuous and positive-definite functionals
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}:{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}(0)=1

[
13
,
22
]
.

The characteristic exponent of the centered Gaussian white noise
W
is given by
\Psi(\xi)=-\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}/2
, which corresponds to (
30
) with
\mu=0
and
\nu=0
.
The characteristic functional of the Gaussian white noise is therefore
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi)=\exp(-\sigma^{2}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{2}^{2}/2)
.
The family of Lévy white noises include the S
\alpha
S white noises and the compound Poisson white noises. These examples, among others, will be considered below.

We say that a generalized random process
S
is
stationary
if
S
has the same law than its shifted version
S(\cdot+x_{0})
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
S
is
stationary at every point
if the random variables
\langle W,\varphi\rangle
and
\langle W,\psi\rangle
are independent as soon as
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
have disjoint support (or equivalently,
\varphi(x).\psi(x)=0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
).

Proposition 3
.

A Lévy white noise
W
is stationary and independent at every point. In particular, the random variables
\langle W,\varphi\rangle
and
\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-x_{0})\rangle
are identically distributed for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and are independent for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi(x)\varphi(x-x_{0})=0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

The stationarity follows from the fact that the characteristic functionals
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}
and
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W(\cdot-x_{0})}
are equal for any
x_{0}
. The independence at every point is proved in
[
16
, Section III.4]
.
Let
X=\langle W,\varphi\rangle
with
W
a Lévy white noise and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
.
We denote by
\Psi
and
\nu
the characteristic exponent and Lévy measure of
W
, respectively.
Then, the characteristic function of
X
is given for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
by

\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{X}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi X}\right]=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi(\xi\varphi(x))\mathrm{d}x\right).

(33)

This simply follows from the fact that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{X}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\langle W,\varphi\rangle}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle W,\xi\varphi\rangle}\right]=\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\xi\varphi)
.
Then,
X
is infinitely divisible and its Lévy measure
\nu_{X}
can be deduced from the one of
W
according to the relation

\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}f(t)\nu_{X}(\mathrm{d}t)=\int_{\mathrm{Supp}(\varphi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}f(t\varphi(x))\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\mathrm{d}x\in[0,\infty]

(34)

for any measurable and positive function
f:\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
The relation (
34
) is proved in
[
41
, Theorem 9.1]
and can be recovered from the Lévy–Khintchine expansion (
30
) injected in (
33
).

3.3
Tempered Lévy White Noises

The goal of this section is to characterize tempered Lévy white noises among the complete family in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
W
be a Lévy white noise in
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

1.

The Lévy white noise
W
is tempered;
i.e.
,
w\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
almost surely.

2.

The characteristic functional
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}
of
W
is continuous over

{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
for the topology induced by
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
.

3.

Let
\Psi
be the characteristic exponent of
W
. Then, there exist
C>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left\lvert\Psi(\xi)\right\rvert\leq C\left(\lvert\xi\rvert^{\epsilon}+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right).

(35)

4.

For any
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that
X_{W,\varphi}=\left(\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely.

5.

The Lévy measure
\nu
of
W
satisfies that
\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(dt)<\infty
for some
\epsilon>0
.

6.

There exists
\epsilon>0
such that, for any
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathbb{E}[|\langle W,\varphi\rangle|^{\epsilon}]<\infty
.

7.

There exists
\epsilon>0
and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\{0\}
such that
\mathbb{E}[|\langle W,\varphi\rangle|^{\epsilon}]<\infty
.

Moreover, if these conditions are not satisfied, then
W\notin{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely.

Remarks.

Theorem
2
provides characterizations of the temperedness of a Lévy white noise
W
on its characteristic functional (2.), its characteristic exponent (3.), and its Lévy measure (5.).
It also recovers the characterization 6. in terms of positive absolute moments that have been demonstrated in
[
6
,
8
]
.
More precisely, the implications
6.\Rightarrow 3.\Rightarrow 1.
were shown in
[
8
]
, revealing that the existence of positive absolute moment is sufficient for
W
to be tempered. The later contribution
[
6
]
then proved that (i) this condition is also necessary and (ii) that
W
is almost surely not tempered when 6. do not hold.

The characterization 7. is a slight variation of 6.: it shows that the moment property is independent from the test function
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\{0\}
.
The key novelty is the characterization 4., which allows us to use the characterization of discrete white noises obtained in Theorem
1
. In particular, this provides a new proof for a Lévy white noise to be tempered or not depending on the existence of positive absolute moments, following an approach different from
[
6
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We will prove the theorem thanks to the relations:

1.\Rightarrow 4.\Rightarrow 6.\Rightarrow 7.\Rightarrow 5.\Rightarrow 3.\Rightarrow 2.\Rightarrow 1.

(36)

The equivalence between 1. and 2. is true for any generalized random process and is not restricted to Lévy white noises. It is established in
[
13
, Section III.6.1, Corollary 2]
and will not be covered in this paper.
On this matter, see also
[
22
, Section 2.5]
. For alternative proofs and discussions restricted to Lévy white noises, see
[
26
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
8
, Theorem 4]
.

1.
\Rightarrow
4.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
. We fix
(v_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that
v_{k}=(v_{k}[n])_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
for any
k\geq 1
and
v_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{Z})
when
k\rightarrow\infty
. This means that
\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha}\rightarrow 0
when
k\rightarrow\infty
for any
\alpha\geq 0
.
We observe that

\langle X_{W,\varphi},v_{k}\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle v_{k}[n]=\left\langle W,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{k}[n]\varphi(\cdot-n)\right\rangle.

(37)

Note that the sums in (
37
) are finite and
\varphi_{k}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{k}[n]\varphi(\cdot-n)\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
for
v_{k}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
. We will prove that

\varphi_{k}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\text{ in }{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}).

(38)

Assume first that (
38
) is established. Since
W\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
a.s., we therefore have that, a.s.,
\langle X_{W,\varphi},v_{k}\rangle=\langle W,\varphi_{k}\rangle\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Thanks to Proposition
1
, this implies that
X_{W,\varphi}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely and 4. is proved.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle|x|^{\alpha}|\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle=|x|^{\alpha}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{k}[n](\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi_{k})(x-n)\right|\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|v_{k}[n]|x|^{\alpha}(\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi_{k})(x-n)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha+2}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}}{(1+|n|)^{\alpha+2}}\left|(\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi_{k})(x-n)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha+2}\lVert(1+|\cdot|)^{\alpha}\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}}{(1+|n|)^{\alpha+2}(1+|x-n|)^{\alpha}}\right).

(39)

We define, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

F(x,y)=\frac{(1+|y-x|)^{\alpha}}{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}(1+|y|)^{\alpha}}.

(40)

If
x
and
y
have the same sign, then
|x-y|\leq\max(|x|,|y|)
and therefore
1+|x-y|\leq 1+\max(|x|,|y|)\leq(1+|x|)(1+|y|)
. If now
x
and
y
have opposite signs, for instance
y\leq 0\leq x
, then

1+|x-y|=1+x-y\leq 1+x-y-xy=(1+x)(1-y)=(1+|x|)(1+|y|)
. This shows that,
0<F(x,y)\leq 1
for anx
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Finally, this implies that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}}{(1+|n|)^{\alpha+2}(1+|x-n|)^{\alpha}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{F(n,x-n)}{(1+|n|)^{2}}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+|n|)^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}<\infty.

(41)

Combining (
3.3
) and (
41
), we just proved that

\lVert\varphi_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha,\beta}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x|^{\alpha}|\mathrm{D}^{\beta}\varphi_{k}(x)|\leq C\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha+2}

(42)

for some constant
C>0
independent from
k\geq 1
.
Since
\lVert v_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha+2}
vanishes for any
\alpha\geq 0
, we deduce that
\lVert\varphi_{k}\rVert_{\infty,\alpha,\beta}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for any
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
when
k\rightarrow\infty
and
X_{W,\varphi}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
almost surely.

4.
\Rightarrow
6.

We first assume that
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
has its support included in
[0,1]
, so that the test functions
\varphi(\cdot-n)
have disjoint supports for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the random variables
\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle
are independent and identically distributed for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
(see Proposition
3
). We apply Theorem
1
and deduce that
\mathbb{E}[|\langle W,\varphi\rangle|^{\epsilon}]<\infty
for some
\epsilon>0
.

For a general
\varphi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
with no support restriction, the random variables
\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle
are not independent in general.
However, the function
\varphi
being compactly supported, there exists
K_{0}\geq 1
such that the functions
\varphi(\cdot+K_{0}n)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, have disjoint supports. We can apply the same argument to the sequence
(\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-K_{0}n)\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and 6. is proved.

6.
\Rightarrow
7.
\Rightarrow
5.

The implication 6.
\Rightarrow
7. is obvious. We assume that
\varphi\neq 0
and
\epsilon>0
are such that
\mathbb{E}[|\langle W,\varphi\rangle|^{\epsilon}]<\infty
. The key is the connection between the moments of an infinitely divisible random variable
X
and the moments of its Lévy measure
\nu_{X}
. According to
[
33
, Theorem 25.3]
, we indeed have that, for any
p>0
,

\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{p}\nu_{X}(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty.

(43)

We apply this relation to
X=\langle W,\varphi\rangle
and
p=\epsilon
to deduce that
\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu_{\langle W,\varphi\rangle}(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty
.
Applying (
34
) with
f(t)=|t|^{\epsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{|t|\geq 1}
then reveales that

\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu_{\langle W,\varphi\rangle}(\mathrm{d}t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\left(\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R},\ |t\varphi(x)|\geq 1\}}|t\varphi(x)|^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)\nu(\mathrm{d}t).

(44)

The function
\varphi
being non identically zero,
there exists an interval
[a,b]
and
m>0
such that
|\varphi(x)|\geq m
for
a\leq x\leq b
. We then deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu_{\langle W,\varphi\rangle}(\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\mathbbm{1}_{|\varphi(x)||t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\geq m^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\int_{a}^{b}\mathbbm{1}_{m|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=m^{\epsilon}(b-a)\int_{|t|\geq 1/m}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)

(45)

where we used that
\mathbbm{1}_{|t\varphi(x)|\geq 1}\geq\mathbbm{1}_{m|t|\geq 1}
for any
x\in[a,b]
.
The finiteness of

\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu_{\langle W,\varphi\rangle}(\mathrm{d}t)
therefore implies that

\int_{|t|\geq 1/m}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty
. Finally, since
\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)
and
\int_{|t|\geq 1/m}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)
differ by a finite constant (given by
c=\int_{\min(1,1/m)\leq|t|\leq\max(1,1/m)}|t|^{p}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)
), we deduce 5.

5.
\Rightarrow
3.

This is a reformulation of
[
7
, Proposition 2.4]
. We repeat the proof for the sake of self-completeness. Note that if
\epsilon>1
, then
\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\leq\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty
. Hence, we can assume that
\epsilon\leq 1
without loss of generality.
We use the Lévy–Khintchine expansion (
30
) of
\Psi
. The function
\xi\mapsto\mathrm{i}\mu\xi-\frac{\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}}{2}
is clearly dominated by
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{\epsilon}+|\xi|^{2}
(since
\epsilon\leq 1
).
It then suffices to bound the last term in (
30
).

Applying the inequality
|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi t}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi t|\leq\xi^{2}t^{2}
, we deduce that

\int_{0<|t|<1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi t}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi t\right|\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\leq\xi^{2}\int_{0<|t|<1}t^{2}\nu(\mathrm{d}t).

(46)

The quantity
\int_{0<|t|<1}t^{2}\nu(\mathrm{d}t)
is finite for any Lévy measure, hence this term is also dominated as expected.

We now use the inequality
|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi t}-1|^{2}=2(1-\cos(\xi t))\leq 2\min(2,\xi^{2}t^{2})\leq 4\min(1,\xi^{2}t^{2})\leq 4|\xi t|^{2\epsilon}
(using that
2\epsilon\leq 2
). This implies that

\int_{|t|\geq 1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi t}-1\right|\nu(\mathrm{d}t)\leq 2|\xi|^{\epsilon}\int_{|t|\geq 1}|t|^{\epsilon}\nu(\mathrm{d}t).

(47)

Combining the different bounds, we obtain 3.

3.
\Rightarrow
2.

The characteristic functional is positive-definite and normalized such that
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(0)=1
. Hence, we deduce that
[
27
, Section 13.4.B]

\left\rvert\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi)-\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\psi)\right\rvert\leq 2\left(1-\Re\left\{\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi-\psi)\right\}\right)

(48)

for any
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\Re\{z\}
stands for the real part of
z\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular,
\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}
is continuous if and only if it is continuous at
\varphi=0
, which we will prove now.
The condition 3. implies that

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi(\varphi(x))\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(\varphi(x))|\mathrm{d}x\leq C(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\epsilon}^{\epsilon}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{2}^{2}).

(49)

Assume that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for the topology of
{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})
.
This readily implies that
\lVert\varphi_{k}\rVert_{\epsilon}^{\epsilon}+\lVert\varphi_{k}\rVert_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0
,
and therefore
\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi(\varphi_{k}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\rightarrow 0
.
Finally,

\left\rvert\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi_{k})-\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(0)\right\rvert=\left\rvert\widehat{\mathscr{P}}_{W}(\varphi_{k})-1\right\rvert=\left\rvert\mathrm{e}^{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi(\varphi_{k}(x))\mathrm{d}x}-1\right\rvert\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}|\mathrm{e}^{0}-1|=0,

(50)

proving 2.

We assume finally that the equivalent conditions 1. to 7. are not satisfied. Set
\Omega_{W}=\{\omega\in\Omega,\ W(\omega)\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\}
. Then, the proof above for the implication
1.\Rightarrow 4.
implies that the sequence
X_{W(\omega),\varphi}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
for
\omega\in\Omega_{W}
. In other terms, we have that
\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{W})\leq\mathscr{P}(X_{W,\varphi}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})\}
.
However, Theorem
1
implies that
X_{W,\varphi}\notin{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
a.s. as soon as it is not a.s. tempered, which is the case here. Hence,

\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{W})\leq\mathscr{P}(X_{W,\varphi}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})\}=0

(51)

and
\mathscr{P}(\Omega_{W})=0
;
i.e.
,
W\notin{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
a.s.

As we have seen, the first characterization of tempered Lévy white noises in terms of moment properties has been obtained in
[
6
]
. The proof’s strategy of Dalang and Humeau is based on (i) the Lévy–Itô decomposition of a Lévy process, (ii) the reduction of the problem to compound Poisson processes, and (iii) the deduction of the corresponding result for Lévy white noises, which are the weak derivatives of Lévy processes).

Our proof rather relies on the random sequences
X_{W,\varphi}=\left(\langle W,\varphi(\cdot-n)\rangle\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and on the fact that tempered i.i.d. random sequences have been characterized in Theorem
1
. This new proof highlights the strong connection between discrete and Lévy white noises.

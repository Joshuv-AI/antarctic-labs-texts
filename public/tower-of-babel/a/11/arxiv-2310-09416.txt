The maximum size of an induced forest in the binomial random graph
Akhmejanova Margarita, Vladislav Kozhevnikov

Abstract
The celebrated Frieze’s result about the independence number of G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) states that it is concentrated in an interval of size o ⁡ ( 1 / p ) o(1/p) for all C ε / n < p = o ⁡ ( 1 ) C_{\varepsilon}/n<p=o(1) . We show concentration in an interval of size o ⁡ ( 1 / p ) o(1/p) for the maximum size (number of vertices) of an induced forest in G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) for all C ε / n < p < 1 − ε C_{\varepsilon}/n<p<1-\varepsilon . Presumably, it is the first generalization of Frieze’s result to another class of induced subgraphs for such a range of p p .

Introduction

For a graph
G
, we denote the maximum number of vertices in its induced forest and tree by
F(G)
and
T(G)
, respectively. Here, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1

Let
p=p(n)\in(0,1)
,
q=1/(1-p)
.
Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sufficiently large constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that if
C_{\varepsilon}/n<p<1-\varepsilon
, then a.a.s. (asymptotically almost surely)

\left\lfloor 2\log_{q}(enp(1-\varepsilon))+3\right\rfloor\leq F(G(n,p))\leq\left\lceil 2\log_{q}(enp(1+\varepsilon))+3\right\rceil,

i.e.
F(G(n,p))
is concentrated in the interval of size
o(1/p)
.

The theorem is similar to the analogous theorem for independent sets given in
[
7
]
. It refines the estimate of the deviation of the size of the largest induced forest from its asymptotic value, which is already known
[
9
]
. The larger the value of
p
, the better estimate is given by the theorem. In the extreme case of
p=\operatorname{const}
it gives the concentration of the size in three points. The proof leverages a combination of the second moment method and Talagrand’s inequality.

The study of induced trees in
G(n,p)
was initiated by Erdős and Palka
[
6
]
. They showed that if
p=\operatorname{const}
, then for every
\varepsilon>0
a.a.s.

(2-\varepsilon)\log_{q}np\leq T(G(n,p))\leq(2+\varepsilon)\log_{q}np.

Fernandez de la Vega
[
4
]
considered the case where the average degree is constant. He proved that a.a.s. such graphs (
G(n,p=\frac{C}{n}),C=\operatorname{const}
) have induced trees of size linear in
n
. This result was also independently verified by Frieze and Jackson
[
8
]
, Kučera and Rödl
[
13
]
, and Łuczak and Palka
[
11
]
. Later Fernandez de la Vega revisited the case
G\left(n,p=\frac{C}{n}\right)
in
[
5
]
and proved, in his own words, "nearly best possible" result for large constant
C
and any fixed
\varepsilon>0
:

\frac{2n(\ln C-\ln\ln C-1)}{C}\leq T(G(n,p))\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)2n\ln C}{C}.

In
[
16
]
, Palka and Ruciński considered the case
p=\frac{C\ln n}{n},C>e
, for which they established that for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
a.a.s.

\left(1/C-\varepsilon\right)\frac{n(\ln\ln n)}{\ln n}\leq T(G(n,p))\leq\left(2/C+\varepsilon\right)\frac{n(\ln\ln n)}{\ln n}.

In 2018, Dutta and Subramanian
[
3
]
for
p\geq n^{-1/2}(\ln n)^{2}
proved that a.a.s

T(G)=2\left(\log_{q}np\right)+O(1/\ln q).

It is worth mentioning that the range of
p
is crucial. For example, for
n^{-2/3+\varepsilon}<p<1/\ln^{2}n
an independence number of
G(n,p)
has already become concentrated on two values, as seen in the recent result of Bohman and Hofstad
[
1
]
.
We also mention that for the case
p=\operatorname{const}
, 2-point concentration results for
T(G)
and
F(G)
were achieved by Kamaldinov, Skorkin, Zhukovskii
[
12
]
and
Krivoshapko, Zhukovskii
[
14
]
, respectively. Also recently, Draganić, Glock
and Krivelevich
[
2
]
proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that a.a.s.
G(n,p)
contains an induced
linear forest
( a forest with a maximum degree of at most
2
) of size at least
(2-\varepsilon)\log_{q}(np)
and components of size
(np)^{1/2}/(\ln np)^{4}
whenever
C_{\varepsilon}/n\leq p\leq n^{-1/2}(\ln n)^{2}
.

4.1
Coding trees with a fixed independent set

It is known that a labelled tree on vertices
\left[1,n\right]
can be encoded by a Prüfer sequence
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-2}\right)\in\left[1,n\right]^{n-2}
. Let’s fix some
m\in\left[1,n\right]
. Then the standard encoding procedure from
[
15
]
can be slightly modified to encode only the trees with the independent set
\left[1,m\right]
by two sequences
a=\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1}\right)\in\left[m+1,n\right]^{m-1}
and
b=\left(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-m-1}\right)\in\left[1,n\right]^{n-m-1}
.
Take any such tree and successively prune it’s leaves, starting from leaves with smallest labels, as in the standard encoding procedure, until only one edge remains. If, however, the pruned leaf has the label in
\left[1,m\right]
, then write the label of its neighbour to the sequence
a
, otherwise, to the sequence
b
.

This modified encoding allows to find the number of such labelled trees on vertices
\left[1,n\right]
with a given degree sequence
\left(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\right)
, which is exactly

\binom{n-m-1}{d_{1}-1,d_{2}-1,\ldots,d_{m}-1,n-1-\left(d_{1}+\ldots,d_{m}\right)}\binom{n+m-2-\left(d_{1}+\ldots,d_{m}\right)}{d_{m+1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1}.

(12)

From (
12
) it is evident that such a tree with the degree sequence
\left(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\right)
exists iff
d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}=2n-2
and
d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m}\leq{n-1}
.

4.2
Counting forests with a fixed induced subforest

Let
K_{n}
be the complete graph on
n
vertices. For any
F\subset{K_{n}}
, let’s define
f(n,h,F)
as the number of rooted (with a distinguished vertex in each component) spanning forests of
K_{n}
with
h
components containing
F
as an induced subgraph.

Let’s consider a forest
F\subset{K_{n}}
with
m
components with numbers of vertices
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
and let
l:=\left\lvert V(F)\right\rvert=f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m}
. Each
h
-component rooted spanning forest of
K_{n}
containing
F
as an induced subgraph can be uniquely constructed as follows:

•

contract each component of
F
into a single vertex;

•

label the contracted components by
\left[1,m\right]
and the rest of the vertices of
K_{n+1}
by
\left[m+1,n-\ell+m\right]
;

•

add one more vertex labelled
n-\ell+m+1
;

•

construct a labelled tree on the vertices
\left[1,n-\ell+m+1\right]
in which
\deg(n-\ell+m+1)=h
;

•

expand the vertices
\left[1,m\right]
(which are the contracted components);

•

remove the vertex labelled
n-\ell+m+1
.

Thus, applying (
12
),

f(n,h,F)=\sum\limits_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-\ell+m}=1}^{+\infty}\binom{n-\ell}{d_{1}-1,d_{2}-1,\ldots,d_{m}-1,n-\ell+m-\left(d_{1}+\ldots,d_{m}\right)}\cdot\\
\cdot\binom{n-\ell+2m-1-\left(d_{1}+\ldots,d_{m}\right)}{d_{m+1}-1,\ldots,d_{n-\ell+m}-1,h-1}\cdot{f_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot{f_{m}^{d_{m}}}}\\
=\sum\limits_{k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}=0}^{+\infty}\binom{n-\ell}{k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}}\cdot{f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot{f_{m}^{k_{m}+1}}}\cdot\sum\limits_{k_{m+1},\ldots,k_{n-\ell+m}=0}^{+\infty}\binom{k_{0}+m-1}{k_{m+1},\ldots,k_{n-\ell+m},h-1}\\
=f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot{f_{m}}\cdot\sum\limits_{k_{0}=0}^{n-\ell}\binom{n-\ell}{k_{0}}l^{n-\ell-k_{0}}\cdot\binom{k_{0}+m-1}{h-1}(n-\ell)^{k_{0}+m-h}

It is easy to see that
f(n,h,F)
depends only on the sizes of the components of
F
. Therefore, we can define

f(k,h,\left\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\right\}):=f(k,h,F).

Thus,

f(k,h,\left\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\right\})=f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot{f_{m}}\sum\limits_{k_{0}=0}^{k-\ell}\binom{k-\ell}{k_{0}}\ell^{k-\ell-k_{0}}\cdot\binom{k_{0}+m-1}{h-1}(k-\ell)^{k_{0}+m-h}.

(13)

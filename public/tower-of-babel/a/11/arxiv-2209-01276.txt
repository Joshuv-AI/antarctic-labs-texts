Distributed Primal-dual Optimization for Heterogeneous Multi-agent Systems
Yichuan Li, Petros Voulgaris, Nikolaos M. Freris

Abstract
Heterogeneous networks comprise agents with varying capabilities in terms of computation, storage, and communication. In such settings, it is crucial to factor in the operating characteristics in allowing agents to choose appropriate updating schemes, so as to better distribute computational tasks and utilize the network more efficiently. We consider the multi-agent optimization problem of cooperatively minimizing the sum of local strongly convex objectives. We propose an asynchronous distributed primal-dual protocol, which allows for the primal update steps to be agent-dependent (an agent can opt between first-order or Newton updates). Our analysis introduces a unifying framework for such hybrid optimization scheme and establishes global linear convergence in expectation, under strongly convex objectives and general agent activation schemes. Numerical experiments on real life datasets attest to the merits of the proposed algorithm.

INTRODUCTION

By harnessing the computational power of multiple machines, distributed optimization aims to integrate a network of agents to solve a minimization problem. The framework has found success across a wide range of fields, including control, smart-grid networks, and machine learning

These challenges require revisiting algorithmic designs to account for the operating characteristics to effectively distribute computational tasks.

We consider the following collaborative network problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathrm{minimize}}\,\,\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(\tilde{x})+g(\tilde{x})\right\},

(1)

where

are local strongly convex objectives, and

is a convex but possibly nonsmooth regularization function. Each agent has access only to its individual objective function, and performs updates using local data. A network-wide solution is achieved asymptotically via an exchange of information among neighboring agents, which serves to effectively integrate global information.

Distributed first order methods

]
-

Second order methods attain acceleration by invoking information pertaining to the (exact or approximate) Hessian matrix of the objective. Recent work on (quasi) Newton methods
[

[

The ability to reach a high-accuracy solution in significantly fewer iterations makes second order algorithms a desirable candidate when abundant computational resources are available. However, each update step of Newton’s method requires to solve a linear system at the cost of
\mathcal{O}(d^{3})
computational costs for general objectives.

Methods that allow agents to choose between first and second order solver are rather scarce in the literature. This consideration becomes significant for large scale heterogeneous networks where machines have varying capacities in terms of computation, communication, storage, and battery levels. In this aspect, a
hybrid

[

In contradistinction, the proposed algorithm can be deployed in general multi-agent settings, (of which client-server is a special case) and achieves a totally asynchronous implementation.

PRELIMINARIES

II-A

Proximal operator

The proximal operator
[
16
]

\textbf{prox}_{g/\mu}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
associated with a function
g(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and a constant
\mu>0
is defined as:

\displaystyle\textbf{prox}_{g/\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\text{argmin}}\left\{g(v)+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\norm{v-x}^{2}\right\}.

(2)

When
g(\cdot)
is convex, the above mapping is single valued. Proximal algorithms
[
17
]
were developed for convex composite problems, where the objective is the sum of two convex functions: one smooth and one possibly non-smooth but proximable (i.e., with efficiently computable proximal mapping). Examples where (
2
) is easy to evaluate are ubiquitous in applications, e.g., the
\ell_{1}-
norm or the indicator functions for hyperplane, halfspace, simplex, orthant, etc.
The vast majority of these methods are first order, i.e., they use the gradient pertaining to the smooth term along with the proximal operator of the non-smooth part to iteratively solve the problem. Using second order information, in this framework, typically poses challenges in ensuring convergence guarantees while maintaining affordable computational costs. This is due to the fact that the norm function in (
2
) has to be scaled according to the Hessian matrix
[
18
]
. Such scaling may render the evaluation of the associated proximal mappings expensive, even when its first-order counterpart admits a closed form solution.

II-C

Primal-dual algorithms

Primal-dual optimization algorithms date back to the work
[
19
]
. By introducing the (augmented) Lagrangian, primal-dual algorithms seek to solve the primal and the dual problem simultaneously, through the use of dual variables. The proliferation of primal-dual methods
[
20
]
is attributed to their wide applicability to distributed optimization. In the following of this subsection, we review one instance of primal-dual algorithms, termed Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)
[
21
]
, which serves as the foundation of our subsequent development. To solve (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,z,\theta;y,\lambda)=F(x)+g(\theta)+y^{\top}(Ax-Bz)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{\top}(S^{\top}x-\theta)+\tfrac{\mu_{z}}{2}\norm{Ax-Bz}^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{\theta}}{2}\norm{S^\top x -\theta}^{2},

(5)

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2nd},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the dual vectors and
\mu_{z},\mu_{\theta}>0
is are scalars that controls the quadratic penalty. ADMM solves (
4
) by sequentially minimizing the augmented Lagrangian with respect to the primal variables
(x,z,\theta)
, and then performs gradient ascent on the dual variable.

\displaystyle x^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{x}{\mathrm{argmin}}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x,z^{t},\theta^{t};y^{t},\lambda^{t}),

(6a)

\displaystyle z^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{z}{\mathrm{argmin}}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x^{t+1},z,\theta^{t};y^{t},\lambda^{t}),

(6b)

\displaystyle\theta^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{\theta}{\mathrm{argmin}}\,\,\mathcal{L}(x^{t+1},z^{t+1},\theta;y^{t},\lambda^{t})

(6c)

\displaystyle y^{t+1}

\displaystyle=y^{t}+\mu_{z}(Ax^{t+1}-Bz^{t+1}).

(6d)

\displaystyle\lambda^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{t}+\mu_{\theta}(S^{\top}x^{t+1}-\theta^{t+1})

(6e)

As a result of introducing the consensus variables

Building on these results, we propose a general framework that accommodates system heterogeneity by allowing agents to select their updates and further compute updates in an asynchronous fashion.

III-A

Hybrid updating

We construct
H^{t}
as follows:

\displaystyle H^{t}=J^{t}+\mu_{z}D+\mu_{\theta}SS^{\top}+\Delta,

(12)

where
J^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{md\times md}
is a block diagonal matrix, whose sub-blocks dictate the updating scheme of the corresponding agent,

is a diagonal matrix with entries equal to the degree of the corresponding agent, and
\Delta
is a constant matrix, whose sub-blocks serve to specify step sizes and provide numerical robustness. In specific, if the
i
-th agent opts to use Newton updates, then

i
-th agent opts to use first-order information, then

and the step size of the gradient descent is determined by specifying

. Note that when

is available. In specific, the

-th agent computes the update direction

\displaystyle(J^{t}_{ii}+\mu_{z}\absolutevalue{\mathcal{N}}_{i}+\delta_{il}\mu_{\theta}+\Delta_{ii})u_{i}^{t}=\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{t})+\phi_{i}^{t}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{\mu_{z}}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(x_{i}^{t}-x_{j}^{t})+\delta_{il}(\lambda^{t}+\mu_{\theta}(x^{t}_{l}-\theta^{t})),

. The block-diagonal structure of

in (

\displaystyle J^{t}+\mu_{z}L_{s}+\mu_{\theta}SS^{\top}+\Delta,

\displaystyle\eta\norm{v^{t+1}_\alpha-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\norm{v^t_\alpha-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}-\norm{v^{t+1}-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}.

(42)

Using (

\displaystyle\eta\norm{v^{t+1}_\alpha-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\tfrac{2m_{f}M_{f}}{m_{f}+M_{f}}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{2}{m_{f}+M_{f}}\norm{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^\star)}^{2}+2(x^{t+1}-x^{\star})^{\top}e^{t}.

We expand
\eta\norm{v^{t+1}_\alpha-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\eta\norm{v^{t+1}_\alpha-v^\star_\alpha}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=\eta\bigg(\epsilon\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}+2\mu_{z}\norm{z^{t+1}-z^\star}^{2}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{2}{\mu_{z}}\norm{\alpha^{t+1}-\alpha^\star}^{2}+\mu_{\theta}\norm{\theta^{t+1}-\theta^\star}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\mu_{\theta}}\norm{\lambda^{t+1}-\lambda^\star}^{2}\bigg).

We proceed to establish upper bounds for each component. From Lemma 2, the following holds:

\displaystyle E_{s}^{\top}(\alpha^{t+1}-\alpha^{\star})+S(\lambda^{t+1}-\lambda^{\star})=-\big\{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^{\star})

\displaystyle+\epsilon(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+\mu_{z}E_{u}^{\top}(z^{t+1}-z^{t})+\mu_{\theta}S(\theta^{t+1}-\theta^{t})+e^{t}\big\}.

Using (

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{\mu_{z}}\norm{\alpha^{t+1}-\alpha^\star}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\mu_{\theta}}\norm{\lambda^{t+1}-\lambda^\star}^{2}\leq\tfrac{5}{\mu_{\theta}\sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{min}}}(\norm{e^t}^{2}

\displaystyle+\norm{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^\star)}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}

\displaystyle+\mu_{\theta}^{2}\norm{\theta^{t+1}-\theta^t}^{2}+\sigma^{L_{u}}_{\mathrm{max}}\mu_{z}^{2}\norm{z^{t+1}-z^t}^{2}),

(44)

where

. Moreover, since

2\mu_{z}\norm{z^{t+1}-z^\star}^{2}\leq\tfrac{\mu_{z}\sigma^{L_{u}}_{\mathrm{max}}}{2}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}.

Similarly, from the dual update (
11b
), the following holds:

\mu_{\theta}\norm{\theta^{t+1}-\theta^\star}^{2}\leq 2\mu_{\theta}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}+\tfrac{2}{\mu_{\theta}}\norm{\lambda^{t+1}-\lambda^t}^{2}.

Using these bounds and considering (
43
), we need to show the following holds for

,

\displaystyle\eta\norm{\va^{t+1}-\vas}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\eta\bigg\{\tfrac{5}{\mu_{\theta}\sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{min}}}\Big[\norm{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^\star)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{2}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}+\mu_{\theta}^{2}\norm{\theta^{t+1}-\theta^t}^{2}+\norm{e^t}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sigma^{L_{u}}_{\mathrm{max}}\mu_{z}^{2}\norm{z^{t+1}-z^t}^{2}\Big]+\tfrac{2}{\mu_{\theta}}\norm{\lambda^{t+1}-\lambda^t}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\epsilon+2\mu_{\theta}+\tfrac{\mu_{z}\sigma^{L_{u}}_{\mathrm{max}}}{2})\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}\bigg\}\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{2m_{f}M_{f}}{m_{f}+M_{f}}\norm{x^{t+1}-x^\star}^{2}+\tfrac{2}{m_{f}+M_{f}}\norm{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^\star)}^{2}

Note that

\displaystyle\zeta\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}_{(\Pi^{t})^{2}}+\eta\bigg\{\tfrac{5}{\mu_{\theta}\sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{min}}}\Big[\norm{\nabla F(x^{t+1})-\nabla F(x^\star)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}_{\epsilon^{2}I+(\Pi^{t})^{2}}+\mu_{\theta}^{2}\norm{\theta^{t+1}-\theta^t}^{2}

\displaystyle\eta\leq\tfrac{\mu_{\theta}\sigma^{+}_{\mathrm{min}}}{5}\cdot\tfrac{\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}_{\epsilon I-\zeta(\Pi^{t})^{2}}}{\norm{x^{t+1}-x^t}^{2}_{\epsilon^{2}I+(\Pi^{t})^{2}}}.

\displaystyle\norm{\va^{t+1}-\vas}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}}=\norm{\va^{t}+\Omega_\alpha^{t+1}(T\va^t-\va^t)-\vas}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}}

\displaystyle+(Tv_{\alpha}^{t}-v_{\alpha}^{t})^{\top}\Omega_{\alpha}^{t+1}\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}\Omega_{\alpha}^{t+1}(Tv_{\alpha}^{t}-v_{\alpha}^{t}).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{t}\left[\norm{\va^{t+1}-\vas}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}}\right]=\norm{\va^{t}-\vas}_{\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}}^{2}+\norm{T\va^t-\va^t}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle+2(v_{\alpha}^{t}-v_{\alpha}^{\star})^{\top}\mathcal{H}(Tv_{\alpha}^{t}-v_{\alpha}^{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\tfrac{p^{\mathrm{min}}\eta}{1+\eta}\right)\norm{\va^t-\vas}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}\Omega_{\alpha}^{-1}},

Proof of Corollary 1
: We first distribute each
\alpha_{k},k\in[n]
, to each edge and label agents and edges with an arbitrary order. For each edge
\mathcal{E}_{k}
, we write
\mathcal{E}_{k}=(i,j)
with the convention
i<j
. Then for each agent
i
, we can divide the incident edges to two groups:
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\{k:\mathcal{E}_{k}=(i,j),j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{k:\mathcal{E}_{k}=(j,i),j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\}
. Consider the activation scheme using
\Omega^{t+1}
. Recall
\alpha^{t+1}_{k}=\alpha^{t}_{k}+\tfrac{\mu_{z}}{2}(x_{i}^{t+1}-x_{j}^{t+1})
. Then the dual updates are described by:

\displaystyle\phi^{t+1}_{i}=\phi^{t}_{i}+\tfrac{\mu_{z}}{2}X^{t+1}_{ii}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(x_{i}^{t+1}-x_{j}^{t+1})

\displaystyle=\phi_{i}^{t}+X^{t+1}_{ii}\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}(\alpha^{t+1}_{k}-\alpha^{t}_{k})+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(\alpha^{t}_{k}-\alpha^{t+1}_{k})\right\}.

Therefore, if
X^{t+1}_{ii}=I_{d}
, then
Y^{t+1}_{kk}=I_{d}
for
k\in P_{i}\cup S_{i}
for the corresponding
\Omega^{t+1}_{\alpha}
, i.e., all incident edges are active. It can be verified that we can map
X^{t+1}
to
Y^{t+1}
as:

Y^{t+1}=\textbf{Blkdiag}\left(\left\lceil{\frac{E_{u}X^{t+1}(\mathbf{1}\otimes I_{d})}{2}}\right\rceil\right),

where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the entry-wise ceiling operation and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the all one vector. To show
\mathbb{E}^{t}[\Omega^{t+1}_{\alpha}]\succ 0
, we only need to show
\mathbb{E}^{t}[Y^{t+1}]\succ 0
, which amounts to showing that
\mathbb{E}^{t}\left[\left\lceil{\frac{E_{u}X^{t+1}(\mathbf{1}\otimes I_{d})}{2}}\right\rceil_{k}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},k\in[n],
is positive definite. We can explicitly express each block as:

\left\lceil{\frac{E_{u}X^{t+1}(\mathbf{1}\otimes I_{d})}{2}}\right\rceil_{k}=\left\lceil{\frac{X^{t+1}_{ii}+X^{t+1}_{jj}}{2}}\right\rceil,

where
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}^{t}\left[\left\lceil{\frac{E_{u}X^{t+1}(\mathbf{1}\otimes I_{d})}{2}}\right\rceil_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{t}\left[\left\lceil{\frac{X^{t+1}_{ii}+X^{t+1}_{jj}}{2}}\right\rceil\right]\succ 0,

which shows that
\mathbb{E}^{t}[Y^{t+1}]\succ 0
.
\blacksquare

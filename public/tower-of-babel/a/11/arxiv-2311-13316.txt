Hardy spaces adapted to elliptic operators on open sets
Sebastian Bechtel, Tim Böhnlein

Abstract
. Let L = − div ( A ∇ ⋅ ) L=-\Div(A\nabla\cdot) be an elliptic operator defined on an open subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , complemented with mixed boundary conditions. Under suitable assumptions on the operator and the geometry, we derive an atomic characterization (depending only on the boundary conditions) for the Hardy space H L 1 \mathrm{H}^{1}_{L} defined using an adapted square function for L L . This generalizes known results of Auscher and Russ [ 7 ] in the case of pure Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions on Lipschitz domains. In particular, we develop a connection between the harmonic analysis of L L and its underlying geometry.

1.
Introduction

Let us start with the classical Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There are many equivalent descriptions for it, including atomic, maximal and square function characterizations. All these perspectives are valuable and prove their merit in various applications. For instance, both the maximal and square function characterizations are essential tools in the treatment of harmonic boundary value problems on the half-space: the maximal characterization allows a better understanding in which sense boundary values can actually be attained, whereas the square function characterization gives rise to solutions of boundary value problems using techniques from evolution equations. The atomic characterization makes cancellation properties of the Hardy space visible and accessible in the treatment of general singular integrals.

The space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be seen as a space adapted to the negative Laplacian
-\Delta
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Consequently, if one is interested in studying boundary value problems with respect to other elliptic operators than the Laplacian
[
6
]
, or maybe even boundary value problems in cylindrical domains
[
5
]
, it seems logical to study Hardy spaces that are adapted to a different elliptic operator than the Laplacian.

Let
d\geq 2
,
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open, and
A
a complex, bounded, elliptic coefficient matrix on
O
. Consider the elliptic operator in divergence form
L=-\Div(A\nabla\cdot)
subject to mixed boundary conditions.
For the moment, the reader can keep a bounded domain subject to pure Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions in mind. Nevertheless, we mention already here that the interplay between different boundary conditions and components of
O
leads to interesting effects that we are going to discuss in a minute. For the precise geometric and operator theoretic setup we refer to Section
2
.
We consider the operator adapted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}(O)
that consists of all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(O)
such that the square function

(1)

\displaystyle(S_{L}f)(x)=\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\fint_{\mathrm{B}(x,t)\cap O}|t\partial_{t}\e^{-t\sqrt{L}}f(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad x\in O,

is again in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(O)
. We are going to omit the underlying set
O
in the notation of function space from now on.

In the application to boundary value problems, adapted Hardy spaces describe the space of admissible boundary data. Therefore, it is desirable to have a description of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
which is independent of the concrete operator
L
. Atomic spaces can provide such a description.
Examples of atomic Hardy spaces on open sets include the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
studied by Coifman–Weiss
[
16
]
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
of Miyachi
[
23
]
. It turns out
[
7
,
13
]
that the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
corresponds to pure Neumann and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
to pure Dirichlet boundary conditions, in other words: under suitable assumptions,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
coincides with
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
if
L
is subject to pure Neumann boundary conditions and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
coincides with
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
if
L
is subject to pure Dirichlet boundary condition. We are going to review Hardy spaces on open sets in more depth in Section
3
.
To the best of our knowledge, if the matrix
A
is not self-adjoint then only the results from
[
7
]
relate square function based spaces with (
L
-independent) atomic spaces.
Their main geometric assumption is that
O
is connected with Lipschitz boundary.
The purpose of this paper is a generalization of
[
7
]
in the following two directions:

(i)

Can we work with a rough and unconnected open set
O
far below the Lipschitz class?

(ii)

Can we replace pure Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions by mixed boundary conditions?

Let us start with an example that highlights that some geometric conditions are necessary.

Example 1.1
.

Consider the punctured plane
O=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with boundary
\partial O=\{0\}
. Then
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(O)=\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence, the negative Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta_{0}
on
O
coincides with the usual negative Laplacian
-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consequently,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{0}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
[
7
]
. But
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
contains functions that are not mean value free, therefore
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{0}}\neq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
. This show that a fatness condition for
\partial O
cannot be avoided.

Pushing the example further, if
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of Hausdorff dimension less than
d-2
, then an application of Netrusov’s theorem
[
1
, Ch. 10]
gives a counterexample for
O=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D
. On the other hand, if the boundary has dimension larger than
d-2
, our theory gives an answer to the affirmative.

As a first gentle step into mixed boundary conditions, consider an open, bounded set
O=O_{1}\cup O_{2}
that consists of two separated connected components. Consider an operator
L
on
O
subject to pure Dirichlet boundary conditions on one component, and pure Neumann boundary conditions on the other component. A natural guess for the corresponding atomic Hardy space is

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(O)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}(O_{1})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}(O_{2}),

where
O_{1}
is the Neumann component and
O_{2}
is the Dirichlet component. Taking a look at the square function (
1
), such a decoupling is not to be expected as the square function always integrates over both components for
t
large enough.
Nevertheless, (
2
) was the correct guess and with its help we eventually even find

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(O)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L_{1}}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L_{2}},

where
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are restrictions of the elliptic operator
L
to the components
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
.
Going one step further, we even want to study the situation where
D\subseteq\partial O
is a non-trivial subset of the boundary of a, say connected, open set
O
. We have indicated in Example
1.1
that Dirichlet boundary conditions can lead to a lack of cancellation for atoms near the boundary in the sense that they are not mean value free anymore, in contrast to the case of classical atoms in the sense of Coifman–Weiss. The right definition for an atomic space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
therefore has to
“interpolate”
the definitions for
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
in a non-trivial way. We comment in Remark
3.2
on the upcoming challenges in this quest. Eventually, we come up with a suitable notion of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atoms in Definition
4.2
.
The abstract main result of this article is the following. The exact assumptions on geometry and elliptic operator will be introduced in Section
2
and we give a precise formulation of the result in Theorem
5.7
.

Theorem
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open and
D\subseteq\partial O
closed.
Assume that
O
is interior thick and locally uniform near
N\coloneqq\partial O\setminus D
, that
D
is porous and that there is some fatness condition away from
N
. Suppose that
L
satisfies Hölder-type Gaussian estimates. Then

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}.

We give some sufficient conditions for our geometric framework.

Example 1.2
.

Interior thickness ensures that
O
is at least a space of homogeneous type. Being a locally uniform domain near
N
is a generalization of Jones’ notion of an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domains
[
20
]
. All open sets whose boundary is represented by a graph are admissible, but also fractals like the von Koch snowflake, see Figure
1
. The condition for
D
means that the Dirichlet part is not of the same dimension as
O
and is essentially always fulfilled. It is in particular the case when
D
is Ahlfors–David regular, in which case also the fatness condition away from the Neumann part follows. Also exterior thick sets (see Proposition
3.3
) satisfy the fatness condition.

Our definition also unifies the cases of pure Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. See Proposition
4.5
for the precise assumptions.

Theorem
.

Under minimal geometric conditions one has

(5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}\qquad\&\qquad\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}.

An essential tool to prove our main result is a full duality theory for
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
. We are going to introduce
D
-adapted spaces
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
and
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
. A key feature of our approach is the fact that the
\mathrm{BMO}
-seminorm is defined by bounded means and not bounded mean oscillation near the Dirichlet boundary part. The following theorem recaptures our duality theory for
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
. Precise versions are given in Theorems
4.10
and
4.13
.

Theorem
.

Under minimal geometric assumptions one has

(6)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}\qquad\&\qquad(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D})^{*}=\mathrm{BMO}_{D}.

We have seen in Example
1.2
that our geometric framework comprises many relevant situations. So far, we have not discussed the Hölder-type Gaussian estimates (see Definition
2.9
) that are imposed on
L
in our main result.
In Lipschitz domains they were pioneered in
[
9
]
.
Recently, they were established in an article by the second-named author together with Ciani and Egert
[
12
]
for mixed boundary conditions.
As a combination of our main result, the Gaussian estimates from
[
12
]
and the recent article
[
13
]
in a self-adjoint setting, we give a full picture (including maximal characterizations) for
-\Delta
subject to mixed boundary conditions on a bounded open set. The following theorem contains the special case where
O
is connected and
D
is non-trivial. The precise formulation in the general case can be found in Theorem
6.5
.

Theorem
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain that is interior thick and let
D\subseteq\partial O
be non-trivial, closed and porous. Suppose that
O
is locally uniform near
N\coloneqq\partial O\setminus D
and fat away from
N
. Then

(7)

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\colon S_{-\Delta_{D}}(f)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{rad}},

where
-\Delta_{D}
is the negative Laplacian subject to mixed boundary conditions. The spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{rad}}
are non-tangential and radial maximal spaces.

In the preceding result we can always replace the Laplacian by an elliptic operator with a real and self-adjoint coefficient matrix. In
d=2
the first identity stays true if all restrictions but ellipticity on the coefficients are dropped
[
12
, Thm. 10.1]
.

Let us emphasize that all assumptions of the last theorem as well as the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
appearing in its conclusion are purely geometrical.

Notation

The space dimension
d\geq 2
is fixed and
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open with diameter
{\mathrm{d}(O)}\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}(O)
. Fix also some closed set
D\subseteq\partial O
called the
Dirichlet part
. Abbreviate function spaces over
O
by
\mathrm{X}\coloneqq\mathrm{X}(O)
, for instance write
\mathrm{L}^{2}
instead of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(O)
. Let
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}(x,r)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the Euclidean ball with center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
r=r(\mathrm{B})>0
. For
c>0
write
c\mathrm{B}(x,r)=\mathrm{B}(x,cr)
and for
j\geq 1
put
C_{j}(\mathrm{B})\coloneqq 2^{j+1}\mathrm{B}\setminus 2^{j}\mathrm{B}
. If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
set
E(x,r)\coloneqq\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap E
. Let
\mathrm{d}(E,F)
denote the distance between sets
E,F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Write
\bm{t}
and
\bm{z}
for the identity functions
t\mapsto t
and
z\mapsto z
defined either on a real interval or a complex sector, respectively. If not mentioned otherwise, all space equalities are understood in the set theoretic sense with equivalence of norms.

2.
Geometric framework and the elliptic operator

2.1.
The geometric setup

We start out with a list of geometric concepts.

Assumptions
.

Throughout this paper we make use of the following assumptions.

(UITC)

O
is
uniformly interior thick
:

(8)

\exists\,c>0\;\forall\,x\in O,r<{\mathrm{d}(O)}\colon\quad|\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap O|\geq cr^{d}.

(D)

D
is
uniformly porous
:

\exists\,\kappa\in(0,1]\;\forall\,x\in D,r<{\mathrm{d}(O)}\,\exists\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\quad\mathrm{B}(y,\kappa r)\subseteq\mathrm{B}(x,r)\setminus\partial O.

(LU)

O
is
locally uniform near
\bm{N}
. There are
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\delta\in(0,\infty]
such that the following properties hold.

(i)

Set
N_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\mathrm{d}(x,N)<\delta\}
. All points
x,y\in N_{\delta}\cap O
with
|x-y|<\delta
can be joined in
O
by an
\varepsilon
-cigar with respect to
\partial N_{\delta}\cap O
: There is a rectifiable curve
\gamma\subseteq O
of length
\ell(\gamma)\leq\nicefrac{{|x-y|}}{{\varepsilon}}
such that we have for all
z\in\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)
that

(9)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(z,\partial N_{\delta}\cap O)\geq\frac{\varepsilon|z-x||z-y|}{|x-y|}.

(ii)

O
has positive radius near
N
: There is some
C>0
such that all connected components
O^{\prime}
of
O
with
\partial O^{\prime}\cap N\neq\varnothing
satisfy
\mathrm{diam}(O^{\prime})\geq C\delta
.

(Fat)

O^{c}
is
locally
\bm{2}
-fat away from
\bm{N}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumption (LU) is a quantitative local connectedness condition near the Neumann part that has been introduced in
[
11
, Def. 2.3]
and is motivated from Jones’ definition of an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain
[
20
]
. Assumption (Fat) imposes a lower bound on the relative
2
-capacity on small scales, see
[
12
, Sec. 3]
for the precise definition. Eventually, it turns out
[
12
, Prop. 3.12]
that (Fat) is under the mere assumption (LU) equivalent to a weak Poincaré inequality for boundary balls (P).

Remark 2.2
.

There is a common variation of (UITC) in which only radii
r\leq 1
are considered. We refer to it as (ITC). It turns out that the constraint
r\leq 1
in (ITC) can be changed to
r\leq r_{0}
for any strictly positive
r_{0}
up to a change of the implied constant
c
. Therefore, (UITC) implies (ITC). The converse is false in general, but holds if
O
is bounded. We are going to write (ITC) instead of (UITC) whenever we want to make explicit that
O
is bounded.
Observe that due to (UITC),
O
is bounded if and only if
O
has finite measure.

We work with a concept of mean value free and locally constant functions that is adapted to
D
. Note that this terminology and notation is non-standard, but turns out to be very natural and convenient for our setting.

Definition 2.3
.

We enumerate the bounded connected components
O^{\prime}
of
O
that satisfy
\partial O^{\prime}\cap D=\varnothing
by
\{O_{m}\}_{m}
and write
I
for the countable index set.

(i)

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The
space
\bm{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{0}}
consists of
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}
such that
\int_{O_{m}}f\,\mathrm{d}x=0
for every
m
.

(ii)

We say that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}
is
locally constant
if
f
restricted to
O_{m}
is constant for every
m
and is zero otherwise. We identify this space with
\ell^{1}(I)
.

In the situation of Definition
2.3
put

O_{N}\coloneqq\bigcup_{m}O_{m}
and
O_{D}\coloneqq O\setminus O_{N}
.
We elaborate on the preceding definition by introducing a notion of
“components”
.

Definition 2.4
.

The elements of
\Sigma\coloneqq\{O_{D},O_{1},\dots,O_{m}\}
are called
components
of
O
. Note that one has the disjoint decomposition
O=\bigcup_{O^{\prime}\in\Sigma}O^{\prime}
by construction.

Remark 2.5
.

Some remarks are in order.

(i)

We stress that components need not be connected components. This is only ensured for the pure Neumann components
O_{m}
and may fail for the component
O_{D}
. The components reflect a decoupling of the elliptic operator
L
that stems from the presence of boundary conditions. We will see in Proposition
2.8
why it is natural to have such a decoupling. In this sense, locally constant functions in the sense of Definition
2.3
are functions that are constant on decoupled parts of the operator
L
and that respect themselves the Dirichlet boundary condition in
D
.

(ii)

Consider for instance the case where
O_{D}
consists of two connected components, both equipped with pure Dirichlet boundary conditions. Large parts of our theory work if we consider the two connected components of
O_{D}
as components of
O
. The notable exception is Lemma
2.6
below. It is of course possible that
O_{D}
satisfies (ITC) whereas this is false for all its connected components. To make our theory robust for such generalizations, we often write
D\cap\partial O^{\prime}
, even though this intersection is always
D
or
\varnothing
with the choice of components from Definition
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
O
is bounded and satisfies
(\mathrm{ITC})
and
(\mathrm{LU})
. Then the components of
O
have a strictly positive distance to each other and satisfy
(\mathrm{ITC})
.

Proof.

For the first claim, let
O^{\prime}
and
O^{\prime\prime}
be components of
O
. Assume without loss of generality that
O^{\prime}
is a pure Neumann component. If
\mathrm{d}(O^{\prime},O^{\prime\prime})<\nicefrac{{\delta}}{{2}}
, then there are
x\in O^{\prime}\cap N_{\delta}
and
y\in O^{\prime\prime}\cap N_{\delta}
with
|x-z|<\delta
. Then (LU) yields a path
\gamma\subseteq O
that connects
x
with
y
. Hence,
O^{\prime}=O^{\prime\prime}
since
O^{\prime}
is connected and the components are disjoint. Consequently,
\mathrm{d}(O^{\prime},O^{\prime\prime})\geq\delta
for distinct components.

We come to the second claim. Since
O
is bounded, it suffices to check (ITC) for balls with radius
r\leq r_{0}
up to our disposal. We take
r_{0}=\delta
.
Let
O^{\prime}
be a component of
O
. Balls centered in
O^{\prime}
cannot hit other components of
O
according to the first established claim from above. Therefore, (ITC) can be transferred from
O
to
O^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
(D)
. Then there are
c>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
such that

|\{x\in O(x_{0},r):\mathrm{d}_{D}(x)<s\}|\leq cs^{\eta}r^{d-\eta}

holds true for all
x_{0}\in O
,
r<{\mathrm{d}(O)}
and
s>0
.

Proof.

We can take
c=|\mathrm{B}(0,1)|
and any
\eta>0
for
r\leq s
. Now, assume that
s\leq r
. Since
\alpha\mapsto x^{\alpha}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
for
x\in(0,1]
, we can ensure that
\eta\in(0,1)
as soon as we have found some
\eta>0
. Using that
D
is uniformly porous, we can mimic the argument in
[
10
, Lem. 5.4.2 & Lem. A.1.8]
to prove the claim. Notice that we do not need the case distiction whether or not
r
is at most
\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}
.

2.2.
Operator theoretic setup

Here, we introduce the elliptic operator
L=-\Div(A\nabla\cdot)
subject to mixed boundary conditions.

To encode mixed boundary conditions, put
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{D}\coloneqq\{\varphi|_{O}:\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\cc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D)\}

and define
\mathrm{W}_{D}^{1,2}
as the closure of
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{D}
in
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(O;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
. We realize the divergence form operator
L=-\Div(A\nabla\cdot)
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
via the form

a(u,v)\coloneqq\int_{O}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad(u,v\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{D}),

for which we impose a
homogeneous Gårding inequality
:

(10)

\displaystyle\exists\,\lambda>0\,\forall\,u\in\mathrm{W}_{D}^{1,2}\colon\quad\re a(u,u)\geq\lambda\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}.

Its domain is given by

\mathsf{D}(L)\coloneqq\left\{u\in\mathrm{W}_{D}^{1,2}:\exists\,Lu\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\,\forall\,v\in\mathrm{W}_{D}^{1,2}:(Lu\,|\,v)_{2}=a(u,v)\right\}.

In general,
L
is not injective. We characterize its null space in the following proposition. A similar observation was indicated in
[
14
, p. 5]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume
(\mathrm{Fat})
and
(\mathrm{LU})
. Then the space
\mathsf{N}(L)
consists exactly of those
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
that are locally constant (in the sense of Definition
2.3
). Consequently,
\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}
and one has the topological splitting
\mathrm{L}^{2}=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}\oplus\mathsf{N}(L)
.
If
D
is empty, the same result holds without the geometric assumptions.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathsf{N}(L)
. Using the ellipticity condition (
10
) and the definition of
L
, we get

(11)

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\re a(u,u)\leq|(Lu\,|\,u)_{2}|=0.

Therefore,
u
is constant on each connected component of
O
. If
O_{D}\neq\varnothing
, let
x\in\partial O_{D}\cap D
. According to
[
12
, Prop. 3.12]
, there are
c,r>0
depending on the geometry such that

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{O_{D}(x,r)}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{O(x,cr)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Therefore,
u
is locally constant in the sense of Definition
2.3
. To the converse, let
u\in\mathrm{W}_{D}^{1,2}
be locally constant. Then,
a(u,v)=0
for all
v\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{D}
and hence
u\in\mathsf{N}(L)
.

The remaining assertions follow readily from the well-known kernel-range splitting for sectorial operators.
∎

21

2.3.
Property
\mathrm{G(}\mu\mathrm{)}

We continue with the precise notion of kernel bounds that we will employ in order to prove our main result, Theorem

.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mu\in(0,1]
. Say that
L
has
property

\bm{\mathrm{G}(\mu)}
if the following holds:

(G1)

For any
t>0
there is a measurable function

such that

(\e^{-t^{2}L}f)(x)=\int_{O}K_{t}(x,y)f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\qquad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2},\text{ a.e.}\;x\in O).

(G2)

There are
b,c>0

|K_{t}(x,y)|\leq ct^{-d}\e^{-b\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{2}}.

(G3)

There is
c>0
such that for every
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in O

0<t<{\mathrm{d}(O)}

|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq ct^{-d-\mu}(|x-x^{\prime}|+|y-y^{\prime}|)^{\mu}.

Let us mention that
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
originally goes back to
[

]
.

Remark 2.10
.

The following facts will be useful in this paper.

(i)

Property
\mathrm{G}(\mu)

(ii)

Lemma 2.11

\displaystyle\e^{-tL}u-u=\int_{0}^{t}\e^{-sL}Lu\,\mathrm{d}s=0,\quad t>0.

3.
Review of Hardy spaces on open sets

There appear different notions of Hardy spaces on an open set
O
in the literature. On the one hand, there are several atomic descriptions, notably those by Coifman–Weiss
[
16
]
and Miyachi
[
23
]
, and on the other hand also
“global”
constructions in which
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the point of departure to define new spaces as subspaces or quotient spaces
[
15
]
.
It turns out that all these spaces can be sorted into two categories: spaces that are associated with an operator subject to pure Dirichlet or pure Neumann boundary conditions.

To find a reasonable definition for the case of mixed boundary conditions, it is intrusive to understand these constructions better. Also, our very general geometric framework allows us to sharpen results on the pure Dirichlet and Neumann cases in the literature.

3.1.
Atomic spaces on an open set

All atomic
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-spaces consist of functions
f
that can be represented by an
atomic representation

f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where
(\lambda_{j})_{j}\in\ell^{1}
and the
a_{j}
are
“atoms”
. The decisive question here is the definition of an atom. In the case of the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
introduced by Coifman–Weiss
[
16
]
, a function
a\colon O\to\mathbb{C}
is called
atom
if there is a ball
\mathrm{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered in
O
such that

(19)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{i})\;\supp(a)\subseteq\mathrm{B}\cap\overline{O},\quad(\mathrm{ii})\;\|a\|_{2}\leq|\mathrm{B}\cap O|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\&\quad(\mathrm{iii})\int_{O}a\;\mathrm{d}x=0.

We refer to the properties (i), (ii) and (iii) as
localization
,
size condition
and
cancellation condition
. Here, the size condition is formulated with
\mathrm{L}^{2}
. In both the Coifman–Weiss and Miyachi cases, a more powerful result with a size condition in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
is feasible, but for the scope of our paper the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-perspective is the right one.
Observe that for
O=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we recover
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let us remark that in the special case where
O
is of finite measure, constant functions are also members of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
even though they are not mean value free.

For the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
by Miyachi
[
23
]
, the notion of an atom is relaxed. First, if
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is a function and
\mathrm{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a ball centered in
O
such that the localization, size and cancellation conditions are fulfilled, then
a=A|_{O}
is an atom. In particular, all Coifman–Weiss atoms are also Miyachi atoms under (UITC).
Second, if
a
is a function and
\mathrm{B}
is a ball centered in
O
that fulfill the localization and size conditions, and
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
but
5\mathrm{B}\cap O^{c}\neq\varnothing
, then
a
is also a Miyachi atom.

There appears another variation of Miyachi atoms in the literature, employed for instance in
[
13
, Def. 4.3]
and implicitly used in
[
7
]
. Given
a
and
\mathrm{B}
satisfying the localization and size condition as before, they either demand that
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
and
4\mathrm{B}\cap\partial O\neq\varnothing
, or that
4\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
and
a
satisfies the cancellation condition.

Proposition 3.1

(both Miyachi spaces coincide)
.

The Miyachi spaces in the sense of
[
23
]
coincide with those in the sense of
[
13
]
.

Proof.

Up to a change of implied constants, it is negligible that one definition is with respect to
4\mathrm{B}
and the other one with respect to
5\mathrm{B}
. In particular, the Miyachi space in the sense of
[
13
]
embeds into the space in the sense of
[
23
]
.

For the converse inclusion, let
a=A|_{O}
be an atom verifying the cancellation condition and such that either
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
or
5\mathrm{B}\cap O^{c}\neq\varnothing
. In the first case,
a
is immediately an atom in the sense of
[
13
]
. Otherwise, we can decompose
a
into a sum of atoms without a cancellation condition, for instance using a localization with the Vitali covering lemma. The details are presented in
[
13
, Proof of Lem. 4.7]
.
In a nutshell, we start with the trivial covering

\mathrm{B}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{B}(x,c\delta(x)),

where
\delta(x)
is the distance from
x
to
\partial O
and
c=\nicefrac{{1}}{{10}}
is a scaling constant. Vitali’s covering lemma yields a countable subcollection
\{x_{j}\}_{j}
such that the balls
\mathrm{B}_{j}\coloneqq\mathrm{B}(x_{j},5c\delta(x_{j}))
are essentially disjoint
and they satisfy
2\mathrm{B}_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial O
as well as
4\mathrm{B}_{j}\cap\partial O\neq\varnothing
by construction. The decomposition of
a
is then defined by localization in virtue of indicator functions to
\mathrm{B}_{j}
.

We remark that if the spaces were defined using cubes instead of balls, this decomposition could be achieved using a local Whitney decomposition relative to
2Q
. This alternative argument is employed in
[
7
, p. 167]
.
∎

The following remark explains why neither of the two definitions is right away suitable to define spaces adapted to mixed boundary conditions.

Remark 3.2
.

The Miyachi space in the sense of
[
13
]
has a priori a better localization of atoms. This is needed in the identification of
L
-adapted spaces, which becomes apparent from the references given in the proof. A naive idea to treat mixed boundary conditions might be to simply replace the distance function from
x
to
\partial O
by the distance function from
x
to
D
. Unfortunately, this does not work. The reason is that balls defined with respect to
\partial O
are either contained completely inside
O
or completely outside of
O
. Balls completely outside
O
can be
“thrown away”
. This is not true anymore in the case of mixed boundary conditions.

The definition that we are going to give in Section
4
is a third alternative way to define the Miyachi space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
as becomes apparent from Proposition
4.5
.

3.2.

The first construction is by means of restriction. Let
|_{O}
be the pointwise restriction of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to

. The

(20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{r}\coloneqq\bigl\{f\colon f=F|_{O},\;F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigr\},\qquad\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{r}}\coloneqq\inf\|F\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

The inclusion

without imposing any geometric assumptions. We mention that this inclusion reuses the argument presented in the proof of Proposition

For the converse inclusion, we only have to consider atoms

supported in a ball
\mathrm{B}
such that
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
but

, because the other atoms are by definition restrictions of atoms for

as an atom localized in the enlarged ball

4.
Duality theory for
D
-adapted spaces

We introduce atomic Hardy spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
subject to a cancellation condition in
D
and provide duality relations with corresponding spaces
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
and
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
.

4.1.
Philosophy behind our atoms

So far we have seen that classical atoms in the sense of Coifman–Weiss correspond to elliptic operators subject to pure Neumann boundary conditions and atoms in the sense of Miyachi to elliptic operators subject to pure Dirichlet boundary conditions. While classical atoms have a cancellation condition up to the boundary, for Miyachi atoms this is not the case anymore. Instead, the boundary conditions are supposed to compensate for a lack of cancellation of the atoms. Indeed, our Lemma
2.19
applied to the pure Dirichlet case gives that the kernel of the operator
\varphi(t^{2}L)
vanishes in
\partial O
. These operators will be the starting point to define operator-adapted spaces in Section
5
and we will see, for instance in the proof of the central Lemma
5.8
, that the cancellation of atoms can often be replaced by a vanishing kernel in a straightforward way.

In the realm of mixed boundary conditions, Lemma
2.19
shows that the kernel of
\varphi(t^{2}L)
vanishes in
D
. Consequently, atoms for a space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
should have the cancellation condition provided they are not close to
D
. We have already discussed in Remark
3.2
that it is not sufficient to simply replace
\partial O
in the definition of a Miyachi atom by
D
. Instead, we provide another variation of Miyachi’s definition that works in the situation of mixed boundary conditions and at the same time allows to treat the pure Dirichlet and Neumann cases in a unified way.

4.2.
Precise definitions

Let
x\in O
and
O^{\prime}
the component of
O
that contains
x
. Write
B(x,r)=\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap O^{\prime}
for the localization of
\mathrm{B}(x,r)
to its component. More generally, if
\mathrm{B}
is a ball centered in a component
O^{\prime}
, write
B=\mathrm{B}\cap O^{\prime}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Any ball
B=B(x,r)
with
x\in O
is called
usual
. If
x\in O^{\prime}
for
O^{\prime}\in\Sigma
and
\mathrm{B}(x,2r)\cap(D\cap\partial O^{\prime})\neq\varnothing
, then say that
B
is
near
\bm{D}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
B
be a (usual) ball and
a\colon O\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable.

(i)

We call
a
a
usual
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
-atom

\supp(a)\subseteq\overline{B},\quad\|a\|_{2}\leq|B|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\&\quad\int_{O}a\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(ii)

We call
a
an
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
-atom near
\bm{D}
, if
B
is near
D
,

\supp(a)\subseteq\overline{B}\quad\&\quad\|a\|_{2}\leq|B|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Definition 4.3
.

The
atomic Hardy space
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
consists of all measurable functions

for which there is a sequence
(\lambda_{j})_{j}\in\ell^{1}
and a sequence of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atoms

such that

pointwise almost everywhere. We equip this space with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}\coloneqq\inf\|(\lambda_{j})_{j}\|_{\ell^{1}},

where the infimum is taken over all atomic decompositions of

.

In addition, we denote by
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D,a}^{1}}
the subspace of all

\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
-atoms
. This space is by construction dense in
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that every atomic decomposition also converges in
\mathrm{L}^{1}
and
\|f\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
for all

. This means that the inclusion map

is a contraction.

The following proposition shows that Definition

indeed extends and unifies the pure Dirichlet and Neumann cases in our geometric setting.

Proposition 4.5

(compatibility with the Coifman–Weiss and Miyachi spaces)
.

Suppose (UITC).
In the pure Neumann case
D=\varnothing
and if the component have positive distance to each other (compare with Lemma
2.6
) one finds
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
. In the pure Dirichlet case
D=\partial O
we recover
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
.

Proof.

We show both cases separately.

pure Neumann case
.
The main difference between
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
is the concept of components in the definition of the former space.
Indeed, if
O
is unbounded and hence has infinite measure,
I=\varnothing
and all
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
-atoms are usual and coincide with the Coifman–Weiss atoms. Therefore,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
. Otherwise, if
O
is bounded, constants are added to
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
and
I\neq\varnothing
.

Start with the inclusion
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
.
All
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
-atoms are usual, so in particular contained in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
.
Fix some
m\in I
and
c_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
. We construct a function
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
that coincides with
c_{m}
on
O_{m}
and is zero otherwise as follows: let
h\in\mathbb{C}
to our disposal. The function
a
given by
h
on
O_{m}
and
-h\frac{|O_{m}|}{|O\setminus O_{m}|}
on
O\setminus O_{m}
is (up to normalization) a Coifman–Weiss atom. Put
c\coloneqq h\frac{|O_{m}|}{|O\setminus O_{m}|}
. Then
a+c
vanishes on
O\setminus O_{m}
and takes the value
h(1+\frac{|O_{m}|}{|O\setminus O_{m}|})
on
O_{m}
. An appropriate choice of
h
gives
h(1+\frac{|O_{m}|}{|O\setminus O_{m}|})=c_{m}
as claimed. Iterating this process gives
the first inclusion.

Next, we show
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)
. It is clear that a constant function is also locally constant. It remains to show the inclusion for an atom
a\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}
.
The discrepancy between a Coifman–Weiss atom and a usual atom for
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
is that the localization condition for Coifman–Weiss atoms ignores the concept of components, the same is true for the cancellation condition. However, if
a
is associated with a ball
\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap O
, and
r
is at most the distance between the components, then
\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap O=B(x,r)
and the localization and cancellation conditions for a usual
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
-atom are verified. Otherwise,
|\mathrm{B}(x,r)\cap O|\simeq|O_{m}|
for any
m
. Put
c=\sum_{m}(a)_{O_{m}}\bm{1}_{O_{m}}
and write
a=\sum_{m}\bm{1}_{O_{m}}(a-(a)_{O_{m}})+c
. The functions
\bm{1}_{O_{m}}(a-(a)_{O_{m}})
are constant multiples of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\varnothing}
-atoms associated with
O_{m}
and
c
is locally constant, hence the second inclusion is complete.

pure Dirichlet case
. Observe that we could equivalently define
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
-atoms near
\partial O
by the condition
5\mathrm{B}\cap\partial O\neq\varnothing
. We use this throughout this proof. Now recall from Section
3
that the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
consists of two types of atoms. (i)
a=A|_{O}
is the restriction of an
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-atom associated with a ball
\mathrm{B}
centered in
O
or (ii)
a
is associated with a ball
\mathrm{B}
that satisfies
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
,
5\mathrm{B}\cap O^{c}\neq\varnothing
and
a
satisfies the localization and size conditions.
We start with the inclusion
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
. Let
a
be of type (i). Then either
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
, in which case
a
is a usual atom for
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
, or
2\mathrm{B}\cap\partial O\neq\varnothing
, in which case
a
is an
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
-atom near
\partial O
. Next, let
a
be an atom of type (ii). Then automatically
a
is an
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
-atom near
\partial O
.

For the converse inclusion
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}
, let
a
be an
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
-atom near
\partial O
associated with
B
. If
2\mathrm{B}\subseteq O
, then
a
is immediately an atom of type (ii). Otherwise, employ the localization argument used in the proof of Proposition
3.1
to conclude. If
a
is a usual
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\partial O}
-atom, then extend
a
from
B
to
\mathrm{B}
by zero. Write
A
for this function on
\mathrm{B}
. By (UITC),
A
is (up to normalization) an
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
atom, so
a=A|_{O}
is of type (i).
∎

The space
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\loc}
in the following definition consists of all measurable functions that are square integrable on any bounded subset of
O
.

Definition 4.6
.

The
space of bounded mean oscillation

\bm{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}
consists of all
f\in\mathrm{L}_{\loc}^{2}
for which the (semi-)norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}\coloneqq\sup_{B\,\text{usual}}\left(\fint_{B}|f-(f)_{B}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\lor\;\sup_{B\,\text{near}\,D}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is finite.

The definition of
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
already indicates the natural
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}-\mathrm{BMO}
-duality. The key observation is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

It holds

(22)

\left|\int_{O}fg\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1},g\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}).

Proof.

Let
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
be an atomic decomposition of
f\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
. Since
g\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
and the series converges in
\mathrm{L}^{1}
we obtain

\int_{O}fg\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\int_{B_{j}}a_{j}g\,\mathrm{d}x.

If
a_{j}
is near
D
, then we directly apply Hölder’s inequality on
\int_{B_{j}}a_{j}g\,\mathrm{d}x
. In the other case we exploit the moment condition of
a_{j}
on
B_{j}
, which allows us to subtract
(g)_{B_{j}}
from
g
. Afterwards, apply Hölder’s inequality. Hence, using the size condition for the atoms and the very definition of the
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
-norm we get

\left|\int_{O}fg\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|(\lambda_{j})_{j}\|_{\ell^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}.

We conclude by taking the infimum over all possible atomic decompositions of
f
.
∎

Remark 4.8

(direct decomposition of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
and
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
)
.

The localization property of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atoms and its counterpart in the
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
-norm give rise to the topological splittings

\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}=\bigoplus_{O^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\mathrm{H}_{D\cap\partial O^{\prime}}^{1}(O^{\prime})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{BMO}_{D}=\bigoplus_{O^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\mathrm{BMO}_{D\cap\partial O^{\prime}}(O^{\prime}).

As a consequence of the preceding remark we are going to impose the following assumption for the rest of this section.

Assumption 4.9
.

Suppose that
O
satisfies (UITC) and consists of only one component.

It follows from Remark
4.8
that all results from this section stay true if
O
consists of multiple components that all satisfy (UITC). According to Corollary
2.12
and Lemma
2.6
, this is a consequence of
(\mathrm{G}(\mu))
in conjunction with (Fat) and (LU), so we can safely use all results developed in this section in the setting
of Theorem
5.7
.

4.3.
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}-\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
-duality

In this part we extend the classical
\mathrm{H}^{1}-\mathrm{BMO}
-duality of Fefferman
[
18
]
. This comprises also the pure Neumann case established by Coifman and Weiss
[
16
]
and we stress that no geometric assumptions are used.

Theorem 4.10
.

The space
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
is the dual space of
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
in the following sense:

(i)

Each
g\in\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
extends to a bounded linear functional on
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
via
\ell_{g}(f)=\int_{O}gf\,\mathrm{d}x
for each
f\in\mathrm{H}_{D,a}^{1}
and
\|\ell_{g}\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}\leq c\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}
for some generic constant
c
.

(ii)

For any
\ell\in(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}
there is a unique
h\in\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
such that
\ell=\ell_{h}
and
\|h\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}\leq c\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}
for some generic constant
c
.

Proof.

Proof of (i).

With (
22
) at our disposal, the argument in
[
16
, p. 632]
applies
verbatim
.

Proof of (ii).
Let
\ell\in(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}
. If
D
is empty, the argument is classical
[
16
, p. 632–634]
. Otherwise, we have to put in some extra effort to improve the classical estimate on balls near
D
. That being said, pick a reference ball
B^{\prime}
near
D
, which is possible in the current case. By definition,
B^{\prime}
also qualifies as a usual ball. Following the classical proof in the case
D=\varnothing
, there exists for any usual ball
B
a mean value free function
F_{B}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(B)
such that
\|F_{B}\|_{2}\leq|B|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}
. We set
f_{B}\coloneqq F_{B}-(F_{B})_{B^{\prime}}
. As in the classical case, the function
f(x)\coloneqq f_{B}(x)
, whenever
x\in B
is a usual ball, is well-defined. We claim that there is a constant
c
depending on
\ell
and
B^{\prime}
such that
h\coloneqq f+c\in\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
and
\ell_{h}=\ell
.

First, it follows readily that

(23)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}|f+(F_{B})_{B^{\prime}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(\fint_{B}|F_{B}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}.

Therefore, the
\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
-norm of
f
is under control on usual balls. The same is true if we add a constant to
f
later on.

Second, to improve the bound on balls
\widetilde{B}
near
D
we construct an auxiliary function. For any such
\widetilde{B}
there is a unique function
g_{\widetilde{B}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\widetilde{B})
that represents
\ell
restricted to
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\widetilde{B})
and fulfills the bound
\|g_{\widetilde{B}}\|_{2}\leq|\widetilde{B}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}
.
Now we can define a function
g
by
g(x)=g_{\widetilde{B}}(x)
whenever
x\in\widetilde{B}
is a ball near
D
. The difference to the classical construction is that
g_{\widetilde{B}}
is unique without the necessity to perform a normalization with respect to a reference ball.
Testing
g-f
with the mean value free functions of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\widetilde{B})
shows that
g-f
is constant on
\widetilde{B}
.
Since
f
is mean value free over the reference ball
B^{\prime}
, averaging
g-f
over
B^{\prime}
shows that this constant is
(g)_{B^{\prime}}
independent of the ball
\widetilde{B}
. Exhausting
O
by such balls
\widetilde{B}
yields
g=f+(g)_{B^{\prime}}
. Therefore,

(24)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{\widetilde{B}}|f+(g)_{B^{\prime}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(\fint_{\widetilde{B}}|g|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}.

Thus, with
c=(g)_{B^{\prime}}
one has
h=f+c\in\mathrm{BMO}_{D}
with
\|h\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}\leq c\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{*}}
as claimed.
∎

4.4.
Predual of the atomic Hardy space

The main goal of this subsection is to determine a predual of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
. To this end, we introduce a
D
-adapted version of the space of vanishing mean oscillation in the spirit of Coifman and Weiss.

Definition 4.11
.

The
space of vanishing mean oscillation
\bm{\mathrm{VMO}_{D}}
is the closure of
\mathrm{C}_{\cc}

with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{D}}
.

Note that
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
is separable as
\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
contains even a
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-dense countable subset.

Remark 4.12
.

There is some flexibility in the definition of
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
. Indeed, it is possible to take the closure of a suitable space of regular functions that vanish only in
D
. We will not pursue this direction in this article.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.13
.

Under
\mathrm{(UITC)}
the dual space of
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
is
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
up to equivalent norms.

This result naturally extends
[
16
, Thm. 4.1]
and we will need it in Section
5.4
to show the inclusion
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
. For its proof, we adapt the arguments in
[
16
]
.
We are going to use a trick to circumvent the
a priori
norming property of
\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
for
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
used in
[
16
]
, which we learned from
[
25
, p. 85]
.

The inclusion
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}}
.
This one is rather easy to see. Indeed, the proof of Lemma
4.7
reveals that this inclusion map is a contraction. Moreover, injectivity follows from the fact that
\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
separates the points of
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{1}
.

The inclusion
\bm{(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
.
By the closed graph theorem, it suffices to show the inclusion in the set theoretic sense. The proof consists of two parts. The first one is a purely functional analytic reduction argument and the second one is a particular
D
-adapted modification of the argument in
[
16
]
.

Write
\mathrm{B}_{X}
for the closed unit ball in a Banach space
X
.

Step 1.
Pick
\ell\in(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}
and assume without loss of generality that
\ell\neq 0
. After normalization we can assume that
\|\ell\|_{(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}}\leq 1
. Let
\widetilde{\ell}\in(\mathrm{BMO}_{D})^{*}
be a Hahn–Banach extension of
\ell
. By Theorem
4.10
there is a universal constant
c>0
such that
\widetilde{\ell}\in c\mathrm{B}_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{**}}
. Now, Goldstine’s theorem
[
24
, Thm. 8.3.17]
implies that
c\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
is
w^{*}
-dense in
c\mathrm{B}_{(\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1})^{**}}
. Using this and the separability of
\mathrm{VMO}_{D}
, we find a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq c\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
such that
f_{n}\to\ell
in the
w^{*}
-topology of
(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}
.

Step 2.
The upshot of the previous discussion is that the proof of the inclusion
(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
boils down to proving that any bounded sequence in
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
admits a subsequence which converges to some
f\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
in the
w^{*}
-topology of
(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}
.

The first auxiliary result is in the spirit of
[
16
, Lem. 4.4]
.
When
D=\varnothing
, our proof gives a technically less involved version of their result in the Euclidean setting.
For non-trivial
D
an additional argument is needed.

Lemma 4.14
.

Suppose Assumption
4.9
. Then any
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
admits an atomic decomposition

f=\sum_{(k,i)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{k,i}a_{k,i}\quad\text{with}\quad\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}\simeq\|(\lambda_{k,i})_{k,i}\|_{\ell^{1}}.

In addition, it holds:

(i)

If
a_{k,i}
is associated with
B_{k,i}
, then
r(\mathrm{B}_{k,i})\simeq 2^{k}
.

(ii)

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, only finitely many balls of
\{B_{k,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
can intersect a compact set in
\overline{O}
.

Proof.

Take an atomic decomposition
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
with
\lambda_{j}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying
\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}
and let
a_{j}
be associated with
B_{j}\coloneqq B(x_{j},r_{j})
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2^{k-1}<r_{j}\leq 2^{k}
. Now let
Q_{k,i}
be the unique cube in the standard half open dyadic grid of cubes with sidelength
2^{k+1}
that contains
x_{j}
. Express this relation by the notation
j\equiv(k,i)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Put
J_{k,i}\coloneqq\{j:j\equiv(k,i)\}
and let
j\in J_{k,i}
. We denote by
\mathrm{B}_{k,i}
a ball centered in
Q_{k,i}\cap O
of smallest radius such that
2Q_{k,i}
is contained. By construction, its radius is comparable to
2^{k}
, so (i) is satisfied. Also, since for fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
cubes from the dyadic grid
\{Q_{k,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are disjoint, a counting argument yields (ii).

To define a new atomic decomposition of
f
put

(25)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in J_{k,i}}\lambda_{j}\quad\&\quad a_{k,i}\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{j\in J_{k,i}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}}{\lambda_{k,i}}.

We check that
a_{k,i}
is (up to a multiplicative constant) an
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atom. First, since by construction
B_{j}\subseteq 2Q_{k,i}\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{k,i}
for
j\in J_{k,i}
,
a_{k,i}
is supported in
\overline{B_{k,i}}
. Second, for
j\in J_{k,i}
it follows from
r(\mathrm{B}_{j})\simeq 2^{k}\simeq r(\mathrm{B}_{k,i})
and (UITC) that
|B_{j}|\geq c^{-2}|B_{k,i}|
for an absolute constant
c>0
. Hence,

(26)

\displaystyle\|a_{k,i}\|_{2}\leq(\lambda_{k,i})^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_{k,i}}|\lambda_{j}|\|a_{j}\|_{2}\leq(\lambda_{k,i})^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_{k,i}}|\lambda_{j}||B_{j}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq c|B_{k,i}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Third, if
B_{k,i}
is usual, then already
B_{j}
was usual for all
j\in J_{k,i}
, so
a_{k,i}
still has mean value zero.
In summary,
a_{k,i}
is a constant multiple of an
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atom.

In summary, we deduce the atomic decomposition

(27)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k,i}(c\lambda_{k,i})(c^{-1}a_{k,i})\quad\text{with}\quad\sum_{k,i}c\lambda_{k,i}\leq 2c\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}.

The second key lemma is an extension of
[
16
, Lem. 4.2]
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Assume
\mathrm{(UITC)}
. Any bounded sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
has a subsequence, which converges to some
f\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
in the
w^{*}
-topology of
(\mathrm{VMO}_{D})^{*}
. Moreover, it holds
\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}\lesssim\sup_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}
.

Proof.

After normalization we can assume that
\sup_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}\leq 1
. Lemma
4.14
furnishes for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
a decomposition
f_{n}=\sum_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)a_{k,i}(n)
with
\lambda_{k,i}(n)\in(0,\infty)
and
\sup_{n}\sum_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)\lesssim 1
. After passing to an appropriate subsequence, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem joint with a diagonalization argument allows us to assume that
\lambda_{k,i}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{k,i}(n)
exists for all
(k,i)
and
\sum_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}\lesssim 1
, see also
[
16
, Lem. 4.3]
. Since
(a_{k,i}(n))_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{2}(B_{k,i})
is bounded, there is some
a_{k,i}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(B_{k,i})
such that
a_{k,i}(n)\to a_{k,i}
weakly in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(B_{k,i})
along a subsequence. A diagonalization argument ensures that we can choose the same subsequence for all
(k,i)
. The weak convergence implies that
a_{k,i}
inherits the size and (potentially) moment conditions from the atoms
(a_{k,i}(n))_{n}
. Thus, each
a_{k,i}
is an
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
-atom associated with
B_{k,i}
. We define the candidate

f\coloneqq\sum_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}a_{k,i}\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}.

By construction,
\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}\lesssim\sup_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}
.
It remains to show convergence.
By a
3\varepsilon
-argument it suffices to show

\langle f_{n}\,|\,\phi\rangle\to\langle f\,|\,\phi\rangle\mathrlap{\qquad(\phi\in\mathrm{C}_{\cc}).}

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
to be chosen. Split

\langle f_{n}\,|\,\phi\rangle=\left(\sum_{k<-K}+\sum_{|k|\leq K}+\sum_{k>K}\right)\sum_{i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)\int_{B_{k,i}}a_{k,i}(n)\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II)}+\mathrm{(III)}.

Compared to the argument in
[
16
]
, the major change lies in the treatment of the small cubes (I) as we have to consider atoms near
D
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. We claim that
K
can be chosen independently of
(k,i)
such that

|(\mathrm{I})|+|(\mathrm{III})|\lesssim\varepsilon.

The same argument will also entail a similar estimate for the decomposition of
f
.

Estimate for (I).
If
B_{k,i}
is usual, then
a_{k,i}(n)
is average free over
B_{k,i}
. Denote by
x_{k,i}
the center of
B_{k,i}
.
Since
\phi
is uniformly continuous, there is some
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|\leq\varepsilon
for all
x,y\in 2B_{k,i}
, where
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
k<-K
. Hence, with
y=x_{k,i}
and using also the size condition for atoms, we infer

\int_{B_{k,i}}|a_{k,i}(n)||\phi-\phi(x_{k,i})|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\varepsilon.

Now, let
B_{k,i}
be near
D
. Since
\phi
vanishes in
D
, we can apply uniform continuity with
y\in D\cap 2B_{k,i}
to give
|\phi(x)|\leq\varepsilon
for all
x\in B_{k,i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
k<-K
. Consequently,

\int_{B_{k,i}}|a_{k,i}(n)||\phi|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\varepsilon.

Combining the last two estimates, we obtain

|\mathrm{(I)}|\lesssim\varepsilon\sum_{k<-K}\sum_{i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)\lesssim\varepsilon.

Estimate for (III).
Using the size condition for atoms, (UITC) and Lemma
4.14
(i), we get

\mathrm{(III)}\leq\|\phi\|_{2}\sum_{k>K}\sum_{i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)|B_{k,i}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|\mathrm{B}_{K,i}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi\|_{2}\sum_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}(n)\lesssim 2^{-K\frac{d}{2}}\|\phi\|_{2}.

The right-hand side can be made smaller than
\varepsilon
provided that
K
is large enough.

Next, by Lemma
4.14
(ii) and the compact support of
\phi
,

\mathrm{(II)}\to\int_{O}\sum_{|k|\leq K,i}\lambda_{k,i}a_{k,i}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad(n\to\infty).

It follows the estimate

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\langle f-f_{n}\,|\,\phi\rangle|\lesssim\varepsilon,

which completes the proof.
∎

5.
Identification of operator-adapted and atomic Hardy spaces

We introduce the operator-adapted spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
and state and proof our main result (Theorem
5.7
). The proof heavily relies on the duality theory for atomic spaces developed in the last section. Throughout, we assume
(\mathrm{G}(\mu))
, (UITC), (Fat) and (LU).
Also (D) is used, but only once. We will make its usage explicit.

5.1.
Relevant definitions

Definition 5.1

(cones and square functions)
.

Let
x\in O
and
h>0
.
For a measurable function
f\colon(0,\infty)\times O\to\mathbb{C}
define its
local square function
by

(S^{\loc}f)(x)\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\mathrm{d}(O)}\fint_{O(x,t)}|f(t,y)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}y}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrlap{\qquad(x\in O).}

For either
\varphi(z)=\sqrt{z}\e^{-\sqrt{z}}
or
\varphi(z)=z\e^{-z}
set

S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)(x)\coloneqq S^{\loc}(\varphi(\bm{t}^{2}L)f)(x)\mathrlap{\qquad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{1},x\in O).}

Analogously,
S
and
S^{\mathrm{glob}}
are defined by the same expression as
S^{\loc}
but with
(0,{\mathrm{d}(O)})
replaced by
(0,\infty)
or
({\mathrm{d}(O)},\infty)
in the
t
-integral, respectively. These square functions lead to the
L
-adapted square functions
S_{\varphi,L}
and
S_{\varphi,L}^{\mathrm{glob}}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Due to (UITC), the local square function can equivalently be expressed via

(28)

\displaystyle(S^{\loc}f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{{\mathrm{d}(O)}}\int_{O(x,t)}|f(t,y)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}y}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad x\in O.

This is not possible for
S^{\mathrm{glob}}
. We will come back to this later in (
58
).

Definition 5.3
.

The
operator-adapted Hardy space
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}
consists of all
f\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{1}
for which
S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}
, where either
\varphi(z)=\sqrt{z}\e^{-\sqrt{z}}
or
\varphi(z)=z\e^{-z}
. Endow
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}\coloneqq\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)\|_{1}+\|f\|_{1}.

Remark 5.4

(dependence on
\varphi
)
.

At this stage, the definition of
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
using either the function
\varphi(z)=\sqrt{z}\e^{-\sqrt{z}}
or
\varphi(z)=z\e^{-z}
is a slight abuse of notation. We will of course treat both of them in all proofs. As a consequence of Theorem
5.7
it will turn out that the definition of
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
is indeed independent of the choice of
\varphi
up to equivalent norms.

Remark 5.5

(relation to the kernel–range splitting)
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}
. Then in particular
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}
. According to Proposition
2.8
this gives
f\in\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}
. A practical consequence is that if
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
on a suitable sector, then
\psi(L)f\in\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}
. Indeed, the functional calculus respects the kernel–range splitting.

Let us show that
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
can be equipped with an equivalent norm. In the sequel we are going to use this fact freely.

Proposition 5.6

(equivalent norm)
.

The space
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
can be equipped with the equivalent norm

\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}\simeq\|S_{\varphi,L}(f)\|_{1}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
.

(1)
\bm{O}
is unbounded
. Observe that
S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}=S_{\varphi,L}
since
O
is unbounded.

To control the
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-norm, suppose first that additionally
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
. Then the theory of pre-adapted spaces that we are going to introduce in Section
6
yields
\|f\|_{1}\lesssim\|S_{\varphi,L}(f)\|_{1}
, see Lemma
6.18
.

Now, we treat a general
f
in the Poisson case
\varphi(z)=\sqrt{z}\e^{-\sqrt{z}}
, with obvious modifications for
\varphi(z)=z\e^{-z}
. Owing to
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
one has the convergence
\e^{-s\sqrt{L}}f\to f
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}
as
s\to 0
. For this approximation, one has the uniform bound
\sup_{s>0}\|\e^{-s\sqrt{L}}f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}
which we are going to present in full detail in Step 2 of the proof of the inclusion
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
(Section
5.4
). Therefore, since
\e^{-s\sqrt{L}}f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
, it follows

(29)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1}=\lim_{s\to 0}\|\e^{-s\sqrt{L}}f\|_{1}\lesssim\liminf_{s\to 0}\|\e^{-s\sqrt{L}}f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}.

Observe that this calculation did not use that
O
is unbounded.

(2)
\bm{O}
is bounded
. Recall that
S_{\varphi,L}(f)\leq S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)+S_{\varphi,L}^{\mathrm{glob}}(f)
. The same argument as in Step 1 gives
\|f\|_{1}\lesssim\|S_{\varphi,L}(f)\|_{1}
, hence it suffices to show
\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\mathrm{glob}}(f)\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}
.

Indeed, using Hölder’s inequality, the averaging trick, the substitution
t\mapsto t+\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}(O)}}{{2}}
, the square function estimate in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
and
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
, we obtain

(30)

\displaystyle\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\mathrm{glob}}(f)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\mathrm{glob}}(f)\|_{2}

(31)

\displaystyle\simeq|O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathrm{d}(O)}^{\infty}\int_{O}|\sqrt{L}\e^{-(t-\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2})\sqrt{L}}\e^{-\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2}\sqrt{L}}f(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(32)

\displaystyle=|O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2}}^{\infty}\int_{O}|\sqrt{L}\e^{-t\sqrt{L}}\e^{-\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2}\sqrt{L}}f(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(33)

\displaystyle\lesssim|O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\varphi,L}(\e^{-\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2}\sqrt{L}}f)\|_{2}

(34)

\displaystyle\lesssim|O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\e^{-\frac{\mathrm{d}(O)}{2}\sqrt{L}}f\|_{2}

(35)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.\qed

5.2.
Precise result and strategy of the proof

Finally, we are able to state our main result.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open and
D\subseteq\partial O
closed. Suppose the geometric conditions
(\mathrm{UITC})
,
(\mathrm{LU})
, (D) and
(\mathrm{Fat})
. Moreover, suppose that
L
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}(\mu))
.
Then

\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}(O)=\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}(O).

To prove it, we show both inclusions separately. The proof for
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
builds on the ideas in
[
7
, Sec. 3.4]
. For the converse inclusion we choose a different route: the aforementioned work takes a detour through a maximal function characterization, which relies on the Lipschitz property of the domain. Taking a direct approach is one of the reasons why we can drastically lower the geometric requirements in our main result. In the self-adjoint case, we obtain nevertheless a maximal function characterization in Section
6
. The strategy for the individual inclusions is as follows.

\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}
.
Taking Remark
4.4
into account, this inclusion can be reinterpreted as proving boundedness of

S^{\loc}_{\varphi,L}\colon\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\to\mathrm{L}^{1}.

Using the atomic decomposition for
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
, it turns out that this follows from the uniform control on
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
-atoms

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{1}\lesssim 1.

Let
a
be associated with
B=\mathrm{B}\cap O^{\prime}
. To prove this bound, we split the
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-integral into a local and a global part,
4B
and
O^{\prime}\setminus 4B
. The
“local”
part can be purely treated by the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-theory of the operator. To control the
“global”
part, we split the
t
-integral in
(S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a))(x)
into several parts that let us exploit decay properties of the kernel stemming from
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
combined with either the cancellation condition of
a
or the vanishing of the kernel of
\varphi(t^{2}L)
in
D
(obtained in Lemma
2.19
), depending on whether the atom is usual or near
D
.

\bm{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
.

We explain the case of unbounded
O
first.
The main tool to show this inclusion is Theorem
4.13
. To make it applicable, we reproduce
f
in the functional calculus for
L
.
After combining the reproducing formula with a duality estimate in tent spaces, it remains to prove that the (local) Carleson functional

C^{\loc}(\varphi(\bm{t}^{2}L^{*})\,\cdot\,)\colon\mathrm{VMO}_{D}\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

that we are going to introduce in (
52
) is bounded. To this aim, we fix a ball
\mathrm{B}
centered in
O
and decompose the test function
\phi\in\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
as
\phi=(\phi)_{B}+(\phi-(\phi)_{B})
. Split
\phi-(\phi)_{B}
further into a
“local”
part
(\phi-(\phi)_{B})\bm{1}_{2B}
and a
“global”
part
(\phi-(\phi)_{B})\bm{1}_{O\setminus 2\mathrm{B}}
. Similarly as in the proof of
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
, the local part
C^{\loc}(\varphi(\bm{t}^{2}L^{*})(\phi-(\phi)_{B})\bm{1}_{2B})
can be controlled by the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-theory of
L
and the global part
C^{\loc}(\varphi(\bm{t}^{2}L^{*})(\phi-(\phi)_{B})\bm{1}_{O\setminus 2\mathrm{B}})
using decay properties of the kernel
q_{t}
. The main innovation lies in controlling
C^{\loc}(\varphi(\bm{t}^{2}L^{*})(\phi)_{B})
. There are three ingredients. First, we use a
D
-adapted conservation property for
(\varphi(t^{2}L))_{t>0}
(see Lemma
5.10
). Second, uniform porosity of
D
is used to estimate the size of boundary strips close to
D
. Let us mention that this is the first and last time in the present work that we need this information. Third, our definition of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
-atoms lets us treat certain large balls as boundary terms of a telescopic sum. At this point, we should mention that this change of definition also leads to a simplified argument in the case of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions compared to
[
7
, Lem. 15]
.

If
O
is bounded, more technical challenges appear, notably a reduction to small scales in the reproducing formula and a localization of
B
to multiple components. In each component, a different constant is subtracted in the decomposition of
\phi
.

The rest of this section is devoted to filling in the details.

5.3.
The inclusion
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}

For this part we need (UITC), (Fat), (LU) and
(\mathrm{G}(\mu))
. The following lemma is central. Recall the kernel
q_{t}
from Definition
2.20
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Assume
\mathrm{(Fat)}
,
\mathrm{(LU)}
and
(\mathrm{G}(\mu))
. Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
a
be an
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
-atom associated with
B=B(x,r)
. Then it holds

\left|\int_{O(x,r)}q_{t}(z,y)a(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\lesssim t^{-d}\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\theta\mu}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(y)}{t}\right)^{-(d+1)(1-\theta)}

for all
y\in O
and
0<t<{\mathrm{d}(O)}
.

Proof.

Fix
y\in O
and
0<t<{\mathrm{d}(O)}
. By (
16
) and the size condition on
a
we obtain

(36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{O(x,r)}q_{t}(z,y)a(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|&\lesssim t^{-d}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(y)}{t}\right)^{-(d+1)}\|a\|_{1}\\
&\leq t^{-d}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(y)}{t}\right)^{-(d+1)}.\end{split}

Next, if
B
is away from
D
, then
a
satisfies the moment condition. Hence, the version of (
17
) for
q_{t}
delivers

(37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{O(x,r)}q_{t}(z,y)a(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|&=\left|\int_{O(x,r)}(q_{t}(z,y)-q_{t}(x,y))a(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|\\
&\lesssim t^{-d}\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\mu}\|a\|_{1}\\
&\leq t^{-d}\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\mu}.\end{split}

If
B
is near
D
, let
x_{B}\in 2\mathrm{B}\cap D
. Lemma
2.19
gives
q_{t}(x_{B},y)=0
. Then a similar calculation as before in which
q_{t}(x_{B},y)
takes the role of
q_{t}(x,y)
gives (
37
) in this situation.

We conclude by taking logarithmic convex combinations between (
36
) and (
37
).
∎

Proof of the inclusion
\bm{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}
..

Since
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{1}
is a contraction it suffices to prove that

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}.

We tacitly use Remark
5.2
in this proof.
Let us assume for the moment that there is some
C>0
such that

(38)

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{1}\leq C

holds true for any
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
-atom
a
. Let
f\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
and fix some atomic decomposition
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
with
\|(\lambda_{j})_{j}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}
. As
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
converges in
\mathrm{L}^{1}
, property
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
implies for all
0<t<{\mathrm{d}(O)}
fixed that

\varphi(t^{2}L)f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\varphi(t^{2}L)a_{j}

converges in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
. This yields for every
x\in O
the estimate

\|\varphi(t^{2}L)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(O(x,t))}^{2}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|\|\varphi(t^{2}L)a_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(O(x,t))}\Bigr)^{2},

which (together with monotone convergence) entails that

\displaystyle S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{{\mathrm{d}(O)}}\|\varphi(t^{2}L)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(O(x,t))}^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{{\mathrm{d}(O)}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|\|\varphi(t^{2}L)a_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(O(x,t))}\right)^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a_{j})(x).

Taking
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-norms on both sides, applying monotone convergence and using (
38
) gives

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(f)\|_{1}\leq C\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|\leq 2C\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}}.

Proof of (
38
).
Let
a
be an
\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}
-atom associated with
B=\mathrm{B}(x_{0},r)\cap O^{\prime}
. The proof is divided into two parts.

Local part.
By Hölder’s inequality and the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-boundedness of
S_{\varphi,L}
, we get

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(4\mathrm{B}\cap O)}\leq|4\mathrm{B}\cap O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{2}\lesssim|4\mathrm{B}\cap O|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|a\|_{2}.

Now, (UITC) for
O
and all its components along with the size condition of
a
provide the uniform bound

\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(4\mathrm{B}\cap O)}\lesssim 1.

Global part.
Fix
x\in O\setminus 4\mathrm{B}
and note that
r<\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}}{{2}}
. To find upper bounds for

S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{{\mathrm{d}(O)}}\int_{O(x,t)}\left|\int_{B}q_{t}(z,y)a(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

we split the time integral into the three parts

0<t\leq r,\quad r<t\leq\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}}{{2}}\quad\&\quad\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}}{{2}}\leq t<{\mathrm{d}(O)}.

We denote the corresponding integrals by (I), (II) and (III), respectively. Hence,

S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)(x)\leq\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II)}+\mathrm{(III)}.

Before we start estimating the right-hand side, we observe that if
t\leq\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}}{{2}}
and
y\in O(x,t)
, then

(39)

\mathrm{d}_{B}(y)\geq\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)-|x-y|\geq\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)-t\geq\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}{2}.

Estimate for (I).
Lemma
5.8
with
\theta=0
joint with (
39
) gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{(I)}\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{O(x,t)}\left(t^{-d}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+1)}t^{d+1}\right)^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim r\,\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+1)}.

Estimate for (II).
Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
d+\theta\mu>(d+1)(1-\theta)>d
. We use this
\theta
in Lemma
5.8
along with (
39
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{(II)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{r}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}{2}}\int_{O(x,t)}\left(t^{-d}\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\mu\theta}t^{(d+1)(1-\theta)}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+1)(1-\theta)}\right)^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\mu\theta}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+1)(1-\theta)}\left(\int_{r}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}{2}}t^{-2(d+\mu\theta-(d+1)(1-\theta))}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{(d+1)(1-\theta)-d}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+1)(1-\theta)}.

Estimate for (III).
Lemma
5.8
with
\theta=1
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{(III)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}{2}}^{\infty}\int_{O(x,t)}\left(t^{-d}\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\mu}\right)^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\mu}\left(\int_{\frac{\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)}{2}}^{\infty}t^{-2(d+\mu)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq r^{\mu}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+\mu)}.

Let
\gamma_{1}\coloneqq 1
,
\gamma_{2}\coloneqq(d+1)(1-\theta)-d
,
\gamma_{3}\coloneqq\mu
and recall that
\gamma_{2}>0
. Combining all three bounds and integrating over

yields

\displaystyle\|S_{\varphi,L}^{\loc}(a)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(O\setminus 4\mathrm{B})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{3}r^{\gamma_{i}}\int_{O\setminus 4\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{d}_{B}(x)^{-(d+\gamma_{i})}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{3}r^{\gamma_{i}}\int_{4r}^{\infty}s^{-\gamma_{i}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\simeq 1.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.4.
The inclusion
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}

In this part we explicitly make use of (D), (UITC) and

, even though the other assumptions from Theorem

We start with the

Lemma 5.10

(
D
-adapted conservation property)
.

Let

be a component of
O
. It holds

(41)

\left|\int_{O^{\prime}}q_{t}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\lesssim\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{D\cap\partial O^{\prime}}(y)}{t}\right)^{-1}\qquad(0<t<{\mathrm{d}(O)},y\in O^{\prime}).

If

\displaystyle P_{s}f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\cap\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}\quad(s>0)\quad\&\quad\sup_{0<s<{\mathrm{d}(O)}}\|P_{s}f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}.

In order to prove the claim, we modify an argument from
[
8
, Prop. 4.5]
to our needs.
First of all,
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
and Remark
5.5
yield
P_{s}f\in\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}
and
\|P_{s}f\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}
for all
0<s<{\mathrm{d}(O)}
. Hence, in the light of Proposition
5.6
, it suffices to show
\|S_{\varphi,L}(P_{s}f)\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}
.
Fix
N>d+1
and define
\varphi_{N}(z)\coloneqq z^{\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}}\e^{-\sqrt{z}}
.
Since
P_{s}f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\cap\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}
, a result from the theory of pre-adapted spaces
[
3
, Prop. 4.4]
provides

\|S_{\varphi,L}(P_{s}f)\|_{1}\lesssim\|S_{\varphi_{N},L}(P_{s}f)\|_{1}.

Now we are going to deal with the issue announced in Remark
5.2
.
Instead of simply working with the power function
\bm{t}^{N-d-1}
, we have to consider the function

(58)

\displaystyle\Phi:t\mapsto\frac{t^{N}}{t|O(x,t)|}.

We claim that it is essentially increasing, that is to say,
s<t
implies
\Phi(s)\leq C\Phi(t)
for an absolute constant
C>0
. Indeed, by (UITC) we only have to deal with the situation
s<{\mathrm{d}(O)}\leq t
. Then estimate with (UITC) and monotonicity properties

(59)

\displaystyle\frac{s^{N}}{s|O(x,s)|}\lesssim s^{N-1-d}\leq{\mathrm{d}(O)}^{N-1-d}=\frac{{\mathrm{d}(O)}^{N-1}}{|O(x,t)|}\leq\frac{t^{N}}{t|O(x,t)|},

which gives the claim.
It follows from (
58
) and the substitution
t^{\prime}=t+s
that

\|S_{\varphi_{N},L}(P_{s}f)\|_{1}\leq\|S_{\varphi_{N},L}(f)\|_{1}\lesssim\|S_{\varphi,L}(f)\|_{1},

where the last step follows again from
[
3
, Prop. 4.4]
. Note that this time we have to split
P_{t}=P_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}}P_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}}
and change the aperture of the cone to ensure the technical constraint that
[
3
, Prop. 4.4]
only applies to
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\cap\overline{\mathsf{R}(L)}
.
This completes the proof of (
57
).

Now, we complete Step 2. By (
57
) and Step 1,
P_{s}f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
with
\|P_{s}f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}\lesssim\|P_{s}f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}
. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
4.14
to the sequence
(P_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}}f)_{n}
. This yields
g\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}
with
\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}
such that
\langle P_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}}f\,|\,\phi\rangle\to\langle g\,|\,\phi\rangle
for every
\phi\in\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
along a subsequence. But owing to
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
,
(P_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is strongly continuous on
\mathrm{L}^{1}
. Therefore, also
\langle P_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}}f\,|\,\phi\rangle\to\langle f\,|\,\phi\rangle
. Since
\mathrm{C}_{\cc}
separates the points of
\mathrm{L}^{1}
,
f=g
follows and completes the proof.
∎

6.
Maximal characterization for hermitian coefficients

Throughout, assume that
L
satisfies
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
and is self-adjoint. The aim of this section is to enrich the result derived in Theorem
5.7
by a maximal function characterization. This extends the results given in
[
13
, Sec. 4]
on the pure Dirichlet and Neumann cases to mixed boundary conditions. For the negative Laplacian subject to mixed boundary conditions on a bounded domain, our very mild geometric assumptions let us recover the full picture of classical Hardy space theory in this way, see Theorem
6.5
.

Definition 6.1
.

For
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
we define the
non-tangential maximal function
adapted to
L
by

(60)

\displaystyle f_{L}^{*}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}\sup_{y\in O(x,t)}|\e^{-tL}f(y)|\mathrlap{\qquad(x\in O),}

and the
radial maximal function
adapted to
L
by

(61)

\displaystyle f_{L}^{+}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}|\e^{-tL}f(x)|\mathrlap{\qquad(x\in O).}

Remark 6.2
.

As a consequence of
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
, pointwise evaluation of
\e^{-tL}f
is well-defined.

This enables us to define
L
-adapted Hardy spaces using a maximal function characterization.

Definition 6.3
.

The
non-tangential
and
radial maximal pre-Hardy spaces

\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}
are defined as

(62)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\colon f_{L}^{*}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\bigr\},\qquad\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}}\coloneqq\|f_{L}^{*}\|_{1},

and

(63)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\colon f_{L}^{+}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\bigr\},\qquad\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}}\coloneqq\|f_{L}^{+}\|_{1}.

Then the
non-tangential
and
radial maximal Hardy spaces

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}
are defined as the completions of
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}
, respectively.

The abstract main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume that the operator
L
is self-adjoint and satisfies
\mathrm{G}(\mu)
for some
\mu\in(0,1]
. Suppose that the geometric requirements
(\mathrm{UITC})
,
(D)
,
(\mathrm{LU})
and
(\mathrm{Fat})
are fulfilled. Then one has the identity

(64)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L,\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}\oplus\ell^{1}(I),

where
\ell^{1}(I)
is the space of locally constant functions introduced in Definition
2.3
.

The framework developed by the second-named author together with Ciani and Egert
[
12
]
lets us conclude a powerful application for the negative Laplacian on a bounded open set.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded, and assume
(\mathrm{ITC})
,
(D)
,
\mathrm{(Fat)}
and
(\mathrm{LU})
. If
-\Delta_{D}
denotes the negative Laplacian subject to mixed boundary conditions, then we have the identities

(65)

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\colon S_{-\Delta_{D}}(f)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{D},\mathrm{rad}},

where the space on the left-hand side is equipped with the norm
\|f\|\coloneqq\|f\|_{1}+\|S_{-\Delta_{D}}(f)\|_{1}
.

Remark 6.6
.

(i)

We wish to emphasize that both the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}\oplus\ell^{1}(I)
and the assumptions of the theorem are purely geometrical and independent of any operator.

(ii)

The theorem is formulated for the negative Laplacian for convenience, but it is true for any elliptic operator with real and self-adjoint coefficient matrix.

We summarize as corollaries the pure Dirichlet and Neumann cases. This takes Proposition
4.5
into account.

Corollary 6.7

(pure Dirichlet case)
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded, and assume
(\mathrm{ITC})
,
(\mathrm{Fat})
, and that
\partial O
is porous.
If
-\Delta_{0}
denotes the negative Laplacian subject to pure Dirichlet boundary conditions, then we have the identities

(66)

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\colon S_{-\Delta_{0}}(f)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mi}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{0},\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{0},\mathrm{rad}},

where the space on the left-hand side is equipped with the norm
\|f\|\coloneqq\|S_{-\Delta_{0}}(f)\|_{1}
.

Corollary 6.8

(pure Neumann case)
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded, and assume
(\mathrm{ITC})
and
(\mathrm{LU})
.
If
-\Delta_{N}
denotes the negative Laplacian subject to pure Neumann boundary conditions, then we have the identities

(67)

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\colon S_{-\Delta_{N}}(f)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CW}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{N},\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{-\Delta_{N},\mathrm{rad}},

where the space on the left-hand side is equipped with the norm
\|f\|\coloneqq\|f\|_{1}+\|S_{-\Delta_{N}}(f)\|_{1}

6.1.
Bridging the theories using pre-adapted spaces

We introduce a pre-adapted version
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{L}
of our space
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
. Such spaces can be defined and studied only assuming that the operator
L
has a decent
\mathrm{L}^{2}

.

For the rest of this section, we suppose at least that

is self-adjoint and that the set

is a space of homogeneous type and the semigroup generated by
-L

Definition 6.9

The square function

[

In virtue of Proposition

Application to the negative Laplacian

6.5
.

6.4
. The geometric requirements are all fulfilled by assumption. Also,
-\Delta_{D}
is self-adjoint. To invoke the theorem, it remains to check

, it follows that the mapping

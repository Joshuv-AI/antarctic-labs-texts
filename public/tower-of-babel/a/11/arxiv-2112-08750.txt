Automorphisms of moduli spaces of principal bundles over a smooth curve
Roberto Fringuelli

Abstract
. For any almost-simple group G G over an algebraically closed field k k of characteristic zero, we describe the automorphism group of the moduli space of semistable G G -bundles over a connected smooth projective curve C C of genus at least 4 4 . The result is achieved by studying the singular fibers of the Hitchin fibration. As a byproduct, we provide a description of the irreducible components of two natural closed subsets in the Hitchin basis: the divisor of singular cameral curves and the divisor of singular Hitchin fibers.

Before presenting the main theorem, we fix some notations. There are three natural ways to produce automorphisms for the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(C)
of semistable
G
-bundles.

(a)

Pull-back along an automorphism
\sigma:C\to C
of the curve, i.e.

E\mapsto\sigma^{*}E.

(b)

Action of an outer automorphism
\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G)
of the group, i.e.

E\mapsto\rho(E):=E\times^{\rho,G}G,

where
E\times^{\rho,G}G
denotes the product
E\times G
modulo the equivalence relation
(eh,g)\sim(e,\rho(h)g)
, for any
e\in E
and
g,h\in G
.

(c)

Twisting by a
\mathscr{Z}(G)
-bundle
Z
over
C
(with
\mathscr{Z}(G)\subset G
center), i.e.

E\mapsto E\otimes Z,

where
E\otimes Z
denotes the fibered product
E\times_{C}Z
modulo the equivalence relation
(et,z)\sim(e,zt)
, for any
e\in E
,
z\in Z
and
t\in\mathscr{Z}(G)
.

For more details, see Section
5
. The connected components
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}_{G}(C)
of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(C)
are in bijection with the elements of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G)
. In general, the automorphisms described in (a), (b) and (c) may move these components. We are only interested in those automorphisms, which fix a given component. When
G
is semisimple, the automorphisms of type (a) and (c) fix the connected components. In particular, they descend to automorphisms of the irreducible variety
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C)
, for any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
. On the other hand, the automorphisms of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(C)
of type (b), preserving the connected component
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C)
, are given by elements in the subgroup of elements in
\operatorname{Out}(G)
fixing
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, i.e.

\operatorname{Out}(G,\delta):=\{\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G)|\pi_{1}(\rho)(\delta)=\delta\}\subset\operatorname{Out}(G).

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem A

(= Proposition

Let
C
be a connected smooth projective
k
-curve of genus
g\geq 4
,
G
be an almost-simple
k
-group and
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
. The morphism

\begin{array}[]{ccl}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))\rtimes\Big(\operatorname{Out}(G,\delta)\times\operatorname{Aut}(C)\Big)&\xrightarrow{\sim}&\operatorname{Aut}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C)\right)\\
(Z,\rho,\sigma)&\mapsto&\Big(E\mapsto\rho((\sigma^{-1})^{*}E)\otimes Z\Big)\end{array}

is an isomorphism of groups, where:

(i)

\operatorname{Aut}(C)
acts on
H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))
by pull-back, i.e.
\sigma.Z=(\sigma^{-1})^{*}Z
, for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(C)
,

(ii)

\operatorname{Out}(G,\delta)

H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))
as follows:
\rho.Z=Z\times^{\rho,\mathscr{Z}(G)}\mathscr{Z}(G)
, for any
\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G)
.

The same holds for the open locus
\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{G}(C)
of stable and simple
G

In particular, the automorphism group of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C)
is generated by the transformations of type (a), (b) and (c).

We also prove a Torelly-type theorem for these moduli spaces.

Theorem C

(= Theorem
4.3
and Corollary
4.4
)
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two connected smooth projective
k
-curves of genus
g\geq 2
and
G
be an almost-simple
k
-group, with the only exception of the cases where
g=2
and
G
is either
\operatorname{SL_{2}}
or
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
, which are excluded.

If
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta_{1}}(C_{1})
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta_{2}}(C_{2})
, for some
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\pi_{1}(G)
, then
C_{1}
is isomorphic to
C_{2}
. The same holds for the locus of stable and simple bundles.

We remark that Theorem
C
has been already proved in greater generality by Biswas and Hoffmann
[
15
]
, with different methods. In particular, they showed that the same statement holds if
G
is a non-abelian reductive group. Furthermore, Theorem
A
and Corollary
B
have been already proved in some cases. When
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, the computation has been carried out by Kouvidakis and Pantev
[
35
]
. An alternative proof has been provided by Hwang and Ramanan
[
32
]
. Later, Biswas, Gómez and Muñoz, after simplifying the proof for
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
in
[
11
]
, extended the result to the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}

[
12
]
. The cases
G
simply-connected of type
E_{6}
and
F_{4}
have been studied by Antón-Sancho
[
5
]
, by adapting the Biswas-Gómez-Muñoz strategy. All the proofs rely on the study of the singular fibers of the Hitchin fibration.

Our contribution consists in providing a unified version of Biswas-Gómez-Muñoz proof, which holds for any almost-simple group
G
. The main difference between our paper and the other ones consists in the study of the Hitchin fibration. Instead of using the theory of spectral covers, we will use the theory of cameral covers.

The main advantage of the cameral covers is that the picture is more uniform with respect to the spectral covers, as the group varies. Just for having an idea: it is well-known that, when
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n},\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, a spectral curve is smooth if and only if the corresponding Hitchin fiber is an abelian variety. For other groups, this statement is false. For example, when
G=\operatorname{SO}_{n}
, all the spectral curves are singular but the generic Hitchin fiber is an abelian variety (see
[
30
, §5.15, 5.17]
). Hence, the study of the relation between spectral curves and Hitchin fibers requires a case-by-case analysis. On the other hand, the generic cameral cover is smooth and the corresponding Hitchin fiber is smooth, for any reductive group
G
. However, unless
G
has semi-simple rank
1
(i.e. the derived subgroup
\mathscr{D}(G)\subset G
is equal to either
\operatorname{SL_{2}}
or
\operatorname{PGL_{2}}
), there are singular cameral covers, whose corresponding Hitchin fiber is an abelian variety. Despite this complication, the theory of cameral covers allows us to provide a uniform description of the locus in the Hitchin basis corresponding to abelian Hitchin fibers, for any reductive group
G
, avoiding the case-by-case analysis. For more details, see Section
3
and references therein.

We now briefly explain the proof of Theorem
A
. We only treat the surjectivity, which is the most delicate part. We start with an isomorphism of the moduli space

\varphi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C)\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C).

The codifferential
d\varphi^{*}
gives a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant birational morphism of the moduli space
\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{G}(C)
of semistable
G
-Higgs bundles, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{G}(C)
by scaling the Higgs field. Furthermore, the morphism
d\varphi^{*}
sits in a commutative diagram of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant morphisms of the following shape

\textstyle{\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{G}(C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\scriptstyle{d\varphi^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{H}^{\delta}_{G}(C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathbb{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}}

where
h
is the Hitchin fibration and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on the Hitchin basis
\mathbb{A}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rank}(G)}\mathbb{A}_{i}
with weight
d_{i}
on the subspace
\mathbb{A}_{i}:=H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{i}})
(in the paper
\omega
is the cotangent bundle of the curve).
In particular, the isomorphism
\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}
preserves:

(i)

the eigenspace
\mathbb{A}_{\max}
in
\mathbb{A}
of highest weight (i.e.
\max
is the integer such that

is the highest number among
d_{1},\ldots,d_{\operatorname{rank}(G)}
),

(ii)

the closed subset
\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname*{ab}}
of
\mathbb{A}
, where the fibers of the Hitchin fibration

are not abelian varieties.

The projectivization
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname*{ab}}\cap\mathbb{A}_{\max})
of the intersection between these two loci is equal to the dual variety of
C
with respect to the linear system
\mathbb{A}_{\max}
. Since the curve is equal to its double dual, the isomorphism
\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}
, restricted to the intersection
\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname*{ab}}\cap\mathbb{A}_{\max}
, produces an isomorphism
\sigma
of the curve
C
. Using this fact, after some work, we are able to give a more explicit description of
\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}
. In particular, we get:

\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{i}}_{C}(-\sigma(p))\right)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{i}}_{C}(-p)),\text{ for any }p\in C.

From this, we are able to show that for a generic
G
-bundle
E
with image
\varphi(E)
, there exists a canonical isomorphism of adjoint bundles

\operatorname*{ad}(E)\xrightarrow[\Psi]{\sim}\operatorname*{ad}(\varphi(E))\Longrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{E}\xrightarrow[\Psi]{\sim}\mathcal{N}_{\varphi(E)},

which preserves the subsets
\mathcal{N}_{E}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\varphi(E)}
of nilpotent elements of
\operatorname*{ad}(E)
and
\operatorname*{ad}(\varphi(E))
, respectively. Using the Springer resolution of the nilpotent cone in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we then show that
\Psi
induces an isomorphism of
G/B
-fibrations over
C

E/B\xrightarrow[\widehat{\Psi}]{\sim}\varphi(E)/B,\quad\text{ where }B\text{ Borel subgroup of }G.

Thanks to a result of Demazure, there exists a canonical isomorphism between the relative tangent bundle of the
G/B
-fibration
E/B
(resp.
\varphi(E)/B
) and the adjoint bundle
\operatorname*{ad}(E)
(resp.
\operatorname*{ad}(\varphi(E))
) and it preserves the Lie-brackets. Hence, the (relative) differential

\operatorname*{ad}(E)=\mathcal{T}_{(E/B)/C}\xrightarrow[d\widehat{\Psi}]{\sim}\mathcal{T}_{(\varphi(E)/B)/C}=\operatorname*{ad}(\varphi(E))

is an isomorphism of Lie algebra bundles. If this happens the
G
-bundles
E
and
\varphi(E)
differ by a combination of the transformations of type (a), (b) and (c). Since
E
was chosen generic and it sits in the same connected component of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(C)
containing
\varphi(E)
, the isomorphism
\varphi
must be in the image of the homomorphism described in Theorem
A
.

Further generalizations.
There are (at least) four ways of generalizing this problem. (1) What happens for a general reductive group? In this generality, the picture is more complicated. As an example, when
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
is the product of two almost-simple groups, the automorphism group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(C)
should contain two copies of
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
. Note that case
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
has been computed in
[
35
]
. (2) Another version of the problem would be to study the automorphism group for the so-called twisted moduli spaces of
G
-bundles (e.g. moduli spaces of vector bundles of fixed rank with determinant isomorphic to a fixed, non-trivial, line bundle). For instance, the twisted case has been treated in
[
35
]
and
[
11
]
for
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
and in
[
12
]
for
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
. (3) Another generalization is the case of parabolic (or, more generally, parahoric)
G
-bundles. When
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n},\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, the problem has been studied in
[
16
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
1
]
. (4) An alternative path could be the study of the automorphisms of the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
of all
G
-bundles. In this case, the automorphisms form a category (more precisely a
2
-group). We hope to come back on these problems in future works.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G

(i)

The inclusion

k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
, is freely generated by homogeneous polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
in
k[\mathfrak{g}]
, resp. in
k[\mathfrak{t}]
, of degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
. Moreover, the degrees do not depend on the choice of the polynomials.

(iii)

The morphism of affine varieties

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\chi:&\mathfrak{g}&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{c}:=\operatorname{Spec}(k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W})\\
&x&\longmapsto&(p_{1}(x),\ldots,p_{r}(x))\end{array}

is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
with weight
1
and it acts on
\mathfrak{c}
as follows:
t.(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}):=(t^{d_{1}}u_{1},\ldots,t^{d_{r}}u_{r})
.

(iv)

The morphism of affine varieties

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\pi:&\mathfrak{t}&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{c}\\
&t&\longmapsto&(p_{1}(t),\ldots,p_{r}(t))\end{array}

is a Galois cover with Galois group
W
. The discriminant (or branch divisor)
\mathfrak{D}
is given by the zeroes of the
W
-invariant function

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha.

(v)

For any simple root
\alpha
, we fix a non-zero vector
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, we set
x_{+}:=\sum_{\alpha\text{ simple }}x_{\alpha}
. Then, there exists a unique
\mathfrak{s}l_{2}
-triple
\{x_{-},h,x_{+}\}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, with
h\in\mathfrak{t}
.

(vi)

With the notations of Point
(\ref{T:konstant5})
. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{x_{+}}
be the Lie algebra of the centraliser of
x_{+}
. Then, the affine subspace
x_{-}+\mathfrak{g}^{x_{+}}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is contained in the open subset
\mathfrak{g}^{\operatorname*{reg}}
. Furthermore, the restriction of
\chi
to this subspace

x_{-}+\mathfrak{g}^{x_{+}}\to\mathfrak{c}

is an isomorphism. Its inverse
\epsilon:\mathfrak{c}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\operatorname*{reg}}
is called
Kostant section
.

(vii)

Let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup with Levi subgroup
M
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the corresponding parabolic subalgebra and
\mathfrak{m}
be its Levi subalgebra. Then the inclusions
\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{p}
induce the isomorphisms of rings
k[\mathfrak{p}]^{P}\cong k[\mathfrak{m}]^{M}\cong k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W_{M}}
, where

is the Weyl group of

.

Proof.

All the facts are proved in
[
34
]
(see also
[
37
, Theorem 2.1]
), with the only exception of the isomorphism

, which has been proved in
[
21
, Lemme 2.7.1]
.
∎

The Weil group
W
acts on the set of roots
\Phi
. We denote by
\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{m}
the sets of
W
-orbits in
\Phi
. This will give us the irreducible decomposition of the discriminant

\mathfrak{D}=\mathfrak{D}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathfrak{D}_{m},

where
\mathfrak{D}_{i}
is the zero-locus of the
W
-invariant function
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{i}}d\alpha
. Furthermore, the singular locus
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname*{sing}}
is the zero loci of the polynomial system

\begin{cases}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha=0,\\
\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\left(\prod_{\beta\in\Phi\setminus\{\alpha\}}d\beta\right)=0.\end{cases}

In other words, it may be described as the image of points in
\mathfrak{t}
contained in at least two distinct hyperplane-roots. In particular, it is a union of irreducible closed subsets in
\mathfrak{c}
of dimension
r-2
. We denote these components by
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname*{sing}}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname*{sing}}_{n}
. It follows from the discussion that the irreducible components of
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname*{sing}}
are in bijection with orbits of the diagonal action of the Weil group
W
on the product
\Phi\times\Phi
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
G
be a reductive group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Here, we collect some facts about the degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
introduced in Theorem
1.1
.

(i)

G
is semisimple if and only if all the degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
are greater or equal than
2
.

(ii)

If
G
almost-simple, the highest degree among
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
is the so-called Coxeter number
h
, which is equal to the number of roots divided by the rank of the maximal torus, i.e.
h:=\frac{|\Phi|}{r}
.

(iii)

G=T
is abelian if and only if all the degrees are equal to
1
. Furthermore, we have
\pi_{G}=\operatorname{Id}:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In general, a reductive group
G
admits a canonical isogeny
\mathscr{Z}(G)^{o}\times\mathscr{D}(G)\to G
, where
\mathscr{Z}(G)^{o}\subset G
is the neutral component of the center
\mathscr{Z}(G)
and
\mathscr{D}(G)\subset G
is the derived subgroup. Then, we have the a commutative diagram of affine schemes

(1.1)

\textstyle{\mathfrak{t}_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{G}}

\textstyle{\mathfrak{c}_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{Z}(G)}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(\operatorname{Id},\pi_{\mathscr{D}(G)})}

\textstyle{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{Z}(G)}\oplus\mathfrak{c}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}}

where the horizontal rows are the morphisms in Theorem
1.1
(\ref{T:konstant4})
.

We do not know an explicit description of the function
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha
as a polynomial in
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
. However, as the next result shows, we have a partial description, which is enough for our purposes (e.g. see Proposition
4.2
)

Lemma 1.4
.

Assume
\mathfrak{g}
simple. If we order the degrees such that
d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{r}
, we have the following equality

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha=cp_{r}^{r}+f_{1}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})+\ldots+f_{m}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}),

where
c\in k\setminus\{0\}
and
f_{i}
are monomials in
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}=k[p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}]
of total degree stricly greater than
r
.

Proof.

Since we assume
\mathfrak{g}
simple, there exists an isomorphism of rings

k[\mathfrak{t}]\cong k[d\alpha_{1},\ldots,d\alpha_{r}],

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
are the simple roots. The polynomial
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha
is an homogeneous polynomial of degree
|\Phi|
in
k[\mathfrak{t}]
. On the other hand,
p_{r}
is an homogeneous polynomial in
k[\mathfrak{t}]
of degree
|\Phi|/r
(see Remark
1.2
). Hence we must have,

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}d\alpha=cp_{r}^{r}+f_{1}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})+\ldots+f_{m}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}),

where
c\in k
and
f_{i}
are monomials in
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}=k[p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}]
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f_{i}\neq dp_{r}^{r}
for some constant
d\in k\setminus\{0\}
, for any
i
. We need to show that
c\neq 0
and
\deg(f_{i})>r
.

c\neq 0
We prove it by contradiction. Assume that
c=0
. Then, any element
t\in\mathfrak{t}
such that
\pi(t)=(0,\ldots,0,a)\in\mathfrak{c}
, for some
a\in k
, is contained in some hyperplane-root
d\alpha:\mathfrak{t}\to k
. In particular, for any
t
of this shape, the kernel of the adjoint homomorphism
[t,-]:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
contains the sublinear space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
. In particular, the kernel of
[t,-]
have dimension at least
r+2
, i.e.
t
is not regular. However, if
a\neq 0
, the element
t
must be cyclic (then regular), see
[
33
, Corollary 9.2]
.

\deg(f_{i})>r
We prove it by contradiction. Assume that
\deg(f_{i})\leq r
, i.e. there exist
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, with
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
i\neq r
, such that
f_{i}=p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{a_{r}}
,
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}\leq r
and
a_{1}d_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}d_{r}=|\Phi|
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
simple, the degree
d_{i}
is stricly smaller than
d_{r}=|\Phi|/r
for any
i<r
. In particular, since there is at least one
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
i<r
, we get the contradiction

|\Phi|=a_{1}d_{1}+\ldots+a_{r}d_{r}<(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r})|\Phi|/r\leq|\Phi|.

∎

2.
Hitchin fibration.

Here, we recall some fundamental facts about (Higgs) bundles on curves and their moduli stacks following the works of Faltings
[
24
]
and Ngô
[
38
]
. Unless otherwise stated, in this section,
G
is a (connected) reductive group and
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.

2.1.
The moduli stack
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
of
G
-Higgs bundles.

For any
G
-bundle
E
over
C
, we denote by
\operatorname*{ad}(E):=E\times^{\operatorname{Ad},G}\mathfrak{g}
the adjoint bundle on
C
of attached to
E
via the adjoint representation
\text{Ad}:G\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
G
-Higgs bundle
is a pair
(E,\theta)
, where
E
is a
G
-bundle over
C
and
\theta
is a global section of the vector bundle
\operatorname*{ad}(E)\otimes\omega
. A
regular
G
-Higgs bundle
is a
G
-Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
such that, for any point
p\in C
, the kernel of the linear morphism of vector spaces

[\theta_{p},-]:\operatorname*{ad}(E_{p})\to\operatorname*{ad}(E_{p})\otimes\omega_{p}

has dimension
r
.

We denote by
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
the moduli stack of
G
-Higgs bundles over
C
and by
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
the moduli stack of
G
-bundles over
C
. They are algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
k
. They are related by the forgetful morphism

\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}\to\mathbf{Bun}_{G}:(E,\theta)\mapsto E.

Consider the affine space
\mathbb{A}
of sections of the vector bundle
\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}:=\mathfrak{c}\times^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\omega^{*}
, where
\omega^{*}:=\omega\setminus\{\operatorname{zero-section}\}
is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-bundle attached to
\omega
. After fixing a set of generators of
k[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}=k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
, we get the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant isomorphisms

\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}\cong\omega^{d_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus\omega^{d_{r}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{A}\cong H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{1}})\oplus\ldots\oplus H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{r}}).

For the rest of the paper, we will fix such a set of free generators, we also assume that
d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{r}
and we set
\mathbb{A}_{i}:=H^{0}(C,\omega^{d_{i}})
. From this decomposition, we deduce that:

\displaystyle\dim\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}(2g-2)+r(1-g)+\#\{i|d_{i}=1\}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}(2g-2)+r(1-g)+\dim\mathscr{Z}(G)=

\displaystyle=\left(r+\frac{|\Phi|}{2}\right)(2g-2)+r(1-g)+\dim\mathscr{Z}(G)=

\displaystyle=\dim G(g-1)+\dim\mathscr{Z}(G).

Indeed, the first two equalities follows by using Riemann-Roch and Remarks
1.2
and
1.3
. We recall that the integers
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
are the degrees of a set of free generators for the ring of
W
-invariants
k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
. Since the Weyl group
W
is generated by
|\Phi|/2
reflections, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(d_{i}-1)=|\Phi|/2
(see
[
41
]
) and, so, the third equality follows immediately. The last equality follows because
\dim G=\dim\mathfrak{g}=r+|\Phi|
.

We call
Hitchin morphism
the following map:

\begin{array}[]{cccl}h:&\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{A}\\
&(E,\theta)&\longmapsto&\left(p_{1}(\theta),\ldots,p_{r}(\theta)\right).\end{array}

Given a section
a\in\mathbb{A}
, we call
Hitchin fiber over
a
the inverse image
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a):=h^{-1}(a)
. The choice of a theta-characteristic
\kappa
(i.e. a line bundle
\kappa
such that
\kappa^{2}=\omega
) and the Kostant section (see Theorem
1.1
(v)) gives a section of the Hitchin morphism

(2.1)

\epsilon_{\kappa}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbf{Higgs}_{G},

called the
Hitchin-Kostant section
, for more details see
[
37
, Proposition 2.5]
and
[
38
, Lemma 2.2.5]
. The image of
\epsilon_{\kappa}
is contained in the locus
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}^{\operatorname*{reg}}
of regular
G
-Higgs bundles, see
[
38
, §4.3.4]
. In particular, we get a family of regular
G
-Higgs bundles over
\mathbb{A}
. For any
a\in\mathbb{A}
, we denote by
(E_{a},\theta_{a}):=\epsilon_{\kappa}(a)
, the corresponding regular
G
-Higgs bundle given by the image of the Hitchin-Kostant section.

We conclude the subsection with some basic facts about these moduli stacks. These facts are probably well-known to the experts. However, we did not find a reference in this generality. For this reason, we added a sketch of the proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following facts hold.

(i)

The connected components of the moduli stacks
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
and
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
are in bijection with
\pi_{1}(G)
.

(ii)

For any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, the corresponding connected component
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}^{\delta}
of the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
is a smooth algebraic stack locally of finite type over
k
. Furthermore, it has dimension
\dim G(g-1)
.

(iii)

For any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, the corresponding connected component
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}^{\delta}
of the moduli stack
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
is an irreducible algebraic stack locally of finite type over
k
. Furthermore, it is a locally complete intersection of pure dimension
2\dim G(g-1)+\dim\mathscr{Z}(G)
.

(iv)

The Hitchin morphism
h^{\delta}:\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}^{\delta}\to\mathbb{A}
is faithfully flat with fibers of pure dimension
\dim G(g-1)
.

Proof.

(\ref{P:hit-prop2})
The moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
is locally of finite type over
k
by
[
8
, Proposition 4.4.5]
and smooth by
[
8
, Proposition 4.5.1]
. For the dimension see
[
8
, Corollary 8.1.9]
.

(\ref{P:hit-prop1})
+
(\ref{P:hit-prop3})
The statement of
(i)
for
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
can be found in
[
31
]
. We now focus on the moduli stack
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
. When
G
is semi-simple, a proof can be found in
[
9
]
. More precisely, by
[
9
, Proposition 1.1.1]
, the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
is
very good
, i.e. for any integer
n>0
, we have that the substack

\{E\in\mathbf{Bun}_{G}|\dim\operatorname{Aut}(E)=n\}=\{E\in\mathbf{Bun}_{G}|,\dim H^{1}(C,\operatorname*{ad}(E)\otimes\omega)=n\}

has codimension strictly greater than
n
in
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
. We remark that the two sets above coincide by Serre duality and by the equality
\dim H^{0}(C,\operatorname*{ad}(E))=\dim\operatorname{Aut}(E)
. The property of being very good implies that the forgetful morphism
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}\to\mathbf{Bun}_{G}

\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}

. Now, we assume

\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}
sits in the following cartesian diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{Higgs}_{G^{\operatorname{ad}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(C,\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{Z}(G)}\otimes\omega)\times\mathbf{Bun}_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the horizontal map on the bottom sends the pair

\displaystyle=\dim\mathscr{Z}(G)(2g-1).

2.2.
Cameral covers.

To any element
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{A}
, the corresponding
cameral curve

C_{a}
is the curve in the total space of the vector bundle
\mathfrak{t}_{\omega}:=\mathfrak{t}\times\omega
defined by the equations

\begin{cases}p_{1}(t)=a_{1}(x),\\
\vdots\\
p_{r}(t)=a_{r}(x),\end{cases}

where
x
is a (local) coordinate for
C
and
t
are the tautological coordinates
(t_{1}\otimes dx,\ldots,t_{r}\otimes dx)
along the fibers of the projection
\mathfrak{t}_{\omega}\to C
.
In other words, the cameral curve sits in the following cartesian diagram

(2.4)

\textstyle{C_{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{a}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{t}_{\omega}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}.}

In the paper, we use the same symbol
\pi
for the morphism
\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{c}
in Theorem
1.1
(\ref{T:konstant4})
and its
\omega
-twisted version
\pi:\mathfrak{t}_{\omega}\to\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}
in (
2.4
). We call

the morphism
\pi_{a}:C_{a}\to C
in (
2.4
).

The
discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}:=\mathfrak{D}\times^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\omega^{*}
is an hypersurface in
\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}
. The image

of a section
a\in\mathbb{A}
always meets the divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}
, because
\omega
is ample. So, we may define some subsets of sections

depending on the position of the curve

with respect to the discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}
. For instance, let
\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}
be the subset of
\mathbb{A}
whose sections are not contained in
\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}
, i.e.

\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}:=\{a\in\mathbb{A}\,|a(C)\nsubseteq\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}\}.

The next proposition resumes the main facts about this set.

Proposition 2.3
.

The open subset
\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}
is not-empty. Moreover, if
a\in\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}
, then the cameral cover
C_{a}
is reduced and connected.

The proof of this fact is due to Ngô
[
38
]
under different hypotheses on the line bundle
\omega
. We briefly explain how to adapt the strategy on
loc.cit.
in our situation.

Proof.

By Remark
1.3
, the morphism
\pi_{G}:\mathfrak{t}_{G}\to\mathfrak{c}_{G}
can be re-written

\left(\operatorname{Id},\pi_{\mathscr{D}(G)}\right):\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{Z}(G)}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}\to\mathfrak{t}_{\mathscr{Z}(G)}\oplus\mathfrak{c}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}.

Hence, we may assume
G
semi-simple. The non-emptiness will follow from Proposition
3.3
. The cameral curve is reduced by
[
38
, Lemme 4.5.1]
and connected by
[
38
, Prop. 4.6.1]
. We remark that the canonical bundle
\omega
does not satisfy the hypothesis
\deg(\mathcal{L})\geq 2g
in
loc.cit.
However, the same result, with the exact same proof, still holds if we assume
\mathcal{L}^{n}
very ample for
n=d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
. Since we are assuming
G
semi-simple, we have
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}\geq 2
(see Remark

of a smooth curve of genus at least two is very ample for any

.
∎

2.3.
Regular centralizer.

Here, we define a moduli stack of torsors under an abelian group scheme

on

Definition 2.4
.

Let

-scheme

for some

Remark 2.5
.

The quotient

is a

-torsion sheaf. Furthermore, the condition of being in

always holds for those roots whose corresponding coroots are primitive (i.e. they are injective as cocharacters). In particular, if the derived subgroup of

does not contain the odd special orthogonal group

(note that

) as a direct factor, the groups

Remark 2.6
.

To be honest, the action is defined using a moduli stack of torsors with respect to an another group scheme: the universal centralizer group scheme, see
[

, §2]
. However, these two groups (and so the moduli stacks of torsors under these groups) are isomorphic, see
[

, Proposition 2.4.7]

[

The next proposition shows the relationship between these group-stacks and the Hitchin fibers.

Proposition 2.7
.

Fix

. The substack
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}^{\operatorname*{reg}}(a)\subset\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a)

Proof.

See
[

.
∎

Proposition 2.8

Proposition 2.9

(Ngô-Bezrukavnikov formula)

Fix

\pi_{a}:C_{a}\to C

Theorem 2.12

(Product Formula)

Let

and

be the branch locus of the corresponding cameral cover. We have a homeomorphism of stacks

\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red}}_{p}(a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Gr}_{J_{a,p}}^{\operatorname{red}}}}{{\times}}\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a).

Furthermore, the restriction to the open substacks of regular elements

\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red,reg}}_{p}(a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Gr}_{J_{a,p}}^{\operatorname{red,reg}}}}{{\times}}\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}\subset\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red}}_{p}(a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Gr}_{J_{a,p}}^{\operatorname{red}}}}{{\times}}\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}

Proof.

We sketch the proof as explained in
[

. By gluing along the Hitchin-Kostant section, we get a morphism

\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red}}_{p}(a)\to\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a),

which sends regular elements in regular

Combining the morphisms (

Remark 2.13
.

We warn the reader that, in this paper, the stack of

Following Definition

-twisted

The main result of this subsection is the existence of sections for the

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
G
be an adjoint reductive group and
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
C
. Then, there exists a section
\sigma:\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\to\mathbf{Higgs}_{G,\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
of the Hitchin morphism

, whose image is contained in the regular locus.

Proof.

We follow the same argument contained in the proof of
[
37
, Proposition 2.5]
(for more details about the differences between our proof and the one in
loc.cit.
, see Remark
2.15
below). Recall that the center
\mathscr{Z}(G)
of a reductive group

is the intersection of the kernels of all the roots. In particular, we have
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(G),\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})/\langle\Phi\rangle
, where
\langle\Phi\rangle
denotes the sublattice generated by the roots. In particular, since we assumed
G
adjoint (i.e.
\mathscr{Z}(G)=\{1\}
), the character lattice
\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\langle\Phi\rangle
is generated by the roots. Let
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
be a set of simple roots for
G
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}
be the dual basis in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},T)
with respect to
\Delta
, i.e.
\alpha_{j}\circ\omega_{i}=t^{\delta_{ij}}
. Then the cocharacter
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{i}
acts by conjugation on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
as it follows

(2.9)

\rho(t).x=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in\mathfrak{t};\\
t^{ht(\alpha)}x&\text{if }x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha};\end{cases}

where
ht(\alpha)=\sum m_{i}
, if
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\alpha_{i}
. By
[
37
, Proposition 2.2]
,
the Kostant section
\epsilon:\mathfrak{c}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{reg}}
(defined in Therorem
1.1
(\ref{T:konstant6})
) is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
\mathfrak{c}
with weights
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
and on
\mathfrak{g}
in the following way:
\rho_{+}(t).x:=\rho(t).(tx)
, for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The homomorphism
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\times G:t\mapsto(t,\rho(t))
and the Kostant section
\epsilon
induce a commutative diagram of quotient stacks

\textstyle{[\mathfrak{c}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{reg}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times G]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}

Pulling-back the above diagram, via the morphism

classifying the line bundle

, we get a section
\epsilon_{\mathcal{L}}:\mathfrak{c}_{\mathcal{L}}\to[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathcal{L}}/G]
of the natural morphism

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{crcl}\sigma:&\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{L}}=H^{0}(C,\mathfrak{c}_{\mathcal{L}})&\longrightarrow&Hom_{C}(C,[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathcal{L}}/G])=\mathbf{Higgs}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{G,\mathcal{L}}\\
&C\xrightarrow{a}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathcal{L}}&\mapsto&C\xrightarrow{a}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathcal{L}}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{\mathcal{L}}}[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathcal{L}}/G]\end{array}

∎

Remark 2.15
.

The proof of the existence of the section
\sigma
in Lemma
2.14
is a modification of the proof of
[
37
, Proposition 2.5]
. In
loc.cit.
the author constructs a section for arbitrary reductive groups under the assumption that

(condition not satisfied by

The reason of the assumption is that, for an arbitrary reductive group, the action of

on

, with weights as in (

Taking the square of the action implies that, if we want to define a section of the Hitchin morphism properly, we need the choice of a root for
\mathcal{L}
.

However, if we assume

is a square.

Following the notation of Subsection

, we denote by

.

Remark 2.16

Since the choice of the line bundle

3.
The Hitchin discriminant.

In this section, we study the geometry of the Hitchin fibers with respect to their image in the Hitchin basis
\mathbb{A}
(see Propositions
3.10
and
3.18
). As a consequence, we show that the locus of non-abelian Hitchin fibers is covered by rational curves (see Theorem
3.23
).

To achieve this, we focus our attention on two subsets of
\mathbb{A}
. The first one, denoted by
\mathbb{A}^{\diamondsuit}
, has been already introduced and studied in
[
38
]
. It parameterizes sections, whose corresponding cameral curves are smooth (see Proposition
3.3
). By the results in
loc.cit.
, the Hitchin fibers over
\mathbb{A}^{\diamondsuit}
are always abelian stacks. However, unless
G
has semi-simple rank
1
, there are sections, whose Hitchin fibers are abelian stacks and their cameral curves are singular. For these reasons, we will introduce another open subset, denoted by
\mathbb{A}^{\operatorname{ab}}
, which will parameterize sections, whose corresponding Hitchin fiber is an abelian stack (see Proposition
3.11
).

Unless otherwise stated, in this section,
G
is a reductive group and
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.

3.1.
A characterization of the regular Hitchin fibers.

In this subsection, we present a criterion to check when a Hitchin fiber (over
\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}
) contains non regular
G
-Higgs bundles.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{A}^{\heartsuit}
. The following facts are equivalent

(i)

\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}^{\operatorname*{reg}}(a)=\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a)
.

(ii)

the connected components of the Hitchin fiber
\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a)
are abelian stacks.

(iii)

the dimension of the affine part of the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}
of
J_{a}
-torsors is zero, i.e.

\delta(a)=\dim\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}^{\operatorname{aff}}=0.

(iv)

for any point
p\in C
such that
a(p)\in\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}
, the image of the differential of the section
a:C\to\mathfrak{c}_{\omega}
at the point

\prod_{q\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red}}_{q}(a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod_{p\in B_{a}}\operatorname{Gr}_{J_{a,q}}^{\operatorname{red}}}}{{\times}}\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}\to\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a),

\operatorname{Spr}^{\operatorname{red}}_{p}(a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{Gr}_{J_{a,p}}^{\operatorname{red}}}}{{\times}}\mathbf{Bun}_{J_{a}}\to\mathbf{Higgs}_{G}(a).

We now describe the affine Springer fiber

The following facts hold.

Proposition 4.2
.

The intersection

is irreducible and

is the dual variety of the curve

for the embedding given by the line bundle
\omega^{d_{r}}
.

Proof.

Fix a section
a=(0,0,\ldots,0,a_{r})\in\mathbb{A}_{r}\subset\mathbb{A}

\mathfrak{D}_{\omega}
to
\operatorname{Spec}(k[p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}]/(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r-1}))\subset\mathfrak{c}
is given by the solution of the polynomial

is contained in

if and only if

and
\operatorname{Im}(da_{p})=(0,\ldots,0)
for some

. The last statement is equivalent to the condition that the section

vanishes at

In particular, the projectivization

is exactly the dual variety of the curve

with respect to the linear system

.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Torelli Theorem)
.

Let

and

be two smooth projective curves. If

By hypothesis, there exists an isomorphism

, we have a cartesian diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{M}^{\delta_{2}}_{G}(C_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d\varphi^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{M}^{\delta_{1}}_{G}(C_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}(H^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{M}^{\delta_{2}}_{G}(C_{2}),\mathcal{O}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Spec}(H^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{M}^{\delta_{2}}_{G}(C_{2}),\mathcal{O}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{\mathbb{A}}}

where the compositions of the vertical maps are the Hitchin morphisms. Note that the linearity of

∎

Corollary 4.4

5.
The tautological automorphisms of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
.

The aim of this section is to define the homomorphism of Theorem
A
and proving its injectivity. Unless otherwise stated, in this section,
G
is a reductive group and
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.

Given a
G
-bundle
E
, we may construct some new (possibly isomorphic)
G
-bundles, by using the automorphisms of the curves, the automorphisms of the group and the
\mathscr{Z}(G)
-bundles on
C
. More precisely, given a
G
-bundle
E
:

(a)

if
\sigma:C\cong C
is an isomorphism, the pull-back
\sigma^{*}E
is a
G
-bundle,

(b)

if
\rho:G\cong G
is a group-isomorphism, then we define the
G
-bundle
\rho(E):=E\times^{\rho,G}G
,

(c)

if
Z
is a
\mathscr{Z}(G)
-bundle, the tensorization by
Z
, denoted by
E\otimes Z
, is a
G
-bundle, where
E\otimes Z
is the fibered product
E\times_{C}Z
modulo the equivalence relation
(et,z)\sim(e,zt)
, for any
e\in E
,
z\in Z
and

.

In particular, they define automorphisms for the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
of
G
-bundles. We remark that some of these automorphisms may be trivial, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\rho\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is an inner automorphism (i.e.
\rho\in\operatorname{Inn}(G)
), then
E\cong\rho(E)
for any
G
-bundle.

Proof.

By hypothesis, there exists
h\in G
such that
hgh^{-1}=\rho(g)
for any
g\in G
. Then, the map

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{f}\rho(E):=E\times^{\rho,G}G

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle\mapsto[eh,1]

is a well-defined morphism of
G
-bundles and it gives the desidered isomorphism. Indeed, for any
e\in E
and
g\in G
, we have

f(eg)=[egh,1]=[ehh^{-1}gh,1]=[eh\rho^{-1}(g),1]=[eh,g]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{def}}}{{=}}f(e)g.

∎

In particular, the isomorphism
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}\to\mathbf{Bun}_{G}:E\mapsto\rho(E)
, induced by an automorphism
\rho\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, depends only on the class
[\rho]
in the quotient
\operatorname{Out}(G):=\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G)
. With abuse of notation, we denote by the same symbol
\rho
an automorphism
\rho
of
G
and its equivalence class
[\rho]
in
\operatorname{Out}(G)
. Next, we want to show that all these automorphisms of
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
actually descend to automorphisms of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
of semistable bundles.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
E
be a simple, resp. stable, resp. semistable,
G
-bundle. Then the following
G
-bundles are simple, resp. stable, resp. semistable.

(i)

\sigma^{*}(E)
, where
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(C)
,

(ii)

\rho(E)
, where
\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G)
,

(iii)

E\mapsto E\otimes Z
, where
Z
is a
\mathscr{Z}(G)
-bundle over
C
.

Proof.

We recall that the operations above define automorphisms of the moduli stack
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}
. In particular, all the bundles in the list have automorphism groups equal to
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
. Hence, if
E
simple, the same holds for the other bundles in the list. We now focus on the (semi)stable case. Fix a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
.

(\ref{L:sss1})
The
P
-reductions of
E
are in bijection with the
P
-reductions of
\sigma^{*}E
. Indeed,
F
is a
P
-reduction of
E
if and only if
\sigma^{*}F
is a
P
-reduction of
\sigma^{*}E
. Furthermore, the pull-back along
\sigma
commutes with the formation of the adjoint bundles and preserves the degree, i.e.

\deg\left(F\times^{P}\mathfrak{p}\right)=\deg\left(\sigma^{*}F\times^{P}\mathfrak{p}\right).

Putting all together, we get that
E
is (semi)stable if and only if
\sigma^{*}E
is (semi)stable.

(\ref{L:sss2})
Since
\rho
is an isomorphism, it induces a bijection between the parabolic subgroups of
G
, i.e.
P
is parabolic if and only if
\rho(P)
is parabolic. Moreover, similarly to the previous point, the
P
-reductions of
E
are in bijection with the
\rho(P)
-reductions of
\rho(E)
. Indeed,
F
is a
P
-reduction of
E
if and only if
\rho(F):=F\times^{\rho,P}\rho(P)
is a
\rho(P)
-reduction of
\rho(E)
. Let
d\rho:\mathfrak{p}\to d\rho(\mathfrak{p})
be the isomorphism of Lie algebras given by
\rho:P\to\rho(P)
. Then, the homomorphism of vector bundles

F\times^{P}\mathfrak{p}\to\rho(F)\times^{\rho(P)}d\rho(\mathfrak{p})=F\times^{\rho,P}d\rho(\mathfrak{p}):[(f,x)]\mapsto[(f,d\rho(x))]

is an isomorphism and, so, they have the same degree. Hence
E
is (semi)stable if and only if
\rho(E)
is (semi)stable.

(\ref{L:sss3})
As in the point
(i)
, the
P
-reductions of
E
are in bijection with the
P
-reductions of
E\otimes Z
. Indeed,
F
is a
P
-reduction of
E
if and only if
F\otimes Z
is a
P
-reduction of
E\otimes Z
. Furthermore, the adjoint bundles

F\times^{P}\mathfrak{p}\cong(F/\mathscr{Z}(G))\times^{P/\mathscr{Z}(G)}\mathfrak{p}\cong(F\otimes Z)\times^{P}\mathfrak{p}

are isomorphic and so they have the same degree. Hence,
E
is (semi)stable if and only if
E\otimes Z
is (semi)stable.
∎

By Lemma
5.2
, all these automorphisms preserve the locus
\mathbf{Bun}_{G}^{(s)s}
of (semi)stable bundles. By the universal property of the good moduli spaces, the automorphisms descend to the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
of semistable
G
-bundles. Putting together all the results, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

We have a homomorphism of groups

\begin{array}[]{cll}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))\rtimes\left(\operatorname{Aut}(C)\times\operatorname{Out}(G)\right)&\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\right)\\
(Z,\sigma,\rho)&\longmapsto\left(E\mapsto\rho((\sigma^{-1})^{*}E)\otimes Z\right)\end{array}

where:

(i)

\operatorname{Aut}(C)
acts on
H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))
by pull-back, i.e.
Z\mapsto(\sigma^{-1})^{*}Z
,

(ii)

\operatorname{Out}(G)
acts on
H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))
as follows
\rho.Z=Z\times^{\rho,\mathscr{Z}(G)}\mathscr{Z}(G)
.

The same holds if we replace
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
with the locus
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of simple and stable
G
-bundles.

We remark that these moduli spaces are disconnected, if

is not simply-connected. So, a priori, these automorphisms may move the connected components of the moduli space

. We are interested in those automorphisms, which fix these components. Before studying them, we need to fix some notations. Any element in
\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\pi_{1}(G)&\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}(\rho)}&\pi_{1}(G)\\

On the other hand, the group

is a (possibly disconnected) multiplicative group. It is isomorphic to

Fix a maximal torus

Lemma 7.9

), then it must be trivial. In particular, two

differs by an element of

G

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(C,\mathscr{Z}(G))\to H^{1}(C,G)\to H^{1}(C,G^{\operatorname{ad}})

In particular, if the set of

The action of

8.
The automorphism group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
: an explicit description.

In this section, using Theorem
A
, we provide the explicit presentations of the automorphism group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}
contained in Corollary
B
. The key point is the explicit computation of the following objects:

(a)

the character group
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(G),\mathbb{G}_{m})
of the center,

(b)

the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G)
,

(c)

the outer automorphism group
\operatorname{Out}(G)
,

(d)

the action of
\operatorname{Out}(G)
on
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(G),\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
\pi_{1}(G)
.

We will complete this task in the next subsections. Our references are
[
25
]
for (a), (b) and
[
17
]
for (c), (d). We now briefly explain the strategy.

The group
\operatorname{Out}(G)
, when
G
is simply-connected and almost-simple, is equal to the automorphism group of its Dynkin diagram, which is well-known. Roughly speaking, each element in
\operatorname{Out}(G)
admits a representative
\rho\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, which fixes a Borel subgroup
B
, its maximal torus and an additional structure given by a certain kind of basis of the nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
B
(the reductive groups together with these data are called
pinned reductive groups
).

Because of these properties, the isomorphism
\rho
induces an isomorphism on the (co)character lattices of the maximal torus and it preserves the set of roots, coroots and simple roots. Hence, it induces an automorphism of the (based) root data and also an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram. Then, we use the description in
[
17
]
of what these automorphisms do on the root data, for describing the action of
\rho
on the
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(G),\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
\pi_{1}(G)
.

More in general, if
G
is an almost-simple group, it admits an isogeny
G^{\operatorname{sc}}\to G
, with kernel
\mu
, from the (unique) simply-connected, almost-simple group of the same type. Furthermore, the isogeny gives the following identification of groups

(8.1)

\operatorname{Out}(G)=\{\rho\in\operatorname{Out}(G^{\operatorname{sc}})|\rho(\mu)\subset\mu\}\subset\operatorname{Out}(G^{\operatorname{sc}}).

In particular,
\operatorname{Out}(G^{\operatorname*{ad}})=\operatorname{Out}(G^{\operatorname{sc}})
. Hence, knowing the action of
\operatorname{Out}(G^{\operatorname{sc}})
on the center of
G^{\operatorname{sc}}
will give the description of
\operatorname{Out}(G)
. On the other hand, knowing the action of
\operatorname{Out}(G)
on
\pi_{1}(G)
will give the description of
\operatorname{Out}(G,\delta)
for any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
.

In this section,
G
is an almost-simple group and
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 4
. For any positive integer

, we denote by
\operatorname{Pic}(C)[l]
the group of

. Note that, there exists an isomorphism of groups
\operatorname{Pic}(C)[l]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z})^{2g}
.

The unique simply-connected and almost-simple group of type

This case is the most delicate. Let us first recall some properties of the root system

We will freely identify

The root (resp. coroot) lattices and the weight (resp. coweight) lattices of

We set

The group

is equal to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}&=&\{0,\epsilon_{1},w_{n},w_{n-1}|\,2\epsilon_{1}=0,\,2w_{n}=\epsilon_{1},\,3w_{n}=w_{n-1}\},&\text{if } n\>\text{ is odd},\\

An almost-simple group
G

is isomorphic to either the (simply-connected) spin group

, or the orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
, or the (adjoint) projective orthogonal group

where

8.4.
Types
E_{6}

Let us first recall some properties of the root system
E_{6}
. Consider the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{8}
endowed with the standard scalar product
(-,-)
and with the canonical bases
\{\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{8}\}
.
Inside
\mathbb{R}^{8}
, we will consider the following subvector space

\displaystyle V(E_{6}):=\{\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{8})\in\mathbb{R}^{8}\>:\xi_{8}=-\xi_{6},\xi_{7}=\xi_{6}\}.

We will freely identify
V(E_{6})
with its dual vector spaces by mean of the (restriction of the) standard scalar product
(-,-)
.
The root (resp. coroot) lattices and the weight (resp. coweight) lattices of
E_{6}
are given by

(8.4)

\displaystyle Q(E_{6})=Q(E_{6}^{\vee})=\left(\{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}\>:(\xi,\xi)\>\text{ is even}\} +\left\langle\frac{\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}}{2}\right\rangle\right)\cap V(E_{6})\subset

\displaystyle\subset P(E_{6})=P(E_{6}^{\vee})=Q(E_{6})+\left\langle w_{1}:=\frac{2}{3}(\epsilon_{8}-\epsilon_{7}-\epsilon_{6})\right\rangle.

In particular, the group
P(E_{6})/Q(E_{6})
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\cong\langle w_{1}\rangle
. An explicit basis of the lattices
Q(E_{6})
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{5}-\epsilon_{6}-\epsilon_{7}+\epsilon_{8}}{2},\alpha_{2}=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2},

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}=\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1},\alpha_{4}=\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{2},\alpha_{5}=\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{3},\alpha_{6}=\epsilon_{5}-\epsilon_{4}.

An almost-simple group of type
E_{6}
is either the simply-connected group
\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}}
or the adjoint group
\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname*{ad}}
. The character lattice
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
is canonically isomorphic to
P(E_{6})/Q(E_{6})
. By
[
17
, Chap.VI, §4.12]
, the group
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}})
is cyclic of order two. A generator is given by an automorphism of
\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}}
, which acts on the lattice
Q(E_{6}^{\vee})
as
-\gamma
, where
\gamma
is the (unique) element in the Weyl group
W_{\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{sc}}_{6}}
which transforms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5},\alpha_{6},\alpha_{2}
to
-\alpha_{6},-\alpha_{5},-\alpha_{4},-\alpha_{3},-\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2}
, respectively. A direct computation shows that
-\gamma
sends
w_{1}\operatorname{mod}Q(E_{6})
to
-w_{1}\operatorname{mod}Q(E_{6})
. From
[
25
, Section 7.4]
, we deduce the following table.

{\small\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\mathfrak{g}&G&\operatorname{Out}(G)&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{Z}(G),\mathbb{G}_{m})&\pi_{1}(G)\\
\hline\cr E_{6}&\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}}&\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}=\langle w_{1}\rangle&\{0\}\\
&\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname*{ad}}&\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}&\{0\}&\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}=\langle w_{1}\rangle\\
\hline\cr\end{array}}

We remark that
\operatorname{Out}(G)
acts on the non-trivial groups by taking the inverse. Putting all together, we have proved the following result.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
G
be an almost-simple group of type
E_{6}
. The isomorphism of Theorem
A
gives the following equalities

\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}^{\delta}(C))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\operatorname{Pic}(C)[3]\rtimes\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\operatorname{Aut}(C)\right),&\text{ if }G=\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{sc}}&\text{ and }\delta\in\{0\},\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\operatorname{Aut}(C),&\text{ if }G=\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{ad}}&\text{ and }\delta=0\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},\\
\operatorname{Aut}(C),&\text{ if }G=\mathbb{E}_{6}^{\operatorname{ad}}&\text{ and }\delta\neq 0\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},\end{array}\right.

where
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
acts on
\operatorname{Pic}(C)[3]
by pull-back and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts on
\operatorname{Pic}(C)[3]
by taking the dual, i.e.
[1].L=L^{-1}=L^{2}
. The same holds for the locus
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of simple and stable bundles.

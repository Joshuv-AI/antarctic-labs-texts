Numerical solution of a nonlinear functional integro-differential equation
Dang Quang A, Pham Huy Dien, Dang Quang Long

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a boundary value problem (BVP) for a fourth order nonlinear functional integro-differential equation. We establish the existence and uniqueness of solution and construct a numerical method for solving it. We prove that the method is of second order accuracy and obtain an estimate for the total error. Some examples demonstrate the validity of the obtained theoretical results and the efficiency of the numerical method.



Keywords:
Nonlinear functional integro-differential equation; Existence and uniqueness of solution; Iterative method; Total error.

34B15, 65L10

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following problem for functional integro-differential equation (FIDE)

\begin{split}u^{(4)}(x)&=f(x,u(x),u(\varphi(x)),\int_{0}^{1}k_{0}(x,t)u(t)dt,\int_{0}^{1}k_{1}(x,t)u(\varphi(t))dt),\\
u(0)&=0,\;u(1)=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(1)=0,\end{split}

(1)

where the function
f(x,u,y,v,z),k_{0}(x,t),k_{1}(x,t)
and
\varphi(t)
are assumed to be continuous,
\varphi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
.
This is a natural combination or hybridization of the following problems:

Problem 1:
The problem for functional differential equation

\begin{split}x^{(2p)}(t)=f(t,x(t),x(\varphi(t))),\quad t\in[a,b],\\
x^{(i)}(a)=a_{i},\;x^{(i)}(b)=b_{i},\quad i=\overline{0,p-1}\end{split}

where
\varphi:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\;a\leq\varphi(t)\leq b,\forall t\in[a,b]
and
f(t,x,y)
are continuous functions.

Problem 2:
The problem for integro-differential equation

\begin{split}y^{(4)}(x)&=f(x,y(x),\int_{0}^{1}k(x,t)y(t)dt),\;0<x<1,\\
y(0)&=0,\;y(1)=0,\;y^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,\;y^{\prime\prime}(1)=0,\end{split}

where the functions
f(x,y,v),k(x,t)
are assumed to be continuous.

Problem 1 was considered in the work of Bica et al.
[
2
]
. For solving this problem, the authors constructed successive approximations for the equivalent integral equation with the use of cubic spline interpolation at each iterative step. The error estimate was obtained under strong conditions including the satisfaction of Lipschitz conditions of the function

in the unbounded domain
[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Although in
[
2

the method is constructed for the general function
\varphi(t)
but in all numerical examples only the particular case
\varphi(t)=\alpha t
was considered and the conditions of convergence were not verified. It is a regret that in all examples the Lipschitz condition for the function
f(x,y,v)
is not satisfied. To overcome the limitations of Bica, very recently in
[
10
]
Dang and Dang proposed a new approach for the third order FDE. In this work the existence and uniqueness of solution and the error estimate of the approximate solution obtained by a discrete iterative method are established under easily verified conditions. This approach is applicable to FDE of any order.

It should be said that functional differential equations (FDE) have numerous applications in engineering and sciences

2
Existence results

To investigate the problem (
1
) for brevity we
denote by
\mathcal{K}_{0},\mathcal{K}_{1}
the integral operators defined by

(\mathcal{K}_{0}u)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}k_{0}(x,t)u(t)dt,\;(\mathcal{K}_{1}u)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}k_{1}(x,t)u(\varphi(t))dt

and
set

\psi(x)=f(x,u(x),u(\varphi(x)),(\mathcal{K}_{0}u)(x),(\mathcal{K}_{1}u)(x)).

(2)

Then the problem becomes

\begin{split}u^{(4)}(x)&=\psi(x),\\
u(0)=0,\;u(1)&=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(1)=0.\end{split}

(3)

It has a solution presented in the form

u(x)=\int_{0}^{1}G(x,s)\psi(s)ds,\quad 0<x<1,

(4)

where
G(x,s)
is the Green function of the operator
u^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)=0
associated with the homogeneous boundary conditions
u(0)=u^{\prime\prime}(0)=u(1)=u^{\prime\prime}(1)=0
,

\displaystyle G(x,s)=\frac{1}{6}\begin{cases}s(x-1)(x^{2}-x+s^{2}),\quad&0\leq s\leq x\leq 1\\
x(s-1)(s^{2}-s+x^{2}),&0\leq x\leq s\leq 1.\end{cases}

(5)

Notice that
G(x,s)\geq 0
for all
(x,s)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
.

It is obvious that the solution (
4
) depending on
\psi
must satisfy the relations (
2
). We shall express this by an operator equation for
\psi
. For this purpose, in the space
C[0,1]
introduce the operator
A
by the formula

(A\psi)(x)=f(x,u(x),u(\varphi(x)),(\mathcal{K}_{0}u)(x),(\mathcal{K}_{1}u)(x)),

(6)

where
u(x)
is the solution of the boundary value problem (
3
).

It is easy to verify the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

If the function
\psi
is a fixed point of the operator
A
, i.e.,
\psi
is the solution of the operator equation

A\psi=\psi,

(7)

where
A
is defined by (
6
),
then the function
u(x)
determined from the BVP (
3
) is a solution of the BVP (
1
). Conversely, if the function
u(x)
is the solution of the BVP (
1
) then the function
\psi(x)
determined from (
2
)
satisfies the operator equation (
7
).

Due to the above lemma we shall study the original BVP (
1
) via the operator equation (
6
).

Set

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{0}&=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|G(x,s)|ds,\\
K_{0}&=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|k_{0}(x,s)|ds,\\
K_{1}&=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|k_{1}(x,s)|ds\end{split}

(8)

It is easy to obtain

M_{0}=\frac{5}{384}.

(9)

Now for any positive number
M
, we define the domain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{M}=\{(x,u,y,v,z)\;|\;&0\leq x\leq 1,\;|u|\leq M_{0}M,\\
&|y|\leq M_{0}M,\;|v|\leq M_{0}K_{0}M,\;|z|\leq M_{0}K_{1}M\}.\end{split}

(10)

As usual, we denote by
B[0,M]
the closed ball centered at
0
with radius
M
in the space
C[0,1]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle B[0,M]=\{u\in C[0,1]\;|\;\|u\|\leq M\},

where
\|u\|=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}|u(x)|
.

Theorem 2.2
(Existence and uniqueness)

Suppose that the functions
k_{0}(x,t)
,
k_{1}(x,t)
are continuous in the square
[0,1]\times[0,1]
, the function
\varphi(t)
is continuous and maps
[0,1]
to
[0,1]
, and there exist numbers
M>0
,
L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\geq 0
such that:

(i)

The function
f(x,u,y,v,z)
is continuous in the domain
\mathcal{D}_{M}
and

|f(x,u,y,v,z)|\leq M
,
\forall(x,u,y,v,z)\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
.

(ii)

|f(x_{2},u_{2},y_{2},v_{2},z_{2})-f(x_{1},u_{1},y_{1},v_{1},z_{1})|\leq L_{0}|u_{2}-u_{1}|+L_{1}|y_{2}-y_{1}|+L_{2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|+|L_{3}|z_{2}-z_{1}|,\;\forall(x_{i},u_{i},y_{i},v_{i},z_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{M},\;i=1,2.

(iii)

q=(L_{0}+L_{1}+L_{2}K_{0}+L_{3}K_{1})M_{0}<1
.

Then the problem (
1
) has a unique solution
u\in C^{4}[0,1]
satisfying
|u(x)|\leq M_{0}M
for any
0\leq x\leq 1.

Proof.

Under the assumptions of the theorem we shall prove that the operator
A
is a contraction mapping in the closed ball
B[O,M]
. Then the operator equation (
7
) has a unique solution
u\in C^{(4)}[0,1]
and this implies the existence and uniqueness of solution of the BVP (
1
).

Indeed, take
\psi\in B[O,M]
. Then the problem (
3
) has a unique solution of the form (
4
). From there and (
8
) we obtain
|u(x)|\leq M_{0}\|\psi\|
for all
x\in[0,1]
.
Further, from the last equation in (
8
) we have the estimate
|(\mathcal{K}_{i}u)(x)|\leq M_{0}K_{i}\|\psi\|
,
x\in[0,1]
. Thus, if
\psi\in B[O,M]
, i.e.,
\|\psi\|\leq M
then for any
x\in[0,1]
we have

|u(x)|\leq M_{0}M,\;|(\mathcal{K}_{i}u)(x)|\leq M_{0}K_{i}M\;(i=0,1).

Therefore,
(x,u(x),u(\varphi(x)),(\mathcal{K}_{0}u)(x)),(\mathcal{K}_{1}u)(x))\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
. By the assumption
(i)
there is

|f(x,u(x),u(\varphi(x)),\mathcal{K}_{0}u(x)),\mathcal{K}_{1}u(x)|\leq M\quad\forall x\in[0,1].

Hence,
|(A\psi)(x)|\leq M,\;\forall x\in[0,1]
and
\|A\psi\|\leq M
. It means that
A
maps
B[O,M]
into itself.

Next, take
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in B[O,M]
. Using the assumption
(ii)
and
(iii)
it is easy to obtain

\displaystyle\|A\psi_{2}-A\psi_{1}\|\leq((L_{0}+L_{1}+L_{2}K_{0}+L_{3}K_{1})M_{0})\|\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\|=q\|\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\|.

Since
q<1
the operator
A
is a contraction map in
B[O,M]
. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Now, in order to study positive solutions of the BVP (
1
) we introduce the domain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{M}^{+}=\{(x,u,y,v,z)\;|\;&0\leq x\leq 1,\;0\leq u\leq M_{0}M,\;|y|\leq M_{0}M,\;\\
&|v|\leq M_{0}K_{1}M,\;|z|\leq M_{0}K_{2}M\}.\end{split}

(11)

and denote

S_{M}=\{\psi\in C[0,1],0\leq\psi(x)\leq M\}.

Theorem 2.3
(Positivity of solution)

If in Theorem
2.2
the condition
(i)
is replaced by the condition

(i^{\prime}):\;0\leq f(x,u,y,v,z)\leq M,\;\forall(x,u,y,v,z)\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{+}
and
f(x,0,0,0,0)\not\equiv 0
,

and in the condition
(ii)
replace
\mathcal{D}_{M}
by
\mathcal{D}_{M}^{+}
,
then the problem (
1
) has a unique positive solution
u\in C^{4}[0,1]
satisfying
0\leq u(x)\leq M_{0}M
for any
0\leq x\leq 1.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
2.2
, where instead of
\mathcal{D}_{M}
and
B[O;M]

there stand
\mathcal{D}_{M}^{+}
and
S_{M}
, we conclude that the problem has a nonnegative solution. Due to the condition
f(x,0,0,0,0)\not\equiv 0
, this solution must be positive.

3
Numerical method

In this section we suppose that all the conditions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied. Then the problem (
1
) has a unique solution. For finding this solution consider the following iterative method:

1.

Given

\psi_{0}(x)=f(x,0,0,0,0).

(12)

2.

Knowing
\psi_{m}(x)

(m=0,1,...)
compute

\begin{split}u_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}G(x,t)\psi_{m}(t)dt,\\
y_{m}(x)&:=u_{m}(\varphi(x))=\int_{0}^{1}G(\varphi(x),t)\psi_{m}(t)dt,\\
v_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}k_{0}(x,t)u_{m}(t)dt,\\
z_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}k_{1}(x,t)y_{m}(t)dt,\\
\end{split}

(13)

3.

Update

\begin{split}\psi_{m+1}(x)&=f(x,u_{m}(x),y_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x)).\end{split}

(14)

This iterative method indeed is the successive iterative method for finding the fixed point of the operator
A
. Therefore, it converges with the rate of geometric progression and there holds the estimate

\|\psi_{m}-\psi\|\leq\frac{q^{m}}{1-q}\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{0}\|=p_{m}d,

where
\psi
is the fixed point of the operator
A
and

p_{m}=\tfrac{q^{m}}{1-q},\;d=\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{0}\|.

(15)

This estimate implies the following result of the convergence of the iterative method (
12
)-(
14
).

Theorem 3.1

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.2
the iterative method (
12
)-(
14
) converges and for the approximate solution
u_{m}(t)
there hold estimates

\displaystyle\|u_{m}-u\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}p_{m}d,

where
u
is the exact solution of the problem (
1
),
p_{m}
and
d
are defined by (
15
).

To numerically realize the above iterative method we construct a corresponding discrete iterative method. For this purpose cover the interval
[0,1]
by the uniform grid
\bar{\omega}_{h}=\{x_{i}=ih,\;h=1/N,i=0,1,...,N\}
and denote by
\Psi_{m}(x),U_{m}(x),Y_{m}(x),V_{m}(x),Z_{m}(x)
the grid functions, which are defined on the grid
\bar{\omega}_{h}
and approximate the functions
\psi(x),u_{m}(x),y_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x)
on this grid.

Consider now the following discrete iterative method:

1.

Given

\Psi_{0}(x_{i})=f(x_{i},0,0,0,0),\ i=0,...,N.

(16)

2.

Knowing
\Psi_{m}(x_{i}),\;m=0,1,...;\;i=0,...,N,
compute approximately the definite integrals (
13
) by the trapezium formulas

\begin{split}U_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j})\Psi_{m}(x_{j}),\\
Y_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(\varphi(x_{i}),x_{j})\Psi_{m}(x_{j}),\\
V_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{0}(x_{i},x_{j})U_{m}(x_{j}),\\
Z_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{1}(x_{i},x_{j})Y_{m}(x_{j}),\;i=0,...,N,\end{split}

(17)

where
\rho_{j}
is the weight of the trapezium formula, namely

\rho_{j}=\begin{cases}1/2,\;j=0,N\\
1,\;j=1,2,...,N-1.\end{cases}

3.

Update

\Psi_{m+1}(x_{i})=f(x_{i},U_{m}(x_{i}),Y_{m}(x_{i}),V_{m}(x_{i}),Z_{m}(x_{i})).

(18)

Remark 1.

In the case if the boundary conditions in the problem (
1
) are non-homogeneous, namely, if

u(0)=c_{1},\;u(1)=c_{2},\;u^{\prime\prime}(0)=c_{3},\;u^{\prime\prime}(1)=c_{4},

then the solution of the equation
u^{(4)}(x)=\psi(x)
satisfying these boundary conditions has the form

u(x)=\int_{0}^{1}G(x,s)\psi(s)ds+p(x),\quad 0<x<1.

(19)

Here

p(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}

(20)

with

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{1},\;a_{1}=-c_{1}+c_{2}-\frac{1}{3}c_{3}-\frac{1}{6}c_{4},

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}c_{3},\;a_{3}=\frac{1}{6}c_{4}-\frac{1}{6}c_{3}.

Then the formulas in (
17
) have the form

\begin{split}U_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j})\Psi_{m}(x_{j})+p(x_{i}),\\
Y_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(\varphi(x_{i}),x_{j})\Psi_{m}(x_{j})+p(\varphi(x_{i})),\\
V_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{0}(x_{i},x_{j})U_{m}(x_{j}),\\
Z_{m}(x_{i})&=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{1}(x_{i},x_{j})Y_{m}(x_{j}),\;i=0,...,N.\end{split}

(21)

In order to get the error estimates for the approximate solution of the problem (
1
) and its derivatives on the grid we need some following auxiliary results.

Proposition 3.2

Assume that the function
f(t,u,y,v,z)
has all continuous partial derivatives up to second order in the domain
\mathcal{D}_{M}
, the kernel functions
k_{0}(x,t),k_{1}(x,t)
have all continuous partial derivatives up to second order in the square
[0,1]\times[0,1]
and the function
\varphi(t)
has continuous derivatives up to second order. Then for the functions
\psi_{m}(x),u_{m}(x),y_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x),m=0,1,...,
constructed by the iterative method (
12
)-(
14
) we have

\psi_{m}(x)\in C^{2}[0,1]
,
u_{m}(x)\in C^{6}[0,1]
,
y_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x)\in C^{2}[0,1]
.

This proposition is obvious.

Proposition 3.3

For any function
\psi(x)\in C^{2}[0,1]
there holds the estimate

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}G(x_{i},t)\psi(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},t_{j})\psi(t_{j})+O(h^{2}),\\
&\int_{0}^{1}G(\varphi(x_{i}),t)\psi(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(\varphi(x_{i}),t_{j})\psi(t_{j})+O(h^{2}).\end{split}

(22)

Proof.

The above estimates are obvious in view of the error estimate of the compound trapezium formula.

Proposition 3.4

Under the assumptions of Proposition
3.2
, for any
m=0,1,...
there hold the estimates

\|\Psi_{m}-\psi_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|U_{m}-u_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|Y_{m}-y_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),

(23)

\begin{split}\|V_{m}-v_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|Z_{m}-z_{m}\|=O(h^{2}).\end{split}

(24)

where
\|.\|=\|.\|_{\bar{\omega}_{h}}
is the max-norm of function on the grid
\bar{\omega}_{h}
.

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction. For
m=0
we have immediately
\|\Psi_{0}-\psi_{0}\|=0
. Next, by the first equation in (
13
) and Proposition
3.3
we have

u_{0}(x_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}G(x_{i},t)\psi_{0}(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},t_{j})\psi_{0}(t_{j})+O(h^{2})

(25)

for any
i=0,...,N
.
On the other hand, in view of the first equation in (
17
) we have

U_{0}(x_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})\Psi_{0}(t_{j}).

(26)

Therefore,
|U_{0}(t_{i})-u_{0}(t_{i})|=O(h^{2})
because
\Psi_{0}(t_{j})=\psi_{0}(t_{j})=f(t_{j},0,0,0,0)
. Consequently,
\|U_{0}-u_{0}\|=O(h^{2})
.

Similarly, we have

\|Y_{0}-y_{0}\|=O(h^{2}).

(27)

Next, by the trapezium formula we have

\displaystyle v_{0}(x_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}k_{0}(x_{i},t)u_{0}(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})u_{0}(t_{j})+O(h^{2}),

while by the third equation in (
17
) we have

\displaystyle V_{0}(x_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})U_{0}(t_{j}),\;i=0,...,N.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big|V_{0}(x_{i})-v_{0}(x_{i})\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})(U_{0}(t_{j})-u_{0}(t_{j}))\Big|+O(h^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}|k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})||U_{0}(t_{j})-u_{0}(t_{j})|+O(h^{2})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}|k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})|+O(h^{2})

\displaystyle\leq CC_{1}h^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}+O(h^{2})=O(h^{2})

because
|U_{0}(t_{j})-u_{0}(t_{j})|\leq Ch^{2},\;|k_{0}(x_{i},t_{j})|\leq C_{1}
, where
C,C_{1}
are some constants. Thus,
\|V_{0}-v_{0}\|=O(h^{2})
.
Analogously, we have
\|Z_{0}-z_{0}\|=O(h^{2})
.

Now suppose that (
23
) and (
24
) are valid for
m\geq 0
. We shall show that these estimates are valid for
m+1
.
By the Lipschitz condition of the function
f
and the estimates (

) and (

) it is easy to obtain the estimate

\|\Psi_{m+1}-\psi_{m+1}\|=O(h^{2}).

Now from the first equation in (
13
) by Proposition
3.3
we have

u_{m+1}(x_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}G(x_{i},t)\psi_{m+1}(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j})\psi_{m+1}(x_{j})+O(h^{2}).

On the other hand by the first formula in (

) we have

U_{m+1}(x_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j})\Psi_{m+1}(x_{j}).

From this equality and the above estimates we obtain the estimate

\|U_{m+1}-u_{m+1}\|=O(h^{2}).

Similarly, we obtain

\|Y_{m+1}-y_{m+1}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|V_{m+1}-v_{m+1}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|Z_{m+1}-z_{k+1}\|=O(h^{2}).

Thus, by induction we have proved the proposition.
Now combining Proposition
3.4
and Theorem
3.1

Theorem 3.5

Assume that all the conditions of Theorem

(28)

Proof.

The first above estimate is easily obtained if representing

Example 1.
Consider the problem

\begin{split}u^{(4)}(x)&=\pi^{4}\sin(\pi x)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\pi x)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(\frac{x}{2})\\
&-\frac{8}{3\pi}\int_{0}^{1}e^{x}\sin(\pi t)u(t)dt+\int_{0}^{1}e^{x}\sin(\pi t)u(\frac{t}{2})dt,\\
u(0)&=0,\;u(1)=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,\;u^{\prime\prime}(1)=0.\end{split}

(29)

In this problem

\displaystyle k_{0}(x,t)=k_{1}(x,t)=e^{x}\sin(\pi t),\;\varphi(t)=\frac{t}{2},

\displaystyle f(x,u,y,v,z)=\pi^{4}\sin(\pi x)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\pi x)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}-\frac{8}{3\pi}v+z.

K_{0}=K_{1}=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|k_{0}(x,s)|ds=\frac{2e}{\pi}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{M}=\{(x,u,y,v,z)\;|\;

\displaystyle 0\leq x\leq 1,\;|u|\leq M_{0}M,\;|y|\leq M_{0}M,\;

\displaystyle|f(x,u,y,v,z)|\leq\pi^{4}+1+M_{0}^{2}M^{2}+(\frac{8}{3\pi}+1)\frac{2e}{\pi}M_{0}M.

2.2

.
Therefore, the problem has a unique solution

satisfying the estimates
|u(x)|\leq 1.3672

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have established the existence, uniqueness and positivity of the solution for a fourth order nonlinear functional integro-differential equation with the Navier boundary conditions and proposed an iterative method at both continuous and discrete levels for finding the solution. The second order of accuracy of the discrete method has been proved. Some examples, where the exact solution is known and is not known, demonstrate the validity of the obtained theoretical results and the efficiency of the iterative method.

The method used in this paper with appropriate modifications can be applied to nonlinear functional integro-differential equations of any order with other boundary conditions and more complicated nonlinear terms. This is the direction of our research in the future.

Analytical aspects of q , r q,r -analogue of poly-Stirling numbers of both kinds
Takao Komatsu

Abstract
The Stirling numbers of type B B of the second kind count signed set partitions. In this paper we provide new combinatorial and analytical identities regarding these numbers as well as Broder’s r r -version of these numbers. Among these identities one can find recursions, explicit formulas based on the inclusion-exclusion principle, and also exponential generating functions. These Stirling numbers can be considered as members of a wider family of triangles of numbers that are characterized using results of Comtet and Lancaster. We generalize these theorems, which present equivalent conditions for a triangle of numbers to be a triangle of generalized Stirling numbers, to the case of the q , r q,r -poly Stirling numbers, which are q q -analogues of the restricted Stirling numbers defined by Broder and having a polynomial value appearing in their defining recursion. There are two ways to do this and these ways are related by a nice identity.

Introduction

The
Stirling number of the second kind
, denoted
S_{n,k}
,
counts the number of partitions of the set
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
into
k
non-empty subsets (see Stanley
[
29
, page 81]
).
Stirling numbers of the second kind arise in a variety of problems in enumerative combinatorics;
they have many combinatorial interpretations and have been generalized in various contexts and in different ways.

The Stirling numbers have also a vast algebraic background and the following recursion can be considered as the link from the combinatorial point of view to its algebraic one:

S_{n,k}=S_{n-1,k-1}+kS_{n-1,k}.

(1)

1.1
The Comtet and Lancaster approaches to Stirling numbers

Comtet
[
5
]
showed that the Stirling numbers of the second kind can be presented
in several equivalent algebraic ways. We provide here the content of Comtet’s theorem, following the presentation
of Wagner
[
31
, Section 7.2, Theorem 7.2.1]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of complex numbers.
The following are equivalent characterizations for an array of numbers
(A_{n,k})_{n,k\geq 0}
:

(1)

Defining equation:
For each
n\geq 0
:

x^{n}=\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}A_{n,k}\cdot(x-b_{0})(x-b_{1})\cdots(x-b_{k-1}).

(2)

Recursion:
For each
n\geq k>0
:

A_{n,k}=A_{n-1,k-1}+b_{k}A_{n-1,k}

with the boundary conditions:

A_{n,0}=b_{0}^{n}
and
A_{0,k}=\delta_{0k}
for each
n\geq k\geq 0
.

(3)

Complete recursion:
For
n\geq k>0
:

A_{n,k}=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}A_{j-1,k-1}b_{k}^{n-j},

subject to the same boundary conditions as in Condition (2).

(4)

Ordinary generating function:
For each
k\geq 0
:

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}A_{n,k}x^{n}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-b_{0}x)\cdots(1-b_{k}x)}.

(5)

Explicit formula:
For each
n\geq k\geq 0
:

A_{n,k}=\sum\limits_{d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=n-k\atop d_{i}\geq 0}b_{0}^{d_{0}}\cdots b_{k}^{d_{k}}.

Note that if
b_{k}=1
for all
k
, the numbers
A_{n,k}
are the binomial coefficients
n\choose k
, and if
b_{k}=k
for all
k
, then the numbers
A_{n,k}
are the ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind.

The
(unsigned) Stirling number of the first kind
, denoted
c_{n,k}
, counts the number of permutations of the set
[n]
having
k
cycles (see Stanley
[
29
, page 32]
).
The recursion satisfied by these numbers is:

c_{n,k}=c_{n-1,k-1}+(n-1)c_{n-1,k}.

(2)

The
(signed) Stirling number of the first kind
, denoted
s_{n,k}
, is defined by the recursion:

s_{n,k}=s_{n-1,k-1}-(n-1)s_{n-1,k},

(3)

and they satisfy some orthogonality relations with the Stirling numbers of the second kind (see e.g.
[
11
, p. 264]
).

Back to the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, an analogue of Comtet’s theorem for these numbers was given by Lancaster
[
16
]
, see also
[
17
]
and Wagner’s book
[
31
, Section 7.2]
(we fix
b_{i}=0
in the original formulation of Lancaster):

Theorem 1.2

(Lancaster)
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of complex numbers.
The following are equivalent characterizations for
(c_{n,k})_{n,k\geq 0}
:

(1)

Defining equation/generating function:

(x+a_{0})(x+a_{1})\cdots(x+a_{n-1}){=}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}\cdot x^{k}.

(2)

Recursion:
For each
n\geq k\geq 0
:

c_{n,k}=c_{n-1,k-1}+a_{n-1}c_{n-1,k}

with the boundary conditions:

c_{n,0}=a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}
and
c_{0,k}=\delta_{0k}
.

(3)

Complete recursion:
For
n\geq k\geq 0
:

c_{n,k}=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}c_{j-1,k-1}\prod\limits_{i=j}^{n-1}a_{i},

subject to the same boundary conditions as in Condition (2).

1.2
Broder’s restricted
r
-Stirling numbers

Broder
[
4
]
(see also
[
23
]
) defined an
r
-version to both kinds of Stirling numbers, which counts set partitions such that the first
r
elements are placed in
r
distinguished parts in the case of the second kind platform, and permutations of
[n]
which are decomposed in
k
cycles such that the elements
1,\dots,r
are in distinguished cycles in the case of the first kind platform.
An excellent textbook that deals with this type of generalization of Stirling numbers is Mező’s book
[
21
]
.

The ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind can also be interpreted as the number of elements of a constant rank in the intersection lattice of hyperplane arrangements of Coxeter type
A
.
Dolgachev-Lunts
[
8
, p. 755]
and Reiner
[
25
, Section 2]
generalized this idea to hyperplane arrangements of Coxeter type
B
(in
[
8
]
, the partitions of type
B
are counted by
\tilde{S}(n,k)_{0}
in their notation).
However, the concept of set partitions of type
B
has already appeared implicitly in Dowling
[
9
]
and also in Zaslavsky
[
34
]
in the form of signed graphs. The Stirling numbers of the second kind of type
B

enumerate the set partitions of type
B
;
the exact definitions will be recalled in Section
3.1
.

The Stirling numbers were further generalized in various ways. Bala
[
1
]
defined generalized Stirling numbers which he called
S_{(a,b,c)}
via their exponential Riordan array. The
r
-Whitney numbers
can be considered as special cases of these numbers for
a=m
and
c=r
(see e.g.
[
19
, Eqn. (1.2)]
).
In this work, we cover the case
(a,b,c)=(2,0,1)
in a combinatorial way, see Corollary
4.2
below in which we derive a special case of Equation (4) of Bala
[
1
]
using our methods. The numbers of the form
S_{(2,0,r)}
were granted a combinatorial interpretation by Corcino
et al

[
6
,
7
]
and Gyimesi and Nyul
[
13
]
, but we chose to take another route and generalize
S_{(2,0,1)}
in a way that will fit more naturally in the sense of Coxeter groups, see
Remarks
3.6
and
3.13

for more elaborate explanations.

Maier
[
18
]
presented a general notation for triangular numbers:

\left|\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right|:=\left[\begin{array}[]{lc|c}\alpha,&\beta&\gamma\\
\alpha^{\prime},&\beta^{\prime}&\gamma^{\prime}\end{array}\right]_{n,k},

which satisfy the following recursion:

\left|\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right|=(\alpha(n-1)+\beta k+\gamma)\left|\begin{array}[]{c}n-1\\
k\end{array}\right|+(\alpha^{\prime}(n-1)+\beta^{\prime}(k-1)+\gamma^{\prime})\left|\begin{array}[]{c}n-1\\
k-1\end{array}\right|

This notation contains our Stirling numbers of type
B
as the special case:

\alpha=0,\beta=2,\gamma=2r+1,\ \ \alpha^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}=0,\gamma^{\prime}=1.

1.3
Poly-Stirling numbers

One type of a generalization of the Stirling numbers is obtained by exchanging
k
in Equation (
1
) or
n-1
in Equation (
2
) for a value
p(k)
or
p(n-1)
(respectively) of a given polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. In this way, we get the
poly-Stirling numbers
(actually one might define the polynomial
p(x)
also over
\mathbb{C}
at the expense of losing the nice combinatorial meaning).

We cite here the definitions of poly-Stirling numbers, as they appear in Miceli
[
22
]
:

Definition 1.3
.

Given any nonzero polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
define the (unsigned) poly-Stirling numbers of the first kind with respect to
p(x)
, denoted by
c_{n,k}^{p(x)}
, by the recursion:

c_{n,k}^{p(x)}=c_{n-1,k-1}^{p(x)}+p(n-1)c_{n-1,k}^{p(x)},

where
c_{0,0}^{p(x)}=1
and
c_{n,k}^{p(x)}=0
if either
k>n
or
k<0
.

In a similar manner, the poly-Stirling numbers of the second kind with respect to
p(x)
, denoted by
S_{n,k}^{p(x)}
, are defined by the recursion:

S_{n,k}^{p(x)}=S_{n-1,k-1}^{p(x)}+p(k)S_{n-1,k}^{p(x)},

where
S_{0,0}^{p(x)}=1
and
S_{n,k}^{p(x)}=0
if either
k>n
or
k<0
.

In the current paper, we follow the work of Miceli
[
22
]
, who defined two natural types of
q
-analogues of poly-Stirling numbers, namely taking the
q
-analogue of
p(x)
to be either
[p(x)]_{q}
(type II
q
-poly-Stirling) or
p([x]_{q})
(type I
q
-poly-Stirling),
where
[n]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
is the
q
-analogue of the number
n
.

We further generalize both Miceli’s types
q
-poly-Stirling numbers to
q,r
-poly Stirling numbers, where the additional
r
stands for the Broder generalization
[
4
]
mentioned above. Actually, we provide a comprehensive analysis of these numbers by generalizing the theorems of Comtet and Lancaster to the
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of both kinds (see Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
5.2
, respectively).

Furthermore, we present orthogonality relations between the first and the second kinds
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of both types I and II.

We also provide some identities pertaining to sum of powers:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=r}^{n}\left([p(j)]_{q}\right)^{k}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=r}^{n}\bigl(p([j]_{q})\bigr)^{k}

(see Theorem
6.3
and Theorem
7.4
below), which generalize several results regarding
the ordinary sum of powers

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}j^{k}
,
see
[
12
,
20
,
28
,
33
]
.

Type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the second kind

In this section, we present a generalization of Comtet’s theorem (Theorem
1.1
above) to the case of type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the second kind, mentioned in the introduction (based on Miceli’s type II
q
-poly-Stirling number of the second kind
[
22
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. The type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by the recurrence relation:

S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=S_{n-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)+[p(k)]_{q}S_{n-1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\quad(r\leq k\leq n,~n\geq 1)

(4)

with
S_{r,r,r}^{p(x)}(q)=1
and
S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=0
for
k<r
,
k>n
or
n<r
.

We present here the generalization of Comtet’s theorem for type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the second kind. For the sake of completeness, the detailed but routine proof can be found in the Appendix.

Theorem 2.2

(Generalization of Comtet’s theorem for type II)
.

Let
p(n)
be a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients and let
\varphi_{k}(x),\ k\geq r
, be defined by

\varphi_{r}(x)=1\mbox{ and }\varphi_{k}(x)=(x-[p(r)]_{q})(x-[p(r+1)]_{q})\cdots(x-[p(k-1)]_{q})\mbox{ \rm for }k>r.

The following are equivalent characterizations for
(S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q))_{n,k,r\mid 0\leq r\leq k\leq n}
(where for all other values of the triple
(n,k,r)
, we assume
S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)=0
):

(1)

Defining equation/Change of bases:
For each
n\geq r
:

x^{n-r}{=}\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n}S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)\cdot\varphi_{k}(x).

(2)

Recursion:
For each
n\geq k>r
:

S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)=S^{p(x)}_{n-1,k-1,r}(q)+[p(k)]_{q}S^{p(x)}_{n-1,k,r}(q)

with the boundary conditions:

S^{p(x)}_{r,r,r}(q)=1
and
S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=0
for
k<r
,
k>n
or
n<r
.

(3)

Complete recursion:
For
n\geq k>r
:

S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}S^{p(x)}_{j-1,k-1,r}(q)\left([p(k)]_{q}\right)^{n-j},

subject to the same boundary conditions as in Condition (2).

(4)

Ordinary generating function:
For each
k\geq r
:

\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)x^{n}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-[p(r)]_{q}x)\cdots(1-[p(k)]_{q}x)}.

(5)

Explicit formula:
For
r\leq k\leq n
:

S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\sum\limits_{i_{r}+i_{r+1}+\cdots+i_{k}=n-k\atop i_{l}\geq 0}\left([p(r)]_{q}\right)^{i_{r}}\cdots\left([p(k)]_{q}\right)^{i_{k}}.

Condition (5) of Theorem
2.2
can be written in an equivalent form, as follows:

Corollary 2.3
.

For
r\leq k\leq n
, we have:

\displaystyle S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{k-1}=0}^{n-k}\bigl([p(k)]_{q}\bigr)^{n-k-j_{k-1}}\sum_{j_{k-2}=0}^{j_{k-1}}\bigl([p(k-1)]_{q}\bigr)^{j_{k-1}-j_{k-2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdots\sum_{j_{r+1}=0}^{j_{r+2}}\bigl([p(r+2)]_{q}\bigr)^{j_{r+2}-j_{r+1}}\sum_{j_{r}=0}^{j_{r+1}}\bigl([p(r+1)]_{q}\bigr)^{j_{r+1}-j_{r}}\bigl([p(r)]_{q}\bigr)^{j_{r}}.

Proof.

The right hand side is equivalent to the right hand side of the equation in Condition (5) of Theorem
2.2
by the following substitutions:
j_{r}=i_{r}
and
j_{m}=i_{r}+i_{r+1}+\cdots+i_{m}
for
r<m<k
.
∎

In the next theorem, we present a new way to write
S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)
, based on its generating function. We start with a lemma:

Lemma 2.4
.

For
n\geq k
and
\ell\geq 2
, we have:

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{(1-a_{1}x)\cdots(1-a_{\ell}x)}\right]=n!\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{a_{j}^{n-k+\ell-1}}{\left[\prod_{i=1,\ i\neq j}^{\ell}(a_{i}-a_{j})\right](1-a_{j}x)^{n+1}}.

Proof.

We prove this identity by induction on
n
.
The base case is
n=1
, in which one has to show that:

\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{(1-a_{1}x)\cdots(1-a_{\ell}x)}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{a_{j}^{\ell-k}}{\left[\prod_{i=1,\ i\neq j}^{\ell}(a_{i}-a_{j})\right](1-a_{j}x)^{2}}.

This equality can be achieved by performing a second induction on
\ell
and using partial fractions.

The induction step on
n
follows due to a simple derivation argument.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

For
r\leq k\leq n
, we have

S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\sum_{j=r}^{k}\frac{\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{n-r}}{\prod_{i=r,i\neq j}^{k}\bigl([p(i)]_{q}-[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)}\,.

Here, the empty product equals
1
as usual.

Proof.

The generating function appearing in Condition (4) of Theorem
2.2
is:

\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)x^{n}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-[p(r)]_{q}x)\cdots(1-[p(k)]_{q}x)}.

Then, we get by Lemma
2.4
for
\ell=k-r+1
and substituting
[p(i)]_{q}
for
a_{i}
:

\displaystyle S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\cdot\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{\prod_{j=r}^{k}\bigl(1-[p(j)]_{q}\cdot x\bigr)}\right]\right|_{x=0}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=r}^{k}\frac{\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{n-r}}{\prod_{i=r,i\neq j}^{k}\bigl([p(i)]_{q}-[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)}.

∎

3
Combinatorial aspects of Stirling numbers of type
B

If we focus on the polynomial
p(k)=2k-1
and
q=1
in Equation (
4
), we actually deal with the Stirling numbers (of the second kind) of type
B
:
S_{n,k}^{B}
and
S_{n,k,r}^{B}
. In this section, we elaborate on these numbers from the combinatorial point of view.

3.1
Set partitions of type
B

We now recall the definition of set partitions for type
B
, which are enumerated by these Stirling numbers (see Dolgachev-Lunts
[
8
, p. 755]
and Reiner
[
25
, Section 2]
; implicitly in Dowling
[
9
]
and Zaslavsky
[
34
]
in the form of signed graphs):

Definition 3.1
.

A set partition of
[\pm n]=\{\pm 1,\dots,\pm n\}
of type
B
is a set partition of the set
[\pm n]
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

•

If
B
appears as a block in the partition, then
-B
(which is obtained by negating all the elements of
B
) also appears in that partition.

•

There exists at most one block satisfying
-B=B
. This block is called the zero block (if it exists, it is a subset of
[\pm n]
of the form
\{\pm i\mid i\in C\}
for some
C\subseteq[n]
).

For example, the following is a set partition of
[\pm 6]
of type
B
:

\{\{1,-1,4,-4\},\{2,3,-5\},\{-2,-3,5\},\{6\},\{-6\}\}.

Note that every non-zero block
B
has a corresponding block
-B
attached to it. For the sake of convenience, we write for the pair of blocks
B,-B
, only the representative block containing the minimal positive number appearing in
B\cup-B
. For example, the pair of blocks

B=\{-2,-3,5\},-B=\{2,3,-5\}
will be represented by the single block
\{2,3,-5\}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S_{n,k}^{B}
be the number of set partitions of type
B
having
k
representative non-zero blocks. This is known as the Stirling number of the second kind of type
B
(see sequence A085483 in OEIS
[
24
]
).

It is easy to see that
S_{n,n}^{B}=S_{n,0}^{B}=1
for each
n\geq 0
. The following recursion for
S_{n,k}^{B}
is well-known (
[
9
, Theorem 7; see the Erratum]
,
[
2
, Corollary 3]
, for
m=2
, and
[
32
, Equation (1)]
, for
m=2,c=1
):

Proposition 3.3
.

For each
1\leq k<n
,

S_{n,k}^{B}=S_{n-1,k-1}^{B}+(2k+1)S_{n-1,k}^{B}.

(5)

We present here a new generalization of the Stirling number of the second kind of type
B
, based on the work of Broder
[
4
]
for type
A
. Define the
r
-Stirling number of the second kind of type
B
as follows:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
S_{n,k,r}^{B}
be the number of set partitions of type
B
of the set
[\pm n]
into
k
non-zero blocks such that the numbers
1,\dots,r
are in distinct non-zero blocks. The numbers
1,\dots,r
will be called special elements. Such set partitions will be called
r
-partitions of
[\pm n]
of type
B
.

For example, the following is an element counted by
S_{7,3,2}^{B}
:

\{\{4,-4\},\{\mathbf{1},3,-5\},\{-1,-3,5\},\{\mathbf{2},6\},\{-2,-6\},\{7\},\{-7\}\}.

Note that the case
r=0
brings us back to the definition of the Stirling number of type
B
given in Definition
3.2
.

The recursion for the
r
-Stirling numbers of type
B
is identical to the one given in Proposition
3.3
, where the only differences are the initial conditions, as the following proposition states:

Proposition 3.5
.

If
n<r
, then
S_{n,k,r}^{B}=0
. If
n=r
, then
S_{n,k,r}^{B}=\delta_{kr}
. If
n>r
, then:

S_{n,k,r}^{B}=S_{n-1,k-1,r}^{B}+(2k+1)S_{n-1,k,r}^{B}.

Remark 3.6
.

In some places, it is custom to deal with the shifted
r
-Stirling number of type
A
,
S_{n+r,k+r,r}
(sometimes denoted by
S_{n,k;r}
), which counts the number of set partitions of the set
\{1,\dots,n+r\}
into
k+r
blocks such that no two elements of the set
\{1,\dots,r\}
share a block (see e.g. Mező’s book
[
21
, Section 8.2]
).
The defining recursion for these numbers is (see e.g. Bala
[
1
, Eqn. (14)]
where
a=1
and
c=r
, Mansour-Ramírez-Shattuck
[
19
, Eqn. (1.2)]
where
m=1
, and Maier
[
18
, Eqn. (1.4)]
where
a=0
and
b=1

S_{n+r,k+r,r}=S_{n+r-1,k+r-1,r}+(k+r)S_{n+r-1,k+r,r}.

(6)

A combinatorial interpretation of
S_{n,k;r}
as counting colored set partitions under some restrictions was provided by Gyimesi and Nyul
[
13
]
, where the recursion (
6
) has the form

S_{n+r,k+r,r}=S_{n+r-1,k+r-1,r}+(mk+r)S_{n+r-1,k+r,r},

(7)

where
m
is the number of colors. This equation previously appeared in Corcino
[
6
]
without combinatorial interpretation, using
\beta
instead of
m
. Some combinatorial interpretation appeared in Corcino’s consequent paper with Aldema
[
7
]
.
These are
r
-Whitney numbers of the lattice of
r
-colored partitions.

Our version for the generalized
r
-Stirling numbers concentrates on the case
m=2
and corresponds to Whitney numbers of a more natural lattice from the algebraic point of view, that of
r
-signed partitions. The case
r=0
is isomorphic to the intersection lattice of the roots of the Coxeter group of type
B
(see also in Section
4.2
below). The corresponding recursion is as follows:

S^{B}_{n+r,k+r,r}=S^{B}_{n+r-1,k+r-1,r}+(2(k+r)+1)S^{B}_{n+r-1,k+r,r},

(8)

or in the abbreviated form, using the shifted notation
S^{B}_{n,k;r}=S^{B}_{n+r,k+r,r}
:

S^{B}_{n,k;r}=S^{B}_{n-1,k-1;r}+(2(k+r)+1)S^{B}_{n-1,k;r}.

(9)

3.2
Some combinatorial identities

In this section, we provide some combinatorial identities.
We start with the following recursion, connecting Stirling numbers of different values of
r
, which is a generalization for type
B
of Equation (8.2) in Mező
[
21
]
:

Proposition 3.7
.

For
n\geq k\geq r
,

S^{B}_{n,k,r}=S^{B}_{n,k,r-1}-(2r-1)S^{B}_{n-1,k,r-1}.

(10)

Proof.

First, we write Equation
(
10
) in an equivalent way:

S^{B}_{n,k,r-1}-S^{B}_{n,k,r}=(2r-1)S^{B}_{n-1,k,r-1}.

Now we show this equality in a combinatorial way. The left-hand side is the size of the set containing the set partitions of
[\pm n]
of type
B
into
k
non-zero blocks, such that the first
r-1
elements are distinguished but the element
r
is not.

On the right-hand side, we count the same set, by first ignoring the element
r
and partitioning the
n-1
remaining elements into
k
non-zero blocks with
r-1
distinguished elements and then placing the element
r
in one of the first
r-1
blocks in either positive or negative sign or in the zero block, so it will not be considered as a distinguished element.
∎

The following result connects the generalized Broder’s
r
-Stirling numbers of type
B
,
S^{B}_{n+r,k+r,r}
(denoted sometimes also as
S^{B}_{n,k;r}
), with the ordinary Stirling numbers of type
B
,
S^{B}_{n,k}
. This result can be considered as a type-
B
generalization of Equation (8.3) in Mező’s book
[
21
]
:

Proposition 3.8
.

For
n\geq k
and
r>0
,

S^{B}_{n+r,k+r,r}=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}{n\choose j}S^{B}_{j,k}(2r)^{n-j}.

(11)

Proof.

Again, we show this equality in a combinatorial way: the left-hand side of Equation (
11
) is the size of the set containing the set partitions of
\{1,\dots,n+r\}
of type
B
into
k+r
non-zero blocks, such that the first
r
elements are distinguished.

The right-hand side describes a different way to count this set: we can assume that the minimal elements of the distinguished blocks are already
1,\dots,r
. Recall that by definition they must be positive. Next, choose the number
j\geq k
of elements that will not be placed in the distinguished blocks (this choice is expressed by the sum over
j
) and choose these elements out of the remaining
n
elements in
{n\choose j}
ways. Next, place these
j
elements in
k
non-distinguished blocks in
S^{B}_{j,k}
ways (the number
S^{B}_{j,k}
includes also the placement of elements in the zero block). Next, arrange the remaining
n-j
elements, which might be positive or negative, since they are not minimal, in the
r
distinguished blocks.
∎

Next, we provide an explicit formula for the
r
-Stirling number of type
B
of the second kind.
We recall the following fact, whose proof is based on the classical inclusion-exclusion argument (see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 7.5]
):

Fact 3.9
.

The number of surjective functions from the set
[n]
to the set
[k]
is

\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}(k-\ell)^{n}.

Therefore, the number of set partitions of the set
[n]
into
k
blocks (of type
A
) is:

S_{n,k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}(k-\ell)^{n}.

In the spirit of the above fact, we present an explicit formula for the Stirling number of type
B
of the second kind:

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
k\leq n
. Then we have:

S^{B}_{n,k}=\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{\ell}\left(\frac{2^{n+1}(k-\ell)^{n+1}-1}{2(k-\ell)-1}\right).

(12)

Proof.

First, we assume that the zero-block contains exactly
u
elements, where
0\leq u\leq n
.
Using an argument similar to the one used to prove Fact
3.9
, we get that the number of set partitions of type
B
of the set
[\pm n]
into
k
non-zero blocks having a zero-block containing exactly
u
elements, is counted by the following formula:

\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{n-u-(k-\ell)}(k-\ell)^{n-u}.

This formula takes into account that all the elements that are not in the zero-block and are not minimal in their blocks can be either positive or negative.

Now, allowing
u
to range from
0
to
n
, we obtain:

\displaystyle S^{B}_{n,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{n-u-(k-\ell)}(k-\ell)^{n-u}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{k}\cdot 2^{u}}2^{\ell}\frac{(k-\ell)^{n}}{(k-\ell)^{u}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{n}\cdot 2^{\ell}(k-\ell)^{n}\sum_{u=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(2(k-\ell))^{u}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{n}\cdot 2^{\ell}(k-\ell)^{n}\left(\frac{2^{n+1}(k-\ell)^{n+1}-1}{2^{n}(k-\ell)^{n}(2(k-\ell)-1)}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{\ell}\left(\frac{2^{n+1}(k-\ell)^{n+1}-1}{2(k-\ell)-1}\right).

∎

We now prove an
r
-version for this result. We start by a formulation for type
A
, which already appeared in Corcino
[
6
]
, with a combinatorial proof based on the inclusion-exclusion principle (similar proof can be found in Corcino and Aldema
[
7
]
):

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
and let
k\leq n
. Then:

S_{n+r,k+r,r}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}{(-1)^{k-\ell}{k\choose\ell}(\ell+r)^{n}}.

(13)

Proof.

Assuming that for each
1\leq i\leq r
, the
i
-th block already contains the element
i
, we are left with the problem of counting the number of functions from the set
[n]
to the set
\{1,\dots,r+k\}
satisfying that the set
\{r+1,\dots,r+k\}
is contained in their image (similar to the functions which we dealt with in Fact
3.9
).
∎

Now we move to the
r
-version for type
B
:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
k\leq n
. Then we have:

S^{B}_{n+r,k+r,r}=\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{\ell}\left(\frac{2^{n+1}(k+r-\ell)^{n+1}-1}{2(k+r-\ell)-1}\right).

(14)

Proof.

Apply the arguments of Propositions
3.10
and
3.11
to get the following expression

\frac{1}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}2^{n-u-(k-\ell)}(k+r-\ell)^{n-u}

(15)

instead of Equation (
12
).

This formula is based on the inclusion-exclusion principle, where we have to insert the
n
elements:
r+1,\dots,r+n
into
k+r
parts. Moreover,
it takes into account the fact that the elements
1,\dots,r
are already positive and minimal in their respective blocks, and hence only
k
more elements should be signed positively.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

A formula similar to the one appearing in Equations (
13
) and (
14
) can be found in the work of Corcino et al
[
6
,
7
]
and Gyimesi and Nyul
[
13
]
. Note however that they deal with a different definition of set partitions of type
B
: they do not have a zero-block and their
r
first blocks (which contain the elements
1,\dots,r
respectively) have no negative
elements, see
[
7
, beginning of Section 3]
and
[
13
, Definition 2.2]
. We have elaborated on the significance of our choice of set partitions of type
B
in Section
1.2
.
Note that it seems that there is a missing condition in the combinatorial interpretation of
(r,\beta)
-Stirling number
[
7
, beginning of Section 3]
(this missing condition does appear in
[
13
, Definition 2.2]
).

3.3
Log-concavity of
S_{n,k,r}^{B}

We recall that a sequence of real numbers
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is called
log-concave
if the following condition holds for each
i\geq 2
:

a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}.

It is well known that the sequence of Stirling numbers of the second kind (as well as of the first kind) is log-concave. Different proofs were given to these facts (see for example
[
14
,
15
]
). It seems that the simplest proof for the log-concavity of ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind was provided by Sagan
[
26
, Theorem 1]
. We present Sagan’s statement here and we use it to prove the log-concavity for type
B
:

Theorem 3.14

(Sagan
[
26
]
)
.

Let
t_{n,k}
be an extended triangular array, satisfying
t_{n,k}=c_{n,k}t_{n-1,k-1}+d_{n,k}t_{n-1,k}
for all
n\geq 1
,
where
t_{n,k},c_{n,k}
and
d_{n,k}
are all non-negative integers. Suppose that:

(i)

c_{n,k}
and
d_{n,k}
are log-concave in
k
,

(ii)

c_{n,k-1}d_{n,k+1}+c_{n,k+1}d_{n,k-1}\leq 2c_{n,k}d_{n,k}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Then
t_{n,k}
is log-concave in
k
.

We have the following result:

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
n,r
be such that
r\leq n
. The sequence of
r
-Stirling numbers of type
B
of the second kind,
S^{B}_{n,k,r}
, is log-concave.

Proof.

The recursion for the
r
-Stirling numbers of type
B
of the second kind,
S^{B}_{n,k,r}
, is:

S_{n,k,r}^{B}=S_{n-1,k-1,r}^{B}+(2k+1)S_{n-1,k,r}^{B}.

Hence, we have:
c_{n,k}=1
and
d_{n,k}=2k+1
, which satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.14
.
∎

4
Exponential generating functions for Stirling numbers of type
B

In this section, we deal with the exponential generating functions of
S_{n,k}^{B}
and
S_{n,k,r}^{B}
(Sections
4.1
and
4.2
respectively). In Section
4.3
, we introduce an application of the exponential generating function of
S_{n,k,r}^{B}
as connection constants between bases of
\mathbb{R}[x]
.

4.1
An exponential generating function of
S_{n,k}^{B}

We have the following result concerning an exponential generating function of
S_{n,k}^{B}
:

Proposition 4.1
.

For
k\geq 0
,

\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{n,k}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}.

(16)

The proof we provide here uses the symbolic approach to compute exponential generating functions for labeled structures due to Flajolet and Sedgewick (see
[
10
, Section II.1]
).
For the sake of self-containment, we list the following labeled structures, most of which can be found in
[
10
]
there:

•

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\{\textcircled{\tiny{1}}\}
be the atomic labeled class having only one element. The corresponding exponential generating function (EGF) is
E(z)=z
.

•

We denote by
2\mathcal{Z}
the direct sum
\mathcal{Z}\oplus\mathcal{Z}
, with corresponding EGF:
E(z)=2z
.

•

For a labeled class
\mathcal{B}
, let
Seq
{}_{k}(\mathcal{B})
be the class containing all the sequences of elements from the class
\mathcal{B}
of length
k
. Provided that the EGF of
\mathcal{B}
is denoted by
B(z)
, the EGF of
Seq
{}_{k}(\mathcal{B})
is
E(z)=B(z)^{k}
.

•

For a labeled class
\mathcal{B}
, let
Set
(\mathcal{B})
be the class of sets of elements taken from
\mathcal{B}
and let
Set
k
(\mathcal{B})
be the class of
k
-sets of elements taken from
\mathcal{B}
. If the EGF of
\mathcal{B}
is denoted by
B(z)
, then the EGF of
Set
(\mathcal{B})
is
e^{B(z)}
, while the EGF of
Set
{}_{k}(\mathcal{B})
is
\frac{1}{k!}B(z)^{k}
.

•

In particular,
Set
(\mathcal{Z})
is the class of urns (or totally disconnected graphs), with corresponding EGF given by
E(z)=e^{z}
.

•

Define
\mbox{{\sc Sset}}(\mathcal{Z})=\mbox{{\sc Set}}(2\mathcal{Z})
and interpret it as the class of signed sets of integers, where by a
signed set
we mean a set in which every element is signed by either
+
or
-
. It is easy to see that the EGF of
Sset
(\mathcal{Z})
is
e^{2z}
.
As usual, the class
Sset
{}_{\geq 1}(\mathcal{Z})
denotes the set of the non-empty signed sets of
Sset
and its EGF is thus
E(z)=e^{2z}-1
.

•

Define an equivalence relation
R
on
Sset
{}_{\geq 1}(\mathcal{Z})
that identifies two signed sets
A
and
B
if
A=-B
(the negation is taken elementwise).
For example, the signed sets
\{2,-3,5,-7\}
and
\{-2,3,-5,7\}
are equivalent. The first set is used as a representative, since its minimal element is positive.

The quotient class will be denoted by
\nicefrac{{\mbox{{\sc Sset}}_{\geq 1}(\mathcal{Z})}}{{R}}
and by its definition, it can be easily observed that its corresponding EGF is
\frac{e^{2z}-1}{2}
.

Now we are ready to supply a symbolic proof for Proposition
4.1
:

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Every set partition of
[\pm n]
of type
B
counted by
S^{B}_{n,k}
is composed of a zero block (which might be empty) followed by
k
signed non-empty blocks, every minimal element of which is positive. Therefore, the combinatorial class we are counting can be written as

\mbox{{\sc Set}}(\mathcal{Z})\cdot\mbox{{\sc Set}}_{k}(\nicefrac{{\mbox{{\sc Sset}}_{\geq 1}(\mathcal{Z})}}{{R}}),

and hence its EGF is
e^{z}\cdot\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{2}\right)^{k}
as claimed.
∎

Sagan and Swanson
[
27
, Theorem 4.3]
provided a
q
-analogue of Proposition
4.1
using
q
-exponentials.

Summing over
k
, we immediately get the following corollary, which is a special case of Bala
[
1
, Eqn. (4)]
for
(a,b,c)=(2,0,1)
:

Corollary 4.2
.

\sum\limits_{k,n}S^{B}(n,k)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}t^{k}=e^{z}e^{(e^{2z}-1)\frac{t}{2}}.

4.2
An exponential generating function for
S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}

We have the following result concerning an exponential generating function for the shifted
r
-Stirling numbers of type
B

S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}
:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k,r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{(2r+1)z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}.

We prove it in two different ways: the first proof is analytical in nature, but is based on a combinatorial lemma, that is itself a generalization of a result appearing in Mező’s book
[
21
, Section 8.2]
. The second proof is similar to the one given for Proposition
4.1
and uses the symbolic approach for exponential generating functions of Flajolet and Sedgewick
[
10
, Section II.1]
.

The analytical proof is based on the following combinatorial identity:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n,k,r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

S_{n,k,r}^{B}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-r}{{n-r}\choose m}\cdot S^{B}_{n-r-m,k-r}\cdot(2r)^{m}.

(17)

Proof.

Recall that the set partitions of type
B
counted by
S_{n,k,r}^{B}
, have
r
distinguished blocks, each of them contains exactly one of the distinguished elements
1,\dots,r
, but might contains additional elements from
r+1,\dots,n
.

Let
m
be the number of additional elements that will occupy the
r
distinguished blocks (note that the absolute value of each of these
m
elements is greater than
r
).
The number
{n-r\choose m}
counts the ways to choose
m
such elements.

Then, we insert the remaining
n-r-m
elements in
k-r
additional blocks in
S^{B}_{n-r-m,k-r}
ways. Next, we fill the
r
distinguished blocks with
r+m
elements in
S^{B}_{r+m,r}
ways.
Note, however, that
S^{B}_{r+m,r}=(2r)^{m}
, since each one of the additional
m
elements can be located in one of the
r
distinguished blocks, and can be either positive or negative.
This gives us the required equality.
∎

Based on this lemma, one can easily deduce an expression for the exponential generating function of
S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}
:

Analytical proof of Theorem
4.3
.

By substituting in Equation (
17
)
n+r
and
k+r
for
n
and
k
respectively, we get:

S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}{n\choose m}S^{B}_{n-m,k}\cdot(2r)^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\leftarrow n-m}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}{n\choose m}\cdot(2r)^{n-m}\cdot S_{m,k}^{B}.

Since the exponential generating function of the (ordinary) Stirling numbers for type
B
is:
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{n,k}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}
by Proposition
4.1
above, we conclude from the rule of product of exponential generating functions (see e.g.
[
30
, Prop. 5.1.1]
) that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}{n\choose m}\cdot(2r)^{n-m}\cdot S_{m,k}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2rz}\cdot\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{(2r+1)z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k},

as required.
∎

Next, we present the second proof of Theorem
4.3
based on the symbolic approach:

Symbolic proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Every
r
-partition of
\{1,\dots,n+r\}
of type
B
(see Definition
3.4
) counted by
S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}
is composed of a zero block which is taken from the set of numbers
\{r+1,\dots,n+r\}
, followed by a sequence of
r
signed sets (each one is preoccupied with a minimal element from the set
\{1,\dots,r\}
) and finally a set of
k
signed sets such that the minimal element of each set is positive.

In symbols, this description amounts to:

\mbox{{\sc Set}}(\mathcal{Z})\cdot\mbox{\sc Seq}_{r}(\mbox{{\sc Sset}}(\mathcal{Z}))\cdot\mbox{{\sc Set}}_{k}(\nicefrac{{\mbox{{\sc Sset}}_{\geq 1}(\mathcal{Z})}}{{R}}),

which contributes

e^{z}\cdot e^{2rz}\cdot\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!2^{k}}e^{(2r+1)z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k},

as claimed.
∎

Note that by substituting
r=0
in Theorem
4.3
, we get again Proposition
4.1
.

The following corollary is a different presentation to a special case of Bala
[
1
, Eqn. (4)]
for
(a,b,c)=(2,0,r)
:

Corollary 4.5
.

\sum\limits_{k,n\geq 0}^{\infty}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}t^{k}=e^{(2r+1)z}e^{t\frac{e^{2z}-1}{2}}.

4.3
An application to connection constants

Given
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{(x+2r)^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{(x-1)(x-3)\cdots(x-2n+1)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

be two sequences of polynomials, forming two different bases of
\mathbb{R}[x]
. The following result presents the numbers
S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}
as connection constants between these two bases of
\mathbb{R}[x]
:

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have:

(x+2r)^{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\cdot(x-1)(x-3)\cdots(x-2k+1).

Proof.

We calculate:

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(2x+2r)^{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2(x+r)z}=e^{(2r+1)z}e^{2\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)z}=e^{(2r+1)z}(1+(e^{2z}-1))^{\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{(2r+1)z}(e^{2z}-1)^{k}\cdot\frac{2^{k}\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(x-\frac{3}{2}\right)\cdots\left(x-k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2^{k}k!}=

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Thm.}\ \ref{thm_exponential}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)(2x-1)(2x-3)\cdots(2x-2k+1)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\cdot(2x-1)(2x-3)\cdots(2x-2k+1)\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

By comparing the coefficients of
z^{n}
, we have:

(2x+2r)^{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}S_{n+r,k+r,r}^{B}\cdot(2x-1)(2x-3)\cdots(2x-2k+1).

By substituting
x
for
2x
, the result follows.
∎

Note that Corollary 2.4 of Sagan and Swanson
[
27
]
is a
q
-analogue for the
r=0
case.

5
The type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the first kind

In this section, we move to the Stirling numbers of the first kind, and present a generalization of Lancaster’s theorem (Theorem
1.2
above) to the case of unsigned type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the first kind, mentioned in the introduction (based on Miceli’s type II
q
-poly-Stirling number of the first kind
[
22
]
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]

Theorem 5.2

(Generalized Lancaster’s theorem for
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers)
.

Let
p(n)
be a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients.
The following are equivalent characterizations for
(c^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q))_{n,k,r\ \mid\ 0\leq r\leq k\leq n}
(where for all other values of the triple
(n,k,r)

(1)

Defining equation/generating function:

(x+[p(r)]_{q})(x+[p(r+1)]_{q})\cdots(x+[p(n-1)]_{q})=\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n}c_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\cdot x^{k-r}.

(2)

Recursion:
For each

c^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}=c_{n-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)+[p(n-1)]_{q}\ c_{n-1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)

with the boundary conditions:

and
c_{r,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\delta_{kr}
.

(3)

Complete Recursion:
For

c_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}c_{j-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)\prod\limits_{i=j}^{n-1}[p(i)]_{q},

subject to the same boundary conditions as in Condition (2).

(4)

Explicit formula:
For

Proof.

We prove only the implications
[(2)
\Longrightarrow
(4)]

[(4)
\Longrightarrow
(2)]

[(2)
\Longrightarrow
(4)]:

We prove the identity by induction on

The base case is:

as required.

Assume correctness for

\displaystyle c_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{2st1})}}{{=}}

In the previous section, we focused on the unsigned version of the

Definition 6.1
.

Let

-poly-Stirling numbers of the first kind, denoted

s_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=s_{n-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)-[p(n-1)]_{q}\ s_{n-1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\quad(r\leq k\leq n,~n\geq 1)

and

(for

,

or

We present here the generalization of Lancaster’s theorem for the signed case, the correctness of which is evident by substituting

in the formulation of the

):

Theorem 6.2

(Generalized Lancaster’s theorem for signed type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the first kind)
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. The following are equivalent characterizations for
(s^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q))_{n,k,r\ \mid\ 0\leq r\leq k\leq n}
(where for all other values of the triple
(n,k,r)
, we assume
s^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)=0

(1)

Defining equation/generating function:

(x-[p(r)]_{q})(x-[p(r+1)]_{q})\cdots(x-[p(n-1)]_{q}){=}\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n}s_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\cdot x^{k-r}.

(2)

Recursion:
For each
n\geq k>r

s^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}=s_{n-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)-[p(n-1)]_{q}\ s_{n-1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q),

with the boundary conditions:

s_{n,r,r}^{p(x)}(q)=(-1)^{n-r}[p(r)]_{q}[p(r+1)]_{q}\cdots[p(n-1)]_{q}

.

(3)

Complete recursion:
For

s_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\sum\limits_{j=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}s_{j-1,k-1,r}^{p(x)}(q)\prod\limits_{i=j}^{n-1}[p(i)]_{q},

subject to the same boundary conditions as in Condition (2).

(4)

Explicit formula:

For

6.1
An application to sum of powers

In this subsection, we provide an application of Theorem
6.2
to the sum of powers of the expressions
[p(j)]_{q}
:

Theorem 6.3
.

For integers
n,k
and
r
with
n\geq r
and

, we have:

\sum_{j=r}^{n}\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{k}=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-r+1}\ell s_{n+1,n+1-\ell,r}^{p(x)}(q)S_{n+k-\ell,n,r}^{p(x)}(q)\,.

Proof.

By Condition (1) of Theorem
6.2
for
n+1

we have:

x^{r}\prod_{j=r}^{n}\bigl(x-[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)=\sum_{k=r}^{n+1}s_{n+1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)x^{k}.

(20)

Next, we substitute
\frac{1}{t}
for
x

\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{r}\prod_{j=r}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{t}-[p(j)]_{q}\right)=\sum_{k=r}^{n+1}s_{n+1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{k}.

Multiplying this by

, yields:

F_{1}(t):=\prod_{j=r}^{n}\bigl(1-t[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)=\sum_{k=r}^{n+1}s_{n+1,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\ t^{n-k+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n-k+1\rightarrow\ell}}{{=}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-r+1}s_{n+1,n+1-\ell,r}^{p(x)}\ t^{\ell}\,.

(21)

Recall from Theorem
2.2
(4) that

\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}S^{p(x)}_{n,k,r}(q)x^{n}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-x[p(r)]_{q})\cdots(1-x[p(k)]_{q})}.

By the substitutions (in this order):
n\rightarrow n+\nu
,
k\rightarrow n
and
x\rightarrow t
, and dividing both sides by

, we get:

\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\infty}S^{p(x)}_{n+\nu,n,r}(q)\ t^{\nu}=\frac{1}{(1-t[p(r)]_{q})\cdots(1-t[p(n)]_{q})}=\prod_{j=r}^{n}\bigl(1-t[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{-1}=:F_{2}(t).

Note that
F_{1}(t)F_{2}(t)=1
. Now, we have:

\frac{d}{dt}\log F_{2}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\sum_{j=r}^{n}-\log\left(1-t[p(j)]_{q}\right)\right]=\sum_{j=r}^{n}\frac{[p(j)]_{q}}{1-t[p(j)]_{q}}=\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{k}t^{k-1}.

(22)

Note that:

\frac{d}{dt}F_{1}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\prod_{j=r}^{n}\bigl(1-t[p(j)]_{q}\bigr)\right]=\sum_{m=r}^{n}-[p(m)]_{q}\prod_{j=r,j\neq m}^{n}\bigl(1-t[p(j)]_{q}\bigr).

Therefore, we have

-\left(\frac{d}{dt}F_{1}(t)\right)F_{2}(t)=\sum_{m=r}^{n}\frac{[p(m)]_{q}}{1-t[p(m)]_{q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:221})}}{{=}}\frac{d}{dt}\log F_{2}(t).

(23)

On the other hand, by Equation (
21
), we also have:

\frac{d}{dt}F_{1}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-r+1}\ell s_{n+1,n+1-\ell,r}^{p(x)}(q)t^{\ell-1}\,.

Combining Equations (
22
) and (

), we have:

-\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1}\ell s_{n+1,n+1-\ell,r}^{p(x)}(q)t^{\ell-1}\right)\left(\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}S_{n+\nu,n,r}^{p(x)}(q)t^{\nu}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{k}t^{k-1},

which can be also written as:

-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\ell s_{n+1,n+1-\ell,r}^{p(x)}(q)S_{n+\nu,n,r}^{p(x)}(q)t^{\ell-1+\nu}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\bigl([p(j)]_{q}\bigr)^{k}t^{k-1}.

Comparing the coefficients of

in both sides (imposing that

), yields the requested formula.
∎

Note that Theorem 1.1 of Merca
[
20
]
is a special case of Theorem
6.3
where
r=0
and
p(x)=x
.

6.2
An application to orthogonality relations

As a consequence of Theorems
2.2
and
6.2
, we get the orthogonality relations between the type II
q,r
-poly-Stirling numbers of the first and second kinds:

Theorem 6.4
.

For

and

, we have:

\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}S_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)s_{k,\ell,r}^{p(x)}(q)=\delta_{n\ell}

\prod_{j=r}^{n-1}\left(x-[p(j)]_{q}\right)=\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n}s_{n,k,r}^{p(x)}(q)\cdot x^{k-r}.

By substituting either one of these equations into the other and changing the order of summations, we readily obtain the orthogonality relations.
∎

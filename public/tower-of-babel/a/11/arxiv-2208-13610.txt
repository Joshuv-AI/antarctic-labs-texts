A note on the CBC-DBD construction of lattice rules with general positive weights
Peter Kritzer

Abstract
Lattice rules are among the most prominently studied quasi-Monte Carlo methods to approximate multivariate integrals. A rank- 1 1 lattice rule to approximate an s s -dimensional integral is fully specified by its generating vector 𝒛 ∈ ℤ s {\bm{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s} and its number of points N N . While there are many results on the existence of “good” rank- 1 1 lattice rules, there are no explicit constructions of good generating vectors for dimensions s ≥ 3 s\geq 3 . This is why one usually resorts to computer search algorithms. In a recent paper by Ebert et al. in the Journal of Complexity, we showed a component-by-component digit-by-digit (CBC-DBD) construction for good generating vectors of rank-1 lattice rules for integration of functions in weighted Korobov classes. However, the result in that paper was limited to product weights. In the present paper, we shall generalize this result to arbitrary positive weights, thereby answering an open question posed in the paper of Ebert et al. We also include a short section on how the algorithm can be implemented in the case of POD weights, by which we see that the CBC-DBD construction is competitive with the classical CBC construction.



Keywords:
Numerical integration; lattice points; quasi-Monte Carlo methods; weighted function spaces;
digit-by-digit construction; component-by-component construction; fast construction.

Introduction

In high-dimensional numerical integration, one frequently uses
quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC)
rules
Q_{N,s}

to efficiently approximate integrals
I_{s}
,

I_{s}(f):=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f({\bm{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}\quad\approx\quad Q_{N,s}(f):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\,f({\bm{x}}_{k})

for a suitably chosen integrand
f
(usually, we assume that
f
is an element of a Hilbert or Banach space,
see below). I.e., a QMC rule is an equal-weight quadrature rule, and it is—opposed to
Monte Carlo
rules—based on
deterministically chosen integration nodes
{\bm{x}}_{0},{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\in[0,1)^{s}
.
A non-trivial question in
this field is how the nodes of a QMC rule can be chosen in order to guarantee a low integration error. Depending on
the properties of the integrand
f
under consideration, two classes of integration node sets have gained most attention
in the past decades, namely
digital nets and sequences
, introduced by Sobol’, Faure, and Niederreiter (see, e.g.,

[
4
,
10
]
), and
lattice point sets
, introduced by Korobov and Hlawka (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
14
]
). In this paper,
we focus on instances of the latter, namely so-called
rank-1 lattice point sets
yielding
lattice rules
when used in QMC rules.
These are sets of integration nodes with
N
points

{\bm{x}}_{k}:=\left(\left\{\frac{kz_{1}}{N}\right\},\ldots,\left\{\frac{kz_{s}}{N}\right\}\right)\in[0,1)^{s},\qquad\text{for }k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\},

and where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of a real
x
.
Note that, given
N
and
s
, the lattice rule is completely determined by the choice of the
generating vector

{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{s}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}:=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
.
We remark that it is sufficient to consider the choice of
z_{j}
modulo
N
since
\{kz_{j}/N\}=(kz_{j}\bmod{N})/N
for integer
k
,
N
, and
z_{j}
.
Obviously, not every choice of a generating vector
{\bm{z}}
also yields a lattice rule of good quality for approximating the integral.
For dimensions
s\leq 2
, explicit constructions of good generating vectors are available, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
10
,
14
]
,
but there are no explicit constructions of good generating vectors known for
s>2
. Therefore, one usually studies search algorithms
for generating vectors of good lattice rules, which are designed to make a certain error criterion sufficiently small.
Korobov
[
7
]
, and later Sloan and his collaborators
[
15
]
, introduced a
component-by-component (CBC) construction
,
which is a greedy algorithm constructing the components
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
of
{\bm{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{s}
successively, choosing one
z_{j}
at a time,
and keeping previous components fixed. It was shown in
[
8
]
for prime
N
and in
[
1
]
for composite
N
that the CBC construction
yields generating vectors with essentially optimal convergence rates for particular spaces of integrands. For suitable choices of function spaces,
there exist fast implementations of CBC constructions, which have a run-time of order
\mathcal{O}(sN\log N)
, see
[
12
,
13
]
.

In this short paper, we consider an alternative to the common CBC search algorithm, namely a so-called CBC-DBD algorithm, which is based on
the idea that the single components of the generating vector
{\bm{z}}
are chosen one after another, and each component is constructed
digit-by-digit (DBD)
,
i.e., the base-2 digits of the components are chosen in a greedy fashion, starting with the least significant digit. The principle idea of this construction is
due to Korobov (see
[
7
]
), and it was shown to work for a modern function space setting in the recent paper
[
5
]
. However,
due to technical difficulties, the main result in
[
5
]
does not hold in full generality, but only with certain restrictions on the
weight
parameters
of the function space involved. In the present paper, we close this gap (see below for a more detailed explanation).

In order to make use of a construction method like the CBC or the CBC-DBD construction, one needs to define the search criterion which the algorithm
is based on. Usually, this criterion is related to the class of integrands under consideration. In
[
5
]
, we consider a Banach space (called
Korobov space

or
Korobov class
) of functions.

We consider integrands
f
with absolutely converging Fourier series,

f({\bm{x}})=\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}}\hat{f}({\bm{m}})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\hat{f}({\bm{m}}):=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f({\bm{x}})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}},

i.e.,
\hat{f}({\bm{m}})
is the
{\bm{m}}
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
, where
{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{x}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_{j}x_{j}
is the vector dot product.
Since the Fourier series are absolutely summable, the Fourier series are pointwise convergent,
1
-periodic, and continuous.
Here, we will consider Banach spaces which are based on assuming sufficient decay of the Fourier coefficients of its elements to guarantee certain smoothness.
These spaces will be denoted by
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
, where
s
denotes the number of variables the functions depend on,
\alpha>1

is a real number frequently referred to as the smoothness parameter, and
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
,
is a set of strictly positive
weights
to model the importance of different subsets of components. In this context,
by the notation “
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
” we mean all finite subsets
{\mathfrak{u}}
of the positive integers. We extend the range of
{\mathfrak{u}}

to all finite subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
since we also would like to include results that hold asymptotically when
s
tends to infinity.

Intuitively, a large
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
corresponds to a high influence of the variables
x_{j}
with
j\in{\mathfrak{u}}
,
while a small
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
means low influence. The idea of weights goes back to Sloan and Woźniakowski
[
16
]
,
and will be made more precise by incorporating the weights in the norm of the space
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
below.
We are interested in conditions on the weights such that we can bound the integration error independently of
s
.
This is called
strong polynomial tractability
, see, e.g.,
[
11
]
, for a general reference.

In the literature on QMC methods, the weights
{\bm{\gamma}}
are not always chosen as fully general. Indeed, a very common variant is
to work with an infinite sequence
(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and to put
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}:=\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j}
, which is the case of
product weights
.
Other variants are, e.g., finite-order weights or product-and-order-dependent (POD) weights, and we refer the reader to
[
3
]
for a more detailed discussion.
The main result in
[
5
]
is shown for the special case of product weights, due to one step in the proof that we were not able to carry out for more general weights.
In the present paper, we close this gap, and show a corresponding result for general weights
{\bm{\gamma}}
. The only assumption we make is that
all
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}
are positive, which is to avoid too much technical notation. Presumably, a similar result also holds for weights that are allowed to be zero.

Now, for a given smoothness parameter
\alpha>1
and strictly positive weights
\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
, we define,
for any
{\bm{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
,

r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{m}}):=\gamma_{\operatorname{supp}({\bm{m}})}^{-1}\prod_{j\in\operatorname{supp}({\bm{m}})}\left|m_{j}\right|^{\alpha},

where
\operatorname{supp}({\bm{m}}):=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}:m_{j}\neq 0\}
is the support of
{\bm{m}}
. We set
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
, so
r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{0}})=1
.
Using this notation, we define the norm of our Banach space
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
,

\|f\|_{E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{{\bm{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}}|\hat{f}({\bm{m}})|\,r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{m}}),\quad\mbox{for}\quad f\in E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha},

(1)

and our weighted function space by

E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{s})\colon\|f\|_{E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}}<\infty\right\}.

The criterion by which we assess the quality of a given rank-1 lattice rule is the
worst-case error
, which is defined as

e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}\\
\|f\|_{E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}\leq 1}\end{subarray}}\left|I_{s}(f)-Q_{N,s}(f,{\bm{z}})\right|,

where we stress the dependence on
{\bm{z}}
in our notation of the error and of the QMC rule
Q_{N,s}
.

Remark 1
.

Note that since
\alpha>1
, the membership of
f
to the space
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
implies the absolute convergence of its Fourier series,
which in turn entails that
f
is continuous and
1
-periodic with respect to each variable. In addition, if
f\in E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
,
f

has
1
-periodic continuous mixed partial derivatives
f^{({\bm{\tau}})}
for any
{\bm{\tau}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{s}
with all
\tau_{j}<\alpha-1
. Furthermore,
it is known that the optimal convergence rate of the worst-case error in the function space is of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha})
. We also remark
that, by slightly modifying the definition of the norm in (
1
), one could define a function space that is similar to
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
,
but a Hilbert space. The latter is frequently studied in the literature on lattice rules (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
), and the worst-case error in

E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
is exactly the square of the worst-case error in the Hilbert case. For consistency with
[
5
]
, we stay with
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}

in this note, and refer to that paper for further information on the properties of the function space, as well as for further references.

We write
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
for the set of natural numbers and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}
for the set of integers and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}:=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
.
To denote sets of components we use fraktur font, e.g.,
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
.
As a shorthand we write
\{k_{1}:k_{2}\}
for the set
\{k_{1},k_{1}+1,\ldots,k_{2}\}
,
for two integers
k_{1},k_{2}
with
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
.
To denote the projection of a vector
{\bm{x}}\in[0,1)^{s}
or
{\bm{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
onto the components
in a set
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}
we write
{\bm{x}}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}
or
{\bm{m}}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=(m_{j})_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}
, respectively.

2
The CBC-DBD construction yields optimal convergence rates for general weights

As outlined above, the paper
[
5
]
shows a result for lattice rules with generating vectors obtained by a CBC-DBD algorithm, which implies
that the lattice rules achieve a convergence order in
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
that can be arbitrarily close to the optimal rate
N^{-\alpha}
.
Furthermore, the error bound can be made independent of the dimension if the weights
{\bm{\gamma}}
satisfy suitable conditions. While the results
in
[
5
]
are limited to product weights, we show their generalization to arbitrary positive weights here. To be more precise, several auxiliary
results in our previous paper hold for general weights, and only some of the proofs there require the assumption of product weights. Here, we will mostly
highlight those passages where there is a significant difference to what is discussed in
[
5
]
.
For consistency, we shall use notation that is as similar as possible to that in
[
5
]
, with only few minor adaptions.

A keystone in the paper
[
5
]
was the following proposition, which implies that in analyzing the worst-case error
e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})
it is sufficient
to consider only a truncated variant of the error expression, namely

T_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{m}}\in M_{N,s}\setminus\{{\bm{0}}\}\\
{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;N)}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{m}})},\quad\mbox{where}\quad M_{N,s}:=\{-(N-1),\ldots,N-1\}^{s}.

(2)

Indeed, we have

Proposition 1
.

[
5
, Proposition 1]

Let
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
be positive weights and let

{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
with
\gcd(z_{j},N)=1

where

Proof.

We refer to
[

]
for a proof.

T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})

for the case when

Theorem 1
.

[
5

Let

{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}

Furthermore, let
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}^{s}

\displaystyle T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}(\log 4+2(1+\log N))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}-\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\,(\log 4)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}\,2|{\mathfrak{u}}|\,\left(1+2\log N\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\,(1+\log N)+\frac{1}{N}\,H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}),

Based on the quantity
H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})
, an averaging argument was used in
[
5
]
to obtain a quality function which serves
as the relevant criterion in the CBC-DBD algorithm. We state its definition

Definition 1

(Digit-wise quality function)
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
be an odd integer, let
n,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
be positive integers, and let
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}

be positive weights. For
1\leq v\leq n
and
1\leq r\leq s
, and odd integers
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the
quality function
h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}
for odd integer
x
as

\displaystyle h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=v}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\equiv 1{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;2)}\end{subarray}}^{2^{t}-1}\left[\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}r-1\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kz_{j}/2^{t})}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}r-1\}}\!\!\!\!\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}\cup\{r\}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kz_{j}/2^{t})}\right)\right].

Note that while the quantity
h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}
depends on the integers
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1}
, this dependency is not explicitly visible in our notation.
Nevertheless, in the following these integers will always be the components of the generating vector which have been selected in the previous steps of our algorithm.
Based on

the component-by-component digit-by-digit (CBC-DBD) algorithm is formulated as follows in

]
.

Algorithm 1

[

Input:
Integer

, dimension

, and positive weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Set
z_{1,n}=1
and

.

for

r=2

to

do

for

to

for

.

end

for

\displaystyle+(\log 4)\gamma_{\{s-1\}}N+(\log 4)\gamma_{\{s\}}N+(\log 4)^{2}\gamma_{\{s-1,s\}}N.

Consequently, we have shown

H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}})\leq\sum_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}H_{s-2,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s-2\}})+N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}.

This yields, by using (
4
) again,

\displaystyle H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\left[H_{s-3,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s-3\}})+(\log 4)({\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}})_{\{s-2\}}N\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(\log 4)H_{s-3,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}\cup\{s-2\}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s-3\}})\right]+N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}H_{s-3,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s-3\}})+N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{s-2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}.

We can use this principle recursively, until we arrive at

H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}})\leq\sum_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}H_{1,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1\}})+N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}.

Furthermore, it can be shown in complete analogy to
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 4]
that

H_{1,n,{\bm{\gamma}}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1\}})=(\log 4)\gamma_{\{1\}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}(N-n-1)\leq(\log 4)\gamma_{\{1\}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}N,

which yields

\displaystyle H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}}_{\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\sum_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|+1}\gamma_{\{1\}\cup{\mathfrak{v}}}+N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{v}}\right|}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}},

as claimed.
∎

We can now use the general estimate in Theorem
4
to also show a more general version of

[
5
, Theorem 5]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
N=2^{n}
, with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
be general positive weights.
Furthermore, denote by
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
the corresponding generating vector constructed by Algorithm
1
. Then there exists a
constant
a>0
, which is independent of
s
and
N
, such that

T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})\leq(1+\log N)\,\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}(a\log N)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}.

(5)

Moreover, if the weights satisfy

\sum_{j\geq 1}\tilde{\gamma}_{j}<\infty,\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{j}:=\max_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}j-1\}}\frac{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}\cup\{j\}}}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{v}}},

then for any
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\delta}>0
, which is again independent of
s
and
N
, such that

T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})\leq\frac{C_{\delta}}{N^{1-\delta}}.

Proof.

We use the bound in Theorem
1
combined with the bound on
H_{s,n,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})
in Theorem
4
to obtain

\displaystyle T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}(\log 4+2(1+\log N))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}\,2|{\mathfrak{u}}|\,\left(1+2\log N\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\,(1+\log N)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}(\log 4+2(1+\log N))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}\,2^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|+1}\,\left(1+2\log N\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\,(1+\log N),

where we used that
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|\leq 2^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}
for non-empty
{\mathfrak{u}}
.
Thus, there exists a constant
a>0
such that

T_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}})\leq(1+\log N)\,\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{N}(a\log N)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|},

which is the bound in (
5
). The second claim in the theorem follows immediately by using
[
5
, Lemma 4]
.
∎

Next, we use Theorem
5
to show a more general version of

, Corollary 1]
. We formulate
this result for weights denoted by
{\bm{\eta}}
, and then describe two special cases in a remark below.

Corollary 1
.

Let
N=2^{n}
, with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
{\bm{\eta}}=\{\eta_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
be general positive weights, satisfying

\sum_{j\geq 1}\tilde{\eta}_{j}<\infty,\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{\eta}_{j}:=\max_{{\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}j-1\}}\frac{\eta_{{\mathfrak{v}}\cup\{j\}}}{\eta_{\mathfrak{v}}}.

(6)

Denote by
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
the generating vector constructed by Algorithm
1
for the weights
{\bm{\eta}}
.
Then, for any
\delta>0
and each
\alpha>1
, the worst-case error
e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}({\bm{z}})

in
E_{s,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}^{\alpha}

satisfies

e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}({\bm{z}})\leq\frac{C}{N^{\alpha}}+\frac{C_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{N^{\alpha(1-\delta)}}

with constants
C_{\alpha},C_{\delta}>0
, where
C_{\alpha}
may depend on
\alpha
, and
C_{\delta}
may depend on
\delta
,
but
C_{\alpha}
and
C_{\delta}
are independent of
s
and
N
.

Proof.

Since
N=2^{n}
and since by the formulation of Algorithm
1
all components of
{\bm{z}}
are odd, we have in particular that
\gcd(z_{j},N)=1
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Therefore, by Proposition
1
, the worst-case error
e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}({\bm{z}})
satisfies

e_{N,s,\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}({\bm{z}})\leq\frac{1}{N^{\alpha}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\eta_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\alpha}\,(4\zeta(\alpha))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}+T_{\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}(N,{\bm{z}}).

Next, we use that for
\alpha\geq 1
we have
\sum_{i}x_{i}^{\alpha}\leq(\sum_{i}x_{i})^{\alpha}
for
x_{i}\geq 0
, and we also use Theorem
5
.
This yields that for any
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\delta}>0
, which is independent of
s
and
N
, such that

T_{\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}(N,{\bm{z}})=\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{m}}\in M_{N,s}\\
{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;N)}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\eta}}^{\alpha}}({\bm{m}})}\leq\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{m}}\in M_{N,s}\\
{\bm{m}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;N)}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{1,{\bm{\eta}}}({\bm{m}})}\right)^{\!\!\alpha}=(T_{1,{\bm{\eta}}}(N,{\bm{z}}))^{\alpha}\leq\frac{C_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{N^{\alpha(1-\delta)}}.

Using Assumption (
6
) and standard arguments (or a similar reasoning as in the proof of

, Lemma 4]
), we also see that

\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}s\}}\eta_{\mathfrak{u}}\,(4\zeta(\alpha))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}<C_{\alpha}

for some constant

. This yields the result.
∎

Remark 3
.

If we choose
{\bm{\eta}}={\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
for positive weights
{\bm{\gamma}}
in Corollary
1
, then
the algorithm can be run independently of
\alpha
, and we get an error bound of almost optimal convergence order for the space

E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}^{\alpha}}^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>1
. In this sense, Algorithm
1
can be said to be universal with respect to
\alpha
.

If, on the other hand, we would like to have an error bound of almost optimal convergence order for the space
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
, then we need to
choose
{\bm{\eta}}={\bm{\gamma}}^{1/\alpha}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{1/\alpha}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
in Corollary
1
, and also run Algorithm
1

for
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1/\alpha}
, i.e., in this case Algorithm
1
is not universal.

3
Efficient implementation of Algorithm
1
for POD weights

In
[
5
, Section 4]
, it was outlined how Algorithm
1
can be efficiently implemented for the case of product weights, and
it was shown that the computation time of such an implementation is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(sN\log N)
. This makes the implementation competitive with
the fast implementation of the classical CBC algorithm, as proposed by Nuyens and Cools, see, e.g.,
[
13
,
12
]
, and also
[
2
]
.

In this section, we will outline that the CBC-DBD algorithm is also competitive for the case of
so-called POD (product-and-order-dependent) weights, which occur in applications of QMC methods to PDEs with random coefficients, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
.
Let us therefore assume that the weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
are POD weights, i.e., they are of the form

\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}=\Gamma_{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j},\quad\mbox{for}\ {\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}},

(7)

where
(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
\Gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
,
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, are positive reals. We set
\Gamma_{0}=1
and also treat the empty product as
one, such that
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
. It is known that there is a fast implementation of the classical CBC algorithm for POD weights, needing
{\mathcal{O}}(s^{2}N\log N)
operations, see again
[
2
]
. We will now show that a similar estimate holds for a suitable implementation of Algorithm
1
.
In the case of POD weights, we obtain from Definition
1
,

\displaystyle h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}(x):=\sum_{t=v}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\

\displaystyle+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}r-1\}}\!\!\!\!\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}\cup\{r\}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kz_{j}/2^{t})}\right)

\displaystyle+\left.\Gamma_{1}\gamma_{r}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\vphantom{\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1{\mathpunct{:}}r-1\}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=v}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\equiv 1{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;2)}\end{subarray}}^{2^{t}-1}\left[\vphantom{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:r-1\}\\

\displaystyle+\left.\left.\frac{\Gamma_{\ell+1}}{\Gamma_{\ell}}\gamma_{r}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:r-1\}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=v}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\equiv 1{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;2)}\end{subarray}}^{2^{t}-1}\left[\vphantom{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:r-1\}\\

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:r-1\}\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|=\ell\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{\ell}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kz_{j}/2^{t})}\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\gamma_{r}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\frac{\Gamma_{\ell+1}}{\Gamma_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:r-1\}\\

For short, we write, for
r\geq 2
,
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
,

, and odd

and note that

h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}(x)=\sum_{t=v}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\

(8)

Note that we have, for any

,

p_{r,\ell,t}(k)=p_{r-1,\ell,t}(k)+\frac{\Gamma_{\ell}}{\Gamma_{\ell-1}}\gamma_{r}\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kz_{r}/2^{t})}\right)\right)p_{r-1,\ell-1,t}(k),

for

and odd

Let

and corresponding odd indices

Next, we show the following proposition, which implies that the computation time and required memory
of Algorithm
2
are competitive with the classical fast CBC algorithm for POD weights.

Proposition 2
.

Let
n,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
N=2^{n}
. For a given set of positive POD weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
,
a generating vector
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
can be computed via Algorithm
2
using

{\mathcal{O}}(s^{2}N\log N)
operations and requiring
{\mathcal{O}}(Ns)
memory.

Proof.

Due to (
8
), the cost of evaluating
h_{r,n,v,{\bm{\gamma}}}(x)
is of order

\sum_{t=v}^{n}2^{t-1}(r-1).

Updating the
P_{r,\ell,v}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,r
and odd
k=1,3,\ldots,2^{v-1}
costs
{\mathcal{O}}(r2^{v-1})
operations.
Hence, the inner loop of Algorithm
2
over
v=2,\ldots,n
takes

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{v=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{t=v}^{n}2^{t-1}(r-1)+r2^{v-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(2^{n}(2+n(r-1)-r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(rN\log N)

operations. Consequently, the outer loop over
r=2,\ldots,s
takes
{\mathcal{O}}(s^{2}N\log N)
operations.

Regarding storage, initializing
P_{1,1,t}
costs
{\mathcal{O}}(N)
memory. Then, updating
P_{r,\ell,v}(k2^{n-v})
costs at most
{\mathcal{O}}(Ns)
memory.

Investigation of Ω c ​ c ​ b \Omega_{ccb} and Ω c ​ b ​ b \Omega_{cbb} baryons in Regge phenomenology
Juhi Oudichhya a, Keval Gandhi b, and Ajay Kumar Rai a

Abstract
Triply heavy baryons with quark content c ​ c ​ b ccb and c ​ b ​ b cbb are investigated within the framework of Regge phenomenology. With the assumption of linear Regge trajectories, we have extracted the relations between Regge parameters and baryon masses. Using these relations, we compute the ground state masses of Ω c ​ c ​ b \Omega_{ccb} and Ω c ​ b ​ b \Omega_{cbb} baryons. Further, the Regge slopes and intercepts are estimated for these baryons to obtain the excited state masses in the ( J , M 2 ) (J,M^{2}) and ( n , M 2 ) (n,M^{2}) planes. Also, using the obtained results we calculate the other properties like magnetic moment and radiative decay width of these triply heavy baryons. We compare our evaluated results with those obtained by the other theoretical approaches, and our results show a general agreement with them. The present study and our predictions will provide significant clues for future experimental research of these unseen triply heavy baryons.

In 1978, Nambu put forth the simplest hypothesis to account for the linear Regge trajectories

Since, there is no experimental evidence is found in the field of triply heavy baryons, but the various theoretical approaches and the predicted results shows that the baryons ranging from light to singly, doubly, and triply heavy follows the linear behaviour

The general form of linear Regge trajectories can be expressed as
[
8
,
9
,
48
]
,

J=\alpha(M)=a(0)+\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}M^{2},

(1)

where
a(0)
and
\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}
denotes the intercept and slope
of the Regge trajectory respectively.
These Regge intercepts and slopes for
different flavors of a baryon multiplet can be related by two relations:

the additivity of intercepts;

a_{ppr}(0)+a_{qqr}(0)=2a_{pqr}(0),

(2)

the additivity of inverse slopes;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{ppr}}+\frac{1}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{qqr}}=\frac{2}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{pqr}},

(3)

where
p,q,
and
r
represent the quark flavors.
These two relations were derived in a
q\overline{q}
string picture of hadrons
[
49
]
and are generalised for baryons as well by assuming the baryon as a quark-diquark string object
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
]
. Also, the ref.
[
51
]
showed that the additivity of inverse Regge slopes is consistent with the formal chiral and heavy quark limits for both mesons and baryons.
For a
detailed description of these two relations, see
[
50
,
48
]
and references therein.

Now using Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) together with Eq. (
3

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{qqr}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{ppr}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M^{2}_{qqr}}\times[(4M^{2}_{pqr}-M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr})

(4)

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{pqr}-M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{ppr}M^{2}_{qqr}}],

and,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{pqr}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{ppr}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4M^{2}_{pqr}}\times[(4M^{2}_{pqr}+M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr})

(5)

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{pqr}-M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{ppr}M^{2}_{qqr}}].

The above obtained Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{[(4M^{2}_{pqr}-M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr})+\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{pqr}-M^{2}_{ppr}-M^{2}_{qqr}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{ppr}M^{2}_{qqr}}]}{2M^{2}_{qqr}}

This is the significant relationship we have derived between the masses of different flavors of baryons. This relation can be used to estimate the mass of any baryon state if all other masses are known. In the present work the ground state masses of

Magnetic moment

The study of electromagnetic properties of baryons is a significant topic for theoretical as well as experimental work. The Magnetic moment is an intrinsic property that aids in understand the shape and other dynamics of transitions in decay modes. The magnetic moment expression can be obtained by operating the spin-flavour wave function to the
z
-component of the magnetic moment operator.
The generalised form of magnetic moment is given as
[
26
,
59
]
.

\mu_{B}=\sum_{i}\langle\phi_{sf}|\mu_{iz}|\phi_{sf}\rangle,

(16)

where,
i
=
c
or
b
.
\phi_{sf}
represents the spin-flavour wave function of the baryons.
The magnetic moment of the individual quark appears as,

\mu_{iz}=\dfrac{e_{i}}{2m_{i}^{eff}}\sigma_{iz},

(17)

here
e_{i}
is the quark charge,
\sigma_{iz}
is the spin of the respective constituent quark
corresponding to the spin flavour wave-function of the baryonic state and
m_{i}^{eff}
is the effective mass of the constituent quarks
[
45
,
59
]
. Since we have not used the constituent quark masses in our formalism to predict the mass spectra. Here we have taken the values of these quark masses,
m_{c}
= 1.275 GeV and
m_{b}
= 4.250 GeV, constituent masses of the charm and bottom quark respectively, from Particle data group (PDG)
[
54
]
.
Using the above equations we have calculated the ground state magnetic moments of
\Omega_{ccb}
and
\Omega_{cbb}
baryons. The final spin-flavor wave function and the evaluated results of magnetic moment along with the predictions of other theoretical approaches are shown in table

Radiative decay width

Radiative decay is the process of
\gamma
emission occurring as the spin state changes from
\frac{3}{2}
to
\frac{1}{2}
. For the better understanding of the intrinsic properties of heavy baryons, the experimental determination of the radiative decay width is important. The expression for radiative decay width is given by
[
59
]
,

\Gamma_{R}=\dfrac{k^{3}}{m_{p}^{2}}\dfrac{2}{2J+1}\dfrac{e^{2}}{4\pi}|\mu_{B\rightarrow B^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2},

(18)

where,

k=\dfrac{M^{2}_{B}-M^{2}_{B^{{}^{\prime}}}}{2M_{B}}

(19)

where
k
is the photon energy,
M_{B}
and
M_{B^{{}^{\prime}}}
are the masses of initial and final baryon state, respectively.
m_{p}
is the photon mass, and J is the initial angular momentum. Also,
\mu_{B\rightarrow B^{{}^{\prime}}}
is the transition magnetic moment which is expressed as,

\mu_{B\rightarrow B^{{}^{\prime}}}=\langle\phi_{B}|\mu_{B\rightarrow B^{{}^{\prime}}}|\phi_{B^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle

(20)

Here,
\phi_{B}
and
\phi_{B^{{}^{\prime}}}
are the wave functions of initial and final baryonic states respectively. Table

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we applied the Regge phenomenology for the calculation of the mass spectra of
\Omega_{ccb}
and
\Omega_{cbb}
baryons. Different results with large uncertainties are found in different theoretical approaches. The ground state masses of
\Omega_{ccb}
and
\Omega_{cbb}
baryons obtained from various studies
vary
in the range of 8.0-8.5 GeV and 11.2-11.7 GeV respectively and our obtained masses fall within this range. The excited states of these triply heavy
baryons have been
less explored theoretically. We compared our obtained results with the masses of various theoretical approaches. Here, we reached the conclusion that the low lying masses are in well agreement with the predictions of some theories. However discrepancies are found
in
excited states.
Since different approaches gives different predictions, hence more comparison of computations with experimental measurements is required.
Also, the mass values which are taken as inputs in the calculation are the experimental masses where available and the theoretical masses which we have calculated in our previous work.
The model’s reliability depend on the availability of experimental data that we have taken as a input mass, hence whatever the mass value predictions we have obtained in the present work follows a general trend with the results of other theoretical models.

Also, there are no fitting parameters involved in our formalism, the Regge parameters are calculated from the experimental masses in the present work. We have also seen that the results obtained in some theoretical approaches like hCQM
[
19
,
20
]
, does not follow the general trend. For example, the highest
1P
state has the lower mass with respect to the lower
1P
state.

Further, the other properties such as magnetic moment and radiative decay widths have been obtained using the mass value predictions. They also shown a general agreement with other the theoretical predictions, whereas some results shows slight discrepancies.

Since, there is no experimental data is available related to triply heavy baryons. Hence, our predicted mass values,
magnetic moment and decay widths
could be extremely valuable in future experimental searches.

The local moduli space of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system
Severin Bunk, Vicente Muñoz, C. S. Shahbazi

Abstract
. We study the deformation theory of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system on a principal bundle with a compact structure group over a compact manifold. We first construct, as an application of the general slice theorem of Diez and Rudolph [ 17 ] , a smooth slice in the tame Fréchet category for the coupled action of bundle automorphisms on metrics and connections. Using this result, together with a careful analysis of the linearization of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system, we realize the moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs modulo automorphism as an analytic set in a finite-dimensional tame Fréchet manifold, extending classical results of Koiso for Einstein metrics and Yang-Mills connections to the Einstein-Yang-Mills system. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of essential deformation of an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair, which we characterize in full generality and explore in more detail in the four-dimensional case, proving a decoupling result for trace deformations when the underlying Einstein-Yang-Mills pair is a Ricci-flat metric coupled to an anti-self-dual instanton. In particular, we find a novel obstruction that does not occur in the separate Einstein or Yang-Mills moduli problems. Finally, we prove that every essential deformation of the four-dimensional Einstein-Yang-Mills system based on a Calabi-Yau metric coupled to an instanton is of restricted type. Notable examples of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs include G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} or Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) holonomy metrics coupled to G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} or Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) instantons, respectively, or zero-slope polystable holomorphic vector bundles on Calabi-Yau manifolds.

Open problems

The present article aims at establishing the foundations of the deformation problem of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system and, as a consequence, it leaves open many possible lines of research. Most of these potential research lines are motivated by extending the theory of Einstien metrics currently available in the literature to the Einstein-Yang-Mills case. We highlight the following potential open problems:

•

Construct examples of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs whose Yang-Mills connection is not an instanton.

•

Classify homogenous Riemannian manifolds carrying natural Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs consisting of the normal homogeneous Riemannian metric and canonical homogeneous connection.

•

Study the vector space of essential deformations of an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair and the rigidity of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs on explicit examples.

•

Study the second variation of the Einstein-Yang-Mills functional and the stability of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs, see
[
39
,
43
,
44
]
and their references and citations for analog results in the Einstein case.

•

Generalize the results of
[
63
]
in order to investigate the second-order deformations of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system.

We hope to address some of these problems in the future.

2.1.
The Atiyah algebroid of a principal bundle

Let
\pi\colon P\to M
be a principal bundle over
M
with structure group
\mathrm{G}
. We denote the principal-bundle right action
\Psi\colon P\times\mathrm{G}\to P
of
\mathrm{G}

\Psi\colon P\times\mathrm{G}\to P\,,\qquad(p,o)\mapsto po\,,\qquad\Psi_{o}(p):=po\,.

We denote by
{\mathfrak{Ad}}(P)=P\times_{\operatorname{Ad}}\mathfrak{g}
the adjoint bundle of Lie algebras associated with

. Taking the differential of the action
\Psi
at each given element

\partial\Psi\colon TP\times\mathrm{G}\to TP\,,\qquad(X,o)\mapsto\mathrm{d}\Psi_{o}(X)\,.

In particular, for every

\mathrm{d}\Psi_{o}\colon TP\to TP

\Psi_{o}\colon P\to P
. The tangent bundle
\varrho\colon TP\to P
admits a trivialization atlas which is equivariant with respect to the action

\mathcal{A}_{P}:=\frac{TP}{\mathrm{G}}\,,

admits a unique vector bundle structure
\pi_{P}\colon\mathcal{A}_{P}\to M
over

such that the canonical quotient projection
\beta_{P}\colon TP\to\mathcal{A}_{P}
is a surjective submersion and a fiber bundle map making the following diagram commutative
[

The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(P)\subset\mathrm{Diff}(P)
is a closed tame Fréchet Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
, locally modeled on the tame Fréchet Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
with the standard bracket of vector fields or, equivalently,
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{P})
with Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}_{P}}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{G}(P)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is a closed tame Fréchet Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
, locally modeled on the Fréchet Lie algebra
\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
with Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{g}
be a
\mathrm{G}
-invariant metric on
P
and consider
\mathfrak{X}(P)
as a tame Fréchet space with respect to the family of Sobolev norms
\left\{\left\lVert-\right\rVert_{s}\right\}_{s>n+4}
constructed out of
\bar{g}
. Let
(X_{k})\subset\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
be a sequence of invariant vector fields on
P
converging to an element
X\in\mathfrak{X}(P)
. By definition, we have:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert X_{k}-X\right\rVert_{s}=0\qquad\forall\,\,s>n+4\,.

For every
o\in\mathrm{G}
we have:

0=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert X_{k}-X\right\rVert_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\Psi_{o}(X_{k})-\mathrm{d}\Psi_{o}\circ\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}_{o}(X)\right\rVert_{s}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert X_{k}-\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}_{o}(X)\right\rVert_{s}\qquad\forall\,\,s>n+4\,,

which implies:

\lim_{k\to\infty}X_{k}=\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}_{o}(X)\,,

for every
o\in\mathrm{G}
and
s>n+4
. By uniqueness of limits we conclude
\mathrm{d}\Psi_{o}(X)=X
whence
X\in\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
is closed in
\mathfrak{X}(P)
. Consequently, we obtain a short exact sequence of tame Fréchet spaces:

0\to\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to\mathfrak{X}(P)\to\mathfrak{X}(P)/\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to 0\,.

Short exact sequences do not necessarily split in the Fréchet category. Equivalently, closed subspaces of a Fréchet space do not necessarily admit a topological complement. Our case is, however, special since the Fréchet space
\mathfrak{X}(P)
is the projective limit of the Sobolev chain
\left\{\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)\,,\,s>n+4\right\}
, where
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)
is the completion of
\mathfrak{X}(P)
in the Sobolev norm
H_{s}=L^{2}_{s}
. In particular:

\mathfrak{X}(P)=\bigcap_{s>n+4}\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)

with the relative topology of the intersection as a subspace of each factor
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)
. Similarly, we denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
the completion of
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
in the Sobolev norm
H_{s}
. Let
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}_{\perp}\subset\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
in
\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)
with respect to its Hilbert inner product. Then:

f_{s}\colon\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\oplus\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}_{\perp}\to\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)\,,\qquad(X_{1},X_{2})\mapsto X_{1}+X_{2}

is an isomorphism of Hilbert spaces. The projective limit of
\left\{f_{s}\right\}_{s>n+4}
preserves continuity and bijectivity and therefore by the open mapping theorem for Fréchet spaces it defines a homeomorphism of tame Fréchet spaces:

f\colon\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\oplus\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}_{\perp}\to\mathfrak{X}(P)\,,\qquad\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}=\bigcap_{s>n+4}\mathfrak{X}_{s}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\,.

Hence
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
admits a topological complement in
\mathfrak{X}(P)
. Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathfrak{X}(P)
and
\phi\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathrm{Diff}(P)
be a Fréchet chart of
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
around the identity element. This chart is equivariant since
\bar{g}
is
\mathrm{G}
-invariant. Shrinking
\mathcal{U}
if necessary, we can write
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{o}\times\mathcal{V}_{o}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{o}\subset\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{o}\subset\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}_{\perp}
are neighborhoods of
0
in
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
and
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}_{\perp}
, respectively. Equivariance of
\phi\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathrm{Diff}(P)
amounts to the identities:

(2.4)

\mathrm{E}^{\bar{g}}_{po}(\mathrm{d}_{p}\Psi_{o}(X_{p}))=\Psi_{o}(\mathrm{E}^{\bar{g}}_{p}(X_{p}))\,,\qquad\forall\,\,p\in P\quad\forall\,\,o\in\mathrm{G}\quad\forall\,\,X\in\mathfrak{X}(P)\,,

where
\mathrm{E}^{\bar{g}}\colon TP\to P
denotes the exponential map associated to
\bar{g}
. The previous equation in turn implies that:

\phi(\mathcal{U}_{o}\times\left\{0\right\})\subseteq\phi(\mathcal{U})\cap\operatorname{Aut}(P)\,.

Conversely, if
X\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\phi(X)\in\phi(\mathcal{U})\cap\operatorname{Aut}(P)
then we have:

\Psi_{o}(\mathrm{E}^{\bar{g}}_{p}(X_{p}))=\mathrm{E}^{\bar{g}}_{po}(X_{po})\,,

which, using Equation (
2.4
), gives
\mathrm{d}_{p}\Psi_{o}(X_{p})=X_{po}
for every
p\in P
and every
o\in\mathrm{G}
. Hence
X\in\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
and
\phi(\mathcal{U}_{o}\times\left\{0\right\})=\phi(\mathcal{U})\cap\operatorname{Aut}(P)
whence
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is a tame Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
.

It remains to show the closedness. Let now
\left\{u_{k}\right\}
be a sequence in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
converging to an element
u\in\mathrm{Diff}(P)
. Since the convergence is uniform it implies
pointwise
convergence. Therefore:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(p)=u(p)

for every
p\in P
. Hence:

u(po)=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(po)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(u_{k}(p)o)=(\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(p))o=u(p)o

for every
p\in P
and every
o\in\mathrm{G}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is closed in
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
. The fact that
\mathcal{G}(P)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is a closed tame Fréchet Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
can be proven similarly after noticing that
\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is closed inside
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{P})
by using the splitting
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
with bracket (
2.2
). In fact,
\mathcal{G}(P)
is not only a projective limit of Hilbert manifolds but of Hilbert groups, since the Lie bracket (
2.2
) restricted to
\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
pointwise identifies with the bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
and involves no
loss of derivatives
.
∎

Note that, in particular, the connected components of the identity
\operatorname{Aut}^{o}(P)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and
\mathrm{Diff}^{o}(M)\subset\mathrm{Diff}(M)
are also tame Fréchet Lie groups. We have the following short exact sequence of tame Fréchet Lie groups:

1\to\mathcal{G}(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathrm{Diff}^{\prime}(M)\to 1\,.

where
\mathrm{Diff}^{\prime}(M)
denotes the group of diffeomorphisms that can be covered by elements in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and that includes all those isotopic to the identity. Taking the differential of this sequence at the identity we obtain a corresponding short exact sequence of Fréchet Lie algebras:

(2.5)

0\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{P})=\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to\mathfrak{X}(M)\to 0

fitting in the following commutative diagram with vertical arrows given by the exponential map in the Fréchet Lie group category:

\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\mathcal{G}(P)
is fiberwise induced by the standard exponential map of
\mathrm{G}
. On the other hand, the short exact sequence (

) can be obtained by applying the global section functor to the Atiyah sequence of

, which therefore can be interpreted as encoding the infinitesimal symmetries of
P
. A choice of smooth connection

determines uniquely a smooth splitting of the sequence (

s_{A}\colon\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\,,

. Given an element

3.
The Einstein-Yang-Mills system on a principal bundle

In this section, we consider the Einstein-Yang-Mills system on a principal bundle and study its action functional, linearization, and some basic examples. The conventions for the various curvature operators and linear operations occurring below are summarized in Appendix
A
.

3.1.
Configuration space and action functional

Let
P
be a principal bundle over
M
with structure group
\mathrm{G}
. Let
\mathfrak{c}
be a non-degenerate bilinear pairing on
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
induced by a positive definite adjoint-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
, which we denote for simplicity by the same symbol. The configuration space of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system is the following product space:

\mathrm{Conf}(P)=\mathrm{Met}(M)\times\mathcal{C}(P)\,,

where
\mathrm{Met}(M)
denotes the convex cone of Riemannian metrics on
M
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
denotes the affine space of connections on
P
, both considered as tame Fréchet manifolds. The tame Fréchet manifold
\mathrm{Met}(M)
is is locally modeled on
\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
whereas the tame Fréchet manifold
\mathcal{C}(P)
is locally modeled on
\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Hence, we consider
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
as a product of tame Fréchet manifolds. Given a connection
A\in\mathcal{C}(P)
, we denote by
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
its curvature. The tangent space
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
of
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
at
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is given by the following Fréchet space:

\displaystyle T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)=\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\oplus\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\,.

The Einstein-Yang-Mills system determined by
\mathfrak{c}
on
P
is defined as the system of partial differential equations obtained through the variational problem defined by the following action functional:

(3.1)

\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathbb{R}\,,\quad(g,A)\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}[g,A]=\int_{M}\left(s^{g}+\kappa|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\right)\nu_{g}\,,

where
\kappa\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
is a sign,
\nu_{g}
denotes the Riemannian volume form associated to
g
and
|{-}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
denotes the norm induced by
g
and
\mathfrak{c}
on the bundle of polyforms taking values in the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
. The action
\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
is usually referred to as the
Einstein-Yang-Mills functional
in the literature. We denote its associated Lagrangian density by:

\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Omega^{n}(M)\,,\quad(g,A)\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}[g,A]=(s^{g}+\kappa|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}})\nu_{g}\,.

which is a smooth map of Fréchet manifolds. The first term in the previous Lagrangian is usually referred to as the
Einstein-Hilbert term
whereas the second term is usually referred to as the
Yang-Mills term
.

Remark 3.1
.

In Lorentzian signature the relative sign of the Einstein-Hilbert and Yang-Mills terms in
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
cannot be chosen at will and it is unambiguously determined to be negative by requiring positivity for the kinetic energy of the Yang-Mills
field
. Therefore, for
\kappa=-1
we can think of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
as the restriction of the Lorentzian Einstein-Yang-Mills Lagrangian to direct product configurations on a manifold of the type
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times M
equipped with the pullback bundle of a principal bundle over
M
. The choice of sign
\kappa=1
on the other hand does not seem to have a clear interpretation in relation to the physics origin of Einstein-Yang-Mills theory, although it has been preferred in the mathematical literature
[
4
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
. In the following, we will simultaneously consider both cases
\kappa\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
.

3.2.
The Einstein-Yang-Mills equations of motion

For further reference we introduce a linear operation
\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
on pairs of forms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Omega^{k}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
taking values in
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
as follows:

(3.2)

\alpha_{1}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\alpha_{2}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\quad(\alpha_{1}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\alpha_{2})(v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle\alpha_{1}(v_{1}),\alpha_{2}(v_{2})\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\langle\alpha_{1}(v_{2}),\alpha_{2}(v_{1})\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\Big),

for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in T^{\ast}M
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
is the positive definite metric on
\wedge T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{G}_{P}
induced naturally by
g
and
\mathfrak{c}
and whose associated norm was denoted by
|{-}|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
in Subsection
3.1
. For standard differential forms not taking values in
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
we introduce an analogous operation denoted simply by
\circ_{g}
. Similarly, for symmetric tensors
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
we define:

\tau_{1}\circ_{g}\tau_{2}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\quad(\tau_{1}\circ_{g}\tau_{2})(v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(g(\tau_{1}(v_{1}),\tau_{2}(v_{2}))+g(\tau_{1}(v_{2}),\tau_{2}(v_{1}))\Big)

The metric induced by
g
on the tensor bundles of
M
will be denoted again by
g
for ease of notation.

Lemma 3.2
.

A pair
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is a critical point of
\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
if and only if it satisfies the following equations:

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{g}-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}\,g=\frac{\kappa}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-\kappa\,F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,,\\[10.0pt]
\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}=0\,,\end{array}

where
\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\colon\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
denotes the formal adjoint of the exterior covariant derivative determined by
A
.

Proof.

The differential:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\oplus\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to C^{\infty}(M)

of
\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to C^{\infty}(M)
at the point
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
evaluated on the tangent vector
(h,a)\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\oplus\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is given by the usual formula as follows:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}(h,a)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}[g_{t},A_{t}]|_{t=0}\,,

where
t\mapsto(g_{t},A_{t})
is a smooth curve in
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
satisfying:

(g_{t},A_{t})|_{t=0}=(g,A)\,,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(g_{t},A_{t})|_{t=0}=(h,a)\,.

By linearity in the arguments of
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
, we can work separately on
h
and
a
. Take first a vector
(0,a)
and consider the curve
t\mapsto(g_{t},A_{t})=(g,A+ta)
. Then
F_{A_{t}}=F_{A}+t\,d_{A}a+t^{2}a\wedge a
. Hence,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F_{A_{t}}|_{t=0}=\mathrm{d}_{A}a
and so:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}[g,A_{t}]|_{t=0}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(s^{g}+\kappa\langle F_{A_{t}},F_{A_{t}}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\right)\nu_{g}|_{t=0}=2\kappa\langle F_{A},d_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\nu_{g}\,.

Therefore, integrating by parts we obtain:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}(0,a)=\int_{M}2\kappa\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\nu_{g}=\int_{M}2\kappa\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A},a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\nu_{g}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}
is the formal adjoint of the exterior covariant derivative
\mathrm{d}_{A}\colon\Omega^{r}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Omega^{r+1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
determined by
A
. Imposing
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}(0,a)=0
for every
a\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
we conclude that
(g,A)
is a critical point of
\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
only if:

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}=0\,.

Now we take a vector
(h,0)
, given by the curve
t\mapsto(g+th,A)
for
|t|<\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough. By Lemma
A.1
we have:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(s^{g_{t}})|_{t=0}=\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h-g(h,\operatorname{Ric}^{g})\,,

which, together with Equation (
A.1
), implies:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(s^{g_{t}}\,\nu_{g_{t}})|_{t=0}=(\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\operatorname{div}_{g}(\operatorname{div}_{g}(h))-g(h,\operatorname{Ric}^{g})+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)s^{g})\nu_{g}\,.

Thus, we conclude:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|_{t=0}\int_{M}s^{g_{t}}\,\nu_{g_{t}}=\int_{M}g(h,-\operatorname{Ric}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}s^{g}g)\nu_{g}\,,

since the integral of the Laplacian and the integral of the divergence vanish on a compact manifold. Regarding the derivative of the Yang-Mills term in the action functional, we compute:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g_{t},\mathfrak{c}}|_{t=0}=-g(h,F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})\,,

where the operation
F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
is defined in
(
3.2
). Altogether, we obtain:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}(h,0)=\int_{M}g\Big(h,-\operatorname{Ric}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\big(s^{g}+\kappa|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\big)g-\kappa\,(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})\Big)\nu_{g}\,.

Therefore, by the discussion above,
(g,A)
is a critical point of
\mathcal{S}_{P,\mathfrak{c}}
if and only if it satisfies the two equations:

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Ric}^{g}-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}\,g=\frac{\kappa}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-\kappa F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}

simultaneously.
∎

Equations (
3.3
) define the
Einstein-Yang-Mills system
. The first equation in (
3.3
) will be referred to as the
Einstein equation
, whereas the second equation in (
3.3
) will be referred to as the
Yang-Mills
equation.

Definition 3.3
.

A pair
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
and a connection
A
on
P
is an
Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
if it solves the Einstein-Yang-Mills system (
3.3
).

If
(g,A)
is an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair such that
F_{A}\neq 0
we will say that
(g,A)
is
non-trivial
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
n=2
then
2\,\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=s^{g}
and the Einstein equation reduces to
|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}=0
. Therefore, every Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
in this dimension consists of a flat metric on
T^{2}
together with a flat connection on
P
. Consequently, in the following, we will assume
n>2
.

For further reference we introduce the
Einstein tensor

\mathbb{G}^{g}
of a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
as:

\mathbb{G}^{g}=\mathrm{Ric}^{g}-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}g\,.

Note that
\nabla^{g\ast}\mathbb{G}^{g}=0
for every
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
.

3.3.
Elementary properties of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs

Taking the trace of the Einstein equation with respect to
g
we obtain:

s^{g}-\frac{1}{2}ns^{g}=\frac{\kappa}{2}n|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-2\kappa\,|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,,

using that
\mathrm{Tr}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})=2|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
.
Solving for
s^{g}
we obtain the following prescription for the scalar curvature of every Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
:

s^{g}=\frac{\kappa(n-4)}{(2-n)}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,.

Note that this equation already shows that the cases
\kappa=-1
and
\kappa=1
may be non-equivalent since
\kappa
prescribes the sign of the scalar curvature and the latter may be obstructed in the positive curvature case
[
35
,
52
]
. Plugging the previous equation back into the Einstein equation, we obtain:

\operatorname{Ric}^{g}=\kappa\left(\frac{1}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\right)\,.

This form of the Einstein equation is sometimes more convenient from the analytic point of view since it only involves the Ricci curvature operator instead of both the Ricci and scalar curvature operators.

Definition 3.5
.

The
energy-momentum tensor
of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system is the following smooth map of Fréchet manifolds:

\mathcal{T}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\qquad(g,A)\mapsto\frac{\kappa}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-\kappa\,F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,,

where
\mathfrak{c}
is the given inner product on
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
.

In terms of the energy-momentum tensor, the Einstein equation of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system adopts the standard form:

\mathbb{G}^{g}=\mathcal{T}(g,A)\,,

where
\mathbb{G}^{g}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
is the Einstein tensor of
g
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The following formula holds:

\nabla^{g\ast}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})(v)=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A},\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\frac{1}{2}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

for every
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
be a local orthonormal frame with dual local coframe
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{n})
around a point
p\in M
, chosen so that
\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}e_{j}(p)=0
. Given
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
we compute at
p
:

\displaystyle-\nabla^{g\ast}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})\,(e_{i},v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}\langle\iota_{e_{i}}F_{A},\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\langle\iota_{e_{i}}F_{A},\iota_{\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\langle\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{i}}F_{A},e^{i}\wedge\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\langle F_{A},e^{i}\wedge\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{i}}(\iota_{v}F_{A})\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\langle\iota_{e_{i}}F_{A},\iota_{\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\langle\iota_{e_{i}}\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{i}}F_{A},\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\langle F_{A},e^{i}\wedge\iota_{v}\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{i}}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}F_{A},\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

where
\nabla^{g,A}
denotes the tensor product of the Levi-Civita connection and the connection induced by
A
on
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
and we have used the Bianchi identity
\mathrm{d}_{A}F_{A}=0
.
∎

By the previous Lemma we immediately obtain the following result for the energy momentum tensor of the Einstein-Yang-Mills theory.

Proposition 3.7
.

The divergence of the energy-momentum tensor evaluated at
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is given by:

-\nabla^{g\ast}(\mathcal{T}(g,A))(v)=\kappa\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A},\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,,\qquad v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,.

In particular,
\nabla^{g\ast}(\mathcal{T}(g,A))=0
whenever
A
is a Yang-Mills connection.

Therefore, the Yang-Mills equation that occurs as part of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system guarantees that the energy-momentum tensor is divergence-free when evaluated on pairs
(g,A)
with
A
Yang-Mills, as expected by physical consistency. For further reference, we introduce the
reversed energy-momentum tensor
as the following smooth map of tame Fréchet manifolds:

(3.4)

\hat{\mathcal{T}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\qquad(g,A)\mapsto\kappa\Big(\frac{1}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\Big)\,,

which is sometimes more convenient than the standard energy-momentum tensor for computations.

3.4.
Examples of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs

Solutions to the Einstein-Yang-Mills equations on a compact manifold are hard to come by except for some notable exceptions. A particular class of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs
(g,A)
can be characterized by requiring that the Ricci tensor of
g
and the reversed energy-momentum tensor of
(g,A)
are both equally proportional to
g
, that is:

(3.5)

\operatorname{Ric}^{g}=\Lambda g=\kappa\Big(\frac{1}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\Big)\,,

for a constant
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
(M,g)
is an Einstein manifold. Within the previous ansatz assume that
A
is not flat. We then have the following distinguished cases:

•

If
n=3
then Equation (
3.5
) reduces to:

\kappa\Lambda g=|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}=\ast_{g}F_{A}\otimes_{\mathfrak{c}}\ast_{g}F_{A}\,,

where
\ast_{g}F_{A}\otimes_{\mathfrak{c}}\ast_{g}F_{A}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
is defined by taking the usual tensor product together with the norm induced by
\mathfrak{c}
on the adjoint bundle. Therefore, no solution exists in dimension three. In fact, when
n=3
it is more natural to assume that
(M,g)
is a metric contact manifold and
g
is
\eta
-Einstein. This produces a natural ansatz for
\operatorname{Ric}^{g}
better suited to fit with the structure of the reversed energy-momentum tensor and can be used to produce Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs, at least when
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{U}(1)
.

•

If
n=4
then the trace of (
3.5
) implies
\Lambda=0
and therefore
g
is Ricci-flat. Furthermore:

F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}=\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g\,.

This equation is satisfied automatically by every
instanton
, namely by every self-dual or anti-self-dual connection. Since instantons satisfy the Yang-Mills equation, every pair
(g,A)
consisting of a Ricci-flat metric
g
and an instanton
A
defines an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. In particular, every poly-stable holomorphic vector bundle over a K3 surface or complex torus canonically defines an associated Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. This will be our main case of study in Section
6
, where we consider the local moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs around a stable holomorphic bundle over K3.

•

Assume
n>4
. By taking the trace of (
3.5
) we conclude that
\Lambda
is necessarily non-zero and its sign is opposite to
\kappa
. In this
generic
dimension it is not evident that (
3.5
) admits solutions. Nonetheless, examples can be found on symmetric spaces equipped with their normal homogeneous Riemannian metric and canonical homogeneous connection
[
34
]
.

3.5.
Lift to the total space
P

Let
\mathrm{Met}(P)
denote the Fréchet manifold of Riemannian metrics on
P
. For every Killing form
\mathfrak{c}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of the structure group
\mathrm{G}
of
P
we have a canonical map:

\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)\,,

which, to every pair
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, associates the following Riemannian metric on
P
:

\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{g,A}(X_{1},X_{2})=g(\mathrm{d}\pi(X_{1}),\mathrm{d}\pi(X_{2}))+\mathfrak{c}(A(X_{1}),A(X_{2}))\,,\quad X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(P)\,,

where
\mathrm{d}\pi\colon TP\to TM
is the differential of the principal bundle projection
\pi\colon P\to M
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For every Killing form
\mathfrak{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
and every pair
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
the Riemannian metric is
\mathrm{G}
-invariant and satisfies:

\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{g,A}(D\Psi(x_{1}),D\Psi(x_{2}))|_{p}=\mathfrak{c}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
p\in P
.

Remark 3.9
.

Conversely, every
\mathrm{G}
-invariant Riemannian metric on
P
that satisfies the previous equation is of the form
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{g,A}
for a certain unique
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
.

Proof.

The result follows directly from the standard properties satisfied by the principal connections of
P
, namely:

\Psi^{\ast}_{o}A=\operatorname{Ad}_{o^{-1}}(A)\,,\quad A(D\Psi(x))=x\,,\quad o\in\mathrm{G}\,,\,\,x\in\mathfrak{g}\,,

together with the invariance of
\mathfrak{c}
under adjoint transformations.
∎

We will refer to elements in the image of
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
as
\mathfrak{c}
-
adapted
Riemannian metrics on
P
. These metrics are sometimes called
Kaluza-Klein metrics
in the literature
[
9
]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

For every Killing form
\mathfrak{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
the map
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
is smooth, tame and injective. Its image
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is a smooth, closed, tame Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Met}(P)
.

Proof.

The fact that
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
is injective follows from the non-degeneracy of
\mathfrak{c}
together with the fact that
\mathrm{d}\pi
is a linear isomorphism when restricted to the horizontal distribution determined by
A
. Since
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
is locally a polynomial with smooth coefficients in the components of
(g,A)
, it is clearly smooth. The fact that
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
is tame follows from the fact that partial differential operators between spaces of smooth sections are tame
[
70
]
. The set of smooth metrics
\mathrm{Met}(P)
is a smooth manifold modeled on the tame Fréchet space
\Gamma(T^{\ast}P\odot T^{\ast}P)
and it is actually a contractible open subset of the latter. The vector space
\Gamma(T^{\ast}P\odot T^{\ast}P)^{\mathrm{G}}
of all
\mathrm{G}
-invariant symmetric tensors on
P
is a closed vector subspace of
\Gamma(T^{\ast}P\odot T^{\ast}P)
and hence a tame Fréchet vector space itself. Consider the following continuous map:

f\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}P\odot T^{\ast}P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to\Gamma(V^{\ast}\odot V^{\ast})\,,\qquad H\mapsto H|_{\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}}-\mathfrak{c}(\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}_{\pi}(-),\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}_{\pi}(-))

where
V^{\ast}
is the vector bundle dual to the vertical distribution
V
in
TP
. We have:

\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})=\mathrm{Met}(P)\cap f^{-1}(0)

which implies, since
\mathrm{Met}(P)
is an open subset of a tame Fréchet space and
f^{-1}(0)
is a closed affine subspace, that
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
is a tame Fréchet manifold modeled on the tame Fréchet space of
\mathrm{G}
-invariant symmetric two-tensors on
P
that vanish on
\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Therefore, the map
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
gives a natural, smooth, and tame correspondence between
\mathfrak{c}
-adapted metrics on
P
and elements of the configuration space of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system on
(P,\mathfrak{c})
.

4.
A slice theorem for Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs

4.1.
Preliminaries

The Fréchet Lie group of automorphisms
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
acts smoothly on the tame Fréchet manifold
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
as follows:

\Phi\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\times\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathrm{Conf}(P)\,,\qquad(g,A,u)\mapsto(f^{\ast}_{u}g,u^{\ast}(A))

where
f^{\ast}_{u}g
denotes the pullback of
g
along the diffeomorphism
f_{u}\colon M\to M
covered by
u\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and
u^{\ast}(A)\in\Omega^{1}(P,\mathfrak{g})
is the pullback of
A
by the diffeomorphism
u\colon P\to P
. Hence, we have that
\Phi
is a smooth tame action.

The goal of this section is to prove a slice theorem for the aforementioned action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. In order to achieve this, the correspondence between elements
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and
\mathfrak{c}
-adapted metrics on
P
established by
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
will be particularly useful. For every
u\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
we have a commutative diagram of Fréchet manifolds:

(4.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Conf}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Met}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Conf}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Met}(P)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{u}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}}

where:

\Phi_{u}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Conf}(P)\,,\qquad(g,A)\mapsto(f^{\ast}_{u}g,u^{\ast}A)\,,

and
u^{\ast}\colon\mathrm{Met}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
denotes the action of the diffeomorphism
u\in\mathrm{Diff}(P)
via pullback. Therefore, the smooth action
\Phi
of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
can be equivalently studied through the pullback action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on the metrics lying in the image of
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
. In particular,
u^{\ast}\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{g,A}\in\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
for every
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and
u\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
.

4.2.
Isotropy subgroups in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and
\mathcal{G}(P)

The
isotropy group

\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(P)
of an element
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is by definition its group of symmetries, namely:

\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}:=\left\{u\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)\,\,\,|\,\,\,(f^{\ast}_{u}g,u^{\ast}(A))=(g,A)\right\}\,.

For further reference, we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{A}\subset\mathcal{G}(P)
the isotropy group of the connection
A
on
P
, namely the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(P)
that preserves
A
under the natural action by pullback.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
be a connection on
P
and fix a point
m\in M
. Then, the map:

\mathcal{I}_{A}\to C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})]\,,\quad u\mapsto u_{m}\coloneqq u_{|P_{m}}

is an isomorphism of groups, where
C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})]
denotes the centralizer of the holonomy of
A
at
m\in M
inside the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})\cong\mathrm{G}
of the fiber
P_{m}
.

Proof.

We first show that the map is well-defined.
Consider the holonomy group
\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A)
of
A
based at a fixed point
m\in M
. A gauge transformation
u\in\mathcal{G}(P)
preserves
A
if and only if it preserves its associated horizontal distribution, namely if and only if it commutes with the parallel transport prescribed by
A
. Therefore, considering loops based at
m\in M
it follows that
u_{m}\in\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})
commutes with
\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A)
for every
u\in\mathcal{I}_{A}
.
Next, we show that the map is injective. Since
M
is connected, if
u\in\mathcal{I}_{A}
is trivial at
m\in M
then
u
acts trivially on
P
. In particular, the evaluation map:

\mathcal{I}_{A}\ni u\mapsto u_{m}\in\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})\,,

is an injective homomorphism. Consequently, the image of
\mathcal{I}_{A}
in
\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})
commutes with
\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A)
.
Finally, we check that the maps is also surjective. Every element
u_{0}\in C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})]
can be extended by the parallel transport prescribed by
A
to a unique gauge transformation
u\in\mathcal{G}(P)
such that
u_{m}=u_{0}
. This extension is independent of the paths used to connect any two given points precisely because
u_{0}
belongs to
C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\operatorname{Aut}(P_{m})]
.
∎

Given a fixed point
m\in M
, by the previous lemma we obtain, c.f. Equation (
2.5
), that
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
fits non-canonically into the following short exact sequence:

(4.2)

1\to C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\mathrm{G}]\to\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\to\mathrm{Iso}(M,g)^{\prime}\to 1\,,

where
\mathrm{Iso}(M,g)^{\prime}
denotes the Lie subgroup of the isometry group of
(M,g)
that can be covered by elements in
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
. Recall that, since both
C[\mathrm{Hol}_{m}(A),\mathrm{G}]
and
\mathrm{Iso}(M,g)^{\prime}
are finite-dimensional Lie groups it follows that
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
is a finite-dimensional Lie group. Furthermore,
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
identifies
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
with the intersection of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and the isometry group of
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{(g,A)}
in
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
. Since the latter is compact and
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is closed in
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
we conclude that
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
is a compact Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
. Using this, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
70
, Page 44]

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. The quotient
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\backslash\operatorname{Aut}(P)
has a unique tame manifold structure such that:

•

The natural projection
\Upsilon\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\backslash\operatorname{Aut}(P)
satisfies
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{u}\Upsilon)=(\mathrm{d}_{e}\mathrm{R}_{u})(T_{e}\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)})
, where
\mathrm{R}_{u}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)
denotes right multiplication.

•

The projection
\Upsilon\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\backslash\operatorname{Aut}(P)
admits local smooth tame sections.

Furthermore, a map
f\colon\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\backslash\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to Y
, where
Y
is a smooth tame Fréchet manifold is smooth tame if and only if
f\circ\Upsilon\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to Y
is smooth tame.

Therefore, in the terminology of
[
17
]
the subgroup
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is a principal Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
.

4.3.
The infinitesimal action and its adjoint map

Given
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, we introduce the
orbit map
of
(g,A)
as the following smooth map of Fréchet manifolds:

\Phi_{(g,A)}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathrm{Conf}(P)\,,\qquad u\mapsto(f^{\ast}_{u}g,u^{\ast}(A))\,.

With this definition, the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)}\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
passing through
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is simply given by
\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{(g,A)})
.
Recall from Proposition
2.7
that the Lie algebra of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is canonically identified with
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{G}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The differential
\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}\colon\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
of
\Phi_{(g,A)}
at the identity in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is given by:

\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}(v,\tau)=(\mathcal{L}_{v}g,\mathrm{d}_{A}\tau+\iota_{v}F_{A})

where we identify
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
through the splitting of the Atiyah sequence (
2.1
) determined by the horizontal distribution associated to
A
. In particular,
T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)}\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is a closed vector subspace of
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
.

Proof.

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and consider a smooth curve
u_{t}\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
such that
u_{0}=\operatorname{Id}
and:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{t}|_{t=0}=X\in\mathfrak{aut}(P)=\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\,.

Using the connection
A
we obtain, as explained in Section
2.1
, a canonical isomorphism of vector bundles
\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
identifying
X=v\oplus\tau
. Here
v
is the unique vector field on
M
lifting through
A
to the horizontal projection of
X
, and
\tau
is the vertical projection understood as a section of the adjoint bundle of
P
. Hence:

\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}(v,\tau)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi_{(g,A)}(u_{t})|_{t=0}=(\mathcal{L}_{v}g,\mathcal{L}_{X}A)\,.

Writing
X=X_{H}\oplus X_{V}
in terms of its horizontal
X_{H}
and vertical
X_{V}
components with respect the decomposition of
TP
determined by
A
we compute:

\mathcal{L}_{X}A=\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}A+\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}A=\mathrm{d}(A(X_{V}))+\Omega_{A}(X)-\frac{1}{2}[A,A](X)=\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}+[A,\bar{\tau}]+\Omega_{A}(X)\,,

where
\Omega_{A}
is the curvature of
A
as a Lie algebra valued two-form on
P
and
\bar{\tau}=A(X_{V})=\mathrm{d}\Psi^{-1}(X_{V})
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-valued function on
P
corresponding to
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Projecting the previous equation to
M
we conclude.
∎

In order to proceed, we endow
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
with the
L^{2}
metric
[\![\cdot,\cdot]\!]_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
determined by
g
and
\mathfrak{c}
, which in our situation is explicitly given at the point
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
by the following expression:

[\![(h,a),(h,a)]\!]_{g,\mathfrak{c}}:=\int_{M}(g(h,h)+|a|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}^{2})\nu_{g}\,,

for every
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. This defines a
weak
Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, invariant under
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
transformations. The symbol of
\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}\colon\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is injective, which implies the following
L^{2}
orthogonal decomposition with closed factors:

T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})\oplus\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}\,,

where
(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
denotes the formal
L^{2}
-adjoint of
\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The adjoint differential operator
(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathfrak{X}(P)^{\mathrm{G}}
on
M
is given by:

(4.3)

(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}(h,a)=\big(2(\nabla^{g\ast}h)^{\sharp_{g}}-(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{\sharp_{g}},\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a\big)\,.

In particular, the orthogonal complement of
T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)}
in
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is given by:

(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}=\left\{(g,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\,\,|\,\,2(\nabla^{g\ast}h)^{\sharp_{g}}=(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{\sharp_{g}}\,,\,\,\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a=0\right\}\,.

Here we have defined
a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
by
(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})(v)=-\langle a,\iota_{v}F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}
for every
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. Consider
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and
X\in\mathfrak{X}(P)^{G}
in the presentation
X=v\oplus\tau
determined by
A
, we compute:

\displaystyle[\![(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})(v,\tau),(h,a)]\!]_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

\displaystyle=[\![(\mathcal{L}_{v}g,\mathrm{d}_{A}\tau+\iota_{v}F_{A}),(h,a)]\!]_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(g(\mathcal{L}_{v}g,h)+\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}\tau+\iota_{v}F_{A},a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}})\nu_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(g(v,2\nabla^{g\ast}h)+\langle\tau,\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\langle F_{A},v\wedge a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}})\nu_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(g(v,2\nabla^{g\ast}h)+\langle\tau,\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\langle a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},v\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}})\nu_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(g(v,2\nabla^{g\ast}h-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})+\langle\tau,\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}})\nu_{g}

\displaystyle=[\![(v,\tau),(2\nabla^{g\ast}h-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a)]\!]_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

and hence we conclude.
∎

The tame Fréchet space
(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is a natural candidate of
infinitesimal slice
for the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. We will verify that this is indeed the case in the following subsection.

4.4.
The slice theorem

In this subsection, we prove a smooth local slice theorem for the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
modelled on the tame Fréchet space
(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}
. We take
[
17
, Definition 2.2]
as our definition of a slice in the tame Fréchet category. The existence of a slice around every
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
can be obtained as an application of the general theorem proved in
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
for tame Fréchet smooth actions. The hypothesis of
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
as well as an explicit proof of the existence of a slice follows by using the celebrated slice theorem for
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
on
\mathrm{Met}(P)
in the Fréchet category
[
16
,
20
,
33
,
70
]
, together with the Fréchet tame identification of
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
with the image of
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
in
\mathrm{Met}(P)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. Then, there exists a slice
\mathbb{S}\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
around
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
which is the image of an equivariant diffeomorphism
\mathrm{E}\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{S}
, where
\mathcal{U}\subset(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}
is an open neighbourhood of
0
in
(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
is a tame Fréchet Lie group acting tamely and properly on the tame Fréchet manifold
\mathrm{Met}(P)
, see
[
33
]
and
[
70
, Page 68]
. Consider
\operatorname{Aut}(P)\subset\mathrm{Diff}(P)
acting on
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})\subset\mathrm{Met}(P)
via diffeomorphisms. By Proposition
2.7
,
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is a closed tame Fréchet Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Diff}(P)
and by Proposition
3.10
,
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
is a closed tame Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Met}(P)
preserved by
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
. Hence, the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
is also tame and proper. This proves point
(i)
in
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
. In particular, given
\bar{g}\in\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
, its orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
under the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}\backslash\operatorname{Aut}(P)
, which by Proposition
4.2
is a tame Fréchet manifold. Again by Proposition
4.2
, we conclude that
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
is a principal tame Fréchet Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
, which proves point
(ii)
in
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
. The fact that the differential of the orbit map
\Phi_{(g,A)}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(P)\to\mathrm{Conf}(P)
has injective symbol, proven explicitly in Lemma
5.7
below, implies that the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
is a tame regular map, which yields point
(iii)
in
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
. By
[
17
, Proposition 3.17]
this shows, in turn, that the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
is a smooth closed submanifold of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and that the
L^{2}
orthogonal complements of
T\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
inside
T\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})|_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}}
assemble into a smooth split normal subbundle
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
of the latter. In particular,
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
is a smooth tame subbundle of
T\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})|_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}}
complementary to
T\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
. Furthermore,
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
inherits a smooth exponential map from that of
\mathrm{Met}(P)
, whose restriction to a neighbourhood of the zero section of the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
is an equivariant local diffeomorphism onto its image. This shows that point
(iv)
in
[
17
, Theorem 3.28]
holds since this exponential map is associated with the restriction to
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
of the Levi-Civita connection of the weak
L^{2}
Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{Met}(P)
. Therefore, we conclude that the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
admits a slice
\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{\bar{g}}
at every
\bar{g}\in\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
, which is realized as the restriction of the
L^{2}
exponential map of
\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
to the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
of
T\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
in
T\mathrm{Im}(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})
. Since
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}
is smooth, tame, and equivariant, defining:

\mathbb{S}_{\bar{g}}:=(\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{\bar{g}})

we obtain a slice for the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
at the unique
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
such that
\bar{g}=\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}_{(g,A)}
. Since
\Theta^{\mathfrak{c}}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Met}(P)
preserves orthogonality with respect to the corresponding
L^{2}
metrics, the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{g}}
corresponds to the
L^{2}
normal bundle of
\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)}
in
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and hence we conclude.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The slice
\mathbb{S}
is tame Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
since it is locally modeled on
(T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{O}_{(g,A)})^{\perp_{g}}
, which, being a closed vector subspace of
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, is a tame Fréchet space.

Remark 4.7
.

Theorem 3.28
[
17
]
implies not only the existence of a slice but of a
linear slice
as defined in
[
17
, Definition 2.8]
. This is important in order to construct Kuranishi models of moduli spaces of geometric structures
[
58
,
18
]
, since it identifies the slice with the quotient by the isotropy group of an open neighborhood of
0
in the tangent space of the slice at the given base point.

As a direct consequence of the slice theorem we obtain the following corollaries, c.f.
[
16
, Proposition 4.1]
and
[
16
, Corollary 4.2]
, where analog statements are proved in the case of Riemannian metrics on a compact manifold. The proofs in our case are formally identical and are therefore omitted.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. There exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of the identity in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
(g,A)
in
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
such that for any
(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}
the symmetry group
\mathcal{I}_{(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})}
of
(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathcal{I}_{(g,A)}
via an element in
\mathcal{U}
.

Corollary 4.9
.

The subset of elements of
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
with trivial isometry group is open in
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
.

Furthermore, the fact that the set of metrics with trivial isometry group and connections with trivial symmetry group is open and dense in
\mathrm{Met}(M)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
, respectively, together with the fact that
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is the direct product of
\mathrm{Met}(M)
and
\mathcal{C}(P)
equipped with the corresponding Fréchet product implies in addition the following result.

Corollary 4.10
.

The subset of elements of
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
with trivial isometry group is open and dense in
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
.

5.
The local Kuranishi model

5.1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we construct the local Kuranishi moduli of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs on
(P,\mathfrak{c})
modulo automorphisms in
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
. In order to do this, we consider the following smooth map of Fréchet manifolds defined by means of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system as follows:

(5.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{E}:=(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)&\to&\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\times\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\\[5.0pt]
(g,A)&\mapsto&\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}(g,A):=-(\mathbb{G}^{g}-\mathcal{T}(g,A))\,,\mathcal{E}_{2}(g,A):=2\kappa\,\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}\right)\end{array}

where
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
denotes the adjoint bundle of
P
,
\mathbb{G}^{g}=\operatorname{Ric}^{g}-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}g
denotes the Einstein tensor of
g
and
\mathcal{T}
denotes the energy-momentum tensor introduced in Definition
3.5
. The space of solutions to the Einstein-Yang-Mills system on
(P,\mathfrak{c})
is given by
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, which we consider to be endowed with the subspace topology. The choice of signs in (
5.1
) is needed later for the self-adjointness of the differential.

Remark 5.1
.

We could have chosen to define the map
\mathcal{E}_{1}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
by using the Einstein equation written in terms of the Ricci curvature and the reverse energy-momentum tensor as
\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=\hat{\mathcal{T}}(g,A)
. This form of the Einstein equation seems to be more convenient to study the linearization of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system since it only involves the Ricci curvature operator instead of the Ricci and scalar curvature operators that are contained in the Einstein tensor. However, the linearization problem in this form turns out to be less transparent since the associated deformation complex is not self-adjoint.

The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
acts on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
equivariantly with respect to
\mathcal{E}
, that is, we have:

\mathcal{E}(f_{u}^{\ast}g,u^{\ast}A)=f_{u}^{\ast}\mathcal{E}(g,A)\,,

for every
u\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
and
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, whence
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
preserves the solution space
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. The quotient:

\mathfrak{M}(P,\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)/\operatorname{Aut}(P)\,,

equipped with quotient topology is the moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that if
[g,A]\in\mathfrak{M}(P,\mathfrak{c})
then
[\lambda g,A]\in\mathfrak{M}(P,\lambda\mathfrak{c})
for every real positive constant
\lambda>0
. This is in sharp contrast to the situation occurring for the moduli space of Einstein metrics, which is preserved by homotheties.

For further reference, we also introduce the smooth map of Fréchet manifolds corresponding to the trace of the Einstein equation of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)\,,

\displaystyle(g,A)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{S}(g,A):=s^{g}-\frac{\kappa(n-4)}{(2-n)}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,.

Clearly we have
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)\subset\mathrm{S}^{-1}(0)
and
\mathfrak{M}(P,\mathfrak{c})\subset\mathrm{S}^{-1}(0)/\operatorname{Aut}(P)
. We expect the latter to be
infinite-dimensional
, exactly as it happens with the moduli space of constant scalar curvature metrics in more than two dimensions.

5.2.
Infinitesimal theory

Let
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. The differentials of
\mathcal{E}_{1}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Omega^{1}(M)
evaluated at
(g,A)
define linear maps of Fréchet spaces:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{1}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Omega^{1}(M)\,,

which we compute in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
the following formulas hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{1}(h,a)

\displaystyle=-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{L}h-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}h-\frac{1}{2}\big(\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)-g(\operatorname{Ric}^{g},h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h\big)g

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\kappa}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}h-\kappa F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+2\kappa F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-\kappa\big(\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\frac{1}{2}g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)\big)g\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2}(h,a)=2\kappa\Big(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}+h(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A})-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}\Big)\,,

where
\Delta^{g}_{L}
denotes the Lichnerowicz Laplacian in the conventions introduced in Appendix
A
.

Remark 5.4
.

The symbol
F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
denotes a symmetric tensor which is defined similarly to
F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
but in terms of
h\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
instead of
g
. More explicitly, we define:

(F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})(v_{1},v_{2}):=g(h,\mathfrak{c}(\iota_{v_{1}}F_{A}\otimes\iota_{v_{2}}F_{A})),

whence in local coordinates we have
(F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})_{ij}=\sum_{a}(F^{a}_{A})_{ik}\,(F^{a}_{A})_{jl}\,h^{kl}
. On the other hand, the symbol
(\,\cdot\,)_{h}^{g}
denotes the linear operation defined in Appendix
A
, see (
A.3
), trivially extended to differential forms taking values on a vector bundle.

Proof.

Let
g_{t}=g+th
and
A_{t}=A+ta
be smooth curves of metrics and connections for
|t|
small enough. Then, to first order in
t
, a direct computation shows that:

\displaystyle\kappa\mathcal{T}(g_{t},A_{t})=

\displaystyle\kappa\mathcal{T}(g,A)+t\Big(\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}h+F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a\Big)

\displaystyle+t\Big(\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-\frac{1}{2}g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)\Big)g+o(t^{2})\,.

The expression for the differential of
\mathcal{E}_{1}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
at
(g,A)
follows now from the previous equation together with Lemma
A.1
and Corollary
A.2
. To compute the differential of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
at
(g,A)
we use that

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\iota_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}^{g,A}F_{A}
on an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
, where
\nabla^{g,A}
denotes the connection induced by
\nabla^{g}
and
A
on
\Omega^{r}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Then the variation of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
with respect to
A
is:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2}(0,a)=2\kappa\Big(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-\sum_{i=1}^{n}[a(e_{i}),\iota_{e_{i}}F_{A}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}\Big)\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}
is the Lie bracket on the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
of
P
. For ease of notation, we define:

a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[a(e_{i}),\iota_{e_{i}}F_{A}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\,.

To compute the variation of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
with respect to the metric we use the formula (
A.2
) together with the identity
\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}=-(-1)^{n}\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A}
, obtaining:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{(g+th)\ast}F_{A}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}-(-1)^{n}t\Big((\mathrm{d}_{g}\star_{\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A}})(h)+\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}(\mathrm{d}_{g}\star_{F_{A}}(h))\Big)+o(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}+t\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}-(-1)^{n}(\ast_{g}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A})^{g}_{h}+\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\ast_{g}F_{A}+\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h})\Big)\Big)+o(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}+t\Big(\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}-(-1)^{n}\ast_{g}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A})^{g}_{h}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}\Big)+o(t^{2})\,.

Here we have used the identity:

(-1)^{n}\ast_{g}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\wedge\ast_{g}F_{A})=\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}\,.

Furthermore, we compute:

(-1)^{n-1}\ast_{g}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A})^{g}_{h}=\ast_{g}(\ast_{g}^{2}\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast_{g}F_{A})^{g}_{h}=(-1)^{n-1}\ast_{g}(\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A})^{g}_{h}=-\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}+h(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A})\,,

where we have used the identity
(-1)^{n}\ast_{g}(\ast_{g}u)_{h}^{g}=\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)u-h(u)
that holds for every one-form
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Therefore, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{(g+th)\ast}F_{A}=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A}+t\Big(h(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A})-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}\Big)+o(t^{2})\,,

Altogether, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2}(h,a)=2\kappa\Big(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}+h(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}F_{A})-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}\Big)\,,

and therefore we conclude.
∎

For ease of notation in the following we set
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}:=(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2})
. At an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)\in\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)
the differential of
\mathcal{E}
simplifies as follows.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(g,A)
be an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. For every
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
the following formulas hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{1}(h,a)

\displaystyle=-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)g-\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h\big)\,g

\displaystyle-\kappa\,h\circ_{g}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})+\frac{\kappa\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)}{2(n-2)}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g-\kappa F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+2\kappa F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-\kappa\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{2}(h,a)

\displaystyle=2\kappa\Big(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}\Big).

Proof.

Since by assumption
(g,A)
is an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair it satisfies:

(5.3)

\operatorname{Ric}^{g}=\kappa\Big(\frac{1}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\Big)\,,\qquad s^{g}=\frac{\kappa(n-4)}{(2-n)}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,,

which we use to compute:

\displaystyle h\circ_{g}\operatorname{Ric}^{g}

\displaystyle=\kappa\Big(\frac{1}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,h-h\circ_{g}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})\Big)\,,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\int_{M}\mathfrak{c}(g(h(e_{i}),F_{A}(e_{j})),\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A}\hat{a}(e_{i},e_{j}))\nu_{g}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}\hat{a},h)\nu_{g}\,.

The terms in the previous equation are adjoint of each other when considered with the factors with which they occur in
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}{\mathcal{E}}
, and hence we conclude. Note that we have used the following identity:

(F_{A})^{g}_{h}(e_{i},e_{j})=g(h(e_{i}),F_{A}(e_{j}))-g(h(e_{j}),F_{A}(e_{i}))

which follows from (
A.3
).
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

For every Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
the vector space of essential deformations
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
is finite-dimensional and isomorphic to the vector space of obstructions
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{(g,A)}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
is finite-dimensional follows from the ellipticity of the complex (
5.5
), which is proven in Lemma
5.7
. By Proposition
5.9
, the differential operator
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}{\mathcal{E}}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is formally self-adjoint, and therefore we obtain:

\displaystyle T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)=\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta^{(1)}_{(g,A)})\oplus\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}{\mathcal{E}})\oplus\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})

\displaystyle T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)=\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta^{(2)}_{(g,A)})\oplus\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}{\mathcal{E}})\oplus\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})

Hence, the identity map on
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
induces a canonical isomorphism between
\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta^{(1)}_{(g,A)})
and
\operatorname{Ker}(\Delta^{(2)}_{(g,A)})
which immediately implies that
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{(g,A)}
are isomorphic as finite-dimensional vector spaces.
∎

By the previous result, if the moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs around an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
is to be of positive dimension then it is necessarily obstructed. That is, the condition that
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{(g,A)}=0
trivializes the moduli problem of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs, exactly as it happens for the moduli problems of Einstein metrics or Yang-Mills connections
[
38
,
42
]
. We proceed now to examine in more detail the conditions for a pair
(h,a)
to be an essential deformation.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(h,a)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
be an infinitesimal deformation of
(g,A)
. Then, the following equations hold, depending on the dimension
n
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h+\kappa\,g((F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o},h^{o})=0\,,\qquad\text{if }\ n=4\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h+\frac{s^{g}}{n}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)=2\kappa\frac{n-4}{2-n}\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\frac{2\kappa}{2-n}g((F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o},h^{o})\,,\qquad\text{if }\ n\neq 4\,,

where
(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o}
and
h^{o}
denote the trace-less projections of
F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}},F_{A}
and
h
, respectively.

Proof.

Since by assumption
(h,a)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and
(g,A)
is Einstein-Yang-Mills,
(g,A)
and
(h,a)
satisfy:

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}(g,A)=0\,,\qquad(h,a)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{S})

where
\mathrm{S}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
is defined in (
5.2
). We compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{S}(h,a)=\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h-g(h,\operatorname{Ric}^{g})+\kappa\frac{4-n}{2-n}\big(2\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)\big).

Substituting the first equation in (
5.3
) into the previous expression and manipulating, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{S}(h,a)=

\displaystyle\,\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{\kappa}{n-2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\kappa}{n-2}g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)+2\kappa\frac{n-4}{n-2}\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

Writing:

h=h^{o}+\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)g\,,\qquad F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}=(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o}+\frac{2}{n}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g

we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{S}(h,a)=

\displaystyle\,\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h+\kappa\frac{4-n}{n(n-2)}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\kappa}{n-2}g((F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o},h^{o})+2\kappa\frac{n-4}{n-2}\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

Hence, if
n=4
, we obtain the first equation in the statement of the Lemma. On the other hand, if
n\neq 4
, then the equation
\mathrm{S}(g,A)=0
implies:

\displaystyle|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}=\kappa\frac{2-n}{n-4}s^{g}\,.

Substituting this equation into the previous expression we obtain the second equation in the statement of the Lemma.
∎

We now consider in more detail the essential deformations of a given Einstein-Yang-Mills pair.

Lemma 5.12
.

The following formula holds:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

for every pair
(h,a)\in\operatorname{Ker}((\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Since
(h,a)
is an essential deformation, it satisfies the slice condition
2\nabla^{g\ast}h=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}
, see (
4.3
). Hence:

2\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h=\nabla^{g\ast}(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}).

We chose a local frame
(e_{i})
of
TM
adapted at a given basepoint and a local frame
(\tau_{\Lambda})
of
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
. We compute:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g\ast}(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}(a_{\Lambda}\otimes\tau_{\Lambda}\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}(F^{\Sigma}_{A}\otimes\tau_{\Sigma}))(e_{i})=-\sum_{i}\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}(F_{A}^{\Sigma}(a_{\Lambda},e_{i})\mathfrak{c}(\tau_{\Lambda},\tau_{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(F_{A}^{\Sigma}(\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}a_{\Lambda},e_{i})\mathfrak{c}(\tau_{\Lambda},\tau_{\Sigma})+F_{A}^{\Sigma}(a_{\Lambda},e_{i})\mathfrak{c}(\nabla^{A}_{e_{i}}\tau_{\Lambda},\tau_{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle F_{A}^{\Sigma},e_{i}\wedge\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}a_{\Lambda}\rangle_{g}\mathfrak{c}(\tau_{\Lambda},\tau_{\Sigma})+\langle F_{A}^{\Sigma},e_{i}\wedge a_{\Lambda}\rangle_{g}\mathfrak{c}(\nabla^{A}_{e_{i}}\tau_{\Lambda},\tau_{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,F_{A}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,,

from which the equation follows.
∎

We give now the final general characterization of the essential deformations of an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)\in\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)
that we will use in the following.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
(g,A)
be an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. A pair
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is an essential deformation of
(g,A)
if and only if it satisfies the following four equations:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)

\displaystyle-\kappa\Big(F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+h\circ_{g}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a\Big)-\frac{\kappa}{n-2}\Big(2\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}-g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)\Big)g=0\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}=0\,,

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{g\ast}h=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a=0\,.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
5.5
after imposing the equation
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{S}(h,a)=0
as computed in the proof of Lemma
5.11
.
∎

Given an essential deformation
(h,a)
, the previous proposition immediately implies that
2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is exact and therefore defines the trivial class in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Given an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
, an arbitrary trace-less symmetric two-tensor
h^{o}
and an element
a\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, we will say that
(h^{o},a)
is
completable
or can be completed into an essential deformation of
(g,A)
if there exists a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
(h=f\,g+h^{o},a)
is an essential deformation. Thus, an obstruction for
(h^{o},a)
to be
completable
is that the cohomology class
[2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
vanishes. When necessary we will denote by
T^{o}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
the subspace of
T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
consisting of elements
(h^{o},a)
with
h^{o}
traceless. If
[2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}]=0
then we say that
(h^{o},a)
is
unobstructed
. In particular, we have the following result.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
(h,a)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
be an infinitesimal deformation of an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
. Then, the following equations hold:

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{s^{g}}{n}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)\nu_{g}=\frac{2\kappa}{2-n}\int_{M}g((F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o},h^{o})\nu_{g}\,,\qquad 0=[2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}]\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

In particular, if
n=4
we have
\int_{M}g((F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})^{o},h^{o})\nu_{g}=0
.

Proof.

Integrate the equations of Lemma
5.11
.
∎

Hence, infinitesimal deformations of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs
(g,A)
are
obstructed
in terms of a cohomology class and of the bilinear
F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
constructed out of
g
and
A
. These are genuine obstructions to the deformation problem of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system.

Remark 5.15
.

In fact, deformations of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system do not decouple even for
pure
metric or Yang-Mills deformations. More specifically, an element
a\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
defines an essential deformation
(0,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
of
(g,A)
if and only if:

(5.13)

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}a=0\,,\qquad F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a=\frac{1}{n-2}\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g\,,\qquad a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}=0

Solutions to the first and second equations above correspond to essential deformations of
A
as a Yang-Mills connection
[
42
]
. Hence, essential deformations of the form
(0,a)
correspond to the subset of the essential deformations of
A
as a Yang-Mills connection that satisfies the third and fourth equations above. On the other hand, if we consider deformations of the form
(h,0)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
we obtain a constrained coupled system that does not reduce to the differential system that characterizes infinitesimal deformations of a Ricci-flat metric.

The previous remark together with Corollary
5.14
shows that the metrics and connections in the moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs are truly coupled. The fact that we are considering simultaneous deformations of
g
and
A
allows for a number of special types of deformations by choosing
h
and
a
of a particular type. An infinitesimal deformation
(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is generated by a smooth family of automorphisms of
P
if and only if it can be written as:

(h,a)=(2\delta^{g}v,\mathrm{d}_{A}\tau+F_{A}(v))

in terms of a vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and a section
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Pairs of this type automatically belong to the kernel of
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
and are essential deformations of the given
(g,A)
if and only if
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is Killing and
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is parallel with respect to the connection induced by
A
on
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
. We consider in the following one of the most natural deformations of
(g,A)
, which consists of simply multiplying
g
by a smooth family of positive functions and results in an infinitesimal deformation of the form
(h,a)=(fg,a)
for a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and an arbitrary infinitesimal deformation
a\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
of
A
.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
(f\,g,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
be an essential deformation of
(g,A)
. If:

\kappa\big(n(n-2)+2(n-2)^{2}-16\kappa(n-2)+8\kappa n\big)(4-n)\leq 0

then
f=0
and
a\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
satisfies (
5.13
).

Proof.

Let
(f\,g,a)
be an essential deformation of
(g,A)
. Using Lemmas
5.11
and
5.12
it follows that
f
satisfies:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}f+\frac{2\kappa(n-4)}{n(n-2)+2(n-2)^{2}-16\kappa(n-2)+8\kappa n}|F_{A}|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}^{2}f=0

Since
\Delta_{g}
is a positive operator the conclusion follows.
∎

5.3.
The Kuranishi model

Let
(g,A)\in\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)
be an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair and fix a slice
\mathbb{S}
around
(g,A)
. Consider the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathbb{S}
:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathbb{S}\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P).

Then, the differential of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}}
defines a linear map:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)

given by evaluation of
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}(h,a)
on
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}
. The local moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs around an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair can be characterized similarly to the case of Einstein metrics or Yang-Mills connections, as proven by Koiso in
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
(g,A)
be an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair and
\mathbb{S}\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
a slice around
(g,A)
. Then, there exists an analytic closed submanifold
\mathcal{Z}_{(g,A)}\subset\mathbb{S}
of
\mathbb{S}
such that:

T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{Z}=\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})\cap\operatorname{Ker}((\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast})

and
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{S}
is an analytic subset of
\mathcal{Z}_{(g,A)}
.

Proof.

By the Hodge decomposition (
5.10
) we have:

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}})=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})\subset T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P).

Denote by:

\mathrm{P}\colon T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})

the natural projection in the orthogonal decomposition given in (
5.10
). Then, the map:

\mathrm{P}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathbb{S}\to\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E})

is a smooth map that has a surjective derivative at
(g,A)\in\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. On the other hand, the slice
\mathbb{S}
as well as the map
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathbb{S}\to T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
can be obtained as the projective limit of their canonical extensions
\mathbb{S}^{s}
and
\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}
to the corresponding Sobolev completions in the Sobolev norm
H_{s}=L^{2}_{s}
with
s>n+4
. Extensions to Sobolev spaces will be denoted with the superscript
s>n+4
. Then:

\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathbb{S}^{s}\to\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s})

has a surjective derivative at
(g,A)
and thus by the inverse function theorem there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{K}^{s}\subset\mathbb{S}^{s}
of
(g,A)
such that:

\mathcal{Z}^{s}=(\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}})^{-1}(0)\cap\mathcal{K}^{s}

is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{S}^{s}
. By the implicit function theorem, it follows that:

T_{(g,A)}\mathcal{Z}^{s}=\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s})\cap\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi^{s}_{(g,A)})^{\ast}\,.

Furthermore, the zero set of
\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}
restricted to
\mathcal{K}^{s}
belongs to the zero set of the restriction of
\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}
to
\mathcal{K}^{s}
and therefore defines an analytic subset of
\mathcal{Z}^{s}
. Fix an integer
s
satisfying
s>n+4
and consider
q\geq s
. Define:

\mathcal{Z}^{q}=\mathcal{Z}^{s}\cap\mathbb{S}^{q}\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}^{q}=\mathcal{K}^{s}\cap\mathbb{S}^{q}

Every
(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}_{(g,A)}
satisfies:

(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}^{q})^{\ast}\circ\mathrm{E}^{-1}_{q}((g^{\prime},A^{\prime}))=0\,,\qquad\mathcal{E}(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})=0

where
\mathrm{E}_{q}\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathcal{E}^{q}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the exponential map. This system is elliptic, hence
(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})
is smooth. Therefore,
\mathcal{Z}^{q}=\mathcal{Z}^{s}
for every
q\geq s
and, in particular,
\mathcal{Z}^{q}
consists only of smooth elements. Since
\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}\colon\mathbb{S}^{s}\to\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s})
has surjective derivative on
\mathcal{Z}^{s}
, hence also on
\mathcal{Z}^{q}
, it follows that for every element:

(h_{o},a_{o})\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}_{(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})}(\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}|_{\mathcal{K}^{q}}))

there exist elements
(h_{1},a_{1})\in\operatorname{Ker}((\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}^{s})^{\ast})
and
(h_{2},a_{2})\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s})
such that:

\mathrm{d}_{(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})}(\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}})(h_{1},a_{1})=(h_{0},a_{0})+(h_{2},a_{2}).

Hence:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s}\circ\mathrm{d}_{(g^{\prime},A^{\prime})}(\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}})(h_{1},a_{1})=\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{s}(h_{0},a_{0})\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)}^{s\ast}(h_{1},a_{1})=0.

Therefore,
(h_{1},a_{1})\in\mathcal{K}^{q}
and consequentely
\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}|_{\mathcal{K}^{q}}
has surjective derivative at every point in
\mathcal{Z}^{q}
, implying that the latter is a closed analytic submanifold of
\mathcal{K}^{q}
. Hence, we end up with a system:

\left\{\mathcal{K}^{q},\mathcal{Z}^{q},\mathrm{P}^{s}\circ\mathcal{E}^{s}_{\mathbb{S}}|_{\mathcal{Z}^{q}}\right\}_{q\geq s}

of open sets, closed analytic submanifolds of the latter, and smooth maps. Since
\mathcal{Z}^{q}
does not depend on
q
and contains only smooth connections, the projective limit gives the desired smooth Fréchet closed submanifold
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{K}
of the open set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{S}
of the slice
\mathbb{S}
.
∎

.

6.
Einstein-Yang-Mills deformations of instantons on Calabi-Yau two-folds

6.1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this section, we take
M
to be four-dimensional, hence
n=4
. In this dimension the Riemannian Hodge dual squares to the identity. We say that a connection
A
on
P
is anti-self-dual if:

\ast_{g}F_{A}=-F_{A}\,.

Self-dual connections are defined similarly in terms of the opposite sign.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
g
be a Ricci-flat metric on
M
and
A
an anti-self-dual connection on
P
. Then
(g,A)
is an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair.

Proof.

Since
g
is Ricci flat its Einstein tensor vanishes. Furthermore, using the identity:

F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}=\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}

it follows that

\mathcal{T}(g,A)=\frac{\kappa}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\,g-\kappa\,F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}

and we conclude.
∎

Therefore, anti-self-dual instantons on Ricci-flat four-manifolds provide, in case they exist, a distinguished class of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs. We will refer to such pairs simply as
anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs
.

6.2.
Infinitesimal deformations of anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs

In this subsection, we prove a refinement of Proposition
5.13
for anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs in four dimensions. As we will see in the following, this refinement allows to
decouple
the differential system determining
(h^{o},a)
from the differential condition satisfied by
\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(g,A)
be an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair on a principal bundle
P
over a four-dimensional manifold
M
. An unobstructed pair
(h^{o},a)\in T^{o}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
is a completable essential deformation of
(g,A)
if and only if:

(6.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h^{o}-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h^{o})-2\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-\frac{1}{6}(\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o})g+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{g}(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})\\
-\kappa\Big(F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}^{2}h^{o}-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g\Big)=0\,,\end{array}

(6.2)

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a=0\,.

If that is the case, the completed essential deformation
(h,a)
satisfies
h=\displaystyle\frac{f}{4}g+h^{o}
, where
\mathrm{d}f=4\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-2\,a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.13
, a pair
(h,a)
is an essential deformation of an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
in four dimensions if and only if the following differential system holds:

(6.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\\
-\kappa\Big(F_{A}\circ_{h,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+h\circ_{g}(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g+\frac{1}{2}g(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}F_{A},h)g\Big)=0\,,\end{array}

(6.4)

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}=0\,,\qquad 2\nabla^{g\ast}h=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a=0\,.

A direct computation shows that:

\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}=\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}\,,

and therefore the first equation in (
6.4
) becomes:

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=0.

In particular, the trace of
h
decouples from this equation. On the other hand, the slice condition
2\nabla^{g\ast}h=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}
is equivalent to:

(6.5)

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)=2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,.

This equation isolates the differential of
\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)
in terms of
h^{o}
and
a
. Substituting now:

h=h^{o}+\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)}{4}g

in (
6.3
) it can be checked that all the terms proportional to
\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)
drop out and only terms involving derivatives of
\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)
remain. Using (
6.5
) as well as Lemma
5.12
in (
6.3
), we substitute the exterior derivative of
\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)
in terms of
h^{o}
,
a
and their derivatives, obtaining Equation (
6.1
). Conversely, let
(h^{o},a)\in T^{o}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
be an unobstructed pair satisfying the differential system given in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
). Since
(h^{o},a)
is unobstructed, there exists a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying:

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}f=2\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}

Defining:

h=\frac{f}{4}g+h^{o}

and using this equation to substitute
h^{o}
in terms of
h
and
f
in Equations (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), we obtain the differential system (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) and hence we conclude.
∎

6.3.
Anti-self-dual infinitesimal deformations

We consider the following smooth map of manifolds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{o}:=(\mathcal{E}^{o}_{1},\mathcal{E}^{o}_{2})\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\times\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})\,,

\displaystyle(g,A)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{o}(g,A):=\mathrm{Ric}^{g}\,,\mathcal{E}_{2}^{o}(g,A):=\ast_{g}F_{A}+F_{A}\right).

We will refer to the differential system
\mathcal{E}_{o}(g,A)=0
as the
anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills system
. The space of solutions to the anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills system is given by
\mathcal{E}^{-1}_{o}(0)\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
, which we consider to be endowed with the subspace topology. The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
acts on
\mathrm{Conf}(P)
equivariantly with respect to
\mathcal{E}_{o}
whence
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
preserves the solution space
\mathcal{E}^{-1}_{o}(0)\subset\mathrm{Conf}(P)
. The quotient:

\mathfrak{M}_{o}(P,\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{E}^{-1}_{o}(0)/\operatorname{Aut}(P)\,,

equipped with quotient topology is the moduli space of
anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The differential of
\mathcal{E}_{o}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(P)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\times\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
at an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair
(g,A)
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{1}(h,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{2}(h,a)

\displaystyle=\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}\,,

where
h=\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)g+h^{o}
.

Proof.

The expression for
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{1}(h,a)
follows directly from Lemma
A.1
after imposing
\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=0
. To obtain
\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{2}(h,a)
we compute:

\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}_{2}(h,a)=\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\ast_{g}F_{A}+\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h}=\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}\,,

and we conclude.
∎

The vector space of
essential deformations
of an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair as an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair, in contrast to as a general Einstein-Yang-Mills pair, is defined as follows:

\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}:=\left\{(h,a)\in T_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)\,\,|\,\,\mathrm{d}_{(g,A)}\mathcal{E}^{o}(h,a)=0,\,\,(\mathrm{d}_{e}\Phi_{(g,A)})^{\ast}(h,a)=0\right\}\,.

Essential deformations of anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs as anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs are characterized by the following proposition.

Proposition 6.4
.

A pair
(h^{o},a)\in T^{o}_{(g,A)}\mathrm{Conf}(P)
can be completed into an essential deformation
(h,a)\in\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}
of
(g,A)
as an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills if and only if:

(6.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h^{o}-\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h^{o})-2\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o}-\frac{1}{6}(\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h^{o})g+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{g}(a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})=0\,,

(6.7)

\displaystyle\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}a=0\,.

In particular,
\langle F_{A},(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}=0
and
\ast_{g}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}

Remark 6.5
.

We have the following explicit formula for
(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
:

(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}(v_{1},v_{2})=-F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{1}),v_{2})+F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{2}),v_{1})\,,

for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Using this equation it can be checked directly that
(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}
is indeed self-dual.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma
6.3
and Theorem
6.2
after formally setting
\kappa=0
.
∎

Since every anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair is automatically Einstein-Yang-Mills, it follows that every essential deformation of
(g,A)
as an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair must be an essential deformation of
(g,A)
as an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. Nonetheless, it is an instructive exercise to verify this explicitly.

Proposition 6.6
.

We have a natural inclusion
\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
.

Proof.

Let
(h,a)\in\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}
. The pair
(h,a)
satisfies (
6.1
) if and only if:

(6.8)

\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}^{2}h^{o}=0.

We compute:

\displaystyle 2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a

\displaystyle=2F^{-}_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(\mathrm{d}_{A}a)^{-}+2F^{-}_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(\mathrm{d}_{A}a)^{+}=\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g+2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(\mathrm{d}_{A}a)^{+}

\displaystyle=\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g-F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}\,.

On the other hand, we have:

\displaystyle-(F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}})(v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\langle F_{A}(v_{1}),F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{2}))\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle F_{A}(v_{2}),F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{1}))\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+(F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A})(v_{1},v_{2})\,.

Furthermore:

\langle F_{A}(v_{1}),F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{2}))\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}+\langle F_{A}(v_{2}),F_{A}(h^{o}(v_{1}))\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}=|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}h^{o}\,,

which finally gives:

2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a=\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g+\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}h^{o}+F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,,

whence (
6.8
) is satisfied. Applying now the differential operator
-\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}
to the first equation in (
6.7
) we obtain:

\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}^{2}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=0\,.

We compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{2}_{A}a=e_{j}\wedge\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{j}}(e_{i}\wedge\nabla^{g,A}_{e_{i}}(a^{\Lambda}\otimes\tau_{\Lambda}))=e_{j}\wedge e_{i}\wedge a^{\Lambda}\otimes\nabla^{A}_{e_{j}}\nabla^{A}_{e_{i}}\tau_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}e_{j}\wedge e_{i}\wedge a^{\Lambda}\otimes[F_{A}(e_{i},e_{j}),\tau_{\Lambda}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}

\displaystyle=F^{\Sigma}_{A}\wedge a^{\Lambda}\otimes[\tau_{\Sigma},\tau_{\Lambda}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}\,.

This implies:

\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}^{2}_{A}a=\ast_{g}(F^{\Sigma}_{A}\wedge a^{\Lambda})\otimes[\tau_{\Sigma},\tau_{\Lambda}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}=-(a^{\Lambda}\lrcorner_{g}F_{A}^{\Sigma})\otimes[\tau_{\Sigma},\tau_{\Lambda}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}=a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,.

Thus, the first equation in (
6.7
) is satisfied as well.
∎

By Proposition
6.1
we have a natural injection:

\mathbb{I}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{o}(P,\mathfrak{c})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{M}(P,\mathfrak{c})

of
\mathfrak{M}_{o}(P,\mathfrak{c})
into the moduli space of Einstein-Yang-Mills pairs. By the structural result given in Theorem
5.17
it is clear that
\mathbb{I}
is a local injective immersion which is isometric with respect to the natural
L^{2}
metric on both
\mathfrak{M}_{o}(P,\mathfrak{c})
and
\mathfrak{M}(P,\mathfrak{c})
. A natural question arises in this context: Is every infinitesimal deformation of
(g,A)
as an Einstein-Yang-Mills pair automatically an infinitesimal deformation as an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair? A positive answer to this question would give the Einstein-Yang-Mills analog of the theorem by which every Ricci flat deformation of a Calabi-Yau metric is again Calabi-Yau.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Calabi-Yau two-fold and let
(g,A)
be an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. Then
\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}=\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}\subset\mathbb{E}_{(g,A)}
if
(g,A)
is an anti-self-dual Einstein-Yang-Mills pair. By Theorem
6.2
and Proposition
6.4
a pair
(h^{o},a)
that can be completed into an essential deformation of
(g,A)
can also be completed into an anti-self-dual essential deformation of
(g,A)
if and only if:

(6.9)

\displaystyle F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}+\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|_{g,\mathfrak{c}}^{2}h^{o}-2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g=0\,,

(6.10)

\displaystyle\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=0\,.

Let
(h,a)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{(g,A)}
. Then,
(h^{o},a)
satisfies:

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a-a\lrcorner_{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=-\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}(\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}})=0\,.

Hence:

\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}}=b\,,

where
b\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}_{A})\subset\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Furthermore, since the left hand side of the previous equation is self-dual,
b
is also self-dual and in addition
b\in\operatorname{Ker}(\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A})\subset\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{G}_{P})
. Hence
b
is a self-dual two-form with values in
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
harmonic with respect to
g
and
A
. Similarly to the proof of Proposition
6.6
, we compute:

\displaystyle 2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{d}_{A}a

\displaystyle=2F^{-}_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(\mathrm{d}_{A}a)^{-}+2F^{-}_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(\mathrm{d}_{A}a)^{+}=\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g+2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}(b-(F_{A})^{g}_{h^{o}})

\displaystyle=2F_{A}\circ_{g,\mathfrak{c}}b+\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle_{g,\mathfrak{c}}g+\frac{1}{2}|F_{A}|^{2}_{g,\mathfrak{c}}h^{o}+F_{A}\circ_{h^{o},\mathfrak{c}}F_{A}\,.

Therefore, equations (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) hold if and only if
b=0
. In order to prove that this is indeed the case, we consider the Weitzenböck formula for the Laplacian associated to
\nabla^{g}
and
A
acting on the bundle of two forms with values in the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{G}_{P}
. A computation gives:

\displaystyle((\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}+\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A})k)(v_{1},v_{2})=(\nabla^{g,A\ast}\nabla^{g,A}k)(v_{1},v_{2})-(\mathcal{R}^{g,A}_{v_{1},e_{i}}k)(e_{i},v_{2})+(\mathcal{R}^{g,A}_{v_{2},e_{i}}k)(e_{i},v_{1})\,,\quad k\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\,,

where
\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A}\colon\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Omega^{3}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is the exterior covariant derivative determined by
\nabla^{g}
and
A
on
\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
,
\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}_{A}\colon\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is its formal adjoint,
\nabla^{g,A}
is the connection induced by
\nabla^{g}
and
A
on
\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
,
(\nabla^{g,A})^{\ast}\colon\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is its formal adjoint and
\mathcal{R}^{g,A}\in\Omega^{2}(\operatorname{End}(\wedge^{2}T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{G}_{P}))
is the curvature of
\nabla^{g,A}
. We have the identity:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}^{g,A}_{v_{1},v_{2}}k)(v_{3},v_{4})=[F_{A}(v_{1},v_{2}),k(v_{3},v_{4})]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}-k(R^{g}_{v_{1},v_{2}}v_{3},v_{4})-k(v_{3},R^{g}_{v_{1},v_{2}}v_{4})\,,

for every
v_{1},\ldots,v_{4}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Using the fact that
g
is Ricci-flat, we obtain:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}^{g,A}_{v_{1},e_{i}}b)(e_{i},v_{2})-(\mathcal{R}^{g,A}_{v_{2},e_{i}}b)(e_{i},v_{1})=[F_{A}(v_{1},e_{i}),b(e_{1},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}-[F_{A}(v_{2},e_{i}),b(e_{1},v_{1})]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}

\displaystyle-b(e_{i},R^{g}_{v_{1},e_{i}}v_{2})+b(e_{i},R^{g}_{v_{2},e_{i}}v_{1})\,.

Using that
F_{A}
is anti-self-dual while
b
is self-dual it follows that:

\displaystyle[F_{A}(v_{1},e_{i}),b(e_{1},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}-[F_{A}(v_{2},e_{i}),b(e_{1},v_{1})]_{\mathfrak{G}_{P}}=0\,.

Furthermore:

\displaystyle b(e_{i},R^{g}_{v_{1},e_{i}}v_{2})-b(e_{i},R^{g}_{v_{2},e_{i}}v_{1})=b(e_{i},R^{g}_{v_{1},v_{2}}e_{i})=0\,,

where we have used the first Bianchi identity for
R^{g}
and the fact that
g
is anti-self-dual since it is Calabi-Yau. We conclude that:

\displaystyle((\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}+\mathrm{d}_{A}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}^{g}_{A})b)(v_{1},v_{2})=(\nabla^{g,A\ast}\nabla^{g,A}b)(v_{1},v_{2})\,,

and therefore
\nabla^{g,A}b=0
. As
(M,g)
is a Calabi-Yau, there is a parallel
(2,0)
-form
\theta
and an associated parallel Kähler form
\omega
. Hence, the complexification of the bundle of self-dual two forms
\wedge_{+}^{2}M
decomposes as follows:

\wedge^{2}_{+}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}=\wedge^{2,0}M\oplus\underline{{\mathbb{R}}}\cdot\omega\oplus\wedge^{0,2}M

is trivial with the trivial connection. Let
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
be a parallel frame of
\Omega_{+}^{2}(M)
. Since the element
b\in\Omega^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
is parallel with respect to
\nabla^{g,A}
, writing
b=\sum x_{a}\,b_{a}
in terms of sections
b_{a}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{G}_{P})
with
a=1,2,3
, we obtain:

0=\nabla^{g,A}b=\sum_{a=1}^{3}x_{a}\,\nabla^{A}b_{a}\,,

and so
\nabla^{A}b_{a}=0
for every
a=1,2,3
.
Assume that they are linearly independent (if not, just throw out those that are linearly dependent and work with
the remaining ones).
Let
\mathfrak{F}_{P}
be the subbundle generated by
the sections
b_{a}
, so we have a decomposition

\mathfrak{G}_{P}=\mathfrak{F}_{P}\oplus\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp}
. The bundle

\mathfrak{F}_{P}
is trivial with a trivial flat connection
and
b=\sum x_{a}\,b_{a}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{F}_{P})
.
Hence
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp})
.

Let
b_{l}
be a frame for
\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp}

\nabla^{A}b_{l}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp})
. As
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp})
, We have that
(F_{A})^{g}_{h_{o}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{P}^{\perp})
and therefore the equation
b=\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+(F_{A})^{g}_{h_{0}}
implies that the
b_{a}
-component of

\alpha_{a},\alpha_{l}\in C^{\infty}(M)

\sum x_{a}\otimes b_{a}=(\mathrm{d}\alpha_{a}+\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{a})\otimes b_{a}

\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{a}=0

\mathrm{d}\alpha_{a}=0

x=0
, and thus

Various differential operators in Riemannian geometry

In order to fix notation and conventions, in this section we collect the Riemannian formulas that we will need throughout the main body of the article. We will mostly follow
[

with an opposite sign for the definition of the Riemann curvature tensor. Let
(M,g)
be a compact and oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
n

\nu_{g}

\wedge T^{\ast}M
of
M
as the subspace of the tensor algebra
\mathcal{T}(M)
of
M
determined by the image of the following vector bundle map:

\wedge T^{\ast}M\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}(M)\,,\qquad\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{r}\to\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}(-1)^{\sigma}\alpha_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{\sigma(r)}\,,

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
\sigma\in S_{r}
runs over all permutations of the set of
r
elements. Therefore we consider differential forms as skew-symmetric
(r,0)
tensors, that is:

(\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{r})(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}(-1)^{\sigma}(\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{r})(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(r)})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}(-1)^{\sigma}\alpha_{1}(v_{\sigma(1)})\cdots\alpha_{r}(v_{\sigma(r)})\,,

for every
v_{1},\ldots v_{r}\in TM
. Consequently, in our conventions the wedge product of two differential forms is given by the formula:

(\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2})(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r_{1}+r_{2}})=\frac{1}{r_{1}!r_{2}!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r_{1}+r_{2}}}(-1)^{\sigma}\omega_{1}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(r_{1})})\omega_{2}(v_{\sigma(r_{1}+1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(r_{1}+r_{2})})

where
\omega_{1}\in\wedge^{r_{1}}T^{\ast}M
,
\omega_{2}\in\wedge^{r_{2}}T^{\ast}M
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r_{1}+r_{2}}\in TM
. In particular, for every
\omega\in\wedge T^{\ast}M
we can write:

\omega=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}\frac{1}{r!}\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{r}}=\sum_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r}}\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{r}}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}\omega_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}e^{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e^{i_{r}}\,,

where
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{n})
is any given coframe.

The metric induced by
g
on the tensor bundles over
M
will be denoted again by
g(\cdot,\cdot)
, whereas the determinant metric determined by
g
on the exterior algebra bundle of
M
will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g}
. In our conventions the Hodge dual operator
\ast_{g}\colon\wedge T^{\ast}M\to\wedge T^{\ast}M
on
(M,g)
is determined by the following equation:

\alpha\wedge\ast_{g}\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}\nu_{g}\,,

for any pair of differential forms
\alpha
and
\beta
on
M
. In particular, if
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{n})
is a local orthonormal frame then:

\ast_{g}(e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{k}})=\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\sum_{i_{k+1},\ldots,i_{n}}\epsilon_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}i_{k+1}\ldots i_{d}}e^{i_{k+1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{n}}\,.

where
\epsilon_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}i_{k+1}\ldots i_{n}}
is the Levi-Civita symbol in the convention
\epsilon_{1\ldots,d}=1
. Furthermore, if
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{r}(M)
we have:

\ast_{g}^{2}\alpha=(-1)^{r(n-r)}\alpha\,,\qquad\langle\alpha,\ast_{g}\beta\rangle_{g}=(-1)^{r(n-r)}\langle\ast_{g}\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}\,.

In addition, we have:

\langle u\wedge\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\langle\alpha,\iota_{u^{\sharp}}\beta\rangle_{g}\,,

for any
u\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,
\alpha\in\Omega^{r}(M)
and
\beta\in\Omega^{r+1}(M)
, where the superscript
\sharp
denotes musical isomorphism with respect to
g
. The exterior derivative of an
r
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{r}(M)
is defined through the following formula:

\mathrm{d}\alpha(v_{0},\ldots,v_{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}v_{i}(\alpha(v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{r}))+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq k}(-1)^{i+j}\alpha([v_{i},v_{j}],v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,\hat{v}_{j},\ldots,v_{r})\,,

where
v_{0},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and the symbol
hat
removes the underlying element. Therefore, in local coordinates, we have:

\mathrm{d}\alpha=\frac{1}{r!}\partial_{i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}\mathrm{d}x^{i_{0}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{i_{r}}\,,

where we are using the Einstein summation convention. Consequently, the local components of
\mathrm{d}\alpha
are given by:

(\mathrm{d}\alpha)_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{r}}=(r+1)\partial_{[i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}]}\,,

where
[i_{0},\ldots,i_{r}]
denotes the skew-symmetrization of
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{r})
with the following normalization factor:

\partial_{[i_{0}}\alpha_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}]}=\frac{1}{(r+1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r+1}}(-1)^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma(i_{0})}\alpha_{\sigma(i_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(i_{r})}\,.

We define the Lie derivative of an
(r,0)
-tensor
\tau\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\otimes^{r}})
by the following expression:

(\mathcal{L}_{v}\tau)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=v\cdot\tau(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tau(v_{1},\ldots,[v,v_{i}],\ldots,v_{r})\,.

Let
\left\{f_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms generated by
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Then, we can equivalently write:

\mathcal{L}_{v}\tau=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f^{\ast}_{t}\tau|_{t=0}\,.

for the Lie derivative of
\alpha
along
v
, where
f^{\ast}_{t}\tau
denotes the pullback of
\tau
by the diffeomorphism
f_{t}\colon M\to M
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, for an
r
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{r}(M)
we have the
Cartan formula
:

\mathcal{L}_{v}\alpha=\mathrm{d}\iota_{v}\alpha+\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,.

The Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)
will be denoted by
\nabla^{g}
. In terms of
\nabla^{g}
the exterior differential of
\alpha\in\Omega^{r}(M)
is given by skew-symmetrization in all entries:

\mathrm{d}\alpha(v_{0},\ldots,v_{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}(\nabla^{g}_{v_{i}}\alpha)(v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{r})

or, equivalently, by:

\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{i}\wedge\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}\alpha

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is a local orthonormal frame and
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{n})
is its dual coframe. The formal adjoint
\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\colon\Omega^{r+1}(M)\to\Omega^{r}(M)
of the exterior derivative with respect to the
L^{2}
norm defined by
g
is given by:

\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\colon\Omega^{r+1}(M)\to\Omega^{r}(M)\,,\qquad\alpha\mapsto-(-1)^{nr}\ast_{g}\mathrm{d}\ast_{g}\alpha\,.

In particular, we have:

\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\alpha=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\iota_{e_{i}}\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}\alpha

in terms of a local orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
and its dual
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{n})
.

Consider now the Levi-Civita connection as a differential operator:

\nabla^{g}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\otimes^{r}})\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\otimes^{r+1}})\,,\qquad\tau\mapsto\nabla^{g}\tau\,.

The formal adjoint of this differential operator with respect to the
L^{2}
norm defined by
g
can be computed to be:

(\nabla^{g\ast}\tau)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}\tau)(e_{i},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})\,,\qquad v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in TM\,,

where
\tau
is an
(r,0)
-tensor and
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is a local orthornormal frame. We will refer to
\nabla^{g\ast}
as the
divergence operator
of
g
. Equivalently, we can write
\nabla^{g\ast}
in terms of the following trace with respect to
g
:

(\nabla^{g\ast}\tau)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=-\mathrm{Tr}_{g}((\nabla^{g}\tau)(\,\cdot\,,v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}))\,,\qquad v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in TM\,.

In particular, the divergence of a tensor
\tau\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\otimes r+1})
is given in local components by:

(\nabla^{g\ast}\tau)_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}=-(\nabla^{g})^{i_{0}}\tau_{i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}\,.

The divergence operator restricts to a differential operator between differential forms which we denote for simplicity by the same symbol. For an
(r+1)
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{r+1}(M)
we have the following relation between the coadjoint and divergence operators:

\nabla^{g\ast}\alpha=\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\alpha\,,\qquad\alpha\in\Omega^{r+1}(M)\,,

whence the restriction of
\nabla^{g\ast}
to the differential forms is precisely the formal adjoint of the exterior derivative. Similarly, the divergence operator defines by restriction a map:

\nabla^{g\ast}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r+1}})\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r}})

between symmetric tensors, which we denote again by the same symbol for ease of notation. On the other hand, in the same way that the skew-symmetrization of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
defines a natural differential operator between differential forms, namely the exterior derivative, the symmetrization
\delta^{g}
of
\nabla^{g}
defines a differential operator between symmetric tensors given by:

\delta^{g}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r}})\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r+1}})\,,\quad\tau\mapsto(\delta^{g}\tau)(v_{0},\ldots,v_{r})=\frac{1}{r+1}\sum_{i=0}^{r}(\nabla^{g}_{v_{i}}\tau)(v_{0},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{r})\,.

It can be checked that as defined above the formal adjoint of
\delta^{g}
is precisely the restriction of
\nabla^{g\ast}
to the symmetric tensors, that is:

\delta^{g\ast}=\nabla^{g\ast}|_{\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r+1}})}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r+1}})\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M^{\odot^{r}})\,.

Using the exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}
and its adjoint operator
\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}
, we define the Laplacian
\Delta_{g}
on differential forms:

\Delta_{g}\colon\Omega^{r}(M)\to\Omega^{r}(M)\,,\quad\alpha\mapsto\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\alpha+\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,

which in this form is sometimes called the
Hodge-de-Rham Laplacian
or the
positive
Laplacian. When acting on functions
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
we have the identities:

\Delta_{g}f=\mathrm{d}^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}f=-\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}f)=(\nabla^{g})^{\ast}\mathrm{d}f\,.

In our conventions the Riemannian curvature
\mathrm{R}^{g}
of
g
is given by:

\mathrm{R}^{g}_{v_{1},v_{2}}v_{3}=\nabla^{g}_{v_{1}}\nabla^{g}_{v_{2}}v_{3}-\nabla^{g}_{v_{2}}\nabla^{g}_{v_{1}}v_{3}-\nabla^{g}_{[v_{1},v_{2}]}v_{3}\,,\qquad v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,.

The Ricci tensor is in turn obtained from
\mathrm{R}^{g}
by taking the following trace:

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}(v_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Tr}(v_{3}\mapsto\mathrm{R}^{g}(v_{3},v_{1})v_{2})\,.

Therefore, in local coordinates, we have:

\operatorname{Ric}^{g}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{R}_{\,\,kij}^{g\,\,\,\,\,\,k}\,.

Finally, the scalar curvature of
g
is given by the trace of the Ricci tensor as defined above. By
lowering
one index with the metric
g
, we will usually consider
\mathrm{R}^{g}
as a section
\mathrm{R}^{g}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{\ast}M\otimes\wedge^{2}T^{\ast}M)
.

For further reference, given
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
fixed, we define the following differential operator in terms of the Levi-Civita connection:

D_{v_{1},v_{2}}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)\qquad g\mapsto\nabla^{g}_{v_{1}}v_{2}\,.

Its differential at a metric
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
defines a map

\mathrm{d}_{g}D_{v_{1},v_{2}}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)

given by:

g(\mathrm{d}_{g}D_{v_{1},v_{2}}h,v_{3})=\frac{1}{2}\big((\nabla^{g}_{v_{1}}h)(v_{2},v_{3})+(\nabla^{g}_{v_{2}}h)(v_{1},v_{3})-(\nabla^{g}_{v_{3}}h)(v_{1},v_{2})\big),

for every
h\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
and
v_{3}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. In the following we consider the Ricci and scalar curvatures as smooth maps of Fréchet manifolds:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,

\displaystyle\qquad g

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{Ric}^{g}\,,

\displaystyle s\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)

\displaystyle\to C^{\infty}(M)\,,

\displaystyle\qquad g

\displaystyle\mapsto s^{g}\,.

Note that the Ricci tensor of
g
satisfies the well-known identity:

\nabla^{g\ast}\operatorname{Ric}^{g}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}s^{g}\,.

We introduce the
Lichnerowicz Laplacian

\Delta^{g}_{L}
associated to a Riemannian metric
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta^{g}_{L}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\Delta^{g}_{L}h=\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g}h+2\,h\circ_{g}\operatorname{Ric}^{g}-2\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)

where:

h\circ_{g}\operatorname{Ric}^{g}(v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\big(g(\iota_{v_{1}}h,\iota_{v_{2}}\operatorname{Ric}^{g})+g(\iota_{v_{2}}h,\iota_{v_{1}}\operatorname{Ric}^{g})\big)\,,

for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in TM
. Furthermore, we have defined
\mathrm{R}_{o}^{g}(h)\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
to be the symmetric tensor determined by:

\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)(v_{1},v_{2})=g(\mathrm{R}^{g}(\,\cdot\,,v_{1},v_{2},\,\cdot\,),h)\,,

for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in TM
. In terms of an orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
we can equivalently write:

\mathrm{R}^{g}_{o}(h)(v_{1},v_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\mathrm{R}^{g}(e_{i},v_{1})v_{2},e_{i})\,.

In particular, the local components of
\mathrm{R}^{g}(h)
are given simply by
\mathrm{R}^{g}(h)_{jk}=\mathrm{R}^{g}_{\,\,ijkl}h^{il}
.

The differentials of the operators
\operatorname{Ric}
and
s
at a point
g
will be denoted respectively by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{g}\mathrm{Ric}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\quad\mathrm{d}_{g}s\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\to C^{\infty}(M)\,.

We have the following classical result.

Lemma A.1
.

[
8
, §1]

The differentials of the Ricci and scalar curvature operators at
g
are respectively given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{L}h-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{g}s(h)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h-g(h,\operatorname{Ric}^{g})\,,

for every
h\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
.

We also define the Einstein curvature operator as the following smooth map:

\mathbb{G}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\to\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,\qquad g\mapsto\mathbb{G}^{g}=\operatorname{Ric}^{g}-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}g

in terms of the Einstein tensor of
g
. The previous lemma immediately implies the following result.

Corollary A.2
.

The differential of the Einstein curvature operator at
g
is given by:

\mathrm{d}_{g}\mathbb{G}(h)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{L}h-\delta^{g}\nabla^{g\ast}h-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)-\frac{1}{2}s^{g}h-\frac{1}{2}\big(\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)+\nabla^{g\ast}\nabla^{g\ast}h-g(\operatorname{Ric}^{g},h)\big)g\,,

for every
h\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
.

Define now the smooth map of Fréchet manifolds
\nu\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\to\Omega^{n}(M)
by
g\mapsto\nu_{g}
. Its differential at
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
is given by:

(A.1)

\mathrm{d}_{g}\nu\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\to\Omega^{n}(M)\,,\qquad h\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\nu_{g}

We use this expression in Section
3
to compute the critical points of the Einstein-Yang-Mills functional.

Given
\alpha\in\Omega^{r}(M)
, consider now the smooth map of Fréchet manifolds:

\star_{\alpha}\colon\mathrm{Met}(M)\to\Omega^{n-r}(M)\,,\quad g\mapsto\ast_{g}\alpha\,.

Its differential at
g\in\mathrm{Met}(M)
defines a linear map:

\mathrm{d}_{g}\star_{\alpha}\colon\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\to\Omega^{n-r}(M)

given by:

(A.2)

(\mathrm{d}_{g}\star_{\alpha})(h)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{g}(h)\ast_{g}\alpha+\ast_{g}(\alpha^{g}_{h})\,,\qquad h\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)\,,

where
(-)^{g}_{h}\colon\Omega^{r}(M)\to\Omega^{r}(M)
is the linear operation defined as
(\alpha)^{g}_{h}=0
if
r=0
and otherwise:

(A.3)

(\alpha)^{g}_{h}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{r+i}g(h(v_{i}),\alpha(v_{1},\ldots,\hat{v}_{i},\ldots,v_{r})),

where
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Equivalently:

(\alpha)^{g}_{h}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}h(e_{k})\wedge\iota_{e_{k}}\alpha

in terms of any orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
.

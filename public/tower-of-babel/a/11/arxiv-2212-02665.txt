Matrix factorizations and 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | k ) \mathfrak{gl}(m|k) -quantum invariants
A. Oblomkov, L. Rozansky

Abstract
. In our previous papers we used the Hilbert scheme of points on ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} in order to construct a triply graded link homology and its 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m ) \mathfrak{gl}(m) version. Here we extend the 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m ) \mathfrak{gl}(m) construction to super-algebras 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | k ) \mathfrak{gl}(m|k) .

2.2.
Matrix Factorizations

Matrix factorizations were introduced by Eisenbud
[
Eis80
]
and later
the subject was further developed by Orlov
[
Orl04
]
. Below we present only the basic definitions and do not present any proofs.

Let us remind that for an affine variety
\mathcal{Z}
and a function
F\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]
there exists a triangulated category
\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{Z},F)
. The objects of the category are pairs

\mathcal{F}=(M_{0}\oplus M_{1},D),\quad D:M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1},\quad D^{2}=F,

where
M_{i}
are free
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]
-modules of finite rank and
D
is a homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]
-modules.

Given
\mathcal{F}=(M,D)
and
\mathcal{G}=(N,D^{\prime})
the linear space of morphisms
\textup{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
consists of homomorphisms of

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]
-modules

\phi=\phi_{0}\oplus\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{i}\in\textup{Hom}(M_{i},N_{i})
such that
\phi\circ D=D^{\prime}\circ\phi
.
Two morphisms
\phi,\rho\in\textup{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
are homotopic if there is homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]
-modules
h=h_{0}\oplus h_{1}
,

h_{i}\in\textup{Hom}(M_{i},N_{i+1})
such that
\phi-\rho=D^{\prime}\circ h-h\circ D
.

In the paper
[
OR18c
]
we introduced a notion of equivariant matrix factorizations which we explain below.
First let us remind the construction of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex.

2.3.
Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

Suppose that
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra. Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is the complex
(V_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}),d)
with
V_{p}(\mathfrak{h})=U(\mathfrak{h})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{p}\mathfrak{h}
and differential
d_{ce}=d_{1}+d_{2}
where:

d_{1}(u\otimes x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i+1}ux_{i}\otimes x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{x}_{i}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p},

d_{2}(u\otimes x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p})=\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}u\otimes[x_{i},x_{j}]\wedge x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{x}_{i}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{x}_{j}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p},

Let us denote by
\Delta
the standard map
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}
defined by
x\mapsto x\otimes 1+1\otimes x
.
Suppose
V
and
W
are modules over the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
then we use notation

V\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}W
for the
\mathfrak{h}
-module which is isomorphic to
V\otimes W
as a vector space, the
\mathfrak{h}
-module structure being defined by
\Delta
. Respectively, for a given
\mathfrak{h}
-equivariant matrix factorization
\mathcal{F}=(M,D)
we denote by
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}\mathcal{F}

the
\mathfrak{h}
-equivariant matrix factorization
(CE_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}\mathcal{F},D+d_{ce})
. The
\mathfrak{h}
-equivariant structure on
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}\mathcal{F}
originates from the
left action of
U(\mathfrak{h})
that commutes with right action on
U(\mathfrak{h})
used in the construction of
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

A slight modification of the standard fact that
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is the resolution of the trivial module implies that
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}M
is a free resolution of the

\mathfrak{h}
-module
M
.

2.4.
Equivariant matrix factorizations

Let us assume that there is an action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathcal{Z}
and
F
is a
\mathfrak{h}
-invariant function.
Then we can construct the following triangulated category
\mathrm{MF}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathcal{Z},W)
.

The objects of the category are triples:

\mathcal{F}=(M,D,\partial),\quad(M,D)\in\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{Z},W)

where
M=M^{0}\oplus M^{1}
and
M^{i}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]\otimes V^{i}
,
V^{i}\in\textup{Mod}_{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\partial\in\oplus_{i>j}\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]}(\Lambda^{i}\mathfrak{h}\otimes M,\Lambda^{j}\mathfrak{h}\otimes M)
and
D
is an odd endomorphism

D\in\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]}(M,M)
such that

D^{2}=F,\quad D_{tot}^{2}=F,\quad D_{tot}=D+d_{ce}+\partial,

where the total differential
D_{tot}
is an endomorphism of
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}M
, that commutes with the
U(\mathfrak{h})
-action.

Note that we do not impose the equivariance condition on the differential
D
in our definition of matrix factorizations. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{F}=(M,D)\in\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{Z},F)
is a matrix factorization with

D
that commutes with
\mathfrak{h}
-action on
M
then
(M,D,0)\in\mathrm{MF}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathcal{Z},F)
.

There is a natural forgetful functor
\mathrm{MF}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathcal{Z},F)\to\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{Z},F)
that forgets about the correction differentials:

\mathcal{F}=(M,D,\partial)\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}:=(M,D).

Given two
\mathfrak{h}
-equivariant matrix factorizations
\mathcal{F}=(M,D,\partial)
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=(\tilde{M},\tilde{D},\tilde{\partial})
the space of morphisms
\textup{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\tilde{\mathcal{F}})
consists of
homotopy equivalence classes of elements
\Psi\in\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]}(\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}M,\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}\tilde{M})
such that
\Psi\circ D_{tot}=\tilde{D}_{tot}\circ\Psi
and
\Psi
commutes with

U(\mathfrak{h})
-action on
\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}M
. Two maps
\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}\in\textup{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\tilde{\mathcal{F}})
are homotopy equivalent if
there is

h\in\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Z}]}(\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}M,\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}\tilde{M})

such that
\Psi-\Psi^{\prime}=\tilde{D}_{tot}\circ h-h\circ D_{tot}
and
h
commutes with
U(h)
-action on

. Thus we get a version of the induction functor for non-reduced spaces:

{\textup{ind}}_{k}:=i_{k*}\circ p_{k}^{*}:\mathrm{MF}_{B_{k}^{2}}(\mathcal{X}(G_{k}),W)\times\mathrm{MF}_{B_{n-k}^{2}}(\mathcal{X}(G_{n-k}),W)\rightarrow\mathrm{MF}_{B_{n}^{2}}(\mathcal{X}(G_{n}),W)

It is shown in proposition 6.1 of
[
OR18c
]
that the Knörrer functor is compatible with the induction functor:

\textup{ind}_{k}\circ(\Phi_{\mathrm{kn}}\times\Phi_{\mathrm{kn}})=\Phi_{\mathrm{kn}}\circ\textup{ind}_{k}.

3.4.
Generators of the finite braid group action

Let us define
B^{2}_{n}
-equivariant embedding
i:\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n})\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
,
\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n}):=\mathfrak{b}_{n}\times B_{n}\times\mathfrak{n}_{n}
.
The pull-back of
\overline{W}
along the map
i
vanishes and the embedding
i
satisfies the conditions for existence of the push-forward

i_{*}:\mathrm{MF}_{B^{2}_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n}),0)\rightarrow\mathrm{MF}_{B^{2}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(G_{n}),\overline{W})
. We denote by
\underline{\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n})]}\in\mathrm{MF}_{B^{2}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n}),0)
the
matrix factorization with zero differential that is homologically non-trivial only in even homological degree. As it is shown in proposition 7.1 of
[
OR18c
]
the
push-forward

\bar{\mathds{1}}_{n}:=i_{*}(\underline{\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{X}}(B_{n})]})

is the unit in the convolution algebra. Similarly,
\mathds{1}_{n}:=\Phi_{\mathrm{kn}}(\bar{\mathds{1}}_{n})
is also a unit in non-reduced case.

Let us first discuss the case of the braids on two strands. The key to construction of the braid group action in
[
OR18c
]
is the following factorization in the case

n=2
:

\overline{W}(X,g,Y)=y_{12}(2g_{11}x_{11}+g_{21}x_{12})g_{21}/\det,

where
\det=\det(g)
and

g=\begin{bmatrix}g_{11}&g_{12}\\
g_{21}&g_{22}\end{bmatrix},\quad X=\begin{bmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
0&x_{22}\end{bmatrix},\quad Y=\begin{bmatrix}0&y_{12}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

Thus we can define the following strongly equivariant Koszul matrix factorization:

\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{+}:=(\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathcal{X}}(G_{2})]\otimes\Lambda\langle\theta\rangle,D,0,0)\in\mathrm{MF}_{B^{2}_{2}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(G_{2}),\overline{W}),

\quad D=\frac{g_{12}y_{12}}{\det}\theta+\left[g_{11}(x_{11}-x_{22})+g_{21}x_{12}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},

where
\Lambda\langle\theta\rangle
is the exterior algebra with one generator.

This matrix factorization corresponds to the positive elementary braid on two strands. The negative elementary braid is defined with twisting of the
B^{2}_{2}
-action. We define:

\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{-}:=\mathcal{C}_{+}\langle-\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\rangle.

Using the induction functor we can extend the previous definition on the case of the arbitrary number of strands. For that we introduce an insertion
functor:

\overline{\textup{Ind}}_{k,k+1}:\mathrm{MF}_{B_{2}^{2}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(G_{2}),\overline{W})\rightarrow\mathrm{MF}_{B_{n}^{2}}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}(G_{n}),\overline{W})

\overline{\textup{Ind}}_{k,k+1}(\mathcal{F}):=\overline{\textup{ind}}_{k+1}(\overline{\textup{ind}}_{k-1}(\bar{\mathds{1}}_{k-1}\times\mathcal{F})\times\bar{\mathds{1}}_{n-k-1}),

and similarly we define non-reduced insertion functor

\textup{Ind}_{k,k+1}:\mathrm{MF}_{B_{2}^{2}}(\mathcal{X}(G_{2}),W)\rightarrow\mathrm{MF}_{B_{n}^{2}}(\mathcal{X}(G_{n}),W).

Thus we define the generators of the braid group as follows:

\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}:=\overline{\textup{Ind}}_{k,k+1}(\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon}),\quad\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}:=\textup{Ind}_{k,k+1}(\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}).

For an element
\beta\in\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
, if we can choose a presentation

\beta=\sigma_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\dots\sigma_{i_{l}}^{\epsilon_{l}}
then the element
\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta}:=\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{(i_{1})}\bar{\star}\dots\bar{\star}\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\epsilon_{l}}^{(i_{l})}
does not depends on the presentation of the braid. Respectively, we use notation

\mathcal{C}_{\beta}=\Phi_{\mathrm{kn}}(\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta})
. Respectively, the finite part
\Phi_{n}
of the homomorphism
\Phi_{n}^{\textup{aff}}
from proposition
2.1.1
is defined by
\Phi_{n}(\beta)=\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
.

3.5.
Braid closure

The braid closure functor
\mathbb{L}
is very natural in context of the reduced spaces:

\mathbb{L}:\overline{\mathrm{MF}_{n}^{\textup{st}}}\to\mathscr{D}_{B}(\left(\mathfrak{b}\times\mathfrak{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\right)^{\textup{st}}),\quad\mathbb{L}(\bar{\mathcal{F}})=j_{e}^{*}(\bar{\mathcal{F}}).

where
j_{e}:\left(\mathfrak{b}_{n}\times 1\times\mathfrak{n}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\right)^{\textup{st}}\to\left(\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\right)^{\textup{st}}
is the inclusion map.

As it is shown in
[
OR18c
]
the
B
acts freely on

(\mathfrak{b}\times\mathfrak{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\textup{st}}
and the corresponding quotient is a smooth manifold that
we call free flag Hilbert scheme:

\mathrm{FHilb}^{\mathrm{free}}_{n}=(\mathfrak{b}\times\mathfrak{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\textup{st}}/B.

Let
(X,Y,v)
be coordinates on the product
\mathfrak{b}\times\mathfrak{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
then
similarly, to the previous case we define super-group restriction functor:

\mathscr{R}^{\prime}_{m|k}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{C}\otimes(\Lambda^{\star}\mathbb{C}^{n},d_{m|k}),\quad d_{m|k}=\sum_{i=1}(X^{m}Y^{k}v)_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}.

The closure functor intertwines the functors
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}_{m|k}
and
\mathscr{R}_{m|k}
because of base change relation:

\mathscr{R}^{\prime}_{m|k}\circ\mathbb{L}\circ\Phi=\mathbb{L}\circ\Phi\circ\mathscr{R}_{m|k}.

It is shown in
[
OR18b
]
that for any
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{MF}^{\textup{st}}_{n}
we have:

\textup{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}_{1})=\mathbb{H}(\mathsf{CH}^{\textup{st}}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathcal{F})).

On the other hand in
[
OR18c
]
it was shown that

\textup{Hom}(\Phi(\bar{\mathcal{F}}),\mathcal{C}_{1})=\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathbb{L}(\bar{\mathcal{F}}))^{T}.

Combining all these observations we obtain another construction for the homology from the introduction

\mathrm{HH}_{m|k}(\beta)=\mathrm{CE}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathscr{R}^{\prime}_{m|k}\circ\mathbb{L}(\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta}))^{T}.

OR18c

(also see

The Harmonic GBC Function Map is a Bijection if the Target Domain is Convex
Chongyang Deng, Tsung-wei Hu, Ming-Jun Lai

Abstract
Harmonic generalized barycentric coordinates (GBC) functions have been used for cartoon animation since an early work in 2006 [ 15 ] . A computational procedure was further developed in [ 20 ] for deformation between any two polygons. The bijectivity of the map based on harmonic GBC functions is still murky in the literature. In this paper we present an elementary proof of the bijection of the harmonic GBC map transforming from one arbitrary polygonal domain V V to a convex polygonal domain W W . This result is further extended to a more general harmonic map from one simply connected domain V V to a convex domain W W if the harmonic map preserves the orientation of the boundary of the domain V V . In addition, we shall point out that the harmonic GBC map is also a diffeomorphism over the interior of V V to the interior of W W . Finally, we remark on how to construct a harmonic GBC map from V V to W W when the number of vertices of V V is different from the number of vertices of W W and how to construct harmonic GBC functions over a polygonal domain with a hole or holes. We also point out that it is possible to use the harmonic GBC map to deform a nonconvex polygon V V to another nonconvex polygon W W by a good arrangement of the boundary map between ∂ V \partial V and ∂ W \partial W . Several numerical deformations based on images are presented to show the effectiveness of the map based on bivariate spline approximation of the harmonic GBC functions.



Key Words:
GBC functions, harmonic GBC map, harmonic map, bijection

Main Results and Their Proofs

In this section, let us start with harmonic functions over simply connected domains in

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
V
and
W
be two simply connected domains. Typically,
V
and
W

are polygons.
Let
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a harmonic function, that is,
\Delta u=0
over

\mathring{V}
, where
\Delta=D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}
is the standard Laplace operator. For two
harmonic functions
u,v
on
V
, let

{\bf F}=(u(x,y),v(x,y))
which will be called a harmonic map on
V
which maps
V
to

\hbox{Im}({\bf F})
, the image of
{\bf F}
over
V
.

The harmonic functions have been studied for more than 100 years and many properties are
known. Let us mention a few properties without proofs which will be used in the following study.
The harmonic function
u
will not achieve its maximum nor minimum inside
V

except for a constant function
u
. When
u
is a harmonic, so are its derivatives.
If a harmonic function

is a constant over
a disk

(open set) inside
V
, then
u
is a constant over
V
. Indeed,
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
over
V
will be zero over
D
and hence
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
is zero over
V
as a harmonic function is like an
analytic function.

Note that when
V,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two separate polygons,

\partial V
and
\partial W
are piecewise linear boundaries, respectively.
By the properties of GBC functions
(
1
) and the definition of
{\bf F}
(
3
),
one can easily check that
{\bf F}|_{\partial V}
is a homeomorphism from

\partial V
to
\partial W
.
We can also see that
{\bf F}|_{\partial V}
preserve the orientation of the boundary
vertices of

as both of them are in the counter-clockwise direction.

Our first main result in this paper is to establish the following

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two polygons.
Let
{\bf F}=(u(x,y),v(x,y))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\mapsto W

be a harmonic GBC function which maps from

to

).
If

is convex, then
{\bf F}
is a bijection from
V
to
W
.

We remark that the above result can be generalized to the general harmonic map

{\bf F}=(u,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\mapsto W
under the assumptions that

•

(1)
{\bf F}|_{\partial V}
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism from the boundary
\partial V
of
V
to the boundary
\partial W

W
.

•

(2)
{\bf F}\in C^{0}(V)\cap C^{1}(\mathring{V})
.

In this general setting, we have

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
V,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be two simply connected domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose

are harmonic functions over
V
.
Let
{\bf F}=(u(x,y),v(x,y))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\mapsto W

.
Suppose that
{\bf F}
satisfies (1) and (2) above.
Then if

is a bijection from

to

.

Note that the map
{\bf F}
is a bijection if
{\bf F}
is surjective and also injective
in the sense that
{\bf F}
maps
V
onto
W
and the
mapping is one-to-one. Before proving Theorem
2.1
, we first establish the surjectivity.

Lemma 2.3
.

As the setting in Theorem
2.2
,
{\bf F}
maps

Proof.

We will show that the image
\hbox{Im}({\bf F})
of

\hbox{Im}({\bf F})=W
.

First of all, if

).
In general, we claim

.
Otherwise, there exists a point

Then there is a line separate

That is, there exist real numbers

For

On the other hand, we want to show that

Now suppose

Recall that
{\bf F}=(u,v)^{\top}

Suppose that

{\rm det}(J_{0})={\rm det}([\nabla u(x_{0},y_{0});\nabla v(x_{0},y_{0})])={\rm det}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x_{0},y_{0})&\frac{\partial}{\partial y}u(x_{0},y_{0})\cr\frac{\partial}{\partial x}v(x_{0},y_{0})&\frac{\partial}{\partial y}v(x_{0},y_{0})\end{bmatrix}\neq 0.

(4)

Since

The Taylor expansion at

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf F}({\bf x})-{\bf F}({\bf x_{0}})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|J_{0}\cdot({\bf x}-{\bf x_{0}})\right\|+o(\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf x_{0}}\right\|)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf x_{0}}\right\|+o(\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf x_{0}}\right\|).

(6)

Hence there exist a

{\bf F}({\bf x})\neq{\bf F}({\bf x_{0}})

The compactness of

to its image
\hbox{Im}({\bf F})
over the ball.

The discussion above leads to the following

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that the Jacobian determinant of
{\bf F}
is not zero at every point of

.
Then

is a local homeomorphism on
\mathring{V}
,

.

Unfortunately, the nonzero Jacobian determinant of

{\bf F}
over

does not guarantee the global injectivity as shown in the following example:

Example 2.5
.

Let
{\bf F}=(u,v)
with

. Then the Jacobian determinant is equals to

for all

.

On the other hand, due to the GBC property, we can see that when

is a triangle, the

is an injection. Indeed, suppose that there are two points
(x_{1},y_{1})
and

in

.
Then we have

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

Let us begin with the set of critical points of harmonic function.

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose that

The critical set

Lemma 2.7

If

Proof.

Let

.
Note that

we have

WLOG, let

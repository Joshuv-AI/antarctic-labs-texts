Foliations and Galois Theory in Positive Characteristic
Przemysław Grabowski

Abstract
We prove a Galois-type correspondence between compositions of purely inseparable field extensions (including infinite ones) and subalgebras of differential operators. This correspondence can be utilized to establish a connection between separable field extensions and purely inseparable field extensions. Specifically, it serves as a progression towards gaining a deeper comprehension of a foliation theory on varieties in positive characteristic.



1
Introduction

Geometric Motivation

In algebraic geometry over
\mathbb{C}
, one can study foliations on normal varieties. A foliation is a subsheaf of a tangent bundle that is closed under Lie brackets. More details on this notion can be found in books
[
Bru15
]
and
[
Foli16
]
. Foliations naturally arise as a generalisation of smooth dominant morphisms. These morphisms inject into foliations by taking their relative tangent bundles. This is an important injection for geometrical applications. However, an analog of this theory in positive characteristic, a theory of
1
-foliations introduced by Ekedahl in
[
Eke87
]
, fails at injecting smooth dominant morphisms into
1
-foliations. Indeed, we have the following.

Example 1.1
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
f,g
be morphisms
\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
corresponding to ring maps
k[x]\to k[x,y]
and
k[x+y^{p}]\to k[x,y]
respectively. These morphisms are not equal, but their relative tangent bundles are equal. Indeed, each is spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
.

Although the operation is not injective, it is useful in a different way. It provides a correspondence between
1
-foliations and purely inseparable morphisms of height at most
1
, see the Proposition 2.4 in
[
Eke87
]
.
It was successfully used to study positive characteristic geometry in many papers, e.g.
[
Miy87
]
,
[
Eke88
]
,
[
Shen10
]
, and
[
Lan15
]
. Nevertheless, the lack of the injectivity from smooth dominant morphisms is problematic, because it prevents us from studying foliations in a characteristic free way.
This issue motivates the following question.

Question 1.2
.

Is it possible to extend
1
-foliation data to get a theory that admits an injection from smooth dominant morphisms to new extended data?

The answer is affirmative. A solution is to use algebras of differential operators instead of modules of vector fields. And, as in the case of vector fields, we have to impose some extra conditions on these algebras. For the modules this is being a Lie algebra. For the algebras this will be being a
\infty
-foliation
.
We will define this notion in the Definition
1.5
after stating our correspondence
1.3
. This correspondence explains what information
arbitrary
algebras of differential operators encode.
After that, we will show that there is an injection from smooth dominant morphisms into
\infty
-foliations, the Proposition
2.22
.
However, in this paper, we work on generic points only, i.e. with fields, to simplify our exposition and to connect our results with Galois theories. Therefore, we only prove an analog of such proposition for fields in positive characteristic.

Our main inspiration is a paper
[
Eke87
]
by Ekedahl.
According to our knowledge, Ekedahl‘s paper is the first one in which an idea of using differential operators instead of vector fields to study foliations in positive characteristic appears. However, he does not study arbitrary algebras of differential operators there, only some of them that he calls
m
-foliations on varieties. Moreover, dual objects of these subalgebras are formal groupoids. This observation has motivated a recent paper
[
McQui22
]
by McQuillan. In his paper, he proposes a characteristic free definition of a foliation in terms of formal groupoids. In the introduction, he states that his notion is a generalisation of Ekedahl‘s foliations.

Our Results

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
k\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\geq 0}K^{p^{n}}
. We assume that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension.

In this paragraph, we state our Galois-type correspondence.
This result is related to purely inseparable Galois theories. These are theories establishing bijections between purely inseparable field extensions and some other structures. We quickly review some of them before the statement, because our correspondence partially overlaps with them.

Historically, the first one is a Jacobson Correspondence from
[
Jac44
]
that covers only subfields
K\supset W\supset K^{p}
. This says that these subfields are in a bijection with
p
-Lie subalgebras of a tangent bundle
TK
. We recall it in
4.3
. Later, in books
[
Jac64
]
,
[
Jac80
]
, the Jacobson Correspondence was extended to a Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence, we recall it in
4.1
.
This is a bijection between arbitrary subfields of
K
and closed
K
-subalgebras of additive operators on
K
, i.e.
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
.
This bijection restricted to subfields
W\subset K
such that
K/W
is purely inseparable maps these subfields onto finite
K
-subalgebras of differential operators
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
by the Lemma
3.5
. This could be called a Jacobson–Bourbaki purely inseparable Galois theory.

There are also other Galois theories that we would like to mention though they do not overlap with our correspondence. In a paper
[
Swe68
]
, Sweedler establishes a bijection between modular subfields
W\subset K
and higher derivations. These subfields are purely inseparable subfields satisfying an extra condition. This bijection is compared with the Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence in Wechter‘s thesis
[
Wach15
]
. Recently, in a paper
[
BraWal22
]
, a new theory was proposed by Brantner and Waldron. Their bijection is between subfields of finite exponents
K\supset W\supset K^{p^{r}}
,
r\geq 0
, and partition Lie algebroids. These algebroids are a derived algebraic geometry generalisation of relative tangent bundles.

We have already observed that the Jacobson–Bourbaki purely inseparable Galois theory states that there is a bijection between subfields
W\subset K
such that
K/W
is purely inseparable and finite
K
-subalgebras
\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}(K)
. This follows from the Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence, but this correspondence says nothing about infinite dimensional subalgebras. Indeed, these subalgebras are not closed, and their closures in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
often coincide, see the Remark
4.26
. Nevertheless, it is possible to extend this purely inseparable Galois theory to cover all subalgebras by replacing purely inseparable subfields with towers of them. This is a part of our main theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.22
)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
k\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\geq 0}K^{p^{n}}
. We assume that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension.

There are natural bijections between the following data on
K
:

•

power towers on
K
: a tower of subfields
W_{\bullet}\subset K
, where
\bullet\geq 0
, such that
i\leq j\Rightarrow W_{j}\cdot K^{p^{i}}=W_{i}
,

•

Jacobson sequences on
K
:
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)
, see the Definition
4.6
,

•

K
-subalgebras of differential operators on
K
:
K\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{k}(K)

Explicitly,
these bijections are given by the following operations:

•

an iterated tangent bundle:
W_{\bullet}\mapsto(T_{W_{0}/W_{1}},T_{W_{1}/W_{2}},T_{W_{2}/W_{3}},\ldots)
,

•

an iterated
\operatorname{Ann}(\bullet)
:
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)\mapsto\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})
,

•

an algebra of relative differential operators:
W_{\bullet}\mapsto\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
,

•

an unpacking:
\mathcal{D}\mapsto\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}\cap TK,\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n}\coloneqq d(K/W_{n-1})(\mathcal{D})\cap TW_{n-1},\ldots\right)
.

Moreover, we have that ranks
\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\coloneqq\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
are finite and nonincreasing:
\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geq\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1})
, and they satisfy
p^{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i})}=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+1}}(W_{i})
.

This theorem covers all
K
-subalgebras of differential operators. And, it does not impose any additional conditions on them such as being closed in a topology.
In this correspondence, purely inseparable subfields are replaced by power towers on
K
.
However, an operation
W\mapsto W\cdot K^{p^{\bullet}}
injects these subfields into power towers on
K
. The image consists of eventually constant power towers on
K
, see the Proposition
2.12
. This recovers the Jacobson–Bourbaki purely inseparable Galois theory from our theorem. Finally, the data of Jacobson sequences with an operation of unpacking is our generalisation of a comparison of the Jacobson Correspondence
4.3
with the Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence
4.1
, see the Remark
4.4
.

Moreover, we can exploit the operation
W\mapsto W\cdot K^{p^{\bullet}}
more. Indeed, this operation extends well to all subfields of
K
, but then it is not injective anymore. It is made precise in the following proposition.

Proposition 1.4

(Proposition
5.1
)
.

Let
W
be a subfield of
K
. Let
W_{\bullet}\coloneqq W\cdot K^{p^{\bullet}}
be its power tower on
K
,
\bullet\geq 0
, then power towers of
W
and
\left(W\cdot k\right)^{s}
on
K
coincide, and
\bigcap_{\bullet\geq 0}W_{\bullet}=\left(W\cdot k\right)^{s}
, where
()^{s}
is a separable closure in
K
.

In particular, an operation
W\mapsto W\cdot K^{p^{\bullet}}
is injective on subfields
W\subset K
such that
k\subset W\subset K
, and
W
is separably closed in
K
.

An image of this operation is particularly well behaved if
K/W
is a separable extension, see the Definition
2.21
. And, these extensions are an analog of smooth dominant morphisms. The image lands in
\infty
-foliations.

Definition 1.5
.

A
\infty
-foliation on
K
is a power tower
W_{\bullet}
on
K
such that it is of a constant degree, i.e.
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+1}}(W_{i})=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+2}}(W_{i+1})
for all
i\geq 0
.

In general, by the Theorem
1.3
, we know that
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+1}}(W_{i})\geq\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+2}}(W_{i+1})
. So, these power towers are visibly distinguished among all power towers. Finally, the following is an analog of the injection of smooth dominant morphisms into foliations for fields in positive characteristic.

Proposition 1.6

(Proposition
2.22
)
.

Let
W
be a subfield of
K
such that
k\subset W\subset K
and
K/W
is separable, then a power tower of
W
on
K
is a
\infty
-foliation on
K
.

Layout

The paper consists of five sections. The first one is the introduction. The second section
2
establishes a basic theory of power towers. The third section
3

4

In the last section

1

2
-foliations.

2
Power Towers

In this section, we study power towers. We recall some preliminaries. The most important one is a notion of a

-basis

. We define power towers and we prove basic facts about them that they are towers of extensions of exponents at most

2.9
,

However, not all power towers are power towers of subfields. We give an example of this in

Lemma 2.9

Let

Proposition 2.10

(Nonincreasing Degrees)
.

Let

be a power tower on

. It satisfies the following sequence of inequalities:

\operatorname{dim}_{W_{1}}(K)\geq\operatorname{dim}_{W_{2}}(W_{1})\geq\ldots\geq\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n}}(W_{n-1})\geq\ldots\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
. We are going to show that
\operatorname{dim}_{w_{n+1}}(W_{n})\geq\operatorname{dim}_{w_{n+2}}(W_{n+1})
by writing some generators of these extensions explicitly. Let
x_{i}\in K^{p^{n+1}}
, where
i=1,\ldots,r
, be a minimal subset such that
W_{n+1}=W_{n+2}(x_{i})

We claim that
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n+2}(x_{i})}(W_{n+2}(x_{i}^{1/p}))=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n+2}}(W_{n+1})
. Indeed, if not, then there is a nontrivial

between monomials

is a

. This contradicts the definition of

‘s.

Now, we have a series of extensions

that gives us

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n+1}}(W_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n+1}(x_{i}^{1/p})}(W_{n})\cdot\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n+1}}(W_{n+1}(x_{i}^{1/p}))

Example 2.17

(Not Algebraically Integrable Power Tower)

Let

be a perfect field of characteristic

Let

W_{n}\coloneqq k(x+y^{p}+\ldots+y^{p^{n-1}},y^{p^{n}}).

The tower

Indeed, if it was a power tower of a subfield

In the above example, we used an object

Definition 2.18

Let

K
. We define a field

W_{\infty}\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\geq 0}W_{n}.

We call it a field of
first integrals
of the power tower
W_{\bullet}
.

First integrals make a recovery of a subfield
W
from its power tower on
K
possible if
W
contains
k
, and
W
is separably closed in
K
. We prove it later in the Proposition
5.1
, because our proof uses some facts about differential operators from the next section
3
.

n
-Foliations

In a paper
[
Eke87
]
, Ekedahl introduced a notion of a
n
-foliation. These are subalgebras of differential operators on a variety satisfying an extra condition. If one transports his definition verbatim to the case of fields, and then uses our correspondence
4.22
to get a power tower, then they would end up with the following notion of a
n
-foliation for fields.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
0\leq n\leq\infty
.
Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
. It is called a
n
-foliation
if it is of length
n
, and it satisfies
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i+1}}(W_{i})=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{j+1}}(W_{j})
for
0\leq i,j<n
.

A rank of a
n
-foliation
W_{\bullet}
is
\operatorname{log}_{p}(\operatorname{dim}_{W_{1}}(K))
.

Fibrations Into
\infty
-Foliations

We prove a positive characteristic version of an injection of smooth dominant morphisms into foliations for fields.
We label it an
injection of fibrations into
\infty
-foliations
.
We start with an example.

Example 2.20
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

Let
K\supset W\supset k
be both purely transcendental extensions.
Then, a power tower of
W
on
K
is a
\infty
-foliation. Indeed, we can write
W=k(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
and
K=W(y_{1},\ldots y_{m})
, where
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
are transcendental bases for
W/k
and
K/W
respectively.
Then

W_{n}=W(y_{1}^{p^{n}},\ldots y_{m}^{p^{n}})=k(x_{1},\ldots x_{r},y_{1}^{p^{n}},\ldots y_{m}^{p^{n}})
.
So,
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{n}}(W_{n-1})=p^{m}
is constant. Therefore,
W_{\bullet}
is a
\infty
-foliation on
K
of rank
m
.

The above example is a special case of the injection. Before stating the general case, we recall the definition of (not finite) separable extensions of fields, see 030O at
[
Sta23
]
.

Definition 2.21
.

A finitely generated extension of fields
A/B
is separable if it admits a separating transcendence basis, i.e. there are algebraically independent over
B
elements
x_{i}\in A
such that
A
is a separable closure of
B(x_{i})
in
A
.

Here is the statement of the injection.

Proposition 2.22

(Fibrations Inject Into
\infty
-Foliations)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

A power tower of a subfield
k\subset W\subset K
on
K
such that
K/W
is separable is a
\infty
-foliation on
K
.

Proof.

We can assume that
W
is separably closed in
K
since power towers of
W
and
W^{s}
coincide.

Let
y_{i}
be a
p
-basis of
W
over
k
, see the Definition
2.6
. Let
x_{i}
be a separating transcendence basis of
K
over
W
. Then
x_{i},y_{i}
are a
p
-basis of
K
over
k
.
In particular, we have that
k(x_{i})^{s}=W
,
W(y_{j})^{s}=K
, and
k(x_{i},y_{j})^{s}=K
.

From the Example
2.20
, we know that a power tower of
k(x_{i})
on
k(x_{i},y_{j})
is a
\infty
-foliation. The rest of the proof is just pulling back this information to a power tower of
W
on
K
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer.
We have that
k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n}})^{s}=W_{n}
, where the separable closure is taken in
K
.
Moreover, we claim that

k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n}})\otimes_{k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n+1}})}W_{n+1}\simeq W_{n+1}(y_{j}^{p^{n}})=W_{n}

via natural maps to
K
.
Of course, we have that
W_{n+1}(y_{j}^{p^{n}})\subset W_{n}
.
We also have
W_{n+1}(y_{j}^{p^{n}})=W_{n+1}(y_{j}^{p^{n}})^{s}\supset k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n}})^{s}=W_{n}
. Therefore
W_{n+1}(y_{j}^{p^{n}})=W_{n}
.

Finally, we can conclude that a dimension of
k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n}})
over
k(x_{i},y_{j}^{p^{n+1}})
is the same as a dimension of
W_{n}
over
W_{n+1}
, because the later comes from the former by extending coefficients to
W_{n+1}
and that operation is flat.
∎

3
Differential Operators

This section is devoted to recalling differential operators in positive characteristic. First, we define these operators
3.1
, describe a filtration by orders, and embed them into additive operators
3.3
. This makes a set of all of differential operators an algebra. This algebra admits a second filtration that we call a canonical filtration by finite subalgebras
3.4
. After that, we demonstrate that any field extension induces a surjective differential between algebras of differential operators
3.13
. This extends a well known differential between tangent spaces. Moreover, we recall how to write down a differential operator in coordinates
3
, and how these formulas behave with respect to differentials between
p
-powers
3.16
.

Construction

This paragraph is based mostly on sections 16.3 and 16.8 from
[
EGAIV
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K/W
be an extension of fields. Let
p_{1},p_{2}:K\to K\otimes_{W}K
be projections. They are
a\mapsto a\otimes 1,1\otimes a
respectively.
We consider
K\otimes_{W}K
as a
K
-algebra via
p_{1}
.
Let
\Delta:K\otimes_{W}K\to K
be a diagonal map. It is given by
a\otimes b\mapsto ab
. Let
J_{K/W}
be the kernel of
\Delta
. Let
n\geq 0
be an integer.

For the extension, we define its space of
principal parts
of order at most
n
by
\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/W}\coloneqq K\otimes_{W}K/J^{n+1}_{K/W}
.

We define a set of differential operators of order at most
n
on
K
relative to
W
by
\operatorname{Diff}^{\leq n}_{W}(K)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/W},K)
.

A set of all
differential operators
on
K
relative to
W
is defined by
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Diff}^{\leq n}_{W}(K)
, where the identifications come from natural surjections of spaces of principal parts.

Here are some formal observations following from the construction.

Definition 3.2
.

The tensor product
K\otimes_{W}K
decomposes into
K\oplus J_{K/W}
by the choice of
p_{1}
, i.e. it is an augmented
K
-algebra. This implies that
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)
decomposes too. We denote this decomposition by
K\oplus{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
.

In the above, we can observe that
{\swabfamily E}(K/W)\cap\operatorname{Diff}^{\leq 1}_{W}(K)
is
T_{K/W}
.

Observation 3.3
.

By using
p_{2}
, we can embed the set
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)
into
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)
. This map is given by

\phi:\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/W}\to K\mapsto D_{\phi}\coloneqq\phi\circ p_{2}:K\to K\otimes_{W}K\to\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/W}\to K,

where
n
is an integer. It is an injection, and the image is closed under composition. This makes the set of differential operators an algebra of operators on
K
. It is covered in the Propositions 16.8.4 and 16.8.9 in
[
EGAIV
]
. Consequently, the algebra of differential operators is a filtered algebra in a natural way. The filtration comes from subsets of order at most
n
. We call this filtration an
order filtration
.

Moreover, the image of
\operatorname{Diff}^{\leq n}_{W}(K)
is precisely a set of operators
D
such that for any
n+1
elements
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in K
we have
[a_{0},[a_{1},[\ldots,[a_{n},D]\ldots]=0
, where
[a,b]=a\circ b-b\circ a
. And, the image of
{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
are differential operators
D
such that
D(1)=0
.

The order filtration is important, and any mention of a graded algebra of differential operators is exactly
the graded algebra associated to this filtration.

Canonical Filtration

We show that in positive characteristic an algebra of differential operators admits another filtration. The new one is given by finite subalgebras corresponding to a power tower of
K^{p^{\infty}}
on
K
. We call it a canonical filtration. It is a well known structure, see for example
[
Smith86
]
, but it is usually written with
\operatorname{End}
instead of
\operatorname{Diff}
. Anyway, our Lemma
3.5
says that both ways are identical.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

Definition 3.4
.

We define
\operatorname{Diff}(K)\coloneqq\operatorname{Diff}_{k}(K)
, and
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)\coloneqq\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n}}}(K)
.

We call a set of
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{\bullet}}(K)
, where
\bullet\geq 0
, a
canonical filtration
of
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
.

We start with a useful observation.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
K/W
be a subfield of finite exponent, then we have
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)=\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)
.

Proof.

We know that one is a subalgebra of the other by
3.3
. And, we can compute from the construction that they have the same finite dimensions over
K
. Therefore, they are equal.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The Lemma
3.5
is not true if a subfield
K/W/k
is not of finite exponent.

We check that the canonical filtration is a filtration.

Proposition 3.7
.

Algebras
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{\bullet}}(K)
are subalgebras of
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
, and we have
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n+1}}(K)
for
n\geq 0
, and
\operatorname{Diff}(K)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
.

Proof.

These algebras are subalgebras from the construction of the embedding in the Observation
3.3
. The inclusions are trivial from the Lemma
3.5
.

Let
J_{p^{n}}
be an ideal in
K\otimes_{k}K
generated by
p^{n}
-powers of elements from
J_{K/k}
, then
K\otimes_{K^{p^{n}}}K=K\otimes_{k}K/J_{p^{n}}
. So, it is enough to compare the ideal
J_{p^{n}}
with powers of the ideal
J_{K/k}
. For example, we have that
J_{K/k}^{p^{n-1}}\supset J_{p^{n}}
. Therefore, we have

\operatorname{Diff}_{k}^{\leq p^{n-1}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K).

Hence, the ascending sum is everything.
∎

A corollary of the above proposition is an infamous fact that differential operators in positive characteristic are not finitely generated. In characteristic zero, the finite generation is true. Even more, they are generated in degree
1
by derivations.

Corollary 3.8
.

A
K
-algebra
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
is not finitely generated unless
K=k
.

Proof.

If
K\neq k
, then each extension
K/K^{p^{n}}
is nontrivial, of finite dimension, and the dimensions grow with
n
. Hence, subalgebras
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
are finite and the inclusions between them are proper. Assume that
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
is finitely generated by elements
D_{i}
, then each of these operators has a finite order. In particular, each
D_{i}
belongs to a subalgebra
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
for some
n
. This means that there must be a single
N
such that all
D_{i}
are in
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{N}}(K)
, but this subset is a subalgebra, therefore the subalgebra generated by all
D_{i}
would be a subalgebra of
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{N}}(K)
. This is a contradiction, because this subalgebra is
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
.
∎

Explicit Formulas via Coordinates

We establish calculus-like formulas for writing down differential operators in coordinates.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

We know from the Definition
2.6
that
K/k
admits a
p
-basis. Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}
be a
p
-basis of
K/k
, where
N
is a transcendence degree of
K
over
k
. Then (16.11.1) from
[
EGAIV
]
says that monomials in
dx_{i}\coloneqq p_{2}(x_{i})-p_{1}(x_{i})
that are nonzero in
\mathcal{P}_{K/k}^{n}
are a basis of
\mathcal{P}_{K/k}^{n}
(the empty monomial
1
is included). We write these monomials using a multi-index notation
d(x)^{e}\coloneqq\prod d(x_{i})^{e_{i}}
, where
e_{i}\geq 0
. We denote a dual basis by
D^{e}
. These can be identified with classical symbols.

Notation 3.9
.

We define a symbol
\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x_{i}^{a}}
to be
D^{e}
for
e=(0,\ldots,0,a,0,\ldots,0)
, where
a\geq 0
is at the
i
-th position.

Lemma 3.10

((16.11.1.7) in
[
EGAIV
]
)
.

We have that
\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x_{i}^{a}}(x_{i}^{b})={{b}\choose{a}}x_{i}^{b-a}
,
and
\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x_{i}^{a}}(x_{j}^{b})=0

for
i\neq j
,
a,b\geq 0
.
∎

There is a unique way to express any differential operator in terms of the above symbols.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Diff}(K)
be a differential operator. Then there are unique coefficients
A_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}}\in K
, almost all equal zero, such that

D=\sum_{a_{i}\geq 0}\left(A_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{a_{i}!}\frac{\partial^{a_{i}}}{\partial x_{i}^{a_{i}}}\right).

Moreover, we have
D(\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{a_{i}})=A_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}}
. Therefore, differential operators are determined by their values on monomials
\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
.

Furthermore, elements from
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
are determined by a property:
D\in\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
if and only if
A_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}}=0
if there is an
i
such that
a_{i}\geq p^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a differential operator on
K
of order at most
M
. It can be uniquely written as a
K
-linear combination of operators
D^{e}
, where
\sum|e_{i}|\leq M
.
Now, by the Lemma
3.10
, we can compute that
D^{e}=\prod_{i}\frac{1}{e_{i}!}\frac{\partial^{e_{i}}}{\partial x_{i}^{e_{i}}}
. This finishes the proof of the unique representation.

The last part follows from the explicit description of
K\otimes_{K^{p^{n}}}K
in terms of monomials
dx^{e}
. Indeed, it is
K
-spanned by monomials with
e_{i}<p^{n}
for all
i
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

We can reduce a number of symbols needed to write any operator as a polynomial in them. Indeed, if we have a
p
-adic expansion of a natural number
a=a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+\ldots
, then an operator
\prod_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x_{i}^{p^{m}}}^{\circ a_{m}}
differs from
\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x_{i}^{a}}
by a integral scalar that is invertible modulo
p
. This means that the symbols
\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x_{i}^{p^{m}}}
generate the algebra of differential operators. And, the ones with
m<n
generate
\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
.

Differential between Algebras of Differential Operators

It is well known that for any map between two spaces
f:X\to Y
, we get a differential between their tangent spaces
df:TX\to TY
. It turns out that
df
naturally extends to a map
df:\operatorname{Diff}(X)\to\operatorname{Diff}(Y)
between their spaces of differential operators. We present how it works for fields. An original construction of
df
is covered around the Proposition (16.4.18) in
[
EGAIV
]
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

Let
k\subset W\subset K
be a subfield. This induces a map:

W\otimes_{k}W\to K\otimes_{k}K,

that, for every
n\geq 0
, induces a map

\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{W/k}=W\otimes_{k}W/J_{W/k}^{n+1}\xrightarrow{\Delta^{\leq n}_{K/W}}K\otimes_{k}K/J_{K/k}^{n+1}=\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/k}.

We can use these to define differentials. Indeed, we have a series of
K
-linear maps:

\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/k},K)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{W/k}\otimes K,K)\leftarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{W}(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{W/k},K)\leftarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{W}(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{W/k},W)\otimes K,

that are given respectively by
\phi\mapsto\phi\circ(\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{W/k}\otimes K\to\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}_{K/k});\ \phi\mapsto(a\otimes x\mapsto x\phi(a));\ \phi\otimes y\mapsto((W\to K)\circ\phi)\cdot y
. We can observe that the arrows pointing left are injections between spaces of the same dimensions, therefore they are isomorphisms.
Now, we compose the arrow pointing right with the inverses of the other arrows to obtain a differential
d_{K/W}^{\leq n}
. These are compatible with each other, thus they define a single differential on the whole algebra of differential operators.

Here is a key theorem about these differentials.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated extension of fields, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
. Let
k\subset W\subset K
be a subfield.
The inclusion
W\subset K
induces a differential:

d(K/W):\operatorname{Diff}(K)\to\operatorname{Diff}(W)\otimes K.

Explicitly, for a given differential operator
D
, its image
d(K/W)(D)
is an operator from
W
to
K
given by restricting
D
to
W
. Moreover, this differential is surjective.

Proof.

The construction of the differential is done above. The restriction part follows from the explicit maps for the
K
-linear maps in the construction. So, the only thing to prove is the surjectivity.

We prove the surjectivity in three steps. Doing so, without loss of generality, we can assume that
W^{s}=W
, because we have
\operatorname{Diff}(W)\otimes K=\operatorname{Diff}(W^{s})\otimes K
since any
p
-basis of
W/k
is a
p
-basis of
W^{s}/k
and any
p
-basis determines all differential operators by the Corollary
3.11
.

First, we assume that there is an integer
n
such that
W\supset K^{p^{n}}
. Then we can filter algebras in the following way:

\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n}}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+1}}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+2}}}(K)\subset\ldots\subset\operatorname{Diff}(K)

\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n}}}(W)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+1}}}(W)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+2}}}(W)\subset\ldots\subset\operatorname{Diff}(W)

From the fact that the differential is a restriction, we get that the differential is compatible with the above filtrations. Indeed, any
K^{p^{n+\bullet}}
-linear map on
K
must be mapped to
K^{p^{n+\bullet}}
-linear map on
W
. Hence, it is enough to show that all maps

\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(K)\to\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(W)\otimes K
are surjective. However, by the Lemma
3.5
, this is equivalent to showing that a restriction map

\operatorname{End}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(K)=\operatorname{Hom}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(K,K)\to\operatorname{End}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(W)\otimes K=\operatorname{Hom}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(W,K)

is surjective. This is trivial. Indeed, let
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(W,K)
. We have that
W\subset K
is a
K^{p^{n+\bullet}}
-linear subspace, so there exists a complementary vector space
V
such that
W\oplus V=K
. Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K^{p^{n+\bullet}}}(K,K)
be an operator that is equal
\phi
on
W
and
0
on
V
, then a restriction to
W
of
\psi
is equal
\phi
. Therefore, the map is surjective.

Second, we assume that
W\subset K
satisfies
W^{1/p^{\infty}}\cap K=W
, where
W^{1/p^{\infty}}
, and the intersection are taken in a perfection of
K
. This means that
W^{1/p^{\infty}}
and
K
are linearly disjoint over
W
. Therefore, by the Theorem A1.3 from
[
Eis96
]
, we have that
K/W
is separable, the Definition
2.21
. This means that there are
p
-bases
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
for
W/k
and
K/W
respectively. Moreover, together, they are a
p
-basis for
K/k
, the Definition
2.6
.
Indeed,
x_{i},y_{j}
are algebraically independent over
k
and
k(x_{i},y_{j})^{s}=\left(k(x_{i})^{s}(y_{j})\right)^{s}=W(y_{j})^{s}=K
. From the Corollary
3.11
, we know that differential operators are determined by values on monomials from a
p
-basis. Consequently, let
\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(W)
. There is a unique operator
\psi\in\operatorname{Diff}(K)
such that
\psi=\phi
on monomials in
x_{i}
and zero on any other monomial in
x_{i},y_{j}
. This means that
d(K/W)(\psi)=\phi\otimes 1
. Hence, the map
d(K/W)
is surjective, because any element of
\operatorname{Diff}(W)\otimes K
is a
K
-linear combination of elements of the form
\phi\otimes 1
.

The last step is a combination of the previous two. Let
k\subset W\subset K
be a subfield. We define
W^{\prime}\coloneqq W^{1/p^{\infty}}\cap K

1
1

1

W^{\prime}
could be called a “perfect saturation” of
W
in
K
.
. An extension
W^{\prime}/W
satisfies the first step by the Lemma
2.5
.
And, an extension
K/W^{\prime}
satisfies the second step, therefore the differential
d(K/W)
is a composition of two surjections:

\operatorname{Diff}(K)\to\operatorname{Diff}(W^{\prime})\otimes K\to\left(\operatorname{Diff}(W)\otimes W^{\prime}\right)\otimes K.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Here are some simple corollaries.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
K,W
be like in
3.13
.
The kernel of
d(K/W)
is equal
\operatorname{Diff}(K)\cdot{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
, i.e. it is a left ideal generated by
{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
.

Proof.

Being in the kernel means to be zero when restricted to
W
. This property is preserved under composition with anything from the left. Moreover,
{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
is in the kernel. Finally, the last fact that
\operatorname{Diff}(K)\cdot{\swabfamily E}(K/W)
is the whole kernel is just dimension counting order by order by writing down explicit formulas for them in terms of diagonal ideals.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
K,W
be like in
3.13
. Then we have a short exact sequence of
K
-vector spaces:

0\to\operatorname{Diff}(K)\cdot{\swabfamily E}(K/W)\to\operatorname{Diff}(K)\to\operatorname{Diff}(W)\otimes K\to 0.

Proof.

This is the surjectivity from
3.13
, and the kernel description from
3.14
.
∎

We finish by providing explicit formulas for how differentials act on the symbols
3.9
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
K
be like in
3.13
. Let
x_{1},\ldots x_{n}
be a
p
-basis for
K/k
, then
x_{1}^{p^{m}},\ldots x_{n}^{p^{m}}
is a
p
-basis for
K^{p^{m}}/k
. Moreover, we have that

d(K/K^{p})\left(\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x_{i}^{p^{m}}}\right)=\frac{1}{p^{m-1}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m-1}}}{\partial\left(x_{i}^{p}\right)^{p^{m-1}}}.

Consequently,

d(K/K^{p^{m}})\left(\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x_{i}^{p^{m}}}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(x_{i}^{p^{m}}\right)}.

Proof.

The fact about
p
-bases is obvious from the perspective that they are also separating transcendental bases, the Definition
2.6
.

The rest follows from comparing values of the symbols on monomials in these
p
-bases by the Corollary
3.11
.
∎

Consequently, we could use notations
\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x_{i}^{p^{m}}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(x_{i}^{p^{m}}\right)}
interchangeably on any subfield
K\supset W\supset k
by identifying them by differentials.

4
Correspondence

This section proves our correspondence
4.22
. We start with recalling Jacobson–Bourbaki
4.1
and Jacobson
4.3
Correspondences that partially overlap with our theorem. Then, we iterate an operation of relative tangent bundle to obtain an iterated Jacobson Correspondence
4.8
. We conclude a correspondence between power towers and subalgebras by restricting a canonical filtration
3.4
to all subalgebras of differential operators and then using a corollary
4.18
of a discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki correspondence
4.2
.
After that, we introduce an operation of unpacking
4.21
that is defined in terms of differentials between algebras of differential operators
3.13
. Its role is to compare subalgebras with
p
-Lie algebras. Finally, we amalgamate all the above with the Proposition
2.10
into the Theorem
4.22
.

Jacobson Theorems

We recall two Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondences, and a corollary called a Jacobson Correspondence.

The first one is a general solution to a Galois problem that is describing all subfields of a given field in terms of a structure. We call it topological, because it involves a topology. More details can be found in the Chapter 8 of
[
Jac80
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Topological Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence)
.

Let
L
be a field. There is a natural order reversing correspondence between subfields
K
of
L
, and closed in a finite topology
L
-subalgebras of additive endomorphisms of
L
.

Explicitly,
this correspondence is given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle K\subset L\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathcal{JB}(K)\coloneqq\operatorname{End}_{K}(L)\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)

\displaystyle L\subset\mathcal{JB}=\overline{\mathcal{JB}}\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{JB})\coloneqq\{x\in L;\forall_{D\in\mathcal{JB}}[x,D]=0\}\subset L.

The second one is called discrete by us, because by restricting ourselves to subfields of finite corank the above topological assumption becomes empty. It is proved directly in
[
Jac64
]
on page 22.

Theorem 4.2

(Discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence)
.

Let
L
be a field. There is a natural order reversing correspondence between subfields
K
of
L
such that
L/K
is finite, and finite dimensional
L
-subalgebras of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)
.

Explicitly,
this correspondence is given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle K\subset L\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathcal{JB}(K)\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)

\displaystyle L\subset\mathcal{JB}\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{JB})\subset L.

Moreover, we have that
\operatorname{dim}_{K}(L)=\operatorname{dim}_{L}(\mathcal{JB}(K))
.

Finally, one can deduce a purely inseparable Galois theory of exponent at most one from the discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence. It was originally proved in
[
Jac44
]
, and later it was written down as a corollary from the discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki correspondence in the Theorem 8.47 of
[
Jac80
]

Theorem 4.3

(Jacobson Correspondence)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/K^{p}
is a finite extension.

There is an inclusion reversing correspondence between subfields
K^{p}\subset W\subset K
and
p
-Lie algebras on
K
.

Explicitly, it is given by

\displaystyle K\supset W\supset K^{p}

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{K/W}\subset TK,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\subset TK

\displaystyle\mapsto K\supset\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F})\supset K^{p},

where
\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\{x\in K|\forall_{D\in\mathcal{F}}D(x)=0\}
.
Moreover,
p^{\operatorname{dim}_{K}(\mathcal{F})}=\operatorname{dim}_{W}(K)
.

We remark on a fact from a prove of the above.

Remark 4.4
.

A part of the proof of the Jacobson Correspondence is showing that a
K
-subspace
\mathcal{F}
of
TK
is a
p
-Lie algebra on
K
if and only if
\left<\mathcal{F}\right>\cap TK=\mathcal{F}
, where
\left<\mathcal{F}\right>
is the smallest subalgebra of
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
containing
\mathcal{F}
. Hence, one could take this as a definition.

Iterated Jacobson Correspondence

We extend the Theorem
4.3
to power towers by simply iterating it. First, we need some definitions.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
.
A sequence
T_{K/W_{\bullet}}\coloneqq(T_{K/W_{1}},T_{W_{1}/W_{2}},\ldots,T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n}},\ldots)
is called an
iterated relative tangent bundle
of
W_{\bullet}
.

Definition 4.6
.

A
Jacobson sequence
on
K
is a sequence of
p
-Lie algebras
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)
such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a
p
-Lie algebra on
K
, and
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}
is a
p
-Lie algebra on
\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
such that we have
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\cap T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})/K^{p^{i}}}=0
.

We say that a Jacobson sequence is of length at most
n
if
\mathcal{F}_{N}=0
for
N>n
. And, we say that it is of length
n
if the
n
is minimal such.

Remark 4.7
.

The above condition
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\cap T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})/K^{p^{i}}}=0
is equivalent to a map

d(\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})/K^{p^{i}}):\ \mathcal{F}_{i+1}\to TK^{p^{i}}\otimes\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
being injective, because
T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})/K^{p^{i}}}=\operatorname{ker}(d(\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})/K^{p^{i}}):TK\to T\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\otimes K)
.

We can state the theorem.

Theorem 4.8

(Iterated Jacobson Correspondence)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/K^{p^{\infty}}
is a finitely generated field extension. Let
n\geq 0
be an integer.

There is a natural bijection between power towers on
K
(of length at most
n
) and Jacobson sequences on
K
(of length at most
n
).

Explicitly, this correspondence is given by

\displaystyle W_{\bullet}\mapsto

\displaystyle T_{K/W_{\bullet}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}).

Moreover, we have
\operatorname{dim}_{W_{m}}(W_{m-1})=p^{\operatorname{dim}_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})}(\mathcal{F}_{m})}
.

Proof.

This moreover part is the moreover part from the Jacobson Correspondence
4.3
. Also, the parts about preserving the lengths, and operations being inverse to each other are trivial. So, the only thing to show is that these operations are well defined.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
. We are going to show that its iterative relative tangent bundle is a Jacobson sequence. The first part of the definition is trivial, because, by the Jacobson Correspondence, we have

W_{n}=\operatorname{Ann}(T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n}})
for all
n\geq 1
and
T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n}}
is a
p
-Lie algebra on
W_{n-1}
. The second part is to show that
T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n}}\cap T_{W_{n-1}/K^{p^{n-1}}}=0
. This follows again from the Jacobson Correspondence and the Definition
2.7
. Indeed, let
\mathcal{F}=T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n}}\cap T_{W_{n-1}/K^{p^{n-1}}}
, then
\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F})=W_{n}\cdot K^{p^{n-1}}=W_{n-1}
, so
\mathcal{F}=T_{W_{n-1}/W_{n-1}}=0
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)
be a Jacobson sequence on
K
. We have that
K\supset\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\supset\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\supset\ldots
, and we need to show that
\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m})\cdot K^{p^{m-1}}=\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})
. By the Jacobson Correspondence, we have that an equality
T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})/\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m})\cdot K^{p^{m-1}}}=T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})/\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m})}\cap T_{\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})/K^{p^{m-1}}}=0
holds, so we get
\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})=\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{m})\cdot K^{p^{m-1}}
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

It is possible to discover the Theorem
4.8
by starting with a subfield
W
of
K
and trying to get the most from the Jacobson Correspondence. First, we compute
T_{K/W}
, and then we check that
\operatorname{Ann}(T_{K/W})=W_{1}
. Now, we can observe that
K\supset W_{1}\supset W
, so we can repeat this procedure for
W_{1}\supset W
. We get
T_{W_{1}/W}
, and

\operatorname{Ann}(T_{W_{1}/W})=W\cdot W_{1}^{p}=W\cdot(W\cdot K^{p})^{p}=W\cdot K^{p^{2}}=W_{2}
.
This pattern continues and it leads us precisely to this theorem.

Here are some examples.

Example 4.10
.

An extension
K/K
corresponds to
(0,0,\ldots)
.

Example 4.11
.

An extension
K/K^{p^{n}}
corresponds to
(TK,TK^{p},\ldots,TK^{p^{n-1}},0,0,\ldots)
.

Example 4.12
.

An extension
K/k
corresponds to
(TK,TK^{p},TK^{p^{2}},\ldots)
.

Example 4.13
.

Let
k
be a perfect field. Let
K=k(x,y)
. Then a power tower of
k(x)
on
k(x,y)
corresponds to a Jacobson sequence

(k(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y},k(x,y^{p})\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{p}},k(x,y^{p^{2}})\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{p^{2}}},\ldots).

The Main Theorem

We prove our correspondence
4.22
by connecting the iterated Jacobson Correspondence
4.8
via an operation of unpacking
4.21
with the discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension, where
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

We start with some definitions. First, we observe that a canonical filtration on differential operators, see the Definition
3.4
, restricts well to all subalgebras.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
K\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}(K)
be a
K
-subalgebra of
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
. It admits a filtration by finite subalgebras
\mathcal{D}_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}\cap\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)
. We call it a canonical filtration of
\mathcal{D}
.

An algebra
\mathcal{D}
is called of height at most
n
if
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{n}
. And, of height
n
, if the
n
is minimal such.

Definition 4.15
.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
. An algebra of differential operators on
K
relative to the tower
W_{\bullet}
is defined by
\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)\coloneqq\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
.

Here are two reality checks.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
. Then
\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)_{n}=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
.

Proof.

It is enough to show
\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{N}}(K)_{n}=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
for any
N\geq n
. But, this is equivalent to
\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{N}}(K)\cap\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
, what is nothing else but an equality
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)\cap\operatorname{End}_{K^{p^{n}}}(K)=\operatorname{End}_{W_{n}}(K)
, by the Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
k\subset W\subset K
be a subfield of
K
. Then we have that
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)
.

Proof.

We look at a canonical filtration:
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)_{n}=\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)\cap\operatorname{Diff}_{K^{p^{n}}}(K)
. The right hand side, by the very construction, is
\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{n}}(K)
. So, subalgebras
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K),\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)
have the same canonical filtrations. Therefore, they are equal.
∎

The following is a key corollary from the discrete Jacobson–Bourbaki Correspondence.

Corollary 4.18
.

Finite
K
-subalgebras of
\operatorname{Diff}(K)
naturally correspond to subfields of
K
of finite exponents by operations
K\supset W\supset K^{p^{n}}\mapsto\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)
, and
\mathcal{D}\mapsto\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{D})\coloneqq\{x\in L;\forall_{D\in\mathcal{D}}[x,D]=0\}
.

Proof.

By the Theorem
4.2
, extensions
K\supset W\supset K^{p^{n}}
corresponds to an inclusion
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)\subset\operatorname{End}_{K^{p^{n}}}(K)
that, by the Lemma
3.5
, is
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{p^{n}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}(K)
.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

An alternative proof of the Corollary
4.18
goes by invocation of a theorem that finite locally free groupoids admit and are determined by their quotients, see 03BE at
[
Sta23
]
. This is the way how Ekedahl approached a similar problem for varieties in his paper
[
Eke87
]
.

We extend the above to power towers.

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/K^{p^{\infty}}
is a finitely generated field extension. We put
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
. There is a natural correspondence between power towers on
K
, and
K
-subalgebras of differential operators on
K
over
k
.

Explicitly,
this correspondence is given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle W_{\bullet}\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{k}(K)

\displaystyle K\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{k}(K)\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{D}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\{x\in L;\forall_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{\bullet}}[x,D]=0\}.

Moreover,
\operatorname{dim}_{K}(\mathcal{D}_{i})=\operatorname{dim}_{W_{i}}(K)
.

Proof.

Any subalgebra
\mathcal{D}
is determined by its canonical filtration, and we have
\mathcal{D}_{1}\subset\mathcal{D}_{2}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{D}
.
By the Corollary
4.18
, there are subfields
W_{i}
corresponding to
\mathcal{D}_{i}
such that we have

\mathcal{D}_{i}=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{i}}(K)
. However, by the Lemmas
4.16
and
4.17
, it means that
W_{\bullet}
is a power tower on
K
.
Therefore, a canonical filtration determines a power tower on
K
, and this power tower determines the canonical filtration on the algebra of differential operators relative to it. This finishes the proof.
∎

The iterated Jacobson Correspondence
4.8
connects with the above proposition via an operation from the following proposition. We call this operation an unpacking. It produces a Jacobson sequence out of a subalgebra of differential operators.

Proposition 4.21

(Unpacking)
.

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a power tower on
K
. Let
(\mathcal{F}_{i})
be an iterated relative tangent bundle of
W_{\bullet}
, and let
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\bullet}}(K)
. Then, for
i\geq 1
, we have

d(K/W_{i-1})(\mathcal{D})\cap TW_{i-1}=\mathcal{F}_{i}.

In particular, the first degree of
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.
\mathcal{D}\cap TK
, is equal
T_{K/W_{1}}
.

Proof.

We start with the first degree. Observe that
\left<T_{K/W_{1}}\right>=\mathcal{D}_{1}
by the Corollary
4.18
and the Theorem
4.3
, because both correspond to the same object, i.e.
W_{1}
. So, by the Remark
4.4
, we have
\mathcal{D}\cap TK=\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap TK=T_{K/W_{1}}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

Let
i>1
. First, we observe that the intersection makes sense, because we can inject
W
-space
TW_{i-1}
into
\operatorname{Diff}(W_{i-1})\otimes K
. Second, we observe that
d(K/W_{i-1})(\mathcal{D})\cap TW_{i-1}=d(K/W_{i-1})(\mathcal{D})\cap\left<TW_{i-1}\right>\otimes K\cap TW_{i-1}=d(K/W_{i-1})(\mathcal{D}_{i})\cap TW_{i-1}
. Therefore, we can work with
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.
By the Lemma
3.5
, it is
\operatorname{End}_{W_{i}}(K)
. By the Proposition
3.13
,
d(K/W_{i-1})(\operatorname{End}_{W_{i}}(K))
is equal
\operatorname{End}_{W_{i}}(K)
restricted to
W_{i-1}
, but this is precisely, as an algebra of operators,
\operatorname{End}_{W_{i}}(W_{i-1})\otimes K
. Therefore,
\operatorname{End}_{W_{i}}(W_{i-1})\otimes K\cap TW_{i-1}=T_{W_{i}/W_{i-1}}=\mathcal{F}_{i}
.
∎

Here is the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
k\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\geq 0}K^{p^{n}}
. We assume that
K/k
is a finitely generated field extension.

There are natural bijections between the following data on
K

•

power towers on
K
, see the Definition
2.7
,

•

Jacobson sequences on
K
, see the Definition
4.6
,

•

-subalgebras of differential operators on

K\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{k}(K)

Explicitly,
these bijections are given by the following operations:

•

an iterated tangent bundle:
W_{\bullet}\mapsto(T_{W_{0}/W_{1}},T_{W_{1}/W_{2}},T_{W_{2}/W_{3}},\ldots)
,

•

an iterated

(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots)\mapsto\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})
,

•

an algebra of relative differential operators:

,

•

\mathcal{D}\mapsto d(K/W_{n-1})(\mathcal{D})\cap TW_{n-1}=\mathcal{F}_{n}

Moreover, we have that ranks

are finite and nonincreasing:
\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geq\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1})

The theorem is an amalgamation of the following results

2.10
.
∎

Remark 4.26
.

Let
K
be like in the Theorem
4.22
. Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{Diff}(K)
be a
K
-subalgebra. Let
W_{\infty}
be first integrals of this algebra.

We can consider
\mathcal{D}
as a subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
be a closure of
\mathcal{D}
in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
in a finite topology. This closure is of the form
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)
, where
W
is a subfield of
K
by the Theorem
4.1
. Actually,
W=W_{\infty}
. Indeed,
\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{D})=W_{\infty}
, so
\operatorname{End}_{W_{\infty}}(K)\supset\mathcal{D}
. Hence,
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)\subset\operatorname{End}_{W_{\infty}}(K)
, so

. But,
\operatorname{End}_{W}(K)\supset\mathcal{D}
, so
\operatorname{const}(\operatorname{End}_{W}(K))=W\subset\operatorname{const}(\mathcal{D})=W_{\infty}
.
However,
\mathcal{D}
is determined by

if and only if a corresponding to it power tower is algebraically integrable, and this may not be the case, e.g.

and

.

Furthermore, one could say that there are more not algebraically integrable power towers than algebraically integrable ones. Indeed, let

be a finite field, then if

has a positive transcendental degree, then there are only countable many subfields between

and

, but there are uncountable many power towers on

. Therefore, in almost all cases, a closure of a subalgebra of differential operators says little about this subalgebra.

Injection of Subfields into Power Towers

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/K^{p^{\infty}}
is a finitely generated field extension. We put
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
.

Let
W
be a subfield of
K
. Then a field of first integrals of a power tower of
W
on
K
is a separable closure of
k\cdot W
in
K
, i.e.
W_{\infty}=\left(k\cdot W\right)^{s}
. Moreover, the power tower of
W
on
K
and a power tower of
W_{\infty}
on
K
are equal.

Proof.

We can assume that
k\subset W
.

First, observe that
(W^{s})_{\bullet}=W_{\bullet}
. Indeed, for every
n\geq 0
, we have
K\supset W^{s}_{n}\supset W_{n}\supset K^{p^{n}}
, so
W^{s}_{n}\supset W_{n}
is purely inseparable. But, on the other hand,
W^{s}_{n}\supset W_{n}
is separable. Therefore, we have
W_{n}^{s}=W_{n}
.

We conclude that
W^{s}\subset W_{n}
for every
n
, hence
W^{s}\subset W_{\infty}
.

We also have
(W_{\infty})^{s}=W_{\infty}
. Indeed, we have
(W_{\infty})_{n}\subset W_{n}
, so
(W_{\infty})^{s}\subset W_{\infty}
. Therefore,
(W_{\infty})^{s}=W_{\infty}
.

Moreover, power towers of
W^{s}
and
W_{\infty}
on
K
are equal since
W^{s}\subset W_{\infty}\subset W_{n}
gives
W_{n}=W^{s}\cdot K^{p^{n}}\subset W_{\infty}\cdot K^{p^{n}}\subset W_{n}
.

Now, we know that we have an inclusion
W^{s}\subset W_{\infty}
, and that both of these fields are separably closed in
K
. Moreover, it is a finitely generated extension of fields. If the extension was nontrivial, then we would have
\Omega_{W_{\infty}/W}\neq 0
. Indeed, if it was nontrivial, then we could find a finite sequence of elements
x_{i}\in W_{\infty}
, where
i=1,\ldots,r
, such that

L_{0}\coloneqq W\subset L_{1}\coloneqq W(x_{1})^{s}\subset L_{2}\coloneqq L_{1}(x_{2})^{s}\subset...\subset L_{r}\coloneqq L_{r-1}(x_{r})^{s}=W_{\infty}

is a sequence of proper extensions, and

is purely inseparable, or purely transcendental over

. A simple calculation shows that

has dimension one, so it is non-zero. Furthermore,
\Omega_{L_{r}/L_{0}}
surjects onto it, so it is non-zero either.

Hence, still assuming that the extension

is nontrivial, we can conclude that
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(W_{\infty})\neq W_{\infty}
, because the algebra contains the dual of
\Omega_{W_{\infty}/W}
. However, by the Corollary

restricted to

-linear operators, we have

0\to\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K){\swabfamily E}_{W}(K/W_{\infty})\to\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)\to\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(W_{\infty})\otimes K\to 0.

And, by the Theorem
4.22
, the Lemma
4.17
, and the equality of power towers of

, we have that
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(K)=\operatorname{Diff}_{W_{\infty}}(K)
. In particular, we have
{\swabfamily E}_{W}(K/W_{\infty})={\swabfamily E}_{W}(K/W)
. However, this means that
{\swabfamily E}_{W}(W_{\infty}/W)=0
, what is the contradiction with the fact that
\operatorname{Diff}_{W}(W_{\infty})\neq W_{\infty}
.
∎

The following extends the Corollary

.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let

be a field of characteristic

K/K^{p^{\infty}}
is a finitely generated field extension. We put

. We have the following diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{K\supset W\supset K^{p^{n}}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{K\supset W=W^{s}\supset k\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Power Towers on $K$},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W\mapsto W_{\bullet}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\bullet}\mapsto W_{\infty}}

, the first arrow is a map from subfields of finite exponent

The upmost left map is an inclusion, because subfields of finite exponent are separably closed in

. Indeed, let

The splitting is precisely the Proposition

Extension Problem

Our
n
-foliations, the Definition
2.19
, are analogs of Ekedahl‘s
n
-foliations from his paper
[
Eke87
]
. In this paper, Ekedahl considers a problem of extending a
1
-foliation to a
2
-foliation. His main result regarding this is an example of a
1
-foliation on a variety that does not admit any extension to a
2
-foliation on the variety, it is on the page 145 there. This example is important, because it is also a positive characteristic counterexample to a lemma used in a proof of a Miyaoka‘s semipositivity theorem, the Example 8.8 in
[
Miy87
]
. However, it is not yet resolved in the literature if being extendable to a
\infty
-foliation on a variety is the only obstruction for
1
-foliations on varieties to satisfy that lemma. Therefore, here, we approach this extension problem for fields as a small step towards a resolution.

First, we define the problem.

Problem 5.3
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
W_{1}
be a subfield of
K
of exponent
1
, i.e.
K\supset W_{1}\supset K^{p}
and
W_{1}\neq K
.
Is there a subfield
W_{2}
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
is a
2
-foliation? Furthermore, are there subfields
W_{n}
for
n\geq 2
such that
W_{\bullet}
is a
\infty
-foliation?

By the following lemma, to answer the second question from the above problem it is enough to answer the first one.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be a
2
-foliation on
K
, and let
W_{2},W_{3}
be a
2
-foliation on
W_{1}
. Then
W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}
is a
3
-foliation on
K
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
W_{3}\cdot K^{p^{2}}=W_{2}
. We have that
W_{1}=W_{2}\cdot K^{p}
, so
W_{1}^{p}=W_{2}^{p}\cdot K^{p^{2}}
. Consequently, we have
W_{2}=W_{3}\cdot W_{1}^{p}=W_{3}\cdot(W_{2}^{p}\cdot K^{p^{2}})=(W_{3}\cdot W_{2}^{p})\cdot K^{p^{2}}=W_{3}\cdot K^{p^{2}}
, because
W_{3}\supset W_{2}^{p}
.
∎

Second, we can write down an obstruction in terms of Jacobson sequences
4.6
. In the below, an abstract
p
-Lie algebra on a field
K
is a
K
-vector space enriched with a Lie bracket, and a
p
-power operation satisfying some axioms to make them behave like
p
-Lie subalgebras of
TK
. It is made precise in a paper
[
Hoch54
]
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
such that
K/K^{p^{\infty}}
is a finitely generated field extension.

Let
W_{1}
be a subfield of
K
of exponent one. Then we have a short exact sequence of abstract
p
-Lie algebras on
W_{1}
:

0\to T_{W_{1}/K^{p}}\to TW_{1}\xrightarrow{d(W_{1}/K^{p})}T_{K^{P}/W_{1}^{p}}\otimes W_{1}\to 0.

This sequence admits a right split of abstract
p
-Lie algebras if and only if
W_{1}
can be extended to a
2
-foliation on
K
. Moreover, all such splittings are in a bijection with all such extensions. Explicitly, if a splitting
\sigma
corresponds to an extension
W_{2}
, then the image of
\sigma
is
T_{W_{1}/W_{2}}
.

Proof.

The exactness of the sequence follows from the Jacobson Correspondence
4.3
applied to extensions
W_{1}\supset K^{p}\supset W^{p}_{1}
.

Any extension of
W_{1}
to a
2
-foliation is a pair of fields
W_{1},W_{2}
. By the Theorem
4.22
, it is equivalent to a pair of
p
-Lie algebras
T_{K/W_{1}},T_{W_{1}/W_{2}}
that is a Jacobson sequence
4.6
, so
T_{W_{1}/W_{2}}\subset TW_{1}
and
T_{W_{1}/W_{2}}\cap T_{W_{1}/K^{p}}=0
. Therefore, any such
T_{W_{1}/W_{2}}
defines a right splitting that preserves Lie brackets, and
p
-powers. And, any such splitting provides such subspace as its image.
∎

Finally, we show that the obstruction is trivial.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
K
be like in the Proposition
5.5
.

Let
W_{1}
be a
1
-foliation on
K
. Then
W_{1}
can be extended to a
2
-foliation on
K
.

Proof.

Let
r
be a rank of a
1
-foliation
W_{1}
. Put
k=K^{p^{\infty}}
. Let
\operatorname{tr.deg}(K/k)=N
.

In the proof, we use a lot of facts about
p
-bases, see the Definition
2.6
and its references.

There are
r
-elements
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{r}\in K
such that
W_{1}(t_{\bullet})=K
. These elements are
p
-independent in
K
, because monomials
\prod_{i}t_{i}^{a_{i}}
, where
0\leq a_{i}<p
, are
W_{1}
-linearly independent. Therefore, they are
K^{p}
-linearly independent, because
W_{1}\supset K^{p}
. Moreover, their
p
-powers
t_{i}^{p}\in W_{1}
are
p
-independent in
W_{1}
, because this means that monomials monomials
\prod_{i}t_{i}^{p\cdot a_{i}}
, where
0\leq a_{i}<p
, are
W_{1}^{p}
-linearly independent, which, by taking
p
-roots, is the above information.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}
be a
p
-basis for
W_{1}/k
. This is equivalent to
da_{\bullet}
being a
W_{1}
-linear basis of
\Omega_{W_{1}/k}
.
Consequently, it admits an exchange lemma, i.e. any
p
-independent set can be completed to an
p
-independent set of maximal size by adding some elements from other maximal size independent set. Therefore, there are
N-r
elements among
a_{i}
, say
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N-r}
, such that
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N-r},t_{1}^{p},t_{2}^{p},\ldots,t_{r}^{p}
is a
p
-basis for
W_{1}/k
.

We claim that
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N-r},t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{r}
is a
p
-basis for
K/k
. Indeed, they are algebraically independent, because if not, then there would be a relation
F
between them, but then
F^{p}
would be such relation for
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N-r},t_{1}^{p},t_{2}^{p},\ldots,t_{r}^{p}
, a contradiction.
Next, we have a diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K=W_{1}(t_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(a_{\bullet},t_{\bullet})^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})^{s}}

wherein the separable closures are taken in
K
.
The extensions
k(a_{\bullet},t_{\bullet})^{s}/k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})^{s}
and
K/W_{1}
are of order
p^{r}
, so the extension
K/k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})^{s}
must be trivial, i.e.
k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})^{s}=K
. Therefore,
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N-r},t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{r}
is a separating transcendental basis, so it is a
p
-basis for
K/k
.

Finally, consider the following diagram of fields:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K=k(a_{\bullet},t_{\bullet})^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{1}=k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{2}\coloneqq k(a_{\bullet},t^{p^{2}}_{\bullet})^{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(a_{\bullet},t_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(a_{\bullet},t^{p}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(a_{\bullet},t^{p^{2}}_{\bullet})}

We claim that
K\supset W_{1}\supset W_{2}
is a
2
-foliation.
Indeed, we have that
W_{1}\supset K^{p}
,
W_{2}\supset K^{p^{2}}
,
[K:W_{1}]=[W_{1}:W_{2}]=p^{r}
, so the only remaining thing to check is
W_{2}\cdot K^{p}=W_{1}
.

It is easy:

W_{1}\supset W_{2}\cdot K^{p}\supset k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(a_{1},t^{p})=k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(t^{p})=k(a_{1}^{p},t^{p})^{s}=W_{1}

where
k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(t^{p})=k(a_{1}^{p},t^{p})^{s}
, because

k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(t^{p})=(k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(t^{p}))^{s}=k(a_{1},t^{p})^{s}
, where the second equality follows from the subfield
k(a_{1},t^{p^{2}})^{s}(t^{p})
being of finite exponent since this implies being separably closed in

.
∎

Examples

We present a simple class of power towers on

. In the below example, we use symbols
\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial y^{p^{m}}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(y^{p^{m}}\right)}

\frac{1}{p^{m}!}\frac{\partial^{p^{m}}}{\partial x^{p^{m}}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(x^{p^{m}}\right)}
interchangeably.

Example 5.7
.

Let
K=k(x,y)
be rational functions in two variables over a perfect field
k
of characteristic

.

We define a power tower of a ’’subfield‘‘

on

, where

It is clear from the definition that

is a power tower on

We can compute Jacobson sequences and subalgebras of differential operators corresponding to these examples, see the Theorem

. We start with the Jacobson sequences.

The first extension

The second extension

generated by a vector field

The third extension

It is easy to check that operators

and span it. In this basis, the extension corresponds to a vector field

Let

. We have that

is spanned by operators

\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{p^{n-1}}}-A_{1}^{p^{n-2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p^{n-2}}}-A_{2}^{p^{n-3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p^{n-3}}}-\ldots-A_{n-2}^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}

Finally, by an operation of unpacking, we can conclude that

Isometries between groups of invertible elements in Fourier-Stieltjes algebras
Osamu Hatori, Shiho Oi

Abstract
. We prove that if open subgroups of the groups of invertible elements in two Fourier-Stieltjes algebras are isometric as metric spaces, then the underlying locally compact groups are topologically isomorphic. We describe the structure of isometric real algebra isomorphisms between Fourier-Stieltjes algebras and apply it to prove the above result.

1.
Introduction

There has been a long-standing effort to determine the conditions of a linear subspace
A
of
C(X)
, a space of complex-valued continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, to ensure that
X
can be uniquely determined. The Banach-Stone theorem is the most prominent result in this field. According to the theorem, in addition to
A=C(X)
, the crucial requirement is that the structure is a Banach space.

This paper focuses on how the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra
B(G)
of a locally compact group
G
encodes its structure. The algebra
B(G)
is defined as the complex linear span of the set of all continuous positive-definite functions on
G
.
It
is a semisimple commutative Banach algebra of all matrix coefficients of continuous unitary representations of
G
.

By
[
11
, Theorem 20.20]
(cf.
[
17
]
) and
[
5
, Section 11.1 Exercise 9]
, we identify
L^{1}(G)^{*}
with
L^{\infty}(G)
for a locally compact group
G
.
Therefore
the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra
B(G)
is identified with the dual of the group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
. The dualities determine the norm on
B(G)
. The enveloping von Neumann algebra of
C^{*}(G)
, denoted as
W^{*}(G)
, is identified with
B(G)^{*}
when
B(G)
is identified with the dual of
C^{*}(G)
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
G
and
H
are locally compact groups, with
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
denoting their respective units.

A celebrated theorem by Walter
[
19
]
states that if
S\colon B(G)\to B(H)
is an isometric complex algebra isomorphism, then
G
and
H
are topologically isomorphic as locally compact groups.
However, it is impractical that the structure of
B(G)
as a Banach space does not completely encode the group structure of
G
. For instance, consider two discrete abelian groups with the same cardinality,
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
. Any bijection
\alpha\colon\Gamma_{2}\to\Gamma_{1}
induces a surjective complex linear isometry from
L^{1}(\Gamma_{1})\to L^{1}(\Gamma_{2})
by
f\mapsto f\circ\alpha
for
f\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{1})
, which in turn induces the complex linear isometry from
B(\widehat{\Gamma_{1}})\to B(\widehat{\Gamma_{2}})
through the Fourier transform. However, it is possible for
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
to be nonisomorphic as groups; for example, if
\Gamma_{1}=\mathbb{Z}
(the integer group) and
\Gamma_{2}=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
B(\widehat{\Gamma_{1}})
and
B(\widehat{\Gamma_{2}})
are isometrically isomorphic as Banach spaces, but the group
\widehat{\Gamma_{1}}
is not isomorphic to
\widehat{\Gamma_{2}}
.

On the other hand, in
[
9
]
, the first author showed that the metric structures of open subgroups of groups of invertible elements in the measure algebras on locally compact
abelian
groups guarantee the structure of the underlying locally
abelian
groups. Investigating whether the same result holds true for noncommutative groups would be interesting and natural.
In this paper, we will provide an effective answer to this question for any locally compact group. The key is to characterize all isometric real algebra isomorphisms between two Fourier-Stieltjes algebras on locally compact groups, which is Theorem
2.14
. Since any complex algebra isomorphism occurs at the same time as a real algebra isomorphism, Theorem
2.14
is a generalization of the theorem proposed by Walter
[
19
]
. We deduce that every isometric real algebra isomorphism between two Fourier-Stieltjes algebras automatically induces a topological group isomorphism between underlying groups. This characterization makes it possible to answer the above
question: Corollary
3.2
.

2.
Isometric real algebra isomorphisms

In this section, we consider surjective real linear isometries that also preserve the multiplication between
B(G)
and
B(H)
.
To distinguish them from usual isometric isomorphisms (which are assumed to be complex linear), we refer to them as isometric real algebra isomorphisms.
We present Theorem
2.14

which characterizes an isometric real algebra isomorphism. This is the result of a comparison with a celebrated theorem of Walter
[
19
]
.
Authors such as Cohen
[
1
]
, Daws
[
2
]
, Ilie
[
12
]
, Ilie and Spronk
[
13
]
, and Le Pham
[
16
]
have extensively studied complex linear homomorphisms on Fourier-Stieltjes algebras and Fourier algebras.
Our study presents a new approach for obtaining a representation of real linear operators on Fourier-Stieltjes algebras.
In contrast to Walter’s work, our results demonstrate that the assumption of real linearity is sufficient for the isomorphism of underlying groups for similar mappings.
Notably, every isometric real algebra isomorphism is either complex linear or conjugate-linear, which is an interesting finding.

Let
T:B(G)\to B(H)
be an isometric real algebra isomorphism. We define
T^{*}:B(H)^{*}\to B(G)^{*}
by

T^{*}(\varphi)(f)=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(Tf)-i\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if)),\quad f\in B(G)

(1)

for
\varphi\in B(H)^{*}
.
Then,
T^{*}
is a surjective real linear isometry.
For the convenience of the readers, we have included this proof.
By a simple calculation, we see that
T^{*}(\varphi)
is a complex linear functional on
B(G)
for
\varphi\in B(H)^{*}
.
We show that
\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|\leq\|\varphi\|
for every
\varphi\in B(H)^{*}
. Let
f\in B(G)
. There exists
0\leq\theta<2\pi
such that

T^{*}(\varphi)(f)=|T^{*}(\varphi)(f)|e^{i\theta}.

Then,

\begin{split}|T^{*}(\varphi)(f)|&=e^{-i\theta}T^{*}(\varphi)(f)=T^{*}(\varphi)(e^{-i\theta}f)\\
&=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(e^{-i\theta}f))\leq|\varphi(T(e^{-i\theta}f))|\\
&\leq\|\varphi\|\|T(e^{-i\theta}f)\|=\|\varphi\|\|e^{-i\theta}f\|=\|\varphi\|\|f\|.\end{split}

Hence, we have

\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|\leq\|\varphi\|

for
\varphi\in B^{*}(H)
. We show that the inverse inequality
\|\varphi\|\leq\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|
. Then,
\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|=\|\varphi\|
, so that
T^{*}
is an isometry. Let
h\in B(H)
. Then,
0\leq\theta<2\pi
exists such that
\varphi(h)=|\varphi(h)|e^{i\theta}
. Then

\begin{split}|\varphi(h)|&=e^{-i\theta}\varphi(h)=\varphi(e^{-i\theta}h)\\
&=\varphi(T(T^{-1}(e^{-i\theta}h)))=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(T^{-1}(e^{-i\theta}h)))\\
&\leq|T^{*}(\varphi)(T^{-1}(e^{-i\theta}h))|\leq\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|\|T^{-1}(e^{-i\theta}h)\|\\
&=\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|\|h\|.\end{split}

As
h\in B(H)
is arbitrary, we have that
\|\varphi\|\leq\|T^{*}(\varphi)\|
. To prove that
T^{*}
is surjective,
we consider
(T^{-1})^{*}\colon B(G)^{*}\to B(H)^{*}
, similar to
T^{*}
;
(T^{-1})^{*}(\psi)(h)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(h))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(ih))
for
h\in B(H)
and
\psi\in B(G)^{*}
. Let
\psi\in B(G)^{*}
be arbitrary.
Put
\varphi^{\prime}=(T^{-1})^{*}(\psi)
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}\in B(H)^{*}
.
For every
f\in B(G)
we have that

\begin{split}T^{*}(\varphi^{\prime})(f)&=\operatorname{Re}\varphi^{\prime}(T(f))-i\operatorname{Re}\varphi^{\prime}(T(if))\\
&=\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(T(f)))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(iT(f))))\\
&-i\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(T(if)))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(iT(if))))\\
&=\operatorname{Re}\psi(f)-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(if)=\psi(f).\end{split}

As
\psi\in B(G)^{*}
is arbitrary we infer that
T^{*}
is surjective.

Let
\sigma(B(G))
and
\sigma(B(H))
be the spectra (the same as the maximal ideal spaces) of

B(G)
and
B(H)
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

T^{*}(\sigma(B(H)))=\sigma(B(G)).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\sigma(B(H))
. For any
f\in B(G)
, we have
\varphi(T(if))^{2}=-(\varphi(Tf))^{2}
. This implies that

\varphi(T(if))=i\varphi(Tf)
or
\varphi(T(if))=-i\varphi(Tf)
.

(2)

Let
f,g\in B(G)
and
\varphi\in\sigma(B(H))
.
As
T
and
\varphi
are multiplicative and real linear we have

\varphi(T(if))\varphi(T(ig))=\varphi(T(if)T(ig))\\
=\varphi(T(-fg))=-\varphi(T(fg))=(i\varphi(T(f)))(i\varphi(T(g))).

(3)

If
\varphi(T(f))\neq 0
and
\varphi(T(g))\neq 0
, then
(
3
)
ensures that

\text{$i\varphi(T(f))=\varphi(T(if))$ if and only if $i\varphi(T(g))=\varphi(T(ig))$}.

(4)

Hence we have

\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(f))\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(g))=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if))\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(ig)).

(5)

If
\varphi(T(f))=0
or
\varphi(T(g))=0
, then we infer that
\varphi(T(if))=0
or
\varphi(T(ig))=0
respectively by (
2
).
Hence, (
5
) also holds for any
f,g\in B(G)
. Applying (
5
)
we have

\begin{split}&T^{*}(\varphi)(f)T^{*}(\varphi)(g)\\
&=(\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(f))-i\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if)))(\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(g))-i\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(ig)))\\
&=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(f))\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(g))-\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(f))\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(g))\\
&-i\left(\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if))\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(g))-\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(if))\operatorname{Re}i\varphi(T(g))\right)\\
&=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(f))\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(g))-\operatorname{Im}\varphi(T(f))\operatorname{Im}\varphi(T(g))\\
&-i\left(\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if))\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(g))-\operatorname{Im}\varphi(T(if))\operatorname{Im}\varphi(T(g))\right)\\
&=T^{*}(\varphi(fg)).\end{split}

Thus
T^{*}(\varphi)
is a multiplicative linear functional. As
T^{*}
is a surjective isometry and
\varphi\neq 0
, we obtain
T^{*}(\varphi)\neq 0
. Hence
T^{*}(\varphi)\in\sigma(B(G))
. To prove that
T^{*}
maps
\sigma(B(H))
onto
\sigma(B(G))
,
we consider
(T^{-1})^{*}\colon B(G)^{*}\to B(H)^{*}
in a similar way as
T^{*}
;
(T^{-1})^{*}(\psi)(h)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(h))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(ih))
for
h\in B(H)
and
\psi\in B(G)^{*}
. Let
\psi\in\sigma(B(G))
be arbitrary. As in the first part, we have
\varphi^{\prime}=(T^{-1})^{*}(\psi)\in\sigma(B(H))
. For every
f\in B(G)
, we have

\begin{split}T^{*}(\varphi^{\prime})(f)&=\operatorname{Re}\varphi^{\prime}(T(f))-i\operatorname{Re}\varphi^{\prime}(T(if))\\
&=\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(T(f)))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(iT(f))))\\
&-i\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(T(if)))-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(T^{-1}(iT(if))))\\
&=\operatorname{Re}\psi(f)-i\operatorname{Re}\psi(if)=\psi(f).\end{split}

As
\psi\in\sigma(B(G))
is arbitrary, we infer that
T^{*}
maps
\sigma(B(H))
onto
\sigma(B(G))
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

T^{*}(i\varphi)=\begin{cases}iT^{*}(\varphi)\quad&\text{if $\varphi\in\sigma(B(H))^{+}$,}\\
-iT^{*}(\varphi)\quad&\text{if $\varphi\in\sigma(B(H))^{-}$}.\end{cases}\\

For any

\begin{split}T^{*}(i\varphi)(f)&=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(if))+i\operatorname{Re}\varphi(T(f))\quad&\text{if $\varphi\in\sigma(B(H))^{+}$,}\\

Proposition 2.5

When

Lemma 2.7
.

We have

Finally, we characterize isometric real algebra isomorphisms on Fourier-Stieltjes algebras. Theorem
2.14
is a real-linear-counterpart of the theorem of Walter
[
19
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
G
and
H
be locally compact groups. The map
T:B(G)\to B(H)
is an isometric real algebra isomorphism if and only if there is a topological isomorphism or a topological anti-isomorphism
\alpha:H\to G
and
b\in G
such that

T(f)(x)=f(b\alpha(x))\quad f\in B(G),\ x\in H,

or

T(f)(x)=\overline{f(b\alpha(x))}\quad f\in B(G),\ x\in H.

Proof.

If
T:B(G)\to B(H)
takes the form
T(f)(x)=f(b\alpha(x))
, where
\alpha:H\to G
is either a topological group isomorphism or a topological anti-group isomorphism, then
[
19
, Corollary of Theorem 3]
shows that
T
is an isometric complex algebra isomorphism from
B(G)
onto
B(H)
.

Similarly, if
T(f)(x)=\overline{f(b\alpha(x))}
, then
T
is an isometric conjugate-linear algebra isomorphism from
B(G)
onto
B(H)
.

We prove the converse statement.
Suppose that
T:B(G)\to B(H)
is an isometric real algebra isomorphism. According to (
6
),
b\in G
such that
T^{*}(w(e_{2}))=w(b)
.

We define
T_{r}:B(G)\to B(G)
by
T_{r}(f)(x)=f(b^{-1}x)
. Since the map
x\mapsto b^{-1}x
is an affine map on
G
,
T_{r}
is an isometric isomorphism on
B(G)
as in the same argument as the first part of the proof (cf.
[
19
]
). It follows that
T\circ T_{r}\colon B(G)\to B(H)
is an isometric real algebra isomorphism. Then we obtain the following equation:

\begin{split}(T\circ T_{r})^{*}(w(e_{2}))f&=\operatorname{Re}w(e_{2})(T\circ T_{r}(f))-i\operatorname{Re}w(e_{2})(T\circ T_{r}(if))\\
&=T^{*}(w(e_{2}))(T_{r}(f))=w(e_{1})f\end{split}

for any
f\in B(G)
. Thus

(T\circ T_{r})^{*}(w(e_{2}))=w(e_{1}).

By applying Proposition
2.5
to
T\circ T_{r}\colon B(G)\to B(H)
, we obtain a topological group isomorphism or a topological anti-group isomorphism
\alpha\colon H\to G
such that

T\circ T_{r}(f)(x)=f(\alpha(x)),\quad x\in H.

or

T\circ T_{r}(f)(x)=\overline{f(\alpha(x))},\quad x\in H.

Thus, for any
f\in B(G)
and
x\in H
, we obtain the desired forms

Tf(x)=T\circ T_{r}\circ T_{r}^{-1}(f)(x)=T_{r}^{-1}(f)(\alpha(x))=f(b\alpha(x)),

or

Tf(x)=T\circ T_{r}\circ T_{r}^{-1}(f)(x)=\overline{T_{r}^{-1}(f)(\alpha(x))}=\overline{f(b\alpha(x))}.

∎

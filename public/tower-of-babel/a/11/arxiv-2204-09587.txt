Heat transfer problem for the Boltzmann equation in a channel with diffusive boundary condition
Renjun Duan, Shuangqian Liu, Tong Yang, Zhu Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the 1D steady Boltzmann flow in a channel. The walls of the channel are assumed to have vanishing velocity and given temperatures θ 0 \theta_{0} and θ 1 \theta_{1} . This problem was studied by Esposito et al [ 13 , 14 ] where they showed that the solution tends to a local Maxwellian with parameters satisfying the compressible Navier-Stokes equation with no-slip boundary condition. However, a lot of numerical experiments reveal that the fluid layer does not entirely stick to the boundary. In the regime where the Knudsen number is reasonably small, the slip phenomenon is significant near the boundary. Thus, we revisit this problem by taking into account the slip boundary conditions. Following the lines of [ 9 ] , we will first give a formal asymptotic analysis to see that the flow governed by the Boltzmann equation is accurately approximated by a superposition of a steady CNS equation with a temperature jump condition and two Knudsen layers located at end points. Then we will establish a uniform L ∞ L^{\infty} estimate on the remainder and derive the slip boundary condition for compressible Navier-Stokes equations rigorously.

Introduction

1.1.
Problem settings

In this paper, we study the steady flow of a rarefied gas in a channel which is bounded by two thermal walls located at
x=0
and
x=1.
The walls are assumed to have a vanishing velocity and given temperatures
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
(
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1}
) respectively. In the kinetic setting, the distribution function satisfies the following 1D rescaled steady Boltzmann equation:

\displaystyle v_{1}\partial_{x}F^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}Q(F^{\varepsilon},F^{\varepsilon}),\quad x\in(0,1),~v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.1)

The parameter
\varepsilon>0
is the Knudsen number which is proportional to the mean free path and is assumed to be small.
The Boltzmann collision term on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) takes the non-symmetric bilinear form of

Q(F_{1},F_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(|v-u|,\omega)[F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})-F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)]\,{\rm d}\omega{\rm d}u,

where the velocity pair
(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})
is defined by the velocity pair
(v,u)
as well as the parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the relation

v^{\prime}=v-[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad u^{\prime}=u+[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,

according to conservation laws of momentum and energy

v^{\prime}+u^{\prime}=v+u,\quad|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|u^{\prime}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|u|^{2}

for elastic collision. For simplicity, we consider the hard sphere model when the collision kernel
B(|v-u|,\omega)=|v-u||\cos\psi|
with relative velocity
v-u
and interaction angle defined by

\cos\psi=\omega\cdot(v-u)/|v-u|
. Denote the outward normal vectors at each boundary point
x\in\{0,1\}
by

n(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} (-1,0,0),\quad&x=0,\\
(1,0,0),\quad&x=1.\end{aligned}\right.

The phase boundary
\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be decomposed into three parts:

\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}=\gamma_{+}\cup\gamma_{0}\cup\gamma_{-},

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\mid n(x)\cdot v\gtrless 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\{0\}\times\{v_{1}\lessgtr 0\}\right)\cup\left(\{1\}\times\{v_{1}\gtrless 0\}\right),

(1.2)

and

\gamma_{0}=\{0,1\}\times\{v_{1}=0\}.

The distribution of gas particles at thermal walls satisfies the diffusive reflection boundary condition, which is given by

F^{\varepsilon}(x,v)|\gamma_{-}=\mu_{\theta_{w}}\int_{\{n(x)\cdot v>0\}}F^{\varepsilon}(x,v)\{n(x)\cdot v\}\,{\rm d}v,\quad x=0,1,

(1.3)

where the wall Maxwellian
\mu_{\theta_{w}}
is

\displaystyle\mu_{\theta_{w}}(v)=\frac{1}{2\pi\theta^{2}_{w}(x)}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2\theta_{w}(x)}},~\theta_{w}(0)=\theta_{0},~\theta_{w}(1)=\theta_{1}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\theta_{0}=1
and
\theta_{1}>\theta_{0}.
Moreover, the total mass of the solution is equal to
1
throughout the paper, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F^{\varepsilon}(x,v)\,{\rm d}v{\rm d}x=1.

(1.4)

1.2.
CNS approximation

We are interested in the behavior of solution
F^{\varepsilon}
in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
that is the hydrodynamic limit of the Boltzmann equation. In the absence of physical boundaries or shocks, it is well-known that the distribution function converges to a local Maxwellian with parameters satisfying the compressible Euler system;
cf.

[
17
]
. The Chapman-Enskog expansion yields the compressible Navier-Stokes system (CNS) as the first order correction. In this subsection, we give a formal derivation of CNS approximation in the setting of this paper. Before this, we define some function spaces which will be used later. Given a local Maxwellian

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{[\rho,u,\theta]}(v)=\frac{\rho}{(2\pi\theta)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{|v-u|^{2}}{2\theta}}

with density
\rho(x)>0
, velocity
u(x)
and temperature
\theta(x)>0
, we define a function space
L^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}
, equipped with the following inner product in
v
:

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{fg}{\mathcal{M}}\,{\rm d}v.

The linearized collision operator around
\mathcal{M}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)=-\left[Q(\mathcal{M},\cdot)+Q(\cdot,\mathcal{M})\right].

The following properties of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
are well-known (cf.
[
16
]
). That is,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
has a null space
\text{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
in
L^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}
that is spanned by the following functions

\chi_{1}=\mathcal{M},~\chi_{i}=\frac{v_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\mathcal{M},~i=1,2,3,~\chi_{4}=\frac{|v|^{2}-3\theta}{\sqrt{6}\theta}\mathcal{M}.

We also define the macroscopic projection operator
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}
as the projection onto
\text{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
. If
f
is orthogonal to null space of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
, the following coercive inequality holds:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}}\geq c_{0}|\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}f|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}},

(1.5)

for some positive constants
c_{0}
, where
\nu(v)=1+|v|
.

Without boundary, by formally passing limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
1.1
) we see that the leading order gives the local Maxwellian
\mathcal{M}
which satisfies
Q(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=0
. Hence,
we set the following expansion

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon G+\varepsilon^{2}F_{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}),

(1.6)

where
G
and
F_{2}
are some correctors to be constructed later.
Inserting the expansion (
1.6
) into (
1.1
) yields the error

\displaystyle E=v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{L}_{M}G+\varepsilon\left[v_{1}\partial_{x}G+\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{2}-Q(G,G)\right]+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Then we can eliminate the terms of
O(1)
and
O(\varepsilon)
order by choosing

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\left[(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}\right],

(1.7)

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\left[(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})v_{1}\partial_{x}G-Q(G,G)\right]+\tilde{F}_{2},

(1.8)

where
\tilde{F}_{2}\in\text{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
will be determined later. Here,

\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}v_{1}\partial_{x}G=0.

(1.9)

It is straightforward to check that (
1.9
) is equivalent to the following 1-D steady compressible Navier-Stokes system for
x\in(0,1)
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{x}(\rho u_{1})=0,\\
&\partial_{x}(\rho u_{1}^{2}+\rho\theta)=\varepsilon\partial_{x}(\frac{4}{3}\iota(\theta)\partial_{x}u_{1}),\\
&\partial_{x}(\rho u_{1}u_{j})=\varepsilon\partial_{x}(\iota(\theta)\partial_{x}u_{j}),\quad j=2,3,\\
&\partial_{x}\left[\rho u_{1}\left(\frac{5\theta}{2}+\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\right)\right]=\varepsilon\partial_{x}\left(\frac{4}{3}\iota(\theta)u_{1}\partial_{x}u_{1}+\sum_{i=2,3}\iota(\theta)u_{i}\partial_{x}u_{i}+\kappa(\theta)\partial_{x}\theta\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

Here, the viscosity
\iota(\theta)
and heat conductivity
\kappa(\theta)
are respectively given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\iota(\theta)=\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}A_{j}(\xi)\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}}^{-1}A_{j}(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}{\rm d}v,\quad i=2\text{ or }3,\\
&\kappa(\theta)=\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}B(\xi)\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}}^{-1}B(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}{\rm d}v,~~\xi=\frac{v-u}{\sqrt{\theta}},\end{aligned}\right.

where

A_{j}(\xi)=\xi_{j}\xi_{1}-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{3}\delta_{1j},\quad B(\xi)=\frac{|\xi|^{2}-5}{2}\xi_{1}

are the Burnett functions. It is well-known (cf.
[
4
]
) that these functions satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}}^{-1}[A_{i}(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}]=\alpha(|\xi|,\theta)A_{i}(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]},

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}}^{-1}[B(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}]=\beta(|\xi|,\theta)B(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]},

with two smooth scalar functions
\alpha(|\xi|,\theta)
and
\beta(|\xi|,\theta)
. After some
scaling, one has

\displaystyle\alpha(\xi,\theta)A_{i}(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}=\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\alpha(\xi,1)A_{i}(\xi)\mu,

\displaystyle\beta(\xi,\theta)B(\xi)\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}=\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\beta(\xi,1)B(\xi)\mu,

where

\mu(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/2}

is the normalized global Maxwellian. Here, the scalar functions

\alpha(\xi,1),\beta(\xi,1)
are determined by

\alpha(\xi,1)A_{i}(\xi)\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1}A_{i}(\xi)\mu,\quad\beta(\xi,1)B(\xi)\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1}B(\xi)\mu,

and
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-[Q(\mu,\cdot)+Q(\cdot,\mu)]
is the linearized collision operator around
\mu
. Then viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients are given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\iota(\theta)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|A_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\alpha(\xi,1)\mu(\xi){\rm d}\xi\right)\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bar{\iota}\theta^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad j=2\text{ or }3,\\
&\kappa(\theta)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|B(\xi)|^{2}\beta(|\xi|,1)\mu(\xi){\rm d}\xi\right)\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bar{\kappa}\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly,
G
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}}^{-1}\left[\sum_{i=1,2,3}A_{i}(\xi)\partial_{x}u_{i}+\frac{\partial_{x}\theta}{\sqrt{\theta}}B(\xi)\right]\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}

\displaystyle=-\left[\alpha(|\xi|,\theta)\left(\sum_{i=1,2,3}A_{i}(\xi)\partial_{x}u_{i}\right)+\beta(|\xi|,\theta)B(\xi)\frac{\partial_{x}\theta}{\sqrt{\theta}}\right]\mathcal{M}_{[1,u,\theta]}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\left[\alpha(\xi,1)\left(\sum_{i=1,2,3}A_{i}(\xi)\partial_{x}{u}_{i}\right)+\beta(\xi,1)B(\xi)\frac{\partial_{x}{\theta}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\right]\mu(\xi).

(1.11)

1.3.
Slip boundary condition

In order to solve compressible Navier-Stokes system (
1.10
) when
x\in(0,1)
, suitable boundary conditions are needed. If we consider the no-slip boundary condition

u(0)=u(1)=0,\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0},\quad\theta(1)=\theta_{1},

the approximation (
1.6
) matches the boundary conditions (
1.3
) up to
O(1)
. However, since
G
contains non-Maxwellian terms, the Chapman-Enskog approximation
\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon G
in general does not match the boundary condition (
1.3
) up to
O(\varepsilon)
, except for the case when

\partial_{x}u(0)=\partial_{x}u(1)=\partial_{x}\theta(0)=\partial_{x}\theta(1)=0.

However, then (
1.10
) is overdetermined. To obtain a more accurate approximation, Coron
[
9
]

formally
derived the slip boundary conditions for compressible Navier-Stokes equations, which are essentially a consequence of the analysis of the Knudsen layer. In what follows, we elaborate the derivation only in one dimensional case. We refer to
[
1
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
for
the physical investigations in general cases.

As in
[
9
]
, since Chapman-Enskog expansion is not valid near the boundary,
we introduce Knudsen layers
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
around boundary points
x=0
and
x=1
respectively. The construction of Knudsen layers relies on the solutions to the following Milne problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{1}\partial_{y}\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\mathcal{F}=0,~y>0,~v\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&\mathcal{F}(0,v)|_{v_{1}>0}=\mathcal{G},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}\mathcal{F}{\rm d}v=0,\\
&\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{F}(y)=\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\text{ exists and belongs to }\text{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{\mu},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

where
\mathcal{G}
is a given incoming distribution function. The well-posedness of (
1.12
) has been shown in
[
3
]
, and is summarized in Lemma
6.1
for later use.

Now we construct the Knudsen layer
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
at the boundary points
x=0
and
x=1
respectively. Let
p\in\{0,1\}
be a boundary point.
We set the boundary conditions of Navier-Stokes system as

\displaystyle u(p)=\varepsilon\tilde{u}(p),~~\theta(p)-\theta_{p}=\varepsilon\tilde{\theta}(p),

(1.13)

where
\tilde{u}(p)
and
\tilde{\theta}(p)
are corrections to be determined later.
Then we expand the boundary values of
\mathcal{M}(x,v)|_{x=p}
and
G(x,v)|_{x=p}
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(p)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\theta_{p}}{2\pi}}\rho(p)\mu_{\theta_{p}}+\varepsilon\left\{\frac{\tilde{u}(p)\cdot v}{\theta_{p}}+\frac{|v|^{2}-3\theta_{p}}{2\theta^{2}_{p}}\tilde{\theta}(p)\right\}\rho(p)\sqrt{\frac{\theta_{p}}{2\pi}}\mu_{\theta_{p}}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{M}_{R}(p)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\theta_{p}}{2\pi}}\rho(p)\mu_{\theta_{p}}+\varepsilon\mathcal{M}_{1}(p)+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{M}_{R}(p),~~p=0,1,

(1.14)

and

\displaystyle G(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu_{\theta_{p}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg\{\alpha\big(\frac{|v|}{\sqrt{\theta_{p}}},1\big)A\big(\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{p}}}\big)\partial_{x}{u}(p)+\beta\big(\frac{|v|}{\sqrt{\theta_{p}}},1\big)B\big(\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{p}}}\big)\frac{\partial_{x}{\theta}(p)}{\sqrt{\theta_{p}}}\bigg\}+\varepsilon G_{R}(p)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=G_{0}(p)+\varepsilon G_{R}(p),~~p=0,1.

(1.15)

Now we consider
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
first.
To compensate
G_{0}
at
x=0
, we take
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,i},i=1,2,3
and
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
as solutions to the Milne problem (
1.12
) with incoming distribution functions

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,i}=\alpha(|v|,1)A_{i}(v)\mu,i=1,2,3
and
\mathcal{G}_{\beta}=\beta(|v|,1)B(v)\mu
.
By
\eqref{C.a.2}_{4}
, there exist positive constants
c_{\alpha,1},c_{\alpha,2},\underline{c},c_{\beta,1}
and
c_{\beta,2}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,1,\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,1}(y)=c_{\alpha,1}\mu+c_{\alpha,2}\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{2}\mu,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,j,\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,j}(y)=\underline{c}v_{j}\mu,~~j=2,3,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\beta,\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(y)=c_{\beta,1}\mu+c_{\beta,2}\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{2}\mu.

Then we define the Knudsen layer
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,2,3}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,i}\left(\frac{\rho(0)x}{\varepsilon},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{0}}}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,i,\infty}\left(\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{0}}}\right)\right]\frac{\partial_{x}u_{i}(0)}{\theta^{2}_{0}}

\displaystyle+\left[\mathcal{F}_{\beta}\left(\frac{\rho(0)x}{\varepsilon},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{0}}}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{\beta,\infty}\left(\frac{v}{\sqrt{\theta_{0}}}\right)\right]\frac{\partial_{x}\theta(0)}{\theta^{5/2}_{0}}.

By a straightforward computation, one has

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{0}|_{x=0}=-G_{0}(0)-\Psi(0),

where

\displaystyle\Psi(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\alpha,1}\sqrt{\theta_{0}}\partial_{x}u_{1}(0)+c_{\beta,1}\partial_{x}\theta(0)}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta_{0}}}\mu_{\theta_{0}}+\sum_{i=2,3}\frac{\underline{c}\partial_{x}{u}_{i}(0)v_{i}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta_{0}}}\mu_{\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{\alpha,2}\sqrt{\theta_{0}}\partial_{x}{u_{1}(0)}+c_{\beta,2}\partial_{x}\theta(0)}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta_{0}}}\frac{|v|^{2}-3\theta_{0}}{2\theta^{2}_{0}}\mu_{\theta_{0}}\in\text{Ker}L_{\mu_{\theta_{0}}}.

(1.17)

Note that the Maxwellian part in (
1.3
) already satisfies the boundary condition (
1.3
) at
x=0
. Then we use
\mathcal{M}_{1}(0)
given in (
1.3

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{1}(0)=0,~~\tilde{u}_{i}(0)=\frac{\underline{c}\partial_{x}u_{i}(0)}{\rho(0)},i=2,3,~~\tilde{\theta}(0)=\frac{c_{\alpha,2}\sqrt{\theta_{0}}\partial_{x}{u_{1}(0)}}{\rho(0)}+\frac{c_{\beta,2}\partial_{x}\theta(0)}{\rho(0)}.

In view of (
1.13

to satisfy the following slip boundary condition:

\displaystyle u_{1}(0)=0,\quad{\rho(0)}{u}_{i}(0)=\varepsilon\underline{c}\partial_{x}u_{i}(0),i=2,3,

\displaystyle\rho(0)[\theta(0)-\theta_{0}]={\varepsilon c_{\alpha,2}\sqrt{\theta_{0}}\partial_{x}{u_{1}(0)}}+{\varepsilon c_{\beta,2}\partial_{x}\theta(0)}

(1.18)

at
x=0.

Similarly, at
x=1

\mathfrak{B}_{1}
at

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{1}=

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,1}\left(\frac{\rho(1)(1-x)}{\varepsilon},\frac{\mathfrak{R}v}{\sqrt{\theta_{1}}}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,1,\infty}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{R}v}{\sqrt{\theta_{1}}}\right)\right]\frac{\partial_{x}u(1)}{\theta^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle+\left[-\mathcal{F}_{\beta}\left(\frac{\rho(1)(1-x)}{\varepsilon},\frac{\mathfrak{R}v}{\sqrt{\theta_{1}}}\right)+\mathcal{F}_{\beta,\infty}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{R}v}{\sqrt{\theta_{1}}}\right)\right]\frac{\partial_{x}\theta(1)}{\theta^{5/2}_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{1}|_{x=1}=-G_{0}(1)-\Psi(1),

\displaystyle\Psi(1)=

\displaystyle\frac{-c_{\alpha,1}\sqrt{\theta_{1}}\partial_{x}u_{1}(1)+c_{\beta,1}\partial_{x}\theta(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta_{1}}}\mu_{\theta_{1}}-\sum_{i=2,3}\frac{\underline{c}\partial_{x}{u}_{i}(1)v_{i}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta_{1}}}\mu_{\theta_{1}}

1.4.
Main result

The paper aims to justify rigorously the slip boundary conditions presented in the previous section. For this, we start with the following expansion

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon G+\varepsilon\mathfrak{B}_{0}+\varepsilon\mathfrak{B}_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}F_{2}+\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}F_{R},

(1.22)

where
\alpha>0
is a positive constant. Here we elaborate the approximate solutions appearing in the expansion: The leading order term
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{[\rho,u,\theta]}
is a local Maxwellian where
[\rho,u,\theta]
satisfies the steady compressible Navier-Stokes equations with
slip
boundary conditions (
1.3
) and (
1.3
). It will be constructed in Sec.
3.1
. The function

is a corrector at order
\varepsilon

1.2
) and it satisfies (
1.7
).
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
are Knudsen layers which are defined in (
1.16
) and (

) respectively. For technical reasons, we need a high-order corrector

Define the weight function

\displaystyle w(v)=(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}e^{\varpi|v|^{2}}

with
\beta>3
and

.
The main result in this paper can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
|\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}|\leq\delta_{0}

\delta_{0}
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0

1.3
) and total mass condition (

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}+\varepsilon^{-1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\alpha}|\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}|.

(1.24)

Here the constant

Remark 1.2
.

Esposito et al. in
[
13
,
14

The hydrodynamic limit is one of the most fundamental problems in kinetic theory. There are extensive studies on the mathematical description of relations between Boltzmann equation and various of hydrodynamic models. Now we review some of them which are most related to the topic of this paper. For more detailed references, we refer to the book by Cercignani
[

Let us first focus on the Euler scaling. The first mathematical proof of the compressible Euler limit was given by Nishida
[
31
]
in the analytic framework. An extension of this result has been made in
[

for the case when the solution contains initial layers.
By using a truncated Hilbert expansion, Caflisch

justified the Euler limit for any given smooth Euler solutions; see also
[

for the result in

[

]
, Lachowicz
[

justified the CNS approximation over the short time interval. Recently, the global-in-time CNS approximation was justified by the second and third authors in a paper with Zhao
[
29
]

for the case when the data are close to the global equilibrium. This result was extended to case of a general bounded domain in
[
10
]
. On the other hand, the hydrodynamic limit to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations for the steady Boltzmann equation in a slab was studied by Esposito-Lebowitz-Marra
[

14

; see also a recent survey
[
15
]
.
We also refer to
[

,
38
,
39
]
for hydrodynamic limits to some wave patterns. Very recently, the compressible Euler limit in the half-space was studied in
[
22

with the specular reflection boundary condition.

In diffusive scaling, there are many interesting results on the hydrodynamic limits to the incompressible fluid systems in different settings,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\varepsilon^{-1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{-2-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{p}\varepsilon^{-1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{p}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\left|\frac{r}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{-}}+C_{p}\left\|\frac{\nu^{-1}wg}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{p}\left|\frac{wr}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right|_{L^{\infty}_{-}}.

(4.5)

Here, the positive constant
C_{p}>0
does not depend on
\varepsilon.

4.1.
L^{2}
-estimate

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F_{R}
be a solution to the linearized problem (
4.1
). Assume that
g
and
r
satisfy (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) respectively. There exists
\tau_{0}>0
, such that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon\ll 1
and for any

\eta\in(0,1)
, it holds

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\gamma})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{+}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\varepsilon\left(\eta+|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\right)\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{\eta}e^{-\frac{\tau_{0}}{\varepsilon}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{\eta}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{r}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{-}}\right).

(4.6)

Here, the constant
C_{\eta}
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Proof:
For simplicity, we denote

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\frac{1}{2\pi\theta_{NS}^{2}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2\theta_{NS}}}.

By taking inner product of (
4.1
) with
F_{R}/\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
over
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and then integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{F_{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{F_{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{F_{R}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{2\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\omega}}|F_{R}|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{gF_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v.

(4.7)

By (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{r\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\{v_{1}>0\}}r(0,v)v_{1}{\rm d}v\right)\times\left(\int_{\{v_{1}<0\}}F_{R}(0,v)|v_{1}|{\rm d}v\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\{v_{1}<0\}}r(1,v)|v_{1}|{\rm d}v\right)\times\left(\int_{\{v_{1}<0\}}F_{R}(1,v)v_{1}{\rm d}v\right)=0.

Thus, by boundary condition
\eqref{C.2.3.1}_{2}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{F_{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}+\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{r\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|r|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|r|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|r|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}.

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{F_{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{F_{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{|F_{R}|^{2}-|\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|r|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}.

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\gamma})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{|r|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}\gamma_{-}.

(4.8)

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\frac{\mathcal{M}}{\rho_{NS}\theta_{NS}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. Then
by coercivity estimate (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{F_{R}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\geq\inf\left\{\rho_{NS}\theta_{NS}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{F_{R}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(4.9)

for some positive constant
c_{1}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. By Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, it holds for any
\eta\in(0,1)
and any
\kappa\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{gF_{R}}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v\right|\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\kappa}\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-2}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.10)

For the last term on the left hand side of (
4.1
), we divide it into the following three parts:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{2\mathcal{M}_{\omega}^{2}}|F_{R}|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{2\mathcal{M}_{\omega}^{2}}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v+2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{2\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})f_{R}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}}{2\mathcal{M}_{\omega}^{2}}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\quad:

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

(4.11)

For
I_{1}
, by integrating (
4.1
) over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and using (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}(x,v){\rm d}v=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(x,v){\rm d}v=0.

Then by (
4.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}(x,v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}(0,v){\rm d}v=\int_{\{v_{1}>0\}}r(0,v)v_{1}{\rm d}v=0,

which implies
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}
is even in
v_{1}.
Then it holds

\displaystyle I_{1}=0.

(4.12)

Now we fix
\kappa\in(0,1)
.
For
I_{2}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we deduce that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\kappa\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})f_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\kappa}\varepsilon^{2}|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.13)

For
I_{3}
, as in
[
23
]
, we write it as

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(1+|v|)^{3}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{|v|\leq\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}}\frac{(1+|v|)^{3}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{|v|>\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}}\frac{(1+|v|)^{3}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x.

For
|v|\leq\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}},
we have

\displaystyle(1+|v|)^{2}\leq C(1+|v|^{2})\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}(\varepsilon+\kappa^{2}).

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{|v|\leq\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}}\frac{(1+|v|)^{3}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}(\varepsilon+\kappa^{2})\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Recall the weight function
w
defined in (
1.23
).
For
|v|>\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, by noticing that

\displaystyle\theta_{NS}(x)\geq\theta_{NS}(0)=1+O(1)\varepsilon,

we have

\displaystyle w^{-1}(1+|v|)^{4}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(\varpi-C\varepsilon)|v|^{2}}(1+|v|)^{-(\beta-4)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\varpi}{2}|v|^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\kappa^{2}\varpi}{2\varepsilon}}.

Then we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{|v|>\kappa\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}}\frac{(1+|v|)^{3}|(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}|^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\kappa^{2}\varpi}{\varepsilon}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|v|)^{-5}{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\kappa^{2}\varpi}{\varepsilon}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.

Combining these two estimates, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq C(\varepsilon+\kappa^{2})\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Ce^{-\frac{\kappa^{2}\varpi}{\varepsilon}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.

(4.14)

Putting estimates (
4.1
)-(
4.14
) together gives

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\gamma})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{+}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\varepsilon+\kappa)\left\|\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\left(\eta+|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\right)\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Ce^{-\frac{\kappa^{2}\varpi}{\varepsilon}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-2}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\kappa}\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon\left|\frac{r}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{-}}^{2}.

(4.15)

Here,
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
\eta\in(0,1)
are two arbitrary constants. By taking
\kappa
suitably small in (
4.1
), we obtain (
4.2
). The proof of Lemma
4.2
is completed.∎

4.2.
L^{p}
-estimates on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

for any
p\in[2,\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\varepsilon\left|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\gamma})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{+}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\left|\frac{r}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right|_{L^{2}_{-}}.

(4.16)

Proof:
The proof is based on the dual argument developed in
[
11
,
12
]
. We need to slightly modify their method here since the reference Maxwellian
\mathcal{M}
depends on
x
. We decompose
F_{R}
as

\displaystyle F_{R}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}+(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{a(x)}{\rho_{NS}}\mathcal{M}+\frac{b(x)\cdot v}{\rho_{NS}\theta_{NS}}\mathcal{M}+\frac{c(x)}{2\rho_{NS}\theta_{NS}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{\theta_{NS}}-3\right)\mathcal{M}+(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}.

Thus, it suffices to obtain
L^{p}
-estimate of
(a,b,c)
. For simplicity, we only estimate
a
because the estimates for
b
and
c
are similar. Let
\phi(x,v)
be any smooth test function. Then taking inner product of
\eqref{C.2.3.1}_{1}
with
\phi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}\phi(1,v)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}\phi(0,v){\rm d}v\right]-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}F_{R}\partial_{x}\phi(x,v){\rm d}v{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}-g\right)(x,v)\phi{\rm d}v{\rm d}x=0.

(4.17)

We take the test function
\phi_{a}=\partial_{x}\psi_{a}\frac{v_{1}(|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS})}{\theta_{NS}^{2}},
where
\psi_{a}
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\partial_{xx}\psi_{a}&=a^{p-1}-\int_{0}^{1}a^{p-1}(\tau){\rm d}\tau,\\
\partial_{x}\psi_{a}(0)&=\partial_{x}\psi_{a}(1)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.18)

One can check that

\displaystyle\psi_{a}=-\int_{0}^{x}\left[a^{p-1}(s)-\int_{0}^{1}a^{p-1}(\tau){\rm d}\tau\right](x-s){\rm d}s

is a solution to (
4.18
) and it satisfies the following estimate

\displaystyle|\psi_{a}|_{C^{1}([0,1])}+|\partial_{xx}\psi_{a}|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{x}}\leq C|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}.

(4.19)

Now we insert
\phi_{\alpha}
into (
4.2
). Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}^{2}(|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS})(|v|^{2}-3\theta_{NS})\mathcal{M}{\rm d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}^{3}(|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS})\mathcal{M}{\rm d}v=0,

we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}\partial_{x}\phi_{a}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}\frac{|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS}}{\theta_{NS}^{2}}v_{1}^{2}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}^{2}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}\partial_{x}\psi_{a}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS}}{\theta^{2}_{NS}}\right){\rm d}v{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=5\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}a\left(a^{p-1}-\int_{0}^{1}a^{p-1}(\tau){\rm d}\tau\right){\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}^{2}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}\partial_{x}\psi_{a}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-10\theta_{NS}}{\theta^{2}_{NS}}\right){\rm d}v{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq 5\varepsilon|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}-C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}|[a,b,c]|_{L^{p}_{x}}|\partial_{x}\psi_{a}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\geq 5\varepsilon|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}-C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}|[a,b,c]|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}.

Here, we have used (
4.19
) and (
4.4
) so that

\int_{0}^{1}a{\rm d}x\cdot\int_{0}^{1}a^{p-1}(\tau){\rm d}\tau=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{R}{\rm d}x{\rm d}v\cdot\int_{0}^{1}a^{p-1}(\tau){\rm d}\tau=0.

Thanks to the Neumann boundary condition
\eqref{C.2.3.17}_{2}
,
the boundary contribution in (
4.2
) vanishes, that is,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}f_{R}\phi_{a}(1,v){\rm d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}f_{R}\phi_{a}(0,v){\rm d}v=0.

(4.21)

By Hölder’s inequality, the rest terms in (
4.2
) are bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{1}(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}\partial_{x}\phi_{a}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}(|\partial_{xx}\psi_{a}|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{x}}+|\partial_{x}\psi_{\alpha}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1},

(4.22)

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}-g\right]\phi_{a}{\rm d}v{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right]|\partial_{x}\psi_{\alpha}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right]|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p-1}.

(4.23)

Substituting (
4.20
)-(
4.2
) into (
4.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\varepsilon|a|_{L^{p}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}|[a,b,c]|_{L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}+C\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

The
L^{p}
-estimates for
b
and
c
can be obtained in the same way. To summarize, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\partial_{x}\theta_{NS}|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}}F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}+C\varepsilon\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\frac{\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}g}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.24)

Combining (
4.2
) with an interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\|\frac{(I-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{M}})F_{R}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\left\|\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

yields (
4.3
). Therefore, the proof of Lemma
4.3
is completed. ∎

4.3.
Weighted
L^{\infty}
estimate

Recall the normalized global Maxwellian
\mu(v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}
and associated linearized collision operator
L=\nu-K
. Define
h=\frac{wF_{R}}{\sqrt{\mu}}
. Then the equation of
h
reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{1}\partial_{x}h+\varepsilon^{-1}\nu(v)h-\varepsilon^{-1}K_{w}h=\varepsilon^{-1}J,\quad x\in(0,1),v\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&h|_{\gamma_{-}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{w}(v)}\int_{\{n(x)\cdot v>0\}}h(x,u)\tilde{w}(u){\rm d}\sigma+q,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.25)

where we have used the notations:

\tilde{w}(v)=\frac{1}{w(v)\sqrt{2\pi\mu(v)}},~{\rm d}\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu(v)\{n(x)\cdot v\}{\rm d}v,~K_{w}h=wK\left(\frac{h}{w}\right).

J
and
q
are inhomogeneous source which are given by

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\frac{w}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left[Q(\mathcal{M}-\mu,\frac{\sqrt{\mu}h}{w})+Q(\frac{\sqrt{\mu}h}{w},\mathcal{M}-\mu)\right]+\frac{wg}{\sqrt{\mu}},

(4.26)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=\frac{w}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\theta_{NS}^{2}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2\theta_{NS}}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2\pi}}\right)\left(\int_{\{n(x)\cdot v>0\}}h(x,u)\tilde{w}(u){\rm d}\sigma\right)+\frac{wr}{\sqrt{\mu}}.

(4.27)

Fix
t>0
as a parameter. Given any
(x,v)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
[X(s),V(s)]
be the backward bi-characteristics, which is determined by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\rm d}X(s)}{{\rm d}s}=V^{1}(s),~\frac{{\rm d}V(s)}{{\rm d}s}=0,\\
&[X(t),V(t)]=[x,v].\end{aligned}\right.

The solution is then given by

[X(s),V(s)]=[X(s;t,x,v),V(s;t,x,v)]=[x-(t-s)v^{1},v].

We then define the backward exit time
t_{\mathbf{b}}(x,v)

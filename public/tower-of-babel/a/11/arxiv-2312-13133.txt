New dimer integrable systems and defects in five dimensional gauge theory Preprint: CGP-23022
Norton Lee

Abstract
We study the relation between the quantum integrable systems derived from the dimer graphs and five dimensional 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supersymmetric gauge theories on S 1 × ℝ 4 S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{4} . We construct integrable systems based on new dimer graphs obtained from modification of hexagon dimer diagram. We study the gauge theories in correspondence to the newly proposed integrable systems. By examining three types of defects – a line defect, a canonical co-dimensional two defect and a monodromy defect – in five-dimensional gauge theory with 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supersymmetry and Ω ε 1 , ε 2 \Omega_{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}} -background. We identify, in the ε 2 → 0 {\varepsilon}_{2}\to 0 limit, the canonical co-dimensional two defect satisfying the Baxter T-Q equation of the generalized A A -type dimer integrable system, and the monodromy defect as its common eigenstate of the commuting Hamiltonians, with the eigenvalues being the expectation value of the BPS Wilson loop in the anti-symmetric representation of the bulk gauge group.

Introduction

The intimite relation between the supersymmetric gauge theory and integrable system with finite number of degrees of freedom has been a fertile research ground over the past few decades.
The physical quantities (coupling, masses) turn out to be parameters of these finite dimensional system. This connection is known as the
Bethe/gauge correspondence
.
The most well-known in this story is that the Seiberg-Witten curve of
\mathcal{N}=2
four dimensional gauge theory can be identified as the spectral curve of the integrable system.

This remarkable correspondence was promoted to the relation between the quantum integrable systems and the
\Omega
-deformed four dimensional supersymmetric gauge theories with two parameters
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
{\varepsilon}_{2}
. The localization technique
Nikita-Shatashvili
;
NRS2011
allows analytic computation of the partition function and certain BPS observables in a large class of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories in four dimension
Nikita:I
. The
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}\to 0
limit reveals the classical integrable system whose phase space is identified as the moduli space of some partial differential equation of gauge theoretic origin
Nekrasov:2012xe
. In the Nekrasov-Shatashvili limit (NS-limit)
{\varepsilon}_{2}\to 0
and
{\varepsilon}_{1}=\hbar
, one expects to find the quantum version of the integrable system. The quantum Hamiltonians are identified with the twisted chiral ring of the two dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
gauge theories.

This correspondence can be easily extended to supersymmetric gauge theory in 5 dimension with eight supercharges compactify on a circle.
The Seiberg-Witten curve corresponds to relativistic integrable system where the compactified circle radius
R
acting as inverse of speed of light
Nekrasov:1996cz
.
In the four-dimensional limit
R\to 0

In particular, the Bethe/gauge correspondence is the tool to determine the Seiberg-Witten curve of the five dimensional gauge theories.

Dimer models and integrable systems

The integrable systems in correspondence to five dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge theories are often relativistic due the the exponential kinetic and potential terms in the Hamiltonians from quantizing the mirror curve of the local Calabi-Yau geometry.

In this paper we consider a class of cluster integrable systems proposed by Goncharov and Kenyon
goncharov2011dimers
: integrable systems in correspondence with the dimer models on a torus. These integrable systems are the generalization of the affine
A
-type relativistic Toda lattice
Eager:2011dp
. According to the correspondence, every dimer model defines an integrable system, whose conserving charges can be systematically calculated based on the perfect matching of the bipartite graph.

The dual graph of the dimer model is a planar, periodic quiver. The quiver gauge theory arises from the worldvolume of stack of D3 probling a singular, toric Calabi-Yau (CY) 3-fold
Benvenuti:2004wx
. There is a string dual in type IIB on
AdS_{5}\times X_{5}

X_{5}

is Sasaki-Einstein. The Sasaki-Einstein manifold

metric depends on two positive integer
(N,\mathsf{k})

\mathsf{k}<N
. As such, the quiver gauge theory and the dimer model are also characterized by
Y^{N,\mathsf{k}}
system.
For the
Y^{N,N}
system it is well known Hexagon tiling
Eager:2011dp
.
The
Y^{N,\mathsf{k}}
,
\mathsf{k}<N
, systems are obtained by introducing impurity to the
Y^{N,N}
quiver. The dimer graph then is obtained by taking the dual of the planar quiver
Franco:2005rj
;
Benvenuti:2004dy

We find that by working with the dimer graph directly, new
Y^{N,k}[S]
system associated to dimer graph different from the standard one from the planar quiver can be generated. These new dimer graphs are characterized by a finite set
S
indicated by how the impurities are introduced to the

Relativistic Toda lattice

The relativistic Toda lattice describes
N
particles on a circle. The
n
-th particle’s position and moment are labeled by the hermitian operators
\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}
and
\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}
satisfying the commutation relation

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n},\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{m}\right]=\hbar_{\text{RT}}\delta_{n,m},\ n,m=1,\dots,N.

(1)

The model depends on a real parameter
R
,
\hbar=R\hbar_{\text{RT}}
.
The Hamilton is

\displaystyle\hat{\rm H}_{\text{RT}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[1+e^{\frac{-1}{2}\hbar}R^{2}e^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n+1}}\right]e^{R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}}.

(2)

The lattice is periodic,
\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{N+n}=\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}
. We can use the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula

\displaystyle e^{\hat{A}}e^{\hat{B}}=e^{\hat{Z}}

(3)

with

\displaystyle\hat{Z}=\hat{A}+\hat{B}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{A},\hat{B}]+\frac{1}{12}[\hat{A},[\hat{A},\hat{B}]]-\frac{1}{12}[\hat{B},[\hat{A},\hat{B}]]+\cdots

(4)

to rewrite the Hamiltonian in the following form

\displaystyle\hat{\rm H}_{\text{RT}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}}+R^{2}e^{R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}+\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n+1}}.

(5)

The relativistic Toda system is integrable. It has
N
commuting Hamiltonians which can be obtained by constructing the Lax matrix

\displaystyle L_{n}(x,R)=\begin{pmatrix}x-\frac{e^{R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}}}{x}&Re^{-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}}\\
-Re^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}+R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

The corresponding monodromy matrix is

\displaystyle{\bf T}(x,R)=L_{N}(x,R)\cdots L_{1}(x,R),

(7)

and the transfer matrix

\displaystyle T(x)={\rm Tr}{\bf T}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}x^{N-2n}\hat{\rm H}_{n}(\mathtt{q}_{1},\mathtt{p}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{q}_{N},\mathtt{p}_{N}).

(8)

The
\hat{\rm H}_{j}
are the commuting Hamiltonians. In particular

\displaystyle\hat{\rm H}_{1}=\hat{\rm H}_{\text{RT}}

(9a)

\displaystyle\hat{\rm H}_{N-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[1+e^{\frac{-1}{2}\hbar}R^{2}e^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n-1}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}}\right]e^{-R\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}}

(9b)

\displaystyle\hat{\rm H}_{N}=\exp\left(R\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}\right)

(9c)

The non-relativistic limit can be obtained in the
R\to 0
limit with
\hbar_{\text{RT}}
fixed. The Hamiltonian (
2
) can be expand in power of
R
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\rm H}_{\text{RT}}=&\ N+R\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}+R^{2}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}^{2}}{2}+e^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n+1}}\right]\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}R^{k+2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\frac{\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}^{k+2}}{(k+2)!}+\frac{1}{k!}e^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}+\frac{\hbar_{\text{RT}}}{2i}\right)^{k}\right]\end{split}

(10)

The coefficient of
R^{1}
is the total momentum. The coefficient of
R^{2}
is the non-relativistic Toda lattice Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{\text{Toda}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\hat{\mathtt{p}}^{2}_{n}}{2}+e^{\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n}-\hat{\mathtt{q}}_{n+1}}.

(11)

\gamma^{\prime}

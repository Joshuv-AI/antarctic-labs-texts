Abstract




Abstract:
The paper concerns the necessary maximum principle for robust optimal control problems of quadratic BSDEs.
The coefficient of the systems depends on the parameter
\theta
,
and the generator of BSDEs is of quadratic growth in
z
. Since the model is uncertain, the variational inequality is proved by weak convergence technique.
In addition, due to the generator being quadratic with respect to
z
, the
forward adjoint equations are SDEs with unbounded coefficient involving
mean oscillation martingales.
Using reverse Hölder inequality and John-Nirenberg inequality, we show that its solutions are continuous with respect to the parameter
\theta
.
The necessary and sufficient conditions for robust optimal control are proved by linearization method.

Key words:
quadratic BSDE, model uncertainty, maximum principle, robust optimal control

AMS subject classifications.
93E20, 60H10, 35K15

1
Introduction

The stochastic maximum principle, namely, the necessary condition for optimality,
is an important approach to study stochastic optimal control problems.
In 1990, Peng
[
27
]
first proved a global maximum principle for classical stochastic control systems.
From then on, this topic has been extensively explored for various stochastic systems. We refer to
Hu and Tessitore
[
10
]
for infinite-dimensional dynamics,
Buckdahn, Li and Ma
[
4
]
for mean-field control systems, Fuhrman,
Hu, Ji and Xue
[
18
]
for fully coupled forward-backward stochastic control systems,
to name but a few.
In addition, many works on maximum principle for the stochastic recursive optimal control problems have been published. For example, Peng
[
28
]

considered a local maximum principle in the case that the control domain is convex and the diffusion coefficient depends on control.
Ji and Zhou
[
22
]
investigated a local maximum principle for stochastic optimal control with terminal state constraints.
Xu
[
31
]
established a stochastic maximum principle for the nonconvex control domain in the case that the diffusion coefficient
is independent of control. Hu and Ji
[
15
]
studied stochastic maximum principle for stochastic recursive optimal control problems under volatility ambiguity. Hu
[
13
]
obtained a global stochastic maximum principle by introducing two new adjoint equations, and solved Peng’s
open problem completely.

In many scenarios, we meet the situation that the model is uncertain and the generator of BSDEs is quadratic growth.
Our motivation of the present paper is to study the control problem of such kind of model. We give two
examples for the concerned model which come from risk-sensitive control problems and optimal strategy problems for large investor in
section 2
.

Inspired by the two examples in
section 2
, we consider the following robust optimal control problem with quadratic generator.
Let
\theta\in\Theta
represent different market conditions, where
\Theta
is a locally compact Polish space with distance
d
.
Assume that
V
is a given nonempty convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and consider the following forward-backward control system

\left\{\begin{aligned} X^{v}_{\theta}(t)=&x+\int_{0}^{t}b_{\theta}(s,X^{v}_{\theta}(s),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}_{\theta}(s)],v(s))ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\theta}(s,X^{v}_{\theta}(s),v(s))dW(s),\ t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{v}_{\theta}(t)=&\Phi_{\theta}(X^{v}_{\theta}(T),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}_{\theta}(T)])+\int_{t}^{T}f_{\theta}(s,X^{v}_{\theta}(s),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}_{\theta}(s)],Y^{v}_{\theta}(s),Z^{v}_{\theta}(s),v(s))ds\\
&-\int_{t}^{T}(Z_{\theta}^{v}(s))^{\top}dW_{s},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
b_{\theta}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma_{\theta}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},

f_{\theta}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \Phi_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
;

f_{\theta}
is quadratic growth with respect to
z
;
v(\cdot)
is a control process;
the precise assumptions on
b_{\theta},\sigma_{\theta},f_{\theta},\Phi_{\theta}
refer to
1
.
In order to obtain a general
result, we assume that the forward stochastic differential equation (SDE, for short) is mean-field type, and consider
the following cost functional

J(v(\cdot))=\sup\limits_{Q\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi_{\theta}(X_{\theta}^{v}(T))+\gamma_{\theta}(Y^{v}_{\theta}(0))\Big]Q(d\theta).

The assumptions on
\phi_{\theta}
and
\gamma_{\theta}
lie in
2
.
The robust optimal control problem with quadratic generator is to minimize the cost function
J(v(\cdot))
over
{\cal V}_{ad}

(see
3.1
). The target of
the present paper is to give the necessary and sufficient conditions of the optimal control.

Note that the solvability of BSDEs with quadratic generators has been intensively investigated over the past twenty years. In 2000, Kobylanski
[
24
]
investigated the existence and uniqueness of one-dimensional BSDEs in the case that the generator
g
is of quadratic growth in
z
and the terminal value
\xi
is bounded.
Briand and Hu
[
2
,
3
]
obtained the existence and uniqueness of one-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth and unbounded terminal value.
The multi-dimensional situation with bounded terminal value was studied by Hu and Tang
[
20
]
and Xing and Zitkovic
[
30
]
;
and the multi-dimensional situation with unbounded terminal value was investigated by Fan, Hu and Tang
[
8
]
.
Some other recent developments concerning the quadratic BSDEs can be found in
Fan, Hu and Tang
[
9
]
, Hu, Li and Wen
[
19
]
, Hao, Hu, Tang and Wen
[
12
]
and so on.
In addition, much progress has been made on stochastic optimal control problems involving quadratic BSDEs. Let us cite
[
25
]
for its applications to the risk-sensitive control problems with the risk-sensitive parameter; and

[
29
,
6
,
14
,
26
]
for the exponential utility maximization problems.

Since the generator is quadratic and the model is uncertain, there are three essential difficulties in the
present work.
(i) The fact that
f
is of quadratic growth in
z
causes that
the variational equations are linear BSDEs with stochastic unbounded Lipschitz coefficients
involving BMO-martingales. Those results for BSDEs with bounded Lipschitz coefficients (see e.g.
[
21
, Lemma 3.2]
)
do not work in this setting.
In order to obtain the maximum principle, we prove a new estimate (
4.4
), i.e.,
for
p\in(1\vee 2p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}^{-1},2)
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\sup\limits_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\Gamma_{\theta}(t)|\delta Y^{\lambda}_{\theta}(t)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(\Gamma_{\theta}(t))^{\frac{2}{p}}|\delta Z^{\lambda}_{\theta}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]=0,

where
\Gamma_{\theta}(t):={\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}dW(s)\Big),\ t\in[0,T].

(ii) The forward adjoint equations are linear SDEs with stochastic unbounded coefficients. We
obtain the wellposedness of this kinds of equations, which develops the work of Gal’chuk
[
11
]
.
The continuity of its solutions with respect to
\theta
, i.e.,
for
1<p<(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)|^{p}\bigg]=0,

is proved by the theory of BMO-martingales (
5.2
),
which is necessary to prove that
\Lambda_{\theta}(\cdot)
defined by (
5.11
) is
{\cal F}
-progressively measurable.
(iii) Since we consider the robust optimal control problems, i.e., the cost functional is a supremum over a set of probability
measures, the classical convex variational method is not suitable for this situation. We adopt the weak convergence technique to prove
the variational inequality.

Compared with the existing literatures, our paper has the following contributions.
First, our model is uncertain and the generator
f
is of quadratic growth in
z
. In this case, the robust optimal control problems rather than classical optimal control problems are considered. We prove a new and nontrivial estimate (
4.4
)
and develop the work of
[
21
]
from the Lipschitz case to quadratic case.
Second, we give the existence and uniqueness result of
L^{p}
-solution to linear SDEs with stochastic unbounded coefficients,
and prove the continuity of its solutions with respect to
\theta
by reverse Hölder inequality and John-Nirenberg inequality.
In addition, the continuity of solution to
linear BSDEs with stochastic unbounded coefficients with respect to
\theta
is obtained.
Third,
since the model is uncertain, the necessary maximum principe is proved by linearization technique and weak convergence method. Moreover, under certain convexity assumptions, with the help of Sion’s minimax theorem, we prove the sufficient maximum principle.

The paper is arranged as follows. In
section 3
, we formulate the problem. Variational inequality is proved in
section 4
.
The
section 5
is devoted to the necessary and sufficient maximum principles.

2
Two examples

In this section, we present two examples which demonstrate the applications of robust optimal control problems of
quadratic BSDEs.

Example 2.1
.

(risk-sensitive control)
Assume that a market is roughly divided into two states
A
and
B
(for example, a share market is divided into a bull market or a bear market) which usually leads to the different coefficients in two states. Let
\theta=1
and 2 denote the state
A
and the state
B
, respectively, which can be characterized as
\theta\in\Theta=\{1,2\}
. Let

{\cal Q}=\{Q^{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,1]\},

where
Q^{\lambda}
is the probability such that
Q^{\lambda}(\{1\})=\lambda,\quad Q^{\lambda}(\{2\})=1-\lambda.

For simplicity, we only consider the 1-dimensional case. Suppose that there exist
N
-individual agents in a system. The state process of the
i
-th agent is described by

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{\theta}^{i,v}(t)&=b_{\theta}^{i}(t,X_{\theta}^{i,v}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{\theta}^{j,v}(t),v(t))dt+\sigma_{\theta}^{i}(t,X_{\theta}^{i,v}(t),v(t))dW^{i}(t),\\
X_{\theta}^{i,v}(t)&=x_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
W^{i},i=1,2,\cdots,N
are independent copies of a 1-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W
;
v(\cdot)
is the control process. By
{\cal V}_{ad}
we denote the set of admissible controls (see
3.1
).
The robust objective functional of the
i
-th agent is

\displaystyle J^{i}(v(\cdot))=\max\limits_{\lambda\in\{0,1\}}\bigg\{\lambda Y^{i,v}_{1}+(1-\lambda)Y^{i,v}_{2}\bigg\}=\sup_{Q^{\lambda}\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}Y^{i,v}_{\theta}Q^{\lambda}(d\theta),

(2.2)

where
(Y_{\theta}^{i,v},Z_{\theta}^{i,v})
is the solution of the following BSDE with quadratic generator (see El Karoui and Hamadenè
[
7
]
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY_{\theta}^{i,v}(t)&=-\frac{\kappa}{2}|Z_{\theta}^{i,v}(t)|^{2}dt+Z_{\theta}^{i,v}(t)dW^{i}(t),\\
Y_{\theta}^{i,v}(T)&=\Phi_{\theta}^{i}(X_{\theta}^{i,v}(T),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{\theta}^{j,v}(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

Note that the parameter
\kappa
is called risk-sensitive parameter. The
i
-th agent wants to minimize her/his objective functional.
The above problem is regarded as a risk-sensitive robust optimal control problem for particle systems.

Now assume that the game is symmetric. In other words, let

b^{i}_{\theta}=b_{\theta},\sigma^{i}_{\theta}=\sigma_{\theta},\Phi^{i}_{\theta}=\Phi_{\theta}.
As
N\rightarrow\infty
in
(
2.1
)-(
2.3
), from strong law of large number, we obtain the following FBSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{\theta}^{v}(t)&=b_{\theta}(t,X_{\theta}^{v}(t),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(t)],v(t))dt+\sigma_{\theta}(t,X_{\theta}^{v}(t),v(t))dW(t),\\
dY_{\theta}^{v}(t)&=-\frac{\kappa}{2}|Z_{\theta}^{v}(t)|^{2}dt+Z_{\theta}^{v}(t)dW(t),\\
X_{\theta}^{v}(t)&=x_{0},\ Y_{\theta}^{v}(T)=\Phi_{\theta}(X_{\theta}^{v}(T),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(T)]),\end{aligned}\right.

and, meanwhile, the cost functional becomes
J(v(\cdot))=\sup\limits_{Q^{\lambda}\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}Y_{\theta}^{v}(0)Q^{\lambda}(d\theta).

Consequently, the control problem can be written as below.

Problem R
Find an optimal control
\bar{v}(\cdot)
such that

J(\bar{v}(\cdot))=\inf_{v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}_{ad}}J(v(\cdot))=\inf_{v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}_{ad}}\sup_{Q^{\lambda}\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}Y_{\theta}^{v}(0)Q^{\lambda}(d\theta).

Example 2.2
.

(optimal strategy for large investor)
Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed convex cone.
Assume that there are
N
investors in a market and
the market consists of a bond and a stock whose prices denoted by
S_{j}(t),j=0,1
are governed by the following ODE and SDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dS_{0}(t)&=S_{0}(t)\Big[r(t)+f^{i}_{0}(t,x^{i,\pi}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{j,\pi}(t),\pi(t))\Big]dt,\\
dS_{1}(t)&=S_{1}(t)\Big[(\mu(t)-f^{i}_{1}(t,x^{i,\pi}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{j,\pi}(t),\pi(t))dt+\sigma dW(t)\Big],\end{aligned}\right.

where
(r,\mu):\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
are the interest rate and return rate, respectively;
\sigma
is the volatility of
stock;
(r,\mu)
are
\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
-adapted and uniformly bounded processes and
\sigma\neq 0
;

W
is a 1-dimensional Brownian motion; for each
i=1,2,\cdots,N
,
f^{i}_{\ell},\ell=0,1:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are two given functions representing the effect of the strategies chosen by the investors on the prices.

The
i
-th investor intends to invest
\pi^{i}(t)
in stock at time
t
and the remaining asset in bond. Under a self-financed portfolio, the wealth process of the
i
-th investor, starting with an initial wealth
x^{i}_{0}
, satisfies the following wealth equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx^{i,\pi}(t)&=\Big(r(t)x^{i,\pi}(t)+(x^{i,\pi}(t)-\pi^{i}(t))f^{i}_{0}(t,x^{i,\pi}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{j,\pi}(t),\pi(t))\\
&\quad\ +\pi^{i}(t)[\mu(t)-r(t)+f^{i}_{1}(t,x^{i,\pi}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{j,\pi}(t),\pi(t))]\Big)dt+\pi^{i}(t)\sigma dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
x^{i,\pi}(0)&=x^{i}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, let
\gamma^{i}>0
be a given parameter and
C^{i}
be the consumption at time T for the
i
-investor. Consider the following cost functional

J^{i}(\pi)=\frac{1}{\gamma^{i}}\ln\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp[-\gamma^{i}(x^{i,\pi}(T)-C^{i})]\Big].

A strategy
\pi
taking values in
K
is called admissible if for any
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\pi(t)|^{2}dt\Big]<\infty.
By
{\cal K}
we denote the
set of all admissible strategies.
The
i
-th investor intends to minimize her/his cost functional over
{\cal K}
.
Next, let
N\rightarrow\infty
and consider the asymptotic behavior of
N
investors. For simplicity, we set
\pi=\pi^{i},x_{0}=x_{0}^{i},\gamma=\gamma^{i},f_{0}=f^{i}_{0},f_{1}=f^{i}_{1}
and
C=C^{i}
. This
optimal strategy problem can be summarized as

Problem
Find an optimal strategy
\bar{\pi}(\cdot)
such that

J(\bar{\pi}(\cdot))=\inf\limits_{\pi(\cdot)\in{\cal K}}\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp[-\gamma(x^{\pi}(T)-C)]\Big],

subject to a mean-field SDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx^{\pi}(t)&=\Big(r(t)x^{\pi}(t)+(x^{\pi}(t)-\pi(t))f_{0}(t,x^{\pi}(t),\mathbb{E}[x^{\pi}(t)],\pi(t))\\
&\quad\ \ +\pi(t)[\mu(t)-r(t)+f_{1}(t,x^{\pi}(t),\mathbb{E}[x^{\pi}(t)],\pi(t))]\Big)dt+\pi(t)\sigma dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
x^{\pi}(0)&=x_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To illustrate our problem in a concise way, set

f_{0}(t,x^{\pi}(t),\mathbb{E}[x^{\pi}(t)],\pi(t)):=\alpha(t),\quad f_{1}(t,x^{\pi}(t),\mathbb{E}[x^{\pi}(t)],\pi(t)):=\beta(t).

Here
\alpha(\cdot)
and
\beta(\cdot)
are two bounded functions.
In order to compute the forward value of a portfolio, let us consider a zero-coupon bond
\Lambda
with a maturity of
T
, which
is a financial asset generating a cash-flow of
1
at time
T
. In other words, there is an
\mathbb{R}
-valued progressively
measurable process
\Gamma
such that the zero-coupon price
\Lambda
satisfies BSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\Lambda(t)&=\Lambda(t)\Big[\Big(r(t)+\Gamma(t)(\mu(t)-r(t)+\beta(t)-\alpha(t))\Big)dt+\Gamma(t)\sigma dW(t)\Big],\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\Lambda(T)&=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, for simplicity, we assume that
\Gamma
is
\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
-adapted and uniformly bounded for any
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

According to
[
29
, Theorem 2.1]
, one can see

J(\bar{\pi})=\inf\limits_{\pi(\cdot)\in{\cal K}}\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp[-\gamma(x^{\pi}(T)-C)]\Big]=-\frac{x_{0}}{\Lambda(0)}+\sup_{v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}}Y^{v}(0),

(2.4)

where
(Y^{v},Z^{v})
satisfies BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY^{v}(t)&=-\Big(-\frac{1}{2\gamma}|\delta(t)+\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(-\delta(t)-\gamma Z^{v}(t))|^{2}-(\delta(t)+\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(-\delta(t)-\gamma Z^{v}(t)))Z^{v}(t)\Big)dt\\
&\quad+Z^{v}(t)dW(t),\ \ \ t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{v}(T)&=C,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

where
\delta(t)=\sigma^{-1}(\mu(t)-r(t)+\beta(t)-\alpha(t)-\sigma^{2}\Gamma(t))
;
\widetilde{K}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}|\sup\limits_{\pi\in K}(-\pi x)<\infty\}
;

\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(u)
is the projection of
u\in\mathbb{R}
on
\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}
;

{\cal V}:=\{v:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\sigma^{-1}\widetilde{K}|\ v\ \text{is}\ \{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}\text{-adapted}\ \text{and}

\text{bounded}.

Since
|\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(u)|\leqslant|u|,
for
\forall u\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
L>0
such that the generator
of (
2.5
) is dominated by
L(1+|Z^{v}(t)|^{2})
.

The cost functional (
2.4
) tells us that solving an optimal strategy problem is equivalent to solving an optimal control problem of BSDE,
whose generator is dominated by a quadratic generator.
Following this line, a natural question is if
\gamma
and
C
depend on a parameter
\theta
and are continuous in
\theta
, how to describe the equivalent
optimal control problem, where
\theta\in\Theta
represents the different market states and
\Theta
is a closed set of
\mathbb{R}
.
Actually, in this case, we need to consider
the following cost functional

\widehat{J}(v(\cdot)):=\sup_{Q\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}Y^{v}_{\theta}(0)Q(d\theta),

where
{\cal Q}
is a weakly compact and convex set of probability measures on
(\Theta,{\cal B}(\Theta))
;

(Y^{v}_{\theta},Z^{v}_{\theta})
satisfies the following BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY^{v}_{\theta}(t)&=-\Big(-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{\theta}}|\delta(t)+\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(-\delta(t)-\gamma_{\theta}Z^{v}(t))|^{2}-(\delta(t)+\Pi_{\sigma^{-1}\tilde{K}}(-\delta(t)-\gamma_{\theta}Z^{v}(t)))Z^{v}(t)\Big)dt\\
&\quad\ +Z^{v}_{\theta}(t)dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{v}_{\theta}(T)&=C_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

Similar to (
2.5
), the generator of (
2.6
) is dominated by
L(1+|Z^{v}_{\theta}(t)|^{2})
, where
L
is a constant independent of
\theta
.
Consequently, the corresponding optimal control problem is formulated as follow.

Problem S
Find an optimal control
\bar{v}(\cdot)
such that

\widehat{J}(\bar{v}(\cdot))=\sup_{v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}}\sup_{Q\in{\cal Q}}\int_{\Theta}Y^{v}_{\theta}(0)Q(d\theta).

3
Problem formulation

3.1
Some properties for BMO-martingales

Denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of all natural numbers and by
\mathbb{R}^{+}
the set of positive real numbers, respectively.
Let the superscript
\top
denote the transpose of vectors or matrices.
Let
M=(M_{t},\mathscr{F}_{t})
be a uniformly integrable martingale with
M_{0}=0
, and we set, for
p\geqslant 1
,

\|M\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}:=\sup_{\tau}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\langle M\rangle_{\infty}-\langle M\rangle_{\tau}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\|_{\infty},

where
\tau
is a stopping time on
[0,T]
.
The class
\big\{M:\|M\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}<\infty\big\}
is denoted by
BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})
. Note that
BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})
is a Banach space under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}
. For simplicity,
BMO_{2}(\mathbb{P})
is written as
BMO
.

Next, we list some properties for
BMO
-martingales. The reader can refer to Kazamaki
[
23
]
for more details.

\bullet
Denote by
{\cal E}(M)
the Doléans–Dade exponential of a continuous local martingale
M
, i.e.,

{\cal E}(M_{t})=\exp\{M_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\langle M\rangle_{t}\}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
.
If
M\in BMO
, then
{\cal E}(M)
is a uniformly integrable martingale.

\bullet
Let
\Psi
be the monotonically decreasing function defined on
(1,\infty)
by

\displaystyle\Psi(p)=\bigg(1+p^{-2}\ln\frac{2p-1}{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1

and then
\Psi(+\infty):=\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\Psi(x)=0
. For
M\in BMO
, we can find a positive
constant
p_{M}
which satisfies
\Psi(p_{M})=\|M\|_{BMO}
. In particular, set

p_{M}=+\infty
if
\|M\|_{BMO}=0
. Then
p_{M}
is uniquely determined.

\bullet
The reverse Hölder inequality: If
p\in(1,p_{M})
, for any stopping time
\tau\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[({\cal E}(M_{T}))^{p}/({\cal E}(M_{\tau}))^{p}|{\cal F}_{\tau}\Big]\leqslant K(p,||M||_{BMO}),\ \text{a.s.},

where

K(p,||M||_{BMO})=2\bigg(1-\frac{2p-2}{2p-1}\exp\bigg\{p^{2}\Big[||M||^{2}_{BMO}+2||M||_{BMO}\Big]\bigg\}\bigg)^{-1}.

\bullet
The John-Nirenberg inequality: For
M\in BMO
, if
\theta\in(0,\|M\|^{-2}_{BMO}),
for any stopping time
\tau\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{\theta(\langle M\rangle_{T}-\langle M\rangle_{\tau})\Big\}\Big|{\cal F}_{\tau}\Big]\leqslant\Big(1-\theta\|M\|_{BMO}^{2}\Big)^{-1},\ \text{a.s.}

\bullet
By
p^{*}_{M}
we denote the conjugate exponent of
p_{M}
, i.e.,

(p_{M})^{-1}+(p^{*}_{M})^{-1}=1
.

For any
p\geqslant 1
,
t\in[0,T]
and a filtration
\mathbb{F}
, we introduce some useful spaces:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})=\Big\{

\displaystyle\varphi:\Omega\times[t,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is
$\mathbb{F}$-adapted, continuous, }

5
Necessary and sufficient maximum principles

The necessary and sufficient maximum principles are discussed in this section.
For this, we introduce the adjoint equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp_{\theta}(t)&=\partial_{y}f_{\theta}(t)p_{\theta}(t)dt+\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(t)p_{\theta}(t)dW(t),\ \ \ t\in[0,T],\\
-dq_{\theta}(t)&=\Big(-\partial_{x}f_{\theta}(t)p_{\theta}(t)+(\partial_{x}b_{\theta}(t))^{\top}q_{\theta}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial^{i}_{x}\sigma_{\theta}(t))^{\top}r^{i}_{\theta}(t)-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x^{\prime}}f_{\theta}(t)p_{\theta}(t)]\\

p_{\theta}(0)&=-\partial_{y}\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0)),\\
q_{\theta}(T)&=-(\partial_{x}\Phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)]))^{\top}p_{\theta}(T)-\mathbb{E}\Big[\partial_{x^{\prime}}\Phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)]))^{\top}p_{\theta}(T)\Big]\\
&\quad+\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

The forward equation in (

The solvability of (

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi_{1}(t)|^{p}dt\Big]<\infty,\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi_{2}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty,

a_{1}\cdot W

1
-dimensional SDE with unbounded coefficient below

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)&=[a_{1}(t)X(t)+\varphi_{1}(t)]dt+[a_{2}(t)X(t)+\varphi_{2}(t)]dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
X(0)&=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

has a unique solution
X(\cdot)\in{\cal S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\tau_{0}=0,\quad\tau_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{t>\tau_{n}:\Big(\int_{\tau_{n}}^{t}|a_{2}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\geqslant 2^{-p-1}C_{p}^{-1}\quad\text{or}\ \int_{\tau_{n}}^{t}|a_{1}(s)|^{p}ds\geqslant 2^{-p-1}\bigg\}\wedge T,

The proof is split into two steps.

Step 1.
We show that the equation (

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Gamma(X)(t)&:=x_{0}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\Big[a_{1}(s)X(s)+\varphi_{1}(s)\Big]ds+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\Big[a_{2}(s)X(s)+\varphi_{2}(s)\Big]dW(s),\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}],\\

It is easy to check

For

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Gamma(\Delta X)(t)&=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}a_{1}(s)\Delta X(s)ds+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}a_{2}(s)\Delta X(s)dW(s),\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}],\\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}|\Gamma(\Delta X)(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{1}}|a_{1}(s)|^{p}ds\cdot\sup_{t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}|\Delta X(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{1}}|a_{2}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\cdot\sup_{t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]}|\Delta X(t)|^{p}\Big].

Recalling the definition of

By the fixed point theorem, it yields that SDE (

) has a unique solution

\displaystyle+\Big\{\mathbb{E}[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t)-\bar{Y}_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)|^{pq^{\prime}}]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\cdot K(pp^{\prime},\|\partial_{z}f_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}),

1<p<p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|I_{1}(t)|^{p}\Big]=0.

For
I_{2}(t)
, since

\Big[e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y}f_{\theta}(s)ds}-e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)ds}\Big]\leqslant e^{C_{1}T}\cdot\Big[e^{Td(\theta,\tilde{\theta})}-1\Big],

we know, for
1<p<p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|I_{2}(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant Le^{C_{1}T}\cdot\Big[e^{Td(\theta,\tilde{\theta})}-1\Big]\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}{\cal E}(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}dW(s))^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant Le^{C_{1}T}\cdot\Big[e^{Td(\theta,\tilde{\theta})}-1\Big]\cdot K(p,\|\partial_{z}f_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}).

Finally, we prove, for

1<p<(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|I_{3}(t)|^{p}\Big]=0.

In fact, due to
|\partial_{y}\gamma_{\tilde{\theta}}(\bar{Y}_{\tilde{\theta}}(0))\cdot e^{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)ds}|\leqslant C
, we only need to prove, for

1<p<(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|{\cal E}(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}dW(s))-{\cal E}(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)^{\top}dW(s))|^{p}\Big]=0.

For convenience, set
X_{\theta}(t)={\cal E}(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}dW(s))
, and
X_{\theta}
satisfies the following SDE

X_{\theta}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)X_{\theta}(s)dW(s),\ t\in[0,T].

Set
\bar{X}(s)=X_{\theta}(s)-X_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)
and
\varphi(s)=(\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)-\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(s))X_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)
. Then

\bar{X}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\bar{X}(s)+\varphi(s)\Big)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

Consider

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\Pi_{\theta}(t)&=(\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(t))^{2}\Pi_{\theta}(t)dt-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\Pi_{\theta}(0)&=1.\end{aligned}\right.

By
5.1
, the above equation has a unique strong solution
\Pi_{\theta}\in{\cal S}^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
for
p>1
.
Then it follows from Itô’s formula to
\bar{X}(s)\Pi_{\theta}(s)
on
[0,t]
that

\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

(5.4)

In addition, consider

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\pi_{\theta}(t)&=\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(t)\pi_{\theta}(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\pi_{\theta}(0)&=1.\end{aligned}\right.

By
5.1
again, the above equation also has a unique strong solution
\pi_{\theta}\in{\cal S}^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
for
p>1
.
Moreover, we know from Itô’s formula that
\Pi_{\theta}(t)\pi_{\theta}(t)=1,\ t\in[0,T]
. Hence,

\Pi_{\theta}(t)^{-1}=\pi_{\theta}(t)={\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r)^{\top}dW(r)\Big).

From Hölder inequality we can see, for

1<p<(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)|^{p}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)\pi_{\theta}(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{p}\cdot\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\pi_{\theta}(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{pq^{\prime}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\pi_{\theta}(t)|^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p+(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})}{2p}

and
q^{\prime}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}
.
Since
pp^{\prime}<p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}
,
it follows from Doob’s inequality and reverse Hölder inequality that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\pi_{\theta}(t)^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\pi_{\theta}(T)^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]\leqslant CK(pp^{\prime},\|\partial_{z}f_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}).

Consequently, by Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{pq^{\prime}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{p\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where
C
depends on
pp^{\prime}
and
\|\partial_{z}f_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}
.
Besides, from (
5.4
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)\Pi_{\theta}(t)|^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s))|^{pp^{\prime}}ds\Big]

(5.6)

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{t}\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s)dW(s)|^{pp^{\prime}}\Big],

where the constant
C>0
depends on
pp^{\prime}
.

Then we estimate the
ds
-term and the
dW
-term in the above inequality one by one. As for the
ds
-term, since

\left\{\begin{aligned} |\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)|&\leqslant C(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(s)|),\\
|\varphi(s)|&\leqslant d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\cdot{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)^{\top}dW(r)\Big),\\
\Pi_{\theta}(s)&=\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{s}(-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}|\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r)|^{2}dr\Big\},\end{aligned}\right.

(5.7)

by Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s))|^{pp^{\prime}}ds\Big]

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(s)|)\cdot{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r)^{\top}(\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r))dr}ds\Big)^{pp^{\prime}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)^{pp^{\prime}}\cdot

\displaystyle e^{Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr\Big)^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)^{pp^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime\prime}}}\cdot

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr\Big)^{pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime\prime}}},

where

p^{\prime\prime}=\frac{p^{\prime}+(p_{\partial_{z}f_{\theta}}\wedge p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})})}{2pp^{\prime}}

and
q^{\prime\prime}=\frac{p^{\prime\prime}}{p^{\prime\prime}-1}
.
Since
|\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)|\leqslant C(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(s)|+|\bar{Z}_{\tilde{\theta}}(s)|)
we know

(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})\cdot W\in BMO
.
Note that
1<p^{\prime}<pp^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}<p_{(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})}
. It follows from Doob’s inequality and
reverse Hölder inequality that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)^{pp^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\leqslant K(pp^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime},\|(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})\cdot W\|_{BMO}).

On the other hand, by Hölder inequality and
\bar{Z}_{\theta}\cdot W\in BMO
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr\Big)^{pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{2Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2q^{\prime\prime}}}\cdot\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|^{2})dr\Big)^{pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2q^{\prime\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{2Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2q^{\prime\prime}}}.

Without loss of generality, assume
d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})<1
. Consequently, it follows from John-Nirenberg inquality
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{2Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})pp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\Big]<\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)dr}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}(T+\frac{T}{\delta})}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\frac{\delta}{4}\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|^{2}dr}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}(T+\frac{T}{\delta})}\Big(1-2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\frac{\delta}{4}\|\bar{Z}_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}^{2}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}e^{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}(T+\frac{T}{\delta})},

where
\delta=\frac{\|\bar{Z}_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}^{-2}}{2Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}
.
Combining the above inequalities, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s))|^{pp^{\prime}}ds\Big]\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}.

(5.9)

Next, we analyse the
dW
-term. Similar to the
ds
-term, based on Buckholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, (
5.7
), Hölder inequality and John-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{t}\Pi_{\theta}(s)\varphi(s)dW(s)|^{pp^{\prime}}\Big]

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)^{pp^{\prime}}\cdot e^{Cpp^{\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})dr}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}{\cal E}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}(r)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(r))^{\top}dW(r)\Big)^{pp^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime\prime}}}\cdot

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{Cpp^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\bar{Z}_{\theta}(r)|)d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})dr}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{pp^{\prime}},

where
C
depends on
\|(\partial_{z}f_{\tilde{\theta}}-\partial_{z}f_{\theta})\cdot W\|_{BMO}\|
and
\partial_{z}f_{\theta}\cdot W\|_{BMO}
, and
p^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime}
are given in (
5.8
).

Finally, taking into account (
5.5
), (
5.6
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}(t)|^{p}\Big]\leqslant Cd(\theta,\tilde{\theta})^{p}.

The proof is complete.

Lemma 5.3
.

Notice that

\displaystyle q_{\theta}(t)-q_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)

\displaystyle I_{1,\theta,\tilde{\theta}}(s)

\displaystyle I_{2,\theta,\tilde{\theta}}(s)

Since

are bounded,
the backward equation in (

) is a mean-field BSDE with Lipschitz coefficient. By

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|q_{\theta}(t)-q_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|r_{\theta}(t)-r_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\bigg[|q_{\theta}(T)-q_{\tilde{\theta}}(T)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|I_{1,\theta,\tilde{\theta}}(t)+I_{2,\theta,\tilde{\theta}}(t)|dt\Big)^{p}\bigg].

Since
\partial_{x}\Phi_{\theta},\partial_{x^{\prime}}\Phi_{\theta},\partial_{x}b_{\theta},\partial_{x}\sigma_{\theta},\partial_{x^{\prime}}b_{\theta},\partial_{x}f_{\theta},\partial_{x^{\prime}}f_{\theta},\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}

Define the Hamiltonian: for
t\in[0,T],x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},p\in\mathbb{R},q\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},v\in V,

\displaystyle H_{\theta}(t,x,x^{\prime},y,z,v,p,q,r)

\displaystyle:=q^{\top}b_{\theta}(t,x,x^{\prime},v)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(r^{i})^{\top}\sigma_{\theta}^{i}(t,x,v)-pf_{\theta}(t,x,x^{\prime},y,z,v)

and its partial derivative with respect to

:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\theta}(t,\omega):=\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)).

Lemma 5.4
.

Under
1
and
2
, the above
\Lambda
is an
{\cal F}
-progressively measurable
process, i.e., for each
t\in[0,T]
, the function
\Lambda_{\theta}(t,\omega):\Theta\times[0,t]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\mathscr{B}(\Theta)\times\mathscr{B}([0,t])\times{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable.

Proof.

Since
\Theta
is a Polish space, for each
M>1
, there exists a compact subset
C^{M}\subset\Theta
such that

\bar{Q}(\theta\notin C^{M})\leqslant\frac{1}{M}
. Then we can find a subsequence of open neighborhoods
\Big(B(\theta_{l},\frac{1}{2M})\Big)_{l=1}^{L_{M}}
such that
C^{M}\subset\bigcup_{l=1}^{L_{M}}\Big(B(\theta_{l},\frac{1}{2M})\Big)
.
By the locally compact property of
\Theta
and using partitions of unity, there exists a sequence of continuous functions

h_{l}:\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with values in
[0,1]
such that

h_{l}(\theta)=0,\ \text{if}\ \theta\notin B\Big(\theta_{l},\frac{1}{2M}\Big),\ l=1,\cdots,L_{M},\ \text{and}\ \sum_{l=1}^{L_{M}}h_{l}(\theta)=1,\ \text{if}\ \theta\in C^{M}.

We choose some
\theta_{l}^{*}
satisfying
h_{l}(\theta_{l}^{*})>0
and define

\Lambda^{M}_{\theta}(t):=\sum_{l=1}^{L_{M}}\Lambda_{\theta_{l}^{*}}(t)h_{l}(\theta)\mathbf{1}_{\{\theta\in C^{M}\}}.

Notice that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt\Big]\leqslant L.
It yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\Lambda_{\theta}^{M}(t)-\Lambda_{\theta}(t)\Big|dt\Big]\bar{Q}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Theta}\sum_{l=1}^{L_{M}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\theta}^{M}(t)-\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt\bigg]h_{l}(\theta)\mathbf{1}_{\{\theta\in C^{M}\}}+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt\bigg]h_{l}(\theta)\mathbf{1}_{\{\theta\notin C^{M}\}}\bar{Q}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\frac{1}{M}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)-\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt\bigg]+L\bar{Q}(\theta\notin C^{M})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\frac{1}{M}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)-\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt\bigg]+\frac{L}{M}.

Here we use the fact that
h_{l}(\theta)=0
whenever
d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\geqslant\frac{1}{2N}
.

Then we prove

\lim\limits_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{d(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\leqslant\frac{1}{M}}\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|\Lambda_{\tilde{\theta}}(t)-\Lambda_{\theta}(t)|dt]=0.

Recall that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\theta}(t,\omega)

\displaystyle=\partial_{v}b_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{v}(t))q_{\theta}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{v}\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t))r^{i}_{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle-\partial_{v}f_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t))p_{\theta}(t).

Since
\partial_{v}b_{\theta},\partial_{v}\sigma^{i}_{\theta},\partial_{v}f_{\theta}
are bounded and Lipschitz continuous in
\theta
, the desired result comes from
5.2
and
5.3
.

Now we are ready to get the necessary condition of the optimal control.

Theorem 5.5
.

(Stochastic Maximum Principle)
Let
\bar{v}(\cdot)
be an optimal control to the problem (
3.3
),
(\bar{X}_{\theta}(\cdot),\bar{Y}_{\theta}(\cdot),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(\cdot))
be the solution to the state equation (
1.1
) with
\bar{v}(\cdot)
,
and
(p_{\theta}(\cdot),q_{\theta}(\cdot),r_{\theta}(\cdot))
be the solution to the adjoint equation (
5.1
) with
\bar{v}(\cdot)
. Under
1
and
2
, there exists a
probability measure
\bar{Q}\in{\cal Q}^{\bar{v}}
such that, for
v\in V
,
dt\times d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v-\bar{v}(t)\rangle\bar{Q}(d\theta)\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Applying Itô’s formula to
(X^{1}_{\theta}(\cdot))^{\top}q_{\theta}(\cdot)+Y^{1}_{\theta}(\cdot)p_{\theta}(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[(\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)))^{\top}X_{\theta}^{1}(T)+\partial_{y}\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0))Y_{\theta}^{1}(0)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle(\partial_{v}b_{\theta}(t))^{\top}q_{\theta}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{v}\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(t))^{\top}r^{i}_{\theta}(t)-\partial_{v}f_{\theta}(t)p_{\theta}(t),v(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle dt\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle dt\Big].

Thanks to the above equality and
Theorem 4.9
, there exists some probability
\bar{Q}\in{\cal Q}^{\bar{v}}
such that, for all
v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}_{ad}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle dt\Big]\bar{Q}(d\theta)\geqslant 0.

Thanks to
5.4
,
the process
\Lambda_{\theta}(\cdot)
is
{\cal F}
-progressively measurable.
Then it follows from Fubini’s Theorem that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Theta}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle\bar{Q}(d\theta)dt\Big]\geqslant 0,

which implies
for
v\in V
,
dt\times d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle\bar{Q}(d\theta)\geqslant 0.

∎

Then we turn to the sufficient condition of the optimal control.

Theorem 5.6
.

Under the assumptions and the settings in Theorem
5.5
, if

H_{\theta}
is convex in
(x,x^{\prime},y,z,v)
and continuous in
t
,
\phi_{\theta}
is convex in
x
,
\gamma_{\theta}
is
convex in
y
and

\Phi_{\theta}
is concave in
(x,x^{\prime})
,
and there exists an admissible control
\bar{v}(\cdot)
and a
probability measure
\bar{Q}\in{\cal Q}^{\bar{v}}
such that for
v\in V
,
dt\times d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v-\bar{v}(t)\rangle\bar{Q}(d\theta)\geqslant 0,

\bar{v}(\cdot)
is an optimal control.

Proof.

For
\theta\in\Theta
and
v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}_{ad}
, by
(X^{v}_{\theta},Y^{v}_{\theta},Z^{v}_{\theta})
we denote the solution to the
equation (
1.1
). Set

\displaystyle(\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda},\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda},\zeta_{\theta}^{\lambda})=(X_{\theta}^{\lambda}-\bar{X}_{\theta},Y_{\theta}^{\lambda}-\bar{Y}_{\theta},Z_{\theta}^{\lambda}-\bar{Z}_{\theta}).

Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(t)&=\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}b_{\theta}(s)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)+\partial_{x^{\prime}}b_{\theta}(s)\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)]+S_{\theta}(s))ds\\
&\ +\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(s)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)+J^{i}_{\theta}(s))dW^{i}(s),\\
\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(t)&=K_{\theta}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}(\partial_{x}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)+\partial_{x^{\prime}}f_{\theta}(s)^{\top}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)]+\partial_{y}f_{\theta}(s)\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)\\
&\quad+\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\zeta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)+L_{\theta}(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\zeta_{\theta}(s)dW(s),\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle S_{\theta}(s)

\displaystyle=b_{\theta}(s,X_{\theta}^{v}(s),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(s)],v(s))-b_{\theta}(s)-\partial_{x}b_{\theta}(s)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)-\partial_{x^{\prime}}b_{\theta}(s)\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)],

\displaystyle J^{i}_{\theta}(s)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(s,X_{\theta}^{v}(s),v(s))-\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(s)-\partial_{x}\sigma^{i}_{\theta}(s)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s),

\displaystyle K_{\theta}(T)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\theta}(X_{\theta}^{v}(T),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(T)])-\Phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)]),

\displaystyle L_{\theta}(s)

\displaystyle=f_{\theta}(s,X_{\theta}^{v}(s),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(s)],Y_{\theta}^{v}(s),Z_{\theta}^{v}(s),v(s))-f_{\theta}(s)-\partial_{x}f_{\theta}(s)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)

\displaystyle-\partial_{x^{\prime}}f_{\theta}(s)\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)]-\partial_{y}f_{\theta}(s)\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s)-\partial_{z}f_{\theta}(s)\zeta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(s).

Applying Ito’s formula to
\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(\cdot)^{\top}q_{\theta}(\cdot)+\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(\cdot)p_{\theta}(\cdot)
and then integrating from
0
to
T
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T))^{\top}\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T)+\partial_{y}\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0))\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(0)]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\Big[p_{\theta}(T)(\Phi_{\theta}(X_{\theta}^{v}(T),\mathbb{E}[X_{\theta}^{v}(T)])-\Phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(T)]))

\displaystyle-p_{\theta}(T)\partial_{x}\Phi(T)\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T)-\mathbb{E}[p_{\theta}(T)\partial_{x^{\prime}}\Phi(T)]\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T)\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\Big(q_{\theta}(t)S_{\theta}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}r^{i}_{\theta}(t)J^{i}_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta}(t)L_{\theta}(t)\Big)dt\Big].

Since
\Phi_{\theta}
is concave in
(x,x^{\prime})
and the Hamiltonian
H_{\theta}
is convex in
(x,x^{\prime},y,z,v)
, it yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T))^{\top}\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T)+\partial_{y}\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0))\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(0)]

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle dt\Big].

Finally, recall the fact that
\bar{Q}\in{\cal Q}^{\bar{v}}
and
\phi_{\theta},\gamma_{\theta}
are convex in
x,y
, respectively,
it follows from Sion’s minimax theorem that

\displaystyle J(v^{\lambda}(\cdot))-J(v(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi_{\theta}(X_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T))-\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T))+\gamma_{\theta}(Y^{\lambda}_{\theta}(0))-\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0))\Big]\bar{Q}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta}(\bar{X}_{\theta}(T))^{\top}\alpha_{\theta}^{\lambda}(T)+\partial_{y}\gamma_{\theta}(\bar{Y}_{\theta}(0))\beta_{\theta}^{\lambda}(0)]\bar{Q}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle dt\Big]\bar{Q}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Theta}\langle\partial_{v}H_{\theta}(t,\bar{X}_{\theta}(t),\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{\theta}(t)],\bar{Y}_{\theta}(t),\bar{Z}_{\theta}(t),\bar{v}(t),p_{\theta}(t),q_{\theta}(t),r_{\theta}(t)),v^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{v}(t)\rangle\bar{Q}(d\theta)dt\Big]\geqslant 0.

The proof is complete.

∎

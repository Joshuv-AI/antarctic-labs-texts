Hybrid rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) lattice gauge theory, the F3 model, and its effective field theory
Jintae Kim, Yun-Tak Oh, Jung Hoon Han

Abstract
A number of exactly solvable spin models, including the Kitaev toric code in two and three dimensions and the X-cube model in three dimensions, can be related to their respective parent lattice gauge theories (LGT) through the mathematical process of ‘Higgsing’. Field theories of the low-energy excitations of these spin models can be developed subsequently, building upon the symmetry of the parent LGTs. Recently, two of the present authors proposed a variant of the three-dimensional toric code which we now call the F3 model, whose elementary excitations consist of freeon and fluxon excitations of the three-dimensional toric code and fracton excitations of the X-cube model. In this work, we identify the parent LGT of the F3 model as the hybrid rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGT, and develop the corresponding field theory. The resulting Lagrangian of the F3 model is that of a three-dimensional toric code with an extra term, which ties the dynamics of fractons to that of fluxons. The matter part of the effective action for the F3 model can be derived as well, by carefully keeping track of the gauge invariance of the F3 model. Hydrodynamic equations of motion of the quasiparticles are derived, which properly reflect their constrained dynamics. Finally, we present a tight-binding model for the quasiparticle motion in the F3 model.



I
Introduction

Exactly solvable spin Hamiltonians such as toric codes (TC) in two and three dimensions
Kitaev 2003
;
von Keyserlingk et al. 2013
;
Freedman and Meyer 2001
and the X-cube model in three dimensions
Vijay et al. 2016
;
Slagle and Kim 2017
;
Nandkishore and Hermele 2019
have been studied extensively. The multi-spin interactions that define various exactly solvable spin models seem at first novel, but in fact have well-defined roots in their corresponding parent lattice gauge theories (LGT)
Ma et al. 2018
;
Bulmash and Barkeshli 2018
;
Tagliacozzo et al. 2013
. The mutually commuting operators in the LGT - also known as generators - become, through Higgsing, the stabilizer operators in the spin Hamiltonian. Various rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGTs and their Higgsed spin models have been analyzed, and it was shown that the parent theory of the TC is the rank-1 U(1) LGT, while that of the X-cube model is the rank-2 U(1) LGT
Bulmash and Barkeshli 2018
. The elementary excitations of the toric code are the electric and magnetic quasiparticles exhibiting mutual anyonic statistics, while those of the X-cube model are the fractons and lineons. Fractons are immobile
but
deconfined in the sense that a single fracton cannot move without extra energy cost, but a four-fracton cluster can be expanded arbitrarily without extra energy. Lineons are subdimensional quasiparticles that can only move freely along a specific axis and are also deconfined
Vijay et al. 2016
.

In an attempt to extend the realm of exactly solvable spin models, two of the present authors proposed a model
Kim and Han 2021
exhibiting features akin to those of the three-dimensional toric code (3DTC) as well as the X-cube model. The new model is obtained from the 3DTC by including an additional twelve-spin interaction which, in the context of the X-cube model, acts as a fracton operator. Excitations in this new model consist, at first sight, of the two kinds of elementary excitations of the 3DTC, augmented by the fracton excitation similar to that in the X-cube model. In a previous publication
Kim and Han 2021
these excitations were dubbed freeons, fluxons, and fractons, respectively, and hence we will call the overall model ‘F3 model’. It was also shown
Kim and Han 2021
that the model is exactly solvable only for the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
Hilbert space at the links of the lattice, and the ground state degeneracy (GSD) of the model is
3^{3}
, same as the GSD of the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
3DTC. In other words, the F3 model remains in the same phase as the 3DTC despite the addition of the local fracton operators. A physical way to understand this is that fractons that are deconfined in the X-cube model become confined in the F3 model because fluxons act as ‘gluons’ between fractons with energy that increases linearly with separation. The confinement of fractons in the F3 model is an indication that the low-energy field theory of the F3 model is more likely to resemble that of 3DTC than that of the X-cube model. We will show that this is indeed the case.

Neither the parent LGT of the F3 model nor its low-energy field theory was properly understood in the previous work
Kim and Han 2021
. We show in this paper that the F3 model is the descendant of the hybrid rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGTs. The field theory of the F3 model is constructed accordingly. In Sec.
II
we review the rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGTs, their descendant exactly solvable spin models, and the corresponding effective field theories (EFTs). Although the discussions in this section overlap with several works in the past, we find it useful to collect known results and present them in a unified fashion, thus paving the way for the development of EFT of the F3 model. Some new insights as well as new results about the toric code and X-cube model in three dimensions are obtained. In Sec.
III
we adopt the strategies developed in Sec.
II
and apply them to the F3 model. The parent LGT of the F3 model is properly identified and is shown to lead to the F3 stabilizer Hamiltonian upon Higgsing. The gauge transformation properties of the various gauge fields are clarified and subsequently exploited to derive the field theories of the matter fields and the gauge fields pertinent to the F3 model. Section
IV
gives the summary. Several technical themes are delegated to the appendices.

II
Review of Rank-1 and Rank-2 U(1) LGT and their descendants

We review the rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGT, their descendant exactly solvable spin models, and the corresponding EFTs.

II.1
Rank-1 U(1) LGT and its descendants

The rank-1 U(1) LGT has the vector gauge fields

\displaystyle\sim A_{i}^{a}+\partial_{a}f_{i}

\displaystyle E_{i}^{a}\rightarrow U_{E}^{\dagger}E_{i}^{a}U_{E}

\displaystyle=E_{i}^{a}+\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}(g_{i}^{c}-g_{i-\hat{b}}^{c})

\displaystyle\sim E_{i}^{a}+\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{b}g_{i}^{c},

\displaystyle U_{A}=e^{i\sum_{j}f_{j}({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}})_{j}},~~~U_{E}=e^{i\sum_{j}{\bm{g}}_{j}\cdot{\bm{B}}_{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a}(E_{i}^{a}-E_{i-\hat{a}}^{a})\sim\sum_{a}\partial_{a}E_{i}^{a}

\displaystyle B_{i}^{a}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}(A_{i}^{b}-A_{i+\hat{c}}^{b})\sim\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{b}A_{i}^{c}.

(3)

Importantly, we have the commutativity of the two generators

\displaystyle\left[\left({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}}\right)_{i},B_{j}^{a}\right]=0,

\displaystyle X_{a,i}

\displaystyle=\exp(-2\pi iE_{i}^{a}/p),

\displaystyle Z_{a,i}

\displaystyle H_{\rm 3DTC}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i}^{x}+\mathcal{C}_{i}^{y}+\mathcal{C}_{i}^{z}),

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(a_{i})^{j}~~~~~~~\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(c_{i}^{a})^{j}

\displaystyle a_{i}

\displaystyle=X_{1,i}X_{1,i-\hat{x}}^{-1}X_{2,i}X_{2,i-\hat{y}}^{-1}X_{3,i}X_{3,i-\hat{z}}^{-1}

\displaystyle c_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle=Z_{2,i}Z_{3,i+\hat{y}}Z^{-1}_{2,i+\hat{z}}Z^{-1}_{3,i}

\displaystyle c_{i}^{y}

\displaystyle=Z_{3,i}Z_{1,i+\hat{z}}Z^{-1}_{3,i+\hat{x}}Z^{-1}_{1,i}

\displaystyle c_{i}^{z}

The EFT for 3DTC is the well-known BF theory, but the treatment of the EFT for the matter fields, i.e., electric and magnetic excitations, is not given with equal care in the literature. Here we derive the EFTs of both matter and gauge fields for the 3DTC and derive the corresponding continuity equations. Furthermore, a tight-binding realization of the
e
-particle and
m

The effective action of the
e
quasiparticles is the usual one,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{e}

\displaystyle=i\psi_{e}^{\dagger}\partial_{t}\psi_{e}-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}|{\bm{D}}_{e}\psi_{e}|^{2},

(9)

with
{\bm{D}}_{e}\psi_{e}=({\bm{\nabla}}-i{\bm{A}})\psi_{e}
. Its form is dictated by the requirement of invariance under the gauge transformation

and
{\bm{A}}\rightarrow{\bm{A}}+{\bm{\nabla}}f
. The mass

is introduced by hand. The EFT of the

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m}

\displaystyle=i{\bm{\psi}}_{m}^{\dagger}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bm{\psi}}_{m}-\frac{1}{2m_{m}}\sum_{a<b}|{\bm{D}}_{m}^{ab}\psi_{m}^{a}\psi_{m}^{b}|^{2},

(10)

with
{\bm{\psi}}_{m}=(\psi_{m}^{x},\psi_{m}^{y},\psi_{m}^{z})
representing the three species of magnetic quasiparticles. The covariant derivative

a,b

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{m}^{ab}\psi_{m}^{a}\psi_{m}^{b}

\displaystyle=\psi_{m}^{a}\partial_{a}\psi_{m}^{b}-\psi_{m}^{b}\partial_{b}\psi_{m}^{a}

\displaystyle-i\sum_{c}\epsilon_{abc}E^{c}\psi_{m}^{a}\psi_{m}^{b}.

(11)

The gauge invariance of the action under
\psi_{m}^{a}\rightarrow e^{ig^{a}}\psi_{m}^{a}
and
E^{a}\rightarrow E^{a}+\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{b}g^{c}
can be readily checked. The action is quartic in the fields and implies that the quasiparticles are inherently interacting. It is well-known that magnetic excitations in the 3DTC always form loops

, which one can now understand as a consequence of the strongly interacting nature of the magnetic quasiparticles.

Some conservation laws of electric and magnetic quasiparticles follow from the matter action:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{e}+{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{j}}^{e}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{m_{a}}+\sum_{b}\partial_{b}[j^{m}]^{ab}=0.

(12)

Explicit expressions of the charge and current densities in terms of the matter fields can be found in the Appendix
A
. The electric part of the conservation law is nothing out of the ordinary. For the magnetic part, a vector of charges
\bm{\rho}^{m}=(\rho^{m_{x}},\rho^{m_{y}},\rho^{m_{z}})
and the corresponding antisymmetric tensor current

give rise to an unusual form of continuity equation. In fact, one can write the current tensor in a vector form
{\bm{j}}=([j^{m}]^{yz},[j^{m}]^{zx},[j^{m}]^{xy})
, introduce the vector charge density
{\bm{\rho}}^{m}=(\rho^{m_{x}},\rho^{m_{y}},\rho^{m_{z}})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bm{\rho}}^{m}+{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{j}}=0.

(13)

\partial_{t}({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}^{m})=0

\displaystyle[A^{a}({\bm{r}}),

\displaystyle E^{bc}({\bm{r}^{\prime}})]=\frac{2\pi i}{p}\epsilon_{abc}\delta^{3}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{A}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})U_{A}={\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})+{\bm{\nabla}}f({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle E^{ab}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{E}^{\dagger}E^{ab}({\bm{r}})U_{E}=E^{ab}({\bm{r}})-\partial_{a}g^{b}({\bm{r}})+\partial_{b}g^{a}({\bm{r}}),

II.2
Rank-2 U(1) LGT and its descendants

(0,-\rho^{x},\rho^{x})
,
(\rho^{y},0,-\rho^{y})
, and
(-\rho^{z},\rho^{z},0)
of the
l_{a}
-quasiparticles. In other words, lineons are composite excitations consisting of a pair of
l_{a}
-quasiparticles. Hence the
l_{a}
-quasiparticles are the more fundamental excitations and we proceed to construct field theories for them, with the lineon dynamics emerging as a natural by-product.

The matter field Lagrangian of fractons and
l_{a}
-quasiparticles can be derived as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}

\displaystyle=i\psi_{f}^{\dagger}\partial_{t}\psi_{f}-\frac{1}{2m_{f}}\sum_{a<b}|{\bm{D}}_{f}^{ab}\psi_{f}|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{l}

\displaystyle=i{\bm{\psi}}_{l}^{\dagger}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bm{\psi}}_{l}-\frac{1}{2m_{m}}\sum_{a}|{\bm{D}}_{l}^{a}{\bm{\psi}}_{l}|^{2},

(31)

where
\psi^{f}
and
{\bm{\psi}}_{l}=(\psi_{l}^{x},\psi_{l}^{y},\psi_{l}^{z})
represent the fracton and the three species of
l_{a}
-quasiparticle fields, respectively. The covariant derivatives are given as

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{f}^{ab}\psi_{f}

\displaystyle=\psi_{f}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\psi_{f}-\partial_{a}\psi_{f}\partial_{b}\psi_{f}+i\sum_{c}|\epsilon_{abc}|A^{c}\psi_{f}

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{l}^{x}{\bm{\psi}}_{l}

\displaystyle=\psi_{l}^{z}\partial_{x}\psi_{l}^{y}-\psi_{l}^{y}\partial_{x}\psi_{l}^{z}+iE^{x}\psi_{l}^{y}\psi_{l}^{z}

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{l}^{y}{\bm{\psi}}_{l}

\displaystyle=\psi_{l}^{z}\partial_{y}\psi_{l}^{x}-\psi_{l}^{x}\partial_{y}\psi_{l}^{z}+iE^{y}\psi_{l}^{x}\psi_{l}^{z}

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{l}^{z}{\bm{\psi}}_{l}

\displaystyle=\psi_{l}^{x}\partial_{z}\psi_{l}^{y}-\psi_{l}^{y}\partial_{z}\psi_{l}^{x}+iE^{z}\psi_{l}^{x}\psi_{l}^{y}.

(32)

One can readily check their gauge covariance under
\psi_{f}\rightarrow e^{if}\psi_{f}
,
\psi_{l}^{a}\rightarrow e^{ig^{a}}\psi_{l}^{a}
, as well as
A^{ab}\rightarrow A^{ab}-\partial_{a}\partial_{b}f
and
E^{ab}\rightarrow E^{ab}+\sum_{c}\epsilon_{abc}(\partial_{c}g^{b}-\partial_{c}g^{a})
. Various covariant derivatives given here, while straightforward to show, were not derived previously.

The continuity equations of
l_{a}
-quasiparticles and fractons follow from the matter Lagrangians (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{f}-\sum_{a<b}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}[j^{f}]^{ab}=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{l_{a}}+\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{c}[j^{l}]^{c}=0.

(33)

Here
\rho^{f}
and
\rho^{l_{a}}
are respectively the densities of fractons and
l_{a}
-quasiparticles, and
[j^{f}]^{ab}
and
\bm{j}^{l}
, respectively a symmetric tensor and a vector, represent the current density of the fracton and
l_{a}
-quasiparticles. These conservation laws, first mentioned in Ref.
Slagle and Kim 2017
, are rigorously derived from the matter field Lagrangian in Eq. (
31
). Charge and current density expressions in terms of matter fields are written in Appendix
B
.

The EFT of the XC model can be constructed by incorporating the conservation laws of quasiparticles by means of Lagrange multipliers:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{XC}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2\pi}\sum_{abc}|\epsilon_{abc}|\frac{1}{2}A^{ab}\partial_{t}E^{c}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}A^{ab}[j^{f}]^{ab}-{\bm{E}}\cdot\bm{j}^{l}

\displaystyle+\frac{p}{2\pi}E^{a0}\Bigl(\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{c}A^{ab}-\frac{2\pi}{p}{\rho}^{l_{a}}\Bigl)

\displaystyle+A^{0}\Bigl(\frac{p}{2\pi}\sum_{abc}|\epsilon_{abc}|\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}E^{c}-\rho^{f}\Bigl).

(34)

The sum is over spatial indices. The commutation relation and the gauge transformation of the
A^{ab}
and
E^{a}
in Eq. (

) are

\displaystyle[E^{a}(\bm{r}),A^{bc}(\bm{r}^{\prime})]=|\epsilon_{abc}|\frac{2\pi i}{p}\delta^{3}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle A^{ab}(\bm{r})\rightarrow A^{ab}(\bm{r})+\partial_{a}\partial_{b}f

\displaystyle E^{a}(\bm{r})\rightarrow E^{a}(\bm{r})-\epsilon_{abc}\partial_{a}g^{c},

(35)

We have given the derivation of the Lagrangian (
34
) starting from the matter Lagrangian and the subsequent conservation laws in Appendix
B
. The Lagrangian appears identical to the one given in Ref.
Slagle and Kim 2017
, but there is a subtle difference in the way the constraints are enforced. In order to properly embed the conservation laws of
l_{a}
-quasiparticles and fractons as well as the identity
\sum_{a}\rho^{l_{a}}=0
in the Lagrangian, the gauge transformations of
A^{0}
and
E^{a0}
must be

\displaystyle A^{0}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{0}(\bm{r})+\partial_{t}f(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptE^{a0}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\rightarrow E^{a0}(\bm{r})+\partial_{t}g^{a}(\bm{r})+g^{0}(\bm{r}).

(36)

Conservation laws of
l_{a}
-quasiparticles and fractons follow from requiring gauge invariance of the Lagrangian. In particular, the gauge transformation involving
g^{0}
gives the term
g^{0}\sum_{a}\rho^{l_{a}}
, which leads to the constraint
\sum_{a}\rho^{l_{a}}=0
.

The lattice model for fracton fields
\psi^{f}_{i}
can be constructed from the gauge principle. Given that fracton fields couple to
{\bm{A}}
which transforms as
A_{i}^{ab}\rightarrow A^{ab}_{i}-f_{i-\hat{a}-\hat{b}}+f_{i-\hat{a}}+f_{i-\hat{b}}-f_{i}

)], we write down the gauge-invariant hopping of the fractons as

\displaystyle(\psi^{f})^{\dagger}_{i-\hat{b}}(\psi^{f})^{\dagger}_{i-\hat{a}}\psi^{f}_{i-\hat{a}-\hat{b}}\psi^{f}_{i}e^{iA^{ab}_{i}}

(37)

and its Hermitian conjugate. The fracton motion clearly preserves the center-of-mass, or the dipole moment. Note that this is a three-dimensional extension of the quadrupole model studied in the context of quadrupole topological insulators
Dubinkin et al. 2020
;
You et al. 2021
.

The
l_{a}
-quasiparticles coupled to the

\displaystyle(\psi^{l_{x}})_{i}^{\dagger}(\psi^{l_{y}})_{i+\hat{z}}^{\dagger}\psi^{l_{x}}_{i+\hat{z}}\psi^{l_{y}}_{i}e^{iE^{xy}_{i}},

\displaystyle(\psi^{l_{y}})_{i}^{\dagger}(\psi^{l_{z}})_{i+\hat{x}}^{\dagger}\psi^{l_{y}}_{i+\hat{x}}\psi^{l_{z}}_{i}e^{iE^{yz}_{i}},

\displaystyle(\psi^{l_{z}})_{i}^{\dagger}(\psi^{l_{x}})_{i+\hat{y}}^{\dagger}\psi^{l_{z}}_{i+\hat{y}}\psi^{l_{x}}_{i}e^{iE^{zx}_{i}}

and their Hermitian conjugates. The first line, for instance, clearly shows that a
z
-lineon motion is made by the coordinated motion of the
l_{x}
and
l_{y}
quasiparticles in the opposite directions along the

The lattice formulation of the fracton and lineon dynamics coupled to the rank-2 gauge fields was not discussed previously, while their derivation is quite straightforward from the gauge principle as shown above.

III
Hybrid rank-1 and rank-2 LGT and F3 model as its descendant

We finally come to the discussion of the F3 model that we proposed in an earlier publication. We first lay out the underlying LGT of the F3 model and its Higgsing, and then construct the relevant effective field theory.

III.1
F3 model from Higgsing the hybrid rank-1 and rank-2 LGT

There are two types of terms in the X-cube model, and we ask if any of them can be added onto the 3DTC without violating the exact solvability. One sees readily that the addition of the magnetic field terms
\mathsf{a}_{i}^{a}
from the X-cube model [Eq. (
28
)] to the 3DTC poses a problem because they in general fail to commute with the magnetic flux operators
c_{i}^{a}
in the 3DTC:
[\mathsf{a}_{i}^{a},c_{j}^{a^{\prime}}]\neq 0
.

The Gauss’s law operator
{\cal B}_{i}
of the X-cube model obviously commutes with the magnetic fields
{\cal C}_{i}^{a}
of the 3DTC as they are built out of the same
Z
operators. On the other hand, the
{\cal B}_{i}
’s commute with the Gauss’s law operator
{\cal A}_{i}
of the 3DTC
only if
the local Hilbert space dimension is
p=3
since commuting the two operators results in the phase
\omega^{3}
, which equals one only for
p=3
or its multiple.
This is the
compatibility condition
between the Gauss’s laws of the rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGTs. When the compatibility condition is fulfilled, we obtain a new, exactly solvable spin Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\text{F3}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}-\sum_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i}-\sum_{i,a}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a},

(39)

which is the F3 model
Kim and Han 2021
. This model is the sum of the 3DTC (
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}
) and the Gauss’s law
\mathcal{B}_{i}
of the X-cube model. The quasiparticle excitations associated with
\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}^{a}_{i}
were respectively dubbed the freeon, fracton, and the fluxon, hence the F3 model. One can think of F3 as the 3DTC modified by the presence of the cube term
-\sum_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i}
. Despite the additional term, the logical operators of the F3 model are identical to those of the 3DTC and consequently they share the same GSD. This observation will permeate the field-theoretic discussion that follows as well.

We can derive the F3 model by Higgsing the gauge theory of two vector gauge fields
\bm{A}_{i}
and
\bm{E}_{i}
which satisfy the commutation relation
[A_{i}^{a},E_{j}^{b}]=i\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}
.
One can identify the three “mutually commuting” generators,
(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{A})_{i}
,
(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E})_{i}
, and
({\bf DE})_{i}
, given by

\displaystyle({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a}(A_{i}^{a}-A_{i-\hat{a}}^{a})

\displaystyle(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E})_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{b,c}\epsilon_{abc}(E_{i}^{b}-E_{i+\hat{c}}^{b})

\displaystyle({\bf DE})_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv E_{i+\hat{x}+\hat{y}}^{z}-E_{i+\hat{x}}^{z}-E_{i+\hat{y}}^{z}+E_{i}^{z}

\displaystyle~~~~+E_{i+\hat{y}+\hat{z}}^{x}-E_{i+\hat{y}}^{x}-E_{i+\hat{z}}^{x}+E_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle~~~~+E_{i+\hat{x}+\hat{z}}^{y}-E_{i+\hat{x}}^{y}-E_{i+\hat{z}}^{y}+E_{i}^{y}.

(40)

One can recognize the first two generators as those of the rank-1 LGT in Eq. (
3
), except for the reversal of the roles
{\bm{A}}\leftrightarrow{\bm{E}}
. The rank-2 U(1) generator
({\bf D}{\bf E})_{i}
is taken from Eq. (
23
). In fact, we do not have strict commutativity of all three operators since the explicit calculation shows

\displaystyle[({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i},({\bf DE})_{j}]

\displaystyle=3i(\delta_{ij}+\delta_{i,j+\hat{x}+\hat{y}}+\delta_{i,j+\hat{x}+\hat{z}}

\displaystyle~~~+\delta_{i,j+\hat{y}+\hat{z}}-\delta_{i,j+\hat{x}}-\delta_{i,j+\hat{y}}

\displaystyle~~~-\delta_{i,j+\hat{z}}-\delta_{i,j+\hat{x}+\hat{y}+\hat{z}}).

(41)

Nevertheless, we can show that the commutativity is recovered after the Higgsing, thus justifying the use of “mutually commuting” operators in parenthesis.

The Higgsing formula is given by

\displaystyle X_{a,i}

\displaystyle=\exp(iA_{i}^{a}),

\displaystyle Z_{a,i}

\displaystyle=\exp(2\pi iE_{i}^{a}/p).

(42)

One can easily identify

\displaystyle\exp((\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{A})_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv a_{i}

\displaystyle\exp(2\pi i({\bf DE})_{i}/p)

\displaystyle\equiv b_{i}

\displaystyle\exp(2\pi i(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E})^{a}_{i}/p)

\displaystyle\equiv c_{i}^{a}

(43)

as the
a_{i},~b_{i},~c_{i}^{a}
operators of the F3 model. By virtue of Eq. (
41
), we see that the commutator
[({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i},({\bf DE})_{j}]
equals
+3i,-3i
or 0, and therefore
[a_{i},b_{j}]=0
provided
p=3
. That is, although the generators themselves do not commute, their Higgsed versions do commute in the special case of
p=3
.

The gauge transformation rules of
\bm{A}_{i}
and
\bm{E}_{i}
are

\displaystyle A^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{A}^{\dagger}A_{i}^{a}U_{A}

\displaystyle A^{x}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{x}_{i}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{y}-\hat{z}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{y}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{z}}-f^{0}_{i}

\displaystyle~~~-f_{i}^{z}+f_{i-\hat{y}}^{z}+f_{i}^{y}-f_{i-\hat{z}}^{y}

\displaystyle\sim A_{i}^{x}-\partial_{y}\partial_{z}f_{i}^{0}-\partial_{y}f_{i}^{z}+\partial_{z}f_{i}^{y}

\displaystyle A^{y}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{y}_{i}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}-\hat{z}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{z}}-f^{0}_{i}

\displaystyle~~~-f_{i}^{x}+f_{i-\hat{z}}^{x}+f_{i}^{z}-f_{i-\hat{x}}^{z}

\displaystyle\sim A_{i}^{y}-\partial_{z}\partial_{x}f_{i}^{0}-\partial_{z}f_{i}^{x}+\partial_{x}f_{i}^{z}

\displaystyle A^{z}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{z}_{i}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}-\hat{y}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{y}}-f^{0}_{i}

\displaystyle~~~-f_{i}^{y}+f_{i-\hat{x}}^{y}+f_{i}^{x}-f_{i-\hat{y}}^{x}

\displaystyle\sim A_{i}^{z}-\partial_{x}\partial_{y}f_{i}^{0}-\partial_{x}f_{i}^{y}+\partial_{y}f_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle E_{i}^{a}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{E}^{\dagger}E_{i}^{a}U_{E}=E_{i}^{a}-g_{i+\hat{a}}+g_{i}

\displaystyle\sim E_{i}^{a}-\partial_{a}g_{i}.

(44)

The two unitary operators are

\displaystyle U_{A}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(i\sum_{j}(f^{0}_{j}({\bf DE})_{j}+{\bm{f}}_{j}\cdot(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E})_{j})\right)

\displaystyle U_{E}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(i\sum_{j}g_{j}({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{j}\right),

(45)

for
\bm{f}=(f^{x},f^{y},f^{z})
. (When
f^{0}_{j}=0
, these unitary operators are those of the 3DTC with the roles of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{E}
interchanged.) Since the generators do not commute,
[({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i},({\bf DE})_{j}]\neq 0
, neither do the unitary operators
U_{A}U_{E}\neq U_{E}U_{A}
. An explicit calculation shows

\displaystyle[\sum_{j}f^{0}_{j}({\bf DE})_{j},\sum_{i}g_{i}({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i}]

\displaystyle=-3i\sum_{i}g_{i}[f^{0}_{i}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}-\hat{y}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{y}-\hat{z}}+f^{0}_{i-\hat{z}-\hat{x}}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{y}}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{z}}-f^{0}_{i-\hat{x}-\hat{y}-\hat{z}}]

\displaystyle=-3i\sum_{i}f^{0}_{i}[g_{i}+g_{i+\hat{x}+\hat{y}}+g_{i+\hat{y}+\hat{z}}+g_{i+\hat{z}+\hat{x}}-g_{i+\hat{x}}-g_{i+\hat{y}}-g_{i+\hat{z}}-g_{i+\hat{x}+\hat{y}+\hat{z}}].

(46)

In the continuum limit one can show that the commutator reduces to
-3i\sum_{i}g_{i}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}f^{0}_{i}=+3i\sum_{i}f^{0}_{i}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}g_{i}
. By choosing
f^{0}
or
g
to be functions of a special type, i.e.

\displaystyle f^{0}_{i}=f^{0}_{1}(i_{x},i_{y})+f^{0}_{2}(i_{y},i_{z})+f^{0}_{3}(i_{z},i_{x})

(47)

(or similarly for
g_{i}
), one can prove the commutator vanishes
[\sum_{j}f^{0}_{j}({\bf DE})_{j},\sum_{i}g_{i}({\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}})_{i}]=0
. We thus obtain valid definitions of unitary operators
U_{A}
and
U_{E}
with
[U_{A},U_{E}]=0
under the (mild) restriction on the gauge functions
f^{0}_{i}
or
g_{i}
. Such a restriction is lifted when we turn off
f^{0}_{i}=0
and the model becomes identical to 3DTC. These transformation rules (and the constraint on the gauge functions) will play a vital role in identifying the field theory description of the F3 model.

III.2
Field theory of F3 model

The F3 model was derived from the hybrid of the rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) LGTs. We now apply the gauge principle to derive the appropriate EFT of the F3 model. There are three types of quasiparticle excitations in the F3 model called the freeons, fractons, and fluxons, which are the excitations of the
\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{a}_{i}
operators. We will use
e
,
f
,
m
to express the quantities related to freeons, fractons, fluxons, respectively.

First of all, the gauge transformation of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{E}
in the continuum field theory follows directly from the lattice transformation rules given in Eq. (
44
)

\displaystyle A^{a}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{A}^{\dagger}A^{a}({\bm{r}})U_{A}

\displaystyle=A^{a}({\bm{r}})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{bc}|\epsilon_{abc}|\partial_{b}\partial_{c}f^{0}({\bm{r}})-({\bm{\nabla}}\times\bm{f}({\bm{r}}))^{a}

\displaystyle\bm{E}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{E}^{\dagger}\bm{E}({\bm{r}})U_{E}=\bm{E}({\bm{r}})-{\bm{\nabla}}g({\bm{r}}).

(48)

The two unitary operators

\displaystyle U_{A}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[i\int d^{3}{\bm{r}}\Bigl(f^{0}({\bm{r}}){\bf DE}(\bm{r})+{\bm{f}}(\bm{r})\cdot\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E}(\bm{r})\Bigl)\right]

\displaystyle U_{E}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[i\int d^{3}{\bm{r}}g({\bm{r}}){\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})\right],

(49)

\bm{f}=(f^{x},f^{y},f^{z})
, are the continuum descendants of the lattice operators given in Eq. (
45
). In particular, we have
{\bf DE}=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}E^{z}+\partial_{y}\partial_{z}E^{x}+\partial_{x}\partial_{y}E^{z}
. As in the lattice consideration, the two unitary operators commute provided we choose either
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}f^{0}({\bm{r}})=0
or
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}g({\bm{r}})=0
. Such condition is fulfilled by choosing
f^{0}
or
g
to be the sum of functions that depend on at most two of the coordinates but not on all three.

The freeon field
\psi_{e}
transforms as
U_{E}^{\dagger}\psi_{e}U_{E}=e^{ig}\psi_{e}
under the constraint

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}}=\rho_{e}=\psi^{\dagger}_{e}\psi_{e}.

(50)

It behaves in the same way as the electric quasiparticle in the 3DTC with the matter field Lagrangin in Eq. (
9
) and the continuity equation
\partial_{t}\rho^{e}+\nabla\cdot\bm{j}^{e}=0
. The other quasiparticles, i.e. fracton and three species of fluxons, can be grouped as
\psi^{\mu}
(
\mu=0
for fracton,
\mu=x,y,z
for fluxons) and transform as

\displaystyle\psi^{\mu}\rightarrow U_{A}^{\dagger}\psi^{\mu}U_{A}=e^{if^{\mu}}\psi^{\mu}

(51)

under the constraints

\displaystyle{\bf DE}(\bm{r})=\rho^{f},~~{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}}={\bm{\rho}}^{m}

(52)

where
{\bm{\rho}}^{m}=(\rho^{m_{x}},\rho^{m_{y}},\rho^{m_{z}})
.
A gauge-invariant Lagrangian can be constructed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}=i\sum_{\mu}(\psi^{\mu})^{\dagger}\partial_{t}\psi^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{a<b}|\bm{D}^{ab}\psi|^{2}

(53)

with the covariant derivatives given by

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{xy}\psi

\displaystyle=\psi^{x}\psi^{y}\psi^{0}\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\psi^{0}-\psi^{x}\psi^{y}\partial_{x}\psi^{0}\partial_{y}\psi^{0}

\displaystyle+{(\psi^{0})}^{2}[\psi^{x}\partial_{x}\psi^{y}-\psi^{y}\partial_{y}\psi^{x}+iA^{z}\psi^{x}\psi^{y}]

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{xz}\psi

\displaystyle=\psi^{x}\psi^{z}\psi^{0}\partial_{x}\partial_{z}\psi^{0}-\psi^{x}\psi^{z}\partial_{x}\psi^{0}\partial_{z}\psi^{0}

\displaystyle+{(\psi^{0})}^{2}[\psi^{z}\partial_{z}\psi^{x}-\psi_{f}^{x}\partial_{x}\psi^{z}+iA^{y}\psi^{y}\psi^{z}]

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{yz}\psi

\displaystyle=\psi^{y}\psi^{z}\psi^{0}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}\psi^{0}-\psi^{y}\psi^{z}\partial_{y}\psi^{0}\partial_{z}\psi^{0}

\displaystyle+{(\psi^{0})}^{2}[\psi^{y}\partial_{y}\psi^{z}-\psi^{z}\partial_{z}\psi^{y}+iA^{x}\psi^{y}\psi^{z}].

(54)

They are quartic in the matter fields and involve the interaction between fractons and fluxons. (The Lagrangian itself is octic in the matter fields.) Once the fracton field is turned off by taking
\psi^{0}=1
, we recover the covariant derivative of the 3DTC given in Eq. (
11
), which again reveals the 3DTC root of the F3 model.

The conservation laws following from the Lagrangian in Eq. (
53
) are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{f}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}j^{z}+\partial_{x}\partial_{z}j^{y}+\partial_{y}\partial_{z}j^{x},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}^{m}

\displaystyle=\nabla\times\bm{j}.

(55)

The quasiparticle density and the current operators are given by

\displaystyle\rho^{f}

\displaystyle={(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}\psi^{0}

\displaystyle{\bm{\rho}}^{m}

\displaystyle=((\psi^{x})^{\dagger}\psi^{x},(\psi^{y})^{\dagger}\psi^{y},(\psi^{z})^{\dagger}\psi^{z})

\displaystyle j^{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2mi}\left[{(\psi^{y})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{z})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{\bm{D}}^{yz}\psi-h.c.\right]

\displaystyle j^{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2mi}\left[{(\psi^{z})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{x})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{\bm{D}}^{xz}\psi-h.c.\right]

\displaystyle j^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2mi}\left[{(\psi^{x})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{y})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{(\psi^{0})}^{\dagger}{\bm{D}}^{xy}\psi-h.c.\right].

(56)

First of all, note that
\partial_{t}{\bm{\rho}}^{m}={\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{j}}
is identical to the conservation of magnetic charges in Eq. (
13
). Furthermore, the constraint
{\bm{\rho}}^{m}={\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}}
given in Eq. (
52
) implies
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{\rho}^{m}=0
, i.e. the magnetic excitations must form closed loops. Time derivatives of both fracton (
\rho^{f}
) and the fluxon (
\bm{\rho}^{m}
) densities are related to the same current density vector
\bm{j}
in Eq. (
55
), which goes to show that fractons and fluxons are not independent entities in the F3 model, but are intertwined objects through the definitions of the current operators
{\bm{j}}
given above and the continuity equation given in Eq. (
55
).

Having established the EFT of the freeon, fracton, and fluxon matter fields, we proceed to construct the gauge field Lagrangian. The starting point, as usual, is the constraints relating the matter and the gauge degrees of freedom:

\displaystyle(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{A})_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{p}\rho^{e}_{i},

\displaystyle(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{E})_{i}

\displaystyle=\bm{\rho}^{m}_{i},

\displaystyle({\bf DE})_{i}

\displaystyle=\rho^{f}_{i}.

(57)

Taking the time derivatives on both sides of the equations and invoking the continuity equation of freeons in Eq. (
12
), and of fluxons and fractons in Eq. (
55
), we obtain three relations

\displaystyle(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bm{A}})_{i}

\displaystyle=-(2\pi/p)(\nabla\cdot\bm{j}^{e})_{i}

\displaystyle(\bm{\nabla}\times\partial_{t}{\bm{E}})_{i}

\displaystyle=(\nabla\times\bm{j})_{i}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}({\bf DE})_{i}

IV
Summary

We have developed an easy-to-implement scheme for deriving field theories of toric code and X-cube model in three dimensions, and applied the method to derive the field theory for the F3 model that two of the present authors wrote down earlier
Kim and Han 2021
. We show that the F3 model follows from Higgsing the hybrid of rank-1 and rank-2 U(1) lattice gauge theories in three dimensions. By consistently applying the gauge principle, we are able to derive the matter and the gauge field Lagrangian for the F3 model in the continuum and also in the lattice form. It turns out the effective field theory of the F3 model is that of the 3D toric code modified by a term, and describe the same universal physics. As a by-product, we construct the matter field Lagrangians both in the continuum and in the lattice form for the well-known 3D toric code and the X-cube model as well.

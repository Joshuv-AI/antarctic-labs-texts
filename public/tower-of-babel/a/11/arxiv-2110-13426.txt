Russo-Dye type Theorem, Stinespring representation, and Radon Nikodým dervative for invariant block multilinear completely positive maps
Anindya Ghatak, Aryaman Sensarma

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate certain basic properties of invariant multilinear CP maps. For instance, we prove Russo-Dye type theorem for invariant multilinear positive maps on both commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and finite-dimensional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. We show that every invariant multilinear CP map is automatically symmetric and completely bounded. Possibly these results are unknown in the literature (see [ 12 , 13 , 16 ] ). Motivated from quantum algorithm simulation [ 7 ] we introduce multilinear version of invariant block CP map φ = [ φ i ​ j ] : M n ​ ( 𝒜 ) k → M n ​ ( ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) ) . \varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B({H})}). Then we derive that each φ i ​ j \varphi_{ij} can be dilated to a common commutative tuple of ∗ * -homomorphisms. As a natural appeal, the suitable notion of minimality has been identified within this framework. A special case of our result recovers a finer version of J. Heo’s Stinespring type dilation theorem of [ 13 ] , and A. Kaplan’s Stinespring type dilation theorem [ 20 ] . As an application, we show Russo-Dye type theorem for invariant multilinear completely positive maps. Finally, using minimal Stinespring dilation we obtain Radon Nikodým theorem in this setup. Our result includes as a special case the Radon-Nikodým theorem of J. Heo from [ 13 ] and the Radon-Nikodým theorem of M. Joița from [ 19 ] .

1.
Introduction

In the late 1960s, W. B. Arveson used CP maps to define non-commutative measures in a seminal paper
[
2
]
. Subsequently, he obtained the Radon-Nikodým theorem of CP maps using the minimality condition of the Stinespring dilation theorem. Over the decades, the theory of CP maps has been widely used as a tool in the study of
C^{*}
-algebras, and Operator Theory
[
17
,
18
]
. Moreover, CP maps has been used in great interest to study Markov maps of quantum probability, quantum channel in
quantum information theory, and in the classification problem of
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
23
]
and references therein).

In 1987, E. Christensen and A. M. Sinclair studied symmetric multilinear completely positive maps and completely bounded maps
\varphi:\mathcal{A}^{k}\to\mathcal{B(H)}

[
8
, p. 153, p. 155]
. They obtained a technical Stinespring type theorem

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]

using Wittstock Hahn-Banach theorem (see
[
26
]
). In 1998, A. M. Sinclair and R. R. Smith characterized that a von Neumann algebra is
injective
if and only if every bilinear completely bounded map can be factorized in
terms of bilinear CP maps
[
25
, Theorem 6.1]
. For more details on completely bounded multilinear maps and their intrinsic connection with the Haagerup tensor norm, we refer to
[
23
, Chapter 17]
.

In 1990, J. P. Antoine and A. Inoue studied representations of invariant sesquilinear forms on partial
*
-algebras as these representations are arising from statistical mechanics and quantum field theory
[
3
]
.
Motivated by the work of J. P. Antoine and A. Inoue
[
3
]
, J. Heo studied multilinear CP maps using invariant property
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. Imposing invariant property,
the author obtained an excellent form of the Stinespring dilation theorem for invariant multilinear completely positive maps which are completely bounded and symmetric as well (cf.
[
12
,
13
]
).

A block map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B})
is defined by

[\varphi_{ij}]([a_{ij}])=[\varphi_{ij}(a_{ij})]\text{ for all }[a_{ij}]\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}).

Given a fixed
[\lambda_{ij}]
positive definite matrix, we can associate a block map
[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
defined by

[\varphi_{ij}][a_{ij}]=[\lambda_{ij}a_{ij}]\text{ for all }[a_{ij}]\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),

is
a positive map. Hence block states can be thought of as a generalization of Schur products.
Block CP maps are of independent interest and these provide various applications (see
[
6
,
23
]
and references therein). In 1999, J. Heo extensively studied block CP maps and derived the Stinespring type theorem using Hilbert
C^{*}
-module technique.

The Aaronson-Ambanis conjecture is a significant proposition in the analysis of boolean functions. It states that for a degree
d
-multilinear polynomial
f:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\longrightarrow[0,1]
, the variable with the highest influence in
f
must have an influence of at least
\text{poly}(\text{var}[f],\frac{1}{d})
. This conjecture raises a structural question about bounded polynomials on the hypercube and remains an open problem of great interest. In a related work, referenced in
[
7
]
, the authors provide a proof for a different result. They show that in any completely bounded degree
d
block-multilinear form with constant variables, there always exists a variable with an influence of at least
\frac{1}{\text{poly}(d)}
. This finding contributes to the exploration of bounded polynomials on the hypercube and provides insights into the conjecture. Furthermore, Montanaro has made progress on the Aaronson-Ambanis conjecture by proving a special case in which the block-multilinear forms satisfy the condition of having all coefficients with the same magnitude. This result, described in
[
22
]
, narrows down the scope of the conjecture and adds to our understanding of the underlying principles. For more comprehensive information and specific details on these findings, we recommend referring to the cited sources
[
1
,
21
]
. Block multilinear forms hold immense significance across multiple domains, with applications ranging from quantum algorithm simulation
[
7
]
to other relevant fields.

Drawing inspiration from quantum algorithm simulation
[
7
]
(thanks to Prof. Mathew Graydon for sharing such ideas), A. Kaplan’s block states
[
20
]
and J. Heo’s work on invariant multilinear CP maps
[
12
]
, we present a novel extension known as block invariant multilinear CP maps. For a detailed and comprehensive exploration of this topic, refer to Section
3

Our proposed model unifies the works of A. Kaplan
[
20
]
, and J. Heo
[

,
13
]
(see Section
5
for more details). In addition, we provide several concrete examples to illustrate the concepts discussed in this topic. These examples serve to demonstrate the usefulness and applicability of the methods and ideas presented, and provide a deeper understanding of the theory.

Let

A map

For fixed

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}

\displaystyle M_{t}\big(\mathcal{A}\big)^{k}

\displaystyle:=\underbrace{M_{t}\big(\mathcal{A}\big)\times M_{t}\big(\mathcal{A}\big)\times\cdots\times M_{t}\big(\mathcal{A}\big)}_{k\text{-times}}\text{~for every~}t\in\mathbb{N}.

-amplification

2.
Russo-Dye type theorem and Properties of multilinear positive maps

In the next result, we prove multilinear analogue of Russo-Dye type Theorem (see Theorem
1.1
). More precisely, this result ensures the norm attainment of multilinear invariant positive maps on
commutative domain at
(1,1,\cdots,1)
point. The idea of the proof is based on using the delicate structure of partition of unity and invariant property.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in IP\big(C(X)^{k},\mathcal{B(H)}\big),
then
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(1,1,\cdots,1)\|.

Proof.

We prove this result for
k=3
and
k=4
as the same argument will follow for
k=2m
and
k=2m-1
for
m\geq 1
. Now, we proceed to prove for
k=3.

(i):
Case 1:
Let
\varphi(1,1,1)=0
and
f,g\in C(X)
such that
g\geq 0
. Consider,

\|f\|^{2}\varphi(1,g,1)-\varphi(f,g,\overline{f})=\varphi(1,g,\|f\|^{2}-f\overline{f})\geq 0.

Thus,
0\leq\varphi(f,g,\overline{f})\leq\|f\|^{2}\varphi(1,g,1)
. Now,

\|g\|\varphi(1,1,1)-\varphi(1,g,1)=\varphi(1,\|g\|-g,1)\geq 0.

Therefore,
\varphi(1,g,1)\leq\|g\|\varphi(1,1,1)
. This gives us
\varphi(1,g,1)=0
. So,
\varphi(f,g,\overline{f})=0
.

Let
f,g,h\in C(X)
. Then,

\varphi(f,g,h)=\varphi(1,g,fh)=\varphi(1,g,k)=\varphi(1,g,k_{1}+ik_{2}),

where
fh=k=k_{1}+ik_{2}
,
k_{1},k_{2}\in C(X)_{\mbox{sa}}
.
Now,

\varphi(1,g,k_{1}+ik_{2})=\varphi(1,g,k_{1})+i\varphi(1,g,k_{2}).

We write
k_{i}=k_{i1}-k_{i2}
, where
k_{i1},k_{i2}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
. Using the multilinearity of
\varphi
and
\varphi(f,g,\overline{f})=0,
we get
\varphi(f,g,h)=0
. Therefore
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|=0
.

Case 2:
Without loss of generality, let
0<\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|\leq 1
. Since
\varphi
is invariant, we get
\|\varphi\|=\sup\{\|\varphi(1,f,g)\|:~\|f\|\leq 1,~\|g\|\leq 1,~~f,g\in C(X)\}
. Let
f,g\in C(X)
, then for
\epsilon>0,
there exist open covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
X
such that
|f(x)-f(x_{i})|<\epsilon
for all
x\in U_{i}
and
|g(y)-g(y_{j})|<\epsilon
for all
y\in V_{j}
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\in X
. Let
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
are non-negative functions on
X
satisfying
\mbox{Supp}~~p_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
,
\mbox{Supp}~~q_{j}\subseteq V_{j}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}=1
. Now,

\displaystyle\big|f(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i}(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x)-f(x_{i}))p_{i}(x)\big|\leq\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x)=\epsilon.

Similarly, we get
\big|g(y)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}g(y_{j})q_{j}(y)\big|<\epsilon
for all
y\in X
. Now,

\displaystyle\|\varphi(1,f,g)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(1,f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\|+\|\varphi(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\|

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\epsilon+\|\varphi(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\|.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Big\|\varphi(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\Big\|\leq\Big\|\varphi(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g-\sum_{j=1}^{m}g(y_{j})q_{j})\Big\|+\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\varphi(1,p_{i},q_{j})\Big\|.

Notice that
\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i}\|\leq\|f\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\leq 1
. Thus,
\Big\|\varphi(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g-\sum_{j=1}^{m}g(y_{j})q_{j})\Big\|\leq\|\varphi\|\epsilon.

Also, notice that
\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n,m}\varphi(1,p_{i},q_{j})=\varphi(1,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}q_{j})=\varphi(1,1,1)\leq 1
and
\left|f(x_{i})\right|,\left|g(y_{j})\right|<1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j\leq m
. By
[
23
]
[Lemma 2.3], we have
\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\varphi(1,p_{i},q_{j})\|\leq 1
. Hence from Equation (
2
) and Equation (
2
), we obtain
\|\varphi(1,f,g)\|\leq\|\varphi\|\epsilon+\|\varphi\|\epsilon+1
. Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain
\|\varphi(1,f,g)\|\leq 1
.

Let
\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|=r\neq 0\leq 1
. Clearly
\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|\leq\|\varphi\|
. Further, we have
\|\dfrac{1}{r}\varphi(1,1,1)\|\leq 1
. Now, using the above argument, we get
\|\dfrac{1}{r}\varphi\|\leq 1
. Hence
\|\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|
.

(ii):
Case 1:
Let
\varphi(1,1,1,1)=0
and
f,g\in C(X)
. Consider
\|f\|^{2}\|g\|^{2}\varphi(1,1,1,1)-\varphi(f,g,\overline{g},\overline{f})=\varphi(1,1,\|g\|^{2},\|f\|^{2})-\varphi(1,1,g\overline{g},f\overline{f})=\varphi(1,1,\|g\|^{2}-g\overline{g},\|f\|^{2})+\varphi(1,1,g\overline{g},\|f\|^{2}-f\overline{f})\geq 0
. Since
\varphi(1,1,1,1)=0,
we get
\varphi(f,g,\overline{g},\overline{f})=0
. Let
f,g,h,k\in C(X)
. Since
\varphi
is invariant, we have
\varphi(f,g,h,k)=\varphi(1,1,gh,fk)
. Now using the same argument of (i), we get
\varphi(f,g,h,k)=0
. Hence
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(1,1,1,1)\|=0
.

Case 2:
Without loss of generality, let
0<\|\varphi(1,1,1,1)\|\leq 1
. Since
\varphi
is invariant we get
\|\varphi\|=\sup\{\|\varphi(1,1,f,g)\|:~f,g\in C(X),~\|f\|,\|g\|\leq 1\}
. Let
f,g\in C(X)
,
\|f\|,\|g\|\leq 1
. Since
X
is compact, for
\epsilon>0
, there exist finite open covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
X
and
x_{i},y_{j}\in X
for
1\leq i\leq n
, for
1\leq j\leq m
such that

\left|f(x)-f(x_{i})\right|<\epsilon
for all
x\in U_{i}
,
\left|g(y)-g(y_{j})\right|<\epsilon
for all
y\in V_{j}
. Let
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
are non-negative functions on
X
satisfying the conditions
\mbox{Supp}~~p_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
,
\mbox{Supp}~~q_{j}\subseteq V_{j}
,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1
and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}=1
.
By providing similar estimations to the case
(k=3),
we get the following:

\displaystyle\|\varphi(1,1,f,g)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(1,1,f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\|+\|\varphi(1,1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\epsilon+\|\varphi(1,1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})p_{i},g-\sum_{j=1}^{m}g(y_{j})q_{j})\|+\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\varphi(1,1,p_{i},q_{j})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\epsilon+\|\varphi\|\epsilon+\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{i})g(y_{j})\varphi(1,1,p_{i},q_{j})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\varphi\|\epsilon+1.

Since
\epsilon>0,
we have
\|\varphi(1,1,f,g)\|\leq 1
. It follows that
\|\varphi\|\leq 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We now present an example of an invariant multilinear positive map on matrix algebras.

Example 2.2
.

The mapping
\varphi:~M_{n}(\mathbb{C})^{3}\longrightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})=\mbox{Tr}(A_{1}A_{3})\mbox{Tr}(A_{2})I,

is positive invariant multilinear map, where
\mbox{Tr}(A)
denotes the normalized trace of
A
.

The following result ensures the norm attainment of multilinear invariant positive maps on finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras at the point
(I,I,\cdots,I)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in IP\big(M_{n}(\mathbb{C})^{k},\mathcal{B(H)}\big),
then
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(I,I,\cdots,I)\|.

Proof.

We show this result for
k=3
and
k=4
as the same line of arguments will follow for
k=2m
and
k=2m-1
for
m\geq 1
. Now, we proceed to prove for
k=3.

Case 1:
Notice that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sup_{\|A_{i}\|\leq 1}\|\varphi(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\|=\sup_{\|A_{i}\|\leq 1}\|\varphi(I,A_{2},A_{1}A_{3})\|~~~~~~(\text{ as }\varphi\text{ is invariant }).

Let
U
,
V
are two unitary matrices, then
U=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{i}
,
V=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}Q_{j}
, where
P_{i}
and
Q_{j}
are projections such that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}Q_{j}=I
. Thus, we get
\varphi(I,U,V)=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n,m}\lambda_{i}\mu_{j}\varphi(I,P_{i},Q_{j})
. Since
\varphi
is invariant, we have
\varphi(I,P_{i},Q_{j})=\varphi(Q_{j},P_{i},Q_{j})\geq 0
. Now,
\varphi(I,I,I)=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n,m}\varphi(I,P_{i},Q_{j})
.
Now, it is immediate to see that

\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(I,I,I)&\varphi(I,U,V)\\
\varphi(I,U,V)^{*}&\varphi(I,I,I)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0.

Invoking
[
23
, Lemma 3.1]
we observe that
\|\varphi(I,U,V)\|\leq\|\varphi(I,I,I)\|
. Using the singular value decomposition, we know that any matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
can be written as an average of two unitary matrices. So, we conclude that
\|\varphi(I,A,B)\|\leq\|\varphi(I,I,I)\|
, where
A,~B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Hence
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(I,I,I)\|
.

Case 2:
Here
\|\varphi\|=\displaystyle\sup_{\|A_{i}\|\leq 1}\|\varphi(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4})\|=\sup_{\|A_{i}\|\leq 1}\|\varphi(I,I,A_{2}A_{3},A_{1}A_{4})\|
. Following the above argument, we obtain the desired result. This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that every positive linear map between two
C^{*}
-algebras is symmetric. In the next result, we show that this result can be extended to invariant positive multilinear maps. We could not find this observation
in the literature
[
12
,
13
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\in IP\big(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{B}\big).
Then
\varphi
is symmetric, that is,
\varphi^{*}=\varphi
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove for
k=3,4.

(i.) Let
\varphi\in IP\big(\mathcal{A}^{3},\mathcal{B}\big).
Since
\varphi
is invariant, it is enough to show that
\varphi^{*}(1,x_{2},x_{3})=\varphi(1,x_{2},x_{3})
, where
x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mbox{sa}}
. We can write
x_{2}=x_{2}^{1}-x_{2}^{2}
and
x_{3}=x_{3}^{1}-x_{3}^{2}
, where
x_{2}^{i},x_{3}^{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
. Now,

\displaystyle\varphi(1,x_{2},x_{3})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{3}^{1}-x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{1}-x_{2}^{2},1)^{*}\quad\quad(\text{Since~}\varphi\text{ is invariant})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{3}^{1},x_{2}^{1},1)^{*}-\varphi(x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{1},1)^{*}-\varphi(x_{3}^{1},x_{2}^{2},1)^{*}+\varphi(x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{2},1)^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{3}^{1},x_{2}^{1},1)-\varphi(x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{1},1)-\varphi(x_{3}^{1},x_{2}^{2},1)+\varphi(x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{2},1)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{3}^{1}-x_{3}^{2},x_{2}^{1}-x_{2}^{2},1)

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,x_{2},x_{3}).\quad\quad(\text{Since~}\varphi\text{ is invariant})

It follows that
\varphi(1,x_{2},x_{3})^{*}=\varphi(x_{3},x_{2},1)^{*}=\varphi(1,x_{2},x_{3})=\varphi(x_{3},x_{2},1)=\varphi^{*}(1,x_{2},x_{3})
.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
,
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
,
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
, where
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mbox{sa}},~i=1,2
. Now,

\displaystyle\varphi(1,u^{*},v^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,u_{1}-iu_{2},v_{1}-iv_{2})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,u_{1},v_{1})^{*}+i\varphi(1,u_{1},v_{2})^{*}+i\varphi(1,u_{2},v_{1})^{*}-\varphi(1,u_{2},v_{2})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(1,u_{1},v_{1})+i\varphi^{*}(1,u_{1},v_{2})+i\varphi^{*}(1,u_{2},v_{1})-\varphi^{*}(1,u_{2},v_{2})=\varphi^{*}(1,u,v).

Therefore, we get
\varphi^{*}(1,u,v)=\varphi(1,u^{*},v^{*})^{*}=\varphi(1,u,v)
.

(ii.) Let
\varphi\in IP\big(\mathcal{A}^{4},\mathcal{B}\big).
Since
\varphi
is invariant, it is enough to show that
\varphi^{*}(1,1,x_{3},x_{4})=\varphi(1,1,x_{3},x_{4})
, where
x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mbox{sa}}
. We can write
x_{3}=x_{3}^{1}-x_{3}^{2}
and
x_{4}=x_{4}^{1}-x_{4}^{2}
, where
x_{3}^{i},x_{4}^{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
. Now,

\displaystyle\varphi(1,1,x_{3},x_{4})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{1},x_{4}^{1})^{*}-\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{1},x_{4}^{2})^{*}-\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{1})^{*}+\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{2})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{1},x_{4}^{1})-\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{1},x_{4}^{2})-\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{1})+\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,1,x_{3}^{1}-x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{1}-x_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{4},x_{3},1,1).

Thus,
\varphi(1,1,x_{3},x_{4})^{*}=\varphi(x_{4},x_{3},1,1)=\varphi(x_{4},x_{3},1,1)^{*}=\varphi^{*}(1,1,x_{3},x_{4})
. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{A}
,
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
,
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
, where
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mbox{sa}},~i=1,2
. Now,
we see the following computations.

\displaystyle\varphi(1,1,u^{*},v^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(1,1,u_{1},v_{1})^{*}+i\varphi(1,1,u_{1},v_{2})^{*}+i\varphi(1,1,u_{2},v_{1})^{*}-\varphi(1,1,u_{2},v_{2})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(1,1,u_{1},v_{1})+i\varphi^{*}(1,1,u_{1},v_{2})+i\varphi^{*}(1,1,u_{2},v_{1})-\varphi^{*}(1,1,u_{2},v_{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(1,1,u,v).

So we get
\varphi^{*}(1,1,u,v)=\varphi(1,1,u^{*},v^{*})^{*}=\varphi(1,1,u,v)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We show that every multilinear invariant CP map is completely bounded. This result is a multilinear version of
[
23
, Proposition 3.6]
. Possibly, it was unknown in the literature
[
12
,
13
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi\in ICP\big(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{B}\big).
Then the following properties hold.

(i)

If
k=3
, then
\varphi
is completely bounded map and
\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|
.

(ii)

If
k=4
, then
\varphi
is completely bounded map and
\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi(1,1,1,1)\|
.

Proof.

(i): Due to invariant property of
\varphi
, we can write,

\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}=\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}\|:~n\in\mathbb{N}\},~~\mbox{where}~~\|\varphi_{n}\|=\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}(1,x,y)\|:~\|x\|,~\|y\|\leq 1\}.

Let
x,y\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})_{\mbox{sa}}
, then we can find
x_{i},y_{i}\geq 0,~i=1,2
such that
x=x_{1}-x_{2},

y=y_{1}-y_{2}
and

\|x_{i}\|\leq\|x\|,

\|y_{i}\|\leq\|y\|
. Here,

\|x_{i}\|\|y_{j}\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)-\varphi_{n}(1,x_{i},y_{j})=\varphi_{n}(1,\|x_{i}\|-x_{i},\|y_{j}\|)+\varphi_{n}(1,x_{i},\|y_{j}\|-y_{j})\geq 0,~~i,j=1,2.

So,
\varphi_{n}(1,x,y)=\varphi_{n}(1,x_{1}-x_{2},y_{1}-y_{2})=\varphi_{n}(1,x_{1},y_{1})+\varphi_{n}(1,x_{2},y_{2})-\varphi_{n}(1,x_{1},y_{2})-\varphi_{n}(1,x_{2},y_{1})
. Now,
\|\varphi_{n}(1,x_{i},y_{j})\|\leq\|x_{i}\|\|y_{j}\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)\|\leq\|x\|\|y\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)\|.
This implies that
\|\varphi_{n}(1,x,y)\|\leq 4\|x\|\|y\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1,)\|.

Let
u,v\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})
such that
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
and
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
, where
u_{i},v_{i}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})_{\mbox{sa}}
and
i=1,2
. Again by some simple calculations, we get
\|\varphi_{n}(1,u,v)\|\leq 16\|u\|\|v\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)\|
. Hence
\|\varphi_{n}\|\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)\|.

Also, it is easy to notice that
\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1)\|=\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|.
Therefore we have,
\|\varphi\|_{cb}\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi(1,1,1)\|.

(ii): Due to invariant property of
\varphi
, we can write,

\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}=\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}\|:~n\in\mathbb{N}\},~~\mbox{where}~~\|\varphi_{n}\|=\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,x,y)\|:~\|x\|,~\|y\|\leq 1\}.

Let
x,y\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})_{\mbox{sa}}
, then
x=x_{1}-x_{2}
and
y=y_{1}-y_{2}
, where
x_{i},~y_{i}\geq 0,~i=1,2
. Note that

\|x_{i}\|\|y_{j}\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1,1)-\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{i},y_{j})=\varphi_{n}(1,1,\|x_{i}\|-x_{i},\|y_{j}\|)+\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{i},\|y_{j}\|-y_{j})\geq 0

for
i,j=1,2.

So,
\varphi_{n}(1,1,x,y)=\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{1}-x_{2},y_{1}-y_{2})=\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{1},y_{1})+\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{2},y_{2})-\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{1},y_{2})-\varphi_{n}(1,1,x_{2},y_{1})
. By following the above arguments, we get
\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,x,y)\|\leq 4\|x\|\|y\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1,1)\|.

Let
u,v\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})
such that
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
and
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
, where
u_{i},v_{i}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A})_{\mbox{sa}},~~i=1,2
. Again by some simple calculations we obtain
\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,u,v)\|\leq 16\|u\|\|v\|\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1,1)\|
. It is easy to see that
\|\varphi_{n}\|\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi_{n}(1,1,1,1)\|=2^{4}\|\varphi(1,1,1,1)\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Therefore,
\|\varphi\|_{cb}\leq 2^{4}\|\varphi(1,1,1,1)\|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we provide an example of a positive multilinear map on a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra that is not CP. This observation contrasts with the well-known theorem due to Stinespring
[
23
, Theorem 3.11]
that every positive map on a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra is automatically CP.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\varphi:(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be an invariant map
defined by
\varphi((a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2}),(c_{1},c_{2}))=a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}
. Clearly
\varphi
is multilinear. We can easily observe that
\varphi
is positive. Now consider
\varphi_{2}:~M_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times M_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times M_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\longrightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The
(1,1)
-th entry of

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\begin{bmatrix}(1,1)&&(0,0)\\
(0,0)&&(0,0)\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}(1,0)&&(1,0)\\
(1,0)&&(1,0)\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}(1,0)&&(-2,0)\\
(-2,0)&&(4,0)\end{bmatrix}\right),

is

-\varphi(e_{1},e_{1},e_{1})
. This implies that
\varphi_{2}
is not positive as
\varphi(e_{1},e_{1},e_{1})=1
. Hence
\varphi
is not CP.

Motivated by the Example
2.6
, we produce a family of invariant positive multilinear maps on a commutative domain that are not CP.

Example 2.7
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
x_{0}\in X
. Consider
\varphi\in IP(C(X)^{3},\mathbb{C})
defined by
\varphi(f,g,h)=f(x_{0})g(x_{0})h(x_{0})
. Following a similar type of calculation as in Example
2.6
we conclude that
\varphi
is not CP.

3.
Block multilinear CP maps

We start this section with the definition of block multilinear CP maps.
For
n,m\geq 1
and fixed
k\geq 1
set

(4)

\displaystyle M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}

\displaystyle:=\underbrace{M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\times M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\times\cdots\times M_{n}(\mathcal{A})}_{k\text{-times}},

(5)

\displaystyle M_{m}\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\big)^{k}

\displaystyle:=\underbrace{M_{m}\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\big)\times M_{m}\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\big)\times\cdots\times M_{m}\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\big)}_{k\text{-times}}.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(B(\mathcal{H}))
be a multilinear map. Then the multilinear map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]
is called
block
if

(6)

\displaystyle[\varphi_{ij}]\left([a_{{1}_{ij}}],[a_{{2}_{ij}}],\ldots,[a_{{k}_{ij}}]\right):=\left[\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k-1}=1}^{n}\varphi_{ij}(a_{{1}_{ir_{1}}},a_{{2}_{r_{1}r_{2}}},\ldots,a_{{k}_{r_{k-1}j}})\right].

A block multilinear map
\varphi
is called a
positive map
if

\varphi([a_{{1}_{ij}}],[a_{{2}_{ij}}],\ldots,[a_{{k}_{ij}}])\geq 0,

whenever
([a_{{1}_{ij}}],[a_{{2}_{ij}}],\ldots,[a_{{k}_{ij}}])=([a_{{k}_{ij}}]^{*},[a_{{k-1}_{ij}}]^{*},\ldots,[a_{{1}_{ij}}]^{*})
with an additional assumption that
[a_{{m}_{ij}}]
is positive when
k=2m-1
.
Given a multilinear map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)}),
the
t
-amplification map is a multilinear map

\varphi_{t}:M_{t}\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\big)^{k}\to M_{t}(M_{n}(B(\mathcal{H})))
defined by

(7)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}([A_{{1}_{ij}}],[A_{{2}_{ij}}],\ldots,[A_{{k}_{ij}}])=\left[\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k-1}=1}^{t}\varphi_{ij}\left(A_{{1}_{ir_{1}}},A_{{2}_{r_{1}r_{2}}},\ldots,A_{{k}_{r_{k-1}j}}\right)\right]

for all
[A_{{1}_{ij}}],[A_{{2}_{ij}}],\ldots,[A_{{k}_{ij}}]\in M_{t}(M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})).

A block multilinear map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]
is called
completely positive
(in short: CP) if
\varphi_{t}
is positive for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A multilinear map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})
is is said to be
invariant

if it satisfies the following:

(i.)

k=2m-1;
for all
A_{1},\ldots,A_{2m-1},C_{1},\ldots,C_{m-1}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}),

[\varphi_{ij}]
satisfies

\displaystyle[\varphi_{ij}]\big(A_{1}C_{1},\ldots,A_{m-1}C_{m-1},A_{m},A_{m+1},\ldots,A_{2m-1}\big)=

\displaystyle[\varphi_{ij}]\big(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m-1},A_{m},C_{m-1}A_{m+1},\ldots,C_{1}A_{2m-1}\big).

(ii.)

k=2m;
for all
A_{1},\ldots,A_{2m},C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}),

[\varphi_{ij}]
satisfies

\displaystyle[\varphi_{ij}]\big(A_{1}C_{1},\ldots,A_{m}C_{m},A_{m+1},\ldots,A_{2m}\big)=

\displaystyle[\varphi_{ij}]\big(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m},C_{m}A_{m+1},\ldots,C_{1}A_{2m}\big).

The
adjoint
of a multilinear block map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})
is denoted and defined by

\varphi^{*}\big(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}\big):=\varphi\big(A_{k}^{*},A_{k-1}^{*},\ldots,A_{1}^{*}\big)^{*}

for all
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}).
A block multilinear map
\varphi
is called
symmetric
if
\varphi^{*}=\varphi.

The next result shows that if a block map possess invariant property then each entry of the block map also possess invariant property.
This observation is a key tool to prove Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k}\to M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})
be an invariant block
k
-linear map.
Then
\varphi_{ij}:\mathcal{A}^{k}\to\mathcal{B(H)}
is an invariant
k
-linear map for
1\leq i,j\leq n.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
k=3,4
and
n=2
.

k=3
):
Let
A_{l}=[a_{l_{ij}}],~l=1,2,3
and
B=[b_{ij}]
. As
\varphi
is invariant, we write
\varphi(A_{1}B,A_{2},A_{3})=\varphi(A_{1},A_{2},BA_{3})
.
Choose
a_{l_{11}}=a_{l},~l=1,2,3
and
b_{11}=b
and other entries are zero.
From the above equation, we obtain
\varphi_{11}(a_{1}b,a_{2},a_{3})=\varphi_{11}(a_{1},a_{2},ba_{3})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence
\varphi_{11}
is invariant. Now, choose
a_{1_{12}}=a_{1}
,
a_{l_{22}}=a_{l}
,
l=2,3
,
b_{11}=b_{22}=b
and other entries are zero. From the above equation we get,
\varphi_{12}(a_{1}b,a_{2},a_{3})=\varphi_{12}(a_{1},a_{2},ba_{3})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b\in\mathcal{A}
. So
\varphi_{12}
is invariant. Next, choose
a_{1_{21}}=a_{1}
,
a_{l_{11}}=a_{l}
,
l=2,3
,
b_{11}=b_{22}=b
and other entries are zero. From the above equation, we conclude that
\varphi_{21}(a_{1}b,a_{2},a_{3})=\varphi_{21}(a_{1},a_{2},ba_{3})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence
\varphi_{21}
is invariant. Again consider
a_{l_{22}}=a_{l}
,
l=1,2,3
,
b_{22}=b
and other entries are zero. The same equation gives us
\varphi_{22}(a_{1}b,a_{2},a_{3})=\varphi_{22}(a_{1},a_{2},ba_{3})

\varphi_{22}

k=4
):
Let
A_{l}=[a_{l_{ij}}],~l=1,2,3
and
B_{m}=[b_{m_{ij}}],~m=1,2

\varphi

\varphi(A_{1}B_{1},A_{2}B_{2},A_{3},A_{4})=\varphi(A_{1},A_{2},B_{2}A_{3},B_{1}A_{4})
.
Choose
a_{l_{11}}=a_{l},~l=1,2,3,4
,
b_{m_{11}}=b_{m},~m=1,2
and other entries are zero. From the above equation we get
\varphi_{11}(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=\varphi_{11}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{2}a_{3},b_{1}a_{4})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{A}

\varphi_{11}
is invariant. Next, choose
a_{1_{12}}=a_{1}
,
a_{l_{22}}=a_{l},~l=2,3,4
and
b_{m_{11}}=b_{m_{22}}=b_{m},~m=1,2
and other entries are zero. From the above equation we observe
\varphi_{12}(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=\varphi_{12}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{2}a_{3},b_{1}a_{4})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence
\varphi_{12}
is invariant.
Again choose
a_{l_{22}}=a_{l},~l=1,2,3,4
and
b_{m_{22}}=b_{m},~m=1,2

Similarly we get
\varphi_{22}(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2},a_{3},a_{4})=\varphi_{22}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{2}a_{3},b_{1}a_{4})

for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
. Thus
\varphi_{21}
is invariant.
This completes the proof.

In the following example, we show that the converse of Lemma

is not true in general. In other words, if each

Example 3.3
.

Define
\Psi:~\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B(H)}

\Psi(a,b,c)=\pi_{1}(b)\pi_{2}(ac),

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are unital
*
-homomorphisms from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B(H)}
.

\Psi

\Psi(1,1,1)=\pi_{1}(1)\pi_{2}(1)\neq 0
.
Now, we consider the block map
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]:M_{2}(\mathcal{A})\times M_{2}(\mathcal{A})\times M_{2}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow M_{2}(\mathcal{B(H)})
, where
\varphi_{ij}=\Psi
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2
.
If possible, let
\varphi
is invariant. Then for
b,c\in\mathcal{A}
with
b\neq 0,

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&&0\\
0&&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&&b\\

2&&1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1&&3\\

b&&c\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&&3\\
0&&4\end{bmatrix}\right).

From the above equation, we get,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}2b\Psi(1,1,1)&&10b\Psi(1,1,1)\\

3.2.

The concept of minimality has received significant attention in research and has proven to be highly applicable across various domains (see
[

,
5
,
15
,
17
]
). Hence, it is both intuitive and imperative to explore the minimality condition in the context of invariant multilinear block CP maps. In this regard, we introduce the minimality condition as a fundamental aspect of our investigation.

Definition 3.6

(Minimality condition)
.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]\in ICP\left(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})\right).
Then a triple
\left(\{\pi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}\right)
is called minimal Stinespring tripple of
\varphi
if

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\overline{{\rm span}}\left\{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}.

Now, we turn our attention to the uniqueness of the Stinespring representation for block multilinear invariant CP maps.

Theorem 3.7

(Uniqueness property)
.

Let
\left(\{\pi^{(p)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V^{(p)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(p)}\right),p=1,2
be two minimal Stinespring triples of
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]\in ICP\left(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathcal{B(H)})\right).
Then there exists a unitary operator

such that

(1)

\pi_{1}^{(2)}(a_{1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(2)}(a_{m})U=U\pi_{1}^{(1)}(a_{1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(1)}(a_{m})
for all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathcal{A}.

(2)

UV^{(1)}_{i}=V_{i}^{(2)}~
for all
1\leq i\leq m.

Proof.

We define a linear map

U:{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(1)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}\to{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(2)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(2)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(2)}_{j}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}

by

U\left(\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(1)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}h_{lj})\right)=\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(2)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(2)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(2)}_{j}h_{lj})

for all
\Big(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(1)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}h_{lj})\in{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(1)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}.

Now, we claim that U is an isometry. It is straight forward to see that

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(2)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(2)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(2)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\big\langle\varphi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*}a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\big\langle\varphi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*},a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m.\\
\end{array}\right.

Since
\left(\{\pi^{(1)}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{V^{(1)}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(1)}\right)
is the minimal Stinespring triple of
\varphi,
following the above procedure, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(1)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}\big\langle\varphi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*}a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{m}\big\langle\varphi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*},a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m.\\
\end{array}\right.

Combining the last two equations, we conclude that

\left\|U\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(1)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|=\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(1)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(1)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(1)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|.

Thus the map
U
is well defined and isometry. Therefore,
U
can be extended uniquely from
\mathcal{K}^{(1)}
to
\mathcal{K}^{(2)}
preserving the isometry property. Without loss of generality, we denote this map by
U
. Clearly, the range of
U
contains
{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(2)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(2)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}V^{(2)}_{j}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}
and it is dense in
\mathcal{K}^{(2)}.
Therefore
U:\mathcal{K}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{K}^{(2)}
is a unitary. Some easy computations yield that
U
satisfies the property (1) and (2).

∎

The following corollary is a multilinear version of Stinespring dilation theorem for block states.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}]\in ICP\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big)
be an invariant multilinear block CP map. Then there are
m
-commuting family of
*
-homomorphisms

{\pi^{\varphi}_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B(K)}},

1\leq i\leq m,
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
,
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
such that

(i)

if
k=2m-1
, then
\varphi_{ij}
is of the form:

\varphi_{ij}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\ldots,a_{2m-1})=\langle\pi^{\varphi}_{1}(a_{m})\pi^{\varphi}_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\cdots\pi^{\varphi}_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})f_{j},f_{i}\rangle,

for all
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2m-1}\in\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

if
k=2m
, then
\varphi_{ij}
is of the form:

\varphi_{ij}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\ldots,a_{2m})=\langle\pi^{\varphi}_{1}(a_{m}a_{m+1})\pi^{\varphi}_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+2})\cdots\pi^{\varphi}_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m})f_{j},f_{i}\rangle

for all
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2m}\in\mathcal{A}
.

4.
Radon Nikodým Theorem for invariant block multilinear CP maps

In the theory of CP maps, the Radon Nikodým derivative plays a crucial role in describing the relationship between two CP maps when one dominates the other
[
2
]
.
Moreover, Radon-Nikodým derivative provides a tool for characterizing the relative behavior of two quantum channels. It allows us to compare the effects of the channels on quantum states and analyze the extent to which one channel can be approximated by another.
We explore the Radon Nikodým Theorem for block multilinear CP maps, which is an application of the minimal Stinespring representation (refer to Theorem
3.7
).

To begin our discussion, it is necessary to establish an order relation (
\leq
) between two block multilinear CP maps.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}],\psi=[\psi_{ij}]\in ICP\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big).

Then
\varphi
dominates
\psi
(in short
\psi\leq\varphi
) if

\varphi-\psi\in ICP\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big).

Let us assume that

\left(\{\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V^{(\varphi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}\right)
and

\left(\{\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V^{(\psi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(\psi)}\right)
be two minimal Stinespring triples of
\varphi
and
\psi
respectively. The commutant of the family

\{\pi_{i}^{(\varphi)}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is defined by the subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)})
by

\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\prime}:=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}):\pi_{i}^{(\varphi)}(a)T=T\pi_{i}^{(\varphi)}(a),\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A},1\leq i\leq m\}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi=[\varphi_{ij}],\psi=[\psi_{ij}]\in ICP\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{A})^{k},M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big).

Then
[\psi_{ij}]\leq[\varphi_{ij}]
if and only if there exists a
0\leq T\leq I
with

T\in\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\prime}

such that each
\psi_{ij}
can be expressed as follow:

(i)

if
k=2m-1
, then
\psi_{ij}
is of the form:

\psi_{ij}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\ldots,a_{2m-1})=V_{i}^{(\varphi)\ast}T\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{m})\pi^{(\varphi)}_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})V^{(\varphi)}_{j},

for all
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2m-1}\in\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

if
k=2m
, then
\psi_{ij}
is of the form:

\psi_{ij}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\ldots,a_{2m})=V_{i}^{(\varphi)\ast}T\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{m}a_{m+1})\pi^{(\varphi)}_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+2})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m})V^{(\varphi)}_{j},

for all
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2m}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

It enough to prove only if part. Recall that
[\psi_{ij}]\leq[\varphi_{ij}].
Since

\left(\{\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V^{(\varphi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}\right),
and
\left(\{\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{V^{(\psi)}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{K}^{(\psi)}\right)
are the minimal dilations of
\varphi
and
\psi,
therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\overline{{\rm span}}\left\{\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{i}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\psi)}=\overline{{\rm span}}\left\{\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{(\psi)}_{i}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}.

In what follows,
we define a map

S:{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{i}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}\to{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{\psi}_{i}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}

by

S\left(\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj})\right)=\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\psi)}_{j}h_{lj})

for all
\Big(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj})\in{\rm span}\left\{\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(\mathcal{A})\big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}\mathcal{H}\big)\right\}.

To see,
S
is well defined, observe that

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\psi)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\big\langle\psi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*}a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\big\langle\psi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*},a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})h_{lj},h_{ri}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\big\langle[\psi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*}a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})]\begin{bmatrix}h_{l1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{ln}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}h_{r1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{rn}\end{bmatrix}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\big\langle[\psi_{ij}(a_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}^{*},a_{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{l_{m-1}},a_{l_{m}})]\begin{bmatrix}h_{l1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{ln}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}h_{r1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{rn}\end{bmatrix}\big\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*}R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*},R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
D_{r_{p}},R_{l_{1}}
are defined in Equation (
14
).
Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\psi)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*}R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},\ldots,D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*},R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
D_{r_{p}},R{l_{1}}
are defined in Equation (
14
).
Since
\psi\leq\varphi,
we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\big\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*}R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\big\langle\varphi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*}R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\big\rangle

whenever
k=2m-1,
and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\psi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*},R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{l,r=1}^{t}\left\langle\varphi(D_{r_{m}}^{*},D_{r_{m-1}}^{*},\ldots,R_{r_{1}}^{*},R_{l_{1}},\ldots,D_{l_{m-1}},D_{l_{m}})h_{l},h_{r}\right\rangle

whenever
k=2m.
Therefore, we have

\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{m}V^{(\psi)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\Big)(\sum_{j=1}^{m}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj})\right\|^{2}.

Consequently,
S:\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}\to\mathcal{K}^{(\psi)}
is well defined
and
\|S\|\leq 1
. Set
T=S^{*}S:\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}\to\mathcal{K}^{(\varphi)}
so that
0\leq T\leq 1.
It is easy to see

SV^{(\varphi)}_{j}=V_{j}^{(\psi)}\text{ and }S\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)=\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}(a)S

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}.
Indeed,

\displaystyle S\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)\Big(\big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj})\Big)

\displaystyle=S\Big(\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)\big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj})\Big)

\displaystyle=\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}(a)\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\psi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\psi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\psi)}_{j}h_{lj})

\displaystyle=\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}(a)S\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{t}\pi^{(\varphi)}_{1}(a_{l_{1}})\cdots\pi^{(\varphi)}_{m}(a_{l_{m}})\big)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{(\varphi)}_{j}h_{lj}).

Moreover,
S^{*}\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}(a)=\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)S^{*},
because,
S\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a^{*})=\pi^{(\psi)}_{i}(a^{*})S.
Since
S^{*}S=T,
if follows from the above intertwiner relations of
S
, we have

T\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)=\pi^{(\varphi)}_{i}(a)T

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}.

Finally, we notice that

\displaystyle\psi_{ij}(a)=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{i}^{(\psi)*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m})V_{j}^{(\psi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
V_{i}^{(\psi)*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m}a_{m+1})V_{j}^{(\psi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(SV_{i}^{(\varphi)})^{*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m})(SV_{j}^{(\varphi)})&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
(SV_{i}^{(\varphi)})^{*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m}a_{m+1})(SV_{j}^{(\varphi)})&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}S^{*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m})SV_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}S^{*}\pi_{1}^{(\psi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\psi)}(a_{m}a_{m+1})SV_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}S^{*}S\pi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\varphi)}(a_{m})V_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}S^{*}S\pi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\varphi)}(a_{m}a_{m+1})V_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m,\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}T\pi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\varphi)}(a_{m})V_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m-1\\
&\\
V_{i}^{(\varphi)*}T\pi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})\cdots\pi_{m}^{(\varphi)}(a_{m}a_{m+1})V_{j}^{(\varphi)}&\text{if}\;k=2m.\\
\end{array}\right.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\in ICP(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{B(H)}),~k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}=\|\varphi(1,1,\ldots,1)\|
.

Proof.

We shall provide an explicit argument for the case
k=2m-1,~m\geq 1
. Similar procedure will follow for
k=2m,~m\geq 1
.
By the multilinear version of Stinespring Theorem, we get
\varphi(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2m-1})=V^{*}\pi_{1}(a_{m})\pi_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})V
, for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2m-1}\in\mathcal{A}
, where
V\in\mathcal{B(H,K)}
and
{\pi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B(K)}},

i=1,2,\ldots,m
, are commuting
*
-homomorphisms (see
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
). Let
\|a_{i}\|\leq 1
for
i=1,2,3,\ldots,2m-1
and
h,k\in\mathcal{H},\|h\|\leq 1,\|k\|\leq 1
. Now,

\displaystyle\big\langle\varphi(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2m-1})h,k\big\rangle=\big\langle V^{*}\pi_{1}(a_{m})\pi_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})Vh,k\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\pi_{1}(a_{m})\pi_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})Vh,Vk\big\rangle.

Putting both sides
|\cdot|
we get,

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\varphi(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2m-1})h,k\big\rangle\big|=\big|\big\langle\pi_{1}(a_{m})\pi_{2}(a_{m-1}a_{m+1})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{1}a_{2m-1})Vh,Vk\big\rangle\big|\leq\|V\|^{2}.

It gives us
\|\varphi\|\leq\|V\|^{2}
. But
\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|=\|V^{*}V\|=\|V\|^{2}
, so we have
\|\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|
. Clearly
\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|\leq\|\varphi\|
and hence
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|
. Then,

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}([a_{1_{ij}}],[a_{2_{ij}}],[a_{3_{ij}}],\ldots,[a_{{2m-1}_{ij}}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{2m-1}=1}^{2}\varphi(a_{1_{ir_{1}}},a_{2_{r_{1}r_{2}}},a_{3_{r_{2}r_{3}}},\ldots,a_{{2m-1}_{r_{2m-2}j}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{2m-1}=1}^{2}V^{*}\pi_{1}(a_{m_{r_{m-1}r_{m}}})\pi_{2}(a_{{m-1}_{r_{m-2}r_{m-1}}}a_{{m+1}_{r_{m}r_{m+1}}})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{1_{ir_{1}}}a_{{2m-1}_{r_{2m-2j}}})V\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}V^{*}&&0\\
0&&V^{*}\end{bmatrix}\left[\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{2m-1}=1}^{2}\pi_{m}(a_{1_{ir_{1}}})\ldots\pi_{1}(a_{m_{r_{m-1}r_{m}}})\ldots\pi_{m}(a_{{2m-1}_{r_{2m-2j}}})\right]\begin{bmatrix}V&&0\\
0&&V\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}V^{*}&&0\\
0&&V^{*}\end{bmatrix}\left[\pi_{m}(a_{1_{ij}})\right]\ldots\left[\pi_{1}(a_{{m}_{ij}})\right]\ldots\left[\pi_{m}(a_{{2m-1}_{ij}})\right]\begin{bmatrix}V&&0\\
0&&V\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}V^{*}&&0\\
0&&V^{*}\end{bmatrix}(\pi_{m})_{2}\big[a_{1_{ij}}\big]\ldots(\pi_{1})_{2}\big[a_{m_{ij}}\big]\ldots(\pi_{m})_{2}\big[a_{{2m-1}_{ij}}\big]\begin{bmatrix}V&&0\\
0&&V\end{bmatrix}.

Now putting both side
\|.\|
we get,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{2}\left(\big[a_{1_{ij}}\big],\big[a_{2_{ij}}\big],\big[a_{3_{ij}}\big],\ldots,\big[a_{{2m-1}_{ij}}\big]\right)\big\|\leq\big\|V^{*}\big\|\big\|V\big\|\big\|\big[a_{1_{ij}}\big]\big\|\big\|\big[a_{2_{ij}}\big]\big\|\big\|\big[a_{3_{ij}}\big]\big\|\ldots\big\|\big[a_{{2m-1}_{ij}}\big]\big\|.

So, we have
\|\varphi_{2}\|\leq\|V\|^{2}
.
Since
\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|=\|V\|^{2}
, we get
\|\varphi_{2}\|\leq\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|=\|\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi_{2}\|
. Similarly we can show that
\|\varphi_{n}\|=\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This gives us
\|\varphi\|_{\mbox{cb}}=\|\varphi(1,1,1,\ldots,1)\|
.
This completes the proof.
∎

On the simplicity of the tensor product of two simple modules of quantum affine algebras
Léa Bittmann, Jian-Rong Li

Abstract
. Lapid and Mínguez gave a criterion for the irreducibility of the parabolic induction σ × π \sigma\times\pi , where σ \sigma is a ladder representation and π \pi is an arbitrary irreducible representation of the general linear group over a non-archimedean field. Through quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality, when k k is large enough, this gives a criterion for the irreducibility of the tensor product of a snake module and any simple representation of the quantum affine algebra U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 k ^ ) U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}}) . The goal of this paper is to add conditions to their criterion such that it works for any k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We prove the criterion in the case where both modules are snake modules or one of them is a fundamental module at an extremity node and the other is any simple module. We also defined a similar criterion in the Grassmannian cluster algebra ℂ [ Gr ( k , n , ∼ ) ] {\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)] , and show that for any k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , two ladders are compatible if and only if the corresponding tableaux satisfy the criterion. This generalizes Leclerc and Zelevinsky’s result that two Plücker coordinates are compatible if and only if they are weakly separated.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra,
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be not a root of unity and consider
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
, the corresponding quantum affine algebra. The quantum affine algebra being a Hopf algebra, its category
\mathscr{C}
of finite-dimensional representations is a tensor category. The simple finite-dimensional representations were classified by Chari-Pressley
[
CP94
]
, they are highest (
l
-)weight representations, with weight in a certain monoid. However, some important questions about the category
\mathscr{C}
remain open. For example, it is not known, in general, whether the tensor product
L(M)\otimes L(N)
of two irreducible finite-dimensional representations is also irreducible. Additionally, for questions related to the cluster algebra structure of the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathscr{C})
(see
[
HL10
,
HL16
]
), it is important to know which irreducible representations
L
are
real
, i.e. its tensor square
L(M)\otimes L(M)
is irreducible.

In theory, one can use the
q
-characters, a refinement of the characters introduced by Frenkel-Reshetikhin
[
FR98
]
, to answer this question. Indeed, given two simple
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
-modules
L(M)
,
L(N)
, their tensor product is simple is and only if their
q
-characters satisfy
\chi_{q}(L(M)\otimes L(N))=\chi_{q}(L(M))\chi_{q}(L(N))=\chi_{q}(L(MN))
. In practice, there is no general formula for computing the
q
-characters of all simple representations. There exists an algorithm established by Frenkel-Mukhin
[
FM01
]
to compute the
q
-character
\chi_{q}(L(M))
starting with the highest weight
M
, but it is known to fail in some cases.

In this paper, we focus on the case of a type
A
quantum affine algebra, that is
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{k}
.
By quantum affine Schur Weyl duality (see
[
CP96b
]
, and the review
[
Gur21b
]
), when
k>N
, the representation theory of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}})
is equivalent to that of the general linear group over a non-archimedean field
GL_{N}(F)
, and this equivalence is
monoidal
: the tensor product is translated into the parabolic induction.

In
[
LM16
]
, Lapid-Mínguez considered a certain class of representations called
ladders
, whose equivalence under the quantum affine Schur Weyl duality are studied under the name
snake
modules (see
[
MY12
]
). They established a combinatorial criterion for the irreducibility of the parabolic induction
\sigma\times\pi
, where
\sigma
is a ladder representation and
\pi
is an arbitrary irreducible representation of
GL_{N}(F)
. Thus, through quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality, when
k
is large enough, Lapid and Mínguez’s criterion gives a criterion of the simplicity of the tensor product of a snake module and any simple module of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}})
.
The goal of this paper is to add conditions to Lapid and Mínguez’s criterion to obtain a criterion which works for any
k\geq 1
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.10
, Proposition
6.11
)
.

Let
L(M),L(M^{\prime})
be simple
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}})
-modules. Assume either
L(M^{\prime})
is a fundamental representation at an extremal node, or both
L(M)
and
L(M^{\prime})
are snake modules. Then the tensor product
L(M)\otimes L(M^{\prime})
is irreducible if and only if the highest weights
M,M^{\prime}
satisfy two combinatorial conditions
{\rm LC}_{k}({\bf m}_{M},{\bf m}_{M^{\prime}})
and
{\rm LC}_{k}({\bf m}_{M^{\prime}},{\bf m}_{M})
.

In order to prove this result, we can still make use of the quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality. More precisely, we use the equivalences of categories between the category
\mathscr{C}
of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}})
-modules and the categories
\mathcal{C}^{G_{N}}
of finite-length
GL_{N}(F)
representations, for
k>N
. We prove an isomorphism of rings between the Grothendieck ring of
\mathscr{C}
and a quotient of the direct sum of the Grothendieck rings
\mathcal{C}^{G_{N}}
(Proposition
3.11
).

An important ingredient of the proof comes from the results of Maxim Gurevich
[
Gur21
]
, who gave an algorithm to compute the decomposition of a tensor product of any two ladder representations into irreducible representations.

Additionally, Hernandez and Leclerc
[
HL10
]
showed that in the case of a quantum affine algebra of type
A
, there is an isomorphism between the cluster algebra structure of the Grothendieck ring of certain subcategories
\mathscr{C}_{\ell}
of
\mathscr{C}
and some Grassmannian cluster algebras. More precisely, there is an isomorphism between

and
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)]
, where

and
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)]
is the quotient of
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)]
by the ideal generated by

,

, where

is a Plücker coordinate.

It is shown in
[

]
that the dual canonical basis of

is parametrized by some rectangular semistandard Young tableaux. In particular, cluster monomials in

are of the form

]

The result of Theorem

translates to the context of the Grassmannian cluster algebra. We obtain a combinatorial criterion on the ladders tableaux

equivalent to the condition

(Corollary

[

]
stating that any two Plücker coordinates are compatible if and only if they are weakly separated .

Note that by adopting the approach of
[

]
, our results can also be applied to quantum affine superalgebras. Then

should be a skew representation while

needs to be replaced by any simple module that is a subquotient of tensor products of evaluation vector representations.

2.1.
Quantum affine algebras

Let

\begin{array}[]{cc}k_{i}k_{i}^{-}=k_{i}^{-}k_{i}=1,\quad k_{i}k_{j}=k_{j}k_{i},&k_{i}x_{j}^{\pm}k_{i}^{-1}=q^{\pm r_{i}C_{ij}}x_{j}^{\pm},\\
{[}x_{i}^{+},x_{j}^{-}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{k_{i}-k_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},&\sum_{r=0}^{1-C_{ij}}(-1)^{r}{\binom{1-C_{ij}}{r}}_{q}(x_{i}^{\pm})^{r}x_{j}^{\pm}(x_{i}^{\pm})^{1-C_{ij}-r}=0.\end{array}

, uniquely determined up to permutation, such that
\pi
is a subrepresentation of
\rho_{1}\times\cdots\times\rho_{r}
. Denote by
{\rm supp}(\pi)=\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r}\}
(not multiset) and it is called supercuspidal support of
\pi

[
LM16
]
.

For a supercuspidal representation
\rho
, denote
{\rm Irr}_{\rho}=\{\pi\in{\rm Irr}:{\rm supp}(\pi)\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\rho}\}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\rho}=\{\rho\nu_{\rho}^{a}:a\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is the
cuspidal line
of
\rho
. If the lines
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{r}
are distinct, then for
\pi_{i}\in\Irr_{\rho_{i}}
, the representation

\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}

is irreducible. Thus in practice, it is enough to consider representations with support inside a single supercuspidal line. We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
the Serre subcategory of
\bigoplus_{N\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(G_{N}))
of representations whose supercuspidal support is a fixed line
\mathbb{Z}_{\rho}
(since these categories are all equivalent, the choice of the supercuspidal representation
\rho
does not matter), its irreducible representations are given by
(\Irr_{\rho}=)\Irr^{\mathbb{Z}}
, which are indexed by the elements of
\mathrm{Mult}
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the Grothendieck ring of this category, it is freely generated, as a commutative ring, by the images of the
{\rm Z}(\Delta)
, for all
\Delta\in\mathrm{Seg}
.

There are two orders on segments in a multisegment: the
left
and
right aligned
orders
\leq_{b}
and
\leq_{e}
. For segments
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
, we have
\Delta\leq_{b}\Delta^{\prime}
if
b(\Delta)\leq b(\Delta^{\prime})
, and either
b(\Delta)<b(\Delta^{\prime})
or
e(\Delta)\leq e(\Delta^{\prime})
. We have
\Delta\leq_{e}\Delta^{\prime}
if
e(\Delta)\leq e(\Delta^{\prime})
, and either
e(\Delta)<e(\Delta^{\prime})
or
b(\Delta)\leq b(\Delta^{\prime})
.

Note that if the segments
\Delta_{i}
of a multisegment
{\bf m}
are ordered decreasingly for either the left or right aligned order, then the mutlisegment
{\bf m}
is ordered. Thus any multisegment can be ordered.

3.
Quantum Affine Schur-Weyl duality

The correspondence between finite dimensional representations of quantum affine algebras of type
A
and finite length representations of
p
-adic general linear groups can be formalized using quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality.

3.1.
The functor
{\mathcal{F}}

The quantum Schur-Weyl duality was introduced by Jimbo in
[
Jim86
]
. Let
V={\mathbb{C}}^{k}
be the natural representation of the quantum algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k})
. Then for all
N\geq 1
, there exists a left action of the Hecke algebra
H_{N}(q^{2})
on
V^{\otimes N}
which commutes with the action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k})
. For
M
a right
H_{N}(q^{2})
-module, one can equip
M\otimes_{H_{N}(q^{2})}V^{\otimes N}
with a left
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k})
-module structure. Moreover, if
N<k
, the functor

(3.1)

M\to M\otimes_{H_{N}(q^{2})}V^{\otimes N}

is an equivalence of categories between the category of finite-dimensional
H_{N}(q^{2})
-modules and the category of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{k})
-modules of level
N
.

In
[
CP96b
]
Chari-Pressley extended this result to the affine case. They showed that if
M
is a right module for the affine Hecke algebra
\hat{H}_{n}(q^{2})
, then the image of (
3.1
) can be endowed with a left
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-module structure. Thus defining a functor between the category of finite-dimensional modules for the affine Hecke algebra
\hat{H}_{N}(q^{2})
the category
\mathscr{C}^{N}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}}
of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules of level
N
. If
N<k
, this functor is again an equivalence of categories.

On the other hand, by a result of Heiermann
[
H11
]
(see also
[
Bor76
]

[
Cas80
]
,
[
BK98
]
,
[
SS12
]
or the survey paper
[
Gur21b
]
), there exists an equivalence of categories between smooth, finite-length representations of
GL_{N}(F)
in a simple Bernstein block, and the category of finite-dimensional right
\hat{H}_{n}(q^{2})
-modules.

Thus, for
k>N
, after reduction to cuspidal lines in the
p
-adic case, and restriction of the quantum parameters for representations of the quantum affine algebra, we obtain equivalences of categories:

(3.2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{k,N}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{N})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{C}^{N,\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}}.

By summing over
N
, we get a functor
{\mathcal{F}}
:

(3.3)

{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\bigoplus_{N\geq 0}\mathscr{C}^{N,{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}}\quad=:\mathscr{C}_{k}.

We recall some properties of the functor
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

The functor
{\mathcal{F}}
is a tensor functor between the tensor category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and the tensor category
\mathscr{C}_{k}
. That is, for any pair of representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
,

{\mathcal{F}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})\cong{\mathcal{F}}(\pi_{1})\otimes{\mathcal{F}}(\pi_{2}).

(2)

The functor
{\mathcal{F}}
is exact.

(3)

The functor
{\mathcal{F}}
send simple modules to simple modules.
For a multisegment
{\bf m}\in\mathrm{Mult}
,
{\mathcal{F}}({\rm Z}({\bf m}))\cong L(M_{\bf m})
, where the factors in the monomial are obtained from the segments of
{\bf m}
by the following correspondence:

(3.4)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{[}a,b]&\mapsto&Y_{b-a+1,-a-b},\\
{[}\frac{1-i-p}{2},\frac{i-p-1}{2}]&\mapsfrom&Y_{i,p}\end{array}

In particular, for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the image of the segment
[a,a]
is a fundamental representation

{\mathcal{F}}({\rm Z}([a,a]))\cong L(Y_{1,-2a}).

3.2.
Quotient and localization of category

3.4.
Quotients of Grothendieck rings

In this section, we make analog considerations as in section

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
be the ideal of the Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{R}
generated by the classes of the segments representations
[{\rm Z}([a,a+m])]
, with
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
m\geq k
, and by the
[Z_{a}]-1
, for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then let
\mathcal{R}_{k}
be the quotient:

\mathcal{R}_{k}:=\mathcal{R}/{\mathcal{I}}_{k}.

Remark 3.8
.

It is expected, in line with Conjecture
3.6
, that
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is the Grothendieck ring of the category
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.

The following provides some information on the quotient ring
\mathcal{R}_{k}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

For
{\bf m}=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\cdots+\Delta_{N}
a multisegment, its image in the quotient ring
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is

(3.11)

[{\rm Z}({\bf m})]_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\text{ if }{\bf m}\notin\mathrm{Mult}_{k+1},\\
{\rm Z}(\overline{{\bf m}}^{k})&\text{ else,}\end{array}\right.

where
\overline{{\bf m}}^{k}=\{\Delta_{i}\in{\bf m}\mid\ell(\Delta_{i})<k\}
.

This can be proven outside the context of the quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality, using know properties on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We first need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{N},\\
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{N}\end{array}\right.
be two
N
-tuples of integers. Let
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
be permutations in
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, such that
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
whenever
a_{i}=a_{i+1}
and
\sigma(i)^{-1}<\sigma(i+1)^{-1}
whenever
b_{i}=b_{i+1}
.

Then if
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma
(for the Bruhat order),

\max\left\{b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\}\geq\max\left\{b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\},

with equality if and only if
\sigma^{\prime}(i_{0})=\sigma(i_{0})
, for all
i_{0}
such that
b_{\sigma(i_{0})}-a_{i_{0}}=\max\left\{b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i}\right\}
.

Proof.

If
\sigma=\id
, then the result is trivial. Otherwise, consider
i<j
such that
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)
, and let
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma\cdot(ij)
. Then
b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}=b_{\sigma(j)}-a_{i}>b_{\sigma(j)}-a_{j}
, as
a_{j}>a_{i}
by the condition on
\sigma
. On the other hand,
b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}>b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i}
, as
b_{\sigma(j)}>b_{\sigma(i)}
, by the condition on
\sigma
. Thus

\displaystyle b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}>\max\left(b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i},b_{\sigma(j)}-a_{j}\right).

Similarly, we have
b_{\sigma^{\prime}(j)}-a_{j}<\min\left(b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i},b_{\sigma(j)}-a_{j}\right)
.

Thus if neither
i
nor
j
are indexes where the maximal difference is reached,

\max\left\{b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\}=\max\left\{b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\}.

Otherwise,

\max\left\{b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\}>\max\left\{b_{\sigma(i)}-a_{i}\mid i\in[N]\right\}.

The general result follows by transitivity of the Bruhat order.
∎

We now prove Proposition
3.9
.

Proof.

As in
[
LM18
]
, consider two
N
-tuples of integers
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{N}
and
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{N}
, and define
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
such that

{\bf m}=[a_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},b_{1}]+[a_{\sigma^{-1}(2)},b_{2}]+\cdots+[a_{\sigma^{-1}(N)},b_{N}],

and satisfying
\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
whenever
a_{i}=a_{i+1}
and
\sigma(i)^{-1}<\sigma(i+1)^{-1}
whenever
b_{i}=b_{i+1}
. Then by
[
LM18
, Corollary 10.1]

(3.12)

{\rm Z}({\bf m})=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma}\sgn(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)P_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma}(1)\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\rm Z}([a_{\sigma^{\prime-1}(i)},b_{i}])\quad\in\mathcal{R},

where
P_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma}
denotes the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial with respect to
\mathcal{S}_{N}
.

Suppose now there exists
i\in[N]
such that
b_{i}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\geq k
(that is
\ell(\Delta_{i})\geq k+1
). Then by Lemma
3.10
, for all
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma
, there exists
j\in[N]
such that
b_{j}-a_{\sigma^{\prime-1}(j)}\geq k
. Hence by (
3.12
),
[{\rm Z}({\bf m})]_{k}=0
.

Suppose now that
\max\{e(\Delta_{i})-b(\Delta_{i})\}<k
and let
J=\left\{i\in[N]\mid e(\Delta_{i})-b(\Delta_{i})=k-1\right\}
. Then from Lemma
3.10
, in the image of (
3.12
) in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
, the terms such that
\sigma^{\prime}(i)\neq\sigma(i)
for at least one

are sent to 0. The result is

[{\rm Z}({\bf m})]_{k}=\sum_{\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime}\leq\tilde{\sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{[N]\setminus J}}P_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma}(1)\prod_{i\in[N]\setminus J}{\rm Z}([a_{\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime-1}(i)},b_{i}]),

where
\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime}
denote the ”flattening” of the permutations
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
, when one has removed all

.

From
[
BW
, Lemma 17]
, we know that for
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
such that
\sigma^{\prime}(i)=\sigma(i)
for all
i\in J
, then
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma
if and only if
\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime}\leq\tilde{\sigma}
. If we consider
\Delta(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma)=\left\{i\in[N]\mid\exists j,\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma^{\prime}(ij)\leq\sigma\right\}
, then from Lemma
3.10
,
J\cap\Delta(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma)=\emptyset
. Thus, using
[

, we know that
P_{\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime},\tilde{\sigma}}=P_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma}
. Thus
[{\rm Z}({\bf m})]_{k}=[{\rm Z}(\overline{{\bf m}}^{k})]_{k}
.
∎

Now consider the quotient ring:

Proof.

Using Proposition

We now use the cluster algebra structure of

Proposition 5.4
.

Let

such that

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=q+r,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=p+i+r-q.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

are weakly separated.

Proof.

Recall that the segments corresponding to

and

are respectively

Suppose first that

. On the one side, both inequalities (

) and (

, the tensor product

is irreducible. On the other side, the segments

and

have the same mid point

4.5
, we deduce that necessarily the one column tableaux corresponding to

and

are weakly separated, which proves the result in this case.

Assume now without loss of generality that

. From Proposition

, the one column tableaux corresponding to

and

are not weakly separated if and only if the segments

and

.

One can easily see that the four inequalities

),(
5.4
) (knowing that

∎

5.2.
Fundamental representations as socles

In the Zelevinsky classification, simple modules corresponding to segments are defined as the socles of an inductive product of characters. We prove the quantum affine analog of this construction: fundamental representations of the quantum affine algebra can be defined as the socle of a tensor product of fundamental representations at node 1 (of the form
L(Y_{1,k})
).

Proposition 5.5
.

For all
(i,p)\in\hat{I}
,
L(Y_{i,p})
is isomorphic to the unique maximal submodule of

(5.8)

L(Y_{1,p+i-1})\otimes L(Y_{1,p+i-3})\otimes\cdots\otimes L(Y_{1,p-i+1}).

Proof.

Let
W
be the tensor product (
5.8
),
W:=\overrightarrow{\bigotimes}_{m=1}^{i}L(Y_{i,p+i-2m+1})
.From Theorem
5.3
, we know that
W
is a cyclic module, generated by the tensor product of the highest weight vectors of its factors, hence it has a unique maximal submodule and a unique simple quotient.

Let us show by induction on
i
that its unique maximal submodule is
L(Y_{i,p})
. The result is trivial for
i=1
.

By induction, the unique maximal submodule of
L(Y_{1,p+i-1})\otimes L(Y_{1,p+i-3})\otimes\cdots\otimes L(Y_{1,p-i+3})
is
L(Y_{i-1,p+1})
. Consider
L(Y_{i-1,p+1})\otimes L(Y_{1,p-i+1})
. By Proposition
5.4
, this tensor product is of length 2 and its unique maximal submodule is
L(Y_{i,p})
. Thus
L(Y_{i,p})
is the unique maximal submodule of
W
.

∎

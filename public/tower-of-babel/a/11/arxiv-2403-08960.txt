Gromoll–Meyer’s actions and the geometry of (exotic) spacetimes
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Lino Grama

Abstract
. Since the advent of new pairwise non-diffeomorphic structures on smooth manifolds, it has been questioned whether two topologically identical manifolds could admit different geometries. Not surprisingly, physicists have wondered whether a smooth structure assumption different from some classical known models could produce different physical meanings. Motivated by the works [ Wit85 , BL91 ] , [ Bra94 , Sła96 ] , in this paper, we inaugurate a very computational manner to produce physical models on classical and exotic spheres that can be built equivariantly, such as the classical Gromoll–Meyer exotic spheres. As first applications, we produce Lorentzian metrics on homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic manifolds that enjoy the same physical properties, such as geodesic completeness, positive Ricci curvature, and compatible time orientation. These constructions can be pulled back to higher models, such as exotic ten spheres bounding spin manifolds, to be approached in forthcoming papers.

A connected semi-Riemannian manifold

2.2.2.
Computational aspects

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a Lorentzian manifold. Then, just as in the Riemannian case, it is possible to consider the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
g
. Moreover, for such a connection is well defined, the Riemann curvature tensor

for every vector fields

in

From the Riemannian curvature tensor

However, the sectional curvature of

if, and only if,

is non-degenerated: It has a positive or negative area. If

then
|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}-\textsl{g}(X,Y)^{2}\neq 0
.

5.1.
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-Fat bundles and a dual leaf type result

We recall that for a general (non-necessarily metric) submersion, the
fatness
condition is an intrinsic property of its horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. Namely, a submersion
\pi:M\rightarrow B
is said to be
fat
if, for every non-zero
X\in\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{V}=[\widetilde{X},\mathcal{H}]^{\mathbf{v}}
for some horizontal extension of
X
, where we denote by
\mathcal{V}
the vertical distribution associated to
\pi
. We observe that often in the literature, the nomenclature for fatness is widely employed, assuming the preexistence of a Riemannian metric with totally geodesic leaves.

Treating the fatness condition for
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-principal bundles is quite different from other structure groups simply due to the fact
\mathfrak{s}^{1}
is the (1
\mathbb{R}
-dimensional) Abelian Lie algebra generated by
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. In this manner, any connection form
\omega
on the bundle and its corresponding curvature form
\Omega
are nothing but ordinary
1
,
2
-forms, respectively, where
d\omega=\Omega
.

It can be inferred from Chern-Weil theory (
[
Zil00
, Corollary 2.11, p. 10]
) that a principal
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-bundle is fat if, and only if the curvature form
\Omega
is a pullback of some symplectic form on the base
B
: Fat
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-principal bundles are (bi)univocally associated with symplectic manifolds.

We recall the following examples of
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-fat Riemannian submersions (see
[
Zil00
]
)

(a)

the Hopf bundles
\mathrm{S}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{S}^{2n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{n}

(b)

any
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-principal Riemannian bundle
(M,\textsl{g})\rightarrow(B,\textsl{g}_{B})
with fibers of length
2\pi
such that
\textsl{g}_{B}
is either

(i)

almost Kähler and so
g
is
K
-contact

(ii)

Kähler and so
g
is Sasakian

(iii)

Kähler Einstein and so
g
is Sasakian Einstein

A metric on a smooth manifold
M
is named
K
-contact if it admits a unit Killing field
V
with
\mathrm{sec}_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)=1
for every non-degenerated vertizontal plane
X\wedge V
. We say further that the metric is regular if the integral curves of
V
define a free-circle action, and it is quasi-regular if they define an almost free-circle action. According to
[
Wad75
]
, the latter is equivalent to requiring that all integral curves of
V
are closed. If it is regular,
M\rightarrow B
is a fat Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers.

A metric is called Sasakian if it admits a unit Killing vector field
X
such that
R_{\textsl{g}}(V,X)Y=\textsl{g}(V,Y)X-\textsl{g}(X,Y)V
for every
X,Y\perp V
. As before, the Sasakian structure is called regular if the integral curves of
V
define a free circle action and quasi-regular if they define an almost free circle action.

5.1.1.
Holonomy and Dual Holonomy fields

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Considering the natural decomposition
TM=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}
where
\mathcal{V}
stands for the sub-bundle of
TM
containing the vectors tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, if
c:I\rightarrow M
is a geodesic such that
\dot{c}(s)\in\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}
for any
s\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
a vector field
\nu
such that
\nu(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{c(s)}
for every
s\in I
is named a dual-holonomy field provided if

(34)

\nabla_{\dot{c}}\nu=S_{\dot{c}}\nu-A^{*}_{\dot{c}}\nu.

Analogously, basic horizontal fields along a vertical curve
\gamma
are horizontal solutions of

(35)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X=-A^{*}_{X}\dot{\gamma}-S_{X}(\dot{\gamma}).

In the above equations,
S_{X}
always stands for the second fundamental form of a leaf. The tensor
A^{*}_{X}
is
g
-dual of the O’Neill tensor
A_{X}Y=\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]^{\mathbf{v}}
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
. Under the totally geodesic fibers assumption, both dual-holonomy and holonomy fields along horizontal geodesics and basic horizontal fields
along vertical geodesics are the Jacobi fields induced
by, respectively, local horizontal lifting and holonomy transport.

Moreover,
A^{*}_{X}\dot{\gamma}
is basic along the geodesic
\exp(sV)
, where
V\in\mathcal{V}
and
X\in\mathcal{H}
is basic. If
g
has non-negative sectional curvature, then for each leaf
L_{p}\in\mathcal{H}
,

T_{p}L_{p}^{\#}\cap\mathcal{V}_{p}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{A_{X}Y(p):X,Y\in\mathcal{H}_{p}\},

where
L_{p}^{\#}
is a dual leaf through
p
:

L^{\#}_{p}:=\{q\in M:\text{there exists a piecewise smooth horizontal curve from $p$ to $q$}\},

as described in
[
Wil07
]
.

Suppose that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by the connected components of the fibers of a Riemannian submersion
\pi:M\rightarrow B
. Recall that the
holonomy group
at some
b\in B
is defined considering: Fixed
b\in B
, consider all simple closed curves in
B
based at
b
. We can define a diffeomorphism of the fiber
F=\pi^{-1}(b)
over
b
by lifting the curve horizontally to points of
F
. The collection of all of these diffeomorphisms forms an appropriate composition rule. Such a group is trivial in the case of a Riemannian product
F\times B\rightarrow B
. Generally, the holonomy group is not a Lie group, but it certainly is for
Homogeneous bundles
. This is precisely the case for fat Riemannian submersions.

For many foliations, such as the Riemannian foliation induced by the isometric action of a compact connected Lie group, it is known that the dual-foliation is a
Singular Riemannian Foliation
(see
[
Wil07
]
). A crucial point to the verification of this is the usage of completeness of each dual leaf.

On the other hand, it is known (for instance, via the now classic example of J. Wolf (
[
Wol64
, Corollary 11.1]
)) that even under very high symmetrical conditions, a Lorentzian manifold needs not to be geodesically complete. We can show, however, that the family of compact Riemannian manifolds with almost semi-free
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-actions, when regarded with the metric
\widetilde{\textsl{g}}_{-r^{2}}
given by Theorem
5.1
, is time-like and space-like complete. In particular, we show that the dual leaf foliation induced by a fixed horizontal distribution has complete leaves for
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-fat foliations. Indeed, the dual foliation has a single leaf, which coincides with
M
. This was already observed for Riemannian metrics in
[
CG23
]
, so we show it works as well for Lorentzian metrics induced by circle actions.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathrm{S}^{1}\rightarrow M\rightarrow B
be a principal
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-fat bundle with connection form
\omega
. If
g
is the unique (up to scaling) connection metric
g
on
M
, any (semi) Riemannian dual foliation for the Lorentzian metric
\widetilde{\textsl{g}}_{-r^{2}}
(for
r>1),
has only one leaf, which coincides with
M
. Moreover, if the action is almost semi-free, the conclusion holds in the principal stratum
M^{reg}
.

Proof.

For the proof, note that if
g
is the unique (up to scaling) connection metric on
\mathrm{S}^{1}\rightarrow M\rightarrow B
and
A:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
is the O’Neill tensor, denoting by
A^{*}_{X}
the
g
-dual to
A_{X}
for any fixed
X\in\mathcal{H}
one has that
{A^{*}_{-r^{2}}}_{X}V=(1-r^{2})^{-1}A^{*}_{X}V
, where
{A^{*}_{-r^{2}}}_{X}
is the
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
-dual tensor to
A_{X}
. In particular, since the orbits of
\mathrm{S},^{1}
are totally geodesic for
g
, these are totally geodesic for
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
as well. So
\mathrm{sec}_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(X,V)=-(1-r^{2})^{-1}|A^{*}_{X}V|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}
for any
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
-orthonormal vertizontal plane. In this manner, the fatness condition ensures that
\mathrm{sec}_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(X,V)<0
.

We claim that each dual leaf is open. Indeed, since the dual foliation only depends on the choice of a horizontal distribution, we take
\mathcal{H}
as the horizontal distribution given by the
g
-orthogonal complement of the Riemannian foliation induced by the orbits of
\mathrm{S}^{1}
. Now take a point
x\in L_{x}^{\#}
, where
L_{x}^{\#}
is the dual leaf through
x
. The fatness condition ensures that any given
V\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
has a constant norm along any horizontal geodesic emanating from

. This follows easily from equation (11) in
[
Spe18
]
: Since

then any dual holonomy

field along the geodesic

has constant norm, since

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}|\nu|^{2}<\mathrm{sec}_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)

implies that

for

. If

then
|\nu|^{2}\rightarrow\infty
for

(if
\lambda>0
) or
-t\nearrow\infty
(if
\lambda<0
). Both cases contradict the compactness of
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, since every compact structure group enjoys the property
|\nu(t)|\leq L|\nu(0)|
for some
L>0
, see Definition 1.3 in
[
Spe18
]
. The claim holds since then the dimension of
T_{c(t)}L_{c(t)}^{\#}\cap\mathcal{V}_{c(t)}
is constant and so
\dim M=\dim L_{x}^{\#}
. ∎

5.2.
A general theorem for Lorentzian manifolds with positive Ricci curvature metrics

We finish this section proving that our manifolds in this paper have positive Ricci curvature—the same holding for the product space that furnishes the considered Lorentzian metrics via Cheeger deformations.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with non-negative sectional curvature. Assume a free isometric
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-action on
M
exists. Then for any
r>1
, the Lorentzian metric
\mathrm{g}_{-r^{2}}
has positive Ricci curvature, the same holding for the Lorentzian manifold
(M\times\mathrm{S}^{1},\textsl{g}-r^{-2}Q)
, where
Q
is the standard bi-invariant metric on
\mathfrak{s}^{1}
.

If the
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-action is almost semi-free, there exists
r^{2}
sufficiently large such that the metric
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
has positive Ricci curvature in
M
. In particular, the metric
\widetilde{\textsl{g}}_{-r^{2}}
given by Theorem
5.1
has positive Ricci curvature.

Proof.

The proof is a generalization of the proof of Theorem
3.3
. We take
\mathrm{S}^{1}
to be totally geodesic for
g
, and so the orbit tensor
P:\mathfrak{m}_{x}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}_{x}
, for every
x\in M
, can be taken equal to the identity, where we decompose

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}_{x}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{x}
orthogonaly with respect to any fixed bi-invariant metric induced from
\mathrm{Iso}(\textsl{g})
. If the
G
-action is free, then
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\cong\mathfrak{g}
for every
x\in M
. Otherwise, in the presence of fixed points
\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\mathfrak{g}
for every fixed point
x
and
P
collapses to the
0
-operator along any geodesic coming from the principal stratum: see Lemma
4
.

We compute the Ricci curvature of the metric
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
. With this aim, we write any vector
\overline{X}\in T_{x}M
as
\overline{X}=X+U^{*}
for
U\in\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}
where the superscript
\ast
reinforces that
U^{*}
is an action vector at
x\in M
. Taking
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
as a
g
-orthonormal basis to
\mathcal{H}_{x}
and
\{e_{n}\}\in\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}
is a unit vector, one gets that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}\cup\left\{\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n}\right\}
is a
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}
-orthogonal basis to
T_{x}M
such that
\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}(\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n},\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n})=-1
. We have added the
\sqrt{-1}
multiplying factor since we are considering
r^{2}-1>0
. So
(1-r^{2})^{1/2}
is a complex number. Therefore, just as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, this choice of basis works as a simple way to write the needed basis explicitly.

Now the needed unreduced sectional curvature can be easily computed for any fixed
\overline{X}\in T_{x}M
as for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}

\displaystyle K_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(\overline{X},e_{i})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-r^{2}}((1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X},e_{i})

\displaystyle=K_{\textsl{g}}((1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X},e_{i})-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}

\displaystyle=K_{\textsl{g}}(X,e_{i})+(1-r^{2})^{-2}K_{\textsl{g}}(U^{*},e_{i})+2(1-r^{2})^{-1}R_{\textsl{g}}(e_{1},X,U^{*},e_{i})-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}

\displaystyle=K_{\textsl{g}}(X,e_{i})+(1-r^{2})^{-2}|A^{*}_{e_{i}}U^{*}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}

where we have used the ’vertizontal’ curvature under totally geodesic fibers is given by
|A^{*}_{e_{i}}U^{*}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}
and that the mixed terms curvature component
R_{\textsl{g}}(e_{1},X,U^{*},e_{i})
vanishes.

Moreover,

\displaystyle K_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(\overline{X},\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-r^{2}}((1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X},\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{-1/2}e_{n})

\displaystyle=K_{\textsl{g}}((1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X},\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{-1/2}e_{n})-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(1-r^{2})^{-1}K_{\textsl{g}}(X,e_{n})-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(1-r^{2})^{-1}|A^{*}_{X}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}|\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}.

The equation on the last line is obtained again due to the vanishing of mixed curvature terms and the fact that the fiber is one-dimensional. This also implies that

\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n}=0.

Just observe that the possible non-vanishing term is
\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{-1/2}\nabla_{X}e_{n}
which coincides with (a multiple of) the shape operator of the fibers, that is
0
due to the totally geodesic assumption. Hence,

(36)

K_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(\overline{X},\sqrt{-1}(1-r^{2})^{1/2}e_{n})=-(1-r^{2})^{-1}|A^{*}_{X}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}.

The same argument to deal with
\nabla_{(1-r^{2})^{-1}\overline{X}}e_{i}
can be employed ensuring that

\nabla_{X+(1-r^{2})^{-1}U^{*}}e_{i}=\nabla_{X}e_{i}=A_{X}e_{i}.

Therefore,

\mathrm{Ric}_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(\overline{X})=\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbf{h}}(X)+(1-r^{2})^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|A^{*}_{e_{i}}U^{*}|^{2}_{\textsl{g}}-3r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|A_{X}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}\\
-(1-r^{2})^{-1}|A^{*}_{X}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}.

Since
\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbf{h}}(X)=\mathrm{Ric}_{M/G}-3\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|A_{X}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}
we have

\mathrm{Ric}_{\textsl{g}_{-r^{2}}}(\overline{X})=\mathrm{Ric}_{M/G}(X)+(1-r^{2})^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|A^{*}_{e_{i}}U^{*}|^{2}_{\textsl{g}}-3(1-r^{2})^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|A_{X}e_{i}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}\\
-(1-r^{2})^{-1}|A^{*}_{X}e_{n}|_{\textsl{g}}^{2}\geq 0.

We prove that
\textsl{g}-r^{-2}Q
has positive Ricci curvature. With this aim, we observe that the vertical space associated with the
\star
-acion defined by equation (

) on

has as vertical space at

Moreover, its
\textsl{g}-r^{-2}Q
-orthogonal complement
\mathcal{H}^{\star}_{(x,q)}
can be described as

\mathcal{H}^{\star}_{(x,q)}=\left\{(X+r^{-2}W^{*},W):X\in\mathcal{H}_{x},~W\in\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Observe that if one takes
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
to be a

-orthonormal set spanning
\mathcal{H}_{x}
and a unit vector

(for

) then the following constitute a desired basis for computing the Ricci curvature of

:

(37)

\left\{\left(e_{i}+r^{-2}e_{n}^{*},e_{n}\right):i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\right\}\cup\left\{\left((1-r^{-2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{n}^{*},(1-r^{-2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{n})\right)\right\}.

Using that any tangent vector to

can be written as

\widetilde{X}:=\left(X+r^{-2}W^{*}+U^{*},W+U\right)
we compute

\displaystyle K_{\textsl{g}-r^{-2}}\left(\widetilde{X},\left(e_{i}+r^{-2}e_{n}^{*},e_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=K_{\textsl{g}}\left(X+r^{-2}W^{*}+U^{*},e_{i}+r^{-2}e_{n}^{*}\right),

\displaystyle i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}

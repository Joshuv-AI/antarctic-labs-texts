Projected transverse momentum resummation in top-antitop pair production at LHC
Wan-Li Ju, Marek Schönherr

Abstract
The transverse momentum distribution of the t ​ t ¯ {t{\bar{t}}} system is of both experimental and theoretical interest. In the presence of azimuthally asymmetric divergences, pursuing resummation at high logarithmic precision is rather demanding in general. In this paper, we propose the projected transverse momentum spectrum d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ q τ {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\tau} , which is derived from the classical q → T \vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}} spectrum by integrating out the rejection component q τ ⟂ q_{\tau_{\perp}} with respect to a reference unit vector τ → \vec{\tau} , to serve as an alternative solution to remove these asymmetric divergences, in addition to the azimuthally averaged case d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ | q → T | {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}|\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}| . In the context of the effective field theories, SCET II and HQET, we will demonstrate that in spite of the q τ ⟂ q_{\tau_{\perp}} integrations, the leading asymptotic terms of d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ q τ {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\tau} still observe the factorisation pattern in terms of the hard, beam, and soft functions in the vicinity of q τ = 0 q_{\tau}=0 GeV. Then, with the help of the renormalisation group equation techniques, we carry out the resummation at NLL+NLO , N 2 LL+N 2 LO , and approximate N 2 LL ′ +N 2 LO accuracy on three observables of interest, d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ q T , in {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\mathrm{T,in}} , d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ q T , out {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\mathrm{T,out}} , and d ​ σ t ​ t ¯ / d ​ Δ ​ ϕ t ​ t ¯ {\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}\Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}} , within the domain M t ​ t ¯ ≥ 400 M_{t\bar{t}}\geq 400~ GeV. The first two cases are obtained by choosing τ → \vec{\tau} parallel and perpendicular to the top quark transverse momentum, respectively. The azimuthal de-correlation Δ ​ ϕ t ​ t ¯ \Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}} of the t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} pair is evaluated through its kinematical connection to q T , out q_{\mathrm{T,out}} . This is the first time the azimuthal spectrum Δ ​ ϕ t ​ t ¯ \Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}} is appraised at or beyond the N 2 LL level including a consistent treatment of both beam collinear and soft radiation.

2.1

Kinematics and the factorised cross section

Figure 1
:

The kinematics on the transverse plane in the laboratory reference frame.

\vec{P}_{t({\bar{t}})}^{\perp}
stands for the transverse momentum
of the (anti-)top quark.

\Delta\Phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}}
is the azimuthal opening angle between
the top and anti-top quarks.

\vec{\tau}
is a unit reference vector in the transverse plane.

We start this section with the elaboration on the kinematics.
As illustrated in Fig.
1
, the main concern of
this work is on the interplay between a reference unit vector

\vec{\tau}
and the transverse momentum
\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}
of the

{t{\bar{t}}}
system.
By means of the reference vector
\vec{\tau}
,
\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}
can be
decomposed into two parts, the projection component

\vec{q}_{\tau_{\|}}
and the rejection one

\vec{q}_{\tau_{\perp}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{q}_{\tau_{\perp}}+\vec{q}_{\tau_{\|}}=q_{\tau_{\perp}}\vec{\tau}\times\vec{n}+q_{\tau_{\|}}\vec{\tau}\,,

(2.1)

where
\vec{n}
is another unit vector pointing to one of beam
directions in the laboratory reference frame.
In the numeric implementation presented in this paper, the
magnitude of the projection
\vec{q}_{\tau_{\|}}
is of
primary interest, which will hereafter be dubbed

q_{\tau}\equiv|\vec{q}_{\tau_{\|}}|
.

The fixed-order calculation on the
q_{\tau}
spectrum can be
realised using the QCD factorisation theorem of
[
110
]
,
that is,

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}^{5}{\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}}{{\rm d}M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{P}_{t}^{\perp}\,{\rm d}Y_{t{\bar{t}}}\,{\rm d}q_{\tau}}\,=\;&\sum_{\mathrm{sign}[P_{t}^{z}]}\frac{1}{16s(2\pi)^{6}}\int{\rm d}^{2}\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}\,\Theta_{\mathrm{kin}}\,\delta\Big[q_{\tau}-|\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\vec{\tau}|\Big]\,\frac{\Sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}{M_{\mathrm{T}}^{t{\bar{t}}}\,|P_{t}^{z}|}\,,\end{split}

(2.2)

where
M_{t{\bar{t}}}
denotes the invariant mass of the
{t{\bar{t}}}
system,
and
s
is the colliding energy.
In this work we will concentrate on
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV throughout.

Y_{t{\bar{t}}}
and
M_{\mathrm{T}}^{t{\bar{t}}}
are for the pseudorapidity
and the transverse mass of the
{t{\bar{t}}}
pair in the laboratory
frame (LF), respectively.

P_{t}^{z}
represents the longitudinal components of the top quark
momentum measured from the
z
-direction rest frame (
z
RF) of the

{t{\bar{t}}}
pair.
The
z
RF can be obtained through boosting the LF along one of the
beam directions until the longitudinal momentum of the
{t{\bar{t}}}

pair has been eliminated.

To perform the integral of
\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}
in Eq. (
2.2
), it is
of essence to establish suitable kinematical boundaries to fulfill
energy-momentum conservation condition.
To this end, we introduce the function
\Theta_{\mathrm{kin}}
to
impose the following constraints,

\begin{split}\Theta_{\mathrm{kin}}\,=\,&\Theta\Big[\sqrt{s}-M_{\mathrm{T}}^{t{\bar{t}}}-|\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}|\Big]\,\Theta\Big[M_{\mathrm{T}}^{t{\bar{t}}}-m_{\mathrm{T}}^{t}-m_{\mathrm{T}}^{{\bar{t}}}\Big]\,\Theta\left[\sinh^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{(M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}+s)^{2}}{4\,s\,{M_{\mathrm{T}}^{t{\bar{t}}}}^{2}}-1}\right)-|Y_{t{\bar{t}}}|\right]\,,\end{split}

(2.3)

where
\Theta[\dots]
is the usual Heaviside function.
Therein,
m_{\mathrm{T}}^{t}
and
m_{\mathrm{T}}^{{\bar{t}}}
are the transverse
masses of the top and anti-top quarks in the LF, respectively.
Finally, Eq. (
2.2
) also entails
{\Sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}
,
encoding the contributions from all partonic processes, defined as,

\begin{split}{\Sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}\,=&\;\sum_{i,j}\int^{1}_{0}\frac{{\rm d}x_{n}}{x_{n}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x_{{\bar{n}}}}{x_{{\bar{n}}}}\,f_{i/N}(x_{n})\,f_{j/\bar{N}}(x_{{\bar{n}}})\,\sum_{r}\,\int\,\prod_{m}^{r}{\rm d}\Phi_{k_{m}}\;(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}\left(p_{i}+p_{j}-p_{t}-p_{\bar{t}}-\sum_{m}k_{m}\right)\\
&\;\times\overline{\sum_{\mathrm{hel,col}}}\left|\mathcal{M}(i+j\to t+\bar{t}+X)\right|^{2}\,.\end{split}

(2.4)

Here
f_{i/N}(x)
is the parton distribution function (PDF) for the
parton
i
within the proton
N
carrying the momentum fraction
x
.

{\rm d}\Phi_{k_{m}}
characterises
the phase space integral of the
m
-th emitted parton, that is,

\begin{split}{\rm d}\Phi_{k_{m}}\,\equiv\,\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k_{m}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\delta(k_{m}^{2})\,\Theta(k_{m}^{0})\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\;\frac{{\rm d}y_{m}}{2\pi}\;\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(2.5)

where
y_{m}
and
\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}
indicate the rapidity and
transverse momentum of the occurring emission, respectively.

|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
is the squared transition amplitude
for the partonic processes of the indices indicated.
Substituting Eq. (
2.4
) into Eq. (
2.2
),
it is ready to perform the fixed-order calculations of the spectra
of
q_{\tau}
.
In the vicinity of
q_{\tau}=0
GeV, however, this perturbative
expansion fails to converge, and
an asymptotic expansion of
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\tau}

can be carried out in the small parameter

\lambda_{\tau}\equiv q_{\tau}/M_{t{\bar{t}}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}}{{\rm d}q_{\tau}}\;\sim\;\sigma^{{t{\bar{t}}}}_{\mathrm{B}}\,\sum_{m,n}\,\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}(M_{t{\bar{t}}})}{4\pi}\right]^{m}\,\left[\underbrace{c^{(0)}_{m,n}\,\frac{\ln^{n}(\lambda_{\tau})}{\lambda_{\tau}}}_{\mathrm{LP}}+\,\underbrace{c^{(1)}_{m,n}\,\ln^{n}(\lambda_{\tau})}_{\mathrm{NLP}}+\,\underbrace{c^{(2)}_{m,n}\,\lambda_{\tau}\ln^{n}(\lambda_{\tau})}_{\mathrm{N}^{2}\mathrm{LP}}+\dots\right]\;,

(2.6)

indicating the leading, next-to-leading and next-to-next-to-leading power
terms in
\lambda_{\tau}
, labelled LP, NLP, and N
2
LP, respectively.
Therein,
\sigma^{{t{\bar{t}}}}_{\mathrm{B}}
is the Born level total
cross section of the process
pp\to{t{\bar{t}}}+X

denotes the strong coupling constant, and

c^{(k)}_{m,n}
represents the coefficient for the asymptotic constituent with the superscript

Thus, conventionally, the leading power terms

c^{(0)}_{m,n}\ln^{n}(\lambda_{\tau})/\lambda_{\tau}

are associated with the most singular behaviors
in the low
q_{\tau}
domain and also the main concern of this work.
It is important to note, however, that also the next-to-leading power
terms,
c^{(1)}_{m,n}\ln^{n}(\lambda_{\tau})

\lambda_{\tau}\to 0

It merits noting that, this ansatz, together with the proposal
of
[

As the first application of the domain completeness, we explore the

From Eq. (

In order to disentangle the influences of those two scales and to
also fulfil the constraints of
[

]

\displaystyle\mathbf{Isotropic}\textrm{-}\mathbf{recoil}:

\displaystyle\;M_{t{\bar{t}}}\gg|\vec{q}_{\tau_{\perp}}|\sim q_{\tau}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Asymmetric}\textrm{-}\mathbf{recoil}:

\displaystyle\;|\vec{q}_{\tau_{\perp}}|\sim M_{t{\bar{t}}}\gg q_{\tau}\,.

(2.8)

To precisely separate both regimes and still cover the complete

\Lambda_{\tau}

non-intersecting intervals read

\Lambda_{\tau}\lesssim|\vec{q}_{\tau_{\perp}}|

However, as demonstrated in
[

Their dependence is thus dropped in the following.
An analogous analysis is also applied to the phase space integral
for the real emissions in Eq. (
2.5

which leads to,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Soft}:\;

\displaystyle\;{y_{m}}\sim 0\,,\quad\quad\quad\quad~|\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}|\sim q_{\tau}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Beam}\textrm{-}\mathbf{collinear}\textrm{-}n:\,

\displaystyle\;{y_{m}}\sim+\ln\lambda_{\tau}\,,\quad\quad|\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}|\sim q_{\tau}\,,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Beam}\textrm{-}\mathbf{collinear}\textrm{-}{\bar{n}}:\;

\displaystyle\;{y_{m}}\sim-\ln\lambda_{\tau}\,,\quad\quad|\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}|\sim q_{\tau}\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Jet}\textrm{-}\mathbf{collinear}\textrm{-}{n_{\mathrm{J}}}:\;

\displaystyle\;{y_{m}}\sim 0\,,\quad\quad\quad\quad~|\vec{k}_{m}^{\perp}|\sim M_{t{\bar{t}}}\,.

3.1

Asymptotic behavior

We start with identifying the singular terms in

Therefore, without loss of generality, we parametrise the

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Sigma}_{{t{\bar{t}}}}\,\sim\,\sum_{m,n}\,\alpha^{m}_{s}(M_{t{\bar{t}}})\;L^{n}_{\mathrm{M}}\,\Big\{s_{m,n}(\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})+a_{m,n}(\mathrm{sign}[b_{\tau_{\|}}],\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})\Big\}\;,

Order by order, there are two dimensionless coefficients,

s_{m,n}
and

.
While both depend on
\beta_{t{\bar{t}}}
,

, and

symmetric

terms (AST) hereafter.
On the other hand, in presence of the helicity-flipping

wide angle soft correlations from

2.38
)-(
2.39

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{{t{\bar{t}}}}

[

67
]

Since the rejection component
b_{\tau_{\perp}}
has been
integrated out in Eq. (
2.42

orientation dependence is reduced to a dependence on
the sign of
b_{\tau_{\|}}
in Eq. (
3.1

Please note, in deriving eq. (
3.1
), we
have set the scales
\mu=\nu=M_{t{\bar{t}}}
in
Eqs. (
2.29
)-(
2.30
) and
then extracted the

terms through

Other scales choices
\{\mu,\nu\}
lead to the same result
after regrouping the dimensional quantities appropriately,
on account of the (rapidity) renormalisation group discussed
in Sec.

From Eq. (

q_{\tau}
spectrum by completing the inverse Fourier
transformation in Eq. (
2.43
).
Exploiting the fact that the function

\cos(b_{\tau_{\|}}q_{\tau})
is even under the integral
over

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}}}{{\rm d}M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{P}_{t}^{\perp}\,{\rm d}Y_{t{\bar{t}}}\,{\rm d}q_{\tau}}\,\sim

\displaystyle\;\sum\limits_{m,n}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}{\rm d}b_{\tau_{\|}}\,\cos(b_{\tau_{\|}}q_{\tau})\,L^{n}_{\mathrm{M}}\,\Big\{s_{m,n}(\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})+a_{m,n}(\mathrm{sign}[b_{\tau_{\|}}],\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})\Big\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\,=

\displaystyle\;\sum\limits_{m,n}\Big\{2s_{m,n}(\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})+a_{m,n}^{+}(\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})+a_{m,n}^{-}(\beta_{t{\bar{t}}},x_{t},Y_{t{\bar{t}}})\Big\}\;\mathcal{F}^{(n)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,,

where

are the decomposition of the AAT components
according to
\mathrm{sign}[b_{\tau_{\|}}]
and the function

\mathcal{F}^{(n)}_{\tau}
is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(n)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})=\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}b_{\tau_{\|}}\,\cos(b_{\tau_{\|}}q_{\tau})\,L^{n}_{\mathrm{M}}\,.

(3.3)

To appraise

, we
follow the strategy of
[

]
and introduce the
generating function,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\tau}(\eta,q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{\eta^{n}}{n!}\,\mathcal{F}^{(n)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=\;\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}{b}_{\tau_{\|}}\,\cos\left({b}_{\tau_{\|}}{q}_{\tau}\right)\,\left(\frac{{b}^{2}_{\tau_{\|}}M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}}\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{4^{-\eta}e^{2\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}\eta}\,\sin(\pi\eta)\,\Gamma[2\eta+1]}{{q}_{\tau}}\,\left(\frac{M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{q^{2}_{\tau}}\right)^{\eta}\,.

(3.4)

\mathcal{F}^{(n)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})

to the

\mathbf{F}_{\tau}(\eta,q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})

with respect to
\eta
at the point
\eta=0
.
The results of the first few ranks read

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=&\;\;0\,,\\
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=&\;-\frac{\pi}{q_{\tau}}\,,\\
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=&\;-\frac{2\pi}{q_{\tau}}\ln\left[\frac{M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{4q_{\tau}^{2}}\right]\,,\\

\mathcal{F}^{(4)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=&\;-\frac{4\pi}{q_{\tau}}\ln^{3}\left[\frac{M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{4q_{\tau}^{2}}\right]\,-\frac{4\pi^{3}}{q_{\tau}}\ln\left[\frac{M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{4q_{\tau}^{2}}\right]\,+\frac{64\pi\zeta_{3}}{q_{\tau}},\\
\vdots\;&\\
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}_{\tau}(q_{\tau},M_{t{\bar{t}}})\,=&\;-\frac{k\pi}{q_{\tau}}\ln^{k-1}\left[\frac{M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}{4q_{\tau}^{2}}\right]+\ldots\,.\end{split}

(3.5)

Here we have focussed on the regime where
q_{\tau}
is small, but
always larger than 0.
Otherwise, contributions such as
\delta[q_{\tau}]
will enter the
expressions above.
From these results, it is observed that the coefficients

and

cannot induce any divergent behaviour by themselves
as exhibited in Eq. (

As a consequence, the resummation of the

spectrum can now be
expressed as an exponentiation of the

the corresponding resummation in the Drell-Yan processes and Higgs
production
[

103

107

108
,
109
,

In Sec.

5

Conclusions

In this paper, we investigated the projected transverse
momentum spectrum
q_{\tau}
, which can be evaluated by
integrating out the rejection components of
\vec{q}_{\mathrm{T}}
with
respect to the reference unit vector
\vec{\tau}
.
We focussed on its singular behaviour in the low
q_{\tau}

domain in particular.

Using the region expansion methods
[
78
,
79
,
80
,
81
]
we resolved
the phase space and loop integrals in terms of the dynamic
regions that capture the asymptotic properties of

{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\tau}
in the vicinity of

q_{\tau}=0
.
It comprises the usual hard, beam-collinear, jet-collinear,
and soft domains.
Then, in the context of the effective field theories,
SCET
II
and HQET, we enumerated the possible configurations constructed
by those momentum modes, and determined the power accuracy
in each case by probing the corresponding factorisation formulae.
It is observed that the leading singular terms of the

q_{\tau}
distribution are governed by the hard, beam-collinear,
and soft regions, akin to the
q_{\mathrm{T}}
spectrum
[
64
,
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
,
74
]
, whilst the higher power regular terms entail
the participations of the jet-collinear regime.

In the subsequent resummation, we focussed on the leading
asymptotic behaviour of
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\tau}
.
By inspecting the corresponding factorisation formula in
impact space, we illustrated that the driving factor of
the singular behaviour in momentum space is the characteristic
logarithm
\ln^{k}[{{b}^{2}_{\tau_{\|}}M_{t{\bar{t}}}^{2}}/{b_{0}^{2}}]
,
for
k\geq 1
, which therefore permitted us to apply the
framework of RGE and RaGE
[
96
,
97
,
98
,
99
]
to accomplish the logarithmic
exponentiation and in turn the resummation.
It is worth emphasizing that in presence of the azimuthal
asymmetric terms, this phenomenon is not general on the process
pp\to{t{\bar{t}}}+X
.
The
q_{\tau}
spectra and the azimuthally averaged case
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\mathrm{T}}
are known to be the only two observables which stem from the double-differential distribution
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\mathrm{T}}
but are free of the azimuthal asymmetric divergences.

4
4

4

It is interesting to note that in a very recent
calculation
[
205
]
on the semi-inclusive
deep-inelastic scattering (SIDIS), the observable

q_{*}
is proposed and also demonstrated to be
promising to cope with the azimuthal asymmetric
divergences.
Within the asymptotic regime, the
q_{*}
spectrum
actually bears a close resemblance to the

q_{\mathrm{T,out}}
distribution used in this
work, up to rapidity-dependent factors.

Although the derivation and findings summarised above
hold for an arbitrary choice of reference vector
\vec{\tau}
,
we focussed on three specific observables to demonstrate
the validity and usefulness of our framework,
q_{\mathrm{T,in}}
,

q_{\mathrm{T,out}}
, and
\Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}}
.
The first two cases are obtained by assigning
\vec{\tau}

to be parallel and perpendicular with respect to the
transverse momentum of the top quark, respectively.
The azimuthal spectrum
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}\Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}}

is extracted from
{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/{\rm d}q_{\mathrm{T,out}}

according to the kinematical relationship in between.
Owing to the independence from azimuthally asymmetric
divergences, we were able to calculate those observables
at
NLL+NLO
,
N
2
LL+N
2
LO
, and approximate
N
2
LL
′
+N
2
LO
(
aN
2
LL
′
+N
2
LO
)
accuracy.
From the numerical results, a manifest perturbative convergence
is observed in the asymptotic regime, i.e. the central values
at the higher precisions are generally contained in the
uncertainty bands of the lower ones and the respective uncertainties
are systematically reduced down to the percent level at

aN
2
LL
′
+N
2
LO
.

During our calculation, we have focussed on the domain

M_{t\bar{t}}\geq 400~
GeV to evade Coulomb divergences
which inevitably manifest themselves in the higher order
corrections.
It is expected that our results can be compared against
the very recent measurement on the double differential spectra

{\rm d}\sigma_{{t{\bar{t}}}}/({\rm d}M_{t{\bar{t}}}\,{\rm d}\Delta\phi_{{t{\bar{t}}}})

[
21
]

within the corresponding
M_{t{\bar{t}}}
slices upon the full publication
of the experimental data.

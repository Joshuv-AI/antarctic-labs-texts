Extracting maximum information from polarised baryon decays via amplitude analysis: the 𝚲 c + → p ​ K − ​ π + {{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+} case
Daniele Marangotto

Abstract




Abstract

We consider what is the maximum information measurable from the decay distributions of polarised baryon decays via amplitude analysis in the helicity formalism. We focus in particular on the analytical study of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay distributions, demonstrating that the full information on its decay amplitudes can be extracted from its distributions, allowing a simultaneous measurement of both helicity amplitudes and the polarisation vector.
This opens the possibility to use the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay for applications ranging from New Physics searches to low-energy QCD studies, in particular its use as absolute polarimeter for the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
baryon.
This result is valid as well for baryon decays having the same spin structure and it is cross-checked numerically by means of a toy amplitude fit with Monte Carlo pseudo-data.

1
Introduction

The study of the complete phase space distributions (
i.e.
the fully differential decay rate) of particle decays via angular or amplitude analysis allows to extract the maximum information about the process, since no integration is performed on the decay degrees of freedom. However, what is this maximum information for a given decay structure? Which parameters describing the phase space distributions can be measured? Indeed, in general, it is not guaranteed that the different functional forms characterising the decay distributions, separable by means of an amplitude fit, yield enough constraints on the parameters describing the decay in a given phenomenological framework. In this article we study the constraints placed by the phase space distributions of baryon decays described in the helicity formalism, showing what information can be obtained under which conditions.

In particular, we focus on the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay, whose amplitude analysis is ongoing at the LHCb experiment
[
1
]
, demonstrating the possibility to extract the full information on its parameters, measuring both helicity amplitudes and the polarisation vector simultaneously, in presence of non-negligible polarisation. Thus, the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay parameters can be considered as physical observables. This result is valid as well for polarised baryon decays having the same spin structure, a first parity-violating decay and a subsequent parity-conserving decay. Since the present article is intended to be a phenomenological study, we will not consider experimental effects: we assume a sufficiently large fit statistics, allowing to effectively separate each phase space dependency, and an adequate description of the invariant mass lineshape functions which parametrise resonant contributions.

The possibility to extract the whole decay amplitude is a remarkable result given the strong interest in the measurement of the associated observables, ranging from New Physics searches to low-energy QCD studies. The full knowledge of the helicity amplitudes characterises each resonant contribution to the decay, both its squared modulus and phase, as well the resonance polarisation. The comparison between observables measured for
C\!P
conjugated decays enables
C\!P
symmetry violation studies for specific contributions or localised in the phase space.
Moreover, the knowledge of a particle decay amplitude allows to add information on its production processes; for instance, the inclusion of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
amplitude model in
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{0}_{b}}\rightarrow{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}l^{-}\bar{\nu}_{l}
angular analyses increases the sensitivity to possible beyond the Standard Model physics contributions
[
2
,
*Shivashankara:2015cta
,
*Li:2016pdv
,
*Datta:2017aue
,
*DiSalvo:2018ngq
,
*Ray:2018hrx
,
*Boer:2019zmp
,
*Penalva:2019rgt
]
.

Considering baryon polarisation, the measurement of its absolute value and direction is essential for a variety of studies. Polarisation measurements for different production mechanisms give precious information on the baryon spin structure and formation process; for heavy baryons they are expected to be closely related to the charm quark polarisation and its originating process
[
10
,
*Falk:1993rf
,
*Galanti:2015pqa
]
. Since the baryon polarisation is difficult to predict in QCD, being related to its non-perturbative regime, such measurements are useful to discriminate among different low energy QCD models.

Focusing on the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
baryon, its main decay channel
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
allows the measurement of its polarisation with the best statistical precision.
Indeed, the two-body decay
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow{\mathchar 28931\relax}\pi^{+}
, which can be used for polarisation measurements since its decay asymmetry parameter is known
[
13
]
, has a lower branching fraction by a factor
\approx 5

[
13
]
and reduced detector reconstruction efficiency because of the large
{\mathchar 28931\relax}
baryon flight distance, especially for fixed-target experiments. Moreover, the use of single resonant components of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay is not feasible because of its complicated decay structure characterised by many overlapping and interfering resonant contributions, making single components hardly to isolate
[
1
]
.

A method to extract the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
polarisation with the best precision is fundamental for the proposed search of charm baryon electromagnetic dipole moments using bent crystals at the LHC
[
14

*Bagli:2017foe
]
, since dipole moments are to be inferred from spin precession.

A measurement of the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
polarisation in the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay mode via amplitude analysis has already been performed by the E791 experiment
[

This study addresses for the first time the question for
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decays.

The decay distribution of different non-leptonic
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}

{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}

2
Formalism

2.1
Polarised Differential Decay Rate

We consider the differential decay rate for polarised particles, see
e.g.
Ref.
[
22
]
for a more complete treatment of the subject. The generic spin state of a statistical ensemble of particles is described by means of a density operator: given an ensemble of spin states
\Ket{\psi}_{i}
occurring with probability
p_{i}
, the density operator is

\hat{\rho}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Ket{\psi}_{i}\Bra{\psi}_{i},

(1)

and the expectation value of any operator
\hat{X}
on the state described by
\hat{\rho}
can be expressed as

\langle\hat{X}\rangle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Braket{\psi|\hat{X}|\psi}_{i}=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\hat{\rho}\hat{X}\right].

(2)

The decay rate of a multi-body decay
A\rightarrow\{i=1,..,n\}
for definite spin eigenstates is the squared modulus of the transition amplitude between the
A
particle initial state
\Ket{s_{A},m_{A}}
and the final particle product state
\Ket{\{s_{i}\},\{m_{i}\}}=\otimes_{i}\Ket{s_{i},m_{i}}
,

\displaystyle p_{m_{A},\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=|\braket{s_{A},m_{A}|\hat{T}|\{s_{i}\},\{m_{i}\}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{A}_{m_{A},\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)|^{2}.

(3)

The label
\Omega
denotes the set of phase space variables describing the decay distributions.

Generic polarisation states are described by introducing the density operators for the initial particle state
\hat{\rho}^{A}
and the final particle state
\hat{\rho}^{\{i\}}
, which are included in the decay rate Eq. (
3
) by inserting suitable identity resolutions,

\displaystyle p(\Omega,\hat{\rho}^{A},\hat{\rho}^{\{i\}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\hat{\rho}^{A}\hat{T}\hat{\rho}^{\{i\}}\hat{T}^{\dagger}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{A},m^{\prime}_{A}}\sum_{\{m_{i}\},\{m^{\prime}_{i}\}}\hat{\rho}^{A}_{m_{A},m^{\prime}_{A}}\hat{\rho}^{\{i\}}_{\{m_{i}\},\{m^{\prime}_{i}\}}\mathcal{A}_{m_{A},\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)\mathcal{A}^{*}_{m^{\prime}_{A},\{m^{\prime}_{i}\}}(\Omega).

(4)

The differential decay rate of a spin 1/2 particle in a generic polarisation state is derived considering its density matrix

\rho^{A}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}+\bm{P}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+P_{z}&P_{x}-iP_{y}\\
P_{x}+iP_{y}&1-P_{z}\\
\end{array}\right),

(5)

in which the polarisation components
P_{x}
,
P_{y}
,
P_{z}
are the expectation values of the three spin operators and
\bm{\sigma}
the three Pauli matrices. The final particle polarisation states, assumed to be unmeasurable, are described by an identity density matrix

\rho^{\{i\}}=\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}.

(6)

The differential rate Eq. (
4
), decomposed into unpolarised, longitudinal (
P_{z}
) and orthogonal (
P_{x}
,
P_{y}
) polarisation parts becomes

\displaystyle p(\Omega,\bm{P})

\displaystyle=p_{unpol}(\Omega)+p_{long}(\Omega,P_{z})+p_{orth}(\Omega,P_{x},P_{y}),

(7)

\displaystyle p_{unpol}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}}\left(|\mathcal{A}_{1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)|^{2}+|\mathcal{A}_{-1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)|^{2}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle p_{long}(\Omega,P_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}P_{z}\sum_{\{m_{i}\}}\left(|\mathcal{A}_{1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)|^{2}-|\mathcal{A}_{-1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)|^{2}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle p_{orth}(\Omega,P_{x},P_{y})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left[(P_{x}-iP_{y})\sum_{\{m_{i}\}}\mathcal{A}_{1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)\mathcal{A}^{*}_{-1/2,\{m_{i}\}}(\Omega)\right].

(10)

2.2
General Properties of the Polarised Decay Rate

For later convenience we consider some properties of the polarised decay rate related to rotational invariance and parity symmetry.

The polarisation vector
\bm{P}
associated to a decaying particle
A
is the only quantity specifying a direction in its rest frame. Therefore, rotational invariance implies that for null polarisation the decay rate must be isotropic in any
A
rest frame defined independently from the decay distributions. In other words, the decay rate specifies the relative angular distribution among daughter particles, but not their global orientation in space.

For non-zero polarisation both the polarisation vector and the daughter particle momenta transform in the same way under rotations. Thus, the relative orientation of daughter particles is independent on the polarisation vector.

The sensitivity of the decay rate to the particle polarisation depends critically on the amount of parity symmetry violation characterising the decay. Parity symmetry requires the decay angular distribution to be equal for
+|\bm{P}|
and
-|\bm{P}|
polarisation values, for any polarisation vector
\bm{P}
, since parity transformation reverses the daughter particle momenta but not the polarisation vector. Therefore, a decay mediated by a parity conserving interaction retains no information on the decaying particle polarisation.
Vice-versa, the sensitivity of the decay rate on parity-violating effects depends critically on the amount of decaying particle polarisation. Indeed, since for zero polarisation there is no preferred direction, the decay rate becomes symmetric under parity transformation, and parity-violating effects cancel.

One can see the combined effect of parity-violation and polarisation as creating an anisotropy along the direction specified by
\bm{P}
. Rotational invariance makes all such directions equivalent, in the sense that a rotation of the system can only change the direction of the anisotropy. Indeed, given a generic polarisation, we can choose the
z
quantisation axis to be along
\bm{P}
when studying the properties of the decay rate other than the polarisation direction. This is why in this article the sensitivity of the decay rate to its parameters like helicity couplings and polarisation modulus is usually studied assuming longitudinal polarisation only. Vice-versa, the anisotropy can be used to determine the polarisation direction from the decay distributions, as will be shown in Section
3
.

Note that, at the level of the observed decay distribution, parity-violation effects originated in the decay process may be influenced by final state interactions. A theoretical study would be needed to disentangle the two contributions from the results of an amplitude fit.

2.3
Helicity Formalism

We briefly introduce the helicity formalism following the method of Ref.
[
23
]
, in which the helicity formalism is revisited in light of its application to multibody polarised particle decays, like the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
one. The “standard” helicity formalism of Ref.
[
24
]
is slightly modified to ease a correct matching of final particles spin states for decays featuring different interfering decay chains. For the case of two-body and three-body via a single intermediate state decays, Secs.
3
and
4
, where single decay amplitudes are involved, the two approaches coincide.

Two-body
A\rightarrow 1,2
decay amplitudes can be expressed in terms of
A
spin state
\ket{s_{A},m_{A}}
, and a
1,2
two-particle state being the product of particle 1 helicity states
\ket{s_{1},\lambda_{1}}
and particle 2 opposite-helicity states
\ket{s_{2},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}
, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m_{A},\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})

\displaystyle=\Braket{\theta_{1},\phi_{1},\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\hat{T}|s_{A},m_{A}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}D^{*s_{A}}_{m_{A},\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\phi_{1},\theta_{1},0),

(11)

in which
\theta_{1},\phi_{1}
are the spherical angles of the particle 1 momentum in the
A
reference system and
D
is a Wigner
D
matrix representing rotations on spin states (see
e.g.
Ref.
[
25
]
for their definition and properties). The use of opposite-helicity states eases the control of phases arising from the helicity rotations. The complex number

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\equiv\Braket{s_{A},m_{A},\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\hat{T}|s_{A},m_{A}},

(12)

called helicity coupling, encodes the decay dynamics and can not depend on
m_{A}
for rotational invariance. The helicity values allowed by angular momentum conservation are

|\lambda_{1}|\leq s_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt|\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\leq s_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt|\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}|\leq s_{A}.

(13)

Multi-body decay amplitudes are treated in the helicity formalism by breaking the decay into sequences of two-body decays introducing suitable intermediate states and summing over their helicity states allowed by Eq. (
13
).

3
Two-body Decay

We consider a two-body decay
A\rightarrow 1,2
of a spin 1/2 particle in the helicity formalism introduced in Section
2.3
. Following Eqs. (
8
), (
9
), the longitudinal polarisation decay rate is,

p(\theta_{1},P_{z})=\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}\left(\frac{1+P_{z}}{2}\text{ }d^{1/2}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}+\frac{1-P_{z}}{2}\text{ }d^{1/2}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}\right),

(14)

with
\theta_{1},\phi_{1}
are the spherical angles of the particle 1 momentum in the
A
reference system.
The rate can not depend on the azimuthal angle
\phi_{1}
for invariance under rotations around the
z
axis. Using the
d
-matrix property

\sum_{m}d^{S}_{m,m^{\prime}}(\theta)^{2}=1,

(15)

and fixing the overall helicity coupling normalisation to

\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}\equiv 1,

(16)

we find that for zero polarisation the decay rate is constant, isotropic as required by rotational invariance. For non-zero polarisation the decay rate takes the well-known form

\displaystyle p(\theta_{1},P_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{P_{z}}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}\left(d^{1/2}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}-d^{1/2}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{P_{z}}{2}\cos\theta_{1}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\text{sign}(\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2})\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha P_{z}\cos\theta_{1}\right),

(17)

in which the explicit expression for the
d
-matrices Eq.(
96
) has been used and the decay asymmetry parameter

\alpha\equiv\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\text{sign}(\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2})\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2},

(18)

is introduced.
The sensitivity to the polarisation is governed by the
\alpha
parity-violating parameter. Indeed
\alpha
is zero if the decay conserves parity, which requires

\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}=\left|\mathcal{H}_{-\lambda_{1},-\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}.

(19)

Note that a fit to the
\cos\theta_{1}
decay distribution can only measure the combination
\alpha P_{z}
: it is not possible to determine separately the polarisation and the
\alpha
parameter values, unless one of the two is available from other measurements. Moreover, the fit is not sensitive to the single helicity couplings, but only to the
\alpha
combination.

For a generic
A
polarisation vector, the decay rate is, following Eqs. (
8
) (
9
), (
10
),

\displaystyle p(\theta_{1},\phi_{1},\bm{P})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\left[(1+P_{z})\text{ }d^{1/2}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}+(1-P_{z})\text{ }d^{1/2}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.2\left(P_{x}\cos\phi_{1}+P_{y}\sin\phi_{1}\right)d^{1/2}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})\text{ }d^{1/2}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}}(\theta_{1})\right].

(20)

The orthogonal polarisation part becomes

\displaystyle p_{\rm orth}(\theta_{1},\phi_{1},P_{x},P_{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\text{sign}(\lambda_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2})\left|\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}\left(P_{x}\cos\phi_{1}+P_{y}\sin\phi_{1}\right)\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(P_{x}\cos\phi_{1}+P_{y}\sin\phi_{1}\right)\sin\theta_{1},

(21)

so that the decay rate is

\displaystyle p(\theta_{1},\phi_{1},\bm{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha P_{z}\cos\theta_{1}+\alpha P_{x}\sin\theta_{1}\cos\phi_{1}+\alpha P_{y}\sin\theta_{1}\sin\phi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha\bm{P}\cdot\bm{\hat{p}}_{1}\right),

(22)

with
\bm{\hat{p}}_{1}
being the particle 1 momentum versor in the
A
reference system.
It features three angular distributions, each describing a different polarisation component, all multiplied by the
\alpha
parameter. Therefore, a fit can determine the polarisation direction, but not its modulus independently of
\alpha
. Note that the orthogonal polarisation part does not add information on the helicity couplings, which enter the decay rate only via the
\alpha
combination.

4
Three-body Decay Via a Single Intermediate State

We consider a three-body decay with a single intermediate state
R
of the form
A\rightarrow R(\rightarrow 1,2),3
, with spin structure
1/2\rightarrow S_{R}(\rightarrow 1/2,0),0
. Summing over the resonance helicity states allowed by angular momentum conservation, the decay amplitude is,

\mathcal{A}_{m_{A},\lambda_{1}}=\sum_{\lambda_{R}=\pm 1/2}\mathcal{H}^{A}_{\lambda_{R},0}D^{*1/2}_{m_{A},\lambda_{R}}(\phi_{R},\theta_{R},0)\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},0}D^{*S_{R}}_{\lambda_{R},\lambda_{1}}(\phi_{1},\theta_{1},0)\mathcal{R}_{R}(m^{2}_{R}),

(23)

in which
\theta_{R},\phi_{R}
are the spherical angles of the
R
momentum in the
A
reference system,
\theta_{1},\phi_{1}
now are the spherical angles of the particle 1 momentum in the
R
helicity reference system,
\lambda_{R}
is the
R
helicity defined from the
A
system and
\lambda_{1}
is the particle 1 helicity defined from the
R
helicity system. The helicity couplings
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{\lambda_{R},0}
and
\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},0}
are associated to the two-body decays
A\rightarrow R,3
and
R\rightarrow 1,2
, respectively. A non-negligible width for the
R
state has been assumed, its invariant mass dependence described by the lineshape function
\mathcal{R}_{R}(m^{2}_{R})
.
The squared modulus is

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{m_{A},\lambda_{1}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},0}\right|^{2}d^{1/2}_{m_{A},1/2}(\theta_{R})^{2}d^{S_{R}}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}}(\theta_{1})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},0}\right|^{2}d^{1/2}_{m_{A},-1/2}(\theta_{R})^{2}d^{S_{R}}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}}(\theta_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle+\left.2\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\hskip-4.0pt\left(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*A}_{-1/2,0}e^{i\phi_{1}}\right)d^{1/2}_{m_{A},1/2}(\theta_{R})d^{1/2}_{m_{A},-1/2}(\theta_{R})\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},0}\right|^{2}d^{s_{R}}_{1/2,\lambda_{1}}(\theta_{1})d^{s_{R}}_{-1/2,\lambda_{1}}(\theta_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\times|\mathcal{R}_{R}(m^{2}_{R})|^{2}.

(24)

Let’s consider the unpolarised decay rate Eq. (
8
): using

\sum_{m_{A}=\pm 1/2}d^{1/2}_{m_{A},\lambda}(\theta_{R})^{2}=1,\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{m_{A}=\pm 1/2}d^{1/2}_{m_{A},1/2}(\theta_{R})\text{ }d^{1/2}_{m_{A},-1/2}(\theta_{R})=0,

(25)

and the explicit
d
-matrix element values Eqs. (
96
), (
97
), we find

\displaystyle p_{\rm unpol}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\right)|g^{S_{R}}_{R}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})|^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\right)|g^{S_{R}}_{R}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})|^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{1}}{2},

(26)

in which

g^{S_{R}}_{R}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})\equiv f(S_{R},\theta_{1})\mathcal{R}_{R}(m^{2}_{R}),

(27)

with
f(S_{R},\theta_{1})
describing the angular distribution of the state
R
for
S_{R}>1/2
, see Appendix
A
. The rate can be arranged in the form (playing with the
\cos^{2}\alpha+\sin^{2}\alpha=1
relation)

p_{\rm unpol}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})=\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\alpha_{A}\alpha_{R}\cos\theta_{1}\right)|g^{S_{R}}_{R}(m^{2}_{R},\theta_{1})|^{2},

(28)

by using the normalisation condition Eq. (
16
) and the definition of the asymmetry parameters for the two-body decays
A\rightarrow R,3
and
R\rightarrow 1,2
,

\alpha_{A}\equiv\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}-\left|\mathcal{H}^{A}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\alpha_{R}\equiv\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}-\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}.

(29)

A fit to the
\cos\theta_{1}
distribution determines the combination
\alpha_{A}\alpha_{R}
, and the two can not be separated unless one of the two is already measured. Note that the different form of the
\cos\theta_{1}
distribution for different
S_{R}
values can be exploited to measure the
R
spin if not known.

The longitudinal polarisation decay rate Eqs. (
8
), (

5.1

\displaystyle\bm{\hat{z}}_{\rm DP}=\bm{\hat{p}}(p),\hskip 28.45274pt\bm{\hat{x}}_{\rm DP}=\frac{\bm{p}(p)\times\bm{p}(K^{-})}{\left|\bm{p}(p)\times\bm{p}(K^{-})\right|}\times\bm{\hat{p}}(p),\hskip 28.45274pt\bm{\hat{y}}_{\rm DP}=\bm{\hat{z}}_{\rm DP}\times\bm{\hat{x}}_{\rm DP},

\bm{a}^{W}_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}=\frac{\bm{p}^{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}}({\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*})\times\bm{p}^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}(p)}{\left|\bm{p}^{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}}({\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*})\times\bm{p}^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}(p)\right|}=-\bm{\hat{y}}_{\rm DP},

(63)

which is opposite to the

R_{y}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}})
, with reversed Wigner angle sign

\beta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}=\theta^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}+\theta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}-\alpha^{W}_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}.

(64)

The decay amplitudes for each decay chain are the product of two two-body decay amplitudes, summed over the proton helicities for
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
and
{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}
chains,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{K^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p},\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{K^{*}}_{m_{p},\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}}}\delta_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}+\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}}}d^{*S_{K^{*}}}_{\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}},0}(\bar{\theta}_{K})\mathcal{R}_{K^{*}}(m^{2}_{K^{-}\pi^{+}}),

(65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},0}d^{1/2}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})d^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}}),

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{++*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}}\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{++*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},0}d^{1/2}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}})d^{S_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p})d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}})\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{p\pi^{+}}).

(67)

The complete amplitude for the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay is obtained summing the amplitudes for all the intermediate resonances and their allowed helicity states,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},m^{2}_{K^{-}\pi^{+}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K^{*}}}\sum_{\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}}}\mathcal{A}^{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow K^{*}_{i}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+})p}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p},\bar{\lambda}_{K^{*}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\mathcal{A}^{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow{\mathchar 28931\relax}_{i}^{*}(\rightarrow pK^{-})\pi^{+}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},m_{p}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{++*}}}\mathcal{A}^{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow{\mathchar 28929\relax}_{k}^{++*}K^{-}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},m_{p}}.

(68)

5.4
Study of the Decay Rate

We divide the study of the decay rate into different parts, investigating which information each gives on the parameters describing the decay distribution. In particular, we will focus on helicity couplings and the polarisation degree. Each complex helicity coupling is equivalent to two real parameters, its real and imaginary part or its modulus and phase. Therefore, we have two real unknowns for each coupling plus the polarisation modulus. Each part of the decay rate having a functional form distinguishable from the others via an amplitude fit, yields a constraint on the combination of parameters involved in that contribution.

First, we study the part of the decay rate without interference terms, consisting of the sum of the rates associated to each single contribution. For the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay, each contribution is given by Eq. (
34
), since intermediate resonances decay via parity-conserving strong interaction,

\displaystyle p^{\rm non-int}(\Omega,P_{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K^{*}}}\left(F_{K_{i}^{*}}+P_{z}\alpha_{K_{i}^{*}}\cos\theta_{K^{*}}\right)|g^{S_{K^{*}_{i}}}_{K^{*}_{i}}(m^{2}_{K^{-}\pi^{+}},\theta_{K})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\left(F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}_{j}^{*}}+P_{z}\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}_{j}^{*}}\cos\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}\right)|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}_{j}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}_{j}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}\left(F_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}_{k}^{*}}+P_{z}\alpha_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}_{k}^{*}}\cos\theta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}\right)|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}_{k}}}_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}_{j}}(m^{2}_{p\pi^{+}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2},

(69)

in which angles are defined in analogy with Eq. (
34
) and the
F_{R}
values are the sum of the helicity couplings squared moduli for the resonance
R
,

F_{R}\equiv\sum_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\left|\mathcal{H}^{R}_{\lambda_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\right|^{2}.

(70)

These are related to the resonance fit fractions
\mathcal{F}_{R}
via

\mathcal{F}_{R}=\int F_{R}|g^{S_{R}}_{R}(m^{2}_{R},\theta^{R}_{1})|^{2}d\Omega.

(71)

The overall normalisation of the decay rate, ensuring it has unit integral over the phase space, is intended to be implicit in the definition of the helicity couplings.

The decay rate part without interference terms gives information on
F_{R}
and the products
P_{z}\alpha_{R}
, but, again, it does not allow to separate the polarisation modulus from the
\alpha
values without an independent measurement of at least one of them.

For later convenience, let’s evaluate the non-interfering decay rate part associated to a
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
resonance in the Dalitz plot amplitude decomposition. Starting from Eq. (
66
), its expression greatly simplifies exploiting the orthogonality of
d
-matrices,

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{\prime{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p},\lambda^{\prime{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}d^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})d^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{\prime}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}f(S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})^{2},

(72)

obtaining

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}=\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},0}\right|^{2}\text{ }d^{1/2}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})d^{1/2}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2}.

(73)

Explicitly we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\left|\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}+\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\right)|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}-\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\right)\sin\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}

\displaystyle\times|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2}=\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}},

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\left|\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left|\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\right|^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}+\left|\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\right|^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\right)|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2}.

(74)

The contribution to the unpolarised decay rate Eq. (
38
) is

\displaystyle p_{\rm unpol}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},m^{2}_{K^{-}\pi^{+}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\left|\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\right|^{2}=F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2},

(75)

while the contribution to the longitudinal polarisation rate Eq. (
40
) is

\displaystyle p_{\rm long}(\Omega,P_{z})

\displaystyle=P_{z}\left[\cos\theta_{p}\text{ }\sum_{m_{p}}\left(\left|\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\right|^{2}-\left|\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\right|^{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 8.0pt-2\sin\theta_{p}\text{ }\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\sum_{m_{p}}\left(\exp i\chi\text{ }\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=P_{z}\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}\left(\cos\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}-\sin\theta_{p}\sin\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}\cos\chi\right)|g^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})|^{2}.

(76)

The above expressions are equivalent to Eq. (
69
), the only difference being in the choice of the angles describing the decay distributions. Indeed, the definition of the helicity angle
\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}
is different in the two cases: while in Eqs. (
75
), (
76
) it is independent of the decay orientation angles (
\theta_{p}
,
\phi_{p}
,
\chi
), in Eq. (
69
)
\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}
depends on a combination of invariant mass and decay orientation degrees of freedom. Of course, nothing changes in terms of sensitivity to the decay parameters: the
\chi
angle dependent term does not add information to the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay parameters.

The
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
contribution is characterised by 4 unknowns related to couplings plus the polarisation modulus, which is shared with the other contributions. This part of the decay rate places two constraints determining the sum of the helicity coupling moduli and their relative difference up to the polarisation factor.

Now, we consider the interference terms between resonance contributions, showing in which way they allow to get maximum information on the decay amplitudes, constraining all the parameters describing the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay distributions.
There are two kind of interference terms: those arising from resonances belonging to the same decay chain and those coming from resonances associated to different decay chains. In the following we will consider one example for each type.

5.4.1
Interference Term
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}-{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}

Let’s consider the interference term between two
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
resonances. Its computation is analogous to the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
decay rate, since both resonances belong to the same decay chain, sharing the same rotation angles,

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},0}\text{ }d^{1/2}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})d^{1/2}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})G^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*},{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}),

(77)

in which

G^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*},{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})\equiv f(S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})f(S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}})\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}}).

(78)

Explicitly (leaving out
G
function arguments),

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}+\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\right)G,

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}-\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}\right)\sin\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}G

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,m_{p}},

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}+\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}{2}\right)G.

(79)

Let’s consider the contribution of this interference term to the decay rate, including its complex conjugate corresponding to the exchange
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}\leftrightarrow{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}
. The unpolarised decay rate part Eq. (
38
) is

\displaystyle p_{\rm unpol}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},m^{2}_{K^{-}\pi^{+}})

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\sum_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left(F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}G\right),

(80)

while the longitudinal polarisation part Eq. (
40
) is

\displaystyle p_{\rm long}(\Omega,P_{z})

\displaystyle=P_{z}\left\{\cos\theta_{p}\text{ }2\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\sum_{m_{p}}\left(\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}-\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 8.0pt-2\sin\theta_{p}\text{ }\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\sum_{m_{p}}\left[\exp i\chi\text{ }\left(\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}+\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,m_{p}}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=2P_{z}\left[\cos\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}\text{ }\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left(\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}G\right)-\sin\theta_{p}\sin\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}\text{ }\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left(\exp i\chi\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}G\right)\right],

(81)

which probe the combinations

\displaystyle F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}\equiv\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}+\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0},

\displaystyle\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}\equiv\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}-\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}.

(82)

The structure of this interference term is similar to the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
decay rate: a
F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}
term probed by the unpolarised decay rate and a polarisation-dependent term driven by a parity-violating asymmetry parameter
\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}
.

To better study the constraints given by this interference term, let’s write both complex couplings and the
G
function in modulus-phase decomposition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}

\displaystyle=P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}\exp i\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0},

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=|G|\exp i\phi_{G}.

(83)

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left(F_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}G\right)/|G|

\displaystyle=P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}+\phi_{G})

\displaystyle+P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}+\phi_{G}),

\displaystyle P_{z}\mathrm{Re}\hskip 2.0pt\left(\alpha_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}G\right)/|G|

\displaystyle=P_{z}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}+\phi_{G})

\displaystyle-P_{z}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}P^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{-1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{-1/2,0}+\phi_{G})

(84)

Since the phase
\phi_{G}
has in general a non-trivial dependence on the resonance invariant mass (
e.g.
relativistic Breit-Wigner lineshapes have such behaviour), the two conditions place four constraints on decay parameters: indeed one has

\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0}+\phi_{G})=\cos(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0})\cos\phi_{G}-\sin(\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{1/2,0}-\Phi^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}_{1/2,0})\sin\phi_{G},

(85)

showing that phase differences among couplings can be probed, not only their cosine, since
\cos\phi_{G}
and
\sin\phi_{G}
functional forms are separable by an amplitude fit. The constraint given by the
\chi
-dependent term is redundant.

Considering the decay rate part associated to
{\mathchar 28931\relax}
and
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}
resonances we have 9 unknowns (8 real couplings +
P_{z}
), and 8 constraints (4 from single decay rates + 4 from the interference term): again, we miss a condition to determine separately the set of helicity couplings and the polarisation.

What happens if we consider a set of three interfering
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
resonances? We have 13 unknowns and 16 constraints (now 12 coming from the three interference terms) and therefore the possibility to measure both the full set of helicity couplings and the polarisation. Practically, to have significant interference effects the three resonances must feature a significant overlap in invariant mass dependence (otherwise the lineshape product
\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}})\mathcal{R}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{\prime*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}})
vanishes) which can make their separation in the amplitude fit difficult, especially if they have the same spin.

5.4.2
Interference Term
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}-{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}

Let’s consider the interference term between one
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}
and one
{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}
resonance. Starting from Eqs. (
66
), (
67
) its expression is

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}\mathcal{O}^{*{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},m_{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{p}}\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}\mathcal{H}^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},0}d^{1/2}_{\nu_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})d^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p})d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.0pt\times\sum_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}\mathcal{H}^{*{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},0}d^{1/2}_{\nu^{\prime}_{{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}},\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}(\theta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}})d^{S_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}_{\lambda_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\theta^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p})d^{1/2}_{m_{p},\lambda^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}}(\beta_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.0pt\times G^{S_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}},S_{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}}_{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*},{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}(m^{2}_{pK^{-}},m^{2}_{p\pi^{+}},\theta^{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{*}}_{p},\theta^{{\mathchar 28929\relax}^{*}}_{p}),

5.4.3
Summary

In the following we summarize the results of the study of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay rate, focusing on which part of the decay rate gives information on which amplitude model parameter, Table

.

The sum of the helicity coupling moduli
F_{R}
for each resonance (hence the fit fraction
\mathcal{F}_{R}
) can be measured from the decay rate without interference. The decay asymmetry parameters can be extracted from the same term but only in presence of a significant polarisation degree.

The sum of helicity coupling products
F_{RR^{\prime}}
characterising the interference between resonances belonging to the same decay channel can be measured in absence of polarisation, while the difference
\alpha_{RR^{\prime}}
needs non-zero polarisation.

The single helicity coupling complex values can be measured if their associate resonance has a significant interference with other two belonging to the same decay channel or one belonging to a different decay channel.

Assuming non-negligible parity-violation effects, the polarisation direction
\hat{\bm{P}}
can be measured from the non-interfering decay rate, as following from the two-body decay rate for generic polarisation Eq. (
22
). Instead, the polarisation modulus
|\bm{P}|
can be determined separately from the helicity couplings only in presence of significant interference among three resonances in the same decay channel or two belonging to different decay channels.

According to the ongoing
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
amplitude analysis from semileptonic decays at the LHCb experiment
[
1
]
, all the requirements needed for extracting maximum information from
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay distributions are met: non-zero
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
polarisation produced in the parent beauty hadron weak decay, significant parity-violation and resonant contributions in all its three decay channels with sizeable interference effects.

7
Conclusions

We perform an analytical study of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay to understand which information can be extracted from its decay distributions. This study holds as well for any three-body decay with spin structure
1/2\rightarrow 1/2\text{ }0\text{ }0
, having a first parity-violating decay to an intermediate state subsequently decaying via parity-conserving interactions.

Considering an amplitude model for the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay written in the helicity formalism we show which parameters of the amplitude model can be measured from which part of the decay rate. We demonstrate how the presence of significant interference effects together with a non-negligible
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
polarisation allows the simultaneous measurement of all the amplitude model parameters, including complex helicity couplings, and the polarisation degree and direction. We highlight the interplay between amplitude model and polarisation degree: significant resonance interference effects are needed to measure the second; while a non-zero polarisation allows to fully determine the decay model.

The analytical study has been cross-checked numerically by means of toy amplitude fits on Monte Carlo pseudo-data.

According to the ongoing studies of
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decays at LHCb
[
1
]
, this decay has all the features needed for the complete determination of its amplitude model parameters. The full determination of the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
amplitude model opens the possibility to use a
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay amplitude model for the different applications presented in Section
1
, ranging from New Physics searches to low-energy QCD studies. In particular the
{{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay can be used as an absolute polarimeter for the
{\mathchar 28931\relax}^{+}_{c}
baryon.

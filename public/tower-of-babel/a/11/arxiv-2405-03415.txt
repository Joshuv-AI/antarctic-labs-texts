Unique solvability and error analysis of the Lagrange multiplier approach for gradient flows
Qing Cheng, Jie Shen, Cheng Wang

Abstract
The unique solvability and error analysis of the original Lagrange multiplier approach proposed in [ 8 ] for gradient flows is studied in this paper. We identify a necessary and sufficient condition that must be satisfied for the nonlinear algebraic equation arising from the original Lagrange multiplier approach to admit a unique solution in the neighborhood of its exact solution, and propose a modified Lagrange multiplier approach so that the computation can continue even if the aforementioned condition is not satisfied. Using Cahn-Hilliard equation as an example, we prove rigorously the unique solvability and establish optimal error estimates of a second-order Lagrange multiplier scheme assuming this condition and that the time step is sufficient small. We also present numerical results to demonstrate that the modified Lagrange multiplier approach is much more robust and can use much larger time step than the original Lagrange multiplier approach.

Introduction

We consider the numerical approximation of a general gradient flow given by

\partial_{t}\phi=-\mathcal{G}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},

(1.1)

where
E(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}^{1/2}\phi,\mathcal{L}^{1/2}\phi)+(F(\phi),1)
, with
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{G}
being positive definite operators in a suitable Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and
F(\phi)
is a nonlinear potential function. An important property of (
1.1
) is an associated energy dissipation law:

\frac{d}{dt}E=-(\mathcal{G}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi}).

(1.2)

It is highly desirable to design numerical schemes which can satisfy a discrete version of (
1.2
).

In recent years, a great deal of efforts have been devoted to construct efficient and accurate energy dissipative schemes for various gradient flows in the form of (
1.1
); we refer to
[
3
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
and the references therein for more details. For example, the convex splitting method
[
2
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
30
]
, which treats the convex part implicitly and the concave part explicitly, ensures the unique solvability and unconditional energy stability at a theoretical level. On the other hand, the price of this numerical approach is associated with a nonlinear solver at each time step, due to the fact that the nonlinear terms in the gradient flow usually correspond to a convex energy. Moreover, some higher order versions of this approach, in both the second and third accuracy orders, have been extensively studied
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
18
,
19
,
28
]
, and the stability analysis for a modified energy functional, composed of the original free energy and a few numerical correction terms, has been reported. Again, a nonlinear solver has to be implemented in these higher order energy stable schemes, which has always been a huge numerical challenge.

To overcome the difficulty associated with a nonlinear solver in the numerical implementation, many linear approach efforts have been made for various gradient flows. In particular, the stabilization method is applied to the Cahn-Hilliard equation
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
27
]
, in which an artificial regularization term is added to ensure the energy stability, either in terms of the original free energy, or a modified energy functional, usually under a global Lipshitz condition on the nonlinear part of the free energy. On the other hand, the IEQ approach proposed in
[
29
]
gives a linear, and unconditionally energy stable (with respect to a modified energy) scheme, but it requires solving a linear system with variable coefficients. The original scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approach proposed in
[
25
]
leads to a linear, decoupled and unconditionally energy stable (with respect to a modified energy) scheme, which is very efficient and easy to implement, while it is not energy dissipative with respect to the original energy. On the other hand, the original Lagrange multiplier approach proposed in
[
9
]
leads to a linear, decoupled and unconditionally energy stable (with respect to the original energy) scheme, combined with a nonlinear algebraic equation for the Lagrange multiplier. In principle, this approach has essentially all the desired attributes for solving gradient flows; however, it is not clear whether the nonlinear algebraic equation admits a unique solution in the desired range.

Although there are ample numerical results indicating that the original Lagrange multiplier approach works well in many applications, but there are cases where exceedingly small time steps are needed or one is unable to find a suitable solution of this nonlinear algebraic equation
[
1
,
8
]
. Therefore, it is very important to identify condition(s) which can ensure the unique solvability, and modify the original Lagrange multiplier approach so that the computation can continue even if theses condition(s) are not satisfied.

We observe from (
2.6
), which is the last equation in the original Lagrange multiplier scheme (
2.4
)-(
2.6
), that the Lagrange multiplier
\eta^{n+1/2}
can not be uniquely determined if
(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1)=0
. Therefore, it is necessary to make the following
assumption on the exact solution:

S_{n}:=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\,F(\Phi)\,d\boldsymbol{x}|_{t=t^{n}}\neq 0.

(1.3)

This assumption, of course, can not be satisfied
a priori
for the whole time interval. Hence, it is necessary to modify the Lagrange multiplier approach so that the computation can continue even if (
1.3
) is not satisfied at some time.

The main purpose of this work is to take the Cahn-Hilliard equation as an example of gradient flow to study the unique solvability of the nonlinear algebraic equation in the original Lagrange multiplier approach, and to carry out its error analysis. The main contributions of this work include:

•

We prove rigorously that if the assumption (
1.3
) is satisfied, then the original Lagrange multiplier scheme (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) admits a unique solution in the interval
[1-\Delta t^{1/2},1-\Delta t^{1/2}]
.

•

We propose a modified Lagrange multiplier approach to deal with the case when
|S_{n}|\ll 1
, and provide numerical results to show that the modified Lagrange multiplier approach is much more robust and can use much larger time steps than the original Lagrange multiplier approach.

•

Given a tolerance
\gamma
, and assume
|S_{n}|\geq\gamma
in the time interval
[0,T]
, we carry out an error analysis and establish optimal error estimates under the condition that the time step
\Delta t\leq(\frac{\gamma}{4})^{4}
.

In fact, the unique solvability of the nonlinear algebraic equation in the Lagrange multiplier approach turns out to be very challenging. First of all, it is observed that the implicit part of the numerical scheme (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) does not correspond to a globally monotone functional in terms of the numerical solution at the next time step. To overcome this difficulty, we have to apply certain local analysis technique to obtain the unique solvability, viewed as a perturbation of the exact solution at each time step. To achieve this goal, an
a-priori
assumption has to be made at the previous time step, in terms of the convergence estimate. With the help of this
a-priori
assumption, the unique solvability can then be carefully proved under the assumption (
1.3
) on the exact solution. Subsequently, to recover the
priori
assumption in the unique solvability analysis, we derive an optimal rate convergence analysis at the next time step. By a mathematical induction argument, we are able to complete the proof of unique solvability and error analysis.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall a second order scheme for the gradient flows using the original Lagrange multiplier approach, and then present a modified Lagrange multiplier approach to deal with the case when (
1.3
) is not satisfied. A numerical example is given to validate the efficiency of the improved Lagrange multiplier approach. In Section
3
, we consider Cahn-Hilliard equation as an example and establish the unique solvability of the nonlinear system of algebraic equations, under an
a-priori
assumption on the previous time step. Afterward, an error analysis is presented in Section
4
, and the
a-priori
assumption is theoretically recovered. Finally, we provide some concluding remarks in Section
5
.

2
The Lagrange multiplier approach and a modified version

We denote
\mu=\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi}=\mathcal{L}\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)
. A second order modified Crank-Nicolson scheme has been proposed for the general gradient flow (
1.1
), based on the original Lagrange multiplier approach
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}=-\mathcal{G}\mu^{n+1},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mu^{n+1}=\mathcal{L}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\eta^{n+1/2}F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Big(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1\Big)=\eta^{n+1/2}\Big(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\Big),

(2.6)

where
\phi^{*,n}=\frac{3}{2}\phi^{n}-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{n-1},\;\eta^{n+1/2}=\frac{\eta^{n+1}+\eta^{n}}{2}
. The energy stability result for the above scheme (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) could be established, following a similar idea as in
[
8
]
; see the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

The numerical scheme (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) is unconditional stable and satisfies the following energy dissipative law

\frac{E^{n+1}-E^{n}}{\delta t}=-(\mathcal{G}\mu^{n+1},\mu^{n+1})-(\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-2\phi^{n}+\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n+1}-2\phi^{n}+\phi^{n-1}),

(2.7)

where the energy
E^{n+1}
is defined as

E^{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1},\phi^{n+1})+\frac{1}{8}(\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})+(F(\phi^{n+1}),1).

(2.8)

Proof.

Taking inner product of (
2.4
), (
2.5
) with
\mu^{n+1}
,
\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}
respectively, we obtain

(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}},\mu^{n+1})=-(\mathcal{G}\mu^{n+1},\mu^{n+1}),

(2.9)

and

(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}},\mu^{n+1})=(\mathcal{L}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\eta^{n+1/2}F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}).

(2.10)

Notice the equality

\begin{split}&(\mathcal{L}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})=\frac{1}{8}\{(\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})-(\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})\\
&+(\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-2\phi^{n}+\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n+1}-2\phi^{n}+\phi^{n-1})\}+\frac{1}{2}\{(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1},\phi^{n+1})-(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n},\phi^{n})\}.\end{split}

(2.11)

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) and using (
2.6
) and (
2.11
), we derived the desired energy dissipative law.

The implementation process of the above scheme can be outlined as follows. First, we define

\phi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\eta^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mu^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\mu_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\eta^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.12)

Substituting
(\phi^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\mu^{n+\frac{1}{2}})
into (
2.4
)-(
2.6
), we are able to obtain
\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
from the following two linear systems

\frac{\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}=-\mathcal{G}\mu_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mu_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{L}(\frac{3}{4}\phi_{1}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),

(2.13)

and

\frac{\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}{{\Delta t}}=-\mathcal{G}\mu_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mu_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{L}(\frac{3}{4}\phi_{2}^{n+1})+F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}).

(2.14)

It is obvious that
\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
can be solved uniquely from (
2.13
)-(
2.14
). Subsequently, a substitution of (
2.12
) into (
2.6
) leads to the following
nonlinear algebraic equation for
\eta^{n+1/2}
:

g_{n}(\eta):=\int_{\Omega}\,\Big(F(\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\eta\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-F(\phi^{n})\Big)\,d\boldsymbol{x}-\eta\int_{\Omega}\,F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})(\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\eta\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-\phi^{n})\,d\boldsymbol{x}=0.

(2.15)

As mentioned in the introduction, (
2.15
) may not be uniquely solvable near
\eta=1
if the assumption (
1.3
) is not satisfied. Hence, we need to modify the Lagrange multiplier approach so that the computation can continue even if (
1.3
) is not satisfied.

Below we introduce a modified Lagrange multiplier approach for gradient flow (
1.1
). For a given tolerance
\gamma\ll 1
, we proceed as follows.

We first compute
\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
from (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), respectively. Then, we set
\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}=\phi_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\phi_{2}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
. If
e_{n+1}=|\frac{\int_{\Omega}F(\tilde{\phi}^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n})dx}{\Delta t}|>\gamma
, we continue with the original Lagrange multiplier approach; otherwise we set
\eta^{n+1/2}=1
and
\phi^{n+1}=\tilde{\phi}^{n+1}
.

3
The unique solvability of (
3.23
)

In this section, we provide the unique solvability analysis of (
3.23
). To fix the idea, we consider the Cahn-Hilliard equation, a typical gradient flow. In this physical model, the energy functional is given by

E(\phi):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{4}\phi^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\ ,

(3.18)

where the constant
\varepsilon>0
stands for the interface width parameter, and
\mathcal{G}=-\Delta
. In turn, the Cahn-Hilliard equation can be written as

\partial_{t}\phi=\Delta\mu=\Delta\left(\phi^{3}-\phi-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\phi\right),

(3.19)

in which
\mu
is the chemical potential

\mu:=\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi}=\phi^{3}-\phi-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\phi.

(3.20)

For simplicity, periodic boundary conditions are imposed for both the phase variable,
\phi
, and the chemical potential,
\mu
. An extension to the case of homogeneous Neumann boundary condition would be straightforward.

In more details, the scheme (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) could be represented as

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}=\Delta\Bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\eta^{n+1/2}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})\Bigr),

(3.21)

\displaystyle\Big(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1\Big)=\eta^{n+1/2}\Big(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\Big).

(3.22)

Meanwhile, the Lagrange multiplier
\eta^{n+1/2}
is a solution of the nonlinear algebraic equation

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptg_{n}(\eta):=\int_{\Omega}\,\Big(F(p^{n}+\eta{\Delta t}q^{n})-F(\phi^{n})\Big)\,d\boldsymbol{x}-\eta\int_{\Omega}\,F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})(p^{n}+\eta{\Delta t}q^{n}-\phi^{n})\,d\boldsymbol{x}=0.

(3.23)

Setting
\phi^{n+1}=p^{n}+\eta^{n+1/2}{\Delta t}q^{n}
and plugging it into (
3.21
), we find that

\displaystyle p^{n}=(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}(\phi^{n}-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}),

(3.24)

\displaystyle q^{n}=(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}).

(3.25)

3.1
The main result

We aim to provide a theoretical analysis for the nonlinear algebraic equation (
3.23
), by making use of certain localized estimates.
The numerical error function is defined as

e^{k}:=\Phi^{k}-\phi^{k},\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(3.26)

in which
\Phi^{k}
is the exact solution to the original PDE (
3.19
), and
\phi^{k}
is denoted as the numerical solution of (
3.21
)-(
3.22
). With an initial data with sufficient regularity, it is assumed that the exact solution
\Phi
has regularity of class
\mathcal{R}
:

\Phi\in\mathcal{R}:=C^{3}(0,T;C^{2})\cap C^{2}(0,T;C^{6})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{8}).

(3.27)

In turn, the following functional bounds are available for the exact solution:

\|\partial_{t}^{3}\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};C^{2})}+\|\Phi_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};C^{6})}+\|\Phi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};C^{6})}\leq A^{*},\,\,\,\|\Phi^{k}\|_{H^{8}}\leq A^{*},\,\,\forall k\geq 0.

(3.28)

To proceed with the nonlinear analysis for (
3.23
), we begin with the following
a priori
assumption for the numerical error at the previous time steps:

\|e^{k}\|_{H^{4}}\leq{\Delta t}^{3/2},\quad\|e^{k}\|_{H^{6}}\leq{\Delta t},\quad\forall k\leq n.

(3.29)

In turn, the following
H^{4}
and
H^{6}
bounds are valid for the numerical solution at the previous time steps:

\displaystyle\|\phi^{k}\|_{H^{6}}\leq\|\Phi^{k}\|_{H^{6}}+\|e^{k}\|_{H^{4}}\leq A^{*}+{\Delta t}\leq A^{*}+1:=\tilde{A}_{1},\quad\mbox{for}\,\,\,k=n,n-1.

(3.30)

In particular, by the fact that
\phi^{*,n}=\frac{3}{2}\phi^{n}-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{n-1}
, we have the following
a-priori
bounds for
\phi^{*,n}
:

\displaystyle\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{2}},\,\,\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{4}},\,\,\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{6}}\leq 2\tilde{A}_{1},\quad\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{1}.

(3.31)

The
a priori
assumption (
3.29
) will be recovered by the convergence estimate at the next time step, as will be demonstrated in the later analysis.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose the exact solution
\Phi
for the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
3.19
), of regularity class
\mathcal{R}
, satisfies the assumption (
1.3
) with
S_{n}\geq 4{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{4}}
. We also make the a-priori assumption (
3.29
). Then the nonlinear scalar equation (
3.23
) has a unique solution in
[1-S_{n}^{2}/16,1+S_{n}^{2}/16]
.

We note that the numerical results presented in the last Section indicate that the condition
S_{n}\geq 4{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{4}}
(i.e.
\Delta t\leq(S_{n}/4)^{4}
) is most likely too pessimistic.

A few preliminary estimates are needed before we can proceed with the proof of this theorem.

3.2
Some preliminary estimates

For the sake of simplicity, we remove the dependent on
n
from all constants
{A_{k}}
below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
\phi^{n}
,
\phi^{n-1}
. Under the assumption (
1.3
) and the a-priori assumption (
3.29
), we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|p^{n}-\phi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{1}{\Delta t},\quad\|p^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{2},\quad\|q^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{3},

(3.32)

\displaystyle\|\phi^{*,n}-\phi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{4}{\Delta t},\quad\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{5},\quad\|\phi^{*,n}-p^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{6}{\Delta t},

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Big(F^{\prime}(p^{n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{n})\Bigr)\|\leq A_{7}{\Delta t},\quad\|\Delta\Big(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{n})\Bigr)\|\leq A_{8}{\Delta t},

(3.34)

\displaystyle\|F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{9}{\Delta t},\quad\|F^{\prime}(\Phi^{n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq A_{10}{\Delta t},

(3.35)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Big(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{n})\Bigr)\|\leq A_{11}{\Delta t},

(3.36)

in which
A_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq 10
) only depends on

.

Proof.

By the representation formula (
3.24
) for
p^{n}
, we see that

\displaystyle p^{n}-\phi^{n}=-\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),

(3.37)

\displaystyle\Delta(p^{n}-\phi^{n})=-\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{3}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}).

(3.38)

Meanwhile, by the
a priori

H^{6}
estimate (
3.30
) for the numerical solution
\phi^{n}
,
\phi^{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})\|,\,\,\|\Delta^{3}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})\|\leq(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4})\tilde{A}_{1}=\tilde{A}_{1}.

(3.39)

On the other hand, the following inequality is always available, because all the eigenvalues associated with the operator

\displaystyle\|(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}f\|\leq\|f\|,\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Then we arrive at

\displaystyle\|p^{n}-\phi^{n}\|\leq\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})\|\leq\tilde{A}_{1}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t},

\displaystyle\|\Delta(p^{n}-\phi^{n})\|\leq\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{3}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})\|\leq\tilde{A}_{1}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}.

For the third inequality in (
3.32
), we begin with the following expansion, based on the fact that

\frac{|S_{n}|}{2}{\Delta t}\leq g_{n}^{\prime}(\eta)\leq\frac{3|S_{n}|}{2}{\Delta t},\,\,\,\mbox{so that}\,\,\,|g_{n}^{\prime}(\eta)|\geq\frac{|S_{n}|}{2}{\Delta t},\,\,\,\mbox{for $1-{\Delta t}^{1/2}\leq\eta\leq 1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}$}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

3.3
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We can now proceed to the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
S_{n}<0
and
g_{n}(1)>0
, as the other cases could be analyzed in the same way. By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq g_{n}(1)\leq A_{12}{\Delta t}^{2},\quad g_{n}^{\prime}(\eta)\geq-\frac{S_{n}}{2}{\Delta t},\,\,\,\mbox{for $1-{\Delta t}^{1/2}\leq\eta\leq 1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}$}.

(3.82)

Then we conclude that,
g_{n}(\eta)
is monotone and increasing over the interval
(1-\delta^{*},1)
, with
\delta^{*}=-\frac{2A_{12}}{S_{n}}{\Delta t}
, so that

\displaystyle g_{n}(1)\geq 0,\,\,\,g_{n}(1-\delta^{*})\leq g_{n}(1)+\frac{S_{n}}{2}{\Delta t}\cdot\delta^{*}\leq A_{12}{\Delta t}^{2}-A_{12}{\Delta t}^{2}=0.

(3.83)

Therefore, there is a unique solution for
g_{n}(\eta)=0
over the interval
(1-\delta^{*},1)
. In addition, we have
\delta^{*}\leq{\Delta t}^{1/2}
, if
{\Delta t}\leq\frac{S^{4}_{n}}{256}\leq\frac{S_{n}^{2}}{4A_{12}}
with
|S_{n}|\ll 1
. Since
g_{n}(\eta)
is increasing over
[1-{\Delta t}^{1/2},1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}]
, such a solution is unique in the interval
[1-{\Delta t}^{1/2},1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}]
. The proof is completed.

4
Error analysis

Given a tolerance
\gamma>0
, we shall assume

\Big|\int_{\Omega}\,F^{\prime}(\Phi)\Phi_{t}\,d\boldsymbol{x}\Big|\geq\gamma,\text{ for }0\leq t\leq T,

(4.84)

which implies in particular that
|S_{n}|\geq\gamma
for all
n\leq T/\Delta t
.

Usually, the above assumption is only satisfied for
0\leq t\leq T_{0}\leq T
, but for the sake of simplicity we shall assume
T_{0}=T
. An error analysis is carried out in this section for the scheme (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) under the above assumption. For the time intervals where (
4.84
) is not satisfied, we use the modified Lagrange multiplier approach by setting
\eta^{n+1/2}=1
and computing
\phi^{n+1}
by (
3.21
).

The main result of this section is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given initial data
\Phi^{0}\in H^{8}_{\rm per}(\Omega)
, suppose the exact solution for the Cahn-Hilliard equation (
3.19
) is of regularity class
\mathcal{R}
,
and satisfies the assumption (
1.3
). Then, provided that
{\Delta t}\leq C\min\Big((\hat{C})^{-2},\hat{C}^{2}\varepsilon^{2},\frac{\gamma^{4}}{4}\Big)
, we have

\|\Delta^{2}e^{m}\|+\Big(\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\Delta^{3}e^{k}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\hat{C}{\Delta t}^{2},

(4.85)

for all positive integers
m
, such that
t^{m}=m{\Delta t}\leq T
, where
\hat{C},C>0
are some positive constants independent of
n
and
{\Delta t}
.

The proof of this theorem will be carried out through a series of intermediate estimates which we describe below.

4.1
Energy stability and the
H^{1}
estimate

The following result could be proved in a straightforward way.

Lemma 4.1
.

If the numerical system (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) is solvable, it is energy stable with respect to the modified energy functional:
\tilde{E}(\phi^{n+1},\phi^{n}):=E(\phi^{n+1})+\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\|^{2}
, i.e., the following energy inequality holds:

\tilde{E}(\phi^{n+1},\phi^{n})\leq\tilde{E}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n-1}),\quad\forall n\geq 0.

(4.86)

Proof.

Taking an inner product with (
3.21
) by
(-\Delta)^{-1}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})
gives

(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\eta^{n+1/2},\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})-\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\Delta(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\Big)=-\frac{1}{{\Delta t}}\|\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}.

(4.87)

Meanwhile, the following estimates are available:

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\eta^{n+1/2},\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})=\Big(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1\Big),\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{scheme-SAVN-2})},

\displaystyle-\Big(\Delta(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\Big)=\Big(\nabla(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1}),\nabla(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{1}{2}(\|\nabla\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\nabla\phi^{n}\|^{2})+\frac{1}{8}(\|\nabla(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\|^{2}-\|\nabla(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})\|^{2}).

Going back (
4.87
), we arrive at the desired inequality (
4.86
).

As a result of this result, we obtain a uniform bound of the original energy functional
E(\phi^{n})
, for any
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle E(\phi^{n})\leq\tilde{E}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n-1})\leq...\leq\tilde{E}(\phi^{0},\phi^{-1})=E(\phi^{0}):=C_{0},\quad\mbox{by taking $\phi^{-1}=\phi^{0}$}.

Meanwhile, since
E(\phi^{n})\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{n}\|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{n}\|^{2}\leq E(\phi^{n})\leq C_{0},\quad\mbox{so that}\,\,\|\nabla\phi^{n}\|\leq(2C_{0})^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1}.

And also, the numerical solution (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) is mass conservative, so that

\displaystyle\overline{\phi^{n}}=\overline{\phi^{n-1}}=...=\overline{\phi^{0}}:=\beta_{0},\quad\forall n\geq 0.

In turn, an application of Poincaré inequality yields a uniform in time
H^{1}
bound for the numerical solution:

\displaystyle\|\phi^{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\Big(|\overline{\phi^{n}}|+\|\nabla\phi^{n}\|\Big)\leq C_{1}:=C(|\beta_{0}|+(2C_{0})^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1}),\quad\forall n\geq 0.

(4.88)

We notice that
C_{1}
is uniform in time, while it depends on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial pattern.

4.2
The
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2})
estimate for the numerical solution

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the assumption (
1.3
) with
\gamma\geq 4{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{4}}
and the a priori assumption (
3.29
), we have

\|\phi^{n}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C_{2},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

(4.89)

in which
C_{2}
depends on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial pattern, while it is independent on
{\Delta t}
and
T
.

Proof.

First, it is noticed that, by the assumptions and Theorem 3.1, the scheme (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) has a unique solution in
1-{\Delta t}^{1/2}\leq\eta\leq 1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}
.
Taking an inner product with (
3.21
) by
2\Delta^{2}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Delta\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\Delta\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\|\Delta(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}(\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1},\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{2}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{n-1}))

(4.90)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta^{n+1/2}{\Delta t}(\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}).

The inner product associated surface diffusion could be analyzed as follows:

\displaystyle(\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1},\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{2}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{n-1}))=\frac{3}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1},\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}\Big)\geq\frac{5}{4}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}.

For the right hand side in (
4.90
), we recall the expansion (
3.44
) and apply the Hölder inequality:

\displaystyle\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|\leq(3\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+1)\|\Delta\phi^{*,n}\|+6\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|\nabla\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

(4.91)

Meanwhile, the following estimates are available, based on Sobolev embedding and weighted inequalities, as well as the uniform in time estimate (
4.88
):

\displaystyle\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{1}}^{5/6}\cdot\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{4}}^{1/6}\leq C\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{1}}^{5/6}\cdot(\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|)^{1/6}

(4.92)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CC_{1}^{5/6}\cdot(C_{1}+\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|)^{1/6},

\displaystyle\|\Delta\phi^{*,n}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi^{*,n}\|^{2/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{1/3}\leq CC_{1}^{2/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{1/3},

(4.93)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{3/4}}\leq C\|\nabla\phi^{*,n}\|^{3/4}\cdot\|\Delta\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{4}}^{1/4}\leq CC_{1}^{3/4}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{1/4}.

(4.94)

Going back (
4.91
), we get

\displaystyle\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|\leq CC_{1}^{3}+CC_{1}^{7/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{2/3}+CC_{1}^{2/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{1/3}.

(4.95)

In turn, an application of Cauchy inequality implies that

\displaystyle 2\eta^{n+1/2}(\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1})\leq 3\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|

(4.96)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 9\varepsilon^{-2}\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{-2}(C_{1}^{6}+C_{1}^{14/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{4/3}+C_{1}^{4/3}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{2/3})+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{10}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}+\frac{9\varepsilon^{2}}{20}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{20}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2},

in which the Young’s inequality has been extensively applied in the fourth step, and the last step is based on the fact that
\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{*,n}\|^{2}\leq\frac{9}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}
. We also notice that
C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}
depends on
C_{1}
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial pattern.

Subsequently, a substitution of (
4.2
) and (
4.96
) into (
4.90
) leads to

\|\Delta\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\Delta\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}{\Delta t}+\frac{9\varepsilon^{2}}{20}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{3\varepsilon^{2}}{10}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}.

In fact, this inequality could be rewritten as

\displaystyle\|\Delta\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{4}{5}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{10}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{5}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{20}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{4}{5}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{10}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}{\Delta t}.

With an introduction of a modified
H^{2}
energy

\displaystyle G^{n}:=\|\Delta\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{4}{5}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{10}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{2}\phi^{n-1}\|^{2},

we get

\displaystyle G^{n+1}+B_{0}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}G^{n+1}\leq G^{n}+C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}{\Delta t},

in which the following elliptic regularity has been used:

\displaystyle B_{1}\|\Delta f\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{2}f\|^{2},\quad\mbox{and}\,\,\,B_{0}=\min\Big(\frac{B_{1}}{10},\frac{1}{8}\Big).

An application of the induction argument implies that

\displaystyle G^{n+1}\leq(1+B_{0}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t})^{-(n+1)}G^{0}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}}{B_{0}\varepsilon^{2}}.

Of course, we could introduce a uniform in time quantity
B_{2}^{*}:=G^{0}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}}{B_{0}\varepsilon^{2}}
, so that
\|\Delta\phi^{n}\|^{2}\leq G^{n}\leq B_{2}^{*}
for any
n\geq 0
. In turn, an application of the elliptic regularity reveals that

\displaystyle\|\phi^{n}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\Big(|\overline{\phi^{k}}|+\|\Delta\phi^{k}\|\Big)\leq C(|\beta_{0}|+(B_{2}^{*})^{1/2}):=C_{2},\quad\forall n\geq 0.

in which the uniform in time constant
C_{2}
depends on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial pattern. This finishes the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Using similar tools, a uniform-in-time
H^{k}
bound for the numerical solution could be established, for any
k\geq 3
, by taking inner product with (
3.21
) by
2(-\Delta)^{k}\phi^{n+1}
, and performing the associated estimates. The details are left to interested readers.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, we have for any
k\geq 3
,

\|\phi^{n}\|_{H^{k}}\leq C_{k},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

(4.97)

in which
C_{k}
depends on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial power, while it is independent on
{\Delta t}
and
T
.

4.3
Estimate for
\|\phi^{m+1}-\phi^{m}\|_{H^{\ell}}

The following estimate is needed in the later analysis.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq m\leq M-1}\|\phi^{m+1}-\phi^{m}\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq D_{\ell}{\Delta t},

(4.98)

where
D_{\ell}>0
is a constant independent of
{\Delta t}
and
T
, dependent on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial style.

Proof.

The numerical scheme (
3.21
) implies that

\|\phi^{m+1}-\phi^{m}\|_{H^{\ell}}={\Delta t}\|\Delta\mu^{m+1/2}\|_{H^{\ell}},\quad\mu^{m+1/2}=F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,m})\eta^{m+1/2}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{m+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{m-1}).

(4.99)

By the expansion for
\mu^{m+1/2}
, the following estimates could be derived, helped by repeated applications of the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding:

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,m})\right\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,m})\right\|_{H^{\ell+2}}\leq C\left\|\phi^{*,m}\right\|_{H^{\ell+2}}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\phi^{m}\right\|_{H^{\ell+2}}^{3}+\left\|\phi^{m-1}\right\|_{H^{\ell+2}}^{3}\right)\leq CC_{\ell+2}^{3},

(4.100)

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{m+1}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{m-1})\right\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\phi^{m+1}\right\|_{H^{\ell+4}}+\left\|\phi^{m-1}\right\|_{H^{\ell+4}}\right)\leq CC_{\ell+4},

(4.101)

with the uniform-in-time estimates (
4.97
) used. Consequently, a substitution of (
4.100
)-(
4.101
) into (
4.99
) yields

\left\|\phi^{m+1}-\phi^{m}\right\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq D_{\ell}{\Delta t},\quad D_{\ell}:=C\left(C_{\ell+2}^{3}+\varepsilon^{2}C_{\ell+4}\right).

Note that
D_{\ell}
depends on
\varepsilon^{-1}
in a polynomial form, since both
C_{\ell+2}
and
C_{\ell+4}
do as well. This completes the proof of Proposition
4.3
.

4.4
A refined estimate for
\eta^{n+1/2}

An
O({\Delta t}^{1/2})
estimate have been derived for
|\eta^{n+1/2}|
in Theorem
3.1
. In fact, by the estimate (
3.82
), we see that

\displaystyle|\eta^{n+1/2}-1|\leq\frac{A_{12}{\Delta t}^{2}}{\frac{|S_{n}|}{2}{\Delta t}}=\frac{2A_{12}{\Delta t}}{|S_{n}|}\leq\frac{2A_{12}{\Delta t}}{\gamma}.

(4.102)

Again, this rough estimate is not sufficient for an optimal rate of convergence. We aim to improve this estimate so that
\eta^{n+1/2}-1=O({\Delta t}^{2})
. The following preliminary lemma is needed.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}-\phi^{*,n}\right\|\leq Q_{0}{\Delta t}^{2},

(4.103)

where
Q_{0}
is a constant independent of
{\Delta t}
, dependent on the exact solution
\Phi
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
.

Proof.

A careful calculation reveals that

\displaystyle\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}-\phi^{*,n}=\frac{p^{n}-\phi^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})

(4.104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\Delta t}(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}\Big(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\Big)-\frac{1}{2}(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\Delta t}\Big((I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}-I\Big)r^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Big({\Delta t}r^{n}-(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})\Big),

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\,\,\,r^{n}=-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}),

in which the representation formulas (
3.25
), (
3.37
) have been applied.

For the first part of (
4.104
), we see that
\Delta^{2}r^{n}=-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{4}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\Delta^{3}F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})
, and the following estimates could be derived by (
4.97
) :

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{4}\phi^{n}\|,\,\,\|\Delta^{4}\phi^{n-1}\|\leq C_{8},

(4.105)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{3}F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|\leq\|F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|_{H^{6}}\leq C(\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{6}}^{3}+\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{6}})\leq C(C_{6}^{3}+C_{6}),

(4.106)

in which the 3-D Sobolev embedding and Hölder inequality have been extensively applied, as well as the fact that
F^{\prime}(\phi)=\phi^{3}-\phi
. Then we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{2}r^{n}\|=\|-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{4}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-1})+\Delta^{3}F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})\|\leq Q_{1}:=\varepsilon^{2}C_{8}+C(C_{6}^{3}+C_{6}).

(4.107)

This in turn leads to

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big((I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}-I\Big)r^{n}\Big\|=\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|(I+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{2}r^{n}\|\leq\frac{3}{4}Q_{1}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}.

For the second part of (
4.104
), we make use of the following expression of
\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1}
(which comes from the numerical scheme (
3.21
)):

\displaystyle\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1}=

\displaystyle\eta^{n-1/2}{\Delta t}\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})-\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2}(\frac{3}{4}\phi^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\phi^{n-2}),\quad\mbox{so that}

\displaystyle{\Delta t}r^{n}-(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})=

\displaystyle(\eta^{n-1/2}-1){\Delta t}\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})+{\Delta t}\Delta(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2}(\phi^{n-1}-\phi^{n-2}).

(4.108)

For the first part of the expansion (
4.108
), from (
4.89
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})\|\leq\|F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(\|\phi^{*,n-1}\|_{H^{2}}^{3}+\|\phi^{*,n-1}\|_{H^{2}})\leq C(C_{2}^{3}+C_{2}).

(4.109)

Its combination with the preliminary rough estimate (
4.102
) implies that

\displaystyle\|(\eta^{n-1/2}-1){\Delta t}\Delta F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})\|\leq\frac{CA_{12}{\Delta t}^{2}}{|S_{n}^{*}|}(C_{2}^{3}+C_{2}).

(4.110)

For the second part of the expansion (
4.108
), we begin with the following identify:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1})=F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(3)})(\phi^{*,n}-\phi^{*,n-1}),

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\,\,\,F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(3)})=(\phi^{*,n})^{2}+\phi^{*,n}\phi^{*,n-1}+(\phi^{*,n-1})^{2}-1.

(4.111)

In turn, extensive applications of Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality indicate that

\displaystyle\|\Delta(F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n-1}))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(3)})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\phi^{*,n}-\phi^{*,n-1}\|_{H^{2}}

(4.112)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(3)})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}(\|\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\phi^{n-1}-\phi^{n-2}\|_{H^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(C_{2}^{2}+1)\cdot D_{2}{\Delta t},

in which the
H^{2}
estimate (
4.89
) and the discrete temporal derivative estimate (
4.98
) have been applied in the last step. For the third part of the expansion (
4.108
), we make use of (
4.98
) again, and get

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Delta^{2}(\phi^{n-1}-\phi^{n-2})\|\leq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\cdot D_{4}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{4}D_{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}^{2}.

(4.113)

Therefore, a substitution of (
4.110
), (
4.112
) and (
4.113
) into (
4.108
) leads to the following estimate:

\|{\Delta t}r^{n}-(\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1})\|\leq Q_{2}{\Delta t}^{2},\quad\mbox{with}\,\,\,Q_{2}=\frac{CA_{12}}{|S_{n}^{*}|}(C_{2}^{3}+C_{2})+C(C_{2}^{2}+1)D_{2}+\frac{1}{4}D_{4}\varepsilon^{2}.

(4.114)

Finally, a combination of (
4.104
), (
4.106
) and (
4.114
) yields the desired result (
4.103
), by taking
Q_{0}=\frac{3}{8}Q_{1}\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{2}
. This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.5
.

As a consequence, we are able to obtain a sharper estimate for
g(1)
, in comparison with Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, we have
|g_{n}(1)|\leq A_{18}{\Delta t}^{3}
, with
Q_{3}
a constant independent of
{\Delta t}
, but dependent on the exact solution
\Phi
.

Proof.

The expansion formula (
3.47
) of
g(1)
indicates that

\displaystyle g_{n}(1)=J_{1}+J_{2},\quad

\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\,\Big(F(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n})-F(\phi^{n})-F^{\prime}(\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2})(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}-\phi^{n})\Big)\,d\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle J_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\,\Big((F^{\prime}(\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}))(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}-\phi^{n})\Big)\,d\boldsymbol{x}.

(4.115)

For the
J_{1}
part, we make use of the following identity

\displaystyle F(x)-F(y)-F^{\prime}(\frac{x+y}{2})(x-y)=\frac{x+y}{8}(x-y)^{3},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathrm{R},\quad\mbox{so that}

\displaystyle F(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n})-F(\phi^{n})-F^{\prime}(\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2})(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}-\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n})\cdot(p^{n}-\phi^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n})^{3}.

In turn, with an application of Hölder inequality, combined with the inequalities (
3.32
), (
3.49
), we obtain

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\|p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\|p^{n}-\phi^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}\cdot|\Omega|

(4.116)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}(A_{2}+CC_{2}+1)(A_{1}+A_{3})^{3}|\Omega|{\Delta t}^{3}.

For the
J_{2}
part, we begin with the following application of intermediate value theorem:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})=F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(4)})\cdot(\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}-\phi^{*,n}),

with
\xi^{(4)}
between
\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}
and
\phi^{*,n}
. By the fact that
F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)=3\phi^{2}-1
and the
L^{\infty}
bounds for
\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}
and
\phi^{*,n}
, we get

\displaystyle\|F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(4)})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\Big(\|\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\phi^{*,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+1\Bigr)\leq C(A_{2}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}+1).

Then we arrive at

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(4)})\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\left\|\frac{p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}+\phi^{n}}{2}-\phi^{*,n}\right\|\cdot\|p^{n}+{\Delta t}q^{n}-\phi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot|\Omega|^{1/2}

(4.117)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(A_{2}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}+1)\cdot Q_{0}{\Delta t}^{2}\cdot(A_{1}+A_{3}){\Delta t}\cdot|\Omega|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CQ_{0}|\Omega|(A_{2}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}+1)(A_{1}+A_{3}){\Delta t}^{3},

in which the estimate (
4.103
) (in Lemma
4.5
) has been applied in the second step. Therefore, a combination of (
4.115
), (
4.116
) and (
4.117
) yields the desired result, by taking
A_{18}=\frac{1}{8}(A_{2}+CC_{2}+1)(A_{1}+A_{3})^{3}|\Omega|+CQ_{0}|\Omega|(A_{2}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}+1)(A_{1}+A_{3})
. This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Next, we have to obtain a refined estimate for
|\eta^{n+1/2}-1|
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, we have
|\eta^{n+1/2}-1|\leq\frac{2A_{18}}{\gamma}{\Delta t}^{2}
with
A_{18}
dependent on the exact solution
\Phi
.

Proof.

Again, it is assumed that
g_{n}(1)>0
, without loss of generality. Following the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we see that

\displaystyle 0\leq g_{n}(1)\leq A_{18}{\Delta t}^{3},\quad g^{\prime}_{n}(\eta)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}{\Delta t},\,\,\,\mbox{for $1-{\Delta t}^{1/2}\leq\eta\leq 1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}$}.

Then we conclude that,
g_{n}(\eta)
is monotone and increasing over the interval
(1-\delta^{**},1)
, with
\delta^{**}=\frac{2A_{18}}{\gamma}{\Delta t}^{2}
, so that

\displaystyle g_{n}(1)\geq 0,\,\,\,g_{n}(1-\delta^{**})\leq g_{n}(1)-\frac{\gamma}{2}{\Delta t}\cdot\delta^{**}\leq A_{18}{\Delta t}^{3}-A_{18}{\Delta t}^{3}=0.

Therefore, there is a unique solution for
g_{n}(\eta)=0
over the interval
(1-\delta^{**},1)
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.7
.

4.5
Proof of Theorem 4.1

First of all, it is clear that both the exact solution
\Phi(\cdot,t)
and the numerical solution
\{\phi^{k}\}
are mass conservative:

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi^{k}}=\overline{\Phi^{0}},\quad\overline{\phi^{k}}=\overline{\phi^{0}},\,\,\,\forall k\geq 0,

(4.118)

in which
\Phi^{k}
is the exact solution evaluated at the time instant
t^{k}
. Then we conclude that the numerical error function must have zero-average at each time step:

\displaystyle\overline{e^{k}}=0,\,\,\,\forall k\geq 0,\quad\mbox{by taking $\phi^{0}=\Phi^{0}$}.

(4.119)

In turn, the following elliptic regularity estimates are available:

\displaystyle\|e^{k}\|_{H^{2m}}\leq C\|\Delta^{m}e^{k}\|,\quad\mbox{for $m=1,2,3$},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(4.120)

First, the
a priori
assumption (
3.29
) is made. As a result, the unique solvability for
\eta^{n+1/2}
(in the range of
1-{\Delta t}^{1/2}\leq\eta^{n+1/2}\leq 1+{\Delta t}^{1/2}
) is assured by Theorem
3.1
, and a refined estimate in Lemma
4.7
becomes available.

The following truncation error analysis for the exact solution can be obtained by using a straightforward Taylor expansion:

\frac{\Phi^{n+1}-\Phi^{n}}{{\Delta t}}=\Delta\Bigl(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta(\frac{3}{4}\Phi^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\Phi^{n-1})\Bigr)+\tau^{n},\quad\Phi^{*,n}=\frac{3}{2}\Phi^{n}-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{n-1},

(4.121)

with
\|\Delta\tau^{n}\|\leq C{\Delta t}^{2}
.

In turn, subtracting the numerical scheme (
3.21
) from the consistency estimate (
4.121
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{{\Delta t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\eta^{n+1/2})\Delta F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})

(4.122)

\displaystyle+\Delta\Bigl(\eta^{n+1/2}(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}))-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta(\frac{3}{4}e^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}e^{n-1})\Bigr)+\tau^{n}.

Taking an inner product with (
4.122
) by
2\Delta^{4}e^{n+1}
gives

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{2}e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\Delta^{2}e^{n}\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}(e^{n+1}-e^{n})\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\Bigl(\Delta^{3}(\frac{3}{2}e^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}e^{n-1}),\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\Bigr)

(4.123)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\eta^{n+1/2}){\Delta t}(\Delta^{2}F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n}),\Delta^{3}e^{n+1})+2\eta^{n+1/2}{\Delta t}(\Delta^{2}(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})),\Delta^{3}e^{n+1})

\displaystyle+2{\Delta t}(\Delta\tau^{n},\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}),

in which the integration by parts have been extensively applied in the derivation. The surface diffusion term could be analyzed similarly as in (
4.2
):

\displaystyle(\Delta^{3}e^{n+1},\Delta^{3}(\frac{3}{2}e^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}e^{n-1}))=\frac{3}{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{3}e^{n+1},\Delta^{3}e^{n-1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n-1}\|^{2}\Big)\geq\frac{5}{4}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n-1}\|^{2}.

The local truncation error term could be bounded by the Cauchy inequality:

\displaystyle 2(\Delta\tau^{n},\Delta^{3}e^{n+1})\leq 2\|\Delta\tau^{n}\|\cdot\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\|\Delta\tau^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}.

(4.124)

For the first nonlinear inner product, we recall the refined estimate,
|\eta^{n+1/2}-1|\leq Q_{3}{\Delta t}^{2}
, as established in Lemma
4.7
. In addition, the following estimate could be derived:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})\leq C(\|\Phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{4}}^{3}+\|\Phi^{*,n}\|_{H^{4}})\leq C((A^{*})^{3}+A^{*}),

(4.125)

based on the fact that
F^{\prime}(\phi)=\phi^{3}-\phi
, and the bound (
3.28
) for the exact solution. Then we arrive at

\displaystyle 2(1-\eta^{n+1/2})(\Delta^{2}F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n}),\Delta^{3}e^{n+1})\leq 3Q_{3}{\Delta t}^{2}\cdot\|\Delta^{2}F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})\|\cdot\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|

(4.126)

\displaystyle\leq C((A^{*})^{3}+A^{*})Q_{3}{\Delta t}^{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|\leq C((A^{*})^{3}+A^{*})^{2}Q_{3}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}{\Delta t}^{4}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}.

For the other nonlinear error term, we begin with the following application of intermediate value theorem:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})=F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(5)})e^{*,n},\,\,\,\mbox{with}

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{(5)})=(\Phi^{*,n})^{2}+\Phi^{*,n}\phi^{*,n}+(\phi^{*,n})^{2}-1,

\displaystyle e^{*,n}=\frac{3}{2}e^{n}-\frac{1}{2}e^{n-1}.

This in turn leads to the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{2}(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n}))\|=

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C((A^{*})^{2}+C_{4}^{2}+1)\cdot\|\Delta^{2}e^{*,n}\|.

Consequently, the following inequality is available:

\displaystyle 2\eta^{n+1/2}(\Delta^{2}(F^{\prime}(\Phi^{*,n})-F^{\prime}(\phi^{*,n})),\Delta^{3}e^{n+1})\leq C((A^{*})^{2}+C_{4}^{2}+1)\|\Delta^{2}e^{*,n}\|\cdot\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|

(4.127)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C((A^{*})^{2}+C_{4}^{2}+1)^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\|\Delta^{2}e^{*,n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C((A^{*})^{2}+C_{4}^{2}+1)^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(\|\Delta^{2}e^{n}\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}e^{n-1}\|^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}.

Subsequently, a substitution of (
4.5

4.124

4.126

4.127

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{2}e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\Delta^{2}e^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C((A^{*})^{3}+A^{*})^{2}Q_{3}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}{\Delta t}^{5}+Q_{4}\varepsilon^{-2}(\|\Delta^{2}e^{n}\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}e^{n-1}\|^{2})+\varepsilon^{-2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta\tau^{n}\|^{2},

with

. Therefore, an application of discrete Gronwall inequality yields the desired convergence estimate (
4.85

Finally, we have to recover the

to close the induction argument. By the convergence estimate (

\|\Delta^{2}e^{n+1}\|\leq\hat{C}{\Delta t}^{2},\quad\varepsilon^{2}{\Delta t}\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|^{2}\leq\hat{C}^{2}{\Delta t}^{4},\,\,\,\mbox{so that}\,\,\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|\leq\hat{C}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{3/2}.

Making use of the elliptic regularity estimate (

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|_{H^{4}}\leq C\|\Delta^{2}e^{n+1}\|\leq Q_{5}\hat{C}{\Delta t}^{2},\quad\|e^{n+1}\|_{H^{6}}\leq C\|\Delta^{3}e^{n+1}\|\leq Q_{6}\hat{C}\varepsilon^{-1}{\Delta t}^{3/2}.

Therefore, the

This finishes the complete induction argument for both the solvability analysis and convergence estimate.

5
Concluding remarks

The unique solvability and error analysis of the original Lagrange multiplier approach, proposed in
[
8
]
for gradient flows, is considered in this article. We first identified a necessary and sufficient condition that must be satisfied for the system of nonlinear algebraic equations, arising from the original Lagrange multiplier approach, to admit a unique solution in the neighborhood of its exact solution. Afterward, we proposed a modified Lagrange multiplier approach to ensure that the computation can continue even if the aforementioned condition was not satisfied.

However, a globally unique solvability of the nonlinear system of algebraic equations is not possible, due to the non-monotone feature of the implicit part in the numerical system. Using the Cahn-Hilliard equation and a second-order modified Crank-Nicholson scheme with a Lagrange multiplier as an example, we have provided a unique solvability analysis using localized estimates in the neighborhood of its exact solution under an
a-priori
assumption, and then carried out a rigorous error analysis, which in turn recovered the
a-priori
assumption using an inductive argument.

The results presented in this paper are the first unique solvability analysis and error estimate for the original Lagrange multiplier approach. In a future work, we shall consider the theoretical analysis for a more general Lagrange multiplier approach for gradient flows with global constraints proposed in
[
9
]
. Due to the multiple Lagrange multipliers involved in the latter approach, the analysis would be much more challenging, while the analytical techniques introduced in this paper may be helpful.

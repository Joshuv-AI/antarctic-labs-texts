Abstract
In this paper we examine the problem of computing majority function M ​ A ​ J n MAJ_{n} on n n bits by depth-two formula, where each gate is a majority function on at most k k inputs. We present such formula that gives the first nontrivial upper bound for this problem, with k = 2 3 ​ n + 4 k=\frac{2}{3}n+4 . This answers an open question in [ 7 ] . We also look at this problem in adaptive setting — when we are allowed to query for value of M ​ A ​ J k MAJ_{k} on any subset, and wish to minimize the number of such queries. We give a simple lower bound for this setting with ⌈ n / k ⌉ \lceil n/k\rceil queries, and we present two algorithms for this model: the first one makes ≈ 2 ​ n k ​ log ⁡ k \approx 2\frac{n}{k}\log k queries in the case when we are limited to the standard majority functions, and the second one makes n k ​ log ⁡ k \frac{n}{k}\log k queries when we are allowed to change the threshold of majority function.

Introduction

We study the problem of computing majority function
MAJ_{n}
if we are only allowed to query values of
MAJ_{k}
(that is, majority on
k
variables) functions.

Majority functions and circuits consisting of them arise in various areas of computational complexity (see e.g.
[
9
,
3
,
4
]
).
Particularly, iterated (or recursive) majority that consists of iterated application of majority of small number of inputs to itself, provides an example of a function with interesting complexity properties in various models (
[
5
,
8
,
10
,
6
]
), and helps in various constructions.

Another motivation for this problem comes from the studies of boolean circuits of constant depth.

\widehat{\textsf{TC}}{}^{0}
is defined as the class of functions computable by constant-depth polynomial-sized circuits consisting of majority gates, and it plays one of the major roles in this area (
[
4
]
).

The first model we look at is a standard boolean circuit of depth two, but where all the gates are threshold functions of at most
k
variables.
It was shown in
[
1
]
that to be able to compute such circuits
k
must be
\Omega(n^{4/5})
.
There is a trivial upper bound
k=n
: just take all the gates on the first level to match inputs (that is, gate
f_{i}=x_{i}=MAJ_{\{i\}}(x)
), and the output gate is a standard majority
MAJ_{n}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
.

[
7
]
did not provide any nontrivial upper bound on
k
and posed an open question whether such bound exists.
We answer this question positively and present the depth-two circuit with
MAJ_{\frac{2}{3}n+4}
gates that computes
MAJ_{n}
function.
This result was independently obtained by Bruno Bauwens by computer search, as told to us in personal communication.
For the sake of completeness, we also give a simple proof of lower bound
k\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2}}n^{2/3}
.

The second model is an adaptive one — algorithm can query the
MAJ_{k}
function on any subset of size at most
k
, and we are interested in the worst case number of queries made to compute
MAJ_{n}
.
This problem was motivated by the proof in
[
7
]
, which does not use any properties of the function in the output gate except for its monotonicity, and also by
[
2
]
, where they study adaptive computation of
MAJ_{n}
with discrepancy queries on
k
elements.
We give a simple proof for the lower bound of
\lceil n/k\rceil
queries, and we also present two algorithms for adaptive setting: one that runs in fixed-threshold setting and which requires
2(\frac{n}{k-4}+1)(\log k+4)\approx 2\frac{n}{k}\log k
queries, and another that runs in adjustable-threshold adaptive model (that is, when the algorithm can specify majority’s threshold) and requires
\lceil n/k\rceil\lceil\log(k+1)\rceil
queries.

Definitions

By
\log
we denote binary logarithm and

n
always means the length of input vector
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.

We are going to study majority functions
MAJ_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=[x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}\geq n/2]
, where
[P]=1
if condition
P
is true, and
[P]=0
otherwise.
We will also call
MAJ_{k}
standard majority functions.
By
sum_{S}(x)
we denote
\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}
.
We define
MAJ_{S}(x)
for
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
as
[sum_{S}(x)\geq|S|/2]
.
We also define
MAJ_{S}(x;t)=[sum_{S}(x)\geq t]
.

Threshold functions are a generalization of majority functions:
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=[\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}x_{n}\geq t]
.

The first (“static“) model that we look at is
MAJ_{k}\circ MAJ_{k}
— it is defined as depth-two formula, consisting of threshold gates
[\sum\alpha_{i}x_{i}\geq t]
such that
\sum\alpha_{i}\leq k
and
\alpha_{i}
are positive integers.

In the second (“adaptive“) model an algorithm is given access to an oracle that knows vector
x
.
There are two variations of this model that we look at.

In the adaptive model with adjustable threshold the oracle receives a pair
(S,t)
, where
S
is a set
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
|S|\leq k
and
t
is an integer, and responds with the value of
MAJ_{S}(x;t)
.

In the adaptive model with fixed threshold the oracle receives a set
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
|S|\leq k
and responds with the value of
MAJ_{S}(x)
.

3
Static model

3.1
Lower bound

Lemma 1
.

For large enough
n
,
k
should be at least
\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2}}n^{2/3}
in order to compute
MAJ_{n}
in
MAJ_{k}\circ MAJ_{k}
model.

This is not the best currently known lower bound, for a more involved proof of
k\geq n^{13/19+o(1)}
see
[
7
]
.
This proof is simpler and is adapted from the proof of a stronger claim in
[
7
]
that shows that for some function in
MAJ_{k}\circ MAJ_{k}
to differ from
MAJ_{n}
on small fraction (less than
1/10
) of inputs
x
with
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
,
k
must be
\Omega(n^{2/3})
.
We provide this proof just for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

Let’s look at the boolean hypercube
H^{n}=\{0,1\}^{n}
.
It is known (
[
11
]
) that among all monotone boolean functions majority is the one with the largest influence — that is, its value changes on the largest possible number of edges of the hypercube.
The number of such edges for
MAJ_{k}
is
k\binom{k}{k/2}\leq k^{1/2}2^{k}
.
So if some gate
f_{i}
depends on
l\leq k
variables, then it changes its value on at most
l^{1/2}2^{l}
edges of
H^{l}
.

Obviously, on each edge
(u,v)
in
H^{n}
such that
\sum_{i}u_{i}<n/2,\sum_{i}v_{i}\geq n/2
, there must be at least one function
f_{i}
in the first level of the formula, such that
f_{i}(u)\not=f_{i}(v)
.
There are
n\binom{n}{n/2}>\frac{n^{1/2}2^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}
such edges.

Let
f_{i}
be some gate in the first level of the formula, without loss of generality assume that
f_{i}
depends only on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}
for
l\leq k
.
Then, for each
u\in H^{l}
there are at most
2^{n-l}/(n-l)^{1/2}
possible continuations
u_{l+1},\ldots,u_{n}
of
u
that have
\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}u_{i}=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
.
This is so, since
u_{l+1},\ldots,u_{n}
must sum to
\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}u_{i}
, and the number of such assignments of
u
is a binomial coefficient.
The largest binomial coefficient
\binom{n-l}{z}
for fixed
n-l
is the central one:
z=\lceil(n-l)/2\rceil
, for which there is a well-known bound
\binom{n-l}{z}\leq 2^{n-l}/(n-l)^{1/2}
.

This means that for a fixed
f_{i}
there are at most
l\frac{2^{l}}{l^{1/2}}\frac{2^{n-l}}{(n-l)^{1/2}}\leq\sqrt{2}k^{1/2}\frac{2^{n}}{n^{1/2}}
such edges
(u,v)
that
\sum_{i}u_{i}<n/2,\sum_{i}v_{i}\geq n/2
and
f_{i}(u)\not=f_{i}(v)
(note that
(n-l)\geq\frac{1}{2}n
for large enough
n
, if
l\leq k=\mathcal{O}(n^{2/3})
).
Summing this for all
f_{i}
we get:

\sqrt{2}k^{3/2}\frac{2^{n}}{n^{1/2}}\geq\frac{n^{1/2}2^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\implies k^{3}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}

∎

3.2
Upper bound

Theorem 2
.

For any
n

MAJ_{n}
is computable by some circuit in
MAJ_{\frac{2}{3}n+4}\circ MAJ_{\frac{2}{3}n+4}
.

Proof.

Case 1.

n
is divisible by
3
,

k=\frac{2}{3}n+1
.

We need to define
\frac{2}{3}n+1
first-level threshold functions
f_{i}
in the formula.
Let
f_{1}=x_{1},\ldots,f_{\frac{1}{3}n}=x_{\frac{1}{3}n}
, each equivalently being a majority function on one corresponding element.
Denote
\frac{2}{3}n
indices of yet unused inputs by
M=\{\frac{1}{3}n+1,\ldots,n\}
.
Then let
f_{\frac{1}{3}n+i}=MAJ_{M}(x;\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+i-1)
.
The output gate is a standard majority on all
f_{i}
.

\left[\sum f_{i}\geq\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right]=\left[\Big(\sum_{\mathclap{i\leq n/3}}x_{i}+\sum_{\mathclap{0\leq i\leq n/3}}[sum_{M}(x)\geq\lceil n/6\rceil+i]\Big)\geq\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right]

If
sum_{M}(x)\geq\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+\frac{1}{3}n=\lceil\frac{1}{2}n\rceil
, then
\sum_{0\leq i\leq\frac{1}{3}n}[sum_{M}(x)\geq\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+i]=\frac{1}{3}n+1
, that means that both output gate and
MAJ_{n}(x)
will be equal to one.

If
\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil\leq sum_{M}(x)\leq\lceil\frac{1}{2}n\rceil
, then
\sum_{i\leq\frac{1}{3}n}x_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq\frac{1}{3}n}[sum_{M}\geq\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+i]=\sum_{i\leq\frac{1}{3}n}x_{i}+sum_{M}(x)-\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+1=\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+1
.
Then the circuit attains value
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil+1\geq\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{1}{2}
. Rearranging, we get that the value of the circuit is
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq\frac{1}{3}n+\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil-\frac{1}{2}=\lceil\frac{1}{2}n\rceil-\frac{1}{2}
, which is equivalent to
[\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}n]=MAJ_{n}(x)
.

If
sum_{M}(x)<\lceil\frac{1}{6}n\rceil
,
MAJ_{n}(x)=0
and the formula’s value is also
0
, since
\sum_{i\leq\frac{1}{3}n}x_{i}\leq\frac{1}{3}n
.

Case 2.

n=3m+1
for some integer
m
,

k=2m+3\leq\frac{2}{3}n+3
.

Let
f_{i}=x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m+1
.
Let
M=\{m+2,\ldots,3m+1\}
.
Then let
f_{m+1+i}=MAJ_{M}(x;\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+i-1)
for
1\leq i\leq m+2
, so there are
2m+3
gates in the first level of the circuit.
The output gate is again a majority on all
f_{i}
.

If
sum_{M}(x)\geq\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+m+1=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+\frac{1}{2}\rceil
, then both
MAJ_{n}(x)
and the output gate are equal to one.

If
\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil\leq sum_{M}(x)\leq\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+m+1
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i\leq m+1}x_{i}+sum_{M}(x)-\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+1=\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+1
.
Then the circuit attains value
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+1\geq m+\frac{3}{2}
.
Rearranging, we get that the value of the circuit is
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq m+\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil+\frac{1}{2}=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+\frac{1}{2}\rceil-\frac{1}{2}
.
Rounding the last part to the smallest greater integer we get that the circuit attains value
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+\frac{1}{2}\rceil
, exactly when
MAJ_{n}(x)=1
.

If
sum_{M}(x)<\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}<m+1+\lceil\frac{1}{2}(m-1)\rceil=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1/2\rceil
, so both
MAJ_{n}(x)
and the circuit will have value
0
.

Case 3.

n=3m+2
for some integer
m
,

k=2m+5\leq\frac{2}{3}n+4
.

Let
f_{i}=x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m+2
.
Let
M=\{m+3,\ldots,3m+2\}
.
Then let
f_{m+2+i}=MAJ_{M}(x;\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil+i-1)
for
1\leq i\leq m+3
, so there are
2m+5
gates in the first level of the circuit.
The output gate is again a majority on all
f_{i}
.

If
sum_{M}(x)\geq\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil+m+2=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m\rceil+1=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1\rceil
, then both
MAJ_{n}(x)
and the output gate are equal to one.

If
\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil\leq sum_{M}(x)\leq\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil+m+2=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1\rceil
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i\leq m+2}x_{i}+sum_{M}(x)-\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil+1=\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{2}m\rceil+2
.
Then the circuit attains value
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}-\lceil\frac{1}{2}m\rceil+2\geq m+\frac{5}{2}
.
Rearranging, we get that the value of the circuit is
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq m+\lceil\frac{1}{2}m\rceil+\frac{1}{2}=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1\rceil-\frac{1}{2}
.
Rounding the last part to the smallest greater integer we get that the circuit attains value
1
iff
\sum_{i}x_{i}\geq\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1\rceil
, exactly when
MAJ_{n}(x)=1
.

If
sum_{M}(x)<\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}<m+2+\lceil\frac{1}{2}m-1\rceil=\lceil\frac{3}{2}m+1\rceil
, so both
MAJ_{n}(x)
and the circuit will have value
0
.

∎

4
Adaptive model

4.1
Lower bound

Lemma 3
.

Any algorithm for computing
MAJ_{n}
in adaptive model requires
\lceil n/k\rceil
queries to oracle.

Proof.

We will set bits of vector
x
the algorithm has not yet asked about right before answering a query from the algorithm.
Initialize a set
S=\varnothing
— this will be the set of all the indices that appeared in algorithm’s requests.
When algorithm makes a request for
MAJ_{T}(x)
, we firstly set
x_{i}
for
i
in
T\backslash S
: set any
z=\lfloor|T\cup S|/2\rfloor-sum_{S}(x)
variables
x_{i}
to
1
and the rest
|T\backslash S|-z
variables to
0
.
Then we update
S:=S\cup T
.
After that we answer the query with
MAJ_{T}(x)
.

Clearly,
sum_{S}(x)=\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor
at each step.
If algorithm has made less than
\lceil n/k\rceil
requests, then
|S|<n
, so there are some variables
that the algorithm knows nothing about.

There are
n-|S|
such variables.
If we set
x_{i}
with
i\not\in S
to
1
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor+n-|S|=n-\lceil|S|/2\rceil\geq n-\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil\geq n-n/2=n/2
, and
MAJ_{n}(x)
will be equal to
1
.
If we set
x_{i}
with
i\not\in S
to
0
, then
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\leq(n-1)/2<n/2
, and
MAJ_{n}(x)
will be equal to
0
.

So, whichever answer the algorithm chooses, there will be two vectors
x
and
y
consistent with all our answers, such that
MAJ_{n}(x)\not=MAJ_{n}(y)
, so on either
x
or
y
the algorithm will err.
∎

4.2
Upper bound in adjustable-threshold setting

Lemma 4
.

There exists an algorithm for adjustable-threshold adaptive model that determines
MAJ_{n}(x)
in
\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil\lceil\log(k+1)\rceil
queries to the oracle.

Proof.

Split
\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil
disjoint blocks
B_{i}
of size at most
k
each.
We know that
MAJ_{B_{i}}(x;0)=1,MAJ_{B_{i}}(x;|B_{i}|+1)=0
, so using binary search we can find such value
h_{i}
that
MAJ_{B_{i}}(x;h_{i})=1,MAJ_{B_{i}}(x;h_{i}+1)=0
.
This means that
sum_{B_{i}}(x)=h_{i}
.
Since binary search works in
\lceil\log(|B_{i}|+1)\rceil
steps, in
\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil\lceil\log(|B_{i}|+1)\rceil
queries we will know
\sum_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i}sum_{B_{i}}(x)=\sum_{i}h_{i}
, and so we will easily find
MAJ_{n}(x)=[\sum_{i}h_{i}\geq\frac{n}{2}]
.
∎

The upper bound for the adaptive model with adjustable threshold turns out to be at most two times better than the one we get in the case of the adaptive model with fixed threshold.

4.3
Upper bound in fixed-threshold setting

Lemma 5
.

There exists an algorithm such that it accepts two disjoint sets
A,B
,
|A|=|B|\leq k
,
MAJ_{A}(x)\not=MAJ_{B}(x)
, and if given access to the oracle from the definition of fixed-threshold adaptive model it finds in at most
\lceil\log(|A|+1)\rceil
queries to the oracle a set
S\subset A\cup B
such that
sum_{S}(x)=|S|/2
and
|S|\geq|A|-1
.

Proof.

We will define such procedure
find_balanced_set(A, B, x)
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{|A|}
be some enumeration of elements from
A
and
B_{1},\ldots,B_{|B|}
be some enumeration of elements from
B
.

Define
c=|A|
,
i(m)=A_{m}
if
m\leq c
, otherwise
i(m)=B_{m-c}
, and
v(m)=x_{i(m)}
.
Also define
f(m)=MAJ_{c}(v(m),\ldots,v(m+c-1))
.
From the specification of
find_balanced_set(A, B, x)
we know that
f(1)\not=f(c+1)
, so we can use binary search to find position
h
such that
f(1)=f(h)\not=f(h+1)=f(c+1)
.
This implies that
v(h)\not=v(h+c)
, and, moreover,
v(h)=f(h)
.

If
c
is even, this implies
sum_{\{i(h+1),\ldots,i(h+c-1)\}}=\frac{c}{2}-1
, so either
sum_{\{i(h),\ldots,i(h+c-1)\}}=\frac{c}{2}
, if
f(h)=1
, or
sum_{\{i(h+1),\ldots,i(h+c)\}}=\frac{c}{2}
, if
f(h)=0
.
The procedure
find_balanced_set(A, B, x)
then returns the set
\{i(h+1-f(h)),\ldots,i(h+c-f(h))\}
.

If
c
is odd,
sum_{\{i(h+1),\ldots,i(h+c-1)\}}=\frac{c-1}{2}
, so the procedure returns the set
\{i(h+1),\ldots,i(h+c-1)\}
.

Clearly, this procedure makes at most
\lceil\log(c+1)\rceil
queries.
∎

Claim 0
.

Suppose that for some set
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
we know that
sum_{S}(x)=|S|/2
.
Then,
MAJ_{n}(x)=MAJ_{\{1,\ldots,n\}\backslash S}(x)
, so we can forget about indices from
S
and not take them into consideration, as though vector
x
has size
n-|S|
.

The basic idea of our adaptive algorithm is simple: on each iteration we find some large set
S
with
sum_{S}(x)=|S|/2
, and remove the variables with indices in
S
by claim
6
.
We find such set
S
by splitting
\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
n/k
subsets of similar sizes, querying each of them, finding two sets of sizes
\approx|S|
with different
MAJ
values on them, and running the
find_balanced_set
procedure from lemma
5
.
This is the main idea, but we have to be careful with the sizes of subsets, since
k
may not divide
n
evenly, and if we are too careless, after several iterations we may have to split the set of current indices again and make queries for each of new subsets, which could significatnly increase the total number of queries.

Theorem 7
.

There exists an algorithm for computing
MAJ_{n}
function in the fixed-threshold adaptive setting using at most
2(\frac{n}{k-4}+1)(\log k+4)
queries.

Proof.

Split the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil
disjoint sets
S_{i}
of sizes
\ell=\lceil\frac{n}{\lceil n/k\rceil}\rceil
or
\ell-1
each.
Enumerate
S_{i}
in such way that
S_{1},\ldots,S_{a}
are of size
\ell
each, and the rest are of size
\ell-1
.

\ell\leq k
, since
\frac{n}{\lceil n/k\rceil}\leq\frac{n}{n/k}=k
.
For all
S_{i}
query
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
.
If all the answers are the same and equal to
y\in\{0,1\}
, then
MAJ_{n}(x)=y
and we are done.

Case 1.
Suppose that there are sets
S_{i}
of both sizes
\ell
and
\ell-1
.
Then, since not all the answers are the same, there are two indices
i
and
j
such that
|S_{i}|=\ell
,
|S_{j}|=\ell-1
and
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)\not=MAJ_{S_{j}}(x)
(otherwise, all
S_{i}
with size
\ell
have
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)=MAJ_{S_{j}}(x)
for all
S_{j}
of size
\ell-1
, so all the subsets
S_{i},S_{j}
have the same answer, a contradiction).

Now we will find a large (with size
\approx\ell
) subset
S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
of indices to exclude from our consideration (by claim
6
) in at most logarithmic in
\ell
number of queries.

Choose any element
y
from
S_{i}
and query
MAJ_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)
.
Suppose that
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)\not=MAJ_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)
.
Clearly,
x_{y}
then is equal to
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
.

If
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
is
0
, then
sum_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)\geq\frac{\ell-1}{2}
and
sum_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)<\frac{\ell}{2}
, implying
\ell
is odd and
sum_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)=\frac{\ell-1}{2}
.
This means that we can ignore indices from
S_{i}\backslash\{y\}
by claim
6
.

If
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
is
1
, then
sum_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)<\frac{\ell-1}{2}
and
sum_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)+1\geq\frac{\ell}{2}
, implying
\ell
is even and
sum_{S_{i}}(x)=\frac{\ell}{2}
.
Again, this means that we can remove indices from
S_{i}
from our consideration by claim
6
.

So, if there are sets
S_{i}
with different sizes, we are left with the case when
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)=MAJ_{S_{i}\backslash\{y\}}(x)
.

Let
M=S_{i}\backslash\{y\}
.
We know that
|M|=|S_{j}|=\ell-1
and that
MAJ_{M}(x)\not=MAJ_{S_{j}}(x)
.

We run
find_balanced_set
from lemma
5
on sets
M
and
S_{j}
, and get the set
S
such that
sum_{S}(x)=|S|/2
. Note that
S
will be of size
\ell-2
(in case when
\ell
is even) or
\ell-1
.
Since total number of elements in
M\cup S_{j}
is
2\ell-2
,
|M\cup S_{j}\backslash S|\in\{\ell-1,\ell\}
.

We then forget about indices in
S
by claim
6
, query
MAJ_{M\cup S_{j}\backslash S}(x)
and repeat the
case 1
until all
S_{i}
are of the same size or have the same answer.

After several iterations we either get sets
S_{i}
with the same answer
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
each and we are done, or we get the sets with the same sizes
|S_{i}|
.

Case 2.
All of the sets
S_{i}
have same sizes.

Take two sets
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
with different answers
MAJ_{S_{i}}\not=MAJ_{S_{j}}
and run the procedure
find_balanced_set(S_i, S_j, x)
.
Let
S
be the answer.
If
|S_{i}|
is even,
|S|=|S_{i}|
, so just exclude
S
from further considerations, query the oracle
MAJ_{S_{i}\cup S_{j}\backslash S}(x)
, and repeat the procedure until all the sets left have the same
MAJ_{S_{i}}(x)
.

The case of odd
|S_{i}|
requires more careful consideration, since if we simply leave elements from
S
(which has size
|S|=|S_{i}|-1
) out,
|S_{i}\cup S_{j}\backslash S|
will be
|S_{i}|+1
, and that may become greater than
k
after several iterations.
If we look at the case of odd
c
in
find_balanced_set
, we will see that instead of the set
\{i(h+1),\ldots,i(h+c-1)\}
of size
c-1
it can as well return the set
\{i(h),\ldots,i(h+c)\}
of size
c+1
, since
v(h)\not=v(h+c)
.

So if
|S_{i}\cup S_{j}\backslash S|>k
, then we add elements
i(h),i(h+c)
to
S
:
S:=S\cup\{i(h),i(h+c)\}
.
We can remove indices from
S
from future considerations by claim
6
, and we query the oracle the value of
MAJ_{S_{i}\cup S_{j}\backslash S}(x)
.
Now we again have sets
S_{i}
of two different sizes, so we return to
case 1
.

The analysis of number of queries this algorithm makes is rather straightforward:
it starts with making
\lceil\frac{n}{\ell-1}\rceil
queries, then on each iteration it discards at least
\ell-2
indices after making at most
\lceil log(\ell+1)\rceil+2
queries.
So the total number of queries made is
\lceil\frac{n}{\ell-1}\rceil+\lceil\frac{n}{\ell-2}\rceil(\lceil\log(\ell+1)\rceil+2)
.
As was stated in the beginning,
\ell\leq k
.
On the other hand,
\ell\geq\frac{n}{\lceil n/k\rceil}\geq\frac{n}{1+n/k}=\frac{1}{1+k/n}k\geq\frac{k}{2}
.
This gives the following upper bound on the number of queries:

2\frac{n}{k-2}+1+2(\frac{n}{k-4}+1)(\log k+3)\leq 2(\frac{n}{k-4}+1)(\log k+4)

∎

5
Conclusion

We have presented new upper bounds for computing majority functions
MAJ_{n}(x)
in two models.

In static model we have constructed the depth-two circuit for
MAJ_{n}
with
MAJ_{\frac{2}{3}n+4}
gates, which gives the first nontrivial upper bound for this problem.

We have defined an adaptive model with two variations, and presented an algorithm for computing
MAJ_{n}
function using
2(\frac{n}{k-4}+1)(\log k+3)\approx 2\frac{n}{k}\log k
queries to the oracle in the fixed-threshold setting, and a simple algorithm for
MAJ_{n}
with
\lceil n/k\rceil\lceil\log(k+1)\rceil\approx\frac{n}{k}\log k
queries in the adjustable-threshold setting.

There is a large gap between current lower and upper bounds for the static model: the best lower bound
[
1
]
is
k\geq\Omega(n^{4/5})
, and our upper bound is
\frac{2}{3}n+4
.
In the adaptive model there is also a gap between the bounds, albeit a logarithmic (in
k
) one: our lower bound is
\lceil n/k\rceil
.
So the natural questions for future consideration are those of narrowing these gaps.

Another interesting possible direction in the adaptive case is the addition of noise to oracle’s answers — with some probability
\varepsilon
oracle gives us a uniformly random answer.

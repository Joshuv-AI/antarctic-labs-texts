Introduction

Let us consider weak solutions of the Poisson equation

\Delta f=g

on the unit ball
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions
f=0
on
\partial B_{1}
. Under the assumption that
f\in L^{1}(B_{1})
one cannot expect
f\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
nor
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
in general. The optimal a-priori estimates are

f\in\bigcap\limits_{1\leq q<\infty}L^{q}(B_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla f\in\bigcap\limits_{1\leq p<2}L^{p}(B_{1})\,,

as well as

f\in\text{BMO}(B_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla f\in L^{2,\infty}(B_{1})\,.

We recall that the weak
L^{2}
space
L^{2,\infty}(B_{1})\supsetneq L^{2}(B_{1})
, is given by

L^{2,\infty}(B_{1})=\{f\,\text{measurable, s.t. }\sup\limits_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda|\{x\in B_{1}\,|\,|f(x)|\geq\lambda\}|^{1/2}<\infty\}\,.

In
[
22
]
H. Wente discovered improved regularity properties for functions whose Laplacian is a Jacobian determinant, i.e. for solutions of

(2)

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,.\end{cases}

Assuming that
a,b\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
where
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the unit disk, one has that the Jacobian
\nabla^{\perp}a\cdot\nabla b
is in
L^{1}
, and as explained above having
\Delta\varphi\in L^{1}(B_{1})
is not sufficient to obtain
L^{2}
integrability of
\nabla\varphi
a priori. Wente’s results show that thanks to the additional structure of the Jacobian,
\varphi
is indeed in
W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}
and the following estimate holds:

(3)

||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+||\nabla\varphi||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leq C||\nabla a||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}||\nabla b||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\,.

In
[
5

F. Bethuel and J. M. Ghidaglia generalized the estimate (
3

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, not necessarily conformal to the open disk (in this case the estimate would be immediate since the equation and the norms involved are all invariant under conformal transformations).

In this note we study on the one hand the
L^{\infty}

|x|^{\alpha}

[
8

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
be the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

for
a,b\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
, where
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the two-dimensional open disk. Then for any
0<\alpha<1
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
, s.t.

||\,|x|^{\alpha}\varphi||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2}+\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,.

Moreover there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

||\,|x|\varphi||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{2}\leq C\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,.

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\log|x||\,|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
be the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

for
a,b\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
, where
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the two-dimensional open disk. Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the set of all such
\varphi
, for varying
a,b\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
. Then,

\forall\beta<1:\quad\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{||\,|x|\nabla\varphi||_{2}}{||\nabla a||_{2}||\,|x|\,|\log|x||^{\beta/2}\nabla b||_{2}}=\infty\,.

No other results of weighted Wented estimates of this form are known to the author.

Wente inequalities on weighted Sobolev spaces have been shown by S. Baraket and L. Chaabane in
[
2
]
. Despite the similar name, these results are fundamentally different from the ones mentioned above, since in their result, given a weight
w

||\nabla a||^{2}_{L^{2}_{w}(B_{1})}=\int_{B_{1}}w|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\,||\nabla b||^{2}_{L^{2}_{w^{-1}}(B_{1})}=\int_{B_{1}}w^{-1}|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx<\infty

are made and the inequality

||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C||\nabla a||_{L_{w}^{2}(B_{1})}||\nabla b||_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}(B_{1})}

is then considered.

Proof of weighted Wente’s inequality

We prove Theorem
1
.

Proof.

By Lemma E.2 in
[
8
]
there exists a dyadic decomposition

(8)

\nabla b=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\nabla b_{j}

converging in
L^{2}
, with

spt(\nabla b_{j})\subset A_{j}\cup A_{j+1}=:\tilde{A}_{j}

for the dyadic annuli

(9)

A_{j}:=B_{2^{-j}}\setminus B_{2^{-j-1}}

and

(10)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\tilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx\,.

Introducing

(11)

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi_{j}=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b_{j}\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

we obtain a
L^{2}
decomposition of
\varphi
, i.e.

\varphi=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}\,,

where the series converges in
L^{2}
. Each of the elements in the series has Laplacian in the Hardy space and satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions. Moreover, the support of the Laplacian of
\varphi_{j}
is an annulus with fixed conformal class and radius comparable to
2^{-j}
.

The standard Wente inequality can be applied to each element of the series to obtain

(12)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq\,C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx

for a constant
C
independent of
j
.

In fact the contribution of
\nabla a
in (
12
) can be localized: by construction

(13)

spt(\nabla b_{j})\subset\tilde{A}_{j}=B_{2^{-j}}\setminus B_{2^{-j-2}}

and choosing a radial cut-off function

(14)

\psi_{j}\in[0,1],\quad spt(\nabla\psi_{j})\subset[2^{-j},2^{-j}-2^{-j-3}]\cup[2^{-j-3},2^{-j-2}],\quad\psi_{j}\equiv 1\,\,\text{on}\,\,[2^{-j-2},2^{-j}]\,,

with

(15)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\psi_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq C\,,

setting
a_{j}:=\psi_{j}(a-c_{j})
, for a constant
c_{j}
to be chosen later,

(16)

\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b_{j}\overset{L^{1}}{=}\nabla a_{j}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b_{j}\,,

(17)

\nabla a_{j}=\psi_{j}\nabla a+(a-c_{j})\nabla\psi_{j}\,.

Introducing the notation
C_{j}:=spt(\psi_{j})
,

(18)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{C_{j}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx+C\fint_{C_{j}}(a-c_{j})^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{C_{j}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,,

for the appropriate choice of
c_{j}
in the Poincaré inequality used in the last step.

In particular,

(19)

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi_{j}=\nabla a_{j}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b_{j}\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

and the Wente inequality (
12

(20)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq\,C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq\,C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\int_{C_{j}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,.

We will first prove that the weighted Wente inequality holds for each
\varphi_{j}
, for
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\,C\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a_{j}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C\int_{B_{1}}2^{-2j\alpha}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a_{j}|^{2}\,dx

which by summing over

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}\,dx\leq\,C\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla b|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\,.

\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}\nabla\varphi_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{j}\,dx\,,

Cj||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||r\sqrt{|\log(r)|}\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha=1\,.\end{cases}

Recall that

(51)

||\tilde{u}||_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}\,,

and hence for the harmonic difference
\tilde{u}-u
,

(52)

||\nabla(\tilde{u}-u)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{\eta})}\leq C_{\eta}||\nabla a||_{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}\,.

In particular,

(53)

\int_{B_{1/2}\setminus B_{2^{-j+1}}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1/2}\setminus B_{2^{-j+1}}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,dx+C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\,.

Moreover,

(54)

\sup\limits_{B_{1}}|\tilde{u}-u|\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}\,,

so that

(55)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\partial B_{1/2}}|u||\nabla u|\,dx\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\,|x|\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\,.

It therefore suffices to prove for some
f:(0,\frac{1}{2})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
|x|^{\alpha}\leq Cf(r)
that

(56)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{2^{-j+1}}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||f(r)\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}

to obtain

(57)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{2^{-j+1}}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||f(r)\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\,,

which together with

(58)

\int_{B_{2^{-j+1}}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C2^{-2j\alpha}||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\,,

which follows from the standard Wente inequality would imply

(59)

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||f(r)\nabla b_{j}||_{2}^{2}\,.

In (
50
) we have shown that (
56
) hold for
f(r)=r\sqrt{|\log(r)|}
in the case
\alpha=1
and
f(r)=|x|^{\alpha}
in the case
0<\alpha<1
. We conclude:

Lemma 3
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
be the solution of the localized Wente equation

\begin{cases}\Delta u=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b_{j}\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,u=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

a,b_{j}\in W^{1,2}(B_{1})
and
spt(\nabla b_{j})\subset\tilde{A_{j}}
. Then for all
0<\alpha<1
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\alpha}|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover there exists a universal constant
C>0
, such that

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla a|^{2}\,dx\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\log|x||\,|\nabla b_{j}|^{2}\,dx.

We can now conclude the proof of Theorem
1
by applying Lemma
3
to each
\varphi_{j}
:

Lemma 4
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
solve

\begin{cases}\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\quad&\text{on }B_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\,\,\,\,\varphi=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,,\end{cases}

Then for all
0<\alpha<1
there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
, such that

||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leq C||\nabla a||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla b||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\,.

Moreover there exists a universal constant
C>0
, such that

(60)

||\,|x|\nabla\varphi||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leq C||\nabla a||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}||r\sqrt{|\log|x||}\nabla b||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\,.

Proof.

By the decomposition introduced above, and Lemma
3
applied to each
\varphi_{i}
,

(61)

\displaystyle||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi_{i}||_{L^{2}}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi_{j}||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}C\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}||\nabla a_{i}||_{2}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla b_{i}||_{2}||\nabla a_{j}||_{2}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla b_{j}||_{2}\quad\text{for}\,0<\alpha<1\\[5.0pt]
C\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}||\nabla a_{i}||_{2}||r\sqrt{\log(r)}\nabla b_{i}||_{2}||\nabla a_{j}||_{2}||r\sqrt{\log(r)}\nabla b_{j}||_{2}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\alpha=1\end{cases}

(62)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}C_{\alpha}||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||\,|x|^{\alpha}\nabla b||_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for}\,0<\alpha<1\\[5.0pt]
C||\nabla a||_{2}^{2}||r\sqrt{\log(r)}\nabla b||_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\alpha=1\,.\end{cases}

This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

We concluded the first part of the paper, namely the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

The case
\alpha=1

The goal of this section is to study the case
\alpha=1
, in particular showing in Theorem 2 that the weight
|x|^{2}\log|x|
obtained in Theorem
1
is optimal.

We start by making the following observation: assume
\varphi\in C^{0}(B_{1})\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
,
\Delta\varphi=0
in
B_{1}\setminus B_{s}
and
\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b
in
B_{s}
as well as
\partial_{r}\varphi=\partial_{-r}\varphi
on
\partial B_{s}
, then

(63)

\Delta\varphi=\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\quad\text{in}\,\,\,B_{1}\,.

Indeed, for
\psi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
,

(64)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\nabla\varphi\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial(B_{1}\setminus B_{s})}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\psi\,dx-\int_{B_{s}}\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\psi\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{s}}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\psi\,dx

(65)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{s}}\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\psi\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{s}}(\partial_{r}\varphi-\partial_{-r}\varphi)\psi\,dx

(66)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{s}}\nabla a\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\psi\,dx\,.

Consider now the following harmonic function on
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
:

(67)

h(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}-x=\partial_{x}\log(|x|)-x\,.

Notice that this function satisfies

(68)

\partial_{r}h(x)=-\frac{x(1+|x|^{2})}{|x|^{3}},

and hence
\int_{\partial B_{s}}\partial_{r}h\,dx=0
for all
0<s<1
, which is a necessary condition in order to be a solution to a Wente type equation. The gradient of
h
is given by
|\nabla h|^{2}=|x|^{-4}+1
, and hence

(69)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{s}}|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\leq Cs^{-2}\,,

for all
\beta\in(0,1)
,

(70)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{s}}|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\leq Cs^{-2+2\beta}\,,

and

(71)

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{s}}|x|^{2}|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\geq\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{s}}\frac{x^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\,dx=\,C\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}(\varphi)\,d\varphi\int_{s}^{1}\frac{1}{r}dr=\,C|\log(s)|\,.

We produce a counterexample to the weights
|x|^{2}
and
|x|^{2}|\log|x||^{1-\epsilon}
in the weighted Wente inequality, by gluing a function
f
on
B_{s}
solving a Wente equation to
h
.

Let
f=x|x|^{\alpha}
. Then

(72)

\partial_{r}f=\frac{x}{|x|}|x|^{\alpha}(1+\alpha)\,,

and

(73)

\Delta f=2\partial_{x}|x|^{\alpha}+x\Delta|x|^{\alpha}=x|x|^{\alpha-2}\alpha(\alpha+2)=\nabla a_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}b\,,

for
b=y
and
a_{\alpha}=(\alpha+2)|x|^{\alpha}
, which gives
\partial_{x}a_{\alpha}=\alpha x|x|^{\alpha-2}(\alpha+2)
.

In order to glue
h
and
K(s,\alpha)f
continuously along
\partial B_{s}
the following needs to be satisfied:

(74)

\frac{1}{s^{2}}-1=K(s,\alpha)s^{\alpha}\implies K(s,\alpha)=s^{-\alpha-2}(1-s^{2})\,,

the normal derivatives then satisfy on
\partial B_{s}
,

(75)

\partial_{r}K(s,\alpha)f=xs^{\alpha-1}(1+\alpha)s^{-\alpha-2}(1-s^{2})=x(1+\alpha)s^{-3}(1-s^{2})\,.

On the other hand, still on
\partial B_{s}
,

(76)

\partial_{-r}h=xs^{-3}{(1+s^{2})}

and in order for the gluing to satisfy
\partial_{r}=\partial_{-r}
on
\partial B_{s}
we need,

(77)

1+s^{2}=(1+\alpha)(1-s^{2})\,,

which is satisfied for
s_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\alpha}{2+\alpha}
. We define

(78)

h_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}h\quad&\text{on}\,\,B_{1}\setminus B_{s_{\alpha}}\\[5.0pt]
K(s_{\alpha},\alpha)f\quad&\text{on}\,\,B_{s_{\alpha}}\,.\end{cases}

Then, by the previous discussion

(79)

\Delta h_{\alpha}=\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}\tilde{b}_{\alpha}

for
\tilde{b}=y
and
\tilde{a}_{\alpha}=K(s_{\alpha},\alpha)(\alpha+2)|x|^{\alpha}
on
B_{s_{\alpha}}
and
\tilde{a}_{\alpha}=K(s_{\alpha},\alpha)(\alpha+2)s_{\alpha}^{\alpha}
on
B_{1}\setminus B_{s_{\alpha}}
. A direct computation shows:

(80)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx=\int_{B_{s_{\alpha}}}K(s_{\alpha},\alpha)^{2}|\nabla a_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s_{\alpha}}}s_{\alpha}^{-2\alpha-4}(1-s_{\alpha}^{2})^{2}\alpha^{2}|x|^{2\alpha-2}(\alpha+2)^{2}\,dx

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Cs_{\alpha}^{-2\alpha-4}(1-s_{\alpha}^{2})^{2}\alpha^{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}\int_{0}^{s_{\alpha}}|x|^{2\alpha-1}\,dx

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cs_{\alpha}^{-2\alpha-4}(1-s_{\alpha}^{2})^{2}\alpha^{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}}{2\alpha}s_{\alpha}^{2\alpha}

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C\alpha(\alpha+2)^{2}s_{\alpha}^{-4}(1-s_{\alpha}^{2})^{2}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C\alpha\frac{(2+\alpha)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{4(2+\alpha)^{2}}{(2+\alpha)^{2}}

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C\frac{(2+\alpha)^{2}}{\alpha}=\,C\frac{2+\alpha}{s_{\alpha}^{2}}\to\infty\quad\text{as}\,\,\alpha\to 0\,,

which is expected given the standard Wente estimate and the growth rate we computed in (
69
). Similarly from the computations above we deduce

(87)

\int_{B_{s_{\alpha}}\setminus B_{s_{\alpha}/2}}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx=\,C\,\frac{2+\alpha}{s_{\alpha}^{2}}\,,

and hence for
\beta\in(0,1)

(88)

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx\geq C(2+\alpha)s_{\alpha}^{2\beta-2}\,,

which is consistent with (
70
) and the main Theorem
1
.
On the other hand,

(89)

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx\leq C,

which shows that the case
\alpha=1
with the weight
|x|^{2}
cannot hold. In fact more can be said, for any
\beta\in(0,1)
and
s_{\alpha}
small enough,

(90)

\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}|\log(|x|)|^{\beta}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx\leq Cs_{\alpha}^{2}|\log(s_{\alpha})|^{\beta}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\tilde{a}_{\alpha}|^{2}\,dx\leq C|\log(s_{\alpha})|^{\beta}

and hence a Weighted Wente inequality of the form
||\,|x|\nabla\varphi||_{2}\leq||\nabla b||_{2}||r|\log(r)|^{\beta/2}\nabla a||_{2}
cannot hold for any
\beta<1
. This shows that our main Theorem
1
is optimal, and

\forall\beta<1:\quad\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{||\,|x|\nabla\varphi||_{2}}{||\nabla a||_{2}||\,|x|\,|\log|x||^{\beta/2}\nabla b||_{2}}=\infty\,,

which also concludes the proof of Theorem 2.

Higher form symmetries and orbifolds of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory
Leonardo Santilli, Richard J. Szabo

Abstract
We undertake a detailed study of the gaugings of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory by its intrinsic charge conjugation 0-form and centre 1-form global symmetries, elucidating their higher algebraic and geometric structures, as well as the meaning of dual lower form symmetries. Our derivations of orbifold gauge theories make use of a combination of standard continuum path integral methods, networks of topological defects, and techniques from higher gauge theory. We provide a unified description of higher and lower form gauge fields for a p p -form symmetry in the geometric setting of p p -gerbes, and derive reverse orbifolds by the dual ( − 1 ) (-1) -form symmetries. We identify those orbifolds in which charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken, and relate the breaking to mixed anomalies involving ( − 1 ) (-1) -form symmetries. We extend these considerations to gaugings by the non-invertible 1-form symmetries of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory by introducing a notion of generalized θ \theta -angle.

Gauging the
\boldsymbol{(d-1)}
-form symmetry

The highest possible symmetry in

Abandon the idea that the orbifold by a

Summary of results

In this paper we shall undertake a detailed study of the algebraic and geometric structures that emerge when gauging a 1-form symmetry in two dimensions, as well as its physical implications. To this aim, we focus on the tractable example of pure Yang–Mills theory with gauge group
G
, which enables us to substantiate our results with explicit computations.

The orbifolds of Yang–Mills theory by an intrinsic finite abelian 1-form symmetry group are derived, utilizing both the standard continuum path integral formalism and techniques from higher gauge theory. One of the upshots of our analysis is that lower form symmetries naturally fit into the hierarchy of higher form symmetries, and can thereby be treated on the same footing. The topological structure of
p
-gerbes automatically includes
p
-form symmetries for any two-dimensional quantum field theory, with
p\in\{1,0,-1\}
, thereby laying the geometric foundation which underlies our novel definition of gauge fields for the
(-1)
-form symmetry. We derive orbifolds by the dual
(-1)
-form symmetry, utilizing both path integral techniques and by summing over topological surface operators, demonstrating that they recover the anticipated partially gauged theories.

Charge conjugation is an intrinsic finite 0-form symmetry of pure Yang–Mills theory, which participates with the intrinsic 1-form symmetries in the natural global automorphism 2-group of the gauge theory.
We show by an explicit computation that the theory after gauging a 1-form symmetry, with gauge group
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
where
N
is even and
k
is any power of 2 that divides
N
, has two degenerate vacua at
\theta=\pi
, and that charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken. Additionally, we rule out the possibility of such spontaneous symmetry breaking for several other gauge groups. We complement the analysis with a study of the orbifolds by charge conjugation symmetry.

We relate this spontaneous symmetry breaking to the presence of an anomaly involving the
(-1)
-form symmetry, which parallels and extends the anomaly in the space of couplings found by
[
35
]
. This means that it is not possible to preserve background gauge invariance when backgrounds for both the 1-form and
(-1)
-form symmetries are turned on. Therefore not only does the language of gerbes includes the treatment of
(-1)
-form symmetries, but it also allows for the extension of the notion of (mixed) ’t Hooft anomalies to such cases.

The abelian 1-form symmetry group arising from the centre of the gauge group
G
is the invertible part of the full 1-form symmetry of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory. We also consider orbifolds by the non-invertible higher form symmetry. We introduce a generalization of the
\theta
-parameter to this non-invertible case, and show that it is given by an isomorphism class of irreducible representations of
G
. Equipped with this notion, we extend our previous results to the orbifolds by non-invertible symmetries. We present an in-depth study of the partition functions, discussing various gaugings with different generalized
\theta
-parameters. In particular, we identify those orbifolds that lead to spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry in this generalized setting.

These results initiate the study of orbifolds by non-invertible higher form symmetries, and yield the first explicit realization of spontaneous symmetry breaking in such orbifold theories.

2.1
Geometry of gauge fields in two dimensions

We start by reviewing basic geometric definitions and how they extend to higher form symmetries. We then provide a definition of lower form gauge transformations, on the same footing of the more widely studied higher form gauge transformations.

Geometric setup

Let
\Sigma
be a closed, connected and oriented Riemann surface with Euler characteristic
\chi
and Kähler 2-form
\omega
, which we assume to be normalized to unit area

\int_{\Sigma}\,\omega=1\ .

Let
G
be a semi-simple, compact and connected Lie group, and let

P\longrightarrow\Sigma

(2.1)

be a principal
G
-bundle with connection
A
. We denote the right action
P\times G\longrightarrow P
of
G
on
P
as
(p,g)\longmapsto p\cdot g
. As usual, we write
\mathrm{ad}\,P:=P\times_{G}\mathrm{ad}\,\mathfrak{g}
for the vector bundle associated to (
2.1
) by the adjoint representation of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The curvature of

is denoted

and the first Chern class of
\mathrm{ad}\,P
is represented by the Chern–Weil form

c_{1}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\frac{F}{2\pi}\ ,

where
\mathrm{Tr}
is a suitable trace on
\mathfrak{g}
.

We denote by

the affine space of
G

\Sigma
, leaving the dependence on

\Sigma

is not simply-connected, then

is a disjoint union of connected components, one for each isomorphism class of
G

2.1
) labelled by elements of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G)
of
G
.

The
\boldsymbol{\theta}
-term

Gauge theories in two dimensions admit a
\theta
-term when the gauge group
G
is not simply-connected. It is an element of (see e.g.
[
43
,
29

)

\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{1}(G),\mathrm{U}(1)\big)^{\pi_{0}(G)}\ ,

where the restriction to the
\pi_{0}(G)
-invariant part stems from gauge invariance. The fundamental group of any compact Lie group
G
is abelian and finitely generated, thus we can restrict to two classes:

(a)

\pi_{1}(G)\cong\Z
, which is the case when the gauge group
G
is
\mathrm{U}(1)
or
\mathrm{U}(N)
. Group homomorphisms
\Z\longrightarrow\mathrm{U}(1)
are classified by phases
\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,\theta}\in\mathrm{U}(1)
, where the parameter
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
is called a
\theta
-angle or
\theta

(b)

\pi_{1}(G)\cong\Z_{k}
is purely torsional, for some

. Group homomorphisms
\Z_{k}\longrightarrow\mathrm{U}(1)
are classified by
k^{\text{th}}

\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\kappa/k}\in\Z_{k}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)
. The parameter
\theta_{\kappa}:=\frac{2\pi\,\kappa}{k}\in[0,2\pi)\cap\frac{2\pi}{k}\Z
is called a discrete
\theta

The most general possibility
\pi_{1}(G)\cong\Z^{r}\oplus\Z_{k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Z_{k_{s}}
is a synthesis of these two cases.

The 2-group global symmetry

Recall that a groupoid is a small category in which every homomorphism is invertible. A (Lie) 2-group is a (Lie) groupoid equipped with a (smooth) monoidal structure that obeys the usual group axioms. In many instances of interest this is understood in a weak sense, i.e. up to natural isomorphism. 2-groups are equivalently presented as crossed modules of groups, which are more commonly used in applications to physics; we direct the reader to
[
44
]
for an extensive introduction to 2-groups. Lie 2-groups arise in e.g. field theories with first order reducible gauge symmetries. Together with their homomorphisms and natural transformations, Lie 2-groups form a bicategory; see e.g.
[
45
]
for an introduction to bicategories and 2-categories.

There is a natural 2-group of automorphisms of the stack of
G
-bundles with connection, which we may present as the action groupoid

\displaystyle\mathsf{Aut}(G)\,\big/\!\!\big/\,G=\big(G\times\mathsf{Aut}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\halign{\hbox to\dimexpr\@tempdima+1em{#}\cr 8.19447pt{\rightarrowfill\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr 8.19447pt{\rightarrowfill\cr}}}\limits^{\!\ \ }_{\!\ }}\mathsf{Aut}(G)\big)\ ,}}

where
g\in G
acts on a group isomorphism
\varphi:G\longrightarrow G
by conjugation
\varphi\longmapsto\mathrm{Ad}(g)\circ\varphi
. The set of isomorphism classes of objects is the group

\pi_{0}\big(\mathsf{Aut}(G)\,\big/\!\!\big/\,G\big)=\mathsf{Out}(G)=:\mathsf{O}\ ,

where
\mathsf{Out}(G)\subset\mathsf{Aut}(G)
is the subgroup of outer automorphisms of
G
. The set of automorphisms of any object projecting to
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathsf{O}
is the group

\pi_{1}\big(\mathsf{Aut}(G)\,\big/\!\!\big/\,G\big)=\mathsf{Z}(G)=:\mathsf{Z}=\pi_{1}(\mathrm{B}\mathsf{Z})\ ,

where
\mathsf{Z}(G)
is the centre of
G
, and
\mathrm{B}\mathsf{Z}=1\,/\!\!/\,\mathsf{Z}=(\mathsf{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\halign{\hbox to\dimexpr\@tempdima+1em{#}\cr 8.19447pt{\rightarrowfill\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr 8.19447pt{\rightarrowfill\cr}}}\limits^{\!\ \ }_{\!\ }}1)}}
is the delooping groupoid of
\mathsf{Z}
.

The automorphism 2-group is an extension

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathsf{Z}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Aut}(G)\,\big/\!\!\big/\,G\longrightarrow\mathsf{O}\longrightarrow 1

in the bicategory of 2-groups. This is alternatively encoded in Hoang data through the four-term exact sequence

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mathsf{Z}\longrightarrow G\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{Ad}\ }\mathsf{Aut}(G)\longrightarrow\mathsf{O}\longrightarrow 1

\big(\mathsf{Aut}(G),G,\mathrm{Ad},\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{Aut}(G)}\big)

This implies that the stack of

\mathsf{Z}(G)
on

Charge conjugation

For
G
a simple compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and non-trivial outer automorphism group, the elements of the charge conjugation group
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\subseteq\mathsf{O}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
are involutive outer automorphisms
\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}
the non-trivial element of
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
, which acts on
\mathfrak{g}
-valued fields by flipping their sign.

By the CP theorem in two dimensions, charge conjugation
\mathsf{C}
is identified with parity
\mathsf{P}
. In two Euclidean dimensions, the non-trivial element in
\mathsf{P}
is an involution that leaves scalar fields unchanged, and reflects the Kähler form of
\Sigma
as
\omega\longmapsto-\omega
.

Higher and lower form symmetries

A
p
-form symmetry acts on extended operators of dimension
\geq p
. This means that a
p
-form symmetry is generated by topological defect operators of codimension
p+1
, which can link non-trivially with the charged operators. On
\Sigma
, the set of allowed codimensions is
\left\{2,1,0\right\}
, thus we will deal with
1
-form, 0-form and
(-1)
-form symmetries.

A 0-form (i.e. ordinary)
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
gauge transformation shifts a scalar field by a 0-form gauge parameter
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\in\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))
, and locally acts on the connection
A
of the principal bundle (
2.1
) as

A\longmapsto A+\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\ .

The 0-form gauge parameter
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
is subject to the semi-classical quantization condition

\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\oint_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\ \in\ \mathrm{i}\Z

(2.2)

for every homology 1-cycle
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z^{2-\chi}
. This condition is non-trivial because
\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
is only defined locally. By splitting the integral into a sum of integrals over open subsets
U_{i}
covering
\gamma
, the transition functions on the various overlaps
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
produce a factor in
2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\Z
. Globally,
\exp\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\in\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathrm{U}(1))
is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-valued function on
\Sigma
and the integers in (
2.2
) are its winding numbers.

Higher form gauge transformations are the immediate generalization of this idea. A 1-form
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
gauge transformation shifts the connection

\displaystyle A\longmapsto A+\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}

by a 1-form gauge parameter
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))
subject to

\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\ \in\ \mathrm{i}\Z\ ,

(2.3)

where again the expression
\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is meant locally; globally it is replaced with the curvature of a connection
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
on a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle over
\Sigma
of first Chern class (
2.3
). For both 0-form and 1-form symmetries, one can consider the action of a cyclic subgroup
\Z_{k}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)
, in which the gauge parameter
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(p)}
is not arbitrarily valued in
\R/\Z
but is in the additive group of
\Z_{k}
, namely it exponentiates to a
k^{\text{th}}
root of unity.

It remains to address the nature of a
(-1)
-form symmetry, as there is no such thing as a
(-1)
-form parameter. However, the discussion thus far suggests that the answer is phrased in terms of the topological surface operators mediating the symmetry transformations, with an integrality condition on the curvature of the gauge parameter. The curvature of the would-be
(-1)
-form is a 0-form, that is, a scalar
\varphi
. For it to be closed means
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
, hence it is constant on every connected component of the spacetime. The semi-classical quantization condition on the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
gauge transformation becomes formally

\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\varphi(\wp)\ \in\ \mathrm{i}\Z

for every homology 0-cycle
[\wp]\in H_{0}(\Sigma,\Z)
. As we are interested in closed and connected Riemann surfaces
\Sigma
,
H_{0}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z
and the quantization condition simply means that
\varphi\in 2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\Z
is a constant integer multiple of
2\pi\,\mathrm{i}
.

Abelian gauge fields

Given a
p
-form symmetry, a
(p+1)
-form gauge field is a local
(p+1)
-form defined on a good open cover of
\Sigma
, whose transition functions involve
p
-form gauge transformations. In other words, it is a
(p+2)
-cocycle for the differential cohomology of
\Sigma
, whose gauge transformations are generated by
(p+1)
-cocycles; see e.g.
[
46
]
for an elaborate review. The curvature
F\in\Omega^{p+2}_{\Z}(\Sigma)
of a differential
(p+2)
-cocycle is the closed
(p+2)
-form with integer periods which represents its characteristic class in the charge group
H^{p+2}(\Sigma,\Z)
for the
p
-form symmetry. Since
p
-form gauge parameters generally come with gauge transformations themselves, gauge fields for a
p
-form symmetry naturally assemble into the structure of a
(p+1)
-groupoid, whose set of
(p+1)
-isomorphism classes has the structure of a
(p+1)
-group. Geometrically, a
p
-form symmetry gives rise to a
p
-gerbe on
\Sigma
with
(p+1)
-connection.
In two dimensions there are four possibilities for differential cocycles:

(i)

For
p=-2
, a differential 0-cocycle is identified with its characteristic class (the component class) in the group
H^{0}(\Sigma,\Z)
of connected components of
\Sigma
; its curvature
F
assigns an integer to each component. Since we assume
\Sigma
is closed and connected,
H^{0}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z
.

(ii)

For
p=-1
, a differential 1-cocycle is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-valued function

, with gauge transformations generated by
H^{0}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z
, acting as shifts of
\frac{1}{2\pi\,\mathrm{i}}\log f
. Its characteristic class (the degree class) lies in the
(-1)
-form charge group
H^{1}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z^{2-\chi}
, given by the winding numbers of

and represented by the curvature
F=\mathrm{d}\log f

(iii)

For
p=0
, a differential 2-cocycle is a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle on
\Sigma
with connection

, whose gauge transformations are generated by
\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathrm{U}(1))
and which assemble into a group symmetry. Its characteristic class (the first Chern class) lies in the
0
-form charge group
H^{2}(\Sigma,\Z)\cong\Z
and is represented (locally) by its curvature
F=\mathrm{d}A
.

(iv)

For
p=1
, a differential 3-cocycle is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-gerbe on
\Sigma
with 2-connection
(A,B)

\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundles with connection, which themselves come with
\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathrm{U}(1))

H^{3}(\Sigma,\Z)=0
for any Riemann surface
\Sigma
. Hence any differential 3-cocycle on
\Sigma
is trivial. In particular, we may set the connection 1-form to
A=0
as the curving
B\in\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))
is a globally defined 2-form on
\Sigma
.

In the following we will insist on the nomenclature ‘
p

2.2
Representation theory

The properties of irreducible representations of

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{SU}(N)}
representations

For
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)
, irreducible representations up to isomorphism are in one-to-one correspondence with partitions
R=(R_{1},R_{2},\dots,R_{N-1})
subject to

R_{1}\geq R_{2}\geq\cdots\geq R_{N-1}\geq 0\ .

Each partition

row consists of
R_{j}

j=1,\dots,N-1

\text{fund}=(1,0,\dots,0)=\Box\ .

It is customary to denote by
|R|
the total number of boxes in the Young diagram corresponding to the irreducible
\mathrm{SU}(N)

R

\lvert R\rvert:=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\,R_{j}\ .

Isomorphism classes of irreducible representations are equivalently specified by their Dynkin labels
[w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{N-1}]
. The two bases are related through

w_{j}=R_{j}-R_{j+1}\ ,

for all
j=1,\dots,N-1
, with
R_{N}:=0
.

Representation category

The finite-dimensional representations of
G
and
G
-equivariant maps between them form a
\C
-linear symmetric monoidal category. In the following we will work with the Grothendieck ring
(\mathfrak{R}(G),\otimes,\oplus)
of this category: the Grothendieck completion of the coarse moduli space of isomorphism classes

\mathfrak{R}(G)\ =\ \Z[\,G\text{-representations}\,]\ \big/\,\cong

is equipped with the tensor product
\otimes
and the direct sum
\oplus
of representations, while the unit is the one-dimensional trivial representation
\emptyset=(0,\dots,0)
.

Characters

Let
\chi_{R}:G\longrightarrow\C
denote the character of a representation
R
of
G
, with
\dim R=\chi_{R}(\mathbbm{1})
the dimension of
R
. They can be regarded as the image of a homomorphism
R\longmapsto\chi_{R}
from the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{R}(G)
to the ring
\mathfrak{C}(G)
of class functions on
G
, which preserves the conjugation involutions:

\displaystyle\overline{\chi_{R}}=\chi_{\overline{R}}\ ,

with
\overline{\chi_{R}(g)}=\chi_{R}(g^{-1})
for
g\in G
(because of unitarity).
That is, they are additive:

\displaystyle\chi_{R}+\chi_{R^{\prime}}=\chi_{R\oplus R^{\prime}}\ ,

and multiplicative:

\displaystyle\chi_{R}\,\chi_{R^{\prime}}=\chi_{R\otimes R^{\prime}}\ .

In particular, the characters of irreducible representations generate the ring
\mathfrak{C}(G)
. They are orthonormal with respect to integration over the bi-invariant normalized Haar measure
\mathrm{d}g
on
G
:

\displaystyle\int_{G}\,\mathrm{d}g\ \overline{\chi_{R_{1}}(g)}\,\chi_{R_{2}}(g)=\delta_{R_{1},R_{2}}\ .

Let
\mathtt{N}^{R_{3}}_{R_{1},R_{2}}\geq 0
denote the fusion numbers appearing as multiplicities in the decomposition of the tensor product
R_{1}\otimes R_{2}=\bigoplus_{R_{3}}\,\mathtt{N}^{R_{3}}_{R_{1},R_{2}}\,R_{3}
into irreducible representations. Then by multiplicativity

\displaystyle\chi_{R_{1}}\,\chi_{R_{2}}=\sum_{R_{3}}\,\mathtt{N}^{R_{3}}_{R_{1},R_{2}}\ \chi_{R_{3}}\ .

Using orthonormality, the fusion numbers can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathtt{N}^{R_{3}}_{R_{1},R_{2}}=\int_{G}\,\mathrm{d}g\ \chi_{R_{1}}(g)\,\chi_{R_{2}}(g)\,\overline{\chi_{R_{3}}(U)}\ .

Frobenius–Schur indicator

For any representation
R
of
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)
, let
\mathrm{FS}_{R}
be its Frobenius–Schur indicator given by

\mathrm{FS}_{R}:=\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\,\mathrm{d}U\ \chi_{R}(U^{2})\ .

If
R
is irreducible, then

\mathrm{FS}_{R}=\begin{cases}\ 1&\text{if }R\text{ is real}\ ,\\
\ -1&\text{if }R\text{ is pseudo-real}\ ,\\
\ 0&\text{if }R\text{ is complex}\ .\end{cases}

(2.10)

In particular,
\mathrm{SU}(N)
does not admit pseudo-real irreducible representations if
N-2\notin 4\Z
.

For later convenience, we introduce the indicator

\delta^{\mathrm{FS}}_{R}:=(\mathrm{FS}_{R})^{2}=\begin{cases}\ 1&\text{if }R\cong\overline{R}\ ,\\
\ 0&\text{otherwise}\ .\end{cases}

(2.11)

2.3
Yang–Mills theory in two dimensions

The gauge theory

We begin by considering pure Yang–Mills theory, with simply-connected gauge group
G
admitting outer automorphisms, on the Riemann surface
\Sigma
. The intrinsic discrete symmetries of this theory participate in the automorphism 2-group
\mathsf{Aut}(G)\,/\!\!/\,G
discussed in Subsection
2.1
: charge conjugation
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\subseteq\mathsf{O}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}:=\mathsf{Out}(G)
and the centre 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}:=\mathsf{Z}(G)
.

The partition function of Yang–Mills theory with gauge group
G
is defined by the path integral

\mathcal{Z}_{G}=\mathcal{Z}_{G}[\Sigma]=\int_{\mathscr{A}}\,\mathscr{D}A\ \exp\Big(\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F\ast F\Big)

(2.46)

with the suitably normalized gauge-invariant symplectic measure
\mathscr{D}A
over the affine space
\mathscr{A}
of
G
-connections on
\Sigma
. In the action functional,
g
is the gauge coupling and
\ast\!
is the Hodge star operator which is compatible with the Kähler form
\omega
on
\Sigma
.

It is often convenient to introduce an additional scalar field
\phi\in\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g})
in the adjoint representation of
G
, and write (
2.46
) in a first order formulation as

\mathcal{Z}_{G}=\int_{\mathscr{A}}\,\mathscr{D}A\ \int_{\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g})}\,\mathscr{D}\phi\ \exp\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,\Big(\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,\phi\ast\phi+\mathrm{i}\,\phi\,F\Big)\ .

(2.47)

The Gaussian integral over
\phi
gives back (
2.46
).

On the other hand, the
BF
-type path integral (
2.47
) can be evaluated exactly, resulting in the Migdal–Rusakov formula
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
]

\mathcal{Z}_{G}=\sum_{R}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ ,

(2.48)

where
C_{2}
is the quadratic Casimir invariant of
G
, and the sum runs over isomorphism classes
R
of irreducible representations of
G
. More precisely, the sum in (
2.48
) is understood to run over the generators of the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{R}(G)
, which describes the Wilson lines of the gauge theory and thus distinguishes between Yang–Mills theories with different gauge groups that integrate the same Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

[
53
]
.

The 1-form symmetry

In any dimension, pure Yang–Mills theory has an intrinsic 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}:=\mathsf{Z}(G)
based on the centre of
G
. We focus on
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)
for concreteness. Locally, the action of
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
shifts
A\longmapsto A+\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
for a 1-form
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
subject to

\oint_{\gamma}\,\frac{N\,\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}{2\pi}\ \in\,\mathrm{i}\Z

for all homology 1-cycles
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(\Sigma,\Z)
.

The Wilson loop in the
G
-representation
R
supported on a closed oriented curve
L\subset\Sigma
is given by

W_{R}(L):=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\ \mathtt{P}\,\exp\,\oint_{L}\,A\ .

The operator
W_{R}(L)
is charged under
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, because it acquires a non-trivial phase under a shift transformation of the gauge connection
A
:

A\ \longmapsto\ A+\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W_{R}(L)\ \longmapsto\ \mathrm{e}^{\,\lvert R\rvert\,\oint_{L}\alpha^{(1)}}\ W_{R}(L)\ .

3.1
The orbifold gauge theories

Let us focus on
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)
, and start with the 1-form part
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
of the automorphism 2-group
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathrm{SU}(N))\,/\!\!/\,\mathrm{SU}(N)

\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}:=\Z^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{k}\ \subseteq\ \Z^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{N}\ .

Gauging the subgroup
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
of the
1
-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
produces the orbifold theory with gauge group
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}

\mathrm{SU}(N)

\pi_{1}(G)\cong\Z_{k}

\theta

\theta_{\kappa}=\frac{2\pi\,\kappa}{k}\quad,\quad\kappa\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}\ .

Proposition 3.1
.

The partition function of the orbifold
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}

\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\,\sum_{\beta=0}^{k-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,\theta_{\kappa}\,\beta}\ \sum_{R}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)-2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta\,\lvert R\rvert/k}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \delta(\lvert R\rvert-\kappa\ \mathrm{mod}\ k)\ .

(3.3)

The sum over

\Sigma

whose Young diagrams contain

(-1)
-form symmetry
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}:=\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
, obtained from the Pontryagin dual group of the gauged subgroup

:

\mathsf{K}=\widehat{\mathsf{B}}=\widehat{\Z_{k}}=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\Z_{k},\mathrm{U}(1)\big)\cong\Z_{k}\ .

At the level of abelian gauge fields, this is a consequence of Pontryagin–Poincaré duality of differential cohomology
[

]
.
By gauging this dual symmetry, we expect to get back the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
theory we started with. We will more generally consider gauging subgroups

, for any

Proposition

was first proven by Witten
[
42
]
using the standard combinatorial approach to quantization of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory, and rederived more recently in
[

,
30
]
from the point of view of gauging the 1-form symmetry. The main novelty that we introduce in the following is a first principles path integral derivation of (

), as well as of the twice-orbifolded partition function, through coupling to a gauge field for the 1-form symmetry. This approach has the advantage of being tailored to the study of anomalies, which we shall undertake in Section
6
.

We conclude this section by offering a simplified treatment and new perspective on the orbifold by the 0-form part
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}:=\mathsf{O}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
of the automorphism 2-group
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathrm{SU}(N))\,/\!\!/\,\mathrm{SU}(N)
, considered in
[
55
]
. The gauging is performed by summing over insertions of networks of topological line defects for the charge conjugation symmetry
\mathsf{C}
, which produces the orbifold Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
with gauge group
\mathrm{SU}(N)\rtimes\mathsf{C}
. We do not discuss the gauging of the dual 0-form symmetry
\widehat{\mathsf{C}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\cong\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
here; the reverse generalized orbifold construction is presented in
[
55
]
by summing over topological networks of Wilson line operators.

3.2
The path integral coupled to a background field

In the following we prove Proposition
3.1
as well as the gauging of the dual symmetry. We begin here with a more direct approach, and then reformulate it in Subsection
3.3
via a lift of the
G

We first couple the theory to a background 2-form gauge field

for the 1-form symmetry

Coupling to a background field

We couple pure Yang–Mills theory to a background 2-form
\Z_{k}
gauge field

for the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}:=\Z^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{k}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}[B]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{A}}\,\mathscr{D}A\ \int_{\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\R/\Z)}\,\mathscr{D}\lambda\ \exp\bigg(\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big[(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})\ast(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt+\int_{\Sigma}\,\lambda\,k\,B\bigg)\ .

Under charge conjugation,

We would now like to pass to the first order formalism as in (

&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\exp\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big(\phi\,(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})\big)+\frac{g^{2}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\phi\ast\phi\right)+\kappa\,B\ .\end{split}

In the following, we will assume that

Abelianization

At this point, the dependence on the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
curvature

One first integrates out the off-diagonal modes of the gauge field

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\phi_{i}^{2}

3.3
Derivation in higher gauge theory

We shall now provide an alternative derivation of (

The gauge 2-group

In implementing (

The main novel point here is that the extension mixes 0-form and 1-form symmetries, which algebraically requires promoting (

Following

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathrm{U}(1)\longrightarrow\mathbbm{U}(N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(N)\longrightarrow 1\ ,

where the strict abelian 2-group

\mathrm{U}(1)

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mathrm{U}(1)\xrightarrow{\ \mathbbm{1}\ }\mathrm{U}(1)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(N)\xrightarrow{\ \mathbbm{1}\ }\mathrm{SU}(N)\longrightarrow 1

The automorphism group of any object projecting to

3.4
Gauging the 1-form symmetry

So far we have derived the expression (

) for the partition function of

Including the path integration over

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}^{\kappa}&=\int_{\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))}\,\frac{\mathscr{D}B}{2\pi}\ \mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}^{\kappa}[B]\\[4.0pt]

&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\int_{\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))}\,\frac{\mathscr{D}B}{2\pi}\ \sum_{\ell\in\Z}\,\delta\Big(\int_{\Sigma}\,\frac{k\,B}{2\pi}-\mathrm{i}\,\ell\Big)\ \exp\Big(\int_{\Sigma}\,B\,(\kappa-\lvert R\rvert)\Big)\ .\end{split}

Of particular interest is the case of

3.6
Gauging the dual
(-1)
-form symmetry

In a
d
-dimensional quantum field theory, gauging a
p
-form symmetry produces a dual
(d-p-2)
-form symmetry. This generalizes the classical result that gauging a 0-form symmetry in two dimensions produces a dual (sometimes called ‘quantum’) 0-form symmetry
[
25
]
.

Gauging the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
in two dimensions produces a
(-1)
-form symmetry
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
, with

\mathsf{K}=\widehat{\mathsf{B}}=\widehat{\Z_{k}}\cong\Z_{k}\ .

This symmetry is generated by codimension zero topological operators, which are Wilson surfaces

S_{\eta}=\exp\,\eta\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\ ,\quad\eta\in\left\{0,1,\dots,k-1\right\}\ .

These operators are topological in the sense that they do not depend on the Kähler metric on
\Sigma
. Alternatively, one may embed the two-dimensional theory as a defect into a three-dimensional quantum field theory and use the flatness of
B
to prove invariance of
S_{\eta}
under small deformations of the surface by the usual arguments.

Let us assume that
m\in\mathbb{N}
divides
k
; the limiting cases
m=1
and
m=k
are allowed. We now proceed to gauge the subgroup
\Z_{m}\subseteq\mathsf{K}
, generated by the topological symmetry operators with charge
\eta=k\,\nu/m
:

S_{\eta=k\,\nu/m}=\exp\,\frac{k}{m}\,\nu\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\ ,\quad\nu\in\left\{0,1,\dots,m-1\right\}\ .

One way to proceed would be to turn on a background 0-form gauge field for
\Z_{m}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
that partially undoes the gauging of
B
. Another approach for gauging such a discrete symmetry is, instead, to sum over insertions of networks of topological operators generating it.

Gauging by summing over topological surface operators

In the present case, each topological symmetry operator fills the whole Euclidean spacetime
\Sigma
, thus we only need to sum over all possible insertions of one such operator: there is only one generating homology class
[\Sigma]
of 2-cycles. Summing (
3.23
) over all operator insertions gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}\big[S_{\eta=k\,\nu/m}\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\R/\Z)}\,\mathscr{D}\lambda\ \int_{\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))}\,\mathscr{D}B\ \sum_{R}\,\left(\dim R\right)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\exp\bigg(\int_{\Sigma}\,B\,\big(\lambda\,k+\kappa-\lvert R\rvert\big)\bigg)\ \sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\exp\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\,\nu\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\bigg)\ .

Integrating out
\lambda
and then
B
we arrive at the simple modification of (
3.2
) given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}^{\kappa}\big[S_{\eta=k\,\nu/m}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}\,\left(\dim R\right)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\delta\Big(\lvert R\rvert-\kappa-\frac{k}{m}\,\nu\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ k\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}\,\left(\dim R\right)^{\chi}\penalty\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \frac{1}{k}\,\sum_{\beta=0}^{k-1}\ \sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\frac{\beta}{k}\,(\kappa-\lvert R\rvert)+2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta\,\frac{\nu}{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}\,\left(\dim R\right)^{\chi}\penalty\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \frac{m}{k}\,\sum_{\beta=0}^{k-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\frac{\beta}{k}\,(\kappa-\lvert R\rvert)}\ \delta(\beta\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{R}\,\left(\dim R\right)^{\chi}\penalty\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \delta\Big(\kappa-\lvert R\rvert\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ \frac{k}{m}\Big)\ .

That is, we recover the theory as if we had gauged only the 1-form symmetry
\Z_{k/m}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, as expected:

\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}^{\kappa}\big[S_{\eta=k\,\nu/m}\big]=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k/m}}^{\kappa\penalty\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ \frac{k}{m}}\ .

Path integral gauging of the dual symmetry

Let us now show how to gauge the dual symmetry
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}=\Z_{m}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
at the level of the path integral. We do so in the limiting case
k=N
to reduce clutter, but the argument goes through in the more general case as well. The 0-form gauge field for the
(-1)
-form symmetry is a scalar

. Recall from Subsection

that a
(-1)
-form gauge transformation shifts
a\longmapsto a+2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n
with

.

Looking back at the extension of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
into
\mathrm{U}(N)
, namely neglecting the Lagrange multiplier

throughout the previous discussions, we would obtain a
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}

. In the

theory, this is broken down to

by the constraint that the period of

lies in

\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}

. In this way we obtain the standard minimal coupling of the 0-form gauge field

for a magnetic symmetry
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
to the

\int_{\Sigma}\,a\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F^{\,\textrm{e}}=N\,\int_{\Sigma}\,a\,B\ ,

3.10

Inserting this term in the relevant part of (

Next we integrate over the gauge orbits

\displaystyle\sum_{R}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \int_{\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{u}(1))}\,\frac{\mathscr{D}B}{2\pi}\ \sum_{\ell\in\Z}\,\delta\Big(\int_{\Sigma}\,\frac{N}{m}\,\frac{B}{2\pi}-\mathrm{i}\,\ell\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\int_{\Omega^{0}(\Sigma,\R/\Z)}\,\mathscr{D}\lambda\ \exp\Big(\int_{\Sigma}\,B\,\big(N\,\lambda+\kappa-\lvert R\rvert\big)\Big)\ .

The integration over

3.7

We state the famous result of

Lemma 3.26
.

The global structure of the gauge group in pure Yang–Mills theory is detected by the spectrum of line operators in the theory.

Consider pure Yang–Mills theory with gauge algebra
\mathfrak{su}(N)
, placed for simplicity on an oriented disk
\mathbb{D}
of area
\frac{1}{2}
, so that a sphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}
obtained from gluing two such disks with opposite orientations has unit area. We wish to determine the gauge group
G
. For this, we start by taking a representation
R
of the simply-connected group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(N)
. That is, we let
R
be an
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-representation. We write
W_{R}:=W_{R}(-\partial\mathbb{D})
and denote by
\langle W_{R}\rangle
the path integral computed with a Wilson line in the representation
R
inserted along the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
with opposite orientation to that induced by the orientation of
\mathbb{D}
:

\langle W_{R}\rangle=\int_{\mathscr{A}}\,\mathscr{D}A\ \exp\Big(\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{D}}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F\ast F\Big)\ \mathrm{Tr}_{R}\ \mathtt{P}\,\exp\Big(\oint_{-\partial\mathbb{D}}\,A\Big)\ .

We compute

\langle W_{R}\rangle=\begin{cases}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,C_{2}(R)}\,\dim R&\text{ if $R$ is a $G$-representation}\ ,\\
\ 0&\text{ otherwise}\ .\end{cases}

(3.27)

Suppose we are given a list of a finite number of values of
\langle W_{R}\rangle
for all representations
R
of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
with
\lvert R\rvert\leq N
. Then we can extract the global form of the gauge group
G
from this list.

For example, depending on whether or not the trivial representation is in the list, we obtain

\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle=\begin{cases}\ 1&\Longrightarrow\ \kappa=0\ ,\\
\ 0&\Longrightarrow\ \text{$G$ is not simply-connected and $\kappa\neq 0$}\ .\end{cases}

From the fundamental representation we infer

\langle W_{\Box}\rangle=\begin{cases}\ N\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,(N-1)}&\Longrightarrow\ \kappa\in\left\{0,1\right\}\ ,\\
\ 0&\Longrightarrow\ \text{$G=\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}$ with (i) $k\geq 2$, $\kappa=0$ or (ii) $k>2$, $\kappa>1$}\ .\end{cases}

Therefore, combining the information about the trivial and fundamental representations, we deduce

G\ =\ \begin{cases}\ \mathrm{SU}(N)&\text{ if both $\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle\neq 0$ and $\langle W_{\Box}\rangle\neq 0$ }\ ,\\
\ [\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k\geq 2}]_{\kappa=0}&\text{ if $\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle\neq 0$ but $\langle W_{\Box}\rangle=0$ }\ ,\\
\ [\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k\geq 2}]_{\kappa=1}&\text{ if $\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle=0$ but $\langle W_{\Box}\rangle\neq 0$ }\ ,\\
\ [\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k>2}]_{\kappa>1}&\text{ if both $\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle=0$ and $\langle W_{\Box}\rangle=0$ \ . }\end{cases}

Going further with higher-dimensional representations, one eventually finds that, among all
R
with
\lvert R\rvert\leq N
, only those with
\lvert R\rvert=\kappa\ \mathrm{mod}\ k
yield a non-vanishing
\langle W_{R}\rangle
. The pair
(k,\kappa)
is thus read off from the non-trivial entries in the list.

This is of course a well-known result
[
53
]
. The point we wish to make here is that, thanks to the formula (
3.27
), Lemma
3.26
finds a concrete and explicit realization in this model. The sphere partition function is written in the form

\mathcal{Z}_{G}[\mathbb{P}^{1}]=\sum_{R}\,\langle W_{R}\rangle^{2}\ ,

with the sum running over all irreducible representations
R
of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
up to isomorphism, but
\langle W_{R}\rangle
is the Wilson loop expectation value taken with respect to the group
G
.

3.8
Charge conjugation symmetry orbifolds

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(N)
possesses an outer automorphism group
\mathsf{C}:=\mathsf{O}=\Z_{2}
, with the non-trivial element denoted
\mathcal{C}
and called charge conjugation in physics parlance. The principal extension of
\mathsf{C}
by the gauge group
\mathrm{SU}(N)
is the disconnected group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
, defined by a short exact sequence of groups

1\longrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(N)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)\longrightarrow\pi_{0}\big(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)\big)\longrightarrow 1\ ,

(3.28)

with

\pi_{0}\big(\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)\big)\cong\mathsf{C}\ .

In physical terms, the extension (
3.28
) corresponds to taking the orbifold by
\mathsf{C}
. Four-dimensional gauge theories with disconnected gauge group
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
have been constructed and studied in
[
66
,
67
,
68
]
.

An irreducible representation
\widetilde{R}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
corresponds to a (possibly reducible) representation
R
of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
which satisfies
\overline{R}\cong R
, where
\overline{R}=\mathcal{C}\cdot R
is the conjugate representation. In particular, both fundamental and anti-fundamental representations of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
are projected out by gauging
\mathsf{C}
, but the bifundamental representation
\Box\,\oplus\,\overline{\Box}
descends to an irreducible representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)

[
66
,
67
]
.

Lemma 3.29
.

Let
R
be a representation of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
that is isomorphic to its charge conjugate,
\overline{R}\cong R
. Then
R
descends to a representation
\widetilde{R}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
, which is irreducible if and only if
R
is reducible.

Gauging charge conjugation via topological symmetry operators

The gauge theory resulting from gauging the outer automorphisms of a generic gauge group
G
in two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory has been analyzed in
[
55
]
. Here we focus on
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)
.

A direct way to gauge a 0-form symmetry in two dimensions is to sum over all possible insertions of networks of topological line operators that generate the symmetry
[
3
]
. The notion of network requires a definition of topologically equivalent classes of insertions of operators. We give a simplified definition here and refer to
[
55
]
for a more careful treatment.

Definition 3.30
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a finite group and consider a two-dimensional quantum field theory with 0-form global symmetry group
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
on
\Sigma
. For every
\alpha\in\mathsf{A}
, let
L_{\alpha}
be a topological loop operator that generates the action of
\alpha
and winds around
[L]\in H_{1}(\Sigma,\Z)
. Fix a basis
\{L_{j}\}_{j=1,\dots,2-\chi}
for
H_{1}(\Sigma,\Z)
. A
network of topological loop operators for
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
, or
\mathsf{A}
-network
for short, is a collection

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{j,\alpha_{j}}\ \big|\ \alpha_{j}\in\mathsf{A}\ ,\ j=1,\dots,2-\chi\right\}\ .

We gauge
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
by summing over the insertions of
\mathsf{C}
-networks on
\Sigma
, which computes the path integral over connections on an
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-bundle
D\times_{\mathsf{C}}P\longrightarrow\Sigma
twisted by a
\mathsf{C}
-bundle
D\longrightarrow\Sigma
. The aim is to illustrate an alternative approach to
[
55
]
, which we still refer to for a more exhaustive analysis. The first case to consider is
\Sigma=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. However, the sphere does not have non-contractible cycles, therefore every network is homologically trivial. We conclude that the partition function on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is insensitive to gauging
\mathsf{C}
. Then the simplest non-trivial example is the torus
\ct^{2}
.

The torus

Let
\Sigma=\ct^{2}
. There exist four basic
\mathsf{C}
-networks

\mathcal{L}_{1}:=\left\{L_{1,1},L_{2,1}\right\}\ ,\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}:=\left\{L_{1,\mathcal{C}},L_{2,1}\right\}\ ,\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}:=\left\{L_{1,1},L_{2,\mathcal{C}}\right\}\ ,\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}:=\left\{L_{1,\mathcal{C}},L_{2,\mathcal{C}}\right\}\ ,

(3.31)

with
(L_{1},L_{2})
denoting the canonical symplectic basis of
H_{1}(\ct^{2},\Z)\cong\Z\oplus\Z
. All other
\mathsf{C}
-networks can be decomposed into superpositions of these loop operators.
With the notation
\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}
for the torus decorated with the
\mathsf{C}
-network
\mathcal{L}
, the orbifold partition function is

\mathcal{Z}_{\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)}[\ct^{2}]=\frac{1}{4}\,\Big(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}[\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}_{1}]+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\big[\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}\big]+\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}[\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}]\Big)\ .

The first contribution involves the trivial network, and thus equals
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}[\ct^{2}]
.

To compute
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\big[\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}\big]
we cut the torus open along its
j^{\text{th}}
cycle, insert the loop operator
L_{j,\mathcal{C}}
and then glue the two sides back together. To perform the gluing we use orthonormality of the characters to compute

\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\mathrm{d}U\ \chi_{R}(U)^{2}=\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\,\mathrm{d}U\ \overline{\chi_{\overline{R}}(U)}\,\chi_{R}(U)=\begin{cases}\ 1&\text{ if }R\cong\overline{R}\ ,\\
\ 0&\text{ otherwise}\ .\end{cases}

We thus get

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\big[\ct^{2}\supset\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}\big]=\sum_{R}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \delta^{\mathrm{FS}}_{R}\ ,

(3.32)

where we recall that the square of the Frobenius–Schur indicator
\delta_{R}^{\mathrm{FS}}
from (
2.11
) vanishes unless
R\cong\overline{R}
, in which case it gives
1
if
R
is irreducible. That is, these contributions truncate the series (
2.48
) to real or pseudo-real irreducible representations of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
.

To better understand the contribution from the fourth
\mathsf{C}
-network in (
3.31
), we may slightly resolve the four-way junction at the intersection of the loops
L_{1,\mathcal{C}}
and
L_{2,\mathcal{C}}
. The result is a non-trivial loop
L_{1,\mathcal{C}}+L_{2,\mathcal{C}}
that winds around the combination of the two generators of
H_{1}(\ct^{2},\Z)
. We compute its contribution below.

CP theorem

Let
\mathsf{C}=\Z_{2}\cong\left\{1,\mathcal{C}\right\}
be the charge conjugation symmetry group as above, and
\mathsf{P}=\Z_{2}\cong\left\{1,\mathcal{P}\right\}
be the parity symmetry group. In two dimensions, charge conjugation
\mathcal{C}
and parity
\mathcal{P}
are equivalent, due to the CP theorem

\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{P}=1

and the
\Z_{2}
relations
\mathcal{C}^{2}=1=\mathcal{P}^{2}
. Therefore, after gauging
\mathsf{C}
we expect to obtain the same partition function as that gotten by projecting the original theory onto its
\mathsf{P}
-invariant sector. We shall now demonstrate this explicitly.

Consider again
\Sigma=\ct^{2}
and insert any of the
\mathsf{C}
-networks from (
3.31
). The trivial
\mathsf{C}
-network gives back
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}[\ct^{2}]
. When the network
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
is inserted, we can transport the gauge connection
A
around
L_{2}
to obtain
A^{\dagger}=-A\in\mathfrak{su}(N)
, and likewise for
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
. This is the same result we obtain when considering two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory on the Klein bottle
\mathbb{K}
. Therefore introducing a topological line operator
L_{\mathcal{C}}
wrapping a non-trivial 1-cycle
L
has the same net effect as cutting
\ct^{2}
open along
L
and closing it with a pair of cross-caps, as illustrated in Figure
2

4
Spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry

In this section we discuss the spontaneous breaking of the discrete 0-form symmetry
{\mathsf{C}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
in the orbifolds of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory by discrete 1-form symmetries, generalizing earlier considerations of
[
54
]
.

4.1
Spontaneous symmetry breaking in U(1) Yang–Mills theory

As a warm-up and for completeness, we begin by stating the result of spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry in
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory. This has been known previously, see in particular
[
56
, Section 2.1]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

In pure
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory with
\theta=\pi
, charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken.

One easy way to prove this is using the partition function

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\theta}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,q^{2}-\mathrm{i}\,\theta\,q}=\sqrt{\frac{g^{2}}{2\pi}}\ \sum_{n\in\Z}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{g^{2}}\,\left(n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\right)^{2}}\ ,

where the second equality follows from Poisson resummation and defines the instanton expansion (after including moduli of flat connections when
\chi\leq 0
). At
\theta=0
, charge conjugation
q\longmapsto-q
sends
n\longmapsto-n
and there is a unique symmetric vacuum state
n=0
. At
\theta=\pi
, there are two degenerate vacua
n=0
and
n=1
. Acting with charge conjugation
q\longmapsto-q
, which now sends
n\longmapsto-n+1
, the two vacuum states are exchanged and the symmetry is spontaneously broken.

Wilson loops in U(1) Yang–Mills theory

Let

W_{r}(L)=\exp\,r\,\oint_{L}\,A

be a Wilson line in
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory, labelled by the irreducible
\mathrm{U}(1)
representation
r
, classified by the integer which we denote with the same symbol
r\in\Z
, and supported on a closed oriented curve
L\subset\Sigma
. Acting with a generic element of the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
symmetry, parametrized by
\vartheta\in[0,2\pi)
, the Wilson line acquires a phase

W_{r}(L)\ \longmapsto\ \mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,r\,\vartheta}\ W_{r}(L)\ .

On the other hand, Wilson lines are also charged under charge conjugation
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
:

\mathcal{C}:\ W_{r}(L)\ \longmapsto\ W_{-r}(L)\ .

The pairs of charge conjugate Wilson lines
W_{\pm r}(L)
have the same 1-form symmetry charge
(-1)^{r}
under the subgroup
\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
at
\vartheta=\pi
.

4.2
Spontaneous symmetry breaking in
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
Yang–Mills theory

It was shown in
[
54
]
that the expression (
3.3
) at
k=2
and
\kappa=1
implies two degenerate vacua that are exchanged under charge conjugation. Therefore charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken in the
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{2}
theory. It is straightforward to extend the argument to the
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
theory when
k
is any power of
2
and
\kappa=\frac{k}{2}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
k\geq 2
be a power of
2
and
N
a multiple of
k
. In pure
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
Yang–Mills theory with
\theta_{\kappa}
-angle given by
\kappa=\frac{k}{2}
, charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken.

Degenerate vacua

The action of charge conjugation on the representation ring
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
has been reviewed in Subsection
2.2
. In particular, the operation
\mathcal{C}
maps the single-column tableau of
\frac{k}{2}
boxes into the single-column tableau of
N-\frac{k}{2}
boxes, both of which have
\frac{k}{2}
mod
\penalty\ k
boxes. For example, if
N=8
and
k=4
, the charge conjugation map is depicted by

By direct computation of the Casimir term, the latter two representations are degenerate vacua if
\kappa=\frac{k}{2}
, thus charge conjugation is spontaneously broken in all such
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
theories.

Wilson loops in
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{SU}(N)}}
Yang–Mills theory

Let us take a step back and consider
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory, prior to orbifolding. Similarly to the abelian case, there exists a subgroup
\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\subset\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
of the 1-form symmetry under which pairs of Wilson lines
W_{R}(L)
and
W_{\overline{R}}(L)
that are exchanged under
\mathcal{C}
have equal charge
(-1)^{|R|}
. This is a direct consequence of the relation

\lvert\overline{R}\rvert=R_{1}\,N-\lvert R\rvert\ .

Taking the orbifold by this
\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
with discrete
\theta
-angle
\theta_{\kappa}
, the Wilson lines with 1-form charge
(-1)^{\kappa}
descend to Wilson lines of the
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{2}
theory, while the others are projected out. The interesting case is
\kappa=1
, in which the trivial representation of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
drops out. Then, as we have seen, there are two degenerate vacua exchanged under charge conjugation.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking for orthogonal gauge algebras

One may attempt a similar analysis for other gauge groups
G
. We consider two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with simple gauge group that integrates the gauge algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(N)
for
N\in 2\N
. The discrete
\theta
-angles are described in Appendix
B.1
.

We perform an explicit analysis of the orbifolds and their vacua in Appendix
B.2
, akin to the
\mathfrak{su}(N)
case. The upshot is that charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken in
\mathrm{SO}(6)/\Z_{2}
Yang–Mills theory, while the theory possesses a unique
\mathcal{C}
-invariant vacuum for every
N>8
.

5
Non-invertible symmetry orbifolds

The 1-form symmetry originating from the centre
\Z_{N}\subset\mathrm{SU}(N)
is only the invertible part of a much larger set of non-invertible 1-form symmetries of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory
[
70
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

A symmetry is
non-invertible
if it is generated by a set of topological defect operators that do not form a group under fusion.

In two spacetime dimensions, finite non-invertible 0-form symmetries are, by definition, generated by topological line operators that form a (unitary) fusion category
[
3
]
. For example, Definition
3.30
immediately extends to non-invertible symmetries by relaxing the group condition: The finite group
\mathsf{A}
is replaced with its representation category
\mathfrak{Rep}(\mathsf{A})
, and the symmetry parameter
\alpha
is taken to be an object of
\mathfrak{Rep}(\mathsf{A})
. These generalized symmetries have been extensively studied, see e.g.
[
3
,
71
,
72
,
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
,
80
,
81
,
82
,
83
]
for an incomplete list of works in continuum quantum field theory. A rigorous approach to gauging non-invertible symmetries was recently given in
[
84
,
85
,
86
,
87
,
88
]
.

On the contrary, non-invertible 1-form symmetries are more elusive.
In this section we undertake a systematic study of the gauging of the non-invertible symmetry of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory and the ensuing effect on Wilson loops. Specifically, we

•

introduce a generalized
\theta
-term in the theory after gauging the non-invertible symmetry; and

•

show how the spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry descends to the theory after gauging the non-invertible symmetry.

As we discuss below, these apply equally well after gauging only a subset of the non-invertible 1-form symmetry.

5.1
The non-invertible 1-form symmetry

It was shown in
[
70
]
that pure
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory in two dimensions admits a vast class of zero-dimensional topological symmetry operators, defined as follows. Let
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
denote the set of conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{SU}(N)
. Take a point
\wp\in\Sigma
and a conjugacy class
[U_{0}]\in\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
, for an arbitrary element
U_{0}\in\mathrm{SU}(N)
. Delete
\wp
and require that the gauge connection
A
has holonomy in
[U_{0}]
around the puncture. The path integral over such gauge fields gives rise to a zero-dimensional gauge-invariant disorder operator
V_{\wp}[U_{0}]
, analogous to a Gukov–Witten operator. It is topological thanks to the invariance of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory under area-preserving diffeomorphisms, and therefore we henceforth omit the subscript
\wp
indicating its location. It is gauge-invariant by construction, as only the conjugacy class of the holonomy is fixed rather than the holonomy itself.

Let us briefly review how these local topological operators act on the Wilson lines. Consider for simplicity a disk
\mathbb{D}
of area
\frac{1}{2}
with a Wilson loop in the representation
R
running along the boundary with opposite orientation, as in Subsection
3.7
. We recall that the path integral with the Wilson loop inserted is denoted
\langle W_{R}\rangle
and evaluates to (
3.27
). If we insert a disorder operator
V[U_{0}]
at an arbitrary point in the interior of the disk, a standard computation shows that

\langle V[U_{0}]\,W_{R}\rangle=\chi_{R}(U_{0})\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,C_{2}(R)}\ ,

(5.2)

which in general differs from the correlator without the insertion of the topological symmetry operators. The two invariant cases are
U_{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{N}
(trivial topological operator) and
R=\emptyset
(trivial Wilson line).

The topological symmetry operators thus constructed are generically non-invertible
[
70
]
. As a concrete example, if
N\in 4\N
then one of the operators is associated to the conjugacy class
[U_{0}]
with representative

U_{0}=\text{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{N/2},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{N/2})\ \in\ \mathrm{SU}(N)\ .

For this choice, the correlator (
5.2
) vanishes for a large set of representations, including the fundamental representation. Therefore the topological symmetry operator annihilates the corresponding Wilson loops, and hence does not admit an inverse.

Demanding invertibility and imposing the special unitary constraint on
U_{0}
, we are left with operators associated to the conjugacy classes of elements
U_{0}\in\mathsf{Z}(\mathrm{SU}(N))=\Z_{N}
, thus recovering the usual 1-form centre symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
as the invertible subset of this larger set of symmetries. The standard group law agrees with the multiplication induced by the fusion product of topological symmetry operators.

5.2
Gauging the non-invertible 1-form symmetry

We have seen in (
5.2
) that inserting a topological pointlike operator
V[U_{0}]
in the correlation functions of Wilson loops projects out all the representations
R
for which
\chi_{R}(U_{0})=0
. Gauging the full non-invertible 1-form symmetry is enforced by integrating over insertions
\left\{V[U_{0}]\ |\ [U_{0}]\in\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}\right\}
in the partition function on
\Sigma
. The integral is a Molien–Weyl projector which has the net effect of projecting out all representations except the trivial one.

While the outcome is in agreement with general arguments, it is not possible to derive it in the path integral formalism. The obstruction is the absence of a definition of the 2-form gauge field
B
when the symmetry is non-invertible.

Gauging subsets of the non-invertible 1-form symmetry

Exactly as it is possible to gauge any subgroup
\mathsf{B}\subseteq\mathsf{Z}
, it should be possible to gauge certain collections within the non-invertible 1-form symmetry; the analogous statement for gauging non-invertible 0-form symmetries was recently derived in
[
87
]
. Indeed, it is possible to gauge the symmetry classified by conjugacy classes
H^{\#}\subseteq\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
for every normal subgroup
H\subseteq\mathrm{SU}(N)
. This is done explicitly by integrating (
5.2
) over
[U_{0}]\in H^{\#}
. In this case the integral is again a Molien–Weyl projector, but we ought to take into account the branching rule under
\mathrm{SU}(N)\longrightarrow H
to decompose the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-representation
R
into
H
-representations. Every
R
whose branching rule yields the trivial representation of
H
(among others) will survive the gauging of the subset of non-invertible symmetries classified by
H^{\#}
.

Generalized
\boldsymbol{\theta}
-term for the non-invertible 1-form symmetry

There are various ways to gauge the invertible 1-form symmetry, landing on different theories labelled by the discrete parameter
\kappa
. We thus propose a generalization of this concept to non-invertible symmetries.

When gauging the standard 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
in Section
3
, we proceeded by introducing a discrete parameter
\kappa
and weighing the action functional accordingly. We sum over insertions of local topological operators labelled by
\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta/N}\in\mathsf{Z}
, for
\beta\in\left\{0,1,\dots,N-1\right\}
, with weight
[
29
]

\chi_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{Z}}(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta/N})=\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\kappa\,\beta/N}

given by the pairing of
\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta/N}\in\mathsf{Z}
with a character
\chi_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{Z}}
of
\mathsf{Z}
labelled by
\kappa\in\left\{0,1,\dots,N-1\right\}
. That is, a
\theta
-term corresponds to turning on a pairing between the local topological operators that generate the symmetry
\mathsf{Z}
and the character lattice of
\mathsf{Z}
.

In this way, we are led to propose an extension of the notion of
\theta
-term to non-invertible symmetries. For the local operators
V[U_{0}]
classified by
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
, we ought to turn on a pairing between
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
and the character lattice
\mathfrak{C}_{\Z}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
. The character lattice is the image of the ring homomorphism
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))\longrightarrow\mathfrak{C}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
with
R\longmapsto\chi_{R}
, under which the character is fixed by
R\in\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
. We conclude

Proposition 5.3
.

For gauging the non-invertible 1-form symmetry, the generalized discrete parameter
\kappa
is an isomorphism class of irreducible representations of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
.

The pairing

The local topological operators
V[U_{0}]
are classified by
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
. The Wilson loops
W_{R}
, charged under the non-invertible symmetry, are classified by the classes
R\in\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
in the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
of the category of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-representations. Taking correlation functions
\langle V[U_{0}]\,W_{R}\rangle
defines a pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
between
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
and
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
:

\langle U_{0},R\rangle:=\langle V[U_{0}]\,W_{R}\rangle\ .

This is valid for every Riemann surface
\Sigma
, even though we only explicitate the expressions for the disk for clarity. In this case, the pairing is given by (
5.2
) and it differs from the canonical pairing by the exponential of the Casimir invariant. We may also introduce a pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
between generators of
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
according to

\langle R_{1}|R_{2}\rangle:=\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\,\mathrm{d}U_{0}\ \langle R_{1},U_{0}\rangle\,\langle U_{0},R_{2}\rangle=\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\mathrm{d}U_{0}\ \langle V[U_{0}]\,W_{R_{1}}\rangle\,\langle V[U_{0}]\,W_{R_{2}}\rangle\ ,

where we used bi-invariance of the Haar measure to extend integration over conjugacy classes
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
to
\mathrm{SU}(N)
.
When
\Sigma
is topologically the disk
\mathbb{D}
, it is given by

\langle R_{1}|R_{2}\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}(R_{2})}\ \delta_{\overline{R_{1}}\,,\,R_{2}}\ ,

(5.4)

where we used orthonormality of the basis of characters. Again, it differs from the canonical pairing through the dependence on the Casimir invariant.

Gauging with generalized
\boldsymbol{\theta}
-term

We have seen that gauging the non-invertible 1-form symmetry with generalized discrete
\theta
-angle labelled by
\kappa
dictates to integrate over insertions of operators
V[U_{0}]
weighted by the character
\chi_{\kappa}(U_{0})
. Taking again the disk topology for concreteness, this yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\,\mathrm{d}U_{0}\ \chi_{\kappa}(U_{0})\,\langle U_{0},R\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{SU}(N)}\,\mathrm{d}U_{0}\ \chi_{\kappa}(U_{0})\,\chi_{R}(U_{0})\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,C_{2}(R)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,C_{2}(R)}\ \delta_{\overline{\kappa}\,,\,R}=\mathrm{e}^{\,\frac{g^{2}}{4}\,C_{2}(\kappa)}\ \langle\kappa|R\rangle\ ,

where for the first equality we used (
5.2
), for the second equality we used orthonormality of the characters, and for the last equality we used the pairing (
5.4
). We thus arrive at a projection onto
R\cong\overline{\kappa}
. Had we defined the Wilson loop with orientation
\partial\mathbb{D}
, instead of
-\partial\mathbb{D}
, we would get the projection onto
R\cong\kappa
. From the considerations so far we arrive at

Proposition 5.5
.

Gauging the non-invertible 1-form symmetry with generalized
\theta
-angle labelled by
\kappa
corresponds to acting with the projector
\langle\kappa|
on the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
.

As a consequence of the non-invertible 1-form symmetry, two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory ‘decomposes’ into infinitely many invertible field theories, labelled by the irreducible representations. Gauging with generalized
\theta
-angle selects one such invertible theory. From this viewpoint, Proposition
5.5
extends the decomposition results in
[
30
]
from the invertible to the full 1-form symmetry.

Another perspective on the generalized
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
-term

We have introduced a representation theoretic definition of
\theta
-terms for the non-invertible symmetry. We now rephrase these findings in the formalism of Kapustin–Seiberg
[
89
]
, see also
[
90
,
91
]
.

Let us start again with
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
and its 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\subseteq\Z_{N}
. Following
[
89
,
90
]
, another presentation of the discrete
\theta
-angle in the orbifold corresponds to stacking the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
theory (before gauging) with a two-dimensional topological field theory, whose partition function is equal to

\exp\left(\kappa\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\right)\ .

Different choices of parameter
\kappa
correspond to different couplings for the background gauge field
B
. Gauging the diagonal 1-form symmetry of the juxtaposed system produces the orbifold
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
Yang–Mills theory with discrete
\theta
-angle
\theta_{\kappa}
.

The difficulty in extending this approach is the lack of notion of background gauge fields for non-invertible symmetries in general. Our previous analysis yields a solution in the particular case of the non-invertible 1-form symmetry of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory. Conjugacy classes
[U_{0}]\in\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
play the role of background gauge fields for this non-invertible symmetry. The possible ways of defining a partition function for these background fields are in one-to-one correspondence with characters of irreducible representations of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
.

Consider the set
\mathfrak{T}
consisting of two-dimensional topological field theories possessing this non-invertible 1-form symmetry and differing by the choice of coupling.
\mathfrak{T}
is endowed with the fusion product
\otimes
(fusion of two-dimensional spacetimes of theories) and the direct sum
\oplus
(disjoint union of theories). From the above discussion, we infer that
(\mathfrak{T},\otimes,\oplus)
acquires the structure of a ring, identified with the character lattice
\mathfrak{C}_{\Z}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
, and hence with the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))
.

Wilson loops in non-invertible symmetry orbifolds

The study of Wilson loops in orbifolds by the non-invertible 1-form symmetry generalizes the program initiated in
[
53
]
. By collecting the expectation values of all the Wilson loops, it is in principle possible to reconstruct which part of the non-invertible symmetry has been gauged, and with which generalized
\theta
-angle. In particular, if
\langle W_{\emptyset}\rangle\neq 0
we already know that the gauging has been performed at
\kappa=\emptyset
(trivial representation).

Suppose, for example, that we start computing
\langle W_{R}\rangle
by successively increasing
\lvert R\rvert
and find

\begin{cases}\ \langle W_{R}\rangle\neq 0&\text{ if }R\in\left\{\emptyset\,,\,\Box\,,\,\Box\!\Box\,,\,\Box\!\Box\!\Box\,,\,\dots\right\}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\ \langle W_{R}\rangle=0&\text{ otherwise}\ .\end{cases}

This is compatible with the branching rules for
\mathrm{SU}(N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(N-1)
. Note that the surviving Young diagrams are also compatible with an
\mathrm{SU}(2)
or
\mathrm{SO}(3)=\mathrm{PSU}(2)
gauge theory, among others. Nevertheless, the values of the non-vanishing
\langle W_{R}\rangle
will make clear that the theory is a non-invertible gauging of an
\mathrm{SU}(N)
theory, because
\dim R
and
C_{2}(R)
are evaluated for the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-representations associated to the single-row Young diagrams. For example, when
N>2

\langle W_{\Box\!\Box\cdots\Box}\rangle\ \neq\ \langle W_{\Box\!\Box\cdots\Box}\rangle\big|_{G=\mathrm{SU}(2)}\ .

In conclusion, from the list of the expectation values of Wilson loops, one can reconstruct the global form of the gauge group even after the projection due to gauging a non-invertible symmetry.

5.3
Spontaneous symmetry breaking in the non-invertible symmetry orbifold

We now assume
N\in 2\N
. The spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry generalizes to orbifolds of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory by a subset of the non-invertible 1-form symmetry. For the invertible part, the subgroup
\Z_{2}\subset\Z_{N}
for
N\in 2\N
consists of the elements
\{\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta/N}\}_{\beta=0,N/2}\cong\left\{1,-1\right\}
, which are mapped into themselves by
\mathcal{C}
. Gauging this
\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
leaves behind elements that are not fixed under the action of
\mathcal{C}
. This argument applies to the gauging of every
\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
with
k
a power of 2 that divides
N
, where some of the elements that survive the gauging are not fixed under
\mathcal{C}
and therefore are able to distinguish a representation from its conjugate.

The generalization of this idea to the non-invertible 1-form symmetry is to gauge the
\mathcal{C}
-invariant collection of all the symmetries. Since the non-invertible topological operators are labelled by conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{SU}(N)^{\#}
, the
\mathcal{C}
-invariant subset consists of those operators labelled by conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{SO}(N)^{\#}
. The centre
\Z_{2}=\mathsf{Z}(\mathrm{SO}(N))
is precisely the invertible 1-form symmetry group
\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
that we have gauged to find spontaneous breaking of charge conjugation symmetry in Subsection
4.2
.

A generalized
\theta
-term that extends
\kappa=1\!\!\mod 2
to the non-invertible symmetry is achieved by choosing
\kappa
to be the vector representation of
\mathrm{SO}(N)
. By their branching rules, both
\Box
and
\overline{\Box}
survive the projection, thus we conclude that charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken in the orbifold.

Proposition 5.6
.

Consider two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with gauge group
\mathrm{SU}(N)
,
N\in 2\N
. In the orbifold theory obtained by gauging the non-invertible 1-form symmetry
\mathrm{SO}(N)^{\#}
with a generalized
\theta
-angle given by the character of the vector representation of
\mathrm{SO}(N)
, charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken.

6
Anomalies

In this section we begin by giving an exhaustive analysis of mixed anomalies in
\mathrm{U}(1)
and
\mathrm{U}(N)
Yang–Mills theories. Then we discuss mixed anomalies present in the orbifolds of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory involving the
(-1)
-form symmetry. The main result is Proposition
6.4
, which we prove directly in the two-dimensional gauge theory.

6.1
Charge conjugation and lower form gauge transformations

Let
\mathsf{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
be a
(-1)
-form symmetry group, which we will assume to be either
\mathrm{U}(1)
or a cyclic subgroup
\Z_{k}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)
. A
\mathsf{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
transformation in two dimensions is generated by a Wilson surface labelled by
\eta\in\mathsf{\Gamma}
. The insertion of these operators in the partition function and correlation functions shifts the
\theta
-parameter as
\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\longmapsto\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\eta
. We have also observed in Subsection
3.6
(and will find again below) that, after turning on a 0-form gauge field, it shifts
\frac{\theta}{2\pi}
by the corresponding background field.

In the language of the
(-1)
-form symmetry, the periodicity of
\theta
becomes invariance under a
(-1)
-form gauge transformation, when a background for the
(-1)
-form symmetry is activated. Conversely, when the theory fails to be invariant under a shift of
\theta
by its periodicity after some background gauge field is turned on, we interpret it as a breaking of the
(-1)
-form symmetry. These ideas will be explicitly realized momentarily.

Having set the stage to treat
(-1)
-form symmetries, we can now state the obvious

Proposition 6.1
.

Consider two-dimensional pure Yang–Mills theory with multiply-connected gauge group
G
, i.e.
\pi_{1}(G)\neq 1
, which admits outer automorphisms. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the non-trivial element in
\mathsf{C}\subseteq\mathsf{Out}(G)
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
the operator
\mathcal{C}
followed by a
(-1)
-form gauge transformation. The theory is invariant under
\mathcal{C}
at
\theta=0
and under
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
at
\theta=\pi
.

The only novelty of this statement is to pinpoint the role of the
(-1)
-form symmetry, which however has important consequences. Indeed, the mixed anomalies involving charge conjugation in the literature typically rely on the non-invariance under shifts of
\theta
. We claim that the anomalous nature of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
originates in an anomalous
(-1)
-form symmetry.

6.2
Anomalies in U(
N
) Yang–Mills theory

Charge conjugation anomaly in U(1) Yang–Mills theory

We consider two-dimensional
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory and state here the result of
[
56
, Section 2]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Two-dimensional pure
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory at
\theta=\pi
suffers from a mixed anomaly between
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and the 1-form symmetry.

The proof is straightforward. The
\theta
-term

\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,F

is not invariant under background
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
-gauge transformations, as these shift the action functional by
\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n
for
n\in\Z
. This is fixed by the usual replacement
F\longmapsto F-B
, but now the counterterm is not invariant under
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. In contrast to the flipping of sign of
F
, the non-invariance of
B
cannot be fixed by a shift
\theta\longmapsto\theta+2\pi
, whereby

\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\ \longmapsto\ \frac{\theta}{2\pi}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B+\int_{\Sigma}\,B\ ,

because the last summand is not integrally quantized. In conclusion, one cannot preserve both charge conjugation and
1
-form symmetry.

Anomaly in the space of couplings in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}(1)}
Yang–Mills theory

It was shown in
[
35
, Section 4]
that pure
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory suffers from an anomaly in the space of coupling constants. This means the impossibility of turning on a background for
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
while at the same time promoting
\theta
to a position-dependent coupling, i.e. an axion field, and preserving background gauge invariance.

Mixed lower-higher form anomaly in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}(1)}
Yang–Mills theory

We now revisit the two anomalies just presented. The difference in our approach with respect to
[
35
]
is that we keep
\theta
fixed and turn on a background gauge field
a
for
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Two-dimensional pure
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory suffers from a mixed anomaly between the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
1-form symmetry and the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}

(-1)
-form symmetry.

Consider the
\theta
-term with a 2-form background
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
gauge field
B
turned on. Introducing the background
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
-gauge field
a
, the non-invariant part of the action functional is

\int_{\Sigma}\,(F-B)\,\left(\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\mathrm{i}\,a\right)\ .

We now leave
\theta
unchanged but act with a gauge transformation of
a
. There is a shift by
\int_{\Sigma}\,B
that is not properly quantized, thus spoiling the invariance.

We conclude that, even at
\theta=\pi
, the mixed anomaly involving
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a consequence of the impossibility of preserving gauge invariance under both
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
and
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
. This is a corollary of Proposition
6.3
combined with Proposition
6.1
. We stress that turning on the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
gauge field
a
breaks the symmetry under naive charge conjugation
\mathcal{C}
explicitly, but in exactly the same way as any
\theta\neq 0
does. The crux of the matter is whether or not
\mathcal{C}
maps the theory to a
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
gauge equivalent theory.

Let us emphasize that this result is analogous to the mixed electric-magnetic anomaly of Maxwell theory in four dimensions. The electric 1-form symmetry and the magnetic
(d-3)
-form symmetry generally both participate in the anomaly in
d
dimensions. For
d=4
both are 1-form symmetries, while for
d=2
the magnetic symmetry is a
(-1)
-form symmetry.

{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}(N)}}
Yang–Mills theory

It is straightforward to extend the argument of
\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory to higher rank gauge groups
\mathrm{U}(N)
for
N>1
. Turning on the background gauge field
B
for the 1-form symmetry
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, the action functional becomes

\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big[(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})\ast(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})\big]+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\left(F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N}\right)\ ,

which fails to be invariant under shifts
\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\longmapsto\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+n
for
n\in\Z
. However, in contrast to the
N=1
case, if

N\,\int_{\Sigma}\,B\ \in\ 2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\Z\ ,

the
2\pi
-periodicity of
\theta
is preserved. In other words, in the
\mathrm{U}(N)
theory for
N>1
, a subgroup
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is anomaly-free. This non-anomalous
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is identified with the 1-form symmetry of
\mathrm{SU}(N)\subset\mathrm{U}(N)
.

6.3
Mixed lower-higher form anomaly

We will now show

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
N
a multiple of
k
, with
k^{2}\notin N\Z
. Pure
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
suffers from a mixed anomaly between the
(-1)
-form symmetry
\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
and the 1-form symmetry
\Z_{N/k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
.

In the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and
\mathrm{PSU}(N)
theories, there cannot be such an anomaly, because the
(-1)
-form symmetry in the former and the 1-form symmetry in the latter are trivial, hence trivially anomaly-free.

Let us quickly recall the setup of Section
3
. The
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
curvature
F^{\,\textrm{e}}
decomposes into the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
curvature
F
and the 2-form gauge field
B
.
The theory has the electric 1-form symmetry
\check{\mathsf{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{N/k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, which we denote with a check to distinguish it from
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
of Section
3
, and the magnetic
(-1)
-form symmetry
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}=\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
. The background gauge fields are respectively the 2-form
\check{B}
and the scalar
K
.

The coupling of the electric 2-form field
\check{B}
to
F^{\,\textrm{e}}
shifts

F^{\,\textrm{e}}\longmapsto F^{\,\textrm{e}}-\check{B}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N}\ .

On the other hand,
K
enters through the magnetic coupling

\frac{k}{N}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,K\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F^{\,\textrm{e}}\ .

(6.5)

The coefficient here, which generalizes the standard coupling (
3.25
) to the case
k<N
, follows from (
3.12
), or equivalently from the fact that, by embedding
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
into the
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
Chern–Weil symmetry, the conserved current is
\ast\,\frac{k}{N}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F^{\,\textrm{e}}
. In any case (
6.5
) is invariant under background
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
-gauge transformations, by recalling that
\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,F^{\,\textrm{e}}\in 2\pi\,\frac{N}{k}\,\Z
.

When both background gauge fields are turned on, the pertinent part of the action functional reads

\frac{k}{N}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,K\,\mathrm{Tr}(F^{\,\textrm{e}}-\check{B}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N})=-k\,\int_{\Sigma}\,K\,\check{B}+2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n\ ,

where
n\in\Z
.
We arrive at the natural modification of the action functional in (
3.24
) given by

\int_{\Sigma}\,\check{B}\,\left(-k\,K+\kappa\right)\ ,

(6.6)

where we do not write explicitly all the gauge-invariant terms that define the theory, and only focus on the anomalous part. This term is invariant under a background
\check{\mathsf{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
-gauge transformation.

However, it is not invariant under a background
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
-gauge transformation unless
\int_{\Sigma}\,k\,\check{B}\in 2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\Z
. This is not generically the case, and there are three possibilities:

1)

If
k=\frac{N}{k}
, then the coefficient in (
6.6
) is just enough to preserve invariance under both
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
and
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
background gauge transformations. More generally, if
k
is an integer multiple of
\frac{N}{k}
, i.e.
k^{2}\in N\,\mathbb{Z}
, then (
6.6
) is also invariant under
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
background gauge transformations.

2)

If
k^{2}\notin N\Z
, then (
6.6
) is improperly quantized and is not invariant under general
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
background gauge transformations.

3)

If
\mathrm{gcd}(k,\frac{N}{k})=m>1
, then a subgroup
\Z_{m}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\subset\check{\mathsf{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is anomaly-free.

The lack of background gauge invariance if both background fields are turned on indicates the presence of a mixed anomaly.

6.4
Mixed 2-group anomaly

As we have seen, charge conjugation
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
acts on the 1-form symmetry
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
.
We can thus consider the non-trivial symmetry extension

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\Z_{N}\longrightarrow\Z_{N}\rtimes\Z_{2}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow 1

in the category of groups. This is the
semi-direct product group
\Z_{N}\rtimes\Z_{2}
in the automorphism 2-group
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathrm{SU}(N))\,/\!\!/\,\mathrm{SU}(N)
of the stack of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-bundles with connection, where the left factor is the 1-form symmetry and the right factor is charge conjugation.

As sets

\displaystyle\Z_{N}\cong\big\{\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta/N}\big\}_{\beta=0,1,\dots,N-1}\qquad,\qquad\mathsf{C}\cong\{1,\mathcal{C}\}\ ,

and
\Z_{N}\rtimes\Z_{2}
contains the same elements as the set
\Z_{N}\times\mathsf{C}
. The group law in
\Z_{N}\rtimes\Z_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{1}/N},1)\cdot(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{2}/N},1)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})/N},1)\ ,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{1}/N},1)\cdot(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{2}/N},\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})/N},\mathcal{C})\ ,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{1}/N},\mathcal{C})\cdot(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{2}/N},1)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})/N},\mathcal{C})\ ,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{1}/N},\mathcal{C})\cdot(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\beta_{2}/N},\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})/N},1)\ ,

for all
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\left\{0,1,\dots,N-1\right\}
.

The symmetry defects for
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
are topological line operators of type
\eta\in\{0,1\}
, while the symmetry defects for
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
are local topological operators of type
\beta\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
. The action of the 0-form symmetry on local operators restricts to give an action on the generators of the 1-form symmetry. By the general argument in
[
26
]
, gauging either
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
or
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
will produce a mixed anomaly between the remaining symmetry and the Pontryagin dual symmetry that emerges after the gauging. We can make this rather explicit in the case at hand when
N\in 2\N
.

Starting from pure
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
, gauging the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
produces the dual
(-1)
-form symmetry
\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}=\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
, where
\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}\cong\Z_{N}
is the Pontryagin dual of
\mathsf{Z}=\Z_{N}
. The orbifold theory is pure
\mathrm{PSU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
and the symmetry group is now the direct product
\Z_{N}\times\Z_{2}
with the remaining 0-form symmetry
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
. Gauging
\Z_{N/k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}\subseteq\widehat{\mathsf{Z}}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
can be achieved through the magnetic coupling of a background 0-form gauge field
a
to the
\mathrm{PSU}(N)
curvature
F^{\,\textrm{e}}
:

\displaystyle\frac{N/k}{N}\int_{\Sigma}\,a\ \mathrm{Tr}\,F^{\,\textrm{e}}=\frac{N}{k}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,a\,B\ .

Charge conjugation acts on this term as
\mathcal{C}:B\longmapsto-B
, as a result of the original non-trivial extension. This is not invariant and, by a computation completely analogous to the one below (
6.6
), the two configurations before and after applying
\mathcal{C}
are not equivalent unless
k^{2}\in N\Z
. Hence there is no way in general to gauge the
(-1)
-form symmetry
\Z_{N/k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
while preserving the 0-form symmetry
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
.

On the other hand, gauging the 0-form symmetry
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
in
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory yields the dual 0-form symmetry
\widehat{\mathsf{C}}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
. The orbifold theory is now
\widetilde{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
Yang–Mills theory with symmetry group the direct product
\Z_{N}\times\Z_{2}
with the remaining 1-form symmetry
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
. As illustrated in Figure
3
, when a local operator of type
\beta
is dragged across a line of type
\eta
, it acquires a phase factor
\lambda_{\beta}(\eta)
, where
\lambda\in Z^{1}(\mathrm{B}\Z_{N},\widehat{\mathsf{C}}\,)
is a 1-cocycle corresponding to the non-trivial extension class. This 1-cocycle therefore becomes a mixed anomaly between
\widehat{\mathsf{C}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
and
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, once
\mathsf{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}
is gauged
[
59
]
.

\bullet
\wp_{\beta}
L_{\eta}
\ =\ \mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,\lambda_{\beta}(\eta)}
\bullet
\wp_{\beta}
L_{\eta}

Applications

We conclude in this final section by briefly describing some simple implications of our results for other field theories. For further phenomenological implications, especially in higher dimensions, see

7.1
The massive Schwinger model

Let us discuss our results for the abelian case in relation with the massive Schwinger model. This is
\mathrm{U}(1)
gauge theory with a massive Dirac fermion in two dimensions. Let
m
denote the bare mass of the fermion field and
g
the gauge coupling as before. At
\theta=\pi
, the phase diagram as a function of the dimensionless parameter
\hat{m}:=\frac{m}{g}
presents a critical point
\hat{m}_{\text{cr}}

[
92
,
93
]
. For
\hat{m}<\hat{m}_{\text{cr}}
the theory has a unique gapped vacuum, while in the large mass phase
\hat{m}>\hat{m}_{\text{cr}}
the vacuum is two-fold degenerate and charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken
[
92
,
93
]
.

From our findings it is clear that, for very large mass, charge conjugation symmetry must be spontaneously broken at
\theta=\pi
. Indeed we expect that, when

\mathrm{U}(1)
Yang–Mills theory in a first approximation. Using the bosonization of the massive Schwinger model

and
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}

\mathsf{C}
is spontaneously broken at
\theta=\pi
in pure Yang–Mills theory, so it must also be in the massive Schwinger model when the mass is very large. In fact, after bosonization our argument becomes identical to the one used in
[

for the two-dimensional abelian Higgs model.

7.3
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformation

The composite operator
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
induces an irrelevant deformation of any two-dimensional quantum field theory with a conserved stress-energy tensor
\mathrm{T}
. It is thus not expected to modify the low energy behaviour of the theory. As a consequence, every symmetry of the original quantum field theory, be it invertible or non-invertible, is either explicitly broken by the term
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
in the action functional, or otherwise it should be preserved at every point of the irrelevant flow triggered by the
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformation.
If the global (possibly non-invertible) symmetry suffers from an anomaly, one invokes the ’t Hooft anomaly matching condition to argue that the full symmetry structure is preserved. Indeed, the anomaly is captured by topological terms, which do not couple to the metric and thus are insensitive to the deformation by the operator
\text{T}\,\overline{\text{T}}
. We now proceed to show explicitly how the latter statement works within the context of our earlier results.

The irrelevant perturbation of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory by the operator
\text{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
was considered in
[
96
,
97
,
98
,
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
]
. Let
G
be a gauge group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(N)
. The
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformed partition function on a closed, connected and oriented Riemann surface
\Sigma
is

\mathcal{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}}[\Sigma]=\sum_{R\,\in\,\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))_{\tau}}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}^{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}}(R;\tau)}\ ,

(7.2)

where
\tau\geq 0
is the deformation parameter. In (
7.2
) we have defined

C_{2}^{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}}(R;\tau):=\frac{C_{2}(R)}{1-\frac{\tau}{N^{3}}\,C_{2}(R)}\ ,

(7.3)

while the instanton analysis of
[
101
]
shows that the sum over irreducible
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-representations
R
is restricted to the subset

\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))_{\tau}:=\big\{R\in\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))\ \big|\ \tau\,C_{2}(R)<N^{3}\big\}\ ,

which guarantees convergence of the series (
7.2
).

Following step by step the derivation of
[
99
]
we find that the
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformation is compatible with the orbifold. The
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformed partition function of
\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
Yang–Mills theory on
\Sigma
with discrete
\theta
-angle
\kappa
is simply

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}}^{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}},\kappa}[\Sigma]=\sum_{R\,\in\,\mathfrak{R}(\mathrm{SU}(N))_{\tau}}\,(\dim R)^{\chi}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,C_{2}^{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}}(R;\tau)}\penalty\ \delta\left(\lvert R\rvert-\kappa\!\!\!\!\mod k\right)\ .

From this expression and the fact that the deformed Casimir (
7.3
) yields the same vacua as the undeformed Casimir invariant, we may state

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
k\geq 2
be a power of
2
and
N\in k\N
. In
G=\mathrm{SU}(N)/\Z_{k}
(resp.
G=\mathrm{U}(1)
)
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformed Yang–Mills theory at
\kappa=\frac{k}{2}
, hence
\theta_{\kappa}=\pi
(resp.
\theta=\pi
), charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken.

This result was expected, because
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
is an irrelevant operator and thus should not change the low energy behaviour of a system, while the spontaneous breaking of
\mathsf{C}
is a statement about the vacua of the theory. An alternative point of view is to say that gauging the 1-form symmetry
\Z_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
corresponds to inserting networks of topological point defects, which are insensitive to the deformation of the metric brought about by the
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deformation.

Non-invertible 1-form symmetry

One main aspect of the theory with partition function (

\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}

persists in the

-deformed theory.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking in the
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}}
-deformed massive Schwinger model

Combining Corollary
7.4
with, for instance, the observations in Subsection

, we predict that charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken, in the large mass phase, in the
\mathrm{T}\,\overline{\mathrm{T}}
-deform-ation of the massive Schwinger model.

7.4
Lower form symmetry in higher dimensions

A version of Proposition
6.1
applies to four and higher even dimensions. The conclusions of this subsection overlap with the recent results of
[
40
]
.

The Gaiotto–Kapustin–Komargodski–Seiberg anomaly

In
[
7
]
, Gaiotto–Kapustin–Komargodski–Seiberg considered four-dimensional pure
\mathrm{SU}(N)
Yang–Mills theory and observed a mixed anomaly between CP symmetry and the 1-form symmetry
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
. The anomaly is signalled by the breakdown of the periodicity
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
when a background 2-form gauge field for
\Z_{N}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is turned on.

Here we consider the four-dimensional theory on a closed four-manifold
M_{4}
with metric whose Hodge star operator is denoted
\ast
. We use the
\theta
-term

-\mathrm{i}\,\theta\,\int_{M_{4}}\,c_{2}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)

where
c_{2}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
is the second Chern class of the adjoint bundle associated to a principal
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-bundle
P\longrightarrow M_{4}
. Since
c_{2}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
is closed, we can consider the Chern–Weil
(-1)
-form symmetry
\mathrm{U}(1)^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
with conserved 0-form current
\ast\,c_{2}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
. Turning on a background
0
-form gauge field
a
as a source for this current, the
\theta
-term becomes

-\mathrm{i}\,\int_{M_{4}}\,c_{2}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)\,\left(\theta+\mathrm{i}\,a\right)\ ,

(7.5)

where now the periodicity
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
is reinterpreted as the invariance under
(-1)
-form gauge transformations.

After coupling this system to a background 2-form gauge field
B
, (
7.5
) ceases to be invariant under shifts
\theta\longmapsto\theta+2\pi
. In the language of
(-1)
-form symmetries,
B
breaks invariance under background
(-1)
-form gauge transformations. The Gaiotto–Kapustin–Komargodski–Seiberg anomaly is thus a mixed anomaly between the 1-form and
(-1)
-form symmetries.

Lower form symmetry in even dimensions

The previous discussion generalizes in the following way. For any closed
2n
-dimensional manifold
M_{2n}
, with metric and corresponding Hodge star operator
\ast
, we consider a principal
G
-bundle
P\longrightarrow M_{2n}
whose adjoint bundle has non-trivial
n^{\text{th}}
Chern class:
c_{n}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)\neq 0
. Then
G
Yang–Mills theory on
M_{2n}
admits the
\theta
-term

\mathrm{i}^{1-n}\,\theta\,\int_{M_{2n}}\,c_{n}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)\ ,

or its appropriate modification as in (
3.4
) for a discrete parameter
\kappa
. The
2\pi
-periodicity of
\theta
, or alternatively the periodic identification
\kappa\sim\kappa+k
, can be interpreted as the gauge invariance under
(-1)
-form Chern–Weil symmetries associated to the conserved 0-form current
\ast\,c_{n}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
.

Turning on a background 2-form gauge field
B
for the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}:=\mathsf{Z}(G)
modifies
c_{n}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
by replacing
F\longmapsto F-B\otimes\mathbbm{1}
in its representation as a Chern–Weil form. Generically, the mixed terms in the expansion involving

\frac{\mathrm{i}\,\theta}{(2\pi)^{n}}\,\int_{M_{2n}}\,B^{j}\wedge\mathrm{Tr}\,F^{l}

may spoil the periodicity of the
\theta
-parameter. By coupling the conserved current
\ast\,c_{n}(\mathrm{ad}\,P)
to a background 0-form gauge field
a
, we recast the breakdown of periodicity as a mixed anomaly between the 1-form symmetry and the
(-1)
-form symmetry.

Appendix B
Orthogonal gauge algebras

B.1
\theta
-terms and anomalies

We consider the family of theories with gauge algebra
\mathfrak{so}(N)
where
N
is even, and analyse the possible
\theta
-angles and anomalies. The ensuing analysis is inspired by
[
103
]
, and adapts the ideas therein to two dimensions.

Consider the Lie group
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
. Its centre is given by

\mathsf{Z}(\mathrm{Spin}(N))=\begin{cases}\ \Z_{2}\times\Z_{2}\ ,&\quad N=0\!\!\!\mod 4\ ,\\
\ \Z_{2}\ ,&\quad N=1\!\!\!\mod 4\ ,\\
\ \Z_{4}\ ,&\quad N=2\!\!\!\mod 4\ ,\\
\ \Z_{2}\ ,&\quad N=3\!\!\!\mod 4\ .\end{cases}

Closely related is the group
\mathrm{SO}(N)
, of which
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
is an extension by the group
\mathsf{A}=\Z_{2}
:

1\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spin}(N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SO}(N)\longrightarrow 1\ .

Principal
\mathrm{SO}(N)
-bundles
P\longrightarrow\Sigma
are characterized by their second Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{2}(P)\in H^{2}(\Sigma,\mathsf{A})
, obstructing the lift of
P
to a
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
-bundle.

On the other hand, the group
\mathrm{O}(N)
is an extension of
\mathrm{SO}(N)
by a different group
\mathsf{C}=\Z_{2}
:

1\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathrm{O}(N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SO}(N)\longrightarrow 1\ .

This
\Z_{2}
group is identified with charge conjugation. The obstruction to reducing an
\mathrm{O}(N)
-bundle
P\longrightarrow\Sigma
to an
\mathrm{SO}(N)
-bundle is its first Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{1}(P)\in H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathsf{C})
. One can also combine the two
\Z_{2}
extensions of
\mathrm{SO}(N)
to form the group
\mathrm{Pin}^{+}(N)
.

Finally, for even
N
, one can further take the quotient
\mathrm{P}\mathrm{SO}(N)=\mathrm{SO}(N)/\Z_{2}
, which corresponds to gauging the intrinsic 1-form symmetry.

For
N=2\!\!\mod 4
, we can start from
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
Yang–Mills theory, based on a principal
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
-bundle
P\longrightarrow\Sigma
, and gauge the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{4}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
in two steps. There is a non-trivial group extension

1\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\Z_{4}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow 1

(B.1)

with
\mathsf{A}\cong\mathsf{B}\cong\Z_{2}
(we denote them with different symbols to avoid confusion). By gauging
\mathsf{A}
we obtain
\mathrm{SO}(N)
Yang–Mills theory.

In more detail, we turn on the background

\beta:=w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)+2\,w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)\ \in\ H^{2}(\Sigma,\Z_{4})\ ,

where
w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)
corresponds to the Stiefel–Whitney class we want to sum over to pass from
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
to
\mathrm{SO}(N)
gauge theory, and
w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)
is the characteristic class of the bundle
P
for the remaining
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
1-form symmetry. In the notation of the main text, we embed
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
into
\mathrm{U}(1)
to obtain the identifications

w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)=\frac{2\,a}{2\pi}\qquad,\qquad w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)=\frac{2\,B}{2\pi}

for 2-form gauge fields
a
and
B
, respectively. A
\Z_{4}
-gauge transformation shifts
\beta
by an element of
H^{2}(\Sigma,4\Z)
, corresponding to the standard gauge transformation of the fields
a
and
B
.

Coupling the
\mathrm{Spin}(N)
gauge theory to such a background allows for a discrete
\theta
-angle classified by
\Z_{4}
, which we write as

2\pi\,\frac{\kappa}{4}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\beta=\pi\,\kappa\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\,w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)+w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)\right)\ .

Here
\kappa
has periodicity
\kappa\sim\kappa+4
. To gauge
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
we sum over topological classes of bundles labelled by
w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)\in H^{2}(\Sigma,\mathsf{A})
.

Having gauged
\mathsf{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, the
\mathrm{SO}(N)
gauge theory has a Pontryagin dual
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}=\widehat{\mathsf{A}}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}

(-1)
-form symmetry. We want to couple the theory to a 0-form gauge field
K
for this
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}

(-1)
-form symmetry. By reasoning along the lines of
[
103
, Section 4]
, but now with the magnetic symmetry being a
(-1)
-form symmetry, we find the coupling

\pi\,\int_{\Sigma}\,w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)\,\left(\kappa-2\,\mathrm{i}\,K\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)\,\left(\kappa-2\,\mathrm{i}\,K\right)\ ,

with the understanding that
w_{2}^{\mathsf{A}}(P)
is summed over while
w_{2}^{\mathsf{B}}(P)
and
K
are background fields which are kept fixed. The first term is gauge-invariant, and it is also invariant under
(-1)
-form background gauge transformations. The second term, however, is not invariant under
\mathsf{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}
background gauge transformations. This is a manifestation of the mixed anomaly of
[
26
]
, except that here it involves the lower form symmetry.

B.2
Orbifolds and spontaneous symmetry breaking

We study orbifolds of two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with gauge group
G
whose gauge algebra is
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(N)
, and analyze the fate of charge conjugation symmetry.

Let us focus on
G=\mathrm{SO}(N)
with
N\in 2\N
; throughout this subappendix only we write
N=2r
. Its tensor representations are in correspondence with Young diagrams of row lengths at most
r
, where the last row length comes with a sign. Spinor representations can also be put in correspondence with Young diagrams whose row lengths are shifted by a half-integer. Of particular interest to us are the following representations:

•

The vector representation
\Box
, with Casimir
C_{2}(\Box)=N-1
.

•

The spinor representation
\mathsf{s}
corresponding to
R_{i}=\frac{1}{2}
for all
i=1,\dots,r
, with Casimir
C_{2}(\mathsf{s})=\frac{N}{8}\,(N-1)
.

•

The cospinor representation
\mathsf{c}
corresponding to
R_{i}=\frac{1}{2}
for all
i=1,\dots,r-1
and
R_{r}=-\frac{1}{2}
, with Casimir
C_{2}(\mathsf{c})=\frac{N}{8}\,(N-1)
.

The spinor and cospinor representations have equal quadratic Casimir invariant as well as equal dimension, and are exchanged under charge conjugation.

We now gauge the 1-form symmetry
\mathsf{B}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
and turn on a discrete
\theta
-angle with
\kappa=1
and periodicity
\kappa\sim\kappa+2
. When
N=2\!\!\mod 4
, and hence
r
is odd, the three representations listed above survive the gauging. When
N=6
, the spinor and cospinor representations have lowest quadratic Casimir invariant, thus we obtain a degenerate vacuum and charge conjugation symmetry is spontaneously broken in
\mathrm{PSO}(6)
Yang–Mills theory. However, for
N>8
the vector representation has lower quadratic Casimir invariant.

We note that for
N=8
, the vector, spinor and cospinor representations of
G=\mathrm{SO}(8)
all have equal quadratic Casimir invariant and equal dimension, as a consequence of the enhancement of the outer automorphism group of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(8)
from the cyclic group
\Z_{2}
of order two to the symmetric group
S_{3}
of degree three, i.e.
\mathrm{SO}(8)
-triality. It would be interesting to engineer a non-abelian orbifold of
\mathrm{SO}(8)
which removes the trivial representation, so that the vacuum is three-fold degenerate and the triality
S_{3}
is spontaneously broken.

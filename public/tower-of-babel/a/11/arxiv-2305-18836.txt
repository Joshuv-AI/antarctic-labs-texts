The Zero Viscosity Limit of Stochastic Navier-Stokes Flows
Daniel Goodair, Dan Crisan

Abstract
We introduce an analogue to Kato’s Criterion regarding the inviscid convergence of stochastic Navier-Stokes flows to the strong solution of the deterministic Euler equation. Our assumptions cover additive, multiplicative and transport type noise models. This is achieved firstly for the typical noise scaling of ν 1 2 \nu^{\frac{1}{2}} , before considering a new parameter which approaches zero with viscosity but at a potentially different rate. We determine the implications of this for our criterion and clarify a sense in which the scaling by ν 1 2 \nu^{\frac{1}{2}} is optimal. To enable the analysis we prove the existence of probabilistically weak, analytically weak solutions to a general stochastic Navier-Stokes Equation on a bounded domain with no-slip boundary condition in three spatial dimensions, as well as the existence and uniqueness of probabilistically strong, analytically weak solutions in two dimensions. The criterion applies for these solutions in both two and three dimensions, with some technical simplifications in the 2D case.

The width of the boundary layer formally scales with the square root of viscosity, fitting for the parabolic Navier-Stokes equations, and is described by the Prandtl Equations in the most commonly studied setting of a no-slip boundary condition (under which the fluid velocity is zero on the boundary) for Navier-Stokes. Indeed this is considered to be the most physically reasonable boundary condition for viscous flow as well discussed in [

Kato’s work shows that under sufficient smoothness of the initial condition, weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary condition converge to strong solutions of the Euler equation with impermeable boundary condition (the normal component of the fluid velocity is zero on the boundary) if and only if

\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}\nu\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla u^{\nu}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{c\nu})}ds=0

where
u^{\nu}

We shall work with a Navier-Stokes Equation perturbed by either an Itô type noise

u_{t}-u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\,ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\Delta u_{s}\,ds+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(u_{s})d\mathcal{W}_{s}+\nabla\rho_{t}=0

or a Stratonovich one

u_{t}-u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\,ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\Delta u_{s}\,ds+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(u_{s})\circ d\mathcal{W}_{s}+\nabla\rho_{t}=0.

(2)

Here

the nonlinear term,
\mathcal{W}

is an operator valued mapping satisfying assumptions to be given in Subsection

The first main contribution of the paper is in showing the existence of such solutions, probabilistically weak in three spatial dimensions but strong in two dimensions. This is a typical reflection of the uniqueness in two dimensions which is unavailable in 3D, as seen in related works on stochastic Navier-Stokes and in more general SPDE theory, see [
21
,
63
,
74
]). These works allow for a transport type noise, though do not extend to our result; in [
74
] the authors consider an abstract variational framework which in large resembles our setting, though the noise operator must have only a small dependency on first order derivatives (relative to viscosity in our context). Such an assumption is unavoidable in the Itô case (
1
), however we alleviate it in a treatment of (
2
). This is critical as we are guided by an application to Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT), one of the aforementioned physical stochastic perturbation principles [
39
] which does not satisfy such a restriction. Debussche, Hug and Mémin show the corresponding results in [
21
] for the Location Uncertainty scheme [
61
], where the noise is specifically chosen so as to conserve energy through a backscattering term which helps the analysis. Moreover the atypical nature of this perturbation renders it difficult to recover the results for more traditional noise of the form (
1
), (
2
). In addition the paper [
63
] of Mikulevicius and Rozovskii deals with a Stratonovich transport noise, though it imposes a stringent coercivity condition and considers only the whole space without boundary.

The theoretical analysis of fluid equations with a transport type noise on a bounded domain has proven of great challenge. For analytically strong solutions, prior to the authors’ works of [
35
,
37
] the only successful existence result of which we are aware was given in [
10
] where the authors assume again that the first order dependency is small (which is necessary in the Itô case), but crucially that the noise terms are traceless under Leray Projection. This assumption is designed to circumvent the technical difficulties of a first order noise operator on a bounded domain, which is well elucidated in [
36
] and the failure of this assumption for stochastic Lie transport is precisely why we could only show the (local) existence of analytically strong solutions to the SALT Navier-Stokes Equation on a bounded domain in vorticity form (the velocity form of the SALT Navier-Stokes Equation on a bounded domain remains an open problem). We are, however, successful in showing the (global) existence of analytically weak solutions for the velocity form in the present paper. The difference lies in the energy norm for the solutions: in the strong case this is produced from a
W^{1,2}
inner product, a space in which the Leray Projector is not an orthogonal projection and indeed does not commute with the derivatives entering into consideration from this inner product. The presence of this Leray Projector prevents us from using the usual cancellation type arguments for transport noise, so without assuming sufficient smallness of the derivative dependency as discussed then we cannot achieve the necessary energy estimates. In contrast weak solutions exist in an energy space generated by the
L^{2}
inner product, in which the Leray Projector is self-adjoint and the preceding ideas can be applied.

The second and titular contribution of the paper is in characterising the zero viscosity limit of these solutions, establishing a stochastic counterpart to Kato’s Criterion. For this we scale the stochastic integral of (
1
), (
2
) with a parameter that must go to zero with viscosity, which is traditionally taken to be
\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}
having been motivated in [
49
] as the only noise scaling which leads to non-trivial limiting measures (in the limit
t\rightarrow\infty
and
\nu\rightarrow 0
) for an additive noise in two dimensions in the absence of a boundary. The significance of this scaling for energy balance is further underlined in [
48
,
50
,
51
] and has been used to study the inviscid limit problem in [
33
,
57
]. We firstly establish our stochastic Kato’s Criterion for this choice of scaling, demonstrating the equivalence of these conditions taken in expectation, again for a general stochastic term which can have arbitrarily high first order dependency. Furthermore a new criterion is presented dependent on an abstract scaling choice, and is specifically analysed in the case where this parameter is some exponent of viscosity. In particular we show that with the choice
\nu^{\beta}
, then for
\frac{1}{4}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
one requires the condition

\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}\nu^{4\beta-1}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla u^{\nu}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{c\nu})}ds=0

in order to deduce the convergence to Euler. For
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}
then the anticipated condition

\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla u^{\nu}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{c\nu})}ds=0

is both necessary and sufficient for the convergence.

Overall the zero viscosity limit of solutions of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equation has so far received little treatment; the works of [
6
,
33
] determine measure theoretic results for the problem posed in two dimensions with periodic boundary conditions and an additive noise. For Navier boundary conditions the convergence of stochastic Navier-Stokes to stochastic Euler in 2D has been proven in [
12
], matching the deterministic result as presented in [
46
], again for additive noise. In the classical case of a no-slip boundary condition, the only results of which we are aware are given in [
57
,
90
]. A stochastic Kato type result is proven in each paper, for the limit to determinstic Euler in [
57
] and stochastic Euler in [
90
], though once more only in 2D and with additive noise. Our result for a general first order noise in 2D and 3D thus represents a distinct addition to the literature.

The structure of the paper is as follows:

•

Section
1
is devoted to the setup of the problem in terms of notation, the functional framework of solutions and the assumptions we impose on the noise. Examples of noise satisfying these requirements are considered in Subsection
1.4

1.5

•

In Section
2
we state and prove the main results regarding the zero viscosity limit, firstly for the noise scaling of
\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}
and then for a general parameter. We inherit the techniques of Kato’s original paper, particularly referring to the boundary corrector function.

•

Section
3

1
. The method of existence is classical in the sense that we consider a finite dimensional approximation with relative compactness arguments due to a tightness criterion, perhaps most similar to the approach of [
74

) in the proof of uniqueness in 2D, which is absent in the aforementioned [
63
] and emphasised by the authors in [
21

•

An appendix, Section
4
, containing useful results from the literature regarding stochastic partial differential equations and tightness criteria concludes the paper.

so is in particular Lipschitz Continuous. Of course

\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{l}-1}\mathbbm{1}_{[t^{l}_{j-1},t^{l}_{j}]}(s)\partial_{j}\phi_{t^{l}_{j}}

so

\partial_{j}\phi_{s}-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{l}-1}\mathbbm{1}_{[t^{l}_{j-1},t^{l}_{j}]}(s)\left[\partial_{j}\phi_{s}-\partial_{j}\phi_{t^{l}_{j}}\right]

thus if
K_{x}
is the constant of Lipschitz Continuity, then

\lVert\partial_{j}\phi(x)-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}(x)\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left([0,t];\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq K_{x}\max_{j}\lvert t^{l}_{j}-t^{l}_{j-1}\rvert

which converges to zero as
l\rightarrow\infty
. Now then we have that

\displaystyle\lVert\phi-\tilde{\phi}^{l}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}\left([0,t];W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lVert\partial_{j}\phi_{s}-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathscr{O}}\lvert\partial_{j}\phi_{s}(x)-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}(x)\rvert^{2}dxds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathscr{O}}\lVert\partial_{j}\phi(x)-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathscr{O}}\lVert\partial_{j}\phi(x)-\partial_{j}\tilde{\phi}^{l}(x)\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}dx

where
c
continues to represent a generic constant. The claim then follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem, with dominating function
4\lVert\partial_{j}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}
which is continuous on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathscr{O}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
hence square integrable. In fact with the observation that the supremum of the integral is bounded by the integral of the supremum, we have in fact shown the stronger convergence in
L^{\infty}\left([0,t];W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
. With the claim established we again look to take limits in (
46
), and using the linearity of
\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{*}
and (
22
) then the convergence of the time integrals follows exactly as in (
47
). As for the stochastic integral we look to first show a convergence in expectation and make use of the Itô Isometry, hence we introduce stopping times

\theta_{n}:=t\wedge\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\int_{0}^{r}\lVert u_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\geq n\right\}

and consider

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}-\phi_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}u_{s},\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}-\phi_{s}\rangle^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\lVert u_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\lVert\tilde{\phi}^{l}_{s}-\phi_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\left[\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\lVert u_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\right]\lVert\tilde{\phi}^{l}-\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,t];L^{2}_{\sigma})}

which approaches zero as
l\rightarrow\infty
. So for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a subsequence indexed by
(l^{n}_{k})_{k}
such that

\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\tilde{\phi}^{l^{n}_{k}}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t\wedge{\theta_{n}}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
\mathbb{R}
. Defining
\Omega_{n}
as this set on which convergence occurs, and
\tilde{\Omega}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{n}
which is again of full measure, then we can take any
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}
and select an
n
such that
\theta_{n}(\omega)\geq t
, which exists from the continuity of
\int_{0}^{\cdot}\lVert u_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds
. For this fixed
n
we may extract the subsequence
(l^{n}_{k})_{k}
and deduce the required convergence, thus concluding the proof.

∎

3.2
Galerkin Scheme

We fix an
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
as in Theorem
1.9
. We shall work with a Galerkin Approximation and do so by considering (
13
) in its spatially strong form, projected by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
to give

u^{n}_{t}=u^{n}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s}\ ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}Au^{n}_{s}\,ds+\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s}ds-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(48)

where
u^{n}_{0}:=\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}
. We wish to show that strong solutions of (
48
) exist, where the notion of solution is typical and as given in [
37
] Proposition 6.1 for
\mathcal{H}:=V_{n}:=\textnormal{span}\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
(which is a Hilbert Space equipped with the
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
or any equivalent inner product). We look to apply Lemma 3.18 of this work to deduce that for any
M>1
and
S>0
, there exists a local strong solution of (
48
) up until the stopping time

\tau^{M,S}_{n}:=S\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\geq M+\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right\}.

Without a direct application of this lemma we reach the conclusion here in the same manner, where one can consider
V:=W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
H:=W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
U:=L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
with (
15
) and the equivalence of the norms on
V_{n}
. Having established local existence of solutions we note the uniqueness en route to showing that such a solution exists on
[0,T]
; recalling (
23
) and (
21
) we can prove the uniqueness identically to Theorem 3.29 of [
37
], noting again the equivalence of the norms on
V_{n}
. From here we deduce the existence of a unique maximal strong solution
(u^{n},\Theta^{n})
as in Theorems 3.32 and 3.34, and from Lemma 3.36 we also have the relation

\mathbbm{P}\left(\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,S}_{n}(\omega)<\Theta^{n}(\omega)\}\right)=1

(49)

for any
M>1
and
S>0
. We must be precise in using the characterisation of
\tau^{M,S}_{n}
, as this was initially defined (in [
37
]) as a first hitting time for a globally existing truncated process, which was then stopped at this time and relabelled to ignore the truncation. The maximal solution
u^{n}
is of course different to this process, but the uniqueness ensures that
\tau^{M,S}_{n}
is genuinely a first hitting time for the maximal
u^{n}
(as this process must be indistinguishable from the truncated one up until this stopping time). To prove the existence on
[0,T]
we want to show that
\mathbbm{P}\left(\{\omega\in\Omega:\Theta^{n}(\omega)\leq T\}\right)=0
. Moreover note that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\{\omega\in\Omega:\Theta^{n}(\omega)\leq T\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{P}\left(\bigcap_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}\right)

from (
49
) and the fact that
\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}
is increasing in
M
. From the characterisation of
\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}
note that

\displaystyle\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\sup_{r\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}(\omega)\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)}\lVert u^{n}_{r}(\omega)\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\geq M+\lVert u^{n}_{0}(\omega)\rVert^{2}\right\}

so a simple application of Chebyshev’s Inequality informs us that

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}\right)\leq\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\right].

(50)

This prompts the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(u^{n},\Theta^{n})
be the maximal strong solution of equation (
48
). There exists a constant
C
independent of
M,n,\nu
such that

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\right]\leq C\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+1\right].

(51)

Proof.

We can apply the Itô Formula to see that for any
0\leq r\leq T
, the identity

\displaystyle\lVert u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}-2\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle\ ds-2\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}Au^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle ds+\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}

holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
. We now immediately simplify the expression, using that
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}
are orthogonal projections in
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as well as the properties (
12
) and (
7
) to see that

\displaystyle\lVert u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle+2\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds=\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle+\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

As a characteristic of the orthogonal projections we can additionally say that
\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}
, so with (
17
) we pass further to the bound

\displaystyle\lVert u^{n}_{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\leq\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}1+\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

We shall now look to take expectation, introducing the notation

\hat{u}^{n}_{\cdot}:=u^{n}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}

(52)

where we appreciate that

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\leq M+\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\leq M+\lVert u_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N}))}.

(53)

This boundedness ensures the integrability of all terms. From here we can take the absolute value of the stochastic integral followed by the supremum over
r\in[0,T]
(we take the supremum for each term on the left hand side individually, then sum them), then apply the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+c

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\hat{u}^{n}_{s},\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
c
continues to represent a generic constant now dependent on
T
and the constant from the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality. We recall again that
\nu
is assumed to be less than
1
. Focusing further on the stochastic term, we use (
18
) to see

\displaystyle c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\hat{u}^{n}_{s},\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}1+\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+c\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

(54)

having applied Young’s Inequality. Substituting this into our running inquality achieves

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\leq 2\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+c+c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

(55)

and in particular

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq 4\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+c+c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

to which an application of the standard Grönwall Inequality yields

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+1\right].

(56)

Of course from (
55
) we also see that

\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\leq\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+c+c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

to which we substitute in (
56
) to the right hand side, then summing the resultant inequality with (
56
) gives

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+1\right]

which can simply be rewritten as (
51
), concluding the proof.
∎

The expectation in (
50
) is thus finite, so taking the limit

achieves that

\mathbbm{P}\left(\{\omega\in\Omega:\Theta^{n}(\omega)\leq T\}\right)=0
. It is further evident from our calculations that for any
\tau
such that
(u^{n},\tau)
is a local strong solution of the equation (

) holds (that is, with
\tau
replacing

) where

is independent of the choice of
\tau
. Therefore we can choose a
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
increasing sequence of stopping times which approach

by definition of the maximal time, and applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem just as we did in the proof yields that

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\right]\leq C\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)+1\right].

(57)

Moreover for any given

and
\omega\in\Omega
, we can choose a
\tau(\omega)>t
such that
u^{n}
does indeed satisfy the identity (
48

. Of course we can bound

and

which is finite independent of

and can be substituted in to (

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}(\omega)\leq T\right\}\right)=0.

(58)

3.3
Tightness

We now look to deduce the existence of a process taken as the limit of

in some sense, which is done through a tightness argument. We pursue this with Lemma
4.1
in the Appendix precisely as in [

\mathcal{H}_{1}:=W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=L^{2}_{\sigma}
. Having already demonstrated (
78

79

T>0
and introducing new notation

u^{n,M}_{\cdot}:=u^{n}_{\cdot\wedge\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}.

(59)

Observe that for any
\varepsilon,\delta>0
and removing the explicit reference to
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n}_{s+\delta}-u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n}_{s+\delta}-u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left[\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}\cup\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n}_{s+\delta}-u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)+\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)\right]

, so indeed

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n}_{s+\delta}-u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)+\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\right]+\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\right]

owing to (
58
). The required condition (
79
) is thus shown with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\lVert u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds=0.

(60)

Therefore the sequence of the laws of
(u^{n})
is tight in the space of probability measures over
L^{2}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
.

Proof.

We shall again use the notation
\hat{u}^{n}
established in (
52
), noting that the dependence on
M
is implicit. Following this our generic constant
c
may also depend on
M
in this proof as this remains fixed for the duration. Similarly to the proof of Proposition
3.1
, observe that for any
s\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}=u^{n}_{0}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\hat{u}^{n}_{r}}\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\ dr-\nu\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}A\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\,dr

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}\hat{u}^{n}_{r}dr-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\hat{u}^{n}_{r}d\mathcal{W}_{r}

where an indicator function has had to be included in the stochastic integral as it may not be the case that
\mathcal{G}(0)=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle u^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-u^{n,M}_{s}=

\tilde{w}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
. Then

\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}\left(\left\{\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}:\tilde{u}_{t}(\omega)=\tilde{w}_{t}(\omega)\quad\forall t\in[0,T]\right\}\right)=1.

Proof.

We make our argument by considering the expectation of the difference of the solutions
\tilde{u},\tilde{w}
, and to do so we need to manufacture an increased regularity through stopping times once more. To this end let’s define the stopping times
(\alpha_{R})
by

\alpha_{R}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\sup_{s\in[0,r]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\lVert\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\geq R\right\}\wedge\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\sup_{s\in[0,r]}\lVert\tilde{w}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\lVert\tilde{w}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\geq R\right\}

and subsequent processes

\tilde{u}^{R}_{\cdot}:=\tilde{u}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\alpha_{R}},\qquad\tilde{w}^{R}_{\cdot}:=\tilde{w}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\alpha_{R}},\qquad\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}=\tilde{u}^{R}-\tilde{w}^{R}.

Moreover the difference process satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{t\wedge\alpha_{R}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\ ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}A\bm{\Psi}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{s}ds-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\alpha_{R}}\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\right)d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

and we can apply the Energy Equality of Proposition
4.5
to see that

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{u}_{t\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{t\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle A\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}.

Motivated by the use of Lemma
4.4
, we consider arbitrary stopping times
0\leq\theta_{j}\leq\theta_{k}\leq T
and substitute
\theta_{j}
into the above, then subtract this from the identity for any
\theta_{j}\leq r\leq T
, to give that

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\tilde{u}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}-2\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\langle A\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds+\nu\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}.

We break this down term by term, starting with the nonlinear term which is by definition

\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle.

Using (
12
) and then (
72
),

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\Psi}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}+\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}}\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\Psi}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert_{1}\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\nu\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}

(73)

where
c
now depends on
\nu
, which is not meaningful here. In addition observe that

\langle A\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}=\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}

(74)

and we combine the next two integrals as

\displaystyle\nu\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle_{(W^{1,2}_{\sigma})^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{\sigma}}+\lVert\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\nu\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq\alpha_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{Q}_{i}\bm{\Psi}_{s},\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{*}\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle+\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

using the definition of the duality pairing and that
\mathcal{P}
is an orthogonal projection in
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We apply (
23
) to bound this again by

c\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\nu\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds

(75)

where the constant
c
now also depends on
R
having used that
\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2},\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq R
(which we remark is only true as
\tilde{u},\tilde{w}
are pathwise continuous in
L^{2}_{\sigma}
). Combining (
73
), (
74
) and (
75
) we see that

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{u}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}+c\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

\displaystyle-2\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\langle\mathcal{G}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{G}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}.

We now take the absolute value on the right hand side, followed by the supremum over
r\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{k}]
, then the expectation and immediately apply the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality to achieve that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{r\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{k}]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We now use (
21
) and follow the same process as (
54
) to obtain that

\displaystyle c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}-\mathcal{G}_{i}\tilde{w}^{R}_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{r\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{k}]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds.

We now use that
\lVert\bm{\Psi}_{r}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}
and rearrange to give

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{r\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{k}]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{\theta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\left(1+\lVert\tilde{u}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{w}^{R}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)\lVert\tilde{u}_{s\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{s\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}ds.

We can now apply Lemma
4.4
to deduce that

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)=0

as of course
\tilde{u}_{0}=\tilde{w}_{0}

\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}-a.s.
. We note that
\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}-\tilde{w}_{r\wedge\alpha_{R}}\rVert^{2}\right)
is a monotone increasing sequence in
R
, hence we take the limit as
R\rightarrow\infty
and apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem to obtain

\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r}-\tilde{w}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)=0

which gives the result.
∎

It is now immediate that Theorem
1.10
holds in this case of the bounded initial condition.

Corollary 3.10.1
.

There exists a unique weak solution
u
of the equation (
13
) with the property that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
.

Proof.

This follows from a classical Yamada-Watanabe type result, proven rigorously in this setting in [

].
∎

To prove Theorem
1.10
it thus only remains to extend the result to an arbitrary
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable
u_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow L^{2}_{\sigma}
, which we now fix.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
:

We first show the existence of such a solution. The idea is as in [
37
] Theorem 3.40 where we use the fact that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
there exists a weak solution
u^{k}
of the equation (
13
) for the initial condition
u_{0}\mathbbm{1}_{k\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert<k+1}
. We argue that the process
u
defined by

u_{t}(\omega):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}u^{k}_{t}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{k\leq\lVert u_{0}(\omega)\rVert<k+1}

is a weak solution. Appreciating that the infinite sum is merely formal and that for each
\omega

u(\omega):=u^{k}(\omega)
for some
k
, then clearly
u
inherits the pathwise regularity of the weak solutions
(u^{k})
. As for the identity (
24
), we introduce the more compact notation

A_{k}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:k\leq\lVert u_{0}(\omega)\rVert<k+1\right\}

and as the
(A_{k})
partition
\Omega
, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle u_{t},\phi\rangle=\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s},\phi\rangle ds-\nu\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle u_{s},\phi\rangle_{1}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{Q}_{i}u_{s},\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{*}\phi\rangle ds-\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}

or equivalently

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle u^{k}_{t},\phi\rangle=\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle u_{0}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u^{k}_{s}}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle ds-\nu\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle_{1}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{Q}_{i}u^{k}_{s},\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{*}\phi\rangle ds-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

We are a little more precise for the stochastic integral as we cannot simply take any random function through the integral, however
A_{k}
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable so it is justified here (see e.g. [
35
] Proposition 1.6.14) hence

\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle\mathcal{G}u_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\langle\mathcal{G}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}=\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{G}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

This identity is granted from
u^{k}
being a weak solution for the initial condition
u_{0}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}
. To conclude the existence we only need to verify the progressive measurability, for which we understand
u
as the pointwise almost everywhere limit of the sequence
\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{k\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert<k+1}\right)
over the product space
\Omega\times[0,t]
equipped with the product sigma algebra
\mathcal{F}_{t}\times\mathcal{B}([0,t])
in
W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. Each
u^{k}
is progressively measurable hence so too is
u^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{k\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert<k+1}
, thus measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}\times\mathcal{B}([0,t])
, and the pointwise almost everywhere limit preserves the measurability which provides the result. This concludes the proof that
u
is a weak solution of (
13
) with the property that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
, and one can show it is the unique such solution identically to Proposition
3.10
.
∎

3.6
Energy Estimates for the Constructed Solution

We prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
:

Of course the existence of a martingale weak solution comes from Theorem
1.9
, so it is only the estimates (
32
) and (

) which must be justified and can be done so for the
\tilde{u}
constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.9
. We start with (
32
): recall in Proposition
3.7
how the regularity of the limit process was obtained from the uniform bounds (
57
). In the same manner it is sufficient to show that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq\left(1+o_{\nu}\right)\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+o_{\nu}

(76)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
u^{n}
is the strong solution of

u^{n}_{t}=u^{n}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u^{n}_{s}}u^{n}_{s}\ ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}Au^{n}_{s}\,ds+\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s}ds-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

in analogy with (
48
), and
o_{\nu}
is independent of
n
. Identically to Proposition
3.1
but simply ignoring the contribution from the Stokes Operator in the inequality and using that the initial condition is deterministic, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+c\nu+c\nu\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds+c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\hat{u}^{n}_{s},\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The final term is controlled similarly again, just a little more precisely:

\displaystyle c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\hat{u}^{n}_{s},\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}+c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)+c\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\hat{u}^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds

so that

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+c\nu+c\left(\nu+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

and furthermore through dividing by
\left(1-\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
and rewriting with the
o_{\nu}
notation,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq\left(1+o_{\nu}\right)\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+o_{\nu}+o_{\nu}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

having rewritten
\frac{1}{1-\nu^{1/2}}
as
1+\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{1-\nu^{1/2}}
. We now apply the standard Grönwall Inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}\right)\leq e^{o_{\nu}}\left[\left(1+o_{\nu}\right)\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+o_{\nu}\right]\leq\left(1+o_{\nu}\right)\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+o_{\nu}

using the convergence
\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}e^{o_{\nu}}=1
, demonstrating (
76
) to conclude the justification of (
32
). Similarly for (
33
) it is sufficient to demonstrate that

\mathbbm{E}\left[\lVert u^{n}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\right]\leq\left(1+o_{\nu}\right)\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+o_{\nu}.

(77)

The proof is near identical to (
76
), though as we do not take the supremum then we take the expectation of the stochastic integral directly so this term is null, and we maintain the term coming from the Stokes Operator. Thus we arrive directly at

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right)+\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds\leq\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}+c\nu+c\nu\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds

from which the remainder of the proof follows as above, noting that

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)+\nu\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{s}\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}dr\right]ds

so we can apply the Grönwall Inequality bounding the entirety of the left hand side as is required.

\mathbbm{F}
to be
\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}
, which is well known to separate points in
\mathcal{Y}^{*}
from a corollary of the Hahn-Banach Theorem which asserts that for every
\phi\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}
there exists a
\psi\in\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}
such that
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}^{*}\times\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}}=\lVert\phi\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}^{*}}
. We also note that condition
(3.3)
in [
43
] is satisfied for
(\mu_{n})
taken to be the sequence of laws of
(\bm{\Psi}^{n})
over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)
, owing to the property (
80
). Indeed as
\mathcal{Y}
is compactly embedded into
\mathcal{H}
then
\mathcal{H}
is compactly embedded into
\mathcal{Y}^{*}
, so one only needs to take a bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}
for this property (3.3). Considering the closed ball of radius
M
in
\mathcal{H}
,
\tilde{B}_{M}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{\Psi}^{n}(\omega)\notin D\left([0,T];\tilde{B}_{M}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{\Psi}^{n}(\omega)\notin C\left([0,T];\tilde{B}_{M}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}>M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}})

from which we see an arbitrarily large choice of
M
will justify (3.3). Therefore by Theorem 3.1 it only remains to show that for every
\psi\in\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}
the sequence of the laws of
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n},\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}^{*}\times\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}}
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}\right)
. By the reflexivity of
\mathcal{Y}
for every
\psi\in\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}
there exists a
y\in\mathcal{Y}
such that
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n},\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}^{*}\times\left(\mathcal{Y}^{*}\right)^{*}}=\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n},y\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}^{*}\times\mathcal{Y}}
and as
\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
, then this is furthermore just
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. The problem is now reduced to showing tightness in
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}\right)
, which by Theorem 1 of [
1
] is satisfied if we can show that for for any sequence of stopping times
(\gamma_{n})
,
\gamma_{n}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,T]
, and constants
(\delta_{n})
,
\delta_{n}\geq 0
and
\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
:

1.

For every
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence of the laws of
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathbb{R}
,

2.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{n})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)=0.

We address each item in turn: as for
1
, we are required to show that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, there exists a compact
K_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\notin K_{\varepsilon}\right\}\right)<\varepsilon.

To this end define
B_{M}
as the closed ball of radius
M
in
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\notin B_{M}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|>M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{E}(\left|\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert y\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}}{M}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\right)

so setting

M:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lVert y\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\right)}

justifies item
1
. As for
2
, note that for each fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{(\gamma_{j}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{\gamma_{j}},y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|

so in particular

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{(\gamma_{j}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{\gamma_{j}},y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right).

As
(\delta_{j})
was an arbitrary sequence of non-negative constants approaching zero, we can generically take
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
2
is implied by (
81
). The proof is complete.

∎

Theorem 4.3

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg Inequality)
.

Let
p,q,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p>q\geq 1
,
m>N(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha}{q}+(1-\alpha)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{m}{N})
. Then there exists a constant
c
(dependent on the given parameters) such that for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\cap W^{m,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\leq c\lVert f\rVert^{\alpha}_{L^{q}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert^{1-\alpha}_{W^{m,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}.

(82)

Proof.

See [
68
] pp.125-126.
∎

Remark
.

In the original paper [
68
], the inequality is stated for only the
m^{\textnormal{th}}
order derivative and with an additional
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{r}}
term on the bounded domain, for any
r>0
. By considering the full
W^{m,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm, one can remove this additional term through interpolation.

Lemma 4.4

(Stochastic Grönwall)
.

Fix
t>0
and suppose that
\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi},\bm{\eta}

c^{\prime},\hat{c},\tilde{c}

C

A Strehl Version of Fourth Franel Sequence
Belbachir Hacène, Otmani Yassine

Abstract
We give a combinatorial identity related to the Franel numbers involving the sum of fourth power of binomial coefficients. Furthermore, investigating in J. Mikić’s proof of the first Strehl Identity, we provide a combinatorial proof of this identity using the double counting argument.



2010 MSC
11B65, 05A19, 05A10.

Introduction

It is well know that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}=2^{n}.

(1)

Also, the sums of power two of binomial coefficients is given by Vandermonde Identity

\beta_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}=\binom{2n}{n},

(2)

it is established that the sequence
\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the following recurrence relation (see
[
2
, Corollary 3.1]
)

n\beta_{n}=2\left(2n-1\right)\beta_{n-1}\text{ where }\beta_{0}=1.

(3)

In 1894, Franel,
[
8
]
, introduced the sequences
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as

f_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{3},

(4)

where he provided that
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
verified the following recurrence
relation

\displaystyle\left(n+1\right)^{2}f_{n+1}=\left(7n^{2}+7n+2\right)f_{n}+8n^{2}f_{n-1}\text{ where }f_{0}=1\text{, }f_{1}=2.

(5)

Later, in 1895, Franel,
[
9
]
, shows that the sequence

\varphi_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{4},

satisfies a three-term recurrence formula as follow

\left(n+1\right)^{3}\varphi_{n+1}=2\left(2n+1\right)\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)\varphi_{n}+4n\left(4n-1\right)\left(4n+1\right)\varphi_{n-1},

(6)

where
\varphi_{0}=1
,
\varphi_{1}=2
(for more details about the recurrence of sums that involving power of binomial coefficient one can see
[
4
]
). In 1905, MacMahon,
[
10
]
, investigated the master Theorem where he found the following identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{3}x^{k}y^{n-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-k}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\left(xy\right)^{k}\left(x+y\right)^{n-2k}.

(7)

Recently, in 1993, through applying the Chu-Vandermonde convolution
[
1
]
, Strehl,
[
12
]
,
obtained the following identity

f_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2k}{n},

(8)

by simplifying
\binom{2k}{n}\binom{n}{k}
on Strehl Identity, we get

f_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2n-2k}{n-k},

(9)

in which Gould,
[
7
]
, derived it using Carlitz formula
[
3
]
. Furthermore, Zhao,
[
13
]
, presents a combinatorial proof of the equivalence between Formula (
7
) (for
x=y=1
) and Identity (
9
), employing free 2-Motzkin paths. Also, J. Mikić et al.
[
11
]
, established a combinatorial proof of Identity (
8
), using double counting argument.

The purpose of this paper is to prove a new extension of Franel number
\varphi_{n}
. Furthermore, we develop the J. Mikić’s argument,
[
11
]
, of the first Strehl Identity to prove Identity (
13
), below combinatorially.

The paper is structured as follow; in Section 2, we present our result, then in Section 3, we provide the combinatorial proof of Identity (
13
), bellow using double counting argument.

2
Main Theorem

Let us star by following lemma.

Lemma 1

(
[
1
,
6
]
)
.

Let
n,m
be positive integers and
x
be complex, we have

\binom{x}{n}\binom{n}{m}=\binom{x}{m}\binom{x-m}{n-m},

(10)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}\binom{m}{n-k}=\binom{x+m}{n},

(11)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\binom{m}{n-k}\binom{x+n-k}{n+m}=\binom{x}{n}\binom{x}{m}.

(12)

Remark 1
.

The Identity (
12
), is exactly the Riordan Identity see
[
6
, (6.45)]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n
be positive integer, the following identity holds true

\varphi_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2k}{n}\binom{2n-k}{n}.

(13)

Proof.

Let
s
be left hand side of (
13
). From (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\binom{2k}{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{k}{i}\binom{k}{n-i},

then

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2n-k}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{k}{i}\binom{k}{n-i},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{n-i}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\binom{n-i}{k-i}\binom{i}{k+i-n}\binom{2n-k}{n},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{n-i}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\binom{n-i}{k-i}\binom{i}{n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n},

set
k-i=j
, we get

\displaystyle s=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{n-i}\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}\binom{n-i}{j}\binom{i}{n-i-j}\binom{2n-i-j}{n},

finally, we apply the Riordan Identity (
12
), where get to result.
∎

Remark 2
.

Follow the same steps of precedent proof we get this general form for all complex
x

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{x}{k}\binom{x}{n-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{n-k}\binom{x+k}{k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n}\binom{n}{k}.

(14)

As a consequence we get for
x=1/2
and
x=-1/2
, the following identities

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n-k}\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)\left(2\left(n-k\right)-1\right)}

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n-k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{\left(2k+1\right)\left(-1\right)^{n-k+1}}{2\left(n-k\right)-1},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n-k}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(-1\right)^{k},

(16)

where (
16
), is Domb numbers
[
5
]
. Furthermore, set
x=-n-1
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{n+k}{k}\binom{2n-k}{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\binom{2n-k}{n}\binom{2\left(n-k\right)}{n}.

(17)

3
Combinatorial Proof

Before staring the combinatorial proof of Identity (
13
), we need to remember some fundamental concepts.

1.

Let
n
be non negative integer,
\left[n\right]
denotes the set
\left\{1,2\ldots,n\right\}
,
\left[0\right]
denotes the empty set
\emptyset
.

2.

Let
A
be finite set, we note by
|A|
the cardinal of
A
.

Now we are already to start our combinatorial proof in two steps. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
Y
be the set define as follow

\displaystyle Y:=\left\{\left(A,B,C\right)|A,B\subset\left[2n\right],C\subset\left[3n\right]\setminus\left[n\right],|A|=|B|=|C|=n,A\subset B\Delta\left[n\right],\right.

\displaystyle\left.B\setminus\left[n\right]\subset C\right\}.

First step. Suppose
k=|B\setminus\left[n\right]|
, it is clear that
0\leq k\leq n
, so we can choose those elements from the set
\left[2n\right]\setminus\left[n\right]
in
\binom{n}{k}
ways. The remain elements
n-k
belong to
B\cap\left[n\right]
, so we can choose them in
\binom{n}{n-k}
ways (see
[
11
]
). Since, the
k
elements of
B\setminus\left[n\right]
must be already in
C
, then the remain elements
n-k
can be choose from remain elements of the set
\left(\left[3n\right]\setminus\left[n\right]\right)\setminus\left(B\setminus\left[n\right]\right)
in
\binom{2n-k}{n-k}
ways. Since,
|B\Delta\left[n\right]|=2k
, then we have
\binom{2k}{n}
ways to choose the elements of the set
A
(see
[
11
]
). We conclude that

|Y|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{2k}{n}\binom{2n-k}{n-k}.

(18)

Second Step. Observe that

\displaystyle Y=\left\{\left(A,B,C\right)|A,B\subset\left[2n\right],C\subset\left[3n\right]\setminus\left[n\right],|A|=|B|=|C|=n,A\setminus\left[n\right]\subset B,\right.

\displaystyle\left.B\setminus\left[n\right]\subset C,A\cap B\cap C\cap\left[n\right]=\emptyset\right\}.

Let us star by counting the number of elements of
A
. Suppose
k=|A\cap\left[n\right]|
. Obviously,
0\leq k\leq n
, we choose those elements from the set
\left[n\right]
in
\binom{n}{k}
ways, the remain
n-k
elements belong to
A\setminus\left[n\right]
and we choose them from the set
\left[2n\right]\setminus\left[n\right]
in
\binom{n}{n-k}
ways. In addition, these elements are in the set
B
, so the remain
k
elements can be choose from
\left[2n\right]\setminus A
in
\binom{n}{k}
ways (for more details see
[
11
]
). Clearly
A\setminus\left[n\right]\subset B\setminus\left[n\right]
, then the
n-k
elements of
A\setminus\left[n\right]
must already be in
C
. The remain elements of
C
can be choose from the set
\left[3n\right]\setminus\left[2n\right]
in
\binom{n}{k}
ways. We conclude that

|Y|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k}.

(19)

From Identity (
18
) and Identity (
19
), the proof of (
13
) is done.

Remark 3
.

Motivated by J. Mikić’s observation about generalization of Identity (
8
), see
[
11
, Remark 1]
, we generalize the set
Y
as follow

\displaystyle Y:=\left\{\left(A,B,C\right)|A,B\subset\left[m\right],C\subset\left[m+n\right]\setminus\left[n\right],|A|=|B|=|C|=n\right.

\displaystyle\left.,A\subset B\Delta\left[n\right],B\setminus\left[n\right]\subset C\right\},

where by double counting argument we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m-n,n)}\binom{m-n}{k}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{2k}{n}\binom{m-k}{n-k}

(20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m-n,n)}\binom{n}{k}\binom{m-n}{n-k}\binom{m-n}{k}\binom{n}{k}.

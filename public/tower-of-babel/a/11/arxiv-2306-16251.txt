Companions to the Andrews-Gordon and Andrews-Bressoud identities, and recent conjectures of Capparelli, Meurman, Primc, and Primc
Matthew C. Russell

Abstract
We find bivariate generating functions for the k = 1 k=1 cases of recently conjectured colored partition identities of Capparelli, Meurman, A. Primc, and M. Primc that are slight variants of the generating functions for the sum sides of the Andrews-Gordon and Andrews-Bressoud identities. As a consequence, we prove sum-to-product identities for these cases, thus proving the conjectures.



1
Introduction.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. A
partition
of
n
is a list of integers
\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k}\right)
such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}=n
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq 1
. We will frequently write this as
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}
.

Our starting point is the following well-known partition theorem of Basil Gordon
[
17
]
, and its analytic form due to George E. Andrews
[
2
]
(see also chapter 7 of Andrews’s text
[
7
]
or chapter 3 of Andrew V. Sills’s text
[
32
]
):

Theorem 1
.

Fix
\ell\geq 1
, and let
0\leq i\leq\ell
. Let
B_{\ell,i}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
satisfying
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell}\geq 2
, and there are at most
i
occurrences of 1 as parts. Let
A_{\ell,i}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts
\not\equiv 0,\pm(i+1)\left(\text{mod }2\ell+3\right)
. Then
A_{\ell,i}(n)=B_{\ell,i}(n)
for all
n
.

Theorem 2
.

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2\ell+2-i},q^{2\ell+3};q^{2\ell+3}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

(1.1)

where
N_{i}=n_{i}+n_{i+1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}
. (For the purposes of our present paper, we will view such functions as formal power series in
q
.) We are using the standard
q
-Pochhammer notation

(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-aq^{j-1}\right)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};q\right)_{n}=\left(a_{1};q\right)_{n}\cdots\left(a_{n};q\right)_{n}
.

The generating function in Theorem
2
was further refined by Andrews
[
4
]
into a bivariate generating function. Let
B_{\ell,i,m}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
with exactly
m
parts satisfying
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell}\geq 2
, and there are at most
i
occurrences of 1 as parts. Then

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}B_{\ell,i,m}(n)x^{m}q^{n}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{x^{N_{1}+N_{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell}}},

(1.2)

Note that these identities all have odd modulus. The even modulus companions were found by David M. Bressoud
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
; these are known as the Andrews-Bressoud identities:

Theorem 3
.

Fix
\ell\geq 1
, and let
0\leq i\leq\ell
. Let
B^{\star}_{\ell,i}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
satisfying
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell}\geq 2
, and, if
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell-1}<2,
then
\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{j+\ell-1}\equiv i\left(\text{mod }2\right)
, and there are at most
i
occurrences of 1 as parts. Let
A^{\star}_{\ell,i}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts
\not\equiv 0,\pm(i+1)\left(\text{mod }2\ell+2\right)
. Then
A^{\star}_{\ell,i}(n)=B^{\star}_{\ell,i}(n)
for all
n
.

Theorem 4
.

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell-1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2\ell+1-i},q^{2\ell+2};q^{2\ell+2}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

(1.3)

Again, this generating function in Theorem
4
can be refined into a bivariate generating function. Let
B^{\star}_{\ell,i,m}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
counted by
B^{\star}_{\ell,i}(n)
with exactly
m
parts. Then

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}B^{\star}_{\ell,i,m}(n)x^{m}q^{n}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{x^{N_{1}+N_{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell-1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}},

(1.4)

For some recent papers on other facets of the Andrews-Gordon and Andrews-Bressoud identities, see
[
1
,
18
,
33
,
36
]
.

of Stefano Capparelli, Arne Meurman, Andrej Primc, and Mirko Primc, in which they conjectured intriguing (colored) partition identities related to standard representations of the affine Lie algebra of type
C_{\ell}^{(1)}
(for

). These (conjectured) identities follow in a long vein of research connected partition identities to the representation theory of affine Lie algebras; see, for instance,
[
25
,
13
,
27
,
28
,
20
,
21
,

]
. Most directly, these conjectures built off of the work of M. Primc and Tomislav Šikić
[

]
.

First, let us consider the conjectures in section 4 of their paper
[
14
]
. Fix
\ell\geq 2
, and construct the following array with
2\ell
rows (and
\ell
copies of
\mathbb{N}
):

\begin{matrix}1&&3&&5&&7&&9&&11&\\
&2&&4&&6&&8&&10&&12\\
1&&3&&5&&7&&9&&11&\\
&2&&4&&6&&8&&10&&12\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&&3&&5&&7&&9&&11&\\
&2&&4&&6&&8&&10&&12\end{matrix}\quad\dots

(1.5)

Parts with the same value (but in different rows) are distinct, and can be thought of as having distinct colors. We will sometimes call these colored partitions.
To the above array we will associate an array of frequencies

\begin{matrix}k_{1}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\\
k_{3}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
k_{2}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&k_{0}&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\end{matrix}\quad\dots,

(1.6)

where each
f_{j}
indicates how many times the corresponding part occurs in the original array. The nonnegative integers
k_{0},\dots,k_{\ell}
provide initial conditions. Note that our ordering of
k_{0},\dots,k_{\ell}
is different than that of the original paper.
We say that an array of frequencies is
\left[k_{0},\dots,k_{\ell}\right]
-admissible if, for all downward paths
\mathcal{Z}
in the frequency array,

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}}m\leq k,

(1.7)

where
k=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}k_{i}
.
Here are two sample downward paths in the case with
\ell=3
:

\begin{matrix}k_{1}&&f_{1}&&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{f_{3}}&&f_{5}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{7}}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&f_{2}&&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{f_{4}}&&f_{6}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{8}}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\\
k_{3}&&f_{1}&&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{f_{3}}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{9}}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{f_{2}}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{10}}&&f_{12}\\
k_{2}&&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{f_{1}}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{9}}&&f_{11}&\\
&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{k_{0}}&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{f_{8}}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\end{matrix}\quad\dots,

(1.8)

Fix
k=1
. For
0\leq i\leq\ell
, let
F(i,j,n)
be the number of
\left[k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\right]=\left[0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0\right]
-admissible colored partitions of
n
with exactly
j
parts, where
k_{i}=1
(and all others are 0). Then, define
P_{i}(z,q)
to be the bivariate generating function

P_{i}(z,q)=\sum_{n,j\geq 0}F(i,j,n)z^{j}q^{n}.

(1.9)

The first goal of this paper will be to prove the following multisum for
P_{i}(z,q):

Theorem 5
.

P_{i}(z,q)=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{z^{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell}}}.

(1.10)

Take a moment to appreciate just how similar the bivariate multisum in Theorem
5
is to the multisum of (
1.2
). The only difference is that the factor
x^{N_{1}+N_{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}
has changed to
z^{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell}}
, which can be rewritten as
z^{N_{1}}
. As an immediate corollary,

P_{i}(1,q)=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2\ell+2-i},q^{2\ell+3};q^{2\ell+3}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

(1.11)

by Theorem
2
.

In

We again form arrays of frequencies, and attach nonnegative integers

. Our definition of an admissible partition is the same as above, and we once again restrict our attention to the case

. See section 5 for specific details.

Again, for

, let

be the number of
\left[k_{0},k_{1}\dots,k_{\ell}\right]=\left[0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0\right]
-admissible colored partitions of

with exactly

. Then, define

(1.14)

We then have the corresponding result here for

:

Theorem 6
.

P^{\star}_{i}(z,q)=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{z^{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell-1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}},

(1.15)

which again is very similar to (

), and which gives us the sum-to-product identity

P^{\star}_{i}(1,q)=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell-1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2\ell+1-i},q^{2\ell+2};q^{2\ell+2}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

by Theorem

.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we produce functional equations for the generating functions for the admissible partitions on arrays with

rows, corresponding to the Andrews-Gordon case. We illustrate our work with an example in the case
\ell=4
. In section 3, we carry out computations to derive relations between multisums based on those on the right side of (

\ell=4

do, in fact, satisfy the same functional equations as the generating functions from section 2, which allows us to prove Theorem

6
. We conclude in section 8 with some avenues for further research.

2
Partitions: Andrews-Gordon case.

Fix
\ell
and take an array with
2\ell
rows to it (with
\ell
copies of
\mathbb{N}
). Define
P_{i}(z,q)
to be the bivariate generating function for
\left[k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\right]=\left[0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0\right]
-admissible partitions, where below
k_{i}=1
and all other
k_{j}=0
:

\begin{matrix}k_{1}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\\
k_{3}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&\cdot&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
k_{2}&&f_{1}&&f_{3}&&f_{5}&&f_{7}&&f_{9}&&f_{11}&\\
&k_{0}&&f_{2}&&f_{4}&&f_{6}&&f_{8}&&f_{10}&&f_{12}\end{matrix}\quad\dots,

(2.1)

Here, for
i
odd,
k_{i}
occurs in the
i^{\text{th}}
row, while for
i
even and
i\geq 2
,
k_{i}
occurs in the
(2\ell+1-i)^{\text{th}}
row. Finally,
k_{0}
always occurs in the
(2\ell)^{\text{th}}
row.

We will now show that these satisfy the following system of functional equations:

\displaystyle P_{i}(z,q)-P_{i+1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}zq^{j}P_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right),\quad\quad 0\leq i\leq\ell-1

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(z,q)-P_{\ell}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}zq^{j}P_{\ell-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)

(2.3)

Throughout the paper, empty sums are defined to equal zero. As an example, the instance of (
2.2
) for
i=0
simply states that
P_{0}(z,q)-P_{1}(zq,q)=0
.

We begin with the case where
i
is odd (and
i\neq\ell
). Suppose that we have an admissible partition counted by
P_{i}(z,q)
, and consider the set of parts
j
that occur in row
i-j+1
for
1\leq j\leq i
. At most one of these parts can occur in this partition. If
j
does occur in the
(i-j+1)^{\text{th}}
row, then that contributes
zq^{j}
to the generating function, and then the remaining parts will be counted by
P_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)
. If none of these parts occur, then deleting those parts from consideration shows that we have a partition that could be counted by
P_{i+1}(zq,q)
. This then produces the functional equation
P_{i}(z,q)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}zq^{j}P_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)+P_{i+1}(zq,q)
.

Second, consider the case where
i
is even (and
i\not\in\{0,\ell\}
). Suppose that we have an admissible partition counted by
P_{i}(z,q)
, and consider the set of parts
j
that occur in row
2\ell-i+j
for
1\leq j\leq i
. At most one of these parts can occur in this partition. If
j
does occur in the
(2\ell-i+j)^{\text{th}}
row, then that contributes
zq^{j}
to the generating function, and then the remaining parts will be counted by
P_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)
. If none of these parts occur, then deleting those parts from consideration shows that we have a partition that could be counted by
P_{i+1}(zq,q)
. Putting everything together produces the functional equation
P_{i}(z,q)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}zq^{j}P_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)+P_{i+1}(zq,q)
.

Next, consider the case
i=\ell
. We follow one of the previous two cases, based on whether
\ell
is odd or even. The only difference is that if none of the highlighted parts
1,\dots,\ell
occur, then deleting those parts from consideration produces a partition that is counted by
P_{\ell}(zq,q)
. This demonstrates
P_{\ell}(z,q)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}zq^{j}P_{\ell-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)+P_{\ell}(zq,q)
.

Finally, consider the case
i=0
. From inspection, it should be clear that
P_{0}(z,q)=P_{1}(zq,q)
. As the sum in (
2.2
) is empty for
i=0
, that finishes off that case.

So our new goal is to prove the following set of identities:

\displaystyle T_{0}(z,q)-T_{1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=0

(4.12)

\displaystyle T_{i}(z,q)-T_{i+1}(zq,q)-T_{i-1}(zq,q)+(1-zq)T_{i}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

(4.13)

\displaystyle T_{\ell}(z,q)-T_{\ell}(zq,q)-T_{\ell-1}(zq,q)+(1-zq)T_{\ell}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0

(4.14)

We now translate these formulas into
S_{\mathbf{v}}
notation. Recalling (
4.1
), we see the identification
T_{i}(z,q)=S_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}(z,q).
So now, the desired identities (
4.12
)–(
4.14
) become

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{t}_{1}}(z,q)-S_{\mathbf{t}_{2}}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=0

(4.15)

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}(z,q)-S_{\mathbf{t}_{i+2}}(zq,q)-S_{\mathbf{t}_{i}}(zq,q)+(1-zq)S_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

(4.16)

\displaystyle S_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}(zq,q)-S_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}(zq,q)+(1-zq)S_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(4.17)

Note that

and
S_{\mathbf{v}}\left(zq^{2},q\right)=S_{\mathbf{v}+\vec{\mathbf{2}}}(z,q)
. This allows us to rewrite these as

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{t}_{1}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{2}}(z,q)

\displaystyle=0

(4.18)

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{i+2}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{i}}(z,q)+(1-zq)S_{\vec{\mathbf{2}}+\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}(z,q)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

(4.19)

\displaystyle S_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}}(z,q)-S_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}(z,q)+(1-zq)S_{\vec{\mathbf{2}}}\left(z,q\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(4.20)

So now Proposition
8
gives us (
4.20
) verbatim. (
4.18
) follows from the fact that
\mathbf{t}_{1}=\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{2}
. Also, we can use Proposition
9
, together with the facts that
\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{t}_{i+1}=\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{i+2}
and

, to obtain (
4.19
). This completes the proof of (
4.2
)–(
4.3
). Finally, checking the initial conditions (

) allows us to complete our proof of Theorem
5

\displaystyle P^{\star}_{i}(z,q)-P^{\star}_{i+1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}zq^{j}P^{\star}_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right),\quad\quad 0\leq i\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle P^{\star}_{\ell}(z,q)-P^{\star}_{\ell-1}(zq,q)

. Suppose that we have an admissible partition counted by

j

i-j+1

j

i=\ell

1,\dots,\ell

. This demonstrates

.

Finally, consider the case
i=0
. From inspection, it should be clear that
P^{\star}_{0}(z,q)=P^{\star}_{1}(zq,q)
. As the sum in (
5.3
) is empty for
i=0
, that case is completed.

Completing the proof: Andrews-Bressoud case

Define (for

We want to show that these satisfy the following system of functional equations, corresponding to (

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{i}(z,q)-T^{\star}_{i+1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}zq^{j}T^{\star}_{i-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right),\quad\quad 0\leq i\leq\ell-1

(7.2)

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{\ell}(z,q)-T^{\star}_{\ell-1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}zq^{j}T^{\star}_{\ell-j+1}\left(zq^{j+1},q\right)

These are very nearly the same equations as in the Andrews-Gordon case; the only difference is that the left side of the second equation went from

to

)–(

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{0}(z,q)-T^{\star}_{1}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=0

(7.4)

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{i}(z,q)-T^{\star}_{i+1}(zq,q)-T^{\star}_{i-1}(zq,q)+(1-zq)T^{\star}_{i}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

(7.5)

but now

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{\ell}(z,q)-2T^{\star}_{\ell-1}(zq,q)+(1-zq)T^{\star}_{\ell}\left(zq^{2},q\right)=0

7.1

\displaystyle S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{1}}(z,q)-S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{2}}(zq,q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}(z,q)-S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{i+2}}(zq,q)-S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{i}}(zq,q)+(1-zq)S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{i+1}}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle S^{\star}_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}(z,q)-2S^{\star}_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}(zq,q)+(1-zq)S^{\star}_{\vec{\mathbf{0}}}\left(zq^{2},q\right)

\displaystyle S^{\star}_{\mathbf{t}_{1}}(z,q)-S^{\star}_{\vec{\mathbf{1}}+\mathbf{t}_{2}}(z,q)

8
Conclusion

We conclude by listing some questions suggested by the present research. We focus on the case with an even number of rows (Theorem
5
) corresponding to the Andrews-Gordon identities, but many of the comments below would equally apply to the other case.

•

As we have seen, taking the usual bivariate Andrews-Gordon and Andrews-Bressoud multisums, which have elegant combinatorial interpretations, and altering the
x^{N_{1}+\cdots+N_{k}}
factor to
z^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}}
produces new bivariate multisums that still have elegant combinatorial interpretations. Can the same be done for other families of multisum identities? One natural candidate would be the Göllnitz-Gordon-Andrews identities
[
3
,
5
]
:

Theorem 15
.

Fix
\ell\geq 1
, and let
0\leq i\leq\ell
. Let
G_{\ell,i,m}(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
with exactly
m
parts and no repeated odd parts satisfying
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell}\geq 2
if
\lambda_{j}
is odd or
\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+\ell}>2
if
\lambda_{j}
is even, and there are at most
i
parts are
\leq 2
. Then,

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}G_{\ell,i,m}(n)x^{m}q^{n}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{x^{N_{1}+\cdots+N_{\ell}}\left(-q,q^{2}\right)_{N_{1}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+2\left(N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}\right)}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{2}}\cdots\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}}

(8.1)

and, if
x=1
in the above equation, we have the infinite product

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{\left(-q,q^{2}\right)_{N_{1}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+2\left(N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}\right)}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{2}}\cdots\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}}

(8.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{4}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2i+1},q^{4\ell-2i+3},q^{4\ell+4};q^{4\ell+4}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

(8.3)

So, are there some combinatorial objects (that feel similar to the colored partitions of this paper) with bivariate generating function

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{z^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}}\left(-q,q^{2}\right)_{N_{1}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+2\left(N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}^{2}+N_{i+1}+N_{i+2}+\cdots+N_{\ell}\right)}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{1}}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{2}}\cdots\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n_{\ell}}}?

(8.4)

•

Can this be extended to obtain a multisum for cases
k>1
? To be more specific: we have a bivariate generating function (Theorem
5
) in the case
k=1
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. But, as observed in
[
14
]
, the case
\ell=1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
also ends up literally as Theorem
2

). Can these ideas be combined appropriately to give an answer for general
k,\ell
?

•

Is there a bijection between the admissible partitions of this paper and the partitions counted by
B_{\ell,i}(n)
in Theorem
1
?

•

There exist many refinements and modifications of the Andrews-Gordon identities. For example, the parity of parts has been investigated
[
8
,
23
,
24
,
22
]
. A typical case to study here studies partitions counted by
B_{\ell,i}(n)
in Theorem

that further have the requirement that even parts must appear an even number of times. Once again, there is a bivariate multisum for these partitions with a factor of

•

Another combinatorial interpretation of the sum on the right side of (

) involves Durfee squares and rectangles

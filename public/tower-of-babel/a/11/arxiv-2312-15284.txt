Global attraction to solitons for 2D Maxwell–Lorentz


Abstract
We consider the 2D Maxwell–Lorentz system which describes a rotating particle in electromagnetic field. The system admits stationary soliton-type solutions. We prove the attraction to solitons for any finite energy solution relying on the conservation of angular momentum.

Introduction

The 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations reads
[
6
,
15
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{E}(x,t)&=&J\nabla B(x,t)-[\dot{q}(t)-\omega(t)J(x-q(t))]\rho(x-q(t))\\
\dot{B}(x,t)&=&-\nabla\cdot(JE(x,t)),\qquad\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x-q(t))\\
m\ddot{q}(t)&=&\langle E(x,t)+B(x,t)[J\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)(x-q(t))],\rho(x\!-\!q(t))\rangle\\
I\dot{\omega}(t)&=&\langle(x\!-\!q(t))\cdot\big[JE(x,t)\!-B(x,t)\dot{q}(t)],\rho(x\!-\!q(t))\rangle\end{array}\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.1)

Here
(E(x,t),B(x,t))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the Maxwell field,
\rho(x-q(t))\in\mathbb{R}
is the charge distribution of
an extended particle centered at a point
q(t)\in R^{2}
,
m
is the mass of the particle,
I
is its moment of inertia,
and
\omega(t)
is the angular velocity of the particle rotation.
The brackets
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denote the inner product in the Hilbert space
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(1.2)

We restrict ourselves to the situation, where the spinning particle is located at the origin, i.e.
q(t)\equiv 0
.
This can be achieved by assuming the (anti-) symmetry conditions

E(-x)=-E(x)
,
B(-x)=B(x)
for the initial fields. Then this property persists for all times.
In this case the Maxwell–Lorentz equations (
1.1
) simplify to the linear system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{E}(x,t)&=&J\nabla B(x,t)+\omega(t)Jx\rho(x),\\
\dot{B}(x,t)&=&-\nabla\cdot JE(x,t),\qquad\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x)\\
I\dot{\omega}(t)&=&\langle x\cdot JE(x,t),\rho(x)\rangle\end{array}\right|.

(1.3)

We assume that real-valued charge density
\rho(x)
is a smooth rapidly decreasing spherically symmetric function, i.e.,

\rho\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\rho(x)=\rho_{1}(|x|).

(1.4)

Moreover, we impose that the total charge vanishes (neutrality of the particle):

\int\rho(x)\,dx=0.

(1.5)

The system admits stationary solutions (solitons)
(E_{\omega}(x),B_{\omega}(x),\omega)

rotating with constant angular velocity
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.
The condition
\hat{\rho}(0)=0
provides that the solitons belong to the finite energy phase space (
2.9
).

One main result is the long-time convergence in weighed energy norms of any finite energy solution with the angular momentum
M

to the soliton with the same angular momentum.

Our proof relies on the regularity of the resolvent
({\bf H}-\lambda)^{-1}
at the threshold
\lambda=0
, where

{\bf H}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&1\\
\Delta-\frac{1}{I}|J\varrho\rangle\langle J\varrho|&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\varrho(x)=x\rho(x)

(1.6)

is the operator defined on the space (
2.9
).
We calculate the Puiseux expansion of the resolvent up to
\lambda^{2}
by suitable development of the
Agmon–Jensen–Kato theory
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
12
]
.
The condition (
1.5
) provides that the resolvent belongs to
C^{2}
at a vicinity of
\lambda=0
.
This fact implies the decay
|t|^{-2}
of the solution to (
4.4
) in weighted energy norms (
3.3
) for sufficiently large moment of inertia
I
.
This decay rate allows us to obtain scattering asymptotics (
3.6
) in the global energy norm (without a weight).

Note that the decay
|t|^{-2}
in the weighted energy norms (
3.3
) does not contradict the well known slow local decay
|t|^{-1}
of
solutions of the 2D wave equation.
Indeed, the slow decay
|t|^{-1}
corresponds to the singularity
of the resolvent
(\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\sim\log\lambda
at the threshold
\lambda=0
.
The corresponding
resonances
are the constant functions.
However, the decay in the weighted norms (
3.3
)
is better since the norm includes spatial derivatives of the
solution (which vanish on the resonances), but does not include the values of the solution.

The global attraction to stationary states was established in

In

The global convergence to solitons of such system without external potential was proved in

The results
[

These results were obtained under the Wiener-type condition on the charge density
\rho(x)

The global convergence to rotating solitons was established in
[
3

for solutions to the 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system with
q(t)\equiv 0

in the case of sufficiently small charge density
\rho
.
The global attraction to the set of all solitons for this system
was established in
[
12
]
under a considerably weaker Wiener-type condition than in
[
2
]
.
The survey of the results for 3D system can be found in
[

8

.

In
[
6

, we have proved the orbital stability of moving and rotating solitons for the 2D Maxwell–Lorentz system (
1.1
).
The proof relies on the reduction of the system by the canonical transformation to a comoving frame
and the conservation of the corresponding linear and angular momenta.

2
The Maxwell potentials

In this section we recall the Hamiltonian
structure for the system (
1.3
) expressed in the Maxwell potentials
A(x,t)=(A_{1}(x,t),A_{2}(x,t))
and
\Phi(x,t)
:

B(x,t)=\nabla\cdot(JA)=\nabla_{1}A_{2}(x,t)-\nabla_{2}A_{1}(x,t),\qquad E(x,t)=-\dot{A}(x,t)-\nabla\Phi(x,t).

(2.1)

We choose the Coulomb gauge

\nabla\cdot A(x,t)=0.

(2.2)

Then the first two equations of (
1.3
) are equivalent to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\ddot{A}(x,t)-\nabla\dot{\Phi}(x,t)&=&-\Delta A(x,t)+\omega(t)Jx\rho(x),\\
-\Delta\Phi(x,t)&=&\rho(x)\end{array}\right|.

(2.3)

The second equation of (
2.3
) can be solved explicitly:

\Phi(x,t)=\Phi(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\log|x-y|\rho(y)dy.

(2.4)

In the Fourier transform
\hat{\Phi}_{0}(k)=\hat{\rho}(k)/k^{2}
.
Hence,
\Phi(x)\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
due to (
1.5
) and spherical symmetry (
1.4
).
Now the last equation of (
1.3
) becomes

I\dot{\omega}(t)=-\langle J(\dot{A}(x,t)+\nabla\Phi(x),\varrho(x)\rangle=-\langle J\dot{A}(x,t),\varrho(x)\rangle,\quad\varrho(x):=x\rho(x)

(2.5)

since the spherical symmetry (
1.4
) implies

\langle J\nabla\Phi(x),\varrho(x)\rangle=\langle x\cdot J\nabla\Phi(x),\rho(x)\rangle=-\langle\nabla\cdot Jk\hat{\Phi}(k),\hat{\rho}(k)\rangle=-\int(k_{2}\nabla_{1}-k_{1}\nabla_{2})\frac{\hat{\rho}^{2}(k)}{k^{2}}dk=0.

The angular momentum is defined as in
[
6
]
:

M=I\omega-\langle A,J\varrho\rangle.

(2.6)

By (
2.5
) and (
2.1
),

\dot{M}(t)=I\dot{\omega}(t)-\langle\dot{A}(x,t),J\varrho(x)\rangle\\
=-\langle J\dot{A},\varrho(x)\rangle-\langle\dot{A},J\varrho(x)\rangle=0.

Thus, the angular momentum is conserved:

M(t)=M
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,
the system (
1.3
) can be rewritten as the linear inhomogeneous system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\dot{A}(x,t)=\Pi(x,t)\\
\dot{\Pi}(x,t)=\Delta A(x,t)-\omega(t)J\varrho(x)\end{array}\right|,

(2.7)

where

\omega(t):=\frac{1}{I}\big(M+\langle A(t),J\varrho\rangle\big).

(2.8)

Let us introduce a phase space for the system (
2.7
). Denote by

\dot{H}^{1}
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to the norm

\|A\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\|\nabla A\|_{L^{2}}
, where
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}

Define the energy phase space

{\cal E}=\dot{H}^{1}\oplus L^{2}.

(2.9)

Obviously, (
2.7
) is the canonical Hamilton system with the Hamilton functional

H(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\int\big(\Pi^{2}+|\nabla A|^{2}\big)dx+\frac{I\omega^{2}}{2},\quad Y:=(A,\Pi)\in{\cal E},

(2.10)

where
\frac{I\omega^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2I}(M+\langle A(x),J\varrho(x)\rangle)^{2}
in accordance with (
2.8
).
The Hamiltonian (
2.10
) is well defined and Fréchet differentiable on the Hilbert phase space (
2.9
).
The well-posedness for the system (
2.7
) is proved in

[

]

Here we prove the decay (

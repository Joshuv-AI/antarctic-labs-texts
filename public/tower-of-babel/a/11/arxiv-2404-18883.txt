A Thom Isotopy Theorem for nonproper semialgebraic maps
Luis Renato Gonçalves Dias, Giovanny Snaider Barrera Ramos

Abstract
. We prove a version of the Thom Isotopy Theorem for nonproper semialgebraic maps f : X → ℝ m f\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m} , where X ⊂ ℝ n X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} is a semialgebraic set and f f is the restriction to X X of a smooth semialgebraic map F : ℝ n → ℝ m F\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m} .

58K30, 14B05

1.
Introduction

Let
F\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic map. We say that
F
is locally trivial at
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
if there exist a neighborhood
U
of
t_{0}
and a diffeomorphism
h\colon F^{-1}(t_{0})\times U\rightarrow F^{-1}(U)
such that
F\circ h=pr_{2}
, where
pr_{2}\colon F^{-1}(t_{0})\times U\rightarrow U
denotes the second projection. The set of the points at which the local triviality of
F
fails is called the bifurcation set of
F
and is denoted by
B(F)
. It is well known that
B(F)
is the union of the singular values set of
F
, denoted by
F({\rm Sing}F)
, and the set of bifurcation values at infinity of
F
.

A full characterisation of
B(F)
is a challenging open problem. In order to estimate
B(F)
, we can use conditions that control the asymptotic behaviour of the fibres of
F
. Rabier
[
11
]
considered the asymptotic critical values set
K_{\infty}(F)
defined by

(1)

\displaystyle K_{\infty}(F):=

\displaystyle\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mid\exists(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{j}\|}=\infty

\displaystyle{\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}F(x_{j})}=t\text{ and }{\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{j}\|\nu(d_{x_{j}}F)}=0\},

where
\nu(d_{x}F):=\inf_{\|\varphi\|=1}\|d_{x}F^{*}(\varphi)\|
and
d_{x}F^{*}
denotes the adjoint of
d_{x}F
. From
[
11
]
, see also
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, it follows that
B(F)\subset F({\rm Sing}F)\cup K_{\infty}(F)
. In fact, Rabier’s result applies to
C^{2}
maps
g\colon M\rightarrow N
, where
M
and
N
are Finsler Manifolds.

In
[
1
]
, the authors considered the following set:

(2)

\mathcal{S}(F)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mid\exists(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset M(F),\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{j}\|=\infty\text{ and }\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}F(x_{j})=y\},

where
M(F)
is the critical locus of the map
(F,\rho):{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
, with
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n}^{2}
. From
[
1
]
, it follows that
\mathcal{S}(F)\subset K_{\infty}(F)
and
B(F)\subset F({\rm Sing}F)\cup\mathcal{S}(F)
. We call
F({\rm Sing}F)\cup\mathcal{S}(F)
the
\rho
non-regular values set of
F
.

We remark that the results presented above hold when we consider the restriction of
F
to a smooth semialgebraic set
X
.

If
X
is a singular set and
\mathcal{W}
is a Whitney stratification of
X
, the Thom Isotopy Theorem (see for instance
[
4
]
) ensures that every smooth map
f\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is locally trivial on any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
, provided that the restriction of
f
to any stratum of
\mathcal{W}
is a submersion and the restriction of
f
to the closure of any stratum is a proper map.

Recently, in the complex case, Ðinh and Jelonek
[
2
]
considered the singular case as follows. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an affine variety and let
F\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be a polynomial map. Let
f\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be the restriction of
F
to
X
and let
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
be a Whitney stratification of
X
. Motivated by (
1
), the authors defined the stratified generalized critical values set of
f
, denoted by
\mathcal{K}(f,\mathcal{W})
, and proved that
B(f)\subset\mathcal{K}(f,\mathcal{W})
. In the real case, Anna Valette
[
12
]
considered stratified submersion
f\colon(X,\Sigma)\rightarrow Y
, where
X
and
Y
are semialgebraic sets, and proved that
B(f)\subset K_{\Sigma}(f)=\bigcup_{S\in\Sigma}K_{\infty}(f_{|_{S}})
. The trivialization obtained in
[
12
]
is a semialgebraic trivialization. In particular, the results of
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
can be seen as a extension of the Thom Isotopy Theorem for nonproper maps.

In this paper, we consider a semialgebraic set
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a Verdier
(w)
stratification
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
of
X
, see Section
2
for details. Given a smooth semialgebraic map
F\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, we consider
f\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
the restriction of
F
to
X
. Motivated by (
2
), we define the stratified
\rho
non-regular values of
f
, denoted by
\mathfrak{S}(f,\mathcal{W})
, see Definition
3.1
. Then, we prove that
B(f)\subset\mathfrak{S}(f,\mathcal{W})
, see Theorem
3.3
. We also prove a trivialization theorem for
f
outside a compact set in Theorem
3.4
.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
is motivated by the arguments presented in
[
2
]
. We have constructed
m
rugose vector fields on
X
. These are defined on the complement of a sphere of sufficiently large radius
R
. They are tangent to
(n-1)
-dimensional spheres, and by integrating the
m
vector fields we obtain the diffeomorphism trivializing
f
outside a compact set. In the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we defined
m
rugose vector fields on the ball of radius
R
. We glue these
2m
rugose fields to obtain
m
rugose vector fields on
X
which trivialize
f
.

In Section
2
we introduce the Verdier stratification, rugose vector fields and results for developing our objective. In section
3
, we proof our main results, namely Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
.

We point out that it follows from
[
1
]
that
\mathfrak{S}(f,\mathcal{W})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(f,\mathcal{W})
. So, our result refines the results of
[
2
,
12
]
.

2.
Stratification and rugose vector fields

2.1.
Stratification

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A stratification of
X
is a collection
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
of pairwise disjoint connected smooth submanifolds of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying the following conditions

i)

X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}X_{\alpha}
.

ii)

\mathcal{W}
is locally finite.

iii)

the frontier condition:
X_{\beta}\cap\overline{X}_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
implies
X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
.

Each
{X}_{\alpha}
is called a stratum of
\mathcal{W}
. If, in addition
X
,
\overline{X}_{\alpha}
and
\overline{X}_{\alpha}\setminus X_{\alpha}
are semialgebraic sets, the stratification
\mathcal{W}
is called an affine stratification of
X
.

2.2.
Whitney stratification

Let
X
be a closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
a stratification of
X
. We start by recalling the conditions of Whitney, see
[
14
, Sections 18-19]
or
[
4
, Chapter 1]
.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We denote by
\overline{xy}
the line passing through the origin and parallel to the line formed by the points
x
and
y
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}
be strata of
\mathcal{W}
such that
X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
and let
y\in X_{\beta}
. The pair
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Whitney
(b)
regular at

, provided that for any sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{\alpha}
such that
{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}x_{n}=y
and
{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}T_{x_{n}}X_{\alpha}=T
, and for any sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X_{\beta}
such that
{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}y_{n}=y
and
{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\overline{x_{n}y_{n}}=l
, we have
l\subset T
.

The pair
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Whitney
(b)
regular if
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Whitney
(b)
regular at any

. The stratification
\mathcal{W}

(b)
stratification if
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Whitney
(b)
regular for any strata
X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}
of

X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be vector subspaces of

\delta(V_{1},V_{2})={\sup_{{a\in V_{1}};\text{ }{\|a\|=1}}}\|a-\pi_{V_{2}}(a)\|,

Definition 2.4
.

Let

\mathcal{W}

X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}

. The pair
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and any

(3)

\delta(T_{y^{\prime}}X_{\beta},T_{x}X_{\alpha})\leq C\|y^{\prime}-x\|.

The pair
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Verdier
(w)
regular if
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Verdier
(w)
regular at any
y\in X_{\beta}
. The stratification
\mathcal{W}
is a Verdier
(w)
stratification if
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
is Verdier
(w)
regular for any strata
X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}
of
\mathcal{W}
such that
X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
.

We have that any semialgebraic set

admits a Whitney
(b)
stratification whose strata are semialgebraic sets, see

[
9
]

[

Let
X

Definition 2.5
.

Let

2.3.
Rugose vector fields

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an affine variety and let
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
be an affine stratification of
X
. A function
\varphi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is called a rugose function if the following conditions are satisfied

(a)

The restriction
\varphi_{|_{X_{\alpha}}}
to any stratum
X_{\alpha}
is a smooth function.

(b)

For any stratum
X_{\alpha}
, and for any
x\in X_{\alpha}
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a constant
C>0
such that

|\varphi(y)-\varphi(x^{\prime})|\leq C\|y-x^{\prime}\|,

for any
y\in X\cap U
and any
x^{\prime}\in X_{\alpha}\cap U
.

A map
\varphi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is called a rugose map if its components functions are rugose functions. A vector field
v
on
X
is called a rugose vector field if
v
is a rugose map and, for any
x\in X
, the vector
v(x)
is tangent to the stratum containing
x
.

We have the following result, see Proposition 4.3 of
[
13
]
:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set and let
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
be an affine Verdier
(w)
stratification of
X
. Let
X_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}
and let
B\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
a closed union of strata. Let
\eta:B\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a rugose map on
B
. Then there exists a rugose map
\bar{\eta}:(B\cup X_{\alpha})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
on
(B\cup X_{\alpha})
that extends
\eta
, that is
\bar{\eta}|_{B}=\eta
.

\square

Under the above conditions, Verdier
[
13
, Corollary 4.4]
proved that if
\eta
is a rugose vector field on
B
, then it extends to a rugose vector field on
A
.

In the next theorem, we consider
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n}^{2}
. We prove that if
\eta
is a rugose vector field on
B
and is tangent to the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
\|x\|
for any
x\in B
, then
\eta
extends to a rugose vector field on
A
and to the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
\|y\|
for any
y\in A
.

.

3.
Main theorem

3.1.
Stratified Milnor set

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set and let
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
be an affine stratification of
X
, see Definition
2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic map, where
\dim X\geq m
. Let
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be the function given by
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x^{2}_{n}
.

For any stratum
X_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{W}
, we denote by
{\rm Sing}f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}}
the critical locus of
f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}}
and let
f({\rm Sing}f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}})
be the critical values set of
f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}}
. We denote by
M(f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}})
the critical locus of
(f,\rho):X_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
and we define:

(8)

{\mathcal{S}_{\infty}}(f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}})=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mid\exists(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset M(f|_{X_{\alpha}}),x_{j}\rightarrow\infty\text{ and }\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}f(x_{j})=y\},

where

(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset M(f|_{X_{\alpha}})
has no convergent subsequences in
M(f|_{X_{\alpha}})
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
M(f,\mathcal{W}):=\cup_{\alpha\in J}M(f_{|_{X\alpha}})
the stratified Milnor set of
f
and we call

(9)

\mathfrak{S}(f,\mathcal{W}):=\bigcup_{\alpha\in J}(f({\rm Sing}f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}})\cup{\mathcal{S}_{\infty}}(f_{|_{X_{\alpha}}})).

the stratified
\rho
non-regular values set of
f
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set and let
\mathcal{W}=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J}
be an affine stratification. Let
f\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic map. Then, for any
\alpha\in J
, the sets

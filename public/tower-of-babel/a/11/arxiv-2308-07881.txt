Representations of Smith algebras which are free over the Cartan subalgebra
Vyacheslav Futorny, Samuel A. Lopes, Eduardo M. Mendonça

Abstract
In this paper, we study the category of modules over the Smith algebra which are free of finite rank over the unital polynomial subalgebra generated by the Cartan element h h and obtain families of such simple modules of arbitrary rank. In the case of rank one we obtain a full description of the isomorphism classes, a simplicity criterion, and an algorithm to produce all composition series. We show that all such modules have finite length and describe the composition factors and their multiplicity. MSC Numbers (2020) : Primary 16S30, 16S99 Keywords : Smith algebra, simple module, U ⁡ ( h ) U(h) -free module

Introduction

In
[
15
]
, Smith defined a class of algebras similar to the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, essentially by replacing the standard relation
[e,f]=h
in
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
with the relation
[e,f]=g(h)
, where
g
is an arbitrary polynomial in
h
. We will denote these algebras by
\mathcal{S}(g)
. Among other results, Smith classified the finite-dimensional simple
\mathcal{S}(g)
-modules, seen as quotients of Verma modules, and introduced an analog of the Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand category
\mathcal{O}
for
\mathcal{S}(g)
.

The Smith algebras have been extensively studied and are related to down-up algebras, a class of algebras introduced by Benkart and Roby in
[
1
]
, inspired by the relations satisfied by the down and up operators on a differential poset. Down-up algebras also display many similarities with enveloping algebras of three-dimensional Lie algebras, and include those Smith algebras
\mathcal{S}(g)
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,g\leq 1
. Later, in
[
4
]
, Cassidy and Shelton introduced generalized down-up algebras, which include all the Smith algebras
\mathcal{S}(g)
.

As with the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, every Smith algebra has a Casimir element, which generates its center and acts as a scalar on simple
\mathcal{S}(g)
-modules. The corresponding factor rings of
\mathcal{S}(g)
by the maximal ideal of the center have been considered by Joseph
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
, where simplicity criteria were given, and by Hodges
[
7
]
, as algebras of invariants of the Weyl algebra under the action of a cyclic group. Allowing
\mathcal{S}(g)
to be defined over a ring, then some of these quotients can further be seen as invariant rings of differential operators on a multiplicity-free representation of an algebraic group under the action of its derived subgroup
[
14
]
. Another interesting connection is with the Zhu algebra of a vertex operator algebra associated to a positive definite rank-one lattice, which is shown in
[
5
]
to be isomorphic to a finite-dimensional quotient of
\mathcal{S}(g)
.

Our main interest is the representation theory of the Smith algebras
\mathcal{S}(g)
. As we mentioned, the finite-dimensional irreducible representations, the Verma modules and category
\mathcal{O}
have already been investigated in
[
15
]
(see also
[
8
]
). In Block’s classification
[
2
]
of simple
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-modules, along with the weight modules one finds also Whittaker modules and other modules defined via localization, the latter being torsion free over the polynomial algebra in
h
. A class of modules which has recently gained a lot of attention in the context of Lie algebras is given by the modules which are free of finite rank over the enveloping algebra of a Cartan subalgebra. These have been introduced and studied in
[
12
,
13
,
16
]
, and they are in a certain sense opposite to weight modules, as the action of the Cartan subalgebra is torsion free, rather than semisimple. In particular, free rank one simple
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules were classified in
[
12
]
, and in
[
16
]
such modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
were also constructed from modules over Witt algebras
W_{n}
.
Similarly, such simple
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-modules were classified in
[
13
]
. These are the only simple finite-dimensional algebras for which
there exist modules that are free over the enveloping algebra of a Cartan subalgebra. Parabolic induction from simple
U(h)
-free modules was studied in
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the category of
\mathcal{S}(g)
-modules which are free of finite rank over the unital subalgebra generated by
h
and obtain families of such simple modules of arbitrary rank. We dedicate particular attention to the case of rank one, where we obtain a full description of the isomorphism classes, a simplicity criterion, and an algorithm to produce all composition series, resulting in a proof that such modules have finite length and in a full description of the composition factors and their multiplicity.

Notations and conventions.

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero. Since a monic polynomial in
\mathbbm{k}[h]
is fully determined by its set of roots, we
get a bijection between the finite submultisets of
\mathbbm{k}
and monic polynomials in
\mathbbm{k}[h]
. For convenience, we associate
the field
\mathbbm{k}
to the zero polynomial.
Given any
f(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
, we let
\,\mathsf{R}_{f}
denote its multiset of roots. Conversely, given any finite multiset
X
of elements of
\mathbbm{k}
, we let
\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
denote the unique monic polynomial such that
\,\mathsf{R}_{\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}}=X
, that is,
\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}=\prod_{\lambda\in X}(h-\lambda)
. Adopting the usual convention that an empty product equals
1
, we assume
\,\mathsf{poly}_{\emptyset}=1
. Moreover, we follow the convention that
\,\mathsf{deg}\,0=-\infty
, with its usual arithmetic properties.

Given a multiset
X
of elements of
\mathbbm{k}
and
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}
, we denote by
X\setminus\left\{\lambda\right\}
(respectively,
X\cup\left\{\lambda\right\}
) the multiset obtained from
X
by reducing (respectively, increasing) by one the multiplicity of
\lambda
in
X
, and proceed similarly for the difference and union of arbitrary multisets. For example,
\left\{1,2,2,5,5,5\right\}\setminus\left\{3,5\right\}=\left\{1,2,2,5,5\right\}
and
\left\{1,2,2,5,5,5\right\}\cup\left\{3,5\right\}=\left\{1,2,2,3,5,5,5,5\right\}
. The cardinality
|X|
of the (finite) multiset
X
is the sum of the multiplicities of its elements. The underlying set obtained from
X
(by eliminating repeated elements) will be denoted by
\underline{X}
. Thus,
|\left\{1,2,2,5,5,5\right\}|=6
and
\underline{\left\{1,2,2,5,5,5\right\}}=\left\{1,2,5\right\}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, set
[n]=\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
, so in particular
[0]=\emptyset
.

The Smith algebra

Fix a polynomial
g(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
. The
Smith algebra

\mathcal{S}(g)
is the unital associative algebra over
\mathbbm{k}
generated by
x,y,h
with definition relations:

[h,y]=y,\quad[h,x]=-x\quad\text{and}\quad[y,x]=g(h).

(2.1)

This algebra was introduced by Smith in
[
15
]
. In case
g(h)=0
, the generators
x
and
y
commute and the representation theory of
\mathcal{S}(0)
assumes characteristics which often diverge from the general theory in case
g\neq 0
. In fact,
\mathcal{S}(0)
is the enveloping algebra of a 3-dimensional solvable (non-nilpotent) Lie algebra.
Thus, henceforth we will always implicitly assume that
g\neq 0
.

By
[
15
, Lemma 1.4]
, there exists
u(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
such that
g(h)=u(h-1)-u(h)
. Moreover,
u(h)
is uniquely determined up to its constant term, which can be arbitrary, and
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(u)=\,\mathsf{deg}\,(g)+1\geq 1
. Fixing one such
u
, we denote the Smith algebra
\mathcal{S}(g)
by
\mathcal{S}_{u}
and remark that
\mathcal{S}_{u}=\mathcal{S}_{u+C}
, for any
C\in\mathbbm{k}
.

Let
z_{u}=xy-u(h)=yx-u(h-1)
. It is easy to see that
z_{u}
is a central element in
\mathcal{S}_{u}
and for this reason we call
z_{u}
the
Casimir element
associated with
u
. In addition, the center
\mathsf{Z}(\mathcal{S}_{u})
of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
is
\mathbbm{k}[z_{u}]
, the polynomial algebra in
z_{u}
(see
[
15
, Proposition 1.5]
, or
[
11
, Proposition 2.9]
).

Next we show that the algebra
\mathcal{S}_{u}
acts on the polynomial algebra
\mathbbm{k}[t]
by differential operators, with scalar central character. Denote by
A_{1}=\mathbbm{k}[t,\partial]
, where
\partial=\frac{d\ }{dt}
, the first Weyl algebra over
\mathbbm{k}
, realized here as the ring of differential operators on
\mathbbm{k}[t]
with polynomial coefficients. Since
g\neq 0
, then
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(u)=\,\mathsf{deg}\,(g)+1\geq 1
and hence
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\neq\emptyset
for any
C\in\mathbbm{k}
. For
C\in\mathbbm{k}
, every root
\lambda\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and submultiset
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
define the polynomials:

Q_{X}(h)=\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{X}(h)=\dfrac{u(h-1)+C}{Q_{X}(h-1)(h-(\lambda+1))}.

Equivalently,
P_{X}(h)=\xi\,\mathsf{poly}_{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus(\{\lambda\}\cup X)}(h-1)
, where
\xi
is the leading coefficient of
u+C
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a morphism of algebra
\varphi_{C,\lambda,X}\colon S_{u}\rightarrow A_{1}
such that

x\mapsto\partial Q_{X}(\theta-1),\quad y\mapsto tP_{X}(\theta+1)\quad\text{and}\quad h\mapsto\theta,

where
\theta=t\partial+\lambda+1
. Moreover,
z_{u}
is mapped to
C
.

Proof.

Define actions of
x,\,y
and
h
on
\mathbbm{k}[t]
as in the statement above.
Since
\theta t=t(\theta+1)
and
\theta\partial=\partial(\theta-1)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left(tP_{X}(\theta+1)\right)\left(\partial Q_{X}(\theta-1)\right)

\displaystyle=t\partial P_{X}(\theta)Q_{X}(\theta-1)=((\theta-1)-\lambda)P_{X}(\theta)Q_{X}(\theta-1),

\displaystyle\left(\partial Q_{X}(\theta-1)\right)\left(tP_{X}(\theta+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\partial tQ_{X}(\theta)P_{X}(\theta+1)=(\theta-\lambda)P_{X}(\theta+1)Q_{X}(\theta).

Then, from the equality
u(h)+C=(h-\lambda)P_{X}(h+1)Q_{X}(h)
, it follows that
[y,x]
acts on
\mathbbm{k}[t]
as
((\theta-1)-\lambda)P_{X}(\theta)Q_{X}(\theta-1)-(\theta-\lambda)P_{X}(\theta+1)Q_{X}(\theta)=u(\theta-1)+C-u(\theta)-C=g(\theta)
, which is the action of
g(h)
. Similarly, the relations
[h,y]=y
and
[h,x]=-x
are also preserved by the action, thus inducing an
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module structure by differential operators on
\mathbbm{k}[t]
, and hence the given morphism of algebras. It is straightforward to show that
\varphi_{C,\lambda,X}(z_{u})=C
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
g(h)=h
, so that
\mathcal{S}(h)\simeq U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Then we can take
u(h)=-\frac{1}{2}h(h+1)
,
C=0
,
\lambda=0
and
X=\left\{-1\right\}
, so that
Q_{X}(h)=h+1
and
P_{X}(h)=-\frac{1}{2}
. We obtain an action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
\mathbbm{k}[t]
where
x
acts by
\partial(t\partial+1)
,
y
acts by
-\frac{1}{2}t
and
h
acts by
t\partial+1
.

Concretely,

\displaystyle x\cdot t^{k}=k(k+1)t^{k-1},\quad y\cdot t^{k}=-\frac{1}{2}t^{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad h\cdot t^{k}=(k+1)t^{k},\quad\text{for all $k\geq 0$.}

Using the fact that the action of
x
lowers the degree
in
t
, annihilating only the constant polynomials, and the action of
y
raises it, a straightforward argument shows that this is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

As a consequence of the previous lemma, and using exponential modules for the Weyl algebra (compare
[
6
]
for the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
),
we can construct a class of non-weight representations of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

(Exponential modules)
Let
p\in\mathbbm{k}[t]
be a polynomial and consider the
A_{1}
-module
\mathbbm{k}[t]e^{p}
. Given
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
\lambda\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
a submultiset, define
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
to be the
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module induced from the
A_{1}
-module
\mathbbm{k}[t]e^{p}
via the map
\varphi_{C,\lambda,X}
from Lemma
2.2
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p\geq 1
. Then
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
is a
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-free module of rank
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p
. Furthermore, if
there is no
\mu\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
such that
\mu-\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
then
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\})
is simple.

Proof.

Let
n=\,\mathsf{deg}\,p
. We claim that
B=\{e^{p},te^{p},\dotsc,t^{n-1}e^{p}\}
is a
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-basis of
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
.

From the relation
(h-(\lambda+1+s))\cdot t^{s}e^{p}=t^{s+1}p^{\prime}e^{p}
we can show, by induction on
s
, that
t^{s}e^{p}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]B
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
, so we conclude that
B
generates
\mathbbm{k}[t]e^{p}
as a
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module. Now notice that
h\cdot qe^{p}=(t(q^{\prime}+qp^{\prime})+(\lambda+1)q)e^{p}
, for all
q\in\mathbbm{k}[t]
. In particular,
h\cdot qe^{p}=\hat{q}e^{p}
, where
\,\mathsf{deg}\,\hat{q}=\,\mathsf{deg}\,p+\,\mathsf{deg}\,q
. Thus, we can conclude that
r(h)\cdot qe^{p}=\hat{q}e^{p}
, for some
\hat{q}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
such that

\,\mathsf{deg}\,\hat{q}=(\,\mathsf{deg}\,r)(\,\mathsf{deg}\,p)+\,\mathsf{deg}\,q.

Suppose, by contradiction, that
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}r_{i}(h)\cdot t^{i}e^{p}=0
, for some
r_{i}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
, not all zero. If there is a unique
i
such that
r_{i}\neq 0
, then
0=r_{i}(h)\cdot t^{i}e^{p}=\hat{q}e^{p}
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,\hat{q}=i+n\,\mathsf{deg}\,r_{i}\geq 0
. Thus
\hat{q}\neq 0
, which contradicts the equality
\hat{q}e^{p}=0
. So assume that at least two of the
r_{i}
are nonzero. Then there are
0\leq i<j\leq n-1
such that
r_{i},r_{j}\neq 0
and
(\,\mathsf{deg}\,r_{i})n+i=(\,\mathsf{deg}\,r_{j})n+j
. Hence
(\,\mathsf{deg}\,r_{i}-\,\mathsf{deg}\,r_{j})n=j-i\in[n-1]
. As
[n-1]
contains no multiples of
n
, this is impossible. Therefore
B
is
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-linearly independent and the claim is proved.

Now consider the case
X=\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
. In this case,
y
acts as multiplication by
\beta t
, for some
\beta\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. Replacing
y
with
y/\beta
, we can, and will, assume that
\beta=1
, for simplicity, so that
y
acts as multiplication by
t
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
be a nonzero submodule. We claim that
t^{i}e^{p}\in V
, for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. First, notice that
(h-(\lambda+1)-yp^{\prime}(y))qe^{p}=tq^{\prime}e^{p}
, for all
q\in\mathbbm{k}[t]
. In particular,
(h-(\lambda+1)-yp^{\prime}(y))t^{j}e^{p}=jt^{j}e^{p}
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, so
\left\{t^{j}e^{p}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
of eigenvectors for the action of
(h-(\lambda+1)-yp^{\prime}(y))
. Thus, this operator has a diagonal action on
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
and hence also on
V
. Since the eigenspaces are one-dimensional,
V
must contain some eigenvector, say
t^{i}e^{p}
, for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
be minimum such that
t^{i}e^{p}\in V
. Using induction on the number of elements of
X
, one can prove that

Q_{X}(h)t^{j}e^{p}=\left(\prod_{\mu\in X}(\lambda+1+j-\mu)+tq_{j}\right)t^{j}e^{p},

for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for some
q_{j}\in\mathbbm{k}[t]
. Since

\displaystyle V\ni x\cdot t^{i}e^{p}

\displaystyle=Q_{X}(\theta)\partial t^{i}e^{p}=Q_{X}(h)(it^{i-1}+t^{i}p^{\prime})e^{p}

\displaystyle=i\left(\prod_{\mu\in X}(\lambda+i-\mu)+tq_{i-1}\right)t^{i-1}e^{p}+Q_{X}(h)p^{\prime}(y)t^{i}e^{p}

\displaystyle=i\prod_{\mu\in X}(\lambda+i-\mu)t^{i-1}e^{p}+\left(iq_{i-1}(y)+Q_{X}(h)p^{\prime}(y)\right)t^{i}e^{p},

we deduce that
i\prod_{\mu\in X}(\lambda+i-\mu)t^{i-1}e^{p}\in V
. By the minimality of
i
, we conclude that
i\prod_{\mu\in X}(\lambda+i-\mu)=0
and from the hypothesis that
\mu-\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
for all
\mu\in X
, it must be that
i=0
. Therefore
V=\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
and the simplicity of
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\})
is established.
∎

3
The category
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-free
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules

Denote by
\mathfrak{U}
the category of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules that are free of finite rank over the subalgebra
\mathbbm{k}[h]
. In this section we describe a skeleton of the category
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
, the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}
consisting of modules that are free of rank one over
\mathbbm{k}[h]
. We show that any module in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
is of finite length, give an algorithm to determine all of its composition series, and give an explicit classification of the simple objects in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
.

Let
M\in\mathfrak{U}
have rank
n
, so we may assume that
M=\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n}
as a
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module. Let
1_{1},\dotsc,1_{n}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n}
be its canonical basis. We have

y(h^{k}\cdot 1_{i})=(h-1)^{k}y1_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad x(h^{k}\cdot 1_{i})=(h+1)^{k}x\cdot 1_{i},\quad\text{for }i\in[n]\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore,

yf(h)\cdot 1_{i}=f(h-1)y\cdot 1_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad xf(h)\cdot 1_{i}=f(h+1)x\cdot 1_{i},\quad\text{for }i\in[n]\text{ and }f(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h].

(3.1)

In particular, the action of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
on
M
is uniquely defined by a choice

\displaystyle y\cdot 1_{i}

\displaystyle=\colon p_{i}=(p_{i,1},p_{i,2},\dotsc,p_{i,n})\in\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n},

(3.2)

\displaystyle x\cdot 1_{i}

\displaystyle=\colon q_{i}=(q_{i,1},q_{i,2},\dotsc,q_{i,n})\in\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n},

(3.3)

for all
i\in[n]
. By considering that
[y,x]=g(h)=u(h-1)-u(h)
, we deduce that the
p_{i,j}
and the
q_{i,j}
must satisfy the relations

g(h)1_{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{i,j}(h-1)p_{j,\ell}(h)-p_{i,j}(h+1)q_{j,\ell}(h)\right)1_{\ell},

for all
i\in[n]
. Writing
Q=(q_{i,j}),P=(p_{i,j})\in M_{n}(\mathbbm{k}[h])
, we see that the above is equivalent to the following matrix equation over
\mathbbm{k}[h]
:

Q(h-1)P(h)-P(h+1)Q(h)=g(h)I,

(3.4)

where
I\in M_{n}(\mathbbm{k}[h])
is the identity matrix. In fact, it is easy to see that (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) define a
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module structure on
M=\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n}
extending the action of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
by multiplication if and only if (
3.4
) holds.

Now, suppose that
M
has a central character
\chi_{M}:\mathbbm{k}[z_{u}]\to\mathbbm{k}
, so that
zm=\chi_{M}(z)m
, for all
z\in\mathsf{Z}(\mathcal{S}_{u})=\mathbbm{k}[z_{u}]
and all
m\in M
. Set
C=\chi_{M}(z_{u})
. Then we have
xy1_{i}=(z_{u}+u)1_{i}=(u+C)1_{i}
, which becomes

(u(h)+C)I=P(h+1)Q(h),

(3.5)

in matrix form. Then (
3.4
) implies that

(u(h-1)+C)I=Q(h-1)P(h),

(3.6)

which translates to
yx1_{i}=(u(h-1)+C)1_{i}
.
Conversely, notice that (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) imply (
3.4
) and moreover that
M
has a central character
\chi_{M}
with
\chi_{M}(z_{u})=C
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
\lambda\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
. Let
p=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\alpha_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbbm{k}[t]
of degree
n\geq 1
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
. By Theorem
2.5
,
\mathcal{E}
is
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-free with basis
\{e^{p},\dotsc,t^{n-1}e^{p}\}
. Hence there is an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-modules
\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
such that

1_{i}\mapsto t^{i-1}e^{p},\quad i\in[n].

Via this isomorphism,
\mathbbm{k}[h]^{n}
inherits from
\mathcal{E}
a structure of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module.

Recall, from Definition
2.4
and Lemma
2.2
, that
h
acts on
\mathcal{E}
as
\theta=t\partial+\lambda+1=\partial t+\lambda
,
x
acts as
\partial Q_{X}(\theta-1)=Q_{X}(\theta)\partial
and
y
acts as
tP_{X}(\theta+1)=P_{X}(\theta)t
. Set
v_{i}=t^{i-1}e^{p}
, for
i\in[n]
. Then we have, for
i\geq 2
,

\displaystyle x\cdot v_{i}

\displaystyle=Q_{X}(\theta)\partial\,t^{i-1}e^{p}=Q_{X}(\theta)(\partial t)t^{i-2}e^{p}=Q_{X}(\theta)(\theta-\lambda)v_{i-1}=Q_{X}(h)(h-\lambda)\cdot v_{i-1}.

We conclude that
q_{i,j}=Q_{X}(h)(h-\lambda)\delta_{i-1,j}
, for all
i,j\in[n]
with
i\geq 2
, where
\delta_{k,\ell}
is the Kronecker delta.

Now we take
i=1
:

\displaystyle x\cdot v_{1}

\displaystyle=Q_{X}(\theta)\partial\,e^{p}=Q_{X}(\theta)p^{\prime}e^{p}=Q_{X}(\theta)\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\alpha_{j}t^{j-1}e^{p}=Q_{X}(h)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\alpha_{j}v_{j}.

We conclude that
q_{1,j}=Q_{X}(h)j\alpha_{j}
, for all
j\in[n]
. Therefore, we obtain

Q(h)=Q_{X}(h)\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&2\alpha_{2}&3\alpha_{3}&\dotsb&(n-1)\alpha_{n-1}&n\alpha_{n}\\
(h-\lambda)&0&0&\dotsb&0&0\\
0&(h-\lambda)&0&\dotsb&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dotsb&0&0\\
0&0&\dotsb&\dotsb&(h-\lambda)&0\end{bmatrix}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle P(h)

\displaystyle=P_{X}(h)\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&\dotsb&0&0\\
0&0&1&\dotsb&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dotsb&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dotsb&0&1\\
\frac{(h-(\lambda+1))}{n\alpha_{n}}&-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{n\alpha_{n}}&-\frac{2\alpha_{2}}{n\alpha_{n}}&\dotsb&-\frac{(n-2)\alpha_{n-2}}{n\alpha_{n}}&-\frac{(n-1)\alpha_{n-1}}{n\alpha_{n}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=P_{X}(h)\left(\mathrm{Comp}\left(\frac{tp^{\prime}-(h-(\lambda+1))}{n\alpha_{n}}\right)\right)^{t},

where
\mathrm{Comp}(f(t))
denotes the companion matrix of
f(t)\in(\mathbbm{k}[h])[t]
, as a polynomial in
t
.

3.1
The category
\mathfrak{U}_{1}

Now we will focus on the category
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules which are free of rank 1 over
\mathbbm{k}[h]
. In the following, we will identify
M\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
with
\mathbbm{k}[h]
, the (left) regular
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module. We set
p_{M}=y\cdot 1
and
q_{M}=x\cdot 1
. Whenever there is no ambiguity, we will simply denote these elements of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
by
p
and
q
, respectively.

Notice that, by Lemma
2.2
, the Casimir element
z_{u}
acts on any exponential module by a scalar. Next, we show that this property holds for all modules in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
. Then
M
admits a central character
\chi_{M}
that satisfies
\chi_{M}(z_{u})=p(h+1)q(h)-u(h)\in\mathbbm{k}
.

Proof.

By equation (
3.4
), we must have

q(h-1)p(h)-p(h+1)q(h)=g(h)=u(h-1)-u(h).

Let
f(h)=q(h)p(h+1)
. Then we have
f(h-1)-f(h)=g(h)
. By
[
12
, Lemma 4]
, the solution of such an equation is unique up to the constant term. Therefore,
f(h)=u(h)+C
, for some
C\in\mathbbm{k}
. In particular
C=p(h+1)q(h)-u(h)
and

z_{u}\cdot 1=(xy-u(h))\cdot 1=x\cdot p-u(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:comutation-with-x-and-y}}}{{=}}p(h+1)q(h)-u(h)=C,

thus proving the lemma.
∎

Let
M\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
and
C=\chi_{M}(z_{u})
. Let
\xi_{C}\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
be the leading coefficient of
u(h)+C
.
Since
u(h)+C=p(h+1)q(h)
, it follows that there is a multiset partition
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}=X\coprod Y
, where
X=\,\mathsf{R}_{q}
and
Y=\,\mathsf{R}_{p(h+1)}=\,\mathsf{R}_{p}-1
. Hence,

q(h)=\xi_{q}\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}=\xi_{q}\prod_{\alpha\in X}(h-\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad p(h)=\xi_{p}\,\mathsf{poly}_{Y+1}=\xi_{p}\prod_{\alpha\in Y}(h-(\alpha+1)),

with
\xi_{q},\xi_{p}\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
the leading coefficients of
q
and
p
, respectively, so that
\xi_{q}\xi_{p}=\xi_{C}
.
Thus,
M=\mathbbm{k}[h]
is described by
C
,
X
and
\xi_{q}
, and we will denote it by
A_{C}(X,\xi_{q})
.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
, let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
defined by

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda x,\quad\varphi_{\lambda}(y)=\lambda^{-1}y\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{\lambda}(h)=h.

For any
M\in\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod}
, define
\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}M\in\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod}
to be the module
M
with
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-action twisted by
\varphi_{\lambda}
, i.e,
s\cdot m=\varphi_{\lambda}(s)m
, for all
s\in\mathcal{S}_{u}
,
m\in\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}M
. This defines a family of functors

\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}:\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod},

for all
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}\mathsf{F}_{\mu}=\mathsf{F}_{\lambda\mu}
, for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. In particular, the
\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}
define category autoequivalences.

Notice now that
\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}A_{C}(X,\xi_{q})=A_{C}(X,\lambda\xi_{q})
, for all
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
, so in particular
A_{C}(X,\xi_{q})=\mathsf{F}_{\xi_{q}}A_{C}(X,1)
. Thus, it suffices to study the modules of the form
A_{C}(X,1)
, which we simply denote by
A_{C}(X)
.

We summarize the above construction.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
C\in\mathbbm{k}
and let
X
be an arbitrary submultiset of the multiset
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
of roots of
u(h)+C
. Let
Y=\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X
, the multiset complement of
X
in
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
. Let
q(h)=\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}=\prod_{\alpha\in X}(h-\alpha)
and
p(h)=\frac{u(h-1)+C}{q(h-1)}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
.
Then
A_{C}(X)=\mathbbm{k}[h]
is the regular
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module, with action extended to
\mathcal{S}_{u}
by

xf(h)=f(h+1)q(h)\quad\text{and}\quad yf(h)=f(h-1)p(h),\quad\text{for all $f(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]$.}

We have proved the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod}
. Then
M\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
if and only if then there exist
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
,
C\in\mathbbm{k}
and a submultiset
X
of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
such that
M\simeq\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}A_{C}(X)
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{k}
,
X
and
X^{\prime}
be submultisets of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C^{\prime}}
, respectively, and
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. Then
\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}A_{C}(X)\simeq\mathsf{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}A_{C^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
if and only if
C=C^{\prime}
,
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
X=X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Assume that
M=\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}A_{C}(X)\simeq\mathsf{F}_{\lambda^{\prime}}A_{C^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})=M^{\prime}
. Then the central characters must be the same, so
C=C^{\prime}
. Moreover, any isomorphism of
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules is in particular an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-modules, and hence given by multiplication by a nonzero scalar. Thus it can be assumed that the identity map is an isomorphism between the given
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules. Then, by checking the action of
x
, we deduce that the isomorphism maps
\lambda q_{M}
to
\lambda^{\prime}q_{M^{\prime}}
. Hence these polynomials have the same roots and the same leading coefficient, and it follows that
X=X^{\prime}
and
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.
∎

From Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
we obtain a classification of the objects in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
.

Corollary 3.12
.

The following family is a skeleton of the category
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
:

\left\{\mathsf{F}_{\lambda}A_{C}(X)\mid C\in\mathbbm{k},\,\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}\text{ and }X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\text{ (a submultiset)}\right\}.

3.2
The exponential modules in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}

From Theorem
2.5
we know that the exponential modules in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
are precisely those of the form
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p=1
. We will see that the latter exhaust all isomorphism classes in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
, except for the isomorphism classes of
A_{C}(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C},\alpha)
, with
C,\alpha\in\mathbbm{k}
and
\alpha\neq 0
. Using the symmetry of the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
, we define
dual
exponential modules
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)^{\vee}
which will cover the remaining isomorphism classes in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
.

Fix
p(t)=\alpha t+\beta
, with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbbm{k}
and
\alpha\neq 0
. We know that
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)\simeq A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi)
, for some submultiset
\widetilde{X}\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{n+C}
and
\xi\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. These are determined by
x\cdot 1=\xi\,\mathsf{poly}_{\widetilde{X}}(h)
in
A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi)
. Since
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{S}_{u}}(A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi))=\mathbbm{k}\,1
, where
1
stands for the identity on
A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi)
, we can assume that the isomorphism
A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi)\rightarrow\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
takes the
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-generators
1\in A_{C}(\widetilde{X},\xi)
to
e^{p}\in\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
. Then from
\xi\,\mathsf{poly}_{\widetilde{X}}(h)=x\cdot 1
we obtain

\displaystyle\xi\,\mathsf{poly}_{\widetilde{X}}(h)\cdot e^{p}=x\cdot e^{p}=Q_{X}(\theta)\partial e^{p}=\alpha\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}(h)\cdot e^{p}

As
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)
is a free
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module on
\left\{e^{p}\right\}
, it follows that
\xi\,\mathsf{poly}_{\widetilde{X}}(h)=\alpha\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}(h)
, so
\xi=\alpha
and
\widetilde{X}=X
.

Combining the preceding considerations with Lemma
3.11
, we obtain a characterization of the exponential modules for
\mathcal{S}_{u}
of rank
1
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
p,\widetilde{p}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p=1=\,\mathsf{deg}\,\widetilde{p}
,
C,\widetilde{C}\in\mathbbm{k}
,
\lambda\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
,
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+\widetilde{C}}
and
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
,
\widetilde{X}\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+\widetilde{C}}\setminus\{\widetilde{\lambda}\}
submultisets. The following hold:

(a)

\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)\simeq A_{C}(X,\alpha)
, where
p^{\prime}(t)=\alpha\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
;

(b)

\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)\simeq\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{p},\widetilde{C},\widetilde{\lambda},\widetilde{X})
if and only if
p^{\prime}=\widetilde{p}^{\prime}
,
C=\widetilde{C}
and
X=\widetilde{X}
.

In particular, all modules
A_{C}(X,\alpha)
are exponential modules, except for
X=\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
. In order to be able to include these latter ones, we define the modules
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)^{\vee}
using the symmetry of
A_{1}
.

Concretely, let
\tau\colon A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}
be the automorphism defined by
t\mapsto\partial
and
\partial\mapsto-t
. Then the algebra morphism
\widetilde{\varphi}_{C,\lambda,X}\colon S_{u}\rightarrow A_{1}
defined by
\widetilde{\varphi}_{C,\lambda,X}=\tau\circ\varphi_{C,\lambda,X}
induces an
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module structure on the
A_{1}
-module
\mathbbm{k}[t]e^{p}
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)^{\vee}
. So
h
acts as
\widetilde{\theta}=\tau(\theta)=-\partial t+\lambda+1
,
x
acts as
-Q_{X}(\widetilde{\theta})t
and
y
acts as
P_{X}(\widetilde{\theta})\partial
.

As before, we obtain a characterization of the modules
\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)^{\vee}
in case
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p=1
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
p\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,p=1
,
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
\lambda\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\{\lambda\}
a submultiset. Then

\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,X)^{\vee}\simeq A_{C}(X\cup\left\{\lambda\right\},\alpha^{-1}),\quad\text{where $\alpha=p^{\prime}(t)\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}$.}

In particular,
A_{C}(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C},\alpha^{-1})\simeq\mathcal{E}(p,C,\lambda,\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus\left\{\lambda\right\})^{\vee}
.

3.3
The submodule structure of
A_{C}(X)

Next, we study the simplicity of the modules
A_{C}(X)
and, moreover, we will produce an algorithm to describe the composition series for these modules. We will find that
A_{C}(X)
always has finite length as an
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module.

Unless otherwise noted, throughout this subsection,
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
X
denotes an arbitrary submultiset of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and the polynomials
p,q\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
are as in Definition
3.9
. In particular,
q
is monic,
X=\,\mathsf{R}_{q}
and
Y=\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X=\,\mathsf{R}_{p(h+1)}
. Define

\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)=\left\{t(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]\mid\text{$t(h)$ is monic, }t(h)\mid t(h-1)p(h)\text{ and }t(h)\mid t(h+1)q(h)\right\}\bigcup\left\{0\right\}.

We think of
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
as a poset, under the polynomial divisibility relation.

Lemma 3.15
.

There is an order reversing bijection between
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
and the lattice of submodules of
A_{C}(X)
. Under this correspondence,
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
is mapped to
t\mathbbm{k}[h]\subseteq A_{C}(X)
. Moreover, if
t\neq 0
then
t\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq A_{C}(\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{q}})
, where
\overline{q}=\frac{t(h+1)q(h)}{t(h)}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be an
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-submodule of
A_{C}(X)
. Then
M
is an ideal of
\mathbbm{k}[h]
, by restriction, so it follows that
M=t(h)\mathbbm{k}[h]
, for some
t(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
. If
t\neq 0
, then we can assume that
t
is monic, in which case
M
determines
t
. Since
M
is stable under the action of
x
, we have

t(h+1)q(h)=xt(h)\in M=t(h)\mathbbm{k}[h],

thus
t(h)
divides
t(h+1)q(h)
. Similarly, looking at the action of
y
, we deduce that
t(h)
divides
t(h-1)p(h)
.

Conversely, let
0\neq t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
and set
\overline{q}=\frac{t(h+1)q(h)}{t(h)},\ \overline{p}=\frac{t(h-1)p(h)}{t(h)}\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
. Notice that
\overline{q}(h)\overline{p}(h+1)=q(h)p(h+1)=u(h)+C
, so that
\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{q}}
and
\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{p}(h+1)}
define a partition of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
. What’s more,

\displaystyle xt(h)f(h)

\displaystyle=t(h+1)f(h+1)q(h)=t(h)f(h+1)\overline{q}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle yt(h)f(h)

\displaystyle=t(h-1)f(h-1)p(h)=t(h)f(h-1)\overline{p},

for all
f(h)\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
.
Hence
t\mathbbm{k}[h]
is a submodule of
A_{C}(X)
isomorphic to
A_{C}(\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{q}})
. The order reversing property is clear.
∎

As all nonzero submodules of
A_{C}(X)
are of the form
A_{C}(X^{\prime})
, for some submultiset
X^{\prime}
of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
, in order to find simplicity criteria and composition series for
A_{C}(X)
, it suffices to determine all of the maximal submodules of
A_{C}(X)
. By the previous result, this is tantamount to finding all minimal elements of
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\left\{1\right\}
, i.e. all
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
with no proper nontrivial factors in
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
.

It will be convenient to introduce a partial order relation on
\mathbbm{k}
, given by
\alpha\preccurlyeq\beta\iff\beta-\alpha=n1_{\mathbbm{k}}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
(recall that
\mathsf{char}(\mathbbm{k})=0
, so
\preccurlyeq
is indeed antisymmetric).

Let
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
and assume that
t\neq 0,1
. Then
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is a nonempty finite multiset with cardinality equal to
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(t)\geq 1
, and thus it decomposes as a finite union of maximal chains (the connected components of the Hasse diagram of the poset
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
):

\displaystyle s_{1}\

\displaystyle:\ \alpha_{1}^{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k_{1}}^{1};

\displaystyle\ \vdots

\displaystyle s_{\ell}\

\displaystyle:\ \alpha_{1}^{\ell}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k_{\ell}}^{\ell}.

Set

\displaystyle t_{s_{i}}(h)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}(h-\alpha^{i}_{j}),

\displaystyle\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)

\displaystyle=t(h)\left(t_{s_{i}}(h)\right)^{-1},

so that
t(h)=t_{s_{1}}(h)\cdots t_{s_{\ell}}(h)=t_{s_{i}}(h)\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)
. Thus
t_{s_{i}}(h)\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)\mid t_{s_{i}}(h-1)\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h-1)p(h)
and, since
\mathsf{gcd}\left(t_{s_{i}}(h),\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)\right)=1
, the latter is equivalent to

\displaystyle t_{s_{i}}(h)\mid t_{s_{i}}(h-1)\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h-1)p(h)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)\mid t_{s_{i}}(h-1)\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h-1)p(h).

(3.16)

Moreover, as
\mathsf{gcd}\left(t_{s_{i}}(h),\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h-1)\right)=1=\mathsf{gcd}\left(t_{s_{i}}(h-1),\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)\right)
, (
3.16
) above is equivalent to

\displaystyle t_{s_{i}}(h)\mid t_{s_{i}}(h-1)p(h)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h)\mid\overline{t_{s_{i}}}(h-1)p(h).

Replacing
p(h)
with
q(h)
we deduce that
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\iff t_{s_{1}},\ldots,t_{s_{\ell}}\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
, and
t\mathbbm{k}[h]=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}t_{s_{i}}\mathbbm{k}[h]
. Thus, we may assume that the roots of
t(h)
form a chain

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k},

with
k\geq 1
. Then, from
t(h)\mid t(h-1)p(h)
we deduce that
\alpha_{1}
is a root of
p
, i.e.
\alpha_{1}-1\in\,\mathsf{R}_{p(h+1)}=Y
. Similarly, from
t(h)\mid t(h+1)q(h)
we deduce that
\alpha_{k}\in\,\mathsf{R}_{q}=X
. We call such a multiset a
(p,q)
-chain
. Note that
\alpha_{k}-(\alpha_{1}-1)=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{1}+1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Lemma 3.17
.

If
\alpha_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k}
is a
(p,q)
-chain, then
s(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{m}(h-(\alpha_{1}+j))\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
, where
m=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{1}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(h-\alpha_{1})s(h-1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{m+1}(h-(\alpha_{1}+j))=s(h)(h-(\alpha_{1}+m+1)),

\displaystyle(h-(\alpha_{1}+m))s(h+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=-1}^{m}(h-(\alpha_{1}+j))=s(h)(h-(\alpha_{1}-1)).

Since
h-\alpha_{1}\mid p(h)
and
h-\alpha_{k}=h-(\alpha_{1}+m)\mid q(h)
, it follows that

\displaystyle s(h)\mid s(h-1)p(h)\quad\text{ and }\quad s(h)\mid s(h+1)q(h).

∎

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
C\in\mathbbm{k}
and
X
be a submultiset of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
, with
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}=X\coprod Y
. Consider the
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
, as given in Definition
3.9
. Then
A_{C}(X)
is simple if and only if
(X-Y)\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}=\emptyset
, i.e. if and only if there are no
\alpha\in Y
and
\beta\in X
such that
\beta-\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Assume that
A_{C}(X)
is simple and assume, by contradiction, that there exist
\alpha\in Y
and
\beta\in X
such that
\beta-\alpha=m+1
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
s(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{m}(h-(\alpha_{1}+j))
, with
\alpha_{1}=\alpha+1
. Then
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(s)=m+1\geq 1
and
\alpha_{1}\preccurlyeq\alpha_{1}+1\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{1}+m=\beta
is a
(p,q)
-chain, so
s(h)\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
by Lemma
3.17
. Hence,
s(h)\mathbbm{k}[h]
is a proper nontrivial submodule of
A_{C}(X)
, a contradiction.

Conversely, suppose that
(X-Y)\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}=\emptyset
and let
S\subseteq A_{C}(X)
be a submodule. Then
S=s(h)\mathbbm{k}[h]
for some
s(h)\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
. If
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(s)\geq 1
, then the multiset
\,\mathsf{R}_{s}
is finite and nonempty. Thus, by the preceding considerations, there is a divisor
t(h)
of
s(h)
with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(t)\geq 1
such that
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is a
(p,q)
-chain, say
\alpha_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k}
, with
\alpha_{1}-1\in Y
and
\alpha_{k}\in X
. Thence,
\alpha_{k}-(\alpha_{1}-1)=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{1}+1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\cap(X-Y)=\emptyset
, a contradiction. Thus, either
s(h)=1
or
s(h)=0
, proving that
A_{C}(X)
is simple.
∎

Let us return to the classification of the minimal elements
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\left\{1\right\}
. We already know that
t=0
is minimal if and only if
(X-Y)\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}=\emptyset
, so let’s assume that
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(t)\geq 1
. From our previous considerations, we know that
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is a
(p,q)
-chain, say
\alpha_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\alpha_{k}
, with
k\geq 1
.

Suppose there is
1\leq i<k
such that
\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_{i}\geq 2
. Then, writing
t(h)=t_{1}(h)t_{2}(h)
with
t_{1}(h)=\prod_{j\leq i}(h-\alpha_{j})
and
t_{2}(h)=\prod_{j>i}(h-\alpha_{j})
, the argument we have used before in (
3.16
) also shows that
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\iff t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
, and
t\mathbbm{k}[h]=t_{1}\mathbbm{k}[h]\cap t_{2}\mathbbm{k}[h]
. Thus, we may further assume that

\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_{i}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, for all
1\leq i<k
. We call such a chain a
gapless chain
.
What’s more, taking
m=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{1}
and
s(h)=\prod_{j=0}^{m}(h-(\alpha_{1}+j))
, we see that
s(h)
divides
t(h)
(because the chain is gapless) and
s(h)\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
, by Lemma
3.17
. As
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(s)=m+1\geq 1
, it follows from the minimality of
t
that
t=s
; whence,
t
is separable. In other words, the roots of
t
are all distinct and form a gapless
(p,q)
-chain
\alpha_{1}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\cdots\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{k}
.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
X
,
Y
and
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
be as above. Then
t
is minimal in
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\left\{1\right\}
if and only if either one of the following conditions hold:

(a)

t=0
and
(X-Y)\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}=\emptyset
(i.e.,
A_{C}(X)
is simple);

(b)

\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is a finite gapless
(p,q)
-chain with no repeated elements, say
\alpha_{1}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\cdots\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{k}
, with
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(t)=k
, such that:

(i)

there is no
i<k
with
\alpha_{i}\in X
;

(ii)

there is no
i>1
with
\alpha_{i}-1\in Y
.

Proof.

The direct implication is clear from the preceding discussion. Conversely, condition
(a)
clearly implies the minimality of
t=0
. So assume that the roots of
t
form the chain
\alpha_{1}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\cdots\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{k}
, satisfying the conditions in
(b)
. In particular,
t\neq 0
, as
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is finite.

Let
s\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\left\{1\right\}
be a divisor of
t
. As
t\neq 0
, also
s\neq 0
and thus
\,\mathsf{deg}\,(s)\geq 1
. Then,
\,\mathsf{R}_{s}\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
and the roots of
s
are of the form
\alpha_{i_{1}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\cdots\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{i_{\ell}}
, with
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}\leq k
. The fact that
s\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
implies that
\,\mathsf{R}_{s}
is a
(p,q)
-chain and
(b)
forces
i_{1}=1
and
i_{\ell}=k
. If
s\neq t
, then there is some
j<\ell
such that
\alpha_{i_{j+1}}-\alpha_{i_{j}}\geq 2
. Hence, by the argument preceding Proposition
3.19
,
(h-\alpha_{i_{1}})\cdots(h-\alpha_{i_{j}})\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
. In particular,
\alpha_{i_{j}}\in X
, forcing
i_{j}=k=i_{\ell}
, which contradicts
j<\ell
. The contradiction implies that
s=t
, proving the minimality of
t
.
∎

Let
C\in\mathbbm{k}
,
X
,
p,q\in\mathbbm{k}[h]
and
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)
be as above. Write
X_{0}=X
,
q_{0}=q
and
p_{0}=p
. The previous proposition provides a method to construct any decreasing chain of submodules

A_{C}(X_{0})\supset t_{1}\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq A_{C}(X_{1})\supset t_{2}t_{1}\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq A_{C}(X_{2})\supset\dotsb,

(3.20)

where
t_{i}
is a minimal element of
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X_{i-1})\setminus\{1\}
. As long as
t_{i}\neq 0
, we can proceed with
q_{i}=\frac{t_{i}(h+1)q_{i-1}(h)}{t_{i}(h)}
,
p_{i}=\frac{t_{i}(h-1)p_{i-1}(h)}{t_{i}(h)}
and
X_{i}=\,\mathsf{R}_{q_{i}}
, for
i\geq 1
. The minimality of
t_{i}
implies that
A_{C}(X_{i-1})/A_{C}(X_{i})
is simple, for all
i\geq 1
. Then, to prove that all objects in
\mathfrak{U}_{1}
have finite length, it is enough to show that, after a finite number of steps, the minimal element obtained is
t_{\ell}=0
.

3.4
Composition series for
A_{C}(X)

Recall the order
\preccurlyeq
defined on
\mathbbm{k}
. Given a multiset
Z\subseteq\mathbbm{k}
and
\beta\in\mathbbm{k}
, denote by
Z_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}
the submultiset
\{\alpha\in Z\mid\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta\}
.
Let
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
be a submultiset and take
\beta\in X
. If
(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}\neq\emptyset
, we denote by
X\star\beta
the submultiset of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
defined by

X\star\beta=\{\hat{\beta}\}\cup X\setminus\{\beta\},

where
\hat{\beta}\in(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}
is uniquely defined by imposing the minimum distance from
\beta
, i.e.,
\beta-\hat{\beta}=\min\{\beta-\alpha\mid\alpha\in(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}\}
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\left\{1\right\}
be minimal and suppose that
t\neq 0
. Set
q=\,\mathsf{poly}_{X}
. Then
\,\mathsf{R}_{t}
is a finite gapless
(p,q)
-chain with no repeated elements, say
\alpha_{1}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\cdots\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{k}
satisfying Proposition
3.19
(b)
. Set
\overline{q}=\frac{t(h+1)q(h)}{t(h)}
. It follows that
\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{q}}=(X\setminus\{\alpha_{k}\})\cup\{\alpha_{1}-1\}
. Furthermore, by Proposition
3.19
(b)
(b)(ii)
,

k=\alpha_{k}-(\alpha_{1}-1)=\min\{\alpha_{k}-\beta\mid\beta\in(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha_{k}}\}.

Thus,
\,\mathsf{R}_{\overline{q}}=X\star\alpha_{k}
. Moreover, with this notation, the chain of submodules (
3.20
) can be written as

A_{C}(X)\supset t_{1}\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1})\supset t_{2}t_{1}\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\beta_{2})\supset\dotsb,

(3.21)

where
\beta_{i}
is the maximal element of the gapless
(p_{i-1},q_{i-1})
-chain corresponding to
t_{i}
, a minimal element of
\mathcal{L}_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{i-1})\setminus\{1\}
which we are assuming to be nonzero.

Now, for any submultiset
Z\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
, define

\ell(Z)=\sum_{\beta\in Z}|(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus Z)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}|\geq 0,

where
|\cdot|
denotes the number of elements of a multiset. Notice that
\ell(Z\star\beta)\leq\ell(Z)-1
, whenever
Z\star\beta
is defined. Finally, recall also that, by Corollary
3.18
,
A_{C}(Z)
is simple if and only if
\ell(Z)=0
.
Therefore, the chain (
3.21
) has maximal length bounded above by
\ell(X)
and
\ell(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})=0
, for some
m\leq\ell(X)
. The last nonzero term of the chain (
3.21
) will be the simple submodule
A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})
.

From the discussion above we obtain our desired result.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
. Then
A_{C}(X)
has finite length, bounded above by
\ell(X)+1
.

The method described above using Proposition
3.19
and the iterative construction in (
3.21
) gives all possible composition series for
A_{C}(X)
. Nevertheless, we will see that, regardless of the choices made, the final multiset
X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m}
obtained, with
\ell(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})=0
, will always be the same. We give an algebraic proof of this result, using the notion of socle of a module
M
, denoted by
\mathrm{soc}(M)
, this being the sum of its simple submodules, or equivalently,
its unique maximal semisimple submodule.

Corollary 3.23
.

Let
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
. Then
\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X)\right)=A_{C}(X^{\star})
, where
X^{\star}=X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m}
is obtained iteratively by the method described above, terminating with
\ell(X^{\star})=0
. In particular,
X^{\star}
depends only on
X
.

Proof.

We have seen that there exist
0\leq m\leq\ell(X)
and
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
such that
A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})
is a submodule of
A_{C}(X)
with
\ell(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})=0
, hence simple and thence contained in
\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X)\right)
.

The
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module
A_{C}(X)
is just the regular module
\mathbbm{k}[h]
, which contains no nontrivial direct sums of submodules. It follows that the same must hold for
A_{C}(X)
as an
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module. Thus, its socle, being nonzero and semisimple, must be simple and equal to
A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})
. So
A_{C}(X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m})
is the unique simple submodule of
A_{C}(X)
, and the last nonzero term in all composition series for
A_{C}(X)
. Now, by Lemma
3.11
, the uniqueness of the multiset
X\star\beta_{1}\star\dotsb\star\beta_{m}
follows.
∎

Remark 3.24
.

Let
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}=R_{1}\coprod\cdots\coprod R_{k}
be the decomposition of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
in to its maximal chains with respect to
\preccurlyeq
. Then
X^{\star}
is the unique submultiset of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
with
\ell(X^{\star})=0
and
|R_{i}\cap X^{\star}|=|R_{i}\cap X|
, for all
i\in[k]
.

Next, we will describe the remaining composition factors of
A_{C}(X)
and their multiplicities, obtaining as a corollary an exact formula for the length of
A_{C}(X)
. Since
A_{C}(X)/\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X)\right)
is finite dimensional,
A_{C}(X^{\star})
occurs with multiplicity one and all the other composition factors, if any, will be finite dimensional.

We summarize the classification of simple
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules of finite dimension given by Smith in
[
15
]
(see also
[
10
]
). Let
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}
, and
\mathbbm{k}_{\lambda}=\mathbbm{k}v_{\lambda}
be the one-dimensional
\mathbbm{k}[h]
-module where
h
acts by
\lambda
. Let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{u}
be the unital subalgebra generated by
h
and
y
. Then
\mathbbm{k}_{\lambda}
becomes a
\mathfrak{b}
-module by defining
yv_{\lambda}=0
. The Verma module of highest weight
\lambda
for
\mathcal{S}_{u}
is defined by

V(\lambda)=\mathcal{S}_{u}\otimes_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathbbm{k}_{\lambda}\simeq\mathbbm{k}[x].

Theorem 3.25
.

[
15
]

Let
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}
, then
V(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule and hence a unique simple subquotient, denoted by
L(\lambda)
. Furthermore, any simple
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module of dimension
j
is isomorphic to

L(\lambda)=V(\lambda)/x^{j}V(\lambda),

for some
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}
, where
j
is the minimal positive integer such that
u(\lambda)-u(\lambda-j)=0
.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
and assume that
A_{C}(X)
is not simple. Let
0\neq t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\{1\}
be minimal. Then
A_{C}(X)/t\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq L(\beta)
, where
\beta
is the maximal element of the gapless
(p,q)
-chain corresponding to
t
.

Proof.

Let
t(h)=(h-(\hat{\beta}+1))(h-(\hat{\beta}+2))\dotsb(h-\beta)
, with
\hat{\beta}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta
. Then
N=A_{C}(X)/t\mathbbm{k}[h]
has dimension equal to
\beta-\hat{\beta}
.
Define
w\neq 0
to be the class of
t(h)/(h-\beta)
in
N
.
A straightforward computation using the fact that
h-(\hat{\beta}+1)
divides
p
shows that

yw=0,\quad hx^{k}w=(\beta-k)x^{k}w,\quad\text{and}\quad yx^{k+1}w=(u(\beta-(k+1))-u(\beta))x^{k}w,

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
k_{0}
be the minimal positive integer such that
x^{k_{0}}w=0
. Then we have

0=yx^{k_{0}}w=(u(\beta-k_{0})-u(\beta))x^{k_{0}-1}w.

As
N
is simple, it follows that
N=\,\mathsf{span}_{\mathbbm{k}}\left\{x^{k}w\mid k=0,\ldots,k_{0}-1\right\}
, a simple
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module of highest weight
\beta
. Thus,
N\simeq L(\beta)
.
Since
x^{k_{0}-1}w\neq 0
, this implies that
u(\beta-k_{0})-u(\beta)=0
, and we have
\beta-\hat{\beta}=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N=k_{0}
.
∎

Remark 3.27
.

As a converse to the previous result, any simple finite-dimensional
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-module
L(\lambda)
can be seen as a quotient of
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
, for some
C\in\mathbbm{k}
and some
X\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
. Indeed, suppose that
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}L(\lambda)=j\geq 1
and set
C=-u(\lambda)
. Then, by Theorem
3.25
,
\lambda,\lambda-j\in\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
and
A_{C}(\left\{\lambda\right\})
is well defined. Moreover,
\lambda-j+1\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\lambda
forms a
(p,q)
-chain for
X=\left\{\lambda\right\}
, so
t_{\lambda}(h)=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(h-(\lambda-i))\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(\left\{\lambda\right\})
, by Lemma
3.17
. Finally, the minimality of
j
given in Theorem
3.25
ensures, by Proposition
3.19
, that
t_{\lambda}\mathbbm{k}[h]
is a maximal submodule of
A_{C}(\left\{\lambda\right\})
, isomorphic to
A_{C}(\left\{\lambda-j\right\})
, and
A_{C}(\left\{\lambda\right\})/t_{\lambda}\mathbbm{k}[h]\simeq L(\lambda)
, by Lemma
3.26
.

Now, for every submultiset
Z
of
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
, define the map
\varphi_{Z}\colon\underline{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
by

\varphi_{Z}(\beta)=\min\left\{|(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus Z)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}|,|Z_{\succcurlyeq\beta}|\right\},

where
Z_{\succcurlyeq\beta}=\{\alpha\in Z\mid\beta\preccurlyeq\alpha\}
and
\underline{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}}
is the underlying set obtained from
\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
.

We are ready to describe the composition factors of
A_{C}(X)
and their multiplicities. It turns out that this is best phrased using the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{u})
, which is the free abelian group on the isomorphism classes of finitely generated
\mathcal{S}_{u}
-modules, modulo the short exact sequences.

Theorem 3.28
.

Consider the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{u})=\{[M]\mid M\in\mathcal{S}_{u}\mathsf{-mod}\}
. Let
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
. Then

[A_{C}(X)]=[A_{C}(X^{\star})]+\sum_{\beta\in\underline{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}}}\varphi_{X}(\beta)[L(\beta)]\in K_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{u}),

where
A_{C}(X^{\star})=\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X)\right)
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
\ell(X)
.

If
\ell(X)=0
, then clearly
\varphi_{X}
is the constant null map,
X=X^{\star}
and the claim is proved.
Suppose that
\ell(X)>0
and assume the claim to be true for any submultiset
Z\subseteq\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}
such that
\ell(Z)<\ell(X)
. There exists a minimal
t\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(X)\setminus\{1\}
and
t\neq 0
, since
\ell(X)>0
. Let
\beta
be the maximal element of the gapless
(p,q)
-chain corresponding to
t
(see Prop.
3.19
). Then, by Lemmas
3.15
and
3.26
, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{C}(X\star\beta)\rightarrow A_{C}(X)\rightarrow L(\beta)\rightarrow 0.

Then
[A_{C}(X)]=[A_{C}(X\star\beta)]+[L(\beta)]\in K_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{u})
. Since
\ell(X\star\beta)\leq\ell(X)-1
, it follows by the induction hypothesis that

[A_{C}(X)]=[L(\beta)]+[A_{C}(X^{\star})]+\sum_{\alpha\in\underline{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}}}\varphi_{X\star\beta}(\alpha)[L(\alpha)]\in K_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{u}),

where
A_{C}(X^{\star})=\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X\star\beta)\right)=\mathrm{soc}\left(A_{C}(X)\right)
.
So it is sufficient to prove that

\varphi_{X}(\beta)=\varphi_{X\star\beta}(\beta)+1\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{X}(\alpha)=\varphi_{X\star\beta}(\alpha),\quad\text{for all $\alpha\neq\beta$}.

(3.29)

Computing
\varphi_{X}
and
\varphi_{X\star\beta}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle|(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X\star\beta)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}|(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\alpha}|,&\alpha\neq\beta;\\
|(\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}\setminus X)_{\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\preccurlyeq$\cr\kern 1.2pt$\mathrel{\raisebox{-0.95pt}{$\rotatebox[origin]{-315.0}{\scalebox{0.55}{$-$}}$}}$\crcr}}}\beta}|-1,&\alpha=\beta;\end{cases}

\displaystyle|(X\star\beta)_{\succcurlyeq\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}|X_{\succcurlyeq\alpha}|,&\alpha\neq\beta;\\
|X_{\succcurlyeq\beta}|-1,&\alpha=\beta.\end{cases}

Thus, (
3.29
) is satisfied and the theorem is proved.
∎

Corollary 3.30
.

The module
A_{C}(X)\in\mathfrak{U}_{1}
has length
\displaystyle 1+\sum_{\beta\in\underline{\,\mathsf{R}_{u+C}}}\varphi_{X}(\beta)
.

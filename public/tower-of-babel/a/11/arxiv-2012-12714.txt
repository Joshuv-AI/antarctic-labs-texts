Marco Cannone, Grzegorz Karch, Dominika Pilarczyk, Gang Wu

Abstract
. We develop mathematical methods which allow us to study asymptotic properties of solutions to the three dimensional Navier-Stokes system for incompressible fluid in the whole three dimensional space. We deal either with the Cauchy problem or with the stationary problem where solutions may be singular due to singular external forces which are either singular finite measures or more general tempered distributions with bounded Fourier transforms. We present results on asymptotic properties of such solutions either for large values of the space variables (so called the far-field asymptotics) or for large values of time.

1.
Introduction

Singular solutions to the Navier-Stokes system

While a proof of a regularity of weak energy solutions to an initial-boundary value problem for
the Navier-Stokes system
seems to be still out of reach, several advances were made in the opposite direction of the study of singular (and sometimes exact) solutions for these equations.
In fact,
as observed by Heywood
[
17
]
, “
it is easy to construct a singular solution of the Navier-Stokes equations that is driven by a singular force. One simply constructs a solenoidal vector field u that begins smoothly and evolves to develop a singularity, and
then defines the force to be the residual.
” Such singular solutions satisfy, usually in a distributional sense, the incompressible Navier-Stokes system

(1.1)

u_{t}+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p=f,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,t>0,

where an external force
f=f(\cdot,t)
is singular for some values of time
t
.
The purpose of this paper is to provide a systematic approach to the study of a large space and time behavior for such singular solutions.
More precisely, we develop mathematical tools which allow us to study asymptotic properties of solutions corresponding to external forces which are either singular finite measures or more general tempered distributions with bounded Fourier transforms.
The following three examples
of singular solutions fall within the scope of this work.

The Slëzkin-Landau solutions

\left\{U^{\beta}(x),P^{\beta}(x)\right\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
satisfy the stationary Navier-Stokes system with the singular external force

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta U^{\beta}+\left(U^{\beta}\cdot\nabla\right)U^{\beta}+\nabla P^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\beta\delta_{0},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot U^{\beta}

\displaystyle=0,

where
\delta_{0}
stands for the Dirac measure supported at the origin and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a constant vector. Such solutions satisfy the system in a weak sense and
in a particular case of
\beta=(\beta_{1},0,0)
, they are
given by the following explicit formulas

(1.3)

\displaystyle U^{\beta}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=2\frac{c|x|^{2}-2x_{1}|x|+cx_{1}^{2}}{|x|(c|x|-x_{1})^{2}},

\displaystyle U^{\beta}_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=2\frac{x_{2}(cx_{1}-|x|)}{|x|(c|x|-x_{1})^{2}},

\displaystyle U^{\beta}_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=2\frac{x_{3}(cx_{1}-|x|)}{|x|(c|x|-x_{1})^{2}},

\displaystyle P^{\beta}(x)

\displaystyle=4\frac{cx_{1}-|x|}{|x|(c|x|-x_{1})^{2}},

where
|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}}
.
The parameter
c
in expressions (
1.3
)
is an arbitrary constant such that
|c|>1
and is related to the parameter

\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
by the formula

(1.4)

\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}(c)=\frac{8\pi c}{3(c^{2}-1)}\Big(2+6c^{2}-3c(c^{2}-1)\log\Big(\frac{c+1}{c-1}\Big)\Big).

Notice that the function
\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}(c)
is decreasing on
(-\infty,-1)
and
(1,+\infty)
. Moreover,
\lim_{c\rightarrow 1}\beta_{1}(c)=+\infty
,
\lim_{c\rightarrow-1}\beta_{1}(c)=-\infty
and
\lim_{|c|\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{1}(c)=0
.

These explicit stationary solutions seem to be first calculated by
Slëzkin
[
44
]
(see Appendix in
[
13
]
for a translation of this paper),
rediscovered by
Landau
[
29
]
, and reported and discussed in other textbooks and papers,
see e.g.
[
30
, p. 82]
,
[
3
, p. 206]
,
[
45
,
10
,
47
]
.
Here, we would like to note that the construction of the Slëzkin-Landau solutions from the work
[
47
]
inspired the authors of
[
10
]
to write their paper.
Recently, Šverák
[
49
]
proved that the Slëzkin-Landau solutions (
1.3
) are the only stationary solutions which are invariant under the natural scaling of the equations, namely under the scaling

(1.5)

u(x)\rightarrow\lambda u(\lambda x)\quad\text{and}\quad p(x)\rightarrow\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x)\quad\text{for}\quad\lambda>0.

A time dependent version
U^{\gamma}=U^{\gamma}(x,t)
of the Slëzkin-Landau solutions (
1.3
)
has been constructed in the paper
[
20
]
by considering
the Cauchy problem

(1.6)

\displaystyle U^{\gamma}_{t}-\Delta U^{\gamma}+(U^{\gamma}\cdot\nabla)U^{\gamma}+\nabla P^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\beta\delta_{\gamma},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,t>0,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot U^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle U^{\gamma}(0)

\displaystyle=0

with constant
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the shifted Dirac delta measure
\delta_{\gamma(t)}=\delta(\cdot-\gamma(t))
, and a Hölder continuous function
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. It was shown in
[
20
]
that
the solutions
U^{\gamma}=U^{\gamma}(x,t)
and
P^{\gamma}=P^{\gamma}(x,t)
to problem (
1.6
) are locally bounded away from the graph of the curve

\Gamma=\{(\gamma(t),t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty)\,:\,t\geqslant 0\}
and are singular along this curve, see Remark
2.7
below for more comments on these solutions.
Moreover, it was proved in the recent paper
[
19
]
that singular solutions of problem (
1.6
) have finite energy, namely,

U^{\gamma}\in C\big([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\big)
.

Another class of singular stationary solutions has been considered recently in a series of papers
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
. Those solutions are invariant under the scaling (
1.5
), they are axisymmetric, and they belong to the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{(x_{1},x_{2})=0\})

with a possible singular ray
\{(x_{1},x_{2})=0\}
. It was shown in the recent preprint
[
37
]
that
such singular solutions satisfy, for a certain range of parameters,
the stationary Navier-Stokes system

(1.7)

\displaystyle-\Delta U+\left(U\cdot\nabla\right)U+\nabla P

\displaystyle=F,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot U

\displaystyle=0,

where
F
is a tempered distribution defined by the formula

(1.8)

F{\varphi}=\left(4\pi c\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log|x_{3}|\partial_{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}-b{\varphi}(0)\right)e_{3}

for every
{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Here,
c,b\in\mathbb{R}
are suitable constants and
e_{3}=(0,0,1)
.
It is well-known that the Fourier transform of the Dirac measure
\delta_{0}\varphi=\varphi(0)
is give by the constant
(2\pi)^{-3/2}

(with the Fourier transform defined as in (
2.6
)). It follows from
Lemma
3.14
below
that the Fourier transform of the distribution
F
defined in (
1.8
) is represented by the bounded function

\widehat{F}(\xi)=\left(4\pi c2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}i\sgn\xi_{3}-b(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)e_{3}.

Cauchy problem in scaling invariant spaces

Before we present the results from this work,
we recall related results on the existence of global-in-time solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for the Navier-Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in scaling invariant spaces.
Here, a Banach space
X
is called scaling invariant if its norm satisfies

\|v\|_{X}=\|\lambda v(\lambda\cdot)\|_{X}
for all
v\in X
and all
\lambda>0
.
There is a rich literature about existence of global-in-time solutions with small initial data in scaling invariant spaces such as homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the Lebesgues space
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the Marcinkiewicz space
L^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
the Besov, Morrey, weak-Morrey, Fourier–Besov, weak-Herz, Fourier–Herz, Besov–Morrey,

BMO^{-1}
, … spaces.
We refer the reader to the review
[
9
]
and to the monograph
[
33
]

for a discussion of those results and for references.
Most of those existence results are proved by using the Kato approach
[
21
]
that consists in applying the Banach fixed point argument in a suitable time-dependent space which norm is composed of two parts, where the usual norm of the persistence space
L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty);X\big)

is supplemented the auxiliary time-weighted norm
\sup_{t>0}t^{\alpha}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{Y}
for another Banach space
Y
.

Navier-Stokes system with external forces

In order to apply the Kato approach to construct small global-in-time solutions to the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
),
one should consider a force
f=f(x,t)
from another Banach space with a norm invariant under the scaling

(1.9)

f_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{3}f(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

Such results are contained, for example, in the paper
[
11
]
, where
f={\rm div}F

with
F\in L^{s}\big((0,\infty),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)

for
2/s+3/q=2
with
2/3<q<\infty
.
More recently, global-in-time solutions have been constructed to this problem in the case of external forces from the Lorentz space
L^{s,\infty}\big((0,\infty),L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)

for
2/s+3/q=3
with
1<q<\infty
(
[
26
]
),
from time-weighted Besov spaces (
[
27
]
),
and from the homogeneous Besov space with both negative and positive differential orders
(
[
25
]
).

The Kato two-norm approach cannot be applied to solutions which do not decay as
t\to\infty
, for example, in the case of the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
) with a time independent external force. Here, global-in-time solutions corresponding to small initial conditions and small external forces should be constructed in a scaling invariant space

L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty),X\big)
,
where only one natural norm
\sup_{t>0}\|\cdot\|_{X}

is involved.
To the best of our knowledge,
the Marcinkiewicz space
L^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(
[
39
,
50
]
),
the space
{\mathcal{PM}}^{2}
(
[
31
,
10
]
),
the Fourier-Besov space
F\dot{B}^{2-\frac{3}{p}}_{p,\infty}
with
p>3
(
[
23
]
),
the weak-Herz spaces (
[
48
]
),
and the weak Morrey space
{\mathcal{M}}_{p,\infty,3-p}
with
p\in(2,3]
(
[
32
]
)
are the only spaces where such an one norm approach is possible.
In general, solutions obtained in this setting are only time-weakly continuous at
t=0

because of the lack of strong continuity at
t=0
of the heat semigroup

\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}=\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
X
.

In this work, we choose an approach from
[
10
]
where small solutions of the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
) have been constructed in the space
{\mathcal{PM}}^{2}
. We are convinced, however, that our ideas presented in this work can be extended to solutions from above mentioned spaces, where one norm approach to construct global-in-time solutions is possible.

Stationary solutions

In this work, we deal also with stationary solutions satisfying the system

(w\cdot\nabla)w-\Delta w+\nabla p=g,\quad\nabla\cdot w=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where the existence and stability of solutions have been studied in several works either in the
case of bounded domains or exterior domains, see
e.g.
the monograph
[
14
]
.
Here, we contribute to the theory on
the existence and the stability of solutions to this stationary system
considered in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
with a singular and sufficiently small (in a suitable sense) external force, see
e.g.

[
43
,
28
,
4
,
5
,
12
,
42
]
and references therein.

Summary of results from this work

As starting point in this work, we consider
a solution
u\in C_{w}\big((0,\infty);\mathcal{PM}^{2}\big)

to the Cauchy problem for the three dimensional Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
)
with a possibly singular external force
f\in C_{w}\big((0,\infty);{\mathcal{PM}}^{0}\big)
, see below for definitions of these spaces.
Such solutions are constructed
in Propositions
2.1

in the case of the Cauchy problem
and in Proposition
2.8
in the case of stationary solutions
which we recall from
[
10
]
.
The use of these spaces allows us to simplify several calculations of this work and we can deal with external forces which are tempered distributions with bounded Fourier transforms.
In Theorem
2.3
, we show that every solution
from
C_{w}\big((0,\infty);{\mathcal{PM}}^{2}\big)

behaves, for large values of
|x|
(which is measured by the Lebesgue norm
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
q\in[2,3)
)
as a solution to the heat equation.
In Theorem
2.10
, we consider stationary solutions and we show their stability in the norms of
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with some
q<3
.
A general asymptotic stability result of solutions to the Cauchy problem for system
(
1.1
) is presented in Theorem
2.13
.
In remarks, we explain how our theorems are related to previous results and, especially, we emphasize how they can be applied to singular solutions discussed above.

2.
Results and comments

We consider mild solutions to the Cauchy problem

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p

\displaystyle=f,\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x).

Namely, applying the Duhamel principle, we deal with the corresponding integral equation

(2.2)

u(t)=S(t)u_{0}-B(u,u)(t)+F(t)

with the heat semigroup

(2.3)

S(t)u_{0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}\textrm{exp}\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)u_{0}(y)\,{\rm d}y,

the bilinear form

(2.4)

B(u,v)(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}\nabla S(t-\tau)\cdot\big(u(\tau)\otimes v(\tau)\big)\,{\rm d}\tau,

and the term obtained from the external force

(2.5)

F(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\mathbb{P}f(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

The Leray projector
\mathbb{P}
in formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.5
)
is defined as the Fourier multiplier
with the matrix component symbol
(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi))_{j,k}=\delta_{jk}-{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}/{|\xi|^{2}}
satisfying the obvious inequalities
|(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi))_{j,k}|\leqslant 1
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and all
j,k\in\{1,2,3\}
. Moreover, for two vector fields
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, we introduce their tensor product

u\otimes v
which is the
3\times 3
matrix whose
(\ell,k)
entry is
u_{\ell}v_{k}
. Here and in what follows the Fourier transform of an integrable function
f
is given by

(2.6)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,{\rm d}x.

The following functional spaces
have been introduced in the work
[
10
]
and
are systematically used in this paper

(2.7)

\mathcal{PM}^{a}\equiv\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\widehat{u}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\|u\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{a}}=\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{a}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|<\infty\},

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
is a given parameter.
We also consider the counterparts of these spaces for time dependent tempered distributions

\mathcal{X}^{a}\equiv C_{w}\big([0,\infty),\mathcal{PM}^{a}\big)\quad\text{with the norm}\quad\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{a}}=\esssup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|u(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{a}}.

Here, the symbol
C_{w}
denotes distributions
u(\cdot,t)
weakly continuous in time
which means that the mappings
t\mapsto\langle u(t),{\varphi}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
are continuous for each
{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(the usual Schwartz class of test functions).

First, we recall a result on the existence of small solutions to the integral equation (
2.2
) in the space
\mathcal{PM}^{2}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
10
, Thm. 4.1]
)
.

There is
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}<\varepsilon
and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}<\varepsilon
then there exists a global-in-time mild solution
u\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
to the Cauchy problem for the Navier-Stokes system (
2.1
). Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that this solution satisfies

\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\leqslant C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}\big)
.

This proposition is obtained from the Banach contracted principle
applied to the “quadratic” equation (
2.2
). We recall that reasoning in the beginning of Section
4
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let us comment the functional spaces used in Proposition
2.1
. We consider external forces
f=f(\cdot,t)

which are time
dependent tempered distributions
satisfying
\esssup_{t>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{f}(\xi,t)|<\infty

in order to obtain solutions in the space
\mathcal{C}_{w}\big([0,\infty),\mathcal{PM}^{2}\big)
.
However,
due to the embedding
\mathcal{PM}^{2}\subset L^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(see Lemma
3.1
below)
as well as the
the
embedding
into the homogeneous Besov space

\mathcal{PM}^{2}\subset\dot{B}^{-1+3/p,\infty}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
p>3
.
(see
[
10
, Lemma 7.1]
),
solutions from Proposition
2.1

agree with mild solutions of problem (
2.1
) constructed in other scaling invariant spaces and discussed in several works, see the review
[
9
]

and the monograph
[
33
]

for other references.

Our first result of this work states that a behavior of mild solutions
to problem (
2.1
)
obtained in Proposition
2.1

is determined in the scale of
L^{p}
-spaces
by the quantity
S(t)u_{0}+F(t)
.
Notice that we do not impose any smallness assumption in Theorem
2.3
below neither on initial data nor external forces.

Theorem 2.3
.

Every mild solution
u\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
of problem (
2.1
) corresponding to an initial datum

u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
and an external force
f\in\mathcal{X}^{0}
satisfies

u(t)-S(t)u_{0}-F(t)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for each
q\in\left[2,3\right)
and all
t>0
.
Moreover, for every
q\in\left[2,3\right)
there exists a constant
C=C(q)>0
such that

(2.8)

\|u(t)-S(t)u_{0}-F(t)\|_{q}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{3-q}{2q}}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

Remark 2.4
.

In fact, in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we show the following more general estimate valid for each
b\in\left[0,2\right]
:

(2.9)

\|u(t)-S(t)u_{0}-F(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

Then, the
L^{q}
-estimate (
2.8
) is obtained from inequality (
2.9
) with
b\in\{0,2\}
combined with the
interpolation inequality from Lemma
3.2
which requires
q\geq 2
.
On the other hand, inequality (
2.9
) with
b\in[0,1)
may be considered as a counterpart of estimate (
2.8
) with
q\in[1,2)
.

Remark 2.5
.

We prove below in Corollary
3.12
that, for each
f\in{\mathcal{X}}^{0}
, we have

F(t)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
q\in[2,3)
and all
t>0
.
On the other hand, in general,
S(t)u_{0}\notin L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
q\in[2,3)

if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
is homogeneous of degree
-1
, see
e.g.

[
8
]
.
Thus, Theorem
2.3
states that a behavior of solutions of problem (
2.1
) with a singular initial datum from
\mathcal{PM}^{2}
and with a singular external force from
{\mathcal{X}}^{0}
is determined
for large values of
|x|

only by
S(t)u_{0}
at the first approximation, see
e.g.

[
7
,
6
,
8
]
for analogous results in the case of
f=0
.
The external force
f=f(\cdot,t)
will appear in a higher order far field asymptotic expansion of solutions which was already observed in the work
[
1
,
2
]
in the case of more regular initial conditions and external forces.

Remark 2.6
.

Recall that
S(t)u_{0}\in C\big([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\big)
for each
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
. Thus,
Theorem
2.3
implies that
in the case of arbitrary
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\cap\mathcal{PM}^{2}
and possibly singular
f\in{\mathcal{X}}^{0}
,
the corresponding solution has a finite energy, namely,
u(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
for each
t\geqslant 0
.
Such a finite energy result was recently obtained in
[
19
]
in the particular case of
singular solutions to problem (
1.6
).

Remark 2.7
.

By methods from this work,
it is also possible to study asymptotic properties of solutions
of problem (
2.1
) in the
L^{q}
-spaces with

q>3
. Here, however, we obtain an asymptotic profile of a solution around its singular points as, for example,
in the work
[
20
]
where singularities of solutions to problem (
1.6
) have been studied.
Assuming
\gamma
to be Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, the paper
[
20
]
compares the solution
(U^{\gamma},P^{\gamma})
of problem (
1.6
) to the Slëzkin-Landau solutions made time dependent by translating the origin to
\gamma(t)
proving that for all
t>0
,

U^{\gamma}(t)-U^{c}(\cdot-\gamma(t))\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\qquad\text{for}\quad 3<q<\frac{3}{2(1-\alpha)}

and

P^{\gamma}(t)-P^{c}(\cdot-\gamma(t))\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\qquad\text{for}\quad\frac{3}{2}<q<\frac{3}{3-2\alpha}.

An analogous result describing
singularities of very weak stationary solutions of the Navier-Stokes system
was obtained in
[
40
]
.
We study this case in Theorem
2.10
below devoted to the stationary Navier-Stokes system.

Next, we present
a counterpart of Theorem
2.3
in the case of
a solution
w=w(x)
to the stationary Navier-Stokes system

(2.10)

(w\cdot\nabla)w-\Delta w+\nabla p=g,\quad\nabla\cdot w=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

with a given external force
g=g(x)
.
In Section
3
,
we define (via the Fourier transform) the bilinear operator

(2.11)

B_{E}(w,v)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathbb{P}\nabla S(t-\tau)\cdot\big(w\otimes v\big)\,{\rm d}\tau

and

(2.12)

G\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{t}S(t-\tau)\mathbb{P}g\,{\rm d}\tau

which allow us to formulate
system (
2.10
) (see e.g.
[
50
, Thm. 1.1]
,
[
10
, Prop. 6.1]
)
in the formally equivalent form

(2.13)

w=-B_{E}(w,w)+G.

As in the case of the Cauchy problem (
2.1
), we study properties of solutions to integral equation (
2.13
) which we also call as mild solutions to system (
2.10
).
The following result recalled from
[
10
]
gives an existence of mild stationary solutions in the space
\mathcal{PM}^{2}
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
10
, Thm. 6.1]
)
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, for every
g\in\mathcal{PM}^{0}
, if
\|g\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}<\varepsilon
then system (
2.10
) has a solution
w\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that this solution satisfies the inequality

\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\leqslant C\|g\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let us quote other related works where small solutions to the stationary Navier-Stokes system (
2.10
) have been constructed for sufficiently small and possibly rough external forces.
In
[
28
]
, an external force belongs to

\mathcal{M}^{-2}_{3,r}
,

r\in(2,3]
, which is the Sobolev-type space based on the Morrey space.
In
[
4
]
,
\Delta^{-1}\mathbb{P}g\in L^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
In the work
[
42
]
,
\Delta^{-1}g\in\mathcal{V}^{1,2}
– a suitable capacity space which can be embedded into the homogeneous Morrey space
\mathcal{M}^{2,2}
.
The authors of
[
12
]
deal with
g=\phi g_{0}+g_{1}
, where
g_{0}
is homogeneous of degree
-3
,
g_{1}
is bounded and decays sufficiently fast at infinity and
\phi
is a smooth cut-off function such that
\phi(x)=0
if
|x|\leqslant 1/2
.

Our next goal is show a certain kind of stability of stationary solutions
from Proposition
2.8

in the class of stationary solutions.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be as in Proposition
2.8

and consider solutions
w_{i}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
to stationary system
(
2.10
) corresponding to external forces
g_{i}\in\mathcal{PM}^{0}
satisfying
\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}<\varepsilon
.
Assume that
g_{1}-g_{2}\in\mathcal{PM}^{b-2}

for some
b\in\left(1,3\right)
.
There exists
\tilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon]
such that if

\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}\leqslant\tilde{\varepsilon}

then
w_{1}-w_{2}\in\mathcal{PM}^{b}
.
Moreover,

(1)

if
b\in\left(1,2\right)
,
then

w_{1}-w_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for each

q\in\left(\tfrac{3}{3-b},3\right)

such that
q\geqslant 2
;

(2)

if
b\in(2,3)
, then
w_{1}-w_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

for each

q\in\left(3,\tfrac{3}{3-b}\right)
.

In both case, the following inequality holds true

(2.14)

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{q}\leqslant C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}^{1-\frac{q-3}{q(b-2)}}\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b-2}}^{\frac{q-3}{q(b-2)}}

for a constant
C>0
.

Remark 2.11
.

Let us illustrate how to use Theorem
2.10

in the case of
b\in(1,2)

to study a far field asymptotic behavior of stationary solutions.
Consider

(2.15)

g_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\subset\mathcal{PM}^{0}\quad\text{satisfying}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2-b}|g_{1}(x)|\,{\rm d}x<\infty

for some
b\in(1,2)
and

g_{2}=\beta\delta_{0}\quad\text{with}\quad\beta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

More generally, we can choose
g_{1}
as a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a finite moment of order
2-b
.
For these two external forces, assumption

g_{1}-g_{2}\in\mathcal{PM}^{b-2}

is satisfied
by Proposition
3.5
, below.
Thus, by Theorem
2.10
, under a suitable smallness assumption, the corresponding solutions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}

of the stationary system (
2.10
)
satisfy
inequality (
2.14
).
In the particular case of
\beta=(\beta_{1},0,0)
, the solution
w_{2}
agrees with the explicit Slëzkin-Landau solution (
1.3
) which is not integrable with the power
q\leqslant 3
for large values of
|x|
.
Hence, Theorem
2.10
states that a far field asymptotic behavior of stationary solutions corresponding to integrable external forces satisfying (
2.15
)
is described by the corresponding Slëzkin-Landau solutions.
Similar results have been already obtained
for stationary solutions to the Navier-Stokes system
on an exterior domain in
[
24
,
18
]
and
on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in
[
12
]
.
In Theorem
2.10
, we generalize all those results by showing
that
assumption
g_{1}-g_{2}\in\mathcal{PM}^{b-2}
for some
b\in(1,2)

determines when two external forces from the space of pseudomeasure

\mathcal{PM}^{0}

(
i.e.
tempered distributions with bounded Fourier transforms)
lead to stationary solutions with the same asymptotic behavior as
|x|\to\infty
.

Remark 2.12
.

On the other hand, Theorem
2.10
for
b\in(2,3)

allows us to study local singularities of stationary solutions.
As an example, we consider again the the Slëzkin-Landau solution
U^{\beta}

with a singularity at the origin which is not integrable with every exponent
q>3
. By Theorem
2.10
, every solution
w_{2}
with a small external force
g_{2}\in\mathcal{PM}^{0}
such that

(2.16)

\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{b-2}\big|\beta(2\pi)^{-3/2}-\widehat{g}_{2}(\xi)\big|<\infty

has a singularity at the origin which asymptotically resembles the singularity of
U^{\beta}
and this is measured by the
L^{q}
-norm with some
q>3
.
Note that, since
b\in(2,3)
,
the assumption (
2.16
) requires from the bounded function
\widehat{g}_{2}(\xi)
to be close to the constant vector

\beta(2\pi)^{-3/2}=\beta\widehat{\delta_{0}}
for large values of
|\xi|
.

Finally, using the framework introduced above, we study an asymptotic stability of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
corresponding to singular external forces.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
u_{i}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
be solutions to the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
with initial data
u_{0i}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
and external forces
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{0}

such that
\|u_{0i}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}<\varepsilon
and
\|f_{i}\|_{{\mathcal{X}}^{0}}<\varepsilon
,
where
\varepsilon>0
is provided by Proposition
2.1
.
Suppose that there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

(2.17)

\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|f_{1}(t)-f_{2}(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{\delta}}<\infty.

Then, for every
q\in\left(3,\frac{3}{1-\delta}\right)
there exists a constant
C=C(q)>0
such that

(2.18)

\|u_{1}(\cdot,t)-u_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{q}\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2q}}\qquad\textit{for all}\quad t>0.

If, moreover,

(2.19)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|f_{1}(t)-f_{2}(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\|S(t)\big(u_{01}-u_{02}\big)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}=0

then

(2.20)

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2q}}\|u_{1}(\cdot,t)-u_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{q}=0.

Remark 2.14
.

In the particular case of a (sufficiently small) time independent external force
f(\cdot,t)=g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, Theorem
2.13

states that each solution
u=u(x,t)
of the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
with an (sufficiently small) initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}

converges as
t\to\infty
towards a stationary solution satisfying system
(
2.10
) and this convergence holds true in the
L^{q}
-norm for each
q\in(3,\infty)
(notice that condition (
2.17
) is fulfiled for each
\delta\in(0,1)
if
f_{1}=f_{2}
).
Such an asymptotic stability result for Leray stationary solutions was obtained in
[
5
]
.
We do not require from solutions to have a finite energy and our approach resembles the one used in
[
15
]
.

Remark 2.15
.

One should be careful with using the stability result from Theorem
2.13
in the case of singular external forces.
Indeed, if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
is homogeneous of degree
-2
and
f\in{\mathcal{X}}^{0}
is invariant under the scaling (
1.9
), then the corresponding solution is self-similar
[
10
, Corollary 4.1]
:

u(x,t)=t^{-1/2}u\left(xt^{-1/2},1\right)

for all
t>0
.
Thus, if moreover,
u(\cdot,1)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
then

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{q}=t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2q}}\|u(\cdot,1)\|_{q}\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t>0.

In such a case, inequality (
2.18
) does not say anything about the asymptotic behavior of solutions.
However, in the particular case of
f=\beta_{1}e_{1}\delta_{0}
the corresponding stationary solution
U^{\beta}
is explicit (
1.3
), homogeneous of degree
-1
and self-similar. Moreover,

U^{\beta}\notin L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for each
q\in[1,\infty]
.
In this case, inequality (
2.18
) states that each solution
u=u(x,t)
of the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
with an (sufficiently small) initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}

and with the external force
f=\beta_{1}e_{1}\delta_{0}

has a singularity at zero of the same shape as the Slëzkin-Landau solution
U^{\beta_{1}}
. This approach was used in
[
20
]
in the study of singularities of solutions to problem (
1.6
).
Thus, additional assumptions (
2.19
) are needed in the study of a large time behavior of singular solutions.

Remark 2.16
.

Asymptotic results in Theorem
2.13
cannot be true for
any
q<3
due to the estimates in Theorem
2.3
.

3.
Review of estimates in
\mathcal{PM}^{a}
-spaces

First, we gather estimates which involve
\mathcal{PM}^{a}
-norms and which
play a crucial role in the proofs of results from Section
2
. Some of the following results can be found in the previous works
[
10
,
20
,
19
]
. For a completeness of the exposition, we recall them in a form, which will be convenient in the proofs of results from this paper.

3.1.
Embedding theorems

The following three lemmas state that tempered distributions from the space
\mathcal{PM}^{a}
for a certain range of the exponent
a>0
, are in fact locally integrable functions.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following embedding holds true

\mathcal{PM}^{a}\subset L^{\frac{3}{3-a},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\text{for each}\quad a\in\left(\tfrac{3}{2},3\right),

where
L^{\frac{3}{3-a},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the weak
L^{p}
-space (the Marcinkiewicz space).

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathcal{PM}^{a}
for some
a\in\left(\frac{3}{2},3\right)
and let
{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be an arbitrary function. Properties of the Fourier transform combined with the Hölder inequality in the Lorentz spaces (see e.g.
[
41
]
) yield

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{w}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{{\varphi}}(\xi)}\,{\rm d}\xi\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|w\|_{{\mathcal{PM}}^{a}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{-a}\big|\widehat{{\varphi}}(\xi)\big|\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant\|w\|_{{\mathcal{PM}}^{a}}\||\cdot|^{-a}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a},\infty}}\|\widehat{{\varphi}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{3-a},1}}.

Now, it suffices to apply the Hausdorff-Young inequality in the Lorentz spaces
[
22
]

\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},r}}\leqslant C\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{p,r}},\quad\textrm{where}\ \ 1<p<2,\ 1\leqslant r<\infty,\quad\textrm{and }\ \ \tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\tfrac{1}{p}=1,

to obtain

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w{\varphi}\,{\rm d}x\bigg|\leqslant C\|w\|_{{\mathcal{PM}}^{a}}\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a},1}}

for all
{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and, by a density argument, for all
{\varphi}\in L^{\frac{3}{a},1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Hence, the distribution
w
defines a continuous linear functional on

L^{\frac{3}{a},1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, consequently,
w\in L^{\frac{3}{3-a},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see e.g.
[
16
, Thm. 1.4.17]
).

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
b\in\left[0,2\right)
and
q\in\left(\frac{3}{3-b},3\right)
such that
q\geqslant 2
,
the following embedding holds true

\mathcal{PM}^{2}\cap\mathcal{PM}^{b}\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(b,q)
such that

(3.1)

\|w\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}^{1-\frac{q-3}{q(b-2)}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}^{\frac{q-3}{q(b-2)}}.

Proof.

For each couple
(q,r)
of positive numbers satisfying

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1\quad\text{with }\quad q\geqslant 2\quad\text{and}\quad q\in\left(\frac{3}{3-b},3\right),

we obtain the inequalities
2r>3
and
br<3
.
Thus, by the classical Hausdorff-Young inequality with these exponents,
we obtain

(3.2)

\begin{split}\|w\|_{L^{q}}^{r}\leqslant&C\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{r}}^{r}\\
=&C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{w}(\xi)|^{r}\,{\rm d}\xi\\
=&C\int_{|\xi|\leqslant K}|\widehat{w}(\xi)|^{r}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\int_{|\xi|\geqslant K}|\widehat{w}(\xi)|^{r}\,{\rm d}\xi\\
\leqslant&C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}^{r}\int_{|\xi|\leqslant K}|\xi|^{-br}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}^{r}\int_{|\xi|\geqslant K}|\xi|^{-2r}\,{\rm d}\xi\\
\leqslant&C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}^{r}K^{3-br}+C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}^{r}K^{3-2r}.\end{split}

Now, we choose

K=\left(\frac{\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}}{\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{b-2}}\quad\text{and}\quad r=\frac{q}{q-1}

to obtain inequality (
3.1
).

\square

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that if
b\in\left[0,3/2\right)
and
q\in\left(\frac{3}{3-b},2\right)
, the proof of Lemma
3.2
implies only that each

w\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}\cap\mathcal{PM}^{b}
satisfies

\widehat{w}\in L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each
\delta\in(0,1)
and
q\in\left(3,\frac{3}{1-\delta}\right)
,
the following embedding holds true

\mathcal{PM}^{2}\cap\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(\delta,q)
such that

(3.3)

\|w\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}^{1-\frac{q-3}{\delta q}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}^{\frac{q-3}{\delta q}}.

Proof.

Here, one should proceed analogously as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
and the only difference consists in a
suitable modification of exponents
in inequality (
3.2
); see also
[
10
, Lemma 7.4]
and
[
20
, Lemma 3.6]
for analogous calculations.

\square

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
b\in(1,2)
.
For every
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2-b}|g(x)|\,{\rm d}x<\infty
and for
\beta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(x)\,{\rm d}x
we have the estimate

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{b-2}\big|\widehat{g}(\xi)-\beta\big|\leqslant C(b)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2-b}|g(x)|\,{\rm d}x

with a constant
C(b)>0
independent of
g
.

Proof.

The estimate is immediate in the case
b=1
. Indeed, since
\beta=\widehat{g}(0)
, by the mean value theorem and properties of the Fourier transform, we obtain

|\xi|^{-1}|\widehat{g}(\xi)-\widehat{g}(0)|\leqslant\|\nabla\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leqslant(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x||g(x)|\,{\rm d}x.

In a more general case, we use the well-known formula (see
e.g.

[
46
, Ch.V, Lemma 2]
)

\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2-b}}=C(b)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\frac{1}{|x|^{1+b}}\,{\rm d}x

valid for every
b\in(-1,2)
and a constant
C(b)>0
.
Thus

\begin{split}|\xi|^{b-2}\big|\widehat{g}(\xi)-\beta\big|&=C(b)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+b}}-\frac{1}{|x|^{1+b}}\right)g(y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\right|\\
&\leqslant C(b)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+b}}-\frac{1}{|x|^{1+b}}\right|\,{\rm d}x|g(y)|\,{\rm d}y.\end{split}

Note that the integral with respect to
x
is finite for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
because its integrand
\big||x-y|^{-1-b}-|x|^{-1-b}\big|
is locally integrable and behaves like
|x|^{-2-b}
when
|x|\to\infty
(here, the assumption
b\in(1,2)
is crucial). Hence, by the change of variables
x=\omega|y|
, it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+b}}-\frac{1}{|x|^{1+b}}\right|\,{\rm d}x=|y|^{2-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\frac{1}{|\omega-y/|y||^{1+b}}-\frac{1}{|\omega|^{1+b}}\right|\,{\rm d}\omega.

Finally, for
b\in(1,2)
, we have got

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|{|\omega-y/|y||^{-1-b}}-{|\omega|^{-1-b}}\right|\,{\rm d}\omega<\infty

which completes the proof of the proposition.

\square

3.2.
Estimates of the heat semigroup

First, we recall properties of the heat semigroup (
2.3
) which satisfies the well-known formula

(3.4)

\widehat{S(t)u_{0}}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi).

Lemma 3.6
.

For each
\delta\geqslant 0
and for every
u_{0}\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
there exists a constant
C=C(\delta)>0
such that the following estimate holds true

(3.5)

\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\quad\textit{for all}\quad t>0.

Proof.

It follows from equation (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2+\delta}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}t^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\sqrt{t}\xi|^{\delta}e^{-|\sqrt{t}\xi|^{2}}

\displaystyle=C(\delta)t^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}

since the function
|\sqrt{t}\xi|^{\delta}e^{-|\sqrt{t}\xi|^{2}}
is bounded
for each
\delta\geqslant 0

with respect to
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
t\geqslant 0
.

\square

3.3.
Estimates of bilinear forms

Next, we gather estimates of the bilinear forms
B(\cdot,\cdot)
from (
2.4
) and
B_{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in (
2.11
), where both quantities should be defined via the Fourier transform as follows. For time dependent tempered distributions
u(\cdot,t)
and
v(\cdot,t)
we put

(3.6)

\widehat{B(u,v)}(\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}i\xi\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{u}(\eta,\tau)\otimes\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta,\tau)\,{\rm d}\eta\,{\rm d}\tau.

and for time independent
w
and
z
, we set

(3.7)

\begin{split}\widehat{B_{E}(w,z)}(\xi)&=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}i\xi\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{w}(\eta)\otimes\widehat{z}(\xi-\eta)\,{\rm d}\eta\,{\rm d}\tau\\
&=\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{w}(\eta)\otimes\widehat{z}(\xi-\eta)\,{\rm d}\eta,\end{split}

where we use the simple formula

\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}s=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}\qquad\text{for each}\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}

to obtain the second equality in (
3.7
). Below, we estimate both bilinear forms in the norms of
\mathcal{PM}^{a}
-spaces using the equation
(see
e.g

[
46
, Ch. V, Sec. 1]
)

(3.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{b}}\,{\rm d}\eta=\frac{C(b)}{|\xi|^{b-1}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}

which holds true for every
b\in(1,3)
and a constant
C(b)>0
independent of
\xi
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exist constants
\eta>0
and
\eta_{E}>0
such that for all
u,v\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
and
w,z\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}

(3.9)

\|B(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\leqslant\eta\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}

and

(3.10)

\|B_{E}(w,z)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\leqslant\eta_{E}\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|z\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}

Proof.

Both inequalities are an immediate consequence of equation (
3.8
) with
b=2
and a slightly more general reasoning is used in the next proof below.

\square

Lemma 3.8
.

For each
b\in\left[0,2\right]
there exists a constant
C=C(b)>0
such that the following inequality holds true for all
u\in\mathcal{X}^{2}

(3.11)

\|B(u,u)(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}\qquad\textit{for all}\quad t>0.

Proof.

By equation (
3.8
), we have got the following estimate

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{u}(\eta,\tau)\otimes\widehat{u}(\xi-\eta,\tau)\,{\rm d}\eta\right|\leqslant\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\eta=\frac{C}{|\xi|}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}

which by formula (
3.6
) implies

\displaystyle\|B(u,u)(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{b}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=C\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2-b}}=Ct^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}^{2}

for all
t>0
.

\square

Lemma 3.9
.

For each
b\in\left(1,3\right)
there exists a constant
C=C(b)>0
such that for all
w\in\mathcal{PM}^{2}
and
z\in\mathcal{PM}^{b}
we have got the inequality

(3.13)

\|B_{E}(w,z)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}\leqslant C(b)\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|z\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}.

Proof.

By equation (
3.8
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{w}(\eta,\tau)\otimes\widehat{z}(\xi-\eta,\tau)\,{\rm d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|z\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{b}}\,{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|\xi|^{b-1}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|z\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}

which, by the formula on the right hand side of
equation (
3.7
), leads to inequality (
3.13
).
\square

Lemma 3.10
.

For each
\delta\in[0,1)
there exists a constant
C=C(\delta)>0
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{X}^{2}
and
v(t)\in\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}
such that
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}<\infty
, we have got

\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|B(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}.

Proof.

By equation (
3.8
) with
b=2+\delta
, we have got the following estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{u}(\eta,\tau)\otimes\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta,\tau)\,{\rm d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|v(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{2+\delta}}\,{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{|\xi|^{1+\delta}}\|u(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}\|v(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}

which, by formula (
3.6
), implies

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|B(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau

for all
t>0
. To complete the proof, we show that the quantity

t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau

is bounded by a constant independent of
\xi
and
t
. Indeed, we split the integral with respect to
\tau
into two parts and we begin with

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\leqslant t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\xi|^{2}e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau=C(\delta)\left|\sqrt{t}\xi\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|\sqrt{t}\xi|^{2}}{2}},

where the function on the right-hand side is bounded for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
t>0
.

Next, we deal with

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\xi|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\leqslant C(\delta)\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\xi|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau=C(\delta)\left(1-e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\right)\leqslant C(\delta)

which completes the proof.

\square

3.4.
Estimates of external forces

Next, we are going to estimated the quantities which depend on external forces: the term
F=F(x,t)
defined in (
2.5
) and
the term
G=G(x,t)
given by formula (

\widehat{F}(\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau

and

(3.15)

\widehat{G}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\tau=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi).

Lemma 3.11
.

For each
b\in\left[0,2\right]
there exists a constant
C=C(b)>0
such that for each
f\in\mathcal{X}^{0}
the following inequality holds true

(3.16)

\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}\leqslant C(b)t^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}\qquad\textit{for all}\quad t>0.

Proof.

The reasoning is analogous to that in the proof of
Lemma
3.8
. Using the definition of
F(t)
in (
3.14
) and of the norm in
\mathcal{PM}^{b}
in (
2.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{b}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{b}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)||\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau)|\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1-e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2-b}}=C(b)t^{\frac{2-b}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}.

\square

Corollary 3.12
.

For each
q\in\left[2,3\right)
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each
f\in\mathcal{X}^{0}
the following inequality holds true

(3.17)

\|F(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{3-q}{2q}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}\qquad\textit{for all}\quad t>0.

Proof.

We combine estimate (
3.16
) for
b=2
and for
b=0
with
the interpolation inequality from Lemma
3.2

in order to obtain the following estimate for each
q\in[2,3)

\displaystyle\|F(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2}}^{1-\frac{3-q}{2q}}\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{0}}^{\frac{3-q}{2q}}\leqslant Ct^{\frac{3-q}{2q}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}^{0}}

for all
t>0
.

\square

Lemma 3.13
.

For each
\delta\in[0,1)
there exists a constant
C(\delta)>0
such that for every
f=f(\cdot,t)

satisfying

\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|f(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{\delta}}<\infty

we obtain

\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\mathbb{P}f(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau\right\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\leqslant C(\delta)\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|f(t)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{\delta}}.

Proof.

By the definition of the
\mathcal{PM}^{\delta+2}
-norm, we have got the inequality

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\mathbb{P}f(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau\right\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{2+\delta}}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|f(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{PM}^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\times\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)|\xi|^{2}}\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau,

where the second factor on the right-hand side is finite by the same argument as the one used in the proof of Lemma
3.10
.

\square

Lemma 3.14
.

For every
{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we define

(3.18)

T{\varphi}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log|x_{3}|\partial_{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}.

Then
T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

(3.19)

\widehat{T}(\xi)=2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}i\sgn\xi_{3}.

Proof.

We repeat the well-known calculations from the study of the principal value distribution (see
e.g.

[
38
, Ch.3]
). First, we show that
T
defined by formula (
3.18
) is a tempered distribution. Indeed, for
{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle|T{\varphi}|

\displaystyle=\left|\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x_{3}|>\varepsilon}\log|x_{3}|\partial_{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x_{3}|>\varepsilon}\frac{1}{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\big(\log|\varepsilon|\left({\varphi}(0,0,-\varepsilon)-{\varphi}(0,0,\varepsilon)\right)\big)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\varepsilon<|x_{3}|<1}\frac{1}{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}+\int_{|x_{3}|\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{x_{3}}{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\varphi}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\int_{|x_{3}|\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{|x_{3}|}|{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})|\,{\rm d}x_{3}<\infty,

because the boundary term, which result from integration by parts, tends to zero as
\varepsilon
goes to zero. Since
i\pi\sgn\xi_{3}
is a Fourier transform of the one-dimensional principal value distribution
-\frac{1}{x_{3}}
(see
e.g.

[
38
, Ch.3]
) applying the three-dimensional definition of the Fourier transform, we obtain

\displaystyle T\widehat{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x_{3}|>\varepsilon}\frac{1}{x_{3}}\widehat{\varphi}(0,0,x_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}=-(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|x_{3}|>\varepsilon}\frac{1}{x_{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}{\varphi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\,{\rm d}x_{3}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}i\pi\sgn\xi_{3}{\varphi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\,{\rm d}\xi.

This proves formula (
3.19
).

\square

4.
Proofs of results from Section
2

Solutions to integral equations (
2.2
) and (
2.13
) are obtained from the Banach fixed point theorem which, in the case of “quadratic” equations, is often reformulated as in the following lemma. We skip its elementary proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space,
L:\,X\to X
be a linear bounded operator such that for a constant
\lambda\in[0,1)
, we have

\|L(x)\|\leqslant\lambda\|x\|
for all
x\in X,

and
B:X\times X\to X
be a bilinear mapping such that

\|B(x_{1},x_{2})\|\leqslant\eta\|x_{1}\|\,\|x_{2}\|\quad\text{for every}\quad x_{1},\,x_{2}\in X

for some constant
\eta>0
. Then, for every
y\in X
satisfying
4\eta\|y\|<(1-\lambda)^{2}
, the equation

(4.1)

x=y+L(x)+B(x,x)

has a solution
x\in X
. In particular, this solution satisfies
\|x\|\leqslant\frac{2\|y\|}{1-\lambda}
,
and it is the only one among all solutions satisfying
\|x\|<\frac{1-\lambda}{2\eta}
.

Results from Section
2
are obtained immediately from estimates in Section
3
.

with

\|F\|_{{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}\leqslant 2\|f\|_{{\mathcal{X}}^{0}}.

Thus,
in order to construct a solution to equation (

it suffices to apply Lemma
4.1
together with estimate (
3.9
).

\square

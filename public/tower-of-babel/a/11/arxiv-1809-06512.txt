Abstract
. In the present paper, we derive the third-order differential subordination and superordination results for some analytic univalent functions defined in the unit disc. These results are associated with generalized Struve functions and are obtained by considering suitable classes of admissible functions. As a consequence, the dual problems which yield the sandwich type relations are presented.

1.2.
Basic facts on differential subordination

In order to achieve our aim in this section, we recall some definitions and preliminary results from the theory of differential subordination and superordination.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are in
\mathcal{H}
. We say that
f
is
subordinate
to
g
, (or
g
is

superordinate
to
f
), write as
f\prec g~\text{in}~\mathcal{U}~\text{ or}~f(z)\prec g(z)\quad(z\in\mathcal{U}),
if there exists a function
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
, satisfying the conditions of the Schwarz lemma

(~\text{i.e.}~\omega(0)=0
and
|\omega(z)|<1)
such that

f(z)=g(\omega(z))\quad(z\in\mathcal{U}).
It follows that

f(z)\prec g(z)\;(z\in\mathcal{U})\Longrightarrow f(0)=g(0)~\text{and}~f(\mathcal{U})\subset g(\mathcal{U}).
In particular, if
g
is
univalent
in
\mathcal{U}
, then
the reverse implication also holds (cf.
[
25
]
).

Definition 1
.

[
[
5
]
, Definition 1, p.440].
Let
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{4}\times\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and the function
h(z)
be univalent in
\mathcal{U}.
If the function
p(z)
is analytic in
\mathcal{U}
and satisfies the following third-order differential subordination

(1.13)

\psi(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z),z^{2}p^{\prime\prime}(z),z^{3}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z);z)\prec h(z),

then
p(z)
is called a
solution
of the differential subordination.

A univalent function
q(z)
is called a
dominant
of the solutions of the differential subordination,or, more simply, a dominant if
p(z)\prec q(z)
for all
p(z)
satisfying (
1.13
). A dominant
\tilde{q}(z)

that satisfies
\tilde{q}(z)\prec q(z)

for all dominants
q(z)
of (
1.13
) is said to be the
best dominant
.

Definition 2
.

[
38
]

Let
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{4}\times\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and the function
h(z)
be univalent in
\mathcal{U}.
If the function
p(z)
and

\psi(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z),z^{2}p^{\prime\prime}(z),z^{3}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z);z)

are univalent in
\mathcal{U}
and satisfies the following third-order differential superordination

(1.14)

h(z)\prec\psi(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z),z^{2}p^{\prime\prime}(z),z^{3}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z);z),

then
p(z)
is called a
solution
of the differential superordination. An analytic function
q(z)
is called a
subordinant
of the solutions of the differential superordination, or more simply a subordinant, if
q(z)\prec p(z)
for all
p(z)
satisfying (
1.14
).

A univalent subordinant
\tilde{q}(z)
that satisfies
q(z)\prec\tilde{q}(z)

for all subordinant
q(z)
of (
1.14
) is said to be the
best subordinant
. We note that both the best dominant and best subordinant are unique up to rotation of
\mathcal{U}
.

.

[
[

]

Definition 4
.

[
[

]

\psi(r,s,t,u;z)\not\in\Omega

where

The next lemma is the foundation result in the theory of third-order differential subordination.

Lemma 5
.

[
[
5
]

p(z)\prec q(z)\qquad(z\in\mathcal{U}).

\phi\left(\frac{{S}_{a+1,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a-1,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a-2,c}f(z)}{z};z\right),

is univalent in
\mathcal{U},
and the condition (
2.15
) and (

) are satisfied, then

h_{1}(z)\prec\phi\left(\frac{{S}_{a+1,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a-1,c}f(z)}{z},\frac{S_{a-2,c}f(z)}{z};z\right)\prec h_{2}(z)

implies that

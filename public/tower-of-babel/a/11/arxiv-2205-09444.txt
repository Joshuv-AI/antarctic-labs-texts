A Choquard type equation with a singular absorption nonlinearity in two dimension
G.C. Anthal , J. Giacomoni and K. Sreenadh

Abstract

We achieve this by approximating the singular function u − β ​ log ⁡ ( u ) u^{-\beta}\log(u) by a function l ϵ ​ ( u ) l_{\epsilon}(u) which pointwisely converges to − u β ​ log ⁡ ( u ) -u^{\beta}\log(u) as ϵ → 0 \epsilon\rightarrow 0 . Introduction

This article is concerned with the study of following problem involving both an absorption type singularity and exponential nonlinearity of Choquard type

(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})\left\{-\Delta u=u^{-\beta}\log(u)\chi_{\{u>0\}}+\lambda\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)f(u),~\text{in}~\Omega,~u\geq 0,~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\right.

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
0<\mu<1
. Here
\chi_{\{u>0\}}
denotes the characteristic function corresponding to the set
\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\}
and by convention
u^{-\beta}\log u\chi_{\{u>0\}}=0
if
u\leq 0
.

The notion of weak solution is as below:

Definition 1.1

By a weak solution of problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})
, we mean a function
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

u^{-\beta}\log u\chi_{\{u>0\}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)

and

\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{u>0\}}u^{-\beta}\log(u)\varphi+\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{F(u(y))f(u(x))\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}},~\text{for any}~\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega).

Here
C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
stands for the functions belonging to
C^{1}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
.
We assume the following hypothesis on the nonlinearity
f
:

(f_{1})

f
is of class
C^{1,\beta}(0,\infty)\cap C[0,\infty)
for some
0<\beta<1
.

(f_{2})

For all
\alpha>0
,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(t)|}{\exp(\alpha t^{2})}=0.

(f_{3})

There exists constant
1<r_{0}<2
such that

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{|f(t)|}{t^{r_{0}}}<\infty.

(f_{4})

There exists
l>0
such that
\displaystyle t\rightarrow\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{t^{l}}
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
.

(f_{5})

There exist
T
,
T_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}>0
such that
0<t^{\gamma_{0}}F(t)\leq T_{0}f(t)
for all
|t|>T
.

(f_{6})

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}|f^{\prime}(t)|<\infty
.

Remark 1.2

Using

(f_{5})
it is easy to show that there are constants
A
,
t_{0}\geq 0
and
\gamma>1
such that

F(t)\geq At^{\gamma}~\text{for all}~t\geq t_{0}.

(1.1)

Furthermore using
(f_{3})
, we see that
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Hence we can extend
f
by
0
on
\mathbb{R}^{-}
and then as a
C^{1}
function on
\mathbb{R}
.

We state our main result:

Theorem 1.3

Suppose that
f
satisfies
(f_{1})-(f_{6})
. Then there exists a
\bar{\lambda}>0
such that for each
0<\lambda<\bar{\lambda}
, problem

has a nonnegative nontrivial solution

.

We employ a variational approach to solve
(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})
. More precisely, we first study the following approximated problem:

(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon,\lambda})\left\{-\Delta u+l_{\epsilon}(u)=\lambda\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)f(u(x)),~\text{in}~\Omega,~u\geq 0,~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\right.

where

l_{\epsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{t^{q}}{(t+\epsilon)^{\beta+q}}\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right),&~t\geq 0,\\
0,&t<0,\end{cases}

and
0<q<1
is such that
q<r_{0}-1
. The energy functional associated to the problem
({\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon,\lambda}})
is given by

J_{\epsilon,\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{2}+\int\limits_{\Omega}L_{\epsilon}dx-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}F(u(x))dydx,~\forall\,u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(1.2)

where
\displaystyle L_{\epsilon}(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}l_{\epsilon}(s)ds
and
\displaystyle F(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}f(s)ds
. In this matter we shall discuss about estimates of
J_{\epsilon,\lambda}
independently of
\epsilon
in Section
2

J_{\epsilon,\lambda}
and using variational arguments, we prove the existence of a nontrivial solution,
u_{\epsilon}
, to
(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon,\lambda})
. Next from a priori estimates and a key point pointwise gradient estimate (in the spirit of works
[
10
,
17
,
25

u_{\epsilon}

(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})

For reader’s convenience, we now present a brief introduction to existence and multiplicity results for the equations involving singular and Choquard nonlinearities.
The equations involving Choquard nonlinearity were introduced by Ph. Choquard in 1976 in the modeling of one-component plasma. Choquard equations are also used to study the model of the polaron, where free electrons in an ionic lattice interact with photons associated to deformations of the lattice or with the polarisation that it creates on the medium. For further details, see for instance
[

[

and references therein for a large overview of current results concerning Choquard problems.

In 1996, Joao Marcos Do Ó in
[
18

studied the following problem

\left\{-\Delta_{n}u=f(x,u)~\text{in}~\Omega,~u\geq 0~\text{in}~\Omega,\right.

\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{n}

and the growth of the nonlinearity

is as

. The author showed the existence of a nontrivial solution under suitable technical assumptions on the nonlinearity

for related results on critical elliptic equations and systems in two dimension. R. Arora and et. al.

Motivated by the above discussion, we aim to study the nonlocal problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})
. In the present paper, in frame of
(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})
we focus on the interaction between an absorption singular nonlinearity and a nonlocal term of Hartree type. Precisely we considered in the right hand side of the equation the competition between combined singularities of types
(a)
and
(b)
with exponential nonlinearity of Choquard type. Up to our knowledge, this interaction has not been investigated in previous contributions and can not be tackled by using monotone methods since maximum principle fails due to the absorption term. Therefore, we first introduce the approximated problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon,\lambda})
and prove that it admits a weak solution for suitable values of
\epsilon
and
\lambda
using the Mountain pass theorem. Moreover we prove uniform estimates independently of
\epsilon

and

independently of
\epsilon

\epsilon

\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
to a nontrivial function. The crucial final step to prove that the limit function is the required nontrivial weak solution of

appeals a delicate uniform gradient estimate of the approximated solutions (see Lemma

Preliminaries

The study of elliptic equations with exponential growth nonlinearities
are motivated by the following Trudinger-Moser inequality, namely

Theorem 2.1

We have,

\exp\left(\alpha w^{2}\right)\in L^{1}(\Omega)~\text{ for every}~w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)~\text{and}~\alpha>0,

and there is a constant
k_{1}>0
such that

\sup\limits_{\|w\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{\Omega}\exp\left(\alpha w^{2}\right)\leq k_{1},~\text{for every}~\alpha\leq 4\pi~\text{and}~w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.1)

The embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\exp\left(|u|^{\beta}\right)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is compact for all
\beta\in[1,2)
and is continuous for
\beta=2
. Consequently the map
T:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
, for
q\in[1,\infty),
defined by

T(u):=\exp\left(|u|^{\beta}\right)
, is continuous with respect to the norm topology, for any
\beta\leq 2
.

The following result due to Lions
[
16
, Section 1.7, Remark I.18]
will play an important role.

Lemma 2.2

Let
\{u_{k}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\|u_{k}\|=1
such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u\neq 0
weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then for every

0<p<\frac{4\pi}{(1-\|u\|^{2})}

we have

\sup_{k}\int\limits_{\Omega}\exp\left(pu_{k}^{2}\right)<a_{2},~\text{for some constant}~a_{2}~\text{independent of}~k.

Now note that using hypothesis
(f_{2})
, we can conclude that for any
\alpha>0
there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\alpha
such that

\max\{|f(t),|F(t)|\}\leq C\exp\left(\alpha t^{2}\right),~\text{for}~t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.2)

For any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, by virtue of Sobolev embedding theorem, we get that
u\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
. Moreover using Trudinger-Moser inequality and (
2.2
), we get

f(u),F(u)\in L^{q}(\Omega)~\text{for any}~q\geq 1.

(2.3)

Now we recall the well known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

Proposition 2.3

[
15
]
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
Let

and
0<\mu<2
with
1/r+1/s+\mu/2=2
,
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),g\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then, there exist a sharp constant
C(r,s,\mu)
independent of

and

such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(r,s,\mu)|f|_{r}|g|_{s}.

(2.4)

\square

By taking

. Also

\mathcal{(}P_{\epsilon,\lambda})

\langle J_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{\prime}(u),\varphi\rangle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}l_{\epsilon}(u)\varphi dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)f(u(x))\varphi(x)dx,

for every

. We will need the following estimates of

.

Proposition 2.4

The following assertions hold.

1.

There exists
\tilde{m}>0
such that

0\leq-l_{\epsilon}(t)\leq t~\text{for all}~t\geq 1-\epsilon~\text{and}~0<\epsilon<1/2,

(2.5)

0<L_{\epsilon}(t)<\widetilde{m}~\text{for any}~0\leq t\leq 1-\epsilon~\text{and}~0<\epsilon<1,

(2.6)

and

|L_{\epsilon}(t)|\leq\frac{t^{2-\beta}}{2-\beta}+\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}+\widetilde{m}.

(2.7)

2.

For each
p_{0}>2
there exists a constant
k_{0}
such that

L_{\epsilon}(t)\geq-k_{0}t^{p_{0}}~\text{for all}~t\geq 1-\epsilon,~0\leq\epsilon\leq 1/2.

(2.8)

3.

There exists a constant
C>0
that does not depend upon
0<\epsilon<1
such that

|tl_{\epsilon}(t)|\leq C(1+t^{2-\beta})~\text{for all}~t>0.

(2.9)

4.

There exist constants
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
(that is independent of
\epsilon>0
) such that

-\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)\geq t~\text{for all}~0\leq t<{\delta_{0}}~\text{and}~0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}.

(2.10)

Proof.
By definition of
l_{\epsilon}
, we have
l_{\epsilon}(t)\geq 0
for
0\leq t\leq 1-\epsilon
and
l_{\epsilon}(t)\leq 0
for
t\geq 1-\epsilon
. Note that for
0<\epsilon<1/2
and

, we have

\displaystyle-l_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{t^{q}}{(t+\epsilon)^{q+\beta}}\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)\leq t(t+\epsilon)^{-\beta-1}\log(t+\epsilon)\leq t.

This proves assertion (
2.5
).

Now since
0<\beta<1
, we choose
\delta>0
such that
0<\beta<\delta<1
. Since

\displaystyle 0\leq l_{\epsilon}(t)\leq-(t+\epsilon)^{-\beta}\log(t+\epsilon)\leq mt^{-\delta},

\displaystyle 0<L_{\epsilon}(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}l_{\epsilon}(s)ds\leq\frac{m}{1-\delta}t^{1-\delta}\leq\widetilde{m}~\text{for all}~0\leq t\leq 1-\epsilon.

This concludes assertion (
2.6
). Inequality (
2.7
) also holds. Indeed using (

\log t\leq t

, we have

\displaystyle|L_{\epsilon}(t)|\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}+\int\limits_{1-\epsilon}^{t}|l_{\epsilon}(s)|ds\leq\widetilde{m}+\int\limits_{1-\epsilon}^{t}s^{-\beta}\log\left(s+\frac{s}{s+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}+\int\limits_{1-\epsilon}^{t}s^{-\beta}\left(s+\frac{\epsilon}{s+\epsilon}\right)ds\leq\widetilde{m}+\int\limits_{0}^{t}(s^{1-\beta}+s^{\beta})ds=\widetilde{m}+\frac{t^{2-\beta}}{2-\beta}+\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}.

Note that for each
p_{0}>2
there exists
k_{0}>0
such that

\widetilde{m}+\frac{t^{2-\beta}}{2-\beta}+\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\leq k_{0}t^{p_{0}}~\text{for all}~t\geq 1/2.

Thus, from (

\displaystyle L_{\epsilon}(s)\geq-\left(\widetilde{m}+\frac{t^{2-\beta}}{2-\beta}+\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)\geq-k_{0}t^{p_{0}}~\text{for all}~t\geq 1-\epsilon~\text{and for every}~0<\epsilon<1/2,

proving (
2.8
). Now we prove (
2.9
). For each
0<\epsilon<1

0<t<1-\epsilon

C>0

\displaystyle|l_{\epsilon}(t)t|\leq-t^{1-\beta}\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)\leq(-\log t)t^{1-\beta}\leq C.

On the other hand for

\displaystyle|l_{\epsilon}(t)t|\leq t^{1-\beta}\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)\leq t^{1-\beta}\log(t+\epsilon)\leq Ct^{2-\beta}.

We conclude that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|l_{\epsilon}(s)s|\leq C(1+t^{2-\beta})~\text{for all}~s\geq 0~\text{and}~0<\epsilon<1.

Lastly, we prove assertion (

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{t}{-\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)}=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2}~\text{if}~0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{3}.

Thus we can choose

t\leq-\log\left(t+\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)~\text{if}~0\leq t<\delta_{0}~\text{and}~0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}.

This completes the proof.

Remark 5.2

The case of singularity
t^{\beta}\log(t)
replaced by singularity
|\log t|^{k-2}\log t,~k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
can be tackled in the similar manner. The function
l_{\epsilon}
in this case takes the form

l_{\epsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}\left|\log\left(t+\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)\right|^{k-2}\log\left(t+\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{t+\epsilon}\right)~&\text{if}~t>0,\\
0~&\text{if}~t=0.\end{cases}

Also the
Z-
function to get the gradient estimate in this can be given as

\displaystyle Z(t)=\begin{cases}t^{2}-\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}|\log s|^{k-2}\log s,~\text{for}~0\leq t\leq t^{\ast},\\
(t^{\ast})^{2}+(t-t^{\ast})(2t^{\ast}-|\log t^{\ast}|^{k-2}\log t^{\ast})-\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t^{\ast}}|\log s|^{k-2}\log sds,~\text{for}~t\geq t^{\ast},\end{cases}

where
t^{\ast}
is such that

\frac{|\log t^{\ast}|^{k-2}}{t^{\ast}}=\frac{2}{k-1}.

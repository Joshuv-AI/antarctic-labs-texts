The Riemann-Roch theorem for the Adams operations
A. Navarro, J. Navarro

Abstract
We prove the classical Riemann-Roch theorems for the Adams operations ψ j \,\psi^{j}\, on K K -theory: a statement with coefficients on ℤ ⁡ [ j − 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[j^{-1}] , that holds for arbitrary projective morphisms, as well as another one with integral coefficients, that is valid for closed immersions. In presence of rational coefficients, we also analyze the relation between the corresponding Riemann-Roch formula for one Adams operation and the analogous formula for the Chern character. To do so, we complete the elementary exposition of the work of Panin-Smirnov that was initiated by the first author in a previous work. Their notion of oriented cohomology theory of algebraic varieties allows to use classical arguments to prove general and neat statements, which imply all the aforementioned results as particular cases.



On a compact Riemann surface, a pioneering work of B. Riemann (
[
13
]
) proved the lower bound

l(D)\,\geq\,{\rm deg}\,D-g+1

for the dimension
l(D)
of the complex vector space of meromorphic functions with zeroes and poles prescribed by a divisor
D
. This inequality unveiled a strong relation between such a complex analytic quantity,
l(D)
, and purely topological invariants; namely, the degree of the divisor and the topological genus
g
of the surface. Not long after that, Riemann’s student G. Roch identified (
[
15
]
) the difference between both sides of the inequality: if
K
denotes the canonical divisor on the surface,

l(D)-l(K-D)\,=\,{\rm deg}D-g+1\ .

After a series of generalizations during the first half of the twentieth century, A. Grothendieck’s approach (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
) entailed a Copernican revolution on the understanding of that formula. First of all, he introduced the
K
-group
K(X)
of vector bundles of an algebraic
k
-variety

and realized that it has a cohomological behaviour: it is a contravariant functor of rings and, for any projective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
between smooth varieties, there exits an exceptional direct image

.
Also,
K
-theory is related to the Chow ring with rational coefficients via a morphisms of functor of rings

, called the Chern character.
The generalization of the Riemann-Roch formula says how to modify this Chern character in order to make it compatible with the corresponding direct images: the correction term is the Todd series of the virtual tangent bundle

1
Oriented cohomology theories on algebraic varieties

We work in the setting of oriented cohomology theories on algebraic varieties, in the sense of Panin-Smirnov (
[
10
]
). Let us briefly recall this formalism, as exposed in
[
9
]
.

Definition 1.1

Let
k
be a field. An
oriented cohomology theory
is a contravariant functor
A\,
from the category of smooth quasi-projective varieties over
k\,
into the category of commutative rings. Part of the data are also
direct images for projective morphisms
; that is to say, a morphism of groups
f_{*}\colon A(Y)\to A(X)
, for any projective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
, such that

\mathrm{Id}_{*}=\mathrm{Id}
,
(fg)_{*}=f_{*}g_{*}\,
and such that the projection formula

f_{*}(f^{*}(x)y)=xf_{*}(y)
holds.

On an oriented cohomology theory
\,A
, any smooth closed subvariety
\,i\colon Z\to X\,
has a
fundamental class

\,[Z]^{A}\coloneqq i_{*}(1)\in A(X)\ ,

and any line
bundle
\,L\to X\,
has a
Chern class

\,c_{1}^{A}(L)\coloneqq s_{0}^{*}(s_{0*}(1))\in A(X)\ ,

where
\,s_{0}\colon X\to L\,
is the zero section.

These data are also assumed to satisfy the following axioms:

1.

The ring morphism
i_{1}^{*}+i_{2}^{*}\colon A(X_{1}\amalg X_{2})\to A(X_{1})\oplus A(X_{2})
is an
isomorphism, where
i_{j}\colon X_{j}\to X_{1}\amalg X_{2}
is the natural immersion (in particular,
A(\emptyset)=0
).

2.

For any affine bundle

\pi\colon P\to X
, the ring morphism
\pi^{*}\colon A(X)\to A(P)
is an isomorphism.

3.

For any smooth closed subvariety
i\colon Z\to X
, the following is an exact sequence:

\ A(Z)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}A(X)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}A(X-Z)\ .

4.

If a morphism
f\colon\bar{X}\to X
is transversal to a smooth closed subvariety

i\colon Z\to X
of codimension
d
, then the following square is commutative:

\textstyle{A(Z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{i_{*}}
\textstyle{A(\bar{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{*}}
\textstyle{A(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{A(\bar{X}).}

5.

For any morphism
f\colon Y\to X
and any projective bundle
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(E)\to X
, the following square is commutative:

\textstyle{A(\mathbb{P}(E))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{*}}
\textstyle{A(\mathbb{P}(f^{*}E))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{*}}
\textstyle{A(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{A(Y).}

6.

If
E\to X
is a vector bundle of rank
r+1
, then the
A(X)
-module in
A(\mathbb{P}(E))
is free of rank
r+1
:

A(\mathbb{P}(E))=A(X)\oplus A(X)x_{E}\oplus\ldots\oplus A(X)x_{E}^{r}.

where
x_{E}=c_{1}^{A}(\xi_{E})
is the first Chern class of the tautological
line bundle.

A
morphism of cohomologies

\varphi\colon A\to\bar{A}\,
is a natural transformation preserving direct images;
i.e., it is a morphism of functors of rings
\,\varphi\colon A\to\bar{A}\,
such that

\varphi(f_{*}(a))=\bar{f}_{*}(\varphi(a))

for any projective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
and any element
a\in A(Y)
.

Example 1.2

1.

Weil cohomologies are oriented cohomologies in the sense above. These examples include singular cohomology of complex varieties, de Rham cohomology of varieties over a field
k
of characteristic zero, or
l
-adic cohomology, with
k
of characteristic different from
l\,
.

2.

The Chow ring
\,CH^{\bullet}(X)\,
of rational equivalence classes of algebraic cycles is also an oriented cohomology theory (
[
4
]
).

3.

Grothendieck’s

-theory is an oriented cohomology theory where the cohomology rings
\,K(X)\,
have no graduation (v. gr.,
[

]
).

4.

Algebraic cobordism
\,\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
, as constructed by Levine and Morel (
[
7
]
), is also an oriented cohomology.

The following fact, commonly known as
Jouanolou’s Trick
, assures that any quasi-projective
k
-variety is “homotopically equivalent” to an affine variety:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
6
]
)

For any quasi-projective
k
-variety
\,X
, there exists an affine bundle
\,P\to X\,
such that
\,P\,
is an affine variety.

In particular, for any given line bundle
L\to X
, there exists an affine bundle
\,p\colon P\to X\,
and a morphism
\,\varphi\colon P\to\mathbb{P}^{d}\,
such that
\,p^{*}L=\varphi^{*}(\xi^{*}_{d})
, where
\,\xi_{d}\to\mathbb{P}^{d}\,
stands for the tautological line bundle.

Definition 1.4

A
commutative formal group law
over a ring
\,R\,
is a power series
\,f(u,v)\in R[[u,v]]\,
that satisfies the axioms for a commutative group operation with
0
as the neutral element; namely,

a)

f(u,v)=f(v,u)\in R[[u,v]]
.

b)

f(u,0)=u\in R[[u]]
.

c)

f(u,f(v,w))=f(f(u,v),w)\in R[[u,v,w]]
.

d)

There exist a series
\,opp(u)\in R[[u]]\,
such that
\,opp(0)=0\,
and
\,f(u,opp(u))=0\in R[[u]]
.

The
additive group law
is
f(u,v)=u+v\ ,
and the
multiplicative

group law
is
f(u,v)=u+v-uv
.

On any oriented cohomology theory
A
, the cohomology rings of projective spaces satisfy the projective bundle theorem (
[
9
, Corollary 1.3]
); that is to say, the maps
c_{1}^{A}(\xi^{*}_{d})\mapsto u
define isomorphisms of rings, for all
d\geq 1
,

A(\mathbb{P}^{d})=A(*)[u]/(u^{d+1})\ .

As a consequence,

Proposition 1.5

Let
\,A\,
be an oriented cohomology theory. There exists a unique commutative formal group law
\,f^{A}(u,v)\in\,A(*)[[u,v]]\,
such that

c_{1}^{A}(L_{x}\otimes L_{y})=f^{A}(x,y)

for any pair of line bundles
L_{x}
,
L_{y}
, with Chern classes
x:=c_{1}^{A}(L_{x})
and
y:=c_{1}^{A}(L_{y})
.

We say that
A
follows the law
f^{A}
, or that the series
f^{A}(u,v)
is the formal group law associated to
A
.

Proof:
Axiom 6 of Definition
1.1
implies that the map
c_{1}(\pi^{*}_{1}\xi^{*}_{d})\mapsto u
,
c_{1}(\pi_{2}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d})\mapsto v
is an isomorphism of rings:

A\left(\mathbb{P}^{d}\times\mathbb{P}^{d}\right)=A(*)[u,v]/(u^{d+1},v^{d+1})\ .

These isomorphisms are compatible with the restrictions
i^{*}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d}\to\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
; therefore, there exists a unique series
f(u,v)\in A(*)[[u,v]]
such that, for any
d
,

c_{1}(\pi_{1}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d})=f(u,v)\ .

Now, for any pair of line bundles
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
on any variety
X
, use Theorem
1.3
to assure the existence of an affine bundle
p\colon P\to X
and maps
h_{1},h_{2}\colon P\to\mathbb{P}^{d}
such that
p^{*}L_{x}=h_{1}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d}
and
p^{*}L_{y}=h_{2}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d}
.

Considering the map
h:=h_{1}\times h_{2}\colon P\to\mathbb{P}^{d}\times\mathbb{P}^{d}
, we can write

\displaystyle p^{*}\left(c_{1}(L_{x}\otimes L_{y})\right)

\displaystyle=c_{1}(p^{*}L_{x}\otimes p^{*}L_{y})=c_{1}(h^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d}\otimes h^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d})=h^{*}\left(c_{1}(\pi_{1}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\xi^{*}_{d})\right)

\displaystyle=h^{*}\left(f(u,v)\right)=f\left(h^{*}u,h^{*}v\right)=f(p^{*}x,p^{*}y)=p^{*}(f(x,y))\ ,

and the series
f(x,y)
satisfies the required property, because
p^{*}
is an isomorphism.

The first three axioms of formal group law follow from the fact that
L_{x}\otimes L_{y}=L_{y}\otimes L_{x}
,
L_{x}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}=L_{x}
and
(L_{x}\otimes L_{y})\otimes L_{z}=L_{x}\otimes(L_{y}\otimes L_{z})
.

As for the last one, let
opp(u)\in A(*)[[u]]
be the unique series such that

c_{1}(\xi_{d})=opp(u)\in A(*)[u]/(u^{d+1})\simeq A(\mathbb{P}^{d})\ ,\qquad\forall\ d\geq 1\ ,

via the identification
u\mapsto c_{1}(\xi^{*}_{d})
. The relation
\xi^{*}_{d}\otimes\xi_{d}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}
implies that this series also satisfies

f(u,opp(u))=0\in A(*)[u]/(u^{d+1})\ ,\qquad\forall\ d\geq 1\ ,

and hence
f(u,opp(u))=0
in
A(*)[[u]]
.

\square

Corollary 1.7

Let

]

In this example, the fundamental class of a smooth closed subvariety

[Y]^{K}=i_{*}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\in K(X)\ ,

and the Chern class of a line bundle

is the element

Line bundles generate this

[
9

Splitting Principle:

Let
A
be an oriented cohomology.
For any vector bundle
E\to X
, there exists a base change
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that
\pi^{*}\colon A(X)\to A(X^{\prime})
is
injective and
\pi^{*}E=L_{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+L_{\alpha_{r}}
is a sum of line bundles
in
K(X^{\prime})
.

These cohomology classes
\,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in A(X^{\prime})\,
appearing in the statement above are called the

of
E
.

Morphism of groups
K\to A

Let
\,K^{+}(X)\,
stand for the commutative monoid generated by the isomorphism classes of vector bundles over
\,X\,
, modulo the relations
\,[E]=[E^{\prime}]+[E^{\prime\prime}]\,
produced by exact sequences
\,0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0\,
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
A
be an oriented cohomology.
Morphisms of functor of monoids
\,\phi\colon K^{+}\to(A,\cdot)
, as well as morphisms of functor of groups
\,\phi\colon K\to A\,
, are determined by their values on the tautological line bundles
\,\{\xi_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof:
Let us only detail the case of morphisms of groups. Let
\,\phi,\bar{\phi}\colon K\to A\,
be morphisms of functor of groups that coincide on each
\xi_{d}
.

By Theorem
1.3
, they coincide on any line bundle
\,L\to X
: let
p\colon P\to X
be an affine bundle and
\,\varphi\colon P\to\mathbb{P}^{d}
a morphism such that
p^{*}L=\varphi^{*}(\xi_{d})
. Then,

p^{*}\phi(L)=\phi(p^{*}L)=\phi(\varphi^{*}\xi_{d})=\varphi^{*}\phi(\xi_{d})=\varphi^{*}\bar{\phi}(\xi_{d})=\bar{\phi}(\varphi^{*}\xi_{d})=\bar{\phi}(p^{*}L)=p^{*}\bar{\phi}(L)\ ,

and therefore
\,\phi(L)=\bar{\phi}(L)
, because
p^{*}
is injective.

As both
\phi
and
\bar{\phi}
are functorial and additive, the Splitting Principle implies that they coincide on any vector bundle
E
and, hence, on any element of the
K
-group.

\square

Proposition 2.2

Let

\,F\in A(*)[[t]]\,

•

: There exists a unique morphism of functor of monoids,

F_{\times}\colon K^{+}\longrightarrow(A,\cdot)\ ,

whose value on the the tautological line bundles is
F_{\times}(\xi_{d}):=F(x_{d})
.

•

Additive extension
: There exists a unique morphism of functor of groups

F_{+}\colon K\longrightarrow A\ ,

whose value on the tautological line bundles is
\,F_{+}(\xi_{d}):=F(x_{d})
.

Proof:
Uniqueness is a consequence of Lemma
2.1
above. To prove the existence, let us define, on a vector bundle

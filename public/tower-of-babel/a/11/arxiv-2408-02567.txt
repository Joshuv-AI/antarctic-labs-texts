Geometry via Plane wave limits
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. Utilizing the covariant formulation of Penrose’s plane wave limit by Blau et al., we construct for any semi-Riemannian metric g g a family of “plane wave limits.” These limits are taken along any geodesic of g g , yield simpler metrics of Lorentzian signature, and are isometric invariants. We show that they generalize Penrose’s limit to the semi-Riemannian regime and, in certain cases, encode g g ’s tensorial geometry and its geodesic deviation. As an application of the latter, we partially extend a well known result by Hawking & Penrose to the semi-Riemannian regime: On any semi-Riemannian manifold, if the Ricci curvature is nonnegative along any complete geodesic without conjugate points that is “causally independent” (in a sense we make precise), then the curvature tensor along that geodesic must vanish in all normal directions. A Morse Index Theorem is also proved for such geodesics.



1.
Introduction

In this article we extend and adapt R. Penrose’s
plane wave limit

[
Pen76
]
as a purely geometric tool. Penrose’s limit is a famous construction in gravitational physics, by which any Lorentzian manifold is shown to admit a so-called
plane wave
spacetime as a limit, this limit being taken along any lightlike geodesic. The plane waves of Penrose’s limit model gravitational radiation and have a rich history. Interestingly, their mathematical discovery, by H. Brinkmann
[
Bri25
]
, predated their discovery within physics; see, e.g.,
[
Sor+17
]
. The literature on plane wave limits is, by now, quite large, and much of the current interest in them is via the AdS/CFT correspondence, as plane wave limits of
\text{AdS}_{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
have been found that provide examples of maximally supersymmetric, quantizable backgrounds in string theory; see
[
BFOP02
]
. For more on their properties, consult
[
Bla+04
,
Bla
]
.

The crucial step for us in utilizing Penrose’s limit geometrically is to work, not with Penrose’s original (and beautiful) local construction, but rather with its (equally beautiful) “covariant formulation” discovered by
[
Bla+04
]
. The latter extends Penrose’s limit beyond local coordinates and onto the entirety of the lightlike geodesic (in Section
2.4
and Theorem
2
we review both constructions). Our first task, therefore, is to extend
[
Bla+04
]
’s formulation to any geodesic
\gamma
— spacelike, timelike, or lightlike — on a semi-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of any signature, thereby defining a “semi-Riemannian plane wave limit,” a limit that is still a Lorentzian plane wave spacetime. Geometrically, arguably the most interesting case is when
g
is Riemannian, and we begin, in Section
2
, with this case. After defining a Riemannian version of our plane wave limit (Definition
1
), our first result (Theorem
1
) details how it encodes the geometry of
g
within its own geometry:

Theorem
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold.

i.

g
is flat
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all flat,

ii.

g
has constant sectional curvature
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all locally conformally flat,

iii.

g
is Ricci-flat
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all Ricci-flat,

iv.

\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is constant along each geodesic
\gamma\!\!\iff\!\!

g
’s plane wave limits all have parallel Ricci tensors,

v.

(\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}})(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)
vanishes along each geodesic
\gamma\!\!\iff\!\!

g
’s plane wave limits are all locally symmetric,

vi.

g
has signed Ricci curvature
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits all have Ricci curvatures of the same sign,

vii.

g
is geodesically complete
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all geodesically complete.

Our second result is more difficult: We show (Theorem
2
) that our Riemannian plane wave limit above can still be realized as an instance of Penrose’s original (locally defined) plane wave limit:

Theorem
.

The plane wave limit of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
along the unit-speed geodesic
\gamma(t)
is the Penrose plane wave limit of the Lorentzian manifold
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
along the lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(t)\!\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\!(t,\gamma(t))
.

In Section
3
we then move to the fully semi-Riemannian regime. Given that our geodesics may now be spacelike, timelike, or lightlike — and that we would like to take the limit along
any
of them — there is a question of how best to define the plane wave limit in this fully general setting. We resolve this question by taking guidance from
[
Bla+04
]
’s crucial observation that what Penrose’s original limit is actually encoding is
geodesic deviation
. Viewed in this light, we present (Definition
2
) a semi-Riemannian plane wave limit — valid for all signatures and geodesics — and show (Proposition
2
) that it continues to encode geodesic deviation — provided that the geodesics
\gamma
along which the limit is taken are “
causally independent
”: There exists along
\gamma
a maximal parallel orthonormal frame
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}\subseteq\gamma^{\prime\perp}
such that
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})=0
whenever
E_{i},E_{j}
have different causal characters. (Riemannian geodesics, as well as timelike or lightlike Lorentzian geodesics, are causally independent by default.) That Definition
2
encodes the geodesic deviation of precisely such geodesics is its primary virtue.

Indeed, for our third and final result, we use our plane wave limit to extend to the semi-Riemannian regime the focusing theorem of S. Hawking & Penrose
[
HP70
]
(see also
[
BEE96
, Prop. 12.10,12.17]
). Namely, on a Lorentzian manifold
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})
, if
\gamma
is a complete timelike or lightlike geodesic without conjugate points such that
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\geq 0
, then
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\big|_{\gamma^{\prime\perp}}=0
. Using Jacobi tensors, this was subsequently extended to Riemannian manifolds in
[
OE80
, Theorem 2]
. Crucial to these results is that the metric be positive-definite on
\gamma^{\prime\perp}\subseteq TM
(or on
\gamma^{\prime\perp}/\gamma^{\prime}
when
\gamma
is lightlike), for otherwise
\gamma
may not extremize the length functional (e.g., when
\gamma
is spacelike). Thus no such result is known on semi-Riemannian manifolds in general, although a Morse Index Theorem (using the Maslov Index) has been established, in
[
Hel94
,
PT02
]
. Using our limit, we prove (Theorem
3
) the following:

Theorem
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian
n
-manifold
(
n\geq 3
)
and
\gamma
a complete causally independent geodesic. If
\gamma
has no conjugate points and
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\geq 0
, then
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\big|_{\gamma^{\prime\perp}}=0
. Furthermore, causally independent geodesic segments always have finitely many conjugate points.

Causal independence is crucial even in the last statement, as conjugate points on an arbitrary spacelike geodesic may accumulate; see
[
Hel94
,
PT03
]
.

2.
A Riemannian plane wave limit

Our goal in this section is fourfold:

1.

To introduce our extension of Penrose’s plane wave limit in the special first case when
(M,g)
is a
Riemannian
manifold (Definition
1
).

2.

To collect together the curvature properties of Lorentzian

), of which plane waves are a special case. Although pp-waves are well known (see, e.g.,
[

,

,

3.

To show how the plane wave limit encodes much of the geometry of

into its own geometry (Theorem

(M,g)
has constant sectional curvature if and only if all of its plane wave limits are locally conformally flat, etc.

4.

Finally, to show how the Riemannian wave plane limit can be realized as an instance of Penrose’s original limit (Theorem
2
), and that, complementing
[
BFW06
]
, it can also be realized from Fermi coordinates.

W_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}-\frac{1}{n-1}\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,h+\cancelto{0}{\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}}{2n(n-1)}h\,{\tiny\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,h},

are

W_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{i},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{j})=-\frac{H_{ij}}{2}+\frac{\Delta H}{2(n-1)}\delta_{ij}.

All of the properties i.-vii. now follow easily (for vi., recall that
h
has harmonic curvature tensor if
(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})^{*}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}=0
, where
(\nabla^{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})^{*}
is the adjoint of
\nabla^{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
; cf., e.g.,
[
Pet16
, p. 59]
). Finally, if
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=(\tilde{\gamma}^{v}(s),\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s),\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s),\dots,\tilde{\gamma}^{n-1}(s))
is any geodesic of
h
, then a straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}\ddot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{v}&=&\!\!-\frac{\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{t}}{2}\left(H_{t}\,\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}H_{i}\,\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{i}\right),\\
\ddot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{t}&=&\!\!0,\\
\ddot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{i}&=&\!\!\frac{(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{t})^{2}}{2}H_{i}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=1,\dots,n-1.\end{array}\right\}

As
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)
is linear, and as
\ddot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{v}
is independent of
\tilde{\gamma}^{v},\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{v}
, these equations will be determined once the (
\tilde{\gamma}^{v}
-independent)
\ddot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{i}
equations are. Thus the completeness of the latter will determine that of the geodesic.
∎

2.3.
Geometric properties preserved by the plane wave limit.

Bringing together Definition
1
and (
8
), let us emphasize the all-important property that the only nonvanishing components of
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}
are

(15)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{i},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{j})=-A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j}).

Recall that constant-curvature manifolds have plane wave limits
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
with
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)x^{i}x^{j}=-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x^{i})^{2}
. Thanks to Proposition
1
, we now know that such
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
’s are locally conformally flat and locally symmetric — suggesting that
g
’s curvature has been encoded, via (
15
), not just in the metric component
(g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{tt}
, but in the actual
tensorial geometry
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
. As our first application of the plane wave limit, we now show that this is no coincidence:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold.

i.

g
is flat
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all flat,

ii.

g
has constant sectional curvature
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all locally conformally flat,

iii.

g
is Ricci-flat
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all Ricci-flat,

iv.

\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is constant along each geodesic
\gamma\!\!\iff\!\!

g
’s plane wave limits all have parallel Ricci tensors,

v.

(\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}})(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)
vanishes along each geodesic
\gamma\!\!\iff\!\!

g
’s plane wave limits are all locally symmetric,

vi.

g
has signed Ricci curvature
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits all have Ricci curvatures of the same sign,

vii.

g
is geodesically complete
\!\!\iff\!\!
its plane wave limits are all geodesically complete.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n-1}\}
be a parallel frame along a geodesic
\gamma(t)
of
(M,g)
as in (
1
), with
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\big|_{\gamma(t)}
the corresponding functions along
\gamma(t)
. As we will be repeatedly calling upon Proposition
1
, recall also that the plane wave limit
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
has
H_{ij}=2A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
. We now proceed case-by-case:

i.

g
is flat if and only if each

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{i})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=0\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=1,\dots,n-1,

because at each
t
this is the sectional curvature of the 2-plane spanned by the orthonormal pair
\{\gamma^{\prime}(t),E_{i}\big|_{\gamma(t)}\}
, and every 2-plane can be represented in this way. That

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\Big(\frac{E_{i}+E_{j}}{\sqrt{2}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\frac{E_{i}+E_{j}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=0

then yields

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=0\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti,j=1,\dots,n-1.

Thus
g
is flat if and only if each
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=0
; by Proposition
1
, this is equivalent to
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
being flat.

ii.

As we saw in Section
2
above,
g
has constant sectional curvature
\lambda
if and only if the plane wave limit
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
along any unit-speed geodesic
\gamma(t)
has
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)x^{i}x^{j}=-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x^{i})^{2}.
By Proposition
1
, such a
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is locally conformally flat (in fact also locally symmetric). For the converse, suppose that every
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is locally conformally flat. Then at each
p\in M
, the following holds: For any geodesic
\gamma(t)
starting at
p
in the direction
\gamma^{\prime}(0)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=V
, and for any orthonormal pair
E_{i},E_{j}\in T_{p}M
orthogonal to
V
, the conditions
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ii}(t)=A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{jj}(t)
and
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=0
yield, at
t=0
,

\hskip 18.7898pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},V,V,E_{i})=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{j},V,V,E_{j})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{V}}}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},V,V,E_{j})=0,

where
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ii}(0)=A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{jj}(0)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{V}}}
. From this it follows that all 2-planes containing
V
have sectional curvatures
\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{V}}}
. Now let
W\in T_{p}M
be any other vector, and choose a unit vector
X
orthogonal to
V
and
W
. Then

\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{V}}}=\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(X,V,V,X)\,}_{\text{$\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(V,X,X,V)$}}=\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{X}}}=\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(W,X,X,W)\,}_{\text{$\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(X,W,W,X)$}}=\lambda_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{W}}}.

Thus
all
2-planes at
T_{p}M
have the same sectional curvatures. But a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with the property that its
pointwise
sectional curvatures are all equal must, as is well known, have constant sectional curvature globally on
M
.

iii.

Next, suppose that
g
is Ricci-flat. Then

(16)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ii}(t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{i})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=-\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=0.

But by (
9
), the only nonvanishing component of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
’s Ricci tensor is
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=-\!\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ii}(t)
, hence
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is Ricci-flat. As for the converse, if each
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is Ricci-flat, then each
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\big|_{\gamma(0)}=0
by (
16
). A polarization argument then yields
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v,w)=0
for all
v,w\in T_{\gamma(0)}M
.

iv.

The case when each
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is constant along
\gamma(t)
is similar to iii.; indeed, if
\gamma^{\prime}(\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}))=0
, then, similarly to (
16
),

-\gamma^{\prime}(\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}))=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ii}(t)\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d(\Delta H)}{dt}=0.

By Proposition
1
, this is equivalent to
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
having parallel Ricci tensor.

v.

Finally, if the symmetric 2-tensor
(\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}})(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)
vanishes, then

(\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}})(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=\gamma^{\prime}({\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j}))=0,

where we note that
\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}E_{i}=0
. Thus each
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
is constant. By Proposition
1
, this is equivalent to
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
being locally symmetric.

vi.

The proof is similar to that of iii. above.

vii.

By Proposition
1
,
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
will be geodesically complete if and only if its corresponding Hamiltonian system (
6
) is complete. As the
H
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is quadratic in
x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1}
, its Hamiltonian system is linear in
x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1}
. Therefore, it will be complete if and only if each
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
is defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which will be the case if and only if the geodesic
\gamma(t)
of
g
is complete.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.4.
Relation to Penrose’s original plane wave limit.

Moving on, we now shed light on the origin of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
by proving that it is, in fact, a limit in Penrose’s original sense. To do so, let us first briefly remark on the latter. On the face of it, Penrose’s original construction would seem to have no analogue in Riemannian geometry. That is because, although it is a “blowing up” process of a Lorentzian metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
, this blowing up takes place not at a point, but rather along a
lightlike
geodesic
\gamma
; i.e., one for which
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=0
but
\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
(see
[
And04
]
). Such geodesics have several non-Riemannian features, but the one in particular on which Penrose’s construction depends is that lightlike geodesics can be used to construct local coordinates
(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
in which
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
will take the form

(17)

\displaystyle(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
1&g_{22}&g_{23}&\cdots&g_{2n}\\
0&g_{32}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{33}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\cdots}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{3n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\vdots}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\ddots}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\vdots}\\
0&g_{n2}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{n3}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\cdots}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{2n}}\end{pmatrix},

where the red submatrix is positive definite (see, e.g.,
[
Pen72
, p. 60-1]
). The matrix (
17
) has the property that its signature is determined solely by the red submatrix, regardless of the components
g_{2j}
. Penrose capitalized on this fact by “zooming infinitesimally close” to the lightlike geodesic that gave rise to (
17
), in such a way as to make each
g_{2j}\to 0
, leaving behind a simpler — yet still Lorentzian, and non-flat — metric in the limit. As shown in
[
Bla+04
]
, this beautiful construction nevertheless masks some important geometry, namely, that Penrose’s limit does not, in fact, rely on the coordinates (
17
), and that what it truly encodes is the
geodesic deviation
of
\gamma(t)
, an issue to which we shall return in Section
3.2
. Guided by
[
Bla+04
]
, we now show that if one lifts geodesics
\gamma(t)
of a Riemannian metric
g
to lightlike geodesics
(t,\gamma(t))
of the Lorentzian metric
-d\tau^{2}+g
, and then takes the plane wave limit of the latter via
[
Bla+04
]
, then this is equivalent to both our Definition
1
and Penrose’s original local construction.

Theorem 2
.

The plane wave limit of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
along the unit-speed geodesic
\gamma(t)
is the Penrose plane wave limit of the Lorentzian manifold
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
along the lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(t)\!\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\!(t,\gamma(t))
.

Proof.

This proof is lengthy and proceeds in several steps. First, let us recall standard facts about product metrics (see, e.g.,
[
O’N83
, p. 89]
). To begin with, a curve
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
in
I\times M
will be a geodesic with respect to
-d\tau^{2}+g
if and only if its projections onto
I
and
M
are geodesics of
-d\tau^{2}
and
g
, respectively. Therefore,
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
will be a geodesic if and only if it is of the form
(at+b,\gamma(t))
with
\gamma(t)
a geodesic of
(M,g)
. So if
\gamma(t)
has unit speed in
(M,g)
, then
\tilde{\gamma}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=(t,\gamma(t))
is a lightlike geodesic of
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
starting at
(0,\gamma(0))
with
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{\tau}\big|_{t}+\gamma^{\prime}(t)\big|_{\gamma(t)}
. The first step in our proof is to take Penrose’s plane wave limit of
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
along
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
in the covariant manner of
[
Bla+04
]
; we claim that doing so will bring us to precisely
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
in (
2
). Indeed, choose any
g
-orthonormal frame
\{E_{2},\dots,E_{n}\}
parallel along
\gamma(t)
in
(M,g)
as in (
1
) and lift it to a frame
\{\widetilde{E}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{n}\}
in
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
along the lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
(the linearly independent set
\{\widetilde{E}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{n},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\}
will span
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\perp}\subseteq TM
; see
[
O’N83
, Lemma 28, p. 142]
). Setting
\tilde{g}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-d\tau^{2}+g
and denoting by
\widetilde{\nabla}
its Levi-Civita connection, note that each
\widetilde{E}_{i}
is parallel along
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
:
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\!\tilde{\gamma}}{\widetilde{E}_{i}}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\!\partial_{\tau}}{\widetilde{E}_{i}}+\widetilde{\nabla}_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{\widetilde{E}_{i}}=0+\nabla_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{E_{i}}=0
. According to
[
Bla+04
]
— and in perfect analogy to (
2
) — the plane wave limit of
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
along the lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
will then be

(18)

\displaystyle\text{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\tilde{\gamma}$}}}$}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=2dvdt+\Big(\!\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}\!A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\tilde{\gamma}$}}}_{ij}(t)x^{i}x^{j}\Big)(dt)^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(dx^{i})^{2},

where, because the Riemann curvature 4-tensor
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\tilde{g}$}}
satisfies

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\tilde{g}$}}(\partial_{\tau},\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)=0\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\tilde{g}$}}(X,Y,Z,V)=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(X,Y,Z,V)

for all vector fields
X,Y,Z,V
that are lifts of vector fields on
M
, we have that

(19)

\displaystyle A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\tilde{\gamma}$}}}_{ij}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\tilde{g}$}}(\widetilde{E}_{i},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\widetilde{E}_{j})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(t)}=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t).

Thus the first step. Next, we will find a coordinate transformation in which
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\tilde{\gamma}$}}}
takes on the local form of Penrose’s original limit. To do so, let us first outline Penrose’s construction. On
(I\times M,-d\tau^{2}+g)
, begin by setting up a “lightlike coordinate system” as in (
17
) that is “centered” on
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
, as follows. First, recall the existence of Riemannian
semigeodesic coordinates
: At each point of
M
, local coordinates
(r,x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
exist in which the integral curves
t\mapsto(t,x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
of
\partial_{r}
are geodesics normal to the level sets of
r
:

(20)

\displaystyle g=(dr)^{2}+\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}g_{ij}(r,x^{2},\dots,x^{n})dx^{i}dx^{j}.

It follows that
\partial_{r}=\text{grad}_{\scalebox{.6}{$g$}}r
and that
r
is a local distance function (see, e.g.,
[
Lee18
, Proposition 6.41, p. 182]
). One may further choose such coordinates so that the integral curve
t\mapsto(t,0,\dots,0)
coincides with the geodesic
\gamma(t)
. Having done so in a neighborhood
\mathscr{U}\subseteq M
, let us restrict
g
to
\mathscr{U}
and
\tilde{g}
to
I\times\mathscr{U}
. Since

\tilde{g}\big|_{I\times\mathscr{U}}=-(d\tau)^{2}+(dr)^{2}+\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}g_{ij}(r,x^{2},\dots,x^{n})dx^{i}dx^{j},

a final change of variables,
\tilde{\tau}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(r+\tau),\tilde{r}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(r-\tau)
, will put
\tilde{g}
into the form (
17
):

(21)

\displaystyle(\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta})=\underbrace{\,\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&g_{22}&g_{23}&\cdots&g_{2n}\\
0&0&g_{32}&g_{33}&\cdots&g_{3n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&g_{n2}&g_{n3}&\cdots&g_{nn}\end{pmatrix}\,}_{(\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta})~\text{in coordinates}~(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{r},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptg_{ij}\big((\tilde{\tau}+\tilde{r})/\sqrt{2},x^{2},\dots,x^{n}\big).

Furthermore, our lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
, originally in the form
(t,t,0,\dots,0)
in
I\times\mathscr{U}
, now has the expression
\tilde{\gamma}(t)=(\sqrt{2}t,0,\dots,0)
in the coordinates
(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{r},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
; i.e., after the affine reparametrization
t\mapsto t/\sqrt{2}
, it is now precisely the integral curve though the origin of the lightlike vector field
\partial_{\tilde{\tau}}
. Finally, let us relabel our coordinate system from
(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{r},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
to
(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})
. The remainder of the proof now proceeds as follows:

1.

The metric (
21
) is now in a state in which to take Penrose’s limit as in Penrose’s original construction, in
[
Pen76
]
. As we show in (
28
) below, doing so will yield a plane wave metric
h_{\scalebox{.4}{PW}}
in so called
Rosen coordinates
.

2.

Following
[
Bla+04
]
, we will then define a coordinate transformation putting
h_{\scalebox{.4}{PW}}
precisely in the form (
2
), with the
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\partial_{\tilde{0}}(h_{\scalebox{.4}{PW}}(\widetilde{E}_{i},\widetilde{E}_{j}))\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}}

2.5.
Relation to Fermi coordinates.

It is also worthwhile discussing the local nature of the Riemannian plane wave limit

in another sense, complementing
[
BFW06
]

\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n-1}\}

(t,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})
can be found in a neighborhood
\mathscr{U}
satisfying the following properties:

i.

The portion of
\gamma(t)
within
\mathscr{U}

\gamma(t)=(t,0,\dots,0)
, with
\partial_{t}\big|_{\gamma(t)}=\gamma^{\prime}(t)
and
\partial_{i}\big|_{\gamma(t)}=E_{i}\big|_{\gamma(t)}

g\big|_{\gamma(t)\cap\mathscr{U}}=\text{diag}(1,1,\dots,1)

ii.

For every
\gamma(t_{0})\in\mathscr{U}
and every
v\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}v^{i}E_{i}\in T_{\gamma(t_{0})}M
orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})
, the geodesic
\gamma_{v}(s)
starting at
\gamma(t_{0})
in the direction
v
has the coordinate expression
\gamma_{v}(s)=(t_{0},sv^{1},\dots,sv^{n-1})
(see, e.g.,
[
Lee18
, Proposition 5.26, p. 136]
; from this it follows that
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{ij}\big|_{\gamma(t)}=0
and that
\partial_{k}g_{ij}\big|_{\gamma(t)}=0
for all
i,j,k=1,\dots,n-1
and
\alpha=t,1,\dots,n-1
).

iii.

Finally, using that
J(s)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\partial_{t}\big|_{\gamma_{v}(s)}
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma_{v}(s)
satisfying

Taylor expands as

(37)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.06749ptg_{tt}\big|_{(t,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})}\!\!

\displaystyle=

[
Gra03

.) Since

and
\partial_{i}\big|_{(t,0,\dots,0)}=E_{i}\big|_{\gamma(t)}
, (
37
) resembles its counterpart in (
2
):

(38)

\displaystyle g_{tt}\big|_{(t,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})}=1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)x^{i}x^{j}+\mathscr{O}(|x|^{3}).

This is more than just a resemblance. Indeed, let us now form the Lorentzian manifold

\gamma(t)
to the lightlike geodesic

2

to

To avoid confusion in what follows, let us relabel the affine parameter as
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=(s,s,0,\dots,0)

(v,u,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})
by setting

v\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}(t-\tau),u\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}(t+\tau).

In these coordinates,
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=(0,s,0,\dots,0)
is an integral curve of
\partial_{u}
. Setting
s=u
, we now extend
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
to the entire domain of
(v,u,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})
by Taylor expanding the components of
g
as above — but with the following stipulation:
We will Taylor expand only the component
g_{uu}
, leaving unchanged all other components from the values they had along
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
.
(By expanding only in directions parallel to
\tilde{\gamma}(u)
; i.e., only along the integral curves of
\partial_{u}
, we are mimicing Penrose’s construction of “zooming infinitesimally close” to the lightlike geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
.) As
\partial_{u}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{\tau})
and
\partial_{v}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}-\partial_{\tau})
, we thus have
(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{vv}=(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{vj}=(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{uj}=0,(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{vu}=\frac{1}{2}
, while

(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})_{uu}=\frac{1}{4}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{\tau},\partial_{t}+\partial_{\tau})\overset{\eqref{eqn:gtt}}{=}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}\Big|_{(0,u,0,\dots,0)}x^{i}x^{j}+\mathscr{O}(|x|^{3}).

Thus with our stipulation in place,
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
locally takes the form

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\big|_{(v,u,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})}=dvdu+\frac{1}{4}\Big(\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\!A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(u)x^{i}x^{j}\Big)(dt)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(dx^{i})^{2}.

After scaling via
u\mapsto 2t
, this is precisely the plane wave limit metric
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
in (
2
) (recall Lemma
1
). This shows that Fermi coordinates along the geodesic
\gamma(t)
will yield
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
in
Brinkmann coordinates
(
5
). Now, if we had instead used Fermi coordinates with respect to a hypersurface
orthogonal
to
\gamma(t)
, then locally
g=(dt)^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}g_{ij}(t,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1})dx^{i}dx^{j}
, with
\gamma(t)=(t,0,\dots,0)
now an integral curve of
\partial_{t}
(see
[
Lee18
, p. 183]
; in the case of a hypersurface, the orthogonality relation is preserved even off of it). If we apply Penrose’s original scaling argument to
-d\tau^{2}+g
, then we would have arrived at
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
in so called
Rosen coordinates
; see
[
Aaz23
]
. Of course, the virtue of Definition
1
, which follows
[
Bla+04
]
, is that it does not rely on any local coordinates of
(M,g)
.

2.6.
Plane wave limit of vector fields.

To close this section, we briefly present a generalization of the Riemannian plane wave limit. Thus, let us suppose that our unit-speed geodesic
\gamma(t)
is in fact an integral curve of a smooth unit-length vector field
Z
with geodesic flow:
\nabla_{\!Z}{Z}=0
. Let
Z^{\perp}\subseteq TM
denote its orthogonal complement, which may or may not be integrable, and define the linear endomorphism

A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}\colon Z^{\perp}\longrightarrow Z^{\perp}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptX\mapsto A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}(X)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\nabla_{\!X}{Z}.

Now let
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n-1}\}
be a local orthonormal frame orthogonal to
Z
and parallel along its integral curves:
\nabla_{\!Z}{E_{i}}=0
for each
i=1,\dots,n-1
(cf., e.g.,
[
Pet16
, p. 237]
). Relative to this frame,
A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}
has (generally non-symmetric) matrix entries
(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{ij}=-g(\nabla_{\!E_{j}}{Z},E_{i})
; their derivatives along
Z
satisfy

\displaystyle Z(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-g(\nabla_{\!Z}{\nabla_{\!E_{j}}{Z}},E_{i})-g(\nabla_{\!E_{j}}{Z},\cancelto{0}{\nabla_{\!Z}{E_{i}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(Z,E_{j},Z,E_{i})-g(\nabla_{\!E_{j}}{\cancelto{0}{\nabla_{\!Z}{Z}}},E_{i})-g(\nabla_{\![Z,E_{j}]}{Z},E_{i}).

As
[Z,E_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{kj}E_{k}
, and as
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(Z,E_{j},Z,E_{i})\big|_{\gamma(t)}=A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
, we thus arrive at the following Bochner-type formula relating
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
and
A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}
:

(39)

\displaystyle A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=-\frac{d(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{ij}}{dt}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{ik}(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{kj}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}=-\frac{d(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}})_{ij}}{dt}+(A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}^{2})_{ij}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}.

Thus, if
Z
and
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n-1}\}
as above are present, then (
39
) directly relates the curvature of the plane wave limit
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
to geometric properties of the flow of
Z
, namely, its divergence (the trace of
A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}
), its “twist” (the anti-symmetric part of
A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}
), and its “shear” (the trace-free symmetric part of
A_{\scalebox{0.5}{$Z$}}
).

3.
A semi-Riemannian plane wave limit

3.1.
semi-Riemannian plane wave limit.

We now generalize to the case of a semi-Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M,g)
of any signature. Let
\gamma(t)
be a maximal geodesic in
(M,g)
of any causal character:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\text{``spacelike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})>0$},\\
\text{``timelike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<0$},\\
\text{``lightlike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=0$}.\end{array}\right.

Whatever the index of
g
, each orthogonal complement
\gamma^{\prime}(t)^{\perp}\subseteq T_{\gamma(t)}M
always has dimension
n-1
(see
[
O’N83
, Lemma 22, p. 49]
). Depending on the causality of the geodesic, however, the following is true:

Lemma 3
.

Let
\gamma(t)
be a geodesic of a semi-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with index
\nu
. At
(T_{\gamma(0)}M,g|_{\gamma(0)})
, choose an orthonormal frame
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}\subseteq T_{\gamma(0)}M
orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
and parallel transport it along
\gamma(t)
, where
r\leq n-1
is the maximal number of orthonormal vectors in
\gamma^{\prime}(0)^{\perp}
possible. Then the following is true:

1.

If
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is spacelike, then
r=n-1
and
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
has index
\nu
.

2.

If
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is timelike, then
r=n-1
and
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
has index
\nu-1
.

3.

If
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is lightlike, then
r=n-2
and
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
has index
\nu-1
.

Proof.

If
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is spacelike or timelike, then
W\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{span}\{\gamma^{\prime}(0)\}\subseteq T_{\gamma(0)}M
is nondegenerate, hence so is
W^{\perp}
and
T_{\gamma(0)}M=W\oplus W^{\perp}
, so that the indices of
W
and
W^{\perp}
sum to
\nu
(see
[
O’N83
, Lemmas 23 & 26, p. 49 & 51]
). This proves 1. and 2. If
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is lightlike, then
W
and
W^{\perp}
are both degenerate. With respect to any orthogonal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{\nu},e_{\nu+1},\dots,e_{n}\}\subseteq T_{\gamma(0)}M
with
e_{1},\dots,e_{\nu}
timelike and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=e_{1}+e_{\nu+1}
, we may represent
\gamma^{\prime}(0)^{\perp}
as
\text{span}\{e_{1}+e_{\nu+1},e_{2},\dots,e_{\nu},e_{\nu+2},\dots,e_{n}\}
. This proves 3.
∎

With that established, we may once again define along
\gamma(t)
the “wave profile” functions (
1
), but this time, with Lemma
3
in mind, we do so as follows,

(44)

\displaystyle A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j}|\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti,j=1,\dots,r,

where
\varepsilon_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g(E_{i},E_{i})=\pm 1
. Although (
44
) incorporates (
1
), do observe that this
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
is not symmetric in general:
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)=-A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ji}(t)
if
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
do not have the same causal character. Nevertheless, we now define the plane wave limit of
(M,g)
along
\gamma(t)
just as we did in Definition
1
:

Definition 2

(semi-Riemannian plane wave limit)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian
n
-manifold
(
n\geq 3
)
and
\gamma(t)
a maximal geodesic with domain
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. The Lorentzian metric
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
defined on
\mathbb{R}\times I\times\mathbb{R}^{r}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r+2}=\{(v,t,x^{1},\dots,x^{r})\}
by

(45)

\displaystyle\text{\emph{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=2dvdt+\Big(\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}\!A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)x^{i}x^{j}\Big)(dt)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(dx^{i})^{2},

where

r\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n-1&\text{if $\gamma^{\prime}$ is spacelike or timelike},\\
n-2&\text{if $\gamma^{\prime}$ is lightlike},\end{array}\right.

with the
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
’s defined via any

\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
parallel along
\gamma(t)

Note that, despite the additional
\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j}|

(M,g)
(recall Lemma

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{i},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{j})\!\!

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:psec}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-\frac{H_{ij}}{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:PWL2}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-\frac{1}{2}(A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)+A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ji}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j}|\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})&\text{if $\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}$},\\
0&\text{if $\varepsilon_{i}\neq\varepsilon_{j}$}.\end{array}\right.

This contrasts with the Riemannian case (
15
). The reason for this our choice of
A^{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{$\gamma$}}}_{ij}(t)
will become clear in Proposition
2
below. Indeed, although it is not immediately clear, what (
44
) has in fact distinguished is the following class of geodesics, a class that includes all geodesics on Riemannian manifolds as well as timelike and lightlike geodesics on Lorentzian manifolds:

Definition 3
.

A geodesic
\gamma(t)
in a semi-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is
causally independent
if there exists a parallel frame
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
along
\gamma(t)
as in Lemma
3
such that
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\big|_{\gamma(t)}=0
whenever
E_{i},E_{j}
do not have the same causal character.

Observe that if
(M,g)
is Riemannian, then all geodesics are causally independent by default; if
(M,g)
is Lorentzian, then the same is true for all timelike and lightlike geodesics. However, spacelike geodesics in Lorentzian manifolds are not, in general, causally independent, nor are spacelike, timelike, or lightlike geodesics on semi-Riemannian manifolds of arbitrary signature. Now, if a geodesic
\gamma(t)
is causally independent, and if
J
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma(t)
of the form
J(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}J^{i}(t)E_{i}
for some smooth functions
J^{1}(t),\dots,J^{r}(t)
, then the significance of Definition
3
can be seen by writing the Jacobi equation
J^{\prime\prime}+R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}=0
in matrix form, where we are assuming here that the first
k
vectors in
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
are the timelike ones:

\begin{pmatrix}\ddot{J}^{1}\\
\ddot{J}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
\ddot{J}^{r}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\,}_{\text{$k\times k$ matrix of timelike $E_{i}$'s}}&O\\
&\\
O&\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\,}_{\text{$(r-k)\times(r-k)$ matrix of spacelike $E_{i}$'s}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}J^{1}\\
J^{2}\\
\vdots\\
J^{r}\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot

Another way of appreciating the “block separability” shown here is via
\gamma(t)
’s index form. Indeed, let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\longrightarrow M
be a causally independent geodesic segment of a semi-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and consider its corresponding index form; let us assume here that
\gamma
is unit-timelike. Recall that the
index form
of
\gamma
is the symmetric bilinear form defined on the (infinite-dimensional) vector space

\displaystyle V_{0}^{\perp}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{\text{piecewise smooth vector fields along $\gamma\,:\,$}

\displaystyle\hskip-72.26999ptg(V,\gamma^{\prime})=0\ ,V(a)=V(b)=0\}

by

I(V,W)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\!\int_{a}^{b}\big(g(V^{\prime},W^{\prime})-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(V,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},W)\big)dt\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptV,W\in V_{0}^{\perp}.

Writing
V=\sum_{i=1}^{r}V^{i}E_{i}
, we may then identify
V_{t}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}V^{i}E_{i}
and
V_{s}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}V^{i}E_{i}
as
V
’s “timelike” and “spacelike” parts, respectively; doing the same for
W
yields
W=W_{t}+W_{s}
. Then the causal independence of
\gamma(t)
is easily seen to yield

(49)

\displaystyle I(V,W)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\!\int_{a}^{b}\big(g(V^{\prime}_{t},W^{\prime}_{t})-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(V_{t},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},W_{t})\big)dt

\displaystyle\ \ -\!\int_{a}^{b}\big(g(V^{\prime}_{s},W^{\prime}_{s})-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(V_{s},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},W_{s})\big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!I(V_{t},W_{t})+I(V_{s},W_{s}),

so that the index form necessarily separates into timelike and spacelike parts as well. This suggests that a Morse Index Theorem should hold for such geodesics — even given the pathological conjugate-point behavior that holds in the semi-Riemannian case in general, as was shown in
[
Hel94
,
PT03
]
. As we’ll see in Proposition
2
and Theorem
3
below, this is indeed the case — and the virtue of the plane wave limit
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is that we can bypass the need to re-analyze the variational calculus involved in the Morse Index Theorem and instead pass over directly to its (already established) Lorentzian version (see (
63
) below). Before doing so, let us provide an example of a causally independent geodesic. Consider the following “pp-wave-type” metric on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{(v,t,x,y)\}
with signature
(-\!-\!++)
and
H(t,x,y)
a smooth function:

h\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=2dvdt+H(t,x,y)(dt)^{2}-(dx)^{2}+(dy)^{2}.

The only difference between the Christoffel symbols of
h
and those of the Lorentzian pp-waves (
7
) is the red plus sign appearing here:

\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\partial_{x}}{\partial_{t}}=\frac{H_{x}}{2}\partial_{v}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\partial_{y}}{\partial_{t}}=\frac{H_{y}}{2}\partial_{v}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\partial_{t}}{\partial_{t}}=\frac{H_{t}}{2}\partial_{v}\,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}+}\,\frac{1}{2}H_{x}\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}H_{y}\partial_{y}.

As a consequence, it is straightforward to verify that, while the only nonvanishing components of the Riemann curvature tensor of
h
are, like (
8
), still

(50)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{x})=-\frac{H_{xx}}{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{y},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{y})=-\frac{H_{yy}}{2},

\displaystyle\hskip-72.26999pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{y})=-\frac{H_{xy}}{2},

the Ricci tensor now takes the form

(51)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=-\frac{1}{2}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}H_{xx}+H_{yy}),

with all other components still vanishing. Now set
H(t,x,y)=x^{2}-y^{2}
and consider the curve
\gamma(s)=(0,s,0,0)
. Since
\gamma^{\prime}(s)=\partial_{t}\big|_{\gamma(s)}
,

h(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\Big|_{\gamma(s)}=H\Big|_{\gamma(s)}=0\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}H_{x}\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}H_{y}\partial_{y}\Big|_{\gamma(s)}=0,

so that
\gamma(s)
is a (complete) lightlike geodesic. Furthermore,
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
are, respectively, unit timelike and spacelike vector fields which, when restricted to
\gamma(s)
, are parallel along it:

\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{\partial_{x}}=\frac{H_{x}}{2}\partial_{v}\Big|_{\gamma(s)}=0=\frac{H_{y}}{2}\partial_{v}\Big|_{\gamma(s)}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}_{\!\gamma^{\prime}}{\partial_{y}}.

We may therefore take
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}=\{\partial_{x}\big|_{\gamma(s)},\partial_{y}\big|_{\gamma(s)}\}
in Lemma
3
. An application of (
50
) thus shows that
\gamma(s)
is causally independent:

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\partial_{x})=-1\hskip 3.61371pt,\hskip 3.61371pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{y},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\partial_{y})=+1\hskip 3.61371pt,\hskip 3.61371pt\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\partial_{y})=0.

(This example generalizes easily to arbitrary dimension and signature.) Now notice one further property of
\gamma(s)
, namely, that it has conjugate points. Indeed, the vector field

J(s)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=(\sin s)\partial_{x}\big|_{\gamma(s)}

is a Jacobi field along
\gamma(s)
. This follows because
J^{\prime\prime}(s)=-(\sin s)\partial_{x}\big|_{\gamma(s)}
and
R_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\big|_{\gamma(s)}=(\sin s)R_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}\big|_{\gamma(s)}
, so that

h(J^{\prime\prime}+R_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(J,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime},\partial_{x})=-(\sin s)h_{xx}+(\sin s)\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\partial_{x},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\partial_{x})=0,

with all other components vanishing also. As we will show in Theorem
3
below, this is no coincidence: The completeness of
\gamma(s)
, together with the fact that
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\geq 0
(recall (
51
)) while
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\neq 0
,
guarantees
it. This is, in fact, the semi-Riemannian generalization of the classical focusing result
[
HP70
]
. As we now show, it is also a natural application of our semi-Riemannian plane wave limit.

3.2.
Geodesic deviation of causally independent geodesics.

To do so, we should first address the reason for the inclusion of the factor “
\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j}|
” in (
44
). As we now show, its inclusion is required so that
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
has the same property as was shown to hold for Penrose’s original limit in
[
Bla+04
]
— namely, so that
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
encodes
\gamma(t)
’s
geodesic deviation
onto the geodesics of the plane wave
(\mathbb{R}^{r+2},g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})
. When
g
is semi-Riemannian, the only caveat is that
\gamma
must be causally independent:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\gamma(t)
be a causally independent geodesic of a semi-Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M,g)

(
n\geq 3
)
and let
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
be the plane wave limit of
(M,g)
along
\gamma(t)
as in Definition
2
. Then any geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfying
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\neq 0
has conjugate points if and only if
\gamma(t)
does.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k},E_{k+1},\dots,E_{r}\}
be a parallel frame along
\gamma(t)
as in Definition
3
, with
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\}
the timelike vectors (by Lemma
3
,
k=\nu
if
\gamma(t)
is spacelike and
k=\nu-1
if
\gamma(t)
is timelike or lightlike,
\nu
being the index of
g
). We may express any normal Jacobi field
J(t)
along
\gamma(t)
as
J(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}J^{i}(t)E_{i}
for some smooth functions
J^{1}(t),\dots,J^{r}(t)
. Now, let
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
be a geodesic of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfying
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\neq 0
. As
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)
must be linear in
s
(recall (
2.2
)), and as we are assuming that
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{t}=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\neq 0
, we may rescale the affine parameter
s
if necessary so that
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)=s
; i.e., so that
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
’s domain coincides with the maximal domain
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of
\gamma(t)
. Suppose that this
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
has a pair of conjugate points; then there exists a nontrivial Jacobi field
\tilde{J}(s)
along
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
vanishing at two distinct points (and hence orthogonal to
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)
). Expressing
\tilde{J}(s)
as
\tilde{J}(s)=(\tilde{J}^{v}(s),\tilde{J}^{t}(s),\tilde{J}^{1}(s),\dots,\tilde{J}^{r}(s))
in the coordinates (
45
), observe that
\tilde{J}^{t}(s)=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\partial_{v})\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}
, too, must be linear in
s
, because
\partial_{v}
is parallel:

\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{t}(s)=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J}^{\prime\prime},\partial_{v})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\tilde{J},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=0.

Thus if
\tilde{J}(s)
is to be zero at two distinct points, then
\tilde{J}^{t}(s)=0
. Furthermore, if we use the fact that
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=0
to solve for
\tilde{J}^{v}
in terms of
\tilde{J}^{i},\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{i}
, then we arrive at the following expression for
\tilde{J}(s)
:

(52)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(s)=-\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}^{i}(s)\Big)\partial_{v}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\partial_{i}\,\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}.

In particular, this shows that the functions
\tilde{J}^{1}(s),\dots,\tilde{J}^{r}(s)
cannot all vanish, as
\tilde{J}(s)
is nontrivial. With this in mind, and recalling that
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\}
are the timelike vectors and
\{E_{k+1},\dots,E_{r}\}
the spacelike ones, we now claim that the following two vector fields along
\gamma(t)
in
(M,g)
,

(53)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{J}^{j}(t)E_{j}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\!\!\sum_{j=k+1}^{r}\!\!\tilde{J}^{j}(t)E_{j},

are both Jacobi fields (by our remark above, note that they cannot both be trivial). Indeed, observe that each
\tilde{J}^{j}(t)=\tilde{J}^{j}(s)
in (
53
) can be expressed as
\tilde{J}^{j}(s)=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\partial_{j})\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}
; differentiating the latter along
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
, and noting via (
7
) that

\nabla^{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}_{\!\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)}\partial_{j}=\nabla^{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}_{\!\partial_{t}}\partial_{j}\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=\frac{1}{2}H_{j}(\tilde{\gamma}(s))\partial_{v}\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)},

we have

\dot{\tilde{J}}^{j}(s)=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J}^{\prime},\partial_{j})+\frac{1}{2}H_{j}(\tilde{\gamma}(s))\cancelto{0}{g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\partial_{v})}\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptj=1,\dots,r,

where the second term on the right-hand sides vanishes because
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\partial_{v})=\tilde{J}^{t}(s)=0
. A second derivative likewise yields
\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{j}(s)=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J}^{\prime\prime},\partial_{j})\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}
, using the fact that
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J},\partial_{v})^{\prime}\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}=0
. The
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Jacobi equation for
\tilde{J}(s)
now yields, for each
j=1,\dots,r
,

\displaystyle\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{j}(s)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{J}^{\prime\prime},\partial_{j})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\tilde{J},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{j})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{i},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{j})\,}_{\text{$\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{i},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\partial_{j})$}}\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:some0}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\text{$\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j}|\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})&\text{if $\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{j}$},\\
0&\text{if $\varepsilon_{i}\neq\varepsilon_{j}$}.\end{array}\right.$}

Consider now
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}
in (
53
), for which
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, hence
\varepsilon_{j}=-1
. For these
j
, the nonzero terms in (
3.2
) will arise when
\varepsilon_{i}=-1
; i.e., when
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
:

(57)

\displaystyle\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{j}(s)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(s)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptj=1,\dots,k,

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:newJg}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(s)}.

Likewise for
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}
in (
53
), for which
j\in\{k+1,\dots,r\}
, so that
\varepsilon_{j}=+1
. Now the nonzero terms in (
3.2
) will arise when
\varepsilon_{i}=+1
, when
i\in\{k+1,\dots,r\}
:

(58)

\displaystyle\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{j}(s)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\!\!\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}\!\!\tilde{J}^{i}(s)\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(s)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptj=k+1,\dots,r,

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:newJg}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\Big|_{\gamma(s)}.

We can now verify that
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}(t)
and
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}(t)
are indeed Jacobi fields along
\gamma(t)
in
(M,g)
. To begin with, the vector field
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime}+R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}
is
g
-orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}
because
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime}
and
R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}
are, hence it lies in
\text{span}\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
(or
\text{span}\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r},\gamma^{\prime}\}
if
\gamma(t)
is lightlike). Verifying that
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime}+R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}=0
therefore means verifying that
g(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime}+R_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime},E_{i})=0
for each
i=1,\dots,r
(as well as with
\gamma^{\prime}
if
\gamma(t)
is lightlike, though this latter verification is trivial, since
g(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=g(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime})^{\prime\prime}=0=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
). To do so, we first call upon (
57
), which tells us that for (timelike)
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
,

g(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime},E_{j})\overset{\eqref{eqn:newJg}}{=}-\ddot{\tilde{J}}^{j}\overset{\eqref{eqn:last_t}}{=}-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptj=1,\dots,k.

For
E_{k+1},\dots,E_{r}
, we use the fact that
\gamma(t)
is causally independent:

g(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}^{\prime\prime},E_{j})\overset{\eqref{eqn:newJg}}{=}0=-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptj=k+1,\dots,r.

Thus
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}(t)
is indeed a Jacobi field along
\gamma(t)
; if it is nontrivial, then it vanishes at two distinct points — in fact at exactly the same affine parameter values as
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
. A similar analysis and conclusion follows for
\tilde{J}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}(t)
, via (
58
). Conversely, if
J(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}J^{i}(t)E_{i}
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma(t)
, then
J(t)=J_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}(t)+J_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}(t)
as in (
53
), and the reverse of the computation that led to (
57
)-(
58
) shows that (
52
), with
J^{i}(s)
in place of
\tilde{J}^{i}(s)
, will be a normal Jacobi field along any geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfying
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)=s
. In particular,
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
will have conjugate points if
\gamma(t)
does — at the same affine parameter values once again. Note that if
J(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}J^{i}(t)E_{i}
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma(t)
and the latter is causally independent, then at least one of
J_{\scalebox{0.6}{$t$}}(t)
or
J_{\scalebox{0.6}{$s$}}(t)
must be nontrivial. Thus the correspondence between Jacobi fields vanishing at two points is at most two-to-one: Up to two along
\gamma(t)
for every one along
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
.
∎

Before using Proposition
2
to generalize
[
HP70
]
, let us record the following easy corollary, which is immediate:

Corollary 1
.

Let
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
be the plane wave limit of a causally independent geodesic
\gamma
. If
\gamma
has a pair of conjugate points, then all geodesics
\tilde{\gamma}
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfying
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\partial_{v})\neq 0
have conjugate points.

3.3.
Application to conjugate points

We now apply Proposition
2
towards extending
[
HP70
]
(see also
[
BEE96
, Prop. 12.10,12.17]
) and
[
OE80
, Theorem 2]
to all causally independent geodesics on semi-Riemannian manifolds:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian
n
-manifold
(
n\geq 3
)
and
\gamma
a complete causally independent geodesic. If
\gamma
has no conjugate points and
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\geq 0
, then
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\big|_{\gamma^{\prime\perp}}=0
. Furthermore, causally independent geodesic segments always have finitely many conjugate points.

Proof.

We will prove that if
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\big|_{\gamma(0)^{\perp}}\neq 0
, then
\gamma(t)
must have a pair of conjugate points. To begin with, given a parallel frame
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
along
\gamma(t)
such that
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(E_{i},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},E_{j})=0
whenever
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
have different causal characters, the condition
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)\big|_{\gamma(0)^{\perp}}\neq 0
implies the existence of a nonzero vector
X\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{i}E_{i}\big|_{\gamma(0)}\in\gamma^{\prime}(0)^{\perp}
such that

(59)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(X,\gamma^{\prime}(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0),X)\neq 0.

Meanwhile, the Ricci tensor of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfies

(60)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})\overset{\eqref{eqn:pRic}}{=}-\!\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{ii}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\gamma$}}(t)\overset{\eqref{eqn:geod2}}{=}\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\geq 0.

By Theorem
1
(vii), if
\gamma(t)
is complete, then the plane wave limit
(\mathbb{R}^{r+2},g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})
will be geodesically complete also. By Proposition
2
, a geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
satisfying
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)=s
has conjugate points if and only if
\gamma(t)
does. For any such geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
, if we set
\widetilde{X}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{i}\partial_{i}\big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(0)}
, where
a^{1},\dots,a^{r}
are the same coefficients as for
X
above (note that
\widetilde{X}
is
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-spacelike), then

(61)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\widetilde{X},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\widetilde{X})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(0)}\overset{\eqref{eqn:psec}}{=}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\widetilde{X},\partial_{t},\partial_{t},\widetilde{X})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(0)}\overset{\eqref{eqn:some0},\eqref{eqn:prop2}}{\neq}0

and

(62)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}\overset{\eqref{eqn:pRic}}{=}\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})\Big|_{\tilde{\gamma}(s)}\overset{\eqref{eqn:prop1}}{\geq}0.

Now pick any timelike such geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
with the additional property that
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0)
is
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthogonal to
\widetilde{X}
. This fact, together with (
61
) and (
62
), are exactly the conditions needed to ensure that
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
will have a pair of conjugate points; see
[
BEE96
, Proposition 12.10]
. By Proposition
2
, so must
\gamma(t)
in
(M,g)
. Finally, the statement about finitely many conjugate points follows from the Lorentzian Morse Index Theorem. Indeed, let
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r+2}
be any timelike geodesic segment of
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
with
\tilde{\gamma}^{t}(s)=s
. Then its index form is given by

I(V,W)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\!\int_{a}^{b}\big(g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(V^{\prime},W^{\prime})-\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}$}}(V,\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},W)\big)ds\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptV,W\in V_{0}^{\perp}.

Recall that the
index of
\tilde{\gamma}
, denoted
\text{Ind}(\tilde{\gamma})
, is defined to be the supremum over the dimensions of all subspaces
A\subseteq V_{0}^{\perp}
on which the restriction
I\big|_{A\times A}
is positive-definite. Next, for each
s_{*}\in(a,b)
, let
\text{dim}(\tilde{J}_{s_{*}})
denote the dimension of the subspace spanned by all Jacobi fields along
\tilde{\gamma}(s)
vanishing at
s=a
and
s=s_{*}
; thus if
\text{dim}(\tilde{J}_{s_{*}})=m>0
, then
\tilde{\gamma}(s_{*})
is conjugate to
\tilde{\gamma}(a)
with multiplicity
m
. The (timelike) Morse Index Theorem (see
[
BEE96
, Theorem 10.27]
) then says that

\text{Ind}(\tilde{\gamma})=\!\!\sum_{s\in(a,b)}\!\!\text{dim}(\tilde{J}_{s})\hskip 14.45377pt\text{and the latter sum is finite}.

By Proposition
2
, we thus have the following “Morse Index Theorem” for causally independent geodesics
\gamma\colon[a,b]\longrightarrow M
of semi-Riemannian manifolds: If we denote by
\text{dim}(J_{t_{*}})
the dimension of the subspace spanned by all Jacobi fields along
\gamma(t)
vanishing at
t=a
and
t=t_{*}
, and if we recall once again that
s=t
, then

(63)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,\text{dim}(J_{t})\leq 2\,\text{dim}(\tilde{J}_{t})\,}_{\text{for each $t\in(a,b)$, by Proposition \ref{prop:conj}}}\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\sum_{t\in(a,b)}\text{dim}(J_{t})\leq 2\,\text{Ind}(\tilde{\gamma}).

Thus the number of conjugate points along
\gamma(t)
in
(M,g)
, counting multiplicity, is not only finite, but at most twice the index of any timelike geodesic segment of the plane wave limit
g^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\gamma$}}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
of
\gamma(t)
.
∎

Note that the factor of two in (

) is to be expected, given our observation about the splitting of the index form in (

). Finally, it is not hard to show that if
\gamma
is spacelike or timelike (but not lightlike), then the conclusion of Theorem

can be strengthened, to
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\cdot)=0
.

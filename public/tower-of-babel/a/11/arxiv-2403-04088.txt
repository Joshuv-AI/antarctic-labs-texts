The classification of endotrivial complexes
Sam K. Miller

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group and k k a field of prime characteristic p p . We give a complete classification of endotrivial complexes, i.e. determine the Picard group ℰ k ​ ( G ) \mathcal{E}_{k}(G) of the tensor-triangulated category K b ​ ( 𝐭𝐫𝐢𝐯 k ​ G ) K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}) , the bounded homotopy category of p p -permutation modules, which Balmer and Gallauer recently considered in [ BG23b ] . For p p -groups, we identify ℰ k ​ ( − ) \mathcal{E}_{k}(-) with the rational p p -biset functor C ​ F b ​ ( − ) CF_{b}(-) of Borel-Smith functions and recover a short exact sequence of rational p p -biset functors constructed by Bouc and Yalçin. As a consequence, we prove that every p p -permutation autoequivalence of a p p -group arises from a splendid Rickard autoequivalence. Additionally, we give a positive answer to a question of Gelvin and Yalçin in [ GY21 ] , showing the kernel of the Bouc homomorphism for an arbitrary finite group G G is described by superclass functions f : s p ​ ( G ) → ℤ f:s_{p}(G)\to\mathbb{Z} satisfying the oriented Artin-Borel-Smith conditions.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of prime characteristic
p
. In
[
Mil24
]
we considered the notion of an endotrivial complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules, i.e. an invertible object in the tensor-triangulated category
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
, the bounded homotopy category of finitely generated
p
-permutation
kG
-modules. This tt-category has been studied in great detail recently by Balmer and Gallauer
[
BG20
,
BG22
,
BG23b
]
; in particular, the authors deduce its Balmer spectrum. Endotrivial complexes are analogues of endotrivial modules, the invertible objects of the stable module category
{}_{kG}\underline{\mathbf{mod}}
, which have been studied extensively by representation theorists (see
[
Maz19
]
for a detailed account). In
[
Mil25
]
, we introduced notions of relatively endotrivial chain complexes, chain complex-theoretic analogues of relatively endotrivial
kG
-modules, which were first introduced by Lassueur
[
Las11
]
.

Our main result of this paper is a complete classification of endotrivial complexes. We determine the parametrizing group
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
, i.e. the Picard group of
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
, see Definition
2.4
.

Theorem

(Corollary
6.4
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group. We have a split exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})\to\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\xrightarrow{h}CF_{b}(G,p)\to 0.

Here,

denotes the additive group of Borel-Smith superclass functions

valued on
p
-subgroups (see Definition
4.4
), and
h
denotes the h-mark homomorphism (see Proposition
2.8
). In particular, if
G
is a
p
-group, we have an isomorphism

.

We apply much of the theory developed in
[
Mil24
,
Mil25
]
to derive this classification and beyond, answering questions concerning endotrivial complexes, the Bouc homomorphism (see Definition
3.11
), and splendid Rickard equivalences. A key structure in this study is the Dade group
D_{k}(G)
of a finite group. First introduced by Dade in
[
Dad78a
]
and
[
Dad78b
]
for the case when

is a
p
-group,

parameterizes the “capped” endopermutation
kG
-modules.
D_{k}(G)
was generalized to all finite groups

by Linckelmann and Mazza in
[

]
, by Lassueur in
[

]
, and by Gelvin and Yalçin in
[
GY21
]
via separate techniques. In Lassueur’s construction, the generalized Dade group

parameterizes the “strongly capped” endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-modules. We define the

-module

as follows:

V(\mathcal{F}_{G}):=\bigoplus_{P\in[s_{p}(G)\setminus\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)]}k[G/P].

-permutation modules of the group

of

-endosplit-trivial complexes, as defined in

]

-endotrivial module. This observation gives us a short exact sequence, which is detailed in Theorem

Bouc and Yalçin’s short exact sequence above determines the kernel of the Bouc homomorphism

Theorem

(Theorem

)
.

We have
\ker(\Psi)=CF_{ba^{+}}(G,p)

The proof relies on the Lefschetz homomorphism

Theorem

(Theorem

)
.

Let
G

p
-group.
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective.

We obtain as a rather striking and surprising corollary that if

is a

Theorem

(Theorem

)
.

Let

Finally, we remark that the classification of endotrivial complexes should have significant implications for our understanding of the geometry of the tensor-triangulated category
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
. Moreover, by the equivalences of categories outlined in
[
BG23a
]
, Corollary

also provides the classification of invertible objects in certain categories of Artin motives and cohomological Mackey functors. We describe one such possibility the classification opens: in
[

, a “twisted cohomology ring” is constructed to determine the topology of the Balmer spectrum of

This also opens the door for gluing techniques with respect to localization, akin to those performed in
[
BBC09
]
. However, we leave this exploration for a future article - the scope of this paper does not include tensor-triangulated geometry.

Remark 2.17

We briefly note on endosplit-trivial complexes in the wild. In this paper, we are mostly concerned with either endotrivial complexes (i.e.

3.
Capped endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions and the Dade group of a finite group

In this section, we construct a short exact sequence relating
V
-endosplit-trivial complexes, endotrivial complexes, and the generalized Dade group as defined by Lassueur in
[
Las13
]
. We also determine up to equivalence which strongly capped endo-
p
-permutation modules, as defined by Lassueur, have endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions.

Definition 3.1
.

(a)

We say a
kG
-module
M
is
endopermutation
(resp.
endo-
p
-permutation
) if
M^{*}\otimes_{k}M
is a permutation module (resp.
p
-permutation module).

(b)

The following definition is due to Dade in
[
Dad78a
]
. Let
P
be a
p
-group. We say an endopermutation
kP
-module
M
is
capped
if
M
has a direct summand with vertex
P
. Equivalently,
M
is capped if
(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)(P)
is nonzero, and equivalently,
M^{*}\otimes_{k}M
has
k
as a direct summand. If
M_{0}
is an indecomposable summand of
M
with vertex
P
, we say
M_{0}
is the
cap
of
M
.

Define an equivalence relation on the set of capped endopermutation
kP
-modules as follows: say
M\sim N
if and only if
M\oplus N
is an endopermutation
kP
-module. Equivalently,
M\otimes_{k}N^{*}
is endopermutation. It follows by a lemma of Dade that each equivalence class has a unique indecomposable representative. Therefore, two endopermutation
kP
-module are equivalent if and only if they have isomorphic caps. Write
D_{k}(P)
for the set of equivalence classes of capped endopermutation modules. This forms an abelian group with addition induced by
\otimes_{k}
, and is called
the Dade group
.

(c)

A generalization of the Dade group to non-
p
-groups was first constructed by Linckelmann and Mazza in
[
LM08
]
, and later described using separate techniques by Lassueur in
[
Las13
]
. We give the definition in
[
Las13
]
. An endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-module
M
is
strongly capped
if
M
is
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endotrivial. In this case,
M
has a unique indecomposable strongly capped direct summand, and all other direct summands are
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-projective. As before, say this unique summand is the
cap
of
M
.

Define an equivalence relation on the set of strongly capped endo-
p
-permutation modules as before: given two endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-modules
M,N
, say
M\sim N
if and only if
M
and
N
have isomorphic caps. It follows that each equivalence class has a unique indecomposable representative. Write
D_{k}(G)
for the set of equivalence classes of strongly capped endo-
p
-permutation modules. This forms an abelian group as before with addition induced by
\otimes_{k}
, and is called
the generalized Dade group
. If
G
is a
p
-group, we recover the classical Dade group, and for this reason, we refer to
D_{k}(G)
as the
Dade group
without further mention.

Recall that
X(G)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of
k
-dimension one
kG
-modules. We have a series of inclusions

X(G)\hookrightarrow T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)\hookrightarrow D_{k}(G)\hookrightarrow T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G).

In general, these maps will not be surjective.

(d)

We extend this notion to
V
-endosplit-trivial
kG
-complexes. Say a chain complex of
kG
-modules
C
is
capped
if
C
is a
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endosplit-trivial complex. In this case,
C
is a shifted endosplit
p
-permutation resolution of a strongly capped endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-module (see
[
Mil25
, Remark 9.11]
).

The equivalence relation used for the prior definition of

is already encoded in
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)
. In this way,
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)
is a chain-complex theoretic “Dade group.”

Definition 3.2
.

(a)

For a
G
-set

, recall

is the kernel of the augmentation homomorphism

. Define the element
\Omega_{X}\in D_{k}(G)
as follows:

\Omega_{X}=\begin{cases}[\Delta(X)]&\text{if }X^{S}=\emptyset.\\
[k]&\text{if }X^{S}\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

This is well-defined since

is

-endotrivial. Indeed, the chain complex

is a
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endosplit-trivial complex.
If

for some

, we set

.

(b)

Let

be the subgroup generated by elements of the form

where

runs over all

is a

(see for instance

The following theorem gives a generating set of

.

Proposition 3.3
.

[

, Lemma 12.1]

The group

is generated by the relative syzygies

, where

This was proven using separate techniques in

as well.

Remark 3.4
.

Let

It follows that every element of

However, every element which has an endosplit

-endotrivial.

This observation is an analogue of the characterization for

for any

-groups. Note that the elements of

Definition 3.5
.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
the subgroup of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
consisting of endotrivial complexes

for which
\mathcal{H}_{C}(1)=[k]
. We call this subgroup
the homology-normalized subgroup of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
.
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
identifies with the subgroup of
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)
consisting of equivalence classes of complexes

as well.

Since

-endosplit-trivial complexes are equivalently shifted endosplit

-permutation resolutions of

-endotrivial

consists of all equivalence classes of strongly capped endo-

-permutation modules which have endosplit

-permutation resolutions. The next theorem characterizes precisely which strongly capped endo-

-permutation modules, up to equivalence in the Dade group, have an endosplit

-permutation resolution.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
M
be a strongly capped endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-module.
[M]\in D_{k}(G)
contains a representative which has an endosplit

-permutation resolution if and only if
[M]\in D_{k}^{\Omega}(G)+T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)
.

Proof.

The reverse direction follows since any
\Omega_{X}\in D_{k}^{\Omega}(G)
has the corresponding endosplit
p
-permutation resolution

and any
[M]\in T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)
has
M[0]
as an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution.

For the forward direction, suppose

has an endo-
p
-permutation resolution. Since

is
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endotrivial, there exists an
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endosplit-trivial complex
C
with
\mathcal{H}(C)=[M]\in D_{k}(G)
.

Theorem

implies that

shares a cap with a tensor product of the chain complexes

, their duals, and elements of

considered as chain complexes in degree 0. It follows that

[M]=\mathcal{H}(C)=[M_{0}]+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\Omega_{Q_{i}}^{\pm 1}\in D_{k}(G),

where each

is some

-subgroup which is non-Sylow, and

is a trivial source

-endotrivial module. Thus

, as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

We have a well-defined group homomorphism
\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)\to D_{k}(G)
induced by
[C]\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{C}(1)
.
\ker(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{im}(\mathcal{H})=D^{\Omega}_{k}(G)+T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)
, so we have a short exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}D_{k}^{\Omega}(G)+T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)\to 0.

In particular, if
G
satisfies
D^{\Omega}_{k}(G)+T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G,S)=D_{k}(G)
, then
\mathcal{H}
is surjective.

Proof.

\mathcal{H}
is a well-defined group homomorphism from Theorem
2.8
after projection, and Theorem

, which demonstrates that the image of

is generated by endo-

-permutation

\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}
is spanned by

, all of which have endo-

-permutation homology.

Given any

-endosplit-trivial complex

\mathcal{H}(C)=[k]\in D_{k}(G)
if and only if
\mathcal{H}(C)=[k]\in T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)

\ker(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)

Now,

follows directly from Theorem

-endosplit-trivial complexes are equivalently shifted endosplit

∎

Corollary 3.9

Let

-endotrivial

Corollary 3.10

Let

Proof.

This follows from the short exact sequence in Theorem

Remark 4.2

If

∎

Theorem 4.12

Let

[

6.2.
The kernel of the Bouc homomorphism

Remark 6.5
.

Theorem
6.4
gives us all possible h-marks which arise from endotrivial complexes, and Theorem

gives a recipe for reconstructing an endotrivial complex from its h-marks, up to a twist by a strongly capped indecomposable endo-

-permutation

-permutation

-group case, the situation is less clear.

Recall in Theorem

Then, the chain complex

is

-endosplit-trivial. One goal is to determine when

has an endotrivial summand, and if not, a

for which

has an endotrivial summand. An even stronger question we can ask is whether

Observe that

is generated by a product of chain complexes representing relative syzygies. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 6.6

Define the subgroup
{}^{\Omega}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)\leq\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)
by

Finally, define
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\Omega}(G):=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cap\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\Omega}(G)
. It is straightforward that

\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}:{}^{\Omega}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}\to D_{k}^{\Omega}(G)

where

is the surjection.

Rephrasing the previous remark, we wish to determine
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\Omega}(G)
or
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\Omega}(G)

[

K/L
and
H/K
.

Let
D=\operatorname{Res}^{N_{G}(L)/L}_{H/L}C(L)
. Then

h_{D}(K/L)=h_{C}(K)\text{ and }h_{D}(L/L)=h_{C}(L)

by
[
Mil24
, Proposition 4.3]
. Moreover, the values of
r
and
l
do not change. Therefore, it suffices to show
h_{D}
satisfies
h_{D}(L/L)\equiv h_{D}(K/L)\mod 2q^{r-l}
. Notice that
H/L
satisfies the conditions of
[
GY21
, Lemma 9.11]
, with
K/L
as the subgroup
Q
in the lemma. Therefore, by
[
GY21
, Lemma 9.11]
, if
h_{D}\in\ker(\Psi)
, the congruence holds, and we are done. This occurs if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{D}(1)=[k]\in D^{\Omega}(H/K)
by Proposition
6.10
. Note that

\operatorname{Res}^{N_{G}(L)/L}_{H/L}\mathcal{H}_{C}(L)=\mathcal{H}_{D}(1)\in D^{\Omega}(H/K).

On the other hand,

\operatorname{Res}^{N_{G}(L)/L}_{H/L}\mathcal{H}_{C}(L)=[k_{\varphi_{L}|_{H/L}}],

where
\varphi_{L}
is the character corresponding to
u
from the decomposition in
[
BC23
, Theorem C]
Thus, it suffices to show that for any
x\in H
,
\varphi_{L}(xL)=1.

Since
h_{C}\in\ker(\Psi)
,
\varphi_{1}
is the trivial character. By
[
BC23
, Theorem C]
, we have that for any
x\in H
,

1=\varphi_{1}(x)=\varphi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}(x\langle x_{p}\rangle)=\varphi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}(x_{p^{\prime}}\langle x_{p}\rangle).

We claim that for every coset
xL
of
H/L
, there exists a
y\in H
for which
\varphi_{L}(yL)=\varphi_{L}(xL)
and
y_{p}
is a generator of
L
, where
y=y_{p}y_{p^{\prime}}
is the unique decomposition of
y
into
p
- and
p^{\prime}
-parts. Indeed, if
\langle x_{p}\rangle=L
,
y=x
suffices. If not, then since
K
is a
p
-group,
x_{p}\in K
, and there exists some
k\in K
for which
x_{p}k
is a generator of
L\leq K
. Let
y=xk
. Then by construction,
\langle y_{p}\rangle=\langle x_{p}k\rangle=L
. Now, since
\varphi_{L}
is a linear Brauer character and
k
is a
p
-element of
H

\varphi_{L}(yL)=\varphi_{L}(xkL)=\varphi_{L}(x_{p^{\prime}}L)=\varphi_{L}(xL),

as desired. Therefore, for every coset
xL\in H/L
, we have

1=\varphi_{1}(y)=\varphi_{L}(yL)=\varphi_{L}(xL),

thus
\varphi_{L}
is the trivial character, and we are done.

∎

Corollary 6.14
.

Let

. For

The

-endosplit-trivial chain complex

For the reverse direction, it follows from the proof of Theorem

It remains unclear what occurs if

7.
Extending virtual modules to invertible complexes for
p
-groups

In this section, we use results from
[
BY07
]
to prove that the Lefschetz homomorphism,

\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG)),\,[C]\mapsto\Lambda(C):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}[C_{i}],

is surjective when
G
is a
p
-group, giving a positive answer for a question posed by the author in
[
Mil24
]
. As a corollary, we show that every
p
-permutation autoequivalence of a
p
-group (as defined by Boltje and Xu in
[
BX08
]
) is the Lefschetz invariant of some splendid Rickard autoequivalence of the group algebra.

7.1.
Surjectivity of the Lefschetz homomorphism

Remark 7.1
.

If
G
is a
p
-group, every permutation
kG
-module is indecomposable. In this case, we have a canonical ring isomorphism

B(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}T(kG),[X]\mapsto[kX].

Moreover,
(-)^{*}
is the identity map on
T(kG)
, therefore
B(G)^{\times}\cong T(kG)^{\times}\cong O(T(kG))
.

B(G)^{\times}
is famously difficult to describe for all finite groups. In fact, by an argument of tom Dieck in
[
tD79
]
, the statement “if
G
has odd order,
B(G)^{\times}\cong C_{2}
” is equivalent to the Feit-Thompson theorem.

However, in the case for which
G
is a
p
-group, Yalçin in
[
Yal05
]
gave a complete generating set of
B(G)^{\times}
, which Bouc later refined to a basis in
[
Bou06
]
by realizing
B^{\times}
as a rational
p
-biset functor. Note the only interesting case is when
p=2
. The biset operations on
B^{\times}
are described by Bouc’s
generalized tensor induction
. For details, we refer the reader to
[
Bou10a
, Section 11.2]
, but they will not be necessary for the scope of this paper.

For any finite group
G
,
B(G)
is be characterized similarly to how endotrivial complexes are via h-marks. For a
G
-set
X
, its
mark at
H
is the integer
|X^{H}|
. The
\mathbb{Z}
-linearization of this assignment yields the
mark homomorphism
.

m:B(G)\to B(G)^{*},X\mapsto(f_{X}:G/H\mapsto|X|^{H}).

The mark homomorphism is injective and full-rank, so
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}m:\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B(G)^{*}
is an isomorphism. However, it is rarely surjective.

Finally, it is easy to see the image of
B(G)^{\times}
consists of functions
f\in B(G)^{*}
which take values only in
\pm 1
.

There are two key insights for the proof of this statement. For these next statements, note
G
is a
p
-group, so
B(G)^{*}\cong CF(G)
.

.

.

7.2.
Every
p
-permutation autoequivalence of a
p
-group arises from a splendid Rickard equivalence

Next, we use Theorem
7.4
to prove that for any every
p
-permutation autoequivalence of a
p
-group
G
,
\gamma\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG)
, there exists a splendid Rickard equivalence
\Gamma\in Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}_{kG})
for which
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\gamma
.

We refer the reader to
[
BP20
]
or
[
BM23
]
for exposition on
p
-permutation equivalences and splendid Rickard equivalence. We take a looser definition of splendid Rickard equivalence which allows for equivalences of direct sums of block algebras (such as group algebras).

Definition 7.5
.

For any
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, define
\Delta_{\phi}(G)\leq G\times G
to be the subgroup defined by

\Delta_{\phi}(G):=\{(\phi(g),g)\in G\times G\mid g\in G\}.

Define
\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}\leq G\times G
to be the subgroup defined by

\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}:=\{(g,\phi(g))\in G\times G\mid g\in G\}.

Observe
\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}\cong\Delta_{\phi^{-1}}(G)
.
We have obvious group homomorphisms

G\cong\Delta_{\phi}(G),\,g\mapsto(\phi(g),g),

and

G\cong\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op},\,g\mapsto(g,\phi(g)).

In this way we identify
kG
-modules with
k\Delta_{\phi}(G)
-modules and
k\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}
-modules.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group and let
\gamma\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG))
be a
p
-permutation equivalence. Then there exists a orthogonal unit
u\in O(T(k[\Delta_{\phi}G]))\cong O(T(kG))
and group automorphism
\phi:G\xrightarrow{\sim}G
such that
\gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}(u)
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
BP15
, Theorem 1.1(e)]
, however we provide a mostly self-contained proof as well.

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(\gamma)
has a maximal
\gamma
-Brauer pair
\omega=(\Delta(G,\phi,G),e\otimes e^{*})
by
[
BP20
, Theorem 10.11]
, where
\phi:G\to G
is a group automorphism and
e
is the unique block of
Z(G)
, and all other maximal elements of
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(\gamma)
are
G\times G
-conjugate to
\omega
, every other element of
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(\gamma)
is
G\times G
-conjugate to some
\omega^{\prime}
satisfying
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
. Therefore, every trivial source
(kG,kG)
-bimodule
M
appearing in
\gamma
has a maximal
M
-Brauer pair
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(M)
satisfying
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
. In particular, there exists a subgroup
P\leq G
for which
M
has
\Delta_{\phi}(P)
as a vertex. Therefore, we have

\gamma=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{a}[\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}P_{i}}k]\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{b}[\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}Q_{i}}k]\right),

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
and each
P_{i},Q_{j}
a subgroup of
G
. Since for any
P\leq G
,

\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}P}=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}\circ\operatorname{Ind}^{\Delta_{\phi}G}_{\Delta_{\phi}P},

the above equality factors as

\gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{a}[\operatorname{Ind}^{\Delta_{\phi}G}_{\Delta_{\phi}P_{i}}k]-\sum_{i=1}^{b}[\operatorname{Ind}^{\Delta_{\phi}G}_{\Delta_{\phi}Q_{i}}k]\right)=:\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}u,

with
u\in T(k[\Delta_{\phi}G])
. It suffices to show
u\in O(T(k[\Delta_{\phi}G]))
. From here, we identify
T(kG)
with
T(k[\Delta_{\phi}(G)])
in the obvious way.

We have that

\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u\right)\cdot_{kG}\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}u\right)^{*}=kG\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG)).

Since
u\in T(kG)
and
G
is a
p
-group,
u
is a sum of transitive indecomposable virtual permutation modules, hence
\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}u
is a sum of transitive indecomposable virtual permutation modules with twisted diagonal vertices. It is a straightforward verification that for any
P\leq G
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
,

\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\varphi}(P)}k\right)^{*}\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\varphi}(P)^{op}}k,

as
(kG,kG)
-bimodules. Therefore,

\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u\right)^{*}=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}u\in T^{\Delta}(kG,kG).

It follows from Bouc’s extended tensor product formula
[
Bou10b
, Theorem 1.1]
that for any
H\leq G
and
kH
-modules
M,N
that

\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(H)}M\otimes_{kG}\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(H)^{op}}N\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta H}(M\otimes_{k}N),

where on the left,
M
is regarded as a
k\Delta_{\phi}H
-module and
N
is regarded as a
k\Delta_{\phi}(H)^{op}
-module. Moreover, this isomorphism is natural in both arguments. Therefore, we have the following chain of equalities.

\displaystyle kG

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u\right)\cdot_{kG}\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u\cdot_{kG}\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}u

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta G}(u\cdot_{k}u)\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG))

\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta G}
induces a split injective group homomorphism
T(kG)\to T^{\Delta}(kG,kG)
with retraction induced by taking
1\times G
-fixed points, see
[
Mil24
, Lemma 4.14]
. Therefore, we have

k=(kG)^{1\times G}=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta G}(u\cdot_{k}u)^{1\times G}=u\cdot_{k}u\in T(kG).

Since
u
contains only permutation modules, as
G
is a
p
-group,
u
is self-dual, and we conclude
u\in O(T(kG))
as desired.
∎

Therefore, every
p
-permutation autoequivalence of a
p
-group is induced twisted diagonally from an orthogonal unit. Given a
p
-permutation equivalence
\gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}u
with
u\in O(T(kG))
, the obvious choice of corresponding splendid Rickard equivalence
\Gamma
which satisfies
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\gamma
should be
\Gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}C
, where
C
is some endotrivial complex for which
\Lambda(C)=u
. Indeed,

\Lambda\left(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}C\right)=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}G}u=\gamma\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG)).

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
M
be a
kG
-module. We have a natural isomorphism

(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}\cong(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M)^{*},

as
(kG,kG)
-bimodules, where the dual induced on the left-hand side arises from the left
kG
-module
k
-dual, and the dual on the right-hand side arises from the bimodule
k
-dual.

Proof.

We first note the bimodule structure on the left- and right-hand sides of the proposed isomorphism.

On the left, for
a,b\in G
,
(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}
, has actions defined by:

\displaystyle a\cdot f\big((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\big)\cdot b

\displaystyle=(a,b^{-1})\cdot f\big((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\big)

\displaystyle=f\big((a^{-1},b)(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\big)

\displaystyle=f\big((a^{-1}g_{1},bg_{2})\otimes m\big),

and on the right,
(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M)^{*}
corresponds to the bottom left composite, that is, it has actions defined by:

\displaystyle a\cdot f\big((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\big)\cdot b

\displaystyle=f\big(b\cdot\big((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\big)\cdot a\big)

\displaystyle=f\big((bg_{1},a^{-1}g_{2})\otimes m\big).

We define an isomorphism
\psi:(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}\cong(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M)^{*}
as follows:

\displaystyle\psi:(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}

\displaystyle\to(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M)^{*}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\big((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\mapsto f((g_{2},g_{1})\otimes m)\big)

Here,
(g_{2},g_{1})\otimes m
as above is considered an element in
\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}
. We first check well-definedness of the mapping with respect to the tensor product. Let
f\in(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}
and
(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\in\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M
. We have for any
g\in G
that

(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m=(g_{1}g^{-1},g_{2}\phi(g^{-1}))\otimes gm.

Then for any
f\in(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}
,

\displaystyle\psi(f)((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\psi(f)((g_{1}g^{-1},g_{2}\phi(g^{-1}))\otimes gm)

\displaystyle=f((g_{2}\phi(g^{-1}),(g_{1}g^{-1})\otimes gm)

\displaystyle=f((g_{2},g_{1})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\psi(f)((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m)

Thus the map is well-defined. We next check that it is a
(kG,kG)
-bimodule homomorphism. Let
a,b\in G
.

\displaystyle\psi(a\cdot f\cdot b)((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\psi(f((a^{-1},b)\cdot-))((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=f((a^{-1}g_{2},bg_{1})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\psi(f)((bg_{1},a^{-1}g_{2})\otimes m)

\displaystyle=(a\cdot\psi(f)\cdot b)((g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m)

Therefore,
\psi
is a
(kG,kG)
-bimodule homomorphism. It is apparent
\psi
is an isomorphism, as its inverse is constructed similarly. Finally we must check naturality. This follows from commutativity of the following diagram, where
f:M\to N
is a
kG
-module homomorphism.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k[G\times G]\otimes_{k\Delta_{\phi}(G)}M)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k[G\times G]\otimes_{k\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}M)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k[G\times G]\otimes_{k\Delta_{\phi}(G)}N)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k[G\times G]\otimes_{k\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}N)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{id}\otimes f)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{id}\otimes f)^{*}}

∎

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group and let
\gamma\in O(T^{\Delta}(kG,kG))
be a
p
-permutation autoequivalence of
kG
. There exists a splendid Rickard equivalence
\Gamma
satisfying
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\gamma.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.6
, we have
\gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u
for some
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
u\in O(T(kG))\cong O(T(k[\Delta_{\phi}(G)]))
, After identifying
O(T(kG))
with
B(G)^{\times}
, by
7.4
, there exists an endotrivial complex
C
satisfying
\Lambda(C)=u
. Moreover, by additivity of induction,

\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C=\gamma=\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}u.

It suffices to show
\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C
is a splendid Rickard autoequivalence of
kG
. We have:

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C)\otimes_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C)^{*}

\displaystyle\cong(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C)\otimes_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}C)^{*}

The above isomorphism is the identification in Lemma
7.7
.

\displaystyle\cong(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C)\otimes_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)^{op}}C^{*})

This follows from the standard natural
kG
-module isomorphism
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}M^{*}\cong(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}M)^{*}
.

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta G}(C\otimes_{k}C^{*})

This follows from the isomorphism used in the proof of Proposition
7.6
.

\displaystyle\cong kG

Thus by
[
Ric96
, Theorem 2.1]
,
\operatorname{Ind}^{G\times G}_{\Delta_{\phi}(G)}C
is a splendid Rickard equivalence.
∎

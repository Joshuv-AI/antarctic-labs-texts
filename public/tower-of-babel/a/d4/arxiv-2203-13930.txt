In

\zeta(2k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2k}

converges to a rational multiple of

Many demonstrations of this fact are now known, and Euler’s discovery is traditionally proven using non-elementary techniques, such as Fourier series or the calculus of residues
[

[
3
]

\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6

only extended recursively for all values

\zeta(2k)=-\dfrac{(-\pi^{2})^{k}}{4^{2k}-4^{k}}\left[\dfrac{4^{k}k}{(2k)!}+{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}}(4^{2\ell}-4^{\ell})\dfrac{4^{k-\ell}}{(2k-2\ell)!}\dfrac{\zeta(2\ell)}{(-\pi^{2})^{\ell}}\right]\phantom{spa}k=1,2,3,\dots

may be derived from previously known formulae

]
.

.

Define

a_{k}=(-1)^{k-1}\left(\dfrac{4^{k}k}{(2k)!}+{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\dfrac{(-1)^{\ell}4^{k-\ell}}{(2k-2\ell)!}a_{\ell}}\right)
.

Our proof’s main ingredient is the Newton Girard formula, which relates power sums with the elementary symmetric polynomials. An elegant proof of this formula was given by Euler in
[
6

Definition.
Given a finite sequence
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
, its
r
-th
power sum

t_{1}^{r}+\dots+t_{n}^{r}
is denoted by
f_{r}
, and its
r
-th
elementary symmetric polynomial

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in{[n]\choose r}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}
is denoted by
e_{r}
, where
{[n]\choose r}
is the family of subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
of cardinality
r
.
Note that
e_{r}
is the coefficient of
t^{n-r}
in the polynomial
(t+t_{1})\dots(t+t_{n})
.

The Newton-Girard formula.
For
k\geq 1
we have

f_{k}=(-1)^{k-1}\left[ke_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell}f_{\ell}e_{k-\ell}\right]

Proof of theorem.

Proof of (i).

The roots of
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{2n\choose 2k}z^{2k}=\frac{1}{2}\left[(z+1)^{2n}+(z-1)^{2n}\right]
are easily verified to be

i\cot(\frac{2\ell-1}{4n}\pi)
,
\ell=1,\dots,2n
,
implying
f(z)=\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}
(z^{2}+\cot^{2}(\frac{2\ell-1}{4n}\pi))
. Thus

\tilde{P}_{k}(n)=e_{k}\left(\cot^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4n}\pi\right),\cot^{2}\left(\frac{3}{4n}\pi\right),\dots,\cot^{2}\left(\frac{2n-1}{4n}\pi\right)\right)={2n\choose 2k}

eqauls a polynomial in
n
of degree
2k
and leading coefficient
\dfrac{4^{k}}{(2k)!}
.
Using induction and applying the Newton-Girard formula, we see that

P_{k}(n)=f_{k}(\cot^{2}(\frac{1}{4n}\pi),\cot^{2}(\frac{3}{4n}\pi),\dots,\cot^{2}(\frac{2n-1}{4n}\pi))

equals a polynomial in
n
of degree
2k
with leading coefficient
a_{k}
. ∎

Proof of (ii).

Similarly to the above, we let
\tilde{Q}_{k}(n)=e_{k}(\sin^{-2}(\frac{1}{4n}\pi),\sin^{-2}(\frac{3}{4n}\pi),\dots,\sin^{-2}(\frac{2n-1}{4n}\pi))
.
Since
\sin^{-2}(x)=1+\cot^{2}(x)
, the easily verified formula
e_{k}(t_{1}+1,\dots,t_{n}+1)=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}e_{\ell}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

implies that
\tilde{Q}_{k}(n)
is also a polynomial in
n
of degree
2k
and leading coefficient
\dfrac{4^{k}}{(2k)!}
.
Again, Newton-Girard implies that
Q_{k}(n)=f_{k}(\sin^{-2}(\frac{1}{4n}\pi),\sin^{-2}(\frac{3}{4n}\pi),\dots,\sin^{-2}(\frac{2n-1}{4n}\pi))
equals a polynomial in
n
of degree
2k
and leading coefficient
a_{k}
. ∎

Proof of (iii).

For
0<x<\frac{\pi}{2}
we have
0<\sin x<x<\tan x
.
Therefore

P_{k}(n)<\displaystyle\left(\frac{4n}{\pi}\right)^{2k}+\left(\frac{4n}{3\pi}\right)^{2k}+\dots+\left(\frac{4n}{(2n-1)\pi}\right)^{2k}<Q_{k}(n)

As
P_{k}(n)\sim Q_{k}(n)\sim a_{k}n^{2k}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(2\ell-1)^{-2k}=\frac{\pi^{2k}}{4^{2k}}a_{k}
.
We finish by noting that
\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(2\ell-1)^{-2k}=(1-2^{-2k})\zeta(2k)
. ∎

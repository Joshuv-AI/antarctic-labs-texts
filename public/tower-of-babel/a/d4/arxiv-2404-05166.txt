Abstract




Abstract:
This paper is concerned with an indefinite linear-quadratic mean field games of stochastic large-population system, where the individual diffusion coefficients can depend on both the state and the control of the agents. Moreover, the control weights in the cost functionals could be indefinite. A direct approach is used to derive the
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium strategy. First, we formally solving an
N
-player game problem within a vast and finite population setting. Subsequently, decoupling or reducing high-dimensional systems by introducing two Riccati equations explicitly yields centralized strategies, contingent on the state of a specific player and the average state of the population. As the population size
N
goes infinity, the construction of decentralized strategies becomes feasible. Then, we demonstrated they are an
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium. Numerical examples are provided to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed strategies.

Keywords:
mean field games, direct approach, linear-quadratic stochastic differential game, forward-backward stochastic differential equation,
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium, indefinite control weight

Introduction

Mean field games
(MFGs) have been attracting growing scholarly attention, finding applications in diverse fields such as system control, applied mathematics, and economics (

MFG theory serves as a framework for describing the behavior of models characterized by large populations, where the influence of the overall population is significant, despite the negligible impact on individual entities.
It investigates the existence of Nash equilibria.
This is accomplished by leveraging the relationship between the finite and corresponding infinite-limit population problems.
The methodological foundations of MFG, initially proposed by Lasry and Lions
[
14
]
and independently by Huang et al.
[
12
]
, have proven effective and tractable for analyzing weakly coupled stochastic controlled systems with mean field interactions, establishing approximate Nash equilibria.
In particular, within the
linear-quadratic
(LQ) framework, MFGs provide a versatile modeling tool applicable to a wide range of practical problems.
The solutions derived from LQ-MFGs exhibit notable and elegant properties.
Current scholarly discourse has extensively explored MFGs, particularly within the LQ framework (
[
15
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
13
]
).
Huang et al.
[
11
]
conducted a study on
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium strategies in the context of LQ-MFGs with discounted costs.
This investigation was rooted in the fixed-point approach. Subsequently, it was applied to scenarios involving long run average costs, in Li and Zhang
[
15
]
.
In the domain of MFGs featuring major players, Huang
[
10
]
delved into continuous-time LQ games, providing insights into
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium strategies.
Huang et al.
[
8
]
introduced a backward-major and forward-minor setup for an LQ-MFG, and decentralized
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium strategies for major and minor agents were obtained.
Huang et al.
[
9
]
examined the backward LQ-MFG of weakly coupled stochastic large population systems under both full and partial information scenarios.
Huang and Li
[
7
]
delved into an LQ-MFG concerning a class of stochastic delayed systems.
Xu and Zhang
[
20
]
explored a general LQ-MFG for stochastic large population systems, where the individual diffusion coefficient is contingent on the state and control of the agent.
Bensoussan et al.
[
1

considered an LQ-MFG with partial observation and common noise.

Conventionally, two approaches are employed in the resolution of mean field games.
One is termed the fixed-point approach (or top-down approach, see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
), which initiates the process by employing mean field approximation and formulating a fixed-point equation.
By tackling the fixed-point equation and scrutinizing the optimal response of a representative player, decentralized strategies can be formulated.
The alternative approach is known as the direct approach (or bottom-up approach, refer to
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
).
This method commences by formally solving an
N
-player game problem within a vast and finite population setting.
Subsequently, by decoupling or reducing high-dimensional systems, centralized control can be explicitly derived, contingent on the state of a specific player and the average state of the population.
As the population size
N
approaches infinity, the construction of decentralized strategies becomes feasible.
In the direct approach, there are no fixed-point conditions.
In
[
13
]
, the authors discussed the connection between these two routes within an LQ setting.

In this paper, we explore an indefinite LQ-MFG, where the individual diffusion coefficients can depend on both the state and the control of the agents, the control weights in the cost functionals could be indefinite.
We solve the problem by directly decoupling high-dimensional
forward-backward stochastic differential equations
(FBSDEs) via introducing two Riccati equations, which in turn gives the centralized strategy.
Then, inspired by the form of above Riccati equations, the limiting form of the Riccati equations is devised and its solvability is analyzed.
The solvability of the original Riccati equations follows from the continuous dependence of the solution on the parameter.
By the law of large numbers, we get the decentralized strategy, and verify that it is an
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium strategy.

The main contributions of the paper are outlined as follows.

\bullet
In the drift and diffusion terms of the individual’s dynamical system, both incorporate the individual’s own state and control.
There is also a coupling of the individual state to the population state average in the cost function.
These new type bring mathematical difficulties, and, to some degree, they have some potential applications in reality.

\bullet
Instead of employing the traditional fixed-point methodology such as
[
20
]
, we adopt a direct approach to address the complexities inherent in our game problem, which was used in
[
13
]
,
[
19
]
.
This strategic shift involves the meticulous decoupling of the high-dimensional Hamiltonian system using mean field approximations.
By disentangling the high-dimensional Hamiltonian system through mean field approximations, we formulate a set of decentralized strategies for all players. This set is subsequently demonstrated to constitute an
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium.

Problem formulation

We consider a large-population system with
N

N

\mathcal{A}_{i}

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx_{i}(t)=&\big[A(t)x_{i}(t)+B(t)u_{i}(t)+f(t)\big]dt\\
&+\big[C(t)x_{i}(t)+D(t)u_{i}(t)+g(t)\big]dW_{i}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
x_{i}(0)=&\ \xi_{i},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where

,
x_{i}(\cdot)
,

\mathcal{A}_{i}

In this paper, for simplicity, we assume the dimensions of state process and control process are both one-dimensional.
Here,
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
,

and

are deterministic
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions.

W_{i}(\cdot),i=1,\cdots,N

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P})
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{\xi_{i},W_{i}(s),s\leq t,1\leq i\leq N\}
.
Denote
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{i}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{\xi_{i},W_{i}(s),s\leq t\},1\leq i\leq N
.
We define the decentralized control set for all agents and the
i
th agent
\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{d}[0,T]

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,u_{N}(\cdot))|u_{i}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{i}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{d}^{i}[0,T]

\displaystyle:=\big\{u_{i}(\cdot)|u_{i}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{i}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\big\},

and the centralized control set for all agents and the

th agent
\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{c}[0,T]

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,u_{N}(\cdot))|u_{i}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}),1\leq i\leq N\big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{c}^{i}[0,T]

\displaystyle:=\big\{u_{i}(\cdot)|u_{i}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\big\}.

The cost functional of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is given by

\displaystyle J^{N}_{i}(u_{i}(\cdot),u_{-i}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Big|\Big|x_{i}(t)-\Gamma(t)x^{(N)}(t)-\eta(t)\Big|\Big|^{2}_{Q(t)}+||u_{i}(t)||^{2}_{R(t)}\right]dt\right.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left.+\Big|\Big|x_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}x^{(N)}(T)-\eta_{0}\Big|\Big|^{2}_{H}\right\},

where

,

,

and

,

and

are scalar constants.

is called the state average or mean field term of all agents,
u_{-i}(\cdot):=(u_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,u_{i-1}(\cdot),u_{i+1}(\cdot),\cdots,u_{N}(\cdot))

We propose the following assumptions:

(A1)

(P)

Design of decentralized strategies

We initially derive the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumption (A1)-(A3), for the initial value
\xi_{i},i=1,\cdots,N
,
(P)
admits an optimal control
\check{u}_{i}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}_{c}^{i}[0,T],i=1,\cdots,N
, if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(i) For
i=1,\cdots,N
, the adapted solution
(\check{x}_{i}(\cdot),\check{p}_{i}(\cdot),\check{q}^{j}_{i}(\cdot),j=1,\cdots,N)
to the FBSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\check{x}_{i}&=\big[A\check{x}_{i}+B\check{u}_{i}+f\big]dt+\big[C\check{x}_{i}+D\check{u}_{i}+g\big]dW_{i},\\
d\check{p}_{i}&=-\left[A\check{p}_{i}+C\check{q}^{i}_{i}+\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)Q\left(\check{x}_{i}-\Gamma\check{x}^{(N)}-\eta\right)\right]dt\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\check{q}_{i}^{j}dW_{j},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\check{x}_{i}(0)&=\xi_{i},\quad\check{p}_{i}(T)=H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\left(\check{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\check{x}^{(N)}(T)-\eta_{0}\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

satisfies the following stationarity condition:

B\check{p}_{i}+D\check{q}^{i}_{i}+R\check{u}_{i}=0,\quad a.e.,\ a.s..

(3.2)

(ii) For
i=1,\cdots,N
, the following convexity condition holds:

\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[Q\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)^{2}\tilde{x}_{i}^{2}+Ru_{i}^{2}\right]dt+H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)^{2}\tilde{x}_{i}^{2}(T)\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall u_{i}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}_{c}^{i}[0,T],

(3.3)

where

represents the solution to the following SDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\tilde{x}_{i}&=\left[A\tilde{x}_{i}+Bu_{i}\right]dt+\left[C\tilde{x}_{i}+Du_{i}\right]dW_{i},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\tilde{x}_{i}(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

Proof.

\mathcal{A}_{i}

3.1
).
For any
u_{i}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}_{c}^{i}[0,T]
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, let

be the solution to the following perturbed state equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx_{i}^{\theta}&=\big[Ax_{i}^{\theta}+B(\check{u}_{i}+\theta u_{i})+f\big]dt+\big[Cx_{i}^{\theta}+D(\check{u}_{i}+\theta u_{i})+g\big]dW_{i},\\
x_{i}^{\theta}(0)&=\xi_{i}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

Then,
\tilde{x}_{i}^{\theta}(\cdot):=\frac{x_{i}^{\theta}(\cdot)-\check{x}_{i}(\cdot)}{\theta}
is independent of
\theta
and satisfies (
3.4
). Applying Itô’s formula to
\check{p}_{i}(\cdot)\tilde{x}_{i}(\cdot)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigg[H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\left(\check{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\check{x}^{(N)}(T)-\eta_{0}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big[\check{p}_{i}(T)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)-\check{p}_{i}(0)\tilde{x}_{i}(0)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Biggl\{-\left[A\check{p}_{i}+C\check{q}^{i}_{i}+\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)Q\left(\check{x}_{i}-\Gamma\check{x}^{(N)}-\eta\right)\right]\tilde{x}_{i}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)Q\left(\check{x}_{i}-\Gamma\check{x}^{(N)}-\eta\right)\tilde{x}_{i}+Bu_{i}\check{p}_{i}+Du_{i}\check{q}^{i}_{i}\right\}dt.

Therefore,

\displaystyle J^{N}_{i}(\check{u}_{i}(\cdot)+\theta u_{i}(\cdot),u_{-i}(\cdot))-J^{N}_{i}(\check{u}_{i}(\cdot),u_{-i}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Big|\Big|x_{i}^{\theta}-\Gamma x^{(N)\theta}-\eta\Big|\Big|^{2}_{Q}+||\check{u}_{i}+\theta u_{i}||^{2}_{R}\right]dt+\Big|\Big|x_{i}^{\theta}(T)-\Gamma_{0}x^{(N)\theta}(T)-\eta_{0}\Big|\Big|^{2}_{H}\right\}

where only here

,

if and only if (

) and (

(3.6)

&P_{N}(T)=H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{K}_{N}+2AK_{N}-P_{N}B\alpha_{N}^{-1}\gamma_{N}-K_{N}B\alpha_{N}^{-1}\left(\beta_{N}+\gamma_{N}\right)\\
&\ -CD\left(P_{N}+\frac{1}{N}K_{N}\right)\alpha_{N}^{-1}\gamma_{N}-\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)Q\Gamma=0,\\
&K_{N}(T)=-H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\Gamma_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\phi}_{N}+fP_{N}+fK_{N}+A\phi_{N}-P_{N}B\alpha_{N}^{-1}\delta_{N}-K_{N}B\alpha_{N}^{-1}\delta_{N}\\
&\ +C\left(P_{N}+\frac{1}{N}K_{N}\right)\left(g-D\alpha_{N}^{-1}\delta_{N}\right)-\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)Q\eta=0,\\
&\phi_{N}(T)=-H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\eta_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.16)

Note that (
3.16
) is a linear backward
ordinary differential equation
(backward ODE). If (
3.14
)-(
3.15
) admit solutions, then (
3.16
) has a solution.
From the above discussion, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem 4.1 on page 47 of Ma and Yong
[
16
]
, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under Assumptions (A1)-(A4), if (
3.14
)-(
3.15
) respectively admits a solution, then Problem
(P)
admits a unique solution.

\check{u}_{i}=-\alpha_{N}^{-1}\left[\beta_{N}\check{x}_{i}+\gamma_{N}\check{x}^{(N)}+\delta_{N}\right],\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

(3.17)

Inspired by the discussion above, we denote

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha:=R+PD^{2},\quad\beta:=BP+PCD,\\
&\gamma:=BK,\quad\delta:=B\phi+PgD,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.18)

where
P
,
K
and
\phi
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{P}+2AP+CPC-\alpha^{-1}\beta^{2}+Q=0,\\
&P(T)=H,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{K}+2AK-KB\alpha^{-1}(\beta+\gamma)-\beta\alpha^{-1}\gamma-Q\Gamma=0,\\
&K(T)=-H\Gamma_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.20)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\phi}+fP+fK+A\phi+CPg-KB\alpha^{-1}\delta-\beta\alpha^{-1}\delta-Q\eta=0,\\
&\phi(T)=-H\eta_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.21)

In the following we will discuss the solvability of the Riccati equations (
3.19
) and (
3.20
).
By (
3.18
), we can rewrite (
3.19
) as

\dot{P}+(2A+C^{2})P+Q-(R+D^{2}P)^{-1}(B+CD)^{2}P^{2}=0,\quad P(T)=H.

Two results for the solvability of this Riccati equation are given in
[
20
]
.
When the solvability condition of the Riccati equation (
3.19
) holds, we reorganize the Riccati equation (
3.20
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} 0=&\ \dot{K}-Q\Gamma+2\big[A-B(R+PD^{2})^{-1}(BP+PCD)\big]K-(R+PD^{2})^{-1}B^{2}K^{2},\\
:=&\ \dot{K}+a+bK+cK^{2},\\
K(T)=&-H\Gamma_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.22)

According to the basic conclusion of ODE, if there exists constant
M\geq 0
, such that

|a(t)|+|b(t)|+|c(t)|\leq M,\quad c(t)\neq 0,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

then (
3.22
) admits a unique solution, i.e., (
3.20
) admits a unique solution.

By leveraging the continuous dependence of the solution on the parameter, as discussed in
[
13
]
, and under the aforementioned conditions, we ascertain that for a sufficiently large
N
, (
3.14
)-(
3.16
) admit solutions, respectively.

Let
N\to\infty
. By the law of large numbers, we may approximate
x^{(N)}
in (
3.6
) with
\bar{x}
, which satisfies

d\bar{x}=\big[(A-B\alpha^{-1}(\beta+\gamma))\bar{x}-B\alpha^{-1}\delta+f\big]dt,\quad\bar{x}(0)=\bar{\xi}.

(3.23)

By Proposition
3.1
, the decentralized strategy for agent
\mathcal{A}_{i},i=1,\cdots,N
may be taken as

\hat{u}_{i}=-\alpha^{-1}[\beta\hat{x}_{i}+\gamma\bar{x}+\delta],

(3.24)

where
\hat{x}_{i}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\hat{x}_{i}=&\ \big[(A-B\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}_{i}-B\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-B\alpha^{-1}\delta+f\big]dt\\
&+\big[(C-D\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}_{i}-D\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-D\alpha^{-1}\delta+g\big]dW_{i},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\hat{x}_{i}(0)=&\ \xi_{i}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.25)

4
\epsilon
-Nash equilibria

In this section, we aim to demonstrate that the decentralized strategies (
3.24
) of agent
\mathcal{A}_{i},i=1,\cdots,N
, constitute an approximated
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that (A1)-(A4) hold. For problem
(P’)
,
(\hat{u}_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,\hat{u}_{N}(\cdot))
given in (
3.24
) constitutes an
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium, where
\epsilon=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)
.

Proof.

Through (
3.25
), we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\hat{x}^{(N)}=&\ \big[(A-B\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}^{(N)}-B\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-B\alpha^{-1}\delta+f\big]dt\\
&+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big[(C-D\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}_{i}-D\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-D\alpha^{-1}\delta+g\big]dW_{j},\\
\hat{x}^{(N)}(0)=&\ \xi^{(N)}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

By (
3.23
) and (
4.1
), it can be verified that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}||\hat{x}^{(N)}-\bar{x}||^{2}dt=O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right).

(4.2)

For
i=1,\cdots,N
, denote
\tilde{u}_{i}(\cdot):=u_{i}(\cdot)-\hat{u}_{i}(\cdot)
and
\tilde{x}_{i}(\cdot):=x_{i}(\cdot)-\hat{x}_{i}(\cdot)
.
Then for fixed
u_{-i}
,
\tilde{x}_{i}(\cdot)
satisfies

d\tilde{x}_{i}=\left[A\tilde{x}_{i}+B\tilde{u}_{i}\right]dt+\left[C\tilde{x}_{i}+D\tilde{u}_{i}\right]dW_{i},\quad\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Thus,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(||\tilde{x}_{i}(t)||^{2}+||\tilde{u}_{i}(t)||^{2}\right)dt<\infty.

From (
2.2
), we have

J_{i}^{N}(u_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot))=J_{i}^{N}(\hat{u}_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot))+\tilde{J}_{i}^{N}(\tilde{u}_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot))+\mathcal{I}_{i}^{N},

(4.3)

where

\tilde{J}_{i}^{N}(\tilde{u}_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot)):=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[\bigg|\bigg|\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}\bigg|\bigg|^{2}_{Q}+||\tilde{u}_{i}||^{2}_{R}\right]dt+\bigg|\bigg|\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\bigg|\bigg|^{2}_{H}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i}^{N}:=\mathbb{E}\Biggl\{

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\bigg[Q\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}\left(\hat{x}_{i}-\Gamma\hat{x}^{(N)}-\eta\right)+R\tilde{u}_{i}\hat{u}_{i}\bigg]dt

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)H\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\left(\hat{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\hat{x}^{(N)}(T)-\eta_{0}\right)\Biggr\}.

Let
\hat{p}_{i}=P\hat{x}_{i}+K\bar{x}+\phi
. By (
3.25
), (
3.23
) and Itô’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle d\hat{p}_{i}=

\displaystyle\big[\dot{P}+P(A-B\alpha^{-1}\beta)\big]\hat{x}_{i}dt+\big[\dot{K}-PB\alpha^{-1}\gamma+K(A-B\alpha^{-1}(\beta+\gamma))\big]\bar{x}dt

\displaystyle+\big[\dot{\phi}-PB\alpha^{-1}\delta+Pf-KB\alpha^{-1}\delta+Kf\big]dt

\displaystyle+P\big[(C-D\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}_{i}-D\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-D\alpha^{-1}\delta+g\big]dW_{i}.

Applying Itô’s formula to
\tilde{x}_{i}(\cdot)\hat{p}_{i}(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[H\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\left(\hat{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\bar{x}(T)-\eta_{0}\right)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\big(P(T)\hat{x}_{i}(T)+K(T)\bar{x}(T)+\phi(T)\big)-\tilde{x}_{i}(0)\big(P(0)\hat{x}_{i}(0)+K(0)\bar{x}(0)+\phi(0)\big)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\left[A\tilde{x}_{i}+B\tilde{u}_{i}\right](P\hat{x}_{i}+K\bar{x}+\phi)+\big[\dot{P}+P(A-B\alpha^{-1}\beta)\big]\tilde{x}_{i}\hat{x}_{i}

\displaystyle+\big[\dot{K}-PB\alpha^{-1}\gamma+K(A-B\alpha^{-1}(\beta+\gamma))\big]\tilde{x}_{i}\bar{x}

\displaystyle+\big[\dot{\phi}-PB\alpha^{-1}\delta+Pf-KB\alpha^{-1}\delta+Kf\big]\tilde{x}_{i}

\displaystyle+\left[C\tilde{x}_{i}+D\tilde{u}_{i}\right]P\big[(C-D\alpha^{-1}\beta)\hat{x}_{i}-D\alpha^{-1}\gamma\bar{x}-D\alpha^{-1}\delta+g\big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\left[\dot{P}+2AP-PB\alpha^{-1}\beta+CP(C-D\alpha^{-1}\beta)\right]\tilde{x}_{i}\hat{x}_{i}+\big[B\hat{p}_{i}+D\hat{q}^{i}_{i}\big]\tilde{u}_{i}

\displaystyle+\left[\dot{K}+2KA-PB\alpha^{-1}\gamma-KB\alpha^{-1}(\beta+\gamma)-CPD\alpha^{-1}\gamma\right]\tilde{x}_{i}\bar{x}

\displaystyle+\biggl[\dot{\phi}+A\phi+Pf+Kf-PB\alpha^{-1}\delta-KB\alpha^{-1}\delta-CPD\alpha^{-1}\delta+CPg\biggr]\tilde{x}_{i}\bigg\}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{-Q\tilde{x}_{i}\hat{x}_{i}+Q\Gamma\tilde{x}_{i}\bar{x}+Q\eta\tilde{x}_{i}-R\hat{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{i}\bigg\}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{-Q\tilde{x}_{i}(\hat{x}_{i}-\Gamma\bar{x}-\eta)-R\hat{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{i}\bigg\}dt,

where
\hat{q}^{i}_{i}:=P\big(C\hat{x}_{i}+D\hat{u}_{i}+g\big)
. The penultimate equation is due to (
3.2
) and (
3.19
)-(
3.21
).
Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i}^{N}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\int_{0}^{T}\bigg[Q\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}\big(\hat{x}_{i}-\Gamma\bar{x}-\eta\big)\ +Q\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}\Gamma\left(\bar{x}-\hat{x}^{(N)}\right)+R\tilde{u}_{i}\hat{u}_{i}\bigg]dt

\displaystyle+H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\big(\hat{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\bar{x}(T)-\eta_{0}\big)

\displaystyle+H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\Gamma_{0}\left(\bar{x}(T)-\hat{x}^{(N)}(T)\right)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl\{\int_{0}^{T}\biggl[\frac{\Gamma}{N}R\hat{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{i}+Q\left(1-\frac{\Gamma}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}\Gamma\left(\bar{x}-\hat{x}^{(N)}\right)\biggr]dt

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma-\Gamma_{0}}{N}H\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\big(\hat{x}_{i}(T)-\Gamma_{0}\bar{x}(T)-\eta_{0}\big)

\displaystyle+H\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{N}\right)\tilde{x}_{i}(T)\Gamma_{0}\left(\bar{x}(T)-\hat{x}^{(N)}(T)\right)\biggr\}=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

Thereby,

J_{i}^{N}(\hat{u}_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot))\leq J_{i}^{N}(u_{i}(\cdot),\hat{u}_{-i}(\cdot))+\epsilon.

Thus,
(\hat{u}_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,\hat{u}_{N}(\cdot))
is an
\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium, where
\epsilon=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)
.
∎

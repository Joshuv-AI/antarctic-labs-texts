Weak convergence of the empirical measure for the Keller-Segel model in both subcritical and critical cases.
Yoan Tardy

Abstract
. We show the weak convergence, up to extraction of a subsequence, of the empirical measure for the Keller-Segel system of particles in both subcritical and critical cases, for general initial conditions. This particle system consists of N N planar Brownian motions interacting through a Coulombian attractive force, which is quite singular. In the subcritical case, a stronger result has been established by Bresch-Jabin-Wang [ 7 ] at the price of two simplifications: the whole space ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} is replaced by a torus and the initial condition is assumed to be regular. In the subcritical case, our proof is fairly straightforward: we use a two particles moment argument, which shows that particles do not aggregate in finite time, uniformly in the number of particles. The critical case requires more work.

65C35, 35K55, 92C17

1.
Introduction and results

1.1.
The model

We consider the classical Keller-Segel model of chemotaxis (or Patlak-Keller-Segel model) in the parabolic-elliptic case, which writes

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta f-2\pi\theta\nabla\cdot(f\nabla c),

(2)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\Delta c+f,

with initial condition
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, where
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
denotes the set of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where
(f,c)
is the unknown of the equation and
f_{t}(x)
and
c_{t}(x)
respectively stand for the density of particles and chemoattractant at position
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and time
t\geq 0
. The scalar
\theta>0
describes the intensity of the attraction of the chemoattractant.

Remark 1
.

In the literature, (
1
) is written

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=

\displaystyle\Delta f-\chi\nabla\cdot(f\nabla c),

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\Delta c+f,

and
f_{0}
is assumed to have a finite mass
M>0
. One can easily convince oneself that setting
\tilde{f}_{t}(x)=f_{t/2}(x)/M
and
c_{t/2}(x)/M=\tilde{c}_{t}(x)
,
(\tilde{f},\tilde{c})
solves (
1
)-(
2
) with
\theta=\chi M/(4\pi)
. Observe that if
\chi=1
, one recovers that the classical threshold
M=8\pi
of (
1
)-(
2
) corresponds to
\theta=2
in our case.

Since
c
is the solution of the Poisson equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
f
as source term, the equation (
1
) can we rewritten

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta f-\theta\nabla\cdot(f(K\star f)),

with

K(x)=-x/\|x\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ if }x\neq 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad K(0)=0,

and
\star
stands for the convolution product. One can understand this equation as the modeling of an infinite number of particles moving as Brownian motions which attract each other with a Coulombian force.

The main interest of this equation is the tight competition between the diffusion and the concentration of particles. As Blanchet-Dolbeaut-Perthame have shown in
[
6
]
, introducing the variance of the problem
V_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\|x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}xf_{t}({\rm d}x)\|^{2}{\rm d}f_{t}(x)
, we informally have, using several integrations by parts, that
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}V_{t}=(2-\theta).
This highlights that there are informally three cases:

•

the subcritical case, which corresponds to the case where
\theta<2
:
V_{t}
tends to infinity as time goes to infinity which suggests that the diffusion is dominant over the concentration,

•

the critical case where
\theta=2
,

•

the supercritical case, where
\theta>2
:
V_{t}
ends up being non-positive, which suggests that a blow-up occurs.

1.2.
Weak solutions

In this paper, we will deal with a very weak type of solution that we define here,
see e.g. the paper of Blanchet-Dolbeault-Perthame
[
6
]
.

Definition 2
.

We say that
f\in C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
is a weak solution of (
1
)-(
2
) with initial condition
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
if for all
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)f_{t}({\rm d}x)=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)f_{0}({\rm d}x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta\varphi(x)f_{s}({\rm d}x){\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)]f_{s}({\rm d}x)f_{s}({\rm d}y){\rm d}s.

Observe that since
K(0)=0
and
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}
, the last integral is well defined. Since
(x,y)\mapsto K(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)]
equals
0
( arbitrarily) on the set
\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x=y\}
, we expect that a satisfactory solution must verify
\int_{0}^{t}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\{x=y\}}f_{s}({\rm d}x)f_{s}({\rm d}y){\rm d}s=0
.

1.3.
The associated trajectories

The equation (
3
) comes from an Eulerian point of view. The following SDE models the same problem with a Lagrangian point of view: we consider the behavior of one typical particle among the other.

Definition 3
.

We say that
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a solution of the stochastic Keller-Segel equation with initial condition
X_{0}
if

(4)

\displaystyle\mbox{for all }t\geq 0,\quad X_{t}=X_{0}+B_{t}+\theta\int_{0}^{t}K\star f_{s}(X_{s}){\rm d}s,

where
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
2
-dimensional Brownian motion and
f_{s}
is the law of
X_{s}
.

The idea is to follow the motion of one specific particle instead of considering the proportion of
particles at a precise place and a precise time. One easily checks from the Itô formula that
if
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a solution of the stochastic Keller-Segel equation (
4
), then
(f_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
a weak solution to (
1
)-(
2
).

We consider for
N\geq 2
and
\theta>0
a natural discretization introduced by Keller-Segel in
[
23
]
of the stochastic Keller-Segel equation (
4
):
we consider
N\geq 2
particles with positions
(X^{i,N}_{t})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
, solving (recall that
K(0)=0
)

(5)

X^{i,N}_{t}=X^{i,N}_{0}+B^{i}_{t}+\frac{\theta}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}){\rm d}s,

where
((B^{i}_{t})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of
N
independent
2
-dimensional Brownian motion independent from
(X^{i,N}_{0})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
. We call such a process a
KS(\theta,N)
-process,
\theta\leq 2(N-2)/(N-1)
and its existence is guaranteed by
[
12
, Theorem 7]
for any initial datum
(X_{0}^{i,N})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
with its law
F^{N}_{0}
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
, where
{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
denotes the set of exchangeable probability measures on
({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N}
with a finite

1
-order moment and such that

F_{0}^{N}(\{\mbox{There exists }i\neq j\in[\![1,N]\!]\mbox{ such that }x^{i}=x^{j}\})=0.

Moreover,
(X^{i,N}_{t})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
is exchangeable and we have, for all
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}||K(X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s})||{\rm d}s<\infty.

1.4.
The subcritical case

We endow the space
C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
with the topology of the uniform convergence on the compact sets,
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
being endowed with the topology of the weak convergence. Our goal is to prove the following result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\theta\in(0,2)
and
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. For each
N\geq N_{0}:=(1+\lceil 2/(2-\theta)\rceil)\vee 5
, consider
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
and a
KS(\theta,N)
-process
(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\geq 0,i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
with initial law
F^{N}_{0}
, as well as the empirical measure for all
t\geq 0
,
\mu^{N}_{t}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i,N}_{t}}
, which of course a.s. belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We assume that
\mu^{N}_{0}
goes weakly to
f_{0}
in probability as
N\to\infty
.

(i) The sequence
((\mu^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0})_{N\geq N_{0}}
is tight in
C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
.

(ii) For any sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that
(\mu^{N_{k}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
goes in law in
C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
as
k\to\infty
to some
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, this limit
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a.s. a weak solution to (
1
)-(
2
) starting from
\mu_{0}=f_{0}
. Moreover, for all
T>0
, all
\gamma\in(\theta,2)
.

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\|x-y\|^{\gamma-2}\mu_{t}({\rm d}x)\mu_{t}({\rm d}y){\rm d}t\Big]<\infty.

Observe that the condition
N\geq N_{0}
implies that the particle system exists thanks to
[
12
, Theorem 7]
. There is no condition at all on
f_{0}
: it can be a Dirac mass, it does not need to have finite moments.

Let us mention that we impose the condition
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
only to apply

[
12
, Theorem 7]
, which concerns the existence of the particle system. The condition is not used anywhere else
in the present paper.
When
\theta<1/2
, a stronger existence result is established, see
[
12
, Theorem 5]
,
and we can assume only that
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
.

For any given
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(possibly with some atomes and/or without a moment of order
1
),
we can easily build
F_{0}^{N}
satisfying all the assumptions: take for example

F_{0}^{N}
the law of

(\chi_{N}(X^{i}_{0})+(1/N)G^{i})_{i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
where
(X^{i}_{0})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d
f_{0}
-distributed random variables,
where
(G^{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d standard
bi-dimensional Gaussian random variables
and where
\chi_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1}\land N\lor(-N),x_{2}\land N\lor(-N))
.

We highlight that Theorem
4
has to be linked with
[
14
]
, where the critical case
was not treated for the sake of conciseness. The relation between these results will be discussed in Section
1.7

1.5.
Existence of the particle system in the critical or supercritical case

In the case where
\theta>2(N-2)/(N-1)
, the existence of a process solving (
5
)
for all time

is not guaranteed. So we need to introduce a notion of local solution.
If
\tau
is a stopping
time for a
filtration
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, a continuous
({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N}
-valued process

(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
is a
KS(\theta,N)
-process on
[0,\tau)
if for all sequences of increasing stopping times
(\tau_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

with respect to the filtration
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}=\tau
a.s.
and for all
n\geq 1
,

(X^{i,N}_{t\land\tau_{n}})_{t\geq 0,i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
is
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted and
(
5
) holds true on
[0,\tau_{n}]
.

For the whole paper, we define for all
N\geq 2
, all
\ell\geq 1
, all
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\tau_{k}^{N,\ell}:=\inf\Big\{t>0:\exists K\subset[\![1,N]\!]:|K|=k\hbox{ and }R_{K}(X^{N}_{t})\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\Big\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\tau^{N}_{k}=\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\tau_{k}^{N,\ell},

where for all
K\subset[\![1,N]\!]
, all
x=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N}
,

S_{K}(x)=\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{i\in K}x^{i}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad R_{K}(x)=\sum_{i\in K}\|x^{i}-S_{K}(x)\|^{2}.

The stopping time
\tau^{N,\ell}_{k}
represents the first time when at least
k
particles are
\ell^{-1}
-close.
Roughly,
\tau^{N}_{k}
represents the first time when at least
k
particles collide. However, this does not follow
from the definition, since the
KS(\theta,N)
-process could be discontinuous at time
\tau^{N}_{k}
.

One can find the following existence theorem in
[
12
, Theorem 7 and Lemma 14]
,
recall that our
\theta
corresponds to
\chi/(4\pi)
in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 5

(Fournier-Jourdain
[
12
]
)
.

If
N\geq 5
,
\theta>0
and
F^{N}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
then there exists a
KS(\theta,N)
-process
(X_{t}^{i,N})_{t\in[0,\tau_{3}^{N}),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
to (
5
)
with initial law
F^{N}_{0}
. The family

((X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{3}^{N})},i\in[\![1,N]\!])
is exchangeable and for any
\ell\geq 1
, any
t\geq 0
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{3}^{N,\ell}}\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{-1}{\rm d}s\Big)<\infty.

Moreover
\tau_{3}^{N}<\infty
a.s. if
\theta>2(N-2)/(N-1)
, and
\tau_{3}^{N}=\infty
a.s. if

\theta\in(0,2(N-2)/(N-1)]
.

The local solution to (
5
) of
[
12
, Theorem 7]
is built from
[
12
, Lemma 14]
.
Hence we actually have
{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{3}^{N,\ell}}\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\alpha-2}{\rm d}s]<\infty,

with some
\alpha<1
. This is stronger than the estimate we recalled in Theorem
5
.

The existence of a global solution to (
5
) is false when
\theta\geq 2
. Indeed, consider for example
in the critical case
\theta=2
. Assume that a global solution
(X^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
exists.
Then the process
R_{[\![1,N]\!]}(X^{N})
would be a squared Bessel process with dimension
0
,
see Lemma
12
. Such a squared Bessel
process reaches
0
in finite time and then remains at
0
for all times, meaning that all the particles
share the same location. As a consequence, the drift term of (
5
) cannot be well-defined.

However, the existence of the particle system until
\tau_{3}^{N}
is sufficient to our purpose:
in the critical case
\theta=2
, which is the object of our next main result,
it holds that
\tau_{3}^{N}
tends to infinity in probability as
N\to\infty
. This will be shown in
Section
5
.

This is coherent with the fact that
the solution of the deterministic Keller-Segel equation (
1
)–(
2
) is global, see
[
14
]
.

1.6.
The critical case

Our second main result is the following.

Theorem 6
.

Assume
\theta=2
. Let
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
that checks
\max_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f_{0}(\{x\})<1
. For each
N\geq N_{0}:=5
, consider
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
and
(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{3}^{N}),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
a
KS(2,N)
-process on
[0,\tau_{3}^{N})
with initial law
F^{N}_{0}
. We set
\mu^{N}_{t}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i,N}_{t}}
for
t\in[0,\tau^{N}_{3})
and we assume that

\mu^{N}_{0}
goes weakly to
f_{0}
in probability as
N\to\infty
.

There exists an increasing sequence of integers
(\ell_{N})_{N\geq N_{0}}
such that, setting

\beta_{N}=\tau_{3}^{N,\ell_{N}}
, it holds that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{N}=\infty
in probability and

(i) the sequence
((\mu^{N}_{t\land\beta_{N}})_{t\geq 0})_{N\geq 5}
is tight in
C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
;

(ii) for any sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that
(\mu^{N_{k}}_{t\land\beta_{N_{k}}})_{t\geq 0}
goes in law,
as
k\to\infty
, in
C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
, to some
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, it holds that

\mu_{t}
is a.s a weak solution to (
1
) starting from
\mu_{0}=f_{0}
. Moreover,

(6)

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\{x=y\}}\mu_{t}({\rm d}x)\mu_{t}({\rm d}y){\rm d}t\Big]=0.

The only condition on
f_{0}
is that it is not a full Dirac mass, it can have any atoms as it wants
provided none of these atoms have mass
1
.

Since
\beta_{N}\to\infty
, the above theorem implies that if we fix
T>0
,

(\mu^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is well-defined with high probability (as
N\to\infty
), is tight in some sense,
and the accumulation points of this sequence are almost surely weak solutions of (
1
)-(
2
)
on
[0,T]
.

1.7.
Comparison with available results and comments

The Keller-Segel equation (
1
)-(
2
) was first introduced by Patlak in
[
29
]
and Keller and Segel in
[
22
]
as a model for chemotaxis. One can find exhaustive content on what is understood about this model in the recent book of Biler
[
3
]
and the review paper of Arumugan-Tyagi
[
1
]
.

The notion of propagation of chaos was introduced by Kac in
[
21
]
, the idea was to make a step toward the rigorous justification of the Boltzmann equation. The idea is to approximate the solution of a mesoscopic PDE by the solution to an associated microscopic problem, which involves interacting particles. The case of Lipschitz continuous interaction kernels is now well-understood, we mention for example McKean in
[
24
]
, Sznitman in
[
31
]
, Méléard in
[
25
]
and Mischler-Mouhot in
[
26
]
for significant contributions to the theory. The case of singular kernels is much more complicated and has been studied for different kinds of kernels concerning the viscous Burgers equation by Bossy-Talay in
[
2
]
and Jourdain in
[
20
]
, the Dyson model by Cépa-Lépingle in
[
10
]
, etc.

A model relatively close to our subject is the
2d
-Navier-Stokes particle system studied by Osada in
[
28
]
where the kernel
K
is replaced by
x^{\perp}/\|x\|^{2}
, which is as singular as the kernel of Keller-Segel,
but is rotative instead of being attractive. This implies that collisions do not occur so that the singularity is not visited.
Osada obtained in
[
28
]
the convergence of the corresponding stochastic
particle system under a large viscosity assumption.
This condition has been removed by Fournier-Hauray-Mischler in
[
13
]
thanks to a compactness-uniqueness method,
and then, more recently, Jabin-Wang obtained in
[
18
]
a quantitative convergence result,
using a
modulated free entropy
method. We mention Serfaty who introduced in
[
32
]
the modulated energy method in the deterministic case.

Let us mention Stevens, who studied in
[
33
]
the case where the chemoattractant is not in the stationary
regime (parabolic-parabolic Keller-Segel equation), by considering a particle system
with two types of particles, representing bacteria and chemoattractant.

This paper uses some ideas already present in the papers of Fournier-Jourdain
[
12
]
and
Fournier-Tardy
[
14
]
. In
[
12
]
, there is already a proof of Theorem

4
in the case where
\theta<1/2
: the estimates there are not sufficiently precise to cover
the whole subcritical case
\theta<2
.
In
[
14
]
, there is a simple proof
of non-explosion of the weak solution to (
1
)-(
2
) in the subcritical case. The proof relies
on a
two-particles
moment method which extends the
simple non-explosion proof elaborated in the paper of Biler-Karch-Laurençot-Nadzieja
[
4
,
5
]

concerning the radially symmetric case, in which case the two-particles moment is not necessary since
it actually rewrites as a simple one-particle moment.
The method introduced in
[
14
]
is simple enough so that we are able to adapt it to show that
the particle system does not explode, uniformly in
N
in some sense. Moreover, at the price of much more work,
we are able to include
the critical case. We highlight that the result of
[
14
]
only concerns the deterministic
equations (
1
)–(
2
) and not the stochastic particle system (
5
).
The novelty of our result is about the convergence of the stochastic particle system to the weak global
solution.

The main drawback of our results lies in the fact that we are not able to show the uniqueness of
the weak solution of (
1
)–(
2
) in order to conclude that the empirical measure truely
converges weakly:
we only have the convergence of some subsequences. Let us mention that the uniqueness
of mild solutions of the Keller-Segel equation is well-established (and easy) under a few regularity
assumptions on the initial condition,
see for example the paper of Olivera-Richard-Tomasevic
[
27
]
. However,
it seems very difficult to prove that our
weak
solution
is
mild
.

Notice that we have not the least assumption on the limiting initial condition
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
except in the critical case
\theta=2
where we impose that
f_{0}
is not a Dirac mass.
This last condition is necessary, since when
\theta=2
, Dirac masses are in some sense stationary solutions,
because we informally have

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\|x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}xf_{t}({\rm d}x)\|^{2}{\rm d}f_{t}(x)=2-\theta=0
,
as mentioned in Subsection
1.1
. A small drawback of our result is the condition

F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{sym,1}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
. As already mentioned, we use nowhere this condition except to apply
the existence result (for the particle system) of Fournier-Jourdain
[
12
]
. We believe
that when
\theta\in(0,2)
, there is actually existence of the particle system (at least for
N
sufficiently
large) without this assumption, but this would require considerably much more work.

Concerning the mean-field limit of the Keller-Segel particle system, let us recall the main available results.

(a) Godinho-Quininao proved in
[
16
]
the convergence of the particle system, following the ideas
of Fournier-Hauray-Mischler found in
[
13
]
, when
K
is replaced by
-x/(2\pi\|x\|^{1+\alpha})
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. This kernel is strictly less singular, this prevents any explosion issue and
so the result is obtained without any discussion on some parameter such as
\theta
in our case.

(b) Olivera-Richard-Tomasevic have shown in
[
27
]
the convergence of a smoothed particle system for every
\theta>0
(even supercritical), where
K
is replaced, roughly, by
-x/(\varepsilon_{N}+||x||^{2})
with
\varepsilon_{N}
going to
0
as

N\to\infty
in such a way that
\lim_{N}\sqrt{N}\varepsilon_{N}=\infty
. We highlight that the convergence is uniform on every compact set of
[0,T^{*})
where
T^{*}
is the maximal time of the unique mild solution of the Keller-Segel equation (which is infinite in the subcritical case).
The method is based on a semigroup approach developed by Flandoli
[
11
]
.

(c) In Theorem
6
of the paper of Fournier-Jourdain
[
12
]
, the convergence of the empirical measure is
shown, up to extraction, when
\theta<1/2
, thus proving Theorem
4
when
\theta<1/2
.
They have a light additional moment condition on
f_{0}
, as well as a (uniform in
N
) light moment condition on

F_{0}^{N}
. However, they do not need to impose that
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{sym,1}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
. When

\theta\in[1/2,2)
, Fournier-Jourdain have to assume that
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{sym,1}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
to prove
the existence of the particle system when
\theta\in[1/2,2)
(they do not study the limit

N\to\infty
in this case). We impose that
F_{0}^{N}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{sym,1}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
precisely to use this existence
result.
Finally, it is worth emphasizing that the convergence in law we prove holds in
{\mathcal{P}}(C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
,
which is weaker than the convergence in law in
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
obtained in
[
12
]
. Again, this is
due to the fact that the more
\theta
is large, the more the attraction is strong and the more the singularity
of the kernel
K
is visited.
With our proof, it might be possible to show a convergence in
{\mathcal{P}}(C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
when

\theta\in(0,1)
, but not when
\theta\in(1,2]
: roughly, our proof relies on the estimate

\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{t}||X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}||^{\gamma-2}]{\rm d}s
for all
\gamma\in(\theta,2)
, all
t\geq 0
,
see Subsection
1.8
. Recalling that
||K(x)||=||x||^{-1}
, this estimate
guarantees that the drift in (
5
) is well-controled, uniformly in
N\geq 2
,
only when
\theta\in(0,1)
, in which case can choose
\gamma\in(\theta,2)
such that
\gamma-2=-1
.

(d) Finally, Bresch-Jabin-Wang studied in
[

the convergence of the empirical measure with quantitative estimates in the case where
\theta<2

by using a
modulated free entropy
method in the same spirit as
[

.
They obtain a full convergence result (not only along a subsequence). However, they make two important

by a torus, and deal with regular initial conditions,
namely at least

of class

on the torus. Replacing

In particular, this implies that the solution

To summarize,

and

Finally, let us mention that Cattiaux-Pedèches give in

1.8.
Main a priori estimate in the subcritical case

The subcritical case (which means the case where
\theta\in(0,2)
) is the easiest to handle and here we briefly present the main computation. The idea is to find an a priori estimate which testifies to the fact that particles don’t have the tendency to aggregate. We fix
\gamma\in(\theta,2)
. We set

S_{t}={\mathbb{E}}[\|X^{1,N}_{t}-X^{2,N}_{t}\|^{\gamma}\wedge 1].

The idea is that when differentiating
S_{t}
, the diffusion makes appear something like

{\mathbb{E}}[\|X^{1,N}_{t}-X^{2,N}_{t}\|^{\gamma-2}]
. If it dominates the drift term (which holds true in
the subcritical case for some well-chosen value of
\gamma
), this will imply,
since
S_{t}
is obviously bounded, an estimate on
\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}[\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}]{\rm d}s
.
In some sense, this tells us that particles do not collide too often.

Informally using the Itô formula we get

(7)

\displaystyle\|X^{1,N}_{t}-X^{2,N}_{t}\|^{\gamma}\wedge 1=\|X^{1,N}_{0}-X^{2,N}_{0}\|^{\gamma}\wedge 1+M_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}\Big(I^{1}_{s}+\frac{\theta}{N}I^{2}_{s}\Big){\rm d}s,

where
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale, and where

\displaystyle I^{1}_{s}=\Big(\gamma^{2}-\frac{2\gamma\theta}{N}\Big)\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2},

\displaystyle I^{2}_{s}=\gamma\sum_{k=3}^{N}(K(X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{k,N}_{s})+K(X^{k,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}))\cdot(X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s})\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}.

Taking expectations in (
7
), we find

(8)

\displaystyle S_{t}=S_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}\Big(I^{1}_{s}+\frac{\theta}{N}I^{2}_{s}\Big)\Big]{\rm d}s.

Using exchangeability we get that
{\mathbb{E}}[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}I^{2}_{s}]
equals

\displaystyle\gamma(N-2){\mathbb{E}}[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}(K(X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{3,N}_{s})+

\displaystyle K(X^{3,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}))

\displaystyle\cdot(X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s})\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}],

so that using exchangeability again,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{N}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}I^{2}_{s}\Big]{\rm d}s=\frac{\gamma\theta(N-2)}{3N}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}[F(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s},X^{3,N}_{s})]{\rm d}s,

where

\displaystyle F(x,y,z)=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{Y}{\|Y\|^{2}}+\frac{Z}{\|Z\|^{2}}\Big)\cdot X\|X\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X\|\leq 1}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{Z}{\|Z\|^{2}}+\frac{X}{\|X\|^{2}}\Big)\cdot Y\|Y\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Y\|\leq 1}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{X}{\|X\|^{2}}+\frac{Y}{\|Y\|^{2}}\Big)\cdot Z\|Z\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Z\|\leq 1},

where we have set
X=x-y
,
Y=y-z
and
Z=z-x
. By furthermore setting
G(x,y,z)=\|X\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X\|\leq 1}+\|Y\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Y\|\leq 1}+\|Z\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Z\|\leq 1}
, we get that
G(x,y,z)+F(x,y,z)
equals

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{X}{\|X\|^{2}}+\frac{Y}{\|Y\|^{2}}+\frac{Z}{\|Z\|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\Big(X\|X\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X\|\leq 1}+Y\|Y\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Y\|\leq 1}+Z\|Z\|^{\gamma-2}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|Z\|\leq 1}\Big),

which is positive according to Lemma
9
. Injecting in (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{N}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}I^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle\Big]{\rm d}s\geq-\frac{\gamma\theta(N-2)}{3N}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}[G(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s},X^{3,N}_{s})]{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma\theta(N-2)}{N}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}]{\rm d}s.

Gathering this and (
8
) we get

\displaystyle S_{t}\geq S_{0}+\gamma(\gamma-\theta)\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}]{\rm d}s.

Since
S_{t}
is bounded by
1
(because
\|X^{1,N}_{t}-X^{2,N}_{t}\|^{\gamma}\wedge 1\leq 1
a.s),
\gamma\in(\theta,2)
, and
S_{0}
is nonnegative, we get that

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|\leq 1}\|X^{1,N}_{s}-X^{2,N}_{s}\|^{\gamma-2}{\rm d}s\Big]\leq\frac{1}{\gamma(\gamma-\theta)}.

This provides some bound saying that uniformly in
N
, the particles do not spend too much time
close to each other. As we will see, this is sufficient to conclude in the subcritical case.
The critical case is of course more complicated.

Lemma 7
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\theta>0
and
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a filtration. If
\tau

({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

KS(\theta,N)

(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}

[0,\tau)
, for all sequence of increasing
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-stopping times
(\tau_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}=\tau
a.s., all

, all

, we have the following identities.

(i) For all
i\in[\![1,N]\!]
, all
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

\displaystyle\varphi(\|X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|^{2})=

\displaystyle\varphi(\|X^{i,N}_{0}\|^{2})+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})X^{i,N}_{s}\cdot{\rm d}B^{i}_{s}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}[\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})+\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})]{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\!\!\frac{2\theta}{N}\!\!\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\!\!\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\!\cdot\!X^{j,N}_{s}\!\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2}){\rm d}s.

(ii) For all
i,j\in[\![1,N]\!]
such that
i\neq j
, all
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

\displaystyle\varphi(\|X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}-X^{j,N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|^{2})=

\displaystyle\varphi(\|X^{i,N}_{0}-X^{j,N}_{0}\|^{2})+M_{t}^{N,n}+I^{N,n}_{t}+\frac{2\theta}{N}J^{N,n}_{t},

where

\displaystyle M^{N,n}_{t}=2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2})(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\cdot{\rm d}(B^{i}_{s}-B^{j}_{s}),

\displaystyle I^{N,n}_{t}=4\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\Big[\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2})+\|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2})\Big]{\rm d}s,

and

\displaystyle J^{N,n}_{t}\!\!=\!\!\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\!\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{N}(K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{k,N}_{s})\!

\displaystyle+\!K(X^{k,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}))

\displaystyle\cdot(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\varphi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2}){\rm d}s.

(iii) Setting
\mu^{N}_{t}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i,N}_{t}}
for all
t\in[0,T)
, we have all
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)\mu^{N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}({\rm d}x)=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)\mu^{N}_{0}({\rm d}x)+\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s})\cdot{\rm d}B^{i}_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta\varphi(x)\mu^{N}_{s}({\rm d}x){\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)]\mu^{N}_{s}({\rm d}x)\mu^{N}_{s}({\rm d}y){\rm d}s.

Proof.

First, (i) follows directly from the Itô formula, knowing that setting
\psi:x\mapsto\varphi(\|x\|^{2})
, we have
\nabla\psi(x)=2x\varphi^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})
and
\Delta\psi(x)=4\Big(\varphi^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})+\|x\|^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\|x\|^{2})\Big)
. Moreover (ii) is true for the same reason so it only remains to show (iii).

We fix
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Applying the Itô formula to
\varphi(X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}})
and summing over
i\in[\![1,N]\!]
, we get

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(X^{i,N}_{0})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s})\cdot{\rm d}B^{i}_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\Delta\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s}){\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\cdot\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s}){\rm d}s.

By symmetrizing, we get that for all
s\in[0,t\wedge\tau_{n}]
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\cdot\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\cdot[\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s})-\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N}_{s})],

which together with (
10
) imply the result.

3.
Compactness

Only in this section,
\theta
will take any positive values, without any other restriction.
We consider
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We consider also for all
N\geq 5
,
F^{N}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{sym,1}^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{N})
with associated random variable
(X^{1,N}_{0},\dots,X^{N,N}_{0})
such that
\mu_{0}^{N}
goes weakly to
f_{0}
in probability as
N\to\infty
where
\mu_{0}^{N}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i,N}_{0}}
. Applying Theorem
5
, there exists a
KS(\theta,N)
-process on
[0,\tau_{3}^{N})
with initial law
F^{N}_{0}
which is denoted
(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\tau_{3}^{N}),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}
. We recall that
N_{0}=(1+\lceil 2/(2-\theta)\rceil)\lor 5

if
\theta\in(0,2)
and
N_{0}=5
if
\theta\geq 2
.
We define the empirical measure for all

and all
t\geq 0

\mu^{N,\beta_{N}}_{t}=\mu^{N}_{t\land\beta_{N}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i,N}_{t\land\beta_{N}}},

where we set

with

It is clear that

(X^{i,N}_{t})_{t\in[0,\beta_{N}),i\in[\![1,N]\!]}

This section is devoted to showing the following Theorem
4
-(i) and Theorem
6
-(i). To this end, we need to prove some preliminary estimates.

Proposition 8
.

(i) There exists some nondecreasing
\psi\in C({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that
\psi(0)=1
,

\psi(2r)\leq C\psi(r)
for some constant
C>0
,
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)=\infty
and

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }T\geq 0,\qquad M_{T}:=\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(\|X_{t\wedge\beta_{N}}^{i,N}\|^{2})\Big]<\infty.

(ii) For all
T>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K_{\varepsilon,T}
a compact set of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that,

\mbox{ for all }N\geq N_{0},\quad{\mathbb{P}}(\forall t\in[0,T],\mu^{N,\beta_{N}}_{t}\in K_{\varepsilon,T})\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

We divide the proof in three steps.

Step 1.
We first show that
(\|X^{1,N}_{0}\|)_{N\geq N_{0}}
is tight. Since

\mu^{N}_{0}
goes weakly to
f_{0}
in probability, we conclude that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\mu^{N}_{0}(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:||x||\geq K\}]\leq f_{0}(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:||x||\geq K\})

for all
K\geq 1
.
Since it holds that
{\mathbb{E}}[\mu^{N}_{0}(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:||x||\geq K\}]={\mathbb{P}}(||X^{1,N}_{0}||\geq K)
, we conclude that

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(||X^{1,N}_{0}||\geq K)=0
.

Step 2.

We conclude the proof of (i). Since
(X^{1,N}_{0})_{N\geq N_{0}}
is tight, by Lemma
A.1
there exists some nondecreasing
\psi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)=\infty
,
\psi(0)=1
,

\psi(2r)\leq C\psi(r)
and
(1+r)|\psi^{\prime}(r)|+r|\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)|\leq C
for some constant
C>0
, and such that

(11)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}{\mathbb{E}}[\psi(\|X^{1,N}_{0}\|^{2})]<\infty.

We fix
T>0
. Applying Lemma
7
-(iii) and taking the supremum we get,

(12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(\|X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\beta_{N}}\|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(\|X^{i,N}_{0}\|^{2})+I^{1}_{T}+I^{2}_{T}+I^{3}_{T},

where

\displaystyle I^{1}_{T}=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\beta_{N}}(|\psi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})|+\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2}))|{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle I^{2}_{T}=2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\beta_{N}}\psi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})X^{i,N}_{s}\cdot{\rm d}B^{i}_{s}\Big),

\displaystyle I^{3}_{T}=\frac{\theta}{N^{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\beta_{N}}\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{j,N}_{s})\cdot[X^{i,N}_{s}\psi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2})-X^{j,N}_{s}\psi^{\prime}(\|X^{j,N}_{s}\|^{2})]{\rm d}s.

where
I^{3}_{T}
was obtained thanks to a symmetrization argument.
Thanks to the hypothesis we made on
\psi
, we have
I^{1}_{T}\leq 2C(T\wedge\beta_{N})
a.s. Moreover, since
||K(x)||\leq||x||^{-1}
, and since
g:x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mapsto x\psi^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})
is differentiable with a bounded differential according to the properties of
\psi
, we get that
I^{3}_{T}\leq\theta\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}(T\wedge\beta_{N})
a.s. Finally, using the Cauchy-Schwarz and the Doob inequalities, we find that there exists a universal constant

, of which the value is allowed to vary, such that

{\mathbb{E}}[I^{2}_{T}]\leq C^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\beta_{N}}(\psi^{\prime}(\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2}))^{2}\|X^{i,N}_{s}\|^{2}{\rm d}s\Big]\Big)^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime}T^{1/2},

where we used the properties of
\psi
.
This implies, by taking the expectation in (
12
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(\|X^{i,N}_{t\wedge\beta_{N}}\|^{2})\Big]\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}\psi(\|X^{1,N}_{0}\|^{2})\Big]+(2C+\theta\|\nabla g\|_{\infty})T+C^{\prime}T^{1/2}.

Combining with (
11
), we get the result.

Step 3.
We fix
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
and, for some positive sequences

(A^{\varepsilon}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\eta^{\varepsilon}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}A^{\varepsilon}_{k}=\infty
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta^{\varepsilon}_{k}=0

Lemma 9
.

For all pair of nonincreasing functions
\varphi,\psi:(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

such that
X+Y+Z=0
, we have

\Delta=[\varphi(\|X\|)X+\varphi(\|Y\|)Y+\varphi(\|Z\|)Z]\cdot[\psi(\|X\|)X+\psi(\|Y\|)Y+\psi(\|Z\|)Z]\geq 0.

More precisely, if e.g.
\|X\|\leq\|Y\|\leq\|Z\|
, we have

\Delta\geq[\varphi(\|X\|)-\varphi(\|Y\|)][\psi(\|X\|)-\psi(\|Y\|)]\|X\|^{2}.

Proof.

We may study only the case where
\|X\|\leq\|Y\|\leq\|Z\|
. Since
Y=-X-Z
,

\displaystyle\varphi(\|X\|)X+\varphi(\|Y\|)Y+\varphi(\|Z\|)Z=\lambda X-\mu Z,

\displaystyle\psi(\|X\|)X+\psi(\|Y\|)Y+\psi(\|Z\|)Z=\lambda^{\prime}X-\mu^{\prime}Z,

where
\lambda=\varphi(\|X\|)-\varphi(\|Y\|)\geq 0
,

\mu=\varphi(\|Y\|)-\varphi(\|Z\|)\geq 0
,

\lambda^{\prime}=\psi(\|X\|)-\psi(\|Y\|)\geq 0
and
\mu^{\prime}=\psi(\|Y\|)-\psi(\|Z\|)\geq 0
. Therefore,

\Delta=\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\|X\|^{2}+\mu\mu^{\prime}\|Z\|^{2}-(\lambda\mu^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}\mu)X\cdot Z\geq\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\|X\|^{2}

as desired, because
X\cdot Z\leq 0
. Indeed,
if
X\cdot Z>0
, then

\|Y\|^{2}=\|Z+X\|^{2}=\|Z\|^{2}+\|X\|^{2}+2X\cdot Z>\|Z\|^{2}\geq\|Y\|^{2}
, which is absurd.

\lim_{k}N_{k}=\infty
and

(\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
goes to
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in law as
k\to\infty
. Moreover we have
\mu_{0}=f_{0}
since by hypothesis,
\mu^{N}_{0}
goes weakly to
f_{0}
in probability as
N\to\infty
. It suffices to prove that
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies (
6
), which we have already seen in Proposition
19
,
and is a weak solution to (
1
)-(
2
), which we now do.

We apply Lemma
7
-(iii) to
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and get

I^{1}_{t}(\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}})-I^{2}_{t}(\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}})=M_{k}(t)+R_{k}(t),

where

\displaystyle M_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\nabla\varphi(X^{i,N_{k}}_{s})\cdot{\rm d}B^{i}_{s},

for all
\nu\in C([0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
,

\displaystyle I^{1}_{t}(\nu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)\nu_{t}({\rm d}x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(x)\nu_{0}({\rm d}x)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta\varphi(x)\nu_{s}({\rm d}x){\rm d}s,

\displaystyle I^{2}_{t}(\nu)=\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)]\nu_{s}({\rm d}x)\nu_{s}({\rm d}y){\rm d}s,

and

\displaystyle R_{k}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t\land\beta_{N_{k}}}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta\varphi(x)\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}}_{s}({\rm d}x){\rm d}s,

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{t\land\beta_{N_{k}}}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)]\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}}_{s}({\rm d}x)\mu^{N_{k},\beta_{N_{k}}}_{s}({\rm d}y){\rm d}s.

Using that
||K(x)||\leq||x||^{-1}\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\{x\neq 0\}}
and that

is bounded, one easily checks that

which tends to

as

-(ii) that

)-(

where

∎

Appendix A
Appendix

We prove here a de La Vallée Poussin type lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

If
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a tight sequence of random variables with values in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, then there exists some nondecreasing
\psi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)=\infty
,
\psi(0)=1
,
\psi(2r)\leq C\psi(r)
and
(1+r)|\psi^{\prime}(r)|+r|\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)|\leq C
for some constant
C>0
and

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[\psi(X_{n})]<\infty.

Proof.

Since
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is tight, there exists a sequence of increasing positive numbers
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that

\sup_{n\geq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{n}\geq a_{k})\leq 2^{-k}.

Moreover we can choose this sequence such that
a_{0}=1
and for all
k\geq 0
,
a_{k+1}\geq 2a_{k}
.
We set, for all
k\geq 0
,
m_{k}=(a_{k+1}-a_{k})^{-1}
. For all
r\geq 0
, we define

\psi(r)=1+\sum_{k\geq 0}\chi(m_{k}(r-a_{k})),

where
\chi
is a smooth nondecreasing function such that
\chi(x)=0
for all
x\leq 0
and
\chi(x)=1
for all
x\geq 1
. Observe that for every
r\geq 0
,
\psi(r)<\infty
since the sum in the definition has a finite number
of non-zero terms.
Clearly,
\psi
is smooth, nondecreasing and
\psi(0)=1
. Noting that
\psi(r)\geq n+1
for all

r\geq a_{n}
and all
n\geq 0

(because then
m_{k}(r-a_{k})\geq 1
for all
k\in[\![0,n-1]\!]
by definition of
m_{k}
, with the convention
that
[\![0,-1]\!]=\emptyset
), we conclude that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)=\infty
.
Moreover, for all
n\geq 0
, since
\chi\leq\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\psi(X_{n})]\leq 1+\sum_{k\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{X_{n}\geq a_{k}}]\leq 1+\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k}=3.

It is finally sufficient to prove that

•

(a)
\sup_{r\geq 0}[(1+r)|\psi^{\prime}(r)|+r|\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)|]<\infty
,

•

(b)
\sup_{r\geq 0}\psi(2r)/\psi(r)<\infty
.

We first prove (a). Since the sum in the definition of
\psi
has a finite number of non-zero terms,
we get

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(r)=\sum_{k\geq 0}m_{k}\chi^{\prime}(m_{k}(r-a_{k})),\qquad\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)=\sum_{k\geq 0}m_{k}^{2}\chi^{\prime\prime}(m_{k}(r-a_{k})).

Since for every
k\geq 0
,
r\mapsto\chi^{\prime}(m_{k}(r-a_{k}))
is supported by
[a_{k},a_{k}+m_{k}^{-1}]=[a_{k},a_{k+1}]

(by definition of
m_{k}
), and since for every
r\geq 0
, there exists a unique
k_{r}\geq 0
such that

a_{k_{r}}\leq r<a_{k_{r}+1}=a_{k_{r}}+m_{k_{r}}^{-1}
, we get
\psi^{\prime}(r)=m_{k_{r}}\chi^{\prime}(m_{k_{r}}(r-a_{k_{r}}))

so that
(1+r)|\psi^{\prime}(r)|
is less than

\displaystyle(1+a_{k_{r}}+m_{k_{r}}^{-1})m_{k_{r}}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}=(m_{k_{r}}+m_{k_{r}}a_{k_{r}}+1)\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{k\geq 0}(m_{k}+m_{k}a_{k}+1)\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

However, for all
k\geq 0
,
m_{k}a_{k}=a_{k}/(a_{k+1}-a_{k})\leq 1/2
since
a_{k+1}\geq 2a_{k}
by construction,
and the sequence
(m_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is clearly bounded, so that
\sup_{r\geq 0}(1+r)|\psi^{\prime}(r)|
is finite. Finally, using the same kind of arguments, we find

r\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq\sup_{k\geq 0}(a_{k}+m_{k}^{-1})m_{k}^{2}\|\chi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{k\geq 0}(a_{k}m_{k}+1)m_{k}\|\chi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}<\infty,

since as already seen, the sequences
(a_{k}m_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(m_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are bounded.

We end up by proving (b). Fix
r\geq 0
and consider
k_{r}
such that

a_{k_{r}}\leq r<a_{k_{r}+1}
. Then we have
\psi(r)\geq k_{r}+1
, as seen a few lines above.
Moreover, since
2r<2a_{k_{r}+1}\leq a_{k_{r}+2}
,
we have
\psi(2r)\leq k_{r}+3
(because
m_{k}(2r-a_{k})\leq 0
for all
k\geq k_{r}+2
). All in all,

\frac{\psi(2r)}{\psi(r)}\leq\frac{k_{r}+3}{k_{r}+1}\leq 3,

which concludes the proof.
∎

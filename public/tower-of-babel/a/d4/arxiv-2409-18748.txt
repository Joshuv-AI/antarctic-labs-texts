On NP-Hardness of L 1 / L 2 L_{1}/L_{2} Minimization and Bound Theory of Nonzero Entries in Solutions
Min Tao, Xiao-Ping Zhang, Yun-Bin Zhao

Abstract
The L 1 / L 2 L_{1}/L_{2} norm ratio has gained significant attention as a measure of sparsity due to three merits: sharper approximation to the L 0 L_{0} norm compared to the L 1 L_{1} norm, being parameter-free and scale-invariant, and exceptional performance with highly coherent matrices. These properties have led to its successful application across a wide range of fields. While several efficient algorithms have been proposed to compute stationary points for L 1 / L 2 L_{1}/L_{2} minimization problems, their computational complexity has remained open. In this paper, we prove that finding the global minimum of both constrained and unconstrained L 1 / L 2 L_{1}/L_{2} models is strongly NP-hard. In addition, we establish uniform upper bounds on the L 2 L_{2} norm for any local minimizer of both constrained and unconstrained L 1 / L 2 L_{1}/L_{2} minimization models. We also derive upper and lower bounds on the magnitudes of the nonzero entries in any local minimizer of the unconstrained model, aiding in classifying nonzero entries. Finally, we extend our analysis to demonstrate that the constrained and unconstrained L p / L q L_{p}/L_{q} ( 0 < p ≤ 1 , 1 < q < + ∞ 0<p\leq 1,1<q<+\infty ) models are also strongly NP-hard.



I
Introduction

Compressive sensing (CS) aims to recover sparse signals using a set of undersampled linear measurements. Consequently, the fundamental problem in CS can be formulated as the following
L_{0}
-minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}}\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{0},\quad\text{subject to}\quad A{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{b}},

(1)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
m\ll n
is a given sensing matrix, and
{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the observation (measurement) vector (
{\mathbf{b}}\neq\mathbf{0}
). Here,
\|\cdot\|_{0}
denotes the
L_{0}
norm, which counts the number of nonzero entries in a vector, and
{\cal X}
represents either
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Problem (1) is known to be NP-hard
[
1
]
, indicating that it cannot be solved in polynomial time as the problem size increases.

As a practical alternative, the
L_{1}
penalty regularization can be employed, replacing the
L_{0}
norm with the
L_{1}
norm, resulting in the following optimization problem:

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}}\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1},\quad\text{subject to}\quad A{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{b}}.

(2)

Several exact recovery conditions have been developed to ensure that the solution of problem (
2
) coincides with the solution of (
1
). These conditions include the exact
L_{1}
-norm recovery condition, which states that a solution
\mathbf{x}_{0}
to
A\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{b}
satisfies
\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\right)
, where
\mu(A)
is the mutual coherence of
A

[
2
]
. Other conditions include the restricted isometry property (RIP)
[
3
,
4
]
, the null space condition
[
5
]
, and the restricted eigenvalue condition (ERC)
[
6
]
, etc. The resulting model in (2) is convex and can be solved to global optimality in polynomial time
[
7
]
.

While
L_{1}
minimization is computationally tractable, it tends to bias toward larger coefficients
[
8
]
, hindering true sparsity. To address this limitation, several nonconvex relaxation techniques have emerged, such as the
L_{p}
model (for
0<p<1
)
[
9
]
, the transformed
L_{1}
norm
[
10
]
, and the capped
L_{1}
norm
[
11
]
, all aimed at promoting better sparsity recovery. In particular, the
L_{1}/L_{2}
ratio has gained attention due to its parameter-free and scale-invariant characteristic, providing a sharper approximation to
L_{0}
norm compared to the
L_{1}
norm (see the landscapes of
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}
,
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}
, and
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}/\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
in Fig.
1
) and demonstrating greater efficiency when dealing with highly coherent sensing matrices
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. As a result,
L_{1}/L_{2}
minimization
[
17
,
14
]
has found successful applications across various domains, highlighting its effectiveness and practicality
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Motivation and related works

Various exact recovery conditions from

[

[

On the other hand, methods such as the scaled gradient projection method (SGPM)

14
]

[

, and ADMM

[
13

are designed for the unconstrained

model. The moving balls approximation algorithm
[
26
]
has been developed to address the constrained model in both noiseless and noisy scenarios.

Additionally, a DCA-type algorithm has been proposed to solve the sorted

minimization problems, which assign higher weights to indices with smaller absolute values and lower weights to larger values, thereby promoting sparsity
[
27

. The proximal subgradient algorithm with extrapolation techniques

Recently, the variational properties of

[
29
]
. These findings demonstrated that proximal-friendly methods, such as ADMM

[
12
]
and ADMM

[
13
]
, preserve the finite identification property

30
]
, which enables the identification of low-dimensional manifolds within a finite number of iterations. A two-phase heuristic acceleration framework, combining ADMM

/ADMM

with a globalized semismooth Newton method, was developed in
[
29
]
, achieving high-order convergence rates while significantly reducing computational complexity. However, these methods typically converge to d-stationary
[
31
,
13
]
or critical points rather than global solutions.

For the analysis of global convergence to a d-stationary or critical point in the SGPM
[
25
,
14
]
, the accelerated schemes
[

]
, ADMM

[

]

[

[

, it is usually assumed that the primal sequence

Moreover,
[

established lower bounds on the absolute values of the nonzero entries of any local optimal solution of the unconstrained model with the

In terms of computational complexity, the constrained model with the

I-B

This paper

We focus on the following constrained

model:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{{\mathbf{x}}}&\displaystyle{\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}}}\\[5.69046pt]
\text{subject to}&\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}:=\{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}\mid A{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{b}}\},\end{array}

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal X}}\gamma\frac{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}}{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|A{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{b}\|_{2}^{2},

(6)

) is a given sensing matrix,

(

is a balancing parameter.

First, we derive a uniform upper bound, in terms of

-norm, for any local minimizer of both the constrained model (

) and the unconstrained model (
6
). This result shows that the boundedness typically required for global convergence analysis of first-order algorithms, such as those in
[

,

,
12
,
13
,
29
,
16
]
, for solving these models can be imposed with

.

Second, we establish either a lower or upper bound for the nonzero entries in any local minimizer of the unconstrained model (
6
). These theoretical results sheds light on the following two phenomena:

1.

As noted in
[
16
]
, when the dynamic range of the ground-truth signal is larger,

minimization yields a smaller relative error. The established bounds in this paper can help explain this phenomenon.

2.

In our previous work
[
29
]
, we introduced a hard-shrinkage step within the heuristic acceleration framework on active manifolds (see
[
29
, Algorithm 1]
). This step, applied between Phase I and Phase II, significantly improved recovery accuracy when processing real data. Further discussions can be found in
[
29
, Section V.C]
.

Last but not least, to the best of our knowledge, the computational complexity of
L_{1}/L_{2}
minimization remains unexploited.
This raises the following fundamental open questions:

•

“
Whether the constrained model (
I-B
) and the unconstrained model (
6
) are NP-hard?
”

•

“
Whether the constrained model (
I-B
) and the unconstrained model (

We answer both questions affirmatively.
In light of the growing interest in ratio minimization problems

,

Theorem 2

\displaystyle r\leq{\sigma_{\min}(A_{\Lambda}^{\top}A_{\Lambda})}^{-1}\left[\|(A_{\Lambda})^{\top}{\mathbf{b}}\|+{\gamma}\left\|\frac{\sign(\mathbf{y})}{r}-\frac{a}{r^{3}}{\mathbf{y}}\right\|\right].

First, we establish the NP-hardness of (

. To show the NP-hardness, we employ a polynomial-time reduction from the well-known NP-complete partition problem
[
38

.
The partition problem can be described as follows: Given a set
\cal S
of rational numbers
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
, can we partition
\cal S
into two disjoint subsets,
{\cal S}_{1}
and
{\cal S}_{2}
, such that the sums of the elements in each subset are equal?

Theorem 4
.

The
L_{1}
over
L_{2}
minimization problem (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}
or
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is NP-hard.

Proof.

First, we prove the NP-hardness of problem (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.
An instance of the partition problem can be reduced to an instance of (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
where the optimal value of
\sqrt{n}
if and only if the former has an equitable bipartition. Define
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})^{\top}
.
Consider the following problem

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}&\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{u}}:=\left({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2n}\\
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n},\;{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\end{subarray}}P({\mathbf{u}})=\displaystyle{\frac{\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{1}}{\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}}}\\[11.38092pt]
s.t.&\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbf{a}}^{\top}&-{\mathbf{a}}^{\top}\\
I_{n}&I_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\mathbf{u}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf 0}\\
{\mathbf{e}}\end{array}\right).\end{array}

which is an instance of (
I-B
) by setting

\displaystyle{\cal A}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbf{a}}^{\top}&-{\mathbf{a}}^{\top}\\
I_{n}&I_{n}\\
\end{array}\right),\;\mbox{and}\;{\textit{ b}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf 0}\\
{\mathbf{e}}\end{array}\right).

By noting

\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|{\mathbf{x}}+{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle P({\mathbf{u}})=\displaystyle{\frac{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}+\|{\mathbf{y}}\|_{1}}{\sqrt{\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}^{2}}}}\geq\displaystyle{\frac{\|{\mathbf{x}}+{\mathbf{y}}\|_{1}}{\|{\mathbf{x}}+{\mathbf{y}}\|_{2}}}=\frac{\|{\mathbf{e}}\|_{1}}{\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}}=\sqrt{n}.

The problem (
IV-A
) achieves a value of
\sqrt{n}
if and only if
x_{i}=1
and
y_{i}=0
, or
x_{i}=0
and
y_{i}=1
, which generates an evenly partitioned set of
\cal S
. Conversely, if the entries of
\cal S
admit an equitable bipartition, then the problem (
IV-A
) achieves the global solution, i.e.,
P({\mathbf{u}})=\sqrt{n}
.

Analogously, the same instance of the partition problem can also be
reduced to (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}&\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{u}}:=\left({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\\
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\end{subarray}}P({\mathbf{u}})=\displaystyle{\frac{\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{1}}{\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}}}\\[14.22636pt]

I_{n}&I_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\mathbf{u}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf 0}\\
{\mathbf{e}}\end{array}\right).\end{array}

With a similar argument, we can show that the global minimum value of

is achieved if and only if

has an equitable bipartition. Therefore, the problem (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

∎

Second, we prove the strong NP-hardness of (
I-B
) when
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}/{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by utilizing
3-partition problem. We recall the strongly NP-hard 3-partition problem: Given a multiset

containing

\mathcal{T}
into
m

Theorem 5
.

The
L_{1}
over
L_{2}
minimization problem (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}
or
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Proof.

First,
we describe a reduction from an instance of the 3-partition problem
to an instance of (
I-B
) with
{\cal X}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
where the optimal value

if and only if the former

Consider the following minimization problem:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rll}\min\limits_{{\mathbf{u}}:={\text{vec}}({\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}_{+}}&P(\mathbf{u})=\displaystyle{\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}}}&\\[8.5359pt]

The first equality constraint implies that

The second equality constraint is equivalent to

Note that the objective function value of (

{\cal T}
. Conversely, if the entries of
{\cal T}
have an equitable 3-partition, then the problem (

) must achieve the global solution, i.e.,

For the same instance of the 3-partition problem, we consider the following minimization problem in the form (

) with

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rll}\min\limits_{{\mathbf{u}}:={\text{vec}}({\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}}&P(\mathbf{u})=\displaystyle{\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}}}&\\[8.5359pt]
s.t.&\left(({\mathbf{e}}^{(m)})^{\top}\otimes I_{n}\right){\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{e}}^{(n)},&\\[2.84544pt]

The optimal value of the problem above is also

) with

∎

Subsequently, we prove the strong NP-hardness of (

) under the same condition

Theorem 7
.

The
L_{1}
over
L_{2}
minimization problem (
6
) with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}
or
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Proof.

We describe a reduction from an instance of the 3-partition problem
to an instance of the

over

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}
if and only if the former

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{{\mathbf{u}}:={\text{vec}}({\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}_{+}}P(\mathbf{u})&=\|\left(({\mathbf{e}}^{(m)})^{\top}\otimes I_{n}\right){\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{e}}^{(n)}\|^{2}\\[5.69046pt]

) satisfies that

VI
Discussion and conclusion

The existing computational methods
[
15
,
13
,
16
,
25
,
14
,
12
,
29
,
26
,
28
,
27
]
for
L_{1}/L_{2}
minimization tend to converge to d-stationary or critical points rather than global solutions. Exact recovery conditions for global solutions of (
I-B
) have been derived in
[
23
]
. This paper presents a formal proof of the difficulty in finding the global optimizer for both (
I-B
) and (
6
).

To transition from a d-stationary or critical point to a local minimizer, the negative curvature algorithm
[
39
]
can be employed. Furthermore, to progress from a local minimizer to a global solution, the simulated annealing (SA) algorithm
[
40
]
can be used. However, it is important to note that convergence to a global solution is guaranteed only with probability 1.

In this work, by leveraging our previous results
[
13
]
, we have derived a uniform upper bound for any local minimizer of the constrained/unconstrained model in terms of the
L_{2}
-norm. Additionally, by applying the first- and second-order necessary conditions for a local minimizer, we have established upper/lower bounds for the nonzero entries of any local solution to the unconstrained model. Moreover, we have shown that the constrained and unconstrained
L_{1}/L_{2}
models are strongly NP-hard. Finally, we have demonstrated that minimizing the
L_{p}
norm (
0<p\leq 1
) over the
L_{q}
norm (
1<q<+\infty
) is also strongly NP-hard.

Abstract
. A topological theorem that appears in a paper by Deligne-Goncharov [ 1 ] states the following. Let ( X , ∗ ) (X,*) be a path connected pointed space with a reasonable topology and denote by ℐ {\mathcal{I}} the augmentation ideal of the group ring ℤ ​ π 1 ​ ( X , ∗ ) {\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,*) . Then for every field F F and positive integer n n , the space of F F -valued linear forms on ℐ / ℐ n + 1 {\mathcal{I}}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1} is naturally isomorphic to H n ​ ( X n , a certain subspace of ​ X n , F ) H^{n}(X^{n},\text{a certain subspace of }X^{n};F) . Among other things we give a simple construction of an isomorphism between ℐ / ℐ n + 1 {\mathcal{I}}/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1} and the integral H n H_{n} of this pair and express the maps that define the Hopf algebra structure on ℤ ​ π 1 ​ ( X , ∗ ) / ℐ n + 1 {\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,*)/{\mathcal{I}}^{n+1} in these terms.

Introduction

This note arose from a desire to better understand a theorem in a paper by Deligne-Goncharov (
[
1
]
, Prop. 3.4), who attribute this to Beilinson. The theorem (and its proof) is of a topological nature and easy to state.
In the version that we discuss here it amounts to the following.

Let
X
be a connected topological space with a reasonable topology (we are more specific about this below) and let
a,b\in X
. Consider the set
\pi_{X}(a,b)
of homotopy classes of paths in
X
from
a
to
b
. Since this is a principal right
\pi_{1}(X,a)
-set, the free abelian group

{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)
it generates is a principal right
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,a)
-module (it is also a principal left
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,b)
-module). So if
{\mathcal{I}}_{a}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,a)
denotes the augmentation ideal, then the quotient module
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)/{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b){\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
is a principal right module for the truncated group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(X,a)/{\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
. Letting
X(n)^{a}_{b}\subset X^{n}
stand for the set of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
for which one of the following identities
x_{1}=a
,
x_{\nu}=x_{\nu+1}
(
\nu=1,\dots,n-1
) or
x_{n}=b
holds, then the theorem identifies the relative homology group
H_{n}(X^{n},X(n)^{a}_{b})
with
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)/{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b){\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
if
b\not=a
and with
{\mathcal{I}}_{a}/{\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
if
b=a
.

This has immediate applications in algebraic geometry, as it gives us a motivic interpretation of the abelian groups
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)/{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b){\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
when
X
is a connected complex algebraic variety. In particular, this provides an alternate way to define mixed Hodge structures on these (originally due to Hain
[
2
]
and based on the reduced bar complex). Our motivation is however different, for we expect this theorem (and its proof ingredients) to help us understand the way a mapping class group acts on the cohomology of the configuration spaces of a surface.

The result as stated above strengthens that in
[
1
]
(where the dual version is proved with field coefficients).
We give in addition a simple explicit description of the map from
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)/{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b){\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}

to
H_{n}(X^{n},X(n)^{a}_{b})
. We complete the story by verifying that the structural maps involving the abelian groups

{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)/{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{a}(X){\mathcal{I}}_{a}^{n+1}
(truncation, inversion, composition, and in case
a=b
, the coproduct) have homologically defined counterparts. This last property guarantees that when
X
is a connected complex algebraic variety, these structural map are morphisms of mixed Hodge structures. Somewhat surprisingly, the composition map, which here takes the form of Theorem
4.2
, turned out to be the least trivial in this respect.

Where the proof of the Beilinson-Deligne-Goncharov is concerned, ours is in the end probably not so different from theirs. Yet we feel that the strengthening presented here and its proof make the discussion more direct (if not shorter) and for a topologist perhaps also more transparent. We also pay attention to homotopy aspects.

{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{X}(a,b)_{n}
which is ‘sufficiently natural’ must be this one.

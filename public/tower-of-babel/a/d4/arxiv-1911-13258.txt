Boundary of the Milnor fiber of a Newton non degenerate surface singularity
Octave Curmi

Abstract
We give an algorithm for the description of the boundary of the Milnor fiber of a Newton non degenerate surface singularity as a graph manifold. This is based on a previous work by the author describing a general method for the computation of the boundary of the Milnor fiber of any reduced non isolated singularity of complex surface.



1
Introduction

The study of Milnor fibers of complex-analytic functions, which began in the second half of the 20th century with the work
[
Mil68
]
, gave rise to a rich interaction between algebra and topology. One of its interesting aspects is that it can be used to provide equations for sophisticated topological objects, such as exotic spheres, see
[
Bri00
, p 46-48]
and
[
Hir95
]
. See also
[
Sea19
, Section 1]
.

In his 1968 book, Milnor explored the topology of isolated singularities of hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
V(f)=\{f=0\}
, introducing two equivalent fibrations, given respectively by the levels of
f/|f|
on spheres centered at the critical points of
f
and by the levels of
f
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The second of these two fibrations has been extended to more general contexts by Lê, see
[
Lê77
]
, and is known as the
Milnor-Lê fibration
. However, the singularities of the ambient space may lead to singular generic fibers. Hamm, in
[
Ham71
]
, provided a setting in which the Milnor-Lê fibration is actually a
smoothing
of
V(f)
, that is, a way to put
V(f)
in a
flat
family of analytic spaces with smooth generic fiber. Namely, if
(X,0)
is a germ of equidimensional complex analytic space, and
f
is any holomorphic function on
(X,0)
such that
V(f)
contains the singular locus
\operatorname*{Sing}(X)
of
X
, then the function
f
provides a smoothing of the singularity
(V(f),0)
.

It is very hard to describe the whole Milnor fiber up to homeomorphisms, even for isolated singularities, and has been realized for only a few types of singularities. It is the case for the Kleinean singularities A, D, E, where the Milnor fiber is unique and diffeomorphic to a tubular neighbourhood of the exceptional divisor of the minimal resolution (see Brieskorn,
[
Bri66
]
), as well as for the singularities of normal toric surfaces, with a description by surgery (see Lisca,
[
Lis08
]
and Némethi & Popescu-Pampu,
[
NPP10
]
), and for sandwich singularities (De Jong & Van Straten,
[
dJvS98
]
). As for nonisolated singularities, the only known case is that of hypersurface singularities of the form
\{f(x,y)+zg(x,y)=0\}
, see Sigurðsson,
[
Sig16
]
. We refer also to the survey
[
Gre19
]
for more about the topology of smoothings and deformations of singularities.

On the other hand, the study of the
boundary

\partial F
of the Milnor fiber of a smoothing has been a very active area of research in the last decades. For an isolated singularity,
\partial F
is unique, and diffeomorphic to the link of the singularity. In today’s words, Mumford proved in
[
Mum61
]
that the link of any isolated singularity of complex surface is a
graph manifold
i.e. a manifold describable using a decorated graph whose vertices represent fibrations in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
over compact surfaces. Waldhausen, in
[
Wal67
]
, later introduced this vocabulary and began studying this class of manifolds in itself. Furthermore, thanks to the work of Grauert (
[
Gra62
]
), one knows exactly which graph manifolds appear as links of isolated singularities of complex surfaces. However, this strong result is tempered by the fact that one still does not know, for example, which of these manifolds appear as links of singularities of
hypersurfaces
of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

Still, one would like to get an analogous result for boundaries of Milnor fibers associated to non isolated singularities. The first steps towards the comprehension of the topology of these manifolds were made by Randell
[
Ran77
]
, then Siersma in
[
Sie91
]
,
[
Sie01
]
, who computed the homology of the boundary
\partial F
of the Milnor fiber in certain cases, and characterized the cases in which
\partial F
is a rational homology sphere.

Concerning the general topology of this manifold, a series of results were aimed at proving that the boundary of the Milnor fibers associated to a non isolated singularity is, again, a graph manifold, see the works
[
MP03
]
,
[
MP04
]
and
[
MP16
]
of Michel & Pichon as well as their work
[
MPW07
]
with Weber for the special case of Hirzebruch surface singularities, and
[
MPW09
]
for the so-called suspensions (
f=g(x,y)+z^{n}
). In
[
FdBMN14
]
, Fernández de Bobadilla & Menegon Neto prove the same result for a larger context, and in
[
NS12
]
, Némethi & Szilárd give a constructive proof for the case of reduced holomorphic functions
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
. It is this last proof that we extend here and in
[
Cur19a
]
to study the boundary of the Milnor fiber of a Newton non degenerate function
f\colon(X_{\sigma},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, where
X_{\sigma}
is the
3
-dimensional normal toric variety associated to a cone
\sigma
, and
V(f)\supset\operatorname*{Sing}(X)
. In this setting, we adapt the general procedure of
[
Cur19a
]
to produce an algorithm for the description of the boundary of the Milnor fiber of
f
, extending the work of Oka in
[
Oka87
]
for non degenerate isolated singularities in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

We prove the following theorem, which appeared for the first time in our thesis
[
Cur19b
]
:

Theorem
.

Let
(X,0)
be the germ at its vertex of a
3
-dimensional toric variety defined by a
3
-dimensional cone in a weight lattice. Let
f\colon(X,0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
be a Newton-nondegenerate function whose zero locus contains
\operatorname*{Sing}(X)
. Then the boundary
\partial F
of its Milnor fiber is a graph manifold determined by the local Newton polyhedron of
f
.

The purely combinatorial nature of the description we make of the manifold
\partial F
opens the way for the computation of a great number of examples through a future implementation in a computer program. But it also calls for more theoretic work, such as for example extending what is done in
[
BN07
]
, where Braun & Némethi do the opposite work, retrieving a possible Newton polyhedron of a function
f:\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
having a given graph manifold as boundary of its Milnor fiber, under the hypothesis that this manifold is a rational homology sphere. Another use of this method would be to answer the widely open question of which manifolds can appear as boundaries of Milnor fibers of non degenerate surface singularities. In this direction, our algorithm already provides the following obstruction (see Proposition
5.5
):

Proposition
.

The normal form of the plumbing graph of the Milnor fiber of a Newton non degenerate singularity of complex surface is planar.

The article is organized in the following way:

•

In Section
2
, we recall the main result of
[
Cur19a
]
, on which we base the algorithm described in this work,

•

in Section
3
, we give the definitions and results of toric geometry which are involved in our construction,

•

Section
4
is dedicated to the construction of the intermediate graph
\overset{\star}{\Gamma}\left(\mathscr{C}_{tot}\right)
, which is one of the main ingredients for the description of the manifold
\partial F
in the general case,

•

in Section
5
, we show why the data of
\overset{\star}{\Gamma}\left(\mathscr{C}_{tot}\right)
is sufficient to proceed to the rest of the computation.

2.1
Milnor fibration on a singular variety

In the sequel, a
variety

Let

In the sequel, if

Theorem 2.1
.

(H. Hamm,
[

, Satz 1.6]
, Lê
[
Lê77
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Given a real analytic function

such that

is isolated in

, and

, denote
X_{\varepsilon}:=X\cap\rho^{-1}([0,\varepsilon])
, and

. Let

be a germ of holomorphic function, such that

is smooth. Then there exists

, such that
\forall\penalty\ \,0<\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0},\exists\penalty\ \delta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\forall\penalty\ \,0<\delta\leqslant\delta_{\varepsilon}
, the map

is a smooth fibration, where
D_{\delta}
denotes the closed disc of radius
\delta
around
0
in
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The map defined in the previous Theorem is called the
Milnor fibration
associated to
f
, and its fiber is called the
Milnor fiber
of the germ of function
f
.

Remark 2.3
.

The Milnor-Lê fibration is also sometimes referred to as the Milnor fibration.

Using transversality arguments, one may show that the diffeomorphism type of the Milnor fiber does not depend on the chosen representative, so we speak about
the Milnor fiber of the germ of function
f\in\pazocal{O}_{X,0}

2.2
Graph manifolds

We explain here the definition of graph manifolds (also called plumbed manifolds) that we refer to. For details, one can consult the foundational articles
[
Mum61
]
and
[
Wal67
]
, as well as the article
[
Neu81
]
for a description of the so-called plumbing calculus and other topological considerations on graph manifolds.

Recall that the Euler number of a fibration in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
over a surface is an integer
e
that characterizes the total space of the fibration up to homeomorphism. See
[
GS99
, Example 4.6.5]
for more details.

Definition 2.4
.

An
orientable plumbing graph
is a graph
\Gamma
with decorations of the following type:

•

Each edge is decorated by a
\oplus
or
\ominus
symbol.

•

Each vertex
v
is decorated by an Euler number
e_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}
and a genus
[g_{v}]
,
g_{v}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 2.5
.

When representing plumbing graphs, we may omit the decorations
\oplus
and
[0]
.

Definition 2.6
.

(Graph manifold)

If
\Gamma
is an orientable plumbing graph, define the oriented
graph manifold

M_{\Gamma}
associated to
\Gamma
in the following way: for each vertex
v
of
\Gamma
decorated by
([g_{v}],e_{v})
, let
M_{v}
be the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-fibration with Euler number
e_{v}
over the closed smooth surface
B_{v}
with genus
g_{v}
. Choose an orientation of the base and of the fibers so that, taken together, they give the orientation of
M_{v}
.

Now, let
\lambda_{v}
be the number of times the vertex
v
appears as endpoint of an edge. Remove from
B_{v}
disjoint open disks
(D_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\lambda_{v}}
, consequently removing as many open solid tori from
M_{v}
. Each
\partial D_{i}
is oriented as boundary component of
B_{v}\setminus\bigsqcup D_{i}
. Denote by
M_{v}^{b}
the resulting circle bundle with boundary. The boundary
\partial M_{v}^{b}
is a disjoint union of tori, denote
\partial M_{v}^{b}=:\bigsqcup T_{i}
.

For every edge between the vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
, glue the manifolds
M_{v}^{b}
and
M_{v^{\prime}}^{b}
in the following way: pick boundary components
T=\partial D\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
M_{v}^{b}
,
T^{\prime}=\partial D^{\prime}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
M_{v^{\prime}}^{b}
, and glue
T
and
T^{\prime}
according to the matrix
\epsilon\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
,
\epsilon
being the sign on the edge.

Finally, we use the convention

M_{\Gamma_{1}\bigsqcup\Gamma_{2}}=M_{\Gamma_{1}}\#M_{\Gamma_{1}}.

In
[
Neu81
]
, the author describes the so-called
plumbing calculus
, which consists in a list of transformations one can apply on a given plumbing graph without changing the associated graph manifold. Although there are infinitely many different graphs encoding the same graph manifold, Neumann provides the definition of the
normal form
of a given plumbing graph, as well as an algorithm to obtain it

Main aspects of the general result

We recall here the main steps of the general proof, and show how they relate to the different steps of the computation shown in Sections
4
and
5
in the setting of toric geometry. We start by introducing a second function

in the following sense:

Definition 2.7
.

We say that the holomorphic function
g\colon(X,0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
is a

if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

The surface
V(g)
is smooth outside the origin.

2.

V(g)\cap\Sigma V(f)=\{0\}
.

3.

The surfaces
V(g)
and
V(f)
intersect transversally outside the origin.

4.

The discriminant locus
\Delta_{(f,g)}
is an analytic curve.

In
[

, Lemma 4.4]

X
of a generic linear form on

is a companion of

This is a germ of
4

Proposition 2.8
.

(
[

, Proposition 11.3.3 for

smooth]
,
[

If

This point on, the goal is therefore to describe the manifold

However, the existence of such a resolution is not obvious, because for arbitrary germs of 4-dimensional real analytic varieties, we expect the preimage of the origin to be

•

The first step is a modification

.

2.

Denote

sent on the origin by

In these conditions, define

•

3.1
Fans, toric varieties and toric modifications

We start with the definition of the affine variety associated to a cone and to a fan, their local properties, and conclude this subsection by discussing the modification associated to a refinement of a fan.

Definition 3.1
.

In this work, a
3
-dimensional
lattice

N
is a free group isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}^{3},0)
. Define the
integral length
of an element
u\in N
as

l(u)=max\{n\in\mathbb{N},\exists\penalty\ v\in N\setminus\{0\}\text{ such that }u=n\cdot v\}.

An element of
N
is called
primitive
if its integral length is equal to
1
.

A
cone

\sigma
in
N
is a subset of
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{3}
of the form

\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle:=\{r_{1}\cdot v_{1}+\dots+r_{k}\cdot v_{k},r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}

for some
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in N
.

A cone
\sigma
is called
strongly convex
if it contains no linear subspace of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

A strongly convex cone is called
regular
if it can be generated by a family of elements of
N
that can be completed in a basis of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
N
.

Definition 3.2
.

Denote by
M=N^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}:=Hom(N,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{3}
the
dual lattice
of
N
. For
\sigma
a convex cone of dimension
d
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, its
dual cone
is

\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}},\forall\penalty\ n\in\sigma,\langle m,n\rangle\geqslant 0\}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}

and its
orthogonal space
is

\sigma^{\perp}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}},\forall\penalty\ n\in\sigma,\langle m,n\rangle=0\}\subset\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}},

where
\langle m,n\rangle
denotes the pairing of
m\in M
and
n\in N
.

Note that if
\sigma
is of dimension
3
, then
\sigma
is regular if and only if
\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}
is regular.

Definition 3.3
.

If
\sigma
is a strongly convex cone in
N
, then

X_{\sigma}:=Spec\left(\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}\cap M]\right)

is the affine toric variety associated to it.

Note that
X_{\sigma}
is an irreducible complex affine variety of dimension
3
. See Example
4.5
for an example of affine toric variety.

Remark 3.4
.

A family
m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in M
generating the semigroup
\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}\cap M
provides an embedding
X_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow Spec\left(\mathbb{C}[\chi^{m_{1}},\cdots,\chi^{m_{k}}]\right)={\mathbb{C}^{k}}
, where
\chi^{m}
denotes the element of
\mathbb{C}[M]
associated to
m\in M
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\sigma
be a cone. A
face
of
\sigma
is any cone of the form

\sigma\cap m^{\perp}=\{n\in\sigma,\langle m,n\rangle=0\},\text{ for some m}\in\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}.

The fact that
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
is denoted
\tau\preceq\sigma
.

Proposition 3.6
.

(See
[
CLS11
, Proposition 1.3.16]
.) If
\tau\subset\sigma
, then the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}]\subset\mathbb{C}[\tau^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}]
gives rise to a canonical birational morphism of algebraic varieties
X_{\tau}\rightarrow X_{\sigma}
. If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then this morphism is an inclusion.

Proof of the birationality.

The cones
\sigma,\tau
are strongly convex, hence the dual cones
\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}},\tau^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}
are
3
-dimensional, and the fraction fields
\mathbb{C}(\sigma^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}\cap M),\mathbb{C}(\tau^{\scalebox{.7}{\checkmark}}\cap M)
are both equal to
\mathbb{C}(M)
.
∎

The following definition will allow us to define non-affine toric varieties:

Definition 3.7
.

A
fan

\pazocal{F}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite set of strongly convex cones such that:

1.

If
\sigma\in\pazocal{F}
, any face of
\sigma
is in
\pazocal{F}
.

2.

The intersection of two cones of
\pazocal{F}
is a face of each.

The

|\pazocal{F}|
of the fan
\pazocal{F}
is the union
\bigcup\limits_{\sigma\in\pazocal{F}}\sigma
of the cones composing it.

Lemma A.8
.

(Computing self-intersections.)

Let
{\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}},E,g,E_{tot}
be as in Definition
A.6
. Let
E^{(1)}
be an irreducible component of
E
. Then the self-intersection
e^{(1)}
of the surface
E^{(1)}
in
{\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}}
verifies the following condition: let

be the intersection points of
E^{(1)}
with other components of
E_{tot}
,
p_{j}\in E_{j}\cap E^{(1)}

n^{(1)}\cdot e^{(1)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\cdot n_{i}=0

where

refers to the sign associated to the intersection
p_{i}
in the following sense: if

+1
to
p
if and only if the combination of the orientations of
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
at
p
provides the ambient orientation of
{\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the standard argument in the holomorphic category. The difference of the two members of the equation is the intersection number of
E^{(1)}
with the cycle defined by
g=0
. This cycle is homologous with that defined by a nearby level of
g
, which does not meet

anymore. The intersection number being invariant by homology, one gets the desired result.

In order to make this argument rigorous, one has to work in convenient tubular neighborhoods of

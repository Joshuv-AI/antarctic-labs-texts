Tri-Hamiltonian Structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik Hierarchy
Shuangxing Li, Si-Qi Liu, Haonan Qu, Youjin Zhang

Abstract
We construct a local tri-Hamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, and compute the central invariants of the associated bihamiltonian structures. We show that the central invariants of one of the bihamiltonian structures are equal to 1 24 \frac{1}{24} , and the dispersionless limit of this bihamiltonian structure coincides with the one that is defined on the jet space of the Frobenius manifold associated with the Gromov-Witten invariants of local ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} . This result provides support for the validity of Brini’s conjecture on the relation of these Gromov-Witten invariants with the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy. Keywords Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy; tri-Hamiltonian structure; bihamiltonian structure; central invariant; Gromov-Witten invariant

Introduction

The Ablowitz-Ladik equation is one of the most important differential-difference nonlinear integrable equations in soliton theory. It was obtained by Ablowitz and Ladik as a discretization of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation and was solved by the inverse scattering method in
[
2
,
3
]
, and has close relations to some other important soliton equations such as the 2D Toda lattice equation
[
20
]
, the relativistic Toda lattice equation
[
18
]
, the Toeplitz lattice equation that appears in the study of random matrix models
[
4
]
. It also characterizes some elementary geometric properties of the motion of discrete curves on the
N
-dimensional spheres
[
8
]
. The importance of the Ablowitz-Ladik equation is manifested in the recent study of the Gromov-Witten invariants of local
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. As it is conjectured by Brini in
[
5
]
that the generating function of these Gromov-Witten invariants is given by a particular tau-function of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy. The purpose of the present paper is to study the multi-Hamiltonian structure of the Abolowitz-Ladik hierarchy, and to answer the question raised by Brini and his collaborators in
[
6
,
7
]
on the existence of a bihamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy.

The Ablowitz-Ladik equation is given by
[
3
]

iU_{n,t}=U_{n+1}+U_{n-1}-2U_{n}\pm U^{*}_{n}U_{n}(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

it is the compatibility condition of the discrete Zakharov-Shabat or AKNS spectral problem
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
21
]

\Lambda\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&q\\
r&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(1.1)

and the evolution of the eigenfunctions

\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}F&G\\
H&K\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(1.2)

where
\Lambda
is the shift operator defined by
\Lambda(f)(n)=f(n+1)
,
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},q,r
are functions of
n,t
with

r(n,t)=\mp q^{*}(n,t)=U_{n}(t),

and

\displaystyle F=i\lambda^{2}-iqr^{-}-i,\quad G=iq\lambda-iq^{-}\lambda^{-1},

\displaystyle H=ir^{-}\lambda-ir\lambda^{-1},\quad K=i+iq^{-}r-i\lambda^{-2}.

Here and in what follows we use the notation

\Lambda(f)(n)=f(n+1)=f^{+},\quad\Lambda^{-1}(f)(n)=f(n-1)=f^{-}.

For general independent unknown functions
q(n),r(n)
, we can
assume that the evolution (
1.2
) of the eigenfunctions is given the functions

which are some appropriate polynomials in
\lambda,\lambda^{-1}
.
The coefficients of these polynomials can be obtained by the compatibility condition of (
1.1

1.2
). This compatibility condition also yields a system of differential-difference equations for
q(n,t),r(n,t)
, and the set of differential-difference equations thus obtained is called the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy in
[
18
]
.
The following three choices of the
evolutions (
1.2
) of the eigenfunctions are given in
[
18
]
as elementary flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}_{t_{-1}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-q^{-}\lambda^{-1}\\
-r\lambda^{-1}&-\lambda^{-2}+q^{-}r\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}_{t_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}_{t_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{2}-qr^{-}&q\lambda\\
r^{-}\lambda&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(1.4)

The corresponding differential-difference equations are given by
[
18
]

\begin{cases}q_{t_{-1}}=q^{-}(1-qr)\\
r_{t_{-1}}=-r^{+}(1-qr)\end{cases},\quad\begin{cases}q_{t_{0}}=-2q\\
r_{t_{0}}=2r\end{cases},\quad\begin{cases}q_{t_{1}}=q^{+}(1-qr)\\
r_{t_{1}}=-r^{-}(1-qr)\end{cases}.

(1.5)

Note that the flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
given by (
1.2

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=i\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{-1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\right).

We can obtain more elementary flows by replacing the r.h.s. of the

t_{-1}
-evolution of (

\lambda^{-1}
,
or by replacing the r.h.s. of the

-evolution of (
1.4

\lambda

t_{0}
-flow.

The flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy can be represented as the Hamiltonian systems with Hamiltonian operator

\mathcal{P}=\begin{pmatrix}0&qr-1\\
1-qr&0\end{pmatrix}.

The Hamiltonians of the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{-1}}
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}

H_{-1}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}q(k)r(k+1),\quad H_{0}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\log(1-q(k)r(k)),\quad H_{1}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}q(k+1)r(k).

A bihamiltonian structure is considered in
[
12
]
for the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy with the first Hamiltonian structure given above.
However, the second Hamiltonian structure given there is nonlocal. The main purpose of the present paper is to study the local multi-Hamiltonian structure of Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy by using a pair of new unknown functions

P=\frac{q}{q^{-}},\quad Q=\frac{q}{q^{-}}(1-q^{-}\,r^{-}),

(1.6)

and to show that in terms of these unknown functions this integrable hierarchy possesses a local tri-Hamiltonian structure
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\mathcal{P}_{3})
. An important property of this tri-Hamiltonian structure is that the bihamiltonian structure
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})
contained in it possesses a dispersionless limit which coincides with the bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type that is defined on the jet space of a generalized Frobenius manifold
[
6
]
. This Frobenius manifold is almost dual to the one that is associated to the Gromov-Witten invariants of local
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. We show that all the central invariants
[
10
,
15
]
of the bihamiltonian structure

equal to
\frac{1}{24}
. Together with the results of classification of infinitesimal deformations of semisimple bihamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type given in
[

15
]
, this fact on the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structure
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})
suggests that the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy should be the topological deformation of its dispersionless limit, and it provides a strong support for the validity of Brini’s conjecture on the close relation between the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy and the Gromov-Witten invariants of local
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

[
10
,
11
,
15
]

are equal to
-\frac{1}{24}

(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})

[

We organize the paper as follows. In Sec. 2 we derive the positive and negative flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy represented in terms of the unkown functions
P,Q

In Sec. 5, we compute the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structures that are given by the tri-Hamiltonian structure. In Sec. 6 and Sec. 7 we study the relation between the positive and negative flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, and establish an equivalence relation between
the bihamiltonian structures
(\mathcal{P}_{3},\mathcal{P}_{2})

(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})

The Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy

Let us first note that the spectral problem (
1.1
) can be equivalently represented as

\psi_{1}^{+}-\left(\lambda+\frac{P}{\lambda}\right)\psi_{1}+Q\psi_{1}^{-}=0,

(2.1)

here the functions
P,Q
are defined by (
1.6

Denote
\phi=\lambda^{n-1}\psi_{1}

2.1

L\phi=z\phi,\quad z=\lambda^{2},

(2.2)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=(1-Q\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\Lambda-P)

\displaystyle=\Lambda+Q-P+Q(\Lambda-Q)^{-1}(Q-P)

\displaystyle=\Lambda+Q-P+Q(Q^{-}-P^{-})\Lambda^{-1}+QQ^{-}(Q^{--}-P^{--})\Lambda^{-2}+\dots.

(2.3)

Definition 2.1
.

We call the hierarchy of differential-difference equations given by the
Lax equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}[(L^{k+1})_{+},L],\quad k\geq 0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}[(M^{k+1})_{-},L],\quad k\geq 0,

(2.5)

the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, where the operators
L
and
M

2.3
) and

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=(\Lambda-P)^{-1}(1-Q\Lambda^{-1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{P}\left(1-\Lambda\frac{1}{P}\right)^{-1}(1-Q\Lambda^{-1})

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{P}\Lambda^{-1}+\frac{Q^{+}}{PP^{+}}-\frac{1}{P}+\left(\frac{Q^{++}}{PP^{+}P^{++}}-\frac{1}{PP^{+}}\right)\Lambda+\dots.

(2.7)

We also call the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{k}}

It was shown in
[
6

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A}{\partial t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}A-A(L^{k+1})_{+}\right),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B}{\partial t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}B-B(L^{k+1})_{+}\right),

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A}{\partial s_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{M}^{k+1})_{-}A-A(M^{k+1})_{-}\right),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B}{\partial s_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{M}^{k+1})_{-}B-B(M^{k+1})_{-}\right),

(2.12)

where

\tilde{L}=AB^{-1},\quad\tilde{M}=BA^{-1}.

By using the identities

\displaystyle(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}A-A(L^{k+1})_{+}

\displaystyle=-(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{-}A+A(L^{k+1})_{-},

\displaystyle(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}B-B(L^{k+1})_{+}

\displaystyle=-(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{-}B+B(L^{k+1})_{-},

\displaystyle(\tilde{M}^{k+1})_{-}A-A(M^{k+1})_{-}

\displaystyle=-(\tilde{M}^{k+1})_{+}A+A(M^{k+1})_{+},

\displaystyle(\tilde{M}^{k+1})_{-}B-B(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{-}

P,Q

3
Hamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy

In this section, we give the Hamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy and derive a recursion relation among its flows.
This recursion relation leads to a tri-Hamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, as we will show in the next section.

Theorem 3.1
.

The flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy can be represented by the following Hamiltonian systems:

\begin{pmatrix}P_{t_{k}}\\
Q_{t_{k}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta P}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta Q}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}P_{s_{k}}\\
Q_{s_{k}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\delta G_{k}}{\delta P}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
\frac{\delta G_{k}}{\delta Q}\end{pmatrix},\quad k\geq 0,

(3.1)

where the Hamiltonian operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
is given by

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}Q\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda Q&(1-\Lambda)Q\vskip 3.44444pt\\
Q(\Lambda^{-1}-1)&0\end{pmatrix},

(3.2)

and the Hamiltonians have the expressions

H_{k}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{k}(n),\quad\textrm{with}\quad h_{k}=\frac{1}{(k+2)!}\res L^{k+2},\quad k\geq-1,

and

G_{0}=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\log P(n),\ G_{k}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{k}(n),\ \textrm{with}\ g_{k}=\frac{1}{k(k+1)!}\res M^{k},\quad k\geq 1.

Here, for an operator
K=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{j}\Lambda^{j}
we define

\res K=a_{0}
, and the variational derivatives of a functional
H=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h(n)
are defined by

\frac{\delta H}{\delta P(n)}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda^{-l}\left(\frac{\partial h(n)}{\partial P(n+l)}\right),\quad\frac{\delta H}{\delta Q(n)}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda^{-l}\left(\frac{\partial h(n)}{\partial Q(n+l)}\right).

Proof.

We first note that the operator
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is a Hamiltonian operator, since if we introduce the unknown functions

v^{1}=Q-P,\quad v^{2}=\log Q

(3.3)

and denote

J=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial u}{\partial P}&\frac{\partial u}{\partial Q}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial P}&\frac{\partial v}{\partial Q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\
0&\frac{1}{Q}\end{pmatrix},

then in terms of the new unknown functions we have the constant Hamiltonian operator

J\mathcal{P}_{1}J^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Lambda-1\vskip 3.44444pt\\
1-\Lambda^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

To derive the Hamiltonian formalism (
3.1
) of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, let us introduce the equivalence relation
\sim
on the space of
differential 1-forms

\omega=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(f_{l}\delta P^{(l)}+g_{l}\delta Q^{(l)}\right),

where
f_{l},g_{l}
are smooth functions of

P^{(k)}(n)=P(n+k),Q^{(k)}(n)=Q(n+k)

(k\in\mathbb{Z})
. We say that two 1-forms are equivalent if their difference can be represented as the action of
\Lambda-1
on another 1-form.
Then we have

\displaystyle\delta h_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial P^{(l)}}\delta P^{(l)}+\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial Q^{(l)}}\delta Q^{(l)}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta P}\delta P+\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta Q}\delta Q.

On the other hand, from the definition of
h_{k}
we have

\displaystyle\delta h_{k}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\res L^{k+1}\delta L=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\res L^{k+1}\left(B^{-1}\delta Q\Lambda^{-1}L-B^{-1}\delta P\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\res\left(\delta Q\Lambda^{-1}L^{k+2}B^{-1}-\delta PL^{k+1}B^{-1}\right).

From the above relation it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta P}=-\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right),\quad\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta Q}=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\res\left(\Lambda^{-1}L^{k+2}B^{-1}\right).

(3.4)

In order to prove that the positive flows of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy (
2.4
) has the Hamiltonian representation given in (
3.1
), we need to verify that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}A-A(L^{k+1})_{+}\right)=\left(\Lambda Q-Q\Lambda^{-1}\right)\left(\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta P}\right)+\left(\Lambda-1\right)Q\left(\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta Q}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\left((\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}B-B(L^{k+1})_{+}\right)=Q(1-\Lambda^{-1})\left(\frac{\delta H_{k}}{\delta P}\right)\Lambda^{-1}.

Remark 3.2

By using the idenities

\displaystyle(\tilde{L}^{k+1})_{+}B-B(L^{k+1})_{+}=Q\Lambda^{-1}\res\left(L^{k+1}\right)-\res\left(\tilde{L}^{k+1}\right)Q\Lambda^{-1}

and (
3.4

\displaystyle\res\left(\Lambda L^{k+1}\right)-\res\left(\tilde{L}^{k+1}\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle-(\Lambda Q-Q\Lambda^{-1})\left(\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right)\right)+(\Lambda-1)Q\left(\res\left(\Lambda^{-1}L^{k+2}B^{-1}\right)\right),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda\left(\res\left(\tilde{L}^{k+1}\right)\right)-\res\left(L^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(\Lambda-1)\left(\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right)\right).

The identities (
3.5
), (

Proof.

By using the definition (

\displaystyle\left[\res\left(\Lambda L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right)-\Lambda Q\left(\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\left[\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\Lambda\right)-Q\Lambda^{-1}\left(\res\left(L^{k+1}B^{-1}\right)\right)\right]

(3.7)

On the other hand, by using the identity (

(3.8)

Then the identity (

Let
\hat{U}=U\times\mathbb{R}^{0|m}

5
Central invariants of the associated bihamiltonian structures

In order to understand properties of the bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies associated to Gromov-Witten invariants and 2d topological field theory,
Dubrovin and the fourth-named author of the present paper proposed a program of classification of deformations bihamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type in
[
9
]
. Together with the second-named author of the present paper, they introduced in
[
10

15
]
the notion of central invariants for any deformation of a given semisimple bihamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type, and proved that two deformations are equivalent under a certain Miura-type transformation if and only if they have the same set of central invariants. In particular, all the central invariants of the deformed bihamiltonian structure of the integrable hierarchy associated to the Gromov-Witten invariants of a target space must equal to a certain constant.

In this section, we compute the central invariants of the bihamiltonian structures

(\mathcal{P}_{i},\mathcal{P}_{j})

(1\leq i\neq j\leq 3)

This result together with properties of the leading terms of these two bihamiltonian structures implies that they are related by a certain Miura-type transformation, and it also provides a support for the validity of Brini’s conjecture on the relation of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy and the Gromov-Witten invariants of local

have the expressions

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{J}^{*}=-\left.\mathcal{P}_{3}\right|_{P\to\hat{P},\,Q\to\hat{Q},\,\Lambda^{\pm}\to\Lambda^{\mp}},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{P}_{3}\mathcal{J}^{*}=-\left.\mathcal{P}_{1}\right|_{P\to\hat{P},\,Q\to\hat{Q},\,\Lambda^{\pm}\to\Lambda^{\mp}}.

Thus the bihamiltonian structure
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2})
is equivalent to
(-{\mathcal{P}}_{3},{\mathcal{P}}_{2})
under the transformation (
6.4
) and the transformation
\varepsilon\to-\varepsilon
.

8
Conclusion

We give in this paper a tri-Hamiltonian structure of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, which yields in particular a local bihamiltonian structure with the dispersionless limit given in
[
6
]
. This dispersionless limit is a bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type defined on the jet space of the generalized 2-dimensional Frobenius manifold given by the potential

F=\frac{1}{2}(v^{1})^{2}v^{2}+v^{1}e^{v^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(v^{1})^{2}\log v^{1}

and the flat metric

\eta=(\eta_{\alpha\beta})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

The above Frobenius manifold has a non-constant unity vector field

e=\frac{v^{1}}{v^{1}-e^{v^{2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{1}}-\frac{1}{v^{1}-e^{v^{2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{2}},

and the Euler vector field

E=v^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{2}}.

Its intersection form is given by

g=(g^{\alpha\beta})=\left(E^{\gamma}\eta^{\alpha\xi}\eta^{\beta\zeta}\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial v^{\gamma}\partial v^{\xi}\partial v^{\zeta}}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}2v^{1}e^{v^{2}}&v^{1}+e^{v^{2}}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
v^{1}+e^{v^{2}}&2\end{pmatrix}.

The flat metric
\eta
and the intersection form
g
induce a bihamiltonian structure of hydrodynamic type
(\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{2})

with the Hamiltonian operators defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{x}\\
\partial_{x}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}2v^{1}e^{v^{2}}\partial_{x}+(v^{1}e^{v^{2}})^{\prime}&(v^{1}+e^{v^{2}})\partial_{x}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
(v^{1}+e^{v^{2}})\partial_{x}+(v^{1}+e^{v^{2}})^{\prime}&2\partial_{x}\end{pmatrix}.

It coincides with the dispersionless limit
(\mathcal{P}_{1;0},\mathcal{P}_{2;0})
of the bihamiltonian structure
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})
of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy given in (
5.2
) under the following change of the unkown functions (
3.3
), i.e.

v^{1}=u^{2}-u^{1},\quad v^{2}=\log u^{2}.

Under this change of the unkown functions, the dispersionless limits of the positive flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}

(k\geq 0)
of the Ablowitz-Ladik hiearrchy also coincide with the following flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{2,k}}
of the Principal Hierarchy of the Frobenius manifold
[
6
]
:

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{2,k}}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta_{2,k+1}}{\partial v^{\beta}\partial v^{\gamma}}v^{\gamma}_{x},\quad k\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle\theta_{2,0}=v^{1},\quad\theta_{2,1}=v^{1}e^{v^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(v^{1})^{2},\quad\theta_{2,2}=\frac{1}{2}v^{1}e^{2v^{2}}+(v^{1})^{2}e^{v^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}(v^{1})^{3},

and in general, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{2,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(e^{v^{2}}-v^{1})^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-k-1,k+1;1;\frac{e^{v^{2}}}{e^{v^{2}}-v^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\sum_{s=0}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{s}\binom{k+s}{s}e^{sv^{2}}(v^{1}-e^{v^{2}})^{k+1-s}.

Define the recursion operator

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}v^{1}+e^{v^{2}}&2v^{1}e^{v^{2}}+(v^{1}e^{v^{2}})^{\prime}\partial_{x}^{-1}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
2&v^{1}+e^{v^{2}}+(v^{1}+e^{v^{2}})^{\prime}\partial_{x}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

then these flows satisfy the recursion relation

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{2,k}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{2,k}}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{k+1}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{2,k-1}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{2,k-1}}\end{pmatrix},\quad k\geq 1.

The dispersionless limit of the negative flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{k}}

(s\geq 0)

can be represented in the form

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{k}}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}h_{k}}{\partial v^{\beta}\partial v^{\gamma}}v^{\gamma}_{x},\quad k\geq 0,

where

h_{0}=v^{2}-\log(v^{1}-e^{v^{2}}),\quad h_{k}=\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\frac{\theta_{2,k-1}}{(v^{1}-e^{v^{2}})^{2k}}.

These flows satisfy the recursion relation

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial s_{0}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial s_{0}}\end{pmatrix}=0,\quad\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial s_{k-1}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial s_{k-1}}\end{pmatrix}=(k+1)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial s_{k}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial s_{k}}\end{pmatrix},\quad k\geq 1.

We note that the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{k}}

(s\geq 0)
do not belong to the Principal Hierarchy of the Frobenius manifold, which consists of the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{2,k}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,k}}

(k\geq 0)
. As it was shown in
[
6
]
,
the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,k}}
are given by the equations

\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{1,0}}=v^{1}_{x}+e^{v^{2}}v^{2}_{x},\quad\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{1,0}}=\frac{v^{1}_{x}}{v^{1}}+v^{2}_{x}

and the following recursion relation:

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{1,k}}\vskip 3.44444pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{1,k}}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{k}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{1,k-1}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{1,k-1}}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{2}{k}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial t^{2,k-1}}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\frac{\partial v^{2}}{\partial t^{2,k-1}}\end{pmatrix},\quad k\geq 1.

We will consider in subsequent publications a certain extension of the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy such that its dispersionless limit contains the whole Principal Hierarchy of the above-mentioned Frobenius manifold, and study Brini’s conjecture on its relation with the Gromov-Witten invariants of local
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

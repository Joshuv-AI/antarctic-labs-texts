Sharp volume and multiplicity bounds for Fano simplices
Andreas Bäuerle

Abstract
. We present sharp upper bounds on the volume, Mahler volume and multiplicity for Fano simplices depending on the dimension and Gorenstein index. These bounds rely on the interplay between lattice simplices and unit fraction partitions. Moreover, we present an efficient procedure for explicitly classifying Fano simplicies of any dimension and Gorenstein index and we carry out the classification up to dimension four for various Gorenstein indices.

1.
Introduction

Fano simplices
are lattice simplices
\Delta
with primitive vertices containing the origin in their interior. Their geometric counterparts are
fake weighted projective spaces

Z
, ie.
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric Fano varieties of Picard number one, where Fano means normal and projective with ample anticanonical class
-\mathcal{K}
. The
Gorenstein index
of
Z
is the smallest positive integer
g
such that
-g\mathcal{K}
is Cartier. For the corresponding Fano simplex
\Delta
, the Gorenstein index equals the smallest positive integer
g
such that the
g
-fold of the dual polytope
\Delta^{*}
is a lattice simplex.

Our first result provides sharp volume bounds. The
normalized volume
of a
d
-dimensional polytope is the
d!
-fold of its euclidean volume.
For simplices of Gorenstein index one (also called
reflexive
simplices), Nill
[
19
]
*Thm. A provides sharp upper bounds on the normalized volume in terms of the dimension of the simplex. We extend these results to Fano simplices of arbitrary Gorenstein index.
We write
\Delta=\Delta(P)
with the
d\times(d+1)
matrix
P
having the vertices of
\Delta
as its columns. For any integer
g\geq 1
we define the
g
-Sylvester sequence

s_{g,1},s_{g,2},\dots
and the
truncated
g
-Sylvester sequence

t_{g,1},t_{g,2},\dots
as

s_{g,1}\ :=\ g+1,\qquad s_{g,k+1}\ :=\ s_{g,k}(s_{g,k}-1)+1,\qquad t_{g,k}\ :=\ s_{g,k}-1.

Theorem 1.1
.

There are sharp upper bounds on the normalized volume of a Fano simplex
\Delta
, only depending on its dimension
d
and its Gorenstein index
g
:

(i)

Assume
(d,g)=(2,1)
. We have the following upper bound on the normalized volume of
\Delta
, which is attained if and only if
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds:

\quad\qquad{\rm Vol}(\Delta)\ \leq\ 9,\qquad\qquad P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&-2\\
0&3&-3\end{array}\right].

(ii)

In all other cases the normalized volume of
\Delta
is bounded from above by

{\rm Vol}(\Delta)\ \leq\ \frac{2t_{g,d}^{2}}{g^{2}}.

Equality holds if and only if we have
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
, where
P
is one of the following:

P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{rrrr}1&1&1&-5\\
0&2&2&-4\\
0&0&6&-6\end{array}\right],

P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{rrrrrr}1&0&\dots&0&\frac{(s_{g,1}-g)}{s_{g,1}}\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}&-\frac{(s_{g,1}+g)}{s_{g,1}}\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&1&\frac{(s_{g,d-1}-g)}{s_{g,d-1}}\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}&-\frac{(s_{g,1}+g)}{s_{g,1}}\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}\\
0&\dots&\dots&0&\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}&-\frac{t_{g,d}}{g}\\
\end{array}\right].

Another invariant of a Fano simplex
\Delta
is its
multiplicity
, ie. the order of the sublattice generated by the vertices of
\Delta
. For Fano simplices having only the origin as an interior lattice point, for instance reflexive ones,
[
2
]
*Thm. 1.1 provides sharp upper bounds on the multiplicity in terms of the dimension.
In our second result we provide multiplicity bounds for arbitrary Fano simplices in terms of the Gorenstein index and the dimension.

Theorem 1.2
.

There are upper bounds on the multiplicity of any Fano simplex
\Delta
, only depending on its dimension
d
and its Gorenstein index
g
:

(i)

Assume
d=3
and
g\in\{1,2\}
. We have the following upper bound on the multiplicity of
\Delta
, which is attained if and only if
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds:

\quad\qquad{\rm mult}(\Delta)\ \leq\ 16g^{2},\qquad\qquad P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&4g-3&4g-3&5-8g\\
0&4g&0&-4g\\
0&0&4g&-4g\end{array}\right].

(ii)

Assume
(d,g)=(4,1)
. We have the following upper bound on the multiplicity of
\Delta
, which is attained if and only if attained if
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds:

\quad\qquad{\rm mult}(\Delta)\ \leq\ 128,\qquad\qquad P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&1&1&1&-7\\
0&2&2&2&-6\\
0&0&8&0&-8\\
0&0&0&8&-8\end{array}\right].

(iii)

In all other cases the multiplicity of
\Delta
is bounded from above by

{\rm mult}(\Delta)\ \leq\ \frac{3t_{g,d-1}^{2}}{g}.

If equality holds, then we either have
(d,g)=(3,3)
and
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds, where

P\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&5&-7\\
0&12&0&-12\\
0&0&12&-12\end{array}\right],

or there are positive integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds, where
P
is the matrix:

\quad\qquad\left[\begin{array}[]{rrrrrrr}1&0&\dots&0&\frac{(s_{g,1}-g)}{s_{g,1}}\frac{t_{g,d-1}}{g}&a_{1}&-\left(\frac{(s_{g,1}+2g)}{s_{g,1}}\frac{t_{g,d-1}}{g}+a_{1}\right)\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&1&\frac{(s_{g,d-2}-g)}{s_{g,d-2}}\frac{t_{g,d-1}}{g}&a_{d-2}&-\left(\frac{(s_{g,d-2}+2g)}{s_{g,d-2}}\frac{t_{g,d-1}}{g}+a_{d-2}\right)\\

0&\dots&\dots&0&0&3t_{g,d-1}&-3t_{g,d-1}\\
\end{array}\right]

Moreover, if
g
is odd, then for

we may choose

2.
Simplices, weight systems and unit fractions

We associate with every IP simplex a unit fraction partition of its Gorenstein index, see Proposition
2.9
.The main result of this section is Proposition
2.10
, which relates the volume and the factor of an IP simplex to its unit fraction partition. Throughout,
N
is a rank
d
lattice for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
. The dual lattice is denoted by
M={\rm Hom}(N,{\mathbb{Z}})
with pairing
{\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle}\colon M\times N\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
. We write
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the corresponding rational vector spaces. We assume that polytopes are always full dimensional, ie.
{\rm lin}(\Delta)=N_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The
normalized volume
of a
d
-dimensional polytope
\Delta
is
{\rm Vol}(\Delta)=d!\,{\rm vol}(\Delta)
, where
{\rm vol}(\Delta)
denotes its euclidean volume. By an
IP polytope
we mean a polytope
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
that contains the origin
\mathbf{0}\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}
in its interior. The
dual
of an IP polytope
\Delta
is the polytope

\Delta^{*}\ :=\ \{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}};\,{\langle u,v\rangle}\geq-1\text{ for all }v\in\Delta\}\ \subseteq\ M_{\mathbb{Q}}.

For a facet
F
of
\Delta
we denote by
u_{F}\in M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
the unique linear form with
{\langle u_{F},v\rangle}=-1
for all
v\in F
. We have

\Delta^{*}\ =\ {\rm conv}(\,u_{F};\,F\text{ facet of }\Delta\,),\qquad\Delta\ =\ \{v\in N_{\mathbb{Q}};\,{\langle u_{F},v\rangle}\geq-1,\,F\text{ facet of }\Delta\}.

A
lattice polytope
is a polytope
\Delta\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}
whose vertices lie in the lattice
N
. A
Fano
polytope is an IP lattice polytope whose vertices are primitive lattice points. We regard two IP polytopes
\Delta\subseteq N_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq N^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
as isomorphic if there is a lattice isomorphism
\varphi\colon N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
mapping
\Delta
bijectively to
\Delta^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.1
.

See
[
9
,
19
]
.
A
weight system
Q
of length
d
is a
(d+1)
-tuple of positive rational numbers
Q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{d})
. The
total weight
of a weight system
Q
is the rational number
|Q|:=q_{0}+\dots+q_{d}
. A weight system
Q
is called
reduced
if it consists of integers and
\gcd(q_{0},\dots,q_{d})=1
holds. A reduced weight system is called
well-formed
if
\gcd(q_{j};\,j=0,\dots,d,\ j\neq i)=1
holds for all
i=0,\dots,d
. Any weight system
Q
can be written as
\lambda(Q)\cdot Q^{\rm red}
with a unique reduced weight system
Q^{\rm red}
and a unique positive rational number
\lambda(Q)
. We call
\lambda(Q)
the
factor
of
Q
and
Q^{\rm red}
the
reduction
of
Q
.

Definition 2.2
.

See
[
9
,
19
]
.
To any IP simplex
\Delta
with vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{d}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
we associate a weight system by

Q_{\Delta}\ :=\ (q_{0},\dots,q_{d}),\qquad q_{i}\ :=\ |\det(\,v_{j};\ j=0,\dots,d,\ j\neq i\,)|.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
a
d
-dimensional IP simplex with vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{d}
and weight system
Q_{\Delta}=(q_{0},\dots,q_{d})
.

(i)

For the total weight we have
|Q_{\Delta}|={\rm Vol}(\Delta)
.

(ii)

If
\Delta
is a Fano simplex, then
Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}
is well-formed.

(iii)

We have
\sum_{i=0}^{d}q_{i}v_{i}=0
and
Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}=(q^{\prime}_{0}.\dots,q^{\prime}_{d})
is the unique reduced weight system satisfying
\sum_{i=0}^{d}q^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}=0
.

(iv)

For any
H\in{\rm GL}(d,N_{\mathbb{Q}})
we have
Q_{H\Delta}=|\det(H)|\,Q_{\Delta}
. In particular, the weight systems of isomorphic IP simplices coincide up to order.

For an IP lattice simplex
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
we denote by
N(\Delta)\subseteq N
the sublattice generated by the vertices of
\Delta
. If
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
is any IP simplex and
\Delta^{\prime}:=g_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)\,\Delta
, then we have

\lambda(\Delta)\ :=\ \lambda(Q_{\Delta})\ =\ \frac{[N:N(\Delta^{\prime})]}{g_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)^{d}}.

In case
\Delta
is a Fano simplex, we write
{\rm mult}(\Delta):=\lambda(\Delta)
and call it the
multiplicity
of
\Delta
. It coincides with the cardinality of the torsion part of the class group
{\rm Cl}(Z)
of the associated fake weighted projective space
Z=Z(\Delta)
. The following Proposition is a reformulation of
[

]

Proposition 2.4
.

To any reduced weight system
Q
of length
d
there exists a
d
-dimensional IP lattice simplex
\Delta(Q)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, unique up to an isomorphism, with

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
an IP simplex.

(i)

The

g_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)\ :=\ \min\{\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1};\ k\Delta\text{ is a lattice simplex}\,\}.

(ii)

The

g(\Delta)\ :=\ g_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)\cdot g_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta^{*}).

(iii)

Assume
\Delta

Remark 2.7
.

If
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}

Definition 2.8
.

Let
g\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. A tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 1}
is called a
unit fraction partition
(ufp for short)
of
g
of length
n
if the following holds:

\frac{1}{g}\ =\ \sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{k}}.

A tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 1}
is called a
unit fraction partition
if it is a ufp of
g
for some
g\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. For a unit fraction partition
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of
g
we call

t_{A}\ :=\ {\rm lcm}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\qquad\lambda(A)\ :=\ \gcd(g,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\qquad A^{{\rm red}}\ :=\ A/\lambda(A)

the
total weight
, the
factor
and the
reduction
of
A
, respectively. A unit fraction partition
A
is called
reduced
if it coincides with its reduction. It is called
well-formed
if
\alpha_{i}\mid{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{j}\,;\,j\neq i)
holds for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
a
d
-dimensional IP simplex of Gorenstein index
g(\Delta)=g
with associated unit fraction partition
A(\Delta)=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})
of

A(\Delta)=A(\Delta^{*})
holds and we have:

(i)

{\rm Vol}(\Delta){\rm Vol}(\Delta^{*})\ =\ \frac{1}{g^{d+1}}\,\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{d},

(ii)

\lambda(\Delta^{*}){\rm Vol}(\Delta)\ =\ \lambda(\Delta){\rm Vol}(\Delta^{*})\ =\ \frac{1}{g^{d}}\frac{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{d}}{{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})},

(iii)

\lambda(\Delta)\lambda(\Delta^{*})\ =\ \frac{1}{g^{d-1}}\frac{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{d}}{{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})^{2}}.

Note that the left hand side of equations (i)-(iii) in Proposition

Proof of Proposition
2.10
.

By Lemma
2.12
the weight systems
Q_{\Delta}
and
Q_{\Delta^{*}}
differ only by a factor. Moreover, the simplices
\Delta

\Delta^{*}
have the same Gorenstein index. Thus the associated unit fraction partitions

and

coincide. Item (i) is an immediate consequence of (ii) and (iii). We prove (ii). Remark

{\rm Vol}(\Delta^{*})\ =\ |Q_{\Delta^{*}}|\ =\ \frac{|Q_{\Delta}|^{d}}{q_{0}\cdots q_{d}}.

We multiply this by the multiplicity
\lambda(\Delta)
and use the identity
\lambda(\Delta)=g|Q_{\Delta}|/t_{A(\Delta)}
to obtain

\lambda(\Delta){\rm Vol}(\Delta^{*})\ =\ \frac{g|Q_{\Delta}|}{t_{A(\Delta)}}\,\frac{|Q_{\Delta}|^{d}}{q_{0}\cdots q_{d}}\ =\ \frac{1}{g^{d}}\frac{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{d}}{{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})}.

Switching the roles of
\Delta
and
\Delta^{*}
and using the fact that they have the same unit fraction partition, we obtain
\lambda(\Delta){\rm Vol}(\Delta^{*})=\lambda(\Delta^{*}){\rm Vol}(\Delta)
. We prove (iii). Let
Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}=(q^{\prime}_{0},\dots,q^{\prime}_{d})
. With Lemma
2.12
we obtain:

\lambda(\Delta^{*})\ =\ \lambda(\Delta)m_{Q_{\Delta}}\ =\ \lambda(\Delta)\frac{|Q_{\Delta}|^{d-1}}{q_{0}\cdots q_{d}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\lambda(\Delta)}\frac{|Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}|^{d-1}}{q^{\prime}_{0}\cdots q^{\prime}_{d}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\lambda(\Delta)}m_{Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}},

Multiplying both sides by
\lambda(\Delta)
yields the identity
\lambda(\Delta)\lambda(\Delta^{*})=m_{Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}}
. We obtain:

m_{Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}}\ =\ \frac{1}{|Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}|^{2}}\frac{|Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}|^{d+1}}{q^{\prime}_{0}\cdots q^{\prime}_{d}}\ =\ \frac{\lambda(\Delta)^{2}}{|Q_{\Delta}|^{2}}\frac{|Q_{\Delta}|^{d+1}}{q_{0}\cdots q_{d}}\ =\ \frac{1}{g^{d-1}}\frac{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{d}}{{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})^{2}}.

∎

It will be convenient to assign unit fraction partitions directly to weight systems and vice versa.

Definition 2.13
.

The
index of a weight system

Q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{d})
is the positive integer

g(Q)\ :=\ \min\left(\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1};\ k|Q|/q_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ for all }i=0,\dots,d\,\right).

The index

3.
Sharp bounds on unit fraction partitions

For a unit fraction partition
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of length
n
we consider

F_{k}(A)\ :=\ \frac{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}{{\rm lcm}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})^{n-k}}.

This is the expression that shows up in Proposition
2.10
for
k=n-2,n-1
and
n
. We give sharp bounds on these expressions among all unit fraction partitions of
g
and completely describe the unit fraction partitions attaining those bounds.

Definition 3.1
.

The
g
-Sylvester sequence

S_{g}=(s_{g,1},s_{g,2},\dots)
and the
truncated
g
-Sylvester sequence

T_{g}=(t_{g,1},t_{g,2},\dots)
for a positive integer
g
are given by

s_{g,1}\ :=\ g+1,\qquad s_{g,k+1}\ :=\ s_{g,k}(s_{g,k}-1)+1,\qquad t_{g,k}:=s_{g,k}-1.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
g\geq 1
and let
n\geq 3
. Let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
a unit fraction partition of
g
with
\alpha_{1}\leq\dots\leq\alpha_{n}
. For the value of
F_{k}
on
A
the following hold:

(i)

F_{n}(A)\leq t_{g,n}^{2}/g
and equality holds if and only if
A=(s_{g,1},\dots,s_{g,n-1},t_{g,n})
.

(ii)

Assume
k=n-1
. If
(n,g)=(3,1)
, then we have
F_{n-1}(A)\leq 9
and equality holds if and only if
A=(3,3,3)
. In all other cases we have

F_{n-1}(A)\ \leq\ \frac{2\,t_{g,n-1}^{2}}{g}.

Equality holds if and only if
A
is one of the following unit fraction partitions:

\qquad\qquad(6,6,6),\qquad(2,6,6,6),\qquad(s_{g,1},\dots,s_{g,n-2},2t_{g,n-1},2t_{g,n-1}).

(iii)

Assume
k=n-2
. If
n=4
and
g\in\{1,2\}
, then we have
F_{n-2}(A)\ \leq\ 16g^{2}
and equality holds if and only if
A=(4g,4g,4g,4g)
. If
(n,g)=(5,1)
, then we have
F_{n-2}(A)\ \leq\ 128
and equality holds if and only if
A=(2,8,8,8,8)
.
In all other cases we have

F_{n-2}(A)\ \leq\ \frac{3\,t_{g,n-2}^{2}}{g}.

Equality holds if and only if
A
is one of the following unit fraction partitions:

\qquad\qquad(12,12,12,12),\qquad(s_{g,1},\dots,s_{g,n-3},3t_{g,n-2},3t_{g,n-2},3t_{g,n-2}).

Remark 3.3
.

In the literature the sequence
S_{1}=(s_{1,1},s_{1,2},\dots)
is known as
Sylvester’s sequence
, see for instance
[
1
]
. Our naming for the sequences
S_{g}
and
T_{g}
is derived from that. We list some properties of the sequences
S_{g}
and
T_{g}
that we will use frequently.

(i)

For any
n\geq 1
we have

\frac{1}{g}\ =\ \frac{1}{s_{g,1}}+\dots+\frac{1}{s_{g,n-1}}+\frac{1}{t_{g,n}}.

(ii)

For any
n\geq 1
we have

\frac{g}{t_{g,n}}\ =\ \frac{1}{s_{g,1}}\cdots\frac{1}{s_{g,n-1}}.

(iii)

For any
g,n\geq 1
we have
s_{g,n+1}>s_{g,n}
and
s_{g+1,n}>s_{g,n}
.

(iv)

For
i\neq j
we have
\gcd(s_{g,i},s_{g,j})=1
.

The strategy for the proof of Theorem
3.2
is as follows: For given
g
and
n
we define a certain compact subset
A_{g}^{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which has the property that for any unit fraction partition
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of
g
with
\alpha_{1}\leq\dots\leq\alpha_{n}
, the point
(1/\alpha_{1},\dots,1/\alpha_{n})
is contained in
A_{g}^{n}
. For
k\in\{n-2,n-1,n\}
we minimize the function
f_{k}(x):=x_{1}\cdots x_{k}
on
A_{g}^{n}
and show that it attains its minimum precisely at the points corresponding to the unit fraction partitions listed in Theorem
3.2
. This strategy for minimizing functions on unit fraction partitions was first used by Izhboldin and Kurliandchik in
[
14
]
, see also
[
3
,
19
]
for generalizations. In
[
3
]
the authors call this type of optimization problems
Izhboldin-Kurliandchik problems
. In the following we adopt their naming convention.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
g,n\geq 1
. We denote by
A_{g}^{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the compact set of all points
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that satisfy the following conditions:

(A1)

x_{1}\geq\dots\geq x_{n}\geq 0
.

(A2)

x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=1/g
.

(A3)

x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\leq g(x_{k+1}+\dots+x_{n})
for all
k=1,\dots,n-1
.

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we denote by
{\rm SUM}(g)
the equality
x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=1/g
, by
{\rm ORD}(k)
we denote the inequality
x_{k}\geq x_{k+1}
and by
{\rm PS}(g,k)
the inequality
x_{1}\cdots x_{k}~\leq~g(x_{k+1}~+~\dots~+~x_{n})
. Thus the set
A_{g}^{n}
consists of the points
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
that satisfy the equality
{\rm SUM}(g)
and the inequalities
{\rm ORD}(k)
and
{\rm PS}(g,k)
for all
k=1,\dots,n-1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

See
[
6
]
*Lemma 4.3. Let
g\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
. For any unit fraction partition
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of
g
with
\alpha_{1}\leq\dots\leq\alpha_{n}
the point
(1/\alpha_{1},\dots,1/\alpha_{n})
is contained in

.

In the following Proposition we gather important properties of the set

. It is a generalization of
[
3
]
*Lemma 4.1.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
g\geq 1
and
n\geq 3
. For any point
x\in A_{g}^{n}
the following hold:

(i)

We have
1/g>x_{1}\geq x_{n}>0
.

(ii)

The inequalities
{\rm ORD}(k)
and
{\rm PS}(g,k)
cannot simultaneously be fulfilled with equality.

(iii)

If for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
the inequality
{\rm PS}(g,i)
is fulfilled with equality for all
i\leq k
, then
x_{i}=1/s_{g,i}
holds for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

We prove (i). Since the
x_{i}
are all non-negative, the equality
{\rm SUM}(g)
implies that
x_{1}\leq 1/g
holds. Assume
x_{n}=0
. Then by the inequality
{\rm PS}(g,n-1)
we have
x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1}=0
. Thus
x_{i}=0
holds for some
i=1,\dots,n-1
. The inequality
{\rm ORD}(j)
thus implies that
x_{j}=0
holds for all
j=i,\dots,n
. We can repeat this argument to obtain
x_{1}=\dots=x_{n}=0
, which contradicts the equality
{\rm SUM}(g)
. Thus
x_{n}>0
holds. We prove (ii). Assume that for some
k
the inequalities
{\rm ORD}(k)
and
{\rm PS}(g,k)
hold simultaneously with equality. Using
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
, we may then write

0\ =\ g(x_{k+1}+\dots+x_{n})-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\ =\ g(x_{k+2}+\dots+x_{n})+x_{k}(g-x_{1}\cdots x_{k-1}).

The first summand on the right hand side is non-negative, the second summand is positive. This means that they cannot add to zero, a contradiction. Thus
{\rm ORD}(k)
and
{\rm PS}(g,k)
cannot simultaneously be fulfilled with equality. We prove (iii) by induction on
i
. In case
i=1
, if
{\rm PS}(g,1)
holds with equality, we get

x_{1}\ =\ g(x_{2}+\dots+x_{n})\ =\ g\left(\frac{1}{g}-x_{1}\right).

Solving this equation for
x_{1}
yields
x_{1}=1/s_{g,1}
. Now assume
i>1
and
x_{j}=1/s_{g,j}
holds for all
j\leq i
. If
{\rm PS}(g,i)
holds with equality, we obtain

\frac{g}{t_{g,i}}x_{i}\ =\ x_{1}\cdots x_{i}\ =\ g(x_{i+1}+\dots+x_{n})\ =\ g\left(\frac{1}{g}-x_{1}-\dots-x_{i}\right)\ =\ g\left(\frac{1}{t_{g,i}}-x_{i}\right).

Solving this equation for
x_{i}
yields
x_{i}=1/s_{g,i}
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

Let

,

and

Lemma 3.9

Let

and

attains its minimum at

(ii)

(iii)

where

. We show that
y^{\prime}
lies in

Proof of Proposition

.

Let
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in A_{g}^{n}

b_{k}w_{d}=a_{kk}w_{k-1}+\dots+a_{kd}w_{d-1}

Let
\Delta:=\Delta(P)
the convex hull of the columns of

. Then
\Delta
is a
d
-dimensional IP lattice simplex whose associated weight system satisfies

-th Gorenstein form

(5.1.1)

u_{kj}\ =\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|w|-w_{k}}{a_{jj}w_{k}}-\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}a_{lj}u_{kl}}{a_{jj}},&\text{ if }j=k+1,\\

with local Gorenstein indices

Proof.

As
P
is of rank

, the polytope
\Delta
is full-dimensional. Its vertices are precisely the columns

. It is thus a lattice simplex. By condition (iii) we have
P\cdot w=0
. With
\beta_{k}:=w_{k}/|w|
we can write

\mathbf{0}\ =\ \beta_{0}v_{0}+\dots+\beta_{d}v_{d},

which is a convex combination of
v_{0},\dots,v_{d}
with non-vanishing coefficients. Thus the origin is contained in the interior of
\Delta
, making it an IP lattice simplex. By Remark
2.3
(ii) we have
Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}=w
. Let
u_{k}=(u_{k1},\dots,u_{kd})
the
k
-th Gorenstein form of
\Delta
. Let
1\leq j\leq d
. If
j\neq k+1
, then
{\langle u_{k},v_{j}\rangle}=-1
holds and for
j=k+1
we have
{\langle u_{k},v_{j}\rangle}=|w|/w_{k}-1
. Solving these equations for

produces the identities in Equation

. The last assertion is just the definition of the Gorenstein index and the local Gorenstein indices of
\Delta

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Delta
a
d
-dimensional IP lattice simplex of Gorenstein index
g
. Then
\Delta\cong\Delta(P)
holds with a matrix
P
as provided by Proposition

.

Proof.

Write
w:=Q_{\Delta}^{\rm red}=(w_{0},\dots,w_{d})
and let
A=A(\Delta)=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d})
the unit fraction partition of
g
associated with
\Delta
. By Proposition
2.15
the reduced weight systems
w
and
Q(A(\Delta))
coincide. Let
P
the
d\times(d+1)
integer matrix whose columns are the vertices of
\Delta
. By bringing
P
in Hermite normal form, we may assume

P\ =\ [\,v_{0}\ \dots\ v_{d}\,]\ =\ \left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1d}&-b_{1}\\
0&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2d}&-b_{2}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&a_{dd}&-b_{d}\\
\end{array}\right],

where
a_{kk}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
holds for all
k=1,\dots,d
as well as
0\leq a_{ik}<a_{kk}
for all
1\leq i<k
. Resolving
P\cdot w=0
for the entries

, we obtain the identity

b_{k}w_{d}\ =\ a_{kk}w_{k-1}+\dots+a_{kd}w_{d-1}

It thus remains to show that for all
k
the diagonal entry
a_{kk}
divides
\alpha_{k-1}
. Consider the following sequence of rational numbers

q_{1}\ :=\ -\frac{1}{a_{11}},\qquad q_{j}\ :=\ -\frac{1+a_{1j}q_{1}+\dots+a_{j-1,j}q_{j-1}}{a_{jj}}.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
u_{k}
the
k
-th Gorenstein form of
\Delta
. For each

{\langle u_{k},v_{j}\rangle}=-1
. Solving this for

we get
u_{kj}=q_{j}
. In particular

is an integer. Evaluating

on

, we obtain

a_{1k}q_{1}+\dots+a_{(k-1)k}q_{k-1}+a_{kk}u_{(k-1)k}\ =\ {\langle u_{k-1},v_{k}\rangle}\ =\ \frac{|w|}{w_{k-1}}-1.

With the definition of

, we can rewrite this equation as

a_{kk}(u_{(k-1)k}-q_{k})\ =\ -\frac{|w|}{w_{k-1}}.

Note that the

-fold of both

and
q_{k}
is an integer. Multiplying both sides by

thus shows that
a_{kk}w_{k-1}
is a divisor of
g|w|=\alpha_{k-1}w_{k-1}
. Clearing

\alpha_{k-1}

∎

Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
provide us with a procedure to enumerate up to isomorphy all IP lattice simplices
\Delta
with a given constellation of local Gorenstein indices
(g_{0},\dots,g_{d})
and given reduced weight system
w
. The list produced may contain redundancies, ie. matrices
P
and
P^{\prime}
that give isomorphic simplices
\Delta(P)
and
\Delta(P^{\prime})
. In practice, we want the list to be redundancy free without having to check each pair of matrices for isomorphy. The solution to this problem is to define a normal form
{\rm NF}(P)
for these matrices
P
, which has the property that two matrices
P
and
P^{\prime}
give isomorphic simplices if and only if their normal forms coincide. The normal form we present in Definition
5.3
is similar to the PALP normal form for lattice polytopes described in
[
17
]
, see also
[
11
]
. To fix some notation, if
B
is a
m\times n
integer matrix with columns
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
, then we denote by
B_{\sigma}
the matrix with columns
b_{\sigma(1)},\dots,b_{\sigma(n)}
. Moreover, by
{\rm HNF}(B)
we denote the hermite normal form of
B
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
P
a
d\times(d+1)
integer matrix whose columns generate
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
as a convex cone. Let
w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{d})
the reduced weight system and
(g_{0},\dots,g_{d})
the local Gorenstein indices of the IP lattice simplex
\Delta(P)
. We denote by
S_{P}
the subset of
S_{d+1}
consisting of all permutations
\sigma\in S_{d+1}
with the following properties:

(i)

If
\sigma(i)\leq\sigma(j)
holds, then
w_{\sigma(i)}\geq w_{\sigma(j)}
.

(ii)

If
\sigma(i)\leq\sigma(j)
and
w_{\sigma(i)}=w_{\sigma(j)}
holds, then
g_{\sigma(i)}\geq g_{\sigma(j)}
.

We define the
normal form
of
P
as

{\rm NF}(P)\ :=\ \min\{{\rm HNF}(P_{\sigma});\ \sigma\in S_{P}\},

where the minimum is taken lexicographically, ie. we write the entries of the matrix
{\rm HNF}(P_{\sigma})=(h_{ij})_{ij}
as a list of integers
(h_{11},\dots,h_{1d},h_{21},\dots,h_{(d+1)d})
and take the lexicographic minimum among those lists.

Proposition 5.4
.

For
d\times(d+1)
integer matrices
P
and
P^{\prime}
, whose columns generate
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
as a convex cone, we have
\Delta(P)\cong\Delta(P^{\prime})
if and only if their normal forms
{\rm NF}(P)
and
{\rm NF}(P^{\prime})
coincide.

Proof.

Assume
\Delta(P)\cong\Delta(P^{\prime})
holds. Then there is a permutation
\sigma\in S_{d+1}
and a
d\times d
unimodular matrix
S
such that
S\cdot P_{\sigma}=P^{\prime}
holds. A quick comparison shows
S_{P}=\sigma S_{P^{\prime}}
. Thus the sets of hermite normal forms, among which the lexicographic minimum is chosen, coincide. We obtain
{\rm NF}(P)={\rm NF}(P^{\prime})
. On the other hand, if
{\rm NF}(P)={\rm NF}(P^{\prime})
holds, then there are
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{d+1}
and unimodular
d\times d
matrices
S
and
S^{\prime}
with
S\cdot P_{\sigma}=S^{\prime}\cdot P^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. Thus
\Delta(P)
and
\Delta(P^{\prime})
are isomorphic.
∎

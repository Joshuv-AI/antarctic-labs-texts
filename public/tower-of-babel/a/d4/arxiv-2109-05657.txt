A differential graded approach to the silting theorem Thanks: MSC2020: 16G10, 16E35, 16E45. Thanks: Key words: 2-term silting complex, t t -structure, differential graded algebra, torsion pair
Zongzhen Xie, Dong Yang and Houjun Zhang

Abstract
. A silting theorem was established by Buan and Zhou as a generalisation of the classical tilting theorem of Brenner and Butler. In this paper, we give an alternative proof of the theorem by using differential graded algebras.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
A
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra. Let
T
be a classical tilting module over
A
and put
B=\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{A}(T)
. Then there are two adjoint pairs of functors between the two categories of finite-dimensional modules

\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(T,?)}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{?\otimes_{B}T}

\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{A}(T,?)}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}^{B}(?,T)}

Put
\mathscr{T}_{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{A}(T,?)),~\mathscr{F}_{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(T,?)),~\mathscr{X}_{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\nolimits(?\otimes_{B}T)
and
\mathscr{Y}_{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{B}_{1}(?,T))
.
The classical tilting theorem of Brenner and Butler
[
10
,
17
]
states that
(\mathscr{T}_{T},\mathscr{F}_{T})
is a torsion pair of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
,
(\mathscr{X}_{T},\mathscr{Y}_{T})
is a torsion pair of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B
, and the restrictions of the above four functors are equivalences

\textstyle{\mathscr{T}_{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(T,?)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{Y}_{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{?\otimes_{B}T}

\textstyle{\mathscr{F}_{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{A}(T,?)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{X}_{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}^{B}(?,T)}

Recently, Buan and Zhou generalised this theorem to a silting theorem in
[
13
]
. Precisely, let
T
be a 2-term silting complex in the bounded homotopy category
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)
of finitely generated projective
A
-modules. For example, any projective resolution of a classical tilting module is isomorphic to a 2-term silting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A)
be the bounded derived category of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 2.5]
)
.

(a)

There is a torsion pair
(\mathscr{T}_{T},\mathscr{F}_{T})
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and a torsion pair
(\mathscr{X}_{T},\mathscr{Y}_{T})
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B
together with equivalences
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A)}(T,?)\colon\mathscr{T}_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{Y}_{T}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A)}(T,\Sigma?)\colon\mathscr{F}_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{X}_{T}
.

(b)

There is a triangle in
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)

\begin{CD}A@>{}>{}>T^{\prime}@>{f}>{}>T^{\prime\prime}@>{}>{}>\Sigma A\end{CD}

with
T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits T
. Let
S
be the mapping cone of

\begin{CD}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)}(T,T^{\prime})@>{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}(T,f)}>{}>\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)}(T,T^{\prime\prime}).\end{CD}

(c)

S
is a 2-term silting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits B)
, where
B=\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)}(T)
.

(d)

There is a surjective algebra homomorphism
\pi\colon A\rightarrow\bar{A}:=\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits B)}(S)
.

(e)

\pi
is an isomorphism if and only if
T
is tilting.

Let
\pi^{*}\colon\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits\bar{A}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
be the restriction functor.

(f)

The functors
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A)}(T,?)
and
\pi^{*}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B)}(S,\Sigma?)
restrict to inverse equivalences between
\mathscr{T}_{T}
and
\mathscr{F}_{S}
.

(g)

The functors
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A)}(T,\Sigma?)
and
\pi^{*}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits B)}(S,?)
restrict to inverse equivalences between
\mathscr{F}_{T}
and
\mathscr{T}_{S}
.

Part (a) of Theorem
1.1
has been generalised to the case when
A
is a ring and
T
is a 2-term large silting complex with
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits
replaced by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}}\nolimits
(the category of all modules), see
[
8
, Section 4.3]
. In this paper, we give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
by using differential graded (=dg) algebras. We establish the result in the following three settings (Section
4.4
):

(1)

the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}}\nolimits A
of all modules, where
A
is a ring;

(2)

the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
of modules which are finitely generated over the base ring
k
, which is noetherian;

(3)

the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{fdmod}}\nolimits A
of modules which are finite-dimensional over the base ring
k
, which is a field.

Note that (3) is a special case of (2). Let us briefly explain the idea. Let
A
be a ring and
T
be a 2-term silting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)
. Part (a) of our result in Setting (1) is the same as
[
8
, Corollary 4.3.3(b)(c)]
and a similar idea as that used for its proof was used in
[
8
, Section 4.3]
. Consider the truncated dg endomorphism algebra
\Gamma
of
T
(the dg endomorphism algebra of
T
, truncated at degree
0
), which is a non-positive dg algebra, and put
B=H^{0}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)}(T)
. Then the derived Hom-functor associated with
T
is a triangle equivalence between the derived categories of
A
and
\Gamma
. Then (a) follows immediately, just as the classical tilting theorem of Brenner and Bulter is a consequence of the derived equivalence between
A
and
B
together with the bijection between torsion pairs and intermediate
t
-structures (see
[
15
, Chapter III, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
7
, Chapter I, Theorem 3.1]
).
The idea for proving (c) and (d) was already explained by Buan and Zhou in the paragraph after
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let us repeat here. Let
p\colon\Gamma\rightarrow B=H^{0}(\Gamma)
be the canonical projection. Then by
[
12
, Proposition A.3]
, the induction functor
p_{*}\colon\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}\nolimits(\Gamma)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}\nolimits(B)\simeq K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits B)
takes
\Sigma A
to a 2-term silting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits B)
. This 2-term silting complex is exactly the complex
S
in (b) and the algebra homomorphism
\pi\colon A\to\bar{A}
in (d) is the one induced by the functor
p_{*}
. The results (f) and (g) follow from the adjunction between
p_{*}
and the restriction functor
p^{*}
. Actually we first establish these results for non-positive dg algebras in Sections
4.2
and
4.3
and then specialise it to algebras in Section
4.4
.

We also study the question when
S
is a tilting complex. We show that this is the case in the following three cases:

-

A
is hereditary,

-

k
is a field and
A
is a finite-dimensional symmetric

-algebra,

-

T
is a left mutation of
A
or a right mutation of
\Sigma A
.

2.1.
Silting objects and
t
-structures

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
\Sigma
. For a subcategory or a set of objects
{\mathcal{S}}
of
\mathcal{T}
, we denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits({\mathcal{S}})
the
thick subcategory
of
\mathcal{T}
generated by
{\mathcal{S}}
,
i.e.
the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
which contains
{\mathcal{S}}
and which is closed under taking direct summands. In the following, we give the definitions of silting objects and
t
-structures. For more details we refer to
[
3
,
25
,
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An object
M
of
\mathcal{T}
is said to be

(1)

presilting
if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}M)=0
for all
i>0
;

(2)

silting
if it is presilting and
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits(M)
;

(3)

tilting
if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}M)=0
for
i\neq 0
and
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits(M)
.

The following result is
[
26
, Theorem 5.5]
(see also
[
20
, Proposition 2.8]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a silting object of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Then

{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\Sigma^{-n}\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(M)*\cdots*\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(M)).

Let
M
be a silting object of
{\mathcal{T}}
and
n
be a positive integer. We say that a silting object
N
of
{\mathcal{T}}
is
n
-term
with respect to
M
if
N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(M)*\cdots*\Sigma^{n-1}\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(M)
. The following lemma is a reformulation of
[
2
, Proposition 3.8]
. One can adapt the proof of
[
19
, Corollary 2.4]
to give a direct proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be silting objects of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Then
N
is
n
-term with respect to
M
if and only if
\Sigma^{n-1}M
is
n
-term with respect to
N
.

Let
A
be a
k
-algebra. Then the free module
A_{A}
of rank
1
is a typical silting object of
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)
. It is in fact a tilting object. We will call silting objects (respectively, tilting objects) of
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)

silting complexes
(respectively,
tilting complexes
), and a silting complex is said to be
n
-term
if it has non-trivial components only in degrees
-(n-1),\ldots,-1,0
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
\emph{t}
-
\emph{s}tructure
on
\mathcal{T}
is a pair
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
of strict (that is, closed under isomorphisms) and full subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
such that the following conditions are satisfied for
\mathcal{T}^{\geq i}:=\Sigma^{-i}\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\leq i}:=\Sigma^{-i}\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}

(1)

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\leq 1}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
;

(2)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)=0
for
X\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
Y\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 1}
;

(3)

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\ast\mathcal{T}^{\geq 1}
.

The
t
-structure
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
is said to be
bounded
if

\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{T}^{\leq i}=\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{T}^{\geq i}.

The category
{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
is called the
heart
of the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
and it is an abelian category due to
[
6
, Théorème 1.3.6]
. For any
Z\in{\mathcal{T}}
, there is a triangle by the condition (3)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma X}

with
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\leq 0}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\geq 1}
.
Due to (1) and (2) this triangle is unique up to a unique isomorphism, so the correspondences
Z\mapsto X
and
Z\mapsto Y
extend to functors
\sigma^{\leq 0}\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\sigma^{\geq 1}\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\geq 1}
, respectively, called the
\emph{t}runcation~~functors
. The functor
\sigma^{\leq 0}
is right adjoint to the inclusion
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
and the functor
\sigma^{\geq 1}
is left adjoint to the inclusion
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 1}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, the functor
H^{0}=\sigma^{\leq 0}\sigma^{\geq 0}\colon{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a cohomological functor.

Example 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an abelian category.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{\mathrm{std}}

.

Let

2.2.
Derived categories of dg algebras

A
dg
k
-algebra

A
is a graded
k
-algebra with a differential
d
which makes
A
a complex of
k
-modules such that the multiplication
A\otimes_{k}A\rightarrow A
is a chain map,
i.e.
the graded Leibniz rule holds:

d(ab)=d(a)b+(-1)^{|a|}ad(b),

where
a
and
b
are homogeneous elements of
A
and
|a|
denotes the degree of
a
. For two dg
k
-algebras
A

and
B
, a dg algebra homomorphism
f\colon A\rightarrow B
is a
k
-algebra homomorphism which is a chain map.

We follow
[
23
,
24
]
to introduce the derived category of a dg algebra. Let
A
be a dg
k
-algebra. For two dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
, the morphism complex
{\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(M,N)
is the complex whose degree
i
component consists of the homogeneous
A
-module homomorphisms from
M
to
N
of degree
i
, and whose differential is given by

d(f)=d_{N}\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ d_{M},

where
f\in{\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(M,N)
is homogeneous. We call
{\mathcal{E}\it{nd}}_{A}(M):={\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(M,M)
the
dg endomorphism algebra
of
M
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{dg}(A)
be the dg category of dg
A
-modules: its objects are dg
A
-modules, and for dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{dg}(A)}(M,N)={\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(M,N)
. Let
K(A)=H^{0}\mathcal{C}_{dg}(A)
be the homotopy category of
\mathcal{C}_{dg}(A)
: its objects are dg
A
-modules, and for two dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{K(A)}(M,N):=H^{0}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{dg}(A)}(M,N)=H^{0}{\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(M,N).

The
derived category

\mathcal{D}(A)
of dg
A
-modules is defined as the triangle quotient of
K(A)
by the subcategory of acyclic dg
A
-modules. A dg
A
-module
P
is said to be
{\mathcal{K}}
-projective
if
{\mathcal{H}\it{om}}_{A}(P,M)
is acyclic whenever
M
is an acyclic dg
A
-module. If
P
is a
{\mathcal{K}}
-projective dg
A
-module and
M
is any dg
A
-module, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}(A)}(P,\Sigma^{i}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{K(A)}(P,\Sigma^{i}M)

(2.1)

for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For example,
A_{A}
is
{\mathcal{K}}
-projective. Thus we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}(A)}(A,\Sigma^{i}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{K(A)}(A,\Sigma^{i}M)=H^{i}(M).

(2.2)

In particular, by Auslander’s projectivisation, there is a
k
-linear equivalence

\displaystyle H^{0}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}(A)}(A,?)\colon\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}(A)}(A_{A})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits H^{0}(A).

(2.3)

By
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
, for any dg
A
-module
M
there is a
{\mathcal{K}}
-projective dg module
P_{M}
together with a quasi-isomorphism
P_{M}\to M
of dg
A
-modules.

Denote by
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(A)
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(A)
whose objects are the dg
A
-modules with bounded total cohomology. Let
\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}\nolimits(A)=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A_{A})
be the
thick subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(A)
generated by
A_{A}
. In the case
A
is a
k
-algebra (viewed as a dg
k
-algebra concentrated in degree
0
), a dg
A
-module is exactly a complex of
A
-modules, we have
\mathcal{D}(A)=\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}}\nolimits A)
, and there are canonical equivalences
K^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\nolimits A)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}\nolimits(A)
and

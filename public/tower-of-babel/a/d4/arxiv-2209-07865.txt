Norm inflation and ill-posedness for the Fornberg-Whitham equation
Jinlu Li, Xing Wu, Yanghai Yu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we prove that the Cauchy problem for the Fornberg-Whitham equation is not locally well-posed in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
with
(s,p,r)\in(1,1+\frac{1}{p})\times[2,\infty)\times[1,\infty]
or
(s,p,r)\in\{1\}\times[2,\infty)\times[1,2]
by showing norm inflation phenomena of the solution for some special initial data.

Keywords:
Fornberg-Whitham equation, Norm inflation, ill-posedness, Besov spaces

MSC (2010):
35Q53, 35B35

1
Introduction

1.1
Model and Known Results

In this paper, we focus on the Cauchy problem of the Fornberg-Whitham (FW) equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-u_{xxt}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}-\frac{9}{2}u_{x}u_{xx}-\frac{3}{2}uu_{xxx}-u_{x}=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.1)

which was first introduced by Whitham
[
28
]
in 1967 and Whitham and Fornberg
[
12
]
in 1978, as a shallow water wave model to study the qualitative behaviors of wave breaking (the solution remains bounded while its slope
becomes unbounded in finite time).

The FW equation was compared with the famous Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation
[
22
]

\displaystyle u_{t}+6uu_{x}=-u_{xxx},

(1.2)

and the classical Camassa-Holm (CH) equation
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
]

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}.

(1.3)

The KdV equation admits solitons or solitary traveling wave solutions. Indeed, the KdV equation in the non-periodic admits the solitary wave solutions with the following form

u(t,x)=\frac{c}{2}{\rm{sech}}^{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}(x-ct)\Big).

And, the CH equation possess exact peaked soliton solutions (peakons) of the form

u(t,x)=ce^{-|x-ct|}.

It is interesting that the FW equation does not only admit solitary traveling wave solutions like the KdV
equation, but also possess peakon solutions (or peaked traveling wave solutions)
[
28
]
as the CH equation which
are of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{8}{9}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x-\frac{4}{3}t|}.

(1.4)

The
\mathrm{FW}
equation (
1.1
) admits the conserved quantities
[
25
]

\displaystyle E_{1}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\mathrm{d}x,\quad E_{2}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle E_{3}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u\left(1-\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u-u^{3}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

A classification of other traveling wave solutions of the FW equation was presented by Yin, Tian and Fan
[
31
]
. It’s worth mentioning that the KdV equation and CH equation are integrable,
and they possess infinitely many conserved quantities, an infinite hierarchy of quasi-local symmetries, a
Lax pair and a bi-Hamiltonian structure. Unlike the Kdv and CH equation, the FW equation is not integrable. Although the FW equation is in a simple form, the only useful
conservation law we know so far is
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
. Therefore, the analysis of the FW equation would be somewhat
more difficult due to the special structure of this equation and the lower regularity of the conservation law. Particularly, the well-posedness theory for the FW equation is not completely understood.
Before recalling the well-posedness results the FW equation, we firstly transform the FW equation (
1.1
) equivalently into the following non-local form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}=\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

By the Galerkin approximation
argument, Holmes
[
17
]
proved the well-posed of FW equation in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with
s>3/2
. Holmes and Thompson
[
18
]
obtained the well-posedness of FW equation in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R}\;\text{or}\;\mathbb{T})(s>3/2,1<r<\infty\;\text{or}\;s=3/2,r=1)
. They also proved that the data-to-solution map
is not uniformly continuous but Hölder continuous in some given topology and presented a blow-up criterion for solutions. Later, Haziot
[
15
]
, Hörmann
[
19
,
21
]
, Wei
[
26
,
27
]
, Wu-Zhang
[
29
]
and Yang
[
30
]
sharpened this
blowup criterion and presented the sufficient conditions about the initial data to lead the wave-breaking phenomena of
the FW equation. The discontinuous traveling waves as weak solutions to the FW equation were
investigated in
[
20
]
.
Recently, Guo
[
13
]
established the local well-posedness (existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence) for the FW equation in both supercritical Besov spaces
B_{p,r}^{s}
with
s>1+\frac{1}{p}
,
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty)
and critical Besov spaces
B_{p,1}^{1+1/p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. We also should mention that the FW equation is ill-posed in
B_{2,\infty}^{3/2}
by the peakon solution (
1.4
) and in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}

(s>1+1/{p},p\in[1,\infty])
by proving the solution map starting from
u_{0}
is discontinuous at
t=0
in the metric of
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
(see our recent papers
[
23
,
24
]
). For the CH equation, Guo-Liu-Molinet-Yin
[
14
]
showed the ill-posedness in the critical Besov spaces
B_{p,r}^{1+1/p}(\mathbb{R}\;\text{or}\;\mathbb{T})
with
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]
(especially in
H^{3/2}
) due to norm inflation. Following their methods, the FW equation is also ill-posed in the above critical Besov spaces.
However, the well-posedness issue of the FW equation in Besov spaces
B_{p,r}^{s}
with
s<1+\frac{1}{p}
appears unsolved.
In this paper, our aim is to provide a rigorous proof of ill-posedness for the FW equation in the low regularity setting by exhibiting a strong instability known as a
Norm Inflation
, i.e. arbitrary small data leads to an arbitrarily large
solution in arbitrary small time, preventing the solution map to be continuous.

1.2
Main Result

Now let us state our main ill-posedness result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

\displaystyle 1<s<1+\frac{1}{p},(p,r)\in[2,\infty)\times[1,\infty]\quad\mathrm{or}\quad s=1,(p,r)\in[2,\infty)\times[1,2].

(1.6)

For any
\delta>0
, there exists initial data satisfying

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\delta,

such that a solution
u(t)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{0}];B^{s}_{p,r})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|u(T_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{some}\quad 0<T_{0}<\delta.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
indicates the ill-posedness of (
1.5
) in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
in the sense that the solution map
B^{s}_{p,r}\ni u_{0}\mapsto u\in B^{s}_{p,r}
is discontinuous with respect to the initial data.

Remark 1.2
.

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
is new for the FW equation even in the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})=B^{s}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
with
s\in[1,\frac{3}{2})
.

1.3
Strategy to Proof

We shall outline the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

•

Firstly, we construct an explicit example for initial data
u_{0}
, where the norm
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}
is sufficiently small.

•

Secondly, we present the lower and upper bounds of lifespan
T^{*}
of the solution to the FW equation (
1.5
) by exploring fully the properties of the flow map
\psi
.

•

Lastly, we mainly observe that the transport term does cause growth of the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{x}
. Precisely speaking, we estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{x}
over
(-\psi(T_{\rm{min}},q_{0}),\psi(T_{\rm{min}},q_{0}))
and obtain that its lower bound can be arbitrarily large.

The structure of the paper.
In Section
2
we provide several key Lemmas. In Section
3
we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.4
The Equation Along the Flow

Given a Lipschitz velocity field
u
, we may solve the following ODE to find the flow induced by
u
:

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi(t,x)=\frac{3}{2}u(t,\psi(t,x)),\\
\psi(0,x)=x,\end{cases}

(2.9)

which is equivalent to the integral form

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)=x+\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}u(\tau,\psi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(2.10)

Because the velocity field is Lipschitz, then we get that for
t\in[0,T^{*})

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\psi(t,x)=\exp\left(\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{x}u(\tau,\psi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau\right)>0.

This shows that
\psi(t,\cdot)
is an increasing diffeomorphism over
\mathbb{R}
, that is, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R},
there holds that
\psi(t,x)<\psi(t,y)
if
x<y
.
Also, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{CT^{*}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{\infty})}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T^{*}}(L^{\infty})}.

(2.11)

In view of (
1.5
), we get that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u(t,\psi(t,x))

\displaystyle=u_{t}(t,\psi(t,x))+u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left(u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}\right)(t,\psi(t,x))

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u(t,\psi(t,x)).

Integrating the above with respect to time variable yields that

\displaystyle u(t,\psi(t,x))=u_{0}(x)+\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u(\tau,\psi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(2.12)

Differentiating (
1.5
) with respect to space variable
x
, we find

\displaystyle u_{tx}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{xx}+\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}=\partial^{2}_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u=:V.

(2.13)

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))

\displaystyle=u_{tx}(t,\psi(t,x))+u_{xx}(t,\psi(t,x))\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left(u_{tx}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{xx}\right)(t,\psi(t,x))

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}(t,\psi(t,x))+V(t,\psi(t,x)),

(2.14)

which means that

\displaystyle u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))=u^{\prime}_{0}(x)-\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}(u_{x})^{2}(\tau,\psi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}V(\tau,\psi(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(2.15)

Differentiating (

Estimation of Lifespan

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that

\displaystyle u_{xx}(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T^{*})\times(\psi(t,-q_{0}),\psi(t,q_{0}))).

Notice that
\partial_{t}u_{x}=-\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}-\frac{3}{2}uu_{xx}+\partial^{2}_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{x}(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T^{*})\times(\psi(t,-q_{0}),\psi(t,q_{0}))).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Next, we present the lower and upper bounds of lifespan
T^{*}
of the solution to the FW equation (
1.5
).

The
L^{\infty}
-norm of any function
u
is preserved under the flow
\psi
, i.e.

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\|u(t,\psi(t,x))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Combing the above and (
2.12
), we obtain that for
t\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using Gronwall’s inequality gives us that for
t\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}e^{t}\leq 3p_{0}q_{0}.

Note that
\left(1-\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}f=G*f
with
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
, by Young’s inequality, we have

\|\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|G_{x}*u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|G_{x}\right\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

In view of the identity
\partial^{2}_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u=-u+(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}u
, we have

\displaystyle|V(t,\psi(t,x))|\leq\|V(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 6p_{0}q_{0}.

Then for all
t\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we deduce from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq-\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}(t,\psi(t,x))+6p_{0}q_{0},\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\geq-\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}(t,\psi(t,x))-6p_{0}q_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.21)

From Fig 2, we see that
u^{\prime}_{0}(x)\leq u^{\prime}_{0}(0)=-2p_{0}e^{-q_{0}}<-p_{0}
if
x\in(-q_{0},q_{0})
. Thus the standard argument of continuity shows

\displaystyle u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq-p_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]\times(-q_{0},q_{0}),

from which and (
2.21
), we have for
(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]\times(-q_{0},q_{0})

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq-\frac{3}{2}\left(u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))+p_{0}q_{0}\right)^{2},\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\geq-\frac{3}{2}\left(u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))-p_{0}q_{0}\right)^{2}.\end{cases}

(2.22)

Solving the differential inequalities (
2.22
) yields for
(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\cap[0,\ln 3]\times(-q_{0},q_{0})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(x)-p_{0}q_{0}}}+p_{0}q_{0}\leq u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(x)+p_{0}q_{0}}}-p_{0}q_{0}.

(2.23)

According to the definition of
u_{0}
, we can deduce that the lifespan
T^{*}
of the solution to (
1.5
) satisfies

\displaystyle T^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\inf_{x\in(-q_{0},q_{0})}\left\{\frac{-1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(x)+p_{0}q_{0}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(q^{-}_{0})+p_{0}q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{p_{0}(1+e^{-2q_{0}}-q_{0})}=:T_{\mathrm{max}},

where we have used that
u^{\prime}_{0}(q_{0}^{-})=-p_{0}(e^{-2q_{0}}+1)
.

It is easy to deduce that for sufficiently small
q_{0}

T_{\mathrm{max}}\in\left(\frac{1}{3p_{0}},\frac{2}{3p_{0}}\right).

Now, we claim that

\displaystyle|u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))|\leq 6p_{0}q_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(-\infty,-q_{0})\cap(q_{0},+\infty),

(2.24)

which tells us that the solution do not blow up when
(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(-\infty,-q_{0})\cap(q_{0},+\infty)
.

Next, we begin to prove (
2.24
).
Due to
u^{\prime}_{0}(x)\in(0,2p_{0}q_{0})
if
x\in(-\infty,-q_{0})
,
from
\eqref{t1}_{1}
,
we have

u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq u^{\prime}_{0}(x)+6p_{0}q_{0}T^{*}\leq 6p_{0}q_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(-\infty,-q_{0}).

Using the standard argument of continuity again, it follows from
\eqref{t1}_{2}
that

u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\geq-6p_{0}q_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(-\infty,-q_{0}).

In summary, it holds that

|u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))|\leq 6p_{0}q_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(-\infty,-q_{0}).

Following the above argument, we also have

|u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))|\leq 6p_{0}q_{0},\quad\forall(t,x)\in t,x)\in[0,T^{*})\times(q_{0},+\infty),

thus the claim holds.

Then, we can deduce that

\displaystyle T^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\inf_{x\in(-q_{0},q_{0})}\left\{\frac{-1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(x)-p_{0}q_{0}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(q^{-}_{0})-p_{0}q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{p_{0}(1+e^{-2q_{0}}+q_{0})}=:T_{\mathrm{min}}.

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

.

According to the definition of
u^{\prime}_{0}(x)
(see Fig 2), we deduce from (

\displaystyle m(t)+p_{0}q_{0}\leq u_{x}(t,\psi(t,x))\leq M(t)-p_{0}q_{0},

(3.25)

where, for notational convenience, we denote

\displaystyle m(t):=\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(q_{0}^{-})-p_{0}q_{0}}}\quad\text{and}\quad M(t):=\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+p_{0}q_{0}}}.

(3.26)

Set
w(t,x):=u_{x}(t,x)
, then we obtain from (
1.5
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(uw)=V,

which implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(w^{2})+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(uw^{2})+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}uw^{2}=2Vw.

(3.27)

Integrating (
3.27
) with respect to space variable
x
over
[\psi(t,-q_{0}),\psi(t,q_{0})]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{t}(w^{2})\mathrm{d}x+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{x}(uw^{2})\mathrm{d}x+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{x}uw^{2}\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}wV\mathrm{d}x.

(3.28)

Direct computations yield that

\displaystyle\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{t}(w^{2})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}w^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{3}{2}u(t,\psi(t,q_{0}))w^{2}(t,\psi^{-}(t,q_{0}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{2}u(t,\psi(t,-q_{0}))w^{2}(t,\psi^{+}(t,-q_{0}))

(3.29)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{x}(uw^{2})\mathrm{d}x=u(t,\psi(t,q_{0}))w^{2}(t,\psi^{-}(t,q_{0}))-u(t,\psi(t,-q_{0}))w^{2}(t,\psi^{+}(t,-q_{0})).

(3.30)

Note
We should mention that the both
w(t,\psi^{-}(t,q_{0}))
and
w(t,\psi^{+}(t,-q_{0}))
exist since
w(t,x)
is uniformly continuous with respect to
x
over
(\psi(t,-q_{0}),\psi(t,q_{0}))
by (
2.19
) and (
2.20
).

Inserting (
3
) and (
3.30
) into (
3.28
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}w^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}\partial_{x}uw^{2}\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}wV\mathrm{d}x.

To simplify notation let

A(t):=\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}w^{2}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x,

combining (
3.25
), then (
3.31
) reduces to

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}u_{x}(t,x)w^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\psi(t,-q_{0})}^{\psi(t,q_{0})}wV\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3}{2}M(t)A(t)+\frac{3}{2}p_{0}q_{0}A(t)-2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

where we have used that

\|V\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 4p_{0}q_{0}.

Solving the above differential inequality gives us that

\displaystyle A(t)\geq\exp(B(t))\left(A_{0}-Cp_{0}^{2}q_{0}^{2}\int^{t}_{0}\exp(-B(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right),

(3.32)

where

B(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\left(-\frac{3}{2}M(t)-1\right)\mathrm{d}\tau.

Easy computations give that

\displaystyle\exp(B(t))=\frac{M(t)}{M(0)}e^{-t},

\displaystyle A_{0}=\int_{|x|\leq q_{0}}\big(u^{\prime}_{0}(x)\big)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\approx p^{2}_{0}q_{0},

\displaystyle M(0)=u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+p_{0}q_{0}=p_{0}q_{0}-2p_{0}e^{-q_{0}},

thus we have

\displaystyle M(T_{\mathrm{min}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}T_{\mathrm{min}}+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+p_{0}q_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p_{0}q_{0}-u^{\prime}_{0}(q_{0}^{-})}+\frac{1}{u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+p_{0}q_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{0}q_{0}-u^{\prime}_{0}(q_{0}^{-})}{u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+2p_{0}q_{0}-u^{\prime}_{0}(q_{0}^{-})}M(0)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{M(0)}{q_{0}},

which gives directly that

\displaystyle\frac{M(T_{\mathrm{min}})}{M(0)}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{q_{0}}.

Also,

\displaystyle\int^{T_{\mathrm{min}}}_{0}\exp(-B(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int^{T_{\mathrm{min}}}_{0}\left(\frac{3}{2}\tau(u^{\prime}_{0}(0)+p_{0}q_{0})+1\right)e^{\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim p_{0}q_{0}T_{\mathrm{min}}^{2}+T_{\mathrm{min}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{p_{0}}.

\displaystyle A(T_{\mathrm{min}})\geq C\left(p^{2}_{0}-p_{0}q_{0}\right)\geq Cp^{2}_{0}.

Notice that (
3.25

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\psi(T_{\mathrm{min}},-q_{0})}^{\psi(T_{\mathrm{min}},q_{0})}w^{p}(T_{\mathrm{min}},x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq cp_{0}^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(p_{0}q_{0}-C\frac{M(0)}{q_{0}}\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq cp_{0}q_{0}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq cp_{0}.

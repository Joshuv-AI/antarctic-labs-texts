Spectral analysis of the Dirac operator with a singular interaction on a broken line
Dale Frymark, Markus Holzmann, Vladimir Lotoreichik

Abstract
. We consider the one-parametric family of self-adjoint realizations of the two-dimensional massive Dirac operator with a Lorentz scalar δ \delta -shell interaction of strength τ ∈ ℝ ∖ { − 2 , 0 , 2 } \tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\} supported on a broken line of opening angle 2 ​ ω 2\omega with ω ∈ ( 0 , π 2 ) \omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}) . The essential spectrum of any such self-adjoint realization is symmetric with respect to the origin with a gap around zero whose size depends on the mass and, for τ < 0 \tau<0 , also on the strength of the interaction, but does not depend on ω \omega . As the main result, we prove that for any N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} and strength τ ∈ ( − ∞ , 0 ) ∖ { − 2 } \tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\} the discrete spectrum of any such self-adjoint realization has at least N N discrete eigenvalues, with multiplicities taken into account, in the gap of the essential spectrum provided that ω \omega is sufficiently small. Moreover, we obtain an explicit estimate on ω \omega sufficient for this property to hold. For τ ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) ∖ { 2 } \tau\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{2\} , the discrete spectrum consists of at most one simple eigenvalue.

1.
Introduction

Dirac operators with singular interactions supported on hypersurfaces
in the Euclidean space serve
as an idealized model for more physically realistic Dirac operators with regular potentials localized in the vicinity of a hypersurface. The Dirac operator with a singular interaction
supported on a sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was first analysed in
[
16
]
, where separation of variables was extensively used. The renewal of interest to this topic is connected with the papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, where singular interactions supported on general closed surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
were analysed, see also
[
6
,
7
]
for further considerations in dimension three,
[
9
,
15
]
for the study of the two-dimensional case, and
[
10
]
for a recent unified treatment for space dimensions two and three.

In this paper we are interested in spectral properties of Dirac operators with singular potentials supported on a broken line, for which there
are several motivations. On the one hand,
the analysis of two-dimensional Dirac operators with singular interactions supported on curves with corner points revealed the importance of the model Dirac operators with singular interactions supported on unbounded broken lines
[
28
,
35
]
. In particular, self-adjointness properties of such Dirac operators with Lorentz scalar
\delta
-shell interactions supported on star graphs were studied in
[
25
]
.

On the other hand, the broken line is a typical example where the existence of geometrically induced eigenvalues is observed.
The relation of the spectral properties of a differential operator and the geometry of the interaction support or the domain in which the operator acts is one of the most classical questions in spectral theory. In particular, one is often interested in situations when a modification of the geometry generates bound states. There is a huge number of results available for Laplacians with different boundary conditions, see the monograph
[
21
]
and the references therein or
[
17
,
18
]
for the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions,
[
23
,
32
]
for related results for Laplacians with Robin boundary conditions, and
[
14
,
22
]
for considerations on the magnetic Laplacian with Neumann boundary conditions.
One interesting example of the above type is the non-relativistic counterpart of the Dirac operator with a singular potential. Consider a Schrödinger operator with a
\delta
-potential of strength
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
supported on a broken line
\Gamma
of opening angle
2\omega
with
\omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, i.e. the operator that is formally given by

T_{\alpha}=-\Delta+\alpha\delta_{\Gamma}.

With the help of the Birman-Schwinger principle it was established in
[
20
]
that
\sigma(T_{\alpha})=\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{\alpha})=[0,\infty)
for
\alpha\geq 0
, while for attractive interactions
\alpha<0
one has
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{\alpha})=[-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4},\infty)
and, if
\Gamma
is not a straight line, then the discrete spectrum of
T_{\alpha}
is non-empty. In
[
24
]
it was shown with the help of the min-max principle and suitable test functions that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
the operator
T_{\alpha}
has at least
N
discrete eigenvalues (taking multiplicities into account), if the opening angle of
\Gamma
is sufficiently small. Finally, it was proved in
[
33
]
with a variational argument that
T_{\alpha}
always has, for
\alpha<0
, a non-empty discrete spectrum, when the interaction is supported on a star graph which is not a straight line, see also
[
34
]
for related considerations for Schrödinger operators with
\delta^{\prime}
-interactions.

The main goal of this paper is to study the spectral properties and to establish the existence of geometrically induced bound states for the two-dimensional Dirac operator with a Lorentz scalar
\delta
-shell interaction supported on the broken line
\Gamma
that is formally given by

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}_{\tau}=-i\sigma_{1}\partial_{1}-i\sigma_{2}\partial_{2}+m\sigma_{3}+\tau\sigma_{3}\delta_{\Gamma}.

Here,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
,
m>0
, and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, and
\sigma_{3}
are the Pauli spin matrices defined in (
1.5
) below. From a physical point of view,
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
describes the propagation of a relativistic quantum particle with mass
m
under the influence of the Lorentz scalar potential
\tau\sigma_{3}\delta_{\Gamma}
. We remark that
\tau=\pm 2
is excluded, as in this case the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
decouples into two Dirac operators acting in the domains with boundary
\Gamma
and infinite mass boundary conditions on

\Gamma
, cf.
[
9
,
10
]
. In particular, in this
confinement
case the spectrum is computed in
[
28
, Proposition 1.12]
, by which the essential spectrum of any self-adjoint realization of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is
(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
and there can only be one eigenvalue having multiplicity one, while the spectrum of any self-adjoint realization of
\mathcal{A}_{-2}
is always the full real line; note that
\tau=-2
in the present manuscript corresponds to
m<0
in
[
28
]
, which can be achieved via a unitary transformation.

Let us describe our results.
The broken line decomposes
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two regions. Let

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(r\cos{\theta},r\sin{\theta})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon r>0,~{\theta}\in(-\omega,\omega)\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

be the wedge of opening angle
2\omega
, and
\Omega_{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}
be the open complement of
\Omega_{+}
. Distinguish
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial\Omega_{+}
as the joint boundary of
\Omega_{\pm}
, and let

be the unit normal vector field at

\Omega_{+}

\omega\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)

\Omega_{+}

In the following, functions

where the matrix-valued function

(1.4)

If

is a smooth bounded domain, then the self-adjointness and some spectral properties of

–including finiteness of the discrete spectrum–were studied in
[

]
, see also
[

[

]
. Eventually, we mention that in the papers

In the case of Dirac operators with zigzag boundary conditions there is a clear picture of the geometrically induced spectrum

However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no result in the literature where the existence of geometrically induced bound states of Dirac operators with singular potentials is shown, which is the main motivation in the present paper.

Coming back to the case that
\Gamma
is the broken line, it is known that the operator
S_{\tau}
is closed and symmetric, cf.
[

]
. Note that for
\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}
the curve

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
,
\omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
,

, and

be any self-adjoint extension of

(i)

\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(A_{\tau,z})=(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
and

has at most one discrete eigenvalue of multiplicity one.

(ii)

If
\tau<0

\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(A_{\tau,z})=\big(-\infty,-m\big|\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\big|\big]\cup\big[m\big|\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\big|,\infty\big)

The claim on the discrete spectrum for

3.
Quadratic Form

Recall that for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
the operator
S_{\tau}
is defined by equation (
1.3
). The main goal in this section is to find a convenient representation of
\|S_{\tau}u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2}
for
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
, see Theorem
3.2
, which is the quadratic form associated with the self-adjoint operator
S^{*}_{\tau}S_{\tau}
. This operator will play a central role in the spectral analysis of the self-adjoint extensions
A_{\tau,z}
of
S_{\tau}
, as, e.g., the difference of the resolvents of
S^{*}_{\tau}S_{\tau}
and
(A_{\tau,z})^{2}
will be compared in Section
5
to determine the essential spectrum of
A_{\tau,z}
. Moreover, this quadratic form will play a crucial role in the proof of the statements about the discrete spectrum of
A_{\tau,z}
in Theorem
1.1
.

In the following we are going to make use of the tangential derivative
\partial_{t}
on the boundary of a Lipschitz hypograph
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For its definition, let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an arc-length parametrization of
\partial\Omega
such that the unit normal vector field
\nu
, which is pointing outwards of
\Omega
, is given by
\nu=(-\dot{\gamma}_{2},\dot{\gamma}_{1})
almost everywhere.
Then, for
\varphi\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
we define

(3.1)

\partial_{t}\varphi(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly, for any
s\in[0,1]
the tangential derivative
\partial_{t}
gives rise to a bounded operator

\partial_{t}\mathchar 58\relax H^{s}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s-1}(\partial\Omega).

We will use that the tangential derivative of the trace of a function
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

\partial_{t}(u|_{\partial\Omega})=\dot{\gamma}\cdot(\gradient u)|_{\partial\Omega}=\nu_{2}(\partial_{1}u)|_{\partial\Gamma}-\nu_{1}(\partial_{2}u)|_{\partial\Gamma}.

Now, we are prepared to state some of the finer details for the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Lipschitz hypograph and let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
be the unit normal vector field at
\partial\Omega
which points outwards of
\Omega
. Then for all
f,g\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
the following holds:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle(\sigma\cdot\gradient)f,(\sigma\cdot\gradient)g\big\rangle_{\Omega}=\big\langle\gradient f,\gradient g\big\rangle_{\Omega}-\big\langle f|_{\partial\Omega},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(g|_{\partial\Omega})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
g\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Then, the identity
(\sigma\cdot\gradient)^{2}=\Delta
and an application of Gauss’ theorem yield

\displaystyle\big\langle(\sigma\cdot\gradient)f,(\sigma\cdot\gradient)g\big\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\big\langle(\sigma\cdot\nu)f|_{\partial\Omega},((\sigma\cdot\gradient)g)|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega}-\big\langle f,(\sigma\cdot\gradient)^{2}g\big\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\big\langle f|_{\partial\Omega},(\sigma\cdot\nu)((\sigma\cdot\gradient)g)|_{\partial\Omega}-\nu\cdot\gradient g|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\big\langle\gradient f,\gradient g\big\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\big\langle f|_{\partial\Omega},\sigma_{1}\nu_{1}\sigma_{2}(\partial_{2}g)|_{\partial\Omega}+\sigma_{2}\nu_{2}\sigma_{1}(\partial_{1}g)|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\big\langle\gradient f,\gradient g\big\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\big\langle f|_{\partial\Omega},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\left(\nu_{1}(\partial_{2}g)|_{\partial\Omega}-\nu_{2}(\partial_{1}g)|_{\partial\Omega}\right)\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\big\langle\gradient f,\gradient g\big\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\big\langle f|_{\partial\Omega},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\partial_{t}(g|_{\partial\Omega})\big\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\big\langle\gradient f,\gradient g\big\rangle_{\Omega}.

This expression agrees with equation (
3.2
), as
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}=i\sigma_{3}
. Since
H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
is dense in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
, a continuity argument yields the claim for
g\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
∎

It is now possible to show a useful representation for the quadratic form associated with the self-adjoint operator
S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau}
, i.e. of the expression

\displaystyle\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(-i\sigma\cdot\gradient)u+m\sigma_{3}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma},\qquad u\in\dom S_{\tau}.

The following result can be seen as a two-dimensional variant of
[
26
, Proposition 3.1]
with the interaction support being the broken line; cf. also
[
2
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
29
, Proposition 14]
for similar formulas.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
. Then, for any
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
, the following holds:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=\|\gradient u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\frac{2m}{\tau}\|u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}.

Proof.

Let
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
be fixed. First, as
\nu
is pointing outside of
\Omega_{+}
and inside of
\Omega_{-}
, Lemma
3.1
yields

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(-i\sigma\cdot\gradient)u+m\sigma_{3}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\|(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+2\real\left(\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u,m\sigma_{3}u\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\|\gradient u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+2\real\left(\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u,m\sigma_{3}u\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle+\langle u_{-}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{-}|_{\Gamma})\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle-\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}.

Recall that
M
is defined by (
1.4
) and note that
\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)=-(\sigma\cdot\nu)\sigma_{3}
, which holds by (
1.6
), implies

\sigma_{3}M=\left(\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}-\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)\right)\sigma_{3}=M^{-1}\sigma_{3}.

Since
\nu
is piecewise constant, this yields

\displaystyle\big\langle Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle i\sigma_{3}(\partial_{t}(Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\big\langle M^{-1}Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})},

cf. (
A.1
) for details. This yields

(3.5)

\displaystyle\big\langle u_{-}|_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{-}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}-\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\big\langle Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle-\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\big\langle M^{-1}Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle-\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},i\sigma_{3}\partial_{t}(u_{+}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2})}=0.

This simplifies equation (
3.4
) and it remains only to recover the last term in equation (
3.3
).

The
identity

\displaystyle\big\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u_{\pm},m\sigma_{3}u_{\pm}\big\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle\Omega_{\pm}}}+\big\langle m\sigma_{3}u_{\pm},-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u_{\pm}\big\rangle_{{}_{\scriptstyle\Omega_{\pm}}}=\pm\big\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{\pm}|_{\Gamma},m\sigma_{3}u_{\pm}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma},

and the fact that
M
commutes with
-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)
allow for the following simplification:

\displaystyle 2\real

\displaystyle\left(\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u,m\sigma_{3}u\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\big\langle-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u,m\sigma_{3}u\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}+\big\langle m\sigma_{3}u,-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)u\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}

\displaystyle=m\big[\big\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{+}|_{\Gamma},u_{+}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}-\big\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{-}|_{\Gamma},u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt-\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}+\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}\Big]

\displaystyle=m\big[\big\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{+}|_{\Gamma},u_{+}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}-\big\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{-}|_{\Gamma},u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt-\big\langle u_{-}|_{\Gamma},-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{+}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}+\big\langle u_{+}|_{\Gamma},-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}\Big]

\displaystyle=m\big[\big\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)(u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}),(u_{+}|_{\Gamma}+u_{-}|_{\Gamma})\big\rangle_{\Gamma}\big]

\displaystyle=m\left\langle-i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)(u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}),-\frac{2}{\tau}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)(u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma})\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=\frac{2m}{\tau}\big\|u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\big\|^{2}_{\Gamma},

where the second to last equality is due to the transmission condition in equation (
2.2
). The claim follows.
∎

4.
Symmetry of the spectrum

Recall that the distinguished self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
is denoted by
A_{\tau,1}
, cf. Theorems
2.2
and
2.4
. In this section we show that the spectrum of
A_{\tau,1}
is symmetric with respect to the origin. Corollary
4.2
then shows that the essential spectrum of any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
is symmetric, which will be helpful in determining further spectral properties in Sections
5
and
6
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
. Then the spectrum of
A_{\tau,1}
is symmetric about the origin, i.e.
{\lambda}\in\sigma(A_{\tau,1})
if and only if
-{\lambda}\in\sigma(A_{\tau,1})
. In particular, this symmetry also holds for the discrete and the essential spectrum of
A_{\tau,1}
.

Proof.

First, recall that the spin-orbit operator
J_{\tau}
is given in equation (
2.3
). Let
{\lambda}
be the unique simple eigenvalue of
J_{\tau}
in the interval
(0,\frac{1}{2})
from Lemma
2.1
and
{\varphi}_{\lambda}
the corresponding eigenvector. Define

\displaystyle v(x(r,{\theta}),y(r,{\theta}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=K_{{\lambda}-1/2}(r)\cdot{\varphi}_{\lambda}({\theta}),

where
x(r,{\theta})=r\cos{\theta}
and
y(r,{\theta})=r\sin{\theta}
.
Then, by Theorem
2.4
the distinguished self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
can be written as

\displaystyle A_{\tau,1}u

\displaystyle=(-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)+m\sigma_{3})u_{+}\oplus(-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)+m\sigma_{3})u_{-},

\displaystyle\dom A_{\tau,1}

\displaystyle=\dom S_{\tau}+\spa\{v\}.

Define the charge conjugation operator by
Cf\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sigma_{1}\overline{f}
. We show that
C
maps
\dom A_{\tau,1}
into itself. Note that
\overline{\sigma_{1}}=\sigma_{1}
,
\overline{\sigma_{2}}=-\sigma_{2}
, and
\overline{\sigma_{3}}=\sigma_{3}
. First, for
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
, the transmission condition in equation (
1.4
) and the anti-commutation relations in (
1.6
) yield

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=C\left[\left(\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}+\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)\right)u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\left[\left(\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}-\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}\nu_{1}-\sigma_{2}\nu_{2})\right)\overline{u_{+}|_{\Gamma}}-\overline{u_{-}|_{\Gamma}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}+\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}\nu_{1}+\sigma_{2}\nu_{2})\right)\sigma_{1}\overline{u_{+}|_{\Gamma}}-\sigma_{1}\overline{u_{-}|_{\Gamma}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}+\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu)\right)Cu_{+}|_{\Gamma}-Cu_{-}|_{\Gamma}\right],

and so
Cu\in\dom S_{\tau}
.
Next, as
K_{{\lambda}-1/2}
is real-valued and scalar, we have

\displaystyle Cv(x(r,{\theta}),y(r,{\theta}))=K_{{\lambda}-1/2}(r)C{\varphi}_{\lambda}({\theta}).

To show that
C{\varphi}_{\lambda}\in\ker(J_{\tau}-{\lambda})
, we first verify that
C{\varphi}_{{\lambda}}\in\dom J_{\tau}
. As
M_{l}
is a restriction of
M
, a similar consideration as above shows first
\sigma_{1}\overline{M_{l}}=M_{l}\sigma_{1}
and further

\displaystyle C{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}}(\omega)=\sigma_{1}\overline{M_{l}{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}(\omega)}=M_{l}\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}(\omega)}=M_{l}C{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}(\omega).

An analogous argument shows that
C{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}(2\pi-\omega)=M_{r}C{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}(-\omega)
, and hence
C{\varphi}_{\lambda}\in\dom J_{\tau}
. Moreover,

\begin{split}J_{\tau}C{\varphi}_{\lambda}&=\left(-i\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}}^{\prime}+\frac{\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}}}{2}\right)\oplus\left(-i\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}}^{\prime}+\frac{\sigma_{1}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}}}{2}\right)\\
&=\sigma_{1}\left[\left(i\sigma_{3}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}^{\prime}}+\frac{\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}}}{2}\right)\oplus\left(i\sigma_{3}\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}^{\prime}}+\frac{\overline{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}}}{2}\right)\right]\\
&=\sigma_{1}\overline{\left[\left(-i\sigma_{3}{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}^{\prime}+\frac{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},+}}{2}\right)\oplus\left(-i\sigma_{3}{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}^{\prime}+\frac{{\varphi}_{{\lambda},-}}{2}\right)\right]}\\
&=\sigma_{1}\overline{{\lambda}{\varphi}_{\lambda}}={\lambda}C{\varphi}_{\lambda}.\end{split}

Since
{\lambda}
is a simple eigenvalue of
J_{\tau}
and
{\varphi}_{\lambda},C{\varphi}_{\lambda}\in\ker(J_{\tau}-{\lambda})
, we find that
C{\varphi}_{\lambda}=a{\varphi}_{\lambda}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence,
Cv\in\spa\{v\}
and for any
u\in\dom A_{\tau,1}
,
Cu\in\dom A_{\tau,1}
.
Finally, using
\sigma_{2}=-\overline{\sigma_{2}}
, we calculate for
u\in\dom A_{\tau,1}

\displaystyle(A_{\tau,1}Cu)_{\pm}

\displaystyle=[(-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)+m\sigma_{3}]Cu_{\pm}=[-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)+m\sigma_{3}]\sigma_{1}\overline{u}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(-i\sigma_{1}\partial_{1}+i\sigma_{2}\partial_{2}-m\sigma_{3})\overline{u}_{\pm}=-\sigma_{1}\overline{(-i\sigma_{1}\partial_{1}-i\sigma_{2}\partial_{2}+m\sigma_{3})u_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=-C(-i(\sigma\cdot\gradient)+m\sigma_{3})u_{\pm}=-C(A_{\tau,1}u)_{\pm}.

If we let
{\lambda}\in\sigma(A_{\tau,1})
and
(u_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\dom A_{\tau,1}
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=1
and
(A_{\tau,1}-{\lambda})u_{n}\to 0
, then
Cu_{n}\in\dom A_{\tau,1}
,
\|Cu_{n}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=1
and

\displaystyle(A_{\tau,1}+{\lambda})Cu_{n}=C(-A_{\tau,1}+{\lambda})u_{n}\to 0,

so that
-{\lambda}\in\sigma(A_{\tau,1})
. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-2,0,2\}
. Let

with
|z|=1
and

be the corresponding self-adjoint extension of

via Theorem

is symmetric with respect to the origin, i.e.
\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})=-\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})

Proof.

The claim holds for the distinguished self-adjoint extension

by Theorem

and since

\displaystyle(A_{\tau,z}-i)^{-1}-(A_{\tau,1}-i)^{-1}

∎

5.
Essential Spectrum

In this section we show the statements about the essential spectrum of any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
in Theorem
1.1
. We begin with a general result for symmetric operators. Then, in Proposition
5.2
we show that
(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)\subset\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})
holds for any self-adjoint extension
A_{\tau,z}
of
S_{\tau}
. Together with Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
5.1
this implies already that this inclusion is an equality if
\tau\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
. Then, in Propositions
5.4
and
5.5
we show with a Neumann bracketing argument and suitable singular sequences that for
\tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\}
one has

\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})=\Bigg[m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2},\infty\Bigg).

This allows us finally with Proposition
5.1
and Corollary
4.2
to determine
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(A_{\tau,z})
also for
\tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be a closed, densely defined, symmetric operator in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with deficiency indices
(n,n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
D
be a self-adjoint extension of
S
.
Then the resolvent difference

W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(S^{*}S+1)^{-1}-(D^{2}+1)^{-1}

is a finite-rank operator with rank at most
n
.

Proof.

The self-adjoint extension
D
can be described by the von Neumann extension theory of symmetric operators. According to
[
37
, Theorem 13.10]
there exists a unitary operator

U\mathchar 58\relax\ker(S^{*}-i)\to\ker(S^{*}+i)

such that the self-adjoint extension
D
is defined as

Du=Su_{0}+i\widetilde{u}-iU\widetilde{u},\qquad\dom D=\{u_{0}+\widetilde{u}+U\widetilde{u}\mathchar 58\relax u_{0}\in\dom S,\,\widetilde{u}\in\ker(S^{*}-i)\}.

Likewise,
[
37
, Proposition 3.7]
says that
u\in\dom S^{*}
admits a unique decomposition
u=u_{0}+\widetilde{u}+\widehat{u}
for
u_{0}\in\dom S
,
\widetilde{u}\in\ker(S^{*}-i)
and
\widehat{u}\in\ker(S^{*}+i)
. Define the linear operator
\Pi\mathchar 58\relax\dom S^{*}\to\ker(S^{*}-i)
such that
\Pi u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{u}
and let
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
be arbitrary. We set

u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(S^{*}S+1)^{-1}f,\qquad v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(D^{2}+1)^{-1}g,

which, in particular, means that
u\in\dom(S^{*}S)
and
v\in\dom(S^{*})
. We calculate that

\displaystyle(Wf,g)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=((S^{*}S+1)^{-1}f,g)_{\mathcal{H}}-(f,(D^{2}+1)^{-1}g)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=(u,D^{2}v)_{\mathcal{H}}-(S^{*}Su,v)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=(u,S^{*}Sv_{0}-\widetilde{v}-U\widetilde{v})_{\mathcal{H}}-(S^{*}Su,v_{0}+\widetilde{v}+U\widetilde{v})_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=(u,-\widetilde{v}-U\widetilde{v})_{\mathcal{H}}-(S^{*}Su,\widetilde{v}+U\widetilde{v})_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-((S^{*}S+1)u,\widetilde{v}+U\widetilde{v})_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-(f,(1+U)\Pi(D^{2}+1)^{-1}g)_{\mathcal{H}},

where the symmetry of
S^{*}S
was used to cancel two terms between the third and fourth equalities.
Hence,

W=-(1+U)\Pi(D^{2}+1)^{-1}

and because
\ran\Pi=\ker(S^{*}-i)
, the rank of
W
can be at most
n
.
∎

In the next proposition we show that the inclusion
(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)\subset\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})
holds for any self-adjoint extension
A_{\tau,z}
of
S_{\tau}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A_{\tau,z}
be any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
defined in (
1.3
). Then the inclusion
(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)\subset\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})
holds.

Proof.

Let
{\lambda}\in(-\infty,-m)\cup(m,\infty)
be fixed and fix a vector
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-m^{2}}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}+{\lambda}\sigma_{0})\zeta\neq 0
. Also let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be chosen so that
\chi(r)=1
for
|r|<1/2
,
\chi(r)=0
for
|r|>1
, and
x_{n}=(-1-n^{2},0)^{T}
. Now, consider the sequence

(5.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\chi\left(\frac{1}{n}|x-x_{n}|\right)e^{i\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-m^{2}}x\cdot e_{1}}\left(\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-m^{2}}\sigma_{1}+m\sigma_{3}+{\lambda}\sigma_{0}\right)\zeta,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
e_{1}=(1,0)^{T}
.
As in
[
8
, Theorem 5.7(i)]
, it is possible to show that

(i)

\psi_{n}\in\dom S_{\tau}
,

(ii)

\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=c>0
for all
n
with a constant
c
independent of
n
,

(iii)

\psi_{n}
converges weakly to zero and,

(iv)

(S_{\tau}-{\lambda})\psi_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

The sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is thus a Weyl sequence for
{\lambda}
and any self-adjoint extension
A_{\tau,z}
of
S_{\tau}
, which implies
(-\infty,-m)\cup(m,\infty)\subset\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})
. Since the essential spectrum of any self-adjoint operator is always closed, the result follows.
∎

Proposition
5.2
can now be combined with Theorem
3.2
to characterize the essential spectrum for any positive interaction strength
\tau
such that
\tau\neq 2
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\tau\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
and
A_{\tau,z}
be any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
, defined in (
1.3
). Then
\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(A_{\tau,z})=(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
.

Proof.

Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
immediately imply that
[m^{2},\infty)\subset\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})
. Moreover, Theorem
3.2
says that for any
u\in\dom(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})

\begin{split}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau}u,u)_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}&=\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=\|\gradient u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\frac{2m}{\tau}\big\|u_{+}|_{\Gamma}-u_{-}|_{\Gamma}\big\|^{2}_{\Gamma}\geq m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\end{split}

which yields the reverse inclusion
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})\subset[m^{2},\infty)
. Hence,
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})=[m^{2},\infty)
. Proposition
5.1
says that
S^{*}_{\tau}S_{\tau}
and
(A_{\tau,z})^{2}
differ by a finite-rank perturbation and so their essential spectra are equal. Corollary
4.2
further shows that the essential spectrum of
A_{\tau,z}
is symmetric about
0
and the result follows.
∎

We now turn our attention to the case where
\tau
is negative such that
\tau\neq-2
. The corresponding claim about the essential spectrum of any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
from Theorem
1.1
will be shown in three steps. Begin by setting
\varepsilon_{\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=m\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}
. First, a Neumann bracketing argument is used to prove that
[\varepsilon_{\tau}^{2},\infty)\subset\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})
. The reverse inclusion is contained in Proposition
5.5
and uses a particular Weyl sequence construction. The final statements then follow by a similar argument as the proof of Corollary
5.3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})\subset\Bigg[m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2},\infty\Bigg).

Proof.

As indicated in Figure
2
, we define for a fixed
\gamma>0
the following domains
\Omega_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

:

\begin{split}\Omega_{1}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sin\omega}+r\cos\varphi,r\sin\varphi\right)\mathchar 58\relax r>0,\varphi\in(-\omega,\omega)\right\},\\
\Omega_{2}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left(-\frac{\gamma}{\sin\omega}+r\cos\varphi,r\sin\varphi\right)\mathchar 58\relax r>0,\varphi\in(\omega,2\pi-\omega)\right\},\\

\Omega_{4}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left(r\cos\omega+s\sin\omega,-r\sin\omega+s\cos\omega\right)\mathchar 58\relax r>\frac{\gamma}{\tan\omega},s\in(-\gamma,\gamma)\right\},\\
\Omega_{5}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\big(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{4}\big)}.\end{split}

In each of the regions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{j}[u]

\displaystyle=\|\gradient u\|^{2}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\Omega_{j}}}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{{}_{\scriptstyle\Omega_{j}}},\quad\dom\mathfrak{b}_{j}=H^{1}(\Omega_{j};\mathbb{C}^{2}),

and for

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{j}\setminus\Gamma_{j};\mathbb{C}^{2})~\mathchar 58\relax~u_{-}|_{\Gamma_{j}}=Mu_{+}|_{\Gamma_{j}}\big\},

where functions

are written with the notation

. The quadratic forms

and

(5.2)

\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})\geq\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T).

Begin by observing that

and

are shifted Neumann Laplacians in

\Omega_{2}

(5.3)

B_{1},B_{2}\geq m^{2}.

Moreover, as
\Omega_{5}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
\dom\mathfrak{b}_{5}
is compactly embedded in

and thus,

(5.4)

\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(B_{5})=\emptyset.

It therefore remains to estimate
\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(B_{j})
,
j\in\{3,4\}
. Let
\Omega_{\gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(-\gamma,\gamma)
and

\displaystyle M_{j}=\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\sigma_{0}+\frac{4\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\nu_{j}),\qquad\nu_{j}=\nu\upharpoonright(\Gamma\cap\Omega_{j}).

Define
\widetilde{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{0\}
.
Then, a rotation by
\pm\omega

(-\frac{\gamma}{\tan\omega},0)

\Omega_{3}

shows that

,
j\in\{3,4\}
, is unitarily equivalent to a self-adjoint operator

associated with the closed and semibounded quadratic form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{j}[u]

\displaystyle=\|\gradient u\|^{2}_{\Omega_{\gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega_{\gamma}}+\frac{2m}{\tau}\big\|u_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}-u_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\big\|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{2},

\displaystyle\dom\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma};\mathbb{C}^{2})\colon u_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=M_{j}u_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\big\},

where

\Theta_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sigma_{0}+i(\sigma\cdot\nu_{j}))

We define for

and note that this form is independent of

and explicitly given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}[u]

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{\Omega_{\gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}}+m^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega_{\gamma}}+\frac{2m}{\tau}\big\|u_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}-u_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\big\|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{2},

\displaystyle\dom\mathfrak{c}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma};\mathbb{C}^{2})\colon u_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=\widetilde{M}u_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\right\}.

Since
\Theta_{j}
is unitary, we have for
j\in\{3,4\}

(5.6)

\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(B_{j})\geq\inf\left\{\frac{\mathfrak{c}[u]}{\|u\|_{\Omega_{\gamma}}^{2}}\colon u\in\dom\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

Let
u\in\dom\mathfrak{c}
. Then, it follows in a similar way as in the proof of
[
26
, Lemma 4.13]
, cf. also Appendix
B
for details, for almost every
x>0
that

(5.7)

\begin{split}\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}&\big(|\partial_{x}u(x,y)|^{2}+|\partial_{y}u(x,y)|^{2}\big)dy+m^{2}\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|u(x,y)|^{2}dy+\frac{2m}{\tau}|u(x,0^{+})-u(x,0^{-})|^{2}\\
&\geq\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}|\partial_{y}u(x,y)|^{2}dy+m^{2}\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|u(x,y)|^{2}dy+\frac{2m}{\tau}|u(x,0^{+})-u(x,0^{-})|^{2}\\
&\geq E(\gamma)\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|u(x,y)|^{2}dy,\end{split}

where
E(\gamma)\in[0,m^{2})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}E(\gamma)=m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2}.

This implies that

\begin{split}\mathfrak{c}[u]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bigg(\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\big(|\partial_{x}u(x,y)|^{2}+|\partial_{y}u(x,y)|^{2}\big)dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+m^{2}\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|u(x,y)|^{2}dy+\frac{2m}{\tau}|u(x,0^{+})-u(x,0^{-})|^{2}\bigg)dx\\
&\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}E(\gamma)\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|u(x,y)|^{2}dydx.\end{split}

Therefore, equations (

)–(

) and (

) imply that with

we have
\inf\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})\geq m^{2}\big(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\big)^{2}
.
∎

We now prove the opposite inclusion.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\}
. Then

\displaystyle\Bigg[m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2},\infty\Bigg)\subset\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau}).

Proof.

Set
z=-m\frac{4\tau}{\tau^{2}+4}
,
x_{n}=n^{2}+K
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and some
K>0
,
\lambda>0
, and choose
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi(r)=1
for
|r|\leq 1/2
and
\chi(r)=0
for
|r|>1
. Define the function

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i{\lambda}\xi}v(\zeta),

\displaystyle\text{ with }\quad v(\zeta)=\begin{cases}ae^{-z\zeta},&\zeta>0,\\
M_{l}ae^{z\zeta},&\zeta<0,\end{cases}\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\xi\\
\zeta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega&\sin\omega\\
\sin\omega&-\cos\omega\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix},

where
0\neq a\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
M_{l}
is the matrix in (
2.4
) and
c>0
is chosen so that

\supp(\psi_{n})\cap\Gamma_{r}=\emptyset
. We will show that

(i)

\psi_{n}\in\dom S_{\tau}
for all
n
,

(ii)

c_{1}\leq\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq c_{2}
for constants
c_{2}\geq c_{1}>0
,

(iii)

\psi_{n}\perp\psi_{m}
for
m\neq n
, so that
\psi_{n}
converges weakly to zero and,

(iv)

there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that for any
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\left(S_{\tau}+\sqrt{m^{2}-z^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}}\right)u,\left(S_{\tau}-\sqrt{m^{2}-z^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}}\right)\psi_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{c_{3}}{n}\left(\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

Combined, these facts show that
\psi_{n}
satisfies Weyl’s criterion for quadratic forms in the sense of
[
27
, Appendix]
and
m^{2}-z^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}=m^{2}\big(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\big)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\in\sigma_{\scriptstyle\text{\rm ess}}(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})
. The choice of
{\lambda}>0
was arbitrary and the essential spectrum is always closed, so the result follows.

It remains to show claims (i)–(iv) above. Item (i) is clear by construction. To see claim (ii), we compute

\begin{split}\|\psi_{n}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{n}\left|\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)\right|^{2}|v(\zeta)|^{2}dxdy\\
&=\int_{-1}^{1}|\chi(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}\int_{-n}^{n}\left|\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)\right|^{2}|v(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta,\end{split}

where the substitution
x^{\prime}=\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}
was done. The properties of the function
v
then imply that claim (ii) is true.

In light of claim (ii), it is easy to see that claim (iii) holds true, as the functions
\psi_{n}
and
\psi_{m}
for
n\neq m
have disjoint supports.

Claim (iv) makes use of Theorem
3.2
and the transformation from the variables
(x,y)
to
(\xi,\zeta)
. Let
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
and set
\tilde{u}(\xi,\zeta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u(x(\xi,\zeta),y(\xi,\zeta))
,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}(\xi,\zeta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\psi_{n}(x(\xi,\zeta),y(\xi,\zeta))
, and
\widetilde{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{0\}
. These facts allow for the definition

\begin{split}I\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigg\langle&\left(S_{\tau}+\sqrt{m^{2}-z^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}}\right)u,\left(S_{\tau}-\sqrt{m^{2}-z^{2}+{\lambda}^{2}}\right)\psi_{n}\bigg\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=\left(z^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right)(\tilde{u},\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\\
&+(\gradient\tilde{u},\gradient\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}}+\frac{2m}{\tau}\int_{x_{n}-n}^{x_{n}+n}(\tilde{u}_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)-\tilde{u}_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi))\overline{(\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)-\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi))}d\xi.\end{split}

Note that
u,\psi_{n}\in\dom S_{\tau}
implies
\tilde{u}_{-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)=M_{l}\tilde{u}_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)
,
\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)=M_{l}\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)
and
M_{l}=M_{l}^{*}
. Integration by parts then yields

(5.8)

\begin{split}I&=(\tilde{u},-\Delta\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}}+\left(z^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right)(\tilde{u},\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\\
&+\int_{x_{n}-n}^{x_{n}+n}\tilde{u}_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)\overline{\left(\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-M_{l})^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)+\left(M_{l}\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}}{\partial\zeta}\bigg|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)-\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}}{\partial\zeta}\bigg|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)\right)\right)}d\xi.\end{split}

We then calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}}{\partial\zeta}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{n^{3/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}ae^{-z\zeta}

\displaystyle\hskip 120.00018pt-\frac{z}{n^{1/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}ae^{-z\zeta},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}}{\partial\zeta}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{n^{3/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}M_{l}ae^{z\zeta}

\displaystyle\hskip 120.00018pt+\frac{z}{n^{1/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}M_{l}ae^{z\zeta}.

Using that
\chi(0)=1
and
\chi^{\prime}(0)=0
we infer

\begin{split}M_{l}\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}}{\partial\zeta}\big|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)-\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}}{\partial\zeta}\big|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)=\frac{z}{n^{1/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}(M_{l}^{2}+\sigma_{0})a=z(M_{l}^{2}+\sigma_{0})\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi).\end{split}

A direct calculation shows that
z(M_{l}^{2}+\sigma_{0})=-\frac{8m\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}M_{l}
and
\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-M_{l})^{2}=\frac{8m\tau}{4-\tau^{2}}M_{l}
. Hence,
we obtain that

(5.9)

\int_{x_{n}-n}^{x_{n}+n}\tilde{u}_{+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)\overline{\left(\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-M_{l})^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)+\left(M_{l}\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,-}}{\partial\zeta}\bigg|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)-\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}_{n,+}}{\partial\zeta}\bigg|_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}(\xi)\right)\right)}d\xi=0.

Returning to the analysis of other terms in
I
, we calculate

\begin{split}\Delta\tilde{\psi}_{n}(\xi,\zeta)&=(z^{2}-\lambda^{2})\tilde{\psi}_{n}(\xi,\zeta)+\frac{1}{n^{5/2}}\chi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)\\
&\quad+\frac{2i\lambda}{n^{3/2}}\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)+\frac{c^{2}}{n^{5/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)\\
&\quad+\frac{2c}{n^{3/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v^{\prime}(\zeta).\end{split}

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

(5.10)

\begin{split}\bigg|&(\tilde{u},-\Delta\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}}+\left(z^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right)(\tilde{u},\tilde{\psi}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigg|\\
&=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{u}(\xi,\zeta)\overline{\bigg(\frac{1}{n^{5/2}}\chi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)+\frac{2i\lambda}{n^{3/2}}\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)}\\
&\quad\qquad\overline{+\frac{c^{2}}{n^{5/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v(\zeta)+\frac{2c}{n^{3/2}}\chi\left(\frac{\xi-x_{n}}{n}\right)\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{c\zeta}{n}\right)e^{i\lambda\xi}v^{\prime}(\zeta)\bigg)}d\xi d\zeta\bigg|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{n}\|u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}.\end{split}

The absolute value of the expression
I
in equation (
5.8
) can thus be estimated by equations (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) and we have

(5.11)

|I|\leq\frac{C}{n}\|u\|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{n}\left(\|S_{\tau}u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

which is claim (iv). The proof of the proposition is therefore complete.
∎

) in Theorem

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\tau\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
,
\omega\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, and let
A_{\tau,z}
be any self-adjoint extension of
S_{\tau}
in (

Proof.

First, Proposition
5.1
, Corollary
5.3
, and Theorem
3.2
imply that
\sigma(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau})=[m^{2},\infty)
. By Corollary
5.3
one has
\sigma_{\rm ess}(A_{\tau,z})=(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
. Recall that by Theorem
2.2
the deficiency indices of
S_{\tau}
are
(1,1)
. Hence, by Proposition
5.1

the resolvent difference

(S_{\tau}^{*}S_{\tau}+1)^{-1}-\left[(A_{\tau,z})^{2}+1\right]^{-1}
is a rank-one operator. The perturbation result
[
13
, §9.3, Theorem 3]
implies that the dimension of the spectral subspace of the self-adjoint operator
(A_{\tau,z})^{2}
corresponding to the interval
(0,m^{2})
is at most one. As the spectral subspace for

Remark 6.2

As a consequence of the above proposition and of the symmetry of the spectrum of the distinguished self-adjoint extension

We remark that the angle

Proposition 6.3

\displaystyle\kappa(\tau)=a^{2}+b^{2}=\frac{(4+\tau^{2})^{2}+16\tau^{2}}{(4-\tau^{2})^{2}},

. We define three disjoint subregions of

\displaystyle\Pi_{L,1}^{+}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{L}^{+}\cap\Omega_{+},

\displaystyle\Pi_{L,2}^{+}

\displaystyle\Pi_{L,3}^{+}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{L}^{+}\cap\{(x,y)\in\Omega^{l}_{-}\colon y>2d\}.

\overline{\Pi_{L}^{+}}=\overline{\cup_{j=1}^{3}\Pi_{L,j}^{+}}
.

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}

has at least

has the same lower bound on the number of discrete eigenvalues with multiplicities taken into account.
∎

The maximum in Proposition

\displaystyle F(\tau)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N^{2}\pi^{2}(4+\tau^{2})^{2}(16\tau^{2}+(4+\tau^{2})^{2}),\qquad G(\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[(4-\tau^{2})^{2}+(4+\tau^{2})^{2}]4|\tau|(4+\tau^{2}),

, the anti-commutation relations in (
1.6
) imply that
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot(\widetilde{\nu}_{r}-\widetilde{\nu}_{l}))
is invertible and hence
f(0)
must be
0
, i.e.
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Moreover, the above calculation shows (
A.3
) for
f\in H^{1}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

On the other hand, a similar consideration as above yields for
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
that
f\in H^{1}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and that (
A.3
) holds in this case as well. Eventually, since
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})
is pointwise unitary, it follows from (
A.3
) that the norms in
H^{1}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
are equivalent.

Finally, as the multiplication by
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})
gives rise to a bounded map in both spaces
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
by items (ii) and (i), respectively, it follows by interpolation that the map in (
A.4
) is well defined and bounded.
∎

In the next lemma we characterize
H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. For this purpose, recall that the space
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
is defined by

H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})=\left\{f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\mathchar 58\relax\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{|x|}dx<\infty\right\}

and that this space, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{|x|}dx,\qquad f\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2}),

is a Hilbert space. We are going to use

(A.5)

H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})=\big[H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\big]_{1/2},

cf.
[
30
, Chapter I, Theorem 11.7 and Remark 11.5]
. In particular, by elementary properties of interpolation spaces this implies that
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
is dense in
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
be defined by (
A.2
). Then,
H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})=H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

First, it follows from (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) that
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})\subset H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. To show the converse inclusion, let
f\in H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Set
\widetilde{\nu}_{r}=\widetilde{\nu}(-1)
and
\widetilde{\nu}_{l}=\widetilde{\nu}(1)
. Then, as
\widetilde{\nu}_{r}
is constant,
f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
implies that
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{r})f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and thus also

i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot(\widetilde{\nu}-\widetilde{\nu}_{r}))f=i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot(\widetilde{\nu}_{l}-\widetilde{\nu}_{r}))\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2}),

where
\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
denotes the characteristic function for
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Since the anti-commutation relations in (
1.6
) imply that
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot(\widetilde{\nu}_{l}-\widetilde{\nu}_{r}))
is invertible, we conclude that
\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Making use of the equivalent Sobolev-Slobodeckii norm in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
, this implies that

\begin{split}\infty&>\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|(\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f)(x)-(\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f)(y)|^{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}dydx\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{(y-x)^{2}}dydx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{x}dx.\end{split}

In a similar way as above, the relations
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{l})f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
imply that
\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and

\begin{split}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{x}dx<\infty.\end{split}

Hence,
f\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
, which finishes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Now, we are prepared to prove Proposition
A.1
:

Proof of Proposition
A.1
.

Let
f,g\in H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Since
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
is dense in the space
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})=H^{1/2}_{\widetilde{\nu}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
such that

\|f_{n}-f\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Taking the definition of the norm in
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
into account this implies, in particular, that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and thus, also
f_{n}^{\prime}\rightarrow f^{\prime}
in
H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Next, as the multiplication by
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})
gives rise to a bounded operator in
H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
by (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), we get that

\big\|i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})(f_{n}-f)\big\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\leq\big\|i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})(f_{n}-f)\big\|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

In particular,
i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f_{n}\rightarrow i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
(i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f_{n})^{\prime}\rightarrow(i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f)^{\prime}
in
H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
Hence, using (
A.3
), we conclude

\begin{split}\big\langle(i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f)^{\prime},g&\big\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big\langle(i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f_{n})^{\prime},g\big\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})f_{n}^{\prime}\overline{g}dx=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{n}^{\prime}\overline{i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})g}dx\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big\langle f_{n}^{\prime},i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})g\big\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}\\
&=\big\langle f^{\prime},i\sigma_{3}(\sigma\cdot\widetilde{\nu})g\big\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})},\end{split}

which is the claimed identity.
∎

Remark A.4
.

The property
u_{\pm}|_{\Gamma}\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})
for any
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
immediately follows from the trace theorem. However, the construction in this appendix obtains the stronger statement that
(u_{\pm}|_{\Gamma}\circ\gamma)\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})
for any
u\in\dom S_{\tau}
as a by-product. As before we identify
\Gamma
with
\mathbb{R}
here in such a way that the origin of the real axis coincides with the corner of the broken line.

Appendix B
Bound for a one-dimensional auxiliary problem

Define for
\gamma>0
and
\tau\in(-\infty,0)\setminus\{-2\}
in
L^{2}((-\gamma,\gamma);\mathbb{C}^{2})
the semibounded and closed quadratic form
\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
by

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}[f]&=\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx+m^{2}\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\frac{2m}{\tau}|f(0^{+})-f(0^{-})|^{2},\\
\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}&=\big\{u\in H^{1}((-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})\colon f(0^{-})=\widetilde{M}f(0^{+})\big\},\end{split}

where
\widetilde{M}
is given by (
5.5
).
This appendix is devoted to the proof that for all
f\in\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
one has the bound

(B.1)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}[f]\geq E(\gamma)\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)}|f(x)|^{2}dx\end{split}

with
E(\gamma)\in[0,m^{2})
such that

(B.2)

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}E(\gamma)=m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2},

which is used in (
5.7
) in the proof of Proposition
5.4
. To verify (
B.1
) we follow closely the proof of the very similar result
[
26
, Lemma 4.13]
, but since the situation in
[
26
]
is slightly different from ours and for completeness we give a full proof here.

The strategy to show (
B.1
)–(
B.2
) is to determine the self-adjoint operator
D_{\gamma}
that is associated with
\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
via Kato’s first representation theorem and to estimate its spectrum. As
\dom D_{\gamma}\subset\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
is compactly embedded in
L^{2}((-\gamma,\gamma);\mathbb{C}^{2})
, its spectrum is purely discrete and hence, it suffices to estimate the smallest eigenvalue
E(\gamma)
of
D_{\gamma}
. Since one has
C_{0}^{\infty}((-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})\subset\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
, one finds that
\dom D_{\gamma}\subset H^{2}((-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})\cap\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
and that the action of
D_{\gamma}
is
D_{\gamma}f=-f^{\prime\prime}+m^{2}f
in
(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}
for
f\in\dom D_{\gamma}
. Moreover, using integration by parts, one gets for any
f\in\dom D_{\gamma}
and any
g\in\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}[f,g]&=\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}(-f^{\prime\prime}+m^{2}f)\overline{g}dx+f^{\prime}(\gamma)\overline{g(\gamma)}-f^{\prime}(0^{+})\overline{g(0^{+})}\\
&\qquad+f^{\prime}(0^{-})\overline{g(0^{-})}-f^{\prime}(-\gamma)\overline{g(-\gamma)}+\frac{2m}{\tau}(f(0^{+})-f(0^{-}))\overline{(g(0^{+})-g(0^{-}))}\\
&=\int_{(-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\}}D_{\gamma}f\overline{g}dx+f^{\prime}(\gamma)\overline{g(\gamma)}-f^{\prime}(-\gamma)\overline{g(-\gamma)}\\
&\qquad+\left[\widetilde{M}f^{\prime}(0^{-})-f^{\prime}(0^{+})+\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-\widetilde{M})^{2}f(0^{+})\right]\overline{g(0^{+})},\end{split}

where in the second step the transmission condition for
f,g\in\dom\mathfrak{d}_{\gamma}
was taken into account. Thus, we conclude that

\begin{split}\dom D_{\gamma}=\bigg\{f\in H^{2}((-\gamma,\gamma)\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C}^{2})&\colon f(0^{-})=\widetilde{M}f(0^{+}),f^{\prime}(\gamma)=f^{\prime}(-\gamma)=0,\\
&\qquad\widetilde{M}f^{\prime}(0^{-})-f^{\prime}(0^{+})+\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-\widetilde{M})^{2}f(0^{+})=0\bigg\}.\end{split}

To compute the smallest eigenvalue
E=E(\gamma)
of
D_{\gamma}
, we set
k=\sqrt{m^{2}-E}
and note first that a corresponding eigenfunction
f_{E}
must be of the form

f_{E}(x)=\begin{cases}a_{+}e^{-kx}+b_{+}e^{kx},&x\in(0,\gamma),\\
a_{-}e^{kx}+b_{-}e^{-kx},&x\in(-\gamma,0),\end{cases}

for some
a_{\pm},b_{\pm}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and it has to fulfil the conditions for
f\in\dom D_{\gamma}
. From the condition
f_{E}^{\prime}(\pm\gamma)=0
we conclude that
a_{\pm}=b_{\pm}e^{2k\gamma}
. Taking this into account, one finds that
f_{E}(0^{-})=\widetilde{M}f_{E}(0^{+})
yields
b_{-}=\widetilde{M}b_{+}
. Hence, the last condition for
f_{E}\in\dom D_{\gamma}
simplifies to

(B.3)

\begin{split}0&=\widetilde{M}f^{\prime}_{E}(0^{-})-f^{\prime}_{E}(0^{+})+\frac{2m}{\tau}(\sigma_{0}-\widetilde{M})^{2}f_{E}(0^{+})\\
&=k(e^{2k\gamma}-1)(\widetilde{M}^{2}+\sigma_{0})b_{+}+\frac{2m}{\tau}(e^{2k\gamma}+1)(\sigma_{0}-\widetilde{M})^{2}b_{+}.\end{split}

By the definition of
\widetilde{M}
one finds with a direct calculation that
\widetilde{M}^{2}+\sigma_{0}=2\frac{4+\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\widetilde{M}
and
(\sigma_{0}-\widetilde{M})^{2}=\frac{4\tau^{2}}{4-\tau^{2}}\widetilde{M}
. Since
\widetilde{M}
is invertible, we find that (
B.3
) is equivalent to

(B.4)

-\frac{4m\tau}{4+\tau^{2}}=k\frac{e^{k\gamma}-e^{-k\gamma}}{e^{k\gamma}+e^{-k\gamma}}=k\tanh(k \gamma)=\mathchar 58\relax F_{\gamma}(k).

Since

F_{\gamma}^{\prime}(k)=\tanh(k \gamma)+\frac{k\gamma}{\cosh^{2}(k\gamma)}>0\quad\text{for}\quad k>0,

\lim_{k\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(k\gamma)=0
, and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}F(k\gamma)=\infty
, we find that
F_{\gamma}\mathchar 58\relax(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is bijective. Hence, the equation (
B.4
) has exactly one solution
k_{\gamma}>0
, which is associated with the smallest eigenvalue of
D_{\gamma}
; we remark that (
B.4
) has infinitely many solutions
k
of the form
k=ix
with
x\in\mathbb{R}
, which are, due to
k=\sqrt{m^{2}-E}
, associated with eigenvalues of
D_{\gamma}
that are larger than
m
.

Finally, to compute the limit of the solution
k_{\gamma}
of (
B.4
) for
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
, we note first that
\tanh(x)<1
for all
x>0
and (
B.4
) yield that
k_{\gamma}>-\frac{4m\tau}{4+\tau^{2}}
for all
\gamma>0
. Thus,
\gamma k_{\gamma}\rightarrow\infty
, as
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
. By using now once more that
k_{\gamma}
satisfies (
B.4
), we obtain

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}k_{\gamma}=\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left(-\frac{4m\tau}{4+\tau^{2}}\frac{1}{\tanh(k_\gamma\gamma)}\right)=-\frac{4m\tau}{4+\tau^{2}}.

Hence, we conclude for the smallest eigenvalue
E(\gamma)
of
D_{\gamma}
via the relation
k_{\gamma}=\sqrt{m^{2}-E(\gamma)}
that

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}E(\gamma)=\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}(m^{2}-k_{\gamma}^{2})=m^{2}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-4}{\tau^{2}+4}\right)^{2},

which is exactly the claim in (
B.2
).

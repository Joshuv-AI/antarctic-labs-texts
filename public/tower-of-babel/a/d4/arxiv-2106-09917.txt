Envy-freeness and Relaxed Stability for Lower-Quotas : A Parameterized Perspective
Girija Limaye

Abstract
We consider the problem of assigning agents to resources under the two-sided preference list model where resources specify an upper-quota and a lower-quota, that is, respectively the maximum and minimum number of agents that can be assigned to it. Different notions of optimality including envy-freeness and relaxed stability are investigated for this setting and the goal is to compute a largest optimal matching. Krishnaa et al. [ 13 ] show that in this setting, the problem of computing a maximum size envy-free matching ( MAXEFM ) or a maximum size relaxed stable matching ( MAXRSM ) is not approximable within a certain constant factor unless 𝖯 = 𝖭𝖯 \sf P=\sf NP . This work is the first investigation of parameterized complexity of MAXEFM and MAXRSM . We show that MAXEFM is W ⁡ [ 1 ] W[1] -hard and MAXRSM is para- 𝖭𝖯 \sf NP -hard when parameterized on several natural parameters derived from the instance. We present kernelization results and FPT algorithms for both problems parameterized on other relevant parameters.

Computing optimal matchings under the two-sided preference list setting is a well-studied problem.

A

Several practical applications naturally impose a

We begin by presenting
an algorithm (
{\sf EXTEND}
) that computes an

extension
of a given
minimal feasible
matching.
A matching is minimal feasible if it is feasible and deleting an edge results in an infeasible matching.
Matching
M^{\prime}
is an extension of

if
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
.
Given a minimal feasible matching
M
in
G
,
our algorithm computes an extension
M^{\prime}

such that if
M^{\prime}
is envy-free in
G
then
M^{\prime}
is a maximum size envy-free matching containing
M
in
G
.
We use the notion of

which is similar to the
threshold resident
as defined in
[
15
]
.
Given a matching

t(b)
of an unmatched resource

is defined as follows.

Definition 6
(Threshold agent)

Threshold agent of an unmatched resource

is
the most-preferred agent
a
in the preference list of
b
such that
a
is matched in
M

and
b\succ_{a}M(a)
, if such agent exists. Otherwise, we let a unique dummy agent

at the end of

preference list to be
t(b)
.

The
{\sf EXTEND}
algorithm (Algorithm
1
) takes a minimal feasible matching
M
in
G

as input. It starts by computing the threshold agents of unmatched resources.
Then it constructs a sub-graph
G^{\prime}
induced by the unmatched resources and
unmatched agents in
M
that the resource prefers over its threshold agent.
For every resource
b
in
G^{\prime}
,
q^{+}(b)
is set to 1.
For every vertex (agent and resource) in
G^{\prime}
its preference list is derived using the preference ordering
same as that in
G
restricted to its neighbors in
G^{\prime}
.
Then the algorithm computes the
agent-optimal
stable matching
M_{s}
in
G^{\prime}
.
The unique agent-optimal stable matching
M_{s}
is obtained
by the agent-proposing Gale and Shapley algorithm
and has the following property – if
N_{s}
is a stable matching in
the instance then for every agent, either
M_{s}(a)=N_{s}(a)
or
M_{s}(a)\succ_{a}N_{s}(a)

[
10
]
.
Finally, it returns
M^{\prime}=M_{s}\cup M
.

The algorithm
{\sf EXTEND}
is similar to Algorithm 2 from
[
15
]

with the following subtle differences.
The initial matching in the algorithm from

]

[

)

Also,

unlike any stable matching computed in the algorithm in
[

]

We use

to design an

many different assignments.

and

(q,\ell_{LQ})

Let

restricted to the

Suppose there exists a matched agent

Next we show that

Then there exist agents

such that

envies

in
M_{e}^{\prime}

Then

is matched in
M_{e}^{\prime}
.
First suppose that
a^{\prime}
is matched in
M_{e}
.
By the choice of

, a matched agent in

does not envy another matched agent in
M_{e}
,

is not matched in
M_{e}
.
Thus,

is either unmatched in
M_{s}
(computed by
{\sf EXTEND}
) or

is matched in

such that

.

Let

.
As discussed earlier,

.
If

is a stable matching in

computed in
{\sf EXTEND}
then,
by the agent-optimality of

,
M_{s}(a)=N(a)

.
In either case,

.
Thus,

envies
a^{\prime}
in

, a contradiction.
If

(a_{1},b_{1})

such that

is unmatched in

.

For the analysis purpose, suppose we run a resource-proposing round
[

]
in

such that all the unmatched resources in

. During this step, if a resource is unmatched for the first time,

be the matching computed by this step. Then,

is stable in

.
By the property of resource-proposing round, either

Now, due to the agent-optimality of

Next, suppose that

For the

.

1

For each assignment
M_{e}
of agents to

-

-

-

.

.

Among all matchings

computed for different assignments

, output the largest size matching.

{\sf ALG}_{rsm}

and

The running time analysis is similar to that for

.

, constructing

Suppose

Let

Our algorithm considers this assignment

blocks relaxed stability of

5
Concluding Remarks

An instance with lower-quotas may not admit a stable, feasible matching and therefore
a relaxation of stability is investigated.
Various optimality notions for lower-quota setting are studied
[
11
,
22
,
6
,
19
]
and
parameterized complexity for the notion studied in
[
11
]
is investigated in
[
18
]
.
In this work, we investigate the notion of envy-freeness and relaxed stability
in presence of lower-quotas.
On one hand, envy-freeness is a natural relaxation of stability that respects
the preferences of agents and resources to the
best possible extent
but an instance may not
admit a feasible, envy-free matching.
On the other hand, an instance always admits a feasible, relaxed stable matching.
Krishnaa et al.
[
13
]
show that computing a maximum size feasible,
envy-free or relaxed stable matching is computationally hard.
Prior to this work, no results about parameterized complexity of computing a largest feasible envy-free or
relaxed stable matching were known.

We present our kernelization and
FPT
results for
ONE
-
ONE
-
LQ
instances.
These results are theoretically challenging as well as practically appealing.
A standard technique of
cloning

[

,

]

can be used to efficiently convert a

-

-
LQ
instance to a
ONE
-

-

.
We observe that the parameters

Thus, our kernelization for

(in Theorem

) and

FPT

)
are also applicable for the

We also remark that algorithm

can be appropriately modified
to handle arbitrary quotas in the

-

Using the modified

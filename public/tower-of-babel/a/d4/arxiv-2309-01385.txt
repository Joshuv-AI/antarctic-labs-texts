Maurer-Cartan type cohomology on generalized Reynolds operators and NS-structures on Lie triple systems
Rahma Gharbi , Sami Mabrouk and Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 18, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to introduce and study the notion of generalized Reynolds operators on Lie triple systems with representations (Abbr. L.t.sRep pairs) as generalization of weighted Reynolds operators on Lie triple systems. First, We construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are generalized Reynolds operators. This allows us to define a Yamaguti cohomology of a generalized Reynolds operator. This cohomology can be seen as the Yamaguti cohomology of a certain Lie triple system with coefficients in a suitable representation. Next, we study deformations of generalized Reynolds operators from cohomological points of view and we investigate the obstruction class of an extendable deformation of order n n . We end this paper by introducing a new algebraic structure, in connection with generalized Reynolds operator, called NS-Lie triple system. Moreover, we show that NS-Lie triple systems can be derived from NS-Lie algebras.



Key words
: Lie triple system, generalized Reynolds operator, Maurer-Cartan element,

L_{\infty}
-algebra, Lie-Yamaguti cohomology, deformation, NS-Lie triple system.

Introduction

The concept of Lie triple system was introduced first by Jacobson
[
15
]
and the present formulation is due to Yamguti
[
29
]
. Moreover, it appeared in Cartan’s work on Riemannian Geometry
[
2
]
and was strongly developed for Symmetric spaces and related spaces. Indeed, the tangent space of a symmetric space is a Lie triple system. It turns out that they have important applications in physics, in particular, in elementary particle theory and the theory of quantum mechanics, as well as numerical analysis of differential equations. They have become an interesting subject in mathematics, their structure have been studied first by Lister in
[
21
]
.

The notion of a Rota-Baxter operator on an associative algebra appeared first in probability theory when G. Baxter studied algebraically the Spitzer identity. The theory was developed first by Rota in the combinatorics field. Later relevant application was considered in the Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization of quantum field theory
[
5
]
. For further details, see
[
11
,
12
]
. A Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra is naturally the operator form of a classical
r
-matrix
[
25
]
under certain conditions. To better understand such connection in general, Kupershmidt introduced the notion of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator (also called
a relative Rota-Baxter operator
[
24
]
or a generalized Rota-Baxter operator
[
28
]
)
on a Lie algebra in
[
18
]
. Recently a deformation theory and a cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on both Lie and associative algebras are studied in
[
7
,
27
]
. Reynolds operators were introduced by Reynolds in
[
23
]
in the study of fluctuation theory in fluid dynamics.
In
[
16
]
, the author coined the concept of the Reynolds operator and regarded the operator as a mathematical subject in general. In
[
31
]
, the authors provided examples and properties of the Reynolds operators and studied the free Reynolds algebras.

NS-algebras were introduced by Leroux in
[
20
]
. In
[
19
]
, the authors studied the relationship between the category of Nijenhuis algebras and the category of NS-algebras. Uchino defined the notion of generalized Reynolds operators on associative algebras and observed that a generalized Reynolds operator induces an NS-algebra in
[
28
]
. In
[
9
]
, Das defined a cohomology of generalized Reynolds operators on associative algebras and a cohomology of NS-algebras, various applications were provided. He also introduced the notions of generalized Reynolds operators, Reynolds operators on Lie algebras and NS-Lie algebras, and showed that NS-Lie algebras are the underlying algebraic structures of generalized Reynolds operators on Lie algebras in
[
8
]
.

Inspired by these works, we aim in this paper to study Maurer-Cartan type cohomology on generalized Reynolds operators on Lie triple systems with representations (Abbr. L.t.sRep pairs). Moreover, we introduce the notion of NS-Lie triple system as the underlying structure of generalized Reynolds operators.

This is paper is organized as follow, in Section
2
, we summarize some basics and briefly recall representations and cohomology
of Lie triple systems. In Section
3
, we introduce a generalization of weighted Reynolds operators on a Lie triple system called generalized Reynolds operators on a Lie triple system and we give some constructions. In Section
4
, we construct an

-algebra whose Maurer–Cartan elements are given by generalized Reynolds operators. This motivates us to
define a Yamaguti cohomology of generalized Reynolds operator on Lie triple system using the underlying Lie triple systems of the generalized Reynolds operator. In Section
5

n
to be extended to order
n+1
. Finally, Section
6
deals a new algebraic structure called NS-Lie triple system that is related to generalized Reynolds operators. We show that Lie
algebras, NS-Lie algebras, Lie triple systems and
NS-Lie triple systems are closely related.

In this paper all vector spaces are considered over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic notions as representations and cohomology theory of Lie triple systems (L.t.s) introduced in
[
21
]
. A Lie triple system is a vector space
L
endowed with a ternary bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}L\otimes L\to L
satisfying

\displaystyle[x,y,z]+[y,z,x]+[z,x,y]=0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle[x,y,[z,t,e]]=[[x,y,z],t,e]+[z,[x,y,t],e]+[z,t,[x,y,e]]\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z,t,e\in L.

(2.2)

A morphism
\phi:(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])\rightarrow(L^{{}^{\prime}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{{}^{\prime}})
of L.t.s is a linear map satisfying :

\phi([x,y,z])=[\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)]^{{}^{\prime}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z\in L.

An isomorphism is a bijective morphism.
We set
X=(x,y)\in L\otimes L
and denote by
\mathfrak R(X),\ \mathfrak D(X):L\rightarrow L
maps defined by
\mathfrak R(X)z=[z,x,y]
and
\mathfrak D(X)z=[x,y,z]
. Then the identities (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) can be rewritten respectively
in the form
\mathfrak D(X)=\mathfrak R(\tau(X))-\mathfrak R(X)
, where
\tau(X)=y\otimes x
, and

\mathfrak D(X)[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]=[\mathfrak D(X)z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]+[z_{1},\mathfrak D(X)z_{2},z_{3}]+[z_{1},z_{2},\mathfrak D(X)z_{3}].

This means that
\mathfrak D(X)
is a derivation with respect to the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
.

In
[
29
]
, K. Yamaguti introduced the notion of representation and cohomology theory of L.t.s. Later, the authors in
[
13
,
14
,
17
]
studied the cohomology theory of L.t.s from a different point of view. K. Yamaguti’s
work can be described as follows.

A representation of a L.t.s
L
on a vector space
M
is a bilinear map
\theta:\otimes^{2}L\rightarrow End(M)
, such that the following conditions are satisfied :

\displaystyle\theta(z,t)\theta(x,y)-\theta(y,t)\theta(x,z)-\theta(x,[y,z,t])+D(y,z)\theta(x,t)=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta(z,t)D(x,y)-D(x,y)\theta(z,t)+\theta([x,y,z],t)+\theta(z,[x,y,t])=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z,t\in L,

(2.4)

where
D(x,y)=\theta(y,x)-\theta(x,y)
. It is denoted by a pair
(M,\theta)
. We say that we have a L.t.sRep pair
and refer to it with the tuple

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
.

Remark 2.1
.

1.

It can be concluded from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle D(z,t)D(x,y)-D(x,y)D(z,t)+D([x,y,z],t)+D(z,[x,y,t])=0.

(2.5)

2.

If
M=L
then
\mathfrak R
is a representation of
L
in it self, which is called the regular representation.

Recall that, a representation of a Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
on a vector space
M
is a linear map
\rho:L\to End(M)
satisfying :

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in L.

Note that a LieRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
is a Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
with a representation
\rho
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
be a LieRep pair.
Then
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta_{\rho})
is a L.t.sRep pair, where

[x,y,z]=[[x,y],z]\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\theta_{\rho}(x,y)=\rho(y)\rho(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z\in L.

(2.6)

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
be a L.t.sRep pair. For each
n\geqslant 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{2n+1}(L,M)
, the vector space of
(2n+1)
-cochains of
L
with coefficients in
M
:
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{2n+1}(L,M)
is a multilinear function of
\psi:\times^{2n+1}L\to M
satisfying

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x,x,y)=0,

\displaystyle{}_{x,y,z}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x,y,z)=0,

where
x,y,z
means that we are taking a cyclic summation.

A Yamaguti coboundary operator
\delta^{2n-1}:\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{2n-1}(L,M)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{2n+1}(L,M)
is defined by:

\displaystyle\delta^{2n-1}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x_{2n},x_{2n+1})\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-1})-\theta(x_{2n-1},x_{2n+1})\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n-2},x_{2n})

\displaystyle+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\cdots,x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=2k+1}^{2n+1}(-1)^{n+k+1}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\cdots,[x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}],\cdots,x_{2n+1}),

(2.7)

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{2n-1}(L,M),n\geqslant 1
, where
\;\widehat{}\;
means that the element is omitted.

Proposition 2.2
.

The Yamaguti coboundary operator
\delta^{2n-1}
defined above is a square zero map, i.e.,
\delta^{2n+1}\circ\delta^{2n-1}=0
.
Therefore, the set of Yamaguti cochains forms a complex with this coboundary as follow:

\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)\overset{\delta^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{3}(L,M)\overset{\delta^{3}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{5}(L,M)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Hence we get the
Yamaguti cohomology group
\mathcal{H}_{L.t.s}^{\bullet}(L,M)=\mathcal{Z}_{L.t.s}^{\bullet}(L,M)/\mathcal{B}_{L.t.s}^{\bullet}(L,M)
, where
\mathcal{Z}_{L.t.s}^{\bullet}(L,M)
is the space of
cocycles and
\mathcal{B}_{L.t.s}^{\bullet}(L,M)
is the space of coboundaries. See
[
29
,
30
]
for more details.
A
1
-cochain
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)

is a
1
-cocycle if

D(x_{1},x_{2})\varphi(x_{3})-\theta(x_{1},x_{3})\varphi(x_{2})+\theta(x_{2},x_{3})\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}])=0.

(2.8)

A
3
-cochain
\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{3}(L,M)
is a
3
-coboundary if there exists a map
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)
such that
\mathcal{H}=\delta^{1}\varphi
.
A
3
-coboundary is called a
3
-cocycle if for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in L
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{1},x_{2})=0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+\mathcal{H}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1})+\mathcal{H}(x_{3},x_{1},x_{2})=0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{2},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}])+D(x_{1},x_{2})\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})-\mathcal{H}([x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}],y_{2},y_{3})-\mathcal{H}(y_{1},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}],y_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}])+\theta(y_{2},y_{3})\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1})-\theta(y_{1},y_{3})\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{2})+D(y_{1},y_{2})\mathcal{H}(x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}).

(2.11)

Theorem 2.3
.

[
4
]

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{Lie}(L,M)
. Then
\omega(x,y,z)=\phi([x,y],z)-\rho(z)\phi(x,y)
is a
3
-cocycle of the L.t.sRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta_{\rho})
given in Theorem
2.1
.

3
Generalized Reynolds operators on Lie triple systems with representations

In this section, we introduce the notion of generalized Reynolds operator on L.t.sRep pairs which provides a generalization of weighted Reynolds operators on L.t.s. Also, We give its characterization by a graph and provide some constructions. This notion is an algebraic analogue of generalized Reynolds operator on a Lie algebra introduced in
[
6
]
and on a
3
-Lie algebras introduced in
[
3
,
26
]
.

3.1
Weighted Reynolds operators on L.t.s

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra and
\lambda
be a non-null scalar . A

\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator
on a Lie algebra is a linear map
\mathcal{R}:L\to L
satisfying:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y]=\mathcal{R}\Big([\mathcal{R}x,y]+[x,\mathcal{R}y]+\lambda[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y]\Big)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in L.

(3.1)

Note that
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{R})
is
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds Lie algebra.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\lambda=-1
, we recover the definition of Reynolds operator on a Lie algebra introduced by A. Das in
[
6
]
in the study of generalized Reynolds operators on a Lie algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a L.t.s and
\lambda
be a scalar. A
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator
on a L.t.s is a linear map
\mathcal{R}:L\to L
satisfying:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y,\mathcal{R}z]=\mathcal{R}\Big([\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y,z]+[x,\mathcal{R}y,,\mathcal{R}z]+[\mathcal{R}x,y,\mathcal{R}z]+\lambda[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y,\mathcal{R}z]\Big)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z\in L.

(3.2)

Note that
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{R})
is
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds L.t.s.

Example 3.1
.

For a fixed complex number
q
,
there is a Lie triple system structure analogue to the Lie algebra of Block type given in
[
1
]
, generated by the basis
\{L_{m,i}|m,i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and given by the following bracket

[L_{m,i},L_{n,j},L_{p,k}]=(n(i+q)-m(j+q))(p(i+j+q)-(m+n)(p+q))L_{m+n+p,i+j+k}.

Let
B(q)_{\geq 0}=\{L_{m,i}|m,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
, then the linear map
\mathcal{R}:B(q)_{\geq 0}\to B(q)_{\geq 0}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(L_{m,i})=\frac{-1}{\lambda(m+i+1)}\ L_{m,i}\ \ \ \ \forall m\in B(q)_{\geq 0},\lambda\neq 0

(3.3)

is a
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator on
B(q)_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{R})
and
(L^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{\prime},\mathcal{R^{\prime}})
be two
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds L.t.s. A linear map
\alpha:L\to L^{\prime}
is called
morphism
of
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds L.t.s if
\alpha
is morphism of L.t.s satisfying
\alpha\circ\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R^{\prime}}\circ\alpha.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{R})
be
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds L.t.s. Define
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{R}}
on
L
by

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathcal{R}}=[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y,z]+[\mathcal{R}x,y,\mathcal{R}z]+[x,\mathcal{R}y,\mathcal{R}z]+\lambda[\mathcal{R}x,\mathcal{R}y,\mathcal{R}z]\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y,z\in L.

(3.4)

Then
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{R})
is a
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds L.t.s. Moreover
\mathcal{R}
is a morphism of
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operators L.t.s from
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{R})
to
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{R})
, that is
\mathcal{R}([x,y,z]_{\mathcal{R}})=[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y),\mathcal{R}(z)].

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}:L\to L
be a
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator on a Lie algebra, then
\mathcal{R}
is a
2\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator on the induced L.t.s.

Example 3.4
.

Let
W
be the Witt algebra generated by basis elements
\{l_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and the Lie bracket given by:

\displaystyle[l_{m},l_{n}]=(m-n)l_{m+n}\ \ \ \ \ \forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.5)

By Theorem
2.1
, we obtain a L.t.s defined by the bracket

\displaystyle[l_{m},l_{n},l_{p}]=(m-n)(m+n-p))l_{m+n+p}\ \ \ \ \ \forall m,n,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
W_{\geq 0}=span\{l_{n}|n\geq 0\}
is a L.t.s subalgebra of
W
. The linear map
\mathcal{R}:W_{\geq 0}\to W_{\geq 0}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(l_{m})=\frac{-1}{\lambda(m+1)}\ l_{m}\ \ \ \ \forall m\in W_{\geq 0}\ and\ \lambda\neq 0

(3.6)

is a

\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator on the Lie algebra
W_{\geq 0}
. Then, according to Proposition
3.3

\mathcal{R}
is a Reynolds operator of weight
2\lambda
on the induced L.t.s.
This example will be more clear in Section
6
when we will introduce NS-Lie triple systems and a functor
from the category of NS-Lie triple systems to the category of generalized Reynolds operator on L.t.s.

3.2
Generalized Reynolds operators on L.t.sRep pairs

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
be a L.t.sRep pair. For any
3
-cocycle
\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{Z}_{L.t.s}^{3}(L,M)
, there is a Lie triple system structure on the direct sum of vector spaces
L\oplus M
, defined by:

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v,z+w]_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big([x,y,z],\ \theta(y,z)u-\theta(x,z)v+D(x,y)w+\mathcal{H}(x,y,z)\Big)\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~~x,y,z\in L.~~u,v,w\in M.

(3.7)

This Lie triple system is called the
twisted semi-direct product Lie triple system
and denoted by
L\ltimes_{\theta}^{\mathcal{H}}M
.

In the sequel,
\mathcal{H}
will always denote a
3
-cocycle.

Definition 3.4
.

A linear map
T:M\to L
is said to be a
generalized Reynolds operator
on a L.t.sRep pair
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
if it satisfies:

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]=T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+\mathcal{H}(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big)\hskip 9.24994pt\forall u,v,w\in M.

(3.8)

Example 3.5
.

Recall that a

Rota-Baxter
operator of weight zero on a
L.t.s
L
is a linear map
\mathcal{R}:L\rightarrow L
satisfying :

[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y),\mathcal{R}(z)]=\mathcal{R}\Big([\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y),z]+[\mathcal{R}(x),y,\mathcal{R}(z)]+[x,\mathcal{R}(y),\mathcal{R}(z)]\Big)\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x,y,z\in L.

Any Rota–Baxter operator of weight zero on a L.t.s is a
generalized Reynolds operator with respect to the regular representation and
\mathcal{H}=0
.

Remark 3.2
.

The notion of generalized Reynolds operator is also called twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator or twisted Kupershmidt operator. In
[
4
]
, the authors
introduced the notion of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a L.t.sRep pair.
It is straightforward to see that generalized Reynolds operators on L.t.sRep pairs are generalization of Rota-Baxter operators of weight 0 and

\mathcal{O}
-operators on L.t.sRep pairs.

Example 3.6
.

Any
\lambda
-weighted Reynolds operator on a L.t.s.
(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
is generalized Reynolds operator with respect to the regular representation, where
\mathcal{H}
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x,y,z)=\lambda[x,y,z]\ \ \ \forall x,y,z\in L.

Example 3.7
.

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
be a L.t.sRep pair. Suppose that
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)

is an invertible
1
-cochain. Then
T=\varphi^{-1}:M\to L
is a generalized Reynolds operator with
\mathcal{H}=-\delta^{1}\varphi
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{1}\varphi(Tu,Tv,Tw)=D(Tu,Tv)w-\theta(Tu,Tw)v+\theta(Tv,Tw)u-\varphi([Tu,Tv,Tw])\hskip 9.24994pt\forall u,v,w\in M.

By applying
T
to both sides we get the result.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two generalized Reynolds operators on a L.t.sRep pair. A morphism of generalized Reynolds operators from
T

to
T^{\prime}
consists of a L.t.s morphism
\phi:L\to L
and an endomorphism
\psi:M\to M
such that

\displaystyle\phi\circ T=T^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\psi,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\psi(\theta(x,y)u)=\theta(\phi(x),\phi(y))\psi(u),

(3.10)

\displaystyle\psi(\mathcal{H}(x,y,z))=\mathcal{H}(\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z))\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\ x,y,z\in L,\ u\in M.

(3.11)

In particular, if both
\phi
and
\psi
are invertible then
(\phi,\psi)
is called an
isomorphism
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)

be a
1
-cochain. Define
\kappa_{\varphi}:L\oplus M\to L\oplus M
by
\kappa_{\varphi}=\begin{pmatrix}Id_{L}&0\\
\varphi&Id_{M}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

(L,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\theta)
be a L.t.sRep pair and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{L.t.s}^{1}(L,M)
be a
1
-cochain. Then
\kappa_{\varphi}
defined above is an isomorphism from the
\mathcal{H}
-twisted semi-direct product L.t.s
L\ltimes_{\theta}^{\mathcal{H}}M
to the
{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
-semi-direct product L.t.s
L\ltimes_{\theta}^{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}M
, where
{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{H}-\delta^{1}\varphi.

The identity (
3.8
) can be characterized by the graph of
T
being a subalgebra.

Proposition 3.9
.

A linear map

6
NS-Lie triple systems

The aim of this section is to introduce the notion of a NS-Lie triple system. A NS-Lie triple system
gives rise to a L.t.s. and a representation on itself. We show that a generalized
Reynolds operator induces a NS-Lie triple system. Thus, NS-Lie triple systems can
be viewed as the underlying algebraic structures of generalized Reynolds operators
on L.t.sRep pairs. Also, we show that Lie
algebras, NS-Lie algebras, Lie triple systems and
NS-Lie triple systems are closely related.

6.1
Definitions and constructions

Definition 6.1
.

\displaystyle\{x_{1},x_{2},[\![y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]\!]\}=\{\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}\},y_{2},y_{3}\}-\{\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}\},y_{1},y_{3}\}+\{y_{1},y_{2},\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{3}\}\}^{\ast},

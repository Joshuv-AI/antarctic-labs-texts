Abstract
. Here, we study a level-set forced mean curvature flow with evolving spirals and the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition, which appears in a crystal growth model. Under some appropriate conditions on the forcing term, we prove that the solution is globally Lipschitz. We then study the large time average of the solution and deduce the asymptotic growth rate of the crystal. Some large time behavior results of the solution are obtained.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
A spiral crystal growth model

Various spiral patterns are observed in many crystal growth situations in practice in which the spirals’ centers are often believed to be the locations of screw dislocations.
In this growth mechanism, the crystal surface has discontinuities in height, which are called steps, along curves that spiral out from these centers.
Atoms bond with the crystal structure with a higher probability near each step, which results in a surface evolution of this step with normal velocity

V=v_{\infty}(\rho_{c}\kappa+1),

(1.1)

where
\kappa
is the curvature of the step.
The constants
\rho_{c}>0
and
v_{\infty}>0
are the critical radius and the step velocity
[
1
]
, respectively.
By rescaling, we may assume that
v_{\infty}\rho_{c}=1
, and thus, (
1.1
) becomes
V=\kappa+v_{\infty}
.
See Figure
1
for an example of a spiral crystal growth with only one screw dislocation.

Fig. 1
.
Spiral crystal growth with a screw dislocation.

We now give a more precise description of the spirals and the set up of the problem following
[
21
,
25
,
26
]
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\Omega
be the centers of the spirals with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for
i\neq j
.
Fix
r>0
sufficiently small so that
\overline{B}(a_{j},r)\subset\Omega
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant N
,
and
\overline{B}(a_{i},r)\cap\overline{B}(a_{j},r)=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
.
Let

\displaystyle W:=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B(a_{j},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\theta(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\arg(x-a_{j}),

where
m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is given for
1\leqslant j\leqslant N
.
The constant
m_{j}=m_{j}^{+}-m_{j}^{-}
with
m_{j}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is the strength of the spiral center
a_{j}
, which is the difference between the strength
m_{j}^{+}
of counterclockwise rotating spirals and
m_{j}^{-}
of clockwise rotating spirals.
Note that
\theta
is a multi-valued function and it is not well defined at
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
, and this is the reason why we consider the problem in
W
instead of
\Omega
.
Although
\theta
is multi-valued,
D\theta
is single-valued.
Indeed, if
f(x)=\arg(x)
, then we can view that

f(x)=\arctan(x_{2}/x_{1})+2\pi l
for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus,

f_{x_{1}}=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right)^{2}}\cdot\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{(x_{1})^{2}}\right)=\frac{-x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\quad f_{x_{2}}=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right)^{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}}\right)=\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}.

Hence, for
a_{j}=(a_{j,1},a_{j,2})
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant N
.

D\theta(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{m_{j}(-(x_{2}-a_{j,1}),x_{1}-a_{j,2})}{|x-a_{j}|^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{m_{j}(x-a_{j})^{\perp}}{|x-a_{j}|^{2}}.

As noted,
D\theta
is well defined and smooth in
W
.
For an auxiliary function
u:W\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, spirals are implicitly defined as, for
t\geqslant 0
,

\Gamma_{t}=\{x\in\overline{W}\,:\,u(x,t)-\theta(x)=2\pi k\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(1.2)

In this paper, we consider a more general setting in which the step velocity might be non homogeneous, that is,
v_{\infty}=c(x)
for
x\in W
.
Then,
V=\kappa+c(x)
, and the auxiliary function
u
satisfied the following level-set forced mean curvature flow PDE

\begin{cases}u_{t}=|D(u-\theta)|\left({\rm div}\,\left(\frac{D(u-\theta)}{|D(u-\theta)|}\right)+c(x)\right)\qquad&\text{ in }W\times(0,\infty),\\
D(u-\theta)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\qquad&\text{ on }\partial W\times(0,\infty),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\qquad&\text{ on }\overline{W},\end{cases}

(1.3)

Here,
{\mathbf{n}}
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial W
.
Throughout this paper, we assume that
c\in C^{1}(\overline{W},(0,\infty))
and
u_{0}\in C^{2}(\overline{W})
with
D(u_{0}-\theta)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\partial W
for compatibility.
The wellposedness of viscosity solutions to (
1.3
) was obtained in
[
21
]
.
The uniqueness of level sets
\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t>0}
with respect to an initial curve
\Gamma_{0}
was proved in
[
13
]
.

Our main goals are to study the Lipschitz regularity, the large time average and behavior of the auxiliary function
u
, and to deduce the asymptotic growth rate of the crystal.

1.2.
Main results

We now describe our main results.
Denote by

\begin{cases}C_{0}:=\max\{-\lambda\,:\,\lambda\ \textrm{is the curvature of $\partial W$ at $x_{0}$ for $x_{0}\in\partial W$}\}\in\mathbb{R},\\
K_{0}:=\min\{d\,:\,d\ \textrm{is the diameter of an open ball inscribed in}\ W\}>0.\end{cases}

In our setting, it is clear that

C_{0}=\max\left\{C_{1},\frac{1}{r}\right\}>0

where

C_{1}:=\max\{-\lambda\,:\,\lambda\ \textrm{is a curvature of $\partial\Omega$ at $x_{0}$ for $x_{0}\in\partial\Omega$}\}\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, if
\Omega
is convex, then
C_{0}=1/r
.

We first have the following uniform Lipschitz estimate for
u
, which is strongly inspired by
[
16
]
.

Theorem
1.1.

Assume that there exists
\delta>0
such that

c(x)^{2}-2|Dc(x)|-2C_{0}|c(x)|-\frac{8C_{0}}{K_{0}}\geqslant\delta\qquad\text{ for all }x\in W.

(1.4)

Let
u
be the unique viscosity solution to (
1.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
L>0
depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{W})}
,
\|D\theta\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
,
\|c\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
, the constants
C_{0}
,
K_{0}
, and
\delta
such that

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(W\times[0,\infty))}+\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(W\times[0,\infty))}\leqslant L.

(1.5)

Remark
1.

In case that
c(x)=c>0
is a constant force term, then (
1.4
) is equivalent to the requirement that

c>C_{0}\left(1+\left(1+\frac{8}{C_{0}K_{0}}\right)^{1/2}\right).

Note that
C_{0}\geqslant 1/r
, which is quite large for
r>0
small.
Besides,
K_{0}
can be quite small if some of the balls
\{B(a_{j},r)\}
are close to each other or close to
\partial\Omega
.
In such situations, we would need to require
c
to be very large in order to have uniform Lipschitz estimate for
u
, which implies that (
1.4
) is rather restrictive.

Example
1.2.

We consider the case that
\Omega=B(0,R)
,
W=B(0,R)\setminus B(0,r)
for given
R\geqslant 2r>0
and
c(x)=c>0
is a constant force term.
Then,
C_{0}=1/r
,
K_{0}=R-r\geqslant r
, and (
1.4
) is equivalent to the requirement that

c>4C_{0}=\frac{4}{r}.

Next, we study the large time average of the auxiliary function
u
.

Theorem
1.3.

Let
u
be the unique viscosity solution to (
1.3
).
For each
t\geqslant 0
, denote by

S(t)=\max_{x\in\overline{W}}u(x,t).

Then, there exists a constant
S=S_{c}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{S(t)}{t}=S_{c}.

If we assume furthermore (
1.4
), then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{u(x,t)}{t}=S_{c}\qquad\text{ uniformly for }x\in\overline{W}.

(1.6)

We now use the formulation described in
[
25
]
to deduce back the asymptotic growth rate of the crystal from the auxiliary function
u
.
Let
k(x,t)\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

-\pi\leqslant u(x,t)-\left(\Theta(x)+2\pi k(x,t)\right)<\pi,

(1.7)

where
\Theta(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\Theta_{j}(x)
, and
\Theta_{j}:\overline{W}\to[0,2\pi)
is the principal value of
\arg(x-a_{j})
.
Denote by
h_{0}>0
the unit height of steps.
We define the height of the crystal at
(x,t)\in\overline{W}\times[0,\infty)
by

h(x,t)=\frac{h_{0}}{2\pi}\left[\Theta(x)+2\pi k(x,t)+\pi\,{\rm sign}\left(u(x,t)-\left(\Theta(x)+2\pi k(x,t)\right)\right)\right].

(1.8)

Theorem
1.4.

Let
u
be the unique viscosity solution to (
1.3
).
Let
S_{c}
be defined as in Theorem
1.3
.
Let
h:\overline{W}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the height of the crystal defined by (
1.7
)–(
1.8
).
Then,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\max_{x\in\overline{W}}h(x,t)}{t}=\frac{h_{0}}{2\pi}S_{c}.

(1.9)

If we assume furthermore (
1.4
), then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(x,t)}{t}=\frac{h_{0}}{2\pi}S_{c}\qquad\text{ uniformly for }x\in\overline{W}.

(1.10)

In general, without assuming (
1.4
), we do not have uniform control on the asymptotic growth rate of the crystal.
Nevertheless, having the asymptotic growth rate of the tip (the highest point) of the crystal in (
1.9
) might be enough for practical purposes.

Definition
1.5.

Let
S_{c}
be as in Theorem
1.3
.
We say that
S_{c}
is the asymptotic growth rate of
u
solving (
1.3
).
We also say that
h_{0}S_{c}/(2\pi)
is the asymptotic growth rate of the corresponding crystal whose height is defined by (
1.7
)–(
1.8
).

We next study the asymptotic growth rate
S_{c}
of
u
, or equivalently,
h_{0}S_{c}/(2\pi)
of
h
.
Various interesting numerical results on the asymptotic growth rate were obtained in
[
25
,
26
]
.

Lemma
1.6.

Assume that
N=1
,
m_{1}=1
,
a_{1}=0
,
W=B(0,R)\setminus B(0,r)
for given
0<r<R
, and
c\in C^{1}(\overline{W},(0,\infty))
.
Then,

\min_{x\in\overline{W}}\frac{c(x)}{|x|}\leqslant S_{c}\leqslant\max_{x\in\overline{W}}\frac{c(x)}{|x|}.

In Propositions
3.1
–
3.3
in Section
3
, we give various situations when

in which
S_{c}=0
.

As the asymptotic growth rate of

is
S_{c}
, it is natural to consider the following ergodic problem

\begin{cases}-|D(v-\theta)|\left({\rm div}\,\left(\frac{D(v-\theta)}{|D(v-\theta)|}\right)+c(x)\right)=-S_{c}\qquad&\text{ in }W,\\
D(v-\theta)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\qquad&\text{ on }\partial W.\end{cases}

(1.11)

We have the following large time behavior result for (
1.3
) in the case that
S_{c}=0
.

Theorem
1.7.

Let
u
be the unique viscosity solution to (
1.3
).
Assume (
1.4
) and
S_{c}=0
.
Then, there exists a viscosity solution
v
to (
1.11
) with
S_{c}=0
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(\cdot,t)-v\|_{L^{\infty}(W)}=0.

The proof of this theorem follows that of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
16

which uses a Lyapunov function, and is hence omitted.
It is worth emphasizing that the condition

in Theorem

Next, we give a result along the line of Lemma
1.6
.
For a given angle
\varphi
, denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}
a linear transformation given by rotating vectors through an angle of
\varphi
counterclockwise in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then, the matrix of

is given by

\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\
\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition
1.8.

Assume that
N=1
,
m_{1}=1
,
a_{1}=0
,
W=B(0,R)\setminus B(0,r)
for given
0<r<R
, and
c(x)=c_{0}|x|
for

for some
c_{0}>0
.
Assume further that
u_{0}(x)=g(x/|x|)

Thus, in general, it is striking that solutions to (

2.
Lipschitz regularity

To obtain Lipschitz estimates, we consider the following approximation, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
T>0
,

\begin{cases}u^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D(u^{\varepsilon}-\theta)|^{2}}\left({\rm div}\,\left(\frac{D(u^{\varepsilon}-\theta)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D(u^{\varepsilon}-\theta)|^{2}}}\right)+c(x)\right)\quad&\text{ in }W\times(0,T],\\
D(u^{\varepsilon}-\theta)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\quad&\text{ on }\partial W\times[0,T],\\
u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\quad&\text{ on }\overline{W}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The following result on a priori estimates on the gradient of
u^{\varepsilon}
implies right away Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
2.1 (A priori estimates).

Assume that
\partial W
is smooth and
c\in C^{\infty}(\overline{W})
.
Assume (
1.4
).
For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
T>0
, assume that
u^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{W}\times(0,T])\cap C^{1}(\overline{W}\times[0,T])
is the unique solution of (
2.1
).
Then, there exists a constant
L>0
depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{W})}
,
\|D\theta\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
,
\|c\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
, the constants
C_{0}
,
K_{0}
, and
\delta
from (
1.4
) such that

\|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(W\times[0,\infty))}+\|Du^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(W\times[0,\infty))}\leqslant L.

(2.2)

The proof below follows the ideas in
[
16
]
, which uses the classical Bernstein method.
We also refer the reader to
[
17
,
24
]
.

Proof.

Firstly, let

M=4\lVert D^{2}(u_{0}-\theta)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\overline{W})}+\lVert(1+|D(u_{0}-\theta)|^{2})^{1/2}c\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\overline{W})}.

(2.3)

We see that
u_{0}\pm Mt
are a supersolution and a subsolution to (
2.1
), respectively.
By the comparison of principle, we yield that, for
s\geqslant 0
,

u_{0}-Ms\leqslant u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s)\leqslant u_{0}+Ms.

(2.4)

Moreover, for each
s\geqslant 0
,
(x,t)\mapsto u^{\varepsilon}(x,t+s)
is a solution to (
2.1
) with initial data
u^{\varepsilon}(x,s)
.
By using the comparison principle once more and (
2.4
), we deduce that, for
t,s\geqslant 0
,

\|u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t+s)-u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(W)}\leqslant\|u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s)-u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{\infty}(W)}\leqslant Ms.

Therefore, we obtain that

\|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(W\times[0,\infty))}\leqslant M.

(2.5)

We next show the boundedness of
Du^{\varepsilon}
.
Denote by

w^{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D(u^{\varepsilon}-\theta)|^{2}}.

We only need to show that

\max_{\overline{W}\times[0,T]}w^{\varepsilon}\leqslant C

(2.6)

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{W})}
,
\|D\theta\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
,
\|c\|_{C^{1}(\overline{W})}
, the constants
C_{0}
,
K_{0}
, and
\delta
from (
1.4
).
The crucial point here is
C
does not depend on
T>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Pick
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\operatorname{argmax}_{\overline{W}\times[0,T]}w^{\varepsilon}
.
If
t_{0}=0
, then

\max_{\overline{W}\times[0,T]}w^{\varepsilon}\leqslant w^{\varepsilon}(x_{0},0)\leqslant\|D(u_{0}-\theta)\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{W})}+1,

and (
2.6
) holds true.

We consider the case
t_{0}>0
.
Write
u=u^{\varepsilon}
,
w=w^{\varepsilon}
from now on for clarity.
Denote by

b^{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{(u-\theta)_{i}(u-\theta)_{j}}{\varepsilon^{2}+|D(u-\theta)|^{2}}.

Differentiate (
2.1
) with respect to
x_{k}
and multiply the result by
(u-\theta)_{k}
to get

(u-\theta)_{k}(u-\theta)_{kt}-(D_{p}b^{ij}\cdot D(u-\theta)_{k})(u-\theta)_{k}(u-\theta)_{ij}-b^{ij}(u-\theta)_{k}(u-\theta)_{kij}\\
-(u-\theta)_{k}c_{k}w-c\frac{(u-\theta)_{k}(u-\theta)_{lk}(u-\theta)_{l}}{w}=0.

In the above, we use the fact that
\theta_{kt}=0
as
\theta
is independent of
t
.
Substituting
ww_{t}=(u-\theta)_{k}(u-\theta)_{kt}
,
ww_{k}=(u-\theta)_{l}(u-\theta)_{kl}
, and
ww_{ij}=(u-\theta)_{kij}(u-\theta)_{k}+b^{kl}(u-\theta)_{ki}(u-\theta)_{lj}
, we yield

ww_{t}-w(D_{p}b^{ij}\cdot Dw)(u-\theta)_{ij}-wb^{ij}w_{ij}+b^{ij}b^{kl}(u-\theta)_{ki}(u-\theta)_{lj}\\
-wD(u-\theta)\cdot Dc-cD(u-\theta)\cdot Dw=0.

(2.7)

There are two cases to be considered
x_{0}\in W
and
x_{0}\in\partial W
.

Case 1:
x_{0}\in W
.

We follow the computations of
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
.
At
(x_{0},t_{0})
, we have
w_{t}\geqslant 0
,
Dw=0
,
D^{2}w\leqslant 0
, and thus

wD(u-\theta)\cdot Dc\geqslant b^{ij}b^{kl}(u-\theta)_{ki}(u-\theta)_{lj}.

We then use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

(\textrm{tr}\alpha\beta)^{2}\leqslant\textrm{tr}(\alpha^{2})\textrm{tr}(\beta^{2})

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
, and put
\alpha=A^{\frac{1}{2}}BA^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
\beta=I_{2}
,
where
A=(b^{ij})
,
B=((u-\theta)_{kl})
,
I_{2}
the identity matrix of size
2
to get
\textrm{tr}(AB)^{2}\geqslant(\textrm{tr}AB)^{2}/\textrm{tr}(I_{2}).

Therefore, at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\displaystyle\left|Dc(x_{0})\right|w^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant wD(u-\theta)\cdot Dc\geqslant b^{ij}b^{kl}(u-\theta)_{ki}(u-\theta)_{lj}=\textrm{tr}(AB)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{(\textrm{tr}AB)^{2}}{\textrm{tr}(I_{2})}=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{t}-c(x_{0})w\right)^{2}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}c(x)^{2}-\left|Dc(x)\right|\geqslant\frac{\delta}{2}>0
by (
1.4
), we imply that at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\delta w^{2}\leqslant 2u_{t}c(x_{0})w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad w\leqslant\frac{2M\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{W})}}{\delta},

which gives (
2.6
).

Case 2:
x_{0}\in\partial W
.

As
\partial W
is
C^{2}
, we assume that
\mathbf{n}
is defined as a
C^{1}
function in a neighborhood of
\partial W
.
Note that the Neumann boundary condition
D(u-\theta)\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0
gives
\left(D^{2}(u-\theta){\mathbf{n}}+D{\mathbf{n}}D(u-\theta)\right)\cdot v=0
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
perpendicular to
{\mathbf{n}}
on

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{R}_{-c_{0}t})Dg\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}+c_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\left(1-Dg\cdot\left(\mathcal{R}_{\pi/2-c_{0}t}\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\right)=c_{0}\left(1-Dg\cdot\left(\mathcal{R}_{-c_{0}t}\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|}\right)\right),

and

Du=\frac{\mathcal{R}_{-c_{0}t}}{|x|}\left(Dg\cdot\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|}\right)\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|}.

Therefore, for
(x,t)\in\overline{W}\times[0,\infty)
, we use the assumption that
\|Dg\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
to yield

|D(u-\theta)|=\frac{1}{|x|}\left(1-Dg\cdot\left(\mathcal{R}_{-c_{0}t}\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|}\right)\right).

As
c(x)=c_{0}|x|
and
\operatorname{div}(x^{\perp}/|x|)=0
, we imply the conclusion.
∎

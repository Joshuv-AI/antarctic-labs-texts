Borel-type subalgebras of the lattice vertex operator algebra
Jianqi Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce and study new classes of sub-vertex operator algebras of the lattice vertex operator algebras (VOAs), which we call the conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs. These CFT-type VOAs, which are not necessarily strongly finitely generated, satisfy properties similar to the usual Borel and parabolic subalgebras of a Lie algebra. For the lowest-rank nontrivial example of Borel-type subVOA V B V_{B} of V ℤ ​ α V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha} , we explicitly determine its Zhu’s algebra A ⁡ ( V B ) A(V_{B}) in terms of generators and relations.

1.
Introduction

Conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subalgebras of the lattice vertex operator algebra (VOA)
V_{L}
associated to a positive-definite even lattice
L
, defined here, are classes of CFT-type, irrational,
C_{1}
-cofinite, or non-
C_{1}
-cofinite VOAs that arise naturally in the Heisenberg module decomposition of a lattice VOA
V_{L}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in L}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
and give rise to solutions of the operator-form classical Yang-Baxter equations on the lattice VOAs. In this paper, we initiate a systematic investigation of these VOAs. In particular, we show that Borel and parabolic-type subVOAs share many properties similar to the usual Borel and parabolic subalgebras of a semisimple Lie algebra. To better understand the structure and representation theory of the Borel-type VOAs, we pick the smallest-rank nontrivial example of a Borel-type VOA
V_{B}=V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha}
, a subVOA of the lattice VOA
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
, and explicitly determine its Zhu’s algebra
A(V_{B})
in terms of generators and relations.

1.1.
Subalgebras of the lattice vertex operator algebra

The lattice VOA

[

]
and Frenkel, Lepowsky, and Meurman in

. Since then, the lattice VOA has been studied extensively: Dong and Lepowsky computed the fusion rules for

in
[
DL93
]
. Dong classified the irreducible (
\theta

and proved the rationality of

in
[
D93
,
D94

The

-orbifold

of the lattice VOA

[

,
ADL05
,
DG98
,
DN99(1)
,
DN99(2)
]
. Using the

and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold constructions for the lattice VOAs, the Schellekens’ conjecture on

[

Although the representation theory of lattice VOAs is well understood

D93
,
D94
,
DL93

, their structural theory often leads to surprising results
[
MS23
]
. On the other hand, it is evident that the structural theory of VOAs resembles semisimple Lie algebras. For instance, the Jacobi identity for VOAs is a formal variable generalization of the usual Jacobi identity
[
FLM88

; the vertex operator

[

[
FHL93
,
L94
]

and parabolic-type subVOA

, together with an auxiliary notion of conic-type subVOA

, of a lattice VOA

is the first attempt in finding the analog of these substructures.
We justify our terminologies by showing that they satisfy similar properties with the usual Borel and parabolic subalgebras of a semisimple Lie algebra in Section
3

first appeared in solving the operator-form classical Yang-Baxter equation (CYBE) on VOAs by Bai, Guo, the author, and Wang in
[

give rise to solutions of the CYBE on

Besides the analogy with classical notions, our definition for the conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs also leads to large classes of CFT-type,
C_{1}
-cofinite or non-
C_{1}
-cofinite examples of VOAs. The

-cofiniteness, introduced by Li in
[

]
, is equivalent to the strongly finite-generation property for vertex algebras defined by Kac in
[
K97
]
. This finiteness condition for VOAs is satisfied by almost all the classical examples of CFT-type VOAs, and is essentially weaker than the more commonly acknowledged

-cofinite condition
[
Z96
]
. Although being a weak condition,

-cofiniteness is sufficient for many good properties of VOAs, like the convergence of iterated intertwining operators
[
H05
]
, the Noetherianity of Zhu’s algebra

[
Liu25
]
, the Morita equivalence between

and the mode transition algebra
[

-cofinite VOA whose irreducible modules are also

-cofinite can give rise to a general conformal field theory that is not logarithmic, and the module category of such VOAs remains to be explored. Adding to the known examples of

-cofinite VOAs, we prove that the conic-type subVOAs of small rank lattice VOAs are

1.2.
The main theorems

To state our main results, and describe how they are proved, we introduce some notation. Let
L
be a positive-definite even lattice in an Euclidean space
E
. Our definitions are based on the key observation that the direct sum of the Heisenberg modules
V_{M}:=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in M}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
associated to any (abelian) submonoid
M
of the lattice
L
is a CFT-type subVOA of
V_{L}
, whose vertex operator
Y_{V_{M}}
is given by the intertwining operators among the Heisenberg modules
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
for
\gamma\in M
. In this way, we can formulate plenty of new examples of subVOAs of
V_{L}
. We say that a submonoid
C\leq L
is of conic-type if
C=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
, where
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
is a basis of
L
; a submonoid
B\leq L
is of Borel-type if
B\cup(-B)=L
,
B\cap(-B)=0
, and
B
is contained in one side of a hyperplane in
E
; a submonoid
P\leq L
is of parabolic-type if it contains a Borel-type submonoid, see Definition
2.1
. The corresponding associated subVOAs
V_{C}
,
V_{B}
, and
V_{P}
of the lattice VOA
V_{L}
are called the conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs, respectively.

Although there are many classes of the Borel-type subVOA of a given lattice VOA
V_{L}
, and their properties are very different, we can still justify our terminologies in terms of analogies and relations with the usual Borel and parabolic subalgebras of a semisimple Lie algebra in Section
4
. First, we prove that there is a correspondence between the degree-one sub-Lie algebras
(V_{B})_{1}
and
(V_{P})_{1}
of the (reductive) Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(V_{L})_{1}

[
DM04
]
and the usual Borel and parabolic subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
, see Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
. Then we prove that conic-type subVOAs
V_{C}
of the ADE-type root lattices
Q
are conjugate under
\mathrm{Aut}(V_{Q})
, see Proposition
4.3
. Next, we prove that conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs are not simple nor rational, see Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.5
, which are parallel to the facts that the Borel and parabolic subalgebras of a Lie algebra are not simple nor semisimple. Then, we introduce a notion of “normalizer”
N_{W}(V)
of a sub-vertex algebra
W\leq V
, which is similar to the notion of “centralizer” or commutant
\mathrm{Com}_{V}(W)
introduced by Frenkel and Zhu in
[
FZ92
]
and is a generalization of the usual normalizer of a subalgebra in a Lie algebra. We prove that
N_{V_{L}}(V_{P})=V_{P}
for any parabolic-type subVOA
V_{P}\leq V_{L}
, see Proposition
4.8
, which is parallel to the facts that
N_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}
and
N_{G}(P)=P
, where
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{g}
is a parabolic subalgebra of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
P\leq G
is a parabolic subgroup of a linear algebraic group
G

[
BT65
,
Hum95
]
.

The
C_{1}
-cofiniteness of these subVOAs, which has no analogs in the classical Lie theory, are discussed in Section
5
. Since these VOAs are irrational, it is well indicated that they are not

-cofinite.
The following is our first main theorem, which concerns the

-cofiniteness of small-rank conic-type VOAs, see Lemma

and Theorem

:

Theorem A
.

The conic-type subVOA
V_{C}=V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha}
of the rank-one lattice VOA
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
is
C_{1}
-cofinite.

Moreover, let

be a rank-two positive-definite even lattice such that
(\alpha|\beta)=-n
,
(\beta|\beta)=2k
and
(\alpha|\alpha)=2\ell

, and

-cofinite.

Theorem
A
leads to new examples of CFT-type

-cofinite VOAs.
A similar argument also proves the

-cofiniteness of a typical parabolic-type subVOA

Theorem B

Let

\begin{split}O(V_{B})=\mathrm{span}\bigg\{\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u,\ \alpha(-1)v+v,\ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha):&\\
n\geq 0,\ u\in V_{B},\ v\in\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha),\ k\geq 2\bigg\}.\end{split}

(1.1)

In particular,

2.
Submonoids of an even lattice

Let

be a lattice in

-basis
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
of

such that
L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{r}
. Moreover,

for all

2.1.
Definition of conic, Borel, and parabolic-type submonoids

We set some notation first. Let
\gamma
be a nonzero vector in the Euclidean space
E
. Let
P(\gamma)
be the hyperplane passing though the origin that is perpendicular to
\gamma
, and let
P^{+}(\gamma)
(resp.
P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)
) be the positive (resp. non-negative) side of
P(\gamma)
. That is,

\displaystyle P(\gamma):

\displaystyle=\{v\in E:(\gamma|v)=0\},

\displaystyle P^{+}(\gamma):

\displaystyle=\{v\in E:(\gamma|v)>0\},

(2.1)

\displaystyle P^{\geq 0}(\gamma):

\displaystyle=P(\gamma)\sqcup P^{+}(\gamma)=\{v\in E:(\gamma|v)\geq 0\}.

2.1.1.
Definition and first properties

Observe
L
is an abelian monoid with the product given by addition and the identity element given by
0
. An
(abelian) submonoid
of
L
is a subset
M\subset L
containing
0\in M
that is closed under the addition of
L
. An
(abelian) sub-semigroup
of
L
is a subset
S\subset L
that is closed under addition of
L
. We introduce the following notions:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L
be a positive definite even lattice in a Euclidean space
(E,(\cdot|\cdot))
.

(1)

A submonoid
C\leq L
is said to be of
conic-type
if there exists a basis
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
of
L
such that
C=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
.

(2)

A submonoid
B\leq L
is said to be of
Borel-type
if it satisfies

(a)

B\cup(-B)=L
,

(b)

B\cap(-B)=\{0\}
,

(c)

There exists a hyperplane
P(\gamma)\subset E
such that
B\subset P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)
.

(3)

A submonoid
P\leq L
is said to be of
parabolic-type
if there exists a Borel-type submonoid
B\leq L
such that
B\subseteq P
.

Recall the automorphism group of the lattice
L
is defined by

\mathrm{Aut}(L):=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{GL}(L):(\sigma\alpha|\sigma\beta)=(\alpha|\beta),\ \forall\alpha,\beta\in L\}=\mathrm{GL}(L)\cap O(E),

(2.2)

which is a finite group
[
CS88
]
. The following properties of these submonoids are immediate consequences of our definition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Conic, Borel, and parabolic-type submonoids of
L
satisfy:

(1)

Every Borel-type submonoid is of parabolic-type. Every Borel-type submonoid contains a conic-type submonoid;

(2)

The sets of conic, Borel, or parabolic-type submonoids of
L
are invariant under the actions of
\mathrm{Aut}(L)
;

(3)

The hyperplane
P(\gamma)
in the Definition
2.1
of Borel-type submonoid is unique.

Proof.

The first claim in
(1)
is clear. Let
B
be a Borel-type submonoid, and let
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
be a basis of
L
. Since
B\cup(-B)=L
, by replacing
\alpha_{i}
with
-\alpha_{i}
, which does not change the fact that
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
is a basis of
L
, we may assume
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}\subseteq B
, then
C=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
is a conic-type submonoid that is contained in
B
.

(2) Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(L)
. Since
\sigma
preserves addition,
\sigma(M)\leq L
is a submonoid for any submonoid
M\leq L
. Moreover, since
\mathrm{Aut}(L)\subset\mathrm{SL}(L)
,
\{\sigma(\alpha_{1}),\dots,\sigma(\alpha_{r})\}
is another
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
L
. Hence
\sigma(C)=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sigma(\alpha_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\sigma(\alpha_{r})
is another conic-type submonoid. Let
B\leq L
be a Borel-type submonoid, we have
L=\sigma(L)=\sigma(B)\cup(-\sigma(B))
and
\sigma(B)\cap(-\sigma(B))=\sigma(B\cap(-B))=\sigma(0)=0
. Furthermore, since
\sigma\in O(E)
(
2.2
), it is easy to see that
\sigma(P^{\geq 0}(\gamma))=P^{\geq 0}(\sigma(\gamma))
, with
\sigma(\gamma)\neq 0
. Thus,
\sigma(B)\subset P^{\geq 0}(\sigma(\gamma))

is a Borel-type submonoid. Finally, it is clear that

is a parabolic-type submonoid since

.

(3) Let

be a Borel-type submonoid such that

. First, we claim that

and
L\cap P^{<0}(\gamma)\subseteq-B
. Indeed, for any
\alpha\in L\cap P^{>0}(\gamma)

\alpha\neq 0
and the subset

\alpha\in-B

.

Suppose there exists another hyperplane

, with

B\cup(-B)=L
,

, and

Write

\gamma^{\prime}=a_{1}^{\prime}\beta_{1}+\dots+a_{r}^{\prime}\beta_{r}

, such that

\displaystyle(\alpha|\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}|a_{j}\beta_{j})=\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{r}a_{r}<0,

\displaystyle(\alpha|\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}|a^{\prime}_{j}\beta_{j})=\lambda_{1}a_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+\lambda_{r}a_{r}^{\prime}>0.

By the first inequality and our claim, we have
\alpha\in L\cap P^{<0}(\gamma)\subseteq-B
. While the second inequality indicates
\alpha\in L\cap P^{>0}(\gamma^{\prime})\subseteq B
. Then
\lambda\in B\cap(-B)=0
, which is a contradiction. Hence the hyperplane that defines Borel-type submonoid is unique.
∎

2.1.2.
Existence of Borel-type submonoid

Next, we show that Borel-type submonoids must exist. Hence the conic-type and parabolic-type submonoids also exist.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
r
be a positive integer. There exists
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, such that

m_{1}\lambda_{1}+m_{2}\lambda_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\lambda_{r}\notin\mathbb{Q},

(2.3)

for any nonzero rational vector
(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

We use induction on
r
to show the existence of
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
. When
r=1
, we may choose
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
. Then clearly
m_{1}\lambda_{1}\notin\mathbb{Q}
for any nonzero rational number
m_{1}
. Assume there exists
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r-1}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
such that (
2.3
) holds for
r-1
.

Note that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{1}+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{2}+\dots+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{r-1}+\mathbb{Q}
is a countable subset of
\mathbb{R}
as the countable union of countable sets is countable. But
\mathbb{R}
is uncountable. Hence there exists some element
\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{1}+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{2}+\dots+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{r-1}+\mathbb{Q})
. Now let
(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}\backslash\{0\}
. If
m_{r}=0
, then by the induction hypothesis, we have
m_{1}\lambda_{1}+m_{2}\lambda_{2}+\dots+m_{r-1}\lambda_{r-1}+0\lambda_{r}\notin\mathbb{Q}
since not all
m_{1},\dots,m_{r-1}
are
0
. Assume
m_{r}\neq 0
. Since
\lambda_{r}\notin\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{1}+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{2}+\dots+\mathbb{Q}\lambda_{r-1}+\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\frac{m_{1}}{m_{r}}\lambda_{1}+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{r}}\lambda_{2}+\dots+\frac{m_{r-1}}{m_{r}}\lambda_{r-1}+\lambda_{r}\notin\mathbb{Q}.

Hence
m_{1}\lambda_{1}+m_{2}\lambda_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\lambda_{r}\notin\mathbb{Q}
. i.e., (
2.3
) holds for

∎

Proposition 2.4

Every positive-definite even lattice

Proof.

We claim that there exists a hyperplane

2.2.

The following subset of

It is well-known that

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
B\leq L
be a Borel-type submonoid, and assume that
\Phi(L)\neq\emptyset
. Then
\Phi^{+}:=B\cap\Phi(L)
is a set of positive roots in the root system
\Phi(L)
.

Proof.

Recall that a subset
\Phi^{+}
of a root system

if the following conditions hold, see
[

,

,
Hum72
]

(1)

For all
\alpha\in\Phi

\alpha
and
-\alpha
is in
\Phi^{+}
.

(2)

For
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi
, one has
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
.

Since the root system satisfies
\Phi(L)=-\Phi(L)
, we have
\Phi(L)\cap(-B)=-(\Phi(L)\cap B)=-\Phi^{+}
. Then by Definition
2.1
, we have
\Phi(L)=(\Phi(L)\cap B)\cup(\Phi(L)\cap(-B))=\Phi^{+}\cup(-\Phi^{+})
, and
\Phi^{+}\cap(-\Phi^{+})\subseteq B\cap(-B)=0
. In particular, for any
\alpha\in\Phi(L)
, either

or

.

Moreover, let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi(L)
. Since
\alpha,\beta\in B
, and

is closed under addition, we have
\alpha+\beta\in B\cap\Phi(L)=\Phi^{+}
. This shows
\Phi^{+}=\Phi(L)\cap B
is a set of positive roots in
\Phi(L)
.
∎

There is a converse of Proposition

for certain even lattices

is also contained in some Borel-type submonoid of

. First, we let

Lemma 2.8

Let

, or

Let

Now assume

is a lattice in the Euclidean space

. Let

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=(P^{+}(\gamma)\cap Q)\sqcup(Q\cap P(\gamma))\sqcup(P^{-}(\gamma)\cap Q)

\displaystyle=\left((P^{+}(\gamma)\cap Q)\sqcup B_{1}\right)\cup\left((-B_{1})\sqcup(P^{-}(\gamma)\cap Q)\right)

Moreover,

, and

∎

Now let

be an arbitrary positive-definite even lattice in

\Phi(L)\neq\emptyset
. Let

\Phi(L)
.

Proposition 2.9
.

With the notations as above, assume

can be extended to a basis of

. i.e.,

We use induction on

. Conversely, let

Since

is a root system of

, or

, there exists a Borel-type submonoid

. Now let

\displaystyle L=L\cap E

\displaystyle=(L\cap P^{+}(\gamma))\sqcup Q(L)\sqcup(L\cap-P^{+}(\gamma))

:

V_{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in L}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha),

(3.7)

where
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\otimes\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in L
.

We shall need the following notion introduced by Huang and Lepowsky in
[
HL96
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

A
vertex algebra without vacuum
is a triple
(V,Y,D)
, where
V
is a vector spaces,
Y:V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)[[z,z^{-1}]],\ a\mapsto Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}z^{-n-1}
is a linear map, and
D:V\rightarrow V
is a linear map, satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

(Truncation property) For any
a,b\in V
,
a_{n}b=0
for
n\gg 0
.

(2)

(
D
-derivative property) For any
a\in V
,
[D,Y(a,z)]=\frac{d}{dz}Y(a,z)
.

(3)

(Skew-symmetry) For any
a,b\in V
,
Y(a,z)b=e^{zD}Y(b,-z)a
.

(4)

(Jacobi identity) For any
a,b,c\in V
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y(a,z_{1})Y(b,z_{2})-z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{-z_{2}+z_{1}}{z_{0}}\right)Y(b,z_{2})Y(a,z_{1})}\displaystyle{=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)Y(Y(a,z_{0})b,z_{2})}.

In particular, a VOA
(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega)
is a vertex algebra without vacuum with
D=L(-1)
. A sub-vertex algebra without vacuum
W\leq V
is a subspace
W\subset V
that is closed under
Y
and
L(-1)
.

3.2.
SubVOAs of
V_{L}
associated to submonoids of

First, we observe that submonoids and semigroups of

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

be a positive-definite even lattice,

be a submonoid, and

be a sub-semigroup. Then

(1)

(V_{M}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in M}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha),Y,\omega,\mathbf{1})
is a CFT-type subVOA of
(V_{L},Y,\omega,\mathbf{1})
.

(2)

(V_{S}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha),Y,L(-1))
is a sub-vertex algebra without vacuum of
(V_{L},Y,L(-1))
. If, furthermore,
S\subset M
and

, then
V_{S}
is an ideal of
V_{M}
.

We call
(V_{M},Y,\omega,\mathbf{1})
(resp.
(V_{S},Y,L(-1))
) the
subVOA (resp. sub-vertex algebra without vacuum) of
V_{L}
associated to
M
(resp.
S
)
.

Proof.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in M
, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle Y(e^{\alpha},z)e^{\beta}

\displaystyle=E^{-}(-\alpha,z)E^{+}(-\alpha,z)e_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}(e^{\beta})=E^{-}(-\alpha,z)E^{+}(-\alpha,z)\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)z^{(\alpha|\beta)}e^{\alpha+\beta},

\displaystyle=\exp(\sum_{n<0}-\frac{\al(n)}{n} z^{-n})\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)z^{(\alpha|\beta)}e^{\alpha+\beta}.

The right-hand-side is contained in
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha+\beta)((z))\subset V_{M}((z))
, in view of the decomposition (
3.7
). More generally, for any
h_{1}(-n_{1}-1)\dots h_{k}(-n_{k}-1)e^{\alpha}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
and
h^{\prime}_{1}(-m_{1}-1)\dots h^{\prime}_{r}(-m_{r}-1)e^{\beta}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)
, with

\displaystyle Y(h_{1}(-n_{1}-1)\dots h_{k}(-n_{k}-1)e^{\alpha},z)h^{\prime}_{1}(-m_{1}-1)\dots h^{\prime}_{r}(-m_{r}-1)e^{\beta}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha+\beta)((z)).

Since

is closed under addition and

is a sub-VOA of

. Since

for each

is closed under addition, and

for any

∎

Remark 3.3
.

The proof of Proposition

essentially depends on the fact that

Y(M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha),z)M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)\subset M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha+\beta)((z)),\quad\alpha,\beta\in L,

(3.8)

where

is the vertex operator of the lattice VOA

.

Using the different types of submonoids in

introduced in Definition

, together with Proposition

, we give the following definition of subVOAs in

:

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a positive-definite even lattice, and let

,
B\leq L
, and

be a conic-type, Borel-type, and parabolic-type submonoid of

(1)

is called a

of

(2)

V_{B}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in B}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)

of

.

(3)

V_{P}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in P}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)

of

.

Let
V_{B}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in B}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
be a Borel-type subVOA.
We may view

as the analog of “Cartan part”, and view

with

as the analog of a “root space” associated to a “positive root”

is not commutative and the “root space”

Remark 3.5
.

We remark some easy facts from our definition:

(1)

The subVOAs
V_{C},V_{B}
and

are of CFT-type. They also have the same vacuum

and Virasoro element

.

(2)

Any Borel-type subVOA

. Any Borel-type subVOA is also of parabolic-type, see Proposition

.

Example 3.6
.

We have natural examples of conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs of low-rank lattices arising from Examples

and
2.6
.

(1)

.

(2)

Let

V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta}

The decompositions

4.
Basic properties of conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs

In this Section, we discuss the basic properties of the conic, Borel, parabolic-type subVOAs. We prove that these CFT-type VOAs are irrational and some of them are
C_{1}
-cofinite. We also show that they share many similar properties as the usual Borel and parabolic Lie subalgebras of a semisimple Lie algebra, which further justifies our terminologies.

4.1.
Relations with the root system and degree-one Lie algebra of
V_{L}

In this subsection, we justify our terminologies of Borel and parabolic-type subVOAs in Definition
3.4
by relating them with the root system
\Phi(L)
of the lattice
L
and the degree-one Lie algebra of a lattice VOA.

For any CFT-type VOA
V=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{n}
, recall that the degree-one subspace
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}
is a Lie algebra with respect to the Lie bracket:

[a,b]=a_{0}b=\Res_{z}Y(a,z)b,\quad a,b\in V_{1},

(4.1)

see
[
Bor86
,
FLM88
]
. It was proved by Dong and Mason that
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}
is a reductive Lie algebra if the VOA
V
is
strongly rational
i.e., if
V
is of CFT-type, rational,
C_{2}
-cofinite,
L(1)V_{1}=0
, and
V\cong V^{\prime}
as
V
-modules
[
DM04
]
.

In particular, let
V_{L}
be a lattice VOA, then
\mathfrak{g}=(V_{L})_{1}
is a reductive Lie algebra since
V_{L}
is strongly rational
[
D93
]
. Recall that
e^{\alpha}\in(V_{L})_{1}
if and only if
(\alpha|\alpha)=2
. i.e.,
\alpha\in\Phi(L)
. Then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}
is abelian if
\Phi(L)=\emptyset
. On the other hand, if
\Phi(L)\neq\emptyset
, then

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi(L)}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha},

(4.2)

where
\mathfrak{s}=H+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi(L)}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}
is a semisimple Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to the ADE-type root system
\Phi(L)
, with the Cartan subalgebra
H=\mathrm{span}\{\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}:\alpha\in\Phi(L)\}
,
\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the root space associated to the root
\alpha\in\Phi(L)
,
\mathfrak{c}
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\mathfrak{h}=H+\mathfrak{c}
.

Using the relations between Borel-type submonoids
B\leq L
and the root system
\Phi(L)
in Section
2.2
, we have can prove the following facts:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
V_{P}
(resp.
V_{B}
) be a parabolic-type (resp. Borel-type) subVOA of
V_{L}
, and assume that
\Phi(L)\neq\emptyset
. Then
\mathfrak{p}=(V_{P})_{1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{b}=(V_{B})_{1}
) is a parabolic (resp. Borel) subalgebra of the reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(V_{L})_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
P\leq L
be a parabolic-type submonoid containing a Borel-type submonoid
B
. By Proposition
2.7
,
B\cap\Phi(L)=\Phi^{+}
is a set of positive roots in
\Phi(L)
. Since
\mathrm{wt}(e^{\alpha})=(\alpha|\alpha)/2>1
for any
\alpha\in B\backslash\Phi(L)
, we have

(V_{B})_{1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in B}\left(M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\right)_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}:\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}\}+\sum_{\alpha\in B\cap\Phi(L)}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha},

(4.3)

which is a Borel Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of the reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

[
B56
,
FH91
]
. Moreover, since
B\subseteq P
, we have
\Phi^{+}\subseteq P\cap\Phi(L)
. Then
P\cap\Phi(L)
is a subset of the root system
\Phi(L)
containing all the positive roots and a subset of negative simple roots. Hence

(V_{P})_{1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in P}\left(M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\right)_{1}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in P\cap\Phi(L)}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}

(4.4)

is a parabolic Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{g}

[
B56
,
BT65
,
FH91
]
.
∎

There is also a converse of Proposition
4.1
that follows from Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
2.9
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
\Phi(L)\neq\emptyset
, and let
\Delta(L)
be a basis of the root system
\Phi(L)
. Assume
\Delta(L)
can be extended to a basis of
L
as in Proposition
2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
(resp.
\mathfrak{p}
) be a Borel (resp. parabolic) Lie subalgebra of the reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(V_{L})_{1}
that contains
\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{span}\{h(-1)\mathbf{1}:h\in\mathfrak{h}\}\subset(V_{L})_{1}
. Then there exists a Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}\leq V_{L}
such that
\mathfrak{b}=(V_{B})_{1}
and a parabolic-type subVOA
V_{P}\leq V_{L}
such that
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq(V_{P})_{1}
.

Proof.

Note that a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
of
\Phi(L)
such that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}
. By Proposition
2.9
, there exists a Borel-type submonoid
B\leq L
such that
\Phi^{+}=B\cap\Phi(L)
. It follows from (
4.3
) that
\mathfrak{b}=(V_{B})_{1}
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}
be a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
. By definition,
\Gamma=\Phi^{+}\cup S
, where
S
is a subset of
\Phi^{-}
generated by some negative simple roots
\{-\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,-\alpha_{i_{k}}\}\subset-\Delta(L)
. Let
B\leq L
be a Borel-type submonoid such that
\Phi^{+}=B\cap\Phi(L)
, then

P:=B+\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i_{1}}+\dots+\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i_{k}}\subset L

is also a submonoid of
L
that contains
B
, and
P
is a parabolic-type submonoid. Since
\Phi^{+}\subset B\cap\Phi(L)\subset P\cap\Phi(L)
and
S\subset(\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i_{1}}+\dots+\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i_{k}})\cap\Phi(L)\subset P\cap\Phi(L)
, we have
\Gamma=\Phi^{+}\cup S\subset P\cap\Phi(L)
. It follows from (
4.4
) that

\mathfrak{p}=\left(\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}\right)\subseteq\left(\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in P\cap\Phi(L)}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}\right)=(V_{P})_{1}.

∎

Certain conic-type subVOAs
V_{C}
satisfy a conjugacy property that is similar to the conjugacy properties of the Borel-subalgebras of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
or the Borel-subgroups of a reductive algebraic group
G
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
Q
be a root lattice associated to the root system
\Phi
of type
A,D
, or
E
. Then the conic-type subVOAs of the form
V_{C_{\Delta}}
, where
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
is a basis of
\Phi
and
C_{\Delta}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
, are conjugate under
\mathrm{Aut}(V_{Q})
.

Proof.

Given a lattice isometry
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
(
2.2
), we can lift it to an element
\hat{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Aut}(V_{Q})
as follows: First, extend
\sigma
to a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism
\sigma:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
by letting
\sigma(\lambda\alpha)=\lambda\sigma(\alpha),
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\in L
. Then define

\hat{\sigma}(h_{1}(-n_{1})\dots h_{r}(-n_{r})e^{\alpha}):=(\sigma h_{1})(-n_{1})\dots(\sigma h_{r})(-n_{r})e^{\sigma\alpha},

(4.5)

where
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
,
n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 1
, and
\alpha\in Q
. In particular,
\hat{\sigma}(M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha))=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\sigma(\alpha))
for any
\alpha\in Q
, see
[
DN99(2)
, Section 2.4]
for more detail. Let
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
be two sets of simple roots of
\Phi
. Then there exists
w\in W(\Phi)
, the Weyl group of
\Phi
, such that
w(\Delta)=\Delta^{\prime}

[
Hum72
]
. Since
w
is generated by simple reflections, we have
w\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
and
w(C_{\Delta})=C_{\Delta^{\prime}}
, see Proposition
2.2
.
Then by (
4.5
), we have
\hat{w}\in\mathrm{Aut}(V_{Q})
and

\hat{w}(V_{C_{\Delta}})=\hat{w}\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in C_{\Delta}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\right)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in C_{\Delta}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,w(\alpha))=\bigoplus_{\beta\in w(C_{\Delta})}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)=V_{C_{\Delta^{\prime}}}.

Thus, elements in
\{V_{C_{\Delta}}:\Delta\ \mathrm{is\ a\ basis\ of\ }\Phi\}
are conjugate under
\mathrm{Aut}(V_{Q})
.
∎

4.2.
Irrationality and non-simplicity

In Lie theory, we know that a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
or a proper parabolic-type subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is not simple nor semisimple as a Lie algebra
[
BT65
,
Hum72
]
. We have similar results for the Borel-type subVOAs.

Proposition 4.4
.

Conic-type subVOAs of a lattice VOA
V_{L}
are
not
simple nor rational.

Proof.

Let
C=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
be a conic-type submonoid of
L
, where
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
is a basis of
L
. Then
S=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\alpha_{r}
is a sub-semigroup of
C
such that
C+S\subseteq S
. By Proposition
3.2
,
V_{C}
has a nontrivial ideal
V_{S}
. Hence
V_{C}
is not simple.

Recall that a rational VOA necessarily has finitely many irreducible modules up to isomorphism
[
DLM98
,
Z96
]
. To show
V_{C}
is irrational, consider the following sequence of sub-semigroups of
C
:

\displaystyle C^{0}:

\displaystyle=C\backslash\{0\}=\{m\alpha_{1}+m_{2}\alpha_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}\in C:m\geq 1\ \mathrm{or}\ m=0,(m_{2},\dots,m_{r})\neq 0\},

\displaystyle C^{1}:

\displaystyle=C\backslash\{0,\alpha_{1}\}=\{m\alpha_{1}+m_{2}\alpha_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}\in C:m\geq 2\ \mathrm{or}\ m=0,(m_{2},\dots,m_{r})\neq 0\},

\displaystyle C^{2}:

\displaystyle=C\backslash\{0,\alpha_{1},2\alpha_{1}\}=\{m\alpha_{1}+m_{2}\alpha_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}\in C:m\geq 3\ \mathrm{or}\ m=0,(m_{2},\dots,m_{r})\neq 0\},

Clearly,
C\supset C^{0}\supset C^{1}\supset C^{2}\supset\dots
. We claim that
C+C^{k}\subseteq C^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
. Indeed, suppose there exists
\alpha=m_{1}\alpha_{1}+m_{2}\alpha_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}\in C
and
\beta=n_{1}\alpha_{1}+n_{2}\alpha_{2}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r}\in C^{k}
such that
\alpha+\beta\notin C^{k}=C\backslash\{0,\alpha_{1},\dots k\alpha_{1}\}
. Then
\alpha+\beta=(m_{1}+n_{1})\alpha_{1}+\dots+(m_{r}+n_{r})\alpha_{r}=p\alpha_{1}
for some
0\leq p\leq k
. Since
m_{i},n_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
, we must have
m_{1}+n_{1}=p
and
m_{j}=n_{j}=0
for all
j\geq 2
. But then
\beta=n_{1}\alpha_{1}\in\{0,\alpha_{1},\dots k\alpha_{1}\}
, a contradiction. Thus,
C+C^{k}\subseteq C^{k}
, and
C^{k}
is a sub-semigroup of
C
for all
k
.

By Proposition
3.2
,
V_{C^{0}}\supset V_{C^{1}}\supset V_{C^{2}}\supset\dots
is a sequence of ideals in the VOA
V_{C}
such that

V_{C}/V_{C^{0}}\cong M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0),\quad V_{C^{k}}/V_{C^{k+1}}\cong M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,(k+1)\alpha_{1}),\quad k\geq 0.

For non-negative integers
p\neq q
, the
V_{C}
-modules
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,p\alpha_{1})
is not isomorphic to
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,q\alpha_{1})
since they are not isomorphic as
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
-modules. Thus,
V_{C}
has infinitely many non-isomorphic irreducible modules. This shows
V_{C}
is irrational.
∎

By adopting a similar idea as the proof of the previous Proposition, we can prove the non-simplicity and irrationality of the Borel-type and certain parabolic-type subVOAs.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
P\leq L
be a parabolic-type submonoid containing
B
such that
P\subset P^{\geq 0}(\gamma)
, where
P(\gamma)
is the unique hyperplane that defines
B
(cf. Proposition
2.2
). Then
V_{P}
is
not
simple nor rational. In particular, Borel-type subVOAs of
V_{L}
are
not
simple nor rational.

Proof.

Let
S^{\prime}:=P\cap P^{+}(\gamma)=B\cap P^{+}(\gamma)
. It is clear that
S^{\prime}
is a nontrivial sub-semigroup of
P
. For any
\alpha\in S^{\prime}
and
\beta\in P
, we have
(\alpha+\beta|\gamma)=(\alpha|\gamma)+(\beta|\gamma)>0
as
(\alpha|\gamma)>0
and
(\beta|\gamma)\geq 0
. Hence
\alpha+\beta\in S^{\prime}
and so
P+S^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}
. Also note that
0\in P\backslash S^{\prime}
. Hence
V_{P}
has a proper ideal
V_{S^{\prime}}
by Proposition
3.2
.

Recall that a VOA
V
is rational if any admissible module
M
of
V
is semisimple
[
DLM98
]
.
Suppose
V_{P}
is rational. Note that
V_{S^{\prime}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S^{\prime}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\leq V_{P}
is an admissible submodule since each
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
with
\alpha\in S^{\prime}
is admissible. Then there exists an admissible submodule
N\leq V_{P}
such that
V_{P}=V_{S^{\prime}}\oplus N
. For any
\alpha\in S^{\prime}
, since
(\alpha|\alpha)/2>0
, we have
V_{S^{\prime}}\subseteq(V_{P})_{+}
. Thus,
\mathbf{1}\in N
and
V_{P}=V_{P}.\mathbf{1}\subseteq N
. Then
V_{S^{\prime}}=0
, which is a contradiction. Hence
V_{P}
is not rational.
∎

4.3.
Normalizer property

In Lie theory, we know that if
P\leq G
is a Borel or parabolic subgroup of a linear algebraic group
G
, then
N_{G}(P)=P
; and if
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{g}
is a Borel subalgebra of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{b}
. See
[
Hum95
, Chapter 23]
. We have a similar result for the parabolic-type subVOAs as well.

Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega)
be a VOA, and
W\leq V
be a sub-vertex algebra. There is an analog of the “centralizer” of
W
in
V
called the commutant defined by Frenkel and Zhu in
[
FZ92
]
. By definition,
\mathrm{Com}_{V}(W)=\{a\in V:w_{j}a=0,\ \mathrm{for\ all}\ j\geq 0,\ w\in W\}
.
We define the normalizer of
W
in
V
as follows:

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega)
be a VOA, and
W\leq V
be a sub-vertex algebra without vacuum.

N_{V}(W):=\{a\in V:a_{j}W\subseteq W,\ \mathrm{for\ any}\ j\geq 0\}

(4.6)

is called the
normalizer of
W
in
V
.

Lemma 4.7
.

N_{V}(W)\leq V
is a sub-vertex algebra of
V
with
W\subseteq N_{V}(W)
. Moreover,
N_{V}(W)
can also be characterized as follows:

N_{V}(W):=\{a\in V:b_{j}a\in W,\ \mathrm{for\ any}\ b\in W,j\geq 0\}.

(4.7)

In particular, we have
\mathrm{Com}_{V}(W)\subseteq N_{V}(W)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{1}_{j}W=0
for any
j\geq 0
, we have
\mathbf{1}\in N_{V}(W)
. Since
Y(W,z)W\subseteq W[[z,z^{-1}]]
, clearly
W\subseteq N_{V}(W)
. Let
a,b\in N_{V}(W)
,
w\in W
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
m\geq 0
. By the Jacobi identity, we have

(a_{n}b)_{m}w=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j}a_{n-j}b_{m+j}w-(-1)^{n}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j}b_{m+n-j}a_{j}w\equiv 0\pmod{W}

since
b_{m+j}w\in W
and
a_{j}w\in W
for any
j\geq 0
. Thus,
N_{V}(W)
is a sub-vertex algebra of
V
.

Moreover, by the skew-symmetry of
Y
, we have
a_{n}b=\sum_{i\geq 0}1/i!(-1)^{n+i+1}L(-1)^{i}b_{n+i}a
. By the definition of vertex algebra without vacuum
3.1
, we have
L(-1)W\subseteq W
. Let
a\in V
be a given element. Then
a_{n}w\in W
for any
n\geq 0
and
w\in W
if and only if
w_{n}a\in W
for any
n\geq 0
and
w\in W
. This shows the definitions of (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) are equivalent.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
M\leq L
be an abelian submonoid. Then
N_{V_{L}}(V_{M})=V_{M}
. In particular, for any parabolic-type submonoid
P\leq L
, we have
N_{V_{L}}(V_{P})=V_{P}
.

Proof.

Note that
V_{L}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)\oplus V_{M}
, in view of (
3.7
). Let
a=u+v\in N_{V_{L}}(V_{M})
with
u=\sum_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}u_{\gamma}
and
v\in V_{M}
, where
u_{\gamma}=0
for all but finitely many
\gamma\in L\backslash M
. Since
V_{M}\subseteq N_{V_{L}}(V_{M})
by Lemma
4.7
, we have
u\in N_{V_{L}}(V_{M})
.

Now we show that
u=0
. Since
0\in M
, we have
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subseteq V_{M}
. Then by (
4.7
), we have
(h(-1)\mathbf{1})_{0}u=\sum_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}h(0)u_{\gamma}=\sum_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}(h|\gamma)u_{\gamma}\in V_{M}\cap\bigoplus_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)=0
. Hence
(h|\gamma)u_{\gamma}=0
for all
\gamma\in L\backslash M
. For a fixed
\gamma\in L\backslash M
, choose
h\in\mathfrak{h}
s.t.
(h|\gamma)\neq 0
, then we have
u_{\gamma}=0
for all
\gamma
, and so
u=\sum_{\gamma\in L\backslash M}u_{\gamma}=0
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

In general, the normalizer
N_{V}(W)
is not necessarily equal to
W
. For example, let
S\subset L
be a sub-semigroup and
0\notin S
. By Proposition
3.2
,
V_{S}\leq V_{L}
is a sub-vertex algebra without vacuum. It is easy to see that
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subset N_{V_{L}}(V_{S})
but
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\nsubset V_{S}
.

5.
Strongly generation property of conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs

In this Section, we discuss the strongly finitely generating property, or equivalently, the
C_{1}
-cofiniteness of conic, Borel, and parabolic-type subVOAs introduced in Definition
3.4
. There is no analog of such property in the classical Lie theory. The reason is that VOAs are structurally modules over certain infinite-dimensional Lie algebras. We show that some of these subVOAs of
V_{L}
are
C_{1}
-cofinite, some of them are not. Hence they give rise to natural examples of CFT-type non-
C_{1}
-cofinite VOAs, which are rare among the classical examples of CFT-type VOAs.

5.1.
C_{1}
-cofiniteness of small rank conic-type subVOAs

The notion of strongly generation property of VOAs was introduced by Kac in
[
K97
]
. A CFT-type VOA
V
is called
strongly generated
by a subset
U\subseteq V
if
V
is spanned by elements of the following form:

u^{1}_{-n_{1}}u^{2}_{-n_{2}}\dots u^{k}_{-n_{k}}u,\quad\mathrm{where}\quad u^{1},\dots,u^{k},u\in U,\quad n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots\geq n_{k}\geq 1.

(5.1)

Let
V
be a CFT-type VOA. The subspace
C_{1}(V)\subset V
was introduced by Li in
[
Li99
]
.

C_{1}(V):=\mathrm{span}\{a_{-1}b:a,b\in V_{+}\}+\mathrm{span}\{L(-1)v:v\in V_{+}\}.

(5.2)

V
is called
C_{1}
-cofinite if
\dim V/C_{1}(V)<\infty
. A similar
C_{1}
-cofinite condition on
V
-modules was introduced by Huang in
[
H05
]
. Karel and Li proved that a VOA
V
is strongly generated by a finite-dimensional subspace
U\subset V
if and only if
V
is
C_{1}
-cofinite
[
Li05
,
KL99
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
C=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{r}
be a conic-type submonoid of
L
. Assume that
(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})\geq 0
, for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq r
. Then
V_{C}
is strongly generated by
U:=\{\mathbf{1},\alpha_{i}(-1)\mathbf{1},e^{\alpha_{i}}:1\leq i\leq r\}.
In particular,
V_{C}
is
C_{1}
-cofinite.

Proof.

Let
W
be the subspace of
V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha}
spanned by elements of the form (
5.1
), with
u^{j},u\in U
.
We need to show that each
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,n_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r})
is contained in
W
, for all non-negative integers
n_{j}\geq 0
. Clearly,
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subset W
. Since
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}e^{\alpha}
, and
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
is strongly generated by
\{\alpha_{1}(-1)\mathbf{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}(-1)\mathbf{1}\}
, we only need to show
e^{n_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r}}\in W
.

The following claim is very useful for our discussion in this Section:

\mathrm{If}\ e^{\alpha}\in W\ \mathrm{and}\ e^{\beta}\in W\ \mathrm{such\ that\ }(\alpha|\beta)\geq 0,\ \ \mathrm{then}\ e^{\alpha+\beta}\in W.

(5.3)

To prove (
5.3
), we observe that

\displaystyle e^{\alpha}_{-(\alpha|\beta)-1}e^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\Res_{z}z^{-(\alpha|\beta)-1}E^{-}(-\alpha,z)E^{+}(-\alpha,z)e_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}e^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\Res_{z}z^{-(\alpha|\beta)-1}E^{-}(-\alpha,z)z^{(\alpha|\beta)}\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)e^{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)e^{\alpha+\beta}\equiv 0\pmod{W}.

Hence
e^{\alpha+\beta}\in W
as
\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)\neq 0
. Furthermore, since
(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq r
and
(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i})=2N_{i}>0
for all
i
, we have

(m_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}|n_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{j}(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})\geq 0,

for any
m_{i},n_{j}\geq 0
. In particular, if
e^{m_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+m_{r}\alpha_{r}}\in W
and
e^{n_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r}}\in W
, we have
e^{(n_{1}+m_{1})\alpha_{1}+\dots+(n_{r}+m_{r})\alpha_{r}}\in W
by (
5.3
). Then it follows from an easy induction that
e^{n_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\alpha_{r}}\in W
for any
n_{i}\geq 0
.
∎

It follows immediately from the Lemma and Example
2.5
that any rank-one conic-type or Borel-type VOA

is
C_{1}
-cofinite. However, the rank-two case is much more complicated. We have the following general theorem:

.

Let

, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}(V_{B})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\{u_{-1}v:u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)\cap(V_{B})_{+},v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma^{\prime})\cap(V_{B})_{+},\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in B\}

\displaystyle\ +\mathrm{span}\{L(-1)w:w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\theta)\cap(V_{B})_{+},\theta\in B\}.

Suppose there exists some
m\geq 1
such that
e^{\beta-m\alpha}\in C_{1}(V_{B})
. Since the Heisenberg modules
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
are in direct sum in
V_{B}
, and they are invariant under
L(-1)
, then by (
3.8
) we have
\beta-m\alpha\in B+B
. Write
\beta-m\alpha=\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}
for some
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in B
. By the definition of
B
, the only possibilities for the pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
are

(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=(\beta-(m+k)\alpha,k\alpha)\quad\mathrm{or}\quad(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=(k\alpha,\beta-(m+k)\alpha),

where
k\geq 0
. Consider the following subspaces of
C_{1}(V_{B})
:

\displaystyle W_{1}:

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\{u_{-1}v:u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta-(m+k)\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+},\ v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}\},

\displaystyle W_{2}:

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\{u^{\prime}_{-1}v^{\prime}:u^{\prime}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+},\ v^{\prime}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta-(m+k)\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}\},

\displaystyle W_{3}:

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\{L(-1)w:w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta-m\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}\}.

We have
e^{\beta-m\alpha}\in W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}
.

Note that
\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})=(\beta-m\alpha|\beta-m\alpha)/2=m^{2}+m+1
. For any homogeneous elements
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta-(m+k)\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}
and
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}
, if
k>0
then

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(u_{-1}v)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{wt}u+\mathrm{wt}v\geq\frac{(\beta-(m+k)\alpha|\beta-(m+k)\alpha)}{2}+\frac{(k\alpha|k\alpha)}{2}

\displaystyle=1+(m+k)+(m+k)^{2}+k^{2}>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha}).

If
k=0
, then
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\cap(V_{B})_{+}\subseteq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)_{t}
, and

\mathrm{wt}(u_{-1}v)\geq\frac{(\beta-m\alpha|\beta-m\alpha)}{2}+\mathrm{wt}(v)\geq(m^{2}-m+1)+1>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha}).

Thus,
\mathrm{wt}(u_{-1}v)>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})
. Similarly, we can show
\mathrm{wt}(u^{\prime}_{-1}v^{\prime})>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})
for any
u^{\prime}_{-1}v^{\prime}\in W_{2}
. Finally, for any
L(-1)w\in W_{3}
, with
w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta-m\alpha)\cap(V_{B})_{+}
, we have
\mathrm{wt}(L(-1)w)=\mathrm{wt}(w)+1>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})
since
\mathrm{wt}(w)\geq\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})
. Therefore, for any homogeneous element
x\in W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}
, we have
\mathrm{wt}(x)>\mathrm{wt}(e^{\beta-m\alpha})
. This contradicts
e^{\beta-m\alpha}\in W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
B=\{m\alpha+n\beta:m,n\in\mathbb{Z},n>0\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha
be the Borel-type submonoid of
A_{2}
as in Example
2.6
. Then
V_{B}
is
not

C_{1}
-cofinite.

Proof.

It suffices to show elements in the set
S=\{\mathbf{1}+C_{1}(V_{B}),e^{\beta-m\alpha}+C_{1}(V_{B}):m\geq 1\}
are linearly independent in
V_{B}/C_{1}(V_{B})
. Indeed, by Lemma
5.6
, these elements are nonzero. Since
L(0)C_{1}(V)\subseteq C_{1}(V)
, it induces a well-defined linear map
L(0):V_{B}/C_{1}(V_{B})\rightarrow V_{B}/C_{1}(V_{B})
. For
m=1,2,\dots
, we have

L(0)(e^{\beta-m\alpha}+C_{1}(V_{B}))=(m^{2}+m+1)(e^{\beta-m\alpha}+C_{1}(V_{B})).

i.e., the elements in
S
are in different grading subspaces of
V_{B}/C_{1}(V_{B})
. Hence the elements in
S
are linearly independent.
∎

V_{M}

6.
The rank-one Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}
of
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}

In this Section, we fix the rank-one lattice
L=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
, with
(\alpha|\alpha)=2N
for some
N\geq 1
, and
\epsilon(m\alpha,n\alpha)=1
, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and study its Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}
, where
B=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha
. Note that
V_{B}
is also of conic-type and parabolic-type, see Example
2.5
.
Our goal is to show that the Zhu’s algebra
A(V_{B})
has the following identification as associative algebras:

A(V_{B})\cong\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle/\langle y^{2},yx+Ny,xy-Ny\rangle,

(6.1)

where
\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle
is the tensor algebra on generators
x
and
y
.

First, we recall the definition and basic properties of Zhu’s algebra
A(V)
in
[
Z96
,
FZ92
]
. Let
(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega)
be a VOA. For homogeneous elements
a,b\in V
, define

\displaystyle a\circ b

\displaystyle:=\Res_{z}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}}{z^{2}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\mathrm{wt}a}{j}a_{j-2}b,

(6.2)

\displaystyle a\ast b

\displaystyle:=\Res_{z}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}}{z}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\mathrm{wt}a}{j}a_{j-1}b.

(6.3)

Let
O(V)=\mathrm{span}\{a\circ b:a,b\in V\}
, and let
A(V):=V/O(V)
. By
[
Z96
, Theorem 2.1.1]
,
O(V)
is a two-sided ideal with respect to
\ast
, and
A(V)
is an associative algebra with respect to
\ast
, with the unit element
[\mathbf{1}]
. By
[
Z96
, Lemma 2.1.3]
, we also have the following formulas:

\displaystyle a\ast b

\displaystyle\equiv\Res_{z}Y(b,z)a\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}b-1}}{z}\pmod{O(V)},

(6.4)

\displaystyle a\ast b-b\ast a

\displaystyle\equiv\Res_{z}Y(a,z)b(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a-1}\pmod{O(V)},

(6.5)

for any homogeneous
a,b\in V
. Furthermore, if
m\geq n\geq 0
, one has

\Res_{z}Y(a,z)b\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a+n}}{z^{2+m}}\equiv 0\pmod{O(V)}.

(6.6)

6.1.
A spanning set of
O(V_{B})

To determine the structure of
A(V_{B})
, we first give a description of
O(V_{B})
in its definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
V_{B}=V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha}
be the Borel-type subVOA of
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
, with
(\alpha|\alpha)=2N
.
Let
O^{\prime}
be the subspace of
V_{B}
spanned by the following elements:

\begin{cases}&\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u,\qquad u\in V_{B},\ \mathrm{and}\ n\geq 0,\\
&\alpha(-1)v+Nv,\qquad v\in\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha),\\
&M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha),\qquad k\geq 2.\end{cases}

(6.7)

We want to show that
O(V_{B})=O^{\prime}
. Then the structure of
A(V_{B})
would be clear.

6.1.1.
Proof of
O^{\prime}\subseteq O(V_{B})

It is not difficult to show
O^{\prime}\subseteq O(V_{B})
. Indeed, it is clear from (
6.6
) that
\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u\in O(V_{B})
, for all
u\in V_{B}
and
n\geq 0
. By
[
Z96
, Theorem 2.1.1]
, we also have

a\ast O(V_{B})\subset O(V_{B}),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad O(V_{B})\ast a\subset O(V_{B}),\quad a\in V.

(6.8)

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
k\geq 2
, we have
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\subset O(V_{B})
.

Proof.

By the proof of Proposition
6.9
, we have
e^{k\alpha}\in O(V_{B})
, for any
k\geq 2
. By (
6.4
), we have
u\ast\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}\equiv\alpha(-1)u\pmod{O(V_{B})}.
Now by (
6.6
) and (
6.8
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)

\displaystyle e^{k\alpha}\equiv(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}\alpha(-1)^{r}e^{k\alpha}\pmod{O(V_{B})}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}e^{k\alpha}\ast(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})\ast\dots\ast(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})\pmod{O(V_{B})}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O(V_{B})},

for any
k\geq 2
,
r\geq 1
, and
n_{1},\dots n_{r}\geq 0
, where the last congruence follows from (
6.8
). Thus we have
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\subset O(V_{B})
, for all
k\geq 2
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
v\in\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha)
, we have
\alpha(-1)v+Nv\in O(V_{B})
.

Proof.

If
m\geq 2
and
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha)
, then by Lemma
6.2
, we have
v\in O(V_{B})
, and

\alpha(-1)v+Nv\equiv v\ast(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})+Nv\equiv 0\pmod{O(V_{B})},

by (
6.8
). Now let
m=1
, by the proof of Proposition
6.9
, we have
\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{1}\equiv 0\pmod{O(V_{B})}.
Let
v=\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)e^{\alpha}
be a general spanning element of
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
, where
r\geq 1
, and
n_{1},\dots n_{r}\geq 0
. Since
[\alpha(-1),\alpha(-p)]=0
for all
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha(-1)v+Nv

\displaystyle=\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)(\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}\alpha(-1)^{r}(\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha})\pmod{O(V_{B})}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}(\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha})\ast(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})\ast\dots\ast(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})\pmod{O(V_{B})}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O(V_{B})},

where the last congruence follows from (
6.8
) and the fact that
\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha}\in O(V_{B})
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
6.3
and Lemma
6.3
that
O^{\prime}\subseteq O(V_{B})
.

6.1.2.
Outline of the strategy for proving
O(V_{B})\subseteq O^{\prime}

Conversely, by the definition of
O(V_{B})
(
6.2
), to show
O(V_{B})\subseteq O^{\prime}
, we need to show that
a\circ u=\Res_{z}Y(a,z)u((1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}/z^{2})\in O^{\prime}
, for any homogeneous
a,u\in V_{B}
.

Note that if
a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha)
and
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,n\alpha)
for some
m,n\geq 1
, then by (
3.3
) and (
3.8
),

a\circ u=\Res_{z}Y(a,z)u\frac{(1+z)^{\mathrm{wt}a}}{z^{2}}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,(m+n)\alpha)((z))\subset O^{\prime}((z)),

as
m+n\geq 2
and
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
k\geq 2
. Since
V_{B}=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{\infty}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha)
as a vector space, it suffices to show:

a\circ u\in O^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for}\quad\begin{cases}a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)&\mathrm{and}\ \ \ u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0),\\
\mathrm{or}&\\
a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)&\mathrm{and}\ \ \ u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha).\end{cases}

(6.9)

We will only present a detailed proof for the first case of (
6.9
). i.e.,
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subseteq O^{\prime}
. The second case follows from a similar argument (see also (3.1.5) and (3.1.6) in
[
FZ92
]
), we omit the detail. Our strategy of proving
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subseteq O^{\prime}
can be outlined as follows:

Step 1.

Let
u=a(-m_{1})\dots a(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
. Use an induction on the length
s
of
u
to show that

\Res_{z}Y(e^{\alpha},z)u\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}\in O^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for\ any}\ n\geq 0.

Note that
N=\mathrm{wt}(e^{\alpha})
.

Step 2.

Let
a=\alpha(-k)e^{\alpha}
for some
k\geq 1
. Use an induction on the number
k\geq 1
, together with the result in Step 1, to show that for any
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
,

\Res_{z}Y(a(-k)e^{\alpha},z)u\frac{(1+z)^{N+k}}{z^{2+n}}\in O^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for\ any}\ n\geq 0.

Note that
N+k=\mathrm{wt}(a(-k)e^{\alpha})
.

Step 3.

Let
a=\alpha(-k)\alpha(-n_{2})\dots\alpha(-n_{r})e^{\alpha}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
, where
k\geq 1
. Use an induction on the length
r
of
a
(with the base case given by Steps 1 and 2), together with another induction on the number
k\geq 1
, to show that for any
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
,

\Res_{z}Y(\alpha(-k)\alpha(-n_{2})\dots\alpha(-n_{r})e^{\alpha},z)u\frac{(1+z)^{N+k+n_{2}+\dots+n_{r}}}{z^{2+n}}\in O^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for\ any}\ n\geq 0.

Note that
N+k+n_{2}+\dots+n_{r}=\mathrm{wt}(\alpha(-k)\alpha(-n_{2})\dots\alpha(-n_{r})e^{\alpha})
.

Then the conclusion in Step 3 would indicate
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subseteq O^{\prime}
, and the proof of the inclusion
O(V_{B})\subseteq O^{\prime}
would be complete.

6.2.
Proof of the main Theorem

Now we carry out the details for proving:

a\circ u\in O^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for}\quad\begin{cases}a=\alpha(-n_{1})\dots\alpha(-n_{r})e^{\alpha}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha),\\
u=\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0),\end{cases}

(6.10)

where
r,s\geq 0
,
n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 1
, and
m_{1}\geq\dots\geq m_{s}\geq 1
, following the steps outlined above.
Then by our discussions in Section
6.1
, this would lead to a proof of the following main theorem in this Section:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
V_{B}=V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha}
be the Borel-type subVOA of
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
, with
(\alpha|\alpha)=2N
. Then

\begin{split}O(V_{B})=O^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}\bigg\{\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u,\ \alpha(-1)v+v,\ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha):&\\
n\geq 0,\ u\in V_{B},\ v\in\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha),\ k\geq 2\bigg\}.\end{split}

(6.11)

6.2.1.
Proof of the claim in
Step 1.

We prove a Lemma first.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
m\geq 1
, we have
\alpha(-m)O^{\prime}\subset O^{\prime}
. For any
a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
, we have
L(-1)a+L(0)a\in O^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
[\alpha(-m),\alpha(-n)]=0
for any
m,n\geq 1
, and
\alpha(-m)M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)\subset M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,k\alpha)
, for any
k\geq 0
, we have
\alpha(-m)O^{\prime}\subset O^{\prime}
, in view of (
6.7
).

Let
a=\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{\alpha}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
, where
r\geq 0
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}\geq 1
. Since
L(-1)e^{\alpha}=(e^{\alpha})_{-2}\mathbf{1}=\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}
, and
[L(-1),\alpha(-m)]=ma(-m-1)
, we have

\displaystyle L(-1)\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{r})e^{\alpha}+L(0)\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{r})\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_{j}\cdot\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{j}-1)\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad+(m_{1}+\dots+m_{k}+N)\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})(\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{s}(\alpha(-m_{j}-1)+\alpha(-m_{j}))\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\widehat{\alpha(-m_{j})}\dots\alpha(-m_{s})e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O^{\prime}},

where the last congruence follows from
\alpha(-1)e^{\alpha}+Ne^{\alpha}\in O^{\prime}
,
\alpha(-m)O^{\prime}\subset O^{\prime}
for any
m\geq 1
, and
\alpha(-n-1)v+\alpha(-n)v\in O^{\prime}
for all
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
and
n\geq 1
, in view of (
6.7
).
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
, and
n\geq 0
. We have

\Res_{z}Y(e^{\alpha},z)u\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}\in O^{\prime}.

(6.12)

Proof.

We use induction on the length
s
of a spanning element
u=\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}
of
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
, where
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}\geq 1
. The base case is
u=\mathbf{1}
. First, we note that

e^{\alpha}_{-j-1}\mathbf{1}=\frac{1}{j!}(L(-1)^{j}e^{\alpha})_{-1}\mathbf{1}=\frac{1}{j!}L(-1)^{j}e^{\alpha},\quad j\geq 0.

Since
e^{\alpha}_{-j-1}\mathbf{1}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)
for any
j\geq 0
, by Lemma
6.5
we have

\displaystyle L(-1)^{j}e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv-L(0)L(-1)^{j-1}e^{\alpha}\pmod{O^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(-1)(N+j-1)L(-1)^{j-1}e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{j}(N+j-1)(N+j-2)\dots(N+1)Ne^{\alpha}\pmod{O^{\prime}}.

Then it follows from the definition of binomial coefficients that

\displaystyle Y(e^{\alpha},z)\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{\alpha}_{-j-1}\mathbf{1}z^{j}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{j!}L(-1)^{j}z^{j}e^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\frac{(N+j-1)(N+j-2)\dots(N+1)N}{j!}z^{j}e^{\alpha}\pmod{O^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(-N-j+1)(-N-j+2)\dots(-N-1)(-N)}{j!}z^{j}e^{\alpha}

(6.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-N}{j}z^{j}e^{\alpha}=(1+z)^{-N}e^{\alpha}.

Now by (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), and the assumption that
n\geq 0
, we have

\Res_{z}Y(e^{\alpha},z)\mathbf{1}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}\equiv\Res_{z}(1+z)^{-N}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}e^{\alpha}=\Res_{z}\frac{1}{z^{2+n}}e^{\alpha}=0\pmod{O^{\prime}}.

This finishes the proof of the base case. Assume the conclusion holds for smaller length
s
. Note that for any
m\geq 1
, we have

[\alpha(-m),Y(e^{\alpha},z)]=\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{-m}{i}Y(\alpha(i)e^{\alpha},z)z^{-m-i}=2NY(e^{\alpha},z)z^{-m}.

Then by
\alpha(-m)O^{\prime}\subset O^{\prime}
in Lemma
6.5
, the base case and the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\Res_{z}Y(e^{\alpha},z)\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}

\displaystyle=\Res_{z}\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})Y(e^{\alpha},z)\mathbf{1}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\Res_{z}\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{j-1})[\alpha(-m_{j}),Y(e^{\alpha},z)]\alpha(m_{j+1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{j=1}^{s}2N\Res_{z}\alpha(-m_{1})\dots\alpha(-m_{j-1})Y(e^{\alpha},z)\alpha(-m_{j+1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{2+n+m_{j}}}\pmod{O^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O^{\prime}}

where the last congruence follows from the induction hypothesis, which indicates that

\Res_{z}Y(e^{\alpha},z)\alpha(-m_{j+1})\dots\alpha(-m_{s})\mathbf{1}((1+z)^{N})/z^{2+n+m_{j}}\in O^{\prime}.

Hence (
6.12
) holds for any
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
and
n\geq 0
.
∎

6.2.2.
Proof of the claim in
Step 2.

Proposition 6.7
.

For any
k\geq 1
,
n\geq 0
, and
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
, we have

\Res_{z}Y(\alpha(-k)e^{\alpha},z)u\frac{(1+z)^{N+k}}{z^{2+n}}\in O^{\prime}.

(6.14)

Proof.

It is easy to derive the following formula from the Jacobi identity of VOAs:

\displaystyle G(\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u)

\displaystyle G(\alpha(-n-1)u+\alpha(-n-1)u)

\displaystyle=G(\alpha(-n-2)\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)e^{\alpha})+G(\alpha(-n-1)\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots e^{\alpha})

for any

as

. Thus,

\displaystyle G:A(V_{B})=V_{B}/O(V_{B})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle/\langle y^{2},yx+Ny,xy-Ny\rangle,\quad\mathrm{such\ that}

\displaystyle G([\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)\mathbf{1}])

\displaystyle=G((-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}[\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}]^{r})=(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}x^{r},

(6.26)

\displaystyle G([\alpha(-n_{1}-1)\dots\alpha(-n_{r}-1)e^{\alpha}])

for any

,

, and
k\geq 2
. Since

is spanned by elements of the form

and

6.4.
Applications of the main theorem

As an immediate application of the structural theorem for
A(V_{B})
, we can classify the irreducible modules over
V_{B}
and compute the fusion rules among them. We can also present a spanning set for
O(V_{A_{1}})
, which was unknown.

6.4.1.
Classification of irreducible modules over
V_{B}

Lemma 6.11
.

If
U\neq 0
is an irreducible module over
A(V_{B})\cong\mathbb{C}[x]\oplus\mathbb{C}y
, then we must have
y.U=0
, and
U\cong\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\alpha
, with
x.e^{\lambda}=(\alpha|\lambda)e^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

By (
6.24
),
\mathbb{C}y
is an ideal of
A(V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha})
. Then
y.U\leq U
is an
A(V_{B})
-submodule, and so
y.U
is either
U
or
0
. If
y.U=U
, then we have
0=y^{2}.U=y.U=U
, a contradiction. Thus,
y.U=0
and
U
is an irreducible module over
\mathbb{C}[x]
. We have
U\cong\mathbb{C}[x]/\mathfrak{m}
, for some maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{C}[x]
. By Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz, we have
\mathfrak{m}=\langle x-\mu\rangle
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Choose
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}
so that
(\alpha|\lambda)=\mu
. Then
U\cong\mathbb{C}[x]/\langle x-(\alpha|\lambda)\rangle\cong\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}
, with
x.e^{\lambda}=(\alpha|\lambda)e^{\lambda}
.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

For any irreducible module
W=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda)
over the Heisenberg VOA
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
,
W
is also an irreducible module over the Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}
, where
Y_{W}:V_{B}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(W)[[z,z^{-1}]]
satisfies
Y_{W}(a,z)=0
, for any
a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,n\alpha)
and
n\geq 1
, and
Y_{W}|_{M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)}
is given by the action of the Heisenberg VOA
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
.

Proof.

By (
3.8
), for any
a\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,n\alpha)
and
b\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha)
, where
m,n\geq 0
,
Y_{W}(a,z)b
is either
0
or given by the Heisenberg module vertex operator. Hence
(W,Y_{W})
is a well-defined module over the Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}
. It is clear that
W
is irreducible.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

\Sigma=\left\{(W=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda),Y_{W}):\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}\alpha\right\}
, with
Y_{W}
defined by Lemma
6.12
, is a complete list of irreducible modules over the rank-one Borel-type subVOA
V_{B}
.

Moreover, the fusion rule between the irreducible
V_{B}
-modules
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda),M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\mu)
, and
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
is the same as the fusion rule of these modules as modules over the Heisenberg VOA. In other words,
N{\binom{M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)}{M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda)\;M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\mu)}}\cong\delta_{\lambda+\mu,\gamma}
.

Proof.

Given a module
(W=M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda),Y_{W})
in
\Sigma
, the bottom degree
W(0)=\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}
is an
A(V_{B})
-module, with the actions of
x=[\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}]
and
y=[e^{\alpha}]
given by

x.e^{\alpha}=o(\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})e^{\lambda}=(\alpha|\lambda)e^{\lambda},\quad y.e^{\lambda}=o(e^{\alpha})e^{\lambda}=\Res_{z}z^{N-1}Y_{W}(e^{\alpha},z)e^{\lambda}=0,

see
[
FZ92
,
Z96
]
. By Lemma
6.12
, such
A(V_{B})
-modules
W(0)
, with
W
varies in
\Sigma
, are all the irreducible modules over
A(V_{B})
up to isomorphism. Then by Theorem 2.2.2 in
[
Z96
]
,
\Sigma
is the complete list of irreducible modules over
V_{B}
. Finally, note that any intertwining operator between modules over the Heisenberg VOA
I\in I{\binom{M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)}{M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\lambda)\;M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\mu)}}
can be naturally lifted up to an intertwining operator
\tilde{I}
of

, since the Jacobi identity of

is

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{W^{3}}(a,z_{1})I(v,z_{2})u-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{-z_{2}+z_{1}}{z_{0}}\right)I(v,z_{2})Y_{W^{2}}(a,z_{1})u

(6.27)

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)I(Y_{W^{1}}(a,z_{0})v,z_{2})u,

and

for

6.4.2.
A spanning set of
O(A_{1})

For the
A_{1}
-root lattice
A_{1}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
with
(\alpha|\alpha)=2
, the following Corollary presents a spanning set of
O(V_{A_{1}})
.

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
L=A_{1}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
. Then
O(V_{A_{1}})
is spanned by the following elements:

\begin{cases}&\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u,\qquad u\in V_{A_{1}},\ \mathrm{and}\ n\geq 0,\\
&\pm\alpha(-1)v+v,\qquad v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm\alpha),\\
&M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm k\alpha),\qquad k\geq 2.\\
&\alpha(-1)^{3}w-\alpha(-1)w,\qquad w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0).\end{cases}

(6.28)

Proof.

Let
O^{\prime\prime}
be the subspace of
V_{A_{1}}
spanned by the elements (
6.28
). Since both
O(V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha})
and
O(V_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(-\alpha)})
are contained in
O(V_{A_{1}})
,
by Lemma
6.2
and
6.3
, we have
\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u\in O(V_{A_{1}})
for any
u\in V_{A_{1}}
,
\pm\alpha(-1)v+v\in O(V_{A_{1}})
for any
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm\alpha)
, and
M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm k\alpha)\subset O(V_{A_{1}})
for all
k\geq 2
. Moreover, since
\mathrm{wt}e^{\alpha}=2
and
(\alpha|-\alpha)=-2
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\alpha}\circ e^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha}_{-2}e^{-\alpha}+e^{\alpha}_{-1}e^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\alpha(-3)\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{4}(\alpha(-2)\alpha(-1)+\alpha(-1)\alpha(-2))\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{3!}\alpha(-1)^{3}\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha(-2)\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{2!}\alpha(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{3}\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{6}\alpha(-1)^{3}\mathbf{1}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{6}(\alpha(-1)^{3}\mathbf{1}-\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1})\pmod{O(V_{A_{1}})}.

Hence
\alpha(-1)^{3}w-\alpha(-1)w\in O(V_{A_{1}})
for any
w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
, in view of the proof of Lemma
6.3
. This shows
O^{\prime\prime}\subseteq O(V_{A_{1}})
. Conversely, with a similar argument as Proposition
6.9
and Corollary
6.10
, we can easily show that

V_{A_{1}}/O^{\prime\prime}\cong\mathbb{C}z\oplus\mathbb{C}[x]/\langle x^{3}-x\rangle\oplus\mathbb{C}y,\quad[e^{-\alpha}]\mapsto z,\ [\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}]\mapsto x,\ [e^{\alpha}]\mapsto y.

In particular, we have
\dim V_{A_{1}}/O^{\prime\prime}=5
. On the other hand, we have
\dim V/O(V_{A_{1}})=5
, since
V_{A_{1}}
has two irreducible modules
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha}
and
V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\alpha}
, whose bottom degrees are of dimensions
1
and
2
, respectively, see
[
D93
, Theorem 3.1]
. Using the one-to-one correspondence between irreducible
A(V_{A_{1}})
-modules and irreducible
V_{A_{1}}
-modules (cf.
[
Z96
, Theorem 2.2.2]
), we have
O^{\prime\prime}=O(V_{A_{1}})
, as there is an epimorphism
V_{A_{1}}/O^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow A(V_{A_{1}})
.
∎

Finite-Time Error Analysis of Soft Q-Learning: Switching System Approach
Narim Jeong, Donghwan Lee

Abstract
Soft Q-learning is a variation of Q-learning designed to solve entropy regularized Markov decision problems where an agent aims to maximize the entropy regularized value function. Despite its empirical success, there have been limited theoretical studies of soft Q-learning to date. This paper aims to offer a novel and unified finite-time, control-theoretic analysis of soft Q-learning algorithms. We focus on two types of soft Q-learning algorithms: one utilizing the log-sum-exp operator and the other employing the Boltzmann operator. By using dynamical switching system models, we derive novel finite-time error bounds for both soft Q-learning algorithms. We hope that our analysis will deepen the current understanding of soft Q-learning by establishing connections with switching system models and may even pave the way for new frameworks in the finite-time analysis of other reinforcement learning algorithms.

PRELIMINARIES

II-A

Markov Decision Problem

We consider a Markov decision process (MDP) that has the state-space
\mathcal{S}:=\{1,2,\ldots,|\mathcal{S}|\}
and action-space
\mathcal{A}:=\{1,2,\ldots,|\mathcal{A}|\}
, where
|\mathcal{S}|
and
|\mathcal{A}|
indicate the cardinality of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
, respectively. Based on a current state
s_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
in time-step
k
, an agent selects an action
a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
with a policy
\pi
. Then, with the state transition probability
P(s_{k}^{\prime}|s_{k},a_{k})
, the state
s_{k}
transits to the next state
s_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
, which incurs a reward
r_{k}:=r(s_{k},a_{k},s_{k}^{\prime})
.

Given an MDP, the Markov decision problem is to discover an optimal policy
\pi^{*}
that maximizes the cumulative discounted rewards, i.e.,

\pi^{*}:=\mathrm{argmax}_{\pi\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r_{k}\Bigg|\pi\right].

Here,
\Theta
is the set of all possible policies,
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\pi]
is the expectation with regard to the policy
\pi
, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discount factor.

The Q-function represents the expected cumulative rewards when action
a
is taken at state
s
and a given policy
\pi
is followed afterwards, denoted as

Q^{\pi}(s,a):=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r_{k}\Bigg|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a,\pi\right],s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}.

Moreover, the optimal Q-function refers to the Q-function produced by the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
, defined as
Q^{*}(s,a):=Q^{\pi^{*}}(s,a)
with
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}
. After the optimal Q-function
Q^{*}
is determined, the optimal policy can be obtained as the greedy policy
\pi^{*}(s)=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q^{*}(s,a)
.

Switching System

II-C

Soft Q-learning

Through this paper, we consider the two different versions of soft Q-learning presented in
Algorithm

1

whereas the Boltzmann operator if

II-D

Assumptions and Definitions

We assume that the state-action pairs
\{(s_{k},a_{k})\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are selected independently and identically distributed under the behavior policy
\pi_{b}

In addition, we adopt the following assumptions for analytical convenience.

Assumption II.1

Throughout this paper, we consider the following assumptions:

1.

The step size

2.

The reward is unit-bounded as follows:

\max_{(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}|r(s,a,s^{\prime})|=:R_{max}\leq 1.

3.

The initial iterate of the Q-functions

and

\left\|Q_{0}^{LSE}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\;\text{and}\;\left\|Q_{0}^{Boltz}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

4.

We note that the assumption

Moreover, the terms listed below will be used frequently in this work.

We revisit the switching system framework for Q-learning in

\displaystyle-(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})^{T}Q_{k}

Using the optimal Bellman equation

Then, the corresponding lower and upper comparison systems are obtained, which are simpler linear or switching systems. Using these comparison systems, a finite-time error bound of LSE soft Q-learning can be derived.

X_{k}
is bounded as

\displaystyle\text{tr}(X_{k})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{6\alpha\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)^{2}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{\beta^{2}d_{min}(1-\gamma)^{3}}

\displaystyle+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-{Q^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rho^{2k}.

The proof of
Lemma

IV.4
are available in Appendix.

Then, we can compute a finite-time error bound for the lower comparison system of LSE soft Q-learning.

Theorem IV.1

For any
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(10)

\displaystyle+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-{Q^{*}}\right\|_{2}\rho^{k}.

Proof:

Using the relation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)^{T}\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\text{tr}\left(\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)^{T}\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\text{tr}\left(\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)^{T}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)^{T}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{tr}(X_{k})

and
Lemma

IV.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{6\alpha\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)^{2}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{\beta^{2}d_{min}(1-\gamma)^{3}}+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-{Q^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rho^{2k}.

Taking the square root on both sides of the above inequality and combining the relation
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}]}
leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{6\alpha\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)^{2}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{\beta^{2}d_{min}(1-\gamma)^{3}}+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{2}\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-{Q^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rho^{2k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-{Q^{*}}\right\|_{2}\rho^{k},

where the subadditivity of the square root function is utilized in the last inequality.
∎

Note that the first term on the right-hand side of (
10
) is the constant error that can be minimized by the smaller step size
\alpha\in(0,1)
and the larger parameter
\beta
. The second term
O(\rho^{k})
exponentially decays as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

IV-C

Upper Comparison System of LSE Soft Q-learning

Let us consider the affine system

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{U}-Q^{*}=

\displaystyle A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}\left(Q_{k}^{U}-Q^{*}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle+\alpha w_{k}^{LSE}+\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

with

A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}:=I+\alpha\left(\gamma DP\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}-D\right)

and the stochastic noise (
6
), which is called
upper comparison system of LSE soft Q-learning
. Supported by the following proposition, this system can be an upper bound for the LSE soft Q-learning iteration.

Proposition IV.3

Suppose
Q_{0}^{U}-Q^{*}\geq Q_{0}^{LSE}-Q^{*}
, where
Q_{0}^{U}-Q^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
and “
\geq
” is the element-wise inequality. Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

Q_{k}^{U}-Q^{*}\geq Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}.

Proof:

To apply the induction argument, assume that
Q_{k}^{U}-Q^{*}\geq Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}
holds for some
k\geq 0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{LSE}-Q^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{LSE}-{Q^{*}}

\displaystyle+\alpha\left\{DR+\gamma DPH_{LSE}^{\beta}\left(Q_{k}^{LSE}\right)-DQ_{k}^{LSE}+w_{k}^{LSE}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{LSE}-{Q^{*}}+\alpha DR

\displaystyle+\alpha\gamma DP\left({\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}Q_{k}^{LSE}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(-DQ_{k}^{LSE}+w_{k}^{LSE}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{LSE}-{Q^{*}}-\alpha\left(\gamma DP{\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}}-D\right)Q^{*}

\displaystyle+\alpha\gamma DP\left({\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}Q_{k}^{LSE}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha\gamma DP\left(\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}Q^{*}-\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}Q^{*}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(-DQ_{k}^{LSE}+w_{k}^{LSE}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{I+\alpha\left(\gamma DP{\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}-D\right)\right\}\left(Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha\gamma DP\left(\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}-\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}\right)Q^{*}

\displaystyle+\alpha w_{k}^{LSE}+\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{I+\alpha\left(\gamma DP{\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}-D\right)\right\}\left(Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha w_{k}^{LSE}+\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{I+\alpha\left(\gamma DP{\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}-D\right)\right\}\left(Q_{k}^{U}-{Q^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle+\alpha w_{k}^{LSE}+\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{U}-Q^{*},

where
Proposition

IV.1
is used in the first inequality and the optimal Bellman equation is utilized in the second equality. The second inequality holds because
\alpha\gamma DP\left(\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}-\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}\right)Q^{*}\leq
\alpha\gamma DP\left(\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}-\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}\right)Q^{*}=0
. Moreover, the last inequality relies on the hypothesis
Q_{k}^{U}-Q^{*}\geq Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}
and the fact that all elements of
I+\alpha\left(\gamma DP{\Pi_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}^{\max}}-D\right)
are non-negative. This brings the proof to its conclusion.
∎

The upper comparison system (
11
) includes the non-constant matrix
A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}
rather than
A_{Q^{*}}
, and
A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}
switches according to the variations of
Q_{k}^{LSE}
. This can be a problem for analyzing the stability since
A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}
and
Q_{k}^{U}-Q^{*}
depend on the same stochastic noise (
6
), and hence, they are statistically dependent. This means that the linear recursion in (
9
) cannot be used again for our analysis of the upper comparison system. To overcome this obstacle, we will apply a different strategy for the analysis of the upper comparison system, which is presented in the next subsection.

IV-D

Error System for LSE Soft Q-learning System Analysis

To overcome the aforementioned difficulty, we examine an error system created by subtracting the lower comparison system (
7
) from the upper comparison system (
11
):

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{U}-Q_{k+1}^{L}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}\left(Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\right)

\displaystyle+B_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)+\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

with

B_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}:=A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}-A_{Q^{*}}.

In this case, the stochastic noise
w_{k}^{LSE}
is eliminated from the error system of LSE soft Q-learning. Here,
A_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}
and
B_{Q_{k}^{LSE}}
alter in response to
Q_{k}^{LSE}
, and
Q_{k}^{L}-Q*
can be regarded as an external disturbance.

The subsequent analysis is based on the following intuition: as
Q_{k}^{L}\rightarrow Q^{*}
in
Theorem

IV.1
, we can easily show
Q_{k}^{LSE}\rightarrow Q^{*}
if we can prove
Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q_{k}^{L}+Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q_{k}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right],

where the last inequality is derived from
Proposition

IV.3
.

Then, we get a finite-time error bound for the LSE soft Q-learning in the following theorem.

Theorem IV.2

For any
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

(14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}k\rho^{k-1}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{{\beta{d_{\min}}(1-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}\rho^{k}.

Proof:

Considering the norm of (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{i+1}^{U}-Q_{i+1}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A_{Q_{i}^{LSE}}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|Q_{i}^{U}-Q_{i}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|B_{Q_{i}^{LSE}}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|Q_{i}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\|\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho\left\|Q_{i}^{U}-Q_{i}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+2\alpha\gamma d_{max}\left\|Q_{i}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)

(15)

using
Lemma

IV.2
and the following results:

\displaystyle\|B_{Q_{i}^{LSE}}\|_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\|A_{Q_{i}^{LSE}}-A_{Q^{*}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\gamma d_{max}\left\|P\left(\Pi_{Q_{i}^{LSE}}^{\max}-\Pi_{Q^{*}}^{\max}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\gamma d_{max}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\gamma\|D\|_{\infty}\|P\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\gamma d_{max}\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ln(|\mathcal{A}|).

Taking the expectation of (
15
) and applying
Theorem

IV.1
leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{i+1}^{U}-Q_{i+1}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{i}^{U}-Q_{i}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]+2\alpha\gamma d_{max}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{i}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho E\left[\left\|Q_{i}^{U}-Q_{i}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\gamma d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle+2\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\rho^{i}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ln(|\mathcal{A}|).

Then, applying the inequality recursively and letting
Q_{0}^{U}=Q_{0}^{L}
produce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\gamma d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{i}

\displaystyle+2\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}k\rho^{k-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{i}.

Noting the relation

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|Q_{0}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2}{1-\gamma}

and
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}{{\rho^{i}}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\rho}
with
II.1
,
IV.1
and
II.2
, the last inequality turns into

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}k\rho^{k-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{{\beta{d_{\min}}(1-\gamma)}}.

Subsequently, combining (
13
),
Theorem

IV.1
, and the above inequality leads to the desired result as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{LSE}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{U}-Q_{k}^{L}\right\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}k\rho^{k-1}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{{\beta{d_{\min}}(1-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle+|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|\left\|Q_{0}^{L}-Q^{*}\right\|_{2}\rho^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}k\rho^{k-1}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{{\beta{d_{\min}}(1-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{2|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}\rho^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{max}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha\gamma d_{max}|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}k\rho^{k-1}+\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{{\beta{d_{\min}}(1-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{1-\gamma}\rho^{k},

where (
16
) is used in the third inequality and
\gamma\in[0,1)
,
Definition

II.1
is utilized in the last inequality.
∎

On the right-hand side of (
14
), the second and third terms result from the difference between the upper comparison system and the lower system, while the last term results from the difference between the lower comparison system and the original system. Furthermore, the second term
O(k\rho^{k-1})
and the last term
O(\rho^{k})
exponentially decay as
k\rightarrow\infty
. The first and third error terms can be reduced by decreasing the step size
\alpha
and increasing the parameter
\beta
, respectively.

V
FINITE-TIME ANALYSIS OF BOLTZMANN SOFT Q-LEARNING

In this section, we study the finite-time error analysis of Boltzmann soft Q-learning. Boltzmann soft Q-learning can be demonstrated in a similar manner to the previous one. However, as a result of changing the bounds for the operator, an alternative analysis in place of the autocorrelation matrix is required to establish the finite-time error bound of the lower comparison system.

V-A

Nonlinear System Representation of Boltzmann Soft Q-learning

Boltzmann soft Q-learning can be expressed as

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{Boltz}=

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{Boltz}+\alpha\Big\{DR+\gamma DPH_{Boltz}^{\beta}\left(Q_{k}^{Boltz}\right)

\displaystyle-DQ_{k}^{Boltz}+w_{k}^{Boltz}\Big\},

where

H_{Boltz}^{\beta}\left(Q_{k}\right):=\left[{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(Q_{k}(1,\cdot))\\
\vdots\\
h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(Q_{k}(|\mathcal{S}|,\cdot))\end{array}}\right],

\displaystyle w_{k}^{Boltz}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})r_{k}

\displaystyle+\gamma(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})(e_{s_{k}^{\prime}})^{T}H_{Boltz}^{\beta}\left(Q_{k}^{Boltz}\right)

\displaystyle-(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})(e_{a_{k}}\otimes e_{s_{k}})^{T}Q_{k}^{Boltz}

\displaystyle-\Big\{DR+\gamma DPH_{Boltz}^{\beta}\left(Q_{k}^{Boltz}\right)-DQ_{k}^{Boltz}\Big\}.

This nonlinear system can also be examined more easily with comparison systems, which are constructed on the basis of the following proposition:

Proposition V.1

For any
Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}
, we have

\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)-\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\leq h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot))\leq\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)

and hence

{\Pi_{Q}^{\max}}Q-\frac{{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}}{\beta}{\bf{1}}\leq H_{Boltz}^{\beta}\left(Q\right)\leq{\Pi_{Q}^{\max}}Q,

where “
\leq
” denotes the element-wise inequality and
1
is a column vector with all elements equal to 1.

The proof of
Proposition

V.1
is supplied in Appendix.

V-B

Lower Comparison System of Boltzmann Soft Q-learning

We take the lower comparison system of Boltzmann soft Q-learning as

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{L}-Q^{*}=

\displaystyle A_{Q^{*}}\left(Q_{k}^{L}-Q^{*}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle+\alpha w_{k}^{Boltz}-\alpha\gamma DP\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}\textbf{1}

with the stochastic noise (

), which can be proved with the following proposition. The proof is included in Appendix.

Proposition V.2

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)+\beta\right)|\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta d_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

V.1

Upper Comparison System of Boltzmann Soft Q-learning

VII
CONCLUSION

In this paper, we present a unique finite-time error analysis for soft Q-learning algorithms that use LSE and Boltzmann operators. Specifically, using the switching system approach, we find the upper and lower bounds of two operators and offer the upper and lower comparison systems of soft Q-learning. By proving that each comparison system converges, it is simple to provide a finite-time analysis of soft Q-learning. We believe that the suggested approach can give further perspectives using simple control-theoretic concepts and supplement the study on the analysis of soft Q-learning. For future works, obtaining tighter error bounds will be beneficial for the next steps. Moreover, we believe that the proposed approach will possibly function as a unified analysis framework to handle further soft Q-learning variations.

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\exp{\left(Q(s,a)\beta\right)}\right)

\displaystyle h_{LSE}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot)).

is a convex function in

h_{LSE}^{\beta}(y)\geq h_{LSE}^{\beta}(x)+\nabla_{x}h_{LSE}^{\beta}(x)^{T}(y-x)\text{, }\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

y=0
, then

\displaystyle h_{LSE}^{\beta}(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h_{LSE}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot))-\nabla_{Q(s,\cdot)}h_{LSE}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot))^{T}Q(s,\cdot)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)-\nabla_{Q(s,\cdot)}h_{LSE}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot))^{T}Q(s,\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)-h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot)),

where the last equality uses the fact that
\nabla_{x}h_{LSE}^{\beta}(x)^{T}x=h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(x)
.
Therefore,

h_{Boltz}^{\beta}(Q(s,\cdot))\geq\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q(s,a)-\frac{\ln(|\mathcal{A}|)}{\beta}.

∎

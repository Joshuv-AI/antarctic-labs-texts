Asymptotic estimates for double-coverings Thanks: The work was supported by the Science Committee of RA, in the frames of the research project 21AG‐1A045
Grigori A. Karagulyan, Vahe G. Karagulyan

Abstract
. A collection of finite sets { A 1 , A 2 , … , A p } \{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\} is said to be a double-covering if each a ∈ ∪ k = 1 p A k a\in\cup_{k=1}^{p}A_{k} is included in exactly two sets of the collection. For fixed integers l l and p p , let μ l , p \mu_{l,p} be the number of equivalency classes of double-coverings with # ⁡ ( A k ) = l \#(A_{k})=l , k = 1 , 2 , … , p k=1,2,\ldots,p . We characterize the asymptotic behavior of the quantity μ l , p \mu_{l,p} as p → ∞ p\to\infty . The results are applied to give an alternative approach to the Bonami-Kiener [ 3 , 8 ] hypercontraction inequality.

Introduction

We will consider a combinatorial object of double-covering, which is partially related to the theory of partitions and the graph theory. The theory of partitions has a rich history that is thoroughly considered in the book by G. Andrews
[
1
]
. A partition of a positive integer
n
is a finite nonincreasing sequence of positive integers
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{k}=n
. The partition function
\nu(n)
is the number of partitions of
n
. One of the crowning achievement of the theory of partition is the precise formula for
\nu(n)
mostly completed by G. Hardy and S.Ramanujan and fully completed by H. Rademacher (see
[
1
]
, Theorem 5.1). The formula doesn’t have a simple form, it is given in the form of an infinite series, where certain trigonometric sums are involved. There is also an asymptotic formula

(1.1)

\nu(n)\sim\frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\left(\pi\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)^{1/2}\right)

due to Meinardus (see
[
1
]
, Theorem 6.2), which will be used below.

A given family of finite sets
A_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,p
, of arbitrary nature (not necessarily different) defines a set-collection denoted by
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Two set-collections
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{p}\}
is said to be equal if there is a one to one mapping
\sigma
on
\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
such that
A_{\sigma(k)}=B_{k}
for all
k=1,2,\ldots,p
. We say that the set-collections
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are isomorphic if there is a one to one mapping

\phi:\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}A_{k}\to\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}B_{k}

such that
\{\phi(A_{1}),\ldots,\phi(A_{p})\}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{p}\}
. The relations of equality and being isomorphic for two set-collections
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}\sim\mathcal{B}
respectively.

Note that the symbol
\sim
here determines an equivalence relation. Let
[A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}]
be the equivalence class (E-class) generated by a set-collection
\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}
. Correspondingly, if
\mathcal{D}
is a family of set-collections, then we denote by
[\mathcal{D}]
the E-classes generated by the elements of
\mathcal{D}
.

Given set-collections
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
determine a new set-collection
\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\}
, which is a collection of all the elements of the set-collections
\mathcal{A}_{k}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A set-collection
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}
is said to be double-covering if each
a\in\cup_{k=1}^{p}A_{k}
is included in exactly two sets
A_{k}
. If each such
a
is covered by even number of sets
A_{k}
, then we say
\mathcal{A}
is an even-covering.

Definition 1.3
.

A set-collection
\mathcal{A}
is said to be connected if for any two different elements
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
there is a sequence of sets
A=E_{0},E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}=B

such that
E_{j}\in\mathcal{A}
,
E_{j}\cap E_{j+1}\neq\varnothing
for any
j=0,1\ldots,m-1
.

Definition 1.4
.

It is clear that every set-collection
\mathcal{A}
can be uniquely written in the form
\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\}
, where each
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is a connected set-collection. In that case we will say
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{A}
. We say a set-collection
\mathcal{A}
is standard if all E-classes of connected components
[\mathcal{A}_{k}]
are different.

The notation
\#(A)
will stand for the number of the elements of a finite set
A
. For given integers
l\geq 2
and
p\geq 2
we consider a class of double-coverings

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\},

satisfying
\#(A_{k})=l
. Clearly the number of different E-classes of each such a class of double-coverings is an integer depending only on
l
and
p
. We denote this number by
\mu_{l,p}
. The number of E-classes of a standard double-covering will be denoted by
\mu_{l,p}(\text{standard})
. For the number
\mu_{l,p}
of the full family of double-coverings sometimes we will also use the notation
\mu_{l,p}(\text{full})
. We will consider also double-coverings (
1.2
) with
\#(A_{k})=l
. The number of such double-coverings and their standard types will be denoted by
\mu_{l,p}(\text{full})
and
\mu_{l,p}(\text{standard})
respectively. Similarly we define a wider class of double-coverings (
1.2
), where
A_{k}
satisfy
1\leq\#(A_{k})\leq l
. We call first and the second kind of coverings
(l,p)
and
(l,p)^{*}
double-coverings respectively. For the number of E-classes of
(l,p)^{*}
-double-coverings we will use the notation
\mu_{l,p}^{*}(\text{full})
.

The quantities
\mu_{l,p}
and
\mu_{l,p}^{*}

Using these estimates, we provide an alternative approach to an inequality involving Rademacher chaos sums proved independently by Bonami
[
3
]
and Kiener
[
8
]
(see also
[
11
]
or
[
2
]
). Let
\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be the sequence of Rademacher functions
r_{n}(x)={\rm sign\,}\left(\sin 2^{n}\pi x\right)
.
The classical Khintchine inequality states that for any
0<p<\infty
, there are constants
A_{p},B_{p}
such that

(1.6)

A_{p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}r_{k}(x)\right\|_{p}\leq B_{p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The Khintchine inequality is a well-known object in analysis and probability with various generalizations and applications. A special case of the inequality was first studied by Khintchine
[
7
]
, proving the right bound of (
1.6
) with
B_{p}=\sqrt{p/2+1}
and
p\geq 2
. Further study of the inequality were given by Littlewood
[
10
]
, Paley and Zygmund
[
13
]
.
Let
A_{p}
and
B_{p}
denote the best constants, for which inequality (
1.6
) holds. It is trivial that
A_{p}=1
if
2\leq p<\infty
and
B_{p}=1
for all
0<p\leq 2
. In 1961 Stechkin
[
14
]
proved
B_{p}=((p-1)!!)^{1/p}
for even integers
p\geq 4
, which then extended for all real numbers
p\geq 3
by Young
[
16
]
. Then solving a long outstanding problem of Littlewood (see.
[
10
]
) Szarek
[
15
]
proved that
A_{1}=1/\sqrt{2}
. In 1982 Haagerup
[
4
]
introduced a new method, enabling to find the sharp constants in remaining cases and recovering the prior results newly. He proved that

(1.7)

B_{p}=2^{1/2}\big(\Gamma(p+1/2)/\pi\big)^{1/p},\quad 2<p<\infty,

and

\displaystyle A_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}2^{1/2-1/p}&\text{ if }&0<p<p_{0},\\
2^{1/2}\big(\Gamma(p+1/2)/\sqrt{\pi}\big)^{1/p}&\text{ if }&p>p_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
1<p_{0}<2
is the solution of the equation
\Gamma(p+1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}/2
.

Now consider orthonormal systems generated by the products of fixed number of Rademacher functions. For a finite set
A\subset\mathbb{N}
we denote

w_{A}(x)=\prod_{k\in A}r_{k}(x).

Recall that such products generate the classical orthonormal system of Walsh functions
W=\{w_{A}:\,A\subset\mathbb{N}\}
. Given integer
l\geq 2
we denote
\mathcal{Z}_{l}=\{A\subset\mathbb{N}:\,\#(A)=l\}
and consider a subsystem
W_{l}=\{w_{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}\}\subset W
of Walsh functions formed by the
l
-wise products of the Rademacher functions. In some literature the system
W_{l}
is called a Rademacher chaos. A multiple numerical sequence
b=\{b_{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}\}
with the norm
\|b\|=\big(\sum|b_{A}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
is said to be finite if there are only finite number of non-zero terms in it. We prove the following bound for the Rademacher chaos sums.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
l,p\geq 2
are integers and
p
is even, then for any finite sequence
\{b_{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}\}
it holds

(1.10)

\frac{\big(p!\mu_{l,p}(\text{standard})\big)^{1/p}}{2\sqrt{3}}\leq\sup_{\|b\|\leq 1}\left\|\sum_{A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}}b_{A}w_{A}\right\|_{p}\leq\sqrt{l!}\cdot\big(p!\mu_{l,p}(\text{full})\big)^{1/p}.

From Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
it immediately follows the Bonami-Kiener hypercontraction inequality, which is an extension of the Kintchine inequality for the Rademacher chaos sums.

Corollary 1.1

(Bonami-Kiener,
[
3
,
8
]
)
.

Let
l\geq 2
be an integer and
p>2
. Then for any finite sequence
b=\{b_{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}\}
, it holds the bound

(1.11)

c_{l}\cdot p^{l/2}\leq\sup_{\|b\|\leq 1}\left\|\sum_{A\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}}b_{A}w_{A}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{l}\cdot p^{l/2},

where
C_{l}>c_{l}>0
are constants, depending only on
l
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

We will need several lemmas. The notation
a\lesssim b
in this section will stand for the inequality
a\leq(c(l))^{p}\cdot b
, where
c(l)
is a constant depending only on the integer
l
. Having
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
simultaneously, we will write
a\asymp b
. We let
\mathbb{N}_{k}=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
and denote by
\pi_{k}
the family of all one-to-one mappings (rearrangements)
\sigma:\mathbb{N}_{k}\to\mathbb{N}_{k}
. In case of even
lp
denote by
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{l,p}
the families of double coverings
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\}
,
A_{j}\subset\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
, satisfying
\#(A_{j})=l
or
\#(A_{j})\leq l
respectively.
It is clear that each double-covering (
1.2
) has its isomorphic set-collection in
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}
. So we can write
\mu_{l,p}^{*}=\#([\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}])
. We will consider different subfamilies of
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
, indicating the nature of the set-collections of the subfamily. For example the subfamily of the standard set-collections of
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}(\text{standard})
, writing also
\mu_{l,p}(\text{standard})=\#([\mathcal{D}_{l,k}(\text{standard})])
. For the full family
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
sometimes we will also use the notation
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}(\text{full})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
lp
is even, then we have

(2.1)

p^{p(l-1)}\lesssim\#(\mathcal{D}_{l,p}(\text{full}))\leq\#(\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}(\text{full}))\lesssim p^{p(l-1)}.

Proof.

Along with
\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}
(
\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
) consider the family
\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}
(
\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
) of ordered-collections
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p})
such that
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\}\in\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}
(
\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
) . Given double-covering
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\}\in\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
and a rearrangement
\sigma\in\pi_{p}
generate another ordered-collection

(2.2)

(A_{\sigma{(1)}},\ldots,A_{\sigma(p)})\in\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}.

It is clear that the number of different such ordered-collections has an upper bound
p!
and this bound is reached when all the sets
A_{j}
are different. On the other hand let us see that such rearrangements
\sigma
can generate at least
p!/2^{p/2}
different ordered-collections (
2.2
) (
p
may also be odd). Indeed, since
\mathcal{A}
is a double-covering it is not possible an equality of three elements
A_{j}
. So one can see that such a lower bound is reached when each
A_{j}
has an equal pair say
A_{j^{\prime}}
, i.e.
A_{j}=A_{j^{\prime}}
. So the number of rearrangements generating the same ordered-collections in (
2.1
) can not exceed
2^{p/2}
. This implies that there are at least
p!/2^{p/2}
different ordered-collections that can be obtained in (
2.1
). Thus, we conclude

(2.3)

\frac{\#(\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*})}{p!}\leq\#(\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*})\leq\frac{2^{p/2}\#(\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*})}{p!},

and the same inequality also for
\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
. First let us prove the upper bound in (
1.5
). Consider the Cartesian product
G_{l,p}=\mathbb{N}_{2}\times\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
. For fixed integers
1\leq l_{j}\leq l
randomly chose pairwise disjoint sets
B_{j}\subset G_{l,p}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,p
, such that
\#(B_{j})=l_{j}
. Clearly the number of such choices of ordered-collections
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{p})
is equal

(2.4)

\binom{pl}{l_{1}}\cdot\binom{pl-l_{1}}{l_{2}}\ldots\binom{pl-l_{1}-\ldots-l_{p-1}}{l_{p}}=\frac{(pl)!}{(l!)^{p}}\lesssim p^{pl}.

Let
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{p})
be one of such a choice and suppose
A_{j}\subset\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
is the projection of
B_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,p
, on
\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
. One can check that among with others in this way we obtain all ordered-collections
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p})\in\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}
with
\#(B_{j})=l_{j}
. On the other hand the number of choices
1\leq l_{j}\leq
is equal
l^{p}
. Thus we conclude
\#(\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*})\lesssim p^{pl}
. Combining this and the right inequality of (
2.3
), we get the upper bound of (
2.1
).

To prove the lower bound of (
2.1
), we first suppose that
p
is even. Then we consider only the ordered-collection
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p})\in\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
, where
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p/2}\}
and
\{A_{p/2+1},\ldots,A_{p}\}
separately form partitions of
\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
. The number of such ordered-collections
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p})
is equal
\left(\frac{(pl/2)!}{(l!)^{p/2}}\right)^{2}
. So we have the bound

\#(\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p})\geq\left(\frac{(pl/2)!}{(l!)^{p/2}}\right)^{2}\gtrsim p^{pl},

which together with (
2.3
) yields the lower bound of (
2.1
). If
p
is odd and
p=2t+1
, then
l
must be even. Consider the collections
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p})\in\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p}
, where each of two set-collections
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}\}
and
\{A_{t+1},\ldots,A_{2t}\}
consists of pairwise disjoint sets from
\mathbb{N}_{pl/2}
, the sets

(2.5)

A_{2t+1}\cap\left(\cup_{k=1}^{t}A_{k}\right),\quad A_{2t+1}\cap\left(\cup_{k=t+1}^{2t}A_{k}\right)

are pairwise disjoint and both have
l/2
elements. Using a similar argument one can calculate that the number of such ordered-collections is equal

\binom{pl/2}{l}\binom{l}{l/2}\left(\frac{(tl)!}{(l!)^{t}}\right)^{2}\gtrsim p^{pl}.

Thus we similarly obtain
\#(\bar{}\mathcal{D}_{l,p})\gtrsim p^{pl}
. So, using the left inequality in (
2.3
), we get lower bound of (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}\in\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}(\text{standard})

\{\sigma(A_{1}),\sigma(A_{2}),\ldots,\sigma(A_{p})\}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\},

\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\}\in\mathcal{D}_{l,p}^{*}

Then we denote

\displaystyle B_{1}^{*}=B_{1},\quad B^{*}_{k}=B_{k}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{k-1}B_{j},\quad k\geq 2,

(2.9)

\displaystyle l_{k}=\#(B_{k}^{*}).

Of coarse, some of
l_{k}
’s can be zero and clearly we have

\sum_{k=1}^{p}l_{k}=pl/2.

Let
\sigma

2.6
). Observe that it can be realized on
B_{1}

different possible ways. Indeed, for the
\sigma

A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}

B_{1}
, the
\sigma
on the elements of
B_{1}
can be realized at most in
l!
different ways. So there are at most
p\cdot(l)!
different realization of
\sigma
on
B_{1}
. Suppose by induction the
\sigma
has been already realized on
\cup_{k=1}^{m}B_{k}
. Clearly, with this we will have determined the
\sigma
-image of
B_{m+1}
as one of the elements of
\mathcal{A}
, and the values of
\sigma
on each element of the set
B_{m+1}\cap\left(\cup_{k=1}^{m}B_{k}\right)
. So to finalize the determination of
\sigma
on
B_{m+1}
it remains only to realize
\sigma
on

or

is odd and so

(p-1)/2

0!
and a single

Now suppose that
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s}\}
is a general standard set-collection, where
\mathcal{A}_{k}
are the connectivity classes of
\mathcal{A}
. By the definition all E-classes

are different. Thus any

) acts inside of each connectivity component

. On the other hand, applying Lemma

which gives the right side inequality in (

where

We have

\displaystyle=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\in\mathcal{Z}_{l}(m)}}{{\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\}\text{ is even}}}}\gamma(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p})

where the first summation is taken over all the isomorphically different standard double-covering of variables such that

(4.2)

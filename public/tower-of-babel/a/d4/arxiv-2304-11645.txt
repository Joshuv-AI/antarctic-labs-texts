Abstract
Given a graph T T and a family of graphs ℋ \mathcal{H} . The generalized Turán number of ℋ \mathcal{H} is the maximum number of copies of T T in an ℋ \mathcal{H} -free graph on n n vertices, denoted by e ​ x ​ ( n , T , ℋ ) ex(n,T,\mathcal{H}) . Let e ​ x ​ ( n , T , ℋ ) ex(n,T,\mathcal{H}) denote the maximum number of copies of T T in an n n -vertex ℋ \mathcal{H} -free graph. Recently, Alon and Frankl (arXiv2210.15076) determined the exact values of ex ⁡ ( n , { K r + 1 , M s + 1 } ) \rm{ex}(n,\{K_{r+1},M_{s+1}\}) , where K r + 1 K_{r+1} and M s + 1 M_{s+1} are complete graph on r + 1 r+1 vertices and matching of size s + 1 s+1 , respectively. Ma and Hou (arXiv2301.05625) gave the generalized version of Alon and Frankl’s Theorem, which determine the exact values of e ​ x ​ ( n , K r , { K k + 1 , M s + 1 } ) ex(n,K_{r},\{K_{k+1},M_{s+1}\}) . Zhang determined the exact values of e ​ x ​ ( n , K r , ℒ n , s ) ex(n,K_{r},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}) , where ℒ n , s \mathcal{L}_{n,s} be the family of all linear forests of order n n with s s edges. Inspired by the work of Zhang and Ma, in this paper, we determined the exact number of e ​ x ​ ( n , { K r + 1 , ℒ n , s } ) ex(n,\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}) .

Introduction

Given a fixed graph
T
and a family of graphs
\mathcal{H}
. A graph
G
is called
\mathcal{H}
-free if for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
,
G
contains no copy of
H
as subgraph. Write
N(G,T)
for the number of copies of
T
in
G
. The generalized Turán number of
\mathcal{H}
is the maximum number of copies of
T
in an
\mathcal{H}
-free graph on
n
vertices and it can also be expressed by the following formula.

\rm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{H})=\mbox{max $\{N(G,T):G$ is an $n$-vertex $\mathcal{H}$-free graph\lx@text@rbrace}.

For
T=K_{2}
, it reduces to the classical Turán number, that is
ex(n,\mathcal{H})=ex(n,K_{2},\mathcal{H})
.

A matching in a graph
G
is a set of non-loop edges with no shared endpoints. Denote by
M_{k}
a matching containing
k
edges. We denote by
\nu(G)
the number of edges in a maximum matching of
G
. A linear forest is graph consisting of vertex disjoint paths or isolated vertices. Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
the set of all linear forests of order
n
with exactly
s
edges. Let
K_{r}
denote a complete graph on
r
vertices. Denote by
E_{k}
a set of
k
vertices in the graph that are not adjacent to each other. The join of two disjoint graphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, denoted by
H_{1}\vee H_{2}
, is the graph whose vertex set is
V(H_{1}\vee H_{2})=V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})
and edge set is
E(H_{1}\vee H_{2})=E(H_{1})\cup E(H_{2})\cup\{xy:x\in V(H_{1}),y\in V(H_{2})\}
.

There are many results about Turán number and generalized Turán number.
For Turán number, Erdös and Gallai
[
3
]
determined the Turán number of
M_{s+1}
. In
[
6
]
, Ning and Wang used the closure technique and the counting technique to determine the Turán number of
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
. Recently, Alon and Frankl
[
2
]
use Tutte-Berge Theorem to determine

.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For
n\geqslant 2s+1
and
r\geqslant 2
,

ex(n,\{K_{r+1},M_{s+1}\})=\mbox{\rm{max}}\left\{t(2s+1,r),t(s,r-1)+(n-s)s\right\}.

For generalized Turán number, Wang
[
8
]
use the shifted method to determine the generalized Turán number of
M_{s+1}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

For any
r\geqslant 2
and
n\geqslant 2s+1
,

ex(n,K_{r},M_{s+1})=\mbox{\rm{max}}\left\{\binom{2s+1}{r},\binom{s}{r}+(n-s)\binom{s}{r-1}\right\}.

In
[
9
]
, Zhang et al. determined the exact values of
ex(n,K_{r},\mathcal{L}_{n,s})
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
9
]
)
.

For any
r\geqslant 2
and
n\geqslant s+1
,

ex(n,K_{r},\mathcal{L}_{n,s})=\mbox{\rm{max}}\left\{\binom{s}{r},\binom{\left\lceil\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rceil}{r}+\left(n-\left\lceil\frac{s+1}{2}\right\rceil\right)\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{r-1}\right\}.

For some integers
t\geqslant k\geqslant r
, let
\Delta_{t,k}^{r}=N(T_{k}(t),K_{r})
. Recently, Ma and Hou
[
5
]
determined the exact values of

.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
5
]
)
.

For
n\geqslant 2s+1
and
k\geqslant r\geqslant 3
,

ex(n,K_{r},\{K_{k+1},M_{s+1}\})=\mbox{\rm{max}}\{\Delta_{2s+1,k}^{r},\Delta_{s,k-1}^{r}+(n-s)\Delta_{s,k-1}^{r-1}\}.

We were inspired by Zhang and Ma’s article. In this paper, we determine the Turán number of

Theorem 1.5

For

2
Preliminaries

Our proof is mainly based on the strong-shifting operation and the strong-closure technique, and we will cover their initial versions first.

Let
V(G),E(G)
be the vertex set and edge set of the graph
G
respectively. For
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
and
e\in E(G)
, we define a
shifting operation

S_{ij}
on
e
as follow:

S_{ij}(e)=\begin{cases}(e-\{j\})\cup\{i\},&\mbox{if $j\in e$, $i\notin e$ and $(e-\{j\})\cup\{i\}\notin E(G)$},\\
e,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We define
S_{ij}(G)
to be a shifted graph on vertex set
V(G)
with edge set
\{S_{ij}(e):e\in E(G)\}
.

In 1981, Kelmans
[
4
]
first introduced the shifting operation. In 1985, Akiyama and Frankl
[
1
]
proved the property
\nu(S_{ij}(G))\leqslant\nu(G)
and used it to give a short proof of Erdös-Gallai theorem. In 2019, Wang
[
8
]
proved the property
N(S_{ij}(G),K_{r})\geqslant N(G,K_{r})
and obtained the exact value of
ex(n,K_{r},M_{s+1})
.
Obviously, doing shifting operation on graph
G
will not change the number of edges of the original graph, i.e.,
e(S_{ij}(G))=e(G)
.
1n 2022, Zhang
[
9
]
proved the following property and obtained the exact value of
ex(n,K_{r},\mathcal{L}_{n,s})
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a graph on vertex set
[n]
. For any
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, we have
S_{ij}(G)
is also
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free if
G
is
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free.

We then introduce a variant of the shifting operation, the strong-shifting operation.
Let
V(G)
,
E(G)
be the vertex set and edge set of the graph
G
respectively. For any
k\in[n]
,
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
and
e=\{j,k\}\in E(G)
, we define a
strong-shifting operation

S^{\prime}_{ij}
on
e
as follow:

S^{\prime}_{ij}(e)=\begin{cases}(e-\{j\})\cup\{i\},&\mbox{if $(e-\{j\})\cup\{i\}\notin E(G)$ and $k,i$ different color},\\
e,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
S^{\prime}_{ij}(G)
be a strong-shifted graph on vertex set
V(G)
with edge set
\{S^{\prime}_{ij}(e):e\in E(G)\}
. Then
e(S^{\prime}_{ij}(G))=e(G)
.

The following lemma is similar to Lemma
2.1
, which introduces that for two endpoints at the edges of different partitions, the graph will keep
\{K_{k+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free after doing the strong-shifting operation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
G
is a graph on vertex set
[n]
. For any
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, we have
S^{\prime}_{ij}(G)
is also
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free if
G
is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free.

Proof.

Let
G
be the graph without both
K_{r+1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
. We apply the strong-shifting operation
S_{ij}^{\prime}
to
G
for all
i,j
for
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
. Finally, we obtain a graph
S_{ij}^{\prime}(G)
. By the definition of strong-shifting operation, the two endpoints of each new edge belong to different partitions. Hence,
S_{ij}^{\prime}(G)
is
K_{r+1}
-free.

By the definition of shifting operation and strong-shifting operation, the edges that need to be changed for strong-shifting operation are a subset of shifting operation. It can be seen from Lemma
2.1
that
S_{ij}^{\prime}(G)
is
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free.
∎

A
complete multipartite graph
is a simple graph whose vertices can be partitioned into sets so that
\{u,v\}
exists if and only if
u
and
v
belong to different sets of the partition. The
Turán graph

T(n,r)
is the complete
r
-partite graph with
n
vertices whose partite sets differ in size by at most one. Let
t(n,r)=|T(n,r)|
be the Turán number. The famous Turán Theorem
[
7
]
states that
ex(n,K_{r+1})=t(n,r)
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
7
]
)
.

Among the
n
-vertex simple graphs with no
r+1
-clique,
T(n,r)
has the maximum number of edges.

Next, we will introduce the closure technique and its variant, which we call the strong-closure technique.

Suppose
G
is a graph with order
n
,
P
is a property that applies to
G
, and
k
is a positive integer. A property

is considered to be
k
-stable, if whenever
G+uv
has property

and
d_{G}(u)+d_{G}(v)\geqslant k
, then

itself also has property

. We can define the

-closure of

, represented by
{cl}_{k}(G)
, as the graph

formed by repeatedly connecting non-adjacent vertices whose degree sum is at least
k
, until
d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(v)<k
for all
uv\notin E(H)
. It is then straightforward to see that if
P
is
k
-stable and
{cl}_{k}(G)
possesses property
P
, then
G
also has property
P
.

In
[
6
]
, Ning and Wang proof that the property
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free is
s
-stable.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Suppose that
u,v\in V(G)
with
d(u)+d(v)\geqslant s
. Then
G
is
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free if and only if
G+\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free.

For our problem, we have the following further lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Suppose that
u,v\in V(G)
with
u,v
belong to different partitions and
d(u)+d(v)\geqslant s
. Then
G
is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free if and only if
G+\{u,v\}
is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free.

Proof.

If
G+\{u,v\}
is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free, then clearly
G
is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free. Therefore we only need to verify the other direction.

If

is
\{K_{r+1},\mathcal{L}_{n,s}\}
-free. Since the added new edge

is between different partitions, it does not increase the number of partitions of the graph

,

is
K_{r+1}
-free. And then by Lemma
2.4
,
G+\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{L}_{n,s}
-free, completing the proof.
∎

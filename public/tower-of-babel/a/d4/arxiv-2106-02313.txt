Algebraic structure underlying spherical, parabolic and prolate spheroidal bases of the nine-dimensional MICZ-Kepler problem
Dai-Nam Le, Van-Hoang Le

Abstract
The nonrelativistic motion of a charged particle around a dyon in ( 9 + 1 ) (9+1) spacetime is known as the nine-dimensional MICZ-Kepler problem. This problem has been solved exactly by the variables-separation method in three different coordinate systems, spherical, parabolic, and prolate spheroidal. In the present study, we establish a relationship between the variable separation and the algebraic structure of S ​ O ​ ( 10 ) SO(10) symmetry. Each of the spherical, parabolic, or prolate spheroidal bases is proved to be a set of eigenfunctions of a corresponding nonuplet of algebraically-independent integrals of motion. This finding also helps us establish connections between the bases by the algebraic method. This connection, in turn, allows calculating complicated integrals of confluent Heun, generalized Laguerre, and generalized Jacobi polynomials, which are important in physics and analytics.

V(v)
satisfy the following equations:

\displaystyle\left\{u\partial_{u}^{2}+4\partial_{u}-J(J+6)u^{-1}+Z/2+Eu/2-P\right\}U(u)=0,

(24)

\displaystyle\left\{v\partial_{v}^{2}+4\partial_{v}-L(L+6)v^{-1}+Z/2+Ev/2+P\right\}V(v)=0,

(25)

with a separation constant
P
. Also, Equation (
24
) and Equation (
25
) have been solved analytically in Ref.
[
44
]

with the separation constant
P
quantized as

\frac{2P}{\sqrt{-2E}}=n+\frac{Q}{2}-J-2n_{p},

with
n_{p}=0,1,2,\ldots,n+Q/2-(L+J)/2
. Then, the basis set of wave functions can be written in the parabolic coordinates as

\displaystyle\Psi^{parabolic}_{n,n_{p},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}(u,v,\varphi,\phi)=

\displaystyle U_{n,n_{p},J}(u)V_{n,n_{p},L}(v)

(26)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{D}_{L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}(\varphi,\phi).

On the other hand, we need to extract the integral of motion corresponding to the separation constant
P
. For this purpose, we first replace
U(u)
and
V(v)
in Equation (
24
) and Equation (
25
) by
U(u)V(v)
and then recombine the two equations with excluding the energy
E
from the equations. As a result, we obtain an equation

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{2uv}{u+v}\right.

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{u}^{2}-\partial_{v}^{2}+4v^{-1}\partial_{v}-4u^{-1}\partial_{u}\right)+\frac{vJ(J+6)}{2u(u+v)}

(27)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{uL(L+6)}{2v(u+v)}+\frac{Z(u-v)}{u+v}\right\}U(u)V(v)=-2PU(u)V(v).

Interestingly, we note that the operator on the left-hand side of Equation (
27
) is similar to the Laplace-Runge-Lenz component
\hat{M}_{9}
written in parabolic coordinates
(u,v)
as

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{9}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2\hat{H}}}

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{2uv}{u+v}\left(\partial_{u}^{2}-\partial_{v}^{2}+4v^{-1}\partial_{v}-4u^{-1}\partial_{u}\right)\right.

(28)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{v\hat{J}^{2}}{2u(u+v)}-\frac{u\hat{L}^{2}}{2v(u+v)}+\frac{Z(u-v)}{u+v}\right\}.

With this observation, we can easily obtain the equation

\hat{M}_{9}\Psi^{parabolic}_{n,n_{p},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}=-\frac{2P}{\sqrt{-2E}}\Psi^{parabolic}_{n,n_{p},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}},

(29)

which means separating the variables in parabolic coordinates corresponding to the operator
\hat{M}_{9}
chosen for the last integral
\hat{D}^{2}_{9}
.

3.3
Prolate spheroidal coordinates

We are now applying the similar procedure to prolate spheroidal coordinates
(\xi,\eta)
, defined as
[
44
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\xi=\frac{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}+\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-a)^{2}}}{a},\\
\eta=\frac{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}-\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-a)^{2}}}{a},\end{array}\right.

(30)

whereas positive parameter
a
is the distance between two centers of prolate spheroidal coordinates. It is interesting to notice that this coordinate system becomes the spherical one when
a\to 0
and the parabolic one when
a\to+\infty
. Changing
\rho,z
variables by the prolate spheroidal coordinates (
\xi,\eta
), we represent Equation (
16
) in the form
[
44
]

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{1}{a^{2}(\xi^{2}-\eta^{2})}\left[2(\xi^{2}-1)\partial_{\xi}^{2}+16\xi\partial_{\xi}+2(1-\eta^{2})\partial_{\eta}^{2}-16\eta\partial_{\eta}\right]-\frac{2Z}{a(\xi+\eta)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.+\frac{L(L+6)}{a^{2}(\xi+\eta)(\xi-1)(1-\eta)}-\frac{J(J+6)}{a^{2}(\xi+\eta)(\xi+1)(1+\eta)}\right\}\psi(\xi,\eta)=E\psi(\xi,\eta).

(31)

Equation (
3.3
) is separable by variables
\xi
and
\eta
as presented for the first time in Ref.
[
44
]
. By setting
\psi(\xi,\eta)=\Xi(\xi)H(\eta)
, this equation is separated into two independent differential equations for
\Xi(\xi)
and
H(\eta)
as

\displaystyle\left\{(\xi^{2}-1)\partial_{\xi}^{2}+8\xi\partial_{\xi}-\frac{L(L+6)}{2(\xi-1)}+\frac{J(J+6)}{2(\xi+1)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+\frac{1}{2}Ea^{2}(\xi^{2}-1)+Za\xi+K\right\}\Xi(\xi)=0,

(32)

\displaystyle\left\{(1-\eta^{2})\partial_{\eta}^{2}-8\eta\partial_{\eta}-\frac{L(L+6)}{2(1-\eta)}-\frac{J(J+6)}{2(1+\eta)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}Ea^{2}(1-\eta^{2})-Za\eta-K\right\}H(\eta)=0,

(33)

with a separation constant
K
. These equations have analytical solutions given in Ref.
[
44
]
via confluent Heun polynomials, where the separation constant
K
was found as a function of
n,L,J,Q
and new quantum number
n_{k}
, a non-negative integer. The basis set of wavefunctions in prolate spheroidal coordinates is then defined as

\displaystyle\Psi^{spheroidal}_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}(\xi,\eta,\varphi,\phi)=

\displaystyle\Xi_{n,n_{k},L,J}(u)H_{n,n_{k},L,J}(\eta)

(34)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{D}_{L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}(\varphi,\phi).

On the other hand, excluding
E
from Equation (
3.3
) and Equation (
33
), we can combine these two equations into one equation as

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{(\xi^{2}-1)(1-\eta^{2})}{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}\right.\left(\partial_{\xi}^{2}+\frac{\xi}{\xi^{2}-1}\partial_{\xi}-\partial_{\eta}^{2}-\frac{\eta}{1-\eta^{2}}\partial_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{L(L+6)}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-\eta}-\frac{1}{\xi-1}+\frac{1}{\xi+\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{J(J+6)}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\frac{1}{\xi+1}-\frac{1}{\xi+\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+\frac{Za(1+\xi\eta)}{\xi+\eta}\right\}\Xi(\xi)H(\eta)=-K\;\Xi(\xi)H(\eta).

(35)

This is the eigen-equation for the separation constant
K
. Amazingly, the operator in the left-hand side of this equation is similar to the operator
\hat{\Lambda}_{M}^{2}=\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+a\sqrt{-2\hat{H}}\,\hat{M}_{9}
written in prolate spheroidal coordinates as

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}^{2}_{M}=

\displaystyle\frac{(\xi^{2}-1)(1-\eta^{2})}{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{2}+\frac{\xi}{\xi^{2}-1}\partial_{\xi}-\partial_{\eta}^{2}-\frac{\eta}{1-\eta^{2}}\partial_{\eta}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-\eta}-\frac{1}{\xi-1}+\frac{1}{\xi+\eta}\right)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\hat{J}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\frac{1}{\xi+1}-\frac{1}{\xi+\eta}\right)+\frac{Za(1+\xi\eta)}{\xi+\eta}.

Therefore, acting operator
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}_{M}
on the wavefunction Equation (
34
) and considering Equation (
3.3
) and Equation (
14
), we have the equation:

\hat{\Lambda}^{2}_{M}\Psi^{spheroidal}_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}=-K\Psi^{spheroidal}_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}.

(37)

This means that the wavefunctions in prolate spheroidal coordinates are eigensolutions of the operator
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}_{M}
. In other words, we can choose the operator
\hat{\Lambda}_{M}^{2}=\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+a\sqrt{-2\hat{H}}\,\hat{M}_{9}
for the last integrals
\hat{D}^{2}_{9}
and this choice corresponds to the variables separation in prolate spheroidal coordinates.

4
Algebraic relations between bases

After constructing the spherical, parabolic, and prolate spheroidal bases, the next important task is to construct transformations between these bases. However, this construction is not trivial. Although the spherical and parabolic bases for the 3D MICZ-KP were first studied by McIntosh and Cisneros in 1970
[
2
]
, the connection between these bases has derived analytically and expressed in
SU(2)
Clebsch-Gordan coefficients in 2005
[
48
]
only. A similar connection was also built for the 5D MICZ-KP by Mardoyan
et al.

[
35
]
and for 9D MICZ-KP in our recent work
[
44
]
. Unfortunately, the connection between the prolate spheroidal and spherical bases is not easy to establish. For the 5D MICZ-KP, Madoyan
et al.

[
35
]
have expanded the prolate spheroidal basis via the spherical basis; however, the expansion coefficients can not be obtained explicitly but satisfy the recurrent equations. For the 9D MICZ-KP, we can not establish the connection between the bases in prolate spheroidal and spherical coordinates because of the complicated integral between the confluent Heun, generalized Laguerre, and generalized Jacobi polynomials. Fortunately, we can perform this task using the algebraic connections given in the previous section and will show in the following.

4.1
Matrix elements of
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
and
\hat{M}_{9}

For further calculations, we derive here some analytical formulae for the matrix elements of the operators
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
and
\hat{M}_{9}
with respect to the spherical basis set Equation (
20
).

As shown in Equation (
21
), the operator
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
in spherical coordinates does not depend on radial variable
r
, on the one hand. On the other hand, this operator depends on angles
\varphi
and
\phi
via the operators
\hat{L}^{2}(\varphi)
and
\hat{J}^{2}(\varphi,\phi)
only. In turn, these operators are diagonal corresponding to the spherical basis set Equation (
20
). Therefore, we will focus only on the part of the wavefunction dependent on the variable
\theta
and hence calculate the matrix elements:

(\hat{\Lambda}^{2})^{n,L,Q,J}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}=\langle\Psi^{spherical}_{n,\lambda^{\prime},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}|\hat{\Lambda}^{2}|\Psi^{spherical}_{n,\lambda,L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}\rangle,

(38)

with the difference only on
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
. From the orthogonality of the spherical basis set and Equation (
22
), we have

(\hat{\Lambda}^{2})^{n,L,Q,J}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}=\lambda(\lambda+7)\delta_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}.

(39)

Oppositely, in the case of Laplace-Runge-Lenz component
\hat{M}_{9}
, the explicit expression Equation (
28
) of this operator in parabolic coordinates depends on angles
\varphi
and
\phi
via operators
\hat{L}^{2}(\varphi)
and
\hat{J}^{2}(\varphi,\phi)
only while depends on variables
u
,
v
via
\partial_{u}
,
\partial_{v}
, and
u,v
. In the parabolic basis set, both
\hat{L}^{2}(\varphi)
and
\hat{J}^{2}(\varphi,\phi)
are also diagonal; thus, only the part of the parabolic wavefunction dependent on variables
(u,v)
is considered to calculate the matrix element

\displaystyle(\hat{M}_{9})^{n,L,Q,J}_{n_{p}^{\prime},n_{p}}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi^{parabolic}_{n,n_{p}^{\prime},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}|\hat{M}_{9}|\Psi^{parabolic}_{n,n_{p},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle=-\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-J-2n_{p}\right)\delta_{n_{p}^{\prime}n_{p}},

where the orthogonality of the parabolic basis set as well as Equation (
29
) are used for simplification.

To calculate the matrix element of operator
\hat{M}_{9}
in the spherical basis set

(\hat{M}_{9})^{n,L,Q,J}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}=\langle\Psi^{spherical}_{n,\lambda^{\prime},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}|\hat{M}_{9}|\Psi^{spherical}_{n,\lambda,L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}\rangle,

(41)

we use the connection between parabolic and spherical bases given in Ref.
[
44
]
as

\Psi_{n,\lambda,L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}^{spherical}=\sum_{n_{p}}W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}\Psi_{n,n_{p},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}^{parabolic}.

(42)

Here in Equation (
42
), matrix element
W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}
of the interbasis transformation between spherical and parabolic bases was first given in Ref.
[
44
]
and is now written in a more compact form in
A
. We can then easily transform the parabolic representation of
\hat{M}_{9}
into the spherical representation by inserting Equation (
42
) and Equation (
40
) into Equation (
41
) as

\displaystyle(\hat{M}_{9})^{n,L,Q,J}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{p}^{\prime},n_{p}}(\hat{M}_{9})^{n,L,Q,J}_{n_{p}^{\prime},n_{p}}W_{\lambda^{\prime};n_{p}^{\prime}}^{n,L,Q,J}W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}

(43)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n_{p}}\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-J-2n_{p}\right)W_{\lambda^{\prime};n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}

\displaystyle=-\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-J\right)\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}+2\sum_{n_{p}}n_{p}W_{\lambda^{\prime};n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}.

The first term in the last line of Equation (
43
) comes from the orthogonality of matrix
W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}
, but the second term needs to be simplified.

For this purpose, we develop a recurrence relation of matrix element
W_{\lambda;n_{p}}^{n,L,Q,J}
and apply it to the second term of Equation (
43
). Detailed calculations are given in
A
. As a result, we obtain a compact form for this second term and consequently calculate the non-zero matrix elements of the Laplace-Runge-Lenz component
\hat{M}_{9}
in the spherical representation as

\displaystyle(\hat{M}_{9})^{n,L,Q,J}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{(J-L)(L+J+6)(2n+Q+8)}{8(\lambda+3)(\lambda+4)}\;\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}

(44)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-B_{\lambda^{\prime}}\;\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}-1,\lambda}-B_{\lambda}\;\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}+1,\lambda},

with the coefficients

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}=\sqrt{\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-\lambda+1\right)\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}+\lambda+7\right)}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\frac{(\lambda-\frac{L+J}{2})(\lambda+6+\frac{L+J}{2})(\lambda+3-\frac{J-L}{2})(\lambda+3+\frac{J-L}{2})}{(\lambda+3)^{2}(2\lambda+7)(2\lambda+5)}}.

(45)

This explicit expression Equation (
44
) of the matrix element is similar to that of the three- and five-dimensional MICZ-Kepler problems
[
35
,
36
,
49
]
.

4.2
Transformation between prolate spheroidal and spherical bases

Once the matrix elements of generalized angular momentum
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
and Laplace-Runge-Lenz component
\hat{M}_{9}
are determined in the spherical representation, the eigenproblem by Equation (
37
) in prolate spheroidal coordinates can be solved using the spherical basis by the following interbasis transformation

\Psi_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}^{spheroidal}=\sum_{\lambda}T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)\Psi_{n,\lambda,L,Q,J,j_{5},\ldots,j_{1},m_{j}}^{spherical}.

(46)

Here, the coefficients must satisfy the normalization condition as

\sum_{\lambda}T_{\lambda;n_{k}^{\prime}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)=\delta_{n_{k}^{\prime}n_{k}}

(47)

and have the natural limits

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\lim_{a\to 0}T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)=\delta_{n+Q/2-\lambda,n_{k}}\\
\lim_{a\to+\infty}T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)=W_{\lambda;n-\frac{L+J-Q}{2}-n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J},\end{array}\right.

(48)

as shown in Ref.
[
44
]
, since the prolate spheroidal coordinates become spherical or parabolic as
a\to 0
or
a\to+\infty
, respectively. We note that the spherical solutions of Equation (
19
) give values of
\lambda
from
(L+J)/2
to
n+Q/2
.

Substituting connection Equation (
46
) into Equation (
37
) and using expressions Equation (
39
) and Equation (
44
) for the matrix elements of operators
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
and
\hat{M}_{9}
in the spherical basis, we get a set of linear equations governing the interbasis coefficients as a three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle\left(A_{\lambda}-K\right)T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)+\tilde{B}_{\lambda}\;T_{\lambda-1;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)+\tilde{B}_{\lambda+1}\;T_{\lambda+1;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)=0,

(49)

where

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}=-\frac{Za(J-L)(L+J+6)}{4(\lambda+3)(\lambda+4)}-\lambda(\lambda+7),

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\lambda}=\frac{2aZ}{2n+Q+8}{B}_{\lambda}.

For the index
\lambda
running from
(L+J)/2
to
n+Q/2
, we have a set of
N+1
linear equations with
N=n+Q/2-(L+J)/2
.
3
3

3

We use the definition of
N
in analytical approach in Ref.
[
44
]
to keep notations consistent.
We can also rewrite them in a matrix form as

\left(\hat{\mathcal{K}}(a)-K\right)\hat{T}(a)=0,

(50)

where the solution is now presented in a column vector

\hat{T}(a)=\left[\ldots,T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J},\ldots\right]^{T}
; and
\hat{\mathcal{K}}(a)
is a tridiagonal squared matrix of order
N+1
as

\hat{\mathcal{K}}(a)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A_{\frac{L+J}{2}}&\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&0&\ldots&0\\
\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&A_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+2}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\tilde{B}_{n+\frac{Q}{2}}&A_{n+\frac{Q}{2}}\end{array}\right].

(51)

We do not use the superscripts
n,L,Q,J
for matrix
\hat{\mathcal{K}}(a)
and column vector
\hat{T}(a)
to avoid the cumbersomeness.

For Equation (
50
), the separation constant
K
is the
n_{k}
-th eigenvalue of matrix
\hat{\mathcal{K}}(a)
. This value depends on
a
and can be calculated for fixed
n,L,Q,J
; thus, we denote the separation constant as
K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)
. Back to Equation (
49
), fortunately, the similar three-term recurrence equations have been investigated in our previous works
[
50
,
51
]
; thus, we can obtain a compact solution of these equations as follows. First, we introduce new coefficients

\displaystyle t_{\lambda}(a)=T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)\times(-1)^{\lambda-\frac{L+J}{2}}\prod_{j=\frac{L+J}{2}+1}^{\lambda}\tilde{B}_{j}.

Then multiply Equation (
49
) by the same factor to rewrite it as a new recurrence equation

\displaystyle t_{\lambda+1}(a)=\left(A_{\lambda}-K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)\right)t_{\lambda}(a)-\tilde{B}_{\lambda}^{2}\;t_{\lambda-1}(a)

(52)

with
\lambda
running from
(L+J)/2
to
n+Q/2
.
It is the well-known recurrence relation of a tridiagonal matrix determinant or so-called continuant
[
52
]
. Hence
t_{\lambda}(a)
can be expressed as a continuant

\displaystyle t_{\lambda}(a)=C\det\left|\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}(a)-K\right|

(53)

with the normalization constant
C
given in Ref.
[
52
]
as

C=\left\{\sum_{\lambda=\frac{L+J}{2}}^{n+\frac{Q}{2}}\prod_{j=\frac{L+J}{2}+1}^{\lambda}\tilde{B}_{j}^{-2}\;\det\left|\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}(a)-K\right|^{2}\right\}^{-1/2}.

(54)

Here, matrix
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}(a)
is a tridiagonal matrix of order

N_{\lambda}=\lambda-\frac{L+J}{2}

\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}(a)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}A_{\frac{L+J}{2}}&\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&0&\ldots&0\\
\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&A_{\frac{L+J}{2}+1}&\tilde{B}_{\frac{L+J}{2}+2}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\tilde{B}_{\lambda-1}&A_{\lambda-1}\end{array}\right].

(55)

Putting the separation constant
K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)
into Equation (
53
), we can calculate
t_{\lambda}(a)
and the coefficient
T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)
consequently. This is a significant advantage of the algebraic approach where the separation constant
K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)
can principally be found just by calculating eigenvalues of the sparse matrix, and the explicit expression of the coefficient
T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)
can be determined by calculating the continuant.

When approaching this problem analytically in Ref.
[
44
]
, we could not calculate the coefficient
T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)
because of the complicated integral

\displaystyle T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)=\int\dots\int r^{8}dr\sin^{7}\theta d\theta d\Omega(\varphi,\phi)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\Psi^{spherical,*}_{n,\lambda,L,Q,J,{j_{5}},{j_{4}}{j_{3}},{j_{2}},{j_{1}},{m_{j}}}(r,\theta,\varphi,\phi)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Psi^{spheroidal}_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J,{j_{5}},{j_{4}}{j_{3}},{j_{2}},{j_{1}},{m_{j}}}\left(\xi,\eta,\varphi,\phi\right),

(56)

with the prolate spheroidal variables replaced by their expression in spherical coordinates. This integral contains three different types of orthogonal polynomial, namely confluently Heun, generalized Laguerre, generalized Jacobi, and on the best of our knowledge, has not been calculated elsewhere. Therefore, the compact expression of
T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)
conducted by the algebraic approach means that the integral Equation (
4.2
) is calculated.

4.3
Spherical and parabolic bases as limits of prolate spheroidal basis

Initially, the prolate spheroidal coordinates have two limits as spherical or parabolic regarding the distance
a
. Correspondingly, when taking the limits of
a\to 0
or
a\to+\infty
, the prolate spheroidal basis of the 9D MICZ-KP must become the spherical basis or parabolic. This interesting property of the prolate spheroidal basis has been verified analytically by examining the prolate spheroidal wavefunction in Ref.
[
44
]
. Now we also give a proof by the algebraic perspective.

First, we examine the limits of separation constant
K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)
, which is the eigenvalue of the characterized equation (
37
) in prolate spheroidal coordinates. Upon the limits of the operator
\hat{\Lambda}_{M}^{2}=\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+a\sqrt{-2\hat{H}}\,\hat{M}_{9}
, Equation (
37
) becomes Equation (
22
) when
a\to 0
and Equation (
29
) when
a\to+\infty
. In turn, these limits lead to the limit relations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lim\limits_{a\to 0}K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)&=-\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-n_{k}\right)\left(n+\frac{Q}{2}-n_{k}+7\right),\\
\lim\limits_{a\to+\infty}\frac{K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)}{a}&=-\frac{2Z\left(n+Q/2-L-2n_{k}\right)}{2n+Q+8},\end{array}\right.

(57)

corresponding to the separation constants of the spherical and parabolic bases, respectively.

Now, we consider the limit of
T_{\lambda;n_{k}}^{n,L,Q,J}(a)
when
a\to 0
. In this case, all elements of the determinant become
A_{\lambda}(0)=-\lambda(\lambda+7)
and
\tilde{B}_{\lambda}(a)=0
; thus, the continuant

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\det\left|\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}-K_{n,n_{k},L,Q,J}(a)\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=\frac{L+J}{2}}^{\lambda-1}\left(n+Q/2-n_{k}-j\right)\left(n+Q/2-n_{k}+j+7\right)

(58)

vanishes for all
\lambda>n+\frac{Q}{2}-n_{k}
, and consequently,

5
Conclusion

In this study, we have explicitly constructed three versions for the last member of the nonuplet of algebraically independent integrals of motion for the nine-dimensional MICZ-Kepler problem and related them to the variable separation of this problem. Particularly, we have shown that the last constant of motion can be either the total generalized angular momentum
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}
or the last component of the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector
\hat{M}_{9}
, or their combination
\hat{\Lambda}^{2}_{M}=\hat{\Lambda}^{2}+a\sqrt{-2\hat{H}}\hat{M}_{9}
corresponding to the spherical, parabolic, or prolate spheroidal bases. Especially, we have variable-separated the Schrödinger equation in spherical, parabolic, and prolate spheroidal coordinates and proved that the separation constants are the chosen integrals of motion.

Another significant result is the relation between the prolate spheroidal and spherical bases. We have established the interbasis transformation coefficients between the bases by using the algebraic structure behind these bases.
Furthermore, we have shown an algebraic approach to calculate the nine-dimensional complicated integrals of confluent Heun, generalized Laguerre, and Jacobi polynomials using the results above.

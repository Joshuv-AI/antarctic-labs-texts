A note on Weyl gauge symmetry in gravity
N. Mohammedi

Abstract
A scale invariant theory of gravity, containing at most two derivatives, requires, in addition to the Riemannian metric, a scalar field and (or) a gauge field. The gauge field is usualy used to construct the affine connection of Weyl geometry. In this note, we incorporate both the gauge field and the scalar field to build a generalised scale invariant Weyl affine connection. The Ricci tensor and the Ricci scalar made out of this generalised Weyl affine connection contain, naturally, kinetic terms for the scalar field and the gauge field. This provides a geometric interpretation for these terms. It is also shown that scale invariance in the presence of a cosmological constant and mass terms is not completely lost. It becomes a duality transformation relating various fields.

2
The usual Weyl geometry

The actions
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of the previous section can be given a geometrical interpretation.
The Christoffel symbols

\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\tau}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\tau\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\tau\mu}-\partial_{\tau}g_{\mu\nu}\right)

(2.1)

transform under the scale symmetry
g_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow e^{\sigma}\,g_{\mu\nu}
as

\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\sigma+\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\sigma-g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\tau}\partial_{\tau}\sigma\right)\,\,\,\,\,.

(2.2)

One can then, with the help of the gauge field
C_{\mu}
, build the scale
invariant symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{{q}^{\prime}}{2}\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}C_{\nu}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}C_{\mu}-g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\sigma}C_{\sigma}\right)\,\,,

(2.3)

As a consequence, the scale invariant Riemann tensor
\widetilde{R}^{\mu}_{\nu\rho\sigma}
is defined as

\widetilde{R}^{\mu}_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\partial_{\rho}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}+\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\rho\alpha}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\nu\sigma}-\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\sigma\alpha}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\nu\rho}\,\,\,.

(2.4)

Finally, the scale invariant Ricci tensor
\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}
and the Ricci scalar
\widetilde{R}
are

\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}=\widetilde{R}^{\alpha}_{\mu\alpha\nu}\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\widetilde{R}=g^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}\,\,\,\,\,.

(2.5)

The expression of the Ricci scalar
\widetilde{R}
is given by

\widetilde{R}=R+\left(3{q^{\prime}}\,\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu}-\frac{3}{2}{{q}^{\prime}}^{2}\,C_{\mu}C^{\mu}\right)\,\,\,\,\,.

(2.6)

Under the local transformation
g_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow e^{\sigma}\,g_{\mu\nu}
, the Ricci scalar
\widetilde{R}
scales as

\widetilde{R}\longrightarrow e^{-\sigma}\,\widetilde{R}\,\,\,.

(2.7)

This suggests that the scale invariant theory should be given by the action

\displaystyle S_{\text{Weyl}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg\{-\frac{\xi}{4\kappa}\widetilde{R}^{2}\Bigg\}\,\,\,.

(2.8)

This last action can be cast in the form

\displaystyle S_{\text{Weyl}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,e^{-q\varphi}\Bigg\{-\widetilde{R}+\kappa\,e^{-q\varphi}\Bigg\}\,\,\,.

(2.9)

The field
\varphi
enters as a non-propagating auxiliary field whose
equations of motion is

e^{-q\varphi}=\frac{\widetilde{R}}{2\kappa}\,\,\,.

(2.10)

The action
S_{\text{Weyl}}
in (
2.8
)
is obtained when using this equation of motion.

Using the explicit expression of
\widetilde{R}
as written (
2.6
), the action (
2.9
) is precisely

S_{2}
of the previous section. The elimination of the gauge field
C_{\mu}
through its equation of motion gives then
the action
S_{1}
.

2.1
The gauge field kinetic term

The scale invariant Ricci tensor
\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}
as defined in (
2.5
)
is not symmetric in its indices. Its anti-symmetric part is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}-\widetilde{R}_{\nu\mu}\right)=-{q^{\prime}}\,C_{\mu\nu}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,C_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu}\,\,\,\,.

(2.11)

Therefore, one could add to (

\displaystyle S_{\text{add}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{q^{\prime}}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}\,\widetilde{R}^{[\mu\nu]}\Bigg\}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{4}C_{\mu\nu}C^{\mu\nu}\Bigg\}\,\,\,.

This known observation assigns a geometric origin to the kinetic term of the gauge field
C_{\mu}
.

3
An alternative way

It is now clear that scale symmetry of gravity requires a scalar field
\varphi
and a gauge field

C_{\mu}
.
Under the scale transformations

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle e^{\sigma(x)}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\varphi+\frac{1}{q}\,\sigma\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle C_{\mu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle C_{\mu}+\frac{1}{{q}^{\prime}}\,\partial_{\mu}\sigma\,\,\,\,\,

(3.1)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle e^{-\sigma(x)}\,\left(R-\Delta R\right)\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle 3q\,\nabla^{2}\varphi-\frac{3}{2}q^{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle e^{-\sigma(x)}\,\left(3q\,\nabla^{2}\varphi-\frac{3}{2}q^{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi+\Delta R\right)\,\,,

\displaystyle 3{q^{\prime}}\,\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu}-\frac{3}{2}{{q}^{\prime}}^{2}\,C_{\mu}C^{\mu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle e^{-\sigma(x)}\,\left(3{q^{\prime}}\,\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu}-\frac{3}{2}{{q}^{\prime}}^{2}\,C_{\mu}C^{\mu}+\Delta R\right)\,\,,

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}

A general Weyl geometry

The variation, due to the rescaling of the metric, of the Christoffel symbols as given in (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{q^{\prime}}^{2}\left(1-\omega\right)^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}\,\widetilde{R}^{[\mu\nu]}\Bigg\}\,\,\,.

5
Application: scale invariance of the Standard Model coupled to gravity

In this section we supplement the Standard Model with the scalar field
\varphi
and the gauge field

C_{\mu}
and couple it to gravity in a scale invariant manner.
The scale transformations of the fields of the Standard Model are

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\,\longrightarrow\,

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sigma(x)}\,H\,\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\psi^{(a)}

\displaystyle\,\longrightarrow\,

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{3}{4}\sigma(x)}\,\psi^{(a)}\,\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\,\longrightarrow\,

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(i)}\,\,\,\,\,\,,

(5.1)

where
H
,
\psi^{(a)}
and
A_{\mu}^{(i)}
represent, respectively, the Higgs doublet, any fermion field and any gauge field
in the Standard Model.

The scale invariant Standard Model coupled to gravity could be described by the action

8
Conclusions

A scale invariant theory of gravity necessitates, beside the metric tensor field
g_{\mu\nu}
, a scalar field
\varphi
and a gauge field
C_{\mu}
.
We have in this note assembled these three fields together and constructed a scale invariant Christoffel symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}

as given in (
4.2
). The scalar field
\varphi
is then a physical field having a positive kinetic term.
As an application of the formalism, we have coupled the Standard Model to gravity in a scale invariant manner. The widely investigated
subject of the non-minimally coupled Standard Model to gravity is obtained as a consequence of the breaking of this scale symmetry.
However, the theory contains an extra gauge field
C_{\mu}
. It would be of great interest to explore the issues brought by the presence
of this gauge field in the context of inflation in the early Universe.

The analyses carried out in this article could be easily generalised to the case of
N
scalar fields
\varphi^{i}
with
i=1,\dots,N
.
The scale invariant Christoffel symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
are then written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{{q}^{\prime}}{2}\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}C_{\nu}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}C_{\mu}-g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\sigma}C_{\sigma}\right)

(8.1)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{{v_{i}}}{2}\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}{\cal D_{\nu}}\varphi^{i}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi^{i}-g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\sigma}{\cal D}_{\sigma}\varphi^{i}\right)\,\,,

where
{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi^{i}=\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{i}-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q_{i}}\,C_{\mu}
and

v_{i}
and
q_{i}
are constants. Each scalar field transforms as
\varphi^{i}\longrightarrow\varphi^{i}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}\sigma
.

The Ricci scalar constructed out of the scale invariant Christoffel symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
in (
8.1
)
is then given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R+3v_{i}\nabla_{\mu}{\cal D}^{\mu}\varphi^{i}+3q^{\prime}\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu}

(8.2)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\left(v_{i}v_{j}{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi^{i}{\cal D}^{\mu}\varphi^{j}+2v_{i}q^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi^{i}C^{\mu}+{q^{\prime}}^{2}C_{\mu}C^{\mu}\right)\,\,\,.

Under the local transformation

scales as

\widetilde{R}\longrightarrow e^{-\sigma}\,\widetilde{R}

Similarly, the anti-symmetric part of the scale invariant Ricci tensor

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{R}_{\mu\nu}-\widetilde{R}_{\nu\mu}\right)=-{q^{\prime}}\left(1-\frac{v_{i}}{q_{i}}\omega\right)\,C_{\mu\nu}\,\,\,\,.

(8.3)

A sum over the repeated index

The scale invariant theory is described by the action

\displaystyle S_{\text{scale}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,e^{-\frac{q_{i}\varphi^{i}}{N}}\Bigg\{-\widetilde{R}+\kappa\,e^{-\frac{q_{j}\varphi^{j}}{N}}\Bigg\}

Finally, we have shown in section 5, that there is a kind of a duality transformation in the theory written in (

Appendix A
Scale invariance of a fermionic kinetic term and a Yukawa coupling

The kinetic part of a Lagrangian for a fermion field
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{fermion}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{-g}\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^{a}E_{a}^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi\,\,\,\,,

(A.1)

where

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{bc}\Sigma_{bc}+\dots\right)\psi\,\,\,\,.

(A.2)

The dots in
D_{\mu}\psi
stand for possible coupling to gauge fields. Here we use the notation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ab}\,e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\mu}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,e^{a}_{\mu}E^{\mu}_{b}=\delta^{a}_{b}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,e^{a}_{\mu}E^{\nu}_{a}=\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.3)

The vielbeins scale as
e^{a}_{\mu}\longrightarrow e^{\sigma/2}\,e^{a}_{\mu}
and their inverses as
E_{a}^{\mu}\longrightarrow e^{-\sigma/2}\,E_{a}^{\mu}
.
Latin indices are raised and lowered with the flat metric
\eta_{ab}
and its inverse
\eta^{ab}
. The spin connection is given by

\omega^{a}_{\mu b}=-E^{\nu}_{b}\left(\partial_{\mu}e^{a}_{\nu}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\,e^{a}_{\alpha}\right)\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.4)

We ave also

\Sigma_{ab}=\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma_{a}\,,\,\gamma_{b}\right]\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\gamma^{a}\gamma^{b}+\gamma^{b}\gamma^{a}=2\eta^{ab}\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.5)

The spin connection transforms under the scaling symmetry
g_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow e^{\sigma}\,g_{\mu\nu}
as

\omega^{a}_{\mu b}\longrightarrow\omega^{a}_{\mu b}+\frac{1}{2}\left(E^{\nu}_{b}\,e^{a}_{\nu}-\eta^{ad}\,E^{\nu}_{d}\,e^{c}_{\mu}\,\eta_{cb}\right)\partial_{\nu}\sigma\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.6)

The covariant derivative then changes as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\psi\longrightarrow e^{-3\sigma/4}\,\left[D_{\mu}\psi-\frac{3}{4}\,\partial_{\mu}\sigma\,\psi+\frac{1}{4}\,\left(\eta^{bc}E^{\nu}_{c}\,e^{a}_{\nu}-\eta^{ad}\,E^{\nu}_{d}\,e^{b}_{\mu}\right)\partial_{\nu}\sigma\,\Sigma_{ab}\,\psi\right]\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.7)

Next, the Lagrangian transforms as

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{fermion}}\longrightarrow L_{\mathrm{fermion}}+i\sqrt{-g}\,\bar{\psi}E_{c}^{\mu}\left[-\frac{3}{4}\,\gamma^{c}\,\partial_{\mu}\sigma+\frac{1}{4}\left(\eta^{bc}\,\gamma^{d}\Sigma_{db}-\eta^{ac}\,\gamma^{d}\,\Sigma_{ad}\right)\partial_{\mu}\sigma\right]\psi\,\,\,\,.

(A.8)

\displaystyle\eta^{bc}\,\gamma^{d}\Sigma_{db}-\eta^{ac}\,\gamma^{d}\,\Sigma_{ad}=3\,\gamma^{c}\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.9)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{fermion}}\longrightarrow L_{\mathrm{fermion}}\,\,\,\,\,\,.

(A.10)

Abstract
We introduce a new method for calculating the O ​ ( α 3 ) \text{O}(\alpha^{3}) hadronic-vacuum-polarization contribution to the muon anomalous magnetic moment from ab-initio lattice QCD. We first derive expressions suitable for computing the higher-order contributions either from the renormalized vacuum polarization function Π ^ ​ ( q 2 ) \widehat{\Pi}(q^{2}) , or directly from the lattice vector-current correlator in Euclidean space. We then demonstrate the approach using previously-published results for the Taylor coefficients of Π ^ ​ ( q 2 ) \widehat{\Pi}(q^{2}) that were obtained on four-flavor QCD gauge-field configurations with physical light-quark masses. We obtain 10 10 ​ a μ HVP , HO = − 9.3 ​ ( 1.3 ) 10^{10}a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,HO}=-9.3(1.3) , in agreement with, but with a larger uncertainty than, determinations from e + ​ e − → hadrons e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons} data plus dispersion relations.



I
Introduction

The anomalous magnetic moment of the muon (
g_{\mu}-2
) is one of the most precisely-determined observables in particle physics, having been measured with an uncertainty of 0.54 parts-per-million by BNL Experiment E821
Bennett et al. 2006
.
Because of this high experimental precision, and because the anomaly is mediated by quantum-mechanical loops in the Standard Model, the muon
g_{\mu}-2
provides stringent constraints on new heavy or weakly-coupled particles.
The present Standard-Model theory value lies below the BNL E821 measurement by more than three standard deviations
Blum et al. 2013
.
To identify definitively whether this deviation is due to new particles or forces, both the theory and measurement errors must be improved.
The Muon
g_{\mu}-2
Experiment recently began running at Fermilab, and aims to reduce experimental error by a factor of four
Grange et al. 2015
.
In parallel, numerous efforts are underway by the lattice-QCD community to tackle the Standard-Model hadronic contributions
Aubin and Blum 2007
;
Boyle et al. 2012
;
Burger et al. 2014
;
Blum et al. 2016
;
Chakraborty et al. 2017a
;
Blum et al. 2017
;
Borsanyi et al. 2017a
;
Della Morte et al. 2017
;
Borsanyi et al. 2017b
;
Blum et al. 2018
, which are the largest source of theory uncertainty
Blum et al. 2013
.

The largest source of uncertainty in the Standard-Model
g_{\mu}-2
is from the
O
(
\alpha^{2}
) hadronic vacuum-polarization (HVP) contribution
Blum et al. 2013
,
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
, which is shown in Fig.
1
.

To match the target experimental precision, however, the error on

The

Jegerlehner 2017a

Keshavarzi et al. 2018

Nevertheless, it is important to check these phenomenological values with

Moreover, if the disagreement between theory and experiment persists or grows with the new Muon
g_{\mu}-2

In this paper we calculate the higher-order HVP contribution to
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}

Details on the lattice-QCD calculations can be found in these works.

III.1
Approximants of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})

We calculate the higher-order contributions to
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
using both Padé and Mellin-Barnes approximants of the renormalized vacuum polarization function in the QED integrals given in Appendix
A
.
Both approaches employ the Taylor coefficients
\Pi_{i}
of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
expanded about
Q^{2}=0
:

\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Pi_{i}Q^{2i}

(5)

As observed in Ref.
Chakraborty et al. 2014
, the
\Pi_{i}
are proportional to the time-moments of the vector-current correlation function, and can be computed with small statistical errors in lattice QCD. Further, with both the Padé and Mellin-Barnes approches, only the first few Taylor coefficients are needed to obtain the leading-order HVP with a sub-percent systematic uncertainty associated with the parameterization of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})

Chakraborty et al. 2017a
;
Charles et al. 2017
.

Following the method introduced by the HPQCD Collaboration
Chakraborty et al. 2014
, we construct the
[n,m]
Padé approximants for the renormalized hadronic vacuum polarization function from the
\Pi_{i}
’s.
The true result for
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
is guaranteed to lie between the
[n,n]
and
[n,n-1]
Padé approximants.
For the leading-order HVP contribution, the Padé approximants provide a sufficiently accurate approximation of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
both at low and high
Q^{2}
that the associated uncertainty in
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
is below 1% by
n=2

Chakraborty et al. 2017a
.
Unfortunately, however, one cannot use the
[n,n-1]
approximants
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
to calculate the contributions to
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
from diagrams (a) and (c). This is because
\widehat{\Pi}^{[n,n-1]}(Q^{2})\sim Q^{2}
as
Q^{2}\to\infty
, making the integrals diverge in this limit.
The integrals using the
[n,n]
Padé approximants are well behaved, but another approach is needed to quantify the uncertainty in the higher-order contributions to
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
from the parameterization of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
.

Recently de Rafael and Charles
et al.
introduced the method of “Mellin-Barnes approximants” to obtain
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
from the Taylor coefficients of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})

de Rafael 2017
;
Charles et al. 2017
.
This approach uses the fact that the hadronic spectral function
{\rm Im}\widehat{\Pi}(q^{2})/\pi
in QCD is positive and approaches a constant as
Q^{2}\to\infty
to identify a class of functions that can be employed as successive approximants to the Mellin transform
{\mathcal{M}}(s)
of the hadronic spectral function.
Given
N
moments of the Mellin transform
{\mathcal{M}}(-n)
, the Mellin-Barnes approximant
{\mathcal{M}}_{N}
smoothly interpolates between these known values, and approaches the asymptotic value of
{\mathcal{M}}(s)
from leading-order perturbative QCD as
s\to\infty
. The Mellin moments are trivially related to the Taylor coefficients of
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
as

{\mathcal{M}}(-n)=4\pi\alpha(-1)^{n}(4m_{\pi}^{2})^{(n+1)}\Pi_{n+1}\,,

(6)

The first term in the moment expansion of the hadronic spectral function provides a rigorous upper bound on
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
and
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}

Bell and de Rafael 1969
. In practice, the
N=1
approximant obtained using
{\mathcal{M}}(0)
from experimental
R_{\gamma}
data yields a value for the leading-order HVP contribution that already agrees with the full result to better than 1%
Charles et al. 2017
.

Figure
4
plots the Padé and Mellin-Barnes approximants for
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
calculated from the first four moments of
R_{\gamma}(s)

Keshavarzi et al. 2018
, and compares them with the exact result obtained from direct integration of
R_{\gamma}(s)
.
The Mellin-Barnes approximants are closer to the exact
\Pi(Q^{2})
than the Padés because they are constrained to satisfy the asymptotic perturbative-QCD behavior as
Q^{2}\to\infty
. However, the rate at which the Mellin-Barnes approximants approach the true
\widehat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
depends upon the specific functional form employed at each order. In particular, the difference between successive approximants is not guaranteed to decrease with increasing
N
.

[2,2]

g_{\mu}-2

IV
Result and outlook

The
\text{O}(\alpha_{\rm EM}^{3})
hadronic-vacuum polarization contribution is a necessary ingredient in an
ab-initio
-QCD determination of the hadronic contributions to
g_{\mu}-2
. Towards this aim, we have introduced a new method for calculating the higher-order HVP contribution from lattice QCD, deriving formulae in terms of either the Euclidean vector-current correlator or the renormalized vacuum polarization function. These are given in Appendix
A
, and are the key results of this work.

We demonstrate the approach using the Taylor coefficents of the renormalized vacuum polarization function at the physical light-quark mass and two lattice spacings from Ref.
Chakraborty et al. 2017a
. The total higher-order hadronic vacuum polarization contribution to
g_{\mu}-2
is obtained by adding our calculation of the quark-connected contribution, Eq. (
9
), to our estimate of the quark-disconnected contribution, Eq. (
11
). Our final result is

10^{10}a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,HO}=-9.3(0.6)_{\rm conn.}(0.1)_{\rm disc.}(1.0)_{{\rm log}^{2}}\,,

(12)

where the first two errors errors are from the quark-connected and quark-disconnected contributions, respectively. We list the error from omission of the “log
2
” terms separately, since it does not arise from the use of lattice QCD to obtain the renormalized vacuum polarization function. This error could be eliminated with a different trick for expressing contribution (a) in terms of
\hat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
than the one employed here. Equation (
12
) is the first lattice-QCD determination of the higher-order hadronic vacuum polarization contribution to
g_{\mu}-2
. It is consistent with determinations from
e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons}
data
Kurz et al. 2016
;
Jegerlehner 2017a
;
Keshavarzi et al. 2018
, but with an approximately ten times larger error.

A significant – and difficult to quantify – uncertainty in Eq. (
12
) stems from our use of approximants for the renormalized vacuum polarization function, which we employ so that we can exploit already-published values of the Taylor coefficients. Our estimated error covers the results for
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,HO}
from both the Padé and Mellin-Barnes approaches; this is based on the empirical observation that the exact result for
\hat{\Pi}(Q^{2})
obtained from
R_{\gamma}(s)
lies between the two types of approximants.
Fortunately, this error can be eliminated by calculating the
\text{O}(\alpha_{\rm EM}^{3})
contributions directly from the lattice vector-current correlators.
We will update our initial results using this theoretically cleaner approach, and also analyze ensembles with finer lattice spacings, in a future work.

Confirmation from independent lattice-QCD calculations is also essential before any results can be combined with experimental
g-2
measurements to test the Standard Model. The tools developed in this paper will enable others to provide this.

A.1
Contribution (a)

A complete analytical result for the contribution from the diagrams in (a) of Fig.

As suggested by Groote

r^{n}

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\left[(a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3})\,\widehat{\Pi}\left(\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{x}\right)\right.

(13)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{(b_{0}+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+b_{3}x^{3})}{x}\,\widehat{\Pi}\left(m_{\mu}^{2}x\right)\right]\,,

with

\displaystyle a_{0}=-\frac{23}{18}\,,\quad b_{0}=\frac{61791297-7818200\pi^{2}}{1200}\,,\quad

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{367}{108}\,,\quad b_{1}=-\frac{724746871}{1200}+\frac{152879\pi^{2}}{2}\,,\quad

\displaystyle a_{2}=-\frac{10079}{1800}\,,\quad b_{2}=\frac{5364282053}{3600}-\frac{377219\pi^{2}}{2}\,,\quad

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{(a)}

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha^{3}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{2}\,G(t)\;\tilde{K}^{(a)}_{\ell}(t)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{K}^{(a)}_{\ell}(t)

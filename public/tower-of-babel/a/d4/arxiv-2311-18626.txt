Hilbert series and degrees of regularity of Oil & \& Vinegar and Mixed quadratic systems
Antonio Corbo Esposito, Rosa Fera, Francesco Romeo

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze the algebraic invariants for two classes of multivariate quadratic systems: systems made by OV quadratic polynomials and systems made by both OV polynomials and fully quadratic ones. For such systems, we explicitly compute the Hilbert series, and we give bounds on the degree of regularity, solving degree and first fall degree that are useful in cryptographic applications.



1.
Introduction

The problem of solving multivariate polynomial systems is one of the hard problems that have been used to build quantum-resistant cryptosystems.
It is interesting to explore how properties, invariants and tools in Commutative Algebra can provide insights into the complexity of solving these problems. In the modern literature, to measure such complexity, various authors have introduced quantities associated with different algebraic properties: the
first fall degree
that is related to the computation of the syzygies of the system, the
degree of regularity
that is computed through the Hilbert series, and the
solving degree
that arises from Gröbner bases. In particular, Syzygies represent relationships between polynomials to spot redundancies or dependencies among them, Hilbert series of a polynomial ring captures informations about the dimensions of the vector spaces of homogeneous polynomials in different degrees, while Gröbner bases are sets of polynomials from which one can derive all the polynomials in an ideal, and they have a specific “leading term” relationship that simplifies many algebraic operations and computations.

Several of the most recent multivariate polynomial schemes, proposed for standardization to NIST competition, derive their security from quadratic polynomial systems over finite fields.
This problem is known as Multivariate Quadratic (MQ) problem.
In 1997, Patarin proposed the
Oil and Vinegar scheme
(OV scheme)
[
15
]
. The main feature of these multivariate quadratic systems relies on separating the variables into two distinct subsets: the subset of variables called “oil” and the one of the variables called “vinegar”. Each polynomial (of degree 2 with coefficients in a finite field) is chosen such that, after an evaluation in the vinegar variables, it is linear only in the oil variables. Such a scheme is based on an under-determined MQ system and the goal is to find one of the many preimages of the original message.
It must be highlighted that in the case in which the number of the oil variables is equal to the number of the vinegar variables, the scheme was already broken by Kipnis and Shamir (see
[
9
]
), therefore it was modified to the so-called “Unbalanced Oil and Vinegar scheme” (UOV), where the number of vinegar variables is greater than the number of oil ones (see
[
8
]
). UOV is still used in many of the recent multivariate schemes (see
[
5
,
3
]
).

In this paper, we analyze the algebraic properties of two kinds of systems: one comprising only OV polynomials, and the other including both OV polynomials and fully quadratic ones. For this kind of schemes we compute the Hilbert series and other algebraic invariants. The Hilbert series of OV-systems is also studied in
[
11
]
, where the authors give a lower bound of its coefficients. We improve the latter result by giving an exact formula as sum of two other Hilbert series. The crucial observation is that the ideal generated by a homogeneous OV system is contained in the ideal
\mathcal{V}
generated by the vinegar variables. The study of the Hilbert series of the OV-system
\mathcal{F}
in the polynomial ring
R
reduces to the study of the Hilbert series of
\mathcal{F}
in the ideal
\mathcal{V}
and we introduce a degree of regularity for OV-systems. We also focus on fields with characteristic 0: we compute some algebraic invariants of
R/\mathcal{F}
, we give an upper bound for the degree of regularity in the over-determined case and we also discuss some relations arising from non-regular sequences, and we give definitions of OV semi-regular sequence; moreover, we show that any quadratic system can be seen as an OV-system up to linear transformation.
We use similar techniques to compute the Hilbert series of mixed systems, observing that the fully quadratic equations can be split into a part in
\mathcal{V}
, involved in the computation with
\mathcal{F}
, and in an oil part that gives contribution to the Hilbert series separately in the polynomial ring in the sole oil variables.
We also analyze OV non-homogeneous quadratic systems and we discuss their first fall degree, degree of regularity and solving degree.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall several definitions that are useful for the sequel; in particular, the algebraic invariants, the cryptographic background and direct computations of the Hilbert series of semi-regular quadratic systems (see
2.6
). In Section
3
, we give a definition of semi–regular sequence on a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and we compute the Hilbert series for the related systems. In Section
4
, we dive into OV homogeneous quadratic systems: we compute their Hilbert series, by using additive property, as the sum of the Hilbert series of the polynomial ring in the oil variables and the Hilbert series of the vinegar ideal modulo the ideal generated by the system (Theorem
4.1
);
in Section
4.1
, by restricting our attention on characteristic-0 fields, we compute height and Krull dimension of the ideal
\mathcal{F}
generated by the system, and since
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}
, these two invariants are both related to
v
(where
v
is the number of the vinegar variables) (Lemma
4.5
); in particular if
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
, then
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
can be regular if and only if
m\leq v
. The latter implies that for any
m\geq v+1
, there are trivial relations arising from any subset of cardinality
v+1
of the
m
equations; such relations appear at degree
v+2
as pointed out in Proposition
4.6
. Moreover if
m\geq n
, we prove that the degree of regularity is bounded by
v+1
(Proposition
4.10
). Thanks to the previous results, we give definitions of semi-regularity for OV systems. Furthermore, in Section
4.2
, by using Noether Normalization and Relative Finiteness Theorem, we show that any quadratic system

can be seen as an OV-system in
v=n-d
vinegar variables where

is the Krull dimension of
R/\mathcal{F}
.

In Section
5
, we introduce mixed systems
\mathcal{P}
, that consist in an OV system
\mathcal{F}
and in a fully quadratic system
Q
. Given the vinegar ideal
\mathcal{V}
, the ideal
\mathcal{V}+Q
can be seen as
\mathcal{V}+Q_{o}
, where

is generated by the oil monomials of the polynomials in

, of the oil variables. Hence, the Hilbert series of

can be splitted as a sum of the Hilbert series of
(\mathcal{V}+Q_{o})/\mathcal{P}
and the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}_{o}/Q_{o}
(Theorem

). Even for those systems we introduce a degree of regularity that, in analogy with the OV system, depends on the index of regularity of one of the summands of the Hilbert Series.
In Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Algebraic Setting

Let
R
be a ring.
The
Krull dimension
of
R
,
\dim R
, is the supremum of the lengths of all chains of prime ideals of
R
.
In other words if

\mathcal{C}:\ \mathfrak{p}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathfrak{p}_{n}

is a chain of prime ideals of
R
, we say that
C
has length
n
. If the supremum of the lengths is not finite, we say that the Krull dimension is
+\infty
.
As an example if
\mathbb{K}
is a field then
\dim\mathbb{K}=0
since
(0)
is the unique prime ideal in any field.
In the whole work we always denote by
\dim
the Krull dimension and by
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}
the dimension of a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. The height of
\mathfrak{p}
is defined as the largest number
h
such that there exists a chain of different prime ideals

\mathfrak{p}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathfrak{p}_{h}=\mathfrak{p}

descending from
\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative ring
R
, the
height
of
I
,
\operatorname{ht}(I)
is defined to be the minimum of the heights of the prime ideals containing the ideal
I
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. An element
r\in R\setminus\{0\}
is a
non-zero divisor on
M
if
rm=0
implies
m=0
for
m\in M
.
A
regular sequence
is a sequence of elements
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}\in R
such that:

a)

r_{1}
is a non-zero divisor on
M
and for any
i\in\{2,\ldots,d\}
the element
r_{i}
is a non-zero divisor on
M/(r_{1},\ldots,r_{i-1})M
;

b)

(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})M\neq M
.

Let
I,J\subseteq R
be ideals. The
colon ideal
is

(I:J)=\{f\in R:fJ\subset I\}.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring on the field
\mathbb{K}
, and we denote by
\mathrm{char}{(\mathbb{K})}
its characteristic.
Given
I\subseteq R
homogeneous ideal, the
Hilbert function

\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is defined by

\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(d):=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/I)_{d}

where
(R/I)_{d}
is the
d
-degree component of the gradation of
R/I
. Hilbert function is known to be additive with respect to exact sequences.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of
R
-modules. Then, for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

\mathrm{HF}_{B}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{A}(d)+\mathrm{HF}_{C}(d).

From Lemma
2.1
, it follows that adding an equation to a given system
\mathcal{F}
results in a decreasing of the Hilbert function, as in the following

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
be homogeneous system of polynomials, let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m+1}\}
with
\deg f_{m}=\delta
. The two systems are related by the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow(R/(\mathcal{F}:f_{m+1}))_{d-\delta}\rightarrow(R/\mathcal{F})_{d}\rightarrow(R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime})_{d}\rightarrow 0

It follows that

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)-\mathrm{HF}_{R/(\mathcal{F}:f_{m+1})}(d-\delta),

in particular

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(d)\leq\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)

Given a formal power series
\sum_{d}a_{d}t^{d}
, we define
\Bigg[\sum_{d}a_{d}t^{d}\Bigg]=\sum_{d}b_{d}t^{d}
where
b_{d}=a_{d}
if
a_{i}>0
for any
0\leq i\leq d
and
b_{d}=0
, otherwise.
The
Hilbert-Poincaré series
of
R/I
is

\mathrm{HS}_{R/I}(t):=\sum_{d\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(d)t^{d}.

By the Hilbert-Serre theorem, the Hilbert-Poincaré series of
R/I
is generated by the
[\cdot]
applied to the power series expansion of a rational function. We call such a function
generating function
. In particular, by reducing this rational function we get

\mathrm{HS}_{R/I}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{\dim R/I}}\Bigg].

that is called
reduced Hilbert series
, where the
[\cdot]
on right-hand-side is intended on its power series expansion. The notation with
[\cdot]
has to be adopted because the formal power series arising from the right-hand-side may have negative coefficients, that contradicts the definition of Hilbert function. However, we highlight that the expansion of the series with negative coefficients is useful to understand the behaviour of the systems in non-homogeneous case (see Lemma
6.2
and Proposition
6.3
). From now on, in the examples we fill free to use the expression
\mathrm{HS}
to denote both the generating function of the series.

It is well-known that for large
d
the Hilbert function
\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(d)
is a polynomial, called
Hilbert polynomial
, denoted by
\mathrm{HP}_{R/I}(t)
. The least
d
for which
\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(d)=\mathrm{HP}_{R/I}(d)
, is called
index of regularity
, denoted by
i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(I)
.
Observe that,
\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(d)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}R_{d}-\dim_{\mathbb{K}}I_{d}
. According to this expression, in the (over)determined case, the index of regularity can be seen as the smallest
d
for which
I_{d}=R_{d}
. For further information on Hilbert series see
[
10
]
.

2.2.
MQ-problem and OV-polynomials

In this section, we recap fundamental notions and definitions that are useful in the sequel.
Our recurring object will be a quadratic polynomial on
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, say

p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}+\gamma

Without any particular assumption on the
\alpha_{ij}
, we will call such a polynomial a
fully quadratic
polynomial.

Moreover, let
v,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be positive integers with
v<n
. A quadratic polynomial of the form

(1)

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ \ \sum\limits_{j=1}^{v}\alpha_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}+\gamma,

namely with no monomial
x_{i}x_{j}
for
i,j\in\{v+1,\ldots,n\}
, is called an
Oil and Vinegar (OV)
polynomial. The variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{v}
are called
vinegar
variables, while the variables
x_{v+1},\ldots,x_{n}
are called
oil
variables. We set
\mathcal{V}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{v})
, namely the ideal generated by the vinegar variables. For an OV-system with
v
vinegar variables, we set
\mathbb{K}_{o}=\mathbb{K}[x_{v+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, to be the polynomial ring in the oil variables

Given a non-homogeneous quadratic polynomial
f
, we call
f^{\mathrm{top}}
the homogeneous polynomial corresponding to the highest-degree monomials of
f
. Given a system of polynomials
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
we call
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{top}}
the system
\{f^{\mathrm{top}}_{1},\ldots,f^{\mathrm{top}}_{m}\}
.

2.3.
Hilbert series of Multivariate polynomials and degree of regularity

We define the
degree of regularity

d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{F})
of a system
\mathcal{F}
of polynomial equations as the index of regularity of
R/\mathcal{F}
.
From the characterization of regular sequences (see
[
1
, Proposition 1.7.4]
), we can obtain the following on quadratic polynomials.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be a homogeneous regular sequence of quadratic polynomials with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. The following are equivalent:

•

the ideal
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
has Krull dimension
n-m
;

•

the Hilbert series of
R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
is

\frac{(1-t^{2})^{m}}{(1-t)^{n}}

Definition 2.4
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and assume that
\mathbb{K}
is an infinite field with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. If
A=R/I
, where
I
is a homogeneous ideal, and
f\in R_{d}
, then
f
is
semi-regular
on
A
if, for every
e\geq d
, the vector space map
A_{e-d}\rightarrow A_{e}
given by multiplication by
f
is of maximal rank (that is, either injective or surjective). A sequence of homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
is a
semi-regular sequence
if each
f_{i}
is semi-regular on
A/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1})
, for
1\leq i\leq m
.

For semi-regular sequence there is a result similar to Proposition
2.3
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
13
]
, Proposition 1)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field and set
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in R
be homogeneous quadratic polynomials. Then
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
is a semi-regular sequence on
R
if and only if

\mathrm{HS}_{R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{l})}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{(1-t^{2})^{l}}{(1-t)^{n}}\Bigg]

for every
1\leq l\leq m
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors adopt a concept of semi-regularity that is useful for cryptographic applications.

Definition 2.6
.

A sequence of quadratic homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
is called
cryptographic semi-regular
if

\mathrm{HS}_{R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{(1-t^{2})^{m}}{(1-t)^{n}}\Bigg]

In
[
1
]
, it is showed that Proposition
2.3
and Definition
2.6
can be restated on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
as follows

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be a homogeneous regular sequence of quadratic polynomials on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The following are equivalent:

•

the ideal
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
has Krull dimension
n-m
;

•

the Hilbert series of
R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
is

\Bigg[\frac{(1+t)^{n}}{(1+t^{2})^{m}}\Bigg]

Definition 2.8
.

A sequence of quadratic homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is called
cryptographic semiregular
if

\mathrm{HS}_{R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{(1+t)^{n}}{(1+t^{2})^{m}}\Bigg]

2.4.
Macaulay Matrix and solving degree

Let us consider a system
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\ldots f_{m}\}
of
m
quadratic equations in
n
variables.
The
homogeneous Macaulay Matrix

M_{d}
of
\mathcal{F}
at degree
d
is the matrix whose columns are all the degree-
d
monomials indexed in descending order from left to right with respect to a given term order, and the rows are obtained by multiplying any
f_{i}
for a monomial
m_{i}
with
\deg m_{i}=d-\deg f_{i}
.
Any entry corresponds to the coefficient of the monomial, on the column, in the corresponding equation, on the row.
Similarly, for a non-homogeneous system
\mathcal{F}
, the
Macaulay Matrix
at degree
d
,
M_{\leq d}
, is the matrix whose columns are labelled by the monomials up to degree
d
, in descending order from left to right, and the rows are obtained by multiplying any
f_{i}
for a monomial
m_{i}
with
\deg m_{i}\leq d-\deg f_{i}
. Similarly to the homogeneous case, any entry corresponds to the coefficient of the monomial, on the column, in the corresponding equation, on the row.
The least degree
d
such that, after Gaussian elimination, the rows of
M_{\leq d}
form a Gröbner basis with respect a term order
<
is called
solving degree
and it is denoted by
\mathrm{solv.deg_{<}(\mathcal{F})}
.

2.5.
Computing coefficients of the Hilbert Series and first fall degree

As follows from the definition of the Hilbert Series,
\mathrm{HF}_{R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})}(d)
corresponds to the total number of monomials of degree
d
in
R
minus the number of independent equations obtained by multiplying all degree
d-2
monomials by the
f_{i}
.
To find the number of independent equations, we should take in account that there are trivial relations among them. We first start from a characteristic-
0
field
\mathbb{K}
and then we particularize the computation on the module
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{2}+x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}+x_{n})
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{k,n}
be the set of degree-
k
monomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Moreover, given
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{k}
the cardinality-
k
subsets of
\mathcal{F}
.

If
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
,
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d-2}{2}\rfloor\}
and
\delta_{i}=d-2-2i
, then we have

\mathrm{Triv}_{d,i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m};\mathbb{K})=\Bigg\{\mu\cdot\prod\limits_{f\in A}f\ |\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta_{i},n},\ \ A\in\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\Bigg\},

and the number of trivial relations at degree
d
is

\mathrm{triv}_{d}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{d-2}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{i-1}|\mathrm{Triv}_{d,i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m};\mathbb{K})|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{d-2}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{n+\delta_{i}-1}{\delta_{i}}\cdot\binom{m}{i+1}.

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and we consider the field equations
{x_{1}^{2}+x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}+x_{n}}
we should add the conditions that
x_{i}^{2}=x_{i}
and hence
f^{2}=f
for any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. These translate in considering the set
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,n}
of the squarefree monomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and the set
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
of all the “monomials” in
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
.
In this case for
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d-2}{2}\rfloor\}
and
\delta_{i}=d-2-2i
, we have

\mathrm{Triv}_{d,i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\Bigg\{\mu\cdot\prod\limits_{f\in A}f\ |\ \mu\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\delta_{i},n},\ \ A\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{i+1}\Bigg\}

and the number of trivial relations at degree
d
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is

\mathrm{triv}_{d}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}(-1)^{i-1}|\mathrm{Triv}_{d,i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m};\mathbb{F}_{2})|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{n}{\delta_{i}}\cdot\binom{m+i}{i+1}.

The degree of regularity defined in Section
2.1
can be seen as the least degree
d
such that the number of equations is greater than the number of monomials and we can explicitly determine any degree-
d
monomial.
Another interesting invariant, that has been introduced in
[
7
]
and modified in
[
6
]
, is the
first fall degree
,
d_{\mathrm{fall}}
, namely the least degree for which there are relations between the degree-
d
equations obtained from the system other than the ones described in the set
\mathrm{Triv}_{d}
in this section. The first fall degree coincides with the maximum degree of polynomials in a solving algorithm such as
F_{4}
and XL. Indeed, when a degree fall happens, one obtains a new polynomial of lower degree that reduces highest degree polynomials, producing new polynomials of lower degree, and so on.

Example 2.11
.

We consider the system
\mathcal{F}
of Example
2.10
. The Macualay Matrix

produces the following set of polynomials

\displaystyle\{

\displaystyle x^{3}+z^{3}+z^{2}+1,\ \ x^{2}y+z^{2}+1,\ \ xy^{2}+z+1,\ \ y^{3},

\displaystyle x^{2}z+z^{3}+z+1,\ \ xyz+z^{2}+z,\ \ y^{2}z+z+1,\ \ xz^{2}+z^{3}+z^{2}+z,

\displaystyle yz^{2}+z^{2}+1,\ \ x^{2}+z^{2},\ \ xy+z+1,\ \ y^{2},

\displaystyle xz+z^{2}+z+1,\ \ yz+z+1,\ \ x+z,\ \ y\}.

hence, there are some degree falls and

. Observe that reducing

by

by

3.
Hilbert Series on Finite fields

In this section we study the case where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and we take in account the field equations. We follow
[
1
, Section 3.2]
and generalize Proposition
2.7
.

We consider the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{k}
for some prime
p
and some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We consider
S=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{q}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}^{q}-x_{n})
.
Although the above ring is not graded, because the ideal
(x_{1}^{q}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}^{q}-x_{n})
is not homogeneous, one may observe that for
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
the degree-
d
monomials of
S
coincide with the degree-
d
monomials of the graded ring
R=S^{h}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{q},\ldots,x_{n}^{q})
.
Hence

\mathrm{HS}_{R}(t)=\frac{(1-t^{q})^{n}}{(1-t)^{n}}=(1+t+\ldots+t^{q-1})^{n}.

Similarly to
[
1
, Definition 3.2.4]
, we give the definition of semi-regular sequence on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence of homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
is
semi-regular
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if

•

\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\neq R
;

•

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
if
f_{i}g=0
in
R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1})
and
\deg(f_{i}g)<i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{F})
, then
g=0
in
R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1},f^{q-1}_{i})
.

We now compute the Hilbert series of a semi-regular system of equations.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
be an ideal of
R
generated by a semi-regular sequence. Let
\delta=\deg f_{m}
and let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1})
. Then for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime})_{d}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{q-1}f^{i}(R/\mathcal{F})_{d-i\delta},

and as a corollary

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(d)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d-i\delta)

Proof.

We treat the case
m=1
, then the argument will inductively follow.
Let
f
be a semi-regular element of
R
and let

M=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{q-1}f^{i}(R/(f)).

We prove that
\varphi:M\rightarrow R
defined by

\varphi((g_{0},\ldots,g_{q-1}))=\sum_{i=0}g_{i}f^{i}\\

is an isomorphism.

Any element of
h\in R
can be written as
h=f^{i}g
where
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
is the maximum power of
f
dividing
h
and
f\not|g
, hence
g\in R/(f)
.
Moreover if
\varphi((g_{0},\ldots,g_{q-1}))=\varphi((h_{0},\ldots,h_{q-1}))
, namely

\sum_{i=0}g_{i}f^{i}=\sum_{i=0}h_{i}f^{i},

for some
g_{i},h_{i}\in R/(f)

then

\sum_{i=0}(g_{i}-h_{i})f^{i}=0

We multiply by
f^{q-1}
to obtain

f^{q-1}(g_{0}-h_{0})=0

From the semi-regularity of
f
this implies
g_{0}-h_{0}=fr
for some
r\in R
and then
g_{0}=h_{0}
in
R/(f)
. By proceeding in this way one gets
g_{i}=h_{i}
for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
. Hence, if
\delta=\deg f
, and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

R_{d}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{q-1}f^{i}(R/(f))_{d-\delta i}

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
be an ideal of
R
generated by a semi-regular sequence and let
d_{i}=\deg f_{i}
. Then

\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{(1+t+\ldots+t^{q-1})^{n}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(1+t^{d_{i}}+\ldots+t^{(q-1)d_{i}})}\Bigg]

Proof.

In the notation of Lemma
3.2
, one has that

\sum\limits_{d}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(d)t^{d}=\sum\limits_{d}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d-id_{m})\Big)t^{d}=

=\sum\limits_{d}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}t^{id_{m}}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)\Big)t^{d}=\sum\limits_{d}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}t^{id_{m}}\Big)\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)t^{d},

that yields

\sum\limits_{d}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)t^{d}=\Bigg[\sum\limits_{d}\frac{1}{(1+t^{d_{m}}+\ldots+t^{(q-1)d_{m}})}\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(d)t^{d}\Bigg]

and hence

\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{1}{(1+t^{d_{m}}+\ldots+t^{(q-1)d_{m}})}\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(t)\Bigg].

By inductively applying this argument one has

\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\Bigg[\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(1+t^{d_{i}}+\ldots+t^{(q-1)d_{i}})}\mathrm{HS}_{R}(t)\Bigg]=\Bigg[\frac{(1+t+\ldots+t^{q-1})^{n}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(1+t^{d_{i}}+\ldots+t^{(q-1)d_{i}})}\Bigg]

and the assertion follows.
∎

\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{q},\ldots,x_{n}^{q})
(in view of Section
3
). To denote the latter case, we simply write
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous system of
m
quadratic
OV
-equations with
v
vinegar variables. Then for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)+\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(d)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, we consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow R/\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow R/\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow 0.

From Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{V}}(d)+\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(d).

The assertion follows by observing that
R/\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbb{K}[x_{v+1},\ldots,x_{n}]=\mathbb{K}_{o}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous system of
m
quadratic
OV
-equations with
v
vinegar variables.

•

If
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, then for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)=\binom{n+d-1}{d}+\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(d)

•

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}
contains the field, then for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)=\binom{n}{d}+\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(d)

In the following corollary we prove that the index of regularity of

coincides with the index of regularity of
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous system of
m
quadratic
OV
-equations with
v
vinegar variables. Then

i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(R/\mathcal{F})=i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F})

Proof.

Let
d=\dim R/\mathcal{F}
and
d^{\prime}=\dim\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}
. From Theorem
4.1
it follows that

\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{n-v}}+\mathrm{HS}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{n-v}}+\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{(1-t)^{d^{\prime}}}.

Since

d=\max\{\dim\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F},\dim\mathbb{K}[x_{v+1},\ldots x_{n}]\}=\max\{d^{\prime},n-v\},

then we divide two cases:

(1)

d=n-v
;

(2)

d=d^{\prime}
.

(1) If
d=n-v
, then we write

\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{n-v}}=\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\frac{1+(1-t)^{n-v-d^{\prime}}h^{\prime}(t)}{(1-t)^{n-v}},

and
\deg h(t)=n-v-d^{\prime}+\deg h^{\prime}(t)
. Moreover,

i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(R/\mathcal{F})=\deg h(t)-n-v+1=\deg h^{\prime}(t)-d^{\prime}+1=i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}).

If
d=d^{\prime}
, then we write

\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d^{\prime}}}=\mathrm{HS}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(t)=\frac{(1-t)^{d^{\prime}-(n-v)}+h^{\prime}(t)}{(1-t)^{d^{\prime}}}

Hence,
\deg h(t)=\max\{d^{\prime}-(n-v),\deg h^{\prime}(t)\}
. If
\deg h^{\prime}(t)<d^{\prime}
, then both
i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(R/\mathcal{F}),i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F})\leq 0
. If
\deg h^{\prime}(t)>d^{\prime}>d^{\prime}-(n-v)
, then

i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(R/\mathcal{F})=\deg h(t)-d^{\prime}+1=\deg h^{\prime}(t)-d^{\prime}+1=i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}).

∎

Motivated by Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.3
, we give the following

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous system of
m
quadratic
OV
-equations with
v
vinegar variables and assume that
\dim\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}=0
. Then, for

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)

Proof.

Since
\dim\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}=0
, then
\mathrm{HS}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(t)=h^{\prime}(t)
with
\deg h^{\prime}(t)=d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{F})-1
, and hence
\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(d)=0
for

.
∎

From Lemma

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{v})
be a homogeneous
OV
-system of
v
equations in
n

f_{1},\ldots,f_{v}
regular sequence. Then for any

with
\deg g=2
there exists

with
\deg h=v
such that
hg\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Any degree-
2

can be written as

g=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{v}x_{j}g_{j}

where
g_{j}
is a degree-1 polynomial.
Since
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}
, then for any
i\in\{1,\ldots v\}
one has

f_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{v}x_{j}f_{ij},

and we call
A
the
v\times v

(f_{ij})
. Observe that
\det(A)
is a degree-
v

We claim that for any
g\in\mathcal{V}
with
\deg g=2
one has

\det(A)g\in\mathcal{F}.

In particular, if
A_{i}
is the matrix
A
in which we replace the
i

f_{1},\ldots f_{v}
, from linear algebra relations we obtain that for any
i\in\{1,\ldots v\}
we have

x_{i}\det(A)=\det(A_{i})\in\mathcal{F},

hence

\det(A)g=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{v}\det(A)x_{j}g_{j}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{v}\det(A_{j})g_{j}\in\mathcal{F}

The claim follows if we prove that
0\neq\det(A)\notin\mathcal{F}
.
If one has
\det(A)=0
, then there exists a linear relation between the rows of the matrix, say for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,v\}
, one has

\sum_{j=1}^{v}\lambda_{j}f_{ji}=0

with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
without loss of generality
\lambda_{v}\neq 0

the latter turns into

\sum_{j=1}^{v}\lambda_{j}f_{j}=0

that implies
\lambda_{v}f_{v}\in(f_{1},\ldots,f_{v-1})
, yielding that the sequence is not regular.
We prove by induction on
v
that
\det(A)\notin\mathcal{F}
. In the following we assume that
x_{1}\notin\operatorname{supp}(f_{ij})
if
j>1
.
If
v=2
, then
f=x_{1}f_{1}+x_{2}f_{2},
and
g=x_{1}g_{1}+x_{2}g_{2}
. We have
\det(A)=f_{1}g_{2}-f_{2}g_{1}
. If
\det(A)\in(f,g)
and since
\deg\det(A)=2
, then there exist
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{K}
such that
\det(A)=\lambda f+\mu g
. We have

\lambda x_{1}f+\mu x_{1}g=x_{1}\det(A)=\det(A_{1})=fg_{2}-f_{2}g,

yielding

(x_{1}\lambda-g_{2})f=-(x_{1}\mu+f_{2})g,

with
(x_{1}\lambda-g_{2})\notin(g)
, because
\deg g_{2}=1
and
\deg(g)=2
, and
(x_{1}\lambda-g_{2}),(x_{1}\mu+f_{2})\neq 0
by our assumption on
f_{2},g_{2}.
Hence,
f,g
is not a regular sequence, and this is a contradiction. We now assume, by induction, the assertion true for any sequence of
v-1
polynomials, and we prove that for
v
.

If one has
\det(A)\in\mathcal{F}
, say

\det(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{v}\lambda_{k}f_{k}

for some
\lambda_{k}\in R
with
\deg\lambda_{k}=v-2
, one has

\det(A_{1})=\sum_{k=1}^{v}\mu_{k}f_{k}

for some
\mu_{k}\in R
with
\deg\mu_{k}=v-1
, then

x_{1}\det(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{v}x_{1}\lambda_{k}f_{k}.

Since
x_{1}\det(A)=\det(A_{1})
, then

(\mu_{v}-x_{1}\lambda_{v})f_{v}=\sum_{k=1}^{v-1}(\mu_{ik}-x_{i}\lambda_{k})f_{k}\in(f_{1},\ldots,f_{v-1}).

We recall that
\mu_{v}=\det(A_{v1}),
that is the complement of the element
f_{v1}
in
A
, that has coefficients in
f_{ij}
with
1\leq i\leq v-1
,
2\leq j\leq v
. Our assumption that
x_{1}\notin\operatorname{supp}(f_{ij})
if
j>1
, implies
(\mu_{v}-x_{1}\lambda_{v})\neq 0
. If
\mu_{v}-x_{1}\lambda_{v}\in(f_{1},\ldots,f_{v-1})
,
then

\mu_{v}-x_{1}\lambda_{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\alpha_{i}f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\sum_{j=1}^{v}\alpha_{i}x_{j}f_{ij}

and

\mu_{v}-\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\sum_{j=2}^{v}\alpha_{i}x_{j}f_{ij}=\rho x_{1}

If
\rho\neq 0
, then a non trivial linear combination on
f_{ij}
is in
(x_{1})
and this is a contradiction to the assumption
x_{1}\notin\operatorname{supp}(f_{ij})
.
If
\rho=0
, then

\mu_{v}\in(f_{1}-x_{1}f_{11},\ldots,f_{v-1}-x_{1}f_{1v-1}),

contradicting the inductive hypothesis.

∎

We give a first definition of semi-regular sequence of
OV
-polynomials.

Definition 4.9
.

Let

m

In the case of overdetermined system, we can bound the degree of regularity.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be a semi-regular system of homogeneous OV-polynomials with
v
vinegar variables and let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})

d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{F})\leq v+1
.

Proof.

To prove the claim, we prove that
d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}=v+1
in the case
m=n
, then from Remark
2.2
it is clear that the more the number of equations increases, the more the degree of regularity decreases.
Let

of
n

n

\frac{(1-t^{2})^{n}}{(1-t)^{n}}=(1+t)^{n},

that is for any

\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(v+1)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(v+1)-\binom{n-v+v+1-1}{v+1}=\binom{n}{v+1}-\binom{n}{v+1}=0,

that is

. Furthermore,

\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{F}}(v)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(v)-\binom{n-1}{v}=\binom{n}{v}-\binom{n-1}{v}=\binom{n-1}{v-1}>0.

Hence,

.
∎

4.2.
Hilbert series of quadratic systems

In this section we use Noether Normalization and Relative Finiteness to study the Hilbert series of any quadratic system. We show that any quadratic system
\mathcal{F}
can be seen as an OV-system with respect to
n-d
variables where
d=\dim R/\mathcal{F}
. We assume that
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous ideal with
\dim R/\mathcal{F}=d
. Then there exists a map

\varphi:\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

such that
\varphi(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathcal{V}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-d})
and for any

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(k)=\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{V}/\varphi(\mathcal{F})}(k)+\binom{d+k-1}{k}

Proof.

The existence of
\varphi
is guaranteed by Noether Normalization, since the field
\mathbb{K}
has characteristic 0 and hence it is infinite. In particular,

\mathbb{K}[y_{n-d+1},\ldots,y_{n}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]/\varphi(\mathcal{F})

is injective and finite, that is
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}
. In this way,
\varphi(\mathcal{F})
is a system of OV-equations with
v=n-d
vinegar variables. Hence the second part follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

5.
Hilbert series of Mixed Systems

In this section, we focus our attention on a MQ-system that consists of homogeneous OV-polynomials as well as homogeneous fully quadratic polynomials.
We set
u
to be the number of fully quadratic polynomials
q_{1},\ldots,q_{u}
and we set
Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{u})
. We consider an OV-system
\mathcal{F}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{e})
and we set
\mathcal{P}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{e},q_{1}\ldots q_{u})
and
m=e+u
. We want to compute the Hilbert series of
\mathcal{P}
. Actually, we have several ways to compute such a series.
For this aim, we consider the ideal
\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q}
, namely the ideal generated by the vinegar variables and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{u}
. We have

\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q}_{o}

where
\mathcal{Q}_{o}
is the ideal generated by the “oil” part of
q_{1},\ldots,q_{u}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{F}+Q
be a mixed system. Then for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(1)

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{P}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q})/\mathcal{P}}(d)+\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}}(d)
;

(2)

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{P}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{F}}(d)-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Q}}(d)
;

(3)

\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{P}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/\mathcal{Q}}(d)-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Q}}(d)
.

Proof.

(1) Similarly to Theorem
4.1
, we consider the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(\mathcal{V}+Q)/\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow R/\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}\longrightarrow 0,

and the assertion follows from Lemma
2.1
.

(2) Since
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}
, then the following exact sequence arises:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow R/\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow R/\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow 0,

From the isomorphism theorem, one has that

\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{Q})/\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{Q}/(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Q}),

and again the thesis follows by applying Lemma
2.1
.

(3) Follows similarly as (2).
∎

We call
degree of regularity

d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(\mathcal{P})
the minimum integer for which
\mathcal{P}_{d}=(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q}_{o})_{d}
, namely
i_{\operatorname{reg}{}}((\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q}_{o})/\mathcal{P})
.

variables is

1+7t+22t^{2}+42t^{3}+57t^{4}+63t^{5}+64t^{6}+64t^{7}+\ldots

Since
22<63
, then the degree of regularity is
5
.

In general, the degree of regularity corresponds to the minimum
d
for which
\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(d)-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}}(d)\leq 0
, and hence
\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(d)\leq\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}}(d)
, where
G
is a cryptographic semiregular system of
m
equations in
n
variables. Indeed, one has

\mathrm{HF}_{(\mathcal{V}+Q)/\mathcal{P}}(d)=\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(d)-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}}(d),

and

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q})_{d}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}R_{d}-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}}(d).

This may not be the case if the system
\mathcal{F}
is underdetermined as showed in the next example.

Example 5.3
.

We consider a system
\mathcal{P}
of
10
equations in
10
variables, where
\mathcal{F}
is an OV system of
6
equations with
3
vinegar variables and hence
u=4
.
The series of the system
\mathcal{P}
is

1+10t+45t^{2}+120t^{3}+210t^{4}+267t^{5}+297t^{6}+354t^{7}+457t^{8}+585t^{9}+\ldots

A cryptographic semiregular system of
10
equations has Hilbert series

1+10t+45t^{2}+120t^{3}+210t^{4}+252t^{5}+210t^{6}+120t^{7}+45t^{8}+10t^{9}+t^{1}0

while the Hilbert series of the system
\mathcal{Q}_{o}
of
4
equations in
7
variables is

1+7t+24t^{2}+56t^{3}+104t^{4}+168t^{5}+248t^{6}+344t^{7}+456t^{8}+585t^{9}+\ldots

The degree of regularity of such system is
8
. In fact, by subtracting the series of
\mathbb{K}_{o}/\mathcal{Q}_{o}
from the series
R/\mathcal{P}
, one obtains

3t+21t^{2}+64t^{3}+106t^{4}+99t^{5}+51t^{6}+10t^{7}+t^{8}+t^{9}\ldots

Notice that the system starts to differ from the cryptographic semiregular system at degree
5
and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}G_{6}>\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q})_{6}
, but
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{P}_{6}<\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{Q})_{6}
, and this is due to the trivial relations that arise from the degree
5=v+2
. Indeed, from Proposition
4.6
and Corollary

, we have

\binom{e}{v+1}=\binom{6}{4}=15

6.
Solving degree and first fall degree of non-homogeneous OV systems

In this section, we analyze the case of non-homogeneous OV (quadratic) systems. Namely, systems
\mathcal{F}
of non-homogeneous polynomials in which
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{top}}
is an OV system. In this case, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is an
NH-OV system
.

Definition 6.1
.

Given a system a NH-OV system
\mathcal{F}
, we define
degree of regularity

d_{\operatorname{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=i(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{top}})
.

From the above Definition and from Proposition
4.10
, the degree of regularity is bounded by
v+1
in the determined case. We analyze the behaviour of both the solving degree and first fall degree.
We premise the following lemmas on the Macaulay Matrix.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a cryptographic semiregular system of
m
equations in
n
variables, and let
H(t)=\sum\limits_{i}h_{i}t^{i}
, with
h_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, be its Hilbert series. Let
d
be the minimum positive integer such that

\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}\leq 0,

then
\mathrm{solv.deg}(G)\leq d
.

Proof.

The solving degree is the minimum
k
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\ker M_{\leq k}=0
. We observe that for any
k
,

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\ker M_{\leq k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}R_{i}-\dim_{\mathbb{K}}M_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}h_{k}

∎

By the previous Lemma we obtain the following

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a NH-OV system of
m
equations in
n
variables with
v
vinegar variables, and let
H(t)=\sum\limits_{i}h_{i}t^{i}
with
h_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be the Hilbert series of a cryptographic semiregular system
G
of
m
equations in
n
variables. Let
d
be the minimum positive integer such that

\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}-\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)\leq 0,

then
\mathrm{solv.deg}(\mathcal{F})\leq d
.

Proof.

The solving degree corresponds is less than or equal to the minimum
d
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\ker M_{\leq d}(\mathcal{F})=0
. Since the degree-
d
monomials in
\mathbb{K}_{o}
do not appear in the system, there are
\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)
zero columns in
M_{d}(\mathcal{F})
that can be removed. Hence, if
G
is a cryptographic semiregular system of
m
equations in
n
variables, there are
\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)-\operatorname{rk}M_{d}(G)
relations in
M_{\leq d}
that are degree fall and can be used to obtain relations on monomials of lower degree. In this case one has that the solving degree is less than or equal to the minimum
d
such that

\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)-\operatorname{rk}M_{d}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(k)\Rightarrow\mathrm{HF}_{\mathbb{K}_{o}}(d)\geq\sum_{k=0}^{d}\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(k),

and the assertion follows.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a NH-OV system of
m
equations in
n
variables with
m\geq n+1
such that
\mathcal{F}^{t}op
is semi-regular. Then

\mathrm{solv.deg}(\mathcal{F})\leq v+1.

Proof.

We prove that in the case
m=n+1
, we have
\mathrm{solv.deg}(\mathcal{F})\leq v+1
.
From Proposition
6.3
, we have to compute the least
d
for which

\binom{n-v+d-1}{d}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{d}\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(k),

where
G
is a cryptographic semiregular system.
We recall that

\mathrm{HS}_{R/G}(t)=\frac{(1-t^{2})^{n+1}}{(1-t)^{n}}=(1+t)^{n+1}(1-t)

Hence
\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(d)=\binom{n+1}{d}-\binom{n+1}{d-1}
for
d\geq 1
.
Observe that

\sum_{k=0}^{d}\mathrm{HF}_{R/G}(k)=\binom{n+1}{d}

and hence

\binom{n-v+d-1}{d}\geq\binom{n+1}{d}

if
d=v+1
.
∎

Even if in general there are no relations between first fall degree, degree of regularity, and solving degree as shown in many examples (see
[
4
,
6
]
), by restricting our focus to quadratic systems, we establish the following bounds.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
G
be a non-homogeneous quadratic system of
m
equations in
n
variables, with
m\geq n
. Then

•

d_{\mathrm{fall}}(G)\leq d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(G)+1
;

•

d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(G)\leq\mathrm{solv.deg}(G)
.

Proof.

(1) Follows from the fact that, given
\mathrm{HS}_{R/G}(t)=\sum\limits_{i}h_{i}t^{i}
,
d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(G)
is the minimum
d
such that
h_{d}\leq 0
. In the case
h_{d}=0
there is no degree fall at degree
d
and
d_{fall}(G)=d_{*}(G)+1
.

(2) Follows from Proposition
6.2
. and from the fact that, given
\mathrm{HS}_{R/G}(t)=\sum\limits_{i}h_{i}t^{i}
,
d_{\operatorname{reg}{}}(G)
is the minimum
d
such that
h_{d}\leq 0
.
∎

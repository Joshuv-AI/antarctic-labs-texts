Isothermal Limit of Entropy Solutions of the Euler Equations for Isentropic Gas Dynamics
Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Fei-Min Huang, Tian-Yi Wang

Abstract
. We are concerned with the isothermal limit of entropy solutions in L ∞ L^{\infty} , containing the vacuum states, of the Euler equations for isentropic gas dynamics. We prove that the entropy solutions in L ∞ L^{\infty} of the isentropic Euler equations converge strongly to the corresponding entropy solutions of the isothermal Euler equations, when the adiabatic exponent γ → 1 \gamma\rightarrow 1 . This is achieved by combining careful entropy analysis and refined kinetic formulation with compensated compactness argument to obtain the required uniform estimates for the limit. The entropy analysis involves careful estimates for the relation between the corresponding entropy pairs for the isentropic and isothermal Euler equations when the adiabatic exponent γ → 1 \gamma\to 1 . The kinetic formulation for the entropy solutions of the isentropic Euler equations with the uniformly bounded initial data is refined, so that the total variation of the dissipation measures in the formulation is locally uniformly bounded with respect to γ > 1 \gamma>1 . The explicit asymptotic analysis of the Riemann solutions containing the vacuum states is also presented.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the isothermal limit of entropy solutions in
L^{\infty}
, containing the vacuum states,
of the Euler equations for isentropic gas dynamics which is
the oldest, but still most prominent,
paradigm for the analysis of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws.
The one-dimensional Euler equations for barotropic gas dynamics take the form:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}m=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}\big(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p\big)=0,\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\rho
denotes the fluid density,
p=p(\rho)
is the pressure, and
m=\rho u
is the momentum.
When
\rho>0
,
u:=\frac{m}{\rho}
represents the fluid velocity.

The pressure-density relation under consideration can be written by scaling as

p=\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma}\qquad\mbox{for $\gamma>1$}

(1.2)

for ideal isentropic gases,
while

p=\rho

(1.3)

for the isothermal gas.

Consider the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
)
with large Cauchy data:

(\rho,m)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x),

(1.4)

with
\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0
.

The global existence of solutions with large initial data
in
L^{\infty}
was first established in DiPerna
[
14
]

for
\gamma=1+\frac{2}{2n+1}
with
n\geq 2
integer.
For the general interval
1<\gamma\leq\frac{5}{3}
,
the global existence problem was solved in
Ding-Chen-Luo
[
12
,
13
]

and Chen
[
2
]
.
The adiabatic exponent range
\gamma\geq 3
was solved in Lions-Perthame-Tadmor
[
19
]
,
and the remaining case
\frac{5}{3}<\gamma<3
was closed in Lions-Perthame-Souganidis
[
18
]
.
Later, the existence problem for the general pressure
p(\rho)
with
(
1.2
) as its leading asymptotic term
as
\rho\to 0

was solved in Chen-LeFloch
[
6
]
,
whose approach further simplifies the proofs for the
\gamma
-law case for all
\gamma>1
.

For the isothermal case (
1.3
),
the first existence result was obtained in Nishida
[
21
]

for
BV
solutions without vacuum for large initial data.
For general
L^{\infty}
solutions containing the vacuum states,
it was first solved in Huang-Wang
[
16
]
; see also
[
17
]
for a different approach.

For the isothermal limit when the adiabatic exponent
\gamma\rightarrow 1
,
the previous analysis is in the framework of
BV
solutions away from the vacuum.
The first result was established by Nishida-Smoller
[
22
]
in the Lagrangian coordinates.
Chen-Christoforou-Zhang
[
4
]
established the

L^{1}
–dependence estimate of the
BV
solutions with respect to
\gamma>1
in the Eulerian coordinates.
The isothermal limit for the BV solutions from the full Euler equations away from the vacuum
was solved by Chen-Christoforou-Zhang in
[
5
]
.
It is well-known that the vacuum states occur generically in the entropy solutions
of the Euler equations, even starting with the initial data without vacuum states.
A prototypical example is the Riemann problem with Riemann initial data away from the vacuum for which
there exists a global Riemann solution consisting of two rarefaction waves with an intermediate
vacuum state for the isentropic Euler equations; see
[
3
]
and §7 below.
For the Riemann solution containing the vacuum states,
the fluid velocity
u
is bounded for
\gamma>1
near the vacuum,
however,
|u|
goes to
\infty
as the density goes to
0
for
\gamma=1
; see also §7.
Another difference is the sound speed as the speed of pressure disturbance travelling through the medium
vanishes as the density goes to
0
for
\gamma>1
, while the sound speed
c
is a fixed constant
independent of the density for
\gamma=1
.
Therefore, the isothermal limit of entropy solutions in
L^{\infty}
, containing the vacuum states,
with large initial data has been a longstanding open problem in the analysis of nonlinear
partial differential equations and mathematical fluid mechanics.

The main objective of this paper is to provide an affirmative answer to this problem by providing
a rigorous convergence proof of the isothermal limit.
More precisely, we prove that the entropy solutions in
L^{\infty}
, containing the vacuum states,
of the isentropic Euler equations
converge strongly to the corresponding entropy solutions of the isothermal Euler equations
when the adiabatic exponent
\gamma\rightarrow 1
.
This is achieved by combining careful entropy analysis and refined kinetic formulation
with compensated compactness argument to obtain the required uniform estimates
for the isothermal limit.
These effective approaches for the analysis of the isentropic Euler equations
are based on the subtle
analysis for the Euler–Poisson–Darboux
equation, while the entropy equation of the isothermal Euler equations
is not governed by the Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation.
The entropy analysis involves careful estimates
for the relation between the corresponding entropy pairs for the isentropic and isothermal Euler equations
when the adiabatic exponent
\gamma\to 1
.
The kinetic formulation for the entropy solutions of the isentropic Euler equations
with the uniformly bounded initial data
is refined, so that the total variation of the dissipation measures in the formulation
is locally uniformly bounded with respect to
\gamma>1
.
The compensated compactness argument is based on
the
H^{-1}
–compactness of entropy dissipation measures,
the div-curl lemma in
[
20
,
25
]
, and the Young measure presentation
theorem (
cf
.
[
1
,
25
]
); see also
[
11
,
15
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows:
In §2, we state some existence results and properties of entropy solutions
of the compressible Euler equations and present the main theorem of this paper, Theorem 2.3,
regarding the isothermal limit of the entropy solutions.
The uniform
L^{\infty}
estimate of the entropy solutions with respect
to the adiabatic exponents
\gamma>1
is shown in §3,
while the refined kinetic formulation is obtained in §4.
The uniform relation between the corresponding entropy pairs
for the isentropic and isothermal Euler equations
is established in §5.
In §6, we complete the proof of the main theorem, Theorem 2.3.
Finally, in §7, the explicit asymptotic analysis of the Riemann solutions
containing the vacuum states
is presented. In particular, the phenomenon of decavitation is shown as
\gamma\to 1
,
which is different from the formation of cavitation and concentration
in the vanishing pressure limit (equivalently, the high Mach limit) presented
in Chen-Liu
[
8
]
.

2.
Entropy Solutions of the Compressible Euler Equations

In this section, we first present some existence results and properties of entropy solutions
of the compressible Euler equations (
1.1
), which can be rewritten as
a hyperbolic system of conservation laws of the form:

\partial_{t}U+\partial_{x}F^{(\theta)}(U)=0

(2.1)

with
U=(\rho,m)
,
F^{(\theta)}(U)=(m,\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p^{(\theta)}(\rho))
,
and
\theta:=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\geq 0
.
The pressure-density relation (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

p^{(\theta)}(\rho)=\frac{\rho^{2\theta+1}}{2\theta+1}.

(2.2)

System (
2.1
) represents the isentropic Euler equations when
\theta>0

and the isothermal Euler equations when
\theta=0
.
Then the entropy pair
(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})
of the mechanical energy and energy flux is of the form:

(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})(\rho,m)=(\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+\rho e^{(\theta)}(\rho),\,\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{3}}{\rho^{2}}+m\frac{{\rm d}(\rho e^{(\theta)}(\rho))}{{\rm d}\rho}),

(2.3)

where
e^{(\theta)}(\rho)=\int^{\rho}_{1}\frac{p^{(\theta)}(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}d\tau
is the specific internal energy.
Notice that
c^{(\theta)}(\rho)=\rho^{\theta}
, which implies that

\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}c^{(\theta)}(\rho)=0
, while
c^{(0)}(\rho)\equiv 1
.

We now introduce the following broad class of entropy solutions:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\theta\geq 0
.
A vector function
U(t,x)=(\rho,m)(t,x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
is called
an entropy solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4
)
with initial data
(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
if,
for any test function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\big(U\,\partial_{t}\phi+F^{(\theta)}(U)\,\partial_{x}\phi\big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{0}(x)\phi(0,x)\,{\rm d}x=0,

(2.4)

and, for any test function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\phi\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\big(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*}(U)\,\partial_{t}\phi+q^{(\theta)}_{*}(U)\,\partial_{x}\phi\big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{(\theta)}_{*}(U_{0})(x)\phi(0,x)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0.

(2.5)

For system (
2.1
), a general entropy pair
(\eta,q)
obeys the following linear hyperbolic system:

\nabla q(\rho,m)=\nabla\eta(\rho,m)\nabla F^{(\theta)}(\rho,m).

(2.6)

A weak entropy is an entropy
\eta(\rho,m)
that vanishes at
\rho=0
(vacuum), and
then the corresponding pair
(\eta,q)
is called a weak entropy pair.
Notice that
u(t,x):=\frac{m(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}
is not well defined on the vacuum set
\{\rho(t,x)=0\}
,
which is one of the main reasons why the weak entropy is used,
while the momentum function
m(t,x)
itself is
well-defined to be
0
on
\{\rho(t,x)=0\}
almost everywhere.
We will often use
m
and
u=\frac{m}{\rho}
alternatively when

\rho>0
without ambiguity.

Any entropy solution
(\rho,m)(t,x)
is also required (whenever available)
to satisfy the additional entropy inequality:

\partial_{t}\eta(\rho,m)+\partial_{x}q(\rho,m)\leq 0

(2.7)

in the sense of distributions for any weak entropy pair
(\eta,q)(\rho,m)
satisfying that

\eta(\rho,m)
is a convex function with respect to

(\rho,m)
.

As in
[
2
,
6
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
,
the weak entropy
\eta(\rho,u)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{\rho\rho}\eta-\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{uu}\eta=0\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\rho>0$},\\[2.84526pt]
\eta|_{\rho=0}=0,\end{cases}

(2.8)

and the corresponding entropy flux
q(\rho,u)
satisfies

\partial_{\rho}q(\rho,u)=u\partial_{\rho}\eta+\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho}\partial_{u}\eta,\qquad\partial_{u}q(\rho,u)=\rho\partial_{\rho}\eta+u\partial_{u}\eta

for a general pressure function
p(\rho)
, including
p^{(\theta)}(\rho)
for
\theta\geq 0
.

2.1.
Isentropic case

When
\theta>0
, the Riemann invariants of system (
2.1
) are

w_{j}^{(\theta)}=\frac{m}{\rho}+(-1)^{j+1}\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\qquad\mbox{for $j=1,2$}.

The weak entropy kernel of (
2.1
) is

\chi^{(\theta)}(\rho;s-u)=a_{\theta}\big[(\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta})^{2}-(s-u)^{2}\big]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}=a_{\theta}[(w_{1}-s)(s-w_{2})]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}

(2.9)

with
a_{\theta}=\theta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\big(\int_{-1}^{1}[1-\tau^{2}]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\,{\rm d}\tau\big)^{-1}
,
which is a fundamental solution of (
2.8
), determined by

\begin{cases}\partial_{\rho\rho}\chi^{(\theta)}-\rho^{\gamma-3}\partial_{uu}\chi^{(\theta)}=0\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\rho>0$},\\[5.69054pt]
\chi^{(\theta)}|_{\rho=0}=0,\quad\chi^{(\theta)}_{\rho}|_{\rho=0}=\delta_{u=s},\end{cases}

where we have used the notation:
[x]_{+}=\max(0,x)
.
Then we have the following representation of weak entropy pairs:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\theta>0
, the weak entropy pair of (
2.1
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\eta^{(\theta)}(\rho,m;\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi^{(\theta)}(\rho;s-\frac{m}{\rho})\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle q^{(\theta)}(\rho,m;\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\theta s+(1-\theta)\frac{m}{\rho}\big)\chi^{(\theta)}(\rho;s-\frac{m}{\rho})\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s,

for any
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Furthermore,
\eta^{(\theta)}(\rho,m;\psi)
is convex if and only if
\psi
is
convex
(
cf.
[
19
]
)
.

Using the standard change of variable
s=u+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\eta^{(\theta)}(\rho,m;\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\big(\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\big)^{2}-(u-s)^{2}\big]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(u+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\tau)[1-\tau^{2}]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\,{\rm d}\tau}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}[1-\tau^{2}]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\,{\rm d}\tau},\qquad

\displaystyle q^{(\theta)}(\rho,m;\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\theta s+(1-\theta){u})\big[\big(\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\big)^{2}-({u}-s)^{2}\big]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s

(2.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u+\rho^{\theta}\tau\right)\psi(u+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\tau)[1-\tau^{2}]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}{\rm d}\tau}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}[1-\tau^{2}]_{+}^{\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}}\,{\rm d}\tau}.

In particular, the energy and energy flux
(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})

may also be obtained by choosing
\psi=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta(2\theta+1)}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Existence Theorem for
\theta>0
)
.

For any
\theta>0
, there exist both a bounded entropy solution
(\rho,m)
of
the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and
a corresponding
bounded dissipation measure
D^{(\theta)}\leq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
such that the weak entropy kernel

\chi^{(\theta)}(\rho;s-\frac{m}{\rho})
satisfies the kinetic formulation
:

\partial_{t}\chi^{(\theta)}+\partial_{x}\big((\theta s+(1-\theta)\frac{m}{\rho})\chi^{(\theta)}\big)=\partial_{ss}D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)

(2.12)

with initial data
(\rho_{0},m_{0})
such that
\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}

and
(\rho_{0},\frac{m_{0}}{\rho_{0}})(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover,
(\rho,m)
satisfies that

\frac{|m(t,x)|}{\rho(t,x)}+\frac{\rho(t,x)^{\theta}-1}{\theta}\leq\Big\|\frac{|m_{0}|}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{\rho_{0}^{\theta}-1}{\theta}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=:w_{0}^{(\theta)},

(2.13)

and
\mbox{\rm supp}\,D^{(\theta)}(t,x;\cdot)\subset[-w_{0}^{(\theta)},w_{0}^{(\theta)}]
.

See
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
for the details.

Remark 2.1
.

The kinetic formulation (
2.12
) is equivalent
to the weak entropy inequality (
2.7
) for
L^{\infty}
solutions.
See Chen-Perepelitsa
[
9
]
and Perthame
[
23
]
.

2.2.
Isothermal case

When
\theta=0
, the Riemann invariants are

w_{j}^{(0)}=\rho e^{(-1)^{j+1}\frac{m}{\rho}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=1,2$}.

Then typical weak entropy pairs are

\eta_{\xi}(\rho,m)=\rho^{\frac{1}{1-\xi^{2}}}e^{\frac{\xi}{1-\xi^{2}}\frac{m}{\rho}},\ \ q_{\xi}(\rho,m)=(\frac{m}{\rho}+\xi)\eta_{\xi}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\xi\in(-1,1)$}.

(2.14)

This generates the following family of weak entropy pairs:

Lemma 2.2
.

When
\theta=0
, the entropy pair
(\eta^{(0)},q^{(0)})
:

\eta^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)=\int_{-1}^{1}\eta_{\xi}(\rho,m)\psi(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi,\quad q^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)=\int_{-1}^{1}\big(\frac{m}{\rho}+\xi\big)\eta_{\xi}(\rho,m)\psi(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi

(2.15)

is a weak entropy pair of (
2.1
) for any
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mbox{\rm supp}\,\psi(\cdot)\Subset(-1,1)
.
Furthermore,
\eta^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)
is convex if and only if
\psi\geq 0
.

The representation formula (
2.15
) is the result in
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
.
It has also been shown
in
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
,
for any
\xi\in(-1,1)
,
\eta_{\xi}(\rho,m)
is strictly convex,
which implies that
\eta^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)
is convex if and only if
\psi
is nonnegative.

Theorem 2.2

(Compactness Framework for
\theta=0
)
.

Let
(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})
be a sequence of approximate solutions
of (
2.1
) with
\theta=0
satisfying

0\leq\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\leq C,\ \ |m^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|\leq\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\big(|\ln\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|+C\big)\qquad\mbox{{\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$},

(2.16)

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
. Assume that there exists a small constant
\delta>0
such that,
for any
\xi\in(-\delta,\delta)
,

\partial_{t}\eta_{\xi}(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})+\partial_{x}q_{\xi}(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})\quad\ \mbox{is compact in }H^{-1}_{\rm loc}

(2.17)

for the weak entropy pairs
(\eta_{\xi},q_{\xi})
defined in (
2.14
).
Then there exist both a subsequence
(
still denoted
)

(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})
and a vector function
(\rho,m)(t,x)
such that

(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})(t,x)\rightarrow(\rho,m)(t,x)\qquad\,\,\mbox{in $L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})$ for all $p\in[1,\infty)\,$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$}.

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
16
]
, Theorem
2.2
is stated when condition (
2.17
)
holds for the case that
\delta=1
.
It can be directly checked that Theorem
2.2
is still valid as stated above
for some small constant
\delta>0
, by following the same argument in
[
16
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

Condition (
2.16
) is equivalent to

0\leq w_{j}^{(0)}(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})\leq C\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $j=1,2$},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

2.3.
Main Theorem

We now state the main theorem of this paper.
Since our main focus is on the isothermal limit
\theta\to 0
, we always assume
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]

for some fixed
\theta_{0}\in(0,\infty)

without loss of generality; for simplicity, we take any fixed
\theta_{0}\in(0,1)
in our analysis from now on throughout this paper.

Theorem 2.3

(Main Theorem)
.

For
\theta>0
, let
(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})(t,x)
be entropy solutions
of (
2.1
) satisfying (
2.12
)–(
2.13
)
with initial data
(\rho^{(\theta)}_{0},m^{(\theta)}_{0})(x)
, as constructed in Theorem
2.1
.
Assume that there exists a constant
w_{0}>0

(
e.g.,
w_{0}=\sup_{0<\theta\leq\theta_{0}}w_{0}^{(\theta)}
)

independent of
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
such that
the initial data
(\rho^{(\theta)}_{0},m^{(\theta)}_{0})(x)
satisfy that

\frac{|m_{0}^{(\theta)}(x)|}{\rho_{0}^{(\theta)}(x)}+\frac{(\rho_{0}^{(\theta)}(x))^{\theta}-1}{\theta}\leq w_{0}\qquad\,\,\,\mbox{{\it a.e.} $x\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(2.18)

Then there exist both a subsequence
(
still denoted
)

(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})
and a vector function
(\rho,m)
such that

(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})(t,x)\rightarrow(\rho,m)(t,x)\quad\,\,\mbox{in $L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})$ for all $p\in[1,\infty)\,\,$ as $\theta\to 0$},

and
(\rho,m)(t,x)
is an entropy solution of (
2.1
) with
\theta=0
satisfying

0\leq\rho(t,x)\leq e^{w_{0}},\ \ |m(t,x)|\leq\rho(t,x)\big(|\ln\rho(t,x)|+w_{0}\big)\qquad\,\,\mbox{{\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$}

and the entropy inequality (
2.7
) in the sense of distributions
for any weak entropy pair
(\eta_{\xi},q_{\xi})
for

\xi\in(-\sqrt{2}+1,\sqrt{2}-1)
.
In particular, for any nonnegative function
\psi(\xi)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

with
{\rm supp}\,\psi(\xi)\Subset(-\sqrt{2}+1,\sqrt{2}-1)
,

(\rho,m)
satisfies

\partial_{t}\eta^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)+\partial_{x}q^{(0)}(\rho,m;\psi)\leq 0

in the sense of distributions.

3.
Uniform
L^{\infty}
–Estimate

In this section, we establish the uniform
L^{\infty}
–estimate of the entropy solutions in Theorem
2.1

with respect to the adiabatic exponents
\gamma>1
,
i.e.
,
\theta>0
.

Lemma 3.1

(Uniform
L^{\infty}
–Bound with respect to
\theta>0
)
.

Let
(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})(t,x)
be the entropy solutions of (
2.1
) with
initial data
(\rho^{(\theta)}_{0},m^{(\theta)}_{0})(x)

satisfying (
2.18
), as
constructed in Theorem
2.1
.
Then, for almost everywhere
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)\leq e^{w_{0}},\qquad|m^{(\theta)}(t,x)|\leq\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)\big(|\ln\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)|+w_{0}\big),

(3.1)

and
(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})
are uniformly bounded with respect to

\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.1
and condition (
2.18
),
for each fixed
\theta>0
,
the entropy solution
(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})
of (
2.1
)
satisfies

0\leq\frac{(\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x))^{\theta}}{\theta}\leq w_{0}+\frac{1}{\theta},\,\,\,\,\frac{|m^{(\theta)}(t,x)|}{\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)}\leq w_{0}+\frac{1-(\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x))^{\theta}}{\theta}\qquad\mbox{for {\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$},

(3.2)

which implies

0\leq\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)\leq(1+\theta w_{0})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq e^{w_{0}}\qquad\mbox{for {\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$}.

(3.3)

On the other hand,

\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)e^{\frac{|m^{(\theta)}(t,x)|}{\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)}}\leq\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)e^{-\frac{\left(\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)\right)^{\theta}-1}{\theta}}e^{w_{0}}\qquad\mbox{for {\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$}.

Using

\rho^{\theta}-1-\theta\ln\rho\geq 0\qquad\mbox{for any $\rho\geq 0$ and $\theta>0$},

(3.4)

we see that

\rho^{(\theta)}e^{-\frac{(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\theta}-1}{\theta}}=e^{-\frac{1}{\theta}((\rho^{(\theta)})^{\theta}-1-\theta\ln\rho^{(\theta)})}\leq 1
,
which leads to

\rho^{(\theta)}(t,x)e^{\frac{|m^{(\theta)}|}{\rho^{(\theta)}}}\leq e^{w_{0}}\qquad\mbox{for {\it a.e.} $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$},

which is uniformly bounded with respect to
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
.

We now show the uniform boundedness of the energy-energy flux pair
(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})(\rho,m)
.
From (
2.3
), for
\theta>0
, we have

(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})(\rho,m)=(\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+\rho\frac{\rho^{2\theta}-1}{2\theta(2\theta+1)},\,\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{3}}{\rho^{2}}+m(\frac{\rho^{2\theta}-1}{2\theta}+\frac{1}{2\theta+1})).

(3.5)

Based on estimates (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) and
formula (
3.5
),
it suffices to estimate
\frac{m^{(\theta)}}{(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{2}{3}}}
.
We first obtain from (
3.3
)–(
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{|m^{(\theta)}|}{(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{2}{3}}}\leq w_{0}(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{1}{3}}+(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{1-(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\theta}}{\theta}\leq w_{0}e^{\frac{w_{0}}{3}}-(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{1}{3}}\ln\rho^{(\theta)}\leq w_{0}e^{\frac{w_{0}}{3}}+3e^{-1}.

Then there exists a universal constant
C:=C(w_{0},\theta_{0})>0
independent of
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
such that

\displaystyle|m^{(\theta)}|+\frac{|m^{(\theta)}|^{2}}{\rho^{(\theta)}}\leq C(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq C,\qquad\big|m^{(\theta)}\frac{\rho^{2\theta}-1}{2\theta}\big|\leq C(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{(\rho^{(\theta)})^{\theta}-1}{\theta}\leq C.

Thus, the uniform boundedness of
(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*},q^{(\theta)}_{*})(\rho,m)
with respect to
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
follows.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Refined Kinetic Formulation for
\theta>0

In this section, we show that the total variation of the dissipation measures

As indicated in Theorem

(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})

Lemma 4.1

(Uniformly Bounded Total Variation of the Dissipation Measures)
.

Let the initial data
(\rho^{(\theta)}_{0},m^{(\theta)}_{0})(x)
satisfy (
2.18

Assume that the entropy solutions
(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})(t,x)

Then the total variation of the dissipation measures
D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)

\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]

For any compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

C(w_{0};K)>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|D^{(\theta)}(\cdot,\cdot;s)|(K)\,{\rm d}s\leq C(w_{0};K),

and
\mbox{\rm supp}\,D^{(\theta)}(t,x;\cdot)\subset[-w_{0},w_{0}]

Proof.

For any convex function
\psi(s)
,
i.e.
,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0

2.1

that the entropy solutions
(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})

\partial_{t}\eta^{(\theta)}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)};\psi)+\partial_{x}q^{(\theta)}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)};\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s\leq 0

in the sense of distributions, since

D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)\leq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

In (
4.2

\psi(s)=\psi_{*}(s):=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta(2\theta+1)}

\partial_{t}\eta^{(\theta)}_{*}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)};\psi)+\partial_{x}q^{(\theta)}_{*}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)};\psi)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)\,{\rm d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)|\,{\rm d}s

\hat{K}:=\mbox{\rm supp}\,\varphi

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|D^{(\theta)}(\cdot,\cdot;s)|(K)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle D^{(\theta)}(\cdot,\cdot;s),\phi\rangle\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{K}}\Big(\eta^{(\theta)}_{*}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})\partial_{t}\phi+q^{(\theta)}_{*}(\rho^{(\theta)},m^{(\theta)})\partial_{x}\phi\Big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(w_{0};K),

3.1

D^{(\theta)}(t,x;s)\leq 0

and equation (

\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
as measures, as indicated in (

Moreover, we know that

Since

\mbox{\rm supp}\,D^{(\theta)}(t,x;\cdot)\subset[-w_{0},w_{0}]

∎

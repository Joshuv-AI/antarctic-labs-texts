On the Sequences of ( q , k ) (q,k) -Generalized Fibonacci Numbers
Gersica Freitas, Alessandra Kreutz, Jean Lelis, Elaine Silva

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the new family of recurrence sequences of ( q , k ) (q,k) -generalized Fibonacci numbers. These sequences naturally extend the well-known sequences of k k -generalized Fibonacci numbers and generalized k k -order Pell numbers. We shall obtain a Binet-style formula and study the asymptotic behavior of dominant root of characteristic equation. Moreover, we shall prove some auxiliary results about these sequences. In particular, we characterize the first ( q , k ) (q,k) -generalized Fibonacci numbers in terms of binary sequences.

11B37, 11B39

1.
Introduction

Recurrence sequences are often the subject of study in the literature, for instance, the Fibonacci sequence and its generalizations have been widely studied due to
interesting results about their
Binet-style
formula and their asymptotic behavior.
The study of recurrence sequences has implications in many areas of research such as Diophantine equations, combinatorial problems and others.

The aim of this paper is to prove some properties associated with a new family of generalized recurrence sequences named
sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers
or simply
(q,k)
-bonacci numbers
which is given recurrently by

(1.1)

F_{q,n}^{(k)}=qF_{q,n-1}^{(k)}+F_{q,n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{q,n-k}^{(k)}\qquad\forall\quad n\geq 2

with the
k
initial conditions given by
F_{q,-(k-2)}^{(k)}=F_{q,-(k-3)}^{(k)}=\cdots=F_{q,0}^{(k)}=0
and
F_{q,1}^{(k)}=1
.

First of all, let us note that the family of the sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers extends well-known recurrence sequences.

For
q=1
and
k=2
we have the sequence of Fibonacci numbers
(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined recurrencely by
F_{n+1}=F_{n}+F_{n-1}
with initial conditions
F_{0}=0
and
F_{1}=1
. Such sequence have been studied and generalized by many authors, see
[
1
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
. Some of these generalizations have received greater prominence over the years. Among these, it is worth mentioning here that the usually called sequences of
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers or
k
-bonacci number occur here when
q=1
and
k\geq 2
, and satisfy the recurrence relation

(1.2)

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)},\ \forall\ n\geq 2,

with the
k
initial conditions given by
F_{-(k-2)}^{(k)}=F_{-(k-3)}^{(k)}=\cdots=F_{0}^{(k)}=0
and
F_{1}^{(k)}=1
.

Another generalization of Fibonacci numbers is the so-called sequence of
q
-Fibonacci numbers or
q
-weight Fibonacci numbers that appear for
q\geq 1
and
k=2
in (
1.1
). These sequences are defined by

(1.3)

F_{q,n}=qF_{q,n-1}+F_{q,n-2}\qquad\forall\quad n\geq 2

with initial conditions
F_{q,0}=0
and
F_{q,1}=1
(see
[
4
,
6
]
). Probably, the best known example of these sequences is the sequence of Pell numbers given by the above recurrence taking
q=2
.

In a recent paper, Kiliç and Taşci
[
8
]
studied the sequences of order-
k
Pell numbers given by

(1.4)

P^{(k)}_{n}=2P^{(k)}_{n-1}+P^{(k)}_{n-2}+\cdots+P^{(k)}_{n-k}\qquad\forall\quad n\geq 2

with
P_{-(k-2)}^{(k)}=P_{-(k-3)}^{(k)}=\cdots=P_{0}^{(k)}=0
and
P_{1}^{(k)}=1
.
Note that in (
1.1
) with
q=2
and
k\geq 3
, we have this
k
-Pell sequence.

In
[
3
]
, Bravo, Herrera and Ramírez presented some combinatorial interpretations for these
k
-Pell numbers while in
[
2
]
, Bravo, Herrera and Luca determined a Binet-style formula for these sequences and proved several results on the asymptotic behavior of the dominant root of their characteristic equation. Furthermore, as shown in
[
2
]
, the sequences of order-
k
Pell numbers have a good asymptotic behavior, as well as the sequences of
k
-generalized Fibonacci numbers.

Since the definition of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci sequence and basic properties about its behavior are the first step to prove new results about Diophantine equations involving these sequences, the main goal of this paper is to extend the main results on asymptotic behavior to the sequences defined in (
1.1
). So, we have the following main theorem that we shall prove in the section 3.

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
(F^{(k)}_{q,n})
be the sequence of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers with
k\geq 2
,
q\geq 3
and
n\geq 2-k
. Then

(a)

F_{q,n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{q,k}(\gamma_{i})\gamma_{i}^{n},

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
are roots of characteristic polynomial
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
, given by

\Phi_{q,k}(t)=t^{k}-qt^{k-1}-t^{k-2}-\cdots-t-1

and

g_{q,k}(x):=\frac{x-1}{(k+1)x^{2}-(q+1)kx+(q-1)(k-1)};

(b)

|F^{(k)}_{q,n}-g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n}|\leq\frac{1}{q},

where
\gamma
is the dominant root of
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
. Moreover,

\gamma^{n-2}<\gamma^{n-1}\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)<F_{q,n}^{(k)}<\gamma^{n-1}\left(\frac{q+2}{q}\right)<\gamma^{n},

for all
n\geq 1
.

We remark that the case
q=1
of this theorem was proved by Dresden and Du in
[
5
]
and the case
q=2
was proved by Bravo, Herrera and Luca in
[
2
]
. Therefore, in this paper we shall consider the case
q\geq 3
.

2.
Preliminary Results

For a detailed study of the sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers,
(F^{(k)}_{q,n}):=(F^{(k)}_{q,n})_{n\geq-(k-2)}
, we consider the characteristic polynomial defined by

(2.1)

\Phi_{q,k}(t)=t^{k}-qt^{k-1}-t^{k-2}-\cdots-t-1

and the auxiliary function

(2.2)

h_{q,k}(t)=(t-1)\Phi_{q,k}(t)=t^{k+1}-(q+1)t^{k}+(q-1)t^{k-1}+1.

Since (
F^{(k)}_{q,n})
is a linear recurrence of order
k
with characteristic polynomial given by (
2.1
) and this polynomial divides the auxiliary function (
2.2
), we deduce that
(F^{(k)}_{q,n})
is also a linear recurrence of order
k+1
with characteristic polynomial
h_{q,k}(t)
. Hence, we obtain our first identity involving the sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci number given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 3
be integer number. Then

(2.3)

F^{(k)}_{q,n}=(q+1)F^{(k)}_{q,n-1}-(q-1)F^{(k)}_{q,n-2}-F^{(k)}_{q,n-k-1}\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad n\geq 3.

Now, we can use Theorem
2
and the fact that, for
-(k-2)\leq n\leq 0
,
F^{(k)}_{q,n}=0
to obtain the first terms in the sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers. Let
q\geq 3
be a integer number and
(U_{q,n})_{n\geq 1}
the sequence given by recurrence

U_{q,n}=(q+1)U_{q,n-1}-(q-1)U_{q,n-2}

for all
n\geq 3
with
U_{q,1}=1
and
U_{q,2}=q
. Then we have that

U_{q,n}=\frac{((q-3)+\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5})\alpha^{n}_{q}+((3-q)+\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5})\beta^{n}_{q}}{2(q-1)\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5}}

for all integer number
n\geq 1
, where
\alpha_{q}
and
\beta_{q}
are the roots of

t^{2}-(q+1)t+(q-1)=0,

given by

\alpha_{q}=\frac{(q+1)+\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5}}{2},\ \beta_{q}=\frac{(q+1)-\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5}}{2}.

In what follows, it will be important to observe that
q<\alpha_{q}<q+1
and
0<\beta_{q}<1
, for all integer number
q\geq 3
. Moreover, let us consider the sequence given by recurrence

V_{q,n}=(q+1)V_{q,n-1}-(q-1)V_{q,n-2}

for all
n\geq 3
with
V_{q,1}=1
and
V_{q,2}=q+1
. So, it is not difficult to prove that
F^{(k)}_{q,n}\leq U_{q,n}
, more precisely, we have the following theorem which can be prove by induction

Theorem 3
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 3
be integer number. Then

(2.4)

F^{(k)}_{q,n}=U_{q,n}

for all
1\leq n\leq k+1
and

(2.5)

F^{(k)}_{q,n}=U_{q,n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-k-1}V_{q,j}F_{q,n-k-j}^{(k)}

for all
n\geq k+2
.

Another important fact about the sequence
(F_{q,n}^{(k)})_{n\geq 0}
is that its generating function

f_{q,k}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F^{(k)}_{q,n}x^{n},

is given by

(2.6)

f_{q,k}(x)=\frac{x}{1-qx-x^{2}-\ldots-x^{k}}.

In the next section, in order to prove the Main Theorem, we shall determine the Binet-style formulas and study the asymptotic behavior of dominant root of characteristic equation to the sequences of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci numbers.

3.
Proof of Main Theorem

3.1.
Binet-Style Formula.

Kalman in
[
7
]
proved that if
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a linear recurrence sequence of order
k\geq 2
satisfies the recurrence

u_{n+k}=c_{k-1}u_{n+k-1}+c_{k-2}u_{n+k-2}+\cdots+c_{1}u_{n+1}+c_{0}u_{n}

for all
n\geq 0
, with initial condition
u_{0}=u_{1}=\cdots=u_{k-2}=0
and
u_{k-1}=1
, where
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{k-1}
are constant. Then

u_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{n}}{P^{\prime}(\alpha_{i})}

where
P(t)=t^{k}-c_{k-1}t^{k-1}-\cdots-c_{1}t-c_{0}
is the characteristic polynomial of
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
are the roots of
P(t)
. Taking
u_{n}=F^{(k)}_{q,n-(k-2)}
for all
n\geq 0
, we have that
P(t)=\Phi_{q,k}(t)
and

F^{(k)}_{q,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\gamma_{i}^{n+(k-2)}}{\Phi^{\prime}_{q,k}(\gamma_{i})}

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
are the roots of
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
. Now, consider the auxiliary function (
2.2
) given by

h_{q,k}(t)=(t-1)\Phi_{q,k}(t)=t^{k+1}-(q+1)t^{k}+(q-1)t^{k-1}+1.

Using that

\Phi^{\prime}_{q,k}(t)=\frac{h^{\prime}_{q,k}(t)(t-1)-h_{q,k}(t)}{(t-1)^{2}}

and
h_{q,k}(\gamma_{i})=0
for all
0\leq i\leq k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{q,k}(\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)\gamma_{i}^{k}-(q+1)k\gamma_{i}^{k-1}+(q-1)(k-1)\gamma_{i}^{k-2}}{\gamma_{i}-1}.

Hence,

F_{q,n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{q,k}(\gamma_{i})\gamma_{i}^{n}

where

g_{q,k}(x):=\frac{x-1}{(k+1)x^{2}-(q+1)kx+(q-1)(k-1)},

and this proves the item (a) of Theorem
1
.

3.2.
Asymptotic Behavior

For integers numbers
k\geq 2
,
q\geq 3
and
n\geq 2-k
, we define
E^{(k)}_{q,n}
as the error of the approximation of the
n
th
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci number with the dominant term of the Binet-style formula of
F^{(k)}_{q,n}
, i.e.,

(3.1)

E^{(k)}_{q,n}=F^{(k)}_{q,n}-g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n},

for
\gamma
the dominant root of
\Phi_{q,k}
.

We remark that given a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{R}[x]
, the set of all possible linear recurrence sequences of real numbers having the characteristic equation
f(x)=0
is a vector space over real numbers. Since
F^{(k)}_{q,n}
and
(\gamma^{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfy the characteristic equation
\Phi_{q,k}(x)=0
, it follows from (
3.1
) that
E^{(k)}_{q,n}
satisfies the same recurrence relation as
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci sequence. That is, for all integer
k\geq 2
we have that

E_{q,n}^{(k)}=qE_{q,n-1}^{(k)}+E_{q,n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+E_{q,n-k}^{(k)}\qquad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 2,

moreover,

(3.2)

E_{q,n}^{(k)}=(q+1)E_{q,n-1}^{(k)}-(q-1)E_{q,n-2}^{(k)}-E_{q,n-k-1}^{(k)}.

Using the fact that
\lim_{n\to\infty}|\gamma_{i}|^{n}=0
for
2\leq i\leq k
and taking into account that

|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|\leq\sum_{j=2}^{k}|g_{q,k}(\gamma_{j})||\gamma_{j}|^{n},

we also deduce that

(3.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|=0.

Our aim is to estimate
|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|
. For this, we remark that Wu and Zang in
[
11
]
proved that for all integer numbers
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m}\geq 1
with
m\geq 2
the polynomial

f(x)=x^{m}-a_{1}x^{m-1}-a_{2}x^{m-2}-\cdots-a_{1}x-a_{m}

has exactly one positive real zero
\alpha
with
a_{1}<\alpha<a_{1}+1
and the others
m-1
zeros of
f(x)
lie within the unit circle in the complex plane. Thus, for all
q\geq 3
the characteristic polynomial
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
has a dominant root
q<\gamma<q+1
and the others roots are into the unit circle.

In what follows, fixed an integer number
q\geq 3
, we will indicate by
\gamma_{k}
the dominant root of
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
. In order to prove the item (b) of Theorem
1
, we shall introduce some auxiliary results about
\gamma_{k}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
q\geq 3
be an integer number fixed,
\gamma_{l}
and
\gamma_{k}
the dominant roots of
\Phi_{q,l}(t)
and
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
, respectively. Then

(i)

for
l>k
, we have that
\gamma_{l}>\gamma_{k}
;

(ii)

if
\alpha_{q}
is the dominant root of
t^{2}-(q+1)t+(q-1)=0
, then

\alpha_{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{k}}\right)<\gamma_{k}<\alpha_{q}.

Proof.

In order to prove the item (i), we proceed by contradiction. Let us assuming that
\gamma_{k}\geq\gamma_{l}
. Thus,
\gamma_{k}^{-i}\leq\gamma_{l}^{-i}
holds for all
i\geq 1
. Taking into account that
\Phi_{q,k}(\gamma_{q,k})=0
, we get

\gamma_{k}^{k}=q\gamma_{k}^{k-1}+\gamma_{k}^{k-2}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}+1,

and the same conclusion remains valid for
\gamma_{l}
. Since
k<l
we have that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{q}{\gamma_{k}}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{2}}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{k}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{q}{\gamma_{l}}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{l}^{2}}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{l}^{k}}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{l}^{k+1}}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{l}^{l}}=1

which is a contradiction. Thus, we conclude that
\gamma_{l}>\gamma_{k}
and this proves the item (i).

Let us consider the item (ii). Note that the auxiliary function given by (
2.2
) can be writing in the form

h_{q,k}(t)=t^{k-1}(t^{2}-(q+1)t+(q-1))+1,

moreover,
\alpha_{q}
is a root of
t^{2}-(q+1)t+(q-1)
. Thus,
h_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})=1
and

\Phi_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})=\frac{h_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})}{\alpha_{q}-1}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{q}-1}>0.

In the other hand, we have that
\alpha_{q}>q
and

\Phi_{q,k}(q)=-q^{k-2}-q^{k-3}-\cdots-q-1<0.

Since
\gamma_{k}
is the only root of
\Phi_{q,k}(x)
such that
\gamma_{k}>1
, we obtain that
q<\gamma_{k}<\alpha_{q}
.

By hypothesis,

\alpha_{q}^{2}-(q+1)\alpha_{q}+(q-1)=0,

and evaluating
h_{q,k}(t)
at
\gamma_{k}
, we have
h_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})=0
and then

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{2}-(q+1)\gamma_{k}+(q-1)=\frac{-1}{\gamma_{k}^{k-1}}.

Subtracting the two expressions above and rearranging some terms, we obtain

(\alpha_{q}-\gamma_{k})(\alpha_{q}+\gamma_{k}-(q+1))=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{k-1}}.

Since
\alpha_{q}>\gamma_{k}>q
and
(\alpha_{q}+\gamma_{k}-(q+1))>q/\alpha_{q}
, we get
\alpha_{q}-\gamma_{k}<\alpha_{q}q^{-k}
. Hence,

\gamma_{k}>\alpha_{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{k}}\right)

and this concludes the proof of (ii).
∎

Now, let us consider that
q\geq 3
and study the rational function
g_{q,k}
that appears in the Binet-style formula given by

g_{q,k}(x)=\frac{x-1}{(k+1)x^{2}-(q+1)kx+(q-1)(k-1)}.

Since
\alpha_{q}
is a root of
x^{2}-(q+1)x+(q-1)
, we get

g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})=\frac{\alpha_{q}-1}{\alpha_{q}^{2}-(q-1)}.

In particular, after some calculations, we have the estimate

(3.4)

\frac{1}{q+1}<g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})<\frac{1}{q},

where we use that
3\leq q<\alpha_{q}<q+1
. Moreover, we remark that
g_{q,k}
has vertical asymptote in

(3.5)

c_{q,k}:=\frac{(q+1)k+\sqrt{k^{2}(q^{2}-2q+5)+4(q-1)}}{2(k+1)}

and is positive and continuous in
(c_{q,k},\infty)
. Further,

g_{q,k}^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{(k+1)(x-1)^{2}+q+k-2}{[(k+1)x^{2}-(q+1)kx+(q-1)(k-1)]^{2}},

is negative in
(c_{q,k},\infty)
, so
g_{q,k}(x)
is decreasing in the same interval. Using this we can prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\gamma_{k}
be a dominant root of
\Phi_{q,k}(t)
,
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 3
integers. Then

\frac{1}{q+1}<g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})<\frac{1}{q}.

Proof.

In order to prove this lemma, we consider three cases. Firstly, we consider the case
k=2
. In this case we have that

g_{q,2}(x)=\frac{x-1}{3x^{2}-2(q+1)x+(q-1)}

and
\gamma_{2}
is the biggest root of
t^{2}-qt-1=0
given by
\gamma_{2}=(q+\sqrt{q^{2}+4})/2
. Since
q\geq 3
, we get

\frac{1}{q+1}<g_{q,2}(\gamma_{2})<\frac{1}{q}.

Now, let us consider the the case
3\leq k\leq q
. Here we use the value
c_{q,k}
defined in (
3.5
) and we remark that it can be rewritten as

c_{q,k}=\frac{(q+1)+\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5+4k^{-2}(q-1)}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

Thus, it is easily seen that
c_{q,k}\to\alpha_{q}
as
k\to\infty
. Since

\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5+4k^{-2}(q-1)}<\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5}+\frac{2\sqrt{q-1}}{k},

we get

(3.6)

c_{q,k}<\left(\alpha_{q}+\frac{\sqrt{q-1}}{k}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

Therefore, we obtain that

c_{q,k}<\alpha_{q}-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}<\alpha_{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{k}}\right)<\gamma_{k}<\alpha_{q},

where the last inequalities are given by Lemma
1
.

Denoting by
G_{q,k}
the value
g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q}-1/(q(q-1)))
, since
g_{q,k}(x)
is decreasing in
(c_{q,k},\infty)
and by (
3.4
), we have that

\frac{1}{q+1}<g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})<g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})<G_{q,k}.

Thus, after some calculations, we obtain that

\displaystyle G_{q,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}}{q\alpha_{q}+(\alpha_{q}-q)-q+2\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{q}(k+1)}{q(q-1)}\right)+\frac{k(q+1)}{q(q-1)}+\frac{k+1}{q^{2}(q-1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}}{q\left(\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\right)},

where we use that
q\geq k\geq 3
and
\alpha_{q}=(q+1+\sqrt{q^{2}-2q+5})/2
. Hence, we get
g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})<1/q
.

Finally, we consider the case
k\geq q+1
and observe that by (
3.6
) we have that

c_{q,k}<\alpha_{q}-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}.

Since
k\geq q+1\geq\alpha_{q}
and
q\geq 3
, we get
k(k+1)\alpha_{q}<q^{k}
. Moreover, using that
k^{2}(k+1)<3^{k}
for all
k\geq 4
, we obtain

c_{q,k}<\alpha_{q}-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}<\alpha_{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{k}}\right)<\gamma_{k}<\alpha_{q}.

Thus, we use again the
g_{q,k}(x)
is decreasing in
(c_{q,k},\infty)
and we get

g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q})<g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})<g_{q,k}\left(\alpha_{q}-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\right).

In particular, by (
3.4
),

\frac{1}{q+1}<g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k}).

In the other hand, let us denote the value
g_{q,k}(\alpha_{q}-1/(k(k+1)))
just by
\overline{G}_{q,k}
. Then, we obtain that

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{q,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}}{q\alpha_{q}+(\alpha_{q}-q)-q+2\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{q}}{k}\right)+\frac{k^{2}(q+1)+1}{k^{2}(k+1)}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}}{q\left(\alpha_{q}-1-\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\right)},

where we use that
k\geq q+1>\alpha_{q}>q\geq 3
. Therefore, we obtain that
g_{q,k}(\gamma_{k})<1/q
and this completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In order to prove the item (b) of Theorem
1
which state that if
q\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
, then

|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|\leq\frac{1}{q}

for all
n\geq 2-k
. Note that, by initial conditions of
(F_{q,n}^{(k)})
, we have
F^{(k)}_{q,n}=0
for all
2-k\leq n\leq 0
. Hence

E_{q,n}^{k}=-g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n}

for all
2-k\leq n\leq 0
. Now let us suppose
n=0
, in this case, by Lemma
2
, we get

|E^{(k)}_{q,0}|=g_{q,k}(\gamma)<1/q.

Moreover, if
2-k\leq n\leq-1
, then
\gamma^{n}\leq\gamma^{-1}<1
and again we obtain that

g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n}\leq g_{q,k}(\gamma)<1/q

for all
k\geq 2
.

By Lemma
2
, we have that
\gamma/(q+1)<g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma<\gamma/q
. Using that
q<\gamma<q+1
, we get
1-1/(q+1)<g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma<1+1/q
. Thus, if
n=1
, then
F_{q,1}^{(k)}=1
and

-\frac{1}{q}<1-g_{q,k}(\gamma)\gamma<\frac{1}{q+1}.

Hence, we obtain that
|E_{q,1}^{k}|\leq 1/q
.

Now, for the sake of contradiction, assume that
|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|>1/q
for some integer
n\geq 2
. Let
n_{0}
be the smallest positive integer with this property. Since
|E_{q,n_{0}-1}^{(k)}|\leq 1/q
and
|E_{q,n_{0}-k}|\leq 1/q
we get

|(q-1)E_{q,n_{0}-1}^{(k)}+E_{q,n_{0}-k}|\leq 1.

According to (
3.2
)

E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}=(q+1)E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}-((q-1)E_{q,n_{0}-1}^{(k)}+E_{q,n_{0}-k}^{(k)})

and so

|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|\geq(q+1)|E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}|-|(q-1)E_{q,n_{0}-1}^{(k)}+E_{q,n_{0}-k}^{(k)}|.

Hence

|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|-|E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}|\geq q|E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}|-|(q-1)E_{q,n_{0}-1}^{(k)}+E_{q,n_{0}-k}^{(k)}|>0

giving

|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|>|E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}|.

Since
n_{0}-k+1<n_{0}
, we infer that
|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}-k+1}|\leq\frac{1}{q}<|E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}|<|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|
and therefore
|(q-1)E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}+E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}-k+1}|<q|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|
. Thus,

|E_{q,n_{0}+2}^{(k)}|\geq(q+1)|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|-|(q-1)E_{q,n_{0}}^{(k)}+E_{q,n_{0}-k+1}^{(k)}|

and we obtain that
|E_{q,n_{0}+2}^{(k)}|>|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|
.

Thus, suppose that
|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}}|<|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+1}|<\cdots<|E^{(k)}_{n_{0}+i-1}|
for some integer

. We have two cases according to whether
n_{0}+i-k-1<n_{0}
or
n_{0}\leq n_{0}+i-k-1
. First, if
n_{0}+i-k-1<n_{0}
, then we get

|E_{q,n_{0}+i-k-1}^{(k)}|\leq\frac{1}{q}<|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}}|<|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|<\cdots<|E^{(k)}_{n_{0}+i-1}|.

In the other hand, if
n_{0}\leq n_{0}+i-k-1
, then we obtain that

|E_{q,n_{0}+i-k-1}^{(k)}|<|E_{q,n_{0}+i-1}^{(k)}|.

In any case, we conclude that
|E_{q,n_{0}+i-k-1}^{(k)}|<|E_{q,n_{0}+i-1}^{(k)}|
. For this reason

|(q-1)E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+i-2}+E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+i-k-1}|<q|E_{q,n_{0}+i-1}^{(k)}|.

Using (
3.2
) again, we get

\displaystyle|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+i}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(q+1)|E^{(k)}_{n_{0}+i-1}|-|(q-1)E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+i-2}+E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}+i-k-1}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle|E_{q,n_{0}+i-1}^{(k)}|.

Therefore,
|E^{(k)}_{q,n_{0}}|<|E_{q,n_{0}+1}^{(k)}|<\cdots<|E^{(k)}_{n_{0}+i-1}|<|E^{(k)}_{n_{0}+i}|
contradicting (

) which says that the error must eventually go to

. Hence, we conclude that
|E_{q,n}^{(k)}|<1/q
for all integer

.

Now, let us consider the exponential growth of
(q,k)
-generalized Fibonacci sequence. Note that, by the first part of item (b) of Theorem
1
, if

Hence, if

for all

.

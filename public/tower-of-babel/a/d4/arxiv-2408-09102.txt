Higher Chow cycles on K ​ 3 K3 surfaces attached to plane quartics
Ken Sato

Abstract
. In this paper, we give an explicit construction of higher Chow cycles of type ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) on K ​ 3 K3 surfaces obtained as quadruple coverings of the projective plane ramified along smooth quartics. The construction uses a pair of bitangents of the quartics. We prove that the higher Chow cycles generate a rank 2 subgroup in the indecomposable part of the higher Chow group for very general members, by using a specialization argument and an explicit computation of the regulator map.



1.
Introduction

Higher Chow groups
{\mathrm{CH}}^{p}(X,q)
of smooth varieties
X
introduced by Bloch are a generalization of the classical Chow groups and are related to many important invariants in algebraic geometry,
K
-theory, and number theory.
However, their explicit structures are still mysterious for almost all varieties when the codimension
p
is greater than 1.
In this paper, we are interested in the first non-classical case
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
for
K3
surfaces
X
.
The
decomposable part
of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
is the subgroup generated by cycles obtained as intersection products of lower codimensional cycles.
Since this is an easily accessible part, we are interested in the quotient of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
by the decomposable part.
This quotient is called the
indecomposable part
and is denoted by
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.

Explicit constructions of non-trivial elements in
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
have been done for some lattice-polarized families of
K3
surfaces (see the papers listed in the introduction of
[
MS23
]
).
In
[
MS23
]
, we construct families of higher Chow cycles on families of
K3
surfaces
X
with non-symplectic involutions
\iota
in a systematic way.
The presence of involutions plays crucial roles in the construction of cycles, and also in the degeneration argument there.

In this paper, we consider the case of non-symplectic automorphisms of order
4
.
The most famous example, discovered by Kondō (
[
Kon00
]
), is the the degree 4 covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
ramified along smooth quartics.
They are naturally parametrized by the moduli space of smooth plane quartics.
After a suitable base change, we have a family
({\mathcal{X}},\sigma)\rightarrow S
of
K3
surfaces with non-symplectic automorphisms of order 4.
We explicitly construct a family of higher Chow cycles
\xi=\{\xi_{s}\}_{s\in S}
on this family.
Our construction uses a pair
(l_{0},l_{1})
of bitangents of the plane quartics.
On the
K3
surfaces
X
associated with the plane quartics, the inverse images of
l_{0},l_{1}
produce two rational curves intersecting at 2 points.
This configuration of rational curves enables us to construct a higher Chow cycle on
X
.
The main theorem of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
4.3
)
For a very general
1
1

1

In this paper, a very general point means that a point outside a union of countably many certain proper analytic subsets.

s\in S
, the images of

and

In particular,
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}({\mathcal{X}}_{s},1)_{\mathrm{ind}}

All previous constructions of higher Chow cycles on
K3
surfaces have been done for lattice-polarized families of
K3

On the other hand, our family of

a lattice-polarized family, and is parametrized by a quotient of a complex ball by a unitary group, as proved by Kondō (

We give the first example of indecomposable higher Chow cycles on a non-lattice-polarized family of

surfaces.

The proof is divided into two parts; a specialization argument for a variant of normal functions and an explicit calculation on the normal function associated with our cycles.

We define the Jacobian

for a
K3

.
Then we have the corresponding Abel-Jacobi map

The advantages of considering

For each member

In Section 2, we recall basic results on higher Chow cycles and normal functions.
In Section 3, we define the Jacobian

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Higher Chow cycles

For a smooth variety
X
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, let
{\mathrm{CH}}^{p}(X,q)
be the higher Chow group defined by Bloch (
[
Blo86
]
).
In this paper, we consider the case
(p,q)=(2,1)
.
An element of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
is called a
(2,1)
-cycle
.
The higher Chow group
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
is isomorphic to the middle homology group of the following complex. For the proof, see, e.g.,
[
Mül98
]
Corollary 5.3.

K_{2}^{\mathrm{M}}({\mathbb{C}}(X))\xrightarrow{T}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}{\mathbb{C}}(C)^{\times}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{div}}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in X^{(2)}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot p

Here
X^{(r)}
denotes the set of all irreducible closed subsets of
X
of codimension
r
.
The map
T
denotes the tame symbol map from the Milnor
K_{2}
-group of the function field
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
.
Therefore, each
(2,1)
-cycle is represented by a formal sum

\sum_{j}(C_{j},f_{j})\in\displaystyle\bigoplus_{C\in X^{(1)}}{\mathbb{C}}(C)^{\times}

(4)

where
C_{j}
are prime divisors on
X
and
f_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}(C_{j})^{\times}
are non-zero rational functions on them such that
\sum_{j}{\mathrm{div}}_{C_{j}}(f_{j})=0
as codimension 2 cycles on
X
.

The intersection product on higher Chow groups induces the following map.

\mathrm{Pic}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})={\mathrm{CH}}^{1}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathrm{CH}}^{1}(X,1)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)

(5)

The image of this map is called the
decomposable part
of
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)
and is denoted by
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
.
A
decomposable cycle
is an element of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
.
The quotient
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)/{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}}
is called the
indecomposable part
of
{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)
and is denoted by
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.
For a
(2,1)
-cycle
\xi
,
\xi_{\mathrm{ind}}
denotes its image in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{ind}}
.

Let
C
be a prime divisor on
X
,
\alpha\in\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\times})
and
[C]\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
be the class corresponding to
C
.
The image of
[C]\otimes\alpha
under (
5
) is represented by
(C,\alpha|_{C})
in the presentation (
4
).

2.2.
The regulator map

For a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Hodge structure
H=(H_{\mathbb{Z}},F^{\bullet})
of weight 2, the generalized intermediate Jacobian of
H
is defined as the quotient group

J(H)=\frac{H_{\mathbb{C}}}{H_{\mathbb{Z}}+F^{2}H_{\mathbb{C}}}.

(6)

This is a generalized complex torus, i.e., a quotient of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear space by a discrete lattice.
Let
X
be a
K3
surface.
We write
J(X)=J(H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}))
and simply call it the
Jacobian
of
X
.
The following
Beilinson regulator map
plays a role of the Abel-Jacobi map in the study of
(2,1)
-cycles (cf.
[
KLM06
]
).

\nu:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X,1)\longrightarrow J(X)

(7)

We recall a formula for the regulator map following
[
Lev88
]
pp. 458–459.
By the intersection product, the Jacobian of
X
is isomorphic to

J(X)\simeq\frac{(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}

(8)

where
(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}
is the dual
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear space of
F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
and we regard
H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
as a subgroup of
(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}
by integration.

Let
\xi
be a
(2,1)
-cycle represented by (
4
).
Let
D_{j}
be the normalization of
C_{j}
and
\mu_{j}:D_{j}\rightarrow X
be the composition of
D_{j}\rightarrow C_{j}\hookrightarrow X
.
We will define a topological 1-chain
\gamma_{j}
on
D_{j}
.
If
f_{j}
is constant, we define
\gamma_{j}=0
.
If
f_{j}
is not constant, we regard

as a finite morphism from
D_{j}
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Then we define
\gamma_{j}
as the pull-back of
[\infty,0]
by
f_{j}
, where

is a path on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
from
\infty
to

along the positive real axis.
By the condition
\sum_{j}{\mathrm{div}}_{C_{j}}(f_{j})=0
,
\gamma=\sum_{j}(\mu_{j})_{*}\gamma_{j}
is a topological 1-cycle on
X
.
Since
H_{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})=0
, there exists a 2-chain
\Gamma
on
X
such that
\partial\Gamma=\gamma
.
In this paper,
\Gamma
is referred to as a

\xi
.
Then the image of
\xi
under the regulator map is represented by the following element in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\ni\text{[}\omega\text{]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\omega+\sum_{j}\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\displaystyle\int_{D_{j}-\gamma_{j}}\log(f_{j})\mu_{j}^{*}\omega}

(9)

where

is the pull-back of a logarithmic function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}-[\infty,0]

.

From the formula, for a prime divisor

(2,1)
-cycle represented by

In particular, we have

\nu({\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)_{\mathrm{dec}})={\mathrm{NS}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\quad(\subset J(X)).

2.3.
Normal functions

We consider the regulator map in the relative setting.
Let
S
be a smooth variety and
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}},F^{\bullet})
be a variation of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Hodge structure of weight 2 over

.
Then

J(\mathcal{H})=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}+F^{2}\mathcal{H}}

is a family of generalized complex tori over

.
Let
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
be a smooth family of
K3
surfaces over
S
.
Let
{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{X}})\rightarrow S
be the family of Jacobians attached to the variation of Hodge structure

.
The fiber of
{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{X}})\rightarrow S
over

Suppose that we have irreducible divisors
{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
on
{\mathcal{X}}
which are smooth over
S
and non-zero rational functions

on
{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
whose zeros and poles are also smooth over
S
.
Assume that they satisfy the condition
\sum_{j}{\mathrm{div}}_{({\mathcal{C}}_{j})_{s}}((f_{j})_{s})=0
on each fiber
{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
.
Then we have a family of
(2,1)
-cycles
\xi=\{\xi_{s}\}_{s\in S}
such that
\xi_{s}\in{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}({\mathcal{X}}_{s},1)
is represented by the formal sum
\sum_{j}(({\mathcal{C}}_{j})_{s},(f_{j})_{s})
.
In this paper, such a family of
(2,1)
-cycles is called an
algebraic family of
(2,1)
-cycles
.
Then the section
S\rightarrow{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{X}});s\mapsto\nu(\xi_{s})
is holomorphic and satisfies the horizontality condition, namely it is a normal function (
[

]

We denote this section by
\nu(\xi)
and call it the
normal function associated with
\xi
.

2.4.
The transcendental regulator map

In Section 5, we compute images of higher Chow cycles under the following variant of the regulator map.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{CH}}^{2}(X,1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(X)\simeq\dfrac{(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dfrac{(H^{2,0}(X))^{\vee}}{H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}}

(11)

where the last projection is induced by

.
This map is called the
transcendental regulator map
.

Let
\omega
be a non-zero holomorphic 2-form on
X
.
Considering the paring with
[\omega]\in H^{2,0}(X)
, we have an isomorphism between the target of (
11
) and the abelian group
{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)
, where
{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)
is the set of periods of

with respect to
\omega

{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)=\left\{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}:\Gamma\text{ is a toplogical 2-cycle on }X.\right\}.

By the formula (

\Phi(\xi)([\omega])\equiv\int_{\Gamma}\omega\mod{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)

(12)

where
\Gamma
is a 2-chain associated with
\xi
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
be a family of
K3
surfaces and
\omega
be a nowhere vanishing relative 2-form.
For an open subset
U
of
S
in the classical topology, a period function with respect to
\omega
on
U
is a holomorphic function on
U
given by
U\ni s\mapsto\int_{\Gamma_{s}}\omega_{s}
, where
\{\Gamma_{s}\}_{s\in U}
is a
C^{\infty}
-family of 2-cycles on
{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
.
The period functions with respect to
\omega
generate (as sheaves of abelian groups) the subsheaf
{\mathcal{P}}_{\omega}
of the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{\mathrm{an}}
of holomorphic functions.
Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\omega}
be the quotient
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{\mathrm{an}}/{\mathcal{P}}_{\omega}
.
For each
s\in S
, the evaluation map
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}};f\mapsto f(s)

{\mathrm{ev}}_{s}:\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\omega})\>{\longrightarrow}\>{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathcal{P}}(\omega).

For an algebraic family
\xi=\{\xi_{s}\}_{s\in S}
of
(2,1)

Thus we have the section

We use the following elementary property of sections of

.
For the proof, see, e.g.,
[

]
, Lemma 2.4.

Lemma 2.1
.

If

is non-zero,

is non-zero for a very general

.
In particular, if

\Phi(\xi_{s})\neq 0

3.
K3

In Section 3.1, we define a variant of the Jacobian for a pair

as above.
In Section 3.2, we recall the Kondō’s family (

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field,
m
be a positive integer and
V_{1}
,…,
V_{m}
be vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}
. We say that tensor rank
\rank(v)
of
v\in V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{m}
is the minimal number such that
v
can be expressed as a sum of
n
product tensors, i.e., tensors of the form
v_{1}\otimes v_{2},\ldots\otimes v_{m}
. Also for a collection of vectors
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}

k
-rank is defined as the largest number
k
such that for any
k
vectors
u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{k}}
we have
\dim\spann(u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{k}})=k
.

In 1977 J. Kruskal proved
[
5
]
that a tensor of a form

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}

constitutes a unique representation as a sum of
n
product tensors if
k
-ranks of the sets
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
,
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
and
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
are large enough. Proof of this theorem in more general form can be found in
[
8
]
. There are some other conditions that imply uniqueness of the tensor rank decompositions in the literature. In this paper we focus on so-called U-condition which was originally introduces in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
; we will use the definition from
[
7
]
.

Denote
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
;
[n..m]=\{n,\ldots,m\}
.
By
\langle A\rangle
denote the linear span of a set

A
in a linear space. We also write
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\rangle
meaning

\langle\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\rangle
. For a sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
let
\omega(\alpha)
be the number of indices
i
for which
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. We say that a set of tensors {
x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}
},
i\in[n]
satisfies the
Condition U
if for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{n}

(1)

\displaystyle\rank\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)\geqslant\min(\omega(\alpha),n-d+2),

where
d
is the dimension of
\langle x_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle
. The aim of this paper is Question 38 from
[
7
]
, we prove the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field;
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
be
linear spaces over
\mathbb{F}
;
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
;

y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{Y}
;
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Assume that

•

x_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
and also
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are not parallel for all
i\neq j
;

•

the set of tensors
{
x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}
}
,
i\in[n]
satisfies the Condition U.

Then for
\theta:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}
we have
\rank\theta=n

and the representation of
\theta
as a sum of
n
product tensors is unique.

Remark 2
.

The theorem holds true if we demand (
1
) not for all
\alpha\in F^{n}
, but only for
\alpha
such that
\alpha=(\eta(x_{1}),\ldots,\eta(x_{n})
for some linear functional
\eta\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
. In other words, following the notations from
[
7
]
, the theorem holds with Condition U replaced by condition W.

2.
Proof of the Theorem
1

We will use the following

Lemma 3

(Lemma 12.5.3.5,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
W
be a
d
-dimensional linear space over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and
\{x_{i}\}
,
\{v_{i}\}
,
i\in[n]
be two sets of vectors such that

•

\langle v_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle=W
;

•

x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are not parallel for any two distinct indices
i,j
;

•

for any hyperplane
H

\displaystyle\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant d-1\Longrightarrow\#(H\cap\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}).

Then there exists a permutation
\pi
of indices
[n]
and the non-zero scalars
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
v_{i}=\lambda_{i}x_{\pi_{i}}
for all
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

Let us outline the idea of the proof. Proceeding by induction one has to prove

•

for any
m
- dimensional subspace
H_{m}

\displaystyle\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant m\Longrightarrow\#(H\cap\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}).

Notice that this assertion for
m=d-1
is given in the lemma and for
m=1
this immediately implies the lemma. For details see Section 12.5.4,
[
6
]
.
∎

Proof of the Theorem
1
.

Assume that
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes u_{i}\otimes w_{i}
for certain
v_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
,
u_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}
,

w_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}
. We need to show that the multisets
\{x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}|i\in[n]\}
and

\{v_{i}\otimes u_{i}\otimes w_{i}|i\in[n]\}
coincide. We do it in two steps. At the first step, we
apply Lemma
3
to prove that

(\heartsuit)
there exists a permutation
\pi
of indices
[n]
and the non-zero
scalars
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
v_{i}=\lambda_{i}x_{\pi_{i}}
for all
i\in[n]
.

In order to prove
(\heartsuit)
, take an arbitrary linear functional
\eta\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
.
We get

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta(x_{i})y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta(v_{i})u_{i}\otimes w_{i}=:\Theta(\eta).

Notice that for
\eta\in\langle v_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle^{\perp}
one has
\Theta(\eta)=0
and the Condition U implies that
\eta(x_{i})=0
for every
i\in[n]
. Therefore,

\langle x_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle\subset\langle v_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle=:W

and without loss of generality we can now assume
\mathcal{X}=W
.

For a hyperplane
H
denote the orthogonal projection onto
H^{\perp}
by
\eta_{H}
. Consider hyperplane
H
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant\dim W-1.

To apply Lemma 3 we need to show that

\displaystyle\#(H\cap\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[n]})\geqslant\#(H\cap\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}),

or equivalently

\displaystyle\#\{i|\eta_{H}(x_{i})=0\}\geqslant\#\{i|\eta_{H}(v_{i})=0\}

Denote the two latter sets by
I
and
J
respectively
then

\rank\Theta(\eta_{H})\leqslant n-|J|\leqslant n-\dim W+1\leqslant n-d+1,

where
d
is the dimension of
\langle x_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle
. On the other hand, from the condition U we kwow

\displaystyle\rank\Theta(\eta_{H})\geqslant\min(|\{i:\eta_{H}(x_{i})\neq 0\}|,n-d+2).

If the latter minimum equals
n-d+2
, this
is a contradiction. Thus

n-|J|\geqslant\rank\Theta(\eta_{H})\geqslant|\{i:\eta_{H}(x_{i})\neq 0\}|=n-|I|.

Therefore
|I|\geqslant|J|
for any hyperplane
H
satisfying (
3
) and we get
(\heartsuit)
by Lemma
3
.

Now, permuting indices and using
(\lambda x)\otimes u\otimes v=x\otimes(\lambda u)\otimes v

we may suppose that
v_{i}=x_{i}
for all
i
. Without loss of generality we may suppose that

x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a basis of
\langle x_{i}|i\in[n]\rangle
. Denote
x_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}x_{j}

for
i\in[d+1..n]
. We get

T_{j}:=y_{j}\otimes z_{j}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=u_{j}\otimes w_{j}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}u_{i}\otimes w_{i},\quad j=1,\ldots,d.

By Condition U, we get

\displaystyle\rank T_{j}=1+|\{i:\alpha_{ij}\neq 0\}|.

Denote

\displaystyle J_{j}=\{j\}\cup\{i:\alpha_{ij}\neq 0\}.

By the above maximal rank condition we get that the vectors
\{y_{i}:i\in J_{j}\}
are linearly independent. Analogously, so are the vectors

\{z_{i}:i\in J_{j}\}
,
\{u_{i}:i\in J_{j}\}
,
\{v_{i}:i\in J_{j}\}
. Next, assume that

k\in J_{j}
but
y_{k}\notin\langle u_{i}:i\in J_{j}\rangle
. Then there exists a projector
P\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\langle u_{i}:i\in J_{j}\rangle

for which
Py_{k}=0
. We get
(P\otimes Id)T_{j}=T_{j}
since
Pu_{i}=u_{i}
for all
i\in J_{j}
, but

\displaystyle(P\otimes Id)T_{j}=(Py_{j})\otimes z_{j}+\sum_{i>d}\alpha_{ij}(Py_{i})\otimes z_{i}

is a representation of

T_{j}
as a sum of less than
|J_{j}|
product tensors. Thus all
y_{k}
with
k\in J_{j}
belong to
the subspace
\langle u_{i}:i\in J_{j}\rangle=:\mathcal{Y}_{j}
. Since
|J_{j}|\leqslant n-d+1
, and any
n-d+1
(and even any
n-d+2
)
y_{j}
’s
are linearly independent by Condition U, we get
\mathcal{Y}_{j}=\langle y_{i}:i\in J_{j}\rangle
. Next,
\dim(\mathcal{Y}_{j}+\mathcal{Y}_{k})=|J_{j}\cup J_{k}|

whenever
j,k\in[d]
and
j\neq k
, that’s because
|J_{j}\cup J_{k}|\leqslant n-d+2
and corresponding
y_{i}
’s with
i\in J_{j}\cup J_{k}

are linearly independent. But
\mathcal{Y}_{j}+\mathcal{Y}_{k}
is also generated by
u_{i}
’s with
i\in J_{j}\cup J_{k}
. Thus, these
u_{i}
’s are also linearly independent.

Let
s\in[d+1..n]
. Since no two
x_{i}
’s are parallel, there exist at least two indices
j,k\in[d]

for which
\alpha_{sj}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{sk}\neq 0
. In other words,
s\in J_{j}\cap J_{k}
. Then,
y_{s}
belongs
to
\mathcal{Y}_{j}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{k}
. This space is generated by
u_{i}
with
i\in J_{j}\cap J_{k}\subset[d+1..n]
. So, we have just
proved that

\displaystyle y_{s}=\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}f_{si}u_{i}

for certain scalars
f_{si}
. Now fix
s,k\in[d+1..n]

such that
s\neq k
. Since
x_{s}
and
x_{k}
are not parallel, we may without loss of generality assume that
\alpha_{s1}\alpha_{k2}\neq\alpha_{k1}\alpha_{s2}
.
Consider the tensor
\alpha_{k2}T_{1}-\alpha_{k1}T_{2}
. It equals

py_{1}\otimes z_{1}+qy_{2}\otimes z_{2}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}r_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=pu_{1}\otimes w_{1}+qu_{2}\otimes w_{2}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}r_{i}u_{i}\otimes w_{i}

for certain coefficients
p,q,r_{d+1},\ldots,r_{n}
, where
r_{i}=\alpha_{k2}\alpha_{i1}-\alpha_{k1}\alpha_{i2}
. In particular,
r_{k}=0
but

r_{s}\neq 0
. As above, we conclude that
y_{s}
belongs to a span of
u_{1},u_{2}
and
\{u_{i}:r_{i}\neq 0\}
. Note that
U:=\{u_{1},u_{2}
,
u_{d+1},\ldots,u_{n}\}

are linearly independent, since
y_{1},y_{2},y_{d+1},\ldots,y_{n}
lie in their span (and any
n-d+2
vectors
y_{i}
’s are linearly independent).
Therefore, the expansion of
y_{s}
in the system
U
does not contain
u_{k}
. This yields
f_{sk}=0
.

So, we proved that
u_{i}
’s are parallel to
y_{i}
’s for
i>d
. Analogously,
w_{i}
are parallel to
z_{i}
for
i>d
, and we get
u_{i}\otimes w_{i}=\lambda_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}

for
i>d
. Fix
i>d
and find
j\in[d]
for which
\alpha_{ij}\neq 0
. Looking at
T_{j}
we get

y_{j}\otimes z_{j}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i})\alpha_{ij}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=u_{j}\otimes w_{j}.

If there are at least two non-zero coefficients in the left hand side, this contradicts to condition (ii). Therefore
\lambda_{i}=1
. This holds for all
i=d+1,\ldots,n
. So,
u_{i}\otimes w_{i}=y_{i}\otimes z_{i}

for all
i>d
. Looking at two expressions for
T_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,d
) we get the same for
j\leqslant d
.
∎

3.
Field dependence of the Condition U

It is well known that tensor rank is field dependent (see section 3.1 in
[
4
]
, or section 2 in
[
1
]
). Here we show that U-condition is also field dependent.

Example.

Consider three tensors over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
:

\displaystyle a\otimes a,\quad b\otimes b,\quad(a+b)\otimes(a+b).

Linear combinations over the field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
are just sums of subsets, and one can check that

(4)

\displaystyle\rank(\alpha_{1}a\otimes a+\alpha_{2}b\otimes b+\alpha_{3}(a+b)\otimes(a+b))=\min(\omega(\alpha),2).

On the other hand, we can consider the same set of tensors over
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\xi]
, where
\xi^{2}=\xi+1
. We have

\displaystyle(1+\xi)a\otimes a+b\otimes b+\xi(a+b)\otimes(a+b)=

\displaystyle=a\otimes a+(1+\xi)b\otimes b+\xi a\otimes b+\xi b\otimes a=

(5)

\displaystyle=(a+\xi b)\otimes(a+\xi b).

Now consider 3 following 3-dimensional tensors:

(6)

\displaystyle a\otimes a\otimes a,\quad b\otimes b\otimes b,\quad c\otimes(a+b)\otimes(a+b).

We have
n=d=3
. Hence (
4
) and (
5
) imply that condition U holds over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and does not hold over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Also from Theorem
1
and the statement below it follows that the uniqueness of tensor decomposition of sum of tensors in (
6
) holds over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and does not hold over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

Statement 4
.

If
n=d
, then the Condition U is not only sufficient for uniqueness of the tensor rank decomposition, but also a necessary one.

Proof.

Indeed, for
n=d
Condition U takes the form

\displaystyle\rank\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)\geqslant\min(\omega(\alpha),2).

Assume that Condition U does not hold. Then there exists
\alpha
such that
\omega(\alpha)\geqslant 2
and

\displaystyle\rank\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)\leqslant 1,

equivalently

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=y\otimes z

for some vectors
y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
z\in Z
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle y_{1}\otimes z_{1}=\alpha_{1}^{-1}\left(y\otimes z-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=\alpha_{1}^{-1}x_{1}\otimes\left(y\otimes z-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}\right)+\sum_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i}=

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{-1}x_{1}\otimes y\otimes z+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(x_{i}-\alpha_{1}^{-1}\alpha_{i}x_{1})\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{i},

therefore the uniqueness does not hold.
∎

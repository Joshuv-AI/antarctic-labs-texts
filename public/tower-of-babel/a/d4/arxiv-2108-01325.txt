The signless Laplacian state transfer in 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graph
Xiao-Qin Zhang, Shu-Yu Cui, Gui-Xian Tian

Abstract
The 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graph of a graph G G , denoted by 𝒬 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{Q}(G) , is the graph derived from G G by plugging a new vertex to each edge of G G and adding a new edge between two new vertices which lie on adjacent edges of G G . In this paper, we consider to study the existence of the signless Laplacian perfect state transfer and signless Laplacian pretty good state transfer in 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graphs of graphs. We show that, if all the signless Laplacian eigenvalues of a regular graph G G are integers, then the 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graph of G G has no signless Laplacian perfect state transfer. We also give a sufficient condition that the 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graph of a regular graph has signless Laplacian pretty good state transfer when G G has signless Laplacian perfect state transfer between two specific vertices. AMS classification: 05C50 15A18 81P68 Keywords: Quantum walk; signless Laplacian matrix; spectrum; 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} -graph; state transfer

Introduction

In 1998, the concept of continuous-time quantum walk on graphs was proposed firstly by Farhi and Gutman in
[
15
]
. Suppose that
M
is a symmetric matrix associated with a connected graph
G
, such as adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix, signless Laplacian matrix of
G
and so on. Then the
transition matrix
of continuous-time quantum walk on
G
is defined by the unitary matrix

H_{M}(t)=\text{exp}(-itM),

(1)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
i^{2}=-1
.
Bose
[
5
]
in
2003
studied the problem of information transition in a quantum spin system. Subsequently,
Christandl et al.
[
7
]
proved that this problem can be reduced to the perfect state transfer in quantum walk. Let
e_{u}^{n}
denote the characteristic vector of order
n
corresponding to the vertex
u
of
G
. In the absence of confusion,
e_{u}^{n}
is abbreviated as
e_{u}
. For two vertices
u
and
v
of
G
, if

\text{exp}(-itM)e_{u}=\lambda e_{v}

(2)

with
|\lambda|=1
, then
G
is said to have
perfect state transfer
relative to matrix
M
between vertices
u
and
v
at time
t
. This concept has played a crucial role in quantum information and quantum algorithm. Characterizing graphs having perfect state transfer has been enjoyed world-wide attention among physic and mathematics communities. However, it is well known that graphs having perfect state transfer are rare. Godsil in
[
17
]
proposed a new concept called pretty good state transfer, whose condition is more relaxing in comparison to perfect state transfer. A graph
G
is said to have
pretty good state transfer
between vertices
u
and
v
at some time
t
if

|\text{exp}(-itM)_{uv}|>1-\epsilon,

(3)

for any
\epsilon>0
.

Many papers have focused mostly on quantum walk relative to adjacency matrix and Laplacian matrix and obtained some excellent results. For example, in the adjacency matrix case, Godsil in
[
18
]
proved that, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exist at most finitely graphs with maximum degree
k
admitting perfect state transfer. Bose et al. in
[
6
]
showed the complete graph
K_{n}
has no perfect state transfer, but
K_{n}
with a missing edge has perfect state transfer between the two vertices of this missing edge for positive integer
n
. Ge et al. in
[
16
]
described some new constructions of perfect state transfer graphs using variants of the double cones and graph products, such as weak and lexicographic products, irregular and glued double cones and so on. Coutinho and Godsil in
[
9
]
also constructed many new graphs of perfect state transfer using graph products and double covers of graphs. Ackelsberg et al. in
[
2
]
showed that the corona of two graphs has no perfect state transfer under suitable conditions, but it has pretty good state transfer in some special cases. For more details on this area, we refer the reader to
[
8
,
17
]
and the cited references therein.

In the Laplacian matrix case, Alvir et al in
[
3
]
studied the
perfect state transfer in Laplacian quantum walks. It was indicated that quantum walks based on the adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix and signless Laplacian matrix are all equivalent for a regular graph. They also investigated the Laplacian perfect state transfer on graph joins and provided a characterization of Laplacian perfect state transfer on the double cones. Coutinho and Liu in
[
10
]
proved that a tree of order
n\geq 3
has no Laplacian perfect state transfer. However, Banchi et al. in
[
4
]
showed that a path of order
n
has Laplacian pretty good state transfer if and only if
n
is a power of 2. Recently, Ackelsberg et al. in
[
1
]
showed that the corona of two graphs has no Laplacian perfect state transfer, but it has Laplacian pretty good state transfer with some mild conditions. Li and Liu in
[
23
]
showed
\mathcal{Q}
-graph of an
r
-regular graph
G
has no Laplacian perfect state transfer when
r+1
is a prime number, but it has Laplacian pretty good state transfer. Liu and Wang in
[
25
]
proved that total graph of an
r
-regular graph
G
has no Laplacian perfect state transfer when
r+1
is not a Laplacian eigenvalue of
G
. They also gave a sufficient condition on Laplacian pretty good state transfer for total graph of a regular graph. For more information about Laplacian state transfer of graphs, readers may refer to
[
3
,
8
,
29
]
and the cited references therein.

Compared with the research on quantum state transfer relative to adjacency matrix and Laplacian matrix of graphs, there have been far less studies on the problem of signless Laplacian state transfer of graphs. For example, Alvir et al in
[
3
]
proved that, if
H
is a
(\frac{n}{2}-1)
-regular graph of order
n\geq 2
, then the double cones
\overline{K}_{2}+H
has signless Laplacian perfect state transfer. It is proved
[
3
]
that there exists somewhat interesting tie between
quantum walks relative to the signless Laplacian matrix of a graph
G
and adjacency matrix of its line graph
l(G)
. With the help of this, Alvir et al
[
3
]
proved that the path
P_{n}
has no signless Laplacian prefect state transfer for
n\geq 5
. Recently, Kempton et al. in
[
21
,
22
]
mainly investigated perfect state transfer and pretty good state transfer with potential. Note that, if the potential on each vertex of graphs equals its degree, then it will result in the signless Laplacian quantum state transfer of graphs. In
2021
, Tian et al.
[
27

showed that there is no signless Laplacian perfect state transfer on corona graph
G\circ K_{m}
if
m
equals one or a prime number. They also showed, if

is an even number, then
K_{2}\circ\overline{K}_{m}
has no signless Laplacian perfect state transfer between two vertices of
K_{2}
. However,
G\circ\overline{K}_{m}
has signless Laplacian pretty good state transfer under some special conditions. For some properties about signless Laplacian matrices and applications in computer science, see
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
28

and the cited references therein.

Motivated by above some results, we mainly consider to study on perfect state transfer and pretty good state transfer relative to signless Laplacian matrix in
\mathcal{Q}
-graph. The

-
graph

[

-graph of a regular graph with a mild condition. Furthermore, we also present a sufficient condition for the
\mathcal{Q}
-graph admitting signless Laplacian pretty good state transfer.

2
Preliminaries

For a graph
G
of order
n
, let
A(G)
and
D(G)
denote its adjacent matrix and degree diagonal matrix, respectively. Then
Q_{G}=A(G)+D(G)
is the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
. The eigenvalues of
Q_{G}
are called the
signless Laplacian eigenvalues
of
G
. Let
q_{0}>q_{1}>\cdots>q_{d}
be all distinct eigenvalues of
Q_{G}
and
x_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,x_{l_{i}}^{(i)}
be the unit orthogonal eigenvectors corresponding to eigenvalue
q_{i}
with multiplicity
l_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,d
. Let
x^{T}
denote the transpose of a column vector
x
. Then, the eigenprojector corresponding to eigenvalue
q_{i}
can be written as the following matrix:

{f_{{q_{i}}}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{{l_{i}}}{x_{j}^{(i)}(x_{j}^{(i)}}{)^{T}},

(4)

and
\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{{q_{i}}}=I_{n}
, where
I_{n}
denotes the identity matrix of order
n
. It is easy to find that the
f_{{q_{i}}}
is idempotent matrix
(f_{{q_{i}}}^{2}=f_{{q_{i}}})
and
f_{{q_{i}}}f_{{q_{k}}}=0
for
i\neq k
. We can obtain the following alternative expression of the signless Laplacian matrix
Q_{G}
by the eigenprojector:

Q_{G}=Q_{G}\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{{q_{i}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}Q_{G}x_{j}^{(i)}(x_{j}^{(i)})^{T}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}q_{i}x_{j}^{(i)}(x_{j}^{(i)})^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}q_{i}f_{{q_{i}}},

(5)

which is called the
spectral decomposition
of
Q_{G}
. Since the continuous-time quantum walk on graph
G
relative to the signless Laplacian matrix
Q_{G}
is the unitary matrix

H_{Q_{G}}(t)=\text{exp}(-itQ_{G}),

(6)

then

H_{Q_{G}}(t)=\text{exp}(-itQ_{G})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-i)^{k}Q_{G}^{k}t^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-i)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{i=d}q_{i}^{k}f_{{q_{i}}}t^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{i=1}^{i=d}\text{exp}(-itq_{i})f_{{q_{i}}}.

(7)

For a vertex
u
of a graph
G
, denote its
signless Laplaian eigenvalue support
in graph
G
by
\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)
, which is the set of all the eigenvalues of
Q_{G}
satisfying
f_{{q_{i}}}e_{u}\neq 0
, where
e_{u}
is called the
characteristic vector
of
u
(the
u
-th entry of the column vector
e_{u}
is one, otherwise zero). The vertices
u
and
v
are said to be
strongly signless Laplacian cospectral
if
f_{q}e_{u}=\pm f_{q}e_{v}
for all eigenvalues
q
of
Q_{G}
. Denote the set of all the eigenvalues such that
f_{q}e_{u}=f_{q}e_{v}
by
S^{+}
, and denote the set of all the eigenvalues such that
f_{q}e_{u}=-f_{q}e_{v}
by
S^{-}
.

The following are some main theorems and lemmas which will help us to study singless Laplacian perfect state transfer or singless Laplacian pretty good state transfer of
\mathcal{Q}
-graph.

Theorem 2.1.

(Coutinho
[
8
]
) Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
with the vertex set
V(G)
, and
u,v\in V(G)
.
Suppose that
q_{0}
is the maxmum signless Laplacian eigenvalue in
G
.
Then
G
admits signless Laplacian perfect state transfer between the vertices
u
and
v
if and only if the following conditions hold.

(i)

Two vertices
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral relative to signless Laplacian.

(ii)

Non-zero elements in
\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)
are either all integers or all quadratic integers. Moreover, there exists a square-free integer
\Delta
and integers
a
,
b_{q}
such that, for each signless Laplacian eigenvalue
q\in\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)
,

q=\frac{1}{2}(a+b_{q}\sqrt{\Delta}).

Here we allow
\Delta=1
if all signless Laplacian eigenvalues in
\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)
are integers, and
a=0
if all signless Laplacian eigenvalues in
\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)
are all multiples of
\sqrt{\Delta}
.

(iii)

q\in{S^{+}}
if and only if
\dfrac{q_{0}-q}{g\sqrt{\Delta}}
is even, where

g=\gcd\left(\left\{\dfrac{q_{0}-q}{\sqrt{\Delta}}:q\in\text{supp}_{Q_{G}}(u)\right\}\right).

Moreover, if the above conditions hold, then the following also hold.

(1)

There exists a minimum time
\tau_{0}>0
at which signless Laplacian perfect state transfer occurs between
u
and
v
, and

\tau_{0}=\frac{1}{g}\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{\Delta}}.

(2)

The time of signless Laplacian perfect state transfer
\tau
is an odd multiple of
\tau_{0}
.

(3)

The phase of signless Laplacian perfect state transfer is given by
\lambda=e^{-itq_{0}}
.

Theorem 2.2.

(Hardy and Wright
[
19
]
) Assume that
1,q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
are linearly independent over the set of rational number
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, for any real numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
and positive real numbers
N
,
\epsilon
, there exist integers
l>N
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
such that

|{lq_{k}-\gamma_{k}-\alpha_{k}}|<\epsilon,

(8)

for each
k=1,\ldots,m
.

Lemma 2.3.

(Richards
[
26
]
) The set
\{\sqrt{\Delta}:\Delta
is a square-free integer
\}
is linearly independent over

\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 2.4.

(Coutinho
[
10
]
) A real number

\lambda
is a quadratic integer if and only if there exist integers
a
,
b
and
\Delta
such that
\Delta
is square-free and one of the following cases holds.

(i)

\lambda=a+b\sqrt{\Delta}
and
\Delta\equiv 2,3\;(\text{mod}\;4)
.

(ii)

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}(a+b\sqrt{\Delta}),\;\Delta\equiv 1\;(\text{mod}\;4)
, and either
a
and
b
are both even or both odd.

Theorem 4.2.

Let

r

Here we only proof the case of non-bipartite graph

Let

be the vertex set of
\mathcal{Q}(G)
, where

\mathcal{Q}(G)
. Also let

\mathcal{Q}(G)

2.1
, we only need to discuss it in the following two cases.

Case 1.
All the elements of
\text{supp}_{Q_{\mathcal{Q}(G)}}(z)
are integers. In this case, we consider the following two subcases: the vertex
z\in V(G)
and
z\in I(G)
.

Subcase 1.1.
The vertex
z\in V(G)
. Since
G
is a connected graph, then there is a positive eigenvalue

. Thus

. It follows from Lemma 4.1 and the (a) of Theorem 3.3 that

. Recall that all of

,
q_{+}
and

are integers. Since

{q_{+}}=\frac{{3r+q-2+\sqrt{{{(r+q-2)}^{2}}+4q}}}{2},\;\;{q_{-}}=\frac{{3r+q-2-\sqrt{{{(r+q-2)}^{2}}+4q}}}{2}.

q_{+}+q_{-}=3r+q-2

. It follows that
\sqrt{(r+q-2)^{2}+4q}
is integer, equivalently,

{(q+r-2)^{2}}<{(q+r-2)^{2}}+4q<{(q+r-2)^{2}}+4(q+r)<{(q+r+2)^{2}}.

{F_{{q_{\pm}}}}e_{z}^{m+n}=\frac{1}{{{{({q_{\pm}}+2-2r-q)}^{2}}+q}}\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{({q_{\pm}}+2-2r-q)({f_{q}}{R_{G}})e_{z}^{m}}\\

Case 2.

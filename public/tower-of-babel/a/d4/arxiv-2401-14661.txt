From Blurry to Brilliant Detection: YOLOv5-Based Aerial Object Detection with Super Resolution
Ragib Amin Nihal, Benjamin Yen, Katsutoshi Itoyama, Kazuhiro Nakadai

Abstract
The demand for accurate object detection in aerial imagery has surged with the widespread use of drones and satellite technology. Traditional object detection models, trained on datasets biased towards large objects, struggle to perform optimally in aerial scenarios where small, densely clustered objects are prevalent. To address this challenge, we present an innovative approach that combines super-resolution and an adapted lightweight YOLOv5 architecture. We employ a range of datasets, including VisDrone-2023, SeaDroneSee, VEDAI, and NWPU VHR-10, to evaluate our model’s performance. Our Super Resolved YOLOv5 architecture features Transformer encoder blocks, allowing the model to capture global context and context information, leading to improved detection results, especially in high-density, occluded conditions. This lightweight model not only delivers improved accuracy but also ensures efficient resource utilization, making it well-suited for real-time applications. Our experimental results demonstrate the model’s superior performance in detecting small and densely clustered objects, underlining the significance of dataset choice and architectural adaptation for this specific task. In particular, the method achieves 52.5% mAP on VisDrone, exceeding top prior works. This approach promises to significantly advance object detection in aerial imagery, contributing to more accurate and reliable results in a variety of real-world applications.

II-C

Super Resolution Network

Super resolution techniques aim to increase the resolution and enhance image details. This is critical for improving object detection in aerial images, where objects tend to appear small and indistinct. However, general super resolution methods developed for natural images do not transfer well to the aerial domain. One notable approach is the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for super resolution. GANs, such as SRGAN
[
24
]
, have shown great potential in producing realistic and high-quality super-resolved images. SRGAN employs a generator network to upscale images and a discriminator network to distinguish between the generated images and real high-resolution images, thus encouraging the generator to produce more authentic results. Another popular super-resolution method is ESRGAN
[
25
]
, which enhances SRGAN by incorporating a feature extraction network. This addition further improves the visual quality of super-resolved images.
While super-resolution techniques have been primarily applied to general imagery, their adoption in the domain of aerial and remote sensing imagery is less explored. While super-resolution techniques have shown promise, off-the-shelf models still struggle with the unique challenges of aerial imagery. As shown in Figure
1
, directly applying pretrained super resolution models can result in artifacts like blurring and distorted textures on aerial images. The unique challenges posed by aerial images, such as variations in scale, illumination, and environmental conditions, necessitate tailored solutions.

In this paper, we extend the application of super-resolution techniques to aerial imagery. By enhancing image quality through super-resolution, we aim to improve the performance of object detection in aerial images, particularly in scenarios with small and challenging-to-detect objects. This integration of super-resolution with custom YOLOv5 architecture represents a novel approach to address the specific challenges of object detection in aerial contexts.

III
PROPOSED METHOD – B2BDET

III-A

Overview

The key objective of this work is to enhance object detection in challenging aerial imagery containing small, blurred objects against complex backgrounds. To achieve this goal, a two-stage pipeline (Fig.
2
) is proposed. The first stage applies super resolution as a preprocessing technique. Super resolution using a GAN framework like SRGAN can enlarge and enhance small, blurry objects by generating realistic high-resolution images from low-resolution aerial photos. This is crucial because the small object sizes and lack of clarity due to distance, motion and atmospheric effects makes detection difficult. The second stage uses a SR YOLOv5 model for optimized object detection on the super resolved aerial images. YOLOv5 provides high accuracy while still allowing real-time performance due to its unified one-stage architecture. Modifying components like its backbone, neck and detection head enables adapting YOLOv5 specifically for challenges inherent in aerial imagery, such as small objects, clustered scenes, and complex backgrounds.

III-B

Super Resolution

To improve the quality of aerial images for object detection, we develop a custom SRGAN tailored to aerial imagery. The overall architecture follows the SRGAN
[
24
]
framework consisting of a generator model for upscaling images and a discriminator model for differentiating real and generated images (Fig
3
). The generator model contains residual blocks with convolutional layers to upscale input images. By competing against the generator, the discriminator provides adversarial feedback that helps produce sharper and more realistic High Resolution images. The SRGAN model is trained on diverse aerial image datasets consisting of satellite, drone, and airborne photos. Using extensive data covering various conditions ensures the model generalizes well to different aerial images for improved super-resolution performance.

The model is trained from scratch on aerial image datasets to learn mappings suitable for this domain.

By tailoring the SRGAN architecture and training for aerial images, we are able to super-resolve input images as a preprocessing step to significantly enhance their quality before object detection.

III-C

SR-YOLOv5: Super Resolved YOLOv5

YOLOv5 was chosen for aerial imaging due to its unified one-stage architecture, state-of-the-art performance, customizable key components, and strong baseline. It offers fast, efficient object classification and bounding box regression, maintains high FPS, and allows domain-specific optimizations, reducing training time. Our custom YOLOv5 architecture, named SR-YOLOv5 for its integration with the super resolution model, is designed to address the specific challenges associated with detecting objects in aerial images. It combines the efficiency of the YOLO framework with modifications that improve object detection accuracy and robustness in the context of aerial imagery (Figure
4
).

Backbone:
The backbone utilizes a reduced CSPDarknet53 model to efficiently extract features from input images. A key modification is the integration of C3STR (3 convolutions with Swin Transformer)
[
17
]
modules, which replace some standard C3 modules in the backbone. The transformer encoder block structure is motivated by the vision transformer. It is designed to capture global information and rich contextual details. Compared to the original bottleneck block, the transformer encoder block has the potential to capture global information with the help of a self-attention mechanism, which is crucial in aerial images. The architecture starts with a "Focus" module, which performs an initial convolution operation. This helps capture low-level features efficiently.
This initial Focus layer is vital for reducing computational costs and enhancing the model’s ability to detect small objects.

Neck:
The architecture employs a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) head as the neck module. The FPN head is responsible for combining features from different levels of the backbone using operations like upsampling and concatenation. This multi-scale feature aggregation mechanism enables the model to utilize information from both lower and higher resolution feature maps. This proves crucial for detecting objects across varying scales, which is common in aerial imagery. The FPN neck allows the model to leverage both semantically strong features to detect larger objects as well as spatially detailed features to localize smaller objects.

Head:
The detection head incorporates modules to expand the model’s depth and receptive field. Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) is utilized to provide a spatial pyramid pooling effect, enhancing the model’s capacity to gather features across multiple scales within a single receptive field. BottleneckCSP modules further contribute to increasing the overall depth and complexity of the model. Together, these components improve the model’s ability to comprehend intricate patterns and represent complex features found in dense aerial imagery.

III-D

Training

During the SRGAN training, The model is trained on diverse aerial image datasets consisting of satellite, drone, and airborne photos using an adversarial loss between the generator and discriminator. After getting the super resolution images, the object detection model is trained using strategies like strong regularization and compound scaling to find the optimal width and depth scaling factors. Data augmentation techniques mimicking the complexity of aerial scenes are used, along with tailored anchor boxes designed to handle small objects based on the training datasets. These training procedures optimize the model for robust performance on the unique challenges presented by aerial imagery.

VisDrone:
Our primary dataset of choice is the VisDrone-2023 dataset, which contains over 6,000 aerial images captured by drones. It has a total of 343,205 annotated object instances across 11 classes such as cars, buses, bicycles, and pedestrians. A key characteristic of VisDrone-2020 is that it is dominated by small objects, with over 87% of instances occupying less than 0.3% of image area. This sets it apart from conventional datasets like COCO and ImageNet that are biased towards large objects. The large number of small objects densely clustered together poses a unique challenge for object detection. Overall, VisDrone-2023’s focus on small objects makes it suitable for evaluating models on the critical task of small object detection in dense aerial scenes. The images cover diverse environments and conditions, testing generalizability of approaches. VisDrone-2023 is the same dataset as VisDrone-2018

IV-B

Implementation Details

The super resolution model was trained on an NVIDIA A100 GPU using a batch size of 16. The model was fine-tuned for 4000 iterations with an ADAM optimizer (learning rate=1e-4).
For object detection, we used a YOLOv5 model trained from scratch. Key hyperparameters include a momentum of 0.843, learning rate schedule from 0.0032 to 0.12, and a weight decay of 0.00036. Data augmentation techniques like mosaic (1.0) and mixup (0.2) were used.
The model was trained for 100 epochs with a cosine learning rate schedule and early stopping. Multi-scale evaluation was used, with input sizes from 3072 to 1408 pixels.
Mean average precision (mAP)
[
29
]
was the primary evaluation metric for object detection performance. mAP provides a comprehensive measure of precision and recall across IoU thresholds and object classes.

IV-C

On VisDrone, our method achieves 52.5% mAP exceeding state-of-the-art techniques like SCA-YOLO (47.4% mAP) and YOLOv4 (30.7% mAP). The significant boost demonstrates the benefits of super resolution and YOLOv5 optimizations for congested small object detection. Table

IV-D

Results on Other Datasets

On NWPU-VHR10, consisting of very high resolution aerial images, our approach attains a 90.5% overall mAP across 10 classes including airplanes, ships, and vehicles. In particular, it scores 99.5% and 96.9% mAP on the airplane and vehicle categories respectively, demonstrating its robustness.

Experiments on the SeaDroneSee maritime search and rescue dataset also show strong results of 76% overall mAP. Despite the challenge of distinguishing between visually similar classes like swimmers, boats, and buoys, the method achieves 96.3% and 93.4% mAP on the boat and jetski categories, validating its efficacy for critical detection tasks.

Finally, on the vehicle-focused VEDAI dataset, the proposed technique secures 77.5% overall mAP, with especially high mAP of 89.3% on cars. This confirms its capability to precisely categorize vehicles in aerial views across varied environments.

Extensive experiments were conducted to evaluate the proposed approach on major aerial image datasets including VisDrone, NWPU-VHR10, SeaDroneSee, and VEDAI. The results demonstrate superior performance over existing methods and validate the effectiveness of the innovations presented in this work.

V
DISCUSSION

V-A

Correlation with number of training instances

There appears to be some correlation between the number of training instances for each object class and the detection performance on that class. For example, on VisDrone the car category with over 200,000 instances secures a high 87.8% AP. In contrast, the awning tricycle category with only 4,000 instances achieves lower 31.7% AP. More training data for a class enables the model to better learn and detect those objects.

V-B

Unlabeled objects

While quantitative results demonstrate clear improvements from our method, they underestimate the full capabilities of B2BDet. A key limitation we encountered stems from incomplete or inconsistent annotation of test images. As illustrated in Figure
6
, qualitative examination reveals that the model can in fact detect small and obscured objects that are visibly present but not annotated in the test data. However, our B2BDet model successfully detects many of these unannotated objects, indicating its strong generalization capabilities. The presence of unlabeled objects negatively biases the quantitative evaluation, since they are counted as false positives despite being valid detections. This suppresses reported performance on metrics like mAP which rely on the annotated ground truth being exhaustive. In essence, the model is penalized for exceeding the capabilities of the human annotation effort. We performed extensive qualitative analysis and found many similar cases where lack of annotations failed to account for all discernible objects. The degree of under-annotation varies across datasets and categories, impacting certain classes more than others. Vehicle and people classes tend to be most affected, as obscured and distant, easily missed in manual labeling.

While annotation quality and coverage affect all models, this issue disproportionately hinders methods with higher generalization like B2BDet. Models that rely more on memorization cannot detect unlabeled instances. Enhanced generalization thus exposes the shortcomings of annotations. This represents an important direction for improvement in data preparation. Expanding the scope and consistency of labeling to match modern model capabilities would provide fairer assessment and prevent under-estimating performance. In the interim, we recommend evaluating on both annotated and unannotated test images to fully gauge model strengths. Qualitative human review on a sample of predictions can help estimate the prevalence of unlabeled objects and compensate when interpreting quantitative results. With these prudent analysis practices, major gains in detection performance can be unlocked without being obscured by evaluation limitations.

V-C

Limitations

While results are promising, there remain limitations and opportunities for further improvement. The model still struggles with extremely small, particularly low-resolution objects under 10 pixels. Occluded objects in dense clusters are another challenge during inference. On boarding cameras with lower image quality, noise and distortion negatively impact detections. Future work should expand testing across more aerial platforms and conditions to uncover additional limitations. Another side is, the model detects objects independently but lacks explicit modeling of inter-object occlusion patterns and relationships. Incorporating contextual information could aid in resolving clustered, overlapping instances.

V-D

Thinking Beyond Incremental Improvements

While this work focuses on advancing aerial object detection, it is worth reflecting on the overall direction of the field. Human perception involves holistic scene understanding rather than isolating objects. Should computer vision aim to mimic such cognition versus pursuing incremental metric gains?
Exploring new architectures and objectives for unified scene representation could complement ongoing improvements in labeling individual objects. Representing overall scene gist, context, configurations and dynamics may unlock new capabilities beyond exhaustive classification.
By incorporating higher-level relationships, physics, conceptual knowledge, and other facets nearer to human reasoning, models can potentially expand from passive recognizers toward more versatile cognitive systems.
While out of scope here, reflecting on these alternative futures and the role of interpretable decisions can guide innovation in comprehensive directions. A diversity of paradigms will likely advance the field collectively.

VI
CONCLUSION

This paper presented a new aerial object detection method using super resolution and optimized YOLOv5. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of the approach for detecting small, dense objects in challenging aerial imagery.
The key innovations include: A custom SRGAN tailored for aerial images to enhance input quality,
a modified YOLOv5 architecture optimized for aerial object detection, and
a two-stage pipeline applying super resolution preprocessing before detection.
The results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on major datasets like VisDrone, NWPU-VHR10, SeaDroneSee, and VEDAI. In particular, the method achieves 52.5% mAP on VisDrone, exceeding top prior works.
This work advances aerial object detection research by addressing limitations of existing methods on small, blurred, clustered object scenarios. The integrated solution synergistically leverages super resolution and tailored YOLOv5 to push boundaries of detection accuracy in dense aerial imagery.
Future work can expand the variety of datasets for more robust generalization. Additional robustness to motion blur, low image quality, and extreme occlusion can be pursued. Investigation into real-time optimization and efficient deployment on aerial platforms provides another valuable direction. By building on the techniques presented, further advances in real-world aerial object detection can be achieved.

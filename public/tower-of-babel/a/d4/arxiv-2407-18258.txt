On Galois covers of curves and arithmetic of Jacobians
Alexandros Konstantinou and Adam Morgan

Abstract
. We study the arithmetic of curves and Jacobians endowed with the action of a finite group G G . This includes a study of the basic properties, as G G -modules, of their ℓ \ell -adic representations, Selmer groups, rational points and Shafarevich–Tate groups. In particular, we show that p ∞ p^{\infty} -Selmer groups are self-dual G G -modules, and give various ‘ G G -descent’ results for Selmer groups and rational points. Along the way we revisit, and slightly refine, a construction going back to Kani and Rosen for associating isogenies to homomorphisms between permutation representations. With a view to future applications, it is convenient to work throughout with curves that are not assumed to be geometrically connected (or even connected); such curves arise naturally when taking Galois closures of covers of curves. For lack of a suitable reference, we carefully detail how to deduce the relevant properties of such curves and their Jacobians from the more standard geometrically connected case.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to establish some basic, yet fundamental, arithmetic properties of curves and their Jacobians in the presence of the action of a finite group of automorphisms. A key motivator for this work is to support applications to the parity conjecture for Jacobians, which appear in work of the authors with V. Dokchitser and Green
[
8
]
. A central theme of that work is that one can study the arithmetic of Jacobians by exploiting
Brauer relations
in the context of function fields of curves; alongside a broader study of group actions on curves over arithmetic fields, this paper sets up the machinery required to do this. With these applications in mind, it will be important to work throughout with a slightly more general notion of ‘curve’ than is perhaps standard. Specifically, our convention is as follows:

Convention 1.1
.

Throughout this paper, curves are assumed to be smooth and proper, but are not assumed to be connected, and neither are their connected components assumed to be geometrically connected.

Such curves emerge naturally in the context of Galois covers; see Example
1.6
below. For completeness, and for lack of a suitable reference, we dedicate part of this paper to carefully detailing the basic properties enjoyed by such curves and their Jacobians. For convenience when stating the other results in the paper, we turn to this first.

1.3.

2.
Étale algebras and curves

In the following two sections, we prove Proposition
1.1
and define the notion of the
S_{n}
-closure of a cover of curves. We begin with a brief review of étale algebras and their corresponding Galois sets. We then discuss curves and their Jacobians. Recall that, as in Convention
1.1
, we assume that all curves are smooth and proper, but do not assume that they are connected, nor that their connected components are geometrically connected. As a result, while much of the material in this section is presumably standard, we have often been unable to locate suitable references. For completeness, we show how to deduce all relevant properties of these curves and their Jacobians from the corresponding, and more standard, results in the geometrically connected case.

Definition 2.3
.

The

-closure

\widetilde{L}
of

is defined to be the étale

-algebra associated via Proposition

Remark 2.4
.

See

]
for an alternative description of the

-closure which carries over to more general ring extensions.

Note that

-action, so by Proposition

we obtain a left action of

Remark 2.6
.

The map (

as the quotient of

by the action of

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that the characteristic of

for some separable polynomial

. Write

Then the algebra

Proof.

Let

be the

in

Since these two

∎

2.1.2.

With a view to applications, we discuss a related notion to that of

.

The inclusion of

If

2.2.

In what follows, we take

3.
Jacobians

We now shift our attention from curves to Jacobians, with Convention
1.1
remaining in place. The purpose of this section is to explain how several standard results for Jacobians of geometrically connected curves extend to the slightly more general setting of Convention
1.1
. As before,
k
denotes a field of characteristic
0
.

3.1.
The geometrically connected case

Let
X
be a geometrically connected
k
-curve. We denote by
\Jac_{X}
the Jacobian of
X
, which is an abelian variety over
k
of dimension equal to the genus of
X
. Denote by
\lambda_{X}
the canonical principal polarisation on
\Jac_{X}
.
Given a non-constant morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
of geometrically connected
k
-curves, we have induced
k
-homomorphisms

f^{*}:\Jac_{Y}\rightarrow\Jac_{X}\quad\textup{ and }\quad f_{*}:\Jac_{X}\rightarrow\Jac_{Y}.

We have
\Jac_{X}(\bar{k})=\textup{Pic}^{0}(X_{\bar{k}})
, which identifies with the group of degree
0
divisors on
X_{\bar{k}}
modulo linear equivalence, and similarly for
Y
. Under this identification,
f_{*}
sends a prime divisor
Q
to
f(Q)
, whilst
f^{*}
is the homomorphism that sends a prime divisor
Q
to

\sum_{P\in f^{-1}(Q)}e(P)P,

where
e(P)
is the ramification degree of
f
at
P
.

The following lemma is standard, but we have been unable to locate a suitable reference.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have
f^{*}=\lambda_{X}^{-1}\circ f_{*}^{\vee}\circ\lambda_{Y}
, where
f_{*}^{\vee}
is the dual of
f_{*}
.

Proof.

It suffices to check this identity after base-change to
\bar{k}
.
Fix any point
P
in
X(\bar{k})
. Then
P
defines a morphism (the Abel–Jacobi map)
\phi^{P}:X\rightarrow\Jac_{X}
induced by the map on divisors

(Q)\longmapsto(Q)-(P).

Similarly, the point
f(P)\in Y(\bar{k})
induces a morphism
\phi^{f(P)}:Y\rightarrow\Jac_{Y}
. From the explicit description of
f_{*}
in terms of divisors, one checks that

(3.2)

\phi^{f(P)}\circ f=f_{*}\circ\phi^{P}.

After applying the functor
\textup{Pic}^{0}_{-/k}
to this equality, the result follows from
[
18
, Lemma 6.9]
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Using the principal polarisations
\lambda_{X}
and
\lambda_{Y}
to identify the Jacobians of
X
and
Y
with their duals, we can express Lemma
3.1
more succinctly as saying that
(f_{*})^{\vee}=f^{*}
.

3.2.
The general case

Let
X/k
be a curve satisfying Convention
1.1
. By definition, we take the Jacobian
\Jac_{X}
of
X
to be the identity component of the relative Picard functor of
X/k
. Let
k(X)
be as in Definition
2.14
. In what follows, we write
\overline{X}=X_{\bar{k}}
for the base-change of

to
\bar{k}
, and write
\bar{k}(X)
as shorthand for
k(X)\otimes_{k}\bar{k}=\bar{k}(\overline{X})
. We have the following basic lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

The Jacobian of
X
is a principally polarised abelian variety over
k
.

Proof.

Since
X
is proper, by
[
21
, Theorem 2]
and
[
12
, Lemma 5.1]
(cf. also
[
12
, Corollary 4.18.3]
),
\textup{Pic}^{0}_{X/k}
is represented by a finite-type commutative group scheme over

. Now decompose
\overline{X}
as in (

), noting that, as we are now working over an algebraically closed field, each

is a geometrically connected curve. Then

(3.5)

(\Jac_{X})_{\bar{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MR2223410}{}{}, Prop. 5.3]}}}}{{=}}\textup{Pic}^{0}_{\overline{X}/\bar{k}}=\prod_{x\in\textup{ Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\textup{Jac}_{\overline{X}_{x}}

is an abelian variety. Thus the same is true of
\Jac_{X}
.

With the curves
\overline{X}_{x}
as above, equip each
\textup{Jac}_{\overline{X}_{x}}
with its canonical principal polarisation, and equip
(\Jac_{X})_{\bar{k}}
with the corresponding product polarisation, which we denote

. We wish to show that
\lambda_{X}
is defined over
k
. To see this, let
P=(P_{x})_{x\in\textup{ Spec }\bar{k}(X)}
be a tuple of
\bar{k}
-points, with
P_{x}\in\overline{X}_{x}(\bar{k})
for each

\phi^{(P)}:\overline{X}=\bigsqcup_{x\in\textup{ Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\overline{X}_{x}\longrightarrow\prod_{x\in\textup{Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\textup{Jac}_{\overline{X}_{x}}=(\Jac_{X})_{\bar{k}}

denote the morphism induced by the individual Abel–Jacobi maps associated to the

. Pulling back line bundles under

gives a homomorphism

\textup{Jac}_{X}^{\vee}(\bar{k})\longrightarrow\textup{Jac}_{X}(\bar{k}).

From

we see that this agrees with the map on

-points induced by

. In particular, this map is independent of the choice of

, hence also

, is defined over

∎

3.2.1.
Jacobians as products of Weil restrictions

We record the following description of the Jacobian as a product of Weil restrictions of Jacobians of geometrically connected curves. We remark that this gives an alternative approach to Lemma

Notation 3.6
.

Let

be a finite field extension, and let

Similarly, for a functor

-schemes to sets, we denote by
\textup{Res}_{k^{\prime}/k}F
the functor from
k
-schemes to sets given by

\textup{Res}_{k^{\prime}/k}F(T)=F(T\times_{k}k^{\prime}).

As in (

as a disjoint union of connected
k
-curves. For

, write

for the function field of

, and write
k_{x}=\bar{k}\cap k(X_{x})
. Per Remark

corresponds to a geometrically connected

-curve

is obtained from

by forgetting the

Lemma 3.7
.

As abelian varieties over
k
, we have

\textup{Jac}_{X}\cong\prod_{x\in\textup{ Spec }k(X)}\textup{Res}_{k_{x}/k}\textup{Jac}_{X^{\prime}_{x}}.

Proof.

The decomposition (
2.15
) gives
\textup{Jac}_{X}\cong\prod_{x\in\textup{ Spec }k(X)}\textup{Jac}_{X_{x}}
. Fixing

, we wish to show that
\textup{Jac}_{X_{x}}\cong\textup{Res}_{k_{x}/k}\textup{Jac}_{X_{x}^{\prime}}
. Since
\textup{Res}_{k_{x}/k}\textup{Jac}_{X^{\prime}_{x}}
is connected, it suffices to show that

\textup{Res}_{k_{x}/k}\textup{Pic}_{X_{x}^{\prime}/k_{x}}\cong\textup{Pic}_{X_{x}/k}.

Denote by

the morphism corresponding to the inclusion of

, and denote by

\textup{Res}_{k_{x}/k}\textup{Pic}_{X_{x}^{\prime}/k_{x}}=g_{*}R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\textup{Pic}_{X_{x}/k}=R^{1}(g\circ f)_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m},

where

denotes the multiplicative group over

. As

is a finite separable extension,

is exact on fppf sheaves (exactness can be checked after pullback to

is a finite disjoint union of copies of

and

agree, giving the result.
∎

It what follows, given a

\textup{Div}(\overline{X})=\oplus_{P\in X(\bar{k})}\mathbb{Z}\cdot P,

\textup{Div}^{0}(\overline{X})
the subgroup of
\textup{Div}(\overline{X})
consisting of elements whose restriction to each connected component of

has degree

. Given

, we can view

as a tuple

(Equivalently, viewing

Writing

for

, we see from (

\textup{Jac}_{X}(\bar{k})=\textup{Div}^{0}(\overline{X})/\textup{Prin}(\overline{X}).

Given

for its class in
\textup{Jac}_{X}(\bar{k})
.

Now let

be a finite surjective morphism. As in the case of geometrically connected curves, we have homomorphisms

(3.10)

f^{*}:\textup{Div}(\overline{Y})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}(\overline{X})\quad\textup{ and }\quad f_{*}:\textup{Div}(\overline{X})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}(\overline{Y})

determined by setting
f_{*}(P)=f(P)
for all

, and setting

for all

. (Again,

denotes the ramification index of

at

Lemma 3.11
.

For all
Z\in\textup{Div}^{0}(\overline{X})
and
Z^{\prime}\in\textup{Div}^{0}(\overline{Y})
, we have

f^{*}[Z^{\prime}]=[f^{*}Z^{\prime}]\quad\textup{ and }\quad f_{*}[Z]=[f_{*}Z].

Proof.

We can formally reduce to the case where
X
and

are geometrically connected as follows. Write
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
as disjoint unions of connected
\bar{k}
-curves as in (

).
For

, write

for the finite surjective morphism

induced by

. Now (the base-change to

of)

gives a homomorphism

\prod_{y\in\textup{ Spec }\bar{k}(Y)}\textup{Jac}_{\overline{Y}_{y}}\longrightarrow\prod_{x\in\textup{ Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\textup{Jac}_{\overline{X}_{x}}.

From the definition of

we see that this corresponds to the matrix of homomorphisms
(\phi_{y,x})
, where for

and

, we have

\phi_{y,x}=\begin{cases}f_{x}^{*}~~&~~f(x)=y,\\
0~~&~~\textup{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Using this, the claim for

reduces to the case of maps between geometrically connected curves. Dualising with respect to the product polarisation and applying Lemma

, we see that

corresponds to the matrix

where, for

and

\phi^{\vee}_{x,y}=\begin{cases}(f_{x})_{*}~~&~~f(x)=y,\\
0~~&~~\textup{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The result for

now similarly reduces to the geometrically connected case. ∎

Remark 3.12

Remark 3.13

3.2.3.
Comparison of differentials.

Let

be a

as in Section

Proposition 3.15
.

The map (

-equivariant and (

\alpha_{X}:\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}(X).

Given a finite surjective morphism

between

-curves, we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{Y})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{(f_{*})^{*}}
\textstyle{\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{X}}
\textstyle{\Omega^{1}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}

Proof.

For

(

)
is an isomorphism independent of

is
[

, Proposition 2.2]
. For the general case, recall that

is defined as the morphism

\overline{X}=\bigsqcup_{x\in\textup{Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\overline{X}_{x}\longrightarrow\prod_{x\in\textup{Spec }\bar{k}(X)}\textup{Jac}_{\overline{X}_{x}}=(\textup{Jac}_{X})_{\bar{k}}

∎

4.1.
Quotients by finite groups

Let

\textup{Spec }\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)^{G}

Proposition 4.1
.

The quotient

for the quotient morphism we have, both as maps between Jacobians and as maps between

\pi_{*}\circ\pi^{*}=|G|\quad\textup{ and }\quad\pi^{*}\circ\pi_{*}=\sum_{g\in G}g_{*}.

When

acts transitively on

denote by

induced by the action of

to

the first morphism being the natural quotient map. This reduces the statement to the corresponding result for the actions of the groups

on the geometrically connected curves

∎

4.3.
From
G
-maps to isogenies

As above, let
X
be a curve over
k
, and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{k}(X)
. In the rest of this section, we show that one can naturally associate homomorphisms between Jacobians of quotients of
X
to certain
G
-equivariant maps between permutation modules. This idea originates in work of Kani–Rosen
[
11
]
, though we follow more closely subsequent work of Chen
[
4
]
, and de Smit and Edixhoven
[
9
]
. See Remark
4.15
below for a comparison between our main result here, Theorem
4.14
, and those works. We will use the following notation concerning permutation modules.

Notation 4.11
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-module. For each subgroup
H
of
G
we have an isomorphism

(4.12)

\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{H}

given by evaluating homomorphisms at the trivial coset. Now suppose we have subgroups
H_{1},...,H_{n}
and
H_{1}^{\prime},...,H_{m}^{\prime}
of
G
, and define
G
-sets

S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}G/H_{i}\quad\textup{ and }\quad S^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}G/H_{j}^{\prime}.

Taking
\mathbb{Z}[S]
and
\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
to be the corresponding permutation modules, (
4.12
) induces isomorphisms

\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],M)\cong\bigoplus_{i}M^{H_{i}}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}],M)\cong\bigoplus_{j}M^{H_{j}^{\prime}}.

Consequently, given
\phi
in
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}])
, the map from
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}],M)
to
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],M)
sending
f
to
f\circ\phi
induces a homomorphism

(4.13)

\phi^{*}:\bigoplus_{j}M^{H_{j}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}M^{H_{i}}.

Moreover, the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}[S]
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
) is canonically self-dual, via the pairing making the elements of
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) an orthonormal basis. Given
\phi
in
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}])
, we denote by
\phi^{\vee}
the corresponding dual homomorphism
\phi^{\vee}:\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[S]
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
H_{1},...,H_{n}
and
H_{1}^{\prime},...,H_{m}^{\prime}
be subgroups of
G
, take

S=\bigsqcup_{i}G/H_{i}\quad\textup{ and }\quad S^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{j}G/H_{j}^{\prime},

and let
\mathbb{Z}[S]
and
\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
denote the corresponding permutation modules.

Then for each
G
-module homomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}[S]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
, there is a
k
-homomorphism of abelian varieties

f_{\phi}:\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}}

such that:

(1)

(duality): denoting by
\phi^{\vee}:\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[S]
the dual homomorphism, we have

f_{\phi^{\vee}}=f_{\phi}^{\vee}.

(Here
f_{\phi}^{\vee}
is the dual of
f_{\phi}
with respect to the product of the canonical principal polarisations.)

(2)

(functoriality): given also
\phi^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime\prime}]
, we have

f_{\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi}=f_{\phi}\circ f_{\phi^{\prime}}.

(3)

(Isogeny criterion): suppose that the map

\phi^{*}:\bigoplus_{j}V_{\ell}(\Jac_{X})^{H_{j}^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}V_{\ell}(\Jac_{X})^{H_{i}}

on rational
\ell
-adic Tate modules is an isomorphism.
Then
f_{\phi}
is an isogeny.

Remark 4.15
.

As mentioned above, a version of of Theorem
4.14
appears in work of Chen
[
4
]
and de Smit and Edixhoven
[
11
]
. Aside from the extra generality afforded by our relaxed notion of a curve, Theorem
4.14
has some additional technical advantages over the maps constructed in those works. Specifically, in place of
f_{\phi}
, Chen uses the map denoted
\alpha(\phi)
in Construction
Construction
. While adequate for his purposes, this map does not (quite) satisfy the duality and functoriality statements in parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
4.14
. By contrast, the work of de Smit and Edixhoven uses the same map
f_{\phi}
as we do, but constructs it only in the category of abelian varieties with isogenies inverted. We are able to improve on this as a consequence of Lemma
4.19
below.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
4.14
, which will take the rest of the section.

Notation 4.16
.

For
g\in G
, denote by
g_{*}
the corresponding automorphism of
\Jac_{X}
(cf. Definition
3.8
).
For a subgroup
H
of
G
, denote by
\pi_{H}:X\rightarrow X/H
the quotient morphism. As in §
3.2.2
, we have associated mutually dual homomorphisms

\pi_{H}^{*}:\Jac_{X/H}\longrightarrow\Jac_{X}\quad\textup{ and }\quad(\pi_{H})_{*}:\Jac_{X}\longrightarrow\Jac_{X/H}.

We also set
\Jac_{X}^{H}=\cap_{h\in H}\ker(h_{*}-1)
. This is a group subvariety of
\Jac_{X}
. The connected component of the identity
(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}
is an abelian subvariety of
\Jac_{X}
.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
.

(1)

We have

(\pi_{H})_{*}\circ(\pi_{H})^{*}=|H|\quad\textup{ and }\quad(\pi_{H})^{*}\circ(\pi_{H})_{*}=\sum_{h\in H}h_{*}.

(2)

The image of
(\pi_{H})^{*}
is contained in
(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}
and the resulting homomorphism

(4.18)

(\pi_{H})^{*}:\Jac_{X/H}\longrightarrow(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}

is an isogeny.

Proof.

(1): Follows from Proposition
4.1
.
(2): We first show that the image of
(\pi_{H})^{*}
is contained in
(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}
. Since
\Jac_{X/H}
is connected, it suffices to show that the image of
(\pi_{H})^{*}
is contained in
\Jac_{X}^{H}
, for which we can assume

. Working under this assumption, note that the map
(\pi_{H})_{*}:\textup{Div}^{0}(X)\rightarrow\textup{Div}^{0}(X/H)
of (
3.10

3.11
we deduce that
(\pi_{H})_{*}:\Jac_{X}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H}
is surjective, so that by (1) the image of
\pi_{H}^{*}
is contained in the image of
\sum_{h\in H}h_{*}
. Since this latter image is contained in
\cap_{h\in H}\textup{ker}(h_{*}-1)
, the result follows. Finally, to show that the map in (
4.18
) is an isogeny, note that by (1) the endomorphism
(\pi_{H})^{*}\circ(\pi_{H})_{*}
of

restricts to multiplication by
|H|
on

. In particular, we have homomorphisms
(\pi_{H})^{*}:\Jac_{X/H}\rightarrow(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}
and
(\pi_{H})_{*}:(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H}
whose composition in each direction is multiplication by

. Thus both maps are isogenies.
∎

We now turn to constructing the map
f_{\phi}
of Theorem
4.14
in the case that

.

Construction
.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be subgroups of
G
, and let
\phi\in\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}])
. Let
\sum_{g\in G}m_{g}g
be any lift of
\phi(H)
under the projection
\mathbb{Z}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}]
. We have an associated endomorphism of
\Jac_{X}
given by

\widetilde{\phi}:=\sum_{g\in G}m_{g}g_{*}.

Since
H^{\prime}
acts trivially on
\Jac_{X}^{H^{\prime}}
, the restriction of
\widetilde{\phi}
to
\Jac_{X}^{H^{\prime}}
is independent of the choice of lift of
\phi(H)
, and maps
\Jac_{X}^{H^{\prime}}
to
\Jac_{X}^{H}
. Consequently (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
), we have a
k
-homomorphism of abelian varieties

\alpha(\phi):=(\pi_{H})_{*}\circ\widetilde{\phi}\circ\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}:\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\Jac_{X/H},

which is again independent of choices.

The following lemma is key to obtaining an association
\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}
satisfying parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
4.14
.

Lemma 4.19
.

For any
\phi\in\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}])
, there exists a unique
k
-homomorphism
f_{\phi}:\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H}
satisfying
|H|f_{\phi}=\alpha(\phi)
. Moreover, we have

(4.20)

\pi_{H}^{*}\circ f_{\phi}=\widetilde{\phi}\circ\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*},

for any choice of
\widetilde{\phi}
as above.

Proof.

Uniqueness is standard. To prove existence it suffices to show that
\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}[|H|]\subseteq\ker(\alpha(\phi))
(cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4]
). For this we can assume that
k=\bar{k}
, which we henceforth do.

In what follows we use the terminology set out in Notation
3.9
to talk about
k
-points of
\Jac_{X/H}
(resp.
\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}
). Let
Z\in\textup{Div}^{0}(X/H^{\prime})
, and suppose that
|H|Z=\textup{div}(\eta)
for some unit
\eta\in k(X/H^{\prime})=L^{H^{\prime}}
. With
\sum_{g\in G}m_{g}g
any lift of
\phi(H)
to
\mathbb{Z}[G]
as above, we see that the quantity

\zeta=\prod_{g\in G}g(\eta)^{m_{g}}

defines an invertible element of
L^{H}=k(X/H)
. Using the fact that both
\pi_{H}^{*}
and
\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}
commute with

(see Remark
3.13
), one computes

|H|\textup{div}(\zeta)=(\pi_{H})_{*}\pi_{H}^{*}(\textup{div}(\zeta))=(\pi_{H})_{*}\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}(\textup{div}(\eta))=|H|(\pi_{H})_{*}\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}(Z).

Thus
(\pi_{H})_{*}\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}(Z)=\textup{div}(\zeta)
, as desired.

Now let
f_{\phi}:\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H}
be as in the statement. It remains to show that
\pi_{H}^{*}\circ f_{\phi}=\widetilde{\phi}\circ\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}.
Since
(\pi_{H})_{*}\pi_{H}^{*}=|H|
, we have
(\pi_{H})_{*}\pi_{H}^{*}f_{\phi}=\alpha(\phi)
. Applying
\pi_{H}^{*}
to this equation gives

N_{H}(\pi_{H}^{*}f_{\phi}-\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*})=0,

where we use the notation
N_{H}=\sum_{g\in G}h_{*}
.
Now both
\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}
and
\pi_{H}^{*}f_{\phi}
map
\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}
into the abelian variety
(\Jac_{X}^{H})^{0}
. Since
N_{H}
acts as multiplication by
|H|
on
\Jac_{X}^{H}
, we see that

|H|(\pi_{H}^{*}f_{\phi}-\widetilde{\phi}\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*})=0,

giving the result.
∎

Definition 4.21
.

Given
\phi
in
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}])
, we define
f_{\phi}:\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H}
to be the
k
-homomorphism afforded by Lemma

.

Remark 4.22
.

From the uniqueness property of
f_{\phi}
we see that the association
\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}
is (contravariantly) functorial. More precisely, given subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}
,
H_{3}
of
G
, and
G
-module homomorphisms

\phi_{1}:\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{1}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{2}]\quad\textup{ and }\quad\phi_{2}:\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{2}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{3}],

we have

f_{\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}}=f_{\phi_{1}}\circ f_{\phi_{2}}.

As explained in Notation

, associated to

is the dual homomorphism
\phi^{\vee}:\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H]

is the dual homomorphism
f_{\phi}^{\vee}:\Jac_{X/H}\rightarrow\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}
defined with respect to the canonical principal polarisations on
\Jac_{X/H}
and
\Jac_{X/H^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 4.23
.

We have
f_{\phi}^{\vee}=f_{\phi^{\vee}}
.

Proof.

It will be convenient to draw on an explicit description of
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}])
in terms of double cosets. Specifically, we have an isomorphism

(4.24)

\mathbb{Z}[H\backslash G/H^{\prime}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}])

which sends a double coset
HgH^{\prime}
to the
G
-homomorphism
\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}
determined by

\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}(H)=\sum_{u\in H/H\cap gH^{\prime}g^{-1}}ugH^{\prime}.

An elementary calculation shows that, for any
g\in G
, we have

(4.25)

\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}^{\vee}=\phi_{H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}.

Writing
N_{H}=\sum_{h\in H}h\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
, we note that in
\mathbb{Z}[G]
we have the identity

(4.26)

\sum_{u\in H/H\cap gH^{\prime}g^{-1}}ugN_{H^{\prime}}=\sum_{t\in HgH^{\prime}}t=\sum_{w\in H^{\prime}/H^{\prime}\cap g^{-1}Hg}N_{H}gw.

We now prove the result. First, by (
4.24
) it suffices to take
\phi=\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}
for some
g\in G
. From (
4.26
) we see that
\sum_{t\in HgH^{\prime}}t
is a lift of
|H^{\prime}|\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}(H)
to
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. In particular, writing
\gamma=\sum_{t\in HgH^{\prime}}t_{*}
, we have

|H||H^{\prime}|f_{\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}}=(\pi_{H})_{*}\circ\gamma\circ\pi_{H^{\prime}}^{*}.

Dualising gives

|H||H^{\prime}|f_{\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}}^{\vee}=(\pi_{H^{\prime}})_{*}\circ\gamma^{\vee}\circ\pi_{H}^{*}.

Further, Remark
3.12
gives

\gamma^{\vee}=\sum_{t\in HgH^{\prime}}(t^{-1})_{*}=\sum_{t\in H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}t_{*}.

Again appealing to (
4.26
) we see that
\sum_{t\in H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}t
is a lift of
|H|\phi_{H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}(H^{\prime})
to
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. The uniqueness property of
f_{\phi}
thus gives

f_{|H|\phi_{H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}}=|H|f_{\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}}^{\vee},

so that
f_{\phi_{H^{\prime}g^{-1}H}}=f_{\phi_{HgH^{\prime}}}^{\vee}
. We now conclude from (
4.25
).
∎

We now define the homomorphism

in the generality claimed in Theorem

by extending additively the association of Definition

Proof of Theorem

5.
Rational points, Selmer groups and Shafarevich–Tate groups

In this section we prove some results concerning the arithmetic of Jacobians of curves with an action of a finite group
G
. As usual, Convention
1.1
is in place.

Some of the results in this section hold more generally for principally polarised abelian varieties, so we expand to this generality where appropriate. The main results of the section are summarised in Theorem
5.2
below, whose proof occupies Sections
5.1
and
5.2
. Section
5.3
gives a complement to Theorem
5.2
concerning height pairings. Finally, in Sections
5.4
and

, we give some auxiliary results concerning the
G
-action on differentials, and the expected order of Shafarevich–Tate groups modulo squares.

Notation 5.1
.

For an abelian variety
A
over a number field
K
, and for an integer

, we denote by

the
n
-Selmer group of

. For a prime

, we write
\textup{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A)=\varinjlim_{n}\textup{Sel}_{p^{n}}(A)
to denote the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group of
A
. We also write

\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)=\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\textup{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

We denote by
\Sha(A)
the Shafarevich–Tate group of
A
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a curve over a number field
K
, and
G
a finite group acting on
X
by
K
-automorphisms. Then we have:

(1)

The quotient morphism
\pi:X\to X/G
induces a
K
-homomorphism
\pi^{*}:\textup{Jac}_{X/G}(K)\to\textup{Jac}_{X}(K)^{G}
with finite kernel and cokernel, and an isomorphism

(5.3)

(\textup{Jac}_{X}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{G}\cong\textup{Jac}_{X/G}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

(2)

Let
p
be a prime. Then
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is a self-dual
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representation, and

(5.4)

\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X})^{G}\cong\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X/G}).

(3)

If
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for a prime
p
, then so is
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X/G})[p^{\infty}].
If
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X})
is finite then so is
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X/G}).

(4)

If
\rho
is a
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representation such that
\langle V_{\ell}(\Jac_{X}),\rho\rangle=0
, then

\langle\textup{Jac}_{X}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q},\rho\rangle=0.

Moreover, for each prime

, we have
\langle\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X}),\rho\rangle=0
.

(Here
\left\langle~,~\right\rangle
denotes the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representations.)

Proof.

(1): that
\pi^{*}
induces an isomorphism as in (
5.3
) follows from Lemma
4.28
, which implies the rest of the part. (2): for self duality, combine Remark
3.12
with Theorem
5.7
below. The isomorphism (
5.4
) follows from Lemma
4.28
. (3): The claim about
p
-primary parts follows from
(1)
and
(2)
. The claim about the full Shafarevich–Tate group follows from this, after noting that
\pi^{*}:\textup{Jac}_{X/G}\rightarrow\textup{Jac}_{X}
induces an isomorphism

∎

5.1.
Isotypic components

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
and let
G
be a finite group acting on
A
by
K
-automorphisms. The following lemma shows that if a given complex representation
\rho
of
G
does not appear in the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
A
for some (equivalently any) prime
\ell
, then it does not appear in various
G
-representations functorially associated to
A
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\rho
be a
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representation such that
\langle\rho,V_{\ell}(A)\rangle=0
for some prime
\ell
. Then, for each prime
p
, we have

\langle\rho,\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)\rangle=\langle\rho,A(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\rangle=0.

Proof.

V_{\ell}(A)
has rational character by
[
19
, Theorem 1, Chapter 25]
. We denote by
F_{\rho}
the field obtained by extending
\mathbb{Q}
by the values of the characters of
\rho
. Then, a positive power of the representation
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\textup{Gal}(F_{\rho}/\mathbb{Q})}\rho^{\sigma}
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
and shares no common constituent with
V_{\ell}(A)
. It therefore suffices to consider the case where
\rho
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
. Denote by
e_{\rho}
the idempotent in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
which projects onto the
\rho
-isotypic part. Clearing denominators, there exists a positive integer
n
for which
ne_{\rho}
lies in
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. Viewed as an endomorphism of
A
,
ne_{\rho}
induces the zero map on
T_{\ell}(A)
. In view of
[
17
, Proposition 12.2]
,
ne_{\rho}
is the zero endomorphism on
A
, and therefore induces the zero map on
\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)
and
A(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, equipped with the action of a finite group
G
. The same proof shows that, if
\langle\rho,V_{\ell}(A)\rangle=0
for some
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representation
\rho
, then
\langle\rho,\Omega^{1}(A)\rangle=0
where
\Omega^{1}(A)
is the
k
-vector space of regular differentials.

5.2.
Self-duality of Selmer groups

Let
K
be a number field. In what follows, the pair
(A,\lambda)
will denote an abelian variety
A/K
, equipped with a principal polarisation
\lambda
defined over
K
. We denote by
\textup{Aut}(A,\lambda)
the group of
K
-automorphisms
g
of
A
preserving the polarisation (i.e. for which
g^{\vee}=\lambda g^{-1}\lambda^{-1}
).
The proof of the following result is modeled on
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(A,\lambda)
be a principally polarised abelian variety, and let
G
be a subgroup of
\textup{Aut}(A,\lambda)
. Then
\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)
is self-dual as a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representation.

Proof.

Following
[
7
]
, given a
K
-isogeny of abelian varieties
f:A\to B
, we write

\begin{split}Q(f)=\#\text{coker}(f:A(K)/A(K)_{\text{tors}}\to B(K)/B(K)_{\text{tors}})\cdot\#\text{ker}(f:\Sha(A)_{\text{div}}\to\Sha(B)_{\text{div}}),\end{split}

where
\Sha_{\text{div}}
denotes the divisible part of
\Sha.
We decompose
\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)
into

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-irreducible representations, and decompose the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice

X_{p}(A)=\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\varinjlim_{n}\textup{Sel}_{p^{n}}(A),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})

into
G
-stable
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-sublattices such that

\mathcal{X}_{p}(A)\cong\bigoplus_{i}\tau_{i}^{n_{\tau_{i}}},\quad X_{p}(A)\cong\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda_{\tau_{i}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\Lambda_{\tau_{i}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\cong\tau_{i}^{n_{\tau_{i}}},

where the
\tau_{i}
are distinct
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-irreducible representations of
G
.
We construct a self-isogeny of
A
with a specified action on the sublattices
\Lambda_{\tau_{i}}
of
X_{p}(A)
. For an irreducible
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representation
\tau\in\{\tau_{i}\}
, the operator
{\textup{dim}(\tau)}\sum_{g\in G}\textup{Tr}(\tau(g))g^{-1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G]
kills every irreducible
G
-constituent
\tau^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{X}_{p}
not isomorphic to
\tau
, and acts via multiplication by
\langle\tau,\tau\rangle\cdot|G|
on every constituent of
\mathcal{X}_{p}
which is isomorphic to
\tau
. We define

z_{\tau}:=|G|\cdot\langle\tau,\tau\rangle+({p-1})\cdot\textup{dim}(\tau)\cdot\sum_{g\in G}\text{Tr}(\tau(g))g^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G].

Elements in a small neighbourhood
U_{\tau}
of
z_{\tau}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G]
act via multiplication by
p\cdot|G|\cdot\langle\tau,\tau\rangle
composed with some isomorphism on
\Lambda_{\tau},
and via multiplication by
|G|\cdot\langle\tau,\tau\rangle
composed with some isomorphism on
\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}
when
\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau
. Similarly, there’s a corresponding neighbourhood
U_{\tau^{*}}
of
z_{\tau^{*}}
, where
\tau^{*}
is the dual representation. Since the inversion map
g\mapsto g^{-1}
is continuous in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
and maps
z_{\tau}
to
z_{\tau^{*}}
, we can find
\sigma=\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\cap U_{\tau}
such that
\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g^{-1}
lies in
U_{\tau^{*}}.
We write
\phi_{\sigma}
to denote the endomorphism of
A
determined by
\sigma
. Since
G
is a subgroup of
\textup{Aut}(A,\lambda)
, then
\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g^{-1}
corresponds to the dual isogeny
\phi_{\sigma}^{\vee}
. By considering their induced actions on the
\Lambda_{\tau_{i}}
, we deduce that

Q(\phi_{\sigma})=(|G|\cdot\langle\tau,\tau\rangle)^{\rk_{p}A}\cdot p^{n_{\tau}\,\text{dim}(\tau)}\quad\textup{ and }\quad{Q}(\phi_{\sigma}^{\vee})=(|G|\cdot\langle\tau^{*},\tau^{*}\rangle)^{\rk_{p}A}\cdot p^{n_{\tau^{*}}\,\text{dim}(\tau^{*})}.

Since
\langle{\tau},\tau\rangle=\langle{\tau}^{*},\tau^{*}\rangle,\textup{dim}(\tau)=\textup{dim}(\tau^{*})
and
{Q}(\phi_{\sigma})={Q}(\phi_{\sigma}^{\vee})
(see
[
7
, Theorem 4.3]
), we deduce that
n_{\tau}=n_{\tau^{*}}
. This holds for all
\tau\in\{\tau_{i}\}
, and so the result follows.
∎

5.3.
Height pairings

Let
K
be a number field and
A/K
an abelian variety. For a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
A
we denote by
h_{\mathcal{L}}
the height function associated to
\mathcal{L}
(cf.
[
14
, Chapter 5, Theorem 3.2]
). We denote by
h_{A}:A(k)\times A^{\vee}(k)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the Neron–Tate height on
A\times A^{\vee}
. It is the height function associated to the Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}_{A}
on
A\times A^{\vee}
. Given a polarisation
\lambda:A\rightarrow A^{\vee}
, we denote by
h_{\lambda}
the pairing
A(k)\times A(k)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

h_{\lambda}(a,b)=h_{A}(a,\lambda(b)).

The following lemma is standard, but we include it for lack of a suitable reference.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\phi:A\rightarrow B
be a homomorphism of abelian varieties over
k
. Then, for any
a\in A(k)
and
b\in B^{\vee}(k)
, we have

h_{B}(\phi(a),b)=h_{A}(a,\phi^{\vee}(b)).

Proof.

By
[
14
, Chapter 5, Proposition 3.3]
, the composition

A\times B^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi\times 1}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\times B^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{B}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R}

is equal to the height function
h_{(\phi\times 1)^{*}\mathcal{P}_{B}}
. Similarly,

h_{A}\circ(1\times\phi^{\vee})=h_{(1\times\phi^{\vee})^{*}\mathcal{P}_{A}}.

It thus suffices to show that the line bundles
(\phi\times 1)^{*}\mathcal{P}_{B}
and
(1\times\phi^{\vee})^{*}\mathcal{P}_{A}
agree in
\textup{Pic}(A\times B^{\vee})
, which is an immediate consequence of the defining property of
\phi^{\vee}
(see e.g.
[
17
, §11]
).
∎

With Lemma
5.8
in hand we can establish the basic compatibility with heights satisfied by the map
\pi^{*}
of Theorem
5.2
(1).

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
X
be a curve over a number field
K
, and
G
a finite subgroup of
K
-automorphisms of
X
. Let
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
denote the quotient morphism. Then, for all
a,b
in
\textup{Jac}_{X/G}(K)
, we have

h_{\textup{Jac}_{X}}\left(\pi^{*}(a),\pi^{*}(b)\right)=|G|\cdot h_{\textup{Jac}_{X/G}}(a,b).

Here the height pairings are defined with respect to the canonical principal polarisations of Lemma
3.4
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
5.8
, recalling that
\pi^{*}
is dual to
\pi_{*}
and that
\pi_{*}\circ\pi^{*}=|G|
(the first of these facts holds by definition, see Definition
3.8
, while the second is Proposition
4.1
).
∎

5.4.
Tate modules and differentials

With a view to applications, we establish some basic auxiliary results concerning the action of a finite group
G
of automorphisms on regular differentials and on Tate modules. In the following subsection,
K
denotes either a number field or the completion of one. Let
A/K
be an abelian variety.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on
A
via
K
-automorphisms. Then

(1)

for any prime
\ell
, the
G
-representations

V_{\ell}(A)\quad\textup{ and }\quad\Omega^{1}(A)\oplus\Omega^{1}(A)^{*}

are isomorphic after extending scalars to
\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

if
\Omega^{1}(A)
is self-dual as a
G
-representation, then
\Omega^{1}(A)^{\oplus 2}
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

if
K=\mathbb{R}
, or
K
is a number field with a real place, then
\Omega^{1}(A)
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

In what follows, for a field extension
F/K
, we will write
\Omega^{1}(A/F)
for the
F
-vector space of regular differentials on
A_{F}
.

(1). Base-changing along a field embedding
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, it suffices to prove the corresponding result for
K
replaced by
\mathbb{C}
and
A
a complex torus. In this setting, the map that sends
\gamma\in H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
to the map
\omega\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega
induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}[G]
-modules

(5.11)

H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{C})^{*}.

Here
\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{C})^{*}
is the dual of
\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{C})
as a complex representation, viewed as an
\mathbb{R}[G]
-module by forgetting the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space structure. Note that for any finite dimensional complex
G
-representation
V
, we have
{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong V\oplus V^{*}
. From this we see that, as
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representations, we have

(5.12)

H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\cong\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{C})\oplus\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{C})^{*}.

The result now follows since, for each prime
\ell
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[G]
-representations

V_{\ell}(A)\cong H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}.

(2). From (
5.12
) we see that
\Omega^{1}(A)\oplus\Omega^{1}(A)^{*}
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
, giving the result.

(3). Under the assumption of the statement, we can choose the embedding
\sigma
appearing in the proof of (1) so that it factors through
\mathbb{R}
. Then, each side of (
5.11
) carries a natural action of complex conjugation, and the isomorphism of (
5.11
) is equivariant for this action. Denoting by
H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})^{+}
the
G
-submodule of
H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
invariant under this action, we obtain from (
5.11
) an isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}[G]
-modules

H_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})^{+}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{R})^{*}.

Thus
\Omega^{1}(A/\mathbb{R})^{*}
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
, from which the result follows.
∎

5.5.
Deficiency and the Shafarevich–Tate group

The aim of this subsection is to extend certain notions introduced by Poonen–Stoll
[
22
]
from geometrically connected curves to the curves of Convention
1.1
.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a local field of characteristic
0
. We define an invariant
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
for each curve
X
over
\mathcal{K}
as follows.

First, recall from
[
22
, §8]
that a geometrically connected curve
X
over
\mathcal{K}
, of genus
g
, is called
deficient
if it has no
\mathcal{K}
-rational divisor of degree
g-1
. For such
X
, we define

\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $X$ is deficient,}\\
1&\textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Next, suppose that
X
is connected (but not necessarily geometrically connected). Let
\mathcal{K}(X)
denote the function field of
X
, let
\mathcal{L}
denote the algebraic closure of
\mathcal{K}
in
\mathcal{K}(X)
, and let
Y
denote the geometrically connected curve over
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to
\mathcal{K}(X)
via Proposition
2.12
(cf. also Remark
2.13
). We then define
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
. Finally, writing
X=\bigsqcup_{i}X_{i}
as a disjoint union of connected components, we define

\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\prod_{i}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X_{i}).

If the field
\mathcal{K}
is clear from context, we will often omit it from the notation.
Further, when
\mathcal{K}=K_{v}
is the completion of a number field
K
at a place
v
, we write
\mu_{v}(X)
in place of
\mu_{K_{v}}(X)
.

Remark 5.14
.

Let
X
be a connected curve over
\mathcal{K}
, and let
X_{0}
be any geometric connected component of
X
. Letting
\mathcal{K}_{0}/\mathcal{K}
be the smallest field extension of
\mathcal{K}
over which
X_{0}
is defined, one checks straight from the definition that
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\mu_{\mathcal{K}_{0}}(X_{0})
.

The following is a generalisation of a result of Poonen–Stoll
[
22
, Theoerem 8, Corollary 12]
, who prove it for geometrically connected curves.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X
be a curve defined over a number field
K
, and write
\text{\tencyr Sh}_{0}(\Jac_{X})
for the quotient of
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X})
by its maximal divisible subgroup. Then

\#\text{\tencyr Sh}_{0}(\Jac_{X})[2^{\infty}]\equiv\prod_{\textup{$v$ place of $K$}}\mu_{v}(X)\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Proof.

Writing
X=\bigsqcup X_{i}
as a disjoint union of connected components, we have
\Jac_{X}=\prod_{i}\Jac_{X_{i}}
. Using this, we reduce to the case that
X
is connected. Let
K(X)
denote the function field of
X
, let
L/K
denote the algebraic closure of
K
in
K(X)
, and let
Y
denote the geometrically connected curve over
L
corresponding to
K(X)
via Proposition
2.12
. As in Remark
2.13
,
X
is isomorphic to the
K
-scheme obtained from
Y
by forgetting the
L
-structure. Observe also that, for each place
v
of
K
, we have
L\otimes_{K}K_{v}=\prod_{w\mid v}L_{w}
, where the product is taken over each place
w
of
L
dividing
v
. Using these two observations, we compute

X\times_{K}K_{v}=(Y\times_{L}L)\times_{K}K_{v}=\bigsqcup_{w\mid v}Y\times_{L}L_{w}.

Since each
Y\times_{L}L_{w}
is a geometrically connected
L_{w}
-curve, we conclude that

(5.16)

\mu_{v}(X)=\prod_{w\mid v}\mu_{L_{w}}(Y).

On the other hand, Lemma
3.7
gives

\Jac_{X}=\textup{Res}_{L/K}\Jac_{Y},

where
\textup{Res}_{L/K}
denotes Weil restriction from
L
to
K
. Shapiro’s lemma then gives
\text{\tencyr Sh}_{0}(\Jac_{X})[2^{\infty}]\cong\Sha_{0}(\Jac_{Y}/L)[2^{\infty}]

(cf.
[
16
, proof of Theorem 1]
).
Combined with (
5.16
), this reduces the result to the corresponding one for the geometrically connected curve
Y/L
, which is
[
22
, Theorem 8, Corollary 12]
.
∎

Nonequilibrium Phase Transition To Temporal Oscillations In Mean-Field Spin Models
Laura Guislain, Eric Bertin

Abstract
We propose a mean-field theory to describe the nonequilibrium phase transition to a spontaneously oscillating state in spin models. A nonequilibrium generalization of the Landau free energy is obtained from the joint distribution of the magnetization and its smoothed stochastic time derivative. The order parameter of the transition is a Hamiltonian, whose nonzero value signals the onset of oscillations. The Hamiltonian and the nonequilibrium Landau free energy are determined explicitly from the stochastic spin dynamics. The oscillating phase is also characterized by a non-trivial overlap distribution reminiscent of a continuous replica symmetry breaking, in spite of the absence of disorder. An illustration is given on an explicit kinetic mean-field spin model.



The emergence of spontaneous oscillations at a collective scale in large assemblies of interacting units is one of the most striking features of nonequilibrium systems.
Beyond the now well-understood synchronization of coupled oscillators
Acebrón et al. 2005
;
Risler et al. 2004
,
spontaneous oscillations also appear in diverse systems of interacting units where individual units do not oscillate in the absence of interactions, making the onset of oscillations a genuinely collective phenomenon.
Such oscillations have been reported for instance in biochemical clocks
Cao et al. 2015
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
;
Aufinger et al. 2022
,
populations of biological cells
Kamino et al. 2017
;
Wang and Tang 2019
, assemblies of active particles with non-reciprocal interactions
Saha et al. 2020
;
You et al. 2020
, nonequilibrium spin systems
Collet et al. 2016
;
De Martino and Barato 2019
;
Dai Pra et al. 2020
as well as population dynamics
Andrae et al. 2010
;
Duan et al. 2019
and socio-economic models
Gualdi et al. 2015
;
Yi et al. 2015
.

In the thermodynamic limit, the onset of spontaneous oscillations is described by a deterministic Hopf bifurcation
Crawford 1991
.
Yet, oscillations often occur in mesoscopic systems like biochemical clocks for which fluctuations play an important role
Fei et al. 2018
, leading to a stochastic Hopf bifurcation
Sagués et al. 2007
;
Xu et al. 2020
and to a finite coherence time of oscillations
Gaspard 2002
;
Barato and Seifert 2016
;
Barato and Seifert 2017
;
Oberreiter et al. 2022
;
Remlein et al. 2022
.
To provide a consistent theoretical ground, the emergence of spontaneous oscillations in large assemblies of interacting units has been characterized as a nonequilibrium thermodynamic phase transition, by identifying the entropy production as a generalized thermodynamic potential whose derivative is discontinuous at the transition

Crochik and Tomé 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2008
;
Xiao et al. 2009
;
Barato and Hinrichsen 2012
;
Tomé and de Oliveira 2012
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
;
Noa et al. 2019
;
Martynec et al. 2020
;
Seara et al. 2021
.
Similar results have also been obtained for the entropy production in population dynamics
Andrae et al. 2010
, and for a nonequilibrium free energy in the context of Turing pattern formation
Falasco et al. 2018
.
However, beyond singularities of thermodynamic potentials, the equilibrium theory of phase transitions and critical phenomena is based on the key concepts of spontaneous symmetry breaking and of associated order parameter
Le Bellac 1992
.
Once the latter is identified, the generic Landau free-energy can be determined unambiguously to characterize the phase transition at mean-field level.
Recent nonequilibrium generalizations of Landau’s theory include the description of relaxation effects
Meibohm and Esposito 2022
;
Holtzman and Raz 2022
, or multiple heat baths and oscillations driven by an oscillatory field
Aron and Chamon 2020
.

In this Letter, we go beyond the thermodynamic approach to phase transitions with spontaneously emerging oscillations, and show how to build a nonequilibrium generalization of the Landau free energy in a class of driven kinetic mean-field spin models, based on the spontaneous breaking of spin-reversal symmetry and time-translation invariance.
The generalized Landau free energy is obtained from the joint distribution of the magnetization and its smoothed stochastic time derivative, at odds with previous generalizations based on magnetization only
Meibohm and Esposito 2022
;
Holtzman and Raz 2022
;
Aron and Chamon 2020
.
Close to the phase transition to an oscillating phase, the nonequilibrium Landau free energy can be expressed in terms of a single order parameter, which is an effective Hamiltonian describing the oscillating dynamics of the magnetization. In addition, we show by evaluating the overlap distribution of spin configurations that the oscillating phase is also characterized by an analogue of the continuous replica symmetry breaking phenomenon observed in disordered systems
Mézard et al. 1987
.

In dynamical systems theory, a limit cycle may be generically described in the plane of a variable and its time derivative.
We aim at building a generalized Landau theory describing finite size fluctuations around the average limit cycle. We thus need to characterize not only the fluctuations of magnetization, but also of its time derivative. Yet, directly considering the time derivative of

leads to diverging, white-noise type fluctuations that are not appropriate to build a Landau theory.
We thus rather aim at defining an observable attached to each microscopic configuration that would play the role of an appropriately smoothed out derivative of the magnetization.

At the transition to spontaneous oscillations,

should change from a paraboloid-like shape to a ‘Mexican-hat’ shape.
To identify the parameter controlling the transition, we start with a quadratic approximation of

for small

\dot{m}
, and look for a change of curvature.
At quadratic order in

4

Assuming

.

For

We no longer assume

The function

The case

In the simple yet generic case where

To sum up, we have shown how the Landau theory of phase transitions can be extended to describe phase transitions to an oscillating phase in nonequilibrium spin models.
While previous nonequilibrium generalizations of the Landau free energy were only based on magnetization and did not address spontaneous oscillations
Meibohm and Esposito 2022
;
Holtzman and Raz 2022
;
Aron and Chamon 2020
, we defined a generalized Landau free energy as the rate function
\phi(m,\dot{m})
associated with the joint distribution of the magnetization
m
and its smoothed stochastic derivative
\dot{m}
defined in Eq. (
1
).
The order parameter of the Landau theory is an effective Hamiltonian
H
, whose nonzero value indicates the presence of oscillations. The expression of
H(m,\dot{m})
and of the nonequilibrium Landau free energy
f(H)
can be determined explicitly from the stochastic spin dynamics. The expansion of
f(H)
is singular close to a tricritical point where paramagnetic, ferromagnetic and oscillating phases meet. Beyond spontaneous breaking of time translation invariance, the oscillating phase is characterized by an overlap distribution reminiscent of continuous replica symmetry breaking, although no disorder is present. Consistently with previous works
Crochik and Tomé 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2008
;
Xiao et al. 2009
;
Barato and Hinrichsen 2012
;
Tomé and de Oliveira 2012
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
;
Noa et al. 2019
;
Martynec et al. 2020
;
Seara et al. 2021
, we also recover that the entropy production density becomes non-zero in the oscillating phase
SM
.
Future work will notably aim at characterizing the transition to oscillating states in finite-dimensional systems using renormalization group methods.

I
Generic spin model

I.1
Definition of the stochastic derivative of the magnetization

We aim at defining a random variable that would play the role of the derivative of the magnetization.
We denote as
\mathcal{C}
the microscopic configuration of the system. We assume a Markov jump dynamics with transition rate
W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C})
from configuration
\mathcal{C}
to configuration
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
We introduce a stochastic derivative
\dot{m}(\mathcal{C})
of the magnetization
m(\mathcal{C})
such that in average,
d\langle m\rangle/dt=\langle\dot{m}\rangle
where the average
\langle\dots\rangle
is defined
\langle x\rangle=\sum_{C}x(\mathcal{C})P(\mathcal{C})
.
Using the master equation

\frac{dP(\mathcal{C})}{dt}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\neq\mathcal{C}}\big[W(\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}^{\prime})P(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C})P(\mathcal{C})\big],

(1)

we obtain after rearranging terms that

\frac{d\langle m\rangle}{dt}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}P(\mathcal{C})\sum_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\neq\mathcal{C}}\left(m(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-m(\mathcal{C})\right)W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C}).

(2)

From Eq. (
2
), we identify the stochastic derivative of the magnetization

\dot{m}(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\neq\mathcal{C}}\left(m\left(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right)-m\left(\mathcal{C}\right)\right)W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C}),

(3)

in such a way that

\frac{d\langle m\rangle}{dt}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}P(\mathcal{C})\,\dot{m}(\mathcal{C}).

(4)

The definition Eq. (
3
) of
\dot{m}
provides a smoothed expression of the time derivative of
m
, in the sense that it is already averaged over possible arrival configurations
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Fluctuations of
\dot{m}
are thus on the same scale as that of
m
, which is appropriate to define a joint probability distribution of
m
and
\dot{m}
and its associated large deviation function.

I.2
Transition rates in the spin model

The microscopic configuration

As the spins are exchangeable random variables in mean-field models, de Finetti’s representation theorem

V
Entropy production

The transition to a limit cycle may also be characterized thermodynamically as a transition from microscopic to macroscopic irreversibility, by introducing the entropy production density
\sigma=\Sigma/N
in the limit
N\to\infty
, where the steady-state entropy production
\Sigma
identifies with the entropy flux
Schnackenberg 1976
;
Gaspard 2004
,

\Sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\big[W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C})P(\mathcal{C})-W(\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}^{\prime})P(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\big]\,\ln\frac{W(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\mathcal{C})}{W(\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}^{\prime})}\,.

(52)

One finds (see below) that in the paramagnetic phase (
\epsilon<0
),
\sigma=0
while in the oscillating phase (
\epsilon>0
),
\sigma\sim\epsilon
becomes non-zero
(similar calculations have been performed in
Xiao et al. 2008
;
Seara et al. 2021
in the context of chemical oscillators).
Hence the entropy production density
\sigma
is also an order parameter of the phase transition to a limit cycle, associated with a macroscopic breaking of time-reversal invariance. The corresponding critical exponent, equal to
1
, is
the same as for the order parameter
\langle\dot{m}^{2}\rangle
characterizing the spontaneous breaking of time translation invariance.
In the nonequilibrium paramagnetic or ferromagnetic phases, the entropy production
\Sigma
remains microscopic, i.e.,
\Sigma=O(N^{0})
.
Note that the transition between paramagnetic and ferromagnetic nonequilibrium phases is expected to be characterized by a cusp of the entropy production
\Sigma

Noa et al. 2019
.
We evaluate here the entropy production in spin models under the assumptions detailed in Sec.
I.2
.
We show that we recover results obtained in the diffusive limit as done in
Xiao et al. 2008
;
Seara et al. 2021
in the context of chemical reactions.

We consider that there are only two different types of transitions

.
Expressing Eq. (

and
\dot{m}
and considering the lowest order in N, the entropy production density
\sigma=\Sigma/N
becomes

\sigma=\sum_{k}\left\langle\left(W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})-W_{k}^{-}(m,\dot{m})\right)\ln\frac{W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})}{W_{k}^{-}(m,\dot{m})}\right\rangle.

(53)

We assume for now that
1-W_{k}^{-}/W_{k}^{+}
is small, such that the entropy production can be approximated as

\sigma=\left\langle\,\sum_{k}\frac{\left(\,W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})-W_{k}^{-}(m,\dot{m})\,\right)^{2}}{W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})}\,\right\rangle.

(54)

We note
\mathbf{A}
the change-of-basis matrix such that
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{d}_{1}=(1,\,0)
and
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{d}_{2}=(0,\,1)
. Using that
(\dot{m},\,Y(m,\dot{m}))=\sum_{k}\left(W_{k}^{+}-W_{k}^{-}\right)\mathbf{d}_{k}
and that
\mathbf{D}=\sum_{k}W_{k}^{+}\mathbf{d}_{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{d}_{k}^{T}
at first order in
1-W_{k}^{-}/W_{k}^{+}
, in the new basis we have

\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}^{+}(m,\dot{m})-W_{1}^{-}(m,\dot{m})\\
W_{2}^{+}(m,\dot{m})-W_{2}^{-}(m,\dot{m})\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{A}\begin{pmatrix}\dot{m}\\
Y(m,\dot{m})\end{pmatrix}

(55)

and

\textbf{Diag}\left(W_{1}^{+}(m,\dot{m}),W_{2}^{+}(m,\dot{m})\right)=\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{T}.

(56)

Hence, the entropy production density can be rewritten as

\sigma=\big\langle\,(\dot{m},Y(m,\dot{m}))^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}^{-1}\cdot(\dot{m},Y(m,\dot{m}))\,\big\rangle.

(57)

Retaining the lowest order of
Y(m,\dot{m})
in
\varepsilon
and using that
\langle V^{\prime}(m)\dot{m}\rangle=0
when
\phi(m,\dot{m})=f(H)
with
H=V(m)+\frac{\dot{m}^{2}}{2}
, the entropy production density becomes

\sigma=\big\langle\,(D^{-1})_{11}\dot{m}^{2}+(D^{-1})_{22}V^{\prime}(m)^{2}\,\big\rangle.

(58)

In the paramagnetic phase (
\varepsilon<0
), one has for finite
N
the scaling
\sigma\sim N^{-1}
because, as shown in the main text,

\langle\dot{m}^{2}\rangle\sim N^{-1},\qquad\langle V^{\prime}(m)^{2}\rangle\sim N^{-1}.

(59)

Therefore in the limit
N\to\infty
, the entropy production density vanishes.
In the oscillating phase (
\varepsilon>0
), the entropy production density
\sigma
converges to a finite value
\sigma\sim\varepsilon
when
N\to\infty
, due to the fact that

\langle\dot{m}^{2}\rangle\sim\varepsilon,\qquad\langle V^{\prime}(m)^{2}\rangle\sim\varepsilon.

(60)

To obtain these results for the entropy production, we assumed that
1-W_{k}^{-}(m,\dot{m})/W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})
was small. Under the assumption that
W_{k}^{\sigma}
is of order
1
(which is verified in the specific model on spins and fields), it is equivalent to assuming that
W_{k}^{+}-W_{k}^{-}
is small.
One has that
W_{k}^{+}(m,\dot{m})-W_{k}^{-}(m,\dot{m})=y^{k}
where

\textbf{y}=(y^{a},y^{b})=\mathbf{A}\begin{pmatrix}\dot{m}\\
Y(m,\dot{m})\end{pmatrix}

(61)

[see Eq. (
55
)], and writing

\textbf{x}=\mathbf{A}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
\dot{m}\end{pmatrix}

(62)

from Eqs. (
4
) and (
8
) one gets that

\frac{d\langle\textbf{x}\rangle}{dt}=\langle\textbf{y}\rangle.

(63)

In a static phase
\langle\textbf{y}\rangle=0
and thus we expect this value to remain small close to the transition line for continuous phase transitions. Therefore, the assumption that
W_{k}^{+}-W_{k}^{-}
is small is valid close to transition lines, for continuous transition from a static phase to either another static phase or to an oscillating phase.

V.2
Generic diffusive limit

The entropy production density may also be derived in the diffusive limit, as done by
Xiao et al. 2008
;
Seara et al. 2021
.
The linear and quadratic terms in
\nabla\phi
of Eq. (3) of the main text correspond to a Fokker-Planck equation on
P(m,\dot{m},t)

\partial_{t}P=\partial_{m}(\dot{m}P)+\partial_{\dot{m}}Y(m,\dot{m})P+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(D_{ij}P).

(64)

We write
\mathbf{x}=(m,\dot{m})
and
\mathbf{y}=(\dot{m},Y(m,\dot{m}))
. We introduce the probability current density
J(\mathbf{x})
such that Eq. (
64
) becomes
\partial_{t}P=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}
with at the lowest order in
N

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x},t)=-\left(\mathbf{y}+\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{D}\cdot\nabla\right)P(\mathbf{x},t)+O(N^{-1}).

(65)

We consider a trajectory
\mathbf{x}(t)=(m(t),\dot{m}(t))
. We define an entropy along the trajectory, as done by
Seifert 2005
,

s(t)=-\ln P(\mathbf{x}(t),t).

(66)

The rate of change of the entropy along a trajectory is

\dot{s}=-\frac{\partial_{t}P(\mathbf{x},t)}{P(\mathbf{x},t)}-\frac{1}{P(\mathbf{x},t)}\,\mathbf{\dot{x}}^{T}\cdot\nabla P(\mathbf{x},t)\,.

(67)

Using the probability current density
\mathbf{J}
, we obtain

\dot{s}=\left[-\frac{\partial_{t}P}{P}+\frac{2N}{P}\mathbf{\dot{x}}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{J}\right]+N\,\mathbf{\dot{x}}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{y}.

(68)

The term within the square bracket in Eq. (
68
) denotes the trajectory-dependent total entropy production
\dot{s}_{tot}
as shown in
Seifert 2005
.
We define the medium entropy production as

\dot{s}_{m}=N\,\mathbf{\dot{x}}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{y}.

(69)

In the limit
N\to\infty
,
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=(\dot{m},Y(m,\dot{m}))=\mathbf{y}
. Averaging over the stationary distribution, we obtain an expression for the entropy production density

\sigma=\frac{1}{N}\langle\dot{s}_{m}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{y}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{D}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{y}\rangle

(70)

which is consistent with the expression Eq. (
57
) derived with the spin-reversal dynamics, under the approximation Eq. (
54
).

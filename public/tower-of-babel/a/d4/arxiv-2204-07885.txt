On the principal minors of the powers of a matrix
Darij Grinberg

Abstract
Abstract. We show that if A A is an n × n n\times n -matrix, then the diagonal entries of each power A m A^{m} are uniquely determined by the principal minors of A A , and can be written as universal (integral) polynomials in the latter. Furthermore, if the latter all equal 1 1 , then so do the former. These results are inspired by Problem B5 on the Putnam contest 2021, and shed a new light on the behavior of minors under matrix multiplication. Keywords: principal minors, determinantal identities, determinants, matrices, determinantal ideals, Putnam contest. MSC2020 classes: 11C20, 14M12, 15A15.

Proof 2.3

(Proof of Theorem

.

The notation

.

Fix

. In other words, there exists an

Now, consider a commutative ring

-matrix

over

.
Let

be the

(numbered in the same order as

). Let

be the

-algebra homomorphism that sends each indeterminate

to the
\left(i,j\right)
-th entry

of

. This homomorphism

\mathbf{A}
to the corresponding entry of

to the corresponding

\displaystyle f\left(P_{n,i,m}\left(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{p}_{2^{n}}\right)\right)

Proof 4.1

(Proof of Theorem
2.2
.)
.

We must prove that all diagonal entries of

A^{m}
belong to
\mathcal{P}
. In other words, we must prove that
\left(A^{m}\right)_{i,i}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
i\in\left[n\right]
.

It is well-known that a polynomial over a matrix ring is “essentially the same as” a matrix with polynomial entries.
In other words, we can identify the ring
R^{n\times n}\left[t\right]

with the ring
\left(R\left[t\right]\right)^{n\times n}
using a
straightforward ring isomorphism (which sends each
\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}C_{i}t^{i}\in R^{n\times n}\left[t\right]
to
\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}C_{i}t^{i}\in\left(R\left[t\right]\right)^{n\times n}
). In the same way, we identify the
ring
R^{n\times n}\left[\left[t\right]\right]
with the ring
\left(R\left[\left[t\right]\right]\right)^{n\times n}
.

Let
B
be the matrix
I_{n}-tA
in the power series ring
R^{n\times n}\left[\left[t\right]\right]
. This matrix
B=I_{n}-tA
is
invertible, and its inverse is

B^{-1}=I_{n}+tA+t^{2}A^{2}+t^{3}A^{3}+\cdots.

(5)

(This can be proved by directly verifying that
I_{n}+tA+t^{2}A^{2}+t^{3}A^{3}+\cdots
is inverse to
I_{n}-tA
. Indeed, both products
\left(I_{n}+tA+t^{2}A^{2}+t^{3}A^{3}+\cdots\right)\cdot\left(I_{n}-tA\right)

and
\left(I_{n}-tA\right)\cdot\left(I_{n}+tA+t^{2}A^{2}+t^{3}A^{3}+\cdots\right)
turn, upon expanding, into sums that telescope to

I_{n}
.)

Since the matrix
B
is invertible, its determinant
\det B
is invertible as
well (since
\left(\det B\right)\cdot\left(\det\left(B^{-1}\right)\right)=\det\left(\underbrace{BB^{-1}}_{=I_{n}}\right)=\det\left(I_{n}\right)=1
).

Now, recall that we must prove that
\left(A^{m}\right)_{i,i}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
i\in\left[n\right]
. So let us fix

. Then, (
5
) yields

\displaystyle\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\left(I_{n}+tA+t^{2}A^{2}+t^{3}A^{3}+\cdots\right)_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\left(I_{n}\right)_{i,i}+tA_{i,i}+t^{2}\left(A^{2}\right)_{i,i}+t^{3}\left(A^{3}\right)_{i,i}+\cdots.

Hence, the

-coefficient of the power series
\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}\in R\left[\left[t\right]\right]
is
\left(A^{m}\right)_{i,i}
. Thus, in order to prove that
\left(A^{m}\right)_{i,i}\in\mathcal{P}
(which is our goal), it suffices to show that all coefficients
of the power series
\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}
belong to
\mathcal{P}
.
In other words, it suffices to show that
\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]

From (

, so that

Hence,

\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}=\dfrac{1}{\det B}\cdot\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}.

(6)

Our next goal is to show that both factors
\dfrac{1}{\det B}
and
\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}

\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]

From

\displaystyle\det B

\displaystyle=\det\left(-tA+1I_{n}\right)=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\det\left(\underbrace{\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(-tA\right)}_{=-t\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}A}\right)\cdot\underbrace{1^{n-\left|P\right|}}_{=1}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\begin{array}[c]{c}\text{by (\ref{eq.det(B+xI)0}), applied to }n\text{, }R\left[\left[t\right]\right]\text{, }-tA\text{ and }1\\
\text{instead of }m\text{, }S\text{, }B\text{ and }x\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\underbrace{\det\left(-t\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}A\right)}_{=\left(-t\right)^{\left|P\right|}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}A\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\left(-t\right)^{\left|P\right|}\underbrace{\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}A\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\in\mathcal{P}\\
\text{(since }\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}A\right)\text{ is}\\
\text{a principal

(9)

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{P}\left[t\right]\subseteq\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right].

constant term is the addend for
P=\varnothing
, but this addend is

\underbrace{\left(-t\right)^{\left|\varnothing\right|}}_{=1}\underbrace{\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{\varnothing}^{\varnothing}A\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=1\\
\text{(since the }0\times 0\text{-matrix}\\
\text{has determinant }1\text{)}\end{subarray}}=1
). Thus, this power
series is invertible in
\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]
.
Therefore,

\dfrac{1}{\det B}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right].

(10)

Now, recall the definition of an adjugate matrix. This definition yields

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(-1\right)^{i+i}}_{=1}\det\left(B_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)=\det\left(B_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\left(-tA+1I_{n}\right)_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\text{since }B=-tA+1I_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(-tA_{\sim i,\sim i}+1I_{n-1}\right)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\text{since }\left(-tA+1I_{n}\right)_{\sim i,\sim i}=-tA_{\sim i,\sim i}+1I_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n-1\right]}\det\left(\underbrace{\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(-tA_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)}_{=-t\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)}\right)\cdot\underbrace{1^{n-1-\left|P\right|}}_{=1}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\begin{array}[c]{c}\text{by (\ref{eq.det(B+xI)0}), applied to }n-1\text{, }R\left[\left[t\right]\right]\text{, }-tA_{\sim i,\sim i}\text{ and }1\\
\text{instead of }m\text{, }S\text{, }B\text{ and }x\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n-1\right]}\underbrace{\det\left(-t\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right)}_{=\left(-t\right)^{\left|P\right|}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n-1\right]}\left(-t\right)^{\left|P\right|}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right).

(13)

Now, let
P
be an arbitrary subset of
\left[n-1\right]
. Write this
subset
P
in the form
P=\left\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r}\right\}
, where

p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}
. Furthermore, let

be the element that satisfies

p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{g}<i\leq p_{g+1}<p_{g+2}<\cdots<p_{r}.

(Here,

will be

if all elements of
P
are
\geq i
, and
g
will be

if all elements of
P
are
<i

removing rows and columns, we have

\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)=\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P^{\prime}}^{P^{\prime}}A,

where
P^{\prime}
is the subset
\left\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{g}\right\}\cup\left\{p_{g+1}+1,p_{g+2}+1,\ldots,p_{r}+1\right\}
of
\left[n\right]
. Hence,
\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)
is a principal submatrix of
A
. Therefore, its
determinant
\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right)
is a principal minor of
A
, thus belongs to

\mathcal{P}
.

Forget that we fixed
P
. We thus have shown that
\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right)\in\mathcal{P}
for each

. Therefore,
(

\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n-1\right]}\left(-t\right)^{\left|P\right|}\underbrace{\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A_{\sim i,\sim i}\right)\right)}_{\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{P}\left[t\right]\subseteq\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right].

(14)

Now, (

\left(B^{-1}\right)_{i,i}=\underbrace{\dfrac{1}{\det B}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]\\
\text{(by (\ref{sol.1/detB-in}))}\end{subarray}}\cdot\underbrace{\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]\\
\text{(by
(\ref{sol.adjBii-in}))}\end{subarray}}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]\cdot\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]\subseteq\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right].

As explained above, this completes our proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Somewhat regrettably, the above proof is the slickest I am aware of. A

Variants

A counterpart of Theorem
2.2

exists as well:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

,

,

,

and

.

Let

. An
\left(i,j\right)

of

shall mean a determinant of

\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{I}^{J}A\right)
, where

and

Proof 5.2

(Proof outline.)
.

This is similar to our above proof of Theorem
2.2
,
but some changes are needed. Most importantly, instead of proving that

\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,i}\in\mathcal{P}\left[\left[t\right]\right]
, we now need to show that
\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,j}\in\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\left[\left[t\right]\right]
(that
is, that all coefficients of the power series
\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{j,i}
belong to
\mathcal{K}_{i,j}
). To do so, we apply the
definition of the adjugate matrix to see that

\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,j}=\left(-1\right)^{j+i}\det\left(B_{\sim j,\sim i}\right).

(15)

We can simplify
B_{\sim j,\sim i}
further to
-tA_{\sim j,\sim i}+\left(I_{n}\right)_{\sim j,\sim i}
(since
B=I_{n}-tA=-tA+I_{n}
), but
unfortunately this is not the same as
-tA_{\sim j,\sim i}+1I_{n-1}
, and thus
we can no longer apply (
4
). Instead, we use a trick:

•

We define
A^{\prime}
to be the matrix obtained from
-tA
by replacing
the
j
-th row by
\left(0,0,\ldots,0,1,0,0,\ldots,0\right)
, where the
only entry equal to
1
is in the
i
-th position.

•

We define
I_{n}^{\prime}
to be the matrix obtained from
I_{n}
by
replacing the
1
in the
j
-th row by a
0
.

•

We define
B^{\prime}
to be the matrix obtained from
B
by replacing
the
j
-th row by
\left(0,0,\ldots,0,1,0,0,\ldots,0\right)
, where the
only entry equal to
1
is in the
i
-th position.

Laplace expansion along the
j
-th row shows that

\det\left(B^{\prime}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{j+i}\det\left(\left(B^{\prime}\right)_{\sim j,\sim i}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{j+i}\det\left(B_{\sim j,\sim i}\right)

(since the matrix
B^{\prime}
differs from
B
only in the
j
-th row, and
thus we have
\left(B^{\prime}\right)_{\sim j,\sim i}=B_{\sim j,\sim i}
).
Comparing this with (
15
), we find

\left(\operatorname*{adj}B\right)_{i,j}=\det\left(B^{\prime}\right).

(16)

Furthermore, recall that
B=I_{n}-tA=-tA+I_{n}
. Thus,
B^{\prime}=A^{\prime}+I_{n}^{\prime}
(based on how
A^{\prime}
,
I_{n}^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
were constructed).

On the other hand, the definition of
I_{n}^{\prime}
shows that

I_{n}^{\prime}
is a diagonal
n\times n
-matrix with diagonal entries

1,1,\ldots,1,0,1,1,\ldots,1
, where the only diagonal entry equal to
0
is
in the
j
-th position. However, another classical fact about determinants
(
[
7
, Theorem 6.4.26]
,
[
5
, Corollary 6.162]
) shows that if

C
is any
n\times n
-matrix, and if
D
is a diagonal
n\times n
-matrix
with diagonal entries
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}
, then

\det\left(C+D\right)=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}C\right)\cdot\prod\limits_{k\in\left[n\right]\setminus P}d_{k}.

We can apply this to
C=A^{\prime}
and
D=I_{n}^{\prime}
and
\left(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}\right)=\underbrace{\left(1,1,\ldots,1,0,1,1,\ldots,1\right)}_{\text{the }0\text{ is in the }j\text{-th
position}}
, and thus obtain

\displaystyle\det\left(A^{\prime}+I_{n}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)\cdot\prod\limits_{k\in\left[n\right]\setminus P}\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }k\neq j;\\
0,&\text{if }k=j\end{cases}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\text{since the }k\text{-th

0,&\text{if }k=j\end{cases}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\subseteq\left[n\right]}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)\cdot\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }j\notin\left[n\right]\setminus P;\\
0,&\text{if }j\in\left[n\right]\setminus P\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\
j\notin\left[n\right]\setminus P\end{subarray}}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\
j\in P\end{subarray}}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\
j\in P\text{ and }i\in P\end{subarray}}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\
j\in P\text{ and
}i\notin P\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=0\\
\text{(since all

\text{are }0\text{ except for

\text{the matrix }\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\text{ has a zero row)}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\
j\in P\text{ and }i\in P\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P}^{P}\left(A^{\prime}\right)\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=\pm\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P\setminus\left\{j\right\}}^{P\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(-tA\right)\right)\\
\text{(by Laplace expansion along the

\text{that was the }j\text{-th row of }A^{\prime}\text{)}\end{subarray}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\

\text{(since
}\det\left(\operatorname*{sub}\nolimits_{P\setminus\left\{j\right\}}^{P\setminus\left\{i\right\}}A\right)\text{ is}\\
\text{an }\left(i,j\right)\text{-quasiprincipal minor of }A\text{)}\end{subarray}}\in\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subseteq\left[n\right];\\

Follow the above proof of Theorem

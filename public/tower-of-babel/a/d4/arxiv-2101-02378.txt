Exact uniform modulus of continuity and Chung’s LIL for the generalized fractional Brownian motion Thanks: *Corresponding author. E-mail: xiaoy@msu.edu.
Ran Wang, Yimin Xiao*

Abstract




Abstract:

The generalized fractional Brownian motion (GFBM)
X:=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with parameters
\gamma\in[0,1)

and
\alpha\in\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2},\,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)
is a centered Gaussian

H
-self-similar process introduced by Pang and Taqqu (2019)
as the scaling limit of power-law shot noise processes, where
H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\in(0,1)
.
When
\gamma=0
,
X
is the ordinary fractional Brownian motion. When
\gamma\in(0,1)
, GFBM
X

does not have stationary increments, and its sample path properties such as Hölder continuity, path
differentiability/non-differentiability, and the functional law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) have been investigated
recently by Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu (2021). They mainly focused on sample path properties that are described in
terms of the self-similarity index
H
(e.g., LILs at infinity or at the origin).

In this paper, we further study the sample path properties of GFBM
X
and establish the exact uniform modulus
of continuity, small ball probabilities, and Chung’s laws of iterated logarithm at any fixed point
t>0
. Our
results show that the local regularity properties away from the origin and fractal properties of GFBM
X
are
determined by the index
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, instead of the self-similarity index
H
. This is in contrast with the
properties of ordinary fractional Brownian motion whose local and asymptotic properties are determined by the
single index
H
.

Keyword:
Gaussian self-similar process; generalized fractional Brownian motion; exact uniform modulus
of continuity; small ball probability; Chung’s LIL; tangent process; Lamperti’s transformation.

MSC:
60G15, 60G17, 60G18, 60G22.

1.
Introduction and main results

The generalized fractional Brownian motion (GFBM, for short)
X:=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a centered Gaussian self-similar
process introduced by Pang and Taqqu
[
28
]
as the scaling limit of power-law shot noise processes. It has
the following stochastic integral representation:

\displaystyle\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-u)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-u)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)|u|^{-\gamma/2}B(du)\right\}_{t\geq 0},

(1.1)

where the parameters
\gamma
and
\alpha
satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma\in[0,1),\ \ \alpha\in\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2},\ \frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right),

(1.2)

and where
B(du)
is a Gaussian random measure in
\mathbb{R}
with the Lebesgue control measure
du
.
It follows that the Gaussian process
X
is self-similar with index
H
given by

\displaystyle H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\in(0,1).

(1.3)

When
\gamma=0
,
X
becomes an ordinary fractional Brownian motion (FBM)
B^{H}
which
can be represented as:

\displaystyle\left\{B^{H}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{d}{=}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-u)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-u)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\right)B(du)\right\}_{t\geq 0}.

(1.4)

However, when
\gamma\neq 0
,
X
does not have the property of stationary increments.

Fractional Brownian motion
B^{H}
has been studied extensively in the literature. It is well known that
B^{H}
arises
naturally as the scaling limit of many interesting stochastic systems. For example,
[
17
]
or

[
30
, Chapter 3.4]
showed that the scaled power-law Poisson shot noise process with stationary
increments converges to
B^{H}
. Pang and Taqqu
[
28
]
studied a class of integrated shot-noise processes
with power-law non-stationary conditional variance functions and proved in their Theorem 3.1 that the corresponding
scaled process converges weakly to GFBM
X
.

As shown by Pang and Taqqu
[
28
]
, GFBM
X
is a natural generalization of the ordinary FBM.
It preserves the self-similarity property while the factor
|u|^{-\gamma/2}
introduces non-stationarity of increments,
which is useful for reflecting the non-stationarity in physical stochastic systems. Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
11
]

raised the interesting question: “How does the parameter
\gamma
affect the sample path properties of GFBM?”
They proved in
[
11
]
that, for any
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, the sample paths of
X
are Hölder continuous in
[0,T]
of
order
H-\varepsilon
and the functional and local laws of the iterated logarithm of
X
are determined by the self-similarity
index
H
. More recently, Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
12
]
studied the semimartingale properties of
GFBM
X
and its mixtures and applied them to model the volatility processes in finance.

In this paper, we study precise local sample path properties of GFBM
X
, including the exact uniform modulus of
continuity, small ball probabilities, Chung’s law of iterated logarithm at any fixed point
t>0
and the tangent
processes. Our main results
are Theorems
1.1
-
1.6
below. They show that the local regularity properties of GFBM
X

away from the origin are determined by the index
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, instead of the self-similarity index
H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
. Our results also imply that the fractal properties of GFBM
X
are determined by

\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, see Remarks
3.1
(ii) and
7.1
below. This is in contrast with the ordinary fractional
Brownian motion whose local, fractal, and asymptotic properties are determined by the single index
H
.
We remark that our results are also useful for studying other fine sample path properties of GFBM
X
. For example,
one can determine the exact Hausdorff measure functions for various fractals generated by the sample paths, and
prove sharp Hölder conditions and tail probability estimates for the local times of GFBM
X
as in, e.g.,

[
32
,
34
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
.

The first result is related to Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 of Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
11
]
and
provides the exact uniform moduli of continuity for
X
and its derivative
X^{\prime}
(when it exists)
in
[a,b]
, where
0<a<b<\infty
are constants.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X:=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be the GFBM defined in (
1.1
) and let
0<a<b<\infty
be constants.

(a).

If
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
, then there exists a constant
\kappa_{1}\in(0,\,\infty)

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}\frac{|X(t+h)-X(t)|}{h^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\ln h^{-1}}}=\kappa_{1},\ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.5)

(b).

If
\alpha=1/2
, then there exists a constant
\kappa_{2}\in(0,\,\infty)

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}\frac{|X(t+h)-X(t)|}{h\ln h^{-1}}\leq\kappa_{2},\ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.6)

(c).

If
\alpha\in(1/2,1/2+\gamma)
, then
X
has a modification that
is continuously differentiable on
[a,b]
and its derivative
X^{\prime}
satisfies that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}\frac{|X^{\prime}(t+h)-X^{\prime}(t)|}{h^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\ln h^{-1}}}=\kappa_{3},\ \ \ \text{a.s.},

(1.7)

where
\kappa_{3}\in(0,\,\infty)
is a constant.

Remark 1.1
.

(i).

Theorem 3.1 in
[
11
]
states that, for all
\varepsilon>0
,
X
has a modification
that satisfies the uniform Hölder condition in
[0,T]
of order
\alpha-\gamma/2+1/2-\varepsilon
.
Our Theorem
1.1
shows that the sample path of
X
on any interval
[a,\,b]
with
a>0

is smoother than its behavior at
t=0
, which is determined by the self-similarity index
H=\alpha-\gamma/2+1/2
as suggested by

\mathbb{E}\left[X(t)^{2}\right]=c(\alpha,\gamma)t^{2H},

with

\displaystyle c(\alpha,\gamma)=\mathcal{B}(2\alpha+1,1-\gamma)+\int_{0}^{\infty}((1+u)^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha})^{2}u^{-\gamma}du.

(1.8)

Here and below,
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Beta function.

(ii).

We believe that the equality in (
1.6
) holds. However, we have not been able to prove this. The
reason is that, when
\alpha=1/2
, the lower bounds in Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2

are different. Similarly, the case of
\alpha=1/2
is excluded in Theorems
1.2
-
1.4
below.

The next two results are on the small ball probabilities of
X
. They show a clear difference for the
two cases of
s\in[0,r]

and
s\in[t-r,t+r]
with
t>r>0
. The small ball probabilities are not only useful for proving Chung’s law
of iterated logarithm (Chung’s LIL, in short) in Theorem
1.4
but also have many other applications.
We refer to Li and Shao
[
21
]
for more information.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,\,1/2)
. Then there exist constants
\kappa_{4},\kappa_{5}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{4}\Big(\frac{r^{H}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,r]}|X(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{5}\Big(\frac{r^{H}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg),

(1.9)

where
H=\alpha-\gamma/2+1/2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(a).

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,\,1/2)
. Then
there exist constants
\kappa_{6},\kappa_{7}\in(0,\infty)

such that for all
t>0,r\in(0,t/2)
and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,r^{\alpha+1/2}\right)
,

\begin{split}\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{6}\,r\,c_{1}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg)\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|h|\leq r}|X(t+h)-X(t)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\\
&\,\leq\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{7}\,r\,c_{2}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg),\end{split}

(1.10)

where
c_{1}(t)=\max\left\{t^{\alpha-\gamma/2-1/2},t^{-\gamma/(2\alpha+1)}\right\}
and
c_{2}(t)=t^{-\gamma/(2\alpha+1)}
.

(b).

Assume
\alpha\in(1/2,\,1/2+\gamma/2)
. Then there
exist constants
\kappa_{8},\kappa_{9}\in(0,\infty)

such that for all
t>0,r\in(0,t/2)
and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,r^{\alpha-1/2}\right)
,

\begin{split}\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{8}\,r\,c_{3}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}}\bigg)\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|h|\leq r}|X^{\prime}(t+h)-X^{\prime}(t)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\\
&\,\leq\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{9}\,r\,c_{4}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}}\bigg),\end{split}

(1.11)

where
c_{3}(t)=\max\left\{t^{\alpha-\gamma/2-3/2},t^{-\gamma/(2\alpha-1)}\right\}
and
c_{4}(t)=t^{-\gamma/(2\alpha-1)}
.

The following are Chung’s laws of the iterated logarithm for
X
and
X^{\prime}
. It is interesting to notice that
the parameters
\gamma
and
\alpha
play different roles. Since
X
and

do not have stationary

\gamma>0

(

) and (

) show that the oscillations decrease at the rate

as

the aforementioned question of Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
11

regarding the effect of the parameter

\gamma
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(a).

If
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
, then there exists a constant
\kappa_{10}\in(0,\infty)

such that for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|X(t+h)-X(t)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}=\kappa_{10}t^{-\gamma/2},\ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.12)

(b).

If
\alpha\in(1/2,\,1/2+\gamma/2)
, then there exists a constant
\kappa_{11}\in(0,\infty)

such that for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|X^{\prime}(t+h)-X^{\prime}(t)|}{r^{\alpha-1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha-1/2}}=\kappa_{11}t^{-\gamma/2},\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.13)

Remark 1.2
.

From the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
, we know that if
\sup_{|h|\leq r}
is
replaced by
\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}

\begin{split}X(t)&=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left((t-u)^{\alpha}-(-u)^{\alpha}\right)(-u)^{-\gamma/2}B(du)+\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\gamma/2}B(du)\\

(b).

Assume

0<s<t<\infty
,

\displaystyle c_{2,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{t^{2-2H}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Y(t)-Y(s)\right)^{2}\right]\leq c_{2,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{s^{2-2H}},

(2.2)

where
c_{2,1}=\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+u)^{2\alpha-2}u^{-\gamma}du
.

Proof.

For any
0<s<t<\infty
, by Taylor’s formula,
there exists a function
\{\theta(u)\}_{u\in(0,\infty)}

valued in
(0,1)
such that for any
u>0
,

\begin{split}(t+u)^{\alpha}-(s+u)^{\alpha}=&\alpha\left[s+u+\theta(u)(t-s)\right]^{\alpha-1}(t-s).\end{split}

(2.3)

Thus, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Y(t)-Y(s)\right)^{2}\right]=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[(t+u)^{\alpha}-(s+u)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}u^{-\gamma}du\\
\geq&\,\alpha^{2}(t-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t+u)^{2(\alpha-1)}u^{-\gamma}du\\
=&\,\alpha^{2}(t-s)^{2}t^{2\alpha-\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+v)^{2\alpha-2}v^{-\gamma}dv\\
=&\,c_{2,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{t^{2-2H}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we can prove that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Y(t)-Y(s)\right)^{2}\right]\leq c_{2,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{s^{2-2H}}.

This proves (
2.2
).
∎

By Lemma
2.1
, the Gaussian property of
Y
, and the Kolmogorov continuity theorem
(see, e.g.,
[
16
, Theorem C.6]
), we know that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
Y
has a modification that is
Hölder continuous with index
1-\varepsilon
on any interval
[a,b]
with
0<a<b
. We will apply this fact in Section 7
to prove Theorems
1.1
,
1.4
-
1.6
, from the results on the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z
.

In the following, we prove the differentiability of
Y
by using the argument in the proof of Lemma 3.6 in
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
-1/2+\gamma/2<\alpha<1/2+\gamma/2
. The Gaussian process
Y=\{Y(t)\}_{t\geq 0}

has a modification (still denoted by
Y
) that is infinitely differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

For any
t>0
, define

Y^{\prime}(t):=\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{0}(t-u)^{\alpha-1}(-u)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}B(du).

(2.4)

The integrand is in
L^{2}((-\infty,0);\mathbb{R})
, and hence
\{Y^{\prime}(t)\}_{t>0}
is a well-defined mean-zero Gaussian process.
For every
s,t\in[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
with
s<t
, applying Taylor’s formula (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Y^{\prime}(t)-Y^{\prime}(s)\right|^{2}\right]\\
=\,&\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(t+u)^{\alpha-1}-(s+u)^{\alpha-1}\right|^{2}u^{-\gamma}du\\
\leq\,&\alpha^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\left[\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\alpha-4}u^{-\gamma}du+\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{2\alpha-4-\gamma}du\right]\cdot|t-s|^{2}\\
\leq\,&\alpha^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\left(\frac{a^{2\alpha-4}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{1}{2\alpha-3-\gamma}\right)|t-s|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.5)

This, together with the Kolmogorov continuity theorem and the arbitrariness of
a
and
b
,
implies that
Y^{\prime}
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
up to a modification.

Assume that
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
(the space of all infinitely differentiable functions with compact supports).
By the stochastic Fubini theorem
[
16
, Corollary 2.9]
and the formula of integration by parts, we know a.s.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}Y^{\prime}(t)\phi(t)dt&=\int_{-\infty}^{0}B(du)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[((t-u)^{\alpha}-(-u)^{\alpha})u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right]\phi(t)dt\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}B(du)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[((t-u)^{\alpha}-(-u)^{\alpha})u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right]\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)dt.\end{split}

(2.6)

Applying the stochastic Fubini theorem again, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}Y^{\prime}(t)\phi(t)dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}Y(t)\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)dt,\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ a.s.}

(2.7)

This means that
Y^{\prime}(t)
is the weak derivative of
Y(t)
for all
t>0
. Since
Y^{\prime}
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
,
(
2.7
) shows that
Y^{\prime}
is in fact almost surely the ordinary derivative of
Y
in
(0,\infty)
. By induction,
we know that
Y
is infinitely differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.
∎

3.
Moment estimates for the increments and one-sided SLND of
Z

Consider the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z=\{Z(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with indices
\alpha
and
\gamma

defined by

\displaystyle Z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\gamma/2}B(du).

(3.1)

This Gaussian process is well defined if the constants
\alpha
and
\gamma
satisfy
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}

and
\gamma<1
and is self-similar with index
H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
. Notice that
H\leq 0
if

-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}
and

H>0
if
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}
.

In this section, we derive optimal estimates on the moment of the increments, prove the existence of the tangent process
and establish the one-sided
strong local nondeterminism for
Z
. These properties are useful for studying sample properties of
Z
.

3.1.
Moment estimates

In the following, Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
provide
optimal estimates on
\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]
. These estimates are essential for
establishing sharp sample path properties of
Z
.
Notice that the upper bounds in (i) and (ii) in Lemma
3.1
below are the same (up to a
constant factor) when
\alpha<1/2
. We will use these bounds for estimating the small ball
probability and the uniform modulus of continuity in Sections 4 and 7.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2\,,1/2]
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. The following statements hold:

(i).

If
0<s<t<\infty
and
0<s\leq 2(t-s)
, then

c_{3,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{t^{\gamma}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,2}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}},

(3.2)

here
c_{3,1}=1/(2\alpha+1)
and
c_{3,2}=2/(1-\gamma)+1/(2\alpha+1)+\mathcal{B}(2\alpha+1,1-\gamma)2^{2\alpha+1}
.

(ii).

If
0<s<t<\infty
and
s>2(t-s)
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}},&\hbox{ if }\ \alpha<1/2,\\
\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\left(1+\ln\big|\frac{s}{t-s}\big|\right),&\hbox{ if }\ \alpha=1/2.\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

Here and below, for two real-valued functions
f
and
g
defined on a set
I
, the notation
f\asymp g

means that

c\leq f(x)/g(x)\leq c^{\prime}\ \ \hbox{ for all }\,x\in I,

for some positive and finite constants
c
and
c^{\prime}
which may depend on
f,\,g
and
I
.

Proof.

For any
0<s<t<\infty
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\\
=&\,\int_{0}^{s}\left((t-u)^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}\right)^{2}u^{-\gamma}du+\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{2\alpha}u^{-\gamma}du\\
=&:\,I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(3.4)

To bound the integral
I_{1}
, we make a change of variable with
u=s-(t-s)v
to obtain

I_{1}=(t-s)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}dv.

(3.5)

In order to estimate
I_{1}
in Case (i), we distinguish the two cases
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
and
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
.

When
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
, we use the fact that

3^{\alpha}-2^{\alpha}\leq(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\leq 1
for all
v\in[0,2]
to derive

\begin{split}I_{1}\leq&\,\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}dv\\
=&\,\frac{s^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha}\\
\leq&\,\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\frac{\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

When
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
, we use the fact that
0<v^{\alpha}-(1+v)^{\alpha}<v^{\alpha}
for all
v\in[0,2]

to derive

\begin{split}I_{1}\leq&\,\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}v^{2\alpha}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}dv\\
=&\,s^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{0}^{1}v^{2\alpha}(1-v)^{-\gamma}dv\\
\leq&\,\mathcal{B}(2\alpha+1,1-\gamma)2^{2\alpha+1}\frac{\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}}.\end{split}

(3.7)

In the above, we made a change of variable and the assumption that
s\leq 2(t-s)
.

For the integral
I_{2}
in (
3.4
), by the change of variable
u=s+(t-s)v
, we have

\begin{split}I_{2}&=(t-s)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-v)^{2\alpha}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}+v\Big)^{-\gamma}\,dv\\
&\leq(t-s)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}\Big)^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}(1-v)^{2\alpha}dv\\
&=\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}\frac{(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

On the other hand, in Case (i),
\big(\frac{s}{t-s}+v\big)^{-\gamma}\geq\big(\frac{t}{t-s}\big)^{-\gamma}
for
all
v\in[0,1]
. Hence,

\begin{split}I_{2}\geq(t-s)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\Big(\frac{t}{t-s}\Big)^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}(1-v)^{2\alpha}dv=\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}\frac{(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}}{t^{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

(3.9)

Consequently, the lower bound in (
3.2
) follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.9
), and
the upper bound in (
3.2
) follows from (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and
(
3.8
).

Now we consider Case (ii). Since
s>2(t-s)
, we write

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}dv\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \quad\ \ \ \ \ \ \qquad\ \ \ +\int_{1}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}dv\Bigg)\\
&=:\big(t-s\big)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\big(I_{1,1}+I_{1,2}\big).\end{split}

(3.10)

We will see that the main term is the integral
I_{1,2}
. For estimating the integral
I_{1,1}
, again
we distinguish the two cases
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
and
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
.

When
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
, we use the facts that

2^{\alpha}-1\leq(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\leq 1
for all
v\in[0,1]

and

\frac{s}{2(t-s)}\leq\frac{s}{t-s}-v\leq\frac{s}{t-s},\quad\forall\,v\in[0,1],

(3.11)

to derive that

\begin{split}\left(2^{\alpha}-1\right)^{2}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s}\Big)^{\gamma}\leq I_{1,1}\leq 2^{\gamma}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s}\Big)^{\gamma}.\end{split}

(3.12)

When
\alpha\in(-1/2,0)
, we use the fact that
(1-2^{\alpha})v^{\alpha}\leq v^{\alpha}-(1+v)^{\alpha}\leq v^{\alpha}
for
v\in[0,1]
and (
3.11
) to get

\begin{split}\frac{\left(1-2^{\alpha}\right)^{2}}{2\alpha+1}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s}\Big)^{\gamma}\leq I_{1,1}\leq\frac{2^{\gamma}}{2\alpha+1}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s}\Big)^{\gamma}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Next we estimate the integral
I_{1,2}
. For
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
, we use the inequality
|(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}|\asymp v^{\alpha-1}
for all
v\in[1,\infty)
to derive

\begin{split}I_{1,2}&\asymp\int_{1}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}v^{2(\alpha-1)}\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\Big)^{-\gamma}\,dv\\
&=\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}\Big)^{2\alpha-1-\gamma}\int^{1}_{\frac{t-s}{s}}w^{2(\alpha-1)}\left(1-w\right)^{-\gamma}\,dw,\end{split}

(3.14)

where the above equality is obtained by the change of variable
v=\frac{s}{t-s}w
. By splitting the last
integral over the intervals
[\frac{t-s}{s},\frac{3}{4}]
and
[\frac{3}{4},1]
, we have

\begin{split}I_{1,2}&\asymp\Big(\frac{s}{t-s}\Big)^{2\alpha-1-\gamma}\bigg(\int^{\frac{3}{4}}_{\frac{t-s}{s}}w^{2(\alpha-1)}dw+\int^{1}_{\frac{3}{4}}w^{2(\alpha-1)}\left(1-w\right)^{-\gamma}\,dw\bigg)\\
&\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\big(\frac{t-s}{s}\big)^{\gamma},&\hbox{ if }-1/2<\alpha<1/2,\\
\big(\frac{t-s}{s}\big)^{\gamma}\left(1+\ln\big|\frac{s}{t-s}\big|\right),&\hbox{ if }\alpha=1/2.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.15)

Combining (
3.10
)-(
3.15
) yields that in Case (ii),

I_{1}\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}},&\hbox{ if }-1/2<\alpha<1/2,\\
\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\left(1+\ln\big|\frac{s}{t-s}\big|\right),&\hbox{ if }\alpha=1/2.\end{array}\right.

(3.16)

It follows from this and (
3.4
) that

\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\geq c_{3,3}\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}},&\hbox{ if }-1/2<\alpha<1/2,\\
\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\left(1+\ln\big|\frac{s}{t-s}\big|\right),&\hbox{ if }\alpha=1/2,\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

where
c_{3,3}>0
is a finite constant.

On the other hand, it follows from (
3.4
), (
3.8
) and (
3.16
) that

\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,4}\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}},&\hbox{ if }\ -1/2<\alpha<1/2,\\
\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\left(1+\ln\big|\frac{s}{t-s}\big|\right),&\hbox{ if }\ \alpha=1/2,\end{array}\right.

(3.18)

where
c_{3,4}>0
is a finite constant. This finishes the proof of (
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
.
Then for every fixed
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]}{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}=c_{3,9}t^{-\gamma},\end{split}

(3.23)

where
c_{3,9}=\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\big]^{2}dv
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider the limit in (
3.23
) as
s\uparrow t
only. For
0<s<t<\infty
,
recall from (
3.4
) the decomposition of
\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]
.

The term
I_{2}
is easier and we handle it first. Since
u^{-\gamma}\in[t^{-\gamma},s^{-\gamma}]
for any
u\in[s,t]
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{t^{\gamma}}\leq I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}}.

(3.24)

This implies that

\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{I_{2}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}=\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}t^{-\gamma}.

(3.25)

For the integral
I_{1}
, we use (
3.5
) and write it as

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\,\int_{0}^{s}\left[(t-u)^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}u^{-\gamma}du\\
=&\,(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
=&(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{2(t-s)}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +(t-s)^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}\int_{\frac{s}{2(t-s)}}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\left(\frac{s}{t-s}-v\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
=:&\,J_{1,1}+J_{1,2}.\\
\end{split}

(3.26)

Notice that, for every
v\in\big(0,s/[2(t-s)]\big)
, we have
s^{-\gamma}\leq\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}\leq 2^{\gamma}s^{-\gamma}
.
Thus the dominated convergence theorem gives

\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{J_{1,1}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}=t^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}dv.

(3.27)

On the other hand, we use the inequality
|(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}|\leq|\alpha|v^{\alpha-1}
for all
v\geq 1
to obtain

\begin{split}J_{1,2}=&\,(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}\int_{\frac{s}{2(t-s)}}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
\leq&\,(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}\int_{\frac{s}{2(t-s)}}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\alpha^{2}v^{2\alpha-2}\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
\leq&\,\alpha^{2}(t-s)^{2\alpha+1}\left(\frac{s}{2(t-s)}\right)^{2\alpha-2}\int_{\frac{s}{2(t-s)}}^{\frac{s}{t-s}}\left(s-v(t-s)\right)^{-\gamma}dv\\
=&\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^{2\alpha-1-\gamma}\,(t-s)^{2}.\end{split}

It follows that

\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{J_{1,2}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}=0.

(3.28)

Therefore, (
3.23
) follows from (
3.25
), (
3.26
), (
3.27
) and
(
3.28
). The proof is complete.
∎

The following lemma deals with the case when

. The latter estimate

\{Z(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has a modification whose sample functions are continuously
differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.

For simplicity, we only consider the case when
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
and
s,t
stay away from the origin.
This is sufficient for our study of the sample path properties of GFBM
X
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. For any
0<a<b<\infty
, it holds that for any

s,t\in[a,\,b]
,

c_{3,10}|t-s|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,11}|t-s|^{2},

(3.29)

here
c_{3,10}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\gamma}b^{2\alpha-2}a^{1-\gamma}
and
c_{3,11}=\alpha^{2}a^{2\alpha-1-\gamma}\mathcal{B}(2\alpha-1,1-\gamma)
.

The process
\{Z(t)\}_{t\in[a,b]}
has a modification, which is still denoted by
Z
, such that its derivative process

\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}_{t\in[a,b]}
is continuous almost surely. Furthermore, there exist constants

c_{3,12},c_{3,13}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
s,t\in[a,b]
,

c_{3,12}|t-s|^{2\alpha-1}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{\prime}(t)-Z^{\prime}(s))^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,13}|t-s|^{2\alpha-1}.

(3.30)

Proof.

The proof of (
3.29
) is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
. Here, we only
prove the lower bound in (
3.29
) and the existence of a modification of
Z

whose sample functions are continuously differentiable on
(0,\infty)
almost surely.

For any
s,t\in[a,b]
with
s<t
, by Taylor’s formula (
2.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\right]\geq&\int_{0}^{s}\left((t-u)^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}\right)^{2}u^{-\gamma}du\\
\geq&\,\alpha^{2}(t-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{s}(t-u)^{2\alpha-2}u^{-\gamma}du\\
\geq&\,\alpha^{2}(t-s)^{2}\,t^{2\alpha-2}\frac{s^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\\
\geq&\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\gamma}b^{2\alpha-2}a^{1-\gamma}(t-s)^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, the lower bound in (
3.29
) holds.

For any
t\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}(t):=\alpha\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\alpha-1}u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}B(du),

(3.31)

with
Z^{\prime}(0)=0
. Notice that, since
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
, the process
\left\{Z^{\prime}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a generalized
Riemann-Liouville FBM with indices
\alpha-1
and
\gamma
. It is self-similar with index
\tilde{H}=\alpha-1/2-\gamma/2
. Hence, by (
3.19
), we see that (
3.30
) holds. By the Kolmogorov
continuity theorem (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Theorem C.6]
), the Gaussian property of
Z^{\prime}
and the arbitrariness of
a
and
b
,
we know that
Z^{\prime}
has a modification (still denoted by
Z^{\prime}
) that is Hölder continuous in
(0,\infty)
with index

\alpha-1/2-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
. With this modification, we define a Gaussian process
\widetilde{Z}=\big\{\widetilde{Z}(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
by

\widetilde{Z}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{\prime}(s)ds,\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Then, by the stochastic Fubini theorem
[
16
, Corollary 2.9]
, we derive that for every
t\geq 0
,

\widetilde{Z}(t)=\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\int_{u}^{t}(s-u)^{\alpha-1}ds\bigg)u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,B(du)=Z(t),\quad\hbox{ a.s.}

Hence
\widetilde{Z}
is modification of the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z=\{Z(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
the sample function of
\widetilde{Z}
is a.s. continuously differentiable in
(0,\,\infty)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
The tangent process of
Z

Let
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
be the space of all continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}
,
endowed with the locally uniform convergence topology. We say that a family of stochastic processes

V_{n}=\{V_{n}(\tau)\}_{\tau\geq 0}
converges weakly (or in distribution) to
V=\{V(\tau)\}_{\tau\geq 0}
in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
,
if
\mathbb{E}[f(V_{n})]\rightarrow\mathbb{E}[f(V)]
for every bounded, continuous function
f:C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
7
]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. For any
t,\,u>0
, let

V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau):=\frac{Z(t+u\tau)-Z(t)}{u^{\alpha+1/2}},\ \ \ \tau\geq 0.

Then, as
u\rightarrow 0+
, the process
\big\{V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau)\big\}_{\tau\geq 0}
converges in distribution to

\big\{c_{3,9}^{1/2}t^{-\gamma/2}B^{\alpha+1/2}(\tau)\big\}_{\tau\geq 0}
in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
,
where
c_{3,9}=\frac{1}{2\alpha+1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[(1+v)^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}\big]^{2}dv
is the constant in
Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

By the self-similarity of
Z
and (
3.23
), we know that for any
t,s>0
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\big[Z(t)Z(s)\big]\\
=&\,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[Z(t)^{2}+Z(s)^{2}-(Z(t)-Z(s))^{2}\big]\\
=&\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathcal{B}(2\alpha+1,1-\gamma)\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}\right)-c_{3,9}t^{-\gamma}|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}\Big)+o(t^{-\gamma}|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}).\end{split}

(3.32)

For any
t>0,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 0
, by (
3.32
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow 0+}\mathbb{E}\left[V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau_{1})V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau_{2})\right]=\frac{c_{3,9}}{2}t^{-\gamma}\left(\tau_{1}^{2\alpha+1}+\tau_{2}^{2\alpha+1}-|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2\alpha+1}\right).

(3.33)

Hence, the Gaussian process
\{V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau)\}_{\tau\geq 0}
converges to
c_{3,9}^{1/2}t^{-\gamma/2}B^{\alpha+1/2}
in finite-dimensional distributions, as
u\rightarrow 0+
, where
B^{\alpha+1/2}
is a FBM with index
\alpha+1/2
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a constant
c_{3,14}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t>0,u>0,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau_{1})-V_{Z}^{t,u}(\tau_{2})\right)^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,14}t^{-\gamma}\left|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\right|^{2\alpha+1}.

(3.34)

Hence, by
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
, we know the family
\big\{V_{Z}^{t,u}\big\}_{u>0}
is tight in

C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
and then it converges in distribution in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
, as
u\rightarrow 0+
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.3.
One-sided strong local nondeterminism

We establish the following one-sided strong local nondeterminism (SLND, in short) for
Z
. This
property is essential for dealing with problems that involve joint distributions of random variables

Z(t_{1}),\ldots,Z(t_{n})
. From the proof, it is clear that GFBM
X
has the same SLND property.

Proposition 3.2
.

(a)

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. For any
constant
b>0
, it holds that for any
s,t\in[0,b]
with
s<t
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Z(t)|Z(r):r\leq s)\geq\frac{1}{(2\alpha+1)b^{\gamma}}|t-s|^{2\alpha+1},

(3.35)

where
\mathrm{Var}(Z(t)|Z(r):r\leq s)
denotes the conditional variance of
Z(t)
given the
\sigma
-algebra

\sigma(Z(r):r\leq s)
.

(b)

Assume
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
.
For any
b>0
, it holds that for any
s,t\in[0,b]
with
s<t
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(Z^{\prime}(t)|Z^{\prime}(r):r\leq s\right)\geq\frac{1}{(2\alpha-1)b^{\gamma}}|t-s|^{2\alpha-1}.

(3.36)

Proof.

Assume that
0\leq s<t\leq b
.
We write
Z(t)
as

\displaystyle Z(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}(t-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}B(du)+\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}B(du).

The first term is measurable with respect to
\sigma\big(B(r):r\leq s\big)
and the second term is independent of
\sigma\big(B(r):r\leq s\big)
.
Since
\sigma\big(Z(r):r\leq s\big)\subseteq\sigma\big(B(r):r\leq s\big)
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Z(t)|Z(r):r\leq s)\geq\,\mathrm{Var}(Z(t)|B(r):r\leq s)=\,\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{2\alpha}u^{-\gamma}du.

This implies (
3.35
).

The proof of (
3.36
) is similar. The details are omitted. The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Lamperti’s transformation of
Z

Inspired by
[
35
]
, we consider the centered stationary Gaussian process
U=\{U(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

defined through Lamperti’s transformation of
Z
:

\displaystyle U(t):=e^{-tH}Z(e^{t})\ \ \ \text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.1)

Let
r_{U}(t):=\mathbb{E}\big[U(0)U(t)\big]
be
the covariance function of
U
. By Bochner’s theorem,
r_{U}
is the Fourier transform of a finite measure
F_{U}

which is called the spectral measure of
U
. Notice that
r_{U}(t)
is an even function and

\displaystyle r_{U}(t)=

\displaystyle\,e^{-tH}\int_{0}^{1\wedge e^{t}}(e^{t}-u)^{\alpha}(1-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\gamma}du\ \ \ \ \text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.2)

We can verify that
r_{U}(t)=O(e^{-t(1-\gamma)/2})
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
It follows that
r_{U}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence the spectral measure
F_{U}
has a continuous spectral
density function
f_{U}
which can be represented as the inverse Fourier transform of
r_{U}(\cdot)
:

f_{U}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r_{U}(t)\cos(t\lambda)\,dt\quad\hbox{ for all }\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

It is known that
U
has the stochastic integral representation:

\displaystyle U(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\lambda t}\,W(d\lambda)\ \ \ \ \text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

(4.3)

where
W
is a complex Gaussian measure with control measure
F_{U}
. Then for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big[\big(U(s)-U(t)\big)^{2}\big]&=2\big(r_{U}(0)-r_{U}(t-s)\big)\\
&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[1-\cos\big((s-t)\lambda\big)\big]\,f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda.\end{split}

(4.4)

The following lemma provides bounds for
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(s)-U(t)\right)^{2}\right]
when
Z
has rough
(fractal) sample paths.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
. Then for any
b>0
, there exist positive constants
\varepsilon_{0}
,
c_{4,1}

and
c_{4,2}
such that for all
s,t\in[0,b]
with
|s-t|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

\begin{split}c_{4,1}|s-t|^{2\alpha+1}\left(1+\left|\ln|t-s|\right|\right)^{\eta}&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(s)-U(t)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq c_{4,2}|s-t|^{2\alpha+1}\left(1+\left|\ln|t-s|\right|\right)^{\eta},\end{split}

(4.5)

where
\eta=0
if
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2)
and
\eta=1
if
\alpha=1/2.

Proof.

Since
U
is stationary, it is sufficient to consider
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(t)-U(0)\right)^{2}\right]
for
t>0
.
It follows from (
4.1
) and the elementary inequality
(x+y)^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2})
that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(t)-U(0)\right)^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z(e^{t})-Z(1)+(e^{-tH}-1)Z(e^{t})\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq 2\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z(e^{t})-Z(1)\right)^{2}\right]+\left(e^{-tH}-1\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[Z(e^{t})^{2}\right]\right).\end{split}

(4.6)

This, together with Lemma
3.1
, implies that the upper bound in (
4.5
)
holds for all
s,t\in[0,b]
with
|s-t|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
for some positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
.

On the other hand, the first equation in (
4.6
) and the inequality
(x+y)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-y^{2}
imply

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(t)-U(0)\right)^{2}\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z(e^{t})-Z(1)\right)^{2}\right]-\left(e^{-tH}-1\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[Z(e^{t})^{2}\right].\\
\end{split}

(4.7)

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that the lower bounds in (
4.5
) holds if
t>0
is small enough, say,

0<t\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The following are truncation inequalities in Loéve
[
22
, Page 209]
that are expressed in terms of the
spectral density function
f_{U}
: for any
u>0
we have

\begin{split}\int_{|\lambda|<u}\ \lambda^{2}f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda&\leq Ku^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\cos(\lambda/u))f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda,\\
\int_{|\lambda|\geq u}\ f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda&\leq Ku\int_{0}^{1/u}dv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(1-\cos(v\lambda)\big)f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda.\end{split}

By these inequalities, (
4.4
) and the upper bound in Lemma
4.1
, we have the
following properties of the spectral density
f_{U}(\lambda)
at the origin and infinity, respectively.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
. There exist positive constants
u_{0}
,

c_{4,3}
and
c_{4,4}
such that for any
u>u_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<u}\lambda^{2}f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda\leq c_{4,3}u^{1-2\alpha}\left(1+|\ln u|\right)^{\eta}

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|\geq u}f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda\leq c_{4,4}u^{-(2\alpha+1)}\left(1+|\ln u|\right)^{\eta}.

(4.9)

In the above,
\eta=0
if
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2)
and
\eta=1
if

5.
Small ball probabilities of
Z

By Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
3.2
, we prove the following estimates
on the small ball probabilities of the Gaussian process
Z
and its derivative
Z^{\prime}
when it exists.

Proposition 5.1
.

(a).

Assume
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,\,1/2)
. There exist constants
c_{5,1},\,c_{5,2}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0,0<\varepsilon<1
,

\begin{split}\exp\bigg(-c_{5,1}\Big(\frac{r^{H}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,r]}|Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-c_{5,2}\Big(\frac{r^{H}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg).\end{split}

(5.1)

(b).

Assume
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\alpha\in(1/2+\gamma/2,3/2)
.
There exist constants
c_{5,3},c_{5,4}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0,0<\varepsilon<1
,

\begin{split}\exp\bigg(-c_{5,3}\Big(\frac{r^{\tilde{H}}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha-1}}\bigg)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,r]}|Z^{\prime}(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-c_{5,4}\Big(\frac{r^{\tilde{H}}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha-1}}\bigg),\end{split}

(5.2)

where
\tilde{H}=\alpha-\gamma/2-1/2
.

Remark 5.1
.

The following are two remarks about Proposition
5.1
.

•

Notice that the case of
\alpha=1/2
is excluded in Proposition
5.1
.
The reason is that, the bounds of the one-sided SLND in Proposition
3.2

and the optimal bounds in (
3.3
) do not coincide when
\alpha=1/2
. The method for proving (
5.1
) will not be able
to prove optimal upper and lower bounds in the case of
\alpha=1/2
.

•

In Part (b), we assume that the self-similarity index
\tilde{H}=\alpha-\gamma/2-1/2
of
Z^{\prime}

is positive. When
\tilde{H}\leq 0
, (
5.2
) does not hold. In fact, by using
\mathbb{E}[Z^{\prime}(t)^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[Z^{\prime}(1)^{2}]\,t^{2\tilde{H}}
, one sees that for any
0<\tau\leq r
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,r]}|Z^{\prime}(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\mathbb{P}\big\{|Z^{\prime}(\tau)|\leq\varepsilon\big\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{|Z^{\prime}(1)|\leq\tau^{-\tilde{H}}\varepsilon\right\}.

If
\tilde{H}<0
, then the last probability goes to 0 as
\tau\to 0
. This implies that for all
r>0
and
\varepsilon>0

we have
\mathbb{P}\big\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,r]}|Z^{\prime}(s)|\leq\varepsilon\big\}=0
.

When
\tilde{H}=0
, the self-similarity implies that the small ball probability in (
5.2
) does not depend
on
r
. We will let
r\to\infty
to show that this probability is in fact 0 for all
\varepsilon>0
. To this end, we
consider the centered stationary Gaussian process
V=\{V(s)\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
defined by
V(s)=Z^{\prime}(e^{s})
,
which is the Lamperti transform of
Z^{\prime}
, and apply Theorem 5.2 of Pickands
[
29
]
. Notice that the
covariance function of
V
,

r_{V}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}(e^{s}-u)^{\alpha-1}u^{-\gamma}\,du,\ \ \text{for }s>0.

Then
r_{V}(s)\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\,e^{(\alpha-1)s}
for all
s
large enough. Since
\alpha=\frac{1+\gamma}{2}<1
,
we have

\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}r_{V}(s)\ln s=0
, so the condition of
[
29
, Theorem 5.2]
is
satisfied. Hence

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,\,t]}V(s)-\sqrt{2\ln t}\bigg)\geq 0,\quad\hbox{ a.s.}

This implies that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,r]}|Z^{\prime}(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,t]}|Z^{\prime}(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}=0.

For proving the lower bound in (
5.1
), we apply the general lower bound on the small ball probability
of Gaussian processes due to Talagrand (cf. Lemma 2.2 of
[
32
]
). We will make use of the following
reformulation of Talagrand’s lower bound given by Ledoux
[
19
, (7.11)-(7.13) on Page 257]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{Z(t)\}_{t\in S}
be a separable, real-valued, centered Gaussian process
indexed by a bounded set
S
with the canonical metric
d_{Z}(s,t)=(\mathbb{E}|Z(s)-Z(t)|^{2})^{1/2}
.
Let
N_{\varepsilon}(S)
denote the smallest number of
d_{Z}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon

needed to cover
S
. If there is a decreasing function
\psi:(0,\delta]\to(0,\infty)
such that

N_{\varepsilon}(S)\leq\psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta]
and there are constants

K_{2}\geq K_{1}>1
such that

K_{1}\psi(\varepsilon)\leq\psi(\varepsilon/2)\leq K_{2}\psi(\varepsilon)

(5.3)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta]
, then there is a constant
K\in(0,\infty)
depending only
on
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and
d_{Z}
such that for all
u\in(0,\delta)
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|Z(s)-Z(t)|\leq u\bigg\}\geq\exp\big(-K\psi(u)\big).

(5.4)

We are ready to prove Proposition
5.1
.

Proof.

We will only prove (
5.1
). The proof of (
5.2
) is the same because
Z^{\prime}
is also
a generalized GFBM with indices
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\alpha-1\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,\,1/2)

By the self-similarity property of
Z
, we know that (
5.1
) is equivalent
to the following statement: there exist constants
c_{5,1},c_{5,2}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<1

\exp\bigg(-c_{5,1}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,\,1]}|Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-c_{5,2}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg).

(5.5)

In order to prove the lower bound in (
5.5
), we take
S=[0,1]
and apply Lemma
5.1
.
For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\,1)
, we construct a covering of
[0,1]
by
sub-intervals of
d_{Z}
-radius
\varepsilon
, which will give an upper bound for
N_{\varepsilon}([0,1]).

Recall that
\mathbb{E}\left[Z(t)^{2}\right]=c\,t^{2H}
for all
t\geq 0
, where
H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}>0
.
Since constants
c
here and those in Lemma
3.1
can be absorbed by the constants

c_{5,1}
and
c_{5,2}
in (
5.5
), without loss of generality we will take these constants to be 1 (otherwise
we consider the processes obtained by dividing
Z
by the maximum and minimum of these constants, respectively,
and prove the upper and lower bounds in (
5.5
) separately.)

Let
t_{0}=0,t_{1}=\varepsilon^{1/H}
. For any
n\geq 2
, if
t_{n-1}
has been defined, we define

t_{n}=t_{n-1}+t_{n-1}^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}.

(5.6)

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(Z(t_{n})-Z(t_{n-1}))^{2}\right]&\leq\frac{c}{t_{n-1}^{\gamma}}\big|t_{n}-t_{n-1}\big|^{2\alpha+1}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.\end{split}

(5.7)

Hence
d_{Z}(t_{n},\,t_{n-1})\leq\,\varepsilon
for all
n\geq 1
.

Since
[0,1]
can be covered by the intervals
[t_{n-1},\,t_{n}]
for
n=1,2,\ldots,L_{\varepsilon}
, where

L_{\varepsilon}
is the largest integer
n
such that
t_{n}\leq 1
, we have
N_{\varepsilon}([0,1])\leq L_{\varepsilon}+1\leq 2L_{\varepsilon}
.

In order to estimate
L_{\varepsilon}
, we write
t_{n}=a_{n}\varepsilon^{1/H}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Then, by (
5.6
), we have
a_{1}=1
,

a_{n}=a_{n-1}+a_{n-1}^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}},\quad\forall\,n\geq 2.

(5.8)

Denote by
\beta=1-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}=\frac{2H}{2\alpha+1}
. We claim that there exist positive and finite
constants
c_{5,5}\leq 2^{-\gamma/(2H\beta)}\beta^{1/\beta}
and
c_{5,6}\geq 1
such that

c_{5,5}\,n^{1/\beta}\leq a_{n}\leq c_{5,6}\,n^{1/\beta},\qquad\forall\,n\geq 1.

(5.9)

We postpone the proof of (
5.9
). Let us estimate
L_{\varepsilon}
and prove the lower bound in (
5.5
) first.

By (
5.9
), we have

L_{\varepsilon}=\max\left\{n:a_{n}\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{H}}\right\}\leq c_{5,5}^{-\beta}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{\beta}{H}}=c_{5,5}^{-\beta}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}.

This implies that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle N_{\varepsilon}([0,1])\leq 2c_{5,5}^{-\beta}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}=:\psi(\varepsilon).

(5.10)

Since the function
\psi(\varepsilon)
satisfies (
5.3
) with
K_{1}=K_{2}=2^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}>1,

we see that the lower bound in (
5.5
) follows from (
5.10
) and (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.1
.

Now we prove (
5.9
) by using induction. Clearly (
5.9
) holds for
n=1
. Assume that it holds
for
n=k
. Then for
n=k+1
, it follows from (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) that

a_{n+1}\leq c_{5,6}\,n^{1/\beta}+\big(c_{5,6}\,n^{1/\beta})^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\leq c_{5,6}\,(n+1)^{1/\beta},

where the last inequality can be checked by using the mean-value theorem and the facts that
c_{5,6}\geq 1

and
0<\beta<1
. This verifies the upper bound in (
5.9
). The desired lower bound for
a_{n+1}

is derived similarly using the mean-value theorem and the fact that
c_{5,5}\leq 2^{-\gamma/(2H\beta)}\beta^{1/\beta}
.
Hence the claim (
5.9
) holds.

Next, we prove the upper bound in (
5.5
). Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2

show that the conditions of Theorem 2.1 of Monrad and Rootzén
[
27
]
are satisfied. Hence the
upper bound in (
5.1
) follows from Theorem 2.1 of
[
27
]
. The proof is complete.
∎

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we can prove the following estimates on the small ball
probabilities for the increments of
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
at points away from the origin. We will use these estimates
to prove Chung’s LILs for
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
. Also, notice that no extra condition of
\alpha-\gamma/2-1/2>0
is
needed for (b).

Proposition 5.2
.

(a).

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,\,1/2)
. Then
there exist constants
c_{5,7},c_{5,8}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t>0,r\in\big(0,t/2\big)

and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,r^{\alpha+1/2}\right)
,

\ \begin{split}\exp\bigg(-c_{5,7}\,r\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg)\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq r}|Z(t+s)-Z(t)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\\
&\leq\,\exp\bigg(-c_{5,8}\,r\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\bigg).\end{split}

(5.11)

(b).

Assume
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
. Then there
exist constants
c_{5,9},c_{5,10}\in(0,\infty)

such that for all
t>0,r\in\big(0,t/2\big)
and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,r^{\alpha-1/2}\right)
,

\begin{split}\exp\bigg(-c_{5,9}\,r\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha-1}}\bigg)\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq r}|Z^{\prime}(t+s)-Z^{\prime}(t)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\\
&\,\leq\exp\bigg(-c_{5,10}\,r\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha-1}}\bigg).\end{split}

(5.12)

Proof.

(a). Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,\,1/2),0<r<t/2
. Let
I(t,r)=[t-r,t+r]
. It
follows from Lemma
3.1
that

\mathbb{E}\big[(Z(s)-Z(s^{\prime}))^{2}\big]\asymp t^{-\gamma}\,|s-s^{\prime}|^{2\alpha+1}\quad\hbox{ for all }\ s,\,s^{\prime}\in I(t,r).

Hence, there exists a constant
c_{5,11}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying that for all
0<\varepsilon<r^{\alpha+1/2}
,

N_{\varepsilon}(I(t,r))\leq c_{5,11}\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}\,r=:\psi(\varepsilon).

Then the function
\psi(\varepsilon)
satisfies (
5.3
) with
K_{1}=K_{2}=2^{\frac{2}{2\alpha+1}}>1
.
Hence the lower bound in (
5.11
) follows from (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.1
.

Next,
Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2

show that the conditions of Theorem 2.1 of Monrad and Rootzén
[
27
]
are satisfied. Hence the
upper bound in (
5.11
) follows from Theorem 2.1 of
[
27
]
.

(b). As noted earlier, when
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
the Gaussian process
\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a generalized

5.12
) follows from (

). This finishes the proof.
∎

6.
Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm for
Z

As applications of small ball probability estimates, Monrad and
Rootzén
[
27
]
, Xiao
[
38
]
, and Li and Shao
[
21
]
established
Chung’s LILs for fractional Brownian motions and other strongly locally nondeterministic Gaussian
processes with stationary increments. Notice that the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z
does not
have stationary increments. Here, we will use the small ball probability estimates and the Lamperti
transformation in the last two sections to establish Chung’s LIL for
Z
at points away from the origin when

\alpha\in(-1/2,\,1/2)
. The case of
\alpha=1/2
is open for
Z
as well as GFBM
X
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,\,1/2)
. There exists a constant
c_{6,1}\in(0,\infty)
such that for every

t>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{|Z(t+s)-Z(t)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}=c_{6,1}t^{-\gamma/2},\ \ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(6.1)

For proving Proposition
6.1
, we will make use of the following zero-one law, which implies
the existence of the limit in the left hand side of (
6.1
). Notice that the constant
c_{6,1}^{\prime}

in (
6.2
) can be
0
or
\infty
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,\,1/2)
. There exists a constant
c_{6,1}^{\prime}\in[0,\infty]
such that for every
t>0
,

\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{|Z(t+s)-Z(t)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}=c_{6,1}^{\prime}t^{-\gamma/2},\ \ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(6.2)

Proof.

We start with the stationary Gaussian process
U=\{U(s)\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
in (
4.3
).
As in the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
37
]
, we write for every
n\geq 1
,

U_{n}(s)=\int_{n-1\leq|\lambda|<n}e^{i\lambda s}\,W(d\lambda).

Then the Gaussian processes
U_{n}=\{U_{n}(s)\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}},\,n\geq 1
, are independent and

U(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}U_{n}(s),

where the series is a.s. uniformly convergent on every compact interval in
\mathbb{R}
. As in the proof of Proposition
2.1
,
we can verify that every
U_{n}(s)
is a.s. continuously differentiable (or as in
[
37
]
, it is sufficient
to show that
U_{n}(s)
is almost Lipschitz on compact intervals).
Therefore, for every
x\in\mathbb{R},N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{\big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(U_{n}(x+s)-U_{n}(s)\right)\big|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}=0.

Hence for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\geq 0
, the event

E_{c}=\bigg\{\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{|U(x+s)-U(x)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}\leq c\bigg\}

is a tail event with respect to
U_{n}\,(n\geq 1)
. By Kolmogorov’s zero-one law, we have
\mathbb{P}(E_{c})=0
or
1
. Let
c_{6,1}^{\prime}=\inf\{c\geq 0:\mathbb{P}(E_{c})=1\}
, with the convention that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. Then
c_{6,1}^{\prime}\in[0,\infty]
and

\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{|U(x+s)-U(x)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha+1/2}}=c_{6,1}^{\prime},\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(6.3)

Moreover,
c_{6,1}^{\prime}
does not depend on
x
because of the stationarity of
U
.

Next, we use (
6.3
) and Lamperti’s transformation to show (
6.2
). For any

t>0
fixed,
0<r<t
and
|s|\leq r
, we write

\begin{split}&Z(t+s)-Z(t)\\
=&\,\big[(t+s)^{H}-t^{H}\big]U(\ln(t+s))+t^{H}\big[U(\ln(t+s))-U(\ln t)\big]\\
=&\,\big[(t+s)^{H}-t^{H}\big]U(\ln(t+s))+t^{H}\big[U\big(\ln t+\ln(1+s/t)\big)-U(\ln t)\big].\end{split}

(6.4)

Since the first term is Lipschitz in
s\in[-r,r]
and
\ln\big(1+\frac{s}{t})\big)\sim s/t
as
s\to 0
,
it can be verified that (
6.2
) follows from (
6.4
) and (
6.3
) with
x=\ln t
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
6.1
that Proposition
6.1
will be established if we show

c_{6,1}^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
. This is where Propositions
3.2
,
5.2
, Lemma
4.2

and the following version of Fernique’s lemma from
[
13
, Lemma 1.1, p.138]
are needed.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\{\xi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a separable, centered, real-valued Gaussian
process. Assume that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\xi(t+h)-\xi(t)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\varphi(h)^{2},\ \ \ t>0,\ \ h>0,

for some continuous nondecreasing function
\varphi
with
\varphi(0)=0
. For any positive integer
k>1
and
any positive constants
t,x
and
\theta(p),p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\xi(s)-\xi(0)|>x\varphi(t)+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\theta(p)\varphi\left(tk^{-2^{p}}\right)\right\}\leq k^{2}e^{-x^{2}/2}+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}k^{2^{p+1}}e^{-\theta(p)^{2}/2}.

We now give the proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Without loss of generality,
we may assume
t>1
. We first prove the lower bound.
For any integer
n\geq 1
, let
r_{n}=e^{-n}
. Let
0<\delta<c_{5,8}
be a constant and consider the event

A_{n}=\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq r_{n}}\big|Z(t+s)-Z(t)\big|\leq\delta^{\alpha+1/2}\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,r_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln(1/r_{n}))^{\alpha+1/2}\bigg\}.

Proposition
5.2
implies that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\{A_{n}\}\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{c_{{5,8}}}{\delta}\ln n\bigg)=n^{-\frac{c_{{5,8}}}{\delta}}.

(6.5)

Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\{A_{n}\}<\infty
, the Borel-Cantelli lemma
implies

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|s|\leq r_{n}}\frac{\big|Z(t+s)-Z(t)\big|}{r_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln(1/r_{n}))^{\alpha+1/2}}\geq\delta^{\alpha+1/2}\,t^{-\gamma/2},\qquad\hbox{ a.s.}

(6.6)

It follows from (
6.6
) and a standard monotonicity
argument that

\liminf_{r\to 0+}\sup_{|s|\leq r}\frac{\big|Z(t+s)-Z(t)\big|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}/(\ln\ln(1/r))^{\alpha+1/2}}\geq c_{{6,2}}\,t^{-\gamma/2},\qquad\hbox{ a.s., }

(6.7)

for some constant
c_{6,2}\in(0,\infty)
which is independent of
t>0
.

The upper bound is a little more difficult to prove due to the dependence structure of
Z
.
In order to create independence, as in Tudor and Xiao
[
35
]
, we will make use
of the following stochastic integral representation of
Z
:

Z(t)=t^{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda),\ \ \ \ \ t>0,

(6.8)

where
H=\alpha-\gamma/2+1/2
. This follows from the spectral representation (
4.3
)
of
U
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
, we take

t_{n}=n^{-n}\ \ \ \hbox{ and }\ \ \ d_{n}=n^{n+1/2-\alpha}.

(6.9)

It is sufficient to prove that there exists a finite
constant
c_{{6,3}}
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\frac{\big|Z(t+s)-Z(t)\big|}{t_{n}^{{\alpha+1/2}}/(\ln\ln(1/t_{n}))^{\alpha+1/2}}\leq c_{{6,3}}t^{-\gamma/2}\qquad\hbox{ a.s.}

(6.10)

Let us define two Gaussian processes
Z_{n}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{n}
by

Z_{n}(t)=t^{H}\int_{|\lambda|\in(d_{n-1},d_{n}]}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda)

(6.11)

and

\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)=t^{H}\int_{|\lambda|\notin(d_{n-1},d_{n}]}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda),

(6.12)

respectively. Clearly
Z(t)=Z_{n}(t)+\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)
for all
t>0
. It is
important to note that the Gaussian processes
Z_{n}\,(n=1,2,\ldots)
are
independent and, moreover, for every
n\geq 1,

Z_{n}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{n}

are independent as well.

Denote
h(r)=r^{\alpha+1/2}\,\big(\ln\ln(1/r)\big)^{-(\alpha+1/2)}
. We make
the following two claims:

(i).

There is a constant
\delta>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\big|Z_{n}(t+s)-Z_{n}(t)\big|\leq\delta^{\alpha+1/2}\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,h(t_{n})\bigg\}=\infty.

(6.13)

(ii).

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\big|\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t+s)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)\big|>\varepsilon\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,h(t_{n})\bigg\}<\infty.

(6.14)

Since the events in (
6.13
) are independent, we see that (
6.10
) follows
from (
6.13
), (
6.14
) and a standard Borel-Cantelli argument.

It remains to verify the claims (i) and (ii) above. By Proposition
5.2

and Anderson’s inequality
[
2
]
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\big|Z_{n}(t+s)-Z_{n}(s)\big|\leq\delta^{\alpha+1/2}\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,h(t_{n})\bigg\}\\
&\geq\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\big|Z(t+s)-Z(s)\big|\leq\delta^{\alpha+1/2}\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,h(t_{n})\bigg\}\\
&\geq\,\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{{5,7}}}{\delta}\ln(n\ln n)\Big)\\
&=\,\big(n\ln n\big)^{-\frac{c_{5,7}}{\delta}}.\end{split}

(6.15)

Hence (i) holds for
\delta\geq c_{5,7}
.

In order to prove (ii), we divide it into two terms: For any
|s|<t_{n}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t+s)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)\big)^{2}\Big)&=\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\big|(t+s)^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln(t+s)}-t^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln t}\big|^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&\quad+\int_{|\lambda|>d_{n}}\big|(t+s)^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln(t+s)}-t^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln t}\big|^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&=:\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}.\end{split}

(6.16)

The second term is easy to estimate: For any
|s|\leq t_{n}
, there exists
c_{6,4}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying that

\mathcal{J}_{2}\leq 2\,\left(t^{2H}+(t+s)^{2H}\right)\,\int_{|\lambda|>d_{n}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\leq c_{{6,4}}\,\,n^{-(2\alpha+1)(n+1/2-\alpha)},

(6.17)

where the last inequality follows from (
4.9
).

For the first term
\mathcal{J}_{1}
, we use the following elementary inequalities:

\displaystyle\left|(x+y)^{H}-x^{H}\right|\leq|H|\max\left\{x^{H-1},(x+y)^{H-1}\right\}|y|,\ \ \ \text{ for all }0\leq|y|<x,

1-\cos x\leq x^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

and
\ln(1+x)\leq x
for all
x\geq 0
to derive that for any
|s|\leq t_{n}
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{1}=&\,\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\left[\left((t+s)^{H}-t^{H}\right)^{2}+2(t+s)^{H}\,t^{H}\Big(1-\cos\Big(\lambda\ln\frac{t+s}{t}\Big)\Big)\right]\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
\leq&\,\frac{1}{H^{2}}\max\left\{t^{2(H-1)},(t+s)^{2(H-1)}\right\}s^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&\quad\ +2(t+s)^{H}\,t^{H}\,\ln^{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)\,\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
\leq&\,c_{6,5}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda+\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right)n^{-2n}.\end{split}

Here
c_{6,5}
is in
(0,\infty)
.
By (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we know

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda+\sup_{n\geq 1}\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda<\infty.

Notice that
2n\geq(2\alpha+1)(n+1/2-\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}\leq c_{6,6}n^{-(2\alpha+1)(n+1/2-\alpha)},

(6.18)

for some constant
c_{6,6}\in(0,\infty)
.

Put
\delta=(2\alpha+1)(1/2-\alpha)
. By Lemma
3.3
, (
6.16
), (
6.17
)
and (
6.18
), there exists a constant
K>0
such that
for
0\leq h\leq t_{n}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(h)^{2}:=K\min\left\{h^{2\alpha+1},\,n^{-(2\alpha+1)n-\delta}\right\}\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\big(\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t+h)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)\big)^{2}\right].

Put
x_{n}=(8\ln n)^{1/2}
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, define

\displaystyle\theta_{n}(p)=\varepsilon(p+1)^{-2}h(t_{n})/\varphi_{n}\left(t_{n}n^{-2^{p}}\right)\ \ \ \ \text{for all }p\geq 1.

For large enough
n
, we have

\theta_{n}(p)>4(\ln n)^{1/2}2^{p/2}\ \ \ \text{for all }p\geq 1

and

x_{n}\varphi_{n}(t_{n})+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\theta_{n}(p)\varphi_{n}\left(t_{n}n^{-2^{p}}\right)<\varepsilon h(t_{n}).

Since

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}e^{-x_{n}^{2}/2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}n^{2^{p+1}}e^{-8(\ln n)2^{p}}<\infty,

by applying Lemma
6.2
with
\xi(s)=\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t\pm s)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)
for
0\leq s\leq t_{n}
, we
obtain that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq t_{n}}\big|\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t+s)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)\big|>\varepsilon\,t^{-\gamma/2}\,h(t_{n})\bigg\}<\infty.

This proves (
6.14
) and hence Proposition
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

In light of Proposition
6.1
, it is natural to study Chung’s LIL of
Z
at the origin.
While doing so, we found that there is an error in the proof of Theorem 3.1 in Tudor and Xiao
[
35
]
, which
gives Chung’s LIL for bifractional Brownian motion at the origin. More precisely, the inequality (3.30) in
[
35
]

does not hold. Hence, Theorem 3.1 in
[
35
]
should be modified as a Chung’s LIL at
t>0
, which is similar
to Theorem 1.4 and Proposition 6.1 in the present paper.

It turns out that Chung’s LIL at the origin for self-similar Gaussian processes that do not have stationary
increments such as GFBM
X
, the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z
, and bifractional Brownian motion
is quite subtle. A different method than that in the proof of Proposition
6.1
or that in
[
35
]
is
needed for proving the desired upper bound. Recently, this problem has been studied in the subsequent paper

[
36
]
by modifying the arguments of Talagrand
[
33
]
.

Similarly, for
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
, we have the following Chung’s LIL for
Z^{\prime}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
. There exists a constant
c_{6,7}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}\frac{|Z^{\prime}(t+s)-Z^{\prime}(t)|}{r^{\alpha-1/2}/(\ln\ln 1/r)^{\alpha-1/2}}=c_{6,7}t^{-\gamma/2}\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(6.19)

Proof.

Recall from (
3.31
) that, for
\alpha\in(1/2,\,3/2)
, the derivative process

\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM with indices
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-1\in(-1/2,\ 1/2)

and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Hence the proof of (
6.19
) follows the same line as in the proof of
Proposition
6.1
. We omit the details.
∎

7.
Proofs of the main theorems

In this section we prove our main results for GFBM
X
stated in Section 1.

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

(a). Assume that
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
. By Proposition
2.1
,
we know that for any
0<a<b<\infty
,
Y(t)
is continuously differentiable on
[a,b]
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\frac{|Y(t+h)-Y(t)|}{h^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\ln h^{-1}}}=0.

By (
1.17
), to prove (
1.5
), it is sufficient to prove that for any
0<a<b<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\frac{|Z(t+h)-Z(t)|}{h^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\ln h^{-1}}}=c_{7,1},

(7.1)

where
c_{7,1}
is a positive constant satisfying

\displaystyle c_{7,2}:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{(\alpha+1/2)^{2}b^{\gamma}c_{3,6}}}\leq c_{7,1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2c_{3,6}}{(\alpha+1/2)c_{3,5}}}=:c_{7,3}.

(7.2)

Here,
c_{3,5},\,c_{3,6}
are constants given in (
3.19
).

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let

J(\varepsilon):=\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq\varepsilon}\frac{|Z(t+h)-Z(t)|}{h^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\ln h^{-1}}}.

Since
\varepsilon\mapsto J(\varepsilon)
is non-decreasing, the limit in the left-hand side of (
7.1
) exists almost
surely. Moreover, the zero-one law for the uniform modulus of continuity in
[
24
, Lemma 7.1.1]
implies
that the limit in (
7.1
) is a constant almost surely. Hence, it remains to prove that with probability one,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}J(\varepsilon)\leq c_{7,3}\

(7.3)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}J(\varepsilon)\geq c_{7,2}.

(7.4)

The proof of (
7.3
) is standard. It follows from (
3.19
) and the metric-entropy bound (cf.
Theorem 1.3.5 in
[
1
]
), or one may prove this directly by applying Fernique’s inequality in Lemma
6.2

and the Borel-Cantelli lemma. The lower bound in (
7.4
) follows from
the one-sided SLND (
3.35
) and Theorem 4.1 in
[
26
]
. This proves (a).

(b). When
\alpha=1/2
, we see that (
3.20
) holds for all
s,t\in[a,b]
. This implies that the canonical
metric of
Z
satisfies

d_{Z}(s,t)\asymp|s-t|\sqrt{1+\big|\ln|s-t|\big|}

on
[a,b]
. Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
small,
the covering number
N_{\varepsilon}([a,b])
of
[a,b]
under
d_{Z}
satisfies

N_{\varepsilon}([a,b])\leq c_{7,4}\varepsilon^{-1}\big(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}\big)^{1/2},

(7.5)

where
c_{7,4}>0
is a finite constant. It follows from Theorem 1.3.5 in
[
1
]
that almost surely for all
\delta>0

small enough,

\begin{split}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{d_{Z}(t,t+h)\leq\delta}\big|Z(t+h)-Z(t)\big|&\leq c\int_{0}^{\delta}\sqrt{\ln N_{\varepsilon}([a,b])}\,d\varepsilon\leq c\delta\sqrt{\ln{\delta}^{-1}}.\end{split}

(7.6)

Notice that, by (
3.20
),
d_{Z}(t,t+h)\leq\delta
is compatible to
|h|\sqrt{\ln|h|^{-1}}\leq\delta
,
up to a constant factor. Hence, (
7.6
) implies that

\begin{split}\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}\big|Z(t+h)-Z(t)\big|\leq c\,r\ln r^{-1}.\end{split}

(7.7)

Now it can be seen that (
1.6
) follows from (
7.7
).

(c). The proof of (
1.7
) is similar: the uniform modulus of continuity of
X^{\prime}
on
[a,b]
is
the same as that of
Z^{\prime}
on
[a,b]
, and the latter can be derived from (
3.30
) in
Lemma
3.3
and the one-sided SLND (
3.36
) in Proposition
3.2
.
We omit the details here. The proof is complete. ∎

7.2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
5.1
, by using Lemmas
2.1
and
5.1
,
we obtain the following lower bounds for the small ball probabilities of
Y
.

Lemma 7.1
.

(a).

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2+\gamma/2)
. Then there exists a
constant
c_{7,5}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0,\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|Y(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq\exp\bigg(-c_{7,5}\,{r^{H}}\,\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg).

(7.8)

(b).

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2+\gamma/2)
. Then there
exists a constant
c_{7,6}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t>0,0<\varepsilon<r<t/2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{|s|\leq r}|Y(t+s)-Y(t)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq\exp\bigg(-c_{7,6}\,r\,t^{H-1}\,\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg).

(7.9)

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By the self-similarity of
X
, we know that (
1.9
)
is equivalent to the following: For any
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{4}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|X(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-\kappa_{5}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg).

(7.10)

By (
5.1
), (
7.8
) and the independence of
Y
and
Z
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|X(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\geq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|Y(s)|\leq\varepsilon/2\bigg\}\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon/2\bigg\}\\
\geq&\,\exp\left(-c_{7,5}\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\exp\bigg(-c_{5,1}\Big(\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg)\\
\geq&\,\exp\bigg(-\left(2c_{7,5}+2^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}c_{5,1}\right)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg),\end{split}

where we have used the facts that
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}

as
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\alpha<1/2
. This proves the lower bound in (
7.10
).

On the other hand, by using the Anderson inequality
[
2
]
and (
5.1
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|X(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}=&\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|Y(s)+Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon\big|Y(s),s\in[0,1]\bigg\}\right]\\
\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|Z(s)|\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq\,\exp\bigg(-c_{5,2}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\bigg).\end{split}

This proves the upper bound in (
7.10
). The proof is complete.
∎

7.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
and the time inversion of
X

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, it is sufficient to prove
(
1.14
)-(
1.16
) for
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
, respectively.

First we consider the case when
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
. It follows from (
6.4
) that for any fixed
t>0
,

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|Z(t+h)-Z(t)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\ln\ln 1/r}}=t^{-\gamma/2}\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|U(x+h)-U(x)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\ln\ln 1/r}},

(7.11)

where
x=\ln t
. By Lemma
4.1
we see that the Gaussian process
\{U(s)-U(0)\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}

satisfies the conditions of Theorem 5.1 in
[
26
]
with
N=1
and
\sigma(s,t)\asymp|s-t|^{\alpha+1/2}
on
any compact interval of
(0,\infty)
. Consequently, there exists a constant
\kappa_{12}\in(0,\infty)
which
does not depend on
x
such that

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|U(x+h)-U(x)|}{r^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\ln\ln 1/r}}=\kappa_{12},\ \ \ \hbox{a.s.}

(7.12)

Combining (
7.11
) and (
7.12
) yields (
1.14
) for
Z
.

When
\alpha=1/2
, we see from Lemma
4.1
that Condition (A1) in
[
26
]
is satisfied with
N=1
and

\sigma(s,t)\asymp|s-t|\sqrt{1+\big|\ln|s-t|\big|}
on any compact interval of
(0,\infty)
. This case is not explicitly
covered by Theorem 5.1 in
[
26
]
. However, an examination of its proof shows that we have

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|h|\leq r}\frac{|U(x+h)-U(x)|}{r\sqrt{\ln(1/r)\ln\ln 1/r}}=\kappa_{13},\ \ \ \hbox{a.s.}

(7.13)

for a constant
\kappa_{13}\in(0,\infty)
that does not depend on
x
.
Therefore, by combining (
7.11
) and (
7.13
) we obtain (
1.15
)
for
Z
.

Finally, when
\alpha\in(1/2,\,1/2+\gamma/2)
, the proof of (
1.16
) for
Z^{\prime}
is similar to that of
(
1.14
) for
Z
given above. There is no need to repeat it. The proof of Theorem
1.5
is complete.
∎

The following result is the
time inversion
property of GFBM
X
. It allows us to improve
slightly Theorem 6.1 in
[
11
]
, where the law of the iterated logarithm of
X

at the origin was proved under the extra condition of
\alpha>0
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a GFBM with parameters
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2+\gamma)
.
Define the Gaussian process
\widetilde{X}=\{\widetilde{X}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
by

\widetilde{X}(0)=0,\ \ \ \widetilde{X}(t)=t^{2H}X\left(1/t\right),\ \ \ \ \ t>0.

Then
X
and
\widetilde{X}
have the same distribution. Consequently, there exists a constant
\kappa_{15}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0+}\frac{|X(r)|}{r^{H}\sqrt{\ln\ln r^{-1}}}=\kappa_{15},\ \ \ \ \ \hbox{a.s.}

(7.14)

Proof.

Since
X
and
\widetilde{X}
are centered Gaussian processes, it is sufficient to check their covariance functions.
For any
0<s\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{X}(s)\widetilde{X}(t)\big]=

\displaystyle\,(st)^{2H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\Big(\frac{1}{s}-u\Big)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-u)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\Big(\frac{1}{t}-u\Big)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-u)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)|u|^{-\gamma}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(t-v\right)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-v)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\left(s-v\right)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-v)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)|v|^{-\gamma}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[X(s)X(t)\right],

where a change of variable
u=v/(st)
is used in the second step. This proves the first part of Proposition
7.1
.

The time inversion property, together with the LIL of
X
at infinity in
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
, implies
(
7.14
). The proof is complete.
∎

7.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

By Proposition
2.1
,
Y(t)
is continuously differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.
Hence for every
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
,
t>0
and for every compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\lim_{u\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{K}}\left|\frac{X(t+u\tau)-X(t)}{u^{\alpha+1/2}}-\frac{Z(t+u\tau)-Z(t)}{u^{\alpha+1/2}}\right|=0,\ \ \ \,\,\text{ a.s.}

(7.15)

Therefore,
X
has the same tangent process as that of

Z
at
t\in(0,\infty)
, and Theorem
1.6
follows from Proposition
3.1
. ∎

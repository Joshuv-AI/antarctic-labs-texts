Abstract
. In the last decade, G. Bharali and A. Zimmer defined a class of domains called Goldilocks domains and they showed that such a domain satisfies a visibility condition with respect to the Kobayashi extremal curves. Inspired by their construction, we define a subclass of Goldilocks domains called strongly Goldilocks domains and we prove a quantitative visibility result on strongly Goldilocks domains. Using our quantitative visibility result, we extend the Gehring-Hayman theorem on simply connected planar domains to strongly Goldilocks domains. As an application of our construction, we also give lower estimates to the Kobayashi distance.

32F45

1.
Introduction

It is well-known that the unit ball endowed with the Kobayashi distance is negatively curved and strongly pseudoconvex domains resemble the unit ball close to the boundary. Therefore it is reasonable to expect the Kobayashi geometry of strongly pseudoconvex domains to exhibit a similar behaviour. However, it is often not possible to study local geometry of the Kobayashi distance. Balogh and Bonk
[
BB
]
showed that the Kobayashi geometry of strongly pseudoconvex domains are hyperbolic in a global sense, that is, strongly pseudoconvex domains are Gromov hyperbolic w.r.t. the Kobayashi distance. Zimmer
[
Zim1
]
and Fiacchi
[
Fia
]
extended their result to smooth bounded convex domains of finite type and smooth bounded pseudoconvex domains of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Another hyperbolic behaviour of geodesic metric spaces is the visibility property. Informally, it means that geodesics joining points close to different boundary points tend to bend inside the metric space. The paper
[
BZ
]
defined the Goldilocks domains by demanding growth conditions on the Kobayashi distance and the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric with no assumption of boundary regularity. There, they showed that Goldilocks domains endowed with the Kobayashi distance satisfy a variant of the visibility property w.r.t. their Euclidean boundaries and provided some applications of visibility. See also
[
BM
,
BG
,
BGZ
,
BNT
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we define a subclass of Goldilocks domains called strongly Goldilocks domains (see Definition
6
). Roughly speaking a strongly Goldilocks domain
\Omega
is a bounded domain with a sufficiently regular boundary and its Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric grows faster than a function
1/\omega_{\Omega},
where
\omega_{\Omega}
satisfies certain growth conditions. In particular, the class of strongly Goldilocks domains contains strongly pseudoconvex domains, smooth bounded pseudoconvex domains of finite type and
m
-convex domains with Dini-smooth boundary.

We first provide a quantitative visibility result on a strongly Goldilocks domain
\Omega.
Explicitly, we prove Theorem
9
which, , shows that if
\gamma:I\longrightarrow\Omega
is a
\lambda
-geodesic (see Definition
1
) joining
x,y\in\Omega,
then
\max\delta_{\Omega}\circ\gamma\geq cg_{\Omega}^{-1}(\|x-y\|),
where
g_{\Omega}
is a function associated to
\Omega
derived from
\omega_{\Omega}
and the constant
c>0
depends only on
\Omega
and
\lambda
. Then Proposition
8
gives an explicit form of this estimate on strongly pseudoconvex domains, smooth bounded pseudoconvex domains of finite type and
m
-convex domains with Dini-smooth boundary.

With our quantitative visibility condition, we give an extension of the classical Gehring-Hayman inequality to convexifiable strongly Goldilocks domains. Namely, our Theorem
14
shows that if
\Omega
is a convexifiable strongly Goldilocks domain with
\gamma
and
g_{\Omega}
as above, we have
l_{e}(\gamma)\leq Cg_{\Omega}(\|x-y\|),
where
l_{e}(\gamma)
denotes the Euclidean length of the curve
\gamma
and
C>0
depends only on
\Omega
(see Corollary
15
for some explicit estimates). This shows that on convexifiable strongly Goldilocks domains, Kobayashi geodesics are also short in Euclidean length. As the situation for convexifiable domains is more intricate than for convex domains, we study the problem in two separate subsection.

Finally, as another application of our quantitative visibility condition, we prove Theorem
23
which provides a relatively sharp lower bound to the Kobayashi distance on strongly Goldilocks domains.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

(i) Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then for
x\in\Omega
,
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we set

\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\displaystyle{\inf\{\lVert x-y\rVert:y\in\partial\Omega\}}\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(x;v):=\displaystyle{\inf\{|\lambda|:x+\lambda{v}\in\partial\Omega,\>\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}}.

(ii) For a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
we set
D_{\gamma}:=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma(t)).

(iii) Let
f,g
be real valued functions taking non-negative values on a set
X
. We say
f\lesssim g
or
g\gtrsim f
if there exists
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in X
. We say
f\sim g
if
f\lesssim g
and
f\gtrsim g
.

2.2.
The Kobayashi distance

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Recall that the Lempert function of
\Omega
is defined as

l_{\Omega}(x,y):=\inf\{\tanh^{-1}(|\alpha|):f\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega),\>f(0)=x,\>f(\alpha)=y\},

where
\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega)
denotes the set of holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\Delta
into
\Omega
.

In general the Lempert function does not satisfy the triangle inequality. The Kobayashi pseudodistance is the largest pseudodistance not exceeding the Lempert function. The Kobayashi length of a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
is defined as

l^{\Omega}_{k}(\gamma):=\displaystyle{\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}l_{\Omega}(x_{j-1},x_{j}):x_{0}=\gamma(0),x_{n}=\gamma(1),\>x_{j}\in\gamma([0,1])\right\}}

and the Kobayashi pseudodistance on
\Omega
is given as

k_{\Omega}(x,y):=\displaystyle{\inf l^{\Omega}_{k}(\gamma)},

where the infimum is taken over all continuous curves joining
x,y\in\Omega
.

Another relevant notion is the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric. For
x\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
it is defined as

\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)=\inf\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega):\varphi(0)=x,\alpha\varphi^{\prime}(0)=v\}.

Due to
[
Roy
, Theorem 1]
we have

k_{\Omega}(x,y)=\displaystyle{\inf\{l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma):\gamma\>\text{is piecewise}\>\mathcal{C}^{1},\>\gamma(0)=x,\>\gamma(1)=y\}},

where

l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt.

The Kobayashi-Buseman pseudometric (see e.g.
[
JP
, p. 135]
)
\hat{k}_{\Omega}
is the largest pseudometric with convex indicatrix not exceeding
k_{\Omega}
. In particular (see e.g.
[
JP
, proof of Theorem 3.6.4]
), if
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
is a piecewise
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-curve, then

(1)

l^{\Omega}_{k}(\gamma)\leq l^{\Omega}_{\hat{\kappa}}(\gamma)\leq l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma),

where
l^{\Omega}_{\hat{\kappa}}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be hyperbolic if
k_{\Omega}
is a distance and complete hyperbolic if
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
is complete as a metric space. The Hopf-Rinow theorem
[
BH
, Proposition 3.7]
asserts that if
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic, then
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
is a geodesic space, that is for any
x,y\in\Omega
there exists a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
joining
x
and
y
such that
k_{\Omega}(x,y)=l^{k}_{\Omega}(\gamma)
. We call such a curve a Kobayashi geodesic. As in general Kobayashi geodesics need not exist, the authors in
[
BZ
]
defined
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-quasi-geodesics and
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-almost-geodesics.
In this paper, we use the concept of
\lambda
-geodesics, whose definition we give below.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a hyperbolic domain,
\lambda\geq 1
. We say that a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
is a
\lambda
-geodesic
for
\kappa_{\Omega},
resp.
\hat{\kappa}_{\Omega},
if for all
s,t\in[0,1]
we have

l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma|_{[s,t]}))\leq\lambda k_{\Omega}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)),\quad\text{resp. }l^{\Omega}_{\hat{\kappa}}(\gamma|_{[s,t]})\leq\lambda k_{\Omega}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)).

It is easy to see that any
\lambda
-geodesic is Lipschitz, so by taking a reparametrization, one sees that
\lambda
-geodesics for
\kappa_{\Omega}
correspond to
(\lambda,0)
-almost-geodesics in the sense of
[
BZ
]
. Moreover, by (
1
), any
1
-geodesic is a Kobayashi geodesic.

Remark 1
.

By
[
BZ
, Proposition 4.6]
, if
\Omega
is a hyperbolic domain and
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
is a Kobayashi geodesic, then
\gamma
is Lipschitz and
l^{\Omega}_{k}(\gamma)=l^{\Omega}_{\hat{\kappa}}(\gamma)
. Thus, in the case of hyperbolicity, any Kobayashi geodesic is
a
1
-geodesic for
\hat{\kappa}_{\Omega}
and vice versa. It remains unclear whether the same is true for
\kappa_{\Omega}.

From now on,
\lambda
-geodesic will mean
\lambda
-geodesic for
\kappa_{\Omega}.
However the reader can verify that our results also remain true for
\lambda
-geodesics for
\hat{\kappa}_{\Omega}.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We say that
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
has
\alpha
-growth
if there exist some
\alpha>0

and
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(k_{\Omega}(x_{0},x)-\alpha\log\frac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right)<\infty.

Let
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth boundary point. We say that
p
is a Dini-smooth boundary point if the inner unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
is a Dini-continuous function near
p
. This means that there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt<\infty
, where

w(t):=\sup\{\|n_{p}-n_{q}\|:p,q\in\partial\Omega\cap U,\>\>\>\|p-q\|<t\}

and
n_{p},n_{q}
denotes the inner unit normal to
\partial\Omega
taken at
p,q
respectively. We say that
\Omega
has Dini-smooth boundary if any
p\in\partial\Omega
is a Dini-smooth boundary point. It is clear that any
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-smooth boundary point is a Dini-smooth boundary point. The following estimate is crucial for our purposes.

Lemma 1
.

[
NA
, Corollary 8]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain with Dini-smooth boundary. Then there exists
C>0
such that we have

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\log\left(1+\dfrac{C\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

Notice that the lemma above implies that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain with Dini-smooth boundary, then
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
has
1/2
-growth.

The next lemma follows from the proof of
[
Bło
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
Nik2
, (2)]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex domain. We have

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\log\left(\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}{\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\right)\right|,\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

The upper bound below easily follows for any domain; for a proof of the lower bound see e.g.
[
Zim1
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a convex domain. We have

\dfrac{1}{2\delta_{\Omega}(x;v)}\leq\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)\leq\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(x;v)},\>\>\>\>\>x\in\Omega,\>\>\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

The following estimate is mentioned in
[
NTry
, p. 633]
and
[
Zim1
, Remark 2.7]
. For convenience of the reader,
we include a proof.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We have

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\min\{\delta_{\Omega}(y;{x-y}),\delta_{\Omega}(x;{x-y})\}}\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
\delta_{\Omega}(x;x-y)\geq\delta_{\Omega}(y;x-y)
. Let
L
denote the complex line passing through
x
and
y
, and
p\in L\cap\partial\Omega
be with
\|y-p\|=\|x-y\|\delta_{\Omega}(y;{x-y})
. Set
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow L
to be the projection of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto
L
in the direction of a supporting hyperplane to
\partial\Omega
at
p
. Note that such an hyperplane exists due to convexity of
\Omega
.

As
\pi(\Omega)
is convex and
\delta_{\pi(\Omega)}(y)=\|y-p\|
, it follows by
[
NTry
, Proposition 2]
that

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq k_{\pi(\Omega)}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(y;x-y)}\right).

∎

2.3.
Gromov hyperbolicity and the visibility property

Definition 3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. The Gromov product of
x,y\in X
w.r.t.
o\in X
is defined to be

(x|y)_{o}:=\dfrac{1}{2}(d(x,o)+d(o,y)-d(x,y)).

A metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
Gromov hyperbolic
if for some
\delta>0
and any
x,y,z,o\in X
we have

(x|y)_{o}\geq\min\{(x|z)_{o},(z|y)_{o}\}-\delta.

We are interested in the case
X=\Omega
and
d=k_{\Omega}
. We say that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is Gromov hyperbolic if
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
is a Gromov hyperbolic
space. Note that the strongly pseudoconvex domains and the smooth bounded pseudoconvex domains of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
are Gromov hyperbolic by
[
BB
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
Fia
, Theorem 1.1]
respectively. Furthermore,
[
Zim1
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that a smooth bounded convex domain is Gromov hyperbolic if and only if it is of finite type.

The following follows from the more general geodesic stability lemma (see e.g.
[
BH
, p. 401, Theorem 1.7]
).

Lemma 5
.

[
BH
, Theorem 1.7]

Let
\Omega
be a complete hyperbolic domain which is Gromov hyperbolic. Let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a Kobayashi geodesic and let
\sigma:[0,1]\to\Omega
be a
\lambda
-geodesic joining the same two points. Then there exists
K>0,
depending only on
\Omega
and
\lambda,
such that for all
t\in[0,1]
there exists
s\in[0,1]
such that
k_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\sigma(s))\leq K
.

We refer the reader to
[
BH
, Part III]
for a detailed discussion on Gromov hyperbolic metric spaces and also to the recent expository article
[
BG
]
where the authors discuss constructions related to Gromov hyperbolicity on complex domains.

Another relevant notion is the visibility property, we follow the definition given in
[
BNT
]
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
p,q\in\partial\Omega
, where
p\neq q
. We say that the pair
\{p,q\}
has
visible
geodesics

if there exist neighborhoods
U,V
of
p,q
respectively such that
\overline{U}\cap\overline{V}=\emptyset
,
and a compact set
K\subset\Omega
such that for any geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega

with
\gamma(0)\in U
,
\gamma(1)\in V
, then
\gamma([0,1])\cap K\neq\emptyset
.

We say that
\Omega
satisfies
the visibility property
if any pair
\{p,q\}\subset\partial\Omega
, where
p\neq q

has visible geodesics.

The definition given above only makes sense when the domain is complete hyperbolic. In
[
BZ
]
(see also
[
BM
]
), the authors gave a more general definition of the visibility property. There they also defined the following class of domains and showed that they satisfy the visibility property in their own context. We recall their definition below.

Definition 5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We say that
\Omega
is
Goldilocks
if:

(i)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}}\frac{M_{\Omega}(r)}{r}dr<\infty,

where

M_{\Omega}(r):=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)}:\|v\|=1,\>\delta_{\Omega}(x)\leq r\right\};

(ii)
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
has
\alpha
-growth for some
\alpha>0
.

Notice that the definition above intuitively gives the reason why the geodesics (or curves that approximate geodesics in a suitable sense) tend to spend as little time as possible near the boundary, the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric grows too quickly but the Kobayashi distance grows too slowly for geodesics to stay close to the boundary.

Recall a geometric notion given in
[
BZ
]
. Roughly speaking we say that a domain
\Omega
satisfies the interior cone condition if any point near its boundary belongs to the central axis of a uniform cone centered at a boundary point.
[
BZ
, Lemma 2.3]
shows that Condition 2 in Definition
5
is satisfied by domains that satisfy the interior cone condition. Moreover, it is not difficult to see that uniform cone condition is satisfied by domains with Lipschitz boundaries, in particular for convex domains.

On the other hand, Condition 1 is satisfied for a large class of domains including bounded pseudoconvex domains of finite type and bounded convex domains with a boundary that is not too flat in the precise sense given in
[
BZ
, Lemma 2.9]
. This discussion shows that although its definition is rather abstract, it is reasonable to expect that ”regular” domains satisfy Goldilocks conditions. As a partial converse, by
[
BZ
, Proposition 2.15]
we have that the Goldilocks domains are pseudoconvex.

3.
Strongly Goldilocks domains

In this section we introduce and give examples of strongly Goldilocks domains, and provide a quantitive visibility condition for such domains.

3.1.
Definition and properties

Definition 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a
bounded
domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Set
d_{\Omega}:=\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(x):x\in\Omega\}
.
We say that
\Omega
is
strongly Goldilocks
(SG) if:

(i)
\Omega
has Dini-smooth boundary;

(ii) The Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric of
\Omega
satisfies

(2)

\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)\geq\dfrac{\|v\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))},\quad x\in\Omega,\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

where
\omega_{\Omega}:(0,d_{\Omega}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a strongly Goldilocks (SG) function, that is,

(a)
\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(r)}{r}
is decreasing and
\frac{w(d_{\Omega})}{d_{\Omega}}\geq e;

(b)
\omega_{\Omega}(r)\log\left(\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(r)}{r}\right)
is increasing;

(c)

\int_{0}^{d_{\Omega}}\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(r)}{r}\log\left(\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(r)}{r}\right)dr<\infty.

Although the conditions above seem somewhat restrictive we will later see that they are natural in the sense that they hold for relatively large class of domains.

Remark 2
.

Note that (a) and (b) above imply that the function
\omega_{\Omega}
is strictly increasing.

Proposition 6
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},

n\geq 2,
and (
2
) holds, then
\omega_{\Omega}(r)\gtrsim\sqrt{r}.

Proof.

Let
q\in\Omega
and
p\in\partial\Omega
be such that
\|p-q\|=\delta_{\Omega}(q)=:c
.
Denote by
n_{p}
the unit inner normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
p.
Set
B
to be the Euclidean ball with center
at
q
and radius
c
. Then for any
v\in T_{p}^{\mathbb{C}}(\partial\Omega)
and
r\in(0,2c)

we have

\dfrac{\|v\|}{\sqrt{(2c-r)r}}=\kappa_{B}(p+rn_{p};v)\geq\kappa_{\Omega}(p+rn_{p};v)\geq\dfrac{\|v\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(r)}.

∎

Observe also that
\omega_{\Omega}\gtrsim M_{\Omega},
where
M_{\Omega}
comes from Definition
5
.
As SG domains have Dini-smooth boundary,
[
BZ
, Lemma 2.3]
shows that the set of SG domains form a strict subset of Goldilocks domains. In particular, by
[
BZ
, Proposition 2.15]
and
[
BZ
, Theorem 1.4]
, they are pseudoconvex and they satisfy the visibility property.

We present some observations which will be useful in what will follow.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\omega:(0,d]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a SG function. For
r\in(0,d],
set

g_{\omega}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\log\left(\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\right)dt.

Then:

(i)

g_{\omega}
is a strictly increasing function, so it has an inverse;

(ii)

g_{\omega}(r)\geq\omega(r);

(iii)

\frac{g_{\omega}(r)}{r}
is a decreasing function.

Proof.

(i) This statement is obvious.

(ii) Since
h(t):=\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\geq e
is decreasing, we have

g_{\omega}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{w(t)}{t}\log\left(\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\right)dt\geq\frac{\omega(r)}{r}\int_{0}^{r}dt=\omega(r).

(iii) It follows from

\left(\frac{g_{\omega}(r)}{r}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\int_{0}^{r}(h(r)\log h(r)-h(t)\log h(t))dt}{r^{2}}\leq 0.

∎

Note that by the properties of functions
\omega,g_{\omega}
we see that if
K\geq 1

(3)

\omega(Kr)\leq K\omega(r)\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>g_{\omega}(Kr)\leq Kg_{\omega}(r).

This property will be important in our estimates in the upcoming sections.

3.2.
Examples

Recall that a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be
m
-convex if there exists
C>0
such that
for every
x\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\|v\|=1
we have

\delta_{\Omega}(x;v)\leq C\delta_{\Omega}^{1/m}(x).

This notion extends the notion of finite type in the case of smooth convex domains (see e.g.
[
Zim1
]
).

We point out that

(4)

\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)\geq\dfrac{c\|v\|}{\delta^{\epsilon}_{\Omega}(x)},\quad x\in\Omega,\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

with:

–
\epsilon=1/2
if
\Omega
is strongly pseudoconvex (see e.g.
[
JP
, Theorem 10.4.2]
);

–
\epsilon=1/m
if
\Omega
is
m
-convex (by Lemma
3
);

–
\epsilon=1/m
if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is pseudoconvex of finite type
m
(see
[
Cat
, Theorem 1]
);

–
\epsilon>0
if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is pseudoconvex of finite type (see
[
Cho
, Theorem 1]
).

Note that such domains are SG (assuming Dini smoothness if
\Omega
is
m
-convex) as the following
proposition shows.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
d>0
. Then
the function
\omega(r):=Cr^{\epsilon}
is SG on (0,d] if and only
C\geq d^{1-\epsilon}e^{c_{\epsilon}}
, where
c_{\epsilon}:=\max\{{-1+1/\epsilon},1\}
.
Moreover, for a fixed constant
c\in(0,1),

g_{\omega}(r)\sim r^{\epsilon}\log(1/r)\quad\text{and}\quad g^{-1}_{\omega}(r)\sim\left(\frac{r}{\log(1/r)}\right)^{1/\epsilon},\quad 0\leq r\leq c.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly. Straightforward calculations lead to

g_{\omega}(r)=\frac{Cr^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\left((1-\epsilon)\log\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}+\log C\right).

Then

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g_{\omega}(r)}{r^{\epsilon}\log(1/r)}=\frac{C(1-\epsilon)}{\epsilon},\quad\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g^{-1}_{\omega}(r)}{(\frac{r}{\log(1/r)})^{1/\epsilon}}=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{s}{(\frac{g_{\omega}(s)}{\log(1/g_{\omega}(s))})^{1/\epsilon}}=\left(\frac{\epsilon}{C(1-\epsilon)}\right)^{1/\epsilon}

and the proof easily follows.
∎

So we may say that a domain
\Omega
is
SG_{\epsilon}
if it has Dini-smooth boundary and (
4
) holds with
\epsilon\in(0,1/2].

3.3.
Quantitative visibility

The goal of this subsection is to present Theorem
9
, which can be considered to be the main theorem of this paper. It concerns SG domains and it gives a way to measure how much the geodesics tend to go inside the domain, in other words, it gives a quantitative version of visibility. Notice that it roughly implies that the larger
\omega_{\Omega}
is the ”less” visible
\Omega
is. In the convex case, by Lemma
3
, this situation occurs when the boundary gets more and more flat. Moreover, it also shows that the geodesics which join points that are further away in the Euclidean distance tend to stay further away from the boundary.

From now on, for a SG domain
\Omega
with a SG function
\omega_{\Omega},
we set
g_{\Omega}:=g_{\omega_{\Omega}}.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\Omega
be a SG domain and
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a
\lambda
-geodesic. Then there exists
C>0,
depending only on
\Omega
and
\lambda,
such that

l_{e}(\gamma)\leq Cg_{\Omega}(D_{\gamma}).

Recall that
D_{\gamma}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma(t))
and
l_{e}(\gamma)
denotes the Euclidean length of
\gamma
.

Let
\Omega
be as above and
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a
\lambda
-geodesic on
\Omega
which joins
x\in\Omega
to
y\in\Omega
. We want to compare its Euclidean length with the quantity
D_{\gamma}
in terms of the function
g_{\Omega}
. To do so, we will first divide
\gamma
into pieces with endpoints of comparable boundary distance.

Set
D:=D_{\gamma}
and let
N_{x},N_{y}
denote the integers such that

\dfrac{D}{e^{N_{x}}}\leq\delta_{\Omega}(x)<\dfrac{D}{e^{N_{x}-1}}\>\text{and}\>\dfrac{D}{e^{N_{y}}}\leq\delta_{\Omega}(y)<\dfrac{D}{e^{N_{y}-1}}.

Travelling from
x
to
y
, for
i\in\{-N_{x}+1,0\}
, set
t_{i}=\min\{t\in[0,1]:\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma(t))=De^{i}\}
,
x_{i}:=\gamma(t_{i})
and for
i\in\{0,N_{y}-1\}
set
t_{i}:=\max\{t\in[0,1]:\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma(t))=De^{-i}\}
,
y_{i}:=\gamma(t_{i})
. Furthermore, let
t_{-N_{x}}:=0
,
t_{N_{y}}:=1
,
x_{-N_{x}}:=x
and
y_{N_{y}}=y
.
We set

\gamma_{i}:=\gamma|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
if
i\leq-1
,
\gamma_{i}:=\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]}
if

and
\gamma_{0}
to be the remaining piece of
\gamma
, i.e. the piece of
\gamma
that connects
x_{0}
to
y_{0}
. Also let
D_{i}:=De^{i}
if
i\leq 0
and
D_{i}=De^{-i}
if

. By construction we have

(5)

\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq eD_{i}\>\>\>\text{if}\>\>\>z\in\gamma_{i}\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(z)\in\{D_{i},eD_{i}\}\>\>\>\text{if $z$ is an endpoint of}\>\gamma_{i}.

Our proof of Theorem

is heavily inspired from
[

]
, where they follow the ideas given in
[
GH
]
. Essentially, the lemma below allows us to get somewhat better bounds.

Lemma 10
.

There exists
C>0,
depending only on
\Omega
and
\lambda
, such that
\forall i\in\{-N_{x},...,N_{y}\}

l_{e}(\gamma_{i})\leq C\omega_{\Omega}(D_{i})\log\left(\dfrac{\omega_{\Omega}(D_{i})}{D_{i}}\right).

Proof.

As
\gamma
is a
\lambda
-geodesic it follows that each
\gamma_{i}
is also a
\lambda
-geodesic. Thus, by Lemma
1
, (
5
) implies that there exists

independent from
\gamma
such that

(6)

l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma_{i})\leq\lambda\log\left(1+\dfrac{C_{0}l_{e}(\gamma_{i})}{D_{i}}\right).

On the other hand, by Definition
6
and (
5
) we have

l_{e}(\gamma_{i})\leq\omega_{\Omega}(eD_{i})l^{\Omega}_{\kappa}(\gamma_{i}).

Setting
C_{2}:=\max\{C_{0},\lambda e\}

), (
6
) and (
7

(8)

l_{e}(\gamma_{i})\leq C_{2}\omega_{\Omega}(D_{i})\log\left(1+\dfrac{C_{2}l_{e}(\gamma_{i})}{D_{i}}\right).

Claim.
For any
C>0
, there exists

such that if

,

and

, then

To see this, set

is a positive continuous function on

and
\lim_{x\to`\infty}h(x)=C

. Since

is an increasing function for

and

we obtain

Recall that we assume that
\omega_{\Omega}(r)/r\geq e

) by setting

,
x:=C_{2}l_{e}(\gamma_{i})/D_{i}
and

\min\{l_{e}(\gamma_{x,z}),l_{e}(\gamma_{z,y})\}\leq l_{e}(\gamma_{x,z})\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{j=-N_{x}}^{i}l_{e}(\gamma_{j})}\leq

4.
A Gehring-Hayman type theorem

Recall the classical Gehring-Hayman inequality for simply connected planar domains.

Theorem 13
.

[
GH
]

Let
G\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected planar domain. There exists
C>0
not depending on
G
such that the inequality

l_{e}(\gamma)\leq Cl_{e}(\gamma^{\prime})

holds, where
\gamma:[0,1]\to G
is a Kobayashi geodesic and
\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\to G
is any other curve joining the same two points.

For a deeper discussion on the above result see the survey
[
PR
]
.

In
[
LWZ
]
, the authors provided extensions of Theorem
13
to strongly pseudoconvex domains and bounded
m
-convex domains with Dini-smooth boundary in

. In this section we will extend and improve
[

, Theorem 4.1]
and
[

, Theorem 1.5]

Let
\Omega
be a domain in

and recall that

,
U_{p}
and a biholomorphism

such that

is a convex boundary point of

is convexifiable if any

is a convexifiable boundary point. The goal of this section is to prove the following result, which extends Theorem

to convexifiable SG domains.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\Omega
be a convexifiable SG domain and
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a Kobayashi geodesic joining

Furthermore, if

\lambda

Combining Proposition

Corollary 15
.

Let

\gamma

-geodesic joining

(ii) Let

The proof of Theorem

is motivated by proof of this statement on the simpler yet less general case where

with the additional assumption that

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
14
in the convex case

Note that since
\Omega
is a convexifiable bounded domain it is complete hyperbolic, hence any
x,y\in\Omega
is joined by a Kobayashi geodesic. Moreover, by Lempert’s Theorem (see e.g.
[
JP
, Theorem 11.2.1]
)
\kappa_{\Omega}=\hat{\kappa}_{\Omega}
, then Remark
1
implies that the Kobayashi geodesics of
\Omega
are also
1
-geodesics.

Having this in mind, let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a Kobayashi geodesic joining
x
to
y
and set
D:=D_{\gamma}
. Choose
z\in\Omega\cap\gamma(I)
such that
\delta_{\Omega}(z)=D
. By Lemma
2
and Lemma
1
we have

\log\left(\dfrac{D}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{D}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{D}{\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\right)

\leq k_{\Omega}(x,z)+k_{\Omega}(z,y)=k_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\log\left(1+\dfrac{C\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}\right).

So we obtain

D\leq C\|x-y\|+\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}.

Case I.
First, we assume that the points are close, that is,

(9)

\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\geq\|x-y\|\>\>\>\>\>\text{so}\>\>\>\>\>D\leq(C+1)\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}.

By Lemma
1
, Definition
6
and the fact that
\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x\geq 0
we have that

(10)

\dfrac{l_{e}(\gamma)}{\omega_{\Omega}(D)}\leq\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dx=k_{\Omega}(x,y)}\leq\frac{C\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}.

Recall that by Remark
2
the function
\omega_{\Omega}
is increasing. Then by (
9
) and (
10
) we get

l_{e}(\gamma)\leq C\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(D)\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)})}\leq C\frac{\omega_{\Omega}((C+1)\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)})\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}.

Continuing above and using (
3
), by the assumption that
\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\geq\|x-y\|
we obtain

l_{e}(\gamma)\leq C\frac{\omega_{\Omega}((C+1)\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)})\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}

\leq C(C+1)\frac{\omega_{\Omega}(\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)})\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}}\leq C(C+1)\omega_{\Omega}(\|x-y\|).

By Proposition
7
this case follows.

Case II.
Now, we assume that the points are far, that is,

(11)

\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\leq\|x-y\|\>\>\>\>\>\text{so}\>\>\>\>\>D\leq(1+C)\|x-y\|.

Using (
3
) again, by Theorem
9
and (
11
), we immediately have

l_{e}(\gamma)\leq C^{\prime}g_{\Omega}(D)\leq C^{\prime}g_{\Omega}((1+C)\|x-y\|)\leq C^{\prime}(1+C)g_{\Omega}(\|x-y\|).

As
\gamma
is chosen arbitrarily, the theorem follows for Kobayashi geodesics.

Now we move on to the case of
\lambda
-geodesics. Assume that
\Omega
is Gromov hyperbolic and let
\sigma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
be a
\lambda
-geodesic joining
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
. Set
D^{\prime}:=D_{\sigma}
and let
z^{\prime}
be a point in the image of
\sigma
satisfying
\delta_{\Omega}(z^{\prime})=D^{\prime}
. Consider a Kobayashi geodesic
\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\Omega
joining
x^{\prime}
to
y^{\prime}
. Lemma
5
implies that there exists

in the image of
\gamma^{\prime}
with

, where

is a constant depending only on

and
\lambda
. Then by Lemma

\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(z^{\prime})}{\delta_{\Omega}(w)}\right)\leq k_{\Omega}(z^{\prime},w)\leq K<\infty.

Hence
D^{\prime}=\delta_{\Omega}(z^{\prime})\leq e^{2K}\delta_{\Omega}(w)\leq e^{2K}D_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.

Therefore by (

) and above we have

(12)

\omega_{\Omega}(D^{\prime})\leq e^{2K}\omega_{\Omega}(D_{\gamma^{\prime}})\>\text{and}\>g_{\Omega}(D^{\prime})\leq e^{2K}g_{\Omega}(D_{\gamma^{\prime}}).

By (

), the estimate

l_{e}(\sigma)\leq\lambda\omega_{\Omega}(D^{\prime})\log\left(1+\dfrac{C\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(x^{\prime})\delta_{\Omega}(y^{\prime})}}\right),

and the estimate

\hfill\square

c>0
such that

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{g_{\Omega}^{-1}(c\|x-y\|)}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a Kobayashi geodesic joining
x
and
y
and let
z
be the point on image of
\gamma
satisfying
\delta_{\Omega}(z)=D_{\gamma}
.

By Theorem
9
, we have that
\|x-y\|\leq l_{e}(\gamma)\leq Cg_{\Omega}(D_{\gamma}).
Then,
by Theorem
20
,

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq k_{\Omega}(x,z)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{c^{\prime}D_{\gamma}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{g_{\Omega}^{-1}(c\|x-y\|)}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right),

where
c=c^{\prime}/C.
(Here we assume that
c^{\prime}\leq 1;
then
c^{\prime}g_{\Omega}^{-1}(r)\geq g_{\Omega}^{-1}(c^{\prime}r)

as a consequence of (
3
)).
∎

Proposition 22
.

Let
\Omega
be a convexifiable SG domain. Then there exists
c>0
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
we have that

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{c\|x-y\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))}\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

Proof.

For convenience, let us first start with the simple case where
\Omega
is convex. By definition we have

(16)

\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(x;v)}\geq\kappa_{\Omega}(x;v)\geq\dfrac{\|v\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))}

so Lemma
4
and Proposition
7
directly implies that

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}\left(x;x-y\right)}\right)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{\|x-y\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))}\right).

So in this case proposition follows.

We will prove the convexifiable case by contradiction. Suppose that such an estimate does not hold, that is, there exists a sequence of points
x_{n},y_{n}\in\Omega
tending to
p,q\in\overline{\Omega}
respectively and a sequence
C_{n}\rightarrow 0
such that

k_{\Omega}(x_{n},y_{n})\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{C_{n}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n}))}\right).

It follows from the inequality
k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq c\|x-y\|
(see e.g.
[
BZ
, Proposition 3.5]
) valid on any bounded domain that we must have
p\in\partial\Omega
.

Furthermore, by Lemma
19
we see that
p
is a
k
-point, so if
x_{n},y_{n}\rightarrow p,q
and
p\neq q
we have

k_{\Omega}(x_{n},y_{n})\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n})}\right)-c\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n}))}\right)-c

therefore we must have
q=p\in\partial\Omega
.

From now on our approach will be similar to the proof of Theorem
20
.
Note that if
f:D_{1}\rightarrow D_{2}
is a biholomorphism of the domains
D_{1},D_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
that extends to the boundary, then

(17)

\|x-y\|\sim\|f(x)-f(y)\|\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>\dfrac{\|x-y\|}{\delta_{D_{1}}\left(x;\dfrac{v}{\|v\|}\right)}\sim\dfrac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|}{\delta_{D_{2}}\left(f(x);\dfrac{df_{x}v}{\|df_{x}v\|}\right)},\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in D_{1},\>\>\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Now, we take a neighbourhood of
p
,
U_{p}
so that
\Psi
is a biholomorphism of
U_{p}
so that
G:=\Psi(\Omega\cap U_{p})
is convex. By (
17
), applying Lemma
4
to
G
and Lemma
17
to
\Omega
and
\Omega\cap U_{p}
gives

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq k_{\Omega\cap U_{p}}(x,y)-c=k_{G}(\Psi(x),\Psi(y))-c

(18)

\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{c^{\prime}\|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)\|}{\delta_{\Omega}\left(x;d\Psi^{-1}_{\Psi(x)}\dfrac{\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)}{\|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)\|}\right)}\right)-c.

Due to (
16
), (
18
) shows that
p=q
case is also impossible. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Although they are proven with global assumptions, we remark that the estimates above are also of local character. That is to say, if
U_{p}
is a small enough convexifiable neighbourhood of
p\in\partial\Omega
is and if the SG domain conditions are satisfied near
p
, there is another neighbourhood of
p
,
U^{\prime}_{p}\subset\subset U_{p}
such that for
x,y\in U^{\prime}_{p}
the estimates given above are satisfied. To see this one may use the localizations
k_{\Omega}\geq k_{\Omega\cap U_{p}}-c
given in Lemma
17
and
ck_{\Omega}\geq k_{\Omega\cap U_{p}}
due to
[
FR
]
. For details, the interested reader may examine the proofs of
[
BNT
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
NT
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Another thing to note is that these estimates are only good when
x,y
are close. By Lemma
19
, any
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
k
-point, thus by Lemma
16
when
x
and
y
are far we have the much better estimate

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Omega}(y)}\right)-c.

It is not difficult to see that the proof of Proposition
22
implicitly suggests that an analogue of Lemma
4
holds on convexifiable SG domains. This means, in some sense, that Proposition
22
is not sharp. We include it in order to compare it with Proposition
21
to get a unified estimate. To state our results, we set

h_{\Omega,1}(x,y,c):=\dfrac{c\|x-y\|}{g_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))},\>\>h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,c):=\dfrac{g_{\Omega}^{-1}(c\|x-y\|)}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)},

and
h_{\Omega}:=\max\{h_{\Omega,1},h_{\Omega,2}\}
. With this notation we have:

Theorem 23
.

Let
\Omega
be a convexifiable SG domain. There exists
c>0
such that the Kobayashi distance on
\Omega
satisfies

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+h_{\Omega}(x,y,c)\right)\left(1+h_{\Omega}(y,x,c)\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

Proof.

We will prove this corollary by comparing the lower bounds given in Proposition
21
and Proposition
22
.

It follows by the definition of
k_{\Omega}
that for any
x,y\in\Omega
and any
\epsilon>0

one may find a point
z\in D
such that
\|x-z\|=\|y-z\|
and
k_{\Omega}(x,y)>k_{\Omega}(x,z)+k_{\Omega}(y,z)-\epsilon.

Set
c:=\frac{c^{\prime}}{4},
where
c^{\prime}>0
is chosen such that Proposition
21
and Proposition
22

hold. As the functions
g_{\Omega}^{-1}(r)/r
and
r/g_{\Omega}(r)
are increasing by Proposition
7
,
we see that we either have

h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,c)\geq h_{\Omega,1}(x,y,c)\geq 1\>\text{or}\>h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,c)\leq h_{\Omega,1}(x,y,c)\leq 1.

Case I.

h_{\Omega,2}\geq h_{\Omega,1}\geq 1
.

Proposition
21
leads to

k_{\Omega}(x,z)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log(h_{\Omega,2}(x,z,c^{\prime}))\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log(h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,2c))\geq

\dfrac{1}{2}\log(2h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,c))\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log(1+h_{\Omega,2}(x,y,c)).

Case II.

1\geq h_{\Omega,1}\geq h_{\Omega,2}
.

By Proposition
7
we have
g_{\Omega}\geq\omega_{\Omega}
, so
Proposition
22
immediately implies that

k_{\Omega}(x,z)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{c^{\prime}\|x-z\|}{\omega_{\Omega}(\delta_{\Omega}(x))}\right)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log(1+h_{\Omega,1}(x,y,c)).

In both cases we obtain

k_{\Omega}(x,z)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+h_{\Omega}(x,y,c)\right).

Analogously,

k_{\Omega}(y,z)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+h_{\Omega}(y,x,c)\right).

Thus

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+h_{\Omega}(x,y,c)\right)\left(1+h_{\Omega}(y,x,c)\right)-\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
the theorem follows.
∎

In order to make a final remark we recall the following result:

Theorem 24
.

[
NT
, Theorem 1.6]

Let
\Omega
be a
(
\mathbb{C}
-)
convexifiable domain of finite
m
-type. Then there exists
c>0
such that the Kobayashi distance on
\Omega
satisfies

k_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq\dfrac{m}{2}\log\left(1+\dfrac{c\|x-y\|}{\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}_{\Omega}(x)}\right)\left(1+\dfrac{c\|x-y\|}{\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}_{\Omega}(y)}\right),\>\>\>\>\>x,y\in\Omega.

If
\Omega
is
m
-convex with Dini-smooth boundary we have

h_{\Omega}(x,y,c)=\max\left\{\dfrac{(c\|x-y\|)^{m}}{(-\log(c\|x-y\|))^{m}\delta_{\Omega}(x)},\dfrac{c\|x-y\|}{-\log(\delta_{\Omega}(x))\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}_{\Omega}(x)}\right\}.

Thus, we see that ignoring the ”parasite” logarithmic terms, on Dini-smooth
m
-convex case, Theorem
23
gives the same estimate as Theorem
24
. In particular, by the examples given in
[
NT
, Section 1.7]
our estimates are pretty sharp. Although our result is slightly worse, it holds for domains with much lower boundary regularity. Moreover, our construction avoids the machinery of peak functions.

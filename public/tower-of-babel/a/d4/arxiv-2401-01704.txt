Global Well-posedness for 2D non-resistive MHD equations in half-space
Zhaoyun Zhang, Xiaopeng Zhao 1. School of Mathematical Sciences, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 200201, China2. College of Sciences, Northeastern University, Shenyang 110819, China

Abstract
This paper focuses on the initial boundary value problem of two-dimensional non-resistive MHD equations in a half space. We prove that the MHD equations have a unique global strong solution around the equilibrium state ( 0 , 𝐞 𝟏 ) (0,\bf{e_{1}}) for Dirichlet boundary condition of velocity and modified Neumann boundary condition of magnetic. MSC : 35A01; 35Q30; 76D05 Key words : Non-resistive MHD, Global regularity, Asymptotic estimates, Half space

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the initial boundary value problem of
2D incompressible non-resistive magnetohydrodynamic equations (GMHD)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u=-\nabla\pi+B\cdot\nabla B,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},~t>0,\\
B_{t}+u\cdot\nabla B=B\cdot\nabla u,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},~t>0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad\nabla\cdot B=0,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},~t>0,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u=u(x,t)=(u^{1}(x,t),u^{2}(x,t))
,
B=B(x,t)=(B^{1}(x,t),B^{2}(x,t))
and
\pi=\pi(x,t)

denote the velocity field, the magnetic field and the pressure, respectively. In addition,
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},x_{1}\in(-\infty,+\infty),x_{2}\in(0,+\infty)\}
, thus, the outer normal vector of
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
is
{\bf n}:=(0,-1)
.

It is well-known that MHD equations model the dynamics of the velocity and magnetic fields, which plays
an important role in describing electrically conducting fluids, such as plasmas, liquid metals. When the magnetic diffusion is included, it is well-known that the 2D MHD equations have the global smooth solution
[
3
,
19
]
. Moreover, by following Kato’s arguments
[
14
]
and applying the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates of Stokes semigroup established in
[
20
]
, there exists a unique strong solution in half-space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
when initial data are small (small condition is unnecessary if
n=2
), e.g.
[
11
,
10
]
.
But without magnetic diffusion, the question of whether smooth solution of the 2D MHD equations develops singularity in finite time has been a long-standing open problem. The local existence result of smooth solutions for non-resistive MHD equations was established in
[
13
,
4
,
5
]
. In addition, more recently Ren et al.
[
17
]
and Lin et al.
[
15
]
have established the existence of global-in-time solutions for initial data are small and sufficiently close to certain equilibrium solutions in whole space, respectively. Further, Ren et al.
[
18
]
have established similar results in a strip domain with non-slip boundary condition and Navier slip boundary condition on the velocity.

In this paper, we are concerned with the non-slip boundary condition on the velocity
u
, i.e.

u(x,t)|_{x\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}}=0,

(1.2)

as well as that the boundary condition for the magnetic
B
, i.e.

\nabla^{T}B(x,t)\cdot{\bf n}|_{x\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}}=0,

(1.3)

where
\nabla^{T}=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})
. More precisely,
we give the detail of the boundary condition for the magnetic
B
.

In fact,
\nabla^{T}B(x,t)\cdot{\bf n}=0
on
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
, which deduces that

\partial_{1}B=0
on
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
,
i.e.
\partial_{1}B^{1}=\partial_{1}B^{2}=0
on
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
.
Due to
\nabla\cdot B=0
,
\partial_{2}B^{2}=0
. Since
\partial_{1}B^{2}=0
and
B^{2}(x_{1},x_{2},t)=0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
, therefore
B^{2}=0
on
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
. The questions about well-posedness become more subtle and more difficult when physical boundaries are present. However, it seems that this question can be handled when boundary conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are given.

We will investigate small perturbations of the system (
1.1
) around the equilibrium state
(0,\bf{e}_{1})
in half-space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
. Hence, setting
b=B-\bf{e}_{1}
and reformulating (
1.1
) as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u-\partial_{1}b=-\nabla\pi+b\cdot\nabla b,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},t>0\\
b_{t}+u\cdot\nabla b-\partial_{1}u=b\cdot\nabla u,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},t>0\\
\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot b=0,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},t>0\\
u=0,~~\nabla^{T}b\cdot{\bf n}=0,~~x\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+},t>0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~~b(x,0)=b_{0}(x),~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

There are two main difficulties in the existence of strong solutions. The first one arises from the partial dissipation of magnetic
b
. In order to control the nonlinearities, it needs the dissipation of velocity
u
and magnetic
b
. It is known that the dissipation of
u
is easy to obtained, whereas the dissipation of
b
is more subtle without the magnetic diffusion. Fortunately, this difficulty can be overcome by a delicate potential estimates. Motivated by
[
18
,
21
]
, through replacing
\Delta\partial_{1}u
by
\partial_{1}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{1}^{2}b+\partial_{1}\nabla\pi-\partial_{1}(b\cdot\nabla b-u\cdot\nabla u)
, inner product with
\mathcal{A}b
, combining with that
{\bf P}
commutates with
\partial_{1}
in half-space, it follows that the dissipation for
b
is of form
\|\partial_{1}\nabla b\|_{L^{2}}
rather than
\|\nabla^{2}b\|_{L^{2}}
. The other difficulty results from the nonlinearity in half-space, i.e.
{\bf P}
cannot communate with
\nabla
. This needs a series of careful energy estimates to deal with that.

As for the long time behaviour of solutions, we are only able to obtain the time asymptotic estimates without decay rates. Without the magnetic diffusion and boundary condition present, it leads to some trouble in dealing with decay rates, which will be left to our future’s work.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we give some basic interpolation inequalities, properties about the Helmholtz projection
{\bf P}
and our main results. The global well-posedness of solutions is established in section 3. Finally, we prove the time asmyptotic estimates in section 4.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle u_{t},\partial_{t}(u\cdot\nabla u)\rangle+\langle u_{t},\partial_{t}(b\cdot\nabla b)\rangle+\langle\partial_{1}b_{t},u_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}}(\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2});

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u_{t},b_{t}\cdot\nabla b\rangle+\langle u_{t},b\cdot\nabla b_{t}\rangle=-\langle b_{t}\cdot\nabla u_{t},b\rangle-\langle b\cdot\nabla u_{t},b_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\|b_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|b\|_{H^{2}}(\|b_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2});

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\partial_{1}u_{t}b_{t}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining these estimates with (

(3.24)

4
Time asymptotic estimates

In this section, notice from (
3.35
) that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}dt\leq O(1),\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt\leq O(1).\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

It follows from (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}|dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq O(1).

(4.2)

Combining (
4.2
) with
(\ref{5.1})_{1}
yields

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+})}=0.

(4.3)

Since the Sobolev’s inequality

\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}

for
2<p<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
, we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u\|_{L^{p}}=0,~~2<p<\infty.

(4.4)

We now aim to get the asymptotic estimate of magnetic field
b
.

For
2<p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|b\|_{L^{p}}^{q}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}^{(1-\frac{2}{p})q}\|b\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}q}d\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\|b\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})q}\|\partial_{1}b\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})q}d\tau

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial_{1}b\|_{H^{1}}^{\sigma}d\tau\leq O(1),

where
q\geq\frac{4p}{p-2}
or
\sigma\geq 2
.
In addition,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\frac{d}{dt}\|b\|_{L^{p}}^{q}|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\|b\|_{L^{p}}^{q-p}\|\partial_{t}b\|_{L^{2}}\|b\|_{L^{2(p-1)}}^{p-1}dt

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial_{t}b\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{p}}^{2(q-p)}\|b\|_{2(p-1)}^{2(p-1)}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial_{t}b\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{p}}^{q}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1),

where

.
Hence,
it follows from (

) and (

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0,~~t\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, that completes the proof of Theorem 2.1.

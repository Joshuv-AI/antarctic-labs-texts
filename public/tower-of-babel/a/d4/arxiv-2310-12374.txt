Free weakly Novikov metabelian algebra of infinite rank
Iritan Ferreira dos Santos, Alexey M. Kuz’min

Abstract
. An explicit base with multiplication table is obtained for the free weakly Novikov metabelian algebra of infinite rank over an arbitrary field of characteristic ≠ 2 \neq 2 . Keywords: Right symmetric algebra, metabelian algebra, Novikov algebra, free algebra of variety. MSC 2020: 17A30, 17A50, 17D25, 17D99.



1.
Introduction

Weakly Novikov rings appeared in papers
by E. Kleinfeld and H. F. Smith
[
2
,
3
]

as the rings satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=(x,z,y)\quad\textit{(the right symmetry identity)},

(2)

\displaystyle x(y,z,t)

\displaystyle=(y,z,xt)\quad\textit{(the weakly Novikov identity)},

where
(x,y,z)=(xy)z-x(yz)
stands, as usual, for the
associator
in variables
x,y,z
.
Note that (
2
) generalizes

(3)

x(yz)=y(xz)\quad\textit{(the left commutativity identity)}.

For more results on nonmultilinear generalizations of identity (
2
),
see recent papers by Samanta and Hentzel
[
12
,
13
]
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic distinct from
2
,

\Weakly
be the variety of algebras over
F
defined by (
1
) and (
2
), and

\Nv
be the Novikov subvariety of
\Weakly
, i. e. the subvariety distinguished by (
3
).
Following
[
5
]
, we set
\mathcal{V}^{(2)}
to be the metabelian subvartiety of a given variety
\mathcal{V}
, i. e. the subvariety distinguished by

(4)

(xy)(zt)=0\quad\textit{(the metabelian identity)}.

It’s known from the paper by Shestakov and Zhang
[
14
]
that
\Nv^{(2)}
is left nilpotent of index not more then
9
.
In the present paper, we obtain an explicit base with multiplication table for the free algebra of
\Weakly^{(2)}
on the countable set of generators.
As a corollary, we prove that
\Weakly^{(2)}
and
\Nv^{(2)}
are both left nilpotent of exact index
5
.

We stress that questions on finding certain effective bases for free algebras in given varieties are strongly motivated by a number of open problems dealing with varieties of all alternative, Jordan, and Maltsev algebras (see, for ex.
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
).

2.1.
Monomial base for the free algebra of
\WeaklyLC^{(2)}

Theorem 1
.

The free algebra

{\mathfrak{A}}=F_{\WeaklyLC^{(2)}}\left<X\right>

possesses a basis formed by all monomials of the form

(5)

x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}R_{x_{k_{1}}}\dots R_{x_{k_{t}}},

such that the variables

x_{j_{1}},\dots,x_{j_{n}}
,
for

n\geqslant 4
,
are invariant with respect to even permutations and the multiplication in

\left({\mathfrak{A}},\cdot\right)
is defined by the table

\displaystyle x_{j}\cdot x_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{i}L_{x_{j}},

\displaystyle\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j}}\right)\cdot x_{k}

\displaystyle=x_{i}L_{x_{j}}R_{x_{k}},

\displaystyle x_{q}\cdot\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j}}R_{x_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=x_{k}L_{x_{i}}L_{x_{j}}L_{x_{q}}-x_{k}L_{x_{q}}L_{x_{i}}L_{x_{j}},

\displaystyle\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}\right)\cdot x_{q}

\displaystyle=x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}R_{x_{q}},

\displaystyle x_{q}\cdot\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}L_{x_{q}},

\displaystyle\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}R_{x_{k_{1}}}\dots R_{x_{k_{t}}}\right)\cdot x_{q}

\displaystyle=x_{i}L_{x_{j_{1}}}\dots L_{x_{j_{n}}}R_{x_{k_{1}}}\dots R_{x_{k_{t}}}R_{x_{q}},

where all possible nonzero products of base monomials are given.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we use metabelian identity (
4
) with no comments.
Consider all possible restrictions of left commutativity identity (
2
) such that one of its variables is assumed to be taken form
{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}
.
First, by

x\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}
, we have

(6)

{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}R_{t}L_{z}L_{y}=0.

Further, by setting

y\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}
, we get

{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}R_{z}R_{t}L_{x}=0.

Moreover, in view of (
6
),

z\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}
, yields

{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}[L_{y},R_{t}]L_{x}={\mathfrak{A}}^{2}L_{y}R_{t}L_{x}=0.

Consequently,

(7)

{\mathfrak{A}}^{3}R_{t}L_{x}=0.

Furthermore,

t\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}
implies

(8)

{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}\left[L_{z}L_{y},L_{x}\right]=0.

Finally, by (
6
) and (
2
), we obtain

xL_{y}L_{z}L_{t}L_{u}=-(z,y,x)L_{t}L_{u}=-(z,y,tx)L_{u}=xL_{t}L_{y}L_{z}L_{u},

i. e.

(9)

{\mathfrak{A}}\left[L_{y}L_{z},L_{t}\right]L_{u}=0.

Therefore, by virute of (
6
)–(
9
),
{\mathfrak{A}}
is spanned by the monomials of type (
5
).

To complete the proof it remains to check that identity (
2
) holds in
\left({\mathfrak{A}},\cdot\right)
.
Indeed, on generators of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, we have

x_{q}\cdot\left(x_{j},x_{i},x_{k}\right)=x_{q}\cdot\left(x_{i}L_{x_{j}}R_{x_{k}}-x_{k}L_{x_{i}}L_{x_{j}}\right)=-x_{k}L_{x_{q}}L_{x_{i}}L_{x_{j}}=\left(x_{j},x_{i},x_{q}\cdot x_{k}\right);

otherwise, (
2
) is an immediate consequence of relations (
6
)-(
8
).
∎

Corollary 1.1
.

Every Lie-nilpotent or Jordan-nilpotent subvariety of index
n
in
\WeaklyLC^{(2)}
is nilpotent of index not more then
2n+1
.

Proof.

Let us prove that, for
{\mathfrak{B}}\in\WeaklyLC^{(2)}
,

H_{x}=R_{x}-L_{x}
,
and

\varTheta_{x}=R_{x}+L_{x}
, each of the relations

{\mathfrak{B}}^{2}H_{x_{1}}\dots H_{x_{n-1}}=0,\quad{\mathfrak{B}}^{2}\varTheta_{x_{1}}\dots\varTheta_{x_{n}-1}=0

yields

{\mathfrak{B}}^{2n+1}=0
.
In the case
n=1
, the affirmation is trivial in view of (
6
).
Further, we set

R^{n}=R_{x_{1}}\dots R_{x_{n}},\quad L^{n}=R_{x_{1}}\dots L_{x_{n}},\quad H^{n}=H_{x_{1}}\dots H_{x_{n}},\quad n\geqslant 2.

By virtue of (
6
) and (
7
), the relations

{\mathfrak{B}}^{2}H^{n}L_{y}=0,\quad{\mathfrak{B}}^{3}R_{y}H^{n}=0

yield, respectively,

{\mathfrak{B}}^{2}L^{n+1}=0,\quad{\mathfrak{B}}^{3}R^{n+1}=0.

Therefore, by Theorem
1
,

{\mathfrak{B}}^{3}T_{x_{1}}\dots T_{x_{2n}}=0,

for all

T_{x_{i}}\in\left\{R_{x_{i}},L_{x_{i}}\right\}
.
∎

Corollary 1.2
.

The flexible and the antiflexible subvarieties of
\WeaklyLC^{(2)}
are both nilpotent of index not more than
5
.
Moreover, for
{\mathfrak{B}}\in\WeaklyLC^{(2)}
and
n\geqslant 2
, the identity

(10)

\left({\mathfrak{B}}^{n},x,y\right)=\pm\left(y,x,{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}\right)

implies

{\mathfrak{B}}^{n+3}=0
.

Proof.

By virtue of (
4
), identity (
10
) yields

{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}R_{x}R_{y}=\mp{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}L_{x}L_{y}.

Thus, in view of (

), we have

{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}L_{x}L_{y}R_{z}=\mp{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}R_{x}R_{y}R_{z}={\mathfrak{B}}^{n}R_{x}L_{y}L_{z}=0.

Similarly, by (

{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}L_{x}R_{y}R_{z}=\mp{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}L_{x}L_{y}L_{z}={\mathfrak{B}}^{n}R_{x}R_{y}L_{z}=0.

Therefore, by Theorem

,

{\mathfrak{B}}^{n+3}=0
.
∎

Main Theorem

3.1.
Auxiliary identities

Let

{\mathfrak{A}}=F_{\Weakly^{(2)}}\left<X\right>

be the free algebra of the variety
\Weakly^{(2)}
on the countable set

X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
of generators;
the symbol
T_{x}\in\{R_{x},L_{x}\}
is used as a common denotation for the
operators

R_{x}
and
L_{x}

of right and left multiplication by
x\in X
, respectively.

Lemma 3.1
.

The free algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
satisfies the identities

(11)

\displaystyle x\cdot(y,z,t)

\displaystyle=\left(y,xz,t\right),

(12)

\displaystyle(x,y,zt)

\displaystyle=(x,zy,t),

(13)

\displaystyle x\cdot y(zt)

\displaystyle=-(x,zy,t),

(14)

\displaystyle x(yz)\cdot t

\displaystyle=\bigl(x,[y,z],t\bigr)+(y,xz,t),

(15)

\displaystyle\left(x,y,z\right)\cdot t

\displaystyle=\Tch(x,y,z,t)+\bigl(x,y\circ z,t\bigr),

where

\Tch(x,y,z,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(xy,z,t)-(y,xz,t)-2(x,yz,t).

Proof.

By virtue of (
2
) and (
1
), we have

x\cdot(y,z,t)=x\cdot(y,t,z)=(y,t,xz)=(y,xz,t).

Thus, (
11
) is proved.
Further, combining (
2
) and (
11
), we get (
12
):

(x,y,zt)=z\cdot(x,y,t)=(x,zy,t).

Furthermore, by (
4
) and (
12
), we obtain (
13
):

x\cdot y(zt)=-(x,y,zt)=-(x,zy,t).

Now, recall that every nonassociative ring satisfies the Teichmüller’s identity

x\cdot(y,z,t)+\left(x,y,z\right)\cdot t=(xy,z,t)-(x,yz,t)+(x,y,zt).

Hence, using (
11
) and (
12
), we have

\left(x,y,z\right)\cdot t=(xy,z,t)-(y,xz,t)-\bigl(x,\left[y,z\right],t\bigr).

Consequently, to prove (
14
) it remains to observe that

\left(x,y,z\right)\cdot t-\left(xy,z,t\right)=-x(yz)\cdot t,

in view of (
4
).
Moreover, taking into account the denotations, introduced above,

\left(x,y,z\right)\cdot t=(xy,z,t)-(y,xz,t)+\bigl(x,\left[z,y\right],t\bigr)=\\
=(xy,z,t)-(y,xz,t)-2(x,yz,t)+\bigl(x,y\circ z,t\bigr)=\\
=\Tch(x,y,z,t)+\bigl(x,y\circ z,t\bigr),

we complete the proof of (
15
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

A monomial

\mu\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{5}

can be nonzero only if it has the form

\mu=wR_{x}R_{y}R_{z}

for some

w\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}

and
x,y,z\in X

Proof.

Let us consider consecutively all possible cases for

\mu\neq wR_{x}R_{y}R_{z}
.
First, by (
2
) and (
4
), we have

(16)

\displaystyle wR_{x}R_{y}L_{z}

\displaystyle=z(w,x,y)-(w,x,zy)=0,

(17)

\displaystyle wR_{x}L_{y}L_{z}

\displaystyle=w(z,y,x)-(z,y,wx)=0.

Similarly, by (
4
) and (
11
), we get

(18)

wL_{x}L_{y}L_{z}=(y,zx,w)-z(y,x,w)=0.

Further, combining (
2
), (
4
), (
11
) with (
16
) and (
17
), we proceed

(19)

\displaystyle wR_{x}L_{y}R_{z}

\displaystyle=wR_{x}R_{z}L_{y}+(y,wx,z)-w(y,x,z)=0,

(20)

\displaystyle wL_{x}R_{y}L_{z}

\displaystyle=wR_{y}L_{x}L_{z}+z(x,w,y)-(x,w,zy)=0.

Now, by (
11
), (
17
), and (
20
), we obtain

(21)

wL_{x}L_{y}R_{z}=wL_{x}R_{z}L_{y}+w[L_{y},R_{z}]L_{x}+(y,xw,z)-x(y,w,z)=0.

Finally, using (
19
), (
21
), and taking into account (
1
), we prove

(22)

wL_{x}R_{y}R_{z}=wR_{y}L_{x}R_{z}-wL_{y}L_{x}R_{z}+(x,w,y)z-(x,y,w)z=0.

∎

3.2.
Pre-base

Recall that a function

f(x,y)

is said to be symmetric w.r.t.
x,y
if

f(x,y)=f(y,x).

Definition
.

The
base elements of
{\mathfrak{A}}^{3}
are all elements
of types
(
i
)–(
v
) defined by the list below, for
x,y,z,t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in X
:

(i)

x(yz)
,

(ii)

(x,t_{1},t_{2})
,

(iii)

\left(x,yt_{1},t_{2}\right)
,

(iv)

\Tch(x,t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
,

(v)

\left(xt_{1}\right)R_{t_{2}}\ldots R_{t_{k}}
,

where the indices
t_{i}
are used each time when the corresponding element possesses, by definition, the symmetry property w.r.t. all the subset
\left\{t_{i}\right\}
of its variables.

Lemma 3.4
.

The ideal
{\mathfrak{A}}^{3}
is spanned by its base elements.

Proof.

First,
taking into account the defining identity (
1
) for
\Weakly^{(2)}
,
it’s not hard to see that the subspace of polynomials of degree
3
in
{\mathfrak{A}}
is spanned by the elements of types (
i
) and (
ii
), in view of the trivial equality

(xy)z=(x,y,z)+x(yz).

Further, identities (
11
), (
13
)–(
15
), yield that
the subspace of polynomials of degree
4
in
{\mathfrak{A}}
is spanned by the elements of types (
iii
) and (
iv
).
While that the symmetry property established for the elements of type (
iii
) is a direct consequence of identity (
11
).
At the same time,
in the case of elements of type (
iv
), the required symmetry property can be obtained as follows.
On one hand, the polynomial

\Tch(x,y,z,t)

is defined as an symmetric element of
z,t
.
On the other hand, identity (
15
) implies
the symmetry of
\Tch(x,y,z,t)
with respect to
y,z
.

Furthermore, by Lemma
3.2
,
the subspace of polynomials of degree
\geqslant 5
in
{\mathfrak{A}}
is spanned by

R
-words.
Moreover, combining the defining identities (
1
) and (

) of
\Weakly^{(2)}

wR_{y}R_{z}=wR_{z}R_{y},\quad w\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}.

Finally, Lemma

\Tch(x,t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\cdot y=\left(xt_{1}\right)R_{t_{2}}R_{t_{3}}R_{y}.

).
∎

3.3.
Base

Theorem 2
.

The free algebra

{\mathfrak{A}}

possesses a base formed by all elements from
X
,
X^{2}
, and all base elements of
{\mathfrak{A}}^{3}

equipped with the multiplication
\cdot
introduced by the following rules:

•

Every nonzero left action of a generator

x\in X

on a base element is defined by one of the following formulas:

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=xy,

\displaystyle x\cdot yz

\displaystyle=x(yz),

\displaystyle x\cdot y(zt)

\displaystyle=-(x,zy,t),

\displaystyle x\cdot(y,t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(y,xt_{1},t_{2}\right),

where
y,z,t,t_{1},t_{2}\in X
.

•

Every nonzero right action of a generator

y\in X

on a base element of degree
\geqslant 2
is defined by one of the following formulas:

\displaystyle xz\cdot y

\displaystyle=(x,z,y)+x(zy),

\displaystyle x(zt)\cdot y

\displaystyle=\bigl(x,[z,t],y\bigr)+(z,xt,y),

\displaystyle\left(x,t_{1},t_{2}\right)\cdot y

\displaystyle=\Tch(x,t_{1},t_{2},y)+\bigl(x,t_{1}\circ t_{2},y\bigr),

\displaystyle\Tch(x,t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\cdot y

\displaystyle=\left(xt_{1}\right)R_{t_{2}}R_{t_{3}}R_{y},

\displaystyle\left(xt_{1}\right)R_{t_{2}}\ldots R_{t_{k}}\cdot y

\displaystyle=\left(xt_{1}\right)R_{t_{2}}\ldots R_{t_{k}}R_{y},\quad k\geqslant 4,

where
x,z,t,t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in X
.

•

All the other products of base elements not listed in the formulas above are null once by definition.

Proof.

First we stress that the multiplication
\cdot
is well-defined.
Indeed, by the direct verification, one can confirm that
all symmetry properties,
required for the elements of types (
ii
)–(
v
),
are inherited by the corresponding right parts in formulas of multiplication table.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the span of all introduced base elements over the field
F
. Consider
{\mathcal{B}}
as an algebra with respect to the multiplication
\cdot
defined by the rule of Theorem
2
.
Then, Lemma
3.4
yields that
{\mathfrak{A}}
could be isomorphic to some quotient of

{\mathcal{B}}
.
Let us prove that actually
{\mathfrak{A}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{B}}
under the isomorphism induced by the identical mapping

X\mapsto X
.

Our proof is by formal checking of defining identities (
2
)–(
4
) of the variety
\Weakly^{(2)}
for the algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
.
First, metabelian identity (
4
) holds in
{\mathcal{B}}
immediately, by definition of the multiplication
\cdot
.
Further, let us set

\bigl(a,b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(a\cdot b)\cdot c-a\cdot(b\cdot c).

In what follows, we assume that

x,y,z,t,u
are variables from
X
and
w
is a common denotation for base elements of
{\mathcal{B}}
such that
neither

w
nor
X\cdot w
,
w\cdot X

lie in the annihilator

\mathrm{Annh}({\mathcal{B}})=F\cdot(X,X^{2},X)

of
{\mathcal{B}}
, i. e.

w\in\bigl\{x,\,xy,\,(x,y,z),\,\Tch(x,y,z,t),\,(xy)R_{z}R_{t}\dots R_{u}\bigr\}.

Let us check the right symmetry of
{\mathcal{B}}
:

\bigl(a,b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}=\bigl(a,c,b\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}.

First, we compute all nonzero associators of type

\bigl(w,b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}
, for
b,c\in X
,
and verify that the obtained elements are
symmetric in
b,c
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\bigl(x,b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=xb\cdot c-x\cdot bc=(x,b,c)+x(bc)-x(bc)=(x,b,c),

\displaystyle\bigl(xy,b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bigl(xy\cdot b\bigr)\cdot c=\bigl((x,y,b)+x(yb)\bigr)\cdot c=

\displaystyle=\Tch(x,y,b,c)+\bigl(x,y\circ b,c\bigr)+\bigl(x,[y,b],c\bigr)+(y,xb,c)=

\displaystyle=\Tch(x,y,b,c)+\bigl(x,yb,c\bigr)+\bigl(x,yb,c\bigr)+(y,xb,c),

\displaystyle\bigl((x,y,z),b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bigl((x,y,z)\cdot b\bigr)\cdot c=

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\Tch(x,y,z,b)+\bigl(x,y\circ z,b\bigr)\Bigr)\cdot c=(xy)R_{z}R_{b}R_{c},

\displaystyle\bigl(\Tch(x,y,z,t),b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\Tch(x,y,z,t)\cdot b\bigr)\cdot c=(xy)R_{z}R_{t}R_{b}R_{c},

\displaystyle\bigl((xy)R_{z}R_{t}\dots R_{u},b,c\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bigl((xy)R_{z}R_{t}\dots R_{u}\cdot b\bigr)\cdot c=(xy)R_{z}R_{t}\dots R_{u}R_{b}R_{c}.

Further, we compare the values of associators in pairs of types

\bigl(a,b,w\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}\,,\quad\bigl(a,w,b\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}\,,\quad a,b\in X,\quad w\notin X.

In the case
w\in X^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl(a,b,xy\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=-a\cdot(b\cdot xy)=-a\cdot b(xy)=(a,xb,y)=(a,xy,b),

\displaystyle\bigl(a,xy,b\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=(a\cdot xy)\cdot b-a\cdot(xy\cdot b)=a(xy)\cdot b-a\cdot\bigl((x,y,b)+x(yb)\bigr)=

\displaystyle=\bigl(a,[x,y],b\bigr)+(x,ay,b)-(x,ay,b)+(a,yx,b)=(a,xy,b).

Furthermore, let
{{\mathcal{B}}}^{n}
be the ideal of

{\mathcal{B}}
spanned by all base elements of degree
\geqslant n
.
We observe that all terms of associators of types

\bigl(X,X,{{\mathcal{B}}}^{3}\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}},\quad\bigl(X,{{\mathcal{B}}}^{3},X\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

are null, by virtue of the relations

X\cdot{{\mathcal{B}}}^{4}=0,\quad X\cdot(X^{2}X)\subseteq\mathrm{Annh}({\mathcal{B}}),\quad X\cdot(X,X,X)\subseteq\mathrm{Annh}({\mathcal{B}}).

Therefore,
{\mathcal{B}}
is right symmetric.

Finally, to complete the proof, we verify that
{\mathcal{B}}
is weakly Novikov.
Indeed, in view of the relations above, all terms of the identity

a\cdot\bigl(b,c,d\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}=\bigl(b,c,a\cdot d\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}\,

are null whenever at least one of its variables
a,b,c,d
takes a value that doesn’t lie in
X
.
Otherwise, we have,

\displaystyle x\cdot\bigl(y,z,t\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=x\cdot(y,z,t)=(y,xz,t),

\displaystyle\bigl(y,z,xt\bigr)_{{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=-y\cdot(z\cdot xt)=-y\cdot z(xt)=(y,xz,t).

∎

Corollaries

We stress that combining Lemma

4.2.
Non-nilpotent subvarieties

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{V}_{f}
is a proper non-nilpotent subvariety of
\Weakly^{(2)}
distinguished by some multilinear identity

f=f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
of degree
4

then
f
should have the form

f=\sum_{\delta\in\mathrm{A}_{4}}\lambda_{\delta}\left(x_{\delta(1)},x_{\delta(2)}x_{\delta(3)},x_{\delta(4)}\right),

where
all

\lambda_{\delta}\in F

and

\mathrm{A}_{4}

is the alternating group on the set

\{1,2,3,4\}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
,
f
can be written down as

f=\sum_{\delta\in\mathrm{A}_{4}}\lambda_{\delta}\left(x_{\delta(1)},x_{\delta(2)}x_{\delta(3)},x_{\delta(4)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mu_{i}\Tch(x_{i},x_{\sigma_{i}(2)},x_{\sigma_{i}(3)},x_{\sigma_{i}(4)}),\quad\lambda_{\delta},\mu_{i}\in F,

where

\sigma_{i}

is the permutation defined by rule (
23
), for
n=4
.
Suppose that at least one of the scalars
\mu_{i}
is nonzero.
Then, with no loss of generality, it’s enough to fix
\mu_{1}=1
and set

x_{1}=w\in X^{2}
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.2
, we get

f(w,x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=wR_{x_{2}}R_{x_{3}}R_{x_{4}}.

However, in view of Theorem
2
, this contradicts with the assumption of non-nilpotency for
\mathcal{V}_{f}
.
The obtained contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4

Proof.

Applying, as above, Theorem

TRAVELING WAVES NEAR POISEUILLE FLOW FOR THE 2D EULER EQUATION
Ángel Castro and Daniel Lear

Abstract
. In this paper we reveal the existence of a large family of new, nontrivial and Lipschitz traveling waves for the 2D Euler equation at an arbitrarily small distance from the Poiseuille flow in H s H^{s} , with s < 3 / 2 s<\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}} , at the level of the vorticity.

1.
Introduction and main result

In this paper we consider the 2D Euler equation for an incompressible and inviscid fluid

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf u}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf u}=

\displaystyle-\nabla p,

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=

\displaystyle 0,

in a periodic channel
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
and with slip boundary condition

(3)

\displaystyle u_{2}\big|_{y=\pm 1}=0.

We also fix the circulation of the velocity, which is conserved. Indeed, we will take

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}u_{1}(x,y,t)dy=\frac{2}{3},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

In this way, the Poiseuille flow, which is given by

(5)

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{P}(x,y)=(y^{2},0),

solves (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) and (
4
) (with pressure
p=0
).

The main objective of this paper is to study the evolution of 2D Euler equation near Poiseuille flow in a periodic channel with slip boundary condition.
We show that the dynamics of 2D Euler at low regularity is quite rich. In fact, we are able to show the existence of traveling waves at a distance as close as we want to Poiseuille measured in the
H^{3/2^{-}}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])
topology, at the level of the vorticity.

Investigating the behavior of Euler equations near shear flows, such as Poiseuille flow, transcends theoretical curiosity and is of paramount importance. Despite its simplicity, Poiseuille flow exhibits rich dynamics and has broad practical implications. For example, in biomedical engineering, it is essential for understanding blood flow in arteries and for designing medical devices such as catheters and drug delivery systems, see
[
31
,
54
]
.

For the sake of clarity, we will now give an elementary statement of our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
0\leq s<\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
and
\epsilon>0
, the 2D Euler system (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) and (
4
) admits a nontrivial traveling wave solution at distance
\epsilon
of the Poiseuille flow. That is, the travelling wave satisfies the smallness condition

\|\omega+2y\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])}<\epsilon,

with
\omega\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])
and whose speed is
O(1)
with respect to
\epsilon
.

Remark 1.2
.

Here and throughout the rest of the paper, “nontrivial solution” means that the velocity is not a shear flow, i.e., that the dependence of
u_{1}
on the horizontal variable
x
is nontrivial.

This result will follow from the combination of Theorems
10.1
and
10.2
where the properties of these solutions will be stated in more detail.
We hope that the techniques introduced here will be sufficiently robust and flexible for studying the existence of traveling wave solutions near other more general shear flows.

1.1.
Motivation and background

The study of the Euler equation in 2D remains a vibrant and active field of research. Unlike its 3D counterpart, the global existence of smooth solutions is a classic result dating back to the 1930’s, see
[
42
,
66
]
. However, understanding the qualitative behavior of these global solutions presents formidable difficulties and challenges.

Understanding the long time behaviour of these solutions is especially difficult due to the absence of a global relaxation mechanism. In light of these challenges, it is natural to investigate possible “end state” configurations to better understand the long-time dynamics. These configurations serve as important benchmarks and may even emerge as dominant aspects of the dynamics of 2D Euler.

There have been some attempts to construct a theory for the generic long-term behavior of solutions to the 2D Euler equation. Numerical simulations suggest that, in the generic case, the solutions, while exhibiting complicated fine-scale behavior, may exhibit certain structures on the unit scale. Examples of such “end states” configurations include stationary, time-periodic, quasi-periodic, and perhaps chaotic states.

In this direction, there are a couple of far-reaching conjectures regarding the long-term behavior of the 2D Euler equation on bounded planar domains
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

•

Conjecture
(Šverák (2011),
[
57
]
).
Generic initial data
\omega_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
gives rise to inviscid incompressible motions whose vorticity orbits
\{\omega(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are not precompact in
L^{2}(M)
.

•

Conjecture
(Shnirelman (2013),
[
56
]
)
For any initial data
\omega_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M),
the collection of
L^{2}(M)
weak limits of the orbit
\{\omega(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
consists of vorticities which generate
L^{2}(M)
precompact orbits under 2D Euler evolution.

This would help explain the local chaos versus global structure phenomenon. We refer the reader to
[
4
,
27
,
28
,
30
]
and references therein for important recent works on these and related problems, and Section 3 of the recent book
[
29
]
for an excellent survey. A rigorous proof of these conjectures is beyond the scope of current PDE techniques. A more realistic and achievable approach is to consider the 2D Euler equations in physically relevant perturbative regimes, which is the problem we consider in this paper below.

A particularly important class of solutions of the Euler equations is given by the steady states, i.e. time-independent flow configurations. Thus, a fundamental question that naturally arises is how the solutions near such steady states behave and, in particular, whether they are stable in an appropriate sense, which must be defined very carefully.

The study of the stability properties of these steady states is a classical topic and a fundamental problem in hydrodynamics. In the 1960’s, Arnold
[
3
]
proved a general stability criteria, using the energy Casimir method, but this does not give asymptotic information on the global dynamics.

The full nonlinear asymptotic stability problem has only been investigated recently, starting with the breakthrough work of Bedrossian-Masmoudi
[
5
]
, who proved
inviscid damping
and nonlinear stability
in the Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}^{s}
, with
s>\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, for perturbations of the Couette flow on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
.

This result does not hold for
s<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, as demonstrated in
[
22
]
by Deng-Masmoudi. See also
[
24
]
.

In very recent and independent works Ionescu-Jia
[
43
,
44
]
and Masmoudi-Zhao
[
51
]
proved that nonlinear asymptotic stability holds true also for perturbations around the Couette flow and more general monotone shear flows in the finite periodic channel
\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]
.

Motivated by the results mentioned above, the linearized equations around more general shear flows have been intensively investigated in the last few years. See, for example,
[
9
,
10
,
23
,
24
,
39
,
45
,
46
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
67
,
68
]
and their references for a complete, but not exhaustive, list of references.

By all the above, an important direction to explore further is how the regularity of initial data may or may not affect the dynamics. In this direction we refer to the work of Lin–Zeng
[
50
]
, where they proved that nonlinear inviscid damping is not true for perturbations of the Couette flow in
H^{s}
with
s<\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
. In addition, the non existence of nontrivial traveling waves close to the Couette in
H^{s}
, with
s>\tfrac{3}{2}
, is also proved.

More specifically, they discovered Kelvin’s cat’s eyes steady states nearby to the Couette shear flow. In fact, the dynamics at this level of regularity exhibit even greater complexity and there are also travelling waves with speed of order O(1) and zero circulation, as shown by the authors in
[
11
]
.

For other related equations like 2D Euler equation with Coriolis force or on a rotating sphere we refer to
[
49
,
58
]
and
[
17
,
55
]
respectively.

Recently, there has been a growing interest in the study of the existence or absence of invariant structures such as steady-states and traveling waves for the incompressible 2D Euler equations near other shear flows. Here, it is important to mention the result of Coti Zelati–Elgindi–Widmayer
[
19
]
. They prove the existence of analytic nontrivial steady-states near Kolmogorov flow
\mathbf{u}_{K}=(\sin(y),0)
in the domain
\mathbb{T}\times[0,2\pi]
. In this result the distance is measured in
C^{w}
(analytic functions). On the contrary, in the domain
\mathbb{T}\times[0,2\pi\delta]
, with
\delta\notin\mathbb{N}
, they show a rigidity result: nonexistence of nontrivial traveling waves near Kolmogorov flow in the
H^{2}
topology. Finally, they also prove the nonexistence of nontrivial traveling waves near the Poiseuille flow in the
H^{5+}
topology in
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
. This last result complements Theorem
1.1
, but it still leaves a gap for
\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}\leq s\leq 5
.

For the Navier-Stokes equation, when the viscosity
\nu>0
is small but nonzero, another significant physical phenomenon emerges, known as
enhanced dissipation
, which contributes to stabilizing the flow. Essentially, the background flow mixes the vorticity field, amplifying the viscous effect in averaging the vorticity function. This leads to a quicker decay of the vorticity compared to when the viscous effect acts alone. Using enhanced dissipation allows for establishing improved nonlinear asymptotic stability results for perturbations of the order
O(\nu^{\gamma})
, with a suitable
\gamma>0
. Determining the smallest
\gamma
for which nonlinear stability remains valid is a crucial challenge and represents an active research area, addressing the “transition threshold conjecture”. For comprehensive insights into enhanced dissipation and transition threshold problems, we direct readers to recent works
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein. In our context, it is pertinent to point out the importance of several recent works
[

]
focused on Poiseuille flow.

1.2.
Tools and ideas behind the proof

In this section, we explain the key ingredients of the proof and highlight the main differences and new challenges that arise in the case of Poiseuille flow with respect to Couette and Taylor-Couette
[
11
,
12
]
.

Step 1: Formulation of the problem via vorticity contour lines.

As in our previous works
[
11
,
12
]
the starting point will be the use of the transport character of the vorticity equation to translate the problem to the study of vorticity contour lines. That is, we will assume that the level sets of the vorticity are given by the family of graphs
(x,y+f({\bf x},t))
, i.e.,

\omega(x,y+f({\bf x},t),t)=\varpi(y),\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

for certain profile
\varpi
which will be fixed in an appropriate manner later. Then, all the information of the problem is encoded in the time-evolution of
f({\bf x},t)
and in
\varpi
. After some algebraic manipulations the problem reduces to the equation

\partial_{t}f({\bf x},t)=\partial_{x}\overline{\Psi}_{\varpi}[f]({\bf x},t),\quad\text{over $\mathbb{T}\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\right)$},

with boundary conditions
f|_{y=\pm 1}=0
and where
\partial_{x}\overline{\Psi}_{\varpi}[\cdot]
is a nonlocal and nonlinear functional.

To search for traveling wave solutions we will take, with a little abuse of notation, the ansatz
f({\bf x},t):=f(x+\lambda t,y)
which will lead to a time-independent equation for
(\lambda,f({\bf x}))
of the form

(6)

F_{\varpi}[\lambda,f]=0,\quad\text{over $\mathbb{T}\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\right)$}.

Bifurcation theory, particularly through the Crandall-Rabinowitz theorem, will be a essential tool in our demonstration. This theory has proven to be invaluable in establishing the existence of solutions to a wide range of equations within the field of fluid mechanics.

Step 2: Bifurcation theory and the profile function
\varpi
.
The above functional equation (
6
) will be solved by using the Crandall-Rabinowitz theorem as one can bifurcate from zero for some values of
\lambda
. The main task of the manuscript will be to check all the required conditions of C-R theorem.

Prior to that, it is important to emphasize that this approach has enabled us to reduce the problem just to the support of the vorticity gradient, where all the dynamics occur.

An important difference, which makes this problem challenging and necessitates novel approaches for its resolution, lies in the fact that for both Couette and Taylor-Couette flow, their respective vorticities remain constant. Consequently, their gradients vanish across the entire working domain. This circumstance allows us to seek perturbed profiles whose gradient is localized in two small regions, each comparable in size to the distance between the time periodic solution and the shear/radial flow.

This is in stark contrast with the case at hand, where the gradient of the associated vorticity is a non-zero constant. This fact implies we are forced to seek new perturbed profiles whose gradient is nontrivial in almost the entirety of the working domain. As we will see in Section
5
, this issue makes the analysis of the speed and direction of bifurcation completely different.

We advance that our perturbed profile
\varpi\in W^{1,\infty}([-1,1])
will be given by an odd function defined through

\varpi^{\prime}(y)=(-2)\begin{cases}1\qquad\phantom{.}0\leq y<a_{\varepsilon},\\
0\qquad a_{\varepsilon}\leq y\leq b_{\varepsilon},\\
1\qquad b_{\varepsilon}<y\leq 1,\end{cases}

with

a_{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},\qquad b_{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}.

Notice that there is a large part where
\varpi^{\prime}\neq 0
and a small part, of order
\varepsilon
, where
\varpi^{\prime}=0
. Both of them will play an essential role in the proof of theorem
1.1
.

Step 3: Regularity of the functional for a Lipschitz profile
\varpi
.

A difficulty to be faced is related to the non explicit form of the Green’s function associated to the Laplacian in a channel
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
. The situation in bounded domains turns out to be more delicate due to the presence of boundaries. This has an impact on the study of the regularity of the functional that will describe the traveling wave solutions. This type of difficulty has appeared previously in the literature, see for example
[
12
]
for the Euler equation in an annular domain or
[
41
]
for the generalized SQG equation in the disc. Here, we will adopt a similar approach to that used in
[
12
]
.

Another new technical difference from our previous results is that we will need to achieve the required regularity of the functional for a Lipschitz profile
\varpi
, in contrast to the smooth profiles used previously in
[
11
,
12
]
. Roughly speaking, we conduct a detailed study through the
L^{p}
theory of the elliptic problem associated with the stream function.

Here, it is important to note that the fact that
\varpi\in W^{1,\infty}([-1,1])
implies that the traveling waves obtained in the paper belong to
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])
, but they are not in
C^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])
. We hope that the same result holds with a smooth regularization of the profile
\varpi
, which would allow us to obtain smooth traveling waves. However, the analysis of the linear operator, needed in the C-R theorem and carried out in Section
5
, would be much more involved with a smooth profile. Because of that, here we work with a Lipschitz profile and pay the price of some complications in the proof of the differentiability of the functional.

Step 4: Analysis of the linear operator.

To implement C-R theorem and generate a nontrivial solution to the equation (
6
), one needs to check the required spectral properties for the linearized operator at
f=0
.

This linear operator is given by
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}\equiv D_{f}F_{\varpi}[0,\lambda]
and for any function

h(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{n}(y)\cos(nx),

we have that

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}h(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{n}h_{n}(y)\cos(nx),\quad\text{over $\mathbb{T}\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\right)$},

where each operator
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{n}
is of integral type. More precisely, after employing the symmetries of the system, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\lambda}h_{n}(y)=(\lambda-\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y))h_{n}(y)-\int_{0}^{1}\varpi^{\prime}(z)G_{n}(y,z)h_{n}(z)dz,\qquad y\in[0,1]\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\right),

with

\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\varpi(\bar{y})d\bar{y}-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\bar{y}}\varpi(z)dzd\bar{y}-\frac{1}{3},

and where
G_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
is just the Green function associated to the operator
\partial_{y}^{2}-n^{2}
on
[0,1]
. That is,

\displaystyle G_{n}(y,z)=\frac{1}{n\sinh(n)}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sinh(n(1-y))\sinh(nz)&\quad z<y,\\
\sinh(ny)\sinh(n(1-z))&\quad z>y.\end{array}\right.

The main core of the paper consists of the study of the spectral properties of
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
. In particular, a large part will be dedicated to obtain an element in its kernel. Here, in this road map of the proof, we give particular attention to this issue since it is the base also to obtain the remaining necessary conditions to apply the C-R theorem.

Step 4.1: Choice of the mode, manipulations and rescaling.
In order to find an element in the kernel, a usual trick is to focus our attention in just one mode. For simplicity, in this problem we fix
m=1
(that is,
h\equiv h_{1}
and
h_{n}\equiv 0,\text{ for all }n\neq m
with
m=1
) and using the fact that
\varpi^{\prime}(y)=-2\chi_{[0,a_{\varepsilon})\cup(b_{\varepsilon},1]}(y)
we have reduced the problem to find
(\lambda,h)
such that

(7)

(\lambda-\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y))h(y)+2\int_{[0,a_{\varepsilon})\cup(b_{\varepsilon},1]}G(y,z)h(z)dz=0,\qquad y\in[0,a_{\varepsilon})\cup(b_{\varepsilon},1],

with the boundary conditions

h(0)=0=h(1).

Remark 1.3
.

Here, it is important to note that the associated integral equation for both Couette and Taylor-Couette flow have an integration domain of size of order
O(\varepsilon)
. This fact play a fundamental role in the proofs in
[
11
,
12
]
. However, in this setting we have a domain of integration of order
O(1)
which requires new ideas and use a completely different approach to proceed.

The main goal is to look for a solution of (
7
) with speed
\lambda
that satisfies
\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(b_{\varepsilon})<\lambda<\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(a_{\varepsilon})
. This means that
\lambda=\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(y^{\ast})
for some
y^{*}\in(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})
. This way we avoid any zero of
(\lambda-\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y))
in
[0,a_{\varepsilon}]\cup[b_{\varepsilon},1]
. In fact,

\lambda-\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y)=\begin{cases}(y-\mu)(y+\mu),\qquad 0\leq y<a_{\varepsilon},\\
(y-\nu)(y-\rho),\qquad b_{\varepsilon}<y\leq 1,\end{cases}

for some
\mu=\mu(\lambda),\nu(\mu),\rho(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(8)

\mu>a_{\varepsilon},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho<\nu<b_{\varepsilon}.

Next we introduce the function

H(y)\equiv\begin{cases}h(y)(y-\mu)(y+\mu),\qquad 0\leq y<a_{\varepsilon},\\
h(y)(y-\nu)(y-\rho),\qquad b_{\varepsilon}<y\leq 1,\end{cases}

which allow us to write (
7
) as

(9)

\displaystyle H(y)+2\int_{0}^{1}G(y,z)\left(\frac{\chi_{[0,a_{\varepsilon})}(z)}{z^{2}-\mu^{2}}+\frac{\chi_{(b_{\varepsilon},1]}(z)}{(z-\nu)(z-\rho)}\right)H(z)dz=0,\qquad y\in[0,a_{\varepsilon})\cup(b_{\varepsilon},1].

Let us rescale equation (
9
) in a suitable manner to fix the domain. To achieve this, we introduce the following auxiliary rescaled variable
x
, defined as

x=\begin{cases}\frac{y}{1-\varepsilon},\qquad\text{for $0\leq y<a_{\varepsilon}$},\\
\frac{y-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon},\qquad\text{for $b_{\varepsilon}<y\leq 1$},\end{cases}

and the rescaled auxiliary function

F(x)=\begin{cases}H((1-\varepsilon)x),\qquad&\text{for $0\leq x<\frac{1}{2}$},\\
H((1-\varepsilon)x+\varepsilon),\qquad&\text{for $\frac{1}{2}<x\leq 1$}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.4
.

Here, we emphasize that
F(x)
does not have to be continuous at the point
x=1/2
.

This procedure allow us to pass from a problem (
9
) in a domain
[0,a_{\varepsilon})\cup(b_{\varepsilon},1]
depending of the parameter
\varepsilon
to a fixed domain
[0,1/2)\cup(1/2,1]
independent of it.
In this way, we have translated our problem to obtain

F(x)=F^{L}(x)\chi_{[0,1/2)}(x)+F^{R}(x)\chi_{(1/2,1]}(x),

where
F^{L}
and
F^{R}
solve respectively

(10)

F^{L}(x)+\int_{0}^{1/2}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\frac{F^{L}(z)}{z^{2}-\tilde{\mu}^{2}}dz+\int_{1/2}^{1}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\frac{F^{R}(z)}{(z-\tilde{\nu})(z-\tilde{\rho})}dz=0,\qquad 0\leq x<1/2,

and

(11)

F^{R}(x)+\int_{0}^{1/2}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\frac{F^{L}(z)}{z^{2}-\tilde{\mu}^{2}}dz+\int_{1/2}^{1}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\frac{F^{R}(z)}{(z-\tilde{\nu})(z-\tilde{\rho})}dz=0,\qquad 1/2<x\leq 1,

with the boundary conditions

(12)

F^{L}(0)=0=F^{R}(1),

where

(13)

\tilde{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{1-\varepsilon},\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\nu}=\frac{\nu-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon},\,\,\tilde{\rho}=\frac{\rho-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.

In the end, the problem reduces to obtain
(F,\tilde{\mu})
solving (
10
)-(
12
).

Step 4.2: Integral equation to ODE transformation.

It is straightforward to check that if the pair
F\equiv(F^{L},F^{R})
solves (
10
)-(
11
), then it also solves the following system of ordinary differential equations

(14)

\displaystyle\left(f^{L}\right)^{\prime\prime}(x)-\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}-\tilde{\mu}^{2}}+(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\right)f^{L}(x)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for }0\leq x<1/2,

(15)

\displaystyle f^{L}(0)

\displaystyle=0,

and

(16)

\displaystyle\left(f^{R}\right)^{\prime\prime}(x)-\left(\frac{2}{(x-\tilde{\nu})(x-\tilde{\rho})}+(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\right)f^{R}(x)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\text{for }1/2<x\leq 1,

(17)

\displaystyle f^{R}(1)

\displaystyle=0.

Due to (
8
) and (
13
) we must impose

(18)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}<\tilde{\nu}<1/2<\tilde{\mu}.

Thus, the only regular-singular points
x=\tilde{\mu}
and
x=\tilde{\nu}
are outside the domain where we are working. In any case, using the Frobenius series method we can obtain two linearly independent solutions of each ODE equation. That is, we are able to
solve (
14
) over
[0,\tilde{\mu})
and (
16
) over
(\tilde{\nu},1]
.

Since we are working with a linear second-order ODE we will include an “extra” boundary condition given by

(19)

\lim_{x\to 1/2^{-}}f^{L}(x)=1=\lim_{x\to 1/2^{+}}f^{R}(x).

Using a linear combination of the two linearly independent solutions obtained earlier for each ODE, we can also obtain a solution for each ODE that satisfies (
15
), (
17
) and (
19
).

Remark 1.5
.

It is important to emphasize that the reciprocal is not true in general. That is, the solution of the ODE system (
14
)-(
15
)-(
19
) and (
16
)-(
17
)-(
19
) is not in general a solution of the original integral system (
10
)-(
11
). We must do a suitable choice of the values at points
x=1/2^{\pm}
.

To recover the solution of the original integral system (
10
)-(
11
), we consider the candidate

(20)

F(x)=Af^{L}(x)\chi_{[0,1/2)}(x)+Bf^{R}(x)\chi_{(1/2,1]}(x).

Step 4.3: Reducing the problem to an equation for
\tilde{\mu}
.

Using our candidate (
20
), it can be verified that

(A,B)
needs to satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\sinh(b_{\varepsilon})I_{1}&\sinh(a_{\varepsilon})I_{2}\\
\sinh(a_{\varepsilon})I_{1}&1+\sinh(b_{\varepsilon})I_{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A\\
B\end{array}\right)=0,

with

I_{1}\equiv\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(1/2^{-},z)\frac{f^{L}(z)}{z^{2}-\tilde{\mu}^{2}}dz,\qquad I_{2}\equiv\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\tilde{G}_{\varepsilon}(1/2^{+},z)\frac{f^{R}(z)}{(z-\tilde{\nu})(z-\tilde{\rho})}dz.

Thus, in order to get a nontrivial solution
(A,B)\neq 0
it must hold

(25)

\displaystyle 1+\sinh(b_{\varepsilon})\left(I_{1}+I_{2}\right)+\left(\sinh^{2}(b_{\varepsilon})-\sinh^{2}(a_{\varepsilon})\right)I_{1}I_{2}=0,

which is actually an equation for
\tilde{\mu}
. Thus, we have finally reduced the problem to find
\tilde{\mu}
satisfying inequalities (
18
) and solving equation (
25
). It is important to note that
\tilde{\mu}
appears implicitly in the expressions of
f^{L}
and
f^{R}
as well.

Step 4.4: Analyzing the limiting problem.

A crucial step to obtain
\tilde{\mu}
solving the equation (
25
) and (
18
) will be to study the limiting problem that appears when we make the parameter
\varepsilon
to tend to zero.

We need to introduce an ansatz for
\tilde{\mu}
. That is,

(26)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{1}\right)\varepsilon+\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\varepsilon),

where
-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}<\mu_{1}<+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and
\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)=O(1)
in terms of
\varepsilon.
The fact that
\mu_{1}\in(-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
implies that
\mu>a_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho<\nu<b_{\varepsilon}
for

The idea is as follows:

Suppose there exist

\displaystyle F_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)-\left(\frac{2}{x^{2}-(1/2)^{2}}+1\right)F_{0}(x)=0,\quad\text{for }0\leq x<1/2\text{ and }1/2<x\leq 1,

with

As we did before, the above system can also be analyzed and solved using the Frobenius series method over

In addition, if we assume

\displaystyle 1+C_{1}\sinh(1/2)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sinh(x)F_{0}(x)-\sinh(1/2)}{x^{2}-1/4}dx

This expression can be understood as an equation for

The real proof consists of using this object

2.1.
The equation for the traveling waves

In order to study (

\displaystyle\omega(x,y+f(x,y,t),t)=\varpi(y),

for some Lipschitz profile

By straightforward computations, we get

\displaystyle\nabla\omega(x,y+f({\bf x},t),t)=\frac{\left(-\partial_{x}f({\bf x},t),1\right)}{1+\partial_{y}f({\bf x},t)}\varpi^{\prime}(y).

Then, using (

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}f({\bf x},t)-\partial_{x}\overline{\Psi}({\bf x},t)\right)\frac{\varpi^{\prime}(y)}{1+\partial_{y}f({\bf x},t)}=0,

where

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}({\bf x},t)\equiv\Psi(x,y+f({\bf x},t),t).

In the following, we will use the notation

\displaystyle\Phi_{f}(x,y)\equiv(x,y+f(x,y)),

and we also define the auxiliary domain

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}\equiv\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]\,:\,(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\Phi_{f}(x,y)\text{ with }x\in\mathbb{T}\text{ and }y\in\supp(\varpi^{\prime})\}.

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}=\supp\left(\nabla\omega\right),

and we emphasize that we just have to solve the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f({\bf x},t)=\partial_{x}\overline{\Psi}({\bf x},t)\quad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\right)$},

with boundary condition

(33)

\displaystyle f\big|_{y=\pm 1}=0,

as we can not move the top and bottom of the channel.

Since we are looking for traveling waves solutions, we will introduce the ansatz

f(x,y,t)\equiv f(x+\lambda t,y)

with a slight abuse of notation. Then, our problem (

\displaystyle\lambda\partial_{x}f({\bf x})=\partial_{x}\overline{\Psi}_{f}({\bf x})\quad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\supp}}(\varpi^{\prime})$},

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{f}({\bf x})=\Psi_{f}(x,y+f(x,y)),

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\Psi_{f}=&\omega_{f}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1],$}\\
\Psi_{f}\big|_{y=\pm 1}=&\mp\frac{1}{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\text{constant}&\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]\setminus\Omega_{f}.$}\end{aligned}\right.

2.1.1.

In addition, we define an auxiliary velocity given by

2.1.2.
Symmetries of the equation

In this section we see that if

\displaystyle\lambda g({\bf x})=\overline{\Psi}_{g}({\bf x})-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}_{g}(\bar{x},y)d\bar{x},\quad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times[-1,-b)\cup(-a,a)\cup(b,1]$},

The proof is reduced to proving that

The boundary condition follows immediately by the definition of

\Delta\Psi_{g}(x,y)=-\Delta\left(\Psi_{f}(x,-y)\right)=-\Delta\Psi_{f}(x,-y)=-\omega_{f}(x,-y).

In addition, since

\Psi_{f}\in C^{2+\beta}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
and finally
\overline{\Psi}_{f}=\Psi_{f}\circ\Phi_{f}\in C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1]).

The continuity of
\overline{\Psi}_{f}
will be studied in the next section where we will even proof that it is differentiable.

4.2.
The continuity of the functional

After introducing and studying the functional (
57
), we are now ready to analyze

\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r @{} >{{}}c<{{}} @{} >{\displaystyle}l}\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}\equiv\overline{\Psi}_{(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}_{(\cdot)}(\bar{x},y)d\bar{x}\colon B_{\varepsilon/4}&\rightarrow&C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}\\
f&\mapsto&\overline{\Psi}_{f}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}_{f}(\bar{x},y)d\bar{x}\end{array}

The fact that
\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}
is well-defined from
B_{\varepsilon/4}
to
C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])
follows straightforward from the above. To conclude that, in fact, is well-defined from
C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
to itself we need to check

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}(\bar{x},y)d\bar{x}=0,

(2)

\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}(x,0)=\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}(x,1)=0
,

(3)

\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}(x,y)=\tilde{F}_{(\cdot)}(-x,y).

That the mean of
\tilde{F}_{f}
is equal to zero follows by its definition. In addition,
\tilde{F}_{f}(x,0)=0
since
\overline{\Psi}_{f}(x,0)=\Psi_{f}(x,0)=0
. Also,
\tilde{F}_{f}(x,1)=0
since
\overline{\Psi}_{f}(x,1)=\Psi_{f}(x,-1)=-1/3
and then

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}(\bar{x},1)d\bar{x}=-\frac{1}{3}.

Finally, we notice that, if
f(x,y)=f(-x,y)
then
\omega_{f}(x,y)=\omega_{f}(-x,y)
. Thus
\Psi_{f}(x,y)=\Psi_{f}(-x,y)
and consequently
\overline{\Psi}_{f}(x,y)=\overline{\Psi}_{f}(-x,y)
.

In summary, we have proved the following Lemmas:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
and
\Psi_{f}
solving (
44
), then
\Psi_{f}\in C^{2+\beta}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])
for all
0<\beta<1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F[f,\lambda](x,y)\equiv\lambda f(x,y)-\tilde{F}_{f}(x,y).
That is,

\displaystyle F[f,\lambda](x,y)=\lambda f(x,y)-\overline{\Psi}_{f}(x,y)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}_{f}(\bar{x},y)d\bar{x}.

Then

\displaystyle F\,:\,B_{\varepsilon/4}\times\mathbb{R}\to C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}

is well-defined and it is continuous from
B_{\varepsilon/4}
to
C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
.

4.3.
The differential of the functional

Now, we compute the differential of
\overline{\Psi}_{f}
with respect to
f
at the point
\eta\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
.
We start computing
D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{\eta}h
for
\eta\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
and
h\in C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
. That is,

(58)

\displaystyle D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{\eta}h(x,y)=h(x,y)\partial_{y}\Psi(x,y+\eta(x,y))+D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h(x,y+\eta(x,y)).

From the Lemma
4.1
we know that
\partial_{y}\Psi(x,y)\in C^{1+\beta}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
for all
0<\beta<1
, and then
\partial_{y}\Psi(x,y+\eta(x,y))\in C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])
. Then, since the first term of the above expression is just a derivative in the classical sense, we only have to prove that there exists the differential
D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h
, which will be presented in the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
and
h\in C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
and consider

\displaystyle\Psi_{(\cdot)}\,:\,B_{\varepsilon/4}\subset C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}\to C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]).

Then, the Gateaux derivative of
\Psi_{f}
with respect to
f
at the point
\eta
in the direction
h
, i.e.
D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h
, is given by solving the problem

(59)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h=&d\omega_{\eta},\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]$},\\
D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h\big|_{y=0}=&0,\\
D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h\big|_{y=1}=&0,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle d\omega_{\eta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\left(\frac{\varpi^{\prime}h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\right)\circ\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}&\qquad 0\leq y<a+\eta(x,a),\\
0&\qquad a+\eta(x,a)\leq y\leq b+\eta(x,b),\\
-\left(\frac{\varpi^{\prime}h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\right)\circ\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}&\qquad b+\eta(x,b)<y\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First of all, we define
\varphi
through the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\varphi=&d\omega_{\eta},\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]$},\\
\varphi\big|_{y=0}=&0,\\
\varphi\big|_{y=1}=&0,\end{aligned}\right.

To conclude that
\varphi=D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}
, we have to show that

(60)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left|\left|\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\right|\right|_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}=0.

Before starting with the main part of the proof, we note that taking
t
small enough we get

\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\in C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]),

This is an immediate consequence of the fact that by definition
d\omega_{\eta}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty.
Then, by elliptic regularity theory
\varphi\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
for
1<p<\infty
and by Morrey’s inequality we get
\varphi\in C^{1+\beta}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
for any
0<\beta<1
.

Now, we are ready to prove (
60
).
By definition we have that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\left(\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\right)=&\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta},\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]$},\\
\left.\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\right|_{y=0,\,1}=&0,\end{aligned}\right.

and by elliptic regularity theory we get

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\right|\right|_{C^{1+\alpha}}\leq\left|\left|\frac{\Psi_{\eta+th}-\Psi_{\eta}}{t}-\varphi\right|\right|_{W^{2,p}}\leq\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{p}},

where in the first step we have used Morrey’s inequality
with
p=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
. This means that (
60
) reduces to proving that

(61)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}=0,\qquad\text{for }2<p<\infty.

Let us compute the difference
\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}
. To do that we define the domains (for
t
small enough)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,0\leq y<\text{min}(a+\eta(x,a),a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a))\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,\text{min}(a+\eta(x,a),a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a))\leq y<\text{max}(a+\eta(x,a),a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a))\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,\text{max}(a+\eta(x,a),a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a))\leq y\leq\text{min}(b+\eta(x,b),b+\eta(x,b)+th(x,b))\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{4}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,\text{min}(b+\eta(x,b),b+\eta(x,b)+th(x,b))<y\leq\text{max}(b+\eta(x,b),b+\eta(x,b)+th(x,b))\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{5}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,\text{max}(b+\eta(x,b),b+\eta(x,b)+th(x,b))<y\leq 1\right\}.

such that

(62)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{5}\Omega_{i}=\mathbb{T}\times[0,1].

Thus, from (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-2\left(\Phi_{\eta+th}^{-1}\right)_{2}+2\left(\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}\right)_{2}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\text{ either}-2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+th}\right)_{2}+2a\text{ or }-2a+2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)_{2}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{2},\\
0&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{3},\\
\text{ either}-2\left(\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+th}\right)_{2}-b\right)\text{ or }2\left(\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)_{2}-b\right)&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{4},\\
-2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+th}\right)_{2}+2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)_{2}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{5}.\end{array}\right.

In
\Omega_{1}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}=-2\frac{\left(\Phi_{\eta+th}^{-1}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}\right)_{2}}{t}-\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}.

Moreover, the first factor of the above expression can be written in a more convenient way as

\displaystyle\left(\Phi_{\eta+th}^{-1}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}\right)_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}\right)_{2}}{ds}ds.

In addition, from

\left(\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1}\right)_{2}\circ\Phi_{\eta+sth}=y,

we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d\left(\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1}\right)_{2}}{ds}\circ\Phi_{\eta+sth}+\frac{d\Phi_{\eta+sth}}{ds}\cdot\left(\nabla\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}\right)_{2}\right)\circ\Phi_{\eta+sth}=0.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d\left(\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1}\right)_{2}}{ds}

\displaystyle=-th\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+tsh}\left(\partial_{y}\left(\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1}\right)_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-t\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}.

We obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}=2\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)ds.

In order to estimate this last term, we split it in the following way

\displaystyle\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}=\frac{h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}}{1+(\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h)\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}}

\displaystyle+h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv I_{1}+I_{2}.

We can bound

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C|\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}|,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}|^{\alpha}+t\right).

Remark 4.4
.

In the rest the value of the constant
C
can change from line to line and depends on
\varepsilon
.

The calculation for
I_{1}
is trivially satisfied. For
I_{2}
we only have to add and subtract the appropriate terms to arrive at a more convenient expression as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\pm\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}-\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}

\displaystyle-t\frac{s\partial_{y}h}{(1+\partial_{y}\eta+st\partial_{y}h)(1+\partial_{y}\eta)}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+sth}.

After that, since
|\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1}-\Phi_{\eta}^{-1}|=|(\Phi_{\eta+sth}^{-1})_{2}-(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta})_{2}|\leq Ct
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\leq Ct^{\alpha}.

In
\Omega_{2}
we have to distinguish between the following two cases:

Case
\eta(x,a)<\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)
:
Note that in this case we have

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}=\frac{-2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+th}\right)_{2}+2a}{t}.

Since
a=\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta+th}\right)_{2}(x,a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a))
we get

\displaystyle\left|a-\left(\Phi_{\eta+th}^{-1}\right)_{2}(x,y)\right|\leq C|a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)-y|.

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{p}}^{p}=

\displaystyle Ct^{-p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{a+\eta(x,y)}^{a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)}|a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)-y|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{p+1}t.

Case
\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)<\eta(x,a)
:
In this case we have

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}=\frac{-2a+2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)_{2}}{t}+\frac{\varpi^{\prime}h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}.

With the first term
\frac{-2a+2\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta}\right)_{2}}{t}
we proceed as before, but using that
a=(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta})_{2}(x,a+\eta(x,a))
. The term
\frac{\varpi^{\prime}h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ{\Phi}_{\eta}^{-1}
can be bounded in
L^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\varpi^{\prime}}{1+\partial_{y}\eta}\circ{\Phi}_{\eta}^{-1}\right|\right|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int^{a+\eta(x,a)}_{a+\eta(x,a)+th(x,a)}dydx\leq Ct.

Combining both cases, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{2})}\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\text{for }1<p<\infty.

In
\Omega_{3}
there is nothing to do. In
\Omega_{4}
we proceed as we did with
\Omega_{2}
and in
\Omega_{5}
as we did with
\Omega_{1}
. Joining all these cases, we finally achieve that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{\omega_{\eta+th}-\omega_{\eta}}{t}-d\omega_{\eta}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}\leq C\left(t^{\alpha}+t^{\frac{1}{p}}\right),\qquad\text{for }1<p<\infty.

Finally, taking the limit of the previous expression, we can conclude that (
61
) is satisfied.
∎

Once we have seen the existence of the Gateaux derivative, we show below that it is continuous.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
and
h\in C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
. Then, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq C||h||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\left(||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||^{\frac{1}{p}}_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}+||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||^{\alpha}_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\right),

for any
p\in(2,\infty).

Proof.

Proceeding as before, using Morrey’s inequality and elliptic regularity theory, we have that

(63)

\displaystyle||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h||_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq||d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}.

Let
\Omega_{i}
be the analogue of the domains defined in (
62
) but now for
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
. We obtain

\displaystyle d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\text{ either }\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\text{ or }-\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{2},\\
0&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{3},\\
\text{ either }\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\text{ or }-\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{4},\\
\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}&\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega_{5}.\end{array}\right.

In
\Omega_{2}
we proceed as follow. In the set
\tilde{I}=\{x\in\mathbb{T}\,:\,\eta_{1}(x,a)<\eta_{2}(x,a)\}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{I}}\int_{a+\eta_{1}(x,a)}^{a+\eta_{2}(x,a)}\left|\frac{2h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C||\eta_{2}(\cdot,a)-\eta_{1}(\cdot,a)||_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{I})}

\displaystyle\leq C||\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

In the set
\mathbb{T}\setminus\tilde{I}
we can produce a similar inequality. In
\Omega_{4}
the same argument yields

(64)

||d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{4})}\leq C||h||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||^{\frac{1}{p}}_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Since
d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}=0
in
\Omega_{3}
we just have to deal with this difference in
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{5}
. The estimate follow the same steps in both domains. In
\Omega_{1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}}{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-\frac{h}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}}+h\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}}-\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{2}.

Let us start with
J_{1}
. We can directly estimate

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-h\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}}\right|\leq C||h||_{C^{1}}|\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}|.

In addition, we can write

(65)

\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}ds.

Since

\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}\circ\Phi_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}=y,

we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}}{ds}\circ\Phi_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}+\frac{d\Phi_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}}{ds}\cdot\nabla\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}\circ\Phi_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}=0.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}}{ds}

\displaystyle=-(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})\circ\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\left(\partial_{y}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})}{1+s\partial_{y}\eta_{1}+(1-s)\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{s\eta_{1}+(1-s)\eta_{2}}.

Therefore, returning to (
65
) we get

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}\right|\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])},

and consequently we obtain that

|J_{1}|\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Let us continue with
J_{2}
. We note that

\displaystyle|J_{2}|=

\displaystyle\left|h\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}}-\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\eta_{2}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right|+C\left|\partial_{y}\eta_{1}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}-\partial_{y}\eta_{2}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C||\eta_{1}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}|\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}|^{\alpha}+C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Since
|\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}|=|\left(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}\right)_{2}-\left(\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{2}}\right)_{2}|\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}
, the above argument yields

(66)

||d\omega_{\eta_{1}}-d\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{5})}\leq C||h||_{C^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||^{\alpha}_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Finally, combining (
63
) together with (
64
)-(
66
), we can conclude our goal.
∎

Now, putting together Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.5
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

The functional

\displaystyle\Psi_{(\cdot)}\,:\,B_{\varepsilon/4}\subset C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}\to C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])

is Frechet differentiable and its derivative is continuous.

It is remain to prove that the differential of
\overline{\Psi}
is continuous. Until now we have proven that this differential is given by (
58
). In order to prove that it is continuous we will need the following auxiliary Lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
. For all
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
\delta>0
(that depends on
f
) such that if

||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\leq\delta,

then

||f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We can approximate
f
by piecewise constant functions
f_{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\chi_{I_{n}}
, where
\{I_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
is a collection of rectangles that cover
\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1]
, in such a way that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}||f-f_{N}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}=0.

\displaystyle||f_{N}||_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\chi_{I_{n}}\right|^{p}dxdy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\chi_{I_{n}}\right|^{p}dxdy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|c_{k}|^{p}\int_{I_{k}}dxdy=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|c_{k}|^{p}|I_{k}|.

Adding and subtracting appropriate terms, we split the term of interest as follows

\displaystyle||f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{2}_{i=1}||f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}-f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}

\displaystyle+||f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Here, we can bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1]}\left|f\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}-f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,a+\eta_{i}(x,a))\cup(b+\eta_{i}(x,b),1]}\left|f-f_{N}\right|^{p}\frac{1}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{i}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{i}}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C||f-f_{N}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}.

Now, we take
N
large enough to have that
||f-f_{N}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}/(100C)
. It remains to estimate

\displaystyle||f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-f_{N}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1]}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cup\Omega_{\text{out}}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}J_{\Phi_{\eta_{1}}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cup\Omega_{\text{out}}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx.

We deal with the integral over
\Omega_{\text{in}}
and
\Omega_{\text{out}}
separately. Here, we recall that

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{in}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,0\leq y<a+\eta_{1}(x,a)\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{out}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{T},\,\,b+\eta_{1}(x,b)<y\leq 1\}.

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in S_{in}}\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx,

where

S_{in}=\{n=1,...,N\,\,:\,\,I_{n}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}}\neq\emptyset\}.

In the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx,

we just have the contribution of, at most, nine terms, for
\delta
small enough. Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|c_{k}(\chi_{I_{k}}-\chi_{I_{k}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})-\sum_{i=1}^{8}c_{k(i)}\chi_{I_{k}(i)}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\right|^{p}dydx,

where
\{I_{k}(i)\}_{i=1}^{8}
are the neighbor rectangles of
I_{k}
. It is clear that each rectangle
I_{k}
has at most
8
neighboring rectangles, in case of having less we simply assume that
I_{k}(i)=\emptyset
for the remaining ones.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|c_{k}(\chi_{I_{k}}-\chi_{I_{k}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{8}\left|c_{k(i)}\chi_{I_{k}(i)}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}\right|^{p}dydx.

Let

\rho=\min_{k\in S_{in}}|I_{k}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}}|.

On the one hand, the term

|\chi_{I_{k}}-\chi_{I_{k}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}|,

over
I_{k}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}}
, is just different from zero in the set
\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}(I_{k})\setminus I_{k}
. The measure of this term can be made as small as we want taking
\delta
small enough. Let us say that

|\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}(I_{k})\setminus I_{k}|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}\rho,

for every
k\in S_{in}
.

On the other hand, the terms
\chi_{I_{k}(i)}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}
, over
I_{k}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}}
, are just different from zero in the set
\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}(I_{k}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}})\cap I_{k(i)}
. The measure of this terms can be made as small as we want taking
\delta
small enough. We will take

|\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}}(I_{k}\cap\Omega_{\text{in}})\cap I_{k(i)}|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}\rho,

for every
k\in S_{in}
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|c_{k}|^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{8}|c_{k(i)}|^{p}\right)\tilde{\varepsilon}\rho

\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{\varepsilon}\left(|c_{k}|^{p}|I_{k}|+\sum_{i=1}^{8}|c_{k(i)}|^{p}|I_{k(i)}|\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in S_{in}}\int_{\Omega_{\text{in}}\cap I_{k}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}(\chi_{I_{n}}-\chi_{I_{n}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\eta_{1}})\right|^{p}dydx\leq C\tilde{\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in S_{in}}|c_{k}|^{p}|I_{k}|\leq C\tilde{\varepsilon}||f_{N}||_{L^{p}}^{p}.

We achieve the conclusion of the Lemma by making a similar argument in
\Omega_{\text{out}}
.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
h\in C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
. The differential

\displaystyle D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{(\cdot)}h\,:\,B_{\varepsilon/4}\to C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])

is continuous in
B_{\varepsilon/4}
.

Proof.

According to (
58
), we have that

\displaystyle D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{\eta}h=\left(\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta}\circ\Phi_{\eta}\right)\cdot h+D_{f}\Psi_{\eta}h\circ\Phi_{\eta}.

We will show that, fixed
\eta_{2}\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
, for all
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
\delta
such that

\displaystyle||D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{\eta_{2}}h||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}

if

\displaystyle||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}<\delta.

We will divide the proof into two parts. Firstly we will prove that

(67)

\displaystyle||\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}.

The most delicate term in this norm is

\displaystyle||\nabla\left(\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\right)||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}.

Since
\nabla\left(\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{i}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}\right)=\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{i}}\cdot\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{i}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}
we can split

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}})\cdot\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}+\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\cdot(\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}).

Then, using the fact that
C^{\alpha}
is a Banach algebra, we find that

\displaystyle||\nabla\left(\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\right)||_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{C^{\alpha}}+||\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}}||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}}.

The first term of the above expression can be made as small as we want since
\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\in C^{2+\beta}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
for all
\beta\in(0,1)
and by hypothesis
||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}<\delta
with
\delta
a sufficiently small free parameter.

Therefore, we only need to focus our attention on the other term.
In addition, the last factor of the above expression can be split as

(68)

\displaystyle||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{C^{\alpha}}+||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}}.

For the last term of the above expression (
68
), we apply Morrey’s inequality with
p=2/(1-\alpha)
.

||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}}\leq||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{W^{1,p}}.

Thus, the delicate term in this norm is

\displaystyle||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}\cdot\nabla(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}})||_{L^{p}}+||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}})\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{L^{p}}||\nabla(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}})||_{L^{\infty}}+||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}||\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{\infty}}.

The first factor can be handle very easily. Note that

||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}||\nabla(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}})||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1}}.

For the other one, we just need to apply Lemma
4.7
with
f\equiv\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}
, since
\Psi_{\eta_{i}}\in W^{3,p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
and consequently
f\equiv\nabla^{2}\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
, to get the desired result.

For the first term of (
68
), we have that

||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\\
\leq||\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||^{\alpha}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle||\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\partial_{y}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla\partial_{y}\left(\Psi_{\eta_{1}}-\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\right)||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]])}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla\partial_{y}\left(\Psi_{\eta_{1}}-\Psi_{\eta_{2}}\right)||_{W^{1,p}}

\displaystyle\leq||\Psi_{\eta_{1}}-\Psi_{\eta_{2}}||_{W^{3,p}}\leq||\omega_{\eta_{1}}-\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{W^{1,p}},

where we have used Morrey’s inequality with
p=2/(1-\alpha)
and elliptic regularity theory.

To do small this last term, we just need to start proceeding as in the Lemma
4.3
to control
||\omega_{\eta_{1}}-\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}
. For the other term, i.e.
||\omega_{\eta_{1}}-\omega_{\eta_{2}}||_{\dot{W}^{1,p}}=||\nabla w_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla w_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}
we start noting that

\nabla w_{\eta_{i}}=\begin{cases}\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{i},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{i}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}^{-1},\qquad&\text{over }\Omega_{\eta_{i}},\\
0,\qquad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta_{i}}\equiv\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]\,:\,(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\Phi_{\eta_{i}}(x,y)\text{ with }x\in\mathbb{T}\text{ and }y\in\supp(\varpi^{\prime})\}.

Thus

\displaystyle\nabla\omega_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla\omega_{\eta_{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1},&\text{over }\Omega_{\eta_{1}}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}},\\
\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1},&\text{over }\Omega_{\eta_{1}}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}^{c},\\
-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1},&\text{over }\Omega_{\eta_{1}}^{c}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}},\\
0,&\text{over }\Omega_{\eta_{1}}^{c}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}^{c}.\end{array}\right.

The terms
\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{i},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{i}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}^{-1}
for
i=1,2
can be bounded in
L^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{i},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{i}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{i}}^{-1}\right|\right|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C\iint_{\left(\Omega_{\eta_{1}}^{c}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}\right)\cup\left(\Omega_{\eta_{1}}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}^{c}\right)}dydx\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}.

Over the region
\Omega_{\eta_{1}}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}
we have

\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}\\
=\left(\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\right)\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}+\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\left(\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}\right).

We will now study each of these terms in more detail. For the first, by adding and subtracting appropriate terms we have

\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}=(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)\frac{\partial_{y}(\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})}{(1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1})(1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2})}+\frac{(-\partial_{x}(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}),0)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}

Since by hypothesis
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in B_{\varepsilon/4}
, in particular both belongs to
C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
and consequently we get

\left|\left|\left(\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{1},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{1}}-\frac{(-\partial_{x}\eta_{2},1)}{1+\partial_{y}\eta_{2}}\right)\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}.

For the second term, we just need to note that
\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}-\varpi^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}^{-1}=0
over
\Omega_{\eta_{1}}\cap\Omega_{\eta_{2}}
. Then, combining the above argument yields

||\nabla w_{\eta_{1}}-\nabla w_{\eta_{2}}||_{L^{p}}\leq C||\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}||_{C^{1+\alpha}}.

Going back in our argument, we conclude that we have proven (
67
).
Finally, we prove that

||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h\circ\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\\
\leq||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h\circ(\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}})||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}+||(D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h)\circ\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\\
\leq||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h||_{C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}||\Phi_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}\\
+||\nabla\Phi_{\eta_{2}}||^{\alpha}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,a)\cup(b,1])}||D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{1}}h-D_{f}\Psi_{\eta_{2}}h||_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}.

To prove smallness it suffices to apply the Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

The functional

\displaystyle F[f,\lambda]=\lambda f-\overline{\Psi}_{f}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Psi}_{f}(\bar{x},\cdot)d\bar{x}

acting from
B_{\varepsilon/4}
to
C^{1+\alpha}_{0,\,\text{even}}
is Frechet differentiable and its derivative is continuous.

5.
Analysis of the linear operator

In order to compute the differential of the functional
F
at the origin we will take
\eta=0
in (
58
). Since
\Phi_{0}(x,y)=(x,y)
, we have that (
44
) translates into

(69)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\Psi_{0}&=\omega_{0}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{T}\times[0,1],$}\\
\Psi_{0}\big|_{y=0}&=0,\\
\Psi_{0}\big|_{y=1}&=-\tfrac{1}{3},\end{aligned}\right.

and (
59
) translates into

(70)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta D_{f}\Psi_{0}h=&d\omega_{0},\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]$},\\
D_{f}\Psi_{0}h\big|_{y=0}=&0,\\
D_{f}\Psi_{0}h\big|_{y=1}=&0,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle d\omega_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\varpi^{\prime}(y)h(x,y)&\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,a),\\
0&\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times[a,b],\\
-\varpi^{\prime}(y)h(x,y)&\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times(b,1].\end{array}\right.

For
\Psi_{0}
, solving (
69
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\int_{0}^{\bar{y}}\varpi(z)dzd\bar{y}+v_{\varpi}y,

with

(71)

\displaystyle v_{\varpi}\equiv-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\bar{y}}\varpi(z)dzd\bar{y}-\frac{1}{3}.

This means that

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\varpi(\bar{y})d\bar{y}+v_{\varpi}=u(y)+v_{\varpi},

where
u
is given by (
2.1.1
). Let us emphasis that the velocity of the flow corresponding with
\varpi
will be given by

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\text{shear}}(y)=(-\Psi^{\prime}_{0}(y),\,0),

which for
\varepsilon=0
is the Poiseuille flow. We find, using (
58
), that

(72)

\displaystyle D_{f}\overline{\Psi}_{0}h(x,y)=h(x,y)\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(y)+D_{f}\Psi_{0}h(x,y).

Since the mean of
h
is equal to zero, we obtain

(73)

\displaystyle D_{f}F[0,\lambda]h=

\displaystyle\lambda h-\Psi_{0}^{\prime}h-D_{f}\Psi_{0}h+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\Psi_{0}^{\prime}(y)h(\bar{x},y)+D_{f}\Psi_{0}h(\bar{x},y)\right)d\bar{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-\Psi_{0}^{\prime}\right)h-D_{f}\Psi_{0}h.

Next, we analyze the term
D_{f}\Psi_{0}h
. In order to do it, we need to introduce the expansions

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}(y)\cos(nx),

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(y)\cos(nx),

where, for simplicity, we used the notation
\varphi\equiv D_{f}\Psi_{0}h
.

Introducing both expansions into (
70
), we have the relation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial^{2}_{y}\varphi_{n}-n^{2}\varphi_{n}=&-\varpi^{\prime}h_{n},\quad y\in[0,a)\cup(b,1],\\
\varphi_{n}(1)=&0,\\
\varphi_{n}(0)=&0.\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, solving the above system, we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(y)=\int_{0}^{1}\varpi^{\prime}(z)G_{n}(y,z)h_{n}(z)dz,

where

\displaystyle G_{n}(y,z)=\frac{1}{n\sinh(n)}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sinh(n(1-y))\sinh(nz)&\quad z<y,\\
\sinh(ny)\sinh(n(1-z))&\quad z>y.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
G_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following properties

Lemma 6.10
.

Let

Proof of (1).

We will start by estimating

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\tilde{\mu}-1/2}{1/2-\tilde{\nu}}\right)=\log\left(\frac{(\tilde{\mu}-1/2)(1/2+\tilde{\nu})}{1/4-\tilde{\nu}^{2}}\right).

Since
\tilde{\nu}^{2}=\tilde{\mu}^{2}-\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)
and
\tilde{\mu}=1/2+\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon
we obtain that the above denominator can be written as

\displaystyle 1/4-\tilde{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle=1/4-(\tilde{\mu}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon})=(1/2-\tilde{\mu})(1/2+\tilde{\mu})+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}=-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(1-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon))\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=(1/2-\mu_{1})\varepsilon-\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right),

where in the last step we have used that
\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{1}\right)+\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon\log^{2}(\varepsilon)
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mu}-1/2}{1/2-\tilde{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\tilde{\mu}-1/2)(1/2+\tilde{\nu})}{1/4-\tilde{\nu}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1/2+\mu_{1}\right)\varepsilon+\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\varepsilon)}{(1/2-\mu_{1})\varepsilon-\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)}(1/2+\tilde{\nu})

\displaystyle=\frac{1/2+\mu_{1}}{1/2-\mu_{1}}\frac{1+\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)}{1/2+\mu_{1}}\varepsilon\log^{2}(\varepsilon)}{1-\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)}{1/2-\mu_{1}}\varepsilon\log^{2}(\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon)}(1/2+\tilde{\nu}),

and we obtain that

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\tilde{\mu}-1/2}{1/2-\tilde{\nu}}\right)=

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1/2+\mu_{1}}{1/2-\mu_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle+\log\left(\frac{1+\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)}{1/2+\mu_{1}}\varepsilon\log^{2}(\varepsilon)}{1-\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{2}(\varepsilon)}{1/2-\mu_{1}}\varepsilon\log^{2}(\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon)}\right)+\log(1/2+\tilde{\nu}).

We can easily calculate that

\displaystyle 1/2+\tilde{\nu}=1+\tilde{\nu}-1/2=1+\frac{\tilde{\nu}^{2}-1/4}{\tilde{\nu}+1/2}=1+\frac{(\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)-1)\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\tilde{\mu}_{1}(\varepsilon)^{2}-\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)}{\tilde{\nu}+1/2},

Finally, since

∎

with

\Omega^{ext}_{f}=\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in T\times[-1,1]\,:\,(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\Phi_{f}(x,y)\text{ with }x\in T\text{ and }y\in\supp(\varpi^{\prime})\}.

Let us start working with the first integral term. Then, making the appropriate change of variables, we obtain

I_{1}=\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{|\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf x}))-\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf y}))|^{2}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))d{\bf y}d{\bf x}.

In order to estimate
I_{1}
, we recall that

\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf x}))=\frac{\left(-\partial_{1}f({\bf x}),1\right)}{1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x})}\varpi^{\prime}(y),\qquad\text{on }\supp(\nabla\omega).

and the fact that
\varpi^{\prime}=(-2)\chi_{[-1,-b_{\varepsilon}]\cup[-a_{\varepsilon},+a_{\varepsilon}]\cup[+b_{\varepsilon},+1]}
. Then, we get

\frac{|\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf x}))-\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf y}))|^{2}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))\\
=\frac{(-2)^{2}}{(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))}\frac{\left(\partial_{1}f({\bf y})(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))-\partial_{1}f({\bf x})(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))\right)^{2}+(\partial_{2}f({\bf y})-\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))^{2}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}.

Since
\Phi_{f}:T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})\rightarrow\Omega^{ext}_{f}
is a diffeomorphism and
||f||_{C^{1+\alpha}(T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime}))}
is small enough, we have that there exists universal positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\leq\frac{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}\leq c_{2}.

In addition, adding and subtracting appropriate terms in the numerator and using the fact that
||f||_{C^{1+\alpha}(T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime}))}
is small enough we arrive to the upper bound

\frac{|\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf x}))-\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf y}))|^{2}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))\\
\leq C\frac{|(\partial_{1}f({\bf y})-\partial_{1}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))+\partial_{1}f({\bf x})(\partial_{2}f({\bf x})-\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}\\
+C\frac{|\partial_{2}f({\bf y})-\partial_{2}f({\bf x})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}},

for some uniform constant
C
.

Then, we have seen that

I_{1}=\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{|\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf x}))-\nabla\omega(\Phi_{f}({\bf y}))|^{2}}{|\Phi_{f}({\bf x})-\Phi_{f}({\bf y})|^{2+2\gamma}}(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf x}))(1+\partial_{2}f({\bf y}))d{\bf y}d{\bf x}\\
\leq C\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{|\nabla f({\bf x})-\nabla f({\bf y})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}\\
=C\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{|\nabla f({\bf x})-\nabla f({\bf y})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2\alpha}}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2(\gamma-\alpha)}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}.

To conclude with
I_{1}
, we just need to note that
||f||_{C^{1+\alpha}(T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime}))}=o(1)
in terms of
\sigma
implies that

\frac{|\nabla f({\bf x})-\nabla f({\bf y})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2\alpha}}=o(1).

Now, recalling that in fact we are just interested in
0<\gamma<1/2
and since
1/2\leq\alpha<1
we obtain that the last term integrate and we get the desired result. That is,

C\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{|\nabla f({\bf x})-\nabla f({\bf y})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2\alpha}}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2(\gamma-\alpha)}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}\\
=Co(1)\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\int_{T\times\supp(\varpi^{\prime})}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2(\gamma-\alpha)}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}=Co(1)=o(1).

To finish with the proof we have to estimate
I_{2}
. We start noting that

I_{2}=\int_{\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\int_{\Omega^{ext}\setminus\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\frac{|\nabla\omega({\bf x})-\nabla\omega({\bf y})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\int_{\Omega^{ext}\setminus\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\frac{|\nabla\omega({\bf x})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}

In addition, by using that
\omega\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, we obtain

I_{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\int_{\Omega^{ext}\setminus\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}.

Now, using the oddness of
f
with respect to the vertical variable, we have that

\int_{\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\int_{\Omega^{ext}\setminus\Omega^{ext}_{f}}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}d{\bf y}d{\bf x}\\
=2\int_{T\times T}dx_{1}dy_{1}\int_{[0,a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})]\cup[b_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},b_{\varepsilon}),1]}dx_{2}\int_{[a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon}),b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})]}dy_{2}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2+2\gamma}}\\
=2\int_{T\times T}dx_{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}\\
+2\int_{T\times T}dx_{1}dy_{1}\int_{b_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}^{1}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}.

We focus on just one term of the above summand, since the other follows by the same argument. For example, we provide all the details for the first one. That is,

\int_{T\times T}dx_{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}.

In fact, taking
\sigma
small enough, we can decompose the double integral in
(x_{2},y_{2})
-variables as follows

\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}=\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{1/2}dy_{2}\\
+\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{1/2}^{b_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},b_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2},

where only the first double integral gives us a singularity in the denominator. For that reason, in the following we will focus only on estimating the most delicate term. That is,

J=\int_{T}dx_{1}\int_{T}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}+f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{1/2}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}.

By making appropriate change of variables, we get that
J
can be written as

\iint_{T\times T}dx_{1}dy_{1}\int_{-f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{a_{\varepsilon}}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})-y_{2}-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}.

Fixed
\varepsilon
, taking
\sigma
sufficiently small, we have

\displaystyle(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle+(x_{2}-y_{2}+f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon}))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}+2(x_{2}-y_{2})(f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon}))

\displaystyle\geq(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\left[1-2\left(\frac{f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}{x_{1}-y_{1}}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\left(1-2||f||_{C^{1+\alpha}(T\times\sup(\varpi^{\prime}))}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\left((x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right),

for some positive constant
c
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{T}dx_{1}\int_{T}dy_{1}\int_{-f(x_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}^{a_{\varepsilon}}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2-f(y_{1},a_{\varepsilon})}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{T}dx_{1}\int_{T}dy_{1}\int_{-||f||_{L^{\infty}}}^{a_{\varepsilon}}dx_{2}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2+||f||_{L^{\infty}}}dy_{2}\frac{1}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}.

Now we compute

\displaystyle\int_{T}\int_{T}\frac{dx_{1}dy_{1}}{\left[(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{2\gamma}}\int_{-\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}^{\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}\int_{-\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}^{\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}\frac{dx_{1}dy_{1}}{\left[1+(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{2\gamma}}\int_{-\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}^{\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}dy_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{1}\frac{1}{\left[1+(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\right]^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{2\gamma}}\int_{-\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}^{\frac{2\pi}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}dy_{1}\leq\frac{C}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{1+2\gamma}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-||f||_{L^{\infty}}}^{a_{\varepsilon}}\int_{a_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2+||f||_{L^{\infty}}}\frac{1}{(y_{2}-x_{2})^{1+2\gamma}}dy_{2}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}\int_{-||f||_{L^{\infty}}}^{a_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{(a_{\varepsilon}-x_{2})^{2\gamma}}-\frac{1}{(1/2+||f||_{L^{\infty}}-x_{2})^{2\gamma}}\right)dx_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\gamma(1-2\gamma)}\left[(a_{\varepsilon}+||f||_{L^{\infty}})^{1-2\gamma}-(1/2+2||f||_{L^{\infty}})^{1-2\gamma}+(1/2-a_{\varepsilon}+||f||_{L^{\infty}})^{1-2\gamma}\right].

Since
0<\gamma<1/2
,
1/2-a_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/2
, and we can make
||f||_{L^{\infty}}
as small as we want by taking
\sigma
small enough, we have proved that
J
is of order
o(1)
.

To sum up, combining all we have proved that

||\nabla(\omega+2y)||^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2^{-}}(\Omega)}=o(1).

Consequently, for any
\epsilon>0
and for any
0\leq\gamma<3/2
, taking
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
small enough, we find a
traveling wave such that its vorticity satisfies
||\omega^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}+2y||_{H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])}<\epsilon.

∎

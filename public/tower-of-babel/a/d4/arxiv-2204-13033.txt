Franz Achleitner, Anton Arnold, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
For linear dynamical systems (in continuous-time and discrete-time) we revisit and extend the concepts of hypocoercivity and hypocontractivity and give a detailed analysis of the relations of these concepts to (asymptotic) stability, as well as (semi-)dissipativity and (semi-)contractivity, respectively. On the basis of these results, the short-time behavior of the propagator norm for linear continuous-time and discrete-time systems is characterized by the (shifted) hypocoercivity index and the (scaled) hypocontractivity index, respectively.



keywords

semi-dissipative Hamiltonian ODEs, hypocoercivity (index), semi-contractive systems, hypocontractivity (index), Cayley transformation

Introduction

In this paper we discuss different concepts that characterize the short and long time behavior of linear continuous-time ordinary differential equations (ODEs)

x^{\prime}(t)=\mathbf{A}_{c}x(t)=-\mathbf{B}x(t)\,,\quad x(0)=x^{0},\quad t\geq 0,

(1.1)

and discrete-time difference equations (DDEs)

x_{k+1}=\mathbf{A}_{d}x_{k}\,,\quad x_{0}=x^{0},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1.2)

with matrices
\mathbf{A}_{c},\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

It is well-known that the long-time behavior of solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be characterized via the spectral properties of the matrices
\mathbf{A}_{c},\mathbf{A}_{d}
or the solutions of Lyapunov equations
[
5
,
13
,
18
,
19
]
.
To understand the short-time behavior of continuous-time systems much progress has recently been made for systems with a semi-dissipative structure, i.e. systems where
\mathbf{A}_{c}
has a semidefinite symmetric part. For this subclass it has recently been observed in
[
2
,
3
]
that the short- and long-time behavior can be characterized via the concept of hypocoercivity and the hypocoercivity index. For this subclass also the analysis of the long-time behavior becomes simpler and more elegant.

In this paper we show that a similar concept of
hypocontractivity and a hypocontractivity index
is analogously available in the discrete-time case and that it can be characterized via the polar decomposition of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.

For both, the continuous- and discrete-time we present a systematic review and analysis of the different concepts and show the subtle differences and similarities to the classical spectral concepts and illustrate these with numerous examples.
Furthermore, we present the close relationship of these concepts to classical controllability and observability concepts in control theory.

Note that we switch in the discussion of (
1.1
) between the classical notation with
\mathbf{A}_{c}
as is common in dynamical systems and the notation with
-\mathbf{B}
as is common in evolution equations.

In Section
2
we recall the concepts of (asymptotic) stability, (semi-)dissipativity, and hypocoercivity for continuous-time systems that have been discussed in
[
3
]
.
To better understand the decay behavior of solutions we extend the concept of hypocoercivity to
shifted hypocoercivity
.
We also show under which linear transformations of the system these properties stay invariant.

In the second part of the paper, in Section
3
we derive the corresponding results for discrete-time systems and, in particular, analyze the relation between (asymptotic) stability, (semi-)contractivity, and hypocontractivity as well as
scaled hypocontractivity
.

The third part in Section
4
studies how the discussed properties are related under Cayley transformations that map between continuous-time and discrete-time systems. We show that many properties including the hypocoercivity index and hypocontractivity index map appropriately. However, in general, the shifted hypocoercivity and scaled hypocontractivity indices are not mapped into each other.
Computationally feasible staircase forms to check hypocoercivity for accretive matrices and hypocontractivity for semi-contractive matrices, and to determine the associated indices are discussed in the Appendix.

The conjugate transpose of a matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is denoted by
\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}}
.
Positive definiteness (semi-definiteness) of a Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{C}
is denoted by
\mathbf{C}>0
(

2
Stability, semi-dissipativity, and hypocoercivity for continuous-time systems

In this section we recall some properties of linear continuous-time systems and their relationship.
Let us give a simplified definition of stability, for the general definition see e.g.
[
5
,
13
]
.

Definition 1
.

The trivial solution
x\equiv 0
of (
1.1
) is called
(Lyapunov) stable
if all solutions of (
1.1
) are bounded for
t\geq 0
, and it is called
asymptotically stable
if it is stable and all solutions of (
1.1
) converge to
0
for
t\to\infty
.

For linear systems (
1.1
) a solution is (asymptotically) stable if and only if the trivial solution
x\equiv 0
is (asymptotically) stable. Therefore, if the trivial solution
x\equiv 0
of (
1.1
) is (asymptotically) stable, then we call the system (
1.1
)
(asymptotically) stable
.

It is well-known, see e.g.
[
5
,
13
]
, that (
1.1
) is
(Lyapunov) stable
if all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
have non-positive real part and the eigenvalues on the imaginary axis are semi-simple, and it is
asymptotically stable
if all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
have negative real part.

A concept closely related to stability is that of (semi-)dissipativity.
Writing
\mathbf{A}_{c}
as the sum of its Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{H}:=(\mathbf{A}_{c}+\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})/2
and skew-Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{S}:=(\mathbf{A}_{c}-\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})/2
, we have the following definition,
[
8
, Definition 4.1.1]
.

Definition 2
.

A matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
dissipative
(resp.
semi-dissipative
) if the Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{H}
is negative definite (resp. negative semi-definite).
For a (semi-)dissipative matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the associated ODE (
1.1
) is called
(semi-)dissipative Hamiltonian ODE
.
Alternatively, a matrix
\mathbf{B}=-\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
accretive
(or
positive semi-dissipative
) if its Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}
is positive semi-definite.

An nice property of a
semi-dissipative Hamiltonian ODE (
1.1
) is that it is (Lyapunov) stable, since for all solutions of (
1.1
) we have

\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\operatorname{d}\!t}\|x(t)\|^{2}=\langle{\mathbf{A}_{c}x(t)}\ ,\,{x(t)}\rangle+\langle{x(t)}\ ,\,{\mathbf{A}_{c}x(t)}\rangle=\langle{x(t)}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}}+\mathbf{A}_{c})x(t)}\rangle\leq 0,\quad t\geq 0,

(2.3)

i.e. the Euclidean norm (which may serve as a
Lyapunov function
), is non-increasing.

The converse is in general not true,
because the Hermitian part of a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}
associated with a stable system (
1.1
) does not have to be negative semi-definite, as the following example shows:

Example 3
.

Consider the matrix

\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}3&3\\
-3&-1\end{bmatrix}

so that
\mathbf{A}_{c}=-\mathbf{B}
has eigenvalues
\lambda=-1\pm i\sqrt{5}
, but the Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{H}
is indefinite with eigenvalues
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{A}_{H}}=1
and
\lambda_{\max}^{\mathbf{A}_{H}}=-3
.
Hence, the norm of solutions of (
1.1
) may increase initially at the rate
e^{t}
.

Remark 4

(Logarithmic Norm)
.

Since the flow generated by (
1.1
) is given by the matrix exponential
e^{\mathbf{A}_{c}t}
, the long-time behavior of the propagator norm
\|e^{\mathbf{A}_{c}t}\|
, or to be precise—its exponential rate—is determined by the
spectral abscissa

\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c}):=\max\{\Re(\lambda)\ |\ \text{$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{A}_{c}$}\}\,,

(2.4)

see e.g.
[
28
]
.

In contrast, the exponential rate of the short-time behavior of
\|e^{\mathbf{A}_{c}t}\|
is determined by the logarithmic norm:
The
logarithmic norm
of a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with respect to an inner product is defined as

\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c}):=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\Re(\langle{x}\ ,\,{\mathbf{A}_{c}x}\rangle)=\max_{\|x\|=1}\Re(\langle{x}\ ,\,{\mathbf{A}_{c}x}\rangle)\ ,

(2.5)

i.e.
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
is the maximal real part of the
numerical range of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
.
Thus, the solutions
x(t)
of (
1.1
) satisfy

\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\operatorname{d}\!t}\|x(t)\|^{2}=\langle{x}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}}+\mathbf{A}_{c})x}\rangle\leq 2\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})\ \|x(t)\|^{2}
,
which implies that

\|x(t)\|\leq e^{\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})\ t}\|x^{0}\|\quad\text{for }t\geq 0\ .

(2.6)

In particular, a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is semi-dissipative if and only if
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})\leq 0
.

A third related concept is that of hypocoercivity for matrices and the associated hypocoercivity index, which was introduced originally in the context of linear operators see
[
1
,
6
,
29
]
.

Definition 5

(Definition 2.5 of
[
2
]
)
.

A matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
coercive
(or
strictly accretive
) if its Hermitian part
\mathbf{C}_{H}
is positive definite, and it is called
hypocoercive
if the spectrum of
\mathbf{C}
lies in the
open
right half plane.
A matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
negative hypocoercive
if the spectrum of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
lies in the
open
left half plane.

The relationship between positive semi-dissipativity and hypocoercivity is characterized by the following result.

Proposition 6

(
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
1
, Lemma 2.4 with Proposition 1(B2), (B4)]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be (positive) semi-dissipative.
Then,
\mathbf{B}
has an eigenvalue on the imaginary axis if and only if
\mathbf{B}_{H}v=0
for some eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
.

Note that, due to the assumptions, purely imaginary eigenvalues of semi-dissipative matrices are necessarily semi-simple, see also
[
21
,
22
]
.
Therefore, an accretive matrix
\mathbf{B}
is hypocoercive if and only if no eigenvector of the skew-Hermitian part lies in the kernel of the Hermitian part.
The latter condition is well known in control theory, and equivalent to the following statements:

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be accretive.
Then the following are equivalent:

(B1)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[{\mathbf{B}_{H}},\mathbf{B}_{S}{\mathbf{B}_{H}},\ldots,(\mathbf{B}_{S})^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{H}}]=n\,.

(2.7)

(B2)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathbf{T}_{m}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{B}_{S}^{j}\mathbf{B}_{H}((\mathbf{B}_{S})^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}>0\,.

(2.8)

(B3)

No eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
lies in the kernel of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
.

(B4)

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B}_{S},\mathbf{B}_{H}]=n
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, in particular for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
.

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in (B1) and (B2) coincide.

Proof.

The equivalence of (B1), (B3), and (B4) and its proof are classical, see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.1]
for real matrices, but its proof extends verbatim to complex matrices; see also
[
1
, Proposition 1]
.
The equivalence of (B1) and (B2) follows from Lemma
59
in the Appendix, setting
\mathbf{D}:=\mathbf{B}_{H}
and
\mathbf{C}:=\mathbf{B}_{S}
.
∎

Remark 8
.

In Lemma
7
we could have alternatively stated the equivalence of the following conditions, that are equivalent to the corresponding ones in Lemma
7
.

(B1’)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[{\mathbf{B}_{H}},\mathbf{B}{\mathbf{B}_{H}},\ldots,\mathbf{B}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{H}}]=n\,.

(B2’)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{B}^{j}\mathbf{B}_{H}(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}>0\,.

(B2”)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\mathbf{B}_{H}\mathbf{B}^{j}>0\,.

(2.9)

(B3’)

No eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}
lies in the kernel of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
.

(B4’)

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B},\mathbf{B}_{H}]=n
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, in particular for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathbf{B}
.

This is easily seen, since every eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}
that is in the kernel of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
is immediately an eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
; and conversely, every eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
that is in the kernel of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
is also an eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}
, see
[
21
]
.
It also follows directly from the staircase forms presented in
[
3
]
.

Remark 9
.

The equivalence of properties stated in Proposition
6
, Lemma
7
and Remark
8
show that e.g. also the coercivity of the associated matrix
\mathbf{T}_{m}
in (
2.8
) could have been used to define hypocoercivity for accretive matrices (in the finite-dimensional setting).
Only future research of bounded and unbounded accretive operators on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces will decide which is the appropriate characterization for accretive operators to be hypocoercive i.e. to generate a uniformly exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Definition 10

(
[
2
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is accretive and hypocoercive.
The
hypocoercivity index (HC-index)
m_{HC}
of the matrix
\mathbf{B}
is defined as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that (
2.8
) holds.

Note that for
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
(by the Cayley-Hamilton theorem applied to (B1’)) it follows immediately that the hypocoercivity index (if it exists) is bounded by
n-1
. More precisely, for a finite hypocoercivity index we even have
m_{HC}\leq\dim\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})\leq n-1
(see Remark 4(b) in
[
1
]
).
Furthermore, a hypocoercive matrix
\mathbf{B}
is coercive if and only if
m_{HC}=0
.

Remark 11
.

Hypocoercive matrices are often called
positively stable
, whereas negative hypocoercive matrices are often called
stable
.
Note also that in
[
3
, Definition 3]
, the HC-index for a semi-dissipative matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is defined as the HC-index of its accretive counterpart
\mathbf{B}=-\mathbf{A}_{c}
.
We do not make use of this convention here.

Phenomenologically, the HC-index of an accretive matrix
\mathbf{B}
describes the structural complexity of the intertwining of the Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}
and skew-Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{S}
(see
[
1
]
for illustrating examples).
Moreover, for a semi-dissipative Hamiltonian ODE (
1.1
), the HC-index characterizes the short-time decay of the spectral norm of the
propagator
of the associated semigroup
S(t):=e^{-\mathbf{B}t}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
t\geq 0
.

Proposition 12

(
[
2
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

Let the ODE system (
1.1
) be semi-dissipative Hamiltonian with
(accretive) matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

(a)

The (accretive) matrix
\mathbf{B}
is hypocoercive (with hypocoercivity index
m_{HC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
)
if and only if

\|e^{-\mathbf{B}t}\|_{2}=1-ct^{a}+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{a+1})\quad\text{ for }t\in[0,\epsilon),

(2.10)

for some
a,c,\epsilon>0
. In this case, necessarily
a=2m_{HC}+1
.

(b)

Consider the ODE (
1.1
) with
\epsilon
-dependent system matrix
B=\epsilon A+C
where
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
B=\epsilon A+C
is hypocoercive for
\epsilon\neq 0
, then the coefficient
c=c_{\epsilon}
in the Taylor expansion of the propagator norm (
2.10
) satisfies

0<\tilde{c}_{2}\,\epsilon^{2m_{HC}}\leq c=c_{\epsilon}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\,\epsilon^{2m_{HC}},

(2.11)

for some positive constants
\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2}>0
independent of
\epsilon\neq 0
.

Remark 14
.

As one of the main applications of the analysis of the three discussed concepts is the study of (semi-)dissipative Hamiltonian systems, a natural concept that could be added to the description of the dynamical system is that of a
Hamiltonian or energy function
.
In the abstract setting that we have discussed so far, the natural energy function is the Euclidean norm of the solution.
Further energy functions will be discussed below.

Remark 15

(logarithmically optimal norms)
.

For a Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, its logarithmic norm
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
and its spectral abscissa
\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c})
are equal,
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})=\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c})
.
In general, however, only the inequality
\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c})\leq\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
holds, see e.g.
[
26
, Lemma 1c]
.
A norm is
logarithmically optimal

with respect to a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}
if its spectral abscissa
\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c})
and logarithmic norm
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
are equal, i.e.
\alpha(\mathbf{A}_{c})=\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
.
Thus the Euclidean norm is logarithmically optimal for all Hermitian matrices.

To analyze the relationship between the different concepts further, in the next section we first discuss the question by which transformations of (
1.1
) we can switch between the different concepts and which transformations leave the different properties invariant.

2.1
Linear transformations that preserve stability, semi-dissipativity, and hypocoercivity

In this section we discuss the classes of linear transformations that preserve the concepts of stability, semi-dissipativity, and hypocoercivity, and also those that map between the different concepts, see also e.g.
[
15
,
16
]
for some references.
The natural classes of linear transformations that preserve the different properties and the HC-index (in case of accretive matrices) are

conjugate transposition

\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}
, due to Definition
10
and Lemma
7
;

unitary congruence transformations

\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{U}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{U}^{\mathsf{H}}
for a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}
, due to Definition
10
and Lemma
7
;

scaling

\mathbf{B}\to t\mathbf{B}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, due to Definition
10
and Lemma
7
;
and, as we will show in Lemma
20
below, the
inversion
of accretive hypocoercive matrices.

It is a classical result, see e.g.
[
5
]
, how to construct a similarity transformation of a “stable” matrix
\mathbf{B}
such that the transformed matrix is accretive:
The origin
x\equiv 0
is a stable state of system (
1.1
) if and only if there exists a positive definite matrix
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
that satisfies the
Lyapunov matrix inequality

\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}\geq 0\,.

(2.12)

A congruence transformation with the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}
, i.e. the inverse of the positive definite square root of
\mathbf{P}
, yields

0\leq\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B})\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}^{1/2}+\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=2\big(\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}\big)_{H}\,.

(2.13)

Hence, the matrix

\widehat{\mathbf{B}}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}

(2.14)

is accretive.
Moreover, the change of basis
\tilde{x}(t):=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}x(t)
transforms (
1.1
) into a semi-dissipative Hamiltonian ODE system of the form

\tilde{x}^{\prime}(t)=-\big(\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}\big)\tilde{x}(t)=-\widehat{\mathbf{B}}\ \tilde{x}(t)\,.

(2.15)

Although
similarity transformations

\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{S}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{S}^{-1}
for invertible matrices
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
preserve the spectrum (and hence (negative) hypocoercivity), they
may change
the HC-index of accretive matrices:

Example 16
.

The matrix

\mathbf{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}

(2.16)

is accretive and hypocoercive with
m_{HC}=1
(having eigenvalues
\lambda_{\pm}=(1\pm i\sqrt{3})/2
).
The positive definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\
-1&2\end{bmatrix}
satisfies the continuous-time Lyapunov equation
\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}=2\Re(\lambda)\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}
. The similarity transformation (
2.14
) yields a coercive matrix

\widehat{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&-\sqrt{3}\\
\sqrt{3}&1\end{bmatrix}\,,

hence
m_{HC}(\widehat{\mathbf{B}})=0
.

In a similar way,
non-unitary
congruence transformations
\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}
for some nonsingular matrix
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
may change the HC-index as the following example demonstrates.

Example 17
.

Consider the accretive matrix

\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\,.

The matrix
\mathbf{B}
has an eigenvalue
i
, hence it is not hypocoercive. A congruence transformation with the (non-unitary) matrix

\mathbf{Q}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{ yields }\quad\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}i&i\\
i&1+i\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}+i\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\,,

which is again accretive (due to Sylvester’s inertia theorem, see e.g.
[
10
]
).
However, the matrix
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}
has eigenvalues
\tfrac{1}{2}+i\ (1\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
, and is hypocoercive with HC-index
m_{HC}=1
.

As we have already discussed, changing the HC-index also changes the short-time behavior of the solutions of the dynamical system (
1.1
).

Example 18
.

Consider the matrix
\mathbf{B}
in Example
3
.
In agreement with Proposition
12
, (the norm of) solutions of the ODE (
1.1
) may have horizontal tangents (at any point
t_{0}\geq 0
) with local behavior
\|x(t)\|=\|x(t_{0})\|-c(t-t_{0})^{3}+{\mathcal{O}}((t-t_{0})^{4})
for some
c>0
.
Proceeding as in
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
, the similarity transformation (
2.14
) with

\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}3&2\\
2&3\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{yields a coercive matrix}\quad\widehat{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\sqrt{5}\\
-\sqrt{5}&1\end{bmatrix}\,.

Accordingly, (the norm of) solutions of the associated ODE (
2.15
) cannot have horizontal tangents (see Figure
2
).

:

x(t)
of the ODE (

Remark 19
.

We note that solutions
\mathbf{P}
to the Lyapunov inequality (

) are typically not unique, and one can use this freedom to determine solutions that optimize certain robustness measures like the distance to instability, see e.g.

.

It is an important observation that semi-dissipativity, hypocoercivity and the HC-index stay invariant when the inverse of a matrix is taken:

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

1.

If
\mathbf{B}
is hypocoercive then
\mathbf{B}
is invertible and
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is hypocoercive.

2.

If
\mathbf{B}
is accretive and invertible then it follows that

a.

If
v\in\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
then
\mathbf{B}v\in\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})
.

b.

\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is accretive.

c.

\dim\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})=\dim\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})
.

3.

If
\mathbf{B}
is accretive and hypocoercive then
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
have the same HC-index.

Proof.

1. A matrix
\mathbf{B}
is hypocoercive if all eigenvalues have positive real-part.
Hence, the matrix
\mathbf{B}
is invertible, and since the eigenvalues of the inverse of
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
are the inverses of the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{B}
, they have positive real-part and
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is hypocoercive.

2a. Writing
\mathbf{B}
as
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{H}+\mathbf{B}_{S}
, it follows that if
v\in\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})
then
\mathbf{B}v=\mathbf{B}_{S}v=-\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}v
.
Thus,

(\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H}(\mathbf{B}v)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mathbf{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}v)+(\mathbf{B}^{-1})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{B}v)\big)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(v-(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}})^{-1}(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}v)\big)=0\ .

(2.17)

2b. To prove that
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is again accretive, we show the following identity:
For all vectors
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, define
v:=\mathbf{B}^{-1}w
, such that

\begin{split}\langle{w}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H}w}\rangle&=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{w}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{B}^{-1}+(\mathbf{B}^{-1})^{\mathsf{H}})w}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbf{B}v}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{B}^{-1}+\mathbf{B}^{-{\mathsf{H}}})\mathbf{B}v}\rangle\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{v}\ ,\,{\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{B}^{-1}+\mathbf{B}^{-{\mathsf{H}}})\mathbf{B}v}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{v}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}+\mathbf{B})v}\rangle=\langle{v}\ ,\,{\mathbf{B}_{H}v}\rangle\geq 0\ ,\end{split}

(2.18)

since
\mathbf{B}
is accretive.
Hence,
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is accretive as well.

2c. Due to part 2a. and a similar statement with the roles of
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
exchanged,
\mathbf{B}
is a bijection from
\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})
to
\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})
.

3. By assumption, the matrix
\mathbf{B}
has a finite HC-index
m_{HC}
which is the smallest integer such that (
2.7
) holds or equivalently, due to (
2.9
), that

\bigcap_{j=0}^{m_{HC}}\ker\big(\mathbf{B}_{H}\mathbf{B}^{j}\big)=\{0\}

holds, see also
[
1
, Remark 4]
.
Hence, there exists a vector
v_{0}\neq 0
such that

\mathbf{B}^{j}v_{0}\in\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})\ ,\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{HC}-1\}\qquad\text{and }\mathbf{B}^{m_{HC}}v_{0}\notin\ker(\mathbf{B}_{H})\ .

(2.19)

Due to 2b., it follows that

\mathbf{B}^{j+1}v_{0}\in\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})\ ,\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{HC}-1\}\qquad\text{and }\mathbf{B}^{m_{HC}+1}v_{0}\notin\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})\ .

(2.20)

The matrix
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
is hypocoercive and accretive with finite HC-index
\widetilde{m}_{HC}:=m_{HC}(\mathbf{B}^{-1})
and hence, there exists a vector
w_{0}\neq 0
such that

(\mathbf{B}^{-1})^{j}w_{0}\in\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})\ ,\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,\widetilde{m}_{HC}-1\}\quad\text{and }(\mathbf{B}^{-1})^{\widetilde{m}_{HC}}w_{0}\notin\ker((\mathbf{B}^{-1})_{H})\ .

(2.21)

To show that
m_{HC}=m_{HC}(\mathbf{B})=m_{HC}(\mathbf{B}^{-1})=\widetilde{m}_{HC}
, suppose that
v_{0}
is a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying (
2.20
) with
m_{HC}=m_{HC}(\mathbf{B})
.
Then
w_{0}:=\mathbf{B}^{m_{HC}}v_{0}
satisfies
w_{0}\neq 0
, hence, (
2.21
) implies that
m_{HC}(\mathbf{B}^{-1})\geq m_{HC}(\mathbf{B})
.
Exchanging the roles of
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{B}^{-1}
shows that
m_{HC}(\mathbf{B}^{-1})\leq m_{HC}(\mathbf{B})
.
Altogether,
m_{HC}(\mathbf{B})=m_{HC}(\mathbf{B}^{-1})
holds.
∎

In this section we have discussed linear transformations and their effects on the concepts of hypocoercivity, stability and semi-dissipativity.
In the next section we discuss how the (concept of the) HC-index for accretive matrices can be transferred to general matrices.

2.2
Shifted hypocoercivity index for general matrices

A possibility to turn a general system (
1.1
) into a semi-dissipative Hamiltonian system is to shift the spectrum.
Consider the transformation

v(t):=\exp(\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}t)x(t)\,,

(2.22)

where
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
is the minimal (real) eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{B}_{H}
.
Then,
v(t)
satisfies the ODE

v^{\prime}(t)=-\underbrace{(\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I})}_{=:\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}}v(t)\ ,

where the Hermitian part
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{H}
of
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}
is indeed positive semi-definite.
Of course, the hypocoercivity index of matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is typically modified by the shift parameter
\lambda
.

Remark 21
.

The transformation (
2.22
) can be motivated as follows:
The propagator for ODE (
1.1
) with
\mathbf{A}_{c}=-\mathbf{B}
satisfies estimate (
2.6
) based on the logarithmic norm
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
.
Therefore, for
t\geq 0
,

1\geq\|e^{\mathbf{A}_{c}t}\|e^{-\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})\ t}=\|e^{(\mathbf{A}_{c}-\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})\mathbf{I})\ t}\|=\|e^{-(\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I})\ t}\|,

since the logarithmic norm
\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c})
can also be characterized as

\mu(\mathbf{A}_{c}):=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\Re(\langle{x}\ ,\,{\mathbf{A}_{c}x}\rangle)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle{x}\ ,\,{\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}}+\mathbf{A}_{c})x}\rangle=\lambda_{\max}^{\mathbf{A}_{H}}=-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\ ,

where
\lambda_{\max}^{\mathbf{A}_{H}}
is the maximal (real) eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{A}_{H}
.

In view of this shifting property, for general linear time-invariant ODE systems (
1.1
) with matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we will define a
shifted hypocoercivity index
which characterizes “the algebraic factor“ in the decay of its propagator norm for short time, see Corollary
26
below.
As a first step, we decompose the matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}
, and let
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
be the minimal (real) eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{B}_{H}
(which could be negative or non-negative).
Then,
the matrix

\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}:=\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}

(2.23)

is accretive and, if
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is hypocoercive, has an HC-index
m_{HC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})
greater than
0
.

In particular,
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is hypocoercive if and only if no eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
associated with
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
is an eigenvector of the skew-Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{S}
of
\mathbf{B}
.

Proof.

If we decompose
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{H}+\mathbf{B}_{S}
into its Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}
and its skew-Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{S}
, then
\mathbf{B}_{H}
has only real eigenvalues.
Consider the matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}:=\mathbf{B}-\lambda\mathbf{I}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\lambda=\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
is the only value for which the Hermitian part of
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is positive semi-definite and singular (hence, if
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is hypocoercive then
m_{HC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})>0
).

The hypocoercivity condition for
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
follows from Lemma
7
, (B3):
Matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
fails to be hypocoercive if and only if an eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{B}_{S}
(which is not changed by the shift) is in the kernel of
(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}+\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{H}})/2=\mathbf{B}_{H}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}
, or equivalently
v
is an eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}_{H}
to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
.
∎

Definition 23
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}
, and let
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
be the minimal (real) eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{B}_{H}
.
If the accretive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}:=\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}
is hypocoercive, then its HC-index
m_{HC}\in\mathbb{N}
is called the
shifted hypocoercivity index (SHC-index)
m_{SHC}
of
\mathbf{B}
.

By definition, an accretive matrix has a (finite) HC-index
m_{HC}
if and only if it is positively stable, see also
[
2
,
3
]
.
However, a general (constant) matrix can have a finite SHC-index
m_{SHC}
without being positively stable, see the following example
and Figure
1
.

Example 24
.

Consider the matrix

\mathbf{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}9&-3\\
3&-1\end{bmatrix}

which has the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{2}=8
and hence is not positively stable.
Its Hermitian part
\mathbf{B}_{H}=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(9,-1)
has the minimal eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}=-1
.
Then, in (
2.23
) we have

\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}=\begin{bmatrix}10&-3\\
3&0\end{bmatrix}

which has eigenvalues
1
and
9
.
Therefore,
m_{SHC}(\mathbf{B})=m_{HC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})=1
.

We have
the following characterization for
accretive matrices to have a (finite) SHC-index.

Corollary 25
.

Let
\mathbf{J},\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
satisfy
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}^{\mathsf{H}}
and
\mathbf{J}=-\mathbf{J}^{\mathsf{H}}

and let
\lambda_{\min}
be the minimal eigenvalue of
\mathbf{R}
.
Define
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}:=\mathbf{R}-\lambda_{\min}\mathbf{I}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(B1)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits([\mathbf{R},\mathbf{J}{\mathbf{R}},\ldots,\mathbf{J}^{m}{\mathbf{R}}]-\lambda_{\min}[\mathbf{I},\mathbf{J},\ldots,\mathbf{J}^{m}])=n\,.

(2.24)

(B2)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{J}^{j}\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{J}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}>\lambda_{\min}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{J}^{j}(\mathbf{J}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\,.

(2.25)

(B3)

No eigenvector of
\mathbf{J}
is an eigenvector to
\lambda_{\min}
of
\mathbf{R}
.

(B4)

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J},\mathbf{R}-\lambda_{\min}\mathbf{I}]=n
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, in particular for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathbf{J}
.

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in (B
1
) and (B
2
) coincide.

Proof.

The Hermitian matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}-\lambda_{\min}\mathbf{I}
is positive semi-definite.
Hence, the statement (which is stated for the original matrix
\mathbf{R}
using
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}-\lambda_{\min}\mathbf{I}
) follows from Lemma
7
.
∎

In the following result we show that we can use the SHC-index to characterize the short-time behavior of the propagator norm for general linear time-invariant systems of ODEs.
For this we denote the solution semigroup pertaining to (
1.1
) by
S(t):=e^{-\mathbf{B}t}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
t\geq 0
.

Corollary 26
.

Consider an ODE (
1.1
) with system matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
If
\mathbf{B}
has a finite SHC-index
m_{SHC}(\mathbf{B})
, then

\|e^{-\mathbf{B}t}\|_{2}=e^{-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}t}\ \big(1-ct^{a}+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{a+1})\big)\quad\text{for }t\to 0+\,,

(2.26)

where
\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}
is the smallest eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{B}_{H}
,
a=2m_{SHC}(\mathbf{B})+1\ (\geq 3)
, and
c>0
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{B}
as in (
2.23
) and compute the HC-index
m_{HC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})\ (\geq 1)
of the accretive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{B}-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}
. Using the decomposition (
2.23
) yields

e^{-\mathbf{B}t}=e^{-(\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}\mathbf{I}+\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})t}=e^{-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}t}\ e^{-\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}t}\ ,\text{ such that }\|e^{-\mathbf{B}t}\|_{2}=e^{-\lambda_{\min}^{\mathbf{B}_{H}}t}\ \|e^{-\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}t}\|_{2}\ .

(2.27)

If an accretive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}
is hypocoercive, i.e. having a finite HC-index
m_{HC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})
(or equivalently
\mathbf{B}
has a finite SHC-index
m_{SHC}(\mathbf{B})
) then (
2.26
) follows from Proposition
12
.
∎

In this section we have gathered and extended results about stable, hypocoercive, and semi-dissipative matrices.
These results have analoga for discrete-time systems that are studied in the next section.

3
Stability, semi-contractivity and hypocontractivity for discrete-time systems

In this section we study the analogous concepts for linear discrete-time systems

x_{k+1}=\mathbf{A}_{d}x_{k}\,,\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,,

(3.28)

for a given matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

Remark 27
.

While the stability analysis in discrete-time systems is well studied in linear algebra and operator theory
[
19
]
using spectral properties and discrete-time Lyapunov equations, we proceed by studying hypocontractivity—the analogon to the concept of hypocoercivity in continuous time—and relating to these classical concepts.

Definition 28
.

The trivial solution
x\equiv 0
of the discrete-time system (
3.28
) is called
stable
if all solutions of (
3.28
) are bounded for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and it is called
asymptotically stable
if it is stable and all solutions of (
3.28
) converge to
0
for
k\to\infty
.

For linear systems (
3.28
) a solution is (asymptotically) stable if and only if the trivial solution
x\equiv 0
is (asymptotically) stable.
Therefore, if the trivial solution
x\equiv 0
of (
3.28
) is (asymptotically) stable then the linear system (
3.28
) is called (asymptotically) stable.

It is well-known that (
3.28
) is stable if all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have modulus less or equal than one and the eigenvalues of modulus one are semi-simple (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.3.11]
); and it is
asymptotically stable
if all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have modulus strictly less than one.

Definition 29
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
have eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
The
spectral radius of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is defined as
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{d}):=\max\{|\lambda_{1}|\,,\ldots\,,|\lambda_{n}|\}
, i.e. as the largest absolute value of its eigenvalues.

Hence, a discrete-time system (
3.28
) is
asymptotically stable
if the spectral radius of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is strictly less than one,
\rho(\mathbf{A}_{d})<1
.

An alternative characterization of (asymptotic) stability can be given via the
discrete-time Lyapunov (
or
Stein) equation
:
System (
3.28
) is asymptotically stable if and only if, for all positive definite Hermitian matrices
\mathbf{Q}

\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{P}=-\mathbf{Q}

(3.29)

has a solution
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{H}}>0
, see
[
13
, Theorem 3.3.49]
which is formally given by

\mathbf{P}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\ ,

(3.30)

see
[
13
, (89b) in §3.3.5]
.
In the discrete-time case the concept of hypocoercivity is replaced by that of hypocontractivity, which we introduce in the next subsection.

3.1
Hypocontractive matrices and the hypocontractivity index

For
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
the spectral norm satisfies

\|\mathbf{A}_{d}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}\|_{2}}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}\big(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}\big)}=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\ ,

(3.31)

where
\lambda_{\max}\big(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}\big)
denotes the largest eigenvalue of the positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}
and
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
is the largest singular value of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
Then, the estimate
\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{n}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{d}\|_{2}^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
yields that
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\leq 1
is a sufficient condition for the stability of (
3.28
).
However,
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\leq 1
is not a necessary condition for (
3.28
) to be stable.

Example 30
.

The eigenvalues of

\mathbf{A}_{d}(\alpha)=\alpha\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\,,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(3.32)

are
\pm\alpha
.
Hence, the discrete-time system (
3.28
) with matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
in (
3.32
) is stable if and only if
\alpha\in[-1,1]
.
But the matrix

\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}=\alpha^{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\
-2&5\end{bmatrix}

(3.33)

has positive eigenvalues
\mu_{\pm}=\alpha^{2}(3\pm\sqrt{8})
and singular values
\sigma_{\pm}=\sqrt{\mu_{\pm}}
with
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})=\sigma_{+}
.
Thus,
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\leq 1
holds if
|\alpha|\leq(3+\sqrt{8})^{-1/2}\leq 1/2
which is strictly less than one.
Hence in this example, the condition
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\leq 1
is sufficient but not necessary to ensure the stability of (
3.28
).

In the following we will need a result relating singular values and eigenvalues.

Proposition 31
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
have singular values
\sigma_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{n}\geq 0
(such that
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})=\sigma_{1}
) and eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
being ordered as
|\lambda_{1}|\geq\ldots\geq|\lambda_{n}|
.
Then,
|\lambda_{1}|\leq\sigma_{1}
.
Moreover, if
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is nonsingular, then
|\lambda_{n}|\geq\sigma_{n}>0
.

Proof.

The statements follow from the bounds in
[
14
, Theorem 5.6.9]
. ∎

We then have the following discrete-time analogon of semi-dissipativity.

Definition 32

(
[
8
, Definition 4.1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and let
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
be the largest singular value (the
spectral norm
) of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
We call
\mathbf{A}_{d}

contractive
if
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})<1
;
and we call
\mathbf{A}_{d}

semi-contractive
if
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})\leq 1
.

Note that sometimes
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is called
contractive
if
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\leq 1
;
and
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is called
strictly contractive
if
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})<1
, see e.g.
[
14
, p. 493]
.
Other related notions are (semi-)convergent matrices, and power-bounded matrices, see
[
14
, p. 180]
.

In the following, we consider the class of semi-contractive matrices
\mathbf{A}_{d}
and present a characterization when (
3.28
) is (asymptotically) stable.
For this we need a concept that corresponds to hypocoercivity in the continuous-time case.

Definition 33
.

A matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
hypocontractive
if all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have modulus strictly less than one.

Consequently, a discrete-time system (
3.28
) is asymptotically stable if and only if the system matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive.
We can also characterize those semi-contractive matrices
\mathbf{A}
which are actually hypocontractive:

Proposition 34
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive.
Then,
\mathbf{A}_{d}
has an eigenvalue of modulus one if and only if some eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
satisfies
v\in\kernel(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive, the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}
is positive semi-definite.
Moreover, if
\mathbf{A}_{d}
has an eigenvalue
\lambda
of modulus
|\lambda|=1
with eigenvector
v\neq 0
, then

0\leq\langle{v}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})v}\rangle=\|v\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{A}_{d}v\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}(1-|\lambda|^{2})=0\,.

Therefore,
v
is in the kernel of the positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}
.

Conversely, if some eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
(associated to an eigenvalue
\lambda
) satisfies
v\in\kernel(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
, then

0=\langle{v}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})v}\rangle=\|v\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{A}_{d}v\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}(1-|\lambda|^{2})\,,

and hence, the eigenvalue
\lambda
has modulus one.
∎

Remark 35
.

In the operator theory setting the matrix
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})^{1/2}
is often called the
defect operator
of the semi-contractive
\mathbf{A}_{d}
and the closure of its image is the
defect space
with its dimension being called the
defect index

d(\mathbf{A}_{d})
. The defect operator and its index are a measure for the distance of an operator from being unitary. See e.g.
[
27
]
.

We again have an equivalent characterization in terms of properties from control theory:

Lemma 36
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(D1)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}),\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}),\ldots,(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{m}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})]=n\,.

(3.34)

(D2)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathbf{D}_{m}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}>0\,.

(3.35)

(D3)

No eigenvector of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
lies in the kernel of
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.

(D4)

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}},\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}]=n
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, in particular for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
.

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in (D1) and (D2) coincide.

Proof.

Like Lemma
7
, this result follows from Theorem 6.2.1 of
[
9
]
and Lemma
59
in the Appendix.
∎

Remark 37
.

In control theory, conditions (D1), (D3), and (D4) in Lemma
36
are equivalent characterizations of the
controllability
of the pair
(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}},\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
, or the dynamical system

x_{k+1}=\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}x_{k}+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})u_{k}.

There is always also the dual concept of
observability
which in this case would correspond to the controllability of
(\mathbf{A}_{d},\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})
.
A dual result to Lemma
36
can then be formulated with this pair. Based on this pair, in
[
25
]
a similar result has been derived (in different terminology).
A similar result for the continuous-time case follows from
[
24
]
.

If we compare Lemma
36
with Lemma
7
, then we need to substitute
\mathbf{B}_{S}
with
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
, and
\mathbf{B}_{H}
with
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}
, respectively.
Using Lemma
36
(D2), we then define the hypocontractivity index.

Definition 38
.

For semi-contractive matrices
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we define the
hypocontractivity index
or
discrete HC-index (dHC-index)
m_{dHC}
as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if it exists) such that (
3.35
) holds.

Remark 39
.

The hypocontractivity index is sometimes also called the
norm-one index
, see
[
11
]
, where it is shown that this index is finite if and only if the spectral radius of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is strictly smaller than one.

Clearly, a semi-contractive matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is contractive if and only if
m_{dHC}=0
.
Since (
3.35
) is a telescopic sum, we have that
\mathbf{D}_{m}=\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{m+1}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m+1}
and thus if a semi-contractive matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is hypocontractive with index
m_{dHC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m_{dHC}+1}
is contractive.
Conversely, if a semi-contractive matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
satisfies that
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m}
is contractive for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive with index
m_{dHC}\leq m-1
.

The following result may be considered as a discrete counterpart of the short-time decay behavior from Proposition
12
.

Theorem 40
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive and hypocontractive.
Its (finite) hypocontractivity index is
m_{dHC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
if and only if

\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\|_{2}=1\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,m_{dHC}\,,\quad\text{and }\ \|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m_{dHC}+1}\|_{2}<1\,.

(3.36)

Proof.

The spectral norm
\|\mathbf{C}\|_{2}
of a matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, i.e. the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

\|\mathbf{C}\|_{2}=\max_{w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\ \|w\|=1}\|\mathbf{C}w\|_{2}
.
If a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive, then the estimates
\|\mathbf{A}_{d}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{d}\|_{2}^{j}\leq 1
hold for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, for vectors
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\|w\|_{2}=1
, we have

\langle{w}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}w}\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}w}\ ,\,{\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}w}\rangle=\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}w\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1=\langle{w}\ ,\,{w}\rangle\,,

such that
0\leq\langle{w}\ ,\,{(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j})w}\rangle
. Therefore, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\mathbf{D}_{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}=\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{m+1}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m+1}\geq 0\,

and hence, the semi-contractive matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
has (finite) hypocontractivity index
m_{dHC}
if and only if (
3.36
) holds.
∎

3.2
Polar decomposition

In
[
3
]
a computationally feasible procedure has been presented to check the conditions of Lemma
7
in the continuous-time case via a staircase form under unitary congruence transformations.
A similar procedure can be derived in the discrete-time case.
It is based on polar decomposition, see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 7.3.1]
, which is the discrete-time analogon of the additive splitting of a matrix into its Hermitian and skew-Hermitian part:

Proposition 41

(Polar decomposition)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

(a)

There exist positive semi-definite Hermitian matrices
\mathbf{P}_{d},\mathbf{Q}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
such that

\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{P}_{d}\mathbf{U}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}\ .

(3.37)

The factors
\mathbf{P}_{d}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
are uniquely determined as
\mathbf{P}_{d}=(\mathbf{A}_{d}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{1/2}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{d}=(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})^{1/2}
.
If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is nonsingular, then
\mathbf{U}_{d}=\mathbf{P}_{d}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{A}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{-1}
is uniquely determined (as well).

(b)

If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is real, then the factors
\mathbf{P}_{d}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
and
\mathbf{U}_{d}
may be taken to be real.

Consider a stable discrete-time system (
3.28
) with matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
Hence, all eigenvalues of matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have modulus less or equal than one.
Then, the polar decomposition (
3.37
) yields that the (largest) singular values of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
,
\mathbf{P}_{d}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
are the same, since
\mathbf{A}_{d}\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}=\mathbf{P}_{d}\mathbf{P}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
and
\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
.

An immediate consequence is that a matrix

\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with polar decomposition (
3.37
)
is semi-contractive if and only if the spectra of
\mathbf{P}_{d}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
(which coincide) are contained in
[0,1]
.
We can rephrase the statement of Proposition
34
as follows:

Proposition 42
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive with polar decomposition
\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{d}=(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})^{1/2}
.
Then,
\mathbf{A}_{d}
has an eigenvalue of modulus one (and hence
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is not hypocontractive)
if and only if some eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
satisfies
v\in\kernel(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})
.

Proof.

For the forward direction we assume that the eigenvalue equation
\mathbf{A}_{d}v=\lambda v
holds for some
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=1
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, Proposition
34
implies that
v\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
, i.e.
0=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})v=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{Q}_{d})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})v
which holds if and only if
0=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})v
, such that
0=\mathbf{U}_{d}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})v=\mathbf{U}_{d}v-\lambda v
.
Hence,
v\in\kernel(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})
is an eigenvector of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
.

Conversely, let
w
be an eigenvector of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
, i.e.
\mathbf{U}_{d}w=\lambda w
with
|\lambda|=1
, that satisfies
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})w=0
.
Then
0=\mathbf{U}_{d}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})w=\lambda w-\mathbf{A}_{d}w
.
∎

Note that, for semi-contractive matrices
\mathbf{A}_{d}
, eigenvalues with modulus one are necessarily semi-simple.
Therefore, a semi-contractive matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}
(with polar decomposition
\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
) is hypocontractive if and only if no eigenvector of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
lies in the kernel of the positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}
.

Using this relationship, we formulate an analogous result to Lemma
36
, in terms of matrices appearing in polar decompositions. It follows again from Theorem 6.2.1 of
[
9
]
and Lemma
59
:

Lemma 43
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive with polar decomposition
\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
(i.e. with
\mathbf{U}_{d}
unitary,
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
semi-contractive Hermitian, and
\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2}=\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}
).
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(D1’)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2}),\mathbf{U}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2}),\ldots,(\mathbf{U}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{m}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2})]=n\,.

(3.38)

(D2’)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{m}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(\mathbf{U}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2})\mathbf{U}_{d}^{j}>0\,.

(3.39)

(D3’)

No eigenvector of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
lies in the kernel of
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2}
.

(D4’)

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{U}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}},\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2}]=n
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, in particular for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathbf{U}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
.

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in (D1’) and (D2’)
coincide.

Note that (D3) and (D3’) are equivalent, due to Proposition
42
and since
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d})=\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}_{d}^{2})
.
Consequently, all conditions of the Lemmata
36
and
43
are equivalent and the smallest possible values of
m
coincide.

3.3
Scaled hypocontractivity index

The analogon to the shifted hypocoercivity index is obtained by scaling.

Lemma 44
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a nonzero matrix, and let
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
be the maximal singular value of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
Then, the matrix

\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}:=(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}

(3.40)

is semi-contractive and, if
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
is hypocontractive, has a discrete HC-index
m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
greater than
0
.

Furthermore,
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
is hypocontractive if and only if the matrices in the polar decomposition of
\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
satisfy that no eigenvector of
\mathbf{Q}_{d}=(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})^{1/2}
associated with the eigenvalue
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
is an eigenvector of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
.

Proof.

Consider the matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}(\sigma):=\sigma^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}
for
\sigma>0
.
Then
\sigma=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
is the only value such that the largest singular value of
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}(\sigma)
is one, since

\sigma_{\max}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})}/\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})=1\,.

Consequently, if the scaled matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
is hypocontractive then its discrete HC-index
m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
is greater than
0
.

To prove the final statement we consider the polar decomposition of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
in the form
\mathbf{A}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
.
Then,
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}=(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}

has the polar decomposition
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}=\mathbf{U}_{d}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{d}
with the same unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}_{d}
, and
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{d}:=\linebreak(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{-1}\mathbf{Q}_{d}
.
Due to Proposition
42
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
is hypocontractive if and only if no eigenvector
v
of
\mathbf{U}_{d}
is in the kernel of
\mathbf{I}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{d}
.
The latter is equivalent to
v
being an eigenvector of
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{d}
to the eigenvalue one, or
v
being an eigenvector of
\mathbf{Q}_{d}
to the eigenvalue
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
.
∎

Definition 45
.

Consider a nonzero matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, and let
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
be the maximal (positive) singular value of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
If the semi-contractive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}:=(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}

m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
then we define the
scaled hypocontractivity index
or
discrete SHC-index (dSHC-index)
m_{dSHC}
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
as
m_{dSHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d}):=m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
.

In analogy to Theorem
40
we then have the following characterization when
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
has a finite scaled hypocontractivity index.

Theorem 46
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be nonzero, and let
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
be the maximal (positive) singular value of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
.
If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
has a finite discrete SHC-index

, then

\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\|_{2}=(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{j}\quad\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,m_{dSHC}\ ,\ \text{and }\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m_{dSHC}+1}\|_{2}<(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{m_{dSHC}+1}\ .

(3.41)

Proof.

We scale
\mathbf{A}_{d}
as in (
3.40
) and compute the discrete HC-index
m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})\ (\geq 1)
of the semi-contractive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}=(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d}))^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{d}
so that
m_{dSHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d}):=m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
.
Using the scaling (
3.40
) yields

\|\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\|_{2}=\big\|\big(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}\big)^{j}\big\|_{2}=\big(\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\big)^{j}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}^{j}\|_{2}\ \text{for all $j\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(3.42)

If the semi-contractive matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}
has a (finite) discrete HC-index
m_{dHC}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d})
(or equivalently the discrete SHC-index
m_{dSHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is finite) then (
3.41
) follows from Theorem
40
.
∎

4
Transformation between discrete-time and continuous-time systems

We have seen the close analogy between the results for the continuous-time and discrete-time case.
In this section we recall that the typical bilinear transformations between continuous-time and discrete-time systems such as the Cayley transformation (in fact of
-\mathbf{A}_{c}
) relate hypocoercive with hypocontractive systems (see e.g.
[
13
]
), and semi-dissipative with semi-contractive systems (see e.g.
[
27
]
).
Moreover, we show that the Cayley transformation (of
-\mathbf{A}_{c}
) directly relates the hypocoercivity and hypocontractivity indices.

Lemma 47
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a matrix such that (
1.1
) is (Lyapunov) stable.
Then, the Cayley transform

\mathbf{A}_{d}:=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}

(4.43)

is well-defined and the following properties hold:

(i)

If
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is negative hypocoercive then
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is semi-dissipative then
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive.
Let
\mathbf{A}_{H}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{c}+\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})
, then the matrix
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})
is a bijection from
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
to
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.
Consequently,
\dim\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})=\dim\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.

Proof.

If the continuous-time system (
1.1
) with system matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is
(Lyapunov) stable
then all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
have non-positive real part and the eigenvalues on the imaginary axis are semi-simple.
Hence, the matrices
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})
,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})
are invertible; and the Cayley transform
\mathbf{A}_{d}=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}
is well-defined.

(i) If
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is negative hypocoercive, then all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have absolute value less than one, hence,
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive.

(ii) If
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is semi-dissipative, then
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c}
is positive dissipative (hence
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c}
is invertible).
It follows that

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}&=\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-{\mathsf{H}}}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}\\
&=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-{\mathsf{H}}}\Big((\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{{\mathsf{H}}}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})-(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})\Big)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}\\
&=-2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-{\mathsf{H}}}(\mathbf{A}_{c}^{{\mathsf{H}}}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}\\
&=-4(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-{\mathsf{H}}}\mathbf{A}_{H}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}\,.\end{split}

(4.44)

Hence, the matrices
-\mathbf{A}_{H}
and
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
are related via a congruence transformation.
Therefore,
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive (or equivalently,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
is positive semi-definite) if
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is semi-dissipative.

Due to (
4.44
), if
v\in\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
then
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})v\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.
Thus,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})\subseteq\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.
Conversely, if
w\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
then
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}w\in\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
.
Thus,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\subseteq\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
.
Altogether,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})
is a bijection from
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
to
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
, and
\dim\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})=\dim\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
.
∎

Lemma 49
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be such that

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is stable and that

Then, the inverse Cayley transform

(i)

If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive then
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is negative hypocoercive.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive then
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is semi-dissipative.
Moreover, with
\mathbf{A}_{H}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{c}+\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})

(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})

\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})
to
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})
and
\dim\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})=\dim\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})

Proof.

Since
-1
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
then the matrices
(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})
,
(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{\mathsf{H}}
are invertible; and the inverse Cayley transform (
4.45
) is well-defined.

(i) If
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is hypocontractive then all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
have modulus less than one, hence, all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
have negative real part.
Thus,
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is negative hypocoercive.

(ii) If
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

x_{k+1}=\mathbf{A}_{d}x_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is stable (due to Proposition

).
Then

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}_{H}&=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{A}_{c}+\mathbf{A}_{c}^{\mathsf{H}})\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\big((\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{I})(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}+(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-{\mathsf{H}}}(\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{I})^{\mathsf{H}}\big)\\

Thus, the matrices
-\mathbf{A}_{H}

(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})

\mathbf{A}_{d}
is semi-contractive (or equivalently,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})

Due to (

Conversely, if

Altogether,

The Cayley transformation also gives a direct relation between the hypocoercivity and hypocontractivity indices.

Theorem 51
.

(i)

Let
\mathbf{A}_{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-dissipative and negative hypocoercive and let
\mathbf{A}_{d}:=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}
.
Then the hypocoercivity index
m_{HC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
and the hypocontractivity index
m_{dHC}
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
are the same, i.e.,
m_{dHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d})=m_{HC}(\mathbf{A}_{c})
.

(ii)

Let
\mathbf{A}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be semi-contractive and hypocontractive and let
\mathbf{A}_{c}:=(\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{I})(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}
.
Then the hypocontractivity index
m_{dHC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
and the hypocoercivity index
m_{HC}
of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
are the same, i.e.,
m_{HC}(\mathbf{A}_{c})=m_{dHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
.

Proof.

(i) Due to the assumptions and Lemma
47
,
\mathbf{A}_{d}=2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}-\mathbf{I}

is semi-contractive and hypocontractive.
Thus, by Lemma
49
, the inverse Cayley transform
(\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{I})(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}
is well-defined and satisfies
(\mathbf{A}_{d}-\mathbf{I})(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}=\mathbf{I}-2(\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}=\mathbf{A}_{c}
.

By assumption, the matrix
\mathbf{A}_{c}=\mathbf{A}_{H}+\mathbf{A}_{S}
has a finite HC-index
m_{HC}=m_{HC}(\mathbf{A}_{c})
which is the smallest integer such that, due to (
2.9
),

\bigcap_{j=0}^{m_{HC}}\ker\big(\mathbf{A}_{H}\mathbf{A}_{c}^{j}\big)=\{0\}.

Hence, there exists a vector
v_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mathbf{A}_{c}^{j}v_{0}\in\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})\ ,\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{HC}-1\}\qquad\text{and }\mathbf{A}_{c}^{m_{HC}}v_{0}\notin\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})\ .

(4.47)

Thus, by Lemma
47
(ii), we obtain that

(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})\mathbf{A}_{c}^{j}v_{0}\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\ ,j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{HC}-1\},\ \text{and }(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})\mathbf{A}_{c}^{m_{HC}}v_{0}\notin\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\ .

(4.48)

Conversely, the existence of some
v_{0}\neq 0
satisfying the “first part” of (
4.48
) with some
m_{HC}\geq 1
implies that the HC-index of
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is at least
m_{HC}
.

The matrix
\mathbf{A}_{d}=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})^{-1}
is hypocontractive with HC-index
m_{dHC}:=m_{dHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Due to (
3.35
) this is the smallest integer such that

\bigcap_{j=0}^{m_{dHC}}\ker\big((\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\big)=\{0\}.

Hence, there exists a vector
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that

w_{0}\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m_{dHC}-1}\ker\big((\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}\big)\ ,\quad\text{ and }w_{0}\notin\ker\big((\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m_{dHC}}\big),\

(4.49)

or equivalently, there exists
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mathbf{A}_{d}^{j}w_{0}\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})\ ,\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,m_{dHC}-1\}\qquad\text{and }\mathbf{A}_{d}^{m_{dHC}}w_{0}\notin\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d}).

(4.50)

Conversely, the existence of some
w_{0}\neq 0
satisfying the “first part” of (
4.50
) with some
m_{dHC}\geq 1
implies that the dHC-index of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is at least
m_{dHC}
.

It remains to show that
m_{HC}(\mathbf{A}_{c})=m_{dHC}(\mathbf{A}_{d})
:
If
m_{HC}=0
, then
\mathbf{A}_{c}
is dissipative such that
\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})=\{0\}
. Hence,
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})=\{0\}
due to Lemma
47
(ii) and
\mathbf{A}_{d}
is contractive, i.e.
m_{dHC}=0

then
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})=\{0\}

\ker(\mathbf{A}_{H})=\{0\}
by Lemma

\mathbf{A}_{c}
is dissipative and

If
m_{HC}\geq 1

v_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy (
4.48
) with

q(\mathbf{A}_{c})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{c})v_{0}\in\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{d})

Conclusions

In this paper we have given a systematic analysis of different concepts related to the stability and short-time behavior of solutions to linear constant coefficient continuous-time and discrete-time systems.
While many results for the continuous-time setting were already established in
[
3
]
we have analyzed under which linear transformations the properties of asymptotic stability, semi-dissipativity and hypocoercivity stay invariant.

For linear time-invariant continuous-time systems, it is well-known that the exponential rate of the short-time behavior of the propagator norm
\|e^{\mathbf{A}_{c}t}\|
is determined by the logarithmic norm of the system matrix.
In this work, we established that the shifted hypocoercivity index characterizes the (remaining) algebraic decay of the propagator norm in the short-time regime.

For each of the continuous-time results we have derived a corresponding result for the discrete-time case.
These include the relation between (asymptotic) stability, semi-contractivity and hypocontractivity.
We have also introduced the new concept of shifted hypocoercivity and scaled hypocontractivity.
We then have analyzed how the properties relate under the Cayley transformation that relates continuous-time and discrete-time systems.
While the role of the hypocontractivity index (or norm-one index) in the discrete-time setting has been recognized before, the corresponding concept—the hypocoercivity index—in the continuous-time setting and its role has been established only recently.

Future work will include the extension of the results of
[
3
]
for linear continuous-time differential-algebraic systems to discrete-time descriptor systems.

Appendix B
Equivalent hypocoercivity conditions

The following lemma is a simple generalization of Lemma 2.3 in
[
4
]
and Proposition 1 in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 59
.

Let
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be positive semi-definite and
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(E1)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[{\mathbf{D}},\mathbf{C}{\mathbf{D}},\ldots,\mathbf{C}^{m}{\mathbf{D}}]=n\,.

(B.56)

(E2)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}>0\,.

(B.57)

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in (E1) and (E2) coincide.

Proof.

First, we show that (E1) is equivalent to:

(E1’)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits[{\mathbf{D}^{1/2}},\mathbf{C}{\mathbf{D}}^{1/2},\ldots,\mathbf{C}^{m}{\mathbf{D}}^{1/2}]=n\,,

with the same
m
as in (E1):

(E1) holds iff the statement

x^{\mathsf{H}}[{\mathbf{D}},\mathbf{C}{\mathbf{D}},\ldots,\mathbf{C}^{m}{\mathbf{D}}]=0\quad\mbox{for some }x\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

i.e.
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}x=0
for
j=0,\ldots,m
implies
x=0
. Now, since
\ker(\mathbf{D})=\ker(\mathbf{D}^{1/2})
, (E1) and (E1’) are equivalent.

Next, let (E1) hold and define

\mathbf{E}:=[\mathbf{D}^{1/2},\,\mathbf{C}\mathbf{D}^{1/2},...,\,\mathbf{C}^{m}\mathbf{D}^{1/2}]\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(m+1)n}\,.

Then,

\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\ni\mathbf{E}\ \mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}})^{j}\geq 0

has rank
n
and (
B.57
) follows.

Conversely, let (E2) hold but assume we had
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits\mathbf{E}<n
.
Then,
\exists\,0\neq x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
x^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{E}=0
.
Hence,
x^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{E}\ \mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}=0
would contradict (
B.57
).
∎

Introduction

Poincaré inequalities are very powerful tools in mathematical analysis which have been extensively
used for the study of PDEs. They
also are of great interest in relation with optimal transport, for the study of some aspects of the
topology of some abstract spaces and also in probabilistic problems. For instance, they can be used for proving existence and regularity of solutions to certain PDEs as well as Harnack’s inequalities,
see the seminal works by De Giorgi
[
5
]
, Nash
[
21
]
and Moser
[
19
,
20
]
. Counterparts of the Poincaré inequalities in a weighted setting have gained a lot of attention in the last decades due to their connection with the regularity of solutions to degenerate or singular PDEs which depend on the particular structure of the weight under consideration (see e.g.
[
8
,
10
,
3
,
12
]
and references therein).

Following these ideas, in this paper we extend some recent results proved in
[
3
]
by studying Rellich-Kondrachov type theorems and weighted Poincaré inequalities on the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{z=(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\}
endowed with the weighted Gaussian measure
\mu:=y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}dz
where
c+1>0
and
a>0
. Our main results are Theorems
3.6
and
3.7
where we prove the compactness of the immersion
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
and the validity, for some constant
C>0
, of the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|u-\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})

(1)

where
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{\mu(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}u\,d\mu(z)
.
Here we set
L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(z))
and

H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):=\{u\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\}.

Besides this we also consider the local case of bounded domains of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
, see Corollary
2.3
and Proposition

, where the measure
\mu
is equivalent to the weighted measure
\nu=y^{c}dz
. We remark that in

, Theorem 4.7, Lemma 5.5]
the same results are proved in the smaller range

is in the Muckenhoupt class

. The strategy therein employed by the authors is based on a spectral decomposition of the space
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
which also allows to obtain optimal constants. On the other hand our approach relies on the derivation of some suitable weighted Hardy-type inequalities and has the advantages of being easily applied in the whole range

The motivations for studying the validity of the Poincaré inequalities (
1
) are due to their essential role in proving, using the method of Nash
[
21
,
9

, Harnack’s inequalities and lower heat kernel estimates for the singular operator

in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
, under Neumann boundary conditions at

. Here

is an elliptic matrix and

satisfies

where

. The interest in this class of singular operators has grown in the last decade as they appear extensively in the literature in
both pure and applied problems also in connection with the theory of nonlocal operators (see e.g. the introductions in
[
22
,
6
,
2
]
and references therein).
In the special case

and

(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
and
(D_{t}-\Delta_{x})^{s}

[

We exploit these aspects in

\displaystyle p_{{\mathcal{L}}}(t,z_{1},z_{2})\geq Ct^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}y_{1}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}y_{2}^{-\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{c}{2\gamma}}\,\exp\left(-\dfrac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{kt}\right),

Similar upper estimates are proved in

We now describe the structure of the paper and the strategy for proving (

).

We start by considering, in Section
2

R^{N+1}_{+}

2
Weighted Poincaré inequality on bounded domains

We work in the space
L^{2}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{c}\,dxdy)
and we assume, throughout the paper,
c+1>0
, so that the measure
d\nu=y^{c}\,dx\,dy
is locally finite on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\}
. We define the Sobolev space

H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):=\{u\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):\nabla u\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\}

equipped with the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}:=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}.

Condition
c+1>0
assures , by
[
14
, Lemma 11.1]
, that any function
u\in H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
has a finite trace
u_{0}=u(\cdot,0)
at
y=0
. Moreover, by
[
15
, Theorem 4.9 and 6.1]
, the set

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty)\right),\ D_{y}u(x,y)=0\ {\rm for}\ y\leq\delta\ {\rm and\ some\ }\delta>0\right\},

(2)

is dense in
H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.

Remark 2.1
.

If a function
u\in H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
has support in
B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b]
, then there exists a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mbox{supp }u_{n}\subseteq B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b]
and
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. For
y
-compact supported functions this follows from
[
15
, Remark 4.14]
but the same proof of
[
15
, Theorem 4.9]
adapted to the simpler case
H^{1}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
gives compactness also in the
x
-variable.

We now focus on domains
Q\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. Here for simplicity we suppose
Q=Q_{x}\times(0,b)
where
Q_{x}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
b>0
but all the results of this section are valid for bounded open connected
Q\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
having Lipschitz boundary. This condition is only required in order to guarantee the validity of the divergence Theorem and to allow integration by parts.

As for the global case, we define analogously
L^{2}_{c}\left(Q\right):=L^{2}(Q,y^{c}\,dxdy)
and similarly
H^{1}_{c}(Q)=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{c}(Q)\ :\ \nabla u\in L^{2}_{c}(Q)\right\}
. As before, by
[
14
, Lemma 11.1]
, any function
u\in H^{1}_{c}(Q)
has finite traces
u(\cdot,y)
for any
0\leq y\leq b
.
We impose also a Dirichlet boundary condition at
y=b
by defining

\displaystyle H^{1,0}_{c}(Q)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{c}(Q)\ :\ \nabla u\in L^{2}_{c}(Q)\quad\text{and}\quad u=0\text{ on }y=b\right\}.

We start by proving some Hardy-type inequalities for functions in
H^{1,0}_{c}(Q)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
c+1>0
and let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
. Then one has

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq\frac{2b^{1-\alpha}}{c+1-2\alpha}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1,0}_{c}(Q).

Proof.

Let
u\in\ H^{1,0}_{c}(Q)
. Then integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|u|^{2}y^{c-2\alpha}\,dy=\frac{1}{c+1-2\alpha}\int_{Q_{x}}\int_{0}^{b}|u|^{2}D_{y}\left(y^{c+1-2\alpha}\right)\,dydx

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{c+1-2\alpha}\int_{Q_{x}}\int_{0}^{b}uD_{y}u\,y^{c+1-2\alpha}\,dy+\frac{1}{c+1-2\alpha}\int_{Q_{x}}\Big[|u(x,y)|^{2}y^{c+1-2\alpha}\Big]^{y=b}_{y=0}dx.

Recalling that, by definition,
u=0
on
y=b
and that has finite trace at
y=0
and since
c+1-2\alpha>0
, the last term satisfies

\displaystyle\Big[|u(x,y)|^{2}y^{c+1-2\alpha}\Big]^{y=b}_{y=0}=0.

Then using Hölder inequality and since
y^{1-\alpha}\leq b^{1-\alpha}
, the previous equality implies

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|u|^{2}y^{c-2\alpha}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{c+1-2\alpha}\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\left\|D_{y}u\,y^{1-\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2b^{1-\alpha}}{c+1-2\alpha}\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}.

Using, for
\epsilon>0
, the Young inequality

2\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq\epsilon\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}^{2}

and choosing
\epsilon
such that
\frac{\epsilon b^{1-\alpha}}{c+1-2\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}^{2}\leq\frac{4b^{2-2\alpha}}{(c+1-2\alpha)^{2}}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}^{2}

which is the required claim.

Choosing
\alpha=0
in Proposition
2.2
, we deduce a Poincaré type inequality for functions in
H^{1,0}_{c}(Q)
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
c+1>0
. Then for some positive constant
C>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq\frac{2b}{c+1}\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1,0}_{c}(Q).

We can now prove a Rellich-Kondrachov type theorem which assures the compactness of the embedding
H_{c}^{1,0}\left(Q\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{c}\left(Q\right)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
Q=Q_{x}\times(0,b)
where
Q_{x}
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
c+1>0
. Then the immersion
H_{c}^{1,0}\left(Q\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{c}\left(Q\right)
is compact.

Proof.

Let
u
be in the unit ball

\mathcal{B}
of
H_{c}^{1,0}\left(Q\right)
and fix
0<\epsilon<b
. Then, choosing a sufficiently small
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
and using Proposition
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{x}\cap[0,\epsilon]}|u|^{2}\,y^{c}\,dz\leq\epsilon^{2\alpha}\int_{Q_{x}\cap[0,\epsilon]}\frac{|u|^{2}}{y^{2\alpha}}\,y^{c}\,dz\leq\epsilon^{2\alpha}\frac{4b^{2-2\alpha}}{(c+1-2\alpha)^{2}}.

Since
L^{2}_{c}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]\right)=L^{2}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b],\,dz\right)
, the compactness of
\mathcal{B}_{|Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]}
in
L^{2}_{c}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]\right)
is classical. This fact and the above estimate show that
\mathcal{B}
is totally bounded.

We also prove, similarly, the compactness of the embedding
H_{c}^{1}\left(Q\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{c}\left(Q\right)
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
c+1>0
and let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
. Then for some positive constant
C>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}_{c}(Q)},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{c}(Q).

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,b[)
be a cut-off function such that
\eta(y)=1
, if
0\leq y\leq\frac{b}{2}
and let us set

\displaystyle u=\eta u+(1-\eta)u:=u_{1}+u_{2}.

Since
u_{1}\in H^{1,0}_{c}\left(Q\right)
, we can apply Proposition
2.2
thus obtaining for some positive constant
C>0
(which may be different in each occurrence)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u_{1}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|D_{y}u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}_{c}\left(Q\right)}.

On the other hand since
\mbox{supp}(u_{2})\subseteq\overline{Q_{x}}\times[\frac{b}{2},b]
, we have trivially

\left\|\frac{u_{2}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}=\left\|\frac{u_{2}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q_{x}\times(\frac{b}{2},b))}\leq\left(\frac{2}{b}\right)^{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}

which added to the previous inequality implies

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}_{c}(Q)}

which is the required claim.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
Q=Q_{x}\times(0,b)
where
Q_{x}
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
c+1>0
. Then the immersion
H_{c}^{1}\left(Q\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{c}\left(Q\right)
is compact.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
Let
u
be in the unit ball

\mathcal{B}
of
H_{c}^{1}\left(Q\right)
and fix
0<\epsilon<b
. Then, choosing a sufficiently small
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
and using Proposition
2.5
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{x}\cap[0,\epsilon]}|u|^{2}\,y^{c}\,dz\leq\epsilon^{2\alpha}\int_{Q_{x}\cap[0,\epsilon]}\frac{|u|^{2}}{y^{2\alpha}}\,y^{c}\,dz\leq C\epsilon^{2\alpha}.

Since
L^{2}_{c}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]\right)=L^{2}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b],\,dz\right)
and
H^{1}_{c}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]\right)=H^{1}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b],\,dz\right)
, the compactness of
\mathcal{B}_{|Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]}
in
L^{2}_{c}\left(Q_{x}\times[\epsilon,b]\right)
is classical. This fact and the above estimate show that
\mathcal{B}
is totally bounded.

We can then deduce a Poincaré type inequality for functions in
H^{1}_{c}(Q)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
c+1>0
and let
Q=Q_{x}\times(0,b)
where
Q_{x}
is a bounded open connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then, setting
\nu=y^{c}dz
, one has for some positive constant
C>0

\displaystyle\left\|u-\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{c}(Q),\quad\text{where}\quad\overline{u}=\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}u\,y^{c}dz.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of the standard proof of the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality (see e.g.
[
7
, Section 5.8, Theorem 1, page 275]
). We argue by contradiction and we assume that there exists a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H^{1}_{c}(Q)
such that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}>n\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}.

Up to replace
u_{n}
with
u_{n}-\overline{u}_{n}
, we can suppose
\overline{u}_{n}=0
. Furthermore, up to renormalize, we can assume, without any loss of generality,
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}=1
, which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}<\frac{1}{n}.

(3)

By Proposition
2.6
, there exists a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})\subseteq(u_{n})
which converges strongly to a function
u\in L^{2}_{c}(Q)
and weakly in
H^{1}_{c}(Q)
. The limit
u
then satisfies clearly
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}=1
and, using (
3
),
u\in H^{1}_{c}(Q)
and moreover
\nabla u=0
. This implies then
u
to be constant and, since
\overline{u}=0
, we must have
u=0
which contradicts
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c}(Q)}=1
.

3
Weighted Gaussian Poincaré inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

In this section we prove Rellich-Kondrachov type theorems and Poincaré type inequalities in weighted Gaussian spaces. We fix
c+1>0
and
a>0
and we consider the weighted Gaussian measure
\mu:=y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}dz

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
which, from the assumptions on

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. By standard computation one has

\displaystyle\mu\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}\,dz=a^{-\frac{N+1+c}{2}}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{c+1}{2}\right)

where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. Indeed

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-a|x|^{2}}\,dx\,\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{c}e^{-ay^{2}}\,dy

\displaystyle\overset{s=ay^{2}}{=}\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{c-1}{2}}e^{-s}\,dy\,a^{-\frac{c+1}{2}}=a^{-\frac{N+1+c}{2}}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{c+1}{2}\right).

We work in the space
L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}dz)
and we define the Sobolev space

H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):=\{u\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}):\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\}

equipped with the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}:=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}.

As in the previous section, condition
c+1>0
assures, by
[
14
, Lemma 11.1]
, that any function
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
has a finite trace
u_{0}=u(\cdot,0)
at
y=0
.

The next results show the density of smooth functions in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The set

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty)\right),\ D_{y}u(x,y)=0\ {\rm for}\ y\leq\delta\ {\rm and\ some\ }\delta>0\right\}

is dense in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.

Proof.

Let us prove preliminarily that functions in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
having compact support in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}
are dense in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. Let
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.
Let
0\leq\phi\leq 1
be a smooth function depending only on the
y
variable which is equal to
1
in
(0,1)
and to
0
for
y\geq 2
and set
\phi_{n}(y)=\phi\left(\frac{y}{n}\right)
. Similarly let
0\leq\eta\leq 1
be a smooth function depending only on the
x
variable which is equal to
1
if
|x|\leq 1
and to
0
for
|x|\geq 2
and set
\eta_{n}(x)=\phi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)
.
Let
u_{n}(x,y)=\eta_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y)u(x,y)
. Then
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
and has compact support in
B^{N}(0,2n)\times[0,2n]
where we set
B^{N}(0,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|\leq r\}
. By the dominated convergence theorem we have
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. Concerning the derivatives we observe that

\displaystyle D_{y}u_{n}=\eta_{n}\phi_{n}D_{y}u+D_{y}\phi_{n}\eta_{n}u,\qquad|D_{y}\phi_{n}|\leq\frac{C}{n}\chi_{[n,2n]}

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}u_{n}=\eta_{n}\phi_{n}\nabla_{x}u+\nabla_{x}\eta_{n}\phi_{n}u,\qquad|\nabla_{x}\eta_{n}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{n}\chi_{[n,2n]}(|x|)

which implies
D_{y}u_{n}\to D_{y}u
,
\nabla_{x}u_{n}\to\nabla_{x}u
in
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
by the dominated convergence theorem again.

Let us now prove that
\mathcal{C}
is dense in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.
Let
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. By the previous step we may assume that
u
has support in
B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b[
for some
b>0
. By
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
and Remark
2.1
there exists
u_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1}_{c}(B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b[)
. Since the measure
\mu
is equivalent to the measure
y^{c}dz
in
B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b[
we then have
H^{1}_{c}(B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b[)=H^{1}_{\mu}(B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b[)
. This proves the required claim.

Remark 3.2
.

As in Remark
2.1
, the above proof shows that if
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
has support in
B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b]
, then there exists a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mbox{supp }u_{n}\subseteq B^{N}(0,b)\times[0,b]
and
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. Moreover, in the range
c\geq 1
, by using
[
14
, Proposition 11.4]
in place of
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
, the same proof of Proposition
3.1
proves that
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
is dense in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})

We state now some weighted Hardy-type inequalities for functions in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. The following Proposition is an adaptation on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
of a classical result for Gaussian measure, see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 8.5.2]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For some positive constant
C>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|u|x|\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\nabla_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right),\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, we can assume, without any loss of generality,
u\in\mathcal{C}
. Integrating by parts one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}|x|^{2}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}|x|^{2}y^{c}e^{-a|z|^{2}}\,dydx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}\left(x,\nabla_{x}(e^{-a|z|^{2}})\right)\,y^{c}dydx=\frac{1}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}\mbox{div}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}x\right)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}u\left(\nabla_{x}u,x\right)\,d\mu(z)+\frac{N}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu(z).

Then using the Hölder and the Young inequalities, the previous equality implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}|x|^{2}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\left\|u|x|\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\left\|\nabla_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\frac{N}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{a}\left\|u|x|\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{2}+\frac{1}{a\epsilon}\left\|\nabla_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{2}+\frac{N}{2a}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}^{2}.

choosing
\frac{\epsilon}{a}=\frac{1}{2}
we obtain the required claim.

In the following Lemma we restrict ourselves to functions vanishing at the boundary
y=0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For some positive constant
C>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|uy\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right),\qquad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}).

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
. We integrate by parts thus obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}y^{2}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}y^{c+2}e^{-a|z|^{2}}\,dydx=-\frac{1}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}y^{c+1}D_{y}(e^{-a|z|^{2}})\,dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}uD_{y}u\,y\,d\mu(z)+\frac{c+1}{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu(z).

The conclusion then follows using the Hölder and the Young inequalities as in the previous proposition.

We now consider, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the weight

\displaystyle y\left(1\wedge y\right)^{-\alpha-1}=\begin{cases}y,\quad&y\geq 1,\\[4.30554pt]
y^{-\alpha},\quad&y<1,\end{cases},\qquad\forall y>0.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
c+1>0
and let
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
. Then for some positive constant
C>0
one has

\displaystyle\left\|uy\left(1\wedge y\right)^{-\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}).

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. By Proposition
3.1
and using a density argument, we can suppose without any loss of generality
u\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,1[)
be a cut-off function such that
\eta(y)=1
, if
0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{2}
and let us set

\displaystyle u=\eta u+(1-\eta)u:=u_{1}+u_{2}.

We focus, preliminarily, on
0\leq y\leq 1
. Since
u_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[\right)
, we can apply Proposition
2.2
thus obtaining

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u_{1}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{c+1-2\alpha}\left\|D_{y}u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}

which, observing that
e^{-a}\leq e^{-ay^{2}}\leq 1
for
y\in[0,1]
, implies

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u_{1}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2e^{a}}{c+1-2\alpha}\left\|D_{y}u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2e^{a}}{c+1-2\alpha}\left(\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right).

On the other hand since
u_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},+\infty[\right)
, we have trivially

\left\|\frac{u_{2}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}=\left\|\frac{u_{2}}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},1[)}\leq{2^{\alpha}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

which added to the previous inequality implies

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,1[)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right).

(5)

We now focus on
y\geq 1
and we argue similarly. We apply Lemma
3.4
to
u_{2}
thus obtaining

\displaystyle\left\|u_{2}y\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},+\infty[)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|D_{y}u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right)

Since we have trivially

\left\|u_{1}y\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},+\infty[)}=\left\|{u_{1}}y\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},1[)}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

summing up the previous inequalities yields

\displaystyle\left\|uy\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\frac{1}{2},+\infty[)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|D_{y}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\right).

(6)

Inequities (
5
) and (
6
) prove the required claim.

We can now prove a Rellich-Kondrachov type theorem which assures the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
c+1>0
. The immersion
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
is compact.

Proof.

We have to prove that the unit ball
\mathcal{B}
of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
is totally bounded in
L^{2}_{\mu}
. Let us fix
\epsilon>0
and let
r>1
to be chosen later. Let us also write
B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|\leq r\}
and
B_{r}^{c}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|>r\}
.

Let us consider, preliminarily,
\mathcal{B}_{|B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]}
. Since in
B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]
the measure
\mu
is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure then
L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)=L^{2}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r],\,dz\right)
and
H^{1}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)=H^{1}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r],\,dz\right)
: the compactness of
\mathcal{B}_{B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]}
in
L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)
is therefore classical and, consequently,
\mathcal{B}_{|B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{,}r,r]}
is totally bounded. Thus there exists a finite number of functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{|B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left\{f\in L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right):\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)}<\epsilon\right\}

Let us treat now
\mathcal{B}_{|\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)^{c}}
and let us fix, with this aim, a sufficiently small
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha<\frac{c+1}{2}
. By Propositions
3.3
and
3.5
we have

\displaystyle\left\|u|x|\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}+\left\|uy\left(1\wedge y\right)^{-\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}).

(7)

Let
u\in\mathcal{B}
. We distinguish the following cases.

(i)

Let us consider
\mathcal{B}_{|\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\frac{1}{r}]}
. Then by (
7
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\frac{1}{r}]\right)}\leq\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\|uy^{-\alpha}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\frac{1}{r}]\right)}=\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\|uy\left(1\wedge y\right)^{-\alpha-1}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\frac{1}{r}]\right)}\leq C\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}.

(ii)

Let us consider
\mathcal{B}_{|\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[r,+\infty[}
. Then similarly by (
7
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[r,+\infty[\right)}\leq\frac{1}{r}\|uy\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[r,+\infty[\right)}=\frac{1}{r}\|uy\left(1\wedge y\right)^{-\alpha-1}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[r,+\infty[\right)}\leq C\frac{1}{r}.

(iii)

Let us consider
\mathcal{B}_{|B_{r}^{c}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]}
. By (
7
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{r}^{c}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)}\leq\frac{1}{r}\|u|x|\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(B_{R}^{c}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)}\leq C\frac{1}{r}.

The above estimates show that, taking a sufficiently large
r
, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\left(B_{r}\times[\frac{1}{r},r]\right)^{c}\right)}\leq\epsilon

Therefore extending the functions
f_{i}
by zero to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left\{f\in L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right):\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)}<2\epsilon\right\}

which implies that
\mathcal{B}
is totally bounded in
L^{2}_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\right)
.

As in the classical theory, we can easily deduce, from the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
, a Poincaré-type inequality for functions in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
c+1>0
. Then one has for some positive constant
C>0

\displaystyle\left\|u-\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}),

where
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{\mu(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}u\,d\mu(z)
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one of Proposition
2.7
. We sketch only the main steps. Up to replace
u
with
u-\overline{u}
, we can suppose
\overline{u}=0
. Then arguing by contradiction, we can assume that there exists a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}=1,\qquad\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}<\frac{1}{n}.

(8)

By Theorem
3.6
, there exists a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})\subseteq(u_{n})
which converges strongly to a function
u\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
and weakly in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
. The limit
u
satisfies, clearly,
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}=1
and, using (
8
), we can also deduce that
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
and
\nabla u=0
which implies
u
to be constant. Since
\overline{u}=0
, we must have
u=0
which contradicts
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}=1
.

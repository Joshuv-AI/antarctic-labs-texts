Observability for the Schrödinger Equation: an Optimal Transportation Approach
François Golse, Thierry Paul

Abstract
. We establish an observation inequality for the Schrödinger equation on 𝐑 d \mathbf{R}^{d} , uniform in the Planck constant ℏ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \hbar\in[0,1] . The proof is based on the pseudometric introduced in [F. Golse, T. Paul, Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. 223 (2017), 57–94]. This inequality involves only effective constants which are computed explicitly in their dependence in ℏ \hbar and all parameters involved.

Observation inequality for the Schrödinger equation

Consider the Schrödinger equation where the (real-valued) potential
V
belongs to
C^{1,1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is such that the quantum Hamiltonian

-\tfrac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+V(y)

has a self-adjoint extension on

(1)

i\hbar{\partial}_{t}\psi(t,y)=\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+V(y)\right)\psi(t,y)\,,\qquad\psi{\big|}_{t=0}=\psi^{in}\,.

In the equation above,
\hbar>0
the reduced Planck constant, and the particle mass is set to
1

An observation inequality for the Schrödinger equation (
1

\|\psi^{in}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dxdt\,,

\mathbf{R}^{d}

Therefore we will suppose in the sequel

We will say that a compact subset

2.
A pseudometric for comparing classical and quantum densities

This section elaborates on
[

]

A density operator on
\mathfrak{H}
is an operator
R\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
such that

R=R^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\operatorname{trace}(R)=1\,.

The set of all density operators on
\mathfrak{H}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
the set of density operators on
\mathfrak{H}
such that

(8)

\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}(-\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+|y|^{2})R^{1/2})<\infty\,.

If
R\in\mathcal{D}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has

(9)

\operatorname{trace}((-\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+|y|^{2})^{1/2}R(-\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+|y|^{2})^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}(-\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}+|y|^{2})R^{1/2})<\infty

as can be seen from the lemma below (applied to
A={\lambda}^{2}|y|^{2}-\hbar^{2}{\Delta}_{y}
and
T=R
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfy
T=T^{*}\geq 0
, and let
A
be an unbounded operator on
\mathfrak{H}
such that
A=A^{*}\geq 0
. Then

\operatorname{trace}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}(A^{1/2}RA^{1/2})\in[0,+\infty]\,.

Proof.

The definition of
T^{1/2}
and
A^{1/2}
can be found in Theorem 3.35 in chapter V, §3 of
[
8
]
, together with the fact that
A^{1/2}
and
T^{1/2}
are self-adjoint.

If
\operatorname{trace}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})<\infty
, then
A^{1/2}T^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and the equality holds by formula (1.26) in chapter X, §1 of
[
8
]
. If
\operatorname{trace}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\infty
, then
\operatorname{trace}(A^{1/2}TA^{1/2})=+\infty
, for otherwise
T^{1/2}A^{1/2}

and its adjoint
A^{1/2}T^{1/2}
would belong to
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
, so that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
, which would be in contradiction with the assumption that
\operatorname{trace}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\infty
.
∎

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi

3.
Evolution of the pseudo-metric under the Schrödinger dynamics

Denote by
t\mapsto(X(t;x,\xi),\Xi(t;x,\xi))
the solution of the Cauchy problem for the Hamiltonian system

\dot{X}=\Xi\,,\quad\dot{\Xi}=-{\nabla}V(X)\,,\qquad(X(0;x,\xi),\Xi(0;x,\xi))=(x,\xi)\,.

Since
V\in C^{1,1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, this solution is defined for all
t\in\mathbf{R}
, for all
x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Henceforth, we denote by
\Phi_{t}
the map
(x,\xi)\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x,\xi):=(X(t;x,\xi),\Xi(t;x,\xi))
, and by
H\equiv H(x,\xi):=\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+V(x)

the Hamiltonian.

On the other hand, assume that
V^{-}\in L^{d/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, so that
\mathcal{H}:=-\tfrac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}{\Delta}+V
is self-adjoint on
\mathfrak{H}
by Lemma 4.8b in chapter VI, §4 of
[
8
]
. Then
U(t):=\exp(it\mathcal{H}/\hbar)
is a unitary group on
\mathfrak{H}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f^{in}
be a probability density on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
which satisfies (
10
), and let
R^{in}\in\mathcal{D}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
. For each
t\geq 0
, set

R(t):=U(t)^{*}R^{in}U(t)\,,\quad f(t,X,\Xi):=f^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(X,\Xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

Then, for each
{\lambda}>0
and each

, one has

E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f(t,\cdot,\cdot),R(t))\leq E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f^{in},R^{in})\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}t\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\,.

This theorem is a slight improvement of Theorem 2.7 in
[
6
]
in the special case
N=1
. For the sake of being complete, we recall the argument in
[
6
]
, with the appropriate modifications.

Proof.

Let
Q^{in}\in\mathcal{C}(f^{in},R^{in})
. Set

Q(t,X,\Xi):=U(t)^{*}Q^{in}\circ\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)U(t)

for all
t\in\mathbf{R}
and a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, and

\mathcal{E}(t):=\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(t,X,\Xi)^{1/2}c_{\lambda}(X,\Xi,y,\hbar D_{y})Q(t,X,\Xi)^{1/2})dXd\Xi\,.

Since
\Phi_{t}
leaves the phase space volume element
dxd\xi
invariant

\mathcal{E}(t)\!=\!\!\!\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\!\!\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)}U(t)c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})U(t)^{*}\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)})dx\xi\,.

By construction,
Q(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}(f(t,\cdot,\cdot),R(t))
. Indeed, for a.e.
(X,\Xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
,

0\leq Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))=Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})

so that
Q(t,X,\Xi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfies

\displaystyle Q(t,X,\Xi)=

\displaystyle U(t)Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))U(t)^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U(t)Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))U(t)^{*}=Q(t,X,\Xi)^{*}\geq 0\,.

Besides

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(t,X,\Xi))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)))=f^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))=f(t,X,\Xi)

while

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}Q(t,X,\Xi)dXd\Xi=U(t)\left(\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}Q^{in}(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi))dXd\Xi\right)U(t)^{*}

\displaystyle=U(t)\left(\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}Q^{in}(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right)U(t)^{*}=U(t)R^{in}U(t)^{*}=R(t)

\displaystyle.

In particular

\mathcal{E}(t)\geq E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f(t),R(t))\,,\qquad\hbox{ for each }t\geq 0\,.

Let
e_{j}(x,\xi,\cdot)
for
j\in\mathbf{N}
be a
\mathfrak{H}
-complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
Q^{in}(x,\xi)
for a.e.
x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)}U(t)c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})U(t)^{*}\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\rho_{j}(x,\xi)\langle U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)\rangle

where
\rho_{j}(x,\xi)
is the eigenvalue of
Q^{in}(x,\xi)
defined by

Q^{in}(x,\xi)e_{j}(x,\xi)=\rho_{j}(x,\xi)e_{j}(x,\xi)\,,\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

If
\phi\equiv\phi(y)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, the map

t\mapsto\langle U(t)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)\phi\rangle

is of class
C^{1}
on
\mathbf{R}
, and one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle U(t)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathcal{H}U(t)\phi\Big|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})\Big|U(t)\phi\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle+\bigg\langle U(t)\phi\Big|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})\Big|\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathcal{H}U(t)\phi\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle U(t)\phi|\{H(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)),c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})\}|U(t)\phi\rangle

\displaystyle.

In other words

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle U(t)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle U(t)\phi\Big|\frac{i}{\hbar}[\mathcal{H},c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})]\Big|U(t)\phi\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle U(t)\phi|\{H(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)),c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})\}|U(t)\phi\rangle

\displaystyle.

A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\{H(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)),c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})\}+\frac{i}{\hbar}[\mathcal{H},c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})]

\displaystyle={\lambda}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left((X_{k}-y_{k})(\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}})+(\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}})(X_{k}-y_{k})\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(({\partial}_{k}V(X)-{\partial}_{k}V(y))(\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}})+(\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}})({\partial}_{k}V(X)-{\partial}_{k}V(y))\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{d}({\lambda}^{2}|X_{k}\!-\!y_{k}|^{2}\!+\!|\Xi_{k}\!-\!\hbar D_{y_{k}}|^{2})\!+\!\frac{1}{{\lambda}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left({\lambda}^{2}|{\partial}_{k}V(X)\!-\!{\partial}_{k}V(y)|^{2}\!+\!|\Xi_{k}\!-\!\hbar D_{y_{k}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{d}({\lambda}^{2}|X_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}+|\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}}|^{2})+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left({\lambda}^{2}|X_{k}-y|^{2}+|\Xi_{k}-\hbar D_{y_{k}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)c_{\lambda}(X,\Xi,y,\hbar D_{y})

\displaystyle.

Hence

\displaystyle\langle U(t)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)\phi\rangle\leq\langle\phi|c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle+\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\langle U(s)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{s}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(s)\phi\rangle ds

so that

\langle U(t)\phi|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)\phi\rangle\leq\langle\phi|c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})|\phi\rangle\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)

for each
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. By density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
in the form domain of
c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle e_{j}(x,\xi)|c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})|e_{j}(x,\xi)\rangle\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)

for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)}U(t)c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})U(t)^{*}\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\rho_{j}(x,\xi)\langle U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)|c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi),y,\hbar D_{y})|U(t)e_{j}(x,\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\sum_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\rho_{j}(x,\xi)\langle e_{j}(x,\xi)|c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})|e_{j}(x,\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)}c_{\lambda}(x,\xi,y,\hbar D_{y})\sqrt{Q^{in}(x,\xi)})

\displaystyle.

Integrating both side of this inequality over
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
shows that

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\,.

Hence, for each
t\geq 0
and each
Q^{in}\in\mathcal{C}(f,R)
, one has

E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f(t),R(t))^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\exp\left(\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\,.

Minimizing the right hand side of this inequality as
Q^{in}
runs through
\mathcal{C}(f^{in},R^{in})
, one arrives at the inequality

E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f(t),R(t))\leq E_{\hbar,{\lambda}}(f^{in},R^{in})\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left({\lambda}+\frac{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}{{\lambda}}\right)t\right)\,.

∎

4.
The observation inequality

In this section, we state and prove an observation inequality for the Schrödinger equation.

Let

be a compact subset of

, let
{\Omega}
be an open set of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
and let

. We recall the “geometric condition” à la Bardos-Lebeau-Rauch
[

]
for this problem:

(GC)

\hbox{for each }(x,\xi)\in K\,,\hbox{ there exists }t\in(0,T)\hbox{ s.t. }X(t;x,\xi)\in{\Omega}\,.

.

be such that

for each

.

For each

, set

R^{in}
is a pure state

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\chi(x)|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx)dt\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{(x,\xi)\in K}\int_{0}^{T}\chi(X(t;x,\xi))dt\iint_{(x,\xi)\in K}\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}[\psi^{in}](x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle-4{\operatorname{Lip}(\chi)}\frac{\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}\right)T\right)-1}{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}\right)}\Delta(\psi^{in}).

on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Lip}(\chi)C(T,\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V))\sqrt{2d\hbar}

In particular, setting

In fact, one can eliminate all mention of the cutoff function

in the final statement, as follows.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
4.1
, one has

C[T,K,{\Omega}]:=\inf_{(x,\xi)\in K}\int_{0}^{T}{\bf 1}_{{\Omega}}(X(t;x,\xi))dt>0\,,

and for each

, denoting

in the Töplitz case, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx)dt\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{(x,\xi)\in K}\int_{0}^{T}{\bf 1}_{\Omega}(X(t;x,\xi))dt\iint_{(x,\xi)\in K}\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}[\psi^{in}](x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle-4\frac{\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}\right)T\right)-1}{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+{\operatorname{Lip}({\nabla}V)^{2}}\right)}\frac{\Delta(\psi^{in})}{\delta}

in the pure state case.

The corollary can be used to obtain an observation inequality for Töplitz operators as “test observables” as follows: let
T>0
be an observation time, let

be a compact subset of the phase-space supporting the initial data, and let

be the open set where one observes the solution of the Schrödinger equation on the time interval

7

for all Töplitz initial density operators whose symbol is supported in

{\Omega}

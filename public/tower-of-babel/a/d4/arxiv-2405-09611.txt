Fermionic quantum criticality through the lens of topological holography
Sheng-Jie Huang

Abstract
We utilize the topological holographic framework to characterize and gain insights into the nature of quantum critical points and gapless phases in fermionic quantum systems. Topological holography is a general framework that describes the generalized global symmetry and the symmetry charges of a local quantum system in terms of a slab of a topological order, termed as the symmetry topological field theory (SymTFT), in one higher dimension. In this work, we consider a generalization of the topological holographic picture for (1+1) d d fermionic quantum phases of matter. We discuss how spin structures are encoded in the SymTFT and establish the connection between the formal fermionization formula in quantum field theory and the choice of fermionic gapped boundary conditions of the SymTFT. We demonstrate the identification and the characterization of the fermionic gapped phases and phase transitions through detailed analysis of various examples, including the fermionic systems with ℤ 2 F \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F} , ℤ 2 × ℤ 2 F \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F} , ℤ 4 F \mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F} , and the fermionic version of the non-invertible Rep ​ ( S 3 ) \text{Rep}(S_{3}) symmetry. Our work uncovers many exotic fermionic quantum critical points and gapless phases, including two kinds of fermionic symmetry enriched quantum critical points, a fermionic gapless symmetry protected topological (SPT) phase, and a fermionic gapless spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) phase that breaks the fermionic non-invertible symmetry.

II
Review: topological holography for bosonic systems

Figure 1:
The sandwich picture in the topological holography. A (1+1)
d
theory can be viewed as a sandwich built from a topological order
\mathcal{T}
in the bulk with a topological gapped boundary conditions on the left and a potentially non-topological boundary condition on the right.

Here we review the basic idea of the topological holography for bosonic systems. The essential idea of the topological holography is to encode the symmetry data and the corresponding symmetry charges of a quantum phase of matter into a

sandwich
, which is a topological order
\mathcal{T}
defined on a slab with appropriate boundary conditions. One of the main benefits of this approach is to separate the topological data from the dynamical contents in the theory of interest. Fig.
1
illustrates the main idea. The bulk of the slab is a topological order that support topological gapped boundary conditions, which will be called a
SymTFT
. The left boundary is chosen to be a topological gapped boundary of the topological order SymTFT, on which some bosonic anyons condensed. The confined topological defect lines on the left topological boundary encodes all the topological data of the symmetry in the bosonic systems. We will thus call the topological boundary on the left of the sandwich the
symmetry boundary
. Fixing the symmetry boundary, different choices of the right boundary result in different bosonic quantum phases. We will refer the right boundary as the
physical boundary
. We note that the right boundary could be non-topological, such as a CFT. Different phases can be analysed on the same footing only when we fix a choice of the symmetry boundary.

Sandwich

Focusing the discussion on the (1+1)
d
bosonic systems, consider a (2+1)
d
SymTFT on an open 3D manifold
M\times I
, where
I=[0,1]
is a 1D interval

In this work, we work in Euclidean signature.

M
is a closed surface. We take
M\times\{0\}
as the symmetry boundary, and
M\times\{1\}
as the physical boundary. The partition function on this (2+1)
d

Z=\braket{\mathcal{A}|\Psi},

(1)

where
|\mathcal{A}\rangle
is the boundary state on the symmetry boundary, which is topological, and
|\Psi\rangle
is the boundary state on the physical boundary. When the surface

is not a sphere (i.e. nonzero genus), the Hilbert space of the bulk topological theory on

can be obtained as the basis of the Hilbert space on the solid torus
D^{2}\times S^{1}

D^{2}
is a disk, with an insertion of anyon
\alpha

S^{1}

The physical boundary state
|\Psi\rangle

|\alpha\rangle

|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{T}}Z_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle.

When

\alpha

Gapped boundaries of a (2+1)

\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{T}}w_{\alpha}\alpha

Physically, the Lagrangian algebra describes condensation of anyons on the boundary: if

In the anyon basis, the partition function of the (1+1)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{T}}w_{\beta}Z_{\alpha}\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{T}}w_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}.

(4)

Therefore, the partition function of the (1+1)
d

\mathcal{T}
on the solid torus with an insertion of of Lagrangian algebra
\mathcal{A}
around

Symmetries and operators

In the sandwich construction, the topological defect lines confined on the left symmetry boundary implement the symmetry of our system. In general, the topological defect lines are described by a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. The anyons in the bulk SymTFT are then described by a modular tensor category called the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.

Local operators can be organized according to their transformations under the fusion category symmetry. As a result, a local operator can be uniquely attached a bulk anyon label
\alpha
with
w_{\alpha}>0
. Intuitively, the local operator
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
corresponds to a bulk anyon line
\alpha
that condenses on the left topological boundary, and stretches across the sandwich.

The symmetry
\mathcal{C}
acts on the local operator as the
half braiding
between the bulk anyon line
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
and the boundary topological line
m\in\mathcal{C}

[
69
,
70
]
. For abelian SymTFT, it’s usually convenient to move the boundary symmetry line into the bulk and the symmetry charges are encoded in the bulk anyon braiding phases.

Phases

Topological holography provides a unified framework to describe gapped and gapless phases. Once the symmetry boundary is fixed with a fusion category symmetry
\mathcal{C}
, different choices of the physical boundary realize different phases of matter. If the physical boundary is also a topological gapped boundary, the sandwich construction realizes a gapped phase in bosonic systems with the symmetry
\mathcal{C}
. If we choose the physical boundary to be gapless, such as a CFT, the sandwich construction realizes a critical point or a gapless phase with the fusion category symmetry
\mathcal{C}
. Such gapless boundaries are also characterized by condensable algebras of the form
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathcal{T}}w_{\beta}\beta

[
49
]
, which satisfy the conditions listed in Appendix
A
except the Lagrangian condition.

Dualities

Topological holography provides a powerful way to systematically understand dualities between (1+1)
d
theories. Given a bulk SymTFT
\mathcal{T}
, in general there can be automorphisms that permutes the bulk anyons:

\rho:\alpha\rightarrow\rho(\alpha).

(5)

Each of this automorphism is an anyonic symmetry of the SymTFT and corresponds to an invertible codimension-1 twist defect
\mathcal{D}
in the bulk, such that a bulk anyon is permuted when it passing through the defect. In the sandwich construction, we can insert this twist defect parallel to the sandwich, giving rise to a new (1+1)
d
phase.

More specifically, consider two topological boundary states
|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle
and
|\mathcal{L}_{2}\rangle
on the physical boundary. The corresponding partition functions are given by
Z_{1}=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{L}_{2}\rangle
, where
\langle\mathcal{A}|
is the topological boundary state on the symmetry boundary. We say that there is a duality between these two gapped phases, if there exist a twist defect
\mathcal{D}
, such that
\mathcal{D}|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{2}\rangle
. Written in terms of the partition functions, this means that
Z_{2}=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{L}_{2}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{D}|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle
.

We can also consider dualities between two critical theories. For two critical boundaries
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
related by the twist defect of this kind:
\mathcal{D}|\Psi_{1}\rangle=|\Psi_{2}\rangle
, there is a duality between the partition functions of these two critical theories:

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}|\Psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{D}|\Psi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|\Psi_{1}\rangle,

(6)

where
\langle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|=\langle\mathcal{A}|\mathcal{D}
. This shows that the partition function of theory
2
is given by the sandwich of theory
1
but with a different choice of the symmetry boundary. We can thus use this relation to write down the partition function for theory
2
by using the characters of theory
1
. This will be the main strategy of this work.

In general, even for any two topological gapped boundaries
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
not related by an anyonic symmetry, there is a corresponding duality between the two bosonic quantum systems, simply given by changing the topological gapped boundary condition on the symmetry boundary. This class of dualities is more general since the fusion category symmetries corresponding to the gapped boundaries
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
might not be equivalent in the sense of fusion categories. More formally, two quantum systems with fusion category symmetries
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are dual to each other in this generalized sense if
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{S})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

IV.1
Gapped phases

If we choose both symmetry and physical boundaries to be gapped boundary conditions, the sandwich construction realizes
(1+1)d
gapped phases with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermion parity symmetry. There are only two possibilities: the trivial and the Kitaev phase. If the physical boundary is the
m
-condensed boundary, it corresponds to the trivial phase. This can be derived from the fact that the partition functions with different spin structures are all equal to
1
by using Eq. (
13
)-(
16

The Kitaev chain corresponds to choosing the physical boundary to be the
e
-condensed boundary. One can check that the partition function in the
(P,P)
spin structure is given by

Z[\text{P},\text{P}]=\frac{S_{em}}{S_{0e}}\langle e|e\rangle=-1

(23)

where we have used Eq. (
20
). The other partition functions are all equal to
1
. Physically, this is the statement that the ground state of a Kitaev chain carries an odd fermion parity with a periodic boundary condition. Note that this identification of the phases is tied to the choice of the symmetry line of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermion parity being the
m
anyon line in the bulk SymTFT. If we choose
e
to be the bulk representative of the fermion parity symmetry, the identification of the gapped phases will reverse.

We note that our choice is consistent with the Jordan–Wigner transformation. The Jordan–Wigner transformation maps the trivial phase of an Ising spin chain to the trivial phase of the fermionic chain, and maps the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase to the Kitave phase. In the sandwich picture of bosonic systems with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, we choose the
e
condensed boundary as our symmetry boundary on the left. It follows that choosing the right boundary to be the
m
condensed boundary produces the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
trivial phase and choosing the
e
condensed boundary on the right produces the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phases. Upon performing the fermionization, we change the symmetry boundary on the left to be the
f
condensed boundary and fix
m
line to be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry generator. From the above discussion, we see that the fermionization in the SymTFT framework indeed maps
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
trivial phase to the trivial phase of the fermionic chain and maps the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase to the Kitaev phase.

V.2.3
\text{Trivial}\leftrightarrow\text{GW}=\text{Majorana}^{2}

Here we consider the transition between the trivial and the Gu-Wen SPT phases. This critical point is realized by tuning the physical boundary of the sandwich to be at the critical point between the gapped boundaries
\mathcal{L}_{5}
and
\mathcal{L}_{6}

\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}

Z[{\text{A},\text{A}}]=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{5,6}\rangle

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{s}

\mathcal{D}|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{6}\rangle,\ \mathcal{D}|\mathcal{L}_{6}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{5}\rangle.

|\psi_{5,6}\rangle=\mathcal{D}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle.

\displaystyle Z[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{5,6}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\mathcal{D}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{3}^{f}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z_{1}^{2}+2Z_{1}Z_{f}+Z_{f}^{2}

\displaystyle=(|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}+\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1})^{2},

\text{Kitaev}\leftrightarrow\text{SSB}=\text{Ising}\times\text{Majorana}

\mathcal{L}_{2}

\mathcal{L}_{4}

|\psi_{2,4}\rangle

|\psi_{1,6}\rangle

\text{Ising}^{2}

\mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{1}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{s}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle=|\psi_{2,4}\rangle

\displaystyle Z[\text{A},\text{A}]

V.2.5
\text{Trivial}\leftrightarrow\text{Kitaev}=\text{Majorana}

Thus far, the transitions discussed in this section are based on the input boundary transition between
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{6}
, which is the
\text{Ising}^{2}
CFT. Here we demonstrate that it’s possible to obtain the other phase transitions by using different input boundary transitions.

We consider the transition between the Trivial and the Kitaev phases. From Table.
3
, we see that this transition sits between the gapped boundary
\mathcal{L}_{2}
and
\mathcal{L}_{6}
. We now discuss a possible boundary phase critical theory as our input. We note that
\mathcal{L}_{2}
and
\mathcal{L}_{6}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric phase respectively if we choose the symmetry boundary to be
\mathcal{L}_{1}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry is broken, we can view the system as two layers of toric code sandwich. The transition described by the Ising CFT only happens at the physical boundary for second layer, and the first layer stays in the m-condensed gapped boundary. The input CFT boundary state on the physical boundary of the sandwich then takes the following form:

|\psi_{26}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\text{TC}}v_{\alpha}Z_{\beta}|\alpha\beta\rangle,

(52)

where TC is the set of anyons in the toric code (we always order the anyons as
(1,e,m,f)

v_{\alpha}=(1,0,1,0)
describes the condensed anyons (
1
and
m

Z_{\beta}
is the Ising partition function in the anyon sector
\beta\in
TC for the second copy. If we calculate the partition function for the bosonic system as the overlap
Z=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}|\psi_{26}\rangle
, it gives the partition function of the Ising CFT.

Now we perform the fermionization by choosing the symmetry boundary to be
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}
and calculate the overlap:

\displaystyle Z[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{26}\rangle

\displaystyle=v_{1}Z_{1}+v_{e}Z_{e}+v_{m}Z_{f}+v_{f}Z_{m}

\displaystyle=Z_{1}+Z_{f}

\displaystyle=|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}+\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1},

(53)

where we have used
v_{\alpha}=(1,0,1,0)
in the third equality. This is precisely the partition function of the free Majorana theory.

VI
Fermionic symmetry enriched quantum critical points

Figure 5:
The sandwich pictures for two different types of the fermionic SEQCPs. (a) The first type of fermionic SEQCPs is obtain from the stacking of the usual SSB transitions with a SPT state. It corresponds to an insertion of a SPT twist defect
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{T}}
in the sandwich picture with the symmetry boundary that can “absorb” the SPT defect. (b) The second type of fermionic SEQCPs is obtain from the a twisted gauging

\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{S}}

Fermionic symmetry enriched quantum critical points (SEQCPs) are quantum critical points generally happens at the transition between a SSB phase, where the bosonic symmetry is completely broken, and a fermionic SPT phase. An important property of these quantum critical points is that the untwisted partition functions are the same as the partition function of the transition between the SSB and the trivial symmetric phases. However, the twisted partition functions differ from the usual SSB transitions and encode the SPT invariants.

We found that there are two different types of the fermionic SEQCPs. The first type of fermionic SEQCPs can be understood as the stacking of the usual SSB transitions with a SPT state (an operation we label as
T
). These kind of the SEQCPs are also called the gapless SPT phases
[
72
,
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
]
. In general, we expect the following form of the partition function:

\displaystyle Z^{\text{SEQCP}}[g,h]=Z^{\text{SSB}}[g,h]Z^{\text{SPT}}[g,h],

(54)

where
Z^{\text{SPT}}[g,h]
is the SPT invariant and
Z^{\text{SSB}}[g,h]
is the partition function of the symmetry breaking transition. In the sandwich picture, it corresponds to a SymTFT with an insertion of a SPT twist defect
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{T}}
and, in particular, the symmetry boundary can “absorb” the defect
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{T}}
, as illustrated in Fig.
5
(a).

The other type of the fermionic SEQCPs is obtained from the usual SSB transition by a twisted gauging
TS
, where
S
is understood as gauging a finite subgroup in the fermionic symmetry group
G_{f}
. In the sandwich picture, it corresponds to an insertion of a twist defect
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{TS}}=\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{T}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{S}}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{S}}
is a defect that implements the
S
gauging and
\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{T}}
is a SPT twist defect, as shown in Fig.
5
(b). We will present examples of both types of the fermionic SEQCPs in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermoinic systems in the following sections.

VI.1
\text{GW}\leftrightarrow(\text{SSB}+\text{Kitaev})=\text{Ising}^{2*}\bf{/Z_{2}^{D}}

Our first example of the symmetry enriched quantum critical point is the transition between the Gu-Wen and the “SSB+Kitaev” phases. We tune the physical boundary of the sandwich to be at the critical point between the gapped boundaries
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{5}
. We denote the corresponding boundary state as
|\psi_{1,5}\rangle
and the partition function of the transition is given by the overlap:

Z^{\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{1,5}\rangle.

(55)

Now consider a twist defect
\mathcal{D}_{s}
obtained from gauging the non-trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SPT state, which implements the anyon permutations shown in Eq. (
30
). The untwisted partition function (in the (A,A) spin structure) of the symmetry enriched CFT can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{1,5}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\mathcal{D}_{s}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{1,6}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z^{\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[\text{A},\text{A}],

(56)

where we have used the fact that
\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\mathcal{D}_{s}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|
in the third equality. We see that the untwisted partition function are the same as the
\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}
CFT, which describes the transition between the trivial and the “SSB+Kitaev” phases.

Now we would like to show that the twisted sectors of the
\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}
theory is different from the
\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}
CFT. We begin with the twisted partition function of the
\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}
CFT with both spatial and temporal twists:

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[(\text{A},\text{P});(1,0)]

\displaystyle=\langle m_{1}\times\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|W_{m_{1}m_{2}}|\psi_{16}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z_{m_{1}}+Z_{f_{1}e_{2}}-Z_{f_{2}}-Z_{e_{1}m_{2}}

\displaystyle=(|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2})|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}+(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1})|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-(|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2})(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1})-|\chi_{\sigma}|^{4}.

(57)

While, in the symmetry enriched CFT
\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}
, the partition function with the same twists is given by

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[(\text{A},\text{P});(1,0)]

\displaystyle=\langle m_{1}\times\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|W_{m_{1}m_{2}}|\psi_{15}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m_{1}\times\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|W_{m_{1}m_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{s}|\psi_{16}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m_{1}e_{2}\times\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|W_{f_{1}f_{2}}|\psi_{16}\rangle

\displaystyle=-Z_{m_{2}}-Z_{e_{1}f_{2}}+Z_{f_{1}}+Z_{m_{1}e_{2}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=-((|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2})|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}+(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1})|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}\\
&\qquad-(|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2})(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}+\chi_{\psi}\overline{\chi}_{1})-|\chi_{\sigma}|^{4})\end{split}

\displaystyle=-Z^{\text{Ising}^{2}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[(\text{A},\text{P});(1,0)],

(58)

where, in the third equality, we push the twist defect
\mathcal{D}_{s}
into the left boundary, which implements the corresponding anyon permutation for the spatial and temporal twists. We see that the twist partition functions Eq. (
58
) and Eq. (
57
) differ by a minus sign, which comes from the SPT invariant of the Gu-Wen state. Physically, it means that the non-trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
flux carries an additional fermion. Similarly, one can show that the fermion parity flux is decorated by a non-trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charge. The twisted partition function takes the following form:

Z^{\text{Ising}^{2*}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[\rho;(g,h)]=Z^{\text{Ising}\bm{/Z_{2}^{D}}}[\rho;(g,h)]Z^{\text{GW}}[\rho;(g,h)],

(59)

where
Z^{GW}[\rho;(g,h)]
is the partition function of the Gu-Wen fermionic SPT phase. This gives an example of the first type of the fermionic SEQCPs.

VI.2
\text{GW}\leftrightarrow\text{SSB}=\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}

Here we discuss another example of the symmetry enriched quantum critical points. We consider the transition between the Gu-Wen and the SSB phases. In the sandwich picture, the physical boundary is tuned to be sitting at the transition between the gapped boundaries
\mathcal{L}_{4}
and
\mathcal{L}_{5}

|\psi_{45}\rangle

Z^{\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{45}\rangle,

(60)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}
denotes the theory of the Gu-Wen SPT phase. As we will show below, this symmetry enriched critical theory carries the topological responses coming from the Gu-Wen phase. We refer to this symmetry enriched critical theory as
\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}
with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry in the Ising and
\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}

To proceed, we consider the duality implemented by the defect
\mathcal{D}_{s}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{D}}

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{1}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{s}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{L}_{1}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{4}\rangle,\ \tilde{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{L}_{2}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{6}\rangle.

(61)

We can now pull the defect
\mathcal{D}_{s}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
from the right physical boundary and write the partition function Eq. (
60

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{45}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\mathcal{D}_{s}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|\psi_{12}\rangle.

(62)

Using the fact that the symmetry boundary
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}
can absorb the gauged SPT defect
\mathcal{D}_{s}
and action of the defect
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}

\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{5}^{f}|,

(63)

the partition function becomes

Z^{\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]=\langle\mathcal{L}_{5}^{f}|\psi_{12}\rangle.

(64)

Now we need to know the boundary state
|\psi_{12}\rangle

1
that, on the bosonic side, this is the critical point between the partially
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
ordered phase and a completely
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry is broken spontaneously during the transition. Therefore, if we view the system as two layers of which each layer has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, we expect that the transition only happens in the first layer and it is described by the Ising CFT. The second layer remains in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase during the transition.

Upon making this assumption, we can write the boundary state
|\psi_{12}\rangle
as

|\psi_{12}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\text{TC}}Z_{\alpha}u_{\beta}|\alpha\beta\rangle,

(65)

where TC is the set of anyons in the toric code,
Z_{\alpha}
is the Ising partition function in the anyon sector
\alpha\in
TC for the first layer, and
u_{\beta}=(1,1,0,0)
describes the condensed anyons (
1
and
e
) on the right physics boundary for the second layer. By taking the overlap
\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}|\psi_{12}\rangle
, we recover the partition function of the Ising CFT stacking with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB gapped sector:

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{B}}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}|\psi_{12}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z_{1}u_{1}+Z_{e}u_{1}+Z_{1}u_{e}+Z_{e}u_{e}

\displaystyle=2(|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}+|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}),

(66)

where we have used
u_{1}=u_{e}=1
, and Eq. (
25
) in the second equality, which is also called a gapless SSB phase
[
68
]
.

Using the boundary state Eq. (
65
), we obtain the untwisted partition function for the Gu-Wen to SSB transition by calulating the overlap Eq. (
64

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{5}^{f}|\psi_{12}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z_{1}u_{1}+Z_{e}u_{1}+Z_{1}u_{f}+Z_{e}u_{f}

\displaystyle=|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}+|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2},

where we have used
u_{1}=1
,
u_{f}=0
, and Eq. (
25

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry is broken spontaneously down to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
, we expect that the transition is described by an Ising CFT, which is consistent with Eq. (
67

\displaystyle Z^{\text{Ising}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{0}}}[\text{A},\text{A}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\psi_{46}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|\psi_{12}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{5}^{f}|\psi_{12}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\chi_{1}|^{2}+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}+|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}.

(68)

We note that Eq. (

\mathcal{H}^{D+1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(1))

D-1
spatial dimensions with non-anomalous
\Gamma

An simple example is given by the
(1+1)d
igSPT phases with
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{4}
,
H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The non-trivial igSPT phase realized in the
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{4}
bosonic lattice model shares the same (emergent) anomaly as the gapless edge modes of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Levin-Gu SPT state.

The sandwich picture of the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
igSPT phase is given by the club sandwich construction as discussed in Ref.
[
48
,
55
]
. We have a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
toric code topological order on the left and a double semion (DS) order on the right. The two topological orders are separated by a gapped domain wall on which the
e^{2}m^{2}
particle in the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
toric code condenses. We will denote the domain wall by
\mathcal{I}_{e^{2}m^{2}}

e^{2}m^{2}
condensation, the deconfined anyons in the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
toric code are
\{[1],[em],[em^{3}],[m^{2}]\}
, which can be identified with the anyons in the double semion order
\{1,s,\bar{s},b\}
, respectively. For the bosonic
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
igSPT phase, the symmetry boundary is chosen to be the charge condensed boundary
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

Since
H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is non-anomalous, we can perform the fermionization with respecting the
H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
subgroup. This corresponds to change the symmetry boundary to be the fermionic gapped boundary
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}
in the club sandwich construction and the global symmetry becomes
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F}
.

On the physical boundary of the club sandwich, we have an edge theory of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Levin-Gu SPT state, which can live at the edge of the DS order. Upon dimensional reduction, the sandwich construction realizes the igSPT state where the low-energy theory realizes the anomalous edge theory of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Levin-Gu SPT state in a system with a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F}
global symmetry.

As a concrete example, we can choose the physical boundary to be the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{2}
CFT
[
66
]
. The partition functions in the DS sectors are given by:

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=|\chi_{0}|^{2},

\displaystyle Z_{s}

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{0},

\displaystyle Z_{\bar{s}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{0}\overline{\chi}_{1},

\displaystyle Z_{b}

\displaystyle=|\chi_{1}|^{2}.

(91)

The gapped domain wall
\mathcal{I}_{e^{2}m^{2}}
in the club sandwich construction can be written as

\mathcal{I}_{e^{2}m^{2}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}\text{TC},\mu\in\text{DS}}W_{\alpha,\mu}|\alpha\rangle\langle\mu|,

(92)

where
W_{\alpha,\mu}=1
only for transparent anyons. The untwisted torus partition function can then be obtained as the overlap with the insertion of the gapped domain wall
\mathcal{I}_{e^{2}m^{2}}

\displaystyle Z_{00}

\displaystyle=\matrixelement{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{f}}{\mathcal{I}_{e^{2}m^{2}}}{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}\text{TC},\mu\in\text{DS}}w_{\alpha}W_{\alpha,\mu}Z_{\mu}

\displaystyle=Z_{1}+Z_{b},

(93)

\displaystyle=|\chi_{0}|^{2}+|\chi_{1}|^{2}.

(94)

where in the second equality
w_{\alpha}=(1,0,1,0)
indexed as
(w_{1},w_{em^{2}},w_{e^{2}},w_{e^{3}m^{2}})
. We find that the untwisted partition function is the same as the usual
\mathrm{U}(1)_{2}
CFT.

The non-trivial characterization is in the twisted partition functions since we can twist by the element in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
. The analysis is parallel to the bosonic
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
igSPT phase by replacing the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
normal subgroup to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

[
55
]
. Here we only give an example to illustrate the main idea and other twisted partition functions can be derived similarly. Consider the partition functions with a
g\in G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
twist in the space direction and a

twist in the time direction:

Z_{g,h}=\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}he^{-\beta\mathcal{H}}.

(95)

Recall that the

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F}

The twisted partition function can be calculated in the club sandwich as

\displaystyle Z_{g,h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in m\times\mathcal{L}_{e},\mu\in\text{DS}}\frac{S_{\alpha,m^{2}}}{S_{0,\alpha}}W_{\alpha,\mu}Z_{\mu}

In this section we focus on fermionic systems with non-invertible symmetries. In particular, we consider 1D fermionic systems with the super fusion category
\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})

\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry in the

and
\text{Rep}(S_{3})

We begin with a review for the bosonic systems with
S_{3}=\{r,s|r^{3}=s^{2}=1,srs=r^{-1}\}

S_{3}
gauge theory with 8 anyon types. Following the convention in Ref.
[

A,B,\dots,H

and

corresponds to the identity representation,
B

C
is the two-dimensional representation.

and
E
are the two fluxes with conjugacy class
[s]=\{s,sr,sr^{2}\}
.
F,G,H
are the three fluxes with conjugacy class
[r]=\{r,r^{2}\}
. The
S
-matrix of the
S_{3}
gauge theory is

S=\frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2&3&3&2&2&2\\
1&1&2&-3&-3&2&2&2\\
2&2&4&0&0&-2&-2&-2\\
3&-3&0&3&-3&0&0&0\\
3&-3&0&-3&3&0&0&0\\
2&2&-2&0&0&4&-2&-2\\
2&2&-2&0&0&-2&-2&4\\
2&2&-2&0&0&-2&4&-2\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(97)

The topological spins are

\{0,0,0,0,\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\},

(98)

for anyons
A,B,\dots,H
. There is a non-trivial automorphism given by exchanging
C
and
F
anyons.

First we consider that the symmetry boundary is chosen by the pure charge condensation
A\oplus B\oplus 2C
, on which the topological lines generate the
S_{3}
(
\text{Vec}_{S_{3}}
) symmetry. In this case, there are four physical boundaries corresponding to four possible gapped phases:

1.

\mathcal{L}_{1}=A\oplus B\oplus 2C
and
H=\{1\}
:
S_{3}
SSB phase. In this case, the
S_{3}
symmetry is completely broken, and there are 6 ground states.

2.

\mathcal{L}_{2}=A\oplus D\oplus F
and
H=S_{3}
:
S_{3}
symmetric phase. There are no Wilson lines connecting the two boundaries, so the
S_{3}
symmetry is unbroken. This is the
S_{3}
symmetric state.

3.

\mathcal{L}_{3}=A\oplus B\oplus 2F
and
H=\mathbb{Z}_{3}
:
Z_{2}
SSB phase. The only nontrivial local operator is the local
B
Wilson line, which braids trivially with the
r
and
r^{2}
lines on the symmetry boundary. Thus the remaining symmetry is
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. There are 2 ground states corresponding to the trivial local operator
{\cal O}_{A}
and the nontrivial local operator
{\cal O}_{B}
, as expected from the
S_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{3}
SSB.

4.

\mathcal{L}_{4}=A\oplus C\oplus D
and
H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
:
Z_{3}
SSB phase. The nontrivial local operators are given by
{\cal O}_{C}^{1},{\cal O}_{C}^{2}
, where
1
and
2
label the condensation channel on the reference boundary (the channel is unique for
C
on the right). They form an irreducible representation under the
S_{3}
symmetry, so the remaining global symmetry is trivial. There are three ground states corresponding to the trivial local operators
{\cal O}_{A}
and the other two nontrivial local operators
{\cal O}_{C}^{1},{\cal O}_{C}^{2}
. The three ground states form a reducible representation, decomposing into the identity rep and the 2-dimensional rep.

If we choose the symmetry boundary to be the flux condensed boundary
\mathcal{L}_{2}=A\oplus D\oplus F
, the topological lines generate the
\text{Rep}(S_{3})
symmetry, which come from the charges
A,B,C
in the bulk. The fusion rule of the
\text{Rep}(S_{3})
symmetry is given by

\displaystyle X_{B}\otimes X_{B}

\displaystyle=X_{A},

\displaystyle X_{B}\otimes X_{C}

\displaystyle=X_{C}\otimes X_{B}=X_{C},

\displaystyle X_{C}\otimes X_{C}

\displaystyle=X_{A}\oplus X_{B}\oplus X_{C}.

(99)

The mapping from the boundary lines to the bulk anyon lines is given by

\displaystyle X_{A}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A\oplus D\oplus F,

\displaystyle X_{B}

\displaystyle\rightarrow B\oplus E\oplus F,

\displaystyle X_{C}

\displaystyle\rightarrow C\oplus D\oplus E\oplus G\oplus H.

(100)

S_{3}

1.

\text{Rep}(S_{3})

symmetry is preserved and there is a unique ground state.

2.

\mathcal{L}_{2}=A\oplus D\oplus F
:
\text{Rep}(S_{3})
SSB phase. There are three ground states, corresponding to the local operators:
{\cal O}_{A},{\cal O}_{D},{\cal O}_{F}
. Note that the local operators are formed by the pure fluxes, which are labeled by conjugacy classes
[g]
. The symmetry actions of the
\text{Rep}(S_{3})
symmetry on these local operators is given by the characters:

U_{\rho}\circ{\cal O}_{[g]}=\chi_{\rho}(g){\cal O}_{[g]}.

(101)

3.

\mathcal{L}_{3}=A\oplus B\oplus 2F
:
\text{Rep}(S_{3})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

{\cal O}_{F}^{1}

4.

\mathcal{L}_{4}=A\oplus C\oplus D

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

{\cal O}_{A},{\cal O}_{D}

{\cal O}_{D}
transformed non-trivially under both
X_{B}

X_{C}

Here we discuss the possible gapped phases in fermionic chians with the super fusion category
\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})
symmetry, which are realized by choosing the physical boundary to be
\mathcal{L}_{i}

\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})

\displaystyle U_{C}

\displaystyle\circ{\cal O}_{A}=2{\cal O}_{A},\quad U_{C}\circ{\cal O}_{F}=-{\cal O}_{F}.

This implies that non-invertible symmetry
X_{C}
is broken spontaneously but the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

3.

\mathcal{L}_{3}=A\oplus B\oplus 2F
:
\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

{\cal O}_{A},{\cal O}_{F}^{1},{\cal O}_{F}^{2}

{\cal O}_{F}^{1}
and
{\cal O}_{F}^{2}
come from the two possible condensation channel on the physical boundary. The symmetry actions on the local operators are given by

\displaystyle U_{B}

\displaystyle\circ{\cal O}_{A}={\cal O}_{A},\quad U_{B}\circ{\cal O}_{F}^{i}={\cal O}_{F}^{i},

\displaystyle U_{C}

\displaystyle\circ{\cal O}_{A}=2{\cal O}_{A},\quad U_{C}\circ{\cal O}_{F}^{i}=-{\cal O}_{F}^{i},

(112)

where
i=1,2
.
This implies that non-invertible symmetry
X_{C}
is broken spontaneously but the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry is unbroken.

4.

\mathcal{L}_{4}=A\oplus C\oplus D

\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})
symmetric Kitaev phase. The
\text{SRep}(S_{3})
symmetry is preserved and there is a unique ground state. However, the system is in the Kitaev phase since the partition function in the
(\text{P},\text{P})

IX
Discussion

In this work, we apply the concept of topological holography to study fermionic gapped phases and phase transitions. Potential CFT descriptions for the phase transitions are proposed based on this framework. We consider a generalization of the sandwich construction tailored to fermionic systems such that the symmetry boundary is a fermionic gapped boundary condition of the SymTFT. In particular, performing the fermionization corresponds to changing the symmetry boundary from a bosonic gapped boundary to a fermionic one. We establish connections between the formal fermionization formula and the choice of fermionic gapped boundary conditions. We also have discuss the encoding of spin structures within the SymTFT framework.

We demonstrate the main idea through various examples, including the fermionic systems with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F}
, and the non-invertible super fusion category
\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})
symmetry. We find many exotic fermionic quantum critical points and gapless phases, including two kinds of fermionic SEQCPs in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermionic chains. The first kind of the fermionic SEQCPs is obtained by stacking with a fermionic SPT states (
T
operation), and the other new type of the fermionic SEQCPs is obtained by a twisted gauging (
T
S
operation). We also find a fermionic gapless SPT phase with
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{F}
symmetry, and a fermionic gapless SSB phase that breaks the fermionic non-invertible symmetry in
\text{SRep}(\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}})
symmetry.

This work opens up many interesting avenues for future exploration. Firstly, it would be interesting to study the fermionic SEQCPs obtained by the twisted gauging more generally. It would also be interesting to apply the topological holography to systematically study the fermionic quantum critical points and gapless phases with emergent anomalies. We expect the general classification of the (2+1)
d
invertible fermionic topological phases with symmetry
[
84
,
85
,
86
]
would be helpful in this direction.

In this work, we only consider a simple example of the fermionic non-invertible symmetry obtained by fermionizing the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
subgroup of the
\text{Rep}(S_{3})
symmetry. A general framework of the fermionization of fusion category symmetries in (1+1)
d
has been developed in Ref.
[
79
]
. It would be interesting to study the fermionic phases with non-invertible symmetry and their phase transitions more systematically from the topological holography picture.

Finally, the topological holography framework for fermionic systems has been formally developed in higher dimensions
[
33
,
63
]
. To apply this framework to physically relevant systems, it is essential to enhance our understanding of the physical picture and develop computational tools for fermionic gapped boundaries in higher-dimensional SymTFTs.

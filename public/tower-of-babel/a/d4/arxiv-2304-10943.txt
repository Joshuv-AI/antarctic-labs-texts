The L 2 L^{2} -unique continuation property on manifolds with bounded geometry and the deformation operator Thanks: M.K. has been partially supported by the Babeş-Bolyai University Grant SEED/2022. N.G. has been partially supported by SPP2026 (DFG)
Nadine Große, Mirela Kohr, Victor Nistor

Abstract
. A differential operator T T satisfies the L 2 L^{2} -unique continuation property if every L 2 L^{2} -solution of T T that vanishes on an open subset vanishes identically. We study the L 2 L^{2} -unique continuation property of an operator T T acting on a manifold with bounded geometry. In particular, we establish some connections between this property and the regularity properties of T T . As an application, we prove that the deformation operator on a manifold with bounded geometry satisfies regularity and L 2 L^{2} -unique continuation properties. As another application, we prove that suitable elliptic operators are invertible (Hadamard well-posedness). Our results apply to compact manifolds, which have bounded geometry.

Background material and notation

We begin with some background material, for the benefit of the reader.
More precisely, we introduce here the needed function spaces and

in this paper. Since we are interested in
systems, all our functions spaces and differential operators will be
defined using sections of vector bundles. We also use this opportunity to
fix the notation. This section contains no new results.
See
[
18
]
and
[
26
]
for more details.

Throughout this paper,
M
will be a (usually non-compact) connected
smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary with metric
g
.

We use the convention that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\ldots,\}
and

{\mathbb{N}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

2.1.
Vector bundles, connections, and tensor products

Let
E\to M
denote a smooth (real or complex) vector bundle endowed with a
fiberwise metric
(\,,\,)_{E}
over our Riemannian manifold
M
and let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E)

be the set of
smooth
sections of
E
. Unless otherwise stated, we assume
that
E
is endowed with a metric preserving connection

\nabla^{E}\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E\otimes T^{*}M)\,.

Let
F\to M
be another vector bundle with connection
\nabla^{F}
. Then

E\otimes F
will be endowed with the product connection
\nabla^{E\otimes F}(\xi\otimes\eta)\mathrel{\ :=\ }(\nabla^{E}\xi)\otimes\eta+\xi\otimes\nabla^{F}\eta
. We will use the notation

V^{\otimes k}\mathrel{\ :=\ }V\otimes V\otimes\ldots\otimes V
for the tensor product
of
V
with itself of
k
-times. In particular, all the tensor product
bundles
E\otimes T^{*\otimes k}M\mathrel{\ :=\ }E\otimes(T^{*}M)^{\otimes k}
will
be endowed with the induced tensor product connections from
E

and from the Levi-Civita connection on
TM
. (Recall that the Levi-Civita
connection on
TM
is the unique metric preserving torsion free connection
on
TM

[
37
]
.)

When there is no risk of confusion, we shall drop the notation for the vector
bundle
E
from the notation of its connection
\nabla^{E}
and write simply
\nabla

instead.

2.2.
Covariant Sobolev spaces

Let us recall now the definition of the covariant Sobolev spaces (or

\nabla
-Sobolev spaces) on Riemannian manifolds. These spaces are defined using
connections, see, for instance
[
5
,
21
,
22
,
26
]
.

2.2.1.
Positive integer order covariant Sobolev spaces

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
E\to M
be a given Hermitian vector bundle with

\|\cdot\|_{E}
denoting the fiberwise norm on
E
.
Let
\,d\operatorname{vol}_{g}
denote the induced volume form on
M
(defined using
the given metric
g
on
M
).
We then have the usual formula for the
L^{p}
-norm:

\|u\|_{L^{p}(M;E)}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\,\begin{cases}\ \left(\int_{M}\|u(x)\|_{E}^{p}\,d\operatorname{vol}_{g}(x)\right)^{1/p}\,,&\quad\mbox{if }\ p<\infty\\
\ \,\operatorname{ess-sup}_{x\in M}\,\|u(x)\|_{E}\,,&\quad\mbox{if }\ p=\infty\,.\end{cases}

Identifying two sections of
E
that are equal on
M
except on
a zero measure set, we then obtain the usual Lebesgue spaces

L^{p}(M;E)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\{u\colon M\to E\mid\|u\|_{L^{p}(M;E)}<+\infty\}/\ker(\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(M;E)})\,.

Let
(f,g)=(f,g)_{L^{2}(M;E)}
denote the scalar (inner) product on
L^{2}(M;E)
.
It is linear in the first variable and conjugate linear in the second one.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E\to M
be a finite dimensional, Hermitian vector bundle with metric preserving connection

\nabla=\nabla^{E}
. Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then

W^{k,p}(M;E)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\{u\in L^{p}(M;E)\mid\nabla^{j}u\in L^{p}(M;E\otimes T^{*\otimes j}M)\,,\,\mbox{ for }\,1\leq j\leq k\}

is the
order
k
,
L^{p}
-type covariant Sobolev space
of
sections of
E
with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(M;E)}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\ell^{p}\mbox{--norm of }\{\|\nabla^{j}(u)\|_{L^{p}(M;E\otimes T^{*\otimes j}M)}\,,\ 0\leq j\leq k\}\,.

We shall write also
H^{k}(M;E)\mathrel{\ :=\ }W^{k,2}(M;E)
.

The adjective “covariant” in “covariant Sobolev spaces” (a term coined in
[
26
]
)
serves to remind us that these spaces depend on the choice of the covariant derivative
\nabla^{E}

on the coefficient bundle
E
. In
[
26
]
, the covariant Sobolev spaces
are also called
\nabla
-Sobolev spaces. Since we use only canonical connections and
work on manifolds with bounded geometry, these spaces
coincide with the usual Sobolev spaces, so we shall drop the connection
\nabla
or the adjective
“covariant” when talking about these spaces.

2.2.2.
Negative order covariant Sobolev spaces

We proceed as usual and we define the negative order (covariant) Sobolev spaces for

p\in[1,\infty)
by

W^{-k,p^{\prime}}(M;E^{*})\mathrel{\ :=\ }W^{k,p}(M;E)^{*}\,,

where
V^{*}
is the complex conjugate dual of
V
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. For simplicity, in the following, we
shall identify
E^{*}
with
E
using the Hermitian metric on
E
, so

W^{-k,p^{\prime}}(M;E^{*})=W^{-k,p^{\prime}}(M;E)
. We set
W^{\infty,p}(M;E)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\cap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W^{k,p}(M;E)

(which we shall use only for
p=\infty
in this paper). The spaces
H^{s}(M;E)
,

s\in\mathbb{R}
, are defined by complex interpolation.

2.3.
Differential operators

Let
E,F\to M
be vector bundles, with
E
endowed with a connection.
A
differential operator acting on sections of
E
with values sections of
F

is an expression of the form

(2.1)

Pu\mathrel{\ :=\ }\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}\nabla^{j}u\,,

with
a_{j}
a section of
\operatorname{Hom}(E\otimes T^{*\otimes j}M;F)
. Let us
assume
E=F
in what follows, for simplicity. Then
\operatorname{Hom}(E\otimes T^{*\otimes j}M;E)\simeq\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes TM^{\otimes j}
, which simplifies the notation, so, in
the following, we shall identify these spaces.
Let
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
. We shall say that the operator

P
of Equation (
2.1
)
has coefficients in

W^{\rho,\infty}
if
a_{j}\in W^{\rho,\infty}(M;\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes TM^{\otimes j})
for all
0\leq j\leq\mu
.
A differential operator
P=\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}\nabla^{j}

with coefficients in
W^{\rho,\infty}
defines a
continuous map

\displaystyle P\,=\,\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}a_{j}\nabla^{j}\colon W^{s+\mu,p}(M;E)\,\to\,W^{s,p}(M;E),\quad 0\leq s\leq\rho\ \mbox{ and }\ p\in[1,\infty]\,.

See
[
5
,
18
,
26
]
for details and further results. It will be
convenient to use the following notation for our sets of differential operators.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, and let

E,F\to M
be Hermitian vector bundles with metric preserving connections.
Then
\mathcal{D}^{\mu,\rho}(M;E,F)
will denote the set of
differential operators of order
\leq\mu
with coefficients in
W^{\rho,\infty}

acting from sections of
E
to sections of
F
. We let
\mathcal{D}^{\mu,\rho}(M;E)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\mathcal{D}^{\mu,\rho}(M;E,E)
.

In
[
26
]
, the differential operators introduced in this section were also
called “
\nabla
-differential operators.” Locally, there is no difference between
the
\nabla
-differential operators and the “usual” differential operators. In
particular, the principal symbol is defined in the same way.

3.
Manifolds with bounded geometry

We now recall some basic material on manifolds with
bounded geometry following
[
5
]
, to which we refer for more

Riemannian manifold

3.1.
Definition of bounded geometry

We now recall the definition
of a “manifold with bounded geometry.”
Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold with metric
g
,
as usual. We let
\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,y)
denote the distance between two points
x,y\in M
.

Definition 3.1
.

A Hermitian vector bundle
E\to M
with metric preserving connection is said to
have
totally bounded curvature
if its curvature
R^{E}

and all covariant derivatives
\nabla^{k}R^{E}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, are bounded on
M
. Also,
we shall say that
M
has
totally bounded curvature

if the tangent bundle
TM\to M
has totally bounded curvature.

We shall use the following standard notation.

Notation 3.2
.

Let us assume
M
to be complete in its metric
g
, for simplicity.

(i)

\exp_{x}^{M}\colon T_{x}M\to M
will be the geodesic exponential map at
x
.

(ii)

B_{x}^{M}(r)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\{y\in M\mid\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,y)<r\}
,
the ball of radius
r
in
M
centered at
x
.

(iii)

\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(x)\!\mathrel{\ :=\ }\!\!\sup\{r\mid\exp_{x}^{M}\colon B_{r}^{T_{x}M}(0)\to M\text{ is a diffeomorphism onto its image}\}
.

(iv)

\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\inf_{x\in M}\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(x)
, the
injectivity radius
of
M
.

The concept of manifolds with bounded geometry (see, for instance,
[
7
,
4
,
10
,
30
,
38
,
41
,
39
,
48
]
) is classical and fundamental
for this paper.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a boundaryless Riemannian manifold. Then
M
has
bounded geometry
if
it has totally bounded curvature and
\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)>0
(see
3.2
).

We shall need the following remark.

Remark 3.4
.

Our definition of differential operators coincides with the one in
[
29
,
30
,
38
]
for manifolds with bounded geometry since in any
normal coordinate chart defined on a ball of radius
r<\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)
, the coefficient
of our differential operator can be expressed in terms of the Christoffel coefficients
and of the bundle morphisms
a_{j}
of Equation
2.1

and hence they and their derivatives are uniformly bounded. This will be discussed
in greater detail in our forthcoming paper
[
17
]
.

Of course, a smooth, compact manifold (without boundary) will have bounded
geometry. Another useful class of manifolds with bounded geometry is the class of manifolds
with cylindrical ends, see Figure
1

manifold.

A manifold with cylindrical ends

Some of the results that hold for these manifolds (compact or with

3.2.
Sobolev spaces via coverings and partitions of unity

From now on, we will assume that
M
is a manifold with bounded geometry and that the
vector bundles
E,F\to M
have totally bounded curvature.
With these assumptions, we now
recall an alternative definition of the Sobolev spaces on
M
with coefficients in

,
see
[

,

]

[

]

Let
{n}
denote the dimension of

We will write
B_{r}^{{n}}(x)
instead of
B_{r}^{\mathbb{R}^{{n}}}(x)
, for simplicity.
Identifying the space
T_{x}M
with
\mathbb{R}^{{n}}
via some choice of an orthonormal basis,
we obtain, for

geodesic normal coordinates

(3.1)

\kappa_{x}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\exp^{M}_{x}\colon B^{{n}}_{r}(0)\to B^{M}_{r}(x)\subset M\,.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry and let
0<r\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)
.
An

-covering subset of

is a subset
\{x_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M

that satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

M\subset\cup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}B_{r}^{M}(x_{\gamma})
.

(ii)

For any
R>0
, there exists
N_{R}\in\mathbb{N}
with the property that, for any

\{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}\mid\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x_{\gamma},y)<R\}

elements.

Note that it is always possible to construct an

-covering set

and the corresponding covering

of

[

,
19
]
and the references therein).
For further purposes, we need the following class of partitions of unity defined using

-covering sets.

Definition 3.6
.

A partition of unity
\{\phi_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}

-covering set

if, for each
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

, the support of

is contained
in

.

In order to deal with the coefficient vector bundles
E,F\to M
,
we will also need to locally trivialize these bundles.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry,
x\in M
,
r<\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)
,
and

be a Hermitian vector bundle with metric preserving
connection. Then the
synchronous trivialization
of

is the vector bundle isomorphism

\xi_{x}\colon E|_{B_{r}^{M}(x)}\to B_{r}^{M}(x)\times E_{x}

.
By abuse of notation, we shall also denote by

\xi_{x}\colon E|_{B_{r}^{M}(x)}\to B_{r}^{{n}}(0)\times\mathbb{C}^{\tau}

the resulting isomorphism obtained using also the geodesic coordinates diffeomorphism

\kappa_{x}\colon B_{r}^{{n}}(0)\to B_{r}^{M}(x)
(of Equation (

)) and some choice

We shall need a definition of Sobolev spaces using partitions of unity

]
and a few standard results. The
following proposition is a direct consequence of Theorems 14 and

[

]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry. Let
E\to M
be a vector
bundle over
M
with totally bounded curvature. Let
\{\phi_{\gamma}\}
be an

r
-uniform partition of unity associated
to an
r
-covering set
\{x_{\gamma}\}\subset M
(Definition
3.6
)
and let
\kappa_{\gamma}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\kappa_{x_{\gamma}}\colon B_{r}^{{n}}(0)\to B_{r}^{M}(x_{\gamma})
and

\xi_{\gamma}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\xi_{x_{\gamma}}\colon E|_{B_{r}^{M}(x)}\to B_{r}^{{n}}(0)\times\mathbb{C}^{\tau}

be as in Definition
3.7
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

|||u|||_{s,p}^{p}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\sum_{\gamma}\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}^{{n}}(0);\mathbb{C}^{\tau})}^{p}

defines a norm equivalent to the norm on
W^{s,p}(M;E)
introduced in
Definition
2.1
using connections.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}}
be the norm on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
That is, if
a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers, then

\|a\|_{\ell^{p}}\,=\,\|(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|_{\ell^{p}}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\begin{cases}\ \big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|a_{n}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}&\mbox{ if }p<\infty\\
\ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|a_{n}|&\mbox{ if }p=\infty\,.\end{cases}

Then
\|a\|_{p}
is
decreasing
in
p\in[1,\infty]
.
It will be convenient to also write

(3.2)

|||u|||_{s,p}\,=\,\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\,.

with
\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}^{{n}}(0);\mathbb{C}^{\tau})}
.

The Rellich-Kondrachov compact embedding theorem

[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
(see also
[
2
,
3
,
28
,
46
]
)
gives then the following extension to manifolds with bounded geometry of the
results of Rellich-Kondrachov and Lions. Related results were obtained in
[
41
]

(but using weighted spaces) and in
[
15
, Appendix A]
for
p=2
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
p,p^{\prime},q\in(1,\infty)
,
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
satisfy the Rellich-Kondrachov conditions:

t<s<s^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad{\max\{t,0\}}-n/q\leq s-n/p\leq s^{\prime}-n/p^{\prime}\,.

Let also
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry and
E\to M
be a hermitian vector
bundle with totally bounded curvature.

(i)

If also
p^{\prime}\leq p
, then we have
W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)\subset W^{s,p}(M;E)

(continuous embedding).

(ii)

Assume furthermore that
\max\{p^{\prime},q\}\leq p
and
s-n/p<s^{\prime}-n/p^{\prime}
. Then,
for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that,
for all
u\in W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)
, we have

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(M;E)}\leq\epsilon\|u\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{W^{t,q}(M;E)}\,.

Proof.

Both statements follows from the analogous statements over an open ball

M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
together with Proposition
3.8
.

Let us see how this is done. We first review the case when
M
is
replaced with an open ball
M_{0}
in a euclidean spaces. We may assume then that

E
is replaced with a trivial bundle
E_{0}\to M_{0}
. The first
statement for
(M_{0},E_{0})
is then nothing but the classical Sobolev embedding,

[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Let us recall next a proof of the second statement for

(M_{0},E_{0})
(recall that
M_{0}
is an open ball).
Let us assume that the statement is not true. Let
us fix
\epsilon>0
. Then, there exists a sequence
u_{n}\in W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M_{0},E_{0})

such that

\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})}>\epsilon\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M_{0},E_{0})}+n\|u_{n}\|_{W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})}\,.

We can assume that
\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M_{0},E_{0})}=1
. In view of the Rellich-Kondrachov
compactness theorem, the natural inclusions

W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M_{0},E_{0})\to W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})

is compact, and hence (eventually after replacing the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

with a subsequence), we may also assume that the sequence

u_{n}
converges in norm to
w\in W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})
. Passing to the limit in the
relation

\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})}>\epsilon\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M_{0},E_{0})}=\epsilon,

we obtain
\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})}\geq\epsilon>0
. Hence
w\neq 0
.
Let us notice that the assumptions also give that
W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})\to W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})

is continuous. Indeed, if
t\geq 0
, this follows directly from the Rellich-Kondrachov embedding.
If
t<0
, then it follows from the continuity of
W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})\to W^{0,q}(M_{0},E_{0})

and of
W^{0,q}(M_{0},E_{0})\to W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})
.
The continuity of
W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})\to W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})
gives that
u_{n}
converges to

w
also in
W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})
. Let
R\mathrel{\ :=\ }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})}
. We have

R<\infty
since
u_{n}
converges in
W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})
. Then, passing to the limit in the relation

R/n\geq\|u_{n}\|_{W^{s,p}(M_{0},E_{0})}/n>\|u_{n}\|_{W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})}\,,

we
obtain
\|w\|_{W^{t,q}(M_{0},E_{0})}=0
. This is in contradiction with the previously
proved relation
w\neq 0
. Thus the
statement (ii) is true if
M=M_{0}
, an open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let us next prove (i) in general. Let
u\in W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)
. We shall write
A\lesssim B

if there is a bound
C\geq 0
independent of the functions involved and
\epsilon>0

such that
A\leq CB
. We shall use below also Equation (
3.2
).
Then
\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}

by the case of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(the Rellich-Kondrachov inequality), and hence

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(M;E)}\

\displaystyle\lesssim|||u|||_{s,p}\mathrel{\ :=\ }\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}\ =:\ |||u|||_{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\,\lesssim\,\|u\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)}\,,

where we have used also the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}}
is decreasing in
p
. This proves (i).

Let us now prove (ii) in the general case. We have successively:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(M;E)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s,p}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big(\epsilon\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}+C_{\epsilon}\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{t,q}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}+C_{\epsilon}\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{t,q}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}+C_{\epsilon}\big\|\big(\|\xi_{\gamma}(\phi_{\gamma}u)\|_{W^{t,q}}\big)_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|u\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{W^{t,q}(M;E)}\,,

where we have used again the decreasing monotony of the
\ell^{p}
-norm and
p\geq p^{\prime}
and
p\geq q
.
This means
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(M;E)}\leq C\epsilon\|u\|_{W^{s^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(M;E)}+CC_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{W^{t,q}(M;E)}
.
To obtain the desired result, one needs only to rescale
\epsilon
by replacing it with
\epsilon/C
.
∎

Of course, if

4.
Regularity estimates for second order strongly elliptic operators

We shall need the regularity result for uniformly strongly elliptic operators on
manifolds bounded geometry
M
stated in the Introduction (and which is a slight
generalization of a result in
[
18
]
). Note, however, that in
[
18
]
, the
authors have used operators in
divergence form
(unlike in this paper), so
the regularity requirements on the coefficients in that and this paper are
(slightly) different.

4.1.
Regularity conditions

Recall the notation
\mathcal{D}^{\mu,\rho}(M;E,F)
for our order
\mu
differential
operators with coefficients in
W^{\rho,\infty}
(Definition
2.2
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{D}^{2,0}(M;E)
. We shall say that
P
is

uniformly strongly elliptic
if there exists
\gamma_{P}>0
such that, for all

x\in M
and all
\eta\in T_{x}^{*}M
and
\zeta\in E_{x}
, we
have that its principal symbol
\sigma_{2}(P)\colon T^{*}M\to\operatorname{End}(E)
satisfies
the inequality

(4.1)

\operatorname{Re}(\sigma_{2}(P)(\eta)\zeta,\zeta)_{E_{x}}\geq\gamma_{P}\|\eta\|^{2}\|\zeta\|^{2}\,.

Of course, the defining relation (
4.1
) is equivalent to

(4.2)

\sigma_{2}(P)(\eta)+\sigma_{2}(P)(\eta)^{*}\geq 2\gamma_{P}\|\eta\|^{2}

(with the inequality in operator sense).
Let us now prove our regularity result stated in the Introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

A differential operator with coefficients in
W^{\rho+3,\infty}
can
be written as a differential operator in divergence form with coefficients
in
W^{\rho+2,\infty}
. (Indeed, in local coordinates, an operator
in divergence form is a linear combination of operators of the form
\partial_{i}a\partial_{j}
.
The claim is obtained by writing
\partial_{i}a\partial_{j}=a\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+\partial_{i}(a)\partial_{j}
,
with the right hand side consisting of operators written in the standard form.
The loss of one order of regularity is due to the appearance of
\partial_{i}(a)
.)
For
t=1
and operators in divergence form with
coefficients in
W^{\rho+2,\infty}
, this result was proved in

[
18
, Theorem 5.12]
. This completes our proof if
t=1
.
Proposition
3.9
for
\epsilon c_{s,1}<1
,
p=p^{\prime}=q=2
,
and
s^{\prime}=s+2
then allows us to extend to the case
t<1
.
The result for
t>1
follows from the result for
t=1
. The proof is
thus complete.
∎

A more general result than this theorem will be provided in
[
16
]
.
We immediately obtain the following result (which eliminates the restriction

s\leq\rho
of the above theorem).

Corollary 4.2
.

We use the notation and assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, except
that we assume that
P
has coefficients in
W^{\infty,\infty}
, but allow
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then there exists
c_{s,t}>0
such that, if
u\in H^{t}(M;E)
and

,
it follows that
u\in H^{s+2}(M;E)
and

\|u\|_{H^{s+2}(M;E)}\leq c_{s,t}\left(\|Pu\|_{H^{s}(M;F)}+\|u\|_{H^{t}(M;E)}\right).

The above result will be used (mostly) for
s=t=0
, which explains why we needed
to include the case

in our regularity result, Theorem

.

5.
Application:
L^{2}
-unique continuation and the deformation operator

We continue to assume that
M
is a manifold with bounded geometry and that

E\to M
is a Hermitian vector bundle with totally bounded curvature.

5.1.
An invertibility result

The next result states that if
T\in\mathcal{D}^{2,\infty}(M;E)
is a uniformly strongly elliptic symmetric
operator on
L^{2}(M;E)
, on which we consider the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(M;E)}
,
then
T
is essentially self-adjoint, that is, its closure is a self-adjoint operator
on
L^{2}(M;E)
. Its proof is standard.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry and
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle
with totally bounded curvature. Let
T\in\mathcal{D}^{2,\infty}(M;E)
be a uniformly strongly elliptic
differential operator and consider the setting of Corollary
4.4
for
s=0
.
If
T\colon H^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M;E)
is symmetric
(
that is,
(Tf,g)=(f,Tg)
, for all

f,g\in H^{2}(M;E)
)
, then
T
is self-adjoint.

Proof.

Let
T^{\dagger}\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)^{*}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)^{*}
be the adjoint of
T
in distribution
sense (with
V^{*}:=\overline{V}^{\prime}
the conjugate linear dual of
V
). Our
assumption is that
T^{\dagger}=T
as
differential operators acting on

{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)
, and hence also as
differential operators acting on
distributions
, since
T
and
T^{\dagger}
are continuous on distributions and

{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)
is dense in the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)^{*}
of distributions. Let
T^{*}
be
the adjoint of
T
in
L^{2}
-sense. (The pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
is
sesquilinear.) Since we know that
T
is closed, by Corollary
4.4
,
it is enough to show that
T^{*}\pm\imath
are both injective (where
\imath\in\mathbb{C}

is such that
\imath^{2}=-1
). Let
\phi
in
the domain of
T^{*}
be such that
(T^{*}+\imath)\phi=0
. Since
\phi\in L^{2}(M;E)
,
it is a distribution, and
T^{\dagger}\phi
is defined. By taking inner products with
test functions
\psi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)
(which are in the domain of
T
), we see that

(T+\imath)\phi=(T^{\dagger}+\imath)\phi=(T^{*}+\imath)\phi=0\,,

in distribution sense. Since
T
is elliptic of order
2>0
, we obtain that

T+\imath
is also elliptic of order
2
. The regularity theorem, Theorem
1.2
,
gives that
\phi\in H^{2}(M;E)
. Hence
\phi
is in the domain of
T
. But then the usual

L^{2}
-estimate

0=\|(T+\imath)\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,=\,((T+\imath)\phi,(T+\imath)\phi)\,=\,\|T\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

gives that
\phi=0
.
∎

Recall the definition of the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property from Definition
1.1
.
The most basic example is the following.

Example 5.2
.

Let
M_{0}
be a connected Riemannian manifold. The exterior derivative

d\colon L^{2}(M_{0})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M_{0},T^{*}M_{0})^{\prime}
has the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property. Indeed,
if
du=0
, then
u
is locally constant, and hence constant, since
M_{0}

was assumed to be connected. If, furthermore,
u
vanishes on some non-empty
open subset of
M_{0}
, then this constant must be zero.

The following result is a key technical application of
L^{2}
-unique continuation
property that allows us to establish the invertibility of our operators
in applications to the Stokes system
[
27
]
. It motivates the
consideration of the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property. Let
X
and
Y
be
Banach spaces. Recall that a (possibly
unbounded) closed operator
T:\mathcal{D}(T)\subset X\to Y
is
Fredholm
if

T^{-1}(0)
and
Y/TX
are finite dimensional. Then its
index
is
\dim T^{-1}(0)-\dim(Y/TX)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry and
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle
with totally bounded curvature. Let
L\in\mathcal{D}^{2,\infty}(M;E)
be a differential operator
(so of order
2
) and
V\in W^{\infty,\infty}(M;\operatorname{End}(E))
(so of order zero) with the
following properties:

(i)

V,L\geq 0
, more precisely, for all
v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)
,

(Lv,v)\geq 0\ \mbox{ and }\ (Vv,v)\geq 0\,.

(ii)

L
is uniformly strongly elliptic
and has the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property (Definition
1.1
).

(iii)

There exists a non-empty open set
{\mathcal{O}}\subset M

such that, if
v\in L^{2}(M;E)
and
(Vv,v)=0
, then
v|_{{\mathcal{O}}}\,=\,0.

(iv)

L+V\colon H^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M;E)
is a Fredholm operator.

Then
L+V\colon H^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M;E)
is invertible.

Proof.

Since
V
has order smaller than the order of
L
and
L
is uniformly strongly
elliptic, by assumption, it follows that
L+V
is also uniformly strongly elliptic.
We then notice that, since
L
and
L+V
are both uniformly strongly elliptic,
both have domain
H^{2}(M;E)
and are closed as unbounded operators on
L^{2}(M;E)

by Corollary
4.4
. The positivity of both
L

and
V
implies also the positivity of
L+V
. (We obtain positivity on the whole
domain since
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\text{c}}(M;E)
is dense in
H^{2}(M;E)
in the graph norm.) In turn, positivity
implies that
L
and
L+V
are symmetric (see, for instance, Proposition 2.12 in
[
12
]
).
Lemma
5.1
then gives that both
L
and

L+V
are self-adjoint. Since
L+V
is Fredholm and self-adjoint, its index will be 0.

Let us assume, by contradiction, that
L+V
is
not
invertible. Since it is
Fredholm of index zero, it must have a non-zero kernel. Thus, there exists
u\in H^{2}(M;E)
,

u\neq 0
, such that
(L+V)u=0
. Then

(Lu,u)+(Vu,u)=0\,.

The relations
(Lu,u)\geq 0
,
(Vu,u)\geq 0
,
and the self-adjointness of
L
, as unbounded operator on
L^{2}(M;E)
, yield

\|L^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2}=(Lu,u)=0\ \mbox{ and }\ (Vu,u)=0\,.

Hence, we obtain
Lu=0
on
M
.
Moreover, assumption (
iii
) and the relation
(Vu,u)=0

imply that
u=0
on the open subset
{\mathcal{O}}
of
M
. The unique continuation property satisfied by the
operator
L
finally shows that
u=0
on
M
, which is a contradiction with the initial assumption that

u\neq 0
is in the kernel of
L+V
. The proof is complete.
∎

See
[
36
]
for a motivation and references related
to the above result. We shall need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry and
E,F\to M
be Hermitian vector bundles with
totally bounded curvature. Let
T\in\mathcal{D}^{1,\infty}(M;E,F)
have the

L^{2}
-unique continuation property. If
T^{*}T
is uniformly strongly elliptic, then it also has the
L^{2}
-unique
continuation property.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{2}(M;E)
be such that
T^{*}Tu=0

and
u=0
on some non-empty open set
\mathcal{O}\subset M
. Since
T^{*}T

is uniformly strongly elliptic, we obtain that
u\in H^{2}(M;E)
by elliptic regularity
(see Theorem
1.2
or Corollary
4.2
). Therefore, we have

\|Tu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,=\,(Tu,Tu)\,=\,(T^{*}Tu,u)\,=\,0\,.

Hence

. The
L^{2}
-unique continuation property for

then gives

.
∎

5.2.
Vanishing of Killing vector fields

We shall need the following lemma (a stronger result can be found in

[
37
, Prop. 8.1.4]
, we include, nevertheless a simple proof, for the
benefit of the reader):

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M_{0}
be a connected Riemannian manifold with positive injectivity radius
(and hence complete).
Let
\mathcal{O}\subset M_{0}
be a closed subset with non-empty
interior and with the following property:

“If
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M_{0}
is a (complete)
geodesic that is minimizing between two points
a,b\in\mathcal{O}
,
then
\gamma\subset\mathcal{O}
.”

Then
\mathcal{O}=M_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subset\mathcal{O}
be the interior of
\mathcal{O}
, which is known to be non-empty
by hypothesis. Let us show that
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is also closed in
M_{0}
.
For
x\in M_{0}
, we shall let
B_{r}^{M_{0}}(x)\subset M_{0}
denote the open ball with center
x
and radius
r

(the set of points
y\in M_{0}
at distance
<r
to
x
). Let

r_{0}>0
be the injectivity radius of
M_{0}
, we claim that

(5.1)

x_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{O}_{0}\,.

Indeed, to prove the claim (
5.1
), let
y\in B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0})
.
Then there is a minimizing geodesic
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M_{0}
joining
y
to
x_{0}
. We can assume
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
.
Then, for
t
small enough,
\gamma(t)\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
and
\gamma
is minimizing between
x_{0}=\gamma(0)

and
\gamma(t)
. It follows by assumption that
\gamma\subset\mathcal{O}
and hence, in particular,
y\in\mathcal{O}
.
This shows that, for every
x_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
, the ball
B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{O}
. Hence also

B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{O}_{0}
since
B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0})
is open. We have thus proved our claim,
that is, Equation (
5.1
).

Let us next show that the proven claim implies that
\mathcal{O}_{0}

is closed. Indeed, let
y\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{0}
. Then
B^{M_{0}}_{r_{0}}(y)\cap\mathcal{O}_{0}

is non-empty. Let
x_{0}\in B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(y)\cap\mathcal{O}_{0}
. Then
y\in B_{r_{0}}^{M_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\mathcal{O}_{0}
. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is
closed, as stated.

This completes the proof. Indeed,
M_{0}
is connected and
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subset M_{0}
is open, closed, and
non-empty, it follows that
\mathcal{O}_{0}=M_{0}
. This proves that
\mathcal{O}=M_{0}
as well, and concludes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{L}_{X}
denote the Lie derivative in the direction of a vector field
X
. Then the
deformation tensor
\operatorname{Def}\,X
is defined as

(5.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Def}\,X=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{L}_{X}g

(see
[
45
, Chapter 2 §3]
). Thus,
\operatorname{Def}\,X
is a symmetric tensor field of type
(0,2)
,
and
\operatorname{Def}\,
will be viewed as an unbounded operator from
L^{2}(TM)
to
L^{2}(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M)
.
Let also
\operatorname{Def}\,^{*}
denote the adjoint of the deformation operator
\operatorname{Def}\,
. If
\operatorname{Def}\,X=0
, then

X
is called a
Killing vector field.

The next corollary will provide us with the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property for the
deformation operator
\operatorname{Def}\,
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
M_{0}
be a connected Riemannian manifold with positive injectivity radius.
Let
X
be a smooth
Killing
vector field on
M_{0}
that vanishes on a non-empty open
subset of
M_{0}
. Then
X=0

(
i.e. it vanishes on
M_{0}
)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset M_{0}
be the set of points where
X=0
,
which contains an open subset of
M_{0}
, by assumption. Since
X
is smooth and
M_{0}
is complete,

X
generates a
local
flow
\phi_{t}\colon M_{0}\to M_{0}
, with
\phi_{t}(x)
defined for

t\in(-\epsilon_{x},\epsilon_{x})
, for some
\epsilon_{x}>0
.
Since
X
is a Killing vector field, the local flow
\phi_{t}

consists of (partially defined) isometries. Moreover,
\phi_{t}=id
on
\mathcal{O}
(since
X=0
there).
We conclude that
\phi_{t}
fixes any minimizing
geodesic between two points of
\mathcal{O}
. Hence any (complete) geodesic minimizing the distance between
two points of
\mathcal{O}
is contained in
\mathcal{O}
. Lemma
5.5

then gives that
\mathcal{O}=M_{0}
. Hence
X=0
everywhere on
M
.
∎

5.3.
Applications to the deformation operator

We continue to assume that
M
is a manifold with bounded geometry. Let
g
be a
Riemannian metric tensor on
M
.
We now prove our main application to the deformation operator.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
M
be a manifold with bounded geometry.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

We have
\operatorname{Def}\,\in\mathcal{D}^{1,\infty}(M;TM,T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M)
.

(ii)

The deformation Laplacian
\mathbf{L}\mathrel{\ :=\ }2\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,
is
uniformly strongly elliptic.

(iii)

If
M
is connected, then
\operatorname{Def}\,
and
\mathbf{L}\mathrel{\ :=\ }2\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,
have
the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property on
M
.

Proof.

(
i
) follows from formula (
5.2
)
and the results in
[
26
]
(see, for instance,
[
26
, Proposition 5.12]
,
noting that manifolds with bounded geometry satisfy the (FFC) condition
by the results in
[
18
]
, Lemma 3.1).

(
ii
) The operator
\mathbf{L}\mathrel{\ :=\ }2\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,
is in

\mathcal{D}^{2,\infty}(M;\operatorname{End}(TM))
by point (
i
)
already proved and
[
26
, Proposition 4.5]
.
The principal symbol of
\mathbf{L}

(a second order differential operator) is given by
[
45
, Chapter 5 §12, (12.20)]

\sigma_{2}(\mathbf{L})(\eta)=|\eta|^{2}({1}+P_{\eta})\geq|\eta|^{2}\,,\ \forall\,\eta\in T_{x}^{*}M\setminus\{0\},

where
P_{\eta}(u):=|\eta|^{-2}(\eta,u)\eta
is the orthogonal projection of
u
along
\eta
(regarded
as a vector) and we use the notation
1
for the identity operator.
Hence
\mathbf{L}
is a uniformly strongly elliptic operator, as asserted.

(
iii
) Let us assume that
M
is a connected manifold and let
X\in L^{2}(M,TM)
be such
that
\operatorname{Def}\,X=0
on
M
. Then
\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,X=0
. Since
\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,

is uniformly strongly elliptic by point (
ii
) already proved, elliptic regularity
(Corollary
4.2
) gives
that
X
is smooth. Since
M
has positive injectivity radius, Lemma
5.5
and,
more precisely, Corollary
5.6
, give
that
X=0
. Hence
\operatorname{Def}\,
has the
L^{2}
unique continuation property. Lemma
5.4
, in
conjunction again with point (
ii
) already proved, gives then that
\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,

also has the
L^{2}
unique continuation property.
∎

Combining with Proposition
5.3
, we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
M
be a
connected
manifold with bounded geometry
and
\mathbf{L}:=2\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,
, as before. Let

V\in W^{\infty,\infty}(M;\operatorname{End}(E))
with the following properties:

(i)

V\geq 0
.

(ii)

There exists a non-empty open subset
{\mathcal{O}}\subset M

such that, if
v\in L^{2}(M;E)
and
(Vv,v)=0
, then
v|_{{\mathcal{O}}}\,=\,0.

(iii)

\mathbf{L}+V\colon H^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M;E)
is a Fredholm operator.

Then
\mathbf{L}+V\colon H^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M;E)
is invertible.

There is an analogous definition of
W^{s,p}
-unique continuation property:

Definition 5.9
.

Let
T
be pseudodifferential operator acting from sections of
E
to sections of
F
.
Let us assume that
T
extends by continuity to a map
T:W^{s,p}(M;E)\to\mathcal{D}(M;F)^{\prime}
.
We shall say that
T
has the
W^{s,p}
-unique continuation property
if,
given
u\in W^{s,p}(M;E)
that vanishes in an open subset
U\subset M_{0}
and
satisfies
Tu\,=\,0
, then
u\,=\,0
.

Then the same proof gives as the proof of the
L^{2}
-unique continuation property for

\operatorname{Def}\,
in Theorem
5.7
(
iii
) gives the following.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
M_{0}
be a connected manifold with bounded geometry and
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
then
\operatorname{Def}\,
has the
H^{s}
-unique continuation property.

Generalizations of this kind are possible for other statements, when suitable
reformulated. For instance, the problem in Lemma
5.4
is to specify
which adjoint one uses. One can still use the adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner
product (not with respect to
H^{s}
), then, as a consequence, one would obtain also
that
\mathbf{L}:=\operatorname{Def}^{*}\operatorname{Def}\,
also has the
H^{s}
-unique continuation
property.

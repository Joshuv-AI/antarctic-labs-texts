Compressing Large Language Models using Low Rank and Low Precision Decomposition
Rajarshi Saha, Naomi Sagan, Varun Srivastava, Andrea J. Goldsmith, Mert Pilanci

Abstract
The prohibitive sizes of Large Language Models (LLMs) today make it difficult to deploy them on memory-constrained edge devices. This work introduces CALDERA – a new post-training LLM compression algorithm that harnesses the inherent low-rank structure of a weight matrix 𝐖 \mathbf{W} by approximating it via a low-rank, low-precision decomposition as 𝐖 ≈ 𝐐 + 𝐋𝐑 \mathbf{W}\approx\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R} . Here, 𝐋 \mathbf{L} and 𝐑 \mathbf{R} are low rank factors, and the entries of 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} , 𝐋 \mathbf{L} and 𝐑 \mathbf{R} are quantized. The model is compressed by substituting each layer with its 𝐐 + 𝐋𝐑 \mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R} decomposition, and the zero-shot performance of the compressed model is evaluated. Additionally, 𝐋 \mathbf{L} and 𝐑 \mathbf{R} are readily amenable to low-rank adaptation, consequently enhancing the zero-shot performance. CALDERA obtains this decomposition by formulating it as an optimization problem min 𝐐 , 𝐋 , 𝐑 ⁡ ∥ ( 𝐐 + 𝐋𝐑 − 𝐖 ) ​ 𝐗 ⊤ ∥ F 2 \min_{\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}}\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2} , where 𝐗 \mathbf{X} is the calibration data, and 𝐐 , 𝐋 , 𝐑 \mathbf{Q},\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R} are constrained to be representable using low-precision formats. Theoretical upper bounds on the approximation error of CALDERA are established using a rank-constrained regression framework, and the tradeoff between compression ratio and model performance is studied by analyzing the impact of target rank and quantization bit budget. Results illustrate that compressing LlaMa- 2 2 7 7 B/ 13 13 B/ 70 70 B and LlaMa- 3 3 8 8 B models using CALDERA outperforms existing post-training LLM compression techniques in the regime of less than 2.5 2.5 bits per parameter. The implementation is available at: https://github.com/pilancilab/caldera .



1
Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) stand out due to their remarkable ability to generate human-like text, thereby supporting a diverse range of applications ranging from writing assistance to code generation.
These models leverage vast datasets and significant computational resources to achieve their impressive functionality.
The architecture of LLMs typically includes multiple layers, each with weight matrices essential for encoding various aspects of the training data – from simple syntactic patterns to complex semantic relationships.
However, the substantial size of these trained models leads to high computational costs and considerable energy consumption during inference, which can be challenging for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
As LLMs continue to expand in scale, compression techniques to reduce the memory and computational requirements of the models are becoming crucial to ensure their broad accessibility.

Due to the correlated nature of language syntax and semantics learned during training, often, the weight matrices of LLMs exhibit redundancy, which manifests as a low-rank structure.
This redundancy suggests the potential for compression without substantial loss in performance.

Significance and Related Works

Recent efforts have explored various avenues for compression, including but not limited to weight pruning, quantization, and the use of parameter-efficient training methods – each approach offering distinct advantages and tradeoffs.
This section briefly reviews the current methodologies, highlighting the contributions and limitations of some studies closely related to this work.

2
Problem Formulation

In a neural network layer, a weight matrix
\mathbf{W}
transforms an input activation
\mathbf{x}
into an output activation given by
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}
.
This transformation can be succinctly described using the matrix’s singular value decomposition (SVD).
For any matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, the SVD is
\mathbf{A}=\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
, where
\sigma_{i},\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}
are the
i^{\rm th}
singular value and the corresponding left and right singular vectors, respectively.
The impact of each singular component
\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
on the matrix’s transformation is determined by the magnitude of
\sigma_{i}
.
Given that weight matrices exhibit a decaying singular value profile (Fig.
2
), indicating an approximate low-rank structure, lesser contributing singular components can be pruned with minimal impact on the functionality of the matrix, ensuring minimal distortion in the output activations.

A

DE
composition with Low-
R
ank
A
daptation approximates the weight matrix of a neural network,
\mathbf{W}
, as a low-precision, low-rank decomposition,
\mathbf{W}\approx\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}
, with all components
\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}
in low-precision format.
Unlike previous works such as
[
13
,
22
,
45
,
44
]
, which represent the low-rank factors
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
in high-precision (16 or 32-bit floating point), this work extends their representation to low-precision.
This further reduces the memory footprint while preserving performance.
Alternatively, for the same memory footprint, it allows the target rank
k
to be higher, thereby capturing the low rank structure with higher fidelity by including more of the higher singular value components.
The following paragraph formalizes this as a constrained optimization problem.

For a given quantizer, let
\mathbb{Q}
denote the set of discrete quantization points in
\mathbb{R}
.
For
\mathrm{B}
-bit quantization, the cardinality of
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies
\log_{2}\lvert\mathbb{Q}\rvert\leq\mathrm{B}
.
Consider a matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.
The goal of this work is to obtain an decomposition
\mathbf{W}\approx\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}
by approximately solving the minimization problem

\min_{\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}}\left\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{n\times d},\;\mathbf{L}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}^{n\times k},\;\text{and }\;\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}^{k\times d}.

(1)

Here,
\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{Q}}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}
denote the lattice codebooks used to quantize
\mathbf{Q}
,
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
, using
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
,
\mathrm{B}_{\rm L}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}
bits, respectively.
Furthermore,
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is a calibration matrix that aims to preserve the Frobenius norm error of the compressed layer output activations.
If
\mathbf{W}
is the first layer’s weight matrix,
\mathbf{X}
includes input embeddings from a calibration dataset, such as a subset of RedPajama
[
34
]
, with the
i^{\rm th}
row representing the
i^{\rm th}
datapoint.
For intermediate layers,
\mathbf{X}
contains the input activations, which are the output activations of the preceding layer.

Using the Frobenius norm of the output of a layer as a proxy objective for quantizing the weight matrices of an LLM is a popular strategy, and was used in prior work of
Nagel et al. [27]
.
This proxy objective function is particularly useful for post-training quantization of LLMs because their large size makes it difficult to apply sophisticated compression methods.

Approximation Error Analysis

The approximation error upper bounds are derived via a rank-constrained regression framework.
Thm.
4.1
below (formally stated and proved in App.
C.4
) is an informal version of the main theoretical result of this paper, and provides an upper bound on the Frobenius norm error when
C
alibration
A
ware
L
ow-Precision
DE
composition with Low-
R
ank
A
daptation approximates a weight matrix
\mathbf{W}
is as
\mathbf{W}\approx\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}
by solving the optimization problem (
1
) using Alg.
1
.
For convenience of analysis, it is assumed that the dynamic range of
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}
, denoted as
\mathrm{R}
, is chosen to be high enough, ensuring it remains unsaturated.
Consequently, for a scalar input the quantization error from
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}
has zero mean and bounded variance, given by
\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}=\frac{\mathrm{R}^{2}}{(2^{\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}}-1)^{2}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Approximation error of
caldera
(Informal)

Given
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
with
m\leq d
, let
\mathbf{D}
be obtained from the LDL decomposition
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=m\mathbf{H}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{\top}
, and
\lambda_{\rm max}
,
\lambda_{\rm min}
denote the max and min eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}
.
Additionally, let
\mathbf{Q}\triangleq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{\sc LDLQ}}(\mathbf{W},\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q})
, where
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}
has dynamic range
\mathrm{R}
and bit-budget
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
, the quantization error be
\boldsymbol{\eta}\triangleq\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}(\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{M})-(\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{M})
, and
\sigma_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{k}\ldots
be the singular values of
\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})^{\top}
.
If the target rank
k
satisfies
0.25\lambda_{\rm min}^{1/2}(m\sigma_{1})^{-1}\lambda_{\rm max}^{-3/2}\sum_{i>k}\sigma_{i}^{2}\leq k\leq m
, and the dynamic ranges of
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
and
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
are set as
\mathrm{R}_{\rm L}=\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{k}\sqrt{m\lambda_{\rm min}}}
and
\mathrm{R}_{\rm R}=\sigma_{1}
, then
\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
returned by Alg.
1
satisfy

\frac{1}{nm}\;\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i>k}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top})+\epsilon\lesssim\frac{4{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}d}\lambda_{\rm max}\mathrm{R}^{2}}{\pi\left(2^{\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}}-1\right)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{k}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n}}\right)^{2}+\epsilon,

while utilizing an average budget of
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{k\sigma_{1}^{3}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}m}\epsilon\sigma_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}}{\lambda_{\rm min}}\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}d/n}}\right)
bits per parameter for the low-rank factors
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
, when
n\approx d
.
Here, the expectation is over the stochasticity of the quantizers.

An informal version of the main result is provided here, and the formal version including specific constant values, along with the derivation, can be found in App.
C.4
.
The requirement
m\leq d
is not restrictive, because when
\mathbf{H}
is positive definite, (
1
) can be rewritten as
\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
, ensuring
m=d
.
This is detailed further in App.
C.5
.
The approximation error upper bound given by Thm.
4.1
can be directly compared with the result of
Chee et al. [3, Thm. 1]
, which states that for vanilla LDLQ without
LPLRFactorize
,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\textrm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}\right)\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}).

(4)

Evidently, Alg.
1
yields a smaller error provided,
\sum_{i>k}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top})<\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top})-\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
can be chosen to be arbitrarily small.
Furthermore, since the expression in Thm.
4.1
consists of two terms, namely, the rank-constrained regression error, which depends on the target rank
k
, and the additive quantization error of
\epsilon
, which is dictated by the bit-budgets used for
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
, this upper bound can be made arbitrarily small by ensuring that the two terms are approximately equal, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
is upper bounded by
2\epsilon
.
This is apparent in the following regimes:

(i)

{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}k\ll n}
: In this regime,
k
is treated as a constant as
n
grows.
Then, if the bit-budget
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
satisfies

\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}\geq\log_{2}\left(2\mathrm{R}(\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}nmd}\;\lambda_{\rm max}}+1\right),\quad\text{then}\quad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leq 2\epsilon.

(ii)

{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}k=\mathrm{O}(n)}
: For a fixed
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
, if
k
is allowed to grow with dimension
n
, then choosing
k
to satisfy

k\geq 2{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n}-(2^{\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}}-1)\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\pi\epsilon)^{1/2}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}md}\lambda_{\rm max}^{-1/2})\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n}}\quad\text{ensures}\quad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leq 2\epsilon.

This implies that the upper bound can be made arbitrarily small by either
\rm(i)
increasing the bit-budget of the backbone, i.e.,
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
, for a fixed rank
k
, or
\rm(ii)
increasing the rank
k
for a fixed
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}
, for example,
\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}=2
.
Alternatively stated, this provides a tunable knob for controlling the error by trading off the allocated bit-budget between the backbone
\mathbf{Q}
and the low-rank factors
\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}
.

4.1
Analysis Outline

In this section, a brief proof sketch is presented, highlighting the major challenges in the proof and how they are addressed.
For analysis,
\mathbf{Q}
is assumed to be updated prior to
\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}
in Alg.
1
.
However, in practice, the update order is inconsequential, and can be swapped, depending on whichever yields a smaller error.
The complete derivation of the approximation error is provided in App.
C
.
A key ingredient of the proof is the solution of the rank-constrained regression problem, which is defined as,

\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}.

(5)

Although this problem is non-convex, it can be solved to global optimality via two SVDs
[
41
]
.
The following lemma characterizes the solution to the optimization problem in (
5
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Given
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and full rank
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, where
m\leq d
.
Let
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
and
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}=\grave{\mathbf{U}}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
denote full SVDs of
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
.
Then, for
k\leq m
, the solution of (
5
) is given by

\mathbf{Z}_{*}\triangleq\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmin}_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\left(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{m}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right),

where
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{I}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is a diagonal matrix consisting of the non-zero singular values of
\mathbf{X}
.
Moreover, denoting the non-zero singular values of
\mathbf{Y}
as
\{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{Y})\}_{i=1}^{m}
, the optimal value of (
7
) is

\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{*}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right).

(6)

The complete lemma (with the case
m>d
), and the derivation, are provided in App.
B
.
Using lemma
4.2
, the approximation error of
LPLRFactorize
is analyzed in App.
C.3
.
Specifically, lemma
C.3
shows that for any input matrix
\mathbf{A}
, Alg.
2
with suitably chosen
\mathrm{B}_{\rm L}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}
, ensures that
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{A})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
, as in (
3
), can be upper bounded by twice the sum of squared trailing singular values, (ref. (
6
)).
While proving lemma
C.3
, it is assumed that if
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
or
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
gets saturated, a trivial output of
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{0},\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}
is returned.
Therefore, lemmas
C.1
and
C.2
specify choosing the dynamic ranges
\mathrm{R}_{\rm R}
and
\mathrm{R}_{\rm L}
to be sufficiently high so that saturation happens with a very low probability.
The proof of Thm.
4.1
is completed by using the LDL decomposition of
m\mathbf{H}
as proposed in
[
3
]
, along with an application of Marchenko-Pastur approximation to bound the expected eigenvalues of the quantization error, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}\right)
, yielding the final inequality.

6
Conclusions

In this work, the problem of obtaining a low-precision and low-rank decomposition of an LLM weight matrix was considered.
A
\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}
decomposition efficiently captures the high singular components of the weight matrix with sufficient fidelity, while coarsely compressing the less significant moderate-to-low singular components.
An optimization-theoretically motivated algorithm was proposed to obtain this decomposition, which iteratively optimized the quantized backbone
\mathbf{Q}
and the low-rank factors
\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}
.
Additionally, it was shown that
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
can be efficiently fine-tuned using low-rank adaptation to boost the zero-shot performance of the quantized model.
By utilizing a rank-constrained regression framework, an upper bound was established on the approximation error of the algorithm, and it was shown that this upper bound can be significantly smaller than prior bounds in the literature.
Finally, the proposed method was empirically evaluated by compressing the LlaMA family of LLMs in the challenging sub-
2.5
bits per parameter regime.
The proposed approach can also be used to complement existing compression strategies; thereby making it efficient to distribute compressed LLMs and deploy them on regular consumer hardware, making them more accessible to researchers.

Appendix B
Rank-constrained Regression

Recall that the submatrix formed by the first
m
columns of the identity matrix
\mathbf{I}
is denoted as
\mathbf{I}_{m}
, and let
\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m}
be the submatrix formed by the last
m
columns.
The dimension of
\mathbf{I}
is inferred depending on context.
Consider the following:

\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}.

(7)

Although this problem is non-convex, it can be solved to global optimality via two SVDs.
The following lemma characterizes the solution the rank-constrained regression in (
5
).

Lemma B.1
.

(Global optimality of rank-constrained regression)

Suppose
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is given, and suppose
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is full rank, i.e.,
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{X})=\min\{m,d\}
, with SVD,
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
.
Furthermore, let
\grave{\mathbf{U}}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
denote the full SVD of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
if
m\leq d
, or the full SVD of
\left(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{I}_{d}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
if
m>d
.
Then, for
k\leq m
, the solution of (
5
) is given by

\mathbf{Z}_{*}:=\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmin}_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\begin{cases}\left(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{m}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right)\quad\text{if}\quad m\leq d,\\
\left(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\right)\hskip 2.15277pt\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(8)

Here,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is a diagonal matrix of the non-zero singular values of
\mathbf{X}
, defined as
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{I}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
when
m\leq d
, and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
when
d<m
.
Additionally, the optimal value is

\displaystyle\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{*}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\begin{cases}\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),\quad\hskip 58.12482pt\text{if}\quad m\leq d,\\
\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left((\mathbf{U}\mathbf{I}_{d})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right)+\left\lVert(\mathbf{U}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2},\quad\;\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(9)

Proof.

Case
\mathbf{m\leq d}
:

Since the full SVD of
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
, where
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, and
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the last
(d-m)
columns of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}
will be zero, i.e.,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d}=\mathbf{0}
.
Let
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be the transformed optimization variable.
Since
\mathbf{V}
is a unitary matrix,
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k
if and only if
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k
.
Splitting
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
into
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime\prime}:=\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{d-m}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-m)\times n}
, it can be seen that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{I}_{d}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d}\overline{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime\prime}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{I}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is a diagonal matrix comprised of the non-zero singular values of
\mathbf{X}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}.

(10)

Note that the objective function value is independent of
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
, as
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
lies in the null space of
\mathbf{X}^{\top}
, and
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime})\leq k
follows from the fact that rank of a submatrix cannot exceed the full matrix.
Since
\mathbf{X}
is full rank,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is invertible, and the minimization in (
10
) is equivalent to

\min_{{\rm rank}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\leq k}\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),

(11)

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
.
The equality in (
11
) follows as a consequence of Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem (lemma
G.1
), which states that
\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
is minimized by taking the best rank-
k
approximation of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
, and the fact that the singular values of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
and
\mathbf{Y}
are the same as
\mathbf{U}
is unitary.
Moreover, as the SVD of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
is
\grave{\mathbf{U}}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{*}:=\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmin}_{{\rm rank}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\leq k}\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\left(\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right)

and,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{*}:=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{*}=\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right).

(12)

In other words, if
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*}
denotes the solution of the middle optimization problem in (
10
),
\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{*}
.
Furthermore, note that setting
\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{d-m}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{*}=\mathbf{0}
ensures
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*})={\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{*})\leq k
, while keeping the objective value in (
10
) unchanged.
Hence, an optimal solution is given by

\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*}=\begin{bmatrix}\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right)\\
\mathbf{0}_{(d-m)\times n}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and, }\quad\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\mathbf{V}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*}=\left(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{m}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right).

(13)

Case
\mathbf{m>d}
:

Recalling that the full SVD of
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
, where
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, and
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, in this case, the last
m-d
rows of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}
will be zero, i.e.,
\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}=\mathbf{0}
.
Denote
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}
.
This time, as
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}
,

\displaystyle\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}+\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}+\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}\sum_{i=k+1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left((\mathbf{U}\mathbf{I}_{d})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right)+\left\lVert(\mathbf{U}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2},

(14)

where
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}:=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}
.
Note that the term
\left\lVert(\mathbf{U}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-d})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
is the irreducible error, and
\rm(i)
is, once again, a consequence of the fact that
\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
is minimized by taking the best rank-
k
approximation of
\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
.
Moreover, since the SVD of
\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
is
\grave{\mathbf{U}}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{*}:=\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmin}_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{I}_{d}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\left(\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{*}=\left(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\grave{\mathbf{U}}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\right).

(15)

∎

Remark
: It is not necessary for
\mathbf{X}
to be full rank.
An equivalent result can be derived with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\dagger}
.

Computational complexity of rank-constrained regression
:
Arriving at the globally optimal solution in lemma
B.1
requires computing two SVDs, namely
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, which entails a complexity of
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}dm^{2}})
, and
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, with a complexity of
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}nm^{2}})
.
Hence, the total computational complexity is
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}m^{2}(n+d)})
.

C.1
Update Equations for Low-Rank Factors in
LPLRFactorize
submodule

As discussed in §
3
, the left and right low-rank factors in the
LPLRFactorize
sub-module (Alg.
2
) are found by solving least squares minimization problem.
The closed form expressions can be obtained by solving the normal equations directly.
In what follows, the same expressions are also derived explicitly via the singular value decomposition, as it gives an expression for the error (for example, refer to (
17
)) – which consists of an additive and irreducible error term.

Updating
\mathbf{L}
with fixed
\mathbf{R}
:
For a fixed
\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
, the left low rank factor is computed in lines
5
and
9
of Alg.
2
by solving

\min_{\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2},\;\text{where}\;\mathbf{Y}:=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}.

(16)

Let
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}
be the full SVD of
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
, and
\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
Recall from App.
B
that
\mathbf{I}
denotes the submatrix formed by the first
m
columns of the identity matrix
\mathbf{I}_{m}
, and
\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m}
is the submatrix formed by the last
m
columns.
Denoting
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}:=\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}
and
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}
, since
\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-k}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}=\mathbf{0}
, it can be seen that

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}}\left\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-43.05542pt=\left\lVert(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{m-k})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}+\min_{\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}.

(17)

The last term of (
17
) is minimized by setting
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\leftarrow\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*}:=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
.
Since
\mathbf{Z}_{*}^{\prime}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}^{\top}\grave{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}
, this yields the left low rank factor to be

\grave{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\implies\grave{\mathbf{L}}=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}^{\top})(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{X}^{\top})^{\dagger}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top})^{-1},

(18)

where
\rm(i)
follows from the explicit expression for the pseudoinverse of the wide matrix
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
.

Updating
\mathbf{R}
with fixed
\mathbf{L}
:
For a fixed
\mathbf{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
, the right low-rank factor is computed in lines
4
and
8
of Alg.
2
by solving

\min_{\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}}\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{L}^{\top}-\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2},\;\text{where}\;\mathbf{Y}:=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}.

(19)

Let
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}
be the full SVD of
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, and
\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Then, denoting
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{L}^{\top}
, the minimization (
19
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}}\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}-\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\equiv\min_{\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}}\lVert\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2},\;\text{where}\;\mathbf{Z}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}.

(20)

Note that (
20
) is an undetermined linear system with multiple solutions.
In particular, since
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, the last
(d-m)
columns of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}
consist of zeros, i.e.,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\overline{\mathbf{I}}_{d-m}=\mathbf{0}
.
Therefore, a solution of this system is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{*}^{\prime\prime}=\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\quad\text{or,}\quad\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{*}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle\quad\text{or,}\quad\mathbf{Z}_{*}^{\prime}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle\quad\text{or,}\quad\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{L}^{\top})^{\dagger}.

(21)

This implies
\grave{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger})^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}
.

The proof is completed by noting that
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger})^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}
, because,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\left(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{\rm X}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm X}^{\dagger}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{\rm X}^{\top}.

(22)

C.2
Dynamic Ranges of Quantizers

Lemmas
C.1
and
C.2
, stated in this section provide sufficient conditions to ensure that the quantizers
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
and
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
, used to quantize the left and right low-rank factors in the
LPLRFactorize
submodule, remain unsaturated.

Lemma C.1
.

(Dynamic range of right quantizer)
Given matrices
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, let
\sigma_{\rm max}
denote the maximum singular value of
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
.
Then, the quantizer
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
remains unsaturated if the dynamic range is chosen to be
\mathrm{R}_{\rm R}=\sigma_{\rm max}
.

Proof.

Note that the input to the right quantizer
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}
satisfies

\lVert\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm max}\leq\lVert\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\rVert_{2}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Since the input to
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
is dependent on the output of
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
, the following lemma
C.2
provides an upper bound on the input to
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
, provided that
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
was unsaturated.

Lemma C.2
.

(Dynamic range of left quantizer)

Given matrices
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, let
\lambda_{\rm min}
denote the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
, and let
\sigma_{\rm max}
and
\sigma_{k}
denote the largest and the
k^{\rm th}
singular values of
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
, respectively.
For some small
\epsilon>0
and an absolute constant
C
, suppose the number of bits for the right quantizer
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm R}
satisfies

\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}\geq\log_{2}\left(\frac{4C\sigma_{\rm max}}{\sigma_{k}\log 2}\left(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{8\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)}\right)\right).

(23)

Then, if the dynamic range of
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
is chosen to be

\mathrm{R}_{\rm L}=\frac{2\sigma_{\rm max}}{\sigma_{k}\sqrt{m\lambda_{\rm min}}},

(24)

then
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
remains unsaturated with probability exceeding
1-0.25\;\epsilon\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{-2}
.

Proof.

In Line
5
of Alg.
2
, the input to
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm L}
is
\grave{\mathbf{L}}_{0}=\left(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right)\left(\mathbf{R}_{0}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}
.
In the rest of the proof, the subscript
0
in
\grave{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
and
\grave{\mathbf{R}}_{0}
is dropped for brevity.
Recall the notation for the full SVD of
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
, and
\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
From Eq. (
18
),
\grave{\mathbf{L}}
can be expressed as
\grave{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
.
Consequently,
\lVert\grave{\mathbf{L}}\rVert_{\rm max}
is upper bounded as

\displaystyle\lVert\grave{\mathbf{L}}\rVert_{\rm max}\leq\lVert\grave{\mathbf{L}}\rVert_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{\dagger}\left(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(ii)}}{{\leq}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(iii)}}{{\leq}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right\rVert_{2}.

(25)

Here,
\rm(i)
holds because
\mathbf{V}_{\rm XR}
is a unitary matrix,
\rm(ii)
follows from submultiplicativity of spectral norm, and
\rm(iii)
follows from the fact that
\left(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{\rm XR}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)^{\top}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{I}
and lemma
G.4
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}=\sigma^{-1}_{\rm min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{\leq}}\left(\left(m\lambda_{\rm min}\right)^{1/2}\sigma_{\rm min}(\mathbf{R})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(ii)}}{{\leq}}(m\lambda_{\rm min})^{-1/2}\left(\sigma_{\rm min}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right)-\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{\rm R}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{-1},

(26)

where
\sigma_{\rm min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\rm XR})
is the smallest non-zero singular value of
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^{\top}
,
\rm(i)
follows from lemma
G.4
and
\rm(ii)
follows from lemma
G.5
.
Here,
\mathbf{E}_{\rm R}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
is the quantization error from quantizing right low-rank factor, and consist of unbiased random variables with bounded variance, as described in lemma
G.6
.
Since
\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}
contains the singular values of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm min}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\grave{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}\sigma_{\rm min}(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}})=\sigma_{k}(\grave{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}})=\sigma_{k}\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(ii)}}{{=}}\sigma_{k}\left(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\right)

(27)

where
\rm(i)
and
\rm(ii)
follow because
\grave{\mathbf{V}}

Since
\mathbf{Q}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{\sc LDLQ}}(\mathbf{W},\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q})
is the
LDLQ
quantizer of
Chee et al. [3]
(or,
BlockLDLQ
quantizer of
Tseng et al. [36]
, which is a successor of
LDLQ
proposed by
Chee et al. [3]
), it is shown that

\mathbf{Q}=\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}(\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{M}),

where
\mathbf{M}
is a strictly upper triangular matrix.
This is a consequence of the fact that
LDLQ
quantizes one column at a time, while simultaneously incorporating a linear feedback from the already quantized columns.
Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}(\mathbf{W}+(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{M})-(\mathbf{W}+(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{M})
denotes the quantization error, then it can be seen that

\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{W}=\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}

(48)

Moreover, since
m\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sigma_{i}^{2}\left((\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}\left((\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{W})^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{\top}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{-\top}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}),

(49)

where
\rm(i)
follows from (
48
).
From (
47
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\lVert(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leq\sum_{i>k}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top})+\epsilon.

(50)

Since
\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{jj}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}^{\top}
, where
D_{jj}
denotes the
j^{\rm th}
diagonal entry of
\mathbf{D}
and
\eta_{j}
is the
j^{\rm th}
column of
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,

\lambda_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}\right)=\lambda_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{jj}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}^{\top}\right)\leq d\;\left({\rm max}_{j}D_{jj}\right)\;\lambda_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}^{\top}\right).

(51)

Note that
\boldsymbol{\eta}
consists of quantization errors, which, for uniformly dithered scalar quantization, are zero mean random variables with bounded variance upper bounded by
\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}=\frac{\mathrm{R}^{2}}{(2^{\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}}-1)^{2}}
.
As the exact error distribution for non-subtractively dithered quantization (which is commonly used in practice), is not fully understood, a simplification is made for easier analysis, assuming the quantizer is subtractively dithered.
For subtractively dithered quantizers, if Schuchman’s conditions are met
[
32
,
11
]
, this results in a uniform error distribution in the interval
\left[-\frac{\Delta}{2},\frac{\Delta}{2}\right]
.
Therefore, assuming quantization errors are independent and identically distributed, for large dimensions
d
, the eigenvalues of
\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}^{\top}
are distributed according to the Marchenko-Pastur distribution, given by
{\rm f}_{\rm mp}(x):=\frac{2d}{\pi n\Delta^{2}x}\sqrt{\left(\lambda_{+}-x\right)\left(x-\lambda_{-}\right)}
, where
\lambda_{\pm}:=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}\left(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}\right)^{2}
.
Furthermore, denoting
q:=\mathrm{F}_{\rm mp}^{-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)
, where
\mathrm{F}_{mp}^{-1}
is the inverse cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
{\rm f}_{\rm mp}(x)
, it can be seen that

\displaystyle\sum_{i>k}\mathbb{E}\lambda_{i}\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\approx(n-k)\int_{\lambda_{-}}^{q}\frac{x}{2\pi(n/d)(\Delta^{2}/4)}\frac{\sqrt{\left(\lambda_{+}-x\right)\left(x-\lambda_{-}\right)}}{x}dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\sqrt{nd}}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\left(\mathrm{F}_{\rm mp}^{-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)-\lambda_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(ii)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\left(n-\frac{k}{2}\right).

(52)

Here,
\approx
denotes that the equaility holds asymptotically,
\rm(i)
follows from upper bounding the integral using the fact that
\left(\lambda_{+}-x\right)\left(x-\lambda_{-}\right)
is maximized at
x=\frac{\lambda_{+}+\lambda_{-}}{2}
.
Furthermore,
\rm(ii)
follows after substituting the values of
\lambda_{\pm}
and using the following linear upper bound on the inverse CDF of Marchenko-Pastur distribution,

\mathrm{F}_{\rm mp}^{-1}(x)\leq\lambda_{+}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}{2}\right)(1-x).

(53)

It can be verified by inspection that
\mathrm{F}_{\rm mp}(x)
is lower bounded by
\left(\frac{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}{2}\right)x-\left(\frac{\lambda_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{-}^{2}}{4}\right)
, and inequality (
53
) is a consequence of this fact.
Furthermore, from lemma
G.3
,
{\rm max}_{j}D_{jj}\leq\lambda_{\rm max}
.
Substituting (
52
) in (
51
), and substituting
\Delta^{2}=\frac{4\mathrm{R}^{2}}{(2^{\mathrm{B}_{\rm Q}}-1)^{2}}
, completes the proof.
∎

Informal version of Thm.
C.4
:
Consider
n\approx d
.
From Thm.
C.4
, a (simplified) asymptotic dependence on the bit-budgets
\mathrm{B}_{\rm L}
and
\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}
can be obtained.
In what follows, constant factors inside the
\log_{2}\left(\cdot\right)
have been ignored.
Comparing the expressions for
\mathrm{B}_{1}
and
\mathrm{B}_{2}
, it can be seen that the desired bit-budgets are

\mathrm{B}_{\rm L}\geq\log_{2}\left(4\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{k}}\sqrt{\frac{nk}{\epsilon}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}}{\lambda_{\rm min}}}\right)\quad\text{and, }\quad\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}\geq\log_{2}\left(2\sigma_{2}\sqrt{\frac{kd}{\epsilon}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}}{\lambda_{\rm min}}}\right).

For

, this yields an average bit-budget of

\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{\rm L}+\mathrm{B}_{\rm R}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{8k}{\epsilon}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{3}}{\sigma_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}}{\lambda_{\rm min}}\sqrt{nd}\right)\;\text{bits per parameter.}

C.5
A simplification for positive definite Hessians

The expressions in (
8
) are a little involved, but they can be simplified if
\mathbf{H}
is positive definite, i.e., all eigenvalues are strictly greater than
0
.
Let
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^{\top}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\mathbf{H}
, and let
\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{U}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{\top}
be its symmetric square root.
Furthermore assume that
\mathbf{H}
is positive definite, i.e., all diagonal entries of
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
are strictly positive.
In practice, this can be ensured by regularizing
\mathbf{H}
through the addition of an identity matrix, scaled by a small amount.

Then, consider the following equivalent optimization problems:

\displaystyle\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{X}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}{\rm Tr}\left({(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{Z})^{\top}}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\qquad\text{(where }\;\mathbf{Y}\triangleq\mathbf{A}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\equiv\min_{{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k}\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

(54)

Here,
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\triangleq\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and the constraint
{\rm rank}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})\leq k
follow from the fact that multiplying
\mathbf{Z}
by an invertible matrix,
\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, keeps its rank unchanged.
The final optimization problem can be solved optimally by considering the SVD of
\mathbf{Y}
as
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
, and the optimal solution is

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}_{*}=\left(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\right),

(55)

where
\mathbf{I}_{k}
denotes the first
k
columns of the identity matrix.
So the final solution is given by,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{*}=\left(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\right).

(56)

This yields a closed form expression for the optimal solution of the rank-constrained regression problem for the case of positive definite Hessians.

Complexity of pre-preprocessing
:
For a given calibration dataset/activation
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, the Hessian is computed as
\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
, which requires
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}md^{2}})
compute.
Subsequently, the LDL decomposition of
\mathbf{H}
is computed, which entails
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}d^{3}})
complexity
[
20
]
.
Moreover,
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}
, which is used in the update equation of
\grave{\mathbf{R}}
is also computed beforehand, with a complexity of
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}d^{3}})
.
therefore, the total complexity of pre-processing is
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}md^{2}+2d^{3}})
.

Complexity of
LDLQ
:
Each iteration of
C
alibration
A
ware
L
ow-Precision
DE
composition with Low-
R
ank
A
daptation invokes a single call to
LDLQ
.
An
LDLQ
call involves updating each column of an
n\times d
iteratively.
From (
2
), updating the
k^{\rm th}
column requires multiplying a matrix (of already quantized columns) in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times(k-1)}
with a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
, implying a complexity of
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n(k-1)})
.
Subsequently, if
\mathrm{Q}_{\rm Q}
is uniform scalar quantization, quantizing the (feedback incorporated)
k^{\rm th}
column entails
\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n})
compute, implying a total of
\mathrm{O}(nk)
compute for the
k^{\rm th}
column.
Hence, quantizing all columns from
k=1,\ldots,n
would require
\mathrm{O}\left(n\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\right)=\mathrm{O}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2}+\frac{3n}{2}\right)=\mathrm{O}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}n^{2}})
compute.

Complexity of
LPLRFactorize
:
Every iteration of
C
alibration
A
ware
L
ow-Precision
DE
composition with Low-
R
ank
A
daptation invokes a call to
LPLRFactorize
, which itself consists of
\rm T_{in}
inner iterations.
Each inner iteration of
LPLRFactorize
, consists of initializing the left and right low rank factors using rank-constrained regression.
As seen in §
B
, it has a computational complexity of
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}m^{2}(n+d)}\right)
.
Subsequently, for any matrix
\mathbf{A}
, the left low rank factor as
\grave{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top})^{-1}
can be found using successive matrix multiplications.
While a gradient descent based algorithm can possibly speed up this computation, in implementation, closed form expressions are computed directly as they are computationally affordable for the hidden dimensions of the LLMs considered.
Computing
\mathbf{A}\left(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top}\right)
requires
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}dk(n+d)}\right)
, computing
(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{R}^{\top})^{-1}
entails
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}dk(k+d)+k^{3}}\right)
, and multiplying them together requires
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}nk^{2}}\right)
.
Keeping the dominant term, the aggregated computational complexity for computing the left low-rank factor in each inner iteration is
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}ndk}\right)
.

Additionally, the left low-rank factor can be computed as
\grave{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger})
, where computing
\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}
is
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}ndk}\right)
.
Since
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}
is already computed beforehand, multiplying
\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}
entails
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}kd^{2}}\right)
.
Once again, keeping the dominant term, the complexity is
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}ndk}\right)
.
Hence, the total complexity of each inner iteration of
LPLRFactorize
is
\mathrm{O}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.55}ndk}\right)
.

Moreover, SVD computations can be sped up with randomized SVD

Computation of Average Bit Budget Per-Parameter

Assume that the dimensions of the seven weight matrices in each transformer layer have dimensions

, where
i\in\{1,\dots,7\}
.
Also, for the models we consider, each transformer layer has matrices of the same dimensions.
In general, if the full-rank matrix
\mathbf{Q}
has a bit budget of
B_{\text{Q}}
, the factors
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
both have a bit budget of
B_{\text{LR}}
, and the rank of the factors is
k
, the average number of bits per parameter is

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left(B_{\text{Q}}n_{i}d_{i}+kB_{\text{LR}}(n_{i}+d_{i})\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{7}n_{i}d_{i}}.

For LLaMa-2 7B, all self-attention matrices are
4096\times 4096
, the
gate
and
up
projections are
11008\times 4096
, and the
down
projection is
4096\times 11008
.
So,
(n_{i},d_{i})=(4096,4096)
for
i\in\{1,\dots,4\}
,
(n_{5},d_{5})=(n_{6},d_{6})=(11008,4096)
, and
(n_{7},d_{7})=(4096,11008)
.

For LLaMa-3 8B,
(n_{1},d_{1})=(n_{4},d_{4})=(4096,4096)
,
(n_{2},d_{2})=(n_{3},d_{3})=(4096,1024)
,
(n_{5},d_{5})=(n_{6},d_{6})=(14336,4096)
, and
(n_{7},d_{7})=(4096,14336)
.

In §
5.3
,
r=64
of the
k
factors are fine-tuned in 16-bit precision.
This results in the following number of bits per parameter:

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left(B_{\text{Q}}n_{i}d_{i}+(k-r)B_{\text{LR}}(n_{i}+d_{i})+16r(n_{i}+d_{i})\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{7}n_{i}d_{i}}.

Appendix G
Auxiliary Results

G.1
Useful Results from Linear Algebra and Probability

This section states some useful linear algebra results as lemmas.
Some of them are stated without proof, which can be easily proved or found in a linear algebra textbook such as
[
10
]
.

Lemma G.1
.

(Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem)

For any matrix
\mathbf{A}
with SVD given by
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
, the solution of
\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmin}_{{\rm rank}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}})\leq k}\lVert\mathbf{A}-\widehat{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}
is given by
\mathbf{A}_{k}=\left(\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\right)
, and
\lVert\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}_{k}\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}=\sum_{i>k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(\mathbf{A})
.

Lemma G.2
.

(
Vershynin [38, Thm 4.4.5]
)

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a
d\times m
random matrix whose entries
X_{ij}
are independent, zero-mean, subgaussian random variables.
Then, for any
t>0
,
\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\right\rVert_{2}\leq CK\left(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{m}+t\right)

with probability exceeding
1-2e^{-t^{2}}
, where
K=\max_{i,j}\left\lVert A_{ij}\right\rVert_{\psi_{2}}
, and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\psi_{2}}
denotes the subgaussian norm, and
C
is an absolute constant.

Remark
:
Lemma
G.2
states a high probability upper bound on the spectral norm of a random matrix in terms of the subgaussian norm of the entries of the matrix.
The subgaussian norm of a subgaussian random variable
X
is defined as
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\psi_{2}}\triangleq\inf\{t\geq 0\;|\;\mathbb{E}[e^{X^{2}/t^{2}}]\leq 2\}
.
It can be shown that any bounded random variable
X
is subgaussian, and satisfies
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\log 2}
.

Lemma G.3
.

Suppose the LDL decomposition of a matrix
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is given by
\mathbf{H}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{\top}
, where
\mathbf{M}
is strictly upper triangular and
\mathbf{D}
is diagonal.
Then,
{\rm max}_{j}D_{jj}\leq\lambda_{\rm max}
, where
\lambda_{\rm max}
denotes the largest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{e}_{j}
denote the
j^{\rm th}
canonical basis vector.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{e}_{j}=\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{I})^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(i)}}{{=}}D_{jj}+\sum_{i>j}M_{ji}^{2}.

(58)

Moreover, from properties of eigenvalues,
\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq\lambda_{\rm max}
.
Therefore, for any
j
,
D_{jj}\leq D_{jj}+\sum_{i>j}M_{ji}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\rm max}
.
Since it holds true for all
j
, this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma G.4
.

(Loewner ordering for matrix products)

For any matrix
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
,

\sigma_{\rm min}^{2}(\mathbf{A})\;\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\preccurlyeq\sigma_{\rm max}^{2}(\mathbf{A})\;\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B}.

Lemma G.5
.

(Minimum singular value)

For matrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
,
\sigma_{\rm min}(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})\geq\sigma_{\rm min}(\mathbf{A})-\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{2}.

G.2
Uniformly dithered scalar quantizer

Let us consider quantizing a scalar
x
with
|x|\leq\mathrm{R}
.
Given
\mathrm{B}
bits, the scalar quantizer with
dynamic range

\mathrm{R}
is described by first specifying the
M=2^{\mathrm{B}}
quantization points

q_{1}=-\mathrm{R},q_{2}=-\mathrm{R}+\Delta,q_{3}=-\mathrm{R}+2\Delta,\ldots,q_{M}=-\mathrm{R}+(M-1)\Delta,

where
\Delta=\frac{2\mathrm{R}}{M-1}
is the resolution.
The quantizer operation is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(x)=\begin{cases}q_{k+1}\;\;\text{with probability}\;\;r,\\
\hskip 5.69054ptq_{k}\;\;\hskip 5.69054pt\text{with probability}\;\;1-r,\end{cases}

(59)

where
k=\mathop{\rm arg\hskip 2.84526ptmax}_{j}\{q_{j}\leq x\}
, i.e.,
x\in[q_{k},q_{k+1})
, and
r=\frac{x-q_{k}}{\Delta}
.
As shown in the following lemma
G.6
, such a quantizer satisfies

\mathbb{E}\;[\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(x)]=x\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(x)-x\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}=\frac{{\rm R}^{2}}{\left(2^{\rm B}-1\right)^{2}},

(60)

i.e., it is unbiased and the error variance depends on
\mathrm{R}
and
\mathrm{B}
.
Here, the
\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
is over the randomness from dithering in (
59
).
If the input
x
to the quantizer falls outside this range, i.e.,
x>{\rm R}
or
x<-{\rm R}
, the quantizer is said to be
saturated
.
To quantize any matrix
\mathbf{X}
,
\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{X})
is obtained by quantizing each entry independently, i.e.,
\left[\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{X})\right]_{ij}\triangleq\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(X_{ij})
.

Lemma G.6
.

For scalar
x
with
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\mathrm{R}
, denote the quantization error of uniformly dithered
\mathrm{B}
–bit scalar quantizer as
\epsilon=\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(x)-x
.
Then,
\mathbb{E}[\epsilon]=0
and
\textrm{Var}\left(\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{\mathrm{R}^{2}}{\left(2^{\mathrm{B}}-1\right)^{2}}
, where the expectation
\mathbb{E}
is over the randomness due to dithering in the quantizer operation.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in[q_{k},q_{k+1})
and
q_{k+1}=q_{k}+\Delta
, where
\Delta=\frac{2\mathrm{R}}{2^{\mathrm{B}}-1}
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}\;{\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}}(x)=q_{k+1}\;\frac{x-q_{k}}{\Delta}+q_{k}\;\left(1-\frac{x-q_{k}}{\Delta}\right)=\frac{\left(q_{k}+\Delta\right)\left(x-q_{k}\right)+q_{k}\left(\Delta-x+q_{k}\right)}{\Delta}=x.

Furthermore, the variance can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}\left(\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{R},\mathrm{B}}(x)-x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(q_{k+1}-x)^{2}\frac{(x-q_{k})}{\Delta}+(q_{k}-x)^{2}\left(1-\frac{x-q_{k}}{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(q_{k+1}-x\right)\left(x-q_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in[q_{k},q_{k+1})}\left(q_{k+1}-x\right)\left(x-q_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(q_{k+1}-\frac{q_{k}+q_{k+1}}{2}\right)\left(\frac{q_{k}+q_{k+1}}{2}-q_{k}\right)=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}=\frac{\mathrm{R}^{2}}{\left(2^{\mathrm{B}}-1\right)^{2}}.

∎

When the input
x
to the quantizer exceeds
\mathrm{R}
, the quantizer is said to be
saturated
, causing the quantization error to deviate from zero mean and bounded variance.
Thus, it is crucial to ensure that the quantizer operates within the
unsaturated
regime with high probability.

It does so by providing an additional degree of freedom to control the compression ratio – namely, the target rank

Theoretical guarantees show improved performance over existing benchmarks, when the matrix being compressed is inherently low-rank to begin with – something that is observed in LLMs.

.

Broader Impacts
: Compressing models enables their deployment in resource-constrained settings, facilitating educational and technological advancements with limited infrastructure.
Deploying on edge devices for inference also enhances privacy by reducing the need for data to be sent to centralized servers for inference, thereby enhancing user privacy and data security.
Additionally, lower computational requirements of inference using compressed models is a step towards adoption of environment-friendly AI strategies.

On the other hand, as the compression is often not always lossless, any technique may result in reduced accuracy or loss of nuanced understanding, potentially impacting the reliability of the models in critical applications.
Conseuqently, due diligence should be exercised when deploying these models.
From a broader perspective, LLMs are quite powerful, and their easier deployment can possibly lead to misuse, such as the spread of misinformation or automated generation of harmful content.
Consequently, robust regulatory frameworks are necessary, as LLMs continue becoming more accessible to the general audience.

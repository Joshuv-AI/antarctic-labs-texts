Abstract
. Previously we observed that newforms obey a strict bias towards root number + 1 +1 in squarefree levels: at least half of the newforms in S k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) ) S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N)) with root number + 1 +1 for N N squarefree, and it is strictly more than half outside of a few special cases. Subsequently, other authors treated levels which are cubes of squarefree numbers. Here we treat arbitrary levels, and find that if the level is not the square of a squarefree number, this strict bias still holds for any weight. In fact the number of such exceptional levels is finite for fixed weight, and 0 if k < 12 k<12 . We also investigate some variants of this question to better understand the exceptional levels.



1.
Introduction

Throughout,
N
and
k
are positive integers with
k
even.
Let
S_{k}(N)=S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the space of weight
k
,
level
N
cusp forms, and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be the
new subspace. The root number of a newform

is the sign in the functional equation for

(-1)^{k/2}
times the
eigenvalue of the Fricke involution
W_{N}=\prod_{p|N}W_{p}

It is known that, on average, 50% of newforms have root number
+1

and 50% of newforms have root number
-1

the distribution of root numbers more precisely.

Let
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\pm}
denote the subspace spanned
by newforms with root number
\pm 1
. Set

In
[
11

implies that newforms obey a strict bias towards

+1
for squarefree levels

\Delta(N,k)\geq 0
for all

and squarefree

Further,
\Delta(N,k)
is an elementary expression in the class number
of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N})

N=2,3

and

(ii)
k=2
and
\dim S_{2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)=0
or

Subsquently, these results were extended to the case of

[
14

(

[
8

(

much more involved, and use Petersson and Jacquet–Zagier

Here we explicitly compute the trace of

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

If

is not the square of a squarefree number, or if

there is a strict bias of newforms toward root number
+1
,

\Delta(N,k)\geq 0
.

(2)

Suppose

square. For sufficiently large

\Delta(N,k)<0
if and only if

(-1)^{k/2}=-\mu(\sqrt{N})=-\prod_{p|N}(-1)
.

(3)

For a fixed

.

Moreover, in (1),
\Delta(N,k)

Precise conditions are given in

It is not too hard to make statements (2) and (3) effective. We
do not explicate this, but refer the interested reader to the proof of

(2).

As in the squarefree case, we in fact get an elementary expression for
the exact size of the bias, and it is essentially the class number
of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N})
. The precise formula for
\Delta(N,k)

breaks up into several cases according to whether

is 1, 2, 3 or 4
times the square of a squarefree number, or none of these

Here we just describe the formula in the generic case,
and refer to
3.2
for all cases.

Write
N=N_{1}N_{2}^{2}
, where
N_{1}
is squarefree.
Define
\beta(N)\in\{1,2,3,4\}
by

(1.1)

\beta(N)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }N_{1}\equiv 1,2\bmod 4,\text{ or }2\parallel N_{2};\\
2&\text{if }N_{1}\equiv 3\bmod 4\quad\text{and }4\mid N_{2};\\
3-{-N_{1}\overwithdelims( )2}&\text{if }N_{1}\equiv 3\bmod 4\quad\text{and }2\nmid N_{2}.\end{cases}

For a fundamental discriminant

be the
“unit group weighted” class number, i.e., one half of the number of integral
units in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
times the usual class number

.
Precisely, set
h^{\prime}(-4)=\frac{1}{2}

and
h^{\prime}(-3)=\frac{1}{3}
, and take

usual class number for

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose

,
2
,

In particular, we always have

. Moreover,

.

Note that if

.

1.1.
Remarks on proof

The proof has in essence two steps.
The quantity
\Delta(N,k)
equals the trace of
(-1)^{k/2}W_{N}

on
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
.
First, we use a trace formula due to Yamauchi
[
17
]
and
Skoruppa–Zagier
[
15
]

which expresses the trace of
W_{N}
on
S_{k}(N)

as an alternating sum of class numbers.
Using class number
relations and elementary but careful analysis, we rewrite
this trace in terms of a single weighted class number
h^{\prime}(-D)

(see
3.1
, which can also be derived from the recent work

[
18
]
).
Second, one can express the trace of
W_{N}
on the new subspace
as an alternating sum over
M^{2}\mid N
of traces on
S_{k}(N/M^{2})
.
Once again, we use class number relations and elementary analysis
to obtain our formula for
\Delta(N,k)
(see
3.2
).

We remark that our approach is much more straightforward
and simple than the approaches taken in
[
14
]
and
[
8
]
.
In
[
11
]
, we restricted to squarefree levels because
our main focus in that paper was dimension formulas with prescribed
local signs at multiple ramified places, and that problem seems
considerably more complicated for non-squarefree levels. The result
therein about root number bias for squarefree levels was merely
a curious observation we made along the way, which was already
immediate from the Yamauchi/Skoruppa–Zagier trace formulas
(though, to our knowledge,
it had not been noticed before). For squarefree levels, both steps
in our present proof are trivial because (i) the trace formula for

W_{N}
on
S_{k}(N)
is very simple for squarefree
N

and (ii) there is no oldform contribution
to this trace that we need to subtract off.

1.2.
Further questions

1.1
naturally prompts 2 questions: (i) what is the
reason for this bias, and (ii) what is the reason for these exceptions.

We briefly speculated on (i) in
[
11
]
, and mentioned
two ideas that at least support the existence of a bias towards
root number
+1
. First, the “
L
-functions from nothing”
perspective suggests that
L
-functions in small weight and level
tend to have root number
+1
(e.g., see
[
11
, p. 10]
).
Second, when
k=2
, at least
for prime levels,
\Delta(p,2)\geq 0
is forced upon us by
the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence together with the
fact that the type number is at least
\frac{1}{2}
times the
class number for maximal orders in quaternion algebras
of prime discriminant.

Anna Medvedovsky has since suggested to me that one can
also reinterpret the
k=2
case for squarefree
N

by comparing the genus of
X_{0}(N)
with its quotient by the
Fricke involution.
One can note that the formulas for
\Delta(N,k)
are essentially
independent of
k
, and thus are controlled by the
k=2
case.
One heuristic reason for this latter fact is that the main term in
dimension formulas of spaces of modular forms is a product
of local factors, including one at infinity for the weight
k
.

Thus there are at least some hints for the above root number bias
that do not only rely on the trace formula, but
I do not have a compelling existential explanation for this bias.
On the other hand, if one just counts elliptic curves up to isogeny by conductor, i.e., weight 2 rational newforms, examining Cremona’s database (see
[
7
]
) shows there is at least an initial
bias towards root number
-1
.

Moving on to (ii), let me first explain how the exceptions to the
strict bias arise in the proof. First, the trace of
(-1)^{k/2}W_{N}

on
S_{k}(N)
is non-negative (see
3.1
). The only
question now is whether this still holds when we subtract off
the oldform contribution, i.e., if

(-1)^{k/2}\sum_{M^{2}|N}\mu(M)\tr W_{N/M^{2}}\geq 0
.
For a fixed
N
, the
the terms for each
M^{2}\mid N
are all similar looking
expressions in
h^{\prime}(-D)
that can be collected together,
except in the special case that
N
is a perfect square.
In that case, the term with
M=\sqrt{N}
is
\mu(M)
times the trace of

W_{N/M^{2}}=W_{1}=\mathrm{Id}

on
S_{k}(1)
, i.e.,
\mu(M)\dim S_{k}(1)
.
Since
\dim S_{k}(1)\simeq\frac{k}{12}
, we find that

\Delta(N,k)\approx\frac{1}{4}\phi(M)+(-1)^{k/2}\mu(M)\frac{k}{12}
.
This yields parts (2) and (3) of
1.1
.

One thing that is special about levels which are perfect squares
is that these are precisely the levels where the newspaces
are not generated by theta series attached to definite
quaternion algebras (see
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
).
In particular if the level
N=M^{2}
, where
M
is squarefree,
then
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
is generated by theta series together with
twists of forms of level 1 and level
M
(now including forms with
nebentypus).

The discussion of the proof above, combined with this consideration
about theta series, suggests that the reason
the strict bias towards root number
+1
does not persist
in (certain) square levels may be due to newforms which are twists from full
level.

In
Section

4
, we describe what happens if one removes
the contribution from twists of level 1 forms.
Specficially, denote by
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}
be the
subspace of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
generated by newforms which are
not twists from level
1
. Let
\Delta(N,k)^{\prime}
be the number of
newforms in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}
with root number
+1
minus the
number of newforms with root number
-1
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
M>1
be squarefree. The spaces
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(M^{2})^{\prime}
have a
strict bias towards root number
+1
, i.e.,
\Delta(M^{2},k)^{\prime}\geq 0
,
for all
k
if and only if

M
is odd and
M
has an odd number of prime factors which
are
3\bmod 4
. Otherwise, for sufficiently large
k
,
the sign of
\Delta(M^{2},k)^{\prime}
is
(-1)^{k/2}\mu(M)
.

Thus even though the proof of our theorems suggests the difference
in root number bias behavior for cubefree square levels comes from
level 1 forms, this difference does not always disappear when
we omit twists of level 1 forms. In fact, when
M
is even and
squarefree, there are no twists from level 1.

From this perspective, it is natural to ask
what happens if one excludes all newforms which are twists from
smaller levels, i.e., if one restricts to minimal newforms.
Since every newform in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(4)
is minimal, and
\Delta(4,k)<0

whenever
k\equiv 0\bmod 4
and
k\geq 12
, the sum of root numbers
over minimal newforms is not always positive.
We also exhibit examples of
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
such that

S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(p^{2})
has more minimal newforms with root number

-1
than
+1
.
On the other hand, we give a simple local condition
which forces root numbers to be
perfectly equidistributed for minimal forms in certain (not necessarily
square) levels.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 1
, and let
S_{k}^{\min}(N)
be the
subspace of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
generated by newforms which are not
twists from smaller levels. Suppose there exists
p^{2}\parallel N

such that
{-N/p^{2}\overwithdelims( )p}=1
. Then
\dim S_{k}^{\min}(N)^{+}=\dim S_{k}^{\min}(N)^{-}
, i.e., root numbers are perfectly balanced
for minimal newforms.

The congruence conditions on primes dividing
N

in these propositions arise from the way that local root
numbers behave under twisting by quadratic characters.

1.3.
Further remarks

As in
[
11
]
, our formula for
\Delta(N,k)
implies
an exact formula for the dimensions

\dim S^{\mathrm{new}}_{k}(N)^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim S^{\mathrm{new}}_{k}(N)\pm\Delta(N,k)\right).

We do not write down these dimension formulas explicitly,
but they immediately follow by comparing
with the explicit formula for
\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
in
[
9
]
.

Consequently, our formula for
\Delta(N,k)
implies
that root numbers of are
+1
(or
-1
) for 50% of newforms as

k+N\to\infty
. We are not aware of an explicit proof of this
fact in the literature for non-squarefree
N
, though proofs were
surely known to experts. In any case, our formulas together
with standard class number bounds yield equidistribution of root
numbers with a very good error estimate.

The original motivation for the dimension formulas for
newforms with prescribed Atkin–Lehner signs in
[
11
]

was to use them to obtain mod 2 congruences in
[
10
]
.
Namely, we showed that perfect equidistribution of Atkin–Lehner
sign patterns in squarefree levels essentially means that every newform
is mod 2 congruent to one with any desired Atkin–Lehner signs at those
places. It may be interesting to see whether the cases of perfect
equidistribution of root numbers in non-squarefree levels we give here
are similarly related to mod 2 congruences.

The work
[
8
]
estabishes biases of root numbers for Hilbert modular
forms for certain levels and base fields. One should be able to imitate

[
15
]
to derive a trace formula for Fricke, as well as Atkin–Lehner,
involutions of Hilbert modular forms. Then we expect that the strategy used
in the present paper (resp. the one in
[
11
]
)
should yield distributions of root numbers (resp. Atkin–Lehner sign patterns)
in quite general settings (resp. for squarefree levels).

As a check on our formulas, we compared them numerically
with newform and root number calculations in Sage

[
4
]
for various ranges of values of
(N,k)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout,
M
and
N
denote positive integers, and
k\geq 2

is even. Let
v_{p}(N)=\max\{r\in\mathbb{Z}:p^{r}\mid N\}
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by

the odd part of

,
i.e.,
N=2^{v_{2}(N)}N^{\mathrm{odd}}
. Let
\square\subset\mathbb{N}

be the multiplicative submonoid of squares. Let
\mu
be the
Möbius function.

Denote by
\tr W_{N}
the trace of
W_{N}
on the
full cusp space
S_{k}(N)
, and by
\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}
the trace on the
new subspace
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. These traces also depend on the
weight

, but we suppress it in our notation.

For a statement
*
,
we use
\delta_{*}
to mean 1 if the statement
*
is satisfied,
and
0
otherwise. E.g.,
\delta_{k=2}
represents the
Kronecker delta function
\delta_{k,2}
.

The following function arises in the trace formula for
W_{N}
.
For
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
set
p_{k}(s)=\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}

where
\rho,\bar{\rho}
are the roots of
X^{2}-sX+1
if

,
and set
p_{k}(2)=k-1
.

2.2.
Class number relations

Let
-D<0
be a discriminant, and
h(-D)
be the class number
of the quadratic order
\mathcal{O}_{D}
of discriminant
-D
. Let
h^{\prime}(-D)
be
the class number weighted by
[\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}:\mathbb{Z}^{\times}]^{-1}
, i.e.,

h^{\prime}(-D)=h(-D)
unless
D<5
, and then
h^{\prime}(-4)=\frac{1}{2}
and
h^{\prime}(-3)=\frac{1}{3}
.
Define the Hurwitz class number
H(-D)
to be
the number of positive definite binary quadratic
forms of discrimimant
\Delta
up to equivalence, where we
weight forms of discriminant
-4
and
-3
by
\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{3}
,
respectively. (The class numbers
h(-D)
and
h^{\prime}(-D)
can be interpreted
in terms of counting primitive forms.) We also set
H(0)=-\frac{1}{12}
.

Now suppose
-D
is a fundamental discriminant, and
\lambda>0
.
Then

(2.1)

h^{\prime}(-\lambda^{2}D)=\lambda\prod_{p|\lambda}(1-{-D\overwithdelims( )p}\frac{1}{p})\,h^{\prime}(-D)=\lambda\sum_{t|\lambda}\mu(t){-D\overwithdelims( )t}\frac{1}{t}\,h^{\prime}(-D).

(E.g., see
[
3
, Corollary 15.40]
.)

We can write the Hurwitz class number in terms of

by

(2.2)

H(-\lambda^{2}D)=\sum_{t|\lambda}h^{\prime}(-Dt^{2}).

Then

(2.3)

H(-\lambda^{2}D)=\sum_{m|\lambda}\sum_{t|m}\mu(t){-D\overwithdelims( )t}\frac{m}{t}h^{\prime}(-D)=\sum_{t|\lambda}\mu(t){-D\overwithdelims( )t}{\sigma(\lambda/t)}h^{\prime}(-D).

3.
Traces on newspace

Since,
\Delta(N,k)=(-1)^{k/2}\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}
we want to
compute
\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}
. A formula for the trace of
a product
T_{n}W_{M}
of Hecke and Atkin–Lehner operators
on
S_{k}(N)
, together with a relation for how the trace on the
newspace is related to traces on full cusp spaces was given
by Yamauchi
[
17
]
. Unfortunately, Yamauchi’s
paper contains some clerical errors. A corrected formulation
of these traces was later given by Skoruppa and Zagier

[
15
]
, and we will use their formulation.

We just need the case that
n=1
and
M=N
, but
some effort is still required to get from the Skoruppa–Zagier
formula to a relatively simple expression for

\Delta(N,k)
. We will first find a simple expression for

\tr W_{N}
, and then use it to get our desired formula for
\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}
.

As explained in
[
15
, pp. 132–133]
,
a newform
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(M)
with root number
(-1)^{k/2}w_{f}

contributes
w_{f}
to
\tr W_{N}
if
N/M\in\square
and

0
otherwise. Thus

\tr W_{N}=\sum_{M\mid N,\,N/M\in\square}\tr W_{M}^{\mathrm{new}},

and so by Möbius inversion,

(3.1)

\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}=\sum_{Q^{2}\mid N}\mu(Q)\tr W_{N/Q^{2}}.

Now a special case of
[
15
, (2.7)]
yields

(3.2)

\tr W_{N}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M|N,N/M\in\square}\mu(\sqrt{N/M})\sum_{s}I_{s}(k,M)-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{N\in\{1,4\}}+\delta_{k=2},

where

I_{s}(k,M)=p_{k}(s/\sqrt{M})H(s^{2}-4M),

and
0\leq s\leq 2\sqrt{M}
such that
\sqrt{MN}\mid s
.
The only way that
s>0
is possible then is if
N\leq 4
.
Note that
p_{k}(0)=(-1)^{(k-2)/2}
, so when

s=0
we get

I_{0}(k,M)=(-1)^{(k-2)/2}H(-4M).

3.1.
Small levels

We first deal with levels
N\leq 4
.

The Fricke involution is trivial if
N=1
. In particular we have

\tr W_{1}=\dim S_{k}(1)=\begin{cases}\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\rfloor&k\not\equiv 2\bmod 12\\
\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\rfloor-1+\delta_{k=2}&k\equiv 2\bmod 12.\\
\end{cases}

The cases
N=2,3
are included in the case of
N
squarefree
(e.g., see
[
11
, Thm 2.2]
). Explicitly, we have

\tr W_{2}=\tr W_{2}^{\mathrm{new}}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k/2}(1-\delta_{k=2})&k\equiv 0,2\bmod 8\\
0&k\equiv 4,6\bmod 8,\end{cases}

and

\tr W_{3}=\tr W_{3}^{\mathrm{new}}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k/2}(1-\delta_{k=2})&k\equiv 0,2,6,8\bmod 12\\
0&k\equiv 4,10\bmod 12.\end{cases}

If
N=4
, we have

\tr W_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left(I_{0}(k,1)+I_{2}(k,1)-I_{0}(k,4)-I_{4}(k,4)\right)-\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{k=2}

Note that
I_{0}(k,1)-I_{0}(k,4)=(-1)^{(k-2)/2}(H(-4)-H(-16))=(-1)^{k/2}
,
and
I_{2}(k,1)=p_{k}(2)H(0)=I_{4}(k,4)
. Thus

\tr W_{4}=\begin{cases}0&k\equiv 0\bmod 4\\
-1+\delta_{k=2}&k\equiv 2\bmod 4.\end{cases}

Then

\tr W_{4}^{\mathrm{new}}=\tr W_{4}-\tr W_{1}=\begin{cases}-\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\rfloor&k\equiv 0,2,4,8\bmod 12\\
-\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\rfloor-1&k\equiv 6,10\bmod 12.\end{cases}

3.2.
Generic levels: trace on full cusp space

Now suppose
N>4
. Then the only
s
-terms in (
3.2
)
are

, and we have

\tr W_{N}=(-1)^{k/2}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M|N,N/M\in\square}\mu(\sqrt{N/M})H(-4M)+\delta_{k=2}.

We can uniquely write
N=N_{1}N_{2}^{2}
where
N_{1}
is squarefree.
If
N/M
is a square, then we can write
M=N_{1}M_{2}^{2}

where
M_{2}\mid N_{2}
. Hence

\tr W_{N}=(-1)^{k/2}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M_{2}|N_{2}}\mu(N_{2}/M_{2})H(-4N_{1}M_{2}^{2})+\delta_{k=2}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
N>4
, and write
N=N_{1}N_{2}^{2}

Proof.

, there is an associated cuspidal automorphic
representation
\pi=\pi_{f}
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
We have a decomposition

\pi=\bigotimes_{p}\pi_{p}\otimes\pi_{\infty}
, where each

\pi_{p}
is an infinite-dimensional irreducible admissible
complex representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We say

\pi_{p}
is the local representation at
p
associated to
f
.
The local conductor
c(\pi_{p})
is the exponent
v_{p}(N)

in the level of
f
.

Assume now that
\psi
is trivial, i.e.,
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
.
Then
\pi
, and each
\pi_{p}
, has trivial central character.
Moreover,
\pi_{p}
is an unramified principal series representation
(i.e.,
c(\pi_{p})=0
) if and only if
p\nmid N
.
Also,
\pi_{p}
is an unramified
quadratic twist (possibly trivial twist) of the Steinberg representation

\mathrm{St}_{p}
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(i.e.,
c(\pi_{p})=1
)
if and only if
p\parallel N
.

Now there are 3 possibilities when
c(\pi_{p})=2
:

\pi_{p}
can be a ramified principal series, a ramified quadratic
twist of Steinberg, or supercuspidal. However, the former two
possibilities do not happen for
p=2
(e.g., see

[
13
, Corollary 4.1]
). In fact there are 2 possibilities
for
\pi_{p}
being a ramified principal series: either it is a ramified
quadratic twist of an unramified principal series, or a minimal twist (a twist
minimizing the local conductor) of
\pi_{p}
is a ramified principal series
of conductor 1 (necessarily the central character is nontrivial
and has conductor 1).
In any event, only the supercuspidal representations are minimal.

Suppose now that
N=M^{2}
where
M>1
is squarefree.
Then the above description of local representations means exactly
one of the following is true for a newform
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
:

(1)

f
is minimal, i.e., no twist (possibly with nebentypus)
has smaller level. Here
\pi_{p}
is supercuspidal for each
p\mid N
.

(2)

f
is a quadratic twist of a level 1 newform. Here
\pi_{p}

is a ramified quadratic twist of an unramified principal series for each
p\mid N
. Necessarily
N
is odd.

(3)

A minimal twist
f^{\prime}
of
f
(possibly with nebentypus) has level
N^{\prime}
strictly between
1
and
N
. Necessarily
v_{2}(N^{\prime})=v_{2}(N)
.

The utility of the local representation theory perspective for us
comes both from a straightforward description of the level of twists
and the fact that root numbers can be read off of the local representations.
Namely, the root number of
f
is the product over
p
of the local
representation root numbers
w_{p}(\pi_{p})

times
(-1)^{k/2}
(which is the local root number of
\pi_{\infty}
).

Recall
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}
(resp.
S_{k}^{\min}(N)
) is defined to be the subspace of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
generated by newforms of types (1) and (3) above
(resp. of type (1) above). Define
\tr W_{N}^{\prime}
and
\tr W_{N}^{\min}

to be the traces of
W_{N}
restricted to the spaces
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}

and
S_{k}^{\min}(N)
, respectively. First we describe
\tr W_{N}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\tr W_{N}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}-(-1)^{k/2}\prod_{p|N}{-1\overwithdelims( )p}\cdot\dim S_{k}(1)&N\text{ odd}\\
\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}&N\text{ even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}=S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
if
N
is even, assume
N
is odd.
Then we simply need to subtract off from
\tr W_{N}^{\mathrm{new}}

the trace of
W_{N}
restricted
to the subspace of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
generated by forms of type (2).
Now we simply use the fact that if
\pi_{p}
is a ramified
quadratic twist of an unramified principal series, then
w_{p}(\pi_{p})={-1\overwithdelims( )p}
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
in terms of local
representations, or
[
1
, Theorem 6]
in terms of modular forms).
∎

Let
\Delta(N,k)^{\prime}=(-1)^{k/2}\tr W_{N}^{\prime}
, which is the difference between
the number of newforms in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)^{\prime}
with root number
+1

and
-1
.

Proof of
1.3
.

When
N
is even, we simply have
\Delta(N,k)^{\prime}=\Delta(N,k)
,
so assume
N
is odd. Then
3.2
combined with the
above lemma shows
\Delta(N,k)^{\prime}
is

\frac{1}{4}(c(-4,M)-\mu(M))-\left(\prod_{p|M}{-1\overwithdelims( )p}-(-1)^{k/2}\mu(M)\right)\dim S_{k}(1)+\mu(M)\delta_{k=2}.

The sum of the first and the last term is always non-negative.
Note the coefficient of
\dim S_{k}(1)
is negative if and only if

\mu(M)=-(-1)^{k/2}
and
\prod_{p|M}{-1\overwithdelims( )p}=1
.
In this case,
\Delta(N,k)^{\prime}<0
if
k
is sufficiently large.
∎

In general to describe
\tr W_{N}^{\min}
, one needs to isolate
the forms which are supercuspidal at each ramified place.
One can presumably do this in a similar way as
4.1
, by subtracting
off the contributions from twists from smaller level. However,
we will restrict ourselves to giving a couple of examples
to show that
(-1)^{k/2}\tr W_{N}^{\min}
can be negative, and then
prove that often there is a quadratic twist which forces

\tr W_{N}^{\min}=0
to get
1.4
.

The example of
N=4
was already given in
Section

1.2
.
The following examples are taken from the LMFDB
[
7
]
.

Example 4.2
.

The space
S^{\mathrm{new}}_{10}(9)
has 3 newforms, all
rational. Two are twists from
S^{\mathrm{new}}_{10}(3)
by the quadratic
character
{-3\overwithdelims( )\cdot}
, and they have root number
+1

(though the corresponding forms on

S^{\mathrm{new}}_{10}(3)
have opposite root numbers).
The other newform in
S^{\mathrm{new}}_{10}(9)
, which is CM and minimal,
has root number
-1
. Hence the sum of root numbers
of minimal newforms in
S_{10}(9)
is
-1
, though

\Delta(10,9)=+1
.

Example 4.3
.

The space
S_{14}^{\min}(49)
has 3 Galois orbits of
newforms: one CM orbit of size 1 and root number
+1
,
one Galois orbit of size 6 and root number
+1
, and one Galois
orbit of size 12 and root number
-1
. Hence the sum of root
numbers for minimal forms is
1\cdot 1+1\cdot 6-1\cdot 12=-5
.
On the other hand,
\Delta(49,14)=2
.
We remark that except for
the first form, all newforms are non-CM, and all of the newforms
have nontrivial inner twists.

Proof of
1.4
.

Here we allow any
N>1
, i.e.,
N
need not be a square or cubefree.
Suppose
p^{2}\parallel N^{\mathrm{odd}}
, and let
\chi

be the quadratic character of conductor
\pm p
. Then twisting any
minimal newform
f\in S_{k}^{\min}(N)
gives another newform of level

N
. The local argument for this is that twisting a supercuspidal
representation
\pi_{p}
with
c(\pi_{p})=2
by
\chi_{p}

yields a supercuspidal representation
\pi_{p}\otimes\chi_{p}

also of local conductor 2.

Hence twisting by
\chi
is an involution on the set of minimal newforms.
Now any supercuspidal representation
\pi_{p}
with
c(\pi_{p})=2

is necessarily minimal, and thus by
[
16
, Proposition 3.5]
,
is induced from the unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Hence by
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
, the local root number of

\pi_{p}\otimes\chi_{p}
is
-{-1\overwithdelims( )p}
times the local root number of

\pi_{p}
. Moreover, for
q\mid p^{-2}N
, twisting by
\chi_{p}
multiplies
the root number of
\pi_{q}
by
{q\overwithdelims( )p}^{v_{q}(N)}
.
In particular, if
{-N/p^{2}\overwithdelims( )p}=1
, then twisting by
\chi

flips the sign of the root number of minimal newforms, which implies

\tr W_{N}^{\min}=0
.
∎

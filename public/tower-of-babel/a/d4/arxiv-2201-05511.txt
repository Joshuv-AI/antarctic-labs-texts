Introduction

Schur multipliers form a class of linear maps on matrix algebras with profound connections in functional analysis, operator algebras, geometric group theory and harmonic analysis. Their definition is extremely simple, we consider maps
S_{M}
determined by

S_{M}(A)=\big(M(j,k)A_{jk}\big)_{jk}

for any
n\times n
matrix
A
and certain function
M:\{1,2,\ldots,n\}^{2}\to\mathbf{C}
. More general index sets correspond to operators
A
acting on
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
for some
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
. Schur multipliers have played a key role in landmark results since the mid 20th century. The celebrated Grothendieck’s inequality is intimately connected to a striking characterization of the operator boundedness of Schur multipliers
[
19
,
46
]
. The impact of Schur multipliers in geometric group theory and operator algebras was early recognized by Haagerup. His pioneering work on free groups
[
20
]
and the research thereafter on semisimple lattices
[
10
,
15
]
encoded deep geometric properties of these groups in terms of approximation properties for Herz-Schur multipliers on their matrix algebras. More recently, strong rigidity properties of high rank lattices were discovered in the remarkable work of Lafforgue and de la Salle
[
30
]
studying
L_{p}
-approximations. The
L_{p}
-theory has gained a considerable momentum since then
[
16
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
34
,
35
,
40
,
41
]
. In a somewhat different direction, Schur multipliers have been the fundamental tool in the investigation of operator-Lipschitz functions. The solution of a longstanding problem going back to Krein was achieved by Potapov and Sukochev in
[
50
]
by unraveling Arazy’s stronger conjecture on Schur multipliers of divided differences in Schatten
p
-classes. Our main result gives a strikingly simple criterion, which goes beyond best-known to date
S_{p}
-estimates for divided differences and Herz-Schur multipliers. It also leads to challenging
L_{p}
-bounds for Fourier multipliers on Lie group algebras.

In this paper, we shall focus on Schur multipliers with index set
(\Omega,\mu)
being the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with its Lebesgue measure. In the context of our main result, this is not a serious restriction and other groups and measure spaces are investigated in
[
14
]
. There exists a very fruitful relation between Fourier multipliers and Schur multipliers with symbols
M(x,y)=m(x-y)
, so called Toeplitz or Herz-Schur. Indeed, let

denote the Schatten

-class acting on

m:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{C}

\widehat{T_{m}f}(\xi)=m(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

S_{m}(A)=\big(m(x-y)A_{xy}),

with
S_{m}
densely defined on
S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\cap S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
. Finding accurate conditions on the symbol

-boundedness of these multipliers is a rather difficult problem. Building on previous work of Bożejko and Fendler, it is known from

(FS)

\hskip 4.0pt\big\|S_{m}:S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,=\,\big\|T_{m}:L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}.

Here,

\hskip-11.0pt\big\|S_{m}\hskip-2.0pt:S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\lesssim\,\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\hskip-2.0pt\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||\xi|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}m(\xi)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}.

The singularity of
\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}m
around

. This is optimal since we may not consider less derivatives or larger upper bounds for them. Moreover, (

-multipliers with arbitrarily large

-multipliers for

NonToeplitz multipliers

Our approach towards the solution is based on a rather elementary observation which led us to think on a possible relation between the theory of Schur multipliers and that of pseudodifferential operators. Let us momentarily argue with the group
\mathbf{Z}
to avoid topological considerations. We know from Fourier-Schur transference (
FS
) (valid for any amenable group) that every Toeplitz symbol
M(j,k)=m(j-k)
gives rise to a Schur multiplier which is identified with the Fourier multiplier
T_{m}
on the torus
\mathbf{T}
. On the other hand, if
\bullet
and
\cdot
respectively stand for the Schur and ordinary matrix products on
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbf{Z}))
, elementary nonToeplitz symbols of the form
M_{\alpha}(j,k)=\alpha(j)
and
M_{\beta}(j,k)=\beta(k)
satisfy the simple identities

M_{\alpha}\bullet A=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha)\cdot A\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 10.00002ptM_{\beta}\bullet A=A\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\beta)

for the diagonal matrices with entries
(\alpha(j))_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}
and
(\beta(k))_{k\in\mathbf{Z}}
. In other words, we have identified “Fourier multipliers” and (left/right) ‘‘pointwise multipliers’’ within the class of Schur multipliers. Then, recalling that every symbol
M
can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle M(j,k)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!M_{r}(k-j,k)\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq 1}m_{r\ell}(j-k)\beta_{\ell}(k),

\displaystyle M(j,k)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!M_{c}(j,j-k)\ =\ \sum_{\ell\geq 1}\alpha_{\ell}(j)m_{c\ell}(j-k),

Schur multipliers are combinations of Fourier and left/right pointwise multipliers. Of course, this arises from the well-known isomorphism
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbf{Z}))\simeq\ell_{\infty}(\mathbf{Z})\rtimes\mathbf{Z}
and a transpose form of it. The formal analog of the above reasoning in Euclidean spaces leads to similar conclusions. It is thus conceivable to think of Schur multipliers on Euclidean spaces as matrix forms of pseudodifferential operators
[
23
,
57
]
. In particular, our viewpoint might look to be rather close to the theory developed in
[
17
]
and one should expect a regularity condition in terms of infinitely many mixed
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}
derivatives of the symbol
M
. Surprisingly, our main result only involves a finite number of
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
derivatives (no mixtures) and allows a singular behavior at the diagonal
x=y
, as predicted by de la Salle.

Theorem A ​​
.

If
M\in C^{[n/2]+1}(\mathbf{R}^{2n}\setminus\{x=y\})
and
1<p<\infty

\big\|S_{M}\hskip-2.0pt:S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big\|\,\lesssim\,\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\big|\big|\big|M\big|\big|\big|_{\mathrm{HMS}},

where we set
|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}:=\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\Big\{\big|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|+\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\}\Big\|_{\infty}.

Examples of NonToeplitz Hörmander-Mikhlin-Schur multipliers in
\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}

Any Toeplitz symbol would be forced to be constant at
x=y+\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}
, unlike above

Theorem A introduces a new class of
S_{p}
-bounded multipliers which we shall refer to as Hörmander-Mikhlin-Schur multipliers. Observe that our condition for Toeplitz symbols
M(x,y)=m(x-y)
reduces to the classical HM condition on
m
. According to (
FS
)+(
HMS
), Theorem A is the full matrix (nonToeplitz/nontrigonometric) form of the Hörmander-Mikhlin multiplier theorem. It admits even weaker regularity assumptions (up to fractional differentiability orders
\sigma>n/2
) expressed in terms of Sobolev norms and even weaker assumptions à la Calderón-Torchinsky for specific values of
p
, more details in Theorems
\mathrm{A^{\prime}}
and Corollary
3.1
below. Moreover, the statement above automatically implies cb-boundedness by
[
30
, Theorem 1.18]
.

The proof is more ingenious than technical. The key novelty is to control the Schur multiplier by two (not just one) “twisted Fourier” multipliers. Then, we refine noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund methods to prove two different endpoint inequalities for the twisted multipliers. Using a canonical form of BMO for matrix algebras —introduced in this paper and for which we reprove after
[
26
]
the expected complex interpolation behavior with the
S_{p}
-scale— both inequalities together yield a BMO endpoint inequality which implies the statement. Note that the twisted multipliers satisfy one endpoint inequality (row or column form) but not the other! With hindsight, this illustrates that singular Schur multipliers are slightly better behaved than operator-valued Calderón-Zygmund operators.

The class of HMS multipliers —enlarged under Sobolev regularity— leads to strong applications in operator algebras and harmonic analysis. Arazy’s conjecture for divided differences
[
50
]
now follows as a particular example. Moreover, we prove that
\alpha
-divided differences of
\alpha
-Hölder functions are also

-bounded as long as we have
|1/p-1/2|<\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}
. It shows a phase transition at
\alpha=1/2
. This is a very satisfactory result which goes beyond Arazy’s conjecture. Second, we obtain strikingly simple

This includes nilpotent Lie groups with subRiemannian metrics, high rank simple Lie groups with exponentially growing metrics and a local HM theorem for arbitrary Lie groups with the Riemannian metric. Altogether this goes far beyond
[
40
]
and we get optimal regularity conditions. On the other hand, the main results in the cocycle-based approach from
[

28

can be deduced and refined for nonorthogonal cocycles as well as for nonunimodular groups. In addition, Theorem A yields new matrix forms of the Littlewood-Paley theorem and admits a discrete formulation. A proof will be given for those applications in the scope of matrix algebras. The rest involve generalized forms of Theorem A and additional techniques postponed to
[

Let us begin with a brief discussion on Schur multipliers over a general

1.1.
Schur multipliers

Given a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
—in this paper we shall be mostly concerned with
(\Omega,\mu)
being an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space with its Lebesgue measure— let
\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\Omega))
be the matrix algebra of bounded linear operators on

and let
S_{\infty}(\Omega)
be the subalgebra of compact operators equipped with the operator norm. If

, the Schatten
p
-class

is the subspace of bounded linear operators on
L_{2}(\Omega)
with finite
p
-norm

S_{p}(\Omega)=\Big\{A\in\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\Omega)):\|A\|_{p}=\mathrm{tr}(|A|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\}.

More generally, we may consider noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces over a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
equipped with a normal semifinite faithful trace
\tau

[
49
]
. A linear map
\Lambda:L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is called completely bounded when
\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}
extends to a bounded operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}),\tau\otimes\mathrm{tr})
for any Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
. If
M_{n}
denotes the algebra of
n\times n
matrices, the cb-norm of

is

\big\|\Lambda:L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\big\|\mathrm{id}\otimes T:L_{p}(M_{n}(\mathcal{M}))\to L_{p}(M_{n}(\mathcal{M}))\big\|.

Informally, a Schur multiplier associated to a measurable function
M:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbf{C}
is the map
S_{M}
sending an operator
A:L_{2}(\Omega)\to L_{2}(\Omega)
with matrix representation
(A_{xy})_{x,y\in\Omega}
to the operator represented by
(M(x,y)A_{xy})_{x,y\in\Omega}
. However, not every such operator admits a kernel or a matrix representation and we must give a more precise definition. When
p=2
, every
A\in S_{2}(\Omega)
admits a matrix representation and
S_{2}(\Omega)
-bounded Schur multipliers
S_{M}
are in one-to-one correspondence with symbols
M\in L_{\infty}(\Omega\times\Omega)
. In addition, we shall say that
S_{M}:S_{p}(\Omega)\to S_{p}(\Omega)
is (completely) bounded for other values of
1\leq p\leq\infty
when its restriction to
S_{2}(\Omega)\cap S_{p}(\Omega)
extends to a (completely) bounded map. By density in Schatten
p
-classes, the extension is necessarily unique. We shall be using two important properties of Schur multipliers:

i)

If
(\Omega,\mu)
has no atoms and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then

\hskip 20.0pt\big\|S_{M}:S_{p}(\Omega)\to S_{p}(\Omega)\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=\big\|S_{M}:S_{p}(\Omega)\to S_{p}(\Omega)\big\|.

ii)

Assume that
\Omega
is locally compact and
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite Radon measure on
\Omega
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then the following holds for any
M:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbf{C}
continuous

\hskip 30.0pt\big\|S_{M}:S_{p}(\Omega)\to S_{p}(\Omega)\big\|=\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal{J}(\Omega,\mu)}\big\|S_{M_{\mid_{\Sigma\times\Sigma}}}:S_{p}(\Sigma)\to S_{p}(\Sigma)\big\|,

with
\mathcal{J}(\Omega,\mu)
the finite sets in the support of
\mu
and
S_{p}(\Sigma)
constructed with the counting measure on
\Sigma
. A similar result holds as well for the cb-norm.

The proofs of both results above appear in
[
30
, Theorems 1.18 and 1.19]
respectively.

Theorem 1.1
.

Complex interpolation gives

\big[\mathrm{BMO},S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big]_{\frac{2}{p}}\simeq_{\mathrm{cb}}\,S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt2\leq p<\infty.

Next, we consider periodic perturbations of

Identifying

In other words,

is the Herz-Schur multiplier which removes the diagonal strip of width

in the Euclidean metric. We could have used a smooth version of it with better boundedness properties, but this will be enough for our purposes. It is clear that
\pi_{k}\circ J_{k}=J_{k}\circ\pi_{k}
and
\pi_{k}\circ J_{k}(A)
is mean-zero in
\mathbf{T}^{n}
for any

\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}}\pi_{k}\circ J_{k}(A)(s)ds=0.

Indeed, we get nonvanishing frequencies

for

Lemma 1.3
.

Given
A\in S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
, we have

\big\|\pi_{k}\circ J_{k}(A)\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{A}}}\leq\big\|\pi\circ J_{k}(A)\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}}+C_{n}2^{-k}\big\|J_{k}(A)\big\|_{S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}.

Proof.
Given a cube
S\subset[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{n}
, let
Q=8^{k}S
and observe that

\displaystyle\hskip-30.0pt-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{S}\Big|\pi_{k}J_{k}(A)(u)--\hskip-10.7pt\int_{S}\pi_{k}J_{k}(A)(v)dv\Big|^{2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}\Big|\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\xi)--\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\eta)d\eta\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\underbrace{-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}\Big|\Big(\big\{e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,p_{k}(x)-p_{k}(y)\rangle}--\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}e^{2\pi i\langle\eta,p_{k}(x)-p_{k}(y)\rangle}d\eta\big\}J_{k}(A)_{xy}\Big)\Big|^{2}d\xi}_{M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))^{2}}.

Then, we may write

\displaystyle\big\|\pi_{k}\circ J_{k}(A)\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{A}}^{c}}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}\\
Q\subset[-\frac{1}{2}8^{k},\frac{1}{2}8^{k}]^{n}\end{subarray}}\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sup_{Q\ \mathrm{small}}\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|+\sup_{Q\ \mathrm{large}}\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|,

where we set
Q
to be small when
\mathrm{diam}(Q)\leq 4^{k}
and large otherwise. If the above supremum is nearly attained at a small cube
Q
with center
c_{Q}
, we let
Q_{0}=Q-c_{Q}
be the translated cube centered at
0
. Recall that
\widetilde{\pi}_{k}(B)(\xi)=u_{k}(\xi)(\mathbf{1}\otimes B)u_{k}^{*}(\xi)
for certain unitary

. This implies that

M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))=M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q_{0}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\hskip 1.0pt\cdot+c_{Q}))=u_{k}(c_{Q})M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q_{0}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))u_{k}(c_{Q})^{*}.

Therefore, the supremum is also nearly attained at
Q_{0}
and

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q_{0}}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q_{0}}(\pi J_{k}(A))\big\|+2\sup_{\xi\in Q_{0}}\big\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}-\pi)J_{k}(A)(\xi)\big\|_{S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\big\|\pi\circ J_{k}(A)\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}}+2\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in Q_{0}\\
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\end{subarray}}\big|1-e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,(x-p_{k}(x))-(y-p_{k}(y))\rangle}\big|\big\|J_{k}(A)\big\|_{S_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}.

The latter supremum is bounded by
C_{n}\mathrm{diam}(Q_{0})8^{-k}\|J_{k}(A)\|_{2}\leq C_{n}2^{-k}\|J_{k}(A)\|_{2}
which gives the desired estimate. Assume now that the supremum defining the
\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{A}}^{c}
seminorm is nearly attained at a large cube
R
. Then we observe

\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{R}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|\leq\Big\|-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{R}\big|\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big\|-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{R}\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\xi)d\xi\Big\|=:\alpha_{R}+\beta_{R}.

Splitting
R
into a disjoint union of
\mathrm{K}
small cubes

of the same measure, we find

\alpha_{R}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{K}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{K}}\Big\|-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q_{j}}\big|\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A)(\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi\Big\|\lesssim\sup_{Q\ \mathrm{small}}\big\|M\hskip-1.0ptO_{Q}(\widetilde{\pi}_{k}J_{k}(A))\big\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{K}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{K}}\beta_{Q_{j}}^{2}.

Moreover, we may assume that

\mathrm{diam}(Q_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}4^{k}.

In particular, it suffices to prove that
\beta_{Q}\lesssim 2^{-k}\|J_{k}(A)\|_{2}
for any such cube, also including
R
. Since
|p_{k}(x)-p_{k}(y)|\gtrsim 2^{-k}
for any pair

with

, the desired inequality reduces to the simple fact that

\Big|-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,p_{k}(x)-p_{k}(y)\rangle}d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{-k}

for every cube
Q
with
\mathrm{diam}(Q)\gtrsim 4^{k}
. The proof for row
\mathrm{BMO}
-spaces is similar.
\square

2.
Hörmander-Mikhlin-Schur multipliers

In this section we prove our main result. In fact, we shall prove a stronger statement which only assumes a fractional regularity order
\sigma=\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
. However, for the sake of clarity we first prove Theorem A and explain the modifications afterwards. The proof will be divided into several steps.

1) Reduction to BMO.
Since
S_{M}^{*}=S_{\overline{M}}
and our HMS conditions in Theorem A are stable by complex conjugation, we may assume with no loss of generality that
p\geq 2
. The boundedness for
p=2
clearly holds since the HMS condition readily implies that
M
is essentially bounded on
\mathbf{R}^{n}\hskip-2.0pt\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
. It suffices to prove the inequality

(2.1)

\big\|S_{M}:S_{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to\mathrm{BMO}\big\|\ \leq\ C|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}.

Theorem A follows from (
2.1
) by Theorem
1.1
and Riesz-Thorin interpolation.

2) Twisted Fourier multipliers.
Define

(2.2)

M_{r}(x,y):=M(y-x,y)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptM_{c}(x,y):=M(x,x-y).

Then we construct the following linear maps on

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{M_{r}}(f)(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}M_{r}(\xi,y)\widehat{f}_{xy}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\langle z,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\Big),

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}(f)(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}M_{c}(x,\xi)\widehat{f}_{xy}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\langle z,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\Big).

The use of both maps (not just one) is critical. Similar maps were already considered in
[
40
,
41
]
and denominated twisted Fourier multipliers. They act on the entry

as the Fourier multiplier with symbol
M_{r}(\cdot,y)
or
M_{c}(x,\cdot)
respectively. Note that the finiteness of
|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}
in the statement readily implies that
M_{r}(\cdot,y)
and

are Mikhlin multipliers and therefore well-defined on
L_{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
. More than that, we shall justify in points 3), 4) and 5) below that the corresponding twisted multipliers are well-defined on

with values in row/column
\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}
. In this part of the proof, we shall concentrate on the inequalities

(2.3)

\displaystyle\big\|S_{M}:S_{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{r}\big\|\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{r}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{r}\big\|,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\big\|S_{M}:S_{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{c}\big\|\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}\big\|.

Let us first focus on the second inequality (

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}(\pi(A))(z)\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(T_{M_{c}(x,\cdot)}\big(\exp(2\pi i\langle x-y,\cdot\rangle)\big)(z)A_{xy}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(M_{c}(x,x-y)\exp(2\pi i\langle x-y,z\rangle)A_{xy}\Big)\ =\ \pi(S_{M}(A)).

Then, we get

\displaystyle\!\!\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}(\pi(A))\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}}\ \leq\ \big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}\big\|\,\|\pi(A)\|_{\infty}.

3) Calderón-Zygmund kernels.
We claim that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{r}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{r}\big\|\!\!

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}\big\|\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}.

This implies Theorem A. The proof requires us to refine well-known arguments from classical Calderón-Zygmund theory in the noncommutative realm. Again, we prove first the column endpoint. We formally have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}(f)(z)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}M_{c}(x,\xi)\widehat{f}_{xy}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\langle z,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\underbrace{\big[M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big]^{\vee}(z-w)}_{k_{c}(x,z-w)}f_{xy}(w)\,dw\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(k_{c}(x,\cdot)*f_{xy}\Big)(z)\,=\,\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}K_{c}(z-w)\cdot f(w)\,dw

with
K_{c}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))
the “diagonal-valued” function

(K_{c}(z)h)(x)=k_{c}(x,z)h(x).

Although it is not meaningful, we shall abusively use the matrix-notation

K_{c}=\mathrm{diag}\Big(k_{c}(x,\cdot)\Big).

The kernel
K_{c}
should be understood as an operator-valued distribution which agrees with a locally integrable operator-valued function on
\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. A careful analysis of these kernels is analogous to that from
[
17
, Section 2]
, where CZ kernels on quantum Euclidean spaces were investigated. In the present context, it helps to understand the behavior of the “diagonal entries”
k_{c}(x,\cdot)=M_{c}(x,\cdot)^{\vee}
of the kernel
K_{c}
. Since we allow
M
to be singular at
x=y
it turns out that
M_{c}(x,\cdot)
may be singular at
0
. This means that its inverse Fourier transform must be understood as a distribution which is locally integrable outside the origin. In particular, the kernel representation above is meaningful when
z\notin\mathrm{supp}_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}f
, the Euclidean support of the matrix-valued function
f
. Under this assumption (standard in CZ theory) the kernel representation above does not require to deal with distributions taking values in operator algebras. More precisely, we shall only use this kernel representation for
z\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\mathrm{supp}_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}f
, which is exactly the framework of the noncommutative CZ theory
[
8
,
27
,
39
]
. In our use of CZ methods, it will be crucial to weaken the kernel condition from
[
27
, Lemma 2.3]
. As usual in this context, we begin by noticing that

\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}}\sim\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\inf_{\alpha_{Q}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))}\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f(z)-\alpha_{Q}\big|^{2}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))},

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the set of Euclidean balls as above. Next, fix
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
nearly attaining the above sup and decompose
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}=f\chi_{5Q}
and
f_{2}=f-f_{1}
. Picking

\alpha_{Q}=-\hskip-10.7pt\int_{Q}\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}(f_{2})(w)dw,

we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f\big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}}\!\!

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\Big\|\Big(-\hskip-11.2pt\int_{Q}\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{1}(z)\big|^{2}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\Big\|\Big(-\hskip-11.2pt\int_{Q}\Big|-\hskip-11.2pt\int_{Q}\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{2}(z)-\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{2}(w)\,dw\Big|^{2}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}\ =\ \mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}.

On the other hand, we recall the following inequalities:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f\big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{R})}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{R})},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f(z)\big|^{2}\,dz\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|f(z)\big|^{2}\,dz\Big\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Inequality (
2.8
) is straightforward from the Hilbert-valued Plancherel theorem and (
2.9
) follows from it, since
M_{c}
-twisted multipliers are right module maps and thus

\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\hskip-2.0pt\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f(z)\big|^{2}dz\Big\|_{\infty}\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-2.0pt\sup_{\|E\|_{S_{2}}=1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\hskip-2.0pt\mathrm{tr}\Big(\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}[fE](z)\big|^{2}\Big)dz=\hskip-2.0pt\sup_{\|E\|_{S_{2}}=1}\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}[fE]\big\|_{2}^{2},

from where our claim follows. By (
2.9
)

\Big\|\Big(-\hskip-11.2pt\int_{Q}\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{1}(z)\big|^{2}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{|Q|}}\Big\|\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|f_{1}(z)\big|^{2}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}.

Since
f_{1}=f\chi_{5Q}
we immediately deduce

(2.10)

\mathrm{A}\,\leq\,5^{\frac{n}{2}}\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}.

To estimate B, the operator-Jensen inequality and the kernel representation yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\Big(-\hskip-11.2pt\int_{Q\times Q}\big|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{2}(z)-\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}f_{2}(w)\big|^{2}dwdz\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{w,z\in Q}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus 5Q}\big(K_{c}(z-s)-K_{c}(w-s)\big)f(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}.

It is very tempting to extract the
L_{\infty}
-norm of
f
by putting the norm inside the integral. Since
|w-s|>2|z-w|
for
s,w,z
as above, we could use
s\mapsto w-s
and
u=w-z
to put the region of integration inside the set
|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|
. This approach from
[
27
, Lemma 2.3]
is however too restrictive for our CZ kernel under the given (sharp) HM conditions. Instead, we set
f_{w}(s)=f(w-s)
and
\Gamma=Q-Q
to obtain the following inequality

(2.11)

\mathrm{B}\,\leq\,\sup_{(u,w)\in\Gamma\times Q}\underbrace{\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)}\big(K_{c}(s-u)-K_{c}(s)\big)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}}_{\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}}.

Then, we use a Littlewood-Paley decomposition of
K_{c}
before loosing cancellation.

4) Hörmander-Mikhlin estimates.
Let us continue with the proof of inequality (
2.7
). According to (
2.10
), we have
\mathrm{A}/\|f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathrm{RHS}\hskip 1.0pt\eqref{Eq-HM2}
. It therefore remains to control the term
\mathrm{B}
. Let
\phi:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
be a smooth function which is identically one in the unit ball and vanishes for
|\xi|>2
. The functions
\psi_{j}(\xi)=\phi(2^{-j}\xi)-\phi(2^{1-j}\xi)
define a Littlewood-Paley partition of unity. That is to say

(2.12)

\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})\subset\big\{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}\big\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\psi_{j}(\xi)=1\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $\xi\neq 0$}.

Decompose

K_{c}=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}K_{cj}

with the pieces given by

K_{cj}=\mathrm{diag}\Big(k_{cj}(x,\cdot)\Big)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptk_{cj}(x,z)=\big[\psi_{j}M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big]^{\vee}(z).

Let us recall that Plancherel theorem gives

(2.13)

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|(-2\pi iz)^{\gamma}k_{cj}(x,z)\big|^{2}dz=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}\big(M_{c}(x,\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)\big)\big|^{2}d\xi

for any multi-index
\gamma
. Given a fixed
(u,w)\in\Gamma\times Q
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|>1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)}\big(K_{cj}(s-u)-K_{cj}(s)\big)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)}\big(K_{cj}(s-u)-K_{cj}(s)\big)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}=:\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{1}+\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{2}.

By the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{1}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|>1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)}K_{cj}(s-u)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|>1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)}K_{cj}(s)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}=:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|>1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{11}(j)+\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{12}(j).

Next, letting
\sigma=[\frac{n}{2}]+1
and using Hölder inequality

\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{11}(j)\,\leq\,\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|s-u|^{2\sigma}|K_{cj}^{*}(s-u)|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\int_{|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|}|s-u|^{-2\sigma}|f_{w}(s)|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
|s+u|\geq|s|-|u|>|u|
when
|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|
and
K_{cj}K_{cj}^{*}=K_{cj}^{*}K_{cj}

Using
\|m\|_{\mathrm{HM}}:=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq\sigma}\big\||\xi|^{|\gamma|}\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}m(\xi)\big\|_{\infty}
and (
2.13
), we find the estimate

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|s|^{2\sigma}|K_{cj}(s)|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\ \sup_{|\gamma|=\sigma}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}\big(\psi_{j}(\xi)M_{c}(x,\xi)\big)\big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=\sigma}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{j}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}M_{c}(x,\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=\sigma}2^{-2j|\alpha|}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi(2^{-j}\xi)|\xi|^{-|\beta|}\big|^{2}d\xi\ \big\|M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{HM}}^{2}.

Since
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subset\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
, this is bounded by
2^{j(n-2\sigma)}\sup_{x}\|M_{c}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{HM}}^{2}
. Thus, by (
2.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|>1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{11}(j)\,\lesssim\,|u|^{\frac{n}{2}-\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big\|M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{HM}}\,\|f\|_{\infty}=\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}

since
\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}M_{c}(x,\xi)=(-1)^{|\gamma|}\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}M(x,x-\xi)
. The sum of
\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{12}(j)
’s admits the same bound with the same argument. Altogether, we have proved the following inequality

(2.15)

\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{1}\,\lesssim\,\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}.

To estimate
\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{2}
we set
A_{w}(Q)=\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{2}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{A_{w}(Q),|s|<2^{-j}}\big(K_{cj}(s-u)-K_{cj}(s)\big)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbf{Z}\\
2^{j}|u|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\int_{A_{w}(Q),|s|\geq 2^{-j}}\big(K_{cj}(s-u)-K_{cj}(s)\big)f_{w}(s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}\!\!=\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{21}+\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{22}.

The
j

\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{21}
is dominated by

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{21}(j)\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\Big\|\int_{|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|}\big|K_{cj}(s-u)^{*}-K_{cj}(s)^{*}\big|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\int_{|s|<2^{-j}}|f_{w}(s)|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!2^{-\frac{jn}{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|}\big|k_{cj}(x,s-u)-k_{cj}(x,s)\big|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!2^{-\frac{jn}{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{|u|<\frac{1}{2}|s|}\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dr}k_{cj}(x,s-ru)\,dr\Big|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!2^{-\frac{jn}{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\nabla k_{cj}(x,s)\big|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}|u|\,\|f\|_{\infty}\ \lesssim\ 2^{j}|u|\,\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}

since the Plancherel theorem and
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subset\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
give

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\nabla k_{cj}(x,s)\big|^{2}\,ds\,=\,4\pi^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\big|M_{c}(x,\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,d\xi\,\lesssim\,2^{j(n+2)}\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

In particular, we get the following bound for the sum of
\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{21}(j)
’s

(2.16)

\sum_{2^{j}|u|\leq 1}\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{21}(j)\lesssim\|M_{c}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}.

As for
\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{22}(j)
we proceed similarly using the weights
|s|^{\pm\sigma}
to obtain

\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{22}(j)\lesssim|u|\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\Big(\underbrace{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|s|^{2\sigma}\big|\nabla k_{cj}(x,s)\big|^{2}\,ds}_{J_{j}(x)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\int_{|s|\geq 2^{-j}}|s|^{-2\sigma}|f_{w}(s)|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Plancherel, the HM condition for
M_{c}
and
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subset\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
, we get

\displaystyle J_{j}(x)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|=\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}[\xi_{k}\psi_{j}(\xi)M_{c}(x,\xi)]\big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|=\sigma}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}[\psi_{j}(\xi)M_{c}(x,\xi)]\big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{|\gamma|=\sigma-1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}[\psi_{j}(\xi)M_{c}(x,\xi)]\big|^{2}d\xi\ \lesssim\ 2^{j(n+2-2\sigma)}\big\|M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{HM}}^{2}.

Since
2\sigma>n
, we obtain the following bound for the sum of
\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{22}(j)
’s

(2.17)

\sum_{2^{j}|u|\leq 1}\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{22}(j)\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big\|M_{c}(x,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{HM}}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Combining (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we conclude

(2.18)

\mathrm{B}_{(u,w)}^{2}\,\lesssim\,\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}.

According to (
2.10

2.11

2.15

\big\|\widetilde{T}_{M_{c}}:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\to\mathrm{BMO}_{\mathcal{R}}^{c}\big\|\,\lesssim\,\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}^{\hbox{}}\Big\||x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{y}^{\gamma}M(x,y)\big|\Big\|_{\infty}.

2.7

\mathrm{B}_{2}
, we set
A_{wj}(Q)=2^{j}\big(\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus(w-5Q)\big)
and rewrite it in terms of
\Phi_{j}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{2}(j)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Big\|\int_{A_{wj}(Q)}\big(\Phi_{j}(s-2^{j}u)-\Phi_{j}(s)\big)f_{w}(2^{-j}s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Big\|\int_{A_{wj}(Q)}\Big[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dr}\Phi_{j}(s-r2^{j}u)\,dr\Big]f_{w}(2^{-j}s)\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\!\,\sup_{0\leq r\leq 1}\,\Big\|\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big(1+|s-r2^{j}u|\big)^{-2\sigma}|f_{w}(2^{-j}s)|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\!\!\!|2^{j}u|\ \Big\|\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big(1+|s-r2^{j}u|\big)^{2\sigma}\big|\nabla\Phi_{j}(s-r2^{j}u)\big|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!C_{\varepsilon}\,|2^{j}u|\,\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\big(1+|s|\big)^{\sigma}\nabla\Phi_{j}(s)\big|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

To estimate the above integral, we note that

\widehat{\nabla\Phi}_{j}(\xi)\!=\!-2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{diag}\Big(\xi_{k}\psi(\xi)M_{c}(x,2^{j}\xi)\Big)\otimes e_{k}.

In particular

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\Big\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\big(1+|s|\big)^{\sigma}\nabla\Phi_{j}(s)\big|^{2}\,ds\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!2\pi\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{diag}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big|\big(1+|s|\big)^{\sigma}\big(\langle\hskip 2.0pt\cdot,e_{k}\rangle\hskip 1.0pt\psi M_{c}(x,2^{j}\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}\big|^{2}\,ds\Big)\Big\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!2\pi\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big\|\langle\hskip 2.0pt\cdot,e_{k}\rangle\hskip 1.0pt\psi M_{c}(x,2^{j}\cdot)\big\|_{W_{2\sigma}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big\|\psi M_{c}(x,2^{j}\cdot)\big\|_{W_{2\sigma}}.

Therefore, we obtain the following upper bound

\mathrm{B}_{2}\,\leq\ C_{\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{2^{j}|u|\leq 1}|2^{j}u|\Big)\,\sup_{(j,x)\in\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}}\big\|\psi M_{c}(x,2^{j}\cdot)\big\|_{W_{2\sigma}}\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

This proves (
2.22
) in the category of Banach spaces. The cb-norm bound holds as
well with the same proof after matrix amplification and the row term is similar.
\square

3.
Applications and discussion

3.1.
HMS
p
-conditions

Given
1<p<\infty
, we may refine Theorem
\mathrm{A}^{\prime}
with lower Sobolev regularity orders for
S_{p}
-boundedness as a function of
|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|
. This natural phenomenon was originally established by Calderón and Torchinsky for Euclidean Fourier multipliers
[
9
]
. The argument uses complex interpolation for analytic families of operators applying Plancherel and Hörmander-Mikhlin theorems to control the endpoints in
L_{2}
and
\mathrm{H}_{1}
or BMO respectively. The modifications in our case are quite simple and left to the reader. Indeed, we just need to follow their reasoning for the two terms in
\big\bracevert\hskip-2.0ptM\hskip-2.0pt\big\bracevert_{\hskip-3.0pt\mathrm{HMS}}
and use Remark
1.5
for interpolation.

Corollary 3.1
.

If
\big|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\big|<\frac{\delta}{n}
and
\frac{n}{q}<\delta<\frac{n}{2}
with
q>2

\big\|S_{M}:S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\leq\,C_{p}\big\bracevert\hskip-2.0ptM\hskip-2.0pt\big\bracevert_{\hskip-3.0pt\mathrm{HMS}_{q\delta}}.

Remark 3.2
.

By
[
18
]
, Corollary
3.1
is optimal since it fails when
|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|>\frac{\delta}{n}
.

3.2.
Operator-Lipschitz functions

Set

M_{f}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{$f:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{C}$}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{Lipschitz}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{$x\neq y$}.

These multipliers were considered by Arazy
[
4
]
, who conjectured
S_{p}
-boundedness for
1<p<\infty
. It implies Krein’s longstanding conjecture for operator-Lipschitz functions —
\|f(A)-f(B)\|_{S_{p}}\leq C_{p}\|A-B\|_{S_{p}}
for any pair of self-adjoint operators
A,B
whose difference lies in the Schatten
p
-class— and it was finally confirmed in 2011 by Potapov and Sukochev
[
50
]
. It trivially follows from our main result.

Corollary 3.3

(Arazy’s conjecture)
.

If
1<p<\infty

\big\|S_{M_{f}}:S_{p}(\mathbf{R})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\leq\,C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

Proof.
Since
n=1
, if
f\in C^{1}(\mathbf{R})
Theorem A and Remark
2.1
give

\big\|S_{M_{f}}\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}(S_{p}(\mathbf{R}))}\,\leq\,C\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\Big(\|M_{f}\|_{\infty}+\Big\||x-y|\Big\{|\partial_{x}M_{f}(x,y)|+|\partial_{y}M_{f}(x,y)|\Big\}\Big\|_{\infty}\Big),

which is trivially bounded above by the Lip-norm of
f
. In general we only know that
f
is a.e. differentiable, but we may apply Theorem A’ for
M_{f}
with (say)
\sigma=\frac{3}{4}
. Then, according to (
2.20
) we see that the above inequality still holds, despite
\partial_{x}M_{f}
and
\partial_{y}M_{f}
are undefined in a zero-measure set.
\square

Other forms of Arazy’s conjecture (
S_{p}\to S_{q}
with
p\neq q
) were also solved in
[
51
]
.

3.3.
Multipliers of
\alpha
-divided differences

If
0<\alpha<1
, set

M_{\alpha f}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{$f:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{C}$}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{$\alpha$-H\"{o}lder}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{$x\neq y$}.

The mapping properties of
S_{M_{\alpha f}}
have remained open so far. Since Theorem
\mathrm{A}^{\prime}
just imposes
\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
derivatives in one dimension, it looks there is room for improvement in Arazy’s conjecture and we confirm it below. Let
\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}=\sup_{x\neq y}|f(x)-f(y)|/|x-y|^{\alpha}
.

Corollary 3.4

(
\alpha
-Arazy)
.

If
|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|<\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}
, then

\big\|S_{M_{\alpha f}}:S_{p}(\mathbf{R})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{R})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\leq\,C_{p}\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}.

In particular, we get complete
S_{p}
-boundedness for
1<p<\infty
as long as
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.
Let
\Psi_{M,j}
be as in (
2.19
). If
|s|
is small, we easily get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{1,qjy}(s)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}}\big|\Psi_{M,j}(x+s,y)-\Psi_{M,j}(x,y)\big|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}|s|^{\alpha},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{2,qjx}(s)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}}\big|\underbrace{\Psi_{M,j}(x,y+s)-\Psi_{M,j}(x,y)}_{\Theta_{M,j,s}(x,y)}\big|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}|s|^{\alpha}.

Indeed, if
|s|
is small enough, the above integrals are supported by the corona-type set
\frac{1}{4}\leq\min\big\{|x-y|,|x+s-y|\big\}\leq\max\big\{|x-y|,|x+s-y|\big\}\leq 4
and we may bound them by the same integrals with
q=1
. Next, we estimate

\displaystyle\big|\Theta_{M,j,s}(x,y)\big|\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\big|\psi(x-y-s)-\psi(x-y)\big|\,\Big|\frac{f(2^{j}x)-f(2^{j}(y+s))}{2^{j\alpha}|x-y-s|^{\alpha}}\Big|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\psi(x-y)\Big|\frac{f(2^{j}x)-f(2^{j}(y+s))}{2^{j\alpha}|x-y-s|^{\alpha}}-\frac{f(2^{j}x)-f(2^{j}y)}{2^{j\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha}}\Big|\lesssim\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}|s|^{\alpha}.

Then, if
q\geq 2
and
0<\delta<\alpha
, we deduce from
[
56
, Section V.3.5]
that

\big\|\Psi_{M,j}(\cdot,y)\big\|_{W_{q\delta}}\,\lesssim\,C_{\varepsilon}\big\|\Psi_{M,j}(\cdot,y)\big\|_{q}+\Big(\int_{|s|<\varepsilon}\frac{\omega_{1,qjy}(s)^{2}}{|s|^{1+2\delta}}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\leq\,C_{\varepsilon\delta}\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\alpha}}.

The same bound applies to
\Psi_{M,j}(x,\cdot)
. The statement follows from Theorem
\mathrm{A^{\prime}}
with
(q,\delta)=(2,\frac{1+2\alpha}{4})
for
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
and from Corollary
3.1
for smaller values of
\alpha
.
\square

3.4.
Schur multipliers over lattices

Now we study the form of Theorem A for Schur multipliers over integer lattices
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
. Other groups are discussed below and deeply investigated in
[
14
]
. Given
\phi:\mathbf{Z}^{n}\to\mathbf{C}
and
j=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n})\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
consider the discrete derivatives

\Delta^{s}\phi(j)=\phi(j+e_{s})-\phi(j)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}

\Delta^{\gamma}=\prod_{s=1}^{n}(\Delta^{s})^{\gamma_{s}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for higher multi-indices}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}).

The operators
\Delta^{\gamma}
are defined in the same way for functions on
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
or
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. When acting on a two-variable function, we shall use
\Delta_{1}^{\gamma}=\Delta^{\gamma}\otimes\mathrm{id}
and
\Delta_{2}^{\gamma}=\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\gamma}
to clarify which variable does the operator act on.

Corollary 3.5

(A discrete formulation)
.

If
1<p<\infty

\big\|S_{M}\hskip-2.0pt:S_{p}(\mathbf{Z}^{n})\to S_{p}(\mathbf{Z}^{n})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\lesssim\,\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\big|\big|\big|M\big|\big|\big|_{\mathrm{HMS}_{\Delta}}

where we set
|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}_{\Delta}}:=\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\Big\||j-k|^{|\gamma|}\Big\{\big|\Delta_{1}^{\gamma}M(j,k)\big|+\big|\Delta_{2}^{\gamma}M(j,k)\big|\Big\}\Big\|_{\infty}.

Proof.
By elementary restriction properties of Schur multipliers, it suffices from Theorem A to find a lifting function
\mathbf{M}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{C}
whose restriction to
\mathbf{Z}^{n}\times\mathbf{Z}^{n}
coincides with
M
and such that

|||\mathbf{M}|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}\,\lesssim\,|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}_{\Delta}}.

Lemma 4.5.1 in
[
54
]
provides
\Theta,\Phi_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
such that

(3.3)

\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\Theta(\cdot+k)\equiv 1,\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{\Theta}_{\mid_{\mathbf{Z}^{n}}}\equiv\delta_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt\partial^{\gamma}(\widehat{\Theta})=\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}(\Phi_{\gamma})

for
|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1
and the backwards derivative given by

\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}=\prod_{s=1}^{n}(\overline{\Delta}^{s})^{\gamma_{s}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{\Delta}^{s}f(\xi)=f(\xi)-f(\xi-e_{s}).

In fact, we shall use (
3.3
) with
\partial^{\gamma}(\widehat{\Theta})=\Delta^{\gamma}(\Phi_{\gamma})
instead, since the proof in
[
54
]
can be easily modified to that end. Construct the lifting multipliers by means of
\Theta
as

\mathbf{M}(x,y)=\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\widehat{\Theta}(x-j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)M(j,k).

By the second identity in (
3.3
) we clearly have

\textbf{M}=M\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{over}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{Z}^{n}\times\mathbf{Z}^{n}.

Next, letting
\tau_{x}(f):=f(x-\cdot)
we use the identities

\tau_{x}\circ\Delta^{\gamma}=(-1)^{|\gamma|}\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}\circ\tau_{x},

\partial^{\gamma}(\widehat{\Theta})(x-j)=\Delta^{\gamma}(\Phi_{\gamma})(x-j)=(-1)^{|\gamma|}\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}\big(\tau_{x}(\Phi_{\gamma})\big)(j).

Then, we deduce the following inequality using summation by parts

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt|x-y|^{|\gamma|}\big|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\mathbf{M}(x,y)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!|x-y|^{|\gamma|}\Big|\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}(\tau_{x}(\Phi_{\gamma}))(j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)M(j,k)\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!|x-y|^{|\gamma|}\Big|\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\Phi_{\gamma}(x-j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)\Delta_{1}^{\gamma}M(j,k)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\big|\Phi_{\gamma}(x-j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)\big||j-k|^{|\gamma|}\big|\Delta_{1}^{\gamma}M(j,k)\big|\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\Big(\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|(x-j)-(y-k)|^{|\gamma|}\big|\Phi_{\gamma}(x-j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)\big|\big|\Delta_{1}^{\gamma}M(j,k)\big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!|||M|||_{\mathrm{HMS}_{\Delta}}\Big(\sum_{j,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\big[1+|(x-j)-(y-k)|^{|\gamma|}\big]\big|\Phi_{\gamma}(x-j)\widehat{\Theta}(y-k)\big|\Big).

The last sum above in uniformly bounded in
x,y
since
\Phi_{\gamma},\Theta
are Schwartz functions. The derivatives of
\mathbf{M}
in the
y
-variable are dealt with in the same way.
\square

3.5.
A matrix Littlewood-Paley theorem

Let
\Psi:\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
be a smooth function which generates a Littlewood-Paley partition of unity. By that we just mean that the dilated functions
\Psi_{j}(x,y)=\Psi(2^{j}x,2^{j}y)
satisfy the following conditions:

a)

\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\Psi_{j}^{2}=1
a.e.

b)

The supports of
\Psi_{j}
have finite overlapping.

Corollary 3.7

(Matrix LP theorem)
.

Let
1<p<\infty:

i)

If
p\leq 2

\|A\|_{S_{p}}\asymp_{\mathrm{cb}}\inf_{S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)=A_{j}+B_{j}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}A_{j}A_{j}^{*}+B_{j}^{*}B_{j}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{S_{p}}.

ii)

If
p\geq 2

\|A\|_{S_{p}}\asymp_{\mathrm{cb}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)^{*}+S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)^{*}S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{S_{p}}.

The constants in the cb-isomorphisms are comparable to
p^{2}/(p-1)
as
p\to 1,\infty
.

Proof.
The terms on the right hand side are usually denoted in the literature as the
S_{p}[RC_{p}]
-norm of the sequence
(S_{\Psi_{j}}(A))_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}
. The norm takes values in the operator spaces
R_{p}+C_{p}
or
R_{p}\cap C_{p}
, according to the value of
p
as above. By the noncommutative Khintchine inequality
[
32
]
, both mostright terms may be written as the
\pm 1
conditional expectation

\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\varepsilon_{j}S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)\Big\|_{S_{p}}\leq_{\mathrm{cb}}C_{p}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big|\Big|\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\varepsilon_{j}\Psi_{j}\Big|\Big|\Big|_{\mathrm{HMS}}\|A\|_{S_{p}}.

The above inequality follows from Theorem A. Property b) of
\Psi_{j}
gives

\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big|\Big|\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\varepsilon_{j}\Psi_{j}\Big|\Big|\Big|_{\mathrm{HMS}}\,\lesssim\,\sup_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}|||\Psi_{j}|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}\,=\,|||\Psi|||_{\mathrm{HMS}}\,<\,\infty.

This proves the lower bounds. The upper bounds follow from a) and duality

\|A\|_{S_{p}}\,=\sup_{\|B\|_{S_{p^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{tr}\big(S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)S_{\Psi_{j}}(B)^{*}\big)\,\lesssim_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\big\|(S_{\Psi_{j}}(A))\big\|_{S_{p}[RC_{p}]},

using the well-known fact that
S_{p}[RC_{p}]^{*}=S_{p^{\prime}}[RC_{p^{\prime}}]
. Recall that we only use one side of the Khintchine inequalities which holds up to constants independent of
p
. Therefore, the final dependence on
p
is only dictated by Theorem A.
\square

Remark 3.8
.

Let
\psi_{0}:\mathbf{R}_{+}\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
denote the radial generator of the function
\psi
constructed in (
2.12
). The choice
\Psi(x,y)=\psi_{0}(|x-y|)
gives the Toeplitz form of Corollary
3.7
, which by Fourier-Schur transference is known to be equivalent to the classical Littlewood-Paley theorem. What appears to be new is the following choice

\Psi(x,y)=\psi_{0}\Big\{\big(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\},

giving smooth coronas
S_{\Psi_{j}}(A)=\mathrm{Cor}_{j}(A)
with nonflat boundary. If
p\geq 2
we get

\|A\|_{S_{p}}\asymp_{\mathrm{cb}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{Cor}_{j}(A)\mathrm{Cor}_{j}(A)^{*}+\mathrm{Cor}_{j}(A)^{*}\mathrm{Cor}_{j}(A)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{S_{p}}.

3.6.
Fourier multipliers in group algebras

Let
\beta:\mathrm{G}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
be an orthogonal cocycle and assume that
m:\mathrm{G}\to\mathbf{C}
satisfies
m=\widetilde{m}\circ\beta
. The main discovery in
[
16
,
27
,
28
]
was that a HM theory in group von Neumann algebras is possible in terms of the
\beta
-lifted symbols
\widetilde{m}
. The main drawbacks of this theory are that it requires orthogonal actions, regularity is measured in terms of the cocycle dimension (which excludes infinite-dimensional cocycles) and imposes auxiliary differential structures which appear to be unnecessary or at least less natural for Lie groups. Under these difficulties, the works
[
34
,
35
,
40
]
provide partial answers for the free group and the special linear groups
S\hskip-1.0ptL_{n}(\mathbf{R})
. However, very little is known on optimal regularity conditions for
L_{p}
-boundedness of Fourier multipliers on Lie group algebras
\mathrm{\mathcal{L}(G)}
in terms of canonical metrics and Lie differentiation. More precisely, let us write
d_{g}^{\gamma}m(g)
for the left-invariant Lie derivative of order
\gamma=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{|\gamma|})

(1\leq j_{i}\leq n)
of the Fourier symbol
m
at
g
. If
\mathrm{G}
is a unimodular Lie group with
\dim\mathrm{G}=n
we want to investigate natural metrics
L:\mathrm{G}\times\mathrm{G}\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
for which the following inequality holds for
1<p<\infty

(HM
G
)

\big\|T_{m}:L_{p}(\mathrm{\mathcal{L}(G)})\to L_{p}(\mathrm{\mathcal{L}(G)})\big\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\,\lesssim\,C_{p}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1}\big\|L(g,e)^{|\gamma|}d_{g}^{\gamma}m(g)\big\|_{\infty}.

Our first result is a
local HM theorem
: Inequality (
HMG
) holds for arbitrary Lie groups with the Riemannian metric for symbols with small support. According to
[
30
]
, locality is necessary for such a general statement. Next, a
HM theorem for stratified Lie groups
: Inequality (
HMG
) holds for the subRiemannian metric and arbitrary symbols. This uses a stratifed Mikhlin condition (a derivative in the
k
-th stratum must be dealt with as a
k
-th order derivative) which is substantially and necessarily different from the (dual) spectral approach developed by Cowling, Müller, Ricci, Stein, etc… Finally, a
HM theorem for high rank simple Lie groups
: Inequality (
HMG
) holds for general symbols and a locally Euclidean metric whose assymptotic behavior is dictated by the adjoint representation. This goes far beyond the nearly optimal results for
S\hskip-1.0ptL_{n}(\mathbf{R})
in
[
40
]
.

These results have been the object of our desire for quite some time. Among other ingredients, the proof requires a generalized form of Theorem A which we postpone to
[
14
]
. The Introduction there provides historical precedents, a detailed account of the motivations and rigorous statements of these
L_{p}
-inequalities. Such form of Theorem A also expands the main multiplier theorems in
[
27
,
28
]
.

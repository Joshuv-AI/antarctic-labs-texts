Consumption-investment optimization with Epstein-Zin utility in unbounded non-Markovian markets
Zixin Feng, Dejian Tian, Harry Zheng

Abstract
The paper investigates the consumption-investment problem for an investor with Epstein-Zin utility in an incomplete market. A non-Markovian environment with unbounded parameters is considered, which is more realistic in practical financial scenarios compared to the Markovian setting. The optimal consumption and investment strategies are derived using the martingale optimal principle and quadratic backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) whose solutions admit some exponential moment. This integrability property plays a crucial role in establishing a key martingale argument. In addition, the paper also examines the associated dual problem and several models within the specified parameter framework.

Introduction

In classical asset pricing models, an agent is assumed to have a time-additive von Neumann-Morgenstern utility, with two well-known types being constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) and constant relative risk aversion (CRRA). Since the landmark work of
Merton (1971)
utilizing the dynamic programming approach, there has been a substantial amount of literature focusing on the optimal consumption and investment problem with these utilities, including
Karatzas, Lehoczky and Shreve (1987)
developing the martingale approach,
Kramkov and Schachermayer (1999)
providing the duality result,
Hu, Imkeller and Müller (2005)
introducing the technique of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) and
Horst et al. (2014)
further proposing a new approach in terms of a fully coupled system of forward backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs).

However, widely used time-additive utilities inattentively restrict an artificial relationship between risk aversion
\gamma
and the elasticity of intertemporal substitution (EIS)
\psi
, leading to abundant asset pricing anomalies such as the risk-free rate puzzle, the equity premium puzzle, etc. To untie risk aversion from EIS,
Epstein-Zin
type recursive utility and its continuous-time counterpart, stochastic differential utility, were proposed by
Epstein and Zin (1989)
and
Duffie and Epstein (1992)
respectively, the latter of which could be defined rigorously using BSDEs.
Kraft and Seifried (2014)
have formally established the connection between these two formulations. One can refer to
Schroder and Skiadas (1999)
;
Seiferling and Seifried (2015)
;
Xing (2017)
for the existence and uniqueness results of Epstein-Zin stochastic differential utility (SDU) and its properties. Compared to time-additive utilities, recursive utilities and SDU provide a more general framework for addressing the aforementioned asset pricing anomalies, as demonstrated in
Bansal and Yaron (2004)
and
Benzoni, Collin-Dufresne and Goldstein (2011)
.

A significant amount of literature has focused on consumption and investment optimization with Epstein-Zin utility. For a non-Markovian setting,
Schroder and Skiadas (1999)
developed the utility gradient approach with
\theta=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-1/\psi}>0
.
Schroder and Skiadas (2003)
;
Schroder and Skiadas (2005)
extended it to include convex trading constraints under this parameter specification with unit EIS.
El Karoui, Peng and Quenez (2001)
stated a maximum principle for generalized recursive utilities by FBSDEs in the presence of nonlinear wealth. For a Markovian financial market,
Kraft, Seifried and Steffensen (2013)
;
Seiferling and Seifried (2015)
;
Kraft, Seiferling and Seifried (2017)
applied the dynamic programming principle to get the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation and established verification theorems, allowing for many possible configurations of
\gamma
and
\psi
.
Xing (2017)
adopted the martingale optimal principle combined with BSDEs for
\gamma,\psi>1
.
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
established a dual inequality between the primal and dual problems when
\gamma\psi\geq 1,\psi>1
or
\gamma\psi\leq 1,\psi<1
. Other papers include
Herdegen, Hobson and Jerome (2022)
for the infinite time Epstein-Zin utility model,
Aurand and Huang (2023)
;
Pu and Zhang (2024)
for applications, etc.

This paper investigates the optimal consumption and investment problem under Epstein-Zin utility in a non-Markovian market with unbounded parameters and
\gamma,\psi>1
, which is beyond
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
that requires bounded interest rate and market price of risk. The main contributions of the paper are the following three aspects. First, we extend the analysis to a non-Markovian model with unbounded parameters, especially unbounded market price of risk.
Schroder and Skiadas (1999)
;
Schroder and Skiadas (2003)
developed the utility gradient approach in a non-Markovian market with bounded market parameters. The former studied the bounded market price of risk and the latter the bounded interest rate.
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
proposed a dual problem in a non-Markovian market with bounded market price of risk. We relax the assumption of bounded parameters and instead consider exponentially integrable parameters. We use the martingale optimal principle to establish a non-Markovian BSDE with unbounded solutions; such a methodology was first proposed by
Hu, Imkeller and Müller (2005)
, then used by
Cheridito and Hu (2011)
for time-additive utilities and later by
Xing (2017)
for the Epstein-Zin framework.

Second, we obtain the martingale property with the martingale optimal principle and the dual equality with the exponential integrability of model parameters. In a non-Markovian market, we cannot rely on HJB equations from
Kraft, Seifried and Steffensen (2013)
;
Kraft, Seiferling and Seifried (2017)
nor Lyapunov functions from
Xing (2017)
, which are only applicable in a Markovian setting. The crucial difficulty with the martingale optimal principle is to verify that a certain exponential local martingale is a true martingale for the candidate optimal strategy.
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
considered a non-Markovian market with bounded interest rate and market price of risk, which ensures the martingale property automatically. We first derive some precise estimates on the solution of the BSDE, especially the exponential integrability property, and then use de La Vall
\rm\acute{e}
e Poussin’s lemma with the relative entropy method to directly characterize the martingale property from the BSDE itself, without relying on specific market structure. To our best knowledge, there are few papers discussing the convex duality in an incomplete market with stochastic differential utility except
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
for a non-Markovian model with bounded parameters. We find that the same duality (in)equality can be constructed for a non-Markovian model with unbounded parameters that have finite exponential moment of some order and, following some verified conclusions from
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
, obtain the optimal state price density and the associated class (D) property.

Third, our approach is applicable to some well-known models with non-Markovian structure and possibly unbounded parameters, such as the Heston model, linear diffusion model and CIR model. Several papers have investigated these models in a Markovian market or a non-Markovian market with bounded parameters, see
Hu, Imkeller and Müller (2005)
;
Cheridito and Hu (2011)
;
Gu, Lin and Yang (2016)
;
Xing (2017)
;
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
;
Feng and Tian (2023)
. Under our assumption that market parameters have an exponential moment of a certain order, BSDE techniques contribute to identify the optimal strategy of consumption and investment in these models.

Motivated by
Hu, Liang and Tang (2018)
and
Delbaen, Hu and Richou (2011)
, both
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024a)
;
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
and we studied Epstein-Zin optimization in unbounded markets, independently of each other. The ideas and methodologies are largely the same. The main differences are threefold: First,
r=0
in
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
while
r
is exponentially integrable in our case. When
r
is unbounded, the solution to the characterizing BSDE (
3.2
) becomes unbounded–a scenario not addressed in
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
. We established the martingale property for the key local martingale with unbounded
r
. Second,
0<\gamma\neq 1,\psi>1
in
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
while
\gamma>1,\psi>1
in our case to ensure the parameter range for the dual problem satisfies
\gamma\psi\geq 1,\psi>1
, which also makes our integrability conditions for the admissible class under convex duality further relaxed. Third,
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
investigated problems with convex closed set constraints while we did not. The analysis of the growth of the generator of the characterizing BSDE becomes more complex, but its growth nature remains fundamentally unchanged. Apart from Epstein-Zin optimization,
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024a)
;
Hu, Liang and Tang (2024b)
also discussed some other topics in utility maximization, an excellent research memoir and reference book.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the Epstein-Zin utility in a non-Markovian market and the associated consumption-investment problem. Section 3 discusses the main results, including the martingale optimal principle, the verification theorem, and the convex duality under our specified parameter conditions. Section 4 presents three examples in non-Markovian markets, including Heston, linear diffusion, and CIR models. Section 5 contains the proofs of main results (Proposition
3.1
, Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
). Section 6 concludes the paper.

2
The consumption-investment optimization under Epstein-Zin utility

In this section, we formulate the consumption-investment optimization problem under Epstein-Zin utility in a non-Markovian financial market.

2.1
The non-Markovian model setup

Given a time horizon
T<+\infty
. Let
\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\left(\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P}\right)
be a filtered probability space in which
\left(\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the natural filtration generated by a
n
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W
, satisfying the usual hypotheses, completeness and right-continuity.

Consider a financial market model comprising of a risk-free asset
S^{0}
and risky assets
S=(S^{1},\cdots,S^{d})
, which satisfy the dynamics

\displaystyle dS_{t}^{0}=r_{t}S_{t}^{0}dt,

\displaystyle dS_{t}=\operatorname{diag}(S_{t})\left[\left(r_{t}\mathbf{1}_{d}+\mu_{t}\right)dt+\sigma_{t}dW_{t}\right],

where diag(
S
) is a diagonal matrix with the elements of
S
on the diagonal and
\mathbf{1}_{d}
is a
d
-dimensional vector with each entry 1. The model coefficients
r
,
\mu
,
\sigma
are
\mathbb{R}
-valued,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued,
\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
-valued predictable stochastic processes, called risk-free rate, excess return and volatility processes respectively. We assume
d\leq n
, so the market can be incomplete.

We need to impose the following assumptions for the main results.

Assumption 2.1
.

For some
p>1
and
q>\max\left\{\frac{p(\gamma-1)(\gamma+2)}{\gamma(1+2(p-1)\gamma)},1\right\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(2p\gamma\int_{0}^{T}r_{s}^{-}ds\right)+\exp\left(q(\gamma-1)\int_{0}^{T}r_{s}^{+}ds\right)+\exp\left(q\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\Sigma_{s}^{-1}\mu_{s}ds\right)\right]<+\infty,

(2.1)

where
\Sigma:=\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
is positive definite and the relative risk aversion
\gamma>1
.

Assumption 2.2
.

r^{-}
is bounded.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumption
2.1
implies that
\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\Sigma_{s}^{-1}\mu_{s}ds\right)\right]<+\infty
, which indicates the market has no arbitrage opportunity by Novikov’s condition. Assumption
2.2
is reasonable from the viewpoint of finance.
There is a large literature on studying the Epstein-Zin utility model with different conditions on the parameters. For example, for Markovian models,
r,\mu,\sigma
are all bounded in
Kraft, Seifried and Steffensen (2013)
;
Kraft, Seiferling and Seifried (2017)
,
r+\frac{\mu^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mu}{2\gamma}
is bounded from below in
Xing (2017)
; for non-Markovian models,
r
is bounded in
Schroder and Skiadas (2003)
,
r
and
\mu^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mu
are both bounded or Markovian in
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
.

Assumption
2.1
looks technical. We thank the referees for suggesting a simplified sufficient condition, Assumption
2.3
, that implies Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
due to the fact that
\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p(\gamma-1)(\gamma+2)}{\gamma(1+2(p-1)\gamma)}={(\gamma-1)(\gamma+2)\over 2\gamma^{2}}<1
and we may choose
p
sufficiently large such that
\max\left\{\frac{p(\gamma-1)(\gamma+2)}{\gamma(1+2(p-1)\gamma)},1\right\}=1
.

Assumption 2.3
.

r^{-}
is bounded and for some
q>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\exp\left(q(\gamma-1)\int_{0}^{T}r_{s}^{+}ds\right)+\exp\left(q\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\Sigma_{s}^{-1}\mu_{s}ds\right)\right]<+\infty.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{+}
be the set of all nonnegative progressively measurable processes on
[0,T]\times\Omega
. For
c\in\mathcal{R}_{+}
,
c_{t}
represents the consumption rate at time
t
when
t<T
and a lump sum consumption when
t=T
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{d}
denote the set of all predictable processes taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

An agent invests in this financial market by selecting a consumption process
c\in\mathcal{R}_{+}
and an investment strategy
\pi\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
such that the associated wealth process
\mathcal{W}^{c,\pi}
is nonnegative. Given an initial wealth
\omega
,
\mathcal{W}^{c,\pi}
is given by, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle d\mathcal{W}_{t}^{c,\pi}=\mathcal{W}_{t}^{c,\pi}\left(\left(r_{t}+\pi_{t}^{\prime}\mu_{t}\right)dt+\pi_{t}^{\prime}\sigma_{t}dW_{t}\right)-c_{t}dt,\quad\mathcal{W}_{0}^{c,\pi}=\omega.

(2.2)

For simplicity of notation, we write
\mathcal{W}
instead of
\mathcal{W}^{c,\pi}
in the rest of the paper.

2.2
Epstein-Zin preferences and the associated optimization problem

Consider an agent whose preference over
\mathcal{R}_{+}
-valued consumption streams is described by a continuous-time stochastic differential utility of
Epstein-Zin
type. To this end, recall the relative risk aversion
\gamma>1
and let
\delta>0
and
\psi>1
stand for the discounting rate and the EIS respectively. Given a bequest utility function
V(c)=\frac{c^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}
, the
Epstein-Zin
utility for a pair of strategy (
c,\pi
) over a time horizon
T
is a semimartingale
V^{c,\pi}
satisfying, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle V_{t}^{c,\pi}=\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}f(c_{s},V_{s}^{c,\pi})ds+V(\mathcal{W}_{T})\Big|\mathscr{F}_{t}\right],

(2.3)

where
f:[0,+\infty)\times(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}
represents the Epstein-Zin aggregator, defined by

\displaystyle f(c,v)=\frac{\delta c^{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}}{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}\left((1-\gamma)v\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}-\delta\theta v,\quad{\rm with\ }\theta:=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}<0.

(2.4)

A pair of strategy
(c,\pi)
is called
admissible
if it belongs to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{(c,\pi):V^{c,\pi}{\rm\ exists\ },V^{c,\pi}<0,~~V^{c,\pi}~and~\ \mathcal{W}^{1-\gamma}e^{Y}{\rm\ are\ of\ class\ (D)}\right\},

(2.5)

where
Y
is defined by BSDE (
3.2
). There is a large amount of literature on the admissible class for the Epstein-Zin optimization including sufficient conditions for the existence of Epstein–Zin utility, which in particular indicates that
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
, see
Xing (2017)
;
Aurand and Huang (2023)
. The class
(\rm D)
condition is first proposed in
Hu, Imkeller and Müller (2005)
for time-additive utility. The class (D) property of
\mathcal{W}^{1-\gamma}e^{Y}
is to ensure that
V
is well-defined. From an economic perspective, admissibility, particularly the class (D) property, ensures that consumption-investment strategies remain financially viable over time.
The reader can refer to Proposition 2.1 in
Matoussi and Xing (2018)
for general discussion on admissible strategies.

The Epstein-Zin utility maximization problem is the following:

\displaystyle V_{0}:=\sup_{(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}}V_{0}^{c,\pi}=\sup_{(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}f(c_{s},V_{s}^{c,\pi})ds+V(\mathcal{W}_{T})\right].

(2.6)

3
Main results

In this section, with the help of the elaborate and effective priori estimate technique of BSDE, Proposition
3.1
investigates a specific BSDE (
3.2
) carefully, which is acquired by the martingale optimal principle. Then, the verification theorem is established for the candidate optimal strategy and the optimal value function (see Theorem
3.1
) in a non-Markovian market environment using the results of Proposition
3.1
. Finally, some connections with Epstein-Zin’s dual result are also discussed.

3.1
Consumption-investment optimization

In accordance with the special formulation of Epstein-Zin utility, for any
(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}
, we construct a utility process:

\displaystyle G_{t}^{c,\pi}:=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{t}^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{t}}+\int_{0}^{t}f\left(c_{s},\frac{\mathcal{W}_{s}^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}\right)ds,\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{W}
is the wealth process in (
2.2
) and
Y
satisfies, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\int_{t}^{T}H(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle H(t,y,z)=

\displaystyle z\left(\frac{1}{2}I_{n}+\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}\sigma_{t}^{\prime}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\sigma_{t}\right)z^{\prime}+\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}\mu_{t}^{\prime}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\sigma_{t}z^{\prime}+\frac{\theta}{\psi}\delta^{\psi}e^{-\frac{\psi}{\theta}y}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\gamma}{2\gamma}\mu_{t}^{\prime}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\mu_{t}+(1-\gamma)r_{t}-\delta\theta.

(3.3)

Remark 3.1
.

H
is chosen to ensure
G^{c,\pi}
in (
3.1
) is a local supermartingale for any control
(c,\pi)
and a local martingale for the candidate optimal control
(c^{*},\pi^{*})
under the parameter specification
\gamma,\psi>1
, see Proposition
3.2
. Similar equations were first introduced by
Hu, Imkeller and Müller (2005)
for the time-additive utility maximization in an incomplete market, where they constructed BSDEs whose generators are quadratic in
z
but don’t contain
y
. The generator (
3.3
) has a quadratic term in
z
and also an exponential term in
y
, which is the same as equation (2.13) in
Xing (2017)
.

The next result plays an important role in proving the martingale property in Theorem
3.1
.
Compared with Proposition 2.9 in
Xing (2017)
, where the author imposed the condition that
r+\frac{\mu^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mu}{2\gamma}
is bounded from below, our integrability condition is stronger as we need it to establish better prior estimates of the solutions that would help us to bypass Lyapunov’s arguments for non-Markovian models.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\gamma,\psi>1
and Assumption
2.1
holds. Then BSDE (
3.2
) admits a unique solution
(Y,Z)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{2p(Y_{\cdot}^{+})_{*}}+e^{q(Y_{\cdot}^{-})_{*}}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\right]<+\infty,

(3.4)

where
(Y_{\cdot})_{*}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}|
. Moreover, both

\displaystyle\left\{\exp\left(2pY_{t}^{+}+2p(\gamma-1)\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}^{-}ds\right)\right\}_{t\in[0,T]}

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle\left\{\exp\left(qY_{t}^{-}+q(\gamma-1)\int_{0}^{t}\left(r^{+}_{s}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\Sigma_{s}^{-1}\mu_{s}\right)ds\right)\right\}_{t\in[0,T]}

(3.6)

are of class
(\rm D)
with
p
and
q
given in Assumption
2.1
. Furthermore, assume Assumption
2.2
also holds, then
Y
is bounded from above by a constant.

Remark 3.2
.

Motivated by
Fan, Hu and Tang (2020)
;
Fan, Hu and Tang (2023)
, we use the test function method and the priori estimate technique, which are standard procedures for the existence of solutions to BSDEs. We find that BSDE (
3.2
) is concave with respect to
Y
; it is therefore natural to use the convex-dual method proposed by
Delbaen, Hu and Richou (2011)
to prove the uniqueness of
(Y,Z)
.

Remark 3.3
.

It follows from (
3.4
) that the solution
Y
to BSDE (
3.2
) is exponentially integrable, and
Z
is square integrable, both under the original probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. We consider the positive and negative parts of
Y
separately to examine its exponential integrability. In particular, the exponential integrability of
Y^{+}
plays a crucial role in proving the martingale property in (
5.15
). Moreover,
e^{Y^{+}}
and
e^{Y^{-}}
possess a stronger property of being uniformly integrable, as shown in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).

The following proposition presents the martingale optimal principle under Assumption
2.1
. We reformulate it from the proof of Lemma B.1 and Theorem 2.14 of
Xing (2017)
in our admissible class
\mathcal{A}
. It is sufficient to show the existence of the optimality that
G^{c,\pi}
is a local supermartingale and
G^{c^{*},\pi^{*}}
is a local martingale, thanks to the special structure of
G^{c,\pi}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose Assumption
2.1
holds and
\gamma,\psi>1
. Let
(Y,Z)
be the solution to BSDE (
3.2
). Then

(i)

For any
(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
G^{c,\pi}
is a local supermartingale.

(ii)

Define the candidate optimal strategy

\displaystyle c^{*}=\delta^{\psi}e^{-\frac{\psi}{\theta}Y}\mathcal{W}^{*},\quad\pi^{*}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\Sigma^{-1}\left(\mu+\sigma Z^{\prime}\right),

(3.7)

then
G^{c^{*},\pi^{*}}
is a local martingale. Furthermore, if
(c^{*},\pi^{*})\in\mathcal{A}
, then
(c^{*},\pi^{*})
is the optimal strategy for problem (
2.6
).

Proof
.

For any
(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}
,
recalling from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle dG_{t}^{c,\pi}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{W}_{t}^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{t}}\Bigg[\left(Z_{t}+(1-\gamma)\pi_{t}^{\prime}\sigma_{t}\right)dW_{t}+\big(\delta\theta e^{-\frac{Y_{t}}{\theta}}\hat{c}_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}-(1-\gamma)\hat{c}_{t}+(1-\gamma)\pi_{t}^{\prime}(\mu_{t}+\sigma_{t}Z_{t}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)}{2})\pi_{t}^{\prime}\Sigma_{t}\pi_{t}-H(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})+(1-\gamma)r_{t}-\delta\theta+\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}Z_{t}^{\prime}\big)dt\Bigg],

(3.8)

where
\hat{c}=\frac{c}{\mathcal{W}}
. The drift term in (
3.8
) is a concave function of
(\hat{c},\pi)
and the maximum is achieved at
(c^{*},\pi^{*})
in (
3.7
) with the maximum value 0 thanks to the definition of

in (
3.3
). Therefore,

is a local supermartingale for any
(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}
and a local martingale for
(c^{*},\pi^{*})
. Taking an appropriate stopping time sequence
\{\varsigma_{n},n\geq 1\}
with
\varsigma_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}T
, for any
(c,\pi)\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[G_{\varsigma_{n}}^{c,\pi}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}f\left(c_{s},\frac{\left(\mathcal{W}_{s}\right)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}\right)ds+\frac{\left(\mathcal{W}_{\varsigma_{n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{\varsigma_{n}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq G_{0}^{c,\pi}=G_{0}^{c^{*},\pi^{*}}=\mathbf{E}\left[G_{\varsigma_{n}}^{c^{*},\pi^{*}}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}f\left(c^{*}_{s},\frac{\left(\mathcal{W}^{*}_{s}\right)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}\right)ds+\frac{\left(\mathcal{W}^{*}_{\varsigma_{n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{\varsigma_{n}}}\right].

This implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{(\mathcal{W}_{\varsigma_{n}})^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{\varsigma_{n}}}+\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}\delta\frac{\left(c_{s}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}}{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}\left((\mathcal{W}_{s})^{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}ds\right]+\delta\theta\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}\frac{(\mathcal{W}_{s}^{*})^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{(\mathcal{W}_{\varsigma_{n}}^{*})^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{\varsigma_{n}}}+\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}\delta\frac{\left(c_{s}^{*}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}}{1-\frac{1}{\psi}}\left((\mathcal{W}_{s}^{*})^{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}ds\right]+\delta\theta\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\varsigma_{n}}\frac{(\mathcal{W}_{s})^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}e^{Y_{s}}ds\right].

If
(c^{*},\pi^{*})\in\mathcal{A}
, sending
n\to\infty
, the monotone convergence theorem and the class (D) property of
(\mathcal{W})^{1-\gamma}e^{Y}
and
(\mathcal{W}^{*})^{1-\gamma}e^{Y}
yields

Remark 4.2

6
Conclusion

This paper studies the optimal consumption–investment problem for an investor with Epstein–Zin utility in a non-Markovian market. The optimal consumption and investment strategies are characterized via a quadratic BSDE. The key observation is that the first component of the solution to this BSDE admits some given exponential moments when the market parameters are exponentially integrable. Consequently, we can verify that a certain exponential local martingale is a true martingale and further obtain the admissibility of the candidate optimal strategy. Then we introduce a dual problem for Epstein-Zin optimization and present the duality equality with non-Markovian markets. Several examples are illustrated and discussed. There remain many open questions, for example, do Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
still hold for Vasicek’s short rate model with unbounded interest rate? We leave this and other questions for future research.

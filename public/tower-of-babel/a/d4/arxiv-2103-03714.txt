Quantum (dis)charge of black hole interiors
Christiane Klein, Jochen Zahn, Stefan Hollands

Abstract
We analyze the “vacuum” polarization induced by a quantum charged scalar field near the inner horizon of a charged black hole in quantum states evolving from arbitrary regular in states. Contrary to naive expectations, we find that near an inner horizon, the transversal component of the expected current density can have either sign depending on the black hole and field parameters. Thus, the inner horizon can be charged or discharged. But we find that it is always discharged close to extremality. We also find that quantum effects dominate in that the strength of the blow up of the quantum current at the inner horizon is state-independent and stronger than that of the current of a classical solution.



I
Introduction

It is well known that black hole (BH) interiors, such as e.g. in the Kerr- or Reissner-Nordström
(-deSitter) spacetimes, pose interesting questions regarding determinism. A common feature of these spacetimes is the existence of an “inner” or “Cauchy” horizon
\mathcal{CH}
, beyond which the evolution of classical or quantum fields cannot be predicted from their initial data on a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
at early times, see Fig.
1
.

The strong cosmic censorship (sCC) conjecture
Penrose 1974
proposes that the issue is academic because
local observables are expected to diverge at
\mathcal{CH}
. A more precise formulation of this idea due to
Christodoulou 2009
states that sCC holds if the stress tensor of a classical field, such as a scalar or a linearized gravitational field, is locally not integrable near
\mathcal{CH}
for generic smooth initial data on
\Sigma
, thus preventing one from continuing the solution beyond
\mathcal{CH}
. Hence, one must understand the
strength of the singularity of physical fields near
\mathcal{CH}
to decide whether sCC saves determinism or not.

{\mathrm{III}}

Estimates for the degree of divergence of an (uncharged) classical Klein-Gordon (KG) field
\Phi
near the Cauchy horizon of a subextremal Reissner-Nordström-deSitter (RNdS) spacetime were suggested already in the 1990’s
Mellor and Moss 1990
;
Mellor and Moss 1992
;
Brady et al. 1998
and have been revisited in recent years
Costa et al. 2015a
;
Costa et al. 2015b
;
Costa et al. 2017
;
Hintz and Vasy 2017
;
Costa and Franzen 2017
;
Cardoso et al. 2018a
:

Hintz and Vasy 2017
have characterized the singular behavior of an arbitrary solution
\Phi
arising from smooth initial data on
\Sigma
by its membership in the Sobolev space
H^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}
near

\mathcal{CH}^{R}
. Here,
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{\kappa_{-}}
where
\alpha
is the spectral gap of quasinormal modes and
\kappa_{-}
is the surface gravity of
\mathcal{CH}
.
By tracing the dependency of
\beta
on the BH mass
M
, charge
Q
, and cosmological constant
\Lambda
, it was found
that
\beta
can become
>\frac{1}{2}

Cardoso et al. 2018a
. This implies that the stress tensor of the classical field
\Phi
is locally integrable (meaning roughly that
T_{VV}\sim V^{-2+2\beta}
in terms of a Kruskal coordinate locating
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
at
V=0
, see Fig.
1
), constituting a violation of sCC.

Given this state of affairs it is of interest to understand whether expectation values of quantum fields diverge near
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
and how the degree of divergence compares to classical fields.

It was already argued heuristically in the 1970’s
Birrell and Davies 1978
that the expected stress tensor in a generic quantum state should diverge at
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
. This was recently proven
Hollands et al. 2020a
for the real KG field in RNdS, in the following sense: in any state
\Psi
which is regular (i.e. “Hadamard”) in a neighborhood of a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
as in Fig.
1
, one has, for
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
,

{\langle T_{VV}\rangle}_{\Psi}\sim CV^{-2},

(1)

with
C
a constant which only depends on the parameters
M,Q,\Lambda
of the spacetime and the mass
\mu
of the field, but not on the quantum state
\Psi
. The state dependence only enters through subleading terms, which are no more singular than the stress tensor for the classical field. The constant
C
has to be determined numerically and was found to be generically nonzero
Hollands et al. 2020b
(see also previous work
Zilberman et al. 2020
on Reissner-Nordström and special states). Interestingly, both signs of
C
can occur.
Assuming that the expected stress tensor backreacts onto the metric via the semiclassical Einstein equation

G_{\nu\rho}+\Lambda g_{\nu\rho}=8\pi({\langle T_{\nu\rho}\rangle}_{\Psi}+E_{\nu\rho}),

(2)

with
E_{\nu\rho}
the stress tensor of the electromagnetic field, the two signs of
C
correspond to distinct behavior (infinite stretching resp. squeezing) of freely falling observers crossing
\mathcal{CH}^{R}

Zilberman et al. 2020
;
Hollands et al. 2020a
.

As the formation of charged BHs necessitates the presence of charged matter, it is actually more natural to consider a charged scalar field
\Phi
with the usual minimal coupling
\nabla_{\nu}\to D_{\nu}=\nabla_{\nu}-iqA_{\nu}
, where
q
is the charge of the field. It is known
Zhu et al. 2014
;
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2014
;
Dias et al. 2019
;
Hod 2018
that for certain values of
(M,Q,\Lambda)
and
(\mu,q)
, there are classical instabilities (exponentially growing modes) already in the exterior region
{\mathrm{I}}
. Excluding such unstable spacetimes,
we have a positive spectral gap
\alpha>0
and again
\Phi\in H^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}
locally near
\mathcal{CH}^{R}

Cardoso et al. 2018b
. The dependence of
\beta=\alpha/\kappa_{-}
on the parameters is influenced by the field charge
q
. There is nevertheless a parameter range–though considerably smaller than for the uncharged scalar field–for which
\beta>\frac{1}{2}

Dias et al. 2019
;
Cardoso et al. 2018b
. A proof that (
1
) also holds for the charged scalar field (with constant
C
now also
q
dependent) is presented in Supplemental Material
Klein et al. 2021
, which includes Refs.
Gérard and Wrochna 2016
;
Verch 1994
.

In the presence of a charged quantum field, backreaction should also take place via the semiclassical Maxwell equation

\nabla^{\rho}F_{\rho\nu}=-4\pi{\langle J_{\nu}\rangle}_{\Psi},

(3)

due to vacuum polarization, i.e., an expectation value for the current
J_{\nu}
. At the event horizon
{\mathcal{H}}^{R}
, this current is responsible for the discharge of the BH via Hawking radiation
Hawking 1975
;
Gibbons 1975
;
Unruh 1976
. While vacuum polarization always discharges the event horizon, its influence on the Cauchy horizon is not obvious.
It has been argued invoking the Schwinger process
Schwinger 1951
that pair creation in the interior (region
{\mathrm{II}}
) should discharge the Cauchy horizon, i.e., there should be a net current from the left Cauchy horizon
(\mathcal{CH}^{L})
to the right one
(\mathcal{CH}^{R})

Herman and Hiscock 1994
;
Sorkin and Piran 2001
.

Their arguments are, however, not completely convincing: The BH interior is not stationary, so the very notion of particle is ambiguous there. Furthermore, already at the classical level the behavior of fields at
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
is due to a very nonlocal effect:
a competition between decay (in region
{\mathrm{I}}
) and blue shift (region
{\mathrm{II}}
). Hence, Schwinger’s formula for pair creation seems not applicable/relevant and a first principle calculation of
{\langle J_{\nu}\rangle}_{\Psi}
at the Cauchy horizon is needed to settle this important question. This is the main novelty presented in our letter. Most interestingly, we find that the relevant component of the current,
{\langle J_{V}\rangle}_{\Psi}
, can have either sign at
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
, depending on the parameters
Q,M,\Lambda
of the spacetime and
\mu,q
of the field. It follows that, via backreaction, the Cauchy horizon can also be charged by quantum effects, contrary to naive expectations
Herman and Hiscock 1994
;
Sorkin and Piran 2001
.

More precisely, we argue that, in any quantum state
\Psi
which is initially Hadamard near
\Sigma
, the expectation value of the current diverges at
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
, with leading divergence

{\langle J_{V}\rangle}_{\Psi}\sim C^{\prime}V^{-1},

(4)

where
C^{\prime}
is independent of
\Psi
and can have either sign. The state dependence again enters through subleading terms which behave as the current of a classical field i.e. roughly as
J_{V}\sim V^{-1+\beta}
.
Hence, as for the stress tensor, quantum effects dominate over classical effects close to the Cauchy horizon.

Our results thus show that the leading divergence of relevant observables near the Cauchy horizon is of quantum origin and state independent (so that no appeal to “generic” initial data is necessary), but also that the ensuing backreaction effects can differ drastically from classical expectations (“mass inflation”)
Poisson and Israel 1989
: Not only is a tidal stretching of observers possible (corresponding to “mass deflation”), but also “charge inflation”. These possibilities clearly correspond to drastically different forms of the terminal singularity replacing the Cauchy horizon.

II
Geometric setup and backreaction

The metric
g
and vector potential
A
of the RNdS spacetime (Fig.
1
) are given in natural units
\hbar=c=G_{N}=4\pi\epsilon_{0}=1
by

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=-f(r)\text{d}t^{2}+f(r)^{-1}\text{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(5a)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}+1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}

(5b)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q}{r}\text{d}t,

(5c)

with
\text{d}\Omega^{2}
the area element of the unit sphere. The function
f(r)
has three positive roots
r_{c}>r_{+}>r_{-}
, corresponding to the cosmological (
\mathcal{H}_{c}
), event (
\mathcal{H}
) and Cauchy, horizon (
\mathcal{CH}
) of the BH.

A useful alternative radial coordinate is the tortoise coordinate
r_{*}
, defined by
f(r)\text{d}r_{*}=\text{d}r
. One defines radial null coordinates

v\equiv t+r_{*}
and
u\equiv t-r_{*}
.
Their ranges are indicated by blue arrows in Fig.
1
, pointing from
-\infty
to
+\infty
.
To extend the metric smoothly across the horizons,
we use the Kruskal coordinates

U\equiv\mp e^{-\kappa_{+}u}
,
V_{c}\equiv-e^{-\kappa_{c}v}
, and
V\equiv-e^{-\kappa_{-}v}
,
where
\kappa_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}|f^{\prime}(r_{i})|
are the surface gravities on the corresponding horizons. For

, we use the
-
sign in the exterior, and the
+
sign in the interior, and the
V
coordinates are defined in the exterior (
V_{c}
) and interior (
V
) respectively. Note that
V=0
on
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
and that, by the tensor transformation law, the constants
C,C^{\prime}

in (
1
) and (
4
) are given by
C=\kappa_{-}^{-2}{\langle T_{vv}\rangle}_{\Psi}
and
C^{\prime}=-\kappa_{-}^{-1}{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}
, where the expectation values are computed on the inner horizon.
In the following, we present our results in terms of
{\langle T_{vv}\rangle}_{\Psi}
and
{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}
.

Before presenting our arguments in favor of (
4
), let us briefly discuss backreaction effects.
We proceed heuristically and assume that the main contribution to
backreaction is spherically symmetric, as the leading divergence in (
4
). Thus, for the metric
g
and the field strength tensor
F
, involving backreaction, we make the ansatz

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=-e^{\sigma}\text{d}u\text{d}v+r^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2},

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q}{2r^{2}}e^{\sigma}\text{d}u\wedge\text{d}v,

(6)

where
\sigma
,
r
, and
Q
are functions of
u
and
v
. By Gauß’s law,
Q(u,v)
is the charge contained within the sphere at
u,v=\text{const}
, which has area
4\pi r^{2}
.

In a first approximation, we now use the expectation value of the current obtained on the RNdS background and obtain, from the
v
component of (
3
),

{\partial}_{v}Q=-4\pi r^{2}{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}.

(7)

Hence, the sign of
{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}
, evaluated on
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
, determines whether quantum effects discharge (
{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}>0
) or charge (
{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\Psi}<0
) the BH interior.
The resulting behavior of the field strength
\frac{Q}{r^{2}}
near
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
is discussed in
Klein et al. 2021
, see also
Zilberman et al. 2020
.

III
The scalar field and its current

The charged scalar field
\Phi
obeys the KG equation

\displaystyle\left[D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2}\right]\Phi

\displaystyle=0,

(8)

The mode ansatz

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell m}=(4\pi|\omega|)^{-1/2}r^{-1}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)e^{-i\omega t}H_{\omega\ell}(r)\,,

(9)

with
Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)
the spherical harmonics, reduces (
8

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r_{*}}^{2}+\left(\omega-\frac{qQ}{r}\right)^{2}-W\right]H_{\omega\ell}(r_{*})=0

(10a)

\displaystyle W=f(r)\left(\frac{\partial_{r}f(r)}{r}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+\mu^{2}\right)\,.

(10b)

Using gauge transformations of the form

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\to A+\frac{Q}{r_{0}}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\to e^{i\tfrac{qQ}{r_{0}}t}\Phi

(11)

we can set the potential to zero at any chosen
r_{0}
. If
r_{0}\in\{r_{+},r_{-},r_{c}\}
, the solutions
h_{\omega\ell}(r_{*},t)=e^{-i\omega t}H_{\omega\ell}(r_{*})

We will denote the gauge where

Following standard procedures

\mathcal{H}_{c}^{-}\cup\mathcal{H}^{-}\cup\mathcal{H}^{L}

and
\sim 0

h^{(+)\mathrm{up}}_{\omega\ell}\sim e^{-i\omega U}
on
\mathcal{H}^{-}\cup\mathcal{H}^{L}

The field operator is then given by an expansion in the positive frequency solutions,

\displaystyle\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\sum_{\lambda,\ell,m}\left(\Phi_{\omega\ell m}^{\lambda}(x)a_{\omega\ell m}^{\lambda}+\Phi_{-\omega\ell m}^{\lambda}(x)b_{\omega\ell m}^{\lambda\dagger}\right)\,,

where the coefficients
a_{\omega\ell m}^{\lambda}

b_{\omega\ell m}^{\lambda}

\mathrm{in}

The observable driving backreaction onto the electromagnetic field is the current density given by

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(x)=iq\left(\Phi(x)(D_{\nu}\Phi)^{*}(x)-\Phi^{*}(x)D_{\nu}\Phi(x)\right)\,.

It is local and non-linear in the field, so it requires renormalization in quantum field theory. To evaluate its expectation value in the state

Hollands et al. 2020a
,
the expectation value in the Unruh state
{\langle J_{V}\rangle}_{\mathrm{U}}

4

Klein et al. 2021
, this is shown rigorously for
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
, analogously to

To evaluate

Sela 2018

Zilberman et al. 2020

Klein and Zahn 2021

v

\mathcal{CH}^{L}
, which coincides with the value on
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
because the state is stationary, by taking the limit of
(
Klein and Zahn 2021

onto the horizon

The ”Boulware modes” defined in
Klein and Zahn 2021
behave as

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{(+)\mathrm{in},{\mathrm{I}}}_{\omega\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{I}}_{(\omega-\omega_{{\mathrm{I}}})\ell}\frac{\omega}{\omega-\omega_{{\mathrm{I}}}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{I}})v}\quad\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{R}

(14a)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{(+)\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{I}}}_{\omega\ell m}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{I}}_{\omega\ell}e^{-i\omega v}\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{R}

(14b)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{(-)\mathrm{in},{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega\ell m}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{T}^{{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega\ell}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{{\mathrm{II}}})v}\qquad\qquad\text{ on }\mathcal{CH}^{L}

(14c)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{(-)\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega\ell m}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega\ell}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{{\mathrm{II}}})v}\qquad\qquad\text{ on }\mathcal{CH}^{L}\,,

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{N}}_{\omega\ell}

Inserting this into

\displaystyle{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\mathrm{U}}

\displaystyle F_{\ell}(\omega)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\omega\csch\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega^{+}}{\kappa_{+}}\right)}{\omega^{+}}\mathrm{Re}\left(\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{I}}_{\omega^{+}\ell}}\mathcal{T}^{{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega^{+}\ell}\mathcal{R}^{{\mathrm{II}}}_{\omega^{+}\ell}\right),

.

IV
Numerical results

To compute the scattering coefficients, (
10a
) has to be solved numerically. Following
Suzuki et al. 1999
, in the case of a conformal mass
\mu^{2}=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}
, one can bring the equation into the form of a Heun differential equation by a special choice of ansatz. The solutions to the Heun equation have been implemented in
Mathematica
as special functions
Hatsuda 2020
. For a general
\mu^{2}
, the same ansatz yields a slightly more general equation, that can however be solved, near any of the horizons, by a power series as described in
Hollands et al. 2020b
. By comparing the solutions around different horizons, one can calculate the scattering coefficients
Hollands et al. 2020b
.

For small enough
\mu
, the integrand in (
15a
) is rapidly decreasing both in
\omega
and
\ell
. We thus calculate the scattering coefficients and the corresponding
F_{\ell}(\omega)
, c.f. (
15b
), for different values of
\omega
and
\ell
up to some maximal values beyond which the contribution to (
15a
) is negligible.
Then, the integral in (
15a
) is estimated by a Riemann sum, which is also used to estimate the errors of this procedure.

Figure

corresponds to the discharge of the Cauchy horizon, while for
{\langle J_{v}\rangle}_{\mathrm{U}}<0

However, away from extremality, a sign change may occur, so that quantum effects can tend to charge Cauchy horizons of BHs which are far enough away from extremality. One may also check that, for charges
q

, i.e. the fine-structure constant, compatible with expectations.

The possibility of a sign change of the current at the inner horizon is in stark contrast to the situation at the outer horizon, where vacuum polarization always tends to discharge the BH

;
Gibbons 1975
in physically reasonable states. We attribute the phenomenon to the scattering of modes entering the BH through the event horizon off the potential barrier (
10b

Note, however, that it is impossible to clearly distinguish the contributions from the inflow of a current through the event horizon and the creation of a current in the BH interior, as the up modes in the interior, which are responsible for the latter effect, are entangled with the up modes in the exterior region, leading to interference effects, manifest in the last term in (
15b

Combining the results for the current and the stress tensor, we find that, for the parameters considered in Fig.

\partial_{v}r=-4\pi r_{-}/\kappa_{-}\langle T_{vv}\rangle_{\Psi}

We note that the parameter range considered in Fig.

To achieve comparability with
Hollands et al. 2020b
;
Cardoso et al. 2018a
, a reasonable performance of the numerical code, and to avoid the classical instability

\Lambda
was chosen unrealistically high, so that the event horizon

which is beyond the regime accessible to our numerical code for realistic BH masses. Similarly, the assumption of a conformal mass for
\Phi
is unrealistic, but semirealistic masses

Hence, our results unfortunately cannot be used to infer the fate of the Cauchy horizon of semirealistic BHs. However, they are sufficient to demonstrate that the naive expectation that quantum effects will always discharge the BH interior is false. It would be interesting to see whether a similar effect occurs for rotating (Kerr) black holes where (dis-)charging
would now correspond to (down-)upspinning of the Cauchy horizon and
J_{v}
would correspond to
T_{v\phi}
.

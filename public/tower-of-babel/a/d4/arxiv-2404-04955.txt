Abstract
. For a right-continuous nondecreasing and unbounded function V V of at most exponential growth, which vanishes on the negative halfline, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the Lebesgue-Stieltjes convolution powers V ∗ ( j ) ​ ( t ) V^{\ast(j)}(t) as both j j and t t tend to infinity. We obtain a comprehensive asymptotic formula for V ∗ ( j ) ​ ( t ) V^{\ast(j)}(t) , which is valid across different regimes of simultaneous growth of j j and t t . Our main technical tool is an exponential change of measure, which is a standard technique in the large deviations theory. Various applications of our result are given.

1.
Introduction

Let
V:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a right-continuous and nondecreasing function vanishing on
(-\infty,0)
. The function
V
can be thought of as the distribution function of a measure
\mu
on the nonnegative half-line, that is,

\mu([0,\,t])=V(t),\quad t\geq 0.

Let
\mu^{\ast(j)}
(respectively,
V^{\ast(j)}
) denote the
j
-th convolution power in the Lebesgue–Stieltjes sense of measure
\mu
(respectively, of function
V
), so that

\mu^{\ast(j)}([0,\,t])=V^{\ast(j)}(t)=\int_{[0,\,t]}V^{\ast(j-1)}(t-y){\rm d}V(y),\quad t\geq 0,\quad j\geq 2.

Let
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers which diverges to
+\infty
as
j\to\infty
.
In this note we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
V^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
as
j\to\infty
. If

\lim_{x\to\infty}V(x)\in(0,\infty),

(1.1)

then, by simple rescaling, we may assume that the limit is equal to
1
and
\mu
is a probability measure. A basic result of probability theory, called the weak law of large numbers, then tells us that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}V^{\ast(j)}(({\tt m}-\varepsilon)j)=0,\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}V^{\ast(j)}(({\tt m}+\varepsilon)j)=1,

(1.2)

where
{\tt m}:=\int_{[0,\,\infty)}x{\rm d}V(x)
is assumed finite. Under the additional assumption
{\tt s}^{2}:=\int_{[0,\,\infty)}x^{2}{\rm d}V(x)-{\tt m}^{2}\in(0,\infty)
, the celebrated central limit theorem provides a refinement of (
1.2
):

\lim_{j\to\infty}V^{\ast(j)}({\tt m}j+x{\tt s}\sqrt{j})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}{\rm e}^{-y^{2}/2}{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

If
{\tt s}^{2}=\infty
or even
{\tt m}=\infty
, so-called ‘stable’ versions of (
1.3
) are still available under conditions of regular variation. This involves replacing
t_{j}=t_{j}(x)={\tt m}j+x{\tt s}\sqrt{j}
on the left-hand side with another appropriate sequence defined by the rate at which
V(t)
tends to
1
. Further results on the asymptotic behavior of
V^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
, for other classes of sequences
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, follow from classic large- and moderate-deviations theory making the picture of asymptotic regimes of
V^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
under assumption (
1.1
) is more or less complete. For this circle of questions we refer to the classic book
[
12
]
.

A related problem has been addressed in the articles
[
6
,
9
,
14
,
16
]
. These are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of
W^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
as
j\to\infty
. Here,
W:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
is a function of a finite support, and
W^{\ast(k)}
is the
k
-fold Lebesgue convolution of
W
with itself defined by
W^{\ast(1)}:=W
and
W^{\ast(k)}(t):=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}W^{\ast(k-1)}(t-s)W(s)
for
k\geq 2
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
. In this setting the connections with probability theory vanish. One particularly interesting aspect of these works is the appearance in the asymptotic behavior of
W^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
of the functions
H_{m}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}zx}H_{m}(x){\rm d}x=\exp(-z^{m})
for
z\in\mathbb{R}
and even
m\geq 2
and
=\exp({\rm i}z^{m})
for
z\in\mathbb{R}
and odd
m\geq 3
. If the function
W
is nonnegative, as in our setting, then the asymptotic shape is driven by the Gaussian density
H_{2}
, similarly to (
1.3
), whereas if the function
W
is complex-valued or real-valued and takes values of both signs, then
H_{m}
may arise with any
m\geq 2
. In
[
5
,
13
,
15
]
the asymptotic behavior of
W^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
as
j\to\infty
was investigated for some particular subclasses of functions
W:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|W(x)|<\infty
. The arguments of all the papers mentioned in this paragraph are heavily based on Fourier analysis.

Returning to our setting of real-valued nondecreasing functions
V
, assume now that the limit in (
1.1
) is equal to
+\infty
, meaning that the measure
\mu
is infinite. As far as we know, under this assumption, the problem of our interest has not been investigated so far, and the present paper aims at (partly) closing this gap. Although our results differ significantly from those obtained in the aforementioned articles, they share some similarities with the classic theory of large deviations for probability measures, see, for instance, Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
or Theorem 1 in
[
11
]
.

2.
The setting

Throughout the paper we assume that

\lim_{x\to\infty}V(x)=+\infty,

(2.1)

and that the sequence
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
grows at least linearly. Also we shall normally write
t
for
t_{j}
. The standing assumption in what follows is

\hat{V}(s):=\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>0,

(2.2)

which means that
\hat{V}(s^{\prime})
is also finite for all
s^{\prime}\geq s
. Depending on
V
the function
\hat{V}
may be finite for all
s>0
or
s\geq s_{1}
or
s>s_{1}
for some
s_{1}>0
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
the domain of
\hat{V}
, that is,

\mathcal{D}:=\{s>0:\hat{V}(s)<\infty\}.

It is a standard fact that if
\hat{V}(s)
is defined for
s>s_{0}
, then it possesses an analytic continuation to the domain
\{s\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}\,s>s_{0}\}
, see
[
19
]
. Thus, the
function

s\mapsto\lambda(s):=\log\hat{V}(s)=\log\int_{[0,\,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)

is well-defined in a sufficiently small complex domain containing
{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}
, the interior of
\mathcal{D}
. Here
\log
is the principal branch of the complex logarithm.

The function
\lambda
is infinitely differentiable on
{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}
. In particular,
-\lambda^{\prime}
is a continuous function on
{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}
. Assumption
(
2.1
) excludes the situation in which
V(x)=a_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{[a_{2},\infty)}(x)
for
x\geq 0
and some positive
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
. The function
\lambda
is then strictly convex on
{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}
which entails that the function
-\lambda^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing on
{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}
. Thus, for every
s\in(s_{-},s_{+})
, where

s_{-}:=\inf_{s\in{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))\quad\text{and}\quad s_{+}:=\sup_{s\in{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{D}}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s)),

the equation
-\lambda^{\prime}(x)=s
has a unique solution. According to Lemma
6.1
in the Appendix

s_{-}=\inf\{x>0:\,V(x)>0\}.

In most applications
s_{+}=+\infty
, and the equation
-\lambda^{\prime}(x)=s
then has a unique solution for all
s>s_{-}
. Nevertheless, the situation where
s_{+}<\infty
is also possible, see Example
2.7
below.

3.
Main results

We call
V
arithmetic, if it is piecewise-constant with jumps at points belonging to the lattice
(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some
\omega_{1}\geq 0
and
\omega_{2}>0
, and nonarithmetic, otherwise. Put
a(j):=(j\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\kappa(j)))^{1/2}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
j\to\infty
and
t=t_{j}\to\infty
in such a way that (
2.3
) holds true.

(a) Assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)a(j)=\infty
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}{\rm e}^{-\gamma j}\kappa(j)a(j)=0
for all
\gamma>0
. Put

T_{j}:=\frac{|\lambda^{\prime}(\kappa(j))|^{3}}{\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\kappa(j))}+\frac{|\hat{V}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\kappa(j))|}{\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\kappa(j))\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}.

Suppose further that

T_{j}=o(a(j)),\quad j\to\infty

(3.1)

and, for every fixed
\gamma>0
,

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sup_{|z|\geq\gamma}\Big|\frac{\hat{V}(\kappa(j)-{\rm i}z/T_{j})}{\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}\Big|<1.

(3.2)

Then

V^{\ast(j)}(t)~\sim~\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\frac{(\hat{V}(\kappa(j)))^{j}{\rm e}^{t\kappa(j)}}{\kappa(j)a(j)},\quad j\to\infty.

(3.3)

(b) Assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)=\theta\in(0,\infty)
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\kappa(j))=\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
and that
V
is nonarithmetic. Then

V^{\ast(j)}(t)~\sim~\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{1/2}\theta}\frac{(\hat{V}(\kappa(j)))^{j}{\rm e}^{t\kappa(j)}}{j^{1/2}},\quad j\to\infty.

(3.4)

Remark 3.2
.

Note the under the assumptions of part (b) relations (
3.1
) hold automatically.

Denote by
p({\tt m},{\tt s}^{2})
the density of a normal random variable with mean
{\tt m}\in\mathbb{R}
and variance
{\tt s}^{2}>0
given by

p({\tt m},{\tt s}^{2};y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\tt s}^{2})^{1/2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{(y-{\tt m})^{2}}{2{\tt s}^{2}}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
V
is nonarithmetic and that
t=\alpha j+y
for some fixed
\alpha>s_{-}
and some

y=y(j)
. Let
\theta
be a unique solution to
-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)=\alpha
and
\sigma^{2}:=\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\theta)
.

If
y(j)=o(j^{2/3})
as
j\to\infty
, then

V^{\ast(j)}(t)~\sim~\frac{1}{\theta}{\rm e}^{\theta t}(\hat{V}(\theta))^{j}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2}j)^{1/2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}j}}=\frac{1}{\theta}{\rm e}^{\theta t}(\hat{V}(\theta))^{j}p(0,\sigma^{2}j;y),\quad j\to\infty.

(3.5)

If
y(j)\sim cj^{2/3}
as
j\to\infty
for some
c>0
, then

V^{\ast(j)}(t)~\sim~\theta^{-1}{\rm e}^{-c^{3}\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)/(6\sigma^{6})}{\rm e}^{\theta t}(\hat{V}(\theta))^{j}p(0,\sigma^{2}j;y),\quad j\to\infty.

(3.6)

If
\lim_{j\to\infty}j^{-2/3}y(j)=\infty
, formulae (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) are no longer valid. Actually, in the setting of Corollary
3.3
the asymptotic behavior of
V^{\ast(j)}(t)
exhibits infinitely many phase transitions, which are indexed by
r\in\mathbb{N}
and depend on whether
y(j)=o(j^{(r+1)/(r+2)})
or
y(j)\sim{\rm const}\cdot j^{(r+1)/(r+2)}
as
j\to\infty
. An inspection of the proof of Corollary
3.3
makes this observation crystal clear.

An interesting problem is to find a sequence
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
V^{\ast(j)}(t_{j})
converges to a finite positive constant. This is accomplished in the following corollary which can also be regarded as a version of (
1.3
).

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume that there exists
\theta^{\ast}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\lambda(\theta^{\ast})=\theta^{\ast}\lambda^{\prime}(\theta^{\ast})
and
V
is nonarithmetic. Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
and let
t=t(j,y)
be a function satisfying

-\lambda^{\prime}\left(\theta^{\ast}-\frac{\log j-y+\varepsilon(j)}{2j\theta^{\ast}\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{\ast})}\right)=\frac{t}{j},\quad j\to\infty

(3.7)

for some
\varepsilon(j)=o(1)
,
j\to\infty
. Then, with
\sigma^{2}:=\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{\ast})
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}V^{\ast(j)}(t)~=~\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{1/2}\theta^{\ast}}{\rm e}^{-y/2}.

(3.8)

Applications

Recall our standing assumption that
V(x)=0
for
x<0

Example
2.1
(continuation).
Recall that in this example
V(x)=bx^{\alpha}
for
x\geq 0
and some

,
\hat{V}(s)=b\Gamma(\alpha+1)s^{-\alpha}

\lambda^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{\alpha}{s^{2}}

\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\kappa(j))=\frac{t^{2}}{\alpha j^{2}},\quad a(j)=\frac{t}{\sqrt{\alpha j}},\quad\kappa(j)a(j)=\sqrt{\alpha j}.

Condition (
3.1
) holds in view of

T_{j}~=~c_{1}\cdot t/j

for a constant

. Condition (
3.2
) reads

\sup_{|z|>\gamma}|1-{\rm i}z|^{-\alpha}=(1+\gamma^{2})^{-\alpha/2}<1\quad\text{for every fixed }\gamma>0

and also holds true. Note that no assumptions have been imposed on

. This ensures that (
3.3
) holds true for any sequence

If

Example
2.2
(continuation with

Recall that in this case
V(t)=t+1
for
t\geq 0

\hat{V}(s)=s^{-1}+1,\quad\lambda(s)=\log(s+1)-\log(s),\quad\lambda^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s},\quad s_{-}=0,\quad s_{+}=+\infty,\\

\kappa(j)=\frac{j}{t}-\frac{j^{2}}{t^{2}}+O\left(\frac{j^{3}}{t^{3}}\right),\quad j\to\infty,

In this case

(4.2)

which under the additional assumption

as

\displaystyle\leq\big|\mathbb{E}[{\rm e}^{{\rm i}z\bar{X}_{j,\,t}/a(j)}]-{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}\big|+|\varphi_{b(j)}(z)-1|{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}+\big|\psi_{\delta(j)}(z/a(j))-1\big|{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\leq 16|z|^{3}{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/3}L_{j}+|z|{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}/b(j)+|z|{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}\delta(j)/(2a(j)).

We have used (
5.8
) to obtain the first term on the right-hand side. The third term is a consequence of the inequality
|1-{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x}-{\rm i}x|\leq x^{2}/2
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, see Lemma 1 on p. 512 in
[
7
]
. Thus, recalling also (
5.9
),

A(j)\leq L_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|z|^{3}{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/3}{\rm d}z+\big({\rm e}^{-cj}+\delta(j)/(2a(j))\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}}|z|{\rm e}^{-z^{2}/2}{\rm d}z~\to~0,\quad j\to\infty.

Now we work with
B(j)
. As we have shown,
L_{j}\leq 4T_{j}/a(j)
. Thus,

B(j)=\int_{(4L_{j})^{-1}<|u|\leq b(j)}\Big|\frac{\hat{V}(\kappa(j)-{\rm i}u/a(j))}{\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}\Big|^{j}{\rm d}u\leq 2b(j)\sup_{|u|\geq(4L_{j}a(j))^{-1}}\Big|\frac{\hat{V}(\kappa(j)-{\rm i}u)}{\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}\Big|^{j}\\
\leq 2b(j)\sup_{|u|\geq(16T_{j})^{-1}}\Big|\frac{\hat{V}(\kappa(j)-{\rm i}u)}{\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}\Big|^{j}.

By virtue of (
3.2
), for large
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{|u|\geq 1/16}\Big|\frac{\hat{V}(\kappa(j)-{\rm i}u/T_{j})}{\hat{V}(\kappa(j))}\Big|\leq{\rm e}^{-c_{1}}

for some
c_{1}>0
. Hence, recalling that
b(j)={\rm e}^{cj}
we obtain
B(j)\leq 2{\rm e}^{(c-c_{1})j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
whenever
c\in(0,c_{1})
. Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}L_{j}=0
, we immediately conclude that
\lim_{j\to\infty}C(j)=0
. Combining fragments together we arrive at (
5.6
).

Let
(\rho(j))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers tending to zero, to be specified below. Replacing in (
5.6
) first
\delta(j)
with
\delta(j)(1\pm 2\rho(j))
and then
x
with
x\mp\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j)
yields

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\frac{a(j)}{(1\pm 2\rho(j))\delta(j)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[(x-y)a(j)\mp\rho(j)\delta(j),(x-y)a(j)+(1\pm\rho(j))\delta(j))\}\\
\times g_{b(j)}(y){\rm d}y-p(0,1;x\mp\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j))\Big|=0.

Thus, given
\varepsilon>0
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and large enough
j
,

D_{j}(x):=\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[(x-y)a(j)+\rho(j)\delta(j),(x-y)a(j)+(1-\rho(j))\delta(j))\}g_{b(j)}(y){\rm d}y\\
\geq(1-2\rho(j))p(0,1;x+\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j))-\varepsilon.

Observe that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

[x-y+\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j),x-y+(1-\rho(j))\delta(j)/a(j))\subseteq[x,x+\delta(j)/a(j))

whenever
|y|\leq\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j)
. This entails

D_{j}(x)\leq\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[xa(j),xa(j)+\delta(j))\}\int_{|y|\leq\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j)}g_{b(j)}(y){\rm d}y\\
+\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\int_{|y|>\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j)}g_{b(j)}(y){\rm d}y.

(5.10)

Recall that so far the sequence
(\delta(j))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
has been an arbitrary sequence satisfying
\delta(j)=o(a(j))
as
j\to\infty
. We shall now also exploit the assumption that
\delta(j)/a(j)
decays subexponentially. Let
(\rho(j))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be defined by

\frac{a(j)}{(\rho(j))^{2}\delta(j)}=b(j)={\rm e}^{cj},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.11)

Such a choice and subexponential decay of
\delta(j)/a(j)
guarantees
\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho(j)=0
.
With this choice of
\rho
and using that
g_{b(j)}(y)=b(j)g(b(j)y)
we conclude from (
5.10
) that

D_{j}(x)\leq\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[xa(j),xa(j)+\delta(j))\}\int_{|y|\leq 1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y\\
+\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\int_{|y|>1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y

and thereupon

\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[xa(j),xa(j)+\delta(j))\}\\
\geq\Big((1-2\rho(j))p(0,1;x+\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j))-\varepsilon-\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\int_{|y|>1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y\Big)\Big(\int_{|y|\leq 1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y\Big)^{-1}.

(5.12)

Uniform continuity of
p
in combination with
\lim_{j\to\infty}\big(\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j)\big)=0
entails

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|p(0,1;x+\rho(j)\delta(j)/a(j))-p(0,1;x)|=0.

Since
\rho
converges to 0 and
g
is the density of a probability distribution,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{|y|\leq 1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y=1
. In view of
\int_{|y|>1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y\leq 4\pi^{-1}\int_{1/\rho(j)}^{\infty}y^{-2}{\rm d}y=4\pi^{-1}\rho(j)
and the facts that
\rho
exhibits an exponential decay, whereas
a(j)/\delta(j)
grows subexponentially, we conclude that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\int_{|y|>1/\rho(j)}g(y){\rm d}y=0.

Letting now in (
5.12
)
j\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0+
we obtain that, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[xa(j),xa(j)+\delta(j))\}\geq p(0,1;x).

The converse inequality for the upper limit
follows analogously but simpler.

Relation (
5.3
) is essentially Stone’s local limit theorem (Corollary 1 in
[
17
]
). In view of this we omit a proof. The only thing that needs to be mentioned is that nonarithmeticity of
V
is equivalent to the inequality

\bigg|\frac{\hat{V}(\theta-{\rm i}u)}{\hat{V}(\theta)}\bigg|<1,\quad u\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

The proof of Proposition
5.2
is complete.

Remark 5.3
.

Recall that a probability measure with the characteristic function
\phi
is called strongly nonlattice,
if

\sup_{|u|>\gamma}|\phi(u)|<1

for every fixed
\gamma>0
, see, for instance, equation (3) in
[
17
]
. Our assumption (
3.2
) is equivalent to

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\sup_{|u|>\gamma}|\mathbb{E}[{\rm e}^{iu(X_{j,t}^{\prime}-t/j)/T_{j}}]|<1

(5.13)

for every fixed
\gamma>0
, where the random variables
X_{j,t}^{\prime}
are as defined in (
5.7
). Thus, (
3.2
) can be regarded as the assumption that the distributions of the normalized random variables
(X_{j,t}^{\prime}-t/j)/T_{j}
are asymptotically strongly nonlattice as
j\to\infty
.

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.1

Suppose that the assumptions of part (a) of Theorem
3.1
hold. Put
\delta(j)=\theta/\kappa(j)
for a fixed
\theta>0
and all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The so defined
\delta(j)
satisfies the assumption of part (i) of Proposition
5.2
. We use a decomposition

\kappa(j)a(j)\mathbb{E}[{\rm e}^{-\kappa(j)\bar{X}_{j,\,t}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\geq 0\}}]=\kappa(j)a(j)\int_{[0,\,\theta a(j))}{\rm e}^{-\kappa(j)y}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\leq y\}\\
+\kappa(j)a(j)\int_{[\theta a(j),\,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-\kappa(j)y}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\leq y\}=:K(j)+L(j).

In view of
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)a(j)=\infty
,

L(j)\leq\kappa(j)a(j){\rm e}^{-\theta\kappa(j)a(j)}~\to~0,\quad j\to\infty.

We proceed by writing

\displaystyle K(j)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(j)a(j)\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\kappa(j)a(j)\rfloor-1}\int_{[i\theta,(i+1)\theta)}{\rm e}^{-y}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\leq y/\kappa(j)\}

\displaystyle\leq\theta\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\kappa(j)a(j)\rfloor-1}{\rm e}^{-i\theta}\Big(\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,t}\in[i\delta(j),(i+1)\delta(j))\}-p(0,1;i\delta(j)/a(j))\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07182pt+\theta\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\kappa(j)a(j)\rfloor-1}{\rm e}^{-i\theta}\big(p(0,1;i\delta(j)/a(j))-p(0,1;0)\big)+p(0,1;0)\theta\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor a(j)\kappa(j)\rfloor-1}{\rm e}^{-i\theta}

\displaystyle=:K_{1}(j,\theta)+K_{2}(j,\theta)+K_{3}(j,\theta).

Note that

\lim_{j\to\infty}K_{3}(j,\theta)=p(0,1;0)\frac{\theta}{1-{\rm e}^{-\theta}}\to p(0,1;0),\quad\theta\to 0+.

Further, given
\varepsilon>0
and picking
\theta>0
sufficiently small, we obtain, for large
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in[0,\lfloor\kappa(j)a(j)\rfloor-1]
,

|p(0,1;i\delta(j)/a(j))-p(0,1;0)|\leq\varepsilon.

This yields
\limsup_{\theta\to 0+}\limsup_{j\to\infty}|K_{2}(j,\theta)|\leq\varepsilon
. Therefore,
\limsup_{\theta\to 0+}\limsup_{j\to\infty}|K_{2}(j,\theta)|=0
, since
K_{2}(j,\theta)
does not depend on
\varepsilon
. Finally, by Proposition
5.2
, given
\varepsilon>0
, for large
j\in\mathbb{N}
and all
i\in[0,\lfloor\kappa(j)a(j)\rfloor-1]
,

\Big|\frac{a(j)}{\delta(j)}\mathbb{P}\{\bar{X}_{j,\,t}\in[i\delta(j),(i+1)\delta(j))\}-p(0,1;i\delta(j)/a(j))\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

As a consequence,
\lim_{j\to\infty}K_{1}(j,\theta)=0
.

We have proved that
\limsup_{j\to\infty}(K(j)+L(j))\leq p(0,1;0)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}
. The converse inequality for the lower limit follows analogously. The proof of Proposition
5.1
is complete under the assumptions of part (a) of Theorem
3.1
.

Under the assumptions of part (b) of Theorem
3.1
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)=\theta\in(0,\infty)
and
\kappa(j)a(j)\to\infty
holds automatically. The proof goes by exactly the same reasoning as in case (a), and we omit further details.

5.3.
Proofs of Corollaries
3.3
and
3.4
.

Proof of Corollary
3.3
.

Note that
t/j\to\alpha>s_{-}
as
j\to\infty
which entails
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)=\theta
.

We start by proving (
3.5
). According to part (b) of Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to check that under the assumption
y(j)=o(j^{2/3})

(\hat{V}(\kappa(j)))^{j}{\rm e}^{t\kappa(j)}~\sim~(\hat{V}(\theta))^{j}{\rm e}^{\theta t}{\rm e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}j}},\quad j\to\infty.

Put
\widetilde{\kappa}(j)=\theta-\kappa(j)
. Using (
2.4
) and Taylor’s expansion we obtain

t/j=\alpha+y/j=-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))=-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)+\sigma^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+O((\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2})\\
=\alpha+\sigma^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+O((\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2}),\quad j\to\infty.

Thus,

\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+O((\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2})=\frac{y}{\sigma^{2}j}

and thereupon

\widetilde{\kappa}(j)=\frac{y}{\sigma^{2}j}+O\left(\frac{y^{2}}{j^{2}}\right),\quad j\to\infty.

(5.14)

It remains to show that

j\lambda(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))+t(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))=\theta t+\lambda(\theta)j-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}j}+o(1),\quad j\to\infty.

(5.15)

This follows by another application of Taylor’s expansion

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544ptj\lambda(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))+t(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))

\displaystyle~=~j\left(\lambda(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2}+O\left((\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{3}\right)\right)+\theta t-t\widetilde{\kappa}(j)

\displaystyle~\overset{\eqref{eq:cor_proof1}}{=}~\theta t+\lambda(\theta)j-y\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+\frac{\sigma^{2}j}{2}(\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2}+O\left(\frac{y^{3}}{j^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle~\overset{\eqref{eq:cor_proof1}}{=}~\theta t+j\lambda(\theta)-\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma^{2}j}+\frac{j\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma^{4}j^{2}}+O\left(\frac{y^{3}}{j^{3}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{y^{3}}{j^{2}}\right)~=~\theta t+j\lambda(\theta)-\frac{y^{2}}{2j\sigma^{2}}+o(1).

Here,
O(y^{3}/j^{2})=o(1)
is secured by the assumption
y(j)=o(j^{2/3})
. This finishes the proof of both (
5.15
) and (
3.5
).

The proof of (
3.6
) goes the same path and only requires an extra term in Taylor’s expansions. A counterpart of (
5.14
) is

\widetilde{\kappa}(j)=\frac{y}{\sigma^{2}j}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)y^{2}}{2\sigma^{6}j^{2}}+o\left(\frac{y^{2}}{j^{2}}\right),\quad j\to\infty.

With this at hand,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptj\lambda(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))+t(\theta-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))

\displaystyle~=~j\left(\lambda(\theta)-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{6}(\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{3}+O\left((\widetilde{\kappa}(j))^{4}\right)\right)+\theta t-t\widetilde{\kappa}(j)

\displaystyle~=~\theta t+\lambda(\theta)j-\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma^{2}j}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)y^{3}}{2\sigma^{6}j^{2}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}j}{2}\left(\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma^{4}j^{2}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)y^{3}}{\sigma^{8}j^{3}}\right)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)y^{3}}{6\sigma^{6}j^{2}}+o(1)

\displaystyle~=~\theta t+\lambda(\theta)j-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}j}-\frac{c^{3}\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{6\sigma^{6}}+o(1),\quad j\to\infty.

The proof of Corollary
3.3
is complete.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4
.

First of all, note that the function
t(j,y)
exists (at least for large enough
j
), for (
3.7
) implies that, for large enough
t>0
,
t/j\geq-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta^{\ast})\in(s_{-},s_{+})
.

Formula (
2.4
) implies that

\kappa(j)=\theta^{\ast}-\frac{\log j-y+\varepsilon(j)}{2j\theta^{\ast}\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{\ast})}=:\theta^{\ast}-\widetilde{\kappa}(j).

In particular,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\kappa(j)=\theta^{\ast}
. According to part (b) of Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to check that under the assumption of Corollary
3.4

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{(\hat{V}(\kappa(j)))^{j}{\rm e}^{t\kappa(j)}}{j^{1/2}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\rm e}^{j(\lambda(\kappa(j))-\kappa(j)\lambda^{\prime}(\kappa(j))})}{j^{1/2}}={\rm e}^{-y/2}.

(5.16)

Using Taylor’s expansion yields

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lambda(\kappa(j))-\kappa(j)\lambda^{\prime}(\kappa(j))=\lambda(\theta^{\ast}-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))-(\theta^{\ast}-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))\lambda^{\prime}(\theta^{\ast}-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))

\displaystyle=\lambda(\theta^{\ast})-\lambda^{\prime}(\theta^{\ast})\widetilde{\kappa}(j)-(\theta^{\ast}-\widetilde{\kappa}(j))(\lambda^{\prime}(\theta^{\ast})-\widetilde{\kappa}(j)\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{\ast}))+O\left(\frac{(\log j)^{2}}{j^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta^{\ast}\sigma^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+O\left(\frac{(\log j)^{2}}{j^{2}}\right),\quad j\to\infty.

This entails

-2^{-1}\log j+j(\lambda(\kappa_{j}(t))-\kappa_{j}(t)\lambda^{\prime}(\kappa_{j}(t)))\\
=-2^{-1}\log j+\theta^{\ast}\sigma^{2}j\widetilde{\kappa}(j)+O\left(\frac{(\log j)^{2}}{j}\right)=-y/2+o(1),\quad j\to\infty

and thereupon (
5.16
).

6.
Appendix

The following result is known if
V
is a distribution function, see, for example, Chapter 9.1.2 in
[
4
]
. We have not been able to locate in the literature its version for arbitrary nondecreasing unbounded functions. Therefore, we give a proof.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a right-continuous and nondecreasing function vanishing on
(-\infty,0)
. Put
x_{0}:=\inf\{x>0:\,V(x)>0\}
and
\lambda(s)=\log\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)
for
s\in\mathcal{D}
. Then

s_{-}=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{D}}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))=\lim_{s\to+\infty}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))=x_{0}.

Proof.

The second equality follows from the monotonicity of
-\lambda^{\prime}
. For the proof of the last equality we distinguish two cases:
x_{0}=0
and
x_{0}>0
.

Case
x_{0}=0
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and note that
\int_{[0,\varepsilon/2]}{\rm d}V(x)=:K(\varepsilon)>0
. Since

\frac{\int_{(\varepsilon,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}{\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{-s\varepsilon/2}\int_{(0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx/2}{\rm d}V(x)}{\int_{[0,\varepsilon/2]}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{-s\varepsilon/2}\int_{(0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx/2}{\rm d}V(x)}{{\rm e}^{-s\varepsilon/2}K(\varepsilon)}~\to~0,\quad s\to\infty

by the monotone convergence theorem, we conclude that,

\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)~\sim~\int_{[0,\varepsilon]}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x),\quad s\to\infty.

Similarly, one can check that

\int_{[0,\infty)}x{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)~\sim~\int_{[0,\varepsilon]}x{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x),\quad s\to\infty

for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
. Thus,

\lim_{s\to+\infty}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{[0,\infty)}x{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}{\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{[0,\varepsilon]}x{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}{\int_{[0,\varepsilon]}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)}\leq\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary we conclude that
\lim_{s\to+\infty}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))=0=x_{0}
.

Case
x_{0}>0
.
This situation can be reduced to the previous one by passing to the function
V_{1}(x):=V(x+x_{0})
. Indeed,

\lambda(s)=\log\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)=\log\int_{[x_{0},\infty)}{\rm e}^{-sx}{\rm d}V(x)=\log\int_{[0,\infty)}{\rm e}^{-s(x+x_{0})}{\rm d}V_{1}(x)=:-sx_{0}+\lambda_{1}(s)

and thereupon

\inf_{s\in\mathcal{D}}(-\lambda^{\prime}(s))=x_{0}+\inf_{s\in\mathcal{D}}(-\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(s))=x_{0}

according to the already considered case
x_{0}=0
.
∎

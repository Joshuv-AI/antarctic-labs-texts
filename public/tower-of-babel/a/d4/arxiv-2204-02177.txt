A note on adiabatic time evolution and quasi-static processes in translation-invariant quantum systems
Vojkan Jakšić, Claude-Alain Pillet ∗, Clément Tauber

Abstract




Dedicated to the memory of Krzysztof Gawędzki

Abstract.

We study the slowly varying, non-autonomous quantum dynamics of a translation invariant spin or fermion system on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. This system is assumed to be initially in thermal equilibrium, and we consider realizations of quasi-static processes in the adiabatic limit. By combining the Gibbs variational principle with the notion of quantum weak Gibbs states introduced in
[
JPT23
]
, we establish a number of general structural results regarding such realizations. In particular, we show that such a quasi-static process is incompatible with the property of approach to equilibrium studied in this previous work.

The adiabatic theorem with a gap.
The formulation and the proof of the adiabatic theorem in quantum mechanics go back to the seminal work of Born and Fock
[
BF28
]
. The first modern proof, in functional analytic framework, is due to Kato
[
Kat50
]
. The method of the proof introduced there played an important role in all the future developments of the subject. For refinements of Kato’s result under the gap assumption made in
[
Kat50
]
, see
[
ASY87
,
JP91
,
Nen93
]
.

Let
H
be a (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operator on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and

V:[0,1]\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

(2)

a continuous map taking its values in the bounded self-adjoint operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
T>0
, we consider the non-autonomous Schrödinger equation

\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\psi(t)=(H+V(t/T))\psi(t),\qquad\psi(s)=f,\qquad s,t\in[0,T].

For any
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Dom}}\nolimits(H)
this equation has a unique solution
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto\psi(t)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Dom}}\nolimits(H)
, which can be written as
\psi(t)=U_{T}^{s/T\to t/T}f
, where
[0,1]\times[0,1]\ni(\sigma,\tau)\mapsto U_{T}^{\sigma\to\tau}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a strongly continuous map taking its values in the unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
and satisfying
U_{T}^{\sigma\to\sigma}=I
,
U_{T}^{\sigma\to\tau}\mathop{\mathrm{Dom}}\nolimits(H)\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Dom}}\nolimits(H)
, and
U_{T}^{\tau\to\upsilon}U_{T}^{\sigma\to\tau}=U_{T}^{\sigma\to\upsilon}
for all
\sigma,\tau,\upsilon\in[0,1]
.
U_{T}
is the propagator associated to the time-dependent Hamiltonian
[0,1]\ni\tau\mapsto T(H+V(\tau))
.

For each
\tau\in[0,1]
, let
E(\tau)
be an eigenvalue of finite multiplicity of
H(\tau)=H+V(\tau)
which is uniformly separated from the remaining part of its spectrum,
i.e.,

\inf_{\tau\in[0,1]}{\rm dist}(E(\tau),{\rm sp}(H(\tau))\setminus\{E(\tau)\})>0,

(3)

and denote by
P(\tau)
the orthogonal projection onto the associated eigenspace.

Theorem 2.1
.

Kat50

Suppose, in addition to (

C^{2}

\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}\|(I-P(\tau))U_{T}^{0\to\tau}P(0)\|=O(T^{-1}).

4.1
Setup

As in
[
JPT23
]
, we will work with quantum spin systems on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and set the inverse temperature to
\beta=1
. All the results and proofs directly extend to the fermionic case. The notation and terminology are the same as in
[
JPT23
]
; see Appendix

Given an interaction
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}^{r}
, the associated local Hamiltonian in a finite cube
\Lambda
is

H_{\Lambda}(\Phi)=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}\Phi(X).

The
C^{\ast}
-dynamics it generates on
\mathfrak{A}
is

\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{t}(A)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\Lambda}(\Phi)}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{\Lambda}(\Phi)},

and its thermodynamic limit

\alpha_{\Phi}^{t}(A)=\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{t}(A),

yields a
C^{\ast}
-dynamics commuting with the natural action of the translation group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\varphi^{x}
.

We consider time-dependent translation invariant interactions described by continuous maps

\Phi:[0,1]\ni\tau\mapsto\Phi_{\tau}\in\mathcal{B}^{r}.

In analogy with Section
3

H_{\Lambda}(\Phi_{\tau})
generate a non-autonomous
C^{\ast}
-dynamics
\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}
on
\mathfrak{A}
, uniquely characterized by the Cauchy problem for the Heisenberg equation of motion

\partial_{\tau}\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)=\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}(\mathrm{i}[H_{\Lambda}(\Phi_{\tau}),A]),\qquad\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\sigma}(A)=A\in\mathfrak{A},\qquad\sigma,\tau\in[0,1].

The following proposition summarizes the basic properties of its thermodynamic limit needed in this paper. The reader
should consult
[
NSY19
]
for a more detailed and more general discussion of non-autonomous
C^{\ast}
-dynamics.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Phi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{B}^{r}

(1)

\sigma,\tau,\upsilon\in[0,1]

\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle A+\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(\sigma,\tau)}\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{n}}}(A)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\tau_{n},&\text{ for }0\leq\sigma\leq\tau\leq 1;\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle A+\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(\sigma,\tau)}\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{n}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{1}}}(A)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\tau_{n},&\text{ for }0\leq\tau\leq\sigma\leq 1;\end{cases}

(23)

the limit being uniform for
\tau-\sigma
in compact subsets of
{]}-\epsilon,\epsilon{[}
. Since the maps
\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}:{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}}\to\mathfrak{A}
are isometric, their limit
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
is norm continuous, and hence uniquely extends by continuity to an isometry on
\mathfrak{A}
. It follows that (
7
) holds for all
A\in\mathfrak{A}
and
\sigma,\tau\in[0,1]
satisfying
|\tau-\sigma|<\epsilon
. Moreover, as norm-limits of
\ast
-morphisms, the maps
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
are themselves
\ast
-morphisms. For
\sigma,\tau,\upsilon\in[0,1]
such that
\max(|\tau-\sigma|,|\sigma-\upsilon|)<\epsilon
, it follows from the continuity of
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
that

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A),

for
A\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}}
. Writing

\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)=\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\upsilon}(A)+(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau})\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)+(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau})\circ(\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau\to\upsilon}-\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon})(A),

and using that

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau})\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau})\circ(\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau\to\upsilon}-\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon})(A)\|\leq 2\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)-\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)\|=0,

we derive

\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\upsilon}(A)=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\upsilon}(A),

(24)

provided that
|\upsilon-\sigma|<\epsilon
. This identity extends by continuity to all
A\in\mathfrak{A}
, and allows to extend the family of
\ast
-morphisms
(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau})_{|\tau-\sigma|<\epsilon}
to arbitrary
\sigma,\tau\in[0,1]
by setting

\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau_{1}}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{n-1}\to\tau_{n}}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{n}\to\tau},

(25)

where
\tau_{0}=\sigma,\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n+1}=\tau
are such that
\max_{k}|\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}|<\epsilon
. One easily checks that the left-hand side of this expression does not depend on the choice of the subdivisions
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}
on the right-hand side. By construction, this extension satisfies (
24
) for all
\sigma,\tau,\upsilon\in[0,1]
. From the telescopic expansion based on (
25
),

\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau_{k-1}\to\tau_{k}}\circ(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{k}\to\tau_{k+1}}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau_{k}\to\tau_{k+1}})\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{k+1}\to\tau_{k+2}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{n}\to\tau}(A),

we derive

\|\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{k}\to\tau_{k+1}}-\alpha_{\Phi,\Lambda}^{\tau_{k}\to\tau_{k+1}})(A_{k})\|,

with
A_{k}=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{k+1}\to\tau_{k+2}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{n}\to\tau}(A)
. It follows that (
7
) holds for all
A\in\mathfrak{A}
and all
\sigma,\tau\in[0,1]
. Note that (
24
) implies that
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\sigma}=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\sigma}=\mathrm{Id}
, which shows that the maps
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
are
\ast
-automorphisms of
\mathfrak{A}
.

(2) The translation invariance
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\varphi^{x}=\varphi^{x}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
follows immediately from Relation (
19
).

(3) For
A\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}}
and
|\tau-\sigma|<\epsilon/2
, combining the Dyson expansion (
23
) with the estimate (
22
), we get

\|\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-A\|\leq 2\epsilon^{-1}\|A\|\mathrm{e}^{d|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A)|}|\tau-\sigma|.

Since
\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
is an isometry and
{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}
, we conclude that the map
(\sigma,\tau)\mapsto\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}
is strongly continuous on the diagonal
(\tau,\tau)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
. For
(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
, Property (
24
) gives

\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma_{0}\to\tau_{0}}(A)=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau_{0}}(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau_{0}\to\tau}(A)-A)+(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\sigma_{0}}(B)-B),

with
B=\sigma_{\Phi}^{\sigma_{0}\to\tau_{0}}(A)
, which shows strong continuity at
(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})
.

From (
24
) and the Dyson expansion (
23
), we further derive that

\partial_{\tau}\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)=\lim_{\upsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\upsilon}\left(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau+\upsilon}(A)-\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)\right)=\lim_{\upsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\upsilon}\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(\alpha_{\Phi}^{\tau\to\tau+\upsilon}(A)-A)=\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau}}(A),

holds in norm for arbitrary
\sigma,\tau\in[0,1]

(4) We will consider the case
\sigma<\tau
, the opposite case is similar. We also observe that Part (1) extends to time-dependent piecewise continuous interactions
\Phi
. Indeed, the continuity was only tacitly used to justify the Riemann integration in the Dyson expansions (

Let
\Phi,\Psi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{B}^{r}
be piecewise continuous and set
\bar{\epsilon}=r/2\max(\|\Phi\|_{r},\|\Psi\|_{r})
. The telescopic expansion

\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{n}}}-\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{n}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{k-1}}}\circ\delta_{(\Phi-\Psi)_{\tau_{k}}}\circ\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{k+1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{n}}},

combined with the estimate (
22
), gives

\sup_{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\in[0,1]}\|\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Phi_{\tau_{n}}}(A)-\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{\Psi_{\tau_{n}}}(A)\|\leq 2n\|A\|\mathrm{e}^{r|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A)|}\bar{\epsilon}^{-n+1}\frac{\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{r}}{r}n!,

for

. Thus, it follows from the Dyson expansion (

) that

\sup_{|\tau-\sigma|\leq\bar{\epsilon}/2}\|\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-\alpha_{\Psi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)\|\leq\frac{4\bar{\epsilon}}{r}\|A\|\mathrm{e}^{r|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A)|}\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{r}.

By density/continuity, we derive that

\lim_{\Psi\to\Phi}\|\alpha_{\Phi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)-\alpha_{\Psi}^{\sigma\to\tau}(A)\|=0

(26)

for all
A\in\mathfrak{A}
and

. Finally, a telescopic expansion analogous to (

with

\Phi_{t}^{(N)}=1_{[0,\upsilon_{0}{[}}(t)\Phi(t)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}1_{[\upsilon_{k},\upsilon_{k+1}{[}}(t)\Phi_{\upsilon_{k}}+1_{[\upsilon_{N},1]}(t)\Phi_{t},

Appendix A
Glossary of terms

More details can be found in
[
JPT23
]
.

•

\mathfrak{A}
: the
C^{\ast}
-algebra of a spin system on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, or equivalently, the gauge-invariant sector of the fermionic CAR algebra on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

•

\mathcal{F}
: the set of all finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

•

\Phi
: an interaction, namely a translation invariant family
\{\Phi(X)\}_{X\in\mathcal{F}}
of self-adjoint elements of
\mathfrak{A}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\Phi)=X
.

•

\mathcal{B}^{r}
: the Banach space of interactions satisfying
\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{r}=\sum_{X\ni 0}\mathrm{e}^{r(|X|-1)}\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty
, (
r>0
).

•

\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\varphi^{x}
: the group action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on
\mathfrak{A}
.

•

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}(\mathfrak{A})
: the set of translation invariant states on
\mathfrak{A}
.

•

s(\nu)
: the specific entropy of a state
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}(\mathfrak{A})
.

•

s(\nu|\omega)
: the specific relative entropy of two states
\nu,\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}(\mathfrak{A})
.

•

P(\Phi)
: the pressure of the interaction
\Phi
.

•

E_{\Phi}=\sum_{X\ni 0}|X|^{-1}\Phi(X)
, so that
\nu(E_{\Phi})
is the expected specific energy of the interaction
\Phi
in the state
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}(\mathfrak{A})
.

•

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Phi)=\{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}(\mathfrak{A})\mid P(\Phi)=s(\nu)-\nu(E_{\Phi})\}
: the set of equilibrium states for
\Phi
(from the Gibbs variational principle).

•

\mathrm{WG}(\Phi)
: the set of weak Gibbs states for
\Phi
, see
[
JPT23
, Section 2.2]
.

•

\alpha_{\Phi}
: the Heisenberg dynamics generated by the (time-independent) interaction
\Phi
.

•

Physical equivalence: two interactions
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{B}^{r}
are physically equivalent iff
\alpha_{\Phi}=\alpha_{\Psi}
, see
[
JPT23
, Theorem 2.7]
.

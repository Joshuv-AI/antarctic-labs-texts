Discrete geodesic local time converges under KPZ scaling
Shirshendu Ganguly, Lingfu Zhang

Abstract
The directed landscape constructed in [ DOV18 ] produces a directed, planar, random geometry, and is believed to be the universal scaling limit of two-dimensional first and last passage percolation models in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class. Geodesics in this random geometry form an important class of random continuous curves exhibiting fluctuation theory quite different from that of Brownian motion. In this vein, counterpart to Brownian local time, BLT (a self-similar measure supported on the set of zeros of Brownian motion), a local time for geodesics, GLT, was recently constructed and used to study fractal properties of the directed landscape in [ GZ22 ] . It is a classical fact and can be proven using the Markovian property of Brownian motion that the uniform discrete measure on the set of zeros of the simple random walk converges to BLT. In this paper, we prove the “KPZ analog” of this by showing that the local times for discrete geodesics in pre-limiting integrable last passage percolation models converge to GLT under suitable scaling guided by KPZ exponents. In absence of any Markovianity, our arguments rely on the recently proven convergence of geodesics in pre-limiting models to that in the directed landscape [ DV21 ] . However, this input concerns macroscopic properties and is too coarse to capture the microscopic information required for local time analysis. To relate the macroscopic and microscopic behavior, a key ingredient is an a priori smoothness estimate of the local time in the discrete model, proved relying on geometric ideas such as the coalescence of geodesics as well as their stability under perturbations of boundary data.

1
Introduction and main results

The zero set of simple random walks is an object whose study is as old as that of the random walk itself. Estimates on the random walk hitting zero are crucial in understanding the properties of random walk bridges and excursions. The Brownian counterpart gives rise to a canonical fractal subset
Z
of the real line analogous to the Cantor set but random. The Hausdorff dimension of
Z
is known to be
1/2
stemming from the fact that the probability a simple random walk is at
0
at time
n
decays as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.
There is a natural measure supported on
Z
known as the Brownian local time (BLT) which can be obtained as the density of the occupation measure of Brownian motion (This is the measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu(A)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{1}(\mathbb{B}_{s}\in A)\mathrm{d}s
) at zero.

While Brownian motion is a canonical one-dimensional random curve, this article focuses on the local time of another class of random curves arising from models of random geometry. A paradigmatic example is what is known as First passage percolation (FPP) where one considers a distortion of the usual graph metric on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

by putting i.i.d. random non-negative weights on the edges and considering the corresponding random weighted path metric. A natural class of random curves arising in this situation is formed by the weight-minimizing paths between points; namely the geodesics in the associated metric space.

The geodesic behavior, encoding a lot of information
about the underlying geometry, has been an important
object of investigation both in the rigorous and non-
rigorous literature going back to the 1960s when the
model of FPP was first proposed by Hammersley and Welsh.

However, despite the interest, the innate hardness of the model has made it very hard to verify rigorously any of these predictions.
Nonetheless, an important related class of models for which some progress has been made is
known as Last passage percolation (LPP). Ignoring
microscopic specifications, in general terms they all
consist of an environment of random noise
through which
directed
paths travel, accruing the
integral of the noise along it — a quantity known as
energy or weight. Given two endpoints, a maximization is done
over the weights of all paths with these endpoints with the
optimizing path still being called a
geodesic
, despite its maximizing nature as opposed to minimizing as in FPP.

Remarkably, particular choices of the underlying noise
environment make LPP “integrable”, admitting certain
exact algebraic properties such as distributional identities
involving eigenvalues of random matrices, young
diagrams, and so on, which provide key inputs for
probabilistic analysis.

Both FPP and LPP models are expected to belong to the
Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class which
encompasses a broad family of models of one-dimensional random growth
which are believed to exhibit certain common features, such as local growth driven by white noise and a
smoothing effect from some notion of surface tension and
a slope-dependent non-linear growth.
It is predicted that the fluctuation theories of such models are dictated by universal scaling exponents and limiting distributions,
exhibited by solutions to a stochastic PDE known as the KPZ equation
[
Cor12
,
Cor16
,
HQ18
]
.
For these
(1+1)
-dimensional models, the resulting
evolution of the growth interface
h(t,x)
is described by
the characteristic triple
(1,\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3})
of
exponents: at time
t
, the value of
h(t,x)
is of order

t^{1}
, with order
t^{1/3}
deviations from its mean, and
further, non-trivial correlations are observed when
x
is
varied on the scale of
t^{2/3}
.
Furthermore,
h(t,x)
on proper centering and scaling
according to these exponents, is expected to converge to a universal limit
t\to\infty

[
QR14
,
MQR16
]
.
In terms of geodesics in, say, LPP, this corresponds to having a weight fluctuation exponent
1/3
and a
transversal fluctuation exponent (deviation from the
straight line joining its endpoints)
2/3
.

Another key feature in such random settings is the
phenomenon of
geodesic coalescence
where
geodesics between different points tend to merge
together and share a significant fraction of their journey,
passing through the atypically high values of the
underlying noise variables. Note that this is quite
different from standard Euclidean geometry where
geodesics are merely straight lines and hence have
trivial intersection patterns.

Owing to such coalescence properties, even though the geodesic is a global object, with high probability, a local segment of it is likely to depend only on the nearby noise. In other words, it exhibits a form of weak decay of correlation property.

In
[
DOV18
]
the directed landscape was constructed.
This is a random continuum metric space expected to be
the universal scaling limit of a class of FPP and LPP
models. Moreover, in a follow-up work
[
DV21
]
, a class
of exactly solvable LPP models was indeed shown to
converge to the landscape. The latter also admits geodesics (a non-trivial fact) and it was further shown that
the pre-limiting geodesics converge to the limiting one in a certain topology.

However, the topology of convergence is not refined enough to conclude the convergence of several observables of interest associated with the geodesics to their limiting counterparts.

In this article, we focus on the convergence of one such observable, namely that of the discrete local time, i.e., the amount of time a pre-limiting discrete geodesic intersects a line.
While defining the discrete local time is straightforward (the formal definition appears later in (
1.2
)), its analog for the directed landscape is rather non-trivial and was one of the key accomplishments in
[
GZ22
]
by the same authors. The notion of local time turned out to be a crucial ingredient in the analysis of the exceptional set of points witnessing geodesic non-coalescence in the above-mentioned paper.

Thus this paper along with
[
GZ22
]
initiates the study of local times in the context of geodesics, akin to the study of the same for random walks and Brownian motion which can be considered classical, and hence forms a part of a bigger program of investigating the similarities and dissimilarities between the geodesic and Brownian motion. The arguments in this paper can be seen as one exploiting the weak decay of correlation the geodesic exhibits as alluded to above — a style of argument that we expect would be useful in several other applications going forward. We now move on to the statement of our results first by
introducing the key objects in play.

1.1
Directed landscape

The directed landscape
\mathcal{L}
is a random continuous function from the parameter space

\mathbb{R}^{4}_{\uparrow}=\{u=(p;q)=(x,s;y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:s<t\}

to
\mathbb{R}
constructed in the breakthrough work
[
DOV18
]
.
It satisfies the following composition law

\mathcal{L}(x,r;z,t)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{L}(x,r;y,s)+\mathcal{L}(y,s;z,t)

(1.1)

for any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
and
r<s<t
.
For a continuous path
\pi:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}
, its weight under the metric
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\|\pi\|_{\mathcal{L}}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{s=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=t}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}(\pi(t_{i-1}),t_{i-1};\pi(t_{i}),t_{i}).

Such a path
\pi
is said to be a geodesic from
(\pi(s),s)
to
(\pi(t),t)
if
\|\pi\|_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}(\pi(s),s;\pi(t),t)
.
It is shown in
[
DOV18
]
that almost surely, there exists at least one geodesic from
p
to
q
for any
u=(p;q)=(x,s;y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{\uparrow}
.
We let
\pi_{u}=\pi_{(p;q)}=\pi_{(x,s;y,t)}:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}
denote any such geodesic from
p
to
q
.
Further, for any fixed
u\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{\uparrow}
, almost surely the geodesic
\pi_{u}
is unique.
Towards establishing its universal nature, in
[
DOV18
,
DV21
]
, the directed landscape
\mathcal{L}
and the associated geodesics are proved to be the scaling limits of their counterparts for various exactly solvable LPP models, including Brownian LPP on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
, Poissonian LPP on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and LPP on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with i.i.d. Exponential or Geometric weights (Exponential/Geometric LPP).

Semi-infinite geodesics:
Going beyond point-to-point geodesics,
for each
p=(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\pi_{p}^{r}:[s,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a semi-infinite geodesic started from
p
in the
r
direction; i.e. we have
t^{-1}\pi_{p}^{r}(t)\to r
as
t\to\infty
, and for any
s\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<\infty
, the restriction of
\pi_{p}^{r}
on
[s_{1},s_{2}]
is a geodesic (from
(\pi_{p}^{r}(s_{1}),s_{1})
to
(\pi_{p}^{r}(s_{2}),s_{2})
).
When
r=0
, we will drop the
r
dependence and simply use
\pi_{p}=\pi_{p}^{0}
.
While the issues regarding the existence and uniqueness of such semi-infinite geodesics are not straightforward, they were treated in a systematic way in
[
GZ22
, Section 4]
and
[
RV21
,
BSS22
]
, with the following established:
almost surely, such semi-infinite geodesic
\pi_{p}
exists for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
;
and for fixed
p
, almost surely there is a unique semi-infinite geodesic.

The landscape and thereby the semi-infinite geodesic
\pi_{(0,0)}
admits invariance under appropriate scalings
guided by the exponent triple
(1,1/3,2/3)
, characteristic
of the KPZ universality class, much like Brownian motion
which is invariant under diffusive scaling. Hence, as in the
case of the latter, both
\mathcal{L}
and
\pi_{(0,0)}
are expected to exhibit various fractal or self-similar properties.

1.2
Pre-limiting model: Exponential LPP

On the pre-limiting side, our choice of the model is Exponential LPP

1

Our arguments would work equally well for Geometric LPP, the other known integrable LPP model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Two other known integrable models are Poissonian LPP on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and Brownian LPP on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and the arguments in this paper also imply similar conclusions for the natural notions of local times in these settings. A longer discussion appears following Theorem

.
Consider directed last passage percolation (LPP) on

with i.i.d. Exponential weights on the vertices, i.e., we have a random field

For any two points

The (almost surely unique) directed path with the maximum weight is the geodesic from

When

.

In the setting of Exponential LPP, the existence and uniqueness of semi-infinite geodesics have been established (see
[
Cou11
,
FP05
]

For any
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, there is an almost surely unique upper-right path from it, denoted as
\Gamma_{p}=\{p=(x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1}),\cdots\}
, such that for any

is

, and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}=1
.

Discrete semi-infinite geodesic:
Such a
\Gamma_{p}
is referred to as the
(1,1)
-direction semi-infinite geodesic from

.

Almost surely, for any sequence
q_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that goes to infinity in the
(1,1)
-direction,
\Gamma_{p,q_{n}}
converges to

in the sense of local limits (i.e., any initial segment of

Also for any

Throughout we shall always assume (the probability

,
and a unique

-direction semi-infinite geodesic

.

Further, for the rest of this paper, we let

be the functions

and

. For each

, we let

Given that the orientation of the directed landscape is vertically up, for comparison purposes it might be more natural to rotate the Exponential LPP model by

1.3
Main result

We first recall the local time for the semi-infinite geodesic
\pi_{(0,0)}(t)
in the directed landscape from
[
GZ22
, Section 7.1]
where it was introduced and constructed.
We start by considering the occupation measure by defining for any
w>0
and
h\geq 0
,

L_{w}(h):=\int_{0}^{h}\mathds{1}[\pi_{(0,0)}(t)\in[-w,w]]dt.

The following proposition defines the local time as the density of the occupation measure at
0.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
GZ22
, Proposition 2.4, Proposition 7.4]
)
.

Almost surely there exists a non-decreasing function
L:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
L(h)=\lim_{w\to 0}(2w)^{-1}L_{w}(h)<0
for any
h\geq 0
.
In addition, there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for any
0\leq g<h
and
M>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}[L(h)-L(g)>M(h-g)^{1/3}]<Ce^{-cM}.

It is clear from the definition that
L
is supported inside the zero set of
\pi_{(0,0)}
. Further, the above estimate indicates, and it was indeed proved in
[
GZ22
]
, that the latter has Hausdorff dimension
1/3.
This is in contrast to the
1/2
dimension-ness of the zero set of Brownian motion. The disparity stems from the fact that while Brownian motion is
\frac{1}{2}^{-}
-Hölder regular,
\pi_{(0,0)}
is
\frac{2}{3}^{-}
-Hölder regular
[
DOV18
, Proposition 12.3]
(see also
[
DSV22
]
where geodesics are shown to have
3/2
-variation).

Correspondingly, in Exponential LPP, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the discrete local time function
L^{(n)}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

L^{(n)}(h)=2^{2/3}n^{-1/3}|\{0\leq i\leq hn:(i,i)\in\Gamma_{(0,0)}\}|.

(1.2)

Here the factor
2^{2/3}n^{-1/3}
makes
L^{(n)}
be in the same scale as
L
, dictated by the appearance of the same factors in the convergence of Exponential LPP to the directed landscape (to be recorded shortly in Section
2.4
).
The main result of this paper is the convergence of
L^{(n)}
to
L
.

Theorem 1.2
.

As
n\to\infty
, we have
L^{(n)}\to L
weakly, in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

We remark that our arguments also go through (essentially verbatim) for other exact-solvable LPP models, such as Geometric, Poissonian, and Brownian LPP. For Geometric LPP, one issue that arises is that of multiple geodesics between vertices owing to the discreteness of vertex weights. In such a situation one could consider the “rightmost” (or “leftmost”) semi-infinite geodesic from
(0,0)
.
In Poissonian LPP, one considers a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the geodesic between two points
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
p\preceq q
(i.e.,
p\leq q
in each co-ordinate) is any up/right path between them, passing through the most number of points.
In this setting, the pre-limiting local time can similarly be defined by counting the number of intersections of the “rightmost” (or “leftmost”) semi-infinite geodesic from
(0,0)
with the diagonal
y=x
.
Finally, in Brownian LPP, the random field is given by a sequence of independent two-sided Brownian motions
\{\mathcal{B}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and for any two points
p,q\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
with
p\preceq q
(i.e.,
p\leq q
in each co-ordinate as before), the geodesic between them is the (almost surely unique) up/right path in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
, which accumulates the largest Brownian motion increments.
In this case, the analogous pre-limiting local time could be taken to be the number of integers
i
such that
(i,i)
is on the semi-infinite geodesic from
(0,0)
.
For each of these exactly-solvable LPP models, using minor variations of the arguments in this paper, one can deduce results analogous to Theorem
1.2
.

As another remark on the generality of the methods in this paper, we point out that (by slightly adapting our arguments) similar convergence of local times can also be proved for point-to-point geodesics, going beyond semi-infinite geodesics, with the limit being the local time of the corresponding point-to-point geodesic in the directed landscape, which can also be constructed using the same methods employed in
[
GZ22
]
for the construction of the semi-infinite geodesic local time.

In terms of convergence from discrete models, it was first shown by Knight in

Convergence of the local time of more general random walks to BLT was later established in

However, perhaps more relevant to our paper is the recent explosion of developments around the understanding of planar models of random geometry in the KPZ universality class (see e.g.
[

,

1.4
Idea of proof

Before outlining the key ideas in the proof it might be instructive to discuss an approach to prove the counterpart statement showing the convergence of the random walk local time to the Brownian version. For example, in
[
MP10
, Section 6.1]
) it is shown that the occupation measure of Brownian motion
\mathcal{B}
of the cylinder
[0,1]\times[-w,w]
is approximated well by the number of times
\mathcal{B}
crosses the cylinder, and hence one can equivalently take the limit of the number of such crossings to construct Brownian local time.
The proof of this equivalence relies heavily on the Markovian nature of
\mathcal{B}
which provides precise crossing number estimates. The exact embedding of random walk in
\mathcal{B}
then allows one to compare the crossing number of the former to the crossing number of the latter, thereby implying convergence.

However, for the geodesic, being a rather global object, there is no Markovianity.
Nonetheless, owing to coalescence, it does, albeit in a rather implicit sense, exhibit some decay of correlation.

Our proof relies on making use of this and the known convergence of Exponential LPP and the associated geodesics to their counterparts in the directed landscape (from
[
DV21
]
).
However, the topology of convergence is in terms of Hausdorff distance between the geodesics thought of as compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is clearly not refined enough to capture the local time on the line
y=0.
Nonetheless, it does help relate the occupation measures of the geodesics in the limiting and pre-limiting settings. For instance, the convergence allows us to deduce that the amount of time the “suitably scaled” pre-limiting geodesic
\Gamma_{0}
spends in the cylinder
[-w,w]\times[0,1]
is close to that spent by
\pi_{(0,0)}
(relying on an apriori estimate on the occupation measure developed in
[
SSZ21
]
which rules out
\Gamma_{(0,0)}
spending a significant amount of time near the boundary of such a cylinder, i.e. in the narrower cylinders
[-w-\delta,-w+\delta]\times[0,1]
or
[w-\delta,w+\delta]\times[0,1]
).

As already stated, the occupation time of the cylinder
[-w,w]\times[0,1]
for
\pi_{(0,0)}
will be denoted by
L_{w}
. The corresponding quantity for
\Gamma_{(0,0)}
will be denoted by
L^{(n)}_{w}
(formal definition appears in (
3.1
)).

With the above notation, the preceding discussion implies that
L^{(n)}_{w}\to L_{w}
as
n\to\infty
.
Further, it was shown in
[
GZ22
, Proposition 2.4]
that
L=\lim_{w\to\infty}\frac{L_{w}}{2w}.
Thus the proof of Theorem
1.2
will be complete once we prove the following key estimate (which most of the paper is devoted to):

\big|L^{(n)}-\frac{L^{(n)}_{w}}{2w}\big|=o_{w}(n^{1/3}),

where
o_{w}(1)
denotes a quantity that converges to
0
with
w.
This will be shown by establishing that the pre-limiting local time exhibits a degree of
smoothness
. More precisely, for any
m,
we will show that the amounts of time spent by the geodesic
\Gamma_{(0,0)}
on the lines
\{x-y=m\}
with
|m|\leq wn^{2/3}
, up to time
n
(i.e., below the line
\{x+y=2n\}
), are very close to each other. For expository purposes, it would be convenient to work with unscaled coordinates in the proof in which the unscaled local time on the line
\{x-y=m\}
will be denoted by
Z^{(n)}[m]
. In this notation it will suffice to show that
\frac{\mathbb{E}[(Z^{(n)}[m]-Z^{(n)}[0])^{2}]}{n^{2/3}}=o_{w}(1)
for all
|m|\leq wn^{2/3}
(recall that
Z^{(n)}[0]
is expected to be of order
n^{1/3}
owing to the transversal fluctuations of
\Gamma_{(0,0)}
being of order
n^{2/3}
below the line
\{x+y=2n\}
).

Note that it is also natural to speculate that
Z^{(n)}[m]
varies smoothly as
m
varies, while the expectation is maximized at
m=0
. Thus we expect
\mathbb{E}[(Z^{(n)}[m]-Z^{(n)}[0])^{2}]
to be of order
w^{4}n^{2/3}
(due to a second order effect) when
|m|
is approximately
wn^{2/3}
.
However, we refrain from pursuing such a refined estimate.

We briefly outline in the remainder of this subsection the key steps involved in establishing the above-mentioned smoothness across Sections
4
and
5
, which can be considered as the main novelty in the paper. The key input is a stability estimate which shows geodesics, between arbitrary nice enough boundary conditions (formal definition appears in Proposition
4.6
), are stable under mild perturbations of the boundary data. This is done in two steps. First, we show that if the boundary data is perturbed, the original and the perturbed geodesic must be close to each other during a large fraction of their journey owing to certain regularity and decay properties of last passage values as the endpoints are varied. The next step is to show that it is unlikely that two geodesic paths spending a lot of time next to each other do not coalesce. This last statement has already been proven and applied in a few other instances (
[
GH20
,
BHS18
,
MSZ21
]
).

Given this general stability, the smoothness of the probability of the geodesic passing through nearby points, and hence the local time, is shown by shifting the noise environment suitably (causing a perturbation of the boundary data as depicted in Figure
3
). Under the natural coupling of the corresponding geodesics, the event of one geodesic passing through a given point and the other geodesic not passing through the corresponding shifted point would entail a non-coalescence of the two geodesics whose probability is shown to be low using the arguments in the preceding paragraph.

We end this discussion by remarking on a technical point that in fact to perform second-moment computations we would need to show the smoothness of two-point functions, i.e., the probability of the geodesic passing through a pair of points, as one of the two points is varied in space.

2.3.1
Busemann functions

Busemann functions were first used to study geodesics in Riemannian manifolds.
They were introduced in the context of first passage percolation (FPP) models by Hoffman

Hof08
]

We now set up the Busemann function in Exponential LPP, which has also been intensively studied and widely used (see e.g.

By the tree structure of semi-infinite geodesics (already alluded to in the earlier discussion on Exponential LPP), for each

For any down-right path

2.4

In

To state the convergence, we need a linear transformation of Exponential LPP to the scaling of the directed landscape. Among other things, the transformation redirects the orientation from the
(1,1)

For any
p=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we let
\mathsf{R}_{n}(p)=(2^{-5/3}n^{-2/3}(x-y),n^{-1}y)

For any set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\mathsf{R}_{n}(A)=\{(\mathsf{R}_{n}(p):p\in A\}
.
We then define a function
\mathcal{K}_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the transformed last-passage times.
It is also a “directed metric” on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(as it satisfies a composition law similar to (
1.1
)).
For any
(x,s;y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, we let

\mathcal{K}_{n}(x,s;y,t)=2^{-4/3}n^{-1/3}F(\mathsf{R}_{n}^{-1}(x,s);\mathsf{R}_{n}^{-1}(y,t)).

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
is the rounded version of last-passage times, defined as follows.
Let
\mathsf{r}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the “rounding” function where

\mathsf{r}(x,y)=\begin{cases}(x,y)&x,y\in\mathbb{Z};\\
(\lfloor x\rfloor,y)&x\not\in\mathbb{Z},y\in\mathbb{Z};\\
(x,\lfloor y\rfloor)&x\in\mathbb{Z},y\not\in\mathbb{Z};\\
(\lceil x\rceil,\lfloor y\rfloor)&x\not\in\mathbb{Z},y\not\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

We then let
F(x,y;z,w)=T_{(\lceil x\rceil,\lceil y\rceil),\mathsf{r}(z,w)}-2d(\mathsf{r}(z,w)-\mathsf{r}(x,y))-\omega_{(x,y)}\mathds{1}[x,y\in\mathbb{Z}]
.
Here
2d(\mathsf{r}(z,w)-\mathsf{r}(x,y))
is the leading linear approximation of
T_{(\lceil x\rceil,\lceil y\rceil),\mathsf{r}(z,w)}
, and
\omega_{(x,y)}\mathds{1}[x,y\in\mathbb{Z}]
is the correction term to make
F
satisfy a composition law (similar to (
1.1
)).
We note that
\mathcal{K}_{n}(x,s;y,t)=-\infty
if
(\lceil ns+2^{5/3}n^{2/3}x\rceil,\lceil ns\rceil)\not\preceq\mathsf{r}(nt+2^{5/3}n^{2/3}y,nt)
, as we let
T_{p,q}=-\infty
when
p\not\preceq q
.

For any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called a

in
\mathcal{K}_{n}
from
p
to
q

\preceq_{A}
on
A
, such that
p\preceq_{A}p^{\prime}\preceq_{A}q
for any
p^{\prime}\in A
;
and for any
p_{1}\preceq_{A}p_{2}\preceq_{A}p_{3}\in A
,

\mathcal{K}_{n}(p_{1};p_{2})+\mathcal{K}_{n}(p_{2};p_{3})=\mathcal{K}_{n}(p_{1};p_{3}).

(2.4)

Such a set
A
is called a
maximal geodesic set
in
\mathcal{K}_{n}
if it is not contained in any other geodesic set from
p
to
q
.
From these definitions, we have that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the set
\mathsf{R}_{n}(\Gamma_{p,q})
is a maximal geodesic set from
\mathsf{R}_{n}(p)
to
\mathsf{R}_{n}(q)
.
Indeed, obviously
\mathsf{R}_{n}(\Gamma_{p,q})
is a geodesic set, and it is maximal because adding any extra point (with any ordering) would violate (
2.4
) (while this can be checked straightforwardly, we omit the somewhat tedious details.)

Finally, for any continuous path
\pi:I\to\mathbb{R}
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is any subset, we define its
graph

\Graph(\pi)
as the set
\{(\pi(t),t):t\in I\}
. We then have the following convergence result.

Theorem 2.14

(
[

)
.

There is a coupling of
\mathcal{L}
with

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the following holds almost surely.
First, for any compact

, we have
\mathcal{K}_{n}\to\mathcal{L}
uniformly in

.
Second, for any

,
and any

.

The convergence of the Exponential LPP semi-infinite geodesic

2.5
Occupation estimates

We will rely on the following crucial tail estimate established in

Lemma 2.16

There are universal constants

Lemma 2.17
.

Almost surely the following holds.
For any

is continuous for

.

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have that there exists a random

, such that for any

\int_{0}^{2^{a}}\mathds{1}[\pi_{\mathbf{0}}(t)\in[i2^{b},(i+2)2^{b}]]dt<R(|a|+|b|+|i|^{1/2}+1)2^{b+a/3}.

Indeed, for any fixed

For any

So the conclusion follows.
∎

.

Without loss of generality, we prove that

.

.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(L^{(n)}(h)-L(h))^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle 3\mathbb{E}[(L^{(n)}(h)-(m/2)L^{(n)}_{1/m}(h))^{2}]+3(m/2)^{2}\mathbb{E}[(L^{(n)}_{1/m}(h)-L_{1/m}(h))^{2}]

For any

.

4
Continuity of occupation measure

In this section, we prove Proposition

.
Without loss of generality, we assume

As already indicated, our general strategy is to write

as an average of local times of

We will prove the following result.

Proposition 4.1

.

By (

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{|m|\leq 2^{5/3}n^{2/3}w}\frac{2^{2/3}n^{-1/3}}{2\lfloor 2^{5/3}n^{2/3}w\rfloor+1}(Z^{(n)}[m]-Z^{(n)}[0])\right|+(4nw)^{-1}.

\displaystyle\leq

>f_{1}(0)+f_{2}(0)+T_{(m_{-}+1,m_{-}),(m,m-1)}

\max_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z},|i|\vee|j|>H_{1}l^{2/3}}f_{1}(i)+f_{3}(j)+T_{(m_{-}+i+1,m_{-}-i),(m+j,m-j-1)}\\

, which depends only on

.
By (

) and (

) from the event

, and the expectation estimate (

), we have that for any

(f_{1}(i)+i^{2}/m_{-}-\beta^{-1}n^{1/3}-\beta i^{2}/n)+f_{2}(j)+(\mathbb{E}[T_{(m_{-}+i+1,m_{-}-i),(m+j,m-j-1)}]+(i-j)^{2}/l)\\
<(f_{1}(0)+\beta^{-1}n^{1/3}+\beta i^{2}/n)+(f_{2}(0)+\beta^{-1}n^{1/3}+\beta j^{2}/n)+\mathbb{E}[T_{(m_{-}+1,m_{-}),(m,m-1)}]+Cn^{1/3},

where

Recall from the beginning of this proof that

Thus when

when

5
Stability with respect to boundary conditions

In this section, we prove Proposition
4.6
.
Throughout this section, we take
H_{1},H_{2}>0
as two fixed constants, and all other constants are allowed to depend on them.

As already indicated in Section
1.4
, the general idea to prove Proposition
4.6
involves two steps. In the first, we show that with high probability, the geodesics
\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{n}
and
\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{3}}^{n}
share a significant part of their journey being quite close to each other; in the second step we show that the conclusion of the first step implies that the geodesics coalesce with high probability. The two steps are carried out in Sections
5.1
and
5.2
respectively.

5.1
Closeness of geodesics at one time

Proposition 5.1
.

For each
\epsilon,\lambda>0
,
0.1<\alpha<0.9
, the following is true for any small enough
w,g>0
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough (depending on
w,g
).
Take any
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
with
|f_{2}-f_{3}|\leq gn^{1/3}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[|\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}-\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{3}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}|<\lambda n^{2/3}]>1-\epsilon.

To prove this, we first prove a limiting version in the directed landscape. While this is in principle not necessary, taking advantage of the local Brownian property (Theorem
2.6
) in the limit is convenient for the exposition and helps make the proof cleaner.

Overall the underlying observation is that the non-closeness of the geodesic implies a “twin-peaks” event, i.e., where certain passage time profiles on
\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}
are nearly maximized at two points with distance
\geq\lambda n^{2/3}
. In the directed landscape, this is essentially the same as a Brownian motion having a “near-maxima” significantly away from the global maximum, an event whose probability is small.
The limiting twin-peaks event estimate is stated as follows.
We let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all upper semi-continuous functions
f:[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
|f|\leq 2^{-4/3}H_{2}
.
For each
f\in\mathscr{F}
we denote its hypograph
\hypo(f)
as

\hypo(f)=\{(x,y):x\in[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}],-2^{-4/3}H_{2}\leq y\leq f(x)\},

which is a closed subset of
[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]\times[-2^{-4/3}H_{2},2^{-4/3}H_{2}]
.
Since the space of all compact subsets of
[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]\times[-2^{-4/3}H_{2},2^{-4/3}H_{2}]
is complete (with the Hausdorff distance metric), for any sequence of functions in
\mathscr{F}
, we can find a subsequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\hypo(f_{k})
converges in the Hausdorff topology as
k\to\infty
.
It is straightforward to check that the limit must be
\hypo(f)
for some function
f\in\mathscr{F}
.

Below we let
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the Hausdorff distance between compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
\epsilon,\lambda>0
,
0.1<\alpha<0.9
, and
H^{\prime}>0
, there is
\delta>0
, such that the following is true.

Take any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{F}
,
define
f_{*}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as

f_{*}(x)=\sup_{|y|,|z|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}}\mathcal{L}(y,0;x,\alpha)+\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha;z,1)+f_{1}(y)+f_{2}(z),

and we let
\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})
denote the following event: for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]
with
x_{2}-x_{1}\geq 2^{-2/3}\lambda
we have
f_{*}(x_{1})\wedge f_{*}(x_{2})\leq\sup_{x\in[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]}f_{*}(x)-2^{-4/3}\delta
(i.e., the event of no-twin-peaks).
Then we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})]>1-\epsilon
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{F}}\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})]=1
.
We argue by contradiction, and suppose that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can find some
\delta_{k}>0
, and
f_{1,k},f_{2,k}\in\mathscr{F}
, such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=0
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}}(f_{1,k},f_{2,k})]=\gamma<1
.
By taking a subsequence we can assume that
\hypo(f_{1,k})\to\hypo(f_{1,\infty})
and
\hypo(f_{2,k})\to\hypo(f_{2,\infty})
in the Hausdorff topology, for some
f_{1,\infty},f_{2,\infty}\in\mathscr{F}
.
In other words, if we let
\tau_{k}=\mathcal{H}(\hypo(f_{1,k}),\hypo(f_{1,\infty}))\vee\mathcal{H}(\hypo(f_{2,k}),\hypo(f_{2,\infty}))
, then
\tau_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

We now explain how we will reach a contradiction.
Define
f_{*,k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f_{*,k}(x)=\sup_{|y|,|z|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}}\mathcal{L}(y,0;x,\alpha)+\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha;z,1)+f_{1,k}(y)+f_{2,k}(z),

for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
On one hand,
f_{*,\infty}
is local Brownian (by Theorem
2.6
), so almost surely it has a unique maximum in any compact interval.
However, we will use
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}}(f_{1,k},f_{2,k})]=\gamma<1
to deduce that with probability
\geq 1-\gamma
, the maximum of
f_{*,\infty}
in
[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]
is not unique.
This can be achieved by bounding the difference between
f_{*,k}
and
f_{*,\infty}
, using Lemma
2.4
.

We next fill in the details of this outlined strategy.
Take
R
as given by Lemma
2.4
, where the compact set
K
is taken as the union of

([-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]\times[0,\alpha/3])\times([-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]\times[2\alpha/3,2\alpha/3+1/3])

and

([-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]\times[2\alpha/3,2\alpha/3+1/3])\times([-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]\times[\alpha/3+2/3,1]).

We take
K
this way so that we can now bound
|\mathcal{L}(y,0;x,\alpha)-\mathcal{L}(y^{\prime},0;x,\alpha)|
and
|\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha;z,1)-\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha;z^{\prime},1)|
, for any
|x|\leq 2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}
and
|y|,|z|,|y^{\prime}|,|z^{\prime}|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}
.

Using
\tau_{k}
defined above, for any
|x|\leq 2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}
and
|y|,|z|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}
, we can find
y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
such that
|y^{\prime}|,|z^{\prime}|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}
,
|y-y^{\prime}|,|z-z^{\prime}|\leq\tau_{k}
, and
f_{1,k}(y^{\prime})\geq f_{1,\infty}(y)-\tau_{k}
,
f_{2,k}(z^{\prime})\geq f_{2,\infty}(z)-\tau_{k}
.
Thus

f_{*,k}(x)-f_{*,\infty}(x)\geq-(C\tau_{k}+2R\tau_{k}^{1/2}\log^{1/2}(1+\tau_{k}^{-1/2})),

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
H^{\prime}
and
H_{1}
.
Similarly, the same lower bound holds for
f_{*,\infty}(x)-f_{*,k}(x)
.
So we have

|f_{*,k}(x)-f_{*,\infty}(x)|\leq C\tau_{k}+2R\tau_{k}^{1/2}\log^{1/2}(1+\tau_{k}^{-1/2}).

Given that
R\leq\tau_{k}^{-1/4}\log^{-1/2}(1+\tau_{k}^{-1/2})
, the right-hand side is
\leq 2\tau_{k}^{1/4}+C\tau_{k}
, and we have that
\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}+2\tau_{k}^{1/4}+C\tau_{k}}(f_{1,\infty},f_{2,\infty})
implies
\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}}(f_{1,k},f_{2,k})
.
Therefore we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}+2\tau_{k}^{1/4}+C\tau_{k}}(f_{1,\infty},f_{2,\infty})]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}}(f_{1,k},f_{2,k})]+\mathbb{P}[R>\tau_{k}^{-1/4}\log^{-1/2}(1+\tau_{k}^{-1/2})].

Thus
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}+2\tau_{k}^{1/4}+C\tau_{k}}(f_{1,\infty},f_{2,\infty})]\leq\gamma
.

If
f_{*,\infty}
has a unique maximum in
[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]
, for any sufficiently large
k
the event
\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta_{k}+2\tau_{k}^{1/4}+C\tau_{k}}(f_{1,\infty},f_{2,\infty})
holds.
So the probability of
f_{*,\infty}
having a unique maximum in
[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]
is at most
\gamma
.

By Theorem
2.6
the functions

x\mapsto\sup_{|y|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}}\mathcal{L}(y,0;x,\alpha)+f_{1,k}(y),\quad x\mapsto\sup_{|z|\leq 2^{-2/3}H_{1}}\mathcal{L}(x,\alpha;z,1)+f_{2,k}(z)

are absolutely continuous with respect to Brownian motions in any compact interval. (Note that the suprema are taken over compact intervals.)
Then we have that their sum
f_{*,\infty}
in
[-2^{-2/3}H^{\prime},2^{-2/3}H^{\prime}]
is absolutely continuous with respect to a Brownian motion, thus has a unique maximum almost surely.
Thus we get a contradiction.
∎

We next use this estimate on the twin-peaks event in the limiting setting to prove Proposition
5.1
.
Note that the estimate in Proposition
5.1
is required to hold uniformly for any
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
.
To achieve this we will take a finite family of functions so that any function in
\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
is close to one in this family.

From now on, let
T_{p,q}^{*}=T_{p,q}-\omega_{p}
for any
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
vertices
p\preceq q
.
Removing the weight of the first vertex as such helps to get useful independence between passage times, as we will soon see.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We first define the no-twin-peaks event in Exponential LPP.
Let
H^{\prime}>0
be a large number depending on
\epsilon,\alpha
, and the requirement for its value will be evident from the arguments below.
For each
f_{1},f_{2}:\llbracket-H_{1}n^{2/3},H_{1}n^{2/3}\rrbracket\to\mathbb{R}
, we let
T_{f_{1},f_{2}}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function where

T_{f_{1},f_{2}}(i)=\max_{|i_{1}|,|i_{2}|\leq H_{1}n^{2/3}}T_{(i_{1},-i_{1}),(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor+i,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-i)}+T^{*}_{(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor+i,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-i),(n+i_{2},n-i_{2})}+f_{1}(i_{1})+f_{2}(i_{2}).

Let

\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta,n}(f_{1},f_{2})
be the event where for any
i_{1},i_{2}\in\llbracket-H^{\prime}n^{2/3},H^{\prime}n^{2/3}\rrbracket
with
i_{2}-i_{1}\geq\lambda n^{2/3}
, one has
T_{f_{1},f_{2}}(i_{1})\wedge T_{f_{1},f_{2}}(i_{2})\leq\max_{|i|\leq H^{\prime}n^{2/3}}T_{f_{1},f_{2}}(i)-\delta n^{1/3}
.

As the no-twin-peaks event only involves the function
T_{f_{1},f_{2}}
on the interval
\llbracket-H^{\prime}n^{2/3},H^{\prime}n^{2/3}\rrbracket
, we need to bound the transversal fluctuation of geodesics.
By Lemma
2.9
, by taking
H^{\prime}>0
large, for any
n
large enough we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{trans}]>1-\epsilon/2
, where
\mathcal{E}_{trans}
is the event

\Gamma_{(-\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor,\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor),(n-\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor,n+\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor)}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}\subset\{(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-i,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor+i):|i|\leq H^{\prime}n^{2/3}\},

and

\Gamma_{(\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor,-\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor),(n+\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor,n-\lfloor H_{1}n^{2/3}\rfloor)}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}\subset\{(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-i,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor+i):|i|\leq H^{\prime}n^{2/3}\}.

Then under
\mathcal{E}_{trans}
, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
we must have that

\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}\subset\{(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-i,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor+i):|i|\leq H^{\prime}n^{2/3}\},

since any two geodesics cannot cross each other twice.

Now by taking
w,g
small and then
n
large, we have that for any
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
with
|f_{2}-f_{3}|\leq gn^{1/3}
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}[|\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}-\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{3}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}|<\lambda n^{2/3}]\\
\geq&\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{g,n}(f_{1},f_{2})]-(1-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{trans}])\\
>&\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{g,n}(f_{1},f_{2})]-\epsilon/2.\end{split}

(5.1)

Here the first inequality is due to that, if the intersections
\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}
and
\Gamma_{f_{1},f_{3}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}
are
\geq\lambda n^{2/3}
from each other, and the transversal fluctuation of geodesics is bounded (i.e.,
\mathcal{E}_{trans}
happens), then the twin-peaks event (i.e., the complement of
\mathsf{NoTP}_{g,n}(f_{1},f_{2})
) must occur.
Then it remains to bound the probability of the latter, for which we use the limiting estimate given by Lemma
5.2
.

For any
f:\llbracket-H_{1}n^{2/3},H_{1}n^{2/3}\rrbracket\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathsf{S}_{n}f
be the function on
[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]
, such that
\mathsf{S}_{n}f(x)=2^{-4/3}n^{-1/3}f(\lfloor(2n)^{2/3}x\rfloor)

Note that this is well defined only when
\lfloor(2n)^{2/3}x\rfloor\geq-H_{1}n^{2/3}
. For
x
near
-2^{-2/3}H_{1}
(i.e., when
\lfloor(2n)^{2/3}x\rfloor<-H_{1}n^{2/3}

\mathsf{S}_{n}f(x)=2^{-4/3}n^{-1/3}f(\lceil(2n)^{2/3}x\rceil)
.

To bound the twin-peaks probability uniformly in the boundary functions, we take a finite family of functions in
\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
to approximate every function in
\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
, and do limit transition for each function in this finite family to use Lemma
5.2
.

Towards this, for any
w,g>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
\{f_{w,g,n;k}\}_{k=1}^{m_{w,g}}\subset\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
, and
\{f_{w,g;k}\}_{k=1}^{m_{w,g}}\subset\mathscr{F}
, where

depends only on
w,g
, such that

1.

\mathsf{S}_{n}f_{w,g,n;k}\to f_{w,g;k}
uniformly as

;

2.

for each

, there is
f_{w,g,n;k}
, such that

.

One way to choose such functions is as follows.
First, take some
\iota>0
small enough (depending on
w,g
).
Let
\{f_{w,g;k}\}_{k=1}^{m_{w,g}}
contain all functions on
[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}]
, which (1) take values in
\iota\mathbb{Z}\cap[-2^{-4/3}H_{2},2^{-4/3}H_{2}]
at
(\iota\mathbb{Z}\cap[-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}])\cup\{-2^{-2/3}H_{1},2^{-2/3}H_{1}\}
, (2) are linear on the rest of the domain, and (3) are in the closure (in the uniform convergence topology) of
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{\mathsf{S}_{n}f:f\in\mathscr{F}_{w,n}\}
.
Then we let
f_{w,g,n;k}
be the function in
\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
with the smallest
\|\mathsf{S}_{n}f_{w,g,n;k}-f_{w,g;k}\|_{\infty}
.

By Lemma
5.2
, we can take
\delta>0
small enough, such that
\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})]>1-\epsilon/4
for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{F}
.
By Theorem
2.14
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta,n}(f_{w,g,n;k_{1}},f_{w,g,n;k_{2}})]=\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{NoTP}_{\delta}(f_{w,g;k_{1}},f_{w,g,k_{2}})]>1-\epsilon/4,

(5.2)

for any
w,g>0
,
1\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq m_{w,g}
.
We next extend this bound to all boundary functions in
\mathscr{F}_{w,n}
.

We note that for any

,

\max_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|T_{f_{1},f_{2}}(i)-T_{f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}}(i)|\leq\max_{|i|\leq H_{1}n^{2/3}}|f_{1}(i)-f_{1}^{\prime}(i)|+\max_{|i|\leq H_{1}n^{2/3}}|f_{2}(i)-f_{2}^{\prime}(i)|.

Then by the second property of

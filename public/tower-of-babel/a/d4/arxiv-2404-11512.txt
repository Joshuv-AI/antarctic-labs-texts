Central limit theorems for Green metrics on hyperbolic groups
Stephen Cantrell, Mark Pollicott

Abstract
. Suppose we have two finitely supported, admissible, probability measures on a hyperbolic group Γ \Gamma . In this article we prove that the corresponding two Green metrics satisfy a counting central limit theorem when we order the elements of Γ \Gamma according to one of the metrics. Our results also apply to various other metrics including length functions associated to Anosov representations and to group actions on hyperbolic metric spaces.



1.
Introduction

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group (for example, a free group, the fundamental group of negatively curved, closed Riemannian manifold, etc). Recently there has been much interest in understanding the statistical behaviour of various natural real valued functions on
\Gamma
. There has been particular attention given to limit theorems in which group elements in
\Gamma
are ordered by their word length with respect to a finite generating set.
More precisely, suppose that
\Psi:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a real valued function on
\Gamma
that counts or represents a quantity of interest (for example a quasi-morphism or the displacement associated to the action of
\Gamma
on a metric space) and that we have fixed a finite generating set
S
for
\Gamma
. Write
S_{n}=\{g\in\Gamma:|x|_{S}=n\}
for the collection of group elements of
S
word length
n
. Multiple authors have studied central limit theorems for the sequence of counting distributions

\frac{1}{\#S_{n}}\#\left\{g\in S_{n}:\frac{\Psi(g)-\Lambda n}{\sqrt{n}}\leq t\right\}\ \text{ where $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$, $n\geq 1$.}

See for example
[
HS09
]
,
[
Riv10
]
,
[
CF10
]
,
[
CL12
]
,
[
CW13
]
,
[
CM15
]
,
[
GTT18
]
,
[
GTT20
]
,
[
CS23
]
,
[
Can23
]
amongst many other works. In all of these works the authors exploit the automatic structure of hyperbolic groups: the fact that hyperbolic groups equipped with a generating set can in some sense be encoded by a finite directed graph. Using this automatic structure the sequence of uniform counting measures on
S_{n}
can be modelled by the stationary measure for a Markov chain (or measure of maximal entropy for a subshift of finite type). Using this correspondence, it is possible to use tools from probability theory to study counting problems for word metrics.

This discussion leads us to ask whether similar central limit theorems hold when we order group elements by a different metric (other than a word metric). For example, is it possible to prove a central limit theorem similar to the above but with the word metric replaced with the displacement function associated to the action of
\Gamma
on a hyperbolic metric space or with a Green metric associated to a random walk? Not much appears to be known about this problem in general. This is likely because, unlike for word metrics, there is not a well understood probabilistic model that emulates counting over spheres for a general metric. Consequently, it is often the case that the asymptotic properties of a particular quantity is known when counting with respect to word metrics but not when counting over other metrics. This is the case for stable commutator length for example. Indeed, the large deviation properties of stable commutator length are well-understood when group elements are ordered by a word length. This is due to Calegari-Walker
[
CW13
]
and Calegari-Maher
[
CM15
]
. However the analogous results when ordering by other metrics are unknown, see Conjecture A.11 in
[
CW13
]
. We discuss these points further in Section
1.4
.

In specific settings some statistical results with counting over non word metrics are known:
[
CP22
]
,
[
SS93
]
,
[
Lal89
]
,
[
BL98
]
. In these works tools from thermodynamic formalism were used and in particular, delicate Dolgopyat type estimates for norms of certain transfer operators were exploited. These estimates are known to hold in only a few particular situations.

The aim of this work is to obtain general counting central limit theorems where we count over metrics that are not word metrics and for which we do not have access to Dolgopyat type estimates for corresponding transfer operators. We prove a central limit theorem that compares pairs of
strongly hyperbolic
metrics on
\Gamma
. These are metrics that are ‘stable at infinity’: their Busseman functions are Hölder continuous cocycles (see Section
2
for the precise definition). Before stating our results we introduce notation and recall some preliminary facts.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denote the normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}
:

\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-u^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}\ du.

Two metrics
d,d_{\ast}
on
\Gamma
are called
roughly similar
if there is
\Lambda,C>0
such that
|d(g,h)-\Lambda d_{\ast}(g,h)|\leq C
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
. The translation length function of
d
is defined as the limit

\ell_{d}[g]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,g^{n})}{n}

and is defined on conjugacy classes
[g]
in
\Gamma
. Here we use
o\in\Gamma
to denote the identity element. We call
\Gamma

non-elementary
if it does not contain a finite index cyclic subgroup and say that
\Gamma
is
virtually free
if it
contains a free group as a finite index subgroup. We say that
d
has
non-arithmetic length spectrum
if
\ell_{d}
does not take values lying in a lattice
a\mathbb{Z}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. When
\Gamma
is not virtually free, every strongly hyperbolic on
\Gamma
has non-arithmetic length spectrum
[
GMM18
]
(also see
[
Can23
, Theorem 1.4]
).
We let

\tau(d_{\ast}/d):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}\frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)}{T}>0

denote the
mean distortion
of
d_{\ast}
with respect to
d
which exists by
[
CT24b
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and take two strongly hyperbolic metrics
d,d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. Suppose either that
\Gamma
is not virtually free or that
\Gamma
is virtually free and that
d
has non-arithmetic length spectrum. Let
\tau=\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
be the mean distortion.
Then the following limit exists

\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}\frac{(d_{\ast}(o,g)-\tau T)^{2}}{T}\geq 0.

Furthermore the following are equivalent:

(1)

\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)=0
; and,

(2)

d
and
d_{\ast}
are roughly similar.

Remark 1.2
.

We identify
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)
with the second derivative at
0
of the
Manhattan curve
for
d_{\ast},d
. See Section
1.3
.

The constant
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)
then appears as the variance in the following central limit theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group and take two strongly hyperbolic metrics
d,d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. Suppose either that
\Gamma
is not virtually free or that
\Gamma
is virtually free and that
d
has non-arithmetic length spectrum. Then there exist
\tau=\tau(d_{\ast}/d)>0
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)\geq 0
such that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
.
Furthermore,
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)=0
if and only if
d
and
d_{\ast}
are roughly similar.

We stress that this result is new even in the case when
\Gamma
is a free group or surface group.

In the rest of this introduction we present some applications of this theorem and then discuss our method of proof. Further applications and results are included in the final section.

1.1.
Random walks

Suppose that
\mu
is a finitely supported probability measure, with symmetric support (i.e.
\mu(g)=\mu(g^{-1})
for all
g\in\Gamma
), on
\Gamma
such that the support of
\mu
generates
\Gamma
as a semi-group. Let
G(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the corresponding
Green function
defined by

G(g,h)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\mu^{\ast n}(g^{-1}h)\ \text{ for $g,h\in\Gamma$}

where
\mu^{\ast n}
denotes the
n
th convolution of
\mu
.
The
Green metric
is defined as

d_{\mu}(g,h)=-\log\left(\frac{G(g,h)}{G(o,o)}\right)

and intuitively
d_{\mu}(g,h)
represents the (minus logarithm of the) probability that a random walk starting at
g
reaches
h
. Then
d_{\mu}
is a strongly hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

[
NŠ16
]
with
v_{d_{\mu}}=1
. Hence we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.4

(CLT for Green metrics)
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group and take two Green metrics
d,d_{\ast}
on
\Gamma
associated to finite, admissible probability measures
\mu,\mu_{\ast}
both with symmetric support. Suppose either that
\Gamma
is not virtually free or that
\Gamma
is virtually free and that
d
has non-arithmetic length spectrum. Then there exists
\tau=\tau(d_{\ast}/d)\geq 1
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)\geq 0
such that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
. Furthermore the following are equivalent

(1)

d
and
d_{\ast}
are roughly similar;

(2)

\sigma^{2}=0
; and,

(3)

\tau=1
.

In fact, it is even possible to weaken the assumption that
\mu,\mu_{\ast}
have finite support to having super-exponential moment by a result of Gouëzel
[
Gou15
]
. Furthermore, in the case when
\Gamma
is a free group or surface group we can also remove the assumption that
\mu,\mu_{\ast}
are symmetric
[
Gou14
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

Our theorem provides an equivalent formulation of the Singularity Conjecture.
Suppose that
\Gamma
is a surface group and that
\mu
is a finitely supported admissible probability measure on
\Gamma
. Let
d_{\mu}
be the corresponding (not necessarily symmetric) Green metric and write
d
for (the lift of) a hyperbolic metric on
\Gamma
coming from the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}
. The Singularity Conjecture predicts that for any
\mu
as above, the corresponding hitting measure
on
\partial\Gamma=S^{1}
is mutually singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. The conjecture is equivalent to the assertion that for
\mu
as above,
d_{\mu}
and
d
are never roughly similar
[
GMM18
]
. Despite recent progress
[
CLP19
]
,
[
GL23
]
,
[
Kos21
]
,
[
KT22
]
, the problem remains open. Theorem
1.3
provides the following equivalent formulation of the singularity conjecture: the metrics
d_{\mu}
and
d
satisfy a non-degenerate central limit theorem (i.e.
\sigma^{2}(d_{\mu}/d)>0
) as in Theorem
1.3
if and only if the hitting measure of
\mu
is mutually singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. This result also generalises to higher dimensions.

1.2.
Anosov representations

Recall that a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
(of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
) is projective Anosov if the first and second singular values of
\rho
separate exponentially as word length increases, i.e. given a finite generating set
S
on
\Gamma
there exists
C,\Lambda>0
such that

\frac{\sigma_{1}(\rho(g))}{\sigma_{2}(\rho(g))}\geq Ce^{\Lambda|g|_{S}}

for all
g\in\Gamma
. Here given
A\in{\rm SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\sigma_{1}(A)\geq\sigma_{2}(A)\geq\ldots\sigma_{d}(A)
are the singular values of
A
.
This is not the original definition of projective Anosov introduced by Labourie
[
Lab06
]
(for surface groups) and extended (to all groups) by Guichard and Wienhard
[
GW12
]
however it is easy to state and is known to be equivalent by the work
[
BPS19
]
. Furthermore, groups admitting a projective Anosov representation are necessarily hyperbolic
[
BPS19
]
.
The Hilbert length functional associated to
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
is given by

\alpha(g,h):=\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(g^{-1}h))-\log\sigma_{d}(\rho(g^{-1}h))\ \text{ for $g,h\in\Gamma$. }

These are examples of strongly hyperbolic metrics (this follows from
[
CT24a
, Lemma 7.1]
). We therefore deduce the following.

Theorem 1.6

(CLT for Anosov representations)
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is non-elementary, is not virtually free and that
\alpha,\alpha_{\ast}:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
are Hilbert length functions associated to two projective Anosov representations
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}),\rho_{\ast}:\Gamma\to{\rm SL}_{m}({\mathbb{R}})
as above. Then there exists
\tau=\tau(\rho_{\ast}/\rho)>0
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}(\rho_{\ast}/\rho)\geq 0
such that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:\alpha(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:\alpha(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\alpha_{\ast}(o,g)-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
.

For Hitchin representations, the variance being
0
in the above theorem determines the representation entirely (i.e.
\sigma=0
if and only if the representations are the same or are contragredient). This also holds for Benoist representations, see
[
LCW22
, Remark 7.11]
.

1.3.
Rigidity results

When comparing metrics
d,d_{\ast}
a natural object to study is their
Manhattan curve

\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
. This curve is defined as follows, for each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}(s)
to be the abscissa of convergence of

\sum_{x\in\Gamma}e^{-sd_{\ast}(o,x)-td(o,x)}

as
t
varies. In
[
CT24a
]
it was shown that for pairs of strongly hyperbolic metrics
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
is analytic. It was also shown that the constant
-\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
is the derivative at
0
of
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}(s)
, i.e.
\theta^{\prime}_{d_{\ast}/d}(0)=-\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
.
By studying the Manhattan curve the first author and Tanaka proved various rigidity results for pairs of metrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. In particular they show that the following are equivalent (see Theorem
2.6
below):

(1)

d
and
d_{\ast}
are roughly similar;

(2)

the Manhattan curve
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
is a straight line;

(3)

\tau(d_{\ast}/d)=v_{d}/v_{d_{\ast}}
; and,

(4)

there exists
\tau>0
such that
\ell_{d}[x]=\tau\ell_{d_{\ast}}[x]
for all
x\in\Gamma
.

Here
v_{d}
(respectively
v_{d_{\ast}}
) is exponential growth rate of
d
(respectively
d_{\ast}
), i.e.

v_{d}=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,x)<T\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ v_{d_{\ast}}=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#\{x\in\Gamma:d_{\ast}(o,x)<T\}.

When any of the above statements fail to hold it was shown that
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
is a globally strictly convex function and in fact
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. A large deviation principle comparing
d,d_{\ast}
was also shown in
[
CT24b
]
.

Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
then provide an additional rigidity characterisation to those above. As with the constant
\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
we can see
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)
in the Manhattan curve
\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
and in this case as a second derivative.

Theorem 1.7
.

The constant
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)\geq 0
appearing in Theorem
1.1
can be realised as
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)=\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}^{\prime\prime}(0)
.

We prove this result in Section
3
along with Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.8
.

This result has the following consequence.
When
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
\mathfrak{g}
and
\rho_{t}
for
-\epsilon<t<\epsilon
is a smooth (non-constant) family of Riemannian surfaces then the Weil-Petersson metric can be recovered as

\left\|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\rho_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\text{WP}}=\frac{3\pi(\mathfrak{g}-1)}{2}\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}\sigma^{2}(d_{t}/d_{0})

where each
d_{t}
for
-\epsilon<t<\epsilon
is the lift of
\rho_{t}
to
\Gamma
. This follows from Theorem
1.1
and
[
PS16
, Lemma 4.2]
and complements results of McMullen
[
McM08
]
.

1.4.
Ideas behind the proof and structure of the paper

We now outline our method of proof. We note that our theorems do not readily follow from the works of the second author and Tanaka
[
CT24a
]
,
[
CT24b
]
(in which the corresponding large deviation principle was proven). This is because, although the methods and techniques introduced in those papers can be used to study Manhattan curves, they cannot be used to study versions of the Manhattan curve with complex perturbations/parameters. Understanding complex versions of Manhattan curves is vital as they are linked to the Fourier transforms of our counting distributions (and hence they determine whether the central limit theorem holds). We also note that it does not appear possible to tackle Theorem
1.3
using random walks on groups as in
[
GTT20
]
or Markov chains as in
[
CS23
]
. This is because, as we discussed above, unlike for counting over group elements of word length at most
T
as in
[
GTT20
]
and
[
CS23
]
, there is not a well understood probabilistic model for counting over the group elements
g
satisfying
d(o,g)<T
.

To prove Theorem
1.3
we use the method of moments and techniques from thermodynamic formalism. That is, we show that for each
p\geq 1
the
p
th moments of the sequence of distributions in Theorem
1.3
converge as
T\to\infty
to the appropriate limit (see equation (
6
)). To do this we

(1)

introduce a two variable Poincaré series
\eta
that encodes information about
d
and
d_{\ast}
;

(2)

prove results about the domain of analyticity and poles of
\eta
(this step uses Cannon’s automatic structure for hyperbolic groups and builds upon recent results from
[
CT24a
]
,
[
Can23
]
); and,

(3)

extract the asymptotics we need for the method of moments using a Tauberian Theorem.

This method of proof was inspired by the work of Morris
[
Mor15
]
on the statistical properties of the Euclidean algorithm and also the work of Hwang and Janson
[
HJ11
]
.

The idea of proving asymptotic counting results through Poincaré series has been used by many authors. The novelty of our current work is overcoming difficult technical issues that appear in (2) above. During this step we use techniques from thermodynamic formalism which we introduce through the use of Cannon’s coding: a finite direct graph that represents our hyperbolic group equipped with a finite generating set (see Definition
2.9
). Our first technical issue stems from the fact that it is not known whether it is alway possible to find a connected Cannon coding. This has been a recurring issue in the ergodic theory of hyperbolic/negatively curved groups. When studying counting results for word metrics, a beautiful argument of Calegari and Fujiwara
[
CF10
]
circumvents this issue by showing that there is consistent behaviour between different connected components in the Cannon graph (specifically the components that are in some sense ‘big’ for the word metric). This argument (and its variants) have been used by many authors
[
GTT20
]
,
[
Can21
]
,
[
CS23
]
,
[
GTT20
]
. Notably, this argument was used by Gouezël in
[
Gou14
]
to study the asymptotic growth rate of the transition probabilities for random walks on hyperbolic groups.

Unfortunately the Calegari - Fujiwara argument and its variants cannot be used in our current setting. This is because these arguments are not suitable for dealing with the large complex perturbations that appear in the study of our Poincaré series in (1). To overcome this issue we combine and then build upon arguments from
[
CT24a
]
and
[
Can23
]
. First we use a result from
[
CT24a
]
that shows that the Mineyev topological flow (a coarse geometric analogue of a geodesic flow) can be coded by the suspension flow over a subshift of finite type coming from the Cannon coding. We then combine this result with an argument from
[
Can23
]
to prove a precise complex version of Calegari-Fujiwara’s argument that compares the components in the Cannon graph that are ‘big’ for our counting metric
d
. This argument culminates in Lemma
2.17
which is the crucial technical result that allows us to carry out our analysis. We hope that this result will have other applications in the ergodic theoretic study of hyperbolic groups.

After obtaining Lemma
2.17
there is one final additional technical difficulty that we need to overcome: in our general setting we do not have access to strong Dolgopyat type bounds for appropriate transfer operators corresponding to our counting metric. To get around this we show that the assumption that
\Gamma
is not virtually free (or
d
has non-arithmetic length spectrum) implies strong enough spectral properties for our transfer operators (Proposition
2.19
) and that these are enough to deduce the central limit theorem.

Organisation

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we cover preliminary material related to hyperbolic groups and geometries. We also, in this section, state and prove the technical domain of analyticity result mentioned in
(2)
above. The proof of this result (which appears as Proposition
2.8
) will occupy most of the second section. In Section
3
we use the method of moments to deduce Theorem
1.3
and in the final section, we present some further applications of our results.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic groups and metrics

We only briefly introduce the required preliminary materials concerning hyperbolic groups and metrics here. See Section 2 of
[
CT24b
]
for a more detailed account.

Definition 2.1
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
\delta
-
hyperbolic
for some
\delta\geq 0
if

(x,y)_{w}\geq\min\left\{(x,z)_{w},(y,z)_{w}\right\}-\delta\quad\text{for all $x,y,z,w\in X$}

where
(x,y)_{w}
is the Gromov product:

(x,y)_{w}=\frac{1}{2}(d(w,x)+d(w,y)-d(x,y))\ \ \text{for $x,y,w\in X$}.

A metric space is called
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
.

A
hyperbolic group
is a finitely generated group
\Gamma
such that the Cayley graph
({\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S),|\cdot|_{S})
equipped with the word metric associated to a finite generating set
S
is a hyperbolic metric space. All hyperbolic groups will be assumed to be
non-elementary
, i.e. they do not contain a finite index cyclic subgroup. In the statement of
Theorem
1.1
and
Theorem
1.3
the assumption of
\Gamma
not being
virtually free
means that
\Gamma
does not contain a free group as a finite index subgroup.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that two metrics
d,d_{\ast}
on
\Gamma
are
quasi-isometric
if there exist constants
L>0
and
C\geq 0
such that

L^{-1}\,d(x,y)-C\leq d_{\ast}(x,y)\leq L\,d(x,y)+C\ \text{ for all $x,y\in\Gamma$}.

Throughout this work,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
will denote the set of metrics which are left-invariant, hyperbolic and quasi-isometric to some (equivalently, any) word metric in
\Gamma
.
Recall that for
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
we use the notation
v_{d}
for the exponential growth rate of
d
, i.e.

v_{d}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}\ \text{(which is necessarily strictly positive)}.

Furthermore, for any
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
there exists
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}e^{v_{d}T}\leq\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,x)<T\}\leq C_{2}e^{v_{d}T}\ \text{ for all $T>0$.}

We recall that two metrics
d,d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
have the property that there exists
\tau,C>0
such that
|\tau d(o,g)-d_{\ast}(o,g)|<C
for all
g\in\Gamma
then we say that
d,d_{\ast}
are
roughly similar
. We recall that
o\in\Gamma
is the identity element.
The translation length function of
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is defined as the limit

\ell_{d}[g]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,g^{n})}{n}

and is defined on conjugacy classes
[g]
in
\Gamma
. The
length spectrum
of
d
is the collection
\{\ell_{d}[g]:g\in\Gamma\}
and we say that
d
has
arithmetic length spectrum
if this set is contained in a lattice
a{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Otherwise we say that
d
has
non-arithmetic length spectrum
.

Fix a metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given an interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and constants
L,C>0
we say that a map
\gamma:I\to\Gamma
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-geodesic
if

L^{-1}\,|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq L\,|s-t|+C\ \text{ for all $s,t\in I$},

and a
C
-rough geodesic
if

|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq|s-t|+C\ \text{ for all $s,t\in I$}.

A

-rough geodesic is referred to as a geodesic.
A metric space
(\Gamma,d)
is called

-

if for each pair of elements

we can find a

-rough geodesic joining

to

. We say that

is

-roughly geodesic for some

.
It is known that every metric in

is roughly geodesic
[
BS00
]
.

Our main results are concerning the following class of metrics.

Definition 2.4
.

A hyperbolic metric
d
in
\Gamma
is called
strongly hyperbolic
if there exist positive constants
c,R_{0}>0
such that for all
R\geq R_{0}
, and all
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in\Gamma
,
if

d(x,y)-d(x,x^{\prime})+d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-d(y,y^{\prime})\geq R,

then

|d(x,y)-d(x^{\prime},y)-d(x,y^{\prime})+d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq e^{-cR}.

Remark 2.5
.

Examples of strongly hyperbolic metrics include:

(1) The Green metric associated to random walk on
\Gamma
as discussed in the introduction.

(2) The Mineyev hat metric introduced in
[
Min05
]
.

(3) The orbit metric coming from a properly discontinuous, cocompact, free and isometric action on a
\text{CAT}(-1)
metric space.

(4) Linear functions on the Cartan algebra associated to Anosov representations.

Hyperbolic groups can be compactified using their Gromov boundary
\partial\Gamma
which consists of equivalence classes
of divergent sequences. We fix a reference metric
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and say that sequence of group elements
\{g_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
diverges
if
(g_{n}|g_{m})_{o}
(computed with respect to
d
) diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity. Two divergent sequences
\{g_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{h_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are
equivalent
if
(g_{n}|h_{m})_{o}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity.
If
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is
C
-roughly geodesic then for each
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
there exists a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\Gamma

such that
\gamma(0)=o
and
\gamma(n)\to\xi
as
n\to\infty
.

The Busemann function
\beta_{w}(g,\xi)
associated to
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
based at

is given by

\beta_{w}(g,\xi)=\sup\left\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}d(g,\xi_{n})-d(w,\xi_{n}):\xi=\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\right\}.

When
d
is strongly hyperbolic

is obtained as a limit

\beta_{w}(g,\xi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(d(g,x_{n})-d(w,x_{n})\right)\quad\text{for $(g,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$},

where
\xi=\{g_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
and is continuous with respect to
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
.
Moreover in this case we have the cocycle identity

\beta_{w}(gh,\xi)=\beta_{w}(h,g^{-1}\xi)+\beta_{w}(gw,\xi)\quad\text{for $w,g,h\in\Gamma$ and $\gamma\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma$}.

Before moving on we recall the following results of the first author and Tanaka.
Recall that the
Manhattan curve

\theta_{d_{\ast}/d}
is defined as follows. For each

to be the abscissa of convergence of

Theorem 2.6

(Theorem 1 of
[

]
)
.

Let

be metrics. Then the limit

\tau(d_{\ast}/d):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}\frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)}{T}>0

exists and is called the

of

with respect to

(2)

We also recall one of the main results from

2.2.

Throughout the rest of this work we fix two strongly hyperbolic metrics

Standing assumptions:

We define the following function.

Definition 2.7

For

Proposition 2.8

For each

The following then hold depending on the parity of

When

To prove this result we will use tools from thermodynamic formalism.
Fix a finite generating set

.

Definition 2.9

Let

(1)

denote the map that sends a finite path to the endpoint of the corresponding path in

(2)

Using

. We will use this notation for Birkhoff sums throughout this work.

Using the same notation as before, let

(The Variational Principle, Theorem 3.5

For

The pressures can be related to the spectral radii of the following linear operators, known as
transfer operators

\Psi\in F_{\theta}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})

e^{2\pi il/p_{\mathcal{C}}}e^{{\rm P}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Psi)}

-maximal components agree.

Proof.

to

p\geq 1
.
We define

\displaystyle G_{1}(s)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\left((d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{2p}+d(o,g)^{p})\right)e^{-sd(o,g)}

\displaystyle G_{2}(s)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\left((d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}+d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2}e^{-sd(o,g)}

\displaystyle G_{3}(s)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}e^{-sd(o,g)}.

Note that
G_{2}=G_{1}+2G_{3}
and that these three series are convergent in
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
.
We also have that

G_{1}(s)=\eta_{2p}(s)-\eta_{0}^{(p)}(s)=\frac{g(s)}{(s-1)^{1+p}}+f(s)

where
g(s),f(s)
are analytic in
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\geq 1
and
g(1)
is a positive real number. Here the power of
p
in parenthesise (in
\eta_{0}^{(p)}
) represents taking the
p
th derivative. We will use this notation throughout the rest of the paper.
It follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{2p}+d(o,g)^{p}\sim\frac{g(1)T^{p}}{p!}

as
T\to\infty
.

We now want to show that
G_{2}
has a pole with the order poles at
s=1
. We begin by calculating

\eta_{p}^{\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}(s)=(-1)^{\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}d(o,g)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}e^{-sd(o,g)}.

Then using the identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-tx}\ dt=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{x}}

for
x>0
we can write

\displaystyle G_{3}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}d(o,g)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}e^{-sd(o,g)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-td(o,g)}\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{p}^{\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}(s+t)}{\sqrt{t}}\ dt.

It follows that
G_{3}
is analytic on
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\geq 1
except for a pole at
s=1
. We also know that
\eta_{p}^{\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}
has a pole of order at most
p+1
at
s=1
. It follows that locally to
s=1
we can write

\eta_{p}^{\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}\frac{a_{j}}{(s-1)^{j}}+h(s)

where
h(s)
is analytic and the
a_{1},\ldots,a_{p+1}
are complex numbers (some of which could be
0
).
Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(s+t-1)^{j}\sqrt{t}}\ dt=\frac{\pi(2j-2)!}{2^{2j-1}((j-1)!)^{2}}\,\frac{1}{(s-1)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}

it follows that in a neighbourhood of
s=1

G_{3}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}\frac{c_{j}}{(s-1)^{j-\frac{1}{2}}}+l(s)

for some analytic function
l
and constants
c_{1},\ldots,c_{p+1}
. Then using the identity
G_{2}=G_{1}+2G_{3}
we see that

G_{2}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}\frac{a_{j}}{(s-1)^{j}}+\frac{b_{j}}{(s-1)^{j-\frac{1}{2}}}+r(s)

where
a_{j},b_{j},r
are analytic maps on
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Furthermore
a_{p+1}(1)=g(1)
(where
g
is the function from our expression for
G_{1}
). We can then apply Proposition
3.1
to
G_{2}
to deduce that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}\left((d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}+d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2}\sim\frac{g(1)T^{p}}{p!}

as
T\to\infty
. It then follows that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}=o(T^{p})

as
T\to\infty
.
To conclude the proof we need to show that the same estimate holds when we replace
d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}
with
T^{\frac{p}{2}}
, i.e. we want to show that
\pi_{p}(T)=o(T^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{T})
.
To do so, note that

(9)

\sum_{d(o,g)<T}d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}=\int_{0}^{T}t^{\frac{p}{2}}\ d\pi_{p}(t)=T^{\frac{p}{2}}\pi_{p}(T)-\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{T}t^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\pi_{p}(t)\ dt.

This follows from a standard integration by-parts formula for the Stiltjes integral.
Now using the fact that
|\pi_{p}(T)|\leq\pi_{p-1}(T)+\pi_{p+1}(T)=O(T^{\frac{p+1}{2}}e^{T})
(where we have used that
p-1
and
p+1
are even and so our estimates on the even moments apply) it is a simple calculation to show that

\left|\int_{0}^{T}t^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\pi_{p}(t)\ dt\right|=O\left(\int_{0}^{T}t^{p-\frac{1}{2}}e^{t}\ dt\right)=o(T^{p}e^{T})

as
T\to\infty
.
Substituting this into
(\ref{eq.pi})
shows that

\pi_{p}(T)=T^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{d(o,g)<T}d(o,g)^{\frac{p}{2}}(d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g))^{p}+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{T}t^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\pi_{p}(t)\ dt\right)=o(T^{p}e^{T})

as
T\to\infty
, as required.
∎

We have shown the following.

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\sum_{d(o,g)<T}\left(\frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)^{p}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if $p$ is odd}\\
\sigma^{p}(p-1)!!&\quad\text{if $p$ is even.}\end{cases}

We are now ready to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

As mentioned, we would like to apply the method of moments
[
Bil11
, Theorem 30.2]
. Considering the moments above along the integers
n\geq 1
we deduce that for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<n\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<n\ \text{ and }\ \frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g)}{\sqrt{n}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
n\to\infty
(in the discrete sense). Now note that there exists
M>0
such that when
T\in[n,n+1)
and
d(o,x)<T
,
|d_{\ast}(o,x)-d(o,x)|\leq M(n+1)
and so for each
n\geq 1
and so there exists
R>0
such that when
T\in[n,n+1)

\left|\frac{d_{\ast}(o,x)-d(o,x)}{\sqrt{T}}-\frac{d_{\ast}(o,x)-d(o,x)}{\sqrt{n}}\right|\leq\frac{R}{\sqrt{n}}

for all
n\geq 1
. Writing

\mathrm{C}(T,t)=\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{d_{\ast}(o,g)-d(o,g)}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}

we therefore have that
\mathrm{C}(n,t)\leq\mathrm{C}(T,t)\leq\mathrm{C}(n,t+Rn^{-1/2})
for each
n\geq 1
,
T\in[n,n+1)
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It follows that for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathrm{C}(T,t)
tends to
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)
as
T\to\infty
(in the continuous sense).

This is almost the statement of the central limit theorem in Theorem
1.3
except we would like
d_{\ast}(o,x)-d(o,x)
replaced with
d_{\ast}(o,x)-T
. However, this follows easily as we have that

\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,x)<T\text{ and }|d(o,x)-T|>T^{1/3}\}=o(\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,x)<T\})

as
T\to\infty
.
∎

4.
Further applications and examples

In this last section we include some further applications of our results and methods. In particular we look at central limit theorems for real valued potentials (other than strongly hyperbolic metrics) on
\Gamma
when we order elements by their length with respect to a strongly hyperbolic metric. They key observation is that our arguments can be applied to potentials
\varphi:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
that satisfies the following properties:

(i)

there exists a finite, symmetric generating set
S
for
\Gamma
and constant
C>0
such that for any
g\in\Gamma
and
s\in S
both
|\varphi(gs)-\varphi(g)|<C
and
|\varphi(sg)-\varphi(g)|<C
; and,

(ii)

there exists a Cannon coding (for some generating set) with corresponding shift space
\Sigma
and a potential
\Psi_{\varphi}
such that for
x\in\Sigma
with
x_{0}=\ast
,
S_{n}\Psi_{\varphi}(x)=\varphi({\bf ev}_{n}(x))
.

Once we have a function
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies these conditions, our arguments apply and we can deduce a central limit theorem for
\varphi
. This can be easily be verified from following our proof of Theorem
1.3
. The non-degeneracy criteria then becomes the following. The central limit theorem comparing
\varphi
and a strongly hyperbolic metric
d
is degenerate if and only if there exists
C>0,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|\varphi(g)-\tau d(o,g)|<C
for all
g\in\Gamma
. We now discuss various examples of
\varphi
satisfying the above conditions.

4.1.
Word metrics and bi-combable functions

As mentioned in the introduction, there has been much interest in counting central limit theorems when group elements are ordered by a word metric. Our methods allow us to prove central limit theorems for word metrics but when the ordering is done with respect to a different metric.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be non-elementary, non-virtually free hyperbolic group and
d
a strongly hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. Fix a word metric
|\cdot|_{S}
on
\Gamma
associated to a finite generating set
S
. Then there exists
\tau>v_{d}/v_{S}
and
\sigma^{2}>0
such that for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{|g|_{S}-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
.

Here
v_{S}
denotes the exponential growth rate of the word distance
d_{S}
associated to
S
. Note that here we know that
\sigma^{2}
, the variance, is strictly positive.

Proof.

We use a Cannon coding for the word metric
S
in the theorem. Then, the constant potential with value
1
on this coding represents the word metric
|\cdot|_{S}
in the sense of
(ii)
above.
It is also clear that
(i)
is satisfied for the map
g\mapsto|g|_{S}
. We therefore have the required properties needed to follow the same proof used to show Theorem
1.3
. To conclude the proof we need to know that
d
and
d_{S}
are never roughly similar to guarantee that
\tau>v_{d}/v_{S}
and that
\sigma^{2}>0
. This follows from and argument of Gouëzel, Matheus and Maucorant
[
GMM18
]
which shows that
d
can not have arithmetic length spectrum (where as it is known that
d_{S}
necessarily does have arithmetic length spectrum). See
[
Can23
, Proposition 1.13]
for further explanation.
∎

In fact we can apply our result to the class of bi-combable functions introduced by Calegari and Fujiwara in
[
CF10
]
(which include word metrics). These functions satisfy
(i)
and
(ii)
above (by definition). An interesting example of bi-combable functions are Brook’s counting quasi-morphisms. Our work applies and we deduce counting central limit theorems comparing bi-combable functions with strongly hyperbolic metrics. Such central limit theorems are always non-degenerate.

4.2.
Quasi-Fuchsian representations

For our final example we compare two distances on a surface group
\Gamma
, i.e.
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed, negatively curved Riemannian surface
(V,\mathfrak{g})
. Given such a group
\Gamma
we can lift the metric
\mathfrak{g}
on the surface to obtain a metric
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\Gamma
. More precisely, if
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
is the universal cover of
(V,\mathfrak{g})
and we fix a base point
o\in\widetilde{V}
then we can define

d_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,h)=d_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}(g\cdot o,h\cdot o)\ \text{ for $g,h\in\Gamma$.}

We can also take a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\simeq\text{Isom}({\mathbb{H}}^{3})
corresponding to convex cocompact isometric action by
\Gamma
on
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. From such a representation we obtain a metric
d_{\rho}
on
\Gamma
by lifting as we did for
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We then have the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a surface group and that
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
d_{\rho}
are as above. Then, there exists
\tau>0
,
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
such that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d_{\mathfrak{g}}(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d_{\mathfrak{g}}(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{d_{\rho}(o,g)-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
. Furthermore,
\sigma^{2}=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
corresponds to a point in Teichmüller space
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}:\Gamma\to{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(i.e.
\mathfrak{g}
has constant negative curvature) and
\rho
is a conjugate representations in
{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, i.e. there exists
M\in{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
\rho(g)=M^{-1}\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(g)M
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Both metrics
d_{\rho}
and
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
are strongly hyperbolic and so Theorem
1.3
applies and the central limit theorem follows. The non-degeneracy equivalence statement follows from a result of Fricker and Furman
[
FF22
, Theorem A]
.
∎

In the above theorem we could swap the roles of
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
d_{\rho}
to obtain a central limit theorem with possibly different mean and variance.

4.3.
Translation distance functions

To finish this work we briefly discuss versions of Theorem
1.3
for translation distance functions. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
be strongly hyperbolic. Then we have that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T,\text{ and }|d(o,g)-\ell_{d}(g)|>T^{1/3}\}\to 0

as
T\to\infty
.

Proof.

Consider the set of elements

\mathcal{U}(T)=\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T,\text{ and }|d(o,g)-\ell_{d}(g)|>T^{1/3}\}.

Now there exists
C>0
such that given
x\in\mathcal{U}(T)
we can find, by
[
CT24b
, Lemma 3.2]
,
g^{\prime}\in[g]
such that
|d(o,g^{\prime})-\ell_{d}(g^{\prime})|=|d(o,g^{\prime})-\ell_{d}(g)|\leq C
. Then we have that
d(o,g^{\prime})\leq\ell_{d}(g)+C\leq T-T^{1/3}
. Hyperbolicity guarantees that the association
g\mapsto g^{\prime}
is at most
K(d(o,g)+1)
to
1
for some
K>0
independent of
g
(i.e it is at most
K(T+1)
to
1
for
g\in\mathcal{U}(T)
). Hence we have that

\#\mathcal{U}(T)\leq K\,(T+1)\#\{g^{\prime}\in\Gamma:d(o,g^{\prime})<T-T^{1/3}\}=O\left(Te^{v_{d}(T-T^{1/3})}\right)

as
T\to\infty
and the result follows.
∎

As a corollary of this lemma and Theorem
1.3
we deduce the following result. Recall that, given
d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and
g\in\Gamma
,
\ell_{d_{\ast}}[g]
is stable translation length of (the conjugacy class containing)
g
with respect to
d_{\ast}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group and take two strongly hyperbolic metrics
d,d_{\ast}
on
\Gamma
. Suppose either that
\Gamma
is not virtually free or that
\Gamma
is virtually free and that
d
has non-arithmetic length spectrum. Then there exist
\tau=\tau(d_{\ast}/d)>0
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)\geq 0
such that

\frac{1}{\#\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\}}\#\left\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,g)<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\ell_{d_{\ast}}[g]-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}\to\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}(t)

as
T\to\infty
.
Furthermore,
\sigma^{2}(d_{\ast}/d)=0
if and only if
d
and
d_{\ast}
are roughly similar.

Given this result it is natural to ask whether we can also replace
d
with
\ell_{d}
and count over conjugacy classes opposed to group elements. We are unsure how to approach this problem and so end the paper with the following question.

Question 4.5
.

Let
d,d_{\ast}
be strongly hyperbolic metrics on a non-elementary hyperbolic group
\Gamma
. Does a central limit theorem hold that compares
\ell_{d}
and
\ell_{d_{\ast}}
on conjugacy classes. That is, does

\frac{1}{\#\{[g]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma):\ell_{d}[g]<T\}}\#\left\{[g]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma):\ell_{d}[g]<T\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\ell_{d_{\ast}}[g]-\tau T}{\sqrt{T}}\leq t\right\}

converge to a normal distribution as
T\to\infty
?

Homological mirror symmetry for Milnor fibers via moduli of A ∞ A_{\infty} -structures
Yankı Lekili, Kazushi Ueda

Abstract
. We show that the base spaces of the semiuniversal unfoldings of some weighted homogeneous singularities can be identified with moduli spaces of A ∞ A_{\infty} -structures on the trivial extension algebras of the endomorphism algebras of the tilting objects. The same algebras also appear in the Fukaya categories of their mirrors. Based on these identifications, we discuss applications to homological mirror symmetry for Milnor fibers, and give a proof of homological mirror symmetry for an n n -dimensional affine hypersurface of degree n + 2 n+2 and the double cover of the n n -dimensional affine space branched along a degree 2 ​ n + 2 2n+2 hypersurface. Along the way, we also give a proof of a conjecture of Seidel from [ 71 ] which may be of independent interest.

1.1.
Moduli of elliptic curves

Our basic starting point is an algebraic variety with an isolated singularity

\displaystyle\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}{\bf w}(x,y)\coloneqq x^{3}+y^{2}=0\right\}.

The cuspidal curve (

Each curve

The

-action extends to the compactified family by

\displaystyle t\cdot([x:y:z];u_{4},u_{6})

\displaystyle=([t^{2}x:t^{3}y:z];t^{4}u_{4},t^{6}u_{6})

(1.6)

\displaystyle=([x:y:t^{-1}z];t^{4}u_{4},t^{6}u_{6}),

(1.7)

which preserves the section

\displaystyle t^{*}(\Omega_{t\cdot u})

\displaystyle=t^{-1}\Omega_{u}.

(1.8)

The curves

\displaystyle\Delta\coloneqq\left\{(u_{4},u_{6})\in U\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}4u_{4}^{3}-27u_{6}^{2}=0\right\}.

If

The base space

Furthermore,

(1.10)

with the moduli stack of stable curves

1.3.
Application to homological mirror symmetry

Let
\check{V}
be a once-punctured torus
viewed as a Weinstein manifold,
and

\displaystyle Z\coloneqq\left\{[x:y:z]\in\mathbb{P}(2,3,1)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}x^{3}+y^{2}+xyz=0\right\}

(1.18)

be a rational curve with a single ordinary double point.
Theorem
1.1
was obtained in
[
48
]

as a tool for proving a quasi-equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\check{V}\right)\simeq\operatorname{perf}Z

(1.19)

of pretriangulated
A_{\infty}
-categories over
\mathbb{Z}

of the split-closed derived Fukaya category
of compact exact Lagrangians in
\check{V}

and the perfect derived category of
Z
.
The strategy is first to identify generators on both sides,
and then match their endomorphism algebras as
A_{\infty}
-algebras.
It is often difficult to explicitly compute such
A_{\infty}
-algebras,
but even if one does,
finding a quasi-isomorphism between two different chain models is usually a hard task.
The computation of cohomology level structures (and matching them) is much easier, and knowing the moduli of
A_{\infty}
-structures allows one to appeal to indirect methods to conclude the proof of the existence of a chain level isomorphism. Such a strategy was applied also for proving homological mirror symmetry in a number of other cases in dimension one. Namely, in
[
49
]
a class of curve singularities
C_{1,n}
for
n\geq 1
were considered, where
C_{1,1}
is the cuspidal curve,
C_{1,2}
is tacnodal curve given by the equation
y^{2}=yx^{2}
, and
C_{1,n}
is the elliptic
n
-fold singularity given by
n
lines in
\mathbb{A}^{n-1}
. These are all the Gorenstein singularities of arithmetic genus one
[
76
, Appendix A]
. Carrying out the above strategy has led to a proof of homological mirror symmetry for
n
-punctured tori
[
50
]
.

The equivalence (
1.19
) is an instance
of homological mirror symmetry at the large volume limit.
The equivalence is known to extend to a formal neighborhood of this limit
to give an equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\check{Y}\right)\simeq\operatorname{perf}\hat{\mathcal{Y}}

(1.20)

over
\mathbb{Z}\left[\kern-1.66669pt\hbox{$\left[\hbox{$q$}\right]$}\kern-1.66669pt\right]

where
\check{Y}
is the compactification of
\check{V}
and

\hat{\mathcal{Y}}
is the Tate elliptic curve,
a formal neighborhood of the nodal curve
Z

(see
[
48
]
for a proof).
A general strategy for proving homological mirror symmetry as in (
1.20
)
introduced in
[
71
]

is to view the categories in (
1.20
)
as deformations of the categories given in (
1.19
).
Hence,
in this context
deducing homological mirror symmetry for the compact manifold
\check{Y}

from homological mirror symmetry for the Weinstein manifold
\check{V}

ultimately reduces to a problem in deformation theory.

1.4.
New results and a general conjectural picture

In this paper, we lay out a program that aims to extend the above results to
higher dimensions, leading to new homological mirror symmetry conjectures for
higher-dimensional Calabi–Yau manifolds at the large volume limit and in its
formal neighborhood. It is based on the relation between homological mirror
symmetry for Calabi–Yau manifolds and homological mirror symmetry for
singularities, which goes back to
[
44
,
58
,
81
]
.

A weighted homogeneous polynomial

{\bf w}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

with an isolated critical point at the origin
is
invertible

if there is an integer matrix

A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}

with non-zero determinant
such that

\displaystyle{\bf w}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{ij}}.

(1.21)

The corresponding
weight system

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n};h)

satisfying
\mathrm{gcd}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},h)=1
is determined uniquely.
(See the beginning of Section
2

of a weight system.)

The
transpose
of
{\bf w}
is defined in
[
10
]
as

\displaystyle\check{{\bf w}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{ji}},

(1.22)

whose exponent matrix
\check{A}

is the transpose matrix of
A
.
We write

\left(\check{d}_{1},\check{d}_{2},\ldots,\check{d}_{n};\check{h}\right)

for the weight system associated with
\check{{\bf w}}
.

The group

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\bf w}\coloneqq\left\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}t_{1}^{a_{11}}\cdots t_{n}^{a_{1n}}=\cdots=t_{1}^{a_{n1}}\cdots t_{n}^{a_{nn}}\right\}

acts naturally on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.
One has a homomorphism

\phi\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\Gamma_{{\bf w}}

sending

to

\left(t^{d_{1}},\ldots,t^{d_{n}}\right)\in\Gamma_{{\bf w}}.

Let

\operatorname{mf}\left(\left[\mathbb{A}^{n}\middle/\Gamma_{{\bf w}}\right],{\bf w}\right)

-equivariant matrix factorizations

For any invertible polynomial

Conjecture 1.5

Let
\check{X}

The generator

used in the proof of Theorem

of the Lefschetz fibration

which is also an object of

[

of the Yoneda algebra

The generator used by Seidel in

is the direct sum of the vanishing cycles
of the Lefschetz fibration

whose fan polytope is polar dual to that of

The generator used by Sheridan in
[
72
]

is the cover of an immersed Lagrangian sphere
in a pair of pants,
which is shown to be the direct sum of vanishing cycles
of the Lefschetz fibration

\check{{\bf w}}^{\prime}\coloneqq(\check{{\bf w}}+1)/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\colon(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\to\mathbb{C}

in
[
62
]

One has

\check{D}\coloneqq\check{V}\setminus(\check{{\bf w}}^{\prime})^{-1}(0)=\check{D}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\check{D}_{n}

where

\check{D}_{i}\coloneqq\check{V}\cap\{x_{i}=0\}.

Let

\mathcal{F}(\check{V},\check{D})

be the relative Fukaya category,
which is an
A_{\infty}
-category
over
\mathbb{C}[q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}]

whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds
of
\check{V}\setminus\check{D}

and compositions are counted
with intersection numbers with
\check{D}_{i}
.
Since
\check{V}
is Stein,
the definition of
\mathcal{F}(\check{V},\check{D})

involves only the classical theory of pseudo-holomorphic maps,
and the coefficient ring is a polynomial ring.
The argument of Seidel
and Sheridan
shows that
the idempotent-complete pretriangulated
A_{\infty}
-category
generated by the full subcategory of

\mathcal{F}(\check{V},\check{D})

consisting of the cover of the immersed Lagrangian sphere
is equivalent to

\operatorname{perf}\left[\mathcal{Z}/K\right]

\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb{C}[q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}][x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(q_{1}x_{1}^{n+1}+\cdots+q_{n}x_{n}^{n+1}+x_{0}\cdots x_{n}).

This suggests generalizations of Conjecture
1.4

to more general partial compactifications
of covers of a pair of pants.

Even if one’s goal is to prove homological mirror symmetry
for a compact Calabi–Yau manifold
over the Novikov field,
it is useful
not to go directly
from a cover of a pair of pants
to the compact Calabi–Yau,
but to divide it into two steps,
first to the Milnor fiber
and then to the compact Calabi–Yau:
The Fukaya category of a cover of a pair of pants
has many deformations,
but it is easy to control the deformation
to the Milnor fiber,
essentially because the Milnor fiber is Stein
and the deformation is locally constant
along a stratification of the base space.
Once one comes to the Milnor fiber,
and take the direct sum of vanishing cycles
as a generator,
then we can understand
not only formal deformations
but the global moduli space of
A_{\infty}
-structures.
It is an interesting problem
to obtain the same level of understanding
for deformations of the Fukaya category
of a cover of a pair of pants,
which would have non-smoothing components in general.

A
weight system
is a sequence

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n};h)

of positive integers satisfying

\displaystyle h>\max\left\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\right\}.

(2.1)

We will always assume

\displaystyle\gcd(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},h)=1

(2.2)

in this paper.
Let
{\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial in
n
variables,
which is weighted homogeneous of weight
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n};h)
;

\displaystyle{\bf w}\left(t^{d_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,t^{d_{n}}x_{n}\right)=t^{h}{\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\quad t\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}.

(2.3)

It is written as the sum of monomials

\displaystyle{\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in I_{\bf w}}c_{\boldsymbol{i}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}},\quad c_{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},

(2.4)

where the index set
I_{\bf w}
is a subset
of the set of non-negative integers satisfying

\displaystyle d_{1}i_{1}+d_{2}i_{2}+\cdots+d_{n}i_{n}=h.

(2.5)

We will always assume that
{\bf w}
determines
the weight system satisfying (
2.2
) uniquely.

Let
\Gamma_{\bf w}
be the commutative algebraic group defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\bf w}\coloneqq\left\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n+1}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}t_{1}^{i_{1}}t_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{i_{n}}=t_{n+1}\text{\ for all\ }(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in I_{\bf w}\right\}.

(2.6)

The group

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\bf w}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma_{\bf w},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})

of characters of
\Gamma_{\bf w}
is written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}_{\bf w}=\left.\mathbb{Z}\chi_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\chi_{n+1}\middle/\left(i_{1}\chi_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}\chi_{n}-\chi_{n+1}\right)_{\boldsymbol{i}\in I_{\bf w}}\right.,

(2.7)

where
\chi_{i}\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{\bf w}
for
1\leq i\leq n+1
is defined by
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\mapsto t_{i}
.
Since the composition
\Gamma_{\bf w}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}

with the first projection is injective,
we will think of
\Gamma_{\bf w}
as a subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
,
and set
\chi_{\bf w}\coloneqq\chi_{n+1}
.
The group
\Gamma_{\bf w}
consists of diagonal transformations of

which keeps
{\bf w}
semi-invariant;

\displaystyle{\bf w}(t\cdot(x_{1},\ldots x_{n}))=\chi_{\bf w}(t){\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\quad t\in\Gamma_{{\bf w}}.

(2.8)

The injective homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

\displaystyle\to\Gamma_{\bf w},\quad t\mapsto\left(t^{d_{1}},\ldots,t^{d_{n}}\right)

fits into the exact sequence

\displaystyle 1\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\xrightarrow{\phi}\Gamma_{\bf w}\to\operatorname{ker}\chi_{\bf w}/\langle j_{\bf w}\rangle\to 1,

(2.10)

where

j_{\bf w}\coloneqq\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}d_{1}/h},\ldots,e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}d_{n}/h}\right)

is the
grading element

generating the cyclic group

\operatorname{ker}\chi_{\bf w}\cap\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})

of order

Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
\Gamma_{\bf w}

containing
\phi({\mathbb{G}}_{m})
as a subgroup of finite index.
For such

,
the kernel of

containing the grading element

The group

We let

\chi_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\chi_{n}

\displaystyle\chi_{0}\coloneqq\chi-\chi_{1}-\cdots-\chi_{n}.

By abuse of notation,

to

and set

3.
Hochschild cohomology via matrix factorizations

The Hochschild cohomology of a scheme
Y

(or more generally a perfect derived stack
[
8
]
)
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{*}(Y)\coloneqq\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{Y\times Y}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\right),

(3.1)

where

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\coloneqq\Delta_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}

and

\Delta\colon Y\to Y\times Y

is the diagonal embedding.
The right hand side of (
3.1
) is isomorphic to the endomorphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{Qcoh}Y)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{L}}(\operatorname{Qcoh}Y,\operatorname{Qcoh}Y)}\left(\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}Y},\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}Y}\right)

(3.2)

of the identity in the
\infty
-category of colimit-preserving endofunctors of
\operatorname{Qcoh}Y

[
79
,
8
]
.

When
Y
is a smooth variety over
\mathbf{k}

(see
[
3
]
for a partial extension to positive characteristics),
one can compute the Hochschild cohomology by appealing to Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg isomorphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{n}(Y)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H^{p}\left(Y,\Lambda^{q}T_{Y}\right).

(3.3)

However,
our main interest is in the case when
Y
is a singular stack.
A generalization of the above decomposition to singular varieties
is given by Buchweitz–Flenner
[
14
]
which states

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{n}(Y)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\operatorname{Ext}^{p}\left(\wedge^{q}\mathbb{L}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y}\right)

(3.4)

where
\mathbb{L}_{Y}
is the cotangent complex over
\mathbf{k}
and

\wedge^{q}
is the derived exterior product.
However,
it is not always straightforward to compute with this,
even when
Y
is a variety.
We will instead use another strategy which uses the function
{\bf w}
more directly.

Let

S\coloneqq\operatorname{Sym}V

be the symmetric algebra
over the vector space

V\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}

of dimension
n+1
, and

\mathbb{A}^{n+1}=\operatorname{Spec}S

be the affine space.
Let further
\Gamma
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

acting linearly on
V
,

\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})
be a character of
\Gamma
,
and

\mathbf{W}\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}_{[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}/\Gamma]}(\chi)\right)\cong(S\otimes\chi)^{\Gamma}

be a non-zero element of weight
\chi
.
The quotient ring
R\coloneqq S/(\mathbf{W})
inherits a
\Gamma
-action.

When
\chi
is isomorphic to the top exterior power of the dual
V^{\vee}

as a
\Gamma
-module,
the bounded derived category
\operatorname{coh}\EuScript{Y}

of coherent sheaves on the quotient stack

\EuScript{Y}\coloneqq\left[\left(\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus{\boldsymbol{0}}\right)\middle/\Gamma\right]

is quasi-equivalent to the idempotent-complete dg category

\operatorname{mf}\left(\left[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\middle/\Gamma\right],\mathbf{W}\right)

of
\Gamma
-equivariant matrix factorizations;

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}\EuScript{Y}\cong\operatorname{mf}\left(\left[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\middle/\Gamma\right],\mathbf{W}\right).

(3.5)

This is first proved by Orlov

[
63
, Theorem 40]

when
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

in the context of triangulated categories. The generalization to a finite extension of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is straightforward.
The quasi-equivalence of dg categories can be found in
[
6
,
15
,
40
,
74
]
.
Note also that by
[
63
, Theorem 39]
,

\operatorname{mf}\left(\left[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\middle/\Gamma\right],\mathbf{W}\right)

is equivalent to the bounded stable derived category of the graded ring
R
, denoted by
D^{b}_{{\mathrm{sing}}}(\operatorname{gr}R)
.
The equivalence (
3.5
) implies the isomorphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\EuScript{Y})\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\mathbb{A}^{n+1},\Gamma,\mathbf{W}),

(3.6)

where the right hand side is the Hochschild cohomology of the dg category

\operatorname{mf}\left(\left[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\middle/\Gamma\right],\mathbf{W}\right),

which can be computed as follows:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
20
,
15
,
67
,
6
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abelian finite extension of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

acting linearly on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}=\operatorname{Spec}S
, and

\mathbf{W}\in S
be a non-zero element of degree

\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}).

Assume that the singular locus of the zero set

Z_{(-\mathbf{W})\boxplus\mathbf{W}}
of the Sebastiani–Thom sum
(-\mathbf{W})\boxplus\mathbf{W}

is contained in the product of the zero sets
Z_{\mathbf{W}}\times Z_{\mathbf{W}}
.
Then

\operatorname{HH}^{t}\left(\mathbb{A}^{n+1},\Gamma,\mathbf{W}\right)

is isomorphic to

\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\chi,\ l\geq 0\\
t-\dim N_{\gamma}=2u\end{subarray}}H^{-2l}(d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma})\otimes\chi^{\otimes(u+l)}\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right.\\
\left.\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\chi,\ l\geq 0\\
t-\dim N_{\gamma}=2u+1\end{subarray}}H^{-2l-1}(d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma})\otimes\chi^{\otimes(u+l+1)}\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right)^{\Gamma}.

(3.7)

Here
H^{i}(d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma})
is the
i
-th cohomology of the Koszul complex

\displaystyle C^{*}(d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma})\coloneqq\left\{\cdots\to\Lambda^{2}V_{\gamma}^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{\otimes(-2)}\otimes S_{\gamma}\to V_{\gamma}^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{\vee}\otimes S_{\gamma}\to S_{\gamma}\right\},

(3.8)

where the rightmost term
S_{\gamma}
sits in cohomological degree 0, and
the differential is the contraction with

\displaystyle d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}\in\left(V_{\gamma}\otimes\chi\otimes S_{\gamma}\right)^{\Gamma}.

(3.9)

The vector space
V_{\gamma}
is the subspace of
\gamma
-invariant elements in
V
,

S_{\gamma}
is the symmetric algebra of
V_{\gamma}
,

\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}
is the restriction of
\mathbf{W}
to
\operatorname{Spec}S_{\gamma}
, and

N_{\gamma}
is the complement of
V_{\gamma}
in
V

so that

V\cong V_{\gamma}\oplus N_{\gamma}

as a
\Gamma
-module.
The vector space on the right hand side of (
3.9
)
is the degree
\chi
part of the space

\Omega_{S_{\gamma}}\cong V_{\gamma}\otimes S_{\gamma}

of Kähler differentials of
S_{\gamma}
,
and
d\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}
is
the exterior derivative
of the polynomial
\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}
.
The zero-th cohomology of the Koszul complex (
3.8
)
is isomorphic to the Jacobi algebra
\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}}
.
If
\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}
has an isolated critical point at the origin,
then the cohomology of (
3.8
)
is concentrated in degree 0,
so that only the summand

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}}\otimes\chi^{\otimes u}\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right)^{\Gamma}

(3.10)

with
l=0
contributes in (
3.7
).

The formula (
3.7
) is an adaptation of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
,
to which we refer the reader for a proof.
The slight difference between
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
and (
3.7
)
comes from the convention for the Koszul complex;
the latter
is convenient in that
when
V
has an additional
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action,
(
3.7
) is equivariant with respect to it.

If the
\Gamma
-action on
V
satisfies

\dim\left(S\otimes\rho\right)^{\Gamma}<\infty

for any
\rho\in\widehat{\Gamma}
,
then one has

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{HH}^{t}(\mathbb{A}^{n+1},\mathbf{W},\Gamma)<\infty

(3.11)

for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
since the Koszul complex (
3.8
) is bounded,
the group
\operatorname{ker}\chi
is finite,
each direct summand in (
3.7
)
is finite-dimensional,
and there are only finitely many
u

contributing to a fixed
t
.

We compute the Hochschild cohomologies
in several classes of examples
in Sections
3.1
–
3.7
below.
The results in Sections
3.2
–
3.5

give examples
where the condition (4) in Theorem
1.6
is satisfied,
and the results in
3.6
and
3.7
are used

1.7

given in Section

.

(6.43)

Assumption (

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\check{V}
be the Milnor fiber
of a weighted homogeneous isolated hypersurface singularity.
If
\check{d}_{0}
is positive (resp. negative),
then for any
K,L\in\mathcal{W}\left(\check{V}\right)
,
the complex
\hom(K,L)
is bounded below (resp. above).

Proof.

By applying a small Hamiltonian isotopy to
K
and
L

if necessary,
one may assume that
a basis of
\hom(K,L)
consists of intersection points
in the interior of the Liouville domain
and Hamiltonian chords in the symplectization end.
The former is finite and hence their Maslov indices are bounded.
The latter correspond bijectively
to Reeb chords between Legendrians on the contact boundary.
The contact boundary can be identified
with the link of the weighted homogeneous singularity
in such a way that
the Reeb flow on the link is the circle action
acting on the coordinates with weights
(\check{d}_{1},\check{d}_{2},\ldots,\check{d}_{n})

(see
[
70
, 4.c]
).
The Reeb flow is periodic;
the time one Reeb flow is the identity,
corresponding to going around the
S^{1}
once.
We say a Reeb chord is
short
(resp.
long
)
if the length is less than or equal to (resp. greater than) one.
Because the Reeb flow is periodic, every long Reeb chord is a concatenation
of a short Reeb chord and a Reeb orbit.
The set of Reeb chords form non-degenerate Morse–Bott components, and
only finitely many components consists of short chords.
Any component consisting of long chords is obtained from
a component consisting of short chords
by concatenating Reeb orbits.
In
[
70
, Lemma 4.15]
,
the index cost of going around the circle once was computed to be
2\check{d}_{0}
.
Since
\check{d}_{0}\neq 0
by assumption,
additivity of Maslov index implies that the complex
\hom(K,L)
is bounded below (resp. above)
if
\check{d}_{0}
is positive (resp. negative).
∎

7.
Symplectic cohomology of the Milnor fiber

In this section,
we recall a spectral sequence converging to
\operatorname{SH}^{*}\left(\check{V}\right)

associated to a normal crossings compactification of
\check{V}

due to
[
59
,
35
]
.
It is based on a standard model of the Reeb flow
in a neighborhood of compactification divisor and can be perceived
as an elaborate version of the standard Morse-Bott model
discussed in
[
69
]
when the compactification divisor is smooth.
See also
[
34
]
and
[
17
]
for related results.

Let
\tilde{Y}
be a smooth projective variety
containing an affine variety with
c_{1}\left(\check{V}\right)=0

in such a way that

\check{D}\coloneqq\tilde{Y}\setminus\check{V}

is a normal crossing divisor;

\displaystyle\check{D}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\check{D}_{i}.

(7.1)

For
J\subset I
, we set
\check{D}_{J}=\bigcap_{i\in J}\check{D}_{i}
, and also set
\check{D}_{\emptyset}=\check{V}
.

Choose a sequence
\kappa=(\kappa_{i})_{i\in I}
of positive integers
such that the divisor

\sum_{i\in I}\kappa_{i}\check{D}_{i}

on
\tilde{Y}
is ample.
Let
(c_{i})_{i\in I}
be another sequence of integers
such that

\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\check{D}_{i}

is linearly equivalent to the canonical divisor of
\tilde{Y}
.
When
\tilde{Y}
is a Calabi–Yau manifold,
one can set

for all
i\in I
.

Still following
[
59
,
35
]
,
for each
J\subset I
,
we let
N\check{D}_{J}
be a small tubular neighborhood of
\check{D}_{J}

such that
N\check{D}_{J}\cap\check{D}_{J^{\prime}}
is a tubular neighborhood of
\check{D}_{J\cup J^{\prime}}
for all
J^{\prime}\subset I
.
Moreover, we require that the boundary
\partial N\check{D}_{J}
intersects
\check{D}_{J^{\prime}}
for all
J^{\prime}\subset I
.
Next, we let

\displaystyle\overset{\circ}{N}\check{D}_{J}=N\check{D}_{J}\setminus\cup_{i\in I}\check{D}_{i}

(7.2)

be the punctured tubular neighborhood.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
59
,
35
]
(see also
[
34
, Remark 3.17]
))
.

There is a cohomological spectral sequence converging to
\operatorname{SH}^{*}(\check{V})
with
E_{1}
-page given by

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\left\{(k_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{I}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\sum k_{i}\kappa_{i}=-p\right\}}H^{p+q-2\sum_{i}k_{i}(c_{i}+1)}\left(\overset{\circ}{N}\check{D}_{{J}_{(k_{i})}}\right)

(7.3)

where

.

Since
\kappa_{i}
is positive for all
i
,
for each
p
,
we have
E_{1}^{p,q}\neq 0
only for finitely many
q
,
and is a finite sum of finite-dimensional vector spaces.
Moreover, if
c_{i}>-1
for all

, then the spectral sequence is regular.

We will apply this spectral sequence
to deduce
\operatorname{SH}^{1}\left(\check{V}\right)=0
,
where
\check{V}
is the Milnor fiber of a weighted homogeneous singularity.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\check{V}
be the Milnor fiber of a weighted homogeneous polynomial
with an isolated critical point at the origin,
d_{0}>0

and
\dim\check{V}\geq 2
, admitting a compactification
to a Calabi–Yau manifold by adding a normal crossing divisor.
One has

\operatorname{SH}^{i}\left(\check{V}\right)=0
for
i<0
,

\operatorname{SH}^{0}\left(\check{V}\right)=\mathbb{C}
,
and

\operatorname{SH}^{1}\left(\check{V}\right)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\check{V}
is simply connected,
we do not get any contribution from
H^{1}(\check{V})=0
.
The vanishing of
c_{i}
and the positivity of
\kappa_{i}
imply that
the orbits coming from the normal crossing divisor contribute to
\operatorname{SH}^{i}\left(\check{V}\right)
for
i\geq 2
.
∎

Now we can prove a generalization
of the non-formality result in
[
47
]
,
which corresponds to the case
{\bf w}=x^{2}+y^{3}
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Under the same assumption as Corollary
7.2
,

\mathcal{A}
is not formal.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.2
,
we have
\operatorname{HH}^{1}(A)\neq 0
.
On the other hand, we know by Corollary

\operatorname{HH}^{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SH}^{1}(\check{V})
,
which is zero by Corollary

.

\mathcal{A}
is not formal.

A non-zero element of
\operatorname{HH}^{1}(A)

is given by the Euler derivation
defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{eu}(x)=\deg(x)x.

(7.4)

Recall that for any

-algebra
\mathscr{A}

that the class of the Euler vector field is killed by the differential on

In dimension 2,

If

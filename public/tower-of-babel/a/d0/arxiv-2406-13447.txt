High-probability minimax lower bounds
Tianyi Ma *, Kabir A. Verchand, Richard J. Samworth * * Statistical Laboratory, University of Cambridge ∘ Industrial and Systems Engineering, Georgia Institute of Technology

Abstract
The minimax risk is often considered as a gold standard against which we can compare specific statistical procedures. Nevertheless, as has been observed recently in robust and heavy-tailed estimation problems, the inherent reduction of the (random) loss to its expectation may entail a significant loss of information regarding its tail behaviour. In an attempt to avoid such a loss, we introduce the notion of a minimax quantile, and seek to articulate its dependence on the quantile level. To this end, we develop high-probability variants of the classical Le Cam and Fano methods, as well as a technique to convert local minimax risk lower bounds to lower bounds on minimax quantiles. To illustrate the power of our framework, we deploy our techniques on several examples, recovering recent results in robust mean estimation and stochastic convex optimisation, as well as obtaining several new results in covariance matrix estimation, sparse linear regression, nonparametric density estimation and isotonic regression. Our overall goal is to argue that minimax quantiles can provide a finer-grained understanding of the difficulty of statistical problems, and that, in wide generality, lower bounds on these quantities can be obtained via user-friendly tools.

Introduction

Over the last 15 years or so, minimax lower bounds, which used to be regarded as primarily a branch of information theory, have entered the statistical mainstream. This transition has been brought about by an increased awareness of what they have to offer to statisticians, in terms of providing a benchmark against which we can compare the performance of different statistical procedures. Their increased prominence can be attributed in part to the surge of interest in high-dimensional statistical problems over the last 25 years, which has encouraged statisticians to move away from traditional diverging sample size asymptotics (with other problem parameters held fixed) and to focus instead on finite-sample performance bounds.

To fix the decision-theoretic foundations that underpin this work, suppose that we are interested in estimating a parameter
\theta
taking values in a parameter space
\Theta
, based on data
X
taking values in a measurable space
\mathcal{X}
. The notation here might suggest that
\Theta
is a subset of a Euclidean space, so it is worth emphasising that we may be interested in estimating more complicated objects like density or regression functions, in which case
\Theta
would be an appropriate function space. Likewise, the data need not be a single observation, but could be a sample of size
n
, for example. Let
\widehat{\Theta}
denote the set of estimators of
\theta\in\Theta
, i.e. the set of measurable functions from
\mathcal{X}
to
\Theta
. Our loss function is a measurable function
L:\Theta\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty)
.

Since
L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(X),\theta\bigr)
is random when
\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}
, it is common to work with the
risk function

R(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(X),\theta\bigr),

a deterministic quantity. A typical ideal goal, then, is to find an estimator
\widehat{\theta}
that achieves the
minimax risk
in the sense that

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}R(\widehat{\theta},\theta)=\inf_{\widetilde{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}R(\widetilde{\theta},\theta).

In this paper, we revisit one of the early choices in the development of this paradigm, namely the reduction of the random
L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(X),\theta\bigr)
to the deterministic risk function. This simplification may fail to tell the whole story regarding the variability and tail behaviour of the loss function for different data realisations. For instance, consider the simple problem of estimating a univariate mean
\theta
with respect to squared error loss
L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\coloneqq(\widehat{\theta}-\theta)^{2}
, based on a sample
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
from a distribution having variance at most
\sigma^{2}
. It is well-known that the minimax risk in this problem is
\sigma^{2}/n
, and is attained by the sample mean
\bar{X}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. On the other hand,
Catoni (2012, Proposition 6.2)
proved that the sample mean has undesirable tail behaviour in the sense that for each
\delta\leq 1/e
, there exists a distribution with mean
\theta
and variance
\sigma^{2}
such that with probability at least
\delta
,

L(\bar{X},\theta)\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n\delta}\biggl(1-\frac{e\delta}{n}\biggr)^{n-1}>\frac{\sigma^{2}}{en\delta}.

This lower bound admits an interpretation as a lower bound on the
(1-\delta)
th quantile of
L(\bar{X},\theta)
. By contrast, the performance of the median-of-means estimator
\widehat{\theta}^{\mathrm{MoM}}_{\delta}

(
Nemirovski and Yudin, 1983
, p. 243)
can be controlled via an upper bound on the
(1-\delta)
th quantile of
L(\widehat{\theta}^{\mathrm{MoM}}_{\delta},\theta)
: with probability at least
1-\delta
,

L(\widehat{\theta}^{\mathrm{MoM}}_{\delta},\theta)\leq C\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}\log(1/\delta)}{n},

for some universal constant
C>0
.

This stark gap in the dependence on
\delta
in the above bounds, and hence the guarantees that can be made with high confidence for the two estimators, makes clear that the reduction to the minimax risk may entail significant information loss. We can retain this information by defining, for
\delta\in(0,1]
, the
minimax
(1-\delta)
th quantile

\mathcal{M}(\delta)\coloneqq\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf\Bigl\{r\in[0,\infty):\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\bigl\{L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(X),\theta\bigr)>r\bigr\}\leq\delta\Bigr\}.

It is often the case that upper bounds on the minimax risk are obtained by finding an estimator
\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}
and integrating a tail bound of the form

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\bigl\{L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(X),\theta\bigr)>r_{\delta}\bigr\}\leq\delta;

in such circumstances, we immediately have
\mathcal{M}(\delta)\leq r_{\delta}
, so upper bounds on the minimax quantile are frequently implicit in upper bound arguments for the minimax risk. The main aim of this work is to introduce user-friendly tools to facilitate lower bounds on
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
; we give a variety of examples where these lower bounds match corresponding upper bounds up to constants, and thereby provide a finer-grained understanding of these statistical challenges.

As two further illustrations of the benefits of minimax quantiles, we first remark that the basis for the analysis of several estimators is a union bound over (potentially exponentially) many different events. Examples include the sample covariance matrix as an estimator of its population counterpart in operator norm
(
Vershynin, 2018
, Theorem 4.6.1)
and the Tukey median
(
Tukey, 1975
;
Chen, Gao and Ren, 2018
)
, as well as tournament and skeleton estimators (
Devroye and Lugosi (2001, Chapters 6 and 7)
and
Chen, Gao and Ren (2016)
). In such cases, it is the behaviour of extreme quantiles of simpler estimators that govern ultimate performance. Second, consider the problem of robust mean estimation with respect to squared error loss in a Huber contamination model
(
Huber, 1964
)
. There, the unboundedness of the loss function, combined with the fact that there is a positive probability with which all data are contaminated, ensures that the minimax risk is infinite, so uninformative from the point of view of comparing estimators; on the other hand, in Corollary
11
we will determine (the finite)
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
for a broad interval of quantile levels
\delta
.

In the high-probability minimax framework, an estimator achieving an upper bound on the minimax quantile
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
may in principle depend on
\delta
. Indeed, this is the case in the median-of-means example discussed above. Nevertheless, there is the potential for a single estimator
\widehat{\theta}
to attain the minimax quantile (up to constants) simultaneously over a wide range of quantile levels. Such adaptation to the quantile level may be interpreted as a further justification for the use of
\widehat{\theta}
.

To outline our contributions in greater detail, in Section
2
, we formally define the minimax quantile
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
, and a closely-related minorant
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
, which we call the
lower minimax quantile
; see Definitions
1
and
3
below. This latter quantity turns out to be especially amenable to analysis via standard tools in the minimax lower bound literature, and in Lemma
5
and Lemma
7
respectively, we apply Le Cam’s two-point lemma and Fano’s inequality to obtain lower bounds on the lower minimax quantile. In fact, in Theorem
8
, we provide a reduction from a local minimax lower bound on the risk to a lower bound on
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
, so the full range of minimax lower bound techniques, including Assouad’s lemma, can be applied.

In Section
3
, we provide a suite of examples to illustrate the power of the framework and give a sense of the breadth of problems to which it may be applied. Indeed, the settings we consider include (robust) multivariate mean estimation, covariance matrix estimation, sparse linear regression, nonparametric density estimation, isotonic regression and stochastic convex optimisation. In all of these examples, we determine the minimax quantile up to multiplicative constants

. One of the main messages we aim to convey through this selection of examples is that it is relatively straightforward to apply our results from Section

1.1
Related work

To the best of our knowledge, our work represents the first systematic study of high-probability minimax lower bounds, though isolated results in certain settings have started to appear in recent years. The importance of the dependence on the quantile level of upper bounds on the minimax quantile has been emphasised in the literature on robust and heavy-tailed estimation
(
Catoni, 2012
;
Lugosi and Mendelson, 2019b
;
Hopkins, 2020
, e.g.)
. On the other hand, lower bounds are scarcer, though
Devroye et al. (2016)
established a lower bound on
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
in univariate mean estimation, and
Depersin and Lecué (2022a)
extended this result to the multivariate case using techniques developed by
Lecué and Mendelson (2013)
based on the Gaussian shift theorem. In particular, our Proposition
10
provides an alternative, streamlined proof of
Depersin and Lecué (2022a, Theorem 4)
. Contemporaneously with our work,
El Hanchi, Maddison and Erdogdu (2024)
obtained an exact but less explicit expression for the minimax quantile in this mean estimation problem (and in linear regression) via a Bayesian argument.

In the context of linear functional estimation,
Donoho and Liu (1991)
characterised
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
in terms of a Hellinger modulus of continuity. Under similar assumptions, explicit universal constants for matching upper and lower bounds on the minimax quantile
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
were provided by
Polyanskiy and Wu (2021)
; see also
Juditsky and Nemirovski (2009)
, whose primary focus is on affine estimators. A quantity similar to
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
was introduced by
Donoho (1994)
to study minimax confidence interval length in the setting of linear functional estimation. He showed that it could be expressed in terms of another modulus of continuity
(see also
Cai, 2012
, Section 5)
.

Recently, in a different yet still univariate setting,
Wang and Ramdas (2024)
and
Duchi and Haque (2024)
invoked information-theoretic techniques to obtain lower bounds on the minimax length of an anytime-valid
(1-\delta)
-level confidence interval.
Tsybakov (2009, Theorem 2.3)
established a lower bound on
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)
in the context of univariate nonparametric regression. See also
Ehrenfeucht et al. (1989)
for early work on sample complexity lower bounds for fixed quantile levels in classification problems within the learning theory community. A closely-related concept to minimax quantiles is that of the Accuracy Confidence (AC) function; indeed, this may be regarded as a generalised inverse of the lower minimax quantile function. Accurate bounds on the AC function in regression and classification problems have been obtained by
DeVore et al. (2006)
,
Temlyakov (2008)
and
Kerkyacharian et al. (2014)
.

In stochastic convex optimisation,
Davis et al. (2021)
consider heavy-tailed stochastic gradients and design methods with logarithmic dependence on the quantile level. Very recently,
Carmon and Hinder (2024)
introduced the notion of a minimax quantile in this setting and applied information-theoretic tools to obtain lower bounds that match their upper bounds up to multiplicative universal constants. In Section
3.6
, we provide a different proof of their lower bound based on our general theory developed in Section
2
in conjunction with Le Cam’s two-point method.

Definition 1

(Minimax quantile)

For

A high-probability upper bound for an estimator implies an upper bound on the minimax quantile. That is, if there exists
\widehat{\theta}_{\delta}\in\widehat{\Theta}

\mathcal{M}(\delta)\leq\mathrm{UB}(\delta)

Proposition 2

For
\delta\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\geq\delta\mathcal{M}(\delta).

Key to our development is the notion of a lower minimax quantile, which turns out to be more amenable to direct analysis.

Definition 3

(Lower minimax quantile)

Theorem 4

For all

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)\leq\mathcal{M}(\delta)\leq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta-\xi).

(1)

Moreover,

The convenience of the lower bound in Theorem

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\,P_{\theta}\bigl\{L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)>r\bigr\}>\delta,

With these notions in place, we now establish high-probability versions of standard information-theoretic tools, beginning with a high-probability variant of Le Cam’s two-point lemma.

Lemma 5

(High-probability Le Cam’s two-point lemma)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and suppose that
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
,
P_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{1}}
and
P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{2}}
satisfy
\mathrm{TV}(P_{1},P_{2})<1-2\delta
. Then, writing
\eta\coloneqq d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})/2
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)\geq g(\eta)
.

In our applications in Section
3

Corollary 6
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and suppose that
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
,
P_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{1}}
and
P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{2}}
satisfy
\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})<\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)
. Then, writing
\eta\coloneqq d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})/2
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)\geq g(\eta)
.

Proof.

By the Bretagnolle–Huber inequality
(
Bretagnolle and Huber, 1979

\displaystyle\mathrm{TV}(P_{1},P_{2})\leq\sqrt{1-\exp\bigl\{-\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})\bigr\}}<1-2\delta.

The result then follows by Lemma
5

The second of our results demonstrates how to convert lower bounds on the minimax risk over bounded subsets of the parameter space into lower bounds on the lower minimax quantile.

Theorem 8

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta_{0})\geq\Delta.

\epsilon>0

\delta\in\bigl(0,\frac{\Delta-g(\epsilon D)}{g((1+\epsilon)D)}\bigr)

\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)\geq g(\epsilon D)

Define

by
h(x)\coloneqq x^{3}+3x^{2}(1-x)
and for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
h^{\circ m}
denote the function obtained by composing
h
with itself
m
times. Let
\delta_{-}\in(0,1/2)
and
\delta_{+}\in(\delta_{-},1/2)
. Then there exists
k\equiv k(\delta_{-},\delta_{+})\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h^{\circ k}(\delta_{+})\leq\delta_{-}
. Moreover, for any such
k
, we have for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{+}]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{1}{(2A)^{k}}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{-}\bigl(\delta_{-},\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes 3^{k}},L\bigr).

3
Examples

With the general techniques of Section
2
now in place, the aim of this section is to determine expressions for the minimax quantiles of loss functions in several examples. In each of the statements to follow, we will consider quantile levels
\delta\leq 1/4
; this value is chosen simply for convenience and could be relaxed to any constant less than
1/2
by, e.g., a further application of Proposition
9
. Throughout this section, we will slightly abuse notation when the loss is of the form
L(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=g(\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|)
for some norm
\|\cdot\|
, by writing
\mathcal{M}\bigl(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},g(\|\cdot\|)\bigr)
in place of
\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)
.

3.1
Multivariate mean estimation

For this basic problem, we determine the minimax quantile in three settings, one for each of the next subsections.

3.1.1
Gaussian mean estimation in squared Euclidean norm

Proposition
10
below recovers the minimax
(1-\delta)
th quantile in multivariate Gaussian mean estimation, up to multiplicative universal constants. The matching upper bound is attained by the sample mean, which is a
\delta
-independent estimator. This result therefore demonstrates the optimality of the sample mean in Gaussian mean estimation in a much stronger manner than usual minimax risk lower bounds.

Proposition 10
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{++}^{d\times d}
,
\Theta\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\coloneqq\{\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta,\Sigma)^{\otimes n}\}
for
\theta\in\Theta
. Then

\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\asymp\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{n}+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{n}.

Proof.

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote orthonormal eigenvectors of
\Sigma
with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{d}
. We capture each of the two additive terms separately, starting with the
\delta
-dependent term. To this end, take
\theta_{1}\coloneqq 0
,
\theta_{2}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)}\,v_{1}
and set
P_{1}=\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{1},\Sigma)^{\otimes n}
and
P_{2}=\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{2},\Sigma)^{\otimes n}
. Then

\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})=n\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{1},\Sigma),\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{2},\Sigma)\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\Bigr)<\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\Bigr).

Thus, applying Theorem
4
in conjunction with Corollary
6
, we deduce that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)}{4n}\geq\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{20n}.

(2)

We turn now to the other term in the lower bound. For
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl\{(v_{j}^{\top}\theta)v_{j}-(v_{j}^{\top}\theta^{\prime})v_{j}\bigr\}\biggr\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl\{v_{j}^{\top}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\bigr\}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}g\bigl(\rho_{j}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\bigr),

where we defined the pseudo-metrics
\rho_{j}:\Theta\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by
\rho_{j}(\theta,\theta)\coloneqq\lvert v_{j}^{\top}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\rvert
for
j\in[d]
and the increasing function
g:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by
g(x)\coloneqq x^{2}
. Now let
\Phi\coloneqq\{0,1\}^{d}
and for
j\in[d]
set
a_{j}\coloneqq\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{j}}{n}}
. For
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d})^{\top}\in\Phi
, take
\theta_{\phi}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}\phi_{j}v_{j}
and let
P_{\phi}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{\phi},\Sigma)^{\otimes n}
. Define
\Theta_{0}\coloneqq\{\theta_{\phi}:\phi\in\Phi\}\subseteq\Theta
with diameter
D\coloneqq\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{n}}
. For
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi
that differ only in the
j
th coordinate, we have
\rho_{j}(\theta_{\phi},\theta_{\phi^{\prime}})=\lvert a_{j}\rvert
and, by Pinsker’s inequality
(
Wainwright, 2019
, Lemma 15.2)
,
\mathrm{TV}(P_{\phi},P_{\phi^{\prime}})\leq\bigl\{\frac{n}{2}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{\phi},\Sigma),\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{\phi^{\prime}},\Sigma)\bigr)\bigr\}^{1/2}=2/3
. Thus, by Assouad’s lemma (Lemma
23
),

\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\max_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}\bigl(\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)\geq\frac{4\tr(\Sigma)}{27n}.

Taking
\epsilon=3/40
in Theorem
8
, we deduce for
\delta\in(0,1/15]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq g(\epsilon D)=\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{100n}.

(3)

Now, recalling the function
h
from Proposition
9
, since
h\bigl(h(1/4)\bigr)<1/15
, we may apply Proposition
9
with
k=2
and (
3
) to deduce that for any
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{2^{6}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 5^{2}\cdot n}.

(4)

Combining Theorem
4
with (
2
) and (
4
) therefore yields that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{2^{7}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 5^{2}\cdot n}+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{40n}.

For the upper bound, consider the sample mean as an estimator. Set
\bar{X}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mathsf{N}(\theta,\Sigma)
, and note that by Jensen’s inequality,
\mathbb{E}(\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{2})\leq\sqrt{\tr(\Sigma)/n}
. Moreover,
\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{2}=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u^{\top}(\bar{X}-\theta)
, so using the fact that
2(a+b)\geq(a^{1/2}+b^{1/2})^{2}
for
a,b\geq 0
by another application of Jensen’s inequality, as well as the Borell–TIS inequality
(see, e.g.,
Adler and Taylor, 2009
, Theorem 2.1.1)
, we have for any
\delta\in(0,1]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}>\frac{2\tr(\Sigma)}{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\biggl(\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{2}>\mathbb{E}(\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{2})+\sqrt{\frac{2\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\biggr)\leq\delta,

as required.
∎

3.1.2
Robust mean estimation in squared Euclidean norm

Recent literature has studied high-probability upper bounds for mean estimation with heavy-tailed data (see, e.g., the review article of
Lugosi and Mendelson (2019a)
and the references therein), or more generally, data from the Huber
\varepsilon
-contamination model
(
Lugosi and Mendelson, 2021
;
Depersin and Lecué, 2022b
, see, e.g.,)
. These upper bound are attained using
\delta
-dependent estimators. As a corollary of Proposition
10
, we provide a matching lower bound on the minimax
(1-\delta)
th quantile up to universal constants in these settings.

Corollary 11
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon\in[0,1)

\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{++}^{d\times d}
,
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\coloneqq\Bigl\{R^{\otimes n}:\,R\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,R=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon Q,\,\mathbb{E}_{P}(X)=\theta,\,\mathrm{Cov}_{P}(X)=\Sigma,\,Q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Bigr\}.

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that if
e^{-cn(1-300\varepsilon)}\leq\delta\leq 1/4
, then

\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\asymp\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{n}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\varepsilon+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{n}.

Proof.

We first consider the lower bound. When
\varepsilon=0
, the claimed lower bound follows from Proposition
10
, so without loss of generality we assume that
\varepsilon>0
. First, since
\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta,\Sigma)^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
, we have by Proposition
10
that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\gtrsim\frac{\tr(\Sigma)}{n}+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(1/\delta)}{n}.

(5)

By the spectral theorem,
\Sigma=V\Lambda V^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
where
\Lambda\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is orthogonal and
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{d}
. Let
a\coloneqq(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})/2
and
b\coloneqq(3\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})/2
. Define random vectors
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})^{\top}\sim P_{X}
and
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d})^{\top}\sim P_{Y}
with independent components satisfying

\displaystyle X_{1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\varepsilon}}\quad&\text{with probability }\varepsilon\\
0&\text{with probability }1-2\varepsilon,\\
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\varepsilon}}\quad&\text{with probability }\varepsilon\end{cases}\ \ Y_{1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\varepsilon}}\quad&\text{with probability }a\\
0&\text{with probability }1-a-b\\
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\varepsilon}}\quad&\text{with probability }b,\end{cases}

and
X_{j}\overset{d}{=}Y_{j}\sim\mathsf{N}(0,\lambda_{j})
for all
j\in\{2,\ldots,d\}
. Then

\Var(X_{1})=\lambda_{1},\quad\Var(Y_{1})=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\varepsilon}\{(a+b)-(b-a)^{2}\}=\lambda_{1}.

Thus
\Cov(X)=\Cov(Y)=\Lambda
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{TV}(P_{X},P_{Y})=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\frac{\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2}}{2}+\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\biggr)=\frac{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\leq\varepsilon.

Define
X^{\prime}\coloneqq VX
,
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq VY
and let
P_{X^{\prime}}
,
P_{Y^{\prime}}
denote the distributions of
X^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime}
respectively. Then
\Cov(X^{\prime})=\Cov(Y^{\prime})=\Sigma
, and by the data processing inequality
(e.g.,
Gerchinovitz, Ménard and Stoltz, 2020
, Lemma 2.1)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{TV}(P_{X^{\prime}},P_{Y^{\prime}})\leq\mathrm{TV}(P_{X},P_{Y})\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.

Then, let
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}\coloneqq\bigl\{R\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\mathbb{E}_{R}(X)=\theta,\mathrm{Cov}_{R}(X)=\Sigma\bigr\}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\Theta}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
. Then, writing
\theta_{1}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime})
and
\theta_{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}(Y^{\prime})
, we have by Theorem
4
and Lemma
25
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}{4}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}\cdot\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{8\varepsilon}=\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\varepsilon}{8}.

(6)

Combining (
5
) and (
6
) proves that the stated lower bound holds for all
\delta\in(0,1/4]
.

The matching upper bound follows from
Depersin and Lecué (2022b, Theorem 2.1)
, by taking
u=\infty
in their notation (note that the lower bound on
\delta
—which in turn implies an upper bound on
\varepsilon
—is implicit from their theorem).
∎

3.1.3
Gaussian mean estimation in
\ell_{\infty}
-norm

In our next result, we switch to
\ell_{\infty}
-loss, and invoke Fano’s lemma (Lemma
7
) for the lower bound. As in Proposition
10
, the matching upper bound is achieved by the sample mean.

Proposition 12
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma>0
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\Theta\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\coloneqq\bigl\{\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta,\Sigma)^{\otimes n}:\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{d\times d}_{++},\,\|\Sigma\|_{\infty}\leq\sigma^{2}\bigr\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\asymp\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(d/\delta)}{n}}.

Remark
.

In our lower bound construction below, we only use distributions whose covariance matrices are scalar multiples of the identity matrix. In that setting, we see from Proposition
12
that the term involving the quantile level
\delta
dominates when
\delta\ll 1/d
, whereas with squared Euclidean error, Proposition
10
reveals that this happens when
\delta\ll e^{-d}
.

Proof.

Define
\theta_{1}\coloneqq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)}\,e_{1}
,
\theta_{2}\coloneqq 0
, and
P_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta_{\ell},\sigma^{2}I_{d})^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{\ell}}
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Then
\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)<\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)
. Hence, by Theorem
4
and Corollary
6
, we have for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{1}{4n}\log\biggl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\biggr)}\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{20n}}.

(7)

Now assume for the time being that
d\geq 4
. For
j\in[d]
, define
\theta^{(j)}\coloneqq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{2n}}\,e_{j}
,
P^{(j)}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta^{(j)},\sigma^{2}I_{d})^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta^{(j)}}
, and define
Q\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\sigma^{2}I)^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{Q}\bigl((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\bigr)
. Then
\mathrm{KL}(P^{(j)},Q)=n\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathsf{N}_{d}(\theta^{(j)},\sigma^{2}I_{d}),\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})\bigr)=\frac{1}{4}\log d
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{d^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{KL}(P^{(j)},Q)+\log(2-d^{-1})}{\log d}\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\log(2-d^{-1})}{\log d}\leq\frac{7}{10}.

Therefore, by applying Theorem
4
, followed by Lemma
7
with
\epsilon=3/10
, we deduce that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{8n}}.

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
) yields that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
and
d\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{8n}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{20n}}\biggr)\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(d/\delta)}{80n}}.

(9)

Now consider the case
d\leq 3
. By (
7
), we have for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{20n}}\geq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(d/\delta)}{40n}}.

(10)

Combining (
9
) and (
10
) proves the lower bound.

For the upper bound, let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
and
\bar{X}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. Then for
j\in[d]
,

\mathbb{P}\biggl(|\bar{X}_{j}-\theta_{j}|>\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(d/\delta)}{n}}\biggr)=2\int_{\sqrt{2\log(d/\delta)}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\delta}{d}.

Applying a union bound, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(\|\bar{X}-\theta\|_{\infty}>\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(d/\delta)}{n}}\biggr)\leq\delta,

so
\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(d/\delta)}{n}}
for
\delta\in(0,1]
.
∎

3.2
Covariance matrix estimation

Here we consider the estimation of a covariance matrix in operator norm based on a sample of size
n
, and determine the minimax quantile up to universal constants. As we show in the proof of Proposition
13
below, the upper bound is attained by the sample covariance matrix, applying the careful analysis of
Koltchinskii and Lounici (2017)
(see also
Zhivotovskiy (2024)
) when the quantile level
\delta
satisfies
\delta\geq e^{-n}
, and the trivial zero estimator when
\delta<e^{-n}
. By contrast, the matrix Bernstein inequality yields sub-optimal concentration as compared to the minimax quantile in Proposition
13
; see Lemma
26
for details. Recall that the
effective rank
of
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d\times d}
is defined as
\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\coloneqq\tr(\Sigma)/\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
, with the convention that
0/0\coloneqq 0
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\sigma>0
,
r\in[1,n\wedge d]
,
\Theta\coloneqq\bigl\{\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d\times d}:\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sigma^{2},\,\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\leq r\bigr\}
, and for
\Sigma\in\Theta
, define
\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}\coloneqq\bigl\{\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma)^{\otimes n}\bigr\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\asymp\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}\coloneqq\lfloor r\rfloor
, so that
r_{0}\geq(r/2)\vee 1
.
First consider the case
\delta\in[e^{-n}/3,1/4]
, and set
a\coloneqq 1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)}\in[1/2,1]
. Define
\Sigma^{(1)},\Sigma^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d\times d}
by

\Sigma^{(1)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}\sigma^{2}&0&0\\
0&\sigma^{2}I_{r_{0}-1}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma^{(2)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{2}I_{r_{0}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then, for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, we have
\|\Sigma^{(\ell)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sigma^{2}
and
\mathbf{r}(\Sigma^{(\ell)})\leq r_{0}\leq r
, so that
\Sigma^{(\ell)}\in\Theta
. Letting
P_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma^{(\ell)})^{\otimes n}
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, we find by Taylor’s theorem with the integral form of the remainder that

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})

\displaystyle=n\cdot\frac{\log(1/a^{2})-(1\!-\!a^{2})}{2}=\frac{n}{2}\cdot\Bigl\{2(1-a)^{2}+2\int_{a}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\Bigl(1-\frac{a}{t}\Bigr)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq 3n(1-a)^{2}=\frac{3}{4}\log\biggl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\biggr)<\log\biggl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\biggr).

Therefore, combining Theorem
4
and Corollary
6
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\geq\frac{\|\Sigma^{(1)}-\Sigma^{(2)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2}=\frac{(1-a^{2})\sigma^{2}}{2}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1\!-\!\delta)}\Bigr)}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{9}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}}.

On the other hand, when
\delta\in(0,e^{-n}/3)
, since
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
is decreasing in
\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\geq\mathcal{M}(e^{-n},\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{9}.

Hence, for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\gtrsim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}.

(11)

Now assume that
r_{0}\geq 20
and define
\Phi\coloneqq\{0,1\}^{r_{0}}
. By the Gilbert–Varshamov lemma
(e.g.,
Massart, 2007
, Lemma 4.7)
, there exists
\Phi_{0}\subseteq\Phi
with
|\Phi_{0}|\geq e^{r_{0}/8}
and
d_{\mathrm{H}}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})>r_{0}/4
for all distinct
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi_{0}
, where
d_{\mathrm{H}}
denotes the Hamming distance. Let
b\coloneqq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{6\sqrt{nr_{0}}}
, and for
\phi\in\Phi_{0}
, define
\Sigma^{(\phi)}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d\times d}
as well as
\Sigma^{(0)}\in\mathcal{S}^{d\times d}_{+}
by

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(\phi)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}I_{r_{0}}+b\phi\phi^{\top}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma^{(0)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}I_{r_{0}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\|\Sigma^{(\phi)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+b\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+br_{0}\leq\sigma^{2}
and
\mathbf{r}(\Sigma^{(\phi)})\leq r_{0}\leq r
for all
\phi\in\Phi_{0}
, so
\Sigma^{(\phi)}\in\Theta
. For
\phi\in\Phi_{0}
, define
P^{(\phi)}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma^{(\phi)})^{\otimes n}
so that
P^{(\phi)}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma^{(\phi)}}
. Then, taking
Q\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma^{(0)})^{\otimes n}
, we have for
\phi\in\Phi_{0}
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(P^{(\phi)},Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\Biggl\{\log\biggl(\frac{(\sigma^{2}/2)^{r_{0}}}{(b\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma^{2}/2)(\sigma^{2}/2)^{r_{0}-1}}\biggr)+\frac{2b\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\Biggl\{\log\biggl(\frac{1}{1+2b\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}/\sigma^{2}}\biggr)+\frac{2b\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\Biggr\}\leq\frac{nb^{2}\|\phi\|_{2}^{4}}{\sigma^{4}}\leq\frac{r_{0}}{36},

where the penultimate inequality follows because
\log(\frac{1}{1+x})+x\leq x^{2}/2
for
x\geq 0
and the final inequality follows since
\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}\leq r_{0}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{|\Phi_{0}|^{-1}\sum_{\phi\in\Phi_{0}}\mathrm{KL}(P^{(\phi)},Q)+\log(2-|\Phi_{0}|^{-1})}{\log|\Phi_{0}|}\leq\frac{r_{0}/36+\log(2)}{r_{0}/8}\leq\frac{1}{2},

(12)

where the final inequality holds under the assumption
r_{0}\geq 20
. Now fix distinct
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r_{0}})^{\top}
,
\phi^{\prime}=(\phi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\phi_{r_{0}}^{\prime})^{\top}\in\Phi_{0}
, and define
S\coloneqq\bigl\{j\in[r_{0}]:\phi_{j}=1\text{ and }\phi^{\prime}_{j}=0\bigr\}
. By reversing the roles of
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
if necessary, we may assume that
|S|\geq r_{0}/8
since
d_{\mathrm{H}}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})>r_{0}/4
. Define
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r_{0}})^{\top}\in\{0,1\}^{r_{0}}
by
v_{j}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\in S\}}
for
j\in[r_{0}]
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Sigma^{(\phi)}-\Sigma^{(\phi^{\prime})}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\frac{v^{\top}(\Sigma^{(\phi)}-\Sigma^{(\phi^{\prime})})v}{\|v\|_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{b(r_{0}/8)^{2}}{r_{0}}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{384}\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{n}}.

(13)

Combining (
12
) and (
13
) with Theorem
4
and Lemma
7
, we deduce that for
\delta\in(0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{768}\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{n}}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{800}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}.

(14)

Hence, combining (
11
) and (
14
) yields that for
r\geq 20
and
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\gtrsim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+\biggl(\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}\biggr)\gtrsim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}.

On the other hand, for
r<20
and
\delta\in(0,1/4]
, we have by (
11
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\gtrsim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}\gtrsim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2}.

This proves the lower bound.

For the upper bound, let
X_{1},\ldots X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma)
where
\Sigma\in\Theta
. When
\delta\in[e^{-n},1/4]
, let
\widehat{\Sigma}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}
. Then by
Koltchinskii and Lounici (2017, Corollary 2)
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}.

On the other hand, when
\delta\in(0,e^{-n})
, let
\widehat{\Sigma}\coloneqq 0
, so that
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sigma^{2}
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}})\lesssim\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\wedge\sigma^{2},

for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
.
∎

3.3
Sparse linear regression

In this section, we consider sparse linear regression with fixed design
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. We will require the weighted restricted eigenvalue (WRE) condition of
Bellec, Lecué and Tsybakov (2018, p. 3616)
. To recall this, for
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
v^{\sharp}=(v_{1}^{\sharp},\ldots,v_{d}^{\sharp})^{\top}\in[0,\infty)^{d}
denote the vector obtained by ordering the absolute values of the components of
v
in decreasing order. For
j\in[d]
and some
\sigma>0
(which will be taken to be the noise standard deviation), let
\lambda_{j}\coloneqq 6\sigma\sqrt{n^{-1}\log(2d/j)}
. Now, for
c_{0}>0
,
s\in[d]
, define the cone

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{WRE}}(s,c_{0})\coloneqq\biggl\{v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}v_{j}^{\sharp}\leq(1+c_{0})\|v\|_{2}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr\}

and let

\vartheta(s,c_{0})\coloneqq\min_{v\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{WRE}}(s,c_{0})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|Xv\|_{2}}{n^{1/2}\|v\|_{2}}.

The following assumption imposes regularity conditions on our design matrix.

Assumption 1
.

Each column of
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has Euclidean norm at most
\sqrt{n}
. Moreover, there exist
c,C>0
such that when
d=1
,
X
satisfies
\|X\|_{2}\geq c\sqrt{n}
; when
d\geq 2
,
X
satisfies
\vartheta(s\vee 2,3)\geq c
and
\max_{T\subseteq[d]:|T|\leq s\vee 2}\|X_{T}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\sqrt{n}
, where
X_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times|T|}
is the submatrix of
X
obtained by extracting the columns with indices in
T
.

Existing results show that if
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has independent
\mathsf{N}(0,1/2)
entries, then there exist universal constants
c^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0
such that, when
d\geq 2
, Assumption
1
holds with
c=1/2
and
C=2
with probability at least
1-e^{-c^{\prime}n}
when
n\geq C^{\prime}s\log(2ed/s)
. Indeed, the verification of the condition
\vartheta(s\vee 2,3)\geq 1/2
is given by
Bellec, Lecué and Tsybakov (2018, Theorem 8.3)
, and verification of the condition
\max_{T\subseteq[d]:|T|\leq s\vee 2}\|X_{T}\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\sqrt{n}\leq 2
can be obtained via concentration results for Gaussian ensembles
(e.g.,
Wainwright, 2019
, Theorem 6.1)
.

We note that the matching upper bound in Proposition
14
below is attained by the SLOPE estimator
(
Bogdan et al., 2015
;
Bellec, Lecué and Tsybakov, 2018
)
, which is
\delta
-independent.

Proposition 14
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in[d]
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\sigma>0
and
\Theta\coloneqq\bigl\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\theta\|_{0}\leq s\bigr\}
. Assume that
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
satisfies Assumption
1
for some
c,C>0
, and for
\theta\in\Theta
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\coloneqq\bigl\{\mathsf{N}_{n}(X\theta,\sigma^{2}I_{n})\bigr\}
.
Then, setting
L(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\coloneqq n^{-1}\|X(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\asymp_{c,C}\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{s\log(ed/s)+\log(1/\delta)}{n}.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{1}\coloneqq 0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\theta_{2}\coloneqq e_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
. For
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, let
P_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{n}(X\theta_{\ell},\sigma^{2}I_{n})
. Then
\mathrm{KL}(P_{1},P_{2})=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\|X\theta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\|\theta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}<\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)
. Therefore, combining Theorem
4
, Assumption
1
(since
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{WRE}}(s\vee 2,3)
when
d\geq 2
) and Corollary
6
, we have for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{1}{4n}\|X(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{c^{2}}{4}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}\log(1/\delta)}{20n}.

(15)

To capture the first term in the lower bound, let us first consider the case where
d\geq 4e
. By Lemma
27
, there exist
M\in\mathbb{N}
with

\log M\geq\frac{3\bigl(1\vee\lfloor s/2\rfloor\wedge\lfloor d/(4e)\rfloor\bigr)}{4}\log\biggl(\frac{d}{4\bigl(1\vee\lfloor s/2\rfloor\wedge\lfloor d/(4e)\rfloor\bigr)}\biggr)

and
\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\bigl\{v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(M)}\bigr\}
such that
\mathcal{V}
forms a Euclidean norm
(1/2)
-packing of the set
\bigl\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\theta\|_{0}\leq 1\vee\lfloor s/2\rfloor\wedge\lfloor d/(4e)\rfloor,\,\|\theta\|_{2}\leq 1\bigr\}
. For
m\in[M]
, define
\theta^{(m)}\coloneqq v^{(m)}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}\log M}{4C^{2}n}}
, so that
\theta^{(m)}\in\Theta
, and define
P^{(m)}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{n}(X\theta^{(m)},\sigma^{2}I_{n})
. Further define
Q\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{n}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{n})
so that
\mathrm{KL}(P^{(m)},Q)=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\|X\theta^{(m)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\log M}{8}
for
m\in[M]
. Since
\log M\geq 3/4
and since
M\mapsto\log(2-M^{-1})/\log M
is decreasing for
M\geq e^{3/4}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{KL}(P^{(m)},Q)+\log(2-M^{-1})}{\log M}\leq\frac{1}{8}+\frac{\log(2-M^{-1})}{\log M}\leq\frac{7}{10}.

Since
\theta^{(m)}-\theta^{(m^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{WRE}}(s\vee 2,3)
for distinct
m,m^{\prime}\in[M]
, we have by Lemma
7
, that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4n}\cdot\min_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\|X(\theta^{(m)}-\theta^{(m^{\prime})})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{c^{2}}{4}\min_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\|\theta^{(m)}-\theta^{(m^{\prime})}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}\{s/4\wedge 3d/(32e)\}\log\bigl(\frac{d}{4s}\bigr)}{2^{6}C^{2}n}\geq\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}s\log(ed/s)}{2^{13}C^{2}n}.

(16)

From (
15
) and (
16
), we deduce that for
d\geq 4e
and
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}\log(1/\delta)}{40n}+\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}s\log(ed/s)}{2^{14}C^{2}n}.

On the other hand, when
d<4e
, we have by (
15
) that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{c^{2}\sigma^{2}\log(1/\delta)}{20n}\geq c^{2}\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{s\log(ed/s)+\log(1/\delta)}{200n}.

This proves the lower bound.

The upper bound is achieved, for example, by the SLOPE estimator
(
Bogdan et al., 2015
)
, with a
\delta
-independent tuning parameter. In particular, applying
Bellec, Lecué and Tsybakov (2018, Eq. (6.2) and (6.5))
with
\gamma=\tau=1/4
in their notation yields that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\leq 100(4+\sqrt{2})\sigma^{2}\biggl(\frac{s\log(ed/s)}{c^{2}n}+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr),

as required.
∎

3.4
Nonparametric density estimation

Turning to a nonparametric statistical problem, we now consider density estimation at a point. To fix the setting, let
\beta,\gamma>0
and
m\coloneqq\lceil\beta\rceil-1
. The
Hölder class

\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
of density functions is the set of all Borel measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying that
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, that
f
is
m
-times differentiable and that

\displaystyle|f^{(m)}(x)-f^{(m)}(x^{\prime})|\leq\gamma|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-m},

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition
15
determines an explicit expression for the minimax quantiles, up to multiplicative factors depending only on
\beta
. We establish, using Lepski’s method, that the upper bound is attainable by a
\delta
-independent estimator in Lemma
29
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\Theta\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
be the Hölder class of density functions and for
f\in\Theta
, define
\mathcal{P}_{f}\coloneqq\{P_{f}^{\otimes n}\}
where
P_{f}
is the distribution with density
f
. Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
L(f_{1},f_{2})\coloneqq\{f_{1}(x_{0})-f_{2}(x_{0})\}^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\asymp_{\beta}\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\cdot\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\biggr\}.

Remark
.

Polyanskiy and Wu (2021, Corollary 2 and Lemma 16)
obtain that for the subclass
\mathcal{F}_{[-1,1]}(\beta,\gamma)
of
\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
consisting of densities supported on
[-1,1]
, and for
\delta\in[e^{-2n},1/16]
, we have

\mathcal{M}(\delta)\asymp_{\beta,\gamma}\biggl(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)},

with the upper bound attained by a
\delta
-dependent estimator.

Proof.

By the translation invariance of
\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
, the minimax quantile
\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)
is the same for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, so assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
.
Let
m\coloneqq\lceil\beta\rceil-1
and take
K:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
to be the infinitely differentiable density given by
K(x)\propto e^{-1/(1-4x^{2})}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|<1/2\}}
, which satisfies

\|K\|_{C^{\beta}}\coloneqq\sup_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|K^{(m)}(x)-K^{(m)}(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-m}}<\infty.

Define
\widetilde{\gamma}\coloneqq\gamma/\|K\|_{C^{\beta}}
, and for
\lambda\in(0,1]
that will be chosen later to depend on
n
,
\beta
and
\delta
, define
g,h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

g(x)\coloneqq\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}K\bigl(\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x\bigr),\qquad h(x)\coloneqq\lambda\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}K\bigl(\lambda^{-1/\beta}\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x\bigr).

(17)

Then
g\in\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
because it is
m
-times differentiable, and for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|g^{(m)}(x)-g^{(m)}(x^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\gamma}^{\frac{m+1}{\beta+1}}\bigl|K^{(m)}\bigl(\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x\bigr)-K^{(m)}\bigl(\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x^{\prime}\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\gamma}^{\frac{m+1}{\beta+1}}\|K\|_{C^{\beta}}\bigl|\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x-\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x^{\prime}\bigr|^{\beta-m}=\gamma|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-m}.

Similarly,
\bigl|h^{(m)}(x)-h^{(m)}(x^{\prime})\bigr|\leq\gamma|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-m}
for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h=\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}
. Moreover,

h(x)\leq\lambda\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}K\bigl(\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}x\bigr)=\lambda g(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Now define
f_{0},f_{1}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle f_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(g+h)(x)+g(2\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}-x)}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}},

(18)

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{g(x)+(g+h)(2\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}-x)}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}};

(19)

see Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the construction in Proposition
15
. Left: The functions
g
(solid) and
h
(dot-dashed) from (
17
). Right: The functions
f_{0}
(solid) and
f_{1}
(dot-dashed) from (
18
) and (
19
).

Since
g,h
are zero outside the interval
[-\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}/2,\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}/2]
, we deduce that
f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
and since
K(0)\geq 1
,

f_{0}(0)-f_{1}(0)=\frac{h(0)}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}}=\frac{\lambda\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}K(0)}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}}\geq\frac{\lambda\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}}{3}.

In addition, writing
I\coloneqq[-\lambda^{1/\beta}\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}/2,\lambda^{1/\beta}\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}/2]
for the support of
h
and
I^{\prime}\coloneqq 2\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1/(\beta+1)}+I
, we have
f_{0}=f_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus(I\cup I^{\prime})
. Thus, since
\log(1+\lambda)\leq\lambda
, it follows that the product densities
\bigl\{f_{j}^{n}:j\in\{0,1\}\bigr\}
given by
f_{j}^{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{j}(x_{i})
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(f_{0}^{n},f_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle=n\mathrm{KL}(f_{0},f_{1})=n\int_{I\cup I^{\prime}}f_{0}\log\Bigl(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{1}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}}\int_{I}\,\biggl\{(g+h)\log\Bigl(\frac{g+h}{g}\Bigr)+g\log\Bigl(\frac{g}{g+h}\Bigr)\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2+\lambda^{(\beta+1)/\beta}}\int_{I}h\log\biggl(1+\frac{h}{g}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}\log(1+\lambda)\int_{I}h\leq\frac{n}{2}\lambda^{(2\beta+1)/\beta}.

Taking
\lambda=\bigl\{\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)\bigr\}^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\in(0,1]
, we have that
\mathrm{KL}(f_{0}^{n},f_{1}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)<\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)
, so by Theorem
4
and Corollary
6
, for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)

\displaystyle\geq\biggl(\frac{\lambda\widetilde{\gamma}^{1/(\beta+1)}}{2\cdot 3}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}}{36\cdot 5^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\|K\|_{C^{\beta}}^{2/(\beta+1)}}\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\biggr\}.

This completes the proofs of the lower bound. The upper bound follows from Lemma
29
, after fixing a bounded kernel supported on
[-1,1]
.
∎

3.5
Isotonic regression

Other natural nonparametric estimation problems are provided by shape-constrained models
(
Groeneboom and Jongbloed, 2014
;
Samworth and Sen, 2018
)
, and here we consider isotonic regression. The matching upper bound is attained by the projected isotonic least squares estimator, which is
\delta
-independent.

Proposition 16
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,
\Theta\coloneqq\bigl\{\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})^{\top}\in[0,1]^{n}:\theta_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\theta_{n}\bigr\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\coloneqq\bigl\{\mathsf{N}_{n}(\theta,I_{n})\bigr\}
for
\theta\in\Theta
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\asymp\biggl(\frac{1}{n^{2/3}}+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\biggr)\wedge 1.

Proof.

First consider the case where
\delta\in[e^{-n}/3,1/4]
.
Let
\theta^{(1)}\coloneqq 0
,
\theta^{(2)}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr)}\,\bm{1}_{n}
and
P^{(\ell)}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}_{n}(\theta^{(\ell)},I_{n})
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Then
\theta^{(\ell)}\in\Theta
and
P^{(\ell)}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta^{(\ell)}}
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})=\frac{1}{2}\log\biggl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\biggr)<\log\biggl(\frac{1}{4\delta(1-\delta)}\biggr).

Therefore, by Corollary
6
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(2)}\|_{2}^{2}}{4n}\geq\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{20n},

(20)

for
\delta\in[e^{-n}/3,1/4]
. Next, for the case where
\delta\in(0,e^{-n}/3)
, we have by (
20
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\mathcal{M}(e^{-n},\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{20}.

(21)

Combining (
20
) and (
21
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{20}\biggl(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n}\wedge 1\biggr),

(22)

for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
.

For the second part of the lower bound, we let
\Theta_{0}\subseteq\Theta
denote the finite set constructed in the proof of
Chatterjee, Guntuboyina and Sen (2015, Theorem 5.3)
to obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\max_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}\bigl(n^{-1}\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2^{9}\cdot 3^{1/3}}\cdot n^{-2/3}.

In this construction,
D\coloneqq\sup_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{0}}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}\in\bigl[\frac{n^{1/6}}{2^{3}\cdot 3^{1/6}},n^{1/6}\bigr]
. Hence by Theorem
8
with
\epsilon=2^{-6}
, we obtain that for
\delta\in(0,1/1000]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},n^{-1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{2^{18}\cdot 3^{1/3}}\cdot n^{-2/3}.

Then, applying Proposition
9
with
k=4
in conjunction with Theorem
4
, we deduce that for
\delta\in(0,1/4]
,

3.6
Stochastic convex optimisation

As our final example, we move away from the traditional setting of statistical estimation and consider stochastic convex optimisation
(
Duchi, 2018
)
, where the goal is to understand the iteration complexity of minimising a convex function in expectation, given access only to queries at random observations. To do so, we require some preliminaries. Let
R>0
, let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and convex such that
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, and let
\mathcal{Y}
be a measurable space. For
P\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})
and
\gamma>0
, let
\mathcal{F}(P,\gamma)
denote the set of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for which

(i)

f(x,\cdot):\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
is
P
-integrable for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
;

(ii)

f(\cdot,y):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex for each
y\in\mathcal{Y}
with subdifferential at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denoted by
\partial_{x}f(x,y)
;

(iii)

for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, define the function
g_{x}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
g_{x}(y)\coloneqq\argmin_{x^{\prime}\in\partial_{x}f(x,y)}\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}
, and assume that
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl\{\|g_{x}(Y)\|_{2}^{2}\bigr\}\leq\gamma^{2}
.

The selected subgradient
g_{x}
in part
(iii)
of this definition is well-defined as the subdifferential is compact and convex
(
Rockafellar, 1997
, Theorem 23.4 and p. 215)
, and moreover
g_{x}
is measurable on
\mathcal{Y}
for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, by
Aliprantis and Border (2007, Theorem 18.19)
. For
f\in\mathcal{F}(P,\gamma)
, define
F_{P}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
F_{P}(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{P}\{f(x;Y)\}
, so that
F_{P}
is convex on
\mathcal{X}
. The
optimality gap

L^{\mathsf{opt}}(\cdot;P,f):\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is defined to be
L^{\mathsf{opt}}(x;P,f)\coloneqq F_{P}(x)-\inf_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}F_{P}(x^{\prime})
. For
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})
and
f\in\mathcal{F}(P_{1},\gamma)\cap\mathcal{F}(P_{2},\gamma)
, the
f
-separation
between
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{sep}(P_{1},P_{2};f)\coloneqq\sup\Bigl\{\Delta\geq 0:\;

\displaystyle L^{\mathsf{opt}}(x;P_{2},f)>\Delta\text{ for all $x\in\mathcal{X}$ with }L^{\mathsf{opt}}(x;P_{1},f)\leq\Delta\Bigr\}.

Now define the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\gamma,R)\coloneqq\Bigl\{(P,f):P\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y}),\,f\in\mathcal{F}(P,\gamma)\text{ and }\|x^{\star}\|_{2}\leq R\text{ for some }x^{\star}\in\argmin_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}F_{P}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\}.

For each
t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
denote the set of all measurable functions from
\mathcal{Y}^{t}
to
\mathcal{X}
; in particular, this includes methods such as stochastic (sub)gradient descent (SGD) or stochastic versions of Newton’s algorithm. For

and

for the optimality gap

is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{opt}}(\delta;T)\coloneqq\inf_{\widehat{x}_{\delta}\in\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_{T}}\sup_{(P,f)\in\mathcal{G}(\gamma,R)}\mathrm{Quantile}\bigl(1-\delta;L^{\mathsf{opt}}(\widehat{x}_{\delta};P,f),P^{\otimes T}\bigr).

By a virtually identical argument to that given in the proof of Theorem
4

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{opt}}(\delta;T)\geq\inf\Bigl\{r\in[0,\infty):\inf_{\widehat{x}_{\delta}\in\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_{T}}\sup_{(P,f)\in\mathcal{G}(\gamma,R)}P^{\otimes T}\bigl\{L^{\mathsf{opt}}(\widehat{x}_{\delta};P,f)>r\bigr\}\leq\delta\Bigr\}\eqqcolon\mathcal{M}_{-}^{\mathsf{opt}}(\delta;T).

(24)

f(y,\cdot):\Theta\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
is continuous for each
y\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Lemma 19
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{\otimes m}\coloneqq\{P_{\theta}^{\otimes m}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{X}^{m}):P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes m}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{\otimes m}:\theta\in\Theta\}
. Suppose that
\Theta
is a non-empty Polish space, that
L:\Theta\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a Carathéodory function, and there exists
A>0
such that
L(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq A\bigl\{L(\theta_{1},\theta_{3})+L(\theta_{2},\theta_{3})\bigr\}
for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\in\Theta
.
Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{1}{2A}\mathcal{M}_{-}\bigl(\delta^{3}+3\delta^{2}(1-\delta),\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes 3},L\bigr).

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
, there exists a measurable function
\widetilde{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\Theta
such that for all
\theta\in\Theta
,

\displaystyle P_{\theta}\bigl\{L(\widetilde{\theta},\theta)<\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon\bigr\}\geq 1-\delta.

Now fix
\theta\in\Theta
, and define the event

\displaystyle E\coloneqq\biggl\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbbm{1}_{\{L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{\ell}),\theta)<\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon\}}\geq 2\biggr\}

so that
P_{\theta}^{\otimes 3}(E)\geq 1-\delta^{3}-3\delta^{2}(1-\delta)
. Further, define
\psi:\mathcal{X}^{3}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pow}(\Theta)
by

\displaystyle\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\biggl\{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta:\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbbm{1}_{\{L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{\ell}),\theta^{\prime})<\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon\}}\geq 2\biggr\}.

For
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}
, let
\bar{\psi}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
denote the closure of
\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
in
\Theta
, and define
S:\mathcal{X}^{3}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pow}(\Theta)
by

\displaystyle S(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\bar{\psi}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\quad&\text{when }\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\neq\emptyset\\
\Theta&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
20
, there exists a measurable function
\widehat{\theta}:\mathcal{X}^{3}\to\Theta
such that
\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
for all
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}
.
For any
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in E
, we have
\theta\in\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, so
\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is non-empty; for such
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, it follows that
\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\bar{\psi}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, so

\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbbm{1}_{\{L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{\ell}),\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}))\leq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon\}}\geq 2.

Therefore, for all
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in E
, there exists
k\in\{1,2,3\}
such that both
L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{k}),\theta\bigr)<\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon
and
L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{k}),\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\bigr)\leq\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+\epsilon
. Thus, for all
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in E
,

\displaystyle L\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\theta\bigr)\leq A\bigl\{L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{k}),\theta)+L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{k}),\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\bigr)\bigr\}\leq 2A\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+2A\epsilon,

and we deduce that

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{\otimes 3}\bigl\{L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\leq 2A\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+2A\epsilon\bigr\}\geq P_{\theta}^{\otimes 3}(E)\geq 1-\delta^{3}-3\delta^{2}(1-\delta).

This holds for all
\theta\in\Theta
, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}\bigl(\delta^{3}+3\delta^{2}(1-\delta),\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes 3},L\bigr)\leq 2A\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)+2A\epsilon,

and the result follows since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary.
∎

The following technical lemma on the existence of a measurable selector is based on the theory of correspondences, so we now present some basic definitions, following
Aliprantis and Border (2007)
. A
correspondence

\varphi
from a set
\mathcal{Y}
to a set
\mathcal{Z}
, written
\varphi:\mathcal{Y}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
, assigns to each
y\in\mathcal{Y}
a subset
\varphi(y)
of
\mathcal{Z}
; in other words, it is a function from
\mathcal{Y}
to
\mathrm{Pow}(\mathcal{Z})
. Its
lower inverse

\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}:\mathrm{Pow}(\mathcal{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{Pow}(\mathcal{Y})
is defined by

\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}(B)\coloneqq\{y\in\mathcal{Y}:\varphi(y)\cap B\neq\emptyset\}.

When
\mathcal{Z}
is a topological space, the
closure correspondence

\bar{\varphi}:\mathcal{Y}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
of
\varphi
assigns to each
y\in\mathcal{Y}
the subset of
\mathcal{Z}
given by the closure of
\varphi(y)
. If, in addition,
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B})
is a measurable space, we say that the correspondence
\varphi:\mathcal{Y}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
is
measurable
if
\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}(F)\in\mathcal{B}
for every closed subset
F
of
\mathcal{Z}
, and
weakly measurable
if
\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}(G)\in\mathcal{B}
for every open subset
G
of
\mathcal{Z}
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space, let
\Theta
be a non-empty Polish space and suppose that
L:\Theta\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a Carathéodory function. Let
\widetilde{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\Theta
be measurable, let
r\in(0,\infty)
and define a correspondence
\psi:\mathcal{X}^{3}\twoheadrightarrow\Theta
by

\displaystyle\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\biggl\{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta:\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbbm{1}_{\{L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{\ell}),\theta^{\prime})<r\}}\geq 2\biggr\}.

Further, define a non-empty correspondence
S:\mathcal{X}^{3}\twoheadrightarrow\Theta
by

\displaystyle S(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\bar{\psi}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\quad&\text{when }\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\neq\emptyset\\
\Theta&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then there exists a measurable function
\widehat{\theta}:\mathcal{X}^{3}\to\Theta
such that
\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
for all
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}
.

Proof.

The map
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\theta^{\prime})\mapsto\bigl(L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{1}),\theta^{\prime}\bigr),L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{2}),\theta^{\prime}\bigr),L\bigl(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{3}),\theta^{\prime}\bigr)\bigr)
is a Carathéodory function from
\mathcal{X}^{3}\times\Theta
to
[0,\infty)^{3}
. Define
G\coloneqq\bigl\{(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{\top}\in[0,\infty)^{3}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbbm{1}_{\{v_{\ell}<r\}}\geq 2\bigr\}
, which is an open subset of
[0,\infty)^{3}
. Then, by
Aliprantis and Border (2007, Lemma 18.7)
, the correspondence
\psi
, which can be equivalently expressed as
\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\bigl\{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta:\bigl(L(\widetilde{\theta}(x_{\ell}),\theta^{\prime})\bigr)_{\ell=1}^{3}\in G\bigr\}
, is measurable, so by
Aliprantis and Border (2007, Lemma 18.2)
it is also weakly measurable. It follows that the lower inverse
\psi^{\mathrm{L}}:\mathrm{Pow}(\Theta)\rightarrow\mathrm{Pow}(\mathcal{X}^{3})
satisfies
\psi^{\mathrm{L}}(\Theta)=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\neq\emptyset\}\in\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 3}
, so
U\coloneqq\psi^{\mathrm{L}}(\Theta)^{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 3}
.
Now define a non-empty correspondence
\varphi:\mathcal{X}^{3}\twoheadrightarrow\Theta
by

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\quad&\text{if }(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in U^{\mathrm{c}}\\
\Theta\quad&\text{if }(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in U.\end{cases}

Then, for any non-empty open set
V\subseteq\Theta
, we have
\psi^{\mathrm{L}}(V)\in\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 3}
, so

\displaystyle\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}(V)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\cap V\neq\emptyset\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\cap V\neq\emptyset\bigr\}\cup U=\psi^{\mathrm{L}}(V)\cup U\in\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 3}.

Since we also have
\varphi^{\mathrm{L}}(\emptyset)=\emptyset\in\mathcal{A}^{\otimes 3}
, we deduce that
\varphi
is weakly measurable, and by
Aliprantis and Border (2007, Lemma 18.3)
, its closure correspondence
\bar{\varphi}
is also weakly measurable. Therefore, by
Aliprantis and Border (2007, Theorem 18.13)
,
\bar{\varphi}
admits a measurable selector
\widehat{\theta}:\mathcal{X}^{3}\to\Theta
; in other words,
\widehat{\theta}
is measurable, with
\widehat{\theta}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\bar{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
for all
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}^{3}
. Since
\bar{\varphi}=S
, the conclusion follows.
∎

We are now in a position to complete the proof of Proposition
9
.

Proof of Proposition
9
.

The sequence
x,h(x),h^{\circ 2}(x),h^{\circ 3}(x),\ldots
is strictly decreasing and converges to zero for
x\in(0,1/2)
. It follows that there exists
k\equiv k(\delta_{-},\delta_{+})\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h^{\circ k}(\delta_{+})\leq\delta_{-}
. Moreover, since
h^{\circ k}(\cdot)
is increasing on
(0,1/2)
, we have
h^{\circ k}(\delta)\leq h^{\circ k}(\delta_{+})\leq\delta_{-}
for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{+}]
. Since
d(\cdot,\theta)
and
d(\theta,\cdot)
are continuous for all
\theta\in\Theta
, and since
g
is continuous, we have that
L
is a Carathéodory function. Hence, by iteratively applying Lemma
19

k
times, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\Theta},L)\geq\frac{1}{(2A)^{k}}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{-}\bigl(h^{\circ k}(\delta),\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes 3^{k}},L\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{(2A)^{k}}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{-}\bigl(\delta_{-},\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}^{\otimes 3^{k}},L\bigr),

Minimax risk lower bounds

In this section, we state some general techniques for minimax risk lower bounds from
Samworth and Shah (2024+)
; similar statements can be found in the books by
Tsybakov (2009)
and
Wainwright (2019)
. We work in the setting of Section
2

Lemma 21

(Le Cam’s two-point lemma)
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
,
P_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{1}}
and
P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{2}}
. Then, writing
\eta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
and
\Theta_{0}\coloneqq\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\geq\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\max_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta_{0})\geq\frac{g(\eta)}{2}\bigl\{1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{1},P_{2})\bigr\}.

Lemma 22

(Fano’s lemma)
.

Let
M\geq 2
,
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{M}\in\Theta
, and
P_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{j}}
for
j\in[M]
. Then, writing
\eta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\min_{1\leq j<k\leq M}d(\theta_{j},\theta_{k})
and
\Theta_{0}\coloneqq\{\theta_{j}:j\in[M]\}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\max_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\geq g(\eta)\biggl\{1-\frac{M^{-1}\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{X})}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathrm{KL}(P_{j},Q)+\log(2-M^{-1})}{\log M}\biggr\}.

Lemma 23

(Assouad’s lemma)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Phi\coloneqq\{0,1\}^{m}
For
\phi\in\Phi
, let
\theta_{\phi}\in\Theta
and
P_{\phi}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{\phi}}
. For
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi
, write
\phi\sim\phi^{\prime}
whenever
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
differ in precisely one coordinate, and
\phi\sim_{j}\phi^{\prime}
when that coordinate is the
j
th. Suppose now that the loss function is of the form

\displaystyle L(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}g\bigl(d_{j}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\bigr),

for
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
, where
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
are pseudo-metrics on
\Theta
with
d_{j}(\theta_{\phi},\theta_{\phi^{\prime}})\geq\alpha_{j}
whenever
\phi\sim_{j}\phi^{\prime}
, and where

is an increasing function satisfying
g(x+y)\leq A\{g(x)+g(y)\}
for all
x,y\in[0,\infty)
and some
A>0
. Then, writing
\Theta_{0}\coloneqq\{\theta_{\phi}:\phi\in\Phi\}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\max_{\theta_{0}\in\Theta_{0}}\sup_{P_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{0}}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta_{0}}}L(\widehat{\theta},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2A}\Bigl\{1-\max_{\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi:\phi\sim\phi^{\prime}}\mathrm{TV}(P_{\phi},P_{\phi^{\prime}})\Bigr\}\sum_{j=1}^{d}g(\alpha_{j}).

Appendix B
Auxiliary lemmas

Our first auxiliary lemma is a minor modification of
Chen, Gao and Ren (2018, Theorem 5.1)
that we apply in the proof of Corollary
11
. To introduce the setting, let
\mathcal{Y}
be a measurable space; for
\theta\in\Theta
, let
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})
and let
\mathcal{R}_{\Theta}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
.

Definition 24
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
. We define the
modulus of continuity
of
\mathcal{R}_{\Theta}
with respect to
\varepsilon
and
g
to be

\displaystyle\omega(\varepsilon,\mathcal{R}_{\Theta},g)\coloneqq\sup\,\biggl\{g\biggl(\frac{d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})}{2}\biggr):\;

\displaystyle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta\text{ and there exist }R_{\theta_{1}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{1}},

\displaystyle R_{\theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{2}}\text{ such that }\mathrm{TV}(R_{\theta_{1}},R_{\theta_{2}})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\biggr\}.

Lemma 25
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\theta\in\Theta
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
, let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}^{n}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\theta}\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\theta,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigl\{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{X}):\,P=(1-\varepsilon)R_{\theta}+\varepsilon Q:R_{\theta}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta}\,\text{ and }Q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})\bigr\}

denote the class of contaminated distributions. Then, for all
\delta\in(0,1/2)
,

\mathcal{M}_{-}(\delta)\geq\omega(\varepsilon,\mathcal{R}_{\Theta},g).

Proof.

We apply the same proof strategy as
Chen, Gao and Ren (2018, Theorem 5.1)
. Consider any
R_{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{1}}
and
R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{2}}
that satisfy
\mathrm{TV}(R_{1},R_{2})\leq\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in[0,\varepsilon]
such that
\mathrm{TV}(R_{1},R_{2})=\varepsilon^{\prime}/(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})
.
Next, for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, define densities

\displaystyle r_{\ell}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}R_{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}R_{1}+\mathrm{d}R_{2}}.

We also define probability distributions
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
on
\mathcal{X}
through their densities

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{1}}{\mathrm{d}R_{1}+\mathrm{d}R_{2}}\coloneqq\frac{(r_{2}-r_{1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{r_{2}\geq r_{1}\}}}{\mathrm{TV}(R_{1},R_{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{2}}{\mathrm{d}R_{1}+\mathrm{d}R_{2}}\coloneqq\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{\{r_{1}\geq r_{2}\}}}{\mathrm{TV}(R_{1},R_{2})}.

Now define
P_{\ell}\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})R_{\ell}+\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Then
P_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta_{\ell}}
for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Moreover, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{1}}{\mathrm{d}R_{1}+\mathrm{d}R_{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})r_{1}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\frac{(r_{2}-r_{1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{r_{2}\geq r_{1}\}}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}/(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})}=(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})\bigl\{r_{1}+(r_{2}-r_{1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{r_{2}\geq r_{1}\}}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})\bigl\{r_{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{\{r_{1}\geq r_{2}\}}\bigr\}=\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{2}}{\mathrm{d}R_{1}+\mathrm{d}R_{2}},

so
P_{1}=P_{2}
. Thus, by Lemma
5
,

\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}\;P\biggl\{L(\widehat{\theta},\theta)\geq g\biggl(\frac{d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})}{2}\biggr)\biggr\}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigl\{1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{1}^{\otimes n},P_{2}^{\otimes n})\bigr\}=\frac{1}{2},

where the inequality follows since
P_{1}^{\otimes n},P_{2}^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}
. Further, since
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{\Theta}
with
\mathrm{TV}(R_{1},R_{2})\leq\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)
were arbitrary, for any
t>0
, we can find
\theta^{(t)}_{1},\theta^{(t)}_{2}\in\Theta
,
R_{1}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{1}}^{(t)}
and
R_{2}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{2}}^{(t)}
such that
\mathrm{TV}(R_{1}^{(t)},R_{2}^{(t)})\leq\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)
and
g\bigl(d(\theta_{1}^{(t)},\theta_{2}^{(t)})/2\bigr)\geq\omega(\varepsilon,\mathcal{R}_{\Theta},g)-t
. Hence, by taking
t\to 0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\in\widehat{\Theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}}P\biggl\{L\big(\widehat{\theta},\,\theta\big)\geq\omega(\varepsilon,\mathcal{R}_{\Theta},g)\biggr\}\geq\frac{1}{2},

so the result follows.

Lemma
26
below applies a non-central version of the matrix Bernstein inequality
(
Tropp, 2012
;
Zhu, Wang and Samworth, 2022
)
to provide a high-probability upper bound on the operator norm of the difference between a sample covariance matrix and its population counterpart.

Lemma 26
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d\times d}
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathsf{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma)
. Then

\displaystyle\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma)^{2}\bigr\}\biggr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{i}X_{i}^{\top})^{2}\bigr\}\biggr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathbf{r}(\Sigma).

(28)

Moreover, writing
\widehat{\Sigma}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}
, if
\frac{\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\{\log(1/\delta)+\log(8d)\}}{n}\leq 1
, then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 513\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\{\log(1/\delta)+\log(8d)\}}{n}}.

(29)

Remark
.

From the bounds on the variance parameter in (
28
), we see that the upper bound in (
29
) is the tightest (up to multiplicative constants) that one can obtain from the matrix Bernstein inequality in the sub-Gaussian regime (i.e. when
\frac{\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\{\log(1/\delta)+\log(8d)\}}{n}\leq 1
). In particular, the
\log(1/\delta)
term in (
29
) appears multiplicatively with the effective rank, in stark contrast with Proposition
13
, where the
\log(1/\delta)
term appears only additively.

Proof.

Since the operator norm is invariant to orthogonal transformations, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Sigma
is diagonal, so that
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}\geq 0
. Then, writing
X_{1}=(X_{11},\ldots,X_{1d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma)^{2}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{1}X_{1}^{\top})^{2}\bigr\}-\Sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}\bigr)-\Sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\mathbb{E}(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}X_{11}^{2}),\ldots,\mathbb{E}(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}X_{1d}^{2})\bigr)-\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\lambda_{1}^{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\biggl(2\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{1}\sum_{j\neq 1}\lambda_{j},\ldots,2\lambda_{d}^{2}+\lambda_{d}\sum_{j\neq d}\lambda_{j}\biggr).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\tr(\Sigma)\leq\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma)^{2}\bigr\}\biggr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\tr(\Sigma).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\tr(\Sigma)\leq\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{i}X_{i}^{\top})^{2}\bigr\}\biggr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 3\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\tr(\Sigma),

and (
28
) follows.

For the second claim, observe that for
p\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl\{(X_{1}X_{1}^{\top})^{p}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2p-2}X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\mathbb{E}(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2p-2}X_{11}^{2}),\ldots,\mathbb{E}(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2p-2}X_{1d}^{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle\preceq\bigl\{\mathbb{E}(\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{4p-4})\bigr\}^{1/2}\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\bigl\{\mathbb{E}(X_{11}^{4})\bigr\}^{1/2},\ldots,\bigl\{\mathbb{E}(X_{1d}^{4})\bigr\}^{1/2}\bigr)

\displaystyle{\preceq}\Bigl(\frac{8}{3}\Bigr)^{p-1}\cdot(2p-2)^{p-1}\tr(\Sigma)^{p-1}\cdot 3^{1/2}\Sigma

\displaystyle{\preceq}\Bigl(\frac{16e\tr(\Sigma)}{3}\Bigr)^{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p\sqrt{p-1}}\cdot p!\cdot\Sigma

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{p!}{2}\cdot\bigl(16\tr(\Sigma)\bigr)^{p-2}\cdot 16\tr(\Sigma)\cdot\Sigma.

Here, Step
(i)
follows by recalling the sub-exponential norm of a random variable
Z
, given by
\|Z\|_{\psi_{1}}\coloneqq\inf\{t>0:\mathbb{E}(e^{|Z|/t})\leq 2\}
, so that
\bigl\|\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|X_{1j}^{2}\|_{\psi_{1}}=8\tr(\Sigma)/3
. Moreover,
(y/r)^{r}\leq e^{y}
for all
y,r\geq 0
, so

\mathbb{E}\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}}{(2p-2)\cdot 8\tr(\Sigma)/3}\biggr)^{2p-2}\biggr\}\leq\mathbb{E}\exp\biggl(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{2}^{2}}{8\tr(\Sigma)/3}\biggr)\leq 2.

Step
(ii)
follows from Stirling’s formula
(e.g.,
Robbins, 1955
, Eq. (1))
. Noting that
X_{1}X_{1}^{\top}
is positive semi-definite, we may apply a non-central version of the matrix Bernstein inequality
(
Zhu, Wang and Samworth, 2022
, Lemma 3)
to both
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
-\widehat{\Sigma}
to deduce that, for all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq t\bigr)\leq 8d\exp\biggl\{-\frac{nt^{2}}{2^{9}(\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}+t)\tr(\Sigma)}\biggr\}.

The result then follows on setting
t=(2^{9}+1)\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\{\log(1/\delta)+\log(8d)\}}{n}}
and invoking the assumption
\frac{\mathbf{r}(\Sigma)\{\log(1/\delta)+\log(8d)\}}{n}\leq 1
.
∎

The following lemma provides a lower bound on the packing number of the set of
s
-sparse vectors in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Very similar results exist in the literature (e.g.
Candès and Davenport (2013, Lemma 3)
or
Wainwright (2019, Exercise 5.8)
); we present a short, constructive proof without imposing a parity restriction on the sparsity level
s
.

Lemma 27
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in[d]
, and let
\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\|v\|_{0}\leq s\}
. There exist
\log M\geq\frac{3s}{4}\log\bigl(\frac{d}{4s}\bigr)
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{M}\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\|v_{j}-v_{k}\|_{2}>1/2
for all distinct
j,k\in[M]
.

Proof.

Let
L\coloneqq\lfloor s/4\rfloor
and let
\Omega_{1}\coloneqq\bigl\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}:\|\omega\|_{0}\leq s\bigr\}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\Omega_{j}\neq\emptyset
, fix an arbitrary
\omega_{j}\in\Omega_{j}
, let

\Omega_{j+1}\coloneqq\bigl\{\omega\in\Omega_{j}:d_{\mathrm{H}}(\omega,\omega_{j})>s/4\bigr\},

and let
M\coloneqq\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\Omega_{j}\neq\emptyset\}
. Then

|\Omega_{j}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\binom{d}{\ell}

for each
j\in[M]
. Since the sets
\{\Omega_{j}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}:j\in[M]\}
form a partition of
\Omega_{1}
, we deduce that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\binom{d}{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}|\Omega_{j}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}|\leq M\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\binom{d}{\ell}.

Hence

M\geq\frac{\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\binom{d}{\ell}}{\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\binom{d}{\ell}}\geq\frac{\binom{d}{s}}{(L+1)\binom{d}{L}}\geq\frac{(d/s)^{s}}{(L+1)(ed/L)^{L}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\log M

\displaystyle\geq s\log\Bigl(\frac{d}{s}\Bigr)-L\log\Bigl(\frac{ed}{L}\Bigr)-\log(L+1)\geq s\log\Bigl(\frac{d}{s}\Bigr)-\frac{s}{4}\log\Bigl(\frac{4ed}{s}\Bigr)-\log\Bigl(\frac{s}{4}+1\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3s}{4}\log\Bigl(\frac{d}{s}\Bigr)-\frac{s}{4}\log(4e^{2})=\frac{3s}{4}\log\Bigl(\frac{d}{(4e^{2})^{1/3}s}\Bigr)\geq\frac{3s}{4}\log\Bigl(\frac{d}{4s}\Bigr).

The points
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{M}
satisfy

d_{\mathrm{H}}(\omega_{j},\omega_{k})>s/4
for all distinct
j,k\in[M]
,
and the result follows by taking,
v_{j}=\omega_{j}/\sqrt{s}
for
j\in[M]
, so that
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
for all
j
.
∎

Recall that in the context of kernel density estimation, a
kernel
is a Borel measurable function
K:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K(u)\,du=1
. It is of
order

\ell\in\mathbb{N}
if
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u^{j}K(u)\,du=0
for all
j\in[\ell-1]
.

Lemma 28
.

Let
\beta,\gamma>0
, let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}f\in\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
K:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a kernel of order
\ell\coloneqq\lceil\beta\rceil
satisfying
\|K\|_{\infty}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\mu_{\beta}(K)\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|^{\beta}|K(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u<\infty.

There exist
c(\beta,K),C(\beta,K)>0
, depending only on
\beta,K
, and a choice of bandwidth
h\equiv h_{n,\beta,\gamma,K,\delta}
such that if
\delta\in\bigl[2e^{-c(\beta,K)\cdot n},1\bigr]
,
then with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\bigl\{\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq C(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)},

where
\widehat{f}_{n}\equiv\widehat{f}_{n,h,K}
is the kernel density estimator with bandwidth
h
and kernel
K
.

Proof.

By
Tsybakov (2009, Proposition 1.2)
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathbb{E}\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr|\leq\frac{\gamma}{(\ell-1)!}\mu_{\beta}(K)h^{\beta}.

(30)

Moreover, by
Tsybakov (2009, Proposition 1.1)
and the first display in the proof of
Samworth and Shah (2024+, Theorem 4.6)
, we have

\displaystyle\Var\bigl(K_{h}(X_{1}-x_{0})\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{h}\|f\|_{\infty}R(K)\leq\frac{1}{h}C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}R(K),

where
R(K)\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K^{2}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
and where
C_{1}(\beta)>0
depends only on
\beta
. Now by Bernstein’s inequality, we have for any
\delta\in(0,1]
that

\mathbb{P}\biggl\{\bigl|\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-\mathbb{E}\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})\bigr|>\biggl(\frac{2C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}R(K)\log(2/\delta)}{nh}\biggr)^{1/2}+\frac{\|K\|_{\infty}\log(2/\delta)}{3nh}\biggr\}\leq\delta.

Hence, when

\displaystyle\log(2/\delta)\leq nh\biggl(\frac{18C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}R(K)}{\|K\|_{\infty}^{2}}\biggr),

(31)

we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\bigl|\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-\mathbb{E}\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})\bigr|\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{2C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}R(K)\log(2/\delta)}{nh}}.

(32)

Combining (
32
) with (
30
), we deduce that when
\delta
satisfies (
31
), we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\bigl|\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-\mathbb{E}\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})\bigr|+\bigl|\mathbb{E}\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{2C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}R(K)\log(2/\delta)}{nh}}+\frac{\gamma}{(\ell-1)!}\mu_{\beta}(K)h^{\beta}.

Now set

\displaystyle C_{2}(\beta,K)\coloneqq\biggl(\frac{C_{1}(\beta)R(K)\{(\ell-1)!\}^{2}}{\mu_{\beta}^{2}(K)}\biggr)^{1/(2\beta+1)},

and

\displaystyle h\equiv h_{n,\beta,\gamma,K,\delta}\coloneqq C_{2}(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{-1/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.

Then there exist
c(\beta,K),C(\beta,K)>0
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\{\widehat{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq C(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)},

with probability at least
1-\delta
, whenever
\delta\geq 2e^{-c(\beta,K)\cdot n}
.
∎

The bandwidth in Lemma
28
depends on
\delta
, which is undesirable. The following lemma uses a Lepski type method to obtain a
\delta
-independent estimator for
f(x_{0})
. The same method was employed by
Devroye et al. (2016, Theorem 4.2)
for heavy-tailed mean estimators.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\beta,\gamma>0
, let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}f\in\mathcal{F}(\beta,\gamma)
, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
K:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a kernel of order
\ell\coloneqq\lceil\beta\rceil
satisfying
\|K\|_{\infty}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\mu_{\beta}(K)\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u|^{\beta}|K(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u<\infty.

There exists a
\delta
-independent estimator
\check{f}(x_{0})\equiv\check{f}_{n,\beta,\gamma,K}(x_{0})
and
\check{C}(\beta,K)>0
depending only on
\beta,K
such that for all
\delta\in(0,1]
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\bigl\{\check{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq\check{C}(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\cdot\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\biggr\}.

Proof.

Let
c(\beta,K)>0
be as in Lemma
28
.
First consider the case
n\leq 3\log(2)/c(\beta,K)
. We set
\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})=0
, and recall from the first display in the proof of
Samworth and Shah (2024+, Theorem 4.6)
that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}
for some
C_{1}(\beta)>0
depending only on
\beta
. Therefore, when
n\leq 3\log(2)/c(\beta,K)
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl\{\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq C_{1}(\beta)^{2}\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\leq C_{2}(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\cdot\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\biggr\},

(33)

for some
C_{2}(\beta,K)>0
depending only on
\beta
and
K
. This proves the claim for
n\leq 3\log(2)/c(\beta,K)
.

Next consider the case where
n>3\log(2)/c(\beta,K)
.
Let
\widehat{f}_{n,\delta}(x_{0})
denote the kernel density estimator of
f(x_{0})
with bandwidth
h_{n,\beta,\gamma,K,\delta}
as in Lemma
28
.
Define
k_{+}\coloneqq\lfloor c(\beta,K)\cdot n/\log(2)-1\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}
, which by assumption, satisfies
k_{+}\geq c(\beta,K)\cdot n/(2\log 2)
.
For
k\in[k_{+}]
, define the interval

\displaystyle\widehat{I}_{k}\coloneqq\Biggl[\widehat{f}_{n,2^{-k}}(x_{0})-

\displaystyle C(\beta,K)^{1/2}\cdot\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2^{k+1})}{n}\biggr)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)},

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{n,2^{-k}}(x_{0})+C(\beta,K)^{1/2}\cdot\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2^{k+1})}{n}\biggr)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}\Biggr].

By Lemma
28
, we have for each
k\in[k_{+}]
that
f(x_{0})\in\widehat{I}_{k}
with probability at least
1-2^{-k}
. Now define
\widehat{k}\coloneqq\min\bigl\{k\in[k_{+}]:\bigcap_{j=k}^{k_{+}}\widehat{I}_{j}\neq\emptyset\bigr\}
. Define the estimator

\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\inf\bigl\{\bigcap_{j=\widehat{k}}^{k_{+}}\widehat{I}_{j}\bigr\}&\text{ if }\bigcap_{j=\widehat{k}}^{k_{+}}\widehat{I}_{j}\neq\emptyset\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and set
\check{f}_{n}(x_{0})\coloneqq 0\vee\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})\wedge C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}
. For
\delta\in[2^{-(k_{+}-1)},1]
, we set
k_{\delta}\coloneqq\min\bigl\{k\in[k_{+}]:\delta\geq 2^{-(k-1)}\bigr\}
, so that
2^{k_{\delta}-2}<1/\delta\leq 2^{k_{\delta}-1}
. Then, by a union bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(f(x_{0})\in\bigcap_{j=k_{\delta}}^{k_{+}}\widehat{I}_{j}\biggr)\geq 1-\sum_{j=k_{\delta}}^{k_{+}}2^{-j}\geq 1-2^{-(k_{\delta}-1)}\geq 1-\delta.

On this event,
\bigcap_{j=k_{\delta}}^{k_{+}}\widehat{I}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, so
\widehat{k}\leq k_{\delta}
. Hence, for all
\delta\in[2^{-(k_{+}-1)},1]
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that
\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})\in\widehat{I}_{k_{\delta}}
, so that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\bigl\{\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4C(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(2^{k_{\delta}+1})}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 4C(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}.

Since
f(x_{0})\in\bigl[0,\,C_{1}(\beta)\gamma^{1/(\beta+1)}\bigr]
, it follows that when
\delta\in[2^{-(k_{+}-1)},1]
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\bigl\{\check{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq\bigl\{\widetilde{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq 4C(\beta,K)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}.

(34)

On the other hand, for
\delta\in(0,2^{-(k_{+}-1)})
,

\displaystyle\bigl\{\check{f}_{n}(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\bigr\}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq C_{1}^{2}(\beta)\cdot\gamma^{2/(\beta+1)}.

(35)

Note that when
\delta=2^{-(k_{+}-1)}
and
n>3\log(2)/c(\beta,K)
, we have that

\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(2^{k_{+}+2}\bigr)\in\biggl[\frac{c(\beta,K)}{2},\frac{4c(\beta,K)}{3}\biggr].

Thus, there exists
C_{3}(\beta,K)\geq 1
depending only on
\beta,K
such that when
\delta\in(0,2^{-(k_{+}-1)})
, we have
1\leq C_{3}(\beta,K)\bigl\{\bigl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\bigr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\bigr\}
, and when
\delta\in[2^{-(k_{+}-1)},1]
, we have

\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\leq C_{3}(\beta,K)\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}\biggr)^{2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\wedge 1\biggr\}.

The final conclusion follows by combining (
33
), (
34
) and (
35
), and setting
\check{C}(\beta,K)\coloneqq C_{2}(\beta,K)\vee\bigl[C_{3}(\beta,K)\bigl\{4C(\beta,K)\vee C_{1}^{2}(\beta)\bigr\}\bigr]
.
∎

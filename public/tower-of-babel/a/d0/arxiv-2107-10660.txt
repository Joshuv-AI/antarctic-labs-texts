Abstract
We introduce a characterisation for split graphs by using edge contraction. First, we use it to prove that any ( 2 ​ K 2 2K_{2} , claw)-free graph with α ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 \alpha(G)\geq 3 is a split graph. Next, we apply it to characterise any pseudo-split graph. Finally, by using edge contraction again, we characterise unbalanced split graphs, which we use to characterise Nordhaus–Gaddum graphs.

A

, where

A split graph,

, is called

if

has a

-partition, where
|K|=\omega(G)
and
|S|=\alpha(G)
, and

, otherwise. Based on the work in
[

]
, the following theorem appears in
[

]

]

Theorem 13

(
[
10
]
,
[

]
,
[
4
]
)
.

For any

-partition of split graph

, exactly one of the following holds:
(i)
|K|=\omega(G)
and
|S|=\alpha(G)
.

(ii)
|K|=\omega(G)-1

|S|=\alpha(G)

(iii)
|K|=\omega(G)
and
|S|=\alpha(G)-1
.

Moreover, in

-partition is unique.

Theorem 14
.

Let

. Then,

, such that
\omega(G/e)=\omega(G)-1

Proof.

Let
G
be an unbalanced split graph, then

can be partitioned into a
KS
-partition, where
|K|=\omega(G)-1
and
|S|=\alpha(G)
. For any two adjacent vertices,
u
and
v\in K
,

can be partitioned into

-partitions, where
|K^{{}^{\prime}}|=\omega(G)-2
and
|S|=\alpha(G)
. Thus,

is an unbalanced split with
\omega(G/uv)=\omega(G)-1
.

Conversely, we prove that if
G
is a balanced split, then for any edge,
e
,
G/e
is a balanced split or
\omega(G/e)=\omega(G)
. Because
G
is a balanced split, then based on Theorem
13
,

can be partitioned into the unique

-partition, where
|K|=\omega(G)
and
|S|=\alpha(G)
. For any two adjacent vertices,
u
and
v\in K

-partitions, where
|K^{{}^{\prime}}|=\omega(G)-1
and

. Thus, either

is an unbalanced split with

and

can be partitioned into

-partitions, where

∎

Hydrodynamical duals of the gravitational axial anomaly and the cosmological constant
R. V. Khakimov, G. Yu. Prokhorov, O. V. Teryaev, V. I. Zakharov

Abstract




Abstract

We construct the hydrodynamic expansion for a rotating and accelerated medium in a curved space-time, and establish a duality between the currents related to the cosmological constant and the acceleration. Then we consider the more general case with a non-zero Weyl tensor, and show the relationship between the current in flat space-time and the gravitational axial anomaly. This generalizes the previous derivation to the case with a non-zero Ricci tensor.

2
Duality of the cosmological constant and acceleration

2.1
Riemann tensor and gradient expansion

We consider an uncharged non-dissipative fluid of massless fermions, moving with four-velocity
\displaystyle u_{\mu}(x)
and characterized by proper temperature
\displaystyle T(x)
, placed in an external gravitational field with the metric
\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)
. The system is assumed to be in a state of global thermodynamic equilibrium
[
21
]
, for which the inverse temperature vector
\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}=\frac{u_{\mu}}{T}
satisfies the Killing equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\beta_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\beta_{\mu}=0\,,

(15)

which means that we work with the hydrodynamic
\displaystyle\beta
-frame. Note that the condition (
15
) is not some artificial condition and is very close to the known criteria of thermal equilibrium; we will consider this in more detail in the Discussion section. In particular, due to (
15
), we obtain for the second-order covariant derivative

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\beta_{\alpha}=-R^{\rho}_{\>\>\mu\nu\alpha}\beta_{\rho}\,.

(16)

The anti-symmetric combination of covariant derivatives forms the thermal vorticity tensor
[
34
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varpi_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\beta_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}\beta_{\mu}\right)\,,

(17)

which has one vector and one pseudo-vector component, corresponding to (‘‘thermal’’) acceleration
\displaystyle\alpha_{\mu}
and vorticity
\displaystyle w_{\mu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{\mu}=\varpi_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}\,,\quad w_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u^{\nu}\varpi^{\alpha\beta}\,,\quad\varpi_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}w^{\alpha}u^{\beta}+\alpha_{\mu}u_{\nu}-\alpha_{\nu}u_{\mu}\,.

(18)

Kinematic vorticity
\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}
is proportional to the ‘‘thermal’’ vorticity
\displaystyle w_{\mu}
, and the kinematic acceleration
\displaystyle a_{\mu}
is proportional to the ‘‘thermal’’ acceleration in the state of global equilibrium (
15
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{\mu}=\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{T}\,,\quad\alpha_{\mu}=\frac{a_{\mu}}{T}\,.

(19)

In the general case the Riemann tensor has the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}-\frac{R}{6}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}-g_{\alpha\nu}g_{\mu\beta}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(R_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}-R_{\alpha\nu}g_{\beta\mu}-R_{\beta\mu}g_{\alpha\nu}\right)\,,

(20)

where
\displaystyle C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
is the Weyl tensor.

Let us start with the simplest case when the Weyl tensor is zero and the Ricci tensor is given by the cosmological constant

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=0\,,\,R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(21)

Then the Riemann tensor can be decomposed into the components

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=\frac{\Lambda}{3}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}-g_{\alpha\nu}g_{\mu\beta}\right)\,,

(22)

which means that we consider de Sitter space-time (
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
) or anti-de Sitter space-time (
\displaystyle\Lambda<0
)
[
35
]
. Our further analysis takes place in a state of global equilibrium (
15
), so we choose static patch of dS space-time, therefore having a time-like Killing vector.

Now we construct the hydrodynamic gradient expansion for the current. The only possibility to add space-time curvature to the current expression is the scalar curvature
\displaystyle R
, which is of the second order in gradients. Then to make this term pseudovector one has to multiply it by the axial quantity
\displaystyle w_{\mu}
, which also contains a derivative and therefore the axial current will be of the third order. Thus, the curvature effects arise only in the third order in gradients. Since the Ricci tensor is proportional to the cosmological constant, this term will have the form
\displaystyle\Lambda w_{\mu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle j_{\mu}^{A(3)}=\xi_{1}(T)w^{2}w_{\mu}+\xi_{2}(T)\alpha^{2}w_{\mu}+\xi_{3}(T)(\alpha w)\alpha_{\mu}+\xi_{\Lambda}(T)\Lambda w_{\mu}\,.

(23)

We will denote the coefficient before the term with the cosmological constant as
\displaystyle\xi_{\Lambda}(T)
. The unknown coefficients
\displaystyle\xi_{n}(T)
and
\displaystyle\xi_{\Lambda}(T)
depend on the proper
temperature
\displaystyle T
.

2.2
Axial current divergence

Due to the symmetry of the chosen Riemann tensor (the Weyl tensor is zero), there is no gravitational anomaly, and the axial current in this case is conserved

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{A}=0\,.

(24)

The covariant derivatives of the kinematic variables can be found from (
15
), (
16
) and the condition
\displaystyle u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla_{\mu}T=T^{2}\alpha_{\mu}\,,\\
\nabla_{\mu}w_{\nu}=T\left(-g_{\mu\nu}(\alpha w)+\alpha_{\mu}w_{\nu}\right)\,,\\
\nabla_{\mu}\alpha_{\nu}=T\left(w^{2}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)-\alpha^{2}u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-w_{\mu}w_{\nu}-u_{\mu}\eta_{\nu}-u_{\nu}\eta_{\mu}\right)-\frac{1}{T}\frac{\Lambda}{3}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)\,,\end{cases}

(25)

where
\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u^{\nu}w^{\rho}\alpha^{\sigma}
.

An important consequence of considering the system in global thermodynamic equilibrium is that hydrodynamics is defined only in terms of the first gradients of velocity (acceleration and vorticity), and all higher derivatives are expressed through the lower ones. This is clearly seen from the system (
25
). Now, substituting (
23
) into (
24
), we obtain an expression for the axial current divergence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu(3)}_{A}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\alpha w)w^{2}\left(T^{2}\xi_{1}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{1}+2T\xi_{3}\right)+(\alpha w)\alpha^{2}\left(T^{2}\xi_{2}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{2}+T^{2}\xi_{3}^{\prime}-T\xi_{3}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\Lambda(\alpha w)\left(T^{2}\xi_{\Lambda}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{\Lambda}-\frac{4}{3T}\xi_{3}-\frac{2}{3T}\xi_{2}\right)=0\,.

The pseudo-scalar terms before each bracket in this expression are independent, so we can equate all expressions in brackets to zero and obtain a system of differential equations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}T^{2}\xi_{1}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{1}+2T\xi_{3}=0\,,\\
T^{2}\xi_{2}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{2}+T^{2}\xi_{3}^{\prime}-T\xi_{3}=0\,,\\
T^{2}\xi_{\Lambda}^{\prime}-3T\xi_{\Lambda}-\frac{4}{3T}\xi_{3}-\frac{2}{3T}\xi_{2}=0\,.\end{cases}

(27)

Based on dimensional analysis (in a massless theory without boundary conditions, etc., temperature
\displaystyle T
is the only dimensional parameter), we move on to the dimensionless coefficients
\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{1}=T^{3}\lambda_{1},\quad\xi_{2}=T^{3}\lambda_{2},\quad\xi_{3}=T^{3}\lambda_{3},\quad\xi_{\Lambda}=T\lambda_{\Lambda}\,.

(28)

And left with the system (
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}
remains arbitrary)

\begin{cases}\lambda_{3}=0\,,\\
\lambda_{\Lambda}=-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{3}\,.\end{cases}

(29)

The first equality in (
29
) provides the conservation of the axial current in flat space-time in the absence of external fields
[
26
]
.

The phenomena analyzed in this paper, from the point of view of conservation laws, can also be derived directly from the equilibrium perturbation theory
[
34
,
26
]
, where they are determined by the effective interaction with boost operator (in the case of acceleration) and with operator of angular momentum (in the case of vorticity). In particular, in this way the coefficients
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{3}
were obtained in flat space-time for massless spin 1/2 field (
9
).
The key is the second of the relations (
29
). The factor
\displaystyle\lambda_{\Lambda}
cannot be obtained in the same way from the density operator in flat space-time, but we can obtain it simply using the duality (
29
) from the flat space transport coefficient
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}
. In particular, using (
9
) and (
29
), we obtain the following expression for the current of massless Dirac fields

\displaystyle\displaystyle j_{A}^{\mu(3)}=\left(\ -\frac{\omega^{2}}{24\pi^{2}}-\frac{a^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}+\frac{R}{96\pi^{2}}\right)\omega^{\mu}\,,

(30)

where we used
\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{R}{4}
.
Thus, we obtained a new term in the axial current proportional to the scalar curvature in the hydrodynamic gradient expansion. This current has the same conductivity as the acceleration term, according to (
29
), which adds an additional element to the hydrodynamic/gravitational duality. We will also return to this issue in the Discussion section. The same expression for the axial current in AdS space-time was obtained by another method in
[
36
]
.

Anomalous transport: gravitational anomaly and cosmological constant

3.1
Riemann tensor and gradient expansion

In this section we consider the more general case of space-time with a non-zero Weyl tensor and generalize the derivation of
[
9
]
to the case with a non-zero Ricci tensor proportional to the cosmological constant. Our starting point will be a general decomposition for the Riemann tensor
[
9

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{\mu}u_{\alpha}A_{\nu\beta}+u_{\nu}u_{\beta}A_{\mu\alpha}-u_{\nu}u_{\alpha}A_{\mu\beta}-u_{\mu}u_{\beta}A_{\nu\alpha}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}u^{\rho}\left(u_{\alpha}B^{\lambda}_{\>\beta}-u_{\beta}B^{\lambda}_{\>\alpha}\right)+

(31)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\lambda\rho}u^{\rho}\left(u_{\mu}B^{\lambda}_{\>\nu}-u_{\nu}B^{\lambda}_{\>\mu}\right)+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\eta\sigma}u^{\rho}u^{\sigma}C^{\lambda\eta}\,.

The tensors
\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}

C_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\eta\rho}\epsilon_{\beta\nu\lambda\gamma}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}R^{\eta\rho\lambda\gamma}\,.\end{cases}

(32)

These tensors have properties

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}=A_{\nu\mu},\quad C_{\mu\nu}=C_{\nu\mu}\,,\quad B^{\mu}_{\mu}=0\,,\quad A_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=C_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=B_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=B_{\nu\mu}u^{\nu}=0\,.

The tensors
\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}
and
\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed into a traceless and a trace parts, and the pseudotensor
\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed into symmetric and anti-symmetric parts

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{A}{3}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{C}{3}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle B^{s}_{\mu\nu}+B^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\mu}^{\mu}=\widetilde{C}_{\mu}^{\mu}=0\,,\quad\widetilde{A}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=\widetilde{C}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=B^{s}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=B^{a}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}=0\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{\mu}=A\,,\quad C_{\mu}^{\mu}=C\,,\quad B^{s}_{\mu\nu}=B^{s}_{\nu\mu}\,,\quad B^{a}_{\mu\nu}=-B^{a}_{\nu\mu}\,.

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}=g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\left(A+C\right)+\left(A_{\mu\nu}+C_{\mu\nu}\right)-Cg_{\mu\nu}+B^{\lambda\alpha}u^{\rho}\left(u_{\nu}\epsilon_{\alpha\lambda\mu\rho}+u_{\mu}\epsilon_{\alpha\lambda\nu\rho}\right)\,.

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}=-C_{\mu\nu}\,,\quad A^{\mu}_{\mu}=0\,,\quad B_{\mu\nu}=B_{\nu\mu}\,.

Discussion

4.1
Thermodynamic equilibrium

From the very beginning we used the assumption that the system is in the so-called global thermodynamic equilibrium, which corresponds to the entropy maximum (as well as invariance of the density operator with respect to the choice of hypersurface)
[
21
]
. Beyond this equilibrium our results may be unfair (or give only zero order contribution). We will discuss the global thermodynamic equilibrium in more detail.

A consequence of this equilibrium is that the inverse temperature vector should be the Killing vector (
15
). Since its square
\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}\beta^{\mu}=T^{-2}>0
is related to the temperature, this vector should be time-like and also future-directed, since
\displaystyle\beta_{0}=u_{0}/T>0

[
41
]
.
The spaces containing timelike Killing vector are well known stationary spaces. In particular, any static space-time whose components do not depend on time is stationary. Thus, our derivation, is valid only for stationary spaces. In Section
2
we considered the case of (A)dS spaces. For (A)dS space we can choose a static metric

\displaystyle\displaystyle ds^{2}=\left(1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}\right)dt^{2}-\frac{1}{1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}}dr^{2}-r^{2}\left(d\Theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\Theta\,d\phi^{2}\right)\,.

(56)

where the positive sign
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
corresponds to the metrics of a static patch in de Sitter space-time, and
\displaystyle\Lambda<0
corresponds to static coordinates of the anti-de Sitter space-time.

Not all types of space-times are stationary and, accordingly, global equilibrium is not always possible. In particular, it is not obvious whether global equilibrium is possible in the expanding Universe, since, for example the Friedman metrics clearly depends on time (but local thermodynamic equilibrium should be possible
[
42
]
).

The conditions of global equilibrium (
15
) are consistent with other known criteria of thermodynamic equilibrium. In particular, there is a well-known Tolman-Ehrenfest thermodynamic equilibrium criterion
[
43
,
44
]
, according to which the temperature in space with the metric
\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x})
is distributed according to the law

\displaystyle\displaystyle T(\mathbf{x})=\frac{T_{0}}{\sqrt{g_{00}(\mathbf{x})}}\,,

(57)

which for stationary space-times can be written in covariant form
[
45
,
44
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle T(\mathbf{x})=\frac{T_{0}}{\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x})\xi^{\mu}(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})\xi^{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}}\,,

(58)

where
\displaystyle\xi^{\mu}
is a dimensionless Killing vector. It is clear, that (
58
) is in full agreement with the global equilibrium, according to which the temperature is also defined in exactly the same way by the Killing vector as
\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}\beta^{\mu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}\beta^{\mu}\beta^{\nu}=T^{-2}
.

Also, it is easy to see that in global equilibrium (
15
) the dissipative contribution to the stress-energy tensor vanishes, which also corresponds to equilibrium
[
46
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{d}iss}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\eta(\nabla_{\mu}u_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}u_{\mu}-u_{\mu}u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\nu}-u_{\nu}u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\mu})-\left(\zeta-\frac{2}{3}\eta\right)\nabla^{\alpha}u_{\alpha}(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu})

(59)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\eta\left[T(\nabla_{\mu}\beta_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\beta_{\mu})+u_{\mu}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\nu}T}{T}-a_{\nu}\right)+u_{\nu}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mu}T}{T}-a_{\mu}\right)\right]+

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left(\zeta-\frac{2}{3}\eta\right)\left(T\nabla_{\alpha}\beta^{\alpha}+\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}T}{T}u^{\alpha}\right)(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu})=0\,.

The same, of course, applies to dissipative effects in a current.

Thus, the conditions of global equilibrium look like a fairly general and natural assumption about the properties of the system. Note also that, generally speaking, equilibrium can lead to additional restrictions on the external field, as was in the case with the electromagnetic field
[

4.3
Duality between hydrodynamics and gravity

The result we obtained continues to develop the entropy approach to gravity, which explains the effects of general relativity using the statistical properties
[
52
]
. Actually, the possibility of such a dual description suggests that the effects in gravitational fields should be accompanied by similar partner-phenomena within statistical approaches.

There are a number of examples where such a relationship has been revealed, and the effects of gravity appear essentially in the limit of zero Newton’s constant or zero curvature. This includes, for example, the well-known Unruh effect
[
49

Our result adds more elements to this duality. Indeed, on the one hand, there is an essentially gravitational effect - a gravitational chiral anomaly (

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\neq 0

4.4
Quantum or classical?

The conclusion we presented is completely unrelated to quantum theory calculations, which could create the illusion of the classical nature of the current (
54

The quantumness of (

\displaystyle a^{2}\omega_{\mu}

5
Conclusion

In this paper we generalize the previously obtained kinematic vortical effect
[
9
]
to the case of a nonzero Ricci tensor, proportional to the cosmological constant. We obtained not only the conservation of a previous result for the KVE, but also an entirely new term in the axial current, generated by the scalar curvature in a vortical fluid. Analyzing the conservation of axial current, we relate this new term with the transport coefficient of the purely kinematic quadratic acceleration term.

We discover relationship between kinematic and gravitational effects, expressed in a system of equations connecting transport coefficients of two types, which indicates the gravitational-hydrodynamic duality and confirms the equivalence principle in the higher orders of gradient expansion. This relationship allows one by considering kinematic effects in a relativistic fluid (for example, in a quark-gluon plasma, in which sufficiently large vorticity and acceleration can be generated), to simulate quantum field effects in the gravitational field in the absence of real strong gravity.

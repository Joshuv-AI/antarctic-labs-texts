Low Morse index for the systole function
Changjie Chen

Abstract
. We show that for any k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 , all critical points for the systole function and the sys_T \syst functions in ℳ g , n \mathcal{M}_{g,n} have index at least k k , for all but finitely many pairs ( g , n ) (g,n) . This implies that the lowest Morse index can be bounded from below by a positive number that goes to infinity as the dimension of the moduli space increases. We conclude by Morse theory that all the rational (co)homology of the Deligne-Mumford compactification ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} of degree at most k k comes from the boundary ∂ ℳ g , n \partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n} for all but finitely many pairs ( g , n ) (g,n) . In particular, we show that there exists a universal constant C > 0 C>0 , such that the lowest Morse index is at least C ​ log ⁡ log ​ ( − χ + 1 ) C\log\log(-\chi+1) . Developing the theorems, we also obtain an explicit upper bound on the cardinality of a j j -system on a ( g , n ) (g,n) -surface.



1.
Introduction

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
of Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
punctures is a fundamental and popular object in many areas to study. It encodes information in algebraic geometry, differential geometry, and topology, bridging several mathematical branches, and has rich algebraic, topological, and geometric structures.

The
systole
function takes the value on a hyperbolic surface
X
of the length of a shortest closed geodesic. Akrout showed in
[
1
]
that the systole function is topologically Morse on any moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. This gives hope for the use of Morse theory on moduli spaces. However, this idea is not realizable, because the function is not differentiable and the base space is not compact. See
[
11
]
for Morse theory.

In
[
4
]
, the author introduces a family of functions:

\syst(X):=-T\log\sum_{\gamma\text{ s.c.g. on }X}e^{-\frac{1}{T}l_{\gamma}(X)},

where s.c.g. stands for simple closed geodesic. The
\syst
functions are Morse on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and behave well with the Weil-Petersson metric, cf. Theorem
2.5
, allowing one to apply Morse theory to study
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

It has also been shown that in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, the set of
\sys
-critical points, the set of
\syst
-critical points, and the set of
eutactic
points, are canonically isomorphic. Therefore, one can unify the term
index
for critical points for either function or for a eutactic point.

In
[
15
]
, Schmutz Scaller constructed a critical point of index
2g-1
in every
\mathcal{M}_{g,0}
and took the guess that the lowest index of the systole function on
\mathcal{M}_{g,0}
is
2g-1
. This was disproved by Fortier Bourque in
[
5
]
, by showing the existence of
g>0
, and a critical point in
\mathcal{M}_{g,0}
, of index at most
\epsilon g
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
.

The first goal of this paper is to prove that the lowest index can be bounded from below by a positive number that goes to infinity as the dimension of the moduli space, or negative Euler characteristic
-\chi
of the surfaces, goes to infinity.

Theorem 1.1

(=
Theorem
5.6
)
.

For any
k\geq 0
, with finitely many exceptions on
(g,n)
, all critical points have Morse index greater than
k
in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, for the systole function or the
\syst
functions.

Equivalently, by properties of the
\syst
functions, all low index critical points of
\syst

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
comes from the boundary

. More precisely, the inclusion
i\colon\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
induces an isomorphism
i_{*}\colon H_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
between low degree (co)homology groups, cf.
[

.

The second goal is to compute for such a lower bound as above.

Theorem 1.2

(=

)
.

There exists a universal constant

such that the lowest index of the systole function and
\syst

is at least

.

We will show a general result on the rank of a set of (Weil-Petersson) gradient vectors of geodesic-length functions, which eventually implies the first theorem above by eutacticity and is also interesting in its own right.

Let

Definition 2.2

(Eutacticity)

A point

\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S(X)}

\spn\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S(X)}

Definition 2.3

(Topological Morse function, cf.
[
12
]
)
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional manifold and

a continuous function.

(1) A point

is called (

-)

if on a

-chart around

is a coordinate function, otherwise it is called (

-) critical.

(2) A

-chart

In this case the

of

is defined to be

.

(3) A continuous function is called

Theorem 2.4

(
[

]
)
.

The systole function is topologically Morse on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
for any
(g,n)
.
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is a critical point if and only if

is eutactic, and in this case the index is equal to
\rank\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S(X)}
.

We have the following theorem on the
\syst

Theorem 2.5

(
[
4
]
)
.

(I) For sufficiently small
T>0
,
\syst
is a
C^{2}
-Morse function on the Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

(II) In
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
,
\syst
-critical points and eutactic points are in pairs, in each of which the two points, say

and

, and the Morse index of

is equal to the eutactic rank of

.

(III) Let

be a boundary stratum of

that is canonically isomorphic to the product

of moduli spaces, and

(2)

And in this case,

(IV) The Weil-Petersson gradient flow of

Remark 2.6
.

The rank statement in both the theorem on
\sys
and
\syst
is what we will use to estimate the index at a critical point.

In particular, (III) gives a description of the critical points in the Deligne-Mumford boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, according to which we can decompose such a critical point into smaller surfaces that are critical points in their respective moduli spaces. We can also construct a critical point by connecting critical points in any moduli space by nodes. As a result, the study of critical points in the Deligne-Mumford compactification can be reduced to each smaller moduli space.

Since the Morse index is compatible with the stratification, the result on low index critical points in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
comes with the result on low index critical points in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, which by Morse theory, gives the consequent (co)homological conclusion on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, see
[
4
]
.

The definition is valid in terms of uniqueness of such minimal subsurface. If two subsurfaces

Definition 3.4

Let

(1)

(2)

Lemma 3.6

Let

Proof.

Note that the boundary of any complementary region is a path in the graph of the curves in

is not connected, regluing the complementary regions, one gets a disconnected surface.

Essentiality of subsurfaces

In the moduli space

A

Here is a slightly different setting. Start with a curve set

on

,

, with

, then possibly under the condition that

\rank\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S_{1}}<\rank\{\nabla l_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in S_{2}}.

5.
Critical points of the Morse functions

Recall that given a hyperbolic surface
X
,
S(X)
denotes the set of shortest closed geodesics on it. Later we may extend the definition of
S(Y)
to a surface
Y
with boundary, or a subsurface
Y
, by including boundary curves when looking for the shortest.

As a set of curves,
S(X)
satisfies certain properties for combinatorial and geometric reasons. We say that a curve on a surface bounds two cusps, if they together form a pair of pants.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in S(X)
, then
i(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\leq 2
, namely,
S(X)
is a 2-system. If
i(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=2
, then at least one of them bounds two cusps.

This is a well-known fact about systole. For proof, one may see
[
7
]
.

Remark 5.2
.

S(X)
is a 1-system when
n(X)=0,1
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Suppose
S(X)
is filling, then
\gamma\in S(X)
is separating if and only if it bounds two cusps.

Lemma 5.4
.

If two distinct geodesics
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
bound the same two cusps on a surface
X
, then
i(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\geq 4
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, since
\gamma_{i}
bounds two cusps, say
p
and
q
, either separates the surface into two parts. Consider
p
and
q
as two marked points on the surface for topological constructions. Let
X_{i}
denote the closed
[0,3]
-subsurface bounded by
\gamma_{i}
that contains the cusps
p
and
q
, then
p,q\in X_{1}\cap X_{2}
. Note that
X_{1}\cap X_{2}
is not path connected, as
p
and
q
cannot be joined by a path in the interior; otherwise, both
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
contract to that path. Since the boundary of the path component containing
p
or
q
is contributed by both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, it contains at least two intersections of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
. The lemma follows.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

For distinct
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in S(X)
, they cannot bound the same two cusps.

Per Remark
2.6

4.8
onto

which is a 2-system, for a hyperbolic surface

6.
Bounding the lowest Morse index

In this section, we compute a lower bound for the lowest index of the systole function on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

\syst

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

Let the

\kiss(X)
of a surface

to bound the

of a subsurface

Lemma 6.1

For a
[g,n]
-surface

\#S(Y)\leq g(4g+2n-3)^{2}+2g+n-3+\binom{n}{2}.

Proof.

Let
S^{2}(X)
be the subset of
S(X)
consisting of curves that bound two cusps, and
S^{1}(X)
the complement. Note that
S^{1}(X)
is a 1-system, and by Lemma
5.1
and Lemma

, we have
\#S^{2}(X)\leq\binom{n}{2}
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that the lowest index of the systole function and
\syst
functions on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is at least
C\log\log(-\chi+1)
.

Proof.

We use the same induction argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
. By Corollary
7.1
in the next section, set
r_{2}=3
. Suppose
r_{k}
is given as in Theorem
4.8
. We will pick

r_{k+1}>\max_{S_{k}\subset S(X),\#S_{k}=r_{k}}\kiss(\overline{\ssh}(S_{k})).

Note that for any

with
\#S_{k}=r_{k}

\#^{p}\ssh(S_{k})\leq 2\binom{r_{k}}{2}

and

\#^{p}\overline{\ssh}(S_{k})\leq 2\#^{p}\ssh(S_{k})+2.

For

, by Lemma
6.1
, we have

\max_{S_{k}}\kiss(\overline{\ssh}(S_{k}))<\frac{1}{2}(r_{k}^{2}+2)(4r_{k}^{2}+5)^{2}+2r_{k}^{2}+1+\frac{1}{2}(r_{k}^{2}+2)^{2}<Cr_{k}^{6}.

We set

On the other hand, for a critical point

and then

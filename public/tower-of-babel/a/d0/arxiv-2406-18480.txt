Hook length biases for self-conjugate partitions and partitions with distinct odd parts
Catherine H. Cossaboom

Abstract
. We establish a hook length bias between self-conjugate partitions and partitions of distinct odd parts, demonstrating that there are more hooks of fixed length t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 among self-conjugate partitions of n n than among partitions of distinct odd parts of n n for sufficiently large n n . More precisely, we derive asymptotic formulas for the total number of hooks of fixed length t t in both classes. This resolves a conjecture of Ballantine, Burson, Craig, Folsom, and Wen.

Introduction

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
of an integer
n\geq 0
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers

\lambda
has size

be the set of all partitions of

to count the number of partitions of

Given two sets of combinatorial objects that enjoy a bijection, one may naively suppose that their arithmetic statistics are equal, at least asymptotically. In the theory of partitions, restricted classes of partitions offer settings where that initial assumption is definitively false. Despite a natural bijection between two classes of partitions, hook numbers of fixed size can be more frequently found in one class than another.

Here, we consider

or

In this paper, we establish hook length biases for two other restricted classes which possess a natural bijection: partitions with distinct odd parts, denoted
\mathcal{DO}(n)

F_{a,b,c}(e^{-z})\sim\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{a,b,c}(0;x)dx=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-ax}}{(1+e^{-2x})^{c}}

since it can be shown analytically that
\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{a,b,c}(z;x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{a,b,c}(0;x)dx.
∎

To simplify notation, define
I(a,c):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-ax}}{(1+e^{-2x})^{c}}dx
. For
t
even, we define

(5)

\beta_{t}^{*}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-4)/6\rfloor}\binom{\frac{t-2k-2}{2}}{2k+1}I\left(2(2k+2),\frac{t-2k}{2}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-2)/6\rfloor}\binom{\frac{t-2k-2}{2}}{2k}I\left(2(2k+1),\frac{t-2k}{2}\right).

For
t
odd, we define

(6)

\beta_{t}^{*}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-1)/6\rfloor}\binom{\frac{t-2k-1}{2}}{2k}I\left(2(2k+1),\frac{t-2k+1}{2}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-5)/6\rfloor}\binom{\frac{t-2k-3}{2}}{2k+1}I\left(2(2k+2),\frac{t-2k-1}{2}\right).

Given this value of
\beta^{*}_{t}
, we have the desired result.

4.1.
A Variation of Wright’s Circle Method

Here, we recall a result of Ngo and Rhoades
[
20
]
, which is a modern formulation of Wright’s circle method
[
24
]
.

In 1971, Wright adapted Hardy and Ramanujan’s circle method
[
17
]
to produce asymptotics for the coefficients of a
q
-series
F(q)
which do not necessarily have a modular transformation law.
F(q)
need only have a “main” singularity at
q=1
and satisfy suitable analytic properties. Given a circle
\mathcal{C}
of radius less than
1
, we define a
major arc
as the region where
F(q)
is large, which is
C^{\prime}=C\cap R_{\Delta}
, and the
minor arc
as
C\setminus C^{\prime}
. The integral taken over
C^{\prime}
constitutes the main term for the coefficients of
F(q)
, while the integral taken over
C\setminus C^{\prime}
constitutes the error term.

Ngo and Rhoades
[
20
, Proposition 1.8]
proved the following, which demonstrates asymptotics for a wide class of functions of the form
L\xi
where
L
is asymptotically “of polynomial size” and
\xi
grows exponentially, with a primary exponential singularity at
1
. We recall this result below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be fixed. Suppose that
c(n)
are integers defined by
\sum_{n\geq 0}c(n)q^{n}=L(q)\xi(q)

for analytic functions
L,\xi
within the unit disk satisfying the following hypotheses for
z=x+iy
with
x>0
,
0\leq|y|\leq\pi
:

(H1)

As
|z|\rightarrow 0
in the bounded cone
R_{\Delta}
as defined in Proposition
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle{L(e^{-z})\sim\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}z^{k}}

for
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
,

(H2)

As
|z|\rightarrow 0
in
R_{\Delta}
, we have

\displaystyle{\xi(e^{-z})=Ke^{A/z}\left(1+O_{\theta}(e^{-B/z})\right)}

for
K,A\geq 0
and
B>A
,

(H3)

As
|z|\rightarrow 0
outside of
R_{\Delta}
, we have

\displaystyle{L(e^{-z})\ll_{\theta}|z|^{-C}}

for some
C>0
, and

(H4)

As
|z|\rightarrow 0
outside of
R_{\Delta}
, we have

\displaystyle{|\xi(e^{-z})|\ll_{\Delta}\xi(|e^{-z}|)e^{-\varepsilon/\Real(z)}}

for some
\varepsilon>0
, depending on
\Delta
.

Then as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have that

c(n)=Ke^{2\sqrt{An}}n^{-1/4}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}p_{r}n^{-r/2}+O\left(n^{-N/2}\right)\right),

where
p_{r}:=\sum_{j=0}^{r}a_{j}c_{j,r-j}
with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
c_{j,r}:=\displaystyle{\frac{\left(-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{A}}\right)^{r}\sqrt{A}^{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2}+r)}{r!\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2}-r)}}
.

In this section, we set
q:=e^{-z}
for
z=x+iy
with
x>0
,
0\leq|y|\leq\pi
. Let
\xi(q):=(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}
,
K_{t}(q):=A^{*}_{t}(q)/(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}
, and
L_{t}(q):=B^{*}_{t}(q)/(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}
, so we have
A_{t}^{*}(q)=K_{t}(q)\xi(q)
and
B_{t}^{*}(q)=L_{t}(q)\xi(q)
. In the following, we bound the major and minor arcs for
\xi(q)
,
K_{t}(q)
, and
L_{t}(q)
.

4.2.

Proposition
3.1
implies Lemma

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
t
and
\Delta>0
, as

Lemma

concerns the region outside of

.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every

and

, as

outside

, we have

Proof.

has a convergent Laurent series near

for all

4.3.
Major and Minor Arc Computations for
L_{t}(q)

Lemma
4.4
follows from Proposition
3.2
, which demonstrates that
K_{t}(q)
is asymptotic to a rational function for every

.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
t
and
\Delta>0
, as
z\rightarrow 0
in
R_{\Delta}
, we have

L_{t}(q)\sim\frac{\beta_{t}^{*}}{z}.

We now bound
|L_{t}(q)|
outside of
R_{\Delta}
. This follows from the fact that
L_{t}^{*}(q)
is nearly rational, where we keep track of the obstruction to rationality in Proposition
4.5
. We follow a method of
[
11
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

We prove the following.

(1)

When
t
is a multiple of
3
,
L_{t}^{*}(q)
is rational in
q
.

(2)

When
t
is not a multiple of
3
, we can express
L^{*}_{t}(q)
as

L^{*}_{t}(q)=\widetilde{L}^{*}_{t}(q)-q^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{3j+\alpha}}{1-q^{2j+\alpha}}

where
\widetilde{L}^{*}_{t}(q)
is rational in
q
,
K
depends only on
t
, and
\alpha=\lceil\frac{t}{3}\rceil
.

Proof.

Consider the sum

F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{q^{2m(2k+a)}}{(-q^{2(m+b)+1},q^{2})}_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}}.

Note that
a
,
b
, and
c
play different roles than in
F_{a,b,c}(q)
, as we require both more precision in the powers of
q
and explicit dependences on
t
and
k
.

Thus, we express
L_{t}^{*}(q)
as

(7)

L_{t}^{*}(q)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-4)/6\rfloor}q^{t+4k^{2}+4k}\binom{\frac{t-2k-2}{2}}{2k}_{q^{2}}F^{\prime}_{t,k,2,0,0}(q)+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-2)/6\rfloor}q^{t+4k^{2}+4k+1}\binom{\frac{t-2k-2}{2}}{2k}_{q^{2}}F^{\prime}_{t,k,1,1,0}(q)}\text{ for }t\text{ even,}\\
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-1)/6\rfloor}q^{t+4k^{2}-4k}\binom{\frac{t-2k-1}{2}}{2k}_{q^{2}}F^{\prime}_{t,k,1,0,1}(q)+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(t-5)/6\rfloor}q^{t+k^{2}+6k+3}\binom{\frac{t-2k-2}{2}}{2k}_{q^{2}}F^{\prime}_{t,k,2,1,-1}(q)}\text{ for }t\text{ odd.}\end{cases}

As in
[
2
, Ch. 3]
, we have the following identity:

(8)

\frac{1}{(-q^{2m+1},q^{2})_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+j-1}{j}_{q^{2}}(-q^{2m+1})^{j}.

By applying (
8
), rearranging, and using geometric series formulas, we express
F^{\prime}_{t,a,b,c}(q)
as follows:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)=\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m(2k+a)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1}{j}_{q^{2}}(-1)^{j}(q^{2(m+b)+1})^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1}{j}_{q^{2}}\frac{(-q^{2b+1})^{j}}{1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\Big(1-q^{2\left({\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1}\right)}\Big)\cdots(1-q^{2(j+1)})}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}-1}}\frac{(-q^{2b+1})^{j}}{1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}}.

We now consider a term in the above sum for a fixed value of
j
. We take two cases, based on whether the term
1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}
cancels with a corresponding term in the numerator.

Case 1:
Suppose that
k\leq\frac{t-2a+c-2}{6}
, implying that
j+2k+a\leq\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1
. For all uses of
F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)
in (
7
), we have that
a\geq 1
, and in turn, that
j+2k+a\geq j+1
. Thus, we have that
1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}
is cancelled out with a factor in the numerator. We find that

\binom{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1}{j}_{q^{2}}\frac{(-q^{2b+1})^{j}}{1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}}=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}-1}}\sum_{s=b}^{b+\frac{t-2k-c}{2}-1}P_{s}(t,k,a,b,c;q^{2})q^{(2s+1)j},

where
P_{s}(t,k,a,c;q)
are polynomials in
q^{2}
which are independent of
j
. Summing over all
j
allows us to write
F^{\prime}_{t,a,b,c}
as a finite sum of geometric series, producing

F^{\prime}_{t,a,b,c}(q)=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}-1}}\sum_{s=b}^{b+\frac{t-2k-c}{2}-1}\frac{P_{s}(t,k,a,b,c;q^{2})}{1-q^{2s+1}}.

Thus,
F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)
is a finite sum of expressions which are rational in
q
.

Case 2:
Suppose that
k>\frac{t-2b+c-2}{6}
, so
j+2k+a>\frac{t-2k+c}{2}
. Here,
1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}
does not cancel out with a term in the numerator. We consider the specific cases of
F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)
that appear in (
7
).

Suppose first that
(a,b,c)=(2,0,0)
, so we have that
k>\frac{t-2a+c-2}{6}=\frac{t-6}{6}
. As in (
7
), this sum only appears at
k
where
k\leq\frac{t-4}{6}
. Since
t
is even, both conditions are satisfied only when
k=\frac{t-4}{6}
. Similarly, both conditions are satisfied only when
k=\frac{t-2}{6}
for when
(a,b,c)=(1,1,0)
,
k=\frac{t-1}{6}
for
(a,b,c)=(1,0,1)
, and
k=\frac{t-5}{6}
when
(a,b,c)=(2,1,-1)
. Each of these
k
values only occurs when
t
is a distinct residue mod
6
that is not divisible by
3
. Therefore, when
3\mid t
,
L_{t}^{*}(q)
is rational. The relevant value of
k
is always equal to
\frac{t-2a+c}{6}
. When
k=\frac{t-2a+c}{6}
, we obtain

\binom{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}+j-1}{j}_{q^{2}}\frac{(-q^{2b+1})^{j}}{1-q^{2(j+2k+a)}}=\frac{\Big(1-q^{2\left({\frac{t+a+c}{3}+j-1}\right)}\Big)\cdots(1-q^{2(j+1)})}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}-1}}\frac{(-q^{2b+1})^{j}}{1-q^{2\left(j+\frac{t+a+c}{3}\right)}}.

We define the expression
\displaystyle{T_{j}(b,d,x;q):=(-1)^{j}\frac{x^{b}q^{j}(1-xq^{2})(1-xq^{4})\cdots(1-xq^{2d})}{1-xq^{2(d+1)}}}
, and we get

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\frac{t-2k+c}{2}-1}}\sum_{j\geq 0}T_{j}\left(b,\frac{t+a+c}{3}-1,q^{2j};q\right).

We define
Q(x,q)
and
R(q)
as polynomials of
x
so that

T_{j}(b,d,x;q)=(-1)^{j}\left(Q(x,q)+\frac{R(q)}{1-xq^{2(d+1)}}\right).

Considering
x\rightarrow 0
, we find that
R(q)=-Q(0,q)
. Further, we evaluate

(9)

R(q)=x^{b}q^{j}(1-xq^{2})(1-xq^{4})\cdots(1-xq^{2d})|_{x=q^{-2(d+1)}}=\frac{q^{j}(-1)^{d}}{q^{(d+2b)(d+1)}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{d}.

Letting
d=\frac{t+a+c}{3}-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{t,k,a,b,c}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{d}}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\left(Q(q^{2j},q)+R(q)+\frac{R(q)q^{2j+d+1}}{1-q^{2j+d+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{d}}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\left(Q(q^{2j},q)-Q(0,q)+\frac{R(q)q^{2j+d+1}}{1-q^{2j+d+1}}\right).

Each term of the polynomial
Q(q^{2j},q)-Q(0,q)
corresponds to a geometric series in
\sum_{j\geq 0}Q(q^{2j},q)
. It then suffices to show the remaining terms corresponding to
R(q)
are rational. By (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{R(q)}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{d}}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{q^{2j+d+1}}{1-q^{2j+d+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{q^{(d+2b)(d+1)}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(-q)^{3j+d+1}}{1-q^{2j+d+1}}.

Suppose
3\nmid t
. In (
7
), the binomial coefficient corresponding to
k=\frac{t-2a+c}{6}
is
1
. Thus, we have

L_{t}^{*}(q)=\widetilde{L}_{t}^{*}(q)-q^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{3j+d+1}}{1-q^{2j+d+1}}

where
\widetilde{L}_{t}^{*}(q)
is rational and
K
is the difference of the power on the leading
q
-term in the expression (a polynomial in
k
and
t
) and
(d+2b)(d+1)
. Each of
k,b,d
is determined as a function of
t
: namely, a linear expression in
t
determined by the residue of
t
mod
6
. Determining the value of
\frac{t+a+c}{3}
for each residue of
t
mod
6
then returns the result. ∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
t
, as
z\rightarrow 0
outside
R_{\Delta}
, we have

|L_{t}(q)|\ll|z|^{-C}

for some nonnegative constant
C
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.5
proves that
L_{t}^{*}(q)
is the difference of a rational function
\widetilde{L}_{t}^{*}(q)
and the series

q^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{3j+\alpha}}{1-q^{2j+\alpha}},

where
K\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Since rational functions in
q
have convergent Laurent expansions near
z=0
,
\widetilde{L}_{t}^{*}(q)
is
O(|z|^{-C^{\prime}})
for some nonnegative constant
C^{\prime}
.

Since
|q^{j}|<1
for all
j
, we apply the triangle inequality to find that

(10)

\Bigg|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{3j+\alpha}}{1-q^{2j+\alpha}}\Bigg|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{q^{3j+\alpha}}{1-q^{2j+\alpha}}\Big|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{q^{2j+\alpha}}{1-q^{2j+\alpha}}\Big|.

Let the series on the right hand side of this inequality be
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
. Further, let
z=x+iy
and
d(n)
be the standard divisor function, i.e.
d(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}1
. Using the reverse triangle inequality, we find

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{q^{j}}{1-q^{j}}\Big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-jx}}{1-e^{-jx}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d(j)e^{-jx}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}je^{-jx}=\frac{e^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}.

Outside of the region
R_{\Delta}
, we have that
\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}x^{2}}.
Thus,
S_{\alpha}
is bounded above by
\frac{\delta^{2}}{|z|^{2}}
. Since
q^{K}
has a Taylor series expansion for all
K
, we then obtain that
L_{t}^{*}(q)\ll|z|^{-C}
for
C=\max\{2,C^{\prime}\}
.
∎

4.4.
Major and Minor Arc Computations for
\xi(q)

We use the modular transformation law for
\mathcal{P}(q)
, as given in
[
4
]
for example, to obtain the modular transformation law for

:

(11)

\xi(q)=\frac{\mathcal{P}(q^{2})\mathcal{P}(q^{2})}{\mathcal{P}(q)\mathcal{P}(q^{4})}=\text{exp}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{24z}+\frac{z}{12}\right)\frac{\mathcal{P}(\omega^{2})^{2}}{\mathcal{P}(\omega)\mathcal{P}(\omega^{4})},

where

. We now determine the behavior of

on the major and minor arcs.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Delta>0.62
be a fixed constant with
\delta=\sqrt{1+\Delta^{2}}
. Let

be a complex number satisfying

with

. Set

. Then, we have

|\xi(e^{-z})-\Psi(z)|<214|\Psi(z)e^{-\pi^{2}/z}|.

Proof.

Using Euler’s Pentagonal Number Theorem, we rewrite (
11
) as

\displaystyle\xi(z)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}(-1)^{m}\left(\omega^{\frac{m(3m+1)}{2}}+\omega^{\frac{m(3m-1)}{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}(-1)^{m}\left(\omega^{2m(3m+1)}+\omega^{2m(3m-1)}\right)\right)\cdot\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}p(m)\omega^{2m}\right)^{2}.

For notational convenience, we let
X
,
Y
, and
Z
denote the above expressions, as follows:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m\geq 1}(-1)^{m}\left(\omega^{\frac{m(3m+1)}{2}}+\omega^{\frac{m(3m-1)}{2}}\right),\ Y:=\sum_{m\geq 1}(-1)^{m}\left(\omega^{2m(3m+1)}+\omega^{2m(3m-1)}\right),\ Z:=\sum_{m\geq 1}p(m)\omega^{2m}.

We now write

\frac{\xi(z)-\Psi(z)}{\Psi(z)}=X(1+Y)^{2}(1+Z)+(Y^{2}+2Y)(1+Z)+Z.

We trivially bound
X
and

using geometric series to find

(12)

|X|<\sum_{m\geq 1}|\omega|^{m}=|\omega|+\frac{|\omega|^{2}}{1-|\omega|},\hskip 18.49988pt|Z|<\frac{|\omega|^{4}}{1-|\omega|}.

Let
\delta=\sqrt{1+\Delta^{2}}
. Inside the region
R_{\Delta}
, we have that
\Real(\frac{1}{z})\geq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}x}
. Then,
x<\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\delta^{2}}
implies

(13)

|\omega|=\exp\left(-\Real\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{z}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\delta^{2}x}\right)<\exp(-1/2)\leq 0.61.

Since
\Delta>0.62
, we find that
\delta>1.175
and
\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2.35\delta}>\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\delta}
. Now, applying the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 3.8]
, we bound
Y
by
23.6|\omega|^{2}
. Further, we bound
X
and
Z
by applying (

) to (

), obtaining

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\xi(z)-\Psi(z)}{\Psi(z)}\Big|

\displaystyle<(|\omega|+2.54|\omega|^{2})(1+23.6|\omega|^{2})^{2}(1+2.54|\omega|^{4})

\displaystyle+(556.96|\omega|^{4}+47.2|\omega|^{2})(1+2.54|\omega|^{4})+2.54|\omega|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq(|\omega|+1.55|\omega|)\cdot 95.68\cdot 1.35+(126.42|\omega|+28.80|\omega|)\cdot 1.36+0.58|\omega|\leq 214|\omega|.

∎

We present an upper bound for
\xi(q)
outside of
R_{\Delta}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
k\geq 4
be an integer,
\Delta
a nonnegative constant, and let
\delta=\sqrt{1+\Delta^{2}}
. Assume that

satisfies
\Delta x\leq|y|\leq\pi
. Then, we have that

|\xi(e^{-z})|\leq C\xi(|e^{-z}|)\cdot e^{-\varepsilon/x}

where
\varepsilon=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)+\frac{5}{4k}>0
and

depend only on
\delta
and

. In particular, we have that
\displaystyle{\xi(e^{-z})\ll_{\Delta}\xi(|e^{-z}|)e^{-\varepsilon/x}}

where
\varepsilon
can be taken to be arbitrarily close to
-\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\left(1-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)
.

Proof.

From (
11
), we have that

\displaystyle|\xi(e^{-z})|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{24x}+\frac{\Real(1/z)}{12}\right)\frac{\mathcal{P}\left(e^{-2\pi^{2}\Real(1/z)}\right)^{2}}{\left|\mathcal{P}\left(e^{-\pi^{2}/z}\right)\mathcal{P}\left(e^{-4\pi^{2}/z}\right)\right|}.

Outside the region
R_{\Delta}
, we have that
\Real\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}x}
. After a second application of (

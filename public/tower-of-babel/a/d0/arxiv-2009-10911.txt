Interplay between superconductivity and non-Fermi liquid at a quantum-critical point in a metal. IV: The γ \gamma model and its phase diagram at 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 .
Yi-Ming Wu, Shang-Shun Zhang, Artem Abanov, Andrey V. Chubukov

Abstract
In this paper we continue with our analysis of the interplay between the pairing and the non-Fermi liquid behavior in a metal for a set of quantum-critical (QC) systems with an effective dynamical electron-electron interaction V ⁡ ( Ω m ) ∝ 1 / | Ω m | γ V(\Omega_{m})\propto 1/|\Omega_{m}|^{\gamma} , mediated by a critical massless boson (the γ \gamma -model). In previous papers we considered the cases 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 and γ ≈ 1 \gamma\approx 1 . We argued that the pairing by a gapless boson is fundamentally different from BCS/Eliashberg pairing by a massive boson as for the former there exists not one but an infinite discrete set of topologically distinct solutions for the gap function Δ n ​ ( ω m ) \Delta_{n}(\omega_{m}) at T = 0 T=0 ( n = 0 , 1 , 2 ​ … n=0,1,2... ), each with its own condensation energy E c , n E_{c,n} . Here we extend the analysis to larger 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 . We argue that the discrete set of solutions survives, and the spectrum of E c , n E_{c,n} get progressively denser as γ \gamma increases towards 2 2 and eventually becomes continuous at γ → 2 \gamma\to 2 . This increases the strength of ”longitudinal” gap fluctuations, which tend to reduce the actual superconducting T c T_{c} compared to the onset temperature for the pairing and give rise to a pseudogap region of preformed pairs. We also detect two features on the real axis, which develop at γ > 1 \gamma>1 and also become critical at γ → 2 \gamma\to 2 . First, the density of states evolves towards a set of discrete δ − \delta- functions. Second, an array of dynamical vortices emerges in the upper frequency half-plane, near the real axis. We argue that these two features come about because on a real axis, the real part of the dynamical electron-electron interaction, V ′ ​ ( Ω ) ∝ cos ⁡ ( π ​ γ / 2 ) / | Ω | γ V^{\prime}(\Omega)\propto\cos(\pi\gamma/2)/|\Omega|^{\gamma} , becomes repulsive for γ > 1 \gamma>1 , and the imaginary V ′′ ( Ω ) ∝ sin ( π γ / 2 ) / | Ω | γ V^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Omega)\propto\sin(\pi\gamma/2)/|\Omega|^{\gamma} , gets progressively smaller at γ → 2 \gamma\to 2 . We speculate that the features on the real axis are consistent with the development of a continuum spectrum of the condensation energy, for which we used Δ n ​ ( ω m ) \Delta_{n}(\omega_{m}) on the Matsubara axis. We consider the case γ = 2 \gamma=2 separately in the next paper.

In this paper, we continue our analysis of the competition between non-Fermi liquid (NFL) physics and superconductivity (SC) near a quantum-critical point (QCP) of an itinerant correlated electron system,

II
\gamma
-model

We consider itinerant fermions at a QCP, interacting by exchanging fluctuations of a
critical order parameter. At a QCP, the propagator of a soft boson becomes massless and mediates singular interaction between fermions. We assume that this interaction is attractive in at least one pairing channel and that a pairing boson can be treated as slow mode compared to a fermion, i.e., at a given momentum
q
, typical fermionic frequency is much larger than typical bosonic frequency. In this situation, the ratio of typical bosonic and fermionic frequencies is a small parameter, analogous to the Migdal parameter in the electron-phonon case. This small parameter allows one to neglect vertex corrections and explicitly integrate feromionic and bosonic propagators over the momentum components. As a result, the problem reduces to a set of coupled integral equations for frequency dependent fermionic self-energy and the pairing vertex, in which an input is the effective, singular, frequency dependent interaction

V(\Omega_{m})={\bar{g}}^{\gamma}/|\Omega_{m}|^{\gamma}
. The exponent
\gamma
is different for different microscopic systems.
This model, nicknamed the
\gamma-
model, has been introduced in Paper I and we refer a reader to the list of references in that paper to earlier works on the justification of the model and its relation to various microscopic quantum-critical systems with momentum and frequency-dependent interaction.

The local interaction
V(\Omega_{m})
contributes to the fermionic self-energy
\Sigma(k_{F},\omega_{m})=\Sigma(\omega_{m})
and the pairing vertex
\Phi(\omega_{m})
.
The coupled equations for
\Sigma(\omega_{m})
and
\Phi(\omega_{m})
have the same structure as Eliashberg equations for a dispersion-less phonon, albeit with the exponent
\gamma
,
instead of
2

The superconducting gap function
\Delta(\omega_{m})
is defined as

\Delta(\omega_{m})=\omega_{m}\frac{\Phi(\omega_{m})}{{\tilde{\Sigma}}(\omega_{m})}=\frac{\Phi(\omega_{m})}{1+\Sigma(\omega_{m})/\omega_{m}}

(2)

The gap function is defined up to an overall

The gap equation for infinitesimally small
\Delta(\omega_{m})

with
E_{c}(0)=E_{c,0}
and

E_{c}(\infty)=E_{c,\infty}
.
In the next Paper V, where we specifically analyze the case
\gamma=2

singular downward renormalization of the stiffness.

IV
Gap function on the real axis

The analysis on the Matsubara axis is sufficient for the computation of the condensation energy at
T=0
and thermodynamic properties at
T>0
. For the analysis of transport and spectroscopic properties, one needs to know the gap function on the real frequency axis. In this section we obtain
\Delta_{n}(\omega)
for real
\omega
.
The gap function on the real axis is complex:
\Delta_{n}(\omega)=\Delta^{\prime}_{n}(\omega)+i\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\omega)
. It is convenient to introduce its
\omega-
dependent phase as
\Delta_{n}(\omega)=|\Delta_{n}(\omega)|e^{i\eta(\omega)}
. As usual,
\eta(\omega)
is defined up to a constant.

IV.1
Eliashberg gap equation on the real axis

Eliashberg gap equation in real frequencies cannot be obtained by
simply replacing
\omega_{m}
by
-i\omega
in (
2
) as
\Delta_{n}(\omega_{m}\to-i\omega)
would have branch cuts
in the upper frequency half-plane, while the actual
\Delta(z)
must be an analytic function.

Below we follow the approach, suggested in Refs
Karakozov et al. 1991
;
Marsiglio and Carbotte 1991
;
Combescot 1995
;
Wu et al. 2019b

for electron-phonon problem, i.e., convert to real axis using the spectral representation
f(i\omega_{m})=(1/\pi)\int dxf^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)/(x-i\omega_{m})
and, where possible, use
\Delta(\omega_{m})
, which we treat as the known function. For our
\gamma
model this procedure yields

\Delta(\omega)B(\omega)=A(\omega)+C(\omega)

(32)

where, as before,
D(\omega)=\Delta(\omega)/\omega
, and

\displaystyle A(\omega)

\displaystyle=\pi T\sum_{\omega_{m}>0}\frac{D(\omega_{m})}{\sqrt{1+D^{2}(\omega_{m})}}\left(V(\omega_{m}+i\omega)+V(\omega_{m}-i\omega)\right)

(33)

\displaystyle B(\omega)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{i\pi}{\omega}T\sum_{\omega_{m}>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+D^{2}(\omega_{m})}}\left(V(\omega_{m}+i\omega)-V(\omega_{m}-i\omega)\right)

\displaystyle C(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Omega V^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Omega)\frac{D(\omega-\Omega)-D(\omega)}{\sqrt{1-D^{2}(\omega-\Omega)}}\left(\tanh\frac{\omega-\Omega}{2T}+\coth\frac{\Omega}{2T}\right)

The functions
A(\omega)
and
B(\omega)
are obtained by just rotating the gap equation from
\omega_{m}
to
-i\omega
, and the function
C(\omega)
contains the extra term that cancels out a parasitic contribution from the branch cuts.

The interaction
V(\Omega)
on the real axis is
V(\omega)=V^{\prime}(\Omega)+iV^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Omega)
, where

V^{\prime}(\Omega)=\left(\frac{{\bar{g}}}{|\Omega|}\right)^{\gamma}\cos{\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}},~~V^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Omega)=\left(\frac{{\bar{g}}}{|\Omega|}\right)^{\gamma}\sin{\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}}{\text{sign}}\omega

(34)

Observe that on the Matsubara axis,
V(\Omega_{m})=({\bar{g}}/|\Omega_{m}|)^{\gamma}
positive (attractive in our notations), on a real axis
V^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega)
is negative (repulsive) for
\gamma>1
.

Below we focus on the case of zero temperature.
At
T=0
, the expressions for
A,B
and
C
reduce to

A(\omega)=\bar{g}^{\gamma}\frac{2}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{m}\frac{D(\omega_{m})}{\sqrt{1+D^{2}(\omega_{m})}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\Omega}{\Omega^{\gamma-1}}\frac{\Omega^{2}-\omega^{2}+\omega^{2}_{m}}{[(\Omega-\omega)^{2}+\omega^{2}_{m}][(\Omega+\omega)^{2}+\omega^{2}_{m}]}

(35)

B(\omega)=\left(1+\bar{g}^{\gamma}\frac{4}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega_{m}}{\sqrt{1+D^{2}(\omega_{m})}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\Omega}{\Omega^{\gamma-1}}\frac{\omega_{m}}{[(\Omega-\omega)^{2}+\omega^{2}_{m}][(\Omega+\omega)^{2}+\omega^{2}_{m}]}\right)

(36)

C(\omega)=i{\bar{g}}^{\gamma}~\sin{\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}}~{\text{sign}}\omega\int_{0}^{|\omega|}\frac{d\Omega}{\Omega^{\gamma}}~~\frac{D(|\omega|-\Omega)-D(|\omega|)}{\sqrt{1-D^{2}(|\omega|-\Omega)}}

(37)

At large
\omega
,

B(\omega)\approx 1+\left(\frac{\bar{g}}{|\omega|}\right)^{\gamma}I_{\gamma},

(38)

where

I_{\gamma}=\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(x-i)^{\gamma}-(x+i)^{\gamma}}{(x^{2}+1)^{\gamma}}

(39)

In the two limits,
I_{1}=\pi/2,I_{2}=1
.

V.3
Finite

Given the equivalence between the number of vortices at complex

The emergence of the array of vortices is another indication
that the pairing at
\gamma>1
, where
V^{\prime}(\omega)
is repulsive, is quite special. The argument for this goes as follows. Take the set of
m

\Delta_{n}(z)=0
, and make it infinite by adding points

at larger

, for which
\Delta_{n}(z_{i})\propto C_{n}/|z_{i}|^{\gamma}

\Delta_{n}(z)
vanishes at

continue analytically from such set and obtain a unique

for all

\Delta_{n}(z)
is non-zero only
because we extended the set of vortex points by adding additional

with

.
The implication is that
\Delta_{n}(z)
for all

is actually determined by the set of complex frequencies, above the crossover scale
\omega_{cr}
.
At
\gamma\to 2
,
\omega_{cr}
tends to infinity. In this limit, the set of the gap functions on the Matsubara axis is determined solely by an essential singularity at

.
This obviously points out that the pairing at
\gamma>1

\gamma\to 2
.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we continued with our analysis of the interplay between the pairing and the non-Fermi liquid behavior
in a metal for a set of quantum-critical (QC) systems with an effective dynamical electron-electron interaction

V(\Omega_{m})\propto 1/|\Omega_{m}|^{\gamma}
, mediated by a critical massless boson (the
\gamma
-model).
In previous papers we considered the cases
0<\gamma<1
and
\gamma\approx 1
. We argued that
the pairing by a gapless boson is fundamentally different from BCS/Eliashberg pairing by a massive boson
as for QC pairing there exists an infinite discrete set of topologically distinct solutions for the gap function
\Delta_{n}(\omega_{m})
at
T=0
(
n=0,1,2...
), each with its own condensation energy
E_{c,n}
.
Here we extended the analysis to larger
1<\gamma<2
. We argued that the discrete spectrum of
E_{c,n}
get progressively denser as
\gamma
increases towards
2
. This increases the strength of
”longitudinal” gap fluctuations, which tend to reduce the actual superconducting
T_{c}
compared to the onset temperature for the pairing. We also reported two new features on the real axis, which again become critical at
\gamma\to 2
. First, the density of states evolves towards a set of discrete
\delta-
functions. Second, on a real axis,
\Delta_{n}(\omega)
with all
n
, including
n=0
, oscillate between
{\bar{g}}
and
\omega_{cr}>{\bar{g}}
, and the phase of the gap
\eta_{n}(\omega)
varies in this range by an integer number of
\pi
.
We associated this variation with the presence of dynamical vortices in the upper half-plane of frequency, at a complex
z=\omega^{\prime}+i\omega^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
. The vortices appear one-by-one at a discrete set of
\gamma_{i}>1
, and the number of vortices tends to infinity at

\gamma\to 2
. We related the emergence of oscillations on the real axis and vortices at complex
z

with the fact that for real
\omega
, the interaction
V^{\prime}(\Omega)\propto\cos(\pi\gamma/2)=/|\Omega|^{\gamma}
becomes repulsive for
\gamma>1
, and the imaginary part
V^{\prime}(\Omega)\propto\sin(\pi\gamma/2)/|\Omega|^{\gamma}
gets progressively smaller as
\gamma\to 2
. We speculated that there is the emergence of an infinite number of vortices and a continuum spectrum of
E_{c,n}
at
\gamma\to 2
are related phenomena.

The case
\gamma=2
requires separate consideration and will be discussed in the next paper in the series. There we also discuss in detail longitudinal gap fluctuations.

A.1
The Linearized gap equation

We choose the unit for frequency to be
\bar{g}
and re-write the linearized Eliashberg equation at zero temperature in dimensionless variable

\Delta({\bar{\omega}})=\frac{1}{2}\int d{\bar{\omega}}^{\prime}\frac{\Delta({\bar{\omega}}^{\prime})-\Delta({\bar{\omega}})\frac{{\bar{\omega}}^{\prime}}{{\bar{\omega}}}}{\rvert{\bar{\omega}}^{\prime}\rvert}\frac{1}{\rvert{\bar{\omega}}-{\bar{\omega}}^{\prime}\rvert^{\gamma}},

(84)

To obtain

\tilde{\Phi}({\bar{\omega}})=S({\bar{\omega}})\frac{\Delta({\bar{\omega}})}{{\bar{\omega}}}.

(85)

The choice of the sign of
i0^{+}
term in

The gap equation in terms of

Let’s define the function

In particular, for

\displaystyle\log B_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\rvert 1-\epsilon_{k\gamma/2}\rvert\coth\left(\pi(k^{\prime}-k)\right)dk^{\prime}+\frac{\pi\chi}{2}\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\coth\left(\pi(k^{\prime}-k)\right)dk^{\prime}

(105)

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi\xi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{-\beta}\coth\left(\pi(k^{\prime}-k)\right)dk^{\prime}+G_{k},

\displaystyle G_{k+in}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{k}+(2l+1)in\pi,~~l,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(106)

The last condition uniquely specifies

, where

is a constant. The latter is irrelevant as one can easily verify that it only contributes to an irrelevant overall factor in
\Delta_{\infty}({\bar{\omega}})
.
Using (
106
) and absorbing the divergent piece into

, we obtain
from (
105

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{k}=B_{k-i0^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg[\frac{1}{2i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\rvert 1-\epsilon_{k\gamma/2}\rvert\coth\left[\pi(k^{\prime}-k+i0^{+})\right]dk^{\prime}

(107)

\displaystyle-\frac{\chi}{2}\log\frac{\sinh[\pi(k_{\gamma}-k+i0^{+})]}{\sinh(\pi k_{\gamma})}+\frac{\xi}{2}\log\frac{\sinh[\pi(k_{\gamma}+k-i0^{+})]}{\sinh(\pi k_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle+(2l+1-\frac{\chi+\xi}{2})\pi k\Bigg].

This
\tilde{b}_{k}
depends on three parameters:
\chi,\xi=\pm 1
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Below we select
\chi,\xi
, and
l
based on the two conditions: (1) the integrand in the r.h.s. of the gap equation (
100
)
must be convergent; (2)
b_{k}
must give rise to
a gap function
\Delta({\bar{\omega}})
, which vanishes at
{\bar{\omega}}\to\infty
.
The first condition puts the following constraints:

•

If
\chi=\xi=1
, then
l=0
.

•

If
\chi=\xi=-1
, then
l=-1
.

•

If
\chi=-\xi=1
, then
l=0,-1
.

•

If
\chi=-\xi=-1
, there is no solution for
l
.

We verified that the second condition is satisfied only if

\chi=-\xi=1
and
l=0,-1
. The dependence on
l
in (
106
), and the difference between the two choices for
l
is an irrelevant overall factor for
\Delta({\bar{\omega}})
. We then concludes that the two conditions uniquely specify
{\tilde{b}}_{k}
.

Substituting these
\chi,\xi
, and
2l+1
into (
106
) and re-expressing the result back in terms of

b_{k}=e^{-\pi k-ik\log(\gamma-1)}{\tilde{b}}_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left[-ik\log(\gamma-1)\right]\exp\Bigg[\frac{1}{2i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\rvert 1-\epsilon_{k^{\prime}\gamma/2}\rvert\coth[\pi(k^{\prime}-k+i0^{+})]dk^{\prime}

(108)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\sinh[\pi(k_{\gamma}-k+i0^{+})]}{\sinh[\pi k_{\gamma}]}-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\sinh[\pi(k_{\gamma}+k-i0^{+})]}{\sinh[\pi k_{\gamma}]}\Bigg].

A.3
Computation of
\Delta_{\infty}({\omega})

We now substitute this

into
Eq. (

)
and compute

\tilde{\Phi}({\omega})
and the gap function
\Delta({\omega})
using

It is convenient to
introduce

. In terms of

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}(y)=y^{1/2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dkb_{k}e^{-ik\log y}.

The

k-i/2
. This is a safe procedure because

(112)

A.4
Series expansion for
\Delta_{\infty}(y)

The function
I_{k}
can be expressed as an infinite product of the Gamma-functions (see Paper I for details). For
b_{k}
, this yields, up to an overall factor,

b_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(1-ik)}{\Gamma(1+ik)}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+i(k+\beta)\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+i(k-\beta)\right)\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+i(k-i\beta_{m})\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{2m}{\gamma}-ik\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-i(k+i\beta_{m})\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{2m}{\gamma}+ik\right)}

(113)

Here
\beta_{m}>0
are the solutions of
\epsilon_{i\beta_{m}}=1
for imaginary argument. There is an infinite set of such
\beta_{m}
, specified by integer
m=0,1,2..
and located at
1/2+2m/\gamma<b_{m}<1/2+2(m+1)/\gamma
(see Fig.

(b) in the main text).

The integral in (
110
) can be evaluated by closing the integration contour along an infinite arc in the complex plane of frequency. For
y<1
, i.e.,
\omega_{m}<{\bar{g}}
, the arc must be in the upper half-plane, and for
y>1
, i.e.,
\omega_{m}>{\bar{g}}
, in the lower half-plane. Viewed as a function of complex

,
b_{k}
has poles from individual
\Gamma
-functions in the upper frequency half-plane at
k=\pm\beta+i(n+1/2)
, where
n=0,1,2..
and at

k=i\beta_{m}+i(n+1/2)
, and in the lower half-plane, at
k=-i(n+1)
and
k=-i(1+2m/\gamma+n)
,
where
n=0,1,2,...
and
m=1,2,...
.

, relevant poles are at

and at

\gamma

A.4.2

For
y>1
,
relevant poles are in the lower half-plane, at

and

This yields the expansion for

\Delta_{\infty}(y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C^{>}_{n}~\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)^{n+1}+\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}D^{>}_{n,m}\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)^{n+1+2(m+1)/\gamma}

We cited this result in Eq. (
14
) in the main text.
The leading term in the series is

, i.e., at
\omega_{m}\gg{\bar{g}}
,
\Delta_{\infty}(\omega)\propto 1/|\omega|^{\gamma}
. The exact

\Delta_{\infty}(\omega)
clearly shows this behavior (see Fig.
4
)

Eq. (
123
) is formally the same as the series expansion result for
\gamma<1
, however the

and
D^{>}_{n,m}
depend on
\gamma
.
We now argue that this dependence is qualitatively different for
\gamma<1
and
\gamma>1
. Namely, we argue below that for
\gamma>1
, there is a universal piece in
\Delta_{\infty}(y)
at large
y
, which does not fit into the power-law series in (

). We obtain this piece by analyzing the form of
b_{k}
in Eq. (
110
) and evaluating the integral over
k
directly, without closing the itegation contour in the complex plane of
k
. While we didn’t compute

and

explicitly,
the presence of a piece that does not fit into power-law series implies that
the series expansion in (

) does not converge at
\gamma>1
(e.g., the coefficients

grow fast enough with

and eventually overcome the smallness of

) and would
yields incorrect results, starting from some critical

, which depends on
y
. In Paper V, where we specifically focus on
\gamma=2
, we show explicitly that this is the case and determine a critical

.

Below we focus on the universal, non-power-law term in
\Delta_{\infty}(y)
. We show that this term is present at
\gamma>1
,
because the complex phase of the integrand in Eq. (
110
) has an extreme at
k=k_{*}\sim y^{1/(\gamma-1)}
.
The universal contribution then comes from the expansion of the integrand around
k=k^{*}
. There is no such extreme for
\gamma<1
.

To identify the universal term, we
consider large
y

and analyze the contribution to the integral in (
110
) from
k
above some

.
We label the corresponding term as

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(y)
.
In explicit form,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty;u}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{k_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}dk{\cos{(I_{k}+k\log y(\gamma-1))}\over(\cosh\left[\pi(k-\beta)\right]\cosh\left[\pi(k+\beta)\right])^{1/2}}.

(124)

For large
k
, the integral, which determines
I_{k}
in Eq. (
112
), is determined by
k^{\prime}\sim k
. This integral contains
{\epsilon}_{\gamma k^{\prime}/2}
. For
\gamma>1
,
{\epsilon}_{\gamma k^{\prime}/2}
is an increasing function of
k^{\prime}
and its leading term at large
k^{\prime}
is

{\epsilon}_{\gamma k^{\prime}/2}\simeq(\gamma-1)(A_{\gamma}k^{\prime})^{\gamma-1}
, where
A_{\gamma}=\gamma\left(\frac{\pi}{2\Gamma(\gamma)\cos(\pi(2-\gamma)/2)}\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)}
. Substituting this form into
Eq. (
112
), we obtain

\displaystyle I(k)+\log{[y(\gamma-1)]}\simeq(\gamma-1)k\ln\frac{ye}{A_{\gamma}k},

(125)

Substituting this form into (
124
), we find that the argument of the integrand has a maximum at

k_{*}=y^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}A_{\gamma}^{-1}.

(126)

Expanding
I(k)+k\log\left[y(\gamma-1)\right]
to quadratic order in
u=k/k_{*}-1
, as

I(k)+k\log\left[y(\gamma-1)\right]\approx(\gamma-1)k_{*}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right),

(127)

substituting into (
124
), and approximating the denominator in (
124
) by its large

value, we obtain

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(y)\sim\sqrt{k_{*}}e^{-\pi k^{*}}\text{Re}\left[e^{-i(\gamma-1)k_{*}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{(\gamma-1)^{2}}\right)}\int_{-[k^{*}(\gamma-1)/2]^{1/2}[1-i\pi/(\gamma-1)]}^{\infty}d{\tilde{u}}e^{i{\tilde{u}}^{2}}\right].

(128)

where
{\tilde{u}}=u((\gamma-1)k^{*}/2)^{1/2}
.
The universal part of this expression is obtained by taking the lower limit to
-\infty
, in which case

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i{\tilde{u}}^{2}}=\sqrt{\pi}e^{i\pi/4}
.
Substituting into (
128
) and expressing
k^{*}
in terms of
|\omega_{m}|/{\bar{g}}
,
we obtain for the universal term

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega_{m})\sim

\displaystyle\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over 2(\gamma-1)}}\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{A_{\gamma}}\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\cos\left[\frac{\gamma-1}{A_{\gamma}}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{(\gamma-1)^{2}}\right)\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right].

(129)

Working along the same lines, but extending the analysis to include the subleading terms at large
k
, we obtain

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega_{m})

with a more accurate argument of the
\cos
:

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega_{m})\sim\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over 2(\gamma-1)}}\exp\left({-\frac{\pi}{A_{\gamma}}\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}}\right)\cos{\Psi_{m}(\omega_{m}/{\bar{g}})}

(130)

where

\Psi_{m}(x)=\frac{\gamma-1}{A_{\gamma}}\left[1-{1\over 2}\left({\pi\over\gamma-1}\right)^{2}\right]x^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2(\gamma-1)A_{\gamma}}x^{\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma)}{\gamma-1}}+\frac{x^{{\gamma(2-\gamma)\over\gamma-1}}-1}{2-\gamma}A_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}-\frac{\pi}{4}.

(131)

At
\gamma\to 2
,
\Psi(x)
acquires a simple form
\Psi(x)=(x^{2}(1-\pi^{2}/2)+\log{x^{2}})/\pi+\pi/4
.

A.5
Analytical continuation

The gap function given by (
110
) can be analytically continued away from the Matsubara axis
by a simple rotation of the argument:
i\omega_{m}\to z=|z|e^{i\psi}
.
Under this transformation,
\log y
in (
110
) transforms into

\log{y_{z}}-i\theta
, where
y_{z}=(\rvert z\rvert/{\bar{g}})^{\gamma}
and

\theta=(\pi/2-\psi)\gamma
. The gap function transforms to

\Delta_{\infty}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\frac{e^{-\theta k}e^{-iI_{k}-ik\log y_{z}}}{\sqrt{\cosh(\pi(k-\beta))\cosh(\pi(k+\beta))}}.

(132)

Applying this transformation, we extend (
131
) to

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(z)\sim\left({\rvert\omega_{m}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over 2(\gamma-1)}}\left(Q(\theta,|z|)+Q(-\theta,|z|)\right)

(133)

where

Q(\theta,|z|)=\exp\left({-\frac{\pi-\theta}{A_{\gamma}}\left({\rvert z\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}}\right)e^{i\Psi(|z|/{\bar{g}},\theta)}

(134)

and

\displaystyle\Psi(x,\theta)=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-1}{A_{\gamma}}\left(1-{1\over 2}\left({\pi-\theta\over\gamma-1}\right)^{2}\right)x^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}+\frac{(\pi-\theta)^{2}}{2(\gamma-1)A_{\gamma}}x^{{\gamma(2-\gamma)\over\gamma-1}}+\frac{x^{{\gamma(2-\gamma)\over\gamma-1}}-1}{2-\gamma}A_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}

(135)

The largest value of
\theta
is on the real axis, where
\psi=0
and
\theta=\pi\gamma/2
.
Here,
Q(\theta,|z|)\gg Q(-\theta,|z|)
.
Keeping only
Q(\theta,|z|)
, we obtain on the real axis,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega)\sim

\displaystyle\left({\rvert\omega\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over 2(\gamma-1)}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi(2-\gamma)}{2A_{\gamma}}\left({\rvert\omega\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}\right)e^{i\Psi_{r}(|\omega|/{\bar{g}})}

(136)

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}(x)=\frac{\gamma-1}{A_{\gamma}}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}(2-\gamma)^{2}}{8(\gamma-1)^{2}}\right)x^{{\gamma\over\gamma-1}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(2-\gamma)^{2}}{8(\gamma-1)A_{\gamma}}x^{{\gamma(2-\gamma)\over\gamma-1}}+\frac{x^{{\gamma(2-\gamma)\over\gamma-1}}-1}{2-\gamma}A_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}

(137)

Comparing this oscillating

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega)
with the regular term on the real axis
e^{i\pi\gamma/2}({\bar{g}}/|\omega|)^{\gamma}
, we see that for
\gamma\leq 2
, the oscillating term is larger in a range

{\bar{g}}<|\omega|<\omega_{cr}
, where
\omega_{cr}\sim{\bar{g}}/(2-\gamma)^{1/2}
has been defined in
(

).

At
\gamma\to 2
,
\omega_{cr}
diverges and

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega)

from (
136
) remains the dominant term in
\Delta_{\infty}(\omega)
at all
\omega>{\bar{g}}
. In this limit,

\Delta_{\infty}^{u}(\omega)=\Delta_{\infty}(\omega)

simplifies to

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}(\omega)

\displaystyle\sim{\rvert{\omega}\rvert\over{\bar{g}}}\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\pi}\left[\left({{\omega}\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{2}+\log\left({{\omega}\over{\bar{g}}}\right)^{2}\right]\right\}.

(138)

We are interested in the following semilinear elliptic problem: { − Δ u + λ u = u p − 1 , x ∈ T , u > 0 , u = 0 on ∂ T , ∫ T u 2 ​ 𝑑 x = c \begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=u^{p-1},x\in T,\\ u>0,u=0\ \text{on}\ \partial T,\\ \int_{T}u^{2}\,dx=c\end{cases} where T = { x ∈ ℝ N : 1 < | x | < 2 } T=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:1<|x|<2\} is an annulus in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , p > 1 p>1 is Sobolev-subcritical, searching for conditions (about c c , N N and p p ) for the existence of positive radial solutions. Introduction

Consider the following problem:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u,\ x\in T,\\
u=0\ \text{on}\ \partial T.\end{cases}

where
T=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:1<|x|<2\}
is an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
,
2<p<2^{\ast}
where

2^{\ast}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
and
2^{\ast}:=+\infty
if
N=2
.

Solutions of (
1.1
) come from establishing the existence of standing wave solutions
\Phi(x,t)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)
of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE):

(1.2)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}+\Delta\Phi+|\Phi|^{p-2}\Phi=0,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times T\\
\Phi(0,x)=\Phi_{0}(x)\end{cases}

When seeking solutions to (
1.1
), two methods are used: the first involves fixing parameter
\lambda
and subsequently searching for solutions as critical points of the
action
functional

\Phi_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda u^{2})-\frac{1}{p}\int_{T}|u|^{p}.

An alternative method is to search for solutions with a prescribed mass, where the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is unknown. The primary objective of this
paper is to prove the existence of positive
normalized solutions
of (
1.1
), which satisfy

(1.3)

\int_{T}u^{2}\,dx=c

where
c>0
is prescribed.

The mass critical exponent, denoted by
p_{c}=2+\frac{4}{N}
, has played a crucial role in analyzing normalized solutions. It distinguishes two distinct regimes: the mass-subcritical regime for
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
and the mass-supercritical regime
for
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{\ast}
. This exponent serves as a threshold for several dynamic properties, including the stability or instability of ground states and the question of global existence versus blow-up (see
[

3.
Asymptotic behaviors when
\lambda\rightarrow-\lambda_{1}

As stated in the introduction, (
1.1
) possesses a unique and non-degenerate solution
u_{\lambda}
in
H_{0,rad}^{1}(T)
for any fixed
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}
. Furthermore,
u_{\lambda}

is the ground state of (
1.1
). Then similar to the proof of
[
23
, Corollary 2.5]
, we know that
\{(\lambda,u_{\lambda}):\lambda>-\lambda_{1}\}
is a
continuous curve in
\mathbb{R}\times H_{0,rad}^{1}(T)
.

Set
d(\lambda)=\int_{T}u_{\lambda}^{2}
, which is a continuous function of
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}
. Note that (
1.1
)
has a solution with
L^{2}
norm
c
is equivalent to that
d(\lambda)=c
for some
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}
. Also, the number of normalized solutions with mass
c
is the number of
\lambda
in
\{\lambda>-\lambda_{1}:d(\lambda)=c\}
. By a standard bifurcation argument,
it is easy to show that
-\lambda_{1}
is a bifurcation point and solution that bifurcates from
-\lambda_{1}
is radial. Use the uniqueness results, we deduce that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\lambda_{1}}d(\lambda)=0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Proof of (1): If
N\geq 3
or if
N=2,p<6
, we have
\lim_{\lambda\to-\lambda_{1}}d(\lambda)=0
,
\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}d(\lambda)=+\infty
.
Hence, for any
c>0
,
d^{-1}(c)
is not empty. Then the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
(1) holds true.

Proof of (2): If
N=2,p=6
, we have
\lim_{\lambda\to-\lambda_{1}}d(\lambda)=0
,
\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}d(\lambda)\in(0,+\infty)
. If we set

\eta_{1}=\sup_{\lambda>-\lambda_{1}}d(\lambda)
. If
\sup_{\lambda>0}d(\lambda)=\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}d(\lambda)
, then
\eta_{1}=\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}d(\lambda)
. Otherwise,

\eta_{1}=\max_{\lambda>0}d(\lambda)>\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}d(\lambda)
. Hence, for any

,

is not empty.
And for any
c>\eta_{1}
,
d^{-1}(c)
is empty. Then we can get the results in Theorem
1.1
(2).

Proof of (3): If
N=2,p>6
, we have
\lim_{\lambda\to-\lambda_{1}}d(\lambda)=0
,
\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}d(\lambda)=0
.
Hence, there exists some
\hat{\lambda}
such that
d(\hat{\lambda})=\sup_{\lambda>0}d(\lambda)=\max_{\lambda>0}d(\lambda)>0
since

is continuous. Set

\eta_{2}=d(\hat{\lambda})=\sup_{\lambda>0}d(\lambda)>0

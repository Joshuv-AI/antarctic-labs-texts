Jacobi forms, Saito-Kurokawa lifts, their Pullbacks and sup-norms on average
Pramath Anamby, Soumya Das

Abstract
. We formulate a precise conjecture about the size of the L ∞ L^{\infty} -mass of the space of Jacobi forms on ℍ n × ℂ g × n \mathbb{H}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{g\times n} of matrix index S S of size g g . This L ∞ L^{\infty} -mass is measured by the size of the Bergman kernel of the space. We prove the conjectured lower bound for all such n , g , S n,g,S and prove the upper bound in the k k aspect when n = 1 n=1 , g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 . When n = 1 n=1 and g = 1 g=1 , we make a more refined study of the sizes of the index-(old and) new spaces, the latter via the Waldspurger’s formula. Towards this and with independent interest, we prove a power saving asymptotic formula for the averages of the twisted central L L -values L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , f ⊗ χ D ) L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{D}) with f f varying over newforms of level a prime p p and even weight k k as k , p → ∞ k,p\to\infty and D D being (explicitly) polynomially bounded by k , p k,p . Here χ D \chi_{D} is a real quadratic Dirichlet character. We also prove that the size of the space of Saito-Kurokawa lifts (of even weight k k ) is k 5 / 2 k^{5/2} by three different methods (with or without the use of central L L -values), and show that the size of their pullbacks to the diagonally embedded ℍ × ℍ \mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H} is k 2 k^{2} . In an appendix, the same question is answered for the pullbacks of the whole space S k 2 S^{2}_{k} , the size here being k 3 k^{3} .

Introduction

This article addresses the classical sup-norm problem in the context of automorphic forms for the spaces of holomorphic Jacobi and Siegel modular forms – these correspond to automorphic forms on the Jacobi group and the Siegel modular group respectively. BM07
]
). Along the way, we write down the main results about Eichler-Zagier correspondence in a way which we believe would be useful in the future. This is the content of subsection

.

The ‘second’ method is presented in section
7.4
. It does not use the geometric side of the Bergman kernel for Jacobi forms of index bigger than
1
, but uses full knowledge of Jacobi Poincaré series of index
1
. One of our motivations behind exploring more than one method is that one of them could have the potential to implement (some variant) the amplification method to improve the individual sup-norm bound for an SK lift. It is not immediately clear how this might work out. This is partly explained by the fact that the standard definition of the SK lifts is via their Fourier expansion—from which the geometric side of the Bergman kernel is hard to study, see section
7.1
.
This led us to make the remark above that the third method, with an improvement in sub-convexity results could be one of the best avenues in this regard.

In the last section, we investigate the size of ‘pullbacks’ (to the diagonal
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
) of an SMF. When the SMF is an SK lift, we have a deep result of Ichino expressing its support in
S_{k}\otimes S_{k}
in terms of central
L
-values. In fact, this was the basis of the works
[

]
and
[

]
, where the

-mass of such pullbacks were investigated. We are interested in the

-mass of the space consisting of the pullbacks (in the spirit of this paper) and show that the size of such pullbacks is either

or

depending on how one normalises the
L^{2}
-norm; it is

if we sum over all the pullbacks (to the diagonal, see 1st paragraph of the introduction)

of
F
with
\left\lVert F\right\rVert=1
, and it is

if we sum the same with
\left\lVert F^{\circ}\right\rVert=1
, provided it is non-zero. This suggests that the bound
\left\lVert F^{\circ}\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll k^{1/2}
might be expected for an
F\in\mathrm{SK}_{k}
, at least when
F
is a Hecke eigenform. Also
\left\lVert F\right\rVert:=\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{2}
throughout this paper.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
F\in\mathrm{SK}_{k}
be a Hecke eigenform such that
F^{\circ}\neq 0
and
\left\lVert F^{\circ}\right\rVert_{2}=1
. Then
\left\lVert F^{\circ}\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll k^{1/2+o(1)}
.

A curious corollary of our calculations is about the linear independence of the square-root of the central
L
-values
L(1/2,\mathrm{sym}^{2}g\times f)
as

vary in
S_{k},S_{2k-2}
respectively, see Corollary

.

Moreover, in an Appendix to section
9
(see section

), we determine the size of pullbacks of the full space

and find the answer to be

– which says that the pullbacks fill up all the mass in

(cf. Theorem

). We point out that
the main idea here is the realization of space of pullbacks of SK lifts (or even for the full space

) as a certain simple subspace of

, which allows us to find their sizes
; and also allows one to talk about the ‘preliminary’ bounds for the size of these pullbacks. Merely using the Fourier expansions of the pullbacks (which involves sums of square-roots of central
L
-values or averages of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight cusp forms, for which we do not have a Petersson formula or some other substitute) gives worse results, see Lemma

in this regard.

We would like to remind the reader that the average results in this paper have their own value: note that merely squaring and adding the best unconditional sup-norm bounds do not usually give the expected average result. The goal of this paper is not to provide individual sup-norm bounds, but to study the BK. Individual sup-norm bounds will be taken up separately.

We end the introduction by mentioning several questions and remarks which can be pursued for further research on the topics considered in this paper.

2.1.
Jacobi forms of type
(n,g)

Let
R
be
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{R}
, then the Heisenberg group
H_{R}^{n,g}
is given by

(2.1)

H_{R}^{n,g}=\{[(\lambda,\mu),\kappa]\,:\,\lambda,\mu\in M_{g,n}(R),\kappa\in M_{g}(R),\kappa+\mu\lambda^{t}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(R)\}

with the composition law

(2.2)

[(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}),\kappa_{1}]\cdot[(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}),\kappa_{2}]:=[(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}),\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}^{t}-\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}^{t}].

The symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(R)
acts on
H_{R}^{n,g}
from right by

(2.3)

[(\lambda,\mu),\kappa]M:=[(\lambda,\mu)M,\kappa];\quad M\in\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(R),\,[(\lambda,\mu),\kappa]\in H_{R}^{n,g}.

The Jacobi group of type
(n,g)
over
R
is defined as

(2.4)

G_{R}^{n,g}:=\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(R)\ltimes H_{R}^{n,g}

with the group law given by

(2.5)

(M_{1},X_{1})(M_{2},X_{2}):=(M_{1}M_{2},(X_{1}M_{2})\cdot X_{2}),\text{ for }M_{1},M_{2}\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(R)\text{ and }X_{1},X_{2}\in H_{R}^{n,g}.

The Jacobi group
G_{R}^{n,g}
acts on
\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C})
by

(2.6)

(M,[(\lambda,\mu),\kappa])\cdot(\tau,z):=(M\langle\tau\rangle,(z+\lambda\tau+\mu)(c\tau+d)^{-1}),

where
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
a,b,c,d\in M_{n}(R)
and
M\langle\tau\rangle=(a\tau+b)(c\tau+d)^{-1}
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
S\in\Lambda_{g}^{+}
,
G_{\mathbb{R}}^{n,g}
acts on functions on
\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C})
as follows:

(2.7)

(\phi|_{k,S}\gamma)(\tau,z):=J_{k,S}(\gamma,(\tau,z))\phi(\gamma(\tau,z)),

where
\gamma=(M,[(\lambda,\mu),\kappa])
and the automorphy factor
J_{k,S}
is given by

(2.8)

J_{k,S}(\gamma,(\tau,z)):=\frac{e(\tr(S(-(z+\lambda\tau+\mu)(c\tau+d)^{-1}c(z+\lambda\tau+\mu)^{t}+\tau[\lambda]+2\lambda z^{t}+\kappa+\mu\lambda^{t}))}{\det(c\tau+d)^{k}}.

The Jacobi group of type
(n,g)
is given by

(2.9)

\Gamma^{J}_{n,g}:=\{(M,X)\in G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{n,g}\,:\,M\in\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\}.

A holomorphic function
\phi
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C})
is called a Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
S
if

(1)

\phi|_{k,S}\gamma=\phi\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma^{J}_{n,g}
.

(2)

\phi
is bounded at the cusps of
\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}
(automatic for
n\geq 2
, due to the Köcher principle).

Any such
\phi
as a Fourier expansion given by

(2.10)

\phi(\tau,z)=\underset{4T-S^{-1}[R]\geq 0}{\sum\limits_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}\sum\limits_{R\in M_{n,g}(\mathbb{Z})}}C_{\phi}(T,R)e(\tr(T\tau+Rz)).

If the Fourier coefficients survive only for
4T-S^{-1}[R]>0
, then
\phi
is called a Jacobi cusp form.
J_{k,S;(n,g)}
(resp.
J^{cusp}_{k,S;(n,g)}
) denotes the space of holomorphic Jacobi forms (resp. Jacobi cusp forms) of weight
k
and index
S
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C})
.

The space of Jacobi cusp forms,
J^{cusp}_{k,S;(n,g)}
is equipped with an inner product and is given by

(2.11)

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Gamma^{J}_{n,g}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C})}\phi(\tau,z)\overline{\psi(\tau,z)}(\det v)^{k}\exp(-2\pi\tr(Sv^{-1}[y]))\frac{du\,dv\,dx\,dy}{(\det(v))^{n+g+1}},

for
\phi,\psi\in J^{cusp}_{k,S;(n,g)}
and
\tau=u+iv
,
z=x+iy
.

At various places we need the following operators
V_{m}
defined on Jacobi forms of degree
1
and index
1
(even though they can be defined for any index). For
\phi\in J_{k,1}^{cusp}
define (cf.
[
EZ85
, § 4 (2)]
)

(2.12)

\displaystyle V_{m}(\phi)(\tau,z)=m^{k-1}\sum\limits_{\gamma}(c\tau+d)^{-k}e(mcz^{2}/(c\tau+d)^{2})\phi\big(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{mz}{c\tau+d}\big),

where
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
runs over a set of representatives
\Gamma_{1}\backslash M_{2,m}(\mathbb{Z})
. Here
M_{2,m}(\mathbb{Z})
denotes the set of size
2
integral matrices with determinant
m
. Then
V_{m}
maps
J_{k,1}^{cusp}
to
J^{cusp}_{k,m}
.
By choosing upper triangular representatives, one arrives at the following Fourier expansion of
V_{m}(\phi)
(cf.
[
EZ85

):

(2.13)

\displaystyle V_{m}(\phi)(\tau,z)=\sum\limits_{n,r}\big(\sum\limits_{a|(n,r,m)}a^{k-1}c_{\phi}(nm/a^{2},r/a)\big)\,e(n\tau+rz).

SK lifts

We now recall the formula for the Fourier coefficients of

in terms of those of the lifted
\phi\in J_{k,1}^{cusp}

(2.14)

F(Z)=\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}\phi_{m,F}(\tau,z)e(m\tau^{\prime}),

where we write

z^{t}&\tau^{\prime}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and

with

[

and this correspondence

is called the SK lift of

For any
\mathcal{D}\equiv 0,-1\bmod{4}

\mathcal{D}>0

\displaystyle a_{F}(T)=\sum\limits_{a|\mathbf{c}(T)}a^{k-1}c_{\phi,F}\left(D/a^{2}\right).

.

2.3.
Bergman Kernel for Jacobi forms

Let
\mathcal{B}_{k,S;(n,g)}
be an orthonormal basis for
J_{k,S;(n,g)}^{cusp}

J_{k,S,(n,g)}^{cusp}

(2.16)

B_{k,S;(n,g)}(\tau,z;\tau_{0},z_{0})=\sum\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{k,S;(n,g)}}\phi(\tau,z)\overline{\phi(\tau_{0},z_{0}}).

It can also be written explicitly as given below. Let
h_{k,S,(n,g)}
be a function on
(\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C}))^{2}
defined as (see e.g.,
[

]

[
SZ89
]

(2.17)

h_{k,S,(n,g)}(\tau,z;\tau_{0},z_{0}):=\det(\tau-\bar{\tau}_{0})^{-k}e\left(-(\tau-\bar{\tau}_{0})^{-1}S[z-\overline{z}_{0}]\right).

Let
k>g+2n
. For
(\tau,z),(\tau_{0},z_{0})\in(\mathbb{H}_{n}\times M_{g,n}(\mathbb{C}))^{2}
the Bergman Kernel for the space of Jacobi forms can be written as (see
[
ZWL10
, Proposition 2]
)

(2.18)

B_{k,S;(n,g)}(\tau,z;\tau_{0},z_{0}):=\lambda_{k,S;(n,g)}\sum\limits_{\xi\in\Gamma^{J}_{(n,g)}}(h_{k,S;(n,g)}|^{(1)}_{k,S}\xi)(\tau,z;\tau_{0},z_{0}),

where
\lambda_{k,S;(n,g)}=2^{-n(n+3)/2}\pi^{-n(n+1)/2}(\det(2S))^{n}\prod_{t=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-t}(k-\frac{g+t}{2}-j)
and

denotes the action of the Jacobi group

with respect to the first pair of variables
(\tau,z)
.

2.4.
Siegel-Jacobi Poincaré series.

For a positive definite matrix

and

, the

-th Jacobi Poincaré series of weight

and index

is defined as (see

with

P_{k,S}^{l,r}(Z,W):=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{J}_{(n,g);\infty}\backslash\Gamma^{J}_{n,g}}e(\mathrm{tr}(lZ))e(\mathrm{tr}(r^{t}W))|_{k,S}\gamma.

One knows that

for

and for any

(see

(2.20)

\langle\phi,P_{k,S}^{l,r}\rangle=\lambda_{k,S;(n,g)}(l,r)C_{\phi}(l,r),

where
\Gamma_{n}(s):=\pi^{n(n-1)/4}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(s-\frac{i}{2})
and

(2.21)

\lambda_{k,S;(n,g)}(l,r):=\frac{\Gamma_{n}(k-\frac{n+g+1}{2})(4l-S^{-1}[r])^{-k+(n+g+1)/2}}{(\pi)^{nk-n(n+g+1)/2}(\det 2S)^{n/2}}.

Let

-th Fourier coefficient of

(2.22)

\sum\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{k,S;(n,g)}}C_{\phi}(l_{1},r_{1})\overline{C_{\phi}(l_{2},r_{2})}=\lambda_{k,S;(n,g)}(l_{2},r_{2})^{-1}C(l_{1},r_{1};P_{k,S}^{l_{2},r_{2}}),

which is the Petersson trace formula for the space

3.
Lower bound for the Bergman kernel for the space of Jacobi forms

When

is via an ‘asymptotic orthogonality’ of Fourier coefficients of cusp forms – see below. This avoids the use of explicit knowledge of the Fourier coefficients of Siegel-(Jacobi) Poincaré series; something which is known to be quite difficult to deal with (cf.
[
DK
]
). When

, of course we can handle them better (cf.
[

]

3.1.
Asymptotic orthogonality of the Fourier coefficients of Siegel–Jacobi Poincaré series

In this section, we prove the following asymptotic orthogonality result (cf.
[
KST11
]
for Siegel cusp forms).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k
be even. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim\nolimits_{k\to\infty}C(l^{\prime},r^{\prime};P_{k,S}^{l,r})=\delta_{S}(l,r;\,l^{\prime},r^{\prime}),

where
\delta_{S}(l,r;\,l^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\#\{(A,\lambda)\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}^{g}\colon\left(\begin{smallmatrix}l&r/2\\
r^{t}/2&S\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)[\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\
\lambda&I_{g}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)]=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}l^{\prime}&r^{\prime t}/2\\
r^{\prime}/2&S\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
.

So from (
2.22
), one can say that the Fourier coefficients are ‘asymptotically orthogonal’ on average over an orthonormal basis as
k\to\infty
, if we also use (
2.21
). This result has application towards the sup-norm problem and other equidistribution problems (cf.
[
KST11
]
,
[
DK
, Lemma 4.1]
and below).

For any
y_{0}>0
, let us define the set

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y_{0})=\{Z=U+iV\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\colon V=y_{0}I_{n}\}.

In
[
KST11
]
, (
3.1
) was established for
n\geq 1,g=0
via a technical lemma which states that for
y_{0}
large enough, for all
Z\in\mathcal{F}(y_{0})
and all
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
c\neq 0
, one has
|\det(cZ+d)|>1

(cf.
[
KST11
, Lemma 5]
). Further, in
[
KST11
, Remark 7]
it was speculated that the above result should hold for all
y_{0}>1
based on Gottschiling’s (see
[
Got59
]
) explicit description of the finitely many boundary components of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Such description is not available for
n>3
(see
[
Jab17
, § 5]
for a partial result when
n=3
), as far as we know.

We prove the following result, which is an improvement over
[
KST11
, Lemma 5]
, in that it holds for all
n\geq 1
, all
y_{0}>1
and does not require the explicit description of the boundary components of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

We write
c_{M},d_{M},\ldots
etc. for the lower blocks of
M\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
y_{0}>1
and
Z\in\mathcal{F}(y_{0})
. Then, for any
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{M}&b_{M}\\
c_{M}&d_{M}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
c_{M}\neq 0

2.16
) and (
1.8
) we have that

(4.25)

\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)=v^{k}e^{-4\pi S[y^{t}]/v}B_{k,S,g}(\tau,z;\tau,z).

Thus the modified Bergman kernel
\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
can now be written as

(4.26)

C_{k,S,g}(v,y)\sum_{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{k}\ \left(j(\gamma,\tau)(\gamma(\tau)-\overline{\tau})\right)^{-k+\frac{g}{2}}\sum_{\eta,\mu}\varepsilon_{S}(\eta,\mu;\gamma)\theta_{S,\eta}(\tau,z)\overline{\theta_{S,\mu}(\tau,z)},

where
C_{k,S,g}(v,y)=\ell\det(2S)^{1/2}\ e^{-4\pi S[y^{t}]/v}
.

From (
4.26
), we can bound
\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
by

(4.27)

\ll C_{k,S,g}(v,y)v^{k}\sum_{\gamma}\left|j(\gamma,\tau)(\gamma(\tau)-\overline{\tau})/2i\right|^{-k+\frac{g}{2}}\big|\sum_{\nu,\mu}\varepsilon_{S}(\nu,\mu;\gamma)\theta_{S,\nu}(\tau,z)\overline{\theta_{S,\mu}(\tau,z)}\big|.

Let
\rho_{S}(\gamma)=\left(\varepsilon_{S}(\nu,\mu;\gamma)\right)_{\nu,\mu}
and
\Theta=(\theta_{S,\mu})_{\mu}
. Then we know that
\rho_{S}(\gamma)
is unitary (see e.g.,
[
Ara92
]
). Thus the sum over
\nu,\mu
is

(4.28)

\displaystyle=\langle\rho_{S}(\gamma)\Theta,\Theta\rangle{\ll}\langle\rho_{S}(\gamma)\Theta,\rho_{S}(\gamma)\Theta\rangle^{1/2}\langle\Theta,\Theta\rangle^{1/2}=\langle\Theta,\Theta\rangle=\sum\limits_{\mu}|\theta_{S,\mu}|^{2},

where we use Cauchy-Schwartz for the first inequality, and the unitary property of
\rho_{S}(\gamma)
for the second. Here
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes the standard inner product on Euclidean space.

Note that from
[
Ara92
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have (see also
[
SZ89
, p. 182, (11)]
for
n=1
)

(4.29)

\sum\limits_{\mu}|\theta_{S,\mu}(\tau,z)|^{2}=(\det S)^{1/2}\ v^{-g/2}\ \Theta^{*}(\tau,z),

where

(4.30)

\displaystyle\Theta^{*}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}}e\left(-S[z-\bar{z}-\lambda\bar{\tau}-\mu](\tau-\bar{\tau})^{-1}-\bar{\tau}S[\lambda]-2\lambda^{t}S\bar{z}\right).

We want good upper bounds for
\Theta^{*}(\tau,z)
. In (
4.30
) we first consider the sum over
\mu
and see that it is bounded by

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(4\pi S[y]/v)\sum\limits_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}}\exp\Big(\frac{\pi(-u^{2}+v^{2})}{v}S[\lambda]-\frac{\pi}{v}S[\mu]-\frac{2\pi}{v}u\lambda^{t}S\mu+4\pi y^{t}S\lambda\Big).

Now considering the sum over
\lambda
as well and bounding it absolutely, we are left with

\displaystyle\Theta^{*}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle\ll\exp(4\pi S[y^{t}]/v)\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mu}\exp\Big(\frac{\pi(-u^{2}+v^{2})}{v}S[\lambda]-\frac{\pi}{v}S[\mu]\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{2\pi}{v}u\lambda^{t}S\mu+4\pi y^{t}S\lambda-2\pi vS[\lambda]-4\pi\lambda^{t}Sy\Big)

(4.32)

\displaystyle\ll\exp(4\pi S[y^{t}]/v)\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mu}\exp\Big(\frac{-\pi(u^{2}+v^{2})}{v}S[\lambda]-\frac{\pi}{v}S[\mu]-\frac{2\pi}{v}u\lambda^{t}S\mu\Big).

We then notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\exp\Big(\frac{-\pi(u^{2}+v^{2})}{v}S[\lambda]-\frac{\pi}{v}S[\mu]-\frac{2\pi}{v}u\lambda^{t}S\mu\Big)=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\exp\Big(-\pi vS[\lambda]-\frac{\pi}{v}S[u\lambda+\mu]\Big).

At this point, we can assume that
S
is diagonal, with
s_{j}
denoting the diagonal elements. Therefore, we can bound (
4.2
) as

(4.33)

\displaystyle\prod\nolimits_{j}\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j},\mu_{j}}\exp\left(-as_{j}\lambda_{j}^{2}-bs_{j}(u\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j})^{2}\right);

where
a\asymp_{g}v
and
b\asymp_{g}v^{-1}
. For the one-dimensional sums, using the well-known bounds on Gaussian exponential integrals (see e.g.,
[
GR14
, No. 3.322]
), we get

(4.34)

\sum\limits_{p,q}\exp(-m(ap^{2}+b(up+q)^{2})\ll\left(1+(am)^{-1/2}\right)\left(1+(bm)^{-1/2}\right).

Thus, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle\Theta^{*}(\tau,z)\ll e^{4\pi S[y^{t}]/v}\prod\nolimits_{j}\Big(1+\sqrt{vs_{j}^{-1}}\Big)\Big(1+\sqrt{(s_{j}v)^{-1}}\Big)\ll e^{4\pi S[y^{t}]/v}\prod\nolimits_{j}\Big(1+\sqrt{vs_{j}^{-1}}\Big).

Now we bound the sum over
\gamma
. Write
r=k-\frac{g}{2}\,(=\ell+1)
and define

(4.36)

M(\tau):=\sum\limits_{\gamma}v^{r}\,\big|(\frac{\gamma(\tau)-\overline{\tau}}{2i})j(\gamma,\tau)\big|^{-r}.

From Corollary 2.16 and Proposition 3.2 in
[
Ste16
]
, we have for any
\eta>0
and
A>0

(4.37)

M(\tau)\ll 1+vr^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}+vr^{-A}.

Then the sum over
\gamma
in (
4.27
) is bounded by
M(\tau)
. Thus, we have

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
(\tau,z)\in\mathcal{F}^{J}
and
\ell
as in (
4.2
),

(4.38)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)\ll\ell\det(2S)\left(1+v\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}+v\ell^{-A}\right)\prod\nolimits_{j}\Big(1+\sqrt{vs_{j}^{-1}}\Big).

4.3.
Final Bounds

With the bounds on BK from the Fourier expansion obtained in subsection
4.1
and the bounds from the geometric side of the BK in subsection
4.2
at hand, we are now ready to prove the upper bound in Theorem
1.5
. To do this, we split the fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}^{J}_{g}
(
\mathcal{F}_{1}
to be precise) into suitable regions and use the bounds from the aforementioned subsections. In particular, we consider the following regions. Recall the definition of
\ell
from (
4.2
).

4.3.1.
v\gg\ell\det(2S)^{1+\epsilon}
:

In this region, we show that
\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
has exponential decay both in
\ell
and
\det(2S)
. To do this, first note that from (
4.7
) and (
4.6
),
\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
is bounded by the quantity

(4.39)

v^{g/2+1}\det(2S)^{1/2}e^{-4\pi S[y]/v}R_{S}(y,v)^{2}\Big(\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0,0)^{2}+\det(2S)^{-g/2-1/2-\epsilon}\mathcal{Q}_{1}(-\frac{g}{4}-\frac{1}{6},-\frac{g}{4}-\epsilon)^{2}\Big).

Next, using
[
DK
, Lemma 4.11]
, for any finite
\alpha,\beta
, we see that the quantity
\mathcal{Q}_{1}(\alpha,\beta)
has exponential type decay in the region
v^{\prime}\gg\ell
. To be precise,

(4.40)

\mathcal{Q}_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\ll\exp(-c_{0}v^{\prime}).

Thus when
v\gg\ell(\det(2S))^{1+\epsilon}
, the quantity
\ \mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
is (with some absolute constant
c>0
)

(4.41)

\displaystyle\ll v^{\prime g/2+1}\det(2S)^{g/2+3/2}\ \prod\nolimits_{j}\left(1+\sqrt{s_{j}v^{-1}}\right)\exp(-c_{0}\,v^{\prime})\ll\exp(-c\,k(\det(2S))^{\epsilon}).

4.3.2.
\ell\ll v\ll\ell(\det(2S))^{1+\epsilon}
:

In this region, from Lemma
4.4
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle\ll\ell^{3/2+\epsilon}(\det(2S))^{2+\epsilon}\prod\nolimits_{j}\big(1+\sqrt{\frac{\ell(\det(2S))^{1+\epsilon}}{s_{j}}}\big)\ll\ell^{3/2+g/2+\epsilon}(\det(2S))^{g/2+3/2+\epsilon}.

4.3.3.
v\ll\ell
:

First, consider the case
v\gg\ell^{1/2+\epsilon}\det(2S)
. In this region, from the first bound in Lemma
4.3
, we have

(4.42)

\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)\ll\ell^{3/2+g/2}\det(2S)^{1/2}.

Note here that the condition on
v
implies that
\det(2S)\ll\ell^{1/2-\epsilon}
.

Next, we consider the intersection of regions
v\ll\ell
and
v\ll\ell^{1/2+\epsilon}\det(2S)
. Thus, we have to worry only about the case
\det(2S)\ll\ell^{1/2-\epsilon}
. In this region, from Lemma
4.4
, we have

(4.43)

\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)\ll\ell^{3/2+\epsilon}\det(2S)\prod\nolimits_{j}\Big(1+\ell^{1/2}s_{j}^{-1/2}\Big)\ll\ell^{3/2+g/2+\epsilon}\det(2S)^{1/2}.

Thus we have the following bound on
\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
(\tau,z)\in\mathcal{F}^{J}
, we have

\mathbb{B}_{k,S,g}(\tau,z)\ll k^{3/2+g/2+\epsilon}(\det(2S))^{g/2+3/2+\epsilon}.

5.
Contribution from index old and new forms.

In this section, we restrict ourselves to
n=1
and
g=1
. We demonstrate that for certain indices
m
, there are ‘
large
’ natural subspaces of
J_{k,m}^{cusp}
. This is done by (unconditionally) computing lower bounds or even the sizes of their respective BK.
Here, by
index old subspaces
(cf.
[
SZ88
, p. 138]
) we mean the image of lower index Jacobi forms in
J_{k,m}^{cusp}
under the (combination of the) Hecke operators
U_{l}
and
V_{m}
. We also show that for BK of the
index newspace
, that is the orthogonal complement of the index old subspace in
J_{k,m}^{cusp}
, the same is true as mentioned above. Unconditionally, we also show that it is possible to improve the upper bound from Proposition
4.5
for some of these old subspaces, which indicate that the bound on BK is closer to the conjectured bound than that implied by Proposition
4.5
.

5.1.
Size of some index-old spaces.

In this section we demonstrate our assertions made above about the sizes of certain index-old subspaces.

5.1.1.
The old-space
U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})

Consider the operator
U_{l}:J_{k,1}\rightarrow J_{k,l^{2}}
. We estimate the contribution of the old-space
U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})
towards the BK for
J_{k,l^{2}}^{cusp}
. That is, we estimate the quantity

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sup(U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})):=\sup\nolimits_{\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum\limits_{\phi}\frac{v^{k}e^{-4\pi l^{2}y^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,lz)|^{2}}{\langle U_{l}\phi,U_{l}\phi\rangle}.

For
U_{l}\phi=\phi(\tau,lz)
, first we compute
\langle U_{l}\phi,U_{l}\phi\rangle
in terms of
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle
. We have an explicit formula for
U_{l}^{*}
but not for
U_{l}^{*}U_{l}
, which we would need, so we compute things directly. Write
d\mu(\tau,z)=dxdydudv/v^{3}
. Then
\langle U_{l}\phi,U_{l}\phi\rangle
equals

\displaystyle\underset{y\bmod v,x\bmod 1}{\int_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}}

\displaystyle v^{k}e^{-4\pi l^{2}y^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,lz)|^{2}d\mu(\tau,z)=l^{-2}\underset{y\bmod v,x\bmod l}{\int_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}}v^{k}e^{-4\pi y^{2}v}|\phi(\tau,z)|^{2}d\mu(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=l^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}_{1},(j-1)v\leq y\leq jv,x\bmod l}v^{k}e^{-4\pi y^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,z)|^{2}d\mu(\tau,z).

Making a change of variable
z\mapsto z+(j-1)\tau
, we get

\displaystyle=l^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}_{1},0\leq y\leq v,x\bmod 1}v^{k}e^{-4\pi(y+(j-1)v)^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,z+(j-1)\tau)|^{2}d\mu(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=l^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}_{1},0\leq y\leq v,x\bmod 1}v^{k}e^{-4\pi(y+(j-1)v)^{2}/v}|e(-(j-1)^{2}\tau-2(j-1)z)|^{2}|\phi(\tau,z)|^{2}d\mu(\tau,z)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=l^{-1}\cdot l\,\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle\phi,\phi\rangle.

The same calculation holds for two forms
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
. In particular, orthogonal forms go to orthogonal forms. Also, note that
U_{l}
is injective. Thus we see that
\sup(U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))
equals

\sup_{\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum_{\phi}\frac{v^{k}e^{-4\pi l^{2}y^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,lz)|^{2}}{\langle U_{l}\phi,U_{l}\phi\rangle}=\,\sup_{\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum_{\phi}\frac{v^{k}e^{-4\pi y^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,z)|^{2}}{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}=\sup(J_{k,1}^{cusp})\asymp k^{2}.

Thus the size of
U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})
is same as that of
J_{k,1}^{cusp}
.

5.1.2.
The old-space
V_{p}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})

Next, we consider the operators
V_{m}:J_{k,1}\rightarrow J_{k,m}
defined for each
m\geq 1
in (
2.12
). When
m=p
, a prime, the image
V_{p}(J_{k,1})
is part of the index-old subspace inside
J_{k,p}^{cusp}
. We will discuss the properties of this operator in more detail in section
7.3.1
. Now we only borrow a fact from Lemma
7.2
. Namely,
\langle V_{p}\phi,V_{p}\phi\rangle=(\lambda_{f}(p)+(p+1)p^{k-2}\lambda_{f}(1))\langle\phi,\phi\rangle\gg p^{k-1}\langle\phi,\phi\rangle
, and that
V_{p}(\phi),V_{p}(\psi)
are orthogonal if
\phi,\psi
are orthogonal (or distinct) Hecke eigenforms. Since
J_{k,1}^{cusp}
admits a Hecke basis, it follows from these two facts that
V_{p}
is injective (but this may not be true for non-prime indices) and thus for a Hecke basis
\{\phi\}
of
J_{k,1}
, we can write

(5.4)

\sup(V_{p}(J_{k,1}^{cusp})):=\sup\nolimits_{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum\limits_{\phi}(\widetilde{V_{p}(\phi)}/\left\lVert V_{p}\phi\right\rVert)^{2},

where, for
\phi\in J^{cusp}_{k,m}
and
m\geq 1
, we define
\widetilde{\phi}(\tau,z)^{2}:=v^{k}e^{-4\pi my^{2}/v}|\phi(\tau,z)|^{2},

and note that it is
\Gamma^{J}
invariant.
From its definition, notice that
\widetilde{V_{m}(\phi)}^{2}
equals

(5.5)

\displaystyle v^{k}e^{-4\pi my^{2}/v}|V_{m}(\phi)|^{2}=m^{-2}v^{k}e^{-4\pi my^{2}/v}\big|\sum_{ad=m}\sum_{b\operatorname{mod}d}a^{k}\phi(\frac{a\tau+b}{d},az)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=m^{-2}\big|\sum_{ad=m}\sum_{b\operatorname{mod}d}(ad)^{k/2}\widetilde{\phi}(\frac{a\tau+b}{d},az)\big|^{2}=m^{k-2}\big|\sum_{d|m}\sum_{b\operatorname{mod}d}\widetilde{\phi}(\frac{a\tau+b}{d},az)\big|^{2}.

Next, we can use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the lower bound on
\left\lVert V_{p}(\phi)\right\rVert
to write

\displaystyle\sup(V_{p}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))

\displaystyle\ll p^{-1}\sup\nolimits_{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum_{\phi}(\sum_{ad=p}d)\cdot\sum_{ad=p}\sum_{b\operatorname{mod}d}\widetilde{\phi}\big(\frac{a\tau+b}{d},az\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll p^{-1}(p+1)\sup\nolimits_{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum_{ad=p}\sum_{b\operatorname{mod}d}\sum_{\phi}\widetilde{\phi}\big(\frac{a\tau+b}{d},az\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll p^{-1}(p+1)^{2}\sup\nolimits_{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}}\sum\limits_{\phi}\widetilde{\phi}(\tau,z)^{2}\ll p\ k^{2}.

Therefore this bound is better than what we would have obtained from Proposition
4.5
.

Now we compute a lower bound for
\sup(V_{m}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))
. For any
m\geq 1
and any fixed
v_{0}>0,y_{0}
, using the Fourier expansion of
V_{m}\phi
from (
2.13
) and
\left\lVert V_{m}\phi\right\rVert^{2}\ll m^{k-1+\epsilon}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}
(see section

), we get that

\displaystyle\sup(V_{m}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{u\operatorname{mod}1}\int_{x\operatorname{mod}1}\sum_{\phi}v_{0}^{k}e^{-\frac{4\pi my_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}}(|V_{m}\phi(u+iv_{0},x+iy_{0})|/\left\lVert V_{m}\phi\right\rVert)^{2}dudx

\displaystyle\geq v_{0}^{k}e^{-\frac{4\pi my_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}}e^{-4\pi v_{0}}\sum_{\phi}\frac{1}{\left\lVert V_{m}\phi\right\rVert^{2}}\left|\int\int\frac{V_{m}\phi(u+iv_{0},x+iy_{0})}{e((u+iv_{0}))}dudx\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{0}^{k}e^{-\frac{4\pi my_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}}e^{-4\pi v_{0}}\sum_{\phi}\frac{\big|c_{V_{m}\phi}(1,0)\big|^{2}}{\left\lVert V_{m}\phi\right\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle\gg m^{-k+1-\epsilon}v_{0}^{k}e^{-\frac{4\pi my_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}}e^{-4\pi v_{0}}\sum_{\phi}\left(\big|c_{\phi}(m,0)\big|/\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=m^{-k+1-\epsilon}v_{0}^{k}e^{-\frac{4\pi my_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}}e^{-4\pi v_{0}}(\lambda_{k,1,4m})^{-1}C(m,0;P_{k,1}^{m,0}).

where
\lambda_{k,1,4m}\sim\Gamma(k-3/2)(4\pi m)^{-k+3/2}
is as in (
4.2
) if we notice that
S=1
and
D=-4m
in loc. cit.
We know that (cf.
[
Das10
, Prop. 6.3]
)
C(t,0;P_{k,1}^{t,0})>1/2
for

. Thus, choosing

,

, we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
,

(5.6)

\sup(V_{m}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))\gg_{\epsilon}k^{2}m^{-1/2-\epsilon}.

We summarize the results of this subsection in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
l\geq 1
, we have
\sup(U_{l}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))\asymp k^{2}l
. For any prime

k^{2}p^{-1/2-\epsilon}\ll\sup(V_{p}(J_{k,1}^{cusp}))\ll k^{2}p
, the lower bound being actually valid for any

.

5.2.
Size of index-new space.

We first define two classical notions of a ‘new-space’ in the literature (which we denote by ”new1” and ”new2”) in the context of
J^{cusp}_{k,m}
, and show that they are actually equal. Apparently these two definitions are in use in
[
MR00
]
, but we couldn’t find a relation between them in
[
MR00
]
.
Even though we work here only with
m
, a prime, for which subsection
5.3
would have been enough, but for future applications in mind and to fill this seeming gap in the literature, we present Lemma
5.2
. We believe that it will be useful for the community. This subsection also explains our need to write subsection
5.3
in the first place, something we believe should be communicated.

We have the following decomposition for
J^{cusp}_{k,m}
into index-new and index-old spaces according to
[
SZ88
, p. 138, (4)]
(see also
[
MR00
, p. 2613, (41)]
). The first definition states that it is just the orthogonal complement of the (explicitly defined) space of index-old forms.

(5.7)

J^{cusp}_{k,m}=J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}\bigoplus^{\perp}\nolimits_{ld^{2}|m,ld^{2}>1}J_{k,m/ld^{2}}|U_{d}\circ V_{l}=J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}\bigoplus\nolimits_{(l,d)}J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m/ld^{2}}|U_{d}\circ V_{l}.

The second definition runs via eigen-packets of elliptic newforms. Let
\{f_{i}\}
denote an orthogonal basis of eigenforms for
S_{2k-2}^{new}(p)
and define,

(5.8)

J_{k,m}^{cusp,new}(f_{i}):=\text{span}\{\phi\in J_{k,m}^{cusp}\,|\,T_{J}(q)\phi=\lambda_{f_{i}}(q)\phi,\text{ for primes }q\nmid m\},

and define
J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}:=\bigoplus_{f_{i}}J_{k,m}^{cusp,new}(f_{i})
(see
[
MR00
, sec. 5.1]
).

Lemma 5.2
.

With the above notations, one has
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}=J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}
.

Proof.

We use the fact that the Hecke operators
T_{J}(n)
for
(n,m)=1
are Hermitian on
J^{cusp}_{k,m}
and that
T_{J}(n)
commutes with
V_{l},U_{d}
when
(n,lmd)=1
(cf.
[
EZ85
, § 4, Corollary 1]
). By abuse of notation, we denote by the same symbol
T_{J}(n)
the Hecke operators on index
m
and for lower index forms. Moreover,
T_{J}(n)
leaves
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}
invariant (
[
SZ88
, p. 138]
).

We first show that
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}\subseteq J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}
. Since
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}
is invariant under the Hermitian
T_{J}(n)
as noted above, we can decompose it into simultaneous eigenspaces for all the
T_{J}(n)
. From
[
SZ88
, p. 138, (ii)]
we know that there exists a Hecke equivariant isomorphism
\mathscr{S}
between
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}
and
M_{2k-2}^{-,new}(m)
for all
m\geq 1
. By looking at the spanning set of newforms in
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}
, therefore the eigenvalues occurring in the eigenspaces under
T_{J}(n)
above must come (only) from those of
M_{2k-2}^{-,new}
and we are done since this is precisely the definition of
J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}
.

Next we show
J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}\subseteq J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}
by induction on
m
. This is clear when
m=1
. In fact, we already have equality by
[
EZ85
, Thm. 5.4]
and standard results on the Shimura correspondence (cf. e.g.,
[
EZ85
, § 5]
). Next, assume that
J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m^{\prime}}\subseteq J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m^{\prime}}
for any
m^{\prime}<m
. Since
J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m}\subseteq J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}
for any
m
, we have
J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m^{\prime}}=J^{cusp,new1}_{k,m^{\prime}}
as our induction hypothesis.

Now consider a newform
f\in S_{2k-2}^{new}(m)
. Write
0\neq\phi\in J^{cusp,new2}_{k,m}(f)
as (with the tuple
(l,d)

Denote the two sums in (

For the error term

Now consider
\mathcal{S}_{2}(n)
. Again from (

(see
[

Note here that, since

\bullet
The case

\displaystyle(1-\frac{p}{p+1})\sum_{e|(d_{2},n)}\delta(d_{1},nd_{2}/e^{2})-\frac{p}{p+1}\sum_{e|(d_{2},n/p^{2})}\delta(d_{1},nd_{2}/e^{2}p^{2}).

Next, we estimate the error term

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}^{k}(n)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{k^{-\frac{13}{12}}}{p+1}\sum_{t\geq 0}\frac{p^{t}}{(p+1)^{2t}}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}|p^{t}}c_{p^{t}}(d_{1})c_{p^{t}}(d_{2})\sum_{v|(n,p)}\sum_{b|(n/v,v)}\sum_{e_{2}|(d_{2},n/b^{2})}\Big(\frac{nd_{1}d_{2}}{b^{2}e_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{3/8+\epsilon}

in the analysis of the quantity

came only from

(cf. (

6.12
)). So the claim is justified. The error terms remain the same.

When it comes to

from above we have to consider terms

7.
Sup-norms of SK lifts on average

In this section, we establish bounds on the size of Bergman kernel for the space of SK lifts in order to prove Theorem
1.1
i.e.,
k^{5/2}\ll\sup(\mathrm{SK}_{k})\ll_{\epsilon}k^{5/2+\epsilon}
. It is natural to utilize the ‘geometric side’ of the BK (as an average of a suitable function over a suitable group) for this space, but it is not clear how to satisfactorily utilize the same, see the next subsection for more remarks. However to avoid this, we will transform the question to one on Jacobi forms of index
1
, which are better understood. We then use the bounds on Bergman kernel for these spaces to obtain the required bounds for SK lifts.

7.1.
Geometric side of the Bergman kernel

For completeness and with a view towards future investigations, we briefly indicate what expression one gets for the geometric side of the BK of SK lifts. We give two approaches, of course the resulting expression must be the same from both of them.

7.1.1.
From the Fourier Expansion

We start with the Fourier expansion of
\mathbb{BK}_{k}(Z,W)
in the

F

\mathrm{SK}_{k}

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{BK}_{k}(Z,W)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{F}\det(Y)^{k}F(Z)\overline{F(W)}=\sum\limits_{F}\det(Y)^{k}F(Z)\sum\limits_{T}\overline{A_{F}(T)}e(-T\overline{W}).

For a discriminant

7.3.
Upper bound - first method.

We now prove the upper bound for the quantity

) and some of the methods in
[
DK

and
[

]

and embed the index-old forms

We begin by estimating the quantity

\begin{split}p_{*}(T)&=\sum_{F}\big|\sum_{a|(n,r,m)}a^{k-1}c_{\phi,F}\left(\frac{D}{a^{2}}\right)\big|^{2}=\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}(aa^{\prime})^{k-1}\sum_{F}c_{\phi,F}\left(\frac{D}{a^{2}}\right)\overline{c_{\phi,F}\left(\frac{D}{a^{\prime 2}}\right)}\\

Now using the bound for the Fourier coefficients of Poincaré series from Proposition

\displaystyle p_{*}(T)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{4^{k}\pi^{2k}D^{k}}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(k-3/2)}\left(\sum_{a}a^{k-1}\left(a^{2k}(D^{-3/2}a^{3}+k^{-5/6}D^{-1+\epsilon}a^{2-\epsilon})\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{F\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{k}}\det(Y)^{k}|F(Z)|^{2}\leq q_{*}(Y)^{2}

Plugging in the bound for

(7.22)

\displaystyle Q(\alpha,\beta;Y):=\frac{(4\pi)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\sum\limits_{T}\mathbf{c}(T)^{\alpha}\det(Y)^{k/2}\det(T)^{k/2-\beta+\epsilon}e^{-2\pi Tr(TY)}.

One has

Proof.

\displaystyle Q(\alpha,

\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\infty}\ll k^{1/4+\epsilon}
for
g
as above. Thus, from (
9.14
), we see that

(9.17)

\sum\nolimits_{g}(vv^{\prime})^{k}\frac{|g(\tau)g(\tau^{\prime})|^{2}}{\langle g,g\rangle^{2}}\ll(k^{1/2+\epsilon})^{2}\cdot k\ll k^{2+\epsilon}.\qed

Corollary 9.6
.

For
F_{f}\in\mathrm{SK}_{k}
a normalized Hecke eigenform, one has the ‘trivial’ bound on its size:
\sup_{\mathbb{H}}|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F^{\circ}_{f}\right\rVert\ll k^{1+\epsilon}
, provided
F^{\circ}_{f}\neq 0
.

Note that we of course have the bound
\sup_{\mathbb{H}}|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F_{f}\right\rVert\ll k^{5/4}
from Corollary
9.2
or from Theorem
1.1
by restriction of domain. If we assume the conjectural size
N(F_{f})\sim 2
as
k\to\infty
(cf.
[
LY14
]
,
[
BKY13
]
), then it is immediate from Corollary
9.6
that
\sup_{\mathbb{H}}|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F_{f}\right\rVert\ll k^{1+\epsilon}
, and
k^{1/2+\epsilon}
if we expect that Corollary
9.6
can be improved to

. To see this, we write and note
|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F_{f}\right\rVert=|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F^{\circ}_{f}\right\rVert\cdot\left\lVert F^{\circ}_{f}\right\rVert/\left\lVert F_{f}\right\rVert\asymp|F^{\circ}_{f}|/\left\lVert F^{\circ}_{f}\right\rVert\cdot N(F_{f})

9.2.
Appendix to section
9

In this section, we calculate the
L^{\infty}
size of the image of the Witt operator on
S^{2}_{k}
which we define by (so
W(F)=F^{\circ}
from the previous section)

(9.18)

\displaystyle W(F)(\tau,\tau^{\prime}):=F_{z=0}.

Since the arguments here are closely related to our ideas in this paper, and especially to this section, we feel it is apt to include this here as an appendix. Assume that
k
is even. Recall that our point of interest is

(9.19)

\displaystyle\sup(W(S^{2}_{k}))=\sup\nolimits_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathbb{H}}\sum\limits_{F\in\mathcal{BW}(S^{2}_{k})}\frac{(v_{1}v_{2})^{k/2}|W(F)|^{2}}{\langle W(F),W(F)\rangle},

where
\mathcal{BW}(S^{2}_{k})
is an orthonormal basis for
W(S^{2}_{k})
.
The main idea is to find a convenient basis for
W(S^{2}_{k})
and work with it, owing to the invariance of the Bergman kernel with respect to the choice of a basis.

First, note that
the size of
S_{k}\otimes S_{k}
is clearly
k^{3}
as its BK is the product of that of
S_{k}
with itself. Secondly, note that since
F(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tau&z\\
z&\tau^{\prime}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right))=F(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tau^{\prime}&z\\
z&\tau\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right))
for all
F\in S^{2}_{k}
, one has
W(F)\in(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}}
, where
(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}}
be the space of all symmetric tensors in
S_{k}\otimes S_{k}
. Its dimension is
s_{k}(s_{k}+1)/2
, where we have put
s_{k}=\dim S_{k}
. From
[
Fre13
, 1.5 Hilfssatz, Folgerung, p.150]
we know that when
k
is even,
W
is surjective. Therefore, if we want to find the size of the space of pullbacks
W(S^{2}_{k})
for
S^{2}_{k}
– we merely have to choose an orthonormal basis of
(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}}
.
Since
2
is invertible for us, it is clear that
(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}}=(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{diag}}
where
(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{diag}}=\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}g_{i}\otimes g_{i},\,g_{i}\in S_{k},\,s\geq 1\}
.
This will indicate how much of the
L^{\infty}
mass of
S^{2}_{k}
is supported by the diagonal.

We will prove that its size is
k^{3}
. To see this, first put
s(f,g)=f\otimes g+g\otimes f
.
Further, let
\{g_{j}\}_{j}
be the normalized Hecke basis for
S_{k}
.
We note that
s(g_{i},g_{j})
,
i\leq j
,
1\leq j\leq s_{k}
is a basis for

(S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}}
. Further, the elements of this basis are pairwise orthogonal. To see this, note that

(9.20)

\displaystyle\langle s(g_{1},g_{2}),s(g_{3},g_{4})\rangle=2\langle g_{1},g_{3}\rangle\langle g_{2},g_{4}\rangle+2\langle g_{1},g_{4}\rangle\langle g_{2},g_{3}\rangle.

Suppose that (without loss of generality)
g_{1},g_{3}
are unequal, hence orthogonal. Then (
9.20
) reduces to
2\langle g_{1},g_{4}\rangle\langle g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
which can be non-zero if and only if
g_{1}=g_{4},g_{2}=g_{3}
.

As before, the quantity which measures the size is the BK: we sum over the modulus square of the elements in an orthonormal basis. Since the BK does not depend on the choice of the orthonormal basis, we can write

(9.21)

\displaystyle\sup((S_{k}\otimes S_{k})^{\mathrm{sym}})=\sum\limits_{i\leq j}\frac{(vv^{\prime})^{k}|s(g_{i},g_{j})|^{2}}{\langle s(g_{i},g_{j}),s(g_{i},g_{j})\rangle}.

Let
P_{n}
be the
n
-th Poincaré series in
S_{k}
. We can write

(9.22)

\displaystyle s(P_{m},P_{n})=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)^{2}}{(4\pi m)^{k-1}(4\pi n)^{k-1}}\sum\limits_{i\leq j}\frac{(\overline{a_{i}(m)}\overline{a_{j}(n)}+\overline{a_{i}(n)}\overline{a_{j}(m)})}{\langle s(g_{i},g_{j}),s(g_{i},g_{j})\rangle}s(g_{i},g_{j});

from which we get a Petersson formula:

(9.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(k-1)^{2}}{(4\pi m)^{k-1}(4\pi n)^{k-1}}\sum\limits_{i\leq j}|a_{i}(m)a_{j}(n)+a_{i}(m)a_{j}(n)|^{2}=p_{m}(m)p_{n}(n).

Now putting
m=n=1
and following the method in section
7.2
, the lower bound
k^{3}
is immediate.

For the upper bound, let
h_{i}
denote the orthonormalized
g_{i}
. Then the RHS of (
9.21
) can be bounded (up to absolute constants) by

(9.24)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\leq j}(v^{k}|h_{i}(\tau)|^{2}\,v^{\prime k}|h_{j}(\tau^{\prime})|^{2}+v^{k}|h_{j}(\tau)|^{2}\,v^{\prime k}|h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})|^{2}),

which is bounded by
(\sum_{i}v^{k}|h_{i}(\tau)|^{2})(\sum_{j}v^{k}|h_{j}(\tau^{\prime})|^{2})\ll k^{3}
by
[
DK
, Section 7.2]
.

Summarizing the above calculations, we can now state the following.

Theorem 9.7
.

With the above notation and setting, if
k
is even,
\sup(W(S^{2}_{k}))\asymp k^{3}.

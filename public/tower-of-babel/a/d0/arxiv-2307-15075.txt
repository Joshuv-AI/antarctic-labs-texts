Manin triples associated to n n -Lie bialgebras
Ying Chen, Chuangchuang Kang, Jiafeng Lü, Shizhuo Yu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Manin triples associated to n n -Lie bialgebras. We introduce the concept of operad matrices for n n -Lie bialgebras. In particular, by studying a special case of operad matrices, it leads to the notion of local cocycle n n -Lie bialgebras. Furthermore, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between the double of n n -Lie bialgebras and Manin triples of n n -Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

Manin triples structures naturally induce a class of quasi-triangular
r
-matrix, which provide a class of Poisson manifolds and Poisson homogeneous spaces in Lie theory
[
20
]
. In 1983, Drinfeld
[
10
]
introduced the notion of Lie bialgebras, which is well established as the infinitesimalisation of quantum groups
[
19
]
.
A Lie bialgebra consists of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a compatible Lie cobracket
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}
, such that the cobracket induces a Lie bracket on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and satisfies the
1
-cocycle condition
[
10
,
18
]
. Moreover, Lie bialgebras exponentiate to Poisson-Lie groups, which has attracted considerable interest from Poisson and symplectic geometers
[
8
]
.
In fact, let
(\mathfrak{g},\delta_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie bialgebra, then there exists a canonical Lie bialgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
induced by Manin triples of Lie algebras. The Lie bialgebra on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is called the double Lie bialgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and it can be used to construct Poisson manifolds
[
17
]
.
The usual interpretation of the
1
-cocycle condition for Lie bialgebras is that
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a
1
-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to the representation
\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}
on the tensor space
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Another way to interpret the
1
-cocycle condition is to decompose
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}
into
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{1}
and
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}
. Here,

\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{1}
and
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}
are
1
-cocycles of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\mathrm{ad}\otimes 1
and
1\otimes\mathrm{ad}
, respectively, satisfying a compatibility condition
[
4
]
. This equivalent interpretation leads to the local cocycle condition and can be understood by an operadic point of view
[
16
]
. Inspired by this,
we extend such structures to the
n
-Lie bialgebras, which are operad matrices of
n
-Lie bialgebras. We also establish a one-to-one correspondence between the double of
n
-Lie bialgebras and Manin triples of
n
-Lie algebras, and get some applications.

In 1985, Filippov
[
12
]
introduced the definition of
n
-Lie algebras (Filippov algebras). His paper considered
n
-ary multi-linear and skew-symmetric operation that satisfy the generalized Jacobi identity, which appear in many fields in mathematics and mathematical physics, particularly,
3
-Lie algebras play an important role in string theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
In 2016, the authors in
[
4
]
introduced two types of
3
-Lie bialgebras, whose compatibility conditions are given by local cocycles and double constructions respectively. The notion of 3-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation (3-Lie CYBE) is derived from local cocycle 3-Lie bialgebras, and the solutions give rise to coboundary local cocycle 3-Lie bialgebras. Meanwhile,
3
-pre-Lie algebras give rise to solutions of the
3
-Lie CYBE. In 2017, the authors in
[
11
]
classified the double construction 3-Lie bialgebras for complex 3-Lie algebras in dimensions 3 and 4 and provided the corresponding pseudo-metric 3-Lie algebras of dimension 8. In
[
6
]
,
n
-Lie coalgebras with rank
r
are defined, their structures are discussed, and
n
-Lie bialgebras are introduced and their structures are investigated.

However, there is currently unknown coboundary theory
or structure for the double space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of
n
-Lie bialgebras, where
\mathfrak{g}
is an
n
-Lie algebra.
Inspired by the notations of local cocycle 3-Lie bialgebras and double
construction 3-Lie bialgebras, it is natural to consider the Manin triples associated to
n
-Lie bialgebras.

The paper is organized as follows. Theorem
4.24
in Section
4
is the main result of the paper and Section
2
as well as Section
3
are the preparations for it. In Section
2
, we introduce some concepts and known results about
n
-Lie algebras that will be used later. In Section
3
, we summarize the coboundary theory of
n
-Lie algebras. We also show that each
n
-Lie bialgebra must have a dual
n
-Lie bialgebra whose dual is the
n
-Lie bialgebra itself. In Section
4
, we define operad matrices of
n
-Lie bialgebras and generalize local cocycle
3
-Lie bialgebras introduced by
[
4
]
to local cocycle
n
-Lie bialgebras (Proposition
4.12
). We also establish a one-to-one correspondence between the double of
n
-Lie bialgebras and Manin triples of
n
-Lie algebras (Theorem
4.24
).

Throughout this paper, all algebras are finite-dimensional and over a field
F
of
characteristic zero.

2.
Preliminary results on
n
-Lie algebras

In this section, we give some preliminaries and basic results on
n
-Lie algebras from
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
n
-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a skew-symmetric
n
-linear map
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]:\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that the following Filippov-Jacobi identity holds, for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

The Filippov-Jacobi identity can be described as for any
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}
, the operator

\mathrm{ad}(X):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathrm{ad}(X)(y):=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y],\quad\forall~y\in\mathfrak{g},

is a derivation in the sense that

(2)

\mathrm{ad}(X)([y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}])=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},\mathrm{ad}(X)(y_{i}),y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

Definition 2.2
.

A
representation
of an
n
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space M is a linear map
\rho:\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(M)
satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})\rho(x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}),

(4)

\displaystyle[\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}),\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1}),

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\hat{x_{i}}
means that the element
x_{i}
is omitted.

We denote the representation by the pair
(M,\rho)
, and say that
M
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module as well. If
\rho=\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is given by

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}(x_{n})=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n}],\quad\forall~x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g},

then the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad})
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and is called the adjoint module of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-Lie algebra, then for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y_{n}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{j-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x_{j}],x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{n}]=0.

Proof.

By (
1
), for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{n}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y_{n}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{j-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x_{j}],x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{n}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]],

implying (
5
).
∎

Let
V
be a vector space,
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
. For each positive integer
k
, we identify the tensor product
\otimes^{k}V
with the space of multi-linear
maps from
\underbrace{V^{*}\times\cdots\times V^{*}}_{k-times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, such that

(6)

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{k},v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle\xi_{k},v_{k}\rangle,\quad\forall~\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k}\in V^{*},v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V,

where
\langle\xi_{i},v_{i}\rangle=\xi_{i}(v_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq k
. For
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V
, define that

v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}sgn(\sigma)v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma(2)}\cdots v_{\sigma(k)}\in\wedge^{k}V\subset\otimes^{k}V.

3.1.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-Lie algebra over

, for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, denote the elements
X=x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1}
in

.
Let
(M,\rho)
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. For any
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}
, and

, the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on

is denoted by
X(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})_{\cdot}a

We know that if
\mathfrak{g}

(\otimes^{p}\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}^{(p)})

(7)

X_{\cdot}(y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{p}):=\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}^{(p)}(y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}(y_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{p}.

Definition 3.1
.

[

]

Let
\mathfrak{g}

-Lie algebra and
(M,\rho)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
k
-cochains
on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in

C^{k}(\mathfrak{g};M):=~\{~linear~maps~u:\underbrace{\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}}_{k-1-times}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow M~\}.

Definition 3.2
.

[
7
]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-Lie algebra and
(M,\rho)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. The coboundary operator of a
k
-cochain
u
on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
M
is the map
\delta_{\rho}:C^{k}(\mathfrak{g};M)\rightarrow C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g};M)
, such that for all
X_{i}=(x_{1}^{i},\cdots,x_{n-1}^{i})\in\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq k
,

,

\displaystyle(\delta_{\rho}u)(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{k},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{1}^{i},\cdots,x_{n-1}^{i})u(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{k},z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k-i+1}\rho(x_{1}^{k},\cdots,\hat{x}_{i}^{k},\cdots,x_{n-1}^{k},z)u(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k-1},\hat{X_{k}},x_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}u(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{k},[x_{1}^{i},\cdots,x_{n-1}^{i},z])

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j}^{k}(-1)^{i}u(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{j-1},\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n-1}[x_{1}^{i},\cdots,x_{n-1}^{i},x_{m}^{j}]\wedge x_{1}^{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{x}_{m}^{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1}^{j},\cdots,X_{k},z),

where

indicates that the element

is omitted.

Proposition 3.3
.

[

For any

[

A

A

By the Proposition

3.2.
n
-Lie bialgebras

Now assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is an
n
-Lie algebra and
\gamma
is a linear map from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}
whose transpose is denoted by
{}^{t}\gamma:\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}

Definition 3.5
.

[
6
]

An
n
-Lie bialgebra
is an
n
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}
such that

(1)

{}^{t}\gamma:\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
defines an
n
-Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, i.e.,
{}^{t}\gamma
is a skew-symmetric
n
-linear map on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
satisfying the Filippov-Jacobi identity;

(2)

\gamma
is a
1
-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
acts on
\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}
by the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}^{(n)}
.

Condition (i) is equivalent to the following two identities, for all
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,

\displaystyle[\xi_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\xi_{\sigma(n)}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=sgn(\sigma)[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}},

\displaystyle[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},[\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{i-1},[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},\eta_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}},\eta_{i+1},\cdots,\eta_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}.

Condition (ii) means that the 2-cochain
\delta\gamma
vanishes, i.e., for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(8)

\gamma([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}])=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\mathrm{ad}^{(n)}_{x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}}(\gamma(x_{i})).

Let
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}:\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the
n
-Lie bracket defined by
\gamma
, that is for all
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
,

(9)

[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}=^{t}\gamma(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}).

Then by the Definition
3.5
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(10)

\langle~[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}},x~\rangle=\langle~\gamma(x),\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}~\rangle.

Let
1:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the identity map, an alternate way of (
8
) is

\displaystyle\langle~[\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]~\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\langle~\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n},\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1\otimes^{j-1}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}}\otimes^{n-j}1)\gamma(x_{i})~\rangle.

Using the Sweedler notation, write
\gamma(x_{i})=z_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1\otimes^{j-1}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}\otimes^{n-j}1)(z_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},z_{j}]\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{n}.

3.3.
The coadjoint representation of
n
-Lie algebras

Now we introduce the definition of the coadjoint representation of
n
-Lie algebras on the dual vector space.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the dual vector space. Set

\mathrm{ad}^{*}=-^{t}\mathrm{ad}
, that is, for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathrm{ad}^{\ast}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}
is the endomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
satisfying

(11)

\langle~\mathrm{ad}^{\ast}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}\xi,x~\rangle=-\langle~\xi,\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}x~\rangle,\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

By (
5
) and (
11
), for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},~y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},~z\in\mathfrak{g},~\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle~\mathrm{ad}_{[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}}^{\ast}\xi,z~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\langle~\xi,[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2},z,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]]~\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\langle~\xi,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2},z,x_{i}],x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n}]~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-i}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\langle~\xi,[x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2},[x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},z]]~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}}^{\ast}\xi,z~\rangle.

And

\displaystyle\langle~\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}\xi,z~\rangle-\langle~\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}^{\ast}\xi,z~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle~\xi,[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},z]]~\rangle-\langle~\xi,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},z]]~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x_{i}],x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n-1},z]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle~\xi,-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1},z]~\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}\xi,z~\rangle.

Then we get for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}-\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{ad}_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1}}^{\ast}.

Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.
∎

3.4.
The dual of
n

Let
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})

-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

By (

\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}

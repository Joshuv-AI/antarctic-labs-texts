Wen-Hui Li, Jian Cao, Da-Wei Niu, Jiao-Lian Zhao, Feng Qi*

Abstract
. In the paper, the authors analytically generalize the Catalan numbers in combinatorial number theory, establish an integral representation of the analytic generalization of the Catalan numbers by virtue of Cauchy’s integral formula in the theory of complex functions, and point out potential directions to further study.

The Catalan numbers

(1.1)

The Catalan numbers

The integral representation

(1.5)

C_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{4}\sqrt{\frac{4-x}{x}}\,x^{n}\textup{d}x,\quad n\geq 0

. We note that, when letting

(1)

Can one establish an explicit formula for the sequence

It is easy to see that

\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\biggl\langle-\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n}\frac{1}{b^{(2n+1)/2}}

2.
An explicit formula for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind

In this section, we will establish an explicit formula for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
, which gives a solution to the first problem posed on page
1.11
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
for
n\geq 0
,
a\geq 0
, and
b>0
can be explicitly computed by

(2.1)

\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\frac{1}{(2n)!!b^{n+1/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}\frac{k![2(n-k)-1]!!}{\bigl(1+a/\sqrt{b}\,\bigr)^{k+1}},

where the double factorial of negative odd integers
-(2\ell+1)
is defined by

(-2\ell-1)!!=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(2\ell-1)!!}=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{(2\ell)!!}{(2\ell)!},\quad\ell\geq 0.

Proof.

The Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})
for
n\geq k\geq 0
are defined in
[
5
, p. 134, Theorem A]
by

\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n-k+1\\
\ell_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}i\ell_{i}=n\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\biggl(\frac{x_{i}}{i!}\biggr)^{\ell_{i}}.

The famous Faà di Bruno formula can be described
[
5
, p. 139, Theorem C]
in terms of the Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})
by

(2.2)

\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}x^{n}}f\circ h(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f^{(k)}(h(x))\bell_{n,k}\bigl(h^{\prime}(x),h^{\prime\prime}(x),\dotsc,h^{(n-k+1)}(x)\bigr),

where
f\circ h
denotes the composite of the
n
-time differentiable functions
f
and
h
.

Let
h=h(x)=\sqrt{b-x}\,
. Then

h^{(k)}(x)=(-1)^{k}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{k}(b-x)^{1/2-k}\to(-1)^{k}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{k}b^{1/2-k},\quad x\to 0

for
k\geq 0
and, in light of the formula (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}^{n}G_{a,b}(x)}{\textup{d}x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\textup{d}^{k}}{\textup{d}h^{k}}\biggl(\frac{1}{a+h}\biggr)\bell_{n,k}\bigl(h^{\prime}(x),h^{\prime\prime}(x),\dotsc,h^{(n-k+1)}(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{[a+h(x)]^{k+1}}\bell_{n,k}\bigl(h^{\prime}(x),h^{\prime\prime}(x),\dotsc,h^{(n-k+1)}(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle\to\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{[a+h(0)]^{k+1}}\bell_{n,k}\biggl(-\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{1}b^{-1/2},\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{2}b^{-3/2},

\displaystyle\quad\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k+1}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n-k+1}b^{1/2-(n-k+1)}\biggr),\quad x\to 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{\bigl(a+\sqrt{b}\,\bigr)^{k+1}}(-1)^{n}b^{k/2-n}\bell_{n,k}\biggl(\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{1},\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{2},\dotsc,\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n-k+1}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}b^{n+1/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k![2(n-k)-1]!!\binom{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{b}\,}{a+\sqrt{b}\,}\biggr)^{k+1},

where we used the formulas

\bell_{n,k}\bigl(abx_{1},ab^{2}x_{2},\dotsc,ab^{n-k+1}x_{n-k+1}\bigr)=a^{k}b^{n}\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})

and

(2.3)

\bell_{n,k}\biggl(\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{1},\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{2},\dotsc,\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n-k+1}\biggr)=(-1)^{n+k}[2(n-k)-1]!!\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}

for
n\geq k\geq 0
, see
[
5
, p. 135]
and the formula (3.6) in the first two lines on
[
36
, p. 168]
respectively. By the way, the formula (
2.3
) is connected with
[
32
, Remark 1]
,
[
37
, Section 1.3]
,
[
42
, Theorem 4]
, and closely related references therein.

The equation (
1.11
) means that

n!\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}G_{a,b}(x)}{\textup{d}x^{n}}.

Consequently, we obtain the explicit formula

\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\frac{1}{(2n)!!b^{n+1/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k![2(n-k)-1]!!\binom{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{b}\,}{a+\sqrt{b}\,}\biggr)^{k+1},

which can be rearranged as (
2.1
). The proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.1

(
[
42
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

The Catalan number
C_{n}
for
n\geq 0
can be explicitly computed by

(2.4)

C_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n+\ell-1}{2\ell}2^{\ell}(n-\ell)!(2\ell-1)!!.

Proof.

This follows from utilizing the relation (
1.13
) and applying
a=\frac{1}{2}
and
b=\frac{1}{4}
in (
2.1
). The proof of Corollary
2.1
is complete.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Taking
a\to 0^{+}
on both sides of the formula (
2.1
) and employing the equation (
1.12
) result in

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+\ell-1}{2\ell}(n-\ell)!(2\ell-1)!!=(2n-1)!!.

Combining (
2.4
) with the first equality in (
1.1

\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n+\ell-1}{2\ell}(n-\ell)!(2\ell-1)!!2^{\ell}=\frac{n!}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.

Stimulated by these two identities and the formula (

) in Theorem

, we would like to ask a question: can one use a simple quantity to express the sum

\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n+\ell-1}{2\ell}(n-\ell)!(2\ell-1)!!t^{\ell}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1,2\}
?

3.
An integral representation for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind

In this section, we will find an integral representation for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
by finding an integral representation of the generating function
G_{a,b}(x)
in (
1.10
), which gives a solution to the second problem posed on page
1.11
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The principal branch of the generating function
G_{a,b}(z)
for
a\geq 0
and
b>0
can be represented by

(3.1)

G_{a,b}(z)=\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{b-z}\,}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{b+t-z}\textup{d}t,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[b,\infty).

Consequently, generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
for
a\geq 0
and
b>0
can be represented by

(3.2)

\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t,\quad n\geq 0.

Proof.

Let

F(z)=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(-z)}{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty),\quad\arg z\in(0,2\pi),

where
i=\sqrt{-1}\,
is the imaginary unit and
\arg z
stands for the principal value of the argument of
z
.
By virtue of Cauchy’s integral formula
[
6
, p. 113]
in the theory of complex functions, for any fixed point
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
, we have

F(z_{0})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi-z_{0}}\textup{d}\xi,

where
L
is a positively oriented contour
L(r,R)
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
, as showed in Figure
1
,

Figure 1.
The positively oriented contour
L(r,R)
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)

satisfying

(1)

0<r<|z_{0}|<R
;

(2)

L(r,R)
consists of the half circle
z=re^{i\theta}
for
\theta\in\bigl[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\bigr]
;

(3)

L(r,R)
consists of the line segments
z=x\pm ir
for
x\in(0,R(r)]
, where
R(r)=\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\,
;

(4)

L(r,R)
consists of the circular arc
z=Re^{i\theta}
for

\theta\in\biggl(\arctan\frac{r}{R(r)},2\pi-\arctan\frac{r}{R(r)}\biggr);

(5)

the line segments
z=x\pm ir
for
x\in(0,R(r)]
cut the circle
|z|=R
at the points
R(r)\pm ir
and
R(r)\to R
as
r\to 0^{+}
.

The integral on the circular arc
z=Re^{i\theta}
equals

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\arcsin[r/R(r)]}^{2\pi-\arcsin[r/R(r)]}\frac{Rie^{i\theta}}{\bigl(Re^{i\theta}-z_{0}\bigr)\bigl[a+\exp\frac{\ln(-Re^{i\theta})}{2}\bigr]}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\arcsin[r/R(r)]}^{2\pi-\arcsin[r/R(r)]}\frac{1}{\bigl(1-\frac{z_{0}}{Re^{i\theta}}\bigr)\bigl[a+\exp\frac{\ln(-Re^{i\theta})}{2}\bigr]}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\arcsin[r/R(r)]}^{2\pi-\arcsin[r/R(r)]}\frac{1}{\bigl(1-\frac{z_{0}}{Re^{i\theta}}\bigr)\bigl[a+\exp\frac{\ln R+i\arg(-Re^{i\theta})}{2}\bigr]}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle\to 0

uniformly as
R\to\infty
.

The integral on the half circle
z=re^{i\theta}
for
\theta\in\bigl[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\bigr]
is

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2}\frac{rie^{i\theta}}{\bigl(re^{i\theta}-z_{0}\bigr)\bigl[a+\exp\frac{\ln(-re^{i\theta})}{2}\bigr]}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2}\frac{re^{i\theta}}{re^{i\theta}-z_{0}}\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(-re^{i\theta})}{2}}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2}\frac{re^{i\theta}}{re^{i\theta}-z_{0}}\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln r+i\arg(-re^{i\theta})}{2}}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2}\frac{re^{i\theta}}{re^{i\theta}-z_{0}}\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{r}\,\exp\frac{i\arg(-re^{i\theta})}{2}}\textup{d}\theta

\displaystyle\to 0

uniformly as
r\to 0^{+}
.

Since

\displaystyle F(x+ir)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(-x-ri)}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln\sqrt{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,+i[\arctan(r/x)-\pi]}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\bigl[\cos\frac{\arctan(r/x)-\pi}{2}+i\sin\frac{\arctan(r/x)-\pi}{2}\bigr]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\bigl[\sin\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}-i\cos\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}\bigr]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\sin\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}-i\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\cos\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\sin\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}+i\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\cos\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}}{\bigl[a+\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\sin\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}\bigr]^{2}+\bigl[\sqrt[4]{x^{2}+r^{2}}\,\cos\frac{\arctan(r/x)}{2}\bigr]^{2}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{a+i\sqrt{x}\,}{a^{2}+x}

as
r\to 0^{+}
and
\overline{F(z)}=F(\bar{z})
, the integral on the line segments
z=x\pm ir
for
x\in(0,R(r)]
is equal to

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Biggl[\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{F(x+ir)}{x+ir-z_{0}}\textup{d}x+\int_{R(r)}^{0}\frac{F(x-ir)}{x-ir-z_{0}}\textup{d}x\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{(x-ir-z_{0})F(x+ir)-(x+ir-z_{0})F(x-ir)}{(x+ir-z_{0})(x-ir-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{(x-z_{0})[F(x+ir)-F(x-ir)]-ir[F(x+ir)+F(x-ir)]}{(x+ir-z_{0})(x-ir-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{(x-z_{0})\bigl[F(x+ir)-F\bigl(\overline{x+ir}\bigr)\bigr]-ir\bigl[F(x+ir)+F\bigl(\overline{x+ir}\bigr)\bigr]}{(x+ir-z_{0})(x-ir-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{(x-z_{0})\bigl[F(x+ir)-\overline{F(x+ir)}\,\bigr]-ir\bigl[F(x+ir)+\overline{F(x+ir)}\,\bigr]}{(x+ir-z_{0})(x-ir-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{R(r)}\frac{(x-z_{0})[2i\Im(F(x+ir))]-ir[2\Re(F(x+ir))]}{(x+ir-z_{0})(x-ir-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2i}{x-z_{0}}\frac{\sqrt{x}\,}{a^{2}+x}\textup{d}x,\quad r\to 0^{+},\quad R\to\infty

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(a^{2}+x)(x-z_{0})}\textup{d}x.

Consequently, it follows that

(3.3)

\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(-z_{0})}{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(a^{2}+x)(x-z_{0})}\textup{d}x

for any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and
\arg z_{0}\in(0,2\pi)
. Due to the point
z_{0}
in (
3.3
) being arbitrary, the integral formula (
3.3
) can be rearranged as

(3.4)

F(z)=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(-z)}{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(a^{2}+x)(x-z)}\textup{d}x

for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and
\arg z\in(0,2\pi)
.

Let

f(z)=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(b-z)}{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[b,\infty),\quad\arg(z-b)\in(0,2\pi).

Then
f(z)=F(z-b)
. Therefore, from (
3.4
), it follows that

f(z)=\frac{1}{a+\exp\frac{\ln(b-z)}{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(a^{2}+x)(b+x-z)}\textup{d}x

for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[b,\infty)
and
\arg(z-b)\in(0,2\pi)
. The integral representation (
3.1
) is thus proved.

Differentiating
n\geq 0
times with respect to
z
on both sides of (
3.1
) and taking the limit
z\to 0
yield

\displaystyle G_{a,b}^{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}z^{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{b-z}\,}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}z^{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{b+t-z}\biggr)\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{n!}{(b+t-z)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\to\frac{n!}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t,\quad z\to 0.

As a result, by virtue of (
1.11
), we have

\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)=\frac{G_{a,b}^{(n)}(0)}{n!}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t.

The integral representation (
3.2
) for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
is thus proved.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

When taking
z=x\in(-\infty,b)
, the integral formula (
3.1
) becomes

(3.5)

\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{b-x}\,}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{b+t-x}\textup{d}t,\quad a\geq 0,b>0.

When taking
x\to b^{-}
, the integral in (
3.5
) converges. Consequently, the integral representation (
3.5
) is valid on
(-\infty,b]
.

Remark 3.2
.

When taking
a=\frac{1}{2}
and
b=\frac{1}{4}
, integral representations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) reduce to (
1.7
) and (
1.6
) respectively.

Remark 3.3
.

Combining the explicit formula (
2.1
) with the integral representation (
3.2
) and simplifying lead to

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t=\frac{\pi}{(2n)!!b^{n+1/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}\frac{k![2(n-k)-1]!!}{\bigl(1+a/\sqrt{b}\,\bigr)^{k+1}}

for
a\geq 0
,
b>0
, and
n\geq 0
.

4.
Potential directions to further study

In this section, we will try to point out two potential directions to further study.

4.1.
Generalized Catalan function of the second kind

Motivated by the integral representation (
3.2
) for generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
, we can consider the function

(4.1)

\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{z+1}}\textup{d}t,\quad a\geq 0,b>0,\Re(z)\geq 0

and call it generalized Catalan function of the second kind, while calling
C(a,b;z)
in (
1.3
) generalized Catalan function of the first kind.

We can study generalized Catalan function of the second kind
\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)
as a function of three variables
a,b
, and
z
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}^{n}\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)}{\textup{d}b^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}b^{n}}\biggl[\frac{1}{(b+t)^{z+1}}\biggr]\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{\langle-(z+1)\rangle_{n}}{(b+t)^{z+n+1}}\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(z+1)_{n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{z+n+1}}\textup{d}t,

where the rising factorial
(z)_{n}
is defined
[
36
,
41
]
by

(z)_{n}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(z+\ell)=\begin{cases}z(z+1)\dotsm(z+n-1),&n\geq 1\\
1,&n=0\end{cases}

which is also known as the Pochhammer symbol or shifted factorial in the theory of special functions
[
17
,
54
]
. This means that generalized Catalan function of the second kind
\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)
is a completely monotonic function
[
23
,
49
,
56
]
with respect to
b\in(0,\infty)
. Utilizing complete monotonicity
[
20
,
49
,
56
]
, we can derive many new analytic properties of generalized Catalan function of the second kind
\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)
.

In a word, employing the integral representation (
4.1
), we believe that we can discover some new properties of generalized Catalan function of the second kind
\mathcal{C}(a,b;z)
, of generalized Catalan numbers of the second kind
\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)
, and the Catalan numbers
C_{n}
. For the sake of length limit of this paper, we would not like to further study in details.

4.2.
Central binomial coefficients

It is known that

(4.2)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}\,}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}=1+2x+6x^{2}+20x^{3}+70x^{4}+252x^{5}+\dotsm,

where
\binom{2n}{n}
is called central binomial coefficient. It has been an attracting point for mathematicians to study central binomial coefficients. For example, we can rewritten
[
45
, Lemma 3]
as

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{1}{(k+1)4^{k}}=2\biggl[1-\frac{1}{4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}\biggr]

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{4^{n-k}}{n-k}=2\binom{2n}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}.

For more information on results at this point, please refer to
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
16
,
26
,
27
,
45
,
52
,
57
]
and closely related references therein.

Combining (
4.2
) with (
1.11
) and (
3.2
) arrives at

\displaystyle\binom{2n}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}x^{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}\,}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}x^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to 0^{+}\\
b\to 1/4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{b-x}\,}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to 0^{+}\\
b\to 1/4\end{subarray}}\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\textup{d}^{n}}{\textup{d}x^{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{b-x}\,}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to 0^{+}\\
b\to 1/4\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}_{n}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to 0^{+}\\
b\to 1/4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,}{a^{2}+t}\frac{1}{(b+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\,}\frac{1}{(1/4+t)^{n+1}}\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1/4+s^{2})^{n+1}}\textup{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2n+1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})^{n+1}}\textup{d}t.

The last three integral representations should provide effective tools for further studying central binomial coefficients
\binom{2n}{n}
.

There are several extensions of central binomial coefficients
\binom{2n}{n}
in the paper
[
55
]
.

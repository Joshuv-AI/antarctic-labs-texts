Convergence of Batch Greenkhorn for Regularized Multimarginal Optimal Transport
Vladimir Kostic, Saverio Salzo, Massimilano Pontil

Abstract
In this work we propose a batch version of the Greenkhorn algorithm for multimarginal regularized optimal transport problems. Our framework is general enough to cover, as particular cases, some existing algorithms like Sinkhorn and Greenkhorn algorithm for the bi-marginal setting, and (greedy) MultiSinkhorn for multimarginal optimal transport. We provide a complete convergence analysis, which is based on the properties of the iterative Bregman projections (IBP) method with greedy control. Global linear rate of convergence and explicit bound on the iteration complexity are obtained. When specialized to above mentioned algorithms, our results give new insights and/or improve existing ones.

Introduction

Over the recent years the field of optimal transport (OT)
[
24
]
has received significant attention in machine learning and data science, as it provides natural and powerful tools to compare probability distributions. In this paper we study a general class of OT problems known as multimarginal optimal transport (MOT), whereby several probability distributions are coupled together in order to compute a measure of their association, see e.g.
[
20
,
5
]
.
MOT is receiving increasing interest due to its numerous applications, ranging from density functional theory in quantum chemistry, to fluid dynamics, to economics, to image processing, just to name a few, see
[
21
]
and
references therein. Particularly, in machine learning, MOT is important for generative adversarial networks (GANs)
[
8
]
, domain adaptation
[
15
]
, Wasserstein barycenters
[
1
]
,
clustering
[
19
]
, and Bayesian inference of joint distributions
[
14
]
,
among others.

We focus on discrete MOT, in which, given
m
finitely supported probability distributions, we wish to compute an optimal joint distribution which solves a linear program, whose objective function
involves a cost tensor and the constraint set requires the joint distribution to have the given individual ones as its marginals. It is well known that addressing directly the MOT problem is computationally prohibitive. Furthermore, unlike the bi-marginal case, MOT is NP-Hard for certain costs, even approximately
[
2
]
. To overcome this issue, regularization techniques have been widely considered. The key insight is to add a strongly convex regularizer to the MOT objective. A popular choice in the bi-marginal setting is entropic regularization, which leads to the well-known Sinkhorn divergence.
However, the study of computational regularized multimarginal optimal transport (RMOT) is less developed, and the objective of this work is to devise efficient algorithms with convergence guarantees.

Related work.

Two popular frameworks for solving regularized OT problems are iterative Bregman projections (IBP) and alternating dual minimization. Both have been extensively studied in the bi-marginal case and two popular offsprings include Sinkhorn and Greenkhorn algorithms. In contrast, the theory of general multimarginal OT is less understood. The multimarginal version of Sinkhron algorithm was first proposed by
[
4
]
and its convergence was established within the framework of
cyclic IBP
in finite dimension. More recently, the work
[
12
]
extended the result to infinite dimension and in
[
7
]
even global linear rate of convergence was obtained. On the other hand, the work
[
17
]
proposed a version of
multimarginal Sinkhorn
where, at each iteration, the marginal to project on is chosen in a
greedy
way. In the same work acceleration using Nesterov’s estimate sequences was also proposed and near-linear time computational complexity bounds were obtained. Finally, alternating dual minimization framework was used in
[
23
]
with Nesterov’s momentum acceleration to build a competitive algorithm against the greedy multimarginal Sinkhorn. Both papers
[
17
,
23
]
derived sub-linear computational complexity bounds for the considered RMOT algorithms, but no linear convergence rates were studied.

Contribution.

After the introduction of RMOT as a Bregman projection problem in Sec.
2
, in Sec.
3
we propose a batch version of the popular Greenkhorn algorithm for RMOT that greedily selects at each iteration a marginal and a batch of its components of prescribed sizes. In Sec.
4
, we establish its linear convergence, providing also explicit rates depending on problem data in two important cases: 1) batch Greenkhorn for bi-marginal optimal transport and 2) (greedy) MultiSinkhorn of
[
17
]
. Moreover, in these two cases we also provide iteration complexity bounds that improve state-of-the-art results.
We stress that the explicit rate we obtain for the greedy multimarginal Sinkhorn is strictly better than the one recently derived in
[
7
]
, for cyclic multimarginal Sinkhorn. This shows the effectiveness of the greedy option for speeding up the convergence of this type of algorithms.

with

.
The space
{\mathbb{X}}
is an Euclidean space when endowed with the standard scalar product and norm, defined as

\langle\pi,\pi^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\pi_{j}\quad\lVert\pi\rVert^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\pi_{j}^{2}\qquad\forall\,\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}.

(1.1)

For every
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
we denote by
\pi\odot\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}

,

For a function

2
Entropic RMOT as the Bregman projection problem

In this section we formally introduce the discrete multimarginal optimal transport problem and its regularized versions, emphasizing its connection with the Bregman projection problem.

Let
\mathsf{a}_{k}\in\Delta_{n_{k}}
,
k\in[m]
be prescribed histograms. MOT consists in solving a linear program

\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})}\langle\mathsf{C},\pi\rangle,

(2.1)

where
\mathsf{C}\in{\mathbb{X}}
is a given cost tensor and
\Pi(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})
, called
transport polytope
, is the covex set of nonnegative tensors in
{\mathbb{X}}
whose marginals are

\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{m}
. More specifically

\Pi(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})=\big\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}_{+}\,\big|\,\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)=\mathsf{a}_{k},\text{ for every }k\in[m]\big\},

(2.2)

where for all
k\in[m]
, we denote by
\mathsf{R}_{k}\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
the
k
-th push-forward projection operator
, defined as

(\forall\,j_{k}\in[n_{k}])\quad\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)_{j_{k}}=\sum_{j_{-k}\in\mathcal{J}_{-k}}\pi_{(j_{-k},j_{k})},

(2.3)

so that
\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)
is the
k
-th marginal of
\pi
.

As noted in the introduction, problem (
2.1
) may be hard to solve. We thus consider a regularized version using the (negative) Boltzmann-Shannon entropy
\mathsf{H}(\pi):=\sum_{j}\pi_{j}(\log\pi_{j}-1)
,
\pi\in\text{\rm dom}\,\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{X}}_{+}
.
For a cost tensor
\mathsf{C}\in{\mathbb{X}}_{+}
, the regularized multimarginal optimal transport (RMOT) problem consists in computing

\pi^{\star}=\argmin_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})}\langle\mathsf{C},\pi\rangle+\eta\mathsf{H}(\pi),

(2.4)

where
\eta>0
is a regularization parameter.
Now, recalling that the Kulback-Leibler (KL) divergence
\mathsf{KL}\colon{\mathbb{X}}\times{\mathbb{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
is the
Bregman distance
associated to the (negative) entropy
\mathsf{H}
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathsf{H}(\pi)-\mathsf{H}(\pi^{\prime})-\langle\pi-\pi^{\prime},\nabla\mathsf{H}(\pi^{\prime})\rangle&\text{if }\pi^{\prime}\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\mathsf{H})\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\pi_{j}\log(\pi_{j}/\pi^{\prime}_{j})-\pi_{j}+\pi^{\prime}_{j}&\text{if }\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}_{++},\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}_{+}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(2.5)

and considering that
\text{\rm dom}\,\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{X}}_{+}
,
it easy to check that (
2.4
) can be re-written as

\pi^{\star}=\argmin_{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)=\mathsf{a}_{k},k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\xi),

(2.6)

where
\xi=\nabla\mathsf{H}^{*}(-\mathsf{C}/\eta)=\exp(-\mathsf{C}/\eta)\in{\mathbb{X}}_{+}
is the
Gibbs kernel
tensor. Hence, the solution of (
2.6
) is nothing but the
KL projection of
\xi
onto the affine set
\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}\,|\,(\forall\,k\in[m])\ \mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)=\mathsf{a}_{k}\}
.
Indeed, in general, for an arbitrary closed convex set
{\cal C}\subset{\mathbb{X}}

such that
\mathsf{C}\cap{\mathbb{X}}_{++}\neq\varnothing
and a point
\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}_{++}
, the KL projection of
\pi
onto
{\cal C}
is defined as

{\cal P}_{\cal C}(\pi):=\argmin_{\gamma\in{\cal C}}\mathsf{KL}(\gamma,\pi),

(2.7)

while its KL distance to
{\cal C}
is defined as
\mathsf{KL}_{{\cal C}}(\pi):=\mathsf{KL}({\cal P}_{{\cal C}}(\pi),\pi)
.

3
Greedy KL projections for entropic RMOT

Now we focus on the entropic-regularized MOT problem (
2.4
),
in the equivalent form (
2.6
).
Recalling definition (
2.3
), we
introduce the linear operator

\mathsf{R}\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}},\qquad\mathsf{R}(\pi)=(\mathsf{R}_{1}(\pi),\dots,\mathsf{R}_{m}(\pi)).

(3.1)

Then, the constraints in (
2.6
) define the affine set

\Pi=\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}\;|\;\mathsf{R}(\pi)=(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})\}.

(3.2)

We recall that the positiveness constrain embodied in problem (
2.4
) can be absorbed in the entropy function
\mathsf{H}
, since
\text{\rm dom}\,\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{X}}_{+}
, so that problem (
2.4
) is equivalent to the computation of the KL projection of the Gibbs kernel
\xi
onto the affine set
\Pi
. However, it is possible to prove (see equation (
A.11
) in Appendix
A
) that

{\cal P}_{\Pi}(\xi)={\cal P}_{\Pi}(\xi\odot\otimes_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{a}_{k})
.
Therefore, we will equivalently target the computation of the KL projection of
the normalized Gibbs kernel

\xi\odot\otimes_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{a}_{k}
.
In order to compute such projection, in the following, we will represent
the set
\Pi
as an intersection of simpler affine sets
and then we will use the alternating KL projection algorithm. More precisely, we will rewrite the set (
3.2
) as an intersection of affine sets, obtained via specific
sketching
, on which KL projections have closed forms.

We consider sketches that cover several popular algorithms (multimarginal Sinkhorn of
[
4
]
, greedy MultiSinkhorn of
[
17
]
, bi-marginal Greenkhorn of
[
3
]
).
To that purpose, for each
k\in[m]
(which refers to the
k
-th marginal) and each batch
L\subset[n_{k}]

we consider the
canonical injection

\mathsf{S}_{(k,L)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{L}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}

of
\mathbb{R}^{L}
into
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
, meaning that for each

\mathsf{u}=(u_{j_{k}})_{j_{k}\in L}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
,

\mathsf{S}_{(k,L)}\mathsf{u}
is the vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}

obtained from the completion of
\mathsf{u}
with zero entries. Then, since the adjoint operator

\mathsf{S}^{*}_{(k,L)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}\to\mathbb{R}^{L}
is the standard projection, we can define

\mathsf{R}_{(k,L)}:=\mathsf{S}^{*}_{(k,L)}\mathsf{R}\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to\mathbb{R}^{L},

(3.3)

and the set

\displaystyle\Pi_{(k,L)}:

\displaystyle=\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}\,|\,\mathsf{S}_{(k,L)}^{*}\mathsf{R}(\pi)=\mathsf{S}_{(k,L)}^{*}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{a}_{m})\}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}\,|\,\mathsf{R}_{(k,L)}(\pi)={\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L}\}

\displaystyle=\{\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}\,|\,(\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi))_{\lvert L}={\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L}\}.

Note that in the definition of
\Pi_{(k,L)}

we ask for the
k
-th marginal of
\pi
to be equal
to
\mathsf{a}_{k}
only on the components in
L
.
Now, given
\tau=(\tau_{k})_{1\leq k\leq m}
a vector of batch sizes, we set

{\cal I}(\tau)=\{(k,L)\,|\,k\in[m],L\subset[n_{k}]\;|\;|L|\leq\tau_{k}\}

and obtain
\Pi=\bigcap_{(k,L)\in{\cal I}(\tau)}\Pi_{(k,L)}
.

Hence, the
iterative Bregman projections
(IBP) algorithm, for problem (
2.6
), leads to the following procedure. Let
\pi^{0}=

\xi\odot\otimes_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{a}_{k}=e^{-\mathsf{C}/\eta}\odot\otimes_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{a}_{k}\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\mathsf{H})={\mathbb{X}}_{++}
and define the sequence
\pi^{t}
recursively as
follows

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{for}\;t=0,1,\ldots\\
\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\text{choose }(k_{t},L_{t})\in{\cal I}(\tau),\\
\pi^{t+1}={\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t}).\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(3.5)

Since the generalized Pythagora’s theorem for Bregman projections
(see equation (
A.10
) in Appendix
A
) yields that
\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t})+\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}))
, in (
3.5
) one may choose the sets in a
greedy
manner as

(k_{t},L_{t})=\argmax_{(k,L)\in{\cal I}(\tau)}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}),

(3.6)

so that

(k_{t},L_{t})=\argmin_{(k,L)\in{\cal I}(\tau)}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}))\,\text{ and }\,\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t+1})=\min_{(k,L)\in{\cal I}(\tau)}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t})).

(3.7)

This means that the next iterate is chosen, among the possible projections,
as the one which is
the closest to the target set
\Pi
. Notable examples of existing algorithms that fit in this framework are greedy multimarginal Sinkhorn of

[
17
]
(
\tau_{k}=n_{k}
) and bi-marginal Greenkhorn of
[
3
]
(
m=2
,
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=1
). We emphasize that
this greedy strategy
typically leads to the best performance, provided that it can be implemented efficiently.
In the following proposition and subsequent remak we show that
the projection onto the sets

\Pi_{(k,L)}
can be computed in a closed form and that the greedy choice of the sets
\Pi_{(k,L)}
’s can indeed be implemented efficiently.
The proof is postponed in Appendix
B
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
\pi\in{\mathbb{X}}_{+}
,
k\in[m]
and
L\subset[n_{k}]
,

{\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi)=\nabla\mathsf{H}^{*}\big(\nabla\mathsf{H}(\pi)+\mathsf{R}_{(k,L)}^{*}(\bar{\mathsf{u}})\big)=\pi\odot\exp\big(\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L)}(\bar{\mathsf{u}})\big),

(3.8)

where

\bar{\mathsf{u}}=\argmin_{\mathsf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}}\mathsf{H}^{*}\big(\nabla\mathsf{H}(\pi)+\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L)}(\mathsf{u})\big)-\mathsf{H}^{*}(\nabla\mathsf{H}(\pi))-\langle{\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L},\mathsf{u}\rangle=\log\frac{{\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L}}{{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)}_{\lvert L}},

(3.9)

and, consequently, for every

,

({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi))_{j}=\pi_{j}\times\begin{cases}\dfrac{a_{k,j_{k}}}{{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)}_{j_{k}}}&\text{if }j_{k}\in L,\\[8.61108pt]
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.10)

Moreover,

\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi)=\mathsf{KL}\big({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi),\pi)=\mathsf{KL}({\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)_{\lvert L}).

(3.11)

.

4
Convergence theory for Batch Greenkhorn algorithm

Results on the convergence of general IBP are typically without any rates
[
6
,
9
,
10
]
, with the notable exception of
[
16
]
where the explicit local linear rate for the greedy IBP was derived. In the following we prove global linear convergence and derive the explicit dependence of the rate on the given data in two important cases. Moreover, we also provide an analysis of the iteration complexity of Algorithm
1
.

Based on the properties of operators
\mathsf{R}
and
\mathsf{R}_{(k,L)}
we can derive the main results using the properties of KL as Bregman divergence. The proofs are given in Appendix
C
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Algorithm
1
converges linearly. More precisely, if
(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t})_{k\in[m]}
are generated by Algorithm
1
, then the primal iterates given by (
3.12
) converge linearly in KL divergence to
\pi^{\star}
given by (
2.6
), i.e.,

(\forall\,t\in\mathbb{N})\quad\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{\star},\pi^{t})\leq\bigg(1-\frac{e^{-(2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+3M_{1})}}{b_{\tau}-1}\bigg)^{t}\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{\star},\pi^{0}),

(4.1)

where
b_{\tau}=\sum_{k\in[m]}\lceil n_{k}/\tau_{k}\rceil
, and
0<M_{1}<+\infty
is a constant independent of the batch sizes that satisfies

\lVert\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{v}^{\star}_{k}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{v}^{t}_{k}\rVert_{\infty}\leq M_{1}

for
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and suppose that
\eta>\varepsilon
.
For Algorithm
1
, the number of iterations required to reach the stopping criterion
d_{t}:=\max_{k\in[m]}\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon
satisfies

t\leq 2+\max_{k\in[m]}\bigg\lceil\frac{n_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bigg\rceil\frac{5M_{2}}{\varepsilon}(2+M_{2}\eta),

(4.2)

where
0<M_{2}<+\infty
is a constant independent of the batch sizes such that
\sum_{k\in[m]}\lVert\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t}\rVert\leq M_{2}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.3
.

We stress that the constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
considered in the above theorems
always exist (see the proofs in the Appendix

), but
we could not have obtained
general explicit expression for them depending on the problem data,
which are valid for any
m
and
(\tau_{k})_{k\in[m]}
.
On the other hand,
in the following, we show the important cases
m=2
and
(\tau_{k})_{k\in[m]}
arbitrary
and

(\tau_{k})_{k\in[m]}=(n_{k})_{k\in[m]}

),

5
Conclusions, limitations and future work

We present a new algorithm for solving multimarginal entropic regularized
optimal transport problems, called
batch Greenkhorn
, which
is an extension of the popular Greenkhorn algorithm, that can handle
multiple marginals and at each iteration select in a greedy fashion
a batch of components of a marginal.
We study the convergence of the algorithm in the
framework of the iterative Bregman projections method,
providing novel linear rate of convergence as well as iteration
complexity bounds. We made a comprehensive comparison with
existing results showing the improvements over the state-of-the-art.
A problem which remains open is that of deriving bounds on the dual variables with
an explicit dependence on the given problem data for
m\geq 3
when the batch is not full. According to our general Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, this will allow to have
explicit linear rate and iteration complexity in all possible cases.
Additional research directions are the extension of our analysis to infinite dimensions and general convex regularizers, implementing batch Greenkhorn with structured costs, and analyze the impact of parallel computations.

Appendix A
Bregman divergences and Bregman projections

In this section we recall few facts on Bregman distance and Bregman projections onto affine sets.
In the following
X
is an Euclidean space and
\phi\colon X\to\left]-\infty,+\infty\right]
is an extended-real valued function. The set of minimizers of the function
\phi

is denoted by
\argmin_{x\in X}\phi(x)
, the
domain
of
\phi
is

\text{\rm dom}\,\phi:=\{x\in X\,|\,\phi(x)<+\infty\}
and
\phi
is
proper
when
\text{\rm dom}\,\phi\neq\varnothing
.
The function
\phi
is
convex
if
\phi(tx+(1-t)y)\leq t\phi(x)+(1-t)\phi(y)
for all
x,y\in\text{\rm dom}\,\phi
and

t\in[0,1]
. If the above inequality is strict when
0<t<1
and
x\neq y
, the function is
strictly convex
.
The function
\phi
is
closed
if the sublevel sets
\{x\in X\,|\,\phi(x)\leq t\}
are closed in
X
for
any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For a convex function
\phi\colon X\to\left]-\infty,+\infty\right]
, we denote by
\phi^{*}
its
Fenchel conjugate
,
that is,
\phi^{*}\colon X\to\left]-\infty,+\infty\right]
,
\phi^{*}(y):=\sup_{x\in X}\{\langle x,y\rangle-\phi(x)\}
. The conjugate of a convex
function is always closed and convex, and if
\phi
is proper closed and convex, then
(\phi^{*})^{*}=\phi
.

A proper closed and convex function
\phi
is
essentially smooth
if it is differentiable on

\mathrm{int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)\neq\varnothing
, and
\lVert\nabla\phi(x_{n})\rVert\to+\infty
whenever

x_{n}\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
and
x_{n}\to x\in\mathrm{bdry}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
. The function
\phi
is

essentially strictly convex
if
\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi^{*})\neq\varnothing
and is strictly convex on every
convex subset of
\text{\rm dom}\,\partial\phi
. A
Legendre
function is a proper closed and convex function
which is also essentially smooth and essentially strictly convex. A function is Legendre if and only if its conjugate is so.
Moreover, if
\phi
is a Legendre function, then
\nabla\phi\colon\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)\to\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi^{*})
and

\nabla\phi^{*}\colon\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi^{*})\to\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
are bijective, inverses of each other, and continuous. Given a Legendre function
\phi
, the
Bregman distance
associated to

\phi
is the function
D_{\phi}\colon X\times X\to[0,+\infty]
such that

D_{\phi}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\phi(x)-\phi(y)-\langle x-y,\nabla\phi(y)\rangle&\text{if }y\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(A.1)

Fact A.1
.

Let
\phi
be a Legendre function on
X
. Then the following hold

(i)

(\forall\,\pi,\gamma\in\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)\quad D_{\phi}(\pi,\gamma)+D_{\phi}(\gamma,\pi)=\langle\pi-\gamma,\nabla\phi(\pi)-\nabla\phi(\gamma)\rangle
.

(ii)

(\forall\,\pi,\gamma\in\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)\quad D_{\phi}(\pi,\gamma)=D_{\phi^{*}}(\nabla\phi(\gamma),\nabla\phi(\pi))
.

(iii)

If
\phi
is twice differentiable, then

(\forall\,\pi,\gamma\in\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)(\exists\,\xi\in[\pi,\gamma])\qquad D_{\phi}(\pi,\gamma)=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\nabla^{2}\phi(\xi)(\pi-\gamma),\pi-\gamma\rangle
,

where
[\pi,\gamma]=\{(1-\alpha)\pi+\alpha\gamma\,|\,\alpha\in[0,1]\}

is the segment with end points
\pi
and
\gamma
.

(iv)

Suppose that
\phi
is twice differentiable on
\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
. Then

\big(\forall\pi\!\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi),\,\nabla^{2}\phi(\pi)\text{ is invertible}\big)\Leftrightarrow\big(\phi^{*}\!\text{ is twice differentiable}\big).

(A.2)

Fact A.2
.

Let
\phi
be a Legendre function on
X
and
\phi^{*}
be it’s Fenchel conjugate. If
\text{\rm dom}\,\phi^{*}
is open, then the following hold

(i)

For every
\pi\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
,
the sublevel sets of
D_{\phi}(\pi,\cdot)
are compact.

(ii)

For every
\pi\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
,
and every sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)

D_{\phi}(\pi,\gamma_{k})\to 0\ \Rightarrow\ \gamma_{k}\to\pi.

(A.3)

Let
{\cal C}\subset X
be an affine set, represented
as follows

A\colon X\to Y,\qquad b\in\text{\rm Im}(A),\qquad{\cal C}:=\{\pi\in X\,|\,A\pi=b\},

(A.4)

for some linear operator
A
between
X
and another Euclidean space
Y
.
Given a Legendre function
\phi\colon X\to\left]-\infty,+\infty\right]
and
\pi\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
, the
Bregman projection
of
\pi
onto
{\cal C}
is defined as
the unique solution, denoted by
{\cal P}^{\phi}_{\cal C}(\pi)
, of the optimization problem

\min_{\gamma\in{\cal C}}D_{\phi}(\gamma,\pi)=\min_{\gamma\in{\cal C}}\phi(\gamma)-\phi(\pi)-\langle\gamma-\pi,\nabla\phi(\pi)\rangle

(A.5)

and the optimal value defines the
Bregman distance from
\pi
to
{\cal C}

and is denoted by
D_{{\cal C}}^{\phi}(\pi)
.
The dual of the above problem is

\min_{\lambda\in Y}\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\pi)+A^{*}\lambda)-\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\pi))-\langle b,\lambda\rangle

(A.6)

and strong duality holds, meaning that

\min_{\gamma\in{\cal C}}D_{\phi}(\gamma,\pi)=-\min_{\lambda\in Y}\big[\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\pi)+A^{*}\lambda)-\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\pi))-\langle b,\lambda\rangle\big].

(A.7)

Moreover, the following KKT conditions hold for
a couple
(\pi^{\star},\lambda^{\star})
solving the primal and dual problem above

\pi^{\star}\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi),\quad A\pi^{\star}=b\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\phi(\pi)+A^{*}\lambda^{\star}=\nabla\phi(\pi^{\star}).

(A.8)

Note that the KKT conditions characterizes the projection, so that

\pi^{\star}={\cal P}^{\phi}_{\cal C}(\pi)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \big(\pi^{\star}\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi),\ A\pi^{\star}=b,\ \text{and}\ \nabla\phi(\pi)-\nabla\phi(\pi^{\star})\in\text{\rm Im}(A^{*})\big).

(A.9)

Finally we mention the
generalized Pythagora’s theorem
. If
{\cal C}_{1}
is an affine set such that
{\cal C}\subset{\cal C}_{1}
, then, for every
\pi\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi)
it holds

D_{\cal C}^{\phi}(\pi)=D_{{\cal C}_{1}}^{\phi}(\pi)+D_{{\cal C}}^{\phi}({\cal P}^{\phi}_{{\cal C}_{1}}(\pi)).

(A.10)

Moreover, in this case
{\cal P}^{\phi}_{\cal C}(\pi)={\cal P}^{\phi}_{\cal C}({\cal P}^{\phi}_{{\cal C}_{1}}(\pi))
, and

(\forall\gamma\in\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm dom}\,\phi))\quad\nabla\phi(\gamma)-\nabla\phi(\pi)\in\text{\rm Im}(A^{*})\Longleftrightarrow{\cal P}_{\cal C}(\gamma)={\cal P}_{\cal C}(\pi).

(A.11)

In the following we let
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\left]-\infty,+\infty\right[
be the (negative)

Boltzmann-Shannon entropy
, that is,

\phi(t)=\begin{cases}t\log t-t&\text{if }t>0\\
0&\text{if }t=0\\
+\infty&\text{if }t<0.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\phi^{*}(s)=\exp(s)
.
We define the Bregman distance associated to
\phi

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\phi(s)-\phi(t)-\phi^{\prime}(t)(s-t)&\text{if }t>0\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}s\log\frac{s}{t}-s+t&\text{if }t>0\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(A.12)

which is nothing but the
Kullback-Leibler divergence
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
M>0
. The following hold.

(i)

The function
\phi
is strongly convex on the interval

\left]0,M\right]

with modulus of strong convexity equal to
1/M
. Moreover,
for every
a>0
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
,
D_{\phi}(s,t)=aD_{\phi}(s/a,t/a)
.

(ii)

The function
\phi^{*}
is strongly convex on the interval

\left[-M,+\infty\right[
with modulus of strong convexity equal to
\exp(-M)
.

Proof.

(i)
: It follows from the fact that the second derivative of
\phi

is
\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=1/t
, which is bounded from below away from zero
on the interval
\left]0,M\right]
by the constant
1/M
. the second part follows directly from the definition (
A.12
).

(ii)
:
It follows from the fact that the second derivative of
\exp
is bounded from below away from zero on the interval
\left[-M,+\infty\right[
by
\exp(-M)
.
∎

The negative entropy and the Kullback-Leibler divergence on
{\mathbb{X}}
are

\mathsf{H}(\gamma)=\sum_{j}\phi(\gamma_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{KL}(\gamma,\pi)=\sum_{j}D_{\phi}(\gamma_{j},\pi_{j}).

(A.13)

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\pi,\gamma,\alpha\in{\mathbb{X}}_{++}
and suppose that

0<M_{1}\leq\min_{j}\frac{\min\{\pi_{j},\gamma_{j}\}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq\max_{j}\frac{\max\{\pi_{j},\gamma_{j}\}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq M_{2}.

Then, setting
\mathsf{A}=\alpha\odot(\cdot)\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
(which is a positive diagonal operator), we have

\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\gamma)\geq\max\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{2}\lVert\log\pi-\log\gamma\rVert_{\mathsf{A}}^{2},\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\lVert\pi-\gamma\rVert_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}^{2}\right\}.

(A.14)

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
A.3
(i)

that, for
a>0
and
s,t>0
such that
s/a,t/a\leq M
, we have

D_{\phi}(s,t)=aD_{\phi}(s/a,t/a)\geq a(2M)^{-1}\lvert s/a-t/a\rvert^{2}=(2M)^{-1}a^{-1}\lvert s-t\rvert^{2}
. Thus, since
\gamma_{j}/\alpha_{j},\pi_{j}/\alpha_{j}\leq M_{2}
,
we have

\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\gamma)=\sum_{j}D_{\phi}(\pi_{j},\gamma_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\lvert\pi_{j}-\gamma_{j}\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\lVert\pi-\gamma\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}.

Now, it follows from Proposition
A.3
(ii)
that
for every
a,s,t>0
such that
s/a,t/a\geq e^{-M}
we have
\log(s/a),\log(t/a)\geq-M
and hence
D_{\phi}(s,t)=aD_{\phi}(s/a,t/a)=aD_{\phi^{*}}(\log(s/a),\log(t/a))\geq a(e^{-M}/2)\lvert\log s-\log t\rvert^{2}
. Therefore, since
\pi_{j}/\alpha_{j},\gamma_{j}/\alpha_{j}\geq M_{1}
, we have

\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\gamma)=\sum_{j}D_{\phi}(\pi_{j},\gamma_{j})\geq\frac{M_{1}}{2}\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\lvert\log\pi_{j}-\log\gamma_{j}\rvert^{2}=\frac{M_{1}}{2}\lVert\log\pi-\log\gamma\rVert_{\mathsf{A}}^{2}.\qed

Proof.

Then it follows from Proposition

(\forall\,j\in\mathcal{J})\quad({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}))_{j}=\pi^{t}_{j}\times\begin{cases}\dfrac{a_{k,j_{k}}}{{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)}_{j_{k}}}&\text{if }j_{k}\in L,\\[8.61108pt]
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(C.13)

and hence

(\forall\,j\in\mathcal{J})\quad\frac{({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}))_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq\frac{\pi^{t}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\max\bigg\{1,\dfrac{a_{k,j_{k}}}{{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)}_{j_{k}}}\bigg\}.

(C.14)

Now, since
\mathsf{C}\geq 0
, we have

\frac{\pi^{t}}{\alpha}=\exp\Big(-\frac{\mathsf{C}}{\eta}+\mathsf{V}^{t}\Big)\leq\exp(\mathsf{V}^{t})\leq\exp(\lVert\mathsf{V}\rVert_{\infty})\leq\exp(M_{1})

(C.15)

and

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi)}_{j_{k}}}{a_{k,j_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{j_{-k}\in\mathcal{J}_{-k}}\pi^{t}_{(j_{-k},j_{k})}}{a_{k,j_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{j_{-k}\in\mathcal{J}_{-k}}\exp\big(-\mathsf{C}_{(j_{-k},j_{k})}/\eta+\mathsf{V}^{t}_{(j_{-k},j_{k})}\big)\prod_{h=1}^{m}a_{h,j_{h}}}{a_{k,j_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{-k}\in\mathcal{J}_{-k}}\exp\big(-\mathsf{C}_{(j_{-k},j_{k})}/\eta+\mathsf{V}^{t}_{(j_{-k},j_{k})}\big)\prod_{h\neq k}^{m}a_{h,j_{h}}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\big(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1}\big)\sum_{j_{-k}\in\mathcal{J}_{-k}}\prod_{h\neq k}^{m}a_{h,j_{h}}

\displaystyle=\exp\big(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1}\big)\prod_{h\neq k}^{m}\bigg(\sum_{j_{h}=1}^{n_{h}}a_{h,j_{h}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\big(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1}\big),

(C.16)

since
\sum_{j_{h}=1}^{n_{h}}a_{h,j_{h}}=1
. Therefore, by (
C.14
),
(
C.15
), and (
C
),

\frac{{\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t})}{\alpha}\leq\exp(M_{1})\exp\big(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+M_{1}\big)=\exp\big(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+2M_{1}\big)

and hence, recalling (
C.15
),

\max\bigg\{\frac{\pi^{t}}{\alpha},\frac{{\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t})}{\alpha}\bigg\}\leq\exp\big(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+2M_{1}\big).

(C.17)

We now prove that

\exp\big(-2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}-3M_{1}\big)b^{-1}_{\tau}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t})=\max_{(k,L)\in\mathcal{I}(\tau)}\mathsf{KL}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}),\pi^{t}).

(C.18)

From this inequality it will follow, using
the Pythgora’s theorem
\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t+1})+\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t})=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})
, that

\exp(-2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}-3M_{1})b^{-1}_{\tau}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})-\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t+1})

(C.19)

and hence

\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t+1})\leq\big(1-\exp(-2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}-3M_{1})b^{-1}_{\tau}\big)\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t}),

(C.20)

which gives the statement. Thus, it remains to prove (
C.18
).
Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
and, for the sake of brevity set

\pi:=\pi^{t},\quad\pi_{k}^{h}:={\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}}(\pi^{t})\quad\mathsf{v}_{k}:=\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}^{t}_{k},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h}:=J^{(k)*}_{L^{h}_{k}}\mathsf{v}_{k}.

(C.21)

Let, for every
k\in[m]
,
(L_{k}^{h})_{1\leq h\leq s_{k}}
be a partition of
[n_{k}]

made of non empty sets of cardinality exactly
\tau_{k}
possibly except for the last one,
such that
L_{k_{t-1}}^{1}=L_{t-1}
, where
s_{k}=\lceil n_{k}/\tau_{k}\rceil
.
Then, it follows from (
C.10
) that

\frac{\pi^{\star}}{\pi}=\frac{\exp(-\mathsf{C}/\eta)\exp(\mathsf{V}^{\star})}{\exp(-\mathsf{C}/\eta)\exp(\mathsf{V}^{t})}=\exp(\mathsf{V}^{\star}-\mathsf{V}^{t}).

(C.22)

Hence, recalling that
\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{m})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}\mathsf{v}_{k}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{v}_{k}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi)+\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\star})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{\star},\pi)+\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\star})

\displaystyle=\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\log(\pi^{\star}/\pi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\mathsf{V}^{\star}-\mathsf{V}^{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{m})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{k}(\mathsf{v}_{k})\rangle.

(C.23)

Moreover, recalling that
\mathsf{R}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}=J^{(k)*}_{L_{k}^{h}}\circ\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}
and

\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h}=J^{(k)*}_{L^{h}_{k}}(\mathsf{v}_{k})
, we have

\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}(\mathsf{v}_{k})=\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}\bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{n_{h}}J^{(k)}_{L_{k}^{h}}\circ J^{(k)*}_{L_{k}^{h}}(\mathsf{v}_{k})\bigg)=\sum_{h=1}^{n_{h}}\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})

(C.24)

and hence

\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi)+\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\star})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle.

(C.25)

Now, recalling the general definition of
\Pi_{(k,L)}
in (
3.4
),
since
\pi_{k}^{h}
and
\pi^{\star}
both belong to
\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}
,
we have that
\pi^{\star}-\pi_{k}^{h}\in\text{\rm Ker}(\mathsf{R}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})})=\text{\rm Im}(\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})})^{\perp}
and hence

\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle=\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi_{k}^{h},\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle+\langle\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle=\langle\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle

and hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi)+\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\star})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\langle\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi,\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\langle\mathsf{A}^{-1}(\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi),\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle_{\mathsf{A}},

(C.26)

where
\mathsf{A}
is the positive diagonal operator defined in Lemma
C.2
.
Now, it follows from (
C.12
), Lemma
A.4
,
and Lemma
C.2
that

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{t},\pi^{\star})

\displaystyle\geq(1/2)\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\lVert\log\pi^{\star}-\log\pi^{t}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

\displaystyle=(1/2)\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\lVert\mathsf{V}^{\star}-\mathsf{V}^{t}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

\displaystyle=(1/2)\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\lVert\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{m})\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

\displaystyle=(1/2)\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lVert\mathsf{v}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}_{k}}.

Moreover, recalling the definition of
\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h}
in (
C.21
), since
\mathsf{v}_{k}=\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}J_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}
and
(J_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k})_{h\in[s_{k}]}

is a finite orthogonal sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}}
w.r.t. the metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathsf{A}_{k}}
, we have

\lVert\mathsf{v}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}_{k}}=\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert J_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}_{k}}=\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}J_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}=\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert\mathsf{R}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}^{*}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}},

(C.27)

where we used equation (
C.8
) from Lemma
C.2
applied to
J_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}

and the fact that, by definition,
\mathsf{R}_{(j,L_{k}^{h})}=J^{(k)*}_{L_{k}^{h}}\mathsf{R}_{k}
.
Overall we get that

\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{t},\pi^{\star})\geq(1/2)\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert\mathsf{R}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}^{*}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

and hence (
C
) yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\langle\mathsf{A}^{-1}(\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi),\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}-\mathsf{KL}(\pi,\pi^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\langle\mathsf{A}^{-1}(\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi),\mathsf{R}^{*}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{h})\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}-\frac{1}{2}\exp(-\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta-M_{1})\lVert\mathsf{R}_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}^{*}\mathsf{v}^{h}_{k}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\exp(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+M_{1})}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert\mathsf{A}^{-1}(\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi)\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+M_{1})}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{h=1}^{s_{k}}\lVert(\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi)\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}},

where in the last inequality we used the Young-Fenchel inequality

\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathsf{A}}\leq\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert a\rVert_{\mathsf{A}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2\mu}\lVert b\rVert_{\mathsf{A}}^{2}
.
Now, recalling that we set
\pi=\pi^{t}
and
\pi_{k}^{h}={\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}}(\pi^{t})
, it follows from (
C.17
) and Lemma
A.4
that

\frac{1}{2}\lVert\pi_{k}^{h}-\pi\rVert_{\Theta^{-1}}^{2}\leq\exp\big(\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+2M_{1}\big)\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{k}^{h},\pi)
, and consequently

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big(2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+3M_{1}\big)\sum_{k\in[m]}\sum_{h\in[s_{k}]}\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{k}^{h},\pi)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big(2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+3M_{1}\big)\bigg(\sum_{k\in[m]}s_{k}-1\bigg)\max_{k\in[m]}\max_{h\in[s_{k}]}\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{k}^{h},\pi)

\displaystyle=\exp\big(2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+3M_{1}\big)\bigg(\sum_{k\in[m]}\lceil n_{k}/\tau_{k}\rceil-1\bigg)\max_{k\in[m]}\max_{h\in[s_{k}]}\mathsf{KL}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}}(\pi^{t}),\pi^{t})

\displaystyle\leq(b_{\tau}-1)\exp\big(2\lVert\mathsf{C}\rVert_{\infty}/\eta+3M_{1}\big)\max_{(k,L)\in{\cal I}(\tau)}\mathsf{KL}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,L)}}(\pi^{t}),\pi^{t}),

where in the second inequality we used that,
for
k=k_{t-1}
and
h=1
,
\pi_{k}^{h}={\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k_{t-1},L_{t-1})}}(\pi^{t})=\pi^{t}
(since by definition
\pi^{t}\in\Pi_{(k_{t-1},L_{t-1})}
), so that
\mathsf{KL}(\pi_{k}^{h},\pi)=0
.
This proves (
C.18
) and the proof is complete.
∎

We now provide a result concerning the convergence of numerical sequences
which is critical to analyze the iteration complexity of the algorithm. This result
has been first showed implicitly in
[
13
]
. We provide here a more explicit version together with a complete proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma C.3
.

Let
M,C>0
and
let
(\delta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(d_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences of positive numbers such that,
for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

(i)

\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}\geq\bigg(\dfrac{d_{t}}{C}\bigg)^{2}
,

(ii)

\delta_{t}\leq Md_{t}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and set
\bar{t}=\min\{t\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,d_{t}\leq\varepsilon\}
.
Then
\bar{t}\leq 1+2MC^{2}/\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Items
(i)
and
(ii)
imply that

\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}\geq\bigg(\frac{\delta_{t}}{MC}\bigg)^{2}.

Therefore, since
\delta_{t}\geq\delta_{t+1}
, we have

\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}_{t}}{M^{2}C^{2}}\geq\frac{\delta_{t}\delta_{t+1}}{M^{2}C^{2}}

and hence, dividing by
\delta_{t}\delta_{t+1}
,

\frac{1}{\delta_{t+1}}-\frac{1}{\delta_{t}}\geq\frac{1}{M^{2}C^{2}}.

Thus,

\frac{1}{\delta_{t}}-\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}-\frac{1}{\delta_{i+1}}\bigg)\geq\frac{t}{M^{2}C^{2}}

and hence we get the following rate of convergence for
the sequence
(\delta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}

\delta_{t}\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{t}{M^{2}C^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}.

(C.28)

Now, we let
\delta\in\left]0,\delta_{0}\right]
. We wish to determine the number of iterations such that
\delta_{t}\leq\delta
. It follows from (
C.28
) that

\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{t}{M^{2}C^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}\leq\delta\ \Leftrightarrow\ \ \frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{t}{M^{2}C^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \ t\geq M^{2}C^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}\bigg).

(C.29)

This means that if we take
t\geq M^{2}C^{2}(1/\delta-1/\delta_{0})
, we have
\delta_{t}\leq\delta
as desired. So we set
t=\lfloor M^{2}C^{2}(1/\delta-1/\delta_{0})\rfloor+1
.
Then, we have
\delta_{t}\leq\delta
. Now we have to cases. Suppose that
t<\bar{t}

and let
s\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
t+s=\bar{t}-1
. Then, for every
i=0,\dots,s
, since

t+i<\bar{t}
, we have
d_{t+i}>\varepsilon
and hence, using
(i)
,

\delta\geq\delta_{t}-\delta_{\bar{t}}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta_{t+i}}-\frac{1}{\delta_{t+i+1}}\bigg)\geq\sum_{i=0}^{s}\frac{d^{2}_{t+i}}{M^{2}C^{2}}\geq(s+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{M^{2}C^{2}},

(C.30)

which implies that
s+1\leq C^{2}\delta/\varepsilon^{2}
. Overall we have

\bar{t}=t+s+1\leq\bigg\lfloor M^{2}C^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}\bigg)\bigg\rfloor+1+C^{2}\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{M^{2}C^{2}}{\delta}-\frac{M^{2}C^{2}}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{C^{2}\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

(C.31)

Note that this inequality is true for any
\delta\in\left]0,\delta_{0}\right]
.
Now, suppose that
M\varepsilon\leq\delta_{0}
. Then we have

\bar{t}\leq 1+\min_{\delta\in\left]0,\delta_{0}\right]}\bigg(\frac{M^{2}C^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{C^{2}\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg)=1+2\frac{MC^{2}}{\varepsilon},

(C.32)

where the minimum is attained at
\delta=M\varepsilon\in\left]0,\delta_{0}\right]
.
On the other hand, if
\delta_{0}<M\varepsilon
, then the minimum on the right hand side of (
C.31
) is attained at
\delta=\delta_{0}
and hence

\bar{t}\leq 1+\frac{C^{2}\delta_{0}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{C^{2}M\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}}=1+\frac{MC^{2}}{\varepsilon}.

(C.33)

In any case, the statement follows.
∎

Remark C.4
.

The statement of Lemma
C.3
is equivalent to the fact that
the sequence
(\min_{0\leq s<t}d_{s})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to zero with rate
{\cal O}(1/t)
, i.e., that
for every integer
t>1
,

\min_{0\leq s<t}d_{s}\leq\frac{2MC^{2}}{t-1}.

Next, we prove the main result on the iteration complexity.

See
4.2

Proof.

For the sake of brevity let
\bar{b}=\max_{k\in[n]}\lceil n_{k}/\tau_{k}\rceil
and set, for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta_{t}:=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})
. Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary. Recalling (
C.23
), we have that

\delta_{t}=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})\leq\sum_{k\in[m]}\langle\pi^{\star}-\pi^{t},\mathsf{R}_{k}^{*}(\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t})\rangle\!=\!\sum_{k\in[m]}\langle\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}),\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t}\rangle,

which, using Hoölder inequality, yields

\delta_{t}\leq\sum_{k\in[m]}\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}\;\lVert\mathsf{v}_{k}^{\star}-\mathsf{v}_{k}^{t}\rVert_{\infty}\leq M_{2}d_{t}.

(C.34)

Now, we prove

\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}\geq\min\Big\{\frac{d_{t}^{2}}{5\bar{b}},\frac{\delta_{t}^{2}}{4M_{2}^{2}\bar{b}}\Big\}\geq\frac{\delta_{t}^{2}}{5M_{2}^{2}\bar{b}}.

(C.35)

Let for every
k\in[m]
,
(L_{k}^{h})_{1\leq h\leq[s_{k}]}
,
s_{k}:=\lceil n_{k}/\tau_{k}\rceil
, be a partition of
[n_{k}]
made of nonempty sets of cardinality exactly
\tau_{k}
, except maybe for the last one, such that
L_{k_{t}}^{1}=L_{t}
(not that necessarily the cardinality of
L_{t}
is
\tau_{k_{t}}
). Then, according to the greedy choice of
(k_{t},L_{t})
we have that

\bar{b}\,\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t})\geq\max_{k\in[m]}s_{k}\max_{h\in[s_{k}]}\;\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}}(\pi^{t})\geq\max_{k\in[m]}\sum_{h\in[s_{k}]}\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k,L_{k}^{h})}}(\pi^{t}).

Thus, equation (
3.11
) of Proposition
3.1
yields

\bar{b}\,\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t})\geq\max_{k\in[m]}\sum_{h\in[s_{k}]}\mathsf{KL}({\mathsf{a}_{k}}_{\lvert L_{k}^{h}},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})_{\lvert L_{k}^{h}})=\max_{k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})).

(C.36)

Now Pinsker’s inequality guaranties that, for every
k\in[m]

\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))\geq\frac{\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\tfrac{2}{3}\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}\rVert_{1}+\tfrac{4}{3}\lVert\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}}=\frac{\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{4}{3}\lVert\pi^{t}\rVert_{1}}\geq\frac{\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}^{2}}{2+\tfrac{4}{3}\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}},

(C.37)

where in the second inequality we used that
\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}\geq\lVert\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}-\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}\rVert_{1}=\lVert\pi^{t}\rVert_{1}-1
.
Thus, solving the quadratic inequality in
\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}\geq 0
we can conclude that

\lVert\mathsf{a}_{k}-\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})\rVert_{1}\leq\tfrac{2}{3}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))+\sqrt{(\tfrac{2}{3}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t})))^{2}+2\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))}.

Therefore, if
\max_{k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))\leq 1
, then
2+4d_{t}/3\leq 5
, and consequently,
\max_{k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))\geq d_{t}^{2}/5
, which,
using Pythagoras theorem and (
C.36
), yields

\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t})-\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi}(\pi^{t+1})=\mathsf{KL}_{\Pi_{(k_{t},L_{t})}}(\pi^{t})\geq\frac{d_{t}^{2}}{5\bar{b}}.

(C.38)

On the other hand, if
\max_{k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}(\mathsf{a}_{k},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{t}))>1
, it follows again from Pythagoras theorem and (
C.36
), that
\delta_{t}-\delta_{t+1}\geq 1/\bar{b}
. Moreover, since
\delta_{t}\leq\delta_{0}\leq M_{2}d_{0}\leq M_{2}(1+\lVert\pi^{0}\rVert_{1})\leq 2M_{2}
, we have that
1\geq\delta_{t}^{2}/(4M_{2}^{2})
, and (
C.35
) follows.

Now, similarly to what was done in the proof of Lemma
C.3

we can derive from (
C.35
) that

\delta_{t}\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}+\frac{t}{5M_{2}^{2}\bar{b}}\bigg)^{-1}.

(C.39)

Thus, if we take
r=\lfloor 5M_{2}^{2}\bar{b}\rfloor+1
we have
\delta_{r}\leq 1
.
Then, by (
3.11
) with
L=[n_{k}]
and Pythagoras theorem we have
that, for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathsf{KL}({\mathsf{a}_{k}},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{r+t}))=\mathsf{KL}({\cal P}_{\Pi_{(k,[n_{k}])}}(\pi^{r+t}),\pi^{r+t})\leq\mathsf{KL}(\pi^{\star},\pi^{r+t})=\delta_{r+t}\leq\delta_{r}\leq 1.

Thus,
\max_{k\in[m]}\mathsf{KL}({\mathsf{a}_{k}},\mathsf{R}_{k}(\pi^{r+t}))\leq 1

and for what we already saw,

\delta_{r+t}-\delta_{r+t+1}\geq\frac{d^{2}_{r+t}}{5\bar{b}}.

(C.40)

In the end the sequence
(\delta_{r+t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the two
assumptions of Lemma
C.3
with
C=\sqrt{5\bar{b}}
and

M=M_{2}
. Thus, we can conclude that the smallest
t
so that
d_{r+t}\leq\varepsilon

satisfies

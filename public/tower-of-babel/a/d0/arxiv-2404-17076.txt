1.
Introduction

For a transcendental entire map
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, the
Fatou set
, denoted by
{\pazocal{F}}(f)
, is defined as the set of points
z\in\mathbb{C}
for which there exists a neighbourhood
U
where the iterates
\{f^{n}\big|_{U}\}_{n\geq 0}
are normal in the sense of Montel. Its complement
{\pazocal{J}}(f)=\mathbb{C}\setminus{\pazocal{F}}(f)
, called the
Julia set
of
f
, is characterize by containing the
chaotic dynamical
part of the map
f
. Moreover, it is a well known result from Baker that repelling periodic points are dense in the Julia set,
[
Bak68
]
.

Given an integer
\ell\geq 2
, for
c\in{\pazocal{D}}(\ell,1)
we consider the one-parameter family of entire maps

f_{c}(z)=\ell z+c-(\ell-1)\log c-e^{z}.

By noting that for
\ell=c=2
, the function
f_{2}
corresponds to the one studied by Bergweiler in
[
Be95-1
]
, we named this as the
Bergweiler’s family
. Following Bergweiler’s ideas, authors in
[
EI23
]
gave a general description of the Fatou set of
f_{c}
, see Theorem
2.1
below. Although
{\pazocal{F}}(f_{c})
contains a hyperbolic Baker domain in the left-half plane, and wandering domains in a vertical strip, is it possible to prove that
f_{c}
is
E-hyperbolic

[
EI23
, Theorem 1]
, which is an extension of the comcept of hyperbolicity (or expanding) of rational maps to transcendental maps, also referred as
topologically hyperbolic
, see
[
Sta90
]
and
[
MU21
]
for further references.

Thermodynamic formalism has proven to be an appropriate theory to provide probabilistic description of the chaotic dynamical part of a dynamical system. In the context of holomorphic expanding/hyperbolic dynamical systems gives a rich and detailed information about the geometry of Julia sets, see
[
Bar95
]
,
[
KU02
]
,
[
MU08
]
, and the general survey
[
MU21
]
and the references therein.

Following ideas of Kotus and Urbański in
[
KU05
]
, the authors in
[
EI23
]
studied the
radial Julia set

{\pazocal{J}}_{r}(f_{\mathbf{c}})
, of each member of the family
f_{c}
obtaining important properties of the geometry of this set by establishing the existence and uniqueness of Gibbs and equilibrium states, studying spectral and asymptotic properties of corresponding Perron-Frobenius operators, showing that dynamical systems are ”strongly” mixing, and satisfy the Central Limit Theorem. Finally, as a main result, they obtained a Bowen’s formula for the Hausdorff dimension of the radial Julia set,
\operatorname{HD}({\pazocal{J}}_{r}(f_{c}))
.

The aim of this paper is to give a continuation of the previous work in
[
EI23
]
, in particular, to prove the real-analyticity of the Bowen’s formula for the map
c\mapsto\operatorname{HD}({\pazocal{J}}_{r}(f_{c}))
. We now briefly describe the organization of this paper.

In Section
2
, the dynamical description of the family
f_{c}
and its Fatou set
{\pazocal{F}}(f_{c})
is given as well as the definition of the projected map
F_{c}:Q\to Q
to the infinite cylinder
Q=\mathbb{C}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z}
, which is the central object of study. In Section
3
, we define the
transfer operator
and enumerate the results on thermodynamic formalism from
[
EI23
]
that will be useful in the main theorem. Section
4
contains
quasi-conformal
facts of the family
F_{c}
which imply its Hölder (analyticity) regularity. Finally, in Section
5
we prove the real-analyticity of the Hausdorff dimension of the radial Julia set.

2.
The Bergweiler’s family

In this section we give a general description of the dynamical plane of each member of the family of entire maps considered in this work.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell\geq 2
, take
c\in{\pazocal{D}}(\ell,1)
and consider the entire map given by

(1)

f_{c}(z):=f_{\ell,c}(z)=\ell z+c-(\ell-1)\log c-e^{z}.

We notice that the special parametrization of the family
f_{c}
, allows us to conclude that
z_{c}=\log c
is a fixed point. Since

(2)

f_{c}^{\prime}(z)=\ell-e^{z},

its multiplier is given by

\lambda_{c}=\ell-c,

which concludes that
z_{c}=\log c
is a (super-)attracting fixed point for
f_{c}
. Furthermore, from (
1
) we notice
f_{c}
has no asymptotic values, and from (
2
) their critical points are

\text{Crit}(f_{c})=\{\log\ell+2\pi ik:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Since
f_{c}(z+2\pi ik)=f_{c}(z)+\ell(2\pi ik)
, the singular values of
f_{c}
are given by

(3)

\text{sing}(f_{c}^{-1})=f_{c}(\text{Crit}(f_{c}))=\{f_{c}(\log\ell)+\ell(2\pi ik):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.\\

We are in position to give a general description of the Fatou set of each member
f_{c}
of the family, see
[
EI23
]
for further details.

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 1,
[
EI23
]
)
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell\geq 2
. For
c\in{\pazocal{D}}(\ell,1)
the Fatou set
{\pazocal{F}}(f_{c})
of the member
f_{c}
as defined in (
1
) consists of the following sets:

•

A univalent invariant hyperbolic Baker domain
{\pazocal{U}}_{\ell}
containing the half-plane
\{{\operatorname{Re}}(z)<-2\ell\}
and all of its preimages.

•

A simply connected (Böttcher) Schröeder domain
{\pazocal{U}}_{0}
containing the (super-)attracting fixed point
z_{c}=\log c
and all of its preimages.

•

Two sets of wandering domains from the
2k\pi i
-traslation of the attracting domain
{\pazocal{U}}_{0}
and all of its preimages.

It is easy to see from the above result, that the Euclidean distance holds

{\operatorname{dist}}({\pazocal{P}}(f_{c}),{\pazocal{J}}(f_{c}))>0,

where
{\pazocal{P}}(f_{c})
denotes the post-singular set of
f_{c}
define as

{\pazocal{P}}(f_{c})=\overline{\bigcup_{n\geq 0}f_{c}^{n}(\text{sing}(f_{c}^{-1}})).

Then
f_{c}
belongs to the class
C
described by Stallard in
[
Sta90
]
, also called E-hyperbolic or topologically hyperbolic class, see
[
MU21
]
for further references. It follows that the Lebesgue measure of the Julia set of
f_{c}
is zero,
[
Sta90
, Theorem B]
.

2.1.
Projection on the cylinder
\mathbb{C}/\sim

Consider the infinite cylinder
Q=\mathbb{C}/\sim
where
z\sim w
if and only if
z-w\in 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}
. The quotient space
\mathbb{C}/\sim
is provided with a Riemann surface structure from the quotient map

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\Pi:&\mathbb{C}&\rightarrow&Q\\
&z&\mapsto&[z].\end{array}

The relation
f_{c}(z+2\pi ik)=f_{c}(z)+\ell(2\pi ik)
means that
f_{c}
respects the equivalence relation
\sim
, so it induces a unique map

F_{c}:Q\to Q

such that
\Pi\circ f_{\mathbf{c}}=F_{c}\circ\Pi
. Following
[
Be95-2
]
we know that
{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})=\Pi({\pazocal{J}}(f_{c}))
, so from now on, we focus on
F_{c}
instead of the original function
f_{c}
. Using Theorem
2.1
we can characterize the dynamics under
F_{c}
of any point in the Fatou set
{\pazocal{F}}(F_{c})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given
z\in{\pazocal{F}}(F_{c})
, there are only two options for the limit of the sequence
\{z_{n}=F_{c}^{n}(z)\}_{n}^{\infty}
either

\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=\log c,

or

\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=-\infty.

On the other hand, since
f_{\mathbf{c}}
is E-hyperbolic, it can be proved that
F_{c}
is an expansive function.

Proposition 2.3

(Proposition 1,
[
EI23
]
)
.

There exist
L>0
and
\kappa>1
such that for every
z\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})
and every
n\geq 1
we have

|(F_{c}^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\geq L\kappa^{n}.

We consider the escaping subset on
{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})
given by

{\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(F_{c})=\{z:\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c}):\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{c}^{n}(z)=+\infty\},

and its corresponding set on
{\pazocal{J}}(f_{\mathbf{c}})=\Pi^{-1}({\pazocal{J}}(F_{c}))
as

{\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(f_{\mathbf{c}})=\{w\in{\pazocal{J}}(f_{\mathbf{c}}):\Pi(w)\in{\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(F_{c})\}.

Given the “asymptotic” similarity to the exponential function

, it is not difficult to notice that
\operatorname{HD}({\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(F))=\operatorname{HD}({\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(f_{c}))=\operatorname{HD}({\pazocal{J}}(F_{c}))=\operatorname{HD}({\pazocal{J}}(f_{c}))=2
, which makes these sets dynamically rather boring.

We denote by
{\pazocal{J}}_{r}(F_{c})
, called the

, to the complement on
{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})
of the escaping set,

{\pazocal{J}}_{r}(F_{c})={\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})\setminus{\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(F_{c})\ \text{ and }\ {\pazocal{J}}_{r}(f_{\mathbf{c}})={\pazocal{J}}(f_{\mathbf{c}})\setminus{\operatorname{I}_{\infty}}(f_{\mathbf{c}}).

This way, the thermodynamic formalism will be developed on this rather interesting set
[
MU21
, Lemma 4]
.

3.1.
Transfer Operator and Topological Pressure

Let
{\pazocal{C}}_{b}={\pazocal{C}}_{b}({\pazocal{J}}(F_{c}))

{\pazocal{L}}_{t}:{\pazocal{C}}_{b}\to{\pazocal{C}}_{b}

{\pazocal{L}}_{t}g(z)=\sum_{x\in F_{c}^{-1}(z)}|F_{c}^{\prime}(x)|^{-t}g(x).

For any

-iterate is defined as

We use the transfer operator and its iterates to define the

of the (non-compact) Julia set of

. First, for every

and every

, we define the

\underline{P}(t,z)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\log{\pazocal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathds{1}(z)\ \text{ and }\ \overline{P}(t,z)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\log{\pazocal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathds{1}(z).

Proposition 3.1

(Proposition 2,
[
EI23
]
)
.

Let
t>1
and
z\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})
, then the following statements hold:

•

\underline{P}(t,z)
and
\overline{P}(t,z)
are independent on the choice of
z\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})
.

•

\|{\pazocal{L}}_{t}\mathds{1}\|_{\infty}=\sup\{{\pazocal{L}}_{t}\mathds{1}(z):z\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c})\}<+\infty
.

•

For every
t>1
,
\underline{P}(t)
and
\overline{P}(t)
are convex, continuous and strictly decreasing functions and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{P}(t)=-\infty
.

•

\lim_{{\operatorname{Re}}(z)\to+\infty}{\pazocal{L}}_{t}\mathds{1}(z)=0.

4.
Quasi-conformal Conjugacies of the Family
\{f_{\mathbf{c}}\}

The following continuity result on the Julia set will be useful in the analyticity of the Hausdorff dimension.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
c_{0},c_{n}\in{\pazocal{D}}(\ell,1)
with
c_{n}\to c_{0}
as
n\to\infty
. If
z_{n}\in{\pazocal{J}}(F_{c_{n}})
for all

, and if
\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=z_{0}
for some

, then

Proof.

From Theorem

{\pazocal{F}}(F_{c})
consists only of the Baker (strip) domain
\Pi({\pazocal{U}}_{\ell})
, the attracting domain
\Pi({\pazocal{U}}_{n})
and all of their preimages for each

. Following ideas in
[

]
we conclude that the mapping

,

is continuous in the Hausdorff metric, which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We say that an entire function

Fix

Consider the multi-valued analytic function

satisfying the equation

. Let

denote the algebraic singularities (
[

, Theorem 2]
) of this function and set

Theorem 4.2

(Theorem 10,
[
EL92
]
)
.

Each

Now, the two covering maps

Assuming that

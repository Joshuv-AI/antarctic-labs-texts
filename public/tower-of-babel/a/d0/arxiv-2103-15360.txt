1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and main results

The Sobolev inequality with exponent 2 states that, for any
n\geq 3
and any
u\in\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):=D^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, it holds that

\displaystyle S\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},

(1.1)

where
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
and
S=S(n)
is a dimensional constant.

It is well-known that the Euler-Lagrange equation of (
1.1
), i.e., critical points of Sobolev inequality, up to scaling, is given by

\displaystyle\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.2)

Throughout this paper, we denote
p=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
.
By
Caffarelli et al. 1989
and
Gidas et al. 1979
, it is known that all the positive solutions are
Talenti bubbles

[
23
]
, i.e.

\displaystyle U[z,\lambda](x):=(n(n-2))^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}.

(1.3)

These are all the minimizers of the Sobolev inequality, up to scaling. There are many interests regarding to the stability of (
1.1
). From the perspective of discrepancy in the Sobolev inequality,
Bianchi and Egnell 1991
gave a quantitative estimate near the minimizers, that is

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda>0,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla(u-\alpha U[z,\lambda])\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(n)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-S^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}}\right).

(1.4)

A natural and more challenging perspective is through Euler-Lagrange equation, that is, whether a function
u
that almost solves (
1.2
) must be quantitatively close to Talenti bubbles. There are many obstacles to address this question. First, (
1.2
) has many other sign-changing solutions
[
12
,
14
]
. Second,
u
could be the sum of many weakly interacting Talenti bubbles even if we restrict to the non-negative functions. In fact, a seminal work of Struwe
[
22
]
showed this is always the case, at least for non-negative functions.

Theorem 1.1

(
Struwe 1984
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\nu\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a sequence of non-negative functions such that
\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|^{2}\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n}
with
S=S(n)
as in (
1.1
), and assume that

\left\|\Delta u_{k}+u_{k}^{2^{*}-1}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Then there exist a sequence
(z_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,z_{\nu}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\nu
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a sequence
(\lambda_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of
\nu
-tuples of positive real numbers such that

\left\|\nabla\left(u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U[z_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}]\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

One can also show that the family of
U[z_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}]
has the so-called weak interaction.

Definition 1.2

(Interaction of Talenti bubbles)
.

Let
U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
and
U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]
be two bubbles. Define the interaction of them by

\displaystyle q(z_{i},z_{j},\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})=\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}+\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}.

(1.5)

We shall write
q_{ij}=q(z_{i},z_{j},\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})
if there is no confusion. Let
\left(U_{i}=U\left[z_{i},\lambda_{i}\right]\right)_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
be a family of Talenti bubbles. We say that the family is
\delta
-interacting if

\displaystyle Q:=\max\{q_{ij}:i,j=1,\cdots,\nu\}<\delta.

(1.6)

Despite the difficulty of non-negativity issue, one can still investigate the problem locally. That is, if
u
is already near to a sum of weakly-interacting Talenti bubbles in
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm, then
\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\|_{H^{-1}}
should control the distance from
\mathcal{T}
, the manifold of sums of weakly-interacting Talenti bubbles. Along this direction,
Ciraolo et al. 2018
obtained the first quantitative estimate
dist(u,\mathcal{T})\leq C\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}}
for all
n\geq 3
when
\nu=1
, i.e., when only one bubble is present. Later,
Figalli and Glaudo 2020
established the following theorem for any finite number of bubbles.

Theorem 1.3

(
Figalli and Glaudo 2020
)
.

For any dimension
3\leq n\leq 5
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N},
there exist a small constant
\delta=\delta(n,\nu)>0
and a large constant
C=C(n,\nu)>0
such that the following statement holds. Let
u\in\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a function such that

\left\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla\tilde{U}_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta,

where
(\tilde{U}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
is a
\delta
-interacting family of Talenti bubbles. Then there exist
\nu
Talenti bubbles
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{\nu}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla U_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\right\|_{H^{-1}}.

(1.7)

However, Figalli and Glaudo constructed some counter examples that (
1.7
) does not work in
n\geq 6
if
\nu>1
. They conjectured that one needs to modify the RHS of (
1.7
) to
\Gamma|\log\Gamma|
when
n=6
and to
|\Gamma|^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma<1
when
n\geq 7
, where
\Gamma=\left\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\right\|_{H^{-1}}
. However, the exact value of
\gamma
is not known.

In this paper, we give affirmative answers to both of them.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
n\geq 6
. There exist a small enough
\delta=\delta(n,\nu)>0
and a large constant
C=C(n,\nu)>0
such that the following statement holds. Let
u\in\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a function such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla\tilde{U}_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta,

(1.8)

where
(\tilde{U}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
is a
\delta
-interacting family of Talenti bubbles. Then there exist
\nu
Talenti bubbles
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{\nu}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla U_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\begin{cases}\Gamma|\log\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n=6,\\
\Gamma^{\frac{p}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n\geq 7,\end{cases}

(1.9)

for
\Gamma=\left\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\right\|_{H^{-1}}
.

Note that our theorem completely solves the remaining cases in higher dimensions
n\geq 6
. Moreover, we improve the conjecture of
[
15
]
when
n=6
. After finding out this intriguing power
\frac{p}{2}
, we went back to check the counter examples in
[
15
]
. Their examples show that there exists
u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
n=7
such that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1},\cdots,z_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\nu}>0\end{subarray}}\|\nabla u-\sum_{i}\nabla U_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim\Gamma^{\frac{9}{10}}.

Notice the fact that
\frac{9}{10}=\frac{p}{2}
when
n=7
exactly implies that (
1.9
) is sharp in this case. Indeed, we can prove that our result (
1.9
) is sharp for all
n\geq 6
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For sufficiently large
R>0
, there exists some
\rho
such that if
u=U[-Re_{1},1]+U[Re_{1},1]+\rho
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0\end{subarray}}\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nabla U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim\begin{cases}\Gamma|\log\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n=6,\\
\Gamma^{\frac{p}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n\geq 7.\end{cases}

(1.10)

As a consequence of Theorem
1.4
, we obtain the following sharp quantitative estimates of Struwe’s decomposition.

Corollary 1.6
.

Suppose
n\geq 6
. There exist a small enough
\delta=\delta(n,\nu)>0
and a large constant
C=C(n,\nu)>0
such that the following statement holds. For any non-negative function
u\in\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n},

there exist
\nu
Talenti bubbles
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{\nu}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla U_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\begin{cases}\Gamma|\log\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n=6,\\
\Gamma^{\frac{p}{2}}&\quad\text{if }\ n\geq 7,\end{cases}

(1.11)

for
\Gamma=\left\|\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}\right\|_{H^{-1}}
. Furthermore, for any
i\neq j
, the interaction between the bubbles can be estimated as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}U_{i}^{p}U_{j}\leq C\left\|\Delta u+u^{p}\right\|_{H^{-1}}.

Finally we remark that recently there has been a growing interest in understanding quantitative stability for functional and geometric inequalities, due to important applications to problems in the calculus of variations and PDEs. For extension of (
1.4
) to Sobolev inequality with general exponents we refer to
Figalli and Neumayer 2019
and the references therein. Stability results on Sobolev inequality can be used to obtain quantitative rates of convergence for fast diffusion equations. We refer to
Bonforte and Figalli 2021
,
del Pino and Sáez 2001
and the references therein.
There is also a rich literature on the study of quantitative versions of the isoperimetric inequality and other geometric inequalities analogous to the Sobolev inequality. (A nice description of comparison between Sobolev inequality and isoperimetric inequality can be found in
Figalli and Glaudo 2020
.) We refer to
Brasco et al. 2015
,
Cavalletti et al. 2019
,
Delgadino et al. 2018
,
Figalli and Glaudo 2020
,
Fusco et al. 2008
,
Maggi 2008
and the references therein.

1.2.
Sketch of the proof

We briefly explain the ideas of our proof. Throughout this paper, we shall write that
a\lesssim b
(resp.
a\gtrsim b)
if
a\leq Cb
(resp.
Ca\geq b)
where
C
is a constant depending only on the dimension
n
and on the number of bubbles
\nu
. The constant
C
may change line by line. Also, we say that
a\approx b
if
a\lesssim b
and
a\gtrsim b
. The integral
\int
always means
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
unless specified. We always denote with
o(1)
any quantity that goes to 0 when
\delta
goes to 0. The common notion
o(Q)
means
o(Q)/Q
goes to 0 when
Q
goes to 0.

Suppose
u
satisfies (
1.8
) with a family of
\delta
-interacting bubbles. Consider the following variational problem

\delta(u):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1},\cdots,z_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\nu}>0\end{subarray}}\left\|\nabla u-\nabla\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U\left[\bar{z}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right]\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

It is easy to know that (for instance, see
[
1
, Appendix A]
) if
\delta
is small enough then such an infimum is achieved by some

\sigma:=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U\left[z_{i},\lambda_{i}\right].

(1.12)

Let us denote
U_{i}:=U\left[z_{i},\lambda_{i}\right]
. Since the bubbles
\tilde{U}_{i}
are
\delta
-interacting, the family
\left(U_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
is
\delta^{\prime}
-interacting for some
\delta^{\prime}
that goes to 0 as
\delta
goes to 0.

Let
\rho:=u-\sigma
be the difference between the original function and the best approximation. Then
\rho
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\Delta\rho+[(\sigma+\rho)^{p}-\sigma^{p}]+\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}+f=0,

(1.13)

where
f=-\Delta u-u|u|^{p-1}
.
Moreover,
\rho
also satisfies the following orthogonal conditions

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla Z_{i}^{a}=0~\text{ for any }1\leq i\leq\nu,1\leq a\leq n+1,\end{split}

(1.14)

where
Z_{i}^{a}
are the (rescaled) derivatives of
U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
with respect to
a
-th component of
z_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
(defined in (
3.1
)).

The linearized operator of (
2.3
) at
0
is
\Delta+p\sigma^{p-1}
, which will have kernels when
\sigma
is the sum of a family of weakly-interacting bubbles. The non-homogeneous term
\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i}U_{i}^{p}
is the main data which encodes the interaction of bubbles. Since the linearized operator have kernels,
f
should be interpreted as some Lagrange multiplier.
The key idea of this paper is to obtain a precise behavior of the first approximation of
\rho
.

To illustrate the main idea, we start with the easiest case. Assume
U_{i}=U[z_{i},1]
is a family of
\delta
-interacting bubbles with the same height. Since
\delta
is very small,
z_{i}
are far from each other. Define
R=\min\{\frac{1}{2}|z_{i}-z_{j}|:i\neq j\}
and then
Q\thickapprox R^{2-n}
.

By some standard finite-dimensional reduction method (see for example
[
11
,
25
]
), given a family of
\left(U_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
which is
\delta^{\prime}
-interacting, we can find a function
\rho_{0}
(in an appropriate space) and a family of scalars
\left(c_{a}^{i}\right)
such that we can solve

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\rho_{0}+\left[\left(\sigma+\rho_{0}\right)^{p}-\sigma^{p}\right]=-(\sigma^{p}-\sum_{j}U_{j}^{p})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n+1}c_{a}^{i}U_{i}^{p-1}Z_{i}^{a},\\
\int\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot\nabla Z_{i}^{a}=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,\nu;\ a=1,\cdots,n+1.\end{cases}

(1.15)

In the core of each bubble
U_{j}
, i.e.
\{x:|x-z_{j}|<R\}
, we have
U_{j}\gg U_{k}
for
k\neq j
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{k=1}^{\nu}U_{k}^{p}\right|\lesssim\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{\nu}U_{j}^{p-1}U_{k}\lesssim\frac{R^{2-n}}{1+|x-z_{j}|^{4}}=R^{2-n}\langle x-z_{i}\rangle^{-4}.

(1.16)

Here
\langle y\rangle=\sqrt{1+|y|^{2}}
. In the outer region
\Omega=\{x:|x-z_{j}|>R,\forall\,j=1,\cdots,\nu\}
, one has

\left|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{k=1}^{\nu}U_{k}^{p}\right|(x)\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}U_{j}^{p}(x)\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\frac{1}{1+|x-z_{j}|^{n+2}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}R^{-4}\langle x-z_{j}\rangle^{2-n}.

Thus the solution
\rho_{0}
to (
1.15
) should have the following control

\displaystyle|\rho_{0}(x)|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}R^{2-n}\langle x-z_{j}\rangle^{-2}\chi_{\left\{|x-z_{j}|<R\right\}}+R^{-4}\langle x-z_{j}\rangle^{4-n}\chi_{\Omega}.

(1.17)

Here
\chi_{\Omega}
is the characteristic function for a set
\Omega
. This type of point-wise estimate is the key to our proof. Notice that
\rho_{0}
decay very slow in the core of each bubble. Next, we shall multiply
\rho_{0}
to (
1.15
)

(1.18)

Here the dimension of the space plays an important role in the integration. Estimates (

Now let
\rho=\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}
. Then (

\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\rho_{1}+\left[\left(\sigma+\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}\right)^{p}-\left(\sigma+\rho_{0}\right)^{p}\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n+1}c_{a}^{j}U_{j}^{p-1}Z_{j}^{a}+f=0,\\
\int\nabla\rho_{1}\cdot\nabla Z_{j}^{a}=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,\nu;\ a=1,\cdots,n+1.\end{cases}

(1.19)

Observe the equation of
\rho_{1}
does not contain the interaction term
\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}
. Therefore,
\rho_{1}
should depend on a higher order of

\|\nabla\rho_{1}\|\lesssim Q^{2}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}}
.
Combining with previous estimate of
\nabla\rho_{0}
, we get

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}+\begin{cases}Q^{\frac{p}{2}},&n\geq 7,\\
Q|\log Q|^{\frac{1}{2}},&n=6.\end{cases}

On the other hand, we shall multiply (
1.13
) by some appropriate
Z_{k}^{n+1}
and integrate it to arrive (cf. Lemma
2.1
)

Q\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}+\left|\int\sigma^{p-1}\rho U_{k}\right|+\int|\rho|^{p}U_{k}.

To establish the above estimates, unlike
[
15
]
, we did not use cut-off functions. Using the point-wise estimate (

) of
\rho_{0}
, we can show that the last two terms are higher order in
Q
and then
Q\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}
. Consequently,
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-1}}^{\frac{p}{2}}
if
n\geq 7
and
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{-1}}|\log\|f\|_{H^{-1}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
if
n=6
. Thus one can establish Theorem
1.4
in this setting.

For a general family of bubbles
\sigma=\sum_{i}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
, things are much more complicate. Since we can not a priori assume that
\lambda_{i}\sim\lambda_{j}
or
|z_{i}-z_{j}|<<\min(\lambda_{i}^{-1},\lambda_{j}^{-1})
, we may have bubble towers, bubble clusters, and these two may be mixed. (We remark that there are many papers in the literature concerning the construction of bubbling clusters or bubbling towers solutions. For bubbling towers we refer to
Del Pino et al. 2003
,
Musso and Pistoia 2010
,
Pistoia and Vétois 2013
and the references therein. For bubbling clusters we refer to
Wei and Yan 2007
,
Wei and Yan 2010
and the references therein.) For instance, if
\sigma
contains bubble towers (that is
\max\{\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{i}\}\gg\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}
), one neither knows the existence of
\rho_{0}
satisfying (
1.15
), nor has a simple control of the interaction as (
1.16
).
These two difficulties are deeply related. The strategy is to design a “good” space for the interaction term
|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i}U_{i}^{p}|
so that (
1.15
) has a solution
\rho_{0}
with the desired control. Choosing the right norm is a very delicate process. We start with just two bubbles and examine the magnitude of the interaction term
(U_{i}+U_{j})^{p}-U_{i}^{p}-U_{j}^{p}
on different regions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fortunately, we obtain a uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{**}
(cf. (
3.15
)) to handle the bubble tower and bubble cluster at the same time, which reduces the amount of work significantly. Then
\|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{k}U_{k}^{p}\|_{**}\leq C(n,\nu)
follows from the bounds of all pairs by a simple inequality.

The existence of
\rho_{0}
satisfying
(
1.15
)
is based on some a prior estimates (cf. Lemma
3.5
). To establish such estimates, we used blow-up argument and divide
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into three types of region: inner, neck, and exterior ones. We manage to prove the leading parts of
W(x)
is a supersolution on the neck and exterior regions. This is the most crucial and technical part of the proof. After establishing the a prior estimate, we get the existence of
\rho_{0}
from standard contraction mapping theorem. Consequently
\|\rho_{0}\|_{*}\leq C(n,\nu)
, which plays the corresponding role as (
1.17
). Then the rest of proof is almost the same.

We also construct an example which shows the exponents in (
1.9
) are sharp. Suppose
\sigma=\tilde{U}_{1}+\tilde{U}_{2}
where
\tilde{U}_{1}:=U[-Re_{1},1]
and
\tilde{U}_{2}=U[Re_{1},1]
. By reduction theorem (see
[
25
]
), one can prove the existence of
\tilde{\rho}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\tilde{\rho}+[(\sigma+\tilde{\rho})^{p}-\sigma^{p}]+\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1,2}\tilde{U}_{i}^{p}+\sum_{i,a}\tilde{c}_{a}^{i}\tilde{U}_{i}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{i}^{a}=0,\\
\int\tilde{U}_{i}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{i}^{a}\tilde{\rho}=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,\nu;\ a=1,\cdots,n+1.\end{cases}

(1.20)

Choose
u=\sigma+\tilde{\rho}
. Then
\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=-\sum_{i,a}\tilde{c}_{a}^{i}\tilde{U}_{i}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{i}^{a}
. We manage to find a good approximation of
\tilde{\rho}
in the interior and exterior region which shows that
\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}
in the interior part is already no less than
Q^{\frac{p}{2}}
when
n=7
and
Q|\log Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}
when
n=6
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we prove the main result assuming several crucial estimates on
\rho
and
\nabla\rho
. Section 3 contains two subsections. The first one proves the existence of the first expansion of
\rho
and its point-wise estimates. We use it to establish the estimates used in the proof of main result in the second subsection. In Section 4, we devote to constructing some example to show that Theorem
1.4
is sharp. The appendix consists of various integral estimates between bubbles and their derivatives.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we will prove the main Theorem
1.4
based on some crucial estimates, whose proofs are deferred to the next section.

Suppose
u=\sigma+\rho
where
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}
is the best approximation (see (
1.12
)). Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta u+u|u|^{p-1}=\Delta(\sigma+\rho)+(\sigma+\rho)|\sigma+\rho|^{p-1}=\Delta\rho+p\sigma^{p-1}\rho+I_{1}+I_{2},\end{split}

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p},\quad I_{2}=(\sigma+\rho)|\sigma+\rho|^{p-1}-\sigma^{p}-p\sigma^{p-1}\rho.

(2.2)

Let
f=-\Delta u-u|u|^{p-1}
. Then (
2.1
) can be reorganized to

\displaystyle\Delta\rho+p\sigma^{p-1}\rho+I_{1}+I_{2}+f=0.

(2.3)

If
n\geq 6
, then
p\in(1,2]
. We have the following elementary inequality

\left|(\sigma+\rho)|\sigma+\left.\rho\right|^{p-1}-\sigma^{p}-\left.p\sigma^{p-1}\rho\right|\lesssim|\rho\right|^{p}.

Thus

|I_{2}|\leq|\rho|^{p}.

Multiplying
Z^{n+1}_{k}
(defined in (
3.1
)) to (
2.3
) and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
give

\displaystyle\left|\int I_{1}Z^{n+1}_{k}\right|\leq\int|fZ^{n+1}_{k}|+\left|\int p\sigma^{p-1}\rho Z^{n+1}_{k}\right|+\int|\rho|^{p}|Z^{n+1}_{k}|.

Here we have used the orthogonal conditions (
1.14
). Using
|Z_{k}^{n+1}|\lesssim U_{k}
and
||U_{k}||_{H^{-1}}\leq C(n)
, we can apply Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int I_{1}Z^{n+1}_{k}\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}+\left|\int\sigma^{p-1}\rho Z_{k}^{n+1}\right|+\int|\rho|^{p}|Z_{k}^{n+1}|.\end{split}

(2.4)

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
u
satisfies (
1.8
) with
\delta
small enough. Then

\displaystyle\int I_{1}Z_{k}^{n+1}=\int I_{1}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}\int U_{i}^{p}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}+o(Q).

(2.5)

Proof.

The first identity follows from the definition
Z_{k}^{n+1}=\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}
. Denote
\sigma=\sigma_{k}+U_{k}
where
\sigma_{k}=\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}U_{i}
. We make the following decomposition

\displaystyle\int I_{1}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=

\displaystyle\int(\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

where

\displaystyle J_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\nu U_{k}\geq\sigma_{k}\}}(pU_{k}^{p-1}\sigma_{k}-\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k},

\displaystyle J_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\nu U_{k}\geq\sigma_{k}\}}(\sigma^{p}-U_{k}^{p}-pU_{k}^{p-1}\sigma_{k})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k},

\displaystyle J_{3}=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\sigma_{k}>\nu U_{k}\}}(p\sigma_{k}^{p-1}U_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k},

\displaystyle J_{4}=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\sigma_{k}>\nu U_{k}\}}(\sigma^{p}-\sigma_{k}^{p}-p\sigma_{k}^{p-1}U_{k})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}.

Notice
|\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}|\lesssim U_{k}
.
Based on the inequality

\displaystyle|(a+b)^{p}-a^{p}-pa^{p-1}b|\lesssim a^{p-2}b^{2}\quad\text{if }a\geq b>0,

(2.6)

we have

\displaystyle|J_{2}|\lesssim\int_{\{\nu U_{k}>\sigma_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p-1}\sigma_{k}^{2}\lesssim\int U_{k}^{p-\epsilon}\sigma_{k}^{1+\epsilon}\approx Q^{1+\epsilon}.

(2.7)

Here
\epsilon>0
is very small, such that
1+\epsilon<p-\epsilon
, and in the last step we have used Lemma
A.3
. Similarly
|J_{4}|\lesssim Q^{1+\epsilon}
. For
J_{3}
,

\displaystyle|J_{3}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{\sigma_{k}\geq\nu U_{k}\}}(p\sigma_{k}^{p-1}U_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p})\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\{\sigma_{k}\geq\nu U_{k}\}}p\sigma_{k}^{p-\epsilon}U_{k}^{1+\epsilon}+\int\left|\sigma_{k}^{p}-\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}\right|U_{k}+\int_{\{\sigma_{k}\geq\nu U_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p+1}.

Using an elementary inequality

\left|\sigma_{k}^{p}-\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}\right|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq\nu\\
i\neq k,j\neq k\end{subarray}}U_{i}^{p-1}U_{j}

and the triple integral estimate in Lemma
A.4
, we have

|J_{3}|\lesssim Q^{1+\epsilon}+\int_{\{\sigma_{k}\geq\nu U_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p+1}.

Consider the second term on the RHS. Lemma
A.1
implies

\int_{\{U_{i}\geq U_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p+1}\leq\int U_{k}^{p-\epsilon}\inf(U_{i}^{1+\epsilon},U_{k}^{1+\epsilon})=O(q_{ik}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}|\log q_{ik}|)=o(Q),

therefore

\int_{\{\sigma_{k}\geq\nu U_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p+1}\leq\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}\int_{\{U_{i}>U_{k}\}}U_{k}^{p+1}=o(Q).

Therefore
|J_{3}|=o(Q)
.
Now consider
J_{1}
,

\displaystyle J_{1}-\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}p\int U_{k}^{p-1}U_{i}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\nu U_{k}<\sigma_{k}\}}pU_{k}^{p-1}\sigma_{k}\lambda_{k}\partial_{k}U_{k}-\int_{\{\nu U_{k}>\sigma_{k}\}}\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=o(Q).

Here we applied the same trick in (
2.7
) to obtain
o(Q)
.
With the above estimates of
J_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
, we can get

\displaystyle\int I_{1}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{\nu}p\int U_{k}^{p-1}U_{i}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}+o(Q).

(2.8)

Simple integration by parts shows that

p\int U_{k}^{p-1}U_{i}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}=\int U_{i}^{p}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}.

Thus (
2.5
) holds.
∎

Now let us go back to (
2.4
). In the next section, we will prove two important estimates

\displaystyle\left|\int\sigma^{p-1}\rho Z_{k}^{n+1}\right|=o(Q)+\|f\|_{H^{-1}},\quad\int|\rho|^{p}|Z_{k}^{n+1}|=o(Q)+\|f\|_{H^{-1}},

(2.9)

in Lemma
3.13
.

Remark 2.2
.

These two terms have rough bounds easily by Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality. Indeed, for instance, when
n\geq 7
, as did in
[
15
, (3.31) and footnote 1]
,

\left|\int\sigma^{p-1}\rho Z_{k}^{n+1}\right|\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}Q^{p-1}

\int|\rho|^{p}|Z_{k}^{n+1}|\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{p}.

By Lemma
2.1
and the above two estimates, one can achieve

\displaystyle Q\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}Q^{p-1}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.

(2.10)

Multiplying (
2.3
) by
\rho
, the approach in
[
15
]
would induce

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim Q^{p-1}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}}
. Plugging in this fact to (
2.10
), we obtain
Q\lesssim Q^{2(p-1)}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}}
, one fails to conclude anything when
2(p-1)\leq 1
(equivalent to
n\geq 10
).
This obstacle actually motivates us to have a better control of
\rho
instead of simply using Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality.

Using (
2.9
), we can prove the following important lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
u
satisfies (
1.8
) with
\delta
small enough, then

\displaystyle Q\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.

(2.11)

Proof.

Lemma
A.3
implies
\int U_{i}^{p}U_{j}\approx q_{ij}
for
i\neq j
. Notice that if
q_{ij}\ll Q
, then

\left|\int U_{i}^{p}\lambda_{j}\partial_{\lambda_{j}}U_{j}\right|\lesssim\int U_{i}^{p}U_{j}\lesssim q_{ij}\ll Q.

We shall collect all the
q_{ij}
such that
q_{ij}\approx Q
and choose
k
such that
\lambda_{k}
is the largest one among them. Notice Lemma
A.2
implies
\int U_{i}^{p}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}\approx-q_{ik}\approx-Q
. Since they have the same sign, Lemma
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\left|\int I_{1}\lambda_{k}\partial_{\lambda_{k}}U_{k}\right|\approx Q.

(2.12)

Plugging in this fact and (
2.9
) to (
2.4
) to obtain

Q\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.

∎

Now we can prove our main result Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

In the next section, we shall prove
\rho=\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}
. By Proposition
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}Q|\log Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }n=6,\\
Q^{\frac{p}{2}}&\text{if }n\geq 7.\end{cases}

(2.13)

By Proposition

, we have

\|\nabla\rho_{1}\|\lesssim Q^{2}+\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.

Since we have shown

in the previous lemma, then

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}\|f\|_{H^{-1}}\left|\log\|f\|_{H^{-1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},&n=6,\\
\|f\|_{H^{-1}}^{\frac{p}{2}},&n\geq 7.\end{cases}

Here we have used the fact that
x|\log x|^{\frac{1}{2}}
is increasing near 0. Therefore (
1.9
) holds.
∎

Proof of Corollary

.

The proof is identical to that of Corollary 3.4 in
[

]
.

∎

3.
Expansion of the error

In this section we will prove the estimates required in Section 2, the most important two parts are a point-wise estimate of
\rho_{0}
and a global
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla\rho_{0}
. We divide the proof of them into two subsections.

3.1.
Existence of the first approximation.

Let us consider the equation (
2.3
) of
\rho
. The linearized operator is
\Delta+p\sigma^{p-1}
,
I_{1}+I_{2}+f
is the non-homogeneous term and
\rho
is the solution.
I_{1}
is the main data which encodes the interaction of bubbles.
I_{2}
is a higher order term in
\rho
and negligible. Since the linearized operator have kernels,
f
should be interpreted as some Lagrange multiplier. Therefore, an approximation of
\rho
can be obtained from studying the linear equation
\Delta\rho_{0}+p\sigma^{p-1}\rho_{0}=I_{1}+I_{2}
.

For
i=1,\cdots,\nu
, define

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{a}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\left.\frac{\partial U[z,\lambda_{i}]}{\partial z^{a}}\right|_{z=z_{i}},\quad Z_{i}^{n+1}=\lambda_{i}\left.\frac{\partial U[z_{i},\lambda]}{\partial\lambda}\right|_{\lambda=\lambda_{i}},

(3.1)

here
z^{a}
is
a
-th component of
z
for
a=1,\cdots,n
. Notice
\{Z_{i}^{a}:i=1,\cdots,\nu\}
is the kernel of
\Delta+pU_{i}^{p-1}
. It is easy to verify
|Z_{i}^{a}|\lesssim U_{i}
for any
i
and
1\leq a\leq n+1
.

Consider the following linear equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\phi+p\sigma^{p-1}\phi=h+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n+1}c_{a}^{i}U_{i}^{p-1}Z_{i}^{a},\\
\int U_{i}^{p-1}\phi Z_{i}^{a}=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,\nu;\ a=1,\cdots,n+1,\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
is a family of
\delta
-interacting bubbles. We always assume
\delta
is very small. We shall use finite-dimensional reduction to prove the solvability of
\phi
given a reasonable
h
in Lemma
3.5
. To that end, we need to set up the norms and spaces.

Let

I=\{1,2,\cdots,\nu\}
, throughout this paper we denote
y_{i}=\lambda_{i}(x-z_{i})
,
i\in I
, and

\displaystyle R_{ij}=\max\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}},\sqrt{\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{i}},\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}|z_{i}-z_{j}|\right\}=\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1}\quad\text{if}\quad i\neq j\in I.

(3.3)

Definition 3.1
.

For any two bubbles
U_{i},U_{j}
, if
R_{ij}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}|z_{i}-z_{j}|
, then we call them a
bubble cluster
, otherwise call them a
bubble tower
.

Let us define

\displaystyle R:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{i\neq j\in I}R_{ij}.

(3.4)

Now we can define
\|\cdot\|_{**}
and
\|\cdot\|_{*}
norms as

\displaystyle\|h\|_{**}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h(x)|V^{-1}(x),\quad\|\phi\|_{*}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi(x)|W^{-1}(x)

(3.5)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}V(x)=&\ \sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}R^{2-n}}{\langle y_{i}\rangle^{4}}\chi_{\{|y_{i}|\leq R\}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}R^{-4}}{\langle y_{i}\rangle^{n-2}}\chi_{\{|y_{i}|\geq R/2\}}\right),\end{split}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}W(x)=&\ \sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}R^{2-n}}{\langle y_{i}\rangle^{2}}\chi_{\{|y_{i}|\leq R\}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}R^{-4}}{\langle y_{i}\rangle^{n-4}}\chi_{\{|y_{i}|\geq R/2\}}\right).\end{split}

(3.7)

Remark 3.2
.

The ad hoc weight
V
is used to capture the behavior of the error term
h=\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}
. Since
\Delta_{x}\phi(x)\sim h(x)
, it is natural to define
W\sim\lambda^{-2}\langle y\rangle^{2}V
.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a small constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\nu)
and large constant
C(n,\nu)
such that if
\delta<\delta_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\|\sigma^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}^{p}\|_{**}\leq C(n,\nu).

(3.8)

Proof.

To make the proof more transparent, we will start with two bubbles.
Consider
U_{1}=U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}]
,
U_{2}=U[z_{2},\lambda_{2}]
, and
h=(U_{1}+U_{2})^{p}-U_{1}^{p}-U_{2}^{p}
.
Because of weak interaction,
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
must be a bubble tower or bubble cluster. Notice that
h
is always positive.

(3.23)

Proof.

We use blow-up arguments to prove (

\left\{U^{(k)}_{i}:=U[z^{(k)}_{i},\lambda^{(k)}_{i}]\right\}_{1\leq i\leq\nu}

For simplicity, here and after we denote

Lemma A.6
.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}\right)^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}^{p}\leq\sum_{i\neq j}[(a_{i}+a_{j})^{p}-a_{i}^{p}-a_{j}^{p}]

Local solvability for a quasilinear wave equation with the far field degeneracy: 1D case
Yuusuke Sugiyama

Abstract
We study the Cauchy problem for the quasilinear wave equation ∂ t 2 u = u 2 ​ a ​ ∂ x 2 u + F ⁡ ( u ) ​ u x \partial^{2}_{t}u=u^{2a}\partial^{2}_{x}u+F(u)u_{x} with a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 and show a result for the local in time existence under new conditions. In the previous results, it is assumed that u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) ≥ c 0 > 0 u(0,x)\geq c_{0}>0 for some constant c 0 c_{0} to prove the existence and the uniqueness. This assumption ensures that the equation does not degenerate. In this paper, we allow the equation to degenerate at spacial infinity. Namely we consider the local well-posedness under the assumption that u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) > 0 u(0,x)>0 and u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) → 0 u(0,x)\rightarrow 0 as | x | → ∞ |x|\rightarrow\infty . Furthermore, to prove the local well-posedness, we find that the so-called Levi condition appears. Our proof is based on the method of characteristic and the contraction mapping principle via weighted L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Cauchy problem of the model quasilinear wave equation in
\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2a}\partial_{x}u)+F(u)u_{x},\ \ (t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
F
is a given function and
a\geq 0
.
The purpose of this paper is to show the local existence and the uniqueness under new conditions.
The existence of solutions to the more general quasi-linear wave equations has been widely known since the 1970s.
Kato
[
12
]
and Hughes, Kato and Marsden
[
9
]
have shown an abstract theorem about the well-posedness
of the system of general quasi-linear wave equations in
L^{2}
Sobolev space.
In
1
dimensional case, the well-posedness in
C^{1}_{b}
class for first order hyperbolic equations has been studied by Douglis
[
5
]
and Hartman and Winter
[
7
]
(see also Majda
[
14
]
and Courant and Lax
[
3
]
),
where
C^{1}_{b}
is a set of continuous and bounded functions whose derivatives are also bounded.
In order to apply these results to the existence problem of (
1
), the following assumption is required:

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\geq c_{0}>0

(1.5)

for a constant
c_{0}
. This condition ensures that the equation in (
1
) is the strictly hyperbolic type near
t=0
. This paper relaxes this condition.
We show the the local existence and the uniqueness of solutions of (
1
) under the assumption that the equation degenerates at spacial infinity.
Namely we weaken (
1.5
) by
u(0,x)>0
and allow that
u(0,x)
can decay to
0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
(more precise assumptions are given later).
To the best of my knowledge, the well-possessedness has never been studied under these types of assumptions.

1.1
Known results

Let us review some results on the solvability for degenerate wave equations (weakly hyperbolic equations).
The existence, nonexistence and regularity of solutions to the following type of linear weakly hyperbolic equations have been studied by many authors (e.g. Oleinik
[
18
]
, Colombini and Spagnolo
[
2
]
, Ivrii and Petkov
[
11
]
and Taniguchi and Tozaki
[
22
]
),

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}u-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}(t,x)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}(t,x)u_{x_{j}}=0,

(1.6)

where
a_{i,j}
and
b_{j}
are smooth functions and
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
is assumed for
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We note that
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=0
corresponds to the degeneracy.
In Oleinik
[
18
]
, (
1.6
) have been solved under the so-called Levi condition:

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\xi_{j}\right)^{2}\leq C_{2}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}+\partial_{t}a_{i,j}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\right).

(1.7)

Even if the Levi condition is assumed, we can only obtain the following energy estimate with the regularity loss for weakly hyperbolic equations:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{t}\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+r_{1}}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s-1+r_{2}}}),

(1.8)

where
s
is an arbitrary real number and
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are non-negative numbers. It is known that this estimate is optimal in the sense of the regularity by observing some
explicit solution to some special linear weakly hyperbolic equations.
Ivrii and Petkov in
[
11
]
have treated the the following model of the
1D
weakly hyperbolic equation:

\displaystyle u_{tt}-t^{2l}u_{xx}+t^{k}u_{x}=0.

They have shown that the Levi condition (
k\geq l-1
) is necessary for the Cauchy problem of this equations to be
C^{\infty}
well-posed.
Colombini and Spagnolo in
[
2
]
have given an example of a
C^{\infty}
function
a(t)\geq 0
such that

u_{tt}-a(t)u_{xx}=0

is not well-posed in
C^{\infty}
. Roughly speaking, highly oscillatory behaviors of
a(t)
near the point that
a(t)=0
causes the ill-posedness. In
[
10
]
, Han has derived an energy inequality with a regularity loss for the linear weakly hyperbolic equation:

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}u-a(t,x)u_{xx}=0,

where
a(t,x)=t^{m}+a_{1}(x)t^{m-1}+a_{2}(x)t^{m-2}+\cdots+a_{m-1}(x)t+a_{m}(x)
.

Manfrin in
[
17
]
have established the local existence and the uniqueness for following
1D
degenerate quasilinear wave equations
with
u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\displaystyle u_{tt}=a(u)\Delta u,

where
a(u)
is a analytic function satisfying
a(0)=0
. This result can be extended to more general degenerate wave equations (see also Manfrin
[
15
,
16
]
).
In Dreher’s paper
[
6
]
, he also has shown the local solvability for
\partial^{2}_{t}u=\partial_{x}(|\partial_{x}u|^{p-2}\partial_{x})
with
p>5
and under the initial condition that

u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{k}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for a large natural number
k
.
Since their proof is based on the Nash-Moser implicit function theorem and the argument in the Oleinik’s paper
[
18
]
, the compactness of the support of initial data is essentially
used. Hence it does not seem difficult to extend Manfrin’s method to the case that initial data are not compactly supported.

In
[
8
]
, Hu and Wang have shown the local existence and uniqueness of solutions to the following variational wave equation:

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}u=c(u,x)\partial_{x}(c(u,x)\partial_{x}u)

(1.9)

with initial data and the function
c(u,x)
satisfying

\displaystyle c(u(0,x),x)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(0,x)\geq c_{0}>0,

\displaystyle c_{u}(u,x)\geq c_{1}>0

for some constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
. The choice of initial data implies that the equation degenerates at
t=0
and that
c(u,x)
becomes positive uniformly and immediately after
t=0
.
The method in
[
8
]
is inspired by Zhang and Zheng’s paper
[
24
]
which studies the existence of solutions to Euler type equation in gas dynamics. In
[
24
]
and
[
8
]
, they use method of characteristic for a new dependent variable
and the fixed point theorem in a special metric space.

1.2
Assumptions and main theorem

Before stating main theorem of this paper, we introduce assumptions on initial data and the function
F
.
We set
\gamma=\gamma(a,\alpha)

\displaystyle\gamma=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&a\geq 1,\\

For initial data

\displaystyle c_{1}\braket{x}^{-\alpha}\leq u_{0}(x)\leq c_{2},

(1.11)

\displaystyle|u_{1}(x)\pm u_{0}^{a}u^{\prime}_{0}(x)|\leq c_{3}\braket{x}^{-\beta},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dx}(u_{1}(x)\pm u_{0}^{a}u^{\prime}_{0}(x))\right|\leq c_{4}\braket{x}^{-\gamma}

(1.13)

with conditions on
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
that

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\beta,

(1.14)

\displaystyle a\alpha\leq\beta,

(1.15)

where

and

is defined by

For the function

\displaystyle|F(\theta)|\leq C_{K}\theta^{a},

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C_{K}\theta^{a-1}

[

Main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
u_{1}\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. Suppose that the conditions (
1.11
)-(
1.17
) hold.
Then there exists a number

depending on the constants in (
1.11
)-(
1.17
) such that the Cauchy problem (
1
) has a unique local solution
u\in C^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
satisfying that for all

\displaystyle C_{1}\braket{x}^{-\alpha}\leq u(t,x)\leq C_{2},

(1.18)

\displaystyle|(u_{t}\pm u^{a}u_{x})(t,x)|\leq C_{3}\braket{x}^{-\beta},

(1.19)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\left((u_{t}\pm u^{a}u_{x})(t,x)\right)|+|\partial_{t}\left((u_{t}\pm u^{a}u_{x})(t,x)\right)|\leq C_{4}\braket{x}^{-\gamma},

(1.20)

where

Theorem
1.1
asserts the local existence and uniqueness of solutions of (

) under
the Levi type condition without the regularity loss.
Our proof is based on the method of characteristic and the contraction mapping principle via weighted
L^{\infty}
estimates.
In contrast to previous results on the existence for strictly hyperbolic equations,

is not bounded .
To avoid this crux, we use the spatial decay of

Remark 1.2

Suppose that initial data is

If

Preliminaries

2.1
Basic formulation for unknown variables
R
and
S

We set
R(t,x)
and
S(t,x)
as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R=\partial_{t}u+u^{a}\partial_{x}u,\\

By (
1

R
and

are solutions to the system of the following first order equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}R-u^{a}\partial_{x}R=N_{1}(u,R,S)+L(u,R,S),\\
\partial_{x}u=\dfrac{1}{2u^{a}}(R-S),\\
\partial_{t}S+u^{a}\partial_{x}S=N_{2}(u,R,S)+L(u,R,S),\end{array}\right.

where we set

L(u,R,S)=\dfrac{F(u)(R-S)}{2u^{a}},

N_{1}(u,R,S)=\dfrac{a}{2u}(R^{2}-RS)

and

N_{2}(u,R,S)=\dfrac{a}{2u}(S^{2}-RS).

Let
x_{\pm}(t)
be characteristic curves on the first and third equations of (
2.1
)
respectively. That is,
x_{+}(t)
and
x_{-}(t)
are solutions to the following differential equations respectively:

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}x_{\pm}(t)=\pm u^{a}(t,x_{\pm}(t)).

(2.8)

When we emphasize the characteristic curves go through
(s,y)
, we denote
x_{\pm}(t)
by
x_{\pm}(t;s,y)
.
That is,
x_{\pm}(t;s,y)
satisfies that

\displaystyle x_{\pm}(t;s,y)=y\pm\int_{s}^{t}u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;s,y))d\tau.

(2.9)

On the characteristic curves,
R
and
S
satisfy that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{d}{dt}R(t,x_{-}(t))=N_{1}(u,R,S)(t,x_{-}(t))+L(u,R,S)(t,x_{-}(t)),\\
\dfrac{d}{dt}S(t,x_{+}(t))=N_{2}(u,R,S)(t,x_{+}(t))+L(u,R,S)(t,x_{+}(t)).\end{array}\right.

We prepare some estimates for characteristic curves for
u\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle 0\leq u^{a}|u_{x}(t,x)|\leq A_{2}\braket{x}^{-\alpha}

The boundedness of

\displaystyle x-A_{1}^{a}|t-s|\leq x_{\pm}(s;t,x)\leq x+A_{1}^{a}|t-s|.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

) and (

First we show the case that
t_{3}=t_{4}=t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle|x_{\pm}(t;t_{1},x_{1})-x_{\pm}(t;t_{2},x_{2})|\leq

\displaystyle|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t}u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t_{1},x_{1}))d\tau-\int_{t_{2}}^{t}u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t_{1},x_{2}))d\tau\right|

\displaystyle|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\left|\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{2}}u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t_{2},x_{2}))d\tau\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left|u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t_{1},x_{1}))-u^{a}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t_{2},x_{2}))\right|d\tau.

\displaystyle(1+A_{1}^{a})(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|t_{1}-t_{2}|)

\displaystyle|x_{\pm}(t;t_{1},x_{1})-x_{\pm}(t;t_{2},x_{2})|\leq 2(1+A_{1}^{a})(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|t_{1}-t_{2}|).

\displaystyle a\int_{0}^{1}(\theta v_{1}+(1-\theta)v_{2})^{a-1}|v_{1}-v_{2}||R_{2x}|d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\braket{x}^{\gamma}|v_{1}-v_{2}||R_{2x}|

Therefore, we obtain that for sufficiently small

In the same way as in the estimate of

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{6}\left(\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\bar{R}_{1}-\bar{R}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}+\|\bar{S}_{1}-\bar{S}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}\right).

4
Concluding remarks

4.1
Physical background

We set a function
G
as a primitive function of
F
such that
G(0)=0
.
Integrating with
x
over
[-\infty,x]
, we formally obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x}u_{tt}dx=\partial_{x}\left(\frac{u^{a+1}}{a+1}\right)+G(u).

Setting
v=\int_{-\infty}^{x}u_{t}dx
and
\sigma(u)=u^{a+1}/a+1
, we have the following 1st order hyperbolic equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-v_{x}=0,\\
v_{t}-\partial_{x}(\sigma(u))=G(u).\end{array}\right.

This equations govern the motion for one dimensional elastic waves with the case that the density of material is equal to
1
.
Unknown functions
u
and
v
describe the differentiations of the displacement
X
with
x
and
t
respectively. Namely
u=X_{x}(t,x)
and
v=X_{t}(t,x)
.
The first equation means the relation
u_{t}=X_{xt}=X_{tx}=v_{x}
. The second equation is Newton’s second since
v_{t}
is the acceleration.
From the definition of
u
,
u
is the strain (more precisely,
(1,1)
component of the stain matrix) and
\sigma(u)
is so-called stress-strain relation.

G
is a external force term depending only on the strain.
The detailed derivation with
G\equiv 0
is given in Cristescu’s book
[
4
]
.

4.2
On the generalization of the main theorem

The our existence theorem is also applicable to
\partial^{2}_{t}u=(c(u)^{2}u_{x})_{x}+F(u)u_{x}
under the following assumptions on
c(\cdot)\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
and
F\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty))

\displaystyle C_{1.K}\theta^{a}\leq c(\theta)\leq C_{2,K},

(4.4)

\displaystyle|c^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C_{3,K}\theta^{a-1},

(4.5)

\displaystyle|c^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\leq C_{4,K}\theta^{a-2},

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle|F(\theta)|\leq C_{5,K}\theta^{a},

(4.7)

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C_{6,K}\theta^{a-1},

(4.8)

where
a\geq 0
,
\theta\in[0,K]
for
K>0
and
C_{j,K}
are positive constants depending on
K
for
j=1,\ldots,6
. For this equation, the unknown valuable
R
and
S
are defined by

\displaystyle R=u_{t}+c(u)u_{x},

\displaystyle S=u_{t}-c(u)u_{x}

and
R
and
S
satisfy that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}R-u^{a}\partial_{x}R=\frac{c^{\prime}}{2c}(RS-S^{2})+F(u)\frac{R-S}{2c},\\
\partial_{t}S+u^{a}\partial_{x}S=\frac{c^{\prime}}{2c}(RS-R^{2})+F(u)\frac{R-S}{2c}.\end{array}\right.

Since we have that
\frac{|c^{\prime}(u)|}{c(u)}\leq C\braket{x}^{\alpha}
from the assumption on

and initial data, we can obtain weighted
L^{\infty}
estimated for
R
and
S
.
The assumption (
4.6
) is used in the proof of the construction of the contraction mapping.

4.3
Finite time blow-up or degeneracy

We define
T^{*}
as the maximal existence time of the solution constructed by Theorem
1.1
.
When
T^{*}<\infty
, we have the following criterion of the break-down:

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\braket{x}^{\beta}u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\braket{x}^{\beta}u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\infty

(4.12)

or

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\inf_{\mathbb{R}}\braket{x}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=0.

(4.13)

We call (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) the blow-up and the degeneracy respectively.
In the case that
F\equiv 0
, we can obtain the non-trivial solutions blow up in finite time, if
R(0,x)
and
S(0,x)
are non-negative.
In fact, we can show that the non-negativity of
R(0,x)
and
S(0,x)
preserves as time goes by, from which we have

. Thus we find that (
4.13
) does not occurs in finite time. Therefore, using the method of Lax
[
13
]
or
[
23
]
(see also

[
1
]
), we have the conclusion. While, in the case that
F\equiv 0
, we can apply main theorems to the equation in (
1
) and
find that (
4.13
) can occur in finite time for non-trivial solution, if
R(0,x)
and
S(0,x)
are non-positive.
Sufficient conditions for the occurrence of (
4.13
) have been studied in the author’s papers
[
20
,
21
]
.

4.4
Multi-dimensional case

The multi-dimensional version of the equation in (
1
) is

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}u=u^{2a}\Delta u+F(u)\cdot\nabla u=0.

The method of characteristic (and Riemann invariant) does not work, even with radial initial data.
In the forthcoming paper, we deal this problem via a local-energy argument.

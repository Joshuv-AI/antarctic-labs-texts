Lower Bounds for Maximum Weight Bisections of Graphs with Bounded Degrees
Stefanie Gerke Gregory Gutin Anders Yeo Yacong Zhou

Abstract
A bisection in a graph is a cut in which the number of vertices in the two parts differ by at most 1. In this paper, we give lower bounds for the maximum weight of bisections of edge-weighted graphs with bounded maximum degree. Our results improve a bound of Lee, Loh, and Sudakov (J. Comb. Th. Ser. B 103 (2013)) for (unweighted) maximum bisections in graphs whose maximum degree is either even or equals 3, and for almost all graphs. We show that a tight lower bound for maximum size of bisections in 3-regular graphs obtained by Bollobás and Scott (J. Graph Th. 46 (2004)) can be extended to weighted subcubic graphs. We also consider edge-weighted triangle-free subcubic graphs and show that a much better lower bound (than for edge-weighted subcubic graphs) holds for such graphs especially if we exclude K 1 , 3 K_{1,3} . We pose three conjectures.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider
edge-weighted
(or,
weighted
) graphs
G=(V,E,w)
, where the weight
w(e)
of each edge
e\in E
is a non-negative real number. Given a graph
G=(V,E)
a
cut
of
G
is the edge set between a non-empty set
A\not=V
and
V\setminus A
. The sets
A
and
V\setminus A
are
partition classes
(or,
parts
).
A
bisection
is a cut in which the sizes of its parts differ by at most 1.

Several researchers
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
have shown that if the chromatic number of
G
is
\chi
, then it admits a bipartition of size at least
\frac{\chi+1}{2\chi}|E(G)|
when
\chi
is odd and
\frac{\chi}{2(\chi-1)}|E(G)|
when
\chi
is even. Thus, it is implied by Brook’s Theorem that if the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
of a graph
G
is at most
k
, then for odd
k
, every graph with maximum degree at most
k
has a cut with at least
\frac{k+1}{2k}|E(G)|
edges, and for even
k
, every graph with maximum degree at most
k
has a cut with
\frac{k+2}{2(k+1)}|E(G)|
edges.

These bounds are tight as the maximum cuts of complete graphs have this size. Note that the bound in the odd case implies the bound in the even case as every graph with maximum degree at most
k
also has maximum degree at most
k+1
.

This result can be easily generalized to edge-weighted graphs
G
by replacing the number of edges
|E(G)|
with the total weight
w(G)
of the edges. We conjecture that the same bound holds not only for a maximum weight cut but also for a maximum weight bisection.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
G
be a graph with maximum degree
\Delta(G)\leq k
. If
k
is odd then
G
has a bisection of weight at least
\frac{k+1}{2k}w(G)
, and if
k
is even then
G
has a bisection of weight at least
\frac{k+2}{2(k+1)}w(G)
.

As far as we know there are no publications on bounds for the maximum bisections of weighted graphs. However, there are some results related to our conjecture for the unweighted case. In
[
9
]
, Lee, Loh, and Sudakov showed the following result by using equitable colourings.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
9
]

If
G
has maximum degree at most
k
, then there exists a bisection of size at least
\frac{k+1}{2k}|E(G)|-\frac{k(k+1)}{4}

if
k
is odd and
\frac{k+2}{2(k+1)}|E(G)|-\frac{k(k+2)}{4}
if

One can generalize this result easily to weighted graphs using an argument similar to that in
[
9
]
. In Section

Let

Lemma 2.1
.

[
7

Let

be a weighted graph and

. Then,
G
has a cut with weight at least
\frac{w(G)+w(B)}{2}
.

We say that

, if

is the union of vertex-disjoint bipartite subgraphs
B_{i}
’s (not necessarily connected) of

with partite sets
(X_{i},Y_{i})
where

|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|
. The following lemma can be proved using a similar argument to the one in the proof of Lemma

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E,w)
be a weighted graph and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
. Then,
G
has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{w(G)+w(B)}{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
|V(G)\setminus V(B)|\leq 1
. For, if there are at least two vertices
\{v,w\}\in V(G)\setminus V(B)
, then we can add the bipartite subgraph
B^{\prime}
with partite sets
\{v\},\{w\}
to
B
and denote the new subgraph again by
B
. Clearly,
B
is still in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
and the weight of
B
does not decrease. Therefore, we only need to prove the bound for this new subgraph
B
.

Suppose that
B=\cup_{i=1}^{t}B_{i}
, where
B_{i}
is a bipartite subgraph with bipartition
(X_{i},Y_{i})
. For each
i\in[t]
, we color the vertices in
X_{i}
red with probability
1/2
and blue otherwise, independently of the colors of the other sets
X_{j}
. If
X_{i}
is coloured red then
Y_{i}
is coloured blue, and if
X_{i}
is coloured blue then
Y_{i}
is coloured red. If there exists a vertex in
V(G)\setminus V(B)
, then we colour it red with probability
1/2
and blue otherwise. Let
X
be the set of red vertices and let
Y
be the set of blue vertices. Consider the bisection
(X,Y)
. In fact,
(X,Y)
is always a bisection since
|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|
for all

and
|V(G)\setminus V(B)|\leq 1
. Observe that each edge in
B
will be in this bisection with probability 1. All other edges will be in this bisection with probability at least 1/2. Thus, by the linearity of expectation, we have

\mathbb{E}(w(X,Y))\geq\frac{w(G)-w(B)}{2}+w(B)=\frac{w(G)+w(B)}{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Since any matching

Corollary 2.3

Let

\frac{w(G)+w(M)}{2}

We will need to use the following bound for chromatic index known as Vizing’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

[

For any simple graph
G

\Delta(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1

From Vizing’s Theorem, every graph is classified into one of two classes. A graph

\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1

Theorem 2.5
.

Every weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{\chi^{\prime}(G)+1}{2\chi^{\prime}(G)}w(G)
. In particular, Conjecture
1.1
holds for all graphs in Class 1.

Proof.

Since the edge set of
G
can be partitioned into
\chi^{\prime}(G)
matching, there is a matching
M
with weight at least
\frac{w(G)}{\chi^{\prime}(G)}
. By Corollary
2.3
,
G
has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{w(G)+w(M)}{2}\geq\frac{\chi^{\prime}(G)+1}{2\chi^{\prime}(G)}w(G)
. Furthermore, if
G
is in Class 1 i.e.
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
, then
G
has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2\Delta(G)}w(G)
.
∎

Conjecture
1.1
for weighted subcubic graphs

In this section, we prove the Conjecture
1.1
for weighted subcubic graphs i.e. when
k=3
.

For any integer
k>0
, a
k
-bisection
of a graph
G
is a bisection
(A,B)
where every component in
G[A]\cup G[B]
is a tree with at most
k
vertices. Mattiolo and Mazzuoccolo
[
12
]
showed the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
12
]

Every cubic multigraph has a 3-bisection.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
F
is a forest with at most
|V(F)|/2
edges, then there is a biparite subgraph
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(F)
such that
E(B)=E(F)
.

Proof.

Let
c(F)
be the number of connected components in
F
,
m_{i}
the number of components with
i
edges and
t
the maximum size of a component in
F
. Since
F
is a forest
|E(F)|=|V(F)|-c(F)\leq|V(F)|/2
,
which implies that
|V(F)|/2\leq c(F)=\sum_{i=0}^{t}m_{i}
. Thus,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}i\,m_{i}=|E(F)|\leq|V(F)|/2\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t}m_{i},

which means that
\sum_{i=2}^{t}m_{i}(i-1)\leq m_{0}
. Note that for any
i\geq 2
, a tree with
i
edges is a bipartite graph in which the sizes of two partite sets differ by at most
i-1
vertices, and therefore, we need at most
\sum_{i=2}^{t}m_{i}(i-1)
isolated vertices to balance the partite sets of every such tree. The inequality
\sum_{i=2}^{t}m_{i}(i-1)\leq m_{0}
guarantees that we have enough isolated vertices to use.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Every weighted cubic multigraph
G
has a bisection
(X,Y)
such that the following holds.

(i)

G[X]\cup G[Y]
is a forest;

(ii)

|(X,Y)|
attains the maximum value among all bisection that satisfy (i);

(iii)

\Delta(G[X])\leq 1
and
|E(G[Y])|\leq|Y|/2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
G
has a bisection (not necessarily a 3-bisection) such that both partition classes induce a forest. Let
(X,Y)
be a maximum bisection subject to the condition that
G[X]\cup G[Y]
is a forest. We will show that
(X,Y)
also meets the last requirement. Assume without loss of the generality that
\Delta(G[X])\leq\Delta(G[Y])
. We will first show that

\Delta(G[X])\leq 1.

(1)

Suppose for a contradiction that
2\leq\Delta(G[X])\leq\Delta(G[Y])
. Then, there exist
v\in X
and
w\in Y
such that
d_{G[X]}(v)\geq 2
and
d_{G[Y]}(w)\geq 2
. Since both
v
and
w
have at most one neighbour in the other part,
G[X\cup\{w\}-\{v\}]
and
G[Y\cup\{v\}-\{w\}]
are still forests. However,
|(X\cup\{w\}-\{v\},Y\cup\{v\}-\{w\})|>|(X,Y)|
, a contradiction.

By (
1
), every connected component of
G[X]
is either an edge or a single vertex and therefore
|E(G[X])|\leq|X|/2
. Thus, since
G
is 3-regular and
|X|=|Y|
(as every graph has an even number of odd degree vertices),

\displaystyle|E(G[Y])|

\displaystyle=\frac{3|Y|-|(X,Y)|}{2}=\frac{3|X|-|(X,Y)|}{2}

\displaystyle=|E(G[X])|\leq|X|/2=|Y|/2,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Every weighted subcubic graph
G
has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{2}{3}w(G)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
G
has at most one vertex with degree not equal to
3
as we may add edges of weight
0
between any two vertices with degree less than
3
. We consider three cases.

Case 1:

G
is 3-regular. Let
(X,Y)
be a bisection as in Lemma
3.3
. If
w(X,Y)\geq\frac{2}{3}w(G)
then the proof is complete. If not, by Lemma
3.2
, there is a bipartite subgraph
B_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(G[Y])
that contains
E(G[Y])
.
B_{1}
together with the maximum matching in
G[X]
forms a bipartite subgraph
B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
that contains all edges in
G[X]\cup G[Y]
. Now

w(B_{2})=w(G[X])+w(G[Y])=w(G)-w(X,Y)>\frac{1}{3}w(G),

and the result follows by Lemma
2.2
.

Case 2:

G
has one vertex with degree one, say
d_{G}(z)=1
. Then, we add two vertices
x
and
y
, two multiple edges
xy
with weight 0 as well as edges
xz
and
yz
with weight 0. Denote the resulting graph by
H
. Clearly,

is 3-regular. Let
(X,Y)
be the bisection obtained by Lemma
3.3
. Note that
x
and
y
are in different parts since
H[X]\cup H[Y]
is a forest (w.l.o.g. we may assume that
x\in X
and
y\in Y
). If
w(X,Y)\geq\frac{2}{3}w(H)
, then
(X\setminus\{x\},Y\setminus\{y\})
is a desired bisection in
G
. Thus, we may assume that
w(H[X\setminus\{x\}]\cup H[Y\setminus\{y\}])>\frac{1}{3}w(H)
and consider the following two subcases.

Subcase 2.1:

z\in Y
. Then since
|E(H[Y])|\leq|Y|/2
and
yz\in H[Y]
,
|E(H[Y\setminus\{y\}])|\leq|Y|/2-1
. Thus, by Lemma
3.2
we can find a bipartite subgraph in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(H[Y\setminus\{y\}])
, which together with the maximum matching in

forms a bipartite subgraph
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(H-\{x,y\})
that contains all edges in
H[X\setminus\{x\}]\cup H[Y\setminus\{y\}]
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
we have a bisection in
H-\{x,y\}
with weight at least
\frac{2}{3}w(H)=\frac{2}{3}w(G)
. Clearly, this is also a desired bisection of
G
.

Subcase 2.2:

z\in X
. If
\Delta(H[Y])=1
then there is a matching that contains all edges in

and the result follows by Corollary
2.3
. It is impossible that

, since, if this were the case, the bisection obtained by swapping the vertex with degree 3 in

and

would yield a larger bisection the partition classes of which would form a forest. Thus, we may assume that there is a vertex

be the neighbour of

(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
still satisfy (i)-(iii) in Lemma
3.3
and
z\in Y^{\prime}
. Therefore, we can now proceed as in Subcase 2.1.

Case 3:
If
G
has a vertex with degree two, say
d_{G}(z)=2
. Then
|V(G)|
is odd. We add a new vertex
z^{\prime}
and an edge
zz^{\prime}
with weight 0. Denote the resulting graph by
H^{\prime}
. By Case 2,
H^{\prime}
has a bisection
(X,Y)
with weight
\frac{2}{3}w(H^{\prime})=\frac{2}{3}w(G)
. Therefore,
(X\setminus\{z^{\prime}\},Y\setminus\{z^{\prime}\})
is a desired bisection of

.

Triangle-free Subcubic Graphs

In this section, we show a lower bound for a maximum weight bisection of a weighted triangle-free subcubic graph, which is not a
K_{1,3}
. Indeed, we show that every triangle-free subcubic graph
G
, which is not a

, has a bisection with weight at least
\frac{613}{855}w(G)\approx 0.716959w(G)
. Note that the maximum bisection of the Petersen graph has at most 11 edges. Therefore,
11/15w(G)\approx 0.733333w(G)
may be the best possible lower bound.

4.1
Bridgress triangle-free subcubic graphs

In this subsection, we prove the above-mentioned lower bound for bridgeless triangle-free subcubic graphs.

Theorem
4.1
is a slight generalization of Petersen’s matching theorem
[

]
, which can be proved by applying Tutte’s theorem
[

]
.
(This result can also be found in Exercise 16.4.8 of Bondy and Murty’s textbook on graph theory

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be any bridgeless cubic graph, and let

be any edge in

. Then,
G
has a perfect matching containing

.

We first show that the following multigraph version of the above theorem holds.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be any bridgeless cubic multigraph, and let

be any edge in
G
. Then,
G
has a perfect matching containing

.

Proof.

We may assume that
G
is connected. Then the result is trivial when there are three multiple edges between two vertices. Thus, we may assume that any pair of vertices in
V(G)
has at most two multiple edges. For any two edges between vertices
x
and
y
in
G
, delete these two edges, add two new vertices
v_{xy}
and
w_{xy}
, also edges
xv_{xy}
,
xw_{xy}
,
yv_{xy}
,
yw_{xy}
and
v_{xy}w_{xy}
. Denote the resulting graph by
G^{\prime}
. Observe that
G^{\prime}
is a bridgeless cubic graph. Note that if there are two edges between
x
and
y
, then any perfect matching in
G^{\prime}
contains
\{xv_{xy},yw_{xy}\}
,
\{xw_{xy},yv_{xy}\}
or
v_{xy}w_{xy}
. Thus, from any perfect matching

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G=(V,E,w)
be a triangle-free graph with a perfect matching
M
. If
\chi^{\prime}(G/M)\leq k
, then there exists a bipartite subgraph
B_{C}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
with weight at least
\frac{1}{k}w(G)+\frac{k-1}{k}w(M)
and a bisection with weight at least
\frac{k+1}{2k}w(G)+\frac{k-1}{2k}w(M)
.

In particular, if
\Delta(G)\leq 3
, then there exists a bipartite subgraph
B_{C}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
with weight at least
\frac{1}{5}w(G)+\frac{4}{5}w(M)
and a bisection with weight at least
\frac{3}{5}w(G)+\frac{2}{5}w(M)
.

Proof.

For any edge
ab\in M
, we denote by
v_{ab}
the vertex corresponding to it in
G/M
. Let the weight
w(v_{ab}v_{cd})
of any edge
v_{ab}v_{cd}
be the sum of the weights of edges in the bipartite graph
G[\{a,b,c,d\}]
(the graph is bipartite as it is triangle-free).

Since
\chi^{\prime}(G/M)\leq k
, there is a matching
M^{\prime}
in
G/M
with weight at least
\frac{1}{k}w(G/M)=\frac{1}{k}(w(G)-w(M))
. Observe that each edge
v_{ab}v_{cd}\in M^{\prime}
corresponds to the bipartite subgraph
G[\{a,b,c,d\}]
which is clearly in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(G)
since
G[\{a,b,c,d\}]
has a perfect matching, and
G
is triangle-free. Let
B
be the union of these bipartite subgraphs as well as edges in
M
which are not contained in any of these bipartite subgraphs. Then,

\displaystyle w(B)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w(M)+w(M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w(M)+\frac{1}{k}(w(G)-w(M))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}w(G)+\frac{k-1}{k}w(M),

and therefore by Lemma
2.2
we have that
G
admits a bisection with weight at least
\frac{w(G)+w(B)}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}(w(G)+\frac{1}{k}w(G)+\frac{k-1}{k}w(M))=\frac{k+1}{2k}w(G)+\frac{k-1}{2k}w(M)
.

If
\Delta(G)\leq 3
, then
\Delta(G/M)\leq 4
. Thus, by Theorem
2.5
, we have
\chi^{\prime}(G/M)\leq 5
, and therefore we are done.
∎

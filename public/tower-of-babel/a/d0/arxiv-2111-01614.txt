Measured foliations at infinity of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds close to the Fuchsian locus
Diptaishik Choudhury

Abstract
. Measured foliations at infinity of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds are a natural analogue at infinity to the bending laminations on the boundary of its convex core. We show that given a pair of arational measured foliations ( 𝖥 + , 𝖥 − ) (\mathsf{F}_{+},\mathsf{F}_{-}) which fill a closed hyperbolic surface S S , for t > 0 t>0 sufficiently small, t ​ 𝖥 + t\mathsf{F_{+}} and t ​ 𝖥 − t\mathsf{F_{-}} can be uniquely realised as the measured foliations at infinity of a quasi-Fuchsian manifold homeomorphic to S × ℝ S\times\mathbb{R} , which is sufficiently close to the Fuchsian locus. Here arationality means that the corresponding measured laminations are maximal. The proof is inspired by Bonahon’s in [ 4 ] which shows that a quasi-Fuchsian manifold close to the Fuchsian locus can be uniquely determined by the data of filling measured bending laminations on the boundary of its convex core. We also interpret the result in half-pipe geometry.

Introduction

T^{*}_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)

and again identify it with the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials

on
(S,[c])
(see §§

denote the space of isotopy classes of quasi-Fuchsian metrics on

There are unique holomorphic maps, well-defined up to right composition by Möbius transformations, from the universal covers

Let

[

\mathsf{F_{+}},\mathsf{F_{-}}\in\mathcal{MF}(S)
. Further, given a pair of measured foliations

So we ask (see Question

[

Now let
\mathcal{MF}_{0}(S)\subset\mathcal{MF}(S)

.

For every pair of measured foliations
(\mathsf{F_{+}},\mathsf{F_{-}})

\epsilon_{\mathsf{F_{\pm}}}>0

and

That is, given the map
\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{QF}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{MF}(\partial^{+}_{\infty}M)\times\mathcal{MF}({\partial^{-}_{\infty}M)}
sending a quasi-Fuchsian metric to the measured foliations at infinity at the positive and negative end respectively; we have a unique solution
g\in\mathcal{QF}(S)
to the equation
\mathfrak{F}(g)=(t\mathsf{F_{+}},t\mathsf{F_{-}})
when restricted to
(\mathsf{F_{+}},\mathsf{F_{-}})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)

small enough. Now let

be the unique holomorphic quadratic differentials realising
\mathsf{H}\in\mathcal{MF}(S)

(S,[c])

,

small enough; then the Schwarzians at infinity of
(M,g)
are

1.1.
Analogy between bending laminations and measured foliations at infinity

There are a few points of analogies between the data on the boundary at infinity and that on the boundary of the convex core of a quasi-Fuchsian manifold which makes Theorem
1.1
really interesting. We denote the convex core of
M
, as
\mathcal{CC}(M)

S\times[-1,1]
. Call
\partial^{+}\mathcal{CC}(M)
and

(see §

and

(see

is the space of measured geodesic laminations on

The similarity between the variational formulae for the dual volume

sending a hyperbolic metric

Outline

We prove Theorem
1.1
by showing the existence of unique paths in

at

where

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic surfaces

We will consider
S
to be a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
. We call
S
to be a hyperbolic surface if we have an atlas
(U_{i},\phi_{i})
on
S
where
\phi_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}
are charts such that at each intersection
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, the composition
\phi_{i}\circ\phi^{-}_{j}
are locally restrictions of elements of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. An alternative defition can be to say that
S
is a closed hyperbolic surface if it carries a complete Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature
-1
. It follows from Gauss-Bonnet theorem that
S
can carry such a metric only when
g>1
. In such a case, one can also state that
S
is isometric to the quotient of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by
\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

On the other hand, a complex structure
c
on
S
consists of an atlas
\left\{U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\right\}
on
S
where
\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are holomorphic maps and the transition functions
\phi_{i}\circ\phi^{-1}_{j}
are biholomorphic maps on
\phi_{i}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
. Given a complex structure
c
, we consider its equivalence class under diffeomorphisms of
S
isotopic to the identity and denote it as
[c]
.

Consider now a Riemannian metric
g
on
S
, we can define:

Definition 2.1
.

A conformal class on a surface
S
is an equivalence class of Riemannian metrics
[[g]]
, where

\displaystyle[[g]]=\left\{e^{2u}g|u:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\right\}

and
u
is smooth.

When
S
is oriented there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of complex structures on
S
under diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity and conformal classes on
S
again up to diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. Owing to this we can also view Teichmüller space as the space of conformal classes on
S
(see
[
43
]
). We will denote both a conformal and complex structure on
S
as
c
. A Riemannian metric on
S
in a conformal class has the local expression
g(z)=\rho(z)dzd\bar{z}
where
\rho(z)\geq 0
is a smooth function on
S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

We will recall now an important lemma concerning the change of Gaussian or intrinsic curvature
K_{g}
, associated to a Riemannian metric
g
under change of conformal factor in the same conformal class. See
[
25
]
among others for a reference:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g
and
g^{\prime}
be two Riemannian metrics on
S
in the same conformal class and let
u:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
g^{\prime}=e^{2u}g
. Let
K_{g}
and
K_{g^{\prime}}
be the Gauss curvatures associated to
g
and
g^{\prime}
respectively. Then
K_{g^{\prime}}=e^{-2u}(-\Delta_{g}u+K_{g})
, where
\Delta_{g}u
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator for the metric
g
.

Here we use the convention that
\Delta_{g}
is negative of the usual analysts Laplacian. That is given the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
g
we define
\Delta_{g}u=-\trace(\hess(u))=-\trace(\nabla\nabla u)
. If we consider
g
to be a conformal metric, then the hyperbolic metric
m
in the conformal class of
g
is given by
m=e^{2u}g
where
u
solves:

\displaystyle-1=e^{-2u}(-\Delta_{g}u+K_{g})

So in dimension
2
, corresponding to every conformal class on
S
, one has a unique hyperbolic metric and also an equivalence class of complex structures.

2.2.
Fuchsian and quasi-Fuchsian
3
-manifolds

We will consider
3
manifolds
M
here which are quotient
\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Associated to the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
one has the limit set
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
which is the set of accumulation points of orbit of
\Gamma
, and it can so shown that it is a subset of
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
. When
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, we call
M
to be a Fuchsian manifold. In this case
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
is a circle on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
. This can be seen as the boundary of the totally geodesic copy of
\mathbb{H}^{2}\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
preserved by the action.

We call a
3
manifold
M
to be quasi-Fuchsian if the
\Gamma<PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is such that
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
is a quasi-circle. To define concretely, one has a quasi-conformal map
\phi:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and a Fuchsian subgroup
\Gamma_{0}
such that
\Gamma:=\phi^{-1}\circ\Gamma_{0}\circ\phi
. To simplify, we can consider
\Gamma
to be quasi-Fuchsian if
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
is a Jordan curve on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

One more charateristic difference between Fuchsian and quasi-Fuchsian manifolds is by the different geometry of their convex hulls. Given the limit set
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
as the setting above, we can consider its convex hull in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. In the Fuchsian case we recover the totally geodesic copy of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
preserved by the action as
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
is a circle. In the quasi-Fuchsian case we have that the convex hull is a closed, convex region which upon taking quotient becomes the convex core
\mathcal{CC}(M)
. The convex core is thus the smallest-non empty convex submanifold contained in the quasi-Fuchsian manifold and it comes with two boundary components since it is
S\times[-1,1]
.

2.3.
Teichmüller space

We will now briefly introduce the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
associated to a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
.

Definition 2.3
.

The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
is the space equivalence classes of complex structures on
S
under diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity.

Owing to the presence of an unique hyperbolic metric in every conformal class or class of complex structure we can alternately define
\mathcal{T}(S)
as the space of equivalence classes of hyperbolic metrics on
S
up to diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity.

Now recall that the boundary at infinity
\partial^{\pm}_{\infty}M
are two copies of
S
which carry their respective complex structures. It so happens that when
M
is Fuchian, the complex structures/hyperbolic metrics are identical and thus if one defines
\mathcal{F}(S)
as the equivalence class of Fuchsian metrics on
M
up to diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity, then
\mathcal{F}(S)\cong\mathcal{T}(S)
.

Now, given two conformal structures
c,c^{\prime}
we can define the notion of quasi-conformal map between them.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}
be two domains and
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
be a homeomorphism with continuous partial derivatives with respect to
z
and
\bar{z}
. We denote
\phi
to be
k
-quasiconformal if

\displaystyle k_{\phi}(z)=\frac{|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)|+|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}(z)|}{|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)|-|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}(z)|}\leq k

for almost every
z\in\Omega
.

We will denote the
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial w}
as
f_{w}
. The Beltrami differential
\mu
associated to the map
\phi
is defined as the ratio

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}=\frac{\phi_{\bar{z}}}{\phi_{z}}

which is defined almost everywhere, is measurable and satisfies
||\mu||_{\infty}<1
. Equivalently
k_{\phi}
has the expression
\frac{1+|\mu|}{1-|\mu|}
, which is bounded above by
k=\frac{1+||\mu||_{\infty}}{1-||\mu||_{\infty}}
and
k_{\phi}
is called the eccentricity coefficient of
\phi
. We also note that a Beltrami differential on
(S,[c])
is a tensor of type
(-1,1)
. We will use this to define the notion of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
S
and how it relates to the tangent and cotangent space of
\mathcal{T}(S)
.

2.4.
Holomorphic quadratic differentials

A holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
(S,c)
is a tensor of type
(2,0)
which in local coordinates can be written as
f(z)dz^{2}
, where
f
is a holomorphic function. The space of holomorphic quadratic differential denoted as
Q(S)
forms a bundle over
\mathcal{T}(S)
, where
\mathcal{T}(S)
is seen as the space of complex structures on
S
up to diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. The fiber over an equivalence class
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, which is denoted as
Q(S,[c])
is a vector space that can be shown to have real dimension
6g-6
(by, for example, the Riemann-Roch formula). Moreover, a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
has zeroes on
S
the degree of which is defined in terms of the degree of the zero of the local Taylor expansion of
q
. To be precise, if
q
has a zero of order
k
at a point
p\in S
then this means that for all chart centred at
p
on
S
,
q
has the local expression
f(z)z^{k}dz^{2}
for some holomorphic function
f(z)
such that
f(0)\neq 0
.. Moreover, it follows from, for example the Riemann-Roch theorem, that the sum of the degrees of all zeroes of
q
on
S
is
4g-4
. The space
Q(S)
further carries a natural stratification depending on the order of the zeroes of
q
. Please consult, for example
[
23
]
for references on this topic.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\overline{k}
be a
n
-tuple of integers
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n})
such that
\sum_{i}^{n}k_{i}=4g-4
. The stratum
Q^{\overline{k}}(S)
is the set of holomorphic quadratic differentials
q
such that the degrees of the zeroes of
q
are given by the
k_{i}
. We say that
q
is generic, if
k_{i}=1
for all
i
.

Holomorphic quadratic differentials with only
4g-4
simple zeroes are termed as generic quadratic differentials and it is known from
[
12
]
that they form a dense open subset of
Q(S)
which will be denoted as
Q_{0}(S)
.

Notice that the product of a Beltrami differential and a holomorphic quadratic differential gives us a
(1,1)
tensor. Let
B(S,c)
denote the vector space of measurable Beltrami differentials on
(S,c)
where an element is expressed locally as
\mu=b(z)\frac{d\bar{z}}{dz}
. From here we have a natural complex pairing between
\mu\in B(S,c)
and
q\in Q(S,c)
as:

\displaystyle\left\langle q,\mu\right\rangle=\int_{(S,c)}q\mu dzd\bar{z}

It follows as a consequence, see
[
23
]
, that:

Proposition 2.6
.

There is an isomorphism of vector spaces between

\displaystyle T_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)\cong B(S,c)/Q(S,c)^{\perp}{\quad and\quad}T^{*}_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)\cong Q(S,c)

where
Q(S,c)^{\perp}=\left\{\mu\in B(S,c)|\left\langle\mu,q\right\rangle=0,\forall q\in Q(S,c)\right\}
.

This allows us to define the Weil-Petersson metric on
T^{*}_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)
as:

\displaystyle\left\langle q_{1},q_{2}\right\rangle_{WP}=\int_{S}\frac{f_{1}(z)\overline{f_{2}(z)}}{\rho(z)}dz\overline{dz}

where the hyperbolic metric in the class
[c]
has the expression
\rho(z)|dz|^{2}
and
q_{i}=f_{i}(z)dz^{2}
for
i=1,2
are two holomorphic quadratic differentials in
Q(S,c)
. This also induces an inner product on the tangent space
T_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)
by duality. The Weil-Petersson metric gives
\mathcal{T}(S)
with the structure of negatively curved Riemannian manifold. On the other hand, the
L^{1}
-norm on the cotangent space
T^{*}_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)
is defined as:

\displaystyle||q||_{1}=\int_{(S,c)}|f(z)|dz\wedge\overline{dz}.

This induces a Teichmüller norm on
T\mathcal{T}(S)
via the duality between Beltrami differentials and holomorphic quadratic differentials. One way to express the associated metric, called the Teichmüller metric
d_{\mathcal{T}(S)}
, is:

(3)

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{T}(S)}([c],[c^{\prime}]):=\frac{1}{2}\inf\left\{\log k_{\phi}|\text{ }\phi:(S,[c])\rightarrow(S,[c^{\prime}])\text{ quasiconformal isotopic to the identity}\right\}

One can consult, for example
[
33
]
, for further details in this topic.

2.5.
Measured foliations on
S
and the space
\mathcal{MF}(S)

Following
[
22
]
we define:

Definition 2.7
.

A smooth measured foliation
\mathsf{F}
on
S
with singularities
\left\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\right\}
of
order
\left\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\right\}
(respectively) is given by an open covering
{U_{i}}
of

and
open sets
\left\{V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\right\}
around
\left\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\right\}
(respectively) along with smooth non-vanishing real valued
1
-forms

defined on

such that:

•

d\phi_{i}=\pm d\phi_{j}
on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}

•

around each

there is an open neighbourhood
V_{l}
and a chart
(x_{1},x_{2}):V_{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
d\phi_{i}=\mathfrak{I}(z^{\frac{k_{l}}{2}+1}dz)
on
U_{i}\cap V_{j}
where
z=x_{1}+\textbf{i}x_{2}
.

Immersed lines on
S
along which

vanish give a

\mathsf{F}
on

and we have a
(k_{j}+2)
pronged singularity at

. Given an arc
\gamma
on

which avoids the zeroes
(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n})
, a measured foliation
\mathsf{F}
associates a transverse measure to

defined as
\mu_{\mathsf{F}}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}|d\phi|
, where
|d\phi|
restricted on each

is given by

This measure is invariant under isotopies that maintain the same end points of

and the transversality of the intersection of

with the given foliation. That is, if
\gamma^{\prime}
is isotopic to
\gamma
with the same end points and maintaining the transversality at every time, then
\mu_{\mathsf{F}}(\gamma)=\mu_{\mathsf{F}}(\gamma^{\prime})
. So, given a class
[\gamma]
, we define:

Definition 2.8
.

The intersection number of
\mathsf{F}
with a isotopy class of closed curves
[\gamma]
avoiding the singularities of
\mathsf{F}
is defined
i([\gamma],\mathsf{F})=\inf_{\gamma\in[\gamma]}i(\gamma,\mathsf{F})=\inf_{\gamma\in[\gamma]}\mu_{\mathsf{F}}(\gamma)
, where the infimum is taken over all
\gamma\in[\gamma]
.

As we can see that the intersection number defines a function from the set of closed curves up to isotopy on
S
to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and we define following, for example
[
15
]
:

Definition 2.9
.

Two measured foliations
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{G}
on
S
are said to be equivalent if they define the same intersection number. The space of equivalence classes of measured foliations on
S
will be denoted as
\mathcal{MF}(S)
.

The space
\mathcal{MF}(S)
can also be defined through a topological equivalence of two foliations which comes via Whitehead moves but we do not elaborate on that. Also it follows from Proposition
2.1
in
[
22
]
that a measured foliation
\mathsf{F}
can have
4g-4
pronged-singularities counted up to multiplicity.

There is also an action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
on
\mathcal{MF}(S)
defined as
t.\mathsf{F}\mapsto t\mathsf{F}
where the latter denotes the measured foliation obtained by multiplying
t
by the
1
-forms
d\phi_{i}
which give us the measured foliation
\mathsf{F}
as par Definition
2.7
.
The space
(\mathcal{MF}(S)\setminus 0)/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is called the space of projectivised measured foliations, denoted as
P\mathcal{MF}(S)
and it is identified with the Thurston boundary of
\mathcal{T}(S)
. We will denote by
[\mathsf{F}]
as the equivalence class of
\mathsf{F}
in
P\mathcal{MF}(S)
. Interested readers can consult, for example
[
15
]
, for further details in this topic.

We also introduce the notion of arational measured foliations here as:

Definition 2.10
.

A measured foliation is said to be arational if all its singularities have
3
prongs and if there are no leaves of the foliation joining the singularities.

We call leaves of the foliations joining prongs of the singularities as saddle connections.

2.6.
Minimal surfaces in hyperbolic
3
manifolds

Let
i:S\rightarrow M
be an immersion of
S
into
M
. Associated to this, we have the data of the first fundamental form denoted as
I
on
S
which is the induced metric on
S
inherited from the ambient space and the second fundamental form denoted a
I\hskip-2.84544ptI
which is a symmetric bilinear form on the tangent bundle
TS
. Associated to the couple
(I,I\hskip-2.84544ptI)
we have a unique self-adjoint operator
B
, called the shape operator, which satisfies the relation

\displaystyle I\hskip-2.84544ptI(x,y)=I(Bx,y)=I(x,By)

where
x,y\in T_{p}S,\forall p\in S
. We can also define another quantity associated to the immersion called the third fundamental form
I\hskip-2.84544ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI
defined as
I\hskip-2.84544ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI(x,y)=I(Bx,By)=I(B^{2}x,y)
for
x,y,B
as before.

The eigenvalues of
B
gives us the principal curvatures associated to the immersion. Since minimal immersions are those immersions for which the mean curvature of
S
is zero we can define as well that:

Definition 2.11
.

An immersion is minimal if and only if
B
is traceless.

On the other hand, assume we are given a smooth Riemannian metric
g
on
S
and a symmetric bilinear form
h
on
TS
. The couple
(g,h)
will be associated to the data of an immersion of
S
if it satisfies the following:

(1)

The Codazzi equation,
d^{\nabla}h=0
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of

.

(2)

The Gauss equation,
K_{I}=-1+\mathsf{det}_{g}(h)
, where
K_{g}
denotes the Gaussian or intrinsic curvature of the metric
g
.

Now we define the notion of an almost-Fuchsian manifold as:

Definition 2.12
.

A quasi-Fuchsian hyperbolic
3
-manifold
M\cong S\times\mathbb{R}
is called almost-Fuchsian if it contains a closed minimal surface homeomorphic to
S
with principal curvatures in
(-1,1)
.

Given an immersed surface in
M
, an outcome of the Codazzi equation is that, the traceless part of the second fundamental form
(I\hskip-2.84544ptI)_{0}
is equal to the real part of a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
.
Now, following Corollary
2.9
of
[
25
]
we can show that if

is an almost-fuchsian manifold then the closed minimal surface it contains is unique. This allows us to parametrise almost-Fuchsian hyperbolic manifolds by an open subset
\Omega
in

, following
[
25
]
, Theorem
2.12
:

Theorem 2.13
.

There exists an open subset
\Omega\subset T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
such that we have the following bijection:

(1)

Given a point
([c],q)\in\Omega
, there exists a unique almost-Fuchsian metric on
M
such that the unique minimal surface has first fundamental form conformal to
[c]
and the second fundamental form
I\hskip-2.84544ptI
is
\mathfrak{R}(q)
.

(2)

Given a almost-Fuchsian metric

on

.

2.7.
Schwarzians at infinity of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds

Recall that the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
is identified with the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. The components
\partial^{+}_{\infty}M
and
\partial^{-}_{\infty}M
are the quotient of domains in
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
under the action of
\Gamma<PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and so they carry canonical
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-structure, that is we have an open covering of
S
by an atlas
(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
such that
\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
are charts to open domains in
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and in the overlap
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
of two charts the change of coordinate map
\phi_{i}\circ\phi^{-1}_{j}
is locally a restriction of a Möbius transformations. Denote the space of equivalence classes of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-structures on
S
under diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity as
\mathcal{CP}(S)
.

Now, given a
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-structure on
S
we have an underlying complex structure as Möbius transformations are biholomorphisms and for
\partial^{\pm}_{\infty}M
it is precisely
[c_{\pm}]
up to equivalence. This gives us a natural forgetful map
\mathcal{CP}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(S)
mapping a
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-structures to the underlying complex one. Now by the Uniformisation theorem any complex structure
c
on
S
arises as the quotient of the action of some discrete subgroup
\Gamma_{c}
of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. As
\Gamma_{c}<PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
as well and
\mathbb{H}^{2}
can be seen as the unit disc
\Delta\subset\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, we have a canonical
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-structure associated to a complex structure which we call the standard Fuchsian complex projective structure and this gives us a continuous section
\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{CP}(S)
.
The Schwarzian derivative yields a parametrisation of the fibers of the forgetful map
\mathcal{CP}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(S)
. In general, given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
,
the Schwarzian derivative of a locally injective holomorphic map
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic quadratic differential defined as:

\displaystyle\sigma(u)=((\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{u^{\prime}})^{{}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{u^{\prime}})^{2})dz^{2}

One way to obtain the expression on the right hand side above is to consider the unique Möbius transformation

M_{u}
which matches with
u
up to second order derivative. The expression above is precisely the difference of
the third order terms in the local Taylor series expansion of
u
and
M_{u}
(see Proposition
6.3.3
of
[
23
]
). Further, they have two remarkable properties:

•

For two locally injective holomorphic maps
u,v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
we have
\sigma(u\circ v)=v^{*}\sigma(u)+\sigma(v)

•

\sigma(A)=0
if and only if
A
is a Möbius transformation.

In particular, there is a unique map defined up to right conjugation by Möbius transformations between a given complex projective structure on
S
and the standard
Fuchsian one which is holomorphic with respect to the underlying canonical complex structure and by virtue of
the properties above, the Schwarzian derivative for this holomorphic map can be defined in a chart independent
way. This is called the Schwarzian parametrisation of a complex projective structure on
S
with respect to
the standard Fuchsian complex projective structure (see
[
13
,
39
]
). When
M\in\mathcal{QF}(S)
, the components of
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}\setminus\Lambda_{\Gamma}
have non-trivial Schwarzian derivatives
associated to them by construction which descend to two holomorphic quadratic differentials on
\partial^{\pm}_{\infty}M
upon
taking quotients. So we define:

Definition 2.14
.

The Schwarzians at infinity
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
are the holomorphic quadratic differentials obtained on
(\partial^{+}_{\infty}M,[c_{+}])
and
(\partial^{-}_{\infty}M,[c_{-}])
by the Schwarzian parametrisation of the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
structures on
\partial^{\pm}_{\infty}M
with respect to the corresponding standard Fuchsian complex projective structure.

Also note that when
M\in\mathcal{F}(S)
due to the second property above the Schwarzians at infinity are zero. What is more important to us from this discussion is that due to this we get another parametrisation of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds by
Q(S)
by considering the Schwarzian derivative and complex structure appearing at one end at infinity. So we can therefore construct a well-defined map

(4)

\mathfrak{A}:\mathcal{QF}(S)\to Q(\partial^{\pm}_{\infty}M)~.

Here
Q(S)
denotes the bundle of holomorphic quadratic differentials over
\mathcal{T}(S)
, whose fiber over a point
(S,[h])
coincides with the vector space
H^{0}((\Sigma,h),K^{2})
, where
K
denotes the canonical divisor of
(S,[h])
. Consequently, the space
Q(S)
is a complex manifold of dimension
3g-3
, where
g
denotes the genus of
S
. In fact, the map
\mathfrak{A}
turns out to be injective, and
\mathcal{QF}(S)
and
Q(\partial^{+}_{\infty}M)
being manifolds of the same real dimension, the invariance of domain theorem implies that its image is an open subset of
Q(\partial^{+}_{\infty}M)
(see §
2.1.3
of
[
10
]
).

3.
Measured foliations realised by holomorphic quadratic differentials.

Now, given
q\in Q(S,c)
, away from its zeroes we can always perform a local change of coordinates
z\mapsto w:=\int\sqrt{q}
on
S
such that

has the local expression

with respect to this coordinate. If we write

then the holomorphic quadratic differential

canonically equips

with two measured foliations:

•

The horizontal measured foliation, which are immersed lines given by

, i.e the horizontal lines of
\mathbb{C}
. Its transverse measure being given by
|\mathfrak{I}\sqrt{dw^{2}}|=|dw_{2}|
.

•

The vertical measured foliation, which are immersed lines along which

, i.e the vertical lines of
\mathbb{C}
.
Its transverse measure being given by
|\mathfrak{R}\sqrt{dw^{2}}|=|dw_{1}|
.

Moreover, notice that the horizontal measured foliations (resp. vertical measured foliations) of quadratic differential
|dw^{2}|
gives us all the horizontal lines (resp. vertical lines) on

, thus inspiring the nomenclature. So we define:

Definition 3.1
.

The horizontal measured foliation
\mathrm{hor}_{[c]}(q)
(resp. vertical measured foliation
\mathrm{ver}_{[c]}(q)
) of

(S,c)
is a smooth singular measured foliation, with singularities at the zeroes of

, which is obtained locally by pulling back the horizontal measured foliations (resp. vertical measured foliation) of

under the change of coordinate

defined above. The transverse measure for the horizontal measured foliation (resp. vertical measured foliation) is given by
|\mathfrak{I}\sqrt{q}|
(resp.
|\mathfrak{R}\sqrt{q}|
).

If the measured foliation
\mathsf{F}
is realised by a holomorphic quadratic differential

then
\mathsf{F}
has a prong of order

at the point where

has a zero of order
k
. Also, if
q
is expressed as
dw^{2}
in local coordinates then
-q
is nothing but the differential
-dw^{2}
, whose horizontal (resp. vertical) foliations are given by the vertical lines (resp. horizontal lines) on
\mathbb{C}
. We thus have the simple but important remark:

Remark 3.2
.

\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(q)
is measure equivalent to
\mathsf{ver}_{[c]}(-q)
in
\mathcal{MF}(S)
for all holomorphic quadratic differential

.

Now we recall a well-known theorem of Teichmüller that enables us to interpret quasi-conformal deformations in terms of measured foliations (see for example
[

]
):

Theorem 3.3

Given two conformal classes

and

to
[c^{\prime}]

is of the form

for some unique holomorphic quadratic differential

. The quadratic differential

is denoted as the initial quadratic differential of the map. There is a quadratic differential

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=\sqrt{k}x,y^{\prime}=(1/\sqrt{k})y

.

By virtue of the above, the metric

. Further, we also have that given

, a quadratic differential

that when
q
is generic, then the sections
q^{+\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
are real-analytic. In fact we can state:

Lemma 3.8
.

A measured foliation is arational if and only if the holomorphic quadratic differential realising it as the horizontal measured foliation over each point in
\mathcal{T}(S)
is generic. Moreover in this case the map
q^{\mathsf{F}}:\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
is smooth.

One implication is obvious as if the quadratic differential is generic then the measured foliation it realises has three prongs at each zero. The other side can be seen easily as if
F
is arational then any Whitehead equivalent measure foliation is isotopic to
F
(since by definition there are no saddle connections to collapse). We also denote the subset of arational measured foliations as
\mathcal{MF}_{0}(S)
and note that this is a dense subset of
\mathcal{MF}(S)
as well.

3.2.
Filling measured foliations

First we recall that:

Definition 3.9
.

A pair of measured foliations
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
is said to fill
S
if for any measured foliation
\mathsf{H}\in\mathcal{MF}(S)
on
S
we have,

\displaystyle i(\mathsf{H},\mathsf{F})+i(\mathsf{H},\mathsf{G})>0

.

Recall that we denote the space of equivalence classes of pairs of filling foliations as
\mathcal{FMF}(S)
. Notice that the pair
(\mathsf{hor}(q),\mathsf{ver}(q))
are transverse and can be shown to satisfy the topological property of filling up
S
, by the following Lemma
2.10
of
[
47
]
:

Lemma 3.10
.

Given a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on a Riemann surface
(S,[c])
, the pair
(\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(q),\mathsf{ver}_{[c]}(q))
fill
S
.

Given a pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
we can thus ask whether under a fixed complex structure up to equivalence, a pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
can be realized as the horizontal and vertical measured foliation of the same holomorphic quadratic differential
q
. The answers are affirmative and can be summarized as:

Theorem 3.11
.

(
[
18
,
45
]
)
A pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
of measured foliations on
S
is filling if and only
there is a complex structure
c
and a holomorphic quadratic differential
q\in(S,c)
such that
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
are respectively measure equivalent to the vertical and horizontal
foliations of
q
. Moreover, the class
[c]
up to diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity is determined uniquely
and for each
c\in[c]
the quadratic differential
q
realising the filling pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
is also unique.

So, for a pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
that fill we have:

Corollary 3.12
.

The sections
q^{\mathsf{F}}
and
q^{-\mathsf{G}}
intersect uniquely in
T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
at the point
([c],q)
determined by Theorem
3.11
. Moreover
\mathsf{F}=\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(q)
and
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(-q)
in
\mathcal{MF}(S)
where
q=q^{\mathsf{F}}=q^{-\mathsf{G}}=-q^{\mathsf{G}}
.

3.3.
Extremal lengths of measured foliations

Given a simple closed curve
\gamma
on
(S,c)
we define its extremal length as

\displaystyle\mathsf{ext}_{c}(\gamma)=\sup_{[[g]]}\frac{l^{2}_{g}(\gamma)}{\text{Area}(g)}

where
l_{g}(\gamma)
is the length computed with respect to
g
and the supremum is taken over all Riemannian metrics in the conformal class
c
. This definition of extremal length on closed curve extends to that of a measured foliation (see
[
24
]
) where extremal length of a foliation
\mathsf{F}
defines a continuous function on
\mathcal{T}(S)

\displaystyle\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F}):\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle[c]\mapsto\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})

where
\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(t\mathsf{F})=t^{2}\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})
for
t>0
. Using the sections
q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c]}
we can also express this as (see
[
22
]
):

Lemma 3.13
.

For
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
, the extremal length of
\mathsf{F}\in\mathcal{MF}(S)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})=\int_{(S,c)}|q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c]}|dz\wedge d\bar{z}=||q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c]}||_{1}=||q^{-\mathsf{F}}_{[c]}||_{1}

Here
||q||_{1}=\int_{S}|\phi(z)|dz\wedge d\bar{z}
where
q=\phi(z)dz^{2}
. Another simple observation that follows from this is:

Corollary 3.14
.

\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})=\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{G})
where
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is determined by Theorem
3.11
.

Proof.

As
q=q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c]}=q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c]}
at
[c]
where the sections
q^{\mathsf{F}}
and
q^{-\mathsf{G}}
intersect (see Remark
3.12
), we get the result using Lemma
3.13
. ∎

We also have a well-known variational formula for extremal lengths originally due to Gardiner (see
[
17
]
) which states:

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
[c_{t}]
for
0\leq t<\epsilon
be a smooth
1
-parameter family of conformal classes and
\mathsf{F}
be a smooth measured foliation in
\mathcal{MF}(S)
then

\displaystyle(d\mathsf{ext}_{[c_{0}]}\mathsf{F})(\mu)=\mathfrak{R}\left\langle q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{0}]},\mu\right\rangle

where
\mu\in T_{[c_{0}]}\mathcal{T}(S)
is the Beltrami differential denoting the derivative
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}[c_{t}]
.

Remark 3.16
.

It is not known if the extremal length function is
C^{2}
in general. See
[
37
,
31
,
38
]
for details on this regularity.That the Hubbard-Masur map may not be
C^{1}
can be now seen from the above formula.

3.4.
Intersection of
q^{\mathsf{F}}
and
q^{-\mathsf{G}}
in
T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)

Using the tools developed thus far we can state:

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
be a pair of measured foliations that fill
S
and
q^{\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{G}}:\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
be the associated sections defined before. Then their images in
T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
intersect uniquely and the projection of the intersection into
\mathcal{T}(S)
is the unique critical point of the function
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G}):\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Moreover when
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)
then the sections intersect transversely.

Proof.

Given,
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
, the sections
q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{0}]},q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]}
intersect if and only if
q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{0}]}=q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]}=-q^{\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]}
from Remark
3.7
. If
\mu
be the Beltrami differential denoting
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}[c_{t}]
, then we have from Lemma
3.15
that:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{ext}_{[c_{t}]}(\mathsf{F})=\mathfrak{R}(\langle q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{0}]},\mu\rangle)=\mathfrak{R}(\langle q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]},\mu\rangle),\mu\in T_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S).

We can also consider
\mathsf{G}
to be a measured foliation realised as the horizontal measured foliation and consider

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{ext}_{[c_{t}]}(\mathsf{G})=\mathfrak{R}(\langle q^{\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]},\mu\rangle),\mu\in T_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S).

Hence
[c_{0}]
is a critical point of the function
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
if and only if
q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{0}]}=-q^{\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{0}]}
. The existence and uniqueness of the critical point follows from Theorem
3.11
and Remark
3.12
.

Now assume
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)
. If
dq^{\mathsf{F}},dq^{-\mathsf{G}}:T\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow TT^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
be the respective differentials then for transversality of intersection we need to show that if
dq^{\mathsf{F}}(\nu)=dq^{-\mathsf{G}}(\nu)
for
\nu\in T\mathcal{T}(S)
then
\nu=0
. Recalling the definitions of the sections
q^{\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{G}}
this amounts to showing:

Lemma 3.18
.

Consider a deformation of the type
(c_{t},q_{t}),t\geq 0
with
(c_{0},q_{0})
being the point of intersection of
q^{\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{G}}
with
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)
. Let
\mathsf{F}_{t},\mathsf{G}_{t}
be the horizontal and vertical measured foliations realised by
(c_{t},q_{t})
and assume
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{F}_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{G}_{t}=0
. Then the deformation is trivial.

Proof.

Recall that
q^{\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{G}}:\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
is a smooth map when
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
are arational. For this, we consider
\pi:\widehat{S}\rightarrow S
to be the canonical double cover branched over the zeroes of
q
(see
[
29
]
, Construction
1.2
and §
5.4
of
[
14
]
) such that
\pi^{*}(q)=\omega_{q}^{2}
where
\omega_{q}
is a holomorphic
1
form on
(\widehat{S},[\widehat{c}])
where
[\widehat{c}]=\pi^{*}[c]
. It follows from Lemma
2
of
[
29
]
that
([c],q)\mapsto([\widehat{c}],\omega_{q}^{2})
is a local embedding, so a deformation
([c_{t}],q_{t})
in the generic stratum induces a deformation
([\widehat{c_{t}}],\omega_{q_{t}}^{2})\in Q(\widehat{S})
maintaining the same strata. Let
\widehat{\mathsf{F}_{t}}
nd
\widehat{\mathsf{G}_{t}}
be the horizontal and vertical foliations realised by
\omega_{q_{t}}^{2}
on
(\widehat{S},[\widehat{c_{t}}])
with
\widehat{\mathsf{F}_{0}}=\widehat{\mathsf{F}}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathsf{G}_{0}}=\widehat{\mathsf{G}}
) being the lift of
\mathsf{F}
(resp.
\mathsf{G}
) in the double cover. Consider now
\gamma
to be a cycle in the relative homology group
H^{-}_{1}(\widehat{S},V_{\omega_{q}};\mathbb{C})
where the latter is the eigenspace of
H_{1}(\widehat{S},V_{\omega_{q}};\mathbb{C})
consisting of cycles invariant under the involution of
\widehat{S}
and the set
V_{\omega_{q}}
denotes the set of zeroes of
\omega_{q}
. The real and imaginary part of the holonomy
\int_{\gamma}\omega_{q}
are precisely the intersection number with the horizontal and vertical foliations of
\omega_{q}^{2}
. This gives us
Period coordinates

\displaystyle\mathsf{per}

\displaystyle:Q_{0}(S)\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{-}(\widehat{S},V_{\omega_{q}};\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{R}^{12g-12}

\displaystyle q\mapsto(\gamma\rightarrow\int_{\gamma}\omega_{q})

which is an immersion. Our assumption then translates to
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}i(\gamma,\widehat{\mathsf{F}_{t}})=0
and
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}i(\gamma,\widehat{\mathsf{G}_{t}})=0
. This gives
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\mathsf{per}(q_{t})(\gamma)=\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}i(\gamma,\widehat{\mathsf{G}_{t}})+\textbf{i}i(\gamma,\widehat{\mathsf{F}_{t}})=0
, where we can assume
\gamma
is fixed when one restricts to deformations maintaining strata. Since the period map is an immersion, it follows that this deformation is necessarily trivial. ∎

Define now :

Definition 3.19
.

For a pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
that fill
S
, we denote
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
to be the critical point in
\mathcal{T}(S)
of the function
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G}):\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

It is a simple observation from the definition that if the transverse measure of a foliation
\mathsf{F}
is given by
|\mathfrak{R}\sqrt{q^{\mathsf{F}}}|
, then the corresponding holomorphic quadratic differential realising the measured foliation
t\mathsf{F}
over the same Riemann surface structure on
S
is nothing but
t^{2}q^{\mathsf{F}}
since then the transverse measure is given by
|\mathfrak{R}\sqrt{t^{2}q^{\mathsf{F}}}|
which is equal to
t|\mathfrak{R}\sqrt{q^{\mathsf{F}}}|
. In the notation of the critical point
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
this implies that:

Lemma 3.20
.

If
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
fill
S
, then
\mathsf{p}(t\mathsf{F},t\mathsf{G})=\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
where
t>0
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathsf{ext}(t\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(t\mathsf{G})=t^{2}(\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G}))
from the definition of extremal length function and hence
\mathsf{ext}(t\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(t\mathsf{G})
and
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
have the same critical points. ∎

Also we have the observation that this point is uniquely determined by the second coordinate. That is:

Lemma 3.21
.

If
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})=\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F^{\prime}},\mathsf{G})
, then
\mathsf{F}=\mathsf{F^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{MF}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})=[c]
be the unique point in
\mathcal{T}(S)
and
q\in Q(S,c)
be the unique holomorphic quadratic differential realising
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
as its horizontal and vertical measured foliations respectively. For the pair
(\mathsf{F}^{\prime},\mathsf{G})
we have that
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{F}^{\prime},\mathsf{G})=p(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})=[c]
. Since on
Q(S,c)
the choice of
q^{\prime}
realising
\mathsf{G}
as its vertical measured foliation is unique from the theorem of Hubbard-Masur, we have that
q^{\prime}=q
. But by definition
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
is measure equivalent to
\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(q^{\prime})=\mathsf{hor}_{[c]}(q)=\mathsf{F}
.∎

3.5.
Quotient of
Q(S)
under the action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and intersection of
[q^{\mathsf{F}}]
and
[q^{-\mathsf{G}}]

There is a natural action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
on
(Q(S,[c])-{0})
which sends every non-zero
q\in Q(S,[c])
to
t^{2}q
,
\forall t\in(0,\infty)
. We can thus define
Q^{1}(S,c)
to be quotient
(Q(S,[c])-{0})/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
under this action. Clearly
Q^{1}(S,c)
is isomorphic to
UT^{*}_{[c]}\mathcal{T}(S)
from Proposition
2.6
, where the latter denotes the unit cotangent space at a point
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
. The next proposition is a similar result for the sections
[q^{\mathsf{F}}]
, which are the images of
q^{\mathsf{F}}
under the quotient map. We can now address the main proposition of this section involving the intersection of the equivalence classes
[q^{\mathsf{F}}]
and
[q^{-\mathsf{G}}]
in
UT^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
for a filling pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
:

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
be a pair filling measured foliations on
S
, then the projection of the intersection of the sections
[q^{\mathsf{F}}]
,
[q^{-\mathsf{G}}]
in
UT^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
onto
\mathcal{T}(S)
is a geodesic line for the Teichmüller metric given by
t\mapsto\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{T}(S)
for
t>0
. Moreover, when
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)
then the sections intersect transversely in
UT^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
.

Proof.

We first note that if a pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
fill
S
then so do the pairs
(t\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
,
(t\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{t}\mathsf{G})
and
(\mathsf{F},t\mathsf{G})
for any
t>0
.

Let
[c_{t}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
be an equivalence class of complex structures such that the two sections
q^{t\mathsf{F}},q^{-\mathsf{G}}
meet over
[c_{t}]
. Then by definition we have
t^{2}q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{t}]}=q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{t}]}
which is equivalent to
tq^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{t}]}=\frac{1}{t}q^{-\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{t}]}
. Since the foliation
t\mathsf{F}
is realised by
t^{2}q^{\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{t}]}
on the same complex structure, we have that
q^{\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F}}_{[c_{t}]}=q^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G}}_{[c_{t}]}
for some
t>0
at the point
[c_{t}]
.

This is equivalent to the fact that
[c_{t}]
is the unique critical point of the function
\mathsf{ext}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
since
(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
fill
S
. As
[c_{t}]
is identified with
\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
, the projection of the intersections is along

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(S)

\displaystyle t\mapsto[c_{t}]=\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})

So it now suffices to show that the path
t\rightarrow\mathsf{p}(t\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
is a geodesic for the Teichmüller metric
d_{\mathcal{T}(S)}
on
\mathcal{T}(S)
. We first note that
[c]
being the critical point
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
for a filling pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
also implies that
[c]
is the critical point for the function

\displaystyle\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G}):\mathcal{T}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

where we use the fact that
\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})=\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{G})
from corollary
3.14
. So
\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
is a critical point for
\mathsf{ext}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F})\mathsf{ext}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
and since
\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(t\mathsf{F})=t^{2}\mathsf{ext}_{[c]}(\mathsf{F})
we also have as a consequence that the point
\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})
is a critical point for the function
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
. Now it has been shown in
[
18
]
that the set of critical points for the function
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
is a Teichmüller geodesic line in
\mathcal{T}(S)
when
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
fill
S
. Moreover, from Lemma
3.21
the map
t\mapsto\mathsf{p}(t\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is injective. Finally, we observe that every critical point of
\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{F})\mathsf{ext}(\mathsf{G})
is also a critical point for
\mathsf{ext}(\alpha\mathsf{F})+\mathsf{ext}(\beta\mathsf{G})
for some
\alpha,\beta>0
and hence the the image of the map
t\rightarrow\mathsf{p}(t\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
is the entire Teichmüller geodesic.

For transversality we can use Proposition
3.17
as the pairs
q^{t\mathsf{F}}
and
q^{-\mathsf{G}}
intersect transversely, i.e,

\displaystyle T_{([c_{t}],q_{t})}T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)=T_{([c_{t}],q_{t})}(q^{t\mathsf{F}}(\mathcal{T}(S)))\bigoplus T_{([c_{t}],q_{t})}(q^{-\mathsf{G}}(\mathcal{T}(S)))

is true for all
t\geq 0
and
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}_{0}(S)
. The result follows when we take quotient.∎

For a given pair
(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
we call
t\mapsto\mathsf{p}(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{F},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathsf{G})\in\mathcal{T}(S)
for
t>0
as
\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{F},\mathsf{G})
.

4.
Necessary condition for paths with small filling measured foliations at infinity

The goal of this section is to establish a necessary conditions that small differentiable paths in
\mathcal{QF}(S)
starting from
\mathcal{F}(S)
should satisfy if the measured foliations at infinity are given by a filling pair
(t\mathsf{F_{+}},t\mathsf{F_{-}})\in\mathcal{FMF}(S)
at first order at

. For this reason following
[

, we will study the curve
\beta_{([c],q)}(t^{2})\in\mathcal{QF}(S)

small enough, which is parametrised by the data of the unique minimal surface it contains, i.e, the first fundamental form

is in the conformal class

and the second fundamental form

is given by

at first-order at

then

is indeed the unique critical point for the functions

and

is the unique holomorphic differential we obtain from the theorem of Gardiner-Masur that realise

on

.

4.1.
Fundamental forms at infinity

Given a minimal surface in an almost-Fuchsian manifold
M
, we can consider the surfaces equidistant from it in
M
at an oriented distance. These surfaces foliate the almost-Fuchsian manifold and we can then compute the associated first and second fundamental forms for these surfaces in terms of the data associated to the minimal embedding. We thus can formulate the following
[
26
]
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be a complete, oriented, smooth surface with principal curvatures in
(-1,1)
immersed minimally into an almost-Fuchsian manifold homeomorphic to
S\times(-\infty,\infty)
and let
(I,I\hskip-2.84544ptI,B)
be the associated data of the immersion. Then
\forall r\in\mathbb{R}
the set of point
S_{r}
at an oriented distance
r
from
S
is a smooth embedded surface with data
(I_{r},I\hskip-2.84544ptI_{r},B_{r})
where :

(1)

I_{r}(x,y)=I((cosh(r)E+sinh(r)B)x,(cosh(r)E+sinh(r)B)y)

(2)

I\hskip-2.84544ptI_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{dI_{r}}{dr}

(3)

B_{r}=(cosh(r)E+sinh(r)B)^{-1}(sinh(r)E+cosh(r)B)

where
E
is the identity operator and
S_{r}
is identified to
S
through the closest point projection.

The fundamental forms at infinity denoted as
I^{*},I\hskip-2.84544ptI^{*}
and introduced in
[
26
]
, quantify the asymptotic behaviour of the quantities described above as

. In particular, it estimates the data at the conformal class at infinity of an almost-Fuchsian manifold
M
with respect to the, unique minimal surface with principal curvature in
(-1,1)
, it contains.

Formally,

\displaystyle I^{*}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}2e^{-2r}I_{r}\hskip 28.45274ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI^{*}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}(I_{r}-I\hskip-2.84544ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI_{r})

. However the lemma above gives us explicit formulae to express the same in terms of
(I,I\hskip-2.84544ptI,I\hskip-2.84544ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI)
and we use that to define:

Definition 4.2

J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
of
\mathcal{QF}(S)
coming from the character variety and the complex structure

on
\mathcal{T}(S)

It is also clear that
\mathcal{F}(S)
is the pre-image of the diagonal. If

is the tangent vector to the path

of quasi-Fuchsian metrics for

at

then the derivative of the Bers map at a point

d_{[g_{0}]}B(v):=(q_{1},q_{2})

where

are two holomorphic quadratic differentials in

denoting tangent vectors to
\mathcal{T}(S)
associated to the variation of the complex structures at two ends at infinity corresponding to the vector

. We thus have that
d_{[g_{0}]}B(J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}v)=(J_{WP}(q_{1}),-J_{WP}(q_{2}))=(\textit{{i}}q_{1},-\textit{{i}}q_{2})
where
\textit{{i}}^{2}=-1
and the minus sign in the second factor is simply due to the opposite orientation of

.

As
\mathcal{F}(S)
is identified with
\mathcal{T}(S)
by considering the unique hyperbolic metric

in each conformal class
[c]
, when we consider a deformation of hyperbolic structures on

\mathfrak{R}(q)
for some

(see

). If one considers the variation of the hyperbolic metrics in the conformal classes associated to the two ends at infinity, then
d_{[c]}B(J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}v)=(\mathfrak{R}(\textit{{i}}q_{1}),\mathfrak{R}(-\textit{{i}}q_{2}))\in T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)\times T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
. We can thus define:

Definition 5.3
.

The normal bundle
N\mathcal{F}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(S)
is the bundle whose fiber
N_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
over each conformal class
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is the vector space isomorphic to the quotient
T_{[c]}\mathcal{QF}(S)/T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
.

The fibers
N_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
are
J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
where
J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
is the almost complex structure of
\mathcal{QF}(S)
. So
N_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
is the set of tangent vectors
v_{([c],q)}
such that
dB(J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}v_{([c],q)})=(\mathfrak{R}(\textit{{i}}q),\mathfrak{R}(\textit{{i}}q))\in T\mathcal{T}(S)\times T\mathcal{T}(S)
for
q\in Q(S,c)
.
Now, let
v_{([c],q)}
be the vector tangent to the path
\beta_{([c],q)}:[0,\epsilon)\rightarrow\mathcal{QF}(S)
at
\mathcal{F}(S)
. That is to say:

\displaystyle v_{([c],q)}=\frac{d}{ds}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=0\end{subarray}}\beta_{([c],q)}(s)\in T_{[c]}\mathcal{QF}(S).

So we formulate:

Proposition 5.4
.

v_{([c],q)}
is an element of
N_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
.

Proof.

As the first fundamental form at infinity
I^{*}_{s}
remains hyperbolic at first order at

it follows from Lemma
4.9
that

\displaystyle dB(v_{([c],q)})=(\mathfrak{R}(q),-\mathfrak{R}(q)).

Then,

\displaystyle dB(J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}v_{([c],q)})=(\mathfrak{R}(\textit{{i}}q),\mathfrak{R}(\textit{{i}}q)).

So
J_{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}v_{([c],q)}\in T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
, i.e, tangent to the Fuchsian locus. The decomposition of the tangent space
T_{[c]}\mathcal{QF}(S)=T_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)\bigoplus JT_{[c]}\mathcal{F}(S)
at the Fuchsian locus then implies that

.∎

Abstract
. In [ GGP1 ] , we considered a family of restriction problems for classical groups (over local and global fields) and proposed precise answers to these problems using the local and global Langlands correspondence. These restriction problems were formulated in terms of a pair W ⊂ V W\subset V of orthogonal, Hermitian, symplectic, or skew-Hermitian spaces. In this paper, we consider a twisted variant of these conjectures in one particular case – that of a pair of skew-Hermitian spaces W = V W=V .

1.
Introduction

In
[
GGP1
]
, we considered a family of restriction problems for classical groups (over local and global fields) and proposed precise answers to these problems using the local and global Langlands correspondence.
These restriction problems were formulated in terms of a pair
W\subset V

of orthogonal, Hermitian, symplectic, or skew-Hermitian spaces.
In this paper, we consider a twisted variant of these conjectures in one particular case – that of a pair of skew-Hermitian spaces
W=V
.

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field and let
E
be a separable quadratic algebra over
F
with
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, the nontrivial element of the Galois group. Let
V
be a non-degenerate skew-Hermitian space of dimension
n
over
E
, with pairing
\langle v,w\rangle
. We may choose an orthogonal basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
of
V
over
E
and define the determinant

\det(V)=\prod_{i}\langle v_{i},v_{i}\rangle,

in which, each term
\langle v_{i},v_{i}\rangle
lies in
E_{0}^{\times}
, where
E_{0}
is the
F
-subspace of
E

consisting of elements of trace
0
and
E_{0}^{\times}=E_{0}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. Since the product of two elements in
E_{0}^{\times}
lies in
F^{\times}
, the determinant lies in
E_{0}^{\times}
when
n
is odd and in
F^{\times}
when
n
is even. Both
E_{0}^{\times}
and
F^{\times}

are principal homogeneous spaces for the group
F^{\times}
,
and the orbit spaces
E_{0}^{\times}/NE^{\times}
and
F^{\times}/NE^{\times}

have cardinality
2
if
E
is a field, and have cardinality 1 otherwise. The determinant, as an element of one of these orbit spaces

E_{0}^{\times}/NE^{\times}
or
F^{\times}/NE^{\times}
,
is independent of the choice of an orthogonal basis, and gives a complete isomorphism invariant of the skew-Hermitian space
V
over
E
.

The isometry group
{\rm U}(V)
has associated to it the Weil representation
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}

(see
[
GGP1
, Pg. 47-50]
). If
E
is a field, then
this complex representation of
{\rm U}(V)
depends on a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
F
and a conjugate-symplectic character
\mu
of
E^{\times}
(i.e., the restriction of
\mu
to
F^{\times}
is the quadratic character
\omega_{E/F}
associated to
E/F
by the local class field theory, so that
\omega_{E/F}:F^{\times}/N(E^{\times})\cong\{\pm 1\}
). For an irreducible representation
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
of
{\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(V)
with a generic L-parameter,
we had considered the problem of determining

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}),

in
[
GGP1
]
.
It is known by the work
[
S
]
that this dimension is
0
or
1
, and the conjecture in
[
GGP1
]
(proved in
[
GI
]
) determines precisely when this dimension is equal to
1
.

If
E=F\times F
,
{\rm U}(V)\cong{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
, the Weil representation
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
could be taken to be
{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
with the natural action of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
on it,
and the resulting Hom space
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2},{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n}))
is the one which intervenes in the local Rankin-Selberg integral for
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)\times{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
.

Here is the simplest twisted variant of the above question that we consider in this paper.
Instead of considering
{\rm U}(V)
as a subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)(F\times F)={\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(V)
, we consider it as a subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)(E)\cong{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
. For an irreducible generic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
, we consider the problem of determining

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}).

We conjecture that this dimension is equal to
1
for a unique (up to isomorphism) skew-Hermitian space
V
of dimension
n
over
E
, whose determinant is related to a local epsilon factor that we describe now.

Let
M
be the Langlands parameter of
\Pi
, thus
M
is an
n
-dimensional representation of the Weil-Deligne group
WD_{E}
of
E
. Associated to
M
, let

{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}
be the conjugate-dual representation of
WD_{E}
, so that
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}
is a conjugate-orthogonal representation of
WD_{E}
of dimension
n^{2}
. Since
\mu|_{F^{\times}}=\omega_{E/F}
,
\mu

is a conjugate-symplectic character of
E^{\times}
,
and hence
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1}
is a conjugate-symplectic representation of
WD_{E}
.
In this paper, we conjecture that the skew-Hermitian space
V
for which
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\not=0

is determined by the identity

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M)(-1)^{n}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2},

where
\psi_{E}
is the additive character of
E
obtained by composing
\psi
with the trace
from
E
to
F
. For the other skew-Hermitian
space

V^{\prime}
of rank
n
over
E
, we conjecture that:

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V^{\prime})}(\Pi,\omega_{V^{\prime},\psi,\mu})=0.

We note that when
n
is even so that
\det(V)\in F^{\times}
,
\mu(\det(V))=\omega_{E/F}(\det(V))=\pm 1
,
\mu(\det(V))=+1
if and only if the group
{\rm U}(V)
is quasi-split. When
n
is odd, the group
{\rm U}(V)
is quasi-split for both of the skew-Hermitian spaces, and
\mu(\det(V))
is a square root of
\omega_{E/F}(-1)
. Likewise, the local root number

\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

is equal to
\pm 1
when
n
is even and is a square root of
\omega_{E/F}(-1)
if
n
is odd.

A related problem that has been studied in the literature is the determination of

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\mathbb{C}).

The third author has proposed precise conjectures about this dimension
[
P2
]
.
Here, we have replaced the trivial representation of
{\rm U}(V)
by a Weil representation, which lies in a one-parameter family (indexed by the characters of
E^{1}
) of the next smallest representations of
{\rm U}(V)
. In retrospect, this appears quite natural and is simpler than this related problem considered in
[
P2
]
. It is also simpler than our original conjecture in the skew-Hermitian case, where we considered
{\rm U}(V)
as a subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)(F\times F)={\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(V)
, whereas

{\rm U}(V)(E)={\rm GL}_{n}(E)
, a simpler group, in particular, the
L
-packets for
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
are singletons.
Note also that for
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})
,
we consider
\epsilon
and
L
-function at
1/2
of
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1}

whereas for
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\mathbb{C})
, one considers the pole at
s=1
of
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}
.

The astute reader can no doubt guess by now the general twisted variant of the GGP conjecture we have in mind.

{\rm U}(V)
as a subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)(E)
and
{\rm U}(V)(F\times F)

We will also consider the twisted period problems over global fields. As in the GGP conjectures, one expects that the nonvanishing of the global period integral here too is equivalent to the nonvanishing of a corresponding central L-value, in the absence of local obstructions. For example, when

E=K

E\neq K
, all possible local scenarios given in the above table will arise. Hence, one of our goals in this paper is to give a uniform formulation of the local conjectures which can be specialized to all the local scenarios in the table.

2.
When
E=K
is a field

In this section, we consider the simpler case
E=K
, which was briefly discussed in the introduction. We shall formulate our conjectures more formally here, in both the local and global setting.

2.1.
Local case.

We assume first that
F
is a local field and
E/F
is a separable quadratic field extension. We will let
E_{0}
denote the
F
-subspace of trace
0
elements in
E
and let
E_{1}\subset E^{\times}
denote the subgroup of norm 1 elements. Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
F
and let
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
be the nontrivial automorphism of
E/F
.

For a skew-Hermitian space
V
over
E
of dimension
n
, we recall that

\det(V)\in\begin{cases}F^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times}),\text{ if $n$ is even;}\\
E_{0}^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times}),\text{ if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

If
F
is nonarchimedean, there are precisely two skew-Hermitian spaces of dimension
n
, distinguished by their determinants. When
F
is archimedean, there are many more skew-Hermitian spaces, distinguished by their signatures.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that all these skew-Hermitian
spaces (of a given dimension) have the same underlying vector
space
V
over
E
, equipped with non-isomorphic skew-Hermitian
forms. Thus the unitary groups

{\rm U}(V)\subset{\rm GL}(V)={\rm Aut}_{E}(V)={\rm GL}_{n}(E)

are all subgroups of a fixed ambient group
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
.

For each skew-Hermitian space
V
over
E
and a conjugate-symplectic character
\mu
of
E^{\times}
, we have the associated Weil representation
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
of
{\rm U}(V)
.
Now for an irreducible representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}(V)\cong{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
, we consider the Hom space

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}).

Here is our main local conjecture in this case.

Conjecture 2.1
.

(i) For any
\Pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm GL}(V))
,

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\leq 1.

(ii) If
\Pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm GL}(V))
is generic, then

\sum_{V}\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})=1.

where the sum is over the equivalence classes of skew-Hermitian structures on
V
.

(iii) For generic
\Pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm GL}(V))
, the unique skew-Hermitian space
V
which gives a nonzero contribution to the above sum satisfies:

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\Pi}(-1)^{n}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2},

where
{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}
is the conjugate-dual representation of
\Pi
and
\omega_{\Pi}
is the central character of
\Pi
.

As noted in the introduction, the ratio of the two sides of (iii) is a priori
\pm 1
.
When
F
is nonarchimedean, the condition (iii) in the conjecture uniquely determines the summand with nonzero contribution to the sum in (ii).
When
F=\mathbb{R}
and
E=\mathbb{C}
, one needs to be more specific about the
V
which gives nonzero contribution. We shall consider this archimedean case in greater detail in §
6
. Note that if we define the discriminant of
V
by

{\rm disc}(V)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\cdot\det(V),

then the formula in (iii) can be
expressed more succinctly as

\mu({\rm disc}(V))=\epsilon(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\Pi}(-1)^{n},

taking note of the fact that
\mu(-1)=\omega_{E/F}(-1)
.
We shall provide some evidence for this conjecture in the next two sections, verifying it for
\dim V\leq 2
and for unitary principal series representations of
{\rm GL}(V)
for
V
of arbitrary dimension over
E
.

In the above formulation, the conjecture does not require the local Langlands correspondence, as the local root number in (iii) can be interpreted as the Rankin-Selberg local root number defined by Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shalika
[
JPSS
]
.

Let
M
denote the Langlands parameter of
\Pi
, so that
M
is an
n
-dimensional representation of the Weil-Deligne group
WD_{E}
of
E
with
\det(M)
corresponding to the central character
\omega_{\Pi}
under the local class field theory. We have noted in the introduction that
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1}
is a conjugate-symplectic representation of
WD_{E}
.
Then Conjecture
2.1
(iii)
can be written as:

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M)(-1)^{n}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}.

Note that, for
e\in E_{0}^{\times}
,

\det(M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee})(e)=\det(M)(e)^{n}/\det(M)(e^{\sigma})^{n}=\det(M)(-1)^{n},

and

\omega_{E/F}(-1)=\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})=(e^{2},e^{2})\quad\text{(Hilbert symbol)}.

Hence the above identity can be expressed as (for
E=K
),

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee})(e)\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})^{n(n-1)/2},

and it is this last statement that generalizes well when we deal with the general case
(where
E\neq K
) later.

2.2.
Global case

Consider now the case when
E/F
is a quadratic extension of global fields with adele rings
\mathbb{A}_{E}
and
\mathbb{A}_{F}
. Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}
.
We shall consider all skew-Hermitian structures on a vector space
V
of dimension
n
over
E
.

Let
\Pi\cong\otimes_{v}\Pi_{v}
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{F})={\rm GL}(V\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{F})={\rm GL}(V\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E})
, thus
\Pi_{v}

are, in particular, generic representations for each place
v
of
E
. For a conjugate-symplectic Hecke character
\mu
of
\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
, we may consider the automorphic Weil
representation
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
of
{\rm U}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
(see
[
GGP1
]
). Now we consider the global period integral

\mathcal{P}_{V}:\Pi\otimes\overline{\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

defined by

\mathcal{P}_{V}(f,\phi)=\int_{[{\rm U}(V)]}f(g)\cdot\overline{\phi(g)}\,dg\quad\text{for $f\in\Pi$ and $\phi\in\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}$,}

where we have written
[{\rm U}(V)]
for the adelic quotient
{\rm U}(V)(F)\backslash{\rm U}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with
dg

the Tamagawa measure on it.

Globally, we are interested in characterizing the nonvanishing of this period integral. Our global conjecture is
the following.

Conjecture 2.2
.

In the above setting, in particular for
V
a
skew-Hermitian space over a global field
E
,
the global period integral
\mathcal{P}_{V}

is nonzero if and only if the following two conditions hold (denoting
V_{v}=V\otimes F_{v}
):

(a)

For all places
v
of
F
,
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})}(\Pi_{v},\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}})\neq 0
.

(b)

L(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})\neq 0.

Further, for a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
,
if
L(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})\neq 0
, then there exists
a unique skew-Hermitian space
V
of dimension
n
over
E

such that the global period integral
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is nonzero.

Observe that if we are given a collection of local skew-Hermitian spaces
\{V_{v}\}
for all places
v
of
F

(of a fixed dimension
n\geq 1
)
then
the adelic skew-Hermitian space
\otimes_{v}V_{v}
is coherent over
F
, i.e. the family of local
skew-Hermitian spaces
V_{v}
comes from a global skew-Hermitian space
V
, if and only if

\prod_{v}\mu_{v}\left(\det(V_{v})\right)=1.

Therefore, given a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
,
if the local skew-Hermitian spaces
\{V_{v}\}
are the ones for which

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})}(\Pi_{v},\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}})\neq 0
for all places
v
of
F
, then
part (iii) of our local Conjecture
2.1
implies that this collection
of local skew-Hermitian spaces
\{V_{v}\}
is coherent over
F
if and only if

\epsilon(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})=1.

Therefore, given a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
for which the
global period integral on
[U(V)]
is nonzero (hence

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})}(\Pi_{v},\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}})\neq 0
for all places
v
of
F
), then

\epsilon(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})=1
.
Thus a necessary condition for the nonvanishing of
L(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})

is satisfied if the global period integral on
[U(V)]
is nonzero. Conversely,
given a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
for which
L(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})\not=0
, hence
\epsilon(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})=1
, we have a global
skew-Hermitian space
V
, unique up to isomorphism, for which Conjecture
2.2
implies
nonvanishing of period integral on
[{\rm U}(V)]
.

2.3.
A refined global conjecture

Not surprisingly, one expects to be able to refine the above global conjecture to a precise formula relating the the global period integral to the central L-value.

For
\Pi\cong\otimes_{v}\Pi_{v}
,
a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{F})={\rm GL}(V\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{F})={\rm GL}(V\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E})
, and

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}\cong\otimes_{v}\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}}
, the Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
,
f_{v},f^{\prime}_{v}\in\Pi_{v}
and
\phi_{v},\phi^{\prime}_{v}\in\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}}
,
we may consider the following integral of matrix coefficients for each place
v
of
F
:

(2.3)

\mathcal{I}_{v}(f_{v},f^{\prime}_{v},\phi_{v},\phi^{\prime}_{v}):=\int_{{\rm U}(V)(F_{v})}\langle g_{v}\cdot f_{v},f^{\prime}_{v}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle g_{v}\cdot\phi_{v},\phi^{\prime}_{v}\rangle}\,\,dg_{v}.

As in
[
X2
]
, it is not hard to see that if
\Pi_{v}
is tempered, this integral is absolutely convergent, so that it defines a
{\rm U}(V_{v})\times{\rm U}(V_{v})
-equivariant linear functional

\mathcal{I}_{v}:\Pi_{v}\otimes\overline{\Pi}_{v}\otimes\overline{\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}}}\otimes\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}.

Now one would like to:

•

show that
\mathcal{I}_{v}
is nonzero if and only if
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)(F_{v})}(\Pi_{v},\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}})\neq 0
;

•

compute this integral at almost all places
v
of
F
where every data involved is unramified.

Without having done this work, we may nonetheless venture a guess here, in analogy with the original GGP case
[
X2
]
.

Conjecture 2.4
.

Suppose that

•

E_{v}/F_{v}
is an unramified quadratic extension of residue characteristic not
2
and
\psi_{v}
has conductor
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}
;

•

\mu_{v}
is unramified;

•

V_{v}
contains a self-dual lattice
\Lambda_{v}
whose stabilizer in
{\rm U}(V_{v})
is a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup
K_{v}
, contained in
\tilde{K}_{v}={\rm GL}(\Lambda_{v})\subset{\rm GL}(V_{v})
;

•

dg_{v}
is the Haar measure on
{\rm U}(V_{v})
which gives
K_{v}
volume
1
;

•

\Pi_{v}
is
\tilde{K}_{v}
-unramified and
f_{v}=f^{\prime}_{v}
is a
\tilde{K}_{v}
-spherical vector of norm 1;

•

\phi_{v}=\phi^{\prime}_{v}
is a
K_{v}
-spherical vector of norm 1
in the Weil representation
\omega_{V_{v},\psi_{v},\mu_{v}}
.

Then

\mathcal{I}_{v}(f_{v},f^{\prime}_{v},\phi_{v},\phi^{\prime}_{v})=\frac{L(1,M_{{\rm GL}(V_{v})}^{\vee})}{L(1,M^{\vee}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})})}\cdot\frac{L(1/2,\Pi_{v}\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}_{v}\times\mu_{v}^{-1})}{L(1,\Pi_{v},{\rm Ad})},

where

L(1,M_{{\rm GL}(V_{v})}^{\vee})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\zeta_{E_{v}}(k)\quad\text{and}\quad L(1,M^{\vee}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}L(k,\omega_{E_{v}/F_{v}}^{k})

are the values at
s=1
of the L-functions of the dual motives of
{\rm GL}(V)
and
{\rm U}(V)
respectively. (One may observe that the expression for
\mathcal{I}_{v}(f_{v},f^{\prime}_{v},\phi_{v},\phi^{\prime}_{v})
given above implies, in particular,
that it is nonzero.)

Given this, it is natural to define a normalized local period integral:

(2.5)

\mathcal{I}_{v}^{\#}=\frac{L(1,M^{\vee}_{{\rm U}(V_{v})})}{L(1,M_{{\rm GL}(V_{v})}^{\vee})}\cdot\frac{L(1,\Pi_{v},{\rm Ad})}{L(1/2,\Pi_{v}\times{}^{\sigma}\!\Pi^{\vee}_{v}\times\mu_{v}^{-1})}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{v}.

We also note that if
E_{v}=F_{v}\times F_{v}
, the analog of the above conjecture holds, and has already been considered in the original
formulation of the refined GGP conjecture for skew-Hermitian spaces in
[
GGP1
]
.

Coming back to the global setting, for each of the groups
{\rm GL}(V)
or
{\rm U}(V)
, we will fix a decomposition of the Tamagawa measures
dg=\prod_{v}dg_{v}
, so that for almost all
v
, the local Haar measures
dg_{v}
give a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup volume
1
. We will also fix a decomposition of the global Petersson inner product as a product of local pairings:

(2.6)

\langle-,-\rangle_{{\rm Pet}}=\prod_{v}\langle-,-\rangle_{v},

and use these
dg_{v}
and
\langle-,-\rangle_{v}
in the definition of the local period integrals
\mathcal{I}_{v}
introduced above.
We can now state:

Conjecture 2.7
.

Given a (tempered) cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}(V)
,

\mathcal{P}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{P}}=\frac{L(1/2,\Pi\times{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1})}{L(1,M_{{\rm U}(V)}^{\vee})}\cdot\left(\frac{L(s,M_{{\rm GL}(V)}^{\vee})}{L(s,\Pi,{\rm Ad})}\right)|_{s=1}\cdot\prod_{v}\mathcal{I}_{v}^{\#}.

as linear functionals on

\Pi\otimes\overline{\Pi}\otimes\overline{\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}}\otimes\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
.

Here, note that
L(s,M_{{\rm GL}(V)}^{\vee})
and
L(s,\Pi,{\rm Ad})
both have a simple pole at
s=1
, so that their ratio is holomorphic and nonzero at
s=1
.

2.4.
Finite fields

We conclude this section by highlighting the restriction problem for skew-Hermitian spaces over a finite field
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In the finite field setting, only the case
E=K
can occur. In this setting, a naive first guess is that for any irreducible generic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
,

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\Pi,\omega)=1

where
\omega
is the Weil representation of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, restricted to the subgroup
{\rm U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. However, an examination of the case
n=1
shows that this cannot literally be the case because
\dim\omega=q
but
{\rm U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
has
q+1
characters. Indeed, the unique nontrivial quadratic character of
{\rm U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is missing from
\omega
. Moreover, experience with the usual GGP problem over finite fields shows that the above branching multiplicity can be larger than 1.
Nonetheless, the naive expectation should be generically true for cuspidal Deligne-Lusztig representations and it is an interesting question to quantify the extent of its failure.

Over finite fields, we can also consider this restriction problem for symplectic groups. For any irreducible generic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, one would thus like to determine

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\Pi,\omega).

It is curious that since the two fold cover of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(E)
splits over
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(F)
, there is no
analogous problem for non-archimedean local fields. Perhaps, one could go to four fold cover
of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(E)
(if the 4th roots of unity are there in
E
) to study the analogous branching problem?

A first study of these branching problems over finite fields has been conducted by Nhat Hoang Le.

3.
Evidence in Low Rank

In this section, we will provide some evidence towards Conjecture
2.1

when
n=\dim V\leq 2
.

3.1.
Rank one case

We begin by examining the case when
\dim V=1
, so that

{\rm GL}(V)=E^{\times}\supset{\rm U}(V)=E_{1}
, where
E_{1}
denotes the subgroup of norm one elements. Given a character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
, we are thus interested in understanding
{\rm Hom}_{E_{1}}(\chi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})
. This is addressed by a theorem of
Moen
[
Mo
]
and Rogawski
[
R
]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\chi
is a character of
E^{\times}
, then

\dim{\rm Hom}_{E_{1}}(\chi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\leq 1

and equality holds if and only if

\mu(\det(V))=\chi(-1)\cdot\epsilon(1/2,\chi^{\sigma}/\chi\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}).

This is precisely what Conjecture
2.1
asserts in the case
\dim V=1
.

3.2.
Rank two case

Suppose now that
\dim V=2
. Skew-Hermitian spaces of rank 2 can be described using quaternion
F
-algebras, as we have exploited in
[
GGP2
]
. More precisely,
for a quaternion
F
-algebra
B
, fix an
F
-algebra embedding
i:E\hookrightarrow B
and write
B=E\oplus E\cdot x
where
x
is an element of
B
such that
xex^{-1}=e^{\sigma}
. Thus
B
is a 2-dimensional
E
-vector space (by left multiplication), and we may identify
{\rm GL}_{E}(B)
with
{\rm GL}_{2}(E)
with respect to the basis
\{1,x\}
.

Now fix a trace 0 element
\delta\in E^{\times}
and set

\langle b_{1},b_{2}\rangle=\delta\cdot(\text{projection of $b_{1}\cdot\bar{b}_{2}$ onto $E$)}

Then
\langle-,-\rangle
is a skew-Hermitian form on
B
; we shall denote this skew-Hermitian space by
V_{B}
. The isomorphism class of
V_{B}
is independent of
x,\delta
, and
V_{B}
is split if and only if
B
is split.

The unitary similitude group
{\rm GU}(V_{B})\subset{\rm GL}(V_{B})={\rm GL}_{2}(E)
can be described by the isomorphism

\iota:(B^{\times}\times E^{\times})/\Delta F^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm GU}(V_{B})\subset{\rm GL}(V_{B})

given by sending
(b,e)\in B^{\times}\times E^{\times}
to the element of
{\rm GL}(V_{B})
whose action on
B
is:

(b,e):y\mapsto e\cdot y\cdot b^{-1}.

The similitude character is:

{\rm sim}(b,e)=N_{E/F}(e)\cdot N_{B}(b)^{-1}.

Hence the unitary group is

{\rm U}(V_{B})\cong\{(b,e)\in(B^{\times}\times E^{\times})/\Delta F^{\times}={\rm GU}(V_{B}):N_{E/F}(e)=N_{B}(b)\}

This is contained in the subgroup

{\rm GU}(V_{B})^{+}\cong\{(b,e)\in(B^{\times}\times E^{\times})/\Delta F^{\times}={\rm GU}(V_{B}):N_{B}(b)\in N_{E/F}(E^{\times})\}

which has index 2 in
{\rm GU}(V_{B})
. Moreover, if
Z=E^{\times}
denotes the center of
{\rm GL}(V_{B})
, then

{\rm GU}(V_{B})^{+}=Z\cdot{\rm U}(V_{B}).

Thus, when working with irreducible representations of
{\rm U}(V_{B})
, there is no difference in working with
{\rm GU}(V_{B})^{+}
instead.

Let us explicate the Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V_{B})
in this framework. The Weil representation
\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}
is reducible but admits a central character decomposition:

\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\lambda]

where the sum runs over the characters of
Z({\rm U}(V_{B}))=E_{1}
and each summand is irreducible or
0
.
We can describe
\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\lambda]
in terms of the description of
{\rm U}(V_{B})
given above. More precisely, suppose that
\lambda=\chi|_{E_{1}}
for a character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
. Consider the L-parameter

N={\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{F}}(\mu\cdot\chi^{-1})\quad\text{ of ${\rm GL}_{2}(F)$,}

and let
\Sigma_{B,N}
be the associated representation of
B^{\times}
. This gives a representation

\Sigma_{B,N}\boxtimes\chi\quad\text{ of $B^{\times}\times E^{\times}$}

which is trivial on
\Delta F^{\times}
, i.e. a representation of
{\rm GU}(V_{B})
. This representation of
{\rm GU}(V_{B})
decomposes into the sum of two irreducible summands when restricted to
{\rm GU}(V_{B})^{+}
. One of these summands is the representation
\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\chi|_{E_{1}}]
whereas the other is
\omega_{\psi^{\prime},\mu,B}[\chi|_{E_{1}}]
, with
\psi^{\prime}
in a different
N(E^{\times})
-orbit as
\psi
.

Now suppose that
\Pi
is an irreducible generic representation of
{\rm U}(V_{B}\otimes_{F}E)\cong{\rm GL}(V_{B})
with L-parameter
M
.
The embedding
{\rm U}(V_{B})\hookrightarrow{\rm U}(V_{B}\otimes_{F}E)
is the natural embedding
{\rm U}(V_{B})\subset{\rm GL}(V_{B})
. Pulling
\Pi
back via
\iota
, and with
\chi:=\omega_{\Pi}
, we see that

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{B})}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,B})

\displaystyle={\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{B})}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\chi|_{E_{1}}])

\displaystyle\cong{\rm Hom}_{(B^{\times})^{+}}(\iota^{*}(\Pi),\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\chi|_{E_{1}}])

\displaystyle\cong{\rm Hom}_{B^{\times}}(\iota^{*}(\Pi),\Sigma_{B,N}).

Now it is important to note that the embedding
\iota:B\hookrightarrow{\rm GL}(V_{B})={\rm GL}_{2}(E)
is not the natural embedding
B^{\times}\hookrightarrow(B\otimes_{F}E)^{\times}\cong{\rm GL}_{2}(E)
, but rather differs from it by the outer automorphism
b\mapsto\bar{b}^{-1}
.
Indeed,
\iota
is the inverse on the central
F^{\times}
.
Taking this into account, we see that the last Hom space above is the space

{\rm Hom}_{B^{\times}}(\Pi^{\vee}\otimes\Sigma_{B,N}^{\vee},\mathbb{C})

of twisted trilinear forms, where
B^{\times}\hookrightarrow(B\otimes_{F}E)^{\times}\cong{\rm GL}_{2}(E)
, with the last isomorphism induced by an
E
-algebra isomorphism
B\otimes_{F}E\cong{\rm M}_{2}(E)
.

By a result of the third author
[
P1
]
, one has

\dim{\rm Hom}_{B^{\times}}(\Pi^{\vee}\otimes\Sigma_{B,N}^{\vee},\mathbb{C})\leq 1

with equality if and only if

\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}^{+}(M^{\vee})\otimes N^{\vee},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)=\mu(\det(V_{B})),

where
As^{+}
is the Asai lift of
M
from
E
to
F
. We refer the reader to §
8.4
below for the definition and properties of
As^{+}
.
Now let us explicate the local root number:

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}^{+}(M^{\vee})\otimes N^{\vee},\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}^{+}(M^{\vee})\otimes{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(\mu^{-1}\cdot\chi),\psi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(M^{\vee}\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1}\otimes\chi),\psi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1}),\psi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}),

where in the second last equality, we have noted that
\chi=\omega_{\Pi}=\det M
, so that
M^{\vee}\otimes\chi\cong M
(since
\dim M=2
), and in the last equality, we have
used the fact that epsilon factors are inductive in dimension zero together with the fact that

\dim(M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee})=4
.

To conclude, we have shown:

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\Pi
, an irreducible generic representation of
{\rm U}(V_{B}\otimes_{F}E)\cong{\rm GL}(V_{B})
,
with L-parameter
M
, a two dimensional representation of
WD_{E}
,

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{B})}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,B})\neq 0\iff\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)=\mu(\det(V_{B})).

This is precisely what Conjecture
2.1
says in the case
n=2
.

3.3.
Global conjecture: rank 1 case

Finally, we can also verify the global Conjecture
2.7
when
\dim_{E}V=1
. Let
\chi
be a Hecke character of
{\rm GL}(V)(\mathbb{A}_{E})=\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
, so that we are considering the global period integral

\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{C}\chi\otimes\overline{\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

defined by

\mathcal{P}(\phi)=\int_{[E_{1}]}\chi(x)\cdot\overline{\phi(x)}\,dx.

Observe that this is simply the (conjugate of the) global theta lifting of
\chi
for the dual pair

{\rm U}_{1}\times{\rm U}_{1}={\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(W),

evaluated at the identity element. Here,
V
is equipped with its given skew Hermitian structure and
W
is the rank 1 Hermitian space
\langle 1\rangle
.
The nonvanishing of
\mathcal{P}
is thus equivalent to the nonvanishing of the global theta lift
\Theta_{V,W,\psi,\mu}(\chi)
of
\chi
. Moreover, when this global theta lift is nonzero, it is isomorphic to the representation
\chi
of
{\rm U}(W)=E_{1}
. Then we have:

\mathcal{P}(\phi_{1})\cdot\overline{\mathcal{P}(\phi_{2})}\cdot\mu([E_{1}])=\langle\Theta(\phi_{2},\chi),\Theta(\phi_{1},\chi)\rangle_{\rm Pet}

where
\mu([E_{1}])=2
is the Tamagawa measure of
{\rm U}(W)
. Now the Petersson inner product of the global theta lift on the RHS is computed by the Rallis inner product formula. This was first done by Tonghai Yang
[
Y
]
and a convenient reference is
[
X2
, Thm. A.4.2]
. One has

\langle\Theta(\phi_{2},\chi),\Theta(\phi_{1},\chi)\rangle_{\rm Pet}=\frac{L(1/2,\chi^{\sigma}\chi^{-1}\cdot\mu^{-1})}{L(1,\omega_{E/F})}\cdot Z^{*}(\phi_{2},\phi_{1})

where

Z^{*}(\phi_{2},\phi_{1})=\int^{*}_{\mathbb{A}_{E}^{1}}\overline{\langle g\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle}\cdot\langle g\chi,\chi\rangle_{{\rm U}(V),{\rm Pet}}\,dg

is the normalized global doubling zeta integral. Since the Tamagawa measure of
{\rm U}(V)
is
2
, one has

\langle g\cdot\chi,\chi\rangle_{{\rm U}(V),{\rm Pet}}=2\cdot\chi(g)

so that

Z^{*}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=2\cdot\int^{*}_{\mathbb{A}_{E}^{1}}\overline{\langle g\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle}\cdot\chi(g)\,dg=\prod_{v}\mathcal{I}_{v}^{\#}(\chi,\chi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}),

where the local factors
\mathcal{I}_{v}^{\#}
are as defined in (
2.3
) and (
2.5
).
Hence we conclude that

(3.3)

\mathcal{P}(\phi_{1})\cdot\overline{\mathcal{P}(\phi_{2})}=\frac{L(1/2,\chi^{\sigma}\chi^{-1}\cdot\mu^{-1})}{L(1,\omega_{E/F})}\cdot\prod_{v}\mathcal{I}_{v}^{\#}(\chi,\chi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}).

This is precisely what Conjecture
2.7
says.

For the case when
\dim_{E}V=2
, Conjecture
2.7
should reduce to Ichino’s formula
[
I
]
relating the (twisted) triple product period integral and the (twisted) triple product L-value. We leave the verification of this to the interested reader.

4.
Mackey Theory: Restriction of Principal Series

In this section, we apply Mackey theory to understand the branching of a principal series representation of
{\rm GL}(V)
to the Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V)
over a non-archimedean local field.

4.1.
Principal series

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
n
over
E
.
For a partition
n=a+b,
with
0<a\leq b\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

V=V_{a}\oplus V_{b}

with
\dim V_{a}=a
and
\dim V_{b}=b
. Consider the maximal parabolic subgroup

P=P_{a,b}=M\cdot N

of
{\rm GL}(V)
stabilizing
V_{a}
, with Levi factor

M={\rm GL}(V_{a})\times{\rm GL}(V_{b}).

Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
be a representation of
{\rm GL}(V_{a})\times{\rm GL}(V_{b})

and consider the (normalized) parabolically induced representation

(4.1)

\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}={\rm Ind}_{P}^{{\rm GL}(V)}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}).

These are the principal series representations we will consider.

4.2.
Skew-Hermitian structures

Recall that there are two inequivalent skew-Hermitian structures on
V
, distinguished by their determinants in
F^{\times}/NE^{\times}
or
E_{0}^{\times}/NE^{\times}
(depending on whether
n=\dim V
is even or odd).
For such a class
\delta
, we let
V_{\delta}
denote the skew-Hermitian structure on
V
with determinant
\delta
, so that

{\rm U}(V_{\delta})\subset{\rm GL}(V)
. We will often drop
\delta
from
V_{\delta}

when a particular skew-Hermitian structure is fixed on
V
. We also let
{\rm rk}(V)
denote the dimension of a maximal isotropic subspace of the skew-Hermitian space
V
; this is sometimes called the Witt index of
V
.

On the other hand,
V
with a roman subscript, such as
V_{a}
, will
denote either just a vector space over
E
or a skew-Hermitian space over
E
of dimension
a
.

4.3.
Mackey theory

For a fixed skew-Hermitian space
V=V_{\delta}
, the goal of this section is to compute

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}),

where
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
denotes the Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V)
associated to
(\psi,\mu)
. In fact, we will consider the
more general

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}).

This will be achieved by using Mackey theory, which requires the determination of the orbits of
{\rm U}(V)
on the partial flag variety
{\rm GL}(V)/P
. In this analysis, each orbit gives rise to a certain induced representation of
{\rm U}(V)
arising from the restriction of the inducing data to the
stabilizer of a point in the orbit.

Thus the representation
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
when restricted to
{\rm U}(V)
comes equipped with a certain
finite filtration by
{\rm U}(V)
-modules in which the open orbits contribute as submodules, and the non-open orbits contribute as
subquotients.

4.4.
Orbits

The following lemma, whose proof will be omitted, summarizes the orbit structure of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P
and is a direct consequence of Witt’s theorem.

Lemma 4.2
.

The orbits of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P_{a,b}
(with
0<a\leq b
) are
represented by the isometry classes of
a
-dimensional
E
-subspaces
X\subset V
, which are themselves
parameterized by the following two invariants:

(1)

the dimension
d
of the kernel of the skew-Hermitian form on
V
restricted to
X
, i.e.
d=\dim(X\cap X^{\perp})
,

d\leq\min\{a,{\rm rk}(V)\}
, and

(2)

the nondegenerate skew-Hermitian form
on
X/(X\cap X^{\perp})
, which can be arbitrary.

In particular, with
E
non-archimedean, one has:

•

For each integer

0\leq d\leq\min\{a,{\rm rk}(V)\},

there are two orbits
[X]
of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P_{a,b}
, with
\dim(X\cap X^{\perp})=d
, unless
d=a
(i.e. when

X/(X\cap X^{\perp})=0
), in which case there is only one.

•

The open orbits correspond to
d=0
, i.e.,
the isomorphism classes of the two nondegenerate skew-Hermitian subspaces of
V
of dimension
a
.

•

There is a unique closed orbit which corresponds to
d=\min\{a,{\rm rk}(V)\}=a
,
except when
n=2a
and
V

does not have an isotropic subspace of dimension
a
, in which case there are two closed orbits corresponding to
d=a-1
.

4.5.
Stabilizers

Let
[X]
be an
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit in
{\rm GL}(V)/P_{a,b}
, represented by an
E
-subspace

X\subset V
of dimension
a
with
\dim(X\cap X^{\perp})=d
. Let us first determine the stabilizer
S=S_{X}
of
X
in
{\rm U}(V)
.

Observe that
S_{X}
preserves the flag

0\subset X\cap X^{\perp}\subset X\subset(X\cap X^{\perp})^{\perp}=X+X^{\perp}\subset V,

and note that

X/(X\cap X^{\perp})\subset(X+X^{\perp})/(X\cap X^{\perp})=:V_{n-2d}

are non-degenerate skew-Hermitian spaces of dimension
a-d
and
n-2d
respectively.
Hence,
S_{X}
is contained in the maximal parabolic subgroup
Q_{d}
of
{\rm U}(V)
stabilizing the isotropic space
X\cap X^{\perp}
. The parabolic subgroup
Q_{d}=M_{d}\cdot N_{d}
can be depicted in matrix form as:

Q_{d}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\perp})&*_{1}&*_{d}\\
0&{\rm U}(V_{n-2d})&*_{2}\\
0&0&*\end{array}\right),

with Levi factor

M_{d}={\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\perp})\times{\rm U}(V_{n-2d})

and unipotent radical
N_{d}
. The center of
N_{d}
is the subgroup

Z_{d}
consisting of matrices with
*_{1}=*_{2}=0
.

It follows that, as a subgroup of
Q_{d}
,
S_{X}
has the form:

S_{X}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}g&*_{12}&*_{13}&*_{4}\\
0&{\rm U}_{a-d}&0&*_{24}\\
0&0&{\rm U}_{b-d}&*_{34}\\
0&0&0&(g^{*})^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

•

g\in{\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\perp})\cong{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
,

•

the entries
*_{12}
and
*_{34}
are arbitrary matrices with entries in
E
of appropriate sizes which determines
*_{24},*_{13}
;

•

the entry
*_{4}
is an arbitrary skew-Hermitian matrix of size
d\times d
.

Let us highlight certain natural subgroups or quotients of
S_{X}
:

•

The unipotent radical
N(S_{X})
of
S_{X}
consists of those matrices which have the identity matrix on each diagonal block. Observe that
N(S_{X})
is in fact the unipotent radical
N_{d}
of the maximal parabolic subgroup
Q_{d}
.

•

The center
Z(S_{X})
of
N(S_{X})
is the subgroup consisting of elements whose only nonzero entry in the upper triangular blocks is
*_{4}
, so that
Z(S_{X})=Z_{d}
.

•

The Levi factor
S_{X}/N(S_{X})
is isomorphic to

{\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\perp})\times{\rm U}_{a-d}\times{\rm U}_{b-d}.

4.6.
Modules

In what follows, we use
{\rm Ind}

for the usual normalized induction, and
{\rm ind}
for the usual normalized induction with compact support, whereas we will use

By Mackey theory, the restriction of the principal series representation

to

has a finite equivariant filtration indexed by the the

-orbits given in Lemma

. For each such
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit

, let
\pi_{X}
denote the associated
{\rm U}(V)
-subquotient of
\pi
. The following proposition determines the representation

.

Proposition 4.3
.

For a
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit
[X]
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P
, with
\dim X\cap X^{\perp}=d
and stabilizer

\pi_{X}\cong\mathfrak{i}nd_{S}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P}^{1/2})|_{S}={\rm ind}_{S}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2})|_{S},

where we have written
\delta_{P/S}=\delta_{P}\delta_{S}^{-1}
.

We note that the representation
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2}

of

4.7.
Branching for
\pi_{X}

We are now ready to consider the branching problem

Since, as a

The result of this key computation is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

For an orbit
[X]
of
{\rm U}(V)
on

and associated

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}\left(\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}\right)

{\cong}\,\,\,{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/N(S)}\left(\delta_{P/S}^{1/2}\cdot(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d},\,\,\delta_{S}^{1/2}\cdot|{\det}_{{\rm GL}_{d}}|^{-1/2}\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}\right)

where we note:

•

S/N(S)\cong{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}_{a-d}\times{\rm U}_{b-d}
;

•

(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}
denotes the un-normalized Jacquet module of
\pi_{1}
with respect to the

(d,a-d)

likewise,
(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}

\pi_{2}
with respect to the
(b-d,d)

•

The characters
\delta_{P/S}
and
\delta_{S}

{\rm U}_{a-d}\times{\rm U}_{b-d}

\delta_{P/S}=|\det|^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{S}(g)=|\det|^{n-d}.

In particular, for the two open orbits

corresponding to

,
we have,

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]{\cong}\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}i=j+k\\
V=V_{a}\oplus V_{b}\end{array}}{\rm Ext}^{j}_{{\rm U}(V_{a})}[\pi_{1}|_{{\rm U}(V_{a})},\omega_{V_{a},\psi,\mu}]\otimes{\rm Ext}^{k}_{{\rm U}(V_{b})}[\pi_{2}|_{{\rm U}(V_{b})},\omega_{V_{b},\psi,\mu}],

where
X=V_{a}
are the isomorphism classes of non-degenerate subspaces of

complement
X^{\perp}=V_{b}

Proof.

For analyzing
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]
, we will need the following generalities on Ext groups and contragredients
(cf.
[
P3
]
for (a) below):

(a) For any two smooth representations

of a
p
-adic group
G

{\rm Ext}_{G}^{i}[U,V^{\vee}]\cong{\rm Ext}_{G}^{i}[V,U^{\vee}].

(b) For

p
-adic group

any smooth representation of
H
with smooth dual

[{\rm ind}_{H}^{G}U]^{\vee}\cong{\rm Ind}_{H}^{G}U^{\vee}.

(c) For a nontrivial character
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

of
{\rm U}(V)

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}^{\vee}\cong\omega_{V,\psi^{-},\mu^{-1}},

where
\psi^{-}(x)=\psi(-x)

We shall also use the following three lemmas whose proofs are left to the reader.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be
p
-adic groups with

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{H}[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]\cong{\rm Ext}^{i}_{H/U}[\pi_{1,U},\pi_{2}].

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

-adic group,

a closed central subgroup. If
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are two

{\rm Ext}_{G}^{i}[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]=0{\rm\penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ \penalty\ all\penalty\ \penalty\ }i\geq 0.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
V=V_{n}
be a skew-Hermitian space over
E
of dimension
n
, and let

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
be a Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V)
. Let
Q_{d}=M_{d}N_{d}
be
the maximal parabolic subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)
stabilizing a

(see §
4.5
), so that
M_{d}\cong{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}(V_{n-2d})
. Then for

, the center of

, we have:

(\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})_{Z_{d}}=(\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})_{N_{d}}\cong(\mu\cdot|-|^{1/2}\circ\det)\otimes\omega_{{V_{n-2d}},\psi,\mu},

as
M_{d}
-modules.

With these preliminaries in place, we now compute:

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[{\rm ind}_{S}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2}),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}],

(by Prop.
4.3
)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\omega_{V,\psi^{-},\mu^{-1}},{\rm Ind}_{S}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2})^{\vee}],

(by (a),(b) and (c))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S}[\delta_{S}^{-1/2}\omega_{V,\psi^{-},\mu^{-1}},(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2})^{\vee}],

(by Frobenius reciprocity)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/Z(S)}[\delta_{S}^{-1/2}(\omega_{V,\psi^{-},\mu^{-1}})_{Z(S)},(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2})^{\vee}],

(by Lemma
4.5
)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/Z(S)}[\delta_{S}^{-1/2}\cdot\mu^{-1}\cdot|\det|^{1/2}\cdot\omega_{{V_{n-2d}},\psi^{-},\mu^{-1}},(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2})^{\vee}]

(by Lemma

)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/Z(S)}[\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2},\,\,\delta_{S}^{1/2}|\det|^{-1/2}\cdot\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}]

(by (a))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/N(S)}[(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}\otimes\delta_{P/S}^{1/2},\,\,\delta_{S}^{1/2}|\det|^{-1/2}\cdot\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}]

where
(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}
denotes the un-normalized Jacquet module of
\pi_{1}
with respect to the

(d,a-d)
parabolic subgroup in
{\rm GL}_{a}(E)
; similarly for
(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}
.
Here we have applied Lemma
4.5
(taking
U=N(S)/Z(S)
) for the last isomorphism for which
it is important to note that

N(S)/Z(S)
maps isomorphically to the product of the unipotent radicals of the
(d,a-d)
-parabolic subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{a}(E)
and the
(b-d,d)
-parabolic subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{b}(E)
.

For the final assertion in the proposition regarding the open orbits corresponding to
d=0
, it suffices to observe that for the direct sum
of nondegenerate skew-Hermitian spaces

, we have the tensor product decomposition of their Weil representations:

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}\cong\omega_{V_{a},\psi,\mu}\otimes\omega_{V_{b},\psi,\mu},

as representations of

[

, completing the proof of Proposition
4.4

∎

4.8.
Branching for
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}

We can now assemble the results of Proposition
4.4
for the various
{\rm U}(V)
-orbits
[X]
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P
to understand the branching problem

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}).

The result is most definitive when only the open orbits have nonzero contribution. The following theorem, which is the main result of this section,
gives a simple sufficient condition (temperedness of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
) for this to happen.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are tempered representations of
{\rm GL}(V_{a})
and
{\rm GL}(V_{b})

(with unitary central characters) , so that
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
is a tempered principal series representation
of
{\rm GL}(V)
for
V=V_{a}+V_{b}
.
If
[X]
is a non-open orbit of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P
, then for
\pi_{X}
, the subquotient of
\pi
supported on the
orbit
[X]
, we have:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]=0,

for all
i\geq 0
.

As a consequence, for all
i\geq 0
, one has:

(4.9)

\bigoplus_{\delta}{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V_{\delta})}[\pi,\omega_{V_{\delta},\psi,\mu}]

\cong\bigoplus_{i=j+k}\left(\bigoplus_{\delta^{\prime}}{\rm Ext}^{j}_{{\rm U}(V_{a,\delta^{\prime}})}[\pi_{1},\omega_{V_{a,\delta^{\prime}},\psi,\mu}]\right)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{\delta^{\prime\prime}}{\rm Ext}^{k}_{{\rm U}(V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}})}[\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}},\psi,\mu}]\right)

where the sums over
\delta
,
\delta^{\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime\prime}
run over
F^{\times}/NE^{\times}
or
E_{0}^{\times}/NE^{\times}
according to the parity of
n,a,b
, respectively.

Hence, for
i=0
, one has:

(4.10)

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{\delta})}[\pi,\omega_{V_{\delta},\psi}]\cong

\bigoplus_{(\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}):V_{a,\delta^{\prime}}\oplus V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}}\cong V_{\delta}}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{a,\delta^{\prime}})}[\pi_{1},\omega_{V_{a,\delta^{\prime}},\psi}]\otimes{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}})}[\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}},\psi}].

In particular,

(4.11)

\bigoplus_{\delta}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{\delta})}[\pi,\omega_{V_{\delta},\psi}]\cong

\left(\bigoplus_{\delta^{\prime}}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{a,\delta^{\prime}})}[\pi_{1},\omega_{V_{a,\delta^{\prime}},\psi}]\right)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{\delta^{\prime\prime}}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}})}[\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{b,\delta^{\prime\prime}},\psi}]\right).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
,
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]
is equal to

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/N(S)}[\delta_{P/S}^{1/2}\cdot(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d},\,\,\delta_{S}^{1/2}\cdot|\det|^{-1/2}\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}].

By Casselman’s criterion, the normalized Jacquet module of a tempered representation with respect to a parabolic
Q=L\cdot U
of
{\rm GL}_{n}
is a tempered representation of the Levi factor
L
up to multiplication by a character
\delta_{Q}^{\alpha/2}
for some real number
\alpha\geq 0
.
Hence, since
\pi_{1}
is tempered,

(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}=\delta_{P_{d,a-d}}^{(1+\alpha)/2}\times({\rm a\penalty\ \penalty\ tempered\penalty\ \penalty\ representation\penalty\ \penalty\ of\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm GL}_{a-d}(E)).

Moreover, for
(g,h)\in{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}_{a-d}(F)
,

\delta_{P_{d,a-d}}(g,h)^{(1+\alpha)/2}=|\det(g)|^{(a-d+\epsilon)/2},\quad\text{with $\epsilon=\alpha\cdot(a-d)$.}

Similarly,

(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}=\delta_{P_{b-d,d}}^{(1+\alpha^{\prime})/2}\times({\rm a\penalty\ \penalty\ tempered\penalty\ \penalty\ representation\penalty\ \penalty\ of\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\rm GL}_{b-d}(E)\times{\rm GL}_{d}(E))

and for
(h,(g^{*})^{-1})\in{\rm U}_{b-d}(F)\times{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
,

\delta_{P_{b-d,d}}(h,(g^{*})^{-1})^{(1+\alpha^{\prime})/2}=|\det(g)|^{(b-d+\epsilon^{\prime})/2},\quad\text{with $\epsilon^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}\cdot(b-d)$.}

Summarizing, we have:

(1)

the representation

A=\delta_{P/S}^{1/2}\cdot(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}

is the twist of a unitary representation of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}(V_{a-d})\times{\rm U}(V_{b-d})
by the character

|\det|^{(a-d+\epsilon)/2}\cdot|\det|^{(b-d+\epsilon^{\prime})/2}|\det|^{d/2}=|\det|^{(n-d+\epsilon+\epsilon^{\prime})/2}

of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
.

(2)

the representation

B=\delta_{S}^{1/2}\cdot|\det|^{-1/2}\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}

is the twist of a unitary representation of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}(V_{n-2d})
by the character

|\det|^{(n-d)/2}\cdot|\det|^{-1/2}=|\det|^{(n-d-1)/2}

of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
.

Thus, when
d>0
, the actions of the center of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
in

S/N(S)={\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}(V_{a-d})\times{\rm U}(V_{b-d})

on the two representations
A
and
B
are different.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.6
,

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{S/N(S)}[A,B]=0,

for all
i\geq 0
(as long as
d\not=0
). This completes the proof
that for a non-open
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit
[X]\subset{\rm GL}(V)/P
, the associated subquotient
\pi_{X}
of the
{\rm U}(V)
-module
\pi
satisfies

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi}]=0\quad\text{for all $i\geq 0$.}

As a consequence of the vanishing of
{\rm Ext}^{i}
,
i\geq 0
, for all non-open orbits, we deduce that

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]=\bigoplus_{X}{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}],

where now
X
runs over the two open orbits of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/P
. Since Proposition
4.4
calculates

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi}]
for the open orbits,
the proof of Theorem
4.8
is completed. ∎

5.
Application to Conjecture
2.1
.

In this section, we deduce the implications of Theorem
4.8
for Conjecture
2.1
.
Indeed, we shall see how Theorem
4.8
allows us to reduce Conjecture
2.1
for tempered representations
to the case of discrete series representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
. This allows us to
prove Conjecture
2.1
for unitary principal series induced from the Borel subgroup. We also investigate if the Mackey theory argument allows one to further
reduce Conjecture
2.1
to the case of supercuspidal representations. As we shall see, we will fall slightly short of that, but we will at least be able to prove Conjecture
2.1
for the Steinberg representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)
.

5.1.
Inductive argument

Let us first record the following consequence of Theorem
4.8
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
V=V_{a}\oplus V_{b}
be a direct sum of nondegenerate skew-Hermitian spaces, and let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be irreducible tempered representations of
{\rm GL}(V_{a})
and
{\rm GL}(V_{b})
respectively. Then we have the following.

(i) If Conjecture
2.1
holds
for
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, then Conjecture
2.1
holds for the unitary principal series representation
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
of
{\rm GL}(V)
.

(ii) If

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V_{a})}(\pi_{1},\omega_{V_{a},\psi,\mu})={\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V_{b})}(\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{b},\psi,\mu})=0\quad\text{ for all $i>0$,}

then

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})=0\quad\text{ for all $i>0$.}

Proof.

The vanishing statement in (ii) follows from Theorem
4.8
, especially (
4.9
).
Likewise, (
4.11
) imply that Conjecture
2.1
(i) and (ii) for
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
follows from the corresponding statements for
\pi_{!}
and
\pi_{2}
.
Thus it remains to verify that the unique
skew-Hermitian space
V_{\delta}
for which
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{\delta})}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{\delta},\psi,\mu})
is nonzero
is as predicted by Conjecture
2.1
(iii).

Assume then that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\neq 0.

By (
4.10
),

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{a})}(\pi_{1},\omega_{V_{a},\psi,\mu})\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{b})}(\pi_{2},\omega_{V_{b},\psi,\mu})\neq 0

for a unique pair of skew-Hermitian spaces
V_{a}
and
V_{b}
satisfying
V_{a}\oplus V_{b}\cong V
.
As we have assumed that Conjecture
2.1
(iii) holds for
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, we have:

\mu(\det(V_{a}))=\epsilon(1/2,\pi_{1}\times{}^{\sigma}\pi_{1}^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\pi_{1}}(-1)^{a}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{a(a-1)/2},

and

\mu(\det(V_{b}))=\epsilon(1/2,\pi_{2}\times{}^{\sigma}\pi_{2}^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\pi_{2}}(-1)^{b}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{b(b-1)/2}.

This implies that for
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
,

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,\pi\times{}^{\sigma}\pi^{\vee}\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\pi}(-1)^{n}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2},

using the facts that
\mu={}^{\sigma}\mu^{-1}
,
\mu(-1)=\omega_{E/F}(-1)
and

\epsilon(\Pi+{}^{\sigma}\Pi^{\vee},\psi_{E})=\det\Pi(-1)

for any representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{m}(E)
.
This completes the proof of the corollary.∎

5.2.
Reduction to discrete series case

Corollary
5.1
allows one to reduce Conjecture
2.1
for tempered representations to the case of discrete series representations.

Corollary 5.2
.

If Conjecture
2.1
holds for all (unitary) discrete series representations of all
{\rm GL}(V)
, then it holds for all (unitary) tempered representations of all
{\rm GL}(V)
.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
5.1
and the fact that any tempered non-discrete-series representation of
{\rm GL}(V)
is irreducibly and unitarily induced from a discrete series representation of a proper parabolic subgroup.
∎

5.3.
Borel-Principal series

In addition, by applying Corollary
5.1
inductively, we deduce:

Corollary 5.3
.

Conjecture
2.1
holds for all irreducible unitary principal series representations of
{\rm GL}(V)

induced from a unitary character of a Borel subgroup.

Proof.

Using Corollary
5.1
, this follows by induction on
\dim V
. The base case, with
\dim V=1
, is
Theorem
3.1
due to Moen and Rogawski.
∎

5.4.
An alternative proof

We now give another proof of Corollary
5.3
as it brings out an interesting structure of the open orbits of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}(V)/B
.

Suppose that

\Pi={\rm Ind}_{B}^{{\rm GL}(V)}(\chi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\chi_{n})\quad\text{(normalized induction),}

so that its L-parameter is

M=\bigoplus_{i}\chi_{i}.

On restriction to
{\rm U}(V)
, Theorem
4.8
inductively implies that only the open
{\rm U}(V)
-orbits on the flag variety
{\rm GL}(V)/B
will contribute
to the Hom space
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi}]
. Moreover, using Lemma
4.2
inductively,
the open orbits can be described as follows. Given an ordered collection

\mathcal{L}=\{L_{1},\cdots,L_{n}\}

of nondegenerate
orthogonal lines in
V
, the
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit of the flag

\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{L}}:L_{1}\subset L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\subset\cdots

is an open orbit, and the stabilizer of
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{L}}
in
{\rm U}(V)
is the subgroup

{\rm U}(\mathcal{L}):=\prod_{i}{\rm U}(L_{i}).

Moreover, all open orbits are given by such an ordered collection
\{L_{i}\}
of isomorphism classes of
(nondegenerate) skew-Hermitian
E
-spaces of dimension 1, subject to the condition that
\oplus_{i}L_{i}\cong V
as skew-Hermitian
E
-spaces; we say that such an
\mathcal{L}
is
V
-relevant. There are thus
2^{n-1}
open orbits, indexed by
V
-relevant
\mathcal{L}
’s. This can also be gleaned from a Galois cohomological argument: having fixed an open orbit over
F
with stabilizer
{\rm U}(\mathcal{L})
in
{\rm U}(V)
and noting that there is exactly one open orbit over
\overline{F}
, the number of open
{\rm U}(V)
-orbits is given by:

{\rm Ker}\left(H^{1}(F,{\rm U}(\mathcal{L}))\rightarrow H^{1}(F,{\rm U}(V))\right)={\rm Ker}\left((F^{\times}/N(E^{\times}))^{n}\rightarrow F^{\times}/N(E^{\times})\right).

Hence, by an inductive application of Theorem
4.8
,
we have

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}({\rm ind}_{{\rm U}(\mathcal{L})}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\boxtimes_{i}\chi_{i}),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})

where the sum runs over
V
-relevant
\mathcal{L}
’s.
By Frobenius reciprocity, and the fact that

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}|_{{\rm U}(\mathcal{L})}\cong\bigotimes_{i}\omega_{L_{i},\psi,\mu},

one deduces that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}}\bigotimes_{i}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{i})}(\chi_{i},\omega_{L_{i},\psi,\mu}).

Now by Theorem
3.1
(the theorem of Moen and Rogawski),

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{i})}(\chi_{i},\omega_{L_{i},\psi,\mu})\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\chi_{i}(-1)=\mu(\det(L_{i})).

Hence, at most one term in the sum over
\mathcal{L}
has nonzero contribution, and this unique
\mathcal{L}
exists if and only if

\mu(\det(V))=\prod_{i}\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\chi_{i}(-1)

To prove Conjecture
2.1
, we explicate:

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i}\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\prod_{i<j}\epsilon\left(1/2,(\chi_{i}/\chi_{j}^{\sigma}+\chi_{j}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}\right).

For
i<j
, observe that

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(1/2,(\chi_{i}/\chi_{j}^{\sigma}+\chi_{j}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{j}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\epsilon(1/2,\chi^{\sigma}_{j}/\chi_{i}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(-1)\cdot\chi_{j}(-1)\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1),

where we have used the following standard properties of the epsilon factor:

(1)

\epsilon(1/2,W,\psi_{E})\cdot\epsilon(1/2,W^{\vee},\psi_{E})=\det(W)(-1)
,

(2)

\epsilon(1/2,W,\psi_{E})=\epsilon(1/2,W^{\sigma},\psi_{E})
.

It follows that:

\prod_{i<j}\epsilon\left(1/2,(\chi_{i}/\chi_{j}^{\sigma}+\chi_{j}/\chi_{i}^{\sigma})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}\right)=\det(M)(-1)^{n-1}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}.

Putting everything together, we see that
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\neq 0
if and only if

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M)(-1)^{n}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2},

as desired.

5.5.
Reduction to supercuspidal case

We have seen
that Conjecture
2.1
for tempered representations can be reduced to the case of discrete series representations by a Mackey theory argument.
In the rest of the section, we investigate if the same argument can be used to reduce Conjecture
2.1
for discrete series representations to the case of supercuspidal representations. It will turn out that this can be done under a certain hypothesis. While we cannot prove this hypothesis in general, it can be shown in some situations. This will allow us to prove Conjecture
2.1
for the Steinberg representation, for example.

Let us first set up some notations and formulate the relevant hypothesis. Suppose that
\pi
is a supercuspidal representation (with unitary central character) of
{\rm GL}(V)={\rm GL}_{m}(E)
.
The parabolically induced representation

\pi|\det|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\times\pi|\det|^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\times....\times\pi|\det|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}

of
{\rm GL}(V^{\oplus n})\cong{\rm GL}_{mn}(E)
is a standard module and thus has a unique irreducible quotient
{\rm Sp}(\pi,n)
, which is often called a Speh representation and is nontempered (if
n>1
).
This parabolically induced representation also has a unique irreducible submodule
{\rm St}(\pi,n)
; this is the “generalized Steinberg” representation, which is a discrete series representation. All the irreducible (unitary) discrete series representations of general linear groups are of the form
{\rm St}(\pi,n)
. The supercuspidal ones are precisely those with
n=1
.

If

\phi_{\pi}:W_{E}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})

is the L-parameter of
\pi
, then the L-parameter of

{\rm St}(\pi,n)
is the representation
\phi_{\pi}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
of the Weil-Deligne group

WD_{E}=W_{E}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We also write
[n]={\rm Sym}^{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
for the unique irreducible

n
-dimensional representation
of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and write
\phi_{\pi}[n]
for the L-parameter of
{\rm St}(\pi,n)
.

To deal with the generalised Steinberg representations, we will need to make
an assumption.
In this,
V,W
are the two isomorphism calasses of skew-Hermitian spaces over
E
of dimension
m
.
Then we make the following assumption:

({\bf Assumption})\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{cases}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V+V)}[{\rm Sp}(\pi,2),\omega_{V+V,\psi,\mu}]=0,\\
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V+W)}[{\rm Sp}(\pi,2),\omega_{V+W,\psi,\mu}]=0.\end{cases}

We remark that this assumption is a case of the nontempered twisted GGP conjecture formulated below in Conjecture
7.2
.

With this assumption formulated, our result is:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\pi
be a supercuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}(V)
with
\dim V=m

(a) Suppose that

and

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V^{n})}({\rm St}(\pi,n),\omega_{V^{n},\psi,\mu})

\displaystyle 0

for the two isomorphism classes of skew-Hermitian spaces

\displaystyle\mu(\det(V^{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\det(V)^{n})

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,\phi_{\pi}[n]\times{}^{\sigma}\phi_{\pi}^{\vee}[n]\times\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{\pi}(-1)^{nm}\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)^{mn(mn-1)/2}.

Proof.

The first assertion of the Theorem (concerning the truth of Conjecture

Proof of Theorem

(b):

Recall from
[

On the other hand, by the Clebsch-Gordon theorem,

In particular, only odd integers

\lambda

of

Proof of Theorem

5.6.
The Steinberg representation

At the moment, we do not know how to prove the (Assumption) that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V^{2})}[{\rm Sp}(\pi,2),\omega_{V^{2},\psi}]={\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V+W)}[{\rm Sp}(\pi,2),\omega_{V+W,\psi}]=0,

except when
m=\dim V=\dim W=1
, i.e., when
\pi
is a character of
E^{\times}
. In this case, the (Assumption) is equivalent to saying that the Weil representation of
{\rm U}(2)

does not contain any one dimensional representation of
{\rm U}(2)
where
{\rm U}(2)
is either of the two
unitary groups in two variables. The next lemma establishes this.

Lemma 5.7
.

For
V
be a skew-Hermitian space over
E
, a nonarchimedean local field, of dimension
d\geq 2
, one has

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\chi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]=0

for any 1-dimensional character
\chi
of
{\rm U}(V)
.

Proof.

If
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, a direct sum of skew-Hermitian spaces, one knows that as representations
of
{\rm U}(V_{1})\times{\rm U}(V_{2})\subset{\rm U}(V)
,

\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}=\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu}\otimes\omega_{V_{2},\psi,\mu}.

Therefore, the proof of the Lemma reduces to the case of
d=2
.

When
d=2
, we have seen in the discussion in §
3.2
that
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
is a direct sum of irreducible summands (with different central characters), each of which has dihedral L-parameters. Hence,
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
does not contain 1-dimensional characters of
{\rm U}(V)
. (This uses the requirement for
E
to be a nonarchimedean local field!)
∎

As a consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 5.8
.

The Steinberg representation
{\rm St}
of
{\rm GL}(V)
satisfies Conjecture
2.1
.

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}({\rm St}(\pi,n),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong{\rm Ext}^{i+1}_{{\rm U}(V)}(K_{n},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}){\rm\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ all\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }i\geq 1.

Using the isomorphisms in (
5.12
) and (
5.15
), we get:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}({\rm St}(\pi,n),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong{\rm Ext}^{i+2}_{{\rm U}(V)}({\rm St}(\pi,n),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}){\rm\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ all\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }i\geq 1.

Now for any two smooth representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of a reductive
p
-adic group
G(F)
, one has

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{G}[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]=0\quad\text{ for any $i>$ the $F$-rank of $G$.}

Hence, we deduce by (

) that

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}({\rm St}(\pi,n),\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})=0{\rm\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ all\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }i\geq 1,

completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.
Archimedean Case

In this section, we consider the archimedean case, so that
{\rm GL}(V)={\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. As mentioned before, the local conjecture does not determine the unique skew-Hermitian space
V
which has nonzero contribution. In this section, we shall explain how the conjecture can be refined in the archimedean case to give a definitive answer.

Recall that Hermitian forms over
\mathbb{C}
are classified by their signatures
(p,q)
. Since skew-Hermitian forms can be obtained from Hermitian ones by scaling-by
i
, we shall likewise say that a skew-Hermitian space has signature
(p,q)
if it has
p
many
i
’s and
q
many
(-i)
’s as its eigenvalues. We will denote the corresponding space as
V_{p,q}
and its isometry group as
{\rm U}(V_{p,q})={\rm U}_{p,q}
. In particular, in rank
1
, the two skew-Hermitian forms are classified by their determinant, which is
i
or
-i
.

An irreducible generic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an irreducible principal series representation:

\Pi={\rm Ind}_{B(\mathbb{C})}^{{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(\chi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\chi_{n})\quad\text{(normalized induction)}

where the
\chi_{j}
’s are characters of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We may write
\chi_{j}
as:

\chi_{j}(z)=|z|^{r_{j}}\cdot\left(\overline{z}/z\right)^{k_{j}/2}

where
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

As in the previous section, we may consider the restriction of the representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

to a subgroup
{\rm U}(V_{p,q})={\rm U}_{p,q}\subset{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by Mackey theory.
The open
{\rm U}(V_{p,q})
-orbits on the flag variety
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})/B

are associated, as in the
p
-adic case, to the ordered collection
of orthogonal (nondegenerate) skew-Hermitian lines
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{1},\cdots,L_{n}\}
,
with
\oplus_{j}L_{j}\cong V_{p,q}
as skew-Hermitian spaces.
This means that
p
of the lines
L_{i}
’s have determinant
i
and the rest have determinant
-i
;
we shall call such
\mathcal{L}
’s to be
V_{p,q}
-relevant.
In particular, the number of open
{\rm U}(V_{p,q})
-orbits is
\binom{n}{p}
.

If we assume that the analog of Theorem
4.8
holds in the archimedean case, then the proof of Corollary
5.3
gives:

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}}\bigotimes_{j}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{j})}(\chi_{j},\omega_{L_{j},\psi,\mu}),

where the sum is taken over those
\mathcal{L}
which are
V_{p,q}
-relevant.
For each
i
, one may apply Theorem
3.1

[
Mo
,
R
]
:

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{j})}(\chi_{j},\omega_{L_{j},\psi,\mu})\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{j}/\overline{\chi_{j}}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\chi_{j}(-1)=\mu(\det(L_{j})),

which shows that at most one
\mathcal{L}
can have a nonzero contribution to the sum in (*).
Now let us explicate this local root number condition.

The conjugate-symplectic character
\mu
of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
has the form

\mu(z)=\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\right)^{\alpha}\quad\text{ with $\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$.}

Observe that

\mu(i)=i^{-2\alpha}.

Then writing
\chi
in place of
\chi_{j}
for simplicity,

\chi/\overline{\chi}\cdot\mu^{-1}:z\mapsto\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\right)^{k-\alpha}.

Hence, if
\psi
is the additive character of
\mathbb{R}
given by

\psi(x)=e^{2\pi ix},

then by
[
T
, 3.2.5]
(see also
[
GGP2
, Prop. 2.1]
)

\epsilon(1/2,\chi/\overline{\chi}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi({\rm Tr}))={\rm sign}(k-\alpha)\cdot i^{2k-2\alpha}={\rm sign}(k-\alpha)\cdot(-1)^{k}\cdot i^{-2\alpha}.

Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{j})}(\chi_{j},\omega_{L_{j},\psi,\mu})\neq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mu(\det(L_{j}))={\rm sign}(k_{j}-\alpha)\cdot i^{-2\alpha},

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\det(L_{j})={\rm sign}(k_{j}-\alpha)\cdot i.

For this to hold with
\mathcal{L}
being
V_{p,q}
-relevant, we need

\#\{j:k_{j}>\alpha\}=p\quad\text{and}\quad\#\{j:k_{j}<\alpha\}=q=n-p.

Hence, our refinement of Conjecture
2.1
in the archimedean case is:

Conjecture 6.1
.

Assume that
E/F=\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}
. Let

\Pi={\rm Ind}_{B(\mathbb{C})}^{{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(\chi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\chi_{n})

be an irreducible generic principal series representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with

\chi_{j}(z)=|z|^{r_{j}}\cdot\left(\overline{z}/z\right)^{k_{j}/2},\quad k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},

and let

\mu(z)=\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\right)^{\alpha}\quad\text{ with $\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$.}

Then for
\psi(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
,

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{p,q})}(\Pi,\omega_{V_{p,q},\psi,\mu})\neq 0

if and only if

\#\{j:k_{j}>\alpha\}=p\quad\text{and}\quad\#\{j:k_{j}<\alpha\}=q=n-p.

We have essentially proved this conjecture by our open-orbit analysis above, under the hypothesis that Theorem
4.8
holds in the archimedean case.
We leave the analysis of non-open orbits and the resulting extension problems to more capable hands.

The Conjecture for A-Parameters

In this section, we shall extend Conjecture
2.1
beyond the setting of generic representations to the setting of nontempered representations of Arthur type, analogous to what we did in
[
GGP3
]
for the classical GGP conjectures. We begin with a brief recollection of this nontempered conjecture from
[
GGP3
]
.

7.1.
Nontempered GGP and Relevance

In
[
GGP3
]
, we considered the problem of determining

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi_{M},\pi_{N})=\text{$0$ or $1$ }

where
\pi_{M}
and
\pi_{N}
are respectively irreducible representations of
{\rm GL}_{n+1}(F)
and
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
of Arthur type, with associated A-parameters

M_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{d_{i}-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad N_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}N_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{e_{i}-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Here,
M_{i}
and
N_{i}
are irreducible bounded admissible representations of the Weil-Deligne group
WD_{F}
and
{\rm Sym}^{d-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is the
d
-dimensional irreducible representation of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(the Arthur
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
), so that
M_{A}
and
N_{A}
are representations of
WD_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of dimension
n+1
and
n
respectively.
The associated A-packets are singletons, containing the irreducible unitary principal series representations:

\pi_{M}=\times_{i=1}^{r}{\rm Sp}(\pi_{M_{i}},d_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{N}=\times_{i=1}^{l}{\rm Sp}(\pi_{N_{i}},e_{i}),

where
\pi_{M_{i}}
refers to the irreducible representation of the appropriate
{\rm GL}
with L-parameter
M_{i}
and
{\rm Sp}(\pi_{M_{i}},d_{i})
denotes the associated Speh representation (as introduced in §
5.5
).

Remark:
We take this opportunity to correct a misnomer in
[
GGP3
, §5]
. In the first paragraph of
[
GGP3
, Pg. 2312]
, the representation with A-parameter
M_{i}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{d_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
was denoted by
{\rm Speh}(\pi_{M_{i}},d_{i})
. Though just a naming convention, it is more customary to denote this representation by
{\rm Speh}(\pi_{M_{i}},d_{i}+1)
. We have followed the latter convention here.

Now the main conjecture in
[
GGP3
]
(for the general linear groups) is that

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi_{M},\pi_{N})=1

if and only if the pair
(M_{A},N_{A})
is a
relevant pair of A-parameters
. This conjecture has now been proven by K.Y. Chan
[
C
]
. Our goal here is to recall the key notion of “relevance” and make a couple of remarks about it, especially in the context of classical groups.

Definition 7.1
.

Given two A-parameters (of arbitrary dimensions) of
{\rm GL}
-groups

M_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}M_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{i}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad N_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}N_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{i}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),

we say that
(M_{A},N_{A})
is a relevant pair if
we have a decomposition of the respective representations of
WD_{F}
as

M_{i}=M_{i}^{+}+M_{i}^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{i}=N_{i}^{+}+N_{i}^{-}

with the property that

M_{i}^{+}=N_{i+1}^{-}\quad\text{for $i\geq 0$ and}\quad M_{i}^{-}=N_{i-1}^{+}\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

This combinatorial definition has a more geometric interpretation which was discussed in
[
GGP3
, §4]
.

7.2.
Relevance for classical groups

We make a few remarks on the relevance condition for classical groups, clarifying
[
GGP3
]
.

•

The first point is minor but worth noting. The typical GGP conjecture (in the context of
{\rm GL}_{n}\times{\rm GL}_{n+1}
say) is formulated as the branching problem of determining

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)^{\Delta}}(\pi_{M}\otimes\pi_{N},\mathbb{C}),\quad\text{rather than }\quad\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi_{M},\pi_{N}).

When formulated in this way, the nontempered GGP conjecture would then say that

\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(F)^{\Delta}}(\pi_{M}\otimes\pi_{N},\mathbb{C})=1

if and only if
(M_{A},N_{A}^{\vee})
is relevant, where
N_{A}^{\vee}
is the dual representation of
N_{A}
.

•

Secondly, in
[
GGP3
, §6]
, we formulated the nontempered GGP conjecture for the classical groups, asserting that the same “relevance” condition plays a crucial role.
We take this opportunity to explicate the relevance notion here.

For classical groups, the branching problem concerns the determination of

\dim{\rm Hom}_{H}(\pi,\nu),

where
\pi
is an irreducible representation of

G=G_{1}\times G_{2}={\rm U}_{n}\times{\rm U}_{m}\quad\text{(say),}

with
n\geq m
,

H={\rm U}_{m}\ltimes N\subset G

is a subgroup with unipotent radical
N
and
\nu
is a certain small representation of
H
. More precisely,
\nu
is a 1-dimensional character if
n\not\equiv m\bmod 2
; this case is referred to as the
Bessel case for Hermitian spaces
. On the other hand, the case when
n\equiv m\bmod 2
is referred to as the
Fourier-Jacobi
case for skew-Hermitian spaces
; in this case,
\nu
is a Weil representation.

The A-parameters for classical groups are likewise finite dimensional representations of

WD_{E}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, where
WD_{E}

is the Weil-Deligne group of
E
, with appropriate (conjugate)-duality conditions.
Suppose we are given A-parameters

M_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}M_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{i}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),

N_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}N_{i}\boxtimes{\rm Sym}^{i}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),

with
M_{i}
and
N_{i}
satisfying appropriate (conjugate-)duality conditions.
We can now summarize the relevance conditions required in each case as follows.

(i)

Orthogonal and symplectic groups (both Bessel and Fourier-Jacobi models): an A-parameter
M_{A}\boxtimes N_{A}
of

G_{1}\times G_{2}
is relevant if and only if
M_{A}=M_{A}^{\vee}
and
N_{A}=N_{A}^{\vee}
form a relevant pair in the sense of Definition
7.1
for
{\rm GL}_{m}\times{\rm GL}_{n}
.

(ii)

Hermitian case (Bessel models): an A-parameter
M_{A}\boxtimes N_{A}
of

G_{1}\times G_{2}
is relevant if and only if
M_{A}^{\vee}
and
N_{A}
form a relevant pair in the sense of
7.1
for
{\rm GL}_{m}\times{\rm GL}_{n}
.

(iii)

Skew-Hermitian case (Fourier-Jacobi model): in this case, the definition of the Weil representation
\nu
requires an extra piece of data, namely a character

\mu:E^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}\quad\text{with $\mu|_{F^{\times}}=\omega_{E/F}$.}

An A-parameter
M_{A}\boxtimes N_{A}
of

G_{1}\times G_{2}
is relevant if and only if
\mu\cdot M_{A}^{\vee}
and
N_{A}
are relevant in the sense of
7.1
for

{\rm GL}_{m}\times{\rm GL}_{n}
.

Hence, with

7.4.
Degenerate principal series

The reader may wonder how we are led to the above conjecture. In fact, we are led to the conjecture by considering the branching problem for degenerate principal series representations. Recall that in the previous three sections, we have appealed to Mackey theory computations to study the twisted branching problem for tempered principal series representations and generalized Steinberg representations. As much of the material there is of a general nature, it is natural to apply them to the analogous restriction problem for degenerate principal series representations. The result is given in the following proposition. Note that the degenerate principal series considered below are of Arthur type. Hence, the proposition serves as a motivation and check for Conjecture
7.2
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let

•

n=a+b
, with
0<a\leq b\in\mathbb{Z}
;

•

\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:E^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be two unitary characters;

•

V=V_{a}\oplus V_{b}
be an
n
-dimensional
E/F
-skew-Hermitian space, with
\dim V_{a}=a
;

•

P=P_{a,b}
the maximal parabolic subgroup of
{\rm GL}(V)
stabilizing
V_{a}
, with Levi factor
{\rm GL}(V_{a})\times{\rm GL}(V_{b})
;

•

\pi=\chi_{1}\times\chi_{2}
be the degenerate principal series representation
of
{\rm GL}(V)
induced from the corresponding 1-dimensional character
(\chi_{1}\circ\det_{V_{a}})\otimes(\chi_{2}\circ\det_{V_{b}})
of
P_{a,b}
.

If

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]\not=0,

then the following holds:

(i)

b=a+1
and,

(ii)

\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu
where
\sigma
is the Galois involution of
E/F
.

Conversely, if
b=a+1
and
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu
, then
there is exactly one skew-Hermitian structure on
V
such that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]=\mathbb{C},

and for the other skew-Hermitian space
V^{\prime}
,

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V^{\prime})}[\pi,\omega_{V^{\prime},\psi,\mu}]=0.

Proof.

We shall apply the results from Mackey theory obtained in Proposition
4.4
.
Recall that the orbits for the action of
{\rm U}(V)
on
X={\rm GL}_{n}(E)/P_{a,b}
are given by Lemma
4.2
. For an
a
-dimensional
subspace
X\subset V
with
\dim(X\cap X^{\perp})=d
with the corresponding subquotient

\pi_{X}
of
\pi
, Proposition
4.4
says that:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]

{\cong}\,\,{\rm Ext}^{i}_{Q/N(Q)}[(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})_{b-d,d}\otimes\delta_{P/Q}^{1/2},\delta_{Q}^{1/2}\cdot|\det|^{-1/2}\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}]

where
Q/N(Q)={\rm GL}_{d}(E)\times{\rm U}_{a-d}\times{\rm U}_{b-d}
and the other notations are as given there.
Applying this to
\pi_{1}=\chi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}=\chi_{2}
, we deduce:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]

\cong{\rm Ext}^{i}_{Q/N(Q)}[\chi_{1}\cdot(\chi_{2}^{\sigma})^{-1}|\det|^{d/2},|\det|^{(n-d)/2}|\det|^{-1/2}\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{n-2d},\psi,\mu}].

We shall now study when this Ext group can be nonzero.

Consider first the case when
[X]
is an open orbit (so that
d=0
) and
i=0
. In this case
each of the
|\det|
factors which
refers to
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
, are trivial for
d=0

it follows by Lemma
5.7
that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]={\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{a})}(\chi_{1},\omega_{V_{a},\psi,\mu})\otimes{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{b})}(\chi_{2},\omega_{V_{b},\psi,\mu})=0.

On the other hand, when
d>0
, it follows by Lemma
4.6
(on matching powers of
|\det|
for the two arguments) that a necessary condition for the nonvanishing of the above Ext group is:

2d+1=n.

Since
d\leq a\leq b<n=(a+b)
, this implies that we must have:

d=a\quad\text{and}\quad b=a+1,

which means that
[X]
is the unique closed orbit of
{\rm U}(V)
on
{\rm GL}_{n}(E)/P_{a,b}
. In particular,
\pi_{X}
is a quotient of
\pi
.

With
a
,
b
and
d
related as above, the Ext group in question is:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}]\cong{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm GL}_{a}(E)\times{\rm U}_{1}}[\chi_{1}\cdot(\chi_{2}^{\sigma})^{-1},\mu\cdot\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu}],

where
\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu}
is a Weil representation of
{\rm U}(V_{1})={\rm U}_{1}
for the 1-dimensional skew-Hermitian space
V_{1}
with
{\rm disc}(V_{1})={\rm disc}(V)
, and we are regarding
\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu}
as a representation of
{\rm GL}_{a}(E)\times{\rm U}_{1}
.
Thus, we see that

\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu

is a necessary condition for the nonvanishing of this Ext group. When this condition holds, the above Ext group becomes
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V_{1})}(1,\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu})
and this vanishes if

(since
{\rm U}(V_{1})
is compact).

We have thus shown that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\chi_{1}\times\chi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})={\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}).

for
[X]
the unique closed
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit on
{\rm GL}(V)/P_{a,b}
, and a necessary condition for the nonvanishing of this Hom space is

b=a+1\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{\sigma}\cdot\mu.

In other words, we have proved the first assertion of the Proposition.

For the converse, since
[X]
is the closed orbit of
{\rm U}(V)
on

{\rm GL}(V)/P_{a,b}
,
we have seen that when the above conditions hold, one has

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\chi_{1}\times\chi_{2},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})={\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{1})}(1,\omega_{V_{1},\psi,\mu}).

One is thus reduced to the

case of Conjecture
2.1
which is known.

The proof of the Proposition is now complete.

8.1.
Biquadratic extension

Let

{\rm Gal}(E/F)\cong{\rm Gal}(L/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Gal}(K/F)\cong{\rm Gal}(L/E)=\langle\tau\rangle.

Observe that the biquadratic field
L
contains a third quadratic subfield
E^{\prime}

\sigma\cdot\tau
. This field
E^{\prime}

8.2.
Skew-Hermitian spaces.

We consider the two isomorphism classes of skew-Hermitian spaces
V
and
V^{\prime}
over
E
of dimension
n
, and make the following
observation:

Lemma 8.1
.

The two skew-Hermitian spaces
V_{K}=V\otimes_{F}K
and
V^{\prime}_{K}=V^{\prime}\otimes_{F}K
are isomorphic over
L
. When
n
is even,
V_{K}\cong V^{\prime}_{K}
is the maximally split skew-Hermitian space; whereas when
n
is odd,
V_{K}\cong V^{\prime}_{K}
is characterized as the unique skew-Hermitian space whose determinant can be represented by elements of
E^{\times}_{0}
. In either case,
{\rm U}(V_{K})\cong{\rm U}(V^{\prime}_{K})
is a quasi-split group.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\det V
and
\det V^{\prime}
belong to the same
N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
-coset, when viewed as elements of
K^{\times}
. Since
\det V
and
\det V^{\prime}
belong to the same
F^{\times}
-coset, it suffices to observe that
F^{\times}\subset N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
. Indeed, since
L
is a biquadratic extension of
F
,
\omega_{L/K}=\omega_{E/F}\circ N_{K/F}
. Hence

\omega_{L/K}(F^{\times})=\omega_{E/F}\left(N_{K/F}(F^{\times})\right)=\omega_{E/F}(F^{\times 2})=1.\qed

In view of the lemma, we may regard
{\rm U}(V)
and
{\rm U}(V^{\prime})
as subgroups of a fixed
{\rm U}(V_{K})={\rm U}(V^{\prime}_{K})
.

8.3.
Local Langlands correspondence

Now we recall the local Langlands correspondence for
{\rm U}(V_{K})
.
An L-parameter for
{\rm U}(V_{K})
is a conjugate-dual
n
-dimensional semisimple representation
M
of the Weil-Deligne group
WD_{L}=W_{L}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of sign
(-1)^{n-1}
. We have studied such conjugate-dual representations in some detail in
[
GGP1
]
and described their associated component groups
A_{M}
. More precisely, we may write

M=\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\otimes M_{i}\oplus P\oplus{}^{\sigma}P^{\vee}

with
M_{i}
distinct conjugate-dual representations of sign
(-1)^{n-1}
,
V_{i}
its multiplicity space and
P
contains all the irreducible summands which are either non-conjugate-dual or conjugate-dual of sign
(-1)^{n}
, with
{}^{\sigma}P^{\vee}
its conjugate-dual. As we discussed in
[
GGP1
, §4]
, the centralizer group of the L-parameter is of the form

C_{M}=\prod_{i\in I}{\rm O}(V_{i})\times(\text{a connected reductive group}).

Hence the component group of
C_{M}
is an elementary abelian 2-group

A_{M}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cdot a_{i},

equipped with a canonical basis indexed by
I
. The element
-1_{M}
gives rise to the element

\sum_{i\in I}\dim(V_{i})\cdot a_{i}\in A_{M},

which generates a subgroup of order
\leq 2
in
A_{M}
. Now
the local Langlands correspondence for
{\rm U}(V_{K})
gives a partition

{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))=\bigsqcup_{M}\Pi_{M},

of
{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))
into the disjoint union of finite subsets, the L-packets, with the sum running over L-parameters of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
. Moreover since we are at the moment
concerned only with the quasi-split group
{\rm U}(V_{K})
, for each parameter
M
of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
, one has a bijection

J:\Pi_{M}\longleftrightarrow{\rm Irr}(A_{M}/\langle-1_{M}\rangle).

Here the bijection
J
is canonical when
n
is odd and depends on the choice of an equivalence class of Whittaker datum for
{\rm U}(V_{K})
when
n
is even. In that case, we have seen in
[
GGP1
]
that the equivalence classes of Whittaker data are parameterized by additive characters of
K
modulo the translation action of
N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
. We shall use the Whittaker datum associated to
\psi_{K}=\psi\circ{\rm Tr}_{K/F}
.

Recall that an L-parameter
M
is generic if the adjoint L-factor
L(s,M,{\rm Ad})
is holomorphic at
s=1
. In that case, the L-packet
\Pi_{M}
contains a unique representation which is generic with respect to the Whittaker datum associated to
\psi\circ{\rm Tr}_{L/K}
. This representation corresponds to the trivial character of
A_{M}
under the bijection
J
.

8.4.
Asai factors

We recall from
[
GGP1
]
the notion of Asai L-factors and
\epsilon
-factors associated to a representation
M
of
WD_{L}
relative to the quadratic extension
L/E
.
If
\tau
denotes the nontrivial element of
{\rm Aut}(L/E)\cong{\rm Aut}(K/F)
, the representation
M\otimes M^{\tau}
is
\tau
-invariant and hence we have a decomposition

{\rm Ind}_{WD_{L}}^{WD_{E}}(M\otimes M^{\tau})={\rm As}_{L/E}^{+}(M)\oplus{\rm As}_{L/E}^{-}(M)

of
WD_{E}
-modules, with
{\rm As}_{L/E}^{\pm}(M)\cong M\otimes M^{\tau}
as
WD_{L}
-modules. On
{\rm As}_{L/E}^{+}(M)
, an element
s\in W_{E}\setminus W_{L}
acts by
v\otimes w\mapsto w\otimes s^{2}\cdot v
, whereas on
{\rm As}_{L/E}^{-}(M)
, this action is twisted by the nontrivial character of
W_{E}/W_{L}
(see
[
GGP1
, Pg. 26-27]
), thus

{\rm As}_{L/E}^{-}(M)={\rm As}_{L/E}^{+}(M)\cdot\omega_{L/E}
.

We record here some useful properties of the functor
{\rm As}^{\pm}_{L/E}
. Later we will deal
exclusively with
{\rm As}^{+}_{L/E}
, dropping the sign
+
.

Lemma 8.2
.

One has:

(a)

If
M=\oplus_{i}M_{i}
, then

{\rm As}_{L/E}^{\epsilon}(M)=\bigoplus_{i}{\rm As}_{L/E}^{\epsilon}(M_{i})\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}{\rm Ind}_{L}^{E}(M_{i}\otimes M_{j}^{\tau}).

(b)

{\rm As}^{\epsilon}_{L/E}(M)^{\vee}\cong{\rm As}^{\epsilon}_{L/E}(M^{\vee})
, where
M^{\vee}
denotes the dual of
M
.

(c)

{\rm As}^{\epsilon}_{L/E}(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})\cong{\rm As}^{\epsilon}_{L/E}(M_{1})\otimes{\rm As}^{\epsilon}_{L/E}(M_{2})
.

(d)

If
\dim M=1
, in which case
M
is treated as a character of
WD_{L}^{ab}=L^{\times}
,

{\rm As}^{+}_{L/E}(M)
is the restriction of
M
from
L^{\times}
to
E^{\times}
.

(e)

As a character of
WD^{ab}_{E}\cong E^{\times}
,

\det({\rm As}^{+}_{L/E}(M))={\rm As}^{+}(\det(M))^{n}\cdot\omega_{L/E}^{n(n-1)/2}=\det(M)|^{n}_{E^{\times}}\cdot\omega_{L/E}^{n(n-1)/2}

where
n=\dim M
.

(f)

If
M
is an L-parameter of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
and hence is conjugate-dual (with respect to
L/K
) of sign
(-1)^{n-1}
, then
{\rm As}^{\pm}_{L/E}(M)
is necessarily conjugate-orthogonal relative to
E/F
.

8.5.
Conjectures

We come now to the restriction problem to be studied. For each of the two skew-Hermitian spaces
V
over
E
, we have the Weil representation
\omega_{V,\psi,\mu}
, where
\mu
is a conjugate-symplectic character of
E^{\times}
. Then we are interested in determining

m_{V}(\pi,\mu):=\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\quad\text{ for $\pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))$.}

Here is our main local conjecture for arbitrary separable quadratic extensions
E,K
of
F
, subsuming the earlier Conjecture

2.1
(for the case
E=K
):

Conjecture 8.3
.

(i)

For each
\pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))
,

m_{V}(\pi,\mu)=\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\leq 1.

(ii)

Let
M
be a generic L-parameter of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
with associated L-packet
\Pi_{M}\subset{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))
. Then

\sum_{V}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{M}}m_{V}(\pi,\mu)=1

where the first sum runs over the two skew-Hermitian spaces over
E
of dimension
n
and the second runs over the L-packet
\Pi_{M}
.

(iii)

The unique
V_{0}
which has nonzero contribution to the sum in (ii) is characterized by

\mu(\det(V_{0}))=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det({\rm As}_{L/E}(M))(e)\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})^{n(n-1)/2}

where
e
is any nonzero trace 0 element of
E
, so that
E=F(e)
.

(iv)

The unique
\pi\in\Pi_{M}
which has nonzero contribution to the sum in (ii) corresponds via the bijection
J
to the character of the local component group
A_{M}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cdot a_{i}
given by:

\displaystyle\chi(a_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{L}^{E}(M_{i}^{\tau}\otimes(M/M_{i}))\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,[{\rm As}(M_{i})+{\rm As}(M)+{\rm As}(M/M_{i})]\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e}),

where
\psi_{E,e}
is the additive character of
E/F
defined by
\psi_{E,e}(x)=\psi(Tr(ex))
.

We make a few remarks on the above conjecture:

(a)

In (iii), the proposed expression for
\mu(\det(V_{0}))
is independent of the choice of the trace 0 element
e
. Moreover, using property (d) in §
8.4
and the fact that
\omega_{L/E}(e)=\omega_{K/F}(N_{E/F}(e))=\omega_{K/F}(-e^{2})
, the equation in (iii) can be explicated as:

\mu(\det(V_{0}))=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M)(e)^{n}\cdot\omega_{K/F}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}.

Though this may be more compact, our original expression has the advantage that it can be specialized to all possible situations for the pair
(E,K)
, as we shall explain below.

(b)

In (iii), observe that if
E=F(e)
and
K=F(k)
with
k\in K^{\times}
a trace zero element, then

\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})=(k^{2},e^{2})_{F}.

In particular, we see that this term only appears when
K
and
E
are both fields (as we are assuming in the conjecture).

(c)

The distinguished character
\chi
in (iv) is indeed trivial on the image of
-1_{M}
in
A_{M}
. Moreover, it is independent of the choice of the trace
0
element
e
. This follows from the fact that
({\rm As}(M_{i})+{\rm As}(M)+{\rm As}(M/M_{i}))\cdot\mu^{-1}
is an even-dimensional conjugate-symplectic representation of
WD_{E}
and hence its determinant is conjugate-orthogonal.

(d)

For the skew-Hermitian case considered in
[
GGP1
]
, we had defined a distinguished character
\chi
of the local component group which gives the unique representation in the L-packet with nonzero branching multiplicity. This distinguished character automatically picks out the skew-Hermitian space
V_{0}
over
E
which supports the nonzero multiplicity, so that (iii) is a consequence of (iv) in the original GGP setting.
In the case here, the distinguished character
\chi
in (iv) gives a representation of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
, but does not specify the
E
-space
V_{0}
. This is why the condition (iii) is needed.

8.6.
Specializations

Though we are assuming that
E\neq K
are distinct quadratic fields in this section, the formulas in Conjecture
8.3
(iii) and (iv) make sense for general
(E,K)
. For this, we need to explain how the L-parameter of
\Pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V_{K}))
gives rise to a representation of
WD_{L}
and how to interpret the Asai lift relative to
L/E
in the various situations.

•

E=K
is a field: this is the setting of §
2
. In this case,
L=E\otimes K
is isomorphic to
E\times E=K\times K
. Note however that the embeddings of
K
and
E
into
L
are different. The embedding of
K
into
L
is the diagonal embedding
x\mapsto(x,x)
, whereas that of
E
into
L
is
x\mapsto(x,x^{\sigma})
, where
{\rm Aut}(E/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. We interpret the Weil-Deligne group of
L
as
WD_{L}=WD_{K}\times WD_{K}=WD_{E}\times WD_{E}
.

Now given an irreducible representation
\Pi
of
{\rm U}(V_{K})={\rm GL}(V)
, its L-parameter
M
is an
n
-dimensional representation of
W_{K}=W_{E}
and this gives rise to the pair
(M,M^{\vee})
which we interpret as a representation of
WD_{L}
. Now the nontrivial element of
{\rm Aut}(L/E)
acts on
L=E\times E
via

(x,y)\mapsto(y^{\sigma},x^{\sigma})
. Thus its induced action on the representations of
WD_{L}
is
(M,M^{\vee})\mapsto({}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee},M^{\sigma})
(the switch, followed by the action of
\sigma
). We interpret the Asai lift as the tensor product representation
M\otimes{}^{\sigma}\!M^{\vee}
of
WD_{E}
.
With these interpretations, the formula in Conjecture
8.3
(iii) specializes to
that in Conjecture
2.1
(iii), in view of the remark (a) in §
8.5
above.

The issue addressed by Conjecture
8.3
(iv) is not relevant in this case since the L-packet of
{\rm U}(V_{K})={\rm GL}(V)
is a singleton. However, we note that with the above interpretations, the RHS of the formula there is equal to
1
.

•

E
is a field and
K=F\times F
, so that
L=K\otimes E=E\times E
and
WD_{L}=WD_{E}\times WD_{E}
: this is the original GGP situation. Then
{\rm U}(V_{K})\cong{\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(V)
and an irreducible representation of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
is of the form
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
with
\pi_{i}\in{\rm Irr}({\rm U}(V))
. The L-parameters of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are conjugate-dual representations
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of
WD_{E}
of sign
(-1)^{n-1}
, giving a representation
(M_{1},M_{2})
of
WD_{L}
. Now since
E
is embedded diagonally in
L=E\times E
, the nontrivial automorphism of
L/E
is the switch of the two factors of
E
in
L
. The Asai lift of
M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2}
from
L
to
E
is interpreted as the internal tensor product
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
. With these interpretations, the formula in Conjecture
8.3
(iii) reads:

\mu(\det(V_{0}))=\epsilon(1/2,M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})(e).

We leave it to the reader to verify that this reduces to the relevant conjecture in
[
GGP1
]
.

•

Compared to the other cases, a peculiarity of the original GGP situation is that
{\rm U}(V_{K})
and
{\rm U}(V^{\prime}_{K})
are not isomorphic when
V
and
V^{\prime}
are the two distinct skew-Hermitian spaces over
E
. Hence, one needs to choose and fix a quasi-split
{\rm U}(V_{K})
to formulate the LLC, before one can consider Conjecture
8.3
(iv). When
\dim V
is even, this choice is unique, but when
\dim V
is odd, this amounts to choosing a trace zero element
e_{0}\in E^{\times}
(the determinant of the distinguished
V
). Moreover, it is no longer the case that the character given in Conjecture
8.3
(iv) is independent of
e
when
\dim V
is odd (though it is still the case when
\dim V
is even).
Thus, in Conjecture
8.3
(iv), one needs to use the distinguished
e_{0}
in the definition of the character
\chi
when
\dim V
is odd. With this caveat, we leave it to the reader to verify that the formula for the character
\chi
in (iv) specializes to the one we had in
[
GGP1
]
.

•

E=F\times F
and
K
is a field, so that
L=K\times K
. Here,
{\rm U}(V)={\rm GL}(V)
and
{\rm U}(V_{K})={\rm GL}(V_{K})
. Given an irreducible generic representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GL}(V_{K})
,
and a conjugate-dual character
\mu=(\nu,\nu^{-1})
of
E^{\times}/F^{\times}=(F^{\times}\times F^{\times})/F^{\times}
, the multiplicity
\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})
should be always nonzero. So we expect the proposed identity in Conjecture
8.3
(iii) to always hold, after appropriate interpretations.

Now the L-parameter of
\Pi

is an
n
-dimensional representation
M
of
WD_{K}
. This gives rise to the pair
(M,M^{\vee})
which we regard as a representation of
WD_{L}=WD_{K}\times WD_{K}
.
The nontrivial automorphism of
L/E
is the component wise action of
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K/F)
on
L=K\times K
, so the Asai lift from
L
to
E
is the pair
({\rm As}_{K/F}(M),{\rm As}_{K/F}(M^{\vee}))
, regarded as a representation of
WD_{E}=WD_{F}\times WD_{F}
. In this case,

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M,M^{\vee})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{K/F}(M)\cdot\nu^{-1},\psi)\cdot\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{K/F}(M)^{\vee}\cdot\nu,\psi).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det({\rm As}_{K/F}(M))(-1)\cdot\nu(-1)^{n}

Moreover, an element
e\in E=F\times F
of trace
0
is of the form
(a,-a)
for
a\in F^{\times}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\det({\rm As}_{L/E}(M,M^{\vee}))(e)

\displaystyle=\det({\rm As}_{K/F}(M))(a)\cdot\det({\rm As}_{K/F}(M))(-a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\det({\rm As}_{K/F}(M))(-1)

and

\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})=\omega_{K/F}(a^{2})=1.

Thus, the RHS of the formula in (iii) is
\nu(-1)^{n}
, which is equal to the LHS.

There is also the case where
E=K=F\times F
, which we will leave to the reader. The main reason for formulating Conjecture
8.3
in a uniform way which allows for specialization to the various cases is that in the global setting to be considered in Section
11
, any one of these local scenarios will arise.

9.
Low Rank Evidences:
E\neq K

Just as for Conjecture
2.1
, we provide here some evidences for Conjecture
8.3
in low rank cases. In particular, we shall show:

Theorem 9.1
.

Conjecture
8.3
holds when
dimV\leq 2
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the verification of the theorem.

9.1.
Rank 1 case

Assume first that
V
is a skew-Hermitian space of dimension 1, so that
{\rm U}(V)=E_{1}\subset{\rm U}(V_{K})=L_{1}
, where
L_{1}
denotes the subgroup of elements
x\in L^{\times}
with
N_{L/K}(x)=1
.
Given a character
\chi
of
L_{1}
, choose an extension
\tilde{\chi}
of
\chi
to
L^{\times}
. Then
the L-parameter of
\chi
is the 1-dimensional conjugate-orthogonal representation
M=\tilde{\chi}/\tilde{\chi}^{\sigma}
of
W_{L}
.
By the theorem of Moen and Rogawski, we know that

{\rm Hom}_{E_{1}}(\chi,\omega_{V,\psi,\mu})\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow\epsilon(1/2,(\tilde{\chi}/\tilde{\chi}^{\sigma})|_{E^{\times}}\otimes\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\chi(-1)=\mu(\det(V)).

The local root number above can be written as

\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}),

whereas

\det({\rm As}(M))(e)=\tilde{\chi}(e)/\tilde{\chi}(e^{\sigma})=\chi(-1).

This shows Conjecture
8.3
when
n=\dim V=1
.

9.2.
Rank 2 case

Suppose now that
\dim V=2
. In this case, we need to verify the independent statements (iii) and (iv) of Conjecture
8.3
.
As we have noted before,
V=V_{B}
is associated with a quaternion
F
-algebra
B
, with

{\rm GU}(V_{B})\cong(B^{\times}\times E^{\times})/\Delta F^{\times}.

The embedding
{\rm GU}(V_{B})\hookrightarrow{\rm GU}(V_{B,K})
is the natural embedding

(B^{\times}\times E^{\times})/\Delta F^{\times}\hookrightarrow((B\otimes_{F}K)^{\times}\times L^{\times})/\Delta K^{\times},

with
B\otimes_{F}K\cong M_{2}(K)
.

A generic L-packet of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
is thus given by an irreducible representation

\Pi\boxtimes\chi\quad\text{ of ${\rm GL}_{2}(K)\times L^{\times}$,}

with
\omega_{\Pi}\cdot\chi|_{K^{\times}}=1
. If
P
is the L-parameter of
\Pi
, then the L-parameter of the corresponding L-packet of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
is the conjugate-symplectic (relative to
L/K
) representation

M=P|_{WD_{L}}\otimes\chi

of
WD_{L}
. On the other hand, the Weil representation
\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}[\chi|_{E^{\times}}]
of
{\rm U}(V_{B})
is an irreducible summand of the representation

\Sigma_{B,N}\otimes\chi

of
B^{\times}\times E^{\times}
restricted to
(B^{\times})^{+}\times E^{\times}
, where as in §
3.2
,
\Sigma_{B,N}
has L-parameter

N={\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(\mu\cdot\chi|_{E^{\times}}^{-1}).

The corresponding L-parameter of
{\rm U}(V_{B})
is the conjugate-symplectic (relative to
E/F
) representation

N|_{WD_{E}}\otimes\chi|_{E^{\times}}.

Now we consider the sum

(9.2)

\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{M}}\dim{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V_{B})}(\pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,B}).

Via the above identifications, one sees that this sum is simply

\dim{\rm Hom}_{(B^{\times})^{+}}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,B})=\dim{\rm Hom}_{B^{\times}}(\Pi,\Sigma_{B,N}).

In other words, we are reduced to a twisted trilinear form problem as in §
3.2
.
Hence, by a result of the third author, cf.
[
P1
]
, this dimension is at most
1
and is nonzero if and only if

(9.3)

\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{K/F}(P)\otimes{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(\mu^{-1}\cdot\chi|_{E^{\times}}),\psi)\cdot\omega_{K/F}(-1)=\mu(\det(V_{B})).

Now the local root number can be explicated as:

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{K/F}(P)\otimes{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}(\mu^{-1}\cdot\chi|_{E^{\times}}),\psi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}({\rm As}_{K/F}(P)|_{WD_{E}}\cdot\chi|_{E^{\times}}\cdot\mu^{-1}),\psi),

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{E}^{F}({\rm As}_{L/E}(P|_{WD_{L}}\cdot\chi)\cdot\mu^{-1}),\psi),

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(P|_{WD_{L}}\cdot\chi)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}),

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E}).

In the above, we have used the facts that

{\rm As}_{K/F}(P)|_{WD_{E}}\cong{\rm As}_{L/E}\left(P|_{WD_{L}}\right)

and

{\rm As}_{L/E}(P|_{WD_{L}})\otimes\chi|_{E^{\times}}={\rm As}_{L/E}(P|_{WD_{L}}\otimes\chi)={\rm As}_{L/E}(M).

On the other hand, with
n=2
,

\det({\rm As}_{L/E}(M))(e)^{n}\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})^{n(n-1)/2}=\det(M)(e)^{2}\cdot\omega_{L/E}(e)\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})=\omega_{K/F}(-1)

since
\det(M)
is conjugate-orthogonal and hence trivial on
e^{2}\in F^{\times}
, and
\omega_{L/E}(e)=\omega_{K/F}(-e^{2})
.
Hence, the equality (
9.3
) is precisely the statement of Conjecture
8.3
(iii).

We now come to Conjecture
8.3
(iv). Continuing with the analysis above, let us fix
V=V_{B}
such that (
9.3
) holds, so that the sum in (
9.2
) is equal to
1
, and we need to determine which element in the L-packet
\Pi_{M}
has nonzero contribution.
Now the members of the L-packet are given by the restriction of
\Pi
to
{\rm GL}_{2}(K)^{+}
. If this restriction is irreducible, then we leave it to the readers to convince themselves that Conjecture
8.3
(iv) holds. Let us examine the more intricate case when this restriction is the sum of two irreducible summands, i.e. when
\Pi
is dihedral with respect to
L/K
.
Thus we see that the problem at hand is a refined version of the twisted trilinear form problem, relative to the embedding
{\rm GL}_{2}(F)\subset{\rm GL}_{2}(K)^{+}
.

Since
\Pi
is dihedral with respect to
L/K
,
P|_{WD_{L}}
is reducible and so is
M=P|_{WD_{L}}\cdot\chi
.
To understand the L-packet, we shall return to the setting of unitary groups, as
\Pi_{M}
can be constructed via theta lifting from rank 1 skew-Hermitian spaces.

9.3.
Unitary theta lifts

Let
M=M_{1}+M_{2}
be an L-parameter of
{\rm U}(V_{B})(K)

with
M_{1}
and
M_{2}

\Pi_{M}
has 2 representations of

{\rm U}(V_{B})(K)

\pi^{+}

\pi^{-}

of

so that
\pi^{+}
is generic with respect to the Whittaker datum determined by
\psi_{K}=\psi\circ{\rm Tr}_{K/F}
. Note that by Lemma
8.1
,

{\rm U}(V_{B})(K)
is always the quasi-split unitary group in two variables, so the representations
on the anisotropic form of
{\rm U}(V_{B})(K)
does not arise in our considerations.
We shall explain how these representations
\pi^{\pm}

can be constructed as theta lifts from
{\rm U}_{1}
.

Let
W^{\pm}
be the two rank 1 Hermitian spaces over
L
with
\omega_{L/K}({\rm disc}(W^{\pm}))=\pm 1
. In particular, the Hermitian form on
W^{+}
is
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y^{\tau}
, with
{\rm Gal}(L/K)=\langle\tau\rangle
. Then for
\epsilon=\pm 1
,
{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})\times{\rm U}(V_{K})

is a reductive dual pair where
V_{K}=V_{B}\otimes_{F}K
.
Likewise, we may consider the rank 2 Hermitian space

W^{\epsilon}_{E}:={\rm Res}_{L/E}(W^{\epsilon})\quad\text{with Hermitian form ${\rm Tr}_{L/E}(-,-)_{W^{\epsilon}}$.}

This rank 2 Hermitian space over
E
has discriminant

{\rm disc}(W^{\epsilon}_{E})=N_{K/F}(k\cdot{\rm disc}(W^{\epsilon}))\in F^{\times}/N(E^{\times})

where
k\in K^{\times}
is any trace 0 element; we leave the verification of this to the reader.
Then
{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon}_{E})\times{\rm U}(V)
is a reductive dual pair, and we have the seesaw diagram:

\textstyle{{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon}_{E})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm U}(V_{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm U}(V).}

To consider the theta correspondences for these two dual pairs, we need to select splitting characters in each case, and to obtain a seesaw identity from the seesaw diagram, we need to select these two sets of splitting characters compatibly. With the goal of obtaining the L-packet
\Pi_{M}
of
{\rm U}(V_{K})
as theta lifts from
{\rm U}(W^{\pm})
, we shall select these splitting characters as follows:

•

recall that
M_{1}
is a conjugate symplectic character of
L^{\times}
relative to
L/K
. Then its restriction
M_{1}|_{E^{\times}}
is a conjugate-orthogonal character of
E^{\times}
relative to
E/F
(because
F^{\times}\subset N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
).

•

For the equal rank dual pair
{\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(W_{E})
over
F
, we use the pair of splitting characters
(M_{1}|_{E^{\times}},M_{1}|_{E^{\times}})
, and the additive character
\psi
of
F
.

•

For the almost equal rank dual pair
{\rm U}(V_{K})\times{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})
over
K
, we use the pair
(M_{1},M_{1}\circ N_{L/E})=(M_{1},M_{1}\cdot M_{1}^{\tau})
and the character
\psi_{K}
of
K
.

With these splitting characters and additive characters fixed, one can consider the associated theta correspondences for the two dual pairs. Moreover, one has the seesaw identity associated to the above seesaw diagram. For this, one needs to specify the irreducible representations one starts with on
{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})
and
{\rm U}(V)
.

(i)

For the dual pair
{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})\times{\rm U}(V_{K})
, if one starts with the character
\chi_{M_{1}^{\tau}M_{2}}
of
{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})
with L-parameter

{\rm U}(V_{K})
has L-parameter
M=M_{1}+M_{2}
. As
\epsilon
varies over
\pm
, the two representations so obtained are the elements
\pi^{\epsilon}
of the L-packet
\Pi_{M}
.

(ii)

For the dual pair
{\rm U}(V)\times{\rm U}(W_{E})
, we start with the Weil representation
\omega_{\psi,\mu,V}[\chi_{M_{1}M_{2}}]
of
{\rm U}(V)
whose central character is the character
\chi_{M_{1}M_{2}}
of
E_{1}
with L-parameter
M_{1}M_{2}
and whose L-parameter is

. Its theta lift to
{\rm U}(W_{E})

From the seesaw identity, we see that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi^{\epsilon},\omega_{\psi,\mu,V})\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(W^{\epsilon})}(\Theta(\omega_{\psi,\mu,V}[\chi_{M_{1}M_{2}}]),\chi_{M_{1}^{\tau}M_{2}}),

so that

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi^{\epsilon},\omega_{\psi,\mu,V})\neq 0\Longrightarrow\Theta(\omega_{\psi,\mu,V}[\chi_{M_{1}M_{2}}])\neq 0.

By the theta dichotomy theorem
[
HKS

GI

\omega_{E/F}(-k^{2})\cdot\epsilon=\omega_{E/F}({\rm disc}(W_{E}^{\epsilon}))=\epsilon(1/2,N\cdot M_{1}|_{E^{\times}}^{-1},\psi_{E,e})\cdot\mu(\det(V)).

The local root number on the RHS is equal to

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{1})^{-1}\cdot\mu,\psi_{E,e})\cdot\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{2})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{1})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})\cdot\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{2})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)

On the other hand, by conjecture

(iii), which we have demonstrated above, we know that

\displaystyle\mu(\det(V))

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\omega_{K/F}(-1)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2}).

Assembling these together, we see that

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,[{\rm As}_{L/E}M_{1}+{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{2})+{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)]\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e})\cdot\omega_{E/F}(k^{2})\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,[{\rm As}_{L/E}M_{1}+{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{2})+{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)]\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e}),

as predicted by Conjecture
8.3
(iv),
where for the second equality, we have used:

\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})=(k^{2},e^{2})_{F}=\omega_{E/F}(k^{2}).

Note that by Lemma
8.2

\epsilon=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{L}^{E}(M_{1}^{\tau}\cdot M_{2})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E,e}).

to obtain the representation

\pi_{X}
of
{\rm U}(V)
supported on the
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit of

.

After the above generalities, we now consider different cases according to the types of

.

Case 1:

X\cap X^{\tau}\not=0
.

The space
X\cap X^{\tau}
is defined over

, so let
Y\subset V
be such that
Y\otimes_{E}L=X\cap X^{\tau}
. The space

is
isotropic, and it is easy to see that
Q_{X}
has the following properties:

(1)

The unipotent radical
N_{Q_{X}}
of

contains the center
Z_{Y}
of the
unipotent radical
N_{Y}
of the parabolic subgroup
P_{Y}\subset{\rm U}(V)
stabilizing the isotropic subspace
Y\subset V
; observe that
Z_{Y}
is the subgroup of
Q_{X}
acting trivially on

.

(2)

The natural map

Q_{X}\rightarrow{\rm GL}(X)\times{\rm U}(X^{\perp}/X)

has kernel
Z_{Y}
. Consider the composite map

Q_{X}\rightarrow Q_{X}/Z_{Y}\rightarrow{\rm GL}(X)\times{\rm U}(X^{\perp}/X)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(X).

Its image is contained in the parabolic subgroup
P_{d,a-d}
stabilizing the subspace
X\cap X^{\tau}\subset X
, and contains the subgroup
{\rm GL}(Y)\subset{\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\tau})
.
Moreover, the image of
N_{Q_{X}}/Z_{Y}
is the unipotent radical of this parabolic subgroup of
{\rm GL}(X)
.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.8
, these properties and Lemma
4.7
imply that:

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{i}(\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[{\rm ind}_{Q_{X}}^{{\rm U}(V)}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P_{X}/Q_{X}}^{1/2}),\omega_{V,\psi}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{i}_{Q_{X}/Z_{Y}}[\delta_{Q_{X}}^{-1/2}(\omega_{V,\psi^{-}})_{Z_{Y}},(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\otimes\delta_{P_{X}/Q_{X}}^{1/2})^{\vee}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{i}_{Q_{X}/N_{Q_{X}}}[(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}\otimes(\pi_{2})\otimes\delta_{P_{X}/Q_{X}}^{1/2}),\delta_{Q_{X}}^{1/2}\cdot|\det|^{-d/2}\cdot\alpha^{-1}\cdot\omega_{n-2a,\psi}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{i}_{Q_{X}/N_{Q_{X}}}(A,B),

where
(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}
denotes the un-normalized Jacquet module of
\pi_{1}
with respect to the
parabolic subgroup
P_{d,a-d}
of
{\rm GL}(X)\cong{\rm GL}_{a}(L)
stabilizing
X\cap X^{\perp}
.
Note also that at elements
(g,1)\in{\rm GL}_{d}(L)\times{\rm GL}_{a-d}(L)
. one has

\delta_{P/Q}(g,1)=|\det(g)|^{a}
and
\delta_{Q}(g,1)=|\det(g)|^{n-2a}
,

and

\displaystyle(\pi_{1})_{d,a-d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{P_{d,a-d}}^{(1+\epsilon)/2}\times{\rm tempered\penalty\ \penalty\ representation\penalty\ \penalty\ of\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\rm GL}_{d}(L)\times{\rm GL}_{a-d}(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\det(g)|^{(d-a+\epsilon)/2}\times{\rm tempered\penalty\ \penalty\ representation\penalty\ \penalty\ of\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\rm GL}_{d}(L)\times{\rm GL}_{a-d}(L).

Here we have used the fact that the normalized Jacquet module of
a tempered representation is a tempered representation up to multiplication by a character
\delta_{P}^{\epsilon/2}
for some
positive real number
\epsilon
.

To show the vanishing of
{\rm Ext}^{i}(A,B)
, we note that

Q_{X}/N_{Q_{X}}
contains
{\rm GL}(Y)
as a direct factor and the above information allows one to see that
the center of
{\rm GL}(Y)
acts on the two representations
A
and
B
via different characters. Therefore, we have shown that

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi}]=0\quad\text{ for all $i\geq 0$.}

Case 2:

X\cap X^{\tau}=0
, but
Z_{0}=X\cap X^{\tau\perp}\not=0
.

In this case,
X+X^{\tau}
is a degenerate
skew-Hermitian space defined over
E
whose nullspace is
Z_{0}+Z_{0}^{\tau}
, i.e.

Z_{0}+Z_{0}^{\tau}=(X+X^{\tau})\cap(X+X^{\tau})^{\perp}.

Let
Z
be the subspace of
V
such that
Z\otimes L=Z_{0}+Z_{0}^{\tau}
, so that

Z
is an isotropic subspace of
V
. In this case, it is easy to see that the subgroup
Q_{X}
of

{\rm U}(V)
preserving

has the following properties:

(1)

Q_{X}
contains the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup of
{\rm U}(V)
stabilizing the isotropic subspace
Z\subset V
.

(2)

The image of the natural map from
Q_{X}
to
{\rm GL}(X)

given as the composite:

Q_{X}\rightarrow{\rm GL}(X)\times{\rm U}(X^{\perp}/X)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(X)

lands inside
the parabolic subgroup defined by the subspace
Z_{0}=X\cap X^{\tau\perp}\subset X
, containing the unipotent radical of this parabolic subgroup
of
{\rm GL}(X)
, as well as

{\rm GL}(X\cap X^{\tau\perp})
.

A similar analysis as in Case 1 (based on appropriate central character analysis) allows us to conclude that:

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{{\rm U}(V)}[\pi_{X},\omega_{V,\psi}]=0,

for all
i\geq 0
.

Case 3:
Both
X\cap X^{\tau}=0
, and
X\cap X^{\tau\perp}=0
.

In this case,
the
{\rm U}(V)
-orbit of
X
is open. Such isotropic spaces
X\subset V_{K}
, up to
{\rm U}(V)
-conjugacy, are in bijective correspondence with
{\rm U}(V)
-conjugacy classes of non-degenerate subspaces
W\subset V
of dimension
2a
,
since such a subspace
W
has, up to
{\rm U}(W)
conjugacy, a unique subspace
X\subset W_{K}
such that

X\cap X^{\tau}=0
and
X\cap X^{\tau\perp}=0
(the proof of this is given in Lemma
10.2
below).

Now let
Q_{X}
be the stabilizer of
X
in
{\rm U}(V)
.
The following lemma allows us to determine this stabilizer:

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
W
be a
2a
-dimensional nondegenerate skew-Hermitian space over
E
and let
X\subset W\otimes_{F}K=W_{K}
be an isotropic subspace of
W_{K}
such that

X\cap X^{\tau}=0
and
X+X^{\tau}=W_{K}
,

where we recall that

{\rm Gal}(K/F)=\langle\tau\rangle
.
Then we have:

(i)

The isotropic subspace
X\subset W_{K}
with the above properties is unique up to the action of
{\rm U}(W)
on
W_{K}
;

(ii)

The stabilizer of
X
in
{\rm U}(W)
is isomorphic to
{\rm U}(W_{X})
,
where
W_{X}
is the skew-Hermitian space on the underlying vector space
X
relative to
L/E^{\prime}
(for

E^{\prime}
the third quadratic field contained in the biquadratic extension
L=E\otimes K
), defined by:

(x_{1},x_{2})=\langle x_{1},\tau x_{2}\rangle.

(iii)

The determinant of the
2a
-dimensional skew-Hermitian space
W
for
E/F
and the
a
-dimensional
skew-Hermitian space
W_{X}
for
L/E^{\prime}
are related by (as elements of
F^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times})
):

\det(W)=N_{L/E}[k^{a}\det(W_{X})]=(-k^{2})^{a}N_{L/E}\det(W_{X}),

where
k
is any nonzero element of
K
whose trace to
F
is zero.

Further, the restriction of the Weil representation
\omega_{W,\psi,\mu}
of
{\rm U}(W)
to
U(W_{X})
is the Weil representation of
\omega_{W_{X},\psi\circ Tr_{E^{\prime}/F},\mu\circ N_{L/E}}
.

Proof.

(i) Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be two
L
-vector subspaces of
W_{K}
satisfying the properties in the Lemma. Let

\phi:X\longrightarrow X^{\prime}

be a
L
-linear isomorphism of vector spaces.
Then
\phi
extends uniquely to a
L
-linear automorphism (still denoted by
\phi
) of
X+X^{\tau}=W_{K}
defined by

\phi(x^{\tau})=\phi(x)^{\tau},\quad\text{ for $x\in X$.}

Since this satisfies
\phi\circ\tau=\tau\phi
, it follows by Galois descent that
\phi
is defined over
E
, i.e
\phi
is obtained by base change from an
E
-linear isomorphism

\phi_{0}:W\longrightarrow W.

Moreover, one checks by a direct computation that
\phi_{0}
preserves the given skew-Hermitian structure on
W
if and only if
\phi
is compatible with the
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian structure on
X
and
X^{\prime}
defined in (ii), i.e.
\phi
is an isomorphism of
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian spaces:

\phi:W_{X}\longrightarrow W_{X^{\prime}}.

Hence, to show that there is an element of
{\rm U}(W)
which carries
X
to
X^{\prime}
, it remains to show that
W_{X}
and
W_{X^{\prime}}
are necessarily isomorphic as
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian spaces. We shall show this and hence complete the proof of (i) only after we demonstrate (iii), using Lemma
10.3
below.

(ii) Taking
X^{\prime}=X
in (i) above, one deduces that the stabilizer of
X
in
{\rm U}(W)
is precisely
{\rm U}(W_{X})
.

(iii) For the
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian space
W_{X}
defined in (ii),
there is a natural
E/F
skew-Hermitian structure on
W_{X}
obtained by
taking the same vector space as
W_{X}
, now treated as an
E
-vector space and denoted by
R_{E}(W_{X})
, with the skew-Hermitian form which
is the
L/E
-trace of the skew-Hermitian form on
W_{X}
.
Define a map
\phi:X\rightarrow W
by

\phi(x)=x+x^{\tau}\in W\quad\text{ for $x\in X$.}

It is easy to check that
\phi
induces an isomorphism of the
E/F
skew-Hermitian spaces
R_{E}(W_{X})
and
W
. Now we appeal to the Lemma
10.3
below to complete the proof of (iii).

Lemma 10.3
.

With the quadratic extensions
E,K,E^{\prime}
of
F
as before, let
\mathcal{W}
be an
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian space with a skew-Hermitian form
\langle-,-\rangle
.
Let
R_{E}(\mathcal{W})
be the same space
\mathcal{W}
regarded as a vector space over
E
,
which comes equipped with a natural
E/F
skew-Hermitian structure
(-,-)
:

(w_{1},w_{2})=\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle+\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle^{\tau}.

Fix an element
k\in K^{\times}
with
{\rm Tr}_{K/F}(k)=0
. Then, with
a=\dim\mathcal{W}
, one has:

k^{a}\det\mathcal{W}\in E^{\prime\times},

and

N_{L/E}(k^{a}\det\mathcal{W}){=}N_{E^{\prime}/F}(k^{a}\det\mathcal{W})=\det R_{E}(\mathcal{W}),

as elements of
F^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times})
.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\ncong\mathcal{W}
is the other
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian space space of the same dimension, then
R_{E}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime})\ncong R_{E}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By writing
\mathcal{W}
as
an orthogonal sum of lines over
L
, we are reduced to prove the Lemma for a 1-dimensional skew-Hermitian space
for
L/E^{\prime}
which we take to be the vector space
L
with the skew-Hermitian structure:

\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle=\ell_{1}x\ell_{2}^{\sigma\tau},

with
x\in L^{\times}
with
x+x^{\sigma\tau}=0
.

This gives rise to an
E/F
skew-Hermitian structure on
L
by:

(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle+\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle^{\tau}=\ell_{1}x\ell_{2}^{\sigma\tau}+\ell_{1}^{\tau}x^{\tau}\ell_{2}^{\sigma}.

For this
E/F
-skew-Hermitian space
L
,
\{1,k\}
is a basis, for which the Gram matrix is given by

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x+x^{\tau}&-k(x-x^{\tau})\\
k(x-x^{\tau})&-k^{2}(x+x^{\tau})\end{array}\right),

so that

\det A=-4k^{2}xx^{\tau}.

Now since
(kx)^{\sigma\tau}=kx
, we see that
kx
belongs to
E^{\prime\times}
, as desired.

For the final statement, it suffices to show that

\det(R_{E}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}))\neq\det(R_{E}(\mathcal{W}))\in F^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times}),

or equivalently that

\omega_{E/F}(\det(R_{E}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}))\cdot det(R_{E}(\mathcal{W})))=-1.

By the identity proved above, this is equivalent to showing that

(\omega_{E/F}\circ N_{E^{\prime}/F})\left(\det(\mathcal{W}^{\prime})\cdot\det(\mathcal{W})\right)=-1.

But this desired identity holds since

\omega_{E/F}\circ N_{E^{\prime}/F}=\omega_{L/E^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
10.3
.
∎

As we mentioned, Lemma
10.3
completes the proof of (iii). The last assertion in Lemma
10.3
also allows us to complete the proof of (i). Indeed, in the proof of (iii), we have shown that
R_{E}(W_{X})\cong W
as
E/F
-skew-Hermitian spaces. Hence, with
X
and
X^{\prime}
as in the proof of (i), we deduce that
R_{E}(W_{X})\cong R_{E}(W_{X^{\prime}})
. In view of the last assertion in Lemma
10.3
, one thus deduces that
W_{X}\cong W_{X^{\prime}}
as
L/E^{\prime}
-skew-Hermitian spaces.

Finally, we observe that the restriction of the Weil representations made in Lemma
10.2
is the
precise version of the well-known assertion that the restriction of a Weil representation of
{\rm Sp}(4n,F)
to
{\rm Sp}(2n,E^{\prime})
takes
a Weil representation of
{\rm Sp}(4n,F)
to a Weil representation of
{\rm Sp}(2n,E^{\prime})
. This completes the proof of Lemma
10.2
.
∎

Applying Lemma
10.2
, we find

Q_{X}\cong{\rm U}(W_{X})\times{\rm U}(W_{X}^{\perp})\subset{\rm U}(V)

with
\dim W_{X}=2a
and
\dim W_{X}^{\perp}=n-2a
.
We can now conclude the proof as in Theorem
4.8
.

The proof of Theorem
10.1
is now complete.

∎

10.3.
Unitary principal series

Using Proposition
10.6
and Theorem
10.1
, we can study the restriction of a unitary principal series

\Pi={\rm Ind}_{B}^{{\rm U}(V_{K})}\chi

to
{\rm U}(V)
and show:

Theorem 10.7
.

Conjecture
8.3
(i)-(iii) hold
for the tempered L-packet
consisting of the constituents of the unitary principal series representation
\Pi
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to that of Corollary
5.3
and it will be convenient to treat the cases of even or odd
\dim V
separately.
We shall only write down the details for the case of even
\dim V
, leaving the odd case as an exercise for the interested reader.

Assume thus that
\dim V=n=2d
is even, so that

\Pi={\rm Ind}_{B}^{{\rm U}(V_{K})}(\chi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\chi_{d})

for some unitary characters
\chi_{i}
of
L^{\times}
.
By Theorem
10.1
, we see that

(10.8)

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,V})\cong\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}}\bigotimes_{i}{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{i})}(\chi_{i},\omega_{\psi,\mu,L_{i}^{E}})

where the sum runs over
V

\mathcal{L}
’s and we have written
L_{i}^{E}
for
{\rm Res}_{L/E}(L_{i})
.

We thus need to analyze the nonvanishing of
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{i})}(\chi_{i},\omega_{\psi,\mu,L_{i}^{E}})
.
As in the
E=K
case, this comes down to an application of the theorem of Moen and Rogawski, i.e. Theorem
3.1
.
Indeed, by the functorial property of the Weil representation, the restriction of
\omega_{\psi,\mu,L_{i}^{E}}
of
{\rm U}(L_{i})
is simply the Weil representation

\omega_{\psi_{K},\mu\circ N_{L/E},L_{i}}
of
{\rm U}(L_{i})
. Hence

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(L_{i})}(\chi_{i},\omega_{\psi,\mu,L_{i}^{E}})\neq 0

if and only if

(10.9)

\mu(N_{L/E}(\det(L_{i})))=\chi_{i}(-1)\cdot\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\tau\sigma}\cdot(\mu\circ N_{L/E})^{-1},\psi_{L}),

where the local root number is considered over
L
.

Hence, we see that at most one
\mathcal{L}
in (
10.8
) has nonzero contribution, and this
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{i}\}
is characterized by having (
10.9
) holding for all
i
.
By Lemma
10.3
,

\det(L_{i}^{E})=N_{L/E}(k\cdot\det(L_{i}))\in F^{\times}/N_{E/F}(E^{\times})

where
k\in K_{0}^{\times}
. Thus, if
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm U}(V)}(\Pi,\omega_{\psi,\mu,V})\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\mu(\det(V))

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\mu(\det(L_{i}^{E}))

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\mu(N_{L/E}(k\cdot\det(L_{i}))

(10.10)

\displaystyle=\omega_{E/F}(-k^{2})^{d}\cdot\prod_{i}\chi_{i}(-1)\cdot\prod_{i}\epsilon(1/2,\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\tau\sigma}\cdot(\mu\circ N_{L/E})^{-1},\psi_{L}),

with the last equality following by (

).

On the other hand, according to Conjecture
8.3
(iii), one should expect that

\mu(\det(V))=\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})\cdot\det({\rm As}(M))(e)\cdot\omega_{K/F}(e^{2})^{n(n-1)/2}

where

is the L-parameter of

in (

).

By Lemma

(a),

{\rm As}_{L/E}(M)=\bigoplus_{i}{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{i})\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}{\rm Ind}_{L}^{E}(M_{i}^{\tau}\otimes M_{j}),

with

and it follows that

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}_{L/E}(M_{i})\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(-1)\cdot\omega_{E/F}(-1)\cdot\epsilon(1/2,{\rm Ind}_{L}^{E}\chi_{i}/\chi_{i}^{\tau\sigma}\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})

(2)

\displaystyle\epsilon(1/2,{\rm As}(M)\cdot\mu^{-1},\psi_{E})=

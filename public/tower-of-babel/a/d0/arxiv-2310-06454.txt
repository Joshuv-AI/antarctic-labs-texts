Abstract

We prove unit-free versions of both the associative and the non-associative Vidav–Palmer theorems. Then these results are applied to prove a unit-free version of the Blecher–Ruan–Sinclair non-associative characterization of unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

Introduction

Let
X
be a normed space over

\mathbb{K}={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
u
be a norm-one element in
X

becomes a non-empty

. Given

of

(X,u)
as the non-empty compact convex

\mathbb{K}

In the case that

is

(X,u)
if

, and we denote by

(X,u)

To be precise, let us say that, as in
[
14

15

Following

{\bf 1}

-algebra
we mean a norm-unital normed complex algebra

Now let us deal with the Blecher–Ruan–Sinclair non-associative characterization of unital

Let

be a vector space over
{\mathbb{K}}

an

\alpha x
and

(for

formed by left and right multiplication
of

in the obvious sense of matrix multiplication.
Given

, and

,
we denote by

the element of

whose principal
diagonal blocks (from top to the bottom) are

(in

) at each entry. In what follows,

will be seen endowed with the
involution and the corresponding

-norm

|\cdot|_{n}
deriving from its natural
identification with the algebra of all linear operators on the

.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
A
be a normed complex algebra. We say that
A

enjoys a matricial
L_{\infty}
-structure

if, for each

, the complex algebra

is equipped with an algebra
norm
\|\cdot\|_{n}
in such a way that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\|\cdot\|_{1}=\|\cdot\|
on
M_{1}(A)=A
,

(ii)

\|\alpha a\beta\|_{n}\leq|\alpha|_{n}\|a\|_{n}|\beta|_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
a\in M_{n}(A)
, and

\alpha,\beta\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
,

(iii)

\|b\oplus c\|_{n+m}=\max\{\|b\|_{n},\|c\|_{m}\}
for all
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

, and
c\in M_{m}(A)
.

A less demanding situation is that of the existence of an algebra norm

on

satisfying

(iv)

\|\alpha a\beta\|_{2}\leq|\alpha|_{2}\|a\|_{2}|\beta|_{2}
for every

(v)

In this case we say that

L_{\infty}^{2}
-structure
. Let us finally recall that, according to

2
The unit-free version of the Vidav–Palmer theorem

Given an element
a
in an algebra
A
, we denote by
L_{a}^{A}
(respectively
R_{a}^{A}
) the operator of left (respectively, right) multiplication by
a
on
A
. Now let
A
be a normed algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
By a
left approximate identity
in
A
we mean a net
u_{\lambda}
in
A
such that
\lim_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}a=a
for every
a\in A
.
Right approximate identities
in
A
are defined analogously, and (two-sided)
approximate identities
in
A
are defined as those nets in
A
which are both left and right approximate identities in
A
. If
A\neq 0
, then we define the
left operator numerical range
of an element
a\in A
relative to
A
by
LOV(A,a):=V(BL(A),I_{A},L_{a}^{A})
.

The key tool in this section is the following lemma, most part of which was proved in
[
35
]
under the unnecessary requirements that the normed algebra is complete and complex.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a nonzero normed algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
, and let
a_{\lambda}

be an approximate identity bounded by
1
in
A
. Consider
A^{\prime\prime}
as a
complete normed algebra under the Arens product, let
{\bf 1}

be a
w^{*}
-cluster point in
A^{\prime\prime}
to the net
a_{\lambda}
, and set
B:=\mathbb{K}{\bf 1}+A\subseteq A^{\prime\prime}
. Then we
have:

(i)

B
becomes a norm-unital
subalgebra of
A^{\prime\prime}
with unit
{\bf 1}
.

(ii)

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
and
a\in A
the equality
\|\alpha{\bf 1}+a\|=\lim_{\lambda}\|\alpha a_{\lambda}+a\|
holds.

(iii)

For
a\in A
the equality
LOV(A,a)=V(B,{\bf 1},a)
holds.

Proof.

Considering
[
35
]
(cf.
[
14
, Lemma 3.5.46]
), it only remains to prove assertion (iii). To this end, we keep in mind assertion (i), and argue as follows.

Let
a
be in
A
. Then, by
[
14
, Lemmas 2.2.24 and 2.1.10]
, we have

LOV(A,a)\subseteq LOV(B,a)=V(B,{\bf 1},a).

On the other hand, since
{\bf 1}
is a
w^{*}
-cluster point in
A^{\prime\prime}
to the net
a_{\lambda}
, and
(L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime}:A^{\prime\prime}\to A^{\prime\prime}
extends
L_{a}^{A}
and is
w^{*}
-continuous, and
a_{\lambda}

is an approximate identity in
A
, we realize that
(L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime}({\bf 1})=a
. Therefore
B
is invariant under
(L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime}
, and
((L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime})_{|B}
(regarded as a mapping from
B
to
B
) is equal to
L_{a}^{B}
. It follows from
[
14
, Lemmas 2.1.10 and 2.2.24, and Corollary 2.1.3]
that

LOV(B,a)=V(BL(B),I_{B},((L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime})_{|B})

\subseteq V(BL(A^{\prime\prime}),I_{A^{\prime\prime}},(L_{a}^{A})^{\prime\prime})=LOV(A,a).

Thus, by means of the double inclusion, the equality
LOV(A,a)=V(B,{\bf 1},a)

has been proved. Since
a
is arbitrary in
A
, the proof of assertion (iii) and of the lemma is concluded.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a
nonzero
normed complex algebra. We say that an element
a\in A
is
left operator hermitian
if
L_{a}^{A}
belongs to
H(BL(A),I_{A})
, and denote by
LOH(A)
the closed real subspace of
A
consisting of all left operator hermitian elements of
A
. If
A
is equal to the complex linear hull of
LOH(A)
, then we say that
A
is a
left operator
V
-algebra.

The notion of a left operator
V
-algebra, just introduced, is a weakening of other notions previously appeared in the literature (see
[
36
, Definition 3.8]
and references therein).

Lemma 2.3
.

The following assertions hold:

(i)

For a norm-unital complex algebra
A
, the equality
LOH(A)=H(A,{\bf 1})
holds, and hence
V
-algebras are precisely the norm-unital left operator
V
-algebras.

(ii)

If
A
is a left operator
V
-algebra having a right approximate identity, then the equality
A=LOH(A)\oplus iLOH(A)
holds,
and hence
A
is endowed with a unique conjugate-linear vector
space involution
*
such that the set of all
*
-invariant elements of
A
coincides with
LOH(A)
.

Proof.

Assertion (i) follows from
[
14
, Lemma 2.1.10]
.

Now let
A
be a nonzero normed complex algebra having a right approximate identity (say
a_{\lambda}
), and let
a
be in
LOH(A)\cap iLOH(A)
. Then
L_{a}^{A}
belongs to
H(BL(A),I_{A})\cap iH(BL(A),I_{A})
, and hence, by
[
14
, Corollary 2.1.13]
,
L_{a}^{A}=0
. Therefore
a=\lim_{\lambda}aa_{\lambda}=\lim_{\lambda}L_{a}^{A}(a_{\lambda})=0
. Thus
LOH(A)\cap iLOH(A)=0
, and (ii) follows.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a left operator
V
-algebra having a right approximate identity. Then the involution
*
given by assertion (ii) in Lemma

will be called

of

.

Now the unit-free version of Theorem
1.1
reads as follows.

Theorem 2.5
.

Nonzero

-algebras are precisely the complete associative left operator
V
-algebras having an approximate identity bounded by
1
, endowed with their natural involutions.

Proof.

Nonzero

-algebras have approximate identities bounded by
1

[
9
, Lemma 39.14]
. Moreover, by
[
14
, Lemma 3.6.24]
, they are complete associative left operator

-algebras whose natural involutions coincide with their involutions as

-algebras. This is
the easy part

Suppose that
A
is a complete associative left operator
V
-algebra and has an approximate identity

bounded by
1
. Let

and

be as in Lemma

. Suppose that

. Then, by Lemma

,

is a

is a

-algebra under its natural involution.
∎

Lemma 3.1

Beginning a general theory of left operator

Left operator

-algebras have played a central role in this paper, so one is tempted to tray to develop a general theory for them. As a first sample in this line, we are provided with the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a complete left operator

-algebra with zero left annihilator. Then the involution of

is continuous.

Proof.

Since
LOH(A)
is a closed real subspace of

, it follows from the completeness of

that the direct sum

is topological.
∎

Now we are going to deal with stability properties of the class of all left operator

-algebras.

The following fact follows straightforwardly from

, Lemma 2.2.24]

Fact 5.2
.

Let

be a normed algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
, and let

.
Therefore, if

.

With the above fact in mind, the next proposition is immediate.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

be a left operator
V
-algebra with zero left annihilator, and let

be a nonzero subalgebra of

invariant under the natural involution of

. Then

is a left operator

-algebra. Moreover, if in addition

has zero left annihilator in itself, then the natural involution of

coincides with the restriction to

of the natural involution of

.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

and

be nonzero normed algebras over
{\mathbb{K}}
, and let

be a contractive algebra homomorphism. Suppose that

has an approximate identity bounded by

. Then for

Proof.

Let

be the approximate identity bounded by

in

whose existence has been assumed. Then

\phi(A)
is a subalgebra of

, and
\phi(a_{\lambda})
is an approximate identity bounded by

in

. Therefore, by Lemma

, we have
LOV(B,\phi(a))\subseteq LOV(\phi(A),\phi(a))
.
But, by Fact

Remark 5.5

According to the easy part of Theorem

, non-commutative

[

(cf.

Let

Let

Theorem 5.8

Let

Remark 5.9

Now we are going to show that left operator
V
-algebras behave reasonably well concerning the so-called “contractive projection problem”. To this end, we begin by formulating the following fact, whose proof is straightforward.

Fact 5.22
.

Let
X
be a normed space over
{\mathbb{K}}
, let
\pi:X\to X
be a contractive linear projection, set
Y:=\pi(X)
, and for
F\in BL(Y)
let

\Phi(F)\in BL(X)

be defined by
\Phi(F)(x):=F(\pi(x))
. Then
\Phi:BL(Y)\to BL(X)
is an isometric algebra homomorphism whose range is equal to
\pi\circ BL(X)\circ\pi
.

In what follows we will apply only that
\Phi
is a linear isometry such that
\Phi(I_{Y})=\pi
.

Lemma 5.23
.

Let
A
be a complex normed algebra, let
\pi:A\to A
be a nonzero contractive linear projection satisfying the “left weak conditional expectation”

\mbox{$\pi(\pi(a)\pi(b))=\pi(a\pi(b))$ \ for all \ $a,b\in A$},

(5.2)

and let
B:=(\pi(A),\odot^{\pi})
denote the normed algebra consisting of the normed space of
\pi(A)
and the product
x\odot^{\pi}y:=\pi(xy)
. Then the inclusion

LOV(B,\pi(a))\subseteq LOV(A,a)

holds for every
a\in A
. Therefore
\pi(LOH(A))\subseteq LOH(B)
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:BL(B)\to BL(A)
be the isometric algebra homomorphism given by Fact
5.22
(when
(A,B)
replaces
(X,Y)
). Then, as observed at the beginning of the proof of
[
36
, Lemma 3.2]
, for
x\in B
we have
\Phi(L_{x}^{B})=\pi\circ L_{x}^{A}\circ\pi
. Now let
a
be in
A
, and note that (
5.2
) can be rewritten as that
\pi\circ L_{\pi(a)}^{A}\circ\pi=\pi\circ L_{a}^{A}\circ\pi
for every
a\in A
. Since
\Phi(I_{B})=\pi
, it follows from
[
14
, Corollary 2.1.2(ii)]
that

LOV(B,\pi(a))=V(BL(A),\pi,\Phi(L_{\pi(a)}^{B}))=V(BL(A),\pi,\pi\circ L_{a}^{A}\circ\pi).

But, since the mapping
G\to\pi\circ G\circ\pi
is a linear contraction from
BL(A)
to
\pi\circ BL(A)\circ\pi
taking
I_{A}
to
\pi
, it follows from
[
14
, Corollary 2.1.2(i)]
that
V(BL(A),\pi,\pi\circ L_{a}^{A}\circ\pi)\subseteq LOV(A,a)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Now we can prove the main result in this section, namely the following.

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
A
be a left operator
V
-algebra, let
\pi:A\to A
be a nonzero contractive linear projection satisfying
the “weak conditional expectation”

\mbox{$\pi(\pi(a)b)=\pi(\pi(a)\pi(b))=\pi(a\pi(b))$ \ for all \ $a,b\in A$},

(5.3)

and set
B:=(\pi(A),\odot^{\pi})
. Then
B
is a left operator
V
-algebra. Moreover, if in addition
A
has a right approximate identity
(
eventually bounded by
1
)
, then we have:

(i)

B
has a right approximate identity
(
eventually bounded by
1
)
, and the natural involution of
B

(
say
\natural
)
is given by
x^{\natural}=\pi(x^{*})
, where
*
denotes the natural involution of
A
.

(ii)

\pi=\pi\circ\pi^{*}
, hence
\pi^{*}=\pi^{*}\circ\pi
, and
\ker(\pi^{*})=\ker(\pi)
is a
*
-invariant subspace of
A
.

(iii)

If
*
is an isometry, then every convex combination of
\pi
and
\pi^{*}
is a contractive linear projection on
A
.

Proof.

Let
x
be in
B
. Then
x=h+ik
for suitable
h,k\in LOH(A)
. Therefore
x=\pi(h)+i\pi(k)
, with
\pi(h),\pi(k)\in LOH(B)
thanks to Lemma
5.23
. Since
x
is arbitrary in
B
, this shows that
B
is a left operator
V
-algebra.

Suppose that
A
has a right approximate identity
a_{\lambda}
(eventually bounded by
1
). Then, by the first equality in (
5.3
), for
a\in A
we have

\lim_{\lambda}\pi(a)\odot^{\pi}\pi(a_{\lambda})=\lim_{\lambda}\pi(\pi(a)\pi(a_{\lambda}))=\lim_{\lambda}\pi(\pi(a)a_{\lambda})=\pi(\pi(a))=\pi(a).

Therefore
\pi(a_{\lambda})
is a right approximate identity (eventually bounded by
1
) for
B
. Let
x
be in
B
. Write
x=h+ik
with

. Then we have

\pi(x^{*})=\pi(h)-i\pi(k)=(\pi(h)+i\pi(k))^{\natural}=\pi(x)^{\natural}=x^{\natural},

where for the second equality we have applied Lemma

again. Thus (i) has been proved.
Now let
a
be in
A
. Then, by Lemma

\pi(a^{*})=(\pi(a))^{\natural}
. Therefore, replacing

. Let

be nonnegative real numbers with

(\alpha\pi+\beta\pi^{*})^{2}=\alpha(\alpha\pi+\beta\pi\circ\pi^{*})+\beta(\alpha\pi^{*}\circ\pi+\beta\pi^{*})=\alpha\pi+\beta\pi^{*}.

This concludes the proof of (iii) and of the theorem.

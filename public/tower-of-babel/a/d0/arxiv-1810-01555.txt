Deformations of Reducible Galois Representations to Hida-families
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. The global deformation theory of residually reducible Galois representations with fixed auxiliary conditions is studied. We show that ρ ¯ : Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ / ℚ ) → GL 2 ⁡ ( 𝔽 ¯ p ) \bar{\rho}:\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}) lifts to a Hida line for which the weights range over a congruence class modulo- p 2 p^{2} . The advantage of the purely Galois theoretic approach is that it allows us to construct p p -adic families of Galois representations lifting the actual representation ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} , and not just the semisimplification.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of characteristic
p>2
and denote by
\mathcal{O}
the ring of Witt vectors
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Fix a continuous Galois representation
\bar{\rho}:\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In this paper, we consider the problem of describing various families of
p
-adic representations which lift
\bar{\rho}
. These representations will be subject to certain local conditions at the primes at which they are allowed to ramify.

We first discuss the case when
\bar{\rho}
is absolutely irreducible. Letting
S
be a finite set of primes containing
p
and the primes at which
\bar{\rho}
is ramified, set
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q})
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
is the maximal algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
such that all primes
v\notin S
are unramified. At each prime
v\notin S
, set
\operatorname{G}_{v}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{v})
and fix an embedding
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
. The functor of deformations of
\bar{\rho}
was introduced by Mazur
[
12
]
, following Hida
[
7
]
. Mazur shows that there is a
universal deformation ring

\operatorname{R}(\bar{\rho})
and a
universal deformation

\rho^{\operatorname{univ}}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\operatorname{R}(\bar{\rho}))
which represents the functor of deformations of
\bar{\rho}
that are unramified away from the set
S
. Boston and Mazur in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
calculate
\operatorname{R}(\bar{\rho})
in many cases of natural interest. In
[
8
]
, Mazur studies the structure of an "infinite fern" in the universal deformation space of Galois representations. The study of deformation functors has to some extent been motivated by Serre’s conjecture. When
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}
is odd, Serre’s conjecture asserts that
\bar{\rho}
lifts to a characteristic zero representation
\rho
attached to a Hecke eigencuspform. The conjecture was settled by Khare-Wintenberger in
[
11
]
. Prior to this development, Ramakrishna in
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
showed via purely Galois theoretic means that if
\bar{\rho}
satisfies some favorable conditions, it exhibits a characteristic zero lift which is
geometric
in the sense of Fontaine-Mazur
[
5
, p. 193]
. The construction involves a purely Galois theoretic argument, involving the study of global deformations satisfying fixed local deformation conditions at the primes
S
and a finite auxiliary set of primes
X
. The auxiliary primes in
X
are called
nice
primes and the deformation functor is of Steinberg type
[
15
, section 4.2]
. When
\bar{\rho}
is
ordinary
, i.e,
\bar{\rho}(\operatorname{G}_{p})
is contained in
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ast&\ast\\
0&\ast\\
\end{array}}\right)\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the deformation condition at
p
consists of ordinary deformations. The functor of global Galois deformations unramified outside
S\cup X
with fixed local conditions is represented by a
universal deformation ring

\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}
. Denote by

\rho_{S\cup X}^{\operatorname{univ}}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X})

the universal deformation (where the determinant character is not fixed). Let
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and set
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})
. The Iwasawa-algebra
\Lambda
is the completed group algebra
\Lambda:=\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]
. Fixing a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
amounts to fixing an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\Lambda\simeq\mathcal{O}[[x]]
, where
\gamma-1
is the variable
x
. Refer to
\operatorname{Spec}\Lambda
as
weight-space
and the map to weight-space
\mathcal{W}t:\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\Lambda
is induced by mapping
x
to
\det\rho_{S\cup X}^{\operatorname{univ}}(\gamma)-1
. For every fixed lift
\kappa:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{1}(\mathcal{O})
of
\det\bar{\rho}
, let
\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}^{\kappa}
denote the
fixed weight
quotient of
\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}
, parametrizing deformations
\rho
for which
\det\rho=\kappa
. Ramakrishna shows that
X
may be chosen so that
\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}^{\kappa}\simeq\mathcal{O}
for each
\kappa
, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 1]
. It follows that there is a canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{R}_{S\cup X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}[[x]]
induced by the map to weight-space. This produces a nice family of ordinary Galois representations, one for each
p
-adic weight. Such a family of
p
-adic Galois representations is represented by a
p
-adic line which is a component in a certain
p
-ordinary local Hecke algebra of specified level (depending on the set of primes
S\cup X
) and arbitrary weight. We refer to such a component as a
Hida-line
. Presentations of deformation rings with prescribed local conditions are studied by Böckle in
[
1
, section 5]
. It is necessary that the local deformation conditions be representable. In this setting, it becomes possible to provide a description for generators and relations in terms of certain Galois cohomology groups with local conditions. The local conditions are associated with the tangent spaces to the various local deformation functors which are to be interpreted as
Selmer conditions
. Even in cases when the structure of the minimal deformation ring
\mathcal{R}_{S}
may be complicated, allowing for additional ramification and imposing local conditions gives a smooth ring
\mathcal{O}[[x]]
. The existence of such families of Galois representations are of genuine interest from the point of view of studying the structure of universal deformation rings.

The goal of this paper is to study the analogous deformation problem with prescribed local conditions in the setting when
\bar{\rho}
is reducible, yet indecomposable. The reducible case presents some technical difficulties, which we explain. Hamblen and Ramakrishna in
[
6
]
show that under some favorable conditions on

\bar{\rho}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi&\ast\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right):\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

there is a finite set of auxiliary primes
X
and a
p
-ordinary lift
\rho:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
which satisfies certain deformation problems at the primes
v\in S\cup X
. By the result of Skinner and Wiles in
[
22
]
, the representation
\rho
arises from a
p
-ordinary Hecke eigencuspform. The primes
v\in X
are called
trivial primes
since the restriction
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
is the trivial representation
[
6
, section 4]
. Each trivial prime
v
is equipped with a deformation functor
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}
(see
[
6
, Definition 26 and 30]
for the definition in the setting when the determinant of the lifts is fixed). The deformation functor at
v
is
not
representable, as we explain. Let
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-space of
2\times 2
-matrices with adjoint Galois action. For
g\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and
v\in\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
, we have that
g\cdot v:=\bar{\rho}(g)v\bar{\rho}(g)^{-1}
. If a functor of deformations
\mathcal{F}
of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
is representable, it comes equipped with a
tangent space

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2}))
, which may be identified with a subspace of
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})
. For
\varrho\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{O}/p^{N})
and
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{F}}
,

(\operatorname{Id}+fp^{N-1})\varrho\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{O}/p^{N}).

In other words, the tangent-space must stabilize the functor of deformations. This is not the case for the deformation problem at a trivial prime
v
. Associated to the deformation functor
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}
at
v
, there is a space of cohomology classes which plays the role of a tangent space for mod
p^{3}
lifts of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
. These stabilize
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(\mathcal{O}/p^{N})
for
N\geq 3
, but not for
N\leq 2
(cf.
[
6
, Corollary 25]
and Proposition
3.8
). It has thus not been possible to formulate what the analogous deformation rings should be in the reducible setting (when local conditions at trivial primes are taken into account) and whether such deformation rings are well-defined. For each choice of lift
\kappa
of
\det\bar{\rho}
, the method of Hamblen-Ramakrishna relies on a choice of a mod-
p^{2}
lift
\rho_{2}
of
\bar{\rho}
(cf.
[
6
, section 5]
) which depends on
\kappa
. The lift
\rho_{2}
is ramified at two trivial primes
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
(in addition to
S
) which depend on the choice of
\kappa
(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 41]
). Thus, when local conditions are prescribed, one does not expect all the lifts obtained from the method Hamblen-Ramakrishna to lie on the same component in a single Hida family, let alone a single Hida-line.

We examine an adaptation of the functor of global deformations, with two crucial differences:

(1)

We restrict the functor to a suitable subcategory
\mathfrak{C}
of the category of coefficient rings.

(2)

Second, we fix a mod

of

and study the functor of deformations of

(restricted to

A coefficient ring

over

(2)

Set

It should be noted here that an alternative approach to studying the deformation theory of residually reducible Galois representations is via pseudo-representations. The study of pseudorepresentations also aids in proving modularity of a characteristic zero geometric lift of
\bar{\rho}
when it is known to exist (see for instance
[
22
]
). In this paper, we study the deformations of the actual representation
\bar{\rho}
and not just its semisimplification. In practice, examples of reducible mod-
p
representations
\bar{\rho}
are constructed via class field theory. A simple application of inflation-restriction shows that a Galois stable line in a mod-
p
ray class group of
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
gives rise to a residually reducible Galois representation
\bar{\rho}
(see
[
20
]
). Depending on the set
S
, there may exist many (non-isomorphic) representations
\bar{\rho}
with the same semisimplification. Every one of the representations
\bar{\rho}
gives rise to a unique Hida-line. This is the advantage of the purely Galois theoretic approach used in this manuscript.

The paper is organized as follows: in section
2
, local deformation conditions at the primes

are discussed. Dimensions of local tangent spaces are calculated and Selmer groups associated to these local deformation conditions are defined. Section
3
introduces the special role played by
trivial
primes, i.e. the auxiliary primes at which deformations are allowed to further ramify. In section
4
the main results of this paper are proved.

Dimension Calculations

In this section, we show that the dimension of

is equal to

\end{array}}\right)

Consequently,

may be identified with the subspace

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=\ker\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\tilde{U})\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{p},\tilde{U}/W)\right\}

.

For the decomposition

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\tilde{U})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)\oplus H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})

let
\pi_{1}
and

denote the projection maps to

and

The map on restriction
\pi_{1}^{\prime}={\pi_{1}}_{\restriction\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}}
induces an exact sequence.

Proposition 2.6
.

The map on restriction
\pi_{1}^{\prime}={\pi_{1}}_{\restriction\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}}
induces a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}^{\prime}}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

As noted earlier,
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
is identified with the subspace

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=\ker\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\tilde{U})\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{p},\tilde{U}/W)\right\}

of
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\tilde{U})
. The kernel of the projection
\pi_{1}:H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\tilde{U})\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)
is
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})
. As a result, the kernel of
\pi_{1}^{\prime}
is the intersection

H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cap\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
, which consists of unramified central classes
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})
. This shows that the sequence is exact in the middle.

The map on the left is clearly injective. We prove that the map
\pi_{1}^{\prime}
is surjective. Let
f\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)
, we show that
f
is in the image of
\pi_{1}^{\prime}
. It suffices to show that there exists an element
f^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})
such that

, i.e. the class

must map to zero in
H^{1}(I_{p},\tilde{U}/W)
.

We observe that the composite of the inclusion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id}
into
\tilde{U}
with the quotient
\tilde{U}\rightarrow\tilde{U}/W
is an isomorphism of
\operatorname{G}_{p}
modules (both spaces are fixed by
\operatorname{G}_{p}
). We simply take
f_{1}
to coincide with
f
modulo
W
w.r.t this isomorphism. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

For the dimension of

we are to consider two cases

h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)=\begin{cases}2\text{ if }\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{p}}\text{ is indecomposable}\\
3\text{ otherwise if }\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{p}}\text{ is a sum of characters}\end{cases}

in other words,

h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)=2+h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}).

Consequently, dimension of
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
is

\begin{split}\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=&h^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id})+h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)\\
=&3+h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})\\
=&2+h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}).\end{split}

Proof.

We will make use of the cohomology sequence associated with the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow U^{0}\rightarrow U\rightarrow U/U^{0}\rightarrow 0.

From the isomorphism
U^{0}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}(\varphi)
and the fact that
\varphi_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{p}}\neq 1,\bar{\chi}^{-1}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{p}}
. The assumptions on

ensure that

. From the Euler characteristic formula we have that

is
1
dimensional.

From the Euler characteristic formula,

and

h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U^{0})=1.

From the long exact sequence, we have the following formula for the dimension of

\begin{split}h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)=&h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U^{0})+h^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U/U^{0})+h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)-1\\
=&2+h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},U)\\
=&\begin{cases}2\text{ if }\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{p}}\text{ is indecomposable}\\

∎

The Selmer group associated to

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},Z},M):=\ker\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},Z},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in Z}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},M)}{\mathcal{L}_{v}}\right\}

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},Z},M^{*}):=\ker\left\{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},Z},M^{*})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in Z}\frac{H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},M^{*})}{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}_{v}}\right\}.

The Proposition below is due to Wiles and follows from the Poitou-Tate long exact sequence for finite modules

Theorem 2.9
.

[
14
, Theorem 8.7.9]

. Let

In the above formula,
\operatorname{G}_{\infty}=\langle c\rangle
. The spaces
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
and

Versal Deformations at Trivial Primes

In this section, we recall the deformation problems at trivial primes due to Hamblen-Ramakrishna. Throughout, we fix a lift
\kappa:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{1}(\mathcal{O})
and study two types of deformation problems

for which the determinant is fixed to be

for the latter condition.

Definition 3.1
.

A prime

(1)

\operatorname{G}_{v}\subset\ker\bar{\rho}
,

(2)

the prime

but

Fact 3.2
.

For a trivial prime

h^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=\begin{cases}3\text{ if }i=0,2,\\
6\text{ if }i=1,\\
\end{cases}\text{and }\hskip 7.11317pth^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=\begin{cases}4\text{ if }i=0,2,\\

\end{cases}

All higher cohomology groups are zero.

Let

\kappa(\sigma_{v})v^{-1}
in
\mathcal{O}

\bar{\rho}
is upper triangular. Let

be a trivial prime,
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction G_{v}}

be a choice of Frobenius at

a generator of the maximal pro-
p

is generated by

and

which are subject to a single relation
\sigma_{v}\tau_{v}\sigma_{v}^{-1}=\tau_{v}^{v}
.

Definition 3.3
.

We proceed to prescribe deformation problems at trivial primes.

•

Let
\mathcal{D}_{v}
be the class of deformations of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction G_{v}}
containing representatives taking

\begin{split}&\sigma_{v}\mapsto(\kappa(\sigma_{v})v^{-1})^{1/2}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}v&x\\

\end{array}}\right)\end{split}

with respect to a basis lifting

and insist that

divides

.

•

Separate
\mathcal{D}_{v}
into two classes, the first class of deformations are those that are ramified modulo

The space

.

Let

Proof.

The dimension of the local tangent spaces at

for the full-adjoint deformation problems are as follows,

\begin{split}&\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\infty}=0\\
&\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})+2\\
&\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v}=h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\text{ for $v\in S\backslash\{p\}$}\\
&\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v}=h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\text{ for $v\notin S$ a trivial prime.}\end{split}

An application of Wiles’ formula yields

(3.1)

\begin{split}&h^{1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})-h^{1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}^{*})\\

&=1+\sum_{v\in S\cup X\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\dim\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v}-h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\right)\\
&=3-h^{0}(\operatorname{G}_{\infty},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\\
&=1.\end{split}

∎

to an irreducible mod

[

The two classes of deformations (ramified mod

and unramified mod

A Purely Galois Theoretic Lifting Construction

In
[
6
, section 5]
, it is shown that there exists a finite set of trivial primes
X_{1}
, that are disjoint from
S
such that
\bar{\rho}
lifts to an irreducible mod
p^{3}
representation

\rho_{3}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X_{1}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/p^{3})

which satisfies the liftable local deformation problems
\mathcal{C}_{v}
at each prime
v\in S\cup X_{1}
. The set of trivial primes
X_{1}
in particular has the property that
\Sh^{2}_{S\cup X_{1}}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})=0
. It is then shown that the set of primes
X_{1}
may be further extended to a finite set of trivial primes
X
containing
X_{1}
such that
h^{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*})=0
. Denote by
\Phi
the collection of versal deformation problems

\Phi=\{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}\}_{v\in S\cup X}
. Denote by
\rho_{2}:=\rho_{3}\mod{p^{2}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
R\in\mathfrak{C}
, let
\rho_{2,R}
denote the deformation with image in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R/\mathfrak{n}_{2})
induced from
\rho_{2}
by the structure map
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow R
. Let
\mathcal{D}ef_{\Phi}:\mathfrak{C}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sets}
be the functor such that
\mathcal{D}ef_{\Phi}(R)
consists of deformations
\rho_{R}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
for which

\rho_{2,R}=\rho_{R}\mod{\mathfrak{n}_{2}}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
R\in\mathfrak{C}
have maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
and let
J
an ideal in
R
. The map of coefficient rings
R\rightarrow R/J
is said to be
nearly small

\mathfrak{m}_{R}J=0
. Recall that it is said to be
small
if it is nearly small and if
J
is principal.

Let
R\rightarrow R/J
be nearly small and
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
be the maximal ideal of
R
. Since there is a unique isomorphism
R/\mathfrak{m}_{R}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras, we simply identify the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}_{R}
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Recall that by definition,
\mathfrak{m}_{R}J=0
. For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
j\in J
, let
aj
denote
\tilde{a}j
where
\tilde{a}\in R
is a lift of
a
. Note that
aj
is well defined since
\tilde{a}j
is independent of the choice of lift
\tilde{a}
. Therefore
J
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Associate to a pure tensor

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)\otimes j\in\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J,

the matrix

\operatorname{Id}+\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}aj&bj\\
cj&dj\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+aj&bj\\
cj&1+dj\\
\end{array}}\right),

which is in the kernel of the reduction map
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R/J)
.

Note that
J^{2}=0
since
J
is contained in
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{R}J=0
. Consider two pure tensors

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\otimes j_{1}\text{ and }\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\\
\end{array}}\right)\otimes j_{2}.

From the relation
j_{1}j_{2}=0
, we have that

\left(\operatorname{Id}+\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{1}j_{1}&b_{1}j_{1}\\
c_{1}j_{1}&d_{1}j_{1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)\left(\operatorname{Id}+\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{2}j_{2}&b_{2}j_{2}\\
c_{2}j_{2}&d_{2}j_{2}\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)

=\operatorname{Id}+\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{1}j_{1}+a_{2}j_{2}&b_{1}j_{1}+b_{2}j_{2}\\
c_{1}j_{1}+c_{2}j_{2}&d_{1}j_{1}+d_{2}j_{2}\\
\end{array}}\right).

Therefore we have an isomorphism (of groups)

\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J\simeq\operatorname{ker}\left\{\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R/J)\right\}.

Remark 4.3
.

The tensor product
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
is a
\operatorname{G}_{v}
-module, via the adjoint action on
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
and the trivial action on
J
. Choosing an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
J
, we find that as a
\operatorname{G}_{v}
-module, the tensor product decomposes into a direct sum of copies of
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
. As a result,
H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J)
is identified with
H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
for all
i\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R\rightarrow R/J
be nearly small and
v\in S
. The exponential map of a pure tensor

X\otimes j\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J

takes
\varrho\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)
to

\exp(X\otimes j)\varrho:=(\operatorname{Id}+X\otimes j)\varrho.

Refer to
\exp(X\otimes j)\varrho
the twist of
\varrho
by
X\otimes j
. This gives a well defined action of
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
on
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)
and the fibers of the reduction map

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R/J)

are
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
pseudotorsors.

Proof.

Let

X\otimes j\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J

be a pure tensor,
\varrho\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)
and denote by
\varrho_{0}:\operatorname{G}_{v}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R/J)
the reduction of
\varrho
modulo
J
. Let
\hat{X}
be a cocycle representing the cohomology class
X
and choose a representative
\hat{\varrho}
for
\varrho
. Set

\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}:\operatorname{G}_{v}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)

to be equal to the twist

\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}:=\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}.

Since
\hat{X}
is a cocycle, it follows that
\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}
is a homomorphism. In greater detail, for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\operatorname{G}_{v}
, we have that

\begin{split}\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}(g_{1})\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}(g_{2})=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{1})\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1})\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{2})\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{2})\\
=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{1})\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1})\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{2})\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1})^{-1}\hat{\varrho}(g_{1}g_{2})\\
=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{1})\otimes j\right)\left(\operatorname{Id}+\left(\bar{\rho}_{\restriction v}(g_{1})\hat{X}(g_{2})\bar{\rho}_{\restriction v}(g_{1})^{-1}\right)\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1}g_{2})\\
=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{X}(g_{1})\otimes j\right)\left(\operatorname{Id}+(g_{1}\hat{X})(g_{2})\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1}g_{2})\\
=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\left(\hat{X}(g_{1})+(g_{1}\hat{X})(g_{2})\right)\otimes j\right)\hat{\varrho}(g_{1}g_{2})\\
=&\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}(g_{1}g_{2}),\end{split}

where
\bar{\rho}_{|v}
is an abbreviation for
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
. It is easy to see that if
\hat{X}
is a coboundary, then
\hat{\varrho}^{\prime}
is strictly equivalent to
\hat{\varrho}
(in the sense of Definition
2.1
). Therefore, this gives a well defined deformation

\varrho^{\prime}:=\left(\operatorname{Id}+X\otimes j\right)\varrho

of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\operatorname{G}_{v}}
. Furthermore,
\varrho^{\prime}
is in fact a deformation of
\varrho_{0}
. This is because
\left(\operatorname{Id}+X\otimes j\right)
reduces to
\operatorname{Id}
and
\varrho
reduces to
\varrho_{0}
modulo
J
. Therefore, there is an action of
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
on the fibres of the reduction map

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R/J).

We show that each fibre is an
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
-pseudotorsor. This means that either the set of lifts
r\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)
of
r_{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{v}(R)
is empty, or the set of lifts is in bijection with
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
. Assume without loss of generality that there exists a lift
r
of
r_{0}
and let
r^{\prime}
be any other lift. It suffices to show that there is a class
Y\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
such that

r^{\prime}=\left(\operatorname{Id}+Y\right)r.

It is easy to see that if such a class
Y
exists then it is necessarily unique. Recall that the kernel of the reduction map

\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R/J)

is identified with
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
. For
g\in\operatorname{G}_{v}
, set

\hat{Y}(g):=r^{\prime}(g)r(g)^{-1}-\operatorname{Id}\in\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J,

where we choose representatives for
r
and
r^{\prime}
.
For
g_{1},g_{2}\in\operatorname{G}_{v}
,

\begin{split}r^{\prime}(g_{1})r^{\prime}(g_{2})=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{Y}(g_{1})\right)r(g_{1})\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{Y}(g_{2})\right)r(g_{2})\\
=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{Y}(g_{1})+g_{1}\hat{Y}(g_{2})\right)r(g_{1}g_{2}),\\
r^{\prime}(g_{1}g_{2})=&\left(\operatorname{Id}+\hat{Y}(g_{1}g_{2})\right)r(g_{1}g_{2}),\end{split}

and since
r^{\prime}(g_{1}g_{2})=r^{\prime}(g_{1})r^{\prime}(g_{2})
it follows that

\hat{Y}
is a cocyle. We set
Y
to be the associated cohomology class in
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J)
. Note
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J)
is identified with
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{v},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}J
(see Remark
4.3
). We have thus shown that there is a well defined class
Y
such that
r^{\prime}=(\operatorname{Id}+Y)r
. This completes the proof.
∎

}f_{2}\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q},S\cup X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}^{*}).

are therefore

Therefore,

. Since

Since

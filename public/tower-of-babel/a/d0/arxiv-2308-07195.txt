Introduction

A central problem in extremal graph theory is to find sufficient degree conditions which force the containment of a given spanning subgraph, and here a classical result of Dirac
[
10
]
from 1952 states that every graph on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree at least
n/2
contains a Hamilton cycle.
In 1995, Bollobás
[
4
]
and Bondy
[
5
]
asked for estimates of the number of distinct Hamilton cycles in graphs satisfying Dirac’s condition. Sárközy, Selkow, and Szemerédi
[
32
]
used the regularity method in 2003 to show that every graph with
n\geq 3
vertices and minimum degree at least
n/2
contains at least
c^{n}n!
Hamilton cycles, for some constant
c>0
. This cannot be improved to any
c>1/2
, as for fixed
p>1/2
the typical random graph
G(n,p)
satisfies Dirac’s condition and has
(1-o(1))^{n}p^{n}n!
distinct Hamilton cycles (see
[
16
]
). In 2009, Cuckler and Kahn
[
8
]
obtained precise estimates of the number of distinct Hamilton cycles in terms of the minimum degree of the host graph.
In particular, they showed that every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
n/2
contains at least
(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))^{n}n!
distinct Hamilton cycles, thus matching the bound given by random graphs.

Our purpose here is to introduce a simple method to bound below the number of copies of many different spanning subgraphs in graphs and hypergraphs with high minimum degree, and apply this to give new counting results in dense hypergraphs (Theorems
1.2
to
1.4
). However, let us already illustrate this method as it would apply to Hamilton cycles in an
n
-vertex graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
with
\varepsilon>0
fixed and
n
large. Set
r=\mu n
with
1/n\ll\mu\ll\varepsilon
, and partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{r}
by choosing the location of each vertex independently and uniformly at random. With probability at least
e^{-n}
, the minimum degree of each subgraph
G[V_{i}]
will be at least
(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})|V_{i}|
and there will be a disjoint collection of

connecting the sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
in a cycle in order and connecting
V_{r}
to

(see Figure

in order. Even though the probability of success here is small, only at least

For each

Dirac’s theorem has been generalised to give minimum degree conditions implying the containment of many other spanning subgraphs, including

1.1
Dirac-type problems in hypergraphs

A
k
-uniform hypergraph (or
k
-graph) is a hypergraph where every edge consists of exactly
k
vertices. For a
k
-graph
H
and a subset of vertices
S\subset V(H)
, the degree of
S
, denoted
d_{H}(S)
, is the number of edges in
H
containing
S
. For
1\leq d\leq k-1
, the minimum
d
-degree of
H
, denoted
\delta_{d}(H)
, is the minimum of
d_{H}(S)
over all subsets
S\subseteq V(H)
with
|S|=d
. When
d=k-1
this is the
minimum codegree
,
\delta(H)=\delta_{k-1}(H)
.

The first subgraphs we consider in hypergraphs are the Hamilton
\ell
-cycles
, a well-studied generalisation of Hamilton cycles to hypergraphs.
For
k\geq 2
and
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
, say that a
k
-graph
C
is an
\ell
-cycle if there is a cyclic ordering
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
of
V(C)
such that every edge of
C
consists of
k
consecutive vertices and every two consecutive edges intersect in exactly
\ell
vertices. If
\ell=k-1
, then
C
is called a
tight cycle
, and, if
\ell=0
, then
C
is a matching. A
k
-graph
H
contains a
Hamilton
\ell
-cycle
if there is an
\ell
-cycle
C\subseteq H
with
V(C)=V(H)
(where it can only exist if
k-\ell
divides
|H|
).

The asymptotic minimum codegree required to guarantee a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle in an
n
-vertex
k
-graph is known due to Rödl, Ruciński, and Szemerédi
[
30
]
if
(k-\ell)|\ell
and to Kühn, Mycroft, and Osthus
[
23
]
if
(k-\ell)\nmid k
, whose results together give the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
\gamma>0
,
k\geq 2
, and
1\leq\ell<k
, there exists
n_{0}
such that the following holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
with
(k-\ell)\mid n
. If
H
is an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with
\delta(H)\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma)n
, where

\delta_{k,\ell}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2}&\text{ if }(k-\ell)\mid k,\\
\dfrac{1}{\lceil\frac{k}{k-\ell}\rceil(k-\ell)}&\text{ if }(k-\ell)\nmid k,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

then
H
contains a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle.

Due to Theorem
1.1
, we say the minimum codegree threshold for a
k
-graph to contain a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle is
\delta_{k,\ell}
.
Note that a Hamilton tight cycle contains a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle for every
\ell
with
(k-\ell)\mid k
. The results in Theorem
1.1
are tight in every case up to the ‘error term’ of
\gamma n
. For more discussion of this, and the seemingly much more difficult problem for other degree bounds

,

, see the survey by Kühn and Osthus
[
26
]
.

We will also consider the powers of Hamilton tight cycles, where, for each
t\geq k\geq 2
, a
k
-graph

is the
(t-k+1)
th power of a tight cycle if there is a cyclic ordering
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}
of

so that
\{v_{i},\ldots,v_{i+t-1}\}
spans a
k
-uniform clique for all

(working modulo

). When

, this coincides with the usual definition of powers of cycles in graphs, where for each
t\geq 2
the minimum degree threshold for the containment of the
(t-1)
th power of a Hamilton cycle was famously shown to be
\frac{t-1}{t}
by Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi
[
22
,
20
]
.
In 2020, Bedenknecht and Reiher
[
3
]
proved that
3
-graphs with minimum codegree at least
(4/5+o(1))n
contain the square of a tight Hamilton cycle (which corresponds to

and

cannot be reduced below

for all

. This was recently widely extended by Pavez-Signé, Sanhueza-Matamala and Stein
[
28
]
, who showed that, with
\gamma>0
fixed, if an
n
-vertex
k
-graph

has minimum codegree

\delta(H)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\binom{t-1}{k-1}+\binom{t-2}{k-2}}+\gamma\right)n,

(1.2)

then
H
contains the
(t-k+1)
th power of a tight Hamilton cycle, provided that
n
is sufficiently large. It is not known whether the bounds given in (
1.2
) are tight up to
\gamma n
, though this is true for the cases
t\geq k=2
and
t=k\geq 2
which were already known
[
30
,
22
]
.

Finally, we will consider factors in hypergraphs. For a
k
-graph

, a
k
-graph

contains an

-factor if it contains a collection of vertex-disjoint copies of

covering every vertex in

. Thus, a necessary condition for an

-factor in

is that

divides

. For

, then, let
\mu_{k,d}(F)
be the smallest number such that for every

, there is

such that if

is an

-vertex graph with

divisible by
|F|
and
\delta_{d}(H)\geq(\mu_{k,d}(F)+\gamma)\binom{n}{k-d}

contains an

-factor. In contrast to the graph case, where the threshold is known (with moreover a much stronger error term) for all fixed

due to Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi
[
15
]
and Kühn and Osthus
[
25
]
, for most cases we do not have good bounds even in the case

(see the survey by
[
33
]
and references therein).

1.2
Counting spanning hypergraphs

As in Cuckler and Kahn’s work on Hamilton cycles
[
8
]
, it is reasonable to believe that an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq\delta n
, for
(k-\ell)\mid n
and
\delta>\delta_{k,\ell}
, should contain at least

(1-o(1))^{n}\cdot\Psi_{k,\ell}(n,\delta)

(1.3)

distinct Hamilton
\ell
-cycles, where
\Psi_{k,\ell}(n,\delta)
denotes the expected number of distinct Hamilton
\ell
-cycles in the binomial random
k
-graph on
n
vertices with edge probability
\delta
. In 2016, Ferber, Krivelevich, and Sudakov
[
12
]
showed that the lower bound (
1.3
) is correct for every
\delta>1/2
and
1\leq\ell\leq k/2
, and asked if this can be extended to
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
and
\delta>\delta_{k,\ell}
. This was partially answered by Glock, Gould, Joos, Kühn, and Osthus
[
13
]
, who showed that for every
\delta>1/2
and
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
, the number of distinct Hamilton
\ell
-cycles is
\exp(n\log n-\Theta(n))
, which is tight up to the
\Theta(n)
error term in the exponent. This result was recently improved by Ferber, Hardiman, and Mond
[
11
]
, who proved that the lower bound (
1.3
) holds for every
\delta>1/2
and
1\leq\ell\leq k-2
, thus settling the problem for every

such that
(k-\ell)\mid k
. Our contribution is to use the simple method outlined above to get a bound matching that in
[
13
]
that holds for any
\delta>\delta_{k,\ell}
(with
\delta_{k,\ell}
as defined in (

)), thus giving a new result when
(k-\ell)\nmid k
and
\ell>k/2
, as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
k\geq 2
,
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
and
\gamma>0
, there exist
n_{0}
and
C
such that the following holds for any
n\geq n_{0}
with
(k-\ell)\mid n
. Any
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma)n
(see (

)) contains at least
\exp(n\log n-Cn)
distinct Hamilton
\ell
-cycles.

No previous bounds on the count of powers of Hamilton tight cycles in hypergraphs with large codegree have been shown (including in graphs), and here we use our technique to similarly get a bound tight up to
\Theta(n)
error term in the exponent, as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
t\geq k\geq 2
and
\gamma>0
, there exist
n_{0}
and
C
such that the following holds for any
n\geq n_{0}
. Any
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
satisfying (
1.2
) contains at least
\exp(n\log n-Cn)
distinct copies of the
(t-k+1)
th power of a Hamilton tight cycle.

-factors in dense hypergraphs. When

described above, if

In each case as part of wider work, for

When

In the graph case (

For a set

2.1
Our main partitioning lemma

Here we prove our main lemma, showing that in any linear (in
|H|
) minimum degree hypergraph
H
there are many partitions of
V(H)
into sets with chosen sizes whose induced subgraphs from
H
have high minimum degree relative to their sizes (see Lemma
2.3
). We need a slightly stronger condition to connect the subgraphs then found in these induced subgraphs, which motivates the following definition of a
good partition
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
\delta\in[0,1]
and
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. For an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
, we say that a partition
V(H)=V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{r}
is
(\mathbf{n},\delta)
-good
if (working modulo
r
in the indices) we have

P1

|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
, and

P2

for each
i\in[r]
and
U\subseteq V_{i-1}\cup V_{i}\cup V_{i+1}
with
|U|=k-1
,
d(U,V_{i})\geq\delta|V_{i}|
.

To find many good partitions, we will choose an appropriate distribution
\mathbf{n}
for the size of the subsets and partition

uniformly at random subject to
P1
. We then show (for the parameters we use, and with

large) that

holds with probability at least

, a relatively small probability but still enough to show that many partitions are good. For any fixed

,

will hold with constant probability, and the only difficulty here is to show that (despite many dependencies) this is true for all

with probability at least

. To do this, we form the random partition iteratively, each time dividing the subsets in two, and tracking how the minimum degree condition (or more precisely something akin to

) changes in the subgraphs induced on the sets. This is inspired by part of the analysis in the work by Barber, Lo, Kühn and Osthus
[
2
]
introducing iterative absorption, and we use some similar calculations to those in

]

For our analysis, we need the following standard concentration result for hypergeometric random variables (see, e.g.,

-connects

.

-path connected. The proof of Lemma

is a very straightforward modification of the original argument of Kühn, Mycroft and Osthus
[

]
for finding Hamilton

Lemma 2.6

Let

Now we are ready for the proof of our first main result.

Definition A.2
.

Let

-good, we then say that

The following result states that most

Lemma A.3

(Lemma 6.2 in

]
)

Let

satisfy
(k-\ell)\nmid k
, and let

. There exists a constant
t=t(k,\ell)
such that if

is an

-vertex

-graph with

(\beta,t)
-bad

]

The last two ingredients we need are, firstly, that the degree conditions are preserved by taking random subsets and, secondly, that hypergraphs with large vertex degree have perfect matchings.

Lemma A.6

(Lemma 8.1 in
[

]
)
.

Let

and let

. Let

|N_{H}(S)\cap\binom{R}{k-d}|\geq\mu\binom{\alpha n}{k-d}-n^{k-d-\frac{1}{3}}
for every

.

Theorem A.7

(Perfect matching theorem
[
9
]
)
.

Let
n,k\geq 2
such that

n

Proof of Lemma

.

We begin by choosing constants

1/n\ll\alpha\ll\beta\ll\theta\ll\theta^{\prime}\ll\gamma\ll\mu\ll 1/k,

and let
t=t(k,\ell)
from Lemma
A.3

\ell
-tuples
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell})
in

, set
H^{\prime}=H-\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell},b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}\}
and
n^{\prime}=|H^{\prime}|=n-2\ell

\delta(H^{\prime})\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma/2)n^{\prime}
as
1/n\ll 1/k
. Using Lemma
A.4
, find an absorbing
\ell
-path
P_{0}
in
H^{\prime}
with at most
\gamma n^{\prime}/16
vertices which can absorb any collection of at most
2\alpha n^{\prime}
pairwise disjoint
(\beta,t)
-good
(k-\ell)
-sets in
V(H^{\prime})
(here we have used that
\delta(H^{\prime})\geq\gamma n^{\prime}/16
). Let
G
be an auxiliary
(k-\ell)
-graph with
V(G)=V(H^{\prime})
and edge set consisting of all those
(k-\ell)
-sets in
H^{\prime}
which are
(\beta,t)
-good. By Lemma
A.3
, for every

we have

d_{G}(v)\geq\binom{n^{\prime}}{k-\ell-1}-\theta n^{\prime k-\ell-1}\geq(1-{\theta^{\prime}})\binom{n^{\prime}}{k-\ell-1}.

Let
R\subset V(H^{\prime})
be a random subset of size
\alpha n^{\prime}
. Then, by Lemma
A.6
, with probability
1-o(1)
,

(i)

d_{G}(v,R)\geq(1-2\theta^{\prime})\binom{|R|}{k-\ell-1}
for every
v\in V(G)\setminus V(P_{0})
, and

(ii)

d_{H}(S,R)\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma/4)|R|
for every

Moreover, as

implies that

. Therefore, there is a choice of

, we have

R1

\alpha n^{\prime}\geq|R^{\prime}|\geq(1-\mu)\alpha n^{\prime}
,

R2

d_{G}(v,R^{\prime})\geq(1-\mu)\binom{|R^{\prime}|}{k-\ell-1}
for every
v\in V(G)\setminus V(P_{0})
, and

d_{H}(S,R^{\prime})\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma/8)|R^{\prime}|
for every
S\in\binom{V(H)}{k-1}
.

Let
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(H^{\prime})\setminus(R^{\prime}\cup V(P_{0}))
be a subset obtained by removing at most
k-\ell
vertices so that
|V^{\prime}|
is divisible by
k-\ell
. We write
H^{\prime\prime}=H[V^{\prime}]
and note that
\delta(H^{\prime\prime})\geq(\delta_{k,\ell}+\gamma/16)|H^{\prime\prime}|
. Use Theorem
1.1
to find a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle in
H^{\prime\prime}
, and hence
H^{\prime\prime}
contains an
\ell
-path
P
with
|P|\geq|H^{\prime\prime}|-2k
. Let
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{\ell}^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=(y_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{\ell}^{\prime})
be the ends of

, and let

Introduction

The goal of this work, refining and extending our earlier paper
[
13
]
,
is to present a context and a point of view for the study of
representations of familiar linear algebraic groups
\mathbb{G}
on vector spaces
V

over a field
k
. It constitutes a refinement and extension of our earlier work. We work
in the modular setting, fixing a field
k
of prime characteristic
p>0
;
our linear algebraic groups are connected affine group schemes of finite type
over
k
; the vector spaces are vector spaces over
k
, not necessarily finite
dimensional. A typical example for
\mathbb{G}
is the simple algebraic group
SL_{n}
.
The representations we consider are “rational”, formally defined as comodules
for the coordinate algebra
k[\mathbb{G}]
with coproduct given by the group
structure of
\mathbb{G}
.

We demonstrate how the theory of support varieties, first developed for finite
groups and eventually extended to all finite group schemes, can be extended
to a good theory of supports for
\mathbb{G}
-modules. Our first formulation is an
evident extension of the theory of
\pi
-points,
M\mapsto\Pi(G)_{M}
, for

\mathbb{G}
-modules
M
with
G
a finite group scheme as introduced by J. Pevtsova
and the author in
[
18
]
. This theory,
M\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{M}
, applies to
any linear algebraic group
\mathbb{G}
over an arbitrary field of
k
of positive
characteristic and any (rational)
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
. However, it is
difficult to make explicit because a point of
\Pi(G)

is an equivalence class of maps to the group algebra of some Frobenius
kernel
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\subset\mathbb{G}
.
Our second formulation
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{G})_{M}
,
valid only if the algebraic group
\mathbb{G}
is of exponential type, uses
varieties of 1-parameter subgroups
\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbb{G}
as introduced in our earlier
paper
[
13
]
.

The underlying justification for a theory of supports is that it is
is sensitive to extensions of representations.
Most support theories are based on cohomology, and
support theory offers a geometric picture of cohomology following foundational
work of D. Quillen
[
29
]
. For example,
the spectrum of the cohomology of the
r
-th Frobenius kernel

(SL_{n})_{(r)}
of
SL_{n}
is homeomorphic to the
variety of
r
-tuples of pairwise commuting,
p
-nilpotent
n\times n

matrices of trace 0
[
35
]
. Our theories are much influenced by
J. Carlson’s consideration of rank varieties for elementary abelian
p
-groups
[
5
]
.

As motivation for considering support theories for linear algebraic groups,
we remind the reader of a few consequences of support theory for
finite groups and finite group schemes.
An important role of support theories is that they offer a means of
classifying categorical structures associated to representation theories which
are “wild” (e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
).
Support theories have led to the identification and study
of various interesting special classes of representations such
as “modules of constant Jordan type”
[
7
]
and “mock injective”
modules
[
14
]
, provided new invariants for
G
-modules
[
21
]
,
and enabled the construction of algebraic vector bundles
[
20
]
,
[
4
]
.
Another fruitful theme has been the application of support theories to
investigate structural properties of the nilpotent cone and related
algebro-geometric objects
[
26
]
.

There are obstructions to formulating a suitable support theory for
\mathbb{G}
-modules. Not only is
rational cohomology of
\mathbb{G}
inadequate for this purpose, but also
the abelian category
Mod(\mathbb{G})
of
\mathbb{G}
-modules rarely has
non-trivial projective objects (see
[
10
]
) and its injective objects are almost
always infinite dimensional. The latter obstruction persuades us to consider
the bounded derived category
D^{b}(Mod(\mathbb{G}))
of the abelian category
Mod(\mathbb{G})

and to formulate associated stable categories. We verify that our constructions

C^{\bullet}\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}
and
C^{\bullet}\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}

are well defined on objects of the stable category
StMod(\mathbb{G})
and satisfy the
standard properties expected of a good theory of supports.
Although
C^{\bullet}\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}
satisfies
numerous good properties, we are far from recognizing which subspaces of

\Pi(\mathbb{G})
are realizable as supports for a bounded complex of
\mathbb{G}
-modules.
We lack the analogue for
\mathbb{G}
-modules of the central result of J. Carlson
(easily extended from finite groups to arbitrary finite group scheme
G
)
that any closed subvariety of
\Pi(G)
can be
realized as the support of some finite dimensional
G
-module
[
6
]
.

,

1.
Comparison of support theories for finite group schemes

We review a few salient features of four formulations of support theories for
modules
M
for a finite group scheme
G

M\mapsto\mathbb{P}|G|_{M};\quad M\mapsto\mathbb{P}V_{r}(G)_{M};\quad M\mapsto\Pi(G)_{M};\quad M\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}_{r}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}

and establish their close relationships.
In Theorem
1.3
, we recall that the cohomological support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}|G|_{M}

agrees with the infinitesimal 1-parameter support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}V_{r}(G)_{M}
for an
infinitesimal group scheme of height
\leq r
provided that
M
is finite dimensional. In Theorem
1.5
,
we recall that the cohomological support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}|G|_{M}
is equivalent to the

\pi
-point support theory
M\mapsto\Pi(G)_{M}
for any finite group scheme provided
M
is
finite dimensional. In Theorem
1.6
, we make explicit the equivalence
between
M\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}
and
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}
.

This section concludes with the formulation the exponential support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}_{r}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}

for a Frobenius kernel of a linear algebraic group
\mathbb{G}
of exponential type. Our definition of a linear
algebraic group of exponential type in Definition
1.8
allows the exponential

\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})\times\mathbb{G}_{a}\ \to\ \mathbb{G}
to be a continuous algebraic map (a rational map with unique
specialization at every geometric point).
In Theorem
1.12
, we demonstrate that the exponential support theory

M\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}_{r}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}
agrees with the infinitesimal 1-parameter support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}

for Frobenius kernels
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
of linear algebraic groups of exponential type.
This exponential support theory is an an extension of joint work with A. Suslin
and C. Bendel for infinitesimal group schemes

[
34
]
,
[
35
]
which itself extends earlier joint work with B. Parshall
[
16
]
.
In some sense,
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{G})_{M}
is the “rank variety” formulation of

M\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{M}
.

Recollection 1.1

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a finite group scheme.
We denote by

\epsilon_{r}:k\mathbb{G}_{a(1)}\simeq k[t]/t^{p}\to k\mathbb{G}_{a},\quad t\mapsto u_{r-1}.

We also use

to denote the factorization of (

) through

The analysis of

Let

k\mathbb{G}_{a}=k[u_{0},\ldots,u_{n},\ldots]/(u_{i}^{p})

4.
Stable module categories

For a finite group scheme
G
, a natural domain for support theory is the stable module
category
StMod(G)
, with triangulated category structure introduced by Happel
[
22
]
.
This triangulated structure utilizes the fact that the group algebra
kG
is self-injective, implying that projective
objects are the same as injective objects in the abelian category
Mod(G)
.
Our primary objective
in this section is to introduce in Definition
4.7
the triangulated categories

StMod(\mathbb{G})
and
stmod(\mathbb{G})
associated to the triangulated structures of the homotopy
categories of (cochain) complexes
\mathcal{K}^{b}(Mod(\mathbb{G}))
and
\mathcal{K}^{b}(mod(\mathbb{G}))
.

We begin by recalling various constructions associated to the abelian category
Mod(G)

of
G
-modules and the full abelian subcategory
mod(G)

of finite dimensional
G
-modules for an affine group scheme
G
over
k
.
We denote by
CH(Mod(G))
the abelian category of (cochain) complexes of
G
-modules
and by
\mathcal{K}(Mod(G))
the associated homotopy category with respect to cochain homotopy equivalence.
The additive category
\mathcal{K}(Mod(G))
has the natural structure of a triangulated category with
basic exact triangles
C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}\to E^{\bullet}\to C^{\bullet}[1]
associated to short
exact sequences
0\to C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}\to E^{\bullet}\to 0
in
CH(Mod(G))
. We are primarily
interested in the analogous structures
CH^{b}(Mod(G)),\ \mathcal{K}^{b}(Mod(G))
whose objects are bounded
complexes of
\mathbb{G}
-modules and their full subcategories
CH^{b}(mod(G)),\ \mathcal{K}^{b}(mod(G))

of bounded complexes of finite dimensional
G
-modules,

We say that a bounded complex
C^{\bullet}
of
G
-modules in
CH^{b}(Mod(G))

has length
\leq d
if there exists some integer
m
such that
C^{i}=0
for
i<m
or
i>m+d
.
In particular, complexes
M[n]
which have the
G
-module
M
in some degree
n
and
0 in all other degrees have length 0.

We recall that the stable module category
StMod(G)
of a finite group scheme
G
is the
additive category whose objects are
G
-modules and whose abelian group of maps

Hom_{StMod(G)}(M,N)
is the quotient of
Hom_{Mod(G)}(M,N)
by the subgroup of maps

M\to N
which factor through an injective
G
-module. One equips
StMod(G)
with
a triangulated structure whose +1 shift is given by sending
M
to the
cokernel
\Omega^{-1}(M)
of a minimal embedding
M\hookrightarrow I_{M}
with
I_{M}

an injective
G
-module, and whose -1 shift is given by sending
M
to the kernel
\Omega^{1}(M)

of a minimal surjection
P_{M}\twoheadrightarrow M
with
P_{M}
a projective
G
-module.
We define
stmod(G)\subset StMod(G)

to be the tensor triangulated subcategory whose objects are finite dimensional
G
-modules.

If
\mathfrak{C}\ \hookrightarrow\ \mathfrak{D}
is an embedding of triangulated categories (which implies by
definition that the image of
\mathfrak{C}
is a full subcategory of
\mathfrak{D}
), then the Verdier
quotient
\mathfrak{D}\ \to\ \mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}
is the universal triangulated map from
\mathfrak{D}
to
a triangulated category
\mathcal{E}
with kernel containing
\mathfrak{C}
. If
\mathfrak{C}
is a thick subcategory
(i.e., containing any summand in
\mathfrak{D}
of an object of
\mathfrak{C}
), then the kernel of

\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}
equals
\mathfrak{C}
. The Verdier quotient is constructed as
the category whose objects are the same as objects of
\mathfrak{D}
and whose maps
X\to Y

in
\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}
are
equivalence classes of “roofs”: triples
X\leftarrow E\to Y
in
\mathfrak{D}
such that the cone of
E\to X

is an object of
\mathfrak{C}
. We refer the reader
to
[
27
]
for a detailed discussion of Verdier quotients of triangulated categories.

Granted the construction of the Verdier quotient, we readily observe that
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}

is the localization
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
, where
mor_{\mathfrak{C}}
is the class of maps
X\to Y

in
\mathfrak{D}
whose cone lies in
\mathfrak{C}
. Namely,
mor_{\mathfrak{C}}
is a “multiplicative system”
as considered for example in
[
36
, 10.3.4]
. Since
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}
sends each
X\to Y

in
mor_{\mathfrak{C}}
to an invertible map,
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
is well defined and factors

\mathfrak{D}\ \to\ \mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}
; on the other hand, granted the existence of
\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
,
the fact that
\mathfrak{C}
lies in the kernel of
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
provides the inverse functor

\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{C}\to\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
factoring
\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}[mor_{\mathfrak{C}}^{-1}]
.

We remind the reader that the bounded derived category of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
,
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
,
is the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
of bounded chain complexes of
\mathcal{A}

by the thick triangulated subcategory
Quasi^{b}(\mathcal{A})
of bounded complexes quasi-isomorphic to 0
(i.e., which are exact). If
\mathcal{A}
has enough injective objects (for example, if
\mathcal{A}
is the category
of
G
-modules for an affine group scheme), then
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
can be defined more simply by first
explicitly defining the derived category
D^{+}(\mathcal{A})
of complexes bounded below using injective
resolutions and then restricting to objects which are bounded complexes.
If
f:I^{\bullet}\to J^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism (i.e., induces an isomorphism in
cohomology) of bounded complexes of injective
G
-modules, then
f

is a chain homotopy equivalence. Consequently, the homotopy category
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}(Inj)
of bounded complexes for
the full additive subcategory
Inj(Mod(G))\subset Mod(G)
embeds as a full subcategory of
D(Mod(G))
.

The following well-known theorem (see, for example,
[
30
, Thm2.1]
) provides motivation
for our constructions. We recall that a perfect complex of
G
-modules for a finite group
scheme
G
is a bounded complex of finite dimensional
G
-modules quasi-isomorphic to a
bounded complex of finite dimensional injective
G
-modules. Thus, the subcategory

Perf(G)\ \subset\ D^{b}(mod(G))
of perfect complexes is the essential image in
D^{b}(mod(G))

of the homotopy category of bounded complexes of finite dimensional injective
G
-modules.

\mathcal{K}^{b}(inj(G))\ \subset\ D^{b}(mod(G))
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
G
is any finite group scheme, then the natural map

mod(G)\ \to\ \mathcal{K}^{b}(mod(G))
induces an equivalence of tensor triangulated categories

\Psi:stmod(G)\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\quad D^{b}(mod(G))/Perf(G).

This theorem is somewhat surprising, even in the case of
\mathbb{G}_{a(1)}
(i.e.,
k[t]/t^{p}
-modules).
Consider the complex
C^{\bullet}
with
C^{0}=k[t]/t^{p}
,
C^{1}=k[t]/t^{p}
and

d:C^{0}\to C^{1}
given by multiplication by
t
; thus
C^{\bullet}\not=0
in
D^{b}(mod(G))

but is 0 in
stmod(G)
. In the proof of Theorem
4.2
, the construction of

\tilde{\Phi}:D^{b}(mod(G))\to stmod(G)
applied to the complex
C^{\bullet}

sends
C^{\bullet}
to the cone of
\tilde{\Phi}(C^{0})=0\in stmod(G)
mapping to

\tilde{\Phi}(C^{1})=0\in stmod(G)
which is 0.

We shall utilize the truncation functors

\tau_{\leq n},\tau_{\geq m}:CH(Mod(G))\ \to CH(Mod(G)),\quad C^{\bullet}\mapsto\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})\hookrightarrow C^{\bullet},\quad C^{\bullet}\twoheadrightarrow\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet}).

Here,
\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})
is the subcomplex of
C^{\bullet}
such that
\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})^{i}=0

for
i>n
,
\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})^{n}=ker\{d^{n}\}
, and
\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})^{i}=C^{i}
for
i<n
;
and
\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet})
is the quotient complex of
C^{\bullet}
such that
\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet})^{n}=0

for
n<m
,
\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet})^{m}=C^{m}/im\{d^{m-1}\}
, and
\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet})^{n}=C^{n}
for
n>m
.
So defined, if
C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism, then
\tau_{\leq n}(C^{\bullet})\to\tau_{\leq n}(D^{\bullet})
and
\tau_{\geq m}(C^{\bullet})\to\tau_{\geq m}(D^{\bullet})
are also quasi-isomorphisms.

In the following theorem, we consider the thick tensor triangulated subcategory

\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G))\ \subset\ D^{b}(Mod(G))
of complexes quasi-isomorphic to bounded complexes
of injective
G
-modules. This is the “essential image” of
\mathcal{K}^{b}(Inj(G))\ \subset D^{b}(Mod(G))
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(see
[
30
, Thm2.1]
)
For any finite group scheme
G
, the natural map

Mod(G)\ \to\ \mathcal{K}^{b}(Mod(G))
(sending the
G
-module
M
to the
chain complex
M[0]
concentrated in degree 0) induces an equivalence of
tensor triangulated categories

\Psi:StMod(G)\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\quad D^{b}(Mod(G))/\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G)).

Furthermore, we construct
\tilde{\Phi}:D^{b}(Mod(G))\ \to\ StMod(G)
inducing the inverse

(4.2.1)

\Phi=\Psi^{-1}:D^{b}(Mod(G))/\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G))\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\quad StMod(G)

sending
M[n]
to
\Omega^{-n}(M)
.

Proof.

Necessarily,
\Psi(\Omega^{n}(M))\ =\ M[-n]\in D^{b}(Mod(G))/Inj^{b}(Mod(G))
.
As explained in Rickard’s proof of
[
30
, Thm2.1]
, an exact triangle
X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
in
StMod(G)
arises from a pushout diagram in

Mod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
from the short exact sequence
0\to X\to I\to X[1]\to 0

(with
I
injective) along
X\to Y
to the short exact sequence

0\to Y\to Z\to X[1]\to 0
. Consequently, the result of applying
\Psi
to a such
an exact triangle in
StMod(G)
,
\Psi(X)\to\Psi(Y)\to\Psi(Z)\to\Psi(X)[1]
,
is isomorphic in
D^{b}(Mod(G))/Inj^{b}(Mod(G))
to the image of the exact
triangle in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(Mod(G))
arising from the short exact sequence

0\to X\to Y\oplus I\to(Y\oplus I)/X\to 0
in
Mod(G)
. Thus,
\Psi
is exact; in
other words, it sends exact triangles to exact triangles.

To show that
\Psi
is a full functor, we first show that
Mod(G)\to D^{b}(Mod(G))
is
full, then observe that
D^{b}(Mod(G))\to D^{b}(Mod(G))/\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G))
is full, so
that
\Psi
must also be full. Let
M,\ N
be
G
-modules and consider a map
from
M[0]
to
N[0]
in
D^{b}(Mod(G))
represented by a “roof”
M[0]\ \leftarrow C^{\bullet}\ \to\ N[0]
in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(Mod(G))
. This roof factors through the quotient map

C^{\bullet}\twoheadrightarrow\tau_{\geq 0}(C^{\bullet})
(which is a quasi-isomorphism).
On the other hand,
M\ \simeq\ ker\{d^{0}\}/im\{d^{-1}\}\hookrightarrow\tau_{\geq 0}(C^{\bullet})^{0}
.
Thus, there is a natural map of roofs from

(M[0]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle=}}{{\leftarrow}}M[0]\to N[0)]
to
(M[0]\ \leftarrow\tau_{\geq 0}(C^{\bullet})\ \to\ N[0])
,
thereby establishing the fullness of
Mod(G)\to D^{b}(Mod(G))
.

We next show that
Hom_{D^{b}(Mod(G))}(M,N)\ \to\ Hom_{D^{b}(Mod(G))/\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G))}(M,N)
is
surjective. Consider a roof of maps
M[0]\ \leftarrow C^{\bullet}\ \to\ N[0]
in
D^{b}(Mod(G))
, with
associated exact triangle
J^{\bullet}\to C^{\bullet}\to M[0]
in
D^{b}(Mod(\mathbb{G}))
for some bounded
complex
J^{\bullet}
of injective
G
-modules. Replacing
C^{\bullet}
by the mapping
cylinder of
J^{\bullet}\to C^{\bullet}
if necessary, we may assume that
J^{\bullet}\hookrightarrow C^{\bullet}

so that there is an isomorphism
C^{\bullet}\to((C^{\bullet}/J^{\bullet})\oplus J^{\bullet})
and
C^{\bullet}\to M[0]

factors as
C^{\bullet}\to((C^{\bullet}/J^{\bullet})\oplus J^{\bullet})\to M[0]
with
C^{\bullet}/J^{\bullet}\to M[0]
an
isomorphism in
D^{b}(Mod(G))
. Thus, the given roof is equal as a map in
D^{b}(Mod(G))/\mathcal{I}nj^{b}(Mod(G))

to a roof of the type considered in the previous paragraph and thus is the image under

\Psi
of a map
M\to N
.

As shown in the proof of
[
30
, Thm2.1]
, exactness and fullness of
\Psi

\mathbb{G}

We remind the reader that a full triangulated subcategory

is the full triangulated subcategory of

whose objects are obtained by repeatedly applying the operations of taking finite sums of objects, taking summands of

E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(k)\otimes X\to X\to F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(k)\otimes X\to(E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(k)\otimes X)[1].

If
\mathfrak{C}
is a tensor ideal inside
stmod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
, then Rickard ’s arguments using
adjunction relating
Hom(M^{\vee},\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{C}}(k))
to
M\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{C}}(k)
(and similarly
for
\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{C}}(k))
) provide a test for whether or not
M\in stmod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})

belongs to
\mathfrak{C}
. This argument requires the compactness of
M

and the tensor ideal condition on
\mathfrak{C}
.

We complement Theorem
6.5
with the following extension of Rickard’s test
in terms of Rickard’s idempotents, applying now to
C^{\bullet}
an object of
stmod(\mathbb{G})
and

\mathfrak{C}\subset stmod(\mathbb{G})
an
(r)
-complete, thick tensor ideal.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group and let
\mathfrak{C}\ \subset\ stmod(\mathbb{G})
be an
thick tensor ideal which is
(r)
-complete. For each
r>0
, we consider the Rickard idempotent functors

E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(-),\ F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(-):StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})\to StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})

for the thick tensor ideal
\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}\subset stmod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
of Definition
6.1

associated to
\mathfrak{C}
.

(1)

C^{\bullet}\ \in\ \mathfrak{C}
if and only if for all
r>0

(6.6.1)

F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\ \simeq\ F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(k)\otimes C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\ =0\quad{\text{i}n}\ StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}).

(2)

C^{\bullet}\ \in\ \mathfrak{C}
if and only if for all
r>0

(6.6.2)

E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\ \simeq\ E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(k)\otimes C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\ \simeq\ C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\quad{\text{i}n}\ StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}).

Proof.

We assume that
\mathfrak{C}\ \subset\ stmod(\mathbb{G})
is an
(r)
-complete thick tensor ideal.
If
C^{\bullet}\in\mathfrak{C}
, then
C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}
for all
r>0
so that by Rickard’s results

F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})=0\in StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for all
r>0
(see
[
18
]
). Conversely,
if
F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})=0\in StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for all
r>0
, then Rickard’s results for
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}

tell us that
C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\in\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}
for all
r>0
; since
\mathfrak{C}\ \subset\ stmod(\mathbb{G})
is

(r)
-complete, this implies that
C^{\bullet}\in\mathfrak{C}
.

If
C^{\bullet}\in\mathfrak{C}
, then Rickard’s results tell us
that
E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\ \simeq\ C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\in StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for all
r>0
. Conversely,
if
E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\ \simeq\ C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}
in
StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for all
r>0
, then Rickard’s
exact triangles

E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\to C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\to F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\to E_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})[1]

imply that
F_{\mathfrak{C}_{(r)}}(C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})=0\in StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for all
r>0

so that (
6.6.1
) implies that
C^{\bullet}\in\mathfrak{C}
.
∎

We next make explicit how our earlier definitions and constructions for subcategories
\mathfrak{C}\ \subset stmod(\mathbb{G})

can be modified to yield similar definitions and constructions for subcategories
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\ \subset StMod(\mathbb{G})
.
We remind the reader that a
localizing subcategory
of a triangulated category admitting arbitrary direct sums
is a full triangulated subcategory closed under isomorphisms and arbitrary direct sums.

In parallel with Definition
6.1
, we denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}
the essential image
of a localizing subcategory
\tilde{C}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G})
under the restriction map

StMod(\mathbb{G})\to StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
; we denote by

(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
the localizing subcategory generated by
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}
.
We say that a localizing subcategory

\tilde{C}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G})
is an
_
(r)
-complete localizing
subcategory of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
if

(6.6.3)

C^{\bullet}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\quad\iff\quad C^{\bullet}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}\ \in\ (\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus},\ \forall r>0.

As for subcategories of
stmod(\mathbb{G})
, we define
\Pi(\mathbb{G},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})\ \subset\ \Pi(\mathbb{G})
for any subcategory

\tilde{C}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G})
to be the subset

\Pi(\mathbb{G},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})\ \equiv\ \bigcup_{C^{\bullet}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}}\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}\ \subset\ \Pi(\mathbb{G}).

We provide a natural analogue of Proposition
6.3
, with
(r)
-complete localizing
subcategories of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
playing the role
(r)
-complete thick ideals of
stmod(\mathbb{G})
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group and let

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G})
be a triangulated subcategory. We denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}

the full subcategory of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
whose objects are
those bounded complexes
C^{\bullet}\in Stmod(\mathbb{G})
with the property
that the restriction of
C^{\bullet}
to
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
lies in
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus}
for every
r>0
.
Then
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}\ \subset StMod(\mathbb{G})
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
is a localizing subcategory of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
.

(2)

(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus}\ =\ (\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus})_{(r)}^{\oplus}
,
so that
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus})^{\oplus}\ =\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
.

(3)

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
is the minimal
(r)
-complete localizing subcategory of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
containing
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
.

(4)

The natural embedding
\Pi(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})\ \hookrightarrow\ \Pi\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus})
is the identity
for each
r>0
.

Proof.

The proof is a repetition of the proof of Proposition
6.3
,
replacing
stmod(\mathbb{G})
by
StMod(\mathbb{G})
, replacing
(r)
-complete thick ideals by
(r)
-complete localizing
subcategories, and replacing
(-)^{\vee}
by
(-)^{\oplus}
.

In more detail, if
C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}\to E^{\bullet}\to C^{\bullet}[1]
is an exact triangle in
StMod(\mathbb{G})
with

C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
, then the restriction of this exact triangle to

StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
is an exact triangle in
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{(r)}^{\oplus}
for all
r>0
so that
E^{\bullet}
is in
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
.
Similarly, if
\{C^{\bullet}_{\alpha},\ \alpha\in A\}

is a family of bounded complexes in
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}\subset StMod(\mathbb{G})
, then the restriction
of
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}C^{\bullet}_{\alpha}
to
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
lies in the localizing subcatory
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus}\ \subset\ StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
for every
r>0
so that
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}C^{\bullet}_{\alpha}

is also in
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
. Thus,
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}\subset StMod(\mathbb{G})
is a localizing subcategory.

The operations on objects of
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
(taking cones and arbitrary direct sums) which produce
objects of
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
beginning with objects of
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
restrict
to internal operations on
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})^{\oplus}_{(r)}
since this is a localizing subcategory,
so that
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})_{(r)}^{\oplus}\ =\ (\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus})_{(r)}^{\oplus}
.
This immediately implies that
(\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus})^{\oplus}\ =\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
and that

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{\oplus}
is an
(r)
-complete localizing subcategory of
StMod(\mathbb{G})
.

Finally, observe that the operations of taking cones and arbitrary direct sums in
StMod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})

preserve the property that if the support of the input object of an operation is contained in

\Pi(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})
, then the support of the output object is also contained in
\Pi(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}})
.
As explained for proof of Proposition
6.3
(4), this implies the last assertion.
∎

The following definition is the evident analogue for subcategories of

of

.

Definition 6.8

Consider a linear algebraic group

We have the following analogue of Proposition
6.4
for
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{X}\subset StMod(\mathbb{G})
.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group and let

be a subset.
Then
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{X}
is an
(r)
-complete localizing tensor ideal of

By Theorem

Assume that

is an object of

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group. The correspondences

X\ \mapsto\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{X},\quad\quad\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\ \mapsto\ \Pi(\mathbb{G},\tilde{\mathfrak{C}})

restrict to give mutually inverse bijections

\longleftrightarrow\quad\quad\{(r){\text{-}}complete,\ localizing\ subcategories\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\subset StMod(\mathbb{G})\}.

The proof that the evident inclusion

-realizable subset of

is a repetition of the argument

To complete the proof, we must show that the natural inclusion

(r)
-complete

\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}\ \hookrightarrow\ StMod(\mathbb{G})

7.
Questions and challenges

Our first remark contrasts our theory of supports
M\mapsto\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{M}

with various cohomological constructions for
\mathbb{G}
.

Remark 7.1
.

The (rational) cohomology
H^{*}(\mathbb{G},k)
is invariant under the (conjugation)
action of
\mathbb{G}
, whereas the action of
\mathbb{G}
on
\Pi(\mathbb{G})
is typically non-trivial. Thus, it is no surprise that if

\mathbb{G}
is semi-simple, then
H^{*}(\mathbb{G},k)
vanishes in positive degrees and
H^{*}(\mathbb{G},M)
is
finite dimensional for any finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-module
[
9
, Thm2.4]
. For
\mathbb{G}
unipotent,

H^{*}(\mathbb{G},k)
does not vanish, but the invariance property implies that
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{\bullet}(\mathbb{G},k)

is “too small” to capture much information about
Mod(\mathbb{G})
if
\mathbb{G}
is not commutative.

On the other hand, if
G
is a finite group scheme, then
|G|\equiv\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{\bullet}(G,k)
leads to a
“good” cohomological support theory for
mod(G)
, namely
M\ \mapsto\ |G|_{M}
,
where
|G|_{M}
is defined as the variety of the annihilator ideal of the
H^{\bullet}(G,k)
-module
Ext_{G}^{*}(M,M)
.
This suggests one might consider for an algebraic group
\mathbb{G}
the inverse system
\{H^{\bullet}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k),r>0\}

associated to the hyperalgebra
\varinjlim_{r}k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
.
However, computations in
[
15
]
indicate that

\varprojlim_{r}H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},M)
as a
\varprojlim_{r}H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)
-module is not useful
in considering
Mod(\mathbb{G})
even in the case of
G
unipotent.

Associating a useful cohomology theory for
Mod(\mathbb{G})
using the “pro-object of finite group schemes”

\cdots\to\mathbb{G}_{(r+1)}\to\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\to\cdots
remains a challenge.

Remark 7.2
.

A major challenge is to establish criteria for subsets
X\ \subset\ \Pi(\mathbb{G})
to be realizable
as
\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{M}
or
\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}
. A better understanding of the support of
\mathbb{G}
-modules
obtained by inducing
H
-modules to
\mathbb{G}
modules for various closed subgroups of

H\ \subset\mathbb{G}
would be valuable when investigating this realizability challenge.

A specific challenge is to search for examples of
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant closed subsets
of
\Pi(\mathbb{G})
which can not be realized as
\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}
for specific groups
\mathbb{G}
.

Remark 7.3
.

It is natural to ask about the Balmer spectrum (see
[
1
]
) of the triangulated category
stmod(\mathbb{G})

in light of Theorem
6.5
. New methods of investigating the Balmer
spectrum in this non-Noetherian setting are are presumably needed. In particular, there are typically
infinitely many isomorphism classes of finite dimensional irreducible
\mathbb{G}
-modules.

As a start, one might investigate the
prime
,
(r)
-complete, tensor ideals of
stmod(\mathbb{G})
.
These should correspond to ideals of the form
\mathfrak{C}_{X}\ \subset\ stmod(\mathbb{G})
as

X
varies over subspaces of the form
\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{C^{\bullet}}
which can not be written a non-trivial union of
the form
\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{D^{\bullet}}\cup\Pi(\mathbb{G})_{E^{\bullet}}
for complexes
C^{\bullet},\ D^{\bullet},\ E^{\bullet}
of
bounded complexes of finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-modules.

Remark 7.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{E})
be an algebraic group of exponential type. The support theory

M\ \mapsto\ \mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{G})_{M}\ \simeq\ \Pi(\mathbb{G})_{M}
extracts
minimal information from the
“local operators”
(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{r}(\underline{B})})_{*}(u_{r-1})

and
\sum_{s\geq 0}^{r-1}(\mathcal{E}_{B_{s}}(u_{s})
at points of
\mathfrak{C}_{r}(\mathbb{G})
.

For example, we only use the zero locus of the stable Jordan type function of Definition
1.10
.
One could ask for restrictions of values of this stable Jordan
type function for various classes of
\mathbb{G}
-modules. As in
[
19
]
,
one could formulate refinements of our support theory
M\ \mapsto\ \mathbb{P}\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{G})_{M}

using this stable Jordan type function.

Remark 7.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{E})
be an algebraic group of exponential type.
We do not utilize the scheme structure of
\mathfrak{C}_{r}(\mathbb{G})
, but only the topological
space of scheme-theoretic points. The scheme structure was used in

[
20
]
and subsequent papers to associate sheaves and vector bundles to
representations of infinitesimal finite group schemes. Can this scheme structure be
used to obtain more information about
\mathbb{G}
-modules not seen by restrictions
to the family
\{\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\}
of Frobenius kernels of
\mathbb{G}
?

Remark 7.6
.

One can consider formal 1-parameter subgroups
for a linear algebraic group of exponential type
(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{E})
. Namely, to an
infinite sequence
\widehat{\underline{B}}=(B_{0},\ldots,B_{n},\ldots)
of pair-wise commuting
K
-points of

\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
and a
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
, one can associate a
p
-nilpotent operator

{\mathcal{E}_{\widehat{\underline{B}}}:}M_{K}\ \to\ M_{K}
generalizing the operators we have considered in
this paper.

At present we lack the technology to use these formal 1-parameter groups to investigate the
derived category
D^{b}(Mod(\mathbb{G}))
. An appealing feature of such formal 1-parameter groups is
that they provide information not seen by
\{\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\}
.

Remark 7.7
.

In other contexts in which there is an analogous classification theorem,
knowledge of “realizable” closed subsets provides insight into the collection of thick tensor ideals
of a “representation category.”
For
\mathfrak{C}\ \subset\ stmod(\mathbb{G})
, could one use some knowledge
of
(r)
-complete, thick tensor ideals to determine interesting classes of finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-modules?

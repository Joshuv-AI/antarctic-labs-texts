The Chambolle–Pock method converges weakly with θ > 1 / 2 \theta>1/2 and τ ​ σ ​ ‖ L ‖ 2 < 4 / ( 1 + 2 ​ θ ) \tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}<4/(1+2\theta)
Sebastian Banert, Manu Upadhyaya, Pontus Giselsson

Abstract
The Chambolle–Pock method is a versatile three-parameter algorithm designed to solve a broad class of composite convex optimization problems, which encompass two proper, lower semicontinuous, and convex functions, along with a linear operator L L . The functions are accessed via their proximal operators, while the linear operator is evaluated in a forward manner. Among the three algorithm parameters τ \tau , σ \sigma , and θ \theta ; τ , σ > 0 \tau,\sigma>0 serve as step sizes for the proximal operators, and θ \theta is an extrapolation step parameter. Previous convergence results have been based on the assumption that θ = 1 \theta=1 . We demonstrate that weak convergence is achievable whenever θ > 1 / 2 \theta>1/2 and τ ​ σ ​ ‖ L ‖ 2 < 4 / ( 1 + 2 ​ θ ) \tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}<4/\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)} . Moreover, we establish tightness of the step size bound by providing an example that is non-convergent whenever the second bound is violated.



1
The Chambolle–Pock method

The Chambolle–Pock method
[
2
]
solves convex-concave saddle-point problems of the form

\displaystyle\minimize_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\maximize_{y\in\mathcal{G}}\;f\mathord{\left(x\right)}+\mathord{\left\langle Lx,y\right\rangle}-g^{*}\mathord{\left(y\right)},

(1)

where
f\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\mathord{\left\{\infty\right\}}
and
g\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\mathord{\left\{\infty\right\}}
are proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous functions,
g^{*}
is the convex conjugate of
g
,
L\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is a nonzero bounded linear operator, and
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
are real Hilbert spaces. This is a primal–dual formulation of the primal composite optimization problem

\displaystyle\minimize_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\;f\mathord{\left(x\right)}+g\mathord{\left(Lx\right)}.

(2)

We assume that (
1
) has at least one solution
(x_{\star},y_{\star})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}
that therefore satisfies the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) condition

\displaystyle-L^{*}y_{\star}

\displaystyle\in\partial f\mathord{\left(x_{\star}\right)},

(3a)

\displaystyle Lx_{\star}

\displaystyle\in\partial g^{*}\mathord{\left(y_{\star}\right)}.

(3b)

The Chambolle–Pock method searches for such KKT points by iterating

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Prox}^{\tau}_{f}\mathord{\left(x_{k}-\tau L^{*}y_{k}\right)},

(4a)

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Prox}^{\sigma}_{g^{*}}\mathord{\left(y_{k}+\sigma L\mathord{\left(x_{k+1}+\theta\mathord{\left(x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right)}\right)}\right)},

(4b)

where each function’s proximal operators, the linear operator
L
and its adjoint
L^{*}
are evaluated once each in every iteration.

This algorithm is guaranteed to converge to a solution whenever
\theta=1
and the positive step size parameters
\tau
and
\sigma
satisfy
\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}<1
as shown in the original work
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, where
\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}
is the operator norm.
It was soon thereafter realized in
[
5
]
that for
\theta=1
, the algorithm is an instance of the preconditioned proximal point method, applied to a specific maximally monotone inclusion problem, with a
strongly positive
and
symmetric
preconditioner.
Strong positivity of the preconditioner is lost for
\theta=1
whenever
\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\geq 1
, disabling analysis via the standard preconditioned proximal point method.
In
[
3
]
, sequence convergence with
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}\leq 1
is established using a modified proximal point analysis and in
[
7
]
, convergence to a solution is established for the wider range
\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}<4/3
. All these results are in finite-dimensional settings and assume
\theta=1
.

The symmetry of the proximal point preconditioner is lost whenever
\theta\neq 1
. Non-symmetric proximal point iterates are often augmented with different types of correction steps such as projection or momentum correction as in
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
4
]
to form convergent algorithms. To the best of our knowledge, no convergence results exist for the pure Chambolle–Pock method, without additional correction, when
\theta\neq 1
.

The contribution of this paper lies in establishing, in infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces, ergodic
\mathcal{O}\mathord{\left(1/n\right)}
primal–dual gap convergence whenever
\theta\geq 1/2
,

\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\leq\frac{4}{1+2\theta},

(5)

and one of these inequalities holds strictly, as well as weak convergence to a solution whenever both these inequalities are strict. The analysis is based on a Lyapunov inequality that has been derived with the aid of the methodology in
[
9
]
. We also establish tightness of the step size bound by providing an example of which sequence convergence is lost whenever

\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\geq\frac{4}{1+2\theta}.

(6)

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
denote real Hilbert spaces. All norms
\mathord{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
are canonical norms where the inner-product will be clear from the context. Suppose that

(7)

The

of

with

is given by

\mathrm{Prox}^{\tau}_{f}\mathord{\left(x\right)}=\argmin_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\mathord{\left(f\mathord{\left(y\right)}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\mathord{\left\lVert y-x\right\rVert}^{2}\right)},

which is uniquely defined. If

, we have from
[
1
, Section 24.1]
that

p=\mathrm{Prox}^{\tau}_{f}\mathord{\left(x\right)}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{1}{\tau}\mathord{\left(x-p\right)}\in\partial f\mathord{\left(p\right)}

(8)

and

f\mathord{\left(\mathrm{Prox}^{\tau}_{f}(x)\right)}<\infty.

(9)

Moreover, the
convex conjugate
of

, denoted

, is the proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function given by
f^{*}(u)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\mathord{\left(\left\langle u,x\right\rangle-f(x)\right)}

.

5
Lyapunov analysis

In this section, we provide a Lyapunov analysis of the Chambolle–Pock method, which serves as the basis for our convergence results. Before presenting this, we establish the nonnegativity of a certain coefficient that emerges in the Lyapunov analysis.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
\theta\geq{1}/{2}
and
\tau,\sigma>0

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle 8\theta-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}

\displaystyle\geq 8\theta-\frac{4\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}}{1+2\theta}=\frac{8\theta-4}{1+2\theta}=4-\frac{8}{1+2\theta}\geq 0,

where the first inequality is strict if
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}<{4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}

∎

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
\theta\geq{1}/{2}
and
\tau,\sigma>0
and that they jointly satisfy
0<\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}\leq{4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}
. Further suppose that at least one of
\theta\geq{1}/{2}
and
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}\leq 4/\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}
holds with strict inequality. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}-1\right)}\mathord{\left(4-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(2\theta+1\right)}\right)}\mathord{\left(4-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(2\theta-1\right)}\right)}}{16\tau\mathord{\left(8\theta-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}\right)}}\geq 0

with strict inequality if
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}<{4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}
and
\theta>{1}/{2}
and equality if
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}={4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}
or
\theta=1/2
.

Proof.

We know from
lemma

3
that the denominator is positive. The numerator consists of three factors. The first factor is positive if
\theta>{1}/{2}
and 0 if
\theta={1}/{2}

and 0 if
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}={4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}

and we conclude, using weak-strong closedness of maximally monotone operators
[
1
, Proposition 20.38]
, that
0\in A(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, implying

\displaystyle 0\in\partial f\mathord{\left(\bar{x}\right)}+L^{*}\bar{y},

\displaystyle 0\in\partial g^{*}(\bar{y})-L\bar{x},

i.e., the weak limit point is a KKT point. Since
\mathord{\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}\|x_{k+1}-x_{\star}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2\sigma}\|y_{k+1}-y_{\star}\|^{2}\right)}_{k=0}^{\infty}
converges and all weak limit points belong to the KKT solution set
Z
, we invoke
[
1
, Lemma 2.47]
to conclude weak convergence to a KKT point.
∎

7
Counterexample

This section proves that the result in
theorem

2
is tight, i.e., there is an example where (weak) convergence fails for each
\theta>1/2
whenever
\tau\sigma\|L\|^{2}\geq 4/\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{R}
, and consider problem (
1
) for the case
f=g^{*}=0
, and
L=1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\minimize_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\maximize_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\;xy.

(17)

The update rule, (
4
), for the Chambolle–Pock method then becomes

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\tau y_{k},

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=y_{k}+\sigma\mathord{\left(x_{k+1}+\theta\mathord{\left(x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right)}\right)},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x_{k+1}\\
y_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\tau\\
\sigma&1-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{k}\\
y_{k}\end{bmatrix},

where we assume that
\tau,\sigma>0
. Therefore,
\mathord{\left(x_{k},y_{k}\right)}_{k=0}^{\infty}
converges for an arbitrary initial point
\mathord{\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
to the solution
\mathord{\left(x_{\star},y_{\star}\right)}=\mathord{\left(0,0\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
if and only if both eigenvalues of the matrix above have magnitude less than
1
. The two eigenvalues of the matrix are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\mathord{\left(2-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}+\sqrt{\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}^{2}-4\right)}}\right)}

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\mathord{\left(2-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}-\sqrt{\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}^{2}-4\right)}}\right)}.

Suppose that the step-size condition of
theorem

2
is violated, i.e., that
\tau\sigma\geq 4/\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}
. In this case, the eigenvalues are distinct real numbers satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\leq-1\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\mathord{\left(2-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}+\sqrt{\tau^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathord{\left(1+\theta\right)}^{2}}\right)}=1.

In particular, the method fails to converge, which proves that the result in
theorem

2
is tight.

Note that the boundary condition
\tau\sigma=4/\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}
implies that
\lambda_{2}=-1
. Although the sequence
\mathord{\left(x_{k},y_{k}\right)}_{k=0}^{\infty}
does not necessarily converge in this setting, we have that
(x_{k+2},y_{k+2})-(x_{k},y_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, i.e., the difference between every second iterate converges to
0
. It turns out that this is not a coincidence as it always holds for the Chambolle–Pock algorithm on the boundary, as shown next.

8
Boundary

In this section, we show that whenever
\theta>1/2
and
\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}={4}/{\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}
, the sequence
\mathord{\left(\|x_{k+2}-x_{k}\|\right)}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is summable, implying that the difference between every second iterate converges to 0.

Due to
lemma

5
, we conclude that the coefficient in front of
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}
in the Lyapunov inequality in
lemma

4
is 0. Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{8\theta-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}}{16\tau}=\frac{8\theta(1+2\theta)-4\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}}{16\tau(1+2\theta)}=\frac{2\theta-1}{4\tau(1+2\theta)}

and

\displaystyle\frac{4\mathord{\left(1-\tau\sigma\theta\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\right)}}{8\theta-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\mathord{\left(1-2\theta\right)}}{\mathord{\left(8\theta-\tau\sigma\mathord{\left\lVert L\right\rVert}^{2}\mathord{\left(4\theta^{2}+1\right)}\right)}(1+2\theta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\mathord{\left(1-2\theta\right)}(1+2\theta)}{(8\theta-4)(1+2\theta)}

\displaystyle=-1,

implying that the Lyapunov inequality in
lemma

4
reads

V_{k+1}-V_{k}+F_{k}+G_{k}\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\mathord{\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}-\sigma\mathord{\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathord{\left(Lx_{k+1}-Lx_{k+2}\right)}-\theta\mathord{\left(Lx_{k}-Lx_{k+1}\right)}\right)}\right\rVert}^{2}\\
&\qquad-\frac{2\theta-1}{4\tau\mathord{\left(1+2\theta\right)}}\mathord{\left\lVert x_{k+2}-x_{k}\right\rVert}^{2},\end{aligned}

where
V_{k}
,
F_{k}
, and
G_{k}
are nonnegative for each nonnegative integers
k
.
Since
\theta>{1}/{2}
, we conclude using a telescoping summation argument that
\mathord{\left(\|x_{k+2}-x_{k}\|\right)}_{k=0}^{\infty}
indeed is summable.

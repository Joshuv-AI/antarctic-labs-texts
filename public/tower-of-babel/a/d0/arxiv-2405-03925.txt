Einstein-Grisaru-Zanon gravity
Ruben Campos Delgado

Abstract




May 2024 IPMU24-0020

Abstract

The leading
(\alpha^{\prime})^{3}
-correction to the gravitational low-energy effective action of closed (type II) superstring theory in four-spacetime dimensions defines the Einstein-Grisaru-Zanon gravity action that is applied for a calculation of the leading corrections to the Schwarzschild solution and the Hubble function in the Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker universe, in the first order with respect to the effective string-generated coupling. The solutions found are compared to the corresponding solutions in the Einstein-Bel-Robinson gravity that also modifies the Einstein gravity by the terms quartic in the spacetime curvature. We consider the black hole shadows in the Einstein-Grisaru-Zanon gravity theory and derive the upper bound on the string coupling parameter from the Hawking temperature of a black hole.

1
Introduction

The nonrenormalizability of the pure Einstein gravity in four spacetime dimensions was proved by Goroff and Sagnotti in 1985 by calculating the two-loop (on-shell) ultra-violet (UV) divergence cubic in the spacetime curvature
[
1
]
. In the case of
N=1
supergravity in four spacetime dimensions, the leading UV-divergence is expected in three loops, being quartic in the space-time curvature. The UV-completion of Einstein gravity in quantum theory is provided by closed (type II) superstring theory, where the
UV-counterterms of the quantized gravity appear with finite coefficients. The gravitational low-energy effective action (LEEA) in closed superstring theory can be computed
perturbatively, with respect to the string slope parameter
\alpha^{\prime}
and the string coupling
g_{s}
, resulting in the infinite series with respect to the spacetime curvature
[
2
]
. For instance, the perturbative
\alpha^{\prime}
-corrections are computable by deriving the multi-loop renormalization group beta-functions of the two-dimensional non-linear sigma-model (NLSM) describing propagation of a test string in spacetime, demanding quantum conformal invariance of the NLSM and, hence, the vanishing beta-functions as the effective equations of motion in spacetime, and then elevating those equations of motion to the LEEA in the critical dimension
[
3
]
. The existence of such action is assured by Zamolodchikov’s
c
-theorem
[
4
]
. This procedure reproduces the Einstein-Hilbert action in the one-loop approximation
[
5
]
, whereas the next
\alpha^{\prime}
-correction derived from the supersymmetric NLSM appears at four loops, being proportional to
(\alpha^{\prime})^{3}
and quartic in the spacetime curvature.

See Ref.
[
6
]
for the non-supersymmetric gravitational LEEA from bosonic closed string theory and its applications to black holes, with the

(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}
-correction being cubic in the spacetime curvature.
The leading perturbative correction to the closed superstring gravitational LEEA was first computed by Grisaru and Zanon in 1986
[
7

, see also Ref.
[
8

[

, see also Ref.
[

for a recent account.

In this Letter we apply the Einstein-Grisaru-Zanon (EGZ) gravitational LEEA to a derivation of the corresponding corrections to the standard metric solutions in Einstein’s General Relativity (GR), and compare them to the similar solutions in the Einstein-Bel-Robinson (EBR) gravity proposed in Ref.
[
11
]
.

2
EGZ versus EBR

The EGZ action is defined by

S_{\rm EGZ}[g]={{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\over{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left(R+{{\gamma}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}J\right),

(1)

where we have introduced the leading superstring correction
[
7
]
,

J=\left(R^{\mu\rho\sigma\nu}R_{\lambda\rho\sigma\tau}+{\textstyle{1\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{2}$}}}}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}\right)R_{\mu}^{\,\,\,\alpha\beta\lambda}R^{\tau}_{\,\,\,\alpha\beta\nu}~~,

(2)

and the new (dimensionless) coupling constant
\gamma
. The
M_{\rm Pl}
is the reduced Planck mass,
M_{\rm Pl}=(8\pi G_{\rm N})^{-1/2}
. The value of
\gamma
cannot be calculated from string theory at present, because the action (
1
) is in four-space dimensions, so that
\gamma
depends upon a compactification from ten to four dimensions and the unknown vacuum expectation value of string dilaton.

The equations of motion from the superstring gravitational LEEA (
1
) have the structure

G_{\mu\nu}-{{\gamma}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}H_{\mu\nu}=0~,

(3)

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is Einstein’s tensor and
H_{\mu\nu}
stands for the contribution of
J
.

Let us investigate how the GZ term affects the Schwarzschild solution in GR, in the first order with respect to
\gamma
,
by using the standard (spherically-symmetric and static) Ansatz for the metric,

ds^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+{{1}\over{B(r)}}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2}~,

(4)

with two functions
A(r)
and
B(r)
, having the form

A(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\gamma\Delta(r),\hskip 17.07164ptB(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\gamma\Sigma(r)~,

(5)

in terms of two unknown functions
\Delta(r)
and
\Sigma(r)
, outside a spherically-symmetric body of mass
M
.

For this purpose, we have evaluate
H_{\mu\nu}
only on the classical Schwarzschild solution, because
H_{\mu\nu}
is multiplied by

\gamma
in the equations of motion (
3
). Using the
xTras
package of Mathematica
[
12
]
we derived the following solution:

A(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\gamma{{2^{11}\pi^{3}G^{6}_{\rm N}M^{3}(8r-11G_{\rm N}M)}\over{r^{10}}}

(6)

and

B(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\gamma{{2^{11}\pi^{3}G^{6}_{\rm N}M^{3}(36r-67G_{\rm N}M)}\over{r^{10}}}~~.

(7)

It is instructive to compare these results with the EBR gravity arising from the SBR gravity
[
11
]
in the absence of the
R^{2}
-term and having the action with the terms quartic in the curvature also,

S_{\rm EBR}[g]={{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\over{2}}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(R-{{\beta}\over{32M_{\rm Pl}^{6}}}T^{2}\right)~,

(8)

where
\beta
is another dimensionless coupling constant, the
T^{2}
stands for the Bel-Robinson (BR) tensor squared,

T^{2}\equiv T_{\rho\nu\lambda\mu}T^{\rho\nu\lambda\mu}
, with
[
13
,
14
]

T^{\rho\nu\lambda\mu}\equiv R^{\rho\sigma\eta\lambda}R^{\nu}{}_{\sigma\eta}{}^{\mu}+{}^{*}R^{\rho\sigma\eta\lambda}{}^{*}R^{\nu}{}_{\sigma\eta}{}^{\mu}=R^{\rho\sigma\eta\lambda}R^{\nu}{}_{\sigma\eta}{}^{\mu}+R^{\rho\sigma\eta\mu}R^{\nu}{}_{\sigma\eta}{}^{\lambda}-{{{1}\over{2}}}g^{\rho\nu}R^{\sigma\eta\xi\lambda}R_{\sigma\eta\xi}{}^{\mu}~,

(9)

and the star denotes the Hodge dual tensor in four dimensions. The BR tensor was introduced by Bel and Robinson
[
13
,
14
]
by analogy with the standard energy-momentum tensor of Maxwell’s theory of electromagnetism,

T^{\rm Maxwell}_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}+{}^{*}F_{\mu\rho}{}^{*}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}~~,\quad F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}~~.

(10)

The BR tensor squared obeys the identity
[
15
,
16
]

T^{2}=-{\textstyle{1\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{4}$}}}}({}^{*}\!R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}{}^{*}\!R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho})^{2}+{\textstyle{1\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{4}$}}}}({}^{*}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho})^{2}={\textstyle{1\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{4}$}}}}(P_{4}^{2}-E^{2}_{4})={\textstyle{1\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{4}$}}}}(P_{4}+E_{4})(P_{4}-E_{4})~,

(11)

where the Euler and Pontryagin topological densities in four dimensions,
E_{4}
and
P_{4}
, respectively, have been introduced. It is worth recalling yet another well-known identity,

E_{4}={\cal G}_{\rm GB}\equiv R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}-4R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}+R^{2}~,

(12)

where the Gauss-Bonnet invariant
{\cal G}_{\rm GB}
has been introduced, because it gives the connection between the
EBR gravity theory (
8
) and the modified gravity theories with the Lagrangians of the form
{\cal L}(R,{\cal G}_{\rm GB})
. In particular,
this connection is useful to claim
\beta>0

[
17
]
.

We consider the EGZ and EBR gravity theories merely perturbatively, in the first order with respect to the new (string) coupling. Therefore, the issue of ghosts does not arise because there are no new degrees of freedom. The BR term was motivated in Ref.
[
18
]
as another ”string-inspired” quantum correction, possibly originating from a non-trivial scalar potential of dilaton and axion in the limit of ignoring their kinetic terms (applicable for slow-roll inflation). The difference between the EGZ and EBR gravity actions in the context
of superstrings/M-theory was noticed in Ref.
[
19
]
, see also Ref.
[
20
]
for the EGZ gravity in higher dimensions.

When using the same Ansatz for a solution to the EBR theory as in Eq. (
4

ds^{2}=-C(r)dt^{2}+{{1}\over{E(r)}}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2}~,

(13)

with

C(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\beta\Xi(r)~,\hskip 17.07164ptE(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\beta\Phi(r)~,

(14)

we find

C(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\beta{{2^{11}\pi^{3}G^{6}_{\rm N}M^{3}(8r-11G_{\rm N}M)}\over{r^{10}}}

(15)

and

E(r)=1-{{2G_{\rm N}M}\over{r}}-\beta{{2^{11}\pi^{3}G^{6}_{\rm N}M^{3}(36r-67G_{\rm N}M)}\over{r^{10}}}~,

(16)

which is the
same
solution as that in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) for EGZ gravity after the identification of

\gamma
and
\beta
. This solution can also be recovered from an independent calculation in Ref.
[
21
]
in the
case of the vanishing cosmological constant. The black hole thermodynamic quantities in EBR gravity, based on the solution above
were also computed in Refs.
[
21
,
22
]
. Therefore, those results are also valid in the EGZ gravity.

Quantum gravity corrections in the gravitational LEEA imply a deviation from the Einstein gravity relation
A(r)=B(r)
or
C(r)=E(r)
. After rewriting
A(r)=1-2\tilde{A}(r)
and
B(r)=1+2\tilde{B}(r)
, the post-Newtonian function is defined by
[
23
]

\gamma_{\text{pN}}(r)={{\tilde{A}(r)}\over{\tilde{B}(r)}}~.

(17)

For a spherically symmetric body with mass
M_{c}
and radius
r_{c}
, the post-Newtonian parameter
\gamma_{\rm pN}(r_{c})
in the leading order with respect to
the string parameter
\gamma
in the EGZ and EBR gravity is thus given by

\gamma_{\text{pN}}=1+{{7\cdot 2^{12}\pi^{3}G^{5}_{\rm N}M^{2}_{c}(2G_{\rm N}M_{c}-r_{c})\gamma}\over{r^{9}_{c}}}~~.

(18)

The local gravity constraints on the post-Newtonian parameter inside the Solar system were obtained by Cassini satellite mission
with the upper bound
[
24
]

|\gamma_{\text{pN}}-1|<2.3\times 10^{-5}~.

(19)

However, this bound does not lead to a meaningful constraint on the parameter
\gamma
when using Eq. (
18
) in application to the Sun or black holes, either primordial or supermassive ones.

3
EGZ and EBR in FLRW

The difference between the EGZ and EBR gravity becomes apparent in a spatially flat FLRW universe characterized by the metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2})~,

(20)

where
a(t)
is the cosmic scale factor.

In both cases (EBR and EGZ) we derived two equations of motion, corresponding to the
tt
and
x_{i}x_{i}
(no sum) components, and verified their consistency.

In terms of the (observable) Hubble function
H=\dot{a}/a
, the equations of motion in the EBR theory are given by

H^{2}+{{3\beta}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}H^{4}\left(-H^{4}+2H\ddot{H}+6H^{2}\dot{H}+3\dot{H}^{2}\right)=0

(21)

and

\begin{gathered}3H^{2}+2\dot{H}-{{3\beta}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}H^{2}\Big[3H^{6}-12H^{3}\ddot{H}-16H\dot{H}\ddot{H}-12\dot{H}^{3}\\
+2H^{4}\left(-6\ddot{H}+\dot{H}\right)-H^{2}\left(2H^{(3)}+45\dot{H}^{2}\right)\Big]=0.\end{gathered}

(22)

In particular, Eq. (
21
) is consistent with the analogous equation derived in Ref.
[
18
]
.

In the EGZ gravity, the equations of motion are

\begin{gathered}H^{2}+{{\gamma}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}\Big[H^{4}\left(-12H^{4}+44H\ddot{H}+132H^{2}\dot{H}+138\dot{H}^{2}\right)\\
+48H^{3}\dot{H}\ddot{H}+12H\dot{H}^{2}\ddot{H}+28H^{2}\dot{H}^{3}-3\dot{H}^{4}\Big]=0\end{gathered}

(23)

and

\begin{gathered}3H^{2}+2\dot{H}+{{\gamma}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}^{6}}}H^{2}\Big\{4H^{2}\left(-9H^{6}+66H^{3}\ddot{H}+66H^{4}\ddot{H}+12\ddot{H}^{2}+11H^{2}H^{(3)}\right)\\
+4\dot{H}\left(9H^{6}+160H^{3}\ddot{H}+72H^{4}\ddot{H}+6\ddot{H}^{2}+12H^{2}H^{(3)}\right)\\
+6\dot{H}^{2}\left(153H^{4}+4H\ddot{H}(11+3H)+2H^{(3)}\right)+564H^{2}\dot{H}^{3}+47\dot{H}^{4}\Big\}=0~\end{gathered}

(24)

where the
H^{(3)}
is the third time derivative. A simple comparison of the equations of motion in the EBR and EGZ theories shows they are different, except the trivial case when all the time derivatives of the Hubble function are ignored.

The reason why the solutions to the EGZ and EBR gravity theories are the same in the Schwarzschild case can be explained by the observation
that in the case of
R=0
and
R_{\mu\nu}=0
, one also has
P_{4}=0
and

J-{{\mathcal{G}^{2}}\over{32}}={{1}\over{8}}R_{\alpha\beta}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho\sigma}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\gamma\rho}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda\tau}R_{\delta\sigma\lambda\tau}-{{1}\over{16}}R_{\alpha\beta}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho\sigma}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\gamma\delta}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda\tau}R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\tau}=0~,

(25)

whereas both the Ricci scalar and the Ricci tensor do not vanish for the FLRW metric.

The solutions to the EBR gravity theory in the FLRW universe were obtained in Ref.
[
18
]
in the more general case including
the
R^{2}
-term (dubbed the SBR gravity). To the end of this Section, we focus on the EGZ gravity. It admits the constant (de Sitter) solution with

H_{0}={{M_{\text{Pl}}}\over{(12\gamma)^{1/6}}}>0.

(26)

Let us consider small perturbations around
H_{0}
with
H(t)=H_{0}+\delta H(t)
. The linearized version of Eq. (
23
)
is then given by

-{{72M_{\text{Pl}}\gamma}\over{(12\gamma)^{7/6}}}\delta H(t)+11\delta\dot{H}(t)+{{11(12\gamma)^{1/6}}\over{3M_{\text{Pl}}}}\delta\ddot{H}(t)=0~,

(27)

whose solution reads

\delta H(t)=c_{+}e^{\alpha_{+}t}+c_{-}e^{\alpha_{-}t}~,

(28)

where
c_{\pm}
are the integration constants and

\alpha_{\pm}=\pm{{(\mp 33+3\sqrt{209})M_{\text{Pl}}}\over{22(12\gamma)^{1/6}}}~~.

(29)

Since
\alpha_{+}
is always positive, the de Sitter solution (
26
) is unstable.

A more general time-dependent solution can be found in the form of Taylor (or Painleve) expansion,

H(t)=\sum_{k=-p}^{+\infty}c_{k}(t-t_{0})^{k}~,

(30)

where
t_{0}
is an integration constant and
p
is a positive integer. We find in the EGZ gravity that the solution to Eq. (
23
) does not include the negative powers, while the first terms of the expansion are given by

H(t)={{M_{\text{Pl}}}\over{(12\gamma)^{1/6}}}+{{13}\over{21}}\left({{2}\over{3\gamma}}\right)^{1/3}M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}(t-t_{0})-{{3445M_{\text{Pl}}^{3}}\over{1617\sqrt{3\gamma}}}(t-t_{0})^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left((t-t_{0})^{3}\right).

(31)

In terms of the dimensionless quantities

\tilde{H}\equiv{{H_{0}}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda(t)\equiv M_{\text{Pl}}(t-t_{0})~,

(32)

the solution (
31
) can be rewritten to

{{H(t)}\over{M_{\text{Pl}}}}=\tilde{H}\left[1+{{13}\over{21}}\left({{16}\over{3\gamma}}\right)^{1/6}\lambda(t)-{{3445}\over{1617}}\left({{4}\over{3\gamma}}\right)^{1/6}\lambda^{2}(t)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\left(\lambda^{3}(t)\right)~.

(33)

Black hole shadows

In this Section we briefly revisit the earlier studies of black hole shadows and confront them with observations in the context of the
superstring gravity, see e.g. Refs.
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
,
by using our results in Eqs. (

The angular radius of a black hole shadow is given by
[
29
,
30
]

\sin^{2}\alpha_{\rm sh}={{h(r_{\rm ph})^{2}}\over{h(r_{\rm obs})^{2}}}~~,

(34)

where

h(r)={{g_{\theta\theta}}\over{g_{rr}}}={{r^{2}}\over{A(r)}}~~,

(35)

and
r_{\rm ph}
,
r_{\rm obs}
are the photon sphere radius and the observer distance, respectively. For an observer far away from a black hole, the observable radius of the black hole shadow is

R_{\rm sh}=r_{\rm obs}\alpha_{\rm sh}\approx{{r_{\rm ph}}\over{\sqrt{A(r_{\rm ph})}}}~~.

(36)

The photon sphere radius can be obtained from the condition
[
29
,
30
]

{{d}\over{dr}}h^{2}(r)\Big|_{r=r_{\rm ph}}=0~.

(37)

In the first order with respect to
\gamma
or
\beta
, we find

r_{\rm ph}=3G_{N}M+\gamma{{45056\pi^{3}}\over{6561G^{2}_{\rm N}M^{5}}}~~,

(38)

where
M
is the black hole mass. Plugging this result into Eq. (
36

R_{\rm sh}=3\sqrt{3}G_{\rm N}M+\gamma{{13312\pi^{3}}\over{2187\sqrt{3}G^{2}_{\rm N}M^{5}}}~~.

(39)

Astrophysical observations with the Event Horizon Telescope measured the angle diameter of the shadow of the black hole
M87^{\ast}
in the center of the galaxy M87 as
\Omega=42\pm 3

\mu

[
31
]
. The distance between the black hole and the Solar system is estimated as
D=16.8\pm 0.8
Mpc. This gives the diameter of the black hole shadow as
d_{\rm sh}=2R_{\rm sh}=D\Omega
and allows us to derive the following expression for

\gamma={{2187\sqrt{3}G^{2}_{\rm N}M^{5}}\over{26624\pi^{3}\hbar^{3}c}}\left(d_{\rm sh}-6\sqrt{3}G_{\rm N}M/c^{2}\right).

When using the known distance
9.33497\cdot 10^{13}~{\rm m}<d_{\rm sh}<1.1552\cdot 10^{14}

5
The upper bound on
\gamma
from Hawking temperature

A better way to derive the upper bound comes from the Hawking temperature of a black hole. In general, a quantum gravity correction should be
small enough for perturbation theory to make sense. The radius of the event horizon is obtained as a solution to

A(r)=0\quad{\rm or}\quad B(r)=0~~.

(41)

In the first order with respect to
\gamma
, it implies

r_{\rm h}=2G_{\rm N}M+\gamma{{20\pi^{3}}\over{G^{2}_{\rm N}M^{5}}}~~.

(42)

The Hawking temperature is given by

T={{\sqrt{A^{\prime}(r_{\rm h})B^{\prime}(r_{\rm h})}}\over{4\pi}}~~,

(43)

where the primes denote the derivatives. After reinstating the fundamental constants, we find

T={{\hbar c^{3}}\over{8\pi G_{\rm N}Mk_{B}}}\left(1-\gamma{{8\pi^{3}\hbar^{3}c^{3}}\over{G_{\rm N}^{3}M^{6}}}\right)~~,

(44)

that is in agreement with the well-known statement that the Hawking temperature of a black hole is decreased in superstring gravity

[
32
]
. Therefore, we have

\gamma<{{G_{\rm N}^{3}M^{6}}\over{8\pi^{3}\hbar^{3}c^{3}}}

(45)

for
any
black hole of mass
M
. Taking the smallest black hole with the Planck mass
M_{\rm Pl}\approx 2.176\cdot 10^{-8}
kg
in (
45
) yields

\gamma<1.62\cdot 10^{-5}~~.

(46)

This bound is stronger than the bounds found in Refs.
[
26
,
27
]
.

On backward problem for a time-fractional fourth order parabolic equation
Subhankar Mondal

Abstract
. This article is concerned with the inverse problem of retrieving the initial value of a time-fractional fourth order parabolic equation from source and final time observation. The considered problem is an ill-posed problem. We obtain regularized approximations for the sought initial value by employing the quasi-boundary value method, its modified version and by Fourier truncation method(FTM). We provide both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies and derive the error estimates for all these methods under some source conditions involving some Sobolev smoothness. As an important implication of the obtained rates, we observe that for both the apriori and aposteriori cases, the rates obtained by all these three methods are same for some source sets. Moreover, we observe that in both the apriori and aposteriori cases, the FTM is free from the so-called saturation effect , whereas the quasi-boundary value method and its modifications possesses the saturation effect for both the cases. Further, we observe that the rates obtained by the FTM is always order optimal for the considered source sets.



Keywords:
backward problem, time-fractional parabolic equation, regularization, quasi boundary value method, truncation method, convergence rate

Introduction

Fourth order parabolic PDE’s have remained an interesting topic of research for many decades. It is known that fourth order parabolic PDE’s are helpful in modelling various natural phenomenon and has found its application in many engineering problems such as, the free vibrations in beams or shafts
[
6
]
, the growth of epitaxial thin film
[
12
]
, thermal grooving by surface mechanism
[
18
]
, and is also used for noise removal and edge preservation in image processing
[
25
]
, to name a few. Recently research activities on inverse problems related to fourth order parabolic PDE has grown up substantially. Let us mention briefly a few recent works related to some inverse problems associated with fourth order parabolic equations.

For
\tau>0,
and some appropriate functions
h,v_{0},
in
[
2
]
the authors considered the system of equations

\begin{cases}v_{t}(x,t)+a(t)v_{xxxx}(x,t)=h(x,t),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\times(0,\tau),\\
v_{x}(0,t)=v_{x}(1,t)=v_{xxx}(0,t)=v_{xxx}(1,t)=0,&\quad\text{on}\quad(0,\tau),\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\end{cases}

and analysed the inverse problem of identifying the time-dependent coefficient
a(t),
called the
Mullins’ coefficient,
from the knowledge of the data
v(x_{0},t),
where
x_{0}\in[0,1]
is any fixed given point and
v
is the solution of the considered PDE. Subsequently, in
[
1
]
, the author considered a more general fourth order parabolic PDE and investigated the inverse source identification problem associated with it. More precisely, the author considered the inverse problem of identification of the source function
f
from the knowledge of final time observation
v(x,\tau)
or the time-integral observation
\int_{0}^{\tau}\omega(t)v(x,t)\,dt,
for
0<x<1,
where
v
is the solution of

\begin{cases}v_{t}(x,t)+v_{xxxx}(x,t)=f(x)g(x,t)+h(x,t),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\times(0,\tau),\\
v(0,t)=\mu_{1}(t),\,\,v(1,t)=\mu_{2}(t),&\quad\text{on}\quad(0,\tau),\\
v_{xx}(0,t)=\mu_{3}(t),\,\,v_{xx}(1,t)=\mu_{4}(t),&\quad\text{on}\quad(0,\tau),\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\end{cases}

for some given functions
\omega,g,h,v_{0},\mu_{i},i=1,2,3,4.

As a follow up to the above developments, in a recent work
[
11
]
, the authors have extended the investigation from integer-order derivative to fractional order setting. They considered an inverse problem of identifying time-dependent source term
f
from the final value
v(\cdot,\tau),
associated with a time-fractional fourth order parabolic PDE as follows:

\begin{cases}{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}v(x,t)+Bv_{xxxx}(x,t)=f(t)g(x,t),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\times(0,\tau),\\
v(1,t)=v_{x}(0,t)=v_{xx}(1,t)=v_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&\quad\text{on}\quad(0,\tau),\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\end{cases}

where
B>0
is a surface diffusion coefficient,
v_{0}
is some appropriate function. Here (and throughout the paper)
{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}
is the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
(cf.
[
20
]
) given by

(1.1)

{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}v(x,t):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v_{s}(x,s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\,ds,\quad&\text{if}\quad 0<\alpha<1,\\
v_{t}(x,t),\quad&\text{if}\quad\alpha=1,\end{cases}

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the standard Gamma function, that is,
\Gamma(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}s^{t-1}\,ds,\,\,t>0.

Note that forward problem associated with such time-fractional fourth order parabolic equations have been studied by many authors in various context, see for instance
[
7
,
10
]
.

It is important to recall that over the last few decades the research on time-fractional backward heat conduction problems (TFBHCP) have gained substantial attention due to its many practical applications. Since the work in
[
14
]
, the literature on TFBHCP and its various variants have grown remarkably (see e.g.,
[
9
,
13
,
23
]
). Considering the importance of fractional-order derivative, it is desirable to study various inverse problem associated with time-fractional fourth order parabolic equations. Recently some works on source identification problem associated with time-fractional fourth order parabolic equations have been reported (see e.g.
[
11
]
), as mentioned earlier. However, to the best of our knowledge, the backward problem for time-fractional fourth order parabolic equations has not been explored yet and thus, this paper intends to contribute in that direction.

In this paper we are interested in the inverse problem of retrieving the initial value from the knowledge of final value and the source function, associated with a time-fractional fourth order parabolic PDE. More precisely, we consider the following: Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\in\{1,2,\ldots,7\},
(see Remark
3.5
for this restriction on
d
) be a bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\tau>0
be fixed. Let
\alpha\in(0,1).
We consider the system

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u=f,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
u=0=\Delta u,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
u(\cdot,\tau)=h,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\tau];L^{2}(\Omega)),

h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}
is the Caputo fractional derivative as defined in (
1.1
). The inverse problem that we consider is:

(IP)
: retrieve the initial value
u(\cdot,0)
from the knowledge of
h
and
f,
where
u
is a solution of (
1.2
).

Similar to TFBHCP, the inverse problem to recover
u(\cdot,0)
from the knowledge of
h,f,
associated with (
1.2
) is an ill-posed problem in the sense that a small perturbations in the data may lead to a large deviation in the sought initial value (see Example
3.9
). Further, the problem of recovering
u(\cdot,t)
for
0<t<\tau
is stable with respect to the perturbations in the data
h,f,
(see Remark
3.8
), possessing the properties similar to that of TFBHCP.

In practice, we shall have access to some measured data, and hence noise in the data is inevitable. We assume that for
\delta>0,

h^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f^{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
are the noisy data satisfying

(1.3)

\|h-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\theta\|f-f^{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\delta^{2},

where
\theta>0
is a fixed known constant that will be specified later (see Proposition
3.4
).

Thus, the inverse problem at hand is to recover
u(\cdot,0)
from the knowledge of
h^{\delta}
and
f^{\delta}
satisfying (
1.3
). Since the problem is ill-posed, in order to obtain stable approximations, we have to employ some regularization methods (see e.g.,
[
4
,
19
]
). We obtain stable approximations by the quasi-boundary value method (QBVM) (see e.g.,
[
8
,
23
]
), it’s modifications (MQBVM) (see e.g.,
[
9
,
15
,
24
]
) and the Fourier truncation method (FTM) (see e.g.,
[
13
,
16
]
). Just to give some insight, in QBVM we perturb the final value in (
1.2
) by adding the initial value multiplied by the regularization parameter, that is, we consider the resulting perturbed term as
u(\cdot,\tau)+\beta u(\cdot,0),
where
\beta>0
is the regularization parameter. For the MQBVM we consider the resulting perturbed term as
u(\cdot,\tau)+\beta(-\Delta)^{q}u(\cdot,0),
where
\beta>0
is the regularization parameter and
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that in literature, the case
q=1,
has been studied extensively for various problems (see e.g.,
[
24
]
), and is known by the name modified quasi-boundary value method. In FTM, we consider the truncated version of the Fourier expansion of the concerned function and the level at which we truncate plays the role of regularization parameter.

It is known in regularization theory that unless we assume some
source condition
(see e.g.,
[
4
,
19
]
) on the unknown, the convergence can be arbitrarily slow
[
22
]
, that is, we can not obtain any convergence rate. In view of this, we assume that the unknown
g=u(\cdot,0)
belongs to some
source sets
which are subsets of some Sobolev spaces
H_{p},
where
p>0
indicates the smoothness property, and then obtain error estimates and hence deduce the convergence rates of the approximations by the considered regularization methods. We shall provide both the apriori and the aposteriori parameter choice strategies and obtain the corresponding error estimates for all of them. As an important implication, we observe that for the FTM, the convergence rates in both the apriori and aposteriori cases is of the form
O(\delta^{p/(p+2)})
for all
p>0,
where
\delta
is the noise as considered in (
1.3
). However, for the QBVM and MQBVM, for both the apriori and aposteriori cases, the convergence rates possesses saturation effect. For the QBVM the best rates possible for the apriori and aposteriori cases is
O(\delta^{1/2})
and for MQBVM the best rates possible for the apriori and aposteriori cases are
O(\delta^{(q+2)/(q+4)})
and
O(\delta^{q/(q+2)}),
respectively, for
q\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Further, we observe that, if
p
belongs to certain interval then the rates of FTM, QBVM, MQBVM are all the same for apriori case, and for certain values of
p
the aposteriori rates for FTM and MQBVM are also the same. Moreover, we shall see that for the considered source sets the rates obtained by the FTM is always order optimal for all
p>0,
but the same is not the case for QBVM and MQBVM (see Remark
5.9
).

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
we list some notations, important facts, results about the
Mittag-Leffler function
and some basic result on calculus, that will be used throughout. In Section
3
we mainly discuss the relation between the sought initial value and the data, that is, the final value and the source function. We show by an example the ill-posedness of the considered inverse problem of retrieving the initial value. We also establish a conditional stability result. Section
4
is all about the QBVM and MQBVM. We provide both apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies and obtain the corresponding error estimates. Section
5
is about the FTM. We systematically define the regularized solutions and provide both apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies and obtain the corresponding convergence rates. Moreover, we discuss about the optimality of the obtained rate.

Preparatory results

.

u(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
if and only if

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

For
h,\tilde{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega),

Proof.

From (

), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\frac{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}t^{\alpha})}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}(h_{n}-\tilde{h}_{n}-\Psi_{f}^{\alpha,n}(\tau)+\Psi_{\tilde{f}}^{\alpha,n}(\tau))|^{2}+|\Psi_{f}^{\alpha,n}(t)-\Psi_{\tilde{f}}^{\alpha,n}(t)|^{2}

where recall that

is as in Proposition

. Now the proof follows from Propositions

and

(Ill-posed)

and

We now obtain an equivalent formulation of the inverse problem of retrieving the initial value from the final value and the source function.

Let
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
f\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
and

be as in Proposition

. Let

be the solution of the backward problem (

). Then from (

from the knowledge of

and

is same as solving the linear operator equation

Tg=\Upsilon_{h,f},

where
T:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

(3.10)

T\phi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

From Theorem
2.1
it follows that
{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}
and thus it follows that

is a compact linear operator of infinite rank. Therefore, solving (

) is an ill-posed problem. Moreover, if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\right)^{2}<\infty

3.9
)
has a unique solution given by

where

is as defined in (
3.5
) and

is as defined in (
3.4
).

We now recall definitions of some Sobolev spaces that are relevant in our analysis.
For
p\geq 0,
let

H_{p}=\big\{\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega):\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\big\}.

It is known that

is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner-product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n},\psi\rangle,\quad\phi,\psi\in H_{p},

and the corresponding norm is given by

\|\phi\|_{H_{p}}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2},\quad\phi\in H_{p}.

Note that

and
H_{1/2}=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

For some
\varrho>0,
we consider the following set

(3.11)

S_{\varrho,p}:=\big\{\phi\in H_{p}:\,\|\phi\|_{H_{p}}\leq\varrho\big\}.

These sets will be the
source sets
that are of interest in our analysis.

We end this section by proving a conditional stability estimate.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
h\in L^{2}(\Omega),f\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
\Upsilon_{h,f}
be as in Proposition
3.4
. Let
g
be the unique solution of (
3.9
). If

for some
\varrho,p>0,
then there exists a constant
C_{3},
depending on
\alpha,\tau
and
C_{1},
such that

\|g\|\leq C_{3}^{p/(p+2)}\|g\|_{H_{p}}^{2/(p+2)}\|\Upsilon_{h,f}\|^{p/(p+2)}.

Proof.

Since
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
3.9
), we have
g=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\varphi_{n}
, where
g_{n}
are as in (
3.5
), that is,

g_{n}=\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}=\frac{h_{n}-\Psi^{\alpha,n}_{f}(\tau)}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2p/(p+2)}\,\left(\frac{|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}}{|{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})|^{(p+2)}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}}{|{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})|^{(p+2)}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}.

Now, from Theorem
2.1
we have

\frac{1}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\leq C_{3}\lambda_{n}^{2},

where
C_{3}=\frac{(1+\lambda_{1}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})\Gamma(1-\alpha)}{C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{2}}.
Thus,
using the fact that

\frac{|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}}{|{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})|^{(p+2)}}=\frac{g_{n}^{2}}{|{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})|^{p}},

we have

\|g\|^{2}\leq C_{3}^{2p/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}g_{n}^{2}\right)^{2/(p+2)},

that is,

\|g\|^{2}\leq C_{3}^{2p/(p+2)}\|\Upsilon_{h,f}\|^{2p/(p+2)}\|g\|_{H_{p}}^{4/(p+2)}.

Now the proof follows.
∎

4.
Quasi-boundary value method and its modification

We have seen in the preceding section that the considered inverse problem of retrieving the initial value from the knowledge of final value and the source term, is an ill-posed problem in the sense that a small perturbations in the data
h
and
f
may lead to a large deviation in the sought solution. But, in practice, we shall have knowledge of the data that are corrupted by some noise. Thus, we have to employ some regularization method to obtain stable approximations. In this section we shall employ the quasi-boundary value method and its modifications, and obtain error estimates for apriori as well as aposteriori parameter choice strategies.

For
\beta>0
and
q={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\},
we consider the following equation

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial^{\alpha}_{t}v+\Delta^{2}v=f,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\

and

Note that

will play the role of regularization parameter.

By separation of variable method, we know that the solution of (

of (

) is given by

LEMMA 4.7
.

For

Then the following holds:

(i)

\Phi
is continuous and increasing.

(ii)

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\Phi(\beta)=0
and
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\Phi(\beta)=\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|.

Proof.

(i).
The continuity follows easily by observing that

(\Phi(\beta))^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2q}(\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n})^{2}}{({E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\beta\lambda_{n}^{q})^{2}}.

In order to show that
\Phi
is increasing, we first observe that for
b,c>0,
the function
\frac{bt}{bt+c}
is increasing for
t\geq 0.
Since
\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|\neq 0,
there exists some
m\in{\mathbb{N}},
such that
\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,m}\neq 0.
Using these facts, it follows that
\Phi
is indeed increasing.

(ii).
From the expression of
(\Phi(\beta))^{2}
it follows that
\lim_{\beta\to 0}\Phi(\beta)=0.
Next, from Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2q}}{({E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\beta\lambda_{n}^{q})^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\tilde{C}/(\beta\lambda_{1}^{q}(1+\lambda_{1}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}))},

where
\tilde{C}=\frac{C_{2}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}.
This implies that

(\Phi(\beta))^{2}\geq\frac{1}{1+\tilde{C}/(\beta\lambda_{1}^{q}(1+\lambda_{1}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}))}\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|^{2}.

However, we also observe that

(\Phi(\beta))^{2}\leq\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|^{2}.

Now the proof follows by taking the limit as
\beta\to\infty.

∎

LEMMA 4.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\xi\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(\beta_{\delta})=\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q}}\varphi_{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}\varphi_{n}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q}\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q}}\varphi_{n}\|

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q}\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q}}\varphi_{n}\|+\sqrt{2}\delta.

\displaystyle(\xi-\sqrt{2})^{2}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

(i).

\displaystyle C_{4}+\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q-p}{q+2}\big[(\beta_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{q+2})^{(q-p)/(q+2)}\big]^{(q+2)/(q-p)}+\frac{p+2}{q+2}\big[C_{4}^{(p+2)/(q+2)}\big]^{(q+2)/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{\delta}\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})+\tilde{\beta}_{\delta}}\varphi_{n}\|+\sqrt{2}\delta.

5.
Fourier truncation method

In this section we obtain regularized approximations by truncating the Fourier expansion of appropriate
L^{2}
functions. The level at which the series expansion is truncated plays the role of regularization parameter. We shall provide both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies and derive the corresponding convergence rates of the approximation. We shall see that the rates obtained by this method are similar for both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice. Moreover, we shall see that the rates obtained by this method does not possess the saturation effect which is in contrast to the rates obtained by quasi-boundary value method and its modifications, as obtained in the previous section.

Throughout this section we assume the following: Let
\delta>0,h,h^{\delta},f,f^{\delta}
be as in Theorem
4.3
, that is,
h,h^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
f,f^{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
and

(5.1)

\|h-h^{\delta}\|^{2}+\theta\|f-f^{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\delta^{2},

where
\theta=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{4}}.

Let
\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}
,
\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}
be as defined in (
3.4
) and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be the solution of (
3.9
). Recall that solving (
3.9
) is an ill-posed problem with respect to the perturbations in the data
h
and
f
. Thus, we aim to obtain stable approximations for the sought initial value
g
by the Fourier truncation method. Towards this, for
N\in{\mathbb{N}},
we define

(5.2)

\begin{cases}g^{N}&:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\varphi_{n},\\
g^{\delta,N}&:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\varphi_{n}.\end{cases}

Here the natural number

plays the role of regularization parameter. We shall choose

in terms of
\delta
so that under certain source condition we can derive the error estimates in terms of the noise
\delta.

First, we obtain an estimate for
\|g-g^{N}\|.
For

recall the source set

as defined in (

).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\|g-g^{N}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho^{2}}{\lambda_{N+1}^{2p}},

∎

We now obtain bound for

Now from the definition of

and

\displaystyle\|g^{N}-g^{\delta,N}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\tilde{C}_{18}}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}^{4}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\tilde{C}_{18}}^{2}\lambda_{N}^{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\tilde{C_{18}}^{2}\lambda_{N}^{4}\delta^{2},

where
C_{18}=\sqrt{2}\tilde{C}_{18}.
Therefore,

\|g^{N}-g^{\delta,N}\|\leq C_{18}\delta\lambda_{N}^{2}.

∎

Remark 5.3
.

Observe that Theorem
5.2
shows that
g^{N}
’s are stable with respect to the perturbations in the data
h
and
f.

\lozenge

We are now in a position to state and prove one of the main result of this section, that is, the apriori parameter choice strategy and the corresponding error estimates. From now onwards we shall use the notation
[[r]]
to denote the greatest integer not exceeding
r,
where
r\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
be as in (
2.2
). For
\delta>0
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}},
let
h,h^{\delta},f,f^{\delta},g^{\delta,N}
be as in Theorem
5.2
. For
\varrho,p>0,
let
g
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Let
C_{18}
be as in Theorem
5.2
. Then for the choice

N_{\delta}=\left[\left[\left(\frac{1}{C_{18}e_{1}^{p}e_{2}^{2}}\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{d/(2p+4)}\right]\right],

there exists a constant
C_{19}>0,
depending on
\alpha,\tau,\lambda_{1},C_{1},e_{1},e_{2},
such that

\|g-g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}\|\leq C_{19}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}.

Proof.

Combining the estimates obtained in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|g-g^{\delta,N}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho}{\lambda_{N+1}^{p}}+C_{18}\delta\lambda_{N}^{2}\leq\frac{\varrho}{\lambda_{N}^{p}}+C_{18}\delta\lambda_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{\eqref{eig-value-bound}}

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho}{e_{1}^{p}N^{2p/d}}+C_{18}\delta e_{2}^{2}N^{4/\delta}.

For
r>0,
let

S(r)=\frac{\varrho}{e_{1}^{p}r^{2p/d}}+C_{18}e_{2}^{2}\delta r^{4/d}.

Then it is easy to verify that
S(r)
attains its minimum at
r_{\delta}
satisfying

\frac{\varrho}{e_{1}^{p}r_{\delta}^{2p/d}}=C_{18}e_{2}^{2}\delta r_{\delta}^{4/d}.

That is, we have,

r_{\delta}=\left(\frac{1}{C_{18}e_{1}^{p}e_{2}^{2}}\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{d/(2p+4)}.

We choose the regularization parameter
N_{\delta}\in{\mathbb{N}}
as
N_{\delta}=\left[\left[r_{\delta}\right]\right],
that is,

N_{\delta}=\left[\left[\left(\frac{1}{C_{18}e_{1}^{p}e_{2}^{2}}\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{d/(2p+4)}\right]\right].

Then with this choice of
N_{\delta},
we have,

\|g-g^{\delta,N}\|\leq C_{18}e_{2}^{2}\,\delta\left(\frac{1}{C_{18}e_{1}^{p}e_{2}^{2}}\,\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{2/(p+2)}=C_{19}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)},

where
C_{19}=\left(\frac{C_{18}e_{2}^{2}}{e_{1}^{2}}\right)^{p/(p+2)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In Theorem
5.4
we have provided a parameter choice strategy and obtained the corresponding convergence rate. However, we observe that the choice of the regularization parameter
N_{\delta}
depends on
p,
which is related to the smoothness of the unknown
g.
Therefore, the parameter choice strategy will work only if we know apriori the smoothness of
g.
But, in practice, it is almost always impossible to know the smoothness apriori. Thus, to overcome this situation, we provide the aposteriori parameter choice that only requires knowledge of the noise
\delta
and the observed data
h^{\delta},f^{\delta}.

5.1.
Error estimates with aposteriori parameter choice

Recall that from Proposition
3.4
, it follows that for
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega)),
we have that
\Upsilon_{h,f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}\,\varphi_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega),
where
\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}
is as defined in (
3.4
). For
N\in{\mathbb{N}},
we let

\Upsilon^{N}_{h,f}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}\,\varphi_{n},\quad\text{and}\quad\Upsilon^{N}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}\,\varphi_{n}.

We define

\zeta(N)=\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|,\quad N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Then it is easily seen that
\zeta(N)\to 0
as
N\to\infty.

Let
\mu>\sqrt{2}.
Then, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small, there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(5.3)

\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|\leq\mu\delta<\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|.

Let
N_{\delta}
be the first natural number that satisfies (
5.3
). This
N_{\delta}
will be the regularization parameter that we are looking for. Note that, clearly the choice of such
N_{\delta}
depends only on the known quantities,
\delta,h^{\delta},f^{\delta}.

We now obtain a bound for
N_{\delta}
in terms of
\delta.

LEMMA 5.5
.

Let
e_{1}
be as in (
2.2
) and
h,f
be the exact data. For
\delta>0,
let the noisy data
h^{\delta}
and
f^{\delta}
be such that it satisfies (
5.1
). Let
\mu>\sqrt{2}
be fixed,
N_{\delta}
be the smallest natural number satisfying (
5.3
) and
C_{2}
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Let
g
be the solution of (
3.9
). If
g\in S_{\varrho,p}
for some
p,\varrho>0,
then there exists a constant
C_{20}>0,
depending on
\alpha,\tau,C_{2},
such that

N_{\delta}\leq\left(\frac{C_{20}}{(\mu-\sqrt{2})e_{1}^{(p+2)}}\,\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{d/(2p+4)}.

Proof.

First we note that

\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}=\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}\,\varphi_{n}=\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})g_{n}\varphi_{n}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}|{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})|^{2}g_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{Theorem\ref{prop_Ea_upper_bound_lower_bound}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{(\Gamma(1-\alpha))^{2}\tau^{2\alpha}\lambda_{n}^{4}}g_{n}^{2}=C_{20}^{2}\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{20}^{2}\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2p}g_{n}^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2p+4}}\leq C_{20}^{2}\frac{1}{\lambda_{N_{\delta}}^{2p+4}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}g_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{20}^{2}\frac{\varrho^{2}}{\lambda_{N_{\delta}}^{2p+4}},

where
C_{20}=\frac{C_{2}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\tau^{\alpha}}.
Thus,

\|\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}\|\leq C_{20}\frac{\varrho}{\lambda_{N_{\delta}}^{p+2}}\leq\frac{C_{20}}{e_{1}^{p+2}}\frac{\varrho}{N_{\delta}^{(2p+4)/d}}.

Now, observe that

\|(\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})-(\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})\|^{2}=\sum_{n=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})-(\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})+(\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|-\|(\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})-(\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}-1}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\mu-\sqrt{2})\delta.

Thus,

(\mu-\sqrt{2})\delta\leq\frac{C_{20}}{e_{1}^{p+2}}\frac{\varrho}{N_{\delta}^{(2p+4)/d}},

and hence

N_{\delta}\leq\left(\frac{C_{20}}{(\mu-\sqrt{2})e_{1}^{(p+2)}}\,\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{d/(2p+4)}.

∎

We now obtain estimate for
\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
g
be the solution of (
3.9
) and
g\in S_{\varrho,p}
for some
\varrho,p>0.
Let
\mu,\delta,N_{\delta}
be as in Lemma
5.5
and
g^{N_{\delta}}
be as defined in (
5.2
). Let
C_{1}
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Then there exists a constant
C_{21}>0,
depending on
\alpha,\tau,\lambda_{1},C_{1},p,\mu,
such that

\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|\leq C_{21}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}.

Proof.

Since
g
is a solution of (
3.9
), recall that
g=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\varphi_{n},
where
g_{n}
is as given in (
3.5
), that is,
g_{n}=\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}.

Thus, from the definition of
g^{N_{\delta}},
we have

\displaystyle\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}}{{E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{4/(p+2)}}{({E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}))^{2}}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2p/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}}{({E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}))^{p+2}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}^{2}}{({E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}))^{p}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left({\tilde{C}_{18}}^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}g_{n}^{2}\right)^{2/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\tilde{C}_{18}}^{2p/(p+2)}\varrho^{4/(p+2)}\left(\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}\right)^{p/(p+2)},

where
\tilde{C}_{18}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha)(1+\tau^{\alpha}\lambda_{1}^{2})}{C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{2}}.

Now, recall that

\|\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}\|\leq\mu\delta

and

\|(\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})-(\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})\|^{2}=\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}|\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}^{\alpha,n}|^{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}(\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h,f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})+(\Upsilon_{h,f}-\Upsilon_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})-(\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h,f}-\Upsilon^{N_{\delta}}_{h^{\delta},f^{\delta}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mu+\sqrt{2})^{2}\delta^{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|^{2}\leq{\tilde{C}_{18}}^{2p/(p+2)}\varrho^{4/(p+2)}(\mu+\sqrt{2})^{2p/(p+2)}\delta^{2p/(p+2)},

that is,

\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|\leq C_{21}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)},

where
C_{21}={\tilde{C}_{18}}^{p/(p+2)}(\mu+\sqrt{2})^{p/(p+2)}.

∎

We are now in a position to state and prove the main result of this section, in which we obtain the rates of convergence corresponding to the aposteriori choice of the parameter
N_{\delta}
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mu>\sqrt{2},\delta>0,
and
N_{\delta}
be as in Lemma
5.5
. Let
g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}
be as in (
5.2
) and
e_{1},e_{2}
be as in (
2.2
). Let
g
be the solution of (
3.9
). If
g\in S_{\varrho,p},
for some
\varrho,p>0,
then there exists a constant
C_{23}>0,
depending on
\mu,\alpha,\tau,\lambda_{1},e_{1},e_{2},p,
such that

\|g-g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}\|\leq C_{23}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}.

Proof.

From the estimate obtained in Theorem
5.2
, we have

\|g^{N_{\delta}}-g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}\|\leq C_{18}\delta\lambda_{N_{\delta}}^{2}\leq C_{22}\delta N_{\delta}^{4/d},

where
C_{22}=e_{2}^{2}C_{18}
and
e_{2}
is as in (
2.2
).

Thus, from Theorem
5.6
, we have

\displaystyle\|g-g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g-g^{N_{\delta}}\|+\|g^{N_{\delta}}-g^{\delta,N_{\delta}}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{21}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}+C_{22}\,\delta N_{\delta}^{4/d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{21}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}+C_{22}\,\delta\left(\frac{C_{20}}{(\mu-\sqrt{2})e_{1}^{p+2}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\delta}\right)^{2/(p+2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{21}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}+C_{22}\left(\frac{C_{20}}{(\mu-\sqrt{2})e_{1}^{p+2}}\right)^{2/(p+2)}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{23}\varrho^{2/(p+2)}\delta^{p/(p+2)},

where
C_{23}=\max\big\{C_{21},C_{22}\big(\frac{C_{20}}{(\mu-\sqrt{2})e_{1}^{p+2}}\big)^{2/(p+2)}\big\}.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

Clearly the rates obtained in Theorems
5.4
and
5.7
, for the apriori and aposteriori case, respectively, are of the same order, that is,
O(\delta^{p/(p+2)})
for all
p>0.
Since
\delta^{p/(p+2)}\to\delta
as
p\to\infty,
it follows that the rates for both apriori and aposteriori, obtained by the Fourier truncation method does not possesses the saturation effect.

\lozenge

Remark 5.9
.

(Optimality)

In Section
2
, we have already observed that the inverse problem of retrieving the initial value
g=u(\cdot,0)
from the knowledge of the final value
h=u(\cdot,\tau)
and the source function
f
is same as solving the linear operator equation

Tg=\Upsilon_{h,f},

where
\Upsilon_{h,f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon^{\alpha,n}_{h,f}\varphi_{n},

\Upsilon_{h,f}^{\alpha,n}
is as defined in (
3.4
), and the compact linear self-adjoint operator
T:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

T\phi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{n}\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
\kappa_{n}={E_{\alpha,1}}(-\lambda_{n}^{2}\tau^{\alpha}).

From Theorem
2.1
, we have
\kappa_{n}>0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}},
and

\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\leq\kappa_{n}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}},\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
\tilde{C}_{1}=\frac{C_{1}\lambda_{1}^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{1}^{2}\tau^{\alpha})\Gamma(1-\alpha)}
and
\tilde{C}_{2}=\frac{C_{2}}{\tau^{\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}.
This shows that,
T
is also a self-adjoint positive operator. Now following the arguments in
[
17
, Section 5]
, we can arrive at the conclusion that for the source set
S_{\varrho,p}
and the operator
T
as considered above, the convergence rate
O(\delta^{p/(p+2)})
is order optimal.

Thus, from Theorems
5.4
and
5.7
, it follows that for the source set
S_{\varrho,p},
the convergence rates obtained by FTM are order optimal for both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies for all
p>0
. Also, from Theorems
4.6
and
4.11
, we observe that for MQBVM: if
0<p\leq q+2
then the rate obtained for the apriori case is order optimal, whereas if
0<p\leq q
then the rate obtained for the apsoteriori case is order optimal.

\lozenge

6.
Conclusion

We have initiated a study on the backward problem of retrieving initial value from final value for a time-fractional fourth order parabolic PDE. Since the considered problem is ill-posed, we have obtained stable approximations by employing quasi-boundary value method, its modifications and the Fourier truncation method. Under some Sobolev smoothness assumption on the sought initial value, we have obtained convergence rates for all these methods under both apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies. We observed that for certain cases all the considered methods produces same convergence rates in both the apriori and aposteriori cases. However, we observed that the rates obtained by QBVM and MQBVM possesses the saturation effect but the rates obtained by FTM is free of such effect. Moreover, we observed that the rates obtained by FTM is order optimal for all values of the smoothness index
p
.

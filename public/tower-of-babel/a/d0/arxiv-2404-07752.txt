Singular systems of linear forms over global function fields
Gukyeong Bang, Taehyeong Kim, Seonhee Lim

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider singular systems of linear forms over global function fields of class number one and give an upper bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the set of singular systems of linear forms by constructing an appropriate Margulis height function on the space of lattices over global function fields.

1.
Introduction

An
m\times n
matrix
\mathbf{A}\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
is called
singular
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
T_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
T\geq T_{0}
, there exist
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{p}\|\leq\epsilon T^{-n/m}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\|\mathbf{q}\|\leq T,

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the sup norm. In 1926, Khintchine
[
Khi26
]
introduced the notion of singularity and showed that the set of such matrices has Lebesgue measure zero. On the other hand, the computation of the Hausdorff dimension of the set of singular matrices has been a challenge until the breakthrough by Das, Fishman, Simmons, and Urbański
[
DFSU24
]
. Before this breakthrough, it was proved in
[
Che11
]
that the Hausdorff dimension of the set of singular pairs (i.e.,
m=2
and
n=1
) is
4/3
. This result was extended to the
m
-dimensional singular vectors (i.e.,
n=1
) in
[
CC16
]
, where the authors proved that the singular set has the Hausdorff dimension
m^{2}/(m+1)
. For general singular matrices, it was shown in
[
KKLM17
]
that the Hausdorff dimension of
m\times n
singular matrices is at most
mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}
, and finally, it was shown in
[
DFSU24
]
that the upper bound is sharp.

In contrast to the intensive research mentioned above, there have been fewer results on singular sets over function fields (see, for example,
[
DX24
]
). In this article, we pursue the method in
[
KKLM17
]
in the function field setting and prove that the Hausdorff dimension of
m\times n
singular matrices over function fields is at most
mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}
.

Let us first introduce the function field setting we will consider. Let
\mathbf{K}
be any global function field over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements for a prime power
q
, that is, the function field of a geometrically connected smooth projective curve
\mathbf{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We fix a discrete valuation
\nu
on
\mathbf{K}
and denote by
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
the completion of
\mathbf{K}
with respect to
\nu
and its valuation ring, respectively. Fix a uniformizer
\pi_{\nu}\in\mathbf{K}
such that
\nu(\pi_{\nu})=1
and denote
\mathbf{k}_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}/\pi_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
and
q_{\nu}=\Card\mathbf{k}_{\nu}
. The absolute value
|\cdot|
associated with
\nu
is defined by
|x|=q_{\nu}^{-\nu(x)}
for any
x\in\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
. For any positive integer
s
, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
the sup norm on
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
and denote by
\dim_{H}
the Hausdorff dimension of subsets of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
with respect to the sup norm.

Let
R_{\nu}
be the affine algebra of the affine curve
\mathbf{C}\smallsetminus\{\nu\}
. Then
R_{\nu}
is discrete in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
and plays a similar role to the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{R}
. We remark that
R_{\nu}
is a Dedekind domain in general, but we will assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain, that is, the ideal class group of
R_{\nu}
is trivial (see
[
Ser77
, II.2]
and
[
Nar04
, Chapter 1]
). The assumption is only needed in order to use the submodularity of the covolume function established in
[
Bang25
]
.

We say that a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in M_{m,n}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
is
singular
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
T_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
T\geq T_{0}
, there exist

\mathbf{p}\in R_{\nu}^{m}
and
\mathbf{q}\in R_{\nu}^{n}
satisfying

{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{p}\|\leq\epsilon T^{-n/m}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\|\mathbf{q}\|\leq T.}

Denote by
\mathrm{Sing}(m,n)
the set of all singular matrices in
M_{m,n}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
.
The main result of this article is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,

\dim_{H}\mathrm{Sing}(m,n)\leq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}.

In fact, we will prove a stronger statement for the dimension of the set of lattices with trajectories “escaping on average”. See Proposition
6.5
and the definition just before the proposition.

In order to estimate the upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set, we mainly use the connection (Subsection
2.2
) between homogeneous dynamics and Diophantine approximation, so-called
Dani’s correspondence
, and the construction (Corollary
5.2
) of an appropriate height function satisfying a certain contraction property, so-called a
Margulis function
which originated in
[
EMM98
]
.

We basically follow the approach of
[
KKLM17
]
to construct an appropriate Margulis function. The first step is to obtain a certain contraction property (Proposition
3.1
) at the linear algebra level, and then move to a contraction property (Corollary
5.1
) in homogeneous space using submodularity.

Gaussian random variables were used in
[
KKLM17
]
to make calculations simpler at the linear algebra level. In contrast, we will use a restriction of the Haar measure instead. We will prove that some analogous properties of the Gaussian measure still hold for the Haar measure. This is the main step (Theorem
3.2
) to obtain the linear algebra level contraction property.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce terms and definitions related to global function fields, homogeneous dynamics to interpret the singularity in terms of dynamics, and exterior algebra to develop the linear algebra level contraction property.
Throughout Sections
3
and
4
, we will prove the contraction property (Proposition
3.1
) at the linear algebra level. In particular, Section
3
is devoted to key linear algebra estimates (Theorem
3.2
), and in Section
4
, we prove the linear algebra level contraction property (Proposition
3.1
) by establishing the Hodge duality over function fields.
Section
5
is devoted to the construction of Margulis functions under the assumption that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain.
Finally, in Section
6
, we estimate the upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set using the Margulis function.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Global function fields

As in the introduction, we consider a global function field
\mathbf{K}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements for a prime power
q
.
We denote by
g
the genus of
\mathbf{C}
.
Recall that we consider a discrete valuation
\nu
on
\mathbf{K}
, the completion
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
of
\mathbf{K}
for
\nu
, its valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
, a uniformizer
\pi_{\nu}\in\mathbf{K}
, the residual field
\mathbf{k}_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}/\pi_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
, its cardinality
q_{\nu}=\Card\mathbf{k}_{\nu}
, the absolute value
|\cdot|
associated with
\nu
, and the affine algebra
R_{\nu}
of the affine curve
\mathbf{C}\smallsetminus\{\nu\}
.

Then the completion
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
of
\mathbf{K}
for
\nu
is the field
\mathbf{k}_{\nu}((\pi_{\nu}))
of Laurent series
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}(\pi_{\nu})^{i}
in the variable
\pi_{\nu}
over
\mathbf{k}_{\nu}
, where
x_{i}\in\mathbf{k}_{\nu}
is zero for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
small enough.
It follows that

|x|=q_{\nu}^{-\sup\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ \forall i<j,\ x_{i}=0\}},

and
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}=\mathbf{k}_{\nu}[[\pi_{\nu}]]
is the local ring of power series
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}x_{i}(\pi_{\nu})^{i}
in the variable
\pi_{\nu}
over
\mathbf{k}_{\nu}
.

For any positive integer
s
, we denote by
\mu_{s}
the Haar measure of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
normalized so that
\mu_{s}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{s})=1
and by
\overline{\mu_{s}}=\mu_{s}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{s}}
the restriction of
\mu_{s}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{s}
.
A
lattice

\Lambda
in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
is a discrete free
R_{\nu}
-submodule in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
that generates
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
as a
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
-vector space.
The
covolume
of
\Lambda
, denoted by
\mathrm{cov}(\Lambda)
, is the Haar measure
\mu_{s}
of a fundamental domain of
\Lambda
in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
.
Note that
R_{\nu}^{s}
is a lattice in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
and by
[
BPP19
, Lemma 14.4]
we have

(2.1)

{\mathrm{cov}(R_{\nu}^{s})=q^{(g-1)s}.}

We say that a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{s}
is
unimodular
if
\mathrm{cov}(\Lambda)=\mathrm{cov}(R_{\nu}^{s})
.

2.2.
Homogeneous dynamics

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Let

G=\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu}),\quad\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(R_{\nu}),\text{ and}\quad X=G/\Gamma.

The homogeneous space
X
can be identified with the
G
-orbit of
R_{\nu}^{m+n}
in the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
. Note that the homogeneous space
X
may not be the full space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
as one can multiply the standard lattice
R_{\nu}^{m+n}
by a constant which is not in
R_{\nu}
but has absolute value 1.
In order to relate Diophantine approximation and homogeneous dynamics, we consider the following diagonal flow:

g_{t}=\left(\begin{matrix}\pi_{\nu}^{-nt}\mathbf{I}_{m}&\\
&\pi_{\nu}^{mt}\mathbf{I}_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{for }t\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then
g_{t}
acts on
X
by left multiplication and the unstable horospherical subgroup for
g_{1}
in
G
is given by

U=\left\{u_{\mathbf{s}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{I}_{m}&\mathbf{s}\\
&\mathbf{I}_{n}\end{matrix}\right):\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})\right\}.

Lemma 2.1

(Dani’s correspondence)
.

A matrix
\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
is singular if and only if the trajectory
\{g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}\Gamma:t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}
is divergent in
X
, that is, leaves every compact subset of
X
.

Proof.

The same proof from
[
Dan85
, §2]
works verbatim in our setting with Mahler compactness criterion over global function fields as in
[
KST17
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

2.3.
Exterior algebra

Given
i,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
i\leq d
, we denote
\llbracket i,d\rrbracket=\{i,i+1,\dots,d\}
.
For any finite subset
S
of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
satisfying
|S|\geq i
, denote the set of subsets of
i
elements in
S
by

{\wp_{i}(S)=\{S^{\prime}\subseteq S:|S^{\prime}|=i\}}

and denote
\wp_{i}^{d}=\wp_{i}(\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket)
.

Denote
\mathbf{v}_{I}=\mathbf{v}_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{j_{i}}
for
I=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{i}\}\in\wp^{d}_{i}
with
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{j_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{j_{i}}\in\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. An element
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}
is called
decomposable
if
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}
for some
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. Arbitrary elements of
\bigwedge^{i}{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}
are linear combinations of decomposable elements.
Let
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. Then
\{\mathbf{e}_{I}\}_{I\in\wp^{d}_{i}}
is the standard basis of
\bigwedge^{i}{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}
. Define the norm on
\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
by

{\|\mathbf{v}\|=\max_{I\in\wp^{d}_{i}}|v_{I}|.}

for
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{I\in\wp^{d}_{i}}v_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
.

Note that any element
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
acts linearly on
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. The action naturally extends to

\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
by

g.(\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{i})=(g\mathbf{v}_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(g\mathbf{v}_{i}).

We denote the orthogonal group for a non-Archimedean field by

(2.2)

{\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})=\{A\in M_{d,d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):|\det{A}|=1\},}

and the special orthogonal group by
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})=\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})\cap\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
.
Note that the non-Archimedean norm on
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
is invariant under
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
(see
[
Wei95
]
), and thus,
the restriction measure
\overline{\mu_{d}}
is
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
-invariant.

3.
Key linear algebra estimates

Throughout Sections
3
and
4
, we will prove the following proposition, which is a global function field analog of
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.5]
.
We remark that all statements in Sections
3
and
4
actually hold for all non-Archimedean local fields, including
p
-adic fields and completions of global function fields, without any condition on the class number.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
i\in\llbracket 1,m+n-1\rrbracket
, let

\beta_{i}:=\begin{cases}\frac{m}{i}&\quad\text{if }i\leq m,\\
\frac{n}{m+n-i}&\quad\text{if }i>m.\end{cases}

Then there exists
c>0
depending only on
m,n
such that

{\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\|g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\leq ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}}

for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and any decomposable
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
.

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
3.7
, which is the first step of Proposition
3.1
.
Note that Proposition
3.7
is an analog of
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 3.1]
, but restricted to wedge vectors of dimension at most
m
, due to the fact that the Hodge dual operator is no longer equivariant under the orthogonal group
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
.
For example, for
A=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
,
*(A\mathbf{e}_{1})\neq A(*\mathbf{e}_{1})
(see Lemma
4.2
for detail).

Another remark is that while the Gaussian measure is used in
[
KKLM17
]
to control an integral over
\mathrm{O}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, we will use the restricted Haar measure
\overline{\mu_{d}}
as an analog.
In
[
Eva01
, Theorem 4.2]
, it is shown that every
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
-invariant probability measure on
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
whose coordinate projections are independent is a normalized restricted measure of the Haar measure on a
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
-module
\pi_{\nu}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Considering Maxwell’s characterization of the Gaussian distribution in the real case,
\overline{\mu_{d}}
is the right analog of (real) Gaussian measure.

Throughout this section, let
i,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with
i\leq d
.
Given a matrix
\mathbf{s}
, we denote the
j
-th column and
j
-th row vectors by
\mathbf{s}_{j}
and
\mathbf{s}^{j}
, respectively.
For
\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
, let
c_{I}(\mathbf{s})
be the coefficient corresponding to
\mathbf{e}_{I}
of
\mathbf{s}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{s}_{i}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
, i.e.,

{\mathbf{s}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{s}_{i}=\sum_{I\in\wp^{d}_{i}}c_{I}(\mathbf{s})\mathbf{e}_{I}.}

In order to prove Proposition
3.7
, we need the following measure estimates:

Theorem 3.2
.

Given
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}
, let
E_{\ell}(d,i)
be the set of
i
-tuples of vectors of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d}
whose wedge product has norm bounded by
q^{-\ell}
, and
F_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
be its subset with the absolute value of the “first” coordinate
c_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}(\mathbf{s})
bounded by
q^{-\ell^{\prime}}
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{\ell}(d,i)=\{\mathbf{s}=(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{i})\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{s}_{i}\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\ell}\};

\displaystyle F_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(d,i)=E_{\ell}(d,i)\cap\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):|c_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}(\mathbf{s})|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\ell^{\prime}}\}.

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, there exists a constant
C_{1}(d,i)>0
depending on
d
and
i
such that

{\mu_{di}(F_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(d,i))\leq C_{1}\binom{\ell^{\prime}-\ell+\binom{d}{i}}{\ell^{\prime}-\ell+1}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i)-\ell^{\prime}}.}

In particular,
\mu_{di}(E_{\ell}(d,i))\leq C_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i+1)}
for a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(d,i)>0
. Consequently, taking
C_{1}(d):=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}C_{1}(d,i)
and
C_{2}(d):=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}C_{2}(d,i)
yields constants depending only on
d
.

Given a
\binom{d}{i}
-tuple
(n_{I})_{I\in\wp_{i}^{d}}
of non-negative integers, consider even more refined subsets

{D_{I}^{d}(\ell)=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):|c_{I}(\mathbf{s})|=q_{\nu}^{-\ell}\}\text{ and }D^{d}_{i}((n_{I})_{I})=\bigcap_{I\in\wp_{i}^{d}}D_{I}^{d}(n_{I}).}

In other words, the set
D^{d}_{i}((n_{I})_{I})
is the subset of
\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{\mathcal{O}_{\nu}}^{d}
consisting of elements whose coefficient
c_{I}
has absolute value exactly
q^{-{n_{I}}}
, for all
I
.

Since we use the sup norm for
\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
, the set
F_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(d,i)
is partitioned into

{F_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(d,i)=\coprod_{(n_{I})_{I\in\wp_{i}^{d}}\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{\wp_{i}^{d}}\,:\,n_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}\geq\ell^{\prime};\,\forall I\in\wp_{i}^{d},\,n_{I}\geq\ell}D^{d}_{i}((n_{I})_{I}).}

We will simultaneously prove the following estimate of
\mu_{di}(D^{d}_{i}((n_{I})_{I}))
.
We define
\exp_{q_{\nu}}(x)=q_{\nu}^{x}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
d,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with

, there exists a constant

such that for any
\binom{d}{i}
-tuple
(n_{I})_{I\in\wp_{i}^{d}}

{\mu_{di}(D^{d}_{i}((n_{I})_{I}))\ \leq\ C\exp_{q_{\nu}}(-\max_{I\in\wp_{i}^{d}}{n}_{I}-(d-i){n}_{I^{\prime}}).}

Remark 3.4

The proofs of Lemma

with

: If Lemma

Step 2
: If Lemma

holds for

.

Step 3

holds for
(d+1,d+1)

(2,1)

Proof of Step 0.
Denote
n_{j}:=n_{\{j\}}.
Since

{D^{d}_{1}((n_{I})_{I})=\{\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d}:|s_{j}|=q_{\nu}^{-n_{j}},\;\;j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\ \},}

the Haar measure of this set is

{\begin{split}\mu_{d}(D^{d}_{1}((n_{I})_{I}))&=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{1}(\{s_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}:|s_{j}|=q_{\nu}^{-n_{j}}\})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(\frac{1}{q_{\nu}^{n_{j}}}-\frac{1}{q_{\nu}^{n_{j}+1}})\\
&\leq\exp_{q_{\nu}}(-\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j})\leq\exp_{q_{\nu}}(-\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq d}n_{j}-(d-1)\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq d}n_{j}),\end{split}}

which implies Lemma
3.3
for
(d,1)
for any

.

Proof of Step 1.
Suppose that Lemma
3.3
holds for
(d,i)
with

{\begin{split}&\mu_{d+1}(\{(v_{j})_{1\leq j\leq d+1}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d+1}:|\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}a_{j}v_{j}|\leq q_{\nu}^{-N}\})\\
&\leq\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d}}\frac{q_{\nu}^{-N}}{|a_{j_{0}}|}d\mu_{d}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=\frac{q_{\nu}^{-N}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|}.\end{split}}

Hence, by the cofactor expansion of
\det M_{\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{v})
along
(d+1)
-th row, we have

(3.12)

{\begin{split}\mu_{d+1}(X_{\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\mathbf{s}))&=\mu_{d+1}(\{(v_{j})\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{d+1}:|\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\tau_{(d+1),\;j}(\mathbf{s})v_{j}|=q_{\nu}^{-N}\})\\
&\leq\frac{q_{\nu}^{-N}}{\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq d+1}(|\tau_{(d+1),\;j}(\mathbf{s})|)}=q_{\nu}^{-N+\ell}.\end{split}}

Therefore, it follows from (
3.10
), (
3.11
), and (
3.12
) that

(3.13)

{\begin{split}\mu_{(d+1)^{2}}(R_{\ell})&\leq\int_{\phi(R_{\ell})}\mu_{d+1}(X_{\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}(\mathbf{s}_{1}))d\mu_{d(d+1)}(\mathbf{s}_{1})\\
&\leq q_{\nu}^{-N+\ell}\mu_{d(d+1)}(\phi(R_{\ell})).\end{split}}

Observe that
\phi(R_{\ell})\subset\{{}^{t}\!A:A\in E_{\ell}(d+1,d)\}
, where
{}^{t}\!A
denotes the transpose of
A
. By the induction hypothesis on
(d+1,d)
of
Step 3
and by
Step 2
for
(d+1,d)
, we have

(3.14)

{\begin{split}\mu_{d(d+1)}(\phi(R_{\ell}))&\leq\mu_{(d+1)d}(E_{\ell}(d+1,d))\\
&\leq C_{2}(d+1,d)q_{\nu}^{-\ell((d+1)-d+1)}=C_{2}(d+1,d)q_{\nu}^{-2\ell}.\end{split}}

Therefore, for any
N\geq 0
, it follows from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{(d+1)^{2}}(D)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mu_{(d+1)^{2}}(R_{\ell})\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-N+\ell}\mu_{d(d+1)}(\phi(R_{\ell}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-N+\ell}C_{2}(d+1,d)q_{\nu}^{-2\ell}=C_{2}(d+1,d)(1-q_{\nu}^{-1})^{-1}q_{\nu}^{-N},

which completes
Step 3
with
C(d+1,d+1)=C_{2}(d+1,d)(1-q_{\nu}^{-1})^{-1}
.
∎

]
. Recall from Section

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
and
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{i}
be independent, identically distributed
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
-invariant random variables on
{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}
with law
\overline{\mu_{d}}
. Then

{\mathbb{E}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}\|^{-\beta})<\infty}

for all
\beta<d-i+1
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{s}_{j}
is the
j
-th column vector of
\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
for every
j\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket
. We will compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}\|^{-\beta})

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{d,i}({\mathbf{K}_{\nu}})}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-\beta}\ d\overline{\mu_{di}}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-\beta}\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s}).

Since the norm takes values in
q_{\nu}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{0\}
, consider the partition
\{E_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
of
M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
, where

(3.15)

{E_{\ell}=E_{\ell}(d,i)=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|=q_{\nu}^{-\ell}\}.}

By Theorem
3.2
, there exists a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)>0
such that
\mu_{di}(E_{\ell})\leq C_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i+1)}
for any
\ell\geq 0
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j}\|^{-\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\int_{E_{\ell}}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-\beta}\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\int_{E_{\ell}}(q_{\nu}^{-\ell})^{-\beta}\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{\ell\beta}\mu_{di}(E_{\ell})\leq C_{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{\ell\beta}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i+1)},

which is finite for any
\beta<d-i+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{i}
be random variables as in Lemma
3.5
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)
the constant as in Theorem
3.2
. Then for every
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}\|^{-(d-i+1)}\mathds{1}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa}))\leq C_{2}\kappa.}

Proof.

Recall
E_{\ell}
as in (
3.15
). Since
\mu_{di}(E_{\ell})\leq C_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i+1)}
and

{\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa}\}=\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{\kappa-1}E_{\ell},}

we have

{\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j}\|^{-(d-i+1)}\mathds{1}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j}\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa}))\ \\
&=\int_{M_{d,i}({\mathbf{K}_{\nu}})}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-(d-i+1)}\mathds{1}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa})\ d\overline{\mu_{di}}(\mathbf{s})\\
&=\int_{M_{d,i}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-(d-i+1)}\mathds{1}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa})\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s})\\
&=\sum_{\ell={0}}^{\kappa-1}\int_{E_{\ell}}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-(d-i+1)}\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s})\\
&=\sum_{\ell={0}}^{\kappa-1}\int_{E_{\ell}}{(q_{\nu}^{-\ell})}^{-(d-i+1)}\ d\mu_{di}(\mathbf{s})\\
&=\sum_{\ell={0}}^{\kappa-1}q_{\nu}^{\ell(d-i+1)}\mu_{di}(E_{\ell})\leq\sum_{\ell={0}}^{\kappa-1}q_{\nu}^{\ell(d-i+1)}C_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(d-i+1)}\leq C_{2}\kappa.\end{split}}

∎

Now we obtain an analog of

Proposition 3.7
.

For
i\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket
, let

.
Then there exists a constant

depending only on

such that

{\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\|g_{t}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})\leq Ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}}

for any

and any decomposable
i
-vector
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
.

Proof.

Set

{I_{t}(\mathbf{v})=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\ \|g_{t}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})}

for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and decomposable
i
-vector
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
.
By
[
Bang25
, Corollary 2.11]
(see also
[
HP
, Lemma 2.7]
for the transitivity on Grassmanian), for any decomposable
i
-vectors
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|
, there exist

with
|c|=1
and
\mathbf{g}\in\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
such that
\mathbf{v}=c\mathbf{g}\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
. Using the
\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
-invariance of
\lambda
and the homogeneity of the norm, we have

{\begin{split}I_{t}(\mathbf{v})&=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\ \|g_{t}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\ \|g_{t}\mathbf{k}(c\mathbf{g}\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})\\
&=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\ \|g_{t}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{g}\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\ \|g_{t}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|^{-\beta_{i}}d\lambda(\mathbf{k})\\
&=I_{t}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\end{split}}

Thus, for any decomposable
i
-vectors
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
, we have

{\|\mathbf{v}\|^{\beta_{i}}I_{t}(\mathbf{v})=I_{t}(\frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|})=I_{t}(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{\|\mathbf{w}\|})=\|\mathbf{w}\|^{\beta_{i}}I_{t}(\mathbf{w}).}

Hence, we have

(3.16)

{I_{t}(\mathbf{v})=C(t)\|\mathbf{v}\|^{-\beta_{i}}}

for some function
C(t)
.

Let
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}
, where

are independent random variables on

\overline{\mu_{m+n}}
.
Since
\beta_{i}=\frac{m}{i}<m+n-i+1
for every

, we obtain
C^{\prime}_{1}:=\mathbb{E}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}\|^{-\beta_{i}})<\infty
by Lemma
3.5
.
Taking the expectation on (
3.16
), and since the distributions of
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{i}
are
\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
-invariant, we obtain

{C(t)=\frac{1}{C^{\prime}_{1}}\mathbb{E}(I_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i}))=\frac{1}{C^{\prime}_{1}}\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i})\|^{-\beta_{i}}).}

Hence, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
c
depending only on
m
and
n
such that

(3.17)

{\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\leq ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}}

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Let
V_{u}
and
V_{s}
be the subspaces of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
spanned by
\{\mathbf{e}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
and
\{\mathbf{e}_{m+j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}

p_{u}^{(1)}:\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}\rightarrow{V_{u}}

{p_{u}^{(i)}(\mathbf{e}_{I})=\bigwedge_{k=1}^{i}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{e}_{\iota_{k}})}

for each

and extending it linearly. Define the projection

Note that

(3.18)

{{p_{u}^{(i)}(\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket})=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v}_{j})}}

for any
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}

For
1<i<m

(3.19)

{\|p_{u}^{(i)}g_{t}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|=\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}g_{t}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|\leq\|g_{t}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|.}

p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots

\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m}

\overline{\mu_{m}}

(3.20)

{B_{i}:=\mathbb{E}(\|p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\|^{-\beta_{i}})<\infty}

.
Hence, it follows from (

), (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and the equality
p_{u}^{(1)}(g_{t}\mathbf{x}_{j})=\pi_{\nu}^{-nt}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{j})
that

(3.21)

{\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(\|g_{t}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}})&\leq\mathbb{E}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}p_{u}^{(1)}(g_{t}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\\
&=\mathbb{E}(q_{\nu}^{-ni\beta_{i}t}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\leq q_{\nu}^{-mnt}\max_{1<i<m}B_{i},\end{split}}

which satisfies (

Now, assume

Thus, for any
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we can separate the left hand side of (

(3.22)

{\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\leq E_{1}+E_{2},}

where

{\begin{split}E_{1}&=\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}));\\
E_{2}&=q_{\nu}^{im{\beta}_{i}t}\mathbb{E}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j}\|^{-\beta_{i}}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t})).\end{split}}

By Lemma
3.6
for
d=m

(3.23)

{\mathbb{E}(\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-(m-i+1)}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}))\leq C_{2}\kappa t}

Since
\beta_{i}=\frac{m}{i}=m-i+1
and
g_{t}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{j})=\pi_{\nu}^{-nt}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{j})
for

3.23

(3.24)

{\begin{split}E_{1}&=\mathbb{E}(\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}{g}_{t}p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{-\beta_{i}}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(i)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{x}_{j})\|>q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}))\\

&\leq q_{\nu}^{-ni\beta_{i}t}(C_{2}\kappa t)=C_{2}\kappa tq_{\nu}^{-mnt}.\end{split}}

To estimate

, we use a direct calculation applying Theorem

, which is a different method from
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 3.1]
using conditional expectation. First, assume
i=1
. We have

(3.25)

{\begin{split}E_{2}&=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\mathbb{E}(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\|^{-m}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}))\\
&=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\int_{{\textbf{K}_{\nu}}^{m+n}}\|\mathbf{s}\|^{-m}\mathds{1}(\|p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{s})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t})d\overline{\mu}_{m+n}(\mathbf{s})\\

&=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{m\ell}\mu_{m+n}(\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{m+n}:\|\mathbf{s}\|=q_{\nu}^{-\ell},\|p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{s})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}\}).\end{split}}

Let
B_{\ell,\kappa t}=\mu_{m+n}(\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{m+n}:\|\mathbf{s}\|=q_{\nu}^{-\ell},\|p_{u}^{(1)}(\mathbf{s})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}\})

(3.27)

Therefore, it follows from (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27

(3.28)

{\begin{split}E_{2}&\leq q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa t-1}q_{\nu}^{m\ell}(q_{\nu}^{-n\ell-m\kappa t})+\sum_{\ell=\kappa t}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{m\ell}(q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)\ell})\Bigr)\\

Note that

{\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa t-1}q_{\nu}^{(m-n)\ell}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{q_{\nu}^{(m-n)\kappa t}-1}{q_{\nu}^{(m-n)}-1}\leq\frac{q_{\nu}^{(m-n)\kappa t}}{1-q_{\nu}^{-n}}&\quad\text{if }m>n\\
\kappa t\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq\frac{\kappa t}{1-q_{\nu}^{-n}}&\quad\text{if }m\leq n.\end{cases}}

Hence, it follows from (

) that

{E_{2}\leq\begin{cases}(1-q_{\nu}^{-n})^{-1}q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(2q_{\nu}^{-n\kappa t})&\quad\text{if }m>n\\

(3.29)

{E_{2}\leq 2(1-q_{\nu}^{-n})^{-1}\kappa tq_{\nu}^{-mnt}}

for any

and
\kappa

>

\max(\frac{m(m+n)}{m},\frac{m(m+n)}{n})
.

i=m
. Define

{Z=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{m+n,m}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\|p_{u}^{(m)}(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{s}_{j})\|\leq q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t}\}.}

Consider a partition
\{R_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
of

defined by

{R_{\ell}=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{m+n,m}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|=q_{\nu}^{-\ell}\}.}

Then, we have

(3.30)

{E_{2}=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\int_{Z}\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{s}_{j}\|^{-1}d\mu_{(m+n)m}(\mathbf{s})=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{\ell}\mu_{(m+n)m}(R_{\ell}\cap Z).}

Note that
R_{\ell}\subset E_{\ell}(m+n,m)
and

which are defined in Theorem
3.2
; indeed, the latter inclusion follows from (

), since

.
If
\ell\geq\kappa t
, we have

since
\|p_{u}^{(m)}(\mathbf{v})\|\leq\|\mathbf{v}\|
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{m}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{m+n}
. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}_{2}=C^{\prime}_{2}(m,n)>0
such that

(3.31)

{\mu_{(m+n)m}(R_{\ell}\cap Z)\leq\mu_{(m+n)m}(E_{\ell}(m+n,m))\leq C^{\prime}_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(n+1)}.}

If
\ell<\kappa t
, by Theorem
3.2
again, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(m,n)>0
such that

(3.32)

{\begin{split}\mu_{(m+n)m}(R_{\ell}\cap Z)&\leq\mu_{(m+n)m}(F_{\ell,\kappa t}(m+n,m))\\
&\leq C_{1}q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t-n\ell}\binom{\kappa t-\ell+\binom{m+n}{m}}{\kappa t-\ell+1}.\end{split}}

Note that given
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there exists a constant
C(a)>0
depending only on

such that

{\binom{x+a}{x+1}\leq\frac{(x+a)^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}\leq C(a)q_{\nu}^{\frac{1}{2}x}}

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
since the exponential growth is faster than the polynomial one. Hence, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
depending only on
m
and
n
such that

{\binom{\kappa t-\ell+\binom{m+n}{m}}{\kappa t-\ell+1}\leq C_{3}q_{\nu}^{\frac{1}{2}(\kappa t-\ell)}}

for any
0\leq\ell\leq\kappa t-1
.

Therefore, it follows from (
3.30
), (
3.31
) and (
3.32
) that

{\begin{split}E_{2}&\leq q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa t-1}q_{\nu}^{\ell}(C_{1}q_{\nu}^{-\kappa t-n\ell}(C_{3}q_{\nu}^{\frac{1}{2}(\kappa t-\ell)}))+\sum_{\ell=\kappa t}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{\ell}(C^{\prime}_{2}q_{\nu}^{-\ell(n+1)}))\\
&=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(C_{1}C_{3}q_{\nu}^{-\frac{1}{2}\kappa t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa t-1}q_{\nu}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\ell}+C^{\prime}_{2}\sum_{\ell=\kappa t}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-n\ell})\\
&=q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(C_{1}C_{3}q_{\nu}^{-\frac{1}{2}\kappa t}\frac{1-q_{\nu}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\kappa t}}{1-q_{\nu}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})}}+C^{\prime}_{2}\frac{q_{\nu}^{-n\kappa t}}{1-q_{\nu}^{-n}})\\
&\leq q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(C_{1}C_{3}q_{\nu}^{-\frac{1}{2}\kappa t}\frac{1}{1-q_{\nu}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})}}+C^{\prime}_{2}\frac{q_{\nu}^{-n\kappa t}}{1-q_{\nu}^{-n}})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \\
&\leq C_{4}q_{\nu}^{m^{2}t}(q_{\nu}^{-\frac{1}{2}\kappa t}+q_{\nu}^{-n\kappa t}),\end{split}}

where
C_{4}=\max(C_{1}C_{3}(1-q_{\nu}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})})^{-1},C^{\prime}_{2}(1-q_{\nu}^{-n})^{-1})
. Hence, we have

(3.33)

{E_{2}\leq C_{4}q_{\nu}^{-mnt}}

for any
\kappa\geq 2(m^{2}+mn)
.

As a summary, it follows from (
3.22
), (
3.24
), (
3.29
) and (
3.33
) that when
i=1
or
i=m
, we have

{\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\leq(C_{1}\kappa t+\max(2(1-q_{\nu}^{-n})^{-1}\kappa t,C_{4}))q_{\nu}^{-mnt}}

for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and any
\kappa>2(m^{2}+mn)
. Thus, by fixing some
\kappa>2(m^{2}+mn)
, we obtain

(3.34)

{\mathbb{E}(\|{g}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{x}_{i})\|^{-\beta_{i}})\leq Ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}}

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, where
C
is a constant depending on
m
and
n
.

Therefore, from (
3.21
) and (
3.34
), we reach the goal (
3.17
).
∎

4.
the Hodge duality and the proof of Proposition
3.1

We let
d=m+n
for this section and the remaining sections. While
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 3.1]
covers all
1\leq i\leq d
using the equivariance of the Hodge duality under
\mathrm{SO}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, the Hodge dual operator is not equivariant under the ultrametric orthogonal group
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
. Instead, we apply the duality to horospherical subgroups.

Note that the exterior powers and their natural basis can be defined for any free
R
-module with scalar in a commutative ring
R
with unity. Thus, we can define the standard Hodge dual operator between
\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
and
\bigwedge^{d-i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}

in the same way as a linear map, even though there is no inner product structure.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{e}_{j}\}_{j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. For each
i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, we define
the Hodge dual operator

*:\bigwedge^{i}{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{d-i}{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}}
linearly by

{*(\mathbf{e}_{I})=\sigma_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I^{c}},}

where
I=\{a_{1}<\cdots<a_{i}\}\in\wp_{i}^{d}
,
I^{c}=\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\smallsetminus I
, and
\sigma_{I}=(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}+\sum_{s=1}^{i}{a_{s}}}
.

Note that the above definition is the same one as in the real case and the term
\sigma_{I}
comes from
\mathbf{e}_{I}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{I^{c}}=\sigma_{I}\mathbf{e}_{\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}
. The following lemma says that some properties related to exterior powers still hold for the global function fields.
This property of the Hodge dual operator is well-known, but we cannot find a suitable reference for non-Archimedean spaces.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
i\in\llbracket 1,d-1\rrbracket
,
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}{{\mathbf{K}_{\nu}}^{d}}
, and
\mathbf{g}\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
, we have

(1)

\|*\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|
;

(2)

*(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{v})={}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!)(*\mathbf{v})
.

Proof.

(
1
) Let
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{I}v_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I}\in\bigwedge^{i}\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
be an
i
-vector. Since

{*\mathbf{v}=*(\sum_{I}v_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I})=\sum_{I}v_{I}\!*\!(\mathbf{e}_{I})=\sum_{I}v_{I}\sigma_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I^{c}}}

and
|\sigma_{I}|=1
for all
I\in\wp_{i}^{d}
, it follows that

{\|*\mathbf{v}\|=\|\sum_{I}v_{I}\sigma_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I^{c}}\|=\max_{I}|v_{I}\sigma_{I}|=\max_{I}|v_{I}|=\|\mathbf{v}\|.}

(
2
) It is enough to show that for any
\mathbf{g}\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbf{K}_{\nu})
and
I\in\wp_{i}^{d}
, we have

(4.1)

{*(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{e}_{I})={}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!)(*\mathbf{e}_{I}).}

First, observe that

(4.2)

{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{e}_{I}=\sum_{J\in\wp_{i}^{d}}(\det\mathbf{g}_{J,I})\mathbf{e}_{J},}

where
\det\mathbf{g}_{I,J}
is the
(I,J)
-minor of
\mathbf{g}
.
Hence, its Hodge dual is

{*(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{e}_{I})=*(\sum_{J}(\det\mathbf{g}_{J,I})\mathbf{e}_{J})=\sum_{J}(\det\mathbf{g}_{J,I})*(\mathbf{e}_{J})=\sum_{J}\sigma_{J}(\det\mathbf{g}_{J,I})\mathbf{e}_{J^{c}}.}

On the other hand, using (
4.2
) with
{}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!)
, we have

\displaystyle{}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!)(*\mathbf{e}_{I})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{I}{}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!)(\mathbf{e}_{I^{c}})=\sum_{J\in\wp_{d-i}^{d}}\sigma_{I}\det({}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!))_{J,I^{c}}\mathbf{e}_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\wp_{i}^{d}}\sigma_{I}\det({}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!))_{J^{c},I^{c}}\mathbf{e}_{J^{c}}.

Therefore, to prove (
4.1
), it suffices to show that

(4.3)

{\det\mathbf{g}_{J,I}=\sigma_{I}\sigma_{J}\det({}^{t}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\!))_{J^{c},I^{c}}}

for any
I,J\in\wp_{i}^{d}
. Since
\det\mathbf{g}=1
, (
4.3
) is Jacobi’s identity, which holds over any commutative ring (see for instance
[
CSS13
, Lemma A.1]
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The Hodge dual operator preserves the decomposability. Indeed, if
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{i}
is a nonzero decomposable vector,
then
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}
are linearly independent over
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}
, and thus it can be extended to a basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{d}\}
of
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}^{d}
. Let
\mathbf{v}
be the
(d,d)
-matrix with columns
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{d}
. Then
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}\mathbf{e}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}
and
\mathbf{v}
is invertible. Hence, by Lemma
4.2
, we have

{\begin{split}*\mathbf{w}&={}^{t}(\mathbf{v}^{-1}\!)\sigma_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}\mathbf{e}_{\llbracket i+1,d\rrbracket}=(\sigma_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}{}^{t}(\mathbf{v}^{-1}\!)\mathbf{e}_{i+1})\wedge\cdots\wedge({}^{t}(\mathbf{v}^{-1}\!)\mathbf{e}_{d}),\end{split}}

which implies that
*\mathbf{w}
is decomposable.

Now, we are ready to prove Proposition
3.1
using the Hodge dual operator.

\Psi_{i}\supsetneq\{L_{i}\}
: Take
L^{\prime}\in\Psi_{i}
such that
L^{\prime}\neq L_{i}
. Since
\dim(L^{\prime}+L_{i})=i+j
for some
j>0
, it follows from
[
Bang25
, Theorem 1.4]
using the above assumption that for all
\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
,

{\begin{split}\alpha_{i}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)&\leq q_{\nu}^{mnt}\alpha_{i}(x)=\frac{q_{\nu}^{mnt}}{\|L_{i}\|}\leq\frac{q_{\nu}^{2mnt}}{\sqrt{\|L^{\prime}\|\|L_{i}\|}}\\
&\leq\frac{q_{\nu}^{2mnt}}{\sqrt{\|L^{\prime}\cap L_{i}\|\|L^{\prime}+L_{i}\|}}\leq q_{\nu}^{2mnt}\sqrt{\alpha_{i+j}(x)\alpha_{i-j}(x)}.\end{split}}

Thus it follows from
\mu_{mn}(M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}))=1
that

{\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\alpha_{i}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)^{\beta_{i}}d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\leq q_{\nu}^{2mnt\beta_{i}}\max_{1\leq j\leq\min\{d-i,i\}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{i+j}(x)\alpha_{i-j}(x)}\right)^{\beta_{i}}.}

Combining the two cases, we obtain Corollary
5.1
.
∎

Using Corollary
5.1
and
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 4.1]
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\beta_{i}
’s and
c>0
be as in Proposition
3.1
. For any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, one can choose constants
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{d}>0
and
T>0
such that for any
x\in X
with
\widetilde{\alpha}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(\omega_{i}\alpha_{i})^{\beta_{i}}
satisfying
\widetilde{\alpha}(x)>T
, we have

\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\leq 3ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\widetilde{\alpha}(x).

Proof.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
To use
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 4.1]
, we set
H=X
,
d=m+n
,
f_{i}=\alpha_{i}
so that
f_{0}=f_{d}=1
, the linear operator
A
on the linear space of real functions on
H
given by

(Af)(x)=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})

for
\beta(i)=1/\beta_{i}
. Note that
i\mapsto\beta(i)
is concave. It follows from Corollary
5.1
that for each
i\in\llbracket 1,d-1\rrbracket
, we have

\begin{split}A(f_{i}^{\beta_{i}})(x)&=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\alpha_{i}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)^{\beta_{i}}d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\\
&\leq ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}f_{i}^{\beta_{i}}(x)+\left(q_{\nu}^{mnt}\right)^{2\beta_{i}}\max_{1\leq j\leq\min\{d-i,i\}}\left(\sqrt{f_{i+j}(x)f_{i-j}(x)}\right)^{\beta_{i}}.\end{split}

Setting
a=ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}
,
\omega=q_{\nu}^{mnt}
, and

, and using
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 4.1]
, we can choose

and

satisfies that

Thus the function
\widetilde{\alpha}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(\omega_{i}\alpha_{i})^{\beta_{i}}
satisfies that for all

\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\leq 2ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\widetilde{\alpha}(x)+C_{0}.

By choosing

, we can conclude Corollary
5.2
.
∎

6.
Covering estimates using Margulis function

In this section, we fix
c>0
as in Proposition
3.1
and for a given
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, fix
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d}
and
T
as in Corollary
5.2
, and write
\widetilde{\alpha}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(\omega_{i}\alpha_{i})^{\beta_{i}}
.
For any
x\in X
,
M>0
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let us define

Z_{x}(M,N,t):=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket,~\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{\ell t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)>M\}.

By Mahler’s compactness criterion for the space of unimodular lattices over global function fields (e.g., see
[
KST17
, Theorem 1.1]
),

\widetilde{\alpha}
is a proper map, hence the set

X_{\leq M}:=\{x\in X:\widetilde{\alpha}(x)\leq M\}

is compact. Similarly, we define
X_{>M}=X\smallsetminus X_{\leq M}
.

First observe that for any
\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
and
x\in X
, it follows from the fact that
u_{\mathbf{s}}\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
and from
[
Bang25
, Lemma 2.5]
that

(6.1)

{\widetilde{\alpha}(u_{\mathbf{s}}x)=\widetilde{\alpha}(x).}

We have the following main proposition. Unlike in the previous sections, from this point on,
\mathbf{s}_{i}
are matrices rather than column vectors of a matrix. We also denote
d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})
by
d\mathbf{s}
for simplicity.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be fixed. For all
x\in X_{>T}
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
M\geq T
, we have

\int_{Z_{x}(M,N-1,t)}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mu_{mn}(\mathbf{s})\leq\left(3ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\right)^{N}\widetilde{\alpha}(x).

Remark 6.2
.

This proposition is a function field version of
[
KKLM17
, Proposition 5.1]
. It is worth noting that while a certain recursive property of Gaussian measures was used in
[
KKLM17
]
, we use convolutions of measures inspired by
[
GS20
]
.

Proof.

For a fixed
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let us denote

Z=\{(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})^{N}:\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)>T\quad\forall k\in\llbracket 1,N-1\rrbracket\}.

Using Corollary
5.2
repeatedly, we have

(6.2)

{\int\cdots\int_{Z}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{N}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)d\mathbf{s}_{N}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{1}\leq\left(3ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\right)^{N}\widetilde{\alpha}(x).}

Observe that

{g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{N}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}=g_{Nt}u_{\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})},}

where
\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}
.
Thus we have

(6.3)

{\begin{split}&\int\cdots\int_{Z}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{N}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)d\mathbf{s}_{N}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{1}=\int\cdots\int_{Z}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})}x)d\mathbf{s}_{N}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{1}\end{split}}

Following
[
GS20
, Section 5]
, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, let
\widetilde{\mu}_{i}
be the Radon measure on
M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
defined by

\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(\mathbf{s})d\widetilde{\mu}_{i}(\mathbf{s})=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}

for
f\in C(M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}))
. Note for future use that
\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=\overline{\mu_{mn}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}=\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\ast\cdots\ast\widetilde{\mu}_{1}
be the measure on

M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
defined by iterated convolutions.
Observe that

(6.4)

{\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}(f)=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\cdots\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N}))d\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{N}.}

Claim 1.

\{(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})^{N}:\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in\phi(Z)\}=Z
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Clearly, the set
Z
is contained in the left hand side set. If
(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})
is an element of the left hand side set,
then there exists
(\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N}^{\prime})\in Z
such that
\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})=\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N}^{\prime})
.
Hence for any
k\in\llbracket 1,N-1\rrbracket
,

{\begin{split}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)&=\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}})^{-1}g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}}x)\\
&=\widetilde{\alpha}((g_{kt}u_{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\prime})}g_{-kt})g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}}x)\\
&=\widetilde{\alpha}(u_{\pi_{\nu}^{-kdt}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\prime})}g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}}x)\\
&=\widetilde{\alpha}(u_{\pi_{\nu}^{-kdt}\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\prime}-\mathbf{s}_{i})}g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}}x)\\
&=\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime}}x)>T.\end{split}}

The fourth line holds since
\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})=\phi(\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N}^{\prime})
and the last line since
\pi_{\nu}^{-kdt}\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\prime}-\mathbf{s}_{i})\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
and (
6.1
). Thus
(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in Z
.
∎

Let us denote by
\varphi_{x}(\mathbf{s})=\mathds{1}_{\phi(Z)}(\mathbf{s})\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)
. Using Fubini’s theorem, it follows from (
6.3
), (
6.4
), and
Claim 1
that

(6.5)

{\int\cdots\int_{Z}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{N}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)d\mathbf{s}_{N}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{1}=\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}(\varphi_{x}).}

While
\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\overline{\mu_{mn}}
, the ultrametric property implies that they coincide. Such an exact coincidence does not occur in the real case. See
[
GS20
, Lemma 5.5]
.

Claim 2.

We have
\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}=\overline{\mu_{mn}}
.

Proof of Claim 2.

Since
\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=\overline{\mu_{mn}}
is invariant under translations on
M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
,
it follows that for any
f\in C(M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}))
,

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}(f)&=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\cdots\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(\mathbf{s}_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i})d\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{N}\\
&=\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}\cdots\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})}f(\mathbf{s}_{1})d\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{s}_{N}\\
&=\overline{\mu_{mn}}(f).\end{split}

∎

It follows from
Claim 2
and the fact that
\phi(Z)\subset M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
that

(6.6)

{\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}(\varphi_{x})=\overline{\mu_{mn}}(\varphi_{x})=\int\mathds{1}_{\phi(Z)}(\mathbf{s})\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mathbf{s}.}

We need the following claim.

Claim 3.
For any
\mathbf{s}\in Z_{x}(M,N-1,t)
,
\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{s})\subset Z
.

Proof of Claim 3.

If
(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{s})
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}\in Z_{x}(M,N-1,t)
,
hence for all
\ell\in\llbracket 1,N-1\rrbracket
,

(6.7)

{\widetilde{\alpha}\left(\left(g_{\ell t}u_{\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}}g_{-\ell t}\right)g_{\ell t}u_{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}}x\right)>M.}

Note that

(6.8)

{g_{\ell t}u_{\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}}g_{-\ell t}=u_{\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1-\ell)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}}}

and
\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{N}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1-\ell)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
. Thus it follows from (
6.1
), (
6.7
), and (
6.8
) that

{\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{\ell t}u_{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\pi_{\nu}^{(i-1)dt}\mathbf{s}_{i}}x)=\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{\ell}}\cdots g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}x)>M\geq T,}

which proves
(\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{N})\in Z
.
∎

Combining (
6.2
), (
6.5
), (
6.6
), and
Claim 3
, we have

\begin{split}\left(3ctq_{\nu}^{-mnt}\right)^{N}\widetilde{\alpha}(x)\geq\widetilde{\mu}^{\ast N}(\varphi_{x})&\geq\int\mathds{1}_{\phi(Z)}(\mathbf{s})\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mathbf{s}\\
&\geq\int_{Z_{x}(M,N-1,t)}\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{Nt}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)d\mathbf{s}.\end{split}

∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be fixed. There exists
M_{0}=M_{0}(t)>T
such that for all
x\in X
,
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,

, the set

\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}(x)}{M}(3ct)^{N}q_{\nu}^{(m+n-1)mntN}

M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
of radius
q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)tN}
.

Proof.

Using (
5.2
), (
6.1
), and the construction of
\widetilde{\alpha}
in Corollary
5.2
,
it follows that for any
x\in X
and
\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})

(6.9)

{\widetilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{\mathbf{s}}x)\leq\max_{i}q_{\nu}^{mnt\beta_{i}}\widetilde{\alpha}(x).}

Setting

for any

For fixed

and

, it follows from Proposition

that

{\mu_{mn}(Z_{x}(M,N,t))\leq\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}(x)}{M}(3ct)^{N}q_{\nu}^{-mntN}.}

Now observe that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
we can partition
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
into
q_{\nu}^{\ell}
disjoint balls of radius
q_{\nu}^{-\ell}
as follows:

(6.11)

{\mathcal{O}_{\nu}=\bigsqcup_{a_{0},\dots,a_{\ell-1}\in\mathbf{k}_{\nu}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}a_{i}\pi_{\nu}^{i}+\pi_{\nu}^{\ell}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}.}

Thus we can partition
M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
into

D_{1},\dots,D_{p}
of radius

. Let
p_{1}

D_{i}

Since

{p_{1}\leq\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}(x)}{M}(3ct)^{N}q_{\nu}^{mn(m+n-1)tN}.}

’s if necessary, we may assume that

.

Now fix any

, then we can take
\mathbf{s}_{0}\in D_{i}

For a given compact subset

Theorem 6.4
.

There exist
t_{0}>0
and a function
C^{\prime\prime}:X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that the following holds. For any

, there exists a compact set
Q=Q(t)
of
X
such that for any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and

, the set

can be covered by

C^{\prime\prime}(x)t^{3N}q_{\nu}^{(m+n-\delta)mntN}

balls in
M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
of radius
q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)tN}
.

Proof.

Let
t>t_{0}
be given, with
t_{0}\geq 2
a large real number, to be determined later on, depending only on
m,n
. Let
\widetilde{\alpha}
and
T
be as in Corollary
5.2
. We will find large enough
M>0
such that the compact set

satisfies the conclusion of the theorem.

For a given
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
x\in X
, let

J_{x}=\{\ell\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket:g_{t\ell}x\notin Q\}.

Then,

Z_{x}(Q,N,t,\delta)=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):|J_{u_{\mathbf{s}}x}|\geq\delta N\}.

For any subset
J\subset\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, let
Z(J)=\{\mathbf{s}\in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):J_{u_{\mathbf{s}}x}=J\}
. Note that

, where the union runs over all subsets
J\subset\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
with
|J|\geq\delta N
. Since the number of such subsets is at most
2^{N}\leq t^{N}
, it is enough to show that for a given subset
J\subset\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, the set
Z(J)
can be covered by
C^{\prime\prime}(x)t^{2N}q_{\nu}^{((m+n)N-|J|)mnt}

, for

We decompose the sets
J
into ordered subintervals
J_{1},\dots,J_{p}
of maximal size such that

. Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{p^{\prime}}
be the ordered maximal subintervals of
\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\smallsetminus J

such that

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{p}J_{i}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{p^{\prime}}I_{j}.

Claim.
For any integer
L\leq N
, if

(6.12)

{\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}J_{i}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}I_{j},}

then the set
Z(J)
can be covered by

(6.13)

{C^{\prime\prime}(x)t^{2L}q_{\nu}^{((m+n)L-|J_{1}|-\cdots-|J_{\ell}|)mnt}}

balls of radius
q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)tL}
.

Proof of Claim.

We prove the claim by induction on
L
. In the first step, if
\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket=J_{1}
, then by choosing
t_{0}\geq 3c
and
M>M_{0}
large enough so that
M\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega_{i}^{\beta_{i}}
hence
\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}(x)}{M}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(x)
,
it follows from Corollary

that the set
Z_{x}(M,L,t)
can be covered by

\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}(x)}{M}(3ct)^{L}q_{\nu}^{(m+n-1)mntL}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(x)t^{2L}q_{\nu}^{((m+n)L-|J_{1}|)mnt}

balls of radius
q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)tL}
.
Since

and
\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket\subset J
, we have

Thus the claim follows.
If
\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket=I_{1}
, then it follows from (

) that
Z(J)\subset M_{m,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
can be covered by
q_{\nu}^{(m+n)Lmnt}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(x)t^{2L}q_{\nu}^{(m+n)Lmnt}
balls of radius
q_{\nu}^{-(m+n)tL}
.

Now, assume that the claim holds for some
L\leq N

Nodal and constant sign solutions for singular elliptic problems
Dumitru Motreanu, Abdelkrim Moussaoui

Abstract
. We establish the existence of multiple solutions for singular quasilinear elliptic problems with a precise sign information: two opposite constant sign solutions and a nodal solution. The approach combines sub-supersolutions method and Leray-Schauder topological degree involving perturbation argument.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J75, 35J62, 35J92.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\left(N\geq 2\right)
be a bounded
domain with
C^{1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the singular
elliptic problem

(\mathrm{P})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u=f(x,u)&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{p}
stands for the
p
-Laplacian operator on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
1<p<\infty
and the reaction term
f(x,u
) is
described by a Carathéodory function
f:\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that can exhibit singularity when
u
approaches zero. This occurs under the following hypothesis on the
nonlinearity
f(x,s)
reflecting the singular character of problem
(\mathrm{P})
:

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}f(x,s)=+\infty\text{ \ uniformly for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

For more information on the study of singular problems we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
and the references therein.

The presence of a singularity in
(\mathrm{P})
represents a major obstacle
to overcome, especially since nonpositive solutions are addressed. Namely,
beside positive and negative solutions, we also discuss the existence of
nodal solutions for
(\mathrm{P})
that are sign changing functions and
inevitably go through the singularity. As far as we know, such solutions for
problems involving singularities have been rarely investigated in the
literature. Actually,
[
16
]
is the only paper that has considered this
issue. The existence of nodal solutions is established for a class of
semilinear singular system by means of the trapping region formed by pairs
of appropriately defined sign changing sub-supersolutions. Exploiting
spectral properties of Laplacian operator as well as adequate truncation,
the author establishes that the nodal solution vanishes on negligible sets.
This is an essential point enabling nodal solutions investigation for
singular problems.

In line with
[
16
]
, by a solution of problem
(\mathrm{P})
we mean any
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
f(x,u)\varphi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\ dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\varphi\ dx,

for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Our main goal is to establish multiplicity result for singular quasilinear
elliptic problem
(\mathrm{P})
, with a precise sign information. We provide
three solutions for
(\mathrm{P})
, two of which are of opposite
constant-sign solutions while the third one is nodal. Our approach combines
sub-supersolution method and topological degree theory. We first establish
the existence of opposite constant-sign solutions
u_{+},u_{-}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
. They are located in positive and negative
intervals formed by two opposite constant sign sub-supersolutions pairs,
constructed through a choice of suitable functions with an adjustment of
adequate constants. Then, focusing on the aforementioned intervals, we show
the existence of a minimal positive solution
u_{+}^{\ast}
and a maximal
negative solution
u_{-}^{\ast}
for problem
(\mathrm{P})
. The argument
is essentially based on the Zorn’s Lemma as well as the
S_{+}
-property of
the negative
p
-Laplacian operator on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

A significant feature in the present work concerns the finding of a nodal
solution for singular problem
(\mathrm{P})
. It is a third solution for
(\mathrm{P})
achieved via the Leray-Schauder degree theory. The latter is
applied to a perturbed system
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon}),
depending on a
parameter
\varepsilon>0,
whose study is relevant for problem
(\mathrm{P})
. Precisely, we prove that the degree on a ball
\mathcal{B}_{R_{\varepsilon}}(0)
, centered at
0
of radius
R_{\varepsilon}
,
encompassing all potential solutions of
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
is
0,

while the degree on
\mathcal{B}_{R_{\varepsilon}}(0)\backslash\mathcal{A}_{R_{\varepsilon}},
where
\mathcal{A}_{R_{\varepsilon}}
is a set
containing only solutions lying inside the interval
]u_{-}^{\ast},u_{+}^{\ast}[,
is equal to
1
. By the excision property of
Leray-Schauder degree, this leads to the existence of a nontrivial solution
u_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
for
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
in
\mathcal{A}_{R_{\varepsilon}}.
Then, a solution
u^{\ast}\in]u_{-}^{\ast},u_{+}^{\ast}[
of
(\mathrm{P})
is derived by passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.
The argument is based on a priori estimates,
dominated convergence theorem as well as
S_{+}
-property of the negative
p
-Laplacian. Due to the extremality of constant-sign solutions
u_{-}^{\ast}

and
u_{+}^{\ast},
then
u^{\ast}
is a third solution of
(\mathrm{P})

that changes sign.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section
2
deals
with opposite constant sign solutions while Section
3
provides a
nodal solution for problem
(\mathrm{P})
.

2.
Constant sign solutions

Given
1<p<\infty
, the spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

are endowed with the usual norms
\|u\|_{p}=(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\ dx)^{1/p}
and
\|u\|_{1,p}=(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\ dx)^{1/p}
, respectively. For a later use, set
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. We will
also use the spaces
C(\overline{\Omega})
and
C_{0}^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})=\{u\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}):u=0\ \mbox{on
$\partial\Omega$}\}
with
\beta\in(0,1)
.

Let us denote by
\lambda_{1,p}
the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{p}
on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
which is given by

(2.1)

\lambda_{1,p}=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x}.

Let
\phi_{1,p}
be the positive normalized eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{p}

corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}
, that is,

-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1,p}=\lambda_{1,p}\phi_{1,p}^{p-1}\ \text{in }\Omega,\ \ \phi_{1,p}=0\ \text{on }\partial\Omega,

\phi_{1,p}>0
in
\Omega
, and
\|\phi_{1,p}\|_{p}=1
. Recall
that there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(2.2)

\phi_{1,p}(x)\geq c_{0}d(x)\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,

where
d(x)
denotes the distance from
x\in\overline{\Omega}
to the
boundary
\partial\Omega
.

For
u,v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, the notation
u\ll v
means

\begin{array}[]{c}u(x)<v(x),\,\,\forall x\in\Omega,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial\eta}<\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{array}

where
\eta
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.

Remark 1
.

Under assumption
(\mathrm{H.1})
, any solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
of
(\mathrm{P})
satisfies
u(x)\neq 0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Otherwise, if
u
vanishes on a set of positive measure, then

(\mathrm{P})
and hypothesis
(\mathrm{H}.1)
are contradictory.

2.1.
Opposite constant sign solutions

For a fixed
\delta>0
, set
\Omega_{\delta}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:d(x)<\delta\right\}
. Let
y,y_{\delta}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
be the unique solutions of the Dirichlet
problems

(2.3)

-\Delta_{p}y=d(x)^{\alpha}\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }y=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(2.4)

-\Delta_{p}y_{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d(x)^{\alpha}&\text{in \ }\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{\delta},\\
-1&\text{in \ }\Omega_{\delta},\end{array}\right.,\text{ \ }y_{\delta}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\alpha\in(-1,0)
. They satisfy

(2.5)

c^{-1}d(x)\leq y_{\delta}(x)\leq y(x)\leq cd(x)\text{ in }\Omega,

with a constant
c>1
(see
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
).

For a constant
C>1
, we also pose

(2.6)

\overline{u}=Cy\text{ \ and \ }\underline{u}=C^{-1}y_{\delta}.

Due to (
2.5
), this gives

(2.7)

\overline{u}\geq\underline{u}\ \text{ in }\ \overline{\Omega},

so we can consider the ordered interval

\left[\underline{u},\overline{u}\right]=\{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):\underline{u}(x)\leq v(x)\leq\overline{u}(x)\ \text{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega\}.

We further assume.

(\mathrm{H}.2)
:

There exist constants
M>0
and
-1<\alpha<0
such that

f(x,s)\leq M(1+s^{\alpha})\text{ \ and \ }f(x,-s)\geq-M(1+|s|^{\alpha}),

for a.e
.
x\in\Omega,
all
s>0.

(\mathrm{H}.3)
:

There exist constants
\beta<0<m
such that

\alpha\geq\beta>-\min\{1,p-1\},

f(x,s)\geq ms^{\beta}\text{ \ and \ }f(x,-s)\leq-m|s|^{\beta},

for a.e
.
x\in\Omega,
all
s>0.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
(\mathrm{H.2})
and
(\mathrm{H.3})
hold. Then
problem
(\mathrm{P})
admits a positive solution
u_{+}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
and a negative solution
u_{-}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
within the ordered intervals
\left[\underline{u},\overline{u}\right]
and
\left[-\overline{u},-\underline{u}\right],
respectively. Moreover, every positive solution
u_{+}
and
negative solution
u_{-}
of
(\mathrm{P})
within
\left[0,\overline{u}\right]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\left[-\overline{u},0\right]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, respectively, satisfy

(2.8)

\underline{u}(x)\leq u_{+}(x)\text{ \ and \ }u_{-}(x)\leq-\underline{u}(x),\text{ }\forall x\in\Omega.

Proof.

By (
2.5
) one has

\displaystyle C^{p-1}d(x)^{\alpha}\geq M\left(\left\|d\right\|_{\infty}^{-\alpha}+(Cc^{-1})^{\alpha}\right)d(x)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq M\left(d(x)^{-\alpha}+(Cc^{-1})^{\alpha}\right)d(x)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq M(1+(Cc^{-1}d(x))^{\alpha})\geq M(1+(Cy(x))^{\alpha})\text{ \ for
all }x\in\overline{\Omega},

provided
C>0
is sufficiently large. Thus, from
(\mathrm{H.2})
, (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), it follows that

(2.9)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\overline{u}\geq M(1+\overline{u}^{\alpha})\geq f(x,\overline{u})\text{ in }\Omega

and

(2.10)

-\Delta_{p}(-\overline{u})\leq-M(1+\overline{u}^{\alpha})\leq f(x,-\overline{u})\text{ in }\Omega.

From (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and
(\mathrm{H.3})
, we get

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\underline{u}\leq C^{-(p-1)}d(x)^{\alpha}\leq C^{-\beta}m(cd(x))^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C^{-\beta}my(x)^{\beta}=m\underline{u}(x)^{\beta}\leq f(x,\underline{u}(x))\text{ in }\Omega

and

(2.12)

-\Delta_{p}(-\underline{u})\geq-m\underline{u}(x)^{\beta}\geq f(x,-\underline{u}(x))\text{ in }\Omega

provided
C>1
is sufficiently large.

In view of (
2.7
), (
2.9
)-(
2.12
), we deduce that
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
and
(-\overline{u},-\underline{u})
form
sub-supersolutions pairs for
(\mathrm{P})
. Then, the sub-supersolution
method (see
[
8
, Theorem 2]
) leads to the existence of a positive
solution
u_{+}\in\left[\underline{u},\overline{u}\right]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
and a negative solution
u_{-}\in\left[-\overline{u},-\underline{u}\right]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
, with some
\tau\in(0,1)
.

Let
u_{+}\in\left[0,\overline{u}\right]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a positive solution of
(\mathrm{P})
. By
(\mathrm{H.3})
, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x,u_{+})\geq mu_{+}^{\beta}\geq m\overline{u}^{\beta}\geq m(Ccd(x))^{\beta}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-(p-1)}d(x)^{\alpha}\geq-\Delta_{p}\underline{u}(x)\text{ \ in }\Omega,

provided
C>1
is sufficiently large and
\delta>0
sufficiently small in (
2.4
). By the weak comparison principle, we infer that property (
2.8
) holds true. Using a similar argument, we show the corresponding
property for a negative solution.
∎

2.2.
Extremal solutions

Theorem 2
.

Under assumptions
(\mathrm{H.2})
and
(\mathrm{H.3})
, problem
(\mathrm{P})
admits a smallest positive solution
u_{+}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
within
[\underline{u},\overline{u}]
and a
biggest negative solution
u_{-}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
within
[-\overline{u},-\underline{u}]
. In addition, it holds

(2.13)

\begin{array}[]{c}\underline{u}\ll u_{+}^{\ast}\text{ \ and }-\underline{u}\gg u_{-}^{\ast}\quad\text{in}\ \Omega.\end{array}

Proof.

We only prove the existence of a smallest positive solution
u_{+}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
within
[\underline{u},\overline{u}]
. The proof for the biggest negative solution in
[-\overline{u},-\underline{u}]
can be carried out in a similar way.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of all functions
w\in[\underline{u},\overline{u}]
that are solutions of
(\mathrm{P})
. From Theorem
1

it is known that
\mathcal{S}
is not empty. We claim that
\mathcal{S}
is
downward directed. To this end, let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
(\underline{u},u_{1})
and
(\underline{u},u_{2})
form pairs of
sub-supersolutions for
(\mathrm{P})
, if we set
\tilde{u}=\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, by virtue of
[
3
, Theorem 3.20]
,
(\underline{u},\tilde{u})
is a pair of sub-supersolution, too. Then, owing to
[
8
, Theorem 2]
, there exists a solution of
(\mathrm{P})
in
[\underline{u},\tilde{u}]\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, which proves the claim.

Now we show that Zorn’s lemma can be applied in
\mathcal{S}
. In this
respect, let us consider a chain
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{S}
with respect
to the order
\geq
. There is a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{C}
such that
\inf C=\inf_{k\geq 1}u_{k}
(see
[
4
, page 336]
) and we can assume that the sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is
decreasing. If
\hat{u}:=\inf\mathcal{C}
, one has
u_{k}\rightarrow\hat{u}
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\hat{u}\in[\underline{u},\overline{u}]
.
Having that each
u_{k}
is a solution of
(\mathrm{P}),
from
(\mathrm{H.2})
, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain

\left\|\nabla u_{k}\right\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{k})u_{k}\ dx\leq M\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}+u_{k}^{\alpha+1})\text{ }dx\leq C_{0}(\left\|\overline{u}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\overline{u}\right\|_{\infty}^{1+\alpha}),

with a constant
C_{0}>0
. Therefore
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and so up to a subsequence we have
u_{k}\rightharpoonup\hat{u}\text{ in }W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Using that

\langle-\Delta_{p}u_{k},u_{k}-\hat{u}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{k})(u_{k}-\hat{u})\ dx,

(\mathrm{H.2})
, (
2.5
), (
2.6
),
u_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
, we find

\displaystyle|f(x,u_{k})(u_{k}-\hat{u})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(1+u_{k}^{\alpha})|u_{k}-\hat{u}|\leq M(1+(Cc)^{-\alpha}d(x)^{\alpha})|u_{k}-\hat{u}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2M(1+(Cc)^{-\alpha}d(x)^{\alpha})\left\|\overline{u}\right\|_{\infty}\text{ \ a.e. in }\Omega.

In view of
[
10
, Lemma, page 726]
, we have
d(x)^{\alpha}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies that

\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\langle-\Delta_{p}u_{k},u_{k}-\hat{u}\rangle=0,

which allows us to invoke the
S_{+}
-property of
-\Delta_{p}
on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
that gives
u_{k}\longrightarrow\hat{u}
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. We conclude that
\hat{u}=\inf\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{S}
. Zorn’s Lemma can be applied which provides a minimal element
u_{+}^{\ast}
of
\mathcal{S}
. Since
\mathcal{S}\subset[\underline{u},\overline{u}]
, by
(\mathrm{H.2})
, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we infer from the regularity result in
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
that
u_{+}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\tau\in(0,1)
.

We claim that
u_{+}^{\ast}
is the smallest solution of
(\mathrm{P})
in
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, if
u\in\mathcal{S}
, there is
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}
with
\tilde{u}\leq u_{+}^{\ast}
and
\tilde{u}\leq u
because
\mathcal{S}
is downward directed. Recalling that
u_{+}^{\ast}
is a
minimal element of
\mathcal{S}
, this results in
u_{+}^{\ast}=\tilde{u}\leq u
. This proves the claim.

What remains to show is the first inequality in (
2.13
). The second one
follows similarly. By
(\mathrm{H.3})
, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), for each
compact set
\mathrm{K}\subset\Omega,
there is a constant
\sigma=\sigma(\mathrm{K})>0
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{+}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x,u_{+}^{\ast})\geq m(u_{+}^{\ast})^{\beta}\geq m\overline{u}^{\beta}\geq m(Ccd(x))^{\beta}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle C^{-(p-1)}d(x)^{\alpha}+\sigma\geq-\Delta_{p}\underline{u}(x)+\sigma\text{ \ in }\mathrm{K}

provided
C>1
is sufficiently large and
\delta>0
sufficiently small in (
2.4
). By the strong comparison principle
[
1
, Proposition
2.6
]

, we infer that property (
2.13
) holds true. A quite similar argument
shows the corresponding property for a biggest negative solution
u_{-}^{\ast}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Nodal solutions

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Under assumptions
(\mathrm{H.1})
,
(\mathrm{H.2})
and
(\mathrm{H.3})
, problem
(\mathrm{P})
possesses a solution
u_{\ast}\in W_{0}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
u_{\ast}\neq u_{+}
and
u_{\ast}\neq u_{-}
. Moreover,
u_{\ast}
is a nodal (sign-changing)
solution.

We will make use of the Leray-Schauder degree theory. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we introduce the function

(3.1)

\gamma_{\varepsilon}(s):=\varepsilon\text{ }sgn(s),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R},

and corresponding to it the regularized problem

(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u=f(x,u+\gamma_{\varepsilon}(u))&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Our goal is to prove that
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
admits a solution
within
[-\underline{u},\underline{u}]
and then, passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, to get the existence of the desired solution
u_{\ast}
for problem
(\mathrm{P})
.

Theorem
1
provides two opposite constant-sign solutions
u_{\varepsilon,+}
and
u_{\varepsilon,-}
for problem
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
with some
\tau\in(0,1)
satisfying (
2.8
). In particular, the set of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})
to
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
is nonempty.

For any
R>0
, set

\mathcal{A}_{R}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{B}_{R}(0)\,:-\underline{u}\leq u\leq\underline{u}\right\}

and

\mathcal{B}_{R}(0)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega}):\,\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}<R\right\}.

We are going to establish the solvability of problem
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})
.

Theorem 4
.

Under assumptions
(\mathrm{H.1})
,
(\mathrm{H.2})
and
(\mathrm{H.3})
, the regularized problem
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon})

possesses a solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in]u_{-}^{\ast},u_{+}^{\ast}[
for
all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, where
u_{-}^{\ast}
and
u_{+}^{\ast}
are
the extremal constant-sign solutions found in Theorem
2
.

3.0.1.
The topological degree on a ball
\mathcal{B}_{R_{\varepsilon}}(0)

We introduce the following homotopy class of problems

(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,t})\qquad-\Delta_{p}u=\mathrm{F}{{}_{\varepsilon,t}(x},u)\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }u=0\text{ \ on }\partial\Omega,

with

\mathrm{F}{{}_{\varepsilon,t}(x},u)=tf(x,u+\gamma_{\varepsilon}(u))+(1-t)(\lambda_{1,p}(u^{+})^{p-1}+1),

for
t\in[0,1]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, where
s^{+}:=\max\{0,s\}.
It is known that the problem

(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,0})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{1,p}(u^{+})^{p-1}+1\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0\text{ \ on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

does not admit solutions
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
(see
[
12
, Proposition
9.64]
).

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
(\mathrm{H.2})
and
(\mathrm{H.3})
hold. Then each
solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,t})
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfies

(3.2)

\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}<R_{\varepsilon},

for all
t\in(0,1]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, provided the constant
R_{\varepsilon}>0
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

The nonlinear regularity theory implies that
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Due to the fact that
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,0})
has no solution, it suffices to prove that the assertion holds for
every
t\in[\delta,1]
with any
\delta\in(0,1)
. Suppose by contradiction
that for every
n
there exist
t_{n}\in[\delta,1]
and a solution
u_{\varepsilon,n}
of
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,t_{n}})
such that

t_{n}\rightarrow t\in[\delta,1]\text{ \ and \ }\theta_{n}:=\|u_{\varepsilon,n}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}\rightarrow\infty\text{ \ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Setting

v_{\varepsilon,n}:=\frac{u_{\varepsilon,n}}{\theta_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),

problem
(\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon,t_{n}})
results in

(3.3)

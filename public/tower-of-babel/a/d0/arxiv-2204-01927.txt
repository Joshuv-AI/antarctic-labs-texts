Random Double Tensors Integrals
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
In this work, we try to build a theory for random double tensor integrals (DTI). We begin with the definition of DTI and discuss how randomness structure is built upon DTI. Then, the tail bound of the unitarily invariant norm for the random DTI is established and this bound can help us to derive tail bounds of the unitarily invariant norm for various types of two tensors means, e.g., arithmetic mean, geometric mean, harmonic mean, and general mean. By associating DTI with perturbation formula, i.e., a formula to relate the tensor-valued function difference with respect the difference of the function input tensors, the tail bounds of the unitarily invariant norm for the Lipschitz estimate of tensor-valued function with random tensors as arguments are derived for vanilla case and quasi-commutator case, respectively. We also establish the continuity property for random DTI in the sense of convergence in the random tensor mean, and we apply this continuity property to obtain the tail bound of the unitarily invariant norm for the derivative of the tensor-valued function.



Index terms—
Einstein product, double tensor integrals (DTI), random DTI, tail bound, Lipschitz estimate, convergence in the random tensor mean, derivative of tensor-valued function

Tail Bound for Random Tensor Integral Norms

3.1
Unitarily Invariant Tensor Norms

Let us represent the Hermitian eigenvalues of a Hermitian tensor
\mathcal{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
in decreasing order by the vector
\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})=(\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}),\cdots,\lambda_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}(\mathcal{H}))
. We use
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
to represent a set of nonnegative (positive) real numbers. Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
be a unitarily invariant tensor norm, i.e.,
\left\|\mathcal{H}\star_{N}\mathcal{U}\right\|_{\rho}=\left\|\mathcal{U}\star_{N}\mathcal{H}\right\|_{\rho}=\left\|\mathcal{H}\right\|_{\rho}
, where
\mathcal{U}
is any unitary tensor. Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the corresponding gauge function that satisfies H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality so that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{H}\right\|_{\rho}=\left\||\mathcal{H}|\right\|_{\rho}=\rho(\vec{\lambda}(|\mathcal{H}|)),

(12)

where
|\mathcal{H}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^{H}\star_{N}\mathcal{H}}
.

We will provide several popular tensor norm examples which can be treated as special cases of unitarily invariant tensor norm. The first one is Schatten
p
-norm for tensors, denoted as
\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{p}
, is defined as:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(\mathrm{Tr}|\mathcal{X}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},

(13)

where
p\geq 1
. If
p=1
, it is the trace norm.

The second one is
k

\mathrm{Tr}_{k}[\mathcal{X}]
, defined by
[
17
]
. It is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{k}[\mathcal{X}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots i_{k}\leq r}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}

(14)

where
1\leq k\leq r
. If
k=1
,
\mathrm{Tr}_{k}[\mathcal{X}]
is reduced as trace norm.

The third one is Ky Fan like
k
-norm
[
18
]
for tensors. For
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\}
, the Ky Fan
k
-norm
[
18
]
for tensors
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
, denoted as
\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{(k)}
, is defined as:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{(k)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(|\mathcal{X}|).

(15)

If
k=1
, the Ky Fan
k
-norm for tensors is the tensor operator norm, denoted as
\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|

to represent any unitarily invariant tensor norm for the tensor

In the following theorem, we will present the tail bound of unitarily invariant tensor norm for a given tensor integral

Theorem 1

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
be independent random Hermitian tensors with
\mathcal{A}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}\star_{1}\mathcal{U}^{H}_{i}
and
\mathcal{B}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mu_{j}\mathcal{U}_{j}\star_{1}\mathcal{U}^{H}_{j}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\psi}(\mathcal{X})\right\|_{\rho}\geq\theta\right)\leq\frac{\left(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\right)^{2}\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}}{\theta}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\psi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j})\right|\right),

(16)

where
\mathbb{E}
is the expectation.

Proof:

Since we have the following norm estimation for
\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\psi}(\mathcal{X})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\psi}(\mathcal{X})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\psi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j})\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}\right\|_{\rho}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\left|\psi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j})\right|\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle=_{2}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\left|\psi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j})\right|\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho},

where
\leq_{1}
comes from triangle inequality of the unitarily invariant norm and
=_{2}
comes from the definition of the unitarily invariant norm.

Then, we have the following bound for
\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\psi}(\mathcal{X})\right\|_{\rho}\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\psi}(\mathcal{X})\right\|_{\rho}\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\left|\psi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j})\right|\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\geq\theta\right)

(18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left|\psi\left(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}\right)\right|\geq\frac{\theta}{\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\left(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\left(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\right)^{2}}{\theta}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\psi\left(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}\right)\right|\right),

where

Continuity of Random Tensor Integral

In this section, we will establish continuity of DTI. We need the following definition to define the convergence in mean for random tensors.

Definition 1

We say that a sequence of random tensor
\mathcal{X}_{n}
converges in the
r
-th mean towards the random tensor
\mathcal{X}
with respect to the tensor norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
, if we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{n}\right\|^{r}_{\rho}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{exists,}

(83)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|^{r}_{\rho}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{exists,}

(84)

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{n}-\mathcal{X}\right\|^{r}_{\rho}\right)=0.

(85)

We adopt the notatation
\mathcal{X}_{n}\xrightarrow{r}\mathcal{X}
to represent that random tensors
\mathcal{X}_{n}
converges in the
r
-th mean to the random tensor
\mathcal{X}
with respect to the tensor norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
.

Besides random tensor convergence definition, we also need to define triple tensor integral and second-order divide difference.

We define
triple tensor integrals
(TTI) with respect to Hermitian tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
such that
\mathcal{A}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}\star_{1}\mathcal{U}^{H}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
,
\mathcal{B}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mu_{j}\mathcal{V}_{j}\star_{1}\mathcal{V}^{H}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mu_{j}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}
and
\mathcal{C}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\nu_{k}\mathcal{W}_{j}\star_{1}\mathcal{W}^{H}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\nu_{k}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}_{k}}
. Given the function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the TTI associated with tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
and the function
\varphi
, denoted as
T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\varphi}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\varphi}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\varphi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j},\nu_{k})\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}\star_{N}\mathcal{Y}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}_{j}},

(86)

where
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
.

The second-order divide difference for a function
f(x)
, denoted as
f^{[2]}(x,y,z)
, can be defined as

\displaystyle f^{[2]}(x,y,z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{f^{[1]}(y,z)-f^{[1]}(x,y)}{z-x}

(87)

Lemma 5

Given three Hermitian tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
such that
\mathcal{A}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}\star_{1}\mathcal{U}^{H}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
,
\mathcal{B}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mu_{j}\mathcal{V}_{j}\star_{1}\mathcal{V}^{H}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\mu_{j}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}
and
\mathcal{C}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\nu_{k}\mathcal{W}_{j}\star_{1}\mathcal{W}^{H}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\nu_{k}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}_{k}}
and the function
f(x)
with
f^{[2]}(x)
bounded by
\Omega_{f^{[2]}}
, we then have the following norm estimate for
T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\varphi}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\varphi}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\right)^{3}\Omega_{f^{[2]}}\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\cdot\left\|\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\rho}.

(88)

Proof:

Since we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\varphi}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\varphi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j},\nu_{k})\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}\star_{N}\mathcal{Y}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}_{j}}\right\|_{\rho}

(89)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\left|\varphi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j},\nu_{k})\right|\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}\star_{N}\mathcal{Y}\star_{N}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}_{j}}\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq_{2}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}}\left|\varphi(\lambda_{i},\mu_{j},\nu_{k})\right|\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\cdot\left\|\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{N}\right)^{3}\Omega_{f^{[2]}}\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\rho}\cdot\left\|\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\rho}

where

7
Conclusions

We first define what is the random DTI and derive the tail bound of the unitarily invariant norm for a random DTI. This bound assists us to establish tail bounds of the unitarily invariant norm for various types of dual tensor means, e.g., arithmetic mean, geometric mean, harmonic mean, and general mean. The random DTI is also being applied to build the random Lipschitz estimate in contexts of random tensors. Finally, we derive the continuity property for random DTI in the sense of convergence in the random tensor mean, and apply this fact to obtain the tail bound of the unitarily invariant norm for the derivative of the tensor-valued function. Possible future works will be to extend DTI to multiple tensor integrals.

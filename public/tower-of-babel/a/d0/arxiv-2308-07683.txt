On the log motivic stable homotopy groups
Doosung Park

Abstract
. We compare the log motivic stable homotopy category and the usual motivic stable homotopy category over a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities. As a consequence, we show that the log motivic stable homotopy groups are isomorphic to the usual motivic stable homotopy groups.

Primary 14F42; Secondary 14A21

1.
Introduction

For a perfect field
k
,
author’s joint work
[
2
]
with Binda-Østvær provides a stable
\infty
-category of log effective motives
\mathrm{logDM}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)
,
which is a place for incorporating various non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology like Hodge cohomology into the motivic framework.
For every separated noetherian log smooth fs log scheme
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
,
the log motive
M(X)\in\mathrm{logDM}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)
can be associated.
Using this,
one can also define log motivic cohomology of
X
.

A natural question in such a non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant theory of motives is whether
\mathrm{logDM}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)
contains Voevodsky’s stable
\infty
-category of motives
\mathrm{DM}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)

[
7
]
as a full subcategory or not.
An affirmative answer implies that the theory of log motives is an extension of the usual theory of motives.
This is answered in
[
2
, Theorem 8.2.16]
under the assumption of resolution of singularities on
k
.
With this assumption,
a consequence is that log motivic cohomology of
Y\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
is isomorphic to the usual motivic cohomology of
Y-\partial Y\in\mathrm{Sm}/k
,
where
Y-\partial Y
denotes the largest open subscheme of
Y
with the trivial log structure.
The proof of this result relies on Voevodsky’s theory of homotopy invariant sheaves with transfers
[
7
, §21-24]
.

In author’s joint work
[
1
]
with Binda-Østvær,
the log motivic stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
is introduced.
Again,
a natural question is whether
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
contains the stable motivic
\infty
-category
\mathrm{SH}(k)
of Morel-Voevodsky
[
8
]
as a full subcategory or not.
We show the following result using a different approach that does not rely on the theory of sheaves with transfers:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
There exist adjoint functors

\omega_{\sharp}:\mathrm{logSH}(k)\rightleftarrows\mathrm{SH}(k):\omega^{*}

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\omega_{\sharp}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}Y_{+})\simeq\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}(Y-\partial Y)_{+}
for
Y\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
.

(2)

\omega^{*}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}(Y-\partial Y)_{+})\simeq\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}Y_{+}
for proper
Y\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
.

(3)

\omega_{\sharp}
preserves colimits and is monoidal.

(4)

\omega^{*}
preserves colimits and is monoidal and fully faithful.

(5)

The essential image of
\omega^{*}
is the full subcategory spanned by
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-local objects of
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
.

Our convention for adjoint functors between categories or
\infty
-categories
F:\mathcal{C}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}:G
is that
F
is a left adjoint of
G
.

The monoidality of
\omega^{*}
immediately implies the following results on the log motivic stable homotopy groups:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
Then for all integers
p
and
q
,
the log motivic stable homotopy group

\pi_{p,q}^{\log}(k):=\pi_{0}(\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}(k)}(\Sigma^{p,q}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}))

is isomorphic to the motivic stable homotopy group

\pi_{p,q}(k):=\pi_{0}(\Hom_{\mathrm{SH}(k)}(\Sigma^{p,q}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})),

where
\mathbf{1}
denotes the units in
\mathrm{SH}(k)
and
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 6.1.4]
,
the logarithmic
K
-theory
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectrum
\mathbf{logKGL}
admits a geometric model using Grassmannians.
We provide an alternative streamlined proof of this in Corollary
4.5

as an application of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
A right adjoint of
\omega

For an fs log scheme
Y
,
let
\underline{Y}
be the underlying scheme of
Y
.
Let
Y-\partial Y
be the largest open subscheme of
Y
with the trivial log structure,
which is possible due to
[
9
, Proposition III.1.2.8(2)]
.
If
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/S
,
then we can regard the closed complement
\partial Y
as a strict normal crossing divisor on
\underline{Y}
.
In this case,
we often use the notation
Y=(\underline{Y},\partial Y)
.

A morphism
f\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
in
\mathrm{lSm}/k
is an
admissible blow-up
if
f
is proper (i.e.,
\underline{f}
is proper) and the induced morphism of schemes
Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}\to Y_{2}-\partial Y_{2}
is an isomorphism.
Let
\mathrm{Adm}
denote the class of admissible blow-ups in
\mathrm{lSm}/k
,
which admits calculus of right fractions in
\mathrm{lSm}/k
by
[
2
, Proposition 7.6.6]
.

We have the functor

\omega\colon\mathrm{lSm}/k\to\mathrm{Sm}/k

given by
\omega(X):=X-\partial X
for
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
.
Since
\omega
is invariant under admissible blow-ups,
we have the induced functor

\omega\colon\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]\to\mathrm{Sm}/k.

For
X\in\mathrm{Sm}/k
,
there exists proper
X^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Sm}/k
containing
X
as an open subscheme whose complement is a strict normal crossing divisor by resolution of singularities.
If
Y
is the fs log scheme over
k
with the underlying scheme
X^{\prime}
and the compactifying log structure
[
9
, Definition III.1.6.1]
associated with the open immersion
X\to X^{\prime}
,
then
Y
is proper,
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/k
, and
Y-\partial Y\simeq X
.
We will use this construction frequently.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
and
Y_{2}
is proper,
then there is a natural isomorphism

\alpha\colon\Hom_{\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Hom_{\mathrm{Sm}/k}(Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1},Y_{2}-\partial Y_{2})

sending a morphism
f\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
to
\omega(f)
.

Proof.

Let
g\colon Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}\to Y_{2}-\partial Y_{2}
be a morphism in
\mathrm{Sm}/k
,
and let
u_{1}\colon Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}\to\underline{Y_{1}}
and
u_{2}\colon Y_{2}-\partial Y_{2}\to\underline{Y_{2}}
be the obvious open immersions of schemes.
Consider the morphism
(u_{1},u_{2}g)\colon Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}\to\underline{Y_{1}}\times\underline{Y_{2}}
,
and let
\underline{Z}
be the closure of its image.
By resolution of singularities,
there exists
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/k
with a proper birational morphism of schemes
\underline{Y}\to\underline{Z}
such that
Y-\partial Y\simeq Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}
.
Since
\underline{Y_{2}}
is proper,

\underline{Z}
is proper over
\underline{Y_{1}}
.
Together with
[
9
, Proposition III.1.6.2]
,
we have an admissible blow-up
Y\to Y_{1}
and a morphism
Y\to Y_{2}
.
Use these two to form a morphism
f\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
in
\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]
such that
\alpha(f)=g
.
This shows that
\alpha
is surjective.

Let
f,f^{\prime}\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be two morphisms in
\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]
.
After replacing
Y_{1}
by its admissible blow-up,
we may assume that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are indeed morphisms in
\mathrm{lSm}/k
.
If
f
and
g
agree on
Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}
,
then we have
f=g
since
Y_{1}-\partial Y_{1}
is dense in
Y_{1}
.
This shows that
\alpha
is injective.
∎

Using resolution of singularities and Proposition
2.1
,
we see that the restriction of
w\colon\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]\to\mathrm{Sm}/k
to proper fs log schemes in
\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]
is an equivalence of categories.
By considering its quasi-inverse,
we obtain a functor

c\colon\mathrm{Sm}/k\to\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]

such that

c(Y-\partial Y)\simeq Y

for proper
Y\in\mathrm{lSm}/k
.
Using Proposition
2.1
again,
we see that the functors

\omega:\mathrm{lSm}/k[\mathrm{Adm}^{-1}]\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Sm}/k:c

form an adjoint pair.

Compactifications of Nisnevich distinguished squares

Recall that
S
is a finite dimensional noetherian scheme.
We often deal with the case when
S=\Spec(k)
.

Let
\mathscr{T}(S)
be a stable
\infty
-category.
We refer to
[
2

\mathrm{lSm}/S
.
The associated topology is the strict Nisnevich topology,
and
sNis

We set
\square:=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)

We assume that there exists a functor

M\colon\mathrm{N}(\mathrm{lSm}/S)\to\mathscr{T}(S)

satisfying the following conditions:

•

(Strict Nisnevich descent)
For every strict Nisnevich distinguished square
Q
in
\mathrm{lSm}/S
,
the square
M(Q)
in
\mathscr{T}(S)
is cocartesian.

•

(Dividing descent)
For every dividing cover (i.e., a surjective proper log étale monomorphism)
f
in
\mathrm{lSm}/S

M(f)
is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{T}(S)
.

•

(
\square
-invariance)
For
Y\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
,
the projection
p\colon Y\times\square\to Y
induces the isomorphism
M(p)\colon M(Y\times\square)\to M(Y)
.

Suppose that we have
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/S
and
W\in\mathrm{lSch}/S
such that
\underline{W}
and
\underline{W}+\partial Y
are strict normal crossing divisors on
\underline{Y}
.
In this setting,
we use the convenient notation

(Y,W):=(\underline{Y},\underline{W}+\partial X),

which adds the additional boundary
W
to
Y
.

We will use the notion of an
n
-cube
in an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
,
see
[
1
, §A.6]
for the details.
A
0
-cube is just an object of
\mathcal{C}
.
For every integer
n\geq 0
,
an
(n+1)
-cube
Q
can be constructed inductively as a morphism of
n
-cubes
Q_{0}\to Q_{1}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
is stable,
then the
total cofiber
\mathrm{tcofib}(Q)
of
Q
has an inductive formula

\mathrm{tcofib}(Q)\simeq\mathrm{cofib}(\mathrm{tcofib}(Q_{0})\to\mathrm{tcofib}(Q_{1})).

The total cofiber of a
0
-cube is its underlying object.
We say that
Q
is
cocartesian
if
\mathrm{tcofib}(Q)\simeq 0
.

Observe that we have

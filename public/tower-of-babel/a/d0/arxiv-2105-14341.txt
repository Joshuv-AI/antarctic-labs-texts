Distribution dependent SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions
Xiliang Fan, Xing Huang, Yongqiang Suo, Chenggui Yuan

Abstract
In this paper we study a class of distribution dependent stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motions with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) H\in(1/2,1) . We prove the well-posedness of this type equations, and then establish a general result on the Bismut formula for the Lions derivative by using Malliavin calculus. As applications, we provide the Bismut formulas of this kind for both non-degenerate and degenerate cases, and obtain the estimates of the Lions derivative and the total variation distance between the laws of two solutions.



AMS Subject Classification: 60H10, 60G22

Keywords: Distribution dependent SDE; fractional Brownian motion; Bismut type formula; Lions derivative; Wasserstein distance

1
Introduction

Distribution dependent stochastic differential equations (SDEs), also called McKean-Vlasov or mean-field SDEs,
were initiated by Kac
[
23
]
in the study of the Boltzman equation and the stochastic toy model.
This type SDEs are equations whose coefficients depend on the distribution of the solution,
and their solutions are often called nonlinear diffusion processes whose transition functions depend on both the current state and distribution of processes.
These distributions dependent SDEs can provide a probabilistic representation to the solutions of a class of nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs), in which a typical example is propagation of chaos phenomenon (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
35
]
and references therein).
Hence, the study of distribution dependent SDEs have received increasing attentions, among which we only mention, for examples,
the works in
[
11
,
12
,
19
]
for large scale social interactions within the memory of mean-field games,

[
9
,
14
,
24
]
for value functions and related PDEs,

[
20
,
21
,
33
,
37
]
for (shift) Harnack type inequalities and gradient estimates, and the references therein.

On the other hand, the Bismut formula, also known as Bismut-Elworthy-Li formula,
was initiated in
[
7
]
by Malliavin calculus and then developed in
[
16
]
by using martingale method,
which is a very effective tool in the analysis of distributional regularity for various stochastic models.
Afterwards, formula of this kind and (shift) Harnack type inequalities for SDEs and SPDEs were obtained via (backward) coupling argument
(see, e.g., the monograph
[
36
]
and references therein).
Recently, in
[
30
]
the Bismut formula for the Lions derivative (
L
-derivative) was established for distribution dependent SDEs with distribution-free noise by using Malliavin calculus, and then applied to the study of estimates on the
L
-derivative and the total variation distance between distributions of solutions with different initial data.
Here, we mention that in
[
5
]
the Bismut formula for initial points was also derived for distribution dependent SDEs,
which can be regarded as a special case of the Bismut formula for the
L
-derivative stated in
[
30
]
.
By introducing the intrinsic and Lions derivatives for probability measures on Banach spaces,
the Bismut formula for the
L
-derivative was given for distribution-path dependent SDEs with distribution-free noise in
[
6
]
.
Adopting method of heat kernel expansion and the technique of freezing distribution,
the Bismut formula as well as estimate of the
L
-derivative for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with distribution dependent noise was obtained in
[
22
]
.

In this paper, we are concerned with the following class of distribution dependent SDEs in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
on the time interval
[0,T]
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t,\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{H},\ \ X_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P}),

where
p\geq 1,\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
denotes the law of
X_{t},B^{H}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/2,1)
,
and the stochastic integral can be regarded as the Wiener integral (see Remark
3.1
below).
Precise assumptions on the coefficients
b:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be specified in later sections,
where
\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
\theta
-th moment.
Let us recall that
B^{H}=(B^{H,1},\cdots,B^{H,d})
with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
is a center Gaussian process with the covariance function
\mathbb{E}(B^{H,i}_{t}B^{H,j}_{s})=R_{H}(t,s)\delta_{i,j}
, where

\displaystyle R_{H}(t,s)=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right),\ \ t,s\in[0,T].

This implies that the relation
\mathbb{E}(|B_{t}^{H,i}-B_{s}^{H,i}|^{q})=C_{q}|t-s|^{qH}
holds for every
q\geq 1
and
i=1,\cdots,d
.
Consequently,
B^{H}
is
(H-\varepsilon)
-order Hölder continuous a.s. for any
\varepsilon\in(0,H)
and is an
H
-self similar process.
This, together with the fact that
B^{1/2}
is a standard Brownian motion, converts fractional Brownian motion into a natural generalization of Brownian motion and leads to many applications in modelling physical phenomena and finance behaviours.

The aim of this paper is to establish a Bismut type
L
-derivative formula for the equation (
1.1
).
We first prove the well-posedness of (
1.1
) under
\mathbb{W}_{\theta}
-Lipschitz conditions with respect to the measure variable,
in which the solution
X
belongs to the space
X\in\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
that generalizes and improves the corresponding one in the existing literature (see Theorem
3.1
and Remark
3.2
below).
Then, with the help of partial derivatives with respect to the initial value and the Malliavin derivative of solutions to (
1.1
) obtained under stronger assumptions than that for well-posedness, we are able to establish a general result concerning the Bismut type
L
-derivative formula for (
1.1
) (see Theorem
4.4
below).
As applications, we provide the Bismut type
L
-derivative formulas for both non-degenerate and degenerate situations.
In addition, to illustrate the power of the Bismut type
L
-derivative formulas, in the case of non-degenerate we obtain the estimates of the
L
-derivative and the total variation distance for the difference between the laws of the solutions
\mathscr{L}_{X_{T}^{\mu}}
and
\mathscr{L}_{X_{T}^{\nu}}
with different initial distributions
\mu
and
\nu
(see Remark
4.3
(ii) below).

The plan of the paper is as follows:
Section 2 is devoted to recalling some useful facts on fractional calculus, fractional Brownian motion and the
L
-derivative.
In Section 3, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a solution to distribution dependent SDE driven by fractional Brownian motion.
In Section 4, we state and prove our main results concerning Bismut type formula for the
L
-derivative of distribution dependent SDE driven by fractional Brownian motion, which are then applied to both non-degenerate and degenerate cases.

2
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to giving some basic elements of fractional calculus involving fractional integral and derivative,
Wiener space associated to fractional Brownian motion and the Lions derivative.

2.1
Fractional integral and derivative

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
.
For
f\in L^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
and
\alpha>0
, the left-sided (respectively right-sided) fractional Riemann-Liouville integral of
f
of order
\alpha

on
[a,b]
is defined as

\displaystyle I_{a+}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{a}^{x}\frac{f(y)}{(x-y)^{1-\alpha}}\text{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mbox{respectively}\ \ I_{b-}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{(-1)^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{x}^{b}\frac{f(y)}{(y-x)^{1-\alpha}}\text{\rm{d}}y\right),

where
x\in(a,b)
a.e.,
(-1)^{-\alpha}=\text{\rm{e}}^{-i\alpha\pi}
and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function.
In particular, when
\alpha=n\in\mathbb{N}
, they are consistent with the usual
n
-order iterated integrals.

Fractional differentiation may be given as an inverse operation.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p\geq 1
.
If
f\in I_{a+}^{\alpha}(L^{p}([a,b],\mathbb{R}))
(respectively
I_{b-}^{\alpha}(L^{p}([a,b],\mathbb{R})))
, then the function
g
satisfying
f=I_{a+}^{\alpha}g
(respectively
f=I_{b-}^{\alpha}g
) is unique in
L^{p}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
and it coincides with the left-sided (respectively right-sided) Riemann-Liouville derivative
of
f
of order
\alpha
shown by

\displaystyle D_{a+}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}\int_{a}^{x}\frac{f(y)}{(x-y)^{\alpha}}\text{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mbox{respectively}\ D_{b-}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{(-1)^{1+\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}\int_{x}^{b}\frac{f(y)}{(y-x)^{\alpha}}\text{\rm{d}}y\right).

The corresponding Weyl representation is of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle D_{a+}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(x)}{(x-a)^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{a}^{x}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{(x-y)^{\alpha+1}}\text{\rm{d}}y\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\mbox{respectively}\ \ D_{b-}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(x)}{(b-x)^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{x}^{b}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{(y-x)^{\alpha+1}}\text{\rm{d}}y\right)\right),

where the convergence of the integrals at the singularity
y=x
holds pointwise for almost all
x
if
p=1
and in the
L^{p}
sense if
p>1
.
For further details, we refer the reader to
[
32
]
.

2.2
Wiener space associated to fractional Brownian motion

For fixed
H\in(1/2,1)
, let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be the canonical probability space associated with fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
.
More precisely,

\Omega
is the Banach space
C_{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
of continuous functions vanishing at
0
equipped with the supremum norm,

\mathscr{F}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra and
\mathbb{P}
is the unique probability measure on
\Omega
such that the canonical process
\{B^{H}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
.
We assume that there is a sufficiently rich sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{0}\subset\mathscr{F}
independent of
B^{H}
such that
for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a random variable
X\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
with distribution
\mu
.
Let
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
be the filtration generated by
B^{H}
, completed and augmented by
\mathscr{F}_{0}
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be the set of step functions on
[0,T]
and
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert space defined as the closure of

\mathscr{E}
with respect to the scalar product

\displaystyle\left\langle(\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{1}]},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{d}]}),(\mathbb{I}_{[0,s_{1}]},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbb{I}_{[0,s_{d}]})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}R_{H}(t_{i},s_{i}).

The mapping
(\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{1}]},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbb{I}_{[0,t_{d}]})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{t_{i}}^{H,i}
can be extended to an isometry between
\mathcal{H}
(also called the reproducing kernel Hilbert space) and the Gaussian space
\mathcal{H}_{1}
associated with
B^{H}
.
Denote this isometry by
\psi\mapsto B^{H}(\psi)
.
On the other hand, it follows from
[
15
]
that
R_{H}(t,s)
has the following integral representation

\displaystyle R_{H}(t,s)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}K_{H}(t,r)K_{H}(s,r)\text{\rm{d}}r,

where
K_{H}
is a square integrable kernel given by

\displaystyle K_{H}(t,s)=\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}F\left(H-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-H,H+\frac{1}{2},1-\frac{t}{s}\right),

in which
F(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function (for details see
[
15
]
or
[
28
]
).

Now, define the linear operator
K_{H}^{*}:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows

\displaystyle(K_{H}^{*}\psi)(s)=K_{H}(T,s)\psi(s)+\int_{s}^{T}(\psi(r)-\psi(s))\frac{\partial K_{H}}{\partial r}(r,s)\text{\rm{d}}r.

According to
[
3
]
,
the relation
\langle K_{H}^{*}\psi,K_{H}^{*}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
holds for all
\psi,\phi\in\mathscr{E}
,
and then by the bounded linear transform theorem,
K_{H}^{*}
can be extended to an isometry between
\mathcal{H}
and
L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consequently, by
[
3
]
again, the process
\{W_{t}=B^{H}((K_{H}^{*})^{-1}{\mathbb{I}}_{[0,t]}),t\in[0,T]\}
is a Wiener process,
and
B^{H}
has the following Volterra-type representation

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{t}^{H}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\text{\rm{d}}W_{s},\ \ t\in[0,T].

Besides, we define the operator
K_{H}:L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow I_{0+}^{H+1/2}(L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by

\displaystyle(K_{H}f)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)f(s)\text{\rm{d}}s.

Due to
[
15
]
, we know that it is an isomorphism and for each
f\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle(K_{H}f)(s)=I_{0+}^{1}s^{H-1/2}I_{0+}^{H-1/2}s^{1/2-H}f.

Then for every
h\in I_{0+}^{H+1/2}(L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, the inverse operator
K_{H}^{-1}
is of the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle(K_{H}^{-1}h)(s)=s^{H-1/2}D_{0+}^{H-1/2}s^{1/2-H}h^{\prime}.

We remark that the injection
R_{H}=K_{H}\circ K_{H}^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Omega
embeds
\mathcal{H}
densely into
\Omega
and
for every
\psi\in\Omega^{*}\subset\mathcal{H}
there holds

\mathbb{E}\text{\rm{e}}^{i\langle B^{H},\psi\rangle}=\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}})
.
Consequently,
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{P})
is an abstract Wiener space in the sense of Gross.

Finally, we give a brief account on the Malliavin calculus for fractional Brownian motion.
Denote
\mathcal{S}
by the set of smooth and cylindrical random variables of the form

\displaystyle F=f(B^{H}(\phi_{1}),\cdot\cdot\cdot,B^{H}(\phi_{n})),

where
n\geq 1,f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is the collection of
f
and all its partial derivatives are bounded,

\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{H},1\leq i\leq n
.
The Malliavin derivative of
F
, denoted by
\mathbb{D}F
, is defined as the
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(B^{H}(\phi_{1}),\cdot\cdot\cdot,B^{H}(\phi_{n}))\phi_{i}.

For any
p\geq 1
, we define the Sobolev space
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
as the completion of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{1,p}^{p}=\mathbb{E}|F|^{p}+\mathbb{E}\|\mathbb{D}F\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Meanwhile, we will denote by
\delta
and
\mathrm{Dom}\delta
the dual operator of
\mathbb{D}
and its domain, respectively.
Let us finish this part by giving a transfer principle that connects the derivative and divergence operators of both processes
B^{H}
and
W
that are needed later on.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
29
, Proposition 5.2.1]

For any
F\in\mathbb{D}_{W}^{1,2}=\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
,

\displaystyle K_{H}^{*}\mathbb{D}F=\mathbb{D}^{W}F,

where
\mathbb{D}^{W}
denotes the derivative operator with respect to the underlying Wiener process
W
appearing in (
2.2
), and
\mathbb{D}_{W}^{1,2}
the corresponding Sobolev space.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
29
, Proposition 5.2.2]

\mathrm{Dom}\delta=(K_{H}^{*})^{-1}(\mathrm{Dom}\delta_{W})
,
and for any
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable
u
in
\mathrm{Dom}\delta
we have
\delta(u)=\delta_{W}(K_{H}^{*}u)
,
where
\delta_{W}
denotes the divergence operator with respect to the underlying Wiener process
W
appearing in (
2.2
).

Remark 2.1
.

The above proposition, together with
[
29
, Proposition 1.3.11]
, yields that
if
K_{H}^{*}u\in L_{a}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
(the closed subspace of
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
formed by the adapted processes),
then
u\in\mathrm{Dom}\delta
.

2.3
The Lions derivative

For
\theta\in[1,\infty)
, let
\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
\theta
-th moment.
We define the
L^{\theta}
-Wasserstein distance on
\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{\theta}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}},\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
.
It is well-known that
(\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathbb{W}_{\theta})
is a Polish space.
Throughout this paper, we use
|\cdot|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the Euclidean norm and inner product, respectively,
and for a matrix, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
either the operator norm or the Euclidean norm.

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}
denotes for the norm of the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
and for a random variable
X
,
\mathscr{L}_{X}
denotes its distribution.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

(1)

f
is called
L
-differentiable at
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if the functional

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)\ni\phi\mapsto f(\mu\circ(\mathrm{Id}+\phi)^{-1}))

is Fréchet differentiable at
0\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
, i.e., there exists a unique
\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(\mu\circ(\mathrm{Id}+\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)-\mu(\langle\gamma,\phi\rangle)}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}}=0.

In this case,
\gamma
is called the
L
-derivative of
f
at
\mu
and denoted by
D^{L}f(\mu)
.

(2)

f
is called
L
-differentiable on
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if the
L
-derivative
D^{L}f(\mu)
exists for all
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If, moreover, for every
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a
\mu
-version
D^{L}f(\mu)(\cdot)
such that
D^{L}f(\mu)(x)
is jointly continuous in
(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
f\in C^{(1,0)}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

(3)

g
is called differentiable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if for any
(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

g(\cdot,\mu)
is differentiable and
g(x,\cdot)
is
L
-differentiable.
If, moreover,
\nabla g(\cdot,\mu)(x)
and
D^{L}g(x,\cdot)(\mu)(y)
are jointly continuous in
(x,y,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we denote
g\in C^{1,(1,0)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

As mentioned in
[
31
, Section 2]
, the above definition of
L
-derivative coincides with the Wasserstein derivative,
which was introduced by P.-L. Lions using probability spaces (
[
10
]
).
Besides, it is easy to see that if
f
is
L
-differentiable at
\mu
, then for any
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
there holds

\displaystyle D^{L}_{\phi}f(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{f(\mu\circ(\mathrm{Id}+\varepsilon\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)}{\varepsilon}=\mu(\langle D^{L}f(\mu),\phi\rangle),

in which
D^{L}_{\phi}f(\mu)
is called the directional
L
-derivative of
f
along
\phi
initiated in
[
2
]
.
When
D^{L}_{\cdot}f(\mu):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear functional,

\phi\mapsto f(\mu\circ(\mathrm{Id}+\phi)^{-1}))
is Gâteaux differentiable at
0
.
In this case, we say that
f
is weakly
L
-differentiable at
\mu
(also called intrinsically differentiable at
\mu
, see
[
6
, Definition 2.1]
or
[
22
, Definition 1.1]
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{L}f(\mu)\|:=\|D^{L}f(\mu)(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\leq 1}|D^{L}_{\phi}f(\mu)|.

For a vector-valued function
f=(f_{i})
or a matrix-valued function
f=(f_{ij})
with
L
-differentiable components, we simply write

\displaystyle D^{L}f(\mu)=(D^{L}f_{i}(\mu))\ \ \mathrm{or}\ \ D^{L}f(\mu)=(D^{L}f_{ij}(\mu)).

Besides, we have the following useful formula for the
L
-derivative, which is due to
[
10
, Theorem 6.5]
and
[
30
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be an atomless probability space and
(X,Y)\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow R^{d},\mathbb{P})
.
If
f\in C^{1,0}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{f(\mathscr{L}_{X+\varepsilon Y})-f(\mathscr{L}_{X})}{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{E}\langle D^{L}f(\mathscr{L}_{X})(X),Y\rangle.

3
Well-posedness of distribution dependent SDE by fractional noise

In this section, we consider the following distribution dependent SDE driven by fractional Brownian motion:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t,\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{H},\ \ X_{0}=\xi,

where the coefficients
b:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
with
p\geq\theta(\geq 1)
.
Now, we introduce the hypotheses under which we will be able to prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
3.1
).

(H)

There exists a non-decreasing function
K(t)
such that for any
t\in[0,T],x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle|b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,y,\nu)|\leq K(t)(|x-y|+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)),\ \ \|\sigma(t,\mu)-\sigma(t,\nu)\|\leq K(t)\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu),

and

\displaystyle|b(t,0,\delta_{0})|+\|\sigma(t,\delta_{0})\|\leq K(t).

For any
p\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
be the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued, continuous
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted processes
\psi
on
[0,T]
satisfying

\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}}:=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\psi_{t}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty,

and let the letter
C
with or without indices denote generic constants, whose values may change from line to line.

Definition 3.1
.

A stochastic process
X=(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a solution of (
3.1
),
if
X\in\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

X_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H},\ \ t\in[0,T].

Remark 3.1
.

Observe that
\sigma(\cdot,\mathscr{L}_{X_{\cdot}})
is a deterministic function,
then
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}
is regarded as a Wiener integral with respect to fractional Brownian motion.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that (H) holds and
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
with
p\geq\theta
and
p>1/H
.
Then the equation (
3.1
) has a unique solution
X\in\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
.

Before proving the theorem, we first present the following Hardy-Littlewood inequality (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Theroem 1, Page 119]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1<\widetilde{p}<\widetilde{q}<\infty
and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}-\alpha
.
If
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belongs to
L^{\widetilde{p}}(0,\infty)
, then
I^{\alpha}_{0+}f(x)
converges absolutely for almost every
x
, and moreover

\|I^{\alpha}_{0+}f\|_{L^{\widetilde{q}}(0,\infty)}\leq C_{\widetilde{p},\widetilde{q}}\|f\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}(0,\infty)}

holds for some positive constant
C_{\widetilde{p},\widetilde{q}}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Define recursively
(X^{n})_{n\geq 1}
as follows:

X^{0}_{t}=\xi,\ t\in[0,T]
and for each
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X^{n-1}_{s},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H},\ \ t\in[0,T].

The rest of the proof will be divided into three steps.

Step 1. Claim: For any
p\geq\theta
and
p>1/H
, if
\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}\Big)<\infty
, then there holds
\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n+1}_{t}|^{p}\Big)<\infty
.
Owing to the Hölder inequality and (H), we have for any
p\geq\theta
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n+1}_{t}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p}+3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X^{n}_{s},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}s\right|^{p}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p}+(3T)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}K^{p}(s)(1+|X_{s}^{n}|+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\delta_{0}))^{p}\text{\rm{d}}s

(3.5)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p}+3^{2(p-1)}(TK(T))^{p}\left(1+2\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right).

Next, we shall provide an estimate for the last term of the right-hand side of (
3.2
),
whose argument is partially borrowed from
[
4
, Theorem 4]
.

We take
\lambda
satisfying
1-H<\lambda<1-1/p
because
pH>1
.
Using the fact that
\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\lambda}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\text{\rm{d}}r=C(\lambda)
, the stochastic Fubini theorem and the Hölder inequality, we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=C(\lambda)^{-p}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\lambda}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\text{\rm{d}}r\right)\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=C(\lambda)^{-p}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\lambda}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right)\text{\rm{d}}r\right|^{p}\right)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\lambda)^{-p}}{(p-1-\lambda p)^{p-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}t^{p-1-\lambda p}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\lambda)^{-p}}{(p-1-\lambda p)^{p-1}}T^{p-1-\lambda p}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r,

where we use the condition
\lambda<1-1/p
in the first inequality.

Notice that for each
r\in[0,T],\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}
is a centered Gaussian random variable.
Then by the Kahane-Khintchine formula, we obtain that there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\lambda-1}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{r}(r-u)^{\lambda-1}\|\sigma(u,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{u}})\|(r-v)^{\lambda-1}\|\sigma(v,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{v}})\||u-v|^{2H-2}\text{\rm{d}}u\text{\rm{d}}v\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,H}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{H}}\|\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\|^{\frac{1}{H}}\text{\rm{d}}s\right)^{pH},

where the last inequality is due to the argument in
[
27
, Theroem 1.1, Page 201]
.

Substituting (
3.13
) into (
3.8
) and using the condition
1-H<\lambda
and Lemma
3.2
with
\widetilde{q}=pH
and
\alpha=1-\frac{1-\lambda}{H}

(imply
\widetilde{p}=\frac{pH}{p(\lambda+H-1)+1}
), we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,p,H}T^{p-1-\lambda p}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{H}}\|\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\|^{\frac{1}{H}}\text{\rm{d}}s\right)^{pH}\text{\rm{d}}r

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,p,H}T^{p-1-\lambda p}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{r}})\|^{\frac{p}{p(\lambda+H-1)+1}}\text{\rm{d}}r\right)^{p(\lambda+H-1)+1}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,p,H}T^{pH-1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}s,

where we use the Hölder inequality in the last inequality, and remark that
C_{\lambda,p,H}
above may depend only on
p
and
H
by choosing proper
\lambda
.

Observe that, by (H) and
p\geq\theta
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}K^{p}(s)\left(1+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\delta_{0})\right)^{p}\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}K^{p}(T)T\left(1+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}\bigg)\right).

Then, plugging this into (
3.17
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{s}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)\leq C_{p,H}K^{p}(T)T^{pH}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}\bigg)\right).

Combining this with (
3.2
) and the assumption that
\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{n}_{t}|^{p})<\infty
yields the desired claim.

Step 2. Existence.

To this end, we shall prove the convergence of
X^{n}
in
\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
with any
p\geq\theta
.
For any
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{n}_{s}-X^{n-1}_{s}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left(b(r,X^{n-1}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}})-b(r,X^{n-2}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\right)\text{\rm{d}}r\right|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left(\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}})-\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\right)\text{\rm{d}}B_{r}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle=:2^{p-1}I_{1}(t)+2^{p-1}I_{2}(t).

For the term
I_{1}(t)
, from (H) and
p\geq\theta
we obtain

(3.22)

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq t^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{s}\left|b(r,X^{n-1}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}})-b(r,X^{n-2}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\right|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r\right)

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leq t^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{s}\left[K(r)\left(|X^{n-1}_{r}-X^{n-2}_{r}|+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\right)\right]^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r\right)

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}K^{p}(t)t^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{s}\left(|X^{n-1}_{r}-X^{n-2}_{r}|^{p}+\mathbb{E}|X^{n-1}_{r}-X^{n-2}_{r}|^{p}\right)\text{\rm{d}}r\right)

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}K^{p}(t)t^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X^{n-1}_{u}-X^{n-2}_{u}|^{p}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r.

As for the term
I_{2}(t)
, owing to
p>1/H
and
p\geq\theta
, (
3.17
) and (H) we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left(\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}})-\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\right)\text{\rm{d}}B_{r}^{H}\right|^{p}\right)

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,H}t^{pH-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}})-\sigma(r,\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})\|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,H}t^{pH-1}\int_{0}^{t}K^{p}(r)\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-1}_{r}},\mathscr{L}_{X^{n-2}_{r}})^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,H}K^{p}(t)t^{pH-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X^{n-1}_{r}-X^{n-2}_{r}|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}r

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,H}K^{p}(t)t^{pH-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X^{n-1}_{u}-X^{n-2}_{u}|^{p}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r.

Plugging (
3.22
) and (
3.26
) into (
3
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{n}_{s}-X^{n-1}_{s}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}K^{p}(t)(2^{p}t^{p-1}+C_{p,H}t^{pH-1})\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X^{n-1}_{u}-X^{n-2}_{u}|^{p}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T,H}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X^{n-1}_{u}-X^{n-2}_{u}|^{p}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r

with
C_{p,T,H}:=2^{p-1}K^{p}(T)(2^{p}T^{p-1}+C_{p,H}T^{pH-1})
.

Hence, by the iteration we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{n}_{s}-X^{n-1}_{s}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C_{1}C_{p,T,H}^{n}\frac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},

where
C_{1}:=\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{1}_{t}-\xi|^{p})<\infty
due to Step 1.

Consequently,
(X^{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{p}([0,T])
with any
p\geq\theta
and
p>\frac{1}{H}
,
and then the limit, denoted by
X
, is a solution of (
3.1
).

Step 3. Uniqueness.

Let
X
and
Y
be two solutions of (
3.1
).
Along the same lines with Step 2, we derive that as in (
3
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C_{p,T,H}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X_{u}-Y_{u}|^{p}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r,\ \ t\in[0,T].

Then, the Gronwall lemma implies that
X_{t}=Y_{t},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}
-a.s.
The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
, the authors considered the equation (
3.1
) with
d=1
, i.e. one-dimensional case.
Under the
\mathbb{W}_{1}
-Lipschitz conditions and
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
(namely
\theta=1
and
p=2
in our result Theorem
3.1
above), they proved the existence and uniqueness of solution
X\in\mathcal{S}^{2}([0,T])
with
\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{2})<\infty
replaced by
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}|^{2}<\infty
.
So in this sense our result extends and improves
[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
.

4
Bismut formulas for the
L
-derivative

In this section, we consider (
3.1
) with distribution independent
\sigma(t)
, that is,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t)\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{H},

where
X_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu
.
The main objective of this section concerns the problem of Bismut formulas for the
L
-derivative of (
4.1
).
We first make necessary preparations concerning the partial derivative with respect to the initial value and the Malliavin derivative of (
4.1
).
In the second part, we will give a general result about Bismut formula for the
L
-derivative of (
4.1
),
the applications to non-degenerate and degenerate cases of (
4.1
) are addressed respectively in the last two parts.

4.1
The partial derivative and the Malliavin derivative of (
4.1
)

We begin with the following assumption.

(A1)

For every
t\in[0,T]
,
b(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{1,(1,0)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla b(t,\cdot,\mu)(x)\|+|D^{L}b(t,x,\cdot)(\mu)(y)|\leq K,\ \ t\in[0,T],\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(|b(t,0,\delta_{0})|+\|\sigma(t)\|)\leq K
.

Note that by the fundamental theorem for Bochner integral (see, for instance,
[
25
, Proposition A.2.3]
) and the definitions of
L
-derivative and the Wasserstein distance, (A1) implies

\displaystyle|b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,y,\nu)|\leq K(|x-y|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)),\ \ t\in[0,T],\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then, it follows from Theorem
3.1
that (
4.1
) has a unique solution.

To investigate the partial derivative with respect to initial value of (
4.1
), we first introduce a family of auxiliary equations.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
, let
(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
solve

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=b(t,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\varepsilon}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t)\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{H},\ \ X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=X_{0}+\varepsilon\eta,

and define

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\frac{X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-X_{t}}{\varepsilon},\ \ t\in[0,T],\ \ \varepsilon>0.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that (A1) holds. Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\bigg)\leq 2\text{\rm{e}}^{8(KT)^{2}}\mathbb{E}|\eta|^{2},

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1],t\in[0,T]}|\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leq\left(2|\eta|^{2}+8(KT)^{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{8(KT)^{2}}\mathbb{E}|\eta|^{2}\right)\text{\rm{e}}^{4(KT)^{2}}.

Proof.

By (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) and (A1), we have for any
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}-X_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}|\eta|^{2}+2\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left(b(r,X^{\varepsilon}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X^{\varepsilon}_{r}})-b(r,X_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{r}})\right)\text{\rm{d}}r\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}|\eta|^{2}+4K^{2}T\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{s}\left(|X^{\varepsilon}_{r}-X_{r}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathscr{L}_{X^{\varepsilon}_{r}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{r}})^{2}\right)\text{\rm{d}}r.

Taking the expectation on both sides of the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}-X_{s}|^{2}\bigg)\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\eta|^{2}+8K^{2}T\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{u\in[0,r]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{u}-X_{u}|^{2}\bigg)\text{\rm{d}}r,

which implies (
4.3
) and then (
4.4
) due to the Gronwall inequality.
∎

With Lemma
4.1
in hand, we can present the partial derivative in initial value of the equation (
4.1
).
Consider now the following linear random ODE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
: for any
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
and
t\in[0,T]
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla_{\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}}b(t,\cdot,\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t})+\left(\mathbb{E}\langle D^{L}b(t,y,\cdot)(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t}),\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}\rangle\right)|_{y=X_{t}}\right]\text{\rm{d}}t,\ \ \Gamma^{\eta}_{0}=\eta,

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle D^{L}b(t,y,\cdot)(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t}),\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}\rangle:=\left(\mathbb{E}\langle D^{L}b_{i}(t,y,\cdot)(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t}),\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}\rangle\right)_{1\leq i\leq d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Obviously, (A1) implies that the ODE has a unique solution
\{\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfying

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}|^{2}\right)\leq C_{T,K}\mathbb{E}|\eta|^{2}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that (A1) holds.
Then for any
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
,
the limit
\nabla_{\eta}X_{t}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\Upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{t},t\in[0,T]
exists in
L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathbb{P})

such that
\nabla_{\eta}X_{t}=\Gamma^{\eta}_{t}
holds for each
t\in[0,T]
, i.e.,
\nabla_{\eta}X_{t}
is the unique solution of (
4.5
).

Proof.

To simplify the notation, we denote

The Harmonic Descent Chain
David J. Aldous, Svante Janson, Xiaodan Li

Abstract
The decreasing Markov chain on {1,2,3, …} with transition probabilities p ⁡ ( j , j − i ) ∝ 1 / i p(j,j-i)\propto 1/i arises as a key component of the analysis of the beta-splitting random tree model. We give a direct and almost self-contained “probability” treatment of its occupation probabilities, as a counterpart to a more sophisticated but perhaps opaque derivation using a limit continuum tree structure and Mellin transforms.



Key words: Markov chain; occupation measure; coupling.

3.2
The maximal coupling regime

, with the
right side equal to K, and for
a>a_{2}(y)
, both sides of (
3.17
) are 0.

Now fix
x
.
For
y\geq x
write

m(x,y):=\max_{x\leq z\leq y}\mathbb{E}_{z}[V_{x}]

and note that
m(x,x)=0
.
If
y>x
, by considering the first step
y\to Y_{1}
, which
goes either into the interval
[1,x]
(probability
q_{x,y}
say) or into
the interval
[x+1,y-1]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{y}[V_{x}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{y}[(\log x-\log Y_{1})^{+}]+\mathbb{P}_{y}(\log x-\log Y_{1}<0)m(x,y-1).

(3.19)

By (
3.17
) with
a=\log y-\log x

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{y}[(\log x-\log Y_{1})^{+}]\leq K\mathbb{P}_{y}(\log x-\log Y_{1}\geq 0)=Kq_{x,y}.

(3.20)

Combining (
3.19
) and (
3.20
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{y}[V_{x}]\leq Kq_{x,y}+(1-q_{x,y})m(x,y-1)

(3.21)

and so the bound
m(x,y)\leq K
holds
by induction on
y=x,x+1,x+2,\ldots
.
∎

Completing the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Given Lemma
3.3
,
to show (
3.4
),
it remains only to consider the case
x_{0}/y_{0}\to 0
.
Use the shift regime dynamics
(x_{0},Y_{t})
in section
3.3

from
Y_{0}=y_{0}
until time

T^{Y}_{x_{0}}:=\min\{t:Y_{t}\leq x_{0}\},

and then use the maximal coupling in section
3.2
.
Lemma
3.4
shows that the overshoot of the first phase

V_{x_{0}}:=\log x_{0}-\log Y_{T^{Y}_{x_{0}}}

has
\mathbb{E}_{y_{0}}[V_{x_{0}}]\leq K
.
Hence the overshoots are tight, and by considering a subsequence we may
assume that
V_{x_{0}}
converges in distribution to some random variable
V
.
For convenience, we may also by the
Skorohod coupling theorem
[
6
, Theorem 4.30]

assume that
V_{x_{0}}
converges to
V
almost surely, and thus

\displaystyle x_{0}/Y_{T^{Y}_{x_{0}}}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}e^{V}>0.

(3.22)

This allows us to
condition on
Y_{T_{x_{0}}}
and
apply Lemma
3.3

(with
X
and
Y
interchanged)
with starting state
(x_{0},Y_{T_{x_{0}}})
.
Hence, for every fixed
k
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x_{0},y_{0}}\left(S^{couple}\leq k\mid Y_{T_{x_{0}}}\right)\to 0\qquad\text{a.s.}

and thus (
3.4
) follows by taking the expectation.
As noted after (
3.4
), this completes the proof of
the main “limits exist” part (
2.1
)
of Proposition
2.1
.
To complete the proof of
Proposition
2.1
,
and by section
2
thus also the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
we need to verify (
2.2
), that is

a(i)=\sum_{j>i}a(j)p(j,i),\quad i\geq 1.

(3.24)

From the pointwise convergence
a(n,j)\to a(j)
, Fatou’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j>i}a(j)p(j,i)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\sum_{j>i}a(n,j)p(j,i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}a(n,i)=a(i).

To prove equality we need to show
that, for every fixed

\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{n}\sum_{j>L}a(n,j)p(j,i)=0.

This is an easy consequence of the overshoot bound, as follows.

The sum above is the probability, starting at

,
which is bounded by the probability that first entrance is in

The latter, in the notation of Lemma

\mathbb{P}_{n}(V_{L}\geq\log L-\log i)

4
Direct proof of Lemma
3.2

Let
Z_{t}:=X_{t}^{-1/2}
. Then (
3.1
) implies that for an infinitesimal
time
dt
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[Z_{dt}]-x^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=dt\sum_{1\leq i<x}\lambda(x,i)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)=dt\sum_{1\leq i<x}\frac{\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{i}}{(x-i)\sqrt{i}{\sqrt{x}}}

\displaystyle=dt\sum_{1\leq i<x}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{i})\sqrt{i}{\sqrt{x}}}\leq\frac{dt}{x}\sum_{1\leq i<x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{dt}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\,dy=2\frac{dt}{\sqrt{x}}=2Z_{0}dt.

(4.1)

Hence, by the Markov property,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}_{x}[Z_{t}]\leq 2Z_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

(4.2)

and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[Z_{t}]\leq e^{2t}Z_{0}=e^{2t}x^{-1/2},\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.3)

Finally, Markov’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{k}\leq t)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\leq k)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z_{t}\geq k^{-1/2})\leq e^{2t}\sqrt{k/x}.

(4.4)

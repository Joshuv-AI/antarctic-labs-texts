Piercing the Veil of TVL: DeFi Reappraised
Yichen Luo, Yebo Feng ✉, Jiahua Xu, Paolo Tasca

Abstract
Total value locked (TVL) is widely used to measure the size and popularity of decentralized finance (DeFi). However, TVL can be manipulated and inflated through “double counting” activities such as wrapping and leveraging. As existing methodologies addressing double counting are inconsistent and flawed, we propose a new framework, termed “total value redeemable (TVR)” , to assess the true underlying value of DeFi. Our formal analysis reveals how DeFi’s complex network spreads financial contagion via derivative tokens, increasing TVL’s sensitivity to external shocks. To quantify double counting, we construct the DeFi multiplier, which mirrors the money multiplier in traditional finance (TradFi). Our measurements reveal substantial double counting in DeFi, finding that the gap between TVL and TVR reached $139.87 billion during the peak of DeFi activity on December 2, 2021, with a TVL-to-TVR ratio of approximately 2. We conduct sensitivity tests to evaluate the stability of TVL compared to TVR, demonstrating the former’s significantly higher level of instability than the latter, especially during market downturns: a 25% decline in the price of Ether (ETH) leads to a $1 billion greater decrease in TVL compared to TVR among leading protocols via asset value depreciation and liquidations triggered by derivative tokens. We also document that the decentralized finance money multiplier is positively correlated with crypto market indicators and negatively correlated with macroeconomic indicators. Overall, our study suggests that TVR is more reliable and stable than TVL.

The double counting problem in decentralized finance is a crucial yet understudied topic in the literature. Many studies use total value locked for protocols valuation and risk monitoring
[
14
,
16
,
24
,
26
]
but overlook the issue of double counting within total value locked. While some existing studies document the complexity and interconnections within decentralized finance at the token level
[
22
]
, protocol level
[
27
]
and sector level
[
4
]
, most of them focus on analyzing the topological features and associated risks of decentralized finance networks rather than their impacts on total value locked. Although a theoretical production-network model has been applied to assess the value added and service outputs across various decentralized finance sectors on Ethereum
[
4
]
, it fails to address double counting within individual sectors.

In this paper, we propose a novel yet effective measurement framework, termed total value redeemable (TVR), to address the double counting problem at the finest granularity—the token level. total value redeemable excludes the value of complex decentralized finance derivatives and borrowed tokens, focusing only on the asset component that contributes directly to the underlying value of decentralized finance that can ultimately be redeemed. By eliminating derivatives, total value redeemable also avoids the inclusion of financial contagion risk, making it a more stable metric than total value locked.

We reappraise the decentralized finance system’s value using the total value redeemable framework with data from 3,570 protocols over five years from DeFiLlama and token categories from CoinMarketCap.
We employ the token category data fetched from CoinMarketCap to identify
“plain tokens”
, i.e. tokens that do not entail any underlying token. The values of these tokens are then aggregated to calculate the total value redeemable for the entire decentralized finance system. Inspired by traditional finance money multiplier, we introduce the decentralized finance money multiplier, which is defined as the ratio of total value locked to total value redeemable. This metric quantifies the extent of double counting in decentralized finance.
Through formalization and sensitivity tests, we compare the stability of total value locked with that of total value redeemable. The formalization reveals that total value locked is highly sensitive to price shocks such as Ether price decline. This sensitivity arises from the endogeneity of the derivative token price and the quantity of derivative tokens staked in protocols for loanable funds. We then conduct simulations to assess the stability of total value locked compared to total value redeemable. The simulation results align with our formalization.

We summarize our main contributions as follows:

1.

By modeling and formalizing total value locked, we reveal the double counting mechanism and financial contagion risk under the total value locked framework. We find existing methodologies addressing double counting either inconsistent or flawed.

2.

To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to introduce an enhanced measurement framework to evaluate value locked within a decentralized finance system without double counting. Our analysis of 3,570 protocols over five years finds a substantial double counting within the DeFi system, up to $139.87 billion with a total value locked-total value redeemable ratio of around 2. This contribution provides decentralized finance users with more accurate and complete information about the value locked in decentralized finance, which supports better decision-making within the community.

3.

Our sensitivity tests reveal that total value locked is highly sensitive to market downturns compared to total value redeemable. A 25% drop in Ether price leads to a significant divergence, resulting in approximately a $1 billion greater decrease in total value locked compared to total value redeemable in a system of six representative decentralized finance protocols in Ethereum.

4.

We are also the first to build the decentralized finance money multiplier based on total value redeemable and total value locked in parallel to the traditional finance macroeconomic money multiplier to quantify the double counting. We document that the decentralized finance money multiplier is positively correlated with crypto market indicators and negatively correlated with macroeconomic indicators.

Enhanced Measurement Framework

In this section, we formalize the double counting problem, identify the instability of total value locked, and introduce our enhanced measurement framework total value redeemable.

3.1
Double Counting Problem

We initially classify decentralized finance tokens into plain tokens and derivative tokens. We provide the following definitions for the plain and underlying tokens:

Definition 2
(Plain Token)

A decentralized finance token without any underlying token.

Definition 3
(Derivative Token)

A decentralized finance receipt token, also known as an I-owe-you token or a depositary receipt token, that is generated in a specified ratio by depositing some underlying tokens into the smart contract of a protocol.

In the example used in
Fig. 2
, Ether is considered as a plain token because it is initially deposited into the system without having an underlying token. In contrast, other tokens (stETH, wstETH, DAI, aDAI, a3CRV, and cvxa3CRV) are considered derivative tokens since they have underlying tokens. Let
\bm{\tau}=[\tau_{i}]_{m\times 1}

m

{\it TVL}=(\mathbf{p}\odot\bm{\tau})^{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{1}+[\mathbf{p}\odot(\mathbf{1}-\bm{\tau})]^{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{1}

3.2
Instability of total value locked

In addition to the inflation issue, derivative tokens can act as channels for the spread of financial contagion, making the total value locked highly sensitive to market downturns. The prices of derivative tokens are endogenously determined by the prices and quantities of their underlying tokens. The pricing mechanism can be described as follows:

(i)

If the derivative token is a stablecoin generated from a collateralized debt position and the aggregate value of its underlying tokens meets or exceeds the value of the stablecoin, the token’s price is pegged to its predetermined fiat currency. This peg is maintained through an overcollateralization mechanism, as discussed in Appendix
Appendix 0.C
.

(ii)

Otherwise, the token price is determined by the ratio of the underlying tokens’ total value to the derivative token’s circulating supply.

In both cases, a short-term fluctuation term
\epsilon_{d}

We write the derivative token price as

\Gamma&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\qquad+\epsilon_{d},

3.3
Total Value Redeemable (TVR)

To address the double counting problem, we introduce the metric total value redeemable.

Definition 4
(Total Value Redeemable)

Token value that can be ultimately redeemed from a decentralized finance protocol or a decentralized finance ecosystem.

We can express the total value redeemable of the entire decentralized finance ecosystem as the sum of the total value of plain tokens including governance tokens, native tokens, and non-crypto-backed stablecoins held by smart contracts in the decentralized finance ecosystem:

{\it TVR}=(\mathbf{p}\odot\bm{\tau})^{T}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\mathbf{1}=\underbrace{(\mathbf{p}\odot\bm{\tau})^{T}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{1}-\bm{\omega})}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\scriptsize plain tokens held by}\\
\text{\scriptsize smart contracts in non-{protocols for loanable funds}s}\end{subarray}}+\underbrace{(\mathbf{p}\odot\bm{\tau})^{T}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}\bm{\omega}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\scriptsize plain tokens held by}\\
\text{\scriptsize smart contracts in {protocols for loanable funds}s}\end{subarray}},

(5)

where
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}=[q^{\prime}_{i,j}]_{m\times n}
, with
m
representing the number of token types and
n
is the number of decentralized finance protocols, denotes the matrix of tokens quantity held by smart contracts across all decentralized finance protocols. Compared to total value locked, total value redeemable excludes the value of derivative and borrowed tokens, considering only the value of plain tokens held by smart contracts to address the double counting problem. The exclusion of inflated values also decreases the complexity of the interplay within the decentralized finance system, mitigating the high sensitivity of the metric concerning the ultimate underlying plain tokens. We also introduce the protocol-level total value redeemable to address the intra-protocol double counting, as discussed in Appendix
Appendix 0.F
.

DeFiLlama provides total value locked adjusted for double counting of blockchains. Additionally, it aggregates chain-level total value locked to compute the adjusted total value locked for the entire decentralized finance ecosystem. However, it does not offer adjusted total value locked for specific protocols. Compared to DeFiLlama’s adjusted total value locked, total value redeemable eliminates double counting with finer granularity by selectively including or excluding tokens during the calculation, resulting in significantly higher accuracy. We provide a detailed comparison in calculation methods between decentralized finance space ecosystem-wide total value redeemable and DeFiLlama-adjusted total value locked in Appendix
Appendix 0.G
.

Empirical Analyses

This section details the data used for measurements and presents empirical results under both the traditional total value locked framework and our proposed total value redeemable framework. We also introduce the decentralized finance money multiplier to quantify double counting in decentralized finance and provide measurement results for individual altchains.

4.2
Total value locked, Adjusted total value locked, and total value redeemable

4.3
DeFi Money Multiplier

The M2 to M0 ratio, known as the traditional
money multiplier
, indicates the extent to which banks can utilize investor deposits. M0 denotes the money base, which includes cash and bank reserves. M2 denotes the supply of private money, which includes cash, checking deposits, and other short-term deposits. Drawing a parallel, we can divide total value locked by total value redeemable to compute the decentralized finance money multiplier.
This ratio reflects the degree of double counting and wrapping effects within the decentralized finance ecosystem, analogous to the money multiplier in traditional finance.
Fig. 7
plots the decentralized finance money multiplier.

Related Work

Several studies explore the role of total value locked in decentralized finance valuation and risk monitoring. Metelski et al.
[
14
]
and Xu et al.
[
34
]
investigate the causal relationship between key decentralized finance performance metrics, such as total value locked, protocol revenues, and the decentralized finance protocol valuations. Stepanova et al.
[
26
]
conduct preliminary descriptive and comparison work on total value locked of 12 most popular decentralized finance protocols. Maouchi et al.
[
16
]
show that total value locked can work as a valuable tool for monitoring market dynamics and assessing the risk of bubbles in the digital financial landscape. Şoiman et al.
[
24
]
use
total value locked divided by market capitalization for decentralized finance valuation and examine whether this metric drives the decentralized finance returns.

Some studies examine the decentralized finance composability and total value locked double counting problem in a limited scope. Kitzler et al.
[
13
]
measure the composition of decentralized finance protocols. Saengchote
[
22
]
examines the flow of DAI, a decentralized finance stablecoin, between protocols using high-frequency transaction-level data. The study also explains how total value locked accounts for repeat value through the wrapping of DAI. Chiu et al.
[
4
]
use a standard theoretical production-network model to assess the value added and service outputs across various decentralized finance sectors on Ethereum.

7
Conclusion

This paper presents a novel yet effective measurement framework, total value redeemable, to thoroughly address the double counting problem in decentralized finance. We find a substantial amount of double counting within the DeFi system. Our sensitivity tests show that total value locked is highly sensitive during market downturns. We also document that the DeFi money multiplier is positively correlated with crypto market indicators and negatively correlated with macroeconomic indicators. Overall, our findings suggest that total value redeemable is more reliable and stable than total value locked.

Appendix 0.C
Overcollateralization and Liquidation

Protocols for loanable funds are decentralized finance protocols that allow users to supply and borrow cryptocurrency
[
20
]
. Analogous to a pawnshop, a protocols for loanable funds adheres to the overcollateralization mechanism, which means the borrower has to pledge collateral of higher total value than the loan
[
17
]
. The ratio of the maximum amount of tokens that can be borrowed to the amount of collateral is referred to as the loan-to-value ratio. protocols for loanable funds includes collateralized debt position such as MakerDAO (orange block in
Fig. 2
) and lending protocols such as Aave (purple block). In contrast, Lido, Uniswap V2, Curve, and Convex are not considered a protocols for loanable funds. In a protocols for loanable funds, a user’s account can have multiple collaterals and debts, which are called its position. We can express the quantities of collaterals of a position as a vector:
\mathbf{c}=\left[q_{i}\right]_{k\times 1}
, where
k
are the number of collateral types. To convert token quantity into token value in USD, let
p
denotes the collateral price. By integrating all collateral prices into a vector
\mathbf{p}_{c}=\left[p_{i}\right]_{k\times 1}
, we can express the value of an account’s collateral as
\mathbf{c}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{c}
. Similarly, we can express the quantities, prices, and total value of debt of a position as
\mathbf{d}
,
\mathbf{p}_{d}
, and
\mathbf{d}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{d}
. Unlike a lending protocol, a collateralized debt position uses staked collateral to issue stablecoins. Stablecoins are a type of crypto asset with a value pegged to a reference point, such as a fiat currency or another cryptocurrency.

During a market downturn, the position can be liquidated, resulting in a portion of the collateral being purchased by the liquidator at a discount when the liquidation is profitable. Whether an account is liquidated depends on its position’s health ratio and liquidation profit. Each type of collateral has its own liquidation threshold
\alpha\in[0,1)
, which represents its borrowing capacity. Let
\bm{\alpha}=\left[\alpha_{i}\right]_{k\times 1}
represents the vector of liquidation thresholds for all types of collaterals in a protocols for loanable funds. Health factor
h
describes the financial health of an account, which can be expressed as

h=\frac{\mathbf{c}^{T}\left(\bm{\alpha}\odot\mathbf{p}_{c}\right)}{\mathbf{d}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{d}},

(6)

where
\odot
denotes the element-wise product. The position will be liquidated if
h\leq 1
and the liquidation profit
\Pi
is positive.

Appendix 0.E
Derivation of
\Delta
and
\Pi

If liquidation happens in a collateralized debt position, a liquidator will repay all debts of the position, with their value denoted as
V_{d}=\mathbf{d}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{d}
according to
Appendix 0.C
. Simultaneously, the collateralized debt position receives and burns all repaid stablecoins (see journal entries of MakerDAO liquidation in
Table 6
). Therefore, the repaid token quantity remains unchanged, hence
\Delta_{t+1}=0
for the repaid token. Next, the liquidator withdraws all collaterals from the collateralized debt position, with their value denoted as
V_{c}=\mathbf{c}^{T}\mathbf{p}_{c}
according to
Appendix 0.C
. The decrease of the collateral token quantity is as follows:
\Delta_{t+1}=-q_{t}
. Before initiating the liquidation, the rational liquidator will compare the bonus
V_{c}-V_{d}
with the gas fee
gasFees
. If the liquidation profit
\Pi=V_{c}-V_{d}-gasFees>0
, the liquidator will trigger the liquidation.

If liquidation happens in a lending protocol, a liquidator will first repay
V_{\textnormal{liq}}
worth of debt and then receive
V_{\textnormal{liq}}(1+b)
worth of collateral. Here, the liquidation spread
b
represents the proportion of the bonus that a liquidator can collect relative to the debt during the liquidation. Before initiating the liquidation, the rational liquidator will compare net revenue
V_{\textnormal{liq}}b
with the transaction fee
gasFees
. If the profit
\Pi=V_{\textnormal{liq}}b-gasFees>0
, the liquidator will trigger the liquidation.

Theoretically, the lending protocol establishes an upper bound for
V_{\textnormal{liq}}
, defined as
\delta V_{d}
. Here, the close factor
\delta
denotes the maximum proportion of the debt allowed to be repaid in a single liquidation. To obtain an analytic result, we assume the liquidator repays exactly this maximum amount, leading to
V_{\textnormal{liq}}=\delta V_{d}
. However, we should also consider the scenario in which the actual collateral value
V_{c}
is lower than the expected collateral
V_{\textnormal{liq}}(1+b)
that the liquidator wants to receive when preparing to liquidate the theoretical maximum amount of debt
\delta V_{d}
. Consequently, the maximum amount of debt that can be covered is determined by the lesser of
\frac{V_{c}}{1+b}
and
\delta V_{d}
, expressed as
V_{\textnormal{liq}}=\min\{\frac{V_{c}}{1+b},\delta V_{d}\}
. Given that a position may include multiple types of collateral and borrowed tokens, we assume the liquidator withdraws and repays each proportionally to their share of the total collateral and debt, respectively. Consequently, the repaid token quantity increase by
\frac{V_{\textnormal{liq}}q}{V_{d}}
, while the collateral token decreases by
\frac{(1+b)V_{\textnormal{liq}}q}{V_{c}}
.

Appendix 0.J

To gain a broader understanding of the double-counting issue, we analyze two niche alternative chains: Hedera ranked 42nd in total value locked and Ultron ranked 21st in total value locked. Our findings indicate that blockchains with limited infrastructure feature simpler token-wrapping networks, which leads to less double counting. Additionally, we observe that the DeFiLlama framework addressing the double counting might deflate the true value redeemable of an altchain.

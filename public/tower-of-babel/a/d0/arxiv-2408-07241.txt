Long Time Dynamics of the Three-Dimensional Nernst-Planck-Darcy Model
Elie Abdo, Đorđe Nikolić

Abstract
. We consider an electrodiffusion model describing the evolution of N N ionic species in a three-dimensional fluid flowing through a porous medium and forced by added body charges. We address the global well-posedness and long-time dynamics of the model. In the absence of added charges, we prove that the ionic concentrations decay exponentially fast in time to their initial spatial averages in all Sobolev norms. When the fluid undergoes the influence of given time-independent charges, we obtain the existence of a finite-dimensional global attractor.

In this paper, we consider an electrodiffusion model describing the nonlinear and nonlocal evolution of
N
ionic concentrations
c_{i}
, with diffusivities
D_{i}
and valences
z_{i}
, interacting with an incompressible fluid, flowing through a porous medium with a velocity
u
and a pressure
p
. The time evolution of each concentration
c_{i}
is described by the Nernst-Planck equations

(1)

\partial_{t}c_{i}+\nabla\cdot j_{i}=\partial_{t}c_{i}+u\cdot\nabla c_{i}-D_{i}\Delta c_{i}-z_{i}D_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)=0,

for
i=1,\dots,N
, where
j_{i}=uc_{i}-D_{i}\nabla c_{i}-z_{i}D_{i}c_{i}\nabla\Phi
are the fluxes,

\Phi
is the electric potential generated by the ionic charge density

(2)

\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}

and some added time-independent body charge
\tilde{\rho}
, and obeying the following Poisson equation

(3)

-\Delta\Phi=\rho+\tilde{\rho}.

The velocity of the fluid and electric forces driven by the total potential are balanced via Darcy’s law

(4)

u+\nabla p=-(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi,\hskip 28.45274pt\nabla\cdot u=0,

which, projected onto the space of divergence-free vectors, reduces to

(5)

u=-\mathbb{P}((\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi).

Here the operator
\mathbb{P}=I+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot
denotes the Leray-Hodge projection.
The model, described by equations (
1
)–(
5

(c_{1}(0),\dots,c_{N}(0))
, is called the Nernst-Planck-Darcy system and will be referred to as NPD. We address the global well-posedness and long-time dynamics of the NPD model for
N

D_{1}=\dots=D_{N}:=D
and valences obeying
|z_{1}|=\dots=|z_{N}|
on the three-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}=[0,2\pi]^{3}
with periodic boundary conditions.

When the fluid is viscous, Darcy’s law is replaced by the Navier-Stokes equations, and the resulting model, called the Nernst-Planck-Navier-Stokes system and abbreviated by NPNS, has been a center of interest over the last two decades. Existence, uniqueness, regularity, analyticity, stability and long-time dynamics of solutions have been widely studied in the literature on two-dimensional domains with and without physical boundaries and under different types of boundary conditions (

As a matter of fact, the two-species model has a nice structure by which the nonlinear aspects of the electromigration terms pose fewer major challenges to the analysis of the model. More precisely, the behavior of the ionic concentrations
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
associated with the two given species is fully determined by that of the charge density
\rho=c_{1}-c_{2}
and the total amount
\sigma=c_{1}+c_{2}
, in which case the coupled migration

(6)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\Phi)\rho dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi)\sigma dx

fully dissipates into
-\|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

L^{2}

\rho

\sigma

N

In this paper, we consider the three-dimensional periodic NPD system in the more general setting of
N
ions with equal diffusivities
D
and valences
|z_{1}|=\dots=|z_{N}|
, and we address the long-time dynamics in the presence and absence of body charges
\tilde{\rho}
. The main challenges arise from:

(1)

The more singular constitutive relation between the velocity
u
and the forces
-\rho\nabla\Phi
, compared to the viscous Nernst-Planck-Navier-Stokes system;

(2)

The nonlinear structure of the electromigration term
\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)
on three-dimensional spatial domains;

(3)

The effects of the added time-independent density
\tilde{\rho}
on the time asymptotic behavior of solutions.

We successfully overcome these challenges and prove the following results:

(1)

Existence of global
C^{\infty}
solutions that decay exponentially fast in time to their initial spatial averages in all Sobolev norms in the absence of body charges. This generalizes the convergence results obtained in
[

for the

N

k\geq 0

(2)

Existence of a smooth finite-dimensional global attractor in the presence of added body charges.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2

7

C

We state and prove a modified version of the Poincaré inequality that will be needed to study the long-time dynamics in

Proposition 2.2
.

(Poincaré inequality) Let

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq Cp^{2}\||f-\bar{f}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{|{\mathbb{T}}^{3}|}\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}^{p}

By the Poincaré inequality applied to the mean-free function

\displaystyle\left\|(f-\bar{f})^{\frac{p}{2}}-\frac{1}{|{\mathbb{T}}^{3}|}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(f-\bar{f})^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right\|_{L^{2}}

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2Cp^{2}}{4}\left\|(f-\bar{f})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{|{\mathbb{T}}^{3}|}\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}^{p}.

3.
Existence and Uniqueness of Solutions

In this section, we prove the existence of unique solutions to the NPD system (
1
)–(
5
) for
H^{1}
initial concentrations.

Definition 1
.

A vector
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
is said to be a strong solution of the NPD system (
1
)–(
5
) on the time interval
[0,T]
if it solves (
1
)–(
5
) in the sense of distributions and obeys the regularity criterion

(21)

c_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{3}))

for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T>0
be an arbitrary time. Let
c_{i}(0)\in H^{1}
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
be nonnegative. Assume that
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{0}+\tilde{\rho})(x)dx=0
. The NPD system (
1
)–(
5
) has a unique strong solution
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
on
[0,T]
. Moreover, there exist nonnegative constants
C_{1},\dots,C_{N}
depending only on the initial data, and nonnegative constants
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{N}
depending only on the body charge
\tilde{\rho}
and the spatial averages of the initial concentrations, and a positive universal constant
c
such that the following estimates

(22)

\|\nabla c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{i}e^{-ct}+\Gamma_{i}

holds for all positive times
t\geq 0
and for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. Moreover,
\Gamma_{1}=\dots=\Gamma_{N}=0
when
\tilde{\rho}=0
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
follows from the following two propositions:

Proposition 3.1
.

(Local Solution)
Let
c_{i}(0)\in H^{1}
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. Assume that
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{0}+\tilde{\rho})(x)dx=0
. Then there exists a time
T_{0}>0
depending only on the size of the initial data in
H^{1}
and the parameters of the problem such that the NPD system (
1
)–(
5
) has a unique strong solution
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
on
[0,T_{0}]
.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
is standard and based on Galerkin approximations, energy estimates, and passage to the limit using the Aubin-Lions lemma. We present the proof in Appendix
A
for the sake of completion.

Proposition 3.2
.

(Blow-up Criterion) Let
T>0
be an arbitrary time. Let
c_{i}(0)\in H^{1}
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
be nonnegative. Assume that
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(\rho_{0}+\tilde{\rho})(x)dx=0
. Suppose
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
is a strong solution to the NPD system (
1
)–(
5
) on
[0,T]
. Then there exist nonnegative constants
C_{1},\dots,C_{N}
depending only on the initial data, and nonnegative constant
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{N}
depending only on the body charge
\tilde{\rho}
and the spatial averages of the initial concentrations, and a positive universal constant
c
such that the following estimates

(23)

\|\nabla c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{i}e^{-ct}+\Gamma_{i}

holds for all positive times
t\geq 0
and for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. Moreover,
\Gamma_{1}=\dots=\Gamma_{N}=0
when
\tilde{\rho}=0
.

Proof.

The nonnegativity of the ionic concentrations is preserved for all times
t\geq 0
, a fact that follows from
[
6
]
. The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1. Conservation Laws.
Integrating the ionic concentration equation (
1
) over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
yields

(24)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c_{i}(x,t)dx=0,

after making use of the divergence-free condition obeyed by the velocity field
u
and integrating by parts. As a consequence, we have

(25)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c_{i}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c_{i}(x,0)dx

for all times
t\geq 0
and all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. In view of the relation (
2
) between the charge density
\rho
and the ionic concentrations, we deduce the following conservation law

(26)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho(x,0)dx

at all times
t\geq 0
.
As the spatial averages of the charge density and ionic concentrations are time-independent, we respectively denote them

(27)

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho(x,t)dx,

(28)

\displaystyle\bar{c_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c_{i}(x,t)dx,

for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

Step 2. A priori Bounds for the Charge Density in
L^{2}
.
Let
\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}
and
\sigma=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}.

Then the time evolutions of
\rho
and
\sigma
are described by

(29)

\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-D\Delta\rho=Dz^{2}\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\Phi)

and

(30)

\partial_{t}(z^{2}\sigma)+u\cdot\nabla(z^{2}\sigma)-D\Delta(z^{2}\sigma)=Dz^{2}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi),

respectively. We multiply the
\rho
-equation (
29
) by
\rho
and the
\sigma
-equation
(
30
)
by
\sigma
, integrate spatially over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, and obtain the energy equalities

(31)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sigma\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\rho dx

and

(32)

\frac{z^{2}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Dz^{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi)\sigma dx+Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho\Delta\Phi\sigma dx

respectively. We add (
31
) and (
32
), make use of the Poisson equation (
3
) obeyed by
\Phi
, and deduce the differential equation

(33)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=-Dz^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\rho(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\sigma dx,

which boils down to

(34)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=-Dz^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\rho\tilde{\rho}\sigma dx

due to the nonnegativity of the ionic concentrations.
An application of Hölder and Young inequalities gives

(35)

\left|Dz^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\rho\tilde{\rho}\sigma dx\right|\leq Dz^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}||\rho\sqrt{\sigma}|dx\leq\frac{Dz^{2}}{2}\|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{Dz^{2}}{2}\|\tilde{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore, we estimate

(36)

\|\tilde{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(\sigma-\bar{\sigma})dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}\bar{\sigma}dx\leq\|\tilde{\rho}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\bar{\sigma}
stands for the time-independent spatial average of
\sigma
over
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. In view of the Poincaré inequality applied to the mean-free function
\sigma-\bar{\sigma}
and Young’s inequality for products, we have

(37)

\|\tilde{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\tilde{\rho}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{Dz^{2}}{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Putting (
34
)–(
37
) together, we infer that

(38)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

As
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{\sigma}
are constants in time, it holds that

(39)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

(40)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

from which we obtain the differential inequality

(41)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the Poincaré inequality, it follows that

(42)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+cD\left(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\bar{\sigma}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, we apply the uniform Gronwall Lemma
2.1
and deduce that

(43)

\|\rho(t)-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma(t)-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq A_{1}e^{-cDt}+A_{2}

holds for all
t\geq 0
, where
A_{1}=\|\rho_{0}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\|\sigma_{0}-\bar{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
A_{2}=C(z,D)\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)
.
We point out that in the case of two ionic species (
N=2
) with valences
z_{1}=z>0
and
z_{2}=-z
, we have the instantaneous estimates

(44)

\|c_{1}(t)-\bar{c}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{2}(t)-\bar{c}_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq A_{1}e^{-cDt}+A_{2}

due to (
43
) and the parallelogram law. In the more general case of
N
ionic species, we need to study the evolution of each ionic concentration.

Step 3. A priori Bounds for the Concentrations in
L^{2}
.

\Gamma_{2,k}

\Gamma_{4,k}
are nonnegative constants depending only on
k
and
\tilde{v}
such that
\Gamma_{2,k}=\Gamma_{4,k}=0
when
\tilde{v}=0
;

(3)

p_{1}\geq 0
and
p_{2}\geq 0
are some nonnegative powers.

In this case, the energy evolution (
93
) gives rise to the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{s}{4}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(95)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\Gamma_{1,k}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{p_{1}}+\Gamma_{2,k}(\tilde{v})\right)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\frac{s}{4}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\left(\Gamma_{3,k}+\Gamma_{4,k}(\tilde{v})\right)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{p_{2}}.

In view of the induction hypothesis (
92
), the conditions of Proposition
2.1
are satisfied. Consequently, an application of the uniform Gronwall Lemma
2.1
gives the desired bound

(96)

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{t}^{t+1}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{s}{4}}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{k}^{1}e^{-c_{k}t}+C_{k}^{2}.

In summary, if the
H^{1}
estimate (
91
) and the nonlinear estimate (
94
) holds, then one obtain the bounds (
107
) describing the long-time behavior of solutions to (
88
). We apply this general framework to address the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the NPD system.

4.2.
Higher-order Sobolev Regularity of Solutions to the NPD System.

In this subsection, we study the smoothness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to the NPD system in the presence and absence of body charges
\tilde{\rho}
. We need the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\in H^{3}
be a mean-free function. Suppose
\Psi
is a solution of the 3D Poisson equation

(97)

-\Delta\Psi=g

with periodic boundary conditions. Let
v=-\mathbb{P}(g\nabla\Psi)
. Then the following interpolation inequalities

(98)

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

(99)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

hold.

Proof.

As
\Delta
and
\mathbb{P}
commutes and
\mathbb{P}
is bounded on
L^{3}
, we have

(100)

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\Delta(g\nabla\Psi)\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\Delta g\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{3}}+C\|\nabla g\cdot\nabla\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{3}}+C\|g\Delta\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{3}}.

Applications of Hölder’s inequality yield

(101)

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\Delta g\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla g\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{6}}+C\|g\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{6}}.

By making use of standard 3D continuous Sobolev embeddings, we have

(102)

\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla\Psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\Psi\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|(-\Delta)^{-1}g\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}},

(103)

\|\nabla\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|\nabla\nabla\Psi\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\Psi\|_{H^{3}}=C\|(-\Delta)^{-1}g\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}},

and

(104)

\|\Delta\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|\Delta\nabla\Psi\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\Psi\|_{H^{4}}\leq C\|(-\Delta)^{-1}g\|_{H^{4}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{2}}.

Consequently, it follows that

(105)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\Delta g\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}+C\|g\|_{H^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}.

Finally, we apply the inequality
\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and deduce (
98
). As for (
99
), we have

(106)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla(g\nabla\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|g\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.1
.

For any time
t\geq 0
, it holds that

(107)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{t}^{t+1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{k}^{1}e^{-c_{k}t}+C_{k}^{2}

for any integer
k\geq 1
, where
C_{k}^{1}
is a positive constant depending only on
k
, the size of the initial data
v_{0}
in
H^{k}
, and
\tilde{\rho}
,
c_{k}
is a positive constant depending only on
k
and the diffusivity
D
, and
C_{k}^{2}
is a nonnegative constant depending only on
k
and
\tilde{\rho}
such that
C_{k}^{2}=0
when
\tilde{\rho}=0
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the general framework introduced in the previous subsection where
v=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
,
\tilde{v}=\tilde{\rho}
,
s=2
,
c=D
, and
f(v,\tilde{v})=(-u\cdot\nabla c_{1}+Dz_{1}\nabla\cdot(c_{1}\nabla\Phi),\dots,-u\cdot\nabla c_{N}+Dz_{N}\nabla\cdot(c_{N}\nabla\Phi))
. When
k=1
, the result follows from Theorem
3.1
and yields the
H^{1}
estimate corresponding to (
91
). We prove that the nonlinear estimate (
94
) holds. We fix an integer
k\geq 3
. Using the fact that
H^{k-1}
is a Banach Algebra, the boundedness of the Leray projector on
L^{2}
, and elliptic estimates, we have

(108)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}dx\right|\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\mathbb{P}((\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}+C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{k-1}}^{4}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

If
k=2
, we make use of the cancellation law

(109)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla\Delta c_{i})\Delta c_{i}dx=0

together with the interpolation inequalities (
98
) and (
99
) to estimate

(110)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})(-\Delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}c_{i}dx\right|=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(-\Delta)(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})(-\Delta)c_{i}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[\|\Delta u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{3}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{6}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{3}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{4}+\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{3}}^{8}+1\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

As for the electromigration term, since
H^{k}
is a Banach Algebra for

, we have

(111)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}dx\right|\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, the nonlinear estimate (
94
) is fulfilled for the 3D NPD system.
∎

5.
Properties of the Solution Map

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the subset of

consisting of vectors
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})
such that:

(1)

\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N}
are nonnegative functions almost everywhere;

(2)

The density
R=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\xi_{i}
obeys
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(R+\tilde{\rho})dx=0
.

We define the solution map

(112)

\mathcal{S}(t):\mathcal{V}\mapsto\mathcal{V}

corresponding to the 3D periodic NPD system by

(113)

\mathcal{S}(t)(c_{1}(0),\dots,c_{N}(0))=(c_{1}(t),\dots,c_{N}(t)).

For each
M>0
, we consider the subset
\mathcal{V}_{M}
of
\mathcal{V}
consisting of vectors
v=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\mathcal{V}
obeying
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}v(x)dx\leq M
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix
M>0
and an integer
k\geq 2
. There exists a radius
R>0
depending only on the density
\tilde{\rho}
,
M
and

such that for any

(c_{1}(0),\dots,c_{N}(0))\in\mathcal{V}_{M}

depending on
the

norm of

such that

\mathcal{S}(t)(c_{1}(0),\dots,c_{N}(0))\in\mathcal{B}_{R,M,k}=\left\{(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})\in\mathcal{V}_{M}\cap(H^{k}\times\dots\times H^{k}):\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}\|_{H^{k}}\leq R\right\}

for all

.

The proof follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The ball

for any

\|\mathcal{S}(t)w_{1}^{0}-\mathcal{S}(t)w_{2}^{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq CK(t)\|w_{1}^{0}-w_{2}^{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},

for any

6.
Backward Uniqueness

In this section, we generalize an argument of
[
9
]
and present a general framework to study the backward uniqueness of solutions to fractionally dissipative PDEs, and we apply it to the NPD system.

6.1.
General Framework for Backward Uniqueness.

For
s\in(0,2]
, we consider the system of fractionally dissipative partial differential equation

(117)

\partial_{t}v+c(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v=f(v,\tilde{v})

with initial data

(118)

v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)

on the
d
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with periodic boundary conditions, where
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
is an
n
-dimensional vector field depending on a spatial variable
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and a time variable
t\in[0,\infty)
,
c
is a positive constant,
\tilde{v}
is a given smooth time-independent function that depends only on
x
, and
f(v,\tilde{v})=(f_{1}(v,\tilde{v}),\dots,f_{n}(v,\tilde{v}))
is an
n
-dimensional vector field consisting of linear or nonlinear operators
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
in
v
. Suppose (
117
) has a unique global-in-time smooth solution denoted by
v(x,t)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be two smooth solutions to (
117
) corresponding to two initial data
v_{1}(0)
and
v_{2}(0)
respectively. Suppose there is a time
T
such that
v_{1}(T)=v_{2}(T)
. Denote by
v
the difference
v_{1}-v_{2}
. If

(119)

\|f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}\leq K_{1}(t)\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+K_{2}(t)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{4}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2},

on
[0,T]
with

(120)

\int_{0}^{T}(K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t))dt<\infty,

then
v_{1}(0)=v_{2}(0)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
v\neq 0
on
[0,T)
. Let
E_{0}=\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}
,
E_{1}=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{4}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}
and
Y(t)=-\log E_{0}
. We note that
Y(t)
is bounded from below and blows up at
t
approaches
T^{-}
. We show that

(121)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}\leq C_{1}(t)\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}+C_{2}(t)

and

(122)

\frac{d}{dt}Y\leq C_{3}(t)\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}+C_{4}(t)

for some functions
C_{1},\dots,C_{4}
obeying

(123)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}+C_{4}\right)dt<\infty.

This implies that
Y\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, contradicting the blow up near
T
. The energies
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
evolve according to

(124)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}E_{0}+cE_{1}=(f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v}),v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}

and

(125)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}E_{1}+c\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}=(f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v}),(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}.

Since
Y
obeys

(126)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{dY}{dt}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\log E_{0}=c\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}-\frac{(f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v}),v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}}{E_{0}}

and

(127)

\displaystyle|(f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v}),v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(K_{1}(t)E_{0}+K_{2}(t)E_{1}+E_{0}),

we infer that the differential inequality (
122
) holds. Now we turn our attention to the time evolution of
\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}
and derive (
121
). We have

(128)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2E_{0}}\frac{dE_{1}}{dt}+\frac{1}{2}E_{1}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c}{E_{0}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{cE_{1}^{2}}{E_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{E_{0}}(f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v}),((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}-E_{1}E_{0}^{-1})v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}.

We compute the following inner product

(129)

\displaystyle(((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}-E_{1}E_{0}^{-1})v,((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}-E_{1}E_{0}^{-1})v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+E_{1}^{2}E_{0}^{-2}\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}-2E_{1}E_{0}^{-1}((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v,v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}+E_{1}^{2}E_{0}^{-1}-2E_{1}^{2}E_{0}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}-E_{1}^{2}E_{0}^{-1},

and deduce the identity

(130)

-\frac{c}{E_{0}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{cE_{1}^{2}}{E_{0}^{2}}=-\frac{c}{E_{0}}\|((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}-E_{1}E_{0}^{-1})v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}.

Consequently, we obtain

(131)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{E_{1}}{E_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{c}{E_{0}}\|((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}-E_{1}E_{0}^{-1})v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2}\leq CE_{0}^{-1}\|f(v_{1},\tilde{v})-f(v_{2},\tilde{v})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{2},

from which we deduce (
121
) after making use of (
119
).
∎

6.2.
Injectivity of the Solution Map

In this subsection, we use the aforementioned general framework to prove the injectivity of the solution map.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
w_{1}=(c_{1}^{1},\dots,c_{N}^{1})
and
w_{2}=(c_{1}^{2},\dots,c_{N}^{2})
be solutions to the NPD system corresponding to two smooth initial data
w_{1}(0)=(c_{1}^{1}(0),\dots,c_{N}^{1}(0))\in\mathcal{V}
and
w_{2}(0)=(c_{1}^{2}(0),\dots,c_{N}^{2}(0))\in\mathcal{V}
respectively. Suppose there is a time
T
such that
w_{1}(T)=w_{2}(T)
. Then
w_{1}(0)=w_{2}(0)
.

Proof.

We define the differences

(132)

w=w_{1}-w_{2},c_{i}=c_{i}^{1}-c_{i}^{2},u=u_{1}-u_{2},\rho=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\Phi=\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}

where

(133)

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}^{1},\rho_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}^{2},-\Delta\Phi_{1}=\rho_{1}+\tilde{\rho},-\Delta\Phi_{2}=\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho},

\displaystyle u_{1}=-\mathbb{P}((\rho_{1}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi_{1}),u_{2}=-\mathbb{P}((\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi_{2}).

Step 1. Velocity Estimates.
The difference
u
obeys the constitutive relation

(134)

u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi_{1})-\mathbb{P}((\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi)

where
\Phi
solves the Poisson equation

(135)

-\Delta\Phi=\rho.

Due to the boundedness of the Leray projector on
L^{3}
, we have

(136)

\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|\rho\nabla\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}+C\|(\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+C\|\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}^{2},

which boils down to

(137)

\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\rho_{1}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

after making use of elliptic estimates and the 3D continuous Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}
in
L^{6}
. Consequently, we infer that

(138)

\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}^{1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|c_{i}^{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Since
\mathbb{P}
is bounded on
H^{2}
and
H^{2}
is a Banach Algebra, we have

(139)

\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|(\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

giving rise to the estimate

(140)

\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}^{2}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}^{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right).

Step 2. Backward Uniqueness.
For a vector
\omega=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})
, we let
R=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\xi_{i}
,
\Psi=(-\Delta)^{-1}(R+\tilde{\rho})
, and
u=-\mathbb{P}((R+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Psi)
, and we consider the operator

(141)

Appendix A
Local Well-Posedness

In this appendix, we present the proof of Proposition

.

Proof.

The proof is standard and based on Galerkin approximations, energy estimates, and passage to the limit using the Aubin-Lions lemma. We only provide a priori bounds for simplicity.
The time evolution of
\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}
is described by

(152)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}dx+Dz_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Delta c_{i}dx.

We estimate

(153)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{3}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{6}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\nabla(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{4}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

using Hölder’s inequality with exponents 6,3,2, the 3D Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality applied to the mean-free vector
\nabla c_{i}
, Young’s inequality for products, the Poincaré inequality applied to the mean-free function
\rho+\tilde{\rho}
, 3D elliptic estimates, and the relation between the density
\rho
and the ionic concentrations. As for the electromigration term, we have

(154)

\displaystyle\left|Dz_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Delta c_{i}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq D|z_{i}|\left(\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta\Phi\|_{L^{3}}+|\bar{c}_{i}|\|\Delta\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

where the 3D elliptic estimate

(155)

\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Delta\Phi\|_{L^{3}}+\|\Delta\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla\rho+\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}

is exploited. Summing the equations (
152

(156)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{4}+C_{\tilde{\rho}}

holds, where

is a positive constant depending only on

. This yields a local solution
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})
on a short time interval
[0,T_{0}]
. In order to prove uniqueness, suppose
(c_{1}^{1},\dots,c_{N}^{1})
and
(c_{1}^{2},\dots,c_{N}^{2})
are two solutions with equal initial data. For

, where

and

\Phi
and velocity difference

satisfy

(157)

-\Delta\Phi=\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}

and

(158)

u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi_{1}+(\rho_{2}+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi)

respectively.
Each
c_{i}
evolves in time according to

(159)

\partial_{t}c_{i}+D\Delta c_{i}=-u\cdot\nabla c_{i}^{1}-u_{2}\cdot\nabla c_{i}+Dz_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi_{1})+Dz_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}^{2}\nabla\Phi),

which gives rise to the energy equation

(160)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}^{1}c_{i}dx+D\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi_{1}+c_{i}^{2}\nabla\Phi)\cdot\nabla c_{i}dx

after multiplying by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}}

(162)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}^{1}c_{i}dx\right|

\displaystyle\left|D\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi_{1}+c_{i}^{2}\nabla\Phi)\cdot\nabla c_{i}dx\right|\leq\frac{D}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\nabla\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|c_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}})_{n}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}[\xi^{j}]

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}^{1}_{w_{0}}[\xi^{j}],\dots,\mathcal{A}^{N}_{w_{0}}[\xi^{j}]),\mathrm{where}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i}_{w_{0}}[\xi^{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(R_{j}\nabla\Phi+(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Psi_{j})\cdot\nabla c_{i}+u\cdot\nabla\xi_{i}^{j}-Dz_{i}\nabla\cdot(\xi_{i}^{j}\nabla\Phi+c_{i}\nabla\Psi_{j}),

and

(177)

(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)_{n}=(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)\wedge I\wedge\dots\wedge I+\dots+I\wedge\dots\wedge I\wedge(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)

respectively. The evolution equation

(178)

\partial_{t}(\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{n})+(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)_{n}(\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{n})=0,

holds and yields

(179)

\frac{d}{dt}V_{n}+Trace((\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)Q_{n})V_{n}=0

where
Q_{n}
is the orthogonal projection in
\mathcal{V}
onto the space spanned by
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{n}
. Consequently, we infer that

(180)

V_{n}(t)\leq V_{N}(0)\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}Trace((\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)Q_{n})ds\right\}

for any
t\geq 0
. For each
t\geq 0
, we let
\left\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\right\}
be a orthonomal set in
\mathcal{V}
spanning the linear span of
\left\{\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{n}\right\}
. Then

(181)

\displaystyle Trace((\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)Q_{n})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(-D\Delta\phi_{j}+\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}[\phi_{j}],-\Delta\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}.

Let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
be the first
n
eigenvalues of the Laplace opeartor
-\Delta
with periodic boundary conditions. On one hand, we have

(182)

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(-D\Delta\phi_{j},-\Delta\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}\geq\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n}\geq CDn^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

(183)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}[\phi_{j}],-\Delta\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}|\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}[\phi_{j}]\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbb{P}(R_{j}\nabla\Phi+(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\phi_{i}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+CD\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla\cdot(\phi_{i}^{j}\nabla\Phi+c_{i}\nabla\Psi_{j})\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

where
\phi_{j}=(\phi_{1}^{j},\dots,\phi_{N}^{j}),R_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\phi_{i}^{j},
and
\Psi_{j}=(-\Delta)^{-1}R_{j}
. We estimate

(184)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}(R_{j}\nabla\Phi+(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\left(\|R_{j}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{3}}+\|(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{j}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{6}}+C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\|R_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}

using the Sobolev inequality, elliptic estimates, and the normalization
\|\phi^{j}\|_{\mathcal{V}}=1
. Similarly, we have

(185)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\phi_{i}^{j}\nabla\Phi+c_{i}\nabla\Psi_{j})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\phi_{i}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\phi_{i}^{j}\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta\Phi\|_{L^{3}}+\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{3}}\|\Delta\Psi_{j}\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}+C\|c_{i}\|_{H^{1}}.

Since
H^{2}
is a Banach Algebra, it holds that

(186)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\nabla\Phi\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}.

Consequently, we infer that

(187)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|(\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}[\phi_{j}],-\Delta\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}+1)+\|c_{i}\|_{H^{1}}\right)\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\|\Delta\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{H^{1}}+1\right).

Since
(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})\in X
, there exists a constant
C(R)
depending only on
R
such that

(188)

\left(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{H^{1}}+1\right)^{2}\leq C(R).

Thus,

(189)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}Trace((\mathcal{A}_{w_{0}}-D\Delta)Q_{n})ds

\displaystyle\geq CDn^{2}t-\frac{CDn^{2}}{2}t-Cn\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho+\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\|c_{i}\|_{H^{1}}+1\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geq CDn^{2}t-C(R)nt\geq CDn^{2}t

provided that
n\geq C(R)
.
∎

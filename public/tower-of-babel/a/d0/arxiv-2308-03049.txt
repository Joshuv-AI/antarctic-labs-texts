Constructing Displacement Vectors
Alon Agin

Abstract
Let v → ∈ ℝ 2 ∖ ℚ 2 \vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2} ,let ‖ ⋅ ‖ \left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert be an arbitrary norm on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , and let ( q n , p → n ) n = 0 ∞ ⊂ ℕ × ℤ 2 \big(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n}\big)_{n=0}^{\infty}\\ \subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2} be the best approximation vectors sequence of v → \vec{v} with respect to ‖ ⋅ ‖ \left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert . We define the nth long displacement vector of v → \vec{v} to be β → n := q n + 1 ​ ( q n ​ v → − p → n ) \vec{\beta}_{n}:=\sqrt{q_{n+1}}\,(q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n}) and prove the existence of long displacement vectors who have non-typical properties – focusing on their length, direction, and congruence class.

Introduction

Throughout this paper bold symbols and texts are used for definitions.

For
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and arbitrary norm
\lVert\cdot\lVert
,
denote
\bm{\langle\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}}
:= min
\{\,\lVert\vec{v}-\vec{p}\lVert\,\,\mid\vec{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,\}
.

Using Minkowski’s Convex Body Theorem, one deduces a generalised Dirichlet Theorem – there exists a minimal positive constant
\gamma_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
such that for all
1<T\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq q\leq T
such that
T^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\leq\gamma_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
.

One then easily concludes that the inequality
q^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\leq\gamma_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
has infinitely many solutions for
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

Define
the best approximation vectors sequence of

\vec{v}
(with respect to
\lVert\cdot\lVert
) to be a sequence

\big(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n}\big)_{n=0}^{\infty}=\big(q_{n}(\vec{v}),\vec{p}_{n}(\vec{v})\big)_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that:

•

1=q_{0}<q_{1}<...<q_{n}<...

•

q_{n}=\min\,\,\{q\in\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}<\langle q_{n-1}\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\}

•

\vec{p}_{n}
realises
\langle q_{n}\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
. I.e.
\langle q_{n}\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}=\lVert q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n}\lVert.

Notice that the vector
\vec{p}_{n}
is not uniquely defined. Nevertheless, by discreteness of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, this ambiguity is relevant only for (at most) a finite number of indices. Hence, all best approximations sequences agree starting from some index.

Consequently, we refer to all of these sequences as if they were unique.

Going back to Dirichlet Theorem, we now conclude that for all
n\geq 0

q_{n}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\,\lVert q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n}\lVert\,<q_{n+1}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\,\lVert q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n}\lVert\,\leq\,\gamma_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}.

(1)

This leads us to define natural objects for the study of Diophantine approximations. If
\big(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n}\big)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is the best approximations sequence of
\vec{v}
(w.r.t
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) then define

the nth short displacement vector
:=
q_{n}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,(q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n})

the nth long displacement vector
:=
q_{n+1}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,(q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n})
.

Note that the long and short displacement vectors have the same direction, but different length.

Many of the classical Diophantine approximations results obtained in the last century can be phrased in terms of these displacement vectors. We denote

\bm{\vec{\alpha}_{n}(\vec{v})}:=\vec{\alpha}_{n}(\vec{v},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)=q_{n}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,(q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n})

\bm{\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})}:=\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)=q_{n+1}^{\frac{1}{d}}\,(q_{n}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{n})

keeping in mind these vectors are norm dependent.

For the case
d=1
, properties of the sequences of the short and long displacement vectors have been studied by many, especially in the context of continued fractions. For higher dimensions, Davenport and Schmidt showed in
[
DS70
]
that for the maximum norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
, for Lebesgue almost every
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert_{\infty}=1
. This result has been generalised for arbitrary norms gradually, culminating with Kleinbock and Rao who recently showed in
[
KR20
]
that for an arbitrary norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
, for almost every
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert=\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
(”almost all vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are not Dirichlet-improvable”). For the lower bound, Chevallier showed in
[
Che01
]
that for almost every
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert=0
.

Regarding direction, Rogers observed in
[
Rog51
]
that for the maximum norm, two consecutive displacement vectors cannot lie in the same quadrant.

Moshchevitin showed in
[
Mos00
]
that this result cannot be generalised to arbitrary norms.

For the case of
d=1
, Bosma, Jager and Wiedijk showed in
[
BJW83
]
that there exist probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that for Lebesgue almost every
v\in\mathbb{R}
the sequence of short displacement vectors
(\alpha_{n}(v))_{n=0}^{\infty}
equidistributes with respect to
\mu_{1}
, and the sequence of long displacement vectors
(\beta_{n}(v))_{n=0}^{\infty}
equidistributes with respect to
\mu_{2}
. This result has been generalised recently by Shapira and Weiss in
[
SW22
]
for an arbitrary dimension and norm for the case of the short displacement vectors (with the measure
\mu_{1}
being norm dependent). Also in
[
SW22
]
, Shapira and Weiss generalised a distribution theorem by Moeckel
[
Moe82
]
regarding congruence properties of the sequence
(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n})
. Informally, they showed that for a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the
(d+1)
integer tuples
\,(q_{n},p_{n,1},...\,,p_{n,d})
are distributed uniformly between residues modulo
m
which are ”valid” (for example need to have
\gcd\,(q_{n},p_{n,1},...\,,p_{n,d})=1
), independently of the norm under consideration.

So we have some understanding of how a typical sequence of the form
(\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v}))_{n=0}^{\infty}
behaves. What about non-typical behaviour?

Define
the Dirichlet spectrum of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) to be

\displaystyle\bm{\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}}:=

\displaystyle\,\{\,\limsup\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\,T^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\bigl(\min_{1\leq q\leq T}\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\bigr)\mid\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}\,\}\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\{\,\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,\,\mid\,\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}\,\}.

By (
1
) we know
\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}\subseteq[0,\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}]
. For the case
d=1
, by many works on continued fractions, we know that the spectrum has quite a complicated structure which we still don’t understand completely (see e.g
[
AS13
]
).

In
[
AS13
]
, Akhunzhanov and Shatskov were the first to consider higher dimensions. Surprisingly, they showed that for
d=2
and for the Euclidean norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}
we have
\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}}=[0,\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}}]=[0,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}]
. Recently, Schleischitz showed in
[
Sch22
]
that for any
2\leq d
and for the maximum norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
we have
\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}}=[0,\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}}]=[0,1]
. He also showed that if
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is an expanding norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
then there exists a constant
M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
such that
[0,M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}]\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
, where we say
a norm is expanding
if for all
\vec{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all
1\leq j\leq d
we have that
\left\lVert\vec{s}\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert\pi_{j}(\vec{s})\right\rVert
, where
\pi_{j}
is the orthogonal projection to the
j
th coordinate axis. Also recently, Kleinbock and Rao showed in
[
KR22
]
that for
d=2
and an arbitrary norm,
\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
is an accumulation point of
\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
.

In
[
AM22a
]
, Akhunzhanov and Moshchevitin introduced a more delicate notion than the Dirichlet spectrum. We say that
\vec{v}
is
badly approximable
if
\inf\limits_{T\geq 1}\,T^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\bigl(\min_{1\leq q\leq T}\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\bigr)>0
.

Now define
the BA Dirichlet spectrum of

\bm{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) to be

\displaystyle\bm{\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{BA}}:=

\displaystyle\,\{\,\limsup\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\,T^{\frac{1}{d}}\,\bigl(\min_{1\leq q\leq T}\langle q\vec{v}\rangle_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\bigr)\mid\vec{v}\,\,is\,\,badly\,\,approximable\,\,\}\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\{\,\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,\,\mid\vec{v}\,\,is\,\,badly\,\,approximable\,\,\}\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\{\,\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,\,\mid\,\,\inf\limits_{n\geq 1}\,\lVert\vec{\alpha}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,>0\,\}.

Clearly we have
\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{BA}\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
. In
[
AM22a
]
it was shown that for
d=2
and the Euclidean norm
\lVert\cdot\lVert=\lVert\cdot\lVert_{2}
we have that
\mathbb{D}^{BA}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
is dense in
[0,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}]
.

We suggest another refinement of the Dirichlet spectrum. We say that
\bm{\vec{v}}
is Dirichlet converging
(w.r.t)
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) if
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert
exists, and define
the

limiting Dirichlet spectrum of
\bm{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) to be

\bm{\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{\lim}}:=\,\{\,\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\,\lVert\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,\,\mid\,\vec{v}\,\,is\,\,Dirichlet\,\,converging\,\}.

As far as we know, very little is known regarding Dirichlet converging vectors and the limiting Dirichlet spectrum. Again we have
\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{\lim}\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
.

In
[
AS13
]
, Akhunzhanov and Shatskov also proved that
\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}}^{\lim}=[0,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}]
. As far as we know, this is the only result concerning
\mathbb{D}_{d,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{\lim}
for
d\geq 2
.  Furthermore, as far as we know,
[
AS13
]
is the first and only proof that the set of Dirichlet converging vectors is even an infinite set (for the case
d=2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}
).

In
[
AS13
]
, Akhunzhanov and Shatskov actually proved a stronger statement than just
\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}}^{\lim}=\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}}=[0,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}]
. They showed that for the case

d=2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}
one can have a much finer control on the lengths of the long displacement vectors rather than only its limit – controlling it at each step. Explicitly, they proved that if
(I_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of closed, non-degenerate intervals with
I_{n}\subset[0,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}\,]
, then there exists continuum many
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert_{2}\,\in I_{n}
for all
n
.

In this paper we generalise techniques developed by Akhunzhanov and Shatskov in
[
AS13
]
in order to show that for an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, one can construct continuum many vectors
\vec{v}
such that the sequence
(\vec{\beta_{n}}(\vec{v}))_{n=0}^{\infty}
has non-typical properties regarding length, direction, and congruence class.

For
\theta
,
\delta
with
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
and
0<\delta
we define

\bm{\Omega(\theta,\delta)}:=\{\,(\,r\cos(\beta),r\sin(\beta)\,)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid r\in\mathbb{R}\,\,and\,\,\theta-\delta\leq\beta\leq\theta+\delta\,\}.

We say that
\bm{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{@}}
is a best ellipsoid norm for
\bm{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
with parameters
\bm{a,c>0,D\geq 1}
if for all
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
it satisfies

\left\lVert\vec{u}\right\rVert_{@}:=\sqrt{au_{1}^{2}+cu_{2}^{2}}\,\leq\,\lVert\vec{u}\lVert\,\leq\,D\sqrt{au_{1}^{2}+cu_{2}^{2}}=D\left\lVert\vec{u}\right\rVert_{@}

(2)

and if it maximises the value

M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}:=\left(\frac{4ac}{4D^{2}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(3)

In this paper we prove the following:

.

Let
\lVert\cdot\lVert
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then there exist an explicit constant
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}>0
such that the following holds:

Let
\theta
,

with

and
\,0<\delta
.

Let
(I_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of closed non-degenerate intervals with
I_{n}\subset[0,M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}]
for all n.

Let

, and let
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\{0,1,...,m-1\}
be a sequence of residues mod
m
such that

for all

, where

,

.

Then there exist continuum many
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with best approximations sequence

=

such that the following hold:

1.

\lVert\,\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\lVert\,\,\in I_{n}
for all
0\leq n
.

2.

\vec{\beta}_{n}(\vec{v})\in\Omega(\theta,\delta)
for all
0\leq n
.

3.

arithmetical control:

for all

.

\vec{v}

\{(q_{n-2},\vec{p}_{n-2}),(q_{n-1},\vec{p}_{n-1}),(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n})\}
form a basis of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
for all

, and if
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{@}
is a best ellipsoid norm for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert

Remark 1.
By equation (
1
) we have
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}\leq\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
, and so
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
is best possible if
\lVert\cdot\lVert
is induced from an inner product induced by a diagonal matrix; when
\left\lVert(x,y)\right\rVert=\sqrt{ax^{2}+cy^{2}}
. For example, if
\lVert\cdot\lVert
is the standard Euclidean norm then indeed
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}=\gamma_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}
.

In short, this follows from the equality

\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}=2\,\,\sqrt{\delta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}\mathbb{B}^{-1}}
, where
\delta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
is the packing density of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
and
\mathbb{B}
is the volume of the unit ball of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. As the packing density of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to the Euclidean norm is
\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{12}}
, the packing density is invariant under linear mappings, and the area of the ellipse defined by
\{(u_{1},u_{2})\,|\,au_{1}^{2}+cu_{2}^{2}=1\}
is equal to
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{4ac}}
, we get that if
\left\lVert(x,y)\right\rVert=\sqrt{ax^{2}+cy^{2}}
then
\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}=(\frac{4ac}{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Plugging
D=1
in (
3
), we get
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}=\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
.

Remark 2.
We phrase result (
3
) in terms of the sequence of denominators
(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, but one can formulate a similar statement regarding any one of the two coordinates of the sequence
(\vec{p}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Remark 3.
As both
\{(q_{n-2},\vec{p}_{n-2}),(q_{n-1},\vec{p}_{n-1}),(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n})\}
and

\{(q_{n-3},\vec{p}_{n-3}),(q_{n-2},\vec{p}_{n-2}),(q_{n-1},\vec{p}_{n-1})\}
form a basis of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we have that
(q_{n},\vec{p}_{n})=s(q_{n-1},\vec{p}_{n-1})+i(q_{n-2},\vec{p}_{n-2})\pm(q_{n-3},\vec{p}_{n-3})
for some
s,i\in\mathbb{Z}
, hence the condition
z_{n}\in\{sz_{n-1}+iz_{n-2}\pm z_{n-3}\mod{m}\,|\,\,i,s\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is optimal within this kind of a construction. It
would be interesting to prove a similar statement with
q_{n}
being any residue mod
m
, but to do so one cannot have that every 3 consecutive best approximation vectors form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.

Corollary 1
.

The Dirichlet and the limiting Dirichlet spectrum of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(with respect to arbitrary norm) always contain a segment.

In detail, if
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then there exist
M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}>0
such that

[0,M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}]\subseteq\mathbb{D}^{\lim}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
. If
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{@}
is a best ellipsoid norm for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
then
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
is the same as in (
3
). If
\left\lVert(x,y)\right\rVert=\sqrt{ax^{2}+cy^{2}}
then
M_{\lVert\cdot\lVert}
is best possible and we have
\mathbb{D}_{2,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}=[0,\gamma_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}]
. In particular, the results in table (
4
) hold. ∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then the set of Dirichlet converging vectors (w.r.t
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
) is uncountable.∎

We present another corollary of Theorem 1 which is formulated in the language of dynamics on the space of lattices.

For
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by

\bm{\left\lVert(\vec{x},z)\right\rVert^{*}}:=\max\{\,\left\lVert\vec{x}\right\rVert,|z|\,\}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{d+1}
be the space of lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of covolume 1, and for a lattice
L\in\mathbb{X}_{d+1}
we define
Minkowski’s first successive minima
(w.r.t
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*}
) to be

\bm{\lambda_{1}(L,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*})}:=\min\{\,\left\lVert\vec{u}\right\rVert^{*}\,|\,\vec{0}\neq\vec{u}\in L\}
, which is well defined by discreteness.

For
\varepsilon>0
define

\bm{\mathbb{K}_{\varepsilon}^{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*}}}:=\{L\in\mathbb{X}_{d+1}\,|\,\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{1}(L,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*})\,\}
.

In particular, by Mahler’s compactness criterion we have that
\{\mathbb{K}_{\varepsilon}^{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*}}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is an exhaustion by compact sets of
\mathbb{X}_{d+1}
.

For
t\geq 0
, we denote by
g_{t}
the diagonal
(d+1)\times(d+1)
matrix

\bm{g_{t}}:=\,
diag
\,(e^{t},\,...\,,e^{t},e^{-dt})
.

From a dynamical point of view, Theorem 1 allows us to construct 3-dimensional lattices such that the trajectory
g_{t}L
has, in some sense, prescribed geometry. For example, it allows us to deduce the following:

Corollary 3
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Then for
M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
as in (
3
), for all
r\in[0,M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{\frac{2}{3}}]
there exist continuum many lattices
L\in\mathbb{X}_{3}
such that

r=\min\,\{\,\varepsilon>0\,\,|\,\,\,g_{t}L\notin\mathbb{K}_{\varepsilon}^{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{*}}\,for\,\,all\,\,t\,\,large\,\,enough\}.

Proof.
For
\vec{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
define
l_{\vec{v}}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-v_{1}\\
0&1&-v_{2}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\,L_{\vec{v}}:=l_{\vec{v}}\,\mathbb{Z}^{3}\in\mathbb{X}_{3}.

Let
r\in[0,M_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{\frac{2}{3}}]
, and by Theorem 1 let
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be one of the uncountably many vectors such that for all
n\geq 0
we have

(see

]
chapter 2).

It turns out that the sequence

is the sequence of all local maxima of

, with the exception that
\lVert q_{0}\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{0}\lVert=\lVert\vec{v}-\vec{p}_{0}\lVert
might also be a maximum at the beginning of the trajectory which correspond to

in the case that

In particular for all

we have

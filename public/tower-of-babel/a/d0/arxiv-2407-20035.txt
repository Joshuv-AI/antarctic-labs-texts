Abstract
. We study some problems on self similar solutions to the Fujita equation when p > ( n + 2 ) / ( n − 2 ) p>(n+2)/(n-2) , especially, the characterization of constant solutions by the energy. Motivated by recent advances in mean curvature flows, we introduce the notion of F − F- functional, F F -stability and entropy for solutions of supercritical Fujita equation. Using these tools, we prove that among bounded positive self similar solutions, the constant solution has the lowest entropy. Furthermore, there is also a gap between the entropy of constant and non-constant solutions. As an application of these results, we prove that if p > ( n + 2 ) / ( n − 2 ) p>(n+2)/(n-2) , then the blow up set of type I blow up solutions is the union of a ( n − 1 ) − (n-1)- rectifiable set and a set of Hausdorff dimension at most n − 3 n-3 .

1.
Introduction

Consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p>1
and
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is well known (see
[
11
]
) that solutions of (
1.1
) may blow up in a finite time. Here a solution
u(x,t)
is said to blow up in a finite time
T
if
u(x,t)
satisfies (
1.1
) and

\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}=\infty.

A point
x_{0}
is called a blow-up point if there exist sequences

\{x_{k}\}
and
\{t_{k}\}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{k}=x_{0},\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}t_{k}=T,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|u(x_{k},y_{k})|=+\infty.

The set
\Sigma
consisting of
all the blow-up points is termed the blow-up set. For any
x_{0}\in\Sigma
,
\mathcal{T}(u,x_{0})
is the set of blow-up limits of
u
at
x_{0}
. In the setting of geometric measure theory,
\mathcal{T}(u,(x_{0},T))
is usually called the set of tangent functions.

The finite time blow up is said to be of type I if

\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}<+\infty,

and of type II if

\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}=+\infty,

where
T
is the maximal existence time of the
L^{\infty}
solution
u
.

In a series of papers, Giga and Kohn
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
studied the asymptotic behavior of blow-up solutions to (
1.1
) when
1<p<p_{s}(n)
, where

p_{s}(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}+\infty,&\text{if}~1\leq n\leq 2,\\
\frac{n+2}{n-2},&\text{if}~n\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

In order to analyzing (
1.1

w(y,\tau)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad x=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}y,\quad T-t=e^{-\tau}.

If

satisfies (
1.1
), then
w
satisfies

\partial_{\tau}w-\Delta w+\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla w+\frac{1}{p-1}w-|w|^{p-1}w=0.

(1.2)

In particular, if
w
is a stationary solution of (
1.2
), then
w
satisfies

\Delta w-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla w-\frac{1}{p-1}w+|w|^{p-1}w=0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.3)

It is clear that (
1.3
) has three constant solutions

\kappa:=(\frac{1}{p-1})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Giga and Kohn
[
13

proved that if

and

is a finite blow up solution of (
1.1
)
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|u(x,t)|<\infty.

(1.4)

Then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-}}u(x_{0}+(T-t)y,t)=0\ \text{or}\ \pm\kappa

uniformly for

\left(\frac{n}{p+1}+\frac{2-n}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy=0

where

is the Gaussian. Second the following Giga-Kohn energy functional

E[w](\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\rho dy-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy

is monotonically decreasing for bounded solutions of (
1.2

In
[
14

, Giga and Kohn proved that if
1<p<p_{s}(n),u_{0}\geq 0

[

Due to the above mentioned results, the blow up phenomenon of (

1.1.
Setting and main results

For any bounded smooth function, let

E(w)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}\rho dy-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy

Since

Theorem 1.1

Assume

1.2.
Idea of the proof:
F
-functional and entropy

For a hypersurface
\Sigma
of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the entropy is defined by

\lambda(\Sigma)=\sup(4\pi t_{0})^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4t_{0}}}dx.

Here the supremum is taking over all
t_{0}>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
This quantity was introduced by Colding -Minicozzi
[
4
]
. As a consequence of Huisken’s monotonicity formula, it is non-increasing along the mean
curvature flow, thus giving a Lyapunov functional. In
[
5
]
, Colding–
Ilmanen–Minicozzi-White proved that within the closed smooth self-shrinking solutions of the mean curvature flow in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, not only does the round sphere
have the lowest entropy, but also there is a
gap to the second lowest. Based on this result, they conjectured that, for
2\leq n\leq 6
, the
round sphere minimizes the entropy among all closed hypersurfaces. Using a cleverly constructed weak mean curvature flow that ensured
the extinction time singularity was of a special type, this conjecture was verified by Bernstein and Wang
[
1
]
.

As pointed by Velázquez in
[
20
]
, the structure of singularities which arise in
mean curvature flow is strikingly similar to those appearing in (
1.1
). Because of this reason, we will borrow some ideas from
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
to consider bounded solutions of (
1.3
). Inspired by the program developed by Colding and Minicozzi
[
4
,
5
]
, we will introduce the notation of
F-
functional and entropy. In the setting of (
1.1
), for a bounded
C^{1}
function
w
, the
F-
functional is defined by

\displaystyle F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

(1.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy,

where for any
(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)
,

G(y,t)=(-4\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{|y|^{2}}{{4t}}}.

In particular,

G(y,-1)=\rho(y)=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{{4}}}.

The motivation of defining
F-
functional in this way comes from Giga-Kohn’s monotonicity formula (see
[
13
, Proposition 3]
). The main property of these functionals is that a bounded function is a critical point of
F_{x_{0},t_{0}}
if and only if it is the time
t=t_{0}
slice of a self similar solution of (
1.1
). Using the
F-
functionals, we can also define the entropy
\lambda(w)
of a bounded smooth function to be the supremum of the
F_{x_{0},t_{0}}
functionals

\lambda(w)=\sup\limits_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t_{0}\in(-\infty,0)}F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w).

(1.10)

Similarly we can define
F-
stable and entropy-stable solutions. Under some conditions, we show that these two definitions are equivalent. Using this fact, we can perturb a bounded non-constant positive solution of (
1.3
), while reducing the entropy and making the solution of (
1.2
) starting at this perturbed function blows up at a finite time. Finally, we use an induction argument to show that the minimizer of
\lambda(w)
among
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}
is attained by the constant. An entropy gap (or energy gap) is also obtained.

Finally, we point out even though Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
starting

\mathcal{S}_{0}

.

3.2.

In this section, we will prove that

is a critical point of

Proposition 3.3
.

A bounded smooth function

is a critical point of

if and only if

is a solution to the equation

(3.2)

Obviously, it is sufficient to prove this for
x_{0}=0
and
t_{0}=-1

First assume

is a critical point of

and

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi\rho dy+\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w\phi\rho dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p-1}w\phi\rho dy.

Thus

It follows that

satisfies (

3.2
) with

This is equivalent to the requirement that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\frac{p+1}{2(p-1)}h\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy+\frac{h}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy

\displaystyle-\frac{h}{(p-1)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}\rho dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi)\rho dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w\phi\rho dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p-1}w\phi dy

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\left[\frac{nh}{2}+\frac{y\cdot y_{0}}{2}-\frac{h|y|^{2}}{4}\right]\rho dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\left[\frac{nh}{2}+\frac{y\cdot y_{0}}{2}-\frac{h|y|^{2}}{4}\right]\rho dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}\left[\frac{nh}{2}+\frac{y\cdot y_{0}}{2}-\frac{h|y|^{2}}{4}\right]\rho dy.

Multiplying both sides of (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{2-n}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy-\frac{n}{2(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}\rho dy

\displaystyle+\frac{n}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}w^{2}\rho dy-\frac{1}{2(p+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy.

For

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{i}|\nabla w|^{2}\rho dy+\frac{1}{4(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{i}w^{2}\rho dy-\frac{1}{2(p+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{i}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy.

It follows that for any

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s(f+\xi)))|_{s=0}<0

for any choice of
h
and
y_{0}
.

In view of Theorem
6.1
, it will be natural to ask whether the constant solution is
F-
stable. By the second variation formula, it is clear that
0
is
F-
stable.
Next, we will show if
w
is the positive solution of (
2.1
), then the only way to decrease the
F_{0,1}
functional is to translate in space; this will not be used elsewhere.

Proposition 6.3
.

If
w
is the positive constant solution of (
2.1
), then for any function
\phi
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{i}\phi\rho dy=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots n,

there exist
y_{0},h
such that

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}\geq 0.

Proof.

By the assumption,

w=\kappa=\left(\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Plugging this into (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}

(6.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\rho dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi^{2}\rho dy+\frac{2hw}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\rho dy-\frac{h^{2}w^{2}}{(p-1)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho dy

Let
L_{0}
be the operator defined by

L_{0}\psi=\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi.

For the operator
L_{0}
, we have the following classical result on its eigenvalues.

Lemma 6.4
.

The eigenvalues of the operator
L_{0}
are given by

\lambda_{k}=-|\alpha|/2,\quad\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n}),

where
\alpha_{i}
is a nonnegative integer for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
a
be a constant such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi-a)\rho dy=0

(6.6)

and let
\phi_{0}(y)=\phi(y)-a
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi_{0}|^{2}\rho dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}^{2}\rho dy

\displaystyle+2\frac{h}{p-1}(\frac{1}{p-1})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}a-\frac{h^{2}}{(p-1)^{2}}(\frac{1}{p-1})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}-a^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\frac{h}{p-1}(\frac{1}{p-1})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-a)^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi_{0}|^{2}\rho dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{0}^{2}\rho dy.

If we choose
h
so that

\frac{h}{p-1}(\frac{1}{p-1})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-a=0,

then Lemma
6.4
implies

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}\geq 0.

Hence the proof of Proposition
6.3
is complete.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

A similar result holds for
-\kappa
.

7.
Entropy

The entropy
\lambda(w)
of a bounded smooth function
w
is defined to be

\lambda(w)=\sup\limits_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t_{0}\in(-\infty,0)}F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w).

Remark 7.1
.

The notion of entropy is used by Colding and Minicozzi (see
[
4
]
) in mean curvature flow to classify generic singularities. This quantity can be used to measure the complexity of self shrinkers. As discussed in
[
4
]
, the advantage of the entropy functional is that it is invariant under dilations, rotations, or translations of
w
. The main disadvantage of the entropy is that for a variation
w_{s}
, it need not depend smoothly on
s
.

Definition 7.2
.

We will say that a bounded function
w
is entropy stable if it is a local minimum for the entropy functional.

7.1.
The entropy is achieved for bounded self similar solutions

Although in the definition of entropy, the supremum is over a noncompact space-time domain, the next lemma shows that for a bounded solution of (
2.1
), the function
(x_{0},t_{0})\rightarrow F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)
has a global maximum at
x_{0}=0,t_{0}=-1
.

Lemma 7.3
.

If
w
is a bounded solution of (
2.1
), then
\lambda(w)
is achieved at
(0,-1)
.

Proof.

For any bounded solution of (
2.1
), set
u(x,t)=(-t)^{-1/(p-1)}w(x/\sqrt{-t})
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)
and
T>t
,

\displaystyle E(s;x,T,u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-s)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(y,T+s)|^{2}G(y-x,s)dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}(-s)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(y,T+s)|^{p+1}G(y-x,s)dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}(-s)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y,T+s)^{2}G(y-x,s)dy.

is nonincreasing with respect to
s
in
(-\infty,-(T-t)]
. By the definition of
u
, we have

\displaystyle E(s;x,T,u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{s}{T+s}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w(y)|^{2}G\left(y-\frac{x}{\sqrt{-(T+s)}},-\frac{s}{T+s}\right)dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}\left(\frac{s}{T+s}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(y)|^{p+1}G\left(y-\frac{x}{\sqrt{-(T+s)}},-\frac{s}{T+s}\right)dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}\left(\frac{s}{T+s}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(y)^{2}G\left(y-\frac{x}{\sqrt{-(T+s)}},-\frac{s}{T+s}\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\frac{x}{\sqrt{-(T+s)}},-\frac{s}{T+s}}(w).

We take

T-t=1,\quad x=x_{0}\sqrt{-\frac{1}{t_{0}}},\quad T=1+\frac{1}{t_{0}}.

Since
E(s;x,t,u)
is nonincreasing with respect to
s
in
(-\infty,-1]
, then

E(-1;x,T,u)=F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)\leq\lim_{s\rightarrow-\infty}E(s;x,T,u)=F_{0,-1}(w).

Since
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0)
can be arbitrary, we conclude that
\lambda(w)
achieves its maximum at
(0,-1)
.
∎

By the definition of the weighted energy
E(w)
and the
F-
functional, it is easy to see that
E(w)=F_{0,-1}(w)
. As a direct consequence of Lemma
7.3
, we have the following.

Corollary 7.4
.

If
w
is a bounded solution of (
2.1
), then
\lambda(w)=E(w).

Lemma
7.3
only tells us the location where the
F-
functional achieves the maximum. For our application later, we also need to show that if
w
is a bounded solution of (
2.1
) and

\nabla w\cdot y_{0}\neq 0,\quad\text{for any}~y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

then
(x_{0},t_{0})\to F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)
has a strict global maximum at
x_{0}=0,t_{0}=-1
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose
w
is a bounded solution of (
2.1
) and for any
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
,
w
is not translation invariant in the
y_{0}
direction.
Then for every
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\sup\{F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w):|x_{0}|+|\log t_{0}|>\epsilon\}\leq\lambda(w)-\delta.

(7.1)

Proof.

Since
w
satisfies (
2.1
),
(0,1)
is a critical point of the
F
-functional. Moreover, the second variation
formula with
\phi\equiv 0
gives that the second derivative of
F_{x(s),t(s)}(w)
at
s=0
along the paths

x(s)=sy_{0},\quad t(s)=-(1+sh)

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla w\cdot y_{0})(\nabla w\cdot y_{0})\rho dy-h^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{p-1}w+\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w\right)^{2}\rho dy.

This expression is clear non-positive. Moreover, the last line vanishes only when

h=0,~~~\nabla w\cdot y_{0}\equiv 0

or

\nabla w\cdot y_{0}\equiv 0,\quad\frac{1}{p-1}w+\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w\equiv 0.

If
y_{0}\neq 0,h=0
, then in both cases
w
is indeed a function in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. By our assumption, this can not happen. If
y_{0}=0,h\neq 0
, then

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w+s\phi))|_{s=0}=0

implies

\frac{1}{p-1}w+\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w\equiv 0.

This is equivalent to
w
is
2/(p-1)-
homogeneous. Since we have assumed
w
is a bounded solution of (
2.1
), then
w\equiv 0
, which contradicts our assumption that
w
is not translation invariant in any direction again. Therefore, we have shown that the second derivative at
(0,1)
is strictly negative. In particular, the function
(x_{0},t_{0})\rightarrow F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)
has a strict local maximum at
(0,1)
. Hence for every
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small there exists
\delta>0
such that

\max_{(x,t)\in\{|x|+|\log t|=\epsilon\}}F_{x,t}(w)<\lambda(w)-\delta.

For every
(x_{0},t_{0})
such that
|x_{0}|+|\log t_{0}|>\epsilon
, it follows from the proof of Lemma
7.3
that for every
s<-1
,

F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)\leq F_{\frac{x_{0}}{\sqrt{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}},-\frac{st_{0}}{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}}(w).

(7.2)

Since

\lim_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{x_{0}}{\sqrt{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}}=0

and

\lim_{s\rightarrow-\infty}-\frac{st_{0}}{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}=-1.

Then there exists
\tilde{s}
such that

\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\sqrt{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}},-\frac{st_{0}}{t_{0}+1+t_{0}s}\right)\in\{|x|+|\log t|=\epsilon\}.

By (
7.2
), we know that
F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)\leq\lambda(w)-\delta
. Hence (
7.1
) holds.
∎

7.2.
The equivalence of F-stability and entropy-stability

Our next objective is to prove that if
w
is a bounded non-constant solution of (
2.1
) and there exists a positive constant
c
such that

|w|\leq c(1+|y|)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7.3)

then
F-
stability and entropy stability are equivalent for
w
. In the course of the proof, we need a result concerning the first eigenfunction of the operator

L\psi=\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{p-1}\psi+p|w|^{p-1}\psi.

Lemma 7.6
.

Assume
w
is a bounded non-constant solution of (
2.1
). Let
\lambda_{1}
be the first eigenvalue of the eigenvalue problem

\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{p-1}\psi+p|w|^{p-1}\psi+\lambda\psi=0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.4)

If
f
is a positive eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{1}
, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(1+|y|)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}f+(1+|y|)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}+1}|\nabla f|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.5)

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}
be the smallest eigenvalue of (
7.4
) and let
f
be a positive eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{1}
. Then
f
satisfies

\Delta f-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla f-\frac{1}{p-1}f+p|w|^{p-1}f+\lambda_{1}f=0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.6)

Since
w
is a bounded non-constant solution of (
2.1
),
\lambda_{1}<1
. Hence
(
7.3
) implies there exists a positive constant
R
such that that

-\frac{2p}{p-1}(n-2-\frac{2p}{p-1})|y|^{-2}+1+p|w|^{p-1}+\lambda_{1}<0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(0).

(7.7)

By taking
R
large enough, we may also assume that

-\frac{1}{p-1}+p|w|^{p-1}+\lambda_{1}<0,\quad\text{in}~|y|\geq B_{R}.

(7.8)

By standard elliptic regularity theory, there exists a positive constant
M
such that

|f|<MR^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~B_{R}.

(7.9)

For any
k\geq 1
, consider the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta g_{k}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla g_{k}-\frac{1}{p-1}g_{k}+p|w|^{p-1}g_{k}+\lambda_{1}g_{k}=0,&\quad\text{in}~B_{R+k}\backslash B_{R},\\
g_{k}=f,&\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R},\\
g_{k}=M|y|^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}},&\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R+k},\end{array}\right.

(7.10)

Since we have assumed that (
7.8
) holds, the zeroth order term of the second order elliptic equation in (
7.10
) has a negative sign. Notice that

(L+\lambda_{1})|y|^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}}=[-\frac{2p}{p-1}(n-2-\frac{2p}{p-1})|y|^{-2}+1+p|w|^{p-1}+\lambda_{1}]|y|^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}}.

Thus, it follows from (
7.7
),
[
16
, Theorem 8.3]
and the maximum principle that for any
k\geq 1
, (
7.10
) has a unique smooth solution
g_{k}
, which is bounded above by
M|y|^{-(2p)/(p+1)}
. By using the maximum principle again,
g_{k}
is bounded below by
0
. Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get from the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem that
\{g_{k}\}
converges to some function
g_{\infty}
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R})
, where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta g_{\infty}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla g_{\infty}-\frac{1}{p-1}g_{\infty}+p|w|^{p-1}g_{\infty}+\lambda_{1}g_{\infty}=0,&\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R},\\
g_{\infty}=f,&\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R}.\end{array}\right.

(7.11)

Moreover,

|g_{\infty}|\leq M|y|^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}.

(7.12)

Multiplying both sides of (
7.11
) and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|\nabla g_{\infty}|^{2}\rho dy<\infty.

(7.13)

We claim that
g_{\infty}=f
.

Indeed, by denoting
h:=f-g_{\infty}
, then
h
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta h-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla h-\frac{1}{p-1}h+p|w|^{p-1}h+\lambda_{1}h=0,&\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R},\\
h=0,&\quad\text{on}~\partial B_{R}.\end{array}\right.

(7.14)

Let
\phi_{k}
be a smooth cutoff function such that
\phi_{k}=1
in
B_{k}
,
\phi_{k}=0
outsider
B_{2k}
and
|\nabla\phi_{k}|\leq C/k
. Multiplying both sides of (
7.14
) by
h\phi_{k}^{2}\rho
and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|\nabla h|^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\rho dy-\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}h^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\rho dy

(7.15)

\displaystyle+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|w|^{p-1}h^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\rho dy+\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}h^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\rho dy

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}h\phi_{k}\nabla h\cdot\nabla\phi_{k}\rho dy.

Recall that we have assumes that (
7.8
) holds, so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|\nabla h|^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\rho dy\leq-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}h\phi_{k}\nabla h\cdot\nabla\phi_{k}\rho dy.

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}}|\nabla h|^{2}\rho dy=0.

Hence
h=0
and the claim is proved.

By (
7.9
) and (
7.12
), there exists a positive constant
C
such that

|f|\leq C(1+|y|)^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.16)

Let

u(x,t)=(-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}w(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}),~~~~\tilde{f}(x,t)=(-t)^{\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{p-1}}f(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}}).

Then
\tilde{f}
satisfies

\partial_{t}\tilde{f}=\Delta\tilde{f}+p|u|^{p-1}\tilde{f},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0).

Combining (
7.3
) and (
7.16
), we see there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p
,
w
and
f
such that

|\nabla\tilde{f}(x,t)|+|\nabla^{2}\tilde{f}(x,t)|\leq C(-t)^{\lambda_{1}},\quad\text{in}~B_{1}\times(-2,0).

(7.17)

This is equivalent to

(1+|y|)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}+1}|\nabla f|+(1+|y|)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}+2}|\nabla^{2}f|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}.

(7.18)

By (
7.16
) and (
7.18
), we see (
7.5
) holds.
∎

Now we can prove the main result in this section.

Theorem 7.7
.

Assume
w
is a bounded non-constant solution of (
2.1
). If there exists a positive constant
C
such that

|w|\leq C(1+|y|)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n},

then there is a variation
w_{s}
with
w_{0}=w
such that

\lambda(w_{s})<\lambda(w)

for all
s\neq 0
. In particular,
w
is not entropy stable.

Proof.

Let us take a one-parameter variation
w_{s}=w+sf
for
s\in[-2\epsilon,2\epsilon]
, where
f
is the first eigenfunction of the operator

L\psi=\Delta\psi-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{p-1}\psi+p|w|^{p-1}\psi.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
f>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we know that for any
x(s)
and
t(s)
with
x(0)=0
and
t(0)=-1
,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w_{s}))|_{s=0}<0.

(7.19)

We will use this to prove that
w
is also not entropy stable. In order to get this, we define a function
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{-}\times[-2\epsilon,2\epsilon]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

G(x_{0},t_{0},s)=F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w_{s}).

(7.20)

We will show that there exists some
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that if
s\neq 0
and
|s|\leq\epsilon_{1}
, then

\lambda(w_{s})\equiv\sup\limits_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t_{0}\in(-\infty,0)}G(x_{0},t_{0},s)<G(0,-1,0)=\lambda(w).

(7.21)

This will give the theorem with
w_{s}
for any
s\neq 0
in
(-\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{1})
.

The proof of (
7.21
) will be divided into the following six steps.

Step 1:

G
has a strict local maximum at
(0,-1,0)
.

Since
w
is a solution of the equation (
1.3
), it follows from Proposition
3.3
that
\nabla G
vanishes at
(0,-1,0)
.
Given
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, the second derivative of

G(sy_{0},-(1+as),bs)
at
s=0
is just

\frac{\partial}{\partial_{ss}}G(sy_{0},-(1+as),bs)|_{s=0}=b^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{2}}(F_{x(s),t(s)}(w_{s}))|_{s=0}

with

x(s)=s\frac{y_{0}}{b},\quad t(s)=-\left(1+\frac{a}{b}s\right).

Thus the second derivative of
G(sy,1+as,0)
at
s=0
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial_{ss}}G(sy_{0},-(1+as),bs)|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla w\cdot y_{0})(\nabla w\cdot y_{0})\rho dy-\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{p-1}w+\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w\right)^{2}\rho dy.

Similar to the reasoning used in the proof of Lemma
7.5
, it is negative. Thus we conclude that the Hessian of
G
at
(0,1,0)
is negative definite. It follows that
G
has a strict local maximum at
(0,1,0)
. In particular, there exists
\epsilon_{2}\in(0,\epsilon)
such that

G(x_{0},t_{0},s)<G(0,-1,0)

(7.22)

provided that
0<x_{0}^{2}+(\log t_{0})^{2}+s^{2}<\epsilon_{2}^{2}.

Step 2:

G(x_{0},t_{0},0)
has a strict global maximum at
(0,-1)
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\delta>0
such that

G(x_{0},t_{0},0)<G(0,-1,0)-\delta

(7.23)

for all
x_{0},t_{0}
with

\epsilon_{2}^{2}/4<x_{0}^{2}+(\log t_{0})^{2}.

Indeed, we get from the definition of
G(x_{0},t_{0},s)
that
G(x_{0},t_{0},0)=F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)
and
G(0,-1,0)=F_{0,-1}(w)=\lambda(w)
. Thus (
7.23
) is a direct consequence of Lemma
7.5
.

Step 3:

|\partial_{s}G|
is bounded on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{-}\times[-2\epsilon,2\epsilon]
.

By the definition of
G
and the first variation formula, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}G(x_{0},t_{0},s)=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla w\cdot\nabla f)G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

(7.24)

\displaystyle-(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w+sf|^{p-1}(w+sf)fG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(p-1)}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fwG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy.

It is clear that
\partial_{s}G
is continuous in all three variables
x_{0},t_{0}
and
s
. We conclude that
|\partial_{s}G|
is bounded on compact subsets.

Step 4:
Given
M\geq 1
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p,w,M
such that if
|x_{0}|\gg M
, then

G(x_{0},t_{0},s)\leq G(x_{0},t_{0},0)+Cs.

By the definition, we have

\displaystyle G(x_{0},t_{0},s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w+sf|^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w+sf|^{p+1}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(w+sf)^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

(7.25)

\displaystyle+s(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla fG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle-s(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p-1}wfG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{s}{p-1}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}wfG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{s^{2}}{2}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f|^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle-\frac{ps^{2}}{2}(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p-1}f^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle+\frac{s^{2}}{2(p-1)}(-t_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{2}G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle-(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(s,w,f)G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy,

where

F(s,w,f)=\frac{1}{p+1}[|w+sf|^{p+1}-|w|^{p+1}]-s|w|^{p-1}wf-\frac{ps^{2}}{2}|w|^{p-1}f^{2}.

We fix a positive constant
M
such that
M\gg 1
. For any
x_{0}
with
|x_{0}|\gg M
, consider the changing of variable

. Then

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla fG(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla f(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz

(7.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

with

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{|z|\leq\frac{|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz,

(7.27)

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{\frac{|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz,

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{|z|\geq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz.

First, we estimate the term
I_{3}
. If
|z|\geq(3|x_{0}|)/(2\sqrt{-t_{0}})
, then

|x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}z}|\geq\sqrt{-t_{0}}|z|-|x_{0}|\geq\sqrt{-t_{0}}|z|-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{-t_{0}}|z|\geq\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{-t_{0}}|z|.

(7.28)

Therefore,

|x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}z}|\geq\frac{|x_{0}|}{2}\geq\frac{M}{2}.

By (
7.3
), Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
7.6
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{|z|\geq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{|z|\geq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z|^{-\frac{4}{p-1}-2}\rho(z)dz.

Taking (
7.28

C
depending only on
n,p
and
w

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\left\{|z|\geq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|z|^{-\frac{4}{p-1}-2}\rho(z)dz

(7.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|z|^{-\frac{4}{p-1}-2}\rho(z)dz\leq C.

Here we have applied the fact that

.

For the term
I_{1}
, we divide the estimate into two cases. If

(7.30)

On the other hand, if

I_{1}\leq C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}|x_{0}|^{-\frac{4}{p-1}-2}\left(\frac{|x_{0}|}{\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right)^{n}\leq C.

(7.31)

Finally, we estimate
I_{2}
as follows,

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\left\{\frac{|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)\rho(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{16t_{0}}}\int_{\left\{\frac{|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{3|x_{0}|}{2\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right\}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|(x_{0}+\sqrt{-t_{0}}z)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{16t_{0}}}(-t_{0})^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\left\{|y|\leq 4|x_{0}|\right\}}|\nabla w_{0}||\nabla f|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{16t_{0}}}(-t_{0})^{-\frac{n}{2}}|x_{0}|^{n-2-\frac{4}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{|x_{0}|}{\sqrt{-t_{0}}}\right)^{n-2-\frac{4}{p-1}}e^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{16t_{0}}}\leq C.

In conclusion, there exist
M\geq 1
and
C
depending only on
n,p,w_{0}
such that if
|x_{0}|\gg M
or
|t_{0}|\gg M
, then

(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy\leq C.

Other terms can be estimated similarly. Therefore, we conclude that there exists
M\geq 1
and
C
depending only on
n,p,w
such that if
|x_{0}|\gg M
or
|t_{0}|\gg M
, then

F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w+sf)\leq F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(w)+Cs.

Step 5:
Given
M\geq 1
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p,w,M
such that if
|x_{0}|\leq M
and
|t_{0}|\gg M
, then

G(x_{0},t_{0},s)\leq G(x_{0},t_{0},0)+Cs.

Similar to the proof of Step 4, we only show that there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p,w,M
such that

(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy\leq C.

(7.32)

There are three cases:

Case 1:

n-5-4/(p-1)>-1
.

Then (

) and Lemma
7.6
imply

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|y|)^{-4-\frac{4}{p-1}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}}.

Since we have assumed that

, it is clear that (
7.32
) holds.

Case 2:

n-5-4/(p-1)<-1
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w||\nabla f|G(y-x_{0},t_{0})dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\{|y|\leq(-t_{0})^{\alpha(p)}\}}(1+|y|)^{-4-\frac{4}{p-1}}dy

\displaystyle+C(-t_{0})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\int_{\{|y|\geq(-t_{0})^{\alpha(p)}\}}(-4\pi t_{0})^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{|y-x_{0}|^{2}}{4t_{0}}}dy\leq C,

where

\alpha(p):=\frac{n-2-\frac{4}{p-1}}{2(n-4-\frac{4}{p-1})}.

Case 3:

n-5-4/(p-1)=-1
.

This case can be proved in the same way as in Case2.

In conclusion, we have shown that both (
7.32
) and Step 5 hold.

Step 6:
There exists
t_{1}
such that if
t_{1}<t_{0}<0
, then

is strictly less than

.

Indeed, since

are bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

satisfies (

), there exists two constant

independent of

By Corollary
7.4
, we have

\lambda(w)=E(w)=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p+1}\rho dy>0.

Therefore, if
-t_{0}
is small enough, then

.

Step 7:
There exists some
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that if
s\neq 0
and

, then

\lambda(w_{s})\equiv\sup\limits_{x_{0},t_{0}}G(x_{0},t_{0},s)<G(0,-1,0)=\lambda(w).

(7.33)

Let us consider three separate regions depending on the size of

. First, it follows from Step 4, Step 5 and Step 6 that there is some

such that (

Secondly, as long as

∎

8.
The energy of self similar solutions

Results obtained in the previous sections hold for all bounded solutions of (

). In this section, we will turn our attention to functions in

. We will prove that if

) holds, then

Lemma 8.1

(Blow up criteria)
.

Let
w
be a positive solution of

\partial_{\tau}w=\Delta w-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla w-\frac{1}{p-1}w+w^{p},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty).

(8.1)

Assume for any
M>0
, there exists a positive constant
C(M)
such that

w\leq C(M)\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,M].

Then for any
\tau\in[0,+\infty)

(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(y,\tau)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy\leq\kappa.

(8.2)

Proof.

This is exactly
[
18
, Proposition 3.8]
.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

If
w
is a bounded positive solution of the equation (
1.3
) such that (
7.3
) holds. Assume

is the first eigenfunction of the operator

L\psi=\Delta\psi-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{p-1}\psi+pw^{p-1}\psi,

(8.3)

Without loss of generality, we may assume
f
is positive in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{\tau}\tilde{w}=\Delta\tilde{w}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\tilde{w}-\frac{1}{p-1}\tilde{w}+\tilde{w}^{p},\\
\tilde{w}(\cdot,0)=w+sf,\end{array}\right.

(8.4)

where
s
is a small positive constant, then
\tilde{w}
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Assume it is false, then
\tilde{w}
exists in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
. Consider the function

\bar{w}(y,\tau)=w(y)+se^{-\lambda_{1}\tau}f(y),

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of the operator defined in (
8.3
).
Since
w
satisfies (
1.3
),
\bar{w}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{\tau}\bar{w}=\Delta\bar{w}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\bar{w}-\frac{1}{p-1}\bar{w}+w^{p}+spw^{p-1}e^{-\lambda_{1}\tau}f,\\
\tilde{w}(\cdot,0)=w+sf.\end{array}\right.

Notice that for any
a\geq 0,b\geq 0
, we have
(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+pa^{p-1}b
. Thus
\bar{w}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{\tau}\bar{w}\leq\Delta\bar{w}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\bar{w}-\frac{1}{p-1}\bar{w}+\bar{w}^{p},\\
\tilde{w}(\cdot,0)=w+sf.\end{array}\right.

By the maximum principle, we have

\tilde{w}\geq\bar{w},\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty).

In particular, for any
\tau\geq 0
,

\displaystyle(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{w}(y,\tau)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy

(8.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{w}(y,\tau)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(y)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy+(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\lambda_{1}\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy.

Since
\lambda_{1}<-1
, we get from (
8.5
) that

(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{w}(y,\tau)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}dy>\kappa

provided that
\tau
is large enough. By Lemma
8.1
, this is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\tau_{1}
be the first blow up time of
\tilde{w}
, then there exist
R>0
and

such that

\tilde{w}\leq c,\quad\text{in}~(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(0))\times(0,\tau_{1}).

In particular, if we use
\Sigma
to denote the blow up set of

, then

is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
w
satisfies (
7.3
), it follows from Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
7.6
that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(1+|y|)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}w+(1+|y|)^{1+\frac{2}{p-1}}|\nabla w|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

(1+|y|)^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}|f|+(1+|y|)^{1+\frac{2p}{p-1}}|\nabla f|\leq C,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus given
\delta_{0}>0,\epsilon>0
, there exists
R
such that if
|y_{0}|>R
and
(z_{0},\tau_{0})\in B_{\delta_{0}}(y_{0})\times(\tau_{1}-\delta_{0}^{2},\tau_{1}+\delta_{0}^{2})

\mathcal{E}_{(z_{0},\tau_{0})}(w+sf)<\epsilon,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{(z_{0},\tau_{0})}(w+sf)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(w+sf)|^{2}e^{-\frac{|y-z_{0}|^{2}}{4\tau_{0}}}dy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}(\tau_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w+sf|^{p+1}e^{-\frac{|y-z_{0}|^{2}}{4\tau_{0}}}dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}(\tau_{0})^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(w+sf)^{2}e^{-\frac{|y-z_{0}|^{2}}{4\tau_{0}}}dy

∎

Proposition 8.4

Step 3
: There exists a smooth solution

\hat{w}(z,\varsigma)=(1-e^{\tau-\tau_{0}})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}w(y+e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}y_{0},\tau)

Then

Constant solutions have the lowest energy

In this section, we will combine the Federer type dimension reduction arguments with the results obtained in the previous sections to prove that the positive constant solution of (

Lemma 9.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
depends only on

Proof.

It follows from (

E(w)=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{p+1}\rho dy.

(9.1)

For any

, it is clear that there exists a point
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
w(y_{0})=\kappa
. Since

, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that there exists a constant

depending only on
n,p
and

such that

Therefore, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p
and

such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{p+1}\rho dy\geq\int_{B_{r_{1}}(y_{0})}w^{p+1}\rho dy\geq C.

(9.2)

By (

) and (

), we have

E(w)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)C.

(9.3)

Next, since

, then

E(w)=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{p+1}\rho dy\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)m^{p+1}.

(9.4)

Combining (
9.3
) and (

), we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

There exists
w_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,m}
such that

E(w_{0})=\inf_{w\in\mathcal{B}_{n,m}}E(w).

Proof.

Let

be a sequence such that

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}E(w_{i})=\inf_{w\in\mathcal{B}_{n,m}}E(w).

It follows from Lemma

that

are uniformly bounded. By the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem, we know that there exists a function

such that
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}w_{i}=w_{0}
. Since the functions

converge to

uniformly on compact subsets of

, then

. The convergence also implies that

so
w_{0}
attains the minima in
\mathcal{B}_{n,m}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose instead that there is a sequence of self-similar solutions

(10.1)

uniformly on compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

1.1

Let

Proof of (

By the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle E(w)=

\displaystyle 2^{n-3-\frac{4}{p-1}}c(n,p)\Gamma(\frac{n-2-\frac{4}{p-1}}{2}),

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x-1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(x-1-\alpha)}-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)}+\frac{1+\alpha}{x-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x-1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(x-1-\alpha)}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1+\alpha}{\left(x-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}(x-1-\alpha+l)^{-2}-\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}(x+l)^{-2}-\frac{1+\alpha}{\left(x-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\phi(x)=

\displaystyle(x-1-\alpha-\frac{1}{2})\log(x-1-\alpha)-(x-1-\alpha)

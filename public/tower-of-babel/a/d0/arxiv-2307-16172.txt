\displaystyle\Omega_{2n+2}=\{k\in\mathbb{C}|(n+1)\pi-\frac{n\pi}{4}\leq\arg(k-1)\leq(n+1)\pi\},

(4.3)

where
n=0,1
;

\Sigma_{j}=e^{\frac{(j-1)i\pi}{2}+\frac{i\pi}{4}}(\Gamma_{+}),\quad\Gamma_{+}=\{k\in\mathbb{R}|k\geq 1\},\quad j=1,2,3,4,

(4.4)

and denote
\Sigma^{(1)}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{4}\Sigma_{j}

4.1
A mixed
\bar{\partial}
-RH problem and its decomposition

In this part, we open the jump line at
k=1
to make a continuous extension, and the first step is to introduce several new functions:

Proposition 8
.

Define functions
R_{j}\equiv R_{j}(k):\bar{\Omega}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},j=1,2,3,4
with the following boundary values:

\displaystyle R_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-r(k),&k\in\Gamma_{+},\\
-r(1),&k\in\Sigma_{1},\end{array}\right.,\qquad R_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-r(k),&k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\Gamma_{+},\\
-r(1),&k\in\Sigma_{2},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle R_{3}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\bar{r}(k),&k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\Gamma_{+},\\
-\bar{r}(1),&k\in\Sigma_{3},\end{array}\right.,\quad R_{4}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\bar{r}(k),&k\in\Gamma_{+},\\
-\bar{r}(1),&k\in\Sigma_{4},\end{array}\right.

And
R_{j}
satisfies the following estimates:

|\bar{\partial}R_{j}|\lesssim|r^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)|+|k-1|^{-1/2},\ for\ k\in\Omega_{j},\ j=1,4;

(4.15)

|\bar{\partial}R_{j}|\lesssim|r^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)|+|k-1|^{-1/2}+\varphi(\mathrm{Re}k),for\ k\in\Omega_{j},\ j=2,3,

(4.16)

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},[0,1])
is a fixed cut-off function with support near 0.

In a small fixed neighborhood of 0,
for\ k\in\Omega_{j},\ j=2,3,\
we have

|\bar{\partial}R_{j}|\lesssim|k|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\ as\ k\rightarrow 0.

(4.17)

Proof.

We first give the proof of (
4.16
) and take
R_{2}(k)
for example. Here we take the cut-off function
\chi_{0}(x)\in\ C^{\infty}_{0}([0,1])
as
\rho_{0}=1
:

\chi_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|x|\leqslant 1/8,\\
0,&|x|\geqslant 1/4.\end{array}\right.

(4.18)

Define the function
R_{2}
as follows:

R_{2}=R_{21}+R_{22},

(4.19)

where

\displaystyle R_{21}=

\displaystyle\left(r(1)-[1-\chi_{0}(\mathrm{Re}k)]r(\mathrm{Re}k)\right)cos^{2}(2\alpha)-r(1),

(4.20)

\displaystyle R_{22}=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\mathrm{Re}k)cos^{2}(2\alpha)+\frac{i}{2}\rho e^{-i\alpha}sin(2\alpha)cos(2\alpha)\chi_{0}(\alpha)\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)+sin^{2}(2\alpha)\chi_{0}(\alpha)\tilde{f}(\mathrm{Re}k)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\rho e^{-i\alpha}sin^{2}(2\alpha)\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)-\frac{1}{8}\rho^{2}e^{-2i\alpha}sin^{2}(2\alpha)\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k),

and

\tilde{f}(\mathrm{Re}k)=-\chi_{0}(\mathrm{Re}k)r(\mathrm{Re}k).

(4.21)

Denote
k=1+\rho e^{i\alpha}
, then
\rho=|k-1|
and

\bar{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\alpha}(\partial_{\rho}+i\rho^{-1}\partial_{\alpha}).

So we have

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}R_{21}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\chi_{0}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)r(\mathrm{Re}k)cos^{2}(2\alpha)-2ie^{i\alpha}\rho^{-1}\chi_{0}(\mathrm{Re}k)r(\mathrm{Re}k)sin(2\alpha)cos(2\alpha)

(4.22)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(1-\chi_{0}(\mathrm{Re}k))r^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)cos^{2}(2\alpha)+2ie^{i\alpha}\rho^{-1}(r(\mathrm{Re}k)-r(1))sin(2\alpha)cos(2\alpha).

Considering that
r(\mathrm{Re}k),\ \rho^{-1}
is bounded in the support of
\chi_{0}(\mathrm{Re}k)
, the first two terms of (
4.22
) are controlled by a function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},[0,1])
. The last term of (
4.22
) is estimated based on the inequality:

|r(\mathrm{Re}k)-r(1)|\lesssim|\mathrm{Re}k-1|^{1/2}\leqslant\rho^{1/2},

(4.23)

and then

|\bar{\partial}R_{21}|\lesssim\varphi(\mathrm{Re}k)+|r^{\prime}(\mathrm{Re}k)|+|k-1|^{-1/2}.

(4.24)

As for
R_{22}
, it’s obvious that it has the same formula as
R_{11,2}
in the proof of Proposition
3
.
We can similarly get the following results:

|\bar{\partial}R_{11,2}|\lesssim\varphi(\mathrm{Re}k),\ k\in\Omega_{11},\quad|\bar{\partial}R_{11,2}|\lesssim|k|^{2},\quad as\ k\to 0.

(4.25)

Combining all above, we derive (
4.16
) and (
4.17
).

Finally, we give the proof of (
4.15
). Define
R_{1}
by

R_{1}(k)=\left(r(1)-r(\mathrm{Re}k)\right)cos(2\alpha)-r(1),

(4.26)

and

∎

Now we introduce a matrix function by the following transformation

Then

is the solution of a mixed

Find a
2\times 2

is continuous in

\bar{\partial}

4.2
Analysis on the pure RH problem

We first give an estimate for the jump matrix of
M^{R}(k)
as follows:

Proposition 9
.

As
t\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\|V^{(1)}(k)-I\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{(1)})}=O(t^{-1/2}).

(4.36)

Proof.

Take the case in
\Sigma_{1}
for example:

\|V^{(1)}(k)-I\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{1})}=\left(\int_{\Sigma_{1}}|r(1)e^{2it\theta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}(e^{-ctl})^{2}\mathrm{d}l\right)^{1/2}\lesssim t^{-1/2},

(4.37)

where
s=1+le^{\frac{3i}{4}},\ \mathrm{Im}s=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}l.

∎

To solve the RH problem
M^{R}(k)
, we introduce an operator
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}:\ L^{2}(\Sigma^{(1)})\to L^{2}(\Sigma^{(1)})
as:

\mathcal{C}_{\omega}f=\mathcal{C}_{-}(f(V^{(1)}-I)),

(4.38)

where
\mathcal{C}_{-}
is the Cauchy projection operator on
\Sigma^{(1)}
. Based on Proposition
9
, we have

\|\mathcal{C}_{\omega_{e}}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|\mathcal{C}_{-}\|_{L^{2}}\|V^{(1)}-I\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-1/2},

(4.39)

thus there exists
\mu(k)
satisfying

(I-\mathcal{C}_{\omega_{e}})\mu(k)=I.

(4.40)

Therefore, there exists a unique solution
M^{R}(k)
for RH problem
11
represented by

M^{R}(k)=I+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma^{(1)}}\frac{\mu(s)(V^{(1)}(s)-I)}{s-k}\mathrm{d}s

(4.41)

with the following property:

Proposition 10
.

M^{R}(k)
has the asymptotic expansion as
k\to 0
:

M^{R}(k)=M^{R}(0)+M^{R}_{1}k+M^{R}_{2}k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}),

(4.42)

where

M^{R}_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma^{(1)}}\frac{\mu(s)(V^{(1)}-I)}{s^{2}}\mathrm{d}s,\quad M^{R}_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma^{(1)}}\frac{\mu(s)(V^{(1)}-I)}{s^{3}}\mathrm{d}s.

(4.43)

Besides, they satisfy the estimates as follows:

|M^{R}(0)-I|\lesssim t^{-1/2},\ |M^{R}_{j}|\lesssim t^{-1/2},\ j=1,2.

(4.44)

Proof.

Considering that
s^{-1}
is bounded on
\Sigma^{(1)}
, the results are obvious, so we omit the proof.
∎

4.4
Long-time asymptotics for the HS equation

Now we give the asymptotic expression of the solution

\displaystyle\widehat{M}(k)=

\displaystyle M^{(2)}(k)M^{R}(k)(R^{(1)})^{-1}(M^{(2)}(0)+M^{(2)}_{1}k+M^{(2)}_{2}k^{2})(M^{R}(0)+M^{R}_{1}k+M^{R}_{2}k^{2})+\mathcal{O}(k^{3})

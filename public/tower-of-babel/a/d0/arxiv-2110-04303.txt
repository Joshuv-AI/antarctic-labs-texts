CFT 2 in the Bulk
Cameron V. Cogburn

Abstract
We study non-Gaussian bulk 2d CFTs in AdS 2 using boundary CFT techniques and recent results in JT/Schwarzian gravity. We highlight the constraints on the operator content of a theory imposed by the boundary conditions by examining the relation between correlator coefficients and OPE coefficients in the presence of a boundary. We then calculate bulk and boundary correlators for various boundary conditions. Schwarzian techniques are used to calculate gravitational correlators perturbatively in 1 / c 1/c .

Introduction

Studying strongly coupled QFTs is difficult as perturbative calculational tools break down in the absence
of a small expansion parameter. By “strongly coupled” we mean the correlators of generic operators are
non-Gaussian, i.e., they do not obey an expansion in a
``1/N"
small parameter.

AdS is a convenient background in which to study strongly coupled systems as it is a maximally
symmetric space that inherently acts as a bulk IR regulator
Callan:1989em
. One way to study
strongly coupled QFTs in AdS non-perturbatively is to make use of a numerical scheme such as a lattice
discretization
Brower:2019kyh
or a Hamiltonian truncation
Hogervorst:2021spa
. Another way is
to use the property that a CFT in AdS
d
is related to its boundary CFT (BCFT) in the
d
-dimensional
upper half-plane
\mathcal{H}^{d}\equiv\{\vec{x}+iy\,|\,y>0;\vec{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d-1}\,,y\in\mathbbm{R}\}
via a Weyl transformation:

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{AdS}_{d}}=y^{-2}(dy^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2})=\Omega^{2}ds^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}\;,

(1.1)

with
\Omega=y^{-1}
. This has been utilized several times before
Doyon:2004fv
;
Aharony:2010ay
;
Carmi:2018qzm
;
Paulos:2016fap
;
Herzog:2019bom
;
Aharony:2015hix
;
Giombi:2020rmc
; therefore we can
use tools from the sizeable literature on BCFTs to study rational CFTs in AdS.

Although the QFT is strongly coupled, gravity can be weakly coupled by inserting factors of the stress
tensor into correlators, e.g.,

\displaystyle\langle\phi\dots\phi T\dots T\rangle\;,

(1.2)

where
\phi
is a scalar and
T
a stress tensor, the latter of which gravitons are coupled to. For a
general non-Gaussian CFT we do not know these; similarly, in dimensions higher than two the
TT
OPE
structure is not unique
Osborn:1993cr
. However, in CFT
2
, adding factors of
T
is controlled by
conformal invariance, making such calculations tractable. Moreover, in 2d it is possible to use the dual 1d
Schwarzian theory to calculate the gravitational correlators of a boundary quantity
\tilde{{\cal O}}
,

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}\rangle_{t}=\int[Dt]\tilde{{\cal O}}e^{-S[t]}\;,\quad S[t]\propto\int dt\,\{t,u\}\;,

(1.3)

that would otherwise be calculated by inserting multiples of the stress tensor into a bulk correlator before
taking it to the boundary.

The purpose of this work is to show that in AdS
2
all the machinery exists to do perturbative
gravitational calculations, and some types of non-perturbative ones, on strongly coupled conformal
theories weakly coupled to gravity, using the unitary minimal models as an explicit example. Section

2
provides an overview of CFT
2
in AdS
2
. We use the Ising model to explicitly
show how boundary correlators can be obtained solely from the bulk-boundary OPE, and how boundary
conditions constrain the bulk and boundary data. In Section
3
, by mapping to the infinite strip
we can study the theory in the presence of two boundaries, such as in RS1 models. In Section

4
we add gravity and calculate the gravitational correction for a generic minimal model
\tilde{\phi}_{2,1}
four-point function using Schwarzian techniques. Section
5
discusses
the case of higher dimensions and how to calculate boundary gravitational corrections therein.
Finally, in Section
6
we conclude with a discussion.

CFT
2
in AdS
2

We begin with a general discussion of putting a 2d CFT in AdS
2
. Since AdS has a conformal boundary
we need to understand how boundary conditions affect the bulk theory. There is an abundance of literature
on CFTs in the presence of a boundary in flat space, from which we review the salient points and apply them
to AdS. The main idea is that we can obtain boundary correlators solely from the bulk-boundary OPE,
without needing to solve for the bulk correlators and then taking them to the boundary. We also review the
constraints imposed from boundary conditions on the bulk correlators and the dual CFT
1
boundary
operators. Boundary correlators are the main ingredient needed for the gravitational calculations of Section

4
.

2.1
Overview: boundary conditions

Many physical systems have some sort of boundary, so the study of conformal theories in the presence
of a boundary has been well developed. The upper half-plane (UHP) is the prototypical space in which to
study the consequences of demanding conformal invariance for a two-dimensional system in the presence
of a boundary. A key point in the analysis is to use the “doubling trick”, in which a mirror image of the
system is introduced in the lower half-plane and the now doubled system is analyzed in the full plane,

\mathbbm{R}^{2}
. Doing this in AdS
2
only introduces a Weyl factor. Upon taking the bulk correlators to
the boundary we encounter a small puzzle, the clarification of which is a theme of Section

2
.

We start by putting a CFT
2
in the UHP with a flat metric and a conformal boundary condition
at the boundary
y=0
by using the aforementioned doubling trick
CARDY1984514
:

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\dots{\cal O}_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle_{\rm UHP}=\langle{\cal O}_{1}(z_{1}){\cal O}_{1}(z^{*}_{1})\dots{\cal O}_{n}(z_{n}){\cal O}_{n}(z_{n}^{*})\rangle\;.

(2.1)

Here
{\cal O}_{i}(z)
is the holomorphic half of the operator
{\cal O}_{i}(z,\bar{z})
and
z=x+iy
. This construction
explicitly preserves all of the symmetries (conformal transformations that leave the
x
-axis invariant as well
as any holomorphic equations of motion for the
{\cal O}
s). We will see later it also makes it convenient to set
different boundary conditions by making different choices about what terms in the OPE of
{\cal O}_{i}(z_{i}){\cal O}_{i}(z^{*}_{i})
are kept.

To map this correlator to AdS
2
, perform a Weyl transformation,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x,y)\rightarrow\Omega^{2}(x,y)g_{\mu\nu}(x,y)\;,

(2.2)

with
\Omega(x,y)=y^{-1}
. Under this transformation the CFT operators transform like

\Omega^{-\Delta}
,
so we have

G^{(n)}_{{\cal O}}(x_{i},y_{i})\equiv\langle{\cal O}_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\dots{\cal O}_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})\rangle_{{\rm AdS}_{2}}=y_{1}^{\Delta_{1}}\dots y_{n}^{\Delta_{n}}\langle{\cal O}_{1}(z_{1}){\cal O}_{1}(z^{*}_{1})\dots{\cal O}_{n}(z_{n}){\cal O}_{n}(z_{n}^{*})\rangle\;.

(2.3)

To obtain the boundary correlator, take the limit as the bulk operators approach the boundary:

\displaystyle G^{(n)}_{{\cal O},\partial}(x_{i})\equiv\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}_{1}(x_{1})\dots\tilde{{\cal O}}_{n}(x_{n})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\lim_{y_{i}\rightarrow 0}y_{1}^{-\tilde{\Delta}_{1}}\dots y_{n}^{-\tilde{\Delta}_{n}}G^{(n)}_{{\cal O}}(x_{i},y_{i})\;.

(2.4)

Here,
\tilde{\Delta}_{i}
are the dimensions of the dual CFT
1
operators. By “dual CFT
1
operator” we
mean the leading operator in the boundary limit of the bulk operator coming from the bulk-boundary operator
expansion

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{i}(x,y)=\sum_{j}(2y)^{\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{i}}C^{a}_{ij}\tilde{{\cal O}}_{j}(x)+\cdots

(2.5)

The
C^{a}_{ij}
are the boundary OPE coefficients and the index
a
signifies that the precise operators in
the expansion are boundary condition-dependent. At the same time, because of the doubling trick, we can
compare the bulk-boundary OPE (
2.5
) with the boundary limit of the
{\cal O}_{i}\times{\cal O}_{i}
bulk
OPE. Taking the bulk OPE

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{i}(z){\cal O}_{i}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}\frac{c_{ii{\cal O}^{\prime}}}{(z-w)^{2h_{i}-h_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}}}{\cal O}^{\prime}(w)

(2.6)

in the boundary limit yields

\displaystyle\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}{\cal O}_{i}(z){\cal O}_{i}(z^{*})=\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}{\cal O}_{i}(x+iy){\cal O}_{i}(x-iy)=\sum_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}c_{ii{\cal O}^{\prime}}(2y)^{-\Delta_{i}+h_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}}{\cal O}^{\prime}(x)+\dots

(2.7)

We see that
\tilde{{\cal O}}
is just the lowest-dimension operator
{\cal O}^{\prime}
in the
{\cal O}_{i}\times{\cal O}_{i}
OPE,
and the dimension
\tilde{\Delta}
of
\tilde{{\cal O}}
is
h_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}
.

Usually the theory is defined so that the lowest-dimension operator in the
{\cal O}_{i}\times{\cal O}_{i}
bulk OPE
is the identity operator. However, we may have or want boundary conditions that necessitate having a
vanishing identity contribution. By inspection of the above OPEs, we see that this means setting the OPE
coefficients for all operators with
h_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}<2h_{i}
to zero, seemingly eliminating the identity as the dual
CFT
1
operator.

We can identify when this is the case by looking at the bulk-boundary OPE (
2.5
). The identity
is not the dual CFT
1
operator when the bulk-boundary coupling coefficient to the identity
A^{a}_{i}\equiv C^{a}_{i\mathbbm{1}}
vanishes. This can easily be read off from the modular matrix, as shown in the
following subsection. The point is that we can obtain the boundary correlators solely from the bulk-boundary
OPE, without needing to solve for bulk correlators and then taking them to the boundary.

As an example, let us compute the boundary
2n
-point functions from bulk
\langle\sigma\sigma\dots\sigma\rangle
correlators of the Ising model. The
\sigma\times\sigma
OPE is

\displaystyle[\sigma]\times[\sigma]=[1]+[\epsilon]\;.

(2.8)

where
[\dots]
indicates the full Virasoro irrep of operators. Pulling off the identity operator in order to
impose Dirichlet boundary conditions, we find that the boundary correlators are

\displaystyle G^{(2n)}_{\sigma,\partial}(x_{i})=\langle\epsilon(x_{1})\dots\epsilon(x_{2n})\rangle=\bigg|\text{Pf}\,\bigg[\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq 2n}\bigg|^{2}\;,

(2.9)

where
Pf
is the Pfaffian. For example, with
n=2
we have

\displaystyle G^{(4)}_{\sigma,\partial}(x_{i})=\langle\epsilon(x_{1})\epsilon(x_{2})\epsilon(x_{3})\epsilon(x_{4})\rangle=\frac{1}{x_{12}x_{34}}+\frac{1}{x_{13}x_{24}}+\frac{1}{x_{14}x_{23}}\;.

(2.10)

Although the Ising model is particularly simple, the above logic applies to any boundary correlator from a
bulk 2d CFT.

Since the bulk-boundary identity coupling
A^{a}_{i}
is dependent on the boundary condition
a
, so is
the dual CFT
1
operator. Using Cardy boundary states we can understand precisely what boundary
conditions correlate to what bulk-boundary coefficients, and hence to what dual CFT
1
operators.

2.2
Boundary states

As we saw above, the bulk-boundary OPE coefficients are dependent on the boundary condition, that is the
physical boundary state. Since Cardy classified the allowed physical boundary states they are also known

A Cardy boundary state

can be expanded in the basis of allowed states (Ishibashi states)

corresponding to the bulk primary fields as

\displaystyle|\tilde{l}\rangle=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{ij}}{\sqrt{S_{0j}}}|j\rrangle\;,

(2.11)

is the modular matrix.

Because the boundary conditions determine the bulk-boundary coefficients

2.4
Bulk data from boundary conditions

We can see how the boundary conditions also set the form of the bulk correlators. Let us restrict the bulk
CFT
2
theory in AdS
2

3
Two Boundaries: The RS1 Setup

Suppose we are interested in analyzing the case of a CFT
2
in the presence of two boundaries in
AdS
2
. Such a situation arises, for example, if we have a Randall-Sundrum 1 (RS1) model with two
branes
Randall:1999ee
. In this model there is a Planck brane at the conformal boundary
y=0
in the
UHP and a TeV brane at a distance
y=L
. Multiple boundaries are also useful, for example, in describing
the RG flow between theories using a RG brane
Gaiotto:2012np
.

A convenient geometry to analyze AdS
2
with two boundaries is the conformally equivalent infinite
strip with finite width
L
. The strip geometry is obtained from the UHP through the conformal
map

\displaystyle w=\frac{L}{\pi}\ln z\;,

(3.1)

where
w=u+iv
and
z=x+iy
are the respective holomorphic coordinates for the strip and for the
UHP. Note how the UHP positive real axis
(x>0)
is mapped to the lower edge of the strip
(v=0)
and the
negative real axis
(x<0)
to the upper edge
(v=L)
. Therefore, we can obtain a strip with different
boundary conditions on its two edges by looking at the UHP with differing boundary conditions along its positive and negative real axis, mediated by a boundary condition-changing operator at the origin. We again using the Ising model to illustrate calculations.

3.1
Boundary correlators

The analysis for the bulk spin two-point function in the flat half-plane metric is done in

DiFrancesco:639405

Poincaré one. Boundary correlators on the strip are obtained by taking
v\rightarrow 0

First consider the case where the two boundaries have the same boundary condition. A two-point function
on the strip is given by

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{1}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1}){\cal O}_{2}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\rangle_{\text{strip}}=\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\frac{dz_{i}}{dw_{i}}\right)^{h_{i}}\left(\frac{d\bar{z}_{i}}{d\bar{w}_{i}}\right)^{\bar{h}_{i}}\Omega^{-\Delta_{i}}_{i}\langle{\cal O}_{1}(z_{1}){\cal O}_{2}(z_{2}){\cal O}_{1}(z^{*}_{1}){\cal O}_{2}(z^{*}_{2})\rangle\;,

where the doubling trick has been applied and the bulk four-point function pre-factor

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\frac{dz_{i}}{dw_{i}}\right)^{h_{i}}\left(\frac{d\bar{z}_{i}}{d\bar{w}_{i}}\right)^{\bar{h}_{i}}\Omega^{-\Delta_{i}}_{i}=\bigg[\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}e^{\frac{\pi u_{1}}{L}}v_{1}^{2}\bigg]^{h_{1}}\bigg[\Big(\frac{\pi}{L}\Big)^{2}e^{\frac{\pi u_{2}}{L}}v_{2}^{2}\bigg]^{h_{2}}\;,

comes from the Weyl transformation from the Poincaré UHP to the flat UHP and then to the

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{1}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1}){\cal O}_{2}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\rangle_{\text{strip},\,\partial}^{(a)}=\lim_{v_{i}\rightarrow 0}v^{-\Delta_{\tilde{{\cal O}}_{1}^{(a)}}}_{1}v^{-\Delta_{\tilde{{\cal O}}_{2}^{(a)}}}_{2}\langle{\cal O}_{1}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1}){\cal O}_{2}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\rangle_{\text{strip}}\;,

3.2
Energy spectrum

Through the boundary correlators we can write down the spectrum of the boundary theory. This is
particularly useful if our setup is a RS1 model where we would like to know the spectrum of particles on the
boundary. The boundary spectrum can easily be read off by expanding the boundary correlator

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}(u_{1})\tilde{{\cal O}}(u_{2})\rangle=\sum_{n}|\langle 0|{\cal O}|n\rangle|^{2}e^{-\Delta E_{n}u}\;,

(3.9)

where
\Delta E_{n}=E_{n}-E_{0}
gives the energy of the particle above the ground state and
|c_{n}|^{2}=|\langle 0|{\cal O}|n\rangle|^{2}

4.1
Gravitational coupling to a non-Gaussian theory

Now we weakly couple gravity to the strongly coupled CFT
2

corrections. For a Gaussian bulk theory, perturbation theory allows gravitational contributions to
be calculated from the Feynman rules (cf. Fig.

theory, normal perturbative techniques are not available.

Instead, gravitational contributions are found by inserting factors of the stress tensor into matter correlation
functions. This is because the graviton

couples to the stress tensor, as seen by expanding the
matter action around the classical AdS
2

g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(c)}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle S_{M}[g]=S_{M}[g^{(c)}]+\int\frac{\delta S}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\int h_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta^{2}S}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}\delta g_{\mu\nu}}h_{\mu\nu}+O(h^{3})\;,

(4.1)

where the stress tensor
T^{\mu\nu}(x)=(2/\sqrt{g})\delta S_{M}/\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)

Gravitational correlators are then found by computing the path integral

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}\rangle_{\text{grav}}=\int[Dh]\,\langle{\cal O}\rangle\,e^{-S_{M}[h]}\;,

(4.2)

where
\langle{\cal O}\rangle
is the matter correlator.
Expanding the action (
4.1

4.2

1/c

gravitational corrections.

For example, suppose we want to calculate the leading

where

is the CFT
1

The correction comes from expanding (

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x\,d^{2}y\sqrt{g_{x}}\sqrt{g_{y}}\,\langle\phi\phi T(x)T(y)\rangle\,H(x,y)\;,

(4.3)

where

4.2

To calculate gravitational corrections using the Schwarzian theory
we use Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity

In Appendix

JT gravity is a 2d dilaton gravity whose boundary description is given by a 1d Schwarzian action,

\displaystyle S[t]=-C\int du\,\{t,u\}\;,\quad\quad\{t,u\}\equiv\frac{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{t^{\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\;,

dynamical field variable and primes denote derivatives with respect to

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}\rangle_{t}=\int[dt]\,\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}\rangle\,e^{-S[t]}\;,

(4.6)

where
\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}\rangle
is the matter boundary correlator.

The 1d Schwarzian theory is a theory of time reparametrizations whose action has a global SL(2,
\mathbbm{R}
)
invariance. The fields

are Nambu-Goldstone
bosons generated from the spontaneous breaking of SL(2,
\mathbbm{R}

solution in the JT
gravity action (

5
5

5

6
Discussion

Analyzing minimal models in AdS
2
allows us to take a strongly coupled CFT and use the power of
boundary CFT and JT/Schwarzian gravity. The result is a family of “solved” bulk theories that we can
calculate perturbative gravitational corrections for to arbitrary order. We illustrated examples of the types of
calculations that can be done in this framework using the Ising model, which can be generalized to any
minimal model. In particular we saw how the physical boundary conditions affect the operator content of the
theory through the relation between the boundary conditions, correlator coefficients, and the OPE
coefficients. For the case of AdS with two boundaries – as found, e.g., in RS1 models – we calculated the energy
spectrum from the boundary correlators. JT gravity was added and the leading gravitational correction to the
non-Gaussian
\tilde{\phi}_{2,1}
minimal model correlators was found.

It is interesting to think about the scenario studied here to the arguments put forth in

Heemskerk:2009pn
. In that work, it is conjectured that a CFT with a large-
N
expansion and a
sufficiently large gap has a local bulk dual description. In the case at hand, the bulk is strongly coupled and
the boundary operator dimensions and correlators do not have a large-
N
factorization. Therefore, in a
sense holography is more general than the requirements of
Heemskerk:2009pn
.

Appendix A
OPE Conventions

In this work we deal with bulk, boundary, and bulk-boundary operator product expansions, with the
additional complication of these being dependent on potentially changing boundary conditions. Here we
define the OPE notations and conventions used throughout the text, based on the notation used in

Lewellen:1991tb
.

For the two-dimensional bulk theory, conformal invariance fixes the form of the one- and two-point
functions. We normalize the vacuum and the primary field
{\cal O}_{i}
with scaling dimensions
(\Delta_{i},\bar{\Delta}_{i})
as

\displaystyle\langle\mathbbm{1}\rangle=1,\quad\langle{\cal O}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\;,\quad\langle{\cal O}_{i}(z,\bar{z}){\cal O}_{j}(0)\rangle=\delta_{ij}\,z^{-2\Delta_{i}}\bar{z}^{-2\bar{\Delta}_{i}}\,.

(A.1)

At short distances the bulk OPE is given by

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{i}(z,\bar{z}){\cal O}_{j}(0)=\sum_{k}C_{ijk}\,z^{\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{j}}\bar{z}^{\bar{\Delta}_{k}-\bar{\Delta}_{i}-\bar{\Delta}_{j}}{\cal O}_{k}(z,\bar{z})+\cdots

(A.2)

Restricting ourselves to the one-dimensional boundary, we now have operators
{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{ab}(x)
that are
primaries under a single Virasoro algebra with conformal dimension
\tilde{\Delta}_{i}
. The superscript
indices
ab
indicate a boundary operator that mediates a change in boundary conditions from type
a
to
type
b
. The boundary correlators are thus

\displaystyle\langle\mathbbm{1}\rangle_{a}=\alpha^{a},\quad\langle{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{ab}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\;,\quad\langle{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{ab}(0){\tilde{\cal O}}_{j}^{cd}(x)\rangle=\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\delta_{ij}\,x^{-2\tilde{\Delta}_{i}}\tilde{\alpha}^{ad}_{i}\;,

(A.3)

with a boundary OPE given by

\displaystyle{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{ab}(0){\tilde{\cal O}}_{j}^{cd}(x)=\sum_{k}\tilde{C}^{abc}_{ijk}\,x^{\tilde{\Delta}_{k}-\tilde{\Delta}_{i}-\tilde{\Delta}_{j}}{\tilde{\cal O}}_{k}^{ac}(x)+\cdots

(A.4)

The normalization constants
\alpha^{a}
and
\alpha^{ad}_{i}
are boundary condition-dependent and arise
from that fact that having canonically fixed the bulk normalizations, we can not do the same with the
boundary ones.

When a bulk operator approaches a boundary with boundary condition
a
, we get a bulk-boundary OPE

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{j}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{i}(2y)^{\tilde{\Delta}_{i}-\Delta_{j}-\bar{\Delta}_{j}}C_{ji}^{a}\,{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{aa}(x)+\cdots

(A.5)

Due to the single Virasoro algebra, the explicit factor of 2 comes from thinking about
(
A.5

bulk OPE.

For the sake of notational easement we drop the boundary condition superscripts when dealing with
constant and uniform
boundary conditions or otherwise when it is unambiguously clear (e.g.,
{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}^{aa}\rightarrow{\tilde{\cal O}}_{i}
,

C_{ji}^{a}\rightarrow C_{ji}

This section aims to concisely review how the Schwarzian action arises as the one-dimensional boundary

E.1

JT gravity is a theory with a two-dimensional metric tensor

and dilaton field
\Phi

\displaystyle S_{\text{JT}}=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left[\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\Phi\left(R-\frac{\Lambda}{L^{2}}\right)+2\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}du\sqrt{h}\,\Phi_{b}K\right]\;,

is its boundary
with induced metric

6
6

6

We have suppressed the purely topological

\displaystyle S_{\text{JT}}\supset\Phi_{b}\left(\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}R+2\int du\sqrt{h}K\right)

The bulk term uses the dilaton as a Lagrange multiplier to set the curvature to the cosmological constant
R=\Lambda/L^{2}=-2

\displaystyle\Phi_{b}=\Phi_{\text{bdy}}=\frac{\Phi_{r}(u)}{\epsilon}\;,\quad\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}=g_{uu}=\frac{t^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime 2}}{z^{2}}\;,\quad ds|_{\text{bdy}}=\frac{du}{\epsilon}\implies\int_{0}^{\beta}ds=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}

(E.3)

\epsilon\rightarrow 0

\Phi_{r}(u)

With this parametrization the induced metric

\displaystyle S_{\text{JT}}\rightarrow-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int\frac{du}{\epsilon}\frac{\Phi_{r}(u)}{\epsilon}K\;,

\displaystyle K=1+\epsilon^{2}\{t,u\}+\cdots\quad\quad\{t,u\}\equiv\frac{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{t^{\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\;,

\Phi_{r}/\epsilon=\Phi_{b}

can be absorbed into the definition of
C
.

In the Schwarzian action (
E.6
), the dynamical field variable
t(u)
represents the
Nambu-Goldstone bosons generated from the spontaneous breaking of SL
(2)
symmetry
by the AdS

E.2
Effective Schwarzian action

By varying the action we get the equation of motion

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{t^{\prime}}\left(\frac{(t^{\prime}\Phi_{r})^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}\right)\right]^{\prime}\longrightarrow\frac{\{t,u\}^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}=0

(E.7)

where in moving from left to right we used that
\Phi_{r}
is constant on the boundary. We see that when
t

is
a linear function of
u
, the equations of motion are satisfied, and represent a saddle in the path integral. Therefore the Schwarzian derivative is invariant under SL(2) symmetry
t\rightarrow(at+b)/(ct+d)
and vanishes for
t(u)=u
.

Since we are on the disk and have a finite boundary length, it is convenient unwrap the circle by reparametrizing

\displaystyle u_{\text{line}}=\tan\frac{\pi}{\beta}t(u_{\text{circle}})

(E.8)

in terms of the diff(
S^{1}
) reparametrization
f
satisfying,

\displaystyle t(u_{\text{circle}}+\beta)=t(u_{\text{circle}})+\beta\;,\quad t^{\prime}(u_{\text{circle}})\geq 0\;.

(E.9)

We use
u\equiv u_{\text{circle}}
everywhere else unless stated otherwise. Because the boundary length

\beta
can be interpreted as the inverse temperature, this makes manifest the finite temperature theory.

Quantum gravitational thermal correlators are found by computing the thermal Schwarzian path integral

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{\beta}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{M}}[\mathcal{D}t]\,\mathcal{O}[t]e^{C\int_{0}^{\beta}du\,\left\{\tan\frac{\pi}{\beta}t,\,u\right\}}

(E.10)

where the integration space
\mathcal{M}=\text{diff}(S^{1})/\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Using the composition property of the Schwarzian,
\{t(g(u)),u\}=(g^{\prime})^{2}\{t,g\}+\{t,u\}
, this becomes

\displaystyle S[t]=-C\int du\,\left\{\tan\frac{\pi}{\beta}t(u),\,u\right\}=\frac{C}{2}\int du\,\left[\left(\frac{t^{\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{2}(t^{\prime})^{2}\right]\;.

(E.11)

E.3
Schwarzian fluctuation theory

To obtain the linearized theory we proceed perturbatively around the saddle by writing
t(u)=u+\epsilon k(u)
and expanding the action in
\epsilon
. To simplify things, let
\beta=2\pi
; to recover the original
u
variable rescale
u\rightarrow(2\pi/\beta)u
. After expanding and integrating by parts, the linearized action is

\displaystyle S_{\epsilon}[t]=\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}du\,\left[(k^{\prime\prime})^{2}-(k^{\prime})^{2}\right]\;.

(E.12)

To quantize the fluctuation theory we invert the kernel in the above action by discrete Fourier
transforming via
\epsilon(u)=\sum_{n}\epsilon_{n}e^{inu}
with
n\in\mathbbm{Z}
and
u\in[0,2\pi]
. This gives

\displaystyle S_{\epsilon}[t]=\frac{C}{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbbm{Z}}\,(n^{4}-n^{2})\epsilon_{n}\epsilon_{-n}\;.

(E.13)

Inverting, we obtain the fluctuation propagator

Recall the integration space is modded out by
gauge redundancy
SL(2,
\mathbbm{R}
)
so we can fix the redundant SL(2,
\mathbbm{R}
) singular modes

in the sum and ignore

\displaystyle\langle\epsilon(u_{1})\epsilon(u_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi C}\sum_{n\neq 0,\pm 1}\frac{e^{in\mathfrak{u}}}{n^{2}(n^{2}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi C}\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{(|\mathfrak{u}|-\pi)^{2}}{2}+(|\mathfrak{u}|-\pi)\sin|\mathfrak{u}|+\frac{5}{2}\cos|\mathfrak{u}|\right)\;,

(E.14)

E.4
Schwarzian correlation functions

To calculate gravitational correlators we need to know how the Schwarzian action (
E.6
)
couples to matter correlators.

As discussed in Section
2
, we know how to quantify any
n
-point boundary correlator

\tilde{{\cal O}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
of a bulk CFT
2
. In 1d reparametrization invariance and conformal
invariance are the same. Under a conformal transformation
\tilde{{\cal O}}
transforms as

\displaystyle\tilde{{\cal O}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=(t^{\prime}_{1})^{\Delta_{1}/2}\dots(t^{\prime}_{n})^{\Delta_{n}/2}\tilde{{\cal O}}(t_{1}(u_{1}),\dots,t_{n}(u_{n}))\;,

(E.15)

where
t^{\prime}_{i}\equiv dt_{i}(u_{i})/du_{i}
.
Subsequently, from the Schwarzian path integral we can compute gravitational correlators

Maldacena:2016upp
;
Blommaert:2019hjr
by computing the quantity

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}\rangle_{t}\equiv\int[\mathcal{D}t]\,\tilde{{\cal O}}(t_{i})\,e^{-S[t]}

(E.16)

after performing a conformal transformation (
E.15
) on
\tilde{{\cal O}}
.

To perform the path integral we expand around the saddle by letting
t(u)=u+\epsilon k(u)
with

\epsilon
small. Expanding the action sends
S[t]\rightarrow S_{\text{eff}}[k]
and expanding

\tilde{{\cal O}}(t_{i})
to second order in
\epsilon
lets us use the fluctuation propagator (
E.3

obtain the full expression for the gravitational correlator

E.5
Two-point functions

Now that know how to calculate gravitational boundary correlators using the Schwarzian, all that remains
is doing it. Here we review simple examples from the literature.

Taking a bulk minimal model two-point function of the operator
{\cal O}
to the boundary gives

\displaystyle G_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})=\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}_{\tilde{\Delta}}(t_{1})\tilde{{\cal O}}_{\tilde{\Delta}}(t_{2})\rangle=\frac{1}{t_{12}^{2\tilde{\Delta}}}\;,

(E.17)

where
\tilde{{\cal O}}
is the dual CFT
1
operator to
{\cal O}
consistent with the boundary conditions.
Under a conformal transformation this becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{12}^{2\tilde{\Delta}}}\longrightarrow\left(\frac{(f^{\prime}(t_{1})f^{\prime}(t_{2}))}{(f(t_{1})-f(t_{2}))^{2}}\right)^{\tilde{\Delta}}\;.

(E.18)

To calculate the gravitational contribution to the two-point function, expand (
E.18
) around
the saddle
f(t)=\tan\frac{t+\epsilon(t)}{2}
to second order in
\epsilon
, giving

\displaystyle G_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{1+\epsilon\mathcal{B}_{1}(t_{12})+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{B}_{2}(t_{12})+O(\epsilon^{3})}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2}\right)^{2\Delta}}\;,

(E.19)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(t_{12})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Delta}\left(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{\tan\frac{t_{12}}{2}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(t_{12})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{4}\left(\frac{k^{2}_{12}}{(\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2})^{2}}-2(k^{\prime 2}_{1}+k^{\prime 2}_{2})\right)+\frac{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}}{2}\left(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-\frac{k_{12}}{\tan\frac{t_{12}}{2}}\right)^{2}\;.

(E.20)

To compute the gravitational correlator substitute (
E.19
) into the effective
Schwarzian path integral with action (
E.12
),

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})\rangle_{f}=\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}\langle\mathcal{B}_{2}(t_{12})\rangle}{t^{2\tilde{\Delta}}_{12}}\;,

(E.21)

where the linear term in
\epsilon
vanishes. The final expression involves contracting factors of the
propagator using (
E.3
) to give

\displaystyle\langle G_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2})^{2\tilde{\Delta}}}\bigg(1+\tilde{\Delta}\frac{2(1-\pi t_{12})+t^{2}_{12}-2\cos t_{12}+2\sin t_{12}(\pi-t_{12})}{8\pi C\sin^{2}\frac{t_{12}}{2}}

\displaystyle-\tilde{\Delta}^{2}\frac{\big(2+(2\pi-t_{12})\cot\frac{t_{12}}{2}\big)\big(-2+t_{12}\cot\frac{t_{12}}{2}\big)}{4\pi C}\bigg)\;,

(E.22)

reproducing the result of
Maldacena:2016upp
. Factorized four-point functions are a straightforward
extension of the two-point function and are also treated in the aforementioned reference.

E.6
Three-point functions

Three-point functions follow a similar pattern to the two-point ones above. First start with the dual
CFT
1
boundary three-point function

\displaystyle G_{3}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\langle\tilde{{\cal O}}_{\tilde{\Delta}}(t_{1})\tilde{{\cal O}}_{\tilde{\Delta}}(t_{2})\tilde{{\cal O}}_{\tilde{\Delta}}(t_{3})\rangle=\frac{N_{3}}{(t_{12}t_{23}t_{13})^{\tilde{\Delta}}}\;,

(E.23)

where
N_{3}
is a normalization constant. Under a conformal transformation this becomes

\displaystyle G_{3}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\longrightarrow\left(\frac{f^{\prime}_{1}f^{\prime}_{2}f^{\prime}_{3}}{f_{12}f_{23}f_{13}}\right)^{\Delta}\;.

(E.24)

Reparametrizing and expanding around the saddle gives

\displaystyle G_{3}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=N_{3}\frac{1+\epsilon C_{1}(t_{123})+\epsilon^{2}C_{2}(t_{123})+O(\epsilon^{3})}{(t_{12}t_{23}t_{13})^{\tilde{\Delta}}}

(E.25)

where

\displaystyle C_{1}(t_{123})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Delta}\left(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}+k^{\prime}_{3}-\frac{k_{12}}{2\tan\frac{t_{12}}{2}}-\frac{k_{3}}{2\tan\frac{t_{23}}{2}}-\frac{k_{13}}{2\tan\frac{t_{13}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle C_{2}(t_{123})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{8}\left(\frac{k^{2}_{12}}{\sin^{2}\frac{t_{12}}{2}}+\frac{k^{2}_{23}}{\sin^{2}\frac{t_{23}}{2}}+\frac{k^{2}_{13}}{\sin^{2}\frac{t_{13}}{2}}-4(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}+k^{\prime}_{3})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{\Delta}^{2}}{2}\left((k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}+k^{\prime}_{3})+\frac{k_{21}\cos t_{12}}{2\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{23}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{13}}{2}}+\frac{k_{13}\cos t_{13}}{2\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{23}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{13}}{2}}+\frac{k_{32}\cos t_{23}}{2\sin\frac{t_{12}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{23}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{13}}{2}}\right)^{2}\;.

(E.26)

Therefore the gravitational contribution to the three-point function is given by

\displaystyle\langle G_{3}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\rangle_{f}=N_{3}\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}\langle C_{2}(t_{123})\rangle+O(\epsilon^{3})}{(t_{12}t_{23}t_{13})^{\tilde{\Delta}}}\;,

(E.27)

where again the
\epsilon
-propagator terms are contracted using (
E.3
), reproducing
Qi:2018rqm
.

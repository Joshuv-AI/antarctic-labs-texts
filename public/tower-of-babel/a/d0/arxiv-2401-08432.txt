1.
Introduction

Let
n
be a positive integer and
d(n)
denote the number of factors of
n
. By the elementary argument (hyperbola method), one can show that

\sum_{n\leq x}d(n)=x\log x+(2\gamma-1)x+O(x^{\theta}),

(1.1)

for any
\theta>1/2
.
The classical Dirichlet divisor problem asks whether we can reduce
\theta
to
x^{1/4+\delta}
for any
\delta>0
. The current record
\theta>131/416
was given by Huxley
[
4
]
. In fact, (
1.1
) gives information concerning the sum of the divisor function over shorter intervals. Specifically, if (
1.1
) holds, then

\frac{1}{x^{\theta}}\sum_{x<n\leq x+x^{\theta}}d(n)\sim\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d(n)\sim\log x.

Let
d_{k}(n)=\sum_{n=m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{k}}1
which is an extension of
d(n)
. We know that for all sufficiently large
x>0

\frac{1}{x^{\theta_{k}}}\sum_{x<n\leq x+x^{\theta_{k}}}d_{k}(n)\sim\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d_{k}(n)\sim P_{k-1}(\log x),

where
0<\theta_{k}<1
depends on
k
(see
[
11
]
) and
P_{k}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
k
that can be calculated explicitly (see
[
13
, Formula (5.36) in Section II.5.4]
).
These results are called
results in all intervals
.

Another direction is
results in almost all intervals
which has developed rapidly in recent years due to the work of Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
8
]
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to[-1,1]
be a multiplicative function. In
2016
, Matomäki and Radziwiłł in their breakthrough paper
[
8
]
proved that, for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
, i.e., for all but
o(X)
choices of
[X,2X]
,

\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}f(n)=o(1),

(1.2)

provided
h\to\infty
with
X\to\infty
.

Motivated by the above, we prove the following result for the sum of
d_{k}(n)
in almost all intervals

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
h\geq(\log X)^{k\log k-k+1+\epsilon}
, then

\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}d_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d_{k}(n)=o(\log^{k-1}x)

for all but at most
o(X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
.

We call a function
f

d_{k}
-bounded if
|f(n)|\leq d_{k}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
1.3
below which deals with the general case for complex valued
d_{k}
bounded multiplicative functions.

Remark
.

Theorem
1.1
improves upon the special case of
[
7
, Theorem 1.7]
under the “almost all” sense. In fact, Mangerel
[
7
]
proved that, if
h\geq h_{0}(\log X)^{(k-1)^{2}}
and
h_{0}\to\infty
as
X\to\infty
, then

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}d_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d_{k}(n)\right|^{2}=o(\log^{2k-2}X).

Mangerel’s result is optimal in the
L_{2}
sense and one can apply Henriot’s bound (Lemma
4.2
) to prove the fact that if
h=o((\log X)^{(k-1)^{2}})
, then,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}d_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d_{k}(n)\right|^{2}dx=(1+o(1))\frac{1}{h}(\log X)^{k^{2}-1}.

(1.3)

We will also show in the following theorem the exponent
k\log k-k+1
in Theorem
1.1
is optimal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X\geq 2
be sufficiently large. Let
\epsilon>0
and

1\leq h\leq(\log X)^{k\log k-k+1-\epsilon}.

Then

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}d_{k}(n)=o(h(\log x)^{k-1})

holds for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
.

Remark
.

Note that Theorem
1.2
was mentioned in
[
10
]
without proof. We give the details of the proof in the end of Section
2
. Combining Theorem
1.2
and (
1.3
), we observe that when
h\leq(\log X)^{k\log k-k+1-\epsilon}
, the main contribution to the integral on the left-hand side of (
1.3
) comes from
o(X)
intervals
[x,x+h]
. In fact, those intervals contain an integer that has at least
(1+o(1))k\log\log X
prime factors.

Comparing Theorem
1.1
to
[
8
, Theorem 1]
or
[
7
, Theorem 1.7]
, it seems possible to further improve the
(\log X)^{\epsilon}
factor in Theorem
1.1
to a slower-growing function
h_{0}(X)
that tends to infinity as
X\to\infty
. By following arguments in this paper with careful calculations, one should be able to prove at least
h_{0}(X)\leq\exp((\log\log X)^{c})
for some
0<c<1
.

Recently, Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
9
]
extended their result to
1
-bounded complex multiplicative functions that might vanish for many primes. For introducing this extension and our general results, we need the following two definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function. We define
t_{0}=t_{0}(f,X)\in[-X,X]
to be (one of)
t\in[-X,X]
which minimizes the following distance

\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it};X)^{2}:=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{|f(p)|-\Re{f(p)p^{-it}}}{p}.

We say that a multiplicative function
g
is almost real-valued, if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{P}\\
g(p)\not\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{|g(p)|}{p}<\infty.

If
g
is almost real-valued, we let
t_{0}=0
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\Delta\geq 1
, a
d_{k}
-bounded multiplicative function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
(\alpha,\Delta)
-non-vanishing if, for any
2\leq w\leq z\leq\Delta
, we have

\sum_{w<p\leq z}\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\geq k\alpha\sum_{w<p\leq z}\frac{1}{p}-O\left(\frac{1}{\log w}\right).

We also define

P_{f}(x):=\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|-1}{p}\right).

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
1
-bounded,
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some small
\theta>0
and let
t_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
.
In recent paper
[
9
, Theorem 1.9]
, Matomäki and Radziwiłł proved that, for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
,

\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\cdot\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|=o(P_{f}(x)),

provided
h=h_{0}\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1+\frac{(|f(p)|-1)^{2}}{p}\right)
, where
h_{0}\to\infty
with
X\to\infty
.

It is natural to consider whether the Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorem can be extended to unbounded multiplicative functions, e.g.
d_{k}
-bounded multiplicative functions.

In
2021
, Mangerel
[
7
]
generalized Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorem to the
d_{k}
-bounded multiplicative functions. Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be
d_{k}
-bounded,
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some small
\theta>0
and let
t_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
. Mangerel proved that, for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
,

\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\cdot\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|=o(P_{f}(x))

for
h_{0}\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1+\frac{(|f(p)|-1)^{2}}{p}\right)\leq h\leq x
, whenever
h_{0}\to\infty
with
X\to\infty
.
Note that in
[
7
]
, his results did not include that in the almost real-valued case one may take
t_{0}=0
. However, there is no doubt that one can give the corresponding result in the almost real-valued case.

In this paper, we will improve Mangerel’s result.
Let

H(f,X,\epsilon):=\frac{(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)\log k}}{P_{f}(X)}\asymp\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)^{(1+\epsilon)\log k-1}\log X,

which is larger than a small power of
\log X
when
k\geq 2
.
We obtain the following theorem

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. For any
\epsilon,\theta>0
, let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be
d_{k}
-bounded
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
non-vanishing multiplicative function. If
h\in[H(f,X,\epsilon),X]
, then

\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\cdot\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|=o(P_{f}(X)).

for all but at most

o(X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
.

Remark
.

One can follow our arguments with careful calculations at every step to prove a quantitative version of Theorem
1.3
, namely,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\cdot\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|dx

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{\log P_{1}}{\log Q_{1}}\right)^{\min\{\frac{k\alpha}{2},1\}}P_{f}(X),

where
P_{1}
and
Q_{1}
will be defined as in (
2.1
).

Letting
f(n)=d_{k}(n)
, we immediately get Theorem
1.1
by replacing
\epsilon
by
\frac{\epsilon}{k\log k}
in Theorem
1.3
.
Our arguments rely on the Matomäki-Radziwiłł methods, which were first introduced in
[
8
]
. Let us use (
1.2
) as an example to introduce their method. For the “relatively long” short intervals, say
h\geq X/(\log X)^{c}
for some
c>0
, one can utilize Lipschitz type results to show that (
1.2
) holds for all
x\in[X,2X]
. For the “relatively short” short intervals, one uses Perron’s formula and considers
L_{2}
norm of the left hand side of (
1.2
). Then the main terms (integrals around
t=0
) of the “short average” and “long average” are cancelled, so one only needs to show that the second moment with
t
far away form zero is small. Their two powerful tools for controlling the second moment are Ramaré’s identity and amplification techniques.

Our new idea is motivated by the methods of Matomäki-Radziwiłł-Tao
[
10
]
, which allows us to restrict our partial sums to integers with relatively small number of prime factors. Let
\Omega(n)
denote the total number of prime factors of
n
(counted with multiplicity). For instance, for the divisor function
d(n)
, we restrict our attention to this set
\{n\in[X,2X]:\Omega(n)\leq 2(1+\epsilon)\log\log X\}
. The reason we can do this is that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\sim X\\
|\Omega(n)-2\log\log X|\geq\epsilon\log\log X\end{subarray}}d(n)=o(X\log X)

(see Lemma
2.3
below).

More generally, for
d_{k}
-bounded multiplicative functions we consider the set

\{n\in[X,2X]:\Omega(n)\leq(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(X)\log X)\}
.

This is advantageous when we apply the mean-value theorem (see Lemma
4.3
below). In the mean-value theorem, one will get an upper bound consisting of a diagonal term and an off-diagonal term. The off-diagonal term will be easily controlled by Henriot’s bound (Lemma
4.2
). However, in case of the diagonal term, e.g.
\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}d_{k}(n)^{2}
, if we directly apply Shiu’s bound, as Mangerel did, we will get the upper bound
X(\log X)^{k^{2}-1}
which is larger than we can afford to have. Fortunately, when we just consider those integers which have at most
(1+\epsilon)k\log\log X
prime factors, we have a pointwise upper bound
d_{k}(n)\leq(\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)k\log k}
. In this way, we get the upper bound
X(\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)k\log k+k-1}
for the diagonal term.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we outline the proof of Theorem
1.3
by reducing Theorem
1.3
to Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, dealing with the “relatively short” and “relatively long” short intervals cases separately, and we will prove Theorem
1.2
in the end of this section. In Section
3
, we will collect some Halász type results which will be used in proving Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, and in the end of Section
3
the almost real-valued case of Proposition
2.2
. Then in Section
4
, we will follow Matomäki-Radziwiłł arguments to prove Proposition
2.1
.

2.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we will firstly state Shiu’s bound which we will use many times and show that we can reduce to the case where
n
is supported on certain subset of
[X,2X]
. Then we state two propositions, one is a Matomäki-Radziwiłł type bound for almost all intervals, the other is a Halász type bound for all “relatively long” short intervals. In the end of the section we will give the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.2
.

Lemma 2.1

(Shiu’s bound)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. Let
\sqrt{X}<Y\leq X
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
Y^{\delta}\leq y\leq Y
. Then

\sum_{Y-y<n\leq Y}|f(n)|\ll_{k,\delta}yP_{f}(X).

Proof.

See
[
12
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
. We define

\displaystyle P_{1}:=\exp((\log\log X)^{1/2}),\quad Q_{1}:=(\log X)^{\epsilon_{0}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle P_{2}:=\exp((\log\log X)^{2}),\quad Q_{2}:=\exp((\log\log X)^{100})

(2.2)

Let
A
denote the set of all
n\in(X,2X]
having at least one prime factor in each interval
[P_{j},Q_{j}]
for
j\in\{1,2\}
.

The following lemma says that one can restrict the sums into
n\in A
. This idea is inspired by the Matomäki-Radziwiłł decomposition (see
[
8
, Section 2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X>0
be sufficiently large and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
d_{k}
-bounded
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1]
. Let

A
as the above.
For any
0<h<X
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
n\not\in A\end{subarray}}|f(n)|=o(hP_{f}(X))

for all but at most
o(X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
.

Proof.

We first notice that, for
j=1,2
, by the Taylor expansion

\prod_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\log\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)\right)\ll\exp\left(-\sum_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right).

Since
f
is
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing, by Mertens’ theorem

\sum_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\geq k\alpha\sum_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\frac{1}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log P_{j}}\right)\geq k\alpha\log\left(\frac{\log Q_{j}}{\log P_{j}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\log P_{j}}\right).

Thus we have

\prod_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)=o(1).

(2.3)

By Shiu’s bound (Lemma
2.1
) and (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
n\not\in A\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 2X+h\\
n\not\in A\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\sum_{n-h\leq x<n}1\leq h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 2X+h\\
n\not\in A\end{subarray}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle hX\prod_{p\leq 2X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|-1}{p}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\prod_{p\in[P_{j},Q_{j}]}\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)=o\left(hX\prod_{p\leq 2X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|-1}{p}\right)\right).

∎

The next lemma tells us that one can ignore integers with large number of prime factors.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X>0
be large and
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. Let
f
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\ll_{\epsilon_{0}}n^{\epsilon_{0}}
for any
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Let

B_{\epsilon}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\Omega(n)\leq(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(x)\log X)\}.

For any
\delta>0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
X<n\leq 3X\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\ll XP_{f}(X)(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{-(1+\epsilon)\log(1+\delta)+\delta}.

(2.4)

Furthermore, for any
0<h<X
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
x<n\leq x+h\end{subarray}}|f(n)|=o(hP_{f}(X))

(2.5)

for all but at most
o(X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
.

Proof.

Since

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
x<n\leq x+h\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
X<n\leq 3X\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\sum_{n-h\leq x<n}1\ll h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
X<n\leq 3X\end{subarray}}|f(n)|.

(
2.5
) follows if we can show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
X<n\leq 3X\end{subarray}}|f(n)|=o(XP_{f}(X)).

(2.6)

This follows from (
2.4
) with
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2(1+\epsilon)}
. Hence it suffices to establish (
2.4
).

By Shiu’s bound, we have, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\not\in B_{\epsilon}\\
X<n\leq 3X\end{subarray}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{X<n\leq 3X}|f(n)|{\bf 1}_{\Omega(n)>(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(x)\log X)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{X<n\leq 3X}|f(n)|(1+\delta)^{\Omega(n)-(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(x)\log X)}

\displaystyle\ll X(1+\delta)^{-(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(x)\log X)}\prod_{p\leq 3X}\left(1+\frac{(1+\delta)|f(p)|-1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll XP_{f}(X)(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{-(1+\epsilon)\log(1+\delta)+\delta}.

Hence our claim follows.
∎

Now, we state two propositions and then combine these with the above lemmas to prove Theorem
1.3
.
Let

\rho_{k,\alpha}:=\frac{k\alpha}{3}-\frac{2k}{3\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)>0\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma:=\frac{1}{4}\min\left\{1,\rho_{k,\alpha}\right\}.

Recall
t_{0}
in Definition
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1

(Matomäki-Radziwiłł type bound)
.

Let
X>0
be a large number. Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
d_{k}
-bounded
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
\epsilon>0
,
A
and
B_{\epsilon/2}
as in Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, and
S=A\cap B_{\epsilon/2}
. Let
H(f,X,\epsilon)\leq h\leq h_{1}=X/(\log X)^{\sigma}
. Then,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in S\\
x<n\leq x+h\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\cdot\frac{1}{h_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in S\\
x<n\leq x+h_{1}\end{subarray}}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|^{2}dx=o(P^{2}_{f}(X)).

Proposition 2.2

(Halász bound)
.

Let
X>0
be a large number. Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
d_{k}
-bounded
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1]
.
One has, for all
h\in[X/(\log X)^{\sigma},x]
and
x\in[X,2X]

\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}-\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|\ll\frac{1}{(\log X)^{\sigma/25}}P_{f}(X)

and

\left|\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}f(n)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}f(n)n^{-it_{0}}\right|\ll\frac{1}{(\log X)^{\sigma/25}}P_{f}(X).

Now we prove Theorem
1.3
assuming Propositions

Lemma 2.4

In this section, we will prove Proposition
2.1
, which is similar to the argument in
[
8
, Section 8]
with slight modifications.

Before proving Proposition
2.1
, Let us first collect some standard mean and large value results for Dirichlet polynomials.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T\geq 1
and
N\geq 2
. Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\leq N}
be a sequence of complex numbers. Then the following bound holds

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}n^{it}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll T\sum_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}+T\sum_{n\leq N}\sum_{1\leq|m|\leq n/T}|a_{n}a_{n+m}|

\displaystyle\ll T\sum_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}+T\sum_{1\leq|m|\leq N/T}\sum_{|n|\leq N}|a_{n}a_{n+m}|.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

(Henriot)
Let
\theta\in(0,1]
and Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. Let
1\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq x^{3\theta/7}
be integers. Assume that
f(pm)=f(p)f(m)
whenever
p\mid r_{1}r_{2}
. Then, for all
x\geq y\geq x^{\theta}
and
K\in[1,x]
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq|k|\leq K\\
(r_{1},r_{2})\mid k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+y\\
r_{1}\mid n,r_{2}\mid n+k\end{subarray}}|f(n)f(n+k)|

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}K\frac{|f(r_{1})f(r_{2})|}{r_{1}r_{2}}y\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1+\frac{2|f(p)|-2}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid r_{1}r_{2}\\
p>K\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1-|f(p)|}{p}\right).

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

The next lemma is a direct corollary of the above lemmas and will be used frequently later.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X\geq Y>0
. Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
d_{k}
-bounded
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1]
. Suppose that
a_{n}
is a complex number for all
n\in[X,2X]
with
|a_{n}|\leq|f(n)|
. Then

\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{X\leq n\leq X+Y}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2}\ll\frac{T}{X^{2}}\sum_{X<n\leq X+Y}|a_{n}|^{2}+\frac{Y}{X}P_{f}(X)^{2}.

(4.1)

In particular, if
B^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\subset B_{\epsilon}
, then

\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{X\leq n\leq X+Y}\frac{f(n){\bf 1}_{B^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2}\ll\left(\frac{TY}{X^{2}}H(f,X,\epsilon)+\frac{Y}{X}\right)P_{f}(X)^{2}=:\frac{Y}{X}\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon).

(4.2)

Proof.

To prove the first statement, we apply Lemma
4.1
and use Lemma
4.2
with
r_{1}=r_{2}=1
to bound the off-diagonal term.

For proving (
4.2
), let
a_{n}=f(n){\bf 1}_{B^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}(n)
in (
4.1
). For all
1\leq n\leq X+Y
,

|a_{n}|\leq k^{(1+\epsilon)\log(P_{f}(X)\log X)}=(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)\log k}.

Hence using Shiu’s bound (Lemma
2.1
)

\sum_{n\leq X+Y}|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\max_{X<n\leq X+Y}|a_{n}|\sum_{X<n\leq X+Y}|f(n)|\ll Y(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)\log k}P_{f}(X).

Recall that

H(f,X,\epsilon):=\frac{(P_{f}(X)\log X)^{(1+\epsilon)\log k}}{P_{f}(X)},

so the lemma follows.
∎

For the rest of the paper, we say that
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{R}
is well spaced if
|t-r|\geq 1
for all
t,r\in\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
\{a_{n}\}_{n\leq N}
is a sequence of complex numbers and
\mathcal{T}\subset[-T,T]
is well spaced. Then

\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left|\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2}\ll\min\left\{\left(1+\frac{T}{X}\right)\log X,\left(1+|\mathcal{T}|\frac{T^{1/2}}{X}\right)\log T\right\}\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Proof.

See
[
5
, Theorems 9.4 and 9.6]
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k,T\geq 1
,
P\geq 10
. For all prime
p\in[P,2P]
, let
a_{p}
be a complex number with
|a_{p}|\leq k
. Let

P(s)=\sum_{P<p\leq 2P}\frac{a_{p}}{p^{s}}

for
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for any well spaced
\mathcal{T}\subset[-T,T]
, one has

(i)

\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}|P(1+it)|^{2}\ll_{k}\frac{1}{\log P}\left(1+|\mathcal{T}|\exp\left(-\frac{\log P}{(\log T)^{2/3+\epsilon}}\right)(\log T)^{2}\right).

(ii)

Let
V\geq 1
. If
\mathcal{T}
consists only of
t\in[-T,T]
with
|P(1+it)|\geq V^{-1}
, then

|\mathcal{T}|\ll T^{2\frac{\log V}{\log P}}V^{2}\exp\left(2k\frac{\log T}{\log P}\log\log T\right).

Proof.

The first statement is
[
8
, Lemma 11]
. The second one follows from the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 8]
with an extra coefficient
|a_{p}|^{2\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log P}\rceil}
which is bounded by
k^{2\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log P}\rceil}\ll\exp(2\log k\frac{\log T}{\log P})

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
d_{k}
-bounded and
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing multiplicative function for some
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1]
. Let
H\geq 1
and
Q\geq P\geq 1
. Let
B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
be as in Lemma
2.3
,
a_{m},b_{m}
and
c_{p}
be complex sequences such that
|a_{m}|,|b_{m}|\leq|f(m){\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(m)|
and
|c_{p}|\leq|f(p)|
with
a_{mp}=b_{m}c_{p}
whenever
p\nmid m
and
P\leq p\leq Q
. For
v\in[H\log P,H\log Q]
, let

Q_{v,H}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\leq p\leq Q\\
e^{v/H}<p\leq e^{(v+1)/H}\end{subarray}}\frac{c_{p}}{p^{s}}

and

R_{v,H}(s)=\sum_{Xe^{-v/H}<m\leq 2Xe^{-v/H}}\frac{b_{m}}{m^{s}}\frac{1}{\#\{P\leq q\leq Q:q\mid m,q\in\mathbb{P}\}+1}

and let
\mathcal{T}\subset[-T,T]\subset[-X,X]
. Then for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}}\left|\sum_{n\sim X}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2}dt\ll H\left(\log\left(\frac{Q}{P}\right)+1\right)\times\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{\mathcal{T}}|Q_{j,H}(1+it)R_{j,H}(1+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{H}+\frac{1}{P}+\left(\frac{\log P}{\log Q}\right)^{k\alpha}\right)\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon^{\prime})

where
\mathcal{I}
is the interval
\lfloor H\log P\rfloor\leq j\leq H\log Q
.

Proof.

Since the upper bound in the above lemma looks slightly different from the upper bounds in
[
8
, Lemma 12]
, we will give a brief proof which is essentially same as the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Let
s=1+it
,
\mathcal{P}=\prod_{P\leq p\leq Q}p
, and
\omega(n;P,Q)=\#\{P\leq p\leq Q:p\mid n\}
. By Ramaré’s identity,

\displaystyle\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}}Q_{v,H}(s)R_{v,H}(s)+\sum_{X{e^{-1/H}}<m\leq Xe^{1/H}}\frac{d_{m}}{m^{s}}+\sum_{2X<m\leq 2Xe^{1/H}}\frac{d_{m}}{m^{s}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Xe^{-1/H}<mp\leq 2Xe^{1/H}\\
p\in[P,Q],p\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{e_{m,p}}{(mp)^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 2X\\
(n,\mathcal{P})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=:B_{1}(s)+B_{2}(s)+B_{3}(s)+B_{4}(s)+B_{5}(s)

where

|d_{m}|\leq|f(m)|{\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(m)\quad\text{and}\quad|e_{m,p}|\ll|f(mp)|{\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(mp)+|f(p)f(m)|{\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}}(m).

First, we notice that by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\int_{\mathcal{T}}|B_{1}(s)|^{2}dt\ll H\left(\log\left(\frac{Q}{P}\right)+1\right)\times\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{\mathcal{T}}\left|Q_{v,H}(1+it)R_{v,H}(1+it)\right|^{2}dt.

By Lemma
4.3
, it is not difficult to show that

\int_{\mathcal{T}}|B_{2}(s)|^{2}ds\ll\frac{1}{H}\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathcal{T}}|B_{3}(s)|^{2}ds\ll\frac{1}{H}\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon^{\prime}).

Now we focus on the integral of
|B_{4}(s)|^{2}
. By Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}}|B_{4}(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll T\sum_{X/3<n\leq 6X}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]\\
m_{1},m_{2}\\
n=p_{1}^{2}m_{1}=p_{2}^{2}m_{2}\end{subarray}}|e_{m_{1}p_{1},p_{1}}e_{m_{2}p_{2},p_{2}}|

\displaystyle+T\sum_{0<|k|\leq 6X/T}\sum_{X/3<n\leq 6X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]\\
m_{1},m_{2}\\
p_{1}^{2}m_{1}=n\\
p_{2}^{2}m_{2}=n+k\end{subarray}}\frac{|e_{m_{1}p_{1},p_{1}}e_{m_{2}p_{2},p_{2}}|}{n(n+k)}

Defining now, for each pair
(p_{1},p_{2})
and
k\geq 0
, the multiplicative function
g_{p_{1},p_{2}}
such that

g_{p_{1},p_{2}}(p^{k})=|f(p^{k})|
when
p\not\in\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
and

g_{p_{1},p_{2}}(p^{k})=\max\{1,|f(p^{k+1})|,|f(p^{k+2})|\}

when
p\in\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
, we obtain the previous expression is

\displaystyle\ll_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{T}{X^{2}}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]}\sum_{\frac{X}{3[p_{1},p_{2}]^{2}}<n\leq\frac{6X}{[p_{1},p_{2}]^{2}}}g_{p_{1},p_{2}}(n)^{2}{\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(n)}

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{X^{2}}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<|k|\leq 6X/T\\
(p_{1}^{2},p_{2}^{2})\mid k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X/3<n\leq 6X\\
p_{1}^{2}\mid n,p_{2}^{2}\mid n+k\end{subarray}}g_{p_{1},p_{2}}(n)g_{p_{1},p_{2}}(n+k).

Applying now, for each
p_{1},p_{2}
, Lemmas
2.1
and
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|B_{4}(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X/3<n\leq 6X\\
p_{3},p_{4}\in[P,Q]\end{subarray}}|g_{p_{3},p_{4}}(n){\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(n)}|\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]}\frac{1}{[p_{1},p_{2}]^{2}}\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|-1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in[P,Q]}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}p_{2}^{2}}\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{2|f(p)|-2}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{P}\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon^{\prime}).

For the case
B_{5}(s)
, we apply Lemma
4.3
with
f=f{\bf 1}_{(n,\mathcal{P})=1}
, which is also multiplicative, and Lemma
2.1
. Recall that
f
is
(\alpha,X^{\theta})
-non-vanishing, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}}|B_{5}(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 2X\\
(n,\mathcal{P})=1\end{subarray}}|f{\bf 1}_{B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}}{\bf 1}_{(n,\mathcal{P})=1}(n)|^{2}+(P_{f{\bf 1}_{(n,\mathcal{P})=1}}(X))^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X}H(f,X,\epsilon^{\prime})P_{f}(X)P_{f{\bf 1}_{(n,\mathcal{P})=1}}(X)+(P_{f{\bf 1}_{(n,\mathcal{P})=1}}(X))^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X}H(f,X,\epsilon^{\prime})P_{f}(X)\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p){\bf 1}_{p\in\mathcal{P}}|-1}{p}\right)+\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p){\bf 1}_{p\in\mathcal{P}}|-1}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X}H(f,X,\epsilon^{\prime})P_{f}(X)^{2}\prod_{p\in[P,Q]}\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)+P_{f}(X)^{2}\prod_{p\in[P,Q]}\left(1-\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T}{X}H(f,X,\epsilon^{\prime})\left(\frac{\log P}{\log Q}\right)^{k\alpha}P_{f}(X)^{2}+\left(\frac{\log P}{\log Q}\right)^{2k\alpha}P_{f}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{\log P}{\log Q}\right)^{k\alpha}\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon^{\prime})

∎

Lemma 4.7

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
x\asymp X
be sufficiently large and

be as in Proposition

. If

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\delta^{-1}}^{\delta^{-1}}F(1+it_{0}+it)\left(\frac{(x+h)^{1+it_{0}+it}-x^{1+it_{0}+it}}{1+it_{0}+it}-\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\frac{(x+h_{1})^{1+it}-x^{1+it}}{h_{1}(1+it)}\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle=o(hP_{f}(X)).

Firstly, by Shiu’s bound (Lemma

\max_{|t|\leq\delta^{-1}}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|\ll\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X\leq n\leq 4X}|f(n)|\ll P_{f}(X).

Secondly, we see that, for

\displaystyle\frac{(x+h)^{1+it_{0}+it}-x^{1+it_{0}+it}}{1+it_{0}+it}-\int_{x}^{x+h}u^{it_{0}}du\frac{(x+h_{1})^{1+it}-x^{1+it}}{h_{1}(1+it)}

\displaystyle\leq h\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in[x,x+h]\\
|t|\leq\delta^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left\{u^{it}-\frac{1}{h_{1}}\int_{x}^{x+h_{1}}v^{it}dv\right\}.

Proposition 4.4

j\in\{1,2\}
, let

Q_{v,j}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{j}\leq p\leq Q_{j}\\
e^{v/H}\leq p\leq e^{(v+1)/H}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{s}},\quad R_{v,j}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Xe^{-v/H}\leq m\leq 2Xe^{-v/H}\\
m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(m)}{m^{s}(1+\omega_{[P_{j},Q_{j}]}(m))},

where
S_{j}
is the set of those integers which have at most
(1+\epsilon/2)\log(P_{f}(x)\log X)-1
prime factors and have at least one prime factor in every interval
[P_{i},Q_{i}]
with
i\in\{1,2\}\setminus\{j\}
. Note that

Q_{v,j}
is non-zero only when

v\in\mathcal{I}_{j}:=\{v:\lfloor H\log P_{j}\rfloor\leq v\leq H\log Q_{j}\}.

We split
[\delta^{-1},(X/h)(\log X)^{\beta}]=:[T_{0},T]
into a disjoint union

\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}\cup\mathcal{U},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}:=\{t\in[T_{0},T]:|Q_{v,1}(1+it_{0}+it)|\leq e^{-\alpha_{1}v/H}\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}:=\{t\in[T_{0},T]:|Q_{v,2}(1+it_{0}+it)|\leq e^{-\alpha_{2}v/H}\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{I}_{2}\}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:=[T_{0},T_{1}]\setminus(\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}).

Following the arguments in
[
8
, section 8]
, and using Lemma
4.6
with
H=H
,
P=P_{j}
,
Q=Q_{j}
,
a_{m}=f(m){\bf 1}_{S}(m)
,
b_{m}=f(m){\bf 1}_{S_{j}}(m)
and
c_{p}=f(p)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}_{j}}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll H\log\left(\frac{Q_{j}}{P_{j}}\right)\times\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{j}}|Q_{v,j}(1+it_{0}+it)R_{v,j}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{H}+\frac{1}{P_{j}}+\left(\frac{\log P_{j}}{\log Q_{j}}\right)^{k\alpha}\right)\mathfrak{S}(T,X,f,\epsilon/2).

By our choice of
H,P_{j},Q_{j}
, we can see that the second term contributes in total to integrals over all
\mathcal{T}_{j}
,

\ll\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{H}+\frac{1}{P_{j}}+\left(\frac{\log P_{j}}{\log Q_{j}}\right)^{k\alpha}\right)\mathfrak{S}(X/h,X,f,\epsilon/2)=o((P_{f}(X))^{2}).

Thus

\int_{T_{0}}^{T}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|dt\ll E_{1}+E_{2}+\int_{\mathcal{U}}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|dt+o(P_{f}(X))^{2}).

where

E_{j}:=H\log Q_{j}\cdot\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{j}}|Q_{v,j}(1+it_{0}+it)R_{v,j}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt.

(1) Bounding
E_{1}
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and the mean-value lemma (Lemma
4.3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\ll H\log Q_{1}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}e^{-2\alpha_{1}v/H}\left(\frac{T}{Xe^{-v/H}}H(f,X,\epsilon/2)+1\right)P_{f}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll H\log Q_{1}\times|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\times\max_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\left\{e^{-2\alpha_{1}v/H}\left(\frac{e^{v/H}}{H(f,X,\epsilon)}H(f,X,\epsilon/2)+1\right)\right\}P_{f}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll H^{2}(\log Q_{1})^{2}P_{1}^{-2\alpha_{1}}\left(\frac{Q_{1}}{H(f,X,\epsilon)}H(f,X,\epsilon/2)+1\right)P_{f}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{P_{1}^{1/100}}P_{f}(X)^{2}=o(P_{f}(X)^{2}).

(2) Bounding
E_{2}
.
In this case, we split further

\mathcal{T}_{2}=\bigcup_{r\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\mathcal{T}_{2,r},

where

\mathcal{T}_{2,r}=\{t\in\mathcal{T}_{1}:|Q_{r,1}(1+it_{0}+it)|>e^{-\alpha_{1}r/H}\}.

Therefore, for some
v\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
and
r\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
,

E_{2}\ll H\log Q_{2}\cdot\#\mathcal{I}_{2}\cdot\#\mathcal{I}_{1}\cdot e^{-2\alpha_{2}v/H}\times\int_{\mathcal{T}_{2,r}}|R_{v,2}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt.

On
\mathcal{T}_{2,r}
, we have
|Q_{r,{1}}(1+it_{0}+it)|>e^{-\alpha_{1}r/H}
. Therefore, for any
l\geq 1
, multiplying the right hand side by the term
(|Q_{r,1}(1+it_{0}+it)|e^{\alpha_{1}r/H})^{2l}\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\ll(H\log Q_{2})^{3}\cdot e^{-2\alpha_{2}v/H}\times\exp(2l\cdot\alpha_{1}r/H)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathcal{T}_{2,r}}|Q_{r,1}(1+it_{0}+it)^{l}R_{v,2}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt.

Choosing

l=l_{v,r}=\left\lceil\frac{v/H}{r/H}\right\rceil\leq\frac{v}{r}+1,

we get

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\ll(H\log Q_{2})^{3}\cdot\exp(-v/(50H)+(1/2-1/25)r/H)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathcal{T}_{2,r}}|Q_{r,1}(1+it_{0}+it)^{l}R_{v,2}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt.

Now, we use Lemma
4.7
which gives

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|Q_{r,1}(1+it_{0}+it)^{l}R_{v,2}(1+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\frac{T}{X}H(f,X,\epsilon/2)+1)P_{f}(X)^{2}k^{2l}(l+1)!^{2}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H(f,X,\epsilon/2)}{H(f,X,\epsilon)}+1\right)P_{f}(X)^{2}\exp(2l\log(kl)).

By the definition of
l
,

\displaystyle l\log(kl)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{v}{r}+1\right)\log(k(v/r+1))\leq\frac{2v}{H}\frac{\log\log Q_{2}}{\log P_{1}}\leq\frac{2v}{H}\frac{100\log\log\log X}{(\log\log X)^{1/2}}.

Recalling the definition of
P_{j}
and
Q_{j}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\ll P_{f}(X)^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{v}{51H}+\frac{r}{2H}+3\log(H\log Q_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\ll P_{f}(X)^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{51}\log P_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log Q_{1}+3\log(P_{1}^{1/6}\log Q_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\ll P_{f}(X)^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{51}(\log\log X)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log\log X+3\log\log X\right)

\displaystyle=o(P_{f}(X)^{2}).

(3).Bounding
.
\int_{\mathcal{U}}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt
.
Let
P=\exp((\log X)^{1-\epsilon_{1}})
for some small
\epsilon_{1}
to be chosen later,
Q=\exp{(\log X/100k^{2}\log\log X)}
and
H_{0}:=\log Q/\log P
. By Lemma
4.6
the integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}|F(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll H_{0}^{2}(\log X)^{2}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)R_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{H_{0}}+\frac{1}{P}+\left(\frac{\log P}{\log Q}\right)^{k\alpha}\right)\mathfrak{S}(X/h,X,f,\epsilon/2),

where

Q_{v,H_{0}}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\leq p\leq Q\\
e^{v/H_{0}}<p\leq e^{(v+1)/H_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{s}},\quad R_{v,H_{0}}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Xe^{-v/H_{0}}<m\leq 2Xe^{-v/H_{0}}\\
m\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{f(m)}{m^{s}(1+\omega_{[P,Q]}(m))}

and
v\in[\lfloor H_{0}\log P\rfloor,H_{0}\log Q]
.
Thus we only need to show that

H_{0}^{2}(\log X)^{2}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)R_{v,H}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt=o((P_{f}(X))^{2}).

Now we split the sum over
S=A\cap B_{\epsilon/2}
as the difference of a sum over
A
and a sum over
A\cap\overline{B_{\epsilon/2}}
. To estimate the sum over
A
, we use Halász type results, which requires multiplicativity. To estimate
A\cap\overline{B_{\epsilon/2}}
, we apply spareness, as demonstrated in Lemma
2.3
.
Let

R^{\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Xe^{-v/H}\leq m\leq 2Xe^{-v/H}\\
m\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{f(m)}{m^{s}(1+\omega_{[P,Q]}(m))}

and

R^{\prime\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Xe^{-v/H}\leq m\leq 2Xe^{-v/H}\\
m\in A\cap\overline{B_{\epsilon/2}}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(m)}{m^{s}(1+\omega_{[P,Q]}(m))}.

We will prove that both

\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)R^{\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)R^{\prime\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt

are
o\left(\frac{(P_{f}(X))^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}(\log X)^{2}}\right)
. For the rest of paper we let
\tilde{R}(s)
denote
R^{\prime}(s)
or
R^{\prime\prime}(s)
. For convenience, we will use a discrete sum to control the integral over
\mathcal{U}
, i.e. we find a well-spaced set
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{U}
such that

\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)\tilde{R}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt\leq 2\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)\tilde{R}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}.

We first claim that the size of
\mathcal{T}
is not too large. By the definition of
\mathcal{U}
, for each
t\in\mathcal{T}
, there is
v\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
such that
|Q_{v,H_{0}}(s)|>e^{-\alpha_{2}v/H_{0}}
. Recall that
P_{2}=\exp((\log\log X)^{2})
. We can apply Lemma
4.5

(ii)
to get that for some small
\eta>0
,

|\mathcal{T}|\ll X^{1/2-\eta+o(1)}.

We split
\mathcal{T}
in order to use a pointwise bound and a large value result. Let

\mathcal{T}_{L}=\{t\in\mathcal{T}:|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|\geq(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}-10}\}

and

\mathcal{T}_{S}=\{t\in\mathcal{T}:|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|<(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}-10}\}.

By Lemma
4.4
and noticing that
P_{f}(X)\ll(\log X)^{k-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{S}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)\tilde{R}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll(\log X)^{-k^{2}-20}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}|\tilde{R}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll(\log X)^{-k^{2}-19}\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{|f(p)|^{2}-1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log X)^{-k^{2}-19}\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{(|f(p)|-1)^{2}}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{2|f(p)|-2}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log X)^{-k^{2}-19}(\log X)^{k^{2}-2k+1}P_{f}(X)^{2}\ll(\log X)^{-19}P_{f}(X).

Hence, we can focus on the sum over
\mathcal{T}_{L}
. By using Lemma
4.5

(ii)
again, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{L}|

\displaystyle\ll\exp\left(2\frac{\log(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+10}}{\log P}\log T+2\log(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+10}+2k\frac{\log T}{\log P}\log\log T\right)

\displaystyle\ll\exp((\log X)^{2\epsilon_{1}}).

The only difference between
R^{\prime}(s)
and
R^{\prime\prime}(s)
is the following.
For
R^{\prime}(s)
, we split
f(n){\bf 1}_{A}(n)
as the difference of two multiplicative functions, namely

f(n){\bf 1}_{A}(n)=f(n)(1-{\bf 1}_{(n,[P_{1},Q_{1}])=1})(1-{\bf 1}_{(n,[P_{2},Q_{2}])=1})
, then we apply Lemma
3.3
, getting

\max_{(\log X)^{\sigma/3}\leq|u|\leq X}|R^{\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|\ll\left(\frac{\log Q}{\log P}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{1}{(\log X)^{\sigma/6}}+\left(\frac{\log Q}{\log P}\right)^{2k}\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{\rho/3}}\right)P_{f}(X).

However, for
R^{\prime\prime}(s)
, we apply (
2.4
) to get that

|R^{\prime\prime}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|\ll\frac{1}{(\log X)^{k\alpha(-(1+\epsilon/2)\log(1+\delta)+\delta)}}P_{f}(X),

for some sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
Thus by Lemma
4.5

(i)
and the above bound, for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{L}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)\tilde{R}_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{\log Q}{\log P}\right)^{2k}\left(\frac{1}{(\log X)^{\sigma/3}}+\left(\frac{\log Q}{\log P}\right)^{4k}\frac{(\log\log X)^{2}}{(\log X)^{2\rho/3}}+\frac{1}{(\log X)^{k\alpha(-(2+\epsilon)\log(1+\delta)+\delta)}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\frac{\exp((\log X)^{2\epsilon_{1}})}{\exp((\log X)^{1/3-2\epsilon_{1}})}\right)\frac{P_{f}(X)^{2}}{\log P}.

Combining the above estimates and choosing
\epsilon_{1}>0
sufficiently small depending on
k,\sigma,\rho,\alpha,\epsilon
, we obtain

H_{0}^{2}(\log X)^{2}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|Q_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)R_{v,H_{0}}(1+it_{0}+it)|^{2}dt=o((P_{f}(X))^{2}).

∎

Constraints on the Dirac spectrum from chiral symmetry restoration
Matteo Giordano

Abstract
I derive constraints on the Dirac spectrum in the chirally symmetric phase of a gauge theory with two massless fermion flavors. Using only general properties of correlation functions of scalar and pseudoscalar bilinears, I prove that in the chiral limit of vanishing fermion mass m m the corresponding susceptibilities and all their derivatives with respect to m 2 m^{2} must be finite. I then use the resulting spectral constraints to show that effective breaking of the anomalous U ​ ( 1 ) A \mathrm{U}(1)_{A} symmetry is allowed in the SU ​ ( 2 ) A \mathrm{SU}(2)_{A} symmetric phase in the chiral limit, and leads to distinctive spectral features: (i) the spectral density must develop a singular O ⁡ ( m 4 ) / λ O(m^{4})/\lambda peak as m → 0 m\to 0 , (ii) the two-point eigenvalue correlator of near-zero modes must be singular, and (iii) near-zero modes cannot be localized. Moreover, in the symmetric phase the topological charge distribution must be indistinguishable from that of an ideal gas of instantons and anti-instantons of vanishing density, to leading order in m m .

Restoration of chiral symmetry is signalled by correlation functions
of local operators related by chiral transformations becoming equal in
the chiral limit. (Here and below, this is always understood to be
taken after the thermodynamic limit.) Susceptibilities, i.e.,
connected correlation functions integrated over spacetime (normalized
by the four-volume), are then also equal in the chiral limit, provided
that the zero-momentum limit corresponding to spacetime integration
commutes with the chiral limit, which is expected to be the case in
the high-temperature symmetric phase where the correlation length of
the system is finite. (The critical temperature is excluded in the
case of continuous transitions, since the correlation length diverges
there.) In the scalar and pseudoscalar sector, susceptibilities can
be expressed in terms of the Dirac eigenvalues only, leading to
necessary conditions required of the Dirac spectrum for

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{A}
restoration. I now outline their derivation;
detailed calculations are reported in the Supplemental

C(m)=\frac{\cos\left({\alpha(m)}\pi/{2}\right)}{(1-\alpha(0))\pi/2}m^{1-\alpha(0)}(c+o(1))
, resulting in

\Delta=c
, and so in effective
\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
breaking if

c\neq 0
. In this case
\rho
cannot be
m^{2}
-differentiable, unless

\alpha(0)=-1
with
\alpha(m)
and
C(m)/m^{2}

m^{2}
-differentiable.
This leads to a surprising and highly nontrivial result: effective

\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
breaking in the symmetric phase requires a singular
peak in
\rho
tending to
C/\lambda
with
C=O(m^{4})
in the chiral
limit, if restoration is nonlocal and
\rho
an ordinary function. (In
Ref.
Aoki et al. 2012
a nonanalytic behavior

\rho\sim\lambda^{\alpha}
was considered, but only for
mass-independent
\alpha>0
. A behavior
\rho\sim 1/\lambda
was
proposed in
Refs.
Alexandru and Horváth 2019
;
Alexandru and Horváth 2021
;
Alexandru and Horváth 2022
in
a different context.) This is possible only if the chiral and
thermodynamic limits do not commute (otherwise one needs a term

m^{2}\Delta\delta(\lambda)
in
\rho
)
Azcoiti 2023
. In
this case one also finds

2\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,C(m)\lambda^{\alpha(m)}=\chi_{t}+o(m^{2})
for the
density of peak modes. This supports the topological origin of the
peak
Edwards et al. 2000
;
Dick et al. 2015
;
Kovács and Vig 2018
;
Ding et al. 2021
;
Vig and Kovács 2021
;
Kaczmarek et al. 2021
;
Kaczmarek et al. 2023
, and is consistent with the
instanton model of Ref.
Kovács 2024
. Moreover, it shows how
strongly suppressed the peak is in the chiral limit, requiring very
large volumes of order
\chi_{t}^{-1}\propto m^{-2}
to become fully
visible. The disappearance of the peak at finite
m
reported in
Refs.
Tomiya et al. 2017
;
Aoki et al. 2021
could then simply be a
finite-volume artifact.

Further constraints are obtained requiring finiteness of the
second-order terms in Eq. (
10
). The first two are

\displaystyle\frac{b_{N_{0}^{2}}-\chi_{t}}{2m^{2}}-m^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial m^{2}}\frac{n_{0}}{m^{2}}-2m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]

\displaystyle=O(m^{2})\,,

(15)

\displaystyle I^{(2)}[\hat{f},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=O(m^{0})\,,

where
\hat{f}=f-m^{2}f^{2}
,

b_{X}=\lim_{\mathrm{V}_{4}\to\infty}{\langle X\rangle_{c}}/{\mathrm{V}_{4}}
,

I^{(2)}[g_{1},g_{2}]=\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,g_{1}(\lambda)g_{2}(\lambda^{\prime})\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)\,,

(16)

and
\rho_{c}^{(2)}
is the connected two-point function,

\displaystyle\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathrm{V}_{4}\to\infty}\left(\mathrm{V}_{4}\left\langle\rho_{U}(\lambda)\rho_{U}(\lambda^{\prime})\right\rangle_{c}\right.

(17)

\displaystyle\left.-\delta(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\left\langle\rho_{U}(\lambda)\right\rangle\right)\,.

Together with the assumption
\langle N_{+}N_{-}\rangle=0
, these constraints
lead to remarkable results. I assume here that
\rho
and

\rho_{c}^{(2)}
are ordinary functions; if deltalike singularities are
present, the arguments below apply to their ordinary parts. Under
general integrability conditions the region

\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}>\delta>0
does not contribute to
m^{2}I^{(2)}
in the
chiral limit, for arbitrarily small
\delta
. If one assumes
furthermore that

\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)=A(m)+B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)
, with

B
vanishing at the origin and obeying the loose bound

|B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq b(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\,2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
with
0<\beta<1
, the first
constraint implies

-\pi^{2}A(0)=\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{\chi_{t}-b_{N_{0}^{2}}}{m^{2}}=\lim_{m\to 0}\lim_{\mathrm{V}_{4}\to\infty}\frac{\langle N_{0}\rangle^{2}}{\mathrm{V}_{4}m^{2}}=\Delta^{\prime}\,.

(18)

(This corrects a result of Ref.
Kanazawa and Yamamoto 2016
where exact
zero modes were not fully accounted for.) On the other hand, the
second constraint implies that

(\pi/4)^{2}A(0)=\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[\hat{f},\hat{f}]=0
, so
\Delta^{\prime}

vanishes. This is possible only if the measure of the positive random
variable
\frac{N_{0}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{V}_{4}}|m|}
is concentrated in zero in the
thermodynamic and chiral limit, implying in turn
\frac{\chi_{t}}{m^{2}}\to 0

and
\Delta=0
.
\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
breaking by
\Delta\neq 0
in the

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{A}
-restored phase requires then a singular behavior at
the origin of the two-point function
\rho_{c}^{(2)}
for
m\neq 0
.

The current wisdom is that in the high-temperature phase of QCD, low
modes are localized below a “mobility edge”,
\lambda_{c}
, and obey
Poisson statistics
Giordano and Kovács 2021
. For a purely Poisson
spectrum of
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}\mathrm{V}_{4}
eigenvalues one has

\rho_{\mathrm{P}\,c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=-\rho_{\mathrm{P}}(\lambda)\rho_{\mathrm{P}}(\lambda^{\prime})/\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}

Guhr et al. 1998
;
Kanazawa and Yamamoto 2016
, so one
expects

|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq C\rho(\lambda;m)\rho(\lambda^{\prime};m)
for some constant
C
if

\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda_{c}
. As localized modes fluctuate
independently of each other, one expects their correlations with modes
beyond the mobility edge to be proportional to their density. It is
then reasonable to assume

|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq C^{\prime}\rho(\lambda;m)
for

\lambda<\lambda_{c}<\lambda^{\prime}
. If
\lambda_{c}
remains finite in the
chiral limit, under the assumptions above
m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]
vanishes
in the chiral limit (and the second constraint in
Eq. (
15
) is satisfied), so
\Delta^{\prime}=0
. Barring
deltalike contributions, the persistence of

\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
-breaking topological effects in the symmetric phase
requires then that near-zero modes be not localized, either because

\lambda_{c}
vanishes in the chiral limit, or because another mobility
edge is present near zero at nonzero
m
. (A mobility edge exactly at
zero, as proposed in
Refs.
Alexandru and Horváth 2021
;
Alexandru and Horváth 2022
;
Meng et al. 2023
, is not
sufficient, as the zero-measure lines
\lambda=0
and
\lambda^{\prime}=0
can
be excluded from the integral defining
I^{(2)}
.) The second possibility
seems more likely in the presence of a singular spectral peak of
topological origin. In any case,
\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
breaking requires
strong (singular) repulsion of near-zero modes. Numerical evidence for
non-Poissonian repulsion toward the chiral limit was found using
staggered fermions
Ding et al. 2021
.

Two more constraints obtained at second order are

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{\partial}{\partial m^{2}}\frac{\chi_{t}}{m^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle<\infty\,,

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{u}^{2}}}{\partial m^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle<\infty\,.

(19)

The first one requires that in the symmetric phase

\chi_{t}=\Delta m^{2}+c_{1}m^{4}+o(m^{4})
. This follows (to all orders
in
m^{2}
) from the argument under Eq. (
9
), since
Q^{2}

admits a representation as the integral of a local
correlator
Hasenfratz et al. 1998
;
Lüscher 2004
;
Vicari and Panagopoulos 2009
. The
second constraint implies that the term in brackets in
Eq. (
11
) is of the form
d_{1}m^{2}+d_{2}m^{4}+o(m^{4})
;
this also follows to all orders from the argument under
Eq. (
9
).

The last two constraints at second order are

\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{8}{m^{2}}\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,\hat{f}(\lambda;m)\rho_{Q^{2}\,c}(\lambda;m)=\lim_{m\to 0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{uu}-\mathcal{C}_{ww}\right)\,,

(20)

where

\rho_{Q^{2}\,c}(\lambda;m)=\lim_{\mathrm{V}_{4}\to\infty}\left\langle Q^{2}\rho_{U}(\lambda)\right\rangle_{c}
, and

\frac{b_{Q^{4}}-\chi_{t}}{m^{2}}=O(m^{2})\,.

(21)

No constraint on
\rho
is found at second order; no more can be
obtained, as higher-order coefficients involve only higher-point
eigenvalue correlators. In Eq. (
20
),

\mathcal{C}_{uu}-\mathcal{C}_{ww}\propto\langle(iP_{a})^{2}(iP_{b})^{2}\rangle-\langle S_{a}^{2}S_{b}^{2}\rangle
(
a\neq b
), so if this is nonzero one has

\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
-breaking effects originating in non-negligible
correlations between zero and nonzero modes.

The constraint Eq. (
21
) requires that in the chiral
limit, to leading order in
m
and to the lowest nontrivial cumulant,
the distribution of
Q
be indistinguishable from that of an ideal
(noninteracting, Poisson-distributed) gas of instantons and
anti-instantons, with equal and vanishingly small densities

\chi_{t}/2=O(m^{2})
. In the symmetric phase, the coefficient
b_{2}
in the
partition function at finite
\theta
angle
Bonati et al. 2013
must
then tend to
-1/12
in the chiral limit. This leads one to expect an
early onset (near the transition temperature
T_{c}
) of an ideal
instanton gaslike behavior for physical quark masses (though not
necessarily that of the usual semiclassical dilute instanton
gas
Gross et al. 1981
;
Boccaletti and Nógrádi 2020
), as seen in the pure gauge
case
Bonati et al. 2013
. On the other hand, the results of
Ref.
Bonati et al. 2016
for
b_{2}
show large deviations from an
ideal gas up to
2T_{c}
, which calls for a deeper scrutiny of the size
of finite-
m
corrections. As will be shown elsewhere, chiral symmetry
restoration actually requires all cumulants to be those of an ideal
gas, a result already obtained in Ref.
Kanazawa and Yamamoto 2015

assuming a free energy analytic in
m^{2}
. (This does not mean that
typical gauge configurations must look like an ordinary ideal
instanton gas
Kanazawa and Yamamoto 2015
.)

Summarizing,
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{A}
restoration does not prevent effective

\mathrm{U}(1)_{A}
breaking in the chiral limit, but puts severe
constraints on the Dirac spectrum for it to happen: the spectral
density must develop a
O(m^{4})/\lambda
peak; the two-point function
of near-zero Dirac modes must be singular; near-zero modes cannot obey
Poisson statistics and be localized; and the distribution of the
topological charge must be the same as that of an ideal instanton gas
with density of order
m^{2}
. These predictions of distinctive spectral
features, that can lead to a more accurate characterization of the
quark-gluon plasma phase of QCD, should be verified by numerical
investigations. The necessary and sufficient condition for chiral
symmetry restoration in the scalar and pseudoscalar sector can
probably be generalized to other sectors and to more massless fermion
flavors by suitably adapting the proof.

I derive here the mass-derivative formula, Eq. (

III

III.1
Spectral density with power-series expansion around

\lambda=0

Assume that
\rho(\lambda;0)
exists and that, at least for
sufficiently small values of
m
,
\rho(\lambda;m)
admits a
convergent power expansion in
\lambda
within a mass-independent
finite radius
\epsilon
around
\lambda=0
,

\rho(\lambda;m)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{n}(m)\lambda^{n}\,,

(S29)

with
\rho_{n}(m)
finite in the chiral limit. One has (for

\delta<\epsilon
)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}\mathcal{C}_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta}^{2}d\lambda\,\frac{h(\lambda)}{\lambda^{2}}\rho(\lambda,0)+\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\left(-\frac{1}{4}\left(\rho_{0}(0)+\rho_{1}(0)\lambda\right)+h(\lambda)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{n+2}(0)\lambda^{n}\right)+\lim_{m\to 0}\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\frac{\rho_{0}(m)+\rho_{1}(m)\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+m^{2}h(\lambda)}\,,

(S30)

and only the last term is potentially divergent. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+m^{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2|m|}+O(1)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+m^{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{1}{|m|}+O(1)\,.

(S31)

Finiteness of
\mathcal{C}_{u}
in the chiral limit requires then

\rho_{0}(m)\frac{\pi}{2}+\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}=O(m)\,.

(S32)

If
\rho_{1}(m)=O(1/\ln\frac{1}{|m|})
, then
\rho_{0}(m)=O(m)
and
finiteness is obvious. If not, then
\rho_{1}(m)\ln\frac{1}{|m|}\to\infty

as
m\to 0
and
\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}
dominates over the
O(m)

term on the right-hand side, so that (for small enough
m
)

\rho_{1}(m)\leq 0
, since
\rho_{0}(m)=\rho(0;m)\geq 0
. One has for the
spectral density

\rho(\lambda;m)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}+O(m)+\rho_{1}(m)\lambda+O(\lambda^{2})\,.

(S33)

Take now

\lambda=\lambda_{\epsilon}(m)\equiv|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}\frac{2}{\pi}(1+\epsilon)
with
\epsilon>0
. Since

\lambda_{\epsilon}(m)=o(m^{0})
, and

\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon}(m)^{2}}{\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}}=O\left(\frac{m^{2}\ln^{2}\frac{1}{|m|}}{\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}}\right)=o(|m|\ln^{2}{\textstyle\frac{1}{|m|}})=o(m^{0})\,,

(S34)

one has

\rho\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}(m);m\right)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}\left(-\epsilon+o(m^{0})\right)\,,

(S35)

but then

0\leq\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{\rho\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}(m);m\right)}{-\frac{2}{\pi}\rho_{1}(m)|m|\ln\frac{1}{|m|}}=-\epsilon<0\,,

(S36)

which is a contradiction. One must then have
\rho_{0}(m)=O(m)
and

\rho_{1}(m)=O(1/\ln\frac{1}{|m|})
. Plugged into the expression for

\Delta
, this result gives

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}2\sum_{n=0}^{3}\rho_{n}(m)|m|^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{|m|}}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\left(z^{2}+1\right)^{2}}=\lim_{m\to 0}2\sum_{n=0}^{1}\rho_{n}(m)|m|^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\left(z^{2}+1\right)^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{\rho_{0}(m)}{|m|}\,,

(S37)

so
\Delta\neq 0
only if
\rho_{0}(m)=\kappa|m|+o(m)
with

\kappa\neq 0
. This is not possible if restoration is nonlocal, since
in that case
\rho
is infinitely differentiable in
m^{2}
at
m=0
.

III.2
Spectral density with power-law behavior near
\lambda=0

I now discuss the case when the spectral density of near-zero modes is
dominated by a (possibly singular) power-law behavior. I assume that

\rho(\lambda;0)
exists;

\rho(\lambda;m)=C(m)\lambda^{\alpha(m)}+\lambda r(\lambda;m)

with
-1<\alpha(m)<1
, at least for small but finite
m

|u(\lambda;m)/\lambda|<c\lambda^{\gamma}
for some
constant

and
\gamma>-1
. This set of rather loose assumptions on
the background
\lambda r(\lambda;m)
guarantees that this gives a
finite contribution to
\mathcal{C}_{u}
, avoiding the need for contrived
cancellations in order to achieve symmetry restoration. One has for
arbitrary
\delta>0

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}\mathcal{C}_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta}^{2}d\lambda\,\frac{h(\lambda)}{\lambda^{2}}\rho(\lambda;0)+\lim_{m\to 0}\left[\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\lambda r(\lambda;m)+C(m)\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\lambda^{\alpha(m)}\right]\,,

(S38)

where the first term is manifestly finite. For the second term

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\lambda r(\lambda;m)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}\left[r(0;m)\left(\ln\frac{\delta}{|m|}+o\left(\ln{\textstyle\frac{1}{|m|}}\right)\right)+\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\lambda^{2}f(\lambda;m)\frac{u(\lambda;m)}{\lambda}\right]<\infty\,,

(S39)

\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\lambda^{2}f(\lambda;m)\frac{u(\lambda;m)}{\lambda}\right|\leq\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+m^{2}}\left|\frac{u(\lambda;m)}{\lambda}\right|\leq c\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,\lambda^{\gamma}=\frac{c\delta^{\gamma+1}}{\gamma+1}\,,

(S40)

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{m\to 0}C(m)\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\lambda^{\alpha(m)}=\lim_{m\to 0}C(m)|m|^{\alpha(m)-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{|m|}}dz\,\frac{z^{\alpha(m)}}{z^{2}+1}

(S41)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{C(m)|m|^{\alpha(m)-1}\frac{\pi}{2}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha(m)}\,,&&&&\text{if }\alpha(0)\neq 1\,,\\
&\lim_{m\to 0}C(m)\ln\frac{2}{|m|}\,,&&&&\text{if
}\alpha(0)=1\,.\end{aligned}\right.

The request from chiral symmetry restoration is
|C_{0}|<\infty

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}2m^{2}\left[\int_{\delta}^{2}d\lambda\,\frac{h(\lambda)^{2}}{\lambda^{4}}\rho(\lambda;0)+\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)^{2}\lambda r(\lambda;m)+C(m)\int_{0}^{\delta}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)^{2}\lambda^{\alpha(m)}\right]\,.

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(m^{0})}{\frac{2}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha(m)}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(m)}\left({\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha(m)}{2}\frac{1+o(m^{0})}{\frac{2}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha(m)}+o(m^{0})\,,

(S52)

independently of
\delta
.
If
\alpha(0)=1
, setting
1-\alpha(m)=\epsilon(m)
one finds

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha(m)}\left({\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}\right)=1+\epsilon(m)O\left(\epsilon(m),{\textstyle\frac{m^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}\right)\,,

(S53)

so if
\epsilon=o\left(1/\ln\frac{1}{|m|}\right)

\frac{\mathcal{I}_{1-\epsilon(m)}\left({\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}\right)}{\ln{\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}}=\frac{1-e^{-\epsilon(m)\ln{\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}}\left(1+\epsilon(m)O\left(\epsilon(m),{\textstyle\frac{m^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}\right)\right)}{\epsilon(m)\ln{\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}(1+O(\epsilon(m)^{2}))}\mathop{\to}_{m\to 0}1\,.

(S54)

For
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(m)}
one has then

\lim_{m\to 0}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(m)}\left({\textstyle\frac{\delta}{|m|}}\right)=\mathcal{J}_{1}\left(\infty\right)=\frac{1}{2}
.

IV
Two-point function

IV.1
Finite
\rho_{c}^{(2)}(0,0;m)

Assuming

\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)=A(m)+B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)
, with

B
integrable, vanishing at the origin, and obeying the bound

|B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq b(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\,2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
for
b>0
,
0<\beta<1
, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}\left[A(m)\left(m\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\right)^{2}+I_{B}(m)\right]=A(0)\mathcal{I}_{0}(\infty)^{2}+\lim_{m\to 0}I_{B}(m)\,,

(S55)

\displaystyle I_{B}(m)

\displaystyle\equiv m^{2}\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,f(\lambda;m)f(\lambda^{\prime};m)B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)\,,

where
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
is defined in Eq. (
S50
).
Using the bound on
B
,

\displaystyle|I_{B}(m)|

\displaystyle\leq bm^{2}\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,f(\lambda;m)f(\lambda^{\prime};m)\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\,2}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq bm^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\int_{0}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,\frac{\left(z^{2}+z^{\prime\,2}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{(z^{2}+1)(z^{\prime\,2}+1)}

(S56)

\displaystyle=bm^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\phi\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\frac{r^{1+\beta}}{{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}r^{4}\left(\sin\phi\right)^{2}+r^{2}+1}\leq bm^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\phi\left\{1+\left(\frac{2}{\sin\phi}\right)^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\frac{r^{1-\beta}}{r^{2}+1}\right\}\,.

The last integral is finite, so
\lim_{m\to 0}I_{B}(m)=0
. Then, using

\mathcal{I}_{0}(\infty)=\frac{\pi}{2}
, one finds

\lim_{m\to 0}4m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]=\pi^{2}A(0)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[\hat{f},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}\left[A(m)\left(m\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\,\hat{f}(\lambda;m)\right)^{2}+\hat{I}_{B}(m)\right]=A(0)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0}(\infty)-\mathcal{J}_{0}(\infty)\right)^{2}+\lim_{m\to 0}\hat{I}_{B}(m)\,,

(S57)

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{B}(m)

\displaystyle\equiv m^{2}\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,\hat{f}(\lambda;m)\hat{f}(\lambda^{\prime};m)B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)\,,

where
\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}
is defined in Eq. (
S50
). Since

\displaystyle|\hat{I}_{B}(m)|

\displaystyle\leq bm^{2}\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,f(\lambda;m)f(\lambda^{\prime};m)\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\,2}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\,,

(S58)

it follows from Eq. (
S56
) that

\lim_{m\to 0}\hat{I}_{B}(m)=0
. Using

\mathcal{J}_{0}(\infty)=\frac{\pi}{4}
, one finds

\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[\hat{f},\hat{f}]=\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}A(0)
.

IV.2
Localized near-zero modes

Assume

|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq C\rho(\lambda;m)\rho(\lambda^{\prime};m)
for
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda_{c}
, and

|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\leq C^{\prime}\rho(\lambda;m)
for

\lambda<\lambda_{c}<\lambda^{\prime}
, for some mass-independent constants

C,C^{\prime}>0
; and that
|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|
is integrable

\forall m\geq 0
for
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda_{c}
. Assume also that

\lim_{m\to 0}\lambda_{c}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to 0}\left[Cm^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda_{c}}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\rho(\lambda;m)\right)^{2}+2C^{\prime}m^{2}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{c}}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\rho(\lambda;m)\int_{\lambda_{c}}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,f(\lambda^{\prime};m)\right]

(S59)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}\left(C\mathcal{C}_{u}^{2}+2C^{\prime}\mathcal{C}_{u}I_{L}(m)\right)\,,

where

\displaystyle I_{L}(m)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\lambda_{c}}^{2}d\lambda\,f(\lambda;m)\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}I_{L}(m)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to 0}\int_{\lambda_{c}}^{2}d\lambda\frac{h(\lambda)}{\lambda^{2}}=\lim_{m\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{c}}{2}\right)^{2}<\infty\,.

(S60)

It follows that
\lim_{m\to 0}m^{2}I^{(2)}[f,f]=0
if

\lim_{m\to 0}\mathcal{C}_{u}<\infty
. Since
0\leq\hat{f}\leq f
for

\lambda\in[0,2]
, a similar calculation shows that

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{m\to 0}I^{(2)}[\hat{f},\hat{f}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to 0}\left[C\mathcal{C}_{u}^{2}+2C^{\prime}\mathcal{C}_{u}I_{L}(m)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}^{4}}\int_{\lambda_{c}}^{2}d\lambda\int_{\lambda_{c}}^{2}d\lambda^{\prime}\,|\rho_{c}^{(2)}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime};m)|\right]<\infty\,.

(S61)

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the stochastic equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Y_{t}(x)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta Y_{t}(x)+Y_{t-}(x)^{\beta}\dot{L}^{\alpha}_{x,t},\qquad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}

where
L^{\alpha}
is a stable noise of index
\alpha
without negative jumps and
\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}
. We show that its solutions are unique in law when
1<\alpha<2
,
0<\beta<1
and
1<\alpha\beta
(see Theorem
1.3
). Proving uniqueness in law, also called
weak uniqueness
, is an important step for establishing that a model of an underlying system of interacting particles converges to a limit.

When
\alpha=2
the above equation is similar to the following SPDE

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Y_{t}(x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta Y_{t}(x)+Y_{t}(x)^{\beta}\dot{W}_{x,t}

where
\dot{W}
is the Gaussian space-time white noise. When
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
this describes the density process of the super-Brownian motion (SBM) in
\mathbb{R}
which can be obtained as the scaling limit of interacting branching Brownian motions. The weak uniqueness of (
1.2
) with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
follows from the martingale problem formulation of SBM using duality (see
[
Per02
, Theorem II.5.1]
). In the
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
case, the weak existence of (
1.2
) was proved by Mueller, Perkins
[
MP92
]
and the weak uniqueness was established by Mytnik
[
Myt98
]
. The question of pathwise uniqueness of (
1.2
) for
\beta>\frac{3}{4}
was settled by Mytnik and Perkins in 2011
[
MP11
]
. Some negative results are also known:
[
MMP14
]
proved pathwise non-uniqueness of solutions to (
1.2
) for
\beta\in(0,\frac{3}{4})
and
[
BMP10
]
showed pathwise non-uniqueness for
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
with an added non-trivial drift.

Let us now consider (
1.1
) where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\alpha\neq 2
, Mueller
[
Mue98
]
proved a certain short time (strong) existence of solution to (
1.1
) under the relations
d<\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{\alpha\beta-(1-\alpha)},\alpha\in(0,1)
. The weak existence was shown by
[
Myt02
]
under the relations
0<\alpha\beta<\frac{2}{d}+1
,
1<\alpha<\min(2,\frac{2}{d}+1)
. When
d=1
and
\alpha\beta=1
it is known that (
1.1
) describes the density of the super-Brownian motion with
\alpha
-stable branching mechanism; see
[
MP03
]
for more details. The weak uniqueness for this case was proved in
[
Myt02
]
while the same for the general case was left open (see
[
Myt02
, Remark 5.9]
). As stated earlier in our main result we resolve the question for the case
d=1
,
1<\alpha<2
,
0<\beta<1
and
1<\alpha\beta
.

It is known that pathwise uniqueness implies weak uniqueness for (
1.1
). But as the coefficient of the noise term in (
1.1
) is not Lipschitz, standard techniques such as Grönwall’s inequality cannot be used to prove pathwise uniqueness of solutions. However, more recently Yang and Zhou
[
YZ17
]
have established pathwise uniqueness for (
1.1
) in the regime
\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}<\beta<\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}
and
d=1
. This region partially, although not fully, overlaps with the one stated in Theorem
1.3
. See the Figure
1
for an illustration of the
(\alpha,\beta)
parameter space.

Figure 1.
Mytnik proved weak uniqueness for of (
1.1
) when
\beta=\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Yang, Zhou showed pathwise uniqueness of this equation when
(\alpha,\beta)
falls in the light gray region. Our main result, Theorem
1.3
, proves weak uniqueness for (
1.1
) when
(\alpha,\beta)
is in the dark gray region.

In the next subsection we precisely define our model and state the main theorem.

1.1.
Model and Main Result

To define our model and state the main result we need to introduce the following notations. Let
\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|
and
\lVert f\rVert_{p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}
for
p\geq 1
be the norms of the spaces
{\bf{L}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
{\bf{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
respectively. The norms on
{\bf{L}}^{\infty}([0,T]\times R)
and
{\bf{L}}^{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
are defined similarly. By
{\bf{L}}^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
we will mean the collection of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(s,x)|^{p}\,ds\,dx<\infty
for all
T\in(0,\infty)
. We also define
\mathcal{S}\equiv\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
to be the space of all smooth rapidly-decreasing functions defined on
\mathbb{R}
whose derivatives of all orders are also rapidly-decreasing. The subsets of
{\bf{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
containing all non-negative functions are denoted by
{\bf{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R})_{+}
and
\mathcal{S}_{+}\equiv\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})_{+}
respectively.

Let
M_{F}\equiv M_{F}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of all non-negative finite measures on the real line,
\mathbb{R}
, with the topology of weak convergence. We denote the space of all cádlág paths in
M_{F}
as
D\equiv D([0,\infty),M_{F})
. This space is equipped with the topology of weak convergence and
\mathcal{B}(D)
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
D
. Similarly,
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra of all Borel measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}
and for
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
we use
|E|
for the Lebesgue measure of
A
. For
\mu\in M_{F}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
we denote
\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,d\mu
. We will often identify a measurable function
f:(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))\to\mathbb{R}
with
f(x)\,dx
, where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure. In this case,
\langle f,\varphi\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\varphi(x)\,dx
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Suppose for each
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
with
|E|<\infty
,
\{L^{\alpha}_{t}(E)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp{(-\lambda L^{\alpha}_{t}(E))}=\exp{(\lambda^{\alpha}t|E|)},

for all
t\geq 0
,
\lambda\geq 0
. Then we call
L^{\alpha}
an
\alpha
-stable martingale measure on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
without negative jumps
.

Observe that
L^{\alpha}(E\times[0,t]):=L^{\alpha}_{t}(E)
is indeed a martingale measure in the sense of Walsh
[
Wal86
, Chapter 2]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
Y_{0}\in M_{F}
. Given an
\alpha
-stable martingale measure
L^{\alpha}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
without negative jumps, a two-parameter stochastic process
\{Y_{t}(x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
defined on the same probability space is said to solve (
1.1
) if the following hold.

(i)

Y
is adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

(ii)

The map
t\mapsto Y_{t}(x)\,dx
defines an
M_{F}
-valued cádlág process. In other words,
Y\in D([0,\infty),M_{F})
a.s..

(iii)

For all
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
t\geq 0
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle=\langle Y_{0},\psi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle\,ds+\int_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(Y_{s-}(x))^{\beta}\psi(x)L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds),

Recall that
[
Myt02
, Theorem 1.5]
guarantees the weak existence of such a solution
Y\equiv(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Also, it was shown in
[
Myt02
, Proposition 4.1]
that

\displaystyle Y\in D([0,\infty),M_{F})\cap{\bf{L}}^{\rho}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})\quad(1<\rho<3).

We need to recall some notions of existence and uniqueness for solutions to (
1.1
) that will be used in this article.

•

(
1.1
) is said to admit a
weak solution
with initial condition
Y_{0}
if there exists a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and an
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
-adapted pair
(Y,L^{\alpha})
such that
L^{\alpha}
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) holds.

•

Weak uniqueness
holds for (
1.1
) if whenever the pairs
(Y,L^{\alpha})
and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{L}^{\alpha})
satisfy (
1.4
) with the same initial condition, they have the same finite dimensional distributions.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
1<\alpha<2
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}<\beta<1
and
Y_{0}\in M_{F}
. Then weak uniqueness holds for solutions to (
1.4
), i.e. if
(Y,L^{\alpha})
and
(\tilde{Y},L^{\alpha})
are both weak solutions of (
1.4
) and
Y_{0}=\tilde{Y}_{0}
, then
Y
and
\tilde{Y}
have the same finite dimensional distributions.

We will now describe our approach for proving this result.

1.2.
Proof Strategy

An approach for showing weak uniqueness of stochastic equations is the following. First, one shows that solutions to (
1.4
) are equivalently also solutions of an appropriate martingale problem. Then it is enough to show that any two solutions to the martingale problem have the same one-dimensional distributions (cf.
[
EK86
, Theorem 4.4.2]
). We may define the (local) martingale problem as follows. For
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
and
t\geq 0
let

(1.5)

\displaystyle M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)=e^{-\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle}-e^{-\langle Y_{0},\psi\rangle}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\langle Y_{s-},\psi\rangle}\left(-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle\right)\,ds

where
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are the coordinate maps on
D
, i.e.
Y_{t}(\omega)=\omega(t)
for
\omega\in D
. A probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
(D,\mathcal{B}(D))
is said to be a solution of the (local) martingale problem for (
1.5
) if for all
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
we have that
\{M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)\}_{t}
is a (local) martingale under
\mathbb{P}
. We know from
[
Myt02
, Proposition 4.1]
that a solution to the local martingale problem (
1.5
) exists with stopping times

(1.6)

\displaystyle\gamma^{Y}(k):=\inf\left\{s\geq 0\middle|\int_{0}^{s}\lVert Y_{r}\rVert^{\alpha\beta}_{\alpha\beta}\,dr>k\right\},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\alpha\beta}
denotes the norm of the space
\bf{L}^{\alpha\beta}(\mathbb{R})
.
[
Myt02
, Proposition 4.1]
also guarantees that, when
Y_{0}\in M_{F}
, for all
t>0
we have
Y_{t}\in M_{F}
as well.

Since
Y_{0}\in M_{F}
is chosen arbitrarily, in light of the above discussion we can rephrase Theorem
1.3
into the equivalent result concerning the martingale problem (
1.5
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, any two solutions of (
1.5
) have same one-dimensional distributions.

Motivated by
[
Myt98
]
we use an
approximating duality
argument and would like to show the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
and
Y
be a solution to (
1.5
) where
Y_{0}
is as in Theorem
1.3
. Then there exists a sequence of processes
\{Z^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
, independent of
Y
, with
Z^{(n)}_{0}=\psi
for all
n\geq 1
such that for each
t\geq 0

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp(-\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\exp(-\langle Y_{0},Z^{(n)}_{t}\rangle).

By the virtue of
[
Myt96
, Theorem 1.3]
this will imply Theorem
1.4
.

Now we shall discuss a formal strategy of how one would prove Theorem
1.5
. Suppose
Y
solves (
1.5
) and
Z
solves SPDE given below

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z_{t}(x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}Z_{t}(x)+Z_{t-}(x)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\dot{L}^{\alpha\beta},\qquad Z_{0}=\psi

or equivalently the local martingale problem

(1.9)

\displaystyle M^{Z}_{t}(\varphi)=e^{-\langle\varphi,Z_{t}\rangle}-e^{-\langle\varphi,Z_{0}\rangle}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\langle\varphi,Z_{s-}\rangle}\left(-\langle\frac{1}{2}\Delta\varphi,Z_{s-}\rangle+\langle\varphi^{\alpha\beta},Z_{s-}^{\alpha},\rangle\right)\,ds,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{+}

is an
\mathcal{F}^{Z}_{t}
-local martingale. Then one could try to establish the following exponential duality relation

(1.10)

\mathbb{E}\exp(-\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle)=\mathbb{E}\exp(-\langle\varphi,Z_{t}\rangle).

Although this duality relationship holds, the required integrability conditions will fail to hold (see
[
EK86
, Theorem 4.4.11]
). Thus one uses the approximate duality technique. In this approach we construct an approximating sequence
Z^{(n)}
to
Z
using the framework of
[
Myt98
]
to prove the Theorem
1.5
.

However there are two key difficulties to overcome. First, we require suitable bounds on the moments of the solutions to (
1.5
) and the second difficulty is the fact that
M^{Y}(\psi)
, as defined above, are only local martingales. We prove the moment estimate result in Proposition
3.1
for the range of
\alpha
and
\beta
stated in Theorem
1.3
. From this we can show that
M^{Y}(\psi)
is indeed a martingale.

Remark 1.6
.

Note that the condition
\alpha\beta>1
is crucial for our argument and the technique of approximate duality. Consequently the case when
\alpha\beta<1
is not covered by this method.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1<\alpha<2
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}<\beta<1

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{t}(x)^{q})\leq C\,t^{-\frac{q}{2}}+C

Proof of Proposition

From (

(3.2)

\displaystyle Y_{t}(x)=P_{t}Y_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}zp_{t-r}(x-y)\tilde{N}_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr).

Let us define

\displaystyle\tau_{N}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mid\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{r}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}^{\alpha\beta}\,dr\geq N\right\},

\displaystyle I:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}zp_{t-r}(x-y)\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})\tilde{N}_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right\rvert^{q}\right]

\displaystyle c\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{2}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{2}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right\rvert^{q/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{2}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{2}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right\rvert^{q/2}\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle+c\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{2}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{2}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right\rvert^{q/2}\right].

Let

\displaystyle I\leq

\displaystyle c\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{2}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{2}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right\rvert^{p/2}\right]^{q/p}

\displaystyle c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{p}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right]^{q/p}

(3.4)

\displaystyle+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{q}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right],

where the second inequality above is due a fact about random sums (see the proof of
[
PZ07

). Now we use the definition of the PRM
N_{0}

for

\displaystyle I\leq

\displaystyle c+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{p}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right]

\displaystyle+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}}z^{q}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})N_{0}(\,dv,\,dy,\,dz,\,dr)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})\,dy\,dr\right]

\displaystyle+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})\,dy\,dr\right]

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c+c\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}+p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q})Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r\leq\tau_{N})\,dy\,dr,

as

.
From (
3.2

3

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{t}(x)^{q}\mathbf{1}(t<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(P_{t}Y_{0}(x))^{q}+c+c\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}+p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q})Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\,dr\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(P_{t}Y_{0}(x))^{q}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{t-r}(x-y)^{p}+p_{t-r}(x-y)^{q})\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]\,dr\,dy.

by applying Fubini’s theorem in the last line. Use the definition of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{t}(x)^{q}\mathbf{1}(t<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle c(P_{t}Y_{0}(x))^{q}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]\,dy

When

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{t}(x)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(t<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle c(P_{t}Y_{0}(x))^{\alpha\beta}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]\,dy.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[P_{s-t}\left(Y_{t}^{\alpha\beta}\right)(x)\mathbf{1}(t<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(P_{s}Y_{0}(x))^{\alpha\beta}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr\,(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s-t}(x-y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(y-z)\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{r}(z)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle c(P_{s}Y_{0}(x))^{\alpha\beta}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr\,(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[P_{s-r}\left(Y_{r}^{\alpha\beta}\right)(x)\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle c(Y_{0})s^{-\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr\,(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[P_{s-r}\left(Y_{r}^{\alpha\beta}\right)(x)\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]

. Here

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{t}(x)^{q}\mathbf{1}(t<\tau_{N})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,t^{-\frac{q}{2}}+c+c\int_{0}^{t}\,dr(t-r)^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{r}(y)^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}(r<\tau_{N})\right]\,dy

We here observe that the previous moment estimate can be utilized to show that the stochastic integrals appearing in (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are martingales. For this we recall the notion of a
class DL
process (see
[
RY99
, Definition IV.1.6]
).

A real valued and adapted stochastic process
X
is said to be of
class DL
if for every
t>0
, the set

\{X_{\tau}:\tau\leq t\text{ is a stopping time}\}

is uniformly integrable. And we know from
[
RY99
, Proposition IV.1.7]
that a local martingale
X
is a martingale if and only if it is of class DL. For practical purposes it is enough to show that there is an
\epsilon>0
such that

\sup_{\tau\leq t}\mathbb{E}(|X_{\tau}|^{1+\epsilon})<\infty

where the supremum is taken over all stopping times
\tau\leq t
.

Using this we observe that
M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)
defined in (
1.5
) is a martingale. This will be crucial for simplifying our approximate duality argument in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For each
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
, the local martingale
M^{Y}(\psi)
is in fact a martingale with respect to
\mathcal{F}^{Y}
, the filtration generated by
Y
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)=e^{-\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle}-e^{-\langle Y_{0},\psi\rangle}-\int_{0}^{t}I(Y_{s-},\psi)\,ds

is an
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
-local martingale, where

I(Y_{s-},\psi)=e^{-\langle Y_{s-},\psi\rangle}\left(-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle\right)

To show that
M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)
is a martingale we show that it is in class DL, i.e. for each
t>0
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t}\mathbb{E}\left(|M^{Y}_{\tau}(\psi)|^{1+\epsilon}\right)<\infty

for some
\epsilon>0
. The supremum ranges over all
\mathcal{F}^{Y}
-stopping times
\tau
that are bounded by
t
.

From the expression above it is enough to prove

(3.16)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\leq t}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}I(Y_{s-},\psi)\,ds\right|^{1+\epsilon}\right)<\infty.

Fix a stopping time
\tau\leq t
. By Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}I(Y_{s-},\psi)\,ds\right|^{1+\epsilon}=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\langle Y_{s-},\psi\rangle}\left(-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle\right)\,ds\right|^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{1+\epsilon}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\langle Y_{s-},\psi\rangle}\left(-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle\right)\,ds\right|^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau^{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle\right|^{1+\epsilon}\,ds

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}t^{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\left(|\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle|^{1+\epsilon}+|\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi^{\alpha}\rangle|^{1+\epsilon}\right)\,ds.

Let
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{\beta}-1<\alpha-1<1
. Again apply Jensen’s inequality, Fubini’s Theorem and Proposition
3.1
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle|^{1+\epsilon}\,ds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{1+\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)\Delta\psi(x)\,dx\right|^{1+\epsilon}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{1}{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)|\Delta\psi(x)|\,dx\right|^{1+\epsilon}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}^{\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}Y_{s-}(x)^{1+\epsilon}|\Delta\psi(x)|\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\frac{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}\,ds+C_{T}\frac{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{T}\frac{\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}(1+t^{1-\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi\rangle|^{1+\epsilon}\,ds\leq

\displaystyle\lVert\psi^{\alpha}\rVert_{1}^{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}Y_{s-}(x)^{\alpha\beta(1+\epsilon)}\psi(x)^{\alpha}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{T}\frac{\lVert\psi^{\alpha}\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\epsilon)}{2}}\,ds+C_{T}\frac{\lVert\psi^{\alpha}\rVert_{1}^{1+\epsilon}}{2^{1+\epsilon}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{T,\psi,\alpha,\beta,\epsilon}(t^{1-\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\epsilon)}{2}}+1)

Note that
1-\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}\geq 0
and

Appendix A
A Grönwall-type Lemma

We first state the ordinary Grönwall lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
T>0
and
f
,
g
and
h
be non-negative integrable functions on
[0,T]
satisfying the following inequality for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(A.1)

\displaystyle f(t)\leq g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}h(s)f(s)\,ds.

Then for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
we have,

(A.2)

\displaystyle f(t)\leq g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)h(s)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{s}h(r)\,dr\right)\,ds.

The proof is omitted as it is standard. We can now use the above result to prove the required estimate.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\gamma,\theta\in(0,1)
and
f:(0,T]\to[0,\infty)
be an integrable function such that and for all
t\in[0,T]

(A.3)

\displaystyle f(t)\leq ct^{-\theta}+c\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\gamma}f(r)\,dr

for some constant
c>0
. Then there exists an integrable function
C_{1}:(0,T]\to[0,\infty)
and a constant
C_{2}>0
such that, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

(A.4)

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C_{1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}C_{1}(s)\exp(C_{2}s)\,ds.

Moreover
C_{1},C_{2}
are independent of the function
f
.

Proof.

Let
k>0
be the smallest integer such that
\gamma<\frac{k}{k+1}
and
t\in[0,T]
. We apply (
A.3
) and use the substitutions
w=\frac{r}{t}
and
v=\frac{r-u}{t-u}
for the first and second integrals in the RHS of the following computation.

\displaystyle f(t)\leq

\displaystyle ct^{-\theta}+c^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\gamma}t^{-\theta}\,dr+c^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{r}(t-r)^{-\gamma}(r-u)^{-\gamma}f(u)\,du\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ct^{-\theta}+c^{2}t^{1-\gamma-\theta}\int_{0}^{1}(1-w)^{-\gamma}w^{-\theta}\,dw+c^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\,duf(u)\int_{u}^{t}\,dr(t-r)^{-\gamma}(r-u)^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(t)+c^{2}\int_{0}^{t}f(u)(t-u)^{1-2\gamma}\,du\int_{0}^{1}\,dv(1-v)^{-\gamma}v^{-\gamma}

(A.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(t)+c^{\prime}_{1}\int_{0}^{t}f(u)(t-u)^{1-2\gamma}\,du

where
c_{1}(t)=ct^{-\theta}+c^{2}B(1-\gamma,1-\theta)\,t^{1-\gamma-\theta}
and
c^{\prime}_{1}=c^{2}B(1-\gamma,1-\gamma)
with
B
here denoting the Beta function. Again applying (
A.3
) to (

) we have

\displaystyle f(t)\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}(t)+c^{\prime}_{2}\int_{0}^{t}f(u)(t-u)^{2-3\gamma}\,du,

where

and
c^{\prime}_{2}=c^{\prime}_{1}cB(1-\gamma,2-2\gamma)
. Continuing this process for

\displaystyle f(t)\leq

\displaystyle c_{k}(t)+c^{\prime}_{k}\int_{0}^{t}f(u)(t-u)^{k-(k+1)\gamma}\,du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{k}(t)+c^{\prime}_{k}T^{k-(k+1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}f(u)\,du.

where the last step is obtained by our assumption on

and have,

for a.e.

. We can thus define

) it is enough to prove that

(B.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t}\lVert w(s)\rVert_{\infty}<\infty

where we have used the notation
w(s)=\dot{v}(s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v(s).

From the proof of
[
Isc86
, Theorem A]
it follows that
w
must satisfy the PDE

(B.4)

\displaystyle w(t)=P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})-\alpha b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}(v(s)^{\alpha-1}w(s))\,ds

where
\tilde{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\varphi-\frac{b_{n}}{2}\varphi^{\alpha}
. This gives us,

(B.5)

\displaystyle\lVert w(t)\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}+\alpha b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert(v(s)^{\alpha-1}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert w(s)\rVert_{\infty}\,ds,

from which using Grönwall’s inequality (see
[
Eva10
, Appendix B2]
) we obtain

(B.6)

\displaystyle\lVert w(t)\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}\exp\left(\alpha b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert v(s)^{\alpha-1}\rVert_{\infty}\,ds\right)<\infty.

As
v
is continuously differentiable (see the proof of
[
Isc86
, Theorem A]
),
s\mapsto w(s,\cdot)
is continuous. This fact along with the above gives us (
B.3
).

Next we show that the map
[0,T]\to\bf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
t\mapsto\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}v(t)
is continuous, i.e.

(B.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}v(s)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}v(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\to 0\text{ as }s\to t\text{ in }[0,T].

Similarly as above, since
v\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{+}
, by (
B
) it is enough to show that

(B.8)

\displaystyle\lVert w(t)-w(s)\rVert_{\infty}\to 0\text{ as }s\to t

and we use (
B.4
) for this purpose.

Let
0\leq s<t\leq T
. Let
f_{r}=v(r)^{\alpha-1}w(r)
. Then from (
B.4
)

\displaystyle w(t)-w(s)=

\displaystyle P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})-P_{s}(\tilde{\varphi})-\alpha b_{n}\left[\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-r}(f_{r})\,dr-\int_{0}^{s}P_{s-r}(f_{r})\,dr\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})-P_{s}(\tilde{\varphi})-\alpha b_{n}\left[\int_{s}^{t}P_{t-r}(f_{r})\,dr+\int_{0}^{s}\left(P_{t-r}(f_{r})-P_{s-r}(f_{r})\right)\,dr\right]

(B.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})-P_{s}(\tilde{\varphi})-\alpha b_{n}\left[\int_{s}^{t}P_{t-r}(f_{r})\,dr+\int_{0}^{s}\left(P_{t-s}(P_{s-r}(f_{r}))-P_{s-r}(f_{r})\right)\,dr\right]

using that fact
P_{t-r}f_{r}=P_{t-s}(P_{s-r}f_{r})
.

As
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})_{+}
, by our definition
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore when
s\to t
,
\lVert P_{t}\tilde{\varphi}-P_{s}\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}\to 0
. For the second term in (
B
), note that if we can prove that

\sup_{r\leq t}\lVert P_{t-r}(f_{r})\rVert_{\infty}<\infty,

it will follow that
\int_{s}^{t}P_{t-r}(f_{r})\,dr\to 0
in
\bf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as
s\to t
. We have, for
x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\left|P_{t-r}(f_{r})(x)\right|\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-y)f_{r}(y)\,dr\right|\leq\lVert f_{r}\rVert_{\infty}=\lVert v(r)^{\alpha-1}w(r)\rVert_{\infty}<\infty.

since know
v(r)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and we have already shown that
\sup_{r\leq t}\lVert w(r)\rVert_{\infty}<\infty
. Similarly, the third term in (
B
) can be shown to be converging to
0
in
\bf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as
s\to t
. This proves (
B.8
) and hence (
B.7
).

(c)
Let
\sigma=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}
. To show
\sup_{s\leq t}\lVert\dot{v}(s)\rVert_{\sigma}<\infty
we again use (
B.4
). Note that

(B.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w(t,x)|^{\sigma}\,dx\leq C\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}|P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})|^{\sigma}\,dx+\alpha b_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}(v(s)^{\alpha-1}w(s))\,ds\right|^{\sigma}\,dx\right].

Using Jensen inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|P_{t}(\tilde{\varphi})|^{\sigma}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t}(x-y)\tilde{\varphi}(y)\,dy\right|^{\sigma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t}(x-y)|\tilde{\varphi}(y)|^{\sigma}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma}<\infty.

By definition of
P_{t-s}
and using Jensen’s inequality once more we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}(v(s)^{\alpha-1}w(s))\,ds\right|^{\sigma}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)v(s,y)^{\alpha-1}w(s,y)\,dy\,ds\right|^{\sigma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\sigma-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)|v(s,y)^{\alpha-1}w(s,y)|^{\sigma}\,dy\,ds\,dx.

Using Fubini’s theorem, as all terms are non-negative, integrating out
x
in the above we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}(v(s)^{\alpha-1}w(s))\,ds\right|^{\sigma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq t^{\sigma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v(s,y)^{\alpha-1}w(s,y)|^{\sigma}\,dy\,ds

(B.11)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert v(s)\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma(\alpha-1)}\lVert w(s)\rVert_{\sigma}^{\sigma}\,ds.

Using (
B
) in (
B.10
) we have

(B.12)

\displaystyle\lVert w(t)\rVert_{\sigma}^{\sigma}\leq C\lVert\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma}+C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert v(s)\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma(\alpha-1)}\lVert w(s)\rVert_{\sigma}^{\sigma}\,ds.

Again by Grönwall’s inequality

(B.13)

\displaystyle\lVert w(t)\rVert_{\sigma}^{\sigma}\leq C\lVert\tilde{\varphi}\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert v(s)\rVert_{\infty}^{\sigma(\alpha-1)}\,ds\right)<\infty

and the required result follows as in the previous part.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of Proposition
3.4

Since the proof is a little long we carry it out in two steps. The first shows that a solution of the weak form (
1.4
) also satisfies a time-dependent version as described in (
C.1
). The proof follows the argument of
[
Shi94
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
T>0
be fixed and assume that
Y
satisfies (
1.4
) and the following conditions hold for
\psi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
.

(i)

The map
[0,T]\ni s\mapsto\psi_{s}\in\bf{L}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R})\cap\bf{L}^{\rho}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous, for some fixed
\eta\in(\frac{1}{\beta},\alpha)
and
\rho\in(\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\wedge 2)
.

(ii)

\sup_{s\leq T}\lVert\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\psi_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}}<\infty
, and

(iii)

s\mapsto\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{s}
is continuous in
\bf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.
\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{s}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\to 0
as
|s-t|\to 0
.

Then for each
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(C.1)

\displaystyle\langle Y_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle=\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)\psi_{s}\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(Y_{s-}(x))^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds).

Proof.

Fix
0\leq t\leq T
and let
\Delta=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=t\}
be a partition of
[0,t]
. For all
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, denote
\pi_{\Delta}(s)=t_{i-1}
and
\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)=t_{i}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle Y_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle-\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\langle Y_{t_{i}},\psi_{t_{i}}-\psi_{t_{i-1}}\rangle-\langle Y_{t_{i}}-Y_{t_{i-1}},\psi_{t_{i-1}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle\,ds+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}\rangle\,ds+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds)\right]

(C.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle+\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}\rangle\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds)

To prove the lemma we have to show that, as
|\Delta|\to 0

(a)

\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle\,ds\to\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle\,ds
a.s. ,

(b)

\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}\rangle\,ds\to\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{s}\rangle\,ds
a.s. , and

(c)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)L^{\alpha}(\,ds,\,dx)\to\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(Y_{s-}(x))^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds)
in probability.

For (a) and (b), we need to show that the integrand converges pointwise (i.e. for each
s
) and that the dominated convergence theorem (DCT) can be applied.

(a) Recall that
s\mapsto Y_{s}
is right continuous measure-valued a.s. and by the definition of weak convergence we have, for each
s\in[0,t]

\displaystyle|\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}-Y_{s},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle|\to 0,\text{ a.s.}

as
\dot{\psi}_{s}
is bounded and continuous (in the space variable).

By Hölder’s inequality, as
\alpha\beta>1
, we have a.s.

\displaystyle|\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}-Y_{s},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert Y_{s}(x)\rvert\lvert\dot{\psi}_{s}(x)\rvert\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}(x)\rvert\lvert\dot{\psi}_{s}(x)\rvert\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lVert Y_{s}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\lVert\dot{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}}+\lVert Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\lVert\dot{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}}.

Therefore, a.s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}-Y_{s},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq(\sup_{s\leq t}\lVert\dot{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}})\left[\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{s}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=(\sup_{s\leq t}\lVert\dot{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}})\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{s}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\,ds\right)^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta}}+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}\,ds\right)^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\sup_{s\leq t}\lVert\dot{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}})\left[\left(t^{\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{s}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}^{\alpha\beta}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}+\left(t^{\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}\rVert_{\alpha\beta}^{\alpha\beta}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\right],

using Jensen in the last line. The quantity above is finite by assumption (ii) and the fact that
Y\in L^{\alpha\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. This implies that
s\mapsto|\langle Y_{\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}(s)}-Y_{s},\dot{\psi}_{s}\rangle|
is a.s. integrable on
[0,t]
.

(b) Fix
s\in[0,t]
. Then

\displaystyle|\langle Y_{s},\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}(\psi_{s}-\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}(\psi_{s}-\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)})(x)|\right)\langle Y_{s},1\rangle,\text{ a.s.}

We know that
Y_{s}
is a finite measure, i.e.
\langle Y_{s},1\rangle<\infty
. Thus the RHS above converges to
0
by our assumption (iii).

Let us introduce a new stopping time: for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sigma_{k}=\inf\{s\geq 0\mid\langle Y_{s},1\rangle>k\}.

For
s\leq\sigma_{k}\wedge t
we have, a.s.

\displaystyle|\langle Y_{s},\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}(\psi_{s}-\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}(\psi_{s}-\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)})(x)|\right)\langle Y_{s},1\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2k\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\leq t}}{{x\in\mathbb{R}}}}|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\psi_{s}(x)|\right)<\infty

by hypothesis. As
\sigma_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
the above is true for all
s\leq t
. Thus we can apply DCT to obtain (b).

(c) Recall the notations introduced in the beginning of Section
2
. We have

(C.3)

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}(\,dx,\,ds)=\int_{0}^{\infty}z\tilde{N}(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

where
N(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)
is a PRM on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)^{2}
with intensity
dx\,m_{0}(dz)\,ds
.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))L^{\alpha}(dx,\,ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))z\tilde{N}(dx,\,dz,\,ds)

(C.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))z\tilde{N}(dx,\,dz,\,ds).

Here we note that
\int_{0}^{1}z^{\rho}m_{0}(dz)<\infty
as
\rho\geq\alpha
and
\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{\eta}m_{0}(dz)<\infty
as
\eta<\alpha
. Using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the first term, Fubini’s theorem and Proposition
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))z\tilde{N}(dx,\,dz,\,ds)\right|^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{2\beta}|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)|^{2}z^{2}N(dx,\,dz,\,ds)\right|^{\rho/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\rho\beta}|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)|^{\rho}z^{\rho}N(dx,\,dz,\,ds)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)\right|^{\rho}\mathbb{E}(Y_{s-}(x)^{\rho\beta})dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\left(\sup_{s}\|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}-\psi_{s}\|_{\rho}\right)^{\rho}\left(\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\rho\beta/2}\,ds\right)

(C.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rho}\left(\sup_{s}\|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}-\psi_{s}\|_{\rho}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\rho\beta/2}\to 0

as
|\Delta|\to 0
. The second inequality again uses the fact about random sums described in
[
PZ07
, Lemma 8.22]
as
\rho/2<1
. The last line follows from our assumption (i) of continuity of the map
s\mapsto\psi_{s}\in{\bf{L}}^{\rho}(\mathbb{R})
, which implies uniform continuity of the same on
[0,t]
. For the second term in (
C
) we proceed as in the previous calculation. Observe that
1<\eta\beta<\alpha
by assumption and thus Proposition
3.1
is applicable in the following.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))z\tilde{N}(dx,\,dz,\,ds)\right|^{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{2\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))^{2}z^{2}N(dx,\,dz,\,ds)\right|^{\eta/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|Y_{s-}(x)^{\eta\beta}(\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x))^{\eta}z^{\eta}\right|N(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Y_{s-}(x)|^{\eta\beta}\left|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)\right|^{\eta}z^{\eta}\,dx\,m_{0}(dz)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left(|Y_{s-}(x)|^{\eta\beta}\right)\left|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)\right|^{\eta}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{\eta\beta/2}\left|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}(x)-\psi_{s}(x)\right|^{\eta}\,dx\,ds

(C.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\left(\sup_{s}\|\psi_{\pi_{\Delta}(s)}-\psi_{s}\|_{\eta}\right)^{\eta}t^{1-\eta\beta/2}.

By assumption (i) the RHS above converges to
0
as
|\Delta|\to 0
. The calculations (
C
), (
C
) and (
C
) together prove (c).
∎

In the last lemma we show how to turn the time dependent weak form of our SPDE (
C.1
), which was proved in the previous result, into a martingale. This will complete the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma C.2
.

If (
C.1
) holds for some smooth
\psi:[0,T]\times R\to\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq t\leq T
, then

(C.7)

\displaystyle M^{Y}_{t}(\psi)=e^{-\langle Y_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle}-e^{-\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle}\left(-\langle Y_{s-},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{s}+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\psi_{s}\rangle+\langle Y_{s-}^{\alpha\beta},\psi_{s}^{\alpha}\rangle\right)\,ds

is an
\mathcal{F}^{Y}
-martingale.

Proof.

The proof is an application of Ito’s formula. Using the representation (
C.3
) and some algebraic manipulations we have,

\displaystyle\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{s}+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\psi_{s}\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z\tilde{N}(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{s}+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\psi_{s}\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z\tilde{N}(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)zN(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z\,dx\,m_{0}(dz)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left[\langle Y_{s},\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{s}+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\psi_{s}\rangle-\int_{1}^{\infty}z\langle Y_{s-}(\cdot)^{\beta},\psi_{s}\rangle\,m_{0}(dz)\right]\,ds

(C.8)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z\tilde{N}(\,dx,\,dz,\,dt)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)zN(\,dx,\,dz,\,dt)

Since
\int_{1}^{\infty}z\,m_{0}(dz)=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}
the above can be written formally as

\displaystyle d\langle Y_{t},\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle\left(\langle Y_{t},\frac{\Delta}{2}\psi_{t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{t}\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\langle Y_{t-}^{\beta},\psi_{t}\rangle\right)\,dt

(C.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{t-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{t}(x)z\tilde{N}(\,dt,\,dz,\,dx)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{t-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{t}(x)zN(\,dt,\,dz,\,dx).

Ito’s formula as given in
[
App09
, Theorem 4.4.7]
can now be applied with
f(x)=e^{-x}
and

\displaystyle G(t)=\left(\langle Y_{t},\frac{\Delta}{2}\psi_{t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{t}\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\langle Y_{t-}^{\beta},\psi_{t}\rangle\right).

We have from (
C
),

\displaystyle e^{-\langle Y_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle}-e^{-\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle}=f(\langle Y_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle)-f(\langle Y_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle)G(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle+Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z)-f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle)\right]N(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle+Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z)-f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle)\right]\tilde{N}(\,dx,\,dz,\,ds)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle+Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)z)-f(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle)-Y_{s-}(x)^{\beta}\psi_{s}(x)zf^{\prime}(\langle Y_{s-},\psi_{s}\rangle)\right]\,ds\,m_{0}(dz)\,dx

\displaystyle=

Abstract
. Inspired by the definition of balanced neighborly spheres, we define balanced neighborly polynomials and study the existence of these polynomials. The goal of this article is to construct balanced neighborly polynomials of type ( k , k , k , k ) (k,k,k,k) over any field K K for all k ≠ 2 k\neq 2 , and show that a balanced neighborly polynomial of type ( 2 , 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2,2) exists if and only if char ⁡ ( K ) ≠ 2 \operatorname{char}(K)\neq 2 . Besides, we also discuss a relation between balanced neighborly polynomials and balanced neighborly simplicial spheres.



1.
Introduction

Finding sharp upper bounds for the Hilbert function of certain graded algebras is
an important problem relating both commutative algebra and combinatorics. One of the most famous results on this topic is Stanley’s solution of the Upper Bound Conjecture, which gives an upper bound on the number of faces of simplicial spheres by translating the problem into a problem on upper bounds of Hilbert function of graded algebras. In this paper,
we study upper bounds for the Hilbert function of Gorenstein algebras defined by certain polynomials, which we call balanced polynomials.

The motivation of this paper comes from the study of face numbers of balanced simplicial complexes. Balanced simplicial complexes are defined by Stanley
[
3
]
and their face number has been studied by
many researchers
[
1
,
2
]
. One interesting problem on balanced simplicial complexes is to find the sharp upper bound of the number of
i
-faces of balanced simplicial spheres with
n
vertices for a fixed
integers
i
and
n
. Recently, to study this problem, Zheng
[
5
]
defined the notion of balanced neighborly spheres and study their existence. Balanced neighborly spheres are important ssince if a
balanced neighborly sphere of type
(k,k,...,k)
exists then it maximizes the numbers of faces of balanced simplicial
(d-1)
-spheres with
kd
vertices. Especially, the paper
[
5
]
proved that balanced neighborly spheres of type
(2,2,2,2)

do not exist, but type
(3,3,3,3)
exist. Also, Zheng asked if balanced neighborly spheres of type
(k,k,k,k)
exist for all
k

[
5
, Question 15]
. Motivated by this work of Zheng, we define balanced neighborly polynomials (see Section 2 for the definition) and study their existence. As we will prove in Proposition 4.1, if balanced neighborly simplicial spheres of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
exist, then balanced neighborly polynomials of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
exist over any field
K.
Thus, the study of the existence problem of balanced neighborly polynomial can be considered as an algebraic abstraction of the existence problem on balanced neighborly spheres. On the other hand, the goal of this paper is to construct a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(k,k,k,k)
over any field
K
for all
k\neq 2
, (see Theorems
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.4
) and show that a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
exists only when
\operatorname{char}(K)\neq 2
(see Theorem
3.1
).
Moreover, the results give an alternative proof for Zheng’s result proving that balanced neighborly simplicial spheres of type
(2,2,2,2)
do not exist.

The structure of this article is organized as follows. In section 2, we discuss basic properties of balanced neighborly polynomials. In section 3, we construct a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(k,k,k,k)
for any
k\neq 2
and prove that there exists a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
only when
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
. In section 4, we discuss a relation between balanced neighborly polynomials and balanced neighborly simplicial spheres.

2.
Balanced neighborly polynomials

In this section we introduce notations that will be used throughout this article.
Let
d,n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}
be positive integers, and let
R:=K[x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq n_{i}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
.
The ring
R
has a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-graded structure by setting
\operatorname{deg}(x_{ij})=\mathfrak{e}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
, where
\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{d}
are the unit vectors of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We say that
f\in R
is a
balanced polynomial
if
f
is
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-graded and
\operatorname{deg}(f)=(1,1,\ldots,1).
For a balanced polynomial
f\in R
and
S\subseteq[d]=\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
, we set

H(f,S)=\dim_{K}\{g(\partial_{ij})f\mid g(x_{ij})\in R_{\mathfrak{e}_{S}}\},

where
\partial_{ij}={\partial}/{\partial x_{ij}}
,
\mathfrak{e}_{S}=\underset{i\in S}{\sum}\mathfrak{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
R_{\mathfrak{e}_{S}}
is the graded component of
R
of degree
\mathfrak{e}_{S}
. Moreover, a balanced polynomial
f
is said to be
neighborly of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if for every subset
S\subseteq[d]
with
|S|\leq\dfrac{d}{2}
, we have
H(f,S)=\underset{k\in S}{\prod}n_{k}
.

For a graded polynomial
f=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathfrak{R}=K[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],
let

\operatorname{ann}(f)=\{g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathfrak{R}\mid g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f=0\},

where

\partial_{i}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.
We say that a polynomial
f
is
squarefree
if
f
is a linear combination of squarefree monomials. For
F\subseteq[n]
, let
x^{F}=\underset{i\in F}{\prod}x_{i}
. If

f=\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\alpha_{F}x^{F},\quad g=\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\beta_{F}x^{F},\text{ where }\alpha_{F},\beta_{G}\in K

are squarefree polynomials having the same degree, then
g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f=\underset{F\subset[n]}{\sum}\alpha_{F}\beta_{F}
. We say that an Artinian graded
K
-algebra

\mathfrak{R}/I=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k=0}^{d}(\mathfrak{R}/I)_{k}
, where
(\mathfrak{R}/I)_{d}\neq 0
is
Gorenstein of socle degree
d
if
0:_{\mathfrak{R}/I}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\mathfrak{R}/I)_{d}
has
K
-dimension
1
, where
I
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{R}
. Then we have the following property.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

(i)

If
f
is a squarefree polynomial of degree
d
, then
x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}\in\operatorname{ann}(f)
and
\mathfrak{R}/(\operatorname{ann}(f))
is an Artinian Gorenstein graded
k
-algebra of socle degree
d
.

(ii)

If
I
is a homogenerous ideal such that
\mathfrak{R}/I
is an Artinian Gorenstein graded
k
-algebra of socle degree
d
and
x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}\in I
, then there is a squarefree polynomial such that
\operatorname{ann}(f)=I.

Proof.

(i) Assume
f
is squarefree. Then
\partial_{i}^{2}f=0,\forall i=1,2,\ldots,n
. So
x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}\in\operatorname{ann}(f)
. Next, we prove that
0:_{\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f))_{d}.
Let
g\in\mathfrak{R}
be a homogeneous element of degree
k<d
with
g\notin\operatorname{ann}(f)
. We must prove that
g
is not an element of
0:_{\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, that is,
x_{j}g\notin\operatorname{ann}(f)
for some
j
. Since
g\notin\operatorname{ann}(f),
we have
g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f\neq 0
. Since
h=g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f
is a squarefree polynomial of degree
\geq 1
,
\partial_{j}(h)\neq 0
for some
j
. From this we obtain that
x_{j}g\notin\operatorname{ann}(f).
Now we prove
\dim_{K}(\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f))_{d}=1.
Indeed, let

f=\underset{F\subset[n],|F|=d}{\sum}\alpha_{F}x^{F}\text{ for }\alpha_{F}\in K,

E_{k}=\operatorname{span}_{K}\{x^{F}:|F|=k\}
and
E=E_{0}\oplus E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}
be the set of all squarefree polynomials at most
k
. We consider the linear map

\displaystyle\psi:E_{d}

\displaystyle\to K

\displaystyle g=\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\beta_{F}x^{F}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\beta_{F}\alpha_{F}=g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f

Then
\operatorname{Ker}\psi=\{g\in E_{d}\mid g(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n})f=0\}=(\operatorname{ann}(f)\cap E)_{d}
. Since
\psi
is a non-zero map,
\dim_{K}\operatorname{Ker}\psi=\dim_{K}E_{d}-1.
Moreover,
\operatorname{ann}(f)=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})\oplus(\operatorname{ann}(f)\cap E)
and
\mathfrak{R}=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})\oplus E
. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{K}(\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f))_{d}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}\mathfrak{R}_{d}-\dim_{K}\operatorname{ann}(f)_{d}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}((x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}\oplus E_{d})-\dim_{K}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}\oplus(\operatorname{ann}(f)\cap E)_{d}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}E_{d}-\dim_{K}\operatorname{Ker}\psi=1.

(ii) Suppose
\mathfrak{R}/I
is Artinian Gorenstein of socle degree
d
and
x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2}\in I
. Then
I_{d}=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}\bigoplus(E\cap I)_{d}
as
K
-vector space and

\displaystyle\dim_{K}(E\cap I)_{d}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}I_{d}-\dim_{K}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}

\displaystyle=(\dim_{K}R_{d}-1)-\dim_{K}(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}E_{d}-1.

Since
(E\cap I)_{d}
is a codimension
1
subspace of
E_{d}
, it can be written as a kernel of a
K
-linear function, that is,

(E\cap I)_{d}=\left(\Big\{\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\beta_{F}x^{F}\in E_{d}\mid\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\alpha_{F}\beta_{F}=0\Big\}\right)

for some
\alpha_{F}\in K
such that
|F|=d.

Let
f=\underset{F\subset[n],|F|=d}{\sum}\alpha_{F}x^{F}
. We claim that
\operatorname{ann}(f)=I
. Indeed,

(E\cap I)_{d}=\left(\Big\{\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\beta_{F}x^{F}\in E_{d}\mid\sum_{F\subset[n],|F|=d}\alpha_{F}\beta_{F}=g(\partial_{ij})f=0\Big\}\right)=(\operatorname{ann}(f)\cap E)_{d}.

In particular,

I_{d}=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}+(E\cap I)_{d}=(x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})_{d}+(E\cap\operatorname{ann}(f))_{d}=\operatorname{ann}(f)_{d}.

Note that for any ideal
J
of
\mathfrak{R}
, if
\mathfrak{R}/J
is an Artinian Gorenstein algebra of socle degree
d
, then
J_{d}
determines the whole
J
. In fact, for any polynomial
g\in\mathfrak{R}
of degree
t<d
, one has
g\in J
if and only if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{d-t}g\subset J_{d}
. Since both
\mathfrak{R}/I
and
\mathfrak{R}/\operatorname{ann}(f)
are Artinian Gorenstein algebras of socle degree
d
,
I_{d}=\operatorname{ann}(f)_{d}
implies
I=\operatorname{ann}(f)
.
∎

(ii) Set

In the next section, we study balanced neighborly polynomials of degree
4
. For this, the next Lemma says that we only need to consider balanced neighborly polynomials of type
(k,k,k,k)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f
be a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If
d
is even then
n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{d}.

Proof.

By the definition of a balanced polynomial
f\in\mathfrak{R}
, we see that the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-degree of
f
is
(1,1,\ldots,1)
. Since
d
is even, we set
d=2m
. Assume that
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}
. Then by Lemma
2.2
(ii), we have

n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{m}=H(f,\{1,2,\cdots,m\})=H(f,\{m+1,\ldots,2m\})=n_{m+1}n_{m+2}\ldots n_{2m}.

Since
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}
, we conclude that
n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{m}=n_{m+1}=\cdots=n_{2m}.

∎

In case
d
is even, Lemma
2.3
for balanced simplicial spheres was proved by Zheng in
[
5
]
.

3.
The existence of balanced neighborly polynomials of type
(k,k,k,k)

Throughout this section, we consider ring

R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}],

where
\operatorname{deg}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{e}_{1}=(1,0,0,0)
,
\operatorname{deg}(y_{i})=\mathfrak{e}_{2}=(0,1,0,0)
,
\operatorname{deg}(z_{i})=\mathfrak{e}_{3}=(0,0,1,0)
,
\operatorname{deg}(w_{i})=\mathfrak{e}_{4}=(0,0,0,1)
. So, if
f\in R
is a balanced polynomial, then we may assume that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in{\{1,2,\ldots,k\}}^{4}}a_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}x_{i_{1}}y_{i_{2}}z_{i_{3}}w_{i_{4}},\quad\text{where }a_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}\in K.

Therefore,

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,2\})

\displaystyle=H(f,\{3,4\})=\dim\operatorname{span}_{K}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}f\mid 1\leq i,j\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,3\})

\displaystyle=H(f,\{2,4\})=\dim\operatorname{span}_{K}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}f\mid 1\leq i,j\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,4\})

\displaystyle=H(f,\{2,3\})=\dim\operatorname{span}_{K}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}f\mid 1\leq i,j\leq k\right\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
if and only if
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
.

Proof.

We first prove that if
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
then there exists a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
. Let

f=-x_{1}y_{1}z_{2}w_{2}-x_{1}y_{2}z_{1}w_{1}+x_{1}y_{2}z_{2}w_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}z_{1}w_{2}+x_{2}y_{1}z_{2}w_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}w_{1}.

We have

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,2\})=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\operatorname{span}_{K}\{-z_{2}w_{2},-z_{1}w_{1}+z_{2}w_{2},-z_{1}w_{2}+z_{2}w_{1},z_{2}w_{1}\},

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,3\})=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\operatorname{span}_{K}\{-y_{2}w_{1},y_{2}w_{2}-y_{1}w_{2},-y_{1}w_{2},y_{2}w_{1}+y_{1}w_{1}\},

\displaystyle H(f,\{1,4\})=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\operatorname{span}_{K}\{-y_{2}z_{1},y_{2}z_{2}-y_{1}z_{2},y_{2}z_{2}+y_{1}z_{2},-y_{1}z_{1}\}.

Then

H(f,\{1,2\})=H(f,\{1,3\})=H(f,\{1,4\})=4.

So,
f
is a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
.
Conversely, suppose that
\operatorname{char}K=2
and let

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=a_{1}x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{3}x_{1}y_{1}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{4}x_{1}y_{1}z_{2}w_{2}+a_{5}x_{1}y_{2}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{6}x_{1}y_{2}z_{1}w_{2}

\displaystyle+a_{7}x_{1}y_{2}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{8}x_{1}y_{2}z_{2}w_{2}+a_{9}x_{2}y_{1}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{10}x_{2}y_{1}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{11}x_{2}y_{1}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{12}x_{2}y_{1}z_{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle+a_{13}x_{2}y_{2}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{14}x_{2}y_{2}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{15}x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{16}x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}w_{2},

where
a_{i}\in K,i=1,2,\ldots,16.

We prove that
f
is not a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
. Let

M=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{4}\\
a_{5}&a_{6}&a_{7}&a_{8}\\
a_{9}&a_{10}&a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{13}&a_{14}&a_{15}&a_{16}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Since
H(f,\{1,2\})
is the
K
-dimension of

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}_{K}

\displaystyle\{a_{1}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{3}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{4}z_{2}w_{2},a_{5}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{6}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{7}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{8}z_{2}w_{2},

\displaystyle a_{9}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{10}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{11}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{12}z_{2}w_{2},a_{13}z_{1}w_{1}+a_{14}z_{1}w_{2}+a_{15}z_{2}w_{1}+a_{16}z_{2}w_{2}\},

H(f,\{1,2\})=4
if and only if
M
is a nonsingular matrix.
Let

N=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{5}&a_{6}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{7}&a_{8}\\
a_{9}&a_{10}&a_{13}&a_{14}\\
a_{11}&a_{12}&a_{15}&a_{16}\\
\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }P=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{3}&a_{5}&a_{7}\\
a_{2}&a_{4}&a_{6}&a_{8}\\
a_{9}&a_{11}&a_{13}&a_{15}\\
a_{10}&a_{12}&a_{14}&a_{16}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

A similar computation shows that
N
(resp.
P
) is a non-singular matrix if and only if
H(f,\{1,3\})=4
(resp.
H(f,\{1,4\})=4
). Moreover, we have
\det(M)-\det(N)=\det(P).
It follows that one of
\det(M),\det(N),
and
\det(P)
must be zero. So,
f
is not a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(2,2,2,2)
.
∎

For an integer
i

[i]_{k}\in\{1,2,.\ldots,k\}

[i]_{k}\equiv i(\text{mod}

k).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be odd and
f=\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq k}x_{i}y_{j}z_{{[j-i]}_{k}}w_{{[i+j]}_{k}}.
Then
f

(k,k,k,k).

4.

In this section, we present a relation between balanced neighborly polynomials and balanced neighborly spheres. We first recall basic definitions on simplicial spheres. Let
\Delta
be a finite simplicial complex on the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Thus
\Delta
is a nonempty collection of subsets of
V
satisfying that
F\in\Delta
and
G\subset F
imply
G\in\Delta.
Elements of
\Delta
are called
faces
of
\Delta
and maximal faces (under inclusion) are called
facets.
The dimension of a face
F\in\Delta
is
\dim F=|F|-1,
and the
dimension of
\Delta
is the maximum dimension of its faces. Faces of dimension
0
are called
vertices
and faces of dimension
1
are called
edges.
Let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over an infinite field
K.
The ring
K[\Delta]=R/I_{\Delta}
, where
I_{\Delta}=(\underset{x_{i}\in F}{\prod}x_{i}\mid F\subset V,F\notin\Delta)
, is called
the Stanley–Reisner ring of
\Delta
.
It is well-known that the Krull dimension of
K[\Delta]
equals
\dim\Delta+1

[
4
, II Theorem 1.3]
. If
\dim K[\Delta]=d,
and a sequence
\Theta=\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d}
of linear forms such that
\dim_{K}K[\Delta]/(\Theta)<\infty
then
\Theta
is called a
linear system of parameters
(l.s.o.p. for short) of
K[\Delta].

A map
\kappa:V\to[d]
is called
a proper
d
-coloring map
of
\Delta,
if we have
\kappa(i)\neq\kappa(j)
for any edge
\{i,j\}\in\Delta.
We say that a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
on
V
is
balanced
(completely balanced in some literature) if its graph is
d
-colorable. Let
\Delta
be a
d-1
dimensional balanced simplicial complex and let
\kappa:V\to[d]
be a proper
d
-coloring map of
\Delta.
Set
V_{k}=\{v\in V\mid\kappa(v)=k\}.
Let
\theta_{k}=\underset{v\in V_{k}}{\sum}x_{v}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,d
. Then
\Theta
is a l.s.o.p of
K[\Delta]

[
4
, III Proposition 4.3]
. Recall that a
simplicial sphere
is a simplicial complex which is homeomorphic to a sphere. That means if
\Delta
is a simplicial sphere then
K[\Delta]
is a Gorenstein ring and
K[\Delta]/(\Theta)
is an Artinian Gorenstein algebra of socle degree
d

[
4
, II Corollary 5.2]
. A balanced simplicial sphere of dimension
d-1
is called
neighborly of type
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
\dim_{K}(K[\Delta]/(\Theta))_{\mathfrak{e}_{S}}=\underset{k\in S}{\prod}n_{k}
for all
S\subset[d]
.

From now on, we assume that
R=K[x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq n_{i}
] and
\Delta
has vertex set
\{x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq n_{i}\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If a balanced neighborly simplicial sphere of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
exists, then a balanced neighborly polynomial of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
exists over any field
K
.

Proof.

Set
R^{\prime}=K[x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq n_{i}-1
]. Then
R/(\Theta)\cong R^{\prime}
, so

R/{(I_{\Delta}+(\Theta))}\cong R^{\prime}/J\text{ for some ideal }J.

By Proposition 4.3 in
[
3
]
, we have
x_{ij}x_{ik}=0
in
R/(I_{\Delta}+(\Theta))
for all
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq j\leq k\leq n_{i}
. It follows that
x_{ij}x_{ik}=0
in
S^{\prime}/J
for all
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq j\leq k\leq n_{i}-1
. Since
R/(I_{\Delta}+\Theta)
is Artinian Gorenstein, Lemma
2.1
shows that there is a balanced polynomial

\operatorname{ann}(f)=J

2.2
, (i).

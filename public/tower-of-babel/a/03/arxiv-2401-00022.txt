A note on the V V -invariant
Aldo Conca

Abstract
. Let R R be a finitely generated ℕ \mathbb{N} -graded algebra domain over a Noetherian ring and let I I be a homogeneous ideal of R R . Given P ∈ Ass ⁡ ( R / I ) P\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I) one defines the v v -invariant v P ​ ( I ) v_{P}(I) of I I at P P as the least c ∈ ℕ c\in\mathbb{N} such that P = I : f P=I:f for some f ∈ R c f\in R_{c} . A classical result of Brodmann [ 1 ] asserts that Ass ⁡ ( R / I n ) \operatorname{Ass}(R/I^{n}) is constant for large n n . So it makes sense to consider a prime ideal P ∈ Ass ⁡ ( R / I n ) P\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I^{n}) for all the large n n and investigate how v P ​ ( I n ) v_{P}(I^{n}) depends on n n . We prove that v P ​ ( I n ) v_{P}(I^{n}) is eventually a linear function of n n . When R R is the polynomial ring over a field this statement has been proved independently also by Ficarra and Sgroi in their recent preprint [ 4 ] .

1.
The
v
-invariant

Let
R
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded algebra domain over a Noetherian ring and let
I
be a non-zero homogeneous ideal of
R
generated by elements of degree
d_{1},\dots,d_{c}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Recently there has been some interest in the study of an invariant associated to
I
as follows.
For
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)
the
v
-invariant
v_{P}(I)
of
I
at
P
is defined as

v_{P}(I)=\min\{u:\mbox{ there exist }f\in R_{u}\mbox{ such that }P=I:f\},

see for example
[
5
]
. A classical result of Brodmann
[
1
]
asserts that
\operatorname{Ass}(R/I^{n})
is constant for large
n
.
Denote by
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
the set of the asymptotic associated primes of
I
, i.e.
{\mathcal{A}}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(R/I^{n})
for large
n
. A quite natural question, discussed by Ficarra and Sgroi in the various arXiv versions of their paper
[
4
]
, is the nature of the asymptotic behaviour of
v_{P}(I^{n})
as a function on
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
for a prime ideal
P\in{\mathcal{A}}(I)
. The goal of this short note is to prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
P\in{\mathcal{A}}(I)
the function
v_{P}(I^{n})
is eventually linear in
n
with leading coefficient in the set
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}\}
.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we need the following characterization of
v_{P}(I)
inspired by
[
5
, 3.2]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Given
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)
let
X_{P}=\{P_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I):P\subsetneq P_{1}\}
.
Let
Q
be the product of the elements in
X_{P}
with the convention that
Q=R
if
X_{P}
is empty. Then
v_{P}(I)
is the smallest
w\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the graded
R
-module
(I:P)/(I:(P+Q^{\infty}))
does not vanish in degree
w
.

Proof.

Let
v=v_{P}(I)
and
w
be the smallest natural number such that
(I:P)/(I:(P+Q^{\infty}))
does not vanish in degree
w
. Let
f\in R_{v}
such that
I:f=P
. In particular
f\in I:P
. We show that
f\not\in I:(P+Q^{\infty})
by contradiction.
If
f\in I:(P+Q^{\infty})
then
fQ^{m}\subseteq I
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
which implies
Q\subseteq P
. Since
Q
is the product of the ideals in
X_{P}
(or
R
) the inclusion
Q\subseteq P
implies that there exists
P_{1}\in X_{P}
such that
P_{1}\subseteq P
, a contradiction because, by definition,
P
is strictly contained in
P_{1}
. Hence
f\not\in I:(P+Q^{\infty})
and
v\geq w
.

Let now
g\in R_{w}
such that
g\in I:P
and
g\not\in I:(P+Q^{\infty})
. We have
P\subseteq I:g
. We claim that
P=I:g
and this implies
v\leq w
. We prove the claim by contradiction. Assume that
P\subsetneq I:g
. Observe that the
I:g
is a proper ideal since
g\not\in I
. The multiplication with
g
embeds
R/(I:g)
in
R/I
. Hence
\operatorname{Ass}(R/I:g)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)
. Then for each
P_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I:g)
we have

P\subsetneq I:g\subseteq P_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I:g)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)

so that
P_{1}\in X_{P}
. Hence
\operatorname{Ass}(R/I:g)\subseteq X_{P}
. Let
Q_{1}
be the product of the primes in
\operatorname{Ass}(R/I:g)
. For some large
m
we have
Q_{1}^{m}\subseteq I:g
, that is,
Q_{1}^{m}g\subseteq I
. Hence
Q^{m}g\subseteq Q_{1}^{m}g\subseteq I
,
i.e.
g\in I:(P+Q^{\infty})
contradicting the assumption.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
P\in{\mathcal{A}}(I)
and let
Q
be the product of the ideals in
\{P_{1}\in{\mathcal{A}}(I):P\subsetneq P_{1}\}
. Consider the Rees ring
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}=\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}(I)
with its natural bigraded structure, i.e.
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}_{(w,n)}=(I^{n})_{w}
for all
(w,n)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
. Consider the extended ideals
{\mathcal{I}}=I\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}=P\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}=Q\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
. Now set

A={\mathcal{I}}:_{\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{P}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad B={\mathcal{I}}:_{\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}}({\mathcal{P}}+{\mathcal{Q}}^{\infty}).

Set
H=A/B
. By construction
H
is a finitely generated bigraded
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
-module whose degree
(w,n)
component equals the degree
w
component of

((I^{n+1}:P)\cap I^{n})/(I^{n+1}:(P+Q^{\infty})\cap I^{n}).

By
[
7
, 4.2]
for large
n
one has
I^{n+1}:I=I^{n}
and so
I^{n+1}:P
is contained in
I^{n}
. Therefore, for large
n
, the degree
(w,n)
component of
H
equals the degree
w
component of

(I^{n+1}:P)/(I^{n+1}:(P+Q^{\infty})).

Hence by
1.2
we have
v_{P}(I^{n+1})=\min\{w:H_{(w,n)}\neq 0\}
for large
n
.

A simple variation of the arguments given in the proof of
[
2
, 3.3]
or
[
3
, 8.3.4]
actually shows that the function
n\to\min\{w:U_{(w,n)}\neq 0\}
is eventually linear in
n
with leading coefficient among the
d_{i}
’s for every finitely generated bigraded
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
-module
U
.

Indeed,
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}
can be presented as a quotient of polynomial extension
A=R[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}]
of
R

bigraded by
\deg y_{i}=(d_{i},1)
for all
i
and
\deg x=(s,0)
for every
x\in R_{s}
.
In
[
2
, 3.3]
and
[
3
, 8.3.4]
the base ring
A_{0}
(which is
R
is our context) is concentrated in degree
0
so that the function
n\to\max\{w:U_{(w,n)}\neq 0\}
is well defined for all finitely generated bigraded
A
-module
U
. As we deal with the function
n\to\min\{w:U_{(w,n)}\neq 0\}
the base ring can be
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded and rest of the arguments of
[
2
, 3.3]
and
[
3
, 8.3.4]
apply to this context verbatim.
∎

Remark 1.3
.

If
J
is a homogeneous reduction of
I
then
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}(I)
is finitely generated as an
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}(J)
-module, see
[
6
, Chap.8]
. Hence the module
H
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is finitely generated over
\operatorname{{\mathcal{R}}}(J)
and the leading coefficient of the function
n\to v_{P}(I^{n})
is equal to the degree of a generator of
J
.

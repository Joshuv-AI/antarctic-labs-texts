1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical background

Given an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and a subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}(E)
, it is natural to consider the twist family of elliptic curves
E_{\zeta}
indexed by cohomology classes
\zeta\in H^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),H)
. When
H=\{\pm 1\}
, the associated twists are called quadratic twists and are in bijection with
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
. Goldfeld’s conjecture predicts that the average rank of quadratic twists
E_{m}
over
\mathbb{Q}
is
1/2
. In fact, the conjecture predicts that when
m
is ordered according to its absolute value, exactly half of the quadratic twists have rank
0
and the other half have rank
1
. The rank distribution in quadratic twist families is studied for instance in
[
BM92
,
HB94
,
KMR13
,
Smi17
,
KL19
,
BKOS19
]
. Set
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3})
and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers. We consider certain families of elliptic curves with rational
3
-isogeny that have complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. For a sixth power free integer
a\neq 0
, set
E_{a}
to be the elliptic curve

E_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a.

Then for a cubefree integer
m
, we have the cubic twists
E_{m^{2}a}
and
E_{m^{4}a}
of
E_{a}
.
Let
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/K)
(resp.
\Sh(E_{a}/K)
) denote the
3
-Selmer group (resp. Tate–Shafarevich group) of
E_{a}
over
K
. The Selmer group sits in a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E_{a}(K)/3E_{a}(K)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E_{a}/K)[3]\rightarrow 0.

It is thus easy to see that
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/K)=0
if and only if

E_{a}(K)[3]=0,\operatorname{rank}(E_{a}(K))=0\text{ and }\Sh(E_{a}/K)[3]=0.

The main focus of this article is to study the following natural question.

Question 1.1
.

Fix a sixth power free integer
a
such that
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/K)=0
. What can one say about the family of cubefree integers
m
for which
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{m^{2}a}/K)=0
and
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{m^{4}a}/K)=0
?

We construct explicit infinite families of numbers
m
for which the above Selmer groups vanish.

1.2.
Main results

We state the main results of this article. It is shown that there is an explicit positive density of primes
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
defined by local congruence conditions, such that if
m
is divisible only by primes
\ell\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
, then,
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{m^{2}a}/K)=0
and
\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{m^{4}a}/K)=0
. In particular,

\operatorname{rank}E_{m^{2}a}(K),\operatorname{rank}E_{m^{4}a}(K)=0\text{ and }\Sh(E_{m^{2}a}/K)[3],\Sh(E_{m^{4}a}/K)[3]=0.

When we specialize to the families
E_{\ell^{2}a}
and
E_{\ell^{4}a}
, where
\ell
is a prime number, we find that our result holds for an explicit positive density of primes
\ell
.

For a prime number
\ell
, set
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. If
E_{a}
has good reduction at
\ell

\widetilde{E}_{a}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
to denote the group of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-points on the reduced curve of
E_{a}
at
\ell
. More generally, if

, for a fixed value of

and
\ell_{i}
prime, then there is a positive density of such numbers for which our result holds.

1.3.
Methodology

The study of Selmer ranks in twist families is related to the study the rank growth and stability of elliptic curves defined over a number field in a
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extension for an odd prime
p
. Let
K
be a number field containing the
p
-th roots of unity and
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
such that the
p
-Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)=0
. Let
L/K
be a cyclic
p
-extension. Then, it is shown that under certain conditions on
E
and
L
, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
as well. This strategy has been adapted from the technique of Mazur–Rubin
[
MR07
,
MRS07
]
, Brau
[
Bra14
]
. In keeping with these works, we study stability questions in prime cyclic extensions for any prime number
p\geq 3
, cf. Theorem
3.12
and Theorem
3.15
for further details.

1.4.
Related work

In recent years, there has been an increasing interest in rank distribution questions for families of elliptic curves from the perspective of
arithmetic statistics
. These techniques involve the parameterization of Selmer groups of elliptic curves and abelian varieties by integral forms in many variables. The distribution properties of such integral forms can be studied via techniques from analytic number theory and the geometry of numbers, and has gained momentum in works of Bhargava–Shankar
[
BS15a
,
BS15b
,
BS13
]
, Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman
[
BES20
]
, Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman
[
ABS22
]
, Shnidman–Weiss
[
SW21
,
SW23
]
etc. Our techniques on the other hand rely on local and global arguments in Galois cohomology (in keeping with those used by Mazur–Rubin, Brau), and one of the consequences is that we obtain very explicit descriptions for the twist families for which the Selmer group vanishes. Related questions have been studied by Satgé
[
Sat86
]
, Iskra
[
Isk96
]
, Goto
[
Got01
]
and Bandini
[
Ban04
]
. It is conceivable that our results should generalize to the context of abelian varieties with complex multiplication. For instance, Jacobians of twist families of bicyclic trigonal curves
y^{3}=f(x^{2})
, cf.
[
SW21
]
for details.

Organization

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be an odd natural number. With respect to notation above, the

-Selmer group of

is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{n}(E/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(K,E[n])\xrightarrow{\Phi}\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{K}}H^{1}(K_{v},E)[n]\right\}.

Here the map
\Phi
is the product of maps
\Phi_{v}
, given by the composite

H^{1}(K,E[n])\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{v}}H^{1}(K_{v},E[n])\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},E)[n].

Let
\Sigma

be the maximal algebraic extension of

\operatorname{G}_{K,\Sigma}
to denote the Galois group

, cf.

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/K).

0\rightarrow E(K)/nE(K)\xrightarrow{\delta_{E,n}}\operatorname{Sel}_{n}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[n]\rightarrow 0.

Given a prime

0\rightarrow E(K)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

As is well known, the Tate–Shafarevich group

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\right)=\operatorname{rank}E(K).

For each integer

It follows from

Let

that have a rational

E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a

is a cubefree integer.
Let

Hence,

is
3

\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\theta_{\varpi}\circ\varphi_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E
be the isogeny which is explicitly given as follows

The kernel of

\{O,(0,\sqrt{a}),(0,-\sqrt{a})\}\subset E(\bar{K})
, cf.
[

the kernel of the map
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E(K)\rightarrow E(K)

Lemma 4.1
.

With respect to notation above, the following are equivalent

(1)

a\notin(K^{\times})^{2}
,

(2)

E(K)[\varphi]=0
,

(3)

E(K)[3]=0
.

Proof.

Note that the point
(0,\sqrt{a})
of

has order
3
and is the generator of

. Thus,

is not a square in

if and only if

is not contained in

. This shows that

i.e., conditions (1) and (2) are equivalent.

There is a natural short exact sequence

Since

is defined over

Thus, one has

∎

4.1.
Results of Mazur and Rubin on the parity of ranks

Note that
E
is isogenous to its quadratic twist by
-3
over
\mathbb{Q}
and hence
\operatorname{rank}E(K)=2\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, the rank of
E(K)
is even. It is however still natural to study the parity of the rank of
E(K)
as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. We briefly discuss parity results of Mazur and Rubin
[
MR07
]
for cubic twist families.

Let
m
be a cubefree integer. Consider the cubic twists
E_{(m)}
and
E_{(m^{2})}
of

, defined as follows

\begin{split}E_{(m)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{m^{2}a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+m^{2}a;\\
E_{(m^{2})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{m^{4}a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+m^{4}a.\end{split}

In the next subsection, it is proven that for many values of
m
, the groups
E_{(m)}(K)
and
E_{(m^{2})}(K)
both have rank
0
. Setting
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=K(\sqrt[3]{m})
, we take
\chi=\chi_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\rightarrow\mu_{3}
to be the cubic character of order
3
defined as follows

\chi(\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\sigma(\sqrt[3]{m})}{\sqrt[3]{m}},

for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
. Since
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{K}}(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}=\mu_{6}
, we find that the action of
\operatorname{G}_{K}
on
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{K}}(E)
is trivial. We view
\mu_{3}
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{K}}(E)
and thus view
\chi
as a cohomology class as follows

\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(\operatorname{G}_{K},\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{K}}(E)\right)=H^{1}\left(\operatorname{G}_{K},\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{K}}(E)\right).

The elliptic curve
E_{(m)}
(resp.
E_{(m^{2})}
) is the twist
E_{\chi}
(resp.
E_{\chi^{2}}
). In other words, there is an isomorphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\chi}\xrightarrow{\sim}E
over
\bar{K}
such that
\phi^{\sigma}=\chi(\sigma)\phi
, cf.
[
Sil09
, section X.2]
. For

, we have that

(4.1)

\sigma(\phi(P))=\chi(\sigma)\phi(\sigma(P)).

Given a
\operatorname{G}_{K}
-module
M
, take

M^{\chi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{m\in M\mid\sigma(m)=\chi(\sigma)m\text{ for all }\sigma\in\operatorname{G}_{K}\}.

In view of (
4.1
), we have the identification
E_{\chi}(K)=E(L)^{\chi}
, and therefore,

E(L)\simeq E(K)\oplus E(L)^{\chi}\oplus E(L)^{\chi^{2}}\simeq E(K)\oplus E_{\chi}(K)\oplus E_{\chi^{2}}(K).

From (
2.4
), one obtains the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E_{\chi}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{3}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{3}(E/L)^{\chi}\rightarrow\operatorname{T}_{3}\left(\Sh(E/L)[3^{\infty}]\right)^{\chi}\rightarrow 0,

and thus,

\operatorname{rank}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{3}(E/L)^{\chi}\right)=\operatorname{rank}E_{\chi}(K)

provided

is finite.

Let
S
be the set of primes of
K
consisting of the prime above
3
, the primes at which
E
has bad reduction and the primes that ramify in
L
. For each prime

, there is a locally defined invariant
\delta_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, cf.
[
MR07
, Definition 4.5]
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{3}[\chi]
be the extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
generated by the values of
\chi
. The following result follows from Theorem A of
loc. cit.

Theorem 4.2

(Mazur–Rubin)
.

With respect to notation above, we have that

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{3}(E/K)\right)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}[\chi]}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{3}(E/L)^{\chi}\right)\equiv\sum_{v\in S}\delta_{v}\pmod{2}.

Note that the value of
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{3}(E/K)\right)
is expected to be even, and thus the above result predicts that

(4.2)

\operatorname{rank}E_{(m)}(K),\operatorname{rank}E_{(m^{2})}(K)\equiv 2\left(\sum_{v\in S}\delta_{v}\right)\pmod{4}.

Since
\operatorname{rank}E_{(m)}(K)=2\operatorname{rank}E_{(m)}(\mathbb{Q})
, the above relation implies that

\operatorname{rank}E_{(m)}(\mathbb{Q}),\operatorname{rank}E_{(m^{2})}(\mathbb{Q})\equiv\left(\sum_{v\in S}\delta_{v}\right)\pmod{2}.

and
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)
.
The representation

\rho=\rho_{E,3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3}),

induces a natural isomorphism between
G
and the image of
\rho
. Let
\ell\nmid 6a
be a prime, then,
\ell
is unramified in
F
. Denote by
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in G
a Frobenius element of
\ell
. Recall that
A
is the subset of
G
consisting of
\sigma
such that

\operatorname{trace}\left(\rho(\sigma)\right)\neq 2\text{ and }\operatorname{det}\left(\rho(\sigma)\right)=1.

By Lemma
4.9
, the following are equivalent

(1)

\ell\equiv 1\mod{3}
and
\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[3]=0
,

(2)

\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in A
.

It is clear that
A
is contained in
H
. Since
a
is assumed to not be square in
K
, we find that
E(K)[3]=0
. By Lemma
4.7
,
-\operatorname{Id}\in A
. Given any matrix
h\in H
, we have that
\operatorname{det}(\rho(h))=1
and
\operatorname{trace}(\rho(h))
is either
1
or
2
. Note that the trace of a matrix with determinant
1
cannot be
0
. If
h\in H
, then since
-\operatorname{Id}\in H
, we have that
-h\in H
. Thus, we find that

(4.3)

\#A=\frac{\#H}{2}.

We compute the density of
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
by applying the Chebotarev density theorem to the field
F\cdot\mathbb{L}
. Note that
F\cap\mathbb{L}=K
by Lemma
4.10
. Thus, the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F\cdot\mathbb{L}/K)\simeq H\times\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/K)
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the subset
A\times\{1\}
contained in
\operatorname{Gal}(F\cdot\mathbb{L}/K)
and view
\mathcal{S}
as a subset of
\operatorname{Gal}(F\cdot\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{Q})
. Note that the set
\mathcal{S}
is stable with respect to the action of conjugation. Let
\ell
be a prime which is unramified in
F\cdot\mathbb{L}
and such that
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then, by Lemma
4.8
, the prime
\ell
splits completely in
\mathbb{L}
, and consequently, conditions (1)–(3) of Definition
4.5
are satisfied. Also
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in A
, it follows from Lemma
4.9
that (4) is also satisfied. Thus, by the Chebotarev density theorem and (
4.3
), we find that

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{a})=\frac{\#\mathcal{S}}{[F\cdot\mathbb{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{\#\mathcal{S}}{2[F\cdot\mathbb{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]}=\frac{\#A}{2\#H\times[\mathbb{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{1}{4[\mathbb{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{1}{8\times 3^{s+1}}.

∎

We prove the main result of this article.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

It is easy to see that
a\notin(K^{\times})^{2}
. The result is a direct consequence of Corollary
4.6
and Proposition
4.11
.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
E
and
K
be as in Theorem
A
. Then, there is a positive density of primes
\ell
such that

\operatorname{rank}E_{\ell^{2}a}(K)=0\text{ and }\operatorname{rank}E_{\ell^{4}a}(K)=0.

Proof.

The result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
A
, upon setting
m=\ell
to be a prime number in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
.

\mathcal{Q}_{-1}

4.5
) consists of the primes

4.6
then asserts that if

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be the elliptic curve

. Given a cube-free integer

Hybrid Receiver Design for Massive MIMO-OFDM with Low-Resolution ADCs and Oversampling
Mengyuan Ma, Nhan Thanh Nguyen, Italo Atzeni, and Markku Juntti

Abstract
Low-resolution analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) and hybrid beamforming have emerged as efficient solutions to reduce power consumption with satisfactory spectral efficiency (SE) in massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems. In this paper, we investigate the performance of a hybrid receiver in massive MIMO orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) uplink systems with low-resolution ADCs and oversampling. Considering both the temporal and spatial correlation of the quantization distortion (QD), we derive a closed-form approximation of the frequency-domain QD covariance matrix, which facilitates the evaluation of the system’s SE. Then we jointly design the analog and digital combiners of the hybrid receiver to maximize the SE. The formulated problem is challenging due to the constant-modulus constraint of the analog combiner and its coupling with the digital one. To overcome these challenges, we transform the objective function into an equivalent but more tractable form and then iteratively update the analog and digital combiners. Numerical simulations verify the superiority of the proposed algorithm over the considered benchmarks and show the resilience of the hybrid receiver to beam squint with low-resolution ADCs. Furthermore, the proposed hybrid receiver design with oversampling can achieve significantly higher energy efficiency compared with the fully digital one.



I
Introduction

Massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technology stands as a cornerstone in current and future wireless systems
[
1
]
, leveraging large antenna arrays to deliver exceptional spectral efficiency (SE)
[
2
]
. However, the deployment of massive MIMO at millimeter-wave and
(sub-)THz
frequencies presents a significant challenge: the high power consumption associated with the numerous radio-frequency (RF) chains poses a threat to the system’s energy efficiency (EE)
[
3
,
4
]
. On the one hand, the analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) are typically the most power-hungry RF components, as their power consumption increases exponentially with the number of resolution bits
[
5
]
. For instance, high-speed ADCs with 8–12 bits operating at 1 Gsample/s can consume several Watts
[
6
]
, which motivates the adoption of low-resolution ADCs. On the other hand, hybrid analog-digital beamforming techniques can effectively reduce the required number of RF chains without unduly compromising the SE
[
7
]
. In this context, the integration of low-resolution ADCs within hybrid beamforming architectures emerges as a compelling strategy to balance energy cost and SE performance
[
8
,
9
]
.

The performance of hybrid receivers with low-resolution ADCs was investigated in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, demonstrating a flexible trade-off between power consumption and SE performance. However, these works mainly focus on narrowband single-carrier systems and, thus, their results are not applicable to wideband multi-carrier systems, e.g., operating in the
(sub-)THz
band. This is because the analog combiner in the hybrid receiver is frequency-flat and must be designed for all the subcarriers. Furthermore, the coarse quantization introduces additional correlation of the time-domain symbols, which affects the frequency-domain performance analysis
[
13
]
. The performance of MIMO orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) uplink systems with low-resolution ADCs was investigated in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. For example, it was shown in
[
14
,
15
]
that few-bit quantization in MIMO-OFDM systems can approach the performance of the full-resolution case. In our previous work
[
17
]
, we showed that oversampling in a MIMO-OFDM uplink system can significantly enhance the sum rate, especially at high signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) and with very low ADC resolutions. However,
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
only investigated fully digital receivers, leaving the hybrid receiver design for low-resolution wideband systems unexplored. Furthermore, while the beam squint effect has been shown to significantly degrade the performance of hybrid beamforming with full-resolution ADCs
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
, its impact on low-resolution systems remains an open question.

To bridge the knowledge gap described above, we herein investigate a massive MIMO-OFDM uplink system employing both low-resolution ADCs and hybrid beamforming. Specifically, we first derive a closed-form approximation for the frequency-domain covariance matrix of the quantization distortion (QD), which considers both the temporal and spatial correlation. With this approximation, we jointly design the analog and digital combiners to maximize the SE. The formulated problem is complicated by the constant-modulus constraint of the analog combiner and its coupling with the digital one. To overcome these challenges, we transform the objective function into an equivalent but more tractable form and then iteratively update the analog and digital combiners. Numerical simulations verify the superiority of the proposed algorithm over the considered benchmarks. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that the performance degradation of the hybrid receiver due to the beam squint effect is less significant with low-resolution ADCs. Lastly, the EE comparison shows that the proposed hybrid receiver design with oversampling significantly outperforms the considered fully digital one.

II
System and Quantization Models

II-A

Signal Model

We consider an uplink massive MIMO system where a base station (BS) equipped with
M
antennas receives signals from
I
single-antenna user equipments (UEs). We assume that the BS deploys a fully connected hybrid beamforming architecture in which each RF chain connects to all the antennas through a phase-shifter network. Let
N_{\rm RF}
be the number of RF chains at the BS. OFDM is assumed over a wideband channel to deal with the frequency selectivity. Specifically, let
\Delta f=\frac{1}{T_{\rm u}}
be the subcarrier spacing, where
T_{\rm u}
denotes the OFDM symbol duration, which is assumed to be fixed. Let
f_{k}=f_{\rm c}+\left(k+1-\frac{N_{\rm c}+1}{2}\right)\Delta f
,
k=0,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1
, denote the
k
-th subcarrier frequency, where
f_{\rm c}
is the center carrier frequency. Among the
N_{\rm c}
subcarriers,
K
of them are dedicated to the signal transmission while the other
N_{\rm c}-K
are employed for oversampling
[
13
]
. Let
\tilde{s}_{i}[k]
be the transmit symbol of the
i
-th UE at the
k
-th subcarrier, with
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\tilde{s}_{i}[k]|^{2}\right]=1
for
k=0,\ldots,K-1
. Note that, when
N_{\rm c}>K
, we have
\tilde{s}_{i}[k]=0
for
k=K,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1
. Since the sampling frequency is
f_{\rm s}=N_{\rm c}\Delta f
and the transmission bandwidth is
B_{\rm w}=K\Delta f
, the oversampling ratio (OSR) is defined as
\beta=\frac{N_{\rm c}}{K}
. We assume that
N_{\rm c}
can be adjusted, while
K
is fixed. Hence,
\beta=1
and
\beta>1
indicate the Nyquist sampling and the oversampling scheme, respectively. The time-domain symbol is obtained by an
N_{\rm c}
-point inverse discrete Fourier transform (IDFT), i.e.,

s_{i}[n]=\sqrt{\frac{p}{N_{\rm c}}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{\rm c}-1}\tilde{s}_{i}[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi nk}{N_{\rm c}}},\quad n=0,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1,

(1)

where
n
represents the index of the time-domain symbol and
p
denotes the average transmit power for the symbols. At the receiver, the time-domain signals are first downconverted to the digital domain and then transformed back to the frequency domain by an
N_{\rm c}
-point discrete Fourier transform (DFT).

Define
\mathbf{s}[n]=\left[s_{1}[n],\ldots,s_{I}[n]\right]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
and let
\mathbf{H}[d]=\left[\mathbf{h}_{1}[d],\cdots,\mathbf{h}_{I}[d]\right]\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M\times I}
denote the channel matrix at the
d
-th delay tap, where
\mathbf{h}_{i}[d]
represents the channel of the
i
-th UE. The discrete-time received signal at the BS at time sample
n
is given by

\mathbf{r}[n]=\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\mathbf{H}[d]\mathbf{s}[n-d]+\mathbf{w}[n],

(2)

with
D=\beta D_{0}
, where
D_{0}
is the maximum number of delay taps under Nyquist sampling and
\mathbf{w}[n]\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}_{\rm n}\mathbf{I}_{M})
represents the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) vector with variance
\sigma_{\rm n}^{2}
. Now, define
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}[k]=[\tilde{s}_{1}[k],\ldots,\tilde{s}_{I}[k]]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
, where
\tilde{s}_{i}[k]
is the frequency-domain signal transmitted by the
i
-th UE, and
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]=[\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{1}[k],\ldots,\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{I}[k]]
, with
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}[k]=\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\mathbf{h}_{i}[d]e^{-j\frac{2\pi dk}{N_{\rm c}}}
. By taking the DFT of both sides of (
2
), the frequency-domain received signal is expressed as

\tilde{\mathbf{r}}[k]=\sqrt{p}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]\tilde{\mathbf{s}}[k]+\tilde{\mathbf{w}}[k],\quad k=0,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1,

(3)

with
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}[k]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm c}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\rm c}-1}\mathbf{r}[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi nk}{N_{\rm c}}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}[k]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm c}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\rm c}-1}\mathbf{w}[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi nk}{N_{\rm c}}}
. Note that
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}[k]=\tilde{\mathbf{w}}[k]
for
k=K,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1
as
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}[k]=\mathbf{0}
in these cases.

II-B

Quantization Model

We assume that the ADCs at all the RF chains are scalar quantizers and have the same number of resolution bits
b
. Define the codebook of a quantizer of
b
bits as
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{0},\ldots,c_{N_{\rm q}-1}\}
, where
N_{\rm q}=2^{b}
is the number of output levels. The set of quantization thresholds is
\mathcal{T}=\{t_{0},\ldots,t_{N_{\rm q}}\}
, where
t_{0}=-\infty
and
t_{N_{\rm q}}=\infty
allow inputs with arbitrary power. For standard Gaussian signals, the Lloyd-Max algorithm
[
21
]
can find optimal sets
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{T}
that achieve the minimum mean squared error (MMSE) between the input and output of the quantizer. Let
Q(\cdot)
denote the quantization function. For a complex signal
x=\Re\{x\}+j\Im\{x\}
, we have

Q(x)=Q(\Re\{x\})+jQ(\Im\{x\})
with
Q(\Re\{x\})=c_{i}
when
\Re\{x\}\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, and
Q(\Im\{x\})
can be obtained in a similar fashion. When the input signal of the quantizer is a vector,
Q(\cdot)
is applied elementwise.

To model the quantization of the received signal in (
2
), we first rewrite (
2
) as

\bar{\mathbf{r}}=\left[\mathbf{r}[N_{\rm c}-1]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{r}[0]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}\right]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=\bar{\mathbf{H}}\bar{\mathbf{s}}+\bar{\mathbf{w}},

(4)

with
\bar{\mathbf{s}}=[\mathbf{s}[N_{\rm c}-1]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{s}[0]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{w}}=\left[\mathbf{w}[N_{\rm c}-1]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{w}[0]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}\right]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
, and where
\bar{\mathbf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{MN_{\rm c}\times IN_{\rm c}}
is a block circulant matrix
[
16
]
. Before quantization, the received signal is first combined in the analog domain. Denote the analog combiner as
\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M\times N_{\rm RF}}
, which has constant-modulus entries. At the BS, the received time-domain signal
\bar{\mathbf{r}}
is first combined with
\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}
and then fed into the ADCs. Therefore, the output of the ADCs is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{z}}=Q(\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\bar{\mathbf{r}}),

(5)

with
\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm RF}=\mathbf{I}_{N_{\rm c}}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}
, where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product. The Bussgang-based additive quantization noise model (BAQNM) allows to model a nonlinear input-output relation of a Gaussian signal as a linear transformation
[
22
]
. With the BAQNM, (
5
) can be expressed as

\bar{\mathbf{z}}=\left[\mathbf{z}[N_{\rm c}-1]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\ldots,\mathbf{z}[0]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}\right]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=\alpha\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\bar{\mathbf{r}}+\bar{{\bm{\eta}}},

(6)

where
\bar{{\bm{\eta}}}=\left[{\bm{\eta}}[N_{\rm c}-1]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\ldots,{\bm{\eta}}[0]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}\right]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
denotes the non-Gaussian QD vector that is uncorrelated with
\bar{\mathbf{r}}
,
\alpha=1-\gamma
represents the Bussgang gain, and
\gamma
denotes the distortion factor of the ADCs, which depends on the number of resolution bits as
\gamma(b)\approx 2^{-1.74b+0.28}

[
22
]
. Furthermore, (
6
) is equivalent to

\mathbf{z}[n]=\alpha\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{r}[n]+{\bm{\eta}}[n],\quad n=0,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1.

(7)

By taking the DFT of both sides of (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{z}}[k]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm c}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\rm c}-1}\mathbf{z}[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi nk}{N_{\rm c}}}=\alpha\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{r}}[k]+\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{p}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]\tilde{\mathbf{s}}[k]+\mathbf{e}[k],\quad k=0,\ldots,N_{\rm c}-1,

(8)

with
\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm c}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\rm c}-1}{\bm{\eta}}[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi nk}{N_{\rm c}}}
and where
\mathbf{e}[k]=\alpha\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}[k]+\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]
includes both the AWGN and QD. Note that the latter is non-Gaussian due to the presence of
\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{\rm RF}}
denote the digital combining vector of the
i
-th UE at the
k
-th subcarrier and define
\mathbf{U}_{\rm BB}[k]=\left[\mathbf{u}_{1}[k],\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{I}[k]\right]
. Finally, the post-combined signal of the
i
-th UE at the
k
-th subcarrier is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{i}[k]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\alpha\sqrt{p}\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}[k]\tilde{s}_{i}[k]}_{\text{desired signal}}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{\alpha\sqrt{p}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j}[k]\tilde{s}_{j}[k]}_{\text{interference}}+\underbrace{\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{e}[k]}_{\text{AWGN and QD}}.

(9)

III
Hybrid Receiver Design

III-A

Problem Formulation

Based on (
II-B
), the signal-to-interference-plus-noise-and-distortion ratio (SINDR) of the
i
-th UE at the

-th subcarrier can be expressed as

\zeta_{i}[k]=\frac{p\alpha^{2}|\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}[k]|^{2}}{p\alpha^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}|\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j}[k]|^{2}+\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{e}_{k}}\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]},

(10)

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{e}_{k}}={\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{e}[k]\mathbf{e}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\right]=\mathbf{C}_{\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{k}}+\alpha^{2}\sigma_{\rm n}^{2}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}

\mathbf{C}_{\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{k}}={\mathbb{E}}\left[\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\right]

23
]

R=\frac{1}{K}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{I}\log_{2}\left(1+\zeta_{i}[k]\right).

(11)

We observe that

is challenging because both the temporal and spatial correlation of the QD must be considered. We herein present a closed-form approximation in the following proposition.

Proposition 1

Let

denote a diagonal matrix with the same diagonal entries as

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{k}}

\displaystyle\approx\gamma(1-\gamma)\frac{p}{N_{\rm c}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{K-1}{\rm diag}(\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\ +\gamma(1-\gamma)\sigma_{\rm n}^{2}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF},

(12)

where its accuracy increases with the ADC resolution.

1

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{r}}[\iota]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{r}[n]\mathbf{r}[n-\iota]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\right]

\mathbf{C}_{{\bm{\eta}}}[\iota]={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\eta}}[n]{\bm{\eta}}[n-\iota]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\right]

[
22
]

\mathbf{C}_{{\bm{\eta}}}[\iota]
includes both the temporal and spatial correlation of the QD.

Define
\mathbf{H}_{\rm e}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}{\rm diag}(\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]\tilde{\mathbf{H}}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF})

\zeta_{i}[k]\approx\frac{|\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}[k]|^{2}}{\sum_{j\neq i}|\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{U}_{\rm RF}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j}[k]|^{2}+\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{u}_{i}[k]},\vskip 6.0pt

We note that

V
Conclusion

We studied the hybrid receiver design in a massive MIMO-OFDM system with low-resolution ADCs and oversampling. Specifically, we first derived a closed-form approximation for the frequency-domain covariance matrix of the QD, which facilitates the evaluation of SE. Then we proposed an efficient algorithm to jointly optimize the analog and digital combiners to maximize the SE. Numerical simulations validated the superiority of the proposed algorithm over the considered benchmarks. Furthermore, the results showed that the hybrid receiver is less affected by the beam squint effect with low-resolution ADCs. In addition, the EE comparison showed that the proposed hybrid receiver design with oversampling significantly outperforms the considered fully digital receiver when slightly more (larger than
3
) ADC bits are used per RF chain.

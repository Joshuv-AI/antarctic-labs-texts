On chromatic vertex stability of 3-chromatic graphs with maximum degree 4
Martin Knor, Mirko Petruševski, Riste Škrekovski, [0.3cm] Slovak University of Technology in Bratislava, Bratislava, Slovakia, [0.1cm] University Ss Cyril and Methodius, MFS, 1000 Skopje, Macedonia, [0.1cm] University of Ljubljana, FMF, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia, [0.1cm] Faculty of Information Studies, 8000 Novo Mesto, Slovenia [0.1cm]

Abstract
The (independent) chromatic vertex stability ( ivs χ ​ ( G ) {\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G) ) vs χ ​ ( G ) {\rm vs_{\chi}}(G) is the minimum size of (independent) set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) such that χ ⁡ ( G − S ) = χ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \chi(G-S)=\chi(G)-1 . In this paper we construct infinitely many graphs G G with Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 4 \Delta(G)=4 , χ ⁡ ( G ) = 3 \chi(G)=3 , ivs χ ​ ( G ) = 3 {\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3 and vs χ ​ ( G ) = 2 {\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2 , which gives a partial negative answer to a problem posed in [ 3 ] .

Introduction

Let
G
be a graph.
Its edge stability number,
\rm{es}_{\chi}(G)
, is the minimum number of edges whose deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
.
Edge stability number was introduced in 1980 by Staton
[
8
]
, and rediscoverd in 2008 by Arumugam, Sahul Hamid and Muthukamatchi
[
4
]
.
For recent results on this invariant see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
]
.

General concept of stability number appeared in
[
5
]
, but the first paper on chromatic vertex stability number was written by Akbari, Beikmohammadi, Klavžar and Movarraei in 2021, see
[
3
]
.
The chromatic vertex stability
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)
of
G
is the minimum number of vertices of
G
such that their deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
.
Analogously, the independent chromatic vertex stability
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
of
G
is the minimum number of independent vertices of
G
such that their deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
. Obviously,
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)\leq{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
.
The main result of
[
3
]
is the following.

Theorem 1
.

If
G
is a graph with
\chi(G)\in\{\Delta(G),\Delta(G)+1\}
then
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
.

The authors showed that as soon as
\chi(G)\leq\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}
the equality
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
need no longer be true. Whether
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
always holds if
\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1\leq\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
was left unanswered, so they asked the following question (see Problem 3.2 in
[
3
]
).

Problem 2
.

Is it true that if
G
is a graph with
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
, then
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
?

In this paper we prove that Problem
2
answers in the negative for
\chi(G)=3
and
\Delta(G)=4
. A simple ‘ladder-like’ counterexample on
9
vertices is depicted in Figure
1
. Notice that the ladder part of the counterexample can be of any length
4k
for
k\geq 2
.

:
A graph
G
with
\Delta(G)=4
and
\chi(G)=3
. The blue independent
3
-set realizes
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
and the yellow
2
-set realizes
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

For each
n\geq 9
there are at least
\max\{1,2^{\lfloor\frac{n-11}{2}\rfloor}\}
planar graphs with
\chi(G)=3
,
\Delta(G)=4
,
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
and
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
.

Observe that if
\chi(G)=3
and
\Delta(G)=4
, then
\chi(G)=\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1=\Delta(G)-1
.
Hence, the bound on
\chi(G)
in Theorem
1
cannot be relaxed
when
\chi(G)=3
.

Proofs

We start with a pair of simple observations followed by a couple of lemmas.

Observation 4
.

For every graph
G
,
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
equals the minimum size of a colour class over all proper
\chi(G)
-colourings of
G
. Hence
|V(G)|\geq{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\cdot\chi(G)
.

Proof.

Let us first notice that there exists a proper
\chi(G)
-colouring of
G
with a colour class of size
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
. Indeed, take
S\subseteq V(G)
to be an independent set of size
|S|={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
such that
\chi(G-S)=\chi(G)-1
.
Use a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-colouring of
G-S
and assign to vertices of
S
a new colour.
So
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
is not less than the minimum size of a colour class over all proper
\chi(G)
-colourings of
G
.

Contrarily, consider a proper
\chi(G)
-colouring of
G
which minimizes the size of a colour class, and let
S
be such a minimum colour class. Then
S
is an independent subset of
V(G)
and
\chi(G-S)\leq\chi(G)-1
. In fact, we must have equality here for otherwise
G
would admit a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-colouring. So
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
is also not more than the minimum size of a colour class over all proper
\chi(G)
-colourings of
G
, which proves our point.

The inequality
|V(G)|\geq{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\cdot\chi(G)
is now an immediate consequence.
∎

Observation 5
.

If
\Delta(G)\leq 2
then
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 2
.
Indeed, if
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=1
then obviously
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=1
as well.
We may also assume that
G
is connected.
Then
G
is either a path or an even cycle.
In either case
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{|V(G)|}{2}\rfloor
.
∎

As already mentioned, we are interested in finding graphs
G
for which
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
and
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)>{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)

Lemma 6

If
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)>{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)
and
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
then
|V(G)|\geq 9
,
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 3
,
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 2
and
\chi(G)\geq 3
. Moreover, if
|V(G)|=9

{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
,
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
and
\chi(G)=3

Proof.

Since
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)>{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)
, we must have
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 2
and consequently
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 3
.

If
\chi(G)\leq 2

\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1

\Delta(G)\leq 2(\chi(G)-1)\leq 2
, which in view of Observation

From the inequality stated in Observation

, that is,
|V(G)|\geq 9
.
And if
|V(G)|=9
then
\chi(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
and
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
.
∎

Our second lemma concerns the case
\chi(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
and
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
. Under the assumption
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
we establish the maximum degree of

and the vertex degrees of every
2
-set which realizes

.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\chi(G)={\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
,
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
and
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
.
Then
\Delta(G)=4
and for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(G)
such that
\chi(G-\{v_{1},v_{2}\})=2
we have
\deg_{G}(v_{1})=\deg_{G}(v_{2})=4
.

Proof.

Let
G
satisfy the assumptions of the lemma and let
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(G)
such that
\chi(G-\{v_{1},v_{2}\})=2
. Then
\Delta(G)\leq 2(\chi(G)-1)=4
.
By way of contradiction, suppose that
\deg_{G}(v_{1})\leq 3
.
Since
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3>2
, we have
v_{1}v_{2}\in E(G)
.
Further, since
\chi(G-v_{2})=3
, there must be an odd cycle in
G-v_{2}
; moreover,
every such cycle passes through
v_{1}
as
G-\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
is bipartite.
Consequently
\deg_{G}(v_{1})=3
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be neighbours of
v_{1}
in
G-v_{2}
. Since every odd cycle in
G-v_{2}
passes through both
u_{1},u_{2}
, we conclude that

G-\{u_{i},v_{2}\}
is bipartite as well,
i\in\{1,2\}
. From
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3>2
it follows that
u_{1}v_{2},u_{2}v_{2}\in E(G)
.
Moreover,
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
, for otherwise
v_{1},v_{2},u_{1},u_{2}
induces
K_{4}
, implying
\chi(G)\geq 4
.

Since
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\geq 3
, there must be an odd cycle in
G-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
.
This cycle cannot pass through
v_{1}
since
\deg_{G-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}}(v_{1})=1
.
If this cycle does not pass through
v_{2}
as well then it is in
G-\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
which means that
\chi(G-\{v_{1},v_{2}\})\geq 3
, a contradiction.
Hence, there is an odd cycle passing through
v_{2}
in
G-\{u_{1},u_{2},v_{1}\}
, which means that
\deg_{G}(v_{2})\geq 5
. This contradiction settles the lemma.
∎

Proposition 8
.

Let
G\in S_{n}
and

e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{t}\in E(G)\backslash E([{C_{\chi}}(G)])
, i.e., each
e_{i}
has at most one endvertex in
{C_{\chi}}(G)
. Let
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{t}
be positive even integers.
For every
i
,
1\leq i\leq t
, subdivide
e_{i}
with
n_{i}
new vertices, and denote the resulting graph by
H
.
Then
\Delta(H)=4
,
\chi(H)=3
,
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(H)=3
and
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(H)=2
. In other words,
H\in S_{n+(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{t})}
.

Proof.

Obviously
\Delta(H)=\Delta(G)=4
.
As
\chi(G)=3
, the graph
G
has an odd cycle.
Since to any edge of this cycle we added an even number (possibly zero) of vertices,
H
also has an odd cycle; thus
\chi(H)\geq 3
.
Moreover, if
S\subseteq V(G)
is such that
G-S
is bipartite then
H-S
is bipartite as well.
Hence,
\chi(H)=3
,
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(H)\leq 3
and
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(H)\leq 2
.

If there is
v\in V(H)
such that
\chi(H-v)=2
, then
v\notin V(G)
.
So
v
is obtained by subdividing an edge, say
xy
, of
G
.
However, as
H-v
is bipartite, both
G-x
and
G-y
are bipartite, a contradiction.
Hence,
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(H)=2
.

Finally, let us show that
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(H)=3
. Supposing the opposite, there are
u,v\in V(H)
such that
\chi(H-\{u,v\})=2
and
uv\notin E(H)
. It cannot be that both
u
and
v
are in
V(G)
, because we did not subdivide edges connecting vertices of

{C_{\chi}}(G)
.
So we may assume that
v
is obtained by subdividing an edge
xy
of
G
, where
y\notin{C_{\chi}}(G)
.
Since
H-\{u,v\}
is bipartite, so is
H-\{u,y\}
.
But then
u
cannot be a vertex of
G
as well.
Hence,
u
is obtained by subdividing an edge
wz
of
G
.
As
H-\{u,y\}
is bipartite, so is
H-\{z,y\}
. However, this contradicts the fact that
y\notin{C_{\chi}}(G)
.

1
to
u
, the colour
2
to
v
and the colour
3
to
w
we obtain a proper
3
-colouring of
G
.

Let us show next that
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
. Since
G-\{a,b,u\}
is bipartite, we have
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)\leq 3
.
Suppose there are non-adjacent vertices
x,y
such that
G-\{x,y\}
is bipartite.
In view of the triangle
uvw
, the intersection
\{x,y\}\cap\{u,v,w\}
is a singleton.
We argue that this intersection is not the vertex
u
due to the triangles
avw
and
bvw
.
Let
P
and
Q
be the two
a-b
paths in
C_{2k}
, and recall that both these paths are of odd lengths. Consequently, each of the cycles
C^{\prime}=P\cup avb
,
C^{\prime\prime}=P\cup awb
,
C^{\prime\prime\prime}=Q\cup avb
, and
C^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}=Q\cup awb
is odd. Hence
\{x,y\}\cap\{v,w\}\neq\emptyset
, which further implies that
\{x,y\}\cap\{a,b\}=\emptyset
. However, then at least one of the cycles
C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},C^{\prime\prime\prime},C^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}
appears in
G-\{x,y\}
. The obtained contradiction shows
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=3
.

Finally, we prove that
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
.
Clearly
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)\leq 2
, because
G-\{v,w\}
is bipartite.
And since
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=1
implies
{\rm ivs_{\chi}}(G)=1
, we have
{\rm vs_{\chi}}(G)=2
.
∎

Note in passing that the order of the bound
|S_{n}|\geq 2^{\lfloor\frac{n-11}{2}\rfloor}
obtained in Theorem
9
is far from (asymptotically) optimal. Propositions
8
and
10
enable one to construct connected planar graphs within
S_{n}
with considerable ease. However, establishing a more precise asymptotic estimate of
|S_{n}|
was not the focus of this short article; instead, the aim was simply to point out to the existence of exponentially many graphs in
S_{n}
.

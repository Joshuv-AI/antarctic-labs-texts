Truncation Error Analysis for an Accurate Nonlocal Approximation to Manifold Poisson Models with Dirichlet Boundary Thanks: This work was supported by NSFC grant 12071244, 11671005.
Yajie Zhang, Zuoqiang Shi

Abstract
In this work, we introduced a class of nonlocal models to accurately approximate the Poisson model on manifolds that are embedded in high dimensional Euclid spaces with Dirichlet boundary. In comparison to the existing nonlocal approximation to Poisson models, instead of utilizing volumetric boundary constraint to reduce the truncation error to its local counterpart, we rely on the Poisson equation itself along the boundary to explicitly express the second order normal derivative by some geometry-based terms, so that to create a new model with 𝒪 ⁡ ( δ ) \mathcal{O}(\delta) truncation error along the 2 ​ δ − 2\delta- boundary layer and 𝒪 ⁡ ( δ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}) at interior, with δ \delta be the nonlocal interaction horizon. Our concentration is on the construction and the truncation error analysis of such nonlocal model. The control on the truncation error is currently optimal among all nonlocal models, and is sufficient to attain second order localization rate that will be derived in our subsequent work.

Manifold Poisson equation, Dirichlet boundary, nonlocal approximation, truncation error analysis, second order convergence.

2
Nonlocal Model

In this section, we briefly introduce how our model is formulated. Recall the Poisson model to be approximated:

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u({x})=f({x})&{x}\in\mathcal{M},\\
u=0&{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

In our nonlocal model, the kernel function
R_{\delta}({x},{y})=C_{\delta}R\big(\frac{|{x}-{y}|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\big)
appeared in (
1.6
) plays a crucial role. A better choice of
R
will result in much less work in the error analysis. To this end, we make the following assumptions on
R(r)
:

1.

Smoothness:
\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}R(r)
is bounded, i.e., for any
r\geq 0
we have
\big|\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}R(r)\big|\leq C
;

2.

Nonnegativity:
R(r)>0
for any
r\geq 0
;

3.

Compact support:
R(r)=0
for any
r>1
;

4.

Nondegenearcy:
\exists\ \delta_{0}>0
so that
R(r)\geq\delta_{0}>0
for
0\leq r\leq 1/2
.

These assumptions are very mild, almost all the smooth functions in the literature satisfy these conditions. The first condition makes
R
not blow up near the origin, while the fourth condition assures a non-degenerate interval for
R
. Despite that the removal of third condition will make the problem more interesting, we keep this condition to avoid complication in the error analysis.
A frequently used kernel function
R
is the following:

R(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos\pi r),&0\leq r\leq 1,\\
0,&r>1.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Based on
R
, we write down the other two related functions
\bar{R}(r)=\int_{r}^{+\infty}R(s)ds
,
\overset{=}{R}(r)=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\bar{R}(s)ds
and their corresponding kernel functions

\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})=C_{\delta}\bar{R}\big(\frac{|{x}-{y}|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\big),\qquad\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})=C_{\delta}\overset{=}{R}\big(\frac{|{x}-{y}|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\big).

(2.3)

It is clear that
\bar{R}
and
\overset{=}{R}
satisfy the assumptions 1-4 as well by replacing
\delta_{0}
with some other positive constants in the fourth condition.

In the next section, we will show the error of (
1.7
) can be improved by adding a term involving the second order normal derivative:

\begin{split}\!\!\!\!\!-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u({y})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}\approx\ &\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}(u({x})-u({y}))R_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}-2\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y}\\
&-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({y}))\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}({y})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y},\qquad{x}\in\mathcal{M}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Here
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}n^{i}n^{j}\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{i}\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{j}u
and
n^{i},\ n^{j}
is the
i,j^{th}
component of
{n}
. For simplicity, we always write
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}
as
n^{i}n^{j}\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}u
or
u_{{n}{n}}
. The last term of (
2.4
) is supported in the layer adjacent to the boundary with width
\delta
.

The main difficulty to apply (
2.4
) into a nonlocal model is the need of handling the term
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}
, which usually keep people away from improving the accuracy of model.
Fortunately, in the case when
u\equiv 0
throughout the boundary, certain relation can be exploited between
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}
and
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}u
after further study on the shape of boundary. In lemma
3.3
of section
3
, we will prove the following equality

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}({y})=\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u({y})+\kappa_{{n}}({y})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y}),\qquad\forall\ {y}\in\partial\mathcal{M}

(2.5)

whenever
u\in H^{4}(\mathcal{M})
and
u\equiv 0
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
. This is fundamental in the geometric analysis. Here
\kappa_{{n}}
is a function that depends only on the shape of
\mathcal{M}
and
\partial\mathcal{M}
. A detailed description of
\kappa_{{n}}
will be given in lemma
3.3
. Specially, in the two dimensional case where
m=2
,

\kappa_{{n}}({y})=\kappa({y})({n}({y})\cdot{n}^{b}({y})),\qquad\forall\ {y}\in\partial\mathcal{M},

(2.6)

where
\kappa({y})
denotes the curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
at
{y}
, and
{n}^{b}({y})
denotes the unit principal normal vector of
\partial\mathcal{M}
at
{y}
.

Based on (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we are ready to set up an equation that approximates the Poisson problem (
2.1
): we express
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}{n}}
in (
2.4
) by the equality (
2.5
) and assign
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u
as
-f
in (
2.4
), then equalize two sides of (
2.4
) after replacing
u
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}
by new variables
u_{\delta}
and
v_{\delta}
to write

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{\delta}({x})=\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f({x})\qquad{x}\in\mathcal{M},

(2.7)

where the operators in (
2.7
) are defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}(u_{\delta}({x})-u_{\delta}({y}))\ {R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},

(2.8)

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{\delta}({x})=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}v_{\delta}({y})\ (2+({x}-{y})\cdot\kappa_{{n}}({y}){n}({y}))\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y},

(2.9)

\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f({x})=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f({y})\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({y}))\ f({y})\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y}.

(2.10)

We aim to approximate the solution
u
of (
2.1
) by
u_{\delta}
, and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}
by
v_{\delta}
. Apparently, (
2.7
) is an equation for
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
and has infinitely many solutions, one more equation for
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
along the boundary is required to assure the uniqueness of solution.
To this end, it is necessary to explore additional relation between
u
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}
. In view of the term
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{\delta}
in (
2.9
), we write down the following two approximations

\begin{split}2\int_{\mathcal{M}}\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})(u({x})-u({y}))d\mu_{{y}}-4\delta^{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({x})d\tau_{y}\\
\approx-2\delta^{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u({y})\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{{y}}\qquad{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M};\end{split}

(2.11)

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big((u({x})-u({y})-({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({x})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({x})\big)\ ({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({x})\kappa_{{n}}({x})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d{y}\approx 0\qquad{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M}.

(2.12)

Here (
2.11
) is a slight modification of (
1.7
), and (
2.12
) is a Taylor expansion residue in the normal direction.
The truncation error analysis will be given in the next section. Next, similar to (
2.7
), we are ready to construct our second nonlocal equation that approximates (
2.1
): we assign
u({x})
in (
2.11
), (
2.12
) as
0
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}u({y})
as
-f({y})
, replace
u({y})
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}
by
u_{\delta}({y})
and
v_{\delta}
, then equalize two sides of (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) to sum up

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})+\tilde{R}_{\delta}({x})v_{\delta}({x})=\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f({x}),\qquad{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M},

(2.13)

where the operators are defined as

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})=\int_{\mathcal{M}}u_{\delta}({y})\ (2-\ ({x}-{y})\cdot\kappa_{{n}}({x}){n}({x}))\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},

(2.14)

\tilde{R}_{\delta}({x})=4\delta^{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y}-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\kappa_{{n}}({x})\ (({x}-{y})\cdot n({x}))^{2}\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},

(2.15)

\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f({x})=-2\delta^{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f({y})\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}.

(2.16)

Here the term
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}u_{\delta}
in (
2.14
) is constructed accordingly with
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{\delta}
in (
2.9
) to assure the elimination of cross terms in the weak formulation of model.
Consequently, we set up our nonlocal model by combining (
2.7
) and (
2.13
):

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{\delta}({x})=\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f({x}),&{x}\in\mathcal{M},\\
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}u_{\delta}({x})+\tilde{R}_{\delta}({x})v_{\delta}({x})=\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f({x}),&{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M}.\end{cases}

(2.17)

Our final destination is to show that (
2.17
) has a unique solution pair
(u_{\delta},v_{\delta})
and
\left\lVert u-u_{\delta}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathcal{M})}
where
u
solves (
2.1
).
Due to its complication in the coercivity analysis, we remain to solve such problem in our subsequent work. In the next section, we will concentrate on the control of the truncation error between (
2.17
) and (
2.1
).

3
Analysis of the Truncation Errors

In this section, we state the main theorem of our paper, which is crucial for our subsequential papers.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{4}(\mathcal{M})
be the solution of
(
2.1
)
, and

r_{in}({x})=\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u({x})-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\frac{\partial u}{\partial{{n}}}({x})-\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f({x}),\qquad{x}\in\mathcal{M},

(3.1)

r_{bd}({x})=\mathcal{D}_{\delta}u({x})+\tilde{R}_{\delta}({x})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{{n}}}({x})-\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}f({x}),\qquad{x}\in\partial\mathcal{M}.

(3.2)

then we can decompose
r_{in}
into
r_{in}=r_{it}+r_{bl}
, where
r_{it}
is supported in the whole domain
\mathcal{M}
, with the following bound

\frac{1}{\delta}\left\lVert r_{it}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert\nabla r_{it}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})};

(3.3)

and
r_{bl}
is supported in the layer adjacent to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
with width
2\delta
:

supp(r_{bl})\subset\{{x}\ \big|\ {x}\in\mathcal{M},\ dist({x},\partial\mathcal{M})\leq 2\delta\ \},

(3.4)

and satisfies the following two estimates

\frac{1}{\delta}\left\lVert r_{bl}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert\nabla r_{bl}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})};

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{M}}r_{bl}({x})\ f_{1}({x})d\mu_{x}\leq&C\delta^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})}(\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert\bar{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert\overset{=}{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}),\\
&\forall\ f_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{M});\end{split}

(3.6)

where the notations
\nabla=\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}
, and

\bar{f}_{1}({x})=\frac{1}{\bar{\omega}_{\delta}({x})}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({y})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},\qquad\overset{=}{f}_{1}({x})=\frac{1}{\overset{=}{\omega}_{\delta}({x})}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({x})\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}

(3.7)

are the weighted average of
f_{1}
in
{B}_{2\delta}({x})
with respect to
\bar{R}
and
\overset{=}{R}
, and

\bar{\omega}_{\delta}({x})=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},\ \overset{=}{\omega}_{\delta}({x})=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y},\qquad\forall\ {x}\in\mathcal{M}.

In addition, we have the following estimate for
r_{bd}
:

\left\lVert r_{bd}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})}.

(3.8)

The constant
C
in (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) depend only on
\mathcal{M}
.

In general, this theorem states that
r_{in}
is
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})

\begin{split}\!\!\!\!\!\bar{r}_{in}=&\int_{\mathcal{M}}f({y})\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}(u({x})-u({y}))\ \bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}+2\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y})\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}\\
&+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({y}))(-f({y})+\kappa_{{n}}({y})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y}))\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}.\end{split}

(3.40)

Our next goal is to control each term of
r_{bd}
.

1.

We start with the control of

. In fact, due to the smoothness of
\partial\mathcal{M}
, for

with

, the term
({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({x})
is
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})
. We then have the following bound for

:

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left\lVert r_{6}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\delta^{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(({x}-{y})\cdot{n}({y}))\ (f({y})-\kappa_{{n}}({y})\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y}))\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y})^{2}d\tau_{x}\\
&\leq C\delta^{4}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\delta^{2}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}u({y})|\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y})^{2}d\tau_{x}\\
&\leq C\delta^{8}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}u({y})|^{2}\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{{y}})\ (\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y})\ d\tau_{x}\\
&\leq C\delta^{6}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}u({y})|^{2}d\tau_{y}=C\delta^{6}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{6}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{3}(\mathcal{M})}.\end{split}

(3.41)

2.

For the next term
r_{7}
, we denote

r_{70}=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y})-\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({x}))\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\Delta_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y})){\overset{\equiv}{R}}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{{y}}.

(3.42)

Here the kernel function
\overset{\equiv}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})=C_{\delta}\overset{\equiv}{R}\big(\frac{|{x}-{y}|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\big)
, and
\overset{\equiv}{R}(r)=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\overset{=}{R}(s)ds
. We compare (
3.42
) with the approximation (
2.4
). Due to the fact that

is an

dimensional manifold with empty boundary, (

) is exactly (
2.4
) after replacing

by
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}
,
R
by
\overset{=}{R}
, and
\mathcal{M}
by
\partial\mathcal{M}
. Consequently,

is a lower order term compared to the other terms in (

). We then have

\begin{split}\left\lVert r_{7}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\leq&4\delta^{8}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\Delta_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial{n}}({y}){\overset{\equiv}{R}}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{{y}})^{2}d\tau_{{x}}+4\delta^{8}\left\lVert r_{70}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\\
\leq&C\delta^{8}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{3}u({y})|{\overset{\equiv}{R}}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{{y}})^{2}d\tau_{{x}}\\
\leq&C\delta^{8}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{3}u({y})|^{2}\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{{y}})\ (\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\ \overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\tau_{y})\ d\tau_{x}\\
\leq&C\delta^{6}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}^{3}u({y})|^{2}d\tau_{y}=C\delta^{6}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{3}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{6}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})}.\end{split}

(3.43)

Next, for the term

\begin{split}|r_{8}|\leq&C\delta\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Big|u({y})-u({x})-({y}-{x})\cdot\nabla u({x})\Big|\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y}\\

&+C\delta^{3}|\nabla^{2}u({x})|.\end{split}

(3.44)

For the second term of (
3.44
), it is clear that its boundary

. What left is the control of the first term of (

We compare

\begin{split}\left\lVert\bar{r}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{3}\ \delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})}=C\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})},\end{split}

\left\lVert r_{8}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\leq C\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})}.

\leq&\ C(\left\lVert r_{it}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert r_{bl}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})})\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{4}(\mathcal{M})},\end{split}

our previous calculation indicates that the same bound holds for

(3.51)

&-2\delta^{2}\int_{{{\Omega}_{J({y})}}}f_{1}({x})\partial_{l}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})\ d\bm{\beta}+r_{53}\\

(4.26)

Here

is the matrix defined in (

) such that

by a calculation on the Jacobian matrix, and
{n}_{\Omega}(\bm{\beta})
is the unit outward normal vector of
\Omega_{J({y})}
at
\bm{\beta}\in\partial\Omega_{J({y})}

For the boundary term of (

\begin{split}&-2\delta^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{1}({x})\xi^{j^{\prime}}\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})n_{\Omega}^{l}(\bm{\beta})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\ dS_{\bm{\beta}}\\

is
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})
.
For the term

\begin{split}r_{57}=&4\delta^{4}\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{1}({x})\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})n_{\Omega}^{l}(\bm{\beta})g^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}({x})\partial_{\partial\Omega}^{\bm{\beta}}\overset{=}{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\ dS_{\bm{\beta}}\\

(4.28)

which indicates that
r_{57}
is
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})
as well by continuity.

Now let us return to (
4.26
). For the interior term of (
4.26

and integrate over

to discover

\begin{split}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}&g^{j}_{1}({y})\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({x})\partial_{l}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{x}d\tau_{y}\\

\leq&C\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}\left\lVert\bar{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+r_{55}\end{split}

(4.29)

Here the lower order terms

\begin{split}r_{51}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({x})g^{j}_{1}({y})\xi^{i^{\prime}}\xi^{j^{\prime}}\xi^{k^{\prime}}(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}\tau\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\partial_{k^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha}+\tau st\bm{\xi})dtd\tau ds)\\
{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{x}d\tau_{y},\end{split}

(4.30)

\begin{split}r_{54}=2\delta^{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{l}\big(f_{1}({x})\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\big)\xi^{j^{\prime}}\bar{R}_{\delta}({x},{y})\ d\bm{\beta},\end{split}

is obviously

\begin{split}r_{53}=-2\delta^{2}\int_{\Omega}(f_{1}({x})\xi^{j^{\prime}}\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha}))r_{52}^{l}({x},{y})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\ d\bm{\beta},\end{split}

(4.32)

\begin{split}r_{58}=-2\delta^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{1}({x})\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha})g^{i^{\prime}l}({x})n_{\Omega}^{l}(\bm{\beta})g^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}({x})r_{52}^{l^{\prime}}({x},{y})\ \sqrt{det\ G(\bm{\beta})}\ dS_{\bm{\beta}},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({x})g^{j}_{1}({y})\frac{1}{2}\xi^{i^{\prime}}\xi^{j^{\prime}}\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha}){R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{x}d\tau_{y}\\

and finally

\begin{split}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}&f_{1}({x})\textbf{g}_{1}({y})\cdot({x}-{y}-\bm{\eta}({x},{y}))R_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{x}d\tau_{y}\\
=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}&\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{1}({x})g^{j}_{1}({y})\frac{1}{2}\xi^{i^{\prime}}\xi^{j^{\prime}}\partial_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j}(\bm{\alpha}){R}_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{x}d\tau_{y}+r_{51}\\
\leq C\delta^{2}&(\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}(\left\lVert\bar{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\lVert{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{M})}).\end{split}

(4.36)

then

Using Newton-Leibniz formula, we can discover

\begin{split}d({x},{y})=&u({x})-u({y})-({x}-{y})\cdot\nabla u({y})-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{i}\eta^{j}(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}u({y}))\\

&\phi^{j^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha+s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}\xi)\nabla^{j^{\prime\prime}}\nabla^{j^{\prime}}\nabla^{j}u(\bm{\phi}(\alpha+s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}\xi))ds_{3}ds_{2}ds_{1}\\

(4.39)

where

(4.40)

with the lower order terms

\begin{split}d_{11}=&\xi^{i}\xi^{i^{\prime}}\xi^{i^{\prime\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s_{1}^{2}s_{2}\frac{d}{ds_{4}}(\partial_{i}\phi^{j}(\alpha+s_{4}s_{3}s_{1}\xi)\partial_{i^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j^{\prime}}(\alpha+s_{4}s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}\xi)\\

\begin{split}d_{13}=&\xi^{i}\xi^{i^{\prime}}\xi^{i^{\prime\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s_{1}^{2}s_{2}\frac{d}{ds_{4}}\partial_{i}\phi^{j}(\alpha+s_{4}s_{3}s_{1}\xi)\partial_{i^{\prime}}\phi^{j^{\prime}}(\alpha+s_{4}s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}\xi)\\

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\mathcal{M}}d_{1i}({x},{y})R_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{y})^{2}d\mu_{x}\leq C\int_{\mathcal{M}}(\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{2}_{1i}({x},{y})R_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{{y}})(\int_{\mathcal{M}}R_{\delta}({x},{y})d\mu_{{y}})d\mu_{x}\\

Back to (

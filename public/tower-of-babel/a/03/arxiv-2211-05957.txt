Linking numbers of modular knots
Christopher-Lloyd Simon

Abstract
. The modular group PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) acts on the hyperbolic plane ℍ ​ ℙ \mathbb{H}\mathbb{P} with quotient the modular surface 𝕄 \mathbb{M} , whose unit tangent bundle 𝕌 \mathbb{U} is a 3 3 -manifold homeomorphic to the complement of the trefoil knot in the sphere 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} . The hyperbolic conjugacy classes of PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) correspond to the closed oriented geodesics in 𝕄 \mathbb{M} . Those lift to the periodic orbits for the geodesic flow in 𝕌 \mathbb{U} , which define the modular knots. The linking numbers between modular knots and the trefoil are well understood. Indeed, É. Ghys showed in 2006 that they are given by the Rademacher invariant of the corresponding conjugacy classes. The Rademacher function Rad : PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) → ℤ \Rad\colon\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z} is a homogeneous quasi-morphism which he recognised with J. Barge in 1992 as half the primitive of the bounded Euler class. We are concerned with the linking numbers between modular knots and derive several formulae with arithmetical, combinatorial, topological and group theoretical flavours. In particular we associate to a pair of modular knots a function defined on the character variety of PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , whose limit at the boundary point recovers their linking number. Moreover, we show that the linking number with a modular knot minus that with its inverse yields a homogeneous quasi-morphism on the modular group, and how to extract a free basis out of these.

Introduction

Context and motivation

The modular group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts properly discontinuously on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
with quotient the modular orbifold
\mathbb{M}
, a hyperbolic surface with conical singularities
i
and
j
of order
2
and
3
, and a cusp
\infty
.
The free homotopy classes of loops in
\mathbb{M}
correspond to the conjugacy classes of its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{M})=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
In particular the hyperbolic conjugacy classes of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
correspond to the closed oriented geodesics in
\mathbb{M}
, called
modular geodesics
.
For hyperbolic
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
the modular geodesic
\alpha
has length
\lambda_{A}
equal to the logarithm of the ratio between its eigenvalues
\epsilon_{A}^{\pm 1}
, in formula:

\disc(A)=\left(\epsilon_{A}-\epsilon_{A}^{-1}\right)^{2}=(\Tr A)^{2}-4=\left(2\sinh\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{A}\right)^{2}

We denote by
I(A,B)
the geometric intersection number between the associated modular geodesics.

The unit tangent bundle
\mathbb{U}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
of
\mathbb{M}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
is a
3
-manifold, and the closed oriented geodesics in
\mathbb{M}
lift to the periodic orbits for the geodesic flow in
\mathbb{U}
.
Hence the primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
correspond to the so called
modular knots
in
\mathbb{U}
, which form the components of the
master modular link
.
The structure of the Seifert fibration
\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{M}
reveals that
\mathbb{U}
is homeomorphic to the complement of a trefoil knot in the sphere. In particular, one may speak of the linking numbers between modular knots and the trefoil and between one another.

Let us recall a combinatorial parametrization of the infinite order conjugacy classes in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The Euclidean algorithm shows that the group
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by the transvections

L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

and more precisely that its submonoid
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
of matrices with non-negative entries is freely generated by
\{L,R\}
.
This submonoid can be identified with its image
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})\subset\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
In
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the conjugacy class of an infinite order element intersects
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
along all cyclic permutations of a non-empty
\{L,R\}
-word.
The conjugacy class is primitive if and only if the cyclic word is primitive, and it is hyperbolic when the cyclic word contains both letters
L
and
R
.

One may try to relate the geometry and topology of the master modular link with the arithmetics and combinatorics of conjugacy classes in the modular group.
Our previous work
[
Sim23
]
relates the geometry of modular geodesics (angles of intersection and lengths of ortho-geodesics) in terms of the arithmetics of conjugacy classes in the modular group (discriminants, cross-ratios between fixed points on the projective line, and their Hilbert symbols).
The main results in this paper will relate the linking numbers of modular knots to the combinatorics of the corresponding cyclic words.

The most immediate measures for the complexity of a binary word are given by the sum and difference between the numbers of letters of each sort.
For an infinite order
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we denote by
\len([A])=\#R+\#L
the combinatorial length and call
\Rad([A])=\#R-\#L
the
Rademacher number
of its conjugacy class.
In his paper
[
Ati87
]
on the Logarithm of the Dedekind eta function, M. Atiyah identified the Rademacher function with no less than six other important functions appearing in diverse areas of mathematics, showing how omnipresent it is.

The function
\Rad\colon\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}
is a quasi-morphism, meaning that it has a bounded derivative

d\Rad\colon\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\times\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}\qquad d\Rad(A,B)=\Rad(B)-\Rad(AB)+\Rad(A)

and is homogeneous, meaning that
\Rad(A^{n})=n\Rad(A)
for infinite order
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This enabled É. Ghys and J. Barge to recognise it in
[
BG92
]
as half the primitive of the bounded Euler class in
H^{2}_{b}(\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z});\mathbb{R})
and explain its ubiquity.
In
[
Ghy07
]
, É. Ghys showed that the linking number of a modular knot with the trefoil equals its Rademacher invariant, and concluded by asking for
arithmetical and combinatorial interpretations of the linking pairing between modular knots
.

In this work, we will derive several formulae for those linking numbers, providing bridges between the arithmetics and geometry, the combinatorics and algebra, or the dynamics and topology of the modular group.

The arithmetic and geometry of the cosines

The journey from arithmetics began with our previous work
[
Sim23
]
. In particular for a field
\mathbb{K}\supset\mathbb{Q}
, we described when two hyperbolic elements
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of the same discriminant
\Delta
are conjugate in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
The obstruction is measured in terms of any intersection angle
\theta
between their modular geodesics
[\alpha],[\beta]
by the class of
\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
modulo the group of norms over
\mathbb{K}
of the extension
\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]
.
When this obstruction vanishes, the elements of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
which conjugate
A
to
B
are paremetrized by the points
(X,Y)\in\mathbb{K}
of the generalised Pell-Fermat conic with equation
X^{2}-\Delta Y^{2}=\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}
.
Hence the geometric quantities
\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}=\tfrac{1+\cos(\theta)}{2}
given for some representatives
A,B\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
whose axes intersect in
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
by

\cos(\theta)=\sign(\Tr(A)\Tr(B))\tfrac{\Tr(AB)-\Tr(AB^{-1})}{\sqrt{\disc A\disc B}}

have an arithmetic meaning, and they will reappear under various forms in the sequel.

Linking functions on the character variety

Let us introduce, for any pair of modular geodesics
[\alpha],[\beta]\subset\mathbb{M}
, the following summations over their oriented intersection angles
\theta\in\,]-\pi,\pi[
:

\Link_{1}(A,B)=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\Cos_{1}(A,B)=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\left(\cos\theta\right)

and study their variations as we deform the metric on
\mathbb{M}
by opening the cusp.

The complete hyperbolic metrics on the orbifold
\mathbb{M}
correspond to the faithful and discrete representations
\rho\colon\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
up to conjugacy.
They form a
1
-dimensional real algebraic set parametrized by
q\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, deforming
L
and
R
according to:

L_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}q&0\\
1&q^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad R_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}q&1\\
0&q^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

The primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
still index the hyperbolic geodesics in the quotient
\mathbb{M}_{q}=\rho_{q}(\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\backslash\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
which do not surround the cusp. The intersection points between
q
-modular geodesics
[\alpha_{q}],[\beta_{q}]\subset\mathbb{M}_{q}
persist by deformation, so we may define the analogous sums
\Link_{q}(A,B)
and
\Cos_{q}(A,B)
over their intersection angles
\theta_{q}\in\,]-\pi,\pi[
of the
\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\cos\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{q}\right)^{2}
and
\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\cos\theta_{q}\right)
.

As
q\to\infty
, the hyperbolic orbifold
\mathbb{M}_{q}
has a convex core which retracts onto a thin neighbourhood of the long geodesic arc connecting its conical singularities, whose preimage in the universal cover
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
is a trivalent tree. In the limit we recover the action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on its Bruhat-Tits building, the infinite planar trivalent tree
\mathcal{T}
, and by studying its combinatorics we shall prove the following.

Theorem 0.1

(Linking and intersection from boundary evaluations)
.

For primitive hyperbolic
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the limits of the functions
\Link_{q}(A,B)
and
\Cos_{q}(A,B)
at the boundary point of the
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-character variety of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
recover their linking and intersection numbers:

\displaystyle\Link_{q}(A,B)\xrightarrow[q\to\infty]{}\lk(A,B)

\displaystyle\Cos_{q}(A,B)\xrightarrow[q\to\infty]{}\lk(A,B)-\lk(A^{-1},B)=2\lk(A,B)-\tfrac{1}{2}I(A,B)

Hence the functions
\Link_{q}
and
\Cos_{q}
interpolate between the geometry at
q=1
of the arithmetic group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and the topology at
q=\infty
of the combinatorial action
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\Aut(\mathcal{T})
.

Linking functions and Alexander polynomials

The graphs of
q\in\mathbb{C}\mapsto\Link_{q}(A,B)\in\mathbb{C}
for various pairs
A,B
suggest that their zeros tend to accumulate on the unit circle. This reminds us of the various results and conjectures concerning the roots of Alexander polynomials, so we propose a possible thread to follow in this direction.

A primitive hyperbolic conjugacy class in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{M})
corresponds to a primitive modular geodesic in
\alpha\subset\mathbb{M}
. It lifts to a modular knot in
\vec{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{U}
which in turns yields a conjugacy class in
\mathcal{B}_{3}=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{U})
.
A conjugacy class in the braid group on three strands defines, by taking its closure, a link
\sigma_{A}
in a solid torus.
In
[
Sim22
, Proposition 5.16]
we relate the Alexander polynomial
\Delta(\sigma_{A})\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
of this link
\sigma_{A}
to the Fricke polynomial
\Tr A_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
of the modular geodesic
[\alpha]
.

Proposition 0.2

(Alexander, Fricke and Rademacher)
.

For a primitive hyperbolic
A\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, the Alexander polynomial of the link
\sigma_{A}
is given in terms of
q=\sqrt{-t}
by:

\Delta(\sigma_{A})=\tfrac{q^{\Rad(A)}-\Tr(A_{q})+q^{-\Rad(A)}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}

Now recall that
\Cos_{q}(A,B)=\Link_{q}(A,B)-\Link_{q}(A,B^{-1})
can be expressed as finite sum of terms:

\cos(A_{q},B_{q})=\sign(\Tr(A_{q})\Tr(B_{q}))\tfrac{\Tr(A_{q}B_{q})-\Tr(A_{q}B_{q}^{-1})}{\sqrt{\disc A_{q}\disc B_{q}}}\qquad\mathrm{where}\qquad\disc(C_{q})=(\Tr C_{q})^{2}-4

This is how one may compare the concentration property for the zeros of
\Link_{q}
around the unit circle with those of Alexander polynomials, but we will not pursue this direction any further.

Linking numbers and homogeneous quasi-morphisms

The limiting values
\Cos_{q}(A,B)\to\lk(A,B)-\lk(A^{-1},B)
will now provide a bridge from the representation theory to the bounded cohomology of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
For every group
\Pi
, the real vector space
PX(\Pi)
of homogeneous quasi-morphisms is a Banach space for the norm
\lVert df\rVert_{\infty}
, as was shown in
[
MM85
,
Iva88
]
.

Theorem 0.3
.

For every hyperbolic
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the function
\Cos_{A}\colon B\mapsto\lk(A,B)-\lk(A^{-1},B)
is a homogeneous quasi-morphism
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of primitive infinite order conjugacy classes in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
the subset of those which are stable under inversion. Choose a partition
\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{-}\sqcup\mathcal{P}_{+}
in two subsets in bijection by the inversion.
We may choose
R\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
, and denote
\Cos_{R}:=\Rad
by convention.

Theorem 0.4
.

The collection of
\Cos_{A}\in PX(\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
for
A\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
is linearly independent and every element
f\in PX(\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
can be written as
f=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}_{+}}c_{A}(f).\Cos_{A}
for unique
c_{A}(f)\in\mathbb{R}
.

To prove the non-triviality and linear independence of the
\Cos_{A}
for
A\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
, we were led to show the non-degeneracy of the linking form, which is interesting in its own right.

Theorem 0.5
.

If hyperbolic
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
are link equivalent, namely
\lk(A,X)=\lk(B,X)
for all hyperbolic
X\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
then they are conjugate.

The results in this section may be compared to the classical representation theory of compact groups, in which the characters of irreducible representations provide an orthonormal basis for the class functions.
Indeed, we have found a family of cosign functions
\Cos_{A}
whose periods correspond to the primitive conjugacy classes of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and they form a basis for the space of quasi-characters
PX(\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
, which is in some sense orthogonal with respect to the linking form (but we refer to the proofs in section
7
for a better explanation of this orthogonality).

1.
The group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
: discriminant and cross-ratio

The group
\PSL_{2}
acts by conjugacy over itself.
In this section, we recall from
[
Sim23
]
the main invariants which enable to describe the orbits of single elements and of pairs of elements.
Those are the discriminant
\disc(A)
and the cross-ratio
\bir(A,B)
.

1.1.
Over a field of characteristic
\neq 2

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
The automorphism group
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
of the projective line
\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}
acts freely transitively on triples of distinct points, and the unique algebraic invariant of four points
u,v,x,y\in\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the cross-ratio:

(1)

\bir(u,v,x,y)=\frac{(v-u)}{(v-x)}\div\frac{(y-u)}{(y-x)}\in\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}

It satisfies in particular
\bir(z,0,1,\infty)=z
and
\bir(z,0,w,\infty)=z/w
, whence the cocycle rule:

\bir(z,v,x,y)=\bir(z,v,w,y)\bir(w,v,x,y).

For a triple
(u,v,w)
of points in
\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we define their Maslov index in
\{0\}\sqcup\mathbb{K}^{\times}/(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}
by lifting them to vectors
(\vec{u},\vec{v},\vec{w})
in
\mathbb{K}^{2}
which are not all zero but with zero sum, and taking the determinant
\det(\vec{u},\vec{v})=\det(\vec{v},\vec{w})=\det(\vec{w},\vec{u})
.
It is preserved by the subgroup
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
, which acts freely transitively on the triples of distinct points with a given Maslov index.

A non-trivial
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
has two fixed points
\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}
in the projective line over
\sqrt{\mathbb{K}}
, that are well defined up to permutation.
The element
\epsilon_{A}^{\pm 2}:=\bir(Ax,\alpha_{\mp},x,\alpha_{\pm})
does not depend on
x\in\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}\}
, and is well defined up to inversion: it is called the
period
of
A
.
The unique algebraic function on
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
which is invariant by conjugacy is the discriminant:

\disc(A)=\left(\epsilon_{A}-\epsilon_{A}^{-1}\right)^{2}

whose class in
\{0\}\sqcup\mathbb{K}^{\times}/(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}
defines the
type
of
A
.
All non-trivial elements with
\disc=0
are conjugate. The elements with
\disc\neq 0
are called
semi-simple
.
The conjugacy classes of elements with
\disc\equiv 1\bmod{(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}}
are uniquely characterised by the value of their discriminant.

Consider
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
of the same type, and fix a square root of
\disc(A)\disc(B)\in(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}
.
Then one may order their fixed points
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})
and
(\beta_{-},\beta_{+})
up to simultaneous inversion, and consistently define their cross-ratio
\bir(A,B)
by:

\bir(A,B):=\bir(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\beta_{+},\beta_{-})\in\mathbb{K}\mathbb{P}^{1}

which is
\notin\{0,1,\infty\}
unless
A
and
B
share a fixed point, and satisfies the symmetry property:

\frac{1}{\bir(A,B)}+\frac{1}{\bir(A,B^{-1})}=1.

We may also define their cosine (using their adjoint action on the Lie algebra
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
as in
[
Sim23
]
), which is related to the cross-ratio by:

(2)

\cos(A,B)=\frac{1}{\bir(A,B)}-\frac{1}{\bir(A,B^{-1})}.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Sim23
]
)
.

Two semi-simple elements
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
are conjugate if and only if
\disc(A)=\Delta=\disc(A)
and
\bir(A,B)\equiv 1\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]}
.

More precisely, if
\Delta\neq 0
and
\bir(A,B)\notin\{1,\infty\}
, then we may lift
A,B
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
so that
\mathfrak{a}=A-\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(A),\mathfrak{b}=B-\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(A)
satisfy
\Tr(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})=-\Delta\cos(A,B)
, and the elements
C\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
such that
CAC^{-1}=B
are parametrized by the
\mathbb{K}
-points of the Pell-Fermat conic:

(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K}\>\colon\>\quad(2x)^{2}+\Delta y^{2}=\bir(A,B)\quad\text{according to}\quad C(x,y)=x(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})+y(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}).

1.2.
Over the real field

The automorphism group
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the complex projective line
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
contains
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as the stabiliser of the real projective line
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The index-two subgroup
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
also preserves the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or equivalently the orientation induced on its boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
, also given by the cyclic order
\cord(x,y,z)\in\{\pm 1\}
of any triple of distinct points
x,y,z
of
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
(which equals their Maslov index).

The complex structure on
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
is conformal to a unique hyperbolic metric. The hyperbolic distance
\lambda
between
w,z\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
can be deduced from the cross-ratio by
\bir(\bar{z},z,\bar{w},w)^{-1}=\left(\cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}
.
This realises
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as the positive isometry group of the hyperbolic plane: it preserves the previous cross-ratio and acts simply-transitively on positive triples of distinct points in
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, thus it preserves the hyperbolic metric and acts simply transitively on the unit tangent bundle of
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.

The type of
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is elliptic or parabolic or hyperbolic according to the value of
\sign\disc(A)\in\{-1,0,1\}
, equal to the number of distinct fixed points in
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
minus
1
.

A hyperbolic
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
by translation along an (oriented) geodesic
\alpha
whose endpoints
\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
are its repulsive and attractive fixed points.
With this order the period satisfies
\epsilon_{A}^{2}>1
.
The translation length
\lambda_{A}=\log(\epsilon_{A}^{2})
yields
\disc(A)=\left(2\sinh\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{A}\right)^{2}
.

2.
The modular group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

2.1.
The modular orbifold

The subgroup
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})

4.
The unit tangent bundle to the modular orbifold

4.1.
Modular knots and links

The Lie group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
identifies with the unit tangent bundle of the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.
Its lattice
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the left with quotient
\mathbb{U}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{M}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.
The fundamental group of
\mathbb{U}
is the preimage of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
in the universal cover of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

\mathfrak{1}\to\mathbb{Z}\to\widetilde{\PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathfrak{1}

[

DP18
]

\mathbb{U}

The closed hyperbolic geodesics in
\mathbb{M}

\mathbb{U}

modular knots
.
Together, they form the

whose components are indexed by the primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes of the modular group.
We wish to relate the geometry and topology of the master modular link with the arithmetic and combinatorial properties of the modular group.

5.1.
Invariants on pairs of conjugacy classes

The action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on

and

enabled us to define conjugacy invariants for pairs
(A,B)
by comparing their stable subsets in
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
or
\mathcal{T}
.
We now explain how to average those in order to obtain functions of pairs of conjugacy classes.

Summing over double cosets

Consider a group
\Pi
acting on a space
\Sigma
and a function
f
defined on
\Sigma\times\Sigma
with values in a commutative group
\Lambda
which is invariant under the diagonal action of
\Pi
:

f\colon\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\Lambda\qquad\forall W\in\Pi,\;\forall a,b\in\Sigma\>\colon\>f(a,b)=f(W\cdot a,W\cdot b).

We define an invariant
F
for pairs of
\Pi
-orbits
[a],[b]
by summing
f
over all pairs of representatives of the orbits considered modulo the diagonal action of
\Pi
.
The pairs of representatives for the orbits are parametrized by the
(U\cdot a,V\cdot b)
for
(U,V)\in\Pi/(\Stab a)\times\Pi/(\Stab b)
, and the quotient of this set by the diagonal action of
\Pi
by left translations is denoted
\Pi/(\Stab a)\times_{\Pi}\Pi/(\Stab b)
.
Consequently, the sum indexed by
(U,V)\in\left(\Pi/\Stab a\right)\times_{\Pi}\left(\Pi/\Stab b\right)
defines our desired invariant:

F([a],[b])=\sum f(U\cdot A,V\cdot b).

This can also be written as the sum over double cosets
W\in(\Stab a)\backslash\Pi/(\Stab b)
:

F([a],[b])=\sum f(a,W\cdot b)

because the map
(\Pi/\Stab a)\times(\Pi/\Stab b)\to(\Stab a)\backslash\Pi/(\Stab b)
sending
(U,V)
to
W=U^{-1}V
is surjective, and its fibers are the orbits under the diagonal action of
\Pi
by left translations.

We will apply this discussion to the action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on itself by conjugacy to obtain invariants for pairs of primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes.
Note that in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the centraliser of a hyperbolic
A
is the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by its primitive root, namely the unique primitive element with a positive power equal to
A
.
Our functions
f(a,b)
will be expressed in terms of geometrical invariants such as
\bir(A,B)
or
\cos(A,B)
, as well as combinatorial invariants such as
\cross(A,B)
and
\cosign(A,B)
.
To ensure that the sum is well defined, it must have finite support or converge in a completion of
\Lambda
for an appropriate norm, and that depends on the behaviour of
f
.

Summing over
\{L,R\}
-words

Consider a function
f
over the pairs of coprime primitive hyperbolic
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, which is invariant under the diagonal action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on itself by left conjugacy.

In order to compute the sum defining
F([A],[B])
, we may group the terms
f(UAU^{-1},VBV^{-1})
according to the
\cosign(UAU^{-1},VBV^{-1})\in\{-1,0,1\}
to obtain:

F=F_{-}+F_{0}+F_{+}

The sum
F_{+}
has finite support, contained in the set of pairs of Euclidean representatives for the conjugacy classes of
A,B
.
Similarly the sum
F_{-}
has finite support, which we may also index by those pairs of Euclidean representatives using the fact that
\cosign(A,B)=-\cosign(A,SBS^{-1})
. Thus for

7.1.
Combinatorial formula: sum of linked patterns

We now derive a combinatorial formula for the linking numbers
[
Sim22
, Proposition 4.34]
arising from a different count of the crossings in the Lorenz template. It follows from the algorithmic formula, but we propose a visual proof.

The monoid
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
freely generated by
\{L,R\}
has the lexicographic order extending
L<R
.
The monoid
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})\setminus\{\mathfrak{1}\}
maps to the set
\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{N}}
of infinite binary sequences by sending a finite word
A
to its periodisation
A^{\infty}
. This map is increasing and injective in restriction to primitive words.
We denote by
\sigma
the Bernoulli shift on
\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{N}}
which removes the first letter, as well as the cyclic shift on
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})\setminus\{\mathfrak{1}\}
which moves the first letter at the end.
These shifts are intertwined by the periodisation map:
(\sigma^{j}A)^{\infty}=\sigma^{j}(A^{\infty})
.
For a pattern
P\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
and an infinite order
A\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, let
\pref_{P}(A^{\infty})=\left[\!\left[{A^{\infty}\in P\cdot\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})}\right]\!\right]\in\{0,1\}
tell whether
P
is a prefix of
A^{\infty}
, and
\occ_{P}(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{\len A}\pref_{P}\left(\sigma^{j}A^{\infty}\right)
count the number of cyclic occurrences of
P
in
A\bmod{\sigma}
.

Recall that
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
are coprime when their positive powers are never conjugate.
Thus
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
are not coprime when they admit cyclic permutations generating submonoids with non-trivial intersection, in other terms if
A^{\infty}=B^{\infty}\bmod{\sigma}
.

Proposition 7.1

(Combinatorial formula: sum of linked patterns)
.

For coprime hyperbolic
A,B\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
the corresponding modular knots have linking number:

(SLP)

\lk(A,B)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{w}\begin{pmatrix}\occ_{RwL}(A)\cdot\occ_{LwR}(B)\\
+\\
\occ_{LwR}(A)\cdot\occ_{RwL}(B)\end{pmatrix}

where the summation extends over all words
w\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
including the empty one.

Visual proof
[
Sim22
, Figure 4.9]
.

Split the Lorenz template by extending the dividing line backwards in time, and observe the crossings appearing in its standard planar projection: they occur in regions arranged according to a binary tree indexed by pairs of words of the form
(RwL,LwR)
.
∎

.

For

we have

if and only if

, which is equivalent to the non-coprimality of

and

.
Hence the coprimality assumption on

and

If

Theorem 7.4

(Link equivalence)

If hyperbolic

\lk(A,C)=\lk(B,C)

Proof.

The set
\mathcal{Z}=R\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})L\sqcup L\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})R

exchanging the extreme letters, having no fixed points.
The free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Omega
generated by the set

is naturally decomposed as the direct sum of rank-

It is therefore endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{Z}
, given by the direct sum of the hyperbolic structures on those planes:

\Omega=\bigoplus_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot z=\bigoplus_{z>\bar{z}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot z\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cdot\bar{z}\qquad(a\cdot b)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}a_{z}b_{\bar{z}}=\sum_{z>\bar{z}}(a_{z}b_{\bar{z}}+a_{\bar{z}}b_{z})

The length function

by the chain of subsets

, which is invariant by the involution.
This induces a filtration of the module
\Omega
by the corresponding chain of sub-modules

with ranks

, all invariant under the orthogonal symmetry.
Thus each unimodular quadratic
\mathbb{Z}
-module

(decomposed as a direct sum of hyperbolic planes) is canonically isomorphic to its dual

.

Now consider cyclic words

corresponding to link equivalent hyperbolic conjugacy classes in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let

and

In particular, for

∎

7.2.

For a group
\Pi
, a function
f\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{R}
is called a

if it has a bounded derivative:

df(A,B)=f(B)-f(AB)+f(A).

A quasi-morphism
f\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{R}
is called
homogeneous
if it is a morphism in restriction to the abelian subgroups of
\Pi
(which for
\Pi=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
means that
f(A^{n})=nf(A)
for all
A\in\Pi
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
).
Observe that a homogeneous quasi-morphism is bounded only if it is trivial, necessarily constant on conjugacy classes, and vanishes on torsion classes.
The real vector space
PX(\Pi)
of homogeneous quasi-morphisms is a Banach space for the norm
\lVert df\rVert_{\infty}
, as was shown in
[
MM85
,
Iva88
]
.

For a pattern
P\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
we define the
P

by

\mas_{P}(A)=\occ_{P}(A)-\occ_{{}^{t}\!P}(A)

Notice that
\mas_{P}(A)=\occ_{P}(A)-\occ_{P}({}^{t}\!A)
and that
{}^{t}\!A
is conjugate to
A^{-1}
by

.
Extending
\mas_{P}(A)=0
for elliptic
A
yields a conjugacy invariant function
\mas_{P}\colon\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular for
P=R
we recover the

as
\mas_{P}(A)=\Rad(A)
.

Further directions of research

8.1.
Linking forms of Fuchsian groups

To begin with, we may compare the definitions of the functions
\Link_{q}
and
\Cos_{q}
and their limiting behaviour at
q=\infty
with similar considerations which have been made for non-oriented loops in a closed surface
S
of genus
g\geq 2
.
Such loops, corresponding to the conjugacy classes of
A,B\in\pi_{1}(S)
up to inversion, define trace functions
\Tr(A),\Tr(B)
on the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety of
\pi_{1}(S)
, whose real locus contains the Teichmüller space of
S
as a Zariski-dense open set.
This character variety carries a natural symplectic structure
[
Gol84
]
, given by the Weil-Petersson symplectic form.

The sum
\Cos_{q}(A,B)
looks very much like Wolpert’s cosine formula
[
Wol82
,
Wol81
]
computing the Poisson bracket
\{\Tr(A),\Tr(B)\}
of the trace functions.
In fact, Wolpert sums
\cross(A,B)\cos(A,B)
over the intersection points
p\in[\alpha]\cap[\beta]
, that is the cosines of the angles turning from
\alpha
to
\beta
in the direction prescribed by the orientation of the surface.
Hence while our cosine formula is a symmetric formula of oriented geodesics, Wolpert’s cosine formula yields a skew-symmetric function of non-oriented geodesics.
Note however that the Teichmüller space of
\mathbb{M}
is reduced to a point so any Poisson structure in the usual sense would be trivial, so in our setting we expect Wolpert’s sum to be identically zero (as corroborated by our computer experimentation).

Moreover, the Weil-Petersson symplectic form has been extended to several compactifications of the character variety
[
PP91
,
SB01
,
MS
]
.
The limits of the Poisson bracket
\{\Tr(A),\Tr(B)\}
at the respective boundary points have been interpreted in
[
Bon92
, Proposition 6]
and
[
MS
]
.
Thus, we may generalise the definitions of our functions

L
q

and

C
q

to oriented geodesics in hyperbolic surfaces and ask for interpretations of their limits at boundary points of the Teichmüller space.
We believe that

L
q

and

C
q

extend by continuity to pairs
A,B
of oriented geodesic currents.
This should be analogous to the extension of the intersection form described in Bonahon
[
Bon88
]
.

Pursuing this direction, one may also wish to replace
\rho
with a representation
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, a metric of negative curvature, or a generalised cross-ratio
[
Ota92
,
LM09
]
.
The aim would be to think of

L
q

and

C
q

as differential forms on the "tangent bundle" to these spaces of representations, metrics or cross-ratios, considered up to appropriate equivalence relations.
Indeed, for any group
\Pi
, the semi-conjugacy classes of representations
\Pi\to\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
correspond
[
Ghy87
]
to the "integral points of the unit ball" in the second bounded cohomology group
H^{2}_{b}(\Pi;\mathbb{R})
, namely the elements represented by bounded
2
-cocycles with values in
\{-1,0,1\}
. For
\Pi=\pi_{1}(S)
it contains
[
BG88
]
the space of differential
2
-forms on
S
, and we suspect that something similar is true for some spaces of generalized cross-ratios, thus we ask:

Question 8.1

How to interpret
\Link_{\rho}(A,B)
or
\Cos_{\rho}(A,B)

8.3.
Special values of Poincaré Series

We may apply the general averaging procedure explained in paragraph

on the character variety of

Their limit at the boundary point

as soon as

converges to an expression of

Various motivations (including special values for Poincaré series
[

,

]

[

]

and

B_{q}

In formula:

So the sum over all double cosets splits as a finite sum computable as explained in

(the topological entropy for the action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the hyperbolic plane).
The infinite sum is a bivariate analog (in

[

]
.
The earliest appearance we found for bivariate series is in
[

, and the only other in
[
Pau13
]
.
When

and

, the real part of the finite sum evaluates to

, but one may wonder about the infinite series (now the order in which we take limits in
s
and
q
may import).

More generally, one strategy to relate modular topology and quadratic arithmetic is to choose
f
with appropriate symmetries and analyticity properties so that the sum over all double cosets can be understood: then one deduces a relationship between a topologically meaningful finite sum, and the infinite series whose special values may be of interest in arithmetic.

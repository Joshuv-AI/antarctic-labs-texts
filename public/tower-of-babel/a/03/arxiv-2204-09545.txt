Abstract
We study singular limits of stochastic evolution equations in the interplay of disappearing strength of the noise and insufficient regularity, where the equation in the limit with noise would not be defined due to lack of regularity. We recover previously known results on vanishing small noise with increasing roughness, but our main focus is to study for fixed noise the singular limit where the leading order differential operator in the equation may vanish. Although the noise is disappearing in the limit, additional deterministic terms appear due to renormalization effects. We separate the analysis of the equation from the convergence of stochastic terms and give a general framework for the main error estimates. This first reduces the result to bounds on a residual and in a second step to various bounds on the stochastic convolution. Moreover, as examples we apply our result to the a singularly regularized Allen-Cahn equation with a vanishing Bilaplacian, and the Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy with space-time white noise in two spatial dimensions.



Keywords:
Stochastic singular limit; stochastic partial differential equation; stochastic Allen-Cahn equation; stochastic Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy; space-time white noise; renormalization.

35K91; 60F05; 60H15; 60H17.

1
Introduction

Various singular limits
for stochastic evolution equations appear in many different examples as in the sharp interface limit for Allen-Cahn (AC) or Cahn-Hilliard (CH) with small noise
[
1
,
20
]
or in the approximation and existence theory of singular stochastic partial differential equations (singular SPDEs)
[
25
,
24
,
14
]
.
Other variants of stochastic singular limits are considered for instance in the study of stochastic slow-fast dynamical systems
[
7
]
, two-scale stochastic optimal control problems
[
23
]
and stochastic modulation equations
[
8
]
.
In the present work, we are especially interested in the case where the deterministic drift has vanishing parts that lead to a lack of regularity in the limit.

The abstract setting which we will study is an SPDE of the type

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+Q_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}W

(1.1)

for some linear operator
A_{\varepsilon}
and a nonlinearity
F_{\varepsilon}
.
The noise term is always given by an additive Gaussian space-time white noise consisting of the derivative
of a standard cylindrical Wiener process
W
and the noise coefficient
Q_{\varepsilon}
, which is a linear operator.
The covariance operator
Q_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}Q_{\varepsilon}

encodes all the information about correlations and regularity of the noise.

We will study (
1.1
) in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
,
where we assume a vanishing noise strength
Q_{\varepsilon}\to 0
, but we also allow for a possibly
increasing spatial roughness of the noise,
in combination with the convergence of the linear operators

A_{\varepsilon}\to A
and of the nonlinearities
F_{\varepsilon}\to F
in a certain sense, where leading order differential operators may vanish in the limit.

The main aim of the paper is to find
interesting asymptotics of the noise coefficient
Q_{\varepsilon}

such that the limit
u
of the
u_{\varepsilon}
unexpectedly satisfies

\partial_{t}u=Au+G(u)

with
G\not=F
. This is typically the case when the noise strength vanishes in the limit, but the noise is to rough to make sense of the limiting equation. We comment below on existing results that treat exactly this situation, but all of the existing cases in the literature treat a fixed PDE, i.e.,
A_{\varepsilon}
and
F_{\varepsilon}
are independent of
\varepsilon
. In our result we want to allow for fixed roughness of the noise (for example the covariance operator
Q_{\varepsilon}
is just a scalar multiple of the identity) and vanishing regularization by higher order operators disappearing in the limit.

In the existence theory of semilinear SPDEs, the convergence of approximations by solutions to regularized equations needs to utilize probabilistic cancellations in order to overcome the pathwise irregularity generated by the Gaussian noise. This leads to a renormalization procedure that generates deterministic counterterms for the limit equation as a new requirement for well-posedness of the stochastic equation. This has been systematically studied in
[
26
,
10
]
.

In order to manage the convergence of the stochastic remainder terms in our approach, we shall in our examples employ a straightforward Fourier series computation which is well-known in the case of periodic boundary conditions
[
24
]
and the idea of which can be originally credited to Da Prato and Debussche
[
13
,
14
]
, see Section
6
for details. Our nonlinear estimates will nevertheless hold in a more general framework, but there we only reduce the key error estimate to various bounds on the stochastic convolution, which still have to be verified for the given domain and the boundary conditions.

We will provide an abstract setting and a fairly general result and apply this to three prototypical examples.
Our first example mainly recovers the well known result of
Hairer, Ryser and Weber
[
28
]
, where they studied the vanishing noise limit for Allen-Cahn equations in two spatial dimensions with regularized noise. This is not possible without using renormalized nonlinearities with diverging constants.
Although this application is not new, we include it to illustrate our result, where this would correspond to the case where both
A_{\varepsilon}
and
F_{\varepsilon}
are independent of
\varepsilon
, and only the noise changes with
\varepsilon
.

In contrast to that our two main examples treat the case of vanishing higher order differential operators where the noise has a fixed roughness.
First for the Allen-Cahn equation with space-time white noise we study the regularization of
A_{\varepsilon}

with an additional Bilaplacian that vanishes in the limit. A main difficulty in our approach is that we had to study error estimates in spaces with
\varepsilon
-dependent norms, which is not necessary in our first example from
[
28
]
.

In our third example we will study an
Allen-Cahn/Cahn-Hilliard homotopy, where again the regularity of the noise is fixed, but both
A_{\varepsilon}
and the nonlinearity
F_{\varepsilon}
have vanishing higher order differential operators, which leads to additional difficulties.

Let us point out that similar results in the same spirit
of
[
28
]
with vanishing noise of increasing roughness were obtained in the literature, but they do not fit into our framework.
First
[
34
]
treats a result for the stochastic nonlinear wave equation. Moreover Flandoli, Galeati, and Luo
[
19
,
18
]
studied the effect of transport noise on the Navier-Stokes and Euler equation, where in the limit of vanishing strength due to increasing roughness of the noise one obtains in the limit a deterministic Navier-Stokes equation with a changed viscosity that depends on the noise.

Let us mention that vanishing noise limits occur also when studying the large deviations principle and small noise asymptotics for semilinear SPDEs, see e.g.
[
17
,
40
,
15
,
30
]
and the references therein. An interesting result is
[
29
]
, where the authors study the large deviation limit in combination with the loss of regularity limit. An asymptotic coupling theorem for the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with small space-time white noise in two dimensions was studied in
[
39
]
.

The dependence of solutions to SPDEs on the convergence of coefficients or parameters in the drift and covariance terms has e.g. been studied in
[
31
,
11
,
22
,
4
]
.

Let us finally remark that regularity structures were studied for equations with Neumann boundary conditions on a square in
[
21
,
27
]
or
on Riemannian manifolds without boundary in
[
3
]
, but the case of general domains
seems to be open. Nevertheless, we will formulate our results also for the general domain case, as we can reduce all nonlinear estimates to simple bounds on the stochastic convolution, which still need to be verified. We shall only briefly comment on the periodic case in the end.

Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we shall present our main results divided into the cases of our prototypical examples which are introduced as well. In Section
3
we formulate and prove our abstract main Theorem
3.9
together with its assumptions.
Here the error estimate is carried over to bounds on the residual. In Section
4
the assumptions are verified for our examples.
The bound on the residual terms for each example is proved in Section

. Here we carry over all error estimates to bounds on the stochastic convolution only. Finally, our main approximation result is completed by examples for the estimates needed for the stochastic convolution in Section

.

2
Results and examples

We are interested in the following
examples which will be covered by our abstract setting. For simplicity of presentation we study a cubic nonlinearity, and differential operators that are diagonal in Fourier space, although our abstract result would allow for a far more general setting.

Moreover, the limiting equations contain only a Laplacian
\Delta
as a leading order term, which is not enough to give a meaning to the equation perturbed by space-time white noise
\partial_{t}W
in two spatial dimensions or more.

2.1
Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy in 2D

A typical example is the stochastic
Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn (CH/AC) equation
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta)(\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon}))+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}W

(2.1)

for a standard cubic nonlinearity
f(u)=u-u^{3}
with space-time white noise
\partial_{t}W

in a two dimensional domain subject to periodic boundary conditions on the flat torus or Neumann boundary conditions on general domains.
In a similar way, we could also consider the equation in three spatial dimensions, but abstain from doing so here.

From the general theory of SPDEs, one is expecting
that in terms of existence and regularity of
solutions this equation for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

behaves similarly to the standard stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation
(case
\varepsilon=1
) — even with space-time white noise.
This equation has unique global solutions for initial conditions in
L^{2}
.
This can be proved in the same way as for the Cahn-Hilliard equation, treated by Da Prato and Debussche
[
12
]
, who were applying the spectral Galerkin method.
In the case of multiplicative noise
the existence and uniqueness of solutions was studied in
[
2
]
.

The main difficulty is that for the convergence
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\to\sigma>0
for

\varepsilon\to 0
one would obtain the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation in the limit,
which is no longer well-posed in dimension
d\geq 2
.
One would need to add diverging renormalization constants to give sense to this case. See for example
[
29
,
28
]
or
[
6
]
among others.

For the main result, we try to determine
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
in such a way that
with high probability

u_{\varepsilon}\approx u+Z_{\varepsilon}

where
Z_{\varepsilon}
is the stochastic convolution, i.e. the solution to (
2.1
) with
f=0
, see (
3.1
) for the definition, and
u
solves the deterministic PDE

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+f(u)-3C_{0}u,

where the constant
C_{0}
depends on the convergence of sequence
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
and is defined in (
6.2
). In fact, it is the limit in quadratic mean of
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
in
H^{-1}
, see (
6.3
).

Remark 2.1
.

In our work we mainly focus on the nonlinear estimates and establish the full approximation result for periodic boundary conditions only. For Neumann boundary conditions, our main results reduce the approximation result to a statement about the stochastic convolution. To evaluate this in full generality seems to be an open problem on general domains.

Note that for
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
too small,
we are just in a large deviation type regime,
where
C_{0}=0
. On the other hand, if
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
is too large, we are in the renormalization regime, where
C_{0}
has to be replaced by an
\varepsilon
-dependent constant that diverges for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let us remark that we do neither need regularity structures, nor paracontrolled distributions for our result.
Our limit is a deterministic PDE and thus we can assume more regularity of the limit
u
. Therefore, mixed terms like
uZ_{\varepsilon}^{2}
are always well-defined in the space where
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
is well-defined.

2.2
Allen-Cahn with higher order regularization

Another example in a similar spirit as the Allen-Cahn equation with regularized noise
is the singular limit with noise subject to the higher order regularization

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}W,

(2.2)

either with periodic or Neumann boundary conditions.
Again, the higher order differential equation is regularizing
and, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, the equation might not be well-defined if
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
converges to a positive constant or is just not vanishing fast enough.

The main results in this case are analogous to the CH/AC-homotopy, but simpler, as we can use less conditions on the stochastic convolution to bound the residual in the main error estimate.

2.3
Allen-Cahn with regularized noise

Hairer, Ryser and Weber
[
28
]
studied AC in two spatial dimensions with regularized noise
subject to periodic boundary conditions, i.e.

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})+\sigma_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}W,

(2.3)

where the operator
Q_{\varepsilon}=\sigma_{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
is split explicitly into the scalar noise strength
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
and spatial correlation given by the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
.
Here the equation is fixed,
but due to the regularization
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}\to I
for constant
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\sigma
the limit
\varepsilon\to 0

is not possible without using renormalized nonlinearities with diverging constants.

The authors also identified three regimes. For fixed (or too large) noise strength the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
would converge to zero.
For too small noise strength the noise would just vanish, and in the intermediate regime, one obtains a non-trivial limit.

An interesting result is
[
29
]
where the authors study the limits of noise strength to zero and loss of regularity of the noise both separately and combined.
Our method partially
recovers their result for the joint limit when the limiting equation is a deterministic PDE.

2.4
Viscous Cahn-Hilliard equation

A model related to the Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy
is the viscous Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation introduced by Novick-Cohen

[
33
]
,
that can be transformed into

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-(1-\alpha-\alpha\Delta)^{-1}\Delta(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon}))+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}W.

Here the case
\alpha=1
corresponds to the AC equation,
while
\alpha=0
is the CH equation.

But this example does not fit into our setting,
as with space-time white noise
only the case
\alpha=0
is well-posed in spatial dimension
d=2
and
d=3
,
while for
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we would need renormalization as the linear operator
does not generate enough regularity of the solution.

3
Abstract approach

Recall our abstract equation (
1.1
) posed in a separable Hilbert space
H
.
The norm of
H
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|
.
In the abstract result, we will not go into the detail
of establishing the existence of solutions,
but comment in our examples on it in more detail.
For a general approach to well-posedness of SPDEs see
[
16
,
32
,
15
]
.

Assumption 3.1
.

Suppose that we have for all
\varepsilon>0
a Gelfand-triple
together with an additional Banach space
X
such that

V_{\varepsilon}\subset X\subset H\simeq H^{\prime}\subset V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}

for a separable and reflexive Banach space
V_{\varepsilon}
with
topological dual
V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
such that all embeddings are continuous and dense.

Let us define the stochastic convolution

Z_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A_{\varepsilon}}Q_{\varepsilon}\,dW(s)

(3.1)

which is in general defined in terms of a

C_{0}
-semigroup
e^{tA_{\varepsilon}}
generated by
A_{\varepsilon}
.
Here we first suppose that
Z_{\varepsilon}
is a
well-defined stochastic process in
X
, more precisely, assume:

Assumption 3.2
.

The process

Z_{\varepsilon}
belongs
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. to
C^{0}([0,T];X)
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
T>0
.

We will later add additional assumptions on
Z_{\varepsilon}
.
In general, we need
Z_{\varepsilon}
to take values in a more regular
space than
H
, as
F_{\varepsilon}
is in our examples in general not defined
on
H
but on a smaller space
X
. See Assumption
3.8
below.

Using the standard transformation,
we define

v_{\varepsilon}:=u_{\varepsilon}-Z_{\varepsilon}\;,

in order to obtain

\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon}+Z_{\varepsilon}).

(3.2)

Remark 3.3
.

As we need
at least some regularity of
u
and
v_{\varepsilon}
for the singular limit,
even at time zero,
we do not treat the substitution with a stationary stochastic convolution,
where the integral in
Z_{\varepsilon}
starts at
-\infty
,
which would be more natural in this context.

We want to show that if
Z_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}\to C_{0}
in a certain sense (in fact, with values in
X
both limits will diverge)
then we can still
have averaging/renormalization effects in
F(v_{\varepsilon}+Z_{\varepsilon})
that appear in the limit and lead to additional terms.

Thus we consider for some nonlinearity
G
the limiting PDE

\partial_{t}u=Au+G(u).

(3.3)

Definition 3.4
.

We say that
u
is a weak solution to (
3.3
) with initial datum
u(0)=u_{0}\in H
in a Gelfand triple

V\subset X\subset H\simeq H^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}

with dense and continuous embeddings for measurable operators
G:X\to V^{\prime}
and
A:V\to V^{\prime}
if
u\in L^{2}([0,T],V)
such that for all
t\in[0,T]

\langle u(t),v\rangle_{H}=\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle Au(s),v\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle G(u(s)),v\rangle\,ds

for every
v\in V
, and all integrals are well defined.

Note that angle brackets
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in the previous definition
denote the dual pairing between
V
and
V^{\prime}
induced by the identification of
H
with its dual
H^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that
u_{\varepsilon}
solves (
1.1
) if
v_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-Z_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution to (
3.2
), where
Z_{\varepsilon}
denotes the stochastic convolution from Assumption
3.2
, and where we define weak solutions to (
3.2
) as in Definition
3.4
, where
V
is replaced by
V_{\varepsilon}
,
A
is replaced by
A_{\varepsilon}
, and
G
is replaced by
F_{\varepsilon}
.

Assumption 3.6
.

Let
G:X\to V^{\prime}
,
F_{\varepsilon}:X\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
,
A:V\to V^{\prime}
, and
A_{\varepsilon}:V_{\varepsilon}\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
be measurable operators.
We assume that weak solutions to (
3.3
) exist in a Gelfand triple

V\subset X\subset H\simeq H^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}

with dense and continuous embeddings such that

u\in L^{2}([0,T],V)\cap C^{0}([0,T],X),\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}([0,T],V^{\prime}).

(3.4)

Moreover, we assume that weak solutions to (
3.2
) exist in the Gelfand triple

V_{\varepsilon}\subset X\subset H\simeq H^{\prime}\subset V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}

such that

v_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon})\cap C^{0}([0,T],X),\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}).

(3.5)

Suppose moreover that the error
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=u-v_{\varepsilon}
is well-defined with

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon}),\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}).

(3.6)

Remark 3.7
.

In order to ensure the existence of solutions,
we of course need additional assumptions on the nonlinearities
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G
.
We comment below in all our examples that it is usually straightforward to ensure that Assumption
3.6
is true.
Moreover, it is usually easy to verify in examples that the solution
u
of (
3.3
) becomes more regular than in (
3.4
) provided the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
is more regular than merely
u_{0}\in H
.

Note that the assumption (
3.6
) implies
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{0}([0,T],H)
and is moreover
sufficient for all the energy estimates,
as in this case
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\in W^{1,1}([0,T])

with
\partial_{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}=2\langle\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle
, see e.g.
[
36
, Proposition III.1.2]
.

The assumption (
3.6
) requires usually higher regularity of
u
as just (
3.4
). In our examples, we have a situation that
V_{\varepsilon}\subset V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\subset V
, for any
\varepsilon>\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
. We may assume as much regularity for the initial datum
u_{0}
of (
3.3
) as needed such that
u\in L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon})
and
\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}[0,T],V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
for every
\varepsilon>0
. We shall not assume any abstract relation of
V_{\varepsilon}
and
V
as the spaces are usually canonically attached to
A_{\varepsilon}
and
A
respectively.

Our approach is based on residual estimates and an approximation result,
where there are basically two possible ways.
One needs to plug the solution to one of the equations for the residual estimate
in the other equation, which yields an approximation result.

We shall calculate the residual of
u
, if plugged into (
3.2
).
Define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t)-A_{\varepsilon}u(t)-F_{\varepsilon}(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-A_{\varepsilon})u(t)+F(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))-F_{\varepsilon}(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle+G(u(t))-F(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))

Note that in the abstract setting, in general, we do not know
whether the residual is defined and sufficiently regular.
This has to be checked in applications, when the bound on the residual necessary for the main theorem have to be checked. Concerning our examples, we shall verify this in Section

5
.

The advantage of the latter rewriting is that the term
F(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))
,
where the renormalization terms will appear, has a fixed nonlinearity
F
,
which simplifies the argument significantly.

The aim is now to choose spaces
V_{\varepsilon}\subset H

such that we can bound the residual in the dual space
V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. We will always use the convention that the
V^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
-norm of the residual is infinite if it is not well-defined in that space.

Note that in some examples the spaces
V_{\varepsilon}
and thus
V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
are independent of
\varepsilon>0
,
but for instance in the Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy, the
\varepsilon
-dependence is natural.

The crucial assumption that hides all the
technicalities in the estimates for the
nonlinear terms is:

Assumption 3.8
.

Consider the spaces
X
and
V_{\varepsilon}
from Assumption
3.1

and suppose
F_{\varepsilon}:X\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and
A_{\varepsilon}:V_{\varepsilon}\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
.
Assume that there exist constants
C\geq 0
,
\delta>0
, and
0\leq c_{\varepsilon}\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
,
such that:

\langle A_{\varepsilon}\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\langle F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\psi)-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi),\varphi\rangle\leq-\delta\|\varphi\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+C\|\varphi\|^{2}

(3.8)

for every choice of
\varphi\in V_{\varepsilon}
and
\psi\in X

with
c_{\varepsilon}\|\psi\|_{X}\leq 1
.

We are ready to state and prove our main theorem.

Theorem 3.9
.

Under Assumptions
3.1
,
3.6
, and
3.8

let
u
,
u_{\varepsilon}
be any two solutions to (
3.3
), (
1.1
), respectively, from Assumption
3.6

and define by
Z_{\varepsilon}
the stochastic convolution from Assumption
3.2
.

Then,
for all
T>0
, there is a constant
K>0
such that
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have that
for all
\gamma>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{[0,T]}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u-Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}>K\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}>(2c_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|u(0)-u_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt>\gamma\right)

Note that for
c_{\varepsilon}>0
the condition
\varepsilon>0
being sufficiently small
in the previous theorem can be quantified by

\sup_{[0,T]}\|u\|_{X}\leq(2c_{\varepsilon})^{-1}
, see (
3.12
).

In all cases where
c_{\varepsilon}=0
we
will use that
1/c_{\varepsilon}=\infty
and no smallness of
\varepsilon
is needed and furthermore
no condition on the
X
-norm of
Z_{\varepsilon}
is needed.

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

For the approximation result consider the error with
u,v_{\varepsilon}
from Assumption
3.6

\varphi_{\varepsilon}=v_{\varepsilon}-u=u_{\varepsilon}-Z_{\varepsilon}-u

which (using the residual) solves

\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}+u+Z_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}(u+Z_{\varepsilon})+\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\;.

(3.9)

Now based on the regularity assumed on
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
we use standard a priori estimates based on

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}=\langle\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle

to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}=\langle A_{\varepsilon}\varphi_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}+u+Z_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}(u+Z_{\varepsilon}),\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u),\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle.

(3.10)

We now use the crucial assumption
3.8

that hides all the technical estimates and assume that

c_{\varepsilon}\|u+Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq 1.

(3.11)

Note that as
u
is independent of
\varepsilon
we have that for
small
\varepsilon>0
such that

c_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{X}\leq 1/2

(3.12)

the equation (
3.11
) is true provided
c_{\varepsilon}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq 1/2
,
which we will assume from now on.
Moreover, recall that
u\in C^{0}([0,T],X)
by assumption.
Thus for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small in the sense of (

),

\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq(2c_{\varepsilon})^{-1}

implies

\sup_{[0,T]}\|u+Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}.

Using (
3.11
) and Assumption
3.8
we obtain from (

)

\partial_{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\leq-2\delta\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+2C\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}+2\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}

Thus by Young inequality

\partial_{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}\leq-\delta\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+2C\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\;.

By Gronwall type arguments we obtain for
t\in[0,T]

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}\leq\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}e^{2Ct}+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2C(t-s)}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,ds\;.

Now we assume that

\int_{0}^{T}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,ds\leq\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}\leq\gamma.

Thus we obtain a constant
K>0
depending only on
\delta
and
T>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}\leq K\gamma\;.

∎

The main task for the remaining parts of the paper is to verify Assumption
3.8
in the given examples
and to show that the bound on the residual needed in the previous theorem is actually small.

The initial conditions will always satisfy
\mathbb{P}\left(\|u(0)-u_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}>\gamma\right)=0
for all
\gamma>0
if we assume
u(0)=u_{\varepsilon}(0)
. However, we will need solutions
u
of the deterministic PDE (
3.3
) to be more regular than (
3.4
), so one can think of
u(0)
as a regularized version of
u_{\varepsilon}(0)
.

4.1
Cahn-Hilliard/Allen-Cahn homotopy in 2D

In (
2.1
), we have either with Neumann or periodic boundary conditions

A_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta)\Delta

and

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta)f(u)

where we use
the standard cubic
f(u)=u-u^{3}
such that via Young inequality

(f(\varphi+\psi)-f(\psi))\varphi=\varphi^{2}-\varphi^{4}-3\varphi^{3}\psi-3\varphi^{2}\psi^{2}\leq\varphi^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\varphi^{4}.

Moreover, it is easy to verify that for
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
we have in
H
, being the standard
L^{2}
-space, that

\langle A_{\varepsilon}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\varepsilon\|\Delta\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-(1-\varepsilon)\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

We choose
V_{\varepsilon}=H^{2}
to be the standard Sobolev space
with the
\varepsilon
dependent norm given by

\|\varphi\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}^{2}=\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\langle A_{\varepsilon}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}\;.

(4.1)

For the nonlinear estimate, we obtain by using the standard cubic

\displaystyle\langle F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\psi)-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi),\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\langle f(\varphi+\psi)-f(\psi),\Delta\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}+(1-\varepsilon)\langle f(\varphi+\psi)-f(\psi),\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+(1-\varepsilon)\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\int_{U}[(\varphi+\psi)^{3}-\psi^{3}]\Delta\varphi\,dx-(1-\varepsilon)\int_{U}[(\varphi+\psi)^{3}-\psi^{3}]\varphi\,dx.

For the cubic terms, we use

\varepsilon\int_{U}\varphi^{3}\Delta\varphi\;dx=-3\varepsilon\int_{U}\varphi^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx\leq 0,

and

\varepsilon\int_{U}\varphi^{2}\psi\Delta\varphi\,dx\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|\Delta\varphi\|\leq 4\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\infty}+\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon\|\Delta\varphi\|^{2}\;.

Moreover,

\varepsilon\int_{U}\varphi\psi^{2}\Delta\varphi\,dx\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\Delta\varphi\|\leq 4\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\psi\|^{4}_{\infty}+\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon\|\Delta\varphi\|^{2}\;.

and finally

-(1-\varepsilon)\int_{U}[(\varphi+\psi)^{3}-\psi^{3}]\varphi\,dx\leq-\frac{1}{4}(1-\varepsilon)\|\varphi\|_{L^{4}}^{4}.

Now,

\langle F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\psi)-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi),\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\|\Delta\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{4}-C\varepsilon\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)\|\varphi\|^{4}_{L^{4}}+C\left(1+\varepsilon\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{4}\right)\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Finally, for
\psi
with
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon^{-1/4}
we have

\langle F_{\varepsilon}(\varphi+\psi)-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi),\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\|\Delta\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{4}-C\varepsilon^{1/2}\right)\|\varphi\|^{4}_{L^{4}}+2C\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Thus for the space
X=C^{0}=C^{0}(\overline{U})
the assumption
3.8

is satisfied for
c_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{1/4}
and small
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, note that by Sobolev embedding
\varphi\in L^{4}
if
\varphi\in H^{2}
.

We can now rewrite our main theorem
3.9
to obtain:

Theorem 4.1
.

In the setting of the CH/AC-homotopy in 2D,
let
u
,
u_{\varepsilon}
be any two solutions to (

), (
1.1
), respectively, from Assumption
3.6

and define by

the stochastic convolution from Assumption

.

Then,
for all

there is a constant

such that
for all

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{[0,T]}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u-Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}>K\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}>1/2\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\|u(0)-u_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt>\gamma\right).

5
Convergence of the residual

In this section, we consider the situation in our examples that the probability of the
L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon})
-norm of the residual being larger than
\gamma>0
is small, and bound it by various probabilities that depend only on the stochastic convolution.
Moreover, we finally fix the
G
in (
3.3
).
In all three examples the limiting equation is

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+f(u)-3C_{0}u

(5.1)

with
G(u)=f(u)-C_{0}u
, where
C_{0}
is defined by the limit of
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The limiting equation is a standard Allen-Cahn equation so that both for Neumann or periodic boundary conditions, we obtain a unique solution

u\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{1})\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})

for initial conditions
u(0)\in H^{1}
via well-known results for nonlinear parabolic PDEs using
for instance spectral Galerkin methods, see e.g.
[
35
,
38
]
.

Thus we fix a sufficiently smooth solution
u
and in the following consider the bound on the residual.
Recall the definition (
3.7
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{\varepsilon}(u)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-A_{\varepsilon})u(t)+F(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))-F_{\varepsilon}(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle+G(u(t))-F(u(t)+Z_{\varepsilon}(t))

which we need by the main theorem to
be bounded in
L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})
.

In the case that
A_{\varepsilon}=A
and
F_{\varepsilon}=F
do not depend on
\varepsilon
, the residual is encoding the information of the renormalization coming from the vanishing noise.

Firstly, we need

\int_{0}^{T}\|(A-A_{\varepsilon})u\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

(5.2)

We will see below that this is assured if
u
is sufficiently regular.

Secondly, we need

\int_{0}^{T}\|F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}(u+Z_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

(5.3)

This term in (
5.3
)
is zero for the singular AC,
but for the CH/AC equation more is needed.
Here,

F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}(u+Z_{\varepsilon})=-\varepsilon(1+\Delta)f(u+Z_{\varepsilon})

and thus we need bounds on
Z_{\varepsilon}
and
u

to get this bounded in
L^{2}([0,T],V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})
.

Thirdly, consider

G(u)=f(u)-3C_{0}u

for our simple cubic, to obtain

F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-G(u)=Z_{\varepsilon}-3u^{2}Z_{\varepsilon}-3u[Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}]-Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}.

For these terms, we need various bounds on
Z_{\varepsilon}
and averaging results from the next section, together with higher regularity of
u
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that the previous statement already focuses on the periodic case, where
C_{0}
is a constant. Due to boundary effects, for the case of Neumann boundary conditions on general domains, the situation of
C_{0}
not being a constant function in the spatial component may occur, see
[
21
]
.

5.1
CH/AC-homotopy in 2D

For (
5.2
) we have
A=\Delta
and
A_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta)\Delta
and thus

A-A_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(1+\Delta)\Delta

where the norm in
V_{\varepsilon}
is

\|u\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}^{2}=\langle(1+(-\Delta)(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta))u,u\rangle.

Suppose that
\{e_{k}\}
,
k\geq 1
, is an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
with corresponding eigenvalues
\{\mu_{k}\}
,
k\geq 1
, then
for
u=\sum_{k\geq 1}u_{k}e_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\|(A-A_{\varepsilon})u\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(1-\mu_{k})^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}}{(1+\mu_{k})(1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon\mu_{k})}u_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\mu_{k}^{2})u_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Thus (
5.2
) holds, provided
u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})
.
We can bound the first term in the residual by
C\varepsilon
.

Note that

\|1+\Delta\|_{L(L^{2},V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2},

(5.4)

which can be seen as follows. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|1+\Delta\|_{L(L^{2},V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}=

\displaystyle\|(1+\Delta)((1+(-\Delta))(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon\Delta))^{-1/2}\|_{L(L^{2},L^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq 1}\left|\frac{1-\mu_{k}}{\sqrt{(1+\mu_{k})(1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon\mu_{k})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq 1}\left|\frac{1-\mu_{k}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon(1+\mu_{k})\mu_{k}}}\right|\leq\sup_{k\geq 1}\left|\frac{\mu_{k}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mu_{k}}\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\inf_{k\geq 1}\mu_{k}}\right)\varepsilon^{-1/2}.

For (
5.3
), we rely on (
5.4
), to bound

F_{\varepsilon}(w)-F(w)=-\varepsilon(1+\Delta)f(w),

where
\|\cdot\|_{L(L^{2},V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}
denotes the operator norm.
Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-F_{\varepsilon}(u+Z_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{1/2}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(u+Z_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{1/2}\left(1+\|u\|^{6}_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})}+\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{6}_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})}\right).

As
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1})
, we can bound the first term above by
C\varepsilon^{1/2}
. For the second term, we need a bound on
Z_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})
.

For the third term
in the residual we use that

\|w\|_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{-1}}

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-G(u)\|^{2}_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|F(u+Z_{\varepsilon})-G(u)\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|Z_{\varepsilon}-3u^{2}Z_{\varepsilon}-3u[Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}]-Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

Now we need smallness of the bounds on
Z_{\varepsilon}
and
Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}
in
L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})

to control the first and last term small.

For the second and third term, we need to bound products in
H^{-1}
using for example the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
v\in H^{-1}
and
w\in H^{1\vee(d/2+s)}
,
s>0
on a
d
-dimensional domain, the pointwise product
v\cdot w
is defined and satisfies

\|vw\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{-1}}\|w\|_{H^{1\vee(d/2+s)}}

where the constant depends on
s
.

Proof.

See for example
[
5
, Theorem 8.1]
.
∎

Then, by additionally using the submultiplicativity of the
H^{1+s}
-norm in two spatial dimensions, by interpolation, and by Hölder’s and Jensen’s inequalities respectively, we derive for suitable
s\in(0,1)
and
p>2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{2}Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|^{4}_{H^{1+s}}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|^{4(1-s)}_{H^{1}}\|u\|^{4s}_{H^{2}}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|^{4(1-s)}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1})}\|u\|^{4s}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}

provided
s
is sufficiently small and
p
sufficiently large.

Similarly, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u[Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}]\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|^{2(1-s)}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1})}\|u\|^{2s}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})}\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}

We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

In the setting of the CH/AC-homotopy in 2D, let
u\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{1})\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})
be a solution to (
5.1
) and fix
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small.
Then for all
T>0
and sufficiently large

there is a constant
\tilde{K}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

and for all
\gamma\geq\varepsilon^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{[0,T]}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u-Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L_{2}}>\tilde{K}\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}>1/2\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|u(0)-u_{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{6}_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)

5.3

6
Stochastic Convolution for the CH/AC-homotopy

In order to finish the full approximation result, we need to establish various bounds on the stochastic convolution.
We shall briefly state the corresponding results on the flat torus only, i.e. for periodic boundary conditions. We assume moreover that the operator
Q_{\varepsilon}
is Fourier-diagonal and hence is equal to the operator square root of the covariance operator
Q_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}Q_{\varepsilon}=Q_{\varepsilon}^{2}
.

For Neumann boundary conditions on a general domain in particular the quantification of the convergence for
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
seems to be an open problem.
So far, we are only aware of the result
[
21
]
with Neumann boundary conditions on the square.

On the two-dimensional torus, we can rely on Fourier series.
Assuming that
Q_{\varepsilon}
is also diagonal in Fourier space,
we have

Z_{\varepsilon}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\alpha_{k}(\varepsilon)I_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)e_{k}\quad\text{with}\quad I_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\,d\beta_{k}(s)

for the Fourier basis
e_{k}
and complex-valued Brownian motion
\beta_{k}
such that
\overline{\beta_{k}}=\beta_{-k}
.
Moreover,
Q_{\varepsilon}e_{k}=\alpha_{k}(\varepsilon)e_{k}
and
for our differential operators

A_{\varepsilon}e_{k}=-\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)e_{k}
with
0\leq\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)\to\infty
for
|k|\to\infty
.
We call the
k
th coefficient in the series the
k
th Fourier mode of
Z_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 6.1
.

In contrast to the existing literature our stochastic convolution

Z_{\varepsilon}
is not stationary. We chose to perform the non-stationary transformation
to the random PDE (
3.2
) in order to have the initial condition unchanged.
But that is not a major issue, and most estimates work exactly the same way.
Only in a few occasions extra effort is needed.

In the example of the CH/AC-homotopy with small space-time white noise, we have

\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon|k|^{2})|k|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{k}(\varepsilon)=\sigma_{\varepsilon}\to 0.

We fix this notation in the sequel so that for the CH/AC-homotopy, we can shall prove the following result related to Theorem
5.3
.

Proposition 6.2
.

In the setting of the CH/AC-homotopy in 2D, let
u\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{1})\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})
be a solution to (
5.1
) and fix
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small.
Then for all
T>0
and sufficiently large
p>2
, and for all
\gamma\geq\varepsilon^{1/2}
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}>1/2\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{6}_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})}>\gamma\right)\Bigg]=0.

Proof.

Firstly, it is well-known using the Kolmogorov continuity theorem or the Sobolev embedding of
W^{\alpha,p}
into
C^{0}
that for all
\delta>0
and
p>1
that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\|Z_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{C^{0}}\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{p}\left(1+\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{|k|^{\delta}}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\right)^{p/2}.

We can extend this result, using for example the celebrated factorization method, to obtain that
for all
\delta>0
,
T>0
and
p>1
there is a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{C^{0}}\right]\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{p}\left(1+\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{|k|^{\delta}}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\right)^{p/2}.

Thus, we obtain by Chebyshev’s inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/4}\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2\varepsilon^{1/4}\sigma_{\varepsilon}\right)^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{[0,T]}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{p/4}\sigma_{\varepsilon}\left(1+\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{|k|^{\delta}}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{for}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

For the convergence above, we can simply evaluate the series by a splitting into
|k|\leq\varepsilon^{-1/2}
and
|k|>\varepsilon^{-1/2}
and using integral comparison theorems to obtain

\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{|k|^{\delta}}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\sim\varepsilon^{\delta/2}\quad\text{ for }\delta>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\sim\log(\varepsilon^{-1}).

We see that, as expected, we are in the critical case in two spatial dimensions,
and the bound for the
C^{0}
-norm of
Z_{\varepsilon}
diverges slowly for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

In a similar way, one can show for
\varepsilon\to 0
that

\mathbb{P}\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{6}_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6})}>\gamma\right)\to 0

and

\mathbb{P}\left(\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}>\gamma\right)\to 0

where, in the last integral, one relies on an additional factor
1/|k|^{2}
appearing due to the
H^{-1}
-norm so that

\mathbb{E}\|Z_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}}\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left(1+\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{|k|^{-2}}{\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}\right)\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left(1+\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{1}{|k|^{4}}\right)

and we can use Gaussianity for higher order moments to yield the result.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

In all three cases above and in the two cases below, we could quantify the convergence in more detail and could even make
\gamma
small and
\varepsilon
-dependent. This would not only give us convergence in probability, but also a rate of convergence.
But for the sake of simplicity of presentation, we are not entering this discussion.

One can also expand
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
in terms of Fourier-modes

Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}I_{k-\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)I_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)e_{k}

From the literature it is well-known that for convergence of the type

\mathbb{E}\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})}\to 0

we need

C_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}

(6.1)

in order to handle the divergent constant terms.
This divergent term matches the zeroth Fourier mode of
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
. All other Fourier modes of
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
remain finite in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.
This is folklore, see for example
[
24
,
13
,
9
]
.

Note that we can always neglect the constant mode
k=0
in
Z_{\varepsilon}
.
The stochastic convolution is not stationary in our case, as
\lambda_{0}(\varepsilon)=0
, but due to
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\to 0
every fixed Fourier mode of
Z_{\varepsilon}

disappears in
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, equality in (
6.1
) is only needed asymptotically for
\varepsilon\to 0
thus we could neglect any finite number of terms in the sum.

We thus define our renormalizing constant to be

C_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{1}{2\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)}.

(6.2)

Remark 6.4
.

By the discussion above, we need
\sigma(\varepsilon)\sim 1/\log(\varepsilon^{-1})
in order to have a nontrivial limit.

Moreover, if
\sigma(\varepsilon)\ll 1/\log(\varepsilon^{-1})
we have
C_{0}=0
. Our main result still applies, but this is an uninteresting case, as we would just prove that sufficiently small noise disappears in the limit.

On the other hand, if
\sigma(\varepsilon)\gg 1/\log(\varepsilon^{-1})
, we are in the regime of triviality observed in
[
28
]
. As the constant
C_{\varepsilon}
diverges, the limiting equation heuristically has an arbitrarily strong linear damping, and thus
u\equiv 0
in the end and
u_{\varepsilon}\approx Z_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
H^{-1}
, which implies triviality. Nevertheless,
u_{\varepsilon}
explodes in
L^{2}
.

It is now a lengthy and tedious, but relatively straightforward computation to show that

\mathbb{E}\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})}\to 0.

(6.3)

The key in this computation is that
C_{0}
together with the small noise strength controls the divergent zeroth Fourier mode in
Z_{\varepsilon}^{2}
.
An expansion of
Z_{\varepsilon}
in terms of Fourier series would yield a threefold sum for the norm, where one can rely on cancellations in expectation due to independence of the Fourier modes of
Z_{\varepsilon}
.

In the end, we crucially rely on the
\varepsilon
-independent bound of the series

\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0,k\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{k-\ell}}.

All these computations are well-known in case when
Z_{\varepsilon}
is a stationary process, but they work exactly the same way here.
Due to the non-stationarity of
Z_{\varepsilon}
additional exponential terms appear in the computations that are usually just discarded via estimates.
Moreover, the additional time integral in the
L^{2}([0,T],H^{-1})
-norm helps, as the integral over the exponential terms generate an additional factor
1/\lambda_{k}(\varepsilon)
that enhances the convergence of the series.

Let us finally remark that due to Nelson’s theorem, see for example
[
37
, Theorem I.22]
, we can always bound higher order moments by powers of the second moment which we just discussed.

In a similar way using Fourier-series expansion we can establish

\mathbb{E}\|Z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\|^{p}_{L^{p}([0,T],H^{-1})}\to 0

but we refrain from giving details of this lengthy calculation, as we are not interested in the precise asymptotics.

Our final result is:

Theorem 6.5
.

For the CH/AC-homotopy with periodic boundary conditions on a two dimensional domain perturbed by space-time white noise of strength

\sigma_{\varepsilon}\sim\frac{1}{\log(\varepsilon^{-1})},

supposing that

\|u_{\varepsilon}(0)-u(0)\|_{L^{2}}\to 0

in probability, we obtain that

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u-Z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2})}\to 0\quad\text{
in probability,}

where
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1})\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{2})
is a solution to

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+u-u^{3}-3C_{0}u

with
C_{0}
defined as in (
6.2
).

We emphasize that for
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\ll 1/\log(\varepsilon^{-1})
the result still holds, but in this case with
C_{0}=0
.

Furthermore, the result is formulated in a weak sense. One
could optimize it and would actually obtain a rate of convergence if the various moments discussed above were computed in more detail.

Finally, recall that in the case of the theorem above the
L^{2}
-norm of
Z_{\varepsilon}
remains bounded in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. In the case where
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\gg\log(\varepsilon^{-1})
, the
L^{2}
-norm of
Z_{\varepsilon}
diverges to infinity and we are in the case of triviality, where the result above will hold for
u=0
,
i.e.
u_{\varepsilon}\approx Z_{\varepsilon}
diverges in
L^{2}
, but converges to
0
in
H^{-1}
.

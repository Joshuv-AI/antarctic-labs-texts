1.
Introduction

For
n\geq 1
, denote by
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
the
n
-th Heisenberg group,
and by
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
any domain (open connected open set).
Let
1<p<\infty
.
A function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called
p
-harmonic (usually called harmonic when
p=2
) in
\Omega
if

u\in HW_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the
p
-subLaplace equation

(1.1)

\Delta_{0,p}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}X_{i}(|Xu|^{p-2}X_{i}u)=0\quad in\ \Omega,

that is,

\int_{\Omega}|Xu|^{p-2}\langle Xu,X\phi\rangle\,dx=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Here
HW_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the collection of functions
u\in L^{p}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)

with their distributional horizontal derivatives
Xu=(X_{1}u,\cdots,X_{2n}u)\in L^{p}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
.

In the linear case
p=2
,

2
-harmonic functions are exactly harmonic functions in
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,
and their
C^{\infty}
-regularity follows from a result by Hörmander
[
6
]
.
In the quasilinear case
p\neq 2
, the study of regularity of
p
-harmonic functions in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}

attracted a lot of attention in past decades.
In particular, their Hölder regularity was established by Lu
[
8
]
and Capogna
[
1
]
. Recently, their Lipshictz regularity and also the Hölder regularity of their horizontal gradients were proved by Domokos and Manfredi
[
4
]
,
Manfredi and Mingione
[
9
]
,
Mingione, Zatorska-Goldstein and Zhong
[
11
]
,
Zhong
[
14
]
, and Mukherjee and Zhong
[
12
]
.

On the other hand, if
p>2
, Capogna
[
1
]
proved the
C^{\infty}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions
u
under an additional assumption that
|Xu|
is strictly bounded from above and also away from
0
. In general,
|Xu|
may vanish in some set and

u
is also not necessarily smooth. In 2005, Domokos and Manfredi
[
3
]
established an interesting second order differentiability: if

(1.2)

\frac{\sqrt{17}-1}{2}<p<\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{2}\ \mbox{when $n=1$ and }2<p<2+\frac{n+n\sqrt{4n^{2}+4n-3}}{2n^{2}+2n-2}\ \mbox{when $n\geq 2$},

then any
p
-harmonic function
u
in
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
belongs to

HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, that is,
the second order distributional horizontal derivative

XXu=(X_{i}X_{j}u)_{2n\times 2n}\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times 2n}).

The restriction of
p
in the range (
1.2
) comes from their approach, which is based on
some subelliptic Cordes estimate built up therein.
For other
p
, it is then open to prove or disprove the
HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions in Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The main aim of this paper is to establish the

HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions with a quantitative upper bound for

(1.3)

\mbox{ $2<p\leq 4$ when $n=1$ and}\ 2<p<3+\frac{1}{n-1}\ \mbox{ when $n\geq 2$.}

Note that, for
1<p<2
,
the
HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity with a quantitative upper bound can be derived directly from
Zhong
[
14
]
(see also
[
12
]
).
Below, we summarize our result and, for the reader’s convenience, the consequence of
Zhong of
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

(1.4)

\mbox{ $1<p\leq 4$ when $n=1$ and}\ 1<p<3+\frac{1}{n-1}\ \mbox{ when $n\geq 2$.}

If
u
is a
p
-harmonic function in a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, then

u\in HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
if
1<p\leq 2
, then

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|XXu|^{2}

\displaystyle\phi^{2}\,dx\leq C(n,p)K_{\phi}\left[\int_{\rm spt\,(\phi)}|Xu|^{{2-p}}\,dx\int_{\rm spt\,(\phi)}|Xu|^{{p+2}}\,dx\right]^{1/2}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

If
p>2
satisfies (
1.4
), then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|XXu|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)\int_{\rm spt(\phi)}|u|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\,dx+C(n,p)K_{\phi}\left[\int_{\rm spt(\phi)}|Xu|^{{4-p}}\,dx\right]^{1/2}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left[\int_{\rm spt(\phi)}|Xu|^{{p+2}}\,dx\int_{\rm spt(\phi)}|Xu|^{{p-2}}\,dx\right]^{1/4}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

In this paper,

always denotes a positive constant depending only on parameters

…, but its value may vary from line to line. For any

we write

Note that, a direct calculation shows that, for each

When

\displaystyle[3+\frac{1}{n-1}]-[2+\frac{n+n\sqrt{4n^{2}+4n-3}}{2n^{2}+2n-2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n(2n^{2}+2n-2)-[n+n\sqrt{4n^{2}+4n-3}](n-1)}{(n-1)(2n^{2}+2n-2)}

\displaystyle=n\frac{(2n+4+\frac{2}{n-1})-[1+\sqrt{4n^{2}+4n-3}]}{2n^{2}+2n-2}

\displaystyle>n\frac{2n+3-\sqrt{(2n+3)^{2}-8n-12}}{2n^{2}+2n-2}

\displaystyle>0.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. The
n
-th Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by a Lie group which has a background manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and whose Lie algebra has a step two stratification
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
=
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V_{1}
has dimension
2n
,
V_{2}
has dimension 1, and
[V_{1},V_{1}]=V_{2}
,
[V_{1},V_{2}]=0
and
[V_{2},V_{2}]=0
.
To be precise, the group multiplication on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
is given by

(x,t)\cdot(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{2n}+x_{2n}^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[x_{i}x_{i+n}^{\prime}-x_{i+n}x^{\prime}_{i}])

for all
(x,t)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n},t)
and

(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(x^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{2n},t^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Associated to this group operation, the canonical basis of the tangent space is given by the left invariant vector fields translated from the identity, that is,

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{x_{i+n}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\qquad i=1,\cdots,n,

\displaystyle X_{i+n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i+n}}+\frac{x_{i}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\qquad i=1,\cdots,n,

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

Write
V_{1}=\,{\rm span\,}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{2n}\}
and
V_{2}=\,{\rm span\,}T
, and notice that

[X_{i},X_{i+n}]=-[X_{i+n},X_{i}]=X_{i}X_{i+n}-X_{i+n}X_{i}=T\ \forall i=1,\cdots,n,

X_{i}T=TX_{i},\quad\mbox{$X_{i}X_{j}=X_{j}X_{i}$, $\forall i,j=1,\cdots,2n,|i-j|\neq n$.}

We refer to
[
2
]
for more background for Heisenberg groups.

Let
\Omega\subset{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
be any domain (open connected subset).
For any
v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
,
denote by
Xv:=(X_{1}v,\cdots,X_{2n}v)
the horizontal derivative;
for any
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, denote by
XXv=(X_{i}X_{j}v)_{2n\times 2n}
the second order horizontal derivative.
For
v\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, both of
Xv
and
XXu
are explained in
distributional sense. If
Xv
and
XXv
are given by some vector-valued functions, their lengths are written respectively as

|Xu|=(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}|X_{i}u|^{2})^{1/2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|XXu|=(\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}|X_{i}X_{j}u|^{2})^{1/2}.

For
1<p<\infty
, the horizontal Sobolev space
HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
is
the collection of all functions
v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
Xv\in L^{p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
, and equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{HW^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\||Xu|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

For any
m\geq 2
, the
m
-order horizontal Sobolev space

HW^{m,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}})
is the collection of all functions
u
with
Xu\in HW^{m-1,p}(\Omega)
, and its norm is defined in a similar way.
For any
m\geq 1
and
p>1
, we write

HW^{m,p}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
as the collection of all functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
so that

u\in HW^{m,p}(U)
for all
U\Subset\Omega
.
We also let
HW^{m,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the
\|\cdot\|_{HW^{m,p}(\Omega)}
-norm.

The following estimate for
v\in HW^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega)
was
obtained by Domokos-Manfredi
[
3
]
.
Note that
v\in HW^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
implies
XXv\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times 2n})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
v

\in

HW_{0}^{2,2}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.1)

\||XXv|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{1+\frac{2}{n}}\|\Delta_{0}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The constants above are sharp when

.

As a consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we immediately have

Corollary 2.2
.

For any

and

, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[|XXv|\phi\right]^{2}\,dx\leq 3\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{0}v)^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx+C(n)\int_{\Omega}\left[|Xv|^{2}|X\phi|^{2}+|v|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\right]\,dx.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
v\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Applying (
2.1
) to

, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|XX(v\phi)|^{2}\,dx\leq(1+\frac{2}{n})\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{0}(v\phi)|^{2}\,dx.

Note that, for any

\displaystyle|XX(v\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|(XXv)\phi+Xv\otimes X\phi+X\phi\otimes Xv+vXX\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta)|XXv|^{2}\phi^{2}-C(n,\eta)\left[|Xv|^{2}|X\phi|^{2}+|v|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\right],

and

\displaystyle|\Delta_{0}(v\phi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=[\Delta_{0}v\phi+2\langle Xv,X\phi\rangle+v\Delta_{0}\phi]^{2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\eta)(\Delta_{0}v)^{2}\phi^{2}+C(n,\eta)\left[|Xv|^{2}|X\phi|^{2}+|v|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\right].

Thus,

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\int_{U}|XXv|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{2}{n})(1+\eta)\int_{U}(\Delta_{0}v)^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+C(n,\eta)\int_{U}\left[|Xv|^{2}|X\phi|^{2}+|v|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\right]\,dx,

which together with a suitable choice of

gives (
2.2
).

Below we assume that

\displaystyle\quad\leq\eta\int_{U}|XXu^{\epsilon}|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx+C(n,p)\int_{U}\left[|Xu^{\epsilon}|^{2}|X\phi|^{2}+|u^{\epsilon}|^{2}|XX\phi|^{2}\right]\,dx

Universality in the number variance and counting statistics of the real and symplectic Ginibre ensemble
Gernot Akemann, Sung-Soo Byun, Markus Ebke, Grégory Schehr

Abstract
. In this article, we compute and compare the statistics of the number of eigenvalues in a centred disc of radius R R in all three Ginibre ensembles. We determine the mean and variance as functions of R R in the vicinity of the origin, where the real and symplectic ensembles exhibit respectively an additional attraction to or repulsion from the real axis, leading to different results. In the large radius limit, all three ensembles coincide and display a universal bulk behaviour of O ⁡ ( R 2 ) O(R^{2}) for the mean, and O ⁡ ( R ) O(R) for the variance. We present detailed conjectures for the bulk and edge scaling behaviours of the real Ginibre ensemble, having real and complex eigenvalues. For the symplectic ensemble we can go beyond the Gaussian case (corresponding to the Ginibre ensemble) and prove the universality of the full counting statistics both in the bulk and at the edge of the spectrum for rotationally invariant potentials, extending a recent work which considered the mean and the variance. This statistical behaviour coincides with the universality class of the complex Ginibre ensemble, which has been shown to be associated with the ground state of non-interacting fermions in a two-dimensional rotating harmonic trap. All our analytical results and conjectures are corroborated by numerical simulations.

There is currently a wide interest in the statistics of point processes, with applications ranging from ecology
[

[

[

[

\mathcal{S}
the full system and by
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
a sub-system of
\mathcal{S}
, the FCS addresses the fluctuations of the total number of points
\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}
inside the region

\mathcal{S}
, with a finite density
\rho

[
34

. In this case,

is simply a Poisson random variable of parameter
\rho|\Omega|

\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}
do coincide, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}]=\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{\Omega})
, indicating that the fluctuations are very large here. Hence the mean does not carry so much information about the random variable
\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}
.

In fact, in many relevant systems in mathematics and physics the corresponding point processes are much more rigid, in the sense that
\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{\Omega})/\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}]\to 0

[

,
88

[

A prominent example of such hyperuniform systems is provided by determinantal point processes (DPP), which include for instance complex random matrix ensembles, non-interacting trapped fermions
[
35
]
and many others, see e.g.
[

56
]

In the context of fermions, FCS turns out to be particularly interesting since, in many cases, the variance

allow to take “pictures” of the positions of the fermions, in particular in two-dimensional systems where most of these experiments are carried out. In fact, it was recently realised that several non-interacting trapped fermionic systems, in one and two dimensions, are related to Gaussian random matrix ensembles with complex entries
[
36

. In particular, in two-dimensions, it was shown that the positions of fermions in a two-dimensional

For such models, analytical progress is possible thanks to the fact that the underlying point processes are DPPs, for which very powerful analytical tools are available, already for a finite number of points

From a mathematical perspective it is natural to ask how robust the predictions of the complex Ginibre ensemble are, i.e. the question of their universality. There are at least two directions how this question of universality can be addressed.
First, one can replace the Gaussian weight in the space of matrix elements with a more general potential, which leads to normal random matrix ensembles, see e.g.
[
16
, Section 5]
.
A second direction is to consider the “cousins” of the GinUE, with real (GinOE) or quaternion matrix elements (GinSE) with independent Gaussian distributions, without further symmetry constraints, cf.
[
17
]
.
Both ensembles lead to Pfaffian point-processes instead, which are technically much harder to study than DPP.

In this paper, we will consider both of these universality questions. Currently, it is not clear how to directly relate the ground state wave function of a Schrödinger equation with an anharmonic potential to a complex ensemble of random matrices with a non-Gaussian distribution in two dimensions. Nonetheless, there have been several examples in one dimension, see e.g.
[

[

, where such a connection could be established. Note also that anharmonic potentials have recently been studied in the context of the quantum Hall effect
[

. Besides, it is not known if the Pfaffian point processes obtained for the GinOE and GinSE enjoy a direct quantum mechanical realisation. However, establishing a mathematically rigorous universality statement within all three ensembles, including normal matrices, gives rise to the expectation that the FCS might also be robust in more general quantum Hamiltonians.

It has been shown previously that the local statistics agrees for all complex eigenvalue correlation functions in all three Ginibre ensembles, away from the real line, both in the bulk and at the edge of the support of the spectrum (see e.g.

It is useful to recall the universality results that have been obtained so far for the Ginibre ensembles. In
[
73
]
, the mean, the variance and all higher order cumulants were determined for the GinUE and its extension to rotational invariant potentials
W(|z|)
, the random normal matrix ensembles in the GinUE class.
(See also
[
8
]
for recent work on the case of the potential
W
with a hard wall.)
In this case, the cumulants are given in terms of poly-logarithms for finite and infinite
N
.
These results were extended to normal symplectic ensembles in the GinSE class for the mean and variance in
[
1
]
. It was found that when measuring the variance in units of the mean density given by the Laplacian of the potential
\Delta W(R)
at radius
R
, the linear growth of the variance in the bulk is universal, i.e., the GinUE and the normal GinSE coincide. The same was found for the universal scaling function of the variance at the edge of the spectrum. (Here, we use the convention
\Delta=\partial\bar{\partial}
, a quarter of the usual Laplacian.)
On the other hand, in the origin regime, the variance depends on the singularities (or zeros) of the potential
W
and on the repulsion from the real axis present in the GinSE class.

In this work we will address the third class, the GinOE, and complete the FCS for the GinSE.
First, we analytically determine the mean and the variance in the GinOE in the origin regime.
For the bulk and edge regime we formulate precise conjectures for the GinOE to be universal, and provide numerical evidence for these conjectures.
We make contact between the origin and bulk regime by taking the large radius limit analytically.
Because, in the GinOE, correlations between real, complex and both kind of eigenvalues have to be considered separately, the computation of the mean and the variance will occupy a substantial part of the paper.
In the last part we explicitly determine all cumulants for finite and large-
N
in the planar symplectic ensembles with rotationally invariant potentials
W
in the GinSE class, and prove their universality in the bulk and edge scaling limit. Indeed, we show that they coincide with those found for the random normal matrix ensembles.

In the next Section

The following main results are presented there. In Subsection

This includes new results for the GinOE. Subsection

These conjectures are supported by numerics.
Finally Subsection

The proofs for the results from Subsection

2.
Main results

In order to present our main results we briefly recall the Ginibre ensembles together with the quantities we need. For the GinOE more details are presented in Appendix
A
. In terms of matrices
G
, the Ginibre ensembles consists of random matrices that have
N^{2}
independent, centred normal real, complex or quaternion matrix elements,
g_{jk}\in\mathcal{N}_{F}(0,(N\beta)^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, without further symmetry constraint. Here the Dyson index
\beta=1,2,4
labels the field
F=\mathbb{R,C,H}
, respectively.

The joint probability distribution function (jpdf) of complex eigenvalues
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
of the GinUE respectively GinSE, normalised by the partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(2,4)}
, reads

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}^{(2)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(2)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{l=1}^{N}e^{-N|z_{l}|^{2}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}^{(4)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{(4)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}|z_{j}-\overline{z}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{l=1}^{N}|z_{l}-\overline{z}_{l}|^{2}e^{-2N|z_{l}|^{2}}.

For
\beta=4
note that an
N\times N
quaternion valued matrix
G
has an infinite number of eigenvalues, however they decompose into
N
equivalence classes of the form
[z_{j}]=\{q^{-1}z_{j}q\mid q\in\mathbb{H}\}
for some
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
j=1,\dots,N
, see
[
80
]
for a discussion.
Furthermore it holds that
[z_{j}]\cap\mathbb{C}=\{z_{j},\overline{z}_{j}\}
and the
2N
complex numbers
\{z_{1},\overline{z}_{1},\dots,z_{N},\overline{z}_{N}\}
are precisely the eigenvalues of the complex
2N\times 2N
representation of
G
.
In (
2.2
) we give the jpdf for
N
complex eigenvalues only, as the remaining
N
are fixed by complex conjugation. In principle, we could restrict the jpdf to the upper-half plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\{z\mid z\in\mathbb{C},\mathrm{Im}(z)>0\}
, because the jpdf is invariant under complex conjugation, cf.
[
64
]
.
However, in much of the literature
[
80
,
61
,
2
]
the jpdf is given on the full complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, the reason being that it is not difficult to find the complex eigenvalue correlation functions in the lower half plane as well.

For the GinOE with
N\times N
real matrices

, we have to distinguish

(non-real) complex eigenvalues, coming in conjugated pairs. Because of

is always of the same parity as

, otherwise the probability of finding

The corresponding jpdfs for fixed

and

are not immediately needed here and are presented in Appendix

for completeness. They depend on
k

complex eigenvalues, with the remaining

given by complex conjugation. In contrast to

Therefore, as most authors, we restrict ourselves to the upper half plane (except for the density, see below).
Notice that all three Ginibre ensembles correspond to a two-dimensional Coulomb gas at inverse temperature

[

2.2.1.
Infinite Ginibre ensembles in the origin scaling limit

Similarly to (

(2.39)

\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}\,\mathcal{N}^{(\beta=2,4)}(D_{R})&:=\int_{D_{R}}R_{1}^{(\beta=2,4)}(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{D_{R}^{2}}\Big(R_{2}^{(\beta=2,4)}(z,w)-R_{1}^{(\beta=2,4)}(z)R_{1}^{(\beta=2,4)}(w)\Big)\,dA(z)\,dA(w).\end{split}

Likewise, for

(2.40)

\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(D_{R}):=\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R})+2\,\operatorname{Cov}\Big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R}),\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R})\Big)+\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R}),

where

(2.41)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R}):=\int_{-R}^{R}R_{1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x)\,dx+\int_{(-R,R)^{2}}\Big(R_{2,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x,y)-R_{1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x)R_{1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(y)\Big)\,dx\,dy,

(2.42)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R}):=\int_{D_{R}}R_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(z)\,dA(z)+\int_{D_{R}^{2}}\Big(R_{2,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(z,w)-R_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(z)R_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(w)\Big)\,dA(z)\,dA(w),

(2.43)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}\Big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R}),\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R})\Big):=\int_{D_{R}}\int_{-R}^{R}\Big(R_{1,\mathbb{R},1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(x,z)-R_{1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x)R_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(z)\Big)\,dx\,dA(z).

In the proof of Proposition

(ii)

GinSE:

(2.49)

Remark 2.7
.

The negative covariance between real and complex eigenvalues can be
intuitively understood as follows. Looking at the joint density (
A.1
), it vanishes both when a complex eigenvalue (pair) becomes real, from the factor in the second line, and when it comes close to a real eigenvalue,
from the two factors in the first line.
The same negative correlation can also be expected on a larger scale

2.2

2.24

2.10
.

Remark 2.8
.

By Proposition

2.3.
Full counting statistics of planar symplectic ensembles

For comparison we present both the FCS for the non-Gaussian extension of the GinUE and GinSE class, the so-called planar ensembles.
The determinantal Coulomb gas ensemble (random normal matrix ensemble)
is defined by the joint density (

), replacing the Gaussian potential

[

Definition 2.12
.

A rotationally invariant potential
W_{N}(z)=g_{N}(|z|)
will be called suitable, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

g_{N}(r)\gg\log r
as
r\to\infty
;

(2)

g_{N}\in C^{2}(0,1]
;

(3)

rg^{\prime}_{N}(r)
increases on
(0,\infty)
;

(4)

\lim\limits_{r\to 0}rg_{N}^{\prime}(r)=0
, and
\lim_{N\to\infty}g^{\prime}_{N}(1)=4
.

Note that condition
(3)
is equivalent to
\Delta W_{N}(z)>0
for
z\neq 0
(here,
\Delta=\partial_{z}\overline{\partial_{z}}=\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})
for
z=x+iy
is one-quarter of the usual Laplacian) and condition
(4)
implies that the limiting droplet is the unit disk.

Let us write

(2.75)

h_{j}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-NW_{N}(z)}\,|z|^{2j}\,dA(z)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-Ng_{N}(r)}\,r^{2j+1}\,dr,

for the non-vanishing
j
-th moments, which are at the same time the squared norms of planar orthogonal polynomials (monomials).
We also write

(2.76)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{j,1}(a)&:=\int_{|z|<a}e^{-NW_{N}(z)}|z|^{2j}\,dA(z)=2\int_{0}^{a}e^{-Ng_{N}(r)}r^{2j+1}\,dr,\\
h_{j,2}(a)&:=\int_{|z|>a}e^{-NW_{N}(z)}|z|^{2j}\,dA(z)=2\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-Ng_{N}(r)}r^{2j+1}\,dr\end{split}

for the truncated moments or squared norms.
We also denote

(2.77)

\mathcal{L}_{j}(a)=\frac{h_{j,1}(a)}{h_{j}},\qquad\mathcal{M}_{j}(a)=\frac{h_{j,2}(a)}{h_{j}}.

Let us recall that
\mathcal{N}_{a}:=\#\{z_{j}:|z_{j}|\leq a\}
denotes the number of particles in the centred disc
D_{a}
of radius
a.

Here,

(2.78)

\operatorname{Li}_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-s}x^{k},\quad|x|<1,

is the definition of the polylogarithm function, that also converges for
|x|=1
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. It is given by analytic continuation otherwise.

Proposition 2.13

(
Finite-
N
expressions of the cumulant generating functions
)
.

Let
W
be a suitable potential, then we have the following for
a\geq 0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

(Random normal matrix ensembles)
We have for the moment generating function

(2.79)

\mathbb{E}_{N,W}^{(\beta=2)}\Big[e^{u\mathcal{N}_{a}}\Big]=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\Big(e^{u}\mathcal{L}_{j}(a)+\mathcal{M}_{j}(a)\Big).

Furthermore, for any
p\geq 2
, the
p

\mathcal{N}_{a}
has the expression

(2.80)

\kappa_{p}^{(\beta=2)}(a)=(-1)^{p+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Li}_{1-p}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{j}(a)}\Big).

We also have for the mean

(2.81)

E_{N,W}^{(\beta=2)}(a):=\mathbb{E}_{N,W}^{(\beta=2)}[\mathcal{N}_{a}]=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{L}_{j}(a).

(ii)

(Planar symplectic ensembles)
We have

(2.82)

\mathbb{E}_{N,W}^{(\beta=4)}\Big[e^{u\mathcal{N}_{a}}\Big]=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\Big(e^{u}\mathcal{L}_{2j+1}(a)+\mathcal{M}_{2j+1}(a)\Big).

Furthermore, for any
p\geq 2
, the
p
-th cumulant of
\mathcal{N}_{a}
has the expression

(2.83)

\kappa_{p}^{(\beta=4)}(a)=(-1)^{p+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Li}_{1-p}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{2j+1}(a)}\Big).

For the mean, we have

(2.84)

E_{N,W}^{(\beta=4)}(a):=\mathbb{E}_{N,W}^{(\beta=4)}[\mathcal{N}_{a}]=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{L}_{2j+1}(a).

Remark 2.15

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Inserting the
1
-point function
\textbf{R}_{N,1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x)
of the real eigenvalues from (

) into the definition of

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}.

For the first term we use

Q(N-1,Nx^{2})=e^{-Nx^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{N^{k}x^{2k}}{k!},\qquad\Gamma\Big(k+\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}},

and obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int_{-a}^{a}Q(N-1,Nx^{2})\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int_{-a}^{a}e^{-Nx^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{N^{k}x^{2k}}{k!}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{\gamma(k+\frac{1}{2},Na^{2})}{k!}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})}{k!}P\Big(k+\frac{1}{2},Na^{2}\Big)\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}P\Big(k+\frac{1}{2},Na^{2}\Big).\end{split}

On the other hand, using integration by parts we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int_{-a}^{a}Q(N-1,Nx^{2})\,dx&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{Na^{2}}\frac{Q(N-1,x)}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{a\sqrt{N}\,\Gamma(N-1,Na^{2})+\gamma(N-\tfrac{1}{2},Na^{2})}{(N-2)!}.\end{split}

This implies the identity (
3.3
).

For the second term we need to distinguish between even and odd
N
because

\displaystyle P\Big(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{Nx^{2}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1-e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{(N-3)/2}\frac{(Nx^{2}/2)^{k}}{k!}&\textup{for odd }N,\\
\displaystyle\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}|x|\Big)-\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{(Nx^{2}/2)^{k-1/2}}{(1/2)_{k}}&\textup{for even }N,\end{cases}

see
[
83
, Section 8.4]
.
Therefore if
N
is odd,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{-a}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}|x|^{N-1}P\Big(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{Nx^{2}}{2}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}x^{N-1}\,dx-2\sum_{k=0}^{(N-3)/2}\frac{(N/2)^{k}}{k!}\int_{0}^{a}e^{-Nx^{2}}x^{N-1+2k}\,dx

\displaystyle=2^{N/2}N^{-N/2}\gamma\Big(\frac{N}{2},\frac{Na^{2}}{2}\Big)-N^{-N/2}\sum_{k=0}^{(N-3)/2}\frac{1}{2^{k}\,k!}\gamma\Big(\frac{N}{2}+k,Na^{2}\Big)

and consequently

\displaystyle=P\Big(\frac{N}{2},\frac{Na^{2}}{2}\Big)-\sum_{k=0}^{(N-3)/2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+k)}{2^{k+N/2}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\,k!}P\Big(\frac{N}{2}+k,Na^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=P\Big(\frac{N}{2},\frac{Na^{2}}{2}\Big)-\frac{1}{2^{N/2}\,(N-2)!!}\sum_{k=0}^{(N-3)/2}\frac{(N+2k-2)!!}{2^{2k}\,k!}P\Big(\frac{N}{2}+k,Na^{2}\Big)

where we used
[
83
, Eq. (5.4.2)]
to write the Gamma-functions in terms of double factorials.
This implies (
3.1
), since
2^{k}k!=(2k)!!
.

On the other hand, if
N
is even,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{-a}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}|x|^{N-1}P\Big(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{Nx^{2}}{2}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}x^{N-1}\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}x\Big)\,dx-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{(N/2)^{k-1/2}}{(1/2)_{k}}\int_{0}^{a}e^{-Nx^{2}}x^{N-2+2k}\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}x^{N-1}\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}x\Big)\,dx-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}N^{-N/2}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{1}{(2k-1)!!}\gamma\Big(\frac{N-1}{2}+k,Na^{2}\Big),

and including the normalisation we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N^{N/2}}{2^{N/2}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\int_{-a}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}|x|^{N-1}P\Big(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{Nx^{2}}{2}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{N/2}}{2^{N/2-1}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}x^{N-1}\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}x\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2^{(N-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2}+k)}{(2k-1)!!}P\Big(\frac{N-1}{2}+k,Na^{2}\Big).

This yields (

), after rewriting the Gamma-functions using
[
83
, Eq. (5.4.2)]
in the second term.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have for the radial density at finite-
N

(3.10)

\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\textbf{R}_{N,1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}\Big(\frac{re^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)\,d\theta=\Big(1-e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)\Big)Q(N-1,r^{2}).

This leads to

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(a)&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}P(k+1,Na^{2})-2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}a}e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)Q(N-1,r^{2})\,dr\end{split}

Proof.

The
1
-point function
\textbf{R}_{N,1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(x)
of the complex eigenvalues is given by

(3.12)

\frac{1}{N}\textbf{R}_{N,1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}\Big(\frac{z}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)=\sqrt{{2}{\pi}}|y|\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}|y|)e^{2y^{2}}Q(N-1,|z|^{2}).

Using

(3.13)

\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y)=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2(t-y)^{2}}\,dt,

with
y=r\sin{\theta}
, we obtain
for the integral (
3.10
)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{N2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\textbf{R}_{N,1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}\Big(\frac{re^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)\,d\theta&=\frac{2}{\pi}Q(N-1,r^{2})\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|y|e^{-2(t-|y|)^{2}+2y^{2}}\,d\theta\,dt\\
&=\frac{4r}{\pi}Q(N-1,r^{2})\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2t^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\sin\theta)e^{4tr\sin\theta}\,d\theta\,dt\\
&=\frac{4r}{\pi}Q(N-1,r^{2})\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2t^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4tr)^{l}}{l!}\frac{2^{l+1}\Gamma\big(\frac{l}{2}+1\big)^{2}}{\Gamma(l+2)}\,dt\\
&=\frac{4r}{\pi}Q(N-1,r^{2})\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(8r)^{l}}{l!}\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{l}{2}+1\big)^{2}}{\Gamma(l+2)}\frac{(-1)^{l}\Gamma\big(\frac{l+1}{2}\big)}{2^{\frac{l+1}{2}}},\end{split}

using the elementary integrals

(3.15)

\int_{0}^{\pi}(\sin\theta)^{l+1}\,d\theta=\frac{2^{l+1}\Gamma(\frac{l}{2}+1)^{2}}{\Gamma(l+2)},\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2t^{2}}t^{l}\,dt=\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{1+\frac{l+1}{2}}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{l+1}{2}\Big).

The duplication formula

(3.16)

\Gamma(2z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}2^{2z-1}\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\tfrac{1}{2}),

can be used to further simplify the sum, and we obtain

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}l-\frac{1}{2}}(-r)^{l}\Gamma\big(\frac{l}{2}+1\big)}{\Gamma(l+2)}&=\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{3k-\frac{1}{2}r^{2k}\Gamma(k+1)}}{\Gamma(2k+2)}-\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2k+1}r^{2k+1}\Gamma\big(k+\frac{3}{2}\big)}{\Gamma(2k+3)}\\
&=\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2k-\frac{1}{2}}r^{2k}}{(2k+1)!!}-\pi\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{k-1}r^{2k+1}}{(k+1)!},\end{split}

after splitting the sum into even and odd indices
l
and applying once more the duplication formula (
3.16
).
Using the expansion of the error function
[
83
, Eq. (7.6.2)]
,

(3.18)

\operatorname{erf}(z)e^{z^{2}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!!}

and
\operatorname{erfc}(z)=1-\operatorname{erf}(z)
we obtain the following identity

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4r}\Big(1-e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{4r}\Big(1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2r)^{n}}{n!}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2r)^{2n}\sqrt{2}r}{(2n+1)!!}\Big),

which agrees with the sum in (
3.17
). This yields (
3.10
).

After having determined the radial density we can compute the mean in (
3.11
) as

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{N,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(a)&=2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}a}r\Big(1-e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)\Big)Q(N-1,r^{2})\,dr\\
&=2\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}a}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{r^{2k+1}}{k!}\,dr-2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}a}e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)Q(N-1,r^{2})\,dr\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}P(k+1,Na^{2})-2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}a}e^{2r^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}r)Q(N-1,r^{2})\,dr.\end{split}

∎

(4.15)

\operatorname{Var}\,\mathcal{N}^{(2)}(D_{R})=\int_{0}^{R}2xe^{-2x^{2}}I_{0}(2x^{2})dx=R^{2}e^{-2R^{2}}(I_{0}(2R^{2})+I_{1}(2R^{2})),

thanks to the anti-derivative given in Remark
2.5
.
The asymptotic behaviours (
2.47
) and (
2.48
) follow from the well-known asymptotic behaviours of the modified Bessel-function

(4.16)

I_{\nu}(x)\sim\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}},&x\to\infty,\\
\dfrac{x^{\nu}}{2^{\nu}\nu!},&x\to 0.\end{cases}

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In order to prepare for the proof in the next subsection, where we will not have all integrals as in (
4.13
) at hand, we present a different determination of
g(R)
starting from (
4.11
). It contains a nice expression closely related to the generating function for
Q(k+1,R^{2})
.

Inserting the series (
2.44
) for the Bessel function into the definition (
4.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle g(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{2k}}{k!}\Big(Q(k+1,R^{2})-P(k+1,R^{2})\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{2k}}{k!}Q(k+1,R^{2})-\frac{e^{R^{2}}}{2}.

Furthermore, using the expression

(4.17)

Q(k+1,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{x^{l}}{l!},

we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{2k}}{k!}Q(k+1,R^{2})

\displaystyle=e^{-R^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{2k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{R^{2l}}{l!}=e^{-R^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil}^{m}\binom{m}{k}R^{2m}.

Here, we have introduced the variable
m=k+l
, with
k\geq l=m-k\geq 0
and
m\geq k
, resulting into the summation boundaries indicated, and
\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil
is the ceiling function. The following two identities follow from the Binomial Theorem, by relabelling one of the sums in the first identity, and using it together with Pascal’s identity to derive the second:

(4.19)

2\sum_{k=m}^{2m}\binom{2m}{k}=2^{2m}+\binom{2m}{m},\qquad\sum_{k=m+1}^{2m+1}\binom{2m+1}{k}=2^{2m}.

Splitting the sum in (
4.18
) into even and odd summands, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{2k}}{k!}Q(k+1,R^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m)!}\Big(2^{2m-1}+\frac{1}{2}\binom{2m}{m}\Big)(R^{2})^{2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)!}2^{2m}(R^{2})^{2m+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2R^{2})^{m}}{m!}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(R^{2})^{2m}}{(m!)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{2R^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}I_{0}(2R^{2}).

This leads again to (
4.14
).

4.2.
Number variance of the GinSE at the origin

For the GinSE, we have

(4.20)

\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})=-\int_{D_{R}}\,dA(z)\int_{D_{R}^{c}}\,dA(w)\,R_{2}^{(4),c}(z,w),

Proof of Proposition

(ii).

For completeness we present

in terms of the limiting kernel at the origin

,

(4.21)

\kappa(z,w)=e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(z-w).

Inserted this into the limit of (
2.7
) defined in (

), we obtain

(4.22)

R_{1}^{(4)}(z)=(\bar{z}-z)e^{-2|z|^{2}}\kappa(z,\bar{z})

and

R_{2}^{(4)}(z,w)=(\bar{z}-z)(\bar{w}-w)e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}\Big(\kappa(z,\bar{z})\kappa(w,\bar{w})-|\kappa(z,w)|^{2}+|\kappa(z,\bar{w})|^{2}\Big).

This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{2}^{(4),c}(z,w)&=-(\bar{z}-z)(\bar{w}-w)e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}\Big(|\kappa(z,w)|^{2}-|\kappa(z,\bar{w})|^{2}\Big)\\
&=-\pi(\bar{z}-z)(\bar{w}-w)e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}\Big(\Big|e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(z-w)\Big|^{2}-\Big|e^{z^{2}+\bar{w}^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(z-\bar{w})\Big|^{2}\Big).\end{split}

In the following, we will use a different expression, directly following from

\kappa(z,w)=\sum_{0\leq l\leq k\leq\infty}\frac{2^{k+l+1}}{(2k+1)!!\,(2l)!!}(z^{2k+1}w^{2l}-w^{2k+1}z^{2l})=:G(z,w)-G(w,z),

see e.g.
[

]

Following the proof in

, using the complex conjugation symmetry and the orthogonality, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})

\displaystyle=2\int_{D}\,dA(z)\int_{D^{c}}\,dA(w)\,(\bar{z}-z)(\bar{w}-w)e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}|\kappa(z,w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\int_{D}\,dA(z)\int_{D^{c}}\,dA(w)\,\bar{z}we^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}G(z,w)G(\bar{w},\bar{z}),

where only the third term in the second line can be shown to contribute
[

]
.
The angular integrals project onto a single sum,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{4k+4}}{(2k+1)!^{2}}\int_{D}\,dA(z)\int_{D^{c}}\,dA(w)\,e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}|z|^{4k+2}|w|^{4k+2}

which leads to (

It remains to show (

).
For this, note that the series expansion for the Bessel function of first kind,

(4.28)

J_{0}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(z/2)^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}},

together with that for the modified Bessel function

in (

), can be used to rewrite the sum in (
4.26
) as the following difference:

(4.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})&=2\int_{D}\,dA(z)\int_{D^{c}}\,dA(w)\,e^{-2|z|^{2}-2|w|^{2}}\Big(I_{0}(4|zw|)-J_{0}(4|zw|)\Big)\\
&=8\int_{0}^{R}\,dr_{1}\int_{R}^{\infty}\,dr_{2}\,e^{-2r_{1}^{2}-2r_{2}^{2}}r_{1}r_{2}\Big(I_{0}(4r_{1}r_{2})-J_{0}(4r_{1}r_{2})\Big).\end{split}

The first integral follows from Proposition
2.4
(i), by substituting

:

\displaystyle 8\int_{0}^{R}\,dr_{1}\int_{R}^{\infty}\,dr_{2}\,e^{-2r_{1}^{2}-2r_{2}^{2}}r_{1}r_{2}I_{0}(4r_{1}r_{2})=R^{2}e^{-4R^{2}}\Big(I_{0}(4R^{2})+I_{1}(4R^{2})\Big).

Next, we evaluate the second integral

(4.30)

8\int_{0}^{R}\,dr_{1}\int_{R}^{\infty}\,dr_{2}\,e^{-2r_{1}^{2}-2r_{2}^{2}}r_{1}r_{2}J_{0}(4r_{1}r_{2})=\int_{0}^{R}\,dr_{1}f(R,r_{1}),

where we defined

(4.31)

f(R,r_{1}):=8\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-2r_{1}^{2}-2r_{2}^{2}}r_{1}r_{2}\,J_{0}(4r_{1}r_{2})\,dr_{2}.

As in the previous subsection we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\int_{0}^{R}f(R,r_{1})\,dr_{1}

\displaystyle=f(R,R)+\int_{0}^{R}\frac{d}{dR}f(R,r_{1})\,dr_{1}

\displaystyle=8R\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-2R^{2}-2r_{2}^{2}}r_{2}\,J_{0}(4Rr_{2})\,dr_{2}-8R\int_{0}^{R}e^{-2r_{1}^{2}-2R^{2}}r_{1}J_{0}(4Rr_{1})\,dr_{1}

\displaystyle=8Re^{-2R^{2}}g(R)

with

(4.32)

g(R):=\Big(\int_{R}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{R}\Big)e^{-2x^{2}}xJ_{0}(4Rx)\,dx,

now containing the function
J_{0}
instead of

. Although the first integral in (

in the literature. Thus, we follow the same strategy as in Remark

(4.33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(R)&=\Big(\int_{R}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{R}\Big)e^{-2x^{2}}x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2Rx)^{2k}}{k!^{2}}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2R^{2})^{k}}{k!}\Big(Q(k+1,2R^{2})-P(k+1,2R^{2})\Big)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2R^{2})^{k}}{k!}Q(k+1,2R^{2})-\frac{1}{4}e^{-2R^{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly to (
4.18
) we can thus write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2R^{2})^{k}}{k!}Q(k+1,2R^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2R^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-2R^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}(2R^{2})^{m}.

Similarly to the Remark
4.1
we can use two combinatorial identities, that follow from the Binomial theorem, this time with alternating coefficients. For that reason the terms
2^{m}
are absent, and we obtain

(4.34)

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=m}^{2m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{k}=(-1)^{m}\binom{2m}{m},\qquad(m\geq 1),

(4.35)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{2m+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m+1}{k}=-(-1)^{m}\binom{2m}{m},\qquad(m\geq 0).

Using this, we have

(4.36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2R^{2})^{k}}{k!}Q(k+1,2R^{2})\\
&=\frac{e^{-2R^{2}}}{2}\bigg(1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m)!}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{2}\binom{2m}{m}(2R^{2})^{2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)!}(-1)^{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}(2R^{2})^{2m+1}\bigg)\\

The last step follows from (

) and from spelling out the hypergeometric function defined in (

), in this case as

{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-4x^{4}\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2x^{2})^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}(2k+1)}.

Inserted into (

g(R)=\frac{1}{4}e^{-2R^{2}}J_{0}(4R^{2})-e^{-2R^{2}}\,R^{2}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-4R^{4}\Big).

Therefore, we have shown that

(4.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})&=R^{2}e^{-4R^{2}}\Big(I_{0}(4R^{2})+I_{1}(4R^{2})\Big)\\
&\quad-2\int_{0}^{R}xe^{-4x^{2}}\left(J_{0}(4x^{2})-4x^{2}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-4x^{4}\Big)\right)\,dx.\end{split}

It follows from

(4.40)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(x^{2}{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-4x^{4}\Big)\Big)=2xJ_{0}(4x^{2})

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}^{(4)}(D_{R})&=R^{2}e^{-4R^{2}}\Big(I_{0}(4R^{2})+I_{1}(4R^{2})\Big)-R^{2}e^{-4R^{2}}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-4R^{4}\Big).\end{split}

2.52

[

, Section 16.11(ii)]

and

(4.41)

{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-x\Big)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}+O(x^{-\frac{3}{2}}),\qquad x\to\infty,

and

(4.42)

{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1,\frac{3}{2};-x\Big)=1+O(x),\qquad x\to 0.

∎

4.3.
Number variance of the GinOE at the origin

In this subsection, we show Theorem

Proposition 4.3

(
Expression of the number variance of the infinite GinOE
)
.

For any radius
R\in[0,\infty)
we have the following.

(i)

We have for the real eigenvalues

(4.46)

\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R})=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}R-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}R\operatorname{erf}(2R)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}R)+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}R)^{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(1-e^{-4R^{2}}\Big).

(ii)

We have for the mixing between real and complex eigenvalues

(4.47)

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{Cov}\Big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R}),\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(D_{R})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}v)v\,e^{v^{2}}\Big(e^{-(\hat{a}-R)^{2}}-e^{-(\hat{a}+R)^{2}}+\sqrt{\pi}(\hat{a}-R)\operatorname{erf}(\hat{a}-R)-\sqrt{\pi}(\hat{a}+R)\operatorname{erf}(\hat{a}+R)\Big)\,dv,

where
\hat{a}\equiv\hat{a}(R)=\sqrt{R^{2}-v^{2}}.

(iii)

We have for the complex eigenvalues

(4.48)

\operatorname{Var}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(D_{R})=2E_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(R)+4\Big(I_{-}(R)-I_{+}(R)\Big)

where

(4.49)

I_{\pm}(R):=I_{\pm,1}(R)+I_{\pm,2}(R)+I_{\pm,3}(R)+I_{\pm,4}(R).

Here,

\displaystyle I_{\pm,1}(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{R}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})e^{(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2}}e^{-(a+b)^{2}}(1+(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\,dy_{1},

\displaystyle I_{\pm,2}(R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{R}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})e^{(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2}}e^{-(a-b)^{2}}(1+(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\,dy_{1},

\displaystyle I_{\pm,3}(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{R}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})e^{(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2}}(a+b)\operatorname{erf}(a+b)(1+2(y_{1}\pm y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\,dy_{1},

where

and

.

With the limiting matrix elements given in (

in the upper-half plane.
On the one hand, it follows from (

(4.59)

R_{1,\mathbb{R},1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(x,u+iv)-R_{1,\mathbb{R}}^{(1)}(x)R_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}(u+iv)=-\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}v)v\,e^{-(x-u)^{2}+v^{2}}.

Note that

Proof.

Combining these with elementary estimates

\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y)\leq e^{-2y^{2}},\qquad 1+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}\leq 1+R^{2},\qquad e^{-2y_{1}y_{2}}\leq 1,

we obtain

(4.72)

\displaystyle|I_{-,1}(R)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{R}e^{-2(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})}\,e^{2(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})-2y_{1}y_{2}-2R^{2}}(1+R^{2})\,dy_{1}\,dy_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}R^{4}e^{-2R^{2}}.

The integrand of

and thus

(4.73)

\displaystyle\lvert I_{-,2}(R)\rvert\leq-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(y_{1}+y_{2})^{2}}(1+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\,dy_{1}=\frac{1}{3\pi}.

For

and

, we observe that for

After expanding the other factors in the integrand using a Taylor series, it can be observed that the error of the approximation is polynomial in

and
y_{2}
. As a result, the integral over this error will remain bounded as

Note that

a+b=\sqrt{R^{2}-y_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{R^{2}-y_{2}^{2}}=2R+O\Big(\frac{1}{R}\Big),\qquad\operatorname{erf}(a+b)=1+O(e^{-4R^{2}}).

Using these, we have

\displaystyle I_{-,3}(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})e^{(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}}\Big(2R+O\Big(\frac{1}{R}\Big)\Big)(1+2(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\,dy_{1}

(4.74)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\pi(2\sqrt{2}-1)-4}{2\pi}+O\Big(\frac{1}{R}\Big).

Here, the double integral has been evaluated as follows. For the inner integral over
y_{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})e^{(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}}(1+2(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2})\,dy_{2}\\
&=\left[\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{2})(y_{2}-y_{1})e^{(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\right]_{0}^{\infty}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2y_{2}^{2}}(y_{2}-y_{1})e^{(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\,dy_{2}\\
&=e^{y_{1}^{2}}\Big(y_{1}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{y_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}e^{-y_{1}^{2}}-y_{1}\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})\Big)\Big)\;.\end{split}

The remaining integral over

contains three parts:

(4.75)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})e^{y_{1}^{2}}\Big(y_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-2\sqrt{2}y_{1}\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})e^{y_{1}^{2}}\Big)\,dy_{1}.

For the first term we obtain

[
52
, 6.238.1]

\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})e^{y_{1}^{2}}y_{1}\,dy_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.

For the third term we have

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})e^{2y_{1}^{2}}y_{1}\,dy_{1}

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{2}\left[\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})\frac{1}{4}e^{2y_{1}^{2}}\right]_{0}^{\infty}+2\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})\frac{-2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-y_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{2y_{1}^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})\Big)\frac{1}{4}e^{2y_{1}^{2}}\,dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}y_{1})e^{y_{1}^{2}}\,dy_{1}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{erfc}(y_{1})\,dy_{1}.

Here, the second integral cancels the second term in (

Similarly, it follows for

and

\displaystyle a+b

\displaystyle=\sqrt{R^{2}-\Big(\frac{Rs+u}{2}\Big)^{2}}+\sqrt{R^{2}-\Big(\frac{Rs-u}{2}\Big)^{2}}=\sqrt{4-s^{2}}\,R+O\Big(\frac{1}{R}\Big),

Similarly, the
v

The limiting integral is easier to compute in the original

Finally, for

6.
Conclusions and open questions

In this paper we have analysed the counting statistics of the number of eigenvalues in a centred disc of radius
R
in the real and symplectic Ginibre ensembles. For the symplectic ensemble we determined the full counting statistics by computing the generating function for all cumulants at finite matrix dimension
N
, for a large class of rotationally invariant non-Gaussian potentials. In the large-
N
limit we could prove that the symplectic ensemble is in the same universality class as its complex counterpart. This holds both in the bulk regime, when the radius is smaller but of the same order as the edge of the limiting support of the eigenvalues, and in the edge regime when choosing the radius in the vicinity of the edge.

For the real Ginibre ensemble, the counting statistics is much more involved, as the correlations of its real and complex eigenvalues have to be considered separately, including their covariance. Therefore, we restricted ourselves to the Gaussian potential and computed the mean number of points at finite and large-
N
, as well as the variance in the large-
N
limit, in both cases in the vicinity of the origin. In the large radius limit, leading into the bulk regime, we found a matching for both quantities with the universality class in the two other ensembles. In a small radius expansion, however, all three ensembles differ, reflecting their different repulsion of eigenvalues from the real axis.
For the bulk and edge regime, based on numerical simulations we conjectured the same universality found for the symplectic ensemble to hold for the real ensemble. It remains an open problem to prove these conjectures, and to extend our computations to the full counting statistics for all cumulants within the real ensemble. This would in principle give access to the full distribution of the number of eigenvalues in a disk of radius
R
. The large deviation regime of this distribution was studied using Coulomb gas techniques in Ref.
[
5
]
. However, one expects that this large deviation regime does not carry any information about the cumulants, which as in the GinUE case
[
70
]
, are probably determined by some “intermediate deviation regime” of the distribution. Because of the intricate Pfaffian structure of the GinOE with two kinds of eigenvalues (complex and real) this remains a challenging task.

Our analysis was driven by the question of universality of the counting statistics, within the three different ensembles and for non-Gaussian deformations of the weight function. While all three Ginibre ensembles enjoy an interpretation as a static Coulomb gas in two dimensions, only for the complex Ginibre ensemble we know of a map to a quantum Hamiltonian for fermions in a rotating harmonic trap, or equivalently in the presence of a magnetic field. It remains an open question if such a map exists also for the real and symplectic Ginibre ensembles studied here, which both lead to Pfaffian point processes.

Appendix A
Integrable structure of the GinOE: jpdf and origin limit

In this appendix we recall some aspects of the integrable structure of the GinOE in terms of Pfaffian determinants.
First, we give the joint density of real and complex eigenvalues of the GinOE. This allows us to define the correlation functions of real, complex or both types of eigenvalues as in (
2.3
). Second, we provide the origin scaling of all three types of correlation functions as they will be needed in the computation of the number variance of the GinOE in this limit.

A real

,
k
has to be of the same parity as
N
, i.e. both are either even or odd, from here on. Consequently,
N-k
is always even.
For the probability

k

[

]

[

Furthermore, since the eigenvalues in the lower half plane are completely fixed by complex conjugation of those in the upper half plane, and in particular are not independent, we can restrict ourselves to
l
complex eigenvalues in the upper half plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, with
\mathrm{Im}(z_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{Im}(z_{l})>0

The partial joint density for such eigenvalues with given

(A.1)

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{N,k,l}^{(1)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},z_{1},\dots,z_{l})&=C_{N,k,l}\prod_{i>j}^{k}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(x_{i}-z_{j})(x_{i}-\bar{z}_{j})\prod_{i>j}^{l}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}|z_{i}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{2}\\

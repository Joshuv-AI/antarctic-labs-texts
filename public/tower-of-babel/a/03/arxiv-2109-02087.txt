Correspondence of Donaldson-Thomas and Gopakumar-Vafa invariants on local Calabi-Yau 4-folds over V 5 V_{5} and V 22 V_{22}
Kiryong Chung, Sanghyeon Lee, Joonyeong Won

Abstract
. We compute Gromov-Witten (GW) and Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants (and also descendant invariants) for local CY 4 4 -folds over Fano 3 3 -folds, V 5 V_{5} and V 22 V_{22} up to degree 3 3 . We use torus localization for GW invariants computation, and use classical results for Hilbert schemes on V 5 V_{5} and V 22 V_{22} for DT invariants computation. From these computations, one can check correspondence between DT and Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) invariants conjectured by Cao-Maulik-Toda in genus 0 0 . Also we can compute genus 1 1 GV invariants via the conjecture of Cao-Toda, which turned out to be 0 0 . These fit into the fact that there are no smooth elliptic curves in V 5 V_{5} and V 22 V_{22} up to degree 3 3 .

14N35, 14C05,14C15, 14J45

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Recently, Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants for Calabi-Yau 4-folds and the correspondence among related invariants, like stable pair (PT) and Gopakumar-Vafa invariants (GV or BPS) have been actively studied in
[
CL14
,
CMT21
,
CMT18
,
CK20
,
CK18
,
CKM21
]
.

Virtual cycles of
\mathrm{DT_{4}}
moduli space has been developed by Borisov-Joyce
[
BJ17
]
and by Oh-Thomas
[
OT20
]
. For a CY 4-fold
X
and a curve class
\beta\in\mathrm{H}_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, we denote
M_{\beta}(X)
the moduli space of 1-dimensional stable sheaves
F
on
X
, such that
[F]=\beta
and
\chi(F)=1
. Then
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
invariants are defined by integrations of cohomology insertions over virtual cycles
[M_{\beta}(X)]^{\mathrm{vir}}
. Note that the virtual cycle is defined via some suitable choice of an orientation on the moduli space
M_{\beta}(X)
.

Insertions are defined by the following way. Consider the product space
M\times X
and a (normalized)universal sheaf
\mathcal{F}
over it. For a cohomology element
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, let

\displaystyle\tau_{i}(\gamma):=(\pi_{M})_{*}(\pi_{X}^{*}\gamma\cup\mathrm{ch}_{\dim(X)+i-1}(\mathcal{F}))\in\mathrm{H}^{*+2(i-1)}(M,\mathbb{Z})

where
M
is an abbreviation of
M_{\beta}(X)
.

\mathrm{DT_{4}}
invariants and descendent invariants (with a single insertion) are defined by

\langle\tau_{i}(\gamma)\rangle_{\beta}:=\int_{[M_{\beta}(X)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\tau_{i}(\gamma)

via some suitable choice of orientation on the moduli space
M_{\beta}
.
\langle\tau_{0}(\gamma)\rangle_{\beta}
is called
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
invariants
, and we denote it by
\mathrm{DT}_{4}(X)(\beta|\gamma)
.
When
i\geq 1
, we call
\tau_{i}(\gamma)

descendant insertions
, and integrations of descendant insertions over the virtual class are called
descendant invariants
.

On the other hand, we can consider
\mathrm{DT_{3}}
invariants on Fano 3-folds. Note that
\mathrm{DT}_{3}
moduli space for a Fano 3-fold is isomorphic to the
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
moduli space of the corresponding local CY 4-fold
[
CL14
, Theorem 6.5]
. Moreover,
\mathrm{DT_{3}}
invariants for a Fano 3-fold is equal to the
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
invariant of the corresponding local CY 4-fold when we choose some suitable orientation in the
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
moduli space
[
Cao19a
,
Cao19b
]
.

Now we review some famous conjectures between
\mathrm{DT_{4}}
and GV invariants. Let us denote
n_{0,\beta}(\gamma)
(resp.
n_{1,\beta}
) be the genus 0 (resp. genus 1) Gopakumar-Vafa invariants on a CY 4-fold
X
defined in
[
KP08
]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
CMT18
, Conjecture 0.2]

Via some suitable choice of orientation on the moduli space, we have

(1)

n_{0,\beta}(\gamma)=\mathrm{DT}_{4}(\beta|\gamma),\;\mathrm{GW}(\gamma)^{X}_{0,\beta}=\sum_{k|\beta}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mathrm{DT}_{4}(X)(\beta/k|\gamma)

for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Note that two equations in (
1
) are equivalent via the definition of
n_{0,\beta}(\gamma)
in
[
KP08
]
. In
[
CMT18
]
,
Conjecture
1.1
is proven for some cases: CY 4-folds with a elliptic structure and local curves/surfaces.
Also, in
[
CT21
]
, the following conjecture is proposed which relates
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
descendent invariants and genus one GV invariants. Let
m_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}
be the meeting invariants defined in Section 0.3 in
[
KP08
]
(for a detail, see Section
4
).

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
CT21
, Conjecture 0.2]

Via some suitable choice of orientation on the moduli space, we have

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{1}(\gamma)\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{0,\beta}(\gamma^{2})}{2(\gamma\cdot\beta)}-\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}\frac{(\gamma\cdot\beta_{1})(\gamma\cdot\beta_{2})}{4(\gamma\cdot\beta)}m_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\geq 1,k|\beta}\frac{(\gamma\cdot\beta)}{k}n_{1,\beta/k}

for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Here, the number
m_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}
is a virtual count of degree
\beta_{1}
curves in
X
which meets with degree
\beta_{2}
, called
meeting invariants
. We will compute these numbers in Section
4
.

In
[
CT21
]
, Conjecture
1.2
is proved in several cases:
CY 4-folds with an elliptic fibration structure and local Fano
3
-fold over

{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. In this paper, as a continuation of the latter case, our study focus on the Fano
3
-folds:
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
. See Section
3.1.1
and
3.1.3
for definitions of
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
. These Fano
3
-folds arise as a minimal compactification of the complex
3
-space
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. For detailed descriptions, see the beginning parts of Section
3.1
.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we compute some low degree
\mathrm{DT_{3}}
invariants for Fano 3-folds
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
.
For the cases
\beta=d\cdot[\text{line}]
,
1\leq d\leq 3
where
[\text{line}]
is a class of
[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
in their projective embedding via the very ample generators, we review some classical results on description of
\mathrm{DT}_{3}
moduli space of
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
and universal sheaves on them. Using this, we will compute
\mathrm{DT}_{3}
invariants of these Fano 3-folds(equivalently
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
invariants of corresponding local CY 4-fold) in Section
3.1
.

On the other hand, in Section
2
we compute twisted Gromov-Witten (GW) invariants on
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
for the cases
\beta=d\cdot[\text{line}]
,
1\leq d\leq 3
, using quantum Lefschetz property
[
KKP03
]
and torus localization
[
GP99
]
. We will introduce some recipe for computing genus zero GW invariants on Grassmannian varieties, which is a direct analogue of the formula in
[
GP99
,
HTK+03
]
for genus zero GW invariants on projective spaces. From these calculations, we obtain the following main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.3

(=Theorem
3.18
)
.

Using the choice of orientation as in
[
CT21
, (0.7)]
on
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
moduli spaces on local CY 4-folds, the Conjecture
1.1
holds for
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
,
\beta=d\cdot[\text{line}]
,
1\leq d\leq 3
.

Theorem 1.4

(=Theorem
4.2
)
.

Using the choice of orientation as in
[
CT21
, (0.7)]
on
\mathrm{DT}_{4}
moduli spaces on local CY 4-folds, the Conjecture
1.2
holds for
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
,
\beta=d\cdot[\text{line}]
,
1\leq d\leq 3
if and only if genus
1
GV invariants
n_{1,\beta}\equiv 0
.

From the fact that the degree of the defining equation of
V_{5}
and

is
\leq 2
and the varieties does not contain plane, one can check that the smooth elliptic curves of the degree
\leq 3
in

2.
Twisted GW invariants on Grassmannians

Let
Y
be a smooth Fano
3
-fold embedded in the Grassmannian variety
G=\mathrm{Gr}(r,d)
, which is a zero section of a bundle over
G
. In this subsection, we present an algorithm of the computation of GW invariants of
|K_{Y}|
, a total space of a canonical line bundle of
Y
. For any smooth projective variety
X
, we denote the moduli space of stable maps, with
k
-marked points with degree
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
by
M_{0,k}(X,\beta)
. When
X=G
, we will denote
M_{0,k}(G,d)
the stable map space with degree
d\cdot[\text{line}]
. We will usually abbreviate
M_{0,k}(X,\beta)
by
M(X)
in the following. In this section, we will introduce classical methods to compute GW invariants, quantum Lefschetz principle and torus localization when the target variety is
|K_{Y}|
. If you are not interested in this part, you may skip the details and just see computational results in Section
2.4
.

Consider the forgetful map
p:M=M_{0,k}(G,d)\to\mathfrak{M}_{0,k}
where
\mathfrak{M}_{0,k}
is the moduli space of prestable genus
0
curves with
k
marked points. We usually will abbreviate
\mathfrak{M}_{0,k}
by
\mathfrak{M}
.
There is a usual (relative) perfect obstruction theory
[
Beh96
]

E_{M(X)/\mathfrak{M}}\to L_{M(X)/\mathfrak{M}}
of
M(X)\to\mathfrak{M}
, where

(2)

\displaystyle E_{M/\mathfrak{M}}:=[R\pi_{*}f^{*}T_{G}]^{\vee}\in D^{b}(M(X)).

Also, as in
[
BF97
]
the virtual cycle correspond to this perfect obstruction theory is given by:

(3)

\displaystyle[M(X)]^{\mathrm{vir}}:=0^{!}_{h^{1}/h^{0}(E_{M/\mathfrak{M}})}[\mathcal{C}_{M(X)/\mathfrak{M}}]\in A_{\mathrm{vdim}}(M(X))

where
h^{1}/h^{0}(E_{M/\mathfrak{M}})
is a vector bundle stack correspond to
E_{M/\mathfrak{M}}
,
\mathcal{C}_{M(X)/\mathfrak{M}}
is the (relative) intrinsic normal cone defined in
[
BF97
]
, and
\mathrm{vdim}
is given by

\mathrm{vdim}=(1-g)(\dim(X)-3)+k-\int_{\beta}c_{1}(K_{X})

which is called virtual dimension.

2.1.
Quantum Lefschetz principle

We will briefly review some aspect of famous quantum Lefschetz principle
[
KKP03
]
in this section. We consider a negative vector bundle
E
on
G
, so that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(G,f^{*}E)=0
for any non-constant morphism
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to G
.
Let
|E|
be the total space of
E
and
p:|E|\to G
be the projection. Consider a stable map
f:C\to|E|
. Then
p\circ f:C\to G
is a stable map and
f
induces an element of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(C,(p\circ f)^{*}E)
. But since
E
is a direct sum of negative degree line bundles,
\mathrm{H}^{0}(C,(p\circ f)^{*}E)=0
.
Hence we have a natural isomorphism of moduli spaces of stable maps:

M_{0,k}(|E|,d)\cong M_{0,k}(G,d).

Note that
M_{0,k}(G,d)
is well known to be smooth. Also, we have the short exact sequence:

0\to p^{*}T_{G}\to T_{|E|}\to p^{*}E\to 0.

From the condition that
E
is negative and the fact that
\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}T_{G})=0
, we obtain the following by taking the higher direct image functor
R\pi_{*}f^{*}
to the above short exact sequence.

E_{M(|E|)/\mathfrak{M}}^{\vee}=\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}T_{|E|}=\left[\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}T_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}E)\right]

Note that
\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}T_{G})\cong T_{M(G)}
. We can easily check that the intrinsic normal cone
\mathcal{C}_{M(G)/\mathfrak{M}}
is
[M(G)/T_{M(G)/\mathfrak{M}}]
and
h^{1}/h^{0}(E^{\vee})=[R^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}E)/T_{M(G)/\mathfrak{M}}]
.
From the definition of the virtual cycle in (
3
), we have

\displaystyle[M(G)]^{\mathrm{vir}}

\displaystyle=0^{!}_{[R^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}E)/T_{M(G)/\mathfrak{M}}]}[M(G)/T_{M(G)/\mathfrak{M}}]

\displaystyle=0^{!}_{R^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}E)}[M(G)]

\displaystyle=e(\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}(f^{*}p^{*}E))\cap[M(G)]

where the second identity comes from properties of Gysin pull-backs via bundle stacks in
[
Kre99
]
.
We call this phenomenon
B-twist
. Note that this phenomenon also arises when we replace
G
by any projective variety.
Using this, we define a
twisted GW invariants
of a Fano variety
X
by:

Definition 2.1

(Twisted GW invariant)
.

The twisted Gromov-Witten invariant a Fano variety
X
is defined by the integration

\mathrm{GW}^{\text{twist}}_{0,\beta}(X)(\gamma):=\int_{[M_{0,1}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}e(\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}f^{*}K_{X})\cup\mathrm{ev}^{*}(\gamma)\in\mathbb{Q}

where
\mathrm{ev}:M_{0,1}(X,\beta)\rightarrow X
be the evaluation map.

Note that twisted GW invariants are usually considered as a definition of GW invariants of the total space
|K_{X}|
, because we can not define it directly since
|K_{X}|
is not compact.

Next we consider a complete intersection in
G=G(r,n)
. Let
E
be the vector bundle on
G
and let
Y\subset G
be a zero section of the generic section
s:\mathcal{O}_{G}\to E
. Assume that
E
is convex, so that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\varphi^{*}E)=0
for any non-constant morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to G
. For example, the direct sum
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})
,
a_{i}>0
of line bundles on
G
is a convex vector bundle.
From the convexity of
E
,
\pi_{*}f^{*}E
is locally free, where
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to M
is the universal curve and
f:\mathcal{C}\to G
is the universal morphism. Let
M(Y)
denote the stable map space
M_{0,k}(Y,d)
. Then we have

E_{M(Y)/\mathfrak{M}}^{\vee}=\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}f^{*}T_{Y}=[\pi_{*}f^{*}T_{G}\to\pi_{*}f^{*}E]=[T_{M}\to\pi_{*}f^{*}E].

Consider the section
s:\mathcal{O}_{G}\to E
and the induced section
\widetilde{s}:M\to\pi_{*}f^{*}E
defined by
\widetilde{s}([(C,f)])=(C,f^{*}s)\in H^{0}(C,f^{*}E)
. Then we have
Z(\widetilde{s})=M(Y)\subset M
.
We can easily check that
\mathcal{C}_{M/\mathfrak{M}}=[C_{M(Y)/M}/T_{M/\mathfrak{M}}]
and
h^{1}/h^{0}(E^{\vee})=[\pi_{*}f^{*}E/T_{M/\mathfrak{M}}]
.
Then by the definition of the virtual cycle, we have

[M(Y)]^{\mathrm{vir}}=0^{!}_{[\pi_{*}f^{*}E/T_{M}]}[C_{M(Y)/M}/T_{M/\mathfrak{M}}]=0^{!}_{\pi_{*}f^{*}E}[C_{M(Y)/M}]\in A_{*}(M(Y)).

Also we have

\iota_{*}[M(Y)]^{\mathrm{vir}}=e(\pi_{*}f^{*}E)\cap[M]\in A_{*}(M)

where
\iota:M(Y)\hookrightarrow M
is the inclusion.
See
[
KKP03
]
for the proof of the general case. We call this phenomenon
A-twist
.

2.2.
Torus localization

We will briefly review some aspect of torus localization
[
GP99
]
in this section. Again we consider the Grassmannian variety
G=\mathrm{Gr}(r,d)
and the stable map space
M=M_{0,k}(G,d)
.
We give a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with weights
-\alpha_{1},\dots,-\alpha_{n}
, which induces the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
and
M
.
Let
M^{F}\subset M
be the fixed locus of the action and let
M^{F}=\bigcup_{i}M^{F}_{i}
be the irreducible decomposition.
Note that
E_{M/\mathfrak{M}}^{\vee}
has an induced
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and we have a decomposition

E_{M/\mathfrak{M}}^{\vee}|_{M^{F}_{i}}\cong N^{\mathrm{fix}}\oplus N^{\mathrm{vir}}

where
N^{\mathrm{fix}}
has weight
0
under the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and
N^{\mathrm{vir}}
is a direct sum of vector bundles with non-zero weights.

Let
(A^{T})_{*}(M)
(resp.
(A^{T})^{*}(M)
) be the equivariant Chow group (resp. equivariant Chow cohomology group) of
M
and
e^{T}(E)
be the equivariant Euler class of a locally free sheaf
E
. If
E_{M}^{\vee}
has locally free resolution
[E_{0}\to E_{1}]
, we define the (equivariant) Euler class by

e^{T}(N^{\mathrm{vir}})=\frac{e^{T}(E_{0}^{m})}{e^{T}(E_{1}^{m})}\in(A^{T})^{*}(M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[t,1/t].

By the virtual localization theorem in
[
GP99
]
, we have

[M]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{i}\frac{[M^{F}_{i}]^{\mathrm{vir}}}{e^{T}(N^{\mathrm{vir}})}\in A^{T}_{*}(M)\cong(A^{T})_{*}(M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[t,1/t].

2.3.
Computation of the virtual normal bundle

For
M=M_{0,k}(G,d)
, we can do more specific computation. In a similar manner as in
[
GP99
]
and
[
HTK+03
]
, which dealt with the case

For a stable map

[

e^{T}(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\dots,x_{k}),\mathcal{O}_{C}))=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathrm{Flags}\\

\displaystyle(\mathrm{H}^{0}-\mathrm{H}^{1})(f^{*}T_{G})

\displaystyle-\bigoplus_{F\in\mathrm{Flags}}T_{p_{i(F)}}G-\bigoplus_{v\in\mathrm{Vertices}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(C_{v},f^{*}T_{G})

Then the equivariant Euler classes of their moving part are given by the followings. For the first term of (

For the fourth term of (

, we have the following. If valency(

)

, then

(c_{1},k)\neq(0,u_{b}),\,(d_{e},u_{a})\end{subarray}}(\frac{c_{1}\alpha_{u_{a}}+c_{2}\alpha_{u_{b}}}{d_{e}}-\alpha_{k}).

2.4.
Computation of GW invariants on Fano
3
-folds

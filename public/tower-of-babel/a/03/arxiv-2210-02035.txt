A Counterexample to a Directed KKL Inequality
Quentin Dubroff, Shivam Nadimpalli, Bhargav Narayanan

Abstract
We show that the natural directed analogues of the KKL theorem [ KKL88 ] and the Eldan–Gross inequality [ EG20 ] from the analysis of Boolean functions fail to hold. This is in contrast to several other isoperimetric inequalities on the Boolean hypercube (such as the Poincaré inequality, Margulis’s inequality [ Mar74 ] and Talagrand’s inequality [ Tal93 ] ) for which directed strengthenings have recently been established.

Introduction

In this note, we consider isoperimetric inequalities over the Boolean hypercube
\{0,1\}^{n}
. Our notation and terminology follow O’Donnell
[
O’D14
]
; in particular, we refer the reader to Chapter 2 of
[
O’D14
]
for further background.

Recall that given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
and an input
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we define the
sensitivity of
f
at
x
as

\mathrm{sens}_{f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\big\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)\neq f(x^{\oplus i})\big\}\qquad\text{where }x^{\oplus i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(x_{1},\ldots,1-x_{i},\ldots,x_{n}).

Two closely related isoperimetric quantities are the
influence of a variable
i\in[n]
on
f
, given by

\mathbf{Inf}_{i}[f]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathop{{\bf Pr}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[f(\boldsymbol{x})\neq f(\boldsymbol{x}^{\oplus i})\right]},

and the
total influence of
f
, given by

\mathbf{I}[f]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{Inf}_{i}[f].

It is easy to check that
\mathbf{I}[f]=\mathop{{\bf E}\/}[\mathrm{sens}_{f}(\boldsymbol{x})]
, and so the total influence of a function is sometimes also referred to as its
average sensitivity
.

To set the stage, we recall perhaps the simplest isoperimetric inequality on the Boolean hypercube, the Poincaré inequality, which says that

\mathbf{I}[f]\geq\operatorname{{\bf Var}}[f].

The follow strengthening of the Poincaré inequality was obtained by Talagrand
[
Tal93
]
, which is known to imply yet another isoperimetric inequality due to Margulis
[
Mar74
]
.

Theorem 1

(Talagrand’s inequality)
.

Given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, we have

\mathop{{\bf E}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\sqrt{\mathrm{sens}_{f}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right]}\geq\Omega{\left(\operatorname{{\bf Var}}[f]\right)}.

An alternative (and incomparable) strengthening of the Poincaré inequality is given by the celebrated Kahn–Kalai–Linial theorem
[
KKL88
]
.

Theorem 2

(KKL inequality)
.

Given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, there exists
i\in[n]
such that

\mathbf{Inf}_{i}[f]\geq\Omega{\left(\operatorname{{\bf Var}}[f]\cdot\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}.

Talagrand
[
Tal97
]
conjectured the following common generalization of
Theorems

1
and
2
, which was proved by Eldan and Gross
[
EG20
]
.

Theorem 3

(Eldan–Gross inequality)
.

Given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, we have

\mathop{{\bf E}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\sqrt{\mathrm{sens}_{f}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right]}\geq\Omega{\left(\operatorname{{\bf Var}}[f]\sqrt{\log{\left(2+\frac{e}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{Inf}_{i}[f]^{2}}\right)}}\right)}.

In this note, we will be concerned with directed versions of such results in the Boolean hypercube. Recall that a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
is said to be
monotone
(resp.
anti-monotone
) if for all
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
x\preccurlyeq y
implies
f(x)\leq f(y)
(resp.
f(x)\geq f(y)
).

x\preccurlyeq y
to mean
x_{i}\leq y_{i}

In connection with the problem of monotonicity testing, Khot, Minzer, and Safra
[
KMS15
]
obtained a “directed” analogue of
Theorem

1
. We write

\mathrm{sens}^{-}_{f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\big\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)>f(x^{\oplus i})\text{ and }x\preccurlyeq x^{\oplus i}\big\}

for the
negative sensitivity of
f
at
x
, and write

\varepsilon(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{g\text{ monotone}}\mathrm{dist}(f,g)\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathrm{dist}(f,g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathop{{\bf Pr}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[f(\boldsymbol{x})\neq g(\boldsymbol{x})\right]}

for the
distance to monotonicity of
f
.

Theorem 4

(Theorem 1.6 of
[
KMS15
]
)
.

Given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, we have

\mathop{{\bf E}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\sqrt{\mathrm{sens}^{-}_{f}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right]}\geq\Omega{\left(\varepsilon(f)\right)}.

Indeed, prior results on monotonicity testing due to Goldreich et al.
[
GGL+00
]
and Chakrabarty and Seshahdri
[
CS16
]
can be viewed as directed analogues of the Poincaré inequality and Margulis’s inequality
[
Mar74
]
respectively. Finally, a directed analogue of an inequality due to Pisier
[
Pis86
]
was obtained by Canonne et al.
[
CCK+21
]
.

Although the directed analogues are known to imply their undirected counterparts (cf. Section 9.4 of
[
KMS15
]
), their proofs bear little resemblance to the proofs in the undirected setting (with the exception of the directed Pisier inequality) and are usually much more involved.

These results suggest an informal analogy between the undirected and the directed cube, with isoperimetric quantities being replaced with their directed counterparts and
\operatorname{{\bf Var}}[f]
being replaced with
\varepsilon(f)
in the latter. Writing

\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\big\{x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)>f(x^{\oplus i})\text{ and }x\preccurlyeq x^{\oplus i}\big\}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}

for the
negative influence of
i
on
f
, we have the following natural directed analog of
Theorem

2
.

Conjecture 1

(Directed KKL inequality)
.

Given a Boolean function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, there exists
i\in[n]
such that

\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]\geq\Omega{\left(\varepsilon(f)\cdot\frac{\log n}{n}\right)}.

2
A Counterexample to Directed KKL

We view
\{0,1\}^{2n}
as
\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}
and construct a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
with

1.

\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]=0
for all
i\in[n]
,

2.

\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]=O(1/n)
for all
i\in[2n]\setminus[n]
, and

3.

\varepsilon(f)=\Omega(1)
,

thereby refuting
1
.

Proof of
Theorem

5
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in{[n]\choose{\log n}}
be drawn independently and uniformly at random. Set

f(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}{\left({\left(\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}x_{j}\right)}\wedge(1-y_{i})\right)}.

We note that this function is closely related to the well-known “Tribes” function due to Ben-Or and Linial
[
BOL85
]
.

It is clear that
f
is monotone in the first
n
coordinates and anti-monotone in the last
n
coordinates; consequently
\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]=0
for all
i\in[n]
. A coordinate
i\in[2n]\setminus[n]
is relevant only on
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
for which
\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}x_{j}=1
; as
|T_{i}|=\log n
, this set has measure at most

\frac{2^{n-\log n}}{2^{n}}=\frac{1}{n}.

It follows that
\mathbf{Inf}^{-}_{i}[f]=O(1/n)
for all
i\in[2n]
.

Before turning to the third item above, we recall the following fact from
[
KMS15
]
without proof.

Lemma 7

(Lemma 3.11 of
[
KMS15
]
)
.

For
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
such that
f
is monotone in the first
n
coordinates and anti-monotone in the last
n
coordinates, we have

\varepsilon(f)=\Theta{\left(\mathop{{\bf E}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\mathop{{\bf Var}\/}_{\boldsymbol{y}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\right]}\right]}\right)}.

Suppose, for convenience, that
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is such that
\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}x_{j}=1
for exactly one
i\in[n]
. Then the restricted function
f(x,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
is simply the anti-dictatorship
(1-y_{i})
, and has
\operatorname{{\bf Var}}{\left[f(x,\cdot)\right]}=\Omega(1)
. We will be done if we can show that this happens for
\Omega(1)
fraction of
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. As before, for fixed
i\in[n]
we have

\mathop{{\bf Pr}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\right]}=\frac{1}{n}\qquad\text{and so}\qquad\mathop{{\bf E}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\#\bigg\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}=1\bigg\}\right]}=1.

By Markov’s inequality, we thus have

\mathop{{\bf Pr}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\#\bigg\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}=1\bigg\}\geq 2\right]}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

We also have

\mathop{{\bf Pr}\/}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\{0,1\}^{n}}{\left[\#\bigg\{i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bigwedge_{j\in T_{i}}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}=1\bigg\}=0\right]}\approx{\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)}^{n}\approx\frac{1}{e},

and so the desired event happens with constant probability, and we are done.
∎

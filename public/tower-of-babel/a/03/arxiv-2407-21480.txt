Categorical properties and homological conjectures for bounded extensions of algebras
Yongyun Qin, Xiaoxiao Xu, Jinbi Zhang* and Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. Following Cibils, Lanzilotta, Marcos, and Solotar, an extension B ⊂ A B\subset A of finite dimensional algebras is bounded if the B B - B B -bimodule A / B A/B is B B -tensor nilpotent, its projective dimension is finite and Tor i B ( A / B , ( A / B ) ⊗ B j ) = 0 {\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0 for all i , j ≥ 1 i,j\geq 1 . We show that for a bounded extension B ⊂ A B\subset A , the algebras A A and B B are singularly equivalent of Morita type with level. Additionally, under mild conditions, their stable categories of Gorenstein projective modules and Gorenstein defect categories are equivalent, respectively. Some homological conjectures are also investigated for bounded extensions, including Auslander-Reiten conjecture, finitistic dimension conjecture, Fg condition, Han’s conjecture, and Keller’s conjecture. Applications to trivial extensions and triangular matrix algebras are given. In course of proof, we give some handy criteria for a functor between module categories to induce triangle functors between stable categories of Gorenstein projective modules and Gorenstein defect categories, which generalise some known criteria, and hence might be of independent interest.

Introduction

Let

{\bf k}
. Denote by
\text{{\rm Mod}}(A)
(resp.
\text{{\rm mod}}(A)

Let

\text{{\rm Mod}}(A)

The

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(A):=\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(A))/{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mathrm{proj}{A}),

has finite global dimension if and only if its singularity category

The singularity category also captures the stable homological features of an algebra
A
(
[
9
]
). Denote by
\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(A)
the stable category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
A
-modules. Following
[

]
, there is a natural triangle functor

which is always fully faithful, and

is an equivalence if and only if

is Gorenstein (see also
[

[

) defined the

as the Verdier quotient

and proved that

. This means that the Gorenstein defect category of

measures how far the algebra

is from being Gorenstein.

The essential image of the functor

is also clear. Denote by
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(A)
the full subcategory of

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(A))
formed by all complexes which are quasi-isomorphic to bounded complexes of Gorenstein projective modules, that is,

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(A)
is the thick subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(A))
generated by

[

The following triangle equivalence

is well known, see, for instance,
[

, Theorem 4.4.1]
,

[
46
, Theorem 4]
,
[

, Lemma 4.1]
, or in a much more general setup
[

.
We will see that the triangulated category

plays an important role in this paper.

Two algebras
A
and
B
are called

provided that there is a triangle equivalence between

and
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(B)

between

and

[

Definition 0.1

(
[
54
]
)

Given two finite dimensional
{\bf k}
-algebras

B

-bimodule and

be a

-bimodule. We say that

with quasi-inverse

This kind of singular equivalences provide rich structural information, and play an important role in the study of singular equivalences and homological properties. For example, it was verified in
[
54

that the finitistic dimension conjecture and Keller’s conjecture for singular Hochschild cohomology are invariant under singular equivalences of Morita type with level. Thus we are interested in constructing singular equivalences of Morita type with level.

In
[
14

, Chen, Liu, and Wang proved that a tensor functor with a bimodule defines a singular equivalence of Morita type with level under certain conditions, and in
[
26

, Dalezios proved that for certain Gorenstein algebras, a singular equivalence induced from tensoring with a complex of bimodules always induces a singular equivalence of Morita type with level. Qin in
[
48

gave a complex version of Chen-Liu-Wang’s work, which extends Dalezios’ result to arbitrary algebras (not limited to Gorenstein algebras).

Given an algebra

0\rightarrow\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{def}}(A)\rightarrow 0.

A natural and fundamental question is: When do two related algebras share the same stable category of Gorenstein projective modules, singularity category and Gorenstein defect category up to triangle equivalence?

Let

.
The first main result we obtain in this paper is as follows.

(Theorems

,

,

{{}_{A}}A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{B}-:\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(B)\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(A):\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}=_{B}A\otimes_{A}-

Criterion 0.4

(Criteria

1.
Bounded extensions

In this section, we introduce the notion of bounded extensions and provide some examples.

Let
B
be a finite dimensional algebra over a fixed field
{\bf k}
and
M
be a
B
-
B
-bimodule. Define
M^{\otimes_{B}0}=B
and
M^{\otimes_{B}(i+1)}=M\otimes_{B}M^{\otimes_{B}i}
for
i\geq 0

is
B
-tensor nilpotent
if
M^{\otimes_{B}p}=0
for some
p\geq 1
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
21
]
)
.

An extension
B\subset A
of finite dimensional algebras is called bounded if

(1)

A/B
is
B
-tensor nilpotent;

(2)

A/B
has finite projective dimension as a
B
-
B
-bimodule;

(3)

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for all
i,j\geq 1
.

Remark 1.2
.

It follows from
[
40
, Proposition 2.4]
that

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for all
i,j\geq 1
if and only if
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A/B)=0
for all
i,j\geq 1
.

Now, we give some examples of extensions which satisfy our definition, but not the definition in
[
23
]
, where it is required that
A/B
is projective as a left or right
B
-module.
More examples can be found in Section
5
.

Let
B
be a finite dimensional algebra and
M
be a finite dimensional
B
-
B
-bimodule.
The trivial extension
A=B\ltimes M
is by definition
B\oplus M
and the multiplication is given
by

(b,m)(b^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(bb^{\prime},mb^{\prime}+bm^{\prime}),b,b^{\prime}\in B,m,m^{\prime}\in M.

The following result is trivial.

Proposition 1.3
.

The trivial extension
B\subset A=B\ltimes M
is a bounded extension if and only if
{\rm pd}(_{B}M_{B})<\infty
,
M^{\otimes_{B}p}=0
for some integer
p
and

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
.

We could produce many examples following the spirit of the above result.

Example 1.4
.

Let
B
be a finite dimensional algebra. Let
{}_{B}U
and

be two modules of finite projective dimension, but not projective. Put

.
Then the trivial extension

is a bounded extension if and only if
{\rm Tor}^{B}_{i}(V,U)=0,\forall i\geq 0
.

2.
Singular equivalences of Morita type with level induced by bounded extensions

In this section, we are concerned about singular equivalences induced by bounded extensions.

For our proof of Theorem
0.3
, we need the following three lemmas whose easy proofs are left to the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
C
be finite dimensional algebras,
M
be an
A
-
B
-bimodule and
N
be a
B
-
C
-bimodule.
If
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,N)=0
for any
i>0
, then
M\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}N\cong M\otimes_{B}N
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(A\otimes_{{\bf k}}C^{\rm op}))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
B\subset A
be an extension of finite dimensional algebras and denote
M=A/B
.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

The functor
A\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}-\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}A:\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(B^{e}))\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(A^{e}))

sends
{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(B^{e}))
to
\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(A^{e}))
.

(2)

If
{\rm pd}(_{B}M_{B})<\infty
, then the functor
{}_{B}M\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}-:\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(B^{e}))\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(B^{e}))

sends
{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(B^{e}))
to
{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(B^{e}))
.

Lemma 2.3

(compare with
[
15
, Section 4]
)
.

Let
B\subset A
be an extension of finite dimensional algebras.
If
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A/B,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
, then the following statements hold:

(1)

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A,A)=0
for each
i\geq 1
;

(2)

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}((A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j},A)=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
;

(3)

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(A,(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}j}\otimes_{B}A)=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
.

The following two lemmas will be crucial in the proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
B\subset A
be an extension of finite dimensional algebras and denote
M=A/B
. Assume that
{\rm pd}(_{B}M_{B})<\infty
and
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for all

.
Then
A\otimes_{B}M^{\otimes_{B}j}\otimes_{B}A\in{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(A^{e}))
for any

.

Proof.

Since
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{B}(M,M^{\otimes_{B}j})=0
for each
i,j\geq 1
,
it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

M\otimes_{B}M\otimes_{B}\cdots\otimes_{B}M\cong M\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}M\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}\cdots\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}M\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(B^{e})),

which belongs to
{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(B^{e}))
by
{\rm pd}(_{B}M_{B})<\infty
and by Lemma
2.2
(2).
Therefore,
M^{\otimes_{B}j}\in{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(B^{e}))
for any
j\geq 1
,
and it follows from Lemma
2.2
(1) that

A\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}M^{\otimes_{B}j}\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}A\in{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(A^{e})).

On the other hand, Lemma
2.3

{\rm pd}(X_{\Lambda})<\infty
, a suitable shift of
F:=X\otimes_{\Lambda}^{{\mathbb{L}}}-
is nonnegative in the sense of
[
34
, Definition 4.1]
. By
[
34
, Theorem 5.3]
,
F
restricts to the stable categories of Gorenstein
projective modules.
∎

The condition that
X^{{\rm tr}_{\Gamma}}=\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)
is perfect as complex of
\Lambda
-modules is somehow strong. We will introduce two alternative conditions in the sequel; see Criteria
3.8
and
3.12
.

We need several notations.
For an algebra
A
, we denote by
\Omega_{A}(-)
the syzygy
endofunctor of the stable module category of
A
. Define

{}^{\perp}A:=\{X\in\text{{\rm mod}}(A)\ |\ {\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,A)=0\mbox{ for all }i>0\}.

Notice that a module in this subcategory is called
semi-Gorenstein projective
by Ringel and Zhang
[
51
]
.
Denote

{\rm Gproj}(A)^{\bot}:=\{X\in\text{{\rm mod}}(A)\mid{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(U,X)=0\mbox{ for all }U\in\mbox{{\rm Gproj}}(A),\,i\geq 1\}.

The second criterion concerns a right exact functor between module categories which, under certain conditions, sends Gorenstein projective modules to Gorenstein projective modules.

Criterion 3.3
.

If
{}_{\Gamma}X
is projective and
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
, then
X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma)
sends Gorenstein projective modules to Gorenstein projective modules and
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U\cong X\otimes_{\Lambda}^{\mathbb{L}}U\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma))
for any
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
. In this case, there is an induced triangle functor

X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
. By definition, there is a complete resolution
P^{\bullet}
of
U
.
It follows from
X\in\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Gamma)
and
P^{i}\in\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Lambda)
that
{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}P_{i}\in\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Gamma)
for each
i
. As
P^{\bullet}
is exact, we see that
{\mathrm{H}}^{i}(X\otimes_{\Lambda}P^{\bullet})={\rm Tor}_{j-i}^{\Lambda}(X,B^{j+1}(P^{\bullet}))
for any
j>i
. Since the projective dimension of
X
as right
\Lambda
-module is assumed to be finite, we can always choose
j
sufficiently big so that this
{\rm Tor}
group vanishes. Thus
X\otimes_{\Lambda}P^{\bullet}
is exact, which also shows that
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{A}(X,U)=0
for
i>0
. By Lemma
2.1
,
{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}U\cong{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}U\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\Lambda)
for
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
.
Note that

{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X\otimes_{\Lambda}P^{\bullet},\Gamma)\cong{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(P^{\bullet},{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)).

It follows from
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
that
{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(P^{\bullet},{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma))
and
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X\otimes_{\Lambda}P^{\bullet},\Gamma)
are exact.
Thus
X\otimes_{\Lambda}P^{\bullet}
is a complete resolution of
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U
, and therefore
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U
is a Gorenstein projective
\Gamma
-module.

For the second statement, it remains to show that the additive functor
X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Gamma)
is a triangle functor.
Indeed, due to
{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}U\cong{}_{\Lambda}X\otimes_{\Gamma}U\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\Lambda)
for
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
,
the functor
{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}-
induces a triangle functor from
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)
to
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Lambda)
. We have thus a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}-}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Gamma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Lambda)/{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Lambda))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}^{\mathbb{L}}-}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)/{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Gamma))}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(\Lambda)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}^{\mathbb{L}}-}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(\Gamma)}

Thus
X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mbox{{\rm{\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}}}(\Gamma)
is a triangle functor.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Although Criterion
3.3
is somehow trivial, it generalises
[
44
, Proposition 3.4]
in which the authors consider the situation where there is a ring homomorphism
f:\Lambda\to\Gamma
and
X={}_{\Gamma}\Gamma_{\Lambda}
.

Remark 3.5
.

In the hypotheses of Criterion
3.3
, if we assume that
{}_{\Gamma}X
is projective, then
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)

\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
is equivalent to saying that
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U
belongs to
{}^{\perp}\Gamma
whenever
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
. In fact, for
G\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
and a deleted projective resolution
Q^{\bullet}
of

,

X\otimes_{\Lambda}Q^{\bullet}
is exact which is a projective resolution of
X\otimes_{\Lambda}G
. By the isomorphism of complexes

{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(Q^{\bullet},{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma))\cong{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X\otimes_{\Lambda}Q^{\bullet},\Gamma),

for any
i>0
, there is an isomorphism

{\rm Ext}_{\Lambda}^{i}(G,{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma))\cong{\rm Ext}_{\Gamma}^{i}(X\otimes_{\Lambda}G,\Gamma).

The equivalence deduces from the above isomorphisms.

The following example verifies the hypothesis of Criterion
3.3
, but not that of Criterion
3.2
.

Example 3.6
.

[
32
, 2 Example]

Let
\Gamma
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra and
\Lambda=kQ/I
be the algebra where

is the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}

and

. Let

. It is clearly that

are projective, and

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite dimensional
{\bf k}
-algebra and
M\in\text{{\rm mod}}(\Sigma)
. If
M
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex with each term in
{\rm Gproj}(\Sigma)^{\bot}
, then
M\in{\rm Gproj}(\Sigma)^{\bot}
.

Proof.

Let
M
quasi-isomorphic to

L^{\bullet}:0\rightarrow L^{s}\rightarrow L^{s+1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow L^{t}\rightarrow 0

with
L^{j}\in{\rm Gproj}(\Sigma)^{\bot},\forall j
.
For any
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Sigma)
, let
(Q^{\bullet},\partial^{\bullet})
be a complete resolution of
U
with
U=\ker(\partial^{0})
. Let
K^{i}:=\ker(\partial^{i})
. Then
K^{i}\in\mbox{{\rm Gproj}}(\Sigma)
. We have

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}_{\Sigma}^{p}(U,M)

\displaystyle={\rm Ext}_{\Sigma}^{j+p}(K^{j},M)

\displaystyle={\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Sigma))}(K^{j},M[j+p])

\displaystyle\cong{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Sigma))}(K^{j},L^{\bullet}[j+p]).

Due to
K^{j}\in\mbox{{\rm Gproj}}(\Sigma)
,
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{\Sigma}(K^{j},L^{q})=0
for
i\geq 1
and
s\leq q\leq t
. By
[
37
, Lemma 1.6]
,
{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Sigma))}(K^{j},L^{\bullet}[j+p])=0
for
j\geq t-p+1
. Then
{\rm Ext}_{\Sigma}^{p}(U,M)=0
for each
p\geq 1
, that is,
M\in{\rm Gproj}(\Sigma)^{\bot}
.
∎

The following result, which is our third criterion, can be considered as a derived version of Criterion
3.3
.

Criterion 3.8
.

Assume that
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex with each term in
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Then the functor
X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma)
induces
X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}-:\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Lemma 2.8]
, the functor
X\otimes_{\Lambda}-:\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma)
induces

X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}-:\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda))\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma)).

It suffices to prove that
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)
for any
U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)
.

Consider a projective resolution
P^{\bullet}
of
X
as
\Gamma\otimes_{k}\Lambda^{{\rm op}}
-module:

\cdots\rightarrow P^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i+1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{0}\rightarrow{{}_{\Gamma}X_{\Lambda}}\rightarrow 0

with
P^{1}={{}_{\Gamma}X_{\Lambda}}
. Denote

P_{\leq 0}^{\bullet}:=(\cdots\rightarrow P^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i+1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{0}\rightarrow 0).

It follows from
{\rm pd}(X_{\Lambda})<\infty
that
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{\Lambda}(X,U)=0
for any
i>0
and any
U\in\mbox{{\rm Gproj}}(\Lambda)
. Indeed, this can
be proved by applying
{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes_{\Lambda}-
to the complete projective resolution of
U
and doing dimension shifting.
Thus the complex
P^{\bullet}\otimes_{\Lambda}U

is exact, and

{{}_{\Gamma}X}\otimes_{\Lambda}U\cong{}_{\Gamma}X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}U\cong P^{\bullet}_{\leq 0}\otimes_{\Lambda}U
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Gamma))
by Lemma
2.1
.

Let
l:=\mathrm{max}\{{\rm pd}(_{\Gamma}X),{\rm pd}(X_{\Lambda})\}
and
N:=\ker(d^{-l+1})
. Then both
{}_{\Gamma}N
and
N_{\Lambda}
are projective. Thus
{{}_{\Gamma}X}\otimes_{\Lambda}U
is quasi-isomorphic to

0\rightarrow N\otimes_{\Lambda}U\rightarrow P^{-l+1}\otimes_{A}U\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\otimes_{\Lambda}U\rightarrow P^{0}\otimes_{\Lambda}U\rightarrow 0,

where
P^{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}U
is a projective
\Gamma
-module for
-l+1\leq i\leq 0
. We will prove that
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma)\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
, and then by Criterion
3.3
, it follows
N\otimes_{\Lambda}U\in{\rm Gproj}(\Gamma)
. Consequently,
X\otimes_{\Lambda}U
is quasi-isomorphic to a complex in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)
. This shows that

X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}-
sends a Gorenstein projective module into
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\rm fGd}(\Gamma)
, thereby proving the statement.

It remains to prove
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma)\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Indeed, denote

P_{[-l+1,0]}^{\bullet}:=(0\rightarrow P^{-l+1}\rightarrow P^{-l+2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{0}\rightarrow 0).

Applying the functor
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(-,\Gamma):\mathrm{D}(\Gamma\otimes_{{\bf k}}\Lambda^{\rm op})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(\Lambda)
to the triangle in
\mathrm{D}(\Gamma\otimes_{{\bf k}}\Lambda^{\rm op})
:

N[l-1]\rightarrow P^{\bullet}_{[-l+1,0]}\rightarrow X\rightarrow N[l],

we obtain the following triangle in
\mathrm{D}(\Lambda)
:

\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(P^{\bullet}_{[-l+1,0]},\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma[-l+1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)[1].

Since
P^{i}
is a projective
\Gamma\otimes_{{\bf k}}\Lambda^{\rm op}
-module for each
-l+1\leq i\leq 0
,
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(P^{i},\Gamma)\in\mathrm{inj}(\Lambda)
and
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(P^{\bullet}_{[-l+1,0]},\Gamma)\cong{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(P^{\bullet}_{[-l+1,0]},\Gamma)\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda))
has all terms lying in
\mathrm{inj}(\Lambda)
.
By assumption,
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)
has all terms in
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Since
\mathrm{inj}(\Lambda)\subseteq{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
, it follows that
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma[-l+1])
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex
with each term in
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Since
N
is projective left
\Gamma
-module,
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma)\cong\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma)
in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda))
. By Lemma
3.7
,
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(N,\Gamma)\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

By the definition of Gorenstein modules, we have
\Lambda\in{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
, and therefore
{\rm proj}(\Lambda)
is contained within
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Consequently, if
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)
is perfect as complex of
\Lambda
-modules, then
\mathbb{R}{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,\Gamma)
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex with each term in
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
. Thus Criterion
3.8
generalises Criterion
3.2
.

However, when
\Lambda
is Gorenstein,
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
is exactly the subcategory of modules of finite projective dimension. In this case, the thick subcategory generated by
{\rm Gproj}(\Lambda)^{\bot}
within
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\text{{\rm mod}}(\Lambda))
is exactly
{\mathrm{K}}^{b}(\mbox{{\rm proj}}(\Lambda))
. Thus, under the Gorenstein condition, the two criteria coincide.

Note that
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}={{}_{B}A}\otimes_{A}-

.
Now we claim that there is an integer

Let us show the claim that there is an integer

such that

Since

4.
Homological conjectures for bounded extensions

In this section, we use the results of Sections
2
and
3
to deal with several homological conjectures for bounded extensions, which are still open. They are stated as follows.

The finitistic dimension conjecture
[
6
]
:
The finitistic dimension of
A
which is the supremum of the projective dimensions of finitely generated
A
-modules with finite projective dimension, is finite.

Han’s conjecture
[
33
]
:
If the Hochschild homology groups
\mathrm{HH}_{n}(A)
vanish for all sufficiently large
n
, then its global dimension is finite.

Keller’s conjecture
[
38
]
:
Let
{\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A)
be the dg enhancement of the singularity category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{sg}}(A)
. Then there is an isomorphism in the homotopy category of
B_{\infty}
-algebras

{\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{{\mathrm{sg}}}(A,A)\cong{\mathrm{C}}^{*}({\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A),{\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A)),

where
{\mathrm{C}}^{*}_{{\mathrm{sg}}}(A,A)
is the singular Hochschild cohomology complex
[
55
]
of
A
, and
{\mathrm{C}}^{*}({\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A),{\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A))
is the Hochschild cochain complex of
{\bf S}_{{\rm dg}}(A)
.

Auslander and Reiten’s Conjecture
[
4
]
:
A finitely generated
A
-module
X
satisfying
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,X\oplus A)=0
for all
i\geq 1
must be projective.

From
[
53
,
28
]
, an algebra
A
is said to satisfy
the Fg condition
if the
Hochschild cohomology ring
HH^{*}(A)
is Noetherian
and the Yoneda algebra
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{*}(A/{\rm rad}A,A/{\rm rad}A)
is a finitely generated

HH^{*}(A)
-module. The Fg condition enables developing support variety theory via Hochschild cohomology
[
53
,
28
]
.

Note that for Han’s conjecture the following result was firstly proved by Cibils, Lanzilotta, Marcos, and Solotar for left or right bounded extensions in
[
23
]
generalising their previous results in
[
20
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
B\subset A
be a bounded extension. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

B
satisfies the finitistic dimension conjecture if and only if so does
A
.

(2)

B
satisfies Han’s conjecture if and only if so does
A
.

(3)

B
satisfies Keller’s conjecture if and only if so does
A
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
, we know that
A
and
B
are singularly equivalent of Morita type with level, which induces an equivalence of singularity categories, thus the finiteness of the global dimension is preserved.
Note that this equivalence
preserves the finitistic dimension conjecture
[
54
]
, Hochschild homology
[
54
]
and Keller’s conjecture
[
13
]
. Thus the statements
(1)-(3) follow.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

Theorem
4.1
(2) extends the main result presented in
[
23
]
from left or right bounded extensions to bounded extensions. Additionally, the approach employed to establish Theorem

(2) differs from that adopted in

, where the Jacobi-Zariski long nearly exact sequence is utilized

.

(2)

More results about Han’s conjecture for Morita context algebras are obtained in

In a forthcoming paper we will study Han’s conjecture from the viewpoint of recollements and ladders of derived categories with applications to Morita context algebras in mind.

(3)

In 2021, Iusenko and MacQuarrie introduced a different generalization of left or right bounded extensions, termed the strongly proj-bounded extensions (see

Abstract
f ⁡ ( P ) f(P) gravity is a novel extension of ECG in which the Ricci scalar in the action is replaced by a function of the curvature invariant P P which represents the contractions of the Riemann tensor at the cubic order p . The present work is concentrated on bounding some f ⁡ ( P ) f(P) gravity models using the concept of energy conditions where the functional forms of f ⁡ ( P ) f(P) are represented as a) f ⁡ ( P ) = α ​ P f(P)=\alpha\sqrt{P} , and b) f ⁡ ( P ) = α ​ exp ⁡ ( P ) f(P)=\alpha\exp(P) , where α \alpha is the sole model parameter. Energy conditions are interesting linear relationships between pressure and density and have been extensively employed to derive interesting results in Einstein’s gravity, and are also an excellent tool to impose constraints on any cosmological model. To place the bounds, we ensured that the energy density must remain positive, the pressure must remain negative, and the EoS parameter must attain a value close to − 1 -1 to make sure that the bounds respect the accelerated expansion of the Universe and are also in harmony with the latest observational data. We report that for both the models, suitable parameter spaces exist which satisfy the aforementioned conditions and therefore posit the f ⁡ ( P ) f(P) theory of gravity to be a promising modified theory of gravitation.

Conclusions

Einsteinian cubic gravity (ECG)
p31

f(P)
gravity is a novel extension of ECG in which the Ricci scalar in the action is replaced by a function of the curvature invariant
P
which represents the contractions of the Riemann tensor at the cubic order
p
. This class of modified gravity theory have been very successful in several cosmological domains such as inflation
p35

p32
;
p33
;
p34

p
;
2p

The present work is concentrated on bounding some
f(P)
gravity models to understand their viability in cosmology. Energy conditions are interesting linear relationships between pressure and density and have been extensively employed to derive interesting results in Einstein’s gravity, and are also an excellent tool to impose constraints on any cosmological model. To place the bounds, we have ensured that the energy density (Eq.
11
) must remain positive and the pressure (Eq.
12
) must be negative to assure that the constraints on
f(P)
gravity models are compatible with the current accelerated expansion of the Universe, and have also investigated to find corners in parameter spaces for a chosen model which permit the EoS parameter (Eq.
13

-1
to ensure that the bounds are in harmony with the current observational data.

In this work, we report bounds for two specific

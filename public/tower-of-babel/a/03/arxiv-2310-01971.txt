A second-order sequential optimality condition for nonlinear second-order cone programming problems Thanks: This is a pre-print of an article published in Computational Optimization and Applications. The version of record is available online at: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10589-025-00649-0 .
Ellen H. FukudaKosuke Okabe

Abstract
In the last two decades, the sequential optimality conditions, which do not require constraint qualifications and allow improvement on the convergence assumptions of algorithms, had been considered in the literature. It includes the work by Andreani et al. (2017), with a sequential optimality condition for nonlinear programming, that uses the second-order information of the problem. More recently, Fukuda et al. (2023) analyzed the conditions that use second-order information, in particular for nonlinear second-order cone programming problems (SOCP). However, such optimality conditions were not defined explicitly. In this paper, we propose an explicit definition of approximate-Karush-Kuhn-Tucker 2 (AKKT2) and complementary-AKKT2 (CAKKT2) conditions for SOCPs. We prove that the proposed AKKT2/CAKKT2 conditions are satisfied at local optimal points of the SOCP without any constraint qualification. We also present two algorithms that are based on augmented Lagrangian and sequential quadratic programming methods and show their global convergence to points satisfying the proposed conditions. Keywords: Optimality conditions, second-order optimality, second-order cone programming, conic optimization.



1
Introduction

We consider the following nonlinear second-order cone programming problem (SOCP):

\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&{f(x)}\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}&g_{i}(x)\in\mathcal{K}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,r,\\
&h(x)=0,\end{array}

(SOCP)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p},\ g_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}},\ i=1,\dots,r
are twice continuously differentiable and
\mathcal{K}_{i}
denotes the
m_{i}
dimensional second-order (or Lorentz) cone, which is defined as

\mathcal{K}_{i}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(w_{0},\overline{w})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}-1}\mid w_{0}\geq\|\overline{w}\|\},&\mbox{if }m_{i}>1,\\
\{w\in\mathbb{R}\mid w\geq 0\},&\mbox{if }m_{i}=1,\\
\end{array}\right.

with
\|\cdot\|
denoting the Euclidean norm. We write the Cartesian product of Lorentz cones by
\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{K}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{K}_{r}
. Also,
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given by
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{r})
where
m=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}
. Thus, the conic constraints can be written simply as
g(x)\in\mathcal{K}
.

The nonlinear SOCP is a particular case of nonlinear symmetric cone programming problem and has a wide range of applications, for instance, in finance, control theory, information theory, and others
[
31
]
. Because of its practical importance, the optimization community has studied its related theory
[
17
,
4
]
and proposed various algorithms for nonlinear SOCPs. Some examples of such algorithms are the sequential quadratic programming (SQP) method
[
27
]
, the augmented Lagrangian
[
30
]
, the exact penalty
[
19
]
, and the interior point method
[
38
]
. Most of these methods generate iterates with limit points satisfying
the first-order optimality conditions.

To solve SOCP, we often aim to find points that satisfy the so-called necessary optimality conditions, which local minimizers of the problem should fulfill. The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are the most well-known first-order necessary optimality conditions, and are satisfied at local optima under some regularity condition which is called constraint qualification (CQ). This means that in the conic context, we usually need to assume, for instance, the Robinson CQ or the nondegeneracy CQ. For optimization problems that lack CQs, it is not possible to guarantee the existence of Lagrange multipliers that satisfy the KKT conditions, and hence it is generally difficult for standard optimization methods to solve them.

Recently, unlike the classical KKT, conditions that use sequences of points and Lagrange multipliers, called sequential optimality conditions have been proposed. One of such conditions is the Approximate KKT (AKKT) conditions
[
3
]
. There are two attractive properties for sequential optimality conditions. First, they are necessary optimality conditions without the requirement of CQs. Second, they have clear relation with the classical optimality conditions, in the sense that they are equivalent when a CQ holds.
In particular, a second-order sequential optimality condition called AKKT2, which incorporates second-order information into the AKKT conditions and is therefore stronger than AKKT, was introduced in the context of NLP
[
6
]
.
It was also shown that some algorithms generate AKKT2 points. However, it has not been fully extended to SOCP yet.
A second-order analysis for SOCP is more complicated than for NLP, since we should consider the curvature of the second-order cone. Second-order analyses in the conic context were first done by
[
28
]
, and later generalized by
[
15
]
. Then, second-order necessary optimality conditions for SOCP were proposed in
[
12
]
. We refer to
[
13
]
for details about optimality conditions for SOCP and general conic optimization. Moreover, the weak second-order necessary condition (WSONC)
[
7
]
was extended to SOCP
[
18
]
. Compared to the classical (or basic) second-order necessary condition (BSONC), the WSONC is more practical. In fact, it is known that some algorithms may produce sequences whose limit points fail to satisfy BSONC
[
9
,
22
]
, whereas WSONC can be fulfilled under weaker assumptions
[
1
,
32
]
. Although second-order analysis for conic programming was considered in these papers, there is still no formal definition of AKKT2 for those problems.

For this reason, in this paper, we formally define the second-order sequential optimality condition AKKT2 for SOCP extending the AKKT2 for NLP. As the AKKT2 for NLP, we prove three properties that are desirable for a second-order sequential optimality condition. First, a local minimum satisfies the condition under a reasonable assumption. Second, if an AKKT2 point fulfills some CQs, it also satisfies WSONC. Third, there exist algorithms that generate AKKT2 points. In particular, we show that with small modifications, an augmented Lagrangian method and a sequential quadratic programming method generate AKKT2 points under appropriate assumptions. We also see that replacing AKKT with another stronger sequential optimality condition called complementary-AKKT (CAKKT)
[
8
]
into our definition of AKKT2 yields the so-called CAKKT2, which generalizes
[
23
]
. Then, similar results are obtained with the new CAKKT2. The main contribution here, compared to the work
[
18
]
on second-order optimality conditions for conic problems, can be summarized as follows:

•

Without an explicit definition of AKKT2, it may be difficult to propose and analyze algorithms that generate AKKT2 points. Here, differently from
[
18
]
, we are able to provide those algorithms.

•

In
[
18
]
, by assuming Robinson CQ and the weak constant rank property (WCR), it is proved that a local minimum satisfies WSONC. Here, since we have an explicit definition of AKKT2 we first (a) prove under an assumption that is not CQ that a local minimum satisfies AKKT2, and then, (b) assuming Robinson CQ and WCR, it satisfies WSONC. The proof then becomes more complicated here because we are not assuming WCR in (a), which was crucial in the proof given in
[
18
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some notations and some basic results which will be useful for our work. In Section
3
, we review the existing second-order analyses for SOCP. In Section
4
, we propose a new second-order sequential optimality condition for SOCP and discuss its properties. In Section
5
, we propose algorithms that generate points that fulfill the proposed condition. In Section
6
we give some conclusions.

3
Existing optimality conditions

In this section, we review the existing optimality conditions of SOCP. We start with the following first-order optimality conditions.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the KKT conditions if
x
is a feasible point of (
SOCP
) and there exist
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\,:=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})}\in\mathcal{K}
which satisfy

\nabla_{x}L(x,\mu,\omega)=0,\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\langle g_{i}(x),\omega_{i}\rangle=0\quad\mbox{for all }i=1,\dots,r,}

where
L(x,\mu,\omega):=f(x)+\langle h(x),\mu\rangle-\langle g(x),\omega\rangle
.

We recall that
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{K}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Lagrange function and
\mu
and
\omega
are the Lagrange multipliers associated with the equality and the conic constraints, respectively. If
(x,\mu,\omega)
satisfies the KKT conditions, we say that
x
is a KKT point. This is one of the most usual necessary conditions for optimality, however, the KKT conditions do not necessarily hold at a local minimum where constraint qualifications (CQ) are violated. One example of CQ is given in the definition below.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a feasible point of (
SOCP
). We say that it satisfies Robinson CQ if
Dh(x)
has full row rank and there exists some
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

g_{i}(x)+Dg_{i}(x)d\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{K}_{i}),\quad Dh(x)d=0,

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

Note that Robinson CQ is a generalization of the Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) for NLP. This CQ requires the feasible set of the problem to be similar to its first-order approximation around
x
. This restriction does not necessarily hold, which makes the KKT conditions not always suitable.

To overcome the above problem, sequential optimality conditions have been proposed in the literature. These are alternative optimality conditions that do not require constraint qualifications. The most popular of these conditions, the Approximate KKT (AKKT), is introduced in
[
3
]
for nonlinear programming and extended to SOCP in
[
2
]
. We introduce the AKKT conditions for (
SOCP
) below.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the AKKT conditions if
x^{*}
is a feasible point of (
SOCP
) and there exist sequences
\{x^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\{\mu^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\{\omega^{k}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
which satisfy

\displaystyle\underset{k\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}x^{k}=x^{*},\ \underset{k\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}\nabla_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})=0,

(3.1)

\displaystyle i\in I_{I}(x^{*})\Rightarrow\omega^{k}_{i}\to 0\ \mathrm{and}

\displaystyle i\in I_{B}(x^{*})\Rightarrow\omega^{k}_{i}\to 0\ \mathrm{or}\ \omega^{k}_{i}\in\mathrm{bd}^{+}(\mathcal{K}_{i})\ \mathrm{with}\ \frac{\overline{\omega^{k}_{i}}}{\|\overline{\omega^{k}_{i}}\|}\to-\frac{\overline{g_{i}(x^{*})}}{\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{*})}\|},

where
g_{i}(x^{*}):=([g_{i}(x^{*})]_{0},\overline{g_{i}(x^{*})})
and
\omega_{i}^{k}:=([\omega_{i}^{k}]_{0},\overline{\omega_{i}^{k}})\in\mathcal{K}_{i}
.

It was shown that a local minimizer of (
SOCP
) satisfies the AKKT conditions and some practical algorithms can generate an AKKT point. Moreover, AKKT implies KKT if Robinson CQ is satisfied (see
[
2
]
).

Another well-known sequential optimality condition is the so-called complementary-AKKT (CAKKT). Its definition is similar to that of AKKT,
except that the conditions involving the limits in (
3.1
) remain unchanged, while the rest is replaced by

\displaystyle g_{i}(x^{k})\circ\omega^{k}_{i}\to 0

\displaystyle\quad i=1,\dots,r,

\displaystyle h_{i}(x^{k})\mu_{i}^{k}\to 0

\displaystyle\quad i=1,\dots,p,

where
g_{i}(x)\circ\omega_{i}
is the Jordan product of
g_{i}(x)
and
\omega_{i}
, i.e.,
g_{i}(x)\circ\omega_{i}:=(\langle g_{i}(x),\omega_{i}\rangle,[g_{i}(x)]_{0}\overline{\omega_{i}}+[\omega_{i}]_{0}\overline{g_{i}(x)})
.
The above definition is defined for nonlinear programming in
[
8
]
and extended for SOCP problems in
[
2
]
. Like the AKKT, local minimizes of (
SOCP
) also satisfies CAKKT, and practical SOCP algorithms can be shown to generate CAKKT points
[
2
,
33
]
. Also, in
[
2
, Section 3]
, it was shown that CAKKT implies AKKT while the converse is not true.

We now state the following result that shows the relation of AKKT (and CAKKT) and a penalty function
[
2
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local minimizer of (
SOCP
). Then, for any given sequence
\{\rho_{k}\}\to+\infty
, there exists a sequence
\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
such that each
x^{k}
is a local minimizer of the penalty function:

F_{k}(x):=f(x)+\frac{1}{4}\|x-x^{*}\|^{4}+\frac{\rho_{k}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x))\|^{2}+\|h(x)\|^{2}\right).

(3.2)

Also, letting
\mu^{k}:=\rho_{k}h(x^{k})
and
\omega^{k}_{i}:=\rho_{k}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))
, then
(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})
satisfies both the AKKT and the CAKKT conditions.

As a way to obtain more information, we can consider the second-order conditions of the problem. To discuss second-order conditions of SOCP, we introduce some concepts associated with second-order analyses. First, we define the weak constant rank (WCR) property that will be necessary to introduce second-order conditions.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that the weak constant rank (WCR) property holds at a feasible point
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of (
SOCP
) if there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
x^{*}
such that

\{\nabla h_{i}(x)\}_{\{i=1,\dots,p\}}\>\bigcup\>\{\nabla g_{ij}(x)\}_{i\in I_{0}(x^{*}),\>j\in\{1,\dots,m_{i}\}}\>\bigcup\>\{Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x)\}_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}

has the same rank for all
x\in\mathcal{N}
, where
\tilde{g}_{i}(x):=(\|\overline{g_{i}(x)}\|,\overline{g_{i}(x)})
and

\Gamma_{i}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\\
0&-I_{m_{i}-1}\end{array}\right)

(3.3)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

When (
SOCP
) is indeed an NLP, i.e.,
m_{i}=1
for all
i
, the above WCR property becomes equivalent to the WCR of NLP introduced in
[
7
]
. Note also that WCR is not a CQ on its own
[
7
, Exampple 5.1]
. Moreover, a well-known CQ for conic optimization is nondegeneracy, which can be seen as a generalization of the linear independence CQ (LICQ) for NLP problems, as it also implies the existence of a unique Lagrange multiplier.
From
[
18
]
, we can note that nondegeneracy implies Robinson CQ and WCR, a property that also holds in NLP. Furthermore, there exist examples of points satisfying Robinson CQ and WCR but not nondegeneracy. Therefore, the joint condition “Robinson CQ and WCR” is strictly weaker than nondegeneracy
[
7
, Example 5.2]
.

The sigma-term introduced in
[
15
]
, which represents a possible curvature of
\mathcal{K}
at
g(x)
, is important to consider second-order conditions of SOCP. We define the sigma-term associated to
x^{*}
as follows:

\sigma(x,\omega):=\sum_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\sigma_{i}(x,\omega),

(3.4)

where

\sigma_{i}(x,\omega):=-\frac{[\omega_{i}]_{0}}{[g_{i}(x)]_{0}}Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}Dg_{i}(x)\quad\mathrm{for\ all\ }i\in I_{B}(x^{*}).

Note that the term
\sigma(x,\omega)
does not appear in NLP. In fact, the difficulty of generalization of second-order analyses from NLP to SOCP arises from this sigma-term.

As a classical second-order condition, we state the basic second-order necessary condition (BSONC), which is satisfied if Robinson CQ holds at a local minimum
x^{*}
of SOCP as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a feasible point of (
SOCP
). We say that
x^{*}
satisfies BSONC if for every
d\in C(x^{*})
there are Lagrange multipliers
\mu^{*}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\omega^{*}_{d}\in\mathcal{K}
such that
(x^{*},\mu^{*}_{d},\omega^{*}_{d})
is a KKT triple and

d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}(\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x^{*},\mu^{*}_{d},\omega^{*}_{d})+\sigma(x^{*},\omega^{*}_{d}))d\geq 0,

where
C(x^{*})
is the critical cone of (
SOCP
) at
x^{*}
.

The critical cone of (
SOCP
) at
x^{*}
is denoted by

C(x^{*}):=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\nabla f(x^{*}),d\rangle=0;Dh(x^{*})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}d=0;\\
Dg_{i}(x^{*})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}d\in T_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(g_{i}(x^{*}))\>\forall i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\end{array}\right\},

where

T_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(g_{i}(x^{*})):=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\{d^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\to d,\exists\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},\alpha_{k}\to 0,\\
\forall k\in\mathbb{N},g_{i}(x^{*})+\alpha_{k}d^{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{i}\end{array}\right\},

is the tangent cone of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
at
g_{i}(x^{*})
. One of the disadvantages of BSONC is that we must find
\mu^{*}_{d}
and
\omega^{*}_{d}
which rely on
d
for all
d\in C(x^{*})
, which is not always practical to calculate. Moreover, there is an example that practical algorithms fail to find a BSONC point even in a simple case
[
22
, Section 2]
.

A more practical optimality condition than BSONC is the weak second-order necessary condition (WSONC), which is defined as follows.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(x^{*},\mu^{*},\omega^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{K}
be a KKT triple. We say that
x^{*}
satisfies WSONC if

d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}(\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x^{*},\mu^{*},\omega^{*})+\sigma(x^{*},\omega^{*}))d\geq 0

for all
d\in S(x^{*})
where

S(x^{*}):=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}Dh(x^{*})d=0;Dg_{i}(x^{*})d=0,i\in I_{0}(x^{*});\\
g_{i}(x^{*})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}Dg_{i}(x^{*})d=0,i\in I_{B}(x^{*})\end{array}\right\}.

Note that
S(x^{*})
is equivalent to the largest subspace contained in
C(x^{*})
, namely,
S(x^{*})=C(x^{*})\cap-C(x^{*})
. Since Lagrange multipliers in WSONC are independent of
d\in S(x^{*})
, it is easier to verify than BSONC.
While some authors question the existence of an algorithm that considers the entire critical cone
C(x^{*})
, WSONC has been shown to be satisfied under weaker assumptions, for example, for barrier-type and augmented Lagrangian methods
[
1
,
32
]
. Although WSONC is a more practical condition, there is still a restriction related to CQs. In fact, Robinson CQ alone is not enough to guarantee fulfillment of WSONC at a local minimizer of SOCP
[
10
]
. Therefore, WSONC requires a stronger CQ such as nondegeneracy or LICQ as an optimality condition. Recently, it has been shown that the strictly weaker condition “Robinson CQ and WCR” is enough for the purpose
[
18
, Theorem 6]
, which is a natural extension of the result in NLP
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

4
Second-order sequential optimality conditions

In this section, we extend concepts of sequential second-order optimality conditions for NLP to SOCP. As it was mentioned before, the condition should have three properties: (i) it is an optimality condition that does not require CQ, (ii) it implies WSONC for a weak constraint qualification, (iii) its validity can be verified in sequences generated by practical algorithms. We define our sequential second-order optimality condition for SOCP as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that the feasible point
x^{*}
satisfies the AKKT2 conditions if there exist sequences
\mathbb{R}^{n}\supset\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
,
\{\mu^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\{\omega^{k}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
satisfying the AKKT conditions and, in addition,
there exist
\{\eta^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p},\ \{\theta_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in I_{0}(x^{*})}\subset\mathbb{R},\{\gamma_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}\supset\{\delta^{k}\}\to 0
such that

\displaystyle d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\left(\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})+\sigma(x^{k},\omega^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta^{k}_{i}\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\gamma_{i}^{k}(Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))(Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I_{0}(x^{*})}\theta_{i}^{k}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}Dg_{i}(x^{k})+\delta_{k}I_{n}\Bigg)d\geq 0\quad

for all
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
k
is sufficiently large, and where
\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}):=\big(\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|,\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\big)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}
.

We call
\{x^{k}\}
satisfying the conditions of Definition
4.1
an AKKT2 sequence. We can easily show that AKKT2 for (
SOCP
) is a generalization of AKKT2 for nonlinear programming
[
6
, Definition 3.1]
, considering
I_{0}(x^{*})
as the set of active indices at
x^{*}
and
I_{B}(x^{*})=\emptyset
. We point out that
\delta_{k}I_{n}
in the definition can be replaced with
\Delta_{k}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0}
where
\Delta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
to have a more general definition. We also note that a definition for CAKKT2 can be similarly provided by simply replacing the first-order conditions AKKT
with CAKKT. This had been done for nonlinear programming in
[
23
]
,
except for the strong complementarity measure.

In the following, we discuss properties of AKKT2 for (
SOCP
). Let us first show the following lemmas which will be used in our main results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a feasible point of (
SOCP
) and let
\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
. Define

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}S(x,x^{*})}:=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,\begin{array}[]{l}Dh(x)d=0;Dg_{i}(x)d=0,i\in I_{0}(x^{*});\\
\tilde{g}_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}Dg_{i}(x)d=0,i\in I_{B}(x^{*})\end{array}\right\},

and let the mapping
x\mapsto S(x,x^{*})
be inner semicontinuous at
x^{*}
. Then, for each
d\in S(x^{*})
, there is a sequence
\{d^{k}\}\subset S(x^{k},x^{*})
which fulfills
\{d^{k}\}\to d
. Moreover, the mapping
x\mapsto S(x,x^{*})
is inner semicontinuous at
x^{*}
if and only if the WCR property holds at
x^{*}
.

Proof.

The second statement is shown in
[
18
, Lemma 1]
.
By inner semicontinuity of
x\mapsto S(x^{k},x^{*})
, we have

S(x^{*})\subseteq\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,\underset{{y\to x^{*}}}{\mathrm{lim}}\ \text{sup\ dist}[d,S(y,x^{*})]=0\right\},

where
\text{dist}[d,S(y,x^{*})]
denotes the distance of
d
to the set
S(y,x^{*})
. Recalling that
d\in S(x^{*})
holds, we obtain

0=\underset{{y\to x^{*}}}{\mathrm{lim}}\ \text{sup\ dist}[d,S(y,x^{*})]=\underset{{n\to\infty}}{\mathrm{lim}}\ \text{sup}\left\{\text{dist}[d,S(y,x^{*})]\,\middle|\,\|y-x^{*}\|\leq\frac{1}{n}\right\}.

Now, let us define
t_{n}:=\text{sup}\{\text{dist}[d,S(y,x^{*})]\mid\|y-x^{*}\|\leq 1/n\}
. It is obvious that
t_{n}\to 0
when
n\to\infty
.
Since
x^{k}\to x^{*}
, there exists a positive integer
k_{n}\geq n
such that
\|x^{k_{n}}-x^{*}\|\leq 1/n
. Therefore,
\text{dist}[d,S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})]\leq t_{n}
holds.
From the definition of distance, we also have
\text{dist}[d,S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})]:=\text{inf}\{\|d-s\|\mid s\in S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})\}
. Taking this into account, there exists
d_{n}\in S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})
such that

\displaystyle\|d_{n}-d\|

\displaystyle<\text{inf}\{\|d-s\|\mid s\in S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})\}+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle=\text{dist}[d,S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})]+\frac{1}{n}.

Therefore, we obtain
\|d_{n}-d\|<t_{n}+1/n\to 0
when
n\to\infty
. In conclusion, we can take a subsequence
\{x^{k_{n}}\}\subset\{x^{k}\}
such that there exists
S(x^{k_{n}},x^{*})\supset\{d_{n}\}\to d
.

\Box

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\beta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{s-1}
. Then, the matrix

P=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta&b^{\top}\\
b&\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\beta}bb^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt}+\xi I_{s-1}\end{array}\right]

is positive semidefinite when
\xi\geq 0
and
\beta>0
. Additionally, if
\xi>0
, then
P
is positive definite.

Proof.

Assume that
\xi\geq 0
and
\beta>0
, and let us prove that
u^{\top}\hskip-1.0ptPu\geq 0
for all
u=(u_{0},\bar{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
. Simple calculations show that

\displaystyle u^{\top}\hskip-1.0ptPu

\displaystyle=\beta u_{0}^{2}+2u_{0}b^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\bar{u}+\frac{1}{\beta}\bar{u}^{\top}\hskip-1.0ptbb^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\bar{u}+\xi\|\bar{u}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\beta}u_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\big(b^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\bar{u}\big)\right)^{2}+\xi\|\bar{u}\|^{2}\geq 0,

which shows that
P\succeq 0
. Now, assume that
\xi>0
and take
u=(u_{0},\bar{u})\neq 0
. If
\bar{u}=0
, then
u_{0}\neq 0
and thus the above inequality holds strictly. When
\bar{u}\neq 0
, we also conclude that
u^{\top}\hskip-1.0ptPu>0
, which means that
P\succ 0
.

\Box

Before presenting the main result, we discuss some assumptions. To do so, we introduce the following generalized Lojasiewicz inequality, which is a weak assumption on the smoothness of the constraint functions
[
8
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

A function
Q
satisfies the generalized Lojasiewicz inequality at
x^{*}
if there exists
\delta>0
and
\vartheta\colon B(x^{*},\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\lim_{x\to x^{*}}\vartheta(x)=0
and

|Q(x)-Q(x^{*})|\leq\vartheta(x)\|\nabla Q(x)\|,

where
B(x^{*},\delta)
is the Euclidean ball with radius
\delta

around
x^{*}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local minimizer of (
SOCP
), and
\{\rho_{k}\}\to+\infty
. Take sequences
\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
,
\{\mu^{k}\}
and
\{\omega_{i}^{k}\}
as in Theorem
3.4
. Assume that the feasibility measure of (
SOCP
) given by

Q(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|h(x)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(-g(x))\|^{2}

(4.1)

satisfies the generalized Lojasiewicz inequality at
x^{*}
.
Then, for all
i
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(\mu_{i}^{k})^{2}}{\rho_{k}}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|\omega_{i}^{k}\|^{2}}{\rho_{k}}=0.

Proof.

For sufficiently large
k
, we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\vartheta(x^{k})=0
and

|Q(x^{k})-Q(x^{*})|\leq\vartheta(x^{k})\|\nabla Q(x^{k})\|.

Since
x^{*}
is feasible,
Q(x^{*})=0
. Thus, from the definition of
Q
,
\mu^{k}
and
\omega^{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}}(\|\mu^{k}\|^{2}+\|\omega^{k}\|^{2})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}}\left(\|\rho_{k}h(x^{k})\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|\rho_{k}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\vartheta(x^{k})\|\rho_{k}\nabla Q(x^{k})\|.

(4.2)

Computing
\nabla Q(x^{k})
, we also have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{k}\nabla Q(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\rho_{k}\left(Jh(x^{k})^{\top}\hskip-1.0pth(x^{k})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}Jg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\|Jh(x^{k})\mu^{k}-Jg(x^{k})^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\omega^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})\|+\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|.

From Theorem
3.4
,
x^{*}
satisfies the AKKT conditions, and from
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, in particular, there exists
\varepsilon_{k}>0
with
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that
\|\nabla_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}
. Thus, (
4.2
) can be written as

\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}}(\|\mu^{k}\|^{2}+\|\omega^{k}\|^{2})\leq\vartheta(x^{k})(\varepsilon_{k}+\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|).

Since
\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|
is bounded, we conclude that the left-hand side of the above inequality converges to
0
, which completes the proof.

\Box

To prove our main theorem, we consider the following assumption.

Assumption 4.6
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local minimizer of (
SOCP
).
Then, one of the following assumptions holds:

(a)

Let
\{\rho_{k}\}\to+\infty
,
\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
and
\{\omega_{i}^{k}\}
as in Theorem
3.4
. Then,
\{[\omega^{k}_{i}]_{0}\}_{k\in\tilde{N}}

is bounded for all
i\in I_{B}(x^{*})
, where

\tilde{N}:=\{k\mid g_{i}(x^{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\setminus(\mathcal{K}_{i}\cup-\mathcal{K}_{i})
}
; or

(b)

The feasibility measure
Q
defined in (
4.1
) satisfies the generalized Lojasiewicz inequality at
x^{*}
.

The above assumption will be used in Lemma
4.7
below and discussed further in Remark
4.8
.
Note that (a) states that when the sequence
\{g_{i}(x^{k})\}
is converging
to a point of the boundary
g_{i}(x^{*})
from the outside of the cone, then the corresponding first-term of the multiplier
[\omega^{k}_{i}]_{0}=\rho_{k}[\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))]_{0}
should be bounded. On the other hand, the assumption (b) only involves the problem’s constraints, instead of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
, and is known to be a weak condition, since it is satisfied when the constraint functions are analytic
[
8
]
.

The following lemma is the crucial part of the theorem that shows that AKKT2 is in fact a second-order necessary optimality condition. We state it as a new lemma just for clarity and because its proof is long enough.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local minimizer of (
SOCP
).
Let
\{x^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{\rho_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
be sequences such that
\{x^{k}\}\to x^{*}
and
\{\rho_{k}\}\to+\infty
. Let
V_{i}^{k}
be an arbitrary element in
\partial\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))
and
\omega^{k}_{i}:=\rho_{k}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))
.
Suppose also that Assumption
4.6
is satisfied.
Then, the inequality below holds:

\displaystyle d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\rho_{k}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}V_{i}^{k}Dg_{i}(x^{k})\right)d

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\left(\sigma(x^{k},\omega^{k})+\sum_{i\in I_{0}(x^{*})}\theta_{i}^{k}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}Dg_{i}(x^{k})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\sum_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\gamma_{i}^{k}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k})(Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}+\delta_{k}I_{n}\right)d

for all
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
k
, with appropriate parameters
\{\theta_{i}^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
\{\gamma_{i}^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\{\delta_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\delta_{k}\to 0
for sufficiently large
k
.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote
u_{i}^{k}:=Dg_{i}(x^{k})d
for simplicity. Then, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\rho_{k}(u_{i}^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}V_{i}^{k}u_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\sigma(x^{k},\omega^{k})d+\sum_{i\in I_{0}(x^{*})}\theta_{i}^{k}\|u_{i}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\gamma_{i}^{k}(u^{k}_{i})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}u^{k}_{i}+\delta_{k}\|d\|^{2}

(4.3)

for appropriate parameters. First, from the definition of
\Gamma_{i}
,
\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k})
and
M_{i}(\xi,w)
, we obtain

(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{if }\>\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}=0,\\
2\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|^{2}M_{i}\left(-1,-\displaystyle{\frac{\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}}{\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|}}\right),&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(4.4)

for all
i\in I_{B}(x^{*})
. Moreover, recalling that
V_{i}^{k}\in\partial\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))
, from Lemma
2.4
(a), (c), (e), for all
i=1,\dots,r
, we have

V_{i}^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{if }g_{i}(x^{k})\in\mbox{int}(\mathcal{K}_{i}),\\
\displaystyle{\alpha_{i}^{k}M_{i}\left(-1,-\frac{\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}}{\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|}\right)}\mbox{ with }\alpha_{i}^{k}\in\left[0,1\right],&\mbox{if }g_{i}(x^{k})\in\mathrm{bd}^{+}(\mathcal{K}_{i}),\\
\displaystyle{M_{i}\left(-\frac{[g_{i}(x^{k})]_{0}}{\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|},-\frac{\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}}{\|\overline{g_{i}(x^{k})}\|}\right)},&\mbox{if }g_{i}(x^{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\setminus(\mathcal{K}_{i}\cup-\mathcal{K}_{i}).\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

Recall that
I_{I}(x^{*}),I_{0}(x^{*})
and
I_{B}(x^{*})
is a partition of
\{1,\dots,r\}
.
Here, we analyze these three cases separately.

First, let
i\in I_{I}(x^{*})
.
Then, for sufficiently large

g_{i}(x^{k})\in\mbox{int}(\mathcal{K}_{i})
.
Hence, from (
4.5
), for sufficiently large
k

(u_{i}^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}V_{i}^{k}u_{i}^{k}=0\quad\mbox{for all }i\in I_{I}(x^{*}).

(4.6)

Now, let
i\in I_{0}(x^{*})
.
Note that the sequence
\{g_{i}(x^{k})\}
may converge to

from the interior, boundary or origin of

and also from the

and

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(V_{i}^{k})\leq 1

Therefore, if we define

\theta_{i}^{k}:=\rho_{k}\quad\mbox{for all }i\in I_{0}(x^{*}),

(4.8)

From (
4

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})}\Bigg(-\rho_{k}(u_{i}^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}V_{i}^{k}u_{i}^{k}+\gamma_{i}^{k}(u^{k}_{i})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}u^{k}_{i}\Bigg)

, let us define

for some

This fact, and the definition of sigma-term show that it is sufficient to prove that

-\rho_{k}V_{i}^{k}+\gamma_{i}^{k}(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))(\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x^{k}))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}-\frac{[\omega_{i}^{k}]_{0}}{[g_{i}(x^{k})]_{0}}\Gamma_{i}+\varphi_{i}^{k}I_{m_{i}}\quad\mbox{for all }i\in I_{B}(x^{*})

(4.10)

for appropriate choices of

Thus, from now on let

.
In this case, we can define

4.1
Validity of the new optimality condition

The following theorem shows that AKKT2 is in fact a second-order optimality condition
that does not require any constraint qualification.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
x^{*}
be a local minimizer of (
SOCP

x^{*}

Proof.

\{\rho_{k}\}

Next, we show that

satisfies the second-order condition.
The definition of

in (

and the local optimality of

give

\nabla F_{k}(x^{k}):=\nabla f(x^{k})+\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}(x^{k}-x^{*})-\rho_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{r}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(-g_{i}(x^{k}))\\

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}F_{k}(x^{k})

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{x}^{2}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})+\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}I_{n}+2(x^{k}-x^{*})(x^{k}-x^{*})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}

\displaystyle\ +\rho_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}-\rho_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{r}Dg_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\chi_{i}^{k}.

4.2
Strength of the new optimality condition

In this section, we show the relationship between AKKT2 and WSONC. The following result shows that AKKT2 is a strong optimality condition in the sense that it implies WSONC under a constraint qualification. This extends the result for NLP

Theorem 4.10

Since

and

\displaystyle d^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Bigg(\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})+\sigma(x^{k},\omega^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta^{k}_{i}\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})\nabla h_{i}(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}

2

4.3
Numerical example

By the definition of the AKKT2 conditions, it is obvious that AKKT2 implies AKKT. We now give an example that shows that AKKT2 is in fact stronger than AKKT. The example is similar to
[
6
, Example 3.1]
. Consider the (
SOCP
) without equality constraints where
n:=2
,
r:=1
,
m_{1}:=2
,

and
g(x):=(1,x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}
. Let
x^{*}:=(1,1)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}
. Since the Lagrangian of the problem is defined by

\nabla_{x}L(x,\omega)=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}-2x_{1}x_{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{2}\\
0&x_{1}\end{pmatrix}\omega,

x^{*}
is an AKKT (and KKT) point with Lagrange multiplier
\omega^{*}=(1/2,-1/2)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}
. Now, we will show that the AKKT2 conditions fail at
x^{*}
. Suppose that AKKT2 holds and let
x^{k}:=(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\to x^{*}
and
d^{k}:=(x_{1}^{k},-x_{2}^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}
. Then, from the definition of AKKT2, we obtain

\displaystyle(d^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\nabla_{x}^{2}L(x^{k},\omega^{k})d^{k}+\delta_{k}\|d^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\omega_{2}^{k}(x_{1}^{k}x_{2}^{k})^{2}+\delta_{k}((x_{1}^{k})^{2}+(x_{2}^{k})^{2})\geq 0.

However, since
x^{k}\to x^{*}
,
\omega^{k}\to\omega^{*}
and
\delta_{k}\to 0
hold, we have

4\omega_{2}^{k}(x_{1}^{k}x_{2}^{k})^{2}+\delta_{k}((x_{1}^{k})^{2}+(x_{2}^{k})^{2})\to-2,

which contradicts the result. Consequently,
x^{*}
is an AKKT point but not an AKKT2 point.

5
Algorithms that generate AKKT2 sequences

As stated previously, having a formal definition of AKKT2 is important because now
we can propose and analyze algorithms. Thus, in this section, we will consider algorithms that generate AKKT2 sequences. We consider two algorithms: an augmented Lagrangian method and a sequential quadratic programming (SQP) method.

5.1
The augmented Lagrangian method

Our approach is based on an existing augmented Lagrangian method for SOCP (see for instance
[
29
]
) and for nonlinear programming, that is proposed in
[
7
, Algorithm 4.1]
and
[
6
, Algorithm 1]
. Note that in
[
6
]
the method is shown to generate AKKT2 sequences.
The augmented Lagrangian function used here is defined as

L_{\rho}(x,\mu,\omega):=f(x)+\frac{1}{2\rho}\left\{\|\mu-\rho h(x)\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(\omega_{i}-\rho g_{i}(x))\|^{2}-\|\omega_{i}\|^{2}\right)\right\},

where
\rho>0
is the penalty parameter. The gradient of
L_{\rho}
with respect to
x
is given by

\nabla_{x}L_{\rho}(x,\mu,\omega)=\nabla f(x)-Dh(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}(\mu-\rho h(x))-\sum_{i=1}^{r}Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(\omega_{i}-\rho g_{i}(x)).

To handle second-order information, we need to obtain the second-order derivative of
L_{\rho}
. However, as it can be seen above, its derivative is not differentiable because of the projection term. To overcome such difficulty, we consider the following.

As an alternative for the second-order derivative of
L_{\rho}
, we define an operator
\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{x}
as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{x}L_{\rho}(x,\mu,\omega)

5.2
The sequential quadratic programming method

Let us now consider an SQP method that generates AKKT2 points.
SQP methods are iterative methods where, at each iteration, the search direction is determined by solving subproblems in which a quadratic model of the objective function is minimized subject to linearized constraints. To ensure global convergence, a penalty function is required. To address ill-posed problems and generate well-defined and practical subproblems, we consider an algorithm based on a stabilized SQP method. This approach was first proposed for NLP in
[
36
]
and later extended to nonlinear semidefinite programming
[
37
]
, which includes SOCP.

In the following, we describe the outline of the algorithm. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the current iteration and
H_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the Hessian of the Lagrangian function or its approximation. For the current point
(x^{k},\mu^{k},\omega^{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{K}
, the algorithm solves the following subproblem:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{{(\xi,\chi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{K}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\langle\nabla f(x^{k})-Dh(x^{k})s^{k},\xi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{k}\xi,\xi\rangle+\frac{\rho_{k}}{2}\|\chi\|^{2}\\[5.0pt]
\mathrm{subject\ to\ }&Dg(x^{k})\xi+\rho_{k}(\chi-t^{k})\in\mathcal{K},\end{array}

(5.2)

where
\rho_{k}>0
is the penalty parameter and

s^{k}:=\mu^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}h(x^{k}),\quad t^{k}:=\omega^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}g(x^{k}),\quad M_{k}:=H_{k}+\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}\nabla h(x^{k})\nabla h(x^{k})^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}.

Note that (
5.2
) always has a strictly feasible point
(\xi,\chi)=(0,t^{k}+(1,0,\dots,0)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt})
, which indicates that it fulfills Slater’s constraint qualification. Moreover, if
M_{k}\succ O
for each iteration, (
5.2
) has a unique global minimizer. Using the solution of the subproblem
(\xi,\chi)
, we define the search direction
p^{k}
and the candidates of Lagrange multipliers
(\bar{\mu}^{k+1},\bar{\omega}^{k+1})
as

p^{k}:=\xi,\quad\bar{\mu}^{k+1}:=\mu^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}(h(x^{k})+Dh(x^{k})\xi),\quad\bar{\omega}^{k+1}:=\chi.

Instead of choosing
(\bar{\mu}^{k+1},\bar{\omega}^{k+1})
as the new Lagrange multipliers immediately, we check their optimality by the so-called VOMF procedure
[
21
]
. In this procedure, each iterate is classified into four groups, V-, O-, M- and F- iterates and we update the Lagrange multipliers in different ways, which enables global convergence.

To introduce VOMF procedure, we give some notations.
We extend the concept of
\epsilon
–active set for nonlinear programming and denote the sets of indices by

\displaystyle I_{0,\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\mid\|g_{i}(x)\|\leq\epsilon\},

\displaystyle I_{B,\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\mid|[g_{i}(x)]_{0}-\|\overline{g_{i}(x)}\||\leq\epsilon,[g_{i}(x)]_{0}>0\},

\displaystyle I_{BB,\epsilon}(x,\omega)

\displaystyle:=\{i\in I_{B,\epsilon}(x)\mid|[\omega_{i}]_{0}-\|\overline{\omega_{i}}\||\leq\epsilon,[\omega_{i}]_{0}>0\}.

We define
r_{V}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
r_{O}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{K}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with

\displaystyle r_{V}(x)

\displaystyle:=\|h(x)\|+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(-g(x))\|,

\displaystyle r_{O}(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=\|\nabla_{x}L(x,\mu,\omega)\|+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|g_{i}(x)\circ\omega_{i}|}+\max\big\{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(-P(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon)),0\big\},

where
g_{i}(x)\circ\omega_{i}
is the Jordan product of
g_{i}(x)
and
\omega_{i}
, and

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(-P(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon))
is the largest eigenvalue of
-P(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon)
, with

\displaystyle P(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon):=

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x,\mu,\omega)-\sum_{i\in I_{BB,\epsilon}(x,\omega)}\frac{[\omega_{i}]_{0}}{[g_{i}(x)]_{0}}Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}Dg_{i}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla h_{i}(x)\nabla h_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I_{0,\epsilon}(x)}\frac{1}{\rho}Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}Dg_{i}(x)+\sum_{i\in I_{B,\epsilon}(x)}\frac{1}{\rho}(Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x))(Dg_{i}(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Gamma_{i}\tilde{g}_{i}(x))^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}.

First, we note that when
i\in I_{B}(x^{*})
, the associate multiplier
\omega^{*}_{i}
should be
0
or in the boundary of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
because of the complementarity condition. Thus, the sigma-term (
3.4
) at such
(x^{*},\omega^{*})
only takes
i
in
\{i\in I_{B}(x^{*})\mid\omega_{i}^{*}\in\mathrm{bd}^{+}(\mathcal{K}_{i})\}
, which justifies the first summation of the above definition.
Furthermore,
r_{V}
and
r_{O}
indicate the violation of the constraints and the optimality measure with respect to the AKKT2 conditions, respectively. The complementarity term in
r_{O}
is actually related to CAKKT conditions, but CAKKT implies AKKT in SOCP
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
. One can also use
\langle g(x),\omega\rangle
as the measure of complementarity, but this would be related to the so-called TAKKT. It is known, however, that TAKKT and AKKT are independent conditions. We also define
\Phi(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon):=r_{V}(x)+\kappa r_{O}(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon)
and
\Psi(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon):=\kappa r_{V}(x)+r_{O}(x,\mu,\omega,\rho,\epsilon)
where
\kappa\in(0,1)
is a weight parameter. Moreover, the merit function of the problem is denoted by

\tilde{F}(x;\rho,\mu,\omega):=f(x)+\frac{1}{2\rho}\|\rho\mu-h(x)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\rho}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\rho\omega-g(x))\|^{2},

where
\rho>0
is a parameter. The gradient of
\tilde{F}
with respect to
x
is given by

\nabla\tilde{F}(x;\rho,\mu,\omega):=\nabla f(x)-Dh(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\left(\mu-\frac{1}{\rho}\right)+Dg(x)^{\top\hskip-1.0pt}\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\omega-\frac{1}{\rho}g(x)\right).

Using the above notations, constants
\mu_{\mathrm{max}}>0,\ \omega_{\mathrm{max}}>0
and positive parameters
\phi_{k},\psi_{k},\gamma_{k}
and
\epsilon_{k}
, we introduce VOMF procedure in Algorithm

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a second-order sequential optimality condition for SOCP called AKKT2, which extends the AKKT2 of NLP. We show that a local minimum of SOCP satisfies the condition under a weak assumption. We also consider its relation with the existing optimality condition WSONC and propose algorithms that generate AKKT2 points. Moreover, similar results are obtained for CAKKT2. As an already ongoing future work, we cite the proposal of AKKT2 conditions for nonlinear semidefinite programming and nonlinear symmetric
conic optimization. It is also important to check the necessity of Assumption
4.6
in the main theorem. Another important work is to define strict CQs for our AKKT2, i.e., the weakest CQ that one can assume that would recover KKT.

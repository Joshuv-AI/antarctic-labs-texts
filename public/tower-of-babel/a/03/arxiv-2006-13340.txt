Uniqueness and Optimality of Dynamical Extensions of Divergences
Gilad Gour

Abstract
We introduce an axiomatic approach for channel divergences and channel relative entropies that is based on three information-theoretic axioms of monotonicity under superchannels (i.e. generalized data processing inequality), additivity under tensor products, and normalization, similar to the approach given for the state domain in Gour and Tomamichel 2020a ; Gour and Tomamichel 2020b . We show that these axioms are sufficient to give enough structure also in the channel domain, leading to numerous properties that are applicable to all channel divergences. These include faithfulness, continuity, a type of triangle inequality, and boundedness between the min and max channel relative entropies. In addition, we prove a uniqueness theorem showing that the Kullback-Leibler divergence has only one extension to classical channels. For quantum channels, with the exception of the max relative entropy, this uniqueness does not hold. Instead we prove the optimality of the amortized channel extension of the Umegaki relative entropy, by showing that it provides a lower bound on all channel relative entropies that reduce to the Kullback-Leibler divergence on classical states. We also introduce the maximal channel extension of a given classical state divergence and study its properties.



I
Introduction

Distinguishability between probability distributions, quantum states, and quantum channels, lies at the heart of several theories in science including information theory, quantum computing, and statistics. Unlike arbitrary vectors, objects like probability vectors, quantum states and quantum channels contain information about physical systems and therefore their distinguishability is typically quantified with functions that are sensitive to this information. For example,
consider an information source (see e.g.
Cover and Thomas 2006
) such as a dice, that ‘emits’ alphabets in
\mathcal{X}
according to either probability distribution
\mathbf{p}\coloneqq\{p_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
or probability distribution
\mathbf{q}\coloneqq\{q_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
. An agent can estimate which of the two distributions corresponds to the source by using the source many times (in the example of a dice, this amounts to rolling it many times). The intuition is that if
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are very distinguishable it would be easier to determine which one of them corresponds to the information source.

One of the key observations in any distinguishability task as above, is that by sending the information source through a communication channel, the agent cannot increase his or her ability to distinguish between the two distributions
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
. This means that if
E
is a column stochastic matrix that corresponds to the communication channel, then the resulting distributions
E\mathbf{p}
and
E\mathbf{q}
are less distinguishable than
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
. In other words, any measure that quantifies the distinguishability between two probability vectors
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
must decrease under any stochastic evolution process that takes the pair
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
to
(E\mathbf{p},E\mathbf{q})
. Hence, many metrics in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such as the Euclidean distance, cannot be used to quantify this notion of distinguishability between two probability vectors (see e.g.
PWP+2006
).

Functions defined on pairs of probabilities that behave monotonically under mapping of the form
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\to(E\mathbf{p},E\mathbf{q})
are called
divergences
, and their monotonicity property is often referred to as
data processing inequality
(DPI). Unlike the trace distance
Wilde 2017
, some divergences are additive under tensor products. In
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
;
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
, relative entropies were defined axiomatically as divergences that satisfy this additivity property. It was shown that the combination of DPI with additivity provides enough structure to deduce a variety of key properties of relative entropies.

Many specific divergences and relative entropies have been extended to the quantum domain, including the trace distance, the Kullback-Leibler divergence, the Renyi divergences,
f
-divergences, and the min and max relative entropies
Umegaki 1962
;
Petz 1986
;
Wilde et al. 2014
;
Müller-Lennert et al. 2013
;
Tomamichel 2015
;
Matsumoto 2018a
;
Hiai and Mosonyi 2017
;
Datta 2009a
.
More recently, several extensions of quantum divergences to channel divergences have also been studied
Cooney et al. 2016
;
Leditzky et al. 2018
;
Berta et al. 2020
;
Gour 2019
;
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Katariya and Wilde 2020
with a variety of applications in resource theories of quantum channels and beyond
Kaur and Wilde 2018
;
Gour and Wilde 2018
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Gour and Winter 2019
;
Wang and Wilde 2019
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Gour and Scandolo 2019
;
Büml et al. 2020
;
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Fang et al. 2020
;
Katariya and Wilde 2020
. In
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
, an axiomatic approach to quantum divergences have been proposed.
This approach is based on three axioms of DPI, additivity, and normalization that were first put forth
in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
for classical divergences.

In this paper we extend further this axiomatic approach to the channel domain. We define dynamical (i.e. channel) divergences as functions that satisfies the DPI with a superchannel, and channel relative entropies as channel divergences that satisfy in addition additivity and appropriate normalization. We show that these axioms provides enough structure that give rise to a variety
of properties that hold for all dynamical divergences and dynamical relative entropies. These include properties that carries over from the state domain like, faithfulness, continuity, optimality, and triangle inequality that holds for all channel relative entropies. We also show that all channel relative entropies must be no greater than the max channel relative entropy, and no smaller than the min channel relative entropy. We then discuss a variety of extensions of classical (state) divergences to quantum-channel divergences. Remarkably, for classical channels, we find that there exists only one classical-channel relative entropy that reduces to the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. In other words, the extension of the KL relative entropy to classical channels is unique.
In the quantum domain, we show that the regularized channel (Umegaki) relative entropy is the smallest extension among all dynamical relative entropies that reduces to the KL-divergence on classical states. In Fig.

(1)

In

;

;
Burniston et al. 2020

Gour and Tomamichel 2020b

Let

1.

(4)

(b)

II.3
Relative Majorization

We say that a pair of vectors
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)
is
relatively majorized
by another pair of vectors
\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)
, and write

(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})

(18)

if there exists a stochastic evolution matrix
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)
such that
(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})=(\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{p}),\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{q}))
. Therefore, the relative Rényi entropies behave monotonically under relative majorization; i.e. if
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})
then
D_{\alpha}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\geqslant D_{\alpha}\big(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\big\|\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\big)
for all
\alpha\in[0,\infty]
. If we have
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\succ(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
then we will write

(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\sim(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\;.

(19)

Relative majorization is a partial order that can be characterized with testing regions. The testing region of a pair of probability vectors
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{D}(n)
is a region in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\coloneqq

(20)

\displaystyle\Big\{(\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{t},\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;:\;0\leqslant\mathbf{t}\leqslant(1,...,1)^{T}\;,\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big\}

where the inequalities are entry-wise. This region is bounded by two curves known as the lower and upper Lorenz curves.

The upper Lorenz curve can be obtained from the lower Lorenz curve by a rotation of a 180 degrees around the point
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Therefore, the lower (or upper) Lorenz curve determines uniquely the testing region. The lower Lorenz curve of a pair of probability vectors
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denoted here by
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
, has
n+1
vertices that can be computed as follows. First, observe that the testing region is invariant under the transformation
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\to(\Pi\mathbf{p},\Pi\mathbf{q})
where
\Pi
is any permutation matrix. Therefore, w.l.o.g. we can assume that the components of
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are arranged such that

\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}}\geqslant\frac{p_{2}}{q_{2}}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\;.

(21)

The
n+1
vertices of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
are the
(0,0)
vertex and the
n
vertices
\{(a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{n}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
, where

a_{\ell}=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}p_{x}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{\ell}=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}q_{x}\;.

(22)

The relevance of testing regions to our study here is the following theorem that goes back to Blackwell
Blackwell 1953
, and that since then have been rediscovered under different names including
d
-majorization
Arthur F. Veinott 1971
, matrix majorization
Dahl 1999
, and thermo-majorization

(see also the book on majorization by
Marshall and Olkin
Marshall et al. 2011
).

Theorem
.

Blackwell 1953

Let
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{P}(n)
and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(m)
be two pairs of probability vectors in dimensions
n
and
m
, respectively. Then,

(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\quad\iff\quad\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\supseteq\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\;.

(23)

The theorem above provides a geometric characterization to relative majorization; that is,
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})
if and only if the lower Lorenz curve of
(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})
is nowhere below the lower Lorenz curve of
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
.

III
Channel Divergences

Quantum divergences have numerous applications in quantum information theory
Wilde 2017
and quantum resource theories
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. As discussed in the preliminary section, their defining property is the DPI. Therefore, to define a channel divergence one has to extend the DPI to the channel domain. Since the most general and physically realizable operation that can be applied to a quantum channel is a superchannel, we will define channel divergences in terms of superchannels.

We first start with the formal definition of a channel divergence and a channel relative entropy.
We will use the notation
\mathbb{D}
for a general channel divergence, to distinguish it from a general quantum divergence
\mathbf{D}
, or a general classical divergence
\mathscr{D}
.

Definition 1
.

Let

\mathbb{D}:\bigcup_{A,B}\Big\{{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)\times{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)\Big\}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

be a function acting on pairs of quantum channels in finite dimensions.

1.

The function
\mathbb{D}
is called a
channel divergence
if it satisfies the generalized Data Processing Inequality (DPI). That is, for any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and a superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

\mathbb{D}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

2.

A channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
is called a
channel relative entropy
if it satisfies the following additional properties:

(a)

Additivity. For any
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)=\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})\;.

(b)

Normalization.

\mathbb{D}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\;\Big\|\;\begin{bmatrix}1/2&0\\
0&1/2\end{bmatrix}\right)=1\;.

where quantum states are viewed as channels with one dimensional input.

Remark
.

The term “channel relative entropy” is sometimes referred in literature to a quantity that extends the Umegaki relative entropy as in (
63
). However, in
Fang et al. 2020
it was shown not to be additive under tensor products and therefore does not fit with our definition above of a relative entropy. Moreover, its regularized version, which was shown in
Fang et al. 2020
to be equal to the amortized divergence (and which is known to be additive
Fang 2020
), is the one that appears in most applications. We therefore choose to call the expression in (
63
) a channel
divergence
rather than a channel relative entropy.

As an example, consider
D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
as defined in (
15
).
This channel divergence has several operational interpretations in quantum information
Kaur and Wilde 2018
;
Gour and Wilde 2018
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Gour and Winter 2019
;
Wang and Wilde 2019
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Gour and Scandolo 2019
;
Büml et al. 2020
;
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Fang et al. 2020
;
Katariya and Wilde 2020
. One can easily verify that it is additive and satisfies the normalization condition of a relative entropy. It also satisfies the DPI as defined above. This can be seen from the fact that if
t\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}
is a CP map, then also
\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}-t\mathcal{M}\right]=\Theta[\mathcal{N}]-t\Theta[\mathcal{M}]
is a CP map, so that
D_{\max}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])\leqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
. Therefore, according to the definition above,
D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
is a channel relative entropy.

Another important example is the hypothesis testing divergence
D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
as defined in (
17
). This divergence plays a key role in hypothesis testing with quantum channels
Wang and Wilde 2019
, although it is not additive (unless
\varepsilon=0
) which means that it is not a relative entropy according to our definition. Still it satisfies the DPI for channels
Gour 2019
.

Also the diamond norm is a channel divergence according to our definition above since it satisfies the channel DPI
Gour 2019
, and it has an operational interpretation in terms of the optimal success probability of distinguishing between two channels (see e.g.
Watrous2018
). This again supports our definition of channel divergences, particularly the extension of the DPI with superchannels. This channel version of the DPI is satisfied by channel divergences that has physical and operational interpretations. We will discuss more examples of such channel divergences with operational interpretation in the concluding section.

Channel divergences can be viewed as a generalization of quantum divergences since the set
{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
with
|A|=1
can be viewed as the set of quantum states
\mathfrak{D}(B)
. Hence, we view here the quantum states in
\mathfrak{D}(B)
as a special type of quantum channels, i.e. those in
{\rm CPTP}(1\to B)
where
1
represents the one dimensional trivial system. However, there is another type of quantum channels that can be identified with quantum states. These are the replacement channels. Let
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(B)
and define the channel
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
with
|A|>1
as

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}(\omega^{A})\coloneqq{\rm Tr}[\omega^{A}]\sigma^{B}\quad\quad\forall\;\omega\in\mathfrak{L}(A)\;.

(27)

Therefore, this channel is uniquely determined by the dimension of system
A
and the state
\sigma^{B}
. It is therefore natural to ask if channel divergences between replacement channels reduce to quantum divergences between the states that define the
replacement channels. Not too surprising, we will see below that the answer to this question is on the affirmative.

III.1
Basic Properties

Channels divergences and relative entropies borrow some of their properties from quantum divergences. In this section we discuss a few of these basic properties. The significance of these properties is that they are satisfied by all channel divergences (or relative entropies) and therefore are not unique to a particular divergence. Later on we will see that some divergences have additional unique properties that do not satisfied by all divergences. We will say that a channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
is faithful if
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=0
implies that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
.

Theorem 1

(Properties of Channel Divergences)
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence. Then,

1.

If
\mathbb{D}(1\|1)=0
(here 1 stands for the trivial channel in
{\rm CPTP}(1\to 1)
) then for any two channels
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant 0\;,

(28)

with equality if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
.

2.

If
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy then
\mathbb{D}(1\|1)=0
.

3.

\mathbb{D}
is faithful if and only if its reduction to classical states (i.e. probability vectors) is faithful.

4.

For any two replacement channels,
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{1}},\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{2}}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
, as defined in (
27
) with
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}(B)
and
|A|>1

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{1}}\big\|\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{2}}\right)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{1}\|\sigma_{2})\;.

(29)

5.

Suppose
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy, and let
\mathcal{E}_{1},...,\mathcal{E}_{n}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be a set of
n
orthogonal quantum channels; i.e. their Choi matrices satisfies
{\rm Tr}[J_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}^{AB}J_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}^{AB}]=0
for all
j\neq k\in[n]
. Then, for any probability vector
\mathbf{p}=\{p_{x}\}_{x=1}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\sum_{x=1}^{n}p_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}
,

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{x}\big\|\mathcal{N}\right)=-\log(p_{x})\quad\forall\;x=1,...,n.

(30)

6.

If
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy then
for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

D_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;,

(31)

where
D_{\min}
and
D_{\max}
are the channel min and max relative entropies as defined in (
17
) and (
15
), respectively.

7.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel relative entropy,
\mathcal{R}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be the completely randomizing channel (i.e.
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{u}^{B}}
meaning
\mathcal{R}(\rho^{A})=\mathbf{u}^{B}
is the maximally mixed state
\mathbf{u}^{B}
for all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
), and
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm CPTP}({A\to B})
be an isometry channel (we assume
|A|\leqslant|B|
). Then,

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\right)=\log|AB|\;.

(32)

8.

If
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy then for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{E})+D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(33)

9.

If
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy then for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})

(34)

\displaystyle\leqslant\min_{0\leqslant s\leqslant 2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{N})}}D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\|\mathcal{M}\right)

Moreover, if
J_{\mathcal{N}},\;J_{\mathcal{E}},
and
J_{\mathcal{M}}
have full support then

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}({\mathcal{N}}\|{\mathcal{M}})-\mathbb{D}({\mathcal{E}}\|{\mathcal{M}})\leqslant\log\left(1+\frac{\|J_{\mathcal{N}}-J_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{E}})\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{M}})}\right)\;.

(35)

Remark
.

Property 8 implies that for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\big|\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{E})-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{F})\big|\leqslant D_{T}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})

(36)

where

D_{T}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max\big\{D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F}),D_{\max}(\mathcal{F}\|\mathcal{E})\big\}

(37)

is a metric (whose state version is known as the Thompson metric) on
{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
. Hence, in particular, any channel relative entropy
\mathbb{D}
is continuous in its second argument on the subset of
{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
consisting of channels that have strictly positive Choi matrices. Similarly, Property 9 implies a continuity in the first argument of
\mathbb{D}
. That is, if
\mathcal{E}
is very close to
\mathcal{N}
then
s
can be taken to be very close to one as long as
\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}})
. Note that in this case, if we also have
\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}})

then the continuity of
D_{\max}
implies that
D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\|\mathcal{M}\right)
goes to zero as
s
goes to one
(recall that if
s=1
then
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{N}
).

III.2
Divergences of Classical Channels

The properties discussed in the previous subsection applies to all quantum-channel divergences. Here we show that there are additional properties satisfied by channel relative entropies, when the inputs are restricted to be classical. That is, in this subsection we consider classical dynamical divergences; i.e. divergences of classical channels. Most interestingly, we will see that there is only one classical-channel relative entropy that reduces to the KL divergence.
We start with the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a classical channel divergence that reduces to the classical (state) divergence
\mathscr{D}
on classical states in
\mathfrak{D}(X)\times\mathcal{D}(X)
. Suppose further that
\mathscr{D}
is quasi-convex. Then, for all classical channels
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)

\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(38)

where

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\max_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}\mathscr{D}\left(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big\|\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\right)

(39)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf_{|Z|\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{D}\left(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q}\right)

where the infimum is over all
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)
that satisfies

(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ\big(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|),\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big)
for all
\;x\in[|X|]
.
Moreover,
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
is a classical channel relative entropy, and
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is a normalized classical channel divergence.

Remark
.

In the next section, we will develop a general framework to extend channel divergences from one domain to a larger one, and
the optimality of the divergences
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
and
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
will follow trivially from that general formalism. Hence, the theorem above can be viewed as a corollary of the third property in Theorem
5
of the next section. Moreover, we will see shortly from the closed formula for
\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
that w.l.o.g. we can bound
|Z|\leqslant|Y|
.

To illustrate the expression above for
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
, in Fig.
2
we draw three lower Lorenz curves associated with the two channels
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)
, where
|X|=2
and
|Y|=4
. In this example, the channel
\mathcal{M}
is defined via the probability vectors
\mathbf{m}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=(1/3,1/4,1/4,1/6)
and
\mathbf{m}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(|1\rangle\langle 1|)=(5/12,1/6,1/4,1/6)
, and the channel
\mathcal{N}
via
\mathbf{n}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=(1/12,1/6,1/3,5/12)

and
\mathbf{n}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(|1\rangle\langle 1|)=(1/12,1/12,1/2,1/3)
. The figure helps to see how to compute the optimal pair
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
that satisfies
both
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{m}_{0},\mathbf{n}_{0})
and
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\succ(\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{1})
. The Lorenz curve,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
, must be the closest possible curve to both
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{m}_{0},\mathbf{n}_{0})
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{1})
and it must also be below both of them. In Fig.
2
, the dashed line is the Lorenz curve of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
. Its vertices are
(0,0)
,
(5/12,1/12)
,
(7/12,2/12)
,
(5/6,7/12)
and
(1,1)
. This reveals that the optimal
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are given by
\mathbf{p}=(5/12,1/6,1/4,1/6)
and
\mathbf{q}=(1/12,1/12,5/12,5/12)
.

For the general case, we provide in the theorem below a closed formula for
\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
for any two classical channels
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)
in finite dimensions
|X|,|Y|<\infty
. For any
x=1,...,|X|
, we will denote by
(\mathbf{m}_{x},\mathbf{n}_{x})\coloneqq\big(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|),\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big)

and by
M=(M_{y|x})
and
N=(N_{y|x})
the
|Y|\times|X|
matrices whose components are
M_{y|x}=\langle y|\mathbf{m}_{x}|y\rangle
and
N_{y|x}=\langle y|\mathbf{n}_{x}|y\rangle
, respectively. We also rearrange the components of the columns of
M
and
N
such that for each
x=1,...,|X|

\frac{M_{1|x}}{N_{1|x}}\geqslant\frac{M_{2|x}}{N_{2|x}}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\frac{M_{|Y||x}}{N_{|Y||x}}\;.

(40)

Therefore, aside from the points
(0,0)
and
(1,1)
, for any
x=1,...,|X|
, the vertices of the lower Lorenz curve of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{m}_{x},\mathbf{n}_{x})
are given by
\{(a_{zx},b_{zx})\}_{z=1}^{|Y|-1}
, where

a_{zx}\coloneqq\sum_{y=1}^{z}M_{y|x}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{zx}\coloneqq\sum_{y=1}^{z}N_{y|x}\;.

(41)

Theorem 3

(Closed Formula)
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a classical divergences and let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)
. Using the notations above, the maximal classical channel extension
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is given by

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;,

(42)

where
\mathbf{p}=\{p_{z}\}_{z=1}^{|Y|}
and
\mathbf{q}=\{q_{z}\}_{z=1}^{|Y|}
are
|Y|
-dimensional probability vectors given by

\displaystyle p_{1}=M_{1x_{1}}\quad{,}\quad p_{z}=a_{zx_{z}}-a_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}

(43)

\displaystyle q_{1}=N_{1x_{1}}\quad{,}\quad q_{z}=b_{zx_{z}}-b_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}\quad\forall\;z=2,...,|Y|

where
x_{1},...,x_{|Y|}\in\{1,...,|X|\}
are defined by induction via the relations

\frac{N_{1x_{1}}}{M_{1x_{1}}}=\min_{x\in[|X|]}\frac{N_{1x}}{M_{1x}}\;,

(44)

and for any
z=2,...,|Y|

\frac{b_{zx_{z}}-b_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}}{a_{zx_{z}}-a_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}}=\min_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}\frac{b_{zx}-b_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}}{a_{zx}-a_{(z-1)x_{z-1}}}\;.

(45)

The maximal divergence
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is in general not additive, and therefore, is not a relative entropy.
In the next result, we prove that if
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy that reduces to the KL relative entropy, then it is unique.

Uniqueness of the Channel KL-Relative Entropy

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a classical channel divergence that reduces to the Kullback-Leibler divergence,
D
, on classical states. If
\mathbb{D}
is continuous in its second argument then for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\max_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}D\left(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big\|\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\right)\;.

(46)

The uniqueness theorem above holds only for the KL divergence and it is not clear to the author if this uniqueness still holds for the case that the KL divergence is replaced with the Rényi divergences. In
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
(cf.
Matsumoto 2018b
;
Wilming et al. 2017
) it was shown that the Umegaki relative entropy (and in particular the KL-divergence) is the only asymptotically continuous divergence. Therefore, the uniqueness theorem above implies that there is only one classical channel divergence that on classical states is asymptotically continuous.

IV
Optimal Extensions

In this section we apply the extension techniques developed in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
for general resource theories to study the optimal extensions of a classical divergence to a channel divergence. We point out that the extensions we consider are optimal in the sense that they are either minimal or maximal among all possible extensions. For example, if
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is a channel divergence that reduces to a classical state divergence
\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})
when
\mathcal{N}=\mathbf{p}
and
\mathcal{M}=\mathbf{q}
are classical states, then
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is bounded from below by the minimal extension of
\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})
to the channel domain, and bounded from above by the maximal extension of
\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})
to the channel domain.
One can also consider extensions of quantum divergences to channel divergences. As we will see, such extensions give rise to additional types of channel divergences. We start with the general framework for channel extensions of divergences.

IV.1
General Framework for Extensions

In the following theorem we apply the results that were given in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
for a general resource theory, to channel-extensions of divergences. We start with the definition of an
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)\subset{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be a subset of quantum channels for any two physical systems
A
and
B
. A function

\mathbf{C}:\bigcup_{A,B}\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)\times\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

is called an
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence if for any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
and any superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
such that
\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
and
\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})

\mathbf{C}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(47)

Any
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence has two optimal extensions to a quantum channel divergence:

1.

The minimal channel-extension, for any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\sup\mathbf{C}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])

(48)

where the supremum is over all systems
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
and all

\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
such that
\Theta[\mathcal{M}],\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
.

2.

The maximal channel-extension, for any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})

(49)

where the infimum is also over all systems
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
, and all
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
such that there exists
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to AB)
that satisfies
\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathcal{E}]
and
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathcal{F}]
.

Remark
.

In the definition above we assumed that
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
is a subset of quantum channels. By taking
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
to be a subset of replacement channels as in (
27
), the extensions above can also be applied to state divergences.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)\subset{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
, and let
\mathbf{C}
be an
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence. Then, its maximal and minimal channel-extensions
\overline{C}
and
\underline{C}
have the following properties:

1.

Reduction.
For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})={\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(50)

2.

Data Processing Inequality.
For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;\text{and},

(51)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

3.

Optimality.
Any quantum channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{C}
on pairs of channels in
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
, must satisfy for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(52)

4.

Sub/Super Additivity.
Suppose
\mathbf{C}
is weakly additive; that is,
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k})=k\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(53)

Then,
\underline{\mathbf{C}}
is super-additive and
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is sub-additve. Explicitly, for any
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\geqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})

(54)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})\;.

5.

Regularization.
If
\mathbf{C}
is weakly additive under tensor products then any weakly additive quantum channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{C}
on pairs of channels in
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
, must satisfy for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;,

(55)

where

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\underline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\;\text{and},

(56)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\overline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\;,

and
\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}
are themselves weakly additive channel divergences.

Remark
.

In the case that
\mathbf{C}
is additive (even weakly additive) the minimal and maximal extensions are super-additive and sub-additive, respectively. This, in turn, implies that the limits in (
56
) exists so that
\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}
and
\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}
are well defined. Moreover, in general, the bounds on
\mathbb{D}
in (
55
) are tighter than the bounds in (
52
). This assertion follows from the fact that that
\underline{\mathbf{C}}
is super-additive and in particular satisfies
\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\geqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
. Similarly, the sub-additivity of
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
implies that
\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
.

In the following subsections we apply Theorem
5
to the cases that
\mathfrak{R}
is the subset of all quantum states (i.e. replacement channels) and the subset of all classical states. We will see that this give rise to several optimal channel-extensions of state divergences. We start, however, by using the theorem above to prove the uniqueness of the max channel relative entropy.

IV.2
Uniqueness of the max relative entropy

The max relative entropy is defined for any
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
as

D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\log\min\Big\{t\;:\;t\sigma\geqslant\rho\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}\;.

(57)

The max relative entropy is unique with respect to its monotonicity property. Unlike the relative entropy and all the other Rényi entropies, it behaves monotonically under any CP map (not necessarily trace preserving or trace non-increasing). More precisely, let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
, and let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CP}(A\to B)
be such that

\mathcal{E}(\rho)
and
\mathcal{E}(\sigma)
are normalized quantum states in
\mathfrak{D}(B)
. Since we do not assume here that
\mathcal{E}
is trace non-increasing we cannot conclude that there exists a CPTP map that achieves the same task; i.e. taking the pair
(\rho,\sigma)
to the pair
(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\mathcal{E}(\sigma))
. Yet, the max divergence behaves monotonically under such maps; explicitly, for a given
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
,

D_{\max}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\|\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\big)\leqslant D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\;,

(58)

for any
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CP}(A\to B)
for which
\mathcal{E}(\rho),\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\in\mathfrak{D}(B)
for the given
\rho
and
\sigma
. Note that we assumed here that
D_{\max}
is defined only on pairs of normalized states. Extensions to subnormalized states can be made using the techniques studied in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
.

For quantum channels,
D_{\max}
has been defined analogously to the states case as
Liu and Winter 2019

D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\log\min\Big\{t\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;t\mathcal{M}\geqslant\mathcal{N}\Big\}\;.

(59)

One can easily see that similar to the states case,
D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
behaves monotonically under any CP preserving (CPP) supermap that takes the pair of channels
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
to any other pair of channels
(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{N}^{\prime})
. Specifically, let
\Theta\in{\rm CPP}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
be a CPP supermap that is not necessarily a superchannel, and suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\coloneqq\Theta[\mathcal{N}]
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\coloneqq\Theta[\mathcal{N}]
are quantum channels in
{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
. Then,

D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\leqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(60)

We show here that the extension of
D_{\max}
from classical states to quantum channels is unique.
We already know from Property 6 of Theorem
1
that any channel relative entropy cannot exceed
D_{\max}
.
In fact, in the proof of Property 6 of Theorem
1
we only use the normalization property of relative entropy. Therefore, one can conclude something slightly stronger that it is even not possible to extend
D_{\max}
to a non-additive channel divergence.

From the optimality property of Theorem
5
it follows that in order to prove uniqueness, it is sufficient to show that the maximal and minimal extensions of
D_{\max}
are equal to each other. Applying the general framework for extensions developed in the previous subsection, the maximal and minimal extensions of
D_{\max}
to quantum channels ,denoted by
\overline{D}_{\max}
and
\underline{D}_{\max}
, respectively, are defined for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N}\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)\end{subarray}}D_{\max}\Big(\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\Big)\;;

(61)

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N}\;,\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\\
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathbf{p}]\;,\;\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathbf{q}]\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(X\to AB)\end{subarray}}D_{\max}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;.

Theorem 6

(Uniqueness of the dynamical max relative entropy)
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence that reduces to
D_{\max}
on classical probability distributions; i.e. for any classical system
X
and
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)
,
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})=D_{\max}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})
. Then, for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(62)

Remark
.

Note that we do not assume that
\mathbb{D}
is a channel relative entropy (i.e. additive), only a divergence that reduces to the max relative entropy on classical states. The main idea of the proof is to show that the two expressions in (
61
) are both equal to
D_{\max}
.

IV.3
Extension from a quantum (state) divergence to a channel divergence

In this section we study the optimal extensions of quantum state divergences to quantum channel divergences.
For a given quantum state divergence
\mathbf{D}
we denote by
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
its minimal channel-extension. According to (
48
) the minimal channel-extension is given by

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sup_{\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to R^{\prime})}\mathbf{D}\big(\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}\right]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}\\
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\Big(\mathcal{E}^{BR\to R^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\Big\|\mathcal{E}^{BR\to R^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\Big)

\displaystyle=\max_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}\mathbf{D}\Big(\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\big\|\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\Big)

(63)

where in the last equality the supremum has been replaced with a maximum since w.l.o.g. we can assume that
|R|=|A|
and that
\psi^{RA}
is a pure state
Cooney et al. 2016
;
Leditzky et al. 2018
.

Similarly, by the definition in (
49
), the maximal channel-extension is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

(64)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Theta\in{\rm SC}(R\to AB)\\
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(R)\end{subarray}}\Big\{\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\;:\;\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\rho],\;\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\sigma]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(RA\to B)\\
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(R)\end{subarray}}\Big\{\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\;:\;\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho},\;\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}\Big\}

(65)

where for any density matrices
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(R)
and channel

\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(RA\to B)
we denote

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rho}^{A\to B}(\omega^{A})=\mathcal{E}^{RA\to B}(\rho^{R}\otimes\omega^{A})\quad\text{and},

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{A\to B}_{\sigma}(\omega^{A})=\mathcal{E}^{RA\to B}(\sigma^{R}\otimes\omega^{A})\quad\forall\omega\in\mathfrak{L}(A)\;.

The channels above have been studied under the name
environment-parametrized channels

Das and Wilde 2019
;
Das and Wilde 2019
;
Takeoka and Wilde 2016
, and the expression in (
63
) has been used in the literature for the cases that
\mathbf{D}
is the trace norm (in which case
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
becomes the diamond norm
Aharonov et al. 1998
), Umegaki relative entropy, and quantum Rényi divergences
Cooney et al. 2016
;
Leditzky et al. 2018
. The following corollary is the restatement of Theorem
5
for the optimal channel-extensions of quantum state divergences.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a quantum (state) divergence, and let
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
be its minimal and maximal extensions to quantum channels.
Then,

1.

Both
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
are quantum-channel divergences.

2.

Both
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
reduces to
\mathbf{D}
on quantum states.

3.

Any other channel divergences
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{D}
on quantum states must satisfy for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(67)

4.

If
\mathbf{D}
is a weakly additive quantum state divergence then
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
is super-additive and
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
is sub-additive with respect to tensor products. Explicitly, for any
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{D}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\geqslant\underline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\underline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})

(68)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{D}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2}).

5.

If
\mathbf{D}
is a weakly additive quantum state divergence then any weakly additive quantum channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{D}
on quantum states, must satisfy for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{D}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{D}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;,

(69)

where

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{D}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\underline{\mathbf{D}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\;\text{and},

(70)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{D}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\overline{\mathbf{D}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\;.

and
\underline{\mathbf{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
are themselves weakly additive normalized channel divergences.

In addition to the corollary above, we have the following property for the maximal extension.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a jointly convex quantum divergence. Then, its maximal channel-extension
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
is also jointly convex.

The channel divergence
\underline{\mathbf{D}}
has been shown in
Gour and Wilde 2018
to satisfy the generalized DPI.
For the case that
\mathbf{D}=D
is the Umegaki relative entropy, it was shown in
Fang et al. 2020
that it satisfies a chain rule. The latter property in particular implies that its regularization can be expressed as
Fang et al. 2020

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}

(71)

\displaystyle\Big\{D\left(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}\left(\rho^{RA}\right)\big\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\left(\sigma^{RA}\right)\right)-D\left(\rho^{RA}\big\|\sigma^{RA}\right)\Big\}

where the expression on the RHS is known as the amortized divergence
Berta et al. 2020
. We will see below that any channel relative entropy that reduces to the Kullback-Leibler divergence on classical states must be no smaller than the above expression.

IV.4
Extensions from classical state divergences to channel divergences

In this subsection we study optimal channel-extensions of a classical state divergence
\mathscr{D}
. We define the following four optimal extensions of
\mathscr{D}
to quantum channel divergences.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathscr{D}:\mathfrak{D}(X)\times\mathfrak{D}(X)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a classical (state) divergence, and let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be two quantum channels. We define four extensions of
\mathscr{D}
to quantum channels:

1.

The minimal extension of
\mathscr{D}
,

\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N}\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{D}\Big(\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}\right]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\Big)\;.

(72)

2.

The maximal extension of
\mathscr{D}
,

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N}\;,\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\\
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathbf{p}]\;,\;\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathbf{q}]\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(X\to AB)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;.

(73)

3.

The geometric extension of
\mathscr{D}
,

\widehat{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|R|\in\mathbb{N}\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to R)\end{subarray}}\overline{\mathscr{D}}\Big(\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}\right]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\Big)

(74)

where
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is the maximal quantum
state
-extension of
\mathscr{D}
.

4.

The min-max extension of
\mathscr{D}
,

\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{\uparrow}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|R|\in\mathbb{N}\;,\;\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(R)\\
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\rho]\;,\;\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\sigma]\\
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(R\to AB)\end{subarray}}\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\rho\|\sigma)\;,

(75)

where
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
is the minimal quantum
state
-extension of
\mathscr{D}
.

From Theorem
5
it follows that all the four functions above satisfy the generalized data processing inequality (and therefore they are indeed divergences), and they all reduce to the classical divergence
\mathscr{D}
on classical states. Moreover, for a pair of quantum states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
we have
\widehat{\mathscr{D}}(\rho\|\sigma)=\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\rho\|\sigma)
and

\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{\uparrow}(\rho\|\sigma)=\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\rho\|\sigma)
. In addition, Theorem
5
implies that any channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
, that reduces on classical states to a classical divergence
\mathscr{D}
, must satisfy for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(76)

This in particular applies to the cases
\mathbb{D}=\widehat{\mathscr{D}}
and
\mathbb{D}=\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{\uparrow}
, and also note that in general, the optimal channel-extensions in (
63
) and (
65
) of a quantum state divergence
\mathbf{D}
, that reduces to a classical state divergence
\mathscr{D}
on classical states, satisfy for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\underline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(77)

IV.4.1
The minimal channel extension and its regularization

Note that the minimal extension
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq

(78)

\displaystyle\sup\mathscr{D}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\big\|\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\Big)

where the supremum is over all systems
X,R
, over all
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(AR)
, and over all
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(BR\to X)
.
Note that w.l.o.g. we can assume that
\psi_{RA}
is a pure state.
There is at least one divergence for which the expression above coincides with the optimization given in (
63
).

Theorem 8
.

Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and for any
\epsilon\in[0,1)
, let
\mathscr{D}=D_{\min}^{\epsilon}
be the classical hypothesis testing divergence (i.e. the classical version of the one defined in (
10
)).
Then,

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(79)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA}))\;.

Remark
.

The above theorem implies that any channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
D_{\min}^{\epsilon}
on
classical
states satisfies for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(80)

The minimal channel extension is typically not additive even if the classical divergence
\mathscr{D}
is additive (i.e.
\mathscr{D}
is a relative entropy).
However, from Theorem
5
we know that if
\mathscr{D}
is a classical relative entropy then its channel extension
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
is super-additive. This means that the limit in its regularization exists;

\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\;.

(81)

From Theorem
5
it follows that
\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
is a weakly additive divergence, and all channel relative entropies
that reduces to
\mathscr{D}
on classical states must be no smaller than it.

Suppose now that
\mathscr{D}=D
is the KL divergence. In this case, we denote by
\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}
its minimal regularized channel extension. Another closely related quantity that plays important role in applications is the regularized version of the minimal channel extension of the (quantum) Umegaki relative entropy, denoted as
D^{\rm reg}
. That is,

D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})

(82)

where

D(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}D\Big(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\big\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{AR})\Big)

(83)

with
D
being the Umegaki relative entropy. Since
\underline{D}^{\rm reg}
is the minimal channel extension we must have

\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(84)

Note that if
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
in the equation above are quantum states (i.e. the input dimension
|A|=1
) then the equality holds. This is due to the fact that that on quantum states, the Umegaki relative entropy
D^{{\rm reg}}(\rho\|\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)
equals to the minimal additive state extension of the KL relative entropy (see (
12
)). We now show that the equality also holds for any two channels.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be two quantum channels. Then,

\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(85)

That is,
D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
is the smallest channel relative entropy that reduces to the KL relative entropy on classical states.

Starting with a classical relative entropy
\mathscr{D}
, the process at which we arrived the weakly additive channel divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
had 2 steps: (1) Extend
\mathscr{D}
to the minimal channel divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
, and (2) regularize
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
to obtain a weakly additive divergence. One can also introduce regularization in the state level and only then apply the channel extension. Specifically, starting with a classical relative entropy
\mathscr{D}
, we apply the following four steps:

1.

Extend
\mathscr{D}
to the minimal quantum state divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
.

2.

Regularize
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
to get a weakly additive quantum state divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
.

3.

Using the minimal extension, extend
\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
to a channel divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}_{ch}
.

4.

Regularize
\underline{\mathscr{D}}_{ch}
to get a weakly additive channel divergence
\underline{\mathscr{D}}_{ch}^{{\rm reg}}
.

Fig.
3
illustrate these four steps.

In
Tomamichel 2015
it was shown that if
\mathscr{D}
is the classical Rényi entropy with
\alpha\in[1/2,\infty]
then
\underline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
is the sandwiched quantum relative entropy of order
\alpha

Wilde et al. 2014
;
Müller-Lennert et al. 2013
. Therefore, in this case,
\underline{\mathscr{D}}_{ch}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
is simply the channel extension of the sandwich relative entropy so that
\underline{\mathscr{D}}_{ch}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\alpha}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

. In Theorem

above we showed that for

.

IV.4.2
The maximal channel extension

To the author’s knowledge, the maximal channel extension in (
73
) is new, and was not studied before.
Note that the infimum in (
73
) can be expressed as

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N}\;,\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\\
\mathcal{M}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}\;,\;\mathcal{N}=\sum_{x}q_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}\\
\{\mathcal{E}_{x}\}\subset{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})

(86)

For the case that
\mathcal{M}
is an isometry we get the following result.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be an isometry channel defined via
\mathcal{V}(\rho)=V\rho V^{*}
, for all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
, and with isometry matrix
V
(i.e.
V^{*}V=I^{A}
). Then, for any
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})=\log{\rm Tr}\left[J_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}J_{\mathcal{V}}\right]\;.

(87)

In section
III.2
we provided a closed formula of this divergence for the classical case. We saw that the formula reveals that this divergence is not additive (even for classical channels), and therefore is not a relative entropy. Recall, however, that from Theorem
5
we know that if
\mathscr{D}
is a classical relative entropy then its channel extension
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is sub-additive. This means that the limit in its regularization exists, and
\overline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}\leqslant\overline{\mathscr{D}}
. The divergence
\overline{\mathscr{D}}^{{\rm reg}}
is weakly additive, and it remains open to determine if it is a relative entropy (i.e. fully additive).

IV.4.3
The geometric channel relative entropy

Given a classical divergence
\mathscr{D}
, its maximal extension to quantum states is given for all
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
by

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|X|\in\mathbb{N},\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)\\
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{p})=\rho,\;\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{q})=\sigma\\
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to A)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;.

(88)

The geometric divergence is defined as the minimal channel-extension of this maximal state-extension of
\mathscr{D}
.
It can be expressed as (cf. (
74
))

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}\overline{\mathscr{D}}\Big(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}\left(\psi^{RA}\right)\Big\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\left(\psi^{RA}\right)\Big)

(89)

For the case that
\mathscr{D}=D_{\alpha}
is the classical Rényi entropy with
\alpha\in(0,2]
and
\alpha\neq 1
, it was proved in
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Katariya and Wilde 2020
that

\widehat{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(90)

where

\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left\|{\rm Tr}_{B}\left[G_{\alpha}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB},J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\right]\right\|_{\infty}&\text{if }\alpha\in(1,2]\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\\
\lambda_{\min}\left({\rm Tr}_{B}\left[G_{\alpha}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB},J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\right]\right)&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1)\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\\
\infty&\text{if }\alpha\in(1,2]\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB})\not\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\\
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{\min}\left({\rm Tr}_{B}\left[G_{\alpha}(J_{\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}^{AB},J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\right]\right)&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1)\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB})\not\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\end{cases}

(91)

with
J_{\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}^{AB}\coloneqq J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB}+\epsilon I^{AB}
and

G_{\alpha}(X,Y)\coloneqq Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Y^{-\frac{1}{2}}XY^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\forall\;X,Y>0

(92)

For
\alpha=1
it is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{\alpha=1}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\widehat{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\|{\rm Tr}_{B}\left[\widehat{G}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB},J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\right]\right\|_{\infty}&\text{if }\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{M}}^{AB})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(J_{\mathcal{N}}^{AB})\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where

\widehat{G}(X,Y)\coloneqq X^{\frac{1}{2}}\log\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}}Y^{-1}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)X^{\frac{1}{2}}\;.

(93)

For all
\alpha\in(0,2]
, the formula above gives for an isometry
\mathcal{V}
and a channel
\mathcal{N}

\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(94)

Hence, due to Theorem
10
,
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})
coincides with the maximal quantum-channel extension
\overline{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})
. However, recall that in general we have
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
, and we saw in Sec.
III.2
that the inequality can be strict even on classical channels. In fact, since
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
is additive, we must have
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{D}_{\alpha}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
, and it is left open if this inequality can be strict for some choices of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
.

The formula (
90
) reveals that
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
is additive (at least for
\alpha\in(0,2]
).
In fact, to the authors knowledge, with the exception of
D_{\max}
, this function is the only known channel relative entropy, since all other channel divergences discussed in this paper are at most known to be weakly additive and the question whether they are fully additive is open. In
Fang and Fawzi 2019

\widehat{D}_{\alpha}
was used to derive upper bounds on certain QIP tasks, and in
Katariya and Wilde 2020
it was used to upper-bound some optimal rates in the context of channel discrimination.

V
Outlook

V.1
Some applications of channel divergences

One of the fundamental tasks in quantum information theory is the distinguishability of two quantum channels
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
. In
Wang and Wilde 2019
it was shown that the asymptotically optimal discrimination rate of
the type-II error exponent for parallel strategy is given by
D^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
, and for the adaptive strategy by the amortized divergence as given in (
71
). Remarkable, in
Fang et al. 2020
it was shown that the amortized divergence equals
D^{\rm reg}
indicating that both the parallel and adaptive strategies yield the same error exponent.

Despite the physical significance of
D^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
for channel discrimination, we know very little about this quantity due to the fact that it is defined in terms of an optimization over a non-compact domain. Therefore, in such scenarios, it is often useful to study the properties of such uncomputable functions.

It is known
Fang 2020
that
D^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is an additive function and therefore it fits our definition of a relative entropy. This immediately implies that it satisfies all the properties outlined in Theorem
1
. Particularly, it satisfies the continuity properties given in (
34
) and (
33
) indicating that if two channels
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
are close (in terms of the Thompson metric
D_{T}
), then they have similar discrimination rates with any other channel
\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
.
We point out that even though the Umegaki relative entropy is continuous (in fact asymptotically continuous) one cannot conclude that these properties carry over to
D^{\rm reg}
due to the optimization involved with unbounded dimensions.

Theorem
9
establishes that
D^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is the smallest channel relative entropy among all channel relative entropies that reduces to the KL-divergence on classical states. This means that it is not possible to find a computable lower bound for
D^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
that is itself a channel divergence. On the other hand, the geometric channel relative entropy
\widehat{D}
provides such an upper bound
Fang and Fawzi 2019
.

Channel relative entropies have several other applications in quantum Shannon theory. For example, consider two distant parties, Alice and Bob, who share a classical-quantum channel
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to B)
. This channel can be used to transmit classical information at a rate that is given by the Holevo information (see e.g.
Wilde 2017
). Interestingly, the Holevo information
\chi(\mathcal{N})
can be expressed as

\chi\left(\mathcal{N}^{X\to B}\right)=\min_{\mathcal{E}}D\left(\mathcal{N}^{X\to B}\big\|\mathcal{E}^{X\to B}\right)

(95)

where the minimum is over all replacement channels of the form
\mathcal{E}(|x\rangle\langle x|)=\sigma^{B}
for all
x
, and a fixed
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(B)
. We can therefore interpret the Holevo quantity as a channel divergence distance to the set of “free” channels.
In this context, the only free channels are the replacement channels. Also the entanglement-assisted classical communication rate can be expressed in a similar fashion. These examples indicate that also in the channel domain divergences and relative entropies as defined in this paper are expected to play a key role in the recently active developing field of dynamical quantum resource theories
Cooney et al. 2016
;
Leditzky et al. 2018
;
Berta et al. 2020
;
Gour 2019
;
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Kaur and Wilde 2018
;
Gour and Wilde 2018
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Gour and Winter 2019
;
Wang and Wilde 2019
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Gour and Scandolo 2019
;
Büml et al. 2020
;
Fang and Fawzi 2019
;
Fang et al. 2020
;
Katariya and Wilde 2020
.

V.2
Conclusions

In this paper we introduced an axiomatic approach to dynamical divergences. This approached is minimalistic in the sense that we only require channel divergences to satisfy the generalized DPI under superchannels, and channel relative entropies to be in addition additive and normalized. Remarkably, we showed that these axioms are sufficient to induce enough structure, leading to numerous properties satisfied by all channel relative entropies. One of our main results, is a uniqueness theorem, Theorem
4
, in which we show that in the classical domain, there exists only one channel relative entropy, that reduces to the Kullback-Leibler divergence on classical states (i.e. probability vectors). In the quantum case, it is known that this uniqueness does not hold even for quantum states, but we were able to show that the amortized relative entropy as defined in (
71
) is the smallest channel relative entropy that reduces to the Kullback-Leibler divergence on classical states.
Due to the one-to-one correspondence between classical entropies and classical relative entropies
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
, this means that the amortized relative entropy is in fact the smallest one that reduces on a pair of classical states
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{u})
(here
\mathbf{u}
is the uniform distribution) to the log of the dimension minus the Shannon entropy.

There are many open problems for future investigations. For example, in the classical domain, for
\alpha\neq 1
, is the classical-channel extension of the
\alpha
-Rényi relative entropy unique? Another interesting problem is whether the regularization of the maximal channel extension of relative entropies coincide with the geometric channel relative entropies. Finally, another interesting question is whether the maximal channel extensions of relative entropies satisfy a ‘chain rule’ similar to the one satisfied by the minimal channel extension (
63
)
Fang et al. 2020
.

Proof of Theorem
1

We prove all the properties outlined in Theorem
1
as separated lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Channel relative entropies are normalized divergences.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel relative entropy, and
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
. Then,
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\otimes 1\|\mathcal{M}\otimes 1)=\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})+\mathbb{D}(1\|1)
. Hence,
\mathbb{D}(1\|1)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence. Then, for any two channels
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
we have

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant 0\;,

(96)

with equality if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)
be a superchannel that takes quantum channels to classical states. Then,

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\right)\geqslant 0

(97)

with equality if
\Theta[\mathcal{N}]=\Theta[\mathcal{M}]
, where we used the fact that on classical states
\mathbb{D}
is a classical divergence.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}},\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be two replacement channels as defined in (
27
), with
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}(B)
and
|A|>1
. Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence. Then,

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\right)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{1}\|\sigma_{2})\;,

(98)

where on the RHS,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are viewed as channels in
{\rm CPTP}(1\to B)
.

Proof.

The proof is a simple consequence of the DPI. On one direction, we define a superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to B)
by imputing the state
|0\rangle\langle 0|\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
into the input of a channel; specifically, for any
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\Theta[\mathcal{E}]\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)\;.

(99)

With this superchannel we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Theta\left[\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}\right]\big\|\Theta\left[\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\right]\right)

(100)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\sigma_{1}\big\|\sigma_{2}\right)\;.

For the other direction, we define a superchannel
\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(B\to AB)
as follows. For any “channel”
\sigma\in{\rm CPTP}(1\to B)
we define

\Upsilon\left[\sigma^{1\to B}\right]=\sigma^{1\to B}\circ{\rm Tr}\;,

(101)

where the trace is acting on the input system
A
.
With this superchannel we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\left(\sigma_{1}\big\|\sigma_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Upsilon[\sigma_{1}]\big\|\Upsilon[\sigma_{2}]\right)

(102)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\sigma^{1\to B}_{1}\circ{\rm Tr}\;\big\|\;\sigma^{1\to B}_{2}\circ{\rm Tr}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\right)\;.

This completes the proof.
∎

The next property is an extension of Lemma 5 in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
to channels.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel relative entropy, and let
\mathcal{E}_{1},...,\mathcal{E}_{n}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be a set of
n
orthogonal quantum channels; i.e. their Choi matrices satisfies
{\rm Tr}[J_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}^{AB}J_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}^{AB}]=0
for all
j\neq k\in[n]
. Then, for any probability vector
\mathbf{p}=\{p_{x}\}_{x=1}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\sum_{x=1}^{n}p_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}
, we have

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{x}\big\|\mathcal{N}\right)=-\log(p_{x})\quad\forall\;x=1,...,n.

(103)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{1},...,\mathcal{E}_{n}
are orthogonal there exists a process POVM
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)
such that

\Theta[\mathcal{E}_{x}]=|x\rangle\langle x|\quad\quad\forall\;x=1,...,n.

(104)

We therefore get from the DPI

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{x}\big\|\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{E}_{x}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\right)

(105)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(|x\rangle\langle x|\Big\|\sum_{y=1}^{n}p_{y}|y\rangle\langle y|\Big)

\displaystyle=-\log(p_{x})\;,

where the last line follows from the classical version of this lemma that was proven in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
.
To prove the other side of the inequality, let
X
be an
n
-dimensional classical system, and let
\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(X\to AB)
be a superchannel defined via

\Upsilon\big[|x\rangle\langle x|\big]\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{x}\;.

(106)

Applying again the DPI gives

\displaystyle-\log(p_{x})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(|x\rangle\langle x|\Big\|\sum_{y=1}^{n}p_{y}|y\rangle\langle y|\Big)

(107)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\Big(\Upsilon\big[|x\rangle\langle x|\big]\Big\|\Upsilon\Big[\sum_{y=1}^{n}p_{y}|y\rangle\langle y|\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{x}\big\|\mathcal{N}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Note that in the proof above, to obtain the bound
\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{E}_{x}\big\|\mathcal{N}\right)\leqslant-\log(p_{x})
we did not assume that that the channels
\{\mathcal{E}_{x}\}
are orthogonal. Therefore, this bounds also holds if
\{E_{x}\}
are not orthogonal. However, we now prove a stronger version of this bound.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence.
For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
we denote its corresponding min and max divergences, respectively, by

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\min}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{D}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Big\|\begin{bmatrix}2^{-D_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}&0\\
0&1-2^{-D_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\max}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{D}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Big\|\begin{bmatrix}2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}&0\\
0&1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}\end{bmatrix}\right)\;.

where the classical states in the input of
\mathbb{D}
on the RHS can be viewed as channels with trivial input systems.

Remark
.

Note that if
\mathbb{D}
is a relative entropy then from Lemma
4
(see also Lemma 5 in
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
) it follows that
\mathbb{D}_{\max}=D_{\max}
and
\mathbb{D}_{\min}=D_{\min}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence.
Then,
\mathbb{D}_{\max}
and
\mathbb{D}_{\min}
are also divergences, and furthermore,

\mathbb{D}_{\min}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{D}_{\max}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\;.

(108)

In particular, if
\mathbb{D}
is a relative entropy then
for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

D_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;,

(109)

where
D_{\min}
and
D_{\max}
are the channel min and max relative entropies as defined in (
17
) and (
15
).

Proof.

We start by proving that
\mathbb{D}_{\max}
and
\mathbb{D}_{\min}
satisfy the DPI.
For this purpose, observe first that for any two binary probability distributions
(p,1-p)
and
(q,1-q)

there exists a classical channel
\mathcal{C}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to X)
satisfying

\mathcal{C}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=|0\rangle\langle 0|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}\left(p|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-p)|1\rangle\langle 1|\right)=q|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-q)|1\rangle\langle 1|

(110)

if and only if
p\leqslant q
. Now, by definition, for any superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\max}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\right)=\mathbb{D}\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|\;\Big\|\;2^{-D_{\max}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])}|0\rangle\langle 0|+\left(1-2^{-D_{\max}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])}\right)|1\rangle\langle 1|\right)

(111)

and also
2^{-D_{\max}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])}\geqslant 2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}
. This means that there exists a classical channel
\mathcal{C}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to X)
satisfying (
110
) with
q=2^{-D_{\max}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])}
and
p=2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}
. Hence, with this classical channel
\mathcal{C}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\max}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(\mathcal{C}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)\;\Big\|\;\mathcal{C}\left(2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}|0\rangle\langle 0|+\big(1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}\big)|1\rangle\langle 1|\right)\Big)

(112)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{D}\Big(|0\rangle\langle 0|\;\Big\|\;2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}|0\rangle\langle 0|+\left(1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}\right)|1\rangle\langle 1|\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}_{\max}\left(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N}\right)\;.

Following the same lines as above, one can prove that also
\mathbb{D}_{\min}
satisfies the DPI. We are now ready to prove the two bounds.

Let
\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
,
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)
, and
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}(\psi)}
denotes the projector to the support of
\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})
. Define the superchannel (in fact process POVM)
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)
with
|X|=2
as

\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\coloneqq{\rm Tr}\big[\mathcal{N}(\psi)\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\psi)\big]|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}+{\rm Tr}\Big[\mathcal{N}(\psi)\left(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\psi)\right)\Big]|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X}\;.

(113)

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)

(114)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|\Big\|{\rm Tr}\big[\mathcal{N}(\psi)\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\psi)\big]|0\rangle\langle 0|+{\rm Tr}\Big[\mathcal{N}(\psi)\left(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\psi)\right)\Big]|1\rangle\langle 1|\right)\;.

Now, since the above equation holds for all
\psi^{RA}
it also holds for the optimal
\psi^{RA}
that satisfies

{\rm Tr}\big[\mathcal{N}(\psi)\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\psi)\big]=2^{-D_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}\;.

(115)

Therefore, substituting this choice of
\psi
in (
114
) gives
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant\mathbb{D}_{\min}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
.

For the second inequality, denote by
t=2^{D_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})}
, and note that in particular,
t\mathcal{N}\geqslant\mathcal{M}
(i.e.
t\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}
is a CP map). Define a superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to AB)
with
|X|=2
by

\Theta\left[|0\rangle\langle 0|\right]=\mathcal{M}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta\left[|1\rangle\langle 1|\right]=\frac{1}{t-1}(t\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M})\;.

(116)

Furthermore, denote

\mathbf{q}^{X}\coloneqq\frac{1}{t}|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}+\frac{t-1}{t}|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X}\;,

(117)

and observe that
\Theta\left[\mathbf{q}^{X}\right]=\mathcal{N}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(\Theta\left[|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\right]\big\|\Theta[\mathbf{q}^{X}]\Big)

(118)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{D}\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\big\|\mathbf{q}^{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, the last basic property of channel divergences that we study in this subsection is the faithfulness of a channel divergence.
Similar to the classical and quantum cases, a channel divergence,
\mathbb{D}
, is said to be faithful if
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=0
implies
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
.
We now prove that channel divergences are faithful if their reduction to classical states is faithful.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel divergence. Then,
\mathbb{D}
is faithful if and only if its reduction to classical (diagonal) states is faithful.

Proof.

Clearly, if
\mathbb{D}
is faithful on quantum channels it is also faithful on classical channels as the latter is a subset of the former. Suppose now that
\mathbb{D}
is faithful on classical states, and suppose by contradiction that there exists
\mathcal{M}\neq\mathcal{N}\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
such that
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=0
. Then, there exists a state
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)
and basis of
RB
such that the diagonal of
\mathcal{M}(\psi^{RA})
in this basis does not equal to the diagonal of
\mathcal{N}(\psi^{RA})
. Let
\Delta\in{\rm CPTP}(RB\to Z)
be the completely dephasing channel in this basis, where
Z\cong RB
is viewed as a classical system with respect to this basis. We therefore have
\Delta\circ\mathcal{M}(\psi^{RA})\neq\Delta\circ\mathcal{N}(\psi^{RA})
.
Now, define a superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to Z)
via

\Theta[\mathcal{E}^{A\to B}]\coloneqq\Delta^{RB\to Z}\circ\mathcal{E}^{A\to B}\circ\psi^{1\to RA}\;,

(119)

where we view the state
\psi
as a channel in
{\rm CPTP}(1\to RA)
. We therefore get that

0=\mathbb{D}\big(\mathcal{M}\big\|\mathcal{N}\big)\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\right)\;.

(120)

But since
\mathbb{D}
is faithful on diagonal states we must have
\Theta[\mathcal{M}]=\Theta[\mathcal{N}]
in contradiction with
\Delta\circ\mathcal{M}(\psi^{RA})\neq\Delta\circ\mathcal{N}(\psi^{RA})
.
Hence,
\mathbb{D}
is faithful also on quantum channels.
∎

Note that the lemma above implies that any channel divergence, that reduces to the Rényi relative entropy of order
\alpha>0
, is faithful. More generally, when combining the above lemma with the condition on faithfulness in Theorem 17 of
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
we get that almost all channel relative entropies are faithful.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel relative entropy, and consider two systems
A
and
B
with
|A|\leqslant|B|
. Let
\mathcal{R}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be the completely randomizing channel, and
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm CPTP}({A\to B})
be an isometry channel. Then,

\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\right)=\log|AB|\;.

(121)

Proof.

From Lemma
5
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\right)

(122)

\displaystyle=\log\min\{t:\;t\mathbf{u}^{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{B}\geqslant\phi^{AB}_{+}\}=\log|AB|\;.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\max_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}\mathbb{D}\Big(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\big\|\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\Big)

(123)

\displaystyle=\max_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}\mathbb{D}\Big(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\big\|\psi^{R}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{B})\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\Big(\mathcal{V}^{A\to B}(\phi^{RA}_{+})\big\|\mathbf{u}^{R}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{B})\Big)

\displaystyle=\log|AB|\;,

where the first inequality follows from (
67
), and the last equality follows from Lemma 5 of
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a channel relative entropy. Then, for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
we have

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{E})+D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})

(124)

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, consider the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}+\varepsilon\mathcal{F},\quad\textrm{where}\quad\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{M}-(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}\right)\;.

(125)

Note that
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
if and only if
\mathcal{M}-(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CP}(A\to B)
or equivalently if and only if

\varepsilon\geqslant 1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})}\;.

(126)

Set
\varepsilon\coloneqq 1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})}
, and define the superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\otimes X\to AB)
, where
X
is a 2-dimensional classical system, such that for all
\mathcal{R}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\Theta\left[\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\right]\coloneqq\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta\left[\mathcal{R}^{A\to B}\otimes|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X}\right]\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{A\to B}\;.

(127)

Such a superchannel can be realized by performing a basis measurement on
X
. If the outcome is 0 then nothing is done to the dynamical system
AB
, whereas if outcome 1 occurs then the input channel of the dynamical system
AB
is replaced with the channel
\mathcal{F}^{A\to B}
. Using additivity and the fact that
\mathbb{D}\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\big\|t|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}+(1-t)|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X}\right)=-\log t
for any
t\in[0,1]
(see Lemma 5 of
Gour and Tomamichel 2020a
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{N}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\big\|\mathcal{E}\otimes((1-\varepsilon)|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}+\varepsilon|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X})\right)+\log(1-\varepsilon)

(128)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\left(\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}\right]\Big\|\Theta\left[\mathcal{M}\otimes\left((1-\varepsilon)|0\rangle\langle 0|^{X}+\varepsilon|1\rangle\langle 1|^{X}\right)\right]\right)+\log(1-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{N}\big\|(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}+\varepsilon\mathcal{F})\right)+\log(1-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{N}\big\|\mathcal{M})\right)-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})\;,

where the inequality follows from the DPI.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be quantum channels. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\leqslant\min_{s\leqslant 2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{N})}}D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\|\mathcal{M}\right)\;.

(129)

Moreover, if
J_{\mathcal{N}},\;J_{\mathcal{E}},
and
J_{\mathcal{M}}
have full support then

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(J_{\mathcal{N}}\|J_{\mathcal{M}})-\mathbb{D}(J_{\mathcal{E}}\|J_{\mathcal{M}})\leqslant\log\left(1+\frac{\|J_{\mathcal{N}}-J_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{E}})\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{M}})}\right)\;.

(130)

Proof.

Let
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to AB)
be a superchannel with
|X|=2
define for any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathfrak{L}(A\to B)
by

\displaystyle\Theta[\mathcal{P}]\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{P}+\varepsilon\mathcal{F}\quad\textrm{where}\quad\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{N}-(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}\right)\;.

(131)

Again, the condition
\epsilon\geqslant 1-2^{-D_{\max}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{N})}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{F}\geqslant 0
which ensures that
\Theta
is indeed a superchannel.
By definition,
\Theta[\mathcal{E}]=\mathcal{N}
. Let
t>1
be the smallest number satisfying

\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\frac{1}{t-1}\left(t\mathcal{M}-\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\right)\geqslant 0\;.

(132)

That is,
t=2^{D_{\max}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\mathcal{M}\right)}
and
\mathcal{R}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
. Finally, define the superchannel

\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(AB\otimes X\to AB)
such that for any
\mathcal{P}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\Upsilon\left[\mathcal{P}\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}\right]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\Theta[\mathcal{P}]&\text{if }x=0\\
\mathcal{R}&\text{if }x=1\end{cases}\;.

(133)

Using the DPI with this superchannel gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})+\log t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(\mathcal{E}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|\Big\|\mathcal{M}\otimes(t^{-1}|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-t^{-1})|1\rangle\langle 1|)\Big)

(134)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{D}\Big(\Upsilon\left[\mathcal{E}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|\right]\Big\|\Upsilon\left[\mathcal{M}\otimes(t^{-1}|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-t^{-1})|1\rangle\langle 1|)\right]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\Big(\Theta\left[\mathcal{E}\right]\Big\|t^{-1}\Theta\left[\mathcal{M}\right]+(1-t^{-1})\mathcal{R}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\big(\mathcal{N}\big\|\mathcal{M}\big)\;.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\big(\mathcal{N}\big\|\mathcal{M}\big)-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\leqslant\log t

(135)

\displaystyle=D_{\max}\left(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\mathcal{M}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{\max}\left((1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{M}+\varepsilon\mathcal{F}\|\mathcal{M}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\|\mathcal{M}\right)\;,

where
s:=1-\epsilon\leqslant 2^{-D_{\max}(J_{\mathcal{E}}\|J_{\mathcal{N}})}
. Finally, to see the bound (
130
), observe that

D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\|\mathcal{M}\right)=\log\min\big\{t\geqslant 0\;:\;t\mathcal{M}\geqslant\mathcal{N}+s(\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{E})\big\}\;.

(136)

Taking

t=1+\frac{1-s}{\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{M}})}\quad\text{and}\quad s=1-\frac{\|J_{\mathcal{N}}-J_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{E}})}

(137)

gives the desired bound (
130
). Note that these choices are consistent with
t\geqslant 0
and
s\leqslant 2^{-D_{\max}(J_{\mathcal{E}}\|J_{\mathcal{N}})}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a classical channel divergence that reduces to the classical (static) divergence
\mathscr{D}
on classical states in
\mathfrak{D}(X)\times\mathcal{D}(X)
. Suppose further that
\mathscr{D}
is quasi-convex. Then, for all
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)

\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(138)

where

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\max_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}\mathscr{D}\left(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big\|\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\right)

(139)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf_{|Z|\in\mathbb{N}}\Big\{\mathscr{D}\left(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q}\right)\;:\;\big(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|),\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big)\prec_{r}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\;\;\forall\;x\in[|X|]\;\;,\;\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)\Big\}\;.

Moreover,
\underline{\mathscr{D}}
is a classical channel relative entropy, and
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
is a normalized classical channel divergence.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
5
by taking
\mathfrak{R}(X\to Y)
to be the set of classical replacement channels, so that the minimal and maximal extensions to all classical channels are given by

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\sup_{Z}\Big\{\mathscr{D}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])\;:\;\Theta\in{\rm SC}(XY\to Z),\;\;\Theta[\mathcal{M}],\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)\Big\}

(140)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf_{Z}\Big\{\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;:\;\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)\;\;,\;\;\exists\Theta\in{\rm SC}(Z\to XY)\;\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathbf{p}]\;\text{,}\;\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathbf{q}]\Big\}\;.

(141)

For the minimal extension, observe that any superchannel
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(XY\to Z)
can be expressed as

\Theta\left[\mathcal{N}^{X\to Y}\right]=\mathcal{E}^{WY\to Z}\circ\mathcal{N}^{X\to Y}(\mathbf{p}^{WX})\quad\forall\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)\;,

(142)

where
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(WY\to Z)
and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(WX)
. Therefore, since
\mathscr{D}
satisfies the DPI it follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}])

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathscr{D}\left(\mathcal{N}^{X\to Y}(\mathbf{p}^{WX})\big\|\mathcal{N}^{X\to Y}(\mathbf{p}^{WX})\right)

(143)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}\mathscr{D}\left(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big\|\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\right)\;,

where the last inequality follows from the quasi-convexity of
\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, the RHS can be achieved by taking
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(XY\to Z)
such that
|W|=1
,
|Z|=|Y|
,
\mathcal{E}=\mathsf{id}^{Y\to Z}
, and
\mathbf{p}^{X}=|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}
.

For the maximal extension, observe that the conditions
\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathbf{p}]
and
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathbf{q}]
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{X\to Y}\left(|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}\right)=\mathcal{E}^{XZ\to Y}\left(|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}\otimes\mathbf{p}^{Z}\right)

(144)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{X\to Y}\left(|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}\right)=\mathcal{E}^{XZ\to Y}\left(|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}\otimes\mathbf{q}^{Z}\right)

where
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(XZ\to Y)
is the post-processing map associated with
\Theta
, and the equalities hold for all
x=1,...,|X|
.
This condition is equivalent to

\big(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|),\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big)\prec_{r}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\;\;\forall\;x=1,...,|X|\;.

(145)

The optimality properties of the maximal and minimal extensions follow from Theorem
5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a classical divergences and let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)
. Using the notations above, the maximal classical channel extension
\overline{\mathscr{D}}

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;,

where
\mathbf{p}=\{p_{z}\}_{z=1}^{|Y|}
and

|Y|

V_{1},...,V_{|Y|-1}
of the optimal lower Lorenz curve
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
(we do not include the vertices
(0,0)
and
(1,1)
).

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
is

V_{1}=(a_{1x_{1}},b_{1x_{1}})
,
where
x_{1}
is chosen such that

\frac{b_{1x_{1}}}{a_{1x_{1}}}=\min_{x\in[|X|]}\frac{b_{1x}}{a_{1x}}\;.

(150)

The reason for that is that among all the lines connecting
(0,0)
to
(a_{1x},b_{1x})
(with
x=1,...,|X|

(0,0)
to
V_{1}

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{m}_{x},\mathbf{n}_{x})
.
The next vertex of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
, is taken to be
V_{2}=(a_{2x_{2}},b_{2x_{2}})
, where
x_{2}
is defined via

\frac{b_{2x_{2}}-b_{1x_{1}}}{a_{2x_{2}}-a_{1x_{1}}}=\min_{x\in[|X|]}\frac{b_{2x}-b_{1x_{1}}}{a_{2x}-a_{1x_{1}}}\;.

V_{2}

V_{1}
and
V_{2}

V_{k-1}

Proof of Theorem
4

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a classical channel divergence that reduces to the Kullback–Leibler divergence,
D
, on classical states. If
\mathbb{D}
is continuous in its second argument then for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(X\to Y)

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\max_{x\in\{1,...,|X|\}}D\left(\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\big\|\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\right)\;.

(152)

We first prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 10
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{r}_{1},...,\mathbf{r}_{a}\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)
, and
\mathbf{s}\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)
. Then,

\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(YZ)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{s}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}\leqslant\log|Z|+\max_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{q})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\prec\mathbf{q}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}

(153)

Proof.

Set
m\coloneqq|Z|
,
n\coloneqq|Y|
, and observe that for any
x=1,...,a
we have
\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{s}\succ\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)}
. Therefore,

\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(YZ)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{s}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}\leqslant\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(YZ)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}

(154)

Now, in
Friedland et al. 2013
it was shown that for a given set of probability vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},...,\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}(k)
there exists an optimal vector
\mathbf{u}
that satisfies (1)
\mathbf{u}\succ\mathbf{v}_{x}
for all
x=1,...,n
, and (2) for any other
\mathbf{w}
for which
\mathbf{w}\succ\mathbf{v}_{x}
must also satisfy
\mathbf{w}\succ\mathbf{u}
. Furthermore, the components of the vector
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},...,u_{k})
are given by

u_{x}=\Omega_{x}-\Omega_{x-1}\quad\text{where}\quad\Omega_{x}\coloneqq\max_{x\in[n]}\sum_{y=1}^{x}(\mathbf{v}_{x})_{y}^{\downarrow}

(155)

and
\{(\mathbf{v}_{x})_{y}^{\downarrow}\}_{y}
are the components of
\mathbf{v}_{x}
arranged in decreasing order.
Applying this formula to our case, we get that the optimal
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{YZ}=\sum_{y,z}p_{yz}|yz\rangle\langle yz|^{YZ}
of the optimization on the RHS of (
154
) has components

p_{yz}=\Omega_{yz}-\Omega_{y(z-1)}\quad;\quad\Omega_{yz}\coloneqq\max_{x\in[a]}\Big\{\sum_{y^{\prime}=1}^{y-1}r_{y^{\prime}|x}+\frac{z}{m}r_{y|x}\Big\}\quad;\quad y\in[n]\;,\;z\in[m]\;.

(156)

with the convention that
\Omega_{y0}\coloneqq\Omega_{(y-1)m}
and
\Omega_{10}=0
. Now, observe that

\mathbf{p}^{YZ}\succ\mathbf{p}^{Y}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{Z}

(157)

since the RHS can be obtained by applying a doubly stochastic matrix to the LHS. Finally, observe that the components of
\mathbf{p}^{Y}
are given by

p_{y}\coloneqq\sum_{z=1}^{m}p_{yz}=\Omega_{ym}-\Omega_{(y-1)m}=\max_{x\in[a]}\sum_{y^{\prime}=1}^{y}r_{y^{\prime}|x}-\max_{x\in[a]}\sum_{y^{\prime}=1}^{y-1}r_{y^{\prime}|x}

(158)

Therefore, the vector
\mathbf{p}^{Y}
is the optimal probability vector that satisfies
\mathbf{r}_{x}\prec\mathbf{p}^{Y}
for all
x\in[a]
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(YZ)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}

\displaystyle=H(\mathbf{p}^{YZ})

(159)

\displaystyle\leqslant H(\mathbf{p}^{Y}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{Z})=\log(m)+H(\mathbf{p}^{Y})

\displaystyle=\log(m)+\max_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)}\Big\{H(\mathbf{q})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{x}\prec\mathbf{q}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x\in[a]\Big\}\;.

This completes the proof.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)

. Then, for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{p}^{\otimes n}\prec\big(\delta,\underbrace{2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)},...,2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}}_{c_{n}\text{-times}},s_{n}\big)

c_{n}\coloneqq\left\lfloor(1-\delta)2^{n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad s_{n}\coloneqq 1-\delta-c_{n}2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}\;.

(161)

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathfrak{T}_{n,\epsilon}(\mathbf{p})

\epsilon
-typical sequences. Then,

\mathbf{p}^{\otimes n}\prec{\rm Pr}\left(x^{n}\not\in\mathfrak{T}_{n,\epsilon}(\mathbf{p})\right)\bigoplus_{x^{n}\in\mathbb{T}_{n,\epsilon}(\mathbf{p})}p_{x^{n}}\;.

(162)

Recall that for any
x^{n}\in\mathfrak{T}_{n,\epsilon}(\mathbf{p})
we have
p_{x^{n}}\leqslant 2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}
. Let
n
be large enough such that
{\rm Pr}\left(x^{n}\not\in\mathfrak{T}_{n,\epsilon}(\mathbf{p})\right)<\delta
. Note that
c_{n}
is the largest integer such that

\delta+c_{n}2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}\leqslant 1\;.

(163)

Therefore, since
\delta+(c_{n}+1)2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}>1
we must have

s_{n}\coloneqq 1-\delta-c_{n}2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}\leqslant 2^{-n\left(H(\mathbf{p})-\epsilon\right)}\;.

(164)

We therefore conclude that (
160
) holds.
∎

We are now ready to prove the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We will start by computing
\overline{D}^{{\rm reg}}
. Denote by
a\coloneqq|X|
and
b\coloneqq|Y|
, and for each
x\in[a]
denote
\mathbf{m}_{x}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)
and
\mathbf{n}_{x}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)\in\mathfrak{D}(Y)
. For any sequence
x^{k}\coloneqq(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in[a]^{k}
denote

\mathbf{m}_{x^{k}}\coloneqq\mathbf{m}_{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{m}_{x_{k}}

(165)

Note that we can express
\mathbf{m}_{x^{k}}
in terms of the type of the sequence
x^{k}

\mathbf{m}_{x^{k}}=\mathbf{m}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{m}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}

(166)

where
\mathbf{t}\coloneqq(t_{1},...,t_{a})
is the type of the sequence
x^{k}
. Similarly,

\mathbf{n}_{x^{k}}
can also be extpressed in terms of
\mathbf{t}
as

\mathbf{n}_{x^{k}}=\mathbf{n}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{n}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\;.

(167)

Denoting by
\mathfrak{T}_{a,k}
the set of all types of sequences in
[a]^{k}
, we get by definition

\overline{D}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\right)=\inf\Big\{D(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;:\;\left(\mathbf{m}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{m}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\;,\;\mathbf{n}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{n}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\right)\prec_{r}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathfrak{T}_{a,k}\Big\}

(168)

where the infimum is over all systems
Z
and all probability vectors
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(Z)
. Suppose first that the channel
\mathcal{N}
is such that all the probability vectors
\mathbf{n}_{1},...,\mathbf{n}_{a}
have positive rational components. In this case, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbf{n}_{x}=\left(\frac{n_{1|x}}{n},...,\frac{n_{b|x}}{n}\right),\quad n_{1|x},...,n_{b|x}\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{y=1}^{b}n_{y|x}=n\quad\forall\;x\in[a]\;.

(169)

For any
x\in[a]
we denote the components of
\mathbf{m}_{x}
by
\{m_{y|x}\}_{y=1}^{b}
, and define

\mathbf{r}_{x}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{y=1}^{b}m_{y|x}\mathbf{u}^{(n_{y|x})}\in\mathfrak{D}(n)\;.

(170)

With these notations we have (see (
26
))

(\mathbf{m}_{x},\mathbf{n}_{x})\sim_{r}(\mathbf{r}_{x},\mathbf{u}^{(n)})

(171)

so that also

(\mathbf{m}_{x^{k}},\mathbf{n}_{x^{k}})\sim_{r}(\mathbf{r}_{x^{k}},\mathbf{u}^{(n^{k})})

(172)

where

\mathbf{r}_{x^{k}}\coloneqq\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\;.

(173)

The above relations give

\displaystyle\overline{D}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\Big\{D(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})\;:\;(\mathbf{r}_{x^{k}},\mathbf{u}^{(n^{k})})\prec_{r}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\;\;,\;\;\forall\;x^{k}\in[a]^{k}\Big\}

(174)

\displaystyle\leqslant\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(n^{k})}\Big\{D(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{u}^{(n^{k})})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{T}_{a,k}\Big\}

\displaystyle=k\log(n)-\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(n^{k})}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes kt_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{a}^{\otimes kt_{a}}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{T}_{a,k}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant(k+\ell a)\log(n)-\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(n^{k+a\ell})}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes(kt_{1}+\ell)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{a}^{\otimes(kt_{a}+\ell)}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{T}_{a,k}\Big\}

where the first inequality follows by restricting
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
to be
n^{k}
-dimensional and taking
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{u}^{(n^{k})}
.
In the last inequality,
\ell
is an integer
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
, and the inequality follows from Lemma
10
.

Denote by
k_{x}\coloneqq kt_{x}+\ell
and observe that from Lemma
11
we have for any
\delta,\epsilon>0
and large enough
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{r}_{x}^{\otimes k_{x}}\prec\big(\delta,\underbrace{2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{x})-\epsilon\right)},...,2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{x})-\epsilon\right)}}_{c_{x}\text{-times}},s_{x}\big)

(175)

where

c_{x}\coloneqq\left\lfloor(1-\delta)2^{k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{x})-\epsilon\right)}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad s_{x}\coloneqq 1-\delta-c_{x}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{x})-\epsilon\right)}\;.

(176)

Let
z\in[a]
be such that
H(\mathbf{r}_{z})=\max_{x\in[a]}H(\mathbf{r}_{x})
. Then, we get that for all
x\in[a]
we have

\mathbf{r}_{x}^{\otimes k_{x}}\prec\mathbf{v}_{x}\coloneqq\big(\delta,\underbrace{2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)},...,2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}}_{c_{x}^{\prime}\text{-times}},s_{x}^{\prime}\big)

(177)

where

c_{x}^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\lfloor(1-\delta)2^{k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad s_{x}^{\prime}\coloneqq 1-\delta-c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\;.

(178)

Hence, from (
174
) and the fact that
\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}}\prec\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{v}_{a}
we get

\displaystyle\overline{D}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant(k+\ell a)\log(n)-\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathfrak{D}(n^{k+a\ell})}\Big\{H(\mathbf{p})\;:\;\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{r}_{a}^{\otimes k_{a}}\prec\mathbf{p}\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{T}_{a,k}\Big\}

(179)

\displaystyle\leqslant(k+\ell a)\log(n)-H(\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathbf{v}_{a})

Continuing

H(\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathbf{v}_{a})=\sum_{x=1}^{a}H(\mathbf{v}_{x})=-a\delta\log\delta-\sum_{x=1}^{n}s_{x}^{\prime}\log s_{x}^{\prime}+\sum_{x=1}^{a}k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}

(180)

Now, since
k_{x}\coloneqq kt_{x}+\ell
, we get that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{k_{x}}{k}=t_{x}
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}=\left\lfloor(1-\delta)2^{k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\right\rfloor 2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}=1-\delta-g\delta_{0,t_{x}}

(181)

where

g\coloneqq\left\lfloor(1-\delta)2^{\ell\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\right\rfloor 2^{-\ell\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}-(1-\delta)

(182)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x=1}^{a}s_{x}\log s_{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x=1}^{a}\left(1-\delta-c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\right)\log\left(1-\delta-c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}\right)

(183)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x=1}^{a}g\delta_{0,t_{x}}\log(g\delta_{0,t_{x}})=0

so that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}H(\mathbf{v}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathbf{v}_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x=1}^{a}k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)c_{x}^{\prime}2^{-k_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)}

(184)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{a}t_{x}\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)(1-\delta-g\delta_{0,t_{x}})

\displaystyle=\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)(1-\delta)\;.

Combining this with (
179
) gives

\overline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\overline{D}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\right)\leqslant\log(n)-\left(H(\mathbf{r}_{z})-\epsilon\right)(1-\delta)

(185)

Since the above equation holds for all
\epsilon,\delta>0
we conclude that

\displaystyle\overline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leqslant\log(n)-H(\mathbf{r}_{z})

(186)

\displaystyle=D(\mathbf{r}_{z}\|\mathbf{u}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in[a]}D(\mathbf{r}_{x}\|\mathbf{u}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in[a]}D(\mathbf{m}_{x}\|\mathbf{n}_{x})

\displaystyle=\underline{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

Combining this with Theorem
2
and Theorem
5
we get

\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\underline{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

(187)

where in the last equality we used the additivity of
\underline{D}
on classical channels. Therefore, all the inequalities above must be equalities so that
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\underline{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
.
This completes the proof for the case that the components of
\mathcal{N}(|x\rangle\langle x|)
are all positive rational numbers.
The proof for arbitrary classical channel
\mathcal{N}
then follows from the continuity of
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
in
\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5

Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a function that maps any pair of quantum systems
A
and
B
to a subset
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)\subset{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
, and let
\mathbf{C}
be an
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence. Then, its maximal and minimal channel-extensions
\overline{C}
and
\underline{C}
have the following properties:

1.

Reduction.
For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)

\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})={\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(188)

2.

Data Processing Inequality.
For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

\underline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(189)

3.

Optimality.
Any quantum channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{C}
on pairs of channels in
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
, must satisfy

\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\quad\forall\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)\;.

(190)

4.

Sub/Super Additivity.
If
\mathbf{C}
is weakly additive under tensor products then
\underline{\mathbf{C}}
is super-additive and
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is sub-additve. Explicitly, for any
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and any
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\in{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\geqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})

(191)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\big\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\|\mathcal{N}_{1})+\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2})\;.

5.

Regularization.
If
\mathbf{C}
is weakly additive under tensor products then any weakly additive quantum channel divergence
\mathbb{D}
that reduces to
\mathbf{C}
on pairs of channels in
\mathfrak{R}(A\to B)
, must satisfy

\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\quad\forall\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)\;,

(192)

where

\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\underline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{\rm reg}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\overline{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\;,

(193)

and
\underline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{C}}^{{\rm reg}}
are themselves weakly additive normalized channel divergences.

Proof.

In
Gour and Tomamichel 2020b
the same theorem was proved for the quantum-state domain. The proof for the channel domain follows similar lines as we show below.

Reduction.
This property follows from the definition and the fact that
\mathbf{C}
satisfies the DPI when restricted to
\mathfrak{R}
(see Definition
2
of an
\mathfrak{R}
-divergence).

Data Processing Inequality.
By definition,

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)

\displaystyle=\sup_{A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}}\Big\{\mathbf{C}\big(\Upsilon\circ\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Upsilon\circ\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\;:\;\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}),\;\;\Upsilon\circ\Theta[\mathcal{M}],\Upsilon\circ\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime\prime}\to B^{\prime\prime})\Big\}

(194)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}}\Big\{\mathbf{C}\big(\Gamma[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Gamma[\mathcal{N}]\big)\;:\;\Gamma\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}),\;\;\Gamma[\mathcal{M}],\Gamma[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime\prime}\to B^{\prime\prime})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;,

where the inequality follows from the replacement of
\Upsilon\circ\Theta
with any
\Gamma\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime})
. Similarly,
let
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime})
and observe

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\inf_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\Big\{\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})\;:\;\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})\;\;,\;\;\exists\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to AB)\;\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{M}=\Upsilon[\mathcal{E}]\;\text{,}\;\mathcal{N}=\Upsilon[\mathcal{F}]\Big\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\Big\{\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})\;:\;\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})\;\;,\;\;\exists\Upsilon\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to AB)\;\text{ s.t. }\Theta[\mathcal{M}]=\Theta\circ\Upsilon[\mathcal{E}]\;\text{,}\;\Theta[\mathcal{N}]=\Theta\circ\Upsilon[\mathcal{F}]\Big\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\Big\{\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})\;:\;\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})\;\;,\;\;\exists\Gamma\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime})\;\text{ s.t. }\Theta[\mathcal{M}]=\Gamma[\mathcal{E}]\;\text{,}\;\Theta[\mathcal{N}]=\Gamma[\mathcal{F}]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathbf{C}}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\;,

where the first inequality follows from the simple fact that if
\mathcal{M}=\Upsilon[\mathcal{E}]
then also
\Theta[\mathcal{M}]=\Theta\circ\Upsilon[\mathcal{E}]
(but the converse is not necessarily true), and the second inequality follows from the replacement of
\Theta\circ\Upsilon
with any
\Gamma\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime})
.

Optimality.
For any
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
such that
\Theta[\mathcal{M}],\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
we have

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant\mathbb{D}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)=\mathbf{C}\big(\Theta[\mathcal{M}]\big\|\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\big)\;.

(195)

Since the inequality above holds for all such
\Theta
it holds also for the supremum over such
\Theta
. That is,

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant\underline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
. Similarly, for any
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{R}(A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime})
for which there exists
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\to AB)
such that
\mathcal{M}=\Theta[\mathcal{E}]
and
\mathcal{N}=\Theta[\mathcal{F}]
we get

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})=\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{E}\|\mathcal{F})\;.

(196)

Since the inequality above holds for all such
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}
it holds also for the infimum over such
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
. That is,

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\leqslant\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
.

Sub/Super Additivity.
The super-additivity of
\underline{\mathbf{C}}
follows from the restriction of
\Theta
in the definition of
\underline{\mathbf{C}}
in (
48
) to have the form
\Theta_{1}\otimes\Theta_{2}
. Similarly, the sub-additivity of
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
follows from the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\Theta
in (
49
) to have the tensor product form
\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}
, and
\Theta_{1}\otimes\Theta_{2}
, respectively.

Regularization.
This property follows directly from the optimality property when replacing
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
in (
190
) with
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
, dividing everything by
n
, taking the limit
n\to\infty
, and using the additivity of
\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a channel divergence that reduces to
D_{\max}
on classical probability distributions; i.e. for any classical system
X
and
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathfrak{D}(X)
,
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})=D_{\max}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})
. Then, for all
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(197)

Proof.

We need to show that for any
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
, the minimal and maximal extensions of
D_{\max}
collapse into a single function given by

\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\overline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\log\min\Big\{t\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;t\mathcal{M}\geqslant\mathcal{N}\Big\}\;.

(198)

Since
\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant\overline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
it is sufficient to prove that
\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\geqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
and
\overline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\leqslant D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
. The latter follows directly from Property 6 of Theorem
1
.
For the first inequality, in the definition of
\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
, we take
\Theta\in{\rm SC}(AB\to X)
to be the superchannel

\Theta[\mathcal{N}]\coloneqq\Delta^{RB\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)

where
R\cong A
,
|X|=|RB|
, and
\Delta^{RB\to X}
is the completely dephasing channel, where the classical basis of
X
is chosen to consist of the eigenvectors of

s\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)-\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\;,

(199)

where
s\coloneqq 2^{D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\big\|\mathcal{N}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\right)}
.
By definition

\displaystyle\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\geqslant D_{\max}\Big(\Delta^{RB\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\Big\|\Delta^{RB\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\Big)

(200)

\displaystyle=\log\min\Big\{t\;:\;\Delta^{RB\to X}\circ\left(t\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}-\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\right)\left(\phi_{+}^{RA}\right)\geqslant 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=\log\min\Big\{t\;:\;s\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}-\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}+\left(t-s\right)\Delta^{RB\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}\geqslant 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=\log s

where the last equality follows from the fact that
(s\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{N})(\phi_{+}^{RA})
has one eigenvalue that is zero (since
s
is the smallest number satisfying
s\mathcal{M}\geqslant\mathcal{N}
). Hence, if
t<s
then
(t-s)\Delta\circ\mathcal{M}(\phi_{+}^{RA})
, which commutes with
(s\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M})(\phi_{+}^{RA})
will make the zero eigenvalue strictly negative.
Hence,

\underline{D}_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\geqslant\log s=D_{\max}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\;.

(201)

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a jointly convex quantum divergence. Then, its maximal channel-extension
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
is also jointly convex.

Proof.

Proof of Theorem
8

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
and for any
\epsilon\in[0,1)
, let
\mathscr{D}=D_{\min}^{\epsilon}
be the classical hypothesis testing divergence.
Then,

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA}))\;.

(203)

Proof.

Observe that from the expression in (
78
)

\underline{D}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\sup_{\psi,\mathcal{E}}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\big\|\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\Big)\;.

(204)

Let
T=\sum_{x}t_{x}|x\rangle\langle x|^{X}
be a classical test operator with
0\leqslant t_{x}\leqslant 1
, and note that

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Tr}\left[T\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\Gamma^{RB}\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\right]

(205)

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Tr}\left[T\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\Gamma^{RB}\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\right]

where
\Gamma^{RB}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{X\to RB}^{{\dagger}}(T)
. Since
0\leqslant T\leqslant I
also
0\leqslant\Gamma^{RB}\leqslant I^{RB}
, and for any
\Gamma^{RB}
with this property there exists
0\leqslant T\leqslant I^{X}
and
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CPTP}(RB\to X)
such that
\mathcal{E}_{X\to RB}^{{\dagger}}(T)=\Gamma^{RB}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\underline{D}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)\;,\;0\leqslant\Gamma^{RB}\leqslant I^{RB}\\
{\rm Tr}\left[\Gamma^{RB}\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\right]\geqslant 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}-\log{\rm Tr}\left[\Gamma^{RB}\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\right]

(206)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{D}(RA)}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{M}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\big\|\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\psi^{RA})\right)

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
9

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be two quantum channels. Then,

\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(207)

That is,
D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
is the smallest weakly additive quantum-channel divergence that reduces to the KL relative entropy on classical states.

Proof.

Due to (
84
) it is left to show that
\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\geqslant D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
. We make use here of the inequality

D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}(D(\rho\|\sigma)+h_{2}(\epsilon))

(208)

Combining this with Lemma
8
gives

\displaystyle D_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})=\underline{D}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\psi,\mathcal{E}}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\big\|\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\Big)

(209)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\sup_{\psi,\mathcal{E}}D\Big(\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{N}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\big\|\mathcal{E}_{BR\to X}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\to B}(\psi_{AR})\Big)+\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}h_{2}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\left(\underline{D}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})+h_{2}(\epsilon)\right)\;.

Diving both sides by
n\in\mathbb{N}
, replacing the pair
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
with
(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n},\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})
, and taking the limit
n\to\infty
followed by
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
gives

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{\min}^{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\right)\leqslant\underline{D}^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})\;.

(210)

In
XinWilde2020
it was shown that the quantity on the LHS equals
D^{{\rm reg}}(\mathcal{M}\|\mathcal{N})
. Therefore, this completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)
be an isometry channel defined via
\mathcal{V}(\rho)=V\rho V^{*}
, for all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(A)
, and with isometry matrix
V
(i.e.
V^{*}V=I^{A}
). Then, for any
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to B)

\overline{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})=\log{\rm Tr}\left[J_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}J_{\mathcal{V}}\right]\;.

(211)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{V}
is an isometry, the condition
\mathcal{V}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}
, can hold only if for all
x
such that
p_{x}\neq 0
we have
\mathcal{E}_{x}=\mathcal{V}
. W.l.o.g. let the
k
first components of
\mathbf{p}
be non-zero, while all the remaining components are zero. This implies that the second condition can be expressed as

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{x=1}^{k}q_{x}\mathcal{V}+\sum_{x=k+1}^{n}q_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}\;.

(212)

Denote by
s\coloneqq\sum_{x=1}^{k}q_{x}
, and observe that there exists such
\{\mathcal{E}_{x}\}_{x=k+1}^{n}
if and only if

\mathcal{N}\geqslant s\mathcal{V}

(213)

or in other words, iff
s^{-1}\geqslant 2^{D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})}
. Consider the classical channel

defined by

\mathcal{C}(|x\rangle\langle x|)=|1\rangle\langle 1|\;\;\forall x=1,...,k\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}(|x\rangle\langle x|)=|2\rangle\langle 2|\;\;\forall x=k+1,...,n\;.

(214)

Therefore, we must have

.
This means that w.l.o.g. we can assume that
\mathbf{p}=|1\rangle\langle 1|
and
\mathbf{q}
is binary; i.e.
\mathbf{q}=s|1\rangle\langle 1|+(1-s)|2\rangle\langle 2|
so that

\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})=-\log(s)
(cf. (
)). But since we must have
s^{-1}\geqslant 2^{D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})}
, the minimum value is achieved when

\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{p}\|\mathbf{q})=D_{\max}(\mathcal{V}\|\mathcal{N})
. This completes the proof.
∎

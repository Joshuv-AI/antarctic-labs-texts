Stress blow-up analysis when suspending rigid particles approach boundary in 3D Stokes flow
Haigang Li, Longjuan Xu, PeiHao Zhang

Abstract
. The stress concentration is a common phenomenon in the study of fluid-solid model. In this paper, we investigate the boundary gradient estimates and the second order derivatives estimates for the Stokes flow when the rigid particles approach the boundary of the matrix in dimension three. We classify the effect on the blow-up rates of the stress from the prescribed various boundary data: locally constant case and locally polynomial case. Our results hold for general convex inclusions, including two important cases in practice, spherical inclusions and ellipsoidal inclusions. The blow-up rates of the Cauchy stress in the narrow region are also obtained. We establish the corresponding estimates in higher dimensions greater than three.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background and Problem Formulation

In high-contrast composite materials, the physical fields in the narrow region between two adjacent inclusions or between the inclusions and the matrix boundary, such as the electric fields and the stress fields, may be concentrated caused by this microstructure and become arbitrary large. In the last two decades, there are many important progresses on the study of the stress concentration phenomena for two close-to-touching particles in the contexts of Laplace equation for electrostatic field, Lamé system for linear elasticity and Stokes equations for viscous flow. In this paper, we investigate the boundary estimates for the gradient of the solutions to the incompressible Stokes
flow in a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d\geq 3)

\mu\,\Delta{\bf u}=\nabla p,\quad\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,

when the rigid particles suspending inside are very close to the matrix boundary
\partial{D}
. The aim is to classify the effect on the blow-up rates of the gradient and the Cauchy stress from various boundary data.

Based on the geometry of the particle and the domain and the given boundary data, we construct a class of auxiliary functions to capture the main singularity of the stress term and the pressure term. We first derive the optimal blow-up rates of the stress, via establishing the pointwise upper bounds in the neck region and the lower bounds at the midpoint of the shortest line in between the particle and the boundary, and then proceed to estimate the second order derivatives provided the domain and the boundary data are sufficiently smooth. The advantage of this method is that when we deal with the pressure term we do not need to solve the stream function. The simplified model in 2D was considered in
[
39
]
. This is a continuation. However, Several estimates have to be improved to asymptotic expansion and new difficulty in the derivation of the optimality of the blow-up rate is overcome. Our results hold for general convex particles, including the two important and useful cases in practical application: spherical inclusions and ellipsoid inclusions in dimension three. Our method also can be extended to deal with the problem in higher dimensions greater than three. The corresponding upper bounds are presented as well.

C^{3}
and the boundary data
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\partial D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, because we will use the

-estimates in
[

, see Step 3 in the proof of Proposition
2.2
for the details. Suppose
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded open set with
C^{3}
boundary, and
D_{1}^{0}
is a
C^{3}
strictly convex subset of
D
, touching
\partial D
, say, at the origin, and the
x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1}
-axes being in their common tangent plane, after a translation and rotation of coordinates if necessary. We use
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1})
. By a translation, set
D_{1}^{\varepsilon}:=D_{1}^{0}+(0^{\prime},\varepsilon)
, for small constant
\varepsilon>0

For the sake of simplicity, we drop the superscript
\varepsilon
and denote

D_{1}:=D_{1}^{\varepsilon}\,\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega:=D\setminus\overline{D}_{1}.

Then
\overline{D}_{1}\subset D
,
\varepsilon:=\mbox{dist}(D_{1},\partial D)
.
We assume that the
C^{3}
norms of
\partial D
and
\partial D_{1}
are bounded by a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Let

\Psi:=\Big\{{\bm{\psi}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})~|~e({\bm{\psi}}):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{\bm{\psi}}+(\nabla{\bm{\psi}})^{\mathrm{T}})=0\Big\},

be the linear space of rigid displacements in

. It is well known that

is a basis of
\Psi
, where
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Consider the following Dirichlet problem, describing one rigid particle suspending in the Stokes flow,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mu\Delta{\bf u}=\nabla p,\quad\quad~~\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,&\hbox{in}~~\Omega:=D\setminus\overline{D}_{1},\\
{\bf u}|_{+}={\bf u}|_{-},&\hbox{on}~~\partial{D_{1}},\\
e({\bf u})=0,&\hbox{in}~~D_{1},\\
\int_{\partial{D}_{1}}\frac{\partial{\bf u}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}_{\alpha}-\int_{\partial{D}_{1}}p\,{\bm{\psi}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nu=0,&\alpha=1,2,\dots,\frac{d(d+1)}{2},\\
{\bf u}={\bm{\varphi}},&\hbox{on}~~\partial{D},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mu>0
,
{\bm{\psi}}_{\alpha}\in\Psi
,

\frac{\partial{\bf u}}{\partial\nu}\big|_{+}:=\mu(\nabla{\bf u}+(\nabla{\bf u})^{\mathrm{T}})\nu
,
\nu
is the unit outward normal vector of
D_{1}
, and the subscript
+
indicates the limit from inside the domain. Furthermore, by using the Gauss theorem and
\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0
in
\Omega
, we know the prescribed velocity field
{\bm{\varphi}}
satisfies the compatibility condition:

\int_{\partial{D}}{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\nu\,=0,

(1.2)

which ensures the existence and uniqueness of the solution. See
[
1
,
46
,
23
]
.

In order to formulate our results precisely, we assume that near the origin the boundaries
\partial D_{1}
and
\partial D
are expressed by the graphs of two functions, respectively. Namely, for some
0<R<1
,

x_{d}=\varepsilon+h_{1}(x^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad x_{d}=h(x^{\prime}),\quad\text{for}~|x^{\prime}|\leq 2R,

where
h_{1}
and
h
are
C^{3}
functions, satisfying

\displaystyle h(x^{\prime})<\varepsilon+h_{1}(x^{\prime}),\quad\mbox{for}~~|x^{\prime}|\leq 2R,

\displaystyle h_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=h(0)=0,\quad\nabla_{x^{\prime}}h_{1}(0)=\nabla_{x^{\prime}}h(0)=0,

\displaystyle h_{1}(x^{\prime})=\kappa_{1}|x^{\prime}|^{2}+

\displaystyle O(|x^{\prime}|^{3}),\quad h(x^{\prime})=\kappa|x^{\prime}|^{2}+O(|x^{\prime}|^{3}),\quad\mbox{for}~~|x^{\prime}|<2R,

where we assume that the constants
\kappa_{1}>\kappa
. For
0\leq r\leq 2R
, we denote the neck region

\Omega_{r}:=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega:~h(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<\varepsilon+h_{1}(x^{\prime}),~|x^{\prime}|<r\right\}.

(1.3)

Throughout this paper,
C
denotes a
universal constant
, which means the value of
C
may vary from line to line, depends only on

, and the upper bounds of the
C^{3}
norm of
\partial{D}
,
\partial{D}_{1}
, but independent of
\varepsilon

1.2.
Upper Bounds of

Let

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot\sigma[{\bf u},p]=0,~~\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,&\hbox{in}~~\Omega,\\

Lower Bounds of

Step 1. Global boundedness of

:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\,C.

We first recall that if

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega_{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}{\bf f}^{(j)}{\bf w}^{(j)}\mathrm{d}x\Big|\leq\,C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x^{\prime}|=R,\\
h(x^{\prime})<x_{3}<\varepsilon+h_{1}(x^{\prime})\end{subarray}}|{\bf w}|\mathrm{d}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}.

(2.24)

By using (
2.18
), the integration by parts with respect to
x^{\prime}
, and (
2.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}{\bf f}^{(1)}{\bf w}^{(1)}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}\mu{\bf w}^{(1)}\big(\partial_{x_{1}x_{1}}{\bf v}^{(1)}+\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}{\bf v}^{(1)}-\partial_{x_{1}x_{3}}{\bf v}^{(3)}\big)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\,C\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}{\bf w}^{(1)}|\big(|\partial_{x_{1}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|+|\partial_{x_{2}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|+|\partial_{x_{3}}{\bf v}^{(3)}|\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+C\,\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x^{\prime}|=R,\\
h(x^{\prime})<x_{3}<\varepsilon+h_{1}(x^{\prime})\end{subarray}}|{\bf w}^{(1)}|\mathrm{d}x_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}{\bf w}^{(1)}|\big(|\partial_{x_{1}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|+|\partial_{x_{2}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|+|\partial_{x_{3}}{\bf v}^{(3)}|\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}=:\mathrm{I}_{11}+\mathrm{I}_{12}.

From (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and (

Combining with Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\mathrm{I}_{11}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}}\big(|\partial_{x_{1}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|^{2}+|\partial_{x_{2}}{\bf v}^{(1)}|^{2}+|\partial_{x_{3}}{\bf v}^{(3)}|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}{\bf w}^{(1)}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}{\bf f}^{(1)}{\bf w}^{(1)}\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}.

Similarly, using (

2.16
), (

\displaystyle\,C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf w}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a non Archimedean local field, with ring of integers denoted by
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
. Let
q
denote the cardinality of of the residue field
k_{F}
of
F
, and
p
the characteristic of
k_{F}
. Let
G
be the group of
F
-rational points of connected reductive algebraic group defined over
F
.

A
supercuspidal pair
in
G
is a pair
(L,\sigma)
, consisting
of a Levi subgroup
L
of a parabolic subgroup of
G
and a smooth irreducible supercuspidal representation
\sigma
of
L
. Given
\pi
a smooth irreducible representation of
G
, there is a supercuspidal pair
(L,\sigma)
such that
\pi
is isomorphic to a subquotient of a parabolically induced representation from
\sigma
. The
G
-conjugacy class of
(L,\sigma)
is uniquely determined and is called the
supercuspidal suppport
of
\pi
.

The group
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)
of unramified characters of
L
acts by tensorisation on the set of irreducible supercuspidal representations of
L
, and we denote by
{\mathfrak{s}}=[L,\sigma]_{G}
the
G
-conjugacy class of the pair

(L,{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)\cdot\sigma)
. Let
{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
be the set of such
{\mathfrak{s}}
. We call
supercuspidal
every
{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
such that
L=G
. By a theorem of Bernstein
[
Ber
]
, the category
{\mathfrak{R}}(G)
of smooth representations of
G
decomposes as a product

(1.0.1)

{\mathfrak{R}}(G)=\prod_{{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{B}}(G)}{\mathfrak{R}}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G),

where
{\mathfrak{R}}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
is the full subcategory consisting of those representations every irreducible subquotient of which has its supercuspidal suppport in
{\mathfrak{s}}
. We denote by
\Irr^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
the set of irreducible objects in
{\mathfrak{R}}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
.

Let
(J,\lambda)
be a pair consisting of a compact open subgroup
J
of
G
and a smooth irreducible representation
\lambda
of
J
.
The pair
(J,\lambda)
(or simply the representation
\lambda
) is called
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical
, a notion introduced by Henniart in
[
He
]
, if every smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
such that
\Hom_{J}(\lambda,\pi)\neq\{0\}
is in
\Irr^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
.

Following Bushnell-Kutzko’s terminology in
[
BK3
]
, we say that the pair
(J,\lambda)
(or simply the representation
\lambda
) is an
{\mathfrak{s}}
-type
for
G
, if it is
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical, and if every representation
\pi
in
\Irr^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
satisfies
\Hom_{J}(\lambda,\pi)\neq\{0\}
. If
(J,\lambda)
is an
{\mathfrak{s}}
-type, then, by
[
BK3
, Theorem 4.3]
, the category
{\mathfrak{R}}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
is canonically equivalent to the category of (left) modules over the convolution
algebra of compactly supported
\lambda
-spherical functions on
G
.

For
G=\GL_{N}(F)
(resp.
G=\SL_{2}(F)
, with
p
odd), and
{\mathfrak{s}}
supercuspidal, every
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical representation of
\GL_{N}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
(resp.
\SL_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
) is actually an
{\mathfrak{s}}
-type, and is induced from a maximal simple type for
G
, see
[
He
,
Pa
]
(resp.
[
La1
]
).

For
G
an arbitrary
p
-adic group, a representation of
G
is said to have
depth zero
if it has non-zero vectors that are invariant under the pro-
p
unipotent radical of a parahoric subgroup of
G
. If
\sigma
has depth zero, then every representation of
G
in the orbit
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)\cdot\sigma
has also depth-zero. In this case, we say that
{\mathfrak{s}}
has depth-zero. Let

be a vertex in the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
, let
G_{x,0}
be the parahoric subgroup of
G
associated to
x
, and let

be the maximal compact subgroup of the

-normalizer

of

. Then
G_{x}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
which contains

as a normal subgroup of finite index, and every depth-zero supercuspidal irreducible representation of
G
is compactly induced from
the extension to
\widetilde{G}_{x}
of the inflation of a cuspidal irreducible representation of the reductive quotient of
G_{x}
, see
[
Mo1
]
or
[
MP
]
. If
{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
is supercuspidal and has depth zero, then again every
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical representation of
G_{x}
is an

{\mathfrak{s}}
-type, see
[
La2
]
.

However, for non-supercuspidal
{\mathfrak{s}}
, in general there exist
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical representations that are not
{\mathfrak{s}}
-types. For

, and
{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
arbitrary, a complete classification of the
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical representations of

, was obtained for

[
He
]
, and for

[

]
.

Let

-type such that

-typical.
It is expected that all the

-typical irreducible representation of

are obtained in this way. For

for all the depth-zero representations of

[

For non-supercuspidal

-types from

-types of Levi subgroups

.

In this article, we focus on the case where

, and prove the following result.

Theorem
.

(See Theorem
1
)

Let
{\mathscr{K}}
be a hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup of
G=\Sp_{4}(F)
, and let
{\mathfrak{s}}=[L,\sigma]_{G}\in{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
, with
L\neq G
. We suppose

The
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical irreducible representations of

-type for

.

Several of the arguments we use in the proof of the theorem above should extend to more general groups, and we plan to explore this in future works.

Let

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\cr 0&\phantom{-}{}^{t}a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\;:\,a\in\GL_{2}(F),b\in\Mat_{2}(F)\right\}\cap G,

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{s}}

is a group (since
J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}
normalizes
J_{L}
).
We set

(4.2.4)

\tau_{L}:=\Ind_{J_{L}}^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}\lambda_{L}.

Now we will prove our main result:

Theorem 1
.

Then
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical irreducible representations of
{\mathscr{K}}
are the irreducible components of
\Ind_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}^{\mathscr{K}}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}})
.

Remark 4.2.5
.

In the case where
\sigma
has depth zero, we can prove it as follows:
Let
\tau
be a
{\mathfrak{s}}
-typical representation of
{\mathscr{K}}
. Let
\lambda
as in (
3.0.2
).
Since
({\mathscr{P}},\lambda)
is a
G
-cover of the pair
({\mathscr{K}}_{L},\tau_{L})
, and hence a
{\mathfrak{s}}
-type, the pair
(J_{\mathfrak{s}},\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}):=({\mathscr{P}},\lambda)
satisfies the theorem by combining Lemma
4.1.5
and Proposition
3.0.3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1.5
, we are reduced to prove that

(4.2.6)

\Hom_{{\mathscr{K}}}(\ind^{{\mathscr{K}}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}\tau_{L},\ind_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}^{{\mathscr{K}}}\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}})\neq 0.

By Frobenius reciprocity, (
4.2.6
) equals

(4.2.7)

\Hom_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}(\Res_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}^{{\mathscr{K}}}\circ\ind^{{\mathscr{K}}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}\tau_{L},\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}).

By the Mackey formula, it equals

(4.2.8)

\bigoplus_{g\in J_{\mathfrak{s}}\backslash{\mathscr{K}}/{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}\Hom_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left(\Ind_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}\cap{}^{g}{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}^{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ{\mathrm{Ad}}(g)\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{g}}(\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}\tau_{L}),\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}\right).

We will consider the term
g=1
in the sum above. Firstly, we observe, that by (
4.2.2
), we have
\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}=\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}
.
Whenever
j_{P}
belongs to
{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}=J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}
, we have
j_{P}=j_{L}j_{U}
, with
j_{L}\in{\mathscr{K}}_{L}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}=J_{L}
and
j_{U}\in{\mathscr{K}}_{U}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}=J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}
. Then, we get

(4.2.9)

(\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}})\tau_{L}(j_{L})=\tau_{L}(j_{L}).

On the other hand, by (
4.2.2
),

\infl_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}/{\mathscr{K}}_{P}^{+}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}/{\mathscr{K}}_{P}^{+}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}=\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}},

and we have

(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}})(\tau_{L})(j_{L})=\tau_{L}(j_{L}).

Thus, we have shown that

(4.2.10)

\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}\cap J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ\infl^{{\mathscr{K}}_{P}}_{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}=\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}.

It follows that the term attached to
g=1
in (
4.2.8
) equals

(4.2.11)

\Hom_{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\left(\ind_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}^{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\circ\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\tau_{L}),\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}\right).

By Frobenius reciprocity, it equals to

(4.2.12)

\Hom_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\left(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\tau_{L}),\Res_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}^{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}\right).

On the other hand, (
4.2.12
) equals

\Hom_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\left(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\tau_{L}),\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\lambda_{L}\right).

since
\Res_{J_{L}}^{J_{\mathfrak{s}}}\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}=\lambda_{L}
and
\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}
is trivial on
J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}
(because
(J_{\mathfrak{s}},\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}})
is a
G
-cover of
(J_{L},\lambda_{L})
). On the other hand, by using the Mackey formula we get

\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\tau_{L})=\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\ind_{J_{L}}^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}\lambda_{L})=\bigoplus_{z\in J_{L}\backslash{\mathscr{K}}_{L}/J_{L}}\ind_{J_{L}\cap J_{L}^{z}}^{J_{L}}\lambda_{L}^{z},

Hence we can deduce that

\Hom_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\left(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\circ\Res^{{\mathscr{K}}_{L}}_{J_{L}}(\tau_{L}),\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\lambda_{L}\right)\neq 0,

by looking at its summand corresponding to
z=1
:

\Hom_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\left(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\ind_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}}\lambda_{L},\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\lambda_{L}\right)=\Hom_{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\left(\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\lambda_{L},\infl_{J_{L}}^{J_{L}J_{\mathfrak{s}}^{+}}\lambda_{L}\right)\neq 0,

and thus the theorem follows.
∎

On the p-rank of singular curves and their smooth models
SADIK TERZİ

Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with the computation of the p p -rank and a a -number of singular curves and their smooth model. We consider a pair X , X ′ X,X^{\prime} of proper curves over an algebraically closed field k k of characteristic p p , where X ′ X^{\prime} is a singular curve which lies on a smooth projective variety, particularly on smooth projective surfaces S S (with p g ​ ( S ) = 0 = q ⁡ ( S ) p_{g}(S)=0=q(S) ) and X X is the smooth model of X ′ X^{\prime} . We determine the p p -rank of X X by using the exact sequence of group schemes relating the Jacobians J X J_{X} and J X ′ J_{X^{\prime}} . As an application, we determine a relation about the fundamental invariants p p -rank and a a -number of a family of singular curves and their smooth models. Moreover, we calculate a a -number and find lower bound for p p -rank of a family of smooth curves.

In the following Proposition we will use the fact that since

Proposition 1

In the given setup, the following relations hold:

\sigma(J_{X})=\sigma(J_{X^{\prime}})-\sigma(G)=\sigma(J_{X^{\prime}})-r

2)

Proof.

1)

We apply
\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},.)
to sequence (1) and we get the exact sequence

0\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},G)\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},J_{X^{\prime}})\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},J_{X})\to\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},G).

We know that the groups

\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},\mathbb{G}_{m})~\mbox{and}~\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},\mathbb{G}_{a})
are trivial (
[

]

\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},G)=\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},\mathbb{G}_{m})^{r}\times\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},G_{u})=\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},G_{u}).

By induction on the chain of successive extensions defining

, we see that

\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\mu_{p},G_{u})

is trivial.

We compare the orders of the groups in sequence (1) to get

p^{\sigma(J_{X^{\prime}})}=p^{\sigma(J_{X})}p^{\sigma(G)}.

Thus we have
\sigma(J_{X})=\sigma(J_{X^{\prime}})-\sigma(G)
.

Since
\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},\mathbb{G}_{a})
is also trivial,
again by induction on the chain of successive extensions we find
\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\mu_{p},G_{u})=0
. Thus we get

Hence

2)

We apply
\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\alpha_{p},.)
to sequence (1) to get the exact sequence

0\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\alpha_{p},G)\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\alpha_{p},J_{X^{\prime}})\to\mbox{Hom}_{k-gr}(\alpha_{p},J_{X})\to\mbox{Ext}^{1}(\alpha_{p},G).

r\geq 0,\,s\geq 0\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}B_{r}^{s}\right)

We will finalize this section by finding a lower bound for the
p

\sigma(X)

is the smooth generalized Fermat curve over algebraically closed field

in
{\mathbf{P}}^{n}

\lambda_{1}x_{0}^{m}+x^{m}_{1}+x^{m}_{3}=0\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n-2}x_{0}^{m}+x^{m}_{1}+x^{m}_{n}=0\end{Bmatrix}\subset{\mathbf{P}}^{n}

where
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n-2}

\lambda_{i}\neq 0\text{ for }i=0,1,...,n-2
. We set

.
As a result, we state the following Theorem:

Theorem 16
.

Let
X
be the generalized Fermat curve
C^{m}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n-2})
in
{\mathbf{P}}^{n}
. We have the following inequality between the

-rank of generalized Fermat curves

\sigma(X)\geq\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{t}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{t}\leq n-2}\sigma(C^{m}(\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2}))

for

.

Proof.

For any
t-1
length subset
\{\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2}\}
of
\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n-2}\}
and for any
(a^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{i_{t}})\in S^{\prime}(r,s)
, set similar to

and defined for
C^{m}(\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2})
, we consider elements
(a_{0},\cdots,a_{n})\in S(r,s)
of the form

m&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We assume
(F^{*})^{l}(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{i_{t}}))\neq 0
for all

, i.e.,

\alpha_{r}^{s}(a^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{i_{t}})

-rank of
C^{m}(\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2})

(F^{*})^{l}(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{i_{t}}))=\displaystyle\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\alpha_{r_{j}}^{s_{j}}(a^{l_{j}}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{l_{j}}_{i_{t}})

C^{m}(\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2})

(F^{*})^{l}(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a_{0},\cdots,a_{n}))=\displaystyle\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\alpha_{r_{j}}^{s_{j}}(a^{l_{j}}_{0},\cdots,a^{l_{j}}_{n})+(\text{ other part })\neq 0

for each
l
and so the
t+1

(a^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\cdots,a^{\prime}_{i_{t}})\in S^{\prime}(r,s)

p
-rank

\sigma(C^{m}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n-2}))
. We have

\displaystyle F^{*}(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}))

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-2}f_{j}\right)(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}))^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{q=0}^{t}f_{i_{q}}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2,\,j\neq i_{q}\\
0\leq q\leq t\end{subarray}}^{n}x^{m}_{j}\right)(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}))^{2}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\left(\prod_{q=0}^{t}f_{i_{q}}\right)\left(\text{ other product}\right)(\alpha_{r}^{s}(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}))^{2}

for each

\sigma(X)\geq\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{t}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{t}\leq n-2}\sigma(C^{m}(\lambda_{i_{0}},\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots,\lambda_{i_{t}-2})).

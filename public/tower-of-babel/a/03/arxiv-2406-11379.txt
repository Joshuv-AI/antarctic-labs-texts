On existence of Sadovskii vortex patch: A touching pair of symmetric counter-rotating uniform vortices
Kyudong Choi, In-Jee Jeong, Young-Jin Sim

Abstract
. The Sadovskii vortex patch is a traveling wave for the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations consisting of an odd symmetric pair of vortex patches touching the symmetry axis. Its existence was first suggested by numerical computations of Sadovskii in [J. Appl. Math. Mech., 1971], and has gained significant interest due to its relevance in inviscid limit of planar flows via Prandtl–Batchelor theory and as the asymptotic state for vortex ring dynamics. In this work, we prove the existence of a Sadovskii vortex patch, by solving the energy maximization problem under the exact impulse condition and an upper bound on the circulation.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the 2D incompressible Euler equations in vorticity form:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad\partial_{t}\theta+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\\
&\quad{\bf u}=k*\theta,\\
&\quad\theta|_{t=0}=\theta_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\theta:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. Based on the Biot–Savart law, the kernel
k
is given as

k({\bf x})=(2\pi)^{-1}(-x_{2},x_{1})/|{\bf x}|^{2},

implying that
{\bf u}=(u^{1},u^{2})=\nabla^{\perp}\psi\mbox{ }
where
\mbox{ }\nabla^{\perp}=(\partial_{x_{2}},-\partial_{x_{1}})
with the stream function
\psi=\psi[\theta]
defined by

\psi:=(-\Delta)_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{-1}\theta=-\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|\cdot|\Big)*\theta.

In this paper, we are concerned with traveling waves of (
1.1
) with the following properties: The function
\theta:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\theta(t,{\bf x})=\Big(\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}\Big)({\bf x}-(W,0)t)

solves (
1.1
) for some constant
W>0
and for a bounded open set
A\subset\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}
satisfying that, for some
R>0
, we have

\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|{\bf x}|<R\}\subset\overline{A\cup A_{-}}

where
A_{-}
is defined as
A_{-}:=\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:(x_{1},-x_{2})\in A\mbox{ }\}.
In other words, we deal with vortex patch solutions whose support in the upper half plane
touches the horizontal axes
and travels with the constant velocity
(W,0)
without change in its profile. Hereafter, we will refer to such a solution as a
Sadovskii vortex patch
in honor of Sadovskii’s contribution
[
80
]
in 1971. While there has been extensive research on such
touching
vortex patches (including
[
22
,
73
,
76
,
82
,
83
,
104
,
78
,
91
]
), most of it has focused on their properties rather than proving their existence. We shall defer the detailed historical discussion to Subsection
1.1
below.

Our main result in this paper gives the existence of a Sadovskii vortex patch as a bounded open set in the upper half plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }x_{2}>0\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, normalized to have unit impulse
\int_{\Omega}x_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1,
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(Touching)
There exists a constant
R>0
such that

\big\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }|{\bf x}|<R\mbox{ }\big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\overline{\Omega\cup\Omega_{-}}.

(ii)

(Traveling wave)
The
(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2})-
function
\theta_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\theta_{0}:=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{-}}

generates a traveling wave in the sense that, for the constant
W>0
given by

W:=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\quad\mbox{ where }{\bf u}_{0}:=k*\theta_{0},

the function
\theta:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\theta(t,{\bf x}):=\theta_{0}({\bf x}-(W,0)t)

solves the 2D incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
).

(iii)

(Steiner symmetric with continuous boundary)
There exists a continuous function

l:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

\Big\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\overline{\Omega}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }x_{2}=s\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},s)\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }|x_{1}|\leq l(s)\mbox{ }\Big\}\quad\mbox{ for each }s\geq 0.

1.1.
Significance of Sadovskii vortex patch

Counter-rotating vortex pairs are one of the simplest flow patterns and easily observed in various situations, most notably in the wake of any flying objects. There have been numerous studies of counter-rotating vortex pairs from experimental, computational, and theoretical point of view. An excellent review of the literature is given in Leweke–Dizès–Williams
[
67
]
(see also Saffman
[
81
]
). While Sadovskii vortex can be thought of just an example of such vortex pairs, it has several distinctive characters as we shall explain below.

History of Sadovskii vortex
. The Prandtl–Batchelor theorem states that, if the vanishing viscosity limit of a two-dimensional flow has closed streamlines, then the limiting vorticity is piecewise constant in each region separated by the streamlines. Therefore, there has been significant interest in understanding potential shape of limiting closed streamlines in various flow geometries. The case of a streamline intersecting the fluid boundary is of particular interest. This was precisely the motivation of Sadovskii (1971)
[
80
]
: in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, he considered the problem of determining a region
\Omega
of uniform vorticity corresponding to a steady flow which is irrotational in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\backslash\Omega
. He assumed that the steady velocity is asymptotic to
-We_{1}
at infinity, which is precisely (
1.2
). The key assumption on
\Omega
was
\overline{\Omega\cap{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}}=[-1,1]
, and the problem was to determine the streamline connecting
(-1,0)
with
(1,0)
in the interior of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
(see Figure
1
). Sadovskii was able to numerically compute the family of such streamlines, parameterized by the strength of the vortex sheet (size of the velocity jump) across this interior streamline. Sadovskii’s original computation indicated that the case when the vortex sheet is absent is distinguished by the fact that the corresponding streamline is orthogonal to the domain boundary; see
[
80
, Figure 3]
. After this work, the term
Sadovskii vortex
was widely used to denote equilibrium inviscid flows involving uniform vorticity regions, potentially with vortex sheets, in various geometries (
[
21
,
23
,
42
,
73
,
3
,
97
]
). Therefore, to avoid confusion, in the current work we reserve the phrase
Sadovskii vortex patch
to denote a uniformly translating vortex patch, touching the boundary of the half-plane,
without
an accompanying vortex sheet. Sadovskii himself wrote
[
80
, p. 734]
that “
An analytical proof of the existence and uniqueness (or otherwise) of the solution of this system does not seem possible.
” While many authors have studied the properties of Sadovskii vortex patch since then (
[
102
,
22
,
21
,
23
,
42
,
52
,
64
,
73
,
76
,
82
,
83
,
78
,
91
,
104
]
), the rigorous proof of its existence seems to be still open. In particular, it is not clear whether the boundary of the vortex region defines a smooth curve up to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, the property assumed by Sadovskii and most of the subsequent authors. The paper
[
102
, Table I]
includes numerical computations for a Sadovskii vortex patch, which shows that when the maximum height from the axis is normalized to be
1
, the length touching the
x_{1}
-axis is approximately
3.398
, the area (or mass) is about
2.47
, and the vertical center of mass is around

[

Sadovskii vortex as the endpoint of the bifurcation diagram
. There exist a large set of counter-rotating vortex pairs, even if we restrict them to the class of symmetric and uniformly-translating ones. In what follows, we shall further assume that the uniformly translating vortex pairs have uniform normalized vorticity (i.e. vortex patch). We further assume for simplicity that the vortex is odd symmetric with respect to the
x_{1}
-axis and non-negative on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. The important physical parameters are the circulation
\Gamma
(=mass), traveling velocity
W
, and the impulse
\mu
. (Note that all of them are positive under our setting.) Using scale invariance of the incompressible Euler equations, one may normalize one of them: our choice is to normalize the impulse to be 1, as seen in the statement of Theorem
1.1
. In the upper half plane, a point vortex (vorticity given as the Dirac delta measure at a point) is formally a traveling wave solution of the Euler equations, and one may de-singularize it to obtain uniformly traveling vortex patches with
\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}}=O(\epsilon^{-1})
and
\Gamma,W,\mu=O(1)
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
(
[
10
,
15
,
100
]
). Normalizing such a vortex to be of unit vorticity can give a uniformly traveling vortex patch with
\Gamma=\mu=1
and
W=O(\epsilon)
. As
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, the patches are asymptotically circular and
W\to 0^{+}
. Then, one may find a branch of uniformly traveling patch solutions by increasing
W
and keeping
\mu=1
. Numerical studies show that there is a critical threshold at which the patch becomes a Sadovskii vortex and bifurcation cannot be continued further (
[
76
,
69
,
67
]
). This phenomenon seems to show relevance of Sadovskii vortex patch in dynamics of solutions; we expand this point further in Subsections
1.3
and
1.4
below.

Sadovskii vortex in three-dimensional flows
. In three-dimensional incompressible inviscid flows, a counter-rotating pair of vortex rings can approach each other, increasing their distance from the symmetry axis and at the same time giving rise to various instabilities. This “head-on collision” scenario of vortex rings was the subject of intense numerical and computational studies (
[
62
,
63
,
84
,
35
,
86
,
68
,
92
,
77
,
75
,
20
,
50
,
88
,
86
,
85
,
18
]
). Employing the axisymmetric Euler equations and imposing exact odd symmetry (this is equivalent with posing the Euler equations on the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
), one can suppress various instabilities and ask the asymptotic behavior of the axial cross section of the rings for large times.

Based on arguments based on variational principles, Childress
[
26
,
27
]
argued that the growth of the vorticity maximum
\|\omega(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}
as well as the distance of the vortex from the symmetry axis can grow at most by
t^{4/3}
as
t\to\infty
. (The exponent
4/3
can be explained by dimensional arguments based on kinetic energy conservation.) Later, Childress and his collaborators (
[
29
,
28
]
) performed numerical computations and provided theoretical and computational evidence that the optimal
t^{4/3}
growth is indeed achieved by vortex rings whose axial cross sections are asymptotic to the Sadovskii vortex after a time-dependent rescaling. As long as the cross section of the vortex ring is sufficiently localized, this is reasonable since far away from the symmetry axis, the axisymmetric Biot–Savart kernel converges to that for the two-dimensional Euler equations (
[
25
]
). Furthermore, note the numerical computations done by Carley
[
18
]
.

Here, it seems that the appearance of this limiting asymptotic profile is not depending sensitively on the shape of the initial vortex ring, and in particular, it is not needed that the initial ring is “attached” to the boundary of the upper half-space. Indeed, this dynamical appearance of the Sadovskii vortex patch was observed earlier and explicitly by Shariff–Leonard–Ferziger in 2008
[
85
]
for the head-on collision of two Hill’s spherical vortices, see
[
85
, Figures 8, 9]
. So far, the best known lower bound in time on the vorticity growth is slower than linear-in-time (
[

) and it seems that understanding stability of the Sadovskii vortex in the two-dimensional case is the natural first step towards establishing the sharp growth
t^{4/3}

1.2.
Technical statements

In this subsection, in order to more easily convey the ideas needed for the proof of our main result (Theorem
1.1
), we divide Theorem
1.1
into the following three theorems : Variational method (Theorem
1.2
), Touching property (Theorem
1.4
), and Boundary behavior (Theorem
1.7
).

To find a traveling wave solution, for some constant
W>0
, we are looking for a steady solution
({\bf U},\omega)
that solves

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad{\bf U}\cdot\nabla\omega=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&\quad{\bf U}=k*\omega-(W,0),\end{aligned}\right.

so that the solution
({\bf u},\theta)
in the form of

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\bf u}(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle={\bf U}({\bf x}-(W,0)t)+(W,0),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\theta(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle=\omega({\bf x}-(W,0)t),

solves (
1.1
). The equations (
1.2
) admits a
vortex patch
solution of a form

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mbox{ }\Psi>0\mbox{ }\}}=\ \left\{\begin{aligned} \mbox{ }{1}\quad&\mbox{for}\mbox{ }\Psi>0,\\
0\quad&\mbox{for}\mbox{ }\Psi\leq 0\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

with the odd symmetry
\omega(x_{1},-x_{2})=-\omega(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }\mbox{ for each }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\mbox{ }
and
{\bf U}=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi
where

\Psi({\bf x}):=\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma

for some constant
\gamma\geq 0
which is referred to as the flux constant in the literature.
One may easily observe that touching of a vortex patch implies
\gamma=0
. We will prove that such a solution with
\gamma=0
can be found, and it is a Sadovskii vortex patch.

We will obtain Theorem
1.1
by the following steps. In Theorem
1.2
, we will find a solution
\omega
of (
1.2
) satisfying that, with the constants
W>0
and
\gamma=0
, we have

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}>0\}}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

In Theorem
1.4
, for the function
\omega
we have found above, we will show that there exists
R>0
such that we have

\big[\mbox{ Touching }\big]\mbox{ }\quad\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:|{\bf x}|<R\mbox{ }\}\subset\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}.

In Theorem
1.7
we will say that the boundary of the set
\{\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\}
is continuous.

In Theorem
1.2
below, we use a variational method to prove that there exists a patch-type solution of (
1.2
) which consequently generates a traveling wave solution of (
1.1
) with the traveling speed
W>0
and the constant
\gamma=0
. In specific, we will solve the variational problem of finding a maximizer
\omega
of the kinetic energy with a fixed constraint on the impulse
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
can be found in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Define
K
by

K:=\Bigg\{\mbox{ }\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}\mbox{ }\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\quad\mbox{for some open bounded set }A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{satisfying}\mbox{ }\int_{A}x_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1\Bigg\}

and define
S\subset K
by the set of maximizers of the kinetic energy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x},\quad\mbox{ where }\mbox{ }{\bf u}:=k*\omega\quad\mbox{ for each }\mbox{ }\omega\in K.

Then the maximal kinetic energy in the class
K
is finite, and the set
S
is nonempty. Moreover, there exist absolute constants
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying
C_{1}<C_{2}
such that, for each
\omega\in S
, there exists a constant
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
which is open bounded, satisfying the following properties:

(i) By denoting
\omega_{\tau}
as the translation of
\omega
of amount
(\tau,0)
by
\omega_{\tau}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\omega(x_{1}-\tau,x_{2})
, we have

\omega_{\tau}=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{-}}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Moreover, we get

\Omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\big\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\psi[\omega_{\tau}]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\big\}

with the constant
W>0
given by

W:=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\quad\mbox{ where }{\bf u}:=k*\omega.

(ii) There exists a function
l:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that we have

\Big\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }x_{2}=s\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }(x_{1},s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }|x_{1}|<l(s)\mbox{ }\Big\}\quad\mbox{ for each }s>0.

Moreover, we have

\Omega\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\big\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }|{\bf x}|<C_{0}\mbox{ }\big\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad C_{1}\mbox{ }<\mbox{ }|\Omega|\mbox{ }<\mbox{ }C_{2}

where
|\cdot|
denotes the (Lebesgue) measure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.2
, the traveling speed is proportional to the maximal kinetic energy. Moreover, observe that the condition
\int_{A}x_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1
in the definition of
K
can be replaced by
\int_{A}x_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\mu
for any

\omega^{\prime}({\bf x}):=\omega(\mu^{-1/3}{\bf x})\quad\mbox{ for }\mbox{ }\omega\in S

gives a one-to-one correspondence between the sets of maximizers. Then we get the same results with adjusted constants
W^{\prime},C_{0}^{\prime},C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2}^{\prime}>0
under the relations

{W^{\prime}=\frac{1}{3\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}^{\prime}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=W\mu^{1/3},}\quad C_{0}^{\prime}=C_{0}\mu^{1/3},\quad C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{1}\mu^{2/3},\quad C_{2}^{\prime}=C_{2}\mu^{2/3}.

\psi_{x_{2}}(0,0)>2W

\psi_{x_{2}}({\bf x})\geq\psi_{x_{2}}(0,0)-C|{\bf x}|^{1/2}>2W-W=W\quad\mbox{ for each }{\bf x}\in B_{R}(0,0)\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

which leads to

\psi({\bf x})=\int_{0}^{x_{2}}\Bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\psi\Bigg)(x_{1},s)\mbox{ }ds\mbox{ }>\mbox{ }Wx_{2}\quad\mbox{ for each }\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in B_{R}(0,0)\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

It gives
R\geq R_{0}>0
.

Lastly, Theorem
1.7
below implies Theorem
1.1
(iii). For its proof, see the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

.

Let
S
be the set of functions in Theorem

1.3.
Key ideas

Let us describe the key ideas that are involved in the proof of the main theorem.

Steady Euler equations and vorticity function
.
We recall the steady Euler equation (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, for an arbitrary traveling speed
W>0
. This equation allows a solution of the form

\omega=f(\Psi)\quad\mbox{ in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

for a function
f
called a
vorticity function
where the adjusted stream function
\Psi
is given by

\Psi({\bf x}):=\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma\quad\mbox{ for some constant }\gamma\geq 0

with the relation
{\bf U}=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi
. The constant
\gamma
is referred to as a
flux constant
. We extend the odd-symmetric solution
\omega
of (
1.2
) as

\omega(x_{1},-x_{2})=-\omega(x_{1},x_{2})\quad\mbox{ for each }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

In this paper, we put the Heaviside step function

(1.5)

f(\cdot)=\mathbf{1}_{\{\cdot>0\}},

(which is the limit of

f(t)=t^{1/(p-1)}_{+}
as
p
goes to infinity
2
2

2

cf. Lamb–Chaplygin dipole for
p=2
(e.g. see
[
2
]
).
)
as the vorticity function to obtain an odd-symmetric vortex patch solution given by

(1.6)

\omega=f(\Psi)=\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}}\quad\mbox{in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

In Theorem
3.3
, we prove that such a solution exists as a maximizer of the kinetic energy among functions under some constraints on their impulse and mass. Each maximizer is symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis in the upper half plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
(so-called
Steiner symmetric about
x_{2}
-axis
; see Definition
3.2
for details).

Touching implies zero flux constant (
\gamma=0
)
.
We here discuss by which conditions a solution (
1.6
) would be a Sadovskii vortex patch. We observe that any solution with positive flux constant
\gamma>0
cannot touch
x_{1}
-axis. Indeed, if
\gamma>0
, then for all sufficiently small
x_{2}>0
we have

{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }\notin\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\},

due to
\mbox{ }\psi(x_{1},x_{2})\Big|_{x_{2}=0}\equiv 0.\mbox{ }
In other words, for
\omega
to generate a Sadovskii vortex patch, we should have
\gamma=0
. However, it is not clear that
\gamma=0
is a sufficient condition for
touching
of the dipole, as the distribution of the stream function
\psi-Wx_{2}
is unknown.

Zero flux constant (
\gamma=0
) implies touching when symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis
.
In this paper, we show that any vortex patch solution, which has zero flux constant and is symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis, always touches
x_{1}
-axis. Unlike
[
8
]
(also see
[
2
, Section 6]
) which used the moving plane method
[
46
]
to produce a Lamb–Chaplygin dipole, we directly compare the distribution of
\psi
and
Wx_{2}
. More specifically, we want to compare the traveling speed
W>0
and the horizontal velocity
u^{1}=\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega]
to guarantee that

\psi_{x_{2}}-W>0\quad\mbox{ near the origin},

so that we get

B\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\psi-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}

for some ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
centered on
x_{1}
-axis. For the comparison, we first split the dipole as

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}=\omega_{+}+\omega_{-},\quad{\bf u}=\nabla^{\perp}\psi[\omega_{+}]+\nabla^{\perp}\psi[\omega_{-}]

where
\omega_{+}
is the nonnegative (upper) part of
\omega
whose support is in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, and obtain the following relation that, on
x_{1}
-axis,

u^{1}=2\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}]

thanks to the symmetry between
A
and
A_{-}
. We may observe that the eddy flow generated by the lower part
\omega_{-}
makes the upper part
\omega_{+}
propagate in the direction
(W,0)
, and vice versa. That is, the traveling speed
W
is the average value of the nonlocal velocity
\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}]
in the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(\omega_{+})\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, i.e. we may compare the traveling speed given by

W=\fint_{{\mathrm{supp}}\,(\omega_{+})}\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}]\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

and the horizontal velocity on
x_{1}
-axis, given by
\mbox{ }u^{1}(x_{1},0)=2\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}](x_{1},0),\mbox{ }
if we can estimate the distribution of the nonlocal velocity
\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}]
in the domain
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
. In Lemma
4.5
, we will prove that the nonlocal velocity
\psi_{x_{2}}[\omega_{-}]
has certain monotonicity and attains its unique maximum at the origin among all points in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
if the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(\omega)
is symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis. Therefore we obtain

u^{1}(0,0)>2W,

which means that the speed at the center of the dipole is more than twice as fast as the traveling speed of the entire dipole. Therefore,
\gamma=0
is an equivalent condition for
touching
, when the dipole vortex is symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis. From now on, we introduce the variational method to obtain a solution with
\gamma=0
.

Variational problem on exact impulse and mass upper bound
.
It is classical to use a variational method when seeking a steady solution to the Euler equations. In this paper, in some admissible set, we shall find a maximizer
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of the kinetic energy

E(\omega)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x},\quad{\bf u}=k*\omega.

Given a triple of parameters

\big(\mu,\mbox{ }\nu,\mbox{ }\lambda\big)\quad\mbox{ where }\mu,\nu,\lambda>0,

the admissible set

K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}

will be taken as the set of functions having the simple forms
\omega=\lambda\cdot(\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}})
for some
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, with the prescribed impulse condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\mu

and the upper bound of mass

\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\nu.

Here,
\lambda
is the strength of the vortex patch.
We denote the set of maximizers as
S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
and the maximal energy as
I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
(see Remark
3.1
for detailed discussions). The simple scaling argument of Remark
3.1
-(v) says that we can kill two parameters of the triple
(\mu,\nu,\lambda)
. For instance, the variational problem of parameters
(\mu,\nu,\lambda)
is equivalent to the problem of
(M,1,1)
where
M=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mu
. Indeed, the sets
S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
and
S_{M,1,1}
are bijective under some scaling map. Therefore we may assume
\nu=\lambda=1
and regard the impulse
\mu
as the only parameter of our variational problem.

Existence of maximizer
.
Theorem
3.3
says that, in the variational problem of the parameter
(\mu,1,1)
, there exists a maximizer, and any maximizer
\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}
has the form (
1.6
):

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\quad\mbox{ for some constants }\mbox{ }W>0,\mbox{ }\gamma\geq 0

with the odd-symmetry in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The proof consists of several steps:

(i) In Lemma
3.6
, we prove the existence of maximizers having the form of a simple function
\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}
. We first find a weak subsequential limit
\omega^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of an energy-maximizing sequence
\omega_{n}
in the larger admissible set: the set of odd-symmetric bounded functions
\omega
satisfying that
0\leq\omega\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with the upper bounds of impulse and mass

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\leq\mu,\quad\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 1.

We may assume that
\omega^{\prime}
and
\omega_{n}
satisfy the Steiner symmetrization condition, since we have

E(\omega^{*})\geq E(\omega)

if
\omega^{*}
is the Steiner symmetrization of
\omega
about
x_{2}
-axis. We will see that the sequence of kinetic energy
E(\omega_{n})
converges to
E(\omega^{\prime})
, implying that
\omega^{\prime}
is a maximizer of kinetic energy in the larger admissible set. Then we prove that
\omega^{\prime}
has the full impulse
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{2}\omega^{\prime}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\mu
and has the form
\omega^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}_{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.

(ii) Proposition
3.11
says that each maximizer
\omega
solves (
1.2
) in the sense that, for some constant
W,\gamma\geq 0
, the relation (
1.6
) holds. Knowing that
\omega
is a simple function
\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}
for some measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we first prove that there exist some constant
W,\gamma\geq 0
such that

A=\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\}.

For this, we generate a perturbation of
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}
in the larger admissible set. We shall construct a function
\eta\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
satisfying that

\eta\leq 0\mbox{ }\mbox{ in }A,\quad\eta\geq 0\mbox{ }\mbox{ in }A^{c},\quad\mbox{and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\eta=0.

Then for small
\epsilon>0
, we have
0\leq\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{A}+\epsilon\cdot\eta\mbox{ }\leq 1
, and the function
\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{A}+\epsilon\cdot\eta\mbox{ }
has the same mass and impulse with
\mathbf{1}_{A}
. The perturbed kinetic energy
E_{\epsilon}
of the odd-extension of
\mbox{ }(\mathbf{1}_{A}+\epsilon\eta)\mbox{ }
is maximized at
\epsilon=0
so as to

\frac{d}{d\epsilon}E_{\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi[\omega]\cdot\eta\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\leq 0.

We will use this inequality to prove that
A=\{\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}
for some constants
W,\gamma
, and the construction of
\eta
is the key part of the proof. We will define
\eta
so that the above inequality gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}(\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma)\mbox{ }h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\leq 0

for an arbitrary function
h
satisfying that
h\leq 0
in
A
and
h\geq 0
in
A^{c}
, which would eventually gives us the result. Meanwhile, the emergence of the constants
W,\gamma
from the formula of
\eta
are necessary. However, we observe that
W,\gamma
are determined by the boundary points of the set
\{\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}
(see (
3.2
)), while we yet only know that
A
is a measurable set. It means that the classical definition of (topological) boundary points is not applicable, and we shall define boundary-like points of general measurable sets which are called
the exceptional points
(see Definition
3.12
). This approach was developed in
[
30
]
.

Small impulse implies
\gamma=0
.
For the variational problem with parameters
(\mu,1,1)
, Lemma
4.4
and the estimate (
4.3
) in Lemma
4.6
guarantee that, if
\mu
is small enough, then we can get
\gamma=0
, i.e.
touching
, and prove that maximizers cannot achieve the full mass. It confirms our heuristic intuition for the case of
\lambda=1
: if the impulse imposed on the maximizers is small enough compared to the mass, each maximizer would rather be concentrated in a bounded region close to the axis than be stretched widely and thin, allowing for mass loss. On the other hand, one may infer that if the impulse dominates over the mass sufficiently, the maximizers would move away from the axis, that is,
\gamma>0
will be large enough.

Estimates using small impulse: admissible set without mass bound
.
Another result of dealing with small impulse, in addition to
touching
, is that we can find maximizers of the variational problems where the mass bound is omitted. In the below, we explain the reason why obtaining the following estimate:

\sup_{\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\mu^{2/3}\quad\mbox{ for small }\mu>0

is sufficient to guarantee the existence of the maximizer of the variational problem without mass bound. The above estimate follows from (
4.5
) in Lemma
4.6
, and we will shortly give the key idea of the estimate.

(i)
Variational problem without mass bound and prior estimate:
We first consider the variational problem of maximizing the kinetic energy in an admissible set given as the set of
L^{1}
simple functions
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}
having the constraint only on the impulse

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\mu\quad\mbox{ for }\mbox{ }\mu>0,

where the set of its maximizer functions is denoted by
S_{\mu,\infty}
, and the maximal energy is denoted by
I_{\mu,\infty}
.
Assuming that the set
S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty, we can obtain
\omega\in S_{\mu,\infty}
from each
\omega^{\prime}\in S_{1,\infty}
by the scaling
\omega(\cdot)=\omega^{\prime}(\mu^{-1/3}(\cdot)),
and get the priori estimate

(1.7)

\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\sim\mu^{2/3}.

(ii)
Difficulty:
It is nontrivial that the set
S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty because it is not clear that

I_{\mu,\infty}<\infty.

Indeed, the kinetic energy is bounded using
\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}
from the inequality (
2.5
):

E(\omega)\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Big)^{1/2},

and it is not clear that

\sup_{\omega\in S_{1,\infty}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}<\infty.

Although the kinetic energy of each function in the admissible set is finite, the maximal energy may be infinite, and there might not be any maximizer in the admissible set.

(iii)
Small impulse estimate guarantees existence of maximizers:

In this paper, in Lemma
4.6
, we obtain the estimation : for small
\mu>0
,

(1.8)

\sup_{\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\mu^{2/3}.

One may notice that this estimate coincides well with the priori estimate. From now on, we explain how the inequality above would imply that the set
S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty (For the formal proof, see Section
5
). In the meantime, we will observe that the power
2/3
is the critical number to solve the variational problem. First, to prove
S_{1,\infty}\neq\emptyset
, it suffices to show that there exists
\nu_{1}>0
such that

(1.9)

S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\nu_{1},1}\quad\mbox{ for any }\mbox{ }\nu>\nu_{1}.

It means that, for fixed impulse, any energy maximizer does not attain arbitrarily large mass, and that the maximal energy
I_{1,\nu,1}
stops increasing for large
\nu>0
. It would imply that

I_{1,\infty}=I_{1,\nu_{1},1}<\infty
and therefore
S_{1,\nu_{1},1}\subset S_{1,\infty}\neq\emptyset
. To show (
1.9
), we need to show that, for some
\nu_{1}>0
and each
\nu>\nu_{1}
, it holds that

\sup_{\omega\in S_{1,\nu,1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\leq\nu_{1},

or equivalently

\sup_{\omega\in S_{\nu^{-3/2},1,1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{\nu_{1}}{\nu}.

If we put
\mu:=\nu^{-3/2}
, we obtain the inequality

\sup_{\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\nu_{1}\cdot\mu^{2/3}

which must holds for small
\mu>0
. Therefore, we note that the estimate (
1.8
) is strong enough to prove that the set
S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty.

(iv)
To obtain (
1.8
) for small impulse:

In Lemma
4.6
, we obtain (
1.8
) by several steps. We sketch the strategy of the proof here: for each
\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}
with the traveling speed
W>0
and the flux constant
\gamma\geq 0
, we have

\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\leq\big|\mbox{ }\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi[\omega]({\bf x})>Wx_{2}\}\mbox{ }\big|

where
|\cdot|
denotes the (Lebesgue) measure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. One may find it inevitable to find the lower bound of
W
in terms of small
\mu>0
for estimating the measure of the set
\{\mbox{ }\psi[\omega]>Wx_{2}\mbox{ }\}
, knowing that function
\psi[\omega]
can be controlled by impulse
\mu
(see Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
). We can show that

\mu^{1/3}\lesssim W

which follows by the Pohožaev identity (
3.5
) in Lemma
3.21
(see
[
7
]
):

I_{\mu,1,1}=\frac{3}{4}W\mu+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\quad(\mbox{ where we know }\gamma=0\mbox{ }\mbox{ for small }\mu\mbox{ })

and by the lower bound estimate of maximal energy

I_{\mu,1,1}\gtrsim\mu^{4/3}\quad(\mbox{ thanks to a scaling })

given in Lemma
4.3
.

1.4.
Further technical discussions

Variational methods for vortex dipoles
.
A vortex dipole is a symmetric pair of vortices with opposite signs that move along the symmetry axis. This type of dipole is often referred to as a
counter-rotating vortex pair
. It is a model of coherent vortex structures in large-scale geophysical flows (for experimental works, see
[
55
]
,
[
40
]
and references therein). The existence via variational method goes back to Kelvin in 1880
[
95
]
, Arnold
[
4
]
in 1966, and Benjamin in 1976
[
5
]
.
In particular, we consider the
vorticity method
, a variational principle based on the
vorticity
. This method for vortex pairs was further developed by Turkington
[
96
]
and Burton
[
7
]
.
We refer to the work of Norbury
[
74
]
and Yang
[
103
]
for the stream function method.

Our variational setting is based on the vorticity method of Friedman–Turkington
[
43
]
in 1981 which was developed for vortex rings. Following
[
43
]
, we consider the class of vorticity

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega\quad\mbox{{is smaller than or equal to given } }\nu>0.\end{split}

After fixing
\nu
(e.g.
\nu=1
), as in
[
43
,
2
,
30
]
, the maximizers for small impulse
\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\omega>0

strictly lose
part of its mass (i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega<1
). Then we prove that the parameter
\nu
on mass becomes unnecessary. In other words, the maximizers are characterized by a single constraint on impulse (when small).
Our Sadovskii vortex patch is obtained as such a maximizer in
K_{\mu,1,1}
(i.e.
\omega\in S_{\mu,1,1}
for
\mu\ll 1
). Thus the Sadovskii patch can be considered as the limiting case
\mu\to 0
of the one parameter family

\mu>0
.

Approximating the point vortex dipole
.
The opposite limit (
\mu\to\infty
in
S_{\mu,1,1}
) can be understood in the following sense.
By scaling, we observe

\omega\in K_{\mu,1,1}\iff\hat{\omega}\in K_{1,1,(1/\varepsilon^{2})}

via the transform

\hat{\omega}(\mathbb{x})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\omega(\frac{\mathbb{x}}{\varepsilon})\quad\mbox{with}\quad\mu=1/\varepsilon.

The class
K_{1,1,(1/\varepsilon^{2})}
consists of
patches of strength
1/\varepsilon^{2}
with the unit vertical center of mass (
1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\omega/\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega
).
As
\varepsilon
goes to
0
, any sequence
\{\omega_{\varepsilon}\}
from the class
K_{1,1,(1/\varepsilon^{2})}
converges to the Dirac point mass: in other words,

(1.10)

\omega_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\delta_{(0,1)}-\delta_{(0,-1)}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The study of approximations to the point dipole, known as the
desingularization problem
, has been a subject of interest and research for a long time (e.g. see the classical book
[
66
]
of Lamb).
We refer to
[
96
,
104
,
90
,
59
,
13
]
and references therein. In general, for small
\varepsilon
, the boundary is smooth and closed to a circle, with the very recent paper
[
16
]
settling the uniqueness in this regime. For an analogue in vortex ring, see the Helmholtz’s rings of small cross-section
[
56
]
. One may wonder interaction between vortex dipoles/rings. For instance, we refer to
[
87
,
37
,
53
]
and references therein for leapfrogging phenomena.

Lamb–Chaplygin dipole and Hill’s spherical vortex
.
While we do not know the exact shape of dipoles including our Sadovskii vortex patch, there are other interesting examples which can be explicitly written. One is the Lamb–Chaplygin dipole
[
66
]
while the other is the Hill’s spherical vortex
[
58
]
. We also refer to
[
57
]
,
[
72
]
,
[
1
]
and references therein. The
Lamb–Chaplygin dipole
is an exact solution to (
1.2
) with zero flux constant
\gamma=0
and the vorticity function given by the Lipschitz function

(1.11)

f(\cdot)=\max\{\cdot,0\}

(see
[
66
]
).
Its support is simply a closed ball centered at the origin, and the dipole is symmetrically placed with opposite signs with respect to
x_{1}
-axis. Thanks to the structure of the vorticity function (
1.11
), the moving plane method
[
46
]
can be applied to characterize the dipole. A variational characterization of the dipole can be found in
[
8
,
9
]
(also see
[
2
]
for stability). For Hill’s spherical vortex, we refer to
[
30
]
and references therein.

Stability questions
.
The variational method produces an extremizer of a conserved quantity in an appropriate admissible class. Since Arnold’s work
[
4
]
, it is quite classical to deduce (Lyapunov) stability for steady solutions characterized by critical points of the energy (also see the explanation in
[
44
]
). The simplest example would be the circular patch (Rankine vortex), whose stability was rigorously proved by
[
99
]
. (Strictly speaking, patch solutions are not regular enough for Arnold’s argument to be directly applicable, cf.
[
89
,
34
]
.)
For vortex pairs, orbital stability was first obtained in
[
11
]
for a certain solutions by the vorticity method. Stability of Lamb–Chaplygin dipoles was proved in
[
2
]
(also see
[
100
,
32
]
). The recent paper of Wang
[
100
]
, which is a refinement of Burton
[
10
]
, proved stability an approximation for point vortex (
1.10
).
We also refer to
[
17
,
14
]
and references therein. All the stability results are restricted on
symmetric
perturbations. In general, non-symmetric disturbances may increase the corresponding energy as explained in
[
82
, p2342]
.
Lastly, uniformly rotating patch solutions are commonly referred to as “
V
–states”. For existence, rigidity and stability, we refer to
[
6
,
98
,
94
,
60
,
19
,
49
,
33
]
and references therein.

Vortex atmosphere is touching
.
In the steady Euler equations (
1.2
), we consider a solution
\omega
satisfying the Steiner symmetry condition and having a form
\mathbf{1}_{A}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{-}}
where

A=\{\mbox{ }\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\}

for some constant
W>0,\mbox{ }\gamma\geq 0
. Then each streamline
\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}:\Psi({\bf x})=C\mbox{ }\}
, generated by the adjusted stream function

\Psi:=\psi[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma

and characterized by
C\in\mathbb{R}
, is a integral curve of the flow velocity. We note that if a single streamline forms a closed curve and surrounds any bounded subset of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, the set travels along with the traveling vortex patch without change in its profile. By the
vortex atmosphere
we mean the union of all such subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. One may observe that the set
\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi({\bf x})-Wx_{2}>0\}
is the vortex atmosphere from the observations that the set
\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:\psi({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-C>0\}
is not bounded for each
C<0
, and is bounded for
C\geq 0
(see the proof of Lemma
3.18
). As we can prove that the set
\{\psi-Wx_{2}>0\}
touches the axis of symmetry (regardless of whether
\gamma>0
or
\gamma=0
), we may conclude that any vortex atmosphere touches the axis of symmetry even when the flux constant
\gamma
of the solution is positive. In other words, any traveling dipole vortex patch which is symmetrically concentrated about
x_{2}
-axis carries its certain neighborhood fluid including a part of the axis of symmetry.

1.5.
Open problems

We clarify several open problems regarding Sadovskii vortex.

Detailed shape
. Due to limitations of the vortex method, unfortunately we are unable to determine many conjectured properties on the shape of the Sadovskii vortex patch.

Conjecture 1
.

The bounded open set
\Omega
defining a Sadovskii vortex patch (as described in Theorem
1.1
) is connected, convex, and makes the right angle with the horizontal axis.

It seems that in general, one needs
certain regularity of the corresponding vorticity function
in order to prove connectedness of maximizers from variational method (e.g. see
[
41
,
12
]
). Our discontinuous vorticity function
f
in (
1.5
) is not regular enough to deduce
\Omega
consists of a single connected component.

Moreover, while several references claim to prove that the touching angle of
\Omega
with the horizontal axis should be
90
degrees using formal expansions of the velocity on the boundary (
[
102
,
103
,
83
]
), it is not even clear that the boundary function
l(s)
given in Theorem
1.1
defines an angle; it might keep oscillating as
s\to 0^{+}
, although the size of the oscillations should go to zero from continuity of
l
up to the boundary. However, under the assumptions that the angle is well-defined and
l
is
smooth up to the boundary
(not merely continuous), it can be made rigorous that the angle must be either
0,90,
or
180
degrees (cf.
[
38
]
). We also remark that proving the existence of
V
–states (uniformly rotating vortex patches) having 90 degree corners seems to remain open (
[
54
,
45
]
).

Furthermore, the existence of Sadovskii vortex accompanying vortex sheets on its boundary seems to be a very difficult open problem.

Variational properties
. There is a possibility that uniqueness still holds for small impulse
\mu>0
which would give stability for symmetric perturbations as in the case of Lamb–Chaplygin dipole
[
2
]
and Hill’s vortex ring
[
30
]
. Formally, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2
.

There exists a universal constant
\mu^{*}>0
such that, for all
0<\mu\leq\mu^{*}
, the set of maximizers
S_{\mu,1,1}
coincides with
x_{1}
-translation and rescaling of a single patch, which then can be referred to as
the
Sadovskii vortex patch. Furthermore, for all
\mu>\mu^{*}
,
S_{\mu,1,1}
only contains patches which are separated from the horizontal axis.

This conjecture is supported by the fact that so far no one was able to numerically find two different shapes of Sadovskii vortex patch, and if true, it would have significant consequences for the initial value problem of the 2D Euler equations. Furthermore, this would also explain why one could not continue, even at the numerical level, the bifurcation curve consisting of vortex dipoles beyond Sadovskii vortex patch. We remark that the analogous conjecture for the vortex rings was established recently in
[
30
]
.

Related to this, Pierrehumbert
[
78
]
and Saffman–Szeto
[
82
, p2342]
discussed the following more general problem.

Question 1
.

Is there a traveling wave
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{-}}
such that
\Omega
is a bounded open set of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying

|\Omega|=1\quad\mbox{while}\quad\quad\mu:=\int_{\Omega}x_{2}\,d{\bf x}<\mu^{*}?

It is possible that there are no such traveling wave patches, even if we do not require them to be maximizers of the energy in a suitable class. We note that Pierrehumbert calculated up to
\mu=0.29
and anticipated that
\mu^{*}\simeq 0.26
is the possible limit. It almost matches to the limiting number in
[
102
, Table I]
, after scaling.

1.6.
Outline of the paper

2

2.2.
Some technical estimates

We will collect some elementary lemmas which will be used in the sequel. In
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we denote the Green’s function
G:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

(2.1)

G({\bf x},{\bf y})\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\Bigg(1+\frac{4x_{2}y_{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}}\Bigg)>0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}.

The following lemma implies that the Green function is integrable for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, in the domain
\{{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }y_{2}<\alpha\}
for any
\alpha>0
. This lemma will be used later in the proof of Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for each
\alpha>0
and
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have

(2.2)

\int_{0<y_{2}\leq\alpha}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C\mbox{ }x_{2}^{1/2}\alpha^{3/2}.

Proof.

For each
\alpha>0
and
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have

\int_{0<y_{2}\leq\alpha}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0<y_{2}\leq\alpha}\log\Bigg(1+\frac{4\alpha x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}}\Bigg)\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{\alpha(4\alpha x_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Big)\mbox{ }dt\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }x_{2}^{1/2}\alpha^{3/2},

using the fact that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}<\infty

which is the consequence of integration by parts.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

For each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, we can define the function
\mathcal{G}[\omega]:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}.

The function
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
is well defined by the following lemma, which is a slight variation of
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
. We will prove that, for each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
, we get
\mathcal{G}[\omega]\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, we have

(2.3)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{1}\mbox{ }x_{2}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\quad\mbox{ for each }\mbox{ }{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

If we additionally assume that
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
, then there exists an absolute constant
C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.4)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{2}\Big(\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/3}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/3}+\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}\mbox{ }\Big)\quad\mbox{ for each }\mbox{ }{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

and that

(2.5)

\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf x})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}d{\bf y}\Bigl|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{2}\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}.

Proof.

The estimates (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) were borrowed from
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
. To show (
2.4
), assume that
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
and split the integral as

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\Big|\int_{|x-y|\geq x_{2}/2}\Big|+\Big|\int_{|x-y|<x_{2}/2}\Big|\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mbox{(I) + (II)}.

For (I), using the inequality,

\log(1+t)\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }t\quad\mbox{ for each }t>0,

we have

\mbox{(I)}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\int_{|x-y|\geq x_{2}/2}\frac{x_{2}y_{2}}{\pi|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}}|\omega({\bf y})|\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{4}{\pi x_{2}}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }=:A.

On the other hand, by (
2.3
), we obtain

\mbox{(I)}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }x_{2}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\mbox{ }=:B.

In sum, we have

\mbox{(I)}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }A^{\frac{1}{3}}B^{\frac{2}{3}}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/3}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/3}.

For (II), we put
\mathcal{B}:=B_{x_{2}/2}({\bf x})
and apply (
2.3
) to have

\mbox{(II)}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\Big(\omega\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\Big)({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }Cx_{2}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B})}^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{B})}^{1/2}.

Here observe that

\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{B})}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{2}{x_{2}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|y_{2}\omega({\bf y})|\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{2}{x_{2}}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}

due to the definition of
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore we have

\mbox{(II)}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}

which completes the proof of (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, the function
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}
, which is the odd extension of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
in Definition

, satisfies
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }C^{1,r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ }
for each

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}\mbox{ }\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{ with }\mbox{ }-\Delta\mbox{ }\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\widetilde{\omega}\quad\mbox{ a.e. }\mbox{ }\mbox{ in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}

where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the odd extension of

It is due to the relation

∎

We also obtain the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
and a different representation of the kinetic energy which were borrowed from

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
, we have

\lim_{|{\bf x}|\to\infty}\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }0.

Moreover, we have

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|{\bf u}\big|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

where
{\bf u}=k*\widetilde{\omega}
. Here
\widetilde{\omega}
is the odd extension of
\omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the kernel
k
is given in the equation
1.1
.

Proof.

By (
2.4
) in Lemma
2.3
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
and for the sequence
\{\omega_{n}\}=\{\omega\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B_{n}(0)}\}
, we have

\Big|\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\Big|\mathcal{G}[\omega-\omega_{n}]({\bf x})\Big|+\Big|\mathcal{G}[\omega_{n}]({\bf x})\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C\mbox{ }\Bigg(C_{n}+\frac{|{\bf x}|}{(|{\bf x}|-n)^{2}}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\Bigg)\quad\mbox{ for each }|{\bf x}|>n,

where

C_{n}:=\|\omega-\omega_{n}\|_{1}^{1/3}\|\omega-\omega_{n}\|_{2}^{1/3}\|x_{2}(\omega-\omega_{n})\|_{1}^{1/3}+\|\omega-\omega_{n}\|_{2}^{1/2}\|x_{2}(\omega-\omega_{n})\|_{1}^{1/2}.

Therefore we have

\limsup_{|{\bf x}|\to\infty}{|\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})|}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C\cdot C_{n}\quad\mbox{ for each }n\geq 1.

Observe that

as

, we obtain

\lim_{|{\bf x}|\to\infty}\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})=0.

This finishes the proof. ∎

3.
Existence of maximizers of patch-type

In this section, we will use the variational method to prove that there exists a patch-type maximizer of the kinetic energy in the admissible class whose setting is from
[
43
]
(see also
[
2
]
). Each maximizer will generate a traveling wave solution of (

). The main result of this section is Theorem

3.1.
Variational problem on exact impulse and mass bound

Given
\mu,\nu,\lambda>0,
we define an admissible set

\displaystyle K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega=\lambda\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A}

a.e.

\displaystyle\mbox{where }A\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\mbox{ is (Lebesgue) measurable, }

\displaystyle\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}=\mu,\quad\|\omega\|_{1}\leq\nu\mbox{ }\Bigl\}

on which its energy functional
E(\cdot):K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

E(\omega)\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf y})\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}d{\bf x}.

Note that, if
\omega
has a bounded support, then Lemma
2.5
implies that

E(\omega)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\bf u}|^{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

where
{\bf u}=k*\widetilde{\omega}
with
k
from (
1.1
) and
\widetilde{\omega}
is the odd extension of
\omega
. We formulate the maximization problem by

I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }:=\sup_{\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}}E(\omega)

and define the set of maximizers as

S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}:=\big\{\mbox{ }\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }E(\omega)=I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }\big\}.

Remark 3.1
.

(i) Recall that, for each
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}
, we have

G({\bf x},{\bf y})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }G({\bf y},{\bf x})>0.

It follows that,for each
\mbox{ }\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
, we have
E(\omega)\geq 0
and
I_{\mu,\nu.\lambda}>0
. Moreover, we get
I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}<\infty
by (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.3
.

(ii) If
f_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the translation of some fixed amount
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
along the
x_{1}
-axis,

i.e.

\mbox{ }f_{\tau}(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }(x_{1}+\tau,x_{2}),

then we have

G({\bf x},{\bf y})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }G(f_{\tau}({\bf x}),f_{\tau}({\bf y})).

Therefore, if
\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
, then any translation
\omega_{\tau}:=\omega\circ f_{\tau}
satisfies

\omega_{\tau}\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad E(\omega)=E(\omega_{\tau}).

(iii) If
g_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the lift by some fixed amount
h>0
along the
x_{2}
-axis,

i.e.

\mbox{ }g_{h}(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }(x_{1},x_{2}+h),

then for each
\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
, we have

E(\omega)<E(\omega\circ g_{-h}).

(iv) For each
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}
, we have

G({\bf x},{\bf y})=G(r{\bf x},r{\bf y})

for any
r>0
. By this property, for each
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}<\infty
, the scaling map given by
\hat{\omega}({\bf x}):=\omega(r{\bf x})
gives the relation

E(\hat{\omega})=r^{-4}E(\omega)\quad\mbox{ for each }r>0.

(v) As in
[
43
]
(and also in
[
2
]
), we can reduce the maximization problem to the case containing a single parameter. Indeed, for each
\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
, consider the scaling

\hat{\omega}({\bf x})=\lambda^{-1}\cdot\omega(\lambda^{-1/2}\nu^{1/2}{\bf x}).

Observe that
\hat{\omega}\in K_{M,1,1}
where
M=\lambda^{1/2}\nu^{-3/2}\mu>0
with the energy
E(\hat{\omega})=\nu^{-2}E(\omega).
Here, given
\mu,\nu,\lambda>0,
the map
\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega}
is a bijective map from
K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
onto
K_{M,1,1}
, and from
S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
onto
S_{M,1,1}
. Therefore, the two maximization problems below are congruent with each other,

I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }=\sup_{\omega\in K_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}}E(\omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad I_{M,1,1}\mbox{ }=\sup_{\omega\in K_{M,1,1}}E(\omega),

with the relation
I_{M,1,1}=\nu^{-2}I_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}\mbox{ }
. We hereafter abbreviate the notations as

K_{M}:=K_{M,1,1},\quad I_{M}:=I_{M,1,1},\quad S_{M}:=S_{M,1,1}.

3.2.
Existence of maximizers

For convenience, we start with a definition that will be frequently used in a sequel.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a nonnegative measurable function

f:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition if

f(x_{1},x_{2})=f(-x_{1},x_{2})\quad\mbox{ for each }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

and for each fixed
x_{2}>0
, the function
f(x_{1},x_{2})
is non-increasing in
x_{1}\geq 0
. We also say that a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition if the function
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
g:=\mathbf{1}_{A}
satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition.

The main theorem of Subsection
3.2
is Theorem
3.3
below. We will prove Theorem
3.3
at the end of Subsection
3.2
after the proof of several key lemmas. Each lemma is quite classical and stems from
[
30
,
2
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
M>0
, we have the following properties.

(i) The set
S_{M}
, which is the set of maximizers in
K_{M}
, is nonempty.

(ii) For each
\omega\in S_{M}
, there exist some constants
W>0,\mbox{ }\gamma\geq 0
that are uniquely determined by
\omega
satisfying

(3.1)

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\big\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

(iii) Each
\omega\in S_{M}
has a bounded support and satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
up to translation. More precisely, for each
\omega\in S_{M}
, there exist
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and an open bounded set

satisfying the Steiner symmetry condition such that, for the translation
\omega_{\tau}
given by
\omega_{\tau}(\cdot):=\omega(\cdot+(\tau,0))
, we have

\omega_{\tau}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{A}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

(iv) For each
\omega\in S_{M}
, if we have
\|\omega\|_{1}<1
, then we get
\gamma=0
, where
\gamma
is given in (ii).

Note that we have the following corollary by the scaling in Remark
3.5
.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any
\mu,\mbox{ }\nu,\mbox{ }\lambda>0
, the set
S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
is nonempty. For each maximizer
\omega\in S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
, there exist some constants
W>0
,
\gamma\geq 0
that are uniquely determined by
\omega
satisfying

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\lambda\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\quad\mbox{ where }\Omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Big\}.

Moreover, each maximizer satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
up to translation. In addition, if we have
\|\omega\|_{1}<\nu
, then we get
\gamma=0
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\mu,\nu,\lambda>0
. Recall that the scaling map
\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega}
given by

\hat{\omega}({\bf x})=\lambda^{-1}\cdot\omega(\lambda^{-1/2}\nu^{1/2}{\bf x})

is a bijective map from
S_{\mu,\nu,\lambda}
onto
S_{M}
where
M=\lambda^{1/2}\nu^{-3/2}\mu>0
. Moreover, under the scaling map
\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega},\mbox{ }
for the constants
W,\hat{W}>0
and
\gamma,\hat{\gamma}\geq 0
satisfying

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\lambda\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\hat{\omega}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\mathcal{G}[\hat{\omega}]-\hat{W}x_{2}-\hat{\gamma}>0\big\}}\qquad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

one can easily check the relations

\hat{W}=W\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}\cdot\nu^{-1/2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\hat{\gamma}=\gamma\cdot\nu^{-1}.

3.2.1.
Existence of maximizers in a larger set

For the proof of Theorem
3.3
, for each
M>0
we define a larger admissible set
\widetilde{K}_{M}\supset K_{M}
as

\widetilde{K}_{M}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }0\leq\omega\leq 1\mbox{ }\mbox{ a.e. }\mbox{ in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\quad\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\leq M,\quad\|\omega\|_{1}\leq 1\mbox{ }\Bigl\},

and

\widetilde{I}_{M}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\sup_{\omega\in\widetilde{K}_{M}}E(\omega)\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }I_{M},\quad\quad\widetilde{S}_{M}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }\omega\in\widetilde{K}_{M}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }E(\omega)=\widetilde{I}_{M}\mbox{ }\Big\}.

Note that
K_{M}
is a set of characteristic functions having full impulse
M
, and
\widetilde{K}_{M}
is a set of nonnegative, bounded functions for which the bound of impulse is
M
. We will prove the existence of a maximizer in
\widetilde{K}_{M}
and show that the maximizer lies actually on
K_{M}
. This would imply that
S_{M}=\widetilde{S}_{M}
, and therefore
S_{M}
is nonempty.

Lemma 3.6
.

For each
M>0
, the set
\widetilde{S}_{M}
is nonempty. In other words, there exists
\omega\in\widetilde{K}_{M}
such that

E(\omega)\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\widetilde{I}_{M}.

Proof.

Consider a maximizing sequence

\{\omega_{n}\}\subset\widetilde{K}_{M}
, i.e.

E(\omega_{n})\to\widetilde{I}_{M}\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Since
\|\omega_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})}\leq\|\omega_{n}\|_{1}^{1/2}\leq 1
for all
n\geq 1,
there exists a subsequence
\{\omega_{n_{k}}\}\subset\{\omega_{n}\}
and
\omega\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
such that
\omega_{n_{k}}
converges weakly to
\omega
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
. For convenience, we assume
\omega_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
as
n\to\infty
. It implies that
\omega\in\widetilde{K}_{M}.
In other words, we have

\mbox{ }0\leq\omega\leq 1\quad\mbox{ a.e. }\mbox{ in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\quad\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\leq M,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|\omega\|_{1}\leq 1.

It remains to prove that
|E(\omega_{n})-E(\omega)|\to 0
, which would imply that
E(\omega)=\widetilde{I}_{M}
and
\omega\in\widetilde{S}_{M}
. The convergence
E(\omega_{n})\to E(\omega)
is obtained from the following lemmas, which are borrowed from
[
2
]
. Lemma
3.7
is a standard argument for the Steiner symmetrization which is well-known from previous works; see, e.g.,
[
41
, Appendix I]
,
[
96
, p.1053]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
For
\omega\geq 0
satisfying
\omega\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and
x_{2}\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, there exists
\omega^{*}\geq 0
such that
\omega^{*}(x_{1},x_{2})=\omega^{*}(-x_{1},x_{2})
, and for each
x>0
, the function
\omega^{*}(x_{1},x_{2})
is non-increasing in
x_{1}>0.\mbox{ }
Moreover,

\|\omega^{*}\|_{q}=\|\omega\|_{q}\quad 1\leq q\leq 2,

\|x_{2}\omega^{*}\|_{1}=\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1},

E(\omega^{*})\geq E(\omega).

Remark 3.8
.

\omega^{*}
in Lemma
3.7
is defined to satisfy

|\{\omega>\alpha\}|=|\{\omega^{*}>\alpha\}|
for all
\alpha\geq 0
. It implies that
0\leq\omega^{*}\leq 1
. In particular,
\omega^{*}
can be chosen to satisfy the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
.

By Lemma
3.7
, we may assume that the maximizing sequence
\omega_{n}
and the weak limit
\omega
satisfy the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
. Then the convergence
E(\omega_{n})\to E(\omega)
is obtained by the Lemma
3.9
below.

Lemma 3.9
.

[
2
, Lemma 3.5]
Let
\{\omega_{n}\}
be a sequence such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\Big\{\|\omega_{n}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}+\|x_{2}\omega_{n}\|_{L^{1}}\Big\}<\infty,

\omega_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega\quad\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Assume that each
\omega_{n}
satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition. Then,

E(\omega_{n})\to E(\omega)\mbox{ }\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

As we obtained that
E(\omega)=\widetilde{I}_{M}
, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

3.2.2.
Maximizers in smaller set

Lemma 3.10
.

For each
M>0
, we have

\widetilde{S}_{M}=S_{M}.

Proof.

Let
M>0
. It suffices to show that
\widetilde{S}_{M}\subset K_{M}
. As the set
\widetilde{S}_{M}
is nonempty by Lemma
3.6
, we choose any
\omega\in\widetilde{S}_{M}
.

We will first prove that
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}=M
. Suppose that we have

\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }<\mbox{ }M.
Consider the translation of
\omega
along
x_{2}
-axis of amount
\tau>0
, say
\omega_{\tau}
. Then for small
\tau>0
, we still have

\|x_{2}\omega_{\tau}\|_{1}\mbox{ }<\mbox{ }M
, and by Remark
3.1
-(iii) we have

E(\omega_{\tau})>E(\omega),

which contradicts our assumption that
\omega\in\widetilde{S}_{M}
. Therefore we have
\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}=M
.

It remains to show that
\omega
is a characteristic function. In other words, we will show that

\big|\{0<\omega<1\}\big|\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }0,

which would imply that, for some measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. We suppose that

\big|\{0<\omega<1\}\big|>0.

Then there exists a small constant
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\big|\{\delta_{0}<\omega<1-\delta_{0}\}\big|\mbox{ }>\mbox{ }0.

Observe that

\big|\{\delta_{0}<\omega<1-\delta_{0}\}\big|\mbox{ }<\mbox{ }\infty
due to
\|\omega\|_{1}\leq 1
. We can choose two functions
h_{1},h_{2}\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
such that

{\mathrm{supp}}\,{(h_{i})}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\delta_{0}<\omega<1-\delta_{0}\mbox{ }\}

for each
i\in\{1,2\}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}h_{1}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}h_{1}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}h_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}h_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1.

So far, note that
\delta_{0}>0
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
are fixed. For an arbitrary
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, we consider an arbitrary function
h\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
such that
\|x_{2}h\|_{1}<\infty
and

\displaystyle h\geq 0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }0\leq\omega\leq\delta\mbox{ }\},

\displaystyle h\leq 0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }1-\delta\leq\omega\leq 1\mbox{ }\}.

We denote

\eta\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }h-\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)\mbox{ }h_{1}-\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)\mbox{ }h_{2}.

Then we get
\eta\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\eta\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\eta\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0.

(Claim 1)
There exists a small constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that, for each
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
, we have
\mbox{ }\omega+\epsilon\eta\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }\widetilde{K}_{M}
.

First, we will show that there exists a small constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that, for each
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
, we have
0\leq\mbox{ }\omega+\epsilon\eta\leq 1
.
Observe that, on
\{0\leq\omega\leq\delta_{0}\}
, we have
h_{1}=h_{2}=0
and thus
\eta=h\geq 0
due to our assumption on
h
. Therefore
\omega+\epsilon\eta\geq 0
on
\{0\leq\omega\leq\delta_{0}\}
. And by choosing small
\epsilon_{0}>0
, we have

0\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\omega+\epsilon\eta\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\delta_{0}+\epsilon_{0}\mbox{ }\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }1\quad\mbox{ for each }\mbox{ }\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}),

on the set
\{0\leq\omega\leq\delta_{0}\}
.
Similarly, for each
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
, we have
0\leq\omega+\epsilon\eta\leq 1
on the set
\{1-\delta_{0}\leq\omega\leq 1\}
.
Lastly, by choosing
\epsilon_{0}<\delta_{0}\mbox{ }/\mbox{ }\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we obtain

0\leq\omega+\epsilon\eta\leq 1\quad\mbox{ for each }\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})

on the set
\{\delta_{0}<\omega<1-\delta_{0}\}.
As we have
0\leq\omega+\epsilon\eta\leq 1
for each
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
, we have

\|\omega+\epsilon\eta\|_{1}=\int(\omega+\epsilon\eta)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\leq 1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|x_{2}(\omega+\epsilon\eta)\|_{1}=\int x_{2}(\omega+\epsilon\eta)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\leq M,

and therefore
\omega+\epsilon\eta\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }\widetilde{K}_{M}.\mbox{ }
This completes the proof of
(Claim 1)
.

Using the assumption that
\omega\in\widetilde{S}_{M}
, the following function
F:[0,\epsilon_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
attains its maximum at
\epsilon=0
,

F(\epsilon)\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }E(\omega+\epsilon\eta)\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\Big(\omega({\bf y})+\epsilon\eta({\bf y})\Big)\Big(\omega({\bf x})+\epsilon\eta({\bf x})\Big)\mbox{ }d{\bf y}d{\bf x}.

Observe that
F
is a polynomial of degree 2. Clearly,
F^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
. By defining the constants

W:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }h_{2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\gamma:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }h_{1}\mbox{ }d{\bf x},

we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf x})\eta({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}d{\bf x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\mbox{ }\eta({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\Bigg[h({\bf y})-\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)h_{1}({\bf y})-\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)h_{2}({\bf y})\Bigg]d{\bf y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})\mbox{ }h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}-\underbrace{\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\mbox{ }h_{2}({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Bigg)}_{=W}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}-\underbrace{\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\mbox{ }h_{1}({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\Bigg)}_{=\gamma}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma\Big)h({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

Then we split the integral into three terms:

0\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\Big)\cdot h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int_{0\leq\omega\leq\delta}+\int_{\delta<\omega<1-\delta}+\int_{1-\delta\leq\omega\leq 1}.

Recall that, for each
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, the function
h\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
is arbitrarily chosen just to satisfy

h\geq 0\quad\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }0\leq\omega\leq\delta\mbox{ }\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad h\leq 0\quad\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }1-\delta\leq\omega\leq 1\mbox{ }\}.

Therefore, for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, we obtain that

\begin{cases}\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma\leq 0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }0\leq\omega\leq\delta\mbox{ }\},\\
\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma=0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\delta<\omega<1-\delta\mbox{ }\},\\
\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma\geq 0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }1-\delta\leq\omega\leq 1\mbox{ }\}.\end{cases}

Due to
\{\mbox{ }0<\omega<1\mbox{ }\}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\delta\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }(0,\delta_{0})}\{\mbox{ }\delta<\omega<1-\delta\mbox{ }\}
, we get

\begin{cases}\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma\leq 0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\},\\
\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma=0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }0<\omega<1\mbox{ }\},\\
\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega](x)-Wx_{2}-\gamma\geq 0\quad\mbox{a.e.}&\mbox{ on }\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=1\mbox{ }\}.\end{cases}

So we obtain
\{\mbox{ }0<\omega<1\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma=0\mbox{ }\}
. Define
\Psi({\bf x}):=\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma
. It remains to prove that

\{\mbox{ }\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in }\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

which would imply that

\{\mbox{ }0<\omega<1\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in }\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\mbox{ }

which contradicts to our assumption that
\big|\{0<\omega<1\}\big|>0
.

(Claim 2)
For each
\omega\in\widetilde{S}_{M}
, we have

\{\mbox{ }\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\Psi\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and
-\Delta\Psi=\omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Therefore we have

\{\mbox{ }\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}\subset\{\mbox{ }\nabla\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}\subset\{\mbox{ }\Delta\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}=\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\}.

This completes the proof of
(Claim 2)
and we finish the proof of Lemma
3.10
. ∎

3.2.3.
Every maximizer generates a traveling wave solution

Note that Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.10
imply that the set
S_{M}
is nonempty. Proposition
3.11
says that each maximizer in
S_{M}
generates a traveling wave solution to (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.11
.

For any
M>0
and for each
\omega\in S_{M},
there exist some constants
W,\gamma\geq 0
that are uniquely determined by
\omega
satisfying that

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\big\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}\quad\mbox{ }\mbox{a.e. }\quad{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

Proof.

Let
M>0
. We choose any
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\in S_{M}
for some measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. For the proof, we first assume the existence of the constants
W,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying that

\Omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\}.

In
(Claim 1)
, we will prove that
W,\gamma
are unique. The existence and non-negativity of such constants will be proved later in
(Claim 2)
and
(Claim 3)
each.

(Claim 1)
If there exist some constants
W,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying that
\Omega=\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\},
then they are uniquely determined by
\omega
.

Assume that we have
\Omega=\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\}
for some constants
W,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We choose any two points
{\bf y},{\bf z}\in\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
such that
y_{2}\neq z_{2}
.
Observe that

\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma=0\mbox{ }\Big\}

by the continuity of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
. Therefore

\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})-Wy_{2}-\gamma\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z})-Wz_{2}-\gamma\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }0

and thus

(3.2)

W\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})-\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z})}{y_{2}-z_{2}},\quad\gamma\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }-\Big(\frac{z_{2}}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Big)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})+\Big(\frac{y_{2}}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Big)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z}).

It implies that
W,\gamma
are explicitly expressed by
\omega
, so they are unique. This completes the proof of
(Claim 1)
.

(Claim 2)
There exist some constants
W,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that we have
\Omega=\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\}
.

The main part of the proof is to generate perturbation of
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
in the set
\widetilde{K}_{M}
to discover the specific form of
\Omega
. As we can see in the proof of the uniqueness of
W,\gamma\geq 0
, these constants depend on the boundary points of
\Omega
. However, we yet only know that
\Omega
is a measurable set, which means that we cannot use the definition of boundary points as usual. We naturally want to define boundary-like points for each measurable set which are called
exceptional points
. This notion appeared in several works including
[
36
,
93
,
65
,
30
]
.

Definition 3.12
.

[
30
, Definition 5.4]

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open. For any (Lebesgue) measurable set
E\subset U
, the density
D_{e}(E)
of
E
is the collection of
{\bf x}\in U
such that

\liminf_{r\searrow 0}\frac{|B_{r}({\bf x})\cap E|}{|B_{r}({\bf x})|}=1.

Similarly, define the dispersion
D_{i}(E)
of
E
by the collection of
{\bf x}\in U
such that

\limsup_{r\searrow 0}\frac{|B_{r}({\bf x})\cap E|}{|B_{r}({\bf x})|}=0.

Set
\mathcal{E}(E):=U\backslash(D_{e}(E)\cup D_{i}(E))
, the set of exceptional points.

The following two propositions say that the set of exceptional points has zero measure, but in most cases, it is a nonempty set. The proof of Proposition
3.13
can be found in
[
101
, Theorem 7.13]
, and the proof of Proposition
3.14
is given in
[
30
, Appendix B]
.

Proposition 3.13
.

[
30
, Lemma 5.5]

E=D_{e}(E)\quad\mbox{and}\quad U\backslash E=D_{i}(E)\quad a.e.

Proposition 3.14
.

[
30
, Lemma 5.6]
Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a non-empty connected open set. Then for any measurable set
E\subset U
with
|E|,|U\backslash E|\in(0,\infty]
, we have
\mathcal{E}(E)\neq\emptyset
.

By Proposition
3.14
, we can obtain exceptional points
{\bf y},{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
s.t.
y_{2}>z_{2}
, and
\{r_{n}\}\searrow 0
such that

\displaystyle\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega\big|,\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega^{c}\big|

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf z})\cap\Omega\big|,\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf z})\cap\Omega^{c}\big|

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle(0,\infty),\quad\mbox{ for each }n\geq 1.

To show this, we apply Proposition
3.14
. We first choose
l>0
such that both
\Omega\cap\{x_{2}>l\}
and
\Omega\cap\{x_{2}<l\}
have finite and positive measures. In order to obtain such
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, put

U:=\{x_{2}>l\},\quad E:=\Omega\cap\{x_{2}>l\}

and choose
{\bf y}\in\mathcal{E}(E)
so that
y_{2}>l
.
Then for any decreasing sequence of positive numbers
\{r_{n}\}\searrow 0
, we can simply check that

\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega\big|,\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\big|B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega^{c}\big|\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }(0,\infty),\quad\mbox{ for each }n\geq 1.

Similarly, we can choose
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
such that
0<z_{2}<l
and do the same process.

Define sequences of sets of positive measures near
{\bf y},{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
as

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{+}:=B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega,

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{-}:=B_{r_{n}}({\bf y})\cap\Omega^{c},

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{+}:=B_{r_{n}}({\bf z})\cap\Omega,

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{-}:=B_{r_{n}}({\bf z})\cap\Omega^{c}.

and sequences of compactly supported and bounded functions as

f_{n}^{\pm}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{|Y_{n}^{\pm}|}\mathbf{1}_{Y_{n}^{\pm}},\quad g_{n}^{\pm}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{|Z_{n}^{\pm}|}\mathbf{1}_{Z_{n}^{\pm}}.

These functions will play as approximations of Dirac delta at
{\bf y}
and
{\bf z}
each, as
n\to\infty
. Recalling the proof of
(Claim 1)
(see the identity (
3.2
)), define the constants

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Bigg)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\mbox{ }-\mbox{ }\Bigg(\frac{1}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Bigg)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z})\mbox{ }\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\gamma:=-\mbox{ }\Bigg(\frac{z_{2}}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Bigg)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\mbox{ }+\mbox{ }\Bigg(\frac{y_{2}}{y_{2}-z_{2}}\Bigg)\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z})\mbox{ }\in\mathbb{R}.

As
n\to\infty
, we have

\int x_{2}f_{n}^{\pm}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\to y_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int x_{2}g_{n}^{\pm}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\to z_{2}.

We denote
\mbox{ }\displaystyle y_{n}^{\pm}:=\int x_{2}f_{n}^{\pm}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mbox{ }\displaystyle z_{n}^{\pm}:=\int x_{2}g_{n}^{\pm}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, by the continuity of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
,

\int\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }f_{n}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\to\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf y})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }g_{n}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\to\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf z})

as
n\to\infty
. In order to construct sequences converging to
W
and
\gamma
, define the sequences of coefficients as

a_{n}^{\pm}:=\frac{y_{n}^{\mp}}{y_{n}^{\mp}-z_{n}^{\pm}},\quad b_{n}^{\pm}:=\frac{z_{n}^{\pm}}{y_{n}^{\mp}-z_{n}^{\pm}},\quad c_{n}^{\pm}:=\frac{1}{y_{n}^{\pm}-z_{n}^{\mp}}.

Note that they are all positive for large
n\geq 1
. Assume that
a_{n}^{\pm},b_{n}^{\pm},c_{n}^{\pm}>0
, for all
n\geq 1
. Using these sequences, we construct functions as

h_{n,1}^{\pm}:=a_{n}^{\pm}g_{n}^{\pm}-b_{n}^{\pm}f_{n}^{\mp},\quad h_{n,2}^{\pm}:=c_{n}^{\pm}(f_{n}^{\pm}-g_{n}^{\mp}).

These functions will be used to construct the perturbation for
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
in the set
\widetilde{K}_{M}
. Observe that

\int h_{n,1}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1,\quad\int x_{2}h_{n,1}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0,

\int h_{n,2}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0,\quad\int x_{2}h_{n,2}^{\pm}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=1.

Observe that the signs
+
and
-
in each coefficient and function are chosen to satisfy

\displaystyle h_{n,1}^{+},\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{+}\mbox{ }\geq 0

on

\displaystyle\Omega,

\displaystyle h_{n,1}^{+},\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{+}\mbox{ }\leq 0

on

\displaystyle\Omega^{c},

and

\displaystyle h_{n,1}^{-},\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{-}\mbox{ }\leq 0

on

\displaystyle\Omega,

\displaystyle h_{n,1}^{-},\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{-}\mbox{ }\geq 0

on

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}.

Now we are ready to prove that
\mbox{ }\Omega=\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\}\mbox{ }
by using perturbation of the function
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
in the set
\widetilde{K}_{M}
. Consider any function
h\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
such that
\|x_{2}h\|_{1}<\infty
and

h\geq 0\quad\mbox{on}\mbox{ }\Omega^{c},\quad h\leq 0\quad\mbox{on}\mbox{ }\Omega.

For any fixed function
h
, we choose the sign superscripts for
h_{n,1}^{\pm}
and
h_{n,2}^{\pm}
as follows. We define
h_{n,1}
and
h_{n,2}
to satisfy

h_{n,1}:=\left\{\begin{matrix}\mbox{ }h_{n,1}^{+}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\int h\geq 0,\\
\mbox{ }h_{n,1}^{-}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\int h<0,\end{matrix}\right.

and

h_{n,2}:=\left\{\begin{matrix}\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{+}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\int x_{2}h\geq 0,\\
\mbox{ }h_{n,2}^{-}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\int x_{2}h<0.\end{matrix}\right.

Moreover we define

W_{n}:=\int\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }h_{n,2}\mbox{ }d{\bf x},\quad\gamma_{n}:=\int\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }h_{n,1}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}.

Observe that
W_{n}\to W
and
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
as
n\to\infty
.
We set

\eta_{n}:=h-\Bigg(\int h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)\mbox{ }h_{n,1}-\Bigg(\int x_{2}h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg)\mbox{ }h_{n,2}.

Naturally
\eta_{n}\leq 0
on
\Omega
and
\eta_{n}\geq 0
on
\Omega^{c}
. Moreover, observe that
\int\eta_{n}=\int x_{2}\eta_{n}=0
. As
\eta_{n}\in L^{\infty}
, by the similar arguments in the proof of
(Claim 1)
of
3.10
, we may show that there exists a small
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\omega+\epsilon\eta_{n}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\widetilde{K}_{M}\quad\mbox{ for each }\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}),

and using the fact that
E(\cdot)
is maximized by
\omega
in the admissible set
\widetilde{K}_{M}
, we obtain

0\geq\frac{d}{d\epsilon}E(\omega+\epsilon\eta_{n})\Big|_{\epsilon=0}=\int\mathcal{G}[\omega]\mbox{ }\eta_{n}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-W_{n}x_{2}-\gamma_{n}\Big)\mbox{ }h\mbox{ }d{\bf x},

for each
n\geq 1
. We can take the limit
n\to\infty
to obtain

0\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }\int\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\Big)\mbox{ }h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}.

Since
h
is arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\geq 0\quad\mbox{a.e. on}\quad\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\leq 0\quad\mbox{a.e. on}\quad\Omega^{c}.

Put
\Psi:=\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\mbox{ }
and observe

\{\mbox{ }\Psi<0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Omega^{c}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\Psi\leq 0\mbox{ }\}.

Since
-\Delta\Psi=\omega
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have

\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\Psi=0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\{\mbox{ }\omega=0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Omega^{c}\mbox{ }\mbox{ a.e. }

and thus
\{\mbox{ }\Psi\leq 0\mbox{ }\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Omega^{c}.
In conclusion, we have

\Omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\big\{\mbox{ }\Psi>0\mbox{ }\big\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\big\}\quad\mbox{ a.e. }

This completes the proof of
(Claim 2)
.

(Claim 3)
The constants
W,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
are non-negative.

If
\gamma<0
, observe that

\Bigl\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }|W|x_{2}<-\gamma\mbox{ }\Bigl\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }Wx_{2}<-\gamma\mbox{ }\Bigl\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Bigl\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Omega.

It implies that
|\Omega|=\infty
, which contradicts to
|\Omega|\leq 1
. Therefore we have
\gamma\geq 0
. If
W<0
, we have

\Bigl\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }-Wx_{2}>\gamma\mbox{ }\Bigl\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Bigl\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Omega,

which also contradicts to
|\Omega|\leq 1
. It follows that
W\geq 0
. This completes the proof of
(Claim 3)
and we finish the proof of Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Lemma
3.15
below says that, if any maximizer in
K_{M}
does not attain the full mass, then the corresponding flux constant
\gamma
vanishes.

Lemma 3.15
.

For any
M>0
and for each
\omega\in S_{M},
if we have
\|\omega\|_{1}<1
, then we get

\gamma=0

where
\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
is given in Proposition
3.11
.

Proof.

Let
M>0
. We choose any
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\in S_{M}
where we have

\Omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\Big\}

where the constants
W,\gamma\geq 0
are given in Proposition
3.11
. We assume that
\|\omega\|_{1}<1
. We now follow the same process in the proof of
(Claim 2)
in Proposition
3.11
in a way that, instead of using
\eta_{n}
, we use the following function

\hat{\eta}_{n}:=h-\Big(\int x_{2}h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Big)h_{n,2}

with the same definitions for
h,h_{n,2}
in the proof of
(Claim 2)
in Proposition
3.11
. As
\|\omega\|_{1}<1
, we have

\|\omega+\epsilon\hat{\eta}_{n}\|_{1}\leq\|\omega\|_{1}+\epsilon\|\hat{\eta}_{n}\|_{1}\leq 1\quad

for small
\epsilon>0
. Moreover, as
\hat{\eta}_{n}\in L^{\infty}
, for small
\epsilon>0
we have
0\leq\omega+\epsilon\hat{\eta}_{n}\leq 1
, and due to
\int x_{2}\hat{\eta}_{n}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0,
we get

\|x_{2}(\omega+\epsilon\hat{\eta}_{n})\|_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{2}\hat{\eta}_{n}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=M.

This implies that there exists a small
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\omega+\epsilon\hat{\eta}_{n}\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }\widetilde{K}_{M}

for each
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
. Then we have

0\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }\frac{d}{d\epsilon}E(\omega+\epsilon\hat{\eta}_{n})\mbox{ }=\int\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-W_{n}x_{2}\Big)\mbox{ }h\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

with the same definition for
W_{n}
with the proof of
(Claim 2)
in Proposition
3.11
. It follows that

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}}\quad\mbox{ a.e. \mbox{ }in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

By the uniqueness of the constant
\gamma\geq 0
given in Proposition
3.11
, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.15
.
∎

3.2.4.
Positivity of traveling speed

Remark 3.16
.

For any
\omega\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, whose odd-extension
\widetilde{\omega}
is a traveling wave solution of the equation (
1.1
), in the form

\omega({\bf x})=\omega_{0}({\bf x}-(W,0)t)

for some
\omega_{0}\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
having bounded support with some constant
W\in\mathbb{R}
, its traveling speed
W
is determined by
\omega_{0}
by the identity in
[
96
, p.1062]
:

(3.3)

W\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\|\omega_{0}\|_{1}^{-1}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathcal{G}[\omega_{0}]_{x_{2}}\cdot\omega_{0}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\|\omega_{0}\|_{1}^{-1}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}^{*}|^{2}}\mbox{ }\omega_{0}({\bf y})\mbox{ }\omega_{0}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}d{\bf x}

where
{\bf y}^{*}=(y_{1},-y_{2})
. Indeed, for the first equality, observe that

W\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega_{0}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{1}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{1}\omega_{t}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}x_{1}({\bf u}\cdot\nabla\omega)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}u_{1}\cdot\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

where we used integration by parts. The second equality is obtained by considering obvious cancellation.

Lemma 3.17
.

For any
M>0
and for each
\omega\in S_{M}
, we have

W\mbox{ }>\mbox{ }0

where
\mbox{ }W=W(\omega)
is given in Proposition
3.11
.

Proof.

Let
M>0
and choose any
\omega\in S_{M}
. We suppose
W=0
. Then
\gamma>0
due to the assumption
\|\omega\|_{1}\leq 1
. By Lemma
2.5
, it is clear that the set
\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\}
is bounded. Then we can derive
W>0
by the identity (
3.3
) in Remark
3.16
, which contradicts our assumption that
W=0
.
∎

3.2.5.
Bounded support of maximizers

In the following lemma, we show that each maximizer
\omega
has a bounded support.

Lemma 3.18
.

For any
M>0
and for each
\omega
in
S_{M}
, the set
\big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}
is bounded, for the corresponding constant
W,\gamma
given in Proposition
3.11
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
M>0
and for each
\omega\in S_{M}
, we have

(3.4)

\Bigg|\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})}{x_{2}}\Bigg|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C\mbox{ }\Big(\mbox{ }\epsilon^{-1}\|\omega\|_{1}+\epsilon^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}\big([x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{1}+\epsilon]\times(0,\infty)\big)}^{1/3}\mbox{ }\Big),

for any
\epsilon>0
and
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.

Remark 3.19
.

We can deduce from the estimate (
3.4
) in Lemma
3.18
below that
\big|\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})/x_{2}\big|\to 0
as
|x_{1}|\to\infty
where the convergence is uniform in
x_{2}
. This estimate will be used later in the proofs of Theorem
4.2
and Lemma
4.7
.

Proof of Lemma
3.18
.

Let
M>0
. As
S_{M}
is nonempty, we choose any
\omega\in S_{M}
. By Proposition
3.11
, we have

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

for some unique constants
W=W(\omega)>0
and
\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
. By (
2.4
) in Lemma
2.3
, we have
\mathcal{G}[\omega]\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. Therefore we obtain

\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }x_{2}<\frac{\|\mathcal{G}[\omega]\|_{L^{\infty}}}{W}\mbox{ }\Big\}.

It remains to prove that there exists a constant
L>0
such that

\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }|x_{1}|\leq L\mbox{ }\Big\}.

Observe that

\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]/x_{2}>W\mbox{ }\Big\}.

By the mean value theorem, for each
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, there exists a constant
c=c({\bf x})\in(0,x_{2})
such that

\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})}{x_{2}}=\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-\mathcal{G}[\omega](x_{1},0)}{x_{2}}=\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{2}}(x_{1},c).

Here we have

\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{2}}(x_{1},c)\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(y_{2}-c)}{(y_{1}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-c)^{2}}\mbox{ }\widetilde{\omega}({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}

where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the odd-extension of
\omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that, for each
\epsilon>0
, we have

\Big|\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{2}}(x_{1},c)\Big|\mbox{ }\lesssim\left[\int_{B^{c}}+\int_{B}\right]\mbox{ }\frac{1}{|(y_{1},y_{2})-(x_{1},c)|}\mbox{ }|\widetilde{\omega}({\bf y})|\mbox{ }d{\bf y}

where
B:=B_{\epsilon}(x_{1},c)
. The first integral satisfies

\int_{B^{c}}\frac{|\widetilde{\omega}({\bf y})|}{|(y_{1},y_{2})-(x_{1},c)|}\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{2}{\epsilon}\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}.

For the second integral, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\int_{B}\frac{|\widetilde{\omega}({\bf y})|}{|(y_{1},y_{2})-(x_{1},c)|}\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\epsilon}r^{-1/2}dr\Bigg)^{2/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{3}(B)}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\epsilon}r^{-1/2}dr\Bigg)^{2/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}(B)}^{1/3}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\epsilon^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}\big([x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{1}+\epsilon]\mbox{ }\times\mbox{ }(0,\infty)\big)}^{1/3}.

In sum, we obtain (
3.4
) as we have

\Bigg|\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})}{x_{2}}\Bigg|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{0}\mbox{ }\Big(\mbox{ }\epsilon^{-1}\|\omega\|_{1}+\epsilon^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}\big([x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{1}+\epsilon]\times(0,\infty)\big)}^{1/3}\mbox{ }\Big)

for some absolute constant
C_{0}>0
. Moreover, note that the right-hand side does not depend on
x_{2}
. Here we put

\epsilon=\frac{3C_{0}\|\omega\|_{1}}{W}

to have

\Big|\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})}{x_{2}}\Big|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{W}{3}+C_{0}\epsilon^{1/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}([x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{1}+\epsilon]\times(0,\infty))}^{1/3}.

Due to
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, the last term tends to 0 as
|x_{1}|\to\infty
, uniformly in
x_{2}
. So there exists a constant
L=L(\omega)>0
such that, for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

|x_{1}|>L\mbox{ }\Rightarrow\mbox{ }\Bigg|\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})}{x_{2}}\Bigg|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{W}{3}+\frac{W}{3}<W,

and thus

\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]/x_{2}>W\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }\subset\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }|x_{1}|\leq L\mbox{ }\Big\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

In the proof above, we essentially showed that, for any
0\leq f\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with
\|x_{2}f\|_{1}<\infty
and for each
W>0
, the set
\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[f]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
M>0
. By Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.10
, the set
S_{M}
is nonempty. Then for any
\omega\in S_{M}
, by Proposition
3.11
, we have

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }1_{\big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}\quad\mbox{ }\mbox{a.e. }\quad{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

for some constants
W,\mbox{ }\gamma\geq 0
which are uniquely determined by
\omega
, and by Lemma
3.17
, we have
W>0
. Since

is the absolute maximum of

, we may assume that

satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition

.
∎

3.3.
Estimate on maximal energy

Using the specific form of the maximizers given in Theorem
3.3
, we obtain the following crucial identity concerning the maximal energy, traveling speed, and impulse.

Lemma 3.21
.

For any
M>0
, we have

(3.5)

I_{M}=\frac{3}{4}WM+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\|\omega\|_{1},

for each
\omega\in S_{M}
with the constants
W=W(\omega)>0,\mbox{ }\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
in Theorem
3.3
.

Remark 3.22
.

The identity in the above lemma can be essentially found in Lemma 9 of
[
7
]
. It is a so-called Pohožaev-type identity (see
[
79
]
). We present the proof below for completeness. For vortex rings, we refer to Lemma 3.2 of
[
43
]
(also see Proposition 5.13 of
[
30
]
).

Proof of Lemma
3.21
.

Let
M>0
. As
S_{M}
is nonempty, we take any
\omega\in S_{M}
. Put
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\big\}}
for the constants
W=W(\omega)>0
and
\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
in Theorem
3.3
.
Let
\widetilde{\omega}
be the odd extension of
\omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and denote
\psi:=\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}
, the odd extension of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
. We first make the following claim.

(Claim)
We have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{\omega}\mbox{ }({\bf x}\cdot\nabla\psi)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }0.

We first observe that, for any scalar function
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\Delta f\cdot({\bf x}\cdot\nabla f)\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\nabla\cdot\Big(\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2}x_{1}\big(f_{x_{1}}^{2}-f_{x_{2}}^{2}\big)+x_{2}f_{x_{1}}f_{x_{2}},\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2}x_{2}\big(-f_{x_{1}}^{2}+f_{x_{2}}^{2}\big)+x_{1}f_{x_{1}}f_{x_{2}}\mbox{ }\Big).

Knowing that
\widetilde{\omega}
is compactly supported by Lemma
3.18
and
\psi\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the relation
-\Delta\psi=\widetilde{\omega}
by Lemma
2.4
, the approximation to smooth function in the bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{\omega}\mbox{ }({\bf x}\cdot\nabla\psi)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }0.

This completes the proof of
(Claim)
.

Since the function
\widetilde{\omega}\mbox{ }({\bf x}\cdot\nabla\psi)
is an even function, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega\mbox{ }({\bf x}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}[\omega])\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=0.

Here, by the relation
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}},
observe that

\omega\cdot(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma)\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma)_{+}

where
s_{+}:=\max\{s,0\},\mbox{ }s\in\mathbb{R}.\mbox{ }
We define
K:=(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma)_{+}
, and using the chain rule (see
[
47
, Theorem 7.8]
), we observe that
K\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
with the relation

\nabla K=\omega\cdot(\nabla\mathcal{G}[\omega]-(0,W)).

Note that we have

0\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega\mbox{ }({\bf x}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}[\omega])\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}{\bf x}\cdot\nabla K\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}Wx_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

where

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}{\bf x}\cdot\nabla K\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

using approximation to smooth function and integration by parts. As we know that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}K\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega\cdot\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma\Big)\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=2I_{M}-WM-\gamma\|\omega\|_{1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}Wx_{2}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=WM,

we get (
3.5
).
∎

By the scaling given in Remark
3.1
-(v) and Remark
3.5
, we have the following corollary.

For any
\mu,\nu,\lambda>0

(3.6)

for each

with the constants

in Corollary

4.

\gamma
of each maximizer vanishes. Accordingly, each traveling vortex patch, generated from any maximizer, touches the horizontal line from which a continuous boundary streamline emerges. The main results of this section are given in Theorem

due to a small impulse

) in Lemma

5
.

-axis.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

4.1.
Vanishing of flux constant

In Lemma
4.4
we will prove
\gamma=0
given that the impulse of the maximizer is small enough. Lemma
4.3
below will be used in the proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
M\in(0,1)
, we have

I_{M}\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }M^{4/3}\cdot I_{1}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
, the set
S_{1}
of maximizers is nonempty. Choose any
f\in S_{1}
so that

\|f\|_{1}\leq 1,\quad\|x_{2}f\|_{1}=1.

After the scaling
g({\bf x})=f(M^{-1/3}{\bf x})
, we have

\|g\|_{1}=M^{2/3}\|f\|_{1}\leq M^{2/3}<1,\quad\|x_{2}g\|_{1}=M\|x_{2}f\|_{1}=M,\quad\mbox{and }\mbox{ }E(g)=M^{4/3}E(f)=M^{4/3}I_{1},

so we have
g\in K_{M}
and

I_{M}\geq E(g)=M^{4/3}I_{1}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a constant
M_{1}>0
such that, for any
M\in(0,M_{1})
and for each
\omega\in S_{M}
, we have

\gamma=0,

where
\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
is given in Theorem
3.3
.

Proof.

Let
M>0
. As
S_{M}
is a nonempty set, we take any
\omega\in S_{M}
with the constants
W=W(\omega)>0,\mbox{ }\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
given in Theorem
3.3
. Then for any
L>0
, we split the integral as

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\int_{0<x_{2}\leq L}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+\int_{x_{2}>L}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

and observe that

M\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }\int_{x_{2}>L}x_{2}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\geq\mbox{ }L\int_{x_{2}>L}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}

and so

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\int_{0<x_{2}\leq L}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+ML^{-1}.

On the other hand, for any
\alpha>0
, we have

(4.1)

\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\omega\cdot\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}{Wx_{2}}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\frac{1}{W\alpha}\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\mathcal{G}[\omega]\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}.

Using the symmetry of
G
and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\mathcal{G}[\omega]\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Bigg[\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}G({\bf y},{\bf x})\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\Bigg]\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\alpha^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}y_{2}^{1/2}\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\alpha^{3/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|^{1/2}_{1}\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}\leq\alpha^{3/2}M^{1/2},

and so we get

\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\frac{M^{1/2}}{W}\alpha^{1/2}.

Therefore we have

(4.2)

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\int_{0<x_{2}\leq L}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+ML^{-1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{L/2^{n+1}<\mbox{ }x_{2}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }L/2^{n}}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+ML^{-1}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\frac{M^{1/2}}{W}L^{1/2}+ML^{-1}.

As the choice of
L>0
is arbitrary, we put
L=W^{2/3}M^{1/3}
to obtain

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\Bigg(\frac{M}{W}\Bigg)^{2/3}.

Knowing that
\|\omega\|_{1}\lesssim(M/W)^{2/3}
, we will show that the following never holds:
there exists a sequence of impulse
\{M_{n}\}\searrow 0
, and a sequence
{\omega_{n}}\subset S_{M_{n}}
, together with a sequence of positive flux constant
\gamma_{n}>0
.

To prove it, we assume that such sequences
\{M_{n}\},\mbox{ }\{\omega_{n}\}
and
\{\gamma_{n}\}
exist. We may assume that
M_{n}<1
for each
n\geq 1
, and we fix any
n\geq 1
. By the assumption
\gamma_{n}>0
, we have
\|\omega_{n}\|_{1}=1
by Lemma
3.15
. Therefore, by the relation
\|\omega\|_{1}\lesssim(M/W)^{2/3}
and Lemma
3.21
, we get

W_{n}\lesssim M_{n},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad I_{M_{n}}\lesssim{M_{n}}^{2}+\gamma_{n}.

Moreover, as
M_{n}\in(0,1)
, by Lemma
4.3
we have the relation

M_{n}^{4/3}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }M_{n}^{2}+\gamma_{n}.

Take
N>1
large enough so that we have
M_{n}^{4/3}\lesssim\gamma_{n}
for each
n\geq N
. Recall that we used the relation
\mbox{ }\omega\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\omega\cdot\mathcal{G}[\omega]/Wx_{2}
in the inequality (
4.1
). As
M_{n}^{4/3}\lesssim\gamma_{n}
, we can instead use the relation
\mbox{ }\omega_{n}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\omega_{n}\cdot\mathcal{G}[\omega_{n}]/\gamma_{n}
to obtain that

\int_{\alpha<x_{2}\leq 2\alpha}\omega_{n}\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }M_{n}^{-5/6}\alpha^{3/2},\quad\mbox{ for any }\mbox{ }\alpha>0.

Similarly with (
4.2
), we then obtain the following relation:

1\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\|\omega_{n}\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{L/2^{k+1}<\mbox{ }x_{2}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }L/2^{k}}\omega_{n}({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}+M_{n}L^{-1}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }M_{n}^{-5/6}L^{3/2}+M_{n}L^{-1},\quad\mbox{ for any }\mbox{ }L>0.

Then by choosing
L=M_{n}^{11/15}
, we have

1\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }M_{n}^{4/15},

which contradicts to our assumption that
\{M_{n}\}\searrow 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Touching of pair of uniform vortices

By Lemma
4.4
, for small
M>0
and for each maximizer
\omega\in S_{M}
, the corresponding flux constant
\gamma
vanishes such that we have

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}}

for the traveling speed
W>0
.
We now assume that the set
\Omega:=\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition, i.e.
\Omega
is concentrated along
x_{2}
-axis. In order to prove that the set
\Omega
touches
x_{1}
-axis at the origin, we have to prove

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(\mathcal{G}[\omega](x_{1},x_{2})-Wx_{2}\Big)\mbox{ }>\mbox{ }0\quad\mbox{ for any small }\mbox{ }|{\bf x}|>0.

In other words, we have to estimate the horizontal velocity
u^{1}
of the fluid near the center of the dipolar vortex. Lemma
4.5
below gives us a strong estimate, which would guarantee that vanishing of
\gamma
is an equivalent condition to touching of a vortex dipole patch.

4.2.1.
Central speed estimation

Lemma 4.5
.

For some constants
W>0
and
\gamma\geq 0
, if a function
\omega
is given by the relation
\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\mbox{ }
for some open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
and the identity

\Omega=\{\mbox{ }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\},

we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(0,0)>2W

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}
is the odd extention of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\}}
with some constants
W>0
and
\gamma\geq 0
. For each
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we put

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}({\bf x})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{-x_{2}+y_{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}}\mbox{ }\widetilde{\omega}({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{-x_{2}+y_{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}}\mbox{ }\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }+\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}^{*}|^{2}}\mbox{ }\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }u^{1}_{l}({\bf x})+u^{1}_{nl}({\bf x})

where
{\bf y}^{*}=(y_{1},-y_{2})
. By the relation
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(0,0)=2u^{1}_{nl}(0,0)
, we need to show that

u^{1}_{nl}(0,0)>W.

Note that the identity (
3.3
) in Remark
3.16
implies that

W=\|\omega\|_{1}^{-1}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}u^{1}_{nl}\cdot\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x},

which means that
W
is the average of
u^{1}_{nl}
in
the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,{(\omega)}
. We will claim that, assuming the Steiner symmetry condition of
\omega
, the function
u^{1}_{nl}
attains its unique maximum at
{\bf x}=(0,0)
among all points in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, which would imply that

u^{1}_{nl}(0,0)>u^{1}_{nl}({\bf x})\quad\mbox{ for all }{\bf x}\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,{(\omega)}\backslash\{(0,0)\}

and therefore
u^{1}_{nl}(0,0)>W.

(Claim 1)

For any
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, we have
u^{1}_{nl}(x_{1},x_{2})=u^{1}_{nl}(-x_{1},x_{2})
. Moreover, for fixed
x_{2}\geq 0
, the function
u^{1}_{nl}(x_{1},x_{2})
decreases in
x_{1}\geq 0
.

Let
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
and assume that
x_{1}\neq 0
. We have

u^{1}_{nl}(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{y_{2}+x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2}}\omega(y_{1}+x_{1},y_{2})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}.

For any fixed
y_{2}>0
satisfying that
\omega(0,y_{2})=1
, observe that there exists a constant
l=l(y_{2})>0
such that

\Big\{\mbox{ }y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega(y_{1},y_{2})=1\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\Big(-l(y_{2}),\mbox{ }l(y_{2})\Big).

Put
A:=\big\{\mbox{ }y_{2}>0\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega(0,y_{2})=1\mbox{ }\big\}
, which is a nonempty open set, and we get

u^{1}_{nl}(x_{1},x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A}\int_{-l(y_{2})-x_{1}}^{l(y_{2})-x_{1}}\frac{y_{2}+x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2}}dy_{1}\mbox{ }dy_{2}.

Observe that, for fixed
y_{2}\in A
, the following function

f(x_{1})\mbox{ }:=\int_{-l(y_{2})-x_{1}}^{l(y_{2})-x_{1}}\frac{y_{2}+x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2}}dy_{1},\quad x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}

satisfies
f(x_{1})=f(-x_{1})
and decreases in
x_{1}\geq 0
. This completes the proof of
(Claim 1)
.

(Claim 2)

The function
g(x_{2}):=u^{1}_{nl}(0,x_{2})
decreases in
x_{2}\geq 0
.

For each
x_{2}\geq 0
,

g(x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{y_{2}+x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2}}\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}.

As in the proof of
(Claim 1)
, we have

g(x_{2})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A}\int_{-l(y_{2})}^{l(y_{2})}\frac{y_{2}+x_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2}}\mbox{ }dy_{1}\mbox{ }dy_{2}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{A}\arctan{\Bigg(\frac{l(y_{2})}{y_{2}+x_{2}}\Bigg)}\mbox{ }dy_{2}.

It implies that
g(x_{2})
decreases strictly in
x_{2}\geq 0
. This completes the proof of
(Claim 2)
.

By
(Claim 1)
and
(Claim 2)
, we have

u^{1}_{nl}(0,0)\mbox{ }>\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}^{-1}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}u^{1}_{nl}\cdot\omega\mbox{ }d{\bf x}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }W,

and so we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(0,0)>2W.\mbox{ }
This completes the proof.
∎

-axis if and only if we get

x_{1}>0
, we have
\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{1}}({\bf x})<0
.

For each
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying
x_{1}>0
, observe that

\displaystyle 2\pi\cdot\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{1}}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\omega({\bf y})(y_{1}-x_{1})\Bigg[\mbox{ }\frac{1}{|{\bf y}-{\bf x}|^{2}}-\frac{1}{|{\bf y}^{*}-{\bf x}|^{2}}\mbox{ }\Bigg]d{\bf y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{-l(y_{2})}^{l(y_{2})}\mbox{ }\frac{4x_{2}y_{2}\mbox{ }(y_{1}-x_{1})}{|{\bf y}-{\bf x}|^{2}|{\bf y}^{*}-{\bf x}|^{2}}\mbox{ }dy_{1}\mbox{ }dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{-l(y_{2})-x_{1}}^{l(y_{2})-x_{1}}\frac{y_{1}\cdot(4x_{2}y_{2})}{(y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}-x_{2})^{2})\cdot(y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2})}\mbox{ }dy_{1}\mbox{ }dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\Big[F\big(l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big)-F\big(-l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big)\Big]\mbox{ }dy_{2}

where, for each fixed
y_{2}\in A\backslash\{x_{2}\}
, the function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{y_{1}\cdot(4x_{2}y_{2})}{(y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}-x_{2})^{2})\cdot(y_{1}^{2}+(y_{2}+x_{2})^{2})}\mbox{ }dy_{1}.

Note that
F
is an even function in
s\in\mathbb{R}
by the odd symmetry of the integrand in
y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we observe that
F(s)
increases strictly in
|s|\geq 0
, since the integrand is positive in
y_{1}>0
. For each
y_{2}\in A\backslash\{x_{2}\}
, we have
l(y_{2})>0
and so we get

\big|l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big|<\big|-l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big|,\quad\mbox{ implying that }\quad F\big(l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big)<F\big(-l(y_{2})-x_{1}\big).

This gives
\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{1}}({\bf x})<0
and completes the proof of
(Claim 2)
.

We will now prove (ii). First we want to prove that
l\in C^{1,r}_{loc}(A)
for each
r\in(0,1).
We define a
C^{1}
function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\Psi({\bf x}):=\mathcal{G}[\omega]({\bf x})-Wx_{2}.

For each
y_{2}\in A
, by
(Claim 1)
and
(Claim 2)
, we have
\Psi\big(l(y_{2}),\mbox{ }y_{2}\big)=0
and
\Psi_{x_{1}}\big(l(y_{2}),\mbox{ }y_{2}\big)<0
. Then the implicit function theorem can be applied to
\Psi
at each point
\big(l(y_{2}),\mbox{ }y_{2}\big)
. That is, for each
y_{2}\in A
, there exists a small neighborhood
U\subset A
containing
y_{2}
such that we have

\Psi(f(s),\mbox{ }s)=0,\quad s\in U

for some function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
which is continuously differentiable in
U
and satisfies
f(y_{2})=l(y_{2})
. In
(Claim 2)
, we proved that
\Psi(x_{1},x_{2})
is strictly decreasing in
x_{1}>0
, and it follows that
f(s)=l(s)
for each
s\in U
. In conclusion,
l
is continuously differentiable in
A
. To show that
l^{\prime}\in C^{r}_{loc}(A)
for each
r\in(0,1)
, we fix any
r\in(0,1)
. It suffices to show that, for any
s\in A
and
\epsilon>0
satisfying that
[s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon]\subset A
, we have

\sup_{0<|s-t|\leq\epsilon}\frac{|\mbox{ }l^{\prime}(s)-l^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ }|}{|s-t|^{r}}<\infty.

By the formula
\Psi(l(s),s)=0
, we have

l^{\prime}(s)=-\frac{\Psi_{x_{2}}(l(s),s)}{\Psi_{x_{1}}(l(s),s)}.

Fix
s\in A
and
\epsilon>0
to satisfy
[s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon]\subset A
. Then for any
t\in[s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon]
, we have

|\mbox{ }l^{\prime}(s)-l^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\frac{\big|(\nabla\Psi)(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|}{\big|\mbox{ }\Psi_{x_{1}}(l(s),s)\cdot\Psi_{x_{1}}(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|}\cdot\big|\mbox{ }(\nabla\Psi)(l(s),s)-(\nabla\Psi)(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|.

We deal with the denominator first. As the continuous function
g(y):=\Psi_{x_{1}}(l(y),y)
satisfies
g<0
in the domain
A
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

g<-C_{0}
in
[s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon]
. Therefore we have

|\mbox{ }l^{\prime}(s)-l^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }\lesssim\big|(\nabla\Psi)(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|\cdot\big|\mbox{ }(\nabla\Psi)(l(s),s)-(\nabla\Psi)(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|.

By Lemma
2.4
, the relation
\nabla\Psi=\nabla\mathcal{G}[\omega]-(0,W)
leads to
\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla\mathcal{G}[\omega]\|_{L^{\infty}}+W<\infty
, so we finally get

|\mbox{ }l^{\prime}(s)-l^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }\lesssim\big|\mbox{ }(\nabla\Psi)(l(s),s)-(\nabla\Psi)(l(t),t)\mbox{ }\big|.

As
\nabla\Psi\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
by Lemma
2.4
, we have

|\mbox{ }l^{\prime}(s)-l^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }\lesssim_{r}\mbox{ }|\mbox{ }(l(s)-l(t))^{2}+(s-t)^{2}\mbox{ }|^{r/2}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }|s-t|^{r}\cdot\big|\mbox{ }\|l^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon])}^{2}+1\mbox{ }\big|^{r/2}

where
\|l^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon])}<\infty
due to
l^{\prime}\in C(A).
Therefore we have
l^{\prime}\in C^{r}_{loc}(A)
.

It remains to show that
l\in C((0,\infty))
. Note that, at each point in
A
and
(0,\infty)\backslash\overline{A}
, the continuity of
l
is trivially obtained using the fact that
A
is an open set. For each
y_{2}\in(0,\infty)\cap\partial A
, we observe that

\mathcal{G}[\omega](0,y_{2})=Wy_{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad l(y_{2})=0

by
(Claim 1)
. We need to show that
\lim_{s\to y_{2}}l(s)=0
. As
\omega
is boundedly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have

\limsup_{s\to y_{2}}l(s)<\infty.

If
l(s)
does not converges to 0 as
s\to y_{2}
, there exists a sequence
\{s_{n}\}\to y_{2}
and a number
L\in(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}l(s_{n})=L.
By the continuity of
\mathcal{G}[\omega]
, we have

\mathcal{G}[\omega](L,y_{2})-Wy_{2}=0,

which means that
\mathcal{G}[\omega](0,y_{2})=\mathcal{G}[\omega](L,y_{2})
. It contradicts to
(Claim 2)
. In sum, we have
l\in C((0,\infty))
.

Finally, we now prove (iii), starting from the following claim.

(Claim 3)

\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(s,0)
strictly decreases in
s\geq 0
.

For
s\geq 0
, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(s,0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{y_{2}}{(y_{1}-s)^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf y}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{A}\int_{-l(y_{2})-s}^{l(y_{2})-s}\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

For fixed
y_{2}\in A
, observe that the integrand

\int_{-l(y_{2})-s}^{l(y_{2})-s}\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}dy_{1}

strictly decreases in
s\geq 0
. This completes the proof of
(Claim 3)
.

Note that we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(0,0)>2W

by Lemma
4.5
. By (
3.4
) in Lemma
3.18
, there exists
K\in(0,\infty)
such that we have

x_{1}>K\mbox{ }\Rightarrow\mbox{ }\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(x_{1},0)=\lim_{x_{2}\searrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega](x_{1},x_{2})}{x_{2}}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }W.

As
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(s,0)
decreases strictly in
s\geq 0
, there exists a unique constant
a\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}(a,0)=W.

It remains to show that
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}l(s)=a
. As
\omega
is boundedly supported, we have

\limsup_{s\searrow 0}{l(s)}<\infty.

Suppose that
l(s)
does not converge to
a
as
s\searrow 0
. Then there exists a sequence
s_{n}^{\prime}\searrow 0
and a number
a^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying that

\lim_{n\to\infty}l(s_{n}^{\prime})\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }a^{\prime}\mbox{ }\neq\mbox{ }a.

In the relation

W=\frac{\mathcal{G}[\omega]\big(\mbox{ }l(s_{n}^{\prime}),\mbox{ }s_{n}^{\prime}\big)}{s_{n}^{\prime}},

due to
\mathcal{G}[\omega]\big(\mbox{ }l(s_{n}^{\prime}),\mbox{ }0\big)=0,
we can use the mean value theorem for each
n\geq 1
to find a sequence
0<s_{n}^{\prime\prime}<s_{n}^{\prime}
such that

W=\widetilde{\mathcal{G}[\omega]}_{x_{2}}\big(\mbox{ }l(s_{n}^{\prime}),\mbox{ }s_{n}^{\prime\prime}\big).

Letting
n\to\infty
gives
W=\mathcal{G}[\omega]_{x_{2}}(a^{\prime},0)
, which contradicts to
a^{\prime}\neq a
. This completes the proof of (iii).
∎

Estimates when flux constant vanishes due to small impulse

Under the small impulse condition, knowing that

Lemma 4.6

Proof.

Let

. As

is nonmepty, we choose any

by Lemma
4.4

(Claim 1)

\quad M^{4/3}\lesssim I_{M}\mbox{ }

\mbox{ }M^{1/3}\lesssim W

4.3

3.21

(Claim 2)

Using the relation

in

\sup_{{\bf x}\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,{(\omega)}}{x_{2}}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}+\|\omega\|_{1}^{1/4}M^{1/6}.

2.1

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{K/2^{n+1}<\mbox{ }x_{2}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }K/2^{n}}\omega({\bf x})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}.

For any

2.1

, we obtain

and thus

. In sum, we get

(Claim 3)

\mbox{ }I_{M}\lesssim M^{4/3},\mbox{ }

\mbox{ }\|\mathcal{G}[\omega]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\lesssim M^{2/3},\mbox{ }
and
\mbox{ }W\lesssim M^{1/3}
.

W\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\frac{4I_{M}}{3M}\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }M^{1/3}.

It completes the proof of (

5.
Variational problem on exact impulse without mass bound

5.1.
Variational problem without mass bound

For
M>0
, we then consider the following admissible set without mass bound,

K_{M,\infty}\mbox{ }:=\mbox{ }\Bigl\{\mbox{ }\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega=\mathbf{1}_{A}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad\mbox{where }A\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\mbox{ is (Lebesgue) measurable, }\quad\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}=M\mbox{ }\Bigl\},

with the maximal energy

I_{M,\infty}\mbox{ }:=\sup_{\omega\mbox{ }\in\mbox{ }K_{M,\infty}}E(\omega)

and the set of maximizers

S_{M,\infty}:=\Big\{\mbox{ }\omega\in K_{M,\infty}\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }E(\omega)=I_{M,\infty}\mbox{ }\Big\}.

Observe that this new admissible set
K_{M,\infty}
does not contain any upper bound condition for mass. We note that the elementary estimate (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.3
:

\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}G({\bf x},{\bf y})\omega({\bf x})\omega({\bf y})\mbox{ }d{\bf x}d{\bf y}\Bigl|\mbox{ }\lesssim\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}\|\omega\|_{2}^{1/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}^{1/2}

does not guarantee that
I_{M,\infty}<\infty
. In this section, we will prove that
I_{M,\infty}<\infty
and
S_{M,\infty}\neq\emptyset
, which is quite surprising. Moreover, this new problem inherits all results from the previous variational problems. The main result of this section is given in Theorem
5.1
below, and this section is dedicated to its proof.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have the following properties:

(i)

I_{1,\infty}<\infty
.

(ii)

S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty.

(iii)

Each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
satisfies the relation

\omega\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}>0\mbox{ }\}}\quad\mbox{ a.e.
\hskip 10.22217ptin }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\quad\mbox{ for the traveling speed }\mbox{ }W:=\frac{4}{3}\mbox{ }I_{1,\infty}.

(iv)

Each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
has a bounded support and satisfies the Steiner symmetry condition in Definition
3.2
up to translation. More precisely, for each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
, there exist
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and an open bounded set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying the Steiner symmetry condition such that, for the translation
\omega_{\tau}
given by
\omega_{\tau}(\cdot):=\omega(\cdot+(\tau,0))
, we have

\omega_{\tau}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\mathbf{1}_{A}\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\mbox{ }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

(v)

There exists an absolute constant
C\geq 1
such that, for each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
, we have

\sup_{{\bf x}\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,{(\omega)}}|{\bf x}|\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{1}{C}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C.

Remark 5.2
.

The key idea of the proof of Theorem
5.1
is to show that

S_{1,\infty}=S_{1,\nu,1}\quad\mbox{ for sufficiently large }\mbox{ }\nu>0.

Remark 5.3
.

In the statements (i)-(iv) of Theorem
5.1
, we can replace
I_{1,\infty},\mbox{ }S_{1,\infty}
with
I_{M,\infty},\mbox{ }S_{M,\infty}
for each
M>0
by the scaling: The scaling map
\phi:\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega}
given by

\hat{\omega}({\bf x})=\omega(M^{-1/3}{\bf x})

maps the set
S_{1,\infty}
to the set
S_{M,\infty}
bijectively, with the scaled traveling speed and the maximal energy given by

W_{M}:=W\cdot M^{1/3}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad I_{M,\infty}=I_{1,\infty}\cdot M^{4/3}.

5.2.
Scaling property

Recall the estimate (
4.3
) in Lemma
4.6
, which gives

\|\omega\|_{1}\lesssim M^{2/3}.

As the impulse
M
is small enough, each maximizer cannot achieve the full mass (which is 1) to attain the maximal energy. Using this property, we can show the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a constant
M_{2}>0
satisfying the following:

For each
M\in(0,M_{2})
and any
\alpha>1
, we have

S_{M}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }S_{M,\alpha,1}.

Proof.

Denote the constant
C>1
in Lemma
4.6
as
C_{0}>1
. Choose any small constant
M_{2}\in(0,1)
satisfying that

M_{2}\leq M_{1}\mbox{ }\mbox{ and }\mbox{ }C_{0}\cdot M_{2}^{2/3}\leq 1.

Let
M\in(0,M_{2})
and
\alpha>1
. As
S_{M,\alpha,1}
is nonempty, choose any
\omega\in S_{M,\alpha,1}
and consider the scaling map

\hat{\omega}({\bf x})=\omega(\alpha^{1/2}{\bf x})

to attain

\|\hat{\omega}\|_{1}=\alpha^{-1}\|\omega\|_{1}\leq 1,\quad\|x_{2}\hat{\omega}\|_{1}=\alpha^{-3/2}\|x_{2}\omega\|_{1}=\alpha^{-3/2}M

and so
\hat{\omega}\in K_{\alpha^{-3/2}M}
. By the bijectivity of the scaling map from
S_{M,\alpha,1}
onto
S_{\alpha^{-3/2}M},\mbox{ }
we get
\mbox{ }\hat{\omega}\in S_{\alpha^{-3/2}M}
. Here, as
\mbox{ }\alpha^{-3/2}M<M_{1}
, we can use the estimate (
4.3
) in Lemma
4.6
to have

\|\hat{\omega}\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{0}\cdot(\alpha^{-3/2}M)^{2/3},

and therefore

\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{0}\cdot M^{2/3}<1.

This implies that
\omega\in K_{M}
. Using the assumption that
\omega\in S_{M,\alpha,1}
and the fact that
K_{M}\subset K_{M,\alpha,1}
, we obtain
\omega\in S_{M}
. In sum, we have
S_{M,\alpha,1}\subset S_{M}
, which leads to
S_{M,\alpha,1}=S_{M}
.∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
M_{2}>0
be the constant in Lemma
5.4
. Then for any
M,\mbox{ }v>0
satisfying
M\nu^{-3/2}<M_{2}
, we have

S_{M,\nu,1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }S_{M,\alpha,1},\quad\mbox{ for all }\alpha>\nu.

Proof.

Let
M,\mbox{ }v,\mbox{ }\alpha>0
and assume that
M\nu^{-3/2}<M_{2}
and
\alpha>\nu
. Note that the scaling map
\phi:\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega}
given by

\hat{\omega}({\bf x})=\omega(\nu^{1/2}{\bf x})

maps the set
S_{M,\nu,1}
onto the set
S_{v^{-3/2}M}
bijectively and the set
S_{M,\alpha,1}
onto the set
S_{v^{-3/2}M,\mbox{ }\alpha/\nu,\mbox{ }1}
bijectively. As
\mbox{ }M\nu^{-3/2}<M_{2}
and
\alpha/\nu>1
, by Lemma
5.4
we have

S_{v^{-3/2}M}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }S_{v^{-3/2}M,\mbox{ }\alpha/\nu,\mbox{ }1},

and therefore

S_{M,\nu,1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }S_{M,\alpha,1}.

∎

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first prove (i),(ii) by the following claim.

(Claim)
There exists a constant
\nu\in(0,\infty)
such that we have

I_{1,\infty}=I_{1,\nu,1}=I_{1,\alpha,1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad S_{1,\infty}=S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\alpha,1}\quad\mbox{ for each }\alpha>\nu.

First, observe that

K_{1,\infty}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\bigcup_{\tau>0}K_{1,\tau,1}.

Then we have

I_{1,\infty}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\sup\mbox{ }\Big\{\mbox{ }E(\omega)\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega\in\bigcup_{\tau>0}K_{1,\tau,1}\mbox{ }\Big\}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }\sup\mbox{ }\Bigg[\bigcup_{\tau>0}\Big\{\mbox{ }E(\omega)\mbox{ }:\mbox{ }\omega\in K_{1,\tau,1}\mbox{ }\Big\}\Bigg]\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}{\mbox{ }I_{1,\tau,1}}.

For the constant
M_{2}>0
in Lemma
5.4
, we fix any
v>0
to satisfy
\nu^{-3/2}<M_{2}
. Then by Corollary
5.5
, we get

I_{1,\nu,1}=I_{1,\alpha,1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\alpha,1}

for any
\alpha>\nu
. So we obtain

I_{1,\infty}\leq I_{1,\nu,1}<\infty.

Moreover, observe that
I_{1,\nu,1}\leq I_{1,\infty}
by the relation
K_{1,\nu,1}\subset K_{1,\infty}.
So we get

I_{1,\infty}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }I_{1,\nu,1}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }I_{1,\alpha,1}\quad\mbox{ for each }\alpha>\nu.

Using this, we observe that
S_{1,\nu,1}\subset S_{1,\infty}
, with relation
S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\alpha,1}
for each
\alpha>\nu
. In addition, we can show
S_{1,\nu,1}\supset S_{1,\infty}
in the following way. We choose any
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
and put
\alpha^{\prime}:=\max\{\nu,\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}\}
to get

\omega\in K_{1,\alpha^{\prime},1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad E(\omega)=I_{1,\infty}=I_{1,\nu,1}=I_{1,\alpha^{\prime},1},

so we obtain
\omega\in S_{1,\alpha^{\prime},1}.
By the relation
S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\alpha^{\prime},1}
due to
\alpha^{\prime}>\nu
, we have
\omega\in S_{1,\nu,1}.
Therefore we obtain

S_{1,\infty}\mbox{ }=\mbox{ }S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,\alpha,1}\quad\mbox{ for each }\alpha>\nu.

It completes the proof of the claim.

Note that the above claim implies that
I_{1,\infty}<\infty
and the set
S_{1,\infty}
is nonempty. By Corollary
3.4
, for each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
there exists
W=W(\omega)>0
and
\gamma=\gamma(\omega)\geq 0
satisfying that

\omega=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mbox{ }\mathcal{G}[\omega]-Wx_{2}-\gamma>0\mbox{ }\}},

where we have
\gamma=0
due to the relations
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}=S_{1,\nu,1}=S_{1,2\nu,1}
and
\|\omega\|_{1}\leq\nu<2\nu
. Moreover, Corollary
3.4
guarantees that
\omega
may be assumed to satisfy the Steiner symmetry condition up to translation. By Corollary
3.23
, we obtain the relation

I_{1,\infty}=\frac{3}{4}W,

which implies that the traveling speed is unique in the class
S_{1,\infty}
. Moreover, for the scaled maximizer
\hat{\omega}\in S_{\nu^{-3/2}}
, we can apply Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
to obtain that

\frac{1}{C_{0}}\Big(\nu^{-3/2}\Big)^{2/3}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\|\hat{\omega}\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{0}\Big(\nu^{-3/2}\Big)^{2/3}\quad\mbox{ and }\sup_{{\bf x}\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,{({\hat{\omega}})}}{|{\bf x}|}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{1}\cdot\Big(\nu^{-3/2}\Big)^{1/3}

for the absolute constants
C_{0}>1,\mbox{ }C_{1}>0
in Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
each. Therefore we have

\frac{1}{C_{0}}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }\|\omega\|_{1}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{0}\quad\mbox{ and }\sup_{{\bf x}\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,{{(\omega)}}}{|{\bf x}|}\mbox{ }\leq\mbox{ }C_{1}.

Note that both inequalities hold for each
\omega\in S_{1,\infty}
. It completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
. ∎

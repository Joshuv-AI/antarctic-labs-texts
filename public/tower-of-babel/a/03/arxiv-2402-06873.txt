Analysis of Unregularized Optimal Control Problems Constrained by the Boussinesq System Thanks: N.J. was supported by the FWF grants P-31400-N32 and I4571-N.
Nicolai Jork, John Sebastian Simon

Abstract
This paper investigates solution stability properties of unregularized tracking-type optimal control problems constrained by the Boussinesq system. In our model, the controls may appear linearly and distributed in both of the equations that constitute the Boussiniesq system and in the objective functional. We establish, not only the existence of weak solutions, but also unique existence of strong solutions in L p L^{p} sense for the Boussinesq system as well as its corresponding linearized and adjoint systems. The optimal control problem is then analyzed by providing the existence of an optimal control, and by establishing first-order necessary and second order sufficient conditions. Then, using assumptions on the joint growth of the first and second variations of the objective functional, we prove the strong metric Hölder subregularity of the optimality mapping, which in turn allows the study of solution stability of the optimal control and states under various linear and nonlinear perturbations. Such perturbations may appear in the Boussinesq system and the objective functional. As an application, we provide a convergence rate for the optimal solutions of the Tikhonov regularized problem as the Tikhonov parameter tends to zero. Furthermore, the obtained stability of the optimal states provides, to the best knowledge of the authors, the first result on the stability of the second-order sufficient condition in affine PDE-constrained optimization under an assumption on the desired profile which is natural for tracking-type objective functionals.

Introduction

Optimal control is concerned with finding a function that steers a given dynamical system according to some goal.
For instance, driving a fluid toward a given velocity field.
In the past few decades, optimal control problems with dynamics given by solutions of partial differential equations have become of great interest among mathematicians and engineers alike.
This is of course because a lot of phenomena related to physics or economics are modeled with such kinds of equations.
Notably challenging are optimal control problems with equations describing nonlinear dynamics, of which one is considered in this paper.
In particular, we shall consider the so-called Boussinesq system that describes a heat-conducting fluid and is governed by the viscous incompressible Navier–Stokes equations and the heat equation.
The fluid is affected by the local variations of the temperature along the direction of the gravitational force, while the temperature is affected by the fluid by virtue of the drift of the fluid velocity.
Such systems, and their variations, have been well-studied due to their ability to model fluid flow phenomena wherein thermal effects cannot be ignored, eg. on atmospheric models
[
19
]
and ocean circulation
[
18
]
.
Mathematically speaking, the Boussinesq system has also gained attention due to its nonlinear nature.
We refer to the recent works

dealing with specific variations of the system we intend to study.

Optimal control problems subject to the Boussinesq system have also been the focus of several papers. For example, in
[
5
]
the authors established the existence of an optimal solution for a tracking-type objective functional and a corresponding Pontryagin minimum principle for the controls appearing in the heat equation. Li
[
23
]
, on another hand, studied a problem where constraints are imposed on the fluid velocity and temperature variable. The author further considered a generalized objective function that consists of a Lipschitz continuous functional with respect to the state variables and a functional that is at least quadratic. We also mention the following works that dealt with optimal control subject to the Boussinesq system
[

In the present paper, the controls may appear both on the momentum and the heat equation and are supported on small regions of the domain. This allows the model to apply to various situations, for instance, a control problem subject to a two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equation, which is reciprocally influenced by a separately controlled heat equation. We study distributed optimal control problems where the control appears at most affinely in the governing state equations and the objective functional.
Affinely controlled optimal control problems have attracted much attention recently as they appear naturally when considering tracking type objective functionals where the Tikhonov regularization term is absent.
This setting promotes a bang-bang structure of the optimal controls.
The study of optimal control problems subject to partial differential equations involving bang-bang solutions can only be traced to a handful of works. We mention
[
13
]
where the authors provided methods for approximating the solutions for a bang-bang optimal control problem for a system governed by the Poisson equation. In the aforementioned paper, the authors also laid out special cases wherein the so-called structural assumption for the adjoint variable will imply that the controls are indeed bang-bang. In
[

[
7

Aside from the necessary and sufficient conditions for the optimization problem, this paper delves into the stability of the optimal controls as well as the optimal states. The analysis in this direction will be aided by the so-called strong metric Hölder subregularity which has been studied in the case of elliptic

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\theta(t)\|_{V}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{c_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}}{2}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{\nu}\|\mathbf{F}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa}\|G(t)\|_{V^{*}},\end{aligned}

(19)

where
c_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}:=\max\left\{1,\|\mathbf{e}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right\}>0
. Ignoring for the meantime the parts with the norms in
\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}
and
V
, Gronwall lemma implies that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(H)}\leq c\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H}\right),

(20)

where
c\approx\exp{\left(c_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}T/2\right)}>
. Integrating (
19
) over
(0,T)
and using (
20
) thus give us

\displaystyle\nu\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}+\kappa\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(V)}\leq c\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H}\right),

(21)

where
c\approx\max\{1,\sqrt{c_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}T}\exp{(c_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}T/2)}\}
. We see that on the discretized level, (
20
) and (
21
) provide the uniform boundedness from which we extract the solution.

For the time derivatives, we see that by definition

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}\leq\left(\nu\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+c_{P,\mathbf{e}_{2}}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(H)}+\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}\right),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\theta\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}\leq\left(\kappa\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(V)}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}}\|\theta\|_{L^{4}}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}\right),

where
c_{P,\mathbf{e}_{2}}:=c_{P}\|\mathbf{e}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}>0
and
c_{P}>0
is the Poincaré constant.

For any variable
\varphi\in L^{2}(I;V)\cap L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
we see by virtue of the Ladyzhenskaya inequality that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{4}}\leq c_{L}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\varphi(t)\|_{V}^{2}\du t\right)^{1/4}\leq c_{L}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(V)}^{1/2},

where
c_{L}>0
is the Ladyzhenskaya constant. From this, we see that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|\partial_{t}\theta\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}\leq&\,c\left(1+\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H}\right)\\
&\times\left(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H}\right),\end{aligned}

where
c>0
is some constant not dependent on
\mathbf{u}
and
\theta
. From the estimate above Aubin-Lions embedding gives us strong convergences on the discretized level. Furthermore, the estimate above implies that
(\mathbf{u},\theta)\in W^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})\times W^{2}(V^{*})
through which the compact embedding
W^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})\times W^{2}(V^{*})\hookrightarrow C(\overline{I};\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}\times H)
validates the spaces where we take the initial conditions.

∎

L^{p}
space, for some
p\geq 2
. Although the analysis that follows is based on the work of C. Gerhardt
[
16

, to the best of our knowledge, the following regularity result is a novelty.
The existence of a unique strong solution to (

)–(

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
\mathbf{F}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{s}(Q)
,
G\in L^{s}(Q)
,
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\boldsymbol{W}^{2-1/s,s}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
and
\theta_{0}\in W^{2-1/s,s}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, the weak solution of (
10
)–(
14
) satisfies
(\mathbf{u},\theta)\in\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{s,\sigma}\times W^{2,1}_{s}
. Furthermore, there exists
p\in L^{s}(I;W^{1,s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})
satisfying – together with
(\mathbf{u},\theta)
– the energy estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{s,\sigma}}+\|\theta\|_{W^{2,1}_{s}}+\|p\|_{L^{s}(I;W^{1,s}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq c\left(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{s}}+\|G\|_{L^{s}}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2-1/s,s}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{2-1/s,s}_{0}(\Omega)}\right),\end{aligned}

(22)

for some constant
c>0
independent of
(\mathbf{u},\theta)
and
p
.

Proof.

Inspired by the work of C. Gerhardt
[
16
]
, we prove the regularity in three steps: i.
s=2
; ii.
2<s<4
; and iii.
4\leq s<\infty
.

Step I.
Let us take the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
10
) and (
12
) with
\varphi=\boldsymbol{A}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}
and
\psi=-\Delta\theta
, respectively. This gives us

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{V}^{2}\Big)+\frac{5\nu}{8}\|\boldsymbol{A}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\frac{4\kappa}{6}\|\Delta\theta(t)\|_{H}^{2}\\
&\leq c\Big(\|\mathbf{e}_{2}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty}}\|\theta(t)\|_{H}^{2}+\|\mathbf{F}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|G(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(\mathbf{u}(t)\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).\end{aligned}

(23)

From Hölder, Galiardo-Nirenberg and Young inequalities we thus get

\displaystyle c\|(\mathbf{u}(t)\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq c\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{4}+\frac{\nu}{8}\|\boldsymbol{A}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2},

\displaystyle c\|\mathbf{u}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq c\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\theta(t)\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{6}\|A\theta(t)\|_{H}^{2}.

These give us

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{V}^{2}\Big)+{\nu}\|\boldsymbol{A}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}^{2}+{\kappa}\|\Delta\theta\|_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\\
&\leq c\Big(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{V}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{V}^{2}\right)\du t\Big).\end{aligned}

Since we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}\du t\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}^{2}<\infty,

we can employ Grönwall inequality which yields

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}^{2}\leq c\Big(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{V}^{2}\Big).

Using the estimate above, we further get

\displaystyle{\nu}\|\boldsymbol{A}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}^{2}+{\kappa}\|\Delta\theta\|_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\leq c\Big(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{V}^{2}\Big).

One can get the estimate for the time derivative by taking
\varphi=\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}
and
\psi=\partial_{t}\theta
and following the same steps. We get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{2,\sigma}}+\|\theta\|_{W^{2,1}_{2}}\leq c\left(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}+\|G\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,2-1/2}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{2,2-1/2}_{0}(\Omega)}\right).\end{aligned}

(24)

Step II.
In this case, it follows that
(\mathbf{u},\theta)\in\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{2,\sigma}\times W^{2,1}_{2}
. This implies, by Sobolev embedding, that
(\mathbf{u},\theta)\in\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{2,\sigma}\times W^{2,1}_{2}\hookrightarrow\boldsymbol{L}^{q_{1}}(Q)\times L^{q_{2}}(Q)
for any
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
. With these observations, we establish our result by showing that
(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{s}(Q)
and
\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta\in L^{s}(Q)
.

Let
\bar{s}\geq 1
be such that
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{\bar{s}}\leq\frac{1}{s}
. By utilizing Hölder and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{s}}&\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\bar{s}}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}^{1/2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{2,\sigma}}^{3/2}.\end{aligned}

Using similar arguments, we can estimate the transport term for the heat as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{s}}&\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\bar{s}}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{4}}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{2,\sigma}}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(V)}^{1/2}\|\theta\|_{W^{2,1}_{2}}^{1/2}.\end{aligned}

As every term on the right-hand side of the two inequalities above is bounded, we thus find by
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
21
, Theorem 4.9.1]
the existence of unique strong solutions. From (
17
) and (
24
), one also get an estimate of the form (
22
).

Step III.

Due to the assumption on the powers of the data, we now have
(\mathbf{u},\theta)\in\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{s_{1},\sigma}\times W^{2,1}_{r_{1}}
for any
s_{1},r_{1}\in(2,4)
, and thus we have the compact embedding
\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{s_{1},\sigma}\times W^{2,1}_{r_{1}}\hookrightarrow C(\overline{Q})^{2}\times C(\overline{Q})
. In this final step, we point out that the crucial part in the proof of the previous cases was estimating the nonlinear parts where we used a
bootstrap
argument. For now, we only establish
(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{s}(Q)
as the other nonlinearity can be handled similarly. From Hölder and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities , and by choosing
s_{1}=3
and letting
\theta=(5s-6)/6s
we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{s}}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{3,\sigma}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{s}}\leq c\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{2,1}_{3,\sigma}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{3}(\boldsymbol{W}^{2,3})}^{\theta}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{3}}^{1-\theta}.

All terms on the right-hand side of the inequality above are bounded due to Step II.

Again, (
17
) and (
24
) implies an estimate of the form (
22
).

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }Q,

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|\partial_{t}\vartheta\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}\leq&\,c\left(1+\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\vartheta_{0}\|_{H}\right)\\

for some constant

Solutions to the adjoint system

The analysis of the optimal control problem will be aided by the so-called adjoint method. For this reason we consider, given
\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}\in W^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})
,
\theta\in W^{2}(V)
,
(\mathbf{F},G)\in\boldsymbol{V}\!^{*}\times V
, the following adjoint system

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{w}-(\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}+(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2})^{\top}\mathbf{w}+\Psi\nabla\theta+\nabla r

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}

\displaystyle\text{in }Q,

(33)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }Q,

(34)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\Psi-\kappa\Delta\Psi-\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla\Psi

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{w}+G

\displaystyle\text{in }Q,

(35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}=0,\quad\Psi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma,

(36)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}(T,\cdot)=\mathbf{w}_{T},\quad\Psi(T,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{T}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

(37)

The weak solution of the system above can be easily proven to exist using the same flow as we did for the nonlinear system. The only differences are:

\bullet\ \
i. to get the
L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
estimates, we mention that we get an estimate of the form (
23
) but have a negative sign on the part with the time derivative. To handle this, we integrate over the interval
(t,T)
for
t\in[0,T)
.

\bullet\ \
ii. the time derivates will satisfy
\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\in L^{4/3}(I;\boldsymbol{V}\!^{*})
and
\partial_{t}\Psi\in L^{4/3}(I;V^{*})
. The reason is that we will encounter the terms
((\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{w})
and
(\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\theta,\Psi)
where
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}
and
\psi\in V
are such that
\max\{\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}},\|\psi\|_{V}\}\leq 1
. We note that in this part of the proof, we should have been able to prove that
\mathbf{w}\in L^{2}(I;\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})\cap L^{\infty}(I;\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})
and
\Psi\in L^{2}(I;V)\cap L^{\infty}(I;H)
. Thus, to handle such terms, we have the following computation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{T}|((\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{w})|^{4/3}\du t&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{V}^{4/3}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2/3}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2/3}\du t\\
&\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}}^{2}\Big)\du t\\
&\leq c\Big(\|\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}\Big).\end{aligned}

The same arguments can be applied to the other term. From these, we prove our claim.

The summary of the existence of a weak solution to the adjoint system is written below.

.

Let
\mathbf{F}\in L^{2}(I;\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})
,
H\in L^{2}(I;V^{*})
,
\mathbf{w}_{T}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}
and

. Then at least one weak solution

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma})}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma})}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(H)}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}(V)}\leq c(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V)}+\|\mathbf{w}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\Psi_{T}\|_{H})

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{4/3}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|\partial_{t}\Psi\|_{L^{4/3}(V^{*})}\leq&\,c\left(1+\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{w}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\Psi_{T}\|_{H}\right)\\
&\times\left(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{V}\!_{\sigma}^{*})}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(V^{*})}+\|\mathbf{w}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}_{\sigma}}+\|\Psi_{T}\|_{H}\right).\end{aligned}

(39)

for some constant

. Furthermore, there exists

such that the solution
(\mathbf{w},\Psi)
and

Let

From the adjoint and linear systems, by using Hölder inequality, and (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\bar{s}}}^{\bar{s}}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\bar{s}}}^{\bar{s}}=\int_{Q}|\mathbf{v}|^{\bar{s}-2}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}+|\vartheta|^{\bar{s}-2}\vartheta\cdot\vartheta\du x\du t\\

4

Here, we prove the existence of at least one global optimal solution and introduce the optimality conditions.
Before we begin, we recall

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\Big\{\boldsymbol{\varrho}:=(\mathbf{q},\Theta)\in\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty}(I\times\omega_{q})\times L^{\infty}(I\times\omega_{h})\Big|\ \underline{\mathbf{q}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 7.7778pt\hskip-7.7778pt\hbox{$\leq$}\hskip-33.33344pt\raisebox{-6.55415pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\bullet$}}}}$}$}}}}\mathbf{q}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 7.7778pt\hskip-7.7778pt\hbox{$\leq$}\hskip-33.33344pt\raisebox{-6.55415pt}{\hbox{$\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\bullet$}}}}$}$}}}}\overline{\mathbf{q}},\,\underline{\Theta}\leq\Theta\leq\overline{\Theta}\Big\}.

where
(\mathbf{u},\theta):=\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})
,
(\mathbf{v},\vartheta):=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho})
,
(\mathbf{w},\Psi):=\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})
and
(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\vartheta})=\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]
.

Proof.

We use the chain rule to prove the theorem. For the first-order derivative, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}&=\alpha_{1}\left(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\mathbf{u}_{d},S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\right)_{Q}+\alpha_{2}\left(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\theta_{d},S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\right)_{Q}\\
&\quad+\beta_{1}(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\mathbf{u}_{T},S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T))_{\Omega}+\beta_{2}\left(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\theta_{T},S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\right)_{\Omega}\\
&=\langle(\alpha_{1}(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\mathbf{u}_{d}),\alpha_{2}(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\theta_{d})),\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\rangle\\
&\quad+\langle g_{T}(\beta_{1}(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\mathbf{u}_{T}),\beta_{2}(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\theta_{T})),\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\rangle\\
&=\langle(\mathbf{w},\Psi),\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\rangle=(\mathbf{w},\delta\mathbf{q})_{\omega_{q}}+(\Psi,\delta\Theta)_{\omega_{f}}.\end{aligned}

Using the chain rule once again, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]&=\alpha_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\alpha_{1}\left(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\mathbf{u}_{d},S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]\right)_{Q}+\alpha_{2}\left(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-\theta_{d},S_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]\right)_{Q}\\
&\quad+\beta_{1}(S_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\mathbf{u}_{T},S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}](T))_{\Omega}+\beta_{2}\left(S_{2}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})(T)-\theta_{T},S_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}](T)\right)_{\Omega}\end{aligned}

Denoting for simplicity for now
(\mathbf{w},\Psi)=\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})
,
(\mathbf{u},\theta)=\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})
and
(\mathbf{v},\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]
we further get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})[\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho},\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}]&=\alpha_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\left(-\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{w}-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\top}\mathbf{w}+\Psi\nabla\theta+\nabla r,\mathbf{v}\right)_{Q}\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\left(-\partial_{t}\Psi-\kappa\Delta\Psi+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\Psi-\mathbf{e}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{w},\vartheta\right)_{Q}+(\mathbf{w}(T),\mathbf{v}(T))_{\Omega}+\left(\Psi(T),\vartheta(T)\right)_{\Omega}\\
&=\alpha_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\left(\mathbf{w},\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla q-\mathbf{e}_{2}\vartheta\right)_{Q}\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\left(\Psi,\partial_{t}\vartheta-\kappa\Delta\vartheta+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\vartheta+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\theta\right)_{Q}\\
&=\alpha_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\|S_{1}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\|S_{2}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})\delta\boldsymbol{\varrho}(T)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad-2((\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})_{Q}-2(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta,\Psi)_{Q}.\end{aligned}

∎

&\quad+M_{\mathcal{A}}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}^{\star}-\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty}}+\|\theta^{\star}-\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\Big(\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\|\boldsymbol{\varrho}-\boldsymbol{\varrho}^{\star}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}\times L^{2}}^{1/2}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}^{\star}-\mathbf{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{1/2}+\|\theta^{\star}-\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\Big)\Big(\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\\

\displaystyle\Big|\Big[J^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho})-J^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\varrho}^{\star})\Big](\boldsymbol{\varrho}-\boldsymbol{\varrho}^{\star})^{2}\Big|\leq\varepsilon\Big(\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\star}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\theta-\theta^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

whenever

.

Remark 4.22

The implication of the proposition above is that one can relax the assumption on the second-order derivative of the objective functional to obtain a sufficient optimality condition. It allows us to have the second variation of the objective functional attain negative values, i.e., we can assume

5
Solution stability

We study the stability properties of optimal controls and states under perturbation in the Boussinesq system and the objective functional.

4.18

Lemma 5.5

4.15

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied a tracking-type optimal control problem subject to the Boussinesq system and the controls appear distributively both on the continuity and heat equations which satisfy a box constraint. Since the objective functional contains no regularization for the controls, the solution is expected to be of bang-bang type. We provided first order necessary and second order sufficient conditions for the optimal control problem. The main objective in this paper was to establish the stability of the problem with respect to several perturbations: i.) linear perturbations on both the objective functional and the state equations; ii.) nonlinear perturbations in the form of the Tikhonov regularization and perturbation on the desired data.

Aside from the said stability results, we also provided the
L^{p}
regularity of the solutions of the Boussinesq system, its linearization and a corresponding adjoint system, which – as far as we are aware – is a novelty. Another novelty that we would want to highlight is the stability of the growth of the second-order derivative of the objective functional with respect to sufficiently small perturbations which does not depend on the extended critical cone of critical directions for the optimal bang-bang control.

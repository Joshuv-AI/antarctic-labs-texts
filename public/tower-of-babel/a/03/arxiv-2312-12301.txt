Holography of New Conformal Higher Spin Gravities in 𝟑 ​ d \boldsymbol{3d}


Abstract
We study the holography of the new conformal higher spin theories imposing general boundary conditions and the near horizon boundary conditions. General boundary conditions lead to the ASA which is a loop algebra of the underlying gauge algebra. The near horizon boundary conditions give u(1) currents at the boundary. For each of the boundary conditions we consider an example of conformal graviton, and its combination with a vector, and a spin-3 field, respectively. The near horizon boundary conditions lead to three dimensional Ba n ~ \tilde{n} ados-Teitelboim-Zanelli (BTZ)-like black hole which can have up to four horizons, Lobachevsky solutions, and to a generalisations of that BTZ-like solution. We verify this result independently obtaining it from the Cotton tensor. We also consider the classification of the one parameter subgroups of the so(3,2) algebra and comment on the classification of the solutions that we obtain within this algebra.

The program of holographic analysis for three-dimensional higher-spin (HS) gauge theories began with

First, in Section 2, we review the new conformal HiSGRA theories. Experts in higher-spin gravity can skip this section and proceed directly to Section 3.
In Section 3, we review the holography of Chern-Simons (CS) theory and comment on general BCs with a fixed chemical potential.

In Section 4, we focus on near-horizon BCs for spin-s fields in the new conformal HiSGRA theories, considering specific examples of CG, CG with a vector field, and CG with a spin-3 field. For these cases, we construct a map between the generators in the conformal ”PKLD” basis of the gauge algebra and those in the ”WL” fundamental representation. This basis simplifies the implementation of near-horizon BCs. The

When expressed in the metric formulation, these BCs yield generalizations of well-known solutions in 3D gravity. For example, for the rotating black hole in 3D, we provide a generalization for 3D CG and comment on higher-spin extensions.

In Section 5, we analyze CG in greater detail by studying the one-parameter subgroups of the

Section 6 summarizes and concludes our results.

Condition for absence of closed timelike curves.
Each Killing vector has an one parameter subgroup of isometries of AdS space. For the Killing vector
\xi
this is written as

\displaystyle P\rightarrow e^{t\xi}P

(5.2)

These mappings for

The quotient space that we obtain by identifying the points of a given orbit that belong to the identification subgroup, inherits a well defined metric from AdS space. The quotient space remains the solution of the equations that we are considering.

Due to the identification process, the curves that are on the same orbit and which join two points from AdS space, are closed in the quotient space. These closed curves should not be timelike or null, in order for the quotient space to have valid causal structure. A necessary condition for these curves not to be closed timelike curves is

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\xi>0

(5.3)

In general this condition is not sufficient to guarantee absence of closed timelike curves (CTC), however in this case it is

In some regions, the Killing vectors that appear in identifications and which give rise to black hole solutions, are timelike or null. These regions have to be cut out from the AdS space, for the identifications to be allowed. We call this new space AdS’. It is geodesically incomplete because there are geodesics that go beyond
\xi\cdot\xi>0

\xi\cdot\xi<0

\xi\cdot\xi=0

6
Conclusion

We have studied the holography of conformal HiSGRA theories by discussing the most general bcs and imposing near-horizon bcs.
For the holography of the most general bcs, we noted that the ASA corresponds to a loop algebra of the underlying gauge algebra. By selecting a specific set of generators from the most general bcs and retaining the chemical potentials, we defined near-horizon bcs, which led to an ASA corresponding to
u(1)
currents.

Imposing the near-horizon bcs, we obtain solutions that can be categorized into classes studied under the classification of the one-parameter subgroups of

SO(3,2)
. More precisely, once the near-horizon bcs are applied to the example with the conformal graviton, a suitable choice of the group element in the transformation from the first-order to the metric formulation can yield different solutions, depending on the choice of the projector
b
. As examples, we obtain solutions with:
(i) four real eigenvalues,
(ii) one real and one imaginary eigenvalue, which should belong to class
I_{d}

(iii) the Lobachevsky solution,
(iv) a solution that generalizes BTZ to the conformal scenario,
(v) a general solution that depends on three arbitrary functions of

\rho
, and
(vi) the simplest example, which reduces to BTZ.

We verify (iv) by solving the Cotton tensor equations of motion, as shown in Appendix B. A simplified form of metric (v) reproduces a 3D analog of the MKR solution.
For the near-horizon bcs of
sl(4)
, we present an example similar to solution (vi) of the conformal graviton. The modification arises due to the number of charges in the bcs. The distinction from the
so(3,2)
case appears when the group element
b
includes a generator that commutes with the generator of translation, multiplied by an arbitrary function of
\rho
. This results in the appearance of an additional term in the metric.

As an example of the metric formulation for the near-horizon bcs of the conformal graviton and a spin-3 field defined from the
sl(5)
algebra, we obtain a solution with one imaginary and one real eigenvalue, which fits the requirements for the solutions that should belong to the
I_{d}
class. For the same bcs, we also find a spin-3 field.

We calculate the entropies for the solutions coming from the near horizon boundary conditions and look for the conditions under which they connect to known results.

The solutions of conformal gravity in 3d have been significantly less studied compared to those of Einstein gravity, so we provide an analysis of the
SO(3,2)
one-parameter subgroups. Part of the analysis follows the approach of
[
28
]
, where the solutions of Einstein gravity in 3d were classified using the
SO(2,2)
one-parameter subgroups. The classification of
SO(3,2)
, in comparison to
SO(2,2)
, is complemented by two additional classes, which do not appear in the
SO(2,2)
case. For the two new classes, denoted as case
I_{d}
and case
V
, we consider the transformations required for constructing the metric.

We also provide specific examples. However, the general solutions that follow a geometric description analogous to geometric description in
[
28
]
, remain a subject for future study.

As shown in Table 1, the definitions of the Killing vectors for the
I_{b}
and
I_{c}
classes contain different components associated with the eigenvalue. The
I_{d}
class, on the other hand, includes the
0
component in both Killing vectors, which complicates constructing a geometric solution analogous to the BTZ construction for the
I_{b}
class.

Appendix A: Holography of Chern-Simons action

Let us review the general approach to holography of the Chern-Simons action in the vielbein formulation.

The action can be written in 2+1 decomposition as

\displaystyle I=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\epsilon^{ij}g_{\mu\nu}(\dot{\omega}^{\mu}_{i}\omega_{j}^{\nu}+\omega_{t}^{\mu}F_{ij}^{\nu})+B(\partial\Sigma)

(8.1)

For
B
the boundary term that depends on the bcs and needs to be included to ensure that the action is gauge invariant. The curvature
F_{ij}^{\mu}

F_{ij}^{\mu}=\partial_{i}\omega_{j}^{\mu}-\partial_{j}\omega_{i}^{\mu}+f^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}\omega^{\nu}\omega^{\rho}

and the
f^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}
are the structure constants of the Lie algebra.
The indices
\mu,\nu
are raised and lowered with the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
which is invertible.

\omega_{t}^{\mu}
is treated as a Lagrange mutliplier and the fields
\omega_{i}^{\mu}
are dynamical fields which satisfy Poisson brackets. The variation of the action with respect to the
\omega_{t}^{\mu}
gives the constraint
G_{a}\equiv\frac{k}{4\pi}g_{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{ij}F_{ij}^{\nu}
which satisfies Poisson bracket algebra

\displaystyle\{G_{\mu}(x),G_{\nu}(y)\}=f^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}G_{\rho}(x)\delta(x,y)

(8.2)

so
G_{\mu}
are generators of gauge transformations which act on the phase space.
The smeared generators
G(\xi^{\mu})=\int_{\Sigma}\xi^{\mu}G_{\mu}+Q(\xi)

give central extension of the algebra of the global charges.
We assume that the parameter
\xi
does not depend on the
\omega_{i}
in the interior, such that the variation of the smeared generator leads to

\displaystyle\delta G=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{ij}\xi_{\mu}D_{i}\delta\omega_{j}^{\mu}+\delta Q(\xi)

(8.3)

for
D_{i}v^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{i}v^{\mu}+f^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}\omega_{i}^{\nu}v^{\rho}
covariant derivative. Here,

is an arbitrary vector field. Integration by parts of the first term leads to the

\displaystyle\delta G=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{ij}D_{i}\xi_{\mu}\delta\omega_{j}^{\nu}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\xi_{\mu}\delta\omega_{k}^{\mu}dx^{k}+\delta Q.

(8.4)

The functional derivative of G then becomes well defined and the surface terms cancel out if we demand that the variation of the charge is given by

\displaystyle\delta Q=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\xi_{\mu}\delta\omega_{i}^{\mu}dx^{i}.

(8.5)

The Poisson bracket of the two smeared generators, with the parameters
\xi

\displaystyle\{G(\xi),G(\lambda)\}=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}D\xi_{a}\wedge D\lambda^{a}

\displaystyle\{G(\xi),G(\lambda)\}=\int_{\Sigma}[\xi,\lambda]^{a}G_{a}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\xi_{a}D\lambda^{a}

(8.7)

For
[\xi,\lambda]^{a}=f^{a}_{bc}\xi^{b}\lambda^{c}

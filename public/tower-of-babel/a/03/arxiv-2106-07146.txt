Large Deviations Asymptotics of Rectangular Spherical Integral
Alice Guionnet, Jiaoyang Huang

Abstract
In this article we study the Dyson Bessel process, which describes the evolution of singular values of rectangular matrix Brownian motions, and prove a large deviation principle for its empirical particle density. We then use it to obtain the asymptotics of the so-called rectangular spherical integrals as m , n m,n go to infinity while m / n m/n converges.



1
Introduction

In this article we shall study the asymptotics of the so-called rectangular spherical integrals, also called Berezin-Karpelevich type integrals in the literature. This type of integrals arises when one studies rectangular matrices and is the natural counterpart of the well known Harish-Chandra -Itzykson-Zuber (HCIZ) integral.
The interest in spherical integrals comes from different fields. Harish-Chandra was motivated by Fourier analysis in semi-simple Lie algebras. They appear in physics as the density in matrix models such as the Ising model
[
25
,
47
,
16
]
or more generally matrix models with an external field
[
11
]
, including the famous Kontsevich matrix model
[
43
]
. Their uses in random matrix theory appeared more recently. First it was shown that spherical integral with a rank one external field gives asymptotically the famous
R
-transform defined by Voiculescu in free probability
[
29
]
as an analogue of Fourier transform. This approach was generalized to the rectangular-free convolution by using rectangular spherical integrals
[
27
]
or to the multiplicative free convolution and the
S
-transform
[
10
,
48
]
.

Knowing the asymptotics of rank one spherical integrals allowed as well to investigate the large deviations for the extreme eigenvalues of random matrices. This approach was introduced in
[
33
]
where it was shown that the probability that the largest eigenvalue of a Wigner matrix takes an unexpected value is the same when the entries are Rademacher or Gaussian. This universality phenomenon was shown to hold for random matrices with i.i.d. entries whose Laplace transform is bounded by the Laplace transform of a Gaussian variable with the same covariance. For more general sub-Gaussian entries, a transition appears in the rate function between large deviations towards a very large value with a heavy tail type rate function, and deviations close to the bulk which are governed by the Gaussian rate function. Such considerations were extended to unitary invariant ensembles
[
35
]
, to the joint distribution of the largest eigenvalue and its eigenvector
[
9
]
, to sum of matrices, to finitely many extreme eigenvalues
[
34
]
. Indeed, the asymptotics of spherical integral could be extended to finite rank external fields
[
34
]
. For small enough matrices, the same asymptotics were shown to extend to the case where the rank goes to infinity more slowly than the dimension
[
19
]
and to full rank matrices
[
18
]
.
However, the limit differs when the rank of both matrices are of the same order and the matrices do not have small norms. Such a limit can as well be used to prove large deviation principles for the empirical measure of the eigenvalues of random matrices
[
4
]
and more generally study the asymptotics of matrix models with an external field
[
11
,
32
]
.

The formula for the asymptotics of HCIZ integrals was foreseen by Matytsin
[
45
]
and then proven rigorously in
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
. Matytsin used the description of Spherical integrals as invariant eigenfunctions of the Laplacian. The approach of
[
30
]
is kind of dual and based on a representation of spherical integrals as the density of a Dyson Brownian motion conditioned at time one, a representation which allows to use large deviations techniques and martingales. In this paper, we follow the same route for the rectangular case but prove a more general large deviation principle for conditioned Dyson Brownian motions.
In fact, the result in
[
30
]
relies on the matrix model, and only concerns the case
\beta=1
or
2
whereas we can deal in this paper with all cases
\beta\geqslant 1
. The extension of
[
30
]
to the rectangular case is a natural step, which however posed significant difficulties for the proof of the lower bound if one uses the methods of
[
30
]
, due to additional singularity of the drifts. We should also mention the heuristics proposed in this setting in
[
26
]
following Matystin’s arguments. One
key idea of this paper is to improve the large deviations lower bound by obtaining better criteria for the uniqueness of solutions to McKean-Vlasov equations with smooth fields inspired from

[
44
]
, rather than the weaker approach developed in
[
15
]
. Another novelty in this paper is a quantitative estimate for the convergence to Dyson Brownian motion with very general potential by a coupling argument, see Proposition
3.5
. Under more restricted assumptions, i.e. the limiting profile has square root behavior around the edge, such quantitative estimates for the convergence has been obtained in
[
40
,
1
,
39
]
by using the characteristic method.
The quantitative estimate for the convergence allows us to efficiently control the locations of each particles and extend our result to Dyson Bessel processes which arises when one considers rectangular matrices and hence derive the limits of rectangular spherical integrals. We now state more precisely our main results.

The rectangular spherical integral is given by

\displaystyle I_{n,m}(A_{n},B_{n})=\iint e^{\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V,

(1.1)

where if
\beta=1
,
U\in{\mathcal{O}}(n),V\in{\mathcal{O}}(m)
follow the Haar distribution over the orthogonal group, and
A_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m},B_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
, whereas for
\beta=2
,
U\in{\mathcal{U}}(n),V\in{\mathcal{U}}(m)
follow the Haar distribution over the unitary group,
A_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times m},B_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times m}
for
\beta=2
. We call such integrals rectangular spherical integrals and shall study their asymptotic behavior when
m
and
n
go to infinity so that
the ratio
m/n
converges towards some

1+\alpha\in[1,\infty)
. This type of spherical integral arises when one studies rectangular matrices and is the natural counterpart of the well known Harish-Chandra -Itzykson-Zuber (HCIZ) integral defined when
\beta=2
and for two self-adjoint matrices
A_{n},B_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
by

I_{n}(A_{n},B_{n})=\int e^{n\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}UB_{n}U^{*})}{\rm d}U\,,

where

This integral was shown by Harish-Chandra
[

(1.2)

where

are eigenvalues of

and

respectively, and

are Vandermonde determinants.
In 2003, Schlittgen and Wettig

[
49

considered a generalization of the above rectangular spherical integral given by

\displaystyle\iint\det[UV]^{\nu}e^{\tau\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*}+VD_{n}^{*}U^{*}C_{n})/2}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V,

(1.3)

where

are

are deterministic

is a non-negative integer. They showed that the generalization of the above integral to the case of unequal dimensions of

\displaystyle\iint e^{\tau\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*}+VD_{n}^{*}U^{*}C_{n})/2}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V=\frac{\tau^{n(m-1)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(m-i)!(n-i)!}{\Delta({\bm{x}}^{2})\Delta({\bm{y}}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}y_{i})^{m-n}}\det[I_{m-n}(2\tau x_{i}y_{j})]_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n},

is an
n\times n

is an

are deterministic

are deterministic

We get the rectangular spherical integral (

2
Dyson Bessel Process

In this section we introduce Dyson Bessel process, which is the singular value process of rectangular matrix brownian motions. Then in section
2.2
, we will write Dyson Bessel process as a change of measure from Dyson Brownian motion using Girsanov’s theorem.

2.1
Decomposition

The rectangular spherical integral (
1.1
) is related to real (
\beta=1
) and complex (
\beta=2
) rectangular random matrices with nonzero mean.
We consider an
n\times m
rectangular random matrices
X_{n}
with nonzero mean,

\displaystyle X_{n}=A_{n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}G_{n},

(2.1)

where
A_{n}={\mathbb{E}}[X_{n}]
is deterministic, and
G_{n}
is an
n\times m
rectangular matrix with independent real (
\beta=1
) or complex (
\beta=2
) Gaussian entries.
We denote the singular value decomposition of
X_{n}=UB_{n}V^{*}
, with
B_{n}=\diag\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n}\}
. Then we can rewrite the law of
X_{n}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta n}{2\pi}}\right)^{\beta mn}e^{-\frac{\beta n}{2}\operatorname{Tr}((X_{n}-A_{n})(X_{n}-A_{n})^{*})}{\rm d}X_{n}\\
&\propto\prod_{i}b_{i}^{\beta(m-n+1)-1}\prod_{i<j}|b^{2}_{i}-b^{2}_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta N}{2}(\sum_{i}b^{2}_{i}+\sum a^{2}_{i})+\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V{\rm d}B_{n}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Therefore, conditioning on the singular values of

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\beta N\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*})]}}{Z^{\beta}_{m,n}}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V.

(2.3)

We study the random matrices
X_{n}
as in (

The Dyson Bessel process (

\displaystyle\theta(s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{n})=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\sum_{i<j}\log(s_{i}+s_{j})+\alpha_{n}n\sum\log s_{k}\right),\quad s_{1}\geqslant s_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant s_{n}.

\displaystyle=\int\frac{x^{2}}{1+\varepsilon x^{2}}{\rm d}\nu_{t}\leqslant{\mathfrak{C}}+\frac{1}{2\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int(f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x))^{2}{\rm d}\nu_{s}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathfrak{C}}+\frac{2}{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{x^{2}}{1+\varepsilon x^{2}}{\rm d}\nu_{s}{\rm d}s={\mathfrak{C}}+\frac{2}{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\nu_{s}(f_{\varepsilon}){\rm d}s.

Grönwall’s inequality then implies that for all
t\leqslant 1

\displaystyle\nu_{t}(f_{\varepsilon})\leqslant e^{2/\beta}{\mathfrak{C}}.

The claim
3.6
follows by sending
\varepsilon
to
0
and monotone convergence theorem.

It was proven in
[
32
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
37
, Theorem 3.3]
that if
\mu_{0}=\nu_{0}
has bounded
5+\varepsilon
moments, i.e.

\displaystyle\int|x|^{5+\varepsilon}{\rm d}\nu_{0}<\infty,

(3.10)

and
\Sigma(\mu_{0}),\Sigma(\mu_{1})
are finite,
then Item (ii) and (iii) hold. This can be extended to the case where
\mu_{0}
has only a finite second moment following the arguments of the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 5.9]
. We briefly recall the main steps of the proof. First recall that free convolution reduces the dynamical entropy
(see
[
15
]
) so that if
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
denotes the semi-circle law with covariance
\varepsilon

\displaystyle S_{\mu_{0}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})\leqslant S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}).

But on the other hand,
H(\nu_{t}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon})
is uniformly bounded by
1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Therefore if we denote by
u^{\varepsilon}
the velocity field of
\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\nu_{t}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}
,

\int_{0}^{1}\int(u^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{1}\int(u^{\varepsilon}_{t}-H\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t+2\int_{0}^{1}\int(H\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\frac{4}{\beta}S_{\mu_{0}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}<\infty\,.

Hence, we can write

S_{\mu_{0}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})=\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int(u^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int(H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t-\beta\int_{0}^{1}\int H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t\,.

(3.11)

For the second term we used the well known formula (recall that

)

\int_{0}^{1}\int(H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int(\frac{d\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}}{\mathrm{d}x})^{3}\mathrm{d}x\,.

Finally for the last term of (

), we observe following
[

, Lemma 5.9]
that the continuity of

implies that

is continuous (thanks to the explicit formulas for the Hilbert transform of measures freely convoluted with the semi-circle laws given by Biane
[

]
). Since it is bounded and

is in

, we see that we can approximate the last term by Riemann sum. Then,
recall that by definition we have

to conclude that

\int_{0}^{1}\int H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\Sigma(\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Sigma(\nu_{1}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon})-\Sigma(\mu_{0}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon})\right)\,.

Hence, (

\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}

is bounded since it is bounded from above as

has bounded second moment and also from below since

\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int(u^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int(H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{\beta}{2}(\Sigma(\nu_{1}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon})-\Sigma(\nu_{0}))\leqslant S_{\mu_{0}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})\leqslant S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})\,.

In fact, because we could have done the same reasoning on the time interval
[0,t]
, we also see that
for all

S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})\geqslant\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int(u^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int(H\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{\beta}{2}(\Sigma(\nu_{s}\boxplus\sigma_{\varepsilon})-\Sigma(\nu_{0})),

which implies that

is uniformly bounded. We can finally let

going to zero to conclude. As a consequence of (

has finite free entropy, i.e.

. We refer the reader to
[

]
for details.

3.1
Large deviations upper bound

In this section, we prove the large deviations upper bound. We recall that the exponential tightness was already proven in this setting in the proof of
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
: for the sake of completeness we will recall this proof but in the new setting of the Bessel Dyson processes, see section
4
. We next prove the large deviations upper bound of Theorem
3.1

\displaystyle\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))\leqslant-S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}).

(3.12)

Take any test function
f_{t}(x)\in{\cal C}_{b}^{2,1}([0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}})
, and use Itô’s lemma to find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm d}\sum_{i}f_{t}(x_{i}(t))=\sum_{i}f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t)){\rm d}x_{i}(t)+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))}{2\beta n}+\partial_{t}f_{t}(x_{i}(t))\right){\rm d}t\\
&=\sum_{i}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))}{2\beta n}+\partial_{t}f_{t}(x_{i}(t))+\frac{f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))}{2n}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i}f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))\frac{{\rm d}W_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\beta n}}\\
&={\rm d}L^{f}_{t}+\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))-f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{j}(t))}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))}{2\beta n}{\rm d}t+\sum_{i}\partial_{t}f_{t}(x_{i}(t)){\rm d}t,\end{split}

(3.13)

where the martingale term is given by

\displaystyle{\rm d}L^{f}_{t}=\sum_{i}f^{\prime}_{t}(x_{i}(t))\frac{{\rm d}W_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\beta n}},\quad\langle L^{f},L^{f}\rangle_{t}=\frac{1}{\beta n}\int^{t}_{0}\sum_{i}(f_{t}^{\prime}(x_{i}(t)))^{2}{\rm d}t.

(3.14)

We recall the empirical particle density
\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
from (
3.1
). With it, we can rewrite (
3.13
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\int f_{t}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{t}-\int f_{t}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{0}\\
&=\frac{L^{f}_{t}}{n}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x\neq y}\frac{f_{s}^{\prime}(x)-f_{s}^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(y){\rm d}s+\frac{1}{2\beta n}\int_{0}^{t}\int f^{\prime\prime}_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{s}f_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}s\\
&=\frac{L^{f}_{t}}{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{f_{s}^{\prime}(x)-f_{s}^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(y)+\int\partial_{s}f_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x)+\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int f^{\prime\prime}_{t}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x)\right){\rm d}s.\end{split}

(3.15)

As
L^{f}
is bounded uniformly for
f\in C^{2,1}_{b}
, we can construct an exponential martingale using the martingale
{\rm d}L^{f}_{t}
from (
3.14
)

\displaystyle D_{t}=e^{nL^{f}_{t}-\frac{n^{2}}{2}\langle L^{f},L^{f}\rangle_{t}},\quad{\mathbb{E}}[D_{t}]={\mathbb{E}}[D_{0}]=1.

(3.16)

Using (
3.15
)
we can rewrite

nL^{f}_{t}-\frac{n^{2}}{2}\langle L^{f},L^{f}\rangle_{t}=n^{2}S^{n}_{t}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}),

where

\displaystyle S^{n}_{t}(\{\nu^{n}_{s},f_{s}\}_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t})

\displaystyle=\int f_{t}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{t}-\int f_{0}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{0}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{f_{s}^{\prime}(x)-f_{s}^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(y){\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{s}f_{s}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}(x){\rm d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}+\frac{1}{4}\right)f^{\prime\prime}_{t}(x){\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}{\rm d}s-\frac{1}{2\beta}\int^{t}_{0}\int(f_{t}^{\prime}(x))^{2}{\rm d}\nu^{n}_{s}{\rm d}s.

We also define

\displaystyle S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{nL^{f}_{1}}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2}\langle L^{f},L^{f}\rangle_{1}\right)=S^{n}_{1}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}).

(3.17)

Then for
\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta)
, we have by uniform (in
n\geqslant 1
) continuity of
\nu\mapsto S^{n}(\{\nu_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})
for any
f\in{\cal C}_{b}^{2,1}([0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}})
,

\displaystyle S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})=S^{n}(\{\nu_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})+\oo_{n}(1).

We can use the exponential martingale (
3.16
) to obtain the large deviations upper bound as follows.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))={\mathbb{E}}\left[\bm{1}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))\frac{e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}}{e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\bm{1}(\{\nu^{n}_{s}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{s}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}\right]\frac{e^{\oo(n^{2})}}{e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}}\\
&\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t}^{n},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}\right]\frac{e^{\oo(n^{2})}}{e^{n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})}}=e^{-n^{2}S^{n}(\{\nu_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})+\oo_{n}(1))}.\end{split}

(3.18)

The large deviations upper bound (
3.12
) follows from rearranging (
3.18
), and taking the infimum over
f\in{\cal C}^{2,1}_{b}
.

3.2
Large deviations Lower Bound

In the rest of this section, we prove the large deviations lower bound of Theorem
3.1
,
namely we show that for any continuous measure-valued process
\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))\geqslant-S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}).

(3.19)

The proof itself will be used to derive the large deviations for Dyson Bessel processes as it allows to control the positions of the extreme particules, see Proposition
3.5
, key to control the singularity at the origin of the Dyson Bessel process.

The proof consists of two steps, in the first step we approximate
\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
by a sequence of measure-valued process with benign properties.

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix a probability measure
\mu_{0}
with finite second moment.
Then, any measure-valued process
\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
with
S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})<\infty

can be approximated by a sequence of measure-valued processes
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
satisfying

•

\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}
has uniformly bounded density
\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon}
,
\supp(\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})
is a single interval for all times
t\in[0,1]
, and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}d(\nu_{t},\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t})=0.

•

The dynamical entropy satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}S_{\nu_{0}^{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})=S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}).

(3.20)

•

The density
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}(x)\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
of the measure-valued process
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
is smooth in both
x,t
, and the corresponding drift
\partial_{x}k^{\varepsilon}_{t}(x)
as defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}u^{\varepsilon}_{t})=0,\quad u_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2}H(\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon})(x)+\partial_{x}k_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x),

is also smooth in both
x,t
.

Above, smooth means differentiable and with continuous derivative (we shall not need more the proof yields eventually the existence of more derivatives).

Proposition 3.4
.

Fix a probability measure
\mu_{0}
satisfying Assumption
1
and
\delta>0
. Let
\tilde{\mu}_{0}
be a compactly supported probability measure such that
d(\mu_{0},\tilde{\mu}_{0})\leqslant\delta/3
.
Let
\{\tilde{\nu}_{t}(x)\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}

be a compactly supported measure-valued process with a smooth density
\tilde{\rho}
in both
x,t
such that
\tilde{\nu}_{0}=\tilde{\mu}_{0}
. Assume that the corresponding drift
\partial_{x}\tilde{k}_{t}
defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}_{t}(x)+\partial_{x}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\tilde{u}_{t})=0,\quad\tilde{u}_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}H(\tilde{\nu}_{t})(x)+\partial_{x}\tilde{k}_{t}(x),

(3.21)

is also smooth in both
x,t
. Then, the following large deviations lower bound holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\tilde{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))\geqslant-S_{\tilde{\mu}_{0}}(\{\tilde{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})+\oo_{\delta}(1).\end{split}

Proof of Large deviations lower bound (
3.19
).

We approximate
\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
by
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
as in Proposition
3.3
and take
\{\tilde{\nu}_{t}(x)\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
equal
\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
in Proposition
3.4
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.
Then it follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta))\geqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\{\nu^{n}_{t}\}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\nu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta/2))\\
&\geqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-S_{\nu_{0}^{\varepsilon}}(\{\nu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}))+\oo_{\delta}(1)\geqslant-S_{\mu_{0}}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}))+\oo_{\delta}(1),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used (

).
The large deviations lower bound follows by taking

In the following proposition, we show that for the Dyson Brownian motion with smooth drift, the locations of its particles are close to the quantiles of the limiting profile. Proposition

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
be a measure valued process with bounded support and with smooth density
\rho_{t}(x)
in both
x,t
, and such that the drift
\partial_{x}k_{t}(x)
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}(x)+\partial_{x}(\rho_{t}(\frac{1}{2}H(\nu_{t})(x)+\partial_{x}k_{t}(x)))=0,

is uniformly Lipschitz :
|\partial_{x}k_{t}(x)-\partial_{x}k_{t}(y)|\leqslant K|x-y|
for all real numbers
x,y
.
We denote by
\gamma_{i}(t)
the
ith

(1/n)
-quantiles of
\rho_{t}
given by

\displaystyle\frac{i-1/2}{n}=\int_{\infty}^{\gamma_{i}(t)}\rho_{t}(x){\rm d}x,\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n.

(3.32)

For
\beta\geqslant 1
, we consider the Dyson Brownian motion with drift
\partial_{x}k_{t}
which is the unique strong solution of

\displaystyle{\rm d}x_{i}(t)=\frac{{\rm d}W_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\beta n}}+\frac{1}{2n}\left(\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\partial_{x}k_{t}(x_{i}(t))\mathrm{d}t.

(3.33)

starting from
(x_{j}(0))_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
.
Let
M
be a positive real number. Then it holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}|x_{i}(t)-\gamma_{i}(t)|\leqslant e^{Kt}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}(|x_{j}(0)-\gamma_{j}(0)|)+\frac{M}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

with probability going to one as
M
goes to infinity.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that if
\nu
is a probability measure with density
\rho
which is
{\cal C}^{k}_{b}
and with compact support, then

is
{\cal C}^{k-1}_{b}
. Indeed, for
\rho

where we noticed that
P.V.\int_{|x-y|\leqslant A+M}\frac{1}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y=0

4
Large deviations for the Dyson Bessel process

In this section, we prove the large deviation principle, Theorem
1.2
, for the symmetrized empirical particle density of the Dyson Bessel process

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}s_{i}(t)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}W_{i}}{\sqrt{\beta n}}+\left(\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{1}{s_{i}(t)-s_{j}(t)}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{1}{s_{i}(t)+s_{j}(t)}+\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2s_{i}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t,\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\end{split}

(4.1)

We can symmetrize the Dyson Bessel process (
4.1
), by setting
s_{-i}(t)=-s_{i}(t),W_{-i}(t)=-W_{i}(t)
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, then for
i\in[\![{-n,n}]\!]\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}s_{i}(t)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}W_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\beta n}}+\left(\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j:j\neq\pm i}\frac{1}{s_{i}(t)-s_{j}(t)}+\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2s_{i}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t,\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{d}W_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\beta n}}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{{\rm d}t}{s_{i}(t)-s_{j}(t)}+\frac{\alpha_{n}-1/(2n)}{2s_{i}(t)}\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

(4.2)

where in the last line we added a term
(1/2n){\rm d}t/(s_{i}(t)-s_{j}(t))
with

, and replaced
\alpha_{n}
by
\alpha_{n}-(1/2n)
.

We denote the law of the Dyson Bessel process by
{\mathbb{P}}
, and the empirical particle density and its symmetrized version as

\displaystyle\nu^{n}_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{s_{i}(t)},\quad\hat{\nu}^{n}_{t}=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{s_{i}(t)}+\delta_{-s_{i}(t)}\right),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant 1.

(4.3)

More generally, for a probability measure
\nu
on the real line we set
\hat{\nu}
to be its symmetrized version
\hat{\nu}(f)=\int(f(x)+f(-x))/2\mathrm{d}\nu
. Reciprocally, if
\nu
is a probability on
(0,\infty)

If

\mathbb{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R})

We recall from (

\hat{\mu}_{0}
and a continuous symmetric measure-valued process

with
\hat{\nu}_{0}=\hat{\mu}_{0}
, we define the following dynamical free entropy:

S_{\hat{\mu}_{0}}^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})=\sup_{f\in{\cal C}^{2,1}_{b}}S^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}),

where

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})

\displaystyle=L^{\alpha}_{1}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t},f_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})-\frac{1}{8\beta}\int^{1}_{0}\int(f_{s}^{\prime}(x)-f_{s}^{\prime}(-x))^{2}{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{s}(x){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\hat{\nu}_{u}(f_{u})-\hat{\nu}_{0}(f_{0})-\int_{0}^{u}\int\partial_{s}f_{s}(x){\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{s}(x){\rm d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{u}\int\frac{f_{s}^{\prime}(x)-f_{s}^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{s}(x){\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{s}(y){\rm d}s

Therefore, the optimization problem (

with probability

.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}{\mathbb{Q}}(\{\tilde{\nu}^{n}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\{\tilde{\rho}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1},\delta/2),\tilde{s}_{n}(t)\geqslant{\mathfrak{a}},0\leqslant t\leqslant 1)\\

5.1
Asymptotics of rectangular spherical integral

As the first application of our large deviation principle for the Dyson Bessel process, we prove Theorem
1.1
the asymptotics of rectangular spherical integral,

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log I_{n,m}(A_{n},B_{n})=\frac{\beta}{2}I^{\alpha}(\hat{\nu}_{A},\hat{\mu}_{B}),\quad I_{n,m}(A_{n},B_{n})=\int e^{\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V,

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We recall from (
2.1
),
X_{n}
is an
n\times m
rectangular random matrix

\displaystyle X_{n}=A_{n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}G_{n}.

where
G_{n}
is an
n\times m
rectangular matrix with independent real (
\beta=1
) or complex (
\beta=2
) Gaussian entries.
We denote the singular value decomposition of
X_{n}
as
X_{n}=UB_{n}V^{*}
. Then the law of
X_{n}
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{Z_{n,m}}\prod_{i}b_{i}^{\beta(m-n+1)-1}\prod_{i<j}|b^{2}_{i}-b^{2}_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta n}{2}(\sum_{i}b^{2}_{i}+\sum a^{2}_{i})+\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A_{n}^{*}UB_{n}V^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V{\rm d}B_{n}.\end{split}

(5.1)

The large deviations principle of Dyson Bessel process gives

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\nu}^{n}_{B}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\hat{\nu}_{B},\delta))=\inf_{\hat{\nu}_{1}=\hat{\nu}_{B}}S_{\hat{\nu}_{A}}^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})+\oo_{\delta}(1),

where
\oo_{\delta}(1)
goes to zero as
\delta
goes to zero.
By integrating (
5.1
) over the ball
{\mathbb{B}}(\hat{\nu}_{B},\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\int_{\hat{\nu}^{n}_{B}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\hat{\nu}_{B},\delta)}\frac{1}{Z_{n,m}}\prod_{i}b_{i}^{\beta(m-n+1)-1}\prod_{i<j}|b^{2}_{i}-b^{2}_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta n}{2}(\sum_{i}b^{2}_{i}+\sum a^{2}_{i})+\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A^{*}UBV^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V{\rm d}B_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n,m}}e^{\frac{\beta n^{2}}{2}(2\alpha\int\log|x|{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{B}+2\Sigma(\hat{\nu}_{B})-(\hat{\nu}_{A}(x^{2})+\hat{\nu}_{B}(x^{2}))+\oo_{\delta}(1))}\int_{\hat{\nu}^{n}_{B}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\hat{\nu}_{B},\delta)}\int e^{\beta n\mathrm{Re}[\operatorname{Tr}(A^{*}UBV^{*})]}{\rm d}U{\rm d}V{\rm d}B_{n}

where we use similar techniques than in
[
6
]
to prove that even though the logarithm is singular,
\int\log|x|{\rm d}\hat{\nu}^{n}_{B}
is close to
\int\log|x|{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{B}
on the ball (and similarly for the non-commutative entropy term).
By rearranging, we obtain the following asymptotics of the spherical integral

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log I_{n,m}(A_{n},B_{n})=-\inf_{\nu_{1}=\hat{\nu}_{B}}S_{\mu_{A}}^{\alpha}(\{\nu_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})\\
&\qquad-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(2\alpha\int\log x{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{B}(x)+2\Sigma(\hat{\nu}_{B})-(\hat{\nu}_{A}(x^{2})+\hat{\nu}_{B}(x^{2}))\right)+\mbox{const}.\end{split}

(5.2)

Thanks to Proposition
4.1
, if
S_{\hat{\nu}_{A}}^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})<\infty
, then
\hat{\nu}_{t}
has a density, i.e.
\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}=\{\hat{\rho}_{t}(x){\rm d}x\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
is a symmetric measure valued process, satisfying the weak limits

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\hat{\rho}_{t}(x){\rm d}x=\hat{\nu}_{A},\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow 1}\hat{\rho}_{t}(x){\rm d}x=\hat{\nu}_{B}.

(5.3)

Let
u_{s}
be the weak solution of the following conservation of mass equation

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\hat{\rho}_{s}+\partial_{x}(\hat{\rho}_{s}u_{s})=0.

(5.4)

We recall the following formula for the dynamical entropy
S_{\hat{\nu}_{A}}^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})
from (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\mu_{0}}^{\alpha}(\{\hat{\nu}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1})&=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int u_{s}^{2}\hat{\rho}_{s}(x){\rm d}x{\rm d}s+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int\hat{\rho}^{3}_{s}(x){\rm d}x{\rm d}s+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\hat{\rho}_{s}(x)}{x^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}s\right.\\
&-\left.\left.\left(\Sigma(\hat{\nu}_{t})+\alpha\int\log|x|{\rm d}\hat{\nu}_{t}(x)\right)\right|_{t=0}^{1}\right).\end{split}

(5.5)

By plugging (
5.5
) into (
5.2
), we obtain the following theorem on the asymptotics of rectangular spherical integral,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&I^{\alpha}(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})=-\inf_{\{\hat{\rho}_{t}\}_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\atop\text{satisfies \eqref{e:bbterm}}}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\int u_{s}^{2}\hat{\rho}_{s}{\rm d}x{\rm d}s+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int\hat{\rho}^{3}_{s}{\rm d}x{\rm d}s+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\hat{\rho}_{s}(x)}{x^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}s\right\}\\
&+(\hat{\nu}_{A}(x^{2}-\alpha\log|x|)+\hat{\nu}_{B}(x^{2}-\alpha\log|x|))-(\Sigma(\hat{\nu}_{A})+\Sigma(\hat{\nu}_{B}))+\mbox{const}.\end{split}

(5.6)

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

In the remaining of this section, we give an informal characterization of the minimizer in (
5.6
), by the complex Burger’s equation.
We denote the minimizer of (

) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(u_{s}^{2}-\pi^{2}(\hat{\rho}^{*}_{s})^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4x^{2}})=0.

(5.7)

We define the function

\displaystyle f_{t}(x)=u_{t}(x)+{\rm i}\pi\hat{\rho}^{*}_{t}(x).

Then thanks to the relations (

) and (

),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{t}(x)+\partial_{x}f_{t}(x)f_{t}(x)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4x^{3}}.

(5.8)

The complex burger’s equation can be solved by characteristic flow formally. Let

\displaystyle\partial_{t}z_{t}=f_{t}(z_{t})=p_{t},

then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{t}(z_{t})=\partial_{t}p_{t}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z_{t}^{3}}

There are two quantities conserved:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(p_{t}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z_{t}^{2}}\right)=2\partial_{t}p_{t}p_{t}-\partial_{t}z_{t}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2z_{t}^{3}}=0,

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle f^{2}_{t}(z_{t})+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z_{t}^{2}}=f^{2}_{0}(z)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z^{2}},

\displaystyle z_{t}f_{t}(z_{t})=zf_{0}(z)+t\left(f^{2}_{0}(z)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z^{2}}\right).

Solving them we get

\displaystyle z_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{f^{2}_{0}(z)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z^{2}}}{\alpha^{2}/4+\left(zf_{0}(z)+t\left(f^{2}_{0}(z)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}}},\quad f_{t}(z_{t})=\frac{zf_{0}(z)+t\left(f^{2}_{0}(z)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4z^{2}}\right)}{z_{t}}.

\tau^{*}
. Let

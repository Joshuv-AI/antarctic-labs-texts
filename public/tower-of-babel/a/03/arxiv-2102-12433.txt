Automorphisms of tropical Hassett spaces
Sam Freedman, Joseph Hlavinka, Siddarth Kannan

Abstract
. Given an integer g ≥ 0 g\geq 0 and a weight vector w ∈ ℚ n ∩ ( 0 , 1 ] n w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n} satisfying 2 ​ g − 2 + ∑ w i > 0 2g-2+\sum w_{i}>0 , let Δ g , w \Delta_{g,w} denote the moduli space of n n -marked, w w -stable tropical curves of genus g g and volume one. We calculate the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( Δ g , w ) \operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w}) for g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 and arbitrary w w , and we calculate the group Aut ⁡ ( Δ 0 , w ) \operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{0,w}) when w w is heavy/light . In both of these cases, we show that Aut ⁡ ( Δ g , w ) ≅ Aut ⁡ ( K w ) \operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w}) , where K w K_{w} is the abstract simplicial complex on { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\} whose faces are subsets with w w -weight at most 1 1 . We show that these groups are precisely the finite direct products of symmetric groups. The space Δ g , w \Delta_{g,w} may also be identified with the dual complex of the divisor of singular curves in the algebraic Hassett space ℳ ¯ g , w \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w} . Following the work of Massarenti and Mella [ MassMella ] on the biregular automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ¯ g , w ) \operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}) , we show that Aut ⁡ ( Δ g , w ) \operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w}) is naturally identified with the subgroup of automorphisms which preserve the divisor of singular curves.



1.
Introduction

Fix integers
g,n\geq 0
such that
2g-2+n>0
, let
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
denote the moduli stack of smooth
n
-marked algebraic curves of genus
g
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
denote its Deligne-Mumford-Knudsen compactification by stable curves. Brendan Hassett
[
hassett
]
has given a large family of alternate modular compactifications of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
: given a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
satisfying

2g-2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}>0,

Hassett constructs a smooth and proper Deligne-Mumford moduli stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
, birational to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, which contains
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
as a dense open substack. The points of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
represent
n
-pointed nodal curves
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, satisfying (i) that the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
K_{C}+\sum w_{i}p_{i}
is ample along each component of
C
, where
K_{C}
is the canonical divisor of
C
, and (ii) if
p_{i_{1}}=\cdots=p_{i_{r}}
, then
w_{i_{1}}+\cdots+w_{i_{r}}\leq 1
. In particular, when
w=(1^{(n)})
is the all
1
’s vector, we have an equality
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

An important feature of the compactifaction
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is that the boundary divisor

\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

is normal crossings. In
[
CGP1
]
, Chan, Galatius, and Payne, following work of Harper
[
Harper
]
and Abramovich-Caporaso-Payne
[
ACP
]
, show how to construct the dual complex
\Delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
of a normal crossings divisor
\mathcal{D}
on a Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
. They study
\Delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
in the case where
\mathcal{X}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{D}=\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, showing that
\Delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})=\Delta_{g,n}
is identified with the link of the cone point in the moduli space
M_{g,n}^{\text{trop}}
of stable
n
-marked tropical curves of genus
g
.

On the other hand, the complement of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
in Hassett’s compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
is not in general normal crossings. However, if we put
\mathcal{M}_{g,w}
for the locus of smooth, but not necessarily distinctly marked, curves in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
, then the complement

\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g,w}

has normal crossings, and the resulting dual intersection complex
\Delta_{g,w}
is the link of the cone point in the moduli space
M_{g,w}^{\text{trop}}
of
n
-marked,
w
-stable tropical curves of genus
g
, as has been established by Ulirsch
[
Ulirsch
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the automorphism groups of the complexes
\Delta_{g,w}
, taken in the category of
symmetric
\Delta
-complexes
, as defined in
[
CGP1
]
and recalled in Section
2
. Given a weight vector
w
, we can form an abstract simplicial complex
K_{w}
with vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
by declaring that a subset
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
belongs to
K_{w}
if and only if
\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}\leq 1
; this construction was considered by Alexeev and Guy
[
alexeev
]
in their work on moduli of weighted stable maps. See Figure
1
for some examples of the complex
K_{w}
. Our first main theorem determines
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w})
in terms of
K_{w}
for
g\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g\geq 1
and suppose
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
for some
n
such that
2g-2+n\geq 3
. Then

\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w}),

where
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
acts by permuting the markings.

Here
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
is viewed as a subgroup of
S_{n}:=\mathrm{Perm}(\{1,\ldots,n\})
. Theorem
1.1
will be proven in Section
, and the failure of the
g=0
case will be further explored and partially remedied in Section
. Following Cavalieri, Hampe, Markwig, and Ranganathan
[
CHMR2014moduli
]
, we refer to weight vectors satisfying the hypotheses of the following theorem as
heavy/light
, with
m
light markings and
n
heavy markings.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
n,m\geq 2
, with
n+m\geq 5
, and put
w=(\varepsilon^{(m)},1^{(n)})
where
\varepsilon\leq 1/m
. Then we have

\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{0,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})\cong S_{m}\times S_{n}.

Heavy/light Hassett spaces are of particular interest: they are also studied in
[
Bergstrom
]
,
[
Chaudhuri
]
,
[
KKL
]
,
[
Modular
]
, and
[
MOP
]
.

It is also interesting to characterize the groups
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
, as in the following theorem. Since it is independent from the rest of the paper, its proof is found in Appendix
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a group. Then there exists
n\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
such that

\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})\cong G

if and only if
G
is isomorphic to the direct product of finitely many symmetric groups.

1.1.
Comparison with the algebraic moduli space

For
g,n\geq 0
such that
2g-2+n\geq 3
, we have isomorphisms

\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,n})\cong S_{n},

following the results of
[
K
]
and
[
Mass
]
; here
S_{n}
acts by relabelling the marked points. The analogous result cannot be true for general weight vectors. Indeed,
[
hassett
, Corollary 4.7]
states that if
w_{i}\leq w_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
and the complexes
K_{w},K_{w^{\prime}}
coincide outside of their
1
-skeletons, then there is an isomorphism of coarse moduli spaces
\overline{M}_{g,w}\cong\overline{M}_{g,w^{\prime}}
. Moreover, by
[
MassMella
, Theorem 3.20]
, the automorphism groups of the stacks and coarse spaces agree. This implies, for example, that when
w=(1^{(n)},1/2^{(m)})
, we have
\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})\cong S_{n+m}
. On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
states that
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})\cong S_{n}\times S_{m}
.

In
[
MassMella
]
, Massarenti and Mella prove that for
g,n\geq 1
such that
2g+2+n\geq 3
, the automorphism group of the moduli stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
is given by the subgroup of
S_{n}
generated by
admissible transpositions
. These are transpositions
(i,j)
such that, for all
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|S|\geq 2
, we have

w_{i}+w(S)\leq 1\iff w_{j}+w(S)\leq 1,

where for a subset
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
we define

w(S):=\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}.

The group generated by admissible transpositions acts on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
by relabelling the marked points, and contracting rational components which become unstable if necessary. We now show that
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})
which preserves the locus
\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
of singular curves.

Lemma 1.4
.

Suppose
g,n\geq 1
with
2g-2+n\geq 3
, and fix
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
. Then

\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w},\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w}),

where
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
acts by permuting the markings.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\sigma
is in the subgroup of
S_{n}
generated by admissible transpositions but
\sigma\notin\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
. Then there exists some
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|S|=2
,
w(S)>1
, but
w(\sigma(S))\leq 1
; say
S=\{i,j\}
. Consider a pointed nodal curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
of arithmetic genus
g
with two irreducible components
T_{1},T_{2}
, so that
T_{2}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and supports the marked points
p_{i},p_{j}
, while the other marked points are distributed distinctly on
T_{1}
. Then
\sigma\cdot(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is obtained from
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
by first permuting the marked points according to
\sigma
, and then contracting the component
T_{2}
to a point so that
p_{\sigma(i)}=p_{\sigma(j)}
(this is necessary because
w_{\sigma(i)}+w_{\sigma(j)}\leq 1
). In particular
\sigma\cdot(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is no longer a singular curve. This shows that
\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w},\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
; to finish, we simply note that when applying
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
to a nodal curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, there is never a need to contract any components, so
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
preserves the boundary.
∎

The simplicial complexes
K_{w}
correspond to the chambers of the
fine chamber decomposition
of
[
hassett
, Section 5]
(see also
[
alexeev
, Section 2]
).

In general, if
\mathcal{D}
is a normal crossings divisor on a variety or DM stack
\mathcal{X}
, one has a homomorphism

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})),

where
\Delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})
is the dual complex of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{X}
. Given Lemma
1.4
, the upshot of Theorem
1.1
is that this map is an isomorphism if we specialize to
\mathcal{D}=\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
and
\mathcal{X}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w}
:

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
g,n\geq 1
with
2g-2+n\geq 3
, and
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
. Then the map

\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w},\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,w})

is an isomorphism.

We can also give a sufficient condition for the groups
\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
to coincide. Recall that a
facet
of a simplicial complex is a face that is maximal with respect to inclusion.

Corollary 1.6
.

Suppose
g,n\geq 1
with
2g-2+n\geq 3
, and
w\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap(0,1]^{n}
. Then, if
K_{w}
has no
1
-dimensional facets, then

\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})=\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that every admissible transposition
\tau=(i,j)
is in
\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
. Suppose
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
satisfies
w(S)\leq 1
, in order to show that
w(\tau(S))\leq 1
. If both
i,j\in S
or
i,j\in S^{c}
, then
w(\tau(S))=w(S)
, so we suppose without loss of generality that
i\in S
while
j\in S^{c}
. Then, if
|S|\geq 3
, by the definition of admissible transposition, we must have
w(\tau(S))\leq 1
. If
|S|=2
, then
S
forms a
1
-simplex of
K_{w}
, and cannot be a facet. Thus there exists some
T\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
S\subsetneq T
and
w(T)\leq 1
. Since
|T|\geq 3
, we have
w(\tau(T))\leq 1
, and
w(\tau(S))<w(\tau(T))
, so
w(\tau(S))\leq 1
and
\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})
, finishing the proof.
∎

The condition of Corollary
1.6
is sufficient but not necessary: indeed, if
w=(1/2^{(n)})
, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,w})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(K_{w})\cong S_{n}
, but
K_{w}
is the complete graph on
n
vertices, so all of its facets are
1
-dimensional.

1.2.
Tropical Hassett spaces excluded by Theorem
1.1

When
g\geq 1
, the space
\Delta_{g,w}
is nonempty as long as
3g-3+n>0
, so the positive genus cases not covered by Theorem
1.1
are
(g,n)=(1,1),(1,2)
. When
n=1
we have
\Delta_{1,w}=\Delta_{1,1}
for any
w
, and this space is a single point, so the automorphism group is trivial. When
n=2
, so
w=(w_{1},w_{2})
, we have
\Delta_{1,w}\cong\Delta_{1,2}
if
w_{1}+w_{2}>1
, so in this case the automorphism group is trivial by
[
K
, Example 2.19]
. When
w_{1}+w_{2}\leq 1
,
\Delta_{1,w}
will be shown to be trivial in Example
.

1.3.
Related work

In the special case
w=(1^{(n)})
, the automorphism group of
\Delta_{g,w}
is known to be
S_{n}
: this is due to Abreu and Pacini
[
AbreuPacini
]
when
g=0
, and to the third author
[
K
]
in arbitrary genus. Indeed, one of the main technical theorems in
[
K
]
is also the driving force behind the calculation in the current paper.

The topology of
\Delta_{g,w}
was studied for
g\leq 1
by Cerbu
et al.
in
[
CMPRS
]
. When
g=0
and the weight vector
w
has at least two entries equal to
1
, the space
\Delta_{0,w}
has the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres, possibly of varying dimension. Closed formulas for the number of spheres are known when
w
is heavy/light. In higher genus, the topology of
\Delta_{g,w}
has been partially explored by Li, Serpente, Yun, and the third author in
[
kannan2020topology
]
. When
g\geq 1
, and for any value of
w
, the space
\Delta_{g,w}
is shown to be simply-connected. Formulas for the Euler characteristic of
\Delta_{g,w}
in terms of the combinatorics of the complex
K_{w}
have also been derived.

The cone complexes
M_{0,w}^{\text{trop}}
were studied in the context of tropical compactification in
[
CHMR2014moduli
]
. The authors showed that the complex
M_{0,w}^{\text{trop}}
can be embedded as a balanced fan
\Sigma_{0,w}
in a real vector space if and only if
w
is heavy/light. In the heavy/light case, they show that the locus
\mathcal{M}_{0,w}
embeds into the toric variety
X(\Sigma_{0,w})
, in such a way that taking the closure of the image gives Hassett’s original compactification. This procedure gives an isomorphism of Chow rings
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w})\cong A^{*}(X(\Sigma_{0,w}))
, allowing for the computation of
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,w})
carried out in
[
KKL
]
.

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we consider the following fractional Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+a(x)|u|^{p-2}u,

(1.1)

\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}<\frac{(2^{1-\frac{4s}{N(p-2)}}-1)Np(p-2)}{(N(p-2)-4s)\|w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}}m_{c},

The fractional Laplacian operator

When we are looking for solutions of problem (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy,

where

is a Hilbert space with the inner product and norm respectively

Another interesting way is to search for solutions with prescribed mass, that is, (
1.2

appears as a lagrange multiplier. This type of solution is called normalized solution, and can be obtained by looking for critical points of the functional

S_{c}:=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|u\|_{2}=c\}.

From the physical point of view, the normalized solution is particularly meaningful, since in addition to there is a conservation of mass, the mass has often an important physical meaning.

Preliminaries

By
[

(-\Delta)^{s}u=-u+u^{p-1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u>0,

where

is determined by

-\lambda_{c}=\left(\frac{c}{c_{0}}\right)^{-\theta-2}>0,\quad c_{0}:=\|w\|_{2},\quad\theta:=\frac{4N-2p(N-2s)}{N(p-2)-4s}>0,

The solution

\displaystyle m_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{c}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=(-\lambda_{c})^{\frac{p}{p-2}-\frac{N}{2s}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{p}dx\Big)

\displaystyle=(-\lambda_{c})^{\frac{p}{p-2}-\frac{N}{2s}}m_{c_{0}}

3
The minimax approach

For
h\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we introduce the scaling

h\star u(x):=e^{\frac{Nh}{2}}u(e^{h}x),

which preserves the
L^{2}
-norm:
\|h\star u\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}
for all
h\in\mathbb{R}
. For
R>0
and
h_{1}<0<h_{2}
, which will be determined later, we set

Q:=B_{R}(0)\times[h_{1},h_{2}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}.

For
c>0
we define

\Gamma_{c}:=\left\{\gamma\in C(Q,S_{c}):\gamma(y,h)=h\star w_{c}(\cdot-y)\text{ for all }(y,h)\in\partial Q\right\}.

We want to find a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) whose energy is given by

m_{a,c}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{c}}\max_{(y,h)\in Q}F(\gamma(y,h)).

(3.1)

In order to develop a min-max argument, we need to prove that

\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{c}}\max_{(y,h)\in\partial Q}F(\gamma(y,h))<m_{a,c}

at least for some suitable choice of
Q
. For this purpose, we will prove Lemma
3.3
, which gives a lower bound of
m_{a,c}
, and Lemma
3.4
, which gives an upper bound of
F\circ\gamma
on the boundary
\partial Q
, for any given
\gamma\in\Gamma_{c}
. The values of
R>0
and
h_{1}<0<h_{2}
will be determined in Lemma
3.4
.

Next, for
h_{1}<0<h_{2}
determined in Lemma
3.4
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=\{D\subset S_{c}:D\text{ is compact and connected, }h_{1}\star w_{c},\ \ h_{2}\star w_{c}\in D\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}:=\{D\in\mathcal{D}:\beta(u)=0\text{ for all }u\in D\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{r}:=\mathcal{D}\cap H_{\mathrm{rad}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and

\displaystyle l_{c}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\max_{u\in D}F_{\infty}(u),

\displaystyle l_{c}^{0}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{0}}\max_{u\in D}F_{\infty}(u),

\displaystyle l_{c}^{r}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{r}}\max_{u\in D}F_{\infty}(u).

By a similar argument as
[
28
]
, we obtain

m_{c}=\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{c}}\max_{t\in(0,1)}F_{\infty}(\sigma(t)),

where

\Sigma_{c}=\{\sigma\in C([0,1],S_{c}):\sigma(0)=h_{1}\star w_{c},\ \ \sigma(1)=h_{2}\star w_{c}\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

l_{c}^{r}=l_{c}^{0}=l_{c}=m_{c}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{r}\subset\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}
, we have
l_{c}^{r}\geq l_{c}^{0}\geq l_{c}
. In order to prove that
l_{c}\geq m_{c}
and
m_{c}\geq l_{c}^{r}
, arguing by contradiction we assume that
l_{c}<m_{c}
. Then
\max\limits_{u\in D}F_{\infty}(u)<m_{c}
for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
, hence
\max\limits_{u\in U_{\delta}(D)}F_{\infty}(u)<m_{c}
for some
\delta>0
, where
U_{\delta}(D)
is a
\delta
-neighborhood of
D
. Observe that
U_{\delta}(D)
is open and connected, so it is path-connected. Therefore there exists a path
\sigma\in\Sigma_{c}
such that
\max\limits_{t\in(0,1)}F_{\infty}(\sigma(t))<m_{c}
, which is a contradiction.

On the other hand, let
\tilde{D}:=\{h\star w_{c}:h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]\}\in\mathcal{D}_{r}
, then

\max_{u\in\tilde{D}}F_{\infty}(u)=\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}F_{\infty}(h\star w_{c})=F_{\infty}(w_{c})=m_{c}.

Hence,
m_{c}\geq l_{c}^{r}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

L_{c}:=\inf\limits_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{0}}}\max\limits_{u\in D}F(u)>m_{c}

\displaystyle\|F_{\infty}^{\prime}(v_{n})-\mu_{n}H^{\prime}(v_{n})\|_{(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{ where }H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle=2m_{a,c}+\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x)|v_{n}|^{p}dx+o(1)

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq 2m_{a,c}+\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{p}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2+\frac{8s}{(Np-4s)a_{\ast}-2N}\Big)m_{a,c}+o(1),

which implies that
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x\cdot\nabla a(x)|v_{n}|^{p}dx\leq\|W\|_{\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{p}^{p}.

(3.23)

In order to see (
3.17
), we set

a_{n}:=\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}v_{n}\|_{2}^{2},\quad b_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x)|v_{n}|^{p}dx,\quad d_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x\cdot\nabla a(x)|v_{n}|^{p}dx.

Then from (
3.21
), (
3.22
), (
3.23
) and the definition of
\lambda_{n}
, up to a subsequence, we can assume that

a_{n}\rightarrow a\geq 0,\quad b_{n}\rightarrow b\geq 0,\quad d_{n}\rightarrow d\geq 0,\quad\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Passing to the limit in (
3.15
), (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), then

a=\frac{2}{p}b+2m_{a,c},

(3.24)

sa+(\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{2})b+\frac{1}{p}d=0,

(3.25)

a=\lambda c^{2}+b.

(3.26)

Now, we claim that
\lambda<0
. In fact, from the above three equalities, we have

\displaystyle-\lambda c^{2}

\displaystyle=b-a

\displaystyle=\frac{p-2}{p}b-2m_{a,c}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-2}{p}\cdot\frac{2ps}{N(p-2)-4s}(2m_{a,c}+\frac{1}{ps}d)-2m_{a,c}

\displaystyle=\frac{p(2s-N)+2N}{N(p-2)-4s}2m_{a,c}+\frac{2(p-2)}{p(N(p-2)-4s)}d

\displaystyle>\frac{p(2s-N)+2N}{N(p-2)-4s}2m_{c}+\frac{2(p-2)}{p(N(p-2)-4s)}d,

where we have used Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, from
2+\frac{4s}{N}<p<2_{s}^{*}
, we have
\lambda<0
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle-\lambda c^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p(2s-N)+2N}{N(p-2)-4s}2m_{a,c}+\frac{2(p-2)}{p(N(p-2)-4s)}d

\displaystyle<\frac{p(2s-N)+2N}{N(p-2)-4s}4m_{c}+\frac{(p-2)\|W\|_{\infty}}{N(p-2)-4s}\cdot\frac{16s}{(Np-4s)a_{\ast}-2N}m_{c}=\delta_{0}.

Thus, we have (
3.17
) holds. Moreover, recalling the definition of
\theta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{0}}{m_{c}}=

\displaystyle\frac{4p(2s-N)+8N}{N(p-2)-4s}+\frac{16s(p-2)\|W\|_{\infty}}{(N(p-2)-4s)((Np-4s)a_{\ast}-2N)}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{12N-6p(N-2s)}{N(p-2)-4s}=3\theta.

∎

The Proofs of Theorems

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Since
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, after passing to a subsequence it converges weakly in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By Lemma
3.9
,
v\geq 0
, and by weak convergence,
v
is a weak solution of

(-\Delta)^{s}v=\lambda v+a(x)|v|^{p-2}v,

such that

\|v\|_{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow 0}\|v_{n}\|_{2}=c.

It remains to prove that
\|v\|_{2}=c
.
Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}v_{n}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\varphi dx-\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}\varphi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x)|v_{n}|^{p-2}v_{n}\varphi dx=o(1)\|\varphi\|

for every
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
\{v_{n}\}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
F_{\lambda}
at level
m_{a,c}-\frac{\lambda}{2}c^{2}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

v_{n}=v+\sum_{j=1}^{k}u^{j}(\cdot-y_{n}^{j})+o(1),

with
u^{j}
being solutions to

(-\Delta)^{s}u^{j}=\lambda u^{j}+|u^{j}|^{p-2}u^{j}

and
|y_{n}^{j}|\rightarrow\infty
. We note that, if
k=0
, then
v_{n}\rightarrow v
strongly in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, hence
\|v\|_{2}=c
and we are done, thus we can assume that
k\geq 1
, or equivalently
\rho:=\|v\|_{2}<c
.

First, we exclude the case
v=0
. In fact, if
v=0
and
k=1
, we would have
u^{1}>0
and
\|u^{1}\|_{2}=c
, so that (
2.11
) gives
m_{a,c}=m_{c}
, which is not possible due to Lemma
3.3
. On the other hand, if
k\geq 2
, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

F_{\lambda}(v_{n})\to\sum_{j=1}^{k}F_{\infty,\lambda}(u^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}F_{\infty}(u^{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u^{j}\|^{2}_{2},\quad c^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|u^{j}\|_{2}^{2}.

Let
\alpha^{2}_{j}=\|u^{j}\|_{2}^{2}
, since
F_{\infty}(u^{j})\geq m_{\alpha_{j}}
and

m_{\alpha}>m_{\beta},\quad\text{if $\alpha<\beta$},

(4.1)

we have
2m_{c}\leq m_{a,c}
, which contradicts Lemma
3.5
.

Therefore, from now on we assume that
v\neq 0
. From
F(v_{n})\rightarrow m_{a,c}
, we deduce that

m_{a,c}-\frac{\lambda}{2}c^{2}=F(v)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\rho^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}F_{\infty}(u^{j})-\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{2}

Using
F_{\infty}(u^{j})\geq m_{\alpha_{j}}
, (
4.1
) and

c^{2}=\rho^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{2},

we have

2m_{c}>m_{a,c}\geq F(v)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{\alpha_{j}}\geq F(v)+m_{\alpha}\geq F(v)+m_{c},

where
\alpha=\max\limits_{j}\alpha_{j}
. Moreover, using the equation for
v
, it is easy to check that

F_{\lambda}(v)=\max_{t>0}F_{\lambda}(tv).

Let
\beta>0
be such that

\lambda_{\beta}=\lambda,

according to (
2.3
). By Appendix in
[
31
]
,
w_{\beta}
satisfies the limit equation with multiplier
\lambda
and
\beta\leq\alpha
. Using Lemma
3.5
and the above arguments, we have

\displaystyle 2m_{c}

\displaystyle>m_{a,c}\geq m_{\alpha}+F(v)=m_{\alpha}+F_{\lambda}(v)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\alpha}+\max_{t>0}F_{\lambda}(tv)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\geq m_{\alpha}+\max_{t>0}F_{\infty,\lambda}(tv)+\frac{\lambda}{2}(c^{2}-\alpha^{2})

\displaystyle\geq m_{\alpha}+F_{\infty,\lambda}(w_{\beta})+\frac{\lambda}{2}(c^{2}-\alpha^{2})

\displaystyle\geq m_{\alpha}+m_{\beta}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(c^{2}-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}).

Since
m_{\beta}\geq m_{\alpha}>m_{c}
and
\lambda<0
, we deduce that

c^{2}-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}>0.

Using Lemma
3.9
and (
2.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2m_{c}

\displaystyle>\frac{c^{\theta}}{\alpha^{\theta}}m_{c}+\frac{c^{\theta}}{\beta^{\theta}}m_{c}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(c^{2}-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})

\displaystyle>\frac{c^{\theta}}{\alpha^{\theta}}m_{c}+\frac{c^{\theta}}{\beta^{\theta}}m_{c}-\frac{\lambda_{0}m_{c}}{2c^{2}}(c^{2}-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}),

where
\lambda_{0}:=\frac{\delta_{0}}{m_{c}}.
Hence, one gets

2+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}>\frac{c^{\theta}}{\alpha^{\theta}}+\frac{c^{\theta}}{\beta^{\theta}}+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{c^{2}}).

(4.2)

Note that the following inequality holds for
A>\theta
(see
[
4
]
)

\min\Big\{x^{-\theta/2}+y^{-\theta/2}+A(x+y):x,y>0,x+y\leq 1\Big\}\geq\frac{3}{2}\theta+2,

and by using
(A_{3})
and (
3.27
), we have

\lambda_{0}=\frac{\delta_{0}}{m_{c}}>\frac{4(p(2s-N)+2N)}{N(p-2)-4s}=2\theta,

so (
4.2
) implies that

2+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}>\frac{3\theta}{2}+2,

which is a contradiction to (
3.27
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

The strategy is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We observe that the results obtained in Section 3 excepting Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
still hold true without any changes in the proof.

First, we claim that if
(A_{6})
holds, then
m_{a,c}<2m_{c}
.

From the definition of
m_{c}
, we have

m_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|w_{c}\|_{p}^{p}

and

s\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x)|w_{c}|^{p}dx=0,

which implies that

\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{2N(p-2)}{N(p-2)-4s}m_{c},\quad\|w_{c}\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{4sp}{N(p-2)-4s}.

(4.3)

Note that

\displaystyle m_{a,c}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{h\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big\{F_{\infty}(h\star w_{c})+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1-a(x))|(h\star w_{c})(x-y)|^{p}dx\Big\}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{h\in\mathbb{R}}\Big\{F_{\infty}(h\star w_{c})+\frac{e^{2sh}}{p}\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}\|w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max_{h\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(e^{2sh}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}\|w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}\Big)-\frac{e^{\frac{p-2}{2}Nh}}{p}\|w_{c}\|_{p}^{p}\Big).

Differentiating with respect to
h
, then there exists
h_{0}
such that

2se^{2sh_{0}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}\|w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}\Big)-\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}e^{\frac{p-2}{2}Nh_{0}}\|w_{c}\|_{p}^{p}=0.

Hence, we obtain that

e^{\frac{p-2}{2}Nh_{0}}=\left\{\frac{N(p-2)-4s}{N(p-2)m_{c}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{c}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}\|w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}\Big)\right\}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{N(p-2)-4s}}.

(4.5)

Combining
(A_{6})
with (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

e^{\frac{p-2}{2}Nh_{0}}<2.

From (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have

m_{a,c}\leq e^{\frac{p-2}{2}Nh_{0}}m_{c}.

This ends the proof.

Second, if
(A_{6})
holds, then Lemma
3.4
still holds true.

Same as the proof of (
3.5
), we can fix
h_{1}<0<h_{2}
such that

\sup_{(y,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{h_{1},h_{2}\}}F(h\star w_{c}(\cdot-y))\leq m_{c}.

For all

, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{|y|\to+\infty}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}F(h\star w_{c}(x-y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{|y|\to+\infty}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}\Big(F_{\infty}(h\star w_{c})+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1-a(x))|(h\star w_{c})(x-y)|^{p}dx\Big).

Since

and

Therefore,

\displaystyle\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1-a(x))|(h\star w_{c})(x-y)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}(1-a(x))|(h\star w_{c})(x-y)|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0)}(1-a(x))|(h\star w_{c})(x-y)|^{p}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|1-a(x)\|_{t_{1}}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}e^{2sh}\|w_{c}\|_{L^{t_{2}p}(B_{R}(y))}^{p}+\|1-a(x)\|_{L^{t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0))}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}\|h\star w_{c}\|_{t_{2}p}^{p}\rightarrow 0.

Using

\limsup_{|y|\to+\infty}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}F(h\star w_{c}(x-y))\leq m_{c}.

\limsup_{|y|\to+\infty}\max_{h\in[h_{1},h_{2}]}F(h\star w_{c}(x-y))=m_{c}.

(4.7)

This together with (
4.6

Local connectedness of boundaries for relatively hyperbolic groups
Ashani Dasgupta, G. Christopher Hruska

Abstract
. Let ( Γ , ℙ ) (\Gamma,\mathbb{P}) be a relatively hyperbolic group pair that is relatively one ended. Then the Bowditch boundary of ( Γ , ℙ ) (\Gamma,\mathbb{P}) is locally connected. Bowditch previously established this conclusion under the additional assumption that all peripheral subgroups are finitely presented, either one or two ended, and contain no infinite torsion subgroups. We remove these restrictions; we make no restriction on the cardinality of Γ \Gamma and no restriction on the peripheral subgroups P ∈ ℙ P\in\mathbb{P} .

1.
Introduction

One of the earliest major theorems proved about hyperbolic groups is the result that the Gromov boundary of a one-ended hyperbolic group is locally connected and without cut points. The proof combines work of Bestvina–Mess, Levitt, Bowditch, and Swarup
[
BM91
,
Lev98
,
Bow99d
,
Swa96
]
.
Notable applications of this result include semistability (due to
Geoghegan

[
BM91
,
GS19
]
), the topological invariance of the JSJ decomposition
[
Bow98
]
, the one-dimensional boundary classification theorem of
[
KK00
]
, and the existence of quasi-hyperbolic planes in hyperbolic groups
[
BK05
]
.

Subsequently Bowditch studied the local connectedness of boundaries of relatively hyperbolic groups.
Bowditch introduces a natural boundary of a relatively hyperbolic pair, the Bowditch boundary, and establishes many of its fundamental properties in
[
Bow12
]
.
In a series of articles
[
Bow99a
,
Bow99b
,
Bow01
]
, he proves that the Bowditch boundary of a relatively hyperbolic group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is locally connected, if certain restrictions are imposed on the subgroups known as peripheral subgroups. These restrictions require peripheral subgroups to be finitely presented, either one or two ended, and to contain no infinite torsion subgroups. In this paper we extend Bowditch’s work and remove all restrictions, proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group pair with connected boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
.
Then
M
is locally connected and every cut point of
M
is the fixed point of a parabolic subgroup.

The boundary
M
has a cut point with stabilizer
P\in\mathbb{P}
if and only if
\Gamma
splits relative to
\mathbb{P}
over a subgroup of
P
.

A splitting as a graph of groups is said to be
relative to
\mathbb{P}
if every member of
\mathbb{P}
is contained in a vertex group.

We note that this theorem is not restricted to finitely generated groups and imposes no restrictions on the peripheral subgroups.
Osin extends the notion of relative hyperbolicity to groups that need not be finitely generated in
[
Osi06b
]
.
The construction and basic properties of the Bowditch boundary have been extended to this more general setting by Gerasimov and Potyagailo
[
Ger12
,
GP15
,
GP16
]
.
The boundary of a relatively hyperbolic group pair is always compact, and when
\Gamma
is countable it is metrizable.
Thus, in the countable case, a connected Bowditch boundary is a Peano continuum.

We note that, throughout this paper, we consider only infinite peripheral subgroups. (If any peripheral subgroup is finite, the boundary contains a dense set of isolated points and, hence, is not connected and also not locally connected.)
Under this convention, the boundary of
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is connected if and only if
\Gamma
does not split over a finite subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
.

In the case that the boundary of
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is connected and contains cut points, the following result gives that the cut point tree in the sense of
[
PS06
]
is simplicial and provides a topological characterization of the boundary as the inverse limit of a tree of compacta in the sense of Świątkowski
[
Świ20
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Cut point tree)
.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic with connected boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
.
Consider the bipartite graph
T
with vertex set
V_{0}\sqcup V_{1}
, where
V_{0}
is the family of nontrivial maximal subcontinua of
M
that are not separated by any cut point of
M
and
V_{1}
is the set of cut points of
M
. Vertices
v\in V_{1}
and
B\in V_{0}
are joined by an edge if
v\in B
. Then we have the following.

(1)

The graph
T
is a simplicial tree equal to the canonical JSJ tree of cylinders for splittings over parabolic subgroups relative to
\mathbb{P}
.

(2)

Each group
\Stab(B)
with
B\in V_{0}
is finitely generated and relatively hyperbolic with boundary
B
, a Peano continuum with no cut points.

(3)

The boundary
M
is equal to the inverse limit of a tree of compacta, obtained by gluing the boundaries
B
of the subgroups
\Stab(B)
along points in the pattern of
T
.

This characterization simplifies related treelike decompositions of boundaries described in the finitely generated case in
[
Bow01
,
Dah03
]
and extends this treelike structure to groups that need not be finitely generated.
The canonical tree of cylinders is called the “maximal peripheral splitting” in
[
Bow01
]
.
See Proposition
2.17
for a description of this canonical tree.

Haulmark–Hruska have used Theorem
1.1
in their proof that the JSJ decomposition of a one-ended relatively hyperbolic pair over elementary subgroups depends only on the topology of the boundary
[
HH23
]
.
Hruska–Walsh have used Theorem
1.1
in their examination of relatively hyperbolic pairs with planar boundary
[
HW23
]
.
Regarding semistability at infinity, Theorem
1.1
, together with work of Mihalik–Swenson and Haulmark–Mihalik, gives the following immediate result (see
[
MS21
,
HM22
]
for more details).

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is nonelementary relatively hyperbolic and
\Gamma
is finitely generated and one ended.

(1)

If each
P\in\mathbb{P}
is one ended and
\Gamma
does not split over any subgroup
Q\leq P
with
P\in\mathbb{P}
, then
\Gamma
is semistable at infinity.

(2)

If each
P\in\mathbb{P}
is finitely presented and semistable at infinity, then
\Gamma
is semistable at infinity.

We also note that the main results of Haulmark
[
Hau19
]
apply to any relatively hyperbolic pair with locally connected boundary. (They are stated there with more restrictive hypotheses, but only local connectedness is used in the proofs.)
Thus we have the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic.
Suppose
\Gamma
does not split relative to
\mathbb{P}
over an elementary subgroup and each
P\in\mathbb{P}
is one ended.
If the boundary
\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is one dimensional, then it is homeomorphic to a circle, a Sierpiński carpet, or a Menger curve.

We remark that de Souza has independently obtained a variation of Bowditch’s local connectivity theorem in
[
Sou
]
using a method that is only valid when the relatively hyperbolic group
\Gamma
is finitely presented and one ended. Theorem
1.1
includes and extends the result of de Souza.

1.1.
Methods of proof

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
roughly follow the same outline as the prior proofs of Swarup and Bowditch. However, several steps require new ideas to extend their conclusions from restricted settings to the broader setting of this paper.

In
[
Bow12
]
, Bowditch constructs the boundary of a relatively hyperbolic pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
and proves that it is compact and Hausdorff, provided that
\Gamma
is countable.
In Section
3
, we show that the same elementary construction extends to the general case with no cardinality restrictions on
\Gamma
. The resulting Bowditch boundary has a simple, concrete description and coincides with the canonical boundary introduced by Gerasimov in
[
Ger12
]
as the abstract Cauchy–Samuel completion of a certain uniform space.
A reader who is only interested in countable relatively hyperbolic groups may safely skip this section, whose only purpose is to extend the reach of this paper beyond countable groups.

In Section
4
, we show that a splitting over parabolic subgroups gives rise to a treelike decomposition of the boundary. This observation is due to Bowditch
[
Bow01
]
in the finitely generated case. Dahmani uses an elaborate hands-on construction of a tree of compacta to prove a combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic group pairs in
[
Dah03
]
.
We simplify this construction by characterizing the treelike structure as the inverse limit of a tree system of compacta in the sense of Świątkowski
[
Świ20
]
.
The inverse limit construction can be used in place of the elaborate construction of
[
Dah03
]
and may lead to a deeper understanding of combination theorems.

This simplification allows us to eliminate all restrictions on the cardinality of
\Gamma
and leads to characterizations of connectedness and local connectedness of boundaries in Section
5
.
Using an accessibility theorem for parabolic splittings (see Proposition
2.17
), we reduce from the general case to the more well-behaved setting of finitely generated relatively hyperbolic groups.
After this point, all groups considered are finitely generated.

Section
6
discusses pretrees and establishes results about non-nesting actions and full quotients of pretrees, which are used in the following two sections.
Section
7
contains a brief summary of the cut point pretree construction of Swenson
[
Swe00
]
and some of its key dynamical properties.
We use this pretree in place of Bowditch’s more technically elaborate “pretree completion” of
[
Bow99d
]
to simplify the analysis in the following section.

Section
8
is a study of a construction due to Bowditch that associates a dual dendrite
D
to each continuum
M
with a preferred family of cut points
C
.
Proposition
8.5
characterizes this dendrite as the maximal Hausdorff quotient of
M
in which any two points are identified if they are not separated by a point of
C
.
Bowditch
[
Bow99d
]
establishes that this dendrite is nontrivial if
M
admits a convergence action by a one-ended group
G
containing no infinite torsion group and
M
has a nonparabolic cut point.
The main goal of Section
8
is to extend this result from one-ended groups to relatively one-ended groups.
This extension is one of the main innovations of the present proof; it makes essential use of the non-nesting property and the results about pretrees established in Sections
6
and
7
.

In Section
9
we verify that a theorem of Levitt
[
Lev98
]
about non-nesting actions on trees extends from finitely presented groups to groups with a finite relative presentation.
Section
10
contains an accessibility theorem for relatively hyperbolic groups and splittings over two-ended loxodromic subgroups. It also contains the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, which combine the ingredients discussed above.

2.
Relatively hyperbolic groups and their splittings

2.1.
Relative hyperbolicity

This section discusses relatively hyperbolic groups and their boundaries.
For more details, see
[
Bow12
,
GP15
,
GP16
]
. Alternative approaches may be found in
[
DS05
,
Osi06b
,
GM08
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
graph

K
is a set
V=V(K)
of
vertices
, a set
E=E(K)
of
oriented edges
, an
initial vertex

\iota(\vec{e}\,)
,
terminal vertex

\tau(\vec{e}\,)
, and
inverse edge

-\vec{e}
for each oriented edge
\vec{e}\in E(K)
satisfying
-\vec{e}\neq\vec{e}
,
-(-\vec{e}\,)=\vec{e}
, and
\iota(\vec{e}\,)=\tau(-\vec{e}\,)
.
An (unoriented)
edge

e
is a pair of oriented edges
\{\vec{e},-\vec{e}\,\}
.

In a graph
K
, a
path
is indicated by giving its sequence of oriented edges or its sequence of vertices (if the graph is simplicial).
A path is
simple
if its vertices are distinct.
A
cycle
is a path from a vertex to itself, and a
circuit
is a cycle with all vertices distinct except for the initial and terminal vertices, which are equal.
A
simplicial tree
is a connected graph with no circuits.

We treat graphs as combinatorial objects, as above, or as topological or metric spaces depending on the context.

Definition 2.2
.

A geodesic space
X
is
\delta
–hyperbolic
if each side of a geodesic triangle lies in the
\delta
–neighborhood of the union of the other two sides.

Definition 2.3
.

A
group pair

(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
consists of a group
\Gamma
together with a finite collection
\mathbb{P}
of subgroups of
\Gamma
.
Given a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
, an action of
\Gamma
on
X
is
relative to
\mathbb{P}
if each member of
\mathbb{P}
has a fixed point in
X
.

Definition 2.4

(Relatively hyperbolic)
.

A connected simplicial graph
K
is
fine
if for each
n
, each edge in
K
is contained in only finitely many circuits of length
n
.
An action of a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
on a graph
K
is
relatively hyperbolic
if the action is relative to
\mathbb{P}
and satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The graph
K
is fine and
\delta
–hyperbolic for some
\delta<\infty
.

(2)

There are finitely many
\Gamma
–orbits
of edges and each edge stabiliser is finite.

(3)

The elements of
\mathbb{P}
are representatives of the conjugacy classes of infinite vertex stabilisers of
K
.

A group pair is
relatively hyperbolic
if the pair admits a relatively hyperbolic action on a graph
K
.
The special case in which
\mathbb{P}
is the empty collection reduces to the notion of a word hyperbolic group.
As our main results are already known in this case, we assume throughout this article that
\mathbb{P}
is nonempty. In this case, we may assume, in addition, that every vertex of
K
has an infinite stabilizer (see
[
Bow12
]
).

Definition 2.5

(Convergence groups)
.

For any compact Hausdorff space
M
, let
\Delta\subset M^{n}
denote the subset of points with at least two coordinates equal.
The
space of distinct
n
–tuples

\Theta^{n}(M)
is the quotient of
M^{n}-\Delta
by the symmetric group
S_{n}
, which acts by permuting the coordinates.
An action of a discrete group on
M
is a
convergence group action
if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

M
is empty, and
G
is a finite group.

(2)

M
contains only one point, and
G
is arbitrary.

(3)

M
contains exactly two points, and
G
is virtually infinite cyclic.

(4)

M
contains at least three points and the action on
\Theta^{3}(M)
is proper.

A subgroup
H
of a convergence group is
elementary
if its limit set
\Lambda H
contains at most two points.
If the limit set of
H
has exactly one point, we say that
H
is
parabolic
, and if it has two points we say that
H
is
loxodromic
. Every loxodromic subgroup is virtually infinite cyclic.
We refer the reader to
[
Tuk94
,
Tuk96
,
Bow99c
]
for more background on convergence groups in the setting of compact Hausdorff spaces that need not be metrizable.

A convergence group action of a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
on a compact Hausdorff space
M
is
relatively hyperbolic
if the elements of
\mathbb{P}
are representatives of the conjugacy classes of maximal parabolic subgroups and the action of
\Gamma
on
\Theta^{2}(M)
is cocompact.
(In the elementary case,
\Theta^{2}(M)
has at most one point, so every action on it is considered to be cocompact.)

Definition 2.6

(Bowditch boundary)
.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
has a relatively hyperbolic action on the graph
K
.
Using a variation of a construction due to Bowditch, Gerasimov constructs a compact Hausdorff space
M=M(K)
on which
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
acts as a relatively hyperbolic convergence group

[
Bow12
,
Ger12
]
.
Although a relatively hyperbolic pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
admits relatively hyperbolic actions on many different graphs
K
, the associated compactum
M=M(K)
is uniquely determined up to
\Gamma
–equivariant homeomorphism
[
Bow12
,
GP16
]
.
This unique compactum
M
is the
Bowditch boundary
, denoted
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
.

Remark 2.7

(On finite generation)
.

The definitions of relative hyperbolicity for a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
above make no restriction on the cardinality of the discrete group
\Gamma
.
It is not clear whether there are interesting examples of uncountable relatively hyperbolic groups.
In practice, one often restricts attention to the case when
\Gamma
is finitely generated.
We note that if
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic then
\Gamma
is countable if and only if the Bowditch boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is metrizable
[
Ger09
]
.
In fact, when
\Gamma
is countable,
M
is the (metrizable) Gromov boundary of a locally finite
\delta
–hyperbolic graph, known as the cusped space, whose geometry is central to many applications in the literature (see
[
GM08
,
Hru10
]
).
Cusped spaces do not play a significant role in this article, except for a brief appearance in the proof of Proposition
10.5
.

2.2.
Splittings of relatively hyperbolic groups

This section summarizes the structure of splittings of relatively hyperbolic groups over parabolic subgroups. The results of this section were first introduced in the finitely generated setting by Bowditch
[
Bow01
]
. This section extends that study to arbitrary relatively hyperbolic pairs and expresses the results in the terminology of Guirardel–Levitt’s development of JSJ theory
[
GL17
]
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be a group pair, and let
\mathbb{A}
be a family of subgroups of
\Gamma
closed under conjugation.
Suppose
\Gamma
acts on a simplicial tree
T
without inversions and with no proper invariant subtree. Then
T
is an
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{P})
–tree
if the action is relative to
\mathbb{P}
and each edge stabilizer is a member of
\mathbb{A}
.
Trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there are
\Gamma
–equivariant maps
T\to T^{\prime}
and also
T^{\prime}\to T
.

Definition 2.8

(Co-elementary)
.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic.
Two infinite elementary subgroups
H
and
K
are
co-elementary
if they have the same limit set in the Bowditch boundary.
The limit point of a parabolic subgroup is a
parabolic point
. Its stabilizer is a maximal parabolic subgroup.
If
\{a,b\}
is the limit set of a loxodromic subgroup
H
, then
H
is a finite index subgroup of the maximal loxodromic subgroup
\Stab\{a,b\}
, which is again virtually cyclic.
In particular, co-elementary loxodromic subgroups are always commensurable.
It follows that each infinite elementary subgroup is contained in a unique maximal elementary subgroup.
For proofs of the above assertions, see
[
Tuk94
,
Tuk96
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

A group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is
relatively one ended
if
\Gamma
is infinite,
\Gamma\notin\mathbb{P}
, and
\Gamma
does not split over a finite subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 2.10

(Cylinders)
.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic
and
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
We will consider two cases in which
\mathbb{E}
denotes either the collection of elementary subgroups or the collection of either finite or parabolic subgroups of
\Gamma
.
Let
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
denote the family of infinite subgroups that are members of
\mathbb{E}
.
The one-ended hypothesis implies that every
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree is actually an
(\mathbb{E}_{\infty},\mathbb{P})
–tree.
Given an
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree
T
, edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
are considered to be equivalent if their stabilizers are co-elementary.
By
[
GL17
, Lem. 9.17]
, the union of all edges in an equivalence class is a subtree of
T
.
(We note that the cited lemma is proved under the hypothesis that
\Gamma
is finitely generated. However, this hypothesis is not required, since all properties of elementary subgroups used in the proof also hold in the present more general setting.)
These subtrees are known as
cylinders
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
T
be an
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree. Suppose
Y
is the cylinder consisting of the equivalence class of the edge
e
.
Then the stabilizer
\Gamma_{Y}
of
Y
is the maximal elementary subgroup containing the stabilizer
\Gamma_{e}
.
Furthermore,
\Gamma_{Y}
fixes a vertex of
T
.

Proof.

Suppose
g
stabilizes the cylinder
Y
. Then
\Gamma_{e}
and
\Gamma_{g(e)}
are co-elementary, so they have the same limit set
L=g(L)
. Thus,
g
lies in the maximal elementary subgroup
\Stab(L)
.
Conversely, if
g\in\Stab(L)
it must stabilize the cylinder
Y
, so that
\Gamma_{Y}=\Stab(L)
.

If
\Gamma_{Y}
is a parabolic subgroup of
\Gamma
, then it has a fixed vertex in
T
by the definition of
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree.
If
\Gamma_{Y}
is loxodromic, then
\Gamma_{e}
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma_{Y}
. Therefore, the edge
e
has a finite orbit under the action of
\Gamma_{Y}
. Since
\Gamma_{Y}
acts on
T
with a bounded orbit, it must fix some vertex of
T
(
[
Ser77
, Prop. I.4.19]
).
∎

Definition 2.12

(Tree of cylinders)
.

The
tree of cylinders

T_{c}
associated to
T
is the bipartite tree with vertex set
V_{0}(T_{c})\sqcup V_{1}(T_{c})
, where
V_{0}(T_{c})
is the set of all vertices
v
of
T
that belong to at least two cylinders,
V_{1}(T_{c})
is the set of cylinders
w
of
T
, and there is an edge between
v
and
w
in
T_{c}
if and only if
v
is a vertex of the subtree
w
.
Equivalent trees have the same tree of cylinders
[
GL17
, Lem. 7.3]
.
(Once again the cited result has a finite generation hypothesis that is not used in the proof.)

Furthermore, we will see that the graph
T_{c}
is an
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree equivalent to
T
.
To verify equivalence, one needs to show that each vertex stabilizer of
T
fixes a vertex of
T_{c}
and vice versa. Let
v
be a vertex of
T
with stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
. If
v
lies in two cylinders, then
v
is a
V_{0}
–vertex of
T_{c}
fixed by
\Gamma_{v}
. On the other hand, if
v
lies in a unique cylinder
Y
, then
Y
is a
V_{1}
–vertex of
T_{c}
fixed by
\Gamma_{v}
.
The converse is given by Lemma
2.11
.
Therefore,
T
and
T_{c}
are equivalent.
It follows that each member of
\mathbb{P}
has a fixed vertex in
T_{c}
. The stabilizer of an edge of
T_{c}
is contained in the stabilizer of a cylinder, so it must be a member of
\mathbb{E}
by Lemma
2.11
.

It follows that
T_{c}
is equal to its own tree of cylinders. In particular,
T
and
T_{c}
have the same family of nonelementary vertex stabilizers (
[
GL07
, Cor. 4.4]
).

Proposition 2.13

(
[
BW13
]
, Rem. 2.2 and Lem. 4.9)
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic. Suppose
T
is a tree on which
\Gamma
acts relative to
\mathbb{P}
. If every edge stabilizer is a finitely generated elementary subgroup, then each vertex stabilizer is hyperbolic relative to a family of representatives of the conjugacy classes in
\Gamma_{v}
of infinite subgroups of the form
\Gamma_{v}\cap gPg^{-1}
for
g\in\Gamma
and
P\in\mathbb{P}
.

We note that the statement of
[
BW13
, Lem. 4.9]
requires that each vertex stabilizer and each edge stabilizer be finitely generated. The finite generation of edge groups is used significantly in the proof, but the finite generation of vertex stabilizers is not used and may be omitted as a hypothesis.

Proposition 2.14

(
cf.

[
GL15
]
, Lem. 3.8)
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic, and suppose
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
Let
\mathbb{E}
be the family of elementary subgroups.
Let
T
be any
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree that is equal to its own tree of cylinders.
Suppose each edge group of
T
is finitely generated.
For each vertex
v\in V_{0}(T)
, the stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
is hyperbolic relative to
a family of subgroups
\mathbb{Q}_{v}=\Inc_{v}\cup\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
, defined as follows:

(1)

\Inc_{v}
consists of representatives of the conjugacy classes in
\Gamma_{v}
of stabilizers of edges incident to
v
in
T
.

(2)

\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
consists of representatives of the conjugacy classes in
\Gamma_{v}
of subgroups
gPg^{-1}
with
g\in\Gamma
and
P\in\mathbb{P}
that stabilize
v
and do not stabilize any edge incident to
v
in
T
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.13
, each
\Gamma_{v}
with
v\in V_{0}(T)
is hyperbolic relative to a family of representatives of the conjugacy classes in
\Gamma_{v}
of infinite subgroups of the form
\Gamma_{v}\cap gPg^{-1}
for
g\in\Gamma
and
P\in\mathbb{P}
.
Observe that the stabilizers of vertices adjacent to
v
are distinct maximal elementary subgroups, so their pairwise intersections are finite.

Suppose

is infinite. Then

stabilizes either

or a vertex

adjacent to

. In either case,

.

]

.
∎

Given a parabolic splitting, one may construct a graph

Definition 2.15

(Compatible action)
.

Suppose

is relatively hyperbolic and relatively one ended.

be the family of finite or parabolic subgroups.

admitting a relatively hyperbolic action of the pair

. If

and

. For each

of

to the unique vertex of

fixed by the parabolic subgroup

.

.
(See

The following theorem due to Osin is often useful when studying splittings over parabolic subgroups.

3.
Constructing the Bowditch boundary

In this section, we study the definition of the Bowditch boundary, proving the equivalence of two constructions of the boundary due to Bowditch
[
Bow12
]
and Gerasimov
[
Ger12
]
.
In each case, a fine
\delta
–hyperbolic graph
K
leads to a canonical compact Hausdorff space that contains the vertex set
V(K)
as a dense subspace.
Bowditch constructs a compact Hausdorff space
\Delta K
whose underlying set is
V(K)\cup\partial K
.
The topology on
\Delta K
is given by an explicit neighborhood basis, which is quite simple and easy to work with directly.
However, the proof in
[
Bow12
]
that
\Delta K
is compact is valid only in the special case that
K
has a countable vertex set.
Gerasimov gives a different definition of a compactum
M=M(K)
for arbitrary
K
by first defining a uniform structure on
V(K)
and then letting
M
be its Cauchy–Samuel completion, a space whose points are the minimal Cauchy filters on
V(K)
. The latter construction is theoretically elegant but quite technically elaborate, and the exact relationship between the compactum
M
and the set
V(K)\cup\partial K
is a bit obscured by this approach.

In this section we show—with no restriction on the cardinality of
K
—that the two constructions lead to spaces that are canonically homeomorphic.
In order to produce this homeomorphism between
\Delta K
and
M(K)
, the main step is Proposition
3.3
, which establishes the compactness of
\Delta K
for an arbitrary fine
\delta
–hyperbolic graph
K
.
We begin with a study of the topology on
\Delta K
introduced in
[
Bow12
, §8]
.

Definition 3.1

(Topology on
\Delta K
)
.

Let
K
be an arbitrary fine
\delta
–hyperbolic graph for some constant
\delta
. Let
\Delta K
denote the disjoint union
V(K)\cup\partial K
.
For points
a,b\in\Delta K
, we denote a geodesic from
a
to
b
by
[a,b]
. For each
a\in\Delta K
and each finite subset
A\subseteq V(K)
, define

M^{\prime}(a,A)=\bigl\{\,{b\in\Delta K}\bigm|{\text{there exists $[a,b]$ such that $[a,b]\cap A-{a}=\varnothing$}}\,\bigr\}.

It is established in
[
Bow12
, §8]
that the sets
M^{\prime}(a,A)
form a base for a topology on
\Delta K
such that for each
a
the family
\bigl\{M^{\prime}(a,A)\}_{A}
is a neighborhood base at
a
.
The space
\Delta K
is Hausdorff and contains
V(K)
as a dense subset.

Suppose
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is any function with
f\geq 1_{\mathbb{N}}
and bounded above by a linear function.
A
simple
f
–quasigeodesic
is a simple path
\gamma
such that for each subpath
\gamma^{\prime}
with endpoints
x
and
y
we have
\operatorname{length}(\gamma^{\prime})\leq f\bigl(d(x,y)\bigr)
.
For each
f
as above, equivalent neighborhood bases for a point

are given by the collections

\bigl\{M^{\prime}_{f}(a,A)\bigr\}_{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl\{P^{\prime}_{f}(a,J)\bigr\}_{J}

defined below, where
A
ranges over all finite sets of vertices and
J
ranges over all finite sets of edges of
K
. (When
a
is a vertex, it suffices to let
J
range over finite sets of edges incident to
a

(1)

The set
M^{\prime}_{f}(a,A)
contains all
b\in\Delta K
such that vertex set of at least one simple
f
–quasigeodesic from
a
to
b
is disjoint from
A-\{a\}
.

(2)

The set
P^{\prime}_{f}(a,J)
contains all
b\in\Delta K
such that edge set of at least one simple
f
–quasigeodesic from
a
to
b
does not intersect
J
.

Shortcutting is a process that produces a simple quasigeodesic from a pair of geodesics as in the following lemma (see
[
Bow12
, §8]
for a proof).

Lemma 3.2

(Shortcutting)
.

There exists a function
f_{1}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
f_{1}\geq 1
and bounded above by a linear function and there exists a constant
R_{1}
, such that
f_{1}
and
R_{1}
depend only on the hyperbolicity constant of
K
and such that the following holds.
Suppose
a
and
b
are two points in
\Delta K
and
c\in V(K)
such that both
a
and
b
are not the same point of
\partial K
. Let
\alpha
be a geodesic from
a
to
c
and
\beta
be a geodesic from
b
to
c
.
Then there exists a simple
f_{1}
–quasigeodesic

\gamma
from
a
to
b
such that
\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\cup\omega\cup\beta^{\prime}
, where the path
\alpha^{\prime}
is a subpath of
\alpha
, the path
\beta^{\prime}
is a subpath of
\beta
, and the path
\omega
has length at most
R_{1}
. If

then the edge of
\gamma
incident to

is contained in
\alpha
.

We write
M^{\prime}_{f_{1}}(a,A)
as
M^{\prime}_{1}(a,A)
and so on.
Bowditch claims that the following result holds for arbitrary
K
in
[
Bow12
, Prop. 8.6]
. However, the proof depends, in an essential manner, on an unstated hypothesis that
K
has a countable vertex set. The following proposition extends Bowditch’s result to the general case, removing this restriction on the cardinality of
K
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The space
\Delta K
is compact.

Proof.

We first note that, for each finite set
A
of vertices of
K
, the finite collection
\mathcal{U}_{A}=\bigl\{\,{M^{\prime}(a,A)}\bigm|{a\in A}\,\bigr\}
is an open cover of
\Delta(K)
.
Indeed, for each
x\in\Delta K
, there exists a geodesic from
x
to some point
a\in A
that contains no points of
A
in its interior, so that
x\in M^{\prime}(a,A)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an arbitrary open cover of
\Delta K
.
In order to show that
\mathcal{U}
has a finite subcover, it suffices to show that for some finite set
A\subset V(K)
the finite cover
\mathcal{U}_{A}
refines
\mathcal{U}
.
Suppose by way of contradiction that for every finite set
A\subset V(K)
, there is at least one
rowdy
element
a_{A}\in A
such that
M^{\prime}(a_{A},A)
is not contained in any
U\in\mathcal{U}
.
Fix any vertex
x_{0}
in
K
. For each finite
A
, choose a geodesic
\alpha_{A}
from
x_{0}
to
a_{A}
.

Observe that for each
a\in V(K)
, each
U\in\mathcal{U}
containing
a
, and each finite set
B\subset V(K)
, there exists a finite set
A
containing
B\cup\{a\}
such that
M^{\prime}(a,A)\subseteq U
.
Indeed, since
U
is open, we may choose a finite set of vertices
F=F(a,U)
such that
a\in M^{\prime}(a,F)\subset U
.
But then the finite set
A=F\cup B\cup\{a\}
satisfies
M^{\prime}(a,A)\subseteq M^{\prime}(a,F)\subseteq U
. In particular, any finite set
B
of vertices with rowdy element
a_{B}\in B
, can be increased to a larger finite set
A
such that
a_{B}
is no longer rowdy with respect to
A
.

We will construct a geodesic ray
\epsilon
with vertices
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
and a nested sequence of cofinal collections of finite vertex sets
\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\cdots
such that, if
A\in\Lambda_{n}
, the geodesic
\alpha_{A}
shares its first
n
edges with
\epsilon
.
(A collection
\Lambda
of finite sets is
cofinal
if for each finite set
A
, some member of
\Lambda
contains
A
.)
We construct
x_{n+1}
and
\Lambda_{n+1}
recursively, beginning with the previously chosen
x_{0}
and the collection of all finite sets for
\Lambda_{0}
.

Assume that
x_{n}
and
\Lambda_{n}
have been previously constructed.
Clearly
x_{n}\in U
for some
U\in\mathcal{U}
, so that
x_{n}
has a basic neighborhood
P_{1}^{\prime}(x_{n},J_{n})\subseteq U
for some finite set of edges
J_{n}
incident to
x_{n}
.
Let
\Lambda^{\prime}_{n}\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
be the subcollection of all finite sets of vertices
B
large enough to contain all endpoints of the edges in
J_{n}
and large enough that
x_{n}
is not rowdy with respect to
B
.

Suppose
B\in\Lambda^{\prime}_{n}
. Then the geodesic
\alpha_{B}
from
x_{0}
to
a_{B}
shares its first
n
edges with
\epsilon
and has length at least
n+1
.
We show that the edge of
\alpha_{B}
following
x_{n}
must be a member of the set
J_{n}
.
If not, then by Lemma
3.2
, for each
b\in M^{\prime}(a_{B},B)
there is a simple
f_{1}
–quasigeodesic
\gamma
from
x_{n}
to
b
whose first edge is contained in
\alpha_{B}
and, thus, is not contained in
J_{n}
.
In particular, the quasigeodesic
\gamma
avoids
J_{n}
, so that
b\in P^{\prime}_{1}(x_{n},J_{n})
. Consequently,
M^{\prime}(a_{B},B)\subseteq P^{\prime}_{1}(x_{n},J_{n})\subseteq U
, contradicting the assumption that
a_{B}
is rowdy in
B
. Therefore, the edge of
\alpha_{B}
following
x_{n}
must be a member of
J_{n}
as claimed.
Since
J_{n}
is finite, we may pass to a cofinal subcollection
\Lambda_{n+1}\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}_{n}
such that all geodesics
\alpha_{C}
with
C\in\Lambda_{n+1}
involve the same edge of
J_{n}
. Letting
x_{n+1}
be the other endpoint of this edge (distinct from
x_{n}
) completes the recursive construction of the ray
\epsilon
.

Let
y\in\partial K
be the endpoint of
\epsilon
. There is some
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that
y\in U
. There exists some finite set
S\subset V(K)
and a basic neighborhood
M^{\prime}_{1}(y,S)\subseteq U
. As
S
is finite, it lies in the ball of radius
r
about
x_{0}
for some
r<\infty
.

Choose
n>r+R_{1}+\delta+1
where
R_{1}
is the constant from Lemma
3.2
and
\delta
is the hyperbolicity constant of
K
.
Choose any
A\in\Lambda_{n}
large enough that
S\subseteq A
.
We claim that
M^{\prime}(a_{A},A)\subseteq M^{\prime}_{1}(y,S)
.

Let
\alpha
be a geodesic from
a_{A}
to
y
. Consider the thin geodesic triangle
T_{thin}
with vertices
y,x_{n},a_{A}
such that the geodesic
\alpha
is the side of the triangle from
y
to
a_{A}
, the side from
x_{n}
to
a_{A}
is contained in the geodesic from
x_{0}
to
a_{A}
that intersects
\epsilon
up to length
n
and the geodesic from
x_{n}
to
y
is contained in
\epsilon
. The geodesic
\alpha
is in the
\delta
–neighborhood of the union of geodesics
[x_{n},y]
and
[x_{n},a_{A}]
. In particular if
z
is any vertex of
\alpha
then
d(x_{0},z)\geq n-\delta=r+R_{1}+1
. Hence
d(x_{0},z)>r
, implying that
z\not\in S
.

Now consider any
x\in M^{\prime}(a_{A},A)
.
Since
S\subseteq A
, there exists a geodesic
\beta
from
a_{A}
to
x
with vertex set disjoint from
S
. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain a simple
f_{1}
–quasigeodesic
\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\cup\omega\cup\beta^{\prime}
from
y
to

where
\alpha^{\prime}\subseteq\alpha
and
\beta^{\prime}\subseteq\beta
and
\omega
has length at most
R_{1}
.
Then
\gamma
misses
S
. Indeed, if
s\in S
intersects
\gamma
then
s\in V(\omega)
. Let
t_{1}
be the vertex at which
\alpha^{\prime}
meets
\omega
.
Then
d(x_{0},t_{1})<d(x_{0},s)+d(s,t_{1})\leq r+R_{1}
, contradicting the fact that
d(x_{0},t_{1})>r+R_{1}+1
. Hence
V(\gamma)\cap S=\varnothing
. Therefore
x\in M^{\prime}_{1}(y,S)
, which establishes that
M^{\prime}(a_{A},A)\subseteq M^{\prime}_{1}(y,S)\subseteq U
.
However, this conclusion contradicts our initial assumption that
a_{A}
is rowdy in
A
.
∎

Next we define a uniformity
\mathcal{D}
on
\Delta K
that induces the given topology.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
J
be any finite set of edges in
K
. Define an entourage
D_{J}\subseteq\Delta K\times\Delta K
that contains all pairs
(a,b)
such that there exists a geodesic from
a
to
b
that does not contain an edge from
J
. In other words,
(a,b)\in D_{J}
if and only if
b\in P^{\prime}(a,J)
.
The proof of Proposition
3.3
shows that the
family of entourages
D_{J}
for all finite edge sets

is a base for the canonical uniform structure
\mathcal{D}
on the compact space
\Delta K
(see
[
Wil70
, §36]
).

Remark 3.5

(Gerasimov’s definition)
.

To any fine
\delta
–hyperbolic graph
K
we have associated a canonical compact Hausdorff space
\Delta K
that contains the vertex set
V(K)
as a dense subspace.
Using a different construction, Gerasimov associates a compact Hausdorff space
M=M(K)
to
K
such that
M
also contains
V(K)
as a dense subspace (see
[
Ger12
]
and
[
GP16
, §2]
).
We note that the space
M
of Gerasimov coincides with the space
\Delta K
constructed above, in the sense that the identity map on
V(K)
extends to a unique homeomorphism
M\to\Delta K
.

Indeed, the canonical uniform structure
\mathcal{D}
on
\Delta K
restricts to a uniform structure, also denoted
\mathcal{D}
, on
V(K)
.
By Proposition
3.3
, the space
\Delta K
is compact and, thus, complete as a uniform space.
As Gerasimov defines
M
to be the Cauchy–Samuel completion of this same uniform space
\bigl(V(K),\mathcal{D}\bigr)
, the claim follows immediately from the uniqueness of completions.

If
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
has a relatively hyperbolic action on the fine hyperbolic graph
K
, then the action of
\Gamma
on its vertex set

induces a relatively hyperbolic convergence group action of the pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
on
\Delta K=M(K)
by
[

]
.
Thus
\Delta K
may be identified with the Bowditch boundary
\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
using the uniqueness theorem of
[
Bow12
]
in the finitely generated case and of
[
GP16
]
in general (see Definition
2.6
).

4.
Trees of compacta

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic and
\Gamma
acts on a simplicial tree
T
relative to
\mathbb{P}
with infinite, finitely generated parabolic edge stabilizers.
In this section, we show that the boundary of
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
may be described as the limit of a tree of compacta formed by gluing the boundaries of the vertex groups along parabolic points in the pattern of the tree
T
and then compactifying.
The main results of this section are inspired by the treelike structures associated to peripheral splittings in
[
Bow01
]
and
[
Dah03
]
.
The inverse limit construction is a minor variation of a construction due to Świątkowski
[
Świ20
]
, which itself is modeled on the Ancel–Siebenmann characterization of trees of manifolds
[
AS85
,
Jak91
]
.

4.1.
The limit of a tree system

Definition 4.1
.

A
tree system

\Theta
of compacta consists of the following data:

(1)

A bipartite tree
T
with bipartition
V(T)=V_{0}(T)\sqcup V_{1}(T)
.

(2)

To each vertex
v
of
V_{0}(T)
, we associate a compact space
M_{v}
called a
component space
. For each
w\in V_{1}
we associate a one-point space
M_{w}
, known as a
peripheral point
.

(3)

To each oriented edge
\vec{e}\in E(T)
, we associate a one-point space
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
and a map
i_{\vec{e}}\colon\Sigma_{\vec{e}}\to M_{t(\vec{e}\,)}
.
We require that in each component space
M_{v}
the adjacent edge spaces have distinct images.

Let
\bigcup\Theta
denote the quotient space of
\bigsqcup_{v\in V(T)}M_{v}
by the equivalence relation generated by
i_{\vec{e}}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\sim i_{-\vec{e}}(\Sigma_{-\vec{e}})
for all oriented edges
\vec{e}\in E(T)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
S
is any subtree of
T
. Let
V(S)=V_{0}(S)\sqcup V_{1}(S)
and
E(S)
denote the set of vertices and the set of edges respectively of
S
.
Let
\Theta_{S}
be the restriction of the tree system
\Theta
to the subtree
S
.

Definition 4.3

(Limit of a tree system)
.

For each finite subtree
F
of
T
, the
partial union
is the compact space
M_{F}=\bigcup\Theta_{F}
.
For a pair of subtrees
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
, the
collapse map

f_{F_{1}F_{2}}\colon M_{F_{2}}\to M_{F_{1}}

is the quotient map obtained by collapsing
M_{v}
to a point for each
v\in V(F_{2})-V(F_{1})
.
Notice that
f_{F_{1}F_{2}}\circ f_{F_{2}F_{3}}=f_{F_{1}F_{3}}
whenever
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}\subseteq F_{3}
. Hence the system of spaces
M_{F}
and maps
f_{FF^{\prime}}
with
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
is an inverse system of compacta indexed by the poset of all finite subtrees
F
of
T
.

The
limit

\lim\Theta

of the tree system

\Theta
is the inverse limit of the above inverse system. Observe that
\lim\Theta
is compact and Hausdorff, since it is an inverse limit of compact Hausdorff spaces.

4.2.
The boundary as a tree system

Suppose
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic.
Let
T
be any simplicial tree on which
\Gamma
acts relative to
\mathbb{P}
with finitely many orbits of edges such that all edge stabilizers are infinite, parabolic, and finitely generated.
Suppose
T
is equal to its tree of cylinders.
Fix a relatively hyperbolic action of the pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
on a fine
\delta
–hyperbolic graph
K
that is compatible with this splitting in the sense of Definition
2.15
.

Definition 4.4
.

A
ray
in a tree is a subgraph isomorphic to
[0,\infty)
with its natural structure as an infinite graph.
Two rays are
equivalent
if their intersection is a ray. An equivalence class of rays is an
end
of the tree.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
K^{\prime}
be the barycentric subdivision of
K
.
We consider
K^{\prime}
and
T
as length metric spaces in which each edge has length one.
Consider the map
f\colon K^{\prime}\to T
defined as follows, using the notation of Definition
2.15
.
Recall that
K
is formed as a quotient of the disjoint union of graphs
K_{v}
for each
v\in V_{0}(T)
and one-point graphs
K_{w}
for each
w\in V_{1}(T)
.
Each edge
e
of
K
lies in the image of
K_{v}
for a unique vertex
v\in V_{0}(T)
.
We map the barycenter
x
of the edge

5.
Connectedness properties of boundaries

This section discusses criteria for the connectedness and local connectedness of the Bowditch boundary.

Recall that a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively one ended if
\Gamma
does not admit an action relative to
\mathbb{P}
on a simplicial tree with finite edge stabilizers.
Relative one-endedness is weaker than absolute one-endedness. For instance, let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a genus one surface with one cusp and let
P
be the fundamental group of the cusp.
Then
\Gamma
is a free group of rank two, which is not one ended. But
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
P
, having a Bowditch boundary homeomorphic to the circle
S^{1}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic.
The boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is connected if and only if
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{P}
.

This proposition was established in the finitely generated case by Bowditch
[
Bow12
, Thm. 10.1]
.
The proof of the forward implication is similar to that given by Bowditch. Bowditch’s argument for the reverse implication is valid only when
\Gamma
is finitely generated. The general case is easily reduced to the finitely generated case using Propositions
2.17
and
4.12
.

Proof.

We first assume that
\Gamma
admits a nontrivial action relative to
\mathbb{P}
on a simplicial tree
T
with finite edge stabilizers. If
K
is any fine hyperbolic graph with a relatively hyperbolic action of
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
, we may map the vertex set
V(K)
equivariantly to the vertex set
V(T)
of
T
. Each edge
e
of
T
, separates
T
into two halftrees
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. Let
A_{i}
be the set of vertices of
K
mapping to the halftree
T_{i}
.
Then the set
J
of edges of
K
connecting a vertex of
A_{1}
to a vertex of
A_{2}
is a finite set.
If
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are vertices of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
respectively, then the basic open sets
P^{\prime}(a_{1},J)
and
P^{\prime}(a_{2},J)
are disjoint and partition
\Delta K=M
.
Thus
M
is disconnected.

For the converse, assume that
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{P}
. Let
T_{JSJ}
be the JSJ tree of cylinders over parabolic subgroups given by Proposition
2.17
.
Then each vertex group
\Gamma_{v}
with
v\in V_{0}(T_{JSJ})
is finitely generated, each edge group is finitely generated, and the pair
(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{Q}_{v})
is relatively hyperbolic, where
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
is the peripheral structure given by Proposition
2.14
.
Furthermore,
\Gamma_{v}
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
.

As
\Gamma_{v}
is finitely generated, one-endedness of the relatively hyperbolic pair
(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{Q}_{v})
implies that its Bowditch boundary
\partial(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{Q}_{v})
is connected by
[
Bow12
, Prop. 10.1]
.
According to Proposition
4.12
, the boundary
\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is the inverse limit of a tree system whose vertex spaces are all connected.
In particular, the factor spaces
M_{F}
of the inverse system are connected.
We conclude that the inverse limit is connected, since an inverse limit of connected compact Hausdorff spaces is always connected
[
Eng89
, Thm. 6.1.20]
.
∎

The following elementary result of Capel provides a criterion for the local connectedness of an inverse limit of compacta.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
Cap54
]
)
.

Let
\{X_{\alpha}\}
be an inverse system such that each bonding map
X_{\alpha}\to X_{\alpha^{\prime}}
is monotone. If each factor space
X_{\alpha}
is compact and locally connected then the inverse limit
\varprojlim X_{\alpha}
is locally connected.

Recall that a map

is
monotone
if

is surjective and for each
z\in Z
the set
f^{-1}(z)
is compact and connected.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})

be a relatively hyperbolic group pair, and suppose the boundary

is connected.
Let

be the tree of cylinders for a splitting of
\Gamma
relative to
\mathbb{P}
over parabolic subgroups.
Suppose for each
v\in V_{0}(T_{c})
that the Bowditch boundary
\partial(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{Q}_{v})
is locally connected.
Then
M
is also locally connected.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.12
, the boundary
M
is an inverse limit of a tree system
\Theta
corresponding to the splitting
T_{c}
.
For each finite subtree
F
of
T_{c}
, the partial union
K_{F}
is formed by gluing finitely many component spaces

in the pattern of

.
Since each
K_{v}
is connected,
K_{F}
is again connected.
As a quotient of a compact, locally connected space,
K_{F}
is also compact and locally connected.

To apply Theorem
5.2
, we only need to show that the bonding map
K_{F_{2}}\to K_{F_{1}}
is monotone whenever

is a subtree of
F_{2}
.
But, according to Definition

, each nontrivial point preimage has the form

for some finite subtree

∎

6.
Pretrees and non-nesting actions

This section reviews the definition and basic properties of pretrees, a general treelike structure explored extensively by Bowditch in
[
Bow99d
]
.
A key result of this section is Lemma
6.5
, which states that a non-nesting action on a pretree induces a non-nesting action on its quotient by any equivariant full relation.
The results of this section lay the foundations for applications to the cut point structure of boundaries in Sections
7
and
8
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
K
be a set with a ternary relation called
betweenness
.
If
y
is between
x
and
z
, we write
xyz
.
Such a structure is a

if it satisfies the following:

(1)

If
y
is between

and
z
, then

.

(2)

y
is between
x
and
z
if and only if
y
is between
z
and
x
.

(3)

If
y
is between
x
and
z
, then
z
is not between
x
and
y
.

(4)

If
xyz
and
y\neq w
then either
xyw
or
wyz
.

For points

in a pretree

, the

(x,y)
is the set of all

between
x
and
y
. The
closed interval

is the set
(x,y)\cup\{x,y\}
. Half-open intervals are defined similarly.
Distinct points

are
adjacent
if the interval
(x,y)
is empty.
A pretree is

if every interval
(x,y)
is finite.
A subset

of a pretree

is

if for all distinct

(x,y)
contains at least one point of
Q
.
We say

is

if it is dense in itself.

A subset

of a pretree

is

if

for all
x,y\in A
. An
arc
of a pretree is a nonempty full subset

such that for all distinct

, we have
xyz
or
yzx
or
zxy
.
Note that every interval is an arc.
A

on an arc

is a linear order

on
A
such that

holds if and only if either
x<y<z
or
z<y<x
. We refer to

as a

Any arc with at least two elements admits precisely two directions.
A
final segment
of a directed arc

is a subset

such that if

and

with
x\leq y
, then

. Two directed arcs

and

are

if they have a common final segment.

A pretree is

if every arc is an interval. A pretree is

if every arc contained in an interval is itself an interval.
We note that if
K
is a complete pretree, then any full subpretree of

is interval complete.

Suppose

is a pretree.
The

of a triple of points

is the unique point

.
A
median pretree
is a pretree in which every triple has a median.

Definition 6.2

(Connector points)
.

Let

be any complete median pretree.
Let

and

be nonempty, full, disjoint subsets of

such that

is also full.
A point

is a

of the ordered pair

in

for all

.

Existence and uniqueness of connector points is given by the following lemma due to Bowditch.

Lemma 6.3

(
[
Bow99d
]
, Lem. 6.13)
.

Let

be a complete median pretree, and let

and
Y
be disjoint, full, nonempty subsets such that

is full.
If the connector point
p(Y,X)
exists, it is unique.
Furthermore, at least one of the pairs
(X,Y)
and
(Y,X)
has a connector point.

A

on a pretree

is an equivalence relation for which every equivalence class is full.
If
\sim
is a full equivalence relation,

denotes the equivalence class of the point

.
The set

has a natural induced pretree structure such that

is between

and

if and only if there exists

such that

for all

and

(see

A point

. The

of a pretree

Lemma 6.4

Let

be any pretree with a full relation

There exists a full subpretree

contained in the interior

that is invariant under all automorphisms of

.

Proof.

If

lies between points

and

mapping to

.
Let

be the smallest full subset of

has the desired properties.

An action of a group

on a pretree

if no

Lemma 6.5
.

If

, then the induced action on

is non-nesting for any equivariant full relation

.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, suppose

Replacing

Suppose

Then there exist

and

On the other hand, suppose

. Then

is between

and

and

Let

We show that

is full. Suppose

and choose

. Since

and

holds. Suppose

and

. Since

and

are full, we have

and
m_{2}\in Y
. Since we have

, and

, we also have

and, thus,

.
A similar argument establishes that

, so that

.
Since

and

are not members of

, it follows that

. Similarly

. Hence

, so that

is full.

Notice that

is non-empty as it contains

. To see that

is full, choose

and

and

.
Clearly if

Since the members of

, the connector point

of

Since

is stabilized as a set by

, the connector point

is fixed by

. This leads to a contradiction, since

is a proper subset of

∎

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting on a pretree

. The action is

Lemma 6.6
.

Suppose

In

Proof.

Choose

intersects

If we choose

, then the fixed point

.

7.
The cut point pretree

In this section, we review a construction due to Swenson
[
Swe00
]
of a cut point pretree and cut point tree dual to a family of cut points in a continuum.
We establish Proposition
7.10
, which describes the structure of this pretree when the continuum admits a convergence group action.

Recall that if
M
is a compact Hausdorff space with at least three points, a
convergence group action
on
M
is an action such that the induced action on the space of distinct triples
\Theta^{3}(M)
is proper.
The action is
minimal
if
M
has no proper subset that is nonempty, closed, and
\Gamma
–invariant.

The main application of the results below is to the Bowditch boundary of a finitely generated relatively hyperbolic group. The compact Hausdorff topology on the boundary
\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})=\Delta K
defined in Section
3
has a countable base whenever
\Gamma
is countable, so the boundary is metrizable in this setting.

In the sequel, we study convergence actions only on metrizable spaces, usually requiring connectedness as well. We refer to Tukia
[
Tuk94
]
for the basic structure theory of convergence groups on metrizable spaces.

Definition 7.1

(Continua)
.

A
continuum
is a nonempty, compact, connected, metrizable topological space.
A continuum is
nontrivial
if it contains more than one point, in which case it has the cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
of the real line. Indeed, any continuum embeds in the Hilbert cube by Urysohn’s theorem, and any nonempty open set in a nontrivial continuum contains a Cantor set (see, for instance,
[
Kec95
, Thm. 6.2]
or
[
Eng89
, Prob. 4.5.5]
).
Note that any continuum containing a cut point must be nontrivial.

Definition 7.2

(
\mathbb{R}
–trees)
.

An
\mathbb{R}
–tree
is a uniquely arc-connected geodesic metric space, or equivalently a geodesic space with no subspace homeomorphic to the circle.
A
topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree
is the underlying topological space of an
\mathbb{R}
–tree.
A
dendrite
is a compact topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree.

For each continuum
M
equipped with a preferred family of cut points

dual to

is constructed in
[
Swe00
]
and described briefly below.

Definition 7.3

(Cut point pretree)
.

Let

be a continuum, and let

be a nonempty family of cut points of
M
.
A pretree structure on

is given by the following betweenness relation:

c
is
between

a
and

if
c\in C
and
M-\{c\}
has a separation into disjoint nonempty open sets
U
and
V
with
a\in U
and
b\in V
.
Consider the following equivalence relation on
M
.

(1)

If
c\in C
, then
c
is equivalent to only itself.

(2)

If

then
a\sim b
if
(a,b)
is empty.

Let
{K}
denote the set of equivalence classes of this relation.
The set
{K}
has an induced pretree structure given by the following betweenness relation:

(1)

If
x,y\in K
and

, we say that
\{c\}
is between

and

if for any

and

.

(2)

If

is between

and

if for all

,

, and

.

The pretree

is a complete median pretree (see
[

]
), called the

dual to

.

Lemma 7.4

(
[
Swe00
]
, Lem. 4)
.

(1)

If

is a nontrivial subcontinuum of

not separated by any point of
C
, then the set

is nonempty, dense in

, and contained in a single equivalence class of

.

(2)

If
x
and
y
are adjacent in

then exactly one of them is a singleton set

with

and the other of them is a non-singleton equivalence class whose closure contains the point

The proof of the lemma is implicit in the proof of
[
Swe00
, Lem. 4]
. We give the details for the benefit of the reader.

Proof.

Suppose the subcontinuum
M^{\prime}
is nontrivial and not separated by any point of
C
.
We first show that
M^{\prime}\cap C
is countable.
For each
c\in M^{\prime}\cap C
, choose subcontinua
A_{c}
and
B_{c}
of

such that

,
A_{c}\cap B_{c}=\{c\}
, and

. Then
\bigl\{\,{B_{c}-\{c\}}\bigm|{c\in M^{\prime}\cap C}\,\bigr\}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty open sets of

. Since

is second countable, this family must be countable.
Any nonempty open set of

is uncountable (see Definition

), so

intersects every nonempty open set of
M^{\prime}
;
i.e.
,

is dense in

. By hypothesis, all points of

are equivalent, establishing (

).

To see (

), suppose

and

are distinct members of

. If neither

nor

is in

, then an element of

must separate them, as

, so they are not adjacent. On the other hand, if

, then

contains a minimal subcontinuum

containing

and

are not separated by any point of

is also not separated by any point of

is a nonempty set by (

). If

is any

is between

and

If

and

are adjacent in

, the only possibility is that exactly one of them, say

, is a member of

.
It remains to show that

is in the closure of the set

is any minimal subcontinuum of

containing

, then

is not separated by any point of

. Thus,

and

is in the closure

of

).
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Any homeomorphism of

that is not an inversion.

We will need to use the following result due to Swenson.

Lemma 7.6

(
[
Swe00
]
, Lem. 7)

Let

Let

(1)

If

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\Gamma
have a convergence group action on

, and let

be loxodromic with fixed points

and
b
in

.
Let
K
be the pretree of

dual to a
\Gamma
–invariant family of cut points

. Suppose

and

in

Proof.

Since there is at most one member of

between

and
[b]
, such an element must be fixed by

. Conversely, suppose

is fixed by

cannot be a single point, since

and

are the only fixed points of

in

. Therefore,

must be a nontrivial subset of

be any compact subset of

is a loxodromic, the sets

and

and

each lie in the closure of

.

We claim that

must be a cut point of

that separates the nontrivial classes

and
Y
.
Then

is the union of two subcontinua

and

with

and

and

. The closed set

contains

but does not contain

is a cut point. Similarly

is also a cut point.

We claim that

and

are adjacent in

The argument of the previous paragraph shows that there can be no cut point between

and

Suppose

and

Then for any

Express

and

We must have

Since

.
Thus

We have contradicted our assumption that

A similar argument shows that

∎

When

is countable, the following construction due to Swenson shows that the cut point pretree may be extended to form a tree by filling the gaps between adjacent elements. (See

Definition 7.8

(Cut point tree)
.

Define

\widehat{K}={K}\cup\bigcup_{\textrm{$\{x,y\}$ adjacent}}I(x,y)

where
I(x,y)
is a copy of the real open interval
(0,1)
. We take
I(y,x)
to be the same arc as

, but with opposite orientation.
There exists a canonical extension of the interval relation to
\widehat{K}
that endows
\widehat{K}
with the structure of a dense, complete pretree such that each
I(x,y)=(x,y)
.
Each homeomorphism of
M
leaving
C

\widehat{K}
by extending linearly to the intervals

.
Furthermore, every interval of
\widehat{K}
is order-isomorphic to an interval of the real line
[
PS06
, Thm. 13]
.
The pretree
\widehat{K}
is called the

of

dual to
C
because it admits a canonical topology as an
\mathbb{R}
–tree such that every homeomorphism of

induces a homeomorphism of

(see
[
PS06
, Thm. 14]

If
\Gamma
has a convergence group action on

, and

–tree on which

acts with several useful dynamical properties

Theorem 7.9

(
[
Swe00
]
, Thm. 8)

If

is a countable convergence group without any infinite torsion subgroup acting on a continuum

with cut points, then

has a non-nesting stable action on a topological

–tree

\Gamma
(viewed as a convergence group), and no point of

.

We extend this theorem to give the following stronger conclusion involving the induced action on the cut point pretree, a conclusion that is implicit in Swenson’s methods of proof.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be a group pair such that
\Gamma
is countable.
Suppose
\Gamma
has a minimal convergence group action relative to
\mathbb{P}
on a continuum

. Let

be a
\Gamma
–invariant family of cut points in

. Suppose
{K}
is the cut point pretree of

.
Then the action of
\Gamma
on

is nontrivial and without inversions.
The action on the interior
{K}^{o}
of

is a non-nesting action such that the stabilizer of each interval of
{K}^{o}
is elementary in the convergence group
\Gamma
. If each

is finitely generated, the action on

is relative to

.

The proof of this result is given below. We note that some parts of the proof follow from arguments given in the proof of
[
Swe00
, Thm. 8]

Before explaining the proof, we state a result of Levitt that will be used in the proof.

Theorem 7.11

(
[

]
, Thm. 3)
.

Let

–tree with a non-nesting action of a group

.

(1)

If

with no fixed point, then

acts

, in the following sense.
There is a proper embedding

whose image

. Such a set

(2)

If

is finitely generated and acts on

contains a hyperbolic element.

Proof of Proposition
7.10
.

Let
\widehat{K}
be the cut point tree of

dual to
C
.
Let

R=\widehat{K}-\{\text{terminal points}\}.

The proof of
[
Swe00
, Thm. 8]
involves several steps, one of which establishes the following.
For any
\Gamma
–invariant subtree

of
\widehat{K}
, let

be the subtree of

obtained by removing all terminal points. Then the action of
\Gamma
on

is non-nesting and the stabilizer of each interval of

is an elementary subgroup of the convergence action of
\Gamma
on

. In this portion of the proof, the hypothesis regarding infinite torsion subgroups is never used.

Applying these conclusions to

itself, we conclude that the action on the interior

of the pretree

is nonnesting and each arc stabilizer in

is elementary.
The action of
\Gamma
on

is without inversions by Corollary

.

Next we show that the action of
\Gamma
on
{K}^{o}
is relative to
\mathbb{P}
. Since the action on

is without inversions, it suffices to show that the action on

is relative to
\mathbb{P}
. Indeed, the action on the pretree extends to the intervals

such that if any point of

fixes the closed interval

pointwise; in particular it fixes

and

It is clear that each

has a fixed point in

. To complete the proof, we need to find such a fixed point that lies in the subtree

It is shown in
[

, Thm. 14]
that the cut point tree
\widehat{K}
has a canonical topology as a topological

–tree.
Each

is finitely generated with a non-nesting action on the topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree

. If no point of

is fixed by

, then by Theorem

some infinite order element

acts hyperbolically on

with axis

fixes no point in

, and there exists a proper embedding of the real line in

whose image

The closure of

is an interval

As

is another such cut point distinct from

But

is a parabolic element of the convergence group, so it has a unique fixed point in

.

To see that

For each pair of open sets

Since its fixed points are separated by

Thus

It follows that

.

The main goal of this section is to prove Proposition

, which extends Bowditch’s result to the more general setting in which the group
\Gamma
acts relative to
\mathbb{P}
and does not split relative to
\mathbb{P}
over a finite or parabolic subgroup.

Definition 8.1

(Dendrite quotient)
.

Let
M
be a continuum and
C
a set of cut points of
M
. For each pair
x,y\in M
, the set of cut points of
M
separating
x
from
y
has a natural linear order.
The
dendrite quotient of
M
dual to
C
, denoted
M/{\approx}
or
D(M,C)
, is formed from
M
by identifying two points
x,y
if the collection of points of
C
separating them does not contain a subset order-isomorphic to the rational numbers.
When endowed with the quotient topology,
D(M,C)
is a dendrite, according to the main theorem of
[
Bow99d
]
.

In order to better understand the dendrite quotient, we examine the following transfinite sequence of quotient pretrees.

Definition 8.2

(A transfinite sequence of quotients)
.

Let
M
be a continuum with a set of cut points
C
.
Let
K
denote the cut point pretree of
M
dual to
C
.
We recursively define a full equivalence relation
\sim_{\alpha}
on
K
with quotient
\pi_{\alpha}\colon K\to K_{\alpha}
for each ordinal
\alpha
.
The pretree
K_{0}
is equal to
K
itself.
If
K_{\alpha}
has been defined for some ordinal
\alpha
, we define
K_{\alpha+1}
by identifying each pair of points
x,y\in K
such that the interval between
\pi_{\alpha}(x)
and
\pi_{\alpha}(y)
in
K_{\alpha}
is finite.
If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, we define
K_{\alpha}
by identifying a pair
x,y\in K
if
\pi_{\beta}(x)=\pi_{\beta}(y)
for some
\beta<\alpha
.

Bowditch shows that the recursive procedure described above eventually stabilizes, and the stable quotient may be described precisely, as follows.

Lemma 8.3

(
[
Bow99d
]
, Lem. 4.3 and 4.4)
.

For any pretree
K
, the transfinite sequence of quotients defined as above eventually stabilizes.
In particular, there exists an ordinal
\alpha_{0}
such that the natural map
K_{\alpha_{0}}\to K_{\alpha_{0}+1}
is a bijection.
Furthermore, the stable quotient
K_{\alpha_{0}}
is equal to the quotient
K/{\approx}
such that
x\approx y
if the pretree interval
(x,y)
in
K
does not contain a subset order-isomorphic to the rational numbers.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
M
be a continuum, let
C
be a set of cut points of
M
, and let
K
be the cut point pretree.
The quotient map
M\to K
induces a bijection from the dendrite
D(M,C)=M/{\approx}
to the pretree
K_{\alpha_{0}}
for some ordinal
\alpha_{0}
.
This bijection is also a pretree automorphism.

Proof.

According to Lemma
8.3
, the stable quotient
K_{\alpha_{0}}
is equal to the quotient
K/{\approx}
.
By Definition
7.3
, the members of
K
have two types, singleton sets
\{c\}
with
c\in C
and equivalence classes of points of
M
not contained in
C
under the relation
a\mathcal{R}b
if
a
and
b
are separated in
M
by no point of
C
.
Any two equivalence classes of the second type are separated by a cut point from

, by definition.
If an interval of

contains a subset

order-isomorphic to the rationals, then

is also isomorphic to the rationals.
Thus,

holds if and only if the members of

between

and

do not contain a subset order isomorphic to the rational numbers.

The relation
\approx
on

pulls back to the relation
\approx
on

such that
x\approx y
if the set of points of

separating
x
from
y
does not contain a subset order isomorphic to the rationals. Therefore, the map

induces a pretree automorphism from the dendrite

to the stable pretree

.
∎

The two constructions of the dendrite
D(M,C)
described above may seem a bit arbitrary. But in fact, the dendrite quotient operation is natural from a categorical point of view, as described in the following proposition.
This proposition is not used in any of our main arguments, and it may safely be skipped.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
D(M,C)
be the dendrite quotient of
M
dual to
C
.
Let
\mathcal{R}
be the relation on
M
such that

holds if

and

are points of
M-C
that are not separated by any point of
C
.
If

is endowed with the quotient topology, then

is equal to the maximal Hausdorff quotient of

.

The

of a space

is the universal Hausdorff quotient

of

such that every map from
X
to a Hausdorff space factors through

(see
[
ML98
, Prop. V.9.2]
or
[
McD06
, Lem. 3.1]
).
The proof of Proposition
8.5
uses the notion of connector points and the following lemma, which will be used several more times in our main results.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be adjacent points of
K_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 1
.
Then one of the pairs
(X,Y)
and
(Y,X)
has a unique connector point and the other pair has no connector point.

Proof.

Recall that
K
is complete and median by Definition
7.3
.
When considered as subsets of
K
, the sets
X
and
Y
are nonempty, disjoint, and full with

also full.
Under those circumstances, Lemma
6.3
shows that at least one of the pairs has a unique connector point.

If the pairs
(X,Y)
and
(Y,X)
both had connector points, then the two connectors

and

would be adjacent in
{K}
and in the same
\sim_{1}
–class, contradicting the fact that
p
and
p^{\prime}
are not
\sim_{\alpha}
–equivalent.
∎

Proof of Proposition
8.5
.

As the dendrite

is Hausdorff and equal to

for some ordinal

, it suffices to show that any continuous map from

to a Hausdorff space factors through the quotient

.

We proceed by induction on
\alpha
.
The claim is trivial for

.
Recall that

is formed from

by identifying adjacent pairs of points

in

. If

Suppose any map from

, and let

is formed from

be adjacent elements of

, which we consider as full disjoint subsets of

Select any

be the set of all

from

\{c\}\in X

.
Furthermore, no point of

is adjacent to

.

We claim that
\hat{C}
has a limit point in common with the set

.
Consider the points

as subsets of

, and choose

and

.
Let

be an irreducible subcontinuum of

from

to

,

i.e
, a subcontinuum of

containing

and

such that no proper subcontinuum of

contains both

and

.
By irreducibility, the points of

are precisely the members of

that separate distinct points of

For each

, let

be the subcontinuum of

.
Then

, whenever

and

.
If we let

be the intersection of all

has empty intersection with

since for each

there exists

and

.

By compactness of

.
Thus the closure of

intersects

.
In order to establish the claim,

is contained in the closure of

.
If

, the intersection

is a subcontinuum of

By Lemma

On the other hand, suppose

If

Since

.
We assume that

does not split relative to

Assume

.
We let

, and let
(K_{\alpha})

.

Lemma 8.7
.

For each ordinal

, the action of

on the interior of

is non-nesting, without inversions, and relative to

.

By Proposition

, the action of

.
Since

Let

be the

–invariant full subpretree of

given by Lemma

.
Since each

has a fixed point

Since

Remark 8.8

(Collapsing simplicial trees)
.

Note that, in the successor case,
K_{\beta+1}
may naturally be considered as a quotient of
K_{\beta}
by the relation that two points
x,y\in K_{\beta}
are equivalent if only finitely many points of
K_{\beta}
lie between
x
and
y
.
Each equivalence class in
K_{\beta}
is a discrete, full, subpretree of
K_{\beta}
, which may be considered as the vertex set of a simplicial tree (see
[
Bow99d
, Lem. 3.34]
).
Two vertices are joined by an edge if and only if they are adjacent in
K_{\beta}
.

According to Proposition
7.10
, the action of
\Gamma
on
K_{0}=K
has no fixed point. We now consider
K_{1}
.
The following lemma depends on our assumption that the family
C
of cut points of
M
contains no parabolic points.

Lemma 8.9
.

The action of
\Gamma
on
K_{1}
does not have a fixed point.

Proof.

Suppose
\Gamma
has a fixed point
X
in
K_{1}
.
Since
K_{1}
is the quotient of
K
by the finite interval relation, the equivalence class
X
is a
\Gamma
–invariant discrete, full subpretree of
K
that may be identified with the set of vertices of a simplicial tree (see Remark
8.8
).
Observe that
X
must contain at least three distinct points. Indeed, as
\Gamma
has no fixed point,
X
contains more than one point. If
X
had exactly two points
a
and
b
, then they would be adjacent, since
X
is full. But
\Gamma
acts without inversions, so it would fix each of
a
and
b
, which is impossible.
Since
X
has three distinct points,
a
,
b
, and
c
, it also contains their median, which lies in the interior
K^{o}
of
K
.

We now consider the nonempty subpretree
X^{o}
obtained from
X
by removing all terminal points, and we consider
X^{o}
to be the set of vertices of a simplicial tree
T
. The action of
\Gamma
on
K^{o}
is non-nesting, relative to
\mathbb{P}
, and without inversions by Proposition
7.10
. Thus, the action of
\Gamma
on
T
is relative to
\mathbb{P}
by Lemma
6.6
and is also clearly without inversions.

By the construction of
K
, the tree
T
is bipartite with bipartition
V(T)=V_{0}(T)\sqcup V_{1}(T)
.
The set
V_{0}(T)
consists of members of
K
that are not contained in
C
, and the set
V_{1}(T)
consists of singleton sets
\{c\}
with
c\in C
.
Since the points of
C
are nonparabolic in the convergence group action on
M
, the stabilizer of a vertex
v\in V_{1}(T)
is either finite or loxodromic in the convergence group
\Gamma
.

We claim that a vertex
v\in V_{1}(T)
is incident to at most one edge of
T
with loxodromic stabilizer. Suppose, by way of contradiction, that edges
e,e^{\prime}
are adjacent to
v
and have loxodromic stabilizers
\Gamma_{e}
and
\Gamma_{e^{\prime}}
.
Since these loxodromic subgroups share a fixed point, they must be co-elementary. Thus there is a loxodromic element
g\in\Gamma
that stabilizes both
e
and
e^{\prime}
.
Suppose
v=\{c\}
for a cut point
c\in C
.
Then there exist
w,w^{\prime}\in V_{0}(T)
members of
K
adjacent to
\{c\}
such that
g
stabilizes
w
and
w^{\prime}
and also fixes
c
.
Let
y\in M
be the other fixed point of
g
. By Lemma
7.7
, every member of
K
stabilized by
g
lies in the closed interval
\bigl[[c],[y]\bigr]
.
But
w
and
w^{\prime}
are members of
K
fixed by
g
and
c
is between them, which is a contradiction.

Consider the tree
\bar{T}
formed from
T
by collapsing to a point each edge whose stabilizer is loxodromic. Then the preimage of each vertex under the collapse
T\to\bar{T}
is a subtree of diameter at most two, since it contains at most one vertex from
V_{0}(T)
. In particular,
T\to\bar{T}
is an equivariant quasi-isometry, so any subgroup of
\Gamma
fixing a vertex of
\bar{T}
also fixes a vertex of
T
.
But
\Gamma
acts on
\bar{T}
without inversions, relative to
\mathbb{P}
, and with only finite edge stabilizers, so
\Gamma
fixes a vertex of
\bar{T}
by our hypothesis that
\Gamma
does not split relative to
\mathbb{P}
over finite subgroups.
Hence,
\Gamma
also fixes a vertex of
T
, contradicting the fact that
\Gamma
has no fixed point in
K
.
∎

The following two lemmas are proved using arguments inspired by the proof of
[
Bow99d
, Thm. 6.1]
.

Lemma 8.10
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two adjacent points in the interior
{K}^{o}_{\alpha}
of
{K}_{\alpha}
for any ordinal
\alpha\geq 1
. Then the stabilizer of
\{X,Y\}
is either finite or a parabolic subgroup of the convergence action on
M
.

Proof.

In a convergence group, any subgroup with no loxodromic elements must be either finite or parabolic. We prove the lemma in contrapositive form, as follows.
Suppose the stabilizer of
\{X,Y\}
contains an infinite order element
\gamma
that acts loxodromically on
M
.
We show that either
X
or
Y
must be terminal in the pretree
K_{\alpha}
.

By Lemma
8.6
, one of the pairs, say
(Y,X)
, has a unique connector point
p=p(Y,X)
and the other pair
(X,Y)
has no connector point.
Recall that the points
X
and
Y
of
K_{\alpha}=K/\!\!\sim_{\alpha}
are subsets of
K
, and
p
is an element of
K
that is also an element of the set
Y
.
Since
\gamma
stabilizes
\{X,Y\}
, the point
p
must be fixed by
\gamma
.

If
x,y\in X
and we direct the intervals
[x,p]
and
[y,p]
so that
x<p
and
y<p
,
then the half-open intervals
[x,p)
and
[y,p)
are cofinal, since the median of
\{x,y,p\}
cannot equal
p
.
We claim that
X
contains a point of
K
that is fixed by
\gamma
.
Suppose, by way of contradiction, that
\gamma
does not fix any point of
X
.
Choose any
y\in X
and choose
x\in[y,p)\cap\bigl[\gamma(y),p\bigr)\subseteq X
.
Now,
\gamma(x)\in\gamma\bigl(y,p)\bigr)=\bigl[\gamma(y),p\bigr)
.
Since
x
and
\gamma(x)
are, by assumption, distinct, and both lie in
\bigl[\gamma(y),p\bigr)
, we must have either
x\in\bigl(p,\gamma(x)\bigr)
or
\gamma(x)\in(p,x)
.
As
p
,
x
, and
\gamma(x)
lie in the interior

, this arrangement violates the non-nesting property of

, established in Proposition
7.10
.

Let
q\in X
be a fixed point of
\gamma
. Since
q\notin Y
, we see that
p\not\sim_{\alpha}q
. It follows that
p\not\sim_{1}q
. In other words, in the pretree
K
, the interval
(p,q)
is infinite.
Since
\gamma
is loxodromic, we must be in the first case of Lemma

. Thus the classes of
p
and
q
in
K
are terminal, contradicting the assumption that
X
and
Y
are non-terminal.
∎

.

For each

, the action of

on

For the successor step, suppose

By way of contradiction, suppose

As explained in Remark

Combining the results established above gives the following conclusion.

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic with boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
.
Assume that
\Gamma
does not split relative to
\mathbb{P}
over a finite or parabolic subgroup.
If
M
has a nonparabolic cut point
c
with orbit

, then the induced action of
\Gamma
on the dendrite quotient

is a convergence group action without a fixed point.
The action on the interior of
D(M,C)
is non-nesting and relative to
\mathbb{P}
with finite interval stabilizers.

We now use the fact that the quotient
D(M,C)
of
M
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

We assume
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is nonelementary, since otherwise the result is trivial.
Thus the action of
\Gamma
on the compactum
M
is a minimal convergence group action.
Since
\Gamma
does not split over a finite or parabolic subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
, the group
\Gamma
is finitely generated (Theorem
2.16
) and the space
M
is connected (Proposition
5.1
) and metrizable (
[

]
).

As explained in Proposition

, the dendrite quotient

is isomorphic as a pretree to the stable pretree

.
Therefore, the action of
\Gamma
on
D
is nontrivial, and the action on its interior is non-nesting and relative to
\mathbb{P}
by Lemma
8.11
.
Since
D
is Hausdorff, the quotient
M\to D
is a closed map. As
\Gamma
has a minimal convergence group action on
M
, the induced action on
D
is also a minimal convergence group action.

Let
I=[x,y]
be a nondegenerate interval in the interior of
D
. The stabilizer of
I
has a subgroup
H
of index at most two fixing
x
and
y
. By non-nesting,
H
fixes
I
pointwise. As
H
is a convergence group on
M
with at least three fixed points,
H
is finite, so that
\Stab(I)
is finite as well.
∎

9.
From topological trees to metric and simplicial trees

In this section, we extend a theorem of Levitt from the setting of finitely presented groups to groups with a finite relative presentation. The main result of the section is Corollary
9.3
, which produces an action on a simplicial tree from a non-nesting action on a topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree.

Definition 9.1
.

Given a group pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
, we say that
\Gamma
is
finitely presented relative to
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
if there exists a finitely generated free group
F
such that
\Gamma
is the quotient of
P_{1}*\cdots*P_{k}*F
by the normal closure of a finite subset.

The following result was established for finitely presented groups by Levitt
[
Lev98
]
. We extend that result to certain relatively finitely presented groups by adapting a technique used in the proof of
[
GL15
, Thm. 9.9]
.

Proposition 9.2
.

Suppose the finitely generated group
\Gamma
is also finitely presented relative to a finite family of finitely generated subgroups
\mathbb{P}
.
If
\Gamma
admits a nontrivial non-nesting action on a topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R
relative to
\mathbb{P}
, then it admits a nontrivial isometric action on some
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R_{0}
relative to
\mathbb{P}
such that every subgroup fixing an interval in
R_{0}
also fixes an interval in
R
.

Proof.

The first step of the proof of
[
Lev98
, Thm. 1]
is to construct a certain resolution of the action on
R
whenever
\Gamma
is finitely presented. As in
[
GL15
]
, we show that such a resolution exists under the weaker hypothesis that
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
has a finite relative presentation and
\Gamma
and each
P\in\mathbb{P}
are finitely generated.
For each
P\in\mathbb{P}
, choose a presentation
P=\langle\,{S_{P}}\mid{\mathcal{W}_{P}}\,\rangle
with
S_{P}
finite.
Fix a presentation
\Gamma=\langle\,{S}\mid{\mathcal{W}}\,\rangle
such that
S
is a finite set containing each
S_{P}
and such that
\mathcal{W}
contains each
\mathcal{W}_{P}
together with a finite set
\mathcal{W}_{0}
of additional relators.

Let
K
be a subtree of
R
that is the convex hull of a finite set of points.
For each
s\in S
, let
K_{s}=K\cap s^{-1}K
and consider the restriction
\phi_{s}\colon K_{s}\to sK_{s}
of
s
.
Choose
K
large enough that each set
K_{s}
contains more than one point, that for each relator
r=s_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots s_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}}
in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
the domain of the map
\phi_{s_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots\phi_{s_{n}}^{\epsilon_{n}}
is nonempty, and that
K
contains a point
x_{P}
fixed by
P
for each
P\in\mathbb{P}
.

Build a foliated
2
–complex
\Sigma
by starting with
K
(foliated by points) and gluing bands
K_{s}\times[0,1]
(foliated by
\{*\}\times[0,1]
) to
K
so that
(x,0)
is glued to
x
and
(x,1)
is glued to
\phi_{s}(x)
.
Then
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
may be naturally identified with the free group on
S
.
By construction, each relator of
\mathcal{W}
is represented (up to free homotopy) by a loop contained in a leaf of
\Sigma
.
The natural homomorphism
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\Gamma
gives rise to a foliated covering space
p\colon\bar{\Sigma}_{\rho}\to\Sigma
.
Furthermore, there exists a unique
\Gamma
–equivariant
resolution map

f\colon\bar{\Sigma}_{\rho}\to R
that restricts to the covering map
p
on some fixed component of
p^{-1}(K)
and is constant on each leaf of the foliation.

The rest of the argument of
[
Lev98
]
follows without change using this resolution map.
The finite system of maps
\{\phi_{s}\}
is closed and non-nesting in the sense of
[
Lev98
]
since the action on
R
is non-nesting.
Given such a system, Levitt shows that
K
admits a probability measure
\mu
with no atom such that each
\phi_{s}
maps the restriction
\mu|K_{s}
onto
\mu|sK_{s}
.
The measure on
K
induces a transverse measure (perhaps not of full support) on the foliated cover
\bar{\Sigma}_{\rho}
. Integration of this measure along paths gives a length pseudometric on
\bar{\Sigma}_{\rho}
. Identifying points
u,v
with
d(u,v)=0
produces a metric
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R_{0}
on which
\Gamma
acts nontrivially and isometrically relative to
\mathbb{P}
such that every subgroup fixing an interval in
R_{0}
also fixes an interval in
R
.
∎

The fundamental structure theorem for stable actions of finitely presented groups on
\mathbb{R}
–trees is the splitting theorem of Bestvina–Feighn
[
BF95
]
.
A theorem of Osin
[
Osi06b
]
states that every relatively hyperbolic pair
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively finitely presented, and if
\Gamma
is finitely generated then so is each
P\in\mathbb{P}
.
A relatively hyperbolic analogue of the Bestvina–Feighn structure theorem due to Guirardel–Levitt
[
GL15
]
gives the following corollary.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic with
\Gamma
finitely generated.
If
\Gamma
acts nontrivially by non-nesting homeomorphisms on a topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R
relative to
\mathbb{P}
with elementary arc stabilizers, then
\Gamma
splits over an elementary subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
. ∎

10.
Cut points and local connectedness

This section contains the proof of the main theorem using the ingredients established above. Broadly speaking, we follow a strategy analogous to that of Swarup and Bowditch
[
Swa96
,
Bow99a
,
Bow99b
,
Bow01
]
.
Various components of Swarup and Bowditch’s proofs require significant extension to apply in the general relatively hyperbolic setting.
In this section, we prove Proposition
10.1
, a key accessibility result for splittings over two-ended groups.
Many other such extensions are established in the sections above.
Once we have these extensions in place, the local connectedness proof follows the overall plan of the original proofs due to Swarup and Bowditch.

If
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic, let
\mathbb{L}
be the family of all loxodromic subgroups,
i.e.
, subgroups that are virtually infinite cyclic and not parabolic.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic such that
\Gamma
is one ended relative to
\mathbb{P}
.

(1)

Let
T
be the tree of cylinders associated to any
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree. Then each vertex stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}=\Inc_{v}\cup\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
, as defined in Proposition
2.14
.

(2)

Suppose
\Gamma
does not split over parabolic subgroups relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
Then there exists an
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree
T_{\ell}
that is equal to its tree of cylinders, such that each vertex stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
is finitely generated and does not split over loxodromic subgroups relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is given by Proposition
2.14
.
We now consider the second assertion.
We may assume
\Gamma
is nonelementary, since the result is trivial in the elementary case.
Let
\mathbb{E}
be the family of all elementary subgroups of
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
. By hypothesis, any
(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{P})
–tree is actually an
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree.
Furthermore,
\Gamma
and all members of
\mathbb{P}
are finitely generated by Theorem
2.16
.
The JSJ tree of cylinders over
\mathbb{E}
relative to
\mathbb{P}
given by Guirardel–Levitt
[
GL17
, Cor. 9.20]
is an
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree
T
with the following property.
If
v
is any vertex of
T
, either
\Gamma_{v}
has no splittings over loxodromic subgroups relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
or
\Gamma_{v}
is an extension with finite kernel of the fundamental group of a compact hyperbolic
2
–orbifold
\Sigma_{v}
with geodesic boundary and the subgroups
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
are the lifts to
\Gamma_{v}
of the fundamental groups of the boundary components of
\Sigma_{v}
.
In the orbifold case, every loxodromic splitting of
\Gamma_{v}
relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
is induced by a splitting of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
dual to a family of disjoint, essential simple closed geodesics of
\Sigma_{v}
by Morgan–Shalen
[
MS84
, Thm. III.2.6]
.
A standard argument involving reduction of Euler characteristic shows that
\Sigma_{v}
may be cut along some finite family of geodesics into pieces containing no essential simple closed geodesic.
Thus, we may refine
T
using splittings of its vertex groups to produce an
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree
T_{\ell}
each of whose vertex groups
\Gamma_{v}
does not split relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
.

Finally we observe that each vertex stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
of
T_{\ell}
is finitely generated.
Indeed,
\Gamma_{v}
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
, but each member of
\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
is conjugate in
\Gamma
to a member of
\mathbb{P}
and each member of
\Inc_{v}
is virtually cyclic.
In particular,
\Gamma_{v}
is hyperbolic relative to finitely generated subgroups, which implies that
\Gamma_{v}
is finitely generated (see
[
Osi06b
,
GP15
]
).
∎

For the next two lemmas, we work under the following assumptions.
Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic.
Assume
\Gamma
does not split relative to
\mathbb{P}
over a finite or parabolic subgroup.
We note that, by Proposition
5.1
the boundary
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is connected.
Furthermore, by Theorem
2.16
the group
\Gamma
is finitely generated.
Suppose the boundary
M
has a nonparabolic cut point
c
with orbit
C=\Gamma(c)
in
M
.
Let
R
be the topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree obtained by removing all terminal points from the dendrite
D(M,C)
.

Lemma 10.2
.

Suppose
\Gamma
splits over a loxodromic subgroup
H
relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
Then the induced action of
H
on the topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Recall that the dendrite
D
is formed from
M
by identifying two points
x
and
y
if the collection of points of
C
separating them does not contain a subset order-isomorphic to the rational numbers.
The action of
\Gamma
on
M
is a convergence group action, and, by Proposition
8.12
, the induced action of
\Gamma
on
D
is also a convergence group action.

By a result of Haulmark–Hruska about splittings of relatively hyperbolic groups over elementary subgroups
[
HH23
, Cor. 6.8]
, the two-point limit set
\Lambda H
is not separated by any cut point of
M
.
In particular, the quotient map
M\to D
sends
\Lambda H
to a single point
z
fixed by
\Gamma_{v}
.
Let
h\in H
be an infinite order element.
If
h
had another fixed point
w\in D
, its preimage in
M
would be a closed
h
–invariant set not intersecting the limit set of
\langle h\rangle
in
M
, which is impossible in a convergence group action (see
[
Tuk94
, §2]
). Here, we use that
D
is a
T_{1}
–space, so that the preimage of

in
M
is closed.
Thus

has

as its unique fixed point in
D
.

We claim that
z
lies in the interior
R
.
Recall that the action of
\Gamma
on
R
is non-nesting by
Proposition
8.12
.
As in the proof of Proposition
7.10
, if the fixed point

of

does not lie in

, then
[
Lev98
, Thm. 3]
implies that
h
fixes a pair of terminal points of

that are the endpoints of an axis.
Since
z
is the unique fixed point of

in

, we conclude that
z
must lie in
R
.
∎

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
T_{\ell}
be an
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree
as in Proposition
10.1
such that each vertex group
\Gamma_{v}
does not split over loxodromic subgroups relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
.
Then the induced action of each
\Gamma_{v}
on the topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree
R
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma_{v}
is finitely generated and hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}=\Inc_{v}\cup\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
.
By Proposition
8.12
, the action of
\Gamma
on
R
is relative to
\mathbb{P}
, so that the restricted action of
\Gamma_{v}
on
R
is relative to
\mathbb{P}_{|\Gamma_{v}}
.
The subgroups
\Inc_{v}
are also finitely generated, since they are virtually cyclic.
By Lemma
10.2
, the action on
R
is also relative to the family of loxodromic edge stabilizers
\Inc_{v}
.

If the action of
\Gamma_{v}
on
R
had no fixed point, it would be a nontrivial non-nesting action relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
such that every interval stabilized by
\Gamma_{v}
has a finite stabilizer by Proposition
8.12
.
But then
\Gamma_{v}
would split over an elementary subgroup relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
by Corollary

.
Any splitting of
\Gamma_{v}
relative to
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
over a finite or parabolic subgroup extends to a splitting of

\mathbb{P}
over finite or parabolic subgroups, so

has no such splitting.
Thus
\Gamma_{v}
must split over a loxodromic subgroup relative to

, contradicting the choice of
T_{\ell}
.
∎

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic.
Suppose
\Gamma
does not split over a finite or parabolic subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
Then
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is connected and every cut point of
M
is a parabolic point.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
2.16
, the space
M
is connected and the group
\Gamma
is finitely generated.
We suppose, by way of contradiction that
M
has a nonparabolic cut point
c
with orbit
C=\Gamma(c)
.
Let
D=D(M,C)
be the dendrite quotient of
M
given by Proposition
8.12
.
If
R
is the topological
\mathbb{R}
–tree obtained from
D
by removing all terminal points, then each interval of
R
is stabilized by a finite subgroup of
\Gamma
.
In particular, an infinite subgroup of
\Gamma
cannot fix more than one point of
R
.

Consider the simplicial
(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{P})
–tree
T_{\ell}
given by Proposition
10.1
. For each vertex
v
of
T_{\ell}
, the stabilizer
\Gamma_{v}
has a fixed point
p_{v}
in
R
. Since
\Gamma_{v}
is infinite, its fixed point

must be unique.
For any edge

of
T_{\ell}
joining vertices
w
and
w
, the group
\Gamma_{e}
stabilizes the arc
[p_{v},p_{w}]
in
R
.
Since
\Gamma_{e}
is also infinite, it follows that the points
p_{v}
and
p_{w}
fixed by
\Gamma_{v}
and
\Gamma_{w}
must be equal, so all vertex groups have the same unique fixed point

in

Since

for any

and any vertex

This conclusion contradicts Proposition

\Gamma
does not have a fixed point in the dendrite

.
∎

Recall that a
Peano continuum
is a compact, connected, locally connected, metrizable space.
Extending a result of Bestvina–Mess
[
BM91
]
, Bowditch shows in
[
Bow01
, §9]
that a relatively hyperbolic group pair satisfying the conclusion of Proposition
10.4
has locally connected boundary.
We provide details for the benefit of the reader.

Proposition 10.5
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic.
Suppose
\Gamma
does not split over a finite or parabolic subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
.
Then
M=\partial(\Gamma,\mathbb{P})
is a Peano continuum without cut points.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated,
M
is the Gromov boundary of a certain
\delta
–hyperbolic space
X
that has constant horospherical distortion in the sense of Bowditch
[
Bow99a
]
.
In particular,
M
is metrizable.

Suppose
M
is not locally connected.
Since
M
is connected,
[
Bow99a
, Thm. 1.1]
implies that the space
X
has a separating horoball based at a point
\eta\in M
.
The base point of any separating horoball in
X
is a cut point of
M=\partial X
(see
[
HR
, Prop. 3.4]
).
By Proposition
10.4
, the cut point
\eta
is a parabolic point of
M
.
However, since
\Gamma
does not split over a parabolic subgroup relative to
\mathbb{P}
, the space
X
cannot have a separating horoball based at a parabolic point
[
Bow99a
, Thm. 1.2]
.
We conclude that
M
is locally connected, so that

is a Peano continuum.

Finally, if the Peano continuum
M
had a cut point
\eta
, then by
Proposition
10.4

such a point would be parabolic. By
[
Bow99a
, Prop. 5.1]
the space
X
would contain a separating horoball based at
\eta
, which is impossible.
∎

The proof of Theorem

.

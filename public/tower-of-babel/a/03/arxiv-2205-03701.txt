A high-order predictor-corrector method for initial value problems with fractional derivative involving Mittag-Leffler kernel: epidemic model case study Journal: a Q1 journal
Sami Aljhani a , Mohd Salmi Md Noorani a , Redouane Douaifia b , Salem Abdelmalek b,c

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a numerical scheme of the predictor-corrector type for solving nonlinear fractional initial value problems, the chosen fractional derivative is called the Atangana-Baleanu derivative defined in Caputo sense (ABC). This proposed method is based on Lagrangian quadratic polynomials to approximate the nonlinearity implied in the Volterra integral which is obtained by reducing the given fractional differential equation via the properties of the ABC-fractional derivative. Through this technique, we get corrector formula with high accuracy which is implicit as well as predictor formula which is explicit and has the same precision order as the corrective formula. On the other hand, the so-called memory term is computed only once for both prediction and correction phases, which indicates the low cost of the proposed method. Also, the error bound of the proposed numerical scheme is offered. Furthermore, numerical experiments are presented in order to assess the accuracy of the new method on two differential equations. Moreover, a case study is considered where the proposed predictor-corrector scheme is used to obtained approximate solutions of ABC-fractional generalized SI (susceptible-infectious) epidemic model for the purpose of analyzing dynamics of the suggested system as well as demonstrating the effectiveness of the new method to solve systems dealing with real-world problems.

2
Preliminaries

The Atangana-Baleanu fractional derivative in the Caputo sense (ABC) is
defined as (cf.
[
6
]
):

{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}f(t)=\displaystyle\frac{AB(\alpha)}{1-\alpha}\int^{t}_{0}f^{\prime}(s)\textbf{E}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\alpha}{1-\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha}\right)ds,

(1)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
AB(\alpha)>0
is a normalization function obeying

AB(0)=AB(1)=1

\left(\text{e.g. }AB(\alpha)=1-\alpha+\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\right)
,
\textbf{E}_{\alpha}(.)
denotes the Mittag-Leffler function of order
\alpha
defined by

\begin{matrix}\textbf{E}_{\alpha}(z)=\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{{z}^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)},&z,\alpha\in\mathbb{C},&\text{and }Re(\alpha)>0,\end{matrix}

(2)

and
\Gamma(.)
denotes Gamma function, defined as

\begin{matrix}\Gamma(\alpha)=\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}dt,&\qquad Re(\alpha)>0.\end{matrix}

(3)

The fractional integral for the ABC, which is newly defined with a nonlocal kernel and does not have singularities at
t=s
, is
defined as follows (cf.
[
27
]
):

\displaystyle{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}I^{\alpha}_{t}f(t)=\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f(t)+\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}f(s)(t-s)^{\alpha-1}ds.

(4)

We consider the initial-value problem with Atangana-Baleanu derivative

\begin{cases}&{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}y\left(t\right)=f\left(t,y\left(t\right)\right),\qquad\qquad\qquad 0<t<T<\infty,\\
\\
&\qquad\quad\,y\left(0\right)=y_{0},\end{cases}

(5)

where
f
is a smooth nonlinear function that guarantees the existence of a unique solution for (
5
), with the fractional order
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
, and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. A continuous function
y(t)
is the solution of (
5
) if and only if it is the solution of following Voltera-integral equation:

\begin{matrix}y(t)&=\displaystyle y_{0}+\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f(t,y(t))+\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}f\left(s,y(s)\right)\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}ds.\end{matrix}

(6)

At point
t_{n+1}=h(n+1)
,
n=0,1,\dots,N\in\mathbb{N}
with
h=\frac{T}{N}
, we get

\begin{split}y_{n+1}&=y_{0}+\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f_{n+1}+\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}f\left(s,y(s)\right)\left(t_{n+1}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}ds\\
&=y_{0}+\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f_{n+1}+\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{n}}f\left(s,y(s)\right)\left(t_{n+1}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}ds+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}f\left(s,y(s)\right)\left(t_{n+1}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}ds\right)\\
&=y_{0}+\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f_{n+1}+y^{lag}_{n+1}+y^{inc}_{n+1},\end{split}

(7)

where,
y^{lag}_{n+1}
and
y^{inc}_{n+1}
are called lag term and increment term respectively, which take the following forms:

y^{lag}_{n+1}:=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,y(s))ds,

(8)

and

y^{inc}_{n+1}:=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,y(s))ds.

(9)

3
Predictor-corrector scheme with quadratic interpolation

To start presenting the proposed numerical method to solve the initial-value problem (
5
), we need the following lemma:

Lemma 1

(
[
18
]
)
Assume that
\psi

\in

\mathbb{P}_{2}([0,T])
, where
\mathbb{P}_{2}([0,T])
is the space of all polynomials of degree less than or equal to two. Let
\psi_{k}
,
k=0,....,N
be the restricted value of
\psi(t)
on
t_{k}
(
0\leq k\leq N
). Then there exist reals
b^{0}_{n+1},b^{1}_{n+1}
and
b^{2}_{n+1}
, such that

\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}\psi(s)ds=B\sum_{j=0}^{2}b^{j}_{n+1}\psi_{n+j-2}.

(10)

Where,
B=\displaystyle\frac{h^{\alpha}}{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)},b^{0}_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha+4}{2},b^{1}_{n+1}=-2(\alpha+3),b^{2}_{n+1}=\frac{2\alpha^{2}+9\alpha+12}{2}
.

Now, we use quadratic Lagrange polynomial of
f(s,y(s))
over the intervals
[t_{i-1},t_{i+1}]
,
1\leq i\leq N-1
:

\displaystyle f(s,y(s))\approx\sum_{l=i-1}^{i+1}f_{l}q^{i}_{l}(s),

(11)

where

\displaystyle q^{i}_{l}(s)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=i-1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{i+1}\frac{s-t_{k}}{t_{l}-t_{k}}.

(12)

But, on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
we can interpolate
f(s,y(s))
with the points
\big\{{t_{0},t_{\frac{1}{2}},t_{1}}\big\}
, then we get

\displaystyle f(s,y(s))\approx f_{0}q^{0}_{0}(s)+f_{\frac{1}{2}}q^{0}_{\frac{1}{2}}(s)+f_{1}q^{0}_{1}(s),

(13)

where

\displaystyle q^{0}_{0}(s)=\displaystyle\frac{(s-t_{0})(s-t_{\frac{1}{2}})}{(t_{1}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{\frac{1}{2}})},\ q^{0}_{\frac{1}{2}}(s)=\frac{(s-t_{0})(s-t_{1})}{(t_{\frac{1}{2}}-t_{1})(t_{\frac{1}{2}}-t_{1})},\ q^{0}_{1}(s)=\frac{(s-t_{0})(s-t_{\frac{1}{2}})}{(t_{1}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{\frac{1}{2}})}.

(14)

The approximation of
y(t_{n+1})
denoting by
\widetilde{y}_{n+1}
, according to (
11
)-(
14
),
\widetilde{y}_{n+1}
takes the following form:

eq216\widetilde{y}_{n+1}=\displaystyle y_{0}+{}^{AB}\widetilde{f}^{P}_{n+1}+\widetilde{y}^{lag}_{n+1}+\widetilde{y}^{inc}_{n+1},

(15)

where

{}^{AB}\widetilde{f}^{P}_{n+1}=\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}f(t_{n+1},\widetilde{y}^{P}_{n+1}),

(16)

\widetilde{y}^{lag}_{n+1}=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2}a^{j,0}_{n+1}\widetilde{f}_{\frac{j}{2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a^{j,k}_{n+1}\widetilde{f}_{k+j-1}\right)

(17)

and

\widetilde{y}^{inc}_{n+1}=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{1}a^{j,n}_{n+1}\widetilde{f}_{n+j-1}+a^{2,n}_{n+1}\widetilde{f}^{P}_{n+1}\right)

(18)

with

\begin{matrix}a^{j,k}_{n+1}=\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}q^{k}_{\widehat{j}}(s)ds,&\textit{for}&j\in\left\{0,1,2\right\},\end{matrix}

(19)

where,

\widehat{j}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}&\text{if }k=0,\\
j+k-1&\text{if }1\leq k\leq n.\end{cases}

(20)

The predictor term can be approximated as follows:

\widetilde{y}^{P}_{n+1}=y_{0}+{}^{AB}\widetilde{f}^{apprx}_{n+1}+\widetilde{y}^{lag}_{n+1}+\frac{\alpha h^{\alpha}}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha+3)}\sum_{j=0}^{2}b^{j}_{n+1}\widetilde{f}_{n+j-2},

(21)

where

{}^{AB}\widetilde{f}^{apprx}_{n+1}=\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}\left(f_{n-2}-3f_{n-1}-3f_{n}\right),\hskip 15.0ptn\geq 2.

(22)

where,

\displaystyle T^{C}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,y(s))ds-AB_{g}\sum_{j=0}^{2}a^{j,0}_{n+1}f_{\frac{j}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -AB_{g}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a^{j,k}_{n+1}f_{k+j-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{1}a^{j,n}_{n+1}f_{n+j-1}+a^{2,n}_{n+1}f^{P}_{n+1}\right)\Biggl|,

(52)

with,
AB_{g}=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}
.

Proof 3

It follows immediately that,

\displaystyle T^{C}_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq AB_{g}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}\Bigl|f(s,y(s))-\sum_{j=0}^{2}q^{0}_{\frac{j}{2}}(s)f_{\frac{j}{2}}\Bigl|ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +AB_{g}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}\Bigl|f(s,y(s))-\sum_{j=0}^{2}q^{k}_{k+j-1}(s)f_{k+j-1}\Bigl|ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +AB_{g}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha-1}\Bigl|f(s,y(s))-\sum_{j=0}^{1}q^{n}_{n+j-1}(s)f_{n+j-1}-q^{n}_{n+1}(s)f^{P}_{n+1}\Bigl|ds.

(53)

Thus, according to the lemma
2
, there exist
\widehat{C},\widetilde{C}>0
such that

T^{C}_{n+1}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}T^{\alpha}}{6AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}h^{3}+\frac{\widehat{C}h^{\alpha}}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}E^{P}_{n+1}+O(h^{3}).

(54)

The desired estimate (
3
) can be derived by direct consideration of the last inequality (
54
).

Theorem 4

(Global Error of the proposed method) With the same assumptions as those of Theorem
2
. Then, we have

E_{n+1}:=\left|y_{n+1}-\widetilde{y}_{n+1}\right|\leq Ch^{3},

(55)

where
C>0
(independent of grid parameters), given
E_{1},E_{2}\leq\widehat{C}_{1}h^{3}
, and
E_{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\widehat{C}_{2}h^{3-\alpha}
, with
\widehat{C}_{1},\widehat{C}_{2}>0
.

Proof 4

By taking into account the previous results, there exist
C_{j}>0
(with
j=1,\dots,10
), such that

\displaystyle E_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq T^{C}_{n+1}+AB_{g}\left|f^{P}_{n+1}-\widetilde{f}^{P}_{n+1}\right|+\frac{L(1-\alpha)}{AB(\alpha)}E^{P}_{n+1}+AB_{g}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left|a^{j,0}_{n+1}\right|\left|f_{\frac{j}{2}}-\widetilde{f}_{\frac{j}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +AB_{g}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left|a^{j,k}_{n+1}\right|\left|f_{k+j-1}-\widetilde{f}_{k+j-1}\right|+AB_{g}\sum_{j=1}^{1}\left|a^{j,n}_{n+1}\right|\left|f_{n+j-1}-\widetilde{f}_{n+j-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq T^{C}_{n+1}+\frac{L(1-\alpha)}{AB(\alpha)}E^{P}_{n+1}+AB_{g}Lh^{\alpha}E_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{1}h^{\alpha}}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}E^{P}_{n+1}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +AB_{g}\widetilde{C}_{2}Lh^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)^{\alpha-1}E_{k}+O(h^{3})

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{3}h^{3}+\widetilde{C}_{4}E^{P}_{n+1}+\frac{L(1-\alpha)}{AB(\alpha)}E^{P}_{n+1}+AB_{g}Lh^{\alpha}E_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{\widetilde{C}_{5}h^{\alpha}}{AB(\alpha)}E^{p}_{n+1}+AB_{g}\widetilde{C}_{6}Lh^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)^{\alpha-1}E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{7}h^{3}+\widetilde{C}_{8}T^{P}_{n+1}+\widetilde{C}_{9}h^{\alpha}E_{\frac{1}{2}}+\widetilde{C}_{10}h^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)^{\alpha-1}E_{k}.

(56)

Consequently, by the discrete Gronwall’s inequality (i.e. lemma
4
) the desired result holds.

{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}v(t)=\gamma u(t)\varphi(v(t))-\sigma v(t),&t>0,\\
u(0)=u_{0}>0,\ v(0)=v_{0}>0,\end{cases}

(64)

where
u(t)
,
v(t)
represent the numbers of susceptible individuals, and infective individuals at time t, respectively.
\Lambda
is the birth rate of the population,
\gamma
is the disease transmission rate,
\mu
is the natural death rate, and
\sigma:=\widetilde{\sigma}+\mu
, with
\widetilde{\sigma}
is the death rate due to disease, which all are positive real numbers, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The incidence function
\varphi(v)
introduces a nonlinear relation between the susceptible individuals and infective individuals. We suppose that
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and satisfies:

\begin{cases}\varphi(0)=0,\\
0<v\varphi^{\prime}(v)\leq\varphi(v),&\forall v>0.\end{cases}

(65)

According to the above condition and Lemma 1 in
[
30
]
, it is classical task to prove the existence of unique solution for system (
64
) (see e.g.
[
31
,
32
]
). As well as, the system (
64
) has a disease-free equilibrium
E_{0}=(u^{0},0)
with
u^{0}:=\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}
, and a unique endemic equilibrium
E^{*}:=(u^{*},v^{*})
if
R_{0}:=\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda\gamma}{\mu\sigma}\varphi^{\prime}(0)>1
(cf.
[
29
]
). In the remainder, the following notation
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=\left[0,+\infty\right)
will be useful. The feasible region of the suggested model (
64
) is given by

\Upsilon:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:x_{1}+x_{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right\}.

(66)

Now, we investigate the global asymptotic stability of the equilibrium points
E_{0}
and
E^{*}
, which is determined by the reproduction number
R_{0}
, as a result, the cases
R_{0}\leqslant 1
and
R_{0}>1
are treated independently.

Firstly, we provide the global stability result of the disease-free equilibrium
E_{0}
.

Theorem 5

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
R_{0}\leqslant 1
. Assume that (
65
) holds. Then, the disease-free equilibrium
E_{0}
is globally asympotatically stable in the feasible region
\Upsilon
.

Proof 5

We begin by rewriting the first equation of the system (
64
), with taking into account this relationship
\Lambda=\mu u^{0}
, such that

{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)=-\Big(\mu+\gamma\varphi(v)\Big)(u-u_{0})-\gamma\varphi(v)u_{0}

(67)

We consider the following candidate Lyapunov function:

V:\left\{(u,v)\in\Upsilon:u>0\right\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},

(68)

such that

V(u,v)=\frac{(u-u^{0})^{2}}{2u^{0}}+v.

(69)

Thanks to the property of ABC-fractional derivative given in Lemma 3.1 from
[
33
]
, we get

\begin{split}{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}V\leq\frac{(u-u^{0})}{u^{0}}{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)+{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}v(t).\end{split}

(70)

Then, by employing (
64
)-(
66
), and Lemma 1
[
30
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}V\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\mu+\gamma\varphi(v)\right)\frac{(u-u^{0})^{2}}{u^{0}}-\gamma u^{0}\varphi(v)+\gamma u\varphi(v)-\sigma v,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\mu+\gamma\varphi(v)\right)\frac{(u-u^{0})^{2}}{u^{0}}+\gamma\varphi^{\prime}(0)u^{0}v-\sigma v,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\mu+\gamma\varphi(v)\right)\frac{(u-u^{0})^{2}}{u^{0}}+\left(\frac{\gamma\Lambda\varphi^{\prime}(0)}{\mu}-\sigma\right)v,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\mu+\gamma\varphi(v)\right)\frac{(u-u^{0})^{2}}{u^{0}}-\sigma\left(1-R_{0}\right)v.

(71)

Therefore,
R_{0}\leqslant 1
, guarantees that
{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}V\leq 0
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Consequently, by the Lyapunov stability theorem (see Theorem 2.7 from
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
), we conclude that the disease-free equilibrium
E^{0}
is globally asymptotically stable in
\Upsilon
.

Secondly, we provide the global stability result of the endemic equilibrium
E^{*}
. Before do that, we state a lemma which will be useful (see Lemma 2 from
[
29
]
):

Lemma 6

Assume that the function
\varphi
satisfied (
65
) and

\begin{matrix}F(x)=x-1-ln(x),&\forall x>0.\end{matrix}

(72)

We thus get the following inequality

\begin{matrix}F\left(\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(v)}{\varphi(v^{*})}\right)\leq F\left(\displaystyle\frac{v}{v^{*}}\right),&\forall v>0,\end{matrix}

(73)

where
v^{*}
is the second component of the equilibrium point
E^{*}
.

Theorem 6

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
R_{0}>1
. Suppose that (
65
) holds. Then, the endemic equilibrium
E^{*}
is globally asymptotically stable in interior of the feasible region
\Upsilon
.

Proof 6

The first two equation of system (
64
) at
E^{*}
, clearly give

\begin{cases}\Lambda=\mu u^{*}+\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*}),\\
v^{*}\sigma=\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*}).\end{cases}

(74)

It follows that

\begin{cases}{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}u=\mu u^{*}\Big(1-\displaystyle\frac{u}{u^{*}}\Big)+\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{u\varphi(v)}{u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})}\right),\\
{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}v=\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(\displaystyle\frac{u\varphi(v)}{u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})}-\displaystyle\frac{v}{v^{*}}\right).\end{cases}

(75)

We consider the following candidate Lyapunov function:

L:\left\{(u,v)\in\Upsilon:u>0,v>0\right\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},

(76)

such that

L(u,v)=u^{*}F\left(\displaystyle\frac{u}{u^{*}}\right)+v^{*}F\left(\displaystyle\frac{v}{v^{*}}\right).

(77)

We now apply the property of ABC-fractional derivative given in Lemma 3.2 from
[
33
]
, to obtain

{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}L\leq\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{u^{*}}{u}\right){}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}u+\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{v^{*}}{v}\right){}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}v

(78)

From (
75
) and by immediately computation, we get

\displaystyle{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}L

\displaystyle\leq\mu u^{*}\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{u}{u^{*}}\right)\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{u^{*}}{u}\right)+\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(\displaystyle\frac{u\varphi(v)}{u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})}-\frac{v}{v^{*}}\right)\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{v^{*}}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(1-\frac{u\varphi(v)}{u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})}\right)\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{u^{*}}{u}\right),

\displaystyle\leq-\mu u^{*}\left(F\Big(\frac{u}{u^{*}}\Big)+F\left(\frac{u^{*}}{u}\right)\right)-\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(F\left(\frac{u^{*}}{u}\right)+F\left(\frac{v^{*}u\varphi(v)}{u^{*}v\varphi(v^{*})}\right)\right)

(79)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\gamma u^{*}\varphi(v^{*})\left(F\left(\frac{\varphi(v)}{\varphi(v^{*})}\right)-F\left(\frac{v}{v^{*}}\right)\right).

(80)

Therefore, the nonnegativity of function
F
(defined in (
72
)) on
(0,+\infty)
, and the lemma
6
ensure that
{}^{ABC}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\,0}D^{\alpha}_{t}L\leq 0
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Consequently, by the Lyapunov stability theorem (see Theorem 2.7 and Theorem 2.8
[
33
]
as well as
[
34
]
), we get the desired result.

Appendix A. Start-up of the Scheme

To find a desired accuracy for
\widetilde{y}_{1}
and
\widetilde{y}_{2}
, we find the approximate solutions at points
t_{\frac{1}{4}}
and
t_{\frac{1}{2}}
using the constant, linear and quadratic interpolation. Let
I^{0}(f_{a})
be the constant interpolation of
f
at point
t=a
that is
I^{0}(f_{a})=f(a)
. Let
I^{1}(f_{a},f_{b})
and
I^{2}(f_{a},f_{b},f_{c})
be linear and quadratic interpolation of
f
with grids
(a,b)
and
(a,b,c)
respectively. In the algorithm below we describe how to find the approximate solution of
y(t)
at points
t_{\frac{1}{4}},t_{\frac{1}{2}},t_{1},
and
t_{2}
(i.e.
\widetilde{y}_{\frac{1}{4}},\widetilde{y}_{\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{y}_{1},
and
\widetilde{y}_{2}
) using a predictor-corrector scheme:

1.

Approximate solution of
y(t)
at point
t_{\frac{1}{4}}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P}_{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}(t_{\frac{1}{4}},\widetilde{y}_{0})+AB_{y}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}(t_{\frac{1}{4}}-s)^{\alpha-1}I^{0}(\widetilde{f}_{0})ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P}_{\frac{1}{4}}+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{\frac{1}{4}}}I^{1}(\widetilde{f}_{0},\widetilde{f}^{P}_{\frac{1}{4}})(t_{\frac{1}{4}}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds.

2.

Approximate solution of
y(t)
at point
t_{\frac{1}{2}}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}(2\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{4}}-\widetilde{f}_{0})+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}I^{0}(\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{4}})(t_{\frac{1}{2}}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{2}}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}}+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}I^{1}(\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{4}},\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}})(t_{\frac{1}{2}}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{\frac{1}{2}}+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}I^{2}(\widetilde{f}_{0},\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{4}},\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{\frac{1}{2}})(t_{\frac{1}{2}}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds.

3.

Approximate solution of
y(t)
at point
t_{1}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{1}}_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}(6\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{2}}-8\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{4}}-3\widetilde{f}_{0})+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}I^{0}(\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{2}})(t_{n}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{2}}_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{1}+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}I^{1}(\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{1})(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{1}+AB_{g}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}I^{2}(\widetilde{f}_{0},\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{2}},\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{1})(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds.

4.

Approximate solution of
y(t)
at point
t_{2}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{1}}_{2}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}(6\widetilde{f}_{1}-8\widetilde{f}_{\frac{1}{2}}-3\widetilde{f}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t_{2}}I^{0}(\widetilde{f}_{1})(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}^{P_{2}}_{2}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{2}}I^{1}(\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}^{P_{1}}_{2})(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds,

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{2}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+AB_{f}\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{2}}I^{2}(\widetilde{f}_{0},\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}^{P_{2}}_{2})(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha-1}ds.

where

\Biggl\{AB_{f}=\frac{1-\alpha}{AB(\alpha)}\,\,\,\,\,\textit{and}\,\,\,\,\,AB_{g}=\frac{\alpha}{AB(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\Biggr\}.

Light-cone sum rules study on the purely non-factorizable Λ c + → Ξ 0 ​ K + \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+} decay
Yu-Ji Shi, Zhen-Xing Zhao

Abstract
We investigate the purely non-factorizable Λ c + → Ξ 0 ​ K + \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+} decay using light-cone sum rules. To extract the decay amplitudes, a three-point correlation function is defined and calculated at hadron and quark-gluon level, respectively. Both the W-exchange and the W-inward emission diagrams are considered in the quark-gluon level calculation, where the two-particle light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) of kaon are used as non-perturbative input. We obtain the decay amplitudes contributed from the twist-2 and twist-3 kaon LCDAs, respectively. The obtained P-wave amplitude is consistent with those predicted in the literature, while the S-wave one is much smaller. This significant difference between the S- and P -wave amplitudes leads to small up-down spin asymmetry, closing to the experimental measurement.

Such decay process is intriguing due to its two-body nature and the appearance of non-factorizable contributions. II
Correlation function in LCSR

In this section, we construct a correlation function to calculate the decay amplitudes of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+}
in the framework of LCSR. The corresponding effective Hamiltonian for this decay is

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm eff}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cs}V_{ud}^{*}\left(C_{1}{\cal O}_{1}+C_{2}{\cal O}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{1}=

\displaystyle\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})c\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})d,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2}=

\displaystyle\bar{s}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})c_{b}\ \bar{u}_{b}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})d_{a},

(1)

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are Wilson coefficients,
{\cal O}_{1,2}
are the four-fermion operators, and the subscripts
a,b
denote color indices. The transition matrix element of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+}
induced by
{\cal O}_{1,2}
can be generally parameterized by two amplitudes
A^{1,2}
and
B^{1,2}
as

\displaystyle\langle\Xi^{0}(p-q)K^{+}(q)|{\cal O}_{i}(0)|\Lambda_{c}^{+}(p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ i\ \bar{u}_{\Xi}(p-q)(A^{i}-B^{i}\gamma_{5})u_{\Lambda_{c}}(p).

(2)

Note that since all the external states are on shell, as functions of the external invariant momentum squares,
A^{i}
and
B^{i}
are just constants only depending on the external state masses:
m_{\Xi},m_{K},m_{\Lambda_{c}}
. In this work, we use
A^{i}_{\rm WE},B^{i}_{\rm WE}
and
A^{i}_{\rm IE},B^{i}_{\rm IE}
to denote the contributions from the WE and IE diagrams as shown in Fig.
1
, respectively.

In the framework of LCSR, to calculate a transition matrix element one has to construct an appropriate correlation function, which will be calculated both at the hadron and quark-gluon level. In the case of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+}
, the correlation function is chosen as

\displaystyle\Pi^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k)=

\displaystyle\ i^{2}\int d^{4}x\ e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x}\int d^{4}y\ e^{i(p-k)\cdot y}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|T\left\{J_{\Xi}(y){\cal O}_{i}(0)\bar{J}_{\Lambda_{c}}(x)\right\}|K^{-}(q)\rangle,

(3)

where the hadron currents are defined as
[
54
,
24
]

\displaystyle J_{\Xi}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(s_{i}^{T}C\gamma^{\mu}s_{j}\right)\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}Q_{k},

\displaystyle J_{\Lambda_{c}}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(u_{i}^{T}C\gamma_{5}d_{j}\right)Q_{k}.

(4)

Instead of setting the kaon as a final state, using the approach proposed in Ref.
[
50
]
, in Eq.(
3
) we set the kaon as an initial state when constructing the correlation function. This arrangement dose not follow the real physical process
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+}
where the Kaon is a final state. However, its advantage is that it enables us to factorize out the matrix element
\langle 0|J_{\Xi}|\Xi\rangle
by inserting the
\Xi
state in the correlation function without any pollution from kaon. On the other hand, we have introduced an auxiliary momentum
k
, leading to extra momentum invariants relied by the correlation function. Conducting analytical continuation for these extra invariants in the hadron level calculation, we can transform the initial kaon state to the final state. This procedure will be explained in the next section.

III
Hadron level calculation in LCSR

In this section, we perform the hadron level calculation for the correlation function defined in Eq. (
3
). It can be found that
q^{2}=m_{K}^{2}
is a constant, and
\Pi^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k)
depends on 5 Lorentz invariants:

\displaystyle p^{2},~~k^{2},~~(p-q)^{2},~~(p-k)^{2},~~{\bar{p}}^{2}=(p-q-k)^{2}.

(5)

Note that the correlation function should be calculated both at the hadron and quark-gluon level. To realize the operator product expansion (OPE) at the quark-gluon level, one has to choose following deep Euclidean regions:
(p-q)^{2},(p-k)^{2},{\bar{p}}^{2}\leq 0
.

We insert a complete set of states with the same quantum numbers as
\Xi^{+}
between
J_{\Xi}(y)
and
{\cal O}_{i}(0)
in the correlation function:

\displaystyle\Pi_{H}^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x}e^{i(p-k)\cdot y}\sum_{\pm^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{l}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E_{l}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|J_{\Xi}(y)|l,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}\rangle\langle l,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}|{\cal O}_{i}(0)\bar{J}_{\Lambda_{c}}(x)|K^{-}(q)\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{s_{\Xi}}^{\infty}ds^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{\Xi}(s^{\prime},\cdots)}{s^{\prime}-(p-k)^{2}},

(6)

where the integration for
\rho_{\Xi}(s^{\prime},\cdots)
represents the contribution from the excited states and continuous spectrum, the
\cdots
denotes the invariants in Eq.(
5
) except
(p-k)^{2}
.
s_{\Xi}
is the threshold parameter for the excited states. The index
\pm^{\prime}
denotes the
positive or negative parity states:
\Xi(\frac{1}{2}^{\pm})
, both of which can be created by the
J_{\Xi}
current, and
\sigma^{\prime}
denotes their spins. As momentum of a one-particle state in the complete set,
l
is on-shell:
l^{2}=m_{\Xi}^{\pm 2}
. Thus the first matrix element in Eq. (
6
) can be simply parameterized by the
\Xi(\frac{1}{2}^{\pm})
pole residues
\lambda_{\Xi^{\pm}}
:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\Xi}(y)|l,\sigma^{\prime},+\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Xi}^{+}u_{\Xi}(l,\sigma^{\prime})e^{-il\cdot y},

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\Xi}(y)|l,\sigma^{\prime},-\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Xi}^{-}i\gamma_{5}u_{\Xi}(l,\sigma^{\prime})e^{-il\cdot y}.

(7)

For simplicity we define
u^{+}\equiv u
and
u^{-}\equiv i\gamma_{5}u
.

M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}

\langle K^{+}(-q)|
.

On the other hand, in Eq. (
6
) the hadronic transition sector involving the external states can be described by a following matrix element

\displaystyle M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}(p,q,l)=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\ e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x}

\displaystyle\times\langle l,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}|{\cal O}_{i}(0)\bar{J}_{\Lambda_{c}}(x)|K^{-}(q)\rangle,

(8)

which depends on 4 invariants:
(p-q)^{2},p^{2},(l-q)^{2},(p-l)^{2}
, and
l^{2}=m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}
is a constant. This matrix element can be considered as a flavor changing transition from
K^{-}(q)
to
\Xi^{+}(l)
with incoming momentum
l-q
, which is intuitively shown by the diagram (a) in Fig.
2
. The momentum conservation in Eq.(
6
) leads to
l=p-k
and the correlation function can be expressed by
M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle\Pi_{H}^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k)=i^{2}\sum_{\pm^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{i}{(p-k)^{2}-m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|J_{\Xi}(0)|p-k,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}\rangle M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}(p,q,p-k)

\displaystyle+\int_{s_{\Xi}}^{\infty}ds^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{\Xi}(s^{\prime},\cdots)}{s^{\prime}-(p-k)^{2}}.

(9)

Now the incoming momentum of
M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}
becomes
\bar{p}=p-k-q

M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}(p,q,p-k)

At the quark-hadron level, the same correlation function will be calculated by OPE, and the result at these two levels should be equivalent:

\displaystyle\Pi_{H}^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k)=\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{i}}(p,q,k).

(10)

Operating the Borel transformation for
(p-k)^{2}

\displaystyle-i^{3}\sum_{\pm^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}e^{-m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}/T^{\prime 2}}\lambda_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}}u_{\Xi}^{\pm}(p-k)M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}(p,q,p-k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{s}^{2}}^{s_{\rm th}^{\Xi}}ds^{\prime}\ e^{-s^{\prime}/T^{\prime 2}}{\rm Im}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{i}}\left[s^{\prime},\cdots,{\bar{p}}^{2}\right],

(11)

where
s_{\rm th}^{\Xi}
is the threshold parameter for the continuous spectrum in the
\Xi
channel.
Note that the right hand side of Eq. (
11
) is an analytic function of
{\bar{p}}^{2}
, and the same as the left
hand side. Therefore one can use analytic continuation to extend
{\bar{p}}^{2}
from deep Euclidean region
to the physical region:
{\bar{p}}^{2}>0

2
, the initial state
|K^{-}(q)\rangle
of the matrix element
M_{K\to\Xi}^{\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime}}
can be replaced by a final state
\langle K^{+}(-q)|
. From this diagram, it can also be found that after the auxiliary momentum

is actually the invariant mass square of the two final states, equivalently, the mass square of the initial state:
{\bar{p}}^{2}=m_{\Lambda_{c}}^{+2}

Now Eq. (
11

\displaystyle-i^{3}\sum_{\pm^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}e^{-m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}/T^{\prime 2}}\lambda_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}}\int d^{4}x\ e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x}

\displaystyle\times u_{\Xi}^{\pm}(p-k,\sigma^{\prime})\langle p-k,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime};K^{+}(-q)|{\cal O}_{i}(0)\bar{J}_{\Lambda_{c}}(x)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{s}^{2}}^{\infty}ds^{\prime}\ e^{-s^{\prime}/T^{\prime 2}}{\rm Im}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{i}}\left[s^{\prime},\cdots,{\bar{p}}^{2}\right].

(12)

On the left hand side of Eq. (
12
), we insert another complete set of states with the same quantum number as
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
between
{\cal O}_{i}(0)
and
{\bar{J}}_{\Lambda_{c}}
, operate Borel transfromation for
(p-q)^{2}
, and use quark-hadron duality to cancel the continuous spectrum contribution, then arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{\pm,\sigma}e^{-m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}/T^{\prime 2}}e^{-m_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm 2}/T^{2}}\lambda_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}}\lambda_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm}u_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}}(p-k,\sigma^{\prime})\langle p-k,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime};K^{+}(-q)|{\cal O}_{i}(0)|p-q,\sigma,\pm\rangle{\bar{u}}_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm}(p-q,\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{4m_{s}^{2}}^{s_{\rm th}^{\Xi}}ds^{\prime}\int_{(m_{c}+m_{s})^{2}}^{s_{\rm th}^{\Lambda_{c}}}ds\ e^{-s^{\prime}/T^{\prime 2}}e^{-s/T^{2}}{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{i}}\left[s^{\prime},s,\cdots,{\bar{p}}^{2}\right],

(13)

where
s_{\rm th}^{\Lambda_{c}}
is the threshold parameter for the continuous spectrum in the
\Lambda_{c}
channel.
Unlike Eq.(
2
), the matrix element above depends on an extra momentum
k
, so that it should be parameterized by 4 amplitudes:

\displaystyle\langle p-k,\sigma^{\prime},\pm^{\prime};K^{+}(-q)|{\cal O}_{i}(0)|p-q,\sigma,\pm\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ i\ {\bar{u}}_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}}(p-k,\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\left[A_{1}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm}-B_{1}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm}\gamma_{5}-A_{2}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm}\frac{\not{q}}{m_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm}}+B_{2}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm}\frac{\not{q}\gamma_{5}}{m_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm}}\right]

\displaystyle\times u_{\Lambda_{c}}^{\pm}(p-q,\sigma).

(14)

Now the amplitudes
A_{1,2}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm},B_{1,2}^{i\pm^{\prime}\pm}
are functions of 2 Lorentz invariants:
p^{2}
and
k^{2}
. Since
k

during the calculation. For the setting of

, the diagram (b) in Fig.
2

p^{2}=m_{\Xi}^{\pm^{\prime}2}

14

), and summing up all the spin indices, one arrives at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{4m_{s}^{2}}^{s_{\rm th}^{\Xi}}ds^{\prime}\int_{(m_{c}+m_{s})^{2}}^{s_{\rm th}^{\Lambda_{c}}}ds\ e^{-s^{\prime}/T^{\prime 2}}e^{-s/T^{2}}{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{i}}\left[s^{\prime},s,\cdots,{\bar{p}}^{2}\right].

(15)

where
p_{1}=p-q,\ p_{2}=p-k
. The pole residues of
\Lambda_{c}
is defined in the same way as that in Eq.(
7
). At this step, the reason for inserting both the positive and negative states becomes clear. Both the left and right hands of Eq.(

\displaystyle A^{i}=

\displaystyle A_{1}^{i++}+\Big(1-\frac{m_{\Xi}^{+}}{m_{\Lambda_{c}}^{+}}\Big)A_{2}^{i++},

IV
Quark-Gluon Level

This section serves to the OPE calculation on the correlation function in Eq. (
3
).
At the quark-gluon level, and in the deep Euclidean region:
(p-k)^{2}\sim(p-q)^{2}\sim{\bar{p}}^{2}\ll 0
, the correlation function can be expressed as a convolution of a perturbative kernel and a nonperturbative matrix element of the kaon. The correlation function corresponding to the WE diagram is .

\displaystyle\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{1}}(p,q,k)_{\kappa\tau}=-\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{2}}(p,q,k)_{\kappa\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -4\delta_{im}\int d^{4}x\ e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x}\int d^{4}y\ e^{i(p-k)\cdot y}

\displaystyle\times\left[\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}D_{u}(y,0)\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})D_{d}(0,x)\gamma_{5}\right]_{\kappa\beta}

\displaystyle\times\left[C\gamma^{\mu}D_{s}(y,0)\gamma_{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})D_{c}(0,x)\right]_{\alpha\tau}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|\bar{u}_{m}^{\beta}(x)s_{i}^{\alpha}(y)|K^{-}(q)\rangle,

(17)

where
\alpha,\beta,\kappa,\tau
are spinor indices and
D_{c,u,s}
are the free propagators of
the
c,u,s
quarks.
The last matrix element can be expressed by the Light-Cone Distribution Amplitudes (LCDAs) of the
kaon. Fig.
3
shows the Feynman diagram for the WE correlation function, where the gray bubble denotes the kaon LCDAs.
The contribution from the two-particle LCDAs of kaon up to twist-3 are defined as
[
55
]

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{u}_{\alpha}^{i}(x)s_{\beta}^{j}(0)|K^{-}(q)\rangle=-\frac{i}{4Nc}\delta_{ij}\int_{0}^{1}du\ e^{-i\bar{u}q\cdot x}

\displaystyle\times\Big[(\not{p}\gamma_{5})_{\beta\alpha}f_{K}\phi_{K}(u)+(\gamma_{5})_{\beta\alpha}\mu_{K}^{2}\phi_{p}(u)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\mu_{K}^{2}(1-\rho_{K}^{2})(\gamma_{5}\sigma_{\mu\nu})_{\beta\alpha}q^{\mu}x^{\nu}\phi_{\sigma}^{\sigma}(u)\Big],

(18)

where
\phi_{K}
is the twist-2 LCDA, and
\phi_{p},\phi_{\sigma}
are the twist-3 LCDAs.
\mu_{K}^{2}=f_{K}m_{K}^{(0)},~~\rho_{K}=m_{K}/m_{K}^{(0)}
with
m_{K}^{(0)}=1.6
GeV being the chiral mass of kaon and
f_{K}=0.156
GeV.
The expressions of the LCDAs read as

\displaystyle\phi_{K}=

\displaystyle 6u{\bar{u}}[1+a_{1}^{K}C_{1}^{3/2}(2u-1)+a_{2}^{K}C_{2}^{3/2}(2u-1)],

\displaystyle\phi_{p}=

\displaystyle 1+\left(30\eta_{3}-\frac{5}{2}\right)C_{2}^{1/2}(2u-1)

\displaystyle+\left(-3\eta_{3}w_{3}-\frac{27}{20}\rho_{K}^{2}-\frac{81}{10}\rho_{K}^{2}a_{2}^{K}\right)C_{4}^{1/2}(2u-1),

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}=

\displaystyle 6u{\bar{u}}\left[1+\left(5\eta_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{3}w_{3}-\frac{7}{20}\rho_{K}^{2}-\frac{3}{15}\rho_{K}^{2}a_{2}^{K}\right)\right],

1/(k_{i}^{2}-m^{2})\to(-2\pi i)\delta(k_{i}^{2}-m^{2})

\displaystyle{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{1}(2)}(s^{\prime},s)_{(2)}=\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}{\rm Disc^{2}}\Pi_{WE}^{{\cal O}_{1}(2)}(s^{\prime},s)_{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{if_{K}}{64\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \phi_{K}(u)\int d\Phi_{2}[(p-uq))^{2},k_{1},k_{2}]\int d\Phi_{2}[({\bar{p}}+{\bar{u}}q))^{2},k_{3},k_{4}]

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{2}[P^{2},k_{i},k_{j}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{4}k_{i}d^{4}k_{j}\delta^{4}(P-k_{i}-k_{j})\delta(k_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2})\delta(k_{j}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}).

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the purely non-factorizable
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+}
decay using light-cone sum rules. A three-point correlation is defined and calculated for extracting the S-wave and P-wave decay amplitudes. This decay is contributed by W-exchange and the W-inward emission diagrams, and both of them considered in the quark-gluon level calculation. The two-particle light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) of kaon are used as non-perturbative input. We seperately presented the decay amplitudes contributed from the twist-2, twist-3p and twist-
3\sigma
kaon LCDAs, respectively. The obtained P-wave amplitude is consistent with those predicted in the literature, while the S-wave one is one order smaller than those in the literature, and also has a different sign. Such significant difference between the S- and P -wave amplitudes leads to small but negative up-down spin asymmetry, and the magnitude is close to the latest BESIII measurement.

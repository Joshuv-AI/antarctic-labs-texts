Nonlinear Graphon mean-field systems
Fabio Coppini, Anna De Crescenzo, Huyên Pham

Abstract
We address a system of weakly interacting particles where the heterogenous connections among the particles are described by a graph sequence and the number of particles grows to infinity. Our results extend the existing law of large numbers and propagation of chaos results to the case where the interaction between one particle and its neighbors is expressed as a nonlinear function of the local empirical measure. In the limit of the number of particles which tends to infinity, if the graph sequence converges to a graphon, then we show that the limit system is described by an infinite collection of processes and can be seen as a process in a suitable L 2 L^{2} space constructed via a Fubini extension. The proof is built on decoupling techniques and careful estimates of the Wasserstein distance.

: 60J60, 05C80, 65K35.

Key words
: graphons, particle systems, heterogenous interaction, propagation of chaos, Fubini extension.

1
Introduction

Motivation for complex heterogenous systems.

Physicists and mathematicians have always been fascinated by modeling complex systems with applications that include evolutionary biology
[
3
,
1
]
, epidemiology
[
27
,
23
]
, game theory and controls
[
21
,
12
]
and economics
[
41
]
. One way to represent this complexity is through a mixture of nonlinear dynamics and non-trivial connections among the particles. In a finite system of interacting particles, it is natural to introduce a graph object describing how each particle is connected to the others: it suffices to multiply the interaction between two particles by the indicator function of the edge connecting the corresponding vertices in the underlying graph. Even if this mathematical representation is simple, it does not say anything about the macroscopic limit of the population, i.e., is the empirical measure converging as the number of particles goes to infinity? How is the graph structure influencing the dynamics? To address these questions several results have been proposed, they usually focus on the study of the empirical measure, or on the particle trajectories, and mainly prove Propagation of Chaos
[
22
,
10
]
, Law of Large Numbers
[
39
,
18
,
5
,
9
]
, Central Limit Theorem
[
10
,
19
]
and Large Deviation Principle
[
39
,
18
,
34
]
. We refer to the literature section below for more references.

While the classical mean-field systems are studied in great generality with respect to the particle interaction, the existing results on particle systems and graph sequences focus on the linear (or scalar) case, i.e., the interaction between the particles is one to one and the sum of the interactions on a single particle is equivalent to depending linearly on the empirical measure. To the authors’ knowledge, the gap between mean-field systems and interacting particles on graph sequences has never been tackled in the literature and demands to be clarified, notably in the application viewpoint where the linear dependence is a strong requirement.
In the case where the particles are influenced by a random noise, the Itô formula
[
37
]
is one of the most important tools used to study such systems. Notably, it can be used to derive the partial differential equation, usually called Vlasov or McKeanVlasov equation, satisfied by the empirical measure as the number of particles goes to infinity. For interacting particle systems, it is well known that the limit equation is nonlinear and it is defined on a suitable space containing the probability measures
[
25
,
15
]
: in many instances, as in the case of control problems
[
20
]
it is important to have an
L^{2}
-formulation. To the authors’ knowledge, in the literature there is no such formulation whenever the limit equation depends on the graph structure: an Hilbertian formulation would benefit the mathematical community as it allows the application of the Itô formula.

Contributions of this work.

The aim of this work is twofold: (1) to extend existing Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and a Propagation of Chaos (POC) for interacting particle systems on graphs to the case where the interaction is Lipschitz with respect to the empirical measure; (2) to define an infinite system of nonlinear McKean-Vlasov equations in a suitable
L^{2}
-space, such that the corresponding non-linear processes are measurable (in some sense to be defined). We remark that, even taken independently, each of these points has not been addressed in the literature: the closest result to (1) is given by
[
5
]
where the dependence with respect to the empirical measure is linear (see Remark
2.1
for a comparison of the particle systems); for (2),
[
4
]
studies linear-quadratic stochastic differential games and makes use of a Fubini extension to carefully define the system in the infinite particle setting, however the dynamics does not involve probability measures. We refer to the next subsection for a review of existing results.

Our results can be established thanks to the advances already made in the case of graphon particle systems
[
5
,
9
]
, the ones involving a suitable extension of the Lebesgue measure to address an uncountable family of stochastic processes
[
4
,
43
]
and known results on the Wasserstein distance
[
24
,
45
]
. The proof of LLN, see Theorem
4.1
, and the one of POC, see Theorem
4.2
, mimic the classical one given by Sznitman
[
44
]
where the finite particle system is paired with a finite sample of a suitable limit system. We recall that this last sample of random variables must have suitable exchangeability properties. In our case, given the underlying graphon structure, the trajectories are still independent of each other, but they are no longer identically distributed. The existence and uniqueness of solutions for the infinite system is obtained with a classical fixed point argument, but now in a suitable
L^{2}
space constructed using a Fubini extension, see Definition
3.1
and Proposition
3.1
. The main difficulty coming from the assumption on the dynamics, is that one has to uniformly control all the local empirical measures: by coupling the trajectories with the ones of the infinite system, this translates into having a uniform estimate on the local empirical measures of the infinite system. This is possible by extending a well-known result by Fournier and Guillin
[
24
]
on the convergence of empirical measures in the Wasserstein distance to a not identically distributed setting, see Lemma
4.1
for a precise statement. We are able to recover the same convergence rate, which depends on the dimension of the space, and thus obtain the expected convergence rate in the POC result. We choose to focus on deterministic dense graph sequences converging to graphons
[
30
]
, instead of more general graph sequences
[
5
,
26
]
. However, we believe that with a bit more work and notational effort, our result can be extended to random graph sequences with diverging average degree that converge to graphon under a suitable renormalisation, we refer to
[
40
,
18
,
5
]
for some results applying to dense and not-so-dense graph sequences. We also refrain from investigating singular interactions
[
33
,
46
]
, but they may represent a subject for further study.

Existing literature.

Beginning with the works
[
22
,
10
]
, numerous papers have discussed interacting particle systems on graph sequences, see
[
18
,
39
,
32
,
5
]
this list being in no way exhaustive. It is now well-known that, for a general dense graph sequence, the empirical measure of a system of interacting particles converges to the solution of a partial differential equation, usually called Vlasov or McKean-Vlasov equation, that depends on the graph limit itself, see
[
17
]
for more discussion on the equation and its relation with the graph limit.
Depending on the assumptions on the interaction, on the initial conditions or on the graph sequence itself, several results have been established. It should be mentioned that in the literature the interaction on a given particle is always assumed to be the sum of the two point particle interactions, i.e., linear with respect to the local empirical measures. When the two-point particle interaction is Lipschitz and the underlying graph sequence is somewhat homogeneous, e.g., Erdősh-Rényi random graphs, classical trajectorial estimates
[
22
,
10
,
18
]
lead to a Law of Large Numbers and propagation of chaos results; these results show that the limit equation is the same of the classical mean-field case
[
44
,
38
,
11
]
. For particle systems where the underlying graph displays some sort of heterogeneity, several methods have been proposed.

The graphon theory
[
31
,
30
]
has found its place in several works
[
39
,
6
,
5
,
7
,
9
]
, notably in the case of graphon mean field systems
[
41
,
12
,
29
]
.
Today, it represents one of the most cited framework when dense graph sequences are in play. The first article to link the cut-distance, the natural distance of the graphon space (see Section
3.2
below), to interacting particle systems is given by
[
39
]
, where the authors consider spatially extended particles that are interacting through a random graph, this last one being sampled from a graphon. Similar results, although in a different metric concerning the graph convergence, are those
[
33
,
32
]
but also the ones about the Kuramoto model in the deterministic setting, see
[
36
,
35
,
14
]
and the references therein. The works
[
5
,
9
]
have extended the work
[
40
]
in two important ways: the first one
[
5
]
considers a large class of particle systems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and shows that, for a graph sequence converging in cut-distance to a deterministic graphon, a Law of Large Numbers holds for the empirical measure and its limit is characterised by being the solution of an infinite system of partial differential equations; the second one
[
9
]
restricts the analysis to particle systems on the one-dimensional torus but under weaker assumptions on the graph convergence, which can be in probability and where the limit graphon can be random itself.
The paper
[
2
]
shows convergence of interacting mean-field particle system with inhomogeneous interactions to graphon mean-field BSDE, and in
[
8
]
, the authors prove a propagation of chaos for a system of forward-backward SDE with graphon interaction arising from mean-field game problem. In both papers, the interaction is of linear form.
For the Central Limit Theorem, we refer to
[
19
]
, where the authors are able to study the global and local fluctuations of the empirical measure in the case of Erdős-Rényi random graphs. Other relevant works concern the long time behavior, we refer to
[
16
,
7
]
. Singular interactions have been studied in
[
33
,
46
]
, the latter being restricted to the case where the graph weights are on the vertices rather than the edges.

Graphon theory comes with some constraints, notably the fact that every vertex has roughly the same order of neighbors but also the symmetry assumed at the level of the limit graph (as a graphon is a symmetric function on the unit square). Two generalisations have been proposed:
[
26
]
proves a Law of Large Numbers under some weak assumption on the degrees of the graph,
[
28
]
precisely focuses on sequences of directed graphs.

Outline of the paper.

The plan of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define the interacting particle systems under consideration and state the main assumption on the particle dynamics. Section
3
is devoted to the

-formulation: it recalls the main results concerning the Fubini extension, the main tools and assumptions to work in the graphon setting and finally states the classical result on existence and uniqueness of solutions. The LLN and POC are stated in the Section

Notations.

We work in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
where

is some positive integer. The Euclidian norm of a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\left|\cdot\right|
, and

, and

is the set of nonnegative finite Borel measures on

When we consider the subspace of probability measures with finite variance, we denote it by
{\cal P}_{2}(E)
. Their elements are denoted by Greek letters, e.g.,
\mu,\nu,\eta\in{\cal P}(E)
.

We equip
{\cal P}_{2}(E)
with the 2-Wasserstein distance
{\cal W}_{2}
so that it is a metric space itself. The 2-Wasserstein distance on
{\cal P}_{2}(E)
is defined by, for
\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}_{2}(E)

{\cal W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}\Big[\boldsymbol{d}(X,Y)^{2}\Big]\right)^{1/2},

(1.1)

where
X,Y
are random variables with law given respectively by
\mu
, and
\nu
.
The Kantorovitch duality, see
[
45
, Theorem 5.10]
, states that for any

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{2}^{2}(\nu,\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup\Bigg\{\int_{E}f(y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{E}g(y)\nu(\mathrm{d}y),\quad\text{with }f,g\in C_{b}(E)\text{ such that }

(1.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptf(x)+g(y)\;\leq\boldsymbol{d}(x,y)^{2}\quad\forall x,y\in E\Bigg\},

(1.3)

where
C_{b}(E)
stands for the space of bounded continuous functions on
E
. In the same book by Villani, see
[
45
, Theorem 6.15]
, one can also find a control on the Wasserstein distance by means of the variation between (signed) measures: if
(E,\boldsymbol{d})
is a Polish space with

, then it holds that for
\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}_{2}(E)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\;2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\boldsymbol{d}(x,x_{0})^{2}\big|\mu-\nu\big|(\mathrm{d}x),

(1.4)

where
\big|\mu-\nu\big|
is the variation of the (signed) measure
\mu-\nu
. Recall that if
M
is a signed measure, then
|M|=M^{+}+M^{-}
, where
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
are respectively the positive and the negative parts of
M
.

2
Mean-field particle systems with graphon interactions

We consider a multi-agent/particle system with heterogenous interactions where each agent interacts with all other agents via an aggregated mean-field effect of the whole population of size
N

X_{t}^{i,N}

the state valued in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
at time

i
(represented by a vertex/node)

\in

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket:=\{1,\dots,N\}

\displaystyle\nu_{t}^{i,N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{ij}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{j,N}},

(2.1)

where
(\xi_{ij}^{N})_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is the interaction matrix and
N_{i}
is the number of edges of node
i

N_{i}

=

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{ij}^{N}
, called degree of interaction. The interaction matrix

is associated to a graphon

, i.e., a symmetric measurable function from
I\times I
into
[0,1]
, with

:=

[0,1]
, via

\displaystyle\xi_{ij}^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{N}(u_{i},u_{j}),

where

=

i/N
represents the label of agent

, in the population of
N
agents, and it is assumed that

is a step graphon, i.e.

\displaystyle G_{N}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\;G_{N}\Big(\frac{\lceil Nu\rceil}{N},\frac{\lceil Nv\rceil}{N}\Big),\quad(u,v)\in I\times I.

(2.2)

We assume that there is no isolated particle, i.e., that for every

\displaystyle\|G_{N}(u,.)\|_{{}_{1}}\;:=\;\int_{[0,1]}G_{N}(u,v)\mathrm{d}v>0,\quad\forall u\in[0,1].

The states of the

\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{i,N}\;=\;b(X_{t}^{i,N},\nu_{t}^{i,N})\mathrm{d}t\;+\;\sigma(X_{t}^{i,N},\nu_{t}^{i,N})\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{u_{i}},\quad 0\leq t\leq T,\;\;\;i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket,

There exists a constant

\displaystyle|b(x,\mu)-b(x^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})|+|\sigma(x,\mu)-\sigma(x^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\left(|x-x^{\prime}|+{\cal W}_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\right),

and

\displaystyle|\sigma(0,\delta_{0})|+|b(0,\delta_{0})|

System (

Concerning (1), we observe that we are able to extend the classical scalar formulation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{i,N}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{ij}^{N}\Big[\tilde{b}(X_{t}^{i,N},X_{t}^{j,N})\mathrm{d}t+\tilde{\sigma}(X_{t}^{i,N},X_{t}^{j,N})\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{u_{i}}\Big],

to the case where the interaction directly depends on the local empirical measure

We are interested in the study of the limiting particles system when the graphon

(and recap in the next section). It is formally expected

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{u}

\displaystyle=\;b\Big(X^{u}_{t},\frac{1}{\int_{I}G(u,v)\mathrm{d}v}\int_{I}G(u,v)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}^{v}}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}v\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\;\sigma\Big(X^{u}_{t},\frac{1}{\int_{I}G(u,v)\mathrm{d}v}\int_{I}G(u,v)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}^{v}}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}v\Big)\;\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{u},\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

In the sequel, we shall address on the one hand the well-posedness, existence and uniqueness of a solution to the system (

3
L^{2}
-formulation of graphon system

We now aim to formulate the graphon system (
2.5
) as an equation in infinite dimension by identifying the collection of state variables of the continuum of agents
\{X^{u}:u\in I\}
with
{\boldsymbol{X}}

=

({\boldsymbol{X}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
where
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{t}
is the mapping:

(\omega,u)\in\Omega\times I

\mapsto

X_{t}^{u}(\omega)

\in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
t

\in

[0,T]
. However, there is a measurability issue due to the fact that
(\omega,u)

\mapsto

W^{u}(\omega)
, hence
X_{t}^{u}(\omega)
, is not jointly measurable in the product space of the usual continuum product and the classical Lebesgue space
(I,{\cal B}_{I},\lambda_{I})
on the index space
I
.
This issue is overcome in
[
4
]
with the notion of rich Fubini extension, previously introduced in
[
43
]
. Indeed, there exists a probability space
(I,{\cal I},\lambda)
extending the usual Lebesque space
(I,{\cal B}_{I},\lambda_{I}(\mathrm{d}u)=\mathrm{d}u)
, and a Fubini extension

(\Omega\times I,{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I},\mathbb{P}\boxtimes\lambda)
of
(\Omega\times I,{\cal F}\otimes{\cal I},\mathbb{P}\otimes\lambda)
, such that

is jointly

-measurable in

. We now review the key properties of

and discuss its use in our framework.

Remark 3.1

Observe that there is no chance to obtain a continuous mapping
u\mapsto X^{u}
. Indeed, continuity would imply measurability with respect to the Lebesgue measure which is not possible.

3.1
Setting and notation

We review the key result that grants the existence of

such that
{\boldsymbol{X}}

=

({\boldsymbol{X}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

-measurable.
We start by recalling the definition of essentially pairwise independent (e.p.i.) random variables indexed by

We refer to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\boxtimes}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times I}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\phi_{t}^{u}(\omega)|^{2}\mathbb{P}\boxtimes\lambda(\mathrm{d}\omega,\mathrm{d}u)\;<\;\infty.

3.2
Graphons

Let
{\cal G}
be the space of bounded, symmetric and
\lambda_{I}
-measurable functions on
I\times I

{\cal G}

[0,1]

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\square}

\displaystyle:=\;\sup_{S,S^{\prime}\in{\cal B}(I)}\Big|\int_{S\times S^{\prime}}G(u,v)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\Big|.

or the stronger assumption

\displaystyle G_{\infty}^{-1}\;:=\;\sup_{u\in I}\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle<\;\infty.

Assumption

requires the degree to be bounded from below. Examples of graphons satisfying this condition are the constant graphon and, more generally, the Stochastic Block model with no isolated population, but also bipartite graphons and Cayley graphons

is a rather weak condition which includes graphons with vanishing degree as

Observe that both Assumption

and

require no regularity on the graphon as a function from

to

When the context is clear, we also view

\in

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptf(x)+g(y)\;\leq|x-y|^{2}\quad\forall x,y\in E\Bigg\}.

\displaystyle\;\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}^{-1}\Big(\int_{E}f(y)\int_{I}G(u,v)\nu^{v}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}v+\int_{E}g(y)\int_{I}G(u,v)\eta^{v}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}v\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{ij}^{N}\mu_{t}^{u_{j}}(A)=2[G_{N}\hat{\mu}_{t}]^{u_{i}}(A),

,

The rest of the proof follows in the same way as in the proof of Theorem 1 in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in A_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{u_{i}}|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\frac{K}{N}\sum_{i\in A_{N}}({\cal E}_{N}^{i}(1)+{\cal E}_{N}^{i}(2))}_{{\cal E}_{N}}+o_{N}(1)+K\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in A_{N}}{\cal G}_{N}^{i}.

(4.33)

By Step 1, we have that

{\cal E}_{N}
goes to zero as
N
goes to infinity.
Let us now check that the last term in (

) converges to zero as

goes to infinity.
To see this, we consider the following operator norm

:

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\infty\to 1}

\displaystyle:=\;\sup_{\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\int_{I}\Big|\int_{I}G(u,v)g(v)\mathrm{d}v\Big|\mathrm{d}u,

(4.34)

and we recall from
[
30
, Lemma 8.11]
that if
\|G-G_{N}\|_{\square}\to 0
, then
\|G-G_{N}\|_{\infty\to 1}\to 0
.
Let us observe that:

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}^{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{G(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}-\frac{G(u_{i},v)}{\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}+\frac{G(u_{i},v)}{\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}-\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\right)

(4.35)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad|x|^{2}(\mathbb{I}_{P}(x)-\mathbb{I}_{N}(x))\mu_{s}^{v}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s

(4.36)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{G(u_{i},v)(\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}-\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1})}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}|x|^{2}(\mathbb{I}_{P}(x)-\mathbb{I}_{N}(x))\mu_{s}^{v}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s

(4.39)

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in A_{N}}{\cal G}_{n}^{i}

We start with the convergence of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left|\sum_{i\in A_{N}}{\cal G}_{N}^{i}(2)\right|

(4.42)

(4.43)

{\cal G}_{N}(2,1)

\displaystyle|{\cal G}_{N}(2,1)|

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\tilde{A}_{N}}\frac{1}{\|G_{N}(u,\cdot)\|_{1}}\int_{I}\left|G\left(\frac{\lceil Nu\rceil}{N},v\right)-G(u,v)\right|\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}u

where we have used (
4.16
) in the third inequality and the final convergence follows from the uniform continuity of
G
in Assumption
3.4
.
To prove the convergence of

, observe that, since Assumption
3.1
(ii) holds, we can find a sequence of simple (hence bounded) functions

such that, for each

0\leq g_{M}(u)\leq\frac{1}{\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}}

\forall u\in I

We now write, for an arbitrary

:

\displaystyle|{\cal G}_{N}(2,2)|

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\tilde{A}_{N}}\frac{1}{\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}}\left|\int_{I}\left(G(u,v)-G_{N}(u,v)\right)\beta(v)\mathrm{d}v\right|\mathrm{d}u

(4.47)

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\tilde{A}_{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}}-g_{M}(u)\right)\left|\int_{I}\left(G(u,v)-G_{N}(u,v)\right)\beta(v)\mathrm{d}v\right|\mathrm{d}u

(4.48)

\displaystyle\quad+K\int_{\tilde{A}_{N}}g_{M}(u)\left|\int_{I}\left(G(u,v)-G_{N}(u,v)\right)\beta(v)\mathrm{d}v\right|\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{M}+K(M)\|G-G_{N}\|_{\square}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\frac{K}{M},

and the rest of the proof is similar to what was done for

4.2
Propagation of chaos

If the limit graphon
G
satisfies both some degree condition (Assumption
3.2
), and some regularity assumption (Assumption
3.5
), then the finite particle system can be controlled trajectory per trajectory. The rate can be computed and turns out to be optimal, i.e., the same of the mean-field behavior
[
24
]
.

Theorem 4.2

Let Assumptions
2.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.5
hold. Suppose that
G_{N}(u_{i},u_{j})

=

G(u_{i},u_{j})
,
i,j

\in

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, and
X^{i,N}_{0}

=

\zeta^{u_{i}}
,

i

\in

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
.
Then, there exists some positive constant
K>0
, depending on
d,\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{u_{i}}\big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle KM_{N}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, see (
4.11
), (
4.25
) and (
4.32
), we have for all
i

\in

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{u_{i}}|^{2}\Big]\leq&K\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|X_{r}^{i,N}-X_{r}^{u_{i}}|^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s\\
&+K{\cal E}_{N}(1)+K{\cal E}_{N}(2)+K{\cal G}_{N},\end{split}

(4.52)

where
{\cal E}_{N}(1):=\max_{i}{\cal E}_{N}^{i}(1)
,
{\cal E}_{N}(2):=\max_{i}{\cal E}_{N}^{i}(2)
and
{\cal G}_{N}:=\max_{i}{\cal G}_{N}^{i}
.
Since
G_{N}(u_{i},u_{j})=G(u_{i},u_{j})
and
\sup_{u\in I}\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}^{-1}<\infty
from Assumption
3.2
, we can assume that there exists a constant
K
such that for
N
large enough and for all
i\in\llbracket 1,\dots,N\rrbracket
,
\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}N=N_{i}\geq KN
.
Thus, from the estimation (
4.2
) in Lemma
4.1
,
we have
{\cal E}_{N}(1)

\leq

KM_{N}
.
Under Assumption
3.5
, we have from Proposition
3.2
that
{\cal E}_{N}(2)\leq K/{N^{2}}
.
On the other hand, we have that:

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\;K\int_{I}\bigg|\frac{G(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}-\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\bigg|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\;K\int_{I}\frac{1}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}|G(u_{i},v)-G_{N}(u_{i},v)|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+K\int_{I}\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\big|\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}-\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\big|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=:{\cal G}^{i}_{N}(1)+{\cal G}^{i}_{N}(2).

Now, since
G_{N}(u_{i},v)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}G(u_{j},u_{k})1_{I_{j}}(u_{i})1_{I_{k}}(v)
, we can write

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}^{i}(1)

\displaystyle:=\;K\int_{I}\frac{1}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}|G(u_{i},v)-G_{N}(u_{i},v)|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\;\frac{K}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\int_{I}\bigg|\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(G(u_{i},v)-G\big(u_{j},u_{k}\big))1_{I_{j}}(u_{i})1_{I_{k}}(v)\bigg|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{K}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\int_{I}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\bigg|G(u_{i},v)-G\big(u_{j},u_{k}\big)\bigg|1_{I_{j}}(u_{i})1_{I_{k}}(v)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{K}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\int_{I}\sum_{j,k}^{N}\frac{1}{N}1_{I_{j}}(u_{i})1_{I_{k}}(v)\mathrm{d}v\;\leq\;\frac{K}{N},

where we use in the second inequality the Lipschitz condition on
G
in Assumption
3.5
, and the fact that
\sup_{u\in I}\|G(u,\cdot)\|_{1}^{-1}<\infty
in Assumption
3.2
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{N}^{i}(2)

\displaystyle:=\;K\int_{I}\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\big|\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}-\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\big|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=K\int_{I}\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\bigg|\int_{I}(G(u_{i},w)-G_{N}(u_{i},w))\mathrm{d}w\bigg|\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\;K\int_{I}\frac{G_{N}(u_{i},v)}{\|G(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}\|G_{N}(u_{i},\cdot)\|_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{I_{j}}\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{d}v\;\leq\;\frac{K}{N}.

We deduce that
{\cal G}_{N}^{i}

\leq

for all
i

\in

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket

A.1
Stochastic integral in the Fubini extension

Let
\Phi\in L^{2}_{\boxtimes}(\Omega\times I;{\cal C}_{d})
. We define the stochastic integral as the following stochastic process for
(\omega,u)\in\Omega\times I

I_{t}^{\Phi}(\omega,u):=\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Bigg)(\omega),

where
\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{t}^{u}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{t}
is the classical integral with respect to the Brownian motion
W^{u}
.
In the following we will consider the filtration
\mathbb{F}
generated by
W
, i.e.
{\cal F}_{t}:=\sigma(W_{s},s\leq t)
.

Let us prove that
I^{\Phi}\in L^{2}_{\boxtimes}(\Omega\times I;{\cal C}_{d})
.
We start by checking the measurability. Since
\Phi\in L^{2}_{\boxtimes}(\Omega\times I)
, we can find a sequence of simple processes
\{\Phi^{n}\}
approximating it in the sense that

\int_{I}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi^{n,u}_{t}-\Phi^{u}_{t}|^{2}dt\bigg]\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)\to 0.

A simple process takes the form

\Phi^{n}_{t}(\omega,u)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\phi^{i}(\omega,u)\mathbb{I}_{[t_{i},t_{i}+1)}(t),

where
\phi^{i}
is
\mathbb{F}_{t_{i}}
-measurable and is
L^{2}_{\boxtimes}(\Omega\times I)
. The integral of
\Phi^{n}
is then defined by

I^{\Phi^{n}}_{t}(\omega,u):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\phi^{i}(\omega,u)(W_{t_{i+1}}(\omega,u)-W_{t_{i}}).

Obviously,
I^{\Phi^{n}}_{t}
is
{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I}
-measurable. Because of the
L^{2}
convergence, there exists a null set
{\cal N}\in{\cal I}
such that for all
u\in I\setminus{\cal N}
it holds that (up to a subsequence)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi_{t}^{n,u}-\Phi_{t}^{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\to 0

and thus

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|{\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{n,u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\to 0.

Following the approach of
[
42
]
, for
u\in{\cal N}^{c}
we define the following functions:

\displaystyle n_{0}(u):=1

\displaystyle n_{k}(u):=\inf\Bigg\{m>n_{k-1}(u)\text{ s.t. }\sup_{p,q\geq m}\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{s}^{p,u}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{q,u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Big|>2^{-k}\Big)<2^{-k}\Bigg\}.

Observe that
n_{k}
is
{\cal I}
-measurable for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, we set

Y_{k}^{u}(\omega):=\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{k}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s},

and observe that
Y
is
{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I}
-measurable since the composition of measurable functions. It is easy to see that for all
u\in{\cal N}^{c}
,
Y^{u}_{k}
converges
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. The stochastic integral is defined for
(\omega,u)
as

\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Bigg)(\omega):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $(\omega,u)\in A^{c}\cup(\Omega\times{\cal N})$}\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}Y_{k}^{u}(\omega)&\text{if $(\omega,u)\in A\setminus(\Omega\times{\cal N})$}\end{cases}

where
A:=\{(\omega,u)\text{ s.t }\lim_{k\to\infty}Y^{u}_{k}(\omega)\text{ exists and is finite }\}
.

We show that
A^{c}\in{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I}
. In fact

\displaystyle A^{c}

\displaystyle=\{(\omega,u)\text{ s.t. }\exists\epsilon>0,\forall K\in\mathbb{N},\exists p,q\in\mathbb{N},p,q\geq K:|Y^{u}_{p}(\omega)-Y^{u}_{q}(\omega)|>\epsilon\}

\displaystyle=\{(\omega,u)\text{ s.t. }\exists\epsilon>0,\forall K\in\mathbb{N},\exists p,q\in\mathbb{N},p,q\geq K:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{p}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{q}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Big|>\epsilon\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}}\bigcap_{K\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{p,q\geq K}\Big\{\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{p}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{q}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Big|>\epsilon\Big\},

and the sets
\Big\{\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{p}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{n_{q}(u),u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Big|>\epsilon\Big\}\in{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I}
. We can then deduce that
\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Bigg)(\omega)
is
{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal I}
-measurable.

The integrability property is ensured by observing that

\mathbb{E}^{\boxtimes}[\sup_{t\leq T}|I^{\Phi}_{t}|^{2}]=\int_{I}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sup_{t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{u}_{s}\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\Big|^{2}\Bigg]\lambda(du)\leq\int_{I}C\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi^{u}_{s}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)<\infty,

where we have used the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and where
C
is a constant depending only on the exponent
2
(and not on
u\in I
).

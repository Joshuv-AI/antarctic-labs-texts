Abstract
. In this article, we introduce the notion of a wedge of graphs and provide detailed computations for the independence complex of a wedge of path and cycle graphs. In particular, we show that these complexes are either contractible or wedges of spheres.

1.
Introduction

A
graph
is an order pair
G=(V,E)
, where
V
is called the set of vertices and
E
is called the set of edges. The set
E
is consist of
2
-element subsets of
V
. A subject topological combinatorics is consist of the study of homotopy invariants
of certain cell complexes constructed using the graph to obtain combinatorial information about the graph
G
. The first example of this is the Lovász
[
9
]
celebrated proof of the Kneser conjecture. The neighborhood complex
\mathcal{N}(G)
of a graph
G
is an abstract simplicial complex whose simplices are subsets of the vertex set
V
having common neighbors.
Lovász uses the connectivity of neighborhood complex
\mathcal{N}(G)
to compute a lower bound on the chromatic number of the corresponding graph.
Lovász stated the similar conjecture that the lower bound on the chromatic number of a graph
G
can be given in terms of the connectivity of certain cell complexes associated with cycle graph and
G
. These complexes are known as
hom complexes
. Babson and Kozlov
[
1
]
proved Lovász conjecture, where they relate these hom complexes to
independence complexes
.

An abstract simplcial complex consist of all independent subsets of
V
is called the
independence complex
of
G
. It is denoted by
\mathcal{I}(G)
.
In the last two decades, the general problem of determining all the possible homotopy type of independence complexes for various classes of graphs has received considerable attention. For example, Kozlov
[
8
]
determined the homotopy type of independence complexes for path and cycle graphs, Kawamura
[
7
,
6
]
for chordal graphs and forests, Bousquet-Mélou, Mireille and Linusson, Svante and Nevo, Eran
[
2
]
for square-grid graphs, Engström
[
4
]
investigated this problem for claw-free graphs, and Raun
[
3
]
for stable Kneser graphs etc.

The purpose of this paper is to introduce the notion of a wedge of graphs and compute the all possible homotopy type of independence complexes of wedges of some classes of graphs. In particular, we consider the class of wedges of path graphs and cycle graphs.
We also describe the relationships between combinatorial and topological invariants associated with the wedge of graphs with the corresponding combinatorial and topological invariants of the components. We hope these computations will be helpful in other parts of mathematics.

The paper is organized as follows: We begin
Section

2
by defining some combinatorial and topological objects associated with a graph. Then we introduce the notion of a wedge of graphs. We end this section by recalling some results about the homotopy type of independence complexes of path graphs and cycle graphs.

Finally in
Section

3
, we prove the main results of this paper. Here, we compute all possible homotopy types of the wedge of finitely many path graphs and wedge of finitely many different cycle graphs. Next, we consider the wedge of cycle and path graphs with respect to different base points and comupute all possible homotopy types of corresponding independence complexes.

Definition 2.1
.

An abstract simplicial complex
K
is a collection of subsets of
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, such that

(1)

\{v_{i}\}\in K
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

(2)

if
\sigma\in K
and
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
\tau\in K
.

The elements of
K
are called faces. The
dimension
of a face
\sigma
is defined as
|\sigma|-1
.
In this paper, by simplicial complex we mean a geometric realization of an abstract simplicial complex. Without loss of generality we use simplicial complex for an abstract simplicial complex.

Now we define an important simplicial complex associated with a graph.

Definition 2.2

(Independence complex)
.

For a finite simple graph
G
, with the vertex set
V
, the independence complex
\mathcal{I}(G)
is the simplicial complex consisting of
all independent (i.e. no two vertices are adjacent) subsets of
V
as its simplices.

The independence complex of a graph
G
is denoted by
\mathcal{I}(G)
.

A
subcomlex
of a simplicial complex is a simplicial complex whose faces are contained in
K
.
There are two important subcomplexes associated with any simplicial complex.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
K
be a (abstract) simplicial complex. The link of a vertex
v\in K
is defined as

\mathrm{lk}(v,K):=\{\sigma\in K\mid v\notin\sigma\text{ and }\sigma\cup{v}\in K\}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
K
be a (abstract) simplicial complex. The deletion of a vertex
v\in K
is defined as

\mathrm{del}(v,K):=\{\sigma\in K\mid v\notin\sigma\}.

Observe that for any vertex in
K
, the subcomplex
\mathrm{lk}(v,K)
is a subcomplex of
\mathrm{del}(v,K)
.
The following is an important result which describes the homotopy type of a simplicial complex in terms of the link and deletion.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
5
, Lemma 2]
)
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex and
v\in K
be a vertex such that
\mathrm{lk}(v,K)
is contractible in
\mathrm{del}(v,K)
. Then
K\simeq\mathrm{del}(v,K)\vee\sum\mathrm{lk}(v,K)
.

Now we define the central object of study in this paper. This will be followed by a few examples. Note that the definition depends on the choice of the so-called ‘wedge point’.

Definition 2.7

(Wedge of graphs)
.

Given a finite family of graphs
G_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
, and
a_{i}\in V_{i}
for all
i
, an
wedge of graphs
is defined to be a graph
G=(V,E)
such that

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{i}V_{i}\setminus\{a_{i}\}\right)\cup\{a\},

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{i}E_{i}\setminus\{e_{j}\in E_{i}|a_{i}\in\partial(e_{j})\}\right)\cup\left\{ab_{k}|a_{i}b_{k}\in E_{i}\right\}.

The common point
a
will be called
wedge point
.

.

Let

\chi(G_{1}\vee G_{2})=\text{max}\{\chi(G_{1}),\chi(G_{2})\}.

3.
Main results

We begin this section by proving a sufficient condition for the link of a vertex to be contractible inside deletion. This is crucial in using the
Lemma

2.6
. Then we compute the homotopy type of wedge of path graphs and a wedge of cycle graphs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph, and
v\in\mathcal{I}(G)
be a vertex of
G
. Let
\sigma\in\textup{del}{\left(v\right)}
be a maximal simplex such that
\sigma\notin\textup{lk}{\left(v,\mathcal{I}(G)\right)}
and
\textup{del}{\left(v,\mathcal{I}(G)\right)}\setminus\{\sigma\}
is contractible. Then

\mathcal{I}(G)\simeq\textup{del}{\left(v,\mathcal{I}(G)\right)}\vee\Sigma\left(\textup{lk}{\left(v,\mathcal{I}(G)\right)}\right).

Proof.

Since
\sigma\notin\textup{lk}{\left(v\right)},~\textup{lk}{\left(v\right)}\subseteq\textup{del}{\left(v\right)}\setminus\{\sigma\}
, and
\textup{del}{\left(v\right)}\setminus\{\sigma\}
is contractible hence we have
\textup{lk}{\left(v\right)}
is contractible in
\textup{del}{\left(v\right)}
. Then the result follows from
Lemma

2.6
.
∎

We start with the case when the wedge is taken to be of two path graphs. We give a complete description of all the cases.
In the later part of the section, this will be generalized in the case of a wedge of finitely many path graphs, using the concept of the terminal wedge.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(P_{m}\underset{a}{\vee}P_{n})\simeq\mathbb{S}^{0}*\mathcal{I}(P_{m-3}\underset{a}{\vee}P_{n}).

Thus we only need to compute the independence complexes for the cases
m=1,2
and
3
(Referring to the diagram

, we assume
n=l+k
).

In this case the wedge is same as

.

Case 2

Take

The case of a wedge of the cycle graphs is not straightforward and hence we need to do a more careful analysis. This is an almost replica of Kozlov’s work.
But here we have more than one cycle, so we need to consider the maximal simplex appropriately inside the deletion which doesn’t belong to the link. Here is the statement of the main result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
C_{m}\underset{a}{\vee}C_{n}
be the wedge of cycle graphs and
G_{m,n}=\mathcal{I}\left(C_{m}\underset{a}{\vee}C_{n}\right)

G_{m,n}\simeq\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n})\right)}\vee\sum\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}.

Proof.

We denote
\mathcal{I}\left(C_{m}\underset{a}{\vee}C_{n}\right)
by
G_{m,n}
.
We show that
\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
is contractible in
\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
.
Then the result follows from
Lemma

2.6
.
Observe that, in general we have

\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}\simeq\mathcal{I}(P_{m-2})*\mathcal{I}(P_{n-2})~~\text{ and }~~\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}\simeq\mathcal{I}(P_{m-1})*\mathcal{I}(P_{n-1}).

We consider the following cases:

Case 1

n=3k
or
m=3k^{\prime}
.

In this case, either
\mathcal{I}(P_{m-2})\sim\mathcal{I}(P_{3(k-1)+1})
or
\mathcal{I}(P_{n-2})\simeq\mathcal{I}(P_{3(k^{\prime}-1)+1})
. From
Theorem

2.12
, we get that either
\mathcal{I}(P_{m-2})
or
\mathcal{I}(P_{n-2})
is contractible. Therefore,
\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
is contractible. This, from
Lemma

2.6
result follows.

Case 2

n=3k+1
or
m=3k^{\prime}+1
.

Now consider the maximal simplex

\sigma=\{a_{1},a_{3},\dots,a_{3k-3},a_{3k-1}\}\cup\{b_{1},b_{3},\dots,b_{3k^{\prime}-3},b_{3k^{\prime}-1}\}.

Then
\sigma\in\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}\setminus\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
. We also have
\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}\simeq\mathcal{I}(P_{3k})*\mathcal{I}(P_{3k^{\prime}})\simeq\mathbb{S}^{k+k^{\prime}+1}
using

2.12

\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}\setminus{\sigma}
is contractible. Now the result follows from
Lemma

3.1
.

Case 2

n=3k+1
and
m=3k^{\prime}+2
.

In this case,
\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
is contractible. Therefore, the result follows from
Lemma

2.6
.

In the remaining cases

Case 3

n=3k+2
and
m=3k^{\prime}+1

and

Case 4

n=3k+2
and
m=3k^{\prime}+2

we get that
\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
is contractible. Therefore, the result follows from
Lemma

2.6

Finally, in any case we have
G_{m,n}\simeq\textup{del}{\left(a,G_{m,n})\right)}\vee\Sigma\textup{lk}{\left(a,G_{m,n}\right)}
.

Let

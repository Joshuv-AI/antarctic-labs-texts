Examples of 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) spaces with non-constant dimension
Mattia Magnabosco

Abstract
In this work, we generalize the results obtained in [ 10 ] , presenting some examples of 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) spaces having different dimensions in different regions, deducing in particular that the topological splitting may fail in 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) spaces. We also observe that any reasonable non-branching condition may fail in 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) spaces and that the existence of an optimal transport map, between two absolutely continuous marginals, is not guaranteed by the 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) condition, without requiring a non-branching assumption. Moreover, we show that the strict 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) condition is strictly stronger than the classical 𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{CD}(0,N) one and it is not stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.



1 Introduction

In their seminal papers
[
17
,
18
,
9
]
, Sturm and Lott–Villani introduced the so-called
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition, a synthetic notion representing a lower
curvature bound by
K
and an upper bound on the dimension by
N
, formulated in the non-smooth setting of metric measure spaces. Their works are based on the observation that, for a (weighted) Riemannian manifold, having Ricci curvature bounded below and dimension bounded above, can be equivalently characterized in terms of a convexity property of the Rényi entropy functional, along Wasserstein geodesics. In particular, this property relies on the theory of optimal transport and does not require the smooth underlying structure, therefore it can be taken as definition of curvature dimension bound for a metric measure space.

In this paper we show different examples of
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces (i.e. spaces satisfying the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition), having different singularities in their metric measure structure. This work is a twofold generalization of
[
10
]
, where an example of a highly branching
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
space with non-constant (topological) dimension is constructed. On the one hand, we extend the result of
[
10
]
to
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces, having a finite dimensional bound
N
. This generalization is somewhat expected but far from trivial, in fact the finite dimensional
\mathsf{CD}
condition implies some properties which are not guaranteed in
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
spaces (for example the Bishop-Gromov inequality). On the other hand, we prove the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition for a class of metric measure spaces which is considerably larger than the one considered in
[
10
]
. This allows to highlight other types of singular behaviour that are proved to be possible in
\mathsf{CD}
spaces.

The main conclusions, which can be drawn from the examples of
\mathsf{CD}
spaces considered in this paper, are the following:

•

The constancy of dimension may fail in
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces, i.e. there exists a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space having different topological and Hausdorff dimensions in different regions (see Section
4.1
). This is particularly interesting if seen in relation with the work of Brué and Semola
[
2
]
, that proves constancy of dimension for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

•

The strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition (see Definition
4.4
) is not stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. Moreover, the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition is strictly stronger than the classical
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
one, i.e. there exists a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space which does not satisfy the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition (see Section
4.2
).

•

Any reasonably meaningful non-branching condition may fail in
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces (see Section
4.2
).

•

The existence of an optimal transport map between two absolutely continuous marginals is not guaranteed in
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces, without assuming a non-branching condition (see Section
4.2
).

•

The topological splitting may fail in
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces, i.e. there exists a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space containing a subset isometric to
\mathbb{R}
, which does not topologically split as the product of
\mathbb{R}
with another space (see Section
4.3
).

This work, as it is for
[
10
]
, is inspired by the work of Ketterer and Rajala
[
8
]
, where similar conclusions were drawn for spaces satisfying the measure contraction property
\mathsf{MCP}(0,N)
. We consider metric measure measure spaces having metric structure and singularities similar to the ones consider in
[
8
]
, but equipped with a more complicated measure that allows to achieve the
\mathsf{CD}
condition (cfr.
[
8
, Remark 1]
). This idea was already developed in
[
10
]
, were the
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
condition was proved in a space with non-constant topological dimension. In this paper, we improve the strategy of
[
10
]
, extending it to a larger class of spaces and refining different estimates and computations to prove the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition. This generalization relies on the relation between the
(0,N)
-convexity of interpolating densities along geodesics and the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition (see
[
18
, Proposition 4.2]
). The combination of this observation with a suitable version of the Jacobi equation proved by Rajala in
[
14
]
, that allows to compute interpolating densities, results in Proposition
2.11
, which will be fundamental to prove the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition.

2 Preliminaries

2.1 CD spaces

A triple
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is called metric measure space if
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a complete and separable metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a locally finite Borel measure on it.
Denote by
\mathscr{P}(X)
the set of Borel probability measures on
X
and by
\mathscr{P}_{2}(X)\subset\mathscr{P}(X)
the set of those having finite second moment. We endow the space
\mathscr{P}_{2}(X)
with the Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
, defined by

W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathsf{Adm}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})}\int\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,y)\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y),

(1)

where
\mathsf{Adm}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is the set of all the admissible transport plans between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, namely all the measures in
\mathscr{P}(X^{2})
such that
(\mathtt{p}_{1})_{\sharp}\pi=\mu_{0}
and
(\mathtt{p}_{2})_{\sharp}\pi=\mu_{1}
. We say that
\pi\in\mathsf{Adm}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is an optimal transport plan between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
if it realizes the infimum in (
1
). Moreover, we say that an optimal transport plan
\pi
is induced by a map if there exists
T
measurable such that
\pi=(\id,T)_{\#}\mu_{0}
, in this case
T
is said to be an optimal transport map.
For every
N>1
, define the Rényi entropy functional

S_{N}(\mu)=-\int\rho^{1-\frac{1}{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

where
\rho
denotes the density of the absolutely continuous part of
\mu
with respect to the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
, i.e.
\mu=\rho\mathfrak{m}+\mu^{s}
with
\mu^{s}\perp\mathfrak{m}
. Call
\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
the set of all probability measures in
\mathscr{P}_{2}(X)
which are absolutely continuous with respect to the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
N>1
, the metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space (or to satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition) if for every pair of measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
there exists a constant speed
W_{2}
-geodesic
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
connecting them, along which the entropy functional
S_{N^{\prime}}
is convex for every
N^{\prime}\geq N
, i.e.

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq(1-t)S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0})+tS_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1}),\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Remark 2.2
.

Every constant speed
W_{2}
-geodesic
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
can be represented by a probability measure
\eta
on the space
\Geo(X)
of constant speed geodesics of
X
(parameterized on
[0,1]
), meaning that
(e_{t})_{\#}\eta=\mu_{t}
for every
t\in[0,1]
, where
e_{t}
is the evaluation map at time
t
.

As every convexity property, the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition can be equivalently characterized just looking at midpoints instead of whole geodesics (cfr.
[
10
, Proposition 1.8]
):

Proposition 2.3
.

The metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space if for every pair
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
there exists a midpoint
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
of
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, such that for every
N^{\prime}\geq N

S_{N^{\prime}}(\nu)\leq\frac{1}{2}S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0})+\frac{1}{2}S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1}).

The
\mathsf{CD}
condition can be defined also when the dimensional parameter takes the value
\infty
, requiring convexity of the entropy functional
\Ent:\mathscr{P}_{2}(X)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, defined as

\Ent(\mu):=\begin{cases}\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}&\text{if }\mu\ll\mathfrak{m}\text{ and }\mu=\rho\mathfrak{m}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

(2)

which is the limit of
N+NS_{N}
as
N\to\infty
.

Definition 2.4
.

A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
space (or to satisfy the
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
condition) if for every pair of measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
there exists a constant speed
W_{2}
-geodesic
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
connecting them, along which the entropy functional
\Ent
is convex, i.e.

\Ent(\mu_{t})\leq(1-t)\Ent(\mu_{0})+t\Ent(\mu_{1}),\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Remark 2.5
.

Observe that the entropy functionals
\Ent
an
S_{N}
(for some
N>1
) have different behaviour on singular measures, in fact
\Ent(\mu)=+\infty
whenever
\mu\not\ll\mathfrak{m}
, while the singular part of
\mu
does not contribute to the value of
S_{N}(\mu)
. This substantial difference is the reason why, in the definition of the
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
condition there is no need to require the Wasserstein geodesic to be contained in
\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
.

The
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition can be defined for every real value of the dimensional parameter
K
, but its formulation becomes more complicated as it involves the distortion coefficients
\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}
. We refer to
[
18
]
for the precise definition of the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition (with
K\neq 0
), this general notion will not be used in this work. Among other nice properties, the
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
condition has the monotonicity in the parameters that we expect from a requirement that represents a lower curvature bound and an upper dimensional bound, i.e.

\mathsf{CD}(K^{\prime},N^{\prime})\implies\mathsf{CD}(K^{\prime\prime},N^{\prime\prime})\quad\text{if }K^{\prime}\geq K^{\prime\prime}\text{ and }N^{\prime}\leq N^{\prime\prime},

for every
K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}\in(1,\infty]
.

2.2
(K,N)
-convexity of functions

Given
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N>0
and an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, a function
g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is said to be
(K,N)
-convex if

g^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq K+\frac{1}{N}(g^{\prime}(x))^{2}\quad\text{for every }x\in I.

In working with
(K,N)
-convex is often convenient to use the following equivalent characterization:
g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is
(K,N)
-convex if and only if the function
g_{N}:=e^{-g/N}
satisfies

g_{N}^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq-\frac{K}{N}g_{N}.

As a consequence, we also deduce that:

g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)\text{ is }(0,N)\text{-convex if and only if $g_{N}$ is concave.}

(3)

Example 2.6
.

The function
-\log:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is
(0,1)
-convex, while for every
K>0
the function

[0,1]\ni x\mapsto Kx^{2}

is
(0,2K)
-convex.

Remark 2.7
.

The
(0,N)
-convexity is invariant by linear reparametrization, meaning that, if
g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is
(0,N)
-convex, then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\neq 0
the function

t\mapsto g(\alpha+\beta t)

is
(0,N)
-convex (where defined). More in general, if
g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is
(K,N)
-convex, then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\neq 0
the function
t\mapsto g(\alpha+\beta t)
is
(\beta^{2}K,N)
-convex.

The
(K,N)
-convexity enjoys different nice properties, among these the following additivity for the constants will be particularly important in section
3
.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
g\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is
(K_{1},N_{1})
-convex and
h\in C^{2}\big(I,\mathbb{R}\big)
is
(K_{2},N_{2})
-convex, then
g+h
is
(K_{1}+K_{2},N_{1}+N_{2})
-convex,

For a proof of this lemma we refer to
[
4
, Lemma 2.10]
.

We conclude the subsection with a technical lemma that will be useful to prove the main result (Theorem
3.1
).

Lemma 2.9
.

Given
A\in[0,\infty)
and
\delta\in\big(-\frac{1}{2^{11}},\frac{1}{2^{11}}\big)
, there exists a function
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
strictly positive on
[0,1)
, with
h(0)=1
,
h(1)=A
and
h\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)=1+\big(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\big)(A-1)
, such that
t\mapsto-\log(h(t))
is
(-2^{21}\,\delta^{2},2)
-convex

Proof
.

Let
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a
C^{2}
function such that
\phi\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)=1
,
\phi(t)=0
if
t\in\big[0,\frac{1}{4}\big]\cup\big[\frac{3}{4},1\big]
and
|\phi^{\prime}|\leq 2^{4},\,|\phi^{\prime\prime}|\leq 2^{7}
on
[0,1]
. Define
h
as

h(t)=1+t(A-1)+\delta\phi(t)(A-1),

observe that

h^{\prime}(t)=(A-1)[1+\delta\phi^{\prime}(t)]\qquad\text{and}\qquad h^{\prime\prime}(t)=\delta\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)(A-1).

Now, we want to prove that

\inf_{t\in[0,1]}(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}[(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime}]^{2}=\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\frac{1}{2}\frac{h^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{h(t)^{2}}-\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h(t)}\geq-2^{21}\,\delta^{2},

(4)

to this aim we divide the problem in two cases.
First of all, we prove (
4
) when
|A-1|\geq 2^{11}\delta
. In this case we have that

\begin{split}|h(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)|=\big|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)\big[\delta(A-1)+\delta(A-1)^{2}(1+&\delta\phi(t))\big]\big|\\
&\leq 2^{7}\left[\frac{1}{2^{11}}|A-1|^{2}+\frac{1}{2^{10}}|A-1|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4}|A-1|^{2}\end{split}

while, on the other hand,

h^{\prime}(t)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|A-1|^{2}.

Putting together these two inequalities, we conclude that

\inf_{t\in[0,1]}(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}[(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime}]^{2}=\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\frac{h^{\prime}(t)^{2}-2h(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2h(t)^{2}}\geq 0,

which in particular implies (
4
).
Assume now that
|A-1|<2^{11}\delta
. Notice that

\inf_{t\in[0,1]}(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}[(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime}]^{2}\geq-\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h(t)}=-\sup_{t\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]}\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h(t)}

where the last equality is true because
\phi
is constant on
\big[0,\frac{1}{4}\big]\cup\big[\frac{3}{4},1\big]
. Moreover, observe that, since
|\delta|<\frac{1}{2^{11}}
, we have
h(t)\geq\frac{1}{8}
on
\big[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\big]
, therefore we deduce

\inf_{t\in[0,1]}(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}[(-\log(h(t)))^{\prime}]^{2}\geq-8\sup_{t\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]}|h^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq-8\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\delta|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)||A-1|\geq-2^{21}\,\delta^{2},

concluding the proof.
∎

2.3 Definition of the Metric Measure Spaces

In this section we define the metric measure spaces that will be studied in the following.
For every
0<k<\frac{1}{4}
, introduce the class

\mathscr{F}_{k}:=\big\{f\in C^{2}\big([-1,1]\big)\,:\,0<f<3k,\,|f^{\prime}|\leq k,\,|f^{\prime\prime}|\leq 1\big\}.

(5)

Then, for every
f\in\mathscr{F}_{k}
define the set

X_{f}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,x\in[-1,1]\text{ and }0\leq y\leq f(x)\}.

This space will be equipped by the distance
\mathsf{d}_{\infty}
induced by the
l_{\infty}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that is

\mathsf{d}_{\infty}\big((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\big)=\max\{|x_{2}-x_{1}|,|y_{2}-y_{1}|\}.

Observe that, since we imposed
k
to be less than
\frac{1}{4}
,
\mathsf{d}_{\infty}
is a geodesic distance on
X_{f}
. Finally, for every
K\geq 1
, define the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{f,K}
on
X_{f}
as

\mathfrak{m}_{f,K}=m_{f,K}(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{L}^{2}|_{X_{f}}:=\frac{1}{f(x)}\exp\left(-K\left(\frac{y}{f(x)}\right)^{2}\right)\cdot\mathscr{L}^{2}|_{X_{f}}.

A simple computation shows that for every
f\in\mathscr{F}_{k}
and
K\geq 1
it holds that

(\mathtt{p}_{x})_{\#}\mathfrak{m}_{f,K}=C_{K}\cdot\chi_{\{-1\leq x\leq 1\}}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{1},

where
C_{K}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-Ky^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y
and
\mathtt{p}_{x}
denotes the projection on the
x
-axis.

In section
3
we will prove that it is possible to find constants
k\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,
K\geq 1
and
N>1
such that, for every
f\in\mathscr{F}_{k}
, the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
satisfies the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition. In the following, we will assume to have fixed an
f\in\mathscr{F}_{k}
and we will develop an argument that only uses the properties of
f
, see (
5
), proving in particular the result for the whole class of functions. Moreover, in order to ease the notation, we will usually denote the space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
simply by
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

2.4 How to Prove Convexity of the Entropy

In this section we prove an important result (Proposition
2.11
) that will be a fundamental ingredient in proving the CD condition. The proof of Proposition
2.11
relies on the possibility to compute the density of a pushforward measure, through Jacobi equation. For example, take two measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{2}
, with densities
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
. If there exists a smooth one-to-one map
T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
, then Jacobi equation ensures that

\rho_{1}(T(x,y))J_{T}(x,y)=\rho_{0}(x,y),

(6)

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
. The assumptions on the map
T
can be relaxed in different ways, in this work we are particularly interested in the following version, which is a straightforward consequence of
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{2}
and assume that there exists a map
T=(T_{1},T_{2})
which is injective outside a
\mu_{0}
-null set, such that
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
. Suppose also that
T_{1}
locally does not depend on the
y
coordinate and it is increasing in
x
, while
T_{2}
is increasing in
y
for every fixed
x
. Then the Jacobi equation (
6
) is satisfied with
J_{T}=\frac{\partial T_{1}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial T_{2}}{\partial y}
.

This proposition shows in particular that the Jacobi equation can be properly adapted to our setting.

Using Jacobi equation we can deduce the following criterion to prove convexity of the entropy, by looking at local quantities instead of global ones.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
and
T:X\to X
be an optimal transport map between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, in particular
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
. Consider a midpoint
\mu_{1/2}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
of
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, assume that
\mu_{1/2}=[M\circ(\id,T)]_{\#}\mu_{0}
where the map
M:X\times X\to X
is a (measurable) midpoint selection. Suppose also that the maps
T
and
M\circ(\id,T):X\to X
are injective outside a
\mu_{0}
-null set and they satisfy the Jacobi equation (
6
), with suitable Jacobian functions
J_{T}
and
J_{M\circ(\id,T)}
. If

\left(m\big(M((x,y),T(x,y))\big)J_{M\circ(\id,T)}(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(m(T(x,y))J_{T}(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{1}{2}(m(x,y))^{\frac{1}{N}}

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
, then

S_{N}(\mu_{1/2})\leq\frac{1}{2}S_{N}(\mu_{0})+\frac{1}{2}S_{N}(\mu_{1}).

(7)

Proof
.

Set
\mu_{0}=\rho_{0}\mathfrak{m}=\tilde{\rho}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{2}
,
\mu_{1}=\rho_{1}\mathfrak{m}=\tilde{\rho}_{1}\mathscr{L}^{2}
and
\mu_{1/2}=\rho_{1/2}\mathfrak{m}=\tilde{\rho}_{1/2}\mathscr{L}^{2}
, observe that, in order to prove (
7
), it is sufficient to prove that

\rho_{1/2}\big(M((x,y),T(x,y))\big)^{-\frac{1}{N}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}(T(x,y))^{-\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}(x,y)^{-\frac{1}{N}},

(8)

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
.
On the other hand, our assumption on the validity of Jacobi equation for
T
ensures that

\tilde{\rho}_{1}(T(x,y))J_{T}(x,y)=\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x,y),

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
, and thus that

\rho_{1}(T(x,y))m(T(x,y))J_{T}(x,y)=\rho_{0}(x,y)m(x,y).

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
.
Analogously, since the Jacobi equation holds for
M\circ(\id,T)
, we can deduce that

\rho_{1/2}\big(M((x,y),T(x,y))\big)m\big(M((x,y),T(x,y))\big)J_{M\circ(\id,T)}(x,y)=\rho_{0}(x,y)m(x,y),

for
\mu_{0}
-almost every

\left(\frac{\rho_{0}(x,y)m(x,y)}{m\big(M((x,y),T(x,y))\big)J_{M\circ(\id,T)}(x,y)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{N}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{0}(x,y)m(x,y)}{m(T(x,y))J_{T}(x,y)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{0}(x,y))^{-\frac{1}{N}}.

2.5 Definition of the Midpoint

According to Proposition
2.3
, in order to prove
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition, it is sufficient to show entropy convexity in a suitable midpoint of any pair of absolutely continuous measures. Observe that in highly branching metric measure spaces, like
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})=(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
, the choice of a midpoint can be done with great freedom. This is because, in general, both the optimal transport map and the geodesic interpolation are not unique. In this section, we present the midpoint selection used in
[
10
]
.
In particular, for any pair
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, we select a suitable optimal transport map
T
between them and then we identify a Wasserstein midpoint with a suitable midpoint interpolation map
M
.
In order to define both the optimal transport map
T
and the midpoint interpolation map
M
, we introduce the sets
V,D,H,H_{0},H_{\frac{1}{2}},H_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as:

V:=\left\{\left(\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right),\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\left|x_{0}-x_{1}\right|<\left|y_{0}-y_{1}\right|\right\},

D:=\left\{\left(\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right),\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\left|x_{0}-x_{1}\right|=\left|y_{0}-y_{1}\right|\right\},

H:=\left\{\left(\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right),\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\left|x_{0}-x_{1}\right|>\left|y_{0}-y_{1}\right|\right\}=H_{0}\cup H_{1},

where

H_{0}:=\left\{\left(\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right),\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{1}{2}\left|x_{0}-x_{1}\right|\geq\left|y_{0}-y_{1}\right|\right\},

H_{1}:=\left\{\left(\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right),\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\left|x_{0}-x_{1}\right|>\left|y_{0}-y_{1}\right|>\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}-x_{1}|\right\}.

First of all we present our optimal transport map selection, which follows the work of Rajala
[
14
]
. In particular, given two absolutely continuous measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, he was able to select, via consecutive minimizations, an optimal transport map with different nice properties, which are summarized in the following statement.

Proposition 2.12
.

Given two measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{2}
, there exists a measurable optimal transport map

between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, injective outside a
\mu_{0}
-null set, with the following properties. For
\mu_{0}
-almost every

, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}T_{1}\text{ is locally constant in }y,\text{ if }((x,y),T(x,y))\in H\text{ and }\\
T_{2}\text{ is locally constant in }x,\text{ if }((x,y),T(x,y))\in V\end{array}.

Moreover, the function
T_{1}(x,y)
is increasing in
x
for every fixed
y
and the function
T_{2}(x,y)
is increasing in
y
for every fixed
x
, therefore for
\mu_{0}
-almost every
(x,y)
it holds

\frac{\partial T_{1}}{\partial x}\geq 0\text{ and }\frac{\partial T_{2}}{\partial y}\geq 0,\text{ if }((x,y),T(x,y))\in H\cup V.

Now fix two measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, observe that, since they are absolutely continuous with respect to the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
, they are absolutely continuous also with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{2}
. Call

and

\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(M(z,w),z)=\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(M(z,w),w)=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{d}_{\infty}(z,w)\quad\text{for every }(z,w)\in X\times X,

the desired midpoint will be
M_{\#}\big((\id,T)_{\#}\mu_{0}\big)=[M\circ(\id,T)]_{\#}\mu_{0}
.

The midpoint interpolation map

,

and

•

If

If

If

and

, introduce the quantity

and consequently define

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\big((x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left(\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}}{2},y_{0}+\tilde{y}(x_{0},x_{1},y_{0})+\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{0}}{2}-\tilde{y}(x_{0},x_{1},y_{0})\right)\left(2\frac{y_{1}-y_{0}}{x_{1}-x_{0}}-1\right)\right).

In the other cases where

can be defined analogously, every proof from now on will be done only taking care of this case, implying it can be easily adapted to the other cases.

The next statement, which combines Proposition 5.3 and Proposition 5.4 in

Proposition 2.13

For

is a midpoint interpolation map and the map

is injective outside a

-null set.

Remark 2.14

3 Proof of CD Condition

In this section we prove the main result of this work, showing the validity of the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)

(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})

[

, refining it in order to obtain the

Theorem 3.1
.

For

such that for every

the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})

\mathsf{CD}(0,N)

Before going into the proof of Theorem
3.1

[
10

Lemma 3.2
.

Having fixed the constant
K\geq 1
and given another constant

, it is possible to find
k
sufficiently small such that the following holds. Given any

y:I=[x_{0},x_{1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

y^{\prime}(x)\geq\frac{1}{4}

y^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq H\frac{k}{f(x)}

x\in I

(i.e.

-\log(m(x,y(x)))
is
(\frac{K}{32f_{I}^{2}},32K)
-convex.

Proof
.

From the proof of
[
10

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(-\log(m(x,y(x)))\big)=\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}+2K\frac{y(x)}{f(x)}\left(\frac{y^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}-\frac{y(x)f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)^{2}}\right),

in particular, keeping in mind that
\left|\frac{y(x)}{f(x)}\right|\leq 1
and
\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|\leq k
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(-\log(m(x,y(x)))\big)\bigg|&\leq\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\bigg|+2K\bigg|\frac{y(x)y^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)^{2}}\bigg|+2K\bigg|\frac{y(x)f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)^{3}}\bigg|\\
&\leq\frac{k}{f(x)}+2K\frac{y^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}+2K\frac{k}{f(x)}\leq 4K\frac{y^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)},\end{split}

where the last inequality holds for
k
sufficiently small.
Moreover, from the computations done in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
, we deduce that for
k
sufficiently small

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\big(-\log(m(x,y(x)))\big)\geq\frac{K}{f(x)^{2}}\,y^{\prime}(x)^{2}.

We can then conclude that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\big(-\log(m(x,y(x)))\big)\geq\frac{K}{f(x)^{2}}\,y^{\prime}(x)^{2}&\geq\frac{K}{32f_{I}^{2}}+\frac{1}{32K}\left[4K\frac{y^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\right]^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{K}{32f_{I}^{2}}+\frac{1}{32K}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(-\log(m(x,y(x)))\big)\right]^{2},\end{split}

which proves the
(\frac{K}{32f_{I}^{2}},32K)
-convexity.
∎

t\mapsto-\log\left(1+t\left(\frac{\partial T_{1}}{\partial x}-1\right)\right)-\log(h(t))-\log\Big(m\Big((1-t)x+tT_{1},z\big((1-t)x+tT_{1}\big)\Big)\Big)

is
(0,32K+3)
-convex. Proceeding as in the first case and keeping in mind the inequality in (

.

If instead

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\tilde{y}(x,T_{1},y)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{f\big(\frac{x+T_{1}}{2}\big)}{f(x)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{f\big(\frac{x+T_{1}}{2}\big)}{f(T_{1})}-1<0,

the argument can be adapted following the same strategy developed in the proof of Theorem 6.1 in

∎

Now, we want to combine Theorem
3.1
with the stability of the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, in order to prove the
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition for singular spaces. To this aim, we introduce the set

\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu:=\big\{f\in C^{2}\big([-1,1]\big)\,:\,0\leq f<3k,\,|f^{\prime}|\leq k,\,|f^{\prime\prime}|\leq 1\big\}\supset\mathscr{F}_{k},

and we extend the definition of the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
to all functions
f\in\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu

2.3
. In particular, given any
\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu

X_{f}
is the same as before, i.e.

X_{f}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,x\in[-1,1]\text{ and }0\leq y\leq f(x)\}.

while the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{f,K}
becomes singular:

\mathfrak{m}_{f,K}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(x)=0\}}\cdot C_{K}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{1}|_{y=0}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(x)>0\}}\cdot\frac{1}{f(x)}\exp\left(-K\left(\frac{y}{f(x)}\right)^{2}\right)\cdot\mathscr{L}^{2}|_{X_{f}},

where, as before,
C_{K}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-Ky^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y
.

Remark 3.3
.

The measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence is a notion of convergence for metric measure spaces that basically combines the Hausdorff convergence for the metric side and the weak convergence for the reference measures. It has different equivalent definitions (see
[
7

), but for the purpose of this paper it is sufficient to consider the definition given in Villani’s book
[

For

K

\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space.

Proof.

For suitable
k
and

3.1
guarantees the existence of

such that

is a

. Now, given
f\in\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
, take a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive numbers converging to

with respect to the the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. From the stability of the

makes the
y

\{f(x)=0\}

f\in\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
increasing and such that
\{f=0\}=[-1,0]
, the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
, which is
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)

\mathsf{CD}

\mathsf{CD}(0,N)

]
.

4.2 Stronger
\mathsf{CD}
conditions

There are different ways to require convexity of the entropy along Wasserstein geodesics and they originate different
\mathsf{CD}
conditions. In particular, the
\mathsf{CD}
condition defined in Section
2.1
is the weakest version, because it just requires the existence of a geodesic along which the entropy is convex. The most natural strengthening brings to the definition of the strong
\mathsf{CD}
condition.

Definition 4.1
.

A metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a strong
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
space (or to satisfy the strong
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
condition) if the entropy functional
\Ent
(see (
2
)) is convex along every constant speed
W_{2}
-geodesic connecting two measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
.

Remark 4.2
.

The strong
\mathsf{CD}
condition is usually defined with the dimensional parameter equal to
\infty
for a technical reason related to Remark
2.5
.

This strengthening of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition is sufficient to show the following result on the geodesic structure of the space, which was proved by Rajala and Sturm in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Every strong
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is essentially non-branching, i.e. for every pair
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, every
\eta\in\mathscr{P}(\Geo(X))
representing a geodesic (see Remark
2.2
) connecting them, is concentrated on a non-branching set of geodesics.

It is easy to realize that the same result cannot be true for
\mathsf{CD}
spaces, an example for this is the metric measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathscr{L}^{2})
, which is a
\mathsf{CD}(0,2)
space but it is not an essentially non-branching space. The same example shows that the strong
\mathsf{CD}
condition is not stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, and this constitutes a major flaw. It is interesting to wonder whether there exists a stronger version of the
\mathsf{CD}
condition, which is stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence and still guarantees some additional properties on the space. To this purpose we consider the definition of the strict
\mathsf{CD}
condition.

Definition 4.4
.

Given
N>1
, the metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space (or to satisfy the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition) if for every pair of measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
there exists
\eta\in\mathscr{P}(\Geo(X))
representing a constant speed
W_{2}
-geodesic
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
connecting them, such that, for every bounded measurable function
f:\Geo(X)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\int f\,\mathrm{d}\eta=1
and every
N^{\prime}\geq N
, the entropy functional
S_{N^{\prime}}
is convex along the geodesic
t\mapsto(e_{t})_{\#}(f\eta)
.

Analogously, it is possible to define the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
condition, which was proved not to be stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence in
[
10
]
. It is then not surprising that the same holds for the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
, in fact proceeding as in
[
10
]
(see in particular Section 7) we can prove the following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Fix constant
k
and
K
such that the conclusions of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
hold with
N>1
.

1.

For every
f\in\mathscr{F}_{k}
the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
satisfy the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition.

2.

Given
f\in\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
increasing and such that
\{f=0\}=[-1,0]
, the metric measure space
(X_{f},\mathsf{d}_{\infty},\mathfrak{m}_{f,K})
does not satisfy the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition.

In particular, according to what we did in the proof of Corollary
3.4
, we conclude that the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition is not stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

Observe also that point 2 in this last proposition, combined with Corollary
3.4
, shows that the strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition is actually strictly stronger than the (classical)
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
condition.

It is also possible to find a requirement which is intermediate between the strong
\mathsf{CD}
condition and the strict
\mathsf{CD}
one. This is called very strict
\mathsf{CD}
condition and was introduced and studied by Schultz in
[
16
]
. The original definition
[
16
, Definition 1]
is given with the dimensional parameter equal to
\infty
, but it can be easily adapted to the finite dimensional case following Definition
4.4
. Generalizing the work of Rajala and Sturm, Schultz proved that the very strict
\mathsf{CD}
condition is sufficient to deduce a non-branching property on the space.

Theorem 4.6
.

Every very strict
\mathsf{CD}(0,\infty)
space is weakly essentially non-branching, i.e. for every pair
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
there exists
\eta\in\mathscr{P}(\Geo(X))
representing a geodesic connecting them, that is concentrated on a non-branching set of geodesics.

Being weakly essentially non-branching, is possibly the weakest meaningful non-branching condition that can be defined in metric measure spaces. However, as a consequence of Corollary
3.4
, we can observe that is not satisfied in every
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
space. In fact, taking
f\in\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathscr{F}_{k}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
increasing and such that
\{f=0\}=[-1,0]
, it is not difficult to see that every
\eta\in\mathscr{P}(\Geo(X))
, representing a geodesic connecting a marginal
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, concentrated in the one dimensional part
X_{f}\cap([-1,0]\times\mathbb{R})
, and a marginal
\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{ac}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, concentrated in the two dimensional part
X_{f}\cap([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
, cannot be concentrated on a non-branching set of geodesics. Moreover, it is possible to observe that it cannot exist an optimal transport plan between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
, which is induced by a map. This proves that the essentially non-branching assumption is necessary to guarantee the existence of an optimal transport map, between absolutely continuous marginals in
\mathsf{CD}(0,N)
spaces (cfr.
[
6
,
15
,
3
,
12
]
).

Finally, we point out that Proposition
4.5
suggest that also the the very strict
\mathsf{CD}
condition should not be stable with respect to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. However, in
[
11
]
a stability result for the very strict
\mathsf{CD}
condition is proved, under some additional metric assumptions on the converging sequence and on the limit space.

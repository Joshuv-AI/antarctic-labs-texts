Grading of affinized Weyl semi-groups of Kac-Moody type
Paul Philippe

Abstract
For any Kac-Moody root datum 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , D. Muthiah and D. Orr have defined a partial order on the semidirect product W + a W^{a}_{+} of the integral Tits cone with the vectorial Weyl group of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , and a compatible length function. We classify covers for this order and show that this length function defines a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -grading of W + a W^{a}_{+} , generalizing the case of affine ADE root systems and giving a positive answer to a conjecture of Muthiah and Orr.



Introduction

Motivations

Reductive groups over p-adic fields

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a split reductive group scheme with the data of a Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}
containing a maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
. Let
W=N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}
be its vectorial Weyl group and
Y
be its coweight lattice:
Y=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbf{T})
.
The action of
W
on
\mathbf{T}
induces an action of
W
on
Y
and allows to form the semidirect product
W^{a}=Y\rtimes W
. This group called the extended affinized Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
, appears naturally in the geometry and the representation theory of
\mathbf{G}
over discretely valued fields. A foundational work in this regard was done by N. Iwahori and H. Matsumoto in 1965 (
[
IM65
]
), when they exhibited a Bruhat decomposition of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
indexed by
W^{a}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a non-archimedean local field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}\subset\mathcal{K}
, uniformizer
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}
and residue field
\mathds{k}_{\mathcal{K}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}/\pi
.
Let
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{K})
, let
K=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}})
its integral points and let
I
be its Iwahori subgroup, defined as
I=\{g\in K\mid g\in\mathbf{B}(\mathds{k}_{\mathcal{K}})\mod\pi\}.
The extended affinized Weyl group can be understood as
N_{G}(\mathbf{T}(\mathcal{K}))/\mathbf{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}})
, so admits a lift in
G
. Then,
G
admits a decomposition in
I
-double cosets indexed by
W^{a}
, the Iwahori-Matsumoto-Bruhat decomposition:

G=\bigsqcup\limits_{\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}}I\pi^{\lambda}wI.

(0.1)

The group
W^{a}
is a finite extension of a Coxeter group and thus admits a Bruhat order which arises from the geometry of the homogeneous space
G/I
: for any
\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}
,
I\pi^{\lambda}wI
is a subvariety of pure dimension
\ell(\pi^{\lambda}w)
in
G/I
, and its closure admits a disjoint decomposition in
I
orbits:

\overline{I\pi^{\lambda}wI}=\bigsqcup\limits_{\pi^{\mu}v\leq\pi^{\lambda}w}I\pi^{\mu}vI

(0.2)

which extends the Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition. The connection between the geometry of
G/I
and the combinatorial structure of
W^{a}
is deeper. In particular,
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials introduced by Kazhdan and Lusztig (
[
KL80
]
), defined as the number of points of certain intersections in
G/I
, are also given by a recursive formula based on the Bruhat order and the Bruhat length of
W^{a}
.

These polynomials appear in many topics around reductive groups over local fields, we aim to develop analogous polynomials when
\mathbf{G}
is replaced by a general Kac-Moody group.

Extension to Kac-Moody groups

Replace
\mathbf{G}
by a general split Kac-Moody group. Kac-Moody group functors are entirely defined by the underlying Kac-Moody root datum
\mathcal{D}
, as defined in
[
Rém02
, §2]
, and reductive groups correspond to root data of finite type. Then the Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition no longer holds on
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{K})
. However there is a partial Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition: there exists a subsemigroup
G^{+}
of
G
such that:

G^{+}=\bigsqcup\limits_{\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}_{+}}I\pi^{\lambda}wI.

(0.3)

The indexing set for this decomposition
W^{a}_{+}
is a subsemigroup of
W^{a}=Y\rtimes W
, and it appears naturally in other related contexts, for example when trying to construct an Iwahori-Hecke algebra for
G

[
BKP16
,
BPGR16
]
. Let us briefly explain how
W^{a}_{+}
is defined:

Let
\Phi
be the real root system of the root datum
\mathcal{D}
. It is an infinite set (unless
\mathcal{D}
is reductive) of linear forms on
Y
coming with a subset of positive roots
\Phi_{+}\subset\Phi
such that
\Phi=\Phi_{+}\sqcup-\Phi_{+}
. Let
Y^{++}=\{\lambda\in Y\mid\forall\alpha\in\Phi_{+},\;\alpha(\lambda)\geq 0\}
and
Y^{+}=W\cdot Y^{++}
. Then
W^{a}_{+}
is defined as
Y^{+}\rtimes W
. In the reductive case,
Y^{+}
coincides with
Y
and thus
W^{a}_{+}=W^{a}
.
However,
W^{a}
can no longer be conceived as a finite extension of a Coxeter system, hence there is a priori no Bruhat order on
W^{a}_{+}
, let alone on
W^{a}
. A well-behaved topology on
G^{+}/I
would allow to define an order on
W^{a}_{+}
through the analog of decomposition (
0.2
), but
G^{+}/I
does not seem to have a natural variety, nor even an ind-variety structure.

An order and two lengths on
W^{a}_{+}

In appendix of their article on the construction of an Iwahori-Hecke algebra for
G
an affine Kac-Moody group over a p-adic field
[
BKP16
, Appendix B2]
, A. Braverman, D. Kazhdan and M. Patnaik propose the definition of a preorder on
W^{a}_{+}
which would replace the Bruhat order of
W^{a}
and they conjecture that it is a partial order. In
[
Mut18
]
, D. Muthiah extends the definition of this preorder to any Kac-Moody group
G
, defines a
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
-valued length compatible with this preorder and hence shows that it is an order. In a joint work
[
MO19
]
, D. Muthiah and D. Orr then show that this length can be evaluated at
\varepsilon=1
to obtain a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued length strictly compatible with the order on
W^{a}_{+}
.

In order to build a Kazhdan-Lusztig theory of p-adic Kac-Moody groups, we want to understand how close this order is to the Bruhat order of an affine Coxeter group, which properties still hold and which do not. The definition of a
\mathbb{Z}
-length is already a significant step, but many important properties, which are known to hold for Bruhat orders, remain unknown in this context. Several were proved only for Kac-Moody root systems of affine simply laced type using the specific structure of affinized Weyl group of
W
in this context.

Choice of vocabulary

The order on
W^{a}_{+}
is often mentioned in the literature as "double affine Bruhat order" and the associated length as "double affine Bruhat length" because it is most studied in the case of
G
a Kac-Moody group of affine type (in which case
W
is an affine Weyl group). In this paper we refer to it as "the affinized Bruhat order" and "the affinized Bruhat length", denoted
\ell^{a}
, because we do not suppose that
W
is an affine Weyl group. Note that, if
W
is finite, then the affinized Bruhat length and order on
W^{a}_{+}
are just the ones induced by its Coxeter group structure.

Main result

Our main result is a positive answer to
[
MO19
, Conjecture 1.5]
in full generality:

For any partial order
\leq
on a set
X
, we say that
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
if
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
and
\{\mathbf{z}\in X\mid\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{y}\}=\{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\}
. A grading of
X
is a length function
\ell
on
X
strictly compatible with
\leq
and such that
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
if and only if
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
and
\ell(\mathbf{y})-\ell(\mathbf{x})=1
. Gradings thus give an easy classification of covers and more generally of saturated chains in
X
. The Bruhat length for a Coxeter group equipped with the Bruhat order is the prototypical example of a grading.

In
[
MO19
]
, Muthiah and Orr prove that if
\Phi
is of affine ADE type, the affinized Bruhat length gives a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
W^{a}_{+}
for the affinized Bruhat order and conjecture this to be true in general. The main result of this paper is a positive answer to this conjecture:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be any Kac-Moody root datum. Then the affinized length
\ell^{a}
on
W^{a}_{+}
defines a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
W^{a}_{+}
strictly compatible with the affinized Bruhat order.
Otherwise said, let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{a}_{+}
be such that
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
. Then

\mathbf{y}\text{ covers }\mathbf{x}\text{ if and only if }\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})=1.

(0.4)

Along the way, we obtain several geometric properties of covers for the affinized Bruhat order which we expect to be insightful even if the root datum is reductive (so
W
is finite and
W^{a}_{+}
is an affine Weyl group) as they only rely on the Coxeter structure of
W
. In particular, we obtain in Proposition
3.20
a classification of covers which generalize results obtained using quantum Bruhat graphs, in the reductive setting by T. Lam and M. Shimozono (
[
LS10
, Proposition 4.4]
) and F. Schremmer
[
Sch23
, Proposition 4.5]
, and in the affine simply laced setting by A. Welch (
[
Wel22
, Theorem 2]
).

Further directions

In an upcoming joint work with A. Hébert, we prove that any element of
W^{a}_{+}
admits a finite number of covers for the affinized Bruhat order. We use this finiteness in the context of masures to define
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, following Muthiah’s strategy exposed in
[
Mut19
]
and the work on twin masures of N. Bardy-Panse, A. Hébert and G. Rousseau
[
BPHR22
]
. Our understanding of covers is useful to compute these
R
-polynomials and we intend to use
R
-polynomials to define
P
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.

Another interesting (but quite long reach) question is the following:
W^{a}_{+}
appears as the affinization of
W
, which may be taken as an affinized version of a finite Coxeter group. Can we iterate the affinization process, e.g to obtain a valid theory for reductive or Kac-Moody groups on valued fields of higher dimensions?

Finally, little is known on the preorder defined on the whole semidirect product
W^{a}
, it could be insightful to study it and to connect it to the failure of the full Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition of
G
.

Organization of the paper

Proof strategy

The global strategy is to construct a non-trivial chain from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{y}
every time
\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{x}
verifies
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})>1
. Let
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}
denote the projection
W^{a}_{+}=Y^{+}\rtimes W\rightarrow Y^{+}
. We distinguish two cases which depend on the form of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
: The first case is when
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})
lies in the orbit of
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
, we call such covers the vectorial covers. The other case is when
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})\notin W\cdot\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
, we call such covers the properly affine covers.

If
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})\in W\cdot\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
, we show that the affinized Bruhat order on
\{\mathbf{z}\in W^{a}_{+}\mid\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{y}\}
is, in some sense, a lift of several Bruhat-like orders on
W
. We are then able to construct chains between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
from chains in
W
, we deduce a classification of vectorial covers. The characterization of properly affine covers is, at first glance, more involved. Through a careful study of the relation between the vectorial chamber containing
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
and the vectorial chamber containing
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})
, we show that the length difference
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})
can be rewritten in a more workable form, making clear the conditions for which it is equal to one. Then the difficulty is to build, explicitly, a non-trivial chain every time one of these conditions is not satisfied.

Organization

Section
1
consists of preliminaries. In Subsection
1.1
we formally define everything we mentioned in this introduction. In particular we give the definition of the affinized Bruhat order and the two affinized Bruhat lengths as they are given in
[
MO19
]
. To be more flexible, we chose to define the affinized Bruhat order on the whole affinized Weyl group
W^{a}=Y\rtimes W
, on which it may not be a preorder.

We show, amongst other preliminary results, that we indeed recover the affinized Bruhat order on
W^{a}_{+}
from this preorder in Subsection
1.3
.

We also give, in Subsection
1.2
, a geometric interpretation of
W^{a}_{+}
and its affinized Bruhat order, which is to be compared with the interpretation of the Bruhat order in the Coxeter complex of a Coxeter group. Even though it is not clearly mentioned in the rest of the paper, this geometric interpretation was very useful to construct chains and understand
W^{a}_{+}
.

In Section
2
, we prove Theorem
A
for vectorial covers. We define relative versions of the Bruhat order on
W
in Subsection
2.1
and we connect theses relative Bruhat orders to the affinized Bruhat length in Subsection
2.2
. This is enough to prove Theorem
A
when
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
(see Theorem
2.13
). Using finer results on parabolic quotients in Subsection
2.3
, we extend it to vectorial covers such that
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})\in W\cdot\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\setminus\{\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\}
(see Theorem
2.18
).

In Section
3
, we deal with properly affine covers. We first show in Subsection
3.1
that these covers are of a very specific form. Namely, if
\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
with
v,w\in W
and
\lambda\in Y^{++}
, then
\mathbf{y}
needs to be of the form
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
or
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
for some
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
.

The strategy is then to get enough necessary conditions on
v,w,\lambda,\beta
for
\mathbf{y}
to cover
\mathbf{x}
, in order to obtain a simplified expression for
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})
. Proposition
3.3
gives a first result in this direction. In Subsection
3.2
we fully exploit this strategy to obtain Expression (
3.15
) for the length difference.

Finally, in Subsections
3.3
and
3.4
, we construct various chains from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{y}
to prove that the quantities appearing in Expression (
3.15
) need to be minimal when
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
, which allows us to conclude the argument in Subsection
3.5
.

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Definitions and notation

Let
\mathcal{D}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I})
be a Kac-Moody root datum as defined in
[
Rém02
, §8]
. It is a quintuplet such that:

1.

I
is a finite indexing set and
A=(a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I\times I}
is a generalized Cartan matrix.

2.

X
and
Y
are two dual free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules of finite rank, we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the duality bracket.

3.

(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
(resp.
(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I}
) is a family of linearly independent elements of
X
(resp.
Y
): the simple roots (resp. simple coroots).

4.

For all
(i,j)\in I^{2}
we have
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle=a_{ij}
.

1.1.1
Vectorial Weyl group

For every
i\in I
set
s_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X):x\mapsto x-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},x\rangle\alpha_{i}
. The generated group
W=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
is the
vectorial Weyl group
of the Kac-Moody root datum.

The duality bracket
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induces a contragredient action of
W
on
Y
, explicitly
s_{i}(y)=y-\langle y,\alpha_{i}\rangle\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. The bracket is then
W
-invariant.

The vectorial Weyl group
W
is a Coxeter group with set of simple reflections
S=\{s_{i}\mid i\in I\}
, in particular it has a Bruhat order
<
and a length function
\ell
compatible with the Bruhat order. We refer to
[
BB05
]
for general definitions and properties of Coxeter groups. A
reflection
in a Coxeter group is any element conjugated to a simple reflection.

1.1.2
Real roots

Let
\Phi=W\cdot\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be the set of real roots of
\mathcal{D}
, it is a root system in the classical sense, but possibly infinite.
In particular let
\Phi_{+}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}\cap\Phi
be the set of positive real roots, then
\Phi=\Phi_{+}\sqcup-\Phi_{+}
, we write
\Phi_{-}=-\Phi_{+}
the set of negative roots.

The set
\Phi^{\vee}=W\cdot\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\mid i\in I\}
is the set of
coroots
, and its subset
\Phi^{\vee}_{+}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\cap\Phi^{\vee}
is the set of
positive coroots
.

To each root
\beta
corresponds a unique coroot
\beta^{\vee}
: if
\beta=w(\alpha_{i})
then
\beta^{\vee}=w(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
. This map
\beta\mapsto\beta^{\vee}
is well defined, bijective between
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
and sends positive roots to positive coroots. Note that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle=2
for all
\beta\in\Phi
.

Moreover to each root
\beta
we associate a reflection
s_{\beta}\in W
: If
\beta=w(\pm\alpha_{i})
then
s_{\beta}:=ws_{i}w^{-1}
. Explicitly it is the map
Y\rightarrow Y
defined by
s_{\beta}(x)=x-\langle\beta^{\vee},x\rangle\beta
. For any
\beta\in\Phi
we have
s_{\beta}=s_{-\beta}
and the map
\beta\mapsto s_{\beta}
forms a bijection between the set of positive roots and the set
\{ws_{i}w^{-1}\mid(w,i)\in W\times I\}
of reflections of
W
.

1.1.3
Inversion sets

For any
w\in W
, let
\mathrm{Inv}(w)=\Phi_{+}\cap w^{-1}(\Phi_{-})=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\mid w(\alpha)\in\Phi_{-}\}
. Theses sets are strongly connected to the Bruhat order, as by
[
Kum02
, 1.3.13]
, for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{+}

\alpha\in\mathrm{Inv}(w)\iff ws_{\alpha}<w\iff s_{\alpha}w^{-1}<w^{-1}.

(1.1)

Moreover, they are related to the Bruhat length:
\ell(w)=|\mathrm{Inv}(w)|
(
[
Kum02
, 1.3.14]
).

1.1.4
Fundamental chamber and Tits cone

We define the (closed) integral fundamental chamber by
Y^{++}=\{\lambda\in Y\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle\geq 0\;\forall i\in I\}
. If
\lambda\in Y^{++}
, we say that it is a
dominant coweight
. Then, the integral Tits cone is
Y^{+}:=\bigcup\limits_{w\in W}w(Y^{++})
. It is a convex cone of
Y
, in particular it is a semigroup for the group operation of
Y
, and it is equal to
Y
if and only if
W
is finite, if and only if
\Phi
is finite, if and only if
A
is of finite type (see
[
Kum02
, 1.4.2]
).

The integral fundamental chamber
Y^{++}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W
on
Y^{+}
, and for any
\lambda\in Y^{+}
we define
\lambda^{++}
to be the unique element of
Y^{++}
in its
W
-orbit.

There is a height function on
Y^{+}
, defined as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\Lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
be a set of fundamental weights, that is to say
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\Lambda_{i}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for any
i,j\in I
. We fix it once and for all. Let
\rho=\sum_{i\in I}\Lambda_{i}
. Then for any
\lambda\in Y
define the
height
of
\lambda
as:

\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda,\rho\rangle.

(1.2)

The height depends on the choice of fundamental weights, but its restriction to
Q^{\vee}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
does not:
\mathrm{ht}(\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}
.

1.1.5
Parabolic subgroups, minimal coset representatives

For
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, let
\Phi_{\lambda}
denote the set
\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=0\}
and
W_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Stab}_{W}(\lambda)
. We say that
\lambda
is
regular
if
\Phi_{\lambda}=0
, or equivalently if
W_{\lambda}=1_{W}
. More generally we say that
\lambda
is
spherical
if
W_{\lambda}
is finite.

Let
v\in W
be such that
\lambda=v\lambda^{++}
. Then
W_{\lambda}v=vW_{\lambda^{++}}
and, since
\lambda^{++}
is dominant,
W_{\lambda^{++}}
is a standard parabolic subgroup, that is a group of the form
W_{J}=\langle s\mid s\in J\rangle
where
J\subset S
is a set of simple reflections. More precisely,
J=\{s\in S\mid s(\lambda^{++})=\lambda^{++}\}
.

By standard Coxeter group theory (see for instance
[
BB05
, Section 2.2]
), for any
u\in W
, the left coset
uW_{\lambda^{++}}=uW_{J}
has a unique representative of minimal length which we denote
u^{J}
, and one has a decomposition
u=u^{J}u_{J}
with
u_{J}\in W_{J}
such that:

\ell(u)=\ell(u^{J})+\ell(u_{J}).

(1.3)

Notation 1.2
.

1.

For any
J\subset S
, we denote by
W^{J}
the set of minimal length representatives for
W_{J}-
cosets in
W
:

w\in W^{J}\iff\forall\widetilde{w}\in W_{J},\;\ell(w\widetilde{w})>\ell(w)\iff\forall s\in J,\;\ell(ws)>\ell(w).

(1.4)

If
\lambda\in Y^{++}
is such that
W_{\lambda}=W_{J}
, then we may use
W^{\lambda}
as an alternative notation for
W^{J}
.

2.

For any
\lambda\in Y^{+}
(not necessarily dominant), we denote by
v^{\lambda}
the minimal length element in
W
which verifies
\lambda=v^{\lambda}\lambda^{++}
:

v^{\lambda}=\min\{v\in W\mid\lambda=v\lambda^{++}\}.

(1.5)

In other words, for any
u\in W
such that
\lambda=u\lambda^{++}
, we have
v^{\lambda}=u^{J}
, where
J
is the set of simple reflections such that
W_{J}=W_{\lambda^{++}}
.

1.1.6
Affinized Weyl semigroup

The action of
W
on
Y
allows to form the semidirect product
Y\rtimes W
, which we denote
W^{a}
. We denote its elements by
\pi^{\lambda}w
with
\lambda\in Y,w\in W
.

By definition,
Y^{+}\subset Y
is stable by the action of
W
on
Y
, therefore we can form
W^{a}_{+}=Y^{+}\rtimes W
which is a subsemigroup of
W^{a}
. This semigroup is called the affinized Weyl semigroup. In
[
MO19
]
Muthiah and Orr define a Bruhat order and an associated length function on
W^{a}_{+}
which we aim to study in this article.

Denote by
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}:W^{a}_{+}\rightarrow Y^{+}
the canonical projection, which sends
\pi^{\lambda}w
onto
\lambda
. Moreover denote by
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}:W^{a}_{+}\rightarrow Y^{++}
the projection to
Y^{++}
:
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x}))^{++}
. Let us call
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
the
coweight
of
\mathbf{x}
, and
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})
its
dominance class
.

1.1.7
Affinized roots

Let
\Phi^{a}=\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}
be
the set of affinized roots
and denote by
\beta+n\pi
the affinized root
(\beta,n)
. The affinized root

or (
n=0

\beta\in\Phi_{+}
) and we write

for the set of positive affinized roots. We have

The semidirect product

acts on

by:

\pi^{\lambda}w(\beta+n\pi)=w(\beta)+(n+\langle\lambda,w(\beta)\rangle)\pi.

(1.6)

For any

For

and

(1.7)

\displaystyle s_{\beta[n]}=\pi^{n\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}.

1.1.8
Bruhat order on
W^{a}_{+}

Recall Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik’s definition of the Bruhat order

introduced in

, Section B. 2]

Let

and let

be such that
\mathbf{x}s_{\beta[n]}\in W^{a}_{+}

\mathbf{x}<\mathbf{x}s_{\beta[n]}\iff\sgn(\beta+n\pi)=\sgn(\mathbf{x}(\beta+n\pi))\iff\mathbf{x}(\beta[n])\in\Phi_{+}^{a}.

(1.9)

Explicitly, if

\displaystyle\sgn(n)(n+\langle\lambda,w(\beta)\rangle)>0

\displaystyle\text{or}\;n=-\langle\lambda,w(\beta)\rangle\;\text{and}\;\sgn(n)w(\beta)>0.

Then we extend this relation by transitivity, which makes it a preorder. Originally, Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik defined it only for affine vectorial Weyl groups, but the definition extends to any vectorial Weyl group and Muthiah showed in
[

As the whole semidirect product

1.1.10
Bruhat order through a right action

In this paper, we consider multiplication by reflections on the left. To switch between the right and left actions note that:

s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{s_{\beta}\lambda+n\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}w=\pi^{\lambda+(n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\beta^{\vee}}ws_{w^{-1}(\beta)}=\pi^{\lambda}ws_{w^{-1}(\beta)[n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle]}.

(1.10)

In particular:

s_{\beta[0]}\pi^{\lambda}w=s_{\beta}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{s_{\beta}\lambda}s_{\beta}w\text{ and }s_{\beta[\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle]}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{\lambda}s_{\beta}w=\pi^{\lambda}ws_{w^{-1}(\beta)}.

(1.11)

Using Formula (
1.10
), the affinized Bruhat order can be recovered using a right action of

on
\Phi^{a}_{+}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}
and
(\beta,n)\in\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}\setminus(\Phi_{-}\times\{0\})

s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w>\pi^{\lambda}w\iff\sgn(n)w^{-1}(\beta)+(|n|-\sgn(n)\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\pi\in\Phi^{a}_{+}.

(1.12)

Remark 1.4
.

The root appearing in the right-hand side of Equivalence (

) is the affinized root
(\pi^{\lambda}w)^{-1}(\beta[n])
.

Proof.

Let
\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}
and
\beta+n\pi\in\Phi^{a}
. Then by Formulas (

) and (
1.10
),

s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w>\pi^{\lambda}w\iff\sgn(\beta+n\pi)=\sgn(w^{-1}(\beta)+(n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\pi).

If

so this is equivalent to

being a positive affinized root, which is Equivalence (

∎

Note that Formula (

) is no longer correct if

and

Applying reflections on the left is better suited for the geometric interpretation we will give in Subsection

1.1.11
Terminology on partially ordered sets

For
p\leq q\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\llbracket p,q\rrbracket
the set
\{r\in\mathbb{Z}\mid p\leq r\leq q\}
. If
p>q
, then
\llbracket p,q\rrbracket
is another notation for
\llbracket q,p\rrbracket
. We also write
\rrbracket p,q\llbracket
for
\llbracket p,q\rrbracket\setminus\{p,q\}
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},\leq)
be a partially ordered set.
For
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{P}
, we say that
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
are comparable if
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
or
\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{x}
. We say that
\mathbf{y}

covers

\mathbf{x}
if
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
and
\{\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{y}\}=\{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\}
, we then write
\mathbf{x}\lhd\mathbf{y}
.
If
\mathcal{P}=W^{+}_{a}
, covers
\mathbf{x}\lhd\mathbf{y}
such that
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})
are called
vectorial covers
and covers which are not vectorial covers are called
properly affine covers
.

A
chain
from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{y}
is a finite sequence
(\mathbf{x}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n})
such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{x}_{n}=\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{x}_{k}\leq\mathbf{x}_{k+1}
for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
. If
\mathcal{P}=W^{a}_{+}
(resp. if
\mathcal{P}
is a Coxeter group), we add the condition that
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}\mathbf{x}_{k}^{-1}
is an affinized reflection (resp. a reflection). A chain is
saturated
if
\mathbf{x}_{k}\lhd\mathbf{x}_{k+1}
for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
.
We say that a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{P}
is
convex
if, for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{P}
,

\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{y}\implies\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{C}.

(1.13)

Equivalently,
\mathcal{C}
is convex if and only if any chain from one element of
\mathcal{C}
to another is contained in
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\ell:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow A
be a function with values in a totally ordered set
A
, we say that it is
order-preserving
if, for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{P}
,

\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}\implies\ell(\mathbf{x})\leq\ell(\mathbf{y}).

(1.14)

We say that
\ell
is a
strictly compatible
(
A
-valued) length function if:

\mathbf{x}<\mathbf{y}\iff\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\text{ are comparable and }\ell(\mathbf{x})<\ell(\mathbf{y}).

(1.15)

We say that a strictly compatible length function
\ell
defines a
grading
of
\mathcal{P}
if:

\mathbf{x}\lhd\mathbf{y}\iff\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}\text{ and }\ell(\mathbf{x})\lhd\ell(\mathbf{y}).

(1.16)

In particular if
A=\mathbb{Z}
or
A=\mathbb{N}
, this rewrites

\mathbf{x}\lhd\mathbf{y}\iff\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}\text{ and }\ell(\mathbf{y})=\ell(\mathbf{x})+1.

(1.17)

For instance, the Bruhat length on a Coxeter group
W
is strictly compatible with the Bruhat order, and defines a
\mathbb{N}
-valued grading of
W
. Muthiah and Orr associated length functions strictly compatible with the Bruhat order on
W^{a}_{+}
, generalizing the classical Bruhat length on Coxeter groups. We now formally introduce these lengths.

1.1.12
Length functions on
W^{a}_{+}

Definition 1.5
.

The
affinized length function
is the map

defined by:

\ell^{a}_{\varepsilon}(\pi^{\lambda}w)=2\mathrm{ht}(\lambda^{++})+\varepsilon(|\{\alpha\in\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\geq 0\}|-|\{\alpha\in\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle<0\}|).

(1.18)

The

is the affinized length function on which we set

(1.19)

Theorem 1.6
.

[

, Theorem 4.24]

[

The affinized length function and the affinized length function with integral values are strictly compatible with the affinized Bruhat order on

1.2
Geometric interpretation

We introduced everything in a very algebraic way, but there is a strong geometric intuition behind root systems, vectorial Weyl groups and the vectorial Bruhat order, developed for instance in the context of buildings in
[
Ron89
]
. There is also a geometrical interpretation of the Bruhat order on
W^{a}_{+}
which we develop in this paragraph, it takes place in the standard apartment of the masure associated to a Kac-Moody group with underlying Kac-Moody datum
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
V=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, the lattice
X
embeds in its dual
V^{\vee}
and the vectorial Weyl group
W
acts naturally on it. Inside
V
we have the (closed) fundamental chamber
C^{v}_{f}=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\alpha_{i}\rangle\geq 0\}
and the Tits cone
\mathcal{T}=W\cdot C^{v}_{f}
. A
vectorial chamber
is a set of the form
w\cdot C^{v}_{f}
for
w\in W
. Since the interior of
C^{v}_{f}
has trivial stabiliser in
W
, the set of chambers is in natural bijection with
W
by
w\mapsto C^{v}_{w}:=w\cdot C^{v}_{f}
.

To each root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
let
M_{\beta}=\{x\in V\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle=0\}
, it is an hyperplane of
V
and, if
\beta=w(\alpha_{i})
with
\alpha_{i}
a simple root, then
C^{v}_{w}\cap C^{v}_{ws_{i}}\subset M_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{T}
. The intersection
C^{v}_{w}\cap C^{v}_{ws_{i}}
is called the panel of type
s_{i}
of
w
.

We can put a structure of simplicial complex on
\mathcal{T}
, for which chambers are the cells of maximal rank and panels are the cells of maximal rank within non-chambers. This simplicial complex is a realization of the
Coxeter complex
of
(W,S)
. Each wall splits the Tits cone in two parts, and separate the set of vectorial chambers in two: say that
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta}
if
w^{-1}(\beta)>0
. In particular since
\beta
is a positive root, the positive side is always the one which contain the dominant chamber.

Then the vectorial Bruhat order can be interpreted by:

s_{\beta}w>w
if and only if, when we split
\mathcal{T}
along
M_{\beta}
the chambers
C^{v}_{w}
and
C^{v}_{f}
are in the same connected component of
\mathcal{T}
, that is to say
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta}
.

Moreover
\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})
the inversion set of
w^{-1}
can be interpreted as the set of walls separating the chamber
C^{v}_{w}=w\cdot C^{v}_{f}
from the fundamental chamber
C^{v}_{f}
.

1.3
Preliminary results

Since the affinized Bruhat order is generated on
W^{a}_{+}
by the relations

s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}>\mathbf{x}\iff\ell^{a}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x})>\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})
for affinized roots
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
, covers are always of this form. In the rest of the paper, we always apply affinized reflections on the left.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}
and
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
. Write
\pi^{\mu}w^{\prime}
for
s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w
and suppose that
(\pi^{\lambda}w)^{-1}(\beta[n])\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
. Then
\lambda\in[\mu,s_{\beta}\mu]
. In particular

\mu\in Y^{+}\implies\lambda\in Y^{+}.

(1.24)

Proof.

Explicitly:

\pi^{\mu}w^{\prime}=\pi^{n\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}.\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{s_{\beta}\lambda+n\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}w.

Thus
\mu=s_{\beta}\lambda+n\beta^{\vee}=\lambda+(n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\beta^{\vee}
and
s_{\beta}\mu=\lambda-n\beta^{\vee}
.

Moreover, since
(\pi^{\lambda}w)^{-1}(\beta[n])\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
, by Formula (
1.12
):

|n|-\sgn(n)\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=\sgn(n)(n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\geq 0.

Therefore, unless
n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=0
,
n
and
n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
have same sign and thus,
\lambda=s_{\beta}\mu+n\beta^{\vee}=\mu-(n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)\beta^{\vee}
lies in
[s_{\beta}\mu,\mu]
. If
n-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=0
then
\mu=\lambda
and the result remains true.

The Tits cone
\mathcal{T}
is convex (
[
Kum02
, Proposition 1.4.2c)]
) and
W
-stable, so if
\mu\in\mathcal{T}
, then
[\mu,s_{\beta}\mu]
is contained in
\mathcal{T}
for any
\beta\in\Phi
. Therefore in the situation above, if
\mu\in Y^{+}=\mathcal{T}\cap Y
, then
\lambda\in[\mu,s_{\beta}\mu]\cap Y\subset\mathcal{T}\cap Y=Y^{+}
, and thus
\mu\in Y^{+}\implies\lambda\in Y^{+}
.
∎

We directly obtain from Lemma

the following result.

Proposition 1.8
.

The affinized Bruhat order defined on

coincides with the restriction of the preorder defined through (

) on the whole semidirect product
W^{a}
.

1.3.1
Properties of the height function

We give here a few elementary results on the height function, which will be useful in our study of the affinized Bruhat length. They are also used in
[
MO19
, Section 3]
.

Proposition 1.9
.

For any
w\in W
,

\rho-w^{-1}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(w)}\gamma.

(1.25)

Proof.

This is
[
Kum02
, 1.3.22 Corollary 3]
, we prove it by induction on the length of
w
.

1.

If
w
is a simple reflection
s_{\alpha}
then
\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\alpha})=\{\alpha\}
and
\rho-s_{\alpha}(\rho)=\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle\alpha=\alpha
since
\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle=1
by definition of
\rho
.

2.

Suppose the result true for elements of length
n
, and suppose that
\ell(w)=n+1
then write
w=w_{1}s_{\alpha}
for
\alpha
a simple root and
w_{1}
an element of length
n
. Then

\rho-w(\rho)=\rho-w_{1}(\rho)+w_{1}(\rho-s_{\alpha}(\rho))=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(w_{1}^{-1})}\gamma+w_{1}(\alpha)

and since
\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})=\mathrm{Inv}(w_{1}^{-1})\sqcup\{w_{1}(\alpha)\}
we get the result for
w
.

∎

Corollary 1.10
.

For any positive root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
we have

2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})}\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle.

(1.26)

Moreover all the terms in the sum are positive.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root. Note that
-s_{\beta}(\beta^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee}
and thus
\langle\beta^{\vee},\rho\rangle=\langle-s_{\beta}(\beta^{\vee}),\rho\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},-s_{\beta}(\rho)\rangle
. Therefore by Proposition
1.9
,

2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})=2\langle\beta^{\vee},\rho\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\rho-s_{\beta}(\rho)\rangle=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})}\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle.

Moreover for any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
, by definition
\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
and
s_{\beta}(\gamma)=\gamma-\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\beta^{\vee}\in\Phi_{-}
so, since
\beta
is a positive root, the coefficient
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle
is necessarily positive.
∎

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
\mu\in Y^{+}
and
u\in W
be such that
\mu=u(\mu^{++})
. Then

\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})=\mathrm{ht}(\mu)-\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\mu,\tau\rangle.

(1.27)

Moreover the terms in this sum are non-positive and

\mathrm{ht}(\mu)\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++}).

(1.28)

The inequality is strict unless
\mu
is dominant.

Proof.

By definition
\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})=\langle u^{-1}(\mu),\rho\rangle=\langle\mu,u(\rho)\rangle
, and by Proposition
1.9
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})=\langle\mu,u(\rho)\rangle=\langle\mu,\rho-\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\tau\rangle=\mathrm{ht}(\mu)-\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\mu,\tau\rangle.

Moreover, for any
\tau\in\Phi
, we have
\langle\mu,\tau\rangle=\langle\mu^{++},u^{-1}(\tau)\rangle
, so
\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})\implies\langle\mu,\tau\rangle\leq 0
and the terms of the above sum are all non-positive, we deduce Formula (
1.28
). If
\mu
is not dominant, then there exists
\tau\in\Phi_{+}
such that
\langle\mu,\tau\rangle<0
, and thus

\mathrm{ht}(\mu)<\mathrm{ht}(s_{\tau}\mu)\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++}).

∎

Amongst other things, Corollary
1.11
directly implies the following result, which was first indicated to the author by Hébert and Muthiah.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{+}
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
such that
s_{\beta}\lambda\neq\lambda
. Suppose that
\mu\in]\lambda,s_{\beta}\lambda[
. Then
\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})<\mathrm{ht}(\lambda^{++})
.

Proof.

Note that we do not suppose
\mu\in Y
, the height function is extended to
V=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
linearly.
Let
t\in]0,1[
be such that
\mu=t\lambda+(1-t)s_{\beta}\lambda
, let
v\in W
be such that
\mu^{++}=v\mu
. Then
\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})=\mathrm{ht}(v\mu)=t\mathrm{ht}(v\lambda)+(1-t)\mathrm{ht}(vs_{\beta}\lambda)
. By Corollary
1.11
,
\mathrm{ht}(v\lambda)\leq\mathrm{ht}(\lambda^{++})
and
\mathrm{ht}(vs_{\beta}\lambda)\leq\mathrm{ht}(\lambda^{++})
and, since
s_{\beta}\lambda\neq\lambda
, at least one of the two inequality is strict. We deduce
\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++})<\mathrm{ht}(\lambda^{++})
.
∎

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}
and
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
such that
s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}
. Then

\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})\iff n\in\{0,\langle\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x}),\beta\rangle\}.

(1.29)

Proof.

To simplify notation, let
\lambda\in Y^{+}
denote
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
. If
n\in\{0,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\}
then by Formula (
1.11
),
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x})\in\{s_{\beta}(\lambda),\lambda\}
and therefore it has same dominance class.

Conversely, if
n\in\rrbracket 0,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\llbracket
then
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x})=s_{\beta}(\lambda)+n\beta^{\vee}\in]\lambda,s_{\beta}(\lambda)[
, and if
n\notin\llbracket 0,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\rrbracket
then
\lambda\in]s_{\beta}(\lambda)+n\beta^{\vee},\lambda-n\beta^{\vee}[=]\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}),s_{\beta}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}))[
.

Either way by Lemma
1.12
,
\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}))\neq\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x}))
and in particular
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x})\neq\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})
.
∎

Remark 1.14
.

Note that if
n=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
, then by Formula (
1.11
),
s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{\lambda}ws_{w^{-1}(\beta)}
. Therefore Proposition
1.13
indicates that, if
\mathbf{y}=s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}
, then
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})
if and only if
\mathbf{y}
is obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by applying a vectorial reflection either on the left hand side or on the right hand side. This justifies the terminology for vectorial covers and properly affine covers.

Proposition 1.15
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{a}_{+}
and suppose that
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
. Then

\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})),

(1.30)

with equality if and only if
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})
.

In particular the function
\mathrm{ht}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}:W^{a}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is order-preserving.

Proof.

It is enough to prove it for cover relations, if
\mathbf{y}=s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}
for some
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
. In that case, by Lemma
1.7
we have
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\in[\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}),s_{\beta}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}))]
. If
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\in\{\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}),s_{\beta}\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})\}
then they have the same dominance class:
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})
and we obtain the equality case.

Otherwise,
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\in]\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}),s_{\beta}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}))[
, necessarily
s_{\beta}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y}))\neq\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})
and by Lemma
1.12
we deduce
\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x}))<\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y}))
.
∎

Corollary 1.16
.

For any
\lambda^{++}\in Y^{++}
, the set
\{\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}\mid\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{++}\}
is convex for the affinized Bruhat order.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.15
the function
W^{a}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}:\,\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathrm{ht}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}
is compatible with the affinized Bruhat order. Suppose that
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{a}_{+}
satisfy
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}+}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})
and
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
. Let
\mathbf{z}\in W^{a}_{+}
be such that
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{y}
. Then by Proposition
1.15
,
\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{z}))\leq\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y}))=\mathrm{ht}(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x}))
. By the equality case in Proposition
1.15
, we deduce
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{z})=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})
.
∎

Remark 1.17
.

Note that, for
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, the set
\{\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}\mid\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{++}\}
is the double
W
-orbit of
\pi^{\lambda}
:

\{\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}\mid\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{++}\}=W\pi^{\lambda}W.

(1.31)

We show in Section
2
that the right
W
-orbits
\pi^{\lambda}W
are also convex for the affinized Bruhat order.

We end this section with several metric properties of Coxeter groups, the results stated are proved in the context of Coxeter complexes and buildings in
[
Ron89
]
.

1.3.2
Metric properties of Coxeter groups

On any Coxeter group
(W_{0},S_{0})
we define a map
d\;:\;W_{0}\times W_{0}\rightarrow W_{0}
by
d(v,w)=v^{-1}w
, called the
vectorial distance
of
W_{0}
, it is
W_{0}
-invariant:
d(uv,uw)=d(v,w)
for any
u,v,w\in W_{0}
. We also define
d^{\mathbb{N}}=\ell\circ d
where
\ell
is the Bruhat length on
(W_{0},S_{0})
(note that
\ell
and
d^{\mathbb{N}}
depend on the set of simple reflections
S_{0}
, but the vectorial distance does not). These maps have properties analogous to the standard distance axioms, which justify the name (see
[
Ron89
, Chapter 3 §1]
).

An unfolded gallery (resp. a gallery) in
W_{0}
from
w
to
v
is a sequence
w=w_{0},\dots,w_{n}=v
such that
d^{\mathbb{N}}(w_{i},w_{i+1})=1
(resp.
d^{\mathbb{N}}(w_{i},w_{i+1})\in\{0,1\}
) for all
i\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
. A gallery is said to be
minimal
if its length
n
is equal to
d^{\mathbb{N}}(w_{1},w_{n})
, a minimal gallery is necessarily unfolded. We refer to
[
Ron89
, Chapter 2]
for properties of minimal galleries, but note that if
(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
is a minimal gallery then
d^{\mathbb{N}}(w_{0},w_{i})=i
and thus
(w_{0},\dots,w_{i})
is a minimal gallery from
w_{0}
to
w_{i}
. Moreover since the distance is
W_{0}
invariant,
(vw_{0},\dots vw_{n})
is also a minimal gallery for any
v\in W_{0}
.
The next Lemma is a reformulation of
[
Ron89
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Lemma 1.18
.

Let
(W_{0},S_{0})
be a Coxeter system and let
v_{1},v_{2},w\in W_{0}
be such that
v_{2}
is not on a minimal gallery from
v_{1}
to
w
. Then there is a reflection
r\in W_{0}
such that
d(v_{1},rw)>d(v_{1},w)
and
d(v_{2},rw)<d(v_{2},w)
.

Proof.

If
v_{2}
is not on a minimal gallery from
v_{1}
to
w
, by
[
Ron89
, Proposition 2.8]
there exists a root
\alpha
(seen as a half-apartment:
\alpha=\{u\in W_{0}\mid\ell(u)<\ell(s_{\alpha}u)\}
) such that
v_{1},w\in\alpha
and
v_{2}\notin\alpha
. Then consider the folding along
\alpha
, defined by:

\forall u\in W_{0},\;\rho_{\alpha}(u)=\begin{cases}s_{\alpha}u&\text{if $u\notin\alpha$}\\
u&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It reduces the vectorial distance (see
[
Ron89
, §2]
) hence

\displaystyle d(v_{1},w)

\displaystyle=d(\rho_{\alpha}(v_{1}),\rho_{\alpha}(s_{\alpha}w))<d(v_{1},s_{\alpha}w),

\displaystyle d(v_{2},s_{\alpha}w)

\displaystyle=d(s_{\alpha}v_{2},w)=d(\rho_{\alpha}(v_{2}),\rho_{\alpha}(w))<d(v_{2},w).

∎

Recall that for
J\subset S
,
W_{J}
is the subgroup generated by the set of simple reflections
J
. The Coxeter system
(W_{J},J)
is an example of Coxeter system for which we will use Lemma
1.18
. For any
w\in W
, the coset
wW_{J}
is convex, in the sense that, if
w_{1},w_{2}\in wW_{J}
then any minimal gallery from
w_{1}
to
w_{2}
lies in
wW_{J}
(see
[
Ron89
, Lemma 2.10]
).

Definition 1.19
.

For any
J\subset S
and
v,w\in W
, the
projection of
w
on
vW_{J}
is the unique element of
vW_{J}
which reaches
\min\limits_{\widetilde{v}\in vW_{J}}d^{\mathbb{N}}(w,\widetilde{v})
. It is denoted
\mathrm{proj}_{vW_{J}}(w)
. Moreover any minimal gallery from
v
to an element of
wW_{J}
goes through
\mathrm{proj}_{vW_{J}}(w)
(see
[
Ron89
, Theorem 2.10]
).

2
Restriction to constant dominance classes

In this section, we study the affinized Bruhat order restricted to a dominance class, that is to say, for a given
\lambda^{++}\in Y^{++}
, we study the restriction of the affinized Bruhat order to the subset
(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})=W\pi^{\lambda^{++}}W
. By Corollary
1.16
these are convex subsets for the affinized Bruhat order. We start by showing that, for any
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, the subset
\pi^{\lambda}W=(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}})^{-1}(\lambda)
of
(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})
is also convex for the affinized Bruhat order.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, recall Notation
1.2
for
v^{\lambda}
. Then
\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})\cap\Phi_{\lambda}=\varnothing
. In particular for any
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
,

\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)<\mathrm{ht}(s_{\beta}\lambda)\iff\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle<0\iff s_{\beta}v^{\lambda}<v^{\lambda}.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{+}
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})\cap\Phi_{\lambda}
, then since
\alpha\in\Phi_{\lambda}
,
s_{\alpha}
fixes
\lambda
, that is
s_{\alpha}\in W_{\lambda}
. Moreover
(v^{\lambda})^{-1}(\alpha)<0
so
s_{\alpha}v^{\lambda}<v^{\lambda}
, this contradicts the minimality of
v^{\lambda}
(note that, as
W_{\lambda}v^{\lambda}=v^{\lambda}W_{\lambda^{++}}
,
v^{\lambda}
is also the minimal representative for the right coset
W_{\lambda}v^{\lambda}
). Hence
\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})\cap\Phi_{\lambda}=\varnothing
, therefore any
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})
verifies
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\neq 0
.

For
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
,
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=\langle\lambda^{++},(v^{\lambda})^{-1}(\beta)\rangle
. Since
\lambda^{++}
is dominant, if this is negative then
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})
, and since
\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})
and
\Phi_{\lambda}
are disjoint, the converse is also true. Since
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})\iff s_{\beta}v^{\lambda}<v^{\lambda}
we deduce the second equivalence in Formula (
2.1
). Moreover
\mathrm{ht}(s_{\beta}\lambda)=\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})
by linearity of the height function, and since
\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})>0
, the first equivalence in Formula (
2.1
) is clear.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The fact that
\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})\cap\Phi_{\lambda}=\varnothing
is visible geometrically in the Coxeter complex of
W
, in which
\Phi_{\lambda}
is the set of walls containing
\lambda
,
\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})
is the set of walls separating
C^{v}_{f}
and
C^{v}_{v}
. The chamber
C^{v}_{v^{\lambda}}
is the closest chamber from the fundamental chamber amongst the chambers containing
\lambda
in their closure, in other words
v^{\lambda}=\mathrm{proj}_{W_{\lambda}}(1_{W})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
\pi^{\lambda}w\in W^{a}_{+}
and
r\in W
is a reflection such that
r\lambda\neq\lambda
. Then

\pi^{r\lambda}rw>\pi^{\lambda}w\iff rv^{\lambda}<v^{\lambda}.

(2.2)

Moreover for any
\lambda^{++}\in Y^{++}
, the restriction of the function
\mathrm{ht}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}
to
(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})
is order-preserving.

Proof.

Suppose that
r\in W
is a reflection which does not fix
\lambda
. By definition there exists a positive root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
such that
r=s_{\beta}
and, since
r
does not fix
\lambda
,
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\neq 0
. Note that
\pi^{r\lambda}rw=s_{\beta[0]}\pi^{\lambda}w
so, using Formula (
1.12
), we have

\pi^{r\lambda}rw>\pi^{\lambda}w\iff-\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle>0\iff\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle<0.

By Lemma
2.1
this is equivalent to
rv^{\lambda}<v^{\lambda}
, and to
\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)<\mathrm{ht}(r\lambda)
. This is enough to obtain Equivalence (
2.2
). Moreover by convexity of
(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})
(see Corollary
1.16
) and by Proposition
1.13
it also implies that
\mathrm{ht}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}\,:\,(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is order-preserving.

∎

Note that the function
\mathrm{ht}\circ\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}
is not order-preserving on the whole semi-group
W^{a}_{+}
. For example suppose that
\lambda\in Y^{++}
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
are such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is also dominant. Then we can check that
\pi^{s_{\beta}(\lambda)}<\pi^{s_{\beta}(\lambda)-\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}
whereas
\mathrm{ht}(s_{\beta}(\lambda)-\beta^{\vee})<\mathrm{ht}(s_{\beta}(\lambda))
.

Proposition
2.3
implies convexity of left
W
-cosets:

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, then the set
\pi^{\lambda}W=\{\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}\mid\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda\}
is convex for the affinized Bruhat order.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\pi^{\lambda}W
such that
\mathbf{x}<\mathbf{y}
and let
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{0}<\mathbf{x}_{1}<\dots\mathbf{x}_{n}=\mathbf{y}
be a chain from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{y}
, in particular for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
, let
\beta_{k}[n_{k}]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
be such that
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=s_{\beta_{k}[n_{k}]}\mathbf{x}_{k}
. For
k\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
, write
\mathbf{x}_{k}=\pi^{\lambda_{k}}w_{k}
with
\lambda_{k}\in Y^{+}
,
w_{k}\in W
. By convexity of
(\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}})^{-1}(\lambda^{++})
,
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}
is constant along the chain, therefore by Proposition
1.13
, for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
we have
\lambda_{k+1}\in\{\lambda_{k},s_{\beta_{k}}(\lambda_{k})\}
. From Proposition
2.3
we deduce that
v^{\lambda_{k+1}}\leq v^{\lambda_{k}}
. Since
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{n}=\lambda
,
v^{\lambda_{0}}=v^{\lambda_{n}}=v^{\lambda}
and thus
v^{\lambda_{k}}=v^{\lambda}
, so
\lambda_{k}=\lambda
for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
. Hence
\pi^{\lambda}W
is convex.
∎

2.1
Relative length on
W

We define a relative length and a relative Bruhat order on
W
, which naturally arises in the study of the affinized length
\ell^{a}
on
W^{a}_{+}
. This connection was already observed by Muthiah and Orr in
[
MO18
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

For any
v,w\in W
let:

\ell_{v}(w)=|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\setminus\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|-|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|.

(2.3)

This is a signed version of the Bruhat length, in particular
\ell_{1}=\ell
.

We associate an order to
\ell_{v}
by setting, for any element
w\in W
and any reflection
r\in W
:
w<_{v}wr
if and only if
\ell_{v}(w)<\ell_{v}(wr)
and then let
<_{v}
be the order generated by these relations, it is strictly compatible with
\ell_{v}
. In particular
<_{1}
is the classical Bruhat order.

As does the Bruhat length, the lengths
\ell_{v}
have a geometric interpretation in the Coxeter complex associated to
(W,S)
. For
M
a wall of the Coxeter complex and
w\in W
, let
\varepsilon_{w}(M)=-1
if
M
separates
C^{v}_{f}
and
C^{v}_{w}
, and
\varepsilon_{w}(M)=+1
otherwise. Then

\ell_{v}(w)=\sum\limits_{M\in\varepsilon_{w}^{-1}(-1)}\varepsilon_{v^{-1}}(M).

(2.4)

We will use this relative length to give an alternative definition of the affinized length. Let us first give an explicit formula for
\ell_{v}
depending only on the classical length
\ell=\ell_{1}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
sv>v
with
v\in W
and
s
a simple reflection then for any
w\in W
,
\ell_{sv}(w)=\ell_{v}(sw)-1
.

Proof.

For any
w\in W
, the map
\gamma\mapsto s\gamma
defines a bijection:

\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\setminus\{\alpha_{s}\}\cong\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\setminus\{\alpha_{s}\}.

Moreover because
sv>v
,
\alpha_{s}\in\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1}s)
and
\alpha_{s}\notin\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})
.

Therefore

|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1}s)\setminus\{\alpha_{s}\}|=|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|

and

|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})\setminus\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1}s)|=|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\setminus(\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})\cup\{\alpha_{s}\})|.

1.

If
\alpha_{s}\in\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})
then
\alpha_{s}\notin\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)
and
\ell_{sv}(w)=|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\setminus\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|-(|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|+1)=\ell_{v}(sw)-1
.

2.

If
\alpha_{s}\notin\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})
then
\alpha_{s}\in\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)
and
\ell_{sv}(w)=(|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\setminus\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|-1)-|\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1}s)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v^{-1})|=\ell_{v}(sw)-1
.

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

For all
v,w\in W
the relative length
\ell_{v}(w)
is given by

\ell_{v}(w)=\ell(v^{-1}w)-\ell(v).

(2.5)

Proof.

Since
\ell=\ell_{1}
, we take a reduced expression for
v
and apply Lemma
2.6
recursively to get the result.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

For any
v\in W
, the relative length
\ell_{v}
is a grading of
(W,<_{v})
.

Proof.

Let
v,w,w^{\prime}\in W
. By proposition
2.7
,
\ell_{v}(w^{\prime})-\ell_{v}(w)=\ell(v^{-1}w^{\prime})-\ell(v^{-1}w)
and
w^{\prime}
covers
w
for
<_{v}
if and only if
v^{-1}w^{\prime}
covers
v^{-1}w
for the (standard) Bruhat order. Since the Bruhat length is a grading of
(W,<_{1})
(see
[
BB05
, Theorem 2.2.6]
),
v^{-1}w^{\prime}
covers
v^{-1}w
if and only if
\ell(v^{-1}w^{\prime})-\ell(v^{-1}w)=1
and
v^{-1}w^{\prime}=v^{-1}wr
for some reflection
r\in W
. Hence
w^{\prime}
covers
w
for
<_{v}
if and only if
\ell_{v}(w^{\prime})-\ell_{v}(w)=1
and
w^{\prime}=wr
for some reflection
r\in W
:
\ell_{v}
is a grading of
(W,<_{v})
.
∎

The order
<_{v}
also has a geometric interpretation which will be important later on, it is given by the following corollary.

Corollary 2.9
.

For any root
\alpha\in\Phi
and elements
w,v\in W
, we have that
w<_{v}s_{\alpha}w
if and only if, in the Coxeter complex of
W
,
C^{v}_{w}
and
C^{v}_{v}
are on the same side of the wall
M_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We have
\ell_{v}(s_{\alpha}w)-\ell_{v}(w)=\ell(v^{-1}s_{\alpha}w)-\ell(v^{-1}w)=d^{\mathbb{N}}(v,s_{\alpha}w)-d^{\mathbb{N}}(v,w)
. By the definition of the Coxeter complex this is positive if and only if
C^{v}_{v}
and
C^{v}_{w}
are on the same side of the wall
M_{\alpha}
.
∎

Therefore
<_{v}

Lemma 2.14
.

Let

for

u_{i-1}^{-1}u_{i}

W_{J}

Proof.

If

1.

By construction, an expression of

2.

Since an expression of

3.

It remains to show that

(a)

Suppose that

(b)

Suppose now that there is

(with the convention that

). Then

∎

Definition 2.15

3.1
A few properties of properly affine covers

We now turn to the case of covers
\pi^{\lambda}w<\pi^{\mu}w^{\prime}
in

with
\mu^{++}\neq\lambda^{++}
. Such covers are called properly affine covers.

By Formula (
1.12
), if
\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w=s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w>\pi^{\lambda}w
with
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
, then

. Conversely, if
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\llbracket 0,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\rrbracket
then

as
\lambda+\mathbb{Z}\beta^{\vee}\not\subset Y^{+}
. The limit cases

dealt with in the previous section.

We first show that properly affine covers occur only for minimal
n

.

\lambda\in Y^{+}
and

, let

For any
\nu\in Y^{+}
if we identify the Coxeter complex of

with the positive Tits cone

is the closest vectorial chamber, from the fundamental chamber, containing

(3.2)

1.12

2.10

2.

, so by Equivalence (

),

(3.3)

\pi^{\lambda}w

1.

(3.4)

2.

Else
s_{\beta}w<_{v^{\mu}}w
so
s_{\beta}w<_{v^{s_{\beta}(\lambda+\sigma\beta^{\vee})}}w
and we have a chain

\pi^{\lambda}w<s_{\beta[-\sigma]}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{s_{\beta}(\lambda+\sigma\beta^{\vee})}s_{\beta}w<\pi^{s_{\beta}(\lambda+\sigma\beta^{\vee})}w<\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w.

(3.5)

Hence the only possible covers (with varying coweights) are for
n\in\{-\sigma,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+\sigma\}
.
∎

Remark 3.2

To follow up on Remark

s_{\beta[\sigma+\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle]}\pi^{\lambda}w=\pi^{\lambda}ws_{w^{-1}(\beta)[\sigma]}

tells us that, if

This is still far from a sufficient condition and many cases of potential covers can still be eliminated. We give another necessary condition for

to be a cover, this is a generalisation of the chains produced in the proof of Theorem

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w=s_{\beta[n]}\pi^{\lambda}w>\pi^{\lambda}w
with
\mu^{++}\neq\lambda^{++}
.
Suppose that

\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w>\pi^{\lambda}w

Proof.

We express the difference of

\ell^{a}_{\varepsilon}(\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w)-\ell^{a}_{\varepsilon}(\pi^{\lambda}w)=2\mathrm{ht}(\mu^{++}-\lambda^{++})+\varepsilon(\ell_{v^{\mu}}(s_{\beta}w)-\ell_{v^{\lambda}}(w)).

(3.6)

If there exists a reflection

and

\pi^{\lambda}w<\pi^{\lambda}rw<\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}rw<\pi^{\mu}s_{\beta}w.

(3.7)

Since

guaranties the existence of

3.2
Another expression for the affinized length difference

Outside of the case of vectorial covers dealt with in Theorems
2.13
and
2.18
, if we write
\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v\lambda}w
with
\lambda\in Y^{++},v,w\in W
with
v
of minimal length in
vW_{\lambda}
, by Proposition
3.1
the only covers are of the form
\mathbf{y}\in\{\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w,\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w\}
for some
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
, so the rest of this paper is dedicated to covers of this sort.

Notation 3.5
.

From now on, unless stated otherwise, we use the following notation:

1.

\lambda\in Y^{++}
is a dominant coweight

2.

\beta\in\Phi_{+}
is a positive root

3.

v\in W^{\lambda}
is the minimal representative of a
W_{\lambda}
-coset.

4.

w\in W
is any element of
W
.

5.

\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
and
\mathbf{y}\in\{\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w,\;\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w\}
are elements of
W^{+}
.

In particular,
\lambda
is a dominant coweight. This choice is made in order to avoid the heavier notation
\lambda^{++}
. Recall from Notation
1.2
that
W^{\lambda}
is the set of minimal coset representatives of
W/W_{\lambda}
, where
W_{\lambda}
is the standard parabolic subgroup
\{w\in W\mid w(\lambda)=\lambda\}
.

In this subsection, we give another expression for the length difference
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})
.

The two following lemmas give information on the vectorial chamber of
v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
be a dominant coweight and let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}\in Y^{+}
. Let
u\in W
be such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
belongs to the vectorial chamber
C^{v}_{u}
, that is to say
u^{-1}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})\in Y^{++}
. Then

\ell(s_{\beta}u)=\ell(s_{\beta})+\ell(u).

(3.10)

Proof.

Let
s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{n}}
be a reduced expression of
u
, so that
\ell(u)=n
and

\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})=\{\tau_{1},s_{\tau_{1}}(\tau_{2}),\dots,s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{n-1}}(\tau_{n})\}.

We show that
s_{\tau_{k+1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{1}}s_{\beta}>s_{\tau_{k}}\ldots s_{\tau_{1}}s_{\beta}
for all
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})
we have
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\alpha\rangle\leq 0
(because
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}\in C^{v}_{u}
). Since
\lambda
is dominant this implies
\langle\beta^{\vee},\alpha\rangle\leq 0
.

Let
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
. Then
\langle\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{k}}(\tau_{k+1})\rangle\leq 0
, so:
s_{\beta}(s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{k}}(\tau_{k+1}))=s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{k}}(\tau_{k+1})-\langle\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau_{1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{k}}(\tau_{k+1})\rangle\beta
is a positive root as a sum of positive roots. Thus
s_{\tau_{k+1}}\ldots s_{\tau_{1}}s_{\beta}>s_{\tau_{k}}\ldots s_{\tau_{1}}s_{\beta}
for any
k\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
and therefore
\ell(s_{\beta}u)=\ell(u^{-1}s_{\beta})=n+\ell(s_{\beta})=\ell(s_{\beta})+\ell(u)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
be a dominant coweight and let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}\in Y^{+}
. Let
v\in W^{\lambda},\,w\in W
and let
u
denote the element
v^{\lambda+\beta^{\vee}}
.

Then, if
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
(resp.
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
) covers
\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
,

\ell(vu)=\ell(v)+\ell(u)\;\text{ (resp. }\ell(vs_{\beta}u)=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta}u)\;\text{and }\ell(vs_{\beta})=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta})\text{)}.

(3.11)

Proof.

To simplify the notation, write
W_{J}
for
W_{(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++}}
. Note that, with the notation of Definition
1.19
,
vu=\mathrm{proj}_{vuW_{J}}(v)
since
u
is the element of minimal length in
uW_{J}
.

Suppose by contradiction that
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
covers
\mathbf{x}
with
\ell(vu)<\ell(v)+\ell(u)
. Then
d^{\mathbb{N}}(1,vu)=\ell(vu)<d^{\mathbb{N}}(1,v)+d^{\mathbb{N}}(v,vu)=\ell(v)+\ell(u)
, so
v
is not on a minimal gallery from
1
to
vu
. Therefore by Lemma
1.18
, there is a reflection
r\in W
such that
d(1,rvu)>d(1,vu)
and
d(1,rv)<d(1,v)
, that is to say
rv<v
and
rvu>vu
.

By minimality of
u
,
r
is not in
vuW_{J}(vu)^{-1}
: otherwise
rvu\in vuW_{J}
verifies
d(v,rvu)=d(rv,vu)<d(v,vu)
, because foldings reduce the vectorial distance and
v,vu
are on different sides of the wall
M_{r}
associated to
r
.

Since
vu
is the projection of
v
on
vuW_{J}
which is convex (see
[
Ron89
, Lemma 2.10]
), and since the wall
M_{r}
separates
v
and
vu
, any element of
vuW_{J}
is on the same side of the wall
M_{r}
as
vu
, so
rvu\widetilde{u}>vu\widetilde{u}
for any
\widetilde{u}\in W_{J}
. In particular, let
\widetilde{u}\in W_{J}
be such that
rvu\widetilde{u}
is the minimal coset representative of
rvuW_{J}
. Then by Proposition
2.3
, since
rvu\widetilde{u}>vu\widetilde{u}
, we have

\pi^{rv(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}rs_{v(\beta)}w=\pi^{rvu\widetilde{u}((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})}rs_{v(\beta)}w<\pi^{vu\widetilde{u}((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})}s_{v(\beta)}w=\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w.

(3.12)

Therefore by Proposition
2.3
for the left and right hand side inequalities and Formula (
3.8
) applied with
(\mu_{0},\alpha_{0},v_{0},w_{0},m)=(\lambda,\beta,rv,rw,1)
for the middle one, we have a chain

\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<\pi^{rv(\lambda)}rw<\pi^{rv(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{rv(\beta)}rw=\pi^{rv(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}rs_{v(\beta)}w<\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w.

(3.13)

Therefore if
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
covers
\mathbf{x}
then
\ell(vu)=\ell(v)+\ell(u)
.

Now assume by contradiction that
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
covers
\pi^{\lambda}w
with
\ell(vs_{\beta}u)<\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta}u)
. Then, similarly there is a reflection
r\in W
such that
rv<v
and
rvs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u}>vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u}
. By Proposition
2.3
for the left and right hand side inequalities and Formula (
3.9
) applied with
(\mu_{0},\alpha_{0},v_{0},w_{0},m)=(\lambda,\beta,rv,rw,1)
for the middle one, we have a chain

\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<\pi^{rv(\lambda)}rw<\pi^{rvs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{rv(\beta)}rw=\pi^{rvs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}rs_{v(\beta)}w<\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w.

(3.14)

We deduce that, if
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
covers
\mathbf{x}
then
\ell(vs_{\beta}u)=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta}u)
. By Lemma
3.6
this is
\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta})+\ell(u)
, by the triangle inequality we deduce that
\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta})\geq\ell(vs_{\beta})\geq\ell(vs_{\beta}u)-\ell(u)=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta})
and we obtain the second equality in this case.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++},v\in W^{\lambda},w\in W
. Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}\in Y^{+}
and let
u
denote
v^{\lambda+\beta^{\vee}}\in W^{(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++}}
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{y}\in\{\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w,\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w\}
covers
\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
. Then

\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})=\Bigg(2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\ell(s_{\beta})\Bigg)-2\Bigg(\ell(u)+\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\Bigg).

(3.15)

Proof.

Let
W_{J}
denote the standard parabolic subgroup
W_{(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++}}
. Recall that
u=v^{\lambda+\beta^{\vee}}
is the minimal element of
W
such that
u((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})=\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
, so it is the minimal representative of the coset
uW_{J}
. By Proposition
2.10
we have

\displaystyle\ell^{a}(\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell^{a}(\pi^{v(\lambda)}w)

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{ht}((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})-2\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)+\ell_{v^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\ell^{a}(\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell^{a}(\pi^{v(\lambda)}w)

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{ht}((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})-2\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)+\ell_{v^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w).

(3.17)

We unwrap these formulas with the help of previous results.

1.

In the case
\mathbf{y}=\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
, let
\widetilde{u}\in W_{J}
be such that
vu\widetilde{u}=(vu)^{J}=v^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}
. The term
\ell_{v^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w)
rewrites as
\ell((u\widetilde{u})^{-1}s_{\beta}v^{-1}w)-\ell(vu\widetilde{u})-\ell(v^{-1}w)+\ell(v)
.
Since
\mathbf{y}>\mathbf{x}
is a covering, by Proposition
3.3
,
vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u}=s_{v(\beta)}(vu)^{J}
is on a minimal gallery from
v
to
w
, so
\ell(v^{-1}w)=\ell((vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})^{-1}w)+\ell(s_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})
. Moreover by Lemma
3.6
,
\ell(s_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})=\ell(s_{\beta})+\ell(u\widetilde{u})
and, by Lemma
3.7
,
\ell(vu)=\ell(v)+\ell(u)
. Finally, by Equation (
1.3
), since
u=u^{J}=v^{\lambda+\beta^{\vee}}
and
vu\widetilde{u}=(vu)^{J}=v^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}
, we have
\ell(u\widetilde{u})=\ell(u)+\ell(\widetilde{u})
and
\ell(vu)=\ell(vu\widetilde{u})+\ell(\widetilde{u})
. Thus

\displaystyle\ell_{v^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w)

\displaystyle=\ell((u\widetilde{u})^{-1}s_{\beta}v^{-1}w)-\ell(v^{-1}w)-\ell(vu\widetilde{u})+\ell(v)

\displaystyle=-\ell(s_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})-\ell(vu)+\ell(\widetilde{u})+\ell(v)

\displaystyle=-\ell(s_{\beta})-\ell(u\widetilde{u})-\ell(u)+\ell(\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle=-\ell(s_{\beta})-2\ell(u).

(3.18)

2.

In the second case, let
\widetilde{u}\in W_{J}
be such that
vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u}=(vs_{\beta}u)^{J}=v^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}
, then
\ell_{v^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w)
rewrites as
\ell((u\widetilde{u})^{-1}v^{-1}w)-\ell(vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})-\ell(v^{-1}w)+\ell(v)
. By Proposition
3.3
,
\ell((u\widetilde{u})^{-1}v^{-1}w)=\ell(v^{-1}w)-\ell(u\widetilde{u})
. By Equation (
1.3
),
\ell(u\widetilde{u})=\ell(u)+\ell(\widetilde{u})
and
\ell(vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})=\ell(vs_{\beta}u)-\ell(\widetilde{u})
. By Lemma
3.7
and
3.6
,
\ell(vs_{\beta}u)=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta}u)=\ell(v)+\ell(s_{\beta})+\ell(u)
.

Thus in this case:

\displaystyle\ell_{v^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}}(s_{v(\beta)}w)-\ell_{v}(w)

\displaystyle=\ell((u\widetilde{u})^{-1}v^{-1}w)-\ell(vs_{\beta}u\widetilde{u})-\ell(v^{-1}w)+\ell(v)

\displaystyle=\ell(v^{-1}w)-\ell(u\widetilde{u})-(\ell(vs_{\beta}u)-\ell(\widetilde{u}))-\ell(v^{-1}w)+\ell(v)

\displaystyle=-\ell(s_{\beta})-2\ell(u).

(3.19)

3.

By Lemma (
1.11
) we have

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{ht}((\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++})

\displaystyle=2(\mathrm{ht}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})-\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle)

\displaystyle=2(\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)+\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle).

(3.20)

By reinjecting Expressions (
3.18
), (
3.20
) in Formula (
3.16
), and Expressions (
3.19
), (
3.20
) in Formula (
3.17
) we obtain either way:

\displaystyle\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)+2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-2\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle-2\mathrm{ht}(\lambda)-\ell(s_{\beta})-2\ell(u)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\ell(s_{\beta})\Bigg)-2\Bigg(\ell(u)+\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\Bigg).

∎

Using Lemma
1.10
, it is easy to see that
2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\ell(s_{\beta})
is always positive and that, on the contrary,
\ell(u)+\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle
is always non-positive. Therefore, the length difference is equal to
1
if and only if in the right-hand side of Equation (
3.15
), the first term is equal to
1
and the second term cancels out. This motivates the following definitions.

Definition 3.9
.

A coweight
\mu\in Y^{+}
is
almost dominant
if and only if

\forall\tau\in\Phi_{+},\;\langle\mu,\tau\rangle\geq-1.

(3.21)

A root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
is
a quantum root
if and only if

\ell(s_{\beta})=2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-1.

(3.22)

The notion of quantum roots comes from the definition of quantum Bruhat graphs, (see
[
LNS+12
, §4.1]
). With Notation
3.5
, in Subsection
3.3
we prove that, if
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
then
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is almost dominant and we prove in Subsection
3.4
that
\beta
needs to be a quantum root.

Remark 3.10
.

Note that if
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is dominant, then the second term in the right-hand side of Expression (
3.15
) immediately cancels out, since in this case
u=1_{W}
. In the reductive case,
\Phi
is finite and therefore if
\lambda
is far enough in the fundamental chamber (meaning that
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle
is large for all
i\in I
, we say that
\lambda
is superregular), then
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is always dominant. Accordingly, covers of
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
for
\lambda
superregular are easier to classify (see
[
LS10
, Proposition 4.4]
,
[
Mil21
, Proposition 4.4]
).

3.3
Almost-dominance in properly affine covers

In this section, we prove that the second term of the right-hand side of Expression (
3.15
) need to be zero when
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
(with Notation
3.5
), through the following proposition:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
and
w\in W
. Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root and suppose that
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
or
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
covers
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
. Then
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is almost dominant, that is to say:

\forall\tau\in\Phi_{+},\;\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\geq-1.

(3.23)

It is deduced from the following two technical lemmas, we give their proofs after the proof of Proposition
3.11
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++},\,v\in W^{\lambda},\,w\in W,\,\beta\in\Phi_{+}
. Suppose that there exists a pair
(\tau,n)\in\Phi_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that:

(i)

n>0

(ii)

\langle\lambda+n\tau^{\vee},\beta\rangle\geq-1

(iii)

n<-\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle
.

Then,
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
and
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
do not cover
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be such that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
lies in
Y^{+}
. Let
\tau\in\Phi_{+}
be such that
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\leq-2
and suppose that
\langle\tau^{\vee},\beta\rangle\leq-2
. Then
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau}(\beta)\rangle\geq-1

Proof of Lemma
3.13
.

The proof relies on the assumption that
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
lies in the Tits cone, which is equivalent to saying that there is only a finite number of positive roots
\alpha
such that
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\alpha\rangle<0
.

We will show that
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},(s_{\tau}s_{\beta})^{n}(\tau)\rangle\geq 0
for
n
large enough implies
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau}(\beta)\rangle\geq-1
, which implies the lemma.
To shorten the computation, let us write
a=-\langle\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle
and
a^{\vee}=-\langle\tau^{\vee},\beta\rangle
. So the assumptions
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\leq-2
and
\langle\tau^{\vee},\beta\rangle\leq-2
imply that
a\geq 2+\langle\lambda,\tau\rangle
and
a^{\vee}\geq 2
.
In the basis
(\beta,\tau)
of
\mathbb{R}\beta\oplus\mathbb{R}\tau
, the matrix of
s_{\tau}s_{\beta}
is
M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&a\\
-a^{\vee}&aa^{\vee}-1\end{pmatrix}
. We have
\chi_{M}=X^{2}+(2-aa^{\vee})X+1
thus, since
aa^{\vee}\geq 4
,
M^{2}=(aa^{\vee}-2)M-I_{2}
. Write
M^{n}=\mu_{n}M+\nu_{n}I_{2}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then an easy computation shows that
\nu_{n}=-\mu_{n-1}
and
\mu_{n+1}=(aa^{\vee}-2)\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1}
. In particular since
aa^{\vee}-2\geq 2
and
\mu_{0}=0<\mu_{1}
, by iteration
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing.

Let
x=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\geq 0
and
y=\langle\lambda,\tau\rangle\in\llbracket 0,a-2\rrbracket
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},(s_{\tau}s_{\beta})^{n}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},a\mu_{n}\beta+((aa^{\vee}-1)\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1})\tau\rangle

(3.38)

\displaystyle=(x+2)\mu_{n}a+((aa^{\vee}-1)\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1})(y-a).

Since
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
lies in the Tits cone,
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},(s_{\tau}s_{\beta})^{n}(\tau)\rangle
is non-negative for
n
large enough. Moreover,
\mu_{n-1}<\mu_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a-y>0
. Therefore we deduce from Equation (
3.38
) that, for
n
large enough,

(x+2)\mu_{n}a\geq(a-y)((aa^{\vee}-1)\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1})>(a-y)\mu_{n}(aa^{\vee}-2).

Hence

(x+2)>(a-y)(a^{\vee}-\frac{2}{a})=aa^{\vee}-a^{\vee}y-2+2\frac{y}{a}.

Therefore
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau}(\beta)\rangle=x+2+a^{\vee}y-aa^{\vee}>-2+2\frac{y}{a}
and, since it is an integer, we deduce
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\tau}(\beta)\rangle\geq-1\geq 1-a^{\vee}
, which proves the result.
∎

.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++},v\in W^{\lambda},w\in W
. Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}

\mathbf{y}\in\{\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w,\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w\}

Then

\displaystyle\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\ell(s_{\beta}).

(3.39)

Let

. Then for any

2.1
,

3.11
that
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle=-1
for any such

\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle=-|\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})|=-\ell(u).

(3.40)

We then directly obtain Expression (
3.39
) from Expressions (
3.15
) and (
3.40
).
∎

3.4
Properly affine covers and quantum roots

We now prove in Proposition

, if
\beta
is not a quantum root, then
\mathbf{y}
do not cover
\mathbf{x}
. This is enough, together with Corollary

, to conclude that
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})=1
.
There is a subtlety if the root
\beta
lies in a subsystem of

3.16

Lemma 3.15
.

A root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
is a quantum root if and only if
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=1
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
.

Proof.

Recall that a quantum root is a root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
such that
2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})=\ell(s_{\beta})+1
. By Corollary
1.10
, this is equivalent to

\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})}\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=\ell(s_{\beta})+1.

(3.41)

For any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
,
\gamma
is a positive root and
s_{\beta}(\gamma)=\gamma-\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\beta
is a negative root, therefore
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\geq 1
. Moreover,
\langle\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle=2
and
|\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})|=\ell(s_{\beta})
. Therefore Equation (
3.41
) is satisfied if and only if
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle
is exactly one for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
.
∎

\pi^{vs_{\gamma}s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee}+\alpha^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}s_{v(\alpha)}w=\pi^{vs_{\gamma}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})}s_{vs_{\gamma}(\alpha)}s_{v(\gamma)}w=s_{vs_{\gamma}(\alpha)[\langle\lambda+\gamma^{\vee},\alpha\rangle]}\pi^{vs_{\gamma}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}w.

(3.45)

Moreover
(s_{v(\gamma)}w)^{-1}(vs_{\gamma}(\alpha))=w^{-1}v(\alpha)
. Thus, since
\langle\lambda+\gamma^{\vee},\alpha\rangle<0
,

(a)

If
w^{-1}v(\alpha)\in\Phi_{-}
, by Formula (
1.12
),
\pi^{vs_{\gamma}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}w<\pi^{vs_{\gamma}s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee}+\alpha^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}s_{v(\alpha)}w
and the chain (
3.43
) still holds.

(b)

If
w^{-1}v(\alpha)\in\Phi_{+}
, then, since
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle-1>0
, by Formula (
1.12
),
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<s_{v(\alpha)[\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle]}\pi^{v(\lambda)}w=\pi^{v(\lambda)}s_{v(\alpha)}w
. Then by Formula (
3.9
) applied with
(\mu_{0},\alpha_{0},v_{0},w_{0},m)=(\lambda,\gamma,v,s_{v(\alpha)}w,1)
we have

\pi^{v(\lambda)}s_{v(\gamma)}s_{v(\beta)}s_{v(\gamma)}w<\pi^{vs_{\gamma}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}s_{v(\gamma)}w=\pi^{vs_{\gamma}s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee}+\alpha^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}s_{v(\alpha)}w

and we have a chain

\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<\pi^{v(\lambda)}s_{v(\alpha)}w<\pi^{vs_{\gamma}s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee}+\alpha^{\vee})}s_{v(\gamma)}s_{v(\alpha)}w<\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w.

(3.46)

∎

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
be a positive root and suppose that there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
such that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\geq 2
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle\neq 3
. Then
\gamma
can be chosen such that
\beta\notin\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
.

Proof.

Note that, by
[
Bar96
, Lemma 1.1.10]
, for any
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi
,
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle
and
\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle
have the same sign, so if
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\geq 2
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle\neq 3
, either
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee}\rangle\geq 4
, either
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=2
and
\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle=1
.
We treat separately these cases:

1.

Let us first suppose that there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
such that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=2
and
\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle=1
. Suppose that
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
, so
s_{\gamma}(\beta)=\beta-\gamma<0
, and
s_{\beta}(\gamma)=\gamma-2\beta<0
. Then we show that
\beta\notin\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\widetilde{\gamma}})
for
\widetilde{\gamma}=-s_{\beta}(\gamma)
:

\displaystyle s_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\beta)=s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}s_{\beta}(\beta)=-s_{\beta}(\beta-\gamma)=\gamma-\beta=-s_{\gamma}(\beta)>0.

Moreover
s_{\beta}(\widetilde{\gamma})=-\gamma<0
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\widetilde{\gamma}\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=2
therefore,
\gamma
can be chosen such that
\beta\notin\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
.

2.

Let us now suppose that there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
such that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\geq 2
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle\geq 4
. Write
\beta=v_{\beta}(\beta_{0})=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{n}}(\beta_{0})
where the
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{0}
are simple roots, and suppose that
n
is of minimal length amongst possible expressions of
\beta
. Therefore
s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{n}}s_{\beta_{0}}s_{\alpha_{n}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
is a reduced expression of
s_{\beta}
and

\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})=\{s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{p-1}}(\alpha_{p})\mid p\leq{n}\}\sqcup\{\beta\}\sqcup\{s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{n}}s_{\beta_{0}}s_{\alpha_{n}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{n+1-p}}(\alpha_{n-p})\mid p\leq n\}.

(3.47)

Let
k
be the smallest such that
\gamma_{k}=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-1}}(\alpha_{k})
verifies
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k}\rangle\geq 2
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k}\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee}_{k},\beta\rangle\geq 4
.

The expression
s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-1}}s_{\alpha_{k}}s_{\alpha_{k-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
is an expression of
s_{\gamma_{k}}
, thus

\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma_{k}})\subset\{s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{p-1}}(\alpha_{p})\mid p\leq{k-1}\}\sqcup\{\gamma_{k}\}\sqcup\{s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k}}s_{\alpha_{k-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k+1-p}}(\alpha_{k-p})\mid p\leq k-1\}.

(3.48)

Suppose by contradiction that
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma_{k}})
. Since
v_{\beta}
is of minimal length,
\beta
is not in the first set, hence there is
p\in\llbracket 1,k-1\rrbracket
such that
\beta=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k}}s_{\alpha_{k-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k+1-p}}(\alpha_{k-p})
.

We show that
\gamma_{k-p}=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-p-1}}(\alpha_{k-p})\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
verifies
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle\geq 2
, which contradicts the minimality of
k
. Note that
\beta=-s_{\gamma_{k}}(\gamma_{k-p})

We compute:

\displaystyle\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-s_{\gamma_{k}}(\gamma_{k-p}^{\vee}),\gamma_{k-p}\rangle

\displaystyle=-(2-\langle\gamma_{k-p}^{\vee},\gamma_{k}\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee}_{k},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k}\rangle\langle\gamma_{k}^{\vee},\beta\rangle-2.

So since
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k}\rangle\langle\gamma_{k}^{\vee},\beta\rangle\geq 4
, we get
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle\geq 2
, and with a similar computation, we find that
\langle\gamma_{k-p}^{\vee},\beta\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle\geq 2
as well, so
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma_{k-p}\rangle\langle\gamma_{k-p}^{\vee},\beta\rangle\geq 4
. This contradicts the minimality of
k
and thus
\beta\notin\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma_{k}})
.

∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
and
w\in W
. Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
and let
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
be such that
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=3,\;\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle=1
.

Then
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
and
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
do not cover
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
.

Proof.

We show that, with the assumptions of the statement,
\beta
and
\gamma
appear as positive roots of a root subsytem of
\Phi
isomorphic to
G_{2}
, and we use this system to construct chains replacing the ones in the proof of Lemma
3.16
.

First, note that
-s_{\gamma}(\beta)
lies in
\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
(so
s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}(\beta)
is positive). Indeed, we can write, as in the proof of Lemma
3.17
,
\beta=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{n}}(\beta_{0})
for a minimal
n
, and
\gamma=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-1}}(\alpha_{k})
for some
k\leq n
. Then, since
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
,
\beta
is also of the form
s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k}}s_{\alpha_{k-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-p+1}}(\alpha_{k-p})
for some
p\leq k-1
, and thus
-s_{\gamma}(\beta)=s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{k-p-1}}(\alpha_{k-p})\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
.
Therefore we have the following positive roots, and their associated coroots (the notation will become clear afterwards):

1.

\theta_{1}:=-s_{\gamma}(\beta)=\gamma-\beta\in\Phi_{+}
, with associated coroot
\theta_{1}^{\vee}=-s_{\gamma}(\beta^{\vee})=3\gamma^{\vee}-\beta^{\vee}
.

2.

\widetilde{\beta}:=-s_{\beta}(\gamma)=3\beta-\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
, with associated coroot
\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee}=-s_{\beta}(\gamma^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee}-\gamma^{\vee}
.

3.

\widetilde{\gamma}:=s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}(\beta)=2\beta-\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
, with associated coroot
\widetilde{\gamma}^{\vee}=s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}(\beta^{\vee})=2\beta^{\vee}-3\gamma^{\vee}
.

Let us also denote
\theta_{2}=s_{\theta_{1}}(\gamma)=3\beta-2\gamma
, with associated coroot
\theta_{2}^{\vee}=\beta^{\vee}-2\gamma^{\vee}
. Then one can check that
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
form the positive simple roots of a
G_{2}
root system (in the sense that
\langle\theta_{1}^{\vee},\theta_{2}\rangle=-3
and
\langle\theta_{2}^{\vee},\theta_{1}\rangle=-1
), such that
\gamma=s_{\theta_{1}}(\theta_{2})
,
\beta=s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})
,
\widetilde{\gamma}=s_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})
and
\widetilde{\beta}=s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\theta_{1}}(\theta_{2})
. However,
\theta_{2}
may not be a positive root in
\Phi
, and we thus need to distinguish these two cases.

Let us first suppose that
\theta_{2}
lies in
\Phi_{+}
. Notice that

\theta_{1}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee}+\theta_{2}^{\vee}=(3\gamma^{\vee}-\beta^{\vee})+(\beta^{\vee}-\gamma^{\vee})+(\beta^{\vee}-2\gamma^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee},

(3.49)

and

s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\widetilde{\beta}}s_{\theta_{2}}=s_{\theta_{1}}(s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\theta_{2}})s_{\theta_{2}}=s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\theta_{1}}s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\theta_{1}}=s_{\theta_{2}}s_{\widetilde{\beta}}s_{\theta_{1}}=s_{\beta}.

(3.50)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\langle\theta_{2}^{\vee},\widetilde{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\beta^{\vee}-2\gamma^{\vee},3\beta-\gamma\rangle=1>0

(3.51)

\displaystyle\langle\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee},\theta_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 2\beta^{\vee}-3\gamma^{\vee},\gamma-\beta\rangle=-1.

(3.52)

1.

Suppose first that
\langle\lambda,\theta_{1}\rangle>0
. Since
\lambda
is dominant and by Computation (
3.51
),(
3.52
),
-\langle\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee},\widetilde{\beta}\rangle<0
and
-\langle\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee},\theta_{1}\rangle\leq 0
. Using Formula (
3.8
) (resp. (
3.9
)) with
(\mu_{0},\alpha_{0},m)=(\lambda,\theta_{2},1)
for the first inequality,
(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee},\widetilde{\beta},1)
for the second and
(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee},\theta_{1},1)
for the third (recall Formulas (
3.49
), (
3.50
)), we obtain the chain (
3.53
) (resp. (
3.54
))

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda)}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{v(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})}s_{v(\widetilde{\beta})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w

(3.53)

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda)}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{2}}(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\pi^{vs_{\theta_{2}}s_{\widetilde{\beta}}(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}s_{v(\widetilde{\beta})}w

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w.

(3.54)

2.

If
\langle\lambda,\theta_{1}\rangle=0
, then
-\langle\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee},\theta_{1}\rangle=1
so the last inequality in the chains (
3.53
) and (
3.54
) do not always hold, we have the following case distinction, which we already encountered in Lemma
3.12
and Lemma
3.16
:

(a)

If
v(\theta_{1})\in\Phi_{+}
, the chain (
3.53
) still holds, else
vs_{\theta_{1}}<v
,

and we instead have the chain

\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{1}}(\lambda)}s_{v(\theta_{1})}w<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{1}}(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{1})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{1}}(\lambda+\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{1})}s_{v(\widetilde{\beta})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

(3.55)

where the last term is actually equal to
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
.

(b)

If

where the last term is actually equal to

since

We now turn to the case of
\theta_{2}\in\Phi_{-}
. Notice that
\beta^{\vee}=-\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\gamma}^{\vee}+\gamma^{\vee}
and

. Moreover,
\langle-\theta_{2}^{\vee},\widetilde{\gamma}\rangle=\langle 2\gamma^{\vee}-\beta^{\vee},2\beta-\gamma\rangle=-1

. Therefore, since
\lambda
is dominant and
-\theta_{2}
is a positive root:

1.

If
\langle\lambda,\widetilde{\gamma}\rangle>0
, then using Formula (
3.8
) (resp. (

)) with

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda)}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{v(\lambda-\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{v(\lambda-\theta_{2}^{\vee}+\widetilde{\gamma}^{\vee})}s_{v(\widetilde{\gamma})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w

\displaystyle\pi^{v(\lambda)}w

\displaystyle<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{2}}(\lambda-\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}w

2.

Suppose now that

and we instead have the chain

\pi^{v(\lambda)}w<\pi^{v(\lambda)}s_{v(\widetilde{\gamma})}w<\pi^{vs_{\theta_{2}}(\lambda-\theta_{2}^{\vee})}s_{v(\theta_{2})}s_{v(\widetilde{\gamma})}w<\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
and
w\in W
. Let
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
and suppose that
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
or
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
cover
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
. Then
\beta
is a quantum root.

Proof.

We prove the converse, suppose that
\beta
is not a quantum root. By Lemma
3.15
, there is
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
such that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\geq 2
. If
\beta\notin\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
we apply Lemma
3.16
. We can also apply it in case
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle\neq 3
by Lemma
3.17
. Finally if
\beta\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\gamma})
and
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vee},\beta\rangle=3
we apply Lemma
3.18
. Therefore if
\beta
is not a quantum root then
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
and
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
do not cover
\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
.
∎

3.5
Conclusion

We now have everything to prove Theorem
A
:

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}\in W^{a}_{+}
are such that
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
. Then

\mathbf{x}\lhd\mathbf{y}\iff\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})=\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})+1.

(3.61)

Proof.

If
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}
with
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})=\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})+1
, then by strict compatibility of
\ell^{a}
(Theorem
1.6
),
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
. Conversely suppose that
\mathbf{y}
covers
\mathbf{x}
. If
\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{x}
have same dominance class then Theorem
2.18
implies that
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})=\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})+1
. Else, if
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{y})\notin W\cdot\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
, by Proposition
3.1
,
\mathbf{y}
is of the form
\pi^{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
or
\pi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
, for
\mathbf{x}=\pi^{v(\lambda)}w
with
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
,
w\in W
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
. Then, by Corollary
3.14
, we have
\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})=2\mathrm{ht}(\beta^{\vee})-\ell(s_{\beta})
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.19
,
\beta
is a quantum root and therefore in this case as well:

\ell^{a}(\mathbf{y})-\ell^{a}(\mathbf{x})=1.

∎

For Kac-Moody root systems, the existence of superregular coweights is not clear a priori. However in an upcoming joint work with Hébert we prove that any Kac-Moody root system admits a finite number of quantum roots, which ensures the existence of superregular coweights. We also use this finiteness to deduce that any element of
W^{+}_{a}
admits a finite number of covers; in particular intervals in
W^{+}_{a}
are finite.

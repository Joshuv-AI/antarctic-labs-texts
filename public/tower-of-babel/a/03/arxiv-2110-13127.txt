On Tate–Shafarevich groups of one-dimensional families of commutative group schemes over number fields
David Harari and Tamás Szamuely

Abstract




Abstract.
Given a smooth geometrically connected curve
\displaystyle C
over a field
\displaystyle k
and a smooth commutative group scheme
\displaystyle G
of finite type over the function field
\displaystyle K
of
\displaystyle C
we study the Tate–Shafarevich groups
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
given by elements of
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)
locally trivial at completions of
\displaystyle K
associated with closed points of
\displaystyle C
. When
\displaystyle G
comes from a
\displaystyle k
-group scheme and
\displaystyle k
is a number field (or
\displaystyle k
is a finitely generated field and
\displaystyle C
has a
\displaystyle k
-point) we prove finiteness of
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
generalizing a result of Saïdi and Tamagawa for abelian varieties. We also give examples of nontrivial
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
in the case when
\displaystyle G
is a torus and prove other related statements.

1.
Introduction

Consider a smooth, proper, geometrically connected curve
\displaystyle X
over a field
\displaystyle k
and denote by
\displaystyle K
its function field. Given a nonempty open subscheme
\displaystyle C\subset X
, denote by
\displaystyle C^{(1)}
the set of closed points of
\displaystyle C
and by
\displaystyle K_{c}
the completion of
\displaystyle K
with respect to the discrete valuation associated with a point
\displaystyle c\in C^{(1)}
.

Now assume
\displaystyle G
is a commutative group scheme over
\displaystyle K
. We define the Tate–Shafarevich groups of
\displaystyle G
relative to
\displaystyle C
by

\cyrille X^{i}_{C}(G):={\rm{Ker}}(H^{i}(K,G)\to\prod_{c\in C^{(1)}}H^{i}(K_{c},G))

for each
\displaystyle i\geq 0
.

The case where
\displaystyle i=1
and
\displaystyle G=A
is an abelian variety has been thoroughly studied in the beautiful paper
[
10
]
by Saïdi and Tamagawa. One of their main unconditional results is the finiteness of
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(A)
for an isotrivial abelian variety
\displaystyle A
and a base field
\displaystyle k
finitely generated over
\displaystyle{\bf Q}
.

Our main result extends (a slightly weaker form of) their theorem to arbitrary commutative group schemes of finite type.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle G_{k}
be a commutative group scheme of finite type over
\displaystyle k
, and set
\displaystyle G:=G_{k}\times_{k}K
. The group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
is finite in each of the following cases:

(a)

\displaystyle k
is a number field;

(b)

\displaystyle k
is finitely generated over
\displaystyle{\bf Q}
and
\displaystyle C
has a rational point.

The proof will be given in the next section. Of course, our main new contribution is the case of groups of multiplicative type. In this respect we ask:

Question 1.2
.

For
\displaystyle k
a finitely generated extension of
\displaystyle{\bf Q}
and
\displaystyle G
a group of multiplicative type over
\displaystyle K
is the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
always finite?

Note that in (
[
10
]
, Proposition A) Saïdi and Tamagawa prove that for
\displaystyle G=A
an abelian variety over
\displaystyle K
the
\displaystyle N
-torsion subgroup of
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(A)
is finite for every
\displaystyle N>0
. The analogous statement for groups of multiplicative type would of course be equivalent to a positive answer to the question above. For the moment we know that the answer is positive for
\displaystyle G
constant (by the theorem above), finite (Proposition
4.4
below) or a stably
\displaystyle K
-rational torus (Corollary
4.3
, pointed out by J-L. Colliot-Thélène).

An even more basic question is whether this group is always 0. In Section 3 we show:

Proposition 1.3
.

There exist examples of smooth proper curves
\displaystyle X
defined over number fields and tori
\displaystyle T
defined over their function fields with
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{X}(T)\neq 0
.

We construct both constant and non-constant examples of such tori in Example
3.7
below.

The last section contains some complements to the results above. For instance, we show that
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
is always 0 for a group scheme associated with a finitely generated Galois module over
\displaystyle K
and that
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}(F)
is finite for a finite étale
\displaystyle K
-group scheme defined over
\displaystyle k
. We also explore a possible strengthening of Question
1.2
. As the reader will see, we deduce Theorem
1.1
in case (b) from the following stronger
statement:
if
\displaystyle k
and
\displaystyle G
are as in the theorem and
\displaystyle C\subset X
is an open subcurve having a rational point
\displaystyle c\in C(k)
, then already for the single point
\displaystyle c
the kernel of the restriction map
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,G\times_{k}C)\to H^{1}(K_{c},G)
has finite image in
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)
. However, in Example
4.6
below we shall give an example of a non-constant torus over
\displaystyle K
where such a kernel is infinite. This shows the limitations of the methods of the present paper and hints at the potential difficulty of Question
1.2
in the non-constant case.

We would like to mention that in the recent preprint
[
9
]
A. and I. Rapinchuk prove finiteness of another kind of Tate–Shafarevich group associated with a torus defined over a finitely generated field. In their context local triviality is imposed with respect to all discrete valuations coming from codimension 1 points of a normal model of the function field that is of finite type over
\displaystyle{\bf Z}
. Ultimately their result relies on fundamental finiteness theorems in étale cohomology. Though in the present paper we only consider function fields of dimension 1, we impose a weaker local triviality condition and such a reduction does not seem to work.

We are grateful to Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène and Olivier Wittenberg for their pertinent remarks and to the referee for their careful reading of the text.
We dedicate this note to the memory of our dear friends Joss Beaumont and Jo Cavalier with whom we had the privilege of discussing similar matters in the past.

2.
Constant commutative group schemes

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
of which we keep the notation and assumptions:
\displaystyle k
is a finitely generated field over
\displaystyle{\bf Q}
,
\displaystyle C
a smooth geometrically connected
\displaystyle k
-curve with function field
\displaystyle K
, and
\displaystyle G_{k}
a commutative group scheme of finite type over
\displaystyle k
, with base change
\displaystyle G:=G_{k}\times_{k}K
to
\displaystyle K
.

We start with a couple of lemmas, of which the first two will only serve in case (a) of the theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
\displaystyle k
is a number field. There exists a finite set
\displaystyle S
of places of
\displaystyle k
containing all archimedean places such that the kernel of the map

(1)

H^{1}(k,G_{k})\to\prod_{c\in C^{(1)}}H^{1}(k(c),G_{k})

is contained in the kernel
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{S}(G_{k})
of the map

(2)

H^{1}(k,G_{k})\to\prod_{v\not\in S}H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k}).

Proof.

We find a finite set
\displaystyle S
of places of
\displaystyle k

containing all archimedean places such that the curve
\displaystyle C

has
\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
-points for every
\displaystyle v\not\in S
, where
\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
is the
henselisation of
\displaystyle k
at
\displaystyle v
. Such an
\displaystyle S
exists by the Lang-Weil estimates
and Hensel’s Lemma. Since

\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
is algebraic over
\displaystyle k
, for every
\displaystyle v\not\in S
the curve
\displaystyle C
has a point
over a finite extension
\displaystyle k^{\prime}
of
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle k^{\prime}\subset k_{v}^{h}\subset k_{v}
.
In particular
\displaystyle C
has a closed point
\displaystyle c
such that the residue field

\displaystyle k(c)
embeds into
\displaystyle k^{\prime}
, which implies that the restriction of each element of the kernel of (
1
) to

\displaystyle H^{1}(k^{\prime},G_{k})
is zero.

Concerning the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{S}(G_{k})
we have the following finiteness statement.

Lemma 2.2
.

Still assuming
\displaystyle k
is a number field, suppose
\displaystyle G_{k}
is an extension of an abelian variety
\displaystyle A_{k}
by a group of multiplicative type
\displaystyle N_{k}
.
If
\displaystyle S
is a finite set of places of
\displaystyle k
containing all archimedean places, the group

\cyrille X^{S}(G_{k})={\rm{Ker}}[H^{1}(k,G_{k})\to\prod_{v\not\in S}H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k})]

has finite
\displaystyle m
-torsion for all
\displaystyle m>0
.

Proof.

First consider a place
\displaystyle v\in S
. In the exact sequence

H^{1}(k_{v},N_{k})\to H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k})\to H^{1}(k_{v},A_{k})

the group
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{v},N_{k})
is finite (see
[
7
]
, I.2.4 and I.2.13) and
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{v},A_{k})
has finite
\displaystyle m
-torsion (see
[
7
]
, I.3.4 and I.3.7). Therefore
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k})
has finite
\displaystyle m
-torsion. Denoting by
\displaystyle\Omega
the set of all places of
\displaystyle k
and setting

(3)

\cyrille X(G_{k})={\rm{Ker}}[H^{1}(k,G_{k})\to\prod_{v\in\Omega}H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k})],

the exact sequence of
\displaystyle m
-torsion subgroups

0\to{}_{m}\cyrille X(G_{k})\to{}_{m}\cyrille X^{S}(G_{k})\to\prod_{v\in S}{}_{m}H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k})

shows that it is enough to prove finiteness of
\displaystyle{}_{m}\cyrille X(G_{k})
.

Fix an open subscheme
\displaystyle U\subset{\rm{Spec\,}}{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
such that
\displaystyle m
is invertible on
\displaystyle U
and moreover
\displaystyle G_{k}
extends to a smooth commutative group scheme
\displaystyle G_{U}
over
\displaystyle U
which is again an extension of an abelian scheme by a group of multiplicative type over
\displaystyle U
. Consider the group

D^{1}(U,G_{U}):={\rm Im}(H^{1}_{c}(U,G_{U})\to H^{1}(U,G_{U}))={\rm{Ker}}(H^{1}(U,G_{U})\to\bigoplus_{v\notin U}H^{1}(k_{v},G_{k}))

where
\displaystyle H^{1}_{c}(U,G_{U})
denotes the compact support cohomology group defined, for instance, in (
[
7
]
, §II.2).
We first contend that the natural map
\displaystyle{}_{m}D^{1}(U,G_{U})\to{}_{m}H^{1}(k,G_{k})
is injective. To see this it will be enough to verify injectivity of the analogous maps
\displaystyle D^{1}(U,G_{U})\{\ell\}\to H^{1}(k,G_{k})\{\ell\}
on
\displaystyle\ell
-primary torsion subgroups for all prime divisors
\displaystyle\ell
of
\displaystyle m
. Observe that the connected component
\displaystyle G^{\circ}_{U}
of the identity in
\displaystyle G_{U}
is a semiabelian scheme and the component group
\displaystyle F_{U}=G_{U}/G^{\circ}_{U}
is a finite étale group scheme. Now consider the commutative diagram with exact rows

(4)

\begin{CD}H^{0}(U,F_{U})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(U,G_{U}^{\circ})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(U,G_{U})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(U,F_{U})\\
@V{}V{\cong}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
H^{0}(k,F_{k})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k,G_{k}^{\circ})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k,G_{k})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k,F_{k}).\end{CD}

Here the first vertical map is an isomorphism by properness of
\displaystyle F_{U}
and the last one is injective by properness of
\displaystyle F_{U}
because a generically trivial
\displaystyle F_{U}
-torsor is trivial by the valuative criterion of properness. The group
\displaystyle H^{1}(U,G_{U}^{\circ})
is torsion by (
[
5
]
, Lemma 3.2 (1)), and its
\displaystyle\ell
-primary torsion injects in
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,G_{k}^{\circ})
by (
[
5
]
, Proposition 4.1 (2)). Injectivity of the third vertical map on
\displaystyle\ell
-primary torsion follows by a diagram chase.

This being said, we observe that the subgroup
\displaystyle{}_{m}\cyrille X(G_{k})\subset H^{1}(k,G_{k})
is contained in
\displaystyle{}_{m}D^{1}(U,G_{U})
. Recall the argument from
[
5
]
: each
\displaystyle m
-torsion element in
\displaystyle\cyrille X(G_{k})
comes from
\displaystyle{}_{m}D^{1}(V,G_{V})
for a suitable open subscheme
\displaystyle V\subset U
, hence from
\displaystyle{}_{m}D^{1}(U,G_{U})
by covariant functoriality of the groups
\displaystyle D^{1}(U,G_{U})
for open immersions (see the proof of Lemma 4.7 in
[
5
]
).
It thus remains to establish the finiteness of
\displaystyle{}_{m}D^{1}(U,G_{U})
. In the exact upper row of diagram (
4
)
the groups
\displaystyle H^{i}(U,F_{U})
are finite for
\displaystyle i=0,1
(for the case
\displaystyle i=1
see
[
7
]
, remark after Theorem 3.1
\displaystyle b)
) and the groups
\displaystyle{}_{m}H^{1}(U,G^{\circ}_{U})
are known to be finite for all
\displaystyle m>0
by (
[
5
]
, Lemma 3.2 (2)). The required finiteness follows since
\displaystyle D^{1}(U,G_{U})
is a subgroup of
\displaystyle H^{1}(U,G_{U})
.
∎

Finally we include for the sake of reference a well-known lemma that is in fact valid over an arbitrary field
\displaystyle k
(or even for an arbitrary integral Dedekind scheme in place of
\displaystyle C
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\displaystyle C^{\prime}\subset C
an open subscheme and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}
a smooth commutative group scheme of finite type over
\displaystyle C
with generic fibre
\displaystyle G
we have an inclusion of subgroups

{\rm Ker}(H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to\bigoplus_{v\in C\setminus C^{\prime}}H^{1}(K_{v},G))\subset{\rm Im}(H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}}))

in
\displaystyle H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Part of the localization sequence in étale cohomology reads

H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to\bigoplus_{v\in C\setminus C^{\prime}}H^{2}_{v}(C,{\mathcal{G}}),

so we are reduced to establishing an inclusion

{\rm Ker}(H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to\bigoplus_{v\in C\setminus C^{\prime}}H^{1}(K_{v},G))\subset{\rm Ker}(H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to\bigoplus_{v\in C\setminus C^{\prime}}H^{2}_{v}(C,{\mathcal{G}})).

We have excision isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{2}_{v}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\cong H^{2}_{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h},{\mathcal{G}})
,
where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h}
is the henselization of the local ring of
\displaystyle C
at
\displaystyle v
. A second localization sequence for
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h}
reads

H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h},{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K_{v}^{h},G)\to H^{2}_{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h},{\mathcal{G}})

where
\displaystyle K_{v}^{h}
is the fraction field of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h}
. The composition of restriction maps
\displaystyle H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K,G)\to H^{1}(K_{v}^{h},G)
makes the diagram

\begin{CD}H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})@>{}>{}>H^{2}_{v}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\cong}V\\
H^{1}(K_{v}^{h},G)@>{}>{}>H^{2}_{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{h},{\mathcal{G}})\end{CD}

commute by functoriality of the localization sequence. Thus our claim follows from the fact that the natural map
\displaystyle H^{1}(K_{v}^{h},G)\to H^{1}(K_{v},G)
is an isomorphism (by the same argument as e.g. in the proof of
[
5
]
, Lemma 2.7).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Chevalley’s theorem our
\displaystyle G_{k}
is an extension of an abelian variety
\displaystyle A_{k}
by an affine group scheme
\displaystyle N_{k}
.
The unipotent part
\displaystyle U_{k}
of
\displaystyle N_{k}
is a normal subgroup scheme of
\displaystyle G_{k}
isomorphic to a product of copies of
\displaystyle{\bf G}_{a}
, whence
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,U_{K})=0
. This shows that the natural map
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)\to H^{1}(K,G/U_{K})
is injective, so in proving the theorem we may replace
\displaystyle G_{k}
by
\displaystyle G_{k}/U_{k}
, i.e. assume
\displaystyle N_{k}
is of multiplicative type. We set
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}:=G_{k}\times_{k}C
,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}:=A_{k}\times_{k}C
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}:=N_{k}\times_{k}C
.

We first show that the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
is contained in the image of the group

{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}}):={\rm{Ker}}(H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\to\prod_{c\in C^{(1)}}H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{G}}_{c}))

in
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)
, where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{c}
denotes the fibre of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}
over the closed point
\displaystyle c\in C
. Each element of
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(G)
lifts to a locally trivial element in
\displaystyle H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})
for some open
\displaystyle C^{\prime}\subset C
, hence to a locally trivial element in
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})
by Lemma
2.3
. The restriction maps
\displaystyle H^{1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{G}})
factor through the maps
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{G}})
, where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is the completion of the local ring of
\displaystyle c
and
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{G}})\cong H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{G}}_{c})
by smoothness of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}
. To conclude the proof of the claim we show injectivity of the maps
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{G}})
. Consider the commutative diagram with exact rows

\begin{CD}H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{A}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{N}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{G}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{A}})\\
@V{\cong}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
H^{0}(K_{c},{\mathcal{A}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{N}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{G}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{A}}).\end{CD}

Properness of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
implies that the first vertical map is an isomorphism and the last one is injective. The second vertical map is injective, for instance, because of the general result of Nisnevich
[
8
]
, whence injectivity of the third map follows by a diagram chase.

Now since the group
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)
is torsion (
[
4
]
,
Corollary 4.23),
it is sufficient to prove that the group
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})
is finitely generated. We first prove this after passing to a suitable finite étale cover of
\displaystyle C
.
Choose a finite
Galois field extension
\displaystyle k_{1}
of
\displaystyle k

such that the base change
\displaystyle N_{k_{1}}
splits as a finite direct product of copies of
\displaystyle{\bf G}_{m}
and of finite constant group schemes isomorphic to
\displaystyle{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z}
for some
\displaystyle m\in{\bf Z}
.
Set
\displaystyle D:=C\times_{k}k_{1}
, and consider the commutative diagram with exact row

\begin{CD}H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{N}})@>{\rho}>{}>H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{G}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&\prod_{d\in D^{(1)}}H^{1}(k_{1}(d),G_{k})@>{}>{}>\prod_{d\in D^{(1)}}H^{1}(k_{1}(d),A_{k}).\end{CD}

We show that the kernel
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(D,{\mathcal{G}})
of the first vertical map is finitely generated.
For this we begin by observing that the kernel
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})
of the second vertical map is finite. Indeed, the map
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})\to H^{1}(k_{1}(D),A_{k_{1}(D)})
is injective because a generically trivial
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
-torsor is trivial by the valuative criterion of properness, so we may identify
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})
with a subgroup of
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{1}(D),A_{k_{1}(D)})
. On the other hand, as in the previous paragraph, an element of
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})
with trivial image in
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{1}(d),A_{k})\cong H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{d},{\mathcal{A}})
has trivial image by the restriction map
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{1}(D),A_{k_{1}(D)})\to H^{1}(k_{1}(D)_{d},A_{k_{1}(D)})
. Thus we have an inclusion
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(D,{\mathcal{A}})\subset\cyrille X^{1}_{D}(A_{k_{1}(D)})
, and the latter group is finite by (
[
10
]
, Theorem 4.1). This being said, it suffices to show that
\displaystyle\rho(H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{N}}))\cap{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(D,{\mathcal{G}})
is finitely generated. The group
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{N}})
splits as a finite product of copies of
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\bf G}_{m})
and of groups of the form
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})
. The unique smooth
\displaystyle k_{1}
-compactification
\displaystyle Y
of
\displaystyle D
has finitely generated Picard group by the Mordell–Weil theorem, whence
\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\bf G}_{m})\cong{\rm{Pic\,}}D

\displaystyle{\rm{Pic\,}}Y

\displaystyle H^{1}(D,{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})

0\to H^{1}(k_{1},{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})\to H^{1}(D,{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})\to H^{1}(\overline{D},{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})

\displaystyle\overline{D}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle H^{1}(\overline{D},{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z})

) we reduce to proving finiteness of the intersection
\displaystyle\rho(H^{1}(k_{1},{\bf Z}/m{\bf Z}))\cap{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(D,{\mathcal{G}})
. But this is an
\displaystyle m

(5)

H^{1}(k_{1},G_{k_{1}})\to\prod_{d\in D^{(1)}}H^{1}(k_{1}(d),G_{k_{1}})

\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{1},G_{k_{1}})

In case (a) of the theorem this kernel is contained in the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{S}(G_{k_{1}})
for a suitable
\displaystyle S

. But the latter group has finite
\displaystyle m
-torsion part by Lemma

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle k_{1}

\displaystyle D

We can now prove that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})
is finitely generated. With the notation
\displaystyle\Gamma:={\rm{Gal\,}}(k_{1}|k)

\begin{CD}H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{G}}(D))@>{}>{}>H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{G}})\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&\prod_{c\in C^{(1)}}H^{1}(k(c),G_{k})@>{}>{}>\prod_{d\in D^{(1)}}H^{1}(k_{1}(d),G_{k_{1}}).\end{CD}

where the second vertical map has finitely generated kernel by the previous paragraph. It therefore suffices to verify finiteness of the intersection of
\displaystyle{\rm Im}(H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{G}}(D))\to H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}}))
with
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})
. Consider the exact sequence

(6)

H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{N}}(D))\to H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{G}}(D))\to H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{A}}(D)).

Since
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(D)
equals
\displaystyle A(k_{1}(D))
by properness of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
over
\displaystyle D
and the latter group is finitely generated by the Mordell–Weil/Lang–Néron theorem, we conclude that
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{A}}(D))
is finite (see e.g.
[
4
]
, Corollary 1.50). In order to treat
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{N}}(D))
, set
\displaystyle L:={\mathcal{N}}(D)/N_{k}(k_{1})
. It is a finitely generated abelian group because
so is
\displaystyle{\bf G}_{m}(D)/{\bf G}_{m}(k_{1})=k_{1}[D]^{*}/k_{1}^{*}

in view of the exact sequence

0\to k_{1}^{*}\to k_{1}[D]^{*}\to{\rm{Div\,}}_{Y\setminus D}\,Y

where the last group denotes divisors on
\displaystyle Y
supported in the (finite) complement of
\displaystyle D
.
Thus in the exact sequence

(7)

H^{1}(\Gamma,N_{k}(k_{1}))\to H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{N}}(D))\to H^{1}(\Gamma,L)

the group
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,L)
is finite for the same reason as above. Hence by exact sequences (
6
) and (
7
) we reduce to showing finiteness of the intersection

{\rm Im}(H^{1}(\Gamma,N_{k}(k_{1}))\to H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}}))\cap{\mathcal{K}}^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}}).

This group in turn is contained in the image of the group

K_{G}:={\rm{Ker}}(H^{1}(k,G_{k})\to\prod_{c\in C^{(1)}}H^{1}(k(c),G_{k}))

in
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})
. But the group
\displaystyle K_{G}
is trivial in case (b) and is finite in case (a) because it is of finite exponent (by a restriction-corestriction argument applied to some residue field
\displaystyle k(c)
) and has finite
\displaystyle m
-torsion by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
.
∎

3.
Non-triviality of Tate–Shafarevich groups

In this section we construct examples of tori
\displaystyle T
with non-trivial
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{X}(T)
as stated in Proposition
1.3
. In the whole section
\displaystyle k
is a number field, otherwise we keep the notation from the introduction.

We start with some statements about the cohomology of
\displaystyle{\bf G}_{m}
that we consider interesting in their own right. Denote by
\displaystyle\Omega_{k}
the set of
places of
\displaystyle k
and by
\displaystyle k_{v}
the completion of
\displaystyle k
at
\displaystyle v\in\Omega_{k}
. Define the locally trivial part of the Brauer group of the curve
\displaystyle X
by

{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X):={\rm{Ker}}({\rm{Br\,}}X\to\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}{k_{v}})).

Proposition 3.1
.

For every nonempty open subcurve
\displaystyle C\subset X
we have an isomorphism

\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})\cong{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X).

In particular the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})
is independent of
\displaystyle C
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Br\,}}X\subset{\rm{Br\,}}K
has trivial restriction to
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}{k_{v}})
for
every place
\displaystyle v
of
\displaystyle k
. If
\displaystyle c
is a closed point of
\displaystyle X
with residue
field
\displaystyle k(c)
and
\displaystyle w
is a place of
\displaystyle k(c)
extending
\displaystyle v
, the commutative diagram of restriction maps

\begin{CD}{\rm{Br\,}}X@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}{k_{v}})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
{\rm{Br\,}}k(c)@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}k(c)_{w}\end{CD}

shows that the evaluation
\displaystyle\alpha(c)\in{\rm{Br\,}}(k(c))
has trivial restriction to

\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}(k(c)_{w})
.
Therefore
\displaystyle\alpha(c)=0
by the Hasse principle for Brauer groups, which in turn shows that the image of
\displaystyle\alpha
in

\displaystyle H^{2}(K_{c},{\bf G}_{m})
is zero thanks to the injection
\displaystyle H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\bf G}_{m})\hookrightarrow H^{2}(K_{c},{\bf G}_{m})
and the isomorphism
\displaystyle H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\bf G}_{m})\cong H^{2}(k(c),{\bf G}_{m})
, where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is the valuation ring of
\displaystyle K_{c}
. This proves that
\displaystyle\alpha\in\cyrille X^{2}_{X}({\bf G}_{m})\subset\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})
.

Conversely, pick
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\in\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})
. We first show that
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
comes from

\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Br\,}}X
. Shrinking
\displaystyle C
if necessary, we may assume that
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
comes from

\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Br\,}}C
, hence the evaluation
\displaystyle\alpha(c)\in{\rm{Br\,}}(k(c))
is
trivial for every closed point
\displaystyle c\in C
. For a place
\displaystyle v
of
\displaystyle k
consider
the henselization
\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
of
\displaystyle k
at
\displaystyle v
. As
\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
is an algebraic
extension of
\displaystyle k
, the image of every
\displaystyle k
-morphism
\displaystyle{\rm{Spec\,}}k_{v}^{h}\to C

is a closed point
\displaystyle c\in C
, which implies by functoriality
that
\displaystyle\alpha(P_{v}^{h})=0
for every
\displaystyle k_{v}^{h}
-point
\displaystyle P_{v}^{h}\in C(k_{v}^{h})

(since by assumption
\displaystyle\alpha(c)=0
). By Greenberg’s approximation theorem,
this also proves that
\displaystyle\alpha(P_{v})=0
for every
\displaystyle k_{v}
-point

\displaystyle P_{v}\in C(k_{v})
. Finally, from (
[
3
]
Th. 2.1.1)
we conclude that
\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Br\,}}X
.

We now prove that the restriction
\displaystyle\alpha_{v}\in{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}k_{v})

of
\displaystyle\alpha
is zero for all places
\displaystyle v
of
\displaystyle k
. Let
\displaystyle k^{\prime}_{v}
be a finite
field extension of
\displaystyle k_{v}
and take
\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{v}\in X(k^{\prime}_{v})
. By the same
argument as above, we have
\displaystyle\alpha(P^{\prime}_{v})=0
if
\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{v}
lies above a closed point of
\displaystyle C
, hence for every
\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{v}\in X(k^{\prime}_{v})
since the evaluation map for
\displaystyle\alpha
is locally constant (
[
2
]
, Proposition 10.5.2). Therefore

\displaystyle\alpha(z_{v})=0
for all zero-cycles
\displaystyle z_{v}
on
\displaystyle X\times_{k}k_{v}
.
By Lichtenbaum duality
[
6
]
for the curve
\displaystyle X\times_{k}k_{v}
it follows
that
\displaystyle\alpha_{v}=0
. This is what we wanted to prove.

Remark 3.2
.

Olivier Wittenberg contributes the following alternative argument for the second part of the above proof which does not use the difficult result from
[
3
]
. As above, we lift
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\in\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})
to
\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Br\,}}C
and prove that
\displaystyle\alpha(z_{v})=0
for all zero-cycles
\displaystyle z_{v}
on

, Theorem 3.5 and Remark 2.10.2) it follows that
\displaystyle\alpha
maps to 0 in
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}(C\times_{k}k_{v})
and therefore
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
maps to 0 in
\displaystyle H^{2}(K\times_{k}k_{v},{\bf G}_{m})
for all
\displaystyle v
. Hence for a closed point
\displaystyle P\in X
the residue of
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
in
\displaystyle H^{1}(k(P),{\bf Q}/{\bf Z})
has trivial image in
\displaystyle H^{1}(k(P)_{w},{\bf Q}/{\bf Z})
for every place
\displaystyle w
of
\displaystyle k(P)
and is therefore trivial by the Chebotarev density theorem. This shows that
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
lifts to an element in
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X)
.

Question 3.3
.

Can one generalize the isomorphism of the proposition to
\displaystyle k
-group schemes other than
\displaystyle{\bf G}_{m}
? In particular, is there an analogous formula for
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}(F_{k})
with
\displaystyle F_{k}
a finite
\displaystyle k
-group scheme?

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle J
be the Jacobian variety of the curve
\displaystyle X
. If
\displaystyle X
has a zero-cycle of degree 1, we have isomorphisms

\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})\cong\cyrille X(J)

for every nonempty open subcurve
\displaystyle C\subset X
.

Here
\displaystyle\cyrille X(J)
denotes the classical Tate-Shafarevich group of
\displaystyle J
, as in (
3
).

Proof.

We have to construct an isomorphism
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X)\cong\cyrille X(J)
thanks to Proposition
3.1
.
Since
\displaystyle X
is a curve, the base change
\displaystyle\overline{X}=X\times_{k}{\bar{k}}
satisfies
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}\overline{X}=0
by Tsen’s theorem.
Thus from the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence and the vanishing of

\displaystyle H^{3}(k,{\bf G}_{m})
one deduces an exact sequence

0\to{\rm{Br\,}}k\to{\rm{Br\,}}X\to H^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})\to 0

where the first map is injective by the assumption that
\displaystyle X
has a zero-cycle of degree 1. More precisely, this assumption gives a splitting of the map
\displaystyle{\rm{Br\,}}k\to{\rm{Br\,}}X
, so the sequence is in fact split exact.

Repeating the argument with
\displaystyle k
replaced by its completions
\displaystyle k_{v}
we obtain a commutative diagram with split exact rows

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}k@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}X@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})@>{}>{}>0\cr&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\cr 0@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}{\rm{Br\,}}k_{v}@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}{k_{v}})@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Since the left vertical map is injective by the Hasse principle for Brauer groups, we obtain an isomorphism

{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X)\cong{\rm{Ker}}(H^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})\to\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})).

On the other hand,
the exact sequence of Galois modules

0\to J({\bar{k}})\to{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X}\to{\bf Z}\to 0

induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{1}(k,J)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}H^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})
in view of the vanishing of
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,{\bf Z})
and the assumption that
\displaystyle X
has a zero-cycle of degree 1. Similarly, we have
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{v},J)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}H^{1}(k_{v},{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})
for
\displaystyle v\in\Omega
and the statement follows.

Remark 3.5
.

By refining the arguments in the above proof one can show that even in the case where
\displaystyle X
has no zero-cycle of degree 1 the finiteness of
\displaystyle\cyrille X(J)
implies the finiteness of
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})
. See the proof of Proposition
4.1
below for some of the ideas involved.

We now construct the promised examples of tori.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\displaystyle L|K
be a finite cyclic Galois extension with Galois group
\displaystyle\Gamma
, and let
\displaystyle Y
be the normalization of
\displaystyle X
in
\displaystyle L
. Denote by
\displaystyle J_{X}
(resp.
\displaystyle J_{Y}
) the Jacobian variety of
\displaystyle X
(resp.
\displaystyle Y
) and suppose that both
\displaystyle X
and
\displaystyle Y

For the normic torus

we have an isomorphism

\cyrille X^{1}_{X}(T)\cong{\rm{Ker}}[\cyrille X(J_{X})\to\cyrille X(J_{Y})].

Proof.

By definition of
\displaystyle T
we have an exact sequence of tori

1\to T\to{\bf R}_{L/K}{\bf G}_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{L/K}}}{{\to}}{\bf G}_{m}\to 1.

In view of Hilbert’s Theorem 90, Shapiro’s lemma and the
\displaystyle 2

and likewise with

.
Therefore

The result now follows from
Theorem
3.4
.

4.
Remarks and Complements

In this section we collect a number of observations related to the results of the previous sections. We begin with the following consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle k
be a number field.
If
\displaystyle F_{k}
is a finite
\displaystyle k
-group scheme and
\displaystyle F=F_{k}\times_{k}K

\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}(F)

This statement is of some interest as one can show using Ono’s lemma (

\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}(F)

-group schemes.

There is an exact sequence of
\displaystyle k
-group schemes

(8)

0\to F_{k}\to Q_{k}\to T_{k}\to 0

where
\displaystyle Q_{k},T_{k}
are
\displaystyle k
-tori and moreover

is quasi-trivial (write the character group of

by quasi-triviality of

. Since

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}(Q)

\displaystyle m
-torsion for all
\displaystyle m>0
. To prove this, by quasi-triviality of
\displaystyle Q

using Shapiro’s lemma. Next, Proposition

applies and yields an isomorphism
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{2}_{C}({\bf G}_{m})\cong{\rm{Br\,}}_{\rm lt}(X).
Now consider the commutative diagram with exact rows

\begin{CD}H^{0}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}k@>{}>{}>{\rm{Br\,}}X@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})\cr&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\cr&&\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}{\rm{Br\,}}k_{v}@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}{\rm{Br\,}}(X\times_{k}{k_{v}})@>{}>{}>\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},{\rm{Pic\,}}\overline{X})\end{CD}

coming from the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence as in the proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle m
-torsion by finiteness of the
\displaystyle m
-Selmer group of the Jacobian of
\displaystyle X

for all but finitely many

Next we show that, in contrast to the case of tori, the Tate–Shafarevich group associated with a finitely generated Galois module is always trivial. The proof, which works over an arbitrary Hilbertian field, is an easy variant of an argument in
[

]

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
\displaystyle k
is finitely generated over
\displaystyle{\bf Q}
and
let
\displaystyle M
be a finitely generated
\displaystyle{\rm{Gal\,}}(\overline{K}|K)
-module considered as an étale locally constant
\displaystyle K
-group scheme. Then
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(M)=0
.

Proof.

Consider
\displaystyle\alpha\in\cyrille X^{1}_{C}(M)
. Shrinking
\displaystyle C
if necessary we
may assume that
\displaystyle M
extends to a smooth group scheme
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}

over
\displaystyle C
and
\displaystyle\alpha
comes from an element in
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}})
. For a closed point
\displaystyle c\in C
with residue field
\displaystyle k(c)

the specialization map
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{M}})\to H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{M}}_{c})

to the fibre
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{c}
is an isomorphism.
Moreover, the
restriction map
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{M}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},M)
is injective. Indeed, denoting by
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh}
the strict henselization of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
and by
\displaystyle K_{c}^{\rm sh}
its fraction field, we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k(c),H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh},{\mathcal{M}}))@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c},{\mathcal{M}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh},{\mathcal{M}})\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(k(c),H^{0}(K_{c}^{\rm sh},{\mathcal{M}}))@>{}>{}>H^{1}(K_{c},{\mathcal{M}})\end{CD}

whose exact rows come from the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence. Here the first vertical map is an isomorphism because
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}
becomes constant on
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh}
, and the group
\displaystyle H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh},{\mathcal{M}})
vanishes because moreover
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{c}^{\rm sh}
is simply connected. This justifies the claimed injectivity,
so our
\displaystyle\alpha
comes from an element in the kernel of the specialisation maps
\displaystyle s_{c}:H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}})\to H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{M}}_{c})
for
\displaystyle c\in C
.

Next, we find a finite Galois extension with group
\displaystyle\Gamma
such that
\displaystyle\alpha
comes from
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,M^{{\rm{Gal\,}}(\overline{K}|L)})
. Let
\displaystyle D
be the normalization of
\displaystyle C
in
\displaystyle L
.
Shrinking again
\displaystyle C
if necessary,
we can assume that the covering
\displaystyle D\to C
is étale and that
\displaystyle\alpha
comes from the subgroup
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{M}}(D))\subset H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}})
.
For
\displaystyle c\in C
let

\displaystyle\Gamma_{c}\subset\Gamma
be the decomposition subgroup of the covering
\displaystyle D/C
at
\displaystyle c
.
The restriction of
\displaystyle s_{c}
to
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{M}}(D))
is given by the restriction
map
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{M}}(D))\to H^{1}(\Gamma_{c},{\mathcal{M}}(D))
, where the target can be identified with a subgroup of
\displaystyle H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{M}}_{c})
because
\displaystyle D\to C
is an étale covering.
Now by the Hilbert irreducibility theorem there are infinitely many closed points
\displaystyle c\in C
such that the fibre of
\displaystyle D\to C

at
\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\Gamma_{c}=\Gamma
. Thus the restriction map

\displaystyle H^{1}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{M}})\to H^{1}(\Gamma_{c},{\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle\alpha=0
.

We now make the following observation. In the proof of Theorem
1.1
(b) we have in fact proven the following stronger
statement:
if
\displaystyle k
and
\displaystyle G
are as in the theorem,
\displaystyle C\subset X
is such that
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle c\in C
is a rational point, then the kernel of the restriction map
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},G)
has finite image in
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,G)
. We now show by way of an example that this stronger statement may fail already if
\displaystyle G
is a non-constant torus over a number field. We shall need the easy lemma:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\displaystyle V
be a smooth and geometrically integral variety over a number field

\displaystyle k
, with function field
\displaystyle K
. For a
\displaystyle k
-torus
\displaystyle T
the kernel of the
restriction map

\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T)\to H^{1}(K,T)

is finite.

Proof.

By the Lang–Weil estimates and Hensel’s lemma we find a finite
set of places
\displaystyle S
of
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle V(k_{v})\neq\emptyset
for all

\displaystyle v\not\in S
. With notation as in Lemma
2.1
, we show that the kernel of
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T)\to H^{1}(K,T)
is contained in the group
\displaystyle\cyrille X^{S}(T)
, which is known to be finite (see Lemma
2.2
). Take
\displaystyle\alpha\in{\rm{Ker}}(H^{1}(k,T)\to H^{1}(K,T))
. We find a nonempty open subscheme
\displaystyle U\subset V
such that the restriction of
\displaystyle\alpha
to

\displaystyle H^{1}(U,T)
is zero. For
\displaystyle v\not\in S
we have
\displaystyle U(k_{v})\neq\emptyset

by the implicit function
theorem, which implies that the
restriction of
\displaystyle\alpha
to
\displaystyle H^{1}(k_{v},T)
of
\displaystyle\alpha
is also zero, as
claimed.

We now come to the promised example.

Example 4.6
.

Let
\displaystyle k
be a number field and
\displaystyle D\to C
a finite étale Galois covering of smooth and geometrically
integral
\displaystyle k
-curves, inducing a field extension
\displaystyle L\supset K
on generic fibres. Assume that
\displaystyle C
contains a rational point
\displaystyle c\in C(k)
such that
the fibre of
\displaystyle D\to C
at
\displaystyle c
consists of a single closed point
\displaystyle d\in D

with residue field
\displaystyle l\supset k
. Fix a
\displaystyle k
-torus
\displaystyle T_{k}
such that the restriction map
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T_{k})\to H^{1}(l,T_{k})
has infinite kernel. For instance, one may take
\displaystyle T_{k}
to be the norm torus
\displaystyle{\bf R}^{1}_{l/k}{\bf G}_{m}
as
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T_{k})\cong k^{*}/N_{l/k}(l^{*})
is known to be infinite by (
[
4
]
, ex. 15.1) and
\displaystyle H^{1}(l,T_{k})=0
. Finally, consider the
\displaystyle L
-torus
\displaystyle T_{L}:=T_{k}\times_{k}L
,
denote by
\displaystyle T
the
\displaystyle K
-torus
\displaystyle T:={\bf R}_{L/K}(T_{L})
obtained by Weil restriction and extend it to a
\displaystyle C
-torus
\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}
.

We now show that the kernel of the map
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{T}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},T)
has infinite image in
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,T)
for every
\displaystyle c\in C(k)
satisfying the above condition. Denoting by
\displaystyle\Sigma
the complement of
\displaystyle C
in
\displaystyle X
, we may identify the image of
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{T}})
in
\displaystyle H^{1}(K,T)
with the subgroup
\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Sigma}(K,T)\subset H^{1}(K,T)
consisting of elements unramified outside
\displaystyle\Sigma
.
Let
\displaystyle\Sigma_{D}
be the set of closed points of
\displaystyle D
lying over
a point of
\displaystyle\Sigma
. By basic properties of the Weil restriction we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Sigma}(K,T)\simeq H^{1}_{\Sigma_{D}}(L,T_{L})
and
\displaystyle H^{1}(K_{c},T)\cong H^{1}(L_{d},T_{L})
.
Hence as before it will suffice to check that the kernel of the specialization map
\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Sigma_{D}}(L,T_{L})\to H^{1}(l,T_{k})
at
\displaystyle d
is infinite.
Now
\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Sigma_{D}}(L,T_{L})
contains the image of the natural map
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T_{k})\to H^{1}(L,T_{L})
which has finite kernel by the above lemma applied to the geometrically integral
\displaystyle k
-curve
\displaystyle D
. On the other hand, the composite map
\displaystyle H^{1}(k,T_{k})\to H^{1}_{\Sigma_{D}}(L,T_{L})\to H^{1}(l,T_{k})
is the natural restriction and therefore has infinite kernel by our assumption. The claim follows.

Finally, we show that if instead of a torus we consider a finite étale group scheme, there is no such counterexample, even over an arbitrary field of characteristic 0.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume
\displaystyle k
is a field of characteristic 0, and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}
is a finite étale group scheme over
\displaystyle C
with generic fibre
\displaystyle F
over
\displaystyle K
. For every closed point
\displaystyle c\in C
the kernel of the restriction map

H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}})\to H^{1}(K_{c},F)

is finite.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
4.4
, it is equivalent to study the kernel of the specialization map
\displaystyle H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}})\to H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{F}}_{c})
, where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
is the fibre of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}
at
\displaystyle c
.

We start with the case
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}=\mu_{n}
. The
\displaystyle n
-torsion subgroup of
\displaystyle{\rm{Pic\,}}X
is finite, being contained in the finite
\displaystyle n
-torsion subgroup of the abelian variety
\displaystyle{\rm{Pic\,}}^{0}(X\times_{k}{\bar{k}})
. Therefore the
\displaystyle n
-torsion subgroup

{}_{n}{\rm{Pic\,}}C\subset{\rm{Pic\,}}C
is also finite because
\displaystyle{\rm{Pic\,}}C
is the quotient of

\displaystyle{\rm{Pic\,}}X
by a finitely generated group (a quotient of the group of divisors supported in
\displaystyle X\setminus C)
.

Using the commutative diagram with exact row

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>k[C]^{*}/k[C]^{*^{n}}@>{}>{}>H^{1}(C,\mu_{n})@>{}>{}>{}_{n}{\rm{Pic\,}}C@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&k(c)^{*}/k(c)^{*^{n}}@>{\cong}>{}>H^{1}(k(c),\mu_{n})\end{CD}

it is therefore sufficient to show finiteness of the kernel of the left vertical map.
In the exact sequence

0\to k^{*}\to k[C]^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Div}_{X\setminus C}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{X\setminus C}\to 0

the group
\displaystyle P_{X\setminus C}
of principal divisors supported in
\displaystyle X\setminus C
is finitely generated, and therefore
\displaystyle k^{*}/k^{*n}
is of finite index in
\displaystyle k[C]^{*}/k[C]^{*n}
. Thus we may replace the latter with the former. Furthermore, after replacing
\displaystyle k(c)
by its Galois closure
\displaystyle k_{1}
it is enough to show finiteness of the kernel of the map
\displaystyle k^{*}/k^{*n}\to k_{1}^{*}/k_{1}^{*n}
, which by Kummer theory and the restriction-inflation sequence identifies with the finite kernel
\displaystyle H^{1}({\rm{Gal\,}}(k_{1}/k),\mu_{n})
of the restriction map

\displaystyle H^{1}(k,\mu_{n})\to H^{1}(k_{1},\mu_{n})
.

In the general case choose a geometrically integral,
finite, Galois étale covering
\displaystyle D
of

\displaystyle C
such that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{D}:={\mathcal{F}}\times_{C}D
is isomorphic to a direct
sum of copies of
\displaystyle\mu_{n_{i}}
for various positive integers
\displaystyle n_{i}
. Set
\displaystyle G={\rm{Gal\,}}(D/C)
, and let
\displaystyle d\in D
be a closed point of
\displaystyle D
lying over
\displaystyle c
.
The Hochschild-Serre
spectral sequence gives the exact row in the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(G,{\mathcal{F}}(D))@>{}>{}>H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{F}}_{D})\\
&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&H^{1}(k(c),{\mathcal{F}}_{c})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(D,{\mathcal{F}}_{d})\end{CD}

Here
\displaystyle H^{1}(G,{\mathcal{F}}(D))
is finite because both
\displaystyle G
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(D)
are finite.
The kernel of the right vertical map is finite by the case
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}=\mu_{n}
, and we are done.

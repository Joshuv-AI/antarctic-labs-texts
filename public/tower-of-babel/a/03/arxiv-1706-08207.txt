Introduction and main results

Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a compact Riemannian surface without boundary,
W^{1,2}(\Sigma)
be the usual Sobolev space.
Define a function space

\mathcal{H}=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma):\,\int_{\Sigma}udv_{g}=0\right\}.

(1)

Let
h
be a positive smooth function on
\Sigma
and
J_{\beta}:W^{1,2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a functional defined by

J_{\beta}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}dv_{g}-\beta\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{u}dv_{g},

(2)

where
\nabla_{g}u
denotes the gradient of
u
and
dv_{g}
denotes the volume element of
(\Sigma,g)
.
In view of the Trudinger-Moser inequality due to Fontana
[
7
]
,

J_{\beta}
has a minimizer on
\mathcal{H}
for any
\beta<8\pi
; while in the case
\beta=8\pi
, the situation becomes subtle.
Using a method of blow-up analysis, Ding-Jost-Li-Wang
[
4
]
proved that if
J_{8\pi}
has no minimizer on
\mathcal{H}
, then

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{8\pi}(u)=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{p\in\Sigma}(A_{p}+2\log h(p)),

(3)

where
A_{p}=\lim_{x\rightarrow p}(G_{p}(x)+4\log r)
is a constant,
r
denotes the geodesic distance between
x
and
p
,
G_{p}
is a Green function
satisfying

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}G_{p}=8\pi\delta_{p}-\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\\[5.16663pt]
\int_{\Sigma}G_{p}dv_{g}=0,\end{cases}

and
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator.
Moreover, they give a geometric condition under which
J_{8\pi}
has a minimizer on
\mathcal{H}
.
Clearly the minimizer is a solution of a Kazdan-Warner equation
[
8
]
, namely

\Delta_{g}u=\frac{8\pi he^{u}}{\int_{\Sigma}he^{u}dv_{g}}-\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}.

(4)

We let
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
be the first eigenvalue of
\Delta_{g}
, say

\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H},\,\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}dv_{g}=1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}dv_{g}.

(5)

It follows from the Poincaré inequality that if
\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
, then

\|u\|_{1,\alpha}=\left(\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}-\alpha u^{2})dv_{g}\right)^{1/2}

defines a Sobolev norm on
\mathcal{H}
. In a previous work
[
15
]
, using the method of blow-up analysis,
we proved the following: for any
\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
,
there holds

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H},\,\|u\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{\Sigma}e^{4\pi u^{2}}dv_{g}<\infty

(6)

and the supremum is attained. As a consequence of (
6
),
there exists some constant
C
depending only on
(\Sigma,g)
and
\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{H}
,

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-8\pi\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{u}dv_{g}\geq-C.

(7)

This improves the Trudinger-Moser inequality of the weak form, namely (
7
) in the case
\alpha=0
.
We refer
the reader to
[
1
,
14
,
10
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
]

for related works involving the norms
\|u\|_{1,\alpha}
.

Our aim in this paper is to achieve an analog of (
3
).
More precisely, we consider functionals

J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}-\alpha u^{2})dv_{g}-\beta\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{u}dv_{g}.

(8)

Obviously, when
\alpha=0
,
J_{\alpha,\beta}
reduces to
J_{\beta}
defined as in (
2
).
Our first result reads

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a compact Riemannian surface without boundary,
h
be a positive smooth function on
\Sigma
,
and
\mathcal{H}
,
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
and
J_{\alpha,\beta}
be defined
as in (
1
), (
5
) and (
8
) respectively. Then we have the following three assertions:

(i)
If
\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
and
J_{\alpha,8\pi}

has no minimizer in
\mathcal{H}
, then there holds

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{p\in\Sigma}(A_{p}+2\log h(p)),

where
A_{p}=\lim_{x\rightarrow p}(G_{p}(x)+4\log r)
is a constant,
r
denotes the geodesic distance between
x
and
p
,

G_{p}
is a Green function
satisfying

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}G_{p}-\alpha G_{p}=8\pi\delta_{p}-\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\\[5.16663pt]
\int_{\Sigma}G_{p}dv_{g}=0;\end{cases}

(9)

(ii)
If
\alpha\geq\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
, then
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)=-\infty;

(iii)
If
\beta>8\pi
, then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)=-\infty
.

Since the Euler-Lagrange equation of a minimum point of
J_{\alpha,8\pi}
on
\mathcal{H}
is

\Delta_{g}u-\alpha u=\frac{8\pi he^{u}}{\int_{\Sigma}he^{u}dv_{g}}-\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)},

(10)

an application of
(i)
of Theorem
1
is the following:

Corollary 2
.

For any
\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
,
if

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)\not=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{p\in\Sigma}(A_{p}+2\log h(p)),

(11)

then the Kazdan-Warner equation (
10
) has a solution
u\in\mathcal{H}
.

As in
[
15
]
, we consider the case that
\alpha

is allowed to be larger than
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
. Precisely, we let

\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)<\lambda_{2}(\Sigma)<\cdots
be all distinct eigenvalues
of
\Delta_{g}
,
E_{\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)}

be the eigenfunction space with respect to
\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)
, namely

E_{\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\,\Delta_{g}u=\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)u\right\},

(12)

and

E_{\ell}=E_{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)}\oplus E_{\lambda_{2}(\Sigma)}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)}
,

\ell=1,2,\cdots
. Define

E_{\ell}^{\perp}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\int_{\Sigma}uvdv_{g}=0,\,\forall v\in E_{\ell}\right\}.

(13)

Now we state an analog of Theorem
1
as follows:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\Sigma,g)
,
h
,
\mathcal{H}
and
J_{\alpha,\beta}
be as in Theorem
1
,
\lambda_{\ell}(\Sigma)

be the
\ell
-th eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
E_{\ell}
and

E_{\ell}^{\perp}
be defined as in (
13
).
Then we have the following three assertions:

(i)
If
\alpha<\lambda_{\ell+1}(\Sigma)
and
J_{\alpha,8\pi}

has no minimizer in
E_{\ell}^{\perp}
, then there holds

\inf_{u\in E_{\ell}^{\perp}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{p\in\Sigma}(A_{\alpha,p}+2\log h(p)),

where
A_{\alpha,p}=\lim_{x\rightarrow p}(G_{\alpha,p}(x)+4\log r)
is a constant,
r
denotes the geodesic distance between
x
and
p
,

G_{\alpha,p}
is a Green function
satisfying

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}G_{\alpha,p}-\alpha G_{\alpha,p}=8\pi\delta_{p}-\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\\[5.16663pt]
\int_{\Sigma}G_{\alpha,p}vdv_{g}=0,\,\forall v\in E_{\ell};\end{cases}

(14)

(ii)
If
\alpha\geq\lambda_{\ell+1}(\Sigma)
, then

\inf_{u\in E_{\ell}^{\perp}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)=-\infty;

(iii)
If
\beta>8\pi
, then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\inf_{u\in E_{\ell}^{\perp}}J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)=-\infty
.

Similar to Corollary
2
, we have the following:

Corollary 4
.

For any
\alpha<\lambda_{\ell+1}(\Sigma)
,
if

\inf_{u\in E_{\ell}^{\perp}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)\not=-8\pi-8\pi\log\pi-4\pi\max_{p\in\Sigma}(A_{\alpha,p}+2\log h(p)),

(15)

then the Kazdan-Warner equation (
10
) has a solution
u\in E_{\ell}^{\perp}
.

We remark that any geometric hypothesis under which (
11
) or (
15
) holds would be extremely interesting.
When
\alpha=0
, a geometric condition was given by Ding-Jost-Li-Wang
[
4
]
. Generally it is difficult to be obtained
possibly because the Green function has at most
C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity for some
0<\gamma<1
in presence of
\alpha
.

Now we describe our method. For the proof of
(ii)
and
(iii)
of Theorems
1
and
3
,
we shall construct suitable function sequences. To prove
(i)
of Theorems
1
and

,

and

.

in

, where

is an appropriate

is fixed, and

The other is that in the process of deriving lower bound of

instead of three regions

,

2.1
Proof of
(ii)
of Theorem
1

Let
\alpha\geq\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
be fixed and
E_{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)}
be the eigenfunction space defined as in (
12
).
Take
0\not\equiv u_{0}\in E_{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)}
. Obviously we have

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g}u_{0}|^{2}dv_{g}=\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\int_{\Sigma}u_{0}^{2}dv_{g}

and thus

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{g}u_{0}|^{2}-\alpha u_{0}^{2})dv_{g}\leq 0.

(16)

Since
\int_{\Sigma}u_{0}dv_{g}=0
, there exists
x_{0}\in\Sigma
and
\delta>0
such that

u_{0}(x)\geq u_{0}(x_{0})/2>0,\quad\forall x\in B_{\delta}(x_{0}).

(17)

It follows from (
16
) and (
17
) that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle J_{\alpha,8\pi}(tu_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{g}u_{0}|^{2}-\alpha u_{0}^{2})dv_{g}-8\pi\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{tu_{0}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-8\pi\log\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}he^{tu_{0}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4\pi tu_{0}(x_{0})-8\pi\log\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}hdv_{g}.

Hence
J_{\alpha,8\pi}(tu_{0})\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and the desired result follows immediately.
\hfill\Box

2.2
Proof of
(iii)
of Theorem
1

Let
\beta>8\pi
be fixed and
i_{g}(\Sigma)
be the injectivity radius of
(\Sigma,g)
.
Fix some point
p\in\Sigma
. Let
r
,
0<r<i_{g}(\Sigma)/2
, be a real number to be determined later. Take a sequence of
functions

M_{k}=M_{k}(x,r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\log k&{\rm when}&\rho\leq rk^{-\frac{1}{4}}\\[6.45831pt]
4\log\frac{r}{\rho}&{\rm when}&rk^{-\frac{1}{4}}<\rho\leq r\\[6.45831pt]
0&{\rm when}&\rho>r,\end{array}\right.

where
x\in\Sigma
,
\rho
denotes the geodesic distance between
x
and
p
, and
k=2,3\cdots
. One calculates

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g}M_{k}|^{2}dv_{g}=(1+O(r))8\pi\log k,

(18)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}M_{k}^{j}dv_{g}=O(1),\quad j=1,2.

(19)

Choose
\zeta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Sigma\setminus B_{2i_{g}(\Sigma)}(p))
satisfying
\zeta\geq 0
and

\zeta\not\equiv 0
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{2i_{g}(\Sigma)}(p)
.
We set

\widetilde{M}_{k}=\widetilde{M}_{k}(x,r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}M_{k}(x,r)&{\rm for}&x\in B_{2i_{g}(\Sigma)}(p)\\[6.45831pt]
t_{k}\zeta(x)&{\rm for}&x\in\Sigma\setminus B_{2i_{g}(\Sigma)}(p),\end{array}\right.

(20)

where
t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that
\widetilde{M}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}
. As a consequence,

t_{k}=-\frac{1}{\int_{\Sigma}\zeta dv_{g}}\int_{\Sigma}M_{k}dv_{g}=O(1).

It follows from (
18
)-(
20
) that

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g}\widetilde{M}_{k}|^{2}dv_{g}-\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\widetilde{M}_{k}^{2}dv_{g}=(1+O(r))8\pi\log k+O(1)

(21)

and

\int_{\Sigma}he^{\widetilde{M}_{k}}dv_{g}\geq\int_{B_{rk^{-{1}/{4}}}(p)}he^{{M}_{k}}dv_{g}\geq(\min_{\Sigma}h)\pi r^{2}\sqrt{k}(1+o_{k}(1)).

(22)

Combining (
21
) and (
22
), we have

J_{\alpha,\beta}(\widetilde{M}_{k})\leq(1+O(r))4\pi\log k-\frac{\beta}{2}\log k+O(1).

Note that
\beta>8\pi
. If
r
is chosen sufficiently small, then we conclude

J_{\alpha,\beta}(\widetilde{M}_{k})\rightarrow-\infty\quad{\rm as}\quad k\rightarrow+\infty.

This completes the proof of
(iii)
of Theorem
1
.
\hfill\Box

. Since the proof of

of Theorem

is very long, we sketch its outline as follows:

Step 1
. For any
\epsilon
,

, there exists a minimizer

for the subcritical functional
J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}
.
By assumption that

has no minimizer on
\mathcal{H}
,
we have
u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=\max_{\Sigma}u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty
as

\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Step 2
. We prove that
u_{\epsilon}(\exp_{x_{\epsilon}}(r_{\epsilon}x))-\max_{\Sigma}u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\varphi(x)

in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
\exp_{x_{\epsilon}}
denotes the exponential map on
(\Sigma,g)
,
r_{\epsilon}
is an appropriate
scale, and
\varphi
can be explicitly written out via a classification result of Chen-Li
[
3
]
.
Moreover, assuming
x_{\epsilon}\rightarrow p
, we show that
u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow G_{p}
weakly in
W^{1,q}(\Sigma)
for any
1<q<2
and in

C^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\Sigma\setminus\{p\})
, where
G_{p}
is a Green function on
(\Sigma,g)
.
Step 3
. Applying the maximum principle
to
u_{\epsilon}-G_{x_{\epsilon}}
and using the
asymptotic behavior of
u_{\epsilon}
derived in Step 2,
we obtain a lower bound of
J_{\alpha,8\pi}
on
\mathcal{H}
.
Step 4
. We construct a sequence of functions
\phi_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}

such that

converges to the lower bound obtained in Step 3.

2.3
Minimizers for subcritical functionals

We first prove that
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)
is attained for any
\beta<8\pi
. Precisely we have

Proposition 5
.

For any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists some function
u_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}\cap C^{1}(\Sigma)
such that

J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\epsilon})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u).

(23)

Moreover,
u_{\epsilon}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{g}u_{\epsilon}-\alpha u_{\epsilon}=8\pi(1-\epsilon)\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}^{-1}he^{u_{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\right)\\[6.45831pt]
\lambda_{\epsilon}=\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g},\quad\int_{\Sigma}u_{\epsilon}dv_{g}=0.\end{array}\right.

(24)

Proof.
Let
0<\epsilon<1
be fixed. Take
u_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{j})\rightarrow\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)

(25)

as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Noting that

\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{j}}dv_{g}\leq\int_{\Sigma}he^{4\pi(1-\epsilon/2)\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}+\frac{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{16\pi(1-\epsilon/2)}}dv_{g},

(26)

we obtain by (
25
) and (
6
),

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)+o_{j}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-8\pi(1-\epsilon)\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{j}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2-\epsilon}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-8\pi(1-\epsilon)\log\int_{\Sigma}he^{4\pi(1-\epsilon/2)\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{4}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-C.

Hence
u_{j}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}
. We can assume without loss of generality that

u_{j}
converges to
u_{\epsilon}
weakly in
\mathcal{H}
, strongly in
L^{q}(\Sigma)
for any
q>0
and
almost everywhere in
\Sigma
. Clearly

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}.

(27)

Moreover an analog of (
26
) implies that
e^{|u_{j}|}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Sigma)
for any
p>0
.
This together with the mean value theorem and the Hölder inequality,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{j}}dv_{g}=\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}.

(28)

Combining (
27
) and (
28
), we conclude (
23
).

Using a method of Lagrange multiplier, one easily gets (
24
), the Euler-Lagrange equation of
the minimizer
u_{\epsilon}
. Applying elliptic estimates to (
24
), we have
u_{\epsilon}\in C^{1}(\Sigma)
.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 6
.

\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{\epsilon}>0
.

Proof.
One may conclude the lemma by using the Jensen inequality. But we prefer a contradiction argument as below.
Clearly

J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\epsilon})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)\leq J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(0)\leq 8\pi|\log\int_{\Sigma}hdv_{g}|.

(29)

If
\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{\epsilon}=0
, then up to a subsequence

J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\epsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}-8\pi(1-\epsilon)\log\lambda_{\epsilon}\rightarrow+\infty,

which contradicts (
29
). Thus we get the desired result.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 7
.

There holds

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u).

Proof.
Though the proof may be obvious for experts, we give the details here for reader’s convenience.
On one hand, for any
\eta>0
, there exists some
u_{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}

such that

J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u_{\eta})<\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)+\eta.

Obviously we have

\displaystyle J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u_{\eta})=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\eta})\geq\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\epsilon})=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u).

Hence

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)<\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)+\eta.

Since
\eta>0
is arbitrary, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u)\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u).

(30)

On the other hand,

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)\leq J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u_{\epsilon})=\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\nu)}(u_{\epsilon}).

Extracting diagonal sequence, we obtain

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}}J_{\alpha,8\pi}(u)\leq\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}J_{\alpha,8\pi(1-\epsilon)}(u_{\epsilon}).

(31)

Combining (
30
) and (
31
), we get the desired result.
\hfill\Box

\mathcal{H}

2.4

Proposition 10

Lemma 11

Let

Multiplying both sides of the equation (

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{g}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\alpha u_{\epsilon}^{2})dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi(1-\epsilon)}{\lambda_{\epsilon}}\int_{\Sigma}hu_{\epsilon}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\pi c_{\epsilon}.

In view of the Trudinger-Moser inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}he^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle C\int_{\Sigma}e^{4\pi\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{2}}{\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}+\frac{\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{16\pi}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle Ce^{\frac{\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{16\pi}}\leq Ce^{\frac{1}{2}c_{\epsilon}}.

Let

Lemma 12

Proof.
In view of (

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{\epsilon}}\psi_{\epsilon}(y)=\alpha r_{\epsilon}^{2}\psi_{\epsilon}(y)+c_{\epsilon}^{-1}\frac{h(\exp_{{x}_{\epsilon}}(r_{\epsilon}y))}{h(p)}e^{{u}_{\epsilon}(\exp_{{x}_{\epsilon}}(r_{\epsilon}y))-c_{\epsilon}}-\frac{8\pi(1-\epsilon)}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}r_{\epsilon}^{2}c_{\epsilon}^{-1}.

\displaystyle Cr_{\epsilon}^{2q-2}c_{\epsilon}^{-q}\int_{\Sigma}|u_{\epsilon}|^{q}dv_{g}

Let

2.5

In this subsection, we shall derive a lower bound of

For any fixed

2.5
) respectively. Using (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma\setminus{B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}}G_{x_{\epsilon}}\left(\Delta_{g}u_{\epsilon}-\alpha u_{\epsilon}+\frac{8\pi(1-\epsilon)}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\right)dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\int_{B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}u_{\epsilon}dv_{g}-\frac{8\pi(1-\epsilon)}{{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Sigma)}\int_{B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}G_{x_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}G_{x_{\epsilon}}\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial n}ds_{g}-\int_{\partial B_{Rr_{\epsilon}}(x_{\epsilon})}u_{\epsilon}\frac{\partial G_{x_{\epsilon}}}{\partial n}ds_{g}.

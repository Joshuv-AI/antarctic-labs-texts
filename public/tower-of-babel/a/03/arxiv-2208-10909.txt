Elliptic equations with a singular drift from a weak Morrey space
Misha Chernobai, Tim Shilkin

Abstract
In this paper we prove the existence, uniqueness and regularity of weak solutions to the Dirichlet problem for an elliptic equation with a drift b b satisfying div b ≤ 0 \mathop{\rm div}b\leq 0 in Ω \Omega . We assume b b belongs to some weak Morrey class which includes in the 3D case, in particular, drifts having a singularity along the axis x 3 x_{3} with the asymptotic b ⁡ ( x ) ∼ c / r b(x)\sim c/r , where r = x 1 2 + x 2 2 r=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}} .



1
Introduction and Main Results

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
n\geq 3
. We consider the
following boundary value problem:

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u\,=\,-\mathop{\rm div}f\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}\ =\ 0.\qquad\quad\end{gathered}\right.

(1.1)

Here
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is unknown,
b:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given functions.
In particular, we are interested in the drift of type

n=3,\qquad b(x)=-\alpha\,\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}},\qquad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},0),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

Motivated by this example, we assume that the drift
b
satisfies the condition

b\in L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega),

(1.3)

where

L^{p,\lambda}_{w}(\Omega)
is the weak Morrey space equipped
with the quasi-norm

\|b\|_{L^{p,\lambda}_{w}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \sup\limits_{x_{0}\in\Omega}\sup\limits_{R<\mathop{\rm diam}\Omega}\,R^{-\frac{\lambda}{p}}\,\|b\|_{L_{p,w}(B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)},

and
L_{p,w}(\Omega)
is the weak Lebesgue space equipped with the quasi-norm

\|b\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \sup\limits_{s>0}s~|\{~x\in\Omega:~|b(x)|>s~\}|^{\frac{1}{p}},

(1.4)

where
|\Omega|
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.
Note that the condition (
1.3
) implies in particular that
b
belongs to the usual Morrey space
L^{p,n-p}(\Omega)
for any
p\in[1,2)
(see Proposition
2.1
below and the definition of Morrey spaces at the end of this section).
Note also that both scales of spaces
L^{p,n-p}(\Omega)
and
L^{p,n-p}_{w}(\Omega)
are critical ones, i.e. these spaces are invariant under the scaling

b^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda\,b(\lambda x),\quad\lambda>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\quad\|b^{\lambda}\|_{L^{p,n-p}_{w}(\Omega_{\lambda})}=\|b\|_{L^{p,n-p}_{w}(\Omega)},

(1.5)

where we assume
\Omega
is star-shaped and denote
\Omega_{\lambda}:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,x/\lambda\in\Omega\,\}
.
Concerning further properties of weak Morrey spaces we refer to
[
12
]
,
[
39
]
. Here we emphasise only that the scale of weak Morrey spaces
L_{w}^{p,n-p}(\Omega)
is convenient for the description of drifts
b
which have a certain asymptotic near some singular submanifold
\Sigma\subset\Omega
. For example, for
n=3
we have

\begin{array}[]{ccl}b(x)\sim\frac{1}{x_{3}},&\Sigma\ =\mbox{ a plane }&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\in L^{1,2}_{w}(\Omega),\\
b(x)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}},&\Sigma\ =\mbox{ a line }&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\in L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega),\\
b(x)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}}},&\Sigma\ =\mbox{ a point }&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\in L^{3,0}_{w}(\Omega)=L_{3,w}(\Omega).\\
\end{array}

We define the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
by

\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ :=\ \int\limits_{\Omega}\eta\,b\cdot\nabla u~dx.

(1.6)

Note that for

b
satisfying (
1.3
) the bilinear form (
1.6
)
generally speaking is not well-defined for
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
where

W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)\,:=\,\{\,u\in L_{p}(\Omega):\,\nabla u\in L_{p}(\Omega)\,\},\qquad p\in[1,+\infty],

is the usual Sobolev space and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the linear space of
C^{\infty}
–smooth functions compactly supported in
\Omega
.

Nevertheless, the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
is
well-defined at least for
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
with
p>2
and

\eta\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
q>\frac{2p}{p-2}
. So,
instead of the standard notion of weak solutions from the energy
class
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
we introduce the definition of
p
-weak
solutions to the problem (
1.1
), see also
[
22
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
31
]
:

Definition 1.1
.

Assume
p>2
,
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and
f\in L_{1}(\Omega)
.
We say
u
is a
p
-weak solution to the problem
(
1.1
) if
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)

and
u
satisfies the identity

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\ +\ \mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ =\ \int\limits_{\Omega}f\cdot\nabla\eta~dx,\qquad\forall~\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).\end{gathered}

(1.7)

If
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
and
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.7
) then we call
u
a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
). Obviously, in this case
p
-weak solutions are some subclass of weak solutions.

Note that if
u
is a weak or a
p
-weak solution to (
1.1
) and
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
then by density arguments we can extend the class of test functions in (
1.7
) from
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to all functions
\eta\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.

In this paper we always assume that the vector field
b
has a sign-defined divergence:

\mathop{\rm div}b\,\leq\,0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

(1.8)

where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
is the space of distributions on
\Omega
.
In the case (
1.2
) the condition (
1.8
) corresponds to
\alpha\geq 0
.
The important impact of the condition (
1.8
) is due to the fact that in this case the quadratic form
\mathcal{B}[u,u]
provides a positive support to the quadratic form of the elliptic operator in (
1.1
) (see Proposition
2.2
below), while in the opposite case
\mathop{\rm div}b\geq 0
in
\Omega
the quadratic form
\mathcal{B}[u,u]
is (formally) non-positive and hence it “shifts” the operator to the “spectral area”. It is well-known that in the case of a non-regular drift
b
the violation of the condition (
1.8
) potentially can ruin the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
) even in the class of smooth solutions. For example, in the case
\Omega=B
where
B:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
the functions

u(x)=c(|x|^{2}-1),\quad b(x)=n\,\tfrac{x}{|x|^{2}},\quad b\in L_{n,w}(B),\quad\mathop{\rm div}b\geq 0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(B)

(1.9)

satisfy (
1.1
) with
f\equiv 0
. Note also that in the regular case
b\in L_{n}(\Omega)
the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
) holds regardless of the sign of
\mathop{\rm div}b
due to the maximum principle, see, for example,
[
16
]
and reference there.

The main results of the present paper are the following two
theorems:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
n\geq 3
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
b
satisfies
(
1.3
), (
1.8
). Then there exist
p>2

depending only on
n
,
\Omega
and
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
such
that for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
there exists a unique
p
-weak
solution
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
to
the problem (
1.1
). Moreover, this solution satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},

(1.10)

with a
constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
n\geq 3
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
b
satisfies
(
1.3
), (
1.8
) and
p>2
. Then for any

q>n
there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\Omega
and
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}

such that
if
u
is a
p
–weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) corresponding to the right-hand side
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
then
u
is Hölder continuous on
\bar{\Omega}
with the exponent
\mu
and the estimate

\|u\|_{C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(1.11)

holds with the
constant
c>0
depending only on
q
,
p
,
n
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
.

The study of the weak solvability and properties of weak solutions to drift-diffusion equations has a long history. Below we focus on the brief overview of results concerning the existence of weak solutions, their uniqueness, boundedness and Hölder continuity.

Very often the study of the problem (
1.1
) is coupled with the study of the dual problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\Delta v-\mathop{\rm div}(bv)\,=\,\mathop{\rm div}g\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
v|_{\partial\Omega}\ =\ 0.\qquad\quad\end{gathered}\right.

(1.12)

In the case of the divergence–free drift

\mathop{\rm div}b=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

(1.13)

problems (
1.1
) and (
1.12
) are equivalent, but for the general drifts it is not the case. The reason is that the dual problem (
1.12
) is presented in the divergent form. Hence weak solutions, depending on the regularity of
b
, in principle are well-defined in a wider class than the energy space
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
(one can consider classes of very weak solutions, renormalized solutions etc.) For example, in a series of papers
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
(see also
[
7
]
and reference there) the existence of weak (distributional) solutions for the problem (
1.12
) in various functional classes under various critical and supercritical assumptions on
b
was studied.

Another approach to the solvability of the problem (
1.1
) with the rough coefficients was developed in a series of papers
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
(see also references there). This approach is applicable to elliptic operators in the non-divergent form (with the main part
-a_{jk}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}
with a non-smooth uniformly elliptic matrix
a(x)=(a_{jk}(x))
instead of the Laplacian) and the drift
b
belonging to the critical Morrey spaces. In this approach the existence of solutions in Sobolev spaces
W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
with some
p>1
is investigated and the equation (
1.1
) is understood a.e. in
\Omega
.

For the problem (
1.1
) weak solutions from
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
formally are well-defined only for
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
which does not cover the case (
1.3
). Nevertheless, weak solutions to elliptic and parabolic equations with rough coefficients are commonly known to possess the
higher integrability
property. This means that the gradient of a weak solution is integrable with some exponent which is slightly greater than 2. One can ask what are the optimal conditions on the drift
b
which provide the higher integrability of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
)? The first results in this direction were obtained earlier in
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
31
]
where the critical drift
b\in L_{n,w}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.8
) was studied. So, our Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as a far away extension of these results. Note also that the 2D case was investigated separately in
[
8
]
and
[
32
]
.

All results concerning weak solvability and properties of weak solutions to the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.12
) formally can be split onto three groups: the results related to the subcrtitical drifts, the results for critical drifts and supercritical results. We remind that we call some functional class
X
supercritical if for
b\in X
and
b^{\lambda}
defined in (
1.5
) the norm
\|b^{\lambda}\|_{X(B)}
computed over a unit ball
B
blows up as
\lambda\to 0
.

The results concerning the drift
b
belonging to subcritical Lesbegue spaces are classical one, see
[
33
]
. The same is true for drifts belonging to the critical Lebesgue space

b\in L_{n}(\Omega).

(1.14)

We call drifts satisfying (
1.14
)
regular
. In the regular case the drift term provides a compact perturbation of the Laplace operator and hence the problem (
1.1
) possesses the Fredholm property (i.e. the existence for (
1.1
) follows from the uniqueness). Moreover, in the case (
1.14
) weak solutions are Hölder continuous and the maximum principle holds (which provides the uniqueness of weak solutions), see the survey of the results, for example, in
[
16
]
. So, we can consider the regular case as a starting point of our investigation. Note that the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of the bilinear form (
1.6
) on the Sololev space
W^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
were investigated in
[
36
]
.

The counterexample (
1.9
) shows that even the minimal relaxation of the condition (
1.14
) (still within the critical scale) can ruin the maximum principle and the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
). So, to recover the uniqueness we need to impose some additional assumptions on
b
. For example, one can consider the condition (
1.8
) or its particular case (
1.13
). (Motivated by the properties of the quadratic form
\mathcal{B}[u,u]
we call the condition (
1.8
) “non-spectral”.) In the present paper we focus only on the critical “non-spectral” case and we are going to investigate the critical “spectral” case
\mathop{\rm div}b\geq 0
in a separate forthcoming paper, see
[
8
]
and
[
32
]
for the related 2D results.

For
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.13
) the uniqueness of weak solutions in the energy class
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
can be found in
[
46
]
(see also
[
45
]
for the parabolic case). In
[
46
]
a counterexample to the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
) with a divergence-free drift was also constructed if the condition
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
is violated (see
[
10
]
and references there for the further developments in this direction).
For
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
satisfying the “non-spectral” condition (
1.8
) the corresponding uniqueness result in the energy class
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
can be easily obtained by a straightforward adaptation of the methods of
[
45
]
,
[
46
]
. For
b\in L_{2,w}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.8
) our Proposition
3.1
below provides a uniqueness result for the problem (
1.1
) in a class of weak solutions which possess the higher integrability property.

The next issue is the boundedness of weak solutions. It is well-known that even in the divergence–free case (
1.13
) the
local
boundedness of weak solutions requires some extra regularity of the drift, see
[
37
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
1
]
, see also a recent survey
[
2
]
and reference there. Nevertheless, our Proposition
3.1
shows that for a drift
b\in L_{2,w}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.8
)
p
–weak solutions to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) are always bounded without any extra assumptions on the regularity of
b
. We emphasize that our result is global (i.e. it requires smooth boundary conditions) and can not be localized. The phenomena of the dependence of local boundedness of weak solutions on their behaviour on the boundary of the domain is discussed in
[
16
]
.

The last issue we are going to discuss is Hölder continuity of weak solutions. Note that under the critical condition (
1.14
) Hölder continuity is the optimal regularity.
For the problems (
1.1
), (
1.12
) with a drift
b
belonging to the subcritical Morrey space
b\in L^{2,\lambda}(\Omega)
with

\lambda\in(n-2,n)
Hölder continuity of weak solutions is known for a long time, see
[
11
]
and
[
38
]
. On the other hand, the counterexample in
[
14
]
shows that even in the divergence–free case (
1.13
) the smallest violation of the critical scale can lead to the loss of continuity by weak solutions. So, in this paper we focus on results concerning drifts belonging to critical spaces.

For the divergence-free drifts from critical spaces the best known for today results were obtained in
[
17
]
,
[
40
]
(see also
[
41
]
) where Hölder continuity of weak solutions was proved for the heat equation with a drift term satisfying (
1.13
) (see also the related result in
[
35
]
). In its elliptic version, these results assume that the drift
b
satisfying (
1.13
) belongs to the space
BMO^{-1}(\Omega)
, i.e. there exists a skew-symmetric matrix
A\in BMO(\Omega)
such that
b=\mathop{\rm div}A
(see the definition of
BMO
space at the end of this section). If we assume
\nabla A\in L^{1,n-1}(\Omega)
(which is in a sense the optimal regularity for the critical Morrey scale
L^{p,n-p}(\Omega)
) then from Trudinger’s imbedding
[
42
]
we obtain
A\in BMO(\Omega)
and hence Hölder continuity of weak solutions follows from
[
17
]
,
[
40
]
.

For the non–divergence free critical drifts satisfying the condition (
1.8
) local a priori estimates of the Hölder norms for Lipschitz continuous solutions to the problem (
1.1
) were obtained earlier in
[
37
]
under the additional restriction

b\in L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)
with
r\in(\tfrac{n}{2},n]
. So, in our Theorem
1.2
we remove this restriction. Note also that our present contribution can be viewed as a multidimensional analogue of the results obtained earlier in
[
8
]
in the 2D case.

Here are some comments and possible extensions:

•

With minor changes in the proofs our technique allows us to obtain results similar to our Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
if we replace the Laplace operator in (
1.1
) by an elliptic operator in the divergence form
-\mathop{\rm div}(a(x)\nabla u)
with a uniformly elliptic matrix
a(x)=(a_{jk}(x))
,
a_{jk}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
. We consider this extension to be obvious.

•

In the case of the Laplace operator in (
1.1
) (or, more generally, in the case of the operator
-\mathop{\rm div}(a(x)\nabla u)
with a sufficiently smooth matrix
a(x)
) our
p
-weak solutions in Theorem
1.1
possesses locally integrable second derivatives
u\in W^{2}_{s,loc}(\Omega)
with some
s>1
(if
f
is sufficiently regular). Hence for
p
-weak solutions the equation (
1.1
) is valid a.e. in
\Omega
. See the related results in
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
for the case of elliptic operators in the non-divergent form with non-smooth coefficients.

•

In our investigation of Hölder continuity of weak solutions in Theorem
1.2
we focus on the “optimal” conditions for
b
but not for
f
. It is well-known (see, for example,
[
20
, Theorem 5.17]
) that Hölder continuity of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
) is valid if the right hand side
f
belongs to some appropriate Morrey space. So, we believe that the condition
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
q>n
in Theorem
1.2
can be relaxed, see related results, for example, in
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.

(f)_{\omega}\ :=\ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\omega}f~dx\ =\ \frac{1}{|\omega|}~\int\limits_{\omega}f~dx,\qquad(f*g)(x)\ :=\ \int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)g(y)~dy.

Auxiliary results

In this section we present several auxiliary results. For reader’s convenience we formulate these results not in their full generality but only in the form they are used in our paper.

The first result shows that by relaxing an exponent of the integrability of a function we can always switch from a weak Morrey norm to a regular one.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
p\in(1,n)
and
1\leq q<p\leq r\leq n
there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
and
r
such that

c_{1}\,\|b\|_{L^{q,n-q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|b\|_{L^{p,n-p}_{w}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,c_{2}\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}

Proof.

The result follows from the Hölder inequality for Lorentz norms, see

[
18
, Section 4.1]
.
∎

The next result shows that under the condition (
1.8
) the quadratic form corresponding the drift term is non-negative.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
p\geq 2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
satisfies
(
1.8
). Let
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
be the bilinear form defined in (
1.6
). Then

\forall\,u\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)\qquad\mathcal{B}[u,u]\ \geq\ 0.

(2.1)

Proof.

The result follows from the inclusions
|u|^{2}\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)
,
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and the inequality

\mathcal{B}[u,u]=\ \frac{1}{2}\,\int\limits_{\Omega}b\cdot\nabla|u|^{2}\,dx\ \geq\ 0.

∎

The next result is the general Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem in Lorentz spaces, see
[
3
, Theorem 5.3.2]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume
\Omega
,
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are bounded domains and assume for some
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
a linear operator
T:L_{p_{j}}(\Omega)\to L_{p_{j}}(\Omega^{\prime})
is bounded for
j=0,1
and

\|T\|_{L_{p_{0}}(\Omega)\to L_{p_{0}}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,\leq\,M_{0},\qquad\|T\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\Omega)\to L_{p_{1}}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,\leq\,M_{1}

Then for any
p\in(p_{0},p_{1})
the operator
T
is bounded from
L_{p,w}(\Omega)
to
L_{p,w}(\Omega^{\prime})
and

\|T\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega)\to L_{p,w}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,\leq\,M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}

where
\theta\in(0,1)
satisfies
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
.

The next proposition proved by Chiarenza and Frasca in
[
9
]
is the well-known extension of the result of C. Fefferman
[
13
]
for
p=2
.
This theorem is one of basic tools in our proofs of both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
p\in(1,n)
,
r\in(1,\frac{n}{p}]
and
V\in L^{r,n-pr}(\Omega)
. Then

\int\limits_{\Omega}|V|\,|u|^{p}\,dx\ \leq\ c_{n,r,p}\,\|V\|_{L^{r,n-rp}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

with the constant
c_{n,r,p}>0
depending only on
n
,
r
and

.

3
Higher integrability of weak solutions

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First we show that in the case of (
1.8
) any
p
–weak solution corresponding to a sufficiently regular right-hand side is bounded, see the related result in
[
34
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
p\geq 2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
,
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
satisfies
(
1.8
),
q>n
and
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
. Then any weak
solution
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
to
the problem (
1.1
) (in the sense of the integral identity (
1.7
)) is essentially bounded and satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(3.1)

with a
constant
c>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
n
and
q
.

Proof.

For
m>0
we define a truncation
T_{m}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
T_{m}(s):=m
for
s\geq m
,
T_{m}(s)=s
for
s<m
.
For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
we also denote
(s)_{+}:=\max\{s,0\}
.
Now we fix some
m>0
and denote
\bar{u}:=T_{m}(u)
.
Then for any
k\geq 0
we have

(\bar{u}-k)_{+}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega),\qquad\nabla(\bar{u}-k)_{+}=\chi_{\Omega[k<u<m]}\nabla u

where
\Omega[k<u<m]=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,k<u(x)<m\,\}
. Approximating
\eta:=(\bar{u}-k)_{+}
by smooth functions we can take
\eta
as a test function in (
1.7
). For
k\geq m
we have
\eta\equiv 0
and hence
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]=0
. For
k<m
the condition (
1.8
) implies

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ =\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\int\limits_{\Omega[k<u<m]}b\cdot\nabla|\eta|^{2}\,dx\ +\ (m-k)\,\int\limits_{\Omega[u\geq m]}b\cdot\nabla u\,dx\\
=\mathcal{B}[\eta,\eta]\ +\ (m-k)\,\int\limits_{\Omega}b\cdot\nabla(u-m)_{+}\,dx\ \geq\ \mathcal{B}[\eta,\eta]\ \geq\ 0\end{gathered}

Hence for
A_{k}:=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,\bar{u}(x)>k\,\}
from (
1.7
) we obtain

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla(\bar{u}-k)_{+}|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ \|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{2}|A_{k}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}},\qquad\forall\,k\geq 0,

which implies (see
[
33
, Chapter II, Lemma 5.3]
)

\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{\Omega}\bar{u}\ \leq\ c\,|\Omega|^{\delta}\,\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},\qquad\delta:=\tfrac{1}{n}-\tfrac{1}{q},

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
and
q
.
As this estimate is uniform with respect to
m>0
we conclude
u
is essentially bounded from above and

\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{\Omega}u\ \leq\ c\,|\Omega|^{\delta}\,\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}.

Applying the same procedure to
\bar{u}:=T_{m}(-u)
instead of
\bar{u}:=T_{m}(u)
we obtain
u\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
as well as (
3.1
).
∎

Proposition
3.1
implies the uniqueness theorem for the problem (
1.1
) in the class of
p
-weak solutions.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
p\geq 2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
satisfies
(
1.8
). Then for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)

the problem (
1.1
) can have at most one weak solution in the class
W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Assume
u_{1}\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
and
u_{2}\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
are two weak solutions of (
1.1
) corresponding to the same
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
. Then
u:=u_{1}-u_{2}
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) corresponding to
f\equiv 0
. From Proposition
3.1
we obtain
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
.
∎

The next proposition is the energy estimate. Note that the constant
c>0
in this estimate does not depend on the drift
b
satisfying the condition (
1.8
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
p\geq 2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
,
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
satisfies
(
1.8
), and
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
q>n
. Then for any weak
solution
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
to
the problem (
1.1
) the following estimate hold:

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
we obtain
u\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
and hence the test function
\eta:=u\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)
is admissible for (
1.7
). From Proposition
2.2
we obtain
\mathcal{B}[u,u]\geq 0
and hence from (

\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \leq\ \int\limits_{\Omega}f\cdot\nabla u\,dx

∎

Our next result is the estimate of the bilinear form corresponding to the drift term. This result is a direct consequence of the Feffermann–Chiarenza–Frasca estimate, see Proposition
2.4
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
r\in\left(\frac{2n}{n+2},2\right)
and
b\in L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
c>0
depending only on
n
and
r
such that for any
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
and
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the following estimate holds:

\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta^{3}\,|b|\,|u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta^{2}u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)}.

(3.2)

Moreover, for any
\theta\in\left(\frac{n}{r}-\frac{n}{2},1\right)
there exists
c>0
depending only on
n
,
r
and
\theta
such that for any
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
and any
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
we have

\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta^{1+\theta}\,|b|\,|u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\theta}\,|\Omega|^{\theta/n}.

(3.3)

If we additionally assume
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)
then the estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) remains true for an arbitrary
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

To prove (
3.2
) we apply the Hölder inequality

\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta^{3}\,|b|\,|u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,\|\zeta^{2}u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}\|\zeta bu\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)}.

Denote
q:=\frac{2n}{n+2}
,
V:=|b|^{q}
,
r_{1}:=\frac{r}{q}
,
r_{1}>1
. Then

V\in L^{r_{1},n-qr_{1}}(\Omega),\qquad\|V\|_{L^{r_{1},n-qr_{1}}(\Omega)}\,=\,\|b\|^{q}_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}

and from Proposition
2.4
we obtain

\|\zeta bu\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c\,\|V\|_{L^{r_{1},n-qr_{1}}(\Omega)}^{1/q}\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality we obtain

\|\zeta^{2}u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,c\,\|\nabla(\zeta^{2}u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

and we arrive at (
3.2
).

Now we turn to the proof of (
3.3
). Take
q\in\left(\frac{2n}{n+2},r\right)
such that

\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\tfrac{1-\theta}{2}+\theta\,\tfrac{n-2}{2n},\qquad q^{\prime}=\tfrac{q}{q-1}.

Then

\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta^{1+\theta}\,|b|\,|u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,\|\zeta^{\theta}u\|_{L_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\zeta bu\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}.

Denote
V:=|b|^{q}
,
r_{1}:=\frac{r}{q}
,
r_{1}>1
. Then
V\in L^{r_{1},n-qr_{1}}(\Omega)
and

\|\zeta bu\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c\,\|V\|_{L^{r_{1},n-qr_{1}}(\Omega)}^{1/q}\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}

On the other hand, we can interpolate

\begin{gathered}\|\zeta^{\theta}u\|_{L_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\,=\|(\zeta|u|)^{\theta}|u|^{1-\theta}\|_{L_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\,\leq\\
\leq\,\|(\zeta|u|)^{\theta}\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\theta}}(\Omega)}\||u|^{1-\theta}\|_{L_{\frac{2}{1-\theta}}(\Omega)}\leq\,c\,\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\end{gathered}

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
.
So, we obtain

\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta^{1+\theta}\,|b|\,|u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ c\,\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}.

Using the Hölder inequality we obtain

\|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\ =\ \|\nabla(\zeta u)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{\theta}{n}}

which completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition is the higher integrability estimate which is the main result of the present section. In this proposition we assume that
b
is smooth and we derive (
1.10
) as an a priori estimate for a sufficiently regular drift.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume
b\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.8
) and assume
f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
q>n
. Let

u\in\overset{\circ}{W^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.1
) in terms of distributions (i.e. the identity (
1.7
) holds).
Then there exists
p>2
depending only on
n
,
q
,
\Omega
and
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
such
that
u\in\overset{\circ}{W^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)
and
the estimate (
1.10
) holds
with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
q
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us fix some

r\in(\frac{2n}{n+2},2)
.
First we consider an internal point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Take

R>0
such that
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and choose a cut-off
function
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))
so that
\zeta\equiv 1

on
B_{R}(x_{0})
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leq c/R
.
Denote
\bar{u}=u-(u)_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}
. Then the function
\eta=\zeta^{4}\bar{u}
is admissible for (
1.7
). It is easy to see that

\mathcal{B}[u,\zeta^{4}\bar{u}]\ =\ \mathcal{B}[\zeta^{2}\bar{u},\zeta^{2}\bar{u}]\ -\ 2\,\int\limits_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\zeta^{3}|\bar{u}|^{2}\,b\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx.

Taking into account (
2.1
) from (
1.7
) we obtain

\|\zeta^{2}\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\ \leq\ c\,\Big(\|\bar{u}\nabla\zeta\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+\|f\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\Big)\ +\ c\,\int\limits_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\zeta^{3}|\bar{u}|^{2}\,b\cdot\nabla\zeta~dx.

We estimate the last term with the help of (
3.2
):

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\zeta^{3}|\bar{u}|^{2}\,b\cdot\nabla\zeta~dx\ \leq\\
\leq\ \|\nabla\zeta\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla(\zeta^{2}\bar{u})\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\,\|\nabla(\zeta\bar{u})\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}.\end{gathered}

Using the Young inequality we get

\begin{gathered}\|\zeta^{2}\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\ \leq\ c\,\Big(\|\bar{u}\nabla\zeta\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+\|f\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\Big)\ +\\
+\ c\,\|\nabla\zeta\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\|\nabla(\zeta\bar{u})\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}.\end{gathered}

(3.4)

Taking into account
\zeta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
and
\|\nabla\zeta\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{c}{R}
and the inequalities

\|\bar{u}\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\,\leq\,c\,\|\nabla u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))},

(3.5)

\|\nabla(\zeta\bar{u})\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\,\leq\,c\,\|\nabla u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))},

we arrive at

\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\ \leq\ c\,\|f\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\ +\ \frac{c}{R}\,\Big(1+\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\|\nabla u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}.

Here
c
is a global constant that depends only on
r
. Next we derive similar estimates near the boundary.
Extend
u
and
f
by zero outside
\Omega
and denote this extension
by
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{f}
. Note that as
\partial\Omega
is
Lipschitz there exist
R_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
depending on
\Omega
such that

\forall\,R<R_{0},\qquad\forall\,x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\qquad|B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|\,\geq\,\delta_{0}|B_{R}|.

(3.6)

Take
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

R<R_{0}/2
and denote
\Omega_{R}(x_{0}):=B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
. Choose a cut-off function
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))
so that
\zeta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then the function

\eta\ :=\ \zeta^{4}u\ \in\ L_{\infty}(\Omega_{2R}(x_{0}))\cap\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega_{2R}(x_{0}))

is admissible for the identity
(
1.7
).
Proceeding similar to the internal case we arrive at the inequality (
3.4
) with
\bar{u}
replaces by
\tilde{u}
. Then instead of (
3.5
) we use the inequality

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L_{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\,\leq\,c\,\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))},

which holds with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
and
\delta_{0}
in (
3.6
) (and independent on
x_{0}
and
R
) as
\tilde{u}
satisfies
|\{\,x\in B_{2R}(x_{0}):\,\tilde{u}(x)=0\,\}|\geq\delta_{0}|B_{2R}|
. Then
after
routine computations we obtain for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
R<R_{0}

\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\ \leq\ c\,\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{2R}(x_{0}))}\ +\ \frac{c}{R}\,\Big(1+\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\|\nabla u\|_{L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega_{2R}(x_{0}))}.

Combining internal and boundary estimates in the standard way and dividing the result by
R^{n/2}
for any
x_{0}\in\bar{\Omega}
and
R<R_{0}
we obtain the reverse Hölder inequality for
\nabla u
:

\begin{gathered}\left(\ \ \ \ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}~dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \leq\ c_{1}\,\left(\ \ \ \ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\Omega_{2R}(x_{0})}|f|^{2}~dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ +\\
+\ c_{2}\,\Big(1+\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\Big)\left(\ \ \ \ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\Omega_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}~dx\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}.\end{gathered}

From Proposition
2.1
we conclude

\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,c\,\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}.

Now the higher integrability of
u
follows via Gehring’s lemma, see, for example,
[
19
, Chapter V]
. Namely,
there exists
p>2
and
c_{*}>0
depending only on
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
such that
\nabla u\in L_{p}(\Omega)
and the
following estimate holds:

\|\nabla u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c_{*}~(\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}).

Combining this estimate with Proposition
3.3
we obtain (
1.10
).
∎

Finally we can prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

We follow the method similar to
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
, and
[
43
]
,
[
44
]
. Let us fix some
q>n
. Let
p>2
be the exponent defined in Proposition
3.5
and denote
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.
As
\Omega
is Lipschitz we can find the sequence of
C^{2}
-smooth domains

\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\Omega_{k+1}\Subset\Omega_{k},\qquad\bigcup\limits_{k}\Omega_{k}=\Omega.

Moreover, it is possible to construct domains
\Omega_{k}
so that the Lipschitz constants of
\partial\Omega_{k}
are controlled uniformly by the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
. In particular, we can assume there are exist positive constants
\hat{\delta}_{0}
and
\hat{R}_{0}
independent on
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\forall\,R<\hat{R}_{0},\qquad\forall\,x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{k}\qquad|B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega_{k}|\,\geq\,\hat{\delta}_{0}\,|B_{R}|.

(3.7)

For a canonical domain given as a subgraph of a Lipschitz function existence of such approximation can be obtained by mollification and shift of the graph, and for general bounded Lipschitz domain the standard localization works.

Now we take a sequence of positive numbers
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that
\varepsilon_{k}<\mathop{\rm dist}\{\bar{\Omega}_{k},\partial\Omega\}
, and define the mollification of the drift
b
:

b_{k}(x)\,:=\,\int\limits_{\Omega}\omega_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x-y)b(y)\,dy,\qquad x\in\Omega,

where
\omega_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-n}\omega(x/\varepsilon)
and
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the standard Sobolev kernel, i.e.

\omega\,\geq\,0,\qquad\mathop{\rm supp}\omega\in\bar{B},\qquad\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\omega(x)\,dx\,=\,1,\qquad\omega(x)=\omega_{0}(|x|).

(3.8)

Then
b_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
and as
\varepsilon_{k}<\mathop{\rm dist}\{\bar{\Omega}_{k},\partial\Omega\}
from (
1.8
) for any
k
we obtain

\mathop{\rm div}b_{k}\,\leq\,0\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{k}.

(3.9)

Moreover, there is a constant
c>0
independent on
k
such that

\|b_{k}\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega_{k})}\,\leq\,c\,\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)},\qquad\|b_{k}-b\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad k\to 0.

(3.10)

For the reader’s convenience
we prove the inequality (
3.10
) in Appendix.

For
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
we can find
f_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
such that
\|f_{k}-f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\to 0
.
Let
u_{k}\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)
be smooth solutions to the regularized problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\Delta u_{k}+b_{k}\cdot\nabla u_{k}\ =\ -\mathop{\rm div}f_{k}\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{k},\\
u_{k}|_{\partial\Omega_{k}}\ =\ 0,\end{gathered}\right.

(3.11)

and extend functions
u_{k}
by zero from
\Omega_{k}
onto
\Omega
. As the drift
b_{k}
of in (
3.11
) satisfies the properties (
3.9
) and (
3.10
)
from Proposition
3.5
we obtain the estimate

\|u_{k}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c\,\|f_{k}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
q
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the constant
\hat{\delta}_{0}
in (
3.7
) which is independent on
k
. Hence we can take a subsequence
u_{k}
such that

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u\quad\mbox{in}\quad W^{1}_{p}(\Omega).

Take any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, due to our construction of the sequence
\Omega_{k}
we can find
N
such that for all
k\geq N,~\mathop{\rm supp}\eta\subset\Omega_{k}
, therefore
\eta
is a suitable test function in (
3.11
). As
b_{k}\to b
in
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
we can pass to the limit in the identity

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\Big(\nabla\eta+b_{k}\eta\Big)\,dx\ =\ \int\limits_{\Omega}f_{k}\cdot\nabla\eta~dx,\end{gathered}

and obtain (
1.7
). Hence
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)
is a
p
-weak solution to the problem (
1.1
). Its uniqueness follows from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4
Modified De Giorgi classes

In this section we introduce the modified De Giorgi classes which are convenient for the study of solutions to the elliptic equations with coefficients from Morrey spaces. The only difference of our classes from the standard De Giorgi classes (see, for example
[
20
, §8.3]
,
[
33
, §II.6]
) is the additional multiplier
1+\frac{R^{\alpha}}{(R-\rho)^{\alpha}}
in the inequalities (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

Definition 4.1
.

For
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
we define
A_{k}:=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,u(x)>k\,\}
. We say
u\in DG(\Omega,k_{0})
if there exist constants
\gamma>0
,
F>0
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
q>n
such that for any
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, any
0<\rho<R
and any
k\geq k_{0}
the following inequality holds

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ \frac{\gamma^{2}}{(R-\rho)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{R^{\alpha}}{(R-\rho)^{\alpha}}\right)\,\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-k|^{2}\,dx\ +\\
+\ F^{2}\,|A_{k}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.\end{gathered}

(4.1)

We write
u\in DG(\Omega)
if
u\in DG(\Omega,k_{0})
for any
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. To avoid overloaded notation, when we need to specify constants in Definition
4.1
we allow some terminological license and say that the class
D(\Omega,k_{0})
corresponds to the constants
\gamma
,
F
,
\alpha
,
q
instead of including these constants in the notation of the functional class.

The second modified De Giorgi class characterizes the behavior of Sobolev functions near the boundary of the domain:

Definition 4.2
.

We say
u\in DG(\partial\Omega)
if
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)
and there exist constants
\gamma>0
,
F>0
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
q>n
,
R_{0}>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, any
0<\rho<R\leq R_{0}
and any
k\geq 0
the following inequality holds

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ \frac{\gamma^{2}}{(R-\rho)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{R^{\alpha}}{(R-\rho)^{\alpha}}\right)\,\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}|u-k|^{2}\,dx\ +\\
+\ F^{2}\,|A_{k}\cap\Omega_{R}(x_{0})|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\end{gathered}

(4.2)

where we denote
\Omega_{R}(x_{0}):=\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})
.

We emphasize that while
u\in DG(\Omega)
implies that (
4.1
) holds for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
the inclusion
u\in DG(\partial\Omega)
implies that (
4.2
) holds only for non-negative
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Such choice of the boundary De Giorgi class is motivated by the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions in (
1.1
).

The main result of this section is the following theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and denote by
DG(\Omega)
and
DG(\partial\Omega)
the modified De Giorgi classes corresponding to some positive constants
F
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
and
q>n
.

(i)

Assume
\pm u\in DG(\Omega)
. Then there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
q
,
\gamma
such that
u\in C^{\mu}_{loc}(\Omega)
and for any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega

\|u\|_{C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})}\ \leq\ c(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\,\Big(\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+F\Big)

with some constant
c(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
\Omega^{\prime}
,
n
,
q
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
.

(ii)

Assume
\pm u\in DG(\Omega)\cap DG(\partial\Omega)
. Then there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
q
,
\gamma
such that
u\in C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})
and

\|u\|_{C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})}\ \leq\ c(\Omega)\,\Big(\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+F\Big)

with some constant
c(\Omega)>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
R_{0}
,
n
,
q
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
.

Our modified De Giorgi classes in Definitions
4.1
and
4.2
are very similar to ones introduced earlier in
[
33
]
.
Our proof of Theorem
4.1
is standard and follows to the general line in
[
33
]
. But due to the minor difference in the proof and for the reader convenience we present the proof of Theorem
4.1
in this section.

First we present some technical lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\{Y_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}
are nonnegative and there exist
\varepsilon>0
,
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
,

b\geq 1
and
c_{0}\geq 1
such that the following inequality holds

Y_{m+1}\ \leq\ c_{0}~b^{m}~Y_{m}^{1+\varepsilon}(1+Y_{m}^{\varepsilon_{1}}),\qquad\forall~m\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.3)

Assume
Y_{0}\leq\theta
where

\theta\ :=\ (2c_{0})^{-1/\varepsilon}~b^{-1/\varepsilon^{2}}
. Then

Y_{m}\ \leq\ \theta~b^{-m/\varepsilon},\qquad\forall~m\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

We use the induction with respect to
m
. For
m=0
the statement holds by assumption. Now assuming
Y_{m}\ \leq\ \theta~b^{-m/\varepsilon}
from (
4.3
) we estimate

\begin{gathered}Y_{m+1}\ \leq\ c_{0}~b^{m}~Y_{m}^{1+\varepsilon}(1+Y_{m}^{\varepsilon_{1}})\ \leq\ c_{0}~b^{m}~(\theta~b^{-m/\varepsilon})^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(1+(\theta~b^{-m/\varepsilon})^{\varepsilon_{1}}\Big)\ \leq\\
\leq\ 2\,c_{0}\,\theta^{1+\varepsilon}\,b^{-\frac{m}{\varepsilon}}\ =\ 2\,c_{0}\,\theta^{1+\varepsilon}\,b^{1/\varepsilon}\,b^{-\frac{m+1}{\varepsilon}}\end{gathered}

To finish the proof we note that
\theta=(2c_{0})^{-1/\varepsilon}~b^{-1/\varepsilon^{2}}
implies
2c_{0}\,\theta^{1+\varepsilon}\,b^{1/\varepsilon}=\theta
. ∎

Now we prove the local maximum estimate:

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
u\in DG(B_{R},k_{0})
. Then
u
is essentially bounded from above in
B_{R/2}
and

\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}}~(u-k_{0})_{+}\ \leq\ c_{*}\,\left[\Big(\ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{B_{R}}|(u-k_{0})_{+}|^{2}~dx\Big)^{1/2}+F\,R^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\right]

(4.4)

where
(u-k_{0})_{+}:=\max\{u-k_{0},0\}
and
c_{*}>0
depends only on
n
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
R=1
and
k_{0}=0
.
Denote

H\ :=\ c_{*}\left[\Big(\ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{B}u_{+}^{2}~dx\ \Big)^{1/2}+F~\right],

where the precise value of the constant
c_{*}=c_{*}(n,\gamma,\alpha)>0
will be fixed later.
Define

\begin{gathered}\rho_{m}:=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2^{m+1}},\quad k_{m}:=H-\tfrac{H}{2^{m}},\quad J_{m}:=\int\limits_{B_{\rho_{m}}}(u-k_{m})^{2}_{+}~dx,\\
\mu_{m}\,:=\,|\{\,x\in B_{\rho_{m}}:\,u(x)>k_{m+1}\,\}|.\end{gathered}

From

J_{m}\geq(k_{m+1}-k_{m})^{2}\mu_{m}

we obtain

\mu_{m}\,\leq\,\frac{2^{2m+2}}{H^{2}}~J_{m}.

(4.5)

Define
\rho_{m}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{m+1}+\rho_{m})
and define cut-off functions

\zeta_{m}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{\rho_{m}^{\prime}}),\qquad\zeta_{m}\equiv 1\quad\mbox{on}\quad B_{\rho_{m+1}},\qquad|\nabla\zeta_{m}|\leq\frac{c}{\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1}}.

From the Hölder inequality and the imbedding
W^{1}_{2}(B_{\rho_{m}^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow L_{\frac{2n}{n-2}(B_{\rho_{m}^{\prime}})}
we obtain

J_{m+1}\ \leq\ c(n)~\mu_{m}^{\frac{2}{n}}~\Big(~\|\nabla(u-k_{m+1})_{+}\|_{L_{2}(B_{\rho_{m}^{\prime}})}^{2}\ +\ \frac{c(n)}{(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1})^{2}}~J_{m}~\Big).

(4.6)

Using
u\in DG(B)
we estimate

\|\nabla(u-k_{m+1})_{+}\|_{L_{2}(B_{\rho_{m}}^{\prime})}^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{\gamma^{2}}{(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1})^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\rho_{m}^{\alpha}}{(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1})^{\alpha}}\right)\,J_{m}\ +\ F^{2}\mu_{m}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

(4.7)

and hence

J_{m+1}\ \leq\ \frac{c(n,\gamma)}{(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1})^{2}}~\mu_{m}^{\frac{2}{n}}~\left(1+\frac{\rho_{m}^{\alpha}}{(\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1})^{\alpha}}\right)~J_{m}\ +\ F^{2}\mu_{m}^{1+\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}}.

Denote

Y_{m}\,:=\,\frac{J_{m}}{H^{2}}.

Taking into account
\rho_{m}-\rho_{m+1}=\frac{1}{2^{m+2}}
,
\rho_{m}\leq 1
from (
4.5
), (
4.6
), (
4.7
)
we obtain

Y_{m+1}\ \leq\ c\,2^{2m(1+\frac{2}{n})}\left(1+2^{\alpha m}\right)\,Y_{m}^{1+\frac{2}{n}}\ +\ c\,\tfrac{F^{2}}{H^{2}}\,2^{m\left(1+\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}\right)}Y_{m}^{1+\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}}.

Using and
\frac{F}{H}\leq 1
we arrive at (
4.3
)

Y_{m+1}\ \leq\ c_{0}~b^{m}~Y_{m}^{1+\varepsilon}(1+Y_{m}^{\varepsilon_{1}}),

(4.8)

where
b:=2^{2+\alpha+\frac{4}{n}}
,
\varepsilon=\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}
,
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{2}{q}
and
c_{0}
depends only on
n
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
. So, the sequence
\{Y_{m}\}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
.
Now fix
c_{*}>\theta^{-1/2}
where
\theta
is defined in Lemma
4.1
.
Then
Y_{0}<\theta
holds and hence by Lemma
4.1
we obtain
Y_{m}\to 0

which gives (
4.4
).
∎

Now we prove so-called “Density lemma” (or “Thin set lemma”):

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
u\in DG(\Omega,k_{0})
. Then there exists

\theta_{0}\in(0,1)
depending only on the constants
n
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
q
in Definition
4.1
of De Giorgi class such that for any

if

|B_{2\rho}\cap A_{k_{0}}|\,\leq\,\theta_{0}\,|B_{2\rho}|

then either

\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}}(u-k_{0})_{+}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{2}~\sup\limits_{B_{4\rho}}(u-k_{0})_{+}

(4.9)

or

\sup\limits_{B_{4\rho}}(u-k_{0})_{+}\ \leq\ 4c_{*}\,F\,(2\rho)^{1-\frac{n}{q}}

(4.10)

where
c_{*}>0
is a constant from (
4.4
).

Proof.

Take

\theta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

c_{*}~\theta_{0}^{1/2}=\frac{1}{4}
.
Assume (
4.10
) does not hold.
Then from (
4.4
) we obtain
(
4.9
).
∎

Now we prove a result called “How to find a thin set”:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume

u\in DG(\Omega,k_{0})
. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and any
\theta\in(0,1)
there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
\theta
,
\delta
and the constants
n
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
q
in Definition
4.1
of De Giorgi class such that if for some
B_{4\rho}:=B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
the following estimate is valid

|B_{2\rho}\setminus A_{k_{0}}|\ \geq\ \delta~|B_{2\rho}|

then either

|B_{2\rho}\cap A_{\bar{k}}|\ \leq\ \theta~|B_{2\rho}|,

(4.11)

or

M(4\rho)-k_{0}\,\leq\,2^{s}\,F\,\rho^{1-\frac{n}{q}}.

(4.12)

Here we denote
\bar{k}=M(4\rho)-\frac{1}{2^{s}}(M(4\rho)-k_{0})
and
M(4\rho)\,:=\,\sup\limits_{B_{4\rho}}u
.

Proof.

Denote
M:=M(4\rho)
and assume
s\in\mathbb{N}
is some number whose value will be fixed later. From
u\in DG(B_{4\rho})
for any
k_{0}\leq k\leq\bar{k}
we obtain

\int\limits_{B_{2\rho}\cap A_{k}}~|\nabla u|^{2}~dxdt\ \leq\ c(n,\gamma,\alpha)~\rho^{n-2}~\left((M-k)^{2}+F^{2}\rho^{2(1-\frac{n}{q})}\right).

(4.13)

Assume (
4.12
) is not valid. Then for any
k_{0}\leq k\leq\bar{k}

F\,\rho^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{2^{s}}\,(M-k_{0})\,=\,M-\bar{k}\,\leq\,M-k

and from (
4.13
) we obtain

\int\limits_{B_{2\rho}\cap A_{k}}~|\nabla u|^{2}~dxdt\ \leq\ 2\,c(n,\gamma,\alpha)~\rho^{n-2}~(M-k)^{2}.

(4.14)

Define

k_{m}:=M-\frac{1}{2^{m}}(M-k_{0})
and denote

D_{m}:=\{\ x\in B_{2\rho}~|~k_{m}\leq u(x)<k_{m+1}\ \}.

For simplicity in the rest of the proof of this lemma we will abuse the notation of
A_{m}
in Definition
4.1
by denoting

A_{m}:=\{\ x\in B_{2\rho}~|~u(x)>k_{m}\ \}.

From the De Giorgi inequality (see
[
33

) we derive

(k_{m+1}-k_{m})|A_{m+1}|\ \leq\ C(n)\frac{|\,B_{2\rho}\,|^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}{|\,B_{2\rho}\setminus A_{m}\,|}\ \int\limits_{D_{m}}|\nabla u(x)|~dx.

Note that

|\,B_{2\rho}\setminus A_{m}\,|\ \geq\ \delta~|B_{2\rho}|

and hence

(k_{m+1}-k_{m})~|A_{m+1}|\ \leq\ \frac{C(n)}{\delta}~\rho\ \int\limits_{D_{m}}|\nabla u|~dx.

Using the Hölder inequality and (
4.14
) we obtain

(k_{m+1}-k_{m})|A_{m+1}|\ \leq\ \frac{C(n,\gamma,\alpha)}{\delta}~\rho^{\frac{n}{2}}\ |\,D_{m}\,|^{1/2}(M-k_{m}).

Note that

k_{m+1}-k_{m}=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}(M-k_{0}),\qquad M-k_{m}=\frac{1}{2^{m}}(M-k_{0}).

Hence

|A_{m+1}|^{2}\ \leq\ C(n,\gamma,\alpha,\delta)~\rho^{n}\ |\,D_{m}\,|,\qquad m=1,2,3,\ldots

Taking a summation over
m
from 1
to
s-1
we gat

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{s-1}|A_{m+1}|^{2}\ \leq\ C(n,\gamma,\alpha,\delta)~\rho^{n}\ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{s-1}|\,D_{m}\,|.

Note that

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{s-1}|\,D_{m}\,|\ =\ |A_{1}\setminus A_{2}|+\ldots+|A_{s-1}\setminus A_{s}|\ =\ |A_{1}\setminus A_{s}|\ \leq\ |B_{2\rho}|,

So, for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain

|A_{s}|\ \leq\ \frac{C(n,\gamma,\alpha,\delta)}{\sqrt{s-1}}~\rho^{\frac{n}{2}}~|B_{2\rho}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be arbitrary. Find

s\in\mathbb{N}
,

, so that

\frac{C(n,\gamma,\alpha,\delta)}{\sqrt{s-1}}~\rho^{\frac{n}{2}}~|B_{2\rho}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \leq\ \theta~|B_{2\rho}|.

Then

|A_{s}|\ \leq\ \theta~|B_{2\rho}|,

which implies (
4.11

∎

Our next result is a local estimate of oscillations:

Lemma 4.5

Assume
\pm u\in DG(\Omega)

(4.15)

Take any
B_{4\rho}:=B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
and denote

m(\rho)=\inf\limits_{B_{\rho}}u,\qquad M(\rho)=\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}}u,\qquad k_{0}:=\frac{m(4\rho)+M(4\rho)}{2}.

Let
\theta_{0}\in(0,1)
be the constant from Lemma
4.3
and denote by

the constant from
Lemma
4.4
which corresponds to
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
,
\theta=\theta_{0}
and the constants

,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
q
in Definition
4.1
of the De Giorgi class. Let
c_{*}>0
be the constant from (
4.4
) and take

c_{1}\ =\ \max\{16c_{*},2^{s+1}\}

(4.16)

and assume

\mathop{\rm osc}\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\ >\ c_{1}\,F\,\rho^{1-\frac{n}{q}}.

does not hold.
Then both (

Consider two cases:

\begin{gathered}\mbox{(1)}\qquad\big|~\{\ u\leq k_{0}\ \}\cap B_{2\rho}~\big|\ \geq\ \tfrac{1}{2}~|B_{2\rho}|,\\
\mbox{(2)}\qquad\big|~\{\ u\geq k_{0}\ \}\cap B_{2\rho}~\big|\ \geq\ \tfrac{1}{2}~|B_{2\rho}|.\end{gathered}

Case (1). Take

\bar{k}:=M(4\rho)-\frac{1}{2^{s}}(M(4\rho)-k_{0}).

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume

3.8

Then

Take arbitrary

B_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\Omega
for any

.
First we consider the case
R\in(0,\varepsilon)

From the definition of the convolution we have a pointwise estimate

\Omega

\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{2}(B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\,\leq\,c\,\left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\,\|b\|_{L^{2,n-2}_{w}(\Omega)}^{2}\,R^{n-2}.

2.3

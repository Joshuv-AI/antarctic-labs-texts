Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be either a Noetherian local ring or a standard graded Noetherian
K
-algebra with unique graded maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Hereafter, we will always assume that
K
is an infinite field and that
\textup{depth}R>0
. These assumptions will be explained in a moment. Let
I\subset R
be an ideal, we assume that
I
is homogeneous if
R
is a
K
-algebra. The ideal
I
is called

(a)

\mathfrak{m}
-full
if
(I\mathfrak{m}:x)=I
for a generic element
x\in\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{m}^{2}
,

(b)

full
if
(I:x)=(I:\mathfrak{m})
for a generic element
x\in\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{m}^{2}
,

(c)

weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full
if
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})=I
.

Here, that
x\in\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is
generic
means that
x
belongs to some non-empty open set of the Zariski topology of
R
.

Let
P
be one of the properties
\{\mathfrak{m}\textit{-full,\ full,\ weakly}\ \mathfrak{m}\textit{-full}\}
and let
I,J\subset R
be two ideals. In
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, Hailong Dao proved that
J
has the property
P
if and only if
J+I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
has the property
P
for all
k\gg 0
(the result is stated in the local case, but the proof carries over in the graded case). In particular, there exists an ideal
I\subset R
for which
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is (weakly)
\mathfrak{m}
-full for all
k\gg 0
if and only if
\textup{depth}R>0

[
10
, Corollary 3.2]
. Thus, under our assumptions, for all
k\gg 0
,
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
has any of the properties
P
considered above. Based on this fact, Dao introduced the following numerical invariants
[
10
, Definition 3.1]
, which we call the
Dao numbers
of
I
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\ell\geq 0\ :\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ \textup{is}\ \mathfrak{m}\textup{-full for all}\ k\geq\ell\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\ell\geq 0\ :\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ \textup{is}\ \textup{full for all}\ k\geq\ell\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\ell\geq 0\ :\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ \textup{is weakly}\ \mathfrak{m}\textup{-full for all}\ k\geq\ell\}.

It is shown in
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
that

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\ \leq\ \mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)\ =\ \mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)

if
K
is infinite and
\textup{depth}R>0
. This fact justifies these recurring assumptions we imposed in the beginning. In
[
10
]
, Dao raised the problem of finding good bounds for the Dao numbers. For reduction ideals of the maximal ideal in a local ring, this question has been answered by Miranda-Neto and Queiroz
[
23
]
.

The concept of
\mathfrak{m}
-fullness was first introduced by David Rees in unpublished work, and later developed by Junzo Watanabe
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
. Recently, fullness of ideals was considered by many reaserchers
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
25
]
. Surprisingly, these concepts are also related to the classical Zariski-Lipman conjecture (about derivations and smoothness) in the open case of surfaces
[
22
, Section 4]
.

It is straightforward to see that a
\mathfrak{m}
-full ideal is weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full. A
reduction
of
\mathfrak{m}
is an ideal
I
such that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}=\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
for some
k
. If
R
is regular (with infinite residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
) and
I
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, then
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-full
[
23
, Corollary 3.11]
. If
R/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite, then integrally closed ideals are
\mathfrak{m}
-full
[
15
, Theorem (2.4)]
. If
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring with coefficients over an infinite field
K
, then any componentwise linear ideal is
\mathfrak{m}
-full
[
8
]
(see also
[
16
, Proposition 18]
). We extend this result to any Koszul algebra (Corollary
2.3
). In
[
11
]

Burch ideals
are introduced. An ideal
I
is called
Burch
if
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})\neq(I:\mathfrak{m})
. If
\textup{depth}R/I=0
and
I
is weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full, then
I
is Burch
[
11
, Corollary 2.4]
.

In the present paper, we determine general bounds for the Dao numbers in both the local and graded settings. Moreover, we will consider Koszul algebras and monomial ideals, and provide more specific bounds for the Dao numbers in such cases.

The paper proceeds as follows. In Section
1
we address Dao’s question and we bound the Dao numbers of any ideal
I
. For this aim, we introduce the
Dao module

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
of
I
which is defined as
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})/(I\mathfrak{m}^{k})
and has the structure of a module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{k}
of
\mathfrak{m}
. The
k
th component of this module is zero if and only if
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full. Since
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{k}=0
for all
k\geq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)
, it follows that
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
has finite length, and thus is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-module. In Corollary
1.2
we note that if
I
is not weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full, then
\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)+1
. In our main Theorem
1.1
we prove that:

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I),\,\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}\}.

Here
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is the extension of the ideal
I
in the ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
, and
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
. Under the more restrictive assumption that
\textup{depth}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)>0
(here
\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k}/I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})
is the associated graded module of
I
), or when
R
is regular (Theorems
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
) then we even have

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I).

This bounds holds because
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
is equal to
(0:_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{+})
under the above assumptions (Corollary
1.5
). Due to these results, we could expect that:

Conjecture 0.1
.

For all ideals
I\subset R
:
\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)
.

In Corollary
1.7
we reobtain the nice result
[
23
, Corollary 3.11]
, due to Miranda Neto and Queiroz, which says that
\mathfrak{d}_{i}(I)=0
for all
i
if
I
is a reduction of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
R
is regular.

In Section
2
, we consider the more tame situation of a Koszul algebra. A standard graded
K
-algebra
R
is
Koszul
if
\textup{reg}_{R}K=0
. A homogeneous ideal
I
of a Koszul algebra
R
is called
componentwise linear
if
I_{\langle j\rangle}
has a linear resolution for all
j
. It turns out that componentwise linear ideals are full,
\mathfrak{m}
-full and weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full (Corollary
2.3
). In the polynomial ring case, componentwise linear ideals coincide with the so-called
completely
\mathfrak{m}
-full ideals

[
17
]
. So the above result is not surprising. To the best of our knowledge, the concept of completely
\mathfrak{m}
-full ideal has not been defined yet in a Koszul algebra. In Theorem
2.5
, we notice that for all
k\geq\textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
,
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is componentwise linear. Hence, the number
\textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
is an upper bound for the Dao numbers of
I
. If
R
is a strongly Koszul algebra, any monomial ideal
I\subset R
with linear quotients order
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\dots<u_{m}
with
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
is componentwise linear (Proposition
2.8
). If
R
is the polynomial ring
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, then by the Björner-Wachs rearrangement lemma
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
4
]
) the condition
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
can be omitted. It is not clear whether this is also the case for any strongly Koszul algebra.

In the final Section
3
, we consider monomial ideals in the standard graded polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)
. It was noted in
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
(Proposition
3.2
) that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\gg 0
, which strengthens the fact that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is componentwise linear for all
k\gg 0
. Analyzing carefully the proof of this fact, which is based on combinatorial arguments, we obtain in Theorem
3.3
the combinatorial bound

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\ \leq\ \min_{\mathcal{O}}(\max_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u}).

Here the minimum runs through all orders
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
. In Corollary
3.4
we prove that
\textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\leq(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u))+1-\mu(I)-\omega(I)
. This bound is sharp. Indeed, equality holds for monomial complete intersections
[
1
, Theorem 4.1(b)]
. Next, in Theorem
3.6
, we show that
\mathfrak{d}_{i}(I)\leq\max\{|{\bf deg}(I)|-n,0\}
for
i=1,2,3
, where
{\bf deg}(I)
is the bounding multidegree of
I

[
12
]
. In particular, if
I
is squarefree, then all Dao numbers are zero. In Corollary
3.10
we compute the Dao numbers of monomial complete intersections. This result shows that in general it may be difficult to compute the Dao numbers of a general complete intersection ideal.

1.
General bounds for the Dao numbers

In this first section, we answer Dao’s question. For this aim, we need to consider Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity over general base rings.

Hereafter, we follows closely
[
2
, Section 8.1]
. Let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a standard graded Noetherian algebra. That is,
R_{0}
is a commutative Noetherian ring and
R
is generated as an
R_{0}
-algebra by finitely many elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
of degree one. Let
Q=R_{+}=\bigoplus_{k>0}R_{k}
be the ideal of
R
generated by the elements of positive degree. Let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. The
initial degree
and the
final degree
of
M
, denoted by
\alpha(M)
and
\omega(M)
, are defined as

\displaystyle\alpha(M)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{j\ :\ (M/QM)_{j}\neq 0\}\ =\ \min\{j\ :\ M_{j}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\omega(M)\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{j\ :\ (M/QM)_{j}\neq 0\}.

The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined as
[
2
, Theorem 8.1.3]
:

\displaystyle\textup{reg}_{R}M\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{j+i\ :\ \textup{H}^{i}_{Q}(M)_{j}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=\ \max\{j-i\ :\ H_{i}({\bf y};M)_{j}\neq 0\}.

Here
\textup{H}_{Q}^{i}(M)
is the
i
th local cohomology module of
M
with support on
Q
, and
H_{i}({\bf y};M)
is the
i
th Koszul homology module of
{\bf y}:y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
with respect to
M
, where
{\bf y}
is a minimal homogeneous system of generators of
Q
.

We record some basic properties which we will need in a moment. These properties are also stated in
[
2
, Page 277, (a), (c) and (d)]
and
[
19
, page 268]
.

(i)

Let
M(j)
be the module
M
whose degrees are shifted by
j
:
M(j)_{i}=M_{i+j}
for all
i
. Then
\omega(M(j))=\omega(M)-j
and
\textup{reg}_{R}M(j)=\textup{reg}_{R}M-j
.

(ii)

Let
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated graded
R
-modules. Then,

\displaystyle\textup{reg}_{R}M\

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{R}N,\,\textup{reg}_{R}P+1\},

\displaystyle\textup{reg}_{R}P\

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{R}N,\,\textup{reg}_{R}M-1\}.

(iii)

If
M_{j}=0
for all
j\gg 0
, then
\textup{reg}_{R}M=\max\{j:M_{j}\neq 0\}
.

(iv)

Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal and
{\bf y}:y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
be a minimal homogeneous system of generators of
Q
. Then

\displaystyle H_{n}({\bf y};R/I)\

\displaystyle\cong\ e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}\ \textup{Soc}_{Q}(R/I)\ =\ e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}\ (0:_{R/I}Q)\

\displaystyle=\ e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}\ (I:Q)/I.

Here,
\textup{Soc}_{Q}(R/I)
is called the
socle
of
I
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
is the basis of a graded free
R
-module of rank
n
, with
\deg e_{i}=1
for all
i
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\max\{j\ :\ \textup{Soc}_{Q}(R/I)_{j}\neq 0\}\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{j-n\ :\ H_{n}({\bf y};R/I)_{j}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{j-i\ :\ H_{i}({\bf y};R/I)_{j}\neq 0\}\

\displaystyle=\ \textup{reg}_{R}R/I.

(v)

Let
I\subset R
be an homogeneous ideal. Then
\textup{reg}_{R}R/I=\textup{reg}_{R}I-1
. This follows because
H_{i}({\bf y};R/I)_{j}\cong H_{i-1}({\bf y};I)_{j}
for all
i
, where
{\bf y}
is a minimal system of generators of
Q
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
be a graded
R
-module and let
\ell\geq 0
be a positive integer. Then
M_{\geq\ell}=\bigoplus_{k\geq\ell}M_{k}
is called a truncation of
M
.

We are now ready to deliver the promised bound for the Dao numbers.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
I
be an ideal. The
Rees algebra
of
I
is the graded ring
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
. Let
J\subset R
be another ideal, then
\mathcal{R}(I,J)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}JI^{k}
is the extension of
J\subset R
in the ring
\mathcal{R}(I)
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}(I,J)
is a finitely generated ideal of
\mathcal{R}(I)
if
J
is a finitely generated ideal of
R
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be either a Noetherian local ring or a standard graded Noetherian
K
-algebra with unique homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. We assume that
K
is infinite and
\textup{depth}R>0
. Let
I\subset R
be an ideal. We assume that
I
is homogeneous if
R
is a
K
-algebra. Then

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I),\,\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}\}.

Here
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

Notice that for all
k\geq 0
we have
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subseteq(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
. Therefore, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\rightarrow(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\rightarrow 0.

(1)

The Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
of
\mathfrak{m}
is a standard graded Noetherian
R
-algebra. Since
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is just the extension of the ideal
I\subset R
in the ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
, it is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-module. Taking the direct sum of the exact sequences (
1
) for all
k\geq 0
, we obtain the short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\rightarrow 0.

(2)

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}(1):_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\ \ (\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}):_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m}\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})

(3)

\displaystyle=\ \ (\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m})(1).

We set
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m})(1)/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)
and notice that for all
k
,

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{k}\ =\ \frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

By
[
10
, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2]
, we have that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is weakly
\mathfrak{m}
-full for all
k\geq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)
, that is
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})=I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
. Thus, we have
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{k}=0
for all
k\geq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)
, and if
\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1\geq 0
then
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1}\neq 0
. It follows that
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-module, and from (iii)

\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\ =\ \max\{\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1,\,0\}.

If
\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0
there is nothing to prove. Thus, we suppose that
\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)>0
. Then
\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1
. From equations (
2
) and (
3
) we obtain the following short exact sequence of graded
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-modules:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)\rightarrow(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m})(1)\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\rightarrow 0.

(4)

Since
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
are finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-modules, it follows that
(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m})(1)
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
-module, as well. Therefore, applying the rules (ii) and (i) to the sequence (
4
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m})(1),\,\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)-1\}

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)_{\geq 1}:_{\mathcal{R}(R)}\mathfrak{m},\,\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)\}-1.

Taking into account that
\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1
, we obtain the asserted inequality for
\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)

We call the

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}

. As shown in the proof, we have

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the same assumptions and notation of Theorem

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\ \leq\ \mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)\ =\ \begin{cases}\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)+1&\textit{if}\ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)\neq 0,\\
\hfill 0&\hfill\textit{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Recall that the

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the same assumptions and notation of Theorem
1.1

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I).

Proof.

If

\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)>0

\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)-1=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)-1=\max\{k:\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{k}\neq 0\}
. Let

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I):_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}Q)_{k}

\displaystyle=\ \{f\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{k}=\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ :\ f\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\}

(5)

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{m}^{k}\cap\{f\in R\ :\ f\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\}

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{m}^{k}\cap(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m}).

We claim that for all

\mathfrak{m}^{k}\cap(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\ =\ (I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m}).

(6)

The assumptions that

\mathfrak{m}^{k}

By our claim (
6

\textup{Soc}_{Q}(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k}\cap(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\ =\ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I).

Now, let

In particular, the proof of Theorem

Corollary 1.5
.

Under the same assumptions and notation of Theorem

\textup{Soc}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I))\ =\ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I).

An ideal
J

of

JI^{k}=I^{k+1}
for some

. Let
J
be a reduction of

. The

Let

-module. Then
\textup{reg}_{R}M_{\geq 1}-1\leq\textup{reg}_{R}M

\textup{reg}_{R}M_{\geq 1}-1\leq\max\{\textup{reg}_{R}M-1,\textup{reg}_{R}M/M_{\geq 1}\}

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be either a Noetherian local ring or a standard graded Noetherian

-algebra with unique homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Let

be a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, which we assume to be homogeneous if

\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m}).

Proof.

For all
k\geq 0
, we have the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\rightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\rightarrow 0.

Taking the direct sum of these short exact sequences for all

of finitely generated graded

\frac{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1)}{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)}\ \ =\ \ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}\ =\ \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\textup{r}_{I}(\mathfrak{m})-1}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}.

Hence, from fact (iii) we have
\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1)/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)=\max\{\textup{r}_{I}(\mathfrak{m})-1,0\}

. Thus, by rule (i) we have that

\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)\ =\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1)\ =\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}-1\ \leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m}).

Suppose now that
\textup{r}_{I}(\mathfrak{m})>0

\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1)/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)=\textup{r}_{I}(\mathfrak{m})-1

. Applying rule (ii) to the above sequence we get

\displaystyle\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1),\,\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}(1)/\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m},I)+1\}

\displaystyle=\ \max\{\textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})_{\geq 1}-1,\,\textup{r}_{I}(\mathfrak{m})\}

\displaystyle\leq\ \textup{reg}_{\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m})}\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{m}),

as desired.

∎

2.
Fullness in Koszul algebras

In this section, we consider the more tame situation of a Koszul algebra and determine bounds for the Dao numbers of an ideal in such a ring. Let
K
be a field and let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a standard graded
K
-algebra. That is,

(i)

R
admits a decomposition
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
as an abelian group

(ii)

R
is generated as an
R_{0}
-algebra by the finite dimensional
K
-vector space
R_{1}
,

(iii)

and
R_{0}=K
.

In this case, we have the surjective homogeneous ring map

\varphi\ :\ S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\rightarrow R

(7)

such that
R_{1}
has a
K
-basis given by elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
of degree one,
\varphi(x_{i})=f_{i}
for all
i
and
\varphi(r)=r
for all
r\in R_{0}=K
. We call (
7
) the
canonical presentation
of
R
. Hence
R\cong S/\ker\varphi
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=R_{+}=\bigoplus_{k>0}R_{k}
.

Notice that we may view the field
K
as a finitely generated graded
R
-module by identifying it with
R/\mathfrak{m}
. Following
[
7
, Definition 4]
, we say that the algebra
R
is
Koszul
if
\textup{reg}_{R}K=0
. In this case,
\textup{reg}_{R}\mathfrak{m}=1
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. We denote by
M_{\langle k\rangle}
the submodule of
M
generated by the
K
-vector space
M_{k}
.

Now, let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal. We say that
I
has
linear resolution
if
\alpha(I)=\omega(I)
(that is,
I
is generated in a single degree) and
\textup{reg}_{R}I=\alpha(I)
. We say that
I
is
componentwise linear
if
I_{\langle j\rangle}
has linear resolution, for all
j
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle k\rangle}=I_{\langle k-1\rangle}\mathfrak{m}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is clear that
I_{\langle k-1\rangle}\mathfrak{m}
is contained in
(I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle k\rangle}
. For the opposite inclusion, observe that the
K
-vector space
(I\mathfrak{m})_{k}
is generated by the non-zero elements of the form
ug
where
u\in\mathfrak{m}
is a monomial and
g
is homogeneous element of
I
such that
\deg(ug)=k
. Therefore, it is enough to show that if
f=ug
is as before, then
f\in I_{\langle k-1\rangle}\mathfrak{m}
. We may assume that
x_{j}
divides
u
. Then, we can write
f=x_{j}((u/x_{j})g)
, where
(u/x_{j})g\in I
has degree
k-1
. Thus,
f\in I_{\langle k-1\rangle}\mathfrak{m}
.

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R
be a Koszul algebra with homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and let
I\subset R
be a componentwise linear ideal. We assume that
\textup{depth}R>0
and
K
is infinite. Then the following properties hold:

(a)

I\mathfrak{m}^{\ell}
is componentwise linear for any
\ell
.

(b)

(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})=I
.

(c)

\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\textup{depth}R>0
, we have that
I\mathfrak{m}
is non-zero.

(a) This fact is well-known. We sketch the proof for completeness. It is enough to prove that
I\mathfrak{m}
is componentwise linear. We must show that for each
j
such that
(I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j\rangle}=I_{\langle j-1\rangle}\mathfrak{m}
is non-zero, this ideal has linear resolution. Notice that
\textup{reg}_{R}(I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j\rangle}\geq j
. On the other hand, by
[
7
, Lemma 4]
and since
\textup{reg}_{R}I_{\langle j-1\rangle}=j-1
, we obtain that
\textup{reg}_{R}(I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j\rangle}\leq\textup{reg}_{R}I_{\langle j-1\rangle}+\textup{reg}_{R}\mathfrak{m}=j
.

(b) Firstly, we assume that
I
has linear resolution, say
d
-linear. It is clear that
I\subseteq(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})
. Suppose for a contradiction that there exists
f\in(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})\setminus I
. Then
\overline{f}=f+I\in(I:\mathfrak{m})/I
, because
f\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I
and
f\notin I
. By fact (iv) recalled at page
(iv)
,
\textup{Tor}^{R}_{n-1}(R/\mathfrak{m},I)\cong\textup{Tor}^{R}_{n}(R/\mathfrak{m},R/I)\cong e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{n}\ \textup{Soc}(R/I)
. Since
I
has a
d
-linear resolution,
\textup{Tor}^{R}_{n-1}(R/\mathfrak{m},I)
is concentrated in degree
n+d-1
. Thus, the
K
-vector space
\textup{Soc}(R/I)
is generated in a single degree
d-1
, and so
\deg(f)=d-1
. By part (a),
I\mathfrak{m}
has a
(d+1)
-linear resolution. Hence, the above argument shows that any non-zero homogeneous element of
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})\setminus I\mathfrak{m}
has degree
d
. Since
I\mathfrak{m}
is generated in degree
d+1
,
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})
is generated in degrees
\geq d
. This contradicts the fact that
\deg(f)=d-1
and shows that
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})=I
.

Now, suppose that
I
is generated in more than one degree. Notice that the ideal
(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})
is homogeneous as well. Thus, by the first part we have

\displaystyle(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})\

\displaystyle=\ \bigoplus_{j}(I\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})_{j}\ =\ \bigoplus_{j}((I\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j+1\rangle}:\mathfrak{m})_{j}

\displaystyle=\ \bigoplus_{j}(I_{\langle j\rangle}\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})_{j}\ =\ \bigoplus_{j}(I_{\langle j\rangle})_{j}

\displaystyle=\ \bigoplus_{j}I_{j}\ =\ I.

(c) It follows by combining (a) with (b).

∎

Again let
R
be a Koszul algebra and
I\subset R
be an ideal. We say that
I
is a
monomial ideal
of
R
if
I
can be generated by monomials of
R
. In such case, we denote by
\mathcal{G}(I)
any minimal monomial generating set of
I
.

We say that
I
has
linear quotients
if there exists a minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)
of
I
and an order
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\dots<u_{m}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):_{R}(u_{j})
is generated by variables, for
j=2,\dots,m
.

Example 2.7
.

[
6
, Example 1.20]
Consider the Koszul algebra

R\ =\ \frac{K[a,b,c,d]}{(ac,ad,ab-bd,a^{2}+bc,b^{2})}.

In this algebra, the ideal
I=(b)
does not have linear resolution. Notice that in the usual polynomial ring
S=K[a,b,c,d]
the ideal
I=(b)
has linear quotients, and thus a linear resolution
[
19
, Proposition 8.2.1]
.

To guarantee that monomial ideals
I\subset R
with linear quotients are componentwise linear we need to impose further conditions on the algebra
R
. As shown in
[
28
]
, we must assume that
R
is
strongly Koszul
. A Koszul algebra
R
is called
strongly Koszul
if there exists a basis
X
of
R_{1}
such that for every proper subset
Y\subset X
and every
x\in X\setminus Y
, there exists a subset
Z\subset X
such that
(Y):_{R}(x)=(Z)
.

A pivotal property of a strongly Koszul algebra
R
is that any ideal of
R
generated by linear forms has linear resolution
[
28
, Lemma 3.3]
. Hence, this fact together with
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
implies that

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be a strongly Koszul algebra and
I\subset R
be a monomial ideal with linear quotients order
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\dots<u_{m}
satisfying
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
. Then
I
is componentwise linear. In particular,
\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0
.

See also
[
9
]
for the exterior algebra case. Example
2.7
shows that we can not drop the assumption that
R
is a strongly Koszul algebra.

If
I
is a monomial ideal in the standard graded polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
having linear quotients order
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\dots<u_{m}
, then by the usual Björner-Wachs rearrangement lemma
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
4
]
) we can always also find an order
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:u_{i_{1}}<\dots<u_{i_{m}}
such that
\deg(u_{i_{1}})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{i_{m}})
. Thus, at least in the case of a polynomial ring, the condition on the degrees
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
given in Proposition
2.8
can be removed. It is not clear whether this is also the case for monomial ideals with linear quotients in a strongly Koszul algebra

3.
The Dao numbers of monomial ideals

While Theorem
2.5
shows that the number
\textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
is an upper bound for the Dao numbers, it may be difficult to compute it. In this section, we consider the more specific class of monomial ideals in a standard graded polynomial ring, and we provide combinatorial bounds for the Dao numbers.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a infinite field
K
and let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be the unique homogeneous maximal ideal.

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. We set
{\bf a}[i]=a_{i}
for all
i
,
{\bf x^{a}}=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{{\bf a}[i]}
. In particular,
{\bf x^{0}}=1
for
{\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
. The monomial
{\bf x^{a}}
is called
squarefree
if
{\bf a}[i]\in\{0,1\}
for all
i
. A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
squarefree
if
I
is generated by squarefree monomials. The next easy observation will be used several times.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Notice that
\textup{depth}S=n\geq 1
. Since
K
is infinite, by
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
we have
\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)
.

We first combinatorially bound
\textup{reg}_{\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)}\textup{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
for a monomial ideal
I\subset S
. Then, by Theorem
2.5
we obtain some bounds for the Dao numbers. For such aim, we recall the next result
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\gg 0
.

Analyzing carefully the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
we will obtain the desired combinatorial bounds for the Dao numbers.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. In this case,
I
has a unique minimal monomial generating set, denoted as usual by
\mathcal{G}(I)
. Fix any order
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\cdots<u_{m}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
. For each
2\leq i\leq m
, let
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):_{S}(u_{i})=(w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,\ell_{i}})
. We set

\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}(\deg(w_{i,j})-1),

for
2\leq i\leq m
, and
\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{1}}=0
. It is clear that
I
has linear quotients with respect to the order
\mathcal{O}
if and only if
\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

Notice that
I\mathfrak{m}
is generated by the set
\{x_{j}u_{i}\ :\ j=1,\dots,n,\ i=1,\dots,m\}
. After removing the non-minimal generators of
I\mathfrak{m}
and any repeated element whenever it appears again, we obtain the minimal generating set
\mathcal{G}(I\mathfrak{m})
. Let
x_{j_{1}}u_{i_{1}},x_{j_{2}}u_{i_{2}}
be two minimal generators of
I\mathfrak{m}
. We set
x_{j_{1}}u_{i_{1}}<x_{j_{2}}u_{i_{2}}
if
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
j_{1}<j_{2}
or
i_{1}<i_{2}
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{1}
this order. It is shown in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
that for all
i
and
j
such that
x_{j}u_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I\mathfrak{m})
we have that
\lambda_{I\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{O}_{1},x_{j}u_{i}}\leq\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}
and if
\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}>0
, then
\lambda_{I\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{O}_{1},x_{j}u_{i}}<\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}
. Hence, iterating this process for all
k\geq 1
, and calling
\mathcal{O}_{k}
the order of
\mathcal{G}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k})
obtained as explained above, we see that for all

k\ \geq\ \max\{\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{i}}\ :\ i=1,\dots,m\}

we have
\lambda_{I\mathfrak{m}^{k},\mathcal{O}_{k},v}=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{G}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k})
. This means, as we observed above, that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
indeed has linear quotients with linear quotients order
\mathcal{O}_{k}
.

Our discussion shows that

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over an infinite field
K
and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \min_{\mathcal{O}}(\max_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u}),

where the minimum is taken over all possible orders
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
.

For two monomials
u
and
v
, we set
u:v=u/\gcd(u,v)
. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{j}\in S
be monomials. Then
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):_{S}(u_{j})
is generated by the monomials
u_{i}:u_{j}
for
i=1,\dots,j-1

[
19
, Proposition 1.2.2]
. We denote by
\mu(I)=\dim_{K}(I/\mathfrak{m}I)
the minimal number of generators of
I
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over an infinite field
K
and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ (\!\!\,\!\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u))+1-\mu(I)-\omega(I).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}:u_{1}<\dots<u_{m}
be an order of
\mathcal{G}(I)
with
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
. For all
2\leq j\leq m
, we have that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):_{S}(u_{j})=(u_{i}:u_{j},i=1,\dots,j-1)
. Hence, for all
2\leq j\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{j}}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(\deg(u_{i}:u_{j})-1)\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(\deg(u_{i})-1).

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\deg(u_{i})=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\!\deg(u)-\deg(u_{m})=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u)-\omega(I)
and
m=\mu(I)
, we conclude that
\lambda_{I,\mathcal{O},u_{j}}\leq(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u))+1-\mu(I)-\omega(I)
, for all
j=1,\dots,m
. The assertion follows from Theorem
3.3
.

∎

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. Even if
I
does not have a unique minimal generating set, all minimal generating sets
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
of
I
have the same number
\mu(I)=\dim_{K}(I/\mathfrak{m}I)
of generators and the number
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(f_{i})
does not depend on the particular minimal generating set. This follows because
I/\mathfrak{m}I
is a graded
K
-vector space. Therefore, in light of the above corollary, it makes sense to ask

Question 3.5
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. It is true that

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ (\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}}\deg(f))+1-\mu(I)-\omega(I),

where
\mathcal{B}
is any graded
K
-basis of
I/\mathfrak{m}I
?

Now, we propose a different bound for the Dao numbers of monomial ideals.

For this aim, following
[
12
]
, we define the
bounding multidegree
of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
to be the vector
{\bf deg}(I)=(\deg_{x_{1}}(I),\dots,\deg_{x_{n}}(I))
, with

\deg_{x_{i}}(I)\ =\ \max_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg_{x_{i}}(u),\ \ \textup{for all}\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Here for a monomial
u={\bf x^{a}}
, we set
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)={\bf a}[i]=\max\{j\ :\ x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. Furthermore, for
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we set
|{\bf a}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf a}[i]
. Thus
\deg({\bf x^{a}})=|{\bf a}|
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\ \leq\ \max\{|{\bf deg}(I)|-n,0\}.

In particular, if
I
is squarefree then
\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0.

In order to prove the theorem, we shall need the following lemma. For an integer
n\geq 1
, we let
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
t\in[n]
and all
k\geq 0
, we have

(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{t})\ =\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}+({\bf x^{a}}/x_{t}\ :\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ {\bf a}[t]>0)\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}.

Proof.

Notice that
\{{\bf x^{a}}u\ :\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ u\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})\}
is a generating set of
I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
. Hence
\{{\bf x^{a}}u:x_{t}\ ,\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ u\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})\}
is a generating set of
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{t})
. Let
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
u\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})
. Then,

{\bf x^{a}}u:x_{t}\ =\ \begin{cases}\ ({\bf x^{a}}/x_{t})u&\text{if}\ {\bf a}[t]>0,\\
\ {\bf x^{a}}(u/x_{t})&\text{if}\ {\bf a}[t]=0,\ x_{t}\ \textup{divides}\ u,\\
\ {\bf x^{a}}u&\textup{if}\ {\bf a}[t]=0,\ x_{t}\ \textup{does not divide}\ u.\end{cases}

Since
u\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})
is arbitrary, from the above computations we see that

(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{t})\ =\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ +\ ({\bf x^{a}}/x_{t}\ :\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ {\bf a}[t]>0)\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\ +\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}.

Finally, from the inclusion
I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\subseteq I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
, the asserted formula follows.

∎

We are now ready for the proof of the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

By Remark
3.1
we have
\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)
for any monomial ideal
I\subset S
. Suppose that for some
i\in[n]
the variable
x_{i}
does not divide any monomial generator of
I
, then by Lemma
3.7
we have that
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{i})=I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
for all
k
. Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\ \subseteq\ (I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\ =\ \bigcap_{t=1}^{n}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{t})\ \subseteq\ (I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{i})\ =\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}.

Equality follows, and the Dao numbers of
I
are zero in this case.

Suppose now that for all
i\in[n]
the variable
x_{i}
divides some monomial generator
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. By Lemma
3.7
, for all
k\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\

\displaystyle=\ \bigcap_{t=1}^{n}\,(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{t})

\displaystyle=\ \bigcap_{t=1}^{n}\big[I\mathfrak{m}^{k}+({\bf x^{a}}/x_{t}\ :\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ {\bf a}[t]>0)\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\big].

Now, let

. It is clear that
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
is contained in
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
. Suppose for a contradiction that there exists a monomial
v\in(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
which does not belong to
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
. Then, by the above formula, we see that

v\ \in\ \bigcap_{t=1}^{n}\big[({\bf x^{a}}/x_{t}\ :\ {\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ {\bf a}[t]>0)\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\big].

(8)

Let
p\in[n]
such that
x_{p}
divides
v
. Since
J=({\bf x^{a}}/x_{p}:{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I),{\bf a}[p]>0)\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
is non-zero by our assumption and
v\in J
, there exist
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with
{\bf a}[p]>0
and a monomial
u\in\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})
such that
({\bf x^{a}}/x_{p})u
divides
v
. From this we see that

\deg_{x_{p}}(v)\geq\deg_{x_{p}}(({\bf x^{a}}/x_{p})u)\geq\deg_{x_{p}}({\bf x^{a}}/x_{p})=\deg_{x_{p}}({\bf x^{a}})-1.

We claim that
\deg_{x_{p}}(v)=\deg_{x_{p}}({\bf x^{a}})-1
. Suppose that
\deg_{x_{p}}(v)>\deg_{x_{p}}({\bf x^{a}})-1
. Then
x_{p}^{{\bf a}[p]}
divides
v
. Now, if

u

, which would imply that

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. For all

,

\textup{Soc}(S/(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}))\ =\ \frac{(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}\ =\ \frac{I\mathfrak{m}^{k}}{I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}}.

In particular,
\beta_{n-1}(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})\ =\ \mu(I\mathfrak{m}^{k})
.

Let
u={\bf x^{a}}\in S
be a monomial. Its

is the set defined as

\textup{supp}(u)\ =\ \{i\ :\ x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}\ =\ \{i\ :\ {\bf a}[i]>0\}.

Next, we compute the Dao numbers of monomial complete intersections.

We call an ideal
I\subset S
a

if
I
is generated by a regular sequence. In particular, it is easy to see that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
with minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
is a complete intersection if and only if
\textup{supp}(u_{i})\cap\textup{supp}(u_{j})\ =\ \emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial complete intersection with minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
.

(a)

If
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})\neq[n]
, then
\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0.

(b)

If
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})=[n]
and
\mathcal{G}(I)
contains only pure powers, then
n=m
, up to relabeling
u_{i}=x_{i}^{a_{i}}
for all
i\in[n]
with
1\leq a_{1}\leq\dots\leq a_{n}
and

\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}-(n-1).

(c)

If
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})=[n]
, and
\mathcal{G}(I)
contains a non-pure power, then

\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0.

Proof.

If
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})\neq[n]
, then the Dao numbers are zero as shown in the first part of the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Statement (a) follows.

Now, assume that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})=[n]
, and
\mathcal{G}(I)
contains only pure powers. Thus

and up to relabeling we can assume that

for all

with
1\leq a_{1}\leq\dots\leq a_{n}
. By Corollary

, we have

\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)\leq(\!\!\,\!\!\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u))+1-\mu(I)-\omega(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}-(n-1).

Thus, it suffices to show that

is not weakly
\mathfrak{m}

. For this aim, consider the monomial

. Let

. Notice that

x_{j}v=u_{j}(\prod_{i\in[n]\setminus\{j\}}x_{i}^{a_{i}-1})\ \in\ I\mathfrak{m}^{\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus\{j\}}a_{i}-(n-1)}.

Now, since for all

\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus\{j\}}\!\!\!a_{i}-(n-1)\ \geq\ \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}-n+1\ =\ k+1

. On the other hand
v\notin I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
, because for each monomial
w\in I\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subset I
there exists
i\in[n]
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(w)\geq a_{i}
. This shows that
\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}-(n-1)
and (b) follows.

Finally, assume that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\textup{supp}(u_{i})=[n]
and that
\mathcal{G}(I)
contains a non-pure power. Up to relabeling, we may assume that
x_{1}x_{2}
divides
u_{1}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Since
I
is a complete intersection,
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
do not divide any other generator
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus\{u_{1}\}
. Hence, by Lemma
3.7
, for all
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{1})\

\displaystyle=\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}+(u_{1}/x_{1})\mathfrak{m}^{k+1},

\displaystyle(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:x_{2})\

\displaystyle=\ I\mathfrak{m}^{k}+(u_{1}/x_{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}.

Assume for a contradiction that
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\neq I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
and let
v\in(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
be a monomial not belonging to
I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
. Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we see that
v\in(u_{1}/x_{1})\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}\cap(u_{1}/x_{2})\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
. Thus
v=(u_{1}/x_{1})w_{1}=(u_{1}/x_{2})w_{2}
where
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
are monomials. The previous equation implies that
\deg_{x_{1}}(v)\geq a_{1}
. Thus
x_{1}
divides
w_{1}
and so
v=(u_{1}/x_{1})(x_{1}(w/x_{1}))=u_{1}(w/x_{1})\in I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
, a contradiction. Hence,
(I\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})=I\mathfrak{m}^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
, and so
\mathfrak{d}_{1}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{2}(I)=\mathfrak{d}_{3}(I)=0
.

∎

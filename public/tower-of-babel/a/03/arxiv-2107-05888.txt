Asymptotic Results for Rough Continuous-state Branching Processes
Wei Xu

Abstract
In this paper we provide explicit representations of Laplace transforms of extinction time and total progeny of rough continuous-state branching processes introduced in [ 7 ] . Also, we show that their tail distributions are much fatter than those of Feller branching diffusions.

1
Introduction

A
rough continuous-state branching process
(rough CB-process) was introduced in
[
7
]
in the generalization of the classical second Ray-Knight theorem to a spectrally positive stable process
\{\xi(t):t\geq 0\}
with index
1+\alpha\in(1,2)
and Laplace exponent

\displaystyle{\it\Phi}(\lambda)=b\lambda+c\lambda^{1+\alpha}=b\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(e^{-\lambda y}-1+\lambda y\big)\nu_{\alpha}(dy),

(1.1)

where
b\geq 0
,
c>0
and the
Lévy measure

\nu_{\alpha}(dy)
is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}(dy):=\frac{c\alpha(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}y^{-\alpha-2}dy.

(1.2)

It is described as the unique weak solution to the stochastic Volterra equation

\displaystyle\qquad X_{\zeta}(t)=\zeta-b\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{c\Gamma(\alpha)}X_{\zeta}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{X_{\zeta}(s)}\int_{(t-s-y)^{+}}^{t-s}\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{c\Gamma(\alpha)}dr\tilde{N}_{\alpha}(ds,dy,dz),

(1.3)

where
\tilde{N}_{\alpha}(ds,dy,dz)
is a compensated Poisson random measure on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with intensity
ds\nu_{\alpha}(dy)dz
.

The rough CB-process
X_{\zeta}
is locally Hölder-continuous of any order strictly less than
\alpha/2
and will fall into the trap
0
in finite time, i.e. its
extinction time

\displaystyle\tau_{X_{\zeta}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{\zeta}(t)=0\}

(1.4)

is finite almost surely and
X_{\zeta}(\tau_{X_{\zeta}}+t)=0
for any
t\geq 0
. Thus its
total progeny
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}X_{\zeta}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}}X_{\zeta}(t)dt

(1.5)

is also finite almost surely.
In this work, we mainly explore the asymptotic behavior of tail distributions of
\tau_{X_{\zeta}}
and
\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}
.

To understand the laws of
\tau_{X_{\zeta}}
and
\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}
, we first study the characteristic functionals of
X_{\zeta}
.
From Theorem 1.3 in
[
7
]
, there exist two continuous functions
\{v_{t}(\lambda):t,\lambda\geq 0\}
and
\{V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda):\lambda\geq 0\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda X_{\zeta}(t)\}\big]=\exp\{-\zeta\cdot v_{t}(\lambda)\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}\}\big]=\exp\{-\zeta\cdot V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)\}.

(1.6)

Enlightened by the method developed in the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
7
]
, we first provide explicit representations of
v_{t}(\lambda)
and
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)
in terms of functionals of scale function and inverse Laplace exponent of
\xi
. Our main idea is characterizing the limits of characteristic functionals of local time processes of rescaled compounded Poisson processes which converge weakly to the stable process
\xi
.
Noting that the function
v_{t}(\lambda)
increases to a finite limit
\bar{v}_{t}
as
\lambda\to\infty
and the function
\bar{v}_{t}
decreases to
0
as
t\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}>t\}=\mathbf{P}\{X_{\zeta}(t)>0\}=1-e^{-\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t}}\sim\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t},

as
t\to\infty
.
Applying Karamata-Tauberian theorem together with our observation that both
v_{t}(\lambda)
and
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)
are regularly varying at infinity, we obtain that both
\tau_{\zeta}
and
\mathbf{T}_{\zeta}
have heavy-tailed distributions.
In precise, for both
b=0
and
b>0
we have as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}>t\}\sim Ct^{-\alpha}.

(1.7)

Differently, as
x\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim C\cdot x^{-\alpha/(1+\alpha)}\mbox{ when }b=0\mbox{ and }\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim C\cdot x^{-\alpha}\mbox{ when }b>0.

(1.8)

These are in sharp contrast to the asymptotic behavior of Feller branching diffusion defined as the unique solution to

\displaystyle Y_{\zeta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta-\int_{0}^{t}bY_{\zeta}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2cY_{\zeta}(s)}dB(s),

(1.9)

where
\{B(t):t\geq 0\}
is a standard Brownian motion.
In detail,
denote by
\tau_{Y_{\zeta}}
and
\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}
its extinction time and total progeny respectively.
Theorem 3.4 in
[
5
, p.17]
together with a simply calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{Y_{\zeta}}>t\}\sim Ct^{-1}\mbox{ when }b=0\mbox{ and }\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{Y_{\zeta}}>t\}\sim Ce^{-bt}\mbox{ when }b>0.

(1.10)

Moreover, from Corollary 10.9 in
[
4
, p.280]
we also have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim C\cdot x^{-1/2}\mbox{ when }b=0\mbox{ and }\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}>x\}\leq Ce^{-\frac{b^{2}}{4c}x}\mbox{ when }b>0.

(1.11)

Organization of this paper.
In Section 2, we first give a criticality for a rough CB-process and then provide the asymptotic results for its extinction time and total progeny.
The proofs are given in Section 3.

2
Main results

Define the
Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\alpha,\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\alpha}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha(n+1))}.

It is locally Hölder-continuous with index
\alpha
; see
[
2
]
for a precise definition of it and a survey of some of its properties, e.g. for any
a\geq 0
we have the well-known Laplace transform

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}ax^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-a\cdot x^{\alpha})dx=\frac{a}{a+\lambda^{\alpha}},\quad\lambda\geq 0.

(2.1)

Let
\{W(x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
be the
scale function
of
\xi
, which is identically zero on
x\in(-\infty,0)
and characterized on
[0,\infty)
as a strictly increasing function whose Laplace transform is given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}W(x)dx=\frac{1}{\mathit{\Phi}(\lambda)},\quad\lambda>0.

(2.2)

The scale function
W
is infinitely differentiable on
(0,\infty)
with derivative denoted as
W^{\prime}
.
Applying integration by parts to (
2.2
) and the using (
2.1
), we see that the derivative function
W^{\prime}
is of the form:

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(x)=c^{-1}x^{\alpha-1}\cdot E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-b/c\cdot x^{\alpha}),\quad x\geq 0.

Specially, when
b=0
we have
E_{\alpha,\alpha}(0)=\Gamma(\alpha)
and

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha-1}}{c\Gamma(\alpha)},\quad W(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{c\Gamma(1+\alpha)}.

(2.3)

When
b>0
, the function
bW^{\prime}
turns to be
Mittag-Leffler probability density function
and hence
bW(t)\to 1
as
t\to\infty
.

From Theorem 1.4 in
[
7
]
, the stochastic Volterra equation (
1.3
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle X_{\zeta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta(1-bW(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{X_{\zeta}(s)}\big(W(t-s)-W(t-s-y)\big)\tilde{N}_{\alpha}(ds,dy,dz).

(2.4)

Taking expectations on both sides of the equation above, we can get the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
t>0
, we have
\mathbf{E}[X_{\zeta}(t)]=\zeta(1-bW(t))
, which is identical to
\zeta
when
b=0
and decreases to
0
as
t\to\infty
when
b>0
.

We say the rough CB-process
X_{\zeta}
is
critical
or
subcritical
according as
b=0
or
b>0
, which is consistent to the criticality for Feller branching diffusions; see Definition 10.4 in
[
4
, p.277]
.

2.1
Extinction time

We now provide an explicit representation for the Laplace exponent
v_{t}(\lambda)
in term of a functional of
W
and then explore the tail distribution of extinction time
\tau_{X_{\zeta}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
\lambda,t\geq 0
, the Laplace exponent
v_{t}(\lambda)
is of the form

\displaystyle v_{t}(\lambda)=\frac{c}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda\cdot\nabla_{x}W(t)}{\lambda W(t)+1}\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{\alpha+1}}

and as
\lambda\to\infty
it increases strictly to

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t}:=\frac{c}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla_{x}W(t)}{W(t)}\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{\alpha+1}}<\infty.

Corollary 2.3
.

The function
\bar{v}_{t}
decreases to
0
as
t\to\infty
. In precise,

(1)

when
b=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}\geq t\}\sim\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t}=\zeta\cdot c\Gamma(1+\alpha)\cdot t^{-\alpha};

(2)

when
b>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}\geq t\}\sim\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t}\sim\frac{\zeta\cdot c}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}t^{-\alpha}.

Remark 2.4
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, from Theorem 3.4 in
[
5
, p.17]
we have when
b=0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Y_{\zeta}(t)\}\big]=\exp\Big\{-\zeta\cdot\frac{\lambda}{1+c\lambda t}\Big\}

and hence as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{Y_{\zeta}}>t\}=1-\exp\Big\{-\frac{\zeta}{ct}\Big\}\sim\frac{\zeta}{ct}.

When
b>0
, from Example 2.3 in
[
5
, p.14]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Y_{\zeta}(t)\}\big]=\exp\Big\{-\zeta\cdot\frac{\lambda e^{-bt}}{1+c\lambda\cdot b^{-1}(1-e^{-bt})}\Big\}

and hence as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{Y_{\zeta}}>t\}=1-\exp\Big\{-\zeta\cdot\frac{e^{-bt}}{c\cdot b^{-1}(1-e^{-bt})}\Big\}\sim\frac{b}{c}e^{-bt}.

2.2
Total progeny

It is obvious that the function
\mathit{\Phi}
is infinitely differentiable and strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
with
\mathit{\Phi}(0)=0
and
\mathit{\Phi}(\lambda)\to\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
.
This allows us to define its inverse
\mathit{\Psi}
on
[0,\infty)
with
\mathit{\Psi}(0)=0
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
we have
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)=c\cdot|\Psi(\lambda)|^{\alpha}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

The function
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)
increases strictly to infinity as
\lambda\to\infty
. In precise,

(1)

when
b=0
, we have
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)=c^{1/(1+\alpha)}\cdot\lambda^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}
and hence as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim\frac{\zeta\cdot c^{1/(1+\alpha)}}{\Gamma(1/(1+\alpha))}\cdot x^{-\alpha/(1+\alpha)};

(2)

when
b>0
, we have
V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)\sim\zeta\cdot c\cdot(\lambda/b)^{\alpha}
as
\lambda\to 0+
and hence as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{X_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim\frac{\zeta\cdot c}{b^{\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\cdot x^{-\alpha}.

Remark 2.7
.

From Corollary 10.9 in
[
4
, p.280]
, for
\lambda\geq-\frac{b^{2}}{4c}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}\}\big]=\exp\Big\{-\zeta\cdot\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}+4c\lambda}-b}{2c}\Big\}.

(2.5)

When
b=0
, we have
\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}\}\big]\sim 1-\zeta\cdot\sqrt{t/c}
as
\lambda\to 0+
. Applying Karamata-Tauberian theorem; see Theorem 8.1.6 in
[
1
, p.333]
, we have as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}>x\}\sim\zeta\cdot\frac{x^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{c}\Gamma(1/2)}.

When
b>0
, by Chebyshev’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}>x\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{e^{\frac{b^{2}}{4c}\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}}>e^{\frac{b^{2}}{4c}x}\}\leq e^{-\frac{b^{2}}{4c}x}\mathbf{E}[e^{\frac{b^{2}}{4c}\mathbf{T}_{Y_{\zeta}}}]\leq\exp\Big\{\frac{b\zeta}{2c}-\frac{b^{2}}{4c}x\Big\}.

3
Proofs

3.1
Compound Poisson Processes

Let
\Lambda
be a Pareto II distribution on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with location
0
and shape
\alpha+1
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Lambda(dx)=(\alpha+1)(1+x)^{-\alpha-2}dx.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\{\xi^{(n)}(t):t\geq 0\}
be a compound Poisson process with drift
-1
, arrival rate
\gamma_{n}>0
, jump size distribution
\Lambda
and initial state
\xi^{(n)}(0)
distributed as

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}(dx):=\alpha\bar{\Lambda}(x)dx=\alpha(1+x)^{-1-\alpha}dx.

(3.1)

Here
\bar{\Lambda}(x):=\Lambda[x,\infty)
is the tail distribution of
\Lambda
.
It is a spectrally positive Lévy process with Laplace exponent

\displaystyle{\it\Phi}^{(n)}(\lambda):=\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\lambda x}-1)\gamma_{n}\Lambda(dx),\quad\lambda\geq 0.

We are interested in the case
\mathbf{E}[\xi^{(n)}(1)]=\gamma_{n}/\alpha-1\leq 0
in which the function
{\it\Phi}^{(n)}
increases strictly to infinity.

Let
c_{0}=(c/\Gamma(1-\alpha))^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}
.
We define the
first passage time
of
\xi^{(n)}
in
(-\infty,0]
by

\displaystyle\tau_{0}^{(n)}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\xi^{(n)}_{t}\leq 0\}.

(3.2)

Let
\{L_{\xi^{(n)}}(y,t):y\in\mathbb{R},t\geq 0\}
be the local time process of
\xi^{(n)}
with

\displaystyle L_{\xi^{(n)}}(y,t):=\#\{s\in(0,t]:\xi^{(n)}(s)=y\}.

We have
L_{\xi^{(n)}}(y,\infty)=\infty
a.s. if and only if
\mathbf{E}[\xi^{(n)}(1)]=0
; equivalently, if and only if
\gamma_{n}=\alpha
.
Let
\{Z^{(n)}_{k}(t):t\geq 0\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. copies of local time process
\{L_{\xi^{(n)}}(t,\tau_{0}^{(n)}):t\geq 0\}
.
We now consider under the following condition, the weak convergences of rescaled processes
\{\xi_{0}^{(n)}(t):t\geq 0\}
and
\{S_{\zeta}^{(n)}(t):t\geq 0\}
with

\displaystyle\xi_{0}^{(n)}(t):=\frac{c_{0}}{n}\xi^{(n)}(n^{1+\alpha}t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{\zeta}^{(n)}(t):=\frac{1}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor c_{0}n^{\alpha}\zeta\rfloor}Z^{(n)}_{k}(nt/c_{0}).

(3.3)

Condition 3.1
.

Assume that
n^{\alpha}(1-\gamma_{n}/\alpha)\to b/c_{0}
as
n\to\infty
.

It is easy to see that the Lévy process
\xi_{0}^{(n)}
has Laplace exponent
n^{1+\alpha}{\it\Phi}^{(n)}(c_{0}\lambda/n)
for
\lambda\geq 0
.
Under Condition
3.1
, a routine computation shows that
n^{1+\alpha}{\it\Phi}^{(n)}(c_{0}\lambda/n)\to{\it\Phi}(\lambda)
as
n\to\infty
.
By Corollary 4.3 in
[
3
, p.440]
, we have
\xi_{0}^{(n)}\to\xi
weakly in
\mathbf{D}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
.
Denote by
\{W^{(n)}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
the scale of
\xi^{(n)}
. We have
W^{(n)}(0)=1
and as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle n^{-\alpha}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})\to c_{0}W(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.4)

The next lemma follows directly from Lemma 2.3 and Proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under Condition
3.1
, for any
\zeta>0
we have
S_{\zeta}^{(n)}
converges weakly to the unique weak solution
X_{\zeta}
to (
1.3
) in
\mathbf{D}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}_{+})
as
n\to\infty
.

3.2
Proofs for Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.3

From Lemma
3.2
, it is necessary to consider the distribution of
Z_{1}^{(n)}
at first.
For
x>0
, denote by
\mathbf{P}_{x}
and
\mathbf{E}_{x}
the probability law and expectation of
\xi^{(n)}
conditioned on
\xi^{(n)}(0)=x
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
n\geq 1
and
x,t>0
with
x\neq t
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(t)}}{1-e^{-\lambda}(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(t)})},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\nabla_{x}W^{(n)}(t)}{W^{(n)}(t)}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{1-e^{-\lambda}(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(t)})}.

(3.6)

For
t>0
, let
\tau_{t}^{+}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\xi^{(n)}(s)>t\}
and
T_{t}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\xi^{(n)}(s)=t\}
. Since
\xi^{(n)}
has neither diffusion nor nonnegative jumps, we have
\tau_{t}^{+}<T_{t}
a.s. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\{\tau_{0}^{(n)}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}+\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\};\tau_{0}^{(n)}>\tau_{t}^{+}\big]

(3.7)

By the strong Markov property of
\xi^{(n)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\};\tau_{0}^{(n)}>\tau_{t}^{+}\big]=e^{-\lambda}\mathbf{E}_{t}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]\mathbf{P}_{x}\{\tau_{t}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{(n)}\}.

(3.8)

Solving (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) with
x=t
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}_{t}\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}}{1-e^{-\lambda}\mathbf{P}_{t}\{\tau_{t}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{-}\}}.

Plugging this back into (
3.7
)-(
3.8
), for
x\neq t
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}+\mathbf{P}_{x}\{\tau_{t}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{-}\}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\mathbf{P}_{t}\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}}{1-e^{-\lambda}\mathbf{P}_{t}\{\tau_{t}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{-}\}}.

From Theorem 8.1(iii) in
[
4
, p.215]
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{t}\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}=W^{(n)}(0)/W^{(n)}(t)
and
\mathbf{P}_{x}\{\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{t}^{+}\}=W^{(n)}(t-x)/W^{(n)}(t)
.
With a simple calculation we can get the desired result.

\Box

Proof for Theorem
2.2
.
From Proposition
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-\lambda Z_{1}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{1-e^{-\lambda}(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(t)})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla_{x}W^{(n)}(t)}{W^{(n)}(t)}\Lambda^{*}(dx)

By the mutual independence among
\{Z^{(n)}_{k}\}_{n\geq 1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda S_{\zeta}^{(n)}(t)\}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbf{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}Z_{1}^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})\Big\}\Big]\Big)^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}c_{0}\zeta\rfloor}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lfloor n^{\alpha}c_{0}\zeta\rfloor}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}v^{(n)}_{t}(\lambda)\Big\},

where

\displaystyle v^{(n)}_{t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c_{0}n^{\alpha}\cdot\log\Big(1-\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}}}{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}}(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla_{x}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})}{W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})}\Lambda^{*}(dx)\Big).

From Lemma
3.2
we have
v^{(n)}_{t}(\lambda)\to v_{t}(\lambda)
as
n\to\infty
.
Noting that

\displaystyle v^{(n)}_{t}(\lambda)\sim\frac{c_{0}n^{\alpha}(1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}})}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}\big[1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}}\big(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})}\big)\big]}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla_{x}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})\Lambda^{*}(dx)}{n^{-\alpha}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})/c_{0}}.

From (
3.4
) and the fact that
W^{(n)}(0)=1
, we have as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle c_{0}n^{\alpha}\Big[1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}}\Big(1-\frac{W^{(n)}(0)}{W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})}\Big)\Big]\to\lambda+1/W(t).

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{x}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})\Lambda^{*}(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{nx}W^{(n)}(nt/c_{0})\Lambda^{*}(n\cdot dx)\to c_{0}^{1+\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{x}W(t)\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{1+\alpha}}.

Putting all estimates above together, we can immediately get the desire result.

Proof for Corollary
2.3
.

Since
X_{\zeta}
falls into the trap
0
in finite time, we have
\bar{v}_{t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
Moreover, we have
\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}>t\}=1-\exp\{-\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t}\}\sim\zeta\cdot\bar{v}_{t}
as
t\to\infty
.
Changing the order of integration, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{x}W(t)\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{x}W^{\prime}(t-s)ds\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{\alpha+1}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}W^{\prime}(t-x)dx\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha ds}{s^{\alpha+1}}=\int_{0}^{t}W^{\prime}(t-x)x^{-\alpha}dx.

(3.10)

When
b=0
, from (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{x}W(t)\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{\alpha+1}}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-x)^{\alpha-1}x^{-\alpha}dx}{c\Gamma(\alpha)}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}{c}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\{\tau_{X_{\zeta}}>t\}\sim\zeta/W(t)=W(t)=\zeta\cdot c\Gamma(1+\alpha)\cdot t^{-\alpha}.

When
b>0
, from (2.4) in
[
7
]
we have as
t\to\infty

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(t)\sim\frac{c\alpha\cdot t^{-\alpha-1}}{b^{2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}=o(t^{-\alpha}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}
be the inverse of
\mathit{\Phi}^{(n)}
. For

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\exp\{-\lambda\tau_{0}^{(n)}\}\big]=\exp\{-x\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}(\lambda)\}.

Proof for Theorem
2.5
.

By the mutual independence among
\{Z^{(n)}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and Proposition
3.4
, for
\lambda\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{-\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{\zeta}^{(n)}(t)dt\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbf{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lambda}{n^{1+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}Z^{(n)}_{k}(t)dt\Big\}\Big]\Big)^{\lfloor c_{0}n^{\alpha}\zeta\rfloor}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lfloor c_{0}n^{\alpha}\zeta\rfloor}{c_{0}n^{\alpha}}V^{(n)}_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)\Big\},

where

\displaystyle V^{(n)}_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}n^{\alpha}\log\Big(1-\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(1-e^{-x\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}(\lambda/n^{1+\alpha})}\big)\Lambda^{*}(dx)\Big).

It is obvious that
V^{(n)}_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)\to V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)
as
n\to\infty
.
Since
n^{1+\alpha}{\it\Phi}^{(n)}(c_{0}\lambda/n)\to{\it\Phi}(\lambda)
as

n\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}(\lambda/n^{1+\alpha})\to c_{0}\mathit{\Psi}(\lambda)
. Thus by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle V^{(n)}_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle c_{0}n^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(1-e^{-x\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}(\lambda/n^{1+\alpha})}\big)\Lambda^{*}(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(1-e^{-x\cdot n\mathit{\Psi}^{(n)}(\lambda/n^{1+\alpha})}\Big)\frac{\alpha n^{\alpha+1}dx}{(1+nx)^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle c_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(1-e^{-x\cdot c_{0}\mathit{\Psi}(\lambda)}\Big)\frac{\alpha dx}{x^{1+\alpha}}.

A simple calculation induces that

V_{\mathbf{T}}(\lambda)=\Gamma(1-\alpha)c_{0}^{1+\alpha}|\varPsi(\lambda)|^{\alpha}=c\cdot|\varPsi(\lambda)|^{\alpha}.

\Box

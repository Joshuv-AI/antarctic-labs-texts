Speed of convergence of Chernoff approximations for two model examples:
heat equation and transport equation

Abstract




Abstract.
Paul Chernoff in 1968 proposed his approach to approximations of one-parameter operator semigroups while trying to give a rigorous mathematical meaning to Feynman’s path integral formulation of quantum mechanics. In early 2000’s Oleg Smolyanov noticed that Chernoff’s theorem may be used to obtain approximations to solutions of initial-value problems for linear partial differential equations (LPDEs) of evolution type with variable coefficients, including parabolic equations, Schrödinger equation, and some other. Chernoff expressions are explicit formulas containing variable coefficients of LPDE and the initial condition, hence they can be used as a numerical method for solving LPDEs. However, the speed of convergence of such approximations at the present time is understudied which makes it risky to employ this class of numerical methods.

In the present paper we take two equations with known solutions (heat equation and transport equation) and study both analytically and numerically the speed of decay of the norm of the difference between Chernoff approximations and exact solutions. We also provide graphical illustrations of convergence and its rate. These model examples, being relatively simple, allow to demonstrate general properties of Chernoff approximations. The observations obtained build a base for the future employment of the approach based on Chernoff’s theorem to the problem of construction of new numerical methods for solving initial-value problem for parabolic LPDEs with variable coefficients.

Keywords:
parabolic partial differential equation, Cauchy problem solution, Chernoff approximations, convergence speed, estimates of convergence rate, numerical experiment

Introduction

The Chernoff theorem
[
3
]
is an effective tool of functional analysis which allows to construct approximations to
C_{0}
-semigroups that, in turn, provide solutions to some evolution equations (e.g., heat equation, parabolic equation with variable coefficients, Schrödinger equation, etc.); standard textbooks on the topic are

If one finds so-called operator-valued Chernoff function then Chernoff’s theorem generates a sequence of functions, also referred to as Chernoff approximations, that converges to the solution of a Cauchy problem for the evolution equation. In many cases constructing Chernoff function is the only method that allows to express the solution in terms of both variable coefficients of the equation and the chosen initial condition.

At the time the subject area has a decent amount of data on different methods to construct Chernoff functions. For instance, the members of Smolyanov’s group implemented the use of integral operators to find the solutions to parabolic equations in a plethora of cases during the last 15 years (refer to pioneering papers
[
18
,

[

,

,

,

In turn, Schrödinger equation also belongs to the class of evolution equations, and the use of the same technique allows to represent the solution in the form of Feynman and quasi-Feynman integral formulas as well, see
[
15
,
13
]
.

When dealing with expressions provided by the Chernoff theorem, one might be interested in obtaining the estimate for the rate of decay of approximation error as
n
tends to infinity. Some results regarding this topic were already proposed for the case of Schrödinger equation in
[
11
]
. Similarly, the question of convergence speed arises for the Trotter product formula
e^{A+B}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(e^{A/n}e^{B/n})^{n}
which we do not exploit in current paper but which is a particular exemplar of Chernoff’s theorem for

(see recent papers
[

,

,

). In a less specific setting, other than within the framework of Chernoff’s theorem, one may refer to the results presented in
[
6
,
1
,
7
]
.

Nevertheless, this area is not well studied at the present time in general. Starting from
[
20
]
members of I.D. Remizov’s group examine the problem systematically. So, this paper is dedicated to the analysis of the convergence speed for some set of initial conditions and Chernoff functions. We suggest few analytic and computational methods in order to obtain the information on the order of approximation subspaces to which the examined model examples belong to as well as demonstrate the dependence of the order of the approximation on the choice of the initial condition. It is also shown on the model example that if the initial condition does not belong to the domain of the generator of the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup then the convergence speed can be slower than the one attained on the functions from the domain.

Conducting this study implies the use of basic methods of infinite-dimensional functional analysis and operator semigroups, as well as the use of Matlab software under student license in order to perform numerical experiments and visualise the results.

Let

-semigroup on a Banach space

-semigroup and denote

Definition 5

Let

•

closed

•

.

Remark
.

If operator
\overline{L}
exists then it is a linear closed operator extending operator

, and
L|_{dom(L)}=\overline{L}|_{dom(L)}

The next set of definitions is provided in the wording of I.D. Remizov

]

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Banach space, and
\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{F})
be a space of all linear bounded operators in
\mathcal{F}
. Let a mapping
G\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{F})
, or the same as a family of linear bounded operators
(G(t))_{t\geq 0}

\mathcal{F}
, also be given. Moreover, assume

is dense in
\mathcal{F}
and a linear operator

is closed. Then function

is called

1.

function

is strongly continuous, or the same as continuous in

endowed with the strong operator topology, i.e., the mapping

for all

2.

G(0)=I
, i.e., for all

we have

;

3.

there exists a dense linear subspace

such that for all

we have

\lim_{t\to+0}(G(t)\varphi-\varphi)/t\overset{denote}{=}G^{\prime}(0)f\in\mathcal{F};

4.

the closure of operator
(G^{\prime}(0),D)
exists and is equal to
(L,dom(L))
.

Remark
.

Conditions (CT3) and (CT4) together mean that there exists a core

for

.

Classical wording of the next theorem can be found in
[
3
,
5
]
. Based on the notion of Chernoff tangency we provide

Theorem 1

(The Chernoff theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Banach space, and
\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{F})
be a space of all linear bounded operators in
\mathcal{F}
. Let a mapping
G\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{F})
, or the same as a family of linear bounded operators
(G(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{F}
, also be given. Moreover, assume having a closed linear operator
L\colon dom(L)\subset\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
. Suppose that the following conditions are met:

(E).

there exists a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{tL})_{t\geq 0}
with generator
(L,dom(L))
;

(CT).

function
G
is Chernoff tangent to operator
L
;

(N).

there exists number
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\|G(t)\|\leq e^{\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Then for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
we have
(G(t/n))^{n}f\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}e^{tL}f
uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for any fixed

, i.e., for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
and each

we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}f-e^{tL}f\right\|=0.

(4)

Definition 7
.

If the above conditions hold then
G
is called a Chernoff function for
L
.

Conjecture 1

3
Exact formulas and estimates for convergence speed

During this section we will propose the results on the approximation speed for some model initial conditions and both one-dimensional transport and heat equations.

3.1
Transport equation

As a preliminary we will commence with the notion of the translation semigroup that gives the solution for one-dimensional transport equation. The examined system of equations is stated in

Definition 9
.

The system of equations defined as

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}_{t}(t,x)=u^{\prime}_{x}(t,x),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(5)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{0}\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
for all
t\geq 0
, is called a Cauchy problem for the first order partial differential equation on the real line, also referred to as the transport equation.

Remark
.

The solution of the system (
5
) and, hence, the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup is given by the formula
u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=u_{0}(x+t)
. This
C_{0}
-semigroup is called the translation semigroup.

First, let us verify that the generator of the translation semigroup is indeed the differentiation operator.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(G(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
C_{0}
-semigroup given by the formula
(G(t)f)(x)=f(x+t)
for
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and
t\geq 0
. Then its generator is differentiation operator
L=\frac{d}{dx}
, and its domain
dom(L)
is

UC^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})\overset{denote}{=}\left\{f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})\;|\;f^{\prime}\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Proof.

Using Definition
4
of the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup we get

dom(L)=\left\{f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})\;|\;\lim_{t\to+0}\left\|\frac{G(t)f-f}{t}-Lf\right\|=0\right\},

(6)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the standard supremum norm in
UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. Applying the definition of the
C_{0}
-semigroup, we obtain

\lim_{t\to+0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|=0.

(7)

Now, let us show that
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous. Take a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=0
and
t_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it follows that
\frac{f(x+t_{n})-f(x)}{t_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}f^{\prime}(x)
uniformly in
UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
norm for some
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since functions given by the formula
\frac{f(x+t_{n})-f(x)}{t_{n}}
are uniformly continuous for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Weierstrass theorem
f^{\prime}
is also uniformly continuous.

Next, we will show that
f^{\prime}
is bounded. By the definition of uniform continuity we have that

\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists\,N=N(\varepsilon)>0\>\colon\forall n\geq N\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\implies\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{f(x+t_{n})-f(x)}{t_{n}}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|<\varepsilon.

So we get
\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|<\left|\frac{f(x+t_{n})-f(x)}{t_{n}}\right|+\varepsilon
. Take
\varepsilon_{0}=1
then
f^{\prime}
is bounded.

Finally, let us show that if
f^{\prime}\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
then
\lim_{t\to-0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|=0
. For all
t<0
we have

\begin{split}\left|\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|&=\left|\frac{f(x-|t|)-f(x)}{-|t|}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|\\
&=\left|\frac{f(x)-f(x-|t|)}{|t|}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|\\
&\leq\left|\frac{f((x-|t|)+|t|)-f(x-|t|)}{|t|}-f^{\prime}(x-|t|)\right|+\left|f^{\prime}(x-|t|)-f^{\prime}(x)\right|.\end{split}

For small
|t|
the former term is small due to (
7
), and the latter one due to
f^{\prime}
being uniformly continuous. So, we get that

\lim_{t\to+0}\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}=f^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{t\to-0}\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}.

Hence, if the limit given by (
6
) exists, meaning
f\in dom(L)
, then
f\in UC^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand, if
f\in UC^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
then by mean value theorem

\left|\frac{f(x+t_{n})-f(x)}{t_{n}}-f^{\prime}(x)\right|=\left|f^{\prime}(x+\theta)-f^{\prime}(x)\right|<\varepsilon\;\mbox{for}\;|t|<\delta\;\mbox{and}\;0<\theta<t_{n},

i.e.,
f\in dom(L)
. So, we proved that
L
is generator of this semigroup and
dom(L)=UC^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Prior to the numerical studies we will find the composition degree of some Chernoff functions
G
for an arbitrary initial condition
u_{0}\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
be the Chernoff function such that for all
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
we have

(G(t)f)(x)=f(x+t+at^{k+1})

(8)

for some fixed
a,k>0
. Then its
n
-th composition degree is given by the formula

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=u_{0}\left(x+t+\frac{at^{k+1}}{n^{k}}\right).

(9)

Proof.

By the definition of Chernoff function we get

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=u_{0}\left(x+n\left(\frac{t}{n}+a\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{k+1}\right)\right)=u_{0}\left(x+t+\frac{at^{k+1}}{n^{k}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
.
∎

Analysis of convergence speed for
u_{0}=[x\to\sin(x)]

Now, let us check the convergence speed for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
and Chernoff function (
8
), the prototype of which was proposed in
[
20
]
. The main result of this paragraph is presented in the following

Theorem 5
.

Let
(e^{tL})_{t\geq 0}
be the translation semigroup on the real line, and
G
be the Chernoff function (
8
) for some fixed
a,k>0
. Then function
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to the approximation subspace of order
\frac{1}{n^{k}}
, and the error is given by the formula

\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|=2\left|\sin(at^{k+1}/2n^{k})\right|.

Proof.

Denote
\xi(n)=\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|
and
\tau=at^{k+1}/n^{k}
. Applying the definition of a supremum norm in
UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and using (
4
), we get

\begin{split}\xi(n)&=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sin(x+t+\tau)-\sin(x+t)\right|\\
&=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(\sin(x)\cos(t+\tau)+\cos(x)\sin(t+\tau))-(\sin(x)\cos(t)+\cos(x)\sin(t))\right|\\
&=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(\cos(t+\tau)-\cos(t))\sin(x)+(\sin(t+\tau)-\sin(t))\cos(x)\right|.\\
\end{split}

The above supremum can be found based on the following assumption: as
\sin
and
\cos
are bounded functions then the value of the supremum is reached in an extremum of the internal function. Let us now find an extreme point. We have that

(\cos(t+\tau)-\cos(t))\cos(x)-(\sin(t+\tau)-\sin(t))\sin(x)=0,

and

\tan(x)=\frac{\cos(t+\tau)-\cos(t)}{\sin(t+\tau)-\sin(t)}=\frac{-2\sin(t+\tau)\sin(\tau)}{2\cos(t+\tau)\sin(\tau)}=\tan(-(t+\tau)),

thus meaning that the extremum is reached at the point
x=-(t+\tau)
regardless of the period of
\tan
. So, we get

\begin{split}\xi(n)&=\left|(\sin(t+\tau)-\sin(t))\cos(t+\tau)-(\cos(t+\tau)-\cos(t))\sin(t+\tau)\right|\\
&=\left|2\cos(t+\tau)\cos(t+\tau)\sin(\tau)+2\sin(t+\tau)\sin(t+\tau)\sin(\tau)\right|\\
&=2\left|(\cos^{2}(t+\tau)+\sin^{2}(t+\tau))\sin(\tau)\right|\\
&=2\left|\sin(\tau)\right|.\\
\end{split}

Now, using Taylor expansion
\sin(z)=z-\frac{z^{3}}{3!}+O(z^{5})
when
z\to 0
, we finally get

\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|=2\sin(\tau)=\frac{at^{k+1}}{n^{k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{k+1}}\right)\;\mbox{when}\;n\to\infty,

which proves that
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to an approximation subspace of order
\frac{1}{n^{k}}
.
∎

Analysis of slow converging approximations for
u_{0}=[x\to\sin(x)]

At last, we will generalize Theorem
5
for an arbitrary function
w\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(e^{tL})_{t\geq 0}
be the translation semigroup on the real line, and
G
be the Chernoff function such that for all
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
we have

(G(t)f)(x)=f(x+t+t\cdot w(1/t)),

(10)

where
w\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}w(n)=0
. Then function
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to the approximation subspace of order
w
, and the error is given by the formula

\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|=2\left|\sin(t/2\cdot w(n/t))\right|.

Proof.

Similarly to Proposition
4
we first obtain
n
-th composition degree for Chernoff function
G
. We get

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=u_{0}\left(x+n\left(\frac{t}{n}+\frac{t}{n}\cdot w(n/t)\right)\right)=u_{0}\left(x+t+w(n/t)t\right).

(11)

It now suffices to set
\tau=t\cdot w(n/t)
and use the same technique as of Theorem
5
. Hence, since
w(n)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
, we have

\xi(n)=2\left|\sin(t/2\cdot w(n/t))\right|=\left|t\cdot w(n/t)+o\left(w^{2}(n)\right)\right|\;\mbox{as}\;n\to\infty,

which proves that
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to an approximation subspace of order
w
.
∎

Later in Section 4 we will check the convergence speed attained for the slow converging Chernoff function
G
given by (
10
) with
w(t)=\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}
for different values of
0<\gamma<1
applied to approximate the solution for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
.

3.2
Heat equation

The next differential equation we are going to analyse is the heat equation. Now, we will state the corresponding Cauchy problem and give an explicit formula for the solution in a form of a
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Definition 10
.

The system of equations defined the following way

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}_{t}(t,x)=Lu(t,x)=a^{2}u^{\prime\prime}_{xx}(t,x),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(12)

where
a>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u_{0}\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
for all
t\geq 0
, is called a Cauchy problem for the heat equation on the real line with constant coefficient of thermal conductivity.

Remark
.

The solution of the system (
12
), and, hence, the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup, is given by the Poisson integral

u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(t,x-y)u_{0}(y)dy,

(13)

where

\Phi(t,x)=\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right).

(14)

We will analyse the performance of approximations for the following three Chernoff functions:

\left(G(t)u_{0}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{4}u_{0}(x+2a\sqrt{t})+\frac{1}{4}u_{0}(x-2a\sqrt{t})+\frac{1}{2}u_{0}(x)

(15)

proposed by I. Remizov in
[
14
]
,

(G(t)u_{0})(x)=\frac{1}{6}u_{0}(x+a\sqrt{6t})+\frac{1}{6}u_{0}(x-a\sqrt{6t})+\frac{2}{3}u_{0}(x)

(16)

proposed by A. Vedenin in
[
20
]
, and

(G(t)u_{0})(x)=\frac{1}{30}u_{0}(x+a\sqrt{12t})+\frac{1}{30}u_{0}(x-a\sqrt{12t})+\frac{3}{10}u_{0}(x+a\sqrt{2t})+\frac{3}{10}u_{0}(x-a\sqrt{2t})+\frac{1}{3}u_{0}(x)

(17)

also proposed by A. Vedenin in oral communication to the author of the present paper in 2020. To simplify the complexity of computational algorithms one might need a general formula for the
n
-th composition degree for the above Chernoff functions. So we present the following

Proposition 7
.

Let
G
be the Chernoff function given by (
15
) for all
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and fixed
a>0
. Then its
n
-th composition degree is given by the formula

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum^{n}_{p=-n}\alpha_{p,n}u_{0}(x+2ap\sqrt{t/n}),

(18)

where for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\{-n,\dots,n\}
coefficients
\alpha_{p,n}
are defined as

\alpha_{p,n}=\sum^{\left[\frac{n-|p|}{2}\right]}_{k=0}C^{n}_{k}C^{k+|p|}_{n-k}2^{n-|p|-2k}.

Proof.

Consider
G(t)=\frac{1}{4}(S_{2a\sqrt{t}}+S_{-2a\sqrt{t}}+2I)
for all
t>0
, where
I
is an identity operator and
S_{\tau}
is a shift operator given by
(S_{\tau}f)(x)=f(x+\tau)
for some
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. It then yields that

\begin{split}G(t)^{n}_{t/n}&=\frac{1}{4^{n}}(S_{2a\sqrt{t/n}}+S_{-2a\sqrt{t/n}}+2I)^{n}\\
&=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{0\leq k+m\leq n}S^{k}_{2a\sqrt{t/n}}S^{m}_{-2a\sqrt{t/n}}2^{n-k-m}\frac{n!}{k!m!(n-k-m)!}\\
&=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{0\leq k+m\leq n}S_{2a(k-m)\sqrt{t/n}}2^{n-k-m}\frac{n!}{k!m!(n-k-m)!}.\end{split}

Now, set
k-m=p
with
-n\leq p\leq n
. We obtain

\begin{split}G(t)^{n}_{t/n}&=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum^{n}_{p=-n}\sum^{\left[\frac{n-|p|}{2}\right]}_{k=0}C^{k}_{n}C^{k+|p|}_{n-k}2^{n-|p|-2k}S_{2ap\sqrt{t/n}}\\
&=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum^{n}_{p=-n}\alpha_{p,n}S_{2ap\sqrt{t/n}},\end{split}

where
\alpha_{p,n}=\sum^{\left[\frac{n-|p|}{2}\right]}_{k=0}C^{n}_{k}C^{k+|p|}_{n-k}2^{n-|p|-2k}
.
∎

Proposition 8

Let

(19)

Analysis of convergence rate for the initial condition
u_{0}(x)=[x\to\sin(x)]

Let us now find the solution of the above Cauchy problem (
12
) for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
. Then the solution of the system (
12
) with initial condition
u_{0}
is given by the formula
u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=e^{-a^{2}t}\sin(x)
.

Proof.

Using (
13
) and substituting
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
, we get

u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\sin(y)dy.

Now, let us apply the following identity
\sin(x)=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}
. So, we obtain

u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2i}\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\exp(iy)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\exp(-iy)dy\right).\\

The former integral now takes the form

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2a\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y-(x+2a^{2}ti))^{2}-(x+2a^{2}ti)^{2}+x^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)dy&=\exp\left(-\frac{-(x+2a^{2}ti)^{2}+x^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)\\
&=\exp\left(ix-a^{2}t\right).\end{split}

Similarly, the latter integral is equal to
\exp\left(-ix-a^{2}t\right)
, i.e., the solution can now be expressed as

u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=e^{-a^{2}t}\left(\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}\right)=e^{-a^{2}t}\sin(x).

(20)

So, we found the solution of the Cauchy problem given by (
12
) for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
.
∎

Prior to analysing the convergence rate for Chernoff function (
15
) and
u_{0}=[x\to\sin(x)]
let us compute its
n
-th composition degree.

Proposition 10
.

Let
G
be the Chernoff function given by (
15
) for all
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and fixed
a>0
. Then its
n
-th composition degree for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)^{2n}\sin(x).

(21)

Proof.

Substituting the initial condition into (

\begin{split}\left(G(t)u_{0}\right)(x)&=\frac{1}{4}\sin(x+2a\sqrt{t})+\frac{1}{4}\sin(x-2a\sqrt{t})+\frac{1}{2}\sin(x)\\
&=\left(\frac{1+\cos(2a\sqrt{t})}{2}\right)\sin(x)\\
&=\cos^{2}(a\sqrt{t})\sin(x).\end{split}

So, for all
t\geq 0
operator
G(t)
is a function multiplication operator, meaning its
n
-th composition degree has the following form

\left(\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}\right)(x)=\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)^{2n}u_{0}(x)=\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)^{2n}\sin(x)

for all
t\geq 0
and

.
∎

Finally, let us find the convergence speed for
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
and the above derived Chernoff function (
21
). The main result of this subsection is presented in the next

Theorem 11
.

Let
(e^{tL})_{t\geq 0}
be the heat
C_{0}
-semigroup, and
G
be the Chernoff function given by (
15
) for all
f\in UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and fixed
a>0
. Then function
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to the approximation subspace of order
\frac{1}{n}
.

Proof.

Applying the definition of a standard norm in
UC_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, we get

\begin{split}\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|&=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)^{2n}\sin(x)-e^{-a^{2}t}\sin(x)\right|\\
&=\left|\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)^{2n}-e^{-a^{2}t}\right|\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sin(x)\right|.\end{split}

Now, let us exploit the following Taylor expansions:

\cos(z)=1-\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+O(z^{4}),

and

\ln(1+z)^{\alpha}=z-\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+O(z^{4}),

and

\exp(z)=1+z+\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+O(z^{3})

when
z\to 0
. So, we have

\begin{split}\left\|\left(G\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)^{n}u_{0}-e^{tL}u_{0}\right\|&=\left|e^{2n\ln\left(\cos\left(a\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)\right)}-e^{-a^{2}t}\right|\\
&=\left|-\frac{a^{4}t^{2}}{6n}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|,\;\mbox{when}\;n\to\infty.\end{split}

This proves that
u_{0}=[x\mapsto\sin(x)]
belongs to the approximation subspace of order
\frac{1}{n}
.
∎

Finding the exact solution for the initial condition
u_{0}=[x\to\exp(-|x|)]

Here we will find the solution of the Cauchy problem given by (
12

u_{0}=[x\mapsto e^{-|x|}]

Theorem 14
.

Suppose
u_{0}=[x\mapsto e^{-|x|}]
. Then the solution of the system (
12
) with initial condition
u_{0}
is given by the formula

u(t,x)=(e^{tL}u_{0})(x)=e^{t-x}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\erfc\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}-\sqrt{t}\right)\right)+e^{t+x}\frac{1}{2}\erfc\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}+\sqrt{t}\right),

(24)

where

\erfc(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{+\infty}_{x}e^{-y^{2}}dy.

(25)

Proof.

First, let us plug the formula giving the initial condition into the solution defined in (
13
) and (
14
). We have

\begin{split}u(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4t}}u_{0}(y)dy\\
&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4t}}e^{-|y|}dy.\end{split}

Now, change the variables within the integral by denoting
x-y=2z\sqrt{t}
. It then yields that
y=x-2z\sqrt{t}
and
dy=2\sqrt{t}dz
. So,

\begin{split}u(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}e^{-z^{2}}e^{-|x-2z\sqrt{t}|}2\sqrt{t}dz\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}}_{-\infty}e^{-(z^{2}-2z\sqrt{t}+x)}dz+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}}e^{-(z^{2}+2z\sqrt{t}-x)}dz\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}}_{-\infty}e^{-(z-\sqrt{t})^{2}+t-x}dz+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}}e^{-(z+\sqrt{t})^{2}+t+x}dz.\end{split}

Now, set
z-t=w
and
z+t=w
for the former respectively latter integral. Hence, we obtain

\begin{split}u(t,x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{t-x}\int^{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}-\sqrt{t}}_{-\infty}e^{-w^{2}}dw+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{t+x}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}+\sqrt{t}}e^{-w^{2}}dw\\
&=e^{t-x}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}-\sqrt{t}}e^{-w^{2}}dw\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{t+x}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}+\sqrt{t}}e^{-w^{2}}dw\\
&=e^{t-x}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\erfc\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}-\sqrt{t}\right)\right)+e^{t+x}\frac{1}{2}\erfc\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}+\sqrt{t}\right).\end{split}

Besides, as
\erfc(x)\xrightarrow{x\rightarrow+\infty}0
, then for each
t>0
one has
u(t,x)\sim e^{t-x}
as
x\to+\infty
and, moreover,
u(t,x)\sim e^{t-|x|}
as
x\to\infty
.
∎

belongs to the higher order approximation subspaces. We will examine later in this chapter that for the heat equation this is not the case already.

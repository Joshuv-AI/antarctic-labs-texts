Quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for classical capillarity problems
Giulio Pascale, Marco Pozzetta

Abstract
. We consider capillarity functionals which measure the perimeter of sets contained in a Euclidean half-space assigning a constant weight λ ∈ ( − 1 , 1 ) \lambda\in(-1,1) to the portion of the boundary that touches the boundary of the half-space. Depending on λ \lambda , sets that minimize this capillarity perimeter among those with fixed volume are known to be suitable truncated balls lying on the boundary of the half-space. We first give a new proof based on an ABP-type technique of the sharp isoperimetric inequality for this class of capillarity problems. Next we prove two quantitative versions of the inequality: a first sharp inequality estimates the Fraenkel asymmetry of a competitor with respect to the optimal bubbles in terms of the energy deficit; a second inequality estimates a notion of asymmetry for the part of the boundary of a competitor that touches the boundary of the half-space in terms of the energy deficit. After a symmetrization procedure, the quantitative inequalities follow from a novel combination of a quantitative ABP method with a selection-type argument.

Introduction

The classical isoperimetric problem in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
n\geq 2
, aims at minimizing the
(n-1)
-dimensional area of boundaries of sets having fixed finite volume. More precisely, given
v>0
, one aims to characterize minimizers to the problem

(1.1)

\inf\left\{P(E)\ :\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},|E|=v\right\},

where
P(E)
denotes the perimeter of
E
(see
Section

2.1
) and
|E|
the Lebesgue measure of
E
. It is well-known that balls (uniquely) minimize (
1.1
), cf.
[
De 58
]
or
[
Mag12
, Chapter 14]
, and this is encoded in the classical isoperimetric inequality

(1.2)

P(E)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
\omega_{n}
denotes the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
To prove a
quantitative
version of (
1.2
) means to estimate the distance of a competitor from the set of minimizers in terms of the energy deficit of the competitor with respect to the infimum of the problem. The first quantitative isoperimetric inequality for (
1.1
) with sharp exponents was proved in
[
FMP08
]
, and it reads

(1.3)

\alpha(E)^{2}\leq C(n)D(E),

where
\alpha(E)
and
D(E)
are respectively the Fraenkel asymmetry and the isoperimetric deficit of
E
, i.e.,

\alpha(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{E\Delta B(|E|,x)}{|E|}\ :\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}\qquad D(E):=\frac{P(E)-P(B(|E|))}{P(B(|E|))},

where
B(v,x)
denotes the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with volume
v
centered at
x
, for
v>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
B(v):=B(v,0)
.
The inequality (
1.3
) improves the previous non-sharp inequality proved in
[
Hal92
]
, after
[
HHW91
,
Fug89
]
.
We also mention
[

,

,

,

,

]
for further quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for possibly anisotropic perimeters,
[

In this paper we prove quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for the following classical capillarity problems.
If

If

More recently, in

The minimality of sets
B^{\lambda}(v,x)
for (
1.4
) comes with an isoperimetric inequality for

1.4
), we define the corresponding Fraenkel asymmetry and isoperimetric deficit by setting

\alpha_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(v,x)|}{v}:x\in\{x_{n}=0\}\right\},\qquad D_{\lambda}(E):=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))},

for any
E\subset\{x_{n}>0\}
with volume
|E|=v
. The infimum defining the asymmetry is, in fact, a minimum.

The first main result of the paper is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in(-1,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
.
There exists a constant
c_{\rm iso}=c_{\rm iso}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
with finite measure there holds

(1.6)

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{2}\leq c_{\rm iso}D_{\lambda}(E).

As for the classical (
1.2
), perturbing the boundary of an optimal bubble only inside the container
\{x_{n}>0\}
, it is possible to check that exponents in (
1.6
) are sharp.

In the context of these capillarity problems it is also spontaneous to consider a notion of asymmetry for the part of the boundary of a set that touches the half-plane
\{x_{n}=0\}
.
For a measurable set
E\subset\{x_{n}>0\}
, we define

\beta_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\cap\{x_{n}=0\}\,\,\Delta\,\,\partial^{*}B^{\lambda}(|E|,x)\cap\{x_{n}=0\}\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}B^{\lambda}(|E|,x)\cap\{x_{n}=0\}\right)}\ :\ x\in\{x_{n}=0\}\right\}.

The previous quantity measures the asymmetry of the set
\partial^{*}E\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
with respect to
(n-1)
-dimensional balls in
\{x_{n}=0\}
having volume equal to the one of the trace of the optimal bubble corresponding to the volume of
E
. We establish the following second quantitative isoperimetric inequality, that provides a quantitative estimate on
\beta_{\lambda}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda\in(-1,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
. There exists a constant
c^{\prime}_{\rm iso}=c^{\prime}_{\rm iso}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
with finite measure there holds

(1.7)

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq c^{\prime}_{\rm iso}\max\left\{D_{\lambda}(E),D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}\right\}.

Strategy of the proof and comments.

Observing that, roughly speaking, the minimization problem (
1.4
) is symmetric with respect to the first
n-1
axes, it is possible to adapt arguments in the spirit of
[
FMP08
]
to see that, in order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, it is sufficient to prove (
1.6
) in the class of Schwarz-symmetric sets, see
Corollary

4.9
. Here a set
E
is said to be Schwarz-symmetric (
Definition

2.1
) if the intersection
E\cap\{x_{n}=t\}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional ball in
\{x_{n}=t\}
centered at
(0,t)
, for any
t
.
However, we point out that it seems not possible to push the strategy of
[
FMP08
]
to the very end to prove
Theorem

1.1
. Indeed the arguments in
[
FMP08
]
require to Schwarz-symmetrize a competitor with respect to a preferred axis depending on the competitor, while in our case it is only possible to symmetrize with respect to the
n
-th axis. One finds an analogous obstruction also in a possible adaptation of the proof via symmetrization revised in
[
Mag08
]
(see also
[
Fus15
]
); in
[
Mag08
]
the quantitative isoperimetric inequality for Schwarz-symmetric sets is eventually obtained performing a quantitative version of Gromov’s proof
[
MS86
]
of the isoperimetric inequality, but again after having symmetrized a competitor with respect to a convenient axis.

The proof of (
1.6
) in the class of Schwarz-symmetric sets is achieved here with a new combination of the so-called selection principle
[
AFM13
,
CL12
]
with an Alexandrov–Bakelman–Pucci-type technique in the spirit of
[
Cin+22
]
.

In the recent
[
Cin+22
]
, the authors prove sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for a class of isoperimetric problems in cones where volume and perimeter are weighted in terms of a function satisfying suitable homogeneity and concavity properties.
The proof in
[
Cin+22
]
stems from the fact that the isoperimetric inequality for the corresponding problem was proved in
[
CRS16
]
by a so-called ABP argument.
The methods that go under the name of ABP techniques were originally employed to derive regularity estimates for second order elliptic equations
[
GT01
, Chapter 9]
and they were applied to give a new direct proof of the classical isoperimetric inequality in
[
Cab00
,
Cab08
]
(see
[
Cab17
]
for a detailed account on the method). More precisely, for the classical isoperimetric problem, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth connected open set, one would consider a solution
u
to

(1.8)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=\frac{P(E)}{|E|}&\text{ on }E,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=1&\text{ on }\partial E,\\
\end{cases}

where
\partial_{\nu}u
denotes outward normal derivative. It is immediate to check that
\nabla u(E^{\prime})\supset B_{1}(0)
where
E^{\prime}:=\{x\in E\ :\ \nabla^{2}u(x)\geq 0\}
, hence the area formula together with the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality readily imply the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality, indeed

\omega_{n}=|B_{1}(0)|\leq\int_{E^{\prime}}\det\nabla^{2}u\leq\int_{E}\left(\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right)^{n}=\frac{P(E)^{n}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}.

Now the rough idea is that a control on the energy deficit should control the "asymmetry" of the solution
u
with respect to the solution corresponding to the optimal shape
B_{1}(0)
, that is the radially symmetric parabola
|x|^{2}/2
. In fact, this is achieved in
[
Cin+22
]
by controlling the asymmetry of a
coupling
function which is defined as a suitable convex envelope of
u
.
Adapting arguments from
[
FMP10
]
, in
[
Cin+22
]
the authors then show that it is possible to employ trace-type theorems to estimate the asymmetry of a competitor set in terms of the asymmetry of the coupling function, which is in turn estimated by the energy deficit.

In
Theorem

3.3
below we will give an ABP proof of the isoperimetric inequality for the problem (
1.4
) by analyzing an elliptic problem analogous to (
1.8
), see (
3.3
). We are then in position to consider a coupling function as done in
[
Cin+22
]
and we can quantitatively estimate its asymmetry, which will be achieved in
Proposition

5.4
. Moreover, Schwarz-symmetric sets that are sufficiently small
C^{1}
-perturbations (in the sense of
Definition

5.10
) of an optimal bubble (
1.5
) readily verify the needed trace-type inequalities that relate asymmetry of the competitor with the asymmetry of the coupling. Hence this establishes the quantitative inequality (
1.6
) for Schwarz-symmetric

-perturbations of optimal bubbles, see
Corollary

5.11
. Observe that, in our setting, isoperimetric sets are just Lipschitz-regular and a set
E
is
C^{1}
-close to an optimal bubble if just the relative boundary
\partial E\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
is close to the relative boundary of an optimal bubble as
C^{1}
-hypersurfaces with boundary.

Once (
1.6
) is proved for
C^{1}
-perturbations of optimal bubbles (
Corollary

5.11
), we want apply a selection-type argument in the spirit of
[

,
CL12
]
in the class of Schwarz-symmetric sets in order to extend the validity of the quantitative inequality to the whole class of Schwarz-symmetric sets. In this way we also avoid the implementation of further technical results that in
[
FMP10
,
Cin+22
]
allow to reduce to just consider sets that enjoy the required trace-type inequalities.

Roughly speaking, in a selection-type argument one argues by contradiction assuming existence of sets contradicting the quantitative isoperimetric inequality and one uses such sets to define an auxiliary minimization problem, cf. (
5.22
). Minimizers to the previous problem still contradict the quantitative isoperimetric inequality, but at the same time they are shown to be small
C^{1}
-perturbations of some isoperimetric set, contradicting the inequality already proved for sets given by small perturbations of optimal ones.

In our case, we will prove that minimizers
E
to the auxiliary minimization problem are
C^{1}
-perturbations of optimal bubbles up to the boundary of the half-space
\{x_{n}>0\}
as a consequence of the classical interior regularity of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of the perimeter (
Definition

A.1
), see
[
Tam84
]
and
[
Mag12
, Chapter 26]
, together with a simple variational argument that allows us to propagate the regularity up to the boundary of the half-space
\{x_{n}>0\}
. This essentially follows from the fact that a Schwarz-symmetric local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer
E
in
\{x_{n}>0\}
is locally of class
C^{1}
and has bounded mean curvature (in a generalized sense, see
Lemma

A.2
); hence a uniform bound on the whole second fundamental form on a portion of boundary
\partial E\cap\{0<x_{n}<\varepsilon\}
follows just by showing that the set
\partial E\cap\{0<x_{n}<\varepsilon\}
is far from the axis of revolution of
E
(see
Lemma

A.4
), and the latter holds in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
by an energy estimate holding for minimizers to the auxiliary minimization problems.

We stress that in our case it is not clear how to apply a Fuglede-type argument
[
CL12
,
Fug89
]
to prove the quantitative inequality for sets given by small
C^{1}
-perturbations of optimal ones. Indeed, the classical Fuglede’s method relies on the precise knowledge of the eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami operator, which is not available for the operator on spherical caps corresponding to optimal bubbles (
1.5
) for generic
\lambda\in(-1,1)
. Moreover, observe that in our case it is not possible to globally parametrize
C^{1}
-close boundaries one on the other as normal graphs in general, introducing a further nontrivial technical difficulty in the implementation of a Fuglede-type argument.

2.1.
Sets of finite perimeter

We recall basic definitions and properties regarding sets of finite perimeter, referring to
[
AFP00
,
Mag12
]
for a complete treatment on the subject.
The perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in an open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(2.1)

P(E,A):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T(x)\,{\rm d}x\ :\ T\in C_{c}^{1}(A;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\,\|T\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

Denoting
P(E):=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter if
P(E)<+\infty
. In such a case, the characteristic function
\chi_{E}
has a distributional gradient
D\chi_{E}
that is a vector-valued Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T(x)\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T\,{\rm d}D\chi_{E},\quad\forall T\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

It can be proved that the set function
P(E,\cdot)
defined in (
2.1
) is the restriction of a nonnegative Borel measure to open sets. The measure
P(E,\cdot)
coincides with the total variation
|D\chi_{E}|
of the distributional gradient, and it is concentrated on the reduced boundary

\partial^{*}E:=\left\{x\in{\rm spt}|D\chi_{E}|\ :\ \exists\,\nu^{E}(x):=-\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))}{|D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))|}\text{ with }|\nu^{E}(x)|=1\right\}.

Introducing the sets of density
t\in[0,1]
points for
E
defined by

E^{(t)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ \lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=t\right\},

we have that the reduced boundary coincides both with
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{(1)}\cup E^{(0)})
and with the set
E^{(1/2)}
up to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible sets. The vector
\nu^{E}
is called the generalized outer normal of
E
.
Moreover
P(E,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
, and the distributional gradient can be written as
D\chi_{E}=-\nu^{E}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
.

We conclude by recalling the definition of Schwarz symmetrization and of Schwarz-symmetric set.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set. Then its Schwarz symmetrization (with respect to the
n
-th axis) is the set

E^{*}:=\{(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\ :\ \omega_{n-1}|x^{\prime}|^{n-1}<\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\}),\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

A Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be Schwarz-symmetric if it coincides with its Schwarz symmetrization, up to negligible sets.

2.2.
Preliminary results on the capillarity functional

In this section we prove some preparatory results on the functional
P_{\lambda}
. From now on,
H
denotes the closed half-space
H:=\{x_{n}\leq 0\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a measurable set. We observe that

P_{\lambda}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}1-\lambda\Braket{e_{n},\nu^{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\nu^{E}
is the generalized outer normal to
E
. In particular, since
|\lambda|<1
, we have that
P_{\lambda}(E)\geq 0
. The previous identity follows from the divergence theorem, indeed

0=\int_{E}\text{div}\,e_{n}\,{\rm d}x=-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)+\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}\Braket{e_{n},\nu^{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

We now aim at proving an approximation result for sets of finite perimeter contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
by sequences of sets having smooth boundary relative in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
. We will employ the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with finite measure. For any
t\geq 0
define the diffeomorphism
\varphi_{t}:\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\to\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
given by
\varphi_{t}(x^{\prime},x_{n}):=(x^{\prime},x_{n}(1+te^{-|x^{\prime}|^{2}}))
, where we wrote
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
for any
x\in\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

(1)

for at most countably many
t\in[0,+\infty)
there holds

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left\{x\in\partial^{*}(\varphi_{t}(E))\setminus H\ :\ \nu^{\varphi_{t}(E)}(x)=\pm e_{n}\right\}\right)>0;

(2)

for at most countably many
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
there holds

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left\{x\in\partial^{*}E\ :\ \nu^{E}(x)=\pm\nu\right\}\right)>0.

Proof.

We begin by proving (1).
Define
f_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
by
f_{t}(x^{\prime}):=1+te^{-|x^{\prime}|^{2}}
,
and let

\begin{split}A_{t}:=\Big\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\;&{\rm with}\;x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\{0\}\ :\ \nu^{E}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\text{ is proportional to }(x_{n}\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),f_{t}(x^{\prime}))\Big\}.\end{split}

We claim that, if
s
,
r>0
, with
s\neq r
, then
A_{r}\cap A_{s}=\emptyset
.
Indeed, if
A_{r}\cap A_{s}\neq\emptyset
, there exist
(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{x}_{n})\in\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
and
\bar{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(\bar{x}_{n}\nabla f_{r}(\bar{x}^{\prime}),f_{r}(\bar{x}^{\prime}))=\bar{\alpha}(\bar{x}_{n}\nabla f_{s}(\bar{x}^{\prime}),f_{s}(\bar{x}^{\prime})).

Since
x_{n}>0
and
x^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
\bar{x}_{n}\nabla f_{r}(\bar{x}^{\prime})=\bar{\alpha}\bar{x}_{n}\nabla f_{s}(\bar{x}^{\prime})
implies
r=\bar{\alpha}\,s
. Thus
f_{r}(\bar{x}^{\prime})=\bar{\alpha}f_{s}(\bar{x}^{\prime})
implies
1=\bar{\alpha}
, which in turn implies
r=s
, contradiction.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
let
M_{k}:=\left\{t>0\ :\ \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A_{t})>1/k\right\}
.
Since
A_{r}\cap A_{s}=\emptyset
for
r\neq s
and since
P(E)<+\infty
, it follows that
M_{k}
is finite for any
k\geq 1
, and thus
\left\{t>0\ :\ \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A_{t})>0\right\}=\bigcup_{k}M_{k}
is countable.

Since the differential of
\varphi_{t}
is represented by the matrix

{\rm d}\varphi_{t}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}&0\\
x_{n}\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime})&f_{t}(x^{\prime})\end{pmatrix},

requiring that
\nu^{\varphi_{t}(E)}(\varphi_{t}(x))=\pm e_{n}
for some
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
means that

\nu^{E}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\text{ is proportional to }(x_{n}\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),f_{t}(x^{\prime})),

see
[
Mag12
, Proposition 17.1]
and
[
AFP00
, Proposition 2.88]
.
Therefore for any
t\not\in\cup_{k}M_{k}
there holds

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left\{x\in\partial^{*}(\varphi_{t}(E))\setminus H\ :\ \nu^{\varphi_{t}(E)}(x)=\pm e_{n}\right\}\right)=0.

The proof of (2) is analogous, noticing that
N_{k}:=\left\{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\ :\ \mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left\{x\in\partial^{*}E\ :\ \nu^{E}=\nu\right\}\right)>1/k\right\}
is finite for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, hence
\cup_{k}N_{k}
is at most countable.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Approximation with regular sets)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with finite measure. Then there exists a sequence of sets
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that

(1)

E_{i}
is a bounded set such that
\partial E_{i}\setminus\partial H
is a smooth hypersurface (possibly with smooth boundary) such that either
\partial E_{i}\cap\partial H=\emptyset
or
\partial E_{i}\setminus\partial H
intersects
\partial H
orthogonally;

(2)

E_{i}\to E
in
L^{1}
,
P(E_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\to P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial E_{i}\cap\partial H)\to\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)
, as
i\to+\infty
.

In particular,
P(E_{i})\to P(E)
and
P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\to P_{\lambda}(E)
as
i\to+\infty
, for any
\lambda\in(-1,1)
;

(3)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E_{i}\ :\ \nu^{E_{i}}(x)=\pm e_{j}\})=0
for any
j=1,\ldots,n-1
;

(4)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E_{i}\setminus H\ :\ \nu^{E_{i}}(x)=\pm e_{n}\})=0
.

Proof.

Since
P(E),|E|<+\infty
, by a diagonal argument we can assume without loss of generality that
E
is bounded.

Step 1.
We first construct a sequence
F_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that 1. and 2. hold with
F_{i}
in place of
E_{i}
.
Let us denote by
F
the union of
E
with the reflection of
E
with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=0\}
. There exists a sequence of smooth sets
\tilde{F}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, symmetric with respect to
\{x_{n}=0\}
, such that they converge to
F
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
P(\tilde{F}_{i})\to P(F)
(see, e.g.,
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.42]
). The fact that
\tilde{F}_{i}
is symmetric with respect to
\{x_{n}=0\}
follows as
\tilde{F}_{i}
can be obtained as superlevel set of a convolution of
\chi_{F}
with a symmetric mollifier.
In particular, if
\partial\tilde{F}_{i}\cap\partial H\neq\emptyset
, then
\partial\tilde{F}_{i}
intersects
H
orthogonally, and thus
\partial\tilde{F}_{i}\cap\partial H
is a smooth
(n-2)
-dimensional manifold.
Let
F_{i}:=\tilde{F}_{i}\setminus H
.
Then
F_{i}\to E
in
L^{1}
and

P(F_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)=\frac{1}{2}P(\tilde{F}_{i})\to\frac{1}{2}P(F)=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H).

Let us define the function

\begin{split}&f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)\\
&f(v)=|v|-\lambda\Braket{e_{n},v}.\end{split}

Note that
f
is continuous; moreover
f(tv)=tf(v)
for any
t\geq 0
and
f
is convex.
Let us set

\begin{split}&\mu_{i}:=\nu^{F_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\partial^{*}F_{i}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H))\\
&\mu:=\nu^{E}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)).\end{split}

Since
\nu^{F_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}F_{i}\to\nu^{E}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
weakly
∗
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\mu_{i}\to\mu
weakly
∗
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
.
Since we already know that
|\mu_{i}|(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\to|\mu|(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
, by Reshetnyak continuity theorem (see, e.g.,
[
AFP00
, Theorem 2.39]
) we deduce
P_{\lambda}(F_{i})=\int f(\mu_{i}/|\mu_{i}|)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{i}|\to\int f(\mu/|\mu|)\,{\rm d}|\mu|=P_{\lambda}(E)
.

Step 2.
Let us consider the flow
\varphi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(x^{\prime},x_{n})

\displaystyle:=(x^{\prime},x_{n}(1+te^{-|x^{\prime}|^{2}})),

for
t\geq 0
.
By
Lemma

2.3
for a.e.
t
we have that
\varphi_{t}(F_{i})
satisfies (4).
Moreover, for any
t\geq 0
there exists a sequence of rotations
\mathcal{R}_{j,t}:(x^{\prime},x_{n})\mapsto(R_{j,t}(x^{\prime}),x_{n})
along the

-th axis converging to the identity such that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{j,t}(\varphi_{t}(F_{i})):\nu^{\mathcal{R}_{j,t}(\varphi_{t}(F_{i}))}(x)=\pm e_{l}\})=0

for all
l=1
,
\dots
,
n-1
. By a diagonal argument, since
\varphi_{t}(F_{i})
maintains orthogonal intersection with

, one extract the desired sequence

.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with finite measure. Then

P_{\lambda}(E)\geq\frac{1-\lambda}{2}P(E).

Proof.

We can assume that
E
is as in the conclusion of
Lemma

2.4
. The claim readily follows from the fact that the orthogonal projection on
\partial H
is
1
-Lipschitz and surjective from
\partial E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
onto
\partial E\cap\partial H
, recalling that
1
-Lipschitz maps do not increase the Hausdorff measure (see, e.g.,
[
AFP00
, Proposition 2.49]
).
∎

3.
Isoperimetric inequality for
P_{\lambda}
, Fraenkel asymmetry and deficit

3.1.
Isoperimetric inequality via ABP method

In this section we give a proof of the isoperimetric inequality for the capillarity functional
P_{\lambda}
exploiting an ABP method. We start by computing the capillarity perimeter of bubbles
B^{\lambda}(v)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There holds
P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))=n\lvert B^{\lambda}\rvert^{\frac{1}{n}}v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
, for any
v\geq 0
and
\lambda\in(-1,1)
.

Proof.

By scale invariance, it is sufficient to prove that
P(B^{\lambda},\{x_{n}>\lambda\})-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{n}=\lambda\})
is equal to
n|B^{\lambda}|
. Indeed, let
u(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
. Then

\begin{split}n|B^{\lambda}|=\int_{B^{\lambda}}\Delta u=P(B^{\lambda},\{x_{n}>\lambda\})+\int_{\partial B^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{n}=\lambda\}}\Braket{-e_{n},x}=P(B^{\lambda},\{x_{n}>\lambda\})-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{n}=\lambda\}).\end{split}

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a connected open set such that
\partial E\setminus H
is a smooth hypersurface with boundary that intersects
\partial H
orthogonally. Then the Neumann problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{\lvert E\rvert}\qquad&\text{in}\quad E,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=1&\text{on}\quad\partial E\setminus\partial H,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=-\lambda&\text{on}\quad\partial E\cap\partial H,\end{cases}

has a solution
u\in C^{1}(\overline{E})\cap C^{\infty}(E)
.

Indeed, existence of a weak solution of (
3.1
) follows by classical arguments exploiting the Riesz representation theorem.
By
[
Nit11
, Proposition 3.6]
there exists
\gamma>0
such that every weak solution is in
C^{0,\gamma}(E)
.
Hence we can apply
[
Lie88
, Theorem 1]
to the equivalent problem

\begin{cases}\Delta u-u=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{\lvert E\rvert}-u=:f\qquad&\text{in}\quad E,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=1&\text{on}\quad\partial E\setminus\partial H,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=-\lambda&\text{on}\quad\partial E\cap\partial H\end{cases}

getting that a weak solution is in fact
C^{1}(\overline{E})
.

Theorem 3.3

(Isoperimetric inequality for
P_{\lambda}
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with
|E|\in(0,+\infty)
. Then

(3.2)

\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\geq\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Moreover, equality occurs in (
3.2
) if and only if
E=B^{\lambda}(|E|)
up to a translation and up to negligible sets.

Proof.

We just give a proof of the inequality (
3.2
) here, referring to
[
Mag12
, Theorem 19.21]
for an alternative proof comprising the characterization of minimizers.

By the standard isoperimetric inequality, we can assume that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)>0
. By
Lemma

2.4
, we can further assume that
E
is a bounded set such that
\partial E\setminus\partial H
is smooth and intersects
\partial H
orthogonally.

Let us further assume for the moment that
E
is connected.
Let
u
be the solution of the Neumann problem

(3.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{\lvert E\rvert}\qquad&\text{in}\quad E,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=1&\text{on}\quad\partial E\setminus\partial H,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=-\lambda&\text{on}\quad\partial E\cap\partial H,\end{cases}

where
\partial u/\partial\nu
denotes the outer normal derivative of
u
on
\partial E
.
Observe that such a solution exists and
u\in C^{1}(\overline{E})\cap C^{\infty}(E)
(see
Remark

3.2
).
We consider the “lower contact set” of
u
defined by

(3.4)

\Gamma_{u}:=\left\{x\in E:u(y)\geq u(x)+\Braket{\nabla u(x),y-x}\;\text{for all}\;y\in\overline{E}\right\}.

We claim that

(3.5)

B^{\lambda}\subset\nabla u(\Gamma_{u}).

To show (
3.5
), take any
p\in B^{\lambda}
. Let
x\in\overline{E}
be a point such that

\min_{y\in\overline{E}}\left\{u(y)-\Braket{p,y}\right\}=u(x)-\Braket{p,x}.

If
x\in\partial E\setminus\partial H
then the exterior normal derivative of
u(y)-\Braket{p,y}
at
x
would be nonpositive and hence
(\partial u/\partial\nu)(x)\leq\lvert p\rvert<1
, a contradiction with (
3.3
). Similarly, if
x\in\partial E\cap\partial H
then
(\partial u/\partial\nu)(x)\leq\Braket{p,-e_{n}}<-\lambda
, a contradiction with (
3.3
). It follows that
x\in E
and, therefore, that
x
is an interior minimum of the function
u(y)-\Braket{p,y}
over
\overline{E}
. In particular
p=\nabla u(x)
and
x\in\Gamma_{u}
, hence Claim (
3.5
) is now proved.

From (
3.5
), since
u\in C^{\infty}(E)
and
\Gamma_{u}\subset E
, we can apply the area formula on
\nabla u
to deduce

(3.6)

\lvert B^{\lambda}\rvert\leq\lvert\nabla u(\Gamma_{u})\rvert=\int_{\nabla u(\Gamma_{u})}\,{\rm d}p\leq\int_{\Gamma_{u}}|\text{det}\,\nabla^{2}u(x)|\,{\rm d}x.

Since points
x\in\Gamma_{u}
are interior minima for
y\mapsto u(y)-\Braket{\nabla u(x),y}
, then
\nabla^{2}u(x)
is positively semi-definite. Hence by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality

|\text{det}\,\nabla^{2}u|=\text{det}\,\nabla^{2}u\leq\left(\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right)^{n}\qquad\text{in }\Gamma_{u}.

Hence

|B^{\lambda}|\leq\int_{\Gamma_{u}}\text{det}\,\nabla^{2}u\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\Gamma_{u}}\left(\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{E}\left(\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x,

since
\Delta u\equiv P_{\lambda}(E)/|E|
.
Plugging in the value of
\Delta u
, the claimed inequality follows.

It remains to consider the case when
E
is not connected, hence when
E
is a disjoint union of finitely many bounded sets
E_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
, such that
\partial E_{i}\setminus\partial H
is smooth and intersects
\partial H
orthogonally.
Summing over
i=1,\ldots,k
the isoperimetric inequality that we just proved for
P_{\lambda}
on each component
E_{i}
, and exploiting the subadditivity of
t\mapsto t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
, the final inequality follows.
∎

3.2.
Asymmetry and deficit

We now define the Fraenkel asymmetry with respect to optimal bubbles
B^{\lambda}(v,x)
and the deficit corresponding to the functional
P_{\lambda}
, proving some preliminary properties on these quantities.

Definition 3.4

(Fraenkel asymmetry)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a Borel set with measure
|E|=v\in(0,+\infty)
. We define

\alpha_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(v,x)|}{v}:x\in\{x_{n}=0\}\right\}.

It is readily checked that the Fraenkel asymmetry of
E
is a minimum.

Definition 3.5

(Isoperimetric deficit)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a Borel set with measure
|E|=v\in(0,+\infty)
. We define

D_{\lambda}(E):=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))}.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists
\bar{c}=\bar{c}(n,\lambda)>0
such that if a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
satisfies
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{c}
then
P(E,\partial H)>0
.

Proof.

If
E
is a Borel set such that
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{c}
and
P(E,\partial H)=0
, then the standard isoperimetric inequality together with
Lemma

3.1
imply

\begin{split}n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}&\leq P(E)=P_{\lambda}(E)<(1+\bar{c})P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|))=n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}(1+\bar{c})|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

Since
|E|>0
for
\bar{c}
small enough, we get a contradiction if
\bar{c}
is sufficiently small.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
E
are sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with finite measure such that
E_{i}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
.
Then

\liminf_{i\to+\infty}P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\geq P_{\lambda}(E),\qquad\liminf_{i\to+\infty}D_{\lambda}(E_{i})\geq D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Exploiting
Remark

2.2
and Reshetnyak lower semicontinuity theorem (see, e.g.,
[
AFP00
, Theorem 2.38]
), one easily checks that
P_{\lambda}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
convergence (see the proof of
Lemma

2.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
E
are sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with finite measure such that
|E|>0
and such that
E_{i}\to E
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
.
Then

\lim_{i\to+\infty}\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{i})=\alpha_{\lambda}(E)

Proof.

The claim is a standard exercise that follows from the fact that the
L^{1}
-convergence holds on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

If
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that
|E_{i}\setminus K|=0
for any
i
for some compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and if

\begin{split}&\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|E_{i}|+P_{\lambda}(E_{i})<\infty,\end{split}

then there exists
E
of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
and
i_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
such that

E_{i_{k}}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}E\qquad\liminf_{k}P_{\lambda}(E_{i_{k}})\geq P_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

By
Corollary

2.5
we have that
P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\geq\frac{1-\lambda}{2}P(E)
. Then
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}P(E_{i})<\infty
. Hence by classical precompactness of sets of finite perimeter (see, e.g.,
[
Mag12
, Theorem 12.26]
) and recalling
Lemma

3.7
, the claim follows.
∎

4.
Reduction to bounded symmetric sets

In the following arguments, in order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, we will repeatedly reduce ourselves to consider sets
E
having isoperimetric deficit smaller than some chosen constant.
This reduction is always possible.

Indeed, let
\delta>0
be some positive constant; if
E
is a set of finite perimeter such that
D_{\lambda}(E)\geq\delta
, since
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq 2
, we immediately get

\begin{split}&\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\leq\frac{4}{\delta}\delta\leq\frac{4}{\delta}D_{\lambda}(E).\end{split}

Therefore, if
Theorem

1.1
is proved on sets with deficit
\leq\delta
, then it is proved for any set.

Hence,

within this section we will assume that
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{c}
for any competitor
E
involved, where
\bar{c}
is given by
Lemma

3.6
.

In particular
P(E,\partial H)>0
.

4.1.
Reduction to bounded sets

In this section we prove that, in order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, it is sufficient to prove the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (
1.6
) among suitably uniformly bounded sets.

From now on, we shall denote
Q_{l}:=[-l,l]^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We start by proving an estimate on the area of horizontal slices of a set in terms of its deficit.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a bounded set of finite perimeter such that
\partial E\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a smooth hypersurface (possibly with smooth boundary) with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and such that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E\setminus H:\nu^{E}(x)=\pm e_{n}\})=0
. Then

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\frac{|E\cap\{x_{n}<t\}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E)\right),

for every
t>0
. In particular

(4.2)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E)\right).

Moreover, if
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a set of finite perimeter with
|F|=|B^{\lambda}|
, then (
4.1
) holds with
F
in place of
E
for almost every
t>0
, and (
4.2
) holds with
F
in place of
E
.

The estimates given by
Lemma

4.1
are clearly nontrivial only when the deficit is sufficiently small. On the other hand, if the deficit
D_{\lambda}(E)
is sufficiently small, since
\omega_{n}/|B^{\lambda}|>1
, (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) essentially yield a quantitative version of
Lemma

3.6
, nontrivial also for slices
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})
with
t>0
as long as
|E\cap\{x_{n}<t\}|
is small.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Let
v_{E}(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})
for any
t>0
, and let
g(t):=|E\cap\{x_{n}<t\}|/|B^{\lambda}|
. By the standard isoperimetric inequality we have that

(4.3)

\begin{split}P(E,\{x_{n}>t\})+v_{E}(t)&=P(E\cap\{x_{n}>t\})\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|E\cap\{x_{n}>t\}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}=P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}(1-g(t))^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\end{split}

for any
t>0
. Moreover, for any
t>0
, we observe that for any
x^{\prime}\in\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H
, the halfline
[0,t]\ni x_{n}\mapsto(x^{\prime},x_{n})
either intersects
\partial^{*}E\cap\{0<x_{n}\leq t\}
or it intersects
E\cap\{x_{n}=t\}
. Therefore

(4.4)

\begin{split}P(E,\{0<x_{n}\leq t\})+v_{E}(t)&\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H),\end{split}

for any
t>0
.
Hence we conclude that

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(1+D_{\lambda}(E))&=P_{\lambda}(E)=P(E,\{x_{n}>t\})+P(E,\{0<x_{n}\leq t\})-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzStimaTaglio2}}{\geq}P(E,\{x_{n}>t\})-v_{E}(t)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzStimaTaglio1}}{\geq}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}(1-g(t))^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-2v_{E}(t),\end{split}

for any
t>0
, which yields (
4.1
). By
[
Mag12
, Theorem 18.11]
(see also
[
FMP08
, Theorem 6.1]
), the function
v_{E}
belongs to
W^{1,1}(0,+\infty)
, thus (
4.2
) follows by letting
t\to 0^{+}
in (
4.1
).

Now if
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is as in the assumptions, let
E_{i}
be given by
Lemma

2.4
applied to
F
, and let
\tilde{E}_{i}:=(|B^{\lambda}|/|E_{i}|)^{\frac{1}{n}}E_{i}
. Hence the inequality (
4.2
) and the right hand side of (
4.1
) applied with
E=\tilde{E}_{i}
pass to the limit as
i\to\infty
. Moreover

|\tilde{E}_{i}\Delta E|=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\tilde{E}_{i}\Delta E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})\,{\rm d}t\geq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\tilde{E}_{i}\cap\{x_{n}=t\})-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})\right|\,{\rm d}t.

Since
|\tilde{E}_{i}\Delta E|\to 0
, the left hand side of (
4.1
) passes to the limit as well as
i\to\infty
, for a.e.
t>0
.
∎

We are ready to prove the claimed reduction to bounded sets. The proof follows the line of
[
FMP08
, Lemma 5.1]
, essentially truncating a competitor with coordinate slabs having estimated width. To give a bound for the truncation in the
n
-th direction will need to modify the argument in
[
FMP08
, Lemma 5.1]
and we will exploit
Lemma

4.1
.

Lemma 4.2

(Reduction to bounded sets)
.

There exist
l=l(n,\lambda)>0
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\lambda)>0
such that, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a set of finite perimeter with
|E|\in(0,+\infty)
, then there exists a set of finite perimeter
E^{\prime}\subset Q_{l}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
such that
|E^{\prime}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and

(4.5)

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})+C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E),\qquad D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

By scale-invariance of the asymmetry and of the deficit, it is sufficient to prove the claim assuming also
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
.
First of all we observe that we may prove the claim assuming that
\partial E\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is smooth and

(4.6)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H:\nu^{E}(x)=\pm e_{i}\})=0

for all
i=1
,
\dots
,
n
. Indeed, if
E
is a generic set of finite perimeter, then by
Lemma

2.4
there exists a sequence of smooth sets
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
E
such that (
4.6
) holds. If we know that the claim holds for
E_{i}
, we get the existence of
E_{i}^{\prime}\subset Q_{l}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
such that (
4.5
) holds with
E,E^{\prime}
replaced by
E_{i},E^{\prime}_{i}
. Hence we can apply
Corollary

3.9
on the sequence
E_{i}^{\prime}
, and by
Lemma

3.7
and
Lemma

3.8
the inequalities (
4.5
) pass to the limit.

Without loss of generality, we can further assume that

(4.7)

D_{\lambda}(E)<(2^{1/n}-1)/4.

Let us consider the axis
x_{1}
first. Thanks to (
4.6
), by
[
Mag12
, Theorem 18.11]
(see also
[
FMP08
, Theorem 6.1]
) we deduce that

v_{E}(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(t,x^{\prime})\in E\})\qquad\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R}

belongs to
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
, hence we may assume that
v_{E}
is continuous. Setting

E_{t}^{-}:=\{x\in E:x_{1}<t\}

and

P_{\lambda}(E,\{x_{1}<t\}):=P(E,\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H:x_{1}<t\})-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H:x_{1}<t\})

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by smoothness of
E
we have that

(4.8)

P_{\lambda}(E_{t}^{-})=P_{\lambda}(E,\{x_{1}<t\})+v_{E}(t),\qquad P_{\lambda}(E\setminus E_{t}^{-})=P_{\lambda}(E,\{x_{1}>t\})+v_{E}(t),

where
P_{\lambda}(E,\{x_{1}>t\})
is defined analogously.
Let us now define the function
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
given by

g(t):=\frac{|E_{t}^{-}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}.

Hence
g
is a nondecreasing
C^{1}
function with
g^{\prime}(t)=v_{E}(t)/|B^{\lambda}|
. Let
-\infty\leq a<b\leq+\infty
be such that
\{t:0<g(t)<1\}=(a,b)
. If
t\in(a,b)
, then by (
3.2
) we have

P_{\lambda}(E_{t}^{-})\geq g(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}).

Similarly,

P_{\lambda}(E\setminus E_{t}^{-})\geq(1-g(t))^{\frac{n-1}{n}}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}).

Therefore, from (
4.6
) and (
4.8
) we get that

P_{\lambda}(E)+2v_{E}(t)\geq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(g(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+(1-g(t))^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)

for all
t\in(a,b)
. Since by definition we have
P_{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(1+D_{\lambda}(E))
, we obtain

(4.9)

v_{E}(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(g(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+(1-g(t))^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E)\right).

Let us now define the concave function

\begin{split}\psi:[0,1]\to[0,+\infty)\qquad\qquad\psi(t):=t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+(1-t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1.\end{split}

Note that
\psi(0)=\psi(1)=0
and
\psi
achieves its maximum at
\psi(1/2)=2^{1/n}-1
, hence by concavity

(4.10)

\psi(t)=\psi\left(2t\frac{1}{2}+0\right)\geq 2t\psi(1/2)+0=2(2^{1/n}-1)t\qquad\forall t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right].

Recall that by (
4.7
) there holds
2D_{\lambda}(E)<\psi(1/2)
. Let
a<t_{1}<t_{2}<b
be such that
g(t_{1})=1-g(t_{2})
and
\psi(g(t_{1}))=\psi(g(t_{2}))=2D_{\lambda}(E)
. Then

(4.11)

\psi(g(t))\geq 2D_{\lambda}(E)\qquad\forall t\in(t_{1},t_{2})

and, by (
4.10
),

(4.12)

g(t_{1})=1-g(t_{2})\leq\frac{D_{\lambda}(E)}{2^{1/n}-1}.

For any
t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}
we have

(4.13)

\begin{split}v_{E}(t)&\overset{\eqref{fmp:5.6}}{\geq}\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(\psi(g(t))-D_{\lambda}(E))=\frac{1}{4}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\psi(g(t))+\frac{1}{4}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(\psi(g(t))-2D_{\lambda}(E))\\
&\overset{\eqref{fmp:5.8}}{\geq}\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|}{4}\psi(g(t)).\end{split}

Since
v_{E}(t)=|B^{\lambda}|g^{\prime}(t)
, we have

(4.14)

t_{2}-t_{1}\overset{\eqref{fmp:5.10}}{\leq}\frac{4}{n}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\psi(g(t))}\,{\rm d}t=\frac{4}{n}\int_{g(t_{1})}^{g(t_{2})}\frac{1}{\psi(s)}\,{\rm d}s\leq\frac{4}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\psi(s)}\,{\rm d}s=:\alpha,

for some
\alpha=\alpha(n)>0
.

Let

\begin{split}&\tau_{1}=\max\left\{t\in(a,t_{1}]:v_{E}(t)\leq\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E)}{2}\right\},\\
&\tau_{2}=\min\left\{t\in[t_{2},b):v_{E}(t)\leq\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E)}{2}\right\}.\end{split}

Note that
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are well defined since
v_{E}
is continuous and
v_{E}(t)\to 0
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
; moreover, by (
4.11
) and (
4.13
),
v_{E}(\tau_{1})=v_{E}(\tau_{2})=\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E)}{2}
. Moreover, from (
4.12
) and by definition of
\tau_{1}
, we have

t_{1}-\tau_{1}\leq\frac{2}{n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E)}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{t_{1}}v_{E}(t)\,{\rm d}t=\frac{2}{nD_{\lambda}(E)}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{t_{1}}g^{\prime}(t)\,{\rm d}t\leq\frac{2g(t_{1})}{nD_{\lambda}(E)}\leq\frac{2}{n(2^{1/n}-1)},

and an analogous estimate holds for
\tau_{2}-t_{2}
.

We consider the truncation
\tilde{E}:=E\cap\{x:\tau_{1}<x_{1}<\tau_{2}\}
. From the above estimate and (
4.14
), we have that
\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}<\beta
for some
\beta=\beta(n)>0
. Moreover by (
4.8
) and (
4.12
), by the definition of
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
, and since
P(E,\{x_{1}<\tau_{1},x_{1}>\tau_{2}\})\geq\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H\cap\{x_{1}<\tau_{1},x_{1}>\tau_{2}\})
(see the proof of
Corollary

2.5
) we can estimate

(4.15)

|\tilde{E}|\geq\left|B^{\lambda}\right|\left(1-2\frac{D_{\lambda}(E)}{2^{1/n}-1}\right),\qquad\qquad P_{\lambda}(\tilde{E})\leq P_{\lambda}(E)+n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E).

We finally define

\sigma:=\left(\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{|\tilde{E}|}\right)^{1/n},\qquad\qquad E^{\prime}:=\sigma\tilde{E}.

Clearly,
|E^{\prime}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and by (
4.15
) we get that
E^{\prime}
is contained in a strip
\{\tau_{1}^{\prime}<x_{1}<\tau_{2}^{\prime}\}
, with
\tau_{2}^{\prime}-\tau_{1}^{\prime}\leq\sigma(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})\leq l^{\prime}
, where
l^{\prime}=l^{\prime}(n,\lambda)>0
. Let us now show that
E^{\prime}
satisfies (
4.5
) for a suitable constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\lambda)>0
that may change from line to line. To this aim, since we are assuming
D_{\lambda}(E)
small by (
4.7
), from (
4.15
) we get that
1\leq\sigma\leq 1+C_{0}D_{\lambda}(E)
, with
C_{0}=C_{0}(n)
. Thus, from (
4.15
) and (
4.7
), we get

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})&=\sigma^{n-1}P_{\lambda}(\tilde{E})\leq\sigma^{n-1}(P_{\lambda}(E)+n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E))\\
&=\sigma^{n-1}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(1+2D_{\lambda}(E))\leq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})(1+C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E)).\end{split}

Hence, the second inequality in (
4.5
) follows.
To prove the first inequality, let us denote by
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,p)
, with
p\in\partial H
, a spherical cap such that
\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})=\frac{|E^{\prime}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,p)|}{|B^{\lambda}|}
.
From the first inequality in (
4.15
), recalling that
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
, we then get

\begin{split}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)&\leq\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,p/\sigma)|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\\
&\leq\frac{|E\Delta\tilde{E}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}+\frac{|\tilde{E}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|/\sigma^{n},p/\sigma)|}{|B^{\lambda}|}+\frac{|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|/\sigma^{n},p/\sigma)\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,p/\sigma)|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\\
&=\frac{|E\setminus\tilde{E}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}+\frac{\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})}{\sigma^{n}}+\frac{|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|/\sigma^{n})|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\\
&\overset{\eqref{fmp:5.12}}{\leq}C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E)+\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})+C_{1}(\sigma-1)\\
&\leq\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})+C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E).\end{split}

Thus the set
E^{\prime}
satisfies (
4.5
) and points in
E^{\prime}
have first coordinate contained in an interval of length bounded by
l^{\prime}
.

Starting from
E^{\prime}
, we can repeat the same construction finitely many times with respect to the axes
x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}
, thus getting a new set, still denoted by
E^{\prime}
, satisfying (
4.5
).

It remains to adapt the construction with respect to the coordinate axis
x_{n}
. In this case we eventually aim at truncating the set
E^{\prime}
in some controlled slab of the form
\{0<x_{n}<\bar{\tau}_{2}\}
. Define

\bar{v}(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(x^{\prime},t)\in E^{\prime}\}),\qquad\bar{E}_{t}^{-}:=\{x\in E^{\prime}:x_{n}<t\},\qquad\bar{g}(t):=\frac{|\bar{E}_{t}^{-}|}{|B^{\lambda}|},

for
t>0
. It is readily checked that, arguing as above, one estimates

(4.16)

\bar{v}(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\psi(\bar{g}(t))-D_{\lambda}(E)\right),

which is analogous to (
4.9
), for any
t
such that
\bar{g}(t)\in(0,1)
. Similarly as before, we define
0<\bar{t}_{1}<\bar{t}_{2}
such that
\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{1})=1-\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{2})
and
\psi(\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{1}))=\psi(\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{2}))=2D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})
. Therefore, using (
4.16
) and the concavity of
\psi
, arguing as before one estimates

(4.17)

\bar{v}(t)\geq\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|}{4}\psi(\bar{g}(t))\qquad\qquad\forall\,t\in[\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2}],

which is analogous to (
4.13
).

Let

A:=\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}-1\right)>0.

We claim that there exists
\bar{\varepsilon}=\bar{\varepsilon}(n,\lambda)>0
such that if
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{\varepsilon}
then

(4.18)

\bar{v}(t)\geq\frac{A}{2}\qquad\text{ for a.e. $t\in(0,\bar{t}_{1})$.}

Indeed, since
D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq C_{1}D_{\lambda}(E)
and
\psi(\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{1}))=2D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})
, then for any
\omega>0
there is
\bar{\varepsilon}=\bar{\varepsilon}(n,\lambda)>0
such that
\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{1})<\omega
whenever
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{\varepsilon}
. Applying
Lemma

4.1
with
F=E^{\prime}
, for almost every
t\in(0,\bar{t}_{1})
we find

(4.19)

\begin{split}\bar{v}(t)&\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\bar{g}(t)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\bar{g}(\bar{t}_{1})\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\omega\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-C_{1}\bar{\varepsilon}\right)\geq\frac{A}{2},\end{split}

provided
\bar{\varepsilon}
is small enough.

Therefore, assuming without loss of generality that
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{\varepsilon}
, since
\bar{g}^{\prime}(t)=\bar{v}(t)/|B^{\lambda}|
we estimate

(4.20)

\begin{split}\bar{t}_{2}\overset{\eqref{fmp:5.10'}}{\leq}\bar{t}_{1}+\frac{4}{n}\int_{\bar{t}_{1}}^{\bar{t}_{2}}\frac{\bar{g}^{\prime}(t)}{\psi(\bar{g}(t))}\,{\rm d}t\overset{\eqref{eq:ClaimFette}}{\leq}\frac{2}{A}\int_{0}^{\bar{t}_{1}}\bar{v}(t)\,{\rm d}t+\frac{4}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\psi}\leq\frac{2|B^{\lambda}|}{A}+\frac{4}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\psi}=:\alpha^{\prime}(n,\lambda).\end{split}

The rest of the construction follows analogously as above by defining

\bar{\tau}_{2}:=\min\left\{t\geq\bar{t}_{2}\ :\ \bar{g}(t)<1,\,\,\bar{v}(t)\leq\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})}{2}\right\},

estimating
\bar{\tau}_{2}-\bar{t}_{2}\leq\beta^{\prime}(n,\lambda)
, hence finally taking the set

(4.21)

\left(\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{|E^{\prime}\cap\{x_{n}<\bar{\tau}_{2}\}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,(E^{\prime}\cap\{x_{n}<\bar{\tau}_{2}\}).

Up to translation along
\partial H
, the set defined in (
4.21
) yields the final one satisfying the claim of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 4.3

(Non-quantitative stability)
.

For any
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
\bar{\delta}=\bar{\delta}(n,\lambda,\bar{\varepsilon})>0
such that if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a Borel set such that
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{\delta}
, then
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

By scale-invariance of the asymmetry and of the deficit, it is sufficient to prove the claim assuming also
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
.
We argue by contradiction.
Suppose there exist a number
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
and a sequence of sets
\{E_{i}\}_{i}
, with
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
and
|E_{i}|=|B^{\lambda}|
, such that
D_{\lambda}(E_{i})<\frac{1}{i}
and
\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{i})>\bar{\varepsilon}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let us consider the sequence of sets
\{E_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i}
, with
E_{i}^{\prime}\subset Q_{l}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
and
|E_{i}^{\prime}|=|B^{\lambda}|
, given by
Lemma

4.2
.
Moreover the Lemma assures that
\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{\prime})>\bar{\varepsilon}/2
for large
i
, and
D_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{\prime})\to 0
.
Since each set
E_{i}^{\prime}
is contained in the same
Q_{l}
, by
Corollary

3.9
we can assume, up to a subsequence, that
E_{i}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}E^{\prime}
for some set
E^{\prime}
of finite perimeter with
|E^{\prime}|=|B^{\lambda}|
.
By the lower semicontinuity of the perimeters we get
P_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
, hence
E^{\prime}=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)
for some
x\in\partial H\cap Q_{l}
by uniqueness of minimizers. The convergence of
E_{i}^{\prime}
to
E^{\prime}
implies that
|E_{i}^{\prime}\Delta E^{\prime}|\to 0
, against the assumption
\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{\prime})>\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

There exist
A_{\lambda},T_{\lambda},\eta>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
\left||E|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\leq\eta
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\eta
there holds

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})\geq A_{\lambda},

for almost every
t\in(0,T_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

Let us prove the inequality assuming
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
first.
Fix
T_{\lambda}^{\prime}>0
such that

\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\frac{|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)\cap\{x_{n}<T_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\geq 1+a,

for some
a>0
.
By
Corollary

4.3
, for any
\omega>0
there is
\eta>0
such that if
D_{\lambda}(E)<\eta
then
|E\cap\{x_{n}<T_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}|\leq|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)\cap\{x_{n}<T_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}|+\omega
. For such a set
E
,
Lemma

4.1
implies that for almost every
t\in(0,T_{\lambda}^{\prime})
there holds

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{x_{n}=t\})&\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\frac{|E\cap\{x_{n}<t\}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-D_{\lambda}(E)\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1-\frac{|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)\cap\{x_{n}<T_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}|+\omega}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-1-\eta\right).\end{split}

Hence for sufficiently small
\eta>0
the right hand side in the previous estimate is bounded below by some constant
A_{\lambda}^{\prime}(n,\lambda)>0
.

For a generic set
E
such that
\left||E|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\leq\eta
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\eta
, the set
E^{\prime}=\left(|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}/|E|^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)E
has measure equal to
|B^{\lambda}|
and deficit
D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq\eta
. Up to decreasing
\eta>0
, applying the first part of the proof to
E^{\prime}
, the desired estimate holds on
E
for
T_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}^{\prime}/2
and
A_{\lambda}=A_{\lambda}^{\prime}/2
.
∎

4.2.
Reduction to
(n-1)
-symmetric sets

In this section we prove that, in order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, it is sufficient to further reduce to show (
1.6
) among
(n-1)
-symmetric sets, i.e., sets which are symmetric with respect to reflection across
n-1
orthogonal hyperplanes, each one orthogonal to
\{x_{n}=0\}
. The results are analogous to
[
Mag08
, Section 6]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a Borel set with finite measure, symmetric with respect to
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
orthogonal half-hyperplanes
H_{j}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H:\Braket{x,\nu_{j}}=0\right\}
for
1\leq j\leq k
, where
|\nu_{j}|=1
and
\Braket{\nu_{j},e_{n}}=0
for any
1\leq j\leq k
. Then

(4.22)

\min_{x\in\partial H}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|,x)|\leq\min_{y\in\partial H\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}H_{j}}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|,y)|\leq 3\min_{x\in\partial H}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|,x)|.

Proof.

We can suppose for simplicity that
\forall j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
we have
\nu_{j}=e_{j}
. If
x^{0}=(x^{0}_{1},\dots,x^{0}_{n-1},0)
is such that
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)
is achieved by
B^{\lambda}(|E|,x^{0})
, then
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)
is achieved also by
B^{\lambda}(|E|,\bar{x}^{0})
, where
\bar{x}^{0}=(-x^{0}_{1},\dots,-x_{k}^{0},x^{0}_{k+1},\dots,

x^{0}_{n-1},0)
. Since
|B^{\lambda}(|E|,x^{0})\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|)|\leq|B^{\lambda}(|E|,x^{0})\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|,\bar{x}^{0})|
we have

\begin{split}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|)|&\leq|E\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)|+|B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)\Delta B^{\lambda}(|E|)|\\
&\leq|E\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)|+|B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,\bar{x}^{0}\right)|\\
&\leq|E\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)|+|B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)\Delta E|+|E\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,\bar{x}^{0}\right)|\\
&=3|E\Delta B^{\lambda}\left(|E|,x^{0}\right)|.\qed\end{split}

Given a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with finite measure and a unit vector
\nu
with
\Braket{\nu,e_{n}}=0
, we denote by
H^{+}_{\nu}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\Braket{x,\nu}>t\}
an open half-space orthogonal to
\nu
where
t\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen in such a way that

|E\cap H_{\nu}^{+}|=\frac{|E|}{2}.

We also denote by
r_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
the reflection with respect to
H_{\nu}:=\partial H_{\nu}^{+}
, and by
H_{\nu}^{-}:=r_{\nu}(H_{\nu}^{+})
the open half-space complementary to
H_{\nu}^{+}
. Finally we write
E_{\nu}^{\pm}:=E\cap H_{\nu}^{\pm}
.

Observe that

(4.23)

D_{\lambda}(E^{\pm}_{\nu}\cup r_{\nu}(E_{\nu}^{\pm}))\leq 2D_{\lambda}(E).

Indeed

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E^{\pm}_{\nu}\cup r_{\nu}(E_{\nu}^{\pm}))-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|))&\leq 2P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(E^{\mp}_{\nu}\cup r_{\nu}(E_{\nu}^{\mp}))-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|))\\
&=2(P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|)))+P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|))-P_{\lambda}(E_{\nu}^{\mp}\cup r_{\nu}(E_{\nu}^{\mp}))\\
&\leq 2(P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|E|))),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the isoperimetric inequality of
Theorem

3.3
.

Lemma 4.6
.

There exist
\bar{C}_{2},\bar{\delta}_{2}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a Borel set with finite measure such that
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{\delta}_{2}
, and if
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are two orthogonal vectors, with
\Braket{\nu_{i},e_{n}}=0
, such that
H_{\nu_{1}}
and
H_{\nu_{2}}
divide
E
in four parts of equal measure, then there exist
i\in\{1,2\}
and
s\in\{+,-\}
such that, setting
E^{\prime}=E^{s}_{\nu_{i}}\cup r_{\nu_{i}}(E^{s}_{\nu_{i}})
, there holds

(4.24)

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{C}_{2}\alpha(E^{\prime}).

Proof.

By scale-invariance of Fraenkel asymmetry, it is sufficient to prove the claim assuming also
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. If
i\in\{1,2\}
and
s\in\{+,-\}
, let
E^{{}^{\prime}s}_{\nu_{i}}
denote the sets obtained by reflecting
E^{s}_{\nu_{i}}
along
H_{\nu_{i}}
and let
B_{i}^{\lambda,s}=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x_{i}^{s})
be four spherical caps such that

|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}s}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,s}|=\min_{x\in H_{\nu_{i}}\cap\partial H}|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}s}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|.

For
i=1,2
, by the triangular inequality we have

(4.25)

\begin{split}\min_{x\in\partial H}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|&\leq|E\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+}|\\
&=|(E\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+})\cap H_{\nu_{i}}^{+}|+|(E\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+})\cap H_{\nu_{i}}^{-}|\\
&\leq|(E\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+})\cap H^{+}_{\nu_{i}}|+|(E\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-})\cap H^{-}_{\nu_{i}}|+|(B_{i}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-})\cap H_{\nu_{i}}^{-}|\\
&=\frac{1}{2}|E^{{}^{\prime}+}_{\nu_{i}}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+}|+\frac{1}{2}|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-}|+\frac{1}{2}|B_{i}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-}|.\end{split}

Once we show that if
D_{\lambda}(E)
is sufficiently small then there exists
c_{n,\lambda}>0
such that at least one the following

(4.26)

\begin{split}&|B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|\leq 2c_{n,\lambda}\Big(|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|\Big)\\
&|B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|\leq 2c_{n,\lambda}\Big(|E_{\nu_{2}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{2}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|\Big)\end{split}

holds, then we soon conclude the proof.
Indeed, assume for example that the first inequality in (
4.26
) holds.
Then, from (
4.22
) and (
4.25
) with
i=1
, we get

\begin{split}\min_{x\in\partial H}|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|&\overset{\eqref{dispense:4.22}}{\leq}(c_{n,\lambda}+1/2)\Big(|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|\Big)\\
&\overset{\eqref{dispense:4.16}}{\leq}3(c_{n,\lambda}+1/2)\Big(\min_{x\in\partial H}|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|+\min_{x\in\partial H}|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|\Big),\end{split}

thus proving (
4.24
) with
\bar{C}_{2}=6(c_{n,\lambda}+1/2)
and
E^{\prime}
equal to
E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}+}
or
E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}-}
.

Observe that, given
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
,
Corollary

4.3
, (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) imply that there exists
\bar{\delta}_{2}(n,\lambda)>0
such that if
D_{\lambda}(E)<\bar{\delta}_{2}
then

(4.27)

\max\left\{\alpha_{\lambda}(E),\frac{|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}\pm}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,\pm}|}{|B^{\lambda}|}\ :\ i=1,2\right\}<\tilde{\varepsilon}.

Thanks to (
4.27
), we can show that the caps
B_{i}^{\lambda,\pm}
get closer and closer to the optimal ones for
E
, as
\bar{\delta}_{2}
decreases.

Indeed, let us assume by contradiction that there exists
\eta>0
such that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
E_{j}
, with
|E_{j}|=|B^{\lambda}|
,
D_{\lambda}(E_{j})<\frac{1}{j}
, with
B^{\lambda}_{j}:=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x_{j})
realizing the asymmetry of
E_{j}
, but for
i\in\{1,2\}
and
s\in\{+,-\}
, if
B^{\lambda,s}_{i,j}:=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x_{i,j}^{s})
is such that

|E^{\prime s}_{j,\nu_{i}^{j}}\Delta B^{\lambda,s}_{i,j}|=\min_{x\in H_{\nu_{i}^{j}}\cap\partial H}|E^{\prime s}_{j,\nu_{i}^{j}}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)|,

where
E^{\prime s}_{j,\nu_{i}^{j}}
is given by reflections of truncations of
E_{j}
along orthogonal subspaces
H_{\nu_{1}^{j}},H_{\nu_{2}^{j}}
, then
|x_{i,j}^{s}-x_{j}|>\eta
for some
i\in\{1,2\},s\in\{+,-\}
and any
j
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that that
i=1
and
s=+
.

Let us translate every set in the above contradiction assumption by
-x_{j}
. Without relabeling the objects involved, up to subsequences, we have that
E_{j}\to B^{\lambda}_{0}:=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)
in
L^{1}
. We can show that
E^{\prime+}_{j,\nu_{1}^{j}}\to B^{\lambda}_{0}
as well.
Indeed, up to a rotation we can further assume that

H_{\nu_{1}^{j}}=\left\{(a_{j},0,\dots,0)+e_{1}^{\perp}\right\},\qquad\nu_{1}^{j}=e_{1},\qquad\forall\,j,

with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By the definition of
H_{\nu_{1}^{j}}
, we have

\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{2}=\frac{|E_{j}|}{2}=|E_{j}\cap\{x_{1}>a_{j}\}|.

Then
\{a_{j}\}
is bounded and, up to subsequence, converges to
a_{\infty}=0
because, if
a_{\infty}\neq 0
, then

E_{j}\cap\{x_{1}>a_{j}\}\to B_{0}^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{1}>a_{\infty}\}

with
\left|B_{0}^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{1}>a_{\infty}\}\right|\neq\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{2}
.
In particular
E_{j,\nu_{1}^{j}}^{\prime+}\to B^{\lambda}_{0}
.
Finally by (
4.27
)

\tilde{\varepsilon}|B^{\lambda}|\geq\lim_{j}\left|E^{\prime+}_{j,\nu_{1}^{j}}\Delta B_{1,j}^{\lambda,+}\right|\geq\min_{x\in\partial H,|x|\geq\eta}\left\{|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)\Delta B^{\lambda}_{0}\right\}=:C(\eta)>0.

But for
j
sufficiently large, since
D_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to 0
, by (
4.27
) we can choose
\tilde{\varepsilon}
such that
\tilde{\varepsilon}|B^{\lambda}|<C(\eta)/2
, getting a contradiction.

We observe that for
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
sufficiently small, that is for
\bar{\delta}_{2}>0
sufficiently small, there exists
c_{n,\lambda}>0
such that for all possible choices of
s,t\in\{+,-\}
there holds

(4.28)

|(B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t})\cap(H_{\nu_{1}}^{s}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{t})|>\frac{|B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t}|}{c_{n,\lambda}}.

We only sketch the argument for (
4.28
). Letting
Q:=(H_{\nu_{1}}^{s}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{t})
and
B_{1}(h):=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,h\,x_{1}^{s}),B_{2}(h):=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,h\,x_{2}^{t})
for
h\in[0,1]
, one can compute

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\big|(B_{1}(h)&\Delta B_{2}(h))\cap Q\big|=\int_{\partial B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h)\cap Q}\Braket{\nu^{B_{1}(h)},x_{2}^{t}-x_{1}^{s}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial B_{2}(h)\cap B_{1}(h)\cap Q}\Braket{\nu^{B_{2}(h)},x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\left(\int_{\partial B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h)\cap Q}\left\langle\nu^{B_{1}(h)},\frac{x_{2}^{t}-x_{1}^{s}}{|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|}\right\rangle+\int_{\partial B_{2}(h)\cap B_{1}(h)\cap Q}\left\langle\nu^{B_{2}(h)},\frac{x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}}{|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|}\right\rangle\right)|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|\\
&=\int_{\partial(B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h))\cap Q}\left\langle\nu^{B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h)},v_{12}^{s,t}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|\\
&\geq c|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|,\end{split}

where
v_{12}^{s,t}
is obviously defined, provided
\tilde{\varepsilon}
is small enough, for some
c=c(n,\lambda)>0
that will change from line to line.
The last estimate follows since
\left\langle\nu^{B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h)},v_{12}^{s,t}\right\rangle\geq 0
pointwise and, for
\tilde{\varepsilon}
small, centers
x_{1}^{s},x_{2}^{t}
are so close that
\left\langle\nu^{B_{1}(h)\cap B_{2}(h)},v_{12}^{s,t}\right\rangle
can be estimated from below by a positive constant on a set of
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measure uniformly bounded from below away from zero. On the other hand one can estimate

|(B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t})|\leq c|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|.

Hence

|(B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t})\cap(H_{\nu_{1}}^{s}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{t})|=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}\big|(B_{1}(h)\Delta B_{2}(h))\cap Q\big|\,{\rm d}h\geq c|x_{1}^{s}-x_{2}^{t}|\geq c|(B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t})|,

and (
4.28
) follows.

Letting

S_{1}=(B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\cap H_{\nu_{1}}^{+})\cup(B_{1}^{\lambda,-}\cap H_{\nu_{1}}^{-}),\quad S_{2}=(B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{+})\cup(B_{2}^{\lambda,-}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{-}),

we deduce

|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|\geq|(S_{1}\Delta S_{2})\cap(H_{\nu_{1}}^{s}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{t})|=|(B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t})\cap(H_{\nu_{1}}^{s}\cap H_{\nu_{2}}^{t})|>\frac{|B_{1}^{\lambda,s}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,t}|}{c_{n,\lambda}}.

In particular we have

(4.29)

\begin{split}&|B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|\leq|B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,+}|+|B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|<2c_{n,\lambda}|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|,\\
&|B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|\leq|B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,+}|+|B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|<2c_{n,\lambda}|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|.\end{split}

If by contradiction (
4.26
) were false, then

(4.30)

|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|<\frac{|B_{1}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{1}^{\lambda,-}|}{2c_{n,\lambda}}\quad\text{and}\quad|E_{\nu_{2}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{2}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|<\frac{|B_{2}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{2}^{\lambda,-}|}{2c_{n,\lambda}}.

Hence

\begin{split}|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|&\leq|S_{1}\Delta E|+|E\Delta S_{2}|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,+}|+|E_{\nu_{i}}^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-}|\right)\overset{\eqref{dispense:4.24}}{<}\frac{1}{4c_{n,\lambda}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}|B_{i}^{\lambda,+}\Delta B_{i}^{\lambda,-}|\overset{\eqref{eq:zzaw}}{\leq}|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|,\end{split}

getting a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.7

(Reduction to
(n-1)
-symmetric sets)
.

There exist
C_{2},\delta_{2}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that, if
E
is a Borel set with
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
,
E\subset Q_{l}
,
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\delta_{2}
, there exists a Borel set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
,
F\subset Q_{2l}
,
|F|=|B^{\lambda}|
, symmetric with respect to
n-1
orthogonal half-hyperplanes (each orthogonal to
\partial H
) and such that

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq C_{2}\alpha_{\lambda}(F),\qquad D_{\lambda}(F)\leq 2^{n-1}D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Let us define
\delta_{2}:=\bar{\delta}_{2}2^{-(n-2)}
, where
\bar{\delta}_{2}
is the constant appearing in
Lemma

4.6
.
We can apply
Lemma

4.6

n-2
times to different pairs of orthogonal vectors in
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-2}\}
normal to corresponding pairs of affine hyperplanes splitting the measure of
E
in two halves. Therefore, also recalling (
4.23
), we find an
(n-2)
-symmetric set
E^{\prime}
such that
|E^{\prime}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{C}_{2}^{n-2}\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime}),\quad D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq 2^{n-2}D_{\lambda}(E).

To perform the last symmetrization, let us consider a half-hyperplane
H_{n-1}
orthogonal to
e_{n-1}
and dividing
E^{\prime}
into two parts of equal measure. For simplicity let us assume that
H_{n-1}=\{x_{n-1}=0\}\setminus H
. We denote by
E^{{}^{\prime}+}
(resp.
E^{{}^{\prime}-}
) the set obtained by the union of
E^{\prime}\cap\{x_{n-1}>0\}
(resp.
E^{\prime}\cap\{x_{n-1}<0\}
) with its reflection along
H_{n-1}
.
By (
4.23
) we have

D_{\lambda}(E^{{}^{\prime}\pm})\leq 2D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq 2^{n-1}D_{\lambda}(E).

Regarding the asymmetry of
E^{{}^{\prime}\pm}
note that since
E^{\prime}
is symmetric with respect to the first
n-2
coordinate hyperplanes,
E^{{}^{\prime}+}
and
E^{{}^{\prime}-}
are
(n-1)
-symmetric.
By
Lemma

4.5
we get

\begin{split}|B^{\lambda}|\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})&\leq|E^{\prime}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)|\\
&=|(E^{\prime}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))\cap\{x_{n-1}>0\}|+|(E^{\prime}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))\cap\{x_{n-1}<0\}|\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Big(|E^{{}^{\prime}+}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)|+|E^{{}^{\prime}-}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)|\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{3|B^{\lambda}|}{2}\Big(\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{{}^{\prime}+})+\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{{}^{\prime}-})\Big).\end{split}

Therefore at least one of the sets
E^{{}^{\prime}+},E^{{}^{\prime}-}
has asymmetry greater than
\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})
and, denoting by
F
this set, we have

\begin{split}&D_{\lambda}(F)\leq 2D_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq 2^{n-1}D_{\lambda}(E)\\
&\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\bar{C}_{2}^{n-2}\alpha_{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq 3\bar{C}_{2}^{n-2}\alpha_{\lambda}(F).\end{split}

Finally, the inclusion
F\subset Q_{2l}
follows since
F
was obtained by performing reflections of
E\subset Q_{l}
along affine hyperplanes of the form
\{x_{j}=a_{j}\}
for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
with
a_{j}\in(-l,l)
.
∎

4.3.
Reduction to Schwarz-symmetric sets

In this section we observe that, in order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, it is sufficient to further reduce to show (
1.6
) just among Schwarz-symmetric sets. The proof is analogous to
[
Fus15
, Proposition 4.9]
.

Lemma 4.8

(Reduction to Schwarz-symmetric sets)
.

There exists
C_{3}=C_{3}(n,\lambda)>0
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. Suppose that
E
is symmetric with respect to the coordinate hyerplanes
\{x_{1}=0\},\ldots,\{x_{n-1}=0\}
and that

D_{\lambda}(E)<1,\qquad E\subset Q_{2l},

where
l=l(n,\lambda)
is as in in
Lemma

4.2
.
Then

(4.31)

|E\Delta E^{*}|\leq C_{3}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{\lambda}(E^{*})\leq D_{\lambda}(E),

where
E^{*}
denotes the Schwarz symmetrization of
E
with respect to the
n
-th axis.

Proof.

The second inequality in (
4.31
) follows from the fact
|E^{*}|=|E|
and
P_{\lambda}(E^{*})

\leq

P_{\lambda}(E)
.
Exploiting Lemma
2.4
, we may assume

(4.32)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial^{*}E\setminus H\ :\ \nu^{E}(x)=\pm e_{n}\})=0,

and thus that
v_{E}\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Indeed, if
E_{i}
is given by
Lemma

2.4
and the claim is proved for
E_{i}
, by the contractivity of Schwartz rearrangement (
[
Mag12
, Exercise 19.14]
), for every
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
and with
i
sufficiently large, we get

\begin{split}|E\Delta E^{*}|&\leq|E\Delta E_{i}|+|E_{i}\Delta E_{i}^{*}|+|E_{i}^{*}\Delta E^{*}|\leq 2|E\Delta E_{i}|+|E_{i}\Delta E_{i}^{*}|\leq 2\tilde{\varepsilon}+C_{3}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E_{i})},\end{split}

and the last term tends to
C_{3}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}
as
i\to\infty
.

For
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(0,\infty)
denote

\begin{split}&v_{E}(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ (x^{\prime},t)\in E\}),\\
&p_{E}(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(\partial^{*}\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ (x^{\prime},t)\in E\}),\end{split}

and employ analogous notation for
E^{*}
.
Since
|\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H|=|\partial^{*}E^{*}\cap\partial H|
, we get

P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\geq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(E^{*})=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)-P(E^{*},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H).

It is therefore possible to reproduce the computation in
[
Fus15
, Proposition 4.9]
verbatim up to
[
Fus15
, Eq. (4.29)]
to estimate the right hand side in the last equation from below. We include the computation for the convenience of the reader. By
[
Mag12
, Theorem 19.11, (19.30)]
one has

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)&-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\geq P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)-P(E^{*},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}-\sqrt{p^{2}_{E^{*}}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}\right)\,{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{p^{2}_{E}-p^{2}_{E^{*}}}{\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}+\sqrt{p^{2}_{E^{*}}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}}\,{\rm d}t\geq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}-p^{2}_{E^{*}}}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}+\sqrt{p^{2}_{E^{*}}+v_{E}^{\prime 2}}\,{\rm d}t}\\
&\geq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}-p^{2}_{E^{*}}}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{2}\frac{1}{P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(E^{*},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}\\
&\geq c(\lambda)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}-p^{2}_{E^{*}}}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{2}\frac{1}{P_{\lambda}(E)+P_{\lambda}(E^{*})},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used
Corollary

2.5
.
Since
D_{\lambda}(E)<1
and
p_{E}\geq p_{E^{*}}
, we have
P_{\lambda}(E^{*})\leq P_{\lambda}(E)\leq 2P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
and

(4.33)

\begin{split}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}&\geq c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}^{2}-p^{2}_{E^{*}}}\,{\rm d}t=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{E}+p_{E^{*}}}\sqrt{p_{E^{*}}}\sqrt{\frac{p_{E}-p_{E^{*}}}{p_{E^{*}}}}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\sqrt{2}c\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{E^{*}}\sqrt{\frac{p_{E}-p_{E^{*}}}{p_{E^{*}}}}\,{\rm d}t,\end{split}

for some constant
c=c(n,\lambda)>0
changing from line to line.
Note that
(E^{*})_{t}
is a
(n-1)
-dimensional ball with the same
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
measure of
E_{t}
.
Then the quantity

\frac{p_{E}(t)-p_{E^{*}}(t)}{p_{E^{*}}(t)}

is the classical isoperimetric deficit in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
of
E_{t}
with respect to the standard perimeter.
By standard the quantitative isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

[
FMP08
]
, the fact that
E_{t}
is
n-1
symmetric with
(E^{*})_{t}
centered at the center of symmetry of
E_{t}
and
Lemma

4.5
, we have

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E_{t}\Delta E^{*}_{t})}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((E^{*})_{t})}\leq c(n)\sqrt{\frac{p_{E}(t)-p_{E^{*}}(t)}{p_{E^{*}}(t)}}.

By (
4.33
) and the inclusion
E\subset Q_{2l}
we conclude

\begin{split}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}&\geq c\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{p_{E^{*}}(t)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((E^{*})_{t})}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E_{t}\Delta E^{*}_{t})\,{\rm d}t\geq\frac{c}{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E_{t}\Delta E_{t}^{*})\,{\rm d}t=\frac{c}{l}|E\Delta E^{*}|.\end{split}

∎

Putting together
Lemma

4.2
,
Lemma

4.7
and
Lemma

4.8
, one immediately gets the following corollary.

.

depending on

such that for every

) holds (with some constant depending on

5.1.
Coupling

We start by recalling the general definition and properties of the restricted envelopes introduced in
[
Cin+22
]
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
Cin+22
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact convex set,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and
u\in C^{0}(\overline{E})\cap C^{2}(E)
. The
K
-envelope of
u
is the function
\bar{u}^{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\bar{u}^{K}(x):=\sup\{a+\Braket{\xi,x}\ :\ \xi\in K,\quad a+\Braket{\xi,y}\leq u(y)\quad\forall y\in\overline{E}\}.

The desired coupling corresponding to a competitor
E
will be essentially the
K
-envelope of the solution to the elliptic problem (
3.3
), for
K
equal to the closure of the optimal bubble
B^{\lambda}
.

Definition 5.2

(
[
Cin+22
, Definition 3.9]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact convex set,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and
u\in C^{0}(\overline{E})\cap C^{2}(E)
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi\in K
, we define

S_{\xi}:=\arg\min_{x\in\overline{E}}\{u(x)-\Braket{\xi,x}\}

and

H(x,\xi,K):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla^{2}u(s_{i})\ :\ \begin{array}[]{lr}1\leq m\leq n+1\\
\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\;\sum\lambda_{i}=1\\
s_{i}\in S_{\xi}\cap E\\
x-\sum\lambda_{i}s_{i}\in N(\xi,K)\end{array}\right\},

where
N(\xi,K)
is the normal cone of
K
at
\xi
, defined as

N(\xi,K):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ \Braket{v,\xi^{\prime}-\xi}\leq 0\;\text{for all}\;\xi^{\prime}\in K\}.

We will need the following

Proposition 5.3

(
[
Cin+22
, Proposition 3.10]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact convex set,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and
u\in C^{0}(\overline{E})\cap C^{2}(E)
. Assume that for any
\xi\in K
it holds
S_{\xi}\subset E
, then
\bar{u}^{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1,1}
convex function such that

\nabla\bar{u}^{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\bar{u}^{K}(E)=K.

Moreover, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
H_{x}\in H(x,\nabla\bar{u}^{K}(x),K)\neq\emptyset
, it holds
\nabla^{2}\bar{u}^{K}(x)\leq H_{x}
.

We will need to associate a coupling only to Lipschitz-regular connected competitors, having
C^{1}
relative boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
. This is established in the next result, whose proof is analogous to the one in
[
Cin+22
]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists
\hat{C}=\hat{C}(n,\lambda)>0
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a connected bounded open set.
Suppose that
E
has Lipschitz boundary and that
\partial E\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
is a hypersurface of class
C^{1}
with boundary.

Then there exists a
1
-Lipschitz convex function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1,1}
such that
\Delta\Psi\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{|E|}
and such that
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\Psi(E)=B^{\lambda}
up to negligible sets.
Moreover, if
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq 1
, then

(5.1)

\int_{E}|\nabla^{2}\Psi-{\rm id}|\,{\rm d}x\leq\hat{C}\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}

(5.2)

\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}(1-|\nabla\Psi|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\hat{C}D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Let us assume first that
\partial E\setminus\partial H
is a smooth hypersurface with smooth boundary intersecting
\partial H
orthogonally.
Let
u:E\to\mathbb{R}
be a solution of (
3.3
) and
(K_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of compact convex sets such that
K_{i}\subset\subset\mathring{K}_{i+1}
and
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}K_{i}=B^{\lambda}
.
We showed in the proof of
Theorem

3.3
that for any
\xi\in B^{\lambda}
the minimum of
u(x)-\Braket{\xi,x}
cannot be achieved on the boundary of
E
.
Moreover, at any point
x\in E
such that
\nabla^{2}u(x)\geq 0
, it holds

0\leq\nabla^{2}u(x)\leq\Delta u(x)\,{\rm id}=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{|E|}\,{\rm id}.

Therefore, recalling
Remark

3.2
, by
Proposition

5.3
we get that
\bar{u}^{K_{i}}
is a sequence of
1
-Lipschitz functions, being suprema of
1
-Lipschitz functions, that is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,1}
on compact sets; hence up to subsequence it converges to a limit function
\Psi
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since
\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(E)=K_{i}
, then
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\Psi(\overline{E})=\overline{B^{\lambda}}
,
\Psi
is a convex function of class
C^{1,1}
, and writing the inequality
\Delta\bar{u}^{K_{i}}\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{|E|}
in the sense of distributions, one checks that it readily passes to the limit as
i\to+\infty
for the function
\Psi
.

To prove that
|\nabla\Psi(E)\Delta B^{\lambda}|=0
, let
Z\subset E
be a compact set and notice that

|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(E\setminus Z)|\leq c(n,|E|,P_{\lambda}(E))|E\setminus Z|,

\begin{split}|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(Z)|&=|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(E\setminus(E\setminus Z))|\geq|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(E)|-|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(E\setminus Z)|=|K_{i}|-c|E\setminus Z|.\end{split}

Passing to the limit we find

|\nabla\Psi(Z)|\geq\left|\limsup_{i}\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(Z)\right|\geq\limsup_{i}|\nabla\bar{u}^{K_{i}}(Z)|\geq|B^{\lambda}|-c|E\setminus Z|,

hence letting
Z\nearrow E
, we get that
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\Psi(E)=B^{\lambda}
up to negligible sets.

Suppose now that
E
is a generic connected set as in the assumptions. If
n\geq 3
we can apply the above argument to a sequence of sets
E_{i}
approximating
E
given by
Lemma

2.4
, suitably modified connecting possibly disconnected components with thin tubes vanishing in the limit. If
n=2
, then
\partial E\setminus\partial H
is a union of
C^{1}
curves, which thus can be approximated by smooth ones touching
\partial H
orthogonally preserving the connectedness of the set. Applying the first part of the proof on the approximating sequence
E_{i}
we get a corresponding sequence of functions
\Psi_{i}
uniformly bounded in
C^{1,1}
on compact sets, hence converging in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}
up to subsequence to a convex function
\Psi
of class
C^{1,1}
with
\Delta\Psi\leq P_{\lambda}(E)/|E|
. Also, since
\nabla\Psi_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\overline{B^{\lambda}}
, then
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\overline{B^{\lambda}}
by
C^{1}_{\rm loc}
-convergence.
Moreover, since
E
has Lipschitz boundary, there exists a sequence of compact sets
Z_{j}\subset E
such that
Z_{j}\subset E_{i}
for any
i\geq i_{j}
and such that
Z_{j}\nearrow E
. Hence one can repeat the above argument with
\Psi_{i},E,Z_{j}
in place of
\bar{u}^{K_{i}},E,Z
, respectively, to deduce that

|\nabla\Psi_{i}(Z_{j})|\geq|B^{\lambda}|-c(n,|E_{i}|,P_{\lambda}(E_{i}))|E_{i}\setminus Z_{j}|\geq|B^{\lambda}|-c(n,|E|,P_{\lambda}(E))|E_{i}\setminus Z_{j}|.

Letting
i\to\infty
first, and then
j\to\infty
, we get that
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\nabla\Psi(E)=B^{\lambda}
up to negligible sets.

We now prove (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). The symbol
\hat{C}
shall denote a positive constant depending on
n,\lambda
changing from line to line.
By the area formula, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the properties of
\Psi
we get

(5.3)

\begin{split}|B^{\lambda}|&=|\nabla\Psi(E)|\leq\int_{E}\det(\nabla^{2}\Psi)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\int_{E}\left(\frac{\Delta\Psi}{n}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{E}\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|E|}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{E}\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}\right)^{n}|B^{\lambda}|=(1+D_{\lambda}(E))^{n}|B^{\lambda}|.\end{split}

Hence

(5.4)

\int_{E}\left(\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|E|}\right)^{n}-\det(\nabla^{2}\Psi)\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq|B^{\lambda}|(1+D_{\lambda}(E))^{n}-|B^{\lambda}|\leq\hat{C}D_{\lambda}(E).

By
[
Cin+22
, Lemma A.1]
applied with
m=n
,
\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{n}=1
,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
equal to the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}\Psi
and
c=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|E|}=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}\geq 1
, we obtain

(5.5)

\begin{split}|\nabla^{2}\Psi-{\rm id}|^{2}&\leq 2|\nabla^{2}\Psi-c\,{\rm id}|^{2}+2|(c-1){\rm id}|^{2}\leq\hat{C}\left(\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|E|}\right)^{n}-\det(\nabla^{2}\Psi)\right)+2n\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)-n|E|}{n|E|}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\hat{C}\left(\left(\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)}{n|E|}\right)^{n}-\det(\nabla^{2}\Psi)+D_{\lambda}(E)^{2}\right).\end{split}

Therefore by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) we get

\int_{E}|\nabla^{2}\Psi-{\rm id}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq\hat{C}D_{\lambda}(E),

which implies (
5.1
).

Arguing as in (
5.3
), by the divergence theorem and using that
\Braket{\nabla\Psi,-e_{n}}\leq-\lambda
since
\nabla\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\overline{B^{\lambda}}
, we get

\begin{split}|B^{\lambda}|&\leq\int_{E}\left(\frac{\Delta\Psi}{n}\right)^{n}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}\int_{E}\Delta\Psi\,{\rm d}x=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}\int_{\partial^{*}E}\Braket{\nabla\Psi,\nu^{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}\left(\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}\Braket{\nabla\Psi,\nu^{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H}\Braket{\nabla\Psi,-e_{n}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}\left(\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}\Braket{\nabla\Psi,\nu^{E}}-1\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+P_{\lambda}(E)\right)\\
&\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}-\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}{n^{n}|E|^{n-1}}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}(1-|\nabla\Psi|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Rearranging terms, since
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq 1
and
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
, we obtain

\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}(1-|\nabla\Psi|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n}-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})^{n}}{P_{\lambda}(E)^{n-1}}\leq\hat{C}D_{\lambda}(E).

∎

We now want to translate the quantitative estimates obtained on the coupling in
Proposition

5.4
into quantitative estimates on the asymmetry of a competitor. We will need some technical results first.

The next lemma is analogous to
[
Cin+22
, Lemma 6.2]
, but with a varying range of parameters that here must depend on
\lambda
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
E\subset[0,\infty)
be a
1
-dimensional set of locally finite perimeter with
|E|<\infty
and set

r_{\lambda}:=\min\left\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\right\},\qquad R_{\lambda}:=\max\left\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\right\}.

There exists
\hat{c}=\hat{c}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any
\frac{7}{8}r_{\lambda}\leq l\leq\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}
there holds

(5.6)

\int_{E\Delta[0,l]}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\leq\hat{c}\left(\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}\right).

Proof.

It holds
E\Delta[0,l]=((l,\infty)\cap E)\cup([0,l]\setminus E)
.
We claim that

(5.7)

\max\left\{\int_{[l,\infty)\cap E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t,\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\right\}\leq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\left(\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\right).

We will estimate the two terms on the left-hand side separately.

Without loss of generality, suppose
[l,\infty)\cap E\neq\emptyset
. Since
|E|<\infty
, then
\partial^{*}E\cap[l,\infty)
is nonempty and we can assume it has finite supremum
\bar{t}
(otherwise the right hand side in (
5.7
) equals
+\infty
).
In particular the right-hand side in (
5.7
) is finite.
It holds

\int_{[l,\infty)\cap E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{l}^{\bar{t}}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\leq\bar{t}^{n-1}|\bar{t}-l|\leq\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t),

and the first term in the left-hand side of (
5.7
) is bounded as wished.

Let us now consider
\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t
.
Its value is a priori bounded by
\left(\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}\right)^{n}
.
If
\partial^{*}E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{3}{4}r_{\lambda}\right]\neq\emptyset
and
\tau
is one of its elements, then

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)&\geq\tau^{n}\left|l-\tau\right|\geq\left(\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{8}r_{\lambda}\geq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}\right)^{n-1}\left|\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}-\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right|\geq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

So from now on we can assume that
\partial^{*}E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{3}{4}r_{\lambda}\right]=\emptyset
.
If
\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{3}{4}r_{\lambda}\right]\setminus E\neq\emptyset
, then
E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{3}{4}r_{\lambda}\right]=\emptyset
and

\begin{split}\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)&\geq\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t=\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\geq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}\right)^{n-1}\left|\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}-\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right|\\
&\geq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

So we can further assume
\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},\frac{3}{4}r_{\lambda}\right]\subset E
.
If
\partial^{*}E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},l\right]=\emptyset
, then
\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]

\subset

E
and there is nothing to prove.
Finally, if
\partial^{*}E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},l\right]\neq\emptyset
, let us denote by
\underline{t}
the infimum of
\partial^{*}E\cap\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{4},l\right]
.
Then

\begin{split}\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t&\leq\int_{\underline{t}}^{l}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\leq l^{n-1}|l-\underline{t}|=\underline{t}^{n-1}|l-\underline{t}|\left(\frac{l}{\underline{t}}\right)^{n-1}\leq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\underline{t}^{n-1}|l-\underline{t}|\\
&\leq\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t).\end{split}

This concludes the proof of the claim (
5.7
).
Hence

\begin{split}\int_{E\Delta[0,l]}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t&\leq\max\left\{\int_{[l,\infty)\cap E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t,\int_{\left[\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2},l\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\right\}+\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\overset{\eqref{CGPROS:claim}}{\leq}\hat{c}(n,\lambda)\left(\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial^{*}E}t^{n-1}|l-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\right)+\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t,\end{split}

and the proof follows.
∎

We shall also need the following standard technical result stating that a Vol’pert property holds for the intersections of a set of finite perimeter with rays from the origin, cf.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with

. If
\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
,
E_{\vartheta}
is a

-dimensional set of locally finite perimeter such that

\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}\cap\{t>0\}=\{t>0\ :\ t\vartheta\in\partial^{*}E\}.

Moreover, if
\eta\in L^{1}(\partial^{*}E)
is nonnegative, we have

(5.8)

\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}\eta\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\left(\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}\eta(t\vartheta)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\right)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta).

Combining

5.5
with

we get the following result that estimates the symmetric difference of a competitor with a bubble that is just close to a standard bubble

. The result is analogous to
[

, Proposition 6.1]
.

Lemma 5.7
.

There exist
\varepsilon,\tilde{c}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a bounded set of finite perimeter.
If
\left|E\cap B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\setminus H\right|
, then

|E\Delta(B_{1}(x_{0})\setminus H)|\leq\tilde{c}\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}||x-x_{0}|-1|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|x_{0}-(0,\dots,0,-\lambda)|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

If
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, depending only on
n,\lambda
, then for any
\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
the set
\{t>0\ :\ |t\vartheta-x_{0}|<1\}
is an open segment
(0,t(\vartheta))
, with
t(\vartheta)
close to the number

T_{\vartheta}\in\left[\min\left\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\right\},\max\left\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\right\}\right]=:[r_{\lambda},R_{\lambda}]

such that
|T_{\vartheta}\vartheta+\lambda e_{n}|=1
.
In particular, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then
\frac{9}{8}R_{\lambda}\geq t(\vartheta)\geq\frac{7}{8}r_{\lambda}
for any
\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)
.

As before, for any
\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H
let

E_{\vartheta}:=\{t\geq 0\ :\ t\vartheta\in E\}.

By coarea formula we get

(5.9)

|E\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)|=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\left(\int_{E_{\vartheta}\Delta[0,t(\vartheta)]}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\right)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta).

By
Lemma

5.5
we obtain

(5.10)

|E\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})\setminus H|\leq c(n,\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\left(\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}|t(\vartheta)-t|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}\right)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta).

For every
t>0
, we claim that

(5.11)

||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1|\geq c(n,\lambda)|t-t(\vartheta)|.

Note that there exists
\delta=\delta(n,\lambda,\varepsilon)\in(0,r_{\lambda}/8)
such that for
t\in[t(\vartheta)-\delta,t(\vartheta)+\delta]
there holds

\left|\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|t\vartheta-x_{0}|\right|=\left|\Braket{(t\vartheta-x_{0})/|t\vartheta-x_{0}|,\vartheta}\right|\geq c(n,\lambda,\varepsilon)>0.

Then, for
t\in[t(\vartheta)-\delta,t(\vartheta)+\delta]
,

|t(\vartheta)-t|\leq c(n,\lambda)||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1|,

and in this case the claim follows.
Regarding the remaining cases, note that

\frac{||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1|}{|t(\vartheta)-t|}\to 1\qquad\text{as}\;|t|\to+\infty

and the claim follows for
t\geq R=R(n,\lambda,\varepsilon)>0
big enough.
Finally, if
0<t<R
and
t\not\in[t(\vartheta)-\delta,t(\vartheta)+\delta]
, then

\left||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1\right|\geq c(n,\lambda,\delta)>0

|t(\vartheta)-t|\leq c(n,\lambda,R)

hence the claim follows as well.

Therefore

(5.12)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}|t-t(\vartheta)|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta)\overset{\eqref{CGPROS:claim2}}{\leq}c(n,\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta).

By
Lemma

5.6
we deduce

(5.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}&||x-x_{0}|-1|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\left(\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}||t\vartheta-x_{0}|-1|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\right)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta).\end{split}

Since
||x-x_{0}|-1|\geq c(n,\lambda)>0
in
B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)
, by coarea formula and relative isoperimetric inequality we get

(5.14)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus H}\left(\int_{\left[0,\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}\right]\setminus E_{\vartheta}}t^{n-1}\,{\rm d}t\right)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\vartheta)&=\left|\left(B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\setminus H\right)\setminus E\right|=\left|\left(B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\setminus H\right)\setminus E\right|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left|\left(B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\setminus H\right)\setminus E\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\left(B_{\frac{r_{\lambda}}{2}}(0)\setminus H\right)}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}||x-x_{0}|-1|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).\end{split}

Putting together (
5.10
), (
5.12
), (
5.13
) and (
5.14
), the proof follows.
∎

We can finally show that if a suitably regular Schwarz-symmetric set satisfies a trace inequality, then the quantitative estimates in
Proposition

5.4
imply a quantitative isoperimetric inequality.

Proposition 5.8
.

There exists
\delta_{5}=\delta_{5}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any
c_{T}>0
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(n,\lambda,c_{T})>0
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a bounded connected open set with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. Suppose that
E
has Lipschitz boundary and that
\partial E\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
is a hypersurface of class
C^{1}
with boundary. Assume that
E
is Schwarz-symmetric with respect to the
n
-th axis and that there exists a constant
c_{T}
such that for every function
f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that the following holds

(5.15)

\int_{E}\,{\rm d}|Df|(x)\geq c_{T}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}{\rm tr}_{E}(|f-c|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

If
D_{\lambda}(E)<\delta_{5}
, then

\alpha^{2}_{\lambda}(E)\leq\gamma D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be given by
Proposition

5.4
.
By (
5.15
) and (
5.1
) we get

\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}|(\nabla\Psi-x)+x_{0}|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)},

where
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{1},\dots,x_{0}^{n})
is the vector whose
i
-th component is the constant
c
of (
5.15
) corresponding to the
i
-th component of
\nabla\Psi-x
. Therefore

(5.16)

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}\left||x-x_{0}|-1\right|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)&\leq\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}|\nabla\Psi-(x-x_{0})|+|1-|\nabla\Psi||\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&\overset{\eqref{CGPROS:4.8}}{\leq}c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}.\end{split}

We observe that if
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,\lambda)
is given by
Lemma

5.7
, then for
\delta_{5}
small enough depending on
\varepsilon
, we ensure that
|x_{0}-(0,\ldots,0,-\lambda)|<\varepsilon
. Indeed, if for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there were
E_{i}
satisfying the hypotheses of
Proposition

5.8
such that
D_{\lambda}(E)<\frac{1}{i}
with corresponding
x_{0,i}
verifying
|x_{0,i}-(0,\ldots,0,-\lambda)|\geq\varepsilon
, passing to limit in (
5.16
) we would get a contradiction with the fact that
E_{i}
converges to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
.

Hence we can apply
Lemma

5.7
. Since
E
is Schwarz-symmetric, we get

(5.17)

\begin{split}|E\Delta B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)|&\leq|E\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)|\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}.\end{split}

Arguing as above, up to taking a smaller
\delta_{5}
, we can assume that
|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|\leq|x_{0}-(0,\ldots,0,-\lambda)|
is so small that

\left|\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|B_{1}(0,\ldots,0,t)\setminus H|\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

for any
t\in[-|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|,|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|]
. Hence

\begin{split}c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzQuasiQuant}}{\geq}\left||B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\setminus H|-|E|\right|=\left||B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\setminus H|-|B_{1}(0,\dots,0,-\lambda)\setminus H|\right|\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|,\end{split}

which implies

(5.18)

|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|\leq c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)},

for a suitable constant. Therefore

(5.19)

\begin{split}\left|\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\setminus H\right)\Delta\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,-\lambda)\setminus H\right)\right|&\leq c(n,\lambda)|x_{0}^{n}+\lambda|\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzVicinanzaXzeron}}{\leq}c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)},\end{split}

where in the first inequality we used that
t\mapsto\left|\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,t)\setminus H\right)\Delta\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,-\lambda)\setminus H\right)\right|
is Lipschitz for some Lipschitz constant
c(n,\lambda)>0
.

Finally

\begin{split}|E&\Delta B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)|\\
&\geq|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\Delta E|-\left|\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,x_{0}^{n})\setminus H\right)\Delta\left(B_{1}(0,\dots,0,-\lambda)\setminus H\right)\right|\\
&\overset{\eqref{zz:QuasiQuant2}}{\geq}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)-c(n,\lambda,c_{T})\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}.\end{split}

∎

In the next lemma we observe that optimal bubbles do satisfy trace inequalities.

Lemma 5.9
.

There exists
\bar{c}=\bar{c}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for every function
f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that the following holds

(5.20)

\int_{B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)}\,{\rm d}|Df|(x)\geq\bar{c}\int_{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H}{\rm tr}_{B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)}(|f-c|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Proof.

The proof follows combining the classical Poincaré inequality
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.44]
with the boundary trace theorem
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.87]
.
∎

We now introduce a notion of
C^{1}
-distance from
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
for sets in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, and we deduce that Schwarz-symmetric sets sufficiently close in
C^{1}
to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
enjoy a quantitative isoperimetric inequality.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\varphi_{\lambda}:\partial B_{1}\setminus H\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H=\{\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,x\ :\ x\in\partial B_{1}\setminus H\}
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a bounded open set. Suppose that
E
has Lipschitz boundary and that
\partial E\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
is a hypersurface of class
C^{1}
with boundary.
Assume that
E
is Schwarz-symmetric. Suppose that there exists a
C^{1}
functions

\begin{split}\varphi:\partial B_{1}\setminus H\to\mathbb{R}\end{split}

whose graph parametrizes the boundary of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, that is

\partial E\setminus H=\{\varphi(x)\,x\ :\ x\in\partial B_{1}\setminus H\}.

We define the
C^{1}
distance of
E
to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
by
{\rm d}_{C^{1}}(E,B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)):=\|\varphi-\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{C^{1}(\partial B_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)}
.

A sequence of sets
E_{j}
as above is said to converge to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
in
C^{1}
if
{\rm d}_{C^{1}}(E_{j},B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
.

Corollary 5.11
.

There exist
\hat{\varepsilon},\hat{\gamma}>0
depending only on
n,\lambda
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be as in
Definition

5.10
. If
{\rm d}_{C^{1}}(E,B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))\leq\hat{\varepsilon}
, then

\alpha^{2}_{\lambda}(E)\leq\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Let
\varphi,\varphi_{\lambda}
be as in
Definition

5.10
.
If
\chi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
is a smooth cut-off function such that
\chi(t)=0
for
t<\tfrac{1}{4}\min\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\}
and such that
\chi(t)=1
for
t>\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}},1-\lambda\}
, we define the diffeomorphism

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\qquad\qquad\psi(x)=\left(1-\chi(|x|)+\chi(|x|)\frac{\varphi\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}{\varphi_{\lambda}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\right)x.

Note that

\|\psi-{\rm id}\|_{C^{1}}\leq c\hat{\varepsilon},\qquad\qquad\psi(\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H)=\partial E\setminus H,

for some
c=c(n,\lambda,\chi)
, if
{\rm d}_{C^{1}}(E,B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))\leq\hat{\varepsilon}<1
.

Let
g\in{\rm Lip}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and define
f:=g\circ\psi
. If
c
is the constant in (
5.20
) corresponding to
f
, then by area formula and (
5.20
) we get

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\setminus H}|g-c|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&\leq C(n,\lambda)\int_{\partial B^{\lambda}\setminus H}|f-c|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n,\lambda)\int_{B^{\lambda}}|\nabla f|\,{\rm d}x\leq C(n,\lambda)\int_{E}|\nabla g|\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

Therefore, if
\hat{\varepsilon}
is small enough, we can apply
Proposition

5.8
with
c_{T}
therein depending on
n,\lambda
only, and we get

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)D_{\lambda}(E).

∎

5.2.
Proof of the first quantitative isoperimetric inequality

We are ready to prove the main quantitative isoperimetric inequality. Let us recall the following immediate result, completely analogous to
[
Fus15
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Lemma 5.12
.

The standard bubble
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
is the unique solution, up to translations along
\partial H
, of

\min\left\{P_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda\left||F|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\ :\ F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\right\},

for any
\Lambda>n
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)
be the constant given by
Corollary

5.11
and let
\delta_{4},\tilde{l}
be given by
Corollary

4.9
.
By
Corollary

4.9
it is sufficient to prove that there exists
\delta\in(0,\delta_{4})
such that, if
E
is a Schwarz-symmetric set contained in
Q_{\tilde{l}}
such that
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E)<\delta
, then
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq 2\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E)}
.

We argue by contradiction. Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be a sequence of Schwarz-symmetric sets contained in
Q_{\tilde{l}}
such that
|E_{j}|=|B^{\lambda}|
, with
P_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
and

(5.21)

\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})>2\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(E_{j})}.

For every
j
we consider a minimizer
F_{j}
of the problem

(5.22)

\min\{P_{\lambda}(F)+|\alpha_{\lambda}(F)-\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})|+\Lambda||F|-|E_{j}||\ :\ F\;\text{Schwarz-symmetric contained in}\,Q_{\tilde{l}}\},

for
\Lambda>0
to be chosen large. Up to subsequence,
F_{j}
converges in
L^{1}
to a minimizer of
F\mapsto P_{\lambda}(F)+\alpha_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda\left||F|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|
, hence, taking
\Lambda>n
, we have that
F_{j}
converges to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
by
Lemma

5.12
. Also, by comparison with with
E_{j}
, we have that
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
.

We prove that
F_{j}
is a local
(\Lambda_{1},r_{0})
-minimizer in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, for some
\Lambda_{1},r_{0}>0
and
j
large.
Let us consider a ball
B_{r}(x)\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, with
r<\min\{r_{0},d(x,\partial H)\}
, and a set
G
such that
F_{j}\Delta G\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
.
Denoting by
(\cdot)^{*}
the Schwarz symmetrization with respect to the
n
-th axis and by
Z:=G\cap Q_{\tilde{l}}
, we have

\begin{split}P(F_{j},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)&\leq P(Z^{*},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+|\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})-\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})|-|\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})|+\Lambda[||Z|-|E_{j}||-||F_{j}|-|E_{j}||]\\
&\leq P(Z,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+|\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})-\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})|+\Lambda|Z\Delta F_{j}|\\
&\leq P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+|\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})-\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})|+\Lambda|G\Delta F_{j}|.\end{split}

For
r_{0}
small enough and
j
sufficiently large we have that
|G|\geq|Z|\geq c(n,\lambda)>0
. Assume for instance that
\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})\geq\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})
(the opposite case being symmetric), then

\begin{split}\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})-\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})&\leq|Z|^{-1}\left(|Z^{*}\Delta F_{j}|+|F_{j}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|F_{j}|)|+\left||Z|-|F_{j}|\right|\right)-|F_{j}|^{-1}|F_{j}\Delta B^{\lambda}(|F_{j}|)|\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)|Z\Delta F_{j}|+c(n,\lambda)\left(|F_{j}|-|Z|\right)+|Z\Delta F_{j}|\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)|G\Delta F_{j}|.\end{split}

Arguing analogously in case
\alpha_{\lambda}(Z^{*})<\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})
, we deduce

P(F_{j},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\leq P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\Lambda_{1}|G\Delta F_{j}|,

for some
\Lambda_{1}=(\Lambda,n,\lambda)
.

By
Theorem

A.3
we know that
\partial^{*}F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
is a
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
manifold and
\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\setminus\partial^{*}F_{j}
has Hausdorff dimension
\leq n-8
. Since
F_{j}
is Schwarz-symmetric, if there exists a point
(r\vartheta,t)\in\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\setminus\partial^{*}F_{j}
for some
r,t>0,\vartheta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
, then
(r\vartheta^{\prime},t)\in\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\setminus\partial^{*}F_{j}
for any
\vartheta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
. Hence
\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\setminus\partial^{*}F_{j}\subset\{te_{n}\ :\ t>0\}
. However by
Theorem

A.3
for every
\varepsilon>0
the set
\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}\geq\varepsilon\}
converges to
\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\{x_{n}\geq\varepsilon\}
in
C^{1,\alpha}
for any
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
. Also, for
j
large we can apply
Corollary

4.4
which implies that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}=t\})\geq A_{\lambda}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T_{\lambda})
, for some
A_{\lambda},T_{\lambda}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
. Then points
te_{n}
for
t\in(0,T_{\lambda}/2)
are points of density
1
for
F_{j}
, hence they belong to the interior of
F_{j}
. Therefore, for
j
large enough,
\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\setminus\partial^{*}F_{j}
must be empty and
\partial F_{j}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
is an axially symmetric hypersurface of class
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
.

By the minimality of the
F_{j}
, (
5.21
) and
Lemma

5.12
we observe that

(5.23)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(F_{j})&+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|+\left|\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})\right|\leq P_{\lambda}(E_{j})\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))}{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}(n,\lambda)}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{j})\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))}{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}(n,\lambda)}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{j}).\end{split}

Therefore, we have that

|\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})|\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))}{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}(n,\lambda)}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{j}).

Since
\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to 0
we get that

\frac{\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})}{\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})}\to 1.

Let
\{\hat{\lambda}_{j}\}\subset(0,\infty)
such that, setting
\tilde{F}_{j}:=\hat{\lambda}_{j}F_{j}
, then
|\tilde{F}_{j}|=|B^{\lambda}|
.
Clearly
\hat{\lambda}_{j}\to 1
since
|F_{j}|\to|B^{\lambda}|
.
Since
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))
and
\Lambda>n
, for
j
sufficiently large we have
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})<\Lambda|F_{j}|
and

\begin{split}\left|P_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})-P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\right|&=P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n-1}-1\right|\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n}-1\right|\leq\Lambda\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n}-1\right|\,|F_{j}|=\Lambda\left||\tilde{F}_{j}|-|F_{j}|\right|.\end{split}

Hence, by definition of
\hat{\lambda}_{j}
and by (
5.23
) we get

(5.24)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})&\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||\tilde{F}_{j}|-|F_{j}|\right|=P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\\
&\overset{\eqref{dispense:5.13}}{\leq}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))}{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}(n,\lambda)}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{j}).\end{split}

Since
\alpha_{\lambda}(F_{j})/\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to 1
as
j\to\infty
we have
\alpha_{\lambda}(E_{j})^{2}<2\alpha_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})^{2}
for
j
sufficiently large.
Hence from (

) we finally obtain

\alpha_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})>\sqrt{2}\hat{\gamma}(n,\lambda)\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})}.

For
t>0
let

\varphi_{\tilde{F}_{j}}^{-}(t):=\begin{cases}\min_{x\in\partial\tilde{F}_{j}\cap\{x_{n}=t\}}\left\{\left|x-te_{n}\right|\right\}\qquad&{\rm if}\quad\partial\tilde{F}_{j}\cap\{x_{n}=t\}\neq\emptyset,\\
0&{\rm if}\quad\partial\tilde{F}_{j}\cap\{x_{n}=t\}=\emptyset.\end{cases}

be the function measuring the distance of

from the
n
-th axis, set to zero in case

. For

. Since
\tilde{F}_{j}
is Schwarz-symmetric and its relative boundary in

regular, then we can write that

Recalling that

-minimizer, by

\Lambda_{1}
for any

Since

for any

. Observe that if we locally parametrize

where

Fix

, up to reparametrization.

Indeed, we can also parametrize
\partial\tilde{F}_{j}
in a neighborhood

with domain contained in some affine hyperplane of the form

.

from the

-th axis. Since

with a parametrization for

.

We further observe that, for
\alpha_{j}
,
\beta_{j}:(a,b)\to(0,\infty)
as above, since

in the notation of

A.4
.
Recalling that
\varphi_{\tilde{F}_{j}}^{-}\geq A^{\prime}_{\lambda}>0

|k_{\gamma_{j}}|\leq 2\Lambda_{1}+\frac{n-2}{A^{\prime}_{\lambda}}.

Observe that the upper bound in (

.

Fix now
q_{0}\in\partial\tilde{F}_{j}\cap\{x_{1}>0,x_{n}=T_{\lambda}\}\cap{\rm span}\{e_{1},e_{n}\}
, let
\gamma_{j}^{0}:[0,l_{0})\to{\rm span}\{e_{1},e_{n}\}

\Braket{\gamma_{j}^{0}(t),e_{n}}\leq T_{\lambda}

\sigma_{j}:[0,L_{j})\to{\rm span}\{e_{1},e_{n}\}

.
Hence, by

in contradiction with (

∎

6.
Second quantitative isoperimetric inequality

We will need the following technical lemma, proving that if the energy
P_{\lambda}(E_{i})
a sequence of sets
E_{i}
converges to the energy of the limit, then the sequence strictly converges in the sense of
BV
functions. The proof essentially follows by analyzing the equality case in the Reshetnyak lower semicontinuity theorem, see
[
AFP00
, Theorem 2.38]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that
E_{i}\to E
in
L^{1}
, for some set of finite perimeter
E
with
|E|<+\infty
. If
P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\to P_{\lambda}(E)
, then

\lim_{i}P(E_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H),\qquad\lim_{i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E_{i}\cap\partial H)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H).

Proof.

Let
f(v):=|v|-\lambda\braket{e_{n},v}
, for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\nu_{i}:=|D\chi_{E_{i}}|\otimes\delta_{\nu^{E_{i}}}
be a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Since
|\nu_{i}|(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq P(E_{i})\leq 2P_{\lambda}(E_{i})/(1-\lambda)
by
Corollary

2.5
, up to subsequence,
\nu_{i}
weakly* converges to a finite measure
\nu
. Up to subsequence, also
|D\chi_{E_{i}}|
weakly* converges to a finite measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
.
Denoting by
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
the natural projection, we have
\pi_{\sharp}\nu_{i}=|D\chi_{E_{i}}|\to\mu=\pi_{\sharp}\nu
. Moreover,
\mu\geq|D\chi_{E}|
by lower semicontinuity. By the disintegration theorem
[
AFP00
, Theorem 2.28]
, we can write
\nu=\mu\otimes\nu_{x}
, for a
\mu
-measurable map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\ni x\mapsto\nu_{x}
, where
\nu_{x}
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Analogously to
[
AFP00
, Eq. (2.30)]
, we observe that

(6.1)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v\,{\rm d}\nu_{x}(v)=\nu^{E}(x)\frac{|D\chi_{E}|}{\mu}(x),

at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
. Indeed, for any continuous function
g
with
{\rm spt}(g)\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
we find

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}g(x)&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v\,{\rm d}\nu_{x}(v)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g(x)\,v\,{\rm d}\nu(x,v)=\lim_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g(x)\,v\,{\rm d}\nu_{i}(x,v)\\
&=-\lim_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}g(x)\,{\rm d}D\chi_{E_{i}}(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}g(x)\,{\rm d}D\chi_{E}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}g(x)\nu^{E}(x)\frac{|D\chi_{E}|}{\mu}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(x).\end{split}

Since
f
is nonnegative, convex and continuous, by
Remark

2.2
we find

(6.2)

\begin{split}\lim_{i}P_{\lambda}(E_{i})&=\lim_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f(\nu^{E_{i}})\,{\rm d}|D\chi_{E_{i}}|=\lim_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(v)\,{\rm d}\nu_{i}(x,v)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(v)\,{\rm d}\nu(x,v)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(v)\,{\rm d}\nu_{x}(v)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v\,{\rm d}\nu_{x}(v)\right)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)\\
&\overset{\eqref{bbb}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f\left(\nu^{E}(x)\frac{|D\chi_{E}|}{\mu}(x)\right)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f(\nu^{E}(x))\,{\rm d}|D\chi_{E}|(x)=P_{\lambda}(E),\end{split}

where in the second inequality we applied Jensen inequality, and where the last equality follows since
f
is positively
1
-homogeneous. Since
\lim_{i}P_{\lambda}(E_{i})=P_{\lambda}(E)
by assumption and since
f
is not affine, equality in Jensen inequality implies that the identity map
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\ni v\mapsto v
is constant
\nu_{x}
-a.e., for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
. This means that
\nu_{x}=\delta_{v_{x}}
for some
v_{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
. Hence (
6.1
) implies

v_{x}=\nu^{E}(x)\frac{|D\chi_{E}|}{\mu}(x),

at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, and since
|v_{x}|=|\nu^{E}(x)|=1
, then
|D\chi_{E}|/\mu(x)=1
at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, and
v_{x}=\nu^{E}(x)

\mu
-almost everywhere. Inserting in (
6.2
) we deduce

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f\left(\nu^{E}(x)\right)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v\,{\rm d}\nu_{x}(v)\right)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H}f(\nu^{E}(x))\,{\rm d}|D\chi_{E}|(x).

Since
f(\nu^{E}(x))>0
and
\mu\geq|D\chi_{E}|
, we deduce that
\mu=|D\chi_{E}|
, and then
|D\chi_{E_{i}}|
weakly* converges to
|D\chi_{E}|
.

We can now fix an increasing sequence of Lipschitz bounded open sets
\Omega_{j}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that
\cup_{j}\Omega_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
and
P(E_{i},\partial\Omega_{j})=P(E,\partial\Omega_{j})=0
for every
i,j
. Hence
\lim_{i}P(E_{i},\Omega_{j})=P(E,\Omega_{j})
for any
j
. Moreover

P(E_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup\Omega_{j}))\leq\frac{1}{1-|\lambda|}\left(P_{\lambda}(E_{i})-\int_{\Omega_{j}}f(\nu^{E_{i}})\,{\rm d}|D\chi_{E_{i}}|\right),

for any
i,j
.
Applying Reshetnyak continuity theorem
[
AFP00
, Theorem 2.39]
on
\Omega_{j}
we get

\limsup_{i}P(E_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup\Omega_{j}))\leq\frac{1}{1-|\lambda|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup\Omega_{j})}f(\nu^{E})\,{\rm d}|D\chi_{E}|\leq\frac{1+|\lambda|}{1-|\lambda|}P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup\Omega_{j})),

for any
j
. Therefore

\limsup_{i}P(E_{i},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\leq P(E,\Omega_{j})+\frac{1+|\lambda|}{1-|\lambda|}P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup\Omega_{j})),

for any
j
. Letting
j\to\infty
, the proof follows.
∎

We will also exploit the concept of
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal set.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter with finite measure, and let
K\geq 1,r_{0}>0
. We say that
E
is a
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal set (relatively in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
) if

P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\leq KP(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H),

for any
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
, for some ball
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
r\leq r_{0}
and
x\in\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
.

Quasiminimal sets have well-known topological regularity properties following from uniform density estimates at boundary points. We recall these facts in the following statement.
The proof follows, for example, by repeatedly applying
[
Kin+13
, Theorem 4.2]
with
X=\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
in domains
\Omega=X\cap B_{r_{0}}(x)
for
x\in X
, in the notation of
[
Kin+13
, Theorem 4.2]
. Observe that in
[
Kin+13
]
, the perimeter functional coincides with the relative perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, hence the definition of quasiminimal set in
[
Kin+13
, Definition 3.1]
coincides with our
Definition

6.2
. Alternatively, the proof follows by adapting the proof of
[
Mag12
, Theorem 21.11]
working with
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal sets instead of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal set, for some
K\geq 1,r_{0}>0
. Then there exist
m=m(n,K,r_{0})\in(0,1)
and
r_{0}^{\prime}=r_{0}^{\prime}(n,K,r_{0})\in(0,r_{0}]
such that

m\leq\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)\setminus H|}\leq 1-m\qquad\forall x\in\overline{\partial E\setminus H},\,\,\forall\,r\in(0,r_{0}^{\prime}].

In particular the set
E^{(1)}
of points of density
1
for
E
is an open representative for
E
.

We will identify a
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal set with its open representative
E^{(1)}
. In order to prove
Theorem

1.2
we need two preparatory lemmas.

Lemma 6.4
.

For any
K\geq 1,r_{0}>0
there exist
\delta_{6},C_{5},C_{6}>0
depending on
n,\lambda,K,r_{0}
such that the following holds. If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a bounded
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal set with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\delta_{6}
, then

(6.3)

d_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\overline{\partial E\setminus H},\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)\setminus H}\right)\leq C_{5}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,x)
is a bubble realizing the asymmetry of
E
. Moreover

(6.4)

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C_{6}D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

Proof.

Up to translation, we can assume that
x=0
. Also, letting
m,r_{0}^{\prime}
be given by
Theorem

6.3
, up to decreasing
r_{0}
we can assume that
r_{0}=r_{0}^{\prime}
.
Let
p\in\overline{\partial E\setminus H}
be such that

d_{0}:={\rm dist}\left(p,\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H}\right)=\max\left\{{\rm dist}\left(y,\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H}\right)\ :\ y\in\overline{\partial E\setminus H}\right\}.

Hence
B_{d_{0}}(p)\cap\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H}=\emptyset
. Then either
B_{d_{0}}(p)\setminus H\subset B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
or
B_{d_{0}}(p)\setminus H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))
. In the first case
Theorem

6.3
implies

m|B_{r}(x)\setminus H|\leq|B_{r}(x)\setminus(H\cup E)|\leq|B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus E|=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\qquad\forall\,r\in(0,\min\{d_{0},r_{0}\}),

while in the second case
Theorem

6.3
implies

m|B_{r}(x)\setminus H|\leq|B_{r}(x)\cap E|\leq|E\setminus B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)|=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\qquad\forall\,r\in(0,\min\{d_{0},r_{0}\}).

Since
|B_{R}(x)\setminus H|\geq Cr^{n}
, then
\min\{d_{0},r_{0}\}^{n}\leq C\alpha_{\lambda}(E)
, for
C=C(n,\lambda,K,r_{0})
. So by
Corollary

4.3
, choosing
\delta_{6}
small enough we have that
\alpha_{\lambda}(E)
is so small that
\min\{d_{0},r_{0}\}=d_{0}
and then

d_{0}^{n}\leq C\alpha_{\lambda}(E).

Since density estimates as those in
Theorem

6.3
hold for
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
, repeating the above argument exchanging the roles of
E
and
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
, (
6.3
) follows.

From (
6.3
), we deduce that

\overline{\partial E\setminus H}\subset\left\{y\in\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\ :\ {\rm dist}\left(y,\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\setminus H}\right)\leq C_{5}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right\}.

Hence

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&\left(\partial^{*}E\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\partial H\right)\\
&\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left\{(x^{\prime},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ (1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-C_{5}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq|x^{\prime}|\leq(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{5}\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right\}\right)\\
&\leq C\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq CD_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}},\end{split}

for some
C=C(n,\lambda,K,r_{0})
, where we used
Theorem

1.1
in the last inequality. Hence (
6.4
) follows.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

There exists
\delta_{7},C_{7}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\delta_{7}
there holds

(6.5)

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C_{7}D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

Proof.

Fix
\Lambda>n
. Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a large cube whose interior contains the closure of
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
, and let
F\subset Q\setminus H
be such that
|B^{\lambda}|/2\leq|F|\leq 2|B^{\lambda}|
. Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be such that
G\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r_{0}}(x)
, for
x\in\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
and
r_{0}\in(0,1)
to be chosen small. Let
Z:=G\cap Q
. Observe that

(6.6)

\begin{split}\left||Z|-|F|\right|&\leq|Z\Delta F|\leq|G\Delta F|^{\frac{1}{n}}|G\Delta F|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}r\,\left(|G|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+|F|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq\overline{C}(n)r_{0}\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the relative isoperimetric inequality in a half-space (see
[
CGR07
]
for the sharp inequality). Let
y,z\in\partial H
be such that

\beta_{\lambda}(F)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)},\qquad\beta_{\lambda}(Z)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}Z\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,z)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,z)\cap\partial H\right)},

Observe that
y,z
exist since
F,Z\subset Q
. Suppose, for instance, that
\beta_{\lambda}(Z)\geq\beta_{\lambda}(F)
. Then

(6.7)

\begin{split}&\beta_{\lambda}(Z)-\beta_{\lambda}(F)\leq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}Z\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}-\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}\\
&\leq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}Z\Delta\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H\right)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}+\\
&\qquad-\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}Z\Delta\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H\right)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}+\frac{\left|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)\right|}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}+\\
&\qquad+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\Delta\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)}\right)\end{split}

By a trace inequality
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.87]
we estimate

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}Z\Delta\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H\right)&\leq C(n)\left(|Z\Delta F|+P(Z,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right)\\
&\leq C(n)\left(|G\Delta F|+P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right)\\
&\leq C(n)\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right),\end{split}

where the last inequality follows as in (
6.6
), and
C
denotes a constant depending on suitable parameters that changes from line to line. For
r_{0}
small, depending only on
n,\lambda
, we can ensure that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)\geq C(n,\lambda)>0.

Finally

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|F|,y)\cap\partial H\right)-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B^{\lambda}(|Z|,y)\cap\partial H\right)\right|&\leq L\left||Z|-|F|\right|\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzqqa}}{\leq}C(n,\lambda)\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right),\end{split}

for a suitable Lipschitz constant
L=L(n,\lambda)
. Therefore (
6.7
) becomes

\beta_{\lambda}(Z)-\beta_{\lambda}(F)\leq C(n,\lambda)\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right).

In case
\beta_{\lambda}(Z)<\beta_{\lambda}(F)
, the very same argument leads to an analogous estimate. Hence

(6.8)

\left|\beta_{\lambda}(Z)-\beta_{\lambda}(F)\right|\leq\widetilde{C}(n,\lambda)\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right).

Up to taking a smaller
r_{0}
, we fix
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that
1-|\lambda|-\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{C}(n,\lambda)-\Lambda\overline{C}(n)r_{0}>0
, and we define

K:=\frac{1+|\lambda|+\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{C}(n,\lambda)+\Lambda\overline{C}(n)r_{0}}{1-|\lambda|-\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{C}(n,\lambda)-\Lambda\overline{C}(n)r_{0}}>1.

Let
\delta_{6},C_{6}
be given by
Lemma

6.4
corresponding to the parameters
K,r_{0}/2
. We want to prove that if
\delta_{7}
is sufficiently small, then

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq 2C_{6}D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

We argue by contradiction assuming that there exist sets
E_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E_{j}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
D_{\lambda}(E_{j})\leq 1/j
such that

(6.9)

\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})>2C_{6}D_{\lambda}(E_{j})^{\frac{1}{2n}},

for any
j
. Up to translation,
E_{j}\to B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)
and
P_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
. Since the trace operator is continuous with respect to strict convergence of
BV
functions, see
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.88]
, by
Lemma

6.1
we deduce that
\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to 0
.

Let
F_{j}
be a minimizer of the problem

(6.10)

\min\left\{P_{\lambda}(E)+\varepsilon_{0}|\beta_{\lambda}(E)-\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})|+\Lambda\left||E|-|E_{j}|\right|\ :\ E\subset Q\right\}.

By
Corollary

3.9
, up to subsequence
F_{j}
converges to a limit set
F
in
L^{1}
. If by contradiction
\beta_{j}(F_{j})\not\to 0
, by
Lemma

5.12
for large
j
we would have that

P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|))+\varepsilon_{0}\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})<P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\varepsilon_{0}|\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})|+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|E_{j}|\right|,

contradicting the minimality of
F_{j}
. Hence
\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})\to 0
. It follows that, up to translation,
F_{j}
converges to
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)
in
L^{1}
. Comparing with
E_{j}
, we also see that
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
.

We want to show that
F_{j}
is
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal for
j
large. Indeed,
|B^{\lambda}|/2\leq|F_{j}|\leq 2|B^{\lambda}|
for
j
large. Hence we can apply (
6.6
) and (
6.8
) with
F=F_{j}
. Letting
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
such that
G\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r_{0}}(x)
, for
x\in\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
, denoting
Z:=G\cap Q
, by minimality of
F_{j}
for (
6.10
) we find

\begin{split}(1-|\lambda|)&P(F_{j},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\leq(1+|\lambda|)P(Z,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\varepsilon_{0}\left|\beta_{\lambda}(Z)-\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})\right|+\Lambda\left||Z|-|F_{j}|\right|\\
&\leq(1+|\lambda|)P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\left(\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{C}(n,\lambda)+\Lambda\overline{C}(n)r_{0}\right)\left(P(G,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+P(F_{j},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\right),\end{split}

proving that
F_{j}
is
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal.

By minimality of
F_{j}
, we have

(6.11)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(F_{j})&+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|+\varepsilon_{0}\left|\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})\right|\leq P_{\lambda}(E_{j})\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{(2C_{6})^{2n}}\beta_{\lambda}^{2n}(E_{j})\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{(2C_{6})^{2n}}\beta_{\lambda}^{2n}(E_{j})\end{split}

Therefore

|\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})-\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})|\leq\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{\varepsilon_{0}(2C_{6})^{2n}}\beta_{\lambda}^{2n}(E_{j}),

and then

\frac{\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})}{\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})}\to 1.

Next we select
\{\hat{\lambda}_{j}\}\subset(0,\infty)
such that, setting
\tilde{F}_{j}:=\hat{\lambda}_{j}F_{j}
, then
|\tilde{F}_{j}|=|B^{\lambda}|
.
Clearly
\hat{\lambda}_{j}\to 1
since
|F_{j}|\to|B^{\lambda}|
.
Since
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\to P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}))
and
\Lambda>n
, for
j
sufficiently large we have
P_{\lambda}(F_{j})<\Lambda|F_{j}|
and

\begin{split}\left|P_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})-P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\right|&=P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n-1}-1\right|\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n}-1\right|\leq\Lambda\left|\hat{\lambda}_{j}^{n}-1\right|\,|F_{j}|=\Lambda\left||\tilde{F}_{j}|-|F_{j}|\right|.\end{split}

Hence, by definition of
\hat{\lambda}_{j}
and by (
6.11
) we get

(6.12)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})&\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||\tilde{F}_{j}|-|F_{j}|\right|=P_{\lambda}(F_{j})+\Lambda\left||F_{j}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\overset{\eqref{aaa}}{\leq}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{(2C_{6})^{2n}}\beta_{\lambda}^{2n}(E_{j}).\end{split}

Since
\beta_{\lambda}(F_{j})/\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})\to 1
as
j\to\infty
and
\beta_{\lambda}
is scale invariant, we have
\beta_{\lambda}(E_{j})^{2n}<2\beta_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})^{2n}
for
j
sufficiently large.
Hence from (
6.12
) we obtain

\beta_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})^{2n}\geq 2^{2n-1}C_{6}^{2n}D_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j}),

that is
\beta_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})\geq 2^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}C_{6}D_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})^{\frac{1}{2n}}
. On the other hand, for
j
large,
\tilde{F}_{j}
is
(K,\hat{\lambda}_{j}r_{0})
-quasiminimal. As
\hat{\lambda}_{j}\to 1
, then
\tilde{F}_{j}
is
(K,r_{0}/2)
-quasiminimal for
j
large. Moreover
D_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})\to 0
. By the choice of
C_{6}
above,
Lemma

6.4
implies that

\beta_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})\leq C_{6}D_{\lambda}(\tilde{F}_{j})^{\frac{1}{2n}},

giving a contradiction.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

By
Lemma

6.5
it follows that for any
A>0
there exists
C_{A}>0
such that for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)\leq A
there holds

(6.13)

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C_{A}D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

Indeed, if
D_{\lambda}(E)\leq\delta_{7}
, for
\delta_{7}
as in
Lemma

6.5
, then (
6.13
) follows with
C_{A}=C_{7}
. Otherwise we just have

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C(n,\lambda)\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)\cap\partial H)\right)\leq C(n,\lambda,A)\frac{\delta_{7}^{\frac{1}{2n}}}{\delta_{7}^{\frac{1}{2n}}}\leq C(n,\lambda,A)D_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

Next we observe that, letting
C_{\lambda}
such that
P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\leq C_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\partial H)
, then for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)\geq\frac{2C_{\lambda}}{1-\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\partial H)
there holds

(6.14)

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C_{8}D_{\lambda}(E),

for a constant
C_{8}=C_{8}(n,\lambda)>0
.

Indeed

P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})\geq(1-\lambda)\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)-C_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\partial H)\geq\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H),

and

\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq C(n,\lambda)\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|,0)\cap\partial H)\right)\leq C(n,\lambda,C_{\lambda})\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H).

Setting now
A:=\frac{2C_{\lambda}}{1-\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\cap\partial H)
in (
6.13
), taking into account (
6.14
) we conclude that for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
there holds

\begin{split}\beta_{\lambda}(E)\leq\max\{C_{A},C_{8}\}\,\max\left\{D_{\lambda}(E),D_{\lambda}(E)^{\frac{1}{2n}}\right\}.\end{split}

∎

Appendix A
Auxiliary results

A.1.
Regularity of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers

We recall definitions and basic properties of local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of the perimeter. A detailed account on the theory of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers can be found in
[
Mag12
]
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter. We say that
E
is a local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the perimeter in
\Omega
, with
\Lambda,r_{0}>0
, if

P(E,B_{r}(x))\leq P(F,B_{r}(x))+\Lambda|E\Delta F|,

whenever
E\Delta F\subset\subset B_{r}(x)\subset\subset\Omega
and
r\leq r_{0}
.

It is well-known that local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers have bounded mean curvature in a generalized sense. We could not find an explicit reference in the literature, hence we provide a proof in the following result.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the perimeter in
\Omega
.
Then there exists
H\in L^{\infty}(P(E,\cdot),\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda
and

\int_{\partial^{*}E}{\rm div}_{T}X=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\Braket{X,H}\qquad\forall X\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
{\rm div}_{T}X
is the tangential divergence of
X
along the (
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. defined) tangent space of
\partial^{*}E
.
We shall refer to
H
as to the (generalized) mean curvature of
E
.

Proof.

Let
X\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{r}(x))
, with
B_{r}(x)\subset\subset\Omega
and
r\leq r_{0}
. Let
\{g_{t}\}_{t<|\eta|}
be the flow of the vector field
X
, and define
F_{t}=g_{t}(E)
. Then
E\Delta F_{t}\subset\subset B_{r}(x)
. Let
f_{t}=g_{t}^{-1}=g_{-t}
.
If
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
, for
t>0
we have

(A.1)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u(f_{t}(y))-u(y)|\,{\rm d}y&=\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}[u(f_{s}(y))]\,{\rm d}s\right|\,{\rm d}y\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla u(f_{s}(y))|\;|X(f_{s}(y))|\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u(f_{s}(y))|\;|X(f_{s}(y))|\frac{Jf_{s}(y)}{Jf_{s}(y)}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\\
&\leq(1+o(1))\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u(f_{s}(y))|\;|X(f_{s}(y))|\;Jf_{s}(y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\\
&=(1+o(1))\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u(z)|\;|X(z)|\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}s\\
&=(1+o(1))t\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u(z)|\;|X(z)|\,{\rm d}z,\end{split}

where
o(1)\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
.

Setting
u=u_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{E}\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}
, then
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\mathcal{L}^{n}\to P(E,\cdot)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
by
[
Mag12
, Proposition 12.20]
. Also

\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|u_{\varepsilon}(f_{t}(y))-\chi_{f_{-t}(E)}(y)\right|\frac{Jf_{t}}{Jf_{t}}\,{\rm d}y\leq 2\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u_{\varepsilon}(z)-\chi_{E}(z)|\,{\rm d}z.

Hence setting
u=u_{\varepsilon}
in (
A.1
) and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
implies

|E\Delta F_{t}|=\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\chi_{F_{t}}-\chi_{E}|\leq(1+o(1))t\int_{B_{r}(x)}|X|\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot).

By
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimality we deduce

P(E,B_{r}(x))-P(F_{t},B_{r}(x))\leq(1+o(1))\Lambda t\int_{B_{r}(x)}|X|\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot).

Dividing by
t>0
and letting
t\to 0^{+}
we get

-\int_{\partial^{*}E}{\rm div}_{T}X\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot)\leq\Lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}|X|\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot).

Up to changing
X
with
-X
we obtain

\left|\int_{\partial^{*}E}{\rm div}_{T}X\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot)\right|\leq\Lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}|X|\,{\rm d}P(E,\cdot),

that implies the existence of the generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}(P(E,\cdot)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{r}(x))
for
E
in
B_{r}(x)
with
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda
. Since
B_{r}(x)
was arbitrary in
\Omega
, by a partition of unity argument the claim follows.
∎

Let us further recall the following fundamental regularity properties of local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers.

Theorem A.3

(
[
Tam84
]
,
[
Mag12
, Theorem 26.3, Theorem 26.6]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Let
E\subset\Omega
be a local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer in
\Omega
. Then the set
E^{(1)}
of points of density
1
for
E
is an open representative for
E
. Moreover, representing
E
with
E^{(1)}
, we have that
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
is a
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
manifold and
\mathcal{H}^{d}(\partial E\cap\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}E)=0
for any
d>n-8
.

Let
E_{i}\subset\Omega
be a sequence of local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers in
\Omega
that converges to
E
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. If
\partial E\cap B_{r}(x)
is of class
C^{2}
, for some
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
, then
\partial E_{i}\cap B_{r/2}(x)
is of class
C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}
for large

and converges to
\partial E\cap B_{r/2}(x)
in
C^{1,\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
.

Thanks to
Theorem

A.3
, we will always identify a local
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer
E
with the open set
E^{(1)}
.

A.2.
Axially symmetric hypersurfaces

We recall a formula for the mean curvature of axially symmetric hypersurfaces of class
W^{2,p}
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
a<b
. Let
\alpha
,
\beta:(a,b)\to(0,\infty)
be
W^{2,p}
functions, with
p\in(1,\infty]
, parametrizing the curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to{\rm span}\{e_{1},e_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
\gamma(t)=(\alpha(t),0,\ldots,0,\beta(t))
, and assume that
|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|=1
and that
\inf_{(a,b)}\alpha>0
. Let
S
be the axially symmetric hypersurface around the
n
-th axis parametrized by

\begin{split}&\varphi_{S}:\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
&\varphi_{S}(\vartheta,t)=(\alpha(t)\vartheta,\beta(t)).\end{split}

Then the vector

H=\left(\Braket{k_{\gamma},\nu}-(n-2)\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\alpha}\right)\left(-\beta^{\prime}\vartheta,\alpha^{\prime}\right),

for every
\vartheta
and a.e.
t
, where
k_{\gamma}
is the curvature of
\gamma
and
\nu(t)=(-\beta^{\prime},0,\ldots,0,\alpha^{\prime})
, is the (generalized) mean curvature of
S
. More precisely

(A.2)

\int_{S}{\rm div}_{T}X=-\int_{S}\braket{X,H},

for any
X\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\rm spt}X\cap\partial S=\emptyset
, where
{\rm div}_{T}X
is the tangential divergence of
X
along
S
.

Proof.

If
\alpha,\beta
are smooth, the claimed formula follows by a direct computation. The statement then follows by approximating
\alpha,\beta
in
W^{2,p}
. The approximating hypersurfaces
S_{i}
converge to
S
in
C^{1}
and the measures
H_{i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}S_{i}
converge to
H\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}S
in duality with compactly supported continuous fields. Hence (
A.2
) passes to the limit.
∎

As a further generalization of the Green’s relation

The relation

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{*}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{L}^{U}}

with equality if

Also analogous to
\mathcal{H}
we have
\tilde{\mathcal{H}^{U}}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}^{U}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{R}^{U}}
and in a similar way
\tilde{\mathcal{D}^{U}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}^{U}}
.

3.
Congruences on variants

An equivalence relation
\rho
on a semigroup
S
is a congruence on

if and only if

(xs,yt)\in\rho

for all pairs
(x,y),(s,t)\in\rho

Definition 6
.

Let

\rho

[a]_{\rho}\ast[b]_{\rho}=[ab]_{\rho}

Let
S

x\,\lambda^{u}\,y\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad ux=uy

and the relation

on

x\rho^{u}y\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad xu=yu.

These relations were among the congruences introduced in the context of sandwich semigroups by Symons(cf.
[
11
]
).

Lemma 2
.

S
is a semigroup,
u\in S
and
S^{u}
the variant of

with respect to

. Then the relations
\lambda^{u}\,\,[\rho^{u}]

is a left [right] congruence on

.

Proof.

As
\lambda^{u}
defined on
S
by

is a congruence, we have

implies
(px,py)\in\lambda^{u}
for every

. Now regarding
\lambda^{u}
as a the relation on

S^{u}
defined by

\displaystyle x\,\lambda^{u}\,y

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow u\star_{u}x=u\star_{u}y

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow uux=uuy

Now for
p\in S^{u}
, we have

\displaystyle uux=uuy

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow puux=puuy

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p\star_{u}ux=p\star_{u}uy

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(px,py)\in\lambda^{u}.

Hence
\lambda^{u}
if a left congruence. Similarly it is easily seen that

.
∎

Lemma 3
.

S

4.
partial order on variants

In any semigroup
(S,\cdot)
for
a,b\in S
the relation
a\leq b
if and only if
a=xb=by,\,\,xa=a
for some
x,y\in S^{1}
is called the natural partial order of
S
. When restricted to the idempotent sets
E(S)
this coinsides with te usual ordering
e\leq f
if and only if
ef=fe=e
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\{S^{e}\,\mid e\in E(S)\}
be the idempotent variant of a semigroup
S
. Then the set of idempotents in
S^{e}
is

E(S^{e})=\{f\in E(S)\,:\,f\leq e\}.

Proof.

Let
f\in E(S)
and
f\leq e
. Then

f\star_{e}f=fef=ff=f

ie.,
f\in E(S^{e})
. Conversly if
f\in E(S^{e})
then

f\star_{e}f=f\Leftrightarrow fef=f

hence
f^{2}=f
and
fe=ef=f
that is
f\leq e

∎

Now define a relation on
E(S^{e})
by,

x\leq_{e}y\Leftrightarrow x\star_{e}y=y\star_{e}x=x

for all
x,y\in E(S^{e}).

Lemma 5
.

The relation
\leq_{e}
defined on
E(S^{e})
is a partial order on
E(S^{e})
.

Proof.

For
x\in E(S^{e})
, we have
x\leq_{e}x=xex=x
since
xe=ex=x
. ie.,
x\leq_{e}x
.
Consider
y,z
also in
E(S^{e})
, with
x\leq_{e}y
and
y\leq_{e}z
, then

x\star_{e}y=y\star_{e}x=x
and
y\star_{e}z=z\star_{e}y=y

x=x\leq_{e}y=x\star_{e}(y\star_{e}z)=(x\star_{e}y)\star_{e}z=x\star_{e}z

and similarly
z\star_{e}x=x
. Also when
x\leq_{e}y
and
y\leq_{e}x
then obviously
x=y
. Hence
\leq_{e}
is a partial oreder on
E(S^{e})
.
∎

Next we generalise this partial order in
E(S^{e})
to the whole of
S^{e}
by defining

a\leq_{e}b
if and only if there exists
x,y\in(S^{e})^{1}
such that

x\star_{e}b=b\star_{e}y=a\quad\text{and}\quad x\star_{e}a=a

.

Remark 1
.

For elements
a,b\in S^{e},\,\,a\leq_{e}b\Rightarrow a\leq b
in
S
.

Proof.

Suppose
a\leq_{e}b
, then

x\star_{e}b=b\star_{e}y=a\quad\text{and}\quad x\star_{e}a=a

for some
x,y\in(S^{e})^{1}
. ie.,
a=xeb=bey
and
a=xea
, thus

a=x^{\prime}b=by^{\prime}
and
a=x^{\prime}a=ay^{\prime}
for some
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in S^{1}
, hence
a\leq b
in
S
.
∎

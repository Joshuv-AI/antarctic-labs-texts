Abstract
. We propose a definition of a quantised 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 {\mathfrak{sl}}_{2} -differential algebra and show that the quantised exterior algebra (defined by Berenstein and Zwicknagl) and the quantised Clifford algebra (defined by the authors) of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 {\mathfrak{sl}}_{2} are natural examples of such algebras.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

16T20, 81R50, 17B37

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra.
H. Cartan introduced the notion of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebras as a generalisation
of differential forms on manifolds with
{\mathfrak{g}}
-action,
[
Car1
,
Car2
]
.
Later
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebras appeared in the study of equivariant cohomology
[
GS
,
AM1
]
,
in Chern–Weil theory
[
AM2
,
Mei
]
, and in relation to (algebraic) Dirac
operators and Vogan’s conjecture
[
AM1
,
HP1
,
HP2
]
.

There have been several attempts to generalise the notion of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebras to the setting of
quantum groups and noncommutative geometry; for example,
see
[
AC
,
SWZ
,
AS
]
.
These works, however, assumed that we either work with a triangular Hopf algebra,
or start with a bicovariant calculus on a quantum group, so they do not directly apply the setting of

U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
since it is only a quasitriangular, see
[
Dri1
,
Dri2
]
,
and its bicovariant differential calculus does not have classical dimension
[
Wor
]
, see
also
[
Jur
]
for the general case.

In this paper we propose a definition of quantised
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-differential algebras and give first examples,
certain quantised Clifford and exterior algebras.
The advantage of our approach is that we start with the quantum exterior algebra defined by Berenstein and
Zwicknagl
[
BZ
]
of the classical dimension instead of a bicovariant calculus.
We use the coboundary structure on the category of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-modules, see
[
Dri2
]
. (As it
was shown in
[
HK
]
such coboundary structure is related to the category of crystals.)

The paper is organised as follows. In §
2
we recall necessary facts about the Drinfeld–Jimbo
quantum group
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
, the quantised adjoint representation and its quantum exterior algebra.
In §
3
we recall the definition of the
q
-deformed Clifford algebra of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
introduced in
[
KP
]
and
define Lie derivatives, contraction operators and the differential on it. We show that the defined operations
enjoy many features of their classical counterparts, in particular, Cartan’s magic formula holds for them.
In §
4
we propose a definition of a quantised
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-differential algebra and show that the
quantised exterior and Clifford algebras of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
are examples of such algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebras

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a complex Lie algebra. Let
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}[\xi]
be the Grassmann algebra with
one generator
\xi
, and let
\mathrm{d}:=\partial_{\xi}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}[\xi]
be the derivation with respect to
\xi
.
Set
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}:={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}[\xi]\inplus{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{d}
. Then

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}=\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1}\oplus\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}\oplus\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie superalgebra where

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1}={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\xi,\qquad\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes 1,\qquad\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}={\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{d}.

For
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
L_{x}:=x\otimes 1\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}
,
\iota_{x}:=x\otimes\xi\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1}
.
The non-zero bracket relations in
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
are defined as

(1)

\displaystyle[L_{x},\iota_{y}]=\iota_{[x,y]},\qquad[L_{x},L_{y}]=L_{[x,y]},\qquad[\iota_{x},\mathrm{d}]=L_{x}\qquad\text{for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$.}

2.1.1.
Digression: semisimple Lie superalgebras

Assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple.
Let
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}(n)
denote the Grassmann algebra with
n
generators

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
. Then
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}(n)
has a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading given by
\deg\xi_{i}=1
. Let
{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n):=\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}(n)
. Clearly,
{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie superalgebra where
\deg\partial_{\xi_{i}}=-1
. Let

{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)_{-1}
denotes the homogeneous component of degree
-1
.
As it was shown in
[
Che
]
,
any semisimple Lie superalgebra is the direct sum of the following summands

\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}\otimes{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}(n)\inplus\mathfrak{v},

where
\mathfrak{s}
is a simple Lie superalgebra,
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\mathfrak{s}
, and
\mathfrak{v}\subset{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)
is such that
the projection

\mathfrak{v}\to{\mathfrak{vect}}(0|n)_{-1}
is onto.
In our case (for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
) we have that
n=1
,
\mathfrak{v}=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}(\partial_{\xi})
,
\tilde{\mathfrak{s}}=\mathfrak{s}={\mathfrak{g}}
.

2.2.
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential spaces and algebras

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential space
is a superspace
V
, together with a
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
structure
\rho\colon\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
.
A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebra
is a superalgebra
A
, equipped with a structure of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential space
such that
\rho(x)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits A
for all
x\in\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
.
Observe that if
A
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebra then the contraction operators
\iota
define a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-equivariant
representation of
U(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1})\cong{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}
on
A
, where
U(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1})
is the universal
enveloping algebra of the Lie superalgebra
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{-1}
.
The idea of a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebra is due to H. Cartan
[
Car2
,
Car1
]
. We follow the terminology and
notation from
[
Mei
]
.

Example
.

Take
A={\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, equipped with the coadjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}}

denoted by
L_{x}
for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. For
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}={\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{1}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}

define the contraction operator by
\iota_{x}f=f(x)
. The odd map
i_{x}
is extended
to
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
by the super Leibniz rule. Let
e_{a}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
f_{a}
be the corresponding
dual basis in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
. The Lie algebra differential on
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
may be written as

\mathrm{d}_{\wedge}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}f_{a}\circ L_{e_{a}},

with
f_{a}
acting by the exterior multiplication.
Then
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebra. One can show that

H({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\mathrm{d}_{\wedge})\cong({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Example
.

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}
has a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
B
(for example, see review in
[
BKLS
]
), used to identify
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Let

\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
be the Clifford algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to
B
define by

\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})=T({\mathfrak{g}})/\left\langle x\otimes y+y\otimes x-2B(x,y)\mid x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}\right\rangle.

Let
z_{i}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, then the Chevalley map (or quantisation)

q_{\Cl}\colon{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
is defined by

z_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge z_{i_{k}}\mapsto z_{i_{1}}\ldots z_{i_{k}}\qquad\text{(and $1\mapsto 1$)},

where
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq\dim{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Set

\gamma=-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{\dim{\mathfrak{g}}}B([z_{a},z_{b}],z_{c})z_{a}\wedge z_{b}\wedge z_{c}\in({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{3}{\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}.

Define the map
\alpha\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
by

\alpha(x)=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{a,b=1}^{\dim{\mathfrak{g}}}B(x,[z_{a},z_{b}])z_{a}z_{b}\quad\text{for $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

The map
\alpha
extends to an algebra homomorphism
\alpha\colon U({\mathfrak{g}})\to\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
.

The Clifford algebra
\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
is a filtered
{\mathfrak{g}}
-differential algebra with differential, Lie derivatives
and contractions given as

\mathrm{d}_{\Cl}=[q_{\Cl}(\gamma),-]_{\Cl},\qquad L_{x}=[\alpha(x),-]_{\Cl},\qquad\iota_{x}=\frac{1}{2}[x,-]_{\Cl},\quad\text{for $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

where
[-,-]_{\Cl}
denotes the supercommutator in
\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
.
The quantisation map
q_{\Cl}\colon{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\Cl({\mathfrak{g}})
intertwines the Lie derivatives and contractions, but does
not intertwine the differential.
The cohomology of
(\Cl({\mathfrak{g}}),\mathrm{d}_{\Cl})
is trivial in all filtration degrees (except if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is abelian, in which case

\mathrm{d}_{\Cl}=0
); for example, see
[
Mei
, §7.1]
.

2.3.
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})

Fix a nonzero
q\in{\mathbb{C}}
which is not a root of unity.
The quantised enveloping algebra
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is the associative algebra with unit
generated by the elements
E
,
F
,
K
, and
K^{-1}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle KE=q^{2}EK,\quad KF=q^{-2}FK,\quad KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1,\quad EF-FE=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}.

A Hopf algebra structure on
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta E=E\otimes K+1\otimes E,\quad\Delta F=F\otimes 1+K^{-1}\otimes F,\quad\Delta K=K\otimes K,\quad\Delta K^{-1}=K^{-1}\otimes K^{-1},

\displaystyle S(E)=-EK^{-1},\quad S(F)=-KF,\quad S(K^{-1})=K,\quad S(K)=K^{-1},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(F)=0,\quad\varepsilon(K)=\varepsilon(K^{-1})=1,

where
\Delta
is the coproduct,
S
is the antipode, and
\varepsilon
is the counit.
In what follows we use Sweedler notation for the coproduct
\Delta x=\sum x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
,
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
be the weight lattice of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
,
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{+}
be the sublattice of dominant weights generated by the fundamental weight
\pi
.
The category of finite dimensional type 1 modules over
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is equivalent to the category of finite
dimensional
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
modules; for example, see
[
EGNO
, §5.8]
or
[
KS
, §3]
. For
\lambda\in{\mathcal{P}}_{+}
we denote the corresponding
type 1 finite dimensional
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-module with highest weight
\lambda
by
V_{\lambda}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
denote the vector subspace of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
spanned by the elements

X=E,\qquad Z=q^{-2}EF-FE,\qquad Y=KF.

The space
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
is closed with respect to the left adjoint action of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
on itself defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}y=\sum x_{(1)}yS(x_{(2)})\qquad\text{for $x,y\in U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$.}

It is easy to see that as a
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-module,
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
is isomorphic to the quantised adjoint
representation
V_{2\pi}
of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
.
In what follows we will use notation
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
to emphasise that elements
X
,
Z
, and
Y
belong to

{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)\subset U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
. In the case when
V_{2\pi}
is treated as an abstract
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-module and
in the case when we will construct quantum exterior and Clifford algebras, we will use the following notation
for basis elements in
V_{2\pi}
:

v_{2}=X,\qquad v_{0}=Z,\qquad v_{-2}=Y.

2.4.
Normalised braiding

The following construction is due to Drinfeld
[
Dri2
]
. Let
\mathsf{C}
be a braided monoidal
category linear over
{\mathbb{C}}[[\hbar]]
and assume that the braiding satisfies

\sigma_{W,V}\circ\sigma_{V,W}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{V\otimes W}+O(\hbar)
. Then the map

\tsigma_{V,W}=\sigma_{V,W}\circ(\sigma_{W,V}\circ\sigma_{V,W})^{-1/2},

is called a normalised braiding and defines a coboundary structure on
\mathsf{C}
in the sense of
[
Dri2
]
.
For details
see
[
EGNO
, Exercise 8.3.25 on p. 202]
.
In particular, we have that
\tsigma^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
.

The category of type one finite-dimensional
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-module is a braided monoidal category where the
braiding
\sigma
is given by the universal
R
-matrix; see
[
EGNO
, §8.3]
for details.
The
R
-matrix braiding
\sigma
satisfies the above condition.
In what follows we denote by
\tsigma
the corresponding normalised braiding.

2.5.
Quantum exterior algebras

Following
[
BZ
]
define the quantum exterior algebra
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
of
V_{2\pi}

as

{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}=T(V_{2\pi})/\langle v\otimes w+\tsigma(v\otimes w)\mid v,w\in V_{2\pi}\rangle

The algebra
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
is generated by
v_{2}
,
v_{0}
,
v_{-2}
subject to the following relations

\displaystyle v_{2}\wedge v_{2}={}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle v_{-2}\wedge v_{-2}={}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle v_{0}\wedge v_{2}={}

\displaystyle{}-q^{-2}v_{2}\wedge v_{0},

\displaystyle v_{-2}\wedge v_{0}={}

\displaystyle{}-q^{-2}v_{0}\wedge v_{-2},

\displaystyle v_{0}\wedge v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(1-q^{4})}{q^{3}}v_{2}\wedge v_{-2},

\displaystyle v_{-2}\wedge v_{2}={}

\displaystyle{}-v_{2}\wedge v_{-2}.

We note that
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded super algebra in the braided monoidal category of type 1
finite-dimensional
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-modules. The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading corresponding to a super algebra structure is
given by setting
p(v_{2})=p(v_{0})=p(v_{-2})={\bar{1}}
, where
p(v)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
denotes the parity of the element
v
.
The algebra
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
is (super)commutative with respect to normalised braiding, i.e.

v\wedge w=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}\wedge\circ\tsigma(v\otimes w)
for all parity homogeneous
v,w\in{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
.

3.
Motivating example:
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})

Fix a non-zero constant
c\in{\mathbb{C}}[q,q^{-1}]
.
First recall from
[
KP
, §2.7]
that
V_{2\pi}
admits a nondegenerate
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-invariant bilinear form given by

\langle v_{2},v_{-2}\rangle=c,\qquad\langle v_{0},v_{0}\rangle=q^{-3}(1+q^{2})c,\qquad\langle v_{-2},v_{2}\rangle=cq^{-2},

Note that the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is symmetric with respect to the normalised braiding
\tsigma
,
i.e.,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\circ\tsigma
.
The
q
-deformed Clifford algebra of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
was defined in
[
KP
, §3]
as filtered
deformation of
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
by the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
:

\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})=T(V_{2\pi})/\langle v\otimes w+\tsigma(v\otimes w)-2\langle v,w\rangle\mid v,w\in V_{2\pi}\rangle,

As it was shown in
[
KP
, Lemma 3.3]
, the algebra
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is generated by
v_{2}
,

v_{0}
,
v_{-2}
satisfying the following relations

\displaystyle v_{2}v_{2}={}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle v_{-2}v_{-2}={}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle v_{0}v_{2}={}

\displaystyle{}-q^{-2}v_{2}v_{0},

\displaystyle v_{-2}v_{0}={}

\displaystyle{}-q^{-2}v_{0}v_{-2},

\displaystyle v_{0}v_{0}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1-q^{4}}{q^{3}}v_{2}v_{-2}+\frac{q^{2}+1}{q}c1,

\displaystyle v_{-2}v_{2}={}

\displaystyle{}-v_{2}v_{-2}+\frac{q^{2}+1}{q^{2}}c1.

It is easy to see that
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is a filtered super algebra in the (braided) monoidal category of

U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-modules. We note that
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is a filtered
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-module where the
elements of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
act by operators of degree
0
.

3.1.
\tsigma
-commutators

For
x,y\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
homogeneous with respect to parity set

[x,y]_{\tsigma}:=\left(m_{\Cl_{q}}-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}m_{\Cl_{q}}\circ\tsigma\right)(x\otimes y),

where
m_{\Cl_{q}}
denotes the multiplication map in
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
.
The map
[-,-]_{\tsigma}
is extended to
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
by linearity.
By construction
[-,-]_{\tsigma}
is
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-equivariant since it is composed from equivariant maps.

Lemma
.

The bracket
[-,-]_{\tsigma}
is
\tsigma
-skew-symmetric:

\displaystyle[\omega,\mu]_{\tsigma}=-(-1)^{p(\omega)p(\mu)}[-,-]_{\tsigma}\circ\tsigma(\omega\otimes\mu)\qquad\text{for $\omega,\mu\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$,}

and has the filtration degree
-1
.

Proof.

The
\tsigma
-skew-symmetricity follows form the definition of
[-,-]_{\tsigma}
.
Since
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is a filtered deformation of the
\tsigma
-supercommutative
algebra
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
, it follows from the definition that the bracket
[-,-]_{\tsigma}
has the
filtration degree
-1
.
∎

3.2.
The
\alpha
and
\beta
maps and Lie derivatives

The quantum moment map (in the sense of
[
Lu
]
) is the algebra map
\alpha_{q}\colon U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})\to\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
defined in
[
KP
, §3.7]
. It is given on generators by

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(E)=-\frac{q}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{2}v_{0},\qquad\alpha_{q}(F)=-\frac{q^{2}}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{0}v_{-2},

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(K)=\frac{q^{3}-q}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{2}v_{-2}+q^{-1},\qquad\alpha_{q}(K^{-1})=-\frac{q^{3}-q}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{2}v_{-2}+q.

Since
\alpha_{q}
is an algebra map it follows that

\alpha_{q}(Z)=\frac{1}{c}v_{2}v_{-2}-1,\qquad\alpha_{q}(Y)=-\frac{q}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{0}v_{-2}.

As it was shown in
[
KP
, Lemma 3.7.1]
the inner
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-action defined by
\alpha

coincides with the natural one:

x\triangleright\omega=\sum\alpha_{q}(x_{(1)})\omega\alpha_{q}(S(x_{(2)}))\qquad\text{for $x\in U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$, $\omega\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$},

where
x\triangleright\omega
denotes the
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-action on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
.

Following the classical situation we define Lie derivatives on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
with respect to elements
of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
by

L_{x}\omega:=x\triangleright\omega\qquad\text{for $x\in U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$, $\omega\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$}.

Classically, the map
\alpha
defined the (adjoint) action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
by taking the (super)commutator; see Example
2.2.2.  Example
.
This is no longer true in the quantum case for
\tsigma
-commutators.
Define a linear map
\beta_{q}\colon{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)\to\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
by

\beta_{q}(X)=\frac{1+q^{2}}{q}\alpha_{q}(X),\quad\beta_{q}(Y)=\frac{1+q^{2}}{q}\alpha_{q}(Z),\quad\beta_{q}(Z)=\frac{1+q^{2}}{q}\alpha_{q}(Y).

The definition of
\beta_{q}
is motivated by the following lemma.

Proposition
.

The
\beta_{q}
-map defines the quantum Hamiltonian with respect to the
\tsigma
-commutator for the action of
elements of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)\subset U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
. Namely, we have that

L_{x}\omega=[\beta_{q}(x),\omega]_{\tsigma}\qquad\text{for $x\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)$, $\omega\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})$.}

Proof.

For
X\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
and
v_{2}\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
, we have that

\displaystyle[\beta_{q}(X),v_{2}]_{\tsigma}={}

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2c}v_{2}v_{0}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2c}v_{2}v_{2}v_{0}=\frac{1}{2q^{2}c}v_{2}v_{2}v_{0}=0=X\triangleright v_{2}.

The computations for other elements of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
and
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
are analogous.
∎

3.3.
Differential

Recall from
[
KP
, §3.4]
that the element

\gamma_{q}=-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}(cv_{0}+v_{2}v_{0}v_{-2})\in\Cl_{q}^{(3)}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})

squares to a scalar. Therefore, we can define a differential on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
by

\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}\omega_{q}=[\gamma_{q},\omega]_{\sigma}=\gamma_{q}\omega-(-1)^{p(\omega)}\omega\gamma_{q},\qquad\omega\in\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}).

Proposition
.

We have that

(1)

\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}x=2\beta_{q}(x)
for

(2)

the differential

Proof.

(1) We have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}v_{2}={}

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}(v_{2}(cv_{0}+v_{2}v_{0}v_{-2})+(cv_{0}+v_{2}v_{0}v_{-2})v_{2})

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}v_{0}={}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\left(\frac{2c(1-q^{4})}{q^{3}}v_{2}v_{-2}+\frac{2c^{2}(q^{2}+1)}{q}-\frac{2c(q^{2}+1)}{q^{3}}v_{2}v_{-2}\right)

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}v_{0}={}

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}(v_{-2}(cv_{0}+v_{2}v_{0}v_{-2})+(cv_{0}+v_{2}v_{0}v_{-2})v_{-2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{c}v_{0}v_{-2}=2\beta_{q}(Y).

(2) The equivariance of

A supervector space
W

{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-differential space if it is equipped with

.

4.3.
Quantum exterior algebra

First note that the associated graded algebra of
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is the quantum exterior
algebra
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
. For
x\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{q}(2)
, the associated graded maps

L_{x}\colon{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}\to V_{2\pi}
to the Lie derivatives

L_{x}\colon\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})\to\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
define an action of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
since the filtration
on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
is compatible with
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-action.

The differential
\mathrm{d}_{\Cl_{q}}
has filtered degree one. Therefore, we
can define the associated graded map
\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}\colon{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
which is

U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-equivariant by construction.
It is easy to see, c.f. Example
2.2.1.  Example
, that
\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}
is nonzero only for

\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}(v_{2})=-\frac{1}{c}v_{2}\wedge v_{0},\quad\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}(v_{0})=\frac{1+q^{2}}{qc}v_{2}\wedge v_{-2},\quad\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}(v_{-2})=-\frac{1}{c}v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}.

It is straightforward to check that
\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}^{2}=0
and that it satisfies the graded Leibniz rule, so it
defines a differential on
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
. Moreover, we have the quantised version of the
formula for differential, see Example
2.2.1.  Example
,

\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}=\frac{q^{4}}{1+q^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{c}v_{2}L_{Y}+\frac{q}{(1+q^{2})c}v_{0}L_{Z}+\frac{1}{q^{2}c}v_{-2}L_{X}\right).

Note that the formulas for the differential depend on the parameter
c
since we identify

{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}^{\ast}
with
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
via the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Similarly, for
x\in{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
we define the contraction operator

\iota_{x}\colon{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}V_{2\pi}
as the associated graded map for the contraction
operator on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
. By construction, the operators
\iota_{x}
define a
U_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
-equivariant representation of

{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
. In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{2}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{2}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{2}=q^{-2}c,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{0}=q^{-3}(1+q^{2})c,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{-2}=c,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{2}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{-2}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}=-\tfrac{1+q^{2}}{q^{3}}cv_{2},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}=cv_{0},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{-2}=-cv_{2},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{2}\wedge v_{-2}=\tfrac{1-q^{2}}{q^{2}}cv_{0},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{-2}=q^{-2}cv_{-2},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=-cv_{0},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=\tfrac{1+q^{2}}{q}cv_{-2},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=0,

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=cv_{2}\wedge v_{0},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{0}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=-\tfrac{1+q^{2}}{q}cv_{2}\wedge v_{-2},

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{-2}}v_{2}\wedge v_{0}\wedge v_{-2}=cv_{0}\wedge v_{-2}.

Cartan’s magic formula for associated graded maps
L_{x}
,
\iota_{x}
, and
\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}

on
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}_{q}V_{2\pi}
follows from the fact that Cartan’s magic formula on
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
has degree
0
.
It can also be checked by direct computations as follows.
First note that for elements of
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{0}V_{2\pi}
and
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{3}V_{2\pi}
Cartan’s magic formula holds trivially.
The operator
\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}\circ\iota_{x}
acts by zero on
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{1}V_{2\pi}

We have that

\iota_{v_{2}}\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}v_{-2}=-\frac{1}{c}\iota_{v_{2}}(v_{0}\wedge v_{-2})=v_{0}=L_{v_{2}}v_{-2}.

The operator
\iota_{x}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\wedge_{q}}

The algebras
\Cl_{q}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})

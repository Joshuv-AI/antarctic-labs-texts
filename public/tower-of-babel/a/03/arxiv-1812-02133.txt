Asymptotic invariants of lattices in locally compact groups
Alessandro Carderi

Abstract
. The aim of this work is to understand some of the asymptotic properties of sequences of lattices in a fixed locally compact group. In particular we will study the asymptotic growth of the Betti numbers of the lattices renormalized by the covolume and the rank gradient, the minimal number of generators also renormalized by the covolume. For doing so we will consider the ultraproduct of the sequence of actions of the locally compact group on the coset spaces and we will show how the properties of one of its cross sections are related to the asymptotic properties of the lattices.



Introduction

Let us consider a real-valued invariant
\mathcal{P}
of countable groups, that is,
\mathcal{P}
assigns to every countable group
\Gamma
a (possibly infinite) real number
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
. The main examples for us will be when
\mathcal{P}=\mathit{d}
is the rank, the minimal number of generators, and when
\mathcal{P}=b_{i}
is the
i
-th Betti number, that is, the dimension of the
i
-th homology group with rational coefficients. However the questions that we address are still interesting and often open for many other invariants such as the rank of the abelianization or the dimension of the homology with coefficients in a finite field. We want to understand the following problem, see
[
33
]
.

Approximation problem for
\mathcal{P}
.

For a countable, residually finite group
\Gamma
and a sequence of finite index subgroups
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
of
\Gamma
such that the index
[\Gamma\colon\Gamma_{n}]
tends to infinity does the sequence
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{n})/[\Gamma\colon\Gamma_{n}]
converge? If so, does the limit depend on the sequence
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
? Can we compute this limit?

A first striking result in this direction is the Lück’s approximation theorem
[
31
]
which states that whenever
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a compact manifold and
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
is a (nested) chain of normal subgroups of
\Gamma

, then the sequence
b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})/[\Gamma\colon\Gamma_{n}]
converges to the
i
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of the group
\Gamma
, which will be denoted by
\beta_{i}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, under some assumptions on the group and on the chain, the approximation problem is completely understood and the limit does not depend on the chain. The needed assumptions on the sequence of finite index subgroups were later weakened by Farber.

Definition
.

A sequence of finite index subgroups
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
is called
Farber
if for every
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{\mathrm{1}_{\Gamma}\}
we have

\lim_{n}\frac{\left|\left\{g\Gamma_{n}\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}\colon\gamma\in g\Gamma_{n}g^{-1}\right\}\right|}{[\Gamma\colon\Gamma_{n}]}=0.

Farber proved in
[
17
]
that the sequence
b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})/[\Gamma\colon\Gamma_{n}]
also converges to
\beta_{i}(\Gamma)
whenever
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
is a Farber and nested chain of finite index subgroups of
\Gamma
. The approximation problem for

was later studied in several different occasion, for example in the case of non Farber chains in
[
9
]
, for sofic approximations in
[
16
]
and in a more general setting in
[
13
]
.

Another invariant for which the approximation problem has been widely studied is the rank. Abért and Nikolov proved in
[
6

that whenever
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group and

is a Farber chain of finite index subgroups, then the sequence

converges towards the cost of the associated profinite action (minus

). In this case however it is still unknown whether the limit depends on the chosen Farber sequence, indeed it is unknown whether all free p.m.p. actions of a fixed countable group have the same cost,
[

In our work we are interested in a variant of the approximation problem for lattices in locally compact groups.

Lattice approximation problem for
\mathcal{P}

For a locally compact, second countable group

The lattice approximation problem was first studied for Betti numbers in

Definition

A sequence of lattices

•

Theorem

(Theorem

The case of Lie groups.

Loosely speaking the assumption of nowhere thinness is needed because the homology/cost of the limit will only depend on the thick part of the quotients by the lattices

Finally if

Ultraproducts of actions

All the above stated theorems will be derived using the notion of ultraproducts of p.m.p. actions of locally compact groups along some fixed ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group

Sketch of the proof(s)

Our understanding of the lattice approximation problem will be derived from the study of the regular ultraproduct and its cross section. Indeed using that the cross section of the regular ultraproduct is the ultraproduct of the cross sections we will reduce the problem to the case of ultraproducts of finite graphed equivalence relations studied in
[
13

Let us fix a connected Lie group

, denote by

a maximal compact subgroup. Put

be a geodesic

and a positive real number

. We will now assume that the union of these balls is
(M/\Gamma_{n})_{\varepsilon}
, which is obviously not true in general but

can be easily sandwiched between two such covers.

Let

be the associated

-invariant subset of

Let us fix an ultrafilter

. For every sequence of pointed

we can form the pointed (at the identity) Hausdorff limit to obtain a

is almost everywhere thick translates to the fact that

For every

Now as above we let

We say that an action is

1.1.
Continuity of actions

Denote by
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\mu)
the group of all measure preserving automorphism of the probability space
(X,\mu)
. This group is equipped with a topology, called the
weak topology
, which is induced by the functions
T\mapsto\mu(A\Delta TA)
for all measurable subsets

is a standard probability space.

An action of a l.c.s.c. group

on a probability space

is

if the induced map
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X,\mu)
is continuous.

Since we will encounter non-continuous actions of locally compact groups, it is convenient to introduce some more terminology. Assume that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts on the probability measure space
(X,\mu)
preserving the measure. Let
U\subseteq G
be a neighborhood of the identity and take
\varepsilon>0
. A measurable subset

is
(U,\varepsilon)
-

if
\sup_{g\in U}\mu(A\Delta gA)\leq\varepsilon
.

Observe that since the action is measure preserving,
A
is
(U,\varepsilon)
-invariant if and only if it is
(U\cup U^{-1},\varepsilon)
-invariant. We say that a measure preserving action of
G
on the probability space
(X,\mu)
is
continuous at the measurable subset

A\subseteq X
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
U
such that
A
is
(U,\varepsilon)
-invariant. Clearly by definition of the weak topology on

Lemma 1.2

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group

A,B\subseteq X
which are
(U,\varepsilon)
-invariant. Then
gA
is
(gUg^{-1},\varepsilon)
-invariant,
X\setminus A
is
(U,\varepsilon)
-invariant and
A\cap B
and
A\cup B
are
(U,2\varepsilon)
-invariant. In particular if

is continuous at
A
and
B
, then it is continuous at
gA
,
A\cap B
and
X\setminus A
.

If a l.c.s.c. group
G
acts in a Borel manner on the standard probability space
(X,\mu)
, then the induced map from
G
to
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\mu)
is Borel and hence is automatically continuous, see Theorem 2.3.3 of
[
22
]
. Therefore any Borel action is automatically continuous. Let us sketch the proof of the analogous statement for general measure spaces.

Proposition 1.3
.

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts measurably on the probability space
(X,\mu)
. Then the action is continuous.

Proof.

Take a measurable subset
A\subseteq X
, let
U\subseteq G
be a symmetric neighborhood of the identity and denote by
\lambda
a Haar measure on
G
. Take
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda(U))
. Since
\Phi^{-1}(A)\subseteq G\times X
is measurable there exists a measurable finite partition
\{W_{i}\}_{i}
of
U
and measurable subsets
B_{i}\subseteq X
such that if we set
T\coloneqq\cup_{i}W_{i}\times B_{i}
, then
\lambda\times\mu(T\Delta(U\times X\cap\Phi^{-1}(A)))<\varepsilon^{2}
. For
g\in G
, set
T_{g}\coloneqq\{x\in X\colon(g,x)\in T\}
and observe that if
g,h\in W_{i}
, then
T_{g}=T_{h}=B_{i}
. Put
V\coloneqq\left\{g\in U\colon\mu(gA\Delta T_{g})\leq\varepsilon\right\}
. Observe that
\lambda\times\mu(U\setminus V)\leq\varepsilon
.

Fix
i
such that
Z\coloneqq W_{i}\cap V
has positive Haar measure. By
[
19
, Proposition 443D (c)]
whenever
E\subseteq G
has positive Haar measure, the set
E^{-1}E
is a neighborhood of the identity. In particular,
Z^{-1}Z
contains an open neighborhood of the identity
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
. For every
u\in U^{\prime}
there are
g,h\in Z
such that
u=g^{-1}h
. Therefore

\mu(uA\Delta A)=\mu(gA\Delta hA)\leq\mu(gA\Delta T_{g})+\mu(hA\Delta T_{h})\leq 2\varepsilon.\qed

Since the groups we consider are separable every continuous action is
locally
described by a separable
\sigma
-algebra.

Lemma 1.4
.

Assume that
G
acts on the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
preserving the measure. Suppose that
P
is a subset of
\mathscr{B}
such that
G
is continuous at every element of
P
. Then
G
acts continuously on the
G
-invariant
\sigma
-subalgebra
\mathscr{B}_{P}
generated by
P
.

If
P
can be generated by a countable subset, then
\mathscr{B}_{P}
is separable.

Proof.

Let
H\leq G
be a dense countable subgroup and let us denote by
\mathcal{A}_{H}
the algebra of subsets generated by
\{hP\}_{h\in H}
. By Lemma
1.2
, the group
G
is continuous at every element of
\mathcal{A}_{H}
. Observe that if
P
is generated by a countable subset, then
\mathcal{A}_{H}
is. Since
H\leq G
is dense and the action is continuous, we have that
\mathcal{A}_{H}
is dense in the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{G}
generated by the
G
-translates of
P
. Remark that
\mathcal{A}_{H}\subseteq\mathscr{B}_{P}
is dense, that is for every element
B\in\mathscr{B}_{P}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
A_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}
such that
\mu(B\Delta A_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
, see for example
[
19
, Proposition 136H]
. Finally, let us check that the action of
G
is continous at every element of
\mathscr{B}_{P}
. Given
B\in\mathscr{B}_{P}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is an element
A_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}
and a neighborhood of the identity
U\subseteq G
such that
A_{\varepsilon}
is
(U,\varepsilon)
-invariant and
\mu(A_{\varepsilon}\Delta B)\leq\varepsilon
. Then
B
is
(U,3\varepsilon)
-invariant, indeed

\sup_{g\in U}\mu(B\Delta gB)\leq\sup_{g\in U}\left(\mu(A_{\varepsilon}\Delta B)+\mu(A_{\varepsilon}\Delta gA_{\varepsilon})+\mu(gB\Delta gA_{\varepsilon})\right)\leq 3\varepsilon.\qed

Suppose that
G
acts on the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
preserving the measure. The set of sub-
\sigma
-algebras of
\mathscr{B}
for which the action of
G
is continuous admits joins, that is for every two continuous sub-
\sigma
-algebras the
\sigma
-algebra generated by them is still continuous. Therefore there exists a maximal sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathscr{B}
for which the action of
G
is continuous. This
\sigma
-algebra is explicit: it is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all the

such that
G
is continuous at
A
.

1.2.
Ultraproducts

We will now define the
regular ultraproduct
of
G
-probability spaces. Let us fix a (non principal) ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets and let
X
be their Cartesian product
X\coloneqq\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
. The
ultraproduct
of the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
is the set
[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}/\sim_{\mathfrak{u}}
where the equivalence relation
\sim_{\mathfrak{u}}
is defined as usual by saying that
(x_{n})_{n}\sim_{\mathfrak{u}}(y_{n})_{n}
if
x_{n}=y_{n}
for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every
n
.

When the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
is clear from the context we put
X_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
. We will denote by
x_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
A_{\mathfrak{u}}
elements and subsets of
X_{\mathfrak{u}}
. For a sequence
\{x_{n}\in X_{n}\}_{n}
we will denote by
[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
its class in
X_{\mathfrak{u}}
and similarly for a sequence of subsets
\{A_{n}\subseteq X_{n}\}_{n}
we will denote its class by
[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
. If each

is a probability space, then there is a canonical way to construct a probability measure on
X_{\mathfrak{u}}
, the Loeb probability space, see for example
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\{(X_{n},\mathscr{B}_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of probability spaces and let
X_{\mathfrak{u}}
be the ultraproduct of the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
. Then there exists a complete probability space
(X_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mu_{\mathfrak{u}})
such that

(1)

for every sequence
\{A_{n}\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\}_{n}
the set
[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
is in
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}([A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{n}(A_{n})
,

(2)

for every
A_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}
there exists a sequence
\{A_{n}\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\}_{n}
such that
\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}(A_{\mathfrak{u}}\Delta[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=0
.

From now on we fix a l.c.s.c. group
G
and we assume that it acts on the sequence of probability spaces
\{(X_{n},\mathscr{B}_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
preserving the measure. We can define an action of
G
on the ultraproduct by the formula
g[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq[gx_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
. This action preserves the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}
on
X_{\mathfrak{u}}
. However even if the action of
G
on
X_{n}
is continuous for every
n
, the action of
G
on the ultraproduct

may be neither continuous nor measurable. The main point of this section is to define a continuous factor of the ultraproduct action.

Definition 1.7
.

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts measurably on the sequence of probability spaces
\{(X_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n}
. We set
[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\coloneqq\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}/\sim^{R}_{\mathfrak{u}}
for the equivalence relation defined by
(x_{n})_{n}\sim^{R}_{\mathfrak{u}}(y_{n})_{n}
if there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
of elements of
G
such that
\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}g_{n}=\mathrm{1}_{G}
and
g_{n}x_{n}=y_{n}
for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every
n
.

If the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
of spaces is clear from the context we will put
X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\coloneqq[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
. We will denote by
[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\in X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
the point associated with the sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
. We will often denote by
A_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
subsets of
X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
. Put
G^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq\left\{(g_{n})_{n}\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}G\colon\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}g_{n}=\mathrm{1}_{G}\right\}.
Since
G
is a topological group multiplication and inversion are continuous in

and therefore
G^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is a group. Whenever
G
acts on the sequence of spaces
\{(X_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n}
we can define an action of

on

by
(g_{n})_{n}[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq[g_{n}x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}.
Observe that
[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}/G_{\mathfrak{u}}^{0}.
We will denote by
\pi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}:[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\rightarrow[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
the associated quotient map. For every

gG^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}g^{-1}=G^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}
so the group
G
acts on

by the same formula

and
\pi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
is
G
-invariant.

If
X_{n}=G/\Gamma_{n}
for some sequence of lattices
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
and

is the normalized Haar measure, then

corresponds to the metric ultraproduct of the sequence

with respect to a suitable proper metric. Observe however that if
\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})
tends to infinity, then the limit metric on
[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
is only defined with values in

. Two points in

are at bounded distance for this limit metric if and only if they are in the same

-orbit. We will now define the measurable structure and the probability measure on

.

Definition 1.8
.

We say that a sequence of subsets

is (
\mathfrak{u}
-)
regular
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a neighborhood of the identity

such that

is measurable for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every
n
and for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every

.

Remark that for every sequence of subsets

and neighborhood of the identity

G_{\mathfrak{u}}^{0}[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[UA_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}

of

.

Definition 1.9
.

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts measurably on the sequence of probability spaces
\{(X_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n}
and let
[X_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
be as in Definition
1.7
. We let
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}
be the smallest
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of

which is generated by the sets
[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
for regular sequences
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
. Then we have that

\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}(A^{R}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}((\pi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R})^{-1}(A_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}))
. We let now
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
be the

-completion on the

. We will call the measure space

the

of the sequence of probability spaces
\{(X_{n},\mathscr{B}_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n}
.

In particular if
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
is regular, we have that

\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}([A_{n}]^{R}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}(G_{\mathfrak{u}}^{0}[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}([A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{n}(A_{n}),

and the action of
G
on

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. group and let
(H_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of subgroups of

. We say that

converges to the subgroup

of

with respect to the

, if for every compact subset

and neighborhood of the identity

, for every

and

admits a converging sub-sequence. Indeed given
(H_{n})_{n}

H_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq\{\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}h_{n}\colon h_{n}\in H_{n}\}
. Then it is easy to remark that

is a closed subgroup and
(H_{n})_{n}
converges to

with respect to the Hausdorff pointed topology along the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. group. Suppose that
G
acts in a Borel manner on the sequence of standard probability spaces
\{(X_{n},\mu_{n})\}_{n}
preserving the measure. For every sequence
\{x_{n}\in X_{n}\}_{n}
the stabilizer

converges to
G_{[x]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}}
in the Hausdorff pointed topology along the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
.

Proof.

Assume that for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every
n
we have that
g_{n}x_{n}=x_{n}
and that the sequence
g_{n}
is bounded. Put
g\coloneqq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}g_{n}
and observe that
(h_{n})_{n}\coloneqq(gg_{n}^{-1})_{n}\in G^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}
. So
g[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[gx_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[h_{n}x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}.
On the other hand assume that
g[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
. Then there exists a sequence
(g_{n})_{n}\in G^{0}_{\mathfrak{u}}
such that
gx_{n}=g_{n}x_{n}
for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every

whence

and
\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}g_{n}^{-1}g=g
.
∎

The great advantage of the regular ultraproduct is that the group
G
acts continuously and measurably on it, see Theorem
1.16
. It follows quite easily from the definition that the action is continuous, however the proof that the action is measurable is more complicated. For this, we will have to understand regular subsets better. These sets behave like compact subsets in a standard probability space.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
be a regular sequence of subsets and let
U_{k}
be neighborhoods of the identity such that
\cap_{k}U_{k}=\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}
. Then
\cap_{k}U_{k}[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
.

Proof.

For the proof we will assume that
U_{k}
is symmetric and that
U_{k}\supset U_{k+1}
for every
k
. An element
[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\in\cap_{k}U_{k}[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
if and only if for every
k
there exists

and a sequence of elements
g^{k}_{n}
such that
u_{k}g_{n}^{k}x_{n}\in A_{n}
for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every

and
\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}g_{n}^{k}=\mathrm{1}_{G}
. Set
\alpha_{k}\coloneqq\{n\geq k\colon g_{n}^{k}\in U_{k}\text{ and }u_{k}g_{n}^{k}x_{n}\in A_{n}\}
. By assumption
\alpha_{k}\in\mathfrak{u}
for every
k
. Set
k_{n}\coloneqq 1
for all

and define
k_{n}
to be the maximum of all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\in\alpha_{k}
otherwise. Put
g_{n}\coloneqq u_{k_{n}}g^{k_{n}}_{n}
. Observe that for every

we have that

. Moreover for every

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 1.12
.

•

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{B_{n}\}_{n}
be regular sequences. Then
\{A_{n}\cap B_{n}\}_{n}

\{A_{n}\cup B_{n}\}_{n}

•

For every

we let

be a regular sequence. Then there exists a regular sequence

\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}(\cap_{i}[A_{n}^{i}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\setminus[A_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R})=0

For every

and

(UA\setminus A)\cup(UB\setminus B)

\{B_{n}\}_{n}
are regular sequences, then
\{A_{n}\cap B_{n}\}_{n}
and

[x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\in[A_{n}\cup B_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}

(g_{n})_{n}\in G_{\mathfrak{u}}^{0}

\mathfrak{u}

Fix now for every

a regular sequence

Lemma 1.14
.

Assume that the l.c.s.c. group
G

\{X\setminus\overline{B}_{r}A_{n}\}_{n}
are regular sequences.

Proof.

Let us prove the lemma for
\{B_{r}A_{n}\}_{n}
, the proof for the other cases is similar. Set
\alpha_{n}(r)\coloneqq\mu_{n}(B_{r}A_{n})
and observe that it is an increasing function. Therefore the function
\alpha_{\mathfrak{u}}(r)\coloneqq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{n}(B_{r}A_{n})
is increasing and hence continuous almost everywhere. Let

be a point of continuity of

. We claim that

is a regular sequence. Indeed let us fix

. By continuity there exists

. For
\mathfrak{u}
-almost all

and

are both small than

. Hence we get that for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost all

that is

which implies that

is regular.
∎

We are now able to prove the following useful characterization.

Proposition 1.15
.

For every
A_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
and every

there is a regular subset

such that
[B_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\subseteq A_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}(A_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\setminus[B_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let us denote by

the set of subsets

for which both

and its complement satisfy the condition in the statement. Observe that since the definition is symmetric

implies that

. Lemma

are in

Using the above lemmas, we can finally show that the group

Theorem 1.16

Proof.

The action is clearly continuous at every regular subset

Let

2.1.
Cross sections and standard factors

Working outside the context of standard probability spaces has the big disadvantage that one cannot use many of the standard facts about ergodic theory. Many of the arguments can be easily adapted to the more general setting, as we have seen for cross sections. In some other cases, it is possible to reduce the problem to the context of standard probability spaces by the means of a
standard factor
. This phenomenon is widely used in the context of model theory: the
downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem
roughly states that every model of a countable theory has a countable model. We will now state and prove a version of this theorem for actions of l.c.s.c. groups, see Proposition
2.12
, and for actions with a fixed cross section, see Proposition
2.13
. These results are not necessary to obtain our main theorems and applications, however these propositions will allow us to ignore some technicalities and, in some sense, always pretend we are working with standard Borel spaces.

The following proposition is a slightly more general version of
[
40
, Proposition B.5]
. We will not present the proof of it since it is basically the same as the case of standard Borel spaces.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. group. Suppose that

measurably and preserving the measure. Assume furthermore that

2.2.
Ultraproducts of cross sections

Now that we have collected enough information about cross sections outside the context of standard probability spaces, we can finally state and prove the main technical theorem of this work: the ultraproduct of cross sections is a cross section of the ultraproduct. This will allow us to reduce problems of convergence of invariants for actions of locally compact groups to invariants of their cross sections.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. unimodular group, fix an open neighborhood of the identity
U^{\prime}\subseteq G
and a Haar measure
\lambda
. Suppose that
G
acts on the sequence of standard probability spaces
(X_{n},\mu_{n})
. Assume that for every
n
there exists a
U^{\prime}
-cross section
Y_{n}\subseteq X_{n}
such that
\{\operatorname{covol}(Y_{n})^{-1}\}_{n}
is bounded and denote by
\nu_{n}
the measure on
Y_{n}
as in Proposition
2.3
. Take a compact neighborhood of the identity
U\subseteq U^{\prime}
. Let

be the ultraproduct of
\{(Y_{n},\nu_{n})\}_{n}
. Consider the function

\displaystyle\Psi\colon G\times Y_{\mathfrak{u}}\rightarrow X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\quad\text{ }\quad\Psi(g,[y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})\coloneqq[\Phi_{n}(g,y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}.

Then

is an external
U
-cross section of the action of
G

G
-invariant measurable subset

with covolume
\operatorname{covol}(Y_{\mathfrak{u}})=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\operatorname{covol}(Y_{n})
.

Let us observe that
G[\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\subseteq X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
is measurable. First since

is compact it is not hard to show that the sequence

is regular. Now take a sequence of open, bounded subsets

such that the sequence
\{U_{i}\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n})\}_{n}
is regular and
\cup_{i}U_{i}=G
. Then by Lemma

we have
\cup_{i}[U_{i}\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=\cup_{i}\overline{U}_{i}[\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=G[\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}

\mu_{n}(\Phi_{n}(U\times Y_{n}))=\lambda(U)/\operatorname{covol}(Y_{n})
and therefore the assumptions implies that

has positive measure. However it is possible that

has measure strictly less than
1
and hence

is not always an external cross section of
X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the Borel subset
Y_{n}\subseteq X_{n}
is a
U
-cross section and we let
\mathcal{R}_{n}
be the cross equivalence relation on
Y_{n}
constructed before Proposition
2.7
. Theorem
2.14
tells us that the ultraproduct
Y_{\mathfrak{u}}
is an external cross section of
X_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}
and we will denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{u}}
the cross equivalence relation on
Y_{\mathfrak{u}}
. We will now prove that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is the ultraproduct of the sequence
\mathcal{R}_{n}
with respect to a preferred sequence of graphings. Before stating the theorem, we will need to fix some terminology.

A
graphing

\Theta=\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of a p.m.p. equivalence relation

on the probability space

is a countable collection of partial measure preserving isomorphisms

of

such that

•

for almost every

and
j\in J
, if
\varphi_{j}(y)
is defined, then

.

A graphing is said to be
generating
if

•

the equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
is the smallest equivalence relation containing the graph of each

for

Clearly given a graphing

of an equivalence relation

by

For cross sections we have a somewhat canonical graphing given by Corollary

Proof of Theorem

and Theorem

We start by proving Theorem

. In the proof we will set

\Psi_{U}\coloneqq\Psi\bigr|_{U\times Y_{\mathfrak{u}}}\text{ and }\Phi_{n,U}\coloneqq\Phi\bigr|_{U\times Y_{n}}.

Let us start by showing that
\Psi_{U}
is injective. For this, take
u,u^{\prime}\in U
and
[y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}},[y^{\prime}_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\in Y_{\mathfrak{u}}

[\Phi_{n}(u,y_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=\Psi(u,[y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=\Psi(u^{\prime},[y_{n}^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=[\Phi_{n}(u^{\prime},y_{n}^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}

and

for
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every
n
and therefore

. Hence
\Psi_{U}
is injective as claimed.

We will now prove that
\Psi_{U}
is a measurable isomorphism with its image. If
B\subseteq U^{\prime}
is a compact subset and
[C_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq Y_{\mathfrak{u}}
is a measurable subset, then
\{\Phi_{n}(B\times C_{n})\}_{n}
is regular. Indeed let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a neighborhood of the identity

such that

. Put

. For
\mathfrak{u}
-almost every

\mu_{n}(V\Phi_{n}(B\times C_{n}))=\operatorname{covol}(Y_{n})^{-1}\lambda(VB)\nu_{n}(C_{n})\leq\operatorname{covol}(Y_{n})^{-1}\lambda(B)\nu_{n}(C_{n})+(3c/2)\varepsilon.

In particular since
\{\Phi_{n}(\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}\times C_{n})\}_{n}

1.13

\Psi(B\times[C_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=B\Psi(\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}\times[C_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}})=B[\Phi_{n}(\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}\times C_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}=[\Phi_{n}(B\times C_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}.

3.
Rank and cost

The

of a measure preserving action of a countable group and more generally of a p.m.p. equivalence relation (with countable orbits) was defined by Levitt in
[

and widely studied in
[

[

is a countable set of measure preserving partially defined bijections of the probability space whose graphs are contained in

and we define the

to be the sum of the measures of the domains of the partially defined bijections. A graphing of
\mathcal{R}
is

if the smallest equivalence relation containing the graphing is (up to a null set)

itself. The
cost of the equivalence relation

\mathcal{R}
is the infimum of the costs of all generating graphings of
\mathcal{R}
.

In our work we will also need the notion of cost for measure preserving actions of unimodular locally compact groups. Observe however, as for the

-Betti numbers

35

[

Definition 3.1

Let

be a unimodular l.c.s.c. group

are cross sections, then

with respect to a fixed Haar measure. The

can also be posed in the context of l.c.s.c. groups: does there exist a l.c.s.c. unimodular group

equipped with a fixed Haar measure

(which does not depend on the measure) since every cross equivalence relation is amenable,

. It has also been announced that

has fixed price

for

and even whether for any l.c.s.c. group

the group

.

The definition of cost clearly makes sense outside the context of standard probability space; one can use an external cross section instead of a cross section. Also remark that combining Proposition

are injective. By compactness there are finitely
\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{1}),\ldots,\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{h})\in\rho^{\Gamma}(Y)
such that
\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{1})=\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{n})=\mathrm{1}_{G}
and
\ell
is contained in
\cup_{i}\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{i})U^{2}
and
\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{i+1})\in U^{4}\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{i})
. Consider a lifting
p
of
\ell
inside
M
whose starting point is the identity. Then there are
\rho(\tilde{y}_{i})\in\rho(\widetilde{Y})
which lifts the elements
\rho^{\Gamma}(y_{i})
such that
\rho^{\Gamma}(\tilde{y}_{1})=\mathrm{1}_{G}
and such that
p
is contained in
\cup_{i}\rho(\tilde{y}_{i})U^{2}
and
\rho(\tilde{y}_{i+1})\in U^{4}\rho(\tilde{y}_{i})
. This implies also that
\tilde{y}_{i+1}\in U^{4}\tilde{y}_{i}
. Finally observe that the image of
\ell
seen as an element of
\pi_{1}(\rho^{\Gamma}(YU^{2}))
inside
\pi_{1}(M/\Gamma)=\Gamma
is the element
\rho(\tilde{y}_{h})\in\Gamma
. Therefore we can use Lemma
3.8
to complete the proof.
∎

Combining the above proposition, Theorem
3.9
and a result of Gelander
[
23
]
we obtain the following.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group without compact factors and fix a Haar measure on
G
. Let
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
be a Farber sequence of torsion free lattices. Then

\mathrm{Cost}\left(G\curvearrowright[G/\Gamma_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}^{R}\right)\geq 1+\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\mathit{d}(\Gamma_{n})-1}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}\geq\mathrm{Cost}(G).

Proof.

Gelander proved in Theorem 1.5, Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.8, Theorem 7.4 and Section 9 of
[
23
]
, that for every such a Lie group

there exists a neighborhood of the identity
U
(which is just a ball of some radius) for which for every lattice
\Gamma
there exists

satisfying that the map induced by the inclusion from
\pi_{1}(\rho^{\Gamma}(YU^{2}))
to
\pi_{1}(M/\Gamma)=\Gamma
is surjective. Therefore given a Farber sequence
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
of lattices we can find cross sections

for which
\Gamma_{n}^{K}=\Gamma_{n}
for every

and hence Theorem
3.9
allows us to conclude the proof.
∎

4.
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers

In the last section, we studied the asymptotics of the minimal number of generators of lattices of unimodular, compactly generated l.c.s.c. groups. We showed that the limit is related to the cost of the group. In this section, we will study the similar question for the Betti numbers of the lattices. Since we will work with higher homological invariants, the hypothesis that
G
is compactly generated is not sufficient anymore. We will need that
G
acts cocompactly and properly on a
nice
topological space.

We say that an open cover of a topological space is a
good cover
if the finite intersections of open sets of the cover are either empty or contractible. The
nerve
of an open cover is the simplicial complex whose vertices are the elements of the open cover and whose
k
-simplices correspond to the intersections of
k+1
open sets. It is well known, see for example Corollary
4G.3
of
[
26
]
, that the nerve of a good cover of a paracompact space is homotopic to the space itself.

Definition 4.1
.

A
well-covered

G
-
space
is a topological space
M
on which
G
acts continuously on the right and such that

•

the action is
proper
, that is the induced map
G\times M\rightarrow M\times M
given by
(g,m)\mapsto(mg^{-1},m)
is proper
5
5

5

A map is proper if the preimage of any compact set is compact.
;

•

the action is
cocompact
, that is the quotient
M/G
is a compact and Hausdorff space;

•

there exists a
G
-
invariant good cover

\mathcal{O}
made of open and relatively compact sets;

•

there are
fine good covers
, that is there are compact subsets
A_{M}\subseteq M
and
A_{G}\subseteq G
such that for every symmetric neighborhood of the identity
W
, there are
O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}\in\mathcal{O}
satisfying
\cup_{i}O_{i}\subseteq A_{M}
,
(\cup_{i}O_{i})G=M
and whenever we have
O_{i}g_{1}\cap O_{j}\neq\emptyset
and
O_{i}g_{2}\cap O_{j}\neq\emptyset
for some
1\leq i,j\leq m
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, then
g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}\in WK
for some compact subgroup
K\subseteq A_{G}
.

Notable examples of well-covered

-spaces are given by isometric, proper and cocompact actions of l.c.s.c. groups on Riemannian manifolds. Indeed the set of balls of arbitrarily small radius form the fine good coverings, see Lemma
4.19
. Another example is given by proper and cocompact simplicial actions on simplicial spaces and in this case the fine good coverings are given by the

of barycentric subdivisions, see Lemma
4.21
. The last condition of Definition
4.1
is inspired by the above two cases, it is a key feature that both open covers share and it is what is needed in order to see

and

as
\Gamma
-simplicial complexes in a coherent way, see

.

For a topological space

we will denote by

the

-th homology group (with rational coefficients). From now on, we will also use the terminology introduced in Definition
3.4
. The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a unimodular l.c.s.c. compactly generated group and fix a Haar measure
\lambda
on
G
. Let
M
be a contractible well-covered
G
-space. Let
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
be a Farber sequence of torsion free lattices of

. Then we have

\beta_{i}(G)\leq\liminf_{n}\frac{b_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})},

and if the sequence
\{\Gamma_{n}\}
is nowhere thin, then

\beta_{i}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{b_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

By
\beta_{i}(G)
we mean the

-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of

as defined in
[

]
. This invariant is equal to the quantity
\beta_{i}(\Gamma)/\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma)
for any lattice

[

. In this work, we will not directly work with the

-Betti numbers of

-Betti numbers of the cross-sections of its actions. Indeed, given any free p.m.p. action of

and given any cross-section

[

]
and the discussions in Section

It is not hard to observe that if

is a cocompact torsion free lattice of

, see Corollary

. In Section

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
G
be a unimodular l.c.s.c. compactly generated group and let us fix a Haar measure
\lambda
on
G
. Let
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
be a Farber sequence of cocompact torsion free lattices of
G
. Assume that one of the following conditions holds.

•

The group

is almost connected.

•

The group
G
is totally disconnected and it acts cocompactly and properly on a contractible simplicial complex.

•

The quotient
G/G_{0}
satisfies the previous condition, where

denotes the connected component of the identity

Then

\beta_{i}(G)\leq\liminf_{n}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}

and if the sequence
\{\Gamma_{n}\}
is nowhere thin, then

\beta_{i}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

Before stating the next theorem we will need the following notation: for an inclusion of topological spaces

.

Let us assume that the well-covered
G

such that

be a lattice and

and we will set

. We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G
be a unimodular l.c.s.c. compactly generated group and let us fix a Haar measure
\lambda
on

. Let

be a contractible well-covered

such that

\beta_{i}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{\nabla_{i}((M/\Gamma_{n})_{\varepsilon},M/\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

Let us give a concrete example. Let

be a Lie group, let us denote by

Theorem

becomes extremely easy to understand whenever we have that

already contains all the homological information of the space, that is when

, or more generally when the dimension of the homology of

, the work of Gromov,

and the more recent work of Gelander,

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume that
G
is a connected semisimple Lie group and let us fix a Haar measure
\lambda
on
G
. Let
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
be a Farber sequence of torsion free lattices of
G
.

•

It always follows that

\beta_{1}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{b_{1}(\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

•

If

has higher rank and
\Gamma_{n}
is not cocompact for every

\beta_{i}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

•

If

has rank
1
and the associated symmetric space

has dimension

, then for every

\beta_{i}(G)=\lim_{n}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{n})}.

If

is cocompact for every

.

Proof.

In all cases we have that
\nabla_{i}((M/\Gamma_{n})_{\varepsilon},M/\Gamma_{n})=b_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})
. The first two points follow from the work of Gelander
[

]
exactly as in the proof of Theorem

[

for the hyperbolic case, and the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence.

Indeed let us fix a lattice

of a rank

or

. For

if follows from the fist part and Poincaré duality implies that the statement holds for

whenever

is cocompact. Therefore let us fix

. The thick-thin decomposition states that there exists

, such that

is composed of a disjoint union of neighborhoods of closed geodesics and cusps. Fix

and let us denote by

and

the open set obtain as the union of the neighborhoods of the closed geodesics and cusps respectively, so that

.

First remark that the cusps are homeomorphic to products of manifolds with the reals. In particular, it is easy to check that
\nabla_{i}((M/\Gamma_{n})_{\varepsilon}\cup B,M/\Gamma_{n})=b_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})
. On the other hand, let us observe that each connected component of

is a disk bundle over a circle. Therefore

is homotopic to a sphere bundle over a circle and its homology vanishes in every dimension not equal to

and

Now if

and hence the map

∎

We do not know whether for a rank

4.2.
From well-covered

Let

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

Theorem

(Theorem

The

,

Remark that since

[g_{n}y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}=g_{\mathfrak{u}}[y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}

then the

with

,

The higher skeletons of

\bigcap_{j=0,\ldots,k}O_{i_{j}}f_{i_{j}}g^{(j)}_{n}\neq\emptyset\text{ for }\mathfrak{u}\text{-almost every }n.

It is easy to see that

is a

and it is the

. Since the action of

\beta_{i}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{K}M)=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\beta_{i}(\Sigma_{n}^{K}M).

We are left to show that

. For this, let us fix

In order to show that the simplicial complex is contractible, we can argue as in

such that any homotopy sphere lying in

\mathfrak{u}
-almost every

\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n}
is a Farber sequence. Indeed, by definition
\Sigma_{n,y_{n}}^{K}M
is the nerve of the cover
\mathcal{O}_{n,y_{n}}/y_{n}\Gamma_{n}y^{-1}_{n}

\mathcal{O}_{n,y_{n}}/y_{n}\Gamma_{n}y^{-1}_{n}
and

coincide on bigger and bigger subsets. In particular for each

, the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{n,y_{n}}^{K,(k)}M
is the nerve of a finite sub-covering of
\mathcal{O}_{n,y_{n}}
of

. Since

is contractible, for every

there is

such that every homotopy sphere in
\Sigma_{n,y_{n}}^{K,(k_{0})}M
has to be null-homotopic in some
\Sigma_{n,y_{n}}^{K,(k_{1})}M

∎

We will now show that the

is small. We say that an open set

is a

if the closure of

meets the complement of
M_{n,y}/(y\Gamma_{n}y^{-1})
in

. Recall that given a neighborhood of the identity
U\subseteq G
and

, we defined

(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{U}=\{g\in G\colon g\Gamma_{n}g^{-1}\cap U=\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}\}.

Remark that given a neighborhood of the identity
U\subseteq G
and any subset
B\subseteq G
, for every
n
we have

(2)

B(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{B^{-1}UB}\subseteq(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{U}.

Indeed if
g\in(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{B^{-1}UB}
and

, then

bg\Gamma_{n}g^{-1}b^{-1}\cap U\subseteq g\Gamma_{n}g^{-1}\cap B^{-1}UB\subseteq\{\mathrm{1}_{G}\}.

Lemma 4.15
.

For every
n
, pick
y_{n}\in Y_{n}
. Then the number of boundary elements in
\mathcal{O}_{n,y_{n}}/(y_{n}\Gamma y^{-1}_{n})
grows sublinearly with respect to
|Y_{n}|
as

tends to infinity (along the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
).

Proof.

As remarked after Lemma
4.13
, the lemma does not depend on the choice of
y_{n}\in Y_{n}
. So let us assume that
y_{n}
is the class of the identity for every
n
. Let us fix
g\in\omega_{n,y_{n}}
and a neighborhood of the identity
B\subseteq G
. Then
g\in(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{B}
if and only if
g\Gamma_{n}\in(X_{n})_{B}
. Lemma
4.10
yields that
V^{2}Y_{n}\supseteq(X_{n})_{V_{1}}
. We claim that
V^{2}\omega_{n,y_{n}}\supseteq(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}
. Indeed, if
g\in(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}
, then
g\Gamma_{n}\in(X_{n})_{V_{1}}
and therefore there are
v\in V^{2}
,
g_{n}\in\omega_{n}
such that
g\Gamma_{n}=vg_{n}y_{n}
. That is,
g\in vg_{n}\Gamma_{n}\subseteq V^{2}\omega_{n,y_{n}}
. In particular, we have that

M_{n,y_{n}}=\cup_{i}O_{i}F\omega_{n,y_{n}}\supseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}V^{2}\omega_{n,y_{n}}\supseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}.

If
g\in\omega_{n,y_{n}}\cap(G)_{(SF)^{-1}V_{1}SF}^{\Gamma_{n}}
, Equation (
2
) yields

\cup_{i}\bar{O}_{i}Fg\subseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}SFg\subseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}SF(G)_{(SF)^{-1}V_{1}SF}^{\Gamma_{n}}\subseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}\subseteq M_{n,y}.

Therefore the number of boundary elements is bounded by

m|F|\left|Y_{n}\setminus(X_{n})_{(SF)^{-1}V_{1}SF}\right|.

Finally remark that since

is Farber,

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\left|Y_{n}\cap(X_{n})_{(SF)^{-1}V_{1}SF}\right|}{|Y_{n}|}=1

so the proof of the lemma is concluded.
∎

As a direct consequence of the above lemma and the Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.16
.

In the above notation, for every
y\in Y_{n}
,

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{b_{i}(M_{n,y}/y\Gamma_{n}y^{-1})}{|Y_{n}|}=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(M_{n,y}/y\Gamma_{n}y^{-1},M/y\Gamma_{n}y^{-1})}{|Y_{n}|}.

Proof.

Fix
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
. To simplify our notation, as for the lemma above, we can assume that
y
is the class of the identity, so that
y\Gamma_{n}y^{-1}=\Gamma_{n}
and set
M_{n}\coloneqq M_{n,y}
.
Denote by
\alpha_{n,i}
the dimension of the kernel of the map induced by the inclusion
H_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n})\rightarrow H_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})
. By definition

\alpha_{n,i}+\nabla_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})=b_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n}).

Denote by
E_{n}\subseteq M_{n}/\Gamma_{n}
the union of the boundary open sets. Set

D_{0}\coloneqq M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},\quad D_{1}\coloneqq(M/\Gamma_{n})\setminus\left((M_{n}/\Gamma_{n})\setminus E_{n}\right).

Clearly
D_{0}\cap D_{1}=E_{n}
. The Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence of the decomposition

tells us that

H_{i}(E_{n})\rightarrow H_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n})\oplus H_{i}(D_{1})\rightarrow H_{i}(M/\Gamma_{n})

is exact for every

and

. Therefore

\alpha_{n,i}\leq b_{i}(E_{n})
. By Lemma
4.15
, we have that
b_{i}(E_{n})
grows sublinearly and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{b_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\alpha_{n,i}+\nabla_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{b_{i}(E_{n})+\nabla_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(M_{n}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}.\qed

Let us now compare the obtained

with the topological spaces in the statement of Theorem

.

Lemma 4.17
.

If
y\in Y_{n}
is the class of the identity, we have that

A_{M}(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{K_{0}^{-1}V_{1}K_{0}}\subseteq M_{n,y}^{K}\subseteq A_{M}(G)_{V}^{\Gamma_{n}}.

Proof.

By hypothesis we have
FY_{n}\subseteq(X_{n})_{V}
. This can be restated as saying that

. Therefore

M_{n,y}^{K}=\cup_{i}O_{i}F\omega_{n,y}\subseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{V}\subseteq A_{M}(G)_{V}^{\Gamma_{n}}.

On the other hand,
\cup_{i}O_{i}K_{0}\supseteq A_{M}

and
V^{2}\omega_{n,y}\supseteq(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}
. Therefore, using Equation (
2
), we obtain

M_{n,y}^{K}=\cup_{i}O_{i}F\omega_{n,y}\supseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}V^{2}\omega_{n,y}\supseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}(G)_{V_{1}}^{\Gamma_{n}}\supseteq\cup_{i}O_{i}K_{0}(G)_{K_{0}^{-1}V_{1}K_{0}}^{\Gamma_{n}}\supseteq A_{M}(G)_{K_{0}^{-1}V_{1}K_{0}}^{\Gamma_{n}}.\qed

Summing up all the previous statements, we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.18
.

For every symmetric neighborhood of the identity
V
, set
V_{2}\coloneqq K_{0}^{-1}V^{-2}K_{0}^{-1}VK_{0}V^{2}K_{0}
. Then
we have

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(A_{M}(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{V_{2}}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}\leq\beta_{i}(G)\operatorname{covol}(Y_{\mathfrak{u}})\leq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(A_{M}(G)^{\Gamma_{n}}_{V}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}.

By assumption, the class of the identity is in

. Denote this class by

. Then
combining Lemma

, Proposition
4.14
and Lemma

, we obtain

\beta_{i}(G)\operatorname{covol}(Y_{\mathfrak{u}})=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\nabla_{i}(M_{n,y_{n}}/\Gamma_{n},M/\Gamma_{n})}{|Y_{n}|}.

Therefore the lemma follows from Lemma

.
∎

Let us conclude the proof of Theorem

. Let us fix a neighborhood of the identity

. Consider a neighborhood of the identity

4.5.
Examples of well-covered spaces

We will now provide some examples of well-covered spaces and in particular we will finish the proof of Corollary
4.3
. All our examples will be
G
-CW complexes, see
[
32
]
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. group and suppose it acts on the Riemannian manifold
M
in a cocompact, isometric and proper way.
Then
M
is a well-covered
G
-space.

Proof.

Let us denote by
d
the geodesic distance on
M
.
Since small geodesic balls of a Riemannian manifold are convex, they form a good cover of the space, see
[
15
]
. We need therefore to show only the last point of Definition
4.1
. Consider a compact subset
C\subseteq M
such that
CG=M
. Set
A_{M}\coloneqq B_{1}^{M}(C)
. Since the action is proper, the set

A_{G}\coloneqq\{g\in G\colon gC\cap C\neq\emptyset\}

is compact. Let us denote by
K_{A}\subseteq A_{G}
the union of all compact subgroups contained in
A_{G}
. Let us fix an open neighborhood of the identity
W\subseteq G
. Remark that
WK_{A}
is open.
For every
x\in C
let us define

\varepsilon(x)\coloneqq d(x,x(G\setminus(WK_{A}))).

Remark that if
xg=x
, then
g
is in a subgroup contained in
A_{G}
, that is
g\in K_{A}
. Moreover since the action is cocompact, and in particular since
M/G
is Hausdorff, each orbit is closed and the map
g\mapsto xg
is closed. Therefore,
x(G\setminus(WK_{A}))
is a closed subset not containing
x
and the function
x\mapsto d(x,x(G\setminus(WK_{A})))
is continuous. In particular,
\varepsilon_{0}\coloneqq\min(\min_{x\in C}\varepsilon(x),1)
is strictly positive.

Since
M/G
is compact, there are finitely many
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in C
such that
\cup_{i}B_{\varepsilon_{0}/4}^{M}(p_{i})G=M
. Now assume that for some
1\leq i,j\leq m
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, we have that

B_{\varepsilon_{0}/4}^{M}(p_{i}g_{1})\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}/4}^{M}(p_{j})\neq\emptyset\text{ and }B_{\varepsilon_{0}/4}^{M}(p_{i}g_{2})\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}/4}^{M}(p_{j})\neq\emptyset.

Then the triangular inequality gives us that
d(p_{i}g_{1},p_{i}g_{2})<\varepsilon_{0}
. Since the action is isometric, we obtain that
d(p_{i}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1},p_{i})<\varepsilon_{0}
. Finally the choice of
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon(p_{i})
tells us that
g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}\in WK_{A}
and the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

Note that in the proof of Lemma
4.19
the assumption that the action is isometric is not crucial. Indeed, it is enough to know that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta
such that whenever
x,y\in M
are such that
d(x,y)<\delta
, then
d(xg,yg)<\varepsilon
for every
g\in G
.

A group
G
is said to be
almost connected
if the quotient of
G
by the connected component of the identity is compact. If
G
is almost connected, then
M\coloneqq K_{0}\backslash G
is a
\underline{\mathrm{E}}G
space for some maximal compact subgroup
K_{0}
of
G
, see
[
2
, Theorem A.5]
and
[
1
, Corollary 4.14]
or
[
32
]
. Observe that any almost connected group admits a normal compact subgroup such that the quotient is a Lie group
[
25
, Theorem 8]
. Therefore
M
is naturally a contractible Riemannian manifold on which
G
acts by isometry and hence
M
is a contractible well-covered
G
-space. We therefore have the following.

Fact
.

Every almost connected group acts on a contractible well-covered
G
-space.

We now examine our second class of examples.

Lemma 4.21
.

Suppose that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts simplicially, properly and cocompactly on a simplicial complex

is a well-covered
G

In order to prove Lemma
4.21
, we could introduce a metric on

and proceed as in Lemma
4.19
. We will present instead a combinatorial proof which uses the barycentric subdivision. Let us recall that if

is a simplicial complex, the

are in correspondence with the simplices of

. Two

-simplices in

are the boundary of a

-simplex in

if and only if one is contained in the other as simplices of

. It is convenient to isolate the following lemma.

Lemma 4.22
.

Suppose that the l.c.s.c. group
G
acts simplicially on a simplicial complex
M
. Suppose that
\sigma_{1}^{1},\sigma_{2}^{1}
are simplices of

and take

such that
\sigma_{1}^{1}
contains both
\sigma_{2}^{1}g_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}^{1}g_{2}

\sigma_{2}^{1}g_{1}=\sigma_{2}^{1}g_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
q^{1}
be any

-simplex of
\sigma_{2}^{1}
. Then both
q^{1}g_{1}
and
q^{1}g_{2}
are

-simplices of
\sigma_{1}^{1}
. If they are distinct, there is a

-simplex in

whose boundary is

. In this case we would have that
q^{1}g_{1}
and

are associated with simplices of

of different dimensions, which is impossible since the action of

is simplicial.
∎

.

Denote by

the barycentric subdivision of

. We claim that the covering by stars of the

, we consider a compact subset

Given a

of

the open star around

. By cocompactness, there are finitely many

for

\cup_{i}S(p_{i}^{2})G=M

and

For every

, we denote by

the simplex of

associated with

•

\sigma^{1}_{j}
contains both
\sigma^{1}_{i}g_{1}
and
\sigma^{1}_{i}g_{2}
;

•

\sigma^{1}_{j}

\sigma^{1}_{i}g_{1}
and
\sigma^{1}_{i}g_{2}

In the first case, Lemma

implies that

, therefore it is contained in a compact subgroup contained in

∎

Well-covered
G
-spaces behave nicely with respect to products.

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be l.c.s.c. groups and let
M_{i}
be well-covered
G_{i}
-spaces for
i=1,2
. Then
M_{1}\times M_{2}
is a well-covered
G_{1}\times G_{2}
-space.

Proof.

Clearly the action of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
on
M_{1}\times M_{2}
is proper and cocompact. Also remark that if
\mathcal{O}^{i}
is a
G_{i}
-invariant good cover of
M_{i}
, then the collection

\mathcal{O}^{1}\times\mathcal{O}^{2}\coloneqq\{O^{1}\times O^{2}\colon O^{i}\in\mathcal{O}^{i},i=1,2\}

is a
G
-invariant good cover and the last condition can be easily verified.
∎

Finally assume that
G
is a general locally compact group and denote by
G_{0}\leq G
the connected component of the identity.

Fact
.

If the group
G/G_{0}
acts cocompactly and properly on a contractible simplicial complex
M_{0}
, then there is a contractible well-covered
G
-space.

We will not prove this fact. In
[
30
]
it is explained how to induce a
\underline{\mathrm{E}}(G/G_{0})
space to a
\underline{\mathrm{E}}G
without changing the quotient. Following their construction, the preimage of each point in the quotient is a countable union of connected Riemannian manifolds. Therefore we can construct good covers by taking the product of the cover by stars of
M_{0}
and small geodesic balls in the manifolds.

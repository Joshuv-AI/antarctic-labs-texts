Morita equivalence classes of blocks with extraspecial defect groups
p

Abstract
We characterise the Morita equivalence classes of blocks with extraspecial defect groups p + 1 + 2 p_{+}^{1+2} for p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , and so show that Donovan’s conjecture and the Alperin-McKay conjecture hold for such p p -groups. For p = 3 p=3 we reduce Donovan’s conjecture for blocks with defect group 3 + 1 + 2 3_{+}^{1+2} to bounding the Cartan invariants for such blocks of quasisimple groups. We apply the characterisation to the case p = 5 p=5 as an example, to list the Morita equivalence classes of such blocks.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
p
be a prime and
(K,{\mathcal{O}},k)
be a modular system with
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
(we will discuss the choice of
{\mathcal{O}}
further later). Donovan’s conjecture, which may be stated over
{\mathcal{O}}
or
k
, predicts that for a given finite
p
-group
P
, there are only finitely many Morita equivalence classes of blocks of finite groups with defect groups isomorphic to
P
. Whilst significant progress on the conjecture has been achieved for the prime two, especially for abelian defect groups, at present for odd primes the only cases for which the conjecture is known are the cyclic
p
-groups and those
p
-groups admitting no fusion system other than that of the
p
-group itself (see
[
20
]
for a recent summary, and also
[
17
]
where progress on the conjecture is recorded). We apply the classification of finite simple groups to give a description of the Morita equivalence classes of
{\mathcal{O}}
-blocks with extraspecial defect groups of order
p^{3}
and exponent
p
when
p\geq 5
, from which it follows that Donovan’s conjecture holds for these
p
-groups, and further one may derive a classification of the Morita equivalence classes. As an illustration, we give the explicit classification of all Morita equivalence classes of blocks with defect group
5_{+}^{1+2}
. A further application is that the Alperin-McKay conjecture holds for blocks with defect group
p_{+}^{1+2}
for
p\geq 5
.

In the following note that a Morita equivalence is called basic if it is induced by an endopermutation source bimodule. Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime. Let
G
be a finite group and
B
a block of
{\mathcal{O}}G
with defect group

. Then
B
is basic Morita equivalent to a block of maximal defect of one of the following:

(a)

D\rtimes\hat{E}
, where
\hat{E}
is a
p^{\prime}
-central extension of a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup
E
of
GL_{2}(p)
acting faithfully on
D
;

(b)

(C_{p}\times C_{p})\rtimes H
, where
SL_{2}(p)\leq H\leq GL_{2}(p)
;

(c)

H
where
PSL_{3}(p)\leq H/Z(H)\leq{\rm Aut}(PSL_{3}(p))
and
Z(H)\leq H^{\prime}
;

(d)

H
where
PSU_{3}(p)\leq H/Z(H)\leq{\rm Aut}(PSU_{3}(p))
and
Z(H)\leq H^{\prime}
;

(e)

2.HS
,
2.HS.2
,
3.McL
,
McL.2
,
2.Ru
,
Co_{3}
,
Co_{2}
or

, where

(f)

He.2
,

,

, where

(h)

.

Further, the Morita equivalence preserves both the fusion of the block and the Külshammer-Puig class of the blocks.

Corollary 1.2

Donovan’s conjecture holds for
{\mathcal{O}}
-blocks for
p_{+}^{1+2}
for primes
p\geq 5
. That is, for each
p\geq 5
there are only finitely many Morita equivalence classes amongst
{\mathcal{O}}
-blocks of finite groups with defect group
p_{+}^{1+2}
.

Numerical invariants for blocks with these defect groups have been studied in
[
28
]
, although they are not completely determined, and in
[
29
]
it is shown that Olsson’s conjecture holds for these blocks, i.e., that the number of irreducible characters of height zero is at most

. Principal blocks with such defect groups have been studied in
[

]
using the classification of finite simple groups, where the existence of a generalised perfect isometry is shown. Some of the methods in that paper motivate those used here.

A consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that the Alperin-McKay conjecture holds in this case. Here
k(B)
denotes the number of irreducible character in
B
. We write
k_{h}(B)
for the number of irreducible characters of height
h
, where the height of an irreducible character

in a block with defect group

is the non-negative integer

such that
\chi(1)_{p}=p^{h}[G:D]_{p}
.

Corollary 1.3

Let
B
be a block of a finite group with defect group
D\cong p_{+}^{1+2}
for
p\geq 5
. Let
b
be the Brauer correspondent of
B
in
N_{G}(D)
. Then
k_{0}(B)=k_{0}(b)
.

Two important components of the proof of Theorem
1.1
are the following: the classification of blocks of quasisimple groups with extraspecial defect groups in
[
5
]
; an analysis of blocks of quasisimple groups with defect group
C_{p}\times C_{p}
and a non-inner automorphism of order
p
, making use of
[
3
]
.

We also obtain detailed information about blocks with defect group
3_{+}^{1+2}
, found in Proposition
6.2
. However, there are infinite classes of blocks with such defect groups within which Morita equivalence has yet to be decided, so we cannot at present extend Theorem
1.1
to
p=3
. However, we can make the following reduction for Donovan’s conjecture. For a block
B
let
{\rm c}(B)
be the largest entry of its Cartan matrix.

Theorem 1.4

Suppose that there is
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\rm c}(B)\leq c
for all quasisimple groups
G
and all blocks
B
of
kG
with defect group

. Then Donovan’s conjecture
holds for
{\mathcal{O}}
-blocks with defect group

.

Note that the fact that we need only examine Cartan invariants for these groups rather than Morita equivalence classes stems from Farrell and Kessar’s determination of Morita-Frobenius numbers of blocks of quasisimple groups in
[
22
]
.

As an example of the application of Theorem

, we give the complete classification of the sixty-two Morita equivalence classes of blocks with defect group

. Similar classifications could be produced for the remaining primes

, although the determination of precise Morita equivalence classes for the blocks with normal defect groups would require ad hoc results in each case.

of exponent

in this article. Whilst many of the reductions for blocks with these defect groups are the same as for the exponent

2
Notation and background results

Let
p
be a prime and let
{\mathcal{O}}
be a complete discrete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field
k
of characteristic
p
, so that
k=\mathcal{O}/J(\mathcal{O})
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the field of fractions for
{\mathcal{O}}
. The results of this paper hold in this generality, however for a canonical statement of Donovan’s conjecture one may take
k
to be the algebraic closure of the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
p
elements and
{\mathcal{O}}
to be the ring of Witt vectors for
k
.

For
G
a finite group, write
{\rm Blk}(G)
for the set of blocks of
\mathcal{O}G
. Denote by

{\rm Irr}(B)
the set of irreducible
\mathcal{K}
-characters belonging to a block
B\in{\rm Blk}(G)
. We will often use
D=D(B)
to denote a defect group of
B
. Denote by
{\rm IBr}(B)
for the set of irreducible Brauer characters in
B
. Write
k(B)=|{\rm Irr}(B)|
and
l(B)=|{\rm IBr}(B)|
. Write
B_{0}(G)
for the principal block of
{\mathcal{O}}G
.

A
B
-subgroup is a
pair
(Q,b_{Q})
, where
Q
is a
p
-subgroup of
G
and
b_{Q}
is a
block of
C_{G}(Q)
with Brauer correspondent
(b_{Q})^{G}=B
. The

B
-subgroups with
|Q|
maximized are called the Sylow

B
-subgroups, and they are the
B
-subgroups for which
Q
is a defect group
for
B
. We denote by
N_{G}(D,b_{D})
the stabilizer in
N_{G}(D)
of
(D,b_{D})
under conjugation.

The
inertial quotient
of
B
is
E=N_{G}(D,b_{D})/DC_{G}(D)
, together with the action of
E
on
D
. The inertial quotient is determined by the fusion system
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{(D,b_{D})}(G,B)
for
B
, sometimes called the Frobenius category. We refer to
[
31
, Section 8.5]
for background on this. Following the presentation in
[
31
, Section 8.14]
, a Külshammer-Puig class is an element of
H^{2}({\rm Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q))
, which is isomorphic to
H^{2}(E,k^{\times})
(see
[
31
, Remark 8.14.3]
).

The Morita equivalences occurring paper are all basic Morita equivalences or source algebra equivalences (a basic Morita equivalence is one induced by an endopermutation source bimodule, for which see
[
31
, Section 9.10]
).

Now suppose
D\lhd G
and let
E
be the inertial quotient of
B
. By
[
31
, Theorem 6.14.1]
,
B
is basic Morita equivalent to a twisted group algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}(D\rtimes E)
, where
\alpha\in H^{2}(E,k^{\times})
. In turn, this is basic Morita equivalent to a block of
{\mathcal{O}}(D\rtimes\hat{E})
, where
\hat{E}
is a central extension of
E
by
Z\leq Z(\hat{E})\cap\hat{E}^{\prime}
with
E
acting faithfully and
Z
acting trivially on
D
. Blocks of
{\mathcal{O}}(D\rtimes\hat{E})
correspond to irreducible characters of
Z
. We remark that the Morita equivalence classes are independent of choice of isoclinism class of central extension. Whilst choice of
E
and
\alpha
determines the Morita equivalence class, it is not always the case that different choices give distinct Morita equivalence classes. It may (and does) happen that distinct choices of
\alpha
give the same Morita equivalence class, as we will see in Section
7
.

The outer automorphism group of
p_{+}^{1+2}
is isomorphic to
GL_{2}(p)
, and the possible inertial quotients correspond to the conjugacy classes of subgroups of
GL_{2}(p)
of order prime to
p
.

We now gather together some elementary results that will be needed later on.

A main task in this paper is to show that if a quasisimple group of Lie type in characteristic different to
p
has a block with defect group
C_{p}\times C_{p}
, then there cannot be a field automorphism of order
p
. The reason for this is roughly that if the field of definition of our group has exponent divisible by
p
, then such small but nontrivial defect groups as these cannot be present. The next lemma is important in showing this. Throughout, we denote by
m_{p}
the
p
-part of an integer
m
.

Suppose
p
is odd. Let
r
be a prime distinct from
p
and
q=r^{f}
for some integer
f\geq 1
. Let
\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}
and let
e
be the multiplicative order of
\epsilon q
modulo
p
, so that
p\mid(q^{e}-\epsilon)
. Write
(q^{e}-\epsilon)_{p}=p^{a}
, where
a\geq 1
is an integer.

Lemma 2.1

With the above notation, if
a=1
, then
p\nmid f
.

Proof
:
Suppose first that
\epsilon=1
. Let
e^{\prime}
be the multiplicative order of
r
modulo
p
. Calculating in
C_{p}
, we have
q^{e^{\prime}}=r^{fe^{\prime}}=1
, so
e\mid e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime}=ee_{1}
for some
e_{1}\geq 1
. But
r^{ef}=1
, so
e^{\prime}\mid(ef)
and write
ef=e^{\prime}f_{1}
. Thus

ef=e^{\prime}f_{1}=ee_{1}f_{1}

and
f=e_{1}f_{1}
. Let
r_{1}=r^{e_{1}}
, so that
|r_{1}|=e
. If
p^{b}=(r_{1}^{e}-1)_{p}
with
b\geq 1
, then
r_{1}^{e}=p^{b}m+1
for some integer
m\geq 1
with
\gcd(m,p)=1
.
If
p\mid f
, then
p\mid f_{1}
and
f_{1}=pf_{2}
as
e_{1}\leq e^{\prime}\leq p-1
.
Thus
q^{e}=(r_{1}^{e})^{f_{1}}=((p^{b}m+1)^{f_{2}})^{p}
. In particular,
p^{b+1}\mid(q^{e}-1)
and
hence
a\geq b+1\geq 2
, a contradiction.

Suppose
\epsilon=-1
. Then
p\mid|U(e,q)|
with
e
minimal.
If
x\in U(e,q)
has order
p
, then
Z(U(e,q))=C_{q+1}\leq C_{U(e,q)}(x)=U(1,q^{e})
and so
e
is odd. In particular,
q^{e}\equiv-1\pmod{p}
and
p\mid(q^{e}+1)
.
Since
p
is odd, we have
p\nmid(q^{e}-1)
and
(q^{2e}-1)_{p}=(q^{e}+1)_{p}
.
So if
a=1
, then
(q^{2e}-1)_{p}=p
and as shown above,
p\nmid 2f
and hence
p\nmid f
.

\Box

Lemma 2.2

Let
H\leq GL_{2}(p)
such that
H
contains a Sylow
p
-subgroup

of
GL_{2}(p)
. Then
P\lhd H
or
SL_{2}(p)\leq H\leq GL_{2}(p)
.

Proof
:
This is well known and follows easily from
[

.

\Box

Lemma 2.3

Let
G
be a finite group with
C_{G}(O_{p}(G))\leq O_{p}(G)\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
and
|G|_{p}=p^{3}
, where

is an odd prime. Then

for some
SL_{2}(p)\leq H\leq GL_{2}(p)
.

Proof
:
We may embed
G/O_{p}(G)
into

. Let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Since

, we must have that
G/O_{p}(G)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of

of order divisible by

that does not normalise a Sylow

-subgroup. Hence by Lemma
2.2

SL_{2}(p)\leq G/O_{p}(G)
and it suffices to show that

must be a split extension. This follows from
[
14
, Theorem 1]
by observing that

fixes only the zero element of the natural module.

\Box

4
Classical groups

In this and the following section we analyse blocks of quasisimple groups in non-defining characteristic with defect group
C_{p}
or

Suppose

and
G={\rm GL}_{n}^{\eta}(q)={\rm GL}^{\eta}(V)
, and let
B_{G}
be a

p
-block of

with a defect group

and label

V=V_{0}\perp V_{+},\quad D_{G}=D_{0}\times D_{+},\quad s=s_{0}\times s_{+},

[formula]

where

,
V_{+}=[D_{G},V]
,

and

s_{+}\in G_{+}:={\rm GL}^{\epsilon}(V_{+})
,

and

. We also denote
{\rm GL}^{\eta}(V)
by

and

Proposition 4.1

Let
K_{u}={\rm SL}^{\eta}(V)={\rm SL}^{\eta}_{n}(q)
,
G={\rm GL}^{\eta}(V)
,
K=K_{u}/Z

for some
Z\leq Z(K_{u})
,

with Sylow
B
-subgroup

(D,b_{K})

B
,

B_{G}\in{\rm Blk}(G)

,
D_{u}=D(B_{u})
such that

and

. Suppose that

or

. Then either any block of

a=1

has no field automorphism of order

.

Proof
:
Let
(s,\kappa)
be the label of

, and
V,D_{G},s
have the
corresponding decomposition (
4
.1). Let
s_{+}=\prod_{\Gamma}s_{\Gamma}

the underlying space of

. Thus

C_{G_{+}}(s_{+})=\prod_{\Gamma}C_{\Gamma},\quad C_{\Gamma}={\rm GL}^{\epsilon_{\Gamma}}(m_{\Gamma},q^{\delta_{\Gamma}})

[formula]

with
m_{\Gamma}=m_{\Gamma}(s_{+})
. We may suppose

, that is, a Sylow subgroup of

D_{+}=\prod_{\Gamma}D_{\Gamma},\quad D_{\Gamma}\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(C_{\Gamma}).

[formula]

So

Since

6
Reductions

The first step of our reduction is to apply Fong-Reynolds and Külshammer-Puig reductions as far as possible, meaning that we may establish a Morita equivalence with what we will call a reduced block. A feature of these particular Morita equivalences is that they preserve both fusion and the Külshammer-Puig classes as reviewed in Section
2

[

. Once we have established that it suffices to consider reduced blocks, we will then analyse their structure.

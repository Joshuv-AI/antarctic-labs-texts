On balanced homodyne measurement – simple proof of wave function collapse
E. Brüning a and S. Nagamachi b b The University of Tokushima, Tokushima 770-8506, Japan

Abstract
Using only elementary calculus we prove that the balanced homodyne detector measures the quadrature phase amplitude of a signal. More precisely, after the measurement of photon numbers l l , the collapse of the composite state of a strong laser beam and a signal approximates the collapse of the signal to the eigen-state of quadrature phase amplitude with eigen-value r r .

Holomorphic representation of CCR

Later we will use the holomorphic representation of the canonical commutation relation defined below (see
[

).

Definition 2.1
.

Consider the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu_{n})
with

u_{j}=x_{j}+\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muy_{j},\ \ d\mu_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})}\frac{dx_{j}dy_{j}}{\pi},\ \ \int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}d\mu_{n}=1

and its subspace
\mathcal{H}
generated by holomorphic functions of
\bar{u}=(\bar{u}_{1},\ldots,\bar{u}_{n})
(anti-holomorphic functions of
u
). For
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
define the inner product by

\langle f|g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\overline{f(\bar{u})}g(\bar{u})d\mu_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\overline{f(\bar{u})}g(\bar{u})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}.

Then the multiplication operator
c_{j}^{*}:f(\bar{u})\rightarrow\bar{u}_{j}f(\bar{u})
and the differentiation
operator
c_{j}:f(\bar{u})\rightarrow\partial f(\bar{u})/\partial\bar{u}_{j}
(
\partial/\partial\bar{u}_{j}=(1/2)(\partial/\partial x_{j}+\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\partial/\partial y_{j})
) are adjoint to each other and satisfy the canonical commutation relations (CCR)

[c_{j},c_{k}]=[c_{j}^{*},c_{k}^{*}]=0,\ \ [c_{j},c_{k}^{*}]=\delta_{j,k}.

This representation of the commutation relations is called the holomorphic representation.

Remark 2.2
.

\{\bar{u}_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\bar{u}_{n}^{k_{n}}/\sqrt{k_{1}!\cdots k_{n}!}\}_{k_{1},\ldots k_{n}=0}^{\infty}
is a complete orthonormal system of
\mathcal{H}
. The following calculations show the orthonormality: If
k\leq l
then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\overline{\bar{u}_{j}^{k}}\bar{u}_{j}^{l}d\mu_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}u_{j}^{k}\bar{u}_{j}^{l}d\mu_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}u_{j}^{k}\bar{u}_{j}^{l}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}u_{j}^{k}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\right)^{l}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial^{l}}{(\partial u_{j})^{l}}u_{j}^{k}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}=k!\delta_{k,l},

if
k>l
we calculate

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\bar{u}_{j}^{l}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{j}}\right)^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial^{k}}{(\partial\bar{u}_{j})^{k}}\bar{u}_{j}^{l}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}=0.

The completeness follows from the fact that holomorphic functions

of
\bar{u}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
have power series expansions

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\bar{\alpha}\cdot u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{j}u_{j}
. Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{\bar{\alpha}\cdot u}f(\bar{u})d\mu_{n}=f(\bar{\alpha}).

Proof.

It follows from the equalities

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{\bar{\alpha}\cdot u}\bar{u}_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\bar{u}_{n}^{k_{n}}d\mu_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{e}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}u_{j}}\bar{u}_{j}^{k_{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}}\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}du_{j}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{\bar{\alpha}\cdot u}f(\bar{u})d\mu_{n}

∎

Corollary 2.4

\mathrm{e}^{\bar{u}\cdot v}

4.9
) is very sensitive to the choice, i.e.,

\frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{[l/2]}}\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{[l/2]+1}}\rightarrow 0

as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
. We choose their geometric mean

\sqrt{\frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{[l/2]}}\frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{[l/2]+1}}}=\frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{l/2}},

then it has a nonzero limit (see Proposition
4.13
).

Now we have the main proposition.

Proposition 4.16
.

For
\alpha=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\left|\alpha\right|
and
\left|\alpha\right|=l/x
with a fixed
0\neq x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mul\varphi/2}{}_{2}\langle\alpha|\varphi,l\rangle\approx\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\varphi}\bar{u}_{1}^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4}\mathrm{e}^{(\cos(\varphi-2\theta)-1)\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu(\sin(\varphi-2\theta))\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}

for sufficient large integer
l=x\left|\alpha\right|
.

Proof.

It follows from (
4.9
), Remark
4.15
, Proposition
4.13
, Proposition
4.14
and Corollary
4.11
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mul\varphi/2}{}_{2}\langle\alpha|\varphi,l\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{\alpha})^{l}}{(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{l/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{j=l/2}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muj\varphi}\frac{j!}{\sqrt{(j+l/2)!(j-l/2)!}}\frac{1}{2^{j}j!}(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{j}

\displaystyle\approx\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{j=l/2}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muj\varphi}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4\mu}\frac{1}{2^{j}j!}(-\bar{u}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2})^{j}

\displaystyle\approx\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\varphi}\bar{u}_{1}^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{j=l/2}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muj\varphi}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4\mu}\frac{1}{2^{j}j!}(\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\left|\alpha\right|^{2})^{j}.

Let
m=\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2
. Then it follows from Lemma
4.8
that the summation
\sum_{j=l/2}^{\infty}
can be replaced by

\sum_{j=m-\lambda\sqrt{m}}^{m+\lambda\sqrt{m}}
. If
j\in[m-\lambda\sqrt{m},m+\lambda\sqrt{m}]
, then
\mu=j/m\in[1-\lambda/\sqrt{m},1+\lambda/\sqrt{m}]
. So,
\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4\mu}
can be replaced by
\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4}
for large
m
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mul\varphi/2}{}_{2}\langle\alpha|\varphi,l\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\varphi}\bar{u}_{1}^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4}\mathrm{e}^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{j=l/2}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muj\varphi}\frac{1}{2^{j}j!}(\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\left|\alpha\right|^{2})^{j}

\displaystyle\approx\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\varphi}\bar{u}_{1}^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4}\mathrm{e}^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muj(\varphi-2\theta)}\frac{\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}{j!}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{x\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\theta}\bar{u}_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\varphi}\bar{u}_{1}^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/4}\mathrm{e}^{(\cos(\varphi-2\theta)-1)\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu(\sin(\varphi-2\theta))\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}

for large
\left|\alpha\right|
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now our main result follows easily.

Thermodynamic Evidence of Proximity to a Kitaev Spin-Liquid in Ag 3 LiIr 2 O 6
Faranak Bahrami, William Lafargue-Dit-Hauret, Oleg I. Lebedev, Roman Movshovich, Hung-Yu Yang, David Broido, Xavier Rocquefelte, Fazel Tafti

Abstract
Kitaev magnets are materials with bond-dependent Ising interactions between localized spins on a honeycomb lattice. Such interactions could lead to a quantum spin-liquid (QSL) ground state at zero temperature. Recent theoretical studies suggest two potential signatures of a QSL at finite temperatures, namely a scaling behavior of thermodynamic quantities in the presence of quenched disorder, and a two-step release of the magnetic entropy. Here, we present both signatures in Ag 3 LiIr 2 O 6 which is synthesized from α \alpha -Li 2 IrO 3 by replacing the inter-layer Li atoms with Ag atoms. In addition, the DC susceptibility data confirm absence of a long-range order, and the AC susceptibility data rule out a spin-glass transition. These observations suggest a closer proximity to the QSL in Ag 3 LiIr 2 O 6 compared to its parent compound α \alpha -Li 2 IrO 3 that orders at 15 K. We discuss an enhanced spin-orbit coupling due to a mixing between silver d d and oxygen p p orbitals as a potential underlying mechanism.



An exciting frontier in condensed matter physics is to design materials where the spin degrees of freedom avoid a magnetically ordered ground state despite strong exchange interactions.
Such compounds release the spin entropy by forming a quantum entangled ground state known as the quantum spin-liquid (QSL)
Knolle and Moessner 2019
;
Takagi et al. 2019
;
Savary and Balents 2016
;
Winter et al. 2016
.
Among various proposals for a QSL, the Kitaev model is especially appealing because it is exactly solvable and can be engineered in real materials
Kitaev 2006
;
Jackeli and Khaliullin 2009
.
The model consists of bond-dependent Ising interactions between localized
S=1/2
spins on a honeycomb lattice,
\mathcal{H}_{K}=-\sum K_{\gamma}\mathbf{S_{i}}^{\gamma}\mathbf{S_{j}}^{\gamma}

Kitaev 2006
;
Knolle and Moessner 2019
.
The ground state is analytically solved by fractionalizing the spin-1/2 operators (
\mathbf{S_{i}}
) into itinerant and localized Majorana fermions
Kitaev 2006
;
Hermanns et al. 2018

Recent Monte Carlo (MC) simulations suggest that by decreasing temperature, the two types of Majoranas undergo two successive cross-overs
Nasu et al. 2015
;
Yamaji et al. 2016
.
First, at a higher temperature
T_{H}
, the itinerant Majoranas form coherent bands.
Second, at a lower temperature
T_{L}

2

Evidence of such behavior is reported in layered iridium oxides,
\alpha

Recently, two approaches have been taken to bring the candidate materials closer to the Kitaev limit.
The first approach was to bring the Ir-O-Ir bond angles closer to 90

This idea led to the discovery of

The second approach was to induce a random bond disorder within the honeycomb layers which is achieved in H

LiIr
2

Kitagawa et al. 2018

Here, we present a third approach based on modifying the inter-layer bonds.

Discussion–
At this point, it is instructive to compare the structural and magnetic parameters between Ag
3
LiIr
2
O
6
and
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3
(Table
2
).
Due to a comparable bond angle
\phi
, the cancellation of Heisenberg interactions across the opposite Ir-O-Ir bonds in Fig.
1
b must be comparable in both compounds.
A comparison of
\Theta_{\textrm{cw}}
and Ir–Ir distance suggests that the exchange coupling strength is also comparable in both compounds.
The main structural difference between the two materials is a 30% larger inter-layer separation in Ag
3
LiIr
2
O
6
.
At first glance, an increased inter-layer separation may suggest increased magnetic fluctuations, hence a weaker AFM order.
However, the exchange interactions in iridate materials are highly anisotropic
Sizyuk et al. 2014
and such an argument does not justify the complete suppression of the AFM order in Ag
3
LiIr
2
O
6
.

A more plausible explanation for the lack of AFM order comes from the density of states (DOS) calculations presented in Fig
4
where a finite weight of silver
4d
orbitals is observed at the Fermi level
E_{F}
.
We present three levels of the DFT calculations following the prior work on
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3

Li et al. 2015
.
First, a plain local density approximation (LDA) is presented in Fig.
4
a to show the
t_{2\textrm{g}}
states just below
E_{F}
and
e_{\textrm{g}}
states above
E_{F}
.
Notice that the majority of Ag electrons (blue line) are between 2 and 4 eV below
E_{F}
; however, a small but finite contribution from silver
d
orbitals is observed near
E_{F}
.
Second, by adding the spin-orbit coupling (LDA+SOC) in Fig.
4
b, the
t_{2\textrm{g}}
levels are split into lower
J_{\textrm{eff}}=3/2
and an upper
J_{\textrm{eff}}=1/2

Third, by adding an exchange potential (LDA+SOC+U) in Fig.
4

J_{\textrm{eff}}=1/2
states to separate the upper and lower Hubbard bands.
These results are identical to
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3
and consistent with the localized effective spin-1/2 Kitaev model
Li et al. 2015

The new finding is the finite weight of silver
4d
orbitals at
E_{F}
which remains unchanged between the LDA and LDA+SOC+U calculations, and suggests a
d
-
p
orbital mixing between the Ag and O atoms.
Whereas the lithium
2s
electrons in
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3
are transferred to oxygen
2p
orbitals in an ionic bond, the silver
4d

3
LiIr
2
O
6
are more extended and bonded to the oxygen
2p
orbitals with a more covalent character.
As a result of such

-
p
mixing, the SOC is effectively increased on the Ir-O-Ir exchange path within the honeycomb layers of Ag
3
LiIr
2
O
6
which enhances the Kitaev coupling.
We emphasize that despite comparable Ir-O-Ir bond angles between
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3
and Ag
3
LiIr
2
O
6
within the honeycomb layers (Table

), the latter compound is closer to the Kitaev limit because of a stronger SOC mediated via the O-Ag-O bonds between the layers.
Thus, our work presents a new approach to optimizing the Kitaev magnets by tuning the inter-layer instead of intra-layer chemical bonds.

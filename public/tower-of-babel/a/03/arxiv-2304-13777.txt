A topological L ω 1 , ω L_{\omega_{1},\omega} -invariant.
B.Zilber

Abstract
We suggest to look at formal sentences describing complex algebraic varieties together with their universal covers as topological invariants. We prove that for abelian varieties and Shimura varieties this is indeed a complete invariant, i.e. it determines the variety up to complex conjugation.

Introduction

The universal (and more general) cover of a complex algebraic variety as an abstract structure has been a subject of study in model theory in recent years, see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. More specifically, given a smooth complex variety
{\mathbb{X}}
and its cover
\pi:{\mathbb{U}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
presented in a natural universal language,
the aim has been to find an
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-sentence
\Sigma_{\mathbb{X}}
which axiomatises
\pi:{\mathbb{U}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
categorically, that is determines the isomorphism type of an uncountable model of
\Sigma_{\mathbb{X}}
uniquely in a given cardinality, up to an abstract isomorphism (ignoring topology).
This proved to be the case in the classes where one has enough knowledge of the Galois action and arithmetic of
{\mathbb{X}},
see references above.

We suggest here that such a
\Sigma_{\mathbb{X}}
can serve as a complete topological invariant of respective
{\mathbb{X}}.
More precisely, assuming that in a given class of varieties
{\mathbb{X}}
such a categorical
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-sentence
\Sigma_{\mathbb{X}}
exists, we expect:

If, for a complex variety
{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime},
its cover structure
\pi^{\prime}:{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}
is a model of the same sentence
\Sigma_{\mathbb{X}}
then there is a biholomorphic isomorphism either between
{\mathbb{X}}
and
{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}
or between
{\mathbb{X}}^{c},
the complex conjugate of
{\mathbb{X}}
, and
{\mathbb{X}}^{\prime}.

(Compare this with speculations in a similar vein presented by M.Gavrilovich in
[
7
]
.)

In this note the cases of abelian varieties and Shimura varieties are elaborated.

Theorem.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
be two complex abelian varieties.

Suppose
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
have the same

Then there is a biholomorphic isomorphism

\varphi:\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{A}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}),\mbox{ or }\varphi:\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{A}_{2}^{c}({\mathbb{C}})

where
\mathbb{A}_{2}^{c}
is the elliptic curve obtained by complex conjugation from
\mathbb{A}_{2}.

Proof.
We assume that
\mathbb{A}_{1}
is over
\mathrm{k}_{1}
and
\mathbb{A}_{2}
over
\mathrm{k}_{2},
subfields of
{\mathbb{C}}.

We will write
{\mathbb{U}}({\mathbb{C}})
for the cover sort for both
abelian varieties, and
\pi_{1}:{\mathbb{U}}\to\mathbb{A}_{1},

\pi_{2}:{\mathbb{U}}\to\mathbb{A}_{2}
the respective covers.

By categoricity and the Fact above there is an abstract isomorphism

\phi
between the cover structures, which have the restrictions to subsets,

\phi_{\mathrm{k}}:\mathrm{k}_{1}\to\mathrm{k}_{2};

\phi_{\Lambda}:\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle\to\langle\Lambda_{2}\rangle

where
\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle=\Lambda_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},

\langle\Lambda_{2}\rangle=\Lambda_{2}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}

and

\phi_{\pi\Lambda}:\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle\to\pi_{2}\langle\Lambda_{2}\rangle.

For
\mathrm{k}_{1}
-interpretable varieties
W
and
V
in
\mathbb{A}_{1}
and a

\mathrm{k}_{1}
-rational map

we define

the
\mathrm{k}_{2}
-rational map between the varieties
W^{\phi},

V^{\phi}

\mathrm{k}_{2}
-interpretable in
\mathbb{A}_{2},
given by application of
\phi.

Note that

\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle
is a topological metric space in the metric of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces. It is
metrically dense in
{\mathbb{U}}({\mathbb{C}})
and so
is
\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle
in
\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}}),
and the same holds for
\mathbb{A}_{2}
and its cover structure.

Note also that
\phi_{\Lambda}
is continuous and so is
\phi_{p\Lambda},
since
\pi_{1},

\pi_{2}
are continuous.

Now we define

\bar{\phi}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ {\mathbb{U}}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{U}}({\mathbb{C}})\\
\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{A}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\end{array},\right.

the maps which are defined by continuous extension of

and

that is, for

z_{i}\in\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle,

\bar{\phi}(z)=\lim\phi_{p\Lambda}(z_{i}).

Thus
\bar{\phi}
is a continuous map between the two complex structures the restrictions
\bar{\phi}_{\Lambda}
and
\bar{\phi}_{\pi\Lambda}
of which coinside with the restrictions of the isomorphism

We are going to prove that it is an isomorphism of the cover structures.

Let
q:\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a regular non-constant map over
\mathrm{k}_{1},
defined on a Zariski open subset
D_{1,q}\subset\mathbb{A}_{1}({\mathbb{C}}).

Let

be the respective map over
\mathrm{k}_{2}.

Note that
\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle\cap D_{1,q}
is dense in

and so

is dense in
{\mathbb{C}}
since
q
is continuous and its image contains a Zariski open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}.

Similarly,

q^{\phi}(\pi_{2}\langle\Lambda_{2}\rangle)
is dense in
{\mathbb{C}}.
Let

\phi_{q}:q(\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle)\to q^{\phi}(\pi_{2}\langle\Lambda_{2}\rangle)

be the restriction of the isomorphism
\phi
on the subsets.

The map
\phi_{q}
is a homeomorphism since

\phi_{\pi\Lambda}
is.

Thus the restriction of the isomorphism
\phi
on the subsets of the form
q(\pi_{1}\langle\Lambda_{1}\rangle)
is conituous.

Define

\bar{\phi}
on
{\mathbb{C}}
by extending

continuously. This also defines
\bar{\phi}
on
{\mathbb{C}}.

If

\bar{\phi}
does not depend on the choice of

More generally, using the fact that

\bar{\phi}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

is the unique continuous map such that for any

Let

-rational map. Then, for all

z=\lim z_{i}

Since

\bar{\phi}

\bar{\phi}(g(z))=\lim\bar{\phi}(g(z_{i}))=\lim\phi(g(z_{i}))=\lim g^{\phi}(\phi(z_{i}))=g^{\phi}(\lim\phi(z_{i}))=g^{\phi}(\bar{\phi}(z)))

\bar{\phi}

Shimura varieties

Let
{\mathbb{X}}_{1},

{\mathbb{X}}_{2}
be connected Shimura varieties (see
[
9
]
, Def.4.4) over their reflex fields
\mathrm{k}_{1},\mathrm{k}_{2}
given by the Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{1}),

(\mathrm{G}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and congruence subgroups

{\mathbb{X}}_{1}=\Gamma_{1}\backslash\mathcal{H}_{1}\mbox{ and }{\mathbb{X}}_{2}=\Gamma_{2}\backslash\mathcal{H}_{2}

A Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},\mathcal{H}),
also associates with each point

h:U(1)\to\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}

and all the homomorphisms are linked by conjugation by elements of

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}.
The image

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}

Since there is only one non-identity continuous automorphism of

u\mapsto u^{-1},

there are exactly two
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugacy classes of homomorphisms

which can be called

\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}^{-}.

It follows from Shimura theory, see
[
3
]
, that

{\mathbb{X}},
a complex Shimura variety and
{\mathbb{X}}^{c}
its complex conjugate,

{\mathbb{X}}=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{H}^{+}\Leftrightarrow{\mathbb{X}}^{c}=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{H}^{-}

(1)

and the action of complex conjugation corresponds to the automorphism
u\to u^{-1}
on
U(1).

Below we use the notation
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
for sets and
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\pm}
and

Fact

[
9
]

Suppose there is a categorical

\Sigma

(2)

First we note that

(3)

(4)

(5)

(6)

(7)

\forall g\in\mathrm{G}_{1}({\mathbb{Q}}),\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\ \ \phi(g\cdot x)=\phi(g)\cdot\phi(x)

(8)

and
\mathrm{G}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})

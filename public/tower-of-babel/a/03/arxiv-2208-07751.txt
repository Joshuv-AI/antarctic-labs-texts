Notations and some auxiliary lemmas

Sobolev spaces:
First, we introduce some notations used in this paper.
For
p\in[1,\,\infty]
, the notation
L^{p}(0,\,T;X)
stands for the set of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\,T)
with values in
X
and
\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{X}
belonging to
L^{p}(0,\,T)

W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=0}^{k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

Besov spaces:

\mathcal{S}
denotes the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing functions,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the
space of tempered distributions,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
the quotient space of tempered distributions which modulo polynomials.
We use
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
to denote the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f
.
To define Besov spaces, we need the following dyadic unity partition
(see e.g.
[
2
]
). Choose two nonnegative radial
functions
\varrho
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

supported respectively in the ball
\mathcal{B}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{4}\}
and the shell
\mathcal{C}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such that

\varrho(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\neq 0.

Then for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\varphi(\xi)=\varrho(\xi/2)-\varrho(\xi)
. Write
h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
and
\tilde{h}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varrho
, then nonhomogeneous dyadic blocks
\Delta_{j}
are defined by

\Delta_{j}u:=0~~\text{if}~~j\leq-2,~~\Delta_{-1}u:=\varrho(D)u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(y)u(x-y)dy,

\text{and}~~\Delta_{j}u:=\varphi\left(2^{-j}D\right)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy~~\text{if}~~j\geq 0.

The nonhomogeneous low-frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
is defined by

S_{j}u:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}u=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{N}\cup{0}.

The homogeneous dyadic blocks
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and homogeneous low-frequency cut-off operators
\dot{S}_{j}
are defined for
\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}u:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{Z}

\text{ and }~~\dot{S}_{j}u:=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{Z}

Now we introduce the definition of Besov spaces. Let
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2},s\in\mathbb{R}
, the nonhomogeneous Besov space

B_{p,r}^{s}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right);\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}:=\left\|2^{js}\right\|\Delta_{j}f\left\|{}_{L^{p}}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\right\}

and the homogeneous space

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)/\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right);\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}:=\left\|2^{js}\right\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\left\|{}_{L^{p}}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\right\}.

Moreover, for
s>0
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we may write the
equivalent norm below in
the nonhomogeneous Besov norm
\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}
as

\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{p}}}+\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}.

Motivated by
[
13
]
, we define
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
to be the class of all tempered distributions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}<\infty~\text{and}~\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}2^{j\alpha}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,~~\text{for any}~1\leq p\leq\infty.

(2.1)

It is clear that the Besov
spaces
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
are included in
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
for any
1\leq q<\infty
. Likewise, one can define the Besov
spaces
{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
similarly.

\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{r_{1}r_{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}=1.

From

v\in L^{r_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p+1,c(\mathbb{N})})
and
\theta\in L^{r_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p+1,\infty})
, we deduce from Lemma
2.3
that

\|(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p+1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}).

(3.19)

From Lemma
2.2
, we infer that

\|\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{r_{2}}(0,T;L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\text{O}(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}).

(3.20)

According to the definition of Besov spaces, we have

\||\theta^{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\|_{L^{p_{4}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p+1}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\|\theta^{\varepsilon}\|^{p-2}_{L^{p_{4}(p-2)}(0,T;L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\|\theta\|_{L^{r_{2}}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p+1,\infty})}^{p-2},

(3.21)

where we used
p_{4}(p-2)=r_{2}
, which means
\frac{p}{r_{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}=1
and
p\geq 2
.

Then substituting (
3.19
)-(
3.21
) into (
3.18
) and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have

\Big|\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big]\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}|\theta^{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}dxds\Big|\leq C\text{o}(1)\|\theta\|^{p-2}_{L^{p_{4}(p-2)}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p+1,\infty})}\rightarrow 0.

Then we have completed the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

4
General helicity conservation for 2-D surface quasi-geostrophic equations

In this section, we are concerned with the helicity conservation of weak solutions for 2-D generalized surface quasi-geostrophic equation (
1.1
). We also show two different approaches to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Approach 1: Littlewood-Paley theory

First, due to the divergence free of velocity
v(x,t)
and applying the operator
S_{N}
to the surface quasi-geostrophic equation (
1.1
), we get

S_{N}\theta_{t}+S_{N}\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)=0,

and

\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta_{t}+\partial_{j}S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)+\partial_{j}S_{N}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)=0.

Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\partial_{i}\partial_{t}S_{N}\theta dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}S_{N}\theta\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta[\partial_{j}S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)+\partial_{j}S_{N}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)]dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx,i=1,2.

Thanks to
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta)S_{N}\theta dx=0
, we may write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\big[\partial_{j}S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)-\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta\big]dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\big[\partial_{j}S_{N}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)-\partial_{j}(S_{N}v_{j}\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta)\big]dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta\partial_{j}(S_{N}v_{j}\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\big[S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)-(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta)\big]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[S_{N}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)-(S_{N}v_{j}\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta)\big]\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}\theta S_{N}v_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)\partial_{j}\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\big[S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)-(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta)\big]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[S_{N}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)-(S_{N}v_{j}\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta)\big]\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)-S_{N}\theta S_{N}v_{j}\big]\partial_{i}\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II+III.

To control
I
, we deduce from the Hölder inequality that

|I|\leq\|S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)-(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{6}}.

Taking advantage of Constantin-E-Titi identity
(
3.1
), Minkowski inequality and the Sobolev inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)-(\partial_{i}S_{N}v_{j}S_{N}\theta)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|\partial_{i}v_{j}(x-y)-\partial_{i}v_{j}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\theta(x-y)-\theta(x)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|\partial_{i}v_{j}-S_{N}\partial_{i}v_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\theta-S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|\partial_{i}v_{j}(x-y)-\partial_{i}v_{j}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla\theta(x-y)-\nabla\theta(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|\partial_{i}v_{j}-S_{N}\partial_{i}v_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla\theta-\nabla S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.2)

Arguing in the same manner as (
3.4
),
we observe that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}v_{j}(x-y)-\partial_{i}v_{j}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{j\leq N}2^{j}|y|\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\sum_{j>N}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(2^{N(\gamma-\alpha)}|y|\sum_{j\leq N}2^{-(N-j)(\gamma-\alpha)}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle+2^{(\gamma-1-\alpha)N}\sum_{j>N}2^{(N-j)(\alpha+1-\gamma)}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[2^{N(\gamma-\alpha)}|y|+2^{(\gamma-1-\alpha)N}\right]\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(2^{N}|y|+1)2^{(\gamma-1-\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N).

(4.3)

where
K_{1}
is defined in (
3.5
) and

\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}=2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Similar to the derivation of (
3.7
) and using (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|\nabla\theta(x-y)-\nabla\theta(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{j\leq N}2^{j}|y|\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\sum_{j>N}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|\sum_{j\leq N}2^{-(N-j)(1-\alpha)}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+2^{-\alpha N}\sum_{j>N}2^{(N-j)\alpha}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|+2^{-\alpha N}\Big]\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}\right)(N)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(2^{N}|y|+1)2^{-\alpha N}\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N).

Some straightforward computations yields

\|\nabla\theta-\nabla S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C2^{-\alpha N}\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N),

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\sum_{j\leq N}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 2^{N(1-\alpha)}\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{j}\right)(N),

(4.6)

where the Sobolev embedding was used and

\tilde{d}_{j}=2^{j\alpha}\|\Delta_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

As a consequence, we know

I\leq 2^{(\gamma-1-\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)2^{-\alpha N}\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)2^{N(1-\alpha)}\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{2}\right)(N).

Repeating the deduction process of
I
, we have

II\leq 2^{(\gamma-3\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{2}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{2}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{2}\right)(N).

Taking advantage of the Hölder inequality, we infer that

III\leq\|S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)-S_{N}\theta S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.7)

Following the path of (
4.3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)-S_{N}\theta S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2N}\int|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|v_{j}(x-y)-v_{j}(x)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\theta(x-y)-\theta(x)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|v_{j}-S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\theta-S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{2N}\int|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|\nabla v_{j}(x-y)-\nabla v_{j}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla\theta(x-y)-\nabla\theta(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla v_{j}-S_{N}\nabla v_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla\theta-\nabla S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

From (
4.3
)-(
4.4
), we have

\|S_{N}(v_{j}\theta)-S_{N}\theta S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 2^{(\gamma-1-\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)2^{-\alpha N}\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N).

(4.8)

It follows from (
4.6
) that

\|\nabla\partial_{j}S_{N}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\sum_{j\leq N}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 2^{N(1-\alpha)}\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{j}\right)(N).

(4.9)

Substituting (
4.8
) (
4.9
) into (
4.7
), we conclude that

III\leq C2^{(\gamma-3\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{j}\right)(N).

Finally, we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int S_{N}\theta\partial_{i}S_{N}\theta dx

\displaystyle\leq C2^{(\gamma-3\alpha)N}\left(K_{1}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\dot{\tilde{d}}_{j}\right)(N)\left(K_{2}\ast\tilde{d}_{j}\right)(N).

(4.10)

At this stage, the rest proof of this theorem is the same as the one of Theorem

1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Approach 2: Constantin-E-Titi type commutator estimates in physical Onsager type spaces

It is obvious that

\theta^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}=0,

and

\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}=0.

Thus, it follows from the direct computation that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\partial_{t}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

(4.11)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}[\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}]dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx,i=1,2.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\theta^{\varepsilon}dx=0
, we can rewrite the above equation (
4.11
) as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{j}(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\big]\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx,

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,t)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,0)\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,0)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dxds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big[(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\big]\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dxds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II+III.

Taking advantage of the Hölder inequality, we get

|I|\leq\|(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\|\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}.

(4.12)

Due to the hypothesis
\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
and the boundedness of Riesz transforms in homogeneous Besov spaces, we obtain
that

\nabla v=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\Lambda^{\gamma-1}\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha-\gamma+1}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})}).

Then we employ (3) in Lemma
2.3

with

q=\frac{6}{5},d=2,q_{1}=\frac{3}{2},q_{2}=\frac{3}{2}<2
and use

\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
and
\nabla v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha-\gamma+1}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
to derive that

\|(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq\text{o}(\varepsilon^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}),

(4.13)

where
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{6}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
and
0<\alpha-\gamma+1<1
were utilized.

By means of
Sobolev embedding theorem, Lemma
2.2
and
\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
, we conclude that

\|\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}).

(4.14)

Plugging (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) into (
4.12
), we arrive at

|I|\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{3\alpha-\gamma}).

Arguing as above, we deduce that

|II|\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{3\alpha-\gamma}).

It is enough to estimate the term
III
. Applying the Hölder inequality once again, we get

|III|\leq\|(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}.

(4.15)

Invoking (2) in Lemma
2.3

with

q=3,d=2,q_{1}=\frac{3}{2}=q_{2}=\frac{3}{2}<2,
we find

\|(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{2\alpha-\gamma+1}),

(4.16)

where we used
\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
and
\nabla v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha-\gamma+1}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})

In the light of Lemma
2.2
, we infer that

\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}).

(4.17)

Substituting
(

) and (
4.17
) into (

|III|\leq C\text{o}(\varepsilon^{3\alpha-\gamma}).

Since we need
0<\alpha-\gamma+1<1
, we discuss in two cases

and
\frac{3}{2}\leq\gamma<2

1.1
. This enables us to complete the proof.

5
Conclusion

We apply the Littlewood-Paley theory as
[
13
]
and the
Constantin-E-Titi type commutator estimates in physical Onsager type spaces
to study the energy (helicity) conservation of weak solutions for the 2-D generalized quasi-geostrophic equation with the velocity
v
determined by
v=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\Lambda^{\gamma-1}\theta
with
0<\gamma<2
, respectively. For
the case
0<\gamma<\frac{3}{2}
, the sufficient conditions for the energy (helicity) conservation of weak solutions of this equation in Onsager’s critical space are derived. For the more singular case
\frac{3}{2}\leq\gamma<2
, we obtain the corresponding results in critical spaces.
Since the Littlewood-Paley decomposition and Besov space and Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
are known for periodic domain, the main results are also valid for periodic case.

A natural question is to extend our results to other models which modifies the velocity. A possible candidate is the inviscid Leary-
\alpha
or Euler-
\alpha
system. After we completed the main part of this paper, we learned the energy conservation of these models recently studied by Boutros-Titi in
[
6
]
and Beekie-Novack in
[
4
]
. Compared with their results, the results here give how the critical regularity for the energy conservation of the weak solutions depends on the
the parameter
\alpha
of the velocity.

The
non-uniqueness of weak solutions to the standard surface quasi-geostrophic equation (
1.3
) can be found in
[
7
,
21
]
.
It would be interesting to show
the weak solutions to the generalized quasi-geostrophic equation (
1.1
)
are not unique.

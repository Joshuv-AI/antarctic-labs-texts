Microlocal Projector for Complete Flow
Sheng-Fu Chiu

Abstract
. In this note we generalize the construction of microlocal projector to the sublevel set of autonomous function with complete Hamiltonian flow under some mild conditions. Furthermore, we mention that the condition of being complete is optimal, by providing a non-example that violates the projector property.

53E50, 57R17, 18G80

1.
Introduction

In symplectic geometry and topology one often considers Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms with compact supports on a symplectic manifold. There are natural reasons regarding such assumption. First, the flow of such Hamiltonian is always complete. Second, they are bounded deformations of the identity and thus distinguish symplectic diffeomorphisms from conformal symplectic diffeomorphisms. Third, the natural compact-open topology on the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms is metrizable and has a countable basis. Nevertheless, relaxations of the condition of compact support are available under certain circumstances.

Let
Q
be a connected smooth manifold and denote the coordinates of its cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q
by
(q,p)
. In
[
2
]
the author constructed a microlocal projector and used it to prove a nonsqueezing result of contact balls. The existence of the microlocal ball projector relies on the geometric fact that on
T^{*}\mathds{R}^{n}
the Hamiltonian function
H_{1}(q,p)=q^{2}+p^{2}
satisfies the following condition:

For each
b\in\mathds{R}
, the set
H_{1}^{-1}((-\infty,b])
is compact.

We also refer the reader to
[
4
, Definition 1.4]
where Kawasaki and Orita made the same assumption to the moment map on
T^{*}Q
and studied its rigid fibers.

On the other hand, even the simplest linear motion in Hamiltonian dynamics has its Hamiltonian
H_{2}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}
, which does not satisfy the above condition. Instead it satisfies the following coercive condition:

For each compact set
K\subset Q
and each
b\in\mathds{R}
,

the set
\{(q,p)|q\in K,H_{2}(q,p)\leq b\}
is compact.

Likewisely in Lagrangian dynamics there are convex and superlinear conditions for the sake of the well-definition of the Legendre transformation.

One could also consider other Hamiltonians such as the superposition of two harmonic oscillators
H_{3}(q,p)=({q_{1}}^{2}+{p_{1}}^{2})-2({q_{2}}^{2}+{p_{2}}^{2})
. The function
H_{3}
does not obey the above coercive condition, nor does it possess any compact level surface. That being said, it is easy to see that every single level surface of
H_{3}
is foliated by compact invariant tori, and on each such leaf the Hamiltonian flow lives and lasts forever.

A rather natural condition one could ask for is the following condition (
\star
):

Definition 1.1
.

A time-independent Hamiltonian function
H:T^{*}Q\rightarrow\mathds{R}
satisfies the condition (
\star
) if:

(1)

(Long-term Completness) The solution
h_{s}\in Ham(T^{*}Q)
of the Hamiltonian equations
(\dot{q}(s),\dot{p}(s))=X_{H}(q(s),p(s))
are defined for all
s\in\mathds{R}
.

(2)

(Short-term Separating) There is a connected open interval
J
, with
J=-J
, such that for any given
0<s\in J
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q
there exists at most one Hamiltonian trajectory
\gamma
from
T^{*}_{q_{1}}Q
to
T^{*}_{q_{2}}Q
with time
s
.

One sees that the above condition (
\star
) applies to many circumstances in symplectic geometry and dynamical systems. In this note we refine the argument in
[
2
]
to construct microlocal projectors for those
H
satisfying the condition (
\star
). Our result is:

Theorem 1.2

(See Theorem
3.2
for its definition of mircolocal projector)
.

Suppose
H:T^{*}Q\rightarrow\mathds{R}
is a function satisfying the condition (
\star
). Let
B_{C}=H^{-1}((-\infty,C))
be an open sublevel set of level
C
. Then there exists a microlocal projector
\mathscr{P}_{C}
for
B_{C}
.

A motivation for complete Hamiltonian flow comes from quantum mechanics. By Stone’s theorem the quantum Hamiltonian observable
\hat{H}
is always given self-adjoint, and its evolution on the quantum system is always governed by the one-parameter unitary group
e^{is\hat{H}}
, which is automatically defined for all times
s\in\mathds{R}
. The microlocal projector in a derived category, in a loose sense, is a classical geometric analogue of the quantum projection operator that plays an essential role in quantum measurements.

We would like to refer the reader to
[
6
]
that partial quasi-morphisms and quasi-states, obtained from spectral invariants, can be defined for Hamiltonians with complete flow. The authors of
[
6
]
also suggested a possible generalization of Mather’s alpha function for such Hamiltonians. We hope that in the future our construction of microlocal projector could shed some light on this matter.

Theory of microlocal sheaves

2.1.
Sheaves and their microlocal supports

Let
\mathds{k}
be a ring. Let

be the derived category of sheaves of
\mathds{k}
-modules on

\mathscr{F}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)

C^{1}

(1)

(R\Gamma_{\{q\in Q|\phi(q)\geq\phi(q_{0})\}}\mathscr{F})_{q_{0}}\ncong 0.

Functorial relations between microsupports are extensively used in the determination of subcategories of certain sheaves. Their explanations and proofs can be found in

5

7

Let

T^{*}X\xleftarrow{f_{\pi}}T^{*}X\times_{X}Y\xrightarrow{f^{t}}T^{*}Y.

The microsupports of the images under Grothendieck’s six functors(

Proposition 2.1
.

[
5

Let
\mathscr{G}\in D(Y)
and assume

is proper on

SS(Rf_{*}\mathscr{G})=SS(Rf_{!}\mathscr{G})\subset f_{\pi}((f^{t})^{-1}(SS(\mathscr{G}))).

Proposition 2.2
.

[
5

Let
\mathscr{F}\in D(X)
and assume

is non-characteristic for

f_{\pi}^{-1}(SS(\mathscr{F}))\cap T_{Y}^{*}X\subset Y\times_{X}T_{X}^{*}X

(1)

SS(f^{-1}\mathscr{F})\subset(f^{t})(f_{\pi}^{-1}(SS(\mathscr{F})))
.

(2)

f^{-1}\mathscr{F}\otimes\omega_{Y/X}\cong f^{!}\mathscr{F}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
5

Let
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\in D(X)

(1)

SS(\mathscr{F})\cap SS(\mathscr{G})^{a}\subset T_{X}^{*}X\Rightarrow SS(\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{G})\subset SS(\mathscr{F})+SS(\mathscr{G})

(2)

SS(\mathscr{F})\cap SS(\mathscr{G})\subset T_{X}^{*}X\Rightarrow SS(\underline{Rhom}(\mathscr{F};\mathscr{G}))\subset SS(\mathscr{F})^{a}+SS(\mathscr{G})

When it comes to the products and projections, a sharper control of microsupports is available. Let
E
be a nontrivial finite-dimensional real vector space and
\pi:X\times E\rightarrow X
be the projection map.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
7

Let
\kappa:T^{*}X\times E\times E^{\star}\rightarrow T^{*}X\times E^{\star}

be the closed embedding

of zero-section. Then for

SS(R\pi_{!}\mathscr{F}),SS(R\pi_{*}\mathscr{F})\subset i^{-1}\overline{\kappa(SS(\mathscr{F}))}.

When

\mathscr{F}\in D(X\times Y)
is nonsingular in a neighborhood of

(x,\omega)
.

2.2.
The Tamarkin categories
\mathcal{D}

It was shown in
[
5
]
that for any
\mathscr{F}
in
D(Q)
the correpsonding microsupport

is always a closed
\mathds{R}^{+}
-conic coisotropic subset of
T^{*}Q

[

considered certain subcategories of the category
D(X\times\mathds{R})
as follows. Denote the coordinate of
T^{*}(Q\times\mathds{R})
by
(q,p,z,\zeta)
where
(q,p)\in T^{*}Q

[

D_{\zeta\leq 0}=\{\mathcal{F}\in D(Q\times\mathds{R})|SS(\mathscr{F})\subset\{\zeta\leq 0\}\}.

Definition 2.5
.

The Tamarkin category is defined to be

(2)

\mathcal{D}(Q\times\mathds{R}):=D_{\zeta\leq 0}(Q\times\mathds{R})^{\perp,l}

Let
\mathscr{F}\in D(Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times\mathds{R})

and
\pi_{Q23R2}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\times\mathds{R}_{1}\times\mathds{R}_{2}\rightarrow Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\times\mathds{R}_{2}

For any subset

2.3.
Sheaf quantization

Let
I\subset\mathds{R}
be an open interval (connected subset of
\mathds{R}
with non-empty interior) containing the origin and let

h:I\times T^{*}Q\rightarrow T^{*}Q
be the Hamiltonian diffeomorphism generated by an autonomous Hamiltonian function
H:T^{*}Q\rightarrow\mathds{R}
.

It is convenient to construct such sheaf quantization using the actional functional (which is implicitly carried out in Guillermou-Kashiwara-Schapira’s
[
3

). Define the action functional
S:I\times T^{*}Q\rightarrow\mathds{R}
by
S(s_{1};q_{1},p_{1})=\int_{\gamma}pdq-Hds
where
\gamma
is the Hamiltonian trajectory
\gamma(s)=h_{s}(q_{1},p_{1})
connecting
\gamma(0)=(q_{1},p_{1})
and
\gamma(s_{1})=(q_{2},p_{2})
. The Hamiltonian diffeomorphism

\tilde{h}:I\times T^{*}Q\times T^{*}_{>0}\mathds{R}\rightarrow T^{*}Q\times T^{*}_{>0}\mathds{R}

(6)

\tilde{h}_{s}(q_{1},p_{1};z,\zeta)=(q_{2},p_{2};z+S(s;q_{1},\frac{p_{1}}{\zeta}),\zeta),

where
(q_{2},p_{2})=h_{s}(q_{1},p_{1})
. The map
\tilde{h}
is homogeneous in the sense that
h\circ(id_{I}\times\rho)=\rho\circ\tilde{h}
as maps from
I\times T^{*}Q\times T^{*}_{>0}\mathds{R}
to
T^{*}Q
. In other words, the following diagram commutes:

(7)

\textstyle{{{T_{\zeta>0}^{*}}(Q\times\mathds{R})\times I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\tilde{h}}
\textstyle{{{T_{\zeta>0}^{*}}(Q\times\mathds{R})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{{T^{*}Q\times I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

Suppose that the flow of

\mathscr{S}\in\mathcal{D}(I\times Q\times Q\times\mathds{R})

Recall from Definition

(9)

\mathscr{S}_{J}:=\mathds{k}_{\{(s,q_{1},q_{2},z)|z+S(s;q_{1},q_{2})\geq 0\}}\in\mathcal{D}(J\times Q\times Q\times\mathds{R}).

[

3.
Microlocal Projector

3.1.
Projectors associated to sublevel sets

This subsection is devoted to the construction of the projection kernel associated with the open sublevel set. We assume

H:T^{*}Q\rightarrow\mathds{R}

is an autonomous Hamiltonian function satisfying the condition (
\star
) (See Definition
1.1
).

For this Hamiltonian
H
we take the object
\mathscr{S}\in\mathcal{D}(I\times Q\times Q\times\mathds{R})
obtained in Proposition
2.8
. By the completeness of
H
we choose the time interval
I
to be the whole
\mathds{R}
. We stick to the notation
I
in order to distinguish the time (in
s
) with the extra action variable
\mathds{R}
(in
z
). We denote the dual of
I
by
I^{*}
(in
t
).

Let us perform the Fourier-Sato-Tamarkin transform on
\mathscr{S}
with respect to the time variable
I\rightsquigarrow I^{*}
:

(10)

\widehat{\mathscr{S}}:=\mathscr{S}\underset{s}{\bullet}\mathds{k}_{\{(s,t,z)|z+st\geq 0\}}[1]\in\mathcal{D}(I^{*}\times Q\times Q\times\mathds{R})

In the realm of functional analysis, the Fourier transform is expected to intertwine uniform distributions and delta functions. Similarly in algebraic microlocal analysis we have the following property for the Fourier-Sato-Tamarkin transform:

(11)

\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\underset{t}{\circ}{\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}}=\mathscr{S}\underset{s}{\bullet}\mathds{k}_{\{(s,t,z)|z+st\geq 0\}}[1]\underset{t}{\circ}{\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}}\cong\mathscr{S}\underset{s}{\bullet}\mathds{k}_{\{s=0,z\geq 0\}}=\mathscr{S}|_{s=0}

which is, by Theorem
2.8
,

(12)

\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\underset{t}{\circ}{\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}}\cong\mathds{k}_{\Delta}.

Note that in the above formulation we write the composition product "
\underset{t}{\circ}
" instead of the convolution product "
\underset{t}{\bullet}
" under the convention that
\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}=\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}_{t}}
lacks the
z
-component. It is easy to verify that the composition
(-)\underset{t}{\circ}{\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}}
is equivalent to the convolution
(-)\underset{t}{\bullet}{\mathds{k}_{\{(t,z)|t\in\mathds{R},z\geq 0\}}}
in
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
C
be a real number and let
B_{C}=\{(q,p)\in T^{*}Q|H(q,p)<C\}
be the open sublevel set of
H
of level
C
.

Definition 3.1

(Microlocal Projectors)
.

We define the microlocal projectors associated to
B_{C}
and
T^{*}Q\setminus B_{C}
respectively by

(13)

\mathscr{P}_{C}:=\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\underset{t}{\circ}\mathds{k}_{\{t<C\}}

and

(14)

\mathscr{Q}_{C}:=\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\underset{t}{\circ}\mathds{k}_{\{t\geq C\}}.

respectively.

Together with
\mathds{k}_{\Delta}
they form the following exact triangle

(15)

\mathscr{P}_{C}\rightarrow\mathds{k}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathscr{Q}_{C}\xrightarrow{+1}

which can be obtained by composing
\widehat{\mathscr{S}}
with the fundamental triangle

(16)

\mathds{k}_{\{t<C\}}\rightarrow\mathds{k}_{\mathds{R}}\rightarrow\mathds{k}_{\{t\geq C\}}\xrightarrow{+1}.

Since
\mathscr{P}_{C}
,
\mathscr{Q}_{C}
, and
\mathds{k}_{\Delta}
are objects of
\mathcal{D}(Q\times Q\times\mathds{R})
, convolutions with them give rise to functors from
\mathcal{D}(Q\times\mathds{R})
to
\mathcal{D}(Q\times\mathds{R})
. Moreover we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

The convolution with the exact triangle

(17)

\mathscr{P}_{C}\rightarrow\mathds{k}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathscr{Q}_{C}\xrightarrow{+1}

gives rise to the semiorthogonal decomposition with respect to the triple of subset categories

(18)

(\mathcal{D}_{B_{C}}(Q\times\mathds{R}),\mathcal{D}(Q\times\mathds{R}),\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}Q\setminus B_{C}}(Q\times\mathds{R})).

\lim\{p_{2}-p_{2}^{\prime}\}=0
and
\lim\{p_{2}^{\prime}\}
is finite. The other conditions from (
44
) then imply that

(47)

t=H(q_{2},\lim\{p_{2}^{\prime}\})=H(q_{2},\lim\{p_{2}\})\geq C,

which contradicts to the assumption

(48)

(t,\tau;q_{1},p_{1};z_{1},1)=v\in T^{*}_{\zeta_{1}>0}((-\infty,C)\times Q_{1}\times\mathds{R}_{1}),

that is
t<C
.

∎

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

∎

By the general property

, all the subcategories mentioned above are thick (closed under direct summands). Therefore Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
together imply Theorem
3.2
.

4.
A Non-example

What if the Hamiltonian flow fails to be complete?

Let us consider the famous Cherry Hamiltonian
[
1
]
on
T^{*}\mathds{R}^{2}
with parameter
\mu
,

(49)

H_{ch}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}({q_{1}}^{2}+{p_{1}}^{2})-({q_{2}}^{2}+{p_{2}}^{2})+\mu[q_{2}({q_{1}}^{2}-{p_{1}}^{2})-2q_{1}p_{1}p_{2}]

The Hamiltonian equations of the flow are

(50)

\begin{split}\dot{q_{1}}&=p_{1}-2\mu q_{2}p_{1}-2\mu q_{1}p_{2}\\
\dot{p_{1}}&=-q_{1}-2\mu q_{1}q_{2}+2\mu p_{1}p_{2}\\
\dot{q_{2}}&=-2p_{2}-2\mu q_{1}p_{1}\\
\dot{p_{2}}&=2q_{2}+\mu{p_{1}}^{2}-\mu{q_{1}}^{2}\end{split}

The Cherry Hamiltonian
H_{ch}
is an example of unstable equilibrium which linearization is stable. In fact, the linear part (corresponding to
\mu=0
) of the Cherry Hamiltonian (
49
) is just the superposition of two simple harmonic oscillators.

For
\mu>0
, on the non-compact level surface
H_{ch}^{-1}(0)
there is a family of solutions of (
50
), parametrized by two parameters
a
and
b
:

(51)

\begin{split}q_{1}(s)=\frac{-\sqrt{2}}{2\mu s+a}\sin(s+b)\quad,&\quad p_{1}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2\mu s+a}\cos(s+b)\\
q_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{2\mu s+a}\sin(2s+2b)\quad,&\quad p_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{2\mu s+a}\cos(2s+2b).\end{split}

The flow on this
H_{ch}^{-1}(0)
cannot be made complete. For example when
(a,b)=(0,0)
, the solution (
51
) is a curve defined for
s\neq 0
:

(52)

\begin{split}q_{1}(s)=\frac{-\sin(s)}{\sqrt{2}\mu s}\qquad,&\qquad p_{1}(s)=\frac{\cos(s)}{\sqrt{2}\mu s}\\
q_{2}(s)=\frac{\sin(2s)}{2\mu s}\qquad,&\qquad p_{2}(s)=\frac{\cos(2s)}{2\mu s}\end{split}

When
s
approaches to
\pm\infty
, the solution curve (
51
) swirls around and approaches to the origin. When
s
approaches to
0
, its
q
-components become stable but the
p
-components totally blow up, in a way depending on the sign of
s
.

Let us show that
H_{ch}
violates the projector property by violating the middle equality in (
47

\lim\{y^{\prime}_{n}-y_{n}\}&=(0,0).\end{split}

We choose
\mu>0
and

such that
\frac{1}{2}+2\mu q_{1}+\mu q_{2}=0

) one has

(54)

\begin{split}H_{ch}(x,y_{n})&=\frac{1}{2}q_{1}^{2}-q_{2}^{2}+\mu q_{1}^{2}q_{2}-(\frac{1}{2}+2\mu q_{1}+\mu q_{2})n\\

and

(55)

\begin{split}H_{ch}(x,y^{\prime}_{n})&=\frac{1}{2}q_{1}^{2}-q_{2}^{2}+\mu q_{1}^{2}q_{2}+1+(2\mu q_{1})[(n+1)-\sqrt{n(n+1)}]\\
&=-2\mu q_{1}^{3}-\frac{2}{\mu}q_{1}-\frac{1}{4\mu^{2}}+1+(2\mu q_{1})[(n+1)-\sqrt{n(n+1)}]\end{split}

It follows that when

(56)

The term

varies, and the term
\lim H_{ch}(x,y^{\prime}_{n})
is always different from
\lim H_{ch}(x,y_{n})
unless

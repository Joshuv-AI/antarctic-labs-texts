IRS-Aided Radar: Enhanced Target Parameter Estimation via Intelligent Reflecting Surfaces Thanks: This work was supported in part by the U.S. National Science Foundation Grant ECCS-1809225.
Zahra Esmaeilbeig †,1 , Kumar Vijay Mishra ‡,2 , and Mojtaba Soltanalian †,3

Abstract
The intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) technology has recently attracted a lot of interest in wireless communications research. An IRS consists of passive reflective elements capable of tuning the phase, amplitude, frequency, and polarization of impinging waveforms. We investigate the deployment of IRS to aid radar systems when the line-of-sight (LoS) link to the targets is weak or blocked. We demonstrate that deployment of multiple IRS platforms provides a virtual or non-line-of-sight (NLoS) link between the radar and target leading to an enhanced radar performance. Numerical experiments indicate that the IRS enhances the target parameter estimation when the LoS link is weaker by ~ 10 − 1 10^{-1} in comparison to the NLoS link.

As expected, the optimized IRS case leads to lower estimation error in comparison with the non-optimized IRS. Consider a pulse-Doppler radar system that transmits a train of
N

N

Assume the target scene consists of a single Swerling-0 model

27
]

\alpha_{{}_{T}}
and the normalized target Doppler shift
\nu
(expressed in radians) relative to the radar; note that, in the formulation of this paper, the target range is assumed to be known.

In the absence of an IRS, the transmit signal is reflected back from the target and collected by the radar receiver (Fig.

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{{}_{T}}h_{{}_{{\textrm{LoS}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x(t-nT_{{}_{{\textrm{PRI}}}})e^{j\nu t}+n(t),

\displaystyle\approx\alpha_{{}_{T}}h_{{}_{{\textrm{LoS}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x(t-nT_{{}_{{\textrm{PRI}}}})e^{j\nu nT_{p}}+n(t),

(2)

accounts for the radar-target-radar channel state information (CSI), and the last approximation follows from the fact that
\nu\ll 1/T_{p}

Each snapshot of the received signal are sampled at the rate
1/T_{{}_{p}}

\lfloor T_{\textrm{PRI}}/T_{p}\rfloor

At this fixed target range in fast-time, we collect all
N

(3)

where
{\mathbf{p}}(\nu)=[1,e^{jT_{{}_{p}}\nu},\ldots,e^{j(N-1)T_{{}_{p}}\nu}]^{T}

The received signal is a superposition of the reflected signals from all NLoS paths as

{\mathbf{y}}=\alpha_{{}_{T}}h_{{}_{{\textrm{LoS}}}}\left[{\mathbf{x}}\odot{\mathbf{p}(\nu)}\right]+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{{}_{T,k}}h_{{}_{{\textrm{NLoS}},k}}\left[{\mathbf{x}}\odot{\mathbf{p}(\nu_{k})}\right]+{\mathbf{n}}.

III
IRS-Aided Target Parameter Estimation

Following the Gauss-Markov theorem
[
36
]
, the BLUE for
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is

\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}=\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{y}}.

(9)

The covariance matrix of
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
is

{\mathbf{C}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}=\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1},

(10)

with the minimum achieved variance for
\hat{\alpha}_{k}
given by

\textrm{var}(\hat{\alpha_{k}})=\left[\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1}\right]_{kk}
. The overall mean-squared-error (MSE) of the proposed estimator is thus
\text{MSE}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}})=\textrm{Tr}\left(\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1}\right)
. The optimal phase-shift matrices
\{{\mathbf{\Theta}_{k}}\},~k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
to minimize the MSE of target cross-section parameter are obtained by solving the following problem

\underset{{\mathbf{\Theta}_{k}},~k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}{\textrm{minimize}}\text{MSE}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}})=\underset{{\mathbf{\Theta}_{k}},~k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}{\textrm{minimize}}\textrm{Tr}\left(\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1}\right).

(11)

The following theorem states that the optimal phase shifts of the
K
different IRS platforms are decoupled. Also, the optimal phase shift for IRS
k
compensates for the total phases in the channels
{\mathbf{g}}_{k}
and
{\mathbf{h}}_{k}
.

Theorem 1
.

The solution to the optimization problem

\underset{{\mathbf{\Theta}_{k}},~~k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}{\textrm{minimize}}~\textrm{MSE}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}),

(12)

is

{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{k}^{*}=\textrm{Diag}\left(e^{\textrm{j}\text{{arg}}({\mathbf{c}}_{k})}\right),

(13)

where
{\mathbf{c}}_{k}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\text{{Diag}}({\mathbf{g}}_{k})^{H}{\mathbf{h}}_{k}
.

Proof.

Define the IRS-observed Doppler shift matrix as
{\mathbf{P}(\nu)}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}[{\mathbf{p}}(\nu_{1}),\ldots,{\mathbf{p}}(\nu_{K})]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K}
. Incorporating this definition in (
7
)-(
8

{\mathbf{A}}=\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{P}(\nu)}\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{h}}_{{}_{\textrm{NLoS}}})
, which is used to compute

\left({\mathbf{A}}^{H}{\mathbf{R}}^{-1}{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{-1}={\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}^{-H}=\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{h}}_{{}_{\textrm{NLoS}}})^{-1}\,\Psi\,\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{h}}_{{}_{\textrm{NLoS}}})^{-H},\\

(14)

with
{\mathbf{\Psi}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}{\mathbf{\Omega}}^{-1}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{\Omega}}^{H}
and
{\mathbf{\Omega}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{P}(\nu)}

Considering the property
{\mathbf{a}}\odot{\mathbf{b}}=\textrm{Diag}({\mathbf{a}}){\mathbf{b}}

{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{k}

{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{H}{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{k}{\mathbf{g}}_{k}={\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{H}[\textrm{diag}({\mathbf{\Theta}}_{k})\odot{\mathbf{g}}_{k}]={\mathbf{c}}_{k}^{H}\textrm{diag}({\mathbf{\Theta}}_{k}).

(15)

It now follows that the optimal solution to (

VI
Summary

We studied the deployment of IRS in narrowband radar sensing and we presented an initial study on the effectiveness of IRS in assisting target estimation in radar. The formulation proposed in this paper is useful as a baseline for other IRS-aided radar settings. We derived the optimal IRS phases in terms of the mean square error of target parameter estimation. We indicated that IRS aids in target parameter estimation when the LoS link is weaker in relative SNR by ~
10^{-1}
than the NLoS link. Our numerical experiments reveal the effectiveness of the IRS even with non-optimized phase shifts.

Abstract
. Let C ⊂ ℙ K 2 C\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{K}^{2} be an algebraic curve over a number field K K , and denote by d K d_{K} the degree of K K over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} . We prove that the number of K K -rational points of height at most H H in C C is bounded by c ​ d 2 ​ H 2 ​ d K / d ​ ( log ⁡ H ) κ cd^{2}H^{2d_{K}/d}(\log H)^{\kappa} where c , κ c,\kappa are absolute constants. We also prove analogous results for global fields in positive characteristic, and, for higher dimensional varieties. The quadratic dependence on d d in the bound as well as the exponent of H H are optimal; the novel aspect is the quadratic dependence on d d which answers a question raised by Salberger. We derive new results on Heath-Brown’s and Serre’s dimension growth conjecture for global fields, which generalize in particular the results by the first two authors and Novikov from the case K = ℚ K={\mathbb{Q}} . The proofs however are of a completely different nature, replacing the real analytic approach previously used by the p p -adic determinant method. The optimal dependence on d d is achieved by a key improvement in the treatment of high multiplicity points on mod p p reductions of algebraic curves.

1.4.
Sketch of the proof

We sketch the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1
for
the basic case
K={\mathbb{Q}}
, which go through with small technical
modifications in the general case. It is relatively easy to reduce to
the case
d>(\log H)^{2}
, because otherwise the result already follows
from known results (from
[
2
]
or
[
5
]
). Note that in this regime, the
H^{2/d}
term
in (
2
) is bounded by a constant, so our goal is to
prove a bound of order
d^{2}(\log H)^{\kappa}
. Also suppose for
simplicity that
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We choose a prime
p
of size roughly
(\log H)^{4}
. Denote by

C_{p}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
the modulo
p
reduction of
C
, fix
a point
P
in
C_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and write
\mu_{P}
for the multiplicity of
P
on
C_{p}
. We will treat each of
the points
P
separately, as there are at most
p^{2}\sim(\log H)^{8}

of them. Our goal is to use the
p
-adic interpolation determinant
method
[
11
,
18
]
to interpolate all
rational points in
C({\mathbb{Q}},H)
reducing to
P
in
C_{p}
by an algebraic
curve of degree
d-1
. This would give us a bound of order
d^{2}
for
the number of points lying over
P
by Bézout. In the original
p
-adic determinant method from
[
11
]
, when one considers
d
as fixed and

H\gg 1
, it is necessary to work with points
P
satisfying

\mu_{P}=1
. If one allows singular points as in
[
18
]
, the determinant method
generally only gives a bound of order
H^{2\mu_{P}/d}
rather than the
requisite
H^{2/d}
. A key observation is that in our special regime

d>(\log H)^{2}
the condition
\mu_{P}=1
can be significantly relaxed
— in fact assuming just
\mu_{P}<d/\log H
we already have

H^{2\mu_{P}/d}\sim 1\sim H^{2/d}.

(9)

In other words, we can treat, with the standard
p
-adic determinant
method, all points
P\in C_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
except those having

\mu_{P}\geqslant d/\log H
. We refer to these latter points as having “high
multiplicity”, and to the others as having “low multiplicity”. We
show in Proposition
1
that all points of high multiplicity lie
in an algebraic curve in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
of degree
N\log H
for some
universal constant
N
; the proof of Proposition
1
relies on some basic intersection theory.

We now repeat this argument with roughly
(\log H)^{4}
different primes

p_{i}
. Any point of
C({\mathbb{Q}},H)
that has low multiplicity modulo one of
these primes is handled by the
p_{i}
-adic interpolation determinant
method as above. Finally, the remaining points lie on a curve of
degree
N\log H
modulo each
p_{i}
. This means that a polynomial
interpolation determinant of degree
N\log H
for these points must
vanish modulo each
p_{i}
. Comparing this with the height bound for the
determinant implies that the determinant vanishes identically, i.e.,
that all remaining points are interpolated by one additional algebraic
curve of degree at most
N\log H<d
. Another application of Bézout’s theorem
concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

To prove Theorem

1

We recall the definition of the multiplicity of a point on a

and let

is a homogenous polynomial of

where the equality holds in the completed local ring

Proposition 1
.

Let

be a field and let

be a homogeneous polynomial in

. Let

lie in a curve

For the proofs of our higher dimensional results like

,
4
,
5
,

to capture the high multiplicity points

Proposition 2

Let

In fact, Proposition

3
, for effective cycles of any
codimension instead of hypersurfaces, that we will prove by induction

Let

If

\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X):=(\dim X-1)!\cdot(\textrm{the leading coefficient of

Recall that an effective cycle in a non-singular variety

for more detail about multiplicities and Hilbert

in

is defined by linearity, that is, by

\deg(X):=\sum_{i}a_{i}\deg(X_{i}),

where

is the degree of

.

Proposition 3
.

Let
F
be any field. Let

,

m\geqslant 1

be an effective cycle in

\Gamma(F)
where
\operatorname{mult}_{P}\Gamma>D/k

depending only on

.

Note that the constants

from Propositions

Let us first recall some basics about multiplicities and intersection
products.

We will use the following lemma in the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma 4
.

Let
F
be any field. Let
X=\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i}
and
Y=\sum_{j}b_{j}Y_{j}
be
pure dimensional effective cycles of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F}
, and
P
a closed

. Suppose that

and
Y
intersect properly,
i.e., for any

, we have
\operatorname{codim}(Z)=\operatorname{codim}(X_{i})+\operatorname{codim}(Y_{j})
. Then we
have

\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot Y)\geq\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X)\operatorname{mult}_{P}(Y),

where
X\cdot Y
is the intersection product on the level of cycles,
which is well-defined since
X
and
Y
intersect properly.

Note that the intersection product
X\cdot Y
in Lemma

4
is a linear combination of the irreducible
components of the

. For the sake of completeness, we
provide a proof for Lemma

of

, we denote by

the intersection multiplicity of

and

[

Lemma 5
.

Assume that
F
is an algebraically closed field. Let
X
be a pure

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F}
, and
P\in X
a closed point. Then for a
general linear subspace
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F}
such that

\dim(L)=\operatorname{codim}(X)
and

, we have

\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X)=i(P,X\cdot L;\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F}).

Proof of Lemma
5
.

We may assume that
X
is an irreducible closed subvariety of

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F}
. It is well-known that the dimension of the tangent cone
at a point of

is equal to
\dim(X)
. Hence it follows from

[
9
, p. 227, Corollary 12.4]
that

i(P,X\cdot L;\mathbb{P}^{n}_{F})=\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X)\operatorname{mult}_{P}(L)=\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X)
for a
general choice of
L
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

We may assume that
F
is algebraically
closed. Take a linear subspace
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of dimension

\operatorname{codim}(X)+\operatorname{codim}(Y)
passing through
P
such that

\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot Y)=i(P,(X\cdot Y)\cdot L;\mathbb{P}^{n})
, by
Lemma
5
. Since
X\cdot L
and

are of
complementary dimension, we have

\begin{split}\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot Y)&=i(P,(X\cdot Y)\cdot L;\mathbb{P}^{n})\\
&=i(P,(X\cdot L)\cdot Y;\mathbb{P}^{n})\\
&\geq\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot L)\operatorname{mult}_{P}(Y),\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from
[
9
, p. 227, Corollary
12.4]
. Now, again in view of Lemma
5
,
take a linear subspace
L^{\prime}
of dimension
\operatorname{codim}(X\cdot L)
passing
through
P
such that

\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot L)=i(P,(X\cdot L)\cdot L^{\prime};\mathbb{P}^{n})
. Then,

\begin{split}\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X\cdot L)&=i(P,(X\cdot L)\cdot L^{\prime};\mathbb{P}^{n})\\
&=i(P,X\cdot(L\cdot L^{\prime});\mathbb{P}^{n})\\
&\geq\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X)\operatorname{mult}_{P}(L\cdot L^{\prime})=\operatorname{mult}_{P}(X),\end{split}

where again the inequality follows from
[
9
, p. 227, Corollary
12.4]
. Lemma
4
is now proved.

Proof of Proposition

This is well-defined because of the product formula. If

.
For an integer

|x|_{v_{\infty}}\leq B^{1/d_{K}},&\text{ if $K$ is a function field},\end{cases}

in
{\mathbb{C}}
. If

(28)

for

, then there exists a point

in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}(K)
for which

(y_{1}:\ldots:y_{n+1})=x
and such that

and such that moreover

is well-defined in

for any prime ideal

for some constant

. Hence, for such

,

Abstract
. Consider the eigenvalue problem of a linear second order elliptic operator: − D Δ φ − 2 α ∇ m ( x ) ⋅ ∇ φ + V ( x ) φ = λ φ in Ω , -D\Delta\varphi-2\alpha\nabla m(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi\ \ \hbox{ in }\Omega, complemented by the Dirichlet boundary condition or the following general Robin boundary condition: ∂ φ ∂ n + β ⁡ ( x ) ​ φ = 0 on ​ ∂ Ω , \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}+\beta(x)\varphi=0\ \ \hbox{ on }\partial\Omega, where Ω ⊂ ℝ N ​ ( N ≥ 1 ) \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1) is a bounded smooth domain, n ⁡ ( x ) n(x) is the unit exterior normal to ∂ Ω \partial\Omega at x ∈ ∂ Ω x\in\partial\Omega , D > 0 D>0 and α > 0 \alpha>0 are, respectively, the diffusion and advection coefficients, m ∈ C 2 ​ ( Ω ¯ ) , V ∈ C ⁡ ( Ω ¯ ) m\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}),\,V\in C(\overline{\Omega}) , β ∈ C ⁡ ( ∂ Ω ) \beta\in C(\partial\Omega) are given functions, and β \beta allows to be positive, sign-changing or negative. In this paper, we aim to establish, as α \alpha approaches ∞ \infty , the asymptotic behavior of the principal eigenvalue under appropriate conditions on the advection function m m . For N = 1 N=1 , we provide a complete characterization of the asymptotic behavior, assuming that the derivative of m m changes sign at most finitely many times. Our findings not only improve upon the previous work in [ 6 , 9 , 50 ] , but also partially address some of the open questions posed in [ 6 ] . Furthermore, our results elucidate the novel influence of boundary conditions on such asymptotics.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following eigenvalue problem of a linear second order elliptic operator with Robin boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-D\Delta\varphi-2\alpha\nabla m(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}+\beta(x)\varphi=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1)
is a bounded smooth domain,
n(x)
is the unit exterior normal to

\partial\Omega
at
x\in\partial\Omega
,
V\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,
\beta\in C(\partial\Omega)
, the advection function
m\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
D>0
and
\alpha>0
stand for, respectively, the diffusion and advection coefficients. It is worth mentioning that the function
\beta
on the boundary condition allows to be positive, sign-changing or negative in our study.

We are also concerned with the eigenvalue problem with Dirichlet boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-D\Delta\varphi-2\alpha\nabla m(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\varphi=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

with
m,\,V
and
\Omega
fulfilling the same smoothness assumptions as before.

Given
D>0,\,\alpha,\,m
and
V
, it is well known that (
1.1
) (and (
1.2
)) admits a smallest eigenvalue (also called as
principal eigenvalue
), denoted by
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
, which corresponds to a positive eigenfunction (called as

). For the existence and uniqueness of the principal eigenvalue of the eigenvalue problems (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
4.1
) in Section 4, one may refer to
[

) (i.e., (
4.8
) below), which will be used frequently later, is also a standard result. This can be easily proved similarly to the classical case of

involving the use of the trace theorem (see
[
31

or
[
47

The main focus of our work is to demonstrate how boundary conditions play a crucial role in the asymptotic behavior of the principal eigenvalue, particularly in the context of large advection rate or large/small diffusion rate. In
[

Let us first recall some existing research works on the asymptotic behavior of the principal eigenvalue with respect to large advection closely related to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). In
[
6
]
, Berestycki, Hamel and Nadirashvili considered the following eigenvalue problem subject to Neumnn boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-D\Delta\varphi-2\alpha v(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

as well as the one subject to Dirichlet boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-D\Delta\varphi-2\alpha v(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\varphi=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a divergence-free vector field;
that is,

{\rm div}\,v=0\ \ \,\mbox{ in}\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(1.5)

Actually,
[
6
]
dealt with a bit more general elliptic operator and also the associated space periodic eigenvalue problem. Note that if

is a gradient field (i.e,
v=\nabla m
), then (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) reduce, respectively, to (
1.1
) with
\beta=0
and (
1.2
).

We now recall the following definition introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 1
.

(i) A function

is said to be a
first integral
of the vector field
v
if

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
v\cdot\nabla u=0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
.

(ii) Denote
\mathcal{I}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{0}
) to be the set of all first integrals of

which belong to
H^{1}(\Omega)
(resp.
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

Berestycki, Hamel and Nadirashvili derived the following important result for both (
1.3
) and (
1.4
);

[
6
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that (
1.5
) holds. The following assertions hold.

(i)
Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.3
) and assume that
v\cdot n=0
in

. Then
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
is bounded and

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\min_{u\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}{D|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}.

(ii)
Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.4
). Then
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty
if and only if
v
has no first integral in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and if
v
has a first integral in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\min_{u\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}{D|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}.

As mentioned in

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\varphi-2\alpha\nabla m(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi+V(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi&&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\

(1.6)

In the following, we shall introduce the main result in
[
9
]
. To do so, we need to recall the definitions of critical points and local maxima for the advection function
m
used in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2
.

Assume that
m\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
.

(i) A point
x\in\Omega
is called an
interior critical
point
of
m
if
|\nabla m(x)|=0
. An interior critical point
x

is called

if
\text{det}(D^{2}m(x))\neq 0
.

(ii) A point
x\in\partial\Omega
is called a
boundary critical
point
of
m
if
|\nabla m(x)|=|n(x)\cdot\nabla m(x)|
. A boundary critical point
x

is called
non-degenerate
if either
|\nabla m(x)|=0
,
\text{det}\left(D^{2}m(x)\right)\neq 0
or
|\nabla m(x)|\neq 0,\text{det}\left(D^{2}m_{\partial\Omega}(x)\right)\neq 0
. Here,
m_{\partial\Omega}(x)
is the restriction of
m(x)
on
\partial\Omega
.

(iii) A point
x\in\overline{\Omega}
is call a
local maximum

of
m
if
m(x)\geq m(y)
for every
y
in a small neighborhood of

x
, and there exists some sequence
\{r_{j}\}
of positive numbers
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}r_{j}=0
and

m(x)>\max_{y\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial B(x,r_{j})}m(y),\ \ \forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

From now on, let us denote

\Sigma=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:\ x\ \,\mbox{is a local maximum of}\ m\}.

Chen and Lou established the following important result;
see
[
9

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that all critical points of
m

are non-degenerate. Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.6
). Then

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\min_{x\in\Sigma}V(x).

We note that since all critical points of

(i) does not apply to problem (

2.
Statement of our main results

In this section, we will present the main results obtained in this paper.
Before going further, let us define the following three subsets of the local maxima set
\Sigma
of
m
:

\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma\cap\Omega,\ \ \Sigma_{2}=\{x\in\Sigma\cap\partial\Omega:\ \ \beta(x)=0\},\ \ \Sigma_{3}=\{x\in\Sigma\cap\partial\Omega:\ \ \beta(x)<0\}.

For the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
), we need the following assumption.

(A1)
: Assume that for every
x_{0}\in\Sigma_{2}
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of

x_{0}
such that
\beta(x)=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap U
.

Concerning the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) with nonnegative
\beta
, our result reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
(A1)
holds,
\beta\geq 0
and all critical points of
m
are non-degenerate. Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.1
). Then the following assertions hold.

(i)
If
\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\neq\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\min_{x\in\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}}V(x).

(ii)
If
\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}=\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty.

Remark 1
.

Regarding Theorem
1
, we would like to make some comments as follows.

Notice that if
\beta=0
on
\partial\Omega
, then
(A1)
holds automatically, and Theorem
1
reduces to Theorem
1.2
obtained by Chen and Lou.

In
[
6
, Page 478]
, Berestycki, Hamel and Nadirashvili proposed several interesting open questions, and one of them was to ask whether one can find a necessary and sufficient condition for the boundedness of the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
with respect to large
\alpha
for similar elliptic operators with Robin boundary condition. Our Theorem
1
above and Theorem
3
below make some progress towards such a question.

For the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem (
1.2
), we have the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that all critical points of
m
are
non-degenerate. Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.2
). Then the following assertions hold.

(i)
If
\Sigma_{1}\neq\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}{\lambda}(\alpha)=\min_{x\in\Sigma_{1}}V(x).

(ii)
If
\Sigma_{1}=\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}{\lambda}(\alpha)=\infty.

Remark 2
.

Regarding Theorem
2
, we want to make some comments as follows.

Under the assumption that all critical points of
m
are
non-degenerate, Theorem
2
shows that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}{\lambda}(\alpha)=\infty
if and only if
m
has no local maximum in the interior of
\Omega
; if
m
has at least one local maximum in the interior of
\Omega
, then
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}{\lambda}(\alpha)
will be determined by Theorem
2
(i). Indeed, such assertions remain true as long as
\beta>0
on
\partial\Omega
by Theorem
1
.

In dimension
N=2
, if
v
is a
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying (
1.5
) and
v\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
, then it is not hard to see that
v
has a first integral, and therefore
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}{\lambda}(\alpha)
is finite and is determined by Theorem
1.1
(ii).
Nevertheless, if
v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is a gradient field, we take
v=\nabla m
in (
1.2
)
so that (
1.5
) becomes
\Delta m=0
on
\overline{\Omega}
, and the above-mentioned condition
v\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
becomes
\frac{\partial m}{\partial n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. In such case,
m
must be a constant so that
v=0
.

In dimension
N=1
, that is,
\Omega
is an open interval, say
\Omega=(0,1)
, then
\Delta m=0
on
\overline{\Omega}
implies
m(x)=ax+b,\,\forall x\in[0,1]
for some constants

, and so

has a first integral in

if and only if
a=0
. It turns out that this result is a very special case of problem (
4.1
) to be treated in Section 4 below. As mentioned before, for any dimension

, Theorem
1.1
(i) and (ii) can allow
v
to be degenerate so that it has a first integral.

The discussion above shows that Theorem
1.1
(ii) does not cover Theorem
2
, and vice versa.

If we allow
\beta
to change its sign or be negative, generally one
can not expect
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
to be bounded from below with respect to all large
\alpha
.
In order to deal with such a case, let us denote

\partial\Omega^{-}=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\ \ \beta(x)<0\},\ \ \ \Omega_{\delta}^{-}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \ \text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega^{-})<\delta\}.

We further formulate the following assumption.

(A2)
: There exist a smooth vector field
\nu(x)

and a small constant
\delta>0
such that
\nu(x)\cdot n(x)\geq\delta

and
\nabla m(x)\cdot\nu(x)\leq 0
for all

.

For the general boundary function
\beta
, our result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
(A1)
holds and all critical points of
m
are
non-degenerate. Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.1
). Then the following assertions hold.

(i)
If
\Sigma_{3}\neq\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=-\infty.

(ii)
If
\Sigma_{3}=\emptyset
,
\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\neq\emptyset

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\min_{\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}}V(x).

(iii)
If
\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\cup\Sigma_{3}=\emptyset

(A2)
holds, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty.

Remark 3
.

Notice that
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
must be bounded from below as
\alpha\to\infty
provided

holds; see Lemma

in Section 3.
Moreover, given

is almost a necessary condition to guarantee the boundedness of

from below as
\alpha\to\infty
. One may refer to Remark

\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
must be bounded from above as

Let

. Then there exists a small constant

is smooth in

Proof of Theorem
5
.

As before, through the transformation

w(x)=e^{\alpha m(x)}\varphi(x)
, problem (
4.1
) reduces to the following one:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle-w^{\prime\prime}(x)+[\alpha^{2}(m^{\prime}(x))^{2}+\alpha m^{\prime\prime}(x)+V(x)]w(x)=\lambda_{1}(\alpha)w(x),\ 0<x<1,\\
\displaystyle(\alpha\hbar_{1}+\ell_{1})w(0)-\hbar_{1}w^{\prime}(0)=(-\alpha\hbar_{2}+\ell_{2})w(1)+\hbar_{2}w^{\prime}(1)=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.7)

For each
\alpha
, when
\hbar_{2}\not=0
,
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
has the variational characterization:

\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\alpha)&=&\displaystyle\min_{w\in H^{1}(0,1),\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}dx=1}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}[(w^{\prime}-\alpha wm^{\prime})^{2}+Vw^{2}]dx+{{\ell_{1}}\over{\hbar_{1}}}w^{2}(0)+{{\ell_{2}}\over{\hbar_{2}}}w^{2}(1)\Big\},\end{array}\right.

(4.8)

and when
\hbar_{2}=0
,
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
admits the variational characterization:

\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\alpha)&=&\displaystyle\min_{w\in H^{1}_{*}(0,1),\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}dx=1}\int_{0}^{1}[(w^{\prime}-\alpha wm^{\prime})^{2}+Vw^{2}]dx+{{\ell_{1}}\over{\hbar_{1}}}w^{2}(0),\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

where
H^{1}_{*}(0,1)=\{g\in H^{1}(0,1):\,g(1)=0\}
.

As before, we shall use
w_{\alpha}
with
\int_{0}^{1}w_{\alpha}^{2}(x)dx=1
to
denote the principal eigenfunction of (
4.7
), and assume that, as
\alpha\to\infty
,

w_{\alpha}^{2}
converges weakly to a unique Radon measure
\mu
with
\mu([0,1])=1
.

We first assume that
\ell_{2}\not=0
and
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}=\{1\}
, and claim
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty
. Obviously, the condition
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}=\{1\}
implies that the function advection
m
must be increasing on
[0,1]
, and so
m^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,1]
.

To show
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty
, we proceed indirectly and suppose that there exists a sequence of
\alpha
, labelled by itself for convenience, such that
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)\leq C

for some constant
C>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Hereafter, the positive constant
C
may change from place to place but does not depend on
\alpha\geq 0
.

As a result, it follows from (
4.3
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) that

\int_{0}^{1}[(w_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\alpha w_{\alpha}m^{\prime})^{2}+Vw_{\alpha}^{2}]dx+{{\ell_{1}}\over{\hbar_{1}}}w_{\alpha}^{2}(0)\leq C,\ \ \forall\alpha\geq 0.

(4.10)

On the other hand, from the proof of Theorem
3
(see (
3.41
)), we obtain

-{{\ell_{1}}\over{\hbar_{1}}}w_{\alpha}^{2}(0)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}[(w_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\alpha w_{\alpha}m^{\prime})^{2}dx+C,\ \ \forall\alpha\geq 0.

(4.11)

Thus, because of
\ell_{1}\hbar_{1}<0
, we infer that

-{{\ell_{1}}\over{\hbar_{1}}}w_{\alpha}^{2}(0)\leq C,\ \ \int_{0}^{1}[(w_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\alpha w_{\alpha}m^{\prime})^{2}dx\leq C,\ \ \forall\alpha\geq 0.

(4.12)

Combined with (
4.12
) and the fact that
m
is increasing on
[0,1]
, one can use the same analysis as in the proofs of
[
50
, Lemma 2.6 and Lemma 2.8]
to conclude that
\mu([0,1))=0
.
Hence,
\mu(\{1\})=1
, which, together with
\int_{0}^{1}w_{\alpha}^{2}(x)dx=1
for all
\alpha
, enables us to
conclude that

\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\|w_{\alpha}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1))}=\infty.

By virtue of this and (
4.12
), an argument similar to that of
[
50
, Lemma 2.7]
can be employed to lead to a contradiction. Therefore,
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\infty
must hold.

We then claim
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=-\infty
under the assumption
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}=\{0\}
. Clearly,

,

2

m

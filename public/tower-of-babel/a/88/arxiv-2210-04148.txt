Trace Formula of Semicommutators
Xiang Tang, Yi Wang, Dechao Zheng

Abstract
For weighted Bergman spaces on the unit disk, we give trace formulas of semicommutators of Toeplitz operators with 𝒞 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ¯ ) \mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}}) symbols. We generalize this formula to weighted Bergman spaces on the unit ball in higher dimensions. Applications and examples on the Hankel operators are also discussed. Keywords: Toeplitz operator, Hankel operator, weighted Bergman space, semi-commutator

Introduction

Commutators of Toeplitz operators have been objects of interest in the study of analytic function spaces for a long time. Various properties, such as compactness, Schatten class membership, trace formulas, were studied in a numerous amount of works (c.f.
[
1
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
20
,
25
,
31
,
33
,
38
,
39
]
). Among others, it is well-known (c.f.
[
22
,
37
]
) that for relatively nice symbols
f
and
g
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the commutator
[T_{f},T_{g}]=T_{f}T_{g}-T_{g}T_{f}
on the Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, is in the trace class, and

\mathrm{Tr}[T_{f},T_{g}]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}g.

(1.1)

This elegant formula is deeply connected to the Pincus function for a pair of noncommuting selfadjoint operators, c.f.
[
8
,
9
,
27
]
.

Our study of trace of semi-commutator is inspired by our investigation
[
30
]
of the Connes-Chern character for the Toeplitz extension (c.f.
[
14
]
). Semi-commutator of Toeplitz operators is the building block in Connes construction. On the other hand, the semi-commutator has its own importance. Let
H^{(t)}_{f}
be the Hankel operator with symbol
f
. The following equation

T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}=-H^{(t)*}_{\bar{f}}H^{(t)}_{g},

(1.2)

provides a natural link between the semi-commutators of Toeplitz operators and Hankel operators, which allows to study the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a Hankel operator by the trace of the associated semi-commutator. We aim in article to establish a generalization of the Helton-Howe trace formula (
1.1
) to semi-commutators, which has not been explored in literature.

Suppose
f
and
g
are two Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{D}
. It is well-known that for any
t>-1
, the semi-commutator
T_{f}T_{g}-T_{fg}
is in the trace class (cf.
[
38
]
). We will establish a trace formula for
T_{f}T_{g}-T_{fg}
when
f,g
are nice function. More generally, we consider all weighted Bergman spaces
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
t>-1
. To distinguish from the Bergman space, we add a superscript “
(t)
”, i.e.,
T^{(t)}_{f}
to denote the Toeplitz operator on
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
with symbol
f
. The explicit definitions are given in Section
2
. We obtain the following trace formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
t>-1
and
f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Then

\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\partial f\wedge\bar{\partial}g+\int_{\mathbb{D}^{2}}\varrho_{t}(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})\Delta f(z)\Delta g(w)\mathrm{d}m(z,w).

(1.3)

Here
\varrho_{t}
is defined as below and is strictly positive on
(0,1)
.

\varrho_{t}(s)=\frac{t+1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{s}^{1}(1-x)^{t}x^{-1}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}x,

where

F(s,x)=-\bigg[x\ln\frac{s}{x}+(1-x)\ln\frac{1-s}{1-x}\bigg].

Moreover,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\partial f\wedge\bar{\partial}g.

(1.4)

Let
H^{(t)}_{f}
be the Hankel operator with symbol
f
. By Equation (
1.2
), we apply the trace formula (
1.3
) to study the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of Hankel operators with
\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
symbols (See Corollary
6.2
and Corollary
6.3
).

Next we generalize Theorem
1.1
to higher dimensions. In general, for
n\geq 2
, semicommutators of Toeplitz operators on
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
with Lipschitz symbols only belong
to
\mathcal{S}^{p}
for
p>n
. In fact, it was shown in
[
35
]
that in the case when
f=\bar{g}
and
g
is anti-holomorphic, their semi-commutator is in the trace class only when
g=0
. To make
T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}
belong to trace class, one generally requires some further assumptions. We do not aim to give a criterion of when semi-commutators belong to the trace class. Instead, we focus on giving a trace formula for relatively nice symbols.

Recall that the Levi form
L_{z}f
of a function
f
at a point
z
is the two form

L_{z}f(\xi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}f(z)\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Define
\partial_{z}f
and
\bar{\partial}_{z}f
as the
n
-vectors that has
\partial_{i}f(z)
and
\bar{\partial}_{i}f(z)
in its
i
-th entry. Then

\langle\partial_{z}f,\overline{z-w}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(z)(z_{i}-w_{i}),\quad\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}f,z-w\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{\partial}_{j}f(w)\overline{(z_{j}-w_{j})}.

We say that
f,g
satisfy

Condition 1
if
f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}})
and there exist
C>0
,
\epsilon>0
, such that

\big|\langle\partial_{z}f,\overline{z-w}\rangle\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}g,z-w\rangle\big|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon},\quad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

(1.5)

We say that
f,g
satisfy

Condition 2
if
f,g
satisfy condition 1, and
f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}})
satisfy the following inequalities.
\forall z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}

\big|\langle\partial_{z}f,\overline{z-w}\rangle L_{w}g(z-w)\big|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{3}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon},

(1.6)

\big|L_{z}f(z-w)\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}g,z-w\rangle\big|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{3}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon},

(1.7)

\big|L_{z}f(z-w)L_{w}g(z-w)\big|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{4}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon}.

(1.8)

Here
|\varphi_{z}(w)|
is the length of the Möbius transform
\varphi_{z}(w)
, or the pseudo-hyperbolic distance of
z
and
w
. We obtain the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
t>2n-3
, and
f,g
satisfy Condition 1,
then the semicommutator
T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}
is in the trace class. If furthermore
f,g
satisfy Condition 2,
then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=

\displaystyle a_{n,t}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\partial f\wedge\bar{\partial}g\wedge\bigg[\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-|w|^{2})\bigg]^{n-1}

(1.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{B}_{n}}\rho_{n,t}(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})L_{z}f(z-w)L_{w}g(z-w)\frac{\mathrm{d}m(z,w)}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2n+2}}.

Here

a_{n,t}=\frac{-\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{n-1}s^{t}\ln s\mathrm{d}s}{\big(B(n,t+1)^{2}\big)n(2\pi i)^{n}},

and

\rho_{n,t}(s)=s^{-n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)!\Gamma^{2}(n+t+1)}{(n-k)!\Gamma(t+1+k)\Gamma(t+1)\pi^{2n}}\int_{s}^{1}F(s,x)x^{n-k-1}(1-x)^{t+k-1}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.10)

In particular,
\rho_{n,t}
is strictly positive on
(0,1)
, and

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{1-n}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\partial f\wedge\bar{\partial}g\wedge\bigg[\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-|w|^{2})\bigg]^{n-1}.

(1.11)

Remark 1.3
.

One can show that if the functions
f
and
g
are in
\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
then they satisfy Condition 1 and 2 with
n=1
. Therefore Theorem
1.2
actually implies Theorem
1.1
. Because trace formulas at dimension
1
are of independent interest, and because the proof gets significantly more complicated at higher dimensions, we first give a complete proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
3
.

Remark 1.4
.

In Lemma
5.5
we give an alternative expression of the first term in the right hand side of (
1.9
), in terms of radial derivatives
R=\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\partial_{i}
. So (
1.9
) and (
1.11
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=

\displaystyle-(2i)^{n}(n-1)!a_{n,t}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{i}g(w)-Rf(w)\bar{R}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\mathrm{d}m(w)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{B}_{n}}\rho_{n,t}(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})L_{z}f(z-w)L_{w}g(z-w)\frac{\mathrm{d}m(z,w)}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2n+2}},

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{1-n}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}\bigg)=\frac{-1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{i}g(w)-Rf(w)\bar{R}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\mathrm{d}m(w).

Remark 1.5
.

It is clear that if we have
H^{(t)}_{\bar{f}}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
and
H^{(t)}_{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}
for some
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
then the semi-commutator
T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}
belongs to the trace class. However, the converse is not true.
The point of Condition 1 and Condition 2 is to give combined conditions of
f
and
g
, instead of separate conditions. The estimates

|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\approx(1-|z|^{2})+(1-|w|^{2})+|z-w|^{2}+|\mathrm{Im}\langle z,w\rangle|,\quad|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}=\frac{|z-P_{z}(w)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(w)|^{2}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}}

give us some insight into the two conditions. In Lemmas
6.4
-
6.6
we give some special cases of Theorem
1.2
that are more intuitive and more convenient to work with.

Our proofs involve applying integration by parts formulas on the unit disk and unit ball (see Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
4.2
). These formulas essentially come from the Cauchy formula and a Bochner-Martinelli type formula we develop in Appendix I.
In Section
4
, integration by parts formulas on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
are developed. The formulas involve auxiliary functions and operations, which we define and study in Appendix II. In Section
5

1.2
. Some applications and examples are given in Section

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic definitions and properties about weighted Bergman spaces and Schatten-
p
class operators.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{n}
be the open unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
the unit sphere. Let
m
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
\sigma
be the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
. Denote
\sigma_{2n-1}=\sigma(\mathbb{S}_{n})=\frac{2\pi^{n}}{(n-1)!}
.

For
t>-1
, define the probability measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
:

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)=\frac{(n-1)!}{\pi^{n}B(n,t+1)}(1-|z|^{2})^{t}\mathrm{d}m(z).

Here
B(n,t+1)
is the Beta function. The weighted Bergman space
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
is the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\lambda_{t})
consisting of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. The reproducing kernel of
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
is

K^{(t)}_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{n+1+t}},\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, the Toeplitz operator
T^{(t)}_{f}
is the compression

T^{(t)}_{f}=P^{(t)}M^{(t)}_{f}|_{L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})},

where
P^{(t)}
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\lambda_{t})
onto
L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, and
M^{(t)}_{f}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\lambda_{t})
. The Hankel operator with symbol
f
is

H^{(t)}_{f}=(I-P^{(t)})M_{f}^{(t)}P^{(t)}.

Using the reproducing kernels, we can write
T^{(t)}_{f},H^{(t)}_{f}
as integral operators. For
h\in L_{a,t}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, we have the following expressions,

T^{(t)}_{f}h(z)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(w)h(w)K^{(t)}_{w}(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(w),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

H^{(t)}_{f}h(z)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\big(f(z)-f(w)\big)h(w)K^{(t)}_{w}(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(w),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

An important tool on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is the Möbius transform.

Definition 2.1
.

For
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
z\neq 0
, the Möbius transform
\varphi_{z}
is the biholomorphic mapping on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
defined as follows.

\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{z-P_{z}(w)-(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}Q_{z}(w)}{1-\langle w,z\rangle},\quad\forall w\in\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}.

Here
P_{z}
and
Q_{z}
denote the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto
\mathbb{C}z
and
z^{\perp}
, respectively.
Define

\varphi_{0}(w)=-w,\quad\forall w\in\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}.

It is well-known that
\varphi_{z}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
satisfying
\varphi_{z}\circ\varphi_{z}=Id
. Also, the two variable function
\rho(z,w):=|\varphi_{z}(w)|=|\varphi_{w}(z)|
defines a metric on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Moreover,
\beta(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\rho(z,w)
coincides with the Bergman metric on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

We list some lemmas that serve as basic tools for our study of Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Most of the following of this section can be found in
[
28
,
38
]
. A proof will be provided when necessary.

For non-negative values
A,B
, by
A\lesssim B
we mean that there is a constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. Sometimes, to emphasize that the constant
C
depends on some parameter
a
, we write
A\lesssim_{a}B
. The notations
\gtrsim,\gtrsim_{a},\approx,\approx_{a}
are defined similarly.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
z,w,\zeta\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

(1)

\frac{1}{1-\langle\varphi_{\zeta}(z),\varphi_{\zeta}(w)\rangle}=\frac{(1-\langle z,\zeta\rangle)(1-\langle\zeta,w\rangle)}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})(1-\langle z,w\rangle)}
.

(2)

1-|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}}
.

(3)

For any
R>0
there exists
C>1
such that whenever
\beta(z,w)<R
,

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|w|^{2}}\leq C,\quad\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{|1-\langle z,\zeta\rangle|}{|1-\langle w,\zeta\rangle|}\leq C.

(4)

The real Jacobian of
\varphi_{z}
is
\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{n+1}}{|1-\langle z,\cdot\rangle|^{2n+2}}
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{n}}{|1-\langle z,\cdot\rangle|^{2n}}
on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
.

(5)

For
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

z-\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})P_{z}(w)+(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}Q_{z}(w)}{1-\langle w,z\rangle}:=\frac{A_{z}w}{1-\langle w,z\rangle},

where
A_{z}=[a_{z}^{ij}]
is an
n\times n
matrix depending on
z
, and
w
is viewed as a column vector.

(6)

There exists
C>0
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
z\neq 0
,

|z-P_{z}(w)|\leq|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-\langle z,w\rangle|,\quad|Q_{z}(w)|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{1/2},

(2.1)

and

|z-w|\leq C|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{1/2}.

(2.2)

In contrast, if
n=1
, then
|z-w|=|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-z\bar{w}|
.

(7)

1-|z|^{2}\leq 2|1-\langle z,w\rangle|
.

Proof.

Most of the above are either well-known (cf.
[
28
,
38
]
) or straightforward to verify. The only part that requires some clarification is the second estimate in (6), i.e.,

|Q_{z}(w)|\lesssim|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{1/2}.

(2.3)

On the one hand, from the definition of
\varphi_{z}(w)
, we easily get the follow inequality,

|Q_{z}(w)|\leq|\varphi_{z}(w)|\frac{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|}{(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}}.

If
|\varphi_{z}(w)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then by (3),
|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\approx 1-|z|^{2}
. From this (
2.3
) follows.

On the other hand, since

2|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\geq 2-2\mathrm{Re}\langle z,w\rangle\geq|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\langle z,w\rangle=|z-w|^{2},

for
|\varphi_{z}(w)|>\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain the following estimates,

|Q_{z}(w)|=|Q_{z}(w-z)|\leq|z-w|\lesssim|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}<2|\varphi_{z}(w)||1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus we get (
2.3
) in both cases. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
28

The two variable function

Lemma 2.4

(Rudin-Forelli type estimates)
.

(1)

Suppose
t>-1
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+1+t+c}}\mathrm{d}m(w)\leq\begin{cases}C(1-|z|^{2})^{-c},&c>0,\\
C\ln\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}},&c=0,\\
C,&c<0,\end{cases}

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\frac{1}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+c}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)\leq\begin{cases}C(1-|z|^{2})^{-c},&c>0,\\
C\ln\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}},&c=0,\\
C,&c<0.\end{cases}

(2.5)

(2)

Suppose
t>-1
,
a,b,c>0
,
a\geq c,b\geq c
, and
a+b<n+1+t+c
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any
z,\xi\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{a}|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{b}}\mathrm{d}m(w)\leq C\frac{1}{|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|^{c}}.

(2.6)

(3)

Suppose
\phi:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
is measurable. Suppose
a>-n
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\phi(s)\lesssim s^{a}(1-s)^{b},\quad s\in(0,1).

Then for any
t>-1-b
,
c>-b
there exists
C>0
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\phi(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+1+t+c}}\mathrm{d}m(w)\leq C(1-|z|^{2})^{-c}.

(2.7)

Proof.

The estimates in (1) are standard Rudin-Forelli estimates. See
[
28
, Proposition 1.4.10]
for a proof.
Let

A=\{w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\leq|1-\langle w,\xi|\};\quad B=\{w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|1-\langle z,w\rangle|>|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|\}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have the following equality,

|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|^{1/2}\leq|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{1/2}+|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{1/2}.

Then we obtain the following bounds,

|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\geq\frac{1}{4}|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|,\forall w\in B;\quad|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|\geq\frac{1}{4}|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|,\forall w\in A.

By assumption,
a\geq c
,
b\geq c
,
a+b-c<n+1+t
, therefore by the standard Rudin-Forelli estimate, we compute the integral as follows,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{a}|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{b}}\mathrm{d}m(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{a}|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{b}}\mathrm{d}m(w)+\int_{B}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{a}|1-\langle w,\xi\rangle|^{b}}\mathrm{d}m(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

In this section we give the proof of Theorem

. The main ingredient of its proof is an integral formula coming from the Cauchy formula.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
t>-1
and
\phi:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
is measurable. Define the operations on
\phi
:

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(s)=\int_{s}^{1}\phi(r)(1-r)^{t}\mathrm{d}r,\quad\mathcal{G}^{(t)}\phi(s)=s^{-1}(1-s)^{-t-1}\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(s).

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
t>-1
, and
\phi:(0,1)\mapsto[0,\infty)
is a measurable function, and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Assume that the two integrals

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\phi(|z|^{2})v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z),\quad\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\mathcal{G}^{(t)}\phi(|z|^{2})\bar{z}\bar{\partial}v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)

both converge absolutely. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\phi(|z|^{2})v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(t+1)\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0)\cdot v(0)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\mathcal{G}^{(t)}\phi(|z|^{2})\bar{z}\bar{\partial}v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z),&v(0)\neq 0,\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0)<\infty,\\
\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\mathcal{G}^{(t)}\phi(|z|^{2})\bar{z}\bar{\partial}v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z),&v(0)=0,\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0)\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume first that
v(0)=0
.
For any
0<r<1
, denote
\sigma_{r}
the Euclidean surface measure on
r\mathbb{T}
. If we apply the Cauchy Formula to
\Omega=r\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
, then we have the following equation of integrals,

\int_{r\mathbb{T}}v(z)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{r}(z)=2r\int_{r\mathbb{D}}\frac{\bar{\partial}v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}m(z).

By assumption the left hand side of (
3.1
) is absolutely integrable, so we compute the integral as follows,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\phi(|z|^{2})v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(t+1)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(r^{2})(1-r^{2})^{t}\bigg\{\int_{r\mathbb{T}}v(z)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{r}(z)\bigg\}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(t+1)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(r^{2})(1-r^{2})^{t}\bigg\{2r\int_{r\mathbb{D}}\frac{\bar{\partial}v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}m(z)\bigg\}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(t+1)}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{|z|}^{1}2r\phi(r^{2})(1-r^{2})^{t}\mathrm{d}r\cdot\frac{\bar{\partial}v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}m(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(t+1)}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg[\int_{|z|^{2}}^{1}\phi(s)(1-s)^{t}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]\frac{\bar{\partial}v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}m(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\mathcal{G}^{(t)}\phi(|z|^{2})\bar{z}\bar{\partial}v(z)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z).

The absolute convergence of the integral in the right hand side of (
3.1
) ensures the third equality above.
This proves the second case.

Now assume that
v(0)\neq 0
, and
\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0)<\infty
. We notice that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\phi(|z|^{2})\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)=2\pi\cdot\frac{t+1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}r\phi(r^{2})(1-r^{2})^{t}\mathrm{d}r=(t+1)\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0).

Then we get the following estimate

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg|\phi(|z|^{2})\big(v(z)-v(0)\big)\bigg|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg|\phi(|z|^{2})v(z)\bigg|\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(z)+(t+1)\mathcal{F}^{(t)}\phi(0)|v(0)|<\infty.

Applying the second case to
v(z)-v(0)
and reorganizing the terms give the first case. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Recall that in Theorem

we defined

F(s,x)=-\bigg[x\ln\frac{s}{x}+(1-x)\ln\frac{1-s}{1-x}\bigg].

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
0<s<x<1
. Then

\iint_{s<s_{1}<s_{2}<x}s_{1}^{-1}(1-s_{1})^{-1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{2}=F(s,x)

Proof.

\displaystyle\iint_{s<s_{1}<s_{2}<x}s_{1}^{-1}(1-s_{1})^{-1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{x}\int_{s_{1}}^{x}s_{1}^{-1}(1-s_{1})^{-1}\mathrm{d}s_{2}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{x}\frac{x-s_{1}}{s_{1}(1-s_{1})}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{x}\frac{x}{s_{1}}-\frac{1-x}{1-s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\ln\frac{x}{s}+(1-x)\ln\frac{1-x}{1-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(s,x).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
0<\epsilon<1
there exists
C>0
such that

\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}s\leq Cx^{2},\quad 0<x<1.

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\ln\frac{s}{x}\mathrm{d}s-(1-x)\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\ln(1-s)\mathrm{d}s+(1-x)\ln(1-x)\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}s.

For
0<x<1
,
\ln(1-x)<0
. Thus the last term in the above is negative. Therefore

\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}s<-x\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\ln\frac{s}{x}\mathrm{d}s-(1-x)\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\ln(1-s)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.5)

For the first term in the right hand side of (
3.5
), take the change of variable
r=\frac{s}{x}
. Then

-x\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}\ln\frac{s}{x}\mathrm{d}s{\mathrel{\to}^{r=s/x}}-x^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-rx)^{-\epsilon}\ln r\mathrm{d}r<-x^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{-\epsilon}\ln r\mathrm{d}r\leq x^{2}.

(3.6)

For the second term in the right hand side of (
3.5
), notice that

-\ln(1-s)\lesssim s.

So

-(1-x)\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-\epsilon}s\mathrm{d}s\leq(1-x)^{1-\epsilon}\int_{0}^{x}s\mathrm{d}s\lesssim x^{2}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) gives the desired result.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{1}\int_{s_{1}}^{1}\int_{s_{2}}^{1}s_{1}^{-1}(1-s_{1})^{-1}s_{3}^{-1}(1-s_{3})^{t}\mathrm{d}s_{3}\mathrm{d}s_{2}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

Clearly the absolute value of the second term has the following bound,

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varrho_{t}(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})\mathrm{d}m(w)\mathrm{d}m(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varrho_{t}(|\zeta|^{2})\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-\zeta\bar{z}|^{4}}\mathrm{d}m(\zeta)\mathrm{d}m(z)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varrho_{t}(s)(1-s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s.

Plugging in the formula of
\varrho_{t}
and applying the Fubini’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varrho_{t}(s)(1-s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t+1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}(1-s)^{-1/2}F(s,x)x^{-1}(1-x)^{t}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t+1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-1/2}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}s\bigg]x^{-1}(1-x)^{t}\mathrm{d}x.

By Lemma

So

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varrho_{t}(s)(1-s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle\frac{t+1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\int_{0}^{x}(1-s)^{-1/2}F(s,x)\mathrm{d}s\bigg]x^{-1}(1-x)^{t}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(t+1)\int_{0}^{1}x(1-x)^{t}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+1)B(2,t+1)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle t^{-1}.

Therefore the second term in (

.

4
Integration by Parts

In the remaining sections of the article, we aim to extend Theorem
1.1
to higher dimensions and prove Theorem
1.2
. Reviewing the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we notice that there are two key ingredients:

(1)

the integral formulas in Lemma
3.3
;

(2)

the auxiliary operations
\mathcal{F}^{(t)}
,
\mathcal{G}^{(t)}
that record the change in
\phi
after each application of the formulas.

With the above tools, we obtain the trace formula in Theorem
1.1
by applying iterations of Lemma
3.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on generalizing (1) and (2). The goal of this section is to establish Lemma
4.2
, which is an analogue of Lemma
3.3
in higher dimensions. Applying iteration of Lemma
4.2
two times, we get Lemma
4.3
. In Appendix II, more general auxiliary operations
\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}
,
\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}
are defined and some basic properties are established.
In Section
5
, we apply Lemma
4.3
to obtain a formula for the semi-commutator (see Lemma
5.1
), and we apply Lemma
4.3
again with
z,w
reversed, to get the trace formula in Theorem
1.2
.

The proof of Lemma
4.2
relies on a Bochner-Martinelli type formula that we establish in Appendix I. Similar integral formulas as in Lemma

were discovered by Charpentier in
[

]
. Such integral formulas were used in
[
26
]
to study Bergman-Besov spaces and in
[
15
]
to study the corona problem on the multiplier algebra of the Drury-Arveson space.

Let us start with a few definitions.
Recall that by Lemma
2.2
(5), for

where

Definition 4.1
.

For multi-indices

In particular,

(4.1)

For multi-indices

\hskip 284.52756ptv(z)\neq 0,\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{n+|\beta|}\phi(0)<\infty,&\\

.

) converges absolutely. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\phi(|\zeta|^{2})\bigg[\sum_{(\kappa,\gamma)\in\Gamma}c_{\kappa,\gamma}\zeta^{\kappa}\bar{\zeta}^{\gamma}\bigg]v(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)!}{\pi^{n}B(n,t+1)}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\phi\phi_{t}(|\zeta|^{2})\bigg[\sum_{(\kappa,\gamma)\in\Gamma}c_{\kappa,\gamma}\zeta^{\kappa}\bar{\zeta}^{\gamma}\bigg]v(\zeta)\mathrm{d}m(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

Suppose
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
are two orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}f_{i};\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}f_{i}.

Then there is a unitary matrix
U=[u_{ij}]
such that

\xi=U\zeta,\quad\omega=U\lambda.

Denote
U^{*}=[u_{ij}^{*}]
.
Therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\bar{\zeta_{i}}}\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial\overline{\xi}_{j}}\bar{u}_{ji}\bar{\zeta}_{i}\bigg)\overline{\big(u^{*}_{ik}\omega_{k}\big)}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}\delta_{jk}\bar{w}_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}\bar{w}_{j}.

(4.8)

In other words, the function

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\partial}_{i}v\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\langle\bar{\partial}v,\lambda\rangle

does not depend on the choice of a basis.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Then

\langle\bar{\partial}_{\zeta}v(\zeta),\zeta\rangle=-\frac{\langle\bar{\partial}\big(v\circ\varphi_{z}\big)(\varphi_{z}(\zeta)),z-\varphi_{z}(\zeta)\rangle}{1-\langle z,\zeta\rangle}.

(4.9)

Proof.

By (
4.8
), both sides of (
4.9
) do not depend on the choice of basis. Thus we may assume
z=(r,0,\ldots,0)

(4.11)

Lemma 5.5
.

For

, the following are equal whenever the integrals converge.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}d_{e_{i},e_{j}}(w)\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{j}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}m(w)=

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{i}g(w)-Rf(w)\bar{R}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\mathrm{d}m(w).

By Definition
4.1
, we consider

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}d_{e_{i},e_{j}}(w)\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{j}g(w)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(A_{w}\zeta\big)_{i}\partial_{i}f(w)\bigg]\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\big(A_{w}\zeta\big)_{j}}\bar{\partial}_{j}g(w)\bigg]\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta)}{\sigma_{2n-1}}.

By an argument similar as in the proof of(
4.8
), the sum in each big bracket is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus the integrand in the left hand side of (
5.1
) does not depend on the choice of a basis. At each
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
w\neq 0
, choose a basis under which

),

\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}d_{e_{i},e_{j}}(w)\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{j}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{n}\bigg[\frac{\partial_{1}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{1}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n-1}}+\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{j}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\bigg].

(5.2)

On the other hand, we compute

\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-|w|^{2})=-\partial\bigg[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}}{1-|w|^{2}}\bigg]=-\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}w_{j}\mathrm{d}w_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2}}-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}}{1-|w|^{2}}.

Thus, we have

\bigg[\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-|w|^{2})\bigg]^{n-1}\bigg|_{w=(w_{1},0,\ldots,0)}=(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\bigg[\frac{\bigwedge_{j=2}^{n}\big(\mathrm{d}w_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}\big)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n-1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{\bigwedge_{j\neq i}\big(\mathrm{d}w_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}\big)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\bigg].

Therefore at

, we have

\displaystyle\partial f\wedge\bar{\partial}g\wedge\bigg[\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-|w|^{2})\bigg]^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!\bigg[\frac{\partial_{1}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{1}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n-1}}+\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{i}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\bigg]\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{d}w_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2i)^{n}(n-1)!\bigg[\frac{\partial_{1}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{1}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n-1}}+\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}\partial_{i}f(w)\bar{\partial}_{i}g(w)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{n}}\bigg]\mathrm{d}m(w).

(5.3)

5

Since both sides are independent of the choice of a basis the equation holds for general

\displaystyle t^{1-n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)!\Gamma^{2}(n+t+1)}{(n-k)!\Gamma(t+1+k)\Gamma(t+1)\pi^{2n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}F(s,x)x^{n-k-1}(1-x)^{t+k-1}\mathrm{d}x(1-s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{1-n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)!\Gamma^{2}(n+t+1)}{(n-k)!\Gamma(t+1+k)\Gamma(t+1)\pi^{2n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\{\int_{0}^{x}F(s,x)(1-s)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}x^{n-k-1}(1-x)^{t+k-1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{1-n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)!\Gamma^{2}(n+t+1)}{(n-k)!\Gamma(t+1+k)\Gamma(t+1)\pi^{2n}}\int_{0}^{1}x^{n-k+1}(1-x)^{t+k-1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{1-n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)!\Gamma^{2}(n+t+1)}{(n-k)!\Gamma(t+1+k)\Gamma(t+1)\pi^{2n}}B(n-k+2,t+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(t^{-1})

\displaystyle\to 0,

as
t
tends to infinity. Combining the above, (
5.5
), and (
1.9
) we obtain (
1.11
). This completes the proof.
∎

6
Applications and Examples

We start this section with some applications of Theorem
1.1
.
Since
|\varphi_{z}(w)|=|\varphi_{w}(z)|
, it follows immediately that the second term in (
1.3
) is symmetric in the symbols
f
and
g
.
As a consequence, the following trace formula for commutators of Toeplitz operators holds.

Corollary 6.1
.

Suppose
t>-1
and
f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Then

\mathrm{Tr}[T^{(t)}_{f},T^{(t)}_{g}]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}g.

(6.1)

For the case when
t=0
, this result is well-known (cf.
[
21
,
37
]
).

We can apply Theorem
1.1
to study Hankel operators. Recall that the Hankel operator with symbol
g
is defined on
L^{2}(\lambda_{t})
by

H^{(t)}_{g}=(I-P^{(t)})M_{g}P^{(t)},

where
P^{(t)}
is the Bergman projection. By the identity

T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}=-H^{(t)*}_{\bar{f}}H^{(t)}_{g},

we have

\mathrm{Tr}(T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg})=-\mathrm{Tr}(H^{(t)*}_{\bar{f}}H^{(t)}_{g})=-\langle H^{(t)}_{g},H^{(t)}_{\bar{f}}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}.

Thus (
1.3
) leads to a formula for the inner product of Hankel operators in the Hilbert-Schmidt class. In particular, it leads to a formula for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of Hankel operators.

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose
t>-1
and
g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Then

\|H^{(t)}_{g}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\bar{\partial}g|^{2}\mathrm{d}m-\int_{\mathbb{D}^{2}}\varrho_{t}(|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2})\Delta\bar{g}(z)\Delta g(w)\mathrm{d}m(z,w).

where
\varrho_{t}
is defined as in Theorem
1.1
. In particular,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|H^{(t)}_{g}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\bar{\partial}g|^{2}\mathrm{d}m.

For Hankel operators with real subharmonic symbols, the second term is non-negative. So the following holds.

Corollary 6.3
.

Suppose
t>-1
and
g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is real-valued and subharmonic in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\|H^{(t)}_{g}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\bar{\partial}g|^{2}\mathrm{d}m,

(6.2)

with equality holds if and only if
g
is harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
.

As explained in the introduction, in this paper we focus more on the trace formula (
1.9
) and asymptotic trace formula (
1.11
) of semi-commutators with relatively nice symbols. Nonetheless, the following lemma and the examples that follow show that Condition 1 is a natural condition to work with.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
n\geq 2
and
f,g
satisfy Condition 1. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
,

\big|\langle\partial_{z}f,\bar{\zeta}\rangle\langle\bar{\partial}_{z}g,\zeta\rangle\big|\leq C\bigg(|P_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{n-2+\epsilon},\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(6.3)

(1)

In the special case when
f=\bar{g}
, (
6.3
) becomes

\big|\langle\bar{\partial}_{z}g,\zeta\rangle\big|\leq C_{1}\bigg(|P_{z}(\zeta)|+(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}|Q_{z}(\zeta)|\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{n-2+\epsilon}{2}},

(6.4)

which is equivalent to Condition 1. Here
C_{1}
is another constant.

(2)

If there are
a,b\geq 0
,
a+b>n-2
such that

\big|\langle\partial_{z}f,\bar{\zeta}\rangle\big|\leq C_{2}\bigg(|P_{z}(\zeta)|+(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}|Q_{z}(\zeta)|\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{a},

(6.5)

\big|\langle\bar{\partial}_{z}g,\zeta\rangle\big|\leq C_{2}\bigg(|P_{z}(\zeta)|+(1-|z|^{2})^{1/2}|Q_{z}(\zeta)|\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{b}

(6.6)

with some constant
C_{2}
, then
f,g
satisfy Condition 1.

Proof.

Note that

|1-\langle z,w\rangle|\approx(1-|z|^{2})+(1-|w|^{2})+|z-w|^{2}+|\mathrm{Im}\langle z,w\rangle|,\ |\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}=\frac{|z-P_{z}(w)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(w)|^{2}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}}.

(6.7)

Take
w=z+\lambda\zeta
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is sufficiently small. Then by definition,

|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}=|\lambda|^{2}\frac{|P_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}}.

Condition 1 implies

\big|\langle\partial_{z}f,\bar{\zeta}\rangle\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}g,\zeta\rangle\big||\lambda|^{2}\leq C|\lambda|^{2}\frac{|P_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon}.

Canceling out
|\lambda|^{2}
and letting
\lambda\to 0
we obtain the first inequality in (
6.3
). The second inequality is proved similarly.

On the other hand, suppose
f=\bar{g}
and (
6.4
) holds. Then

\displaystyle\big|\langle\bar{\partial}_{z}g,z-w\rangle\big|^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\bigg(|z-P_{z}(w)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(w)|^{2}\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{n-2+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\bigg(|z-P_{z}(w)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(w)|^{2}\bigg)|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n-2+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon}.

Equivalently,

\big|\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}g,z-w\rangle\big|^{2}\lesssim|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon}

and also

\big|\langle\partial_{z}\bar{g},\overline{z-w}\rangle\big|^{2}\lesssim|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{n+\epsilon}.

Multiplying the two inequalities and taking square root gives Condition 1 for
f=\bar{g}
. This proves (1). Statement (2) is proved in the same way as (1). We omit the details.
∎

Similarly, one may give sufficient conditions for Condition 2 in terms of growth rates of second order derivatives. Taking the case
f=\bar{g}
for example, we have the following.

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose
n\geq 2
and
g\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. If

satisfies (

) and for some constant
C>0
and

|L_{z}g(\zeta)|\leq C\bigg(|P_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}+(1-|z|^{2})|Q_{z}(\zeta)|^{2}\bigg)(1-|z|^{2})^{a},

then
f=\bar{g}
and
g
satisfy Condition 1 and 2.

The Schatten class criterion of Hankel operators is throughly studied, c.f.
[
3
,
2
,
24
,
34
,
36
]
. There are also some results on the Schatten norms of Hankel operator with anti-holomorphic symbols, c.f.
[

]
[

is consistent with that of

in Li and Luecking’s Theorem 3.1 when

Lemma 6.6

Suppose

Proof.

By (

is bounded away from

for

∎

In the case when
\mathrm{supp}f
and

do meet on the boundary, Condition 1 gives us an idea of how much decay is needed when they meet. See the following example.

Example 6.7
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\psi
be a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
function on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\psi^{\prime}(s)=0\text{ for }s<0,\text{ and }|\psi^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim s^{1+\epsilon}\text{ for }s\geq 0.

Let
n=2
and

f(z)=\psi(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}),\quad g(z)=\psi(|z_{2}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{2}).

Then we compute

|\partial_{z}f|\begin{cases}=0,&\text{if }|z_{1}|<|z_{2}|\\
\lesssim(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2})^{1+\epsilon},&\text{if }|z_{1}|\geq|z_{2}|\end{cases},\quad|\bar{\partial}_{w}g|\begin{cases}=0,&\text{if }|w_{2}|<|w_{1}|\\
\lesssim(|w_{2}|^{2}-|w_{1}|^{2})^{1+\epsilon},&\text{if }|w_{2}|\geq|w_{1}|\end{cases}

Whenever

|z-w|\approx|z_{1}-w_{1}|+|z_{2}-w_{2}|\geq|z_{1}|-|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|-|z_{2}|=(|z_{1}|-|z_{2}|)+(|w_{2}|-|w_{1}|).

So we have the following estimate

|\partial_{z}f||\bar{\partial}_{w}g|\lesssim|z-w|^{2+2\epsilon}.

Using the above inequality, we reach the following estimate,

|\langle\partial_{z}f,\overline{z-w}\rangle||\langle\bar{\partial}_{w}g,z-w\rangle|\lesssim|z-w|^{2}|\partial_{z}f||\bar{\partial}_{w}g|\lesssim|z-w|^{4+2\epsilon}\lesssim|\varphi_{z}(w)|^{2}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2+\epsilon}.

So Condition 1 is satisfied and by Theorem

,
T^{(t)}_{f}T^{(t)}_{g}-T^{(t)}_{fg}
is in the trace class.

Appendix I: A Formula of Bochner-Martinelli Type

Recall that
R=\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}
is the radial derivative operator, and
\bar{R}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{z}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{z_{i}}
. In this appendix we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose
r>0
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
|\alpha|\geq|\beta|
and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{r\mathbb{B}_{n}})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{r\mathbb{S}_{n}}z^{\alpha}\bar{z}^{\beta}v(z)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{r}(z)

(7.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\alpha,\beta}\sigma_{2n-1}r^{2|\beta|+2n-1}v(0)+2r^{2|\beta|+2n-1}\int_{r\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{z^{\alpha}\bar{z}^{\beta}}{|z|^{2|\beta|+2n}}\bar{R}v(z)\mathrm{d}m(z).

Here

a_{\alpha,\beta}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{(n-1)!\alpha!}{(n-1+|\alpha|)!}.

Lemma
7.1
can be verified directly on
v(z)=z^{\gamma}\bar{z}^{\iota}
, and then using approximation of
v
by polynomials. For future reference, we show in the rest of this appendix that it can be viewed as a special case of a Bochner-Martinelli type formula (see Proposition
7.5
below).

For a
(p,q)
-form
u=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}u_{I,J}\mathrm{d}z_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{J}
,

\partial u=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\partial_{k}u_{I,J}\mathrm{d}z_{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{J},\quad\bar{\partial}u=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\bar{\partial}_{k}u_{I,J}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{J}.

Then
\mathrm{d}=\partial+\bar{\partial}
is the exterior derivative.

In some of the estimates, we may abuse notations and use
\partial f
,
\bar{\partial}f
to denote holomorphic, and anti-holomorphic gradient of a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
function
f
, i.e.,

\partial f(z)=(\partial_{1}f(z),\partial_{2}f(z),\ldots,\partial_{n}f(z)),\quad\bar{\partial}f(z)=(\bar{\partial}_{1}f(z),\bar{\partial}_{2}f(z),\ldots,\bar{\partial}_{n}f(z)),

considered as column vectors.

Cauchy Formula.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded open set with
\mathscr{C}^{1}
boundary and
0\in\Omega
. Then for every
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}z=v(0)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\bar{\partial}v(z)}{z}\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}.

Its generalization to higher dimensions is the Bochner-Martinelli Formula.

Bochner-Martinelli Formula.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
\mathscr{C}^{1}
boundary and
0\in\Omega
. Then for every
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}})
,

\int_{\partial\Omega}v(z)k_{BM}(z)=v(0)+\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}v(z)\wedge k_{BM}(z).

(7.2)

Here
k_{BM}
is the Bochner-Martinelli kernel, defined by

k_{BM}(z)=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(2\pi i)^{n}}|z|^{-2n}\partial|z|^{2}\wedge\big(\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\big)^{n-1}.

(7.3)

In this section, we prove a generalization of the above Bochner-Martinelli Formula (
7.2
). First, we review the definition of currents on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Definition 7.2
.

For
p,q=0,\ldots,n
, denote
\mathscr{D}^{p,q}
the locally convex space of smooth
(p,q)
-forms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with compact support. The topology of
\mathscr{D}^{p,q}
is defined by the collection of semi-norms

\|u\|_{\mathscr{D}^{p,q}(K),N}:=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\sup_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\sup_{z\in K}|\partial^{\alpha}\bar{\partial}^{\beta}u_{I,J}(z)|,\quad u=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}u_{I,J}(z)\mathrm{d}z_{I}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{J},

where
N
ranges over all positive integers, and
K
is any compact subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The space of currents of bidegree
(p,q)
, denoted by
{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p,q}
, is the dual space of
\mathscr{D}^{n-p,n-q}
, endowed with the weak* topology. The currents in
{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p,q}
can be viewed as
(p,q)
-forms with distribution coefficients. In particular, any
(p,q)
-form with locally integrable coefficients is a current of bidegree
(p,q)
. With the identification of Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{d}m(z)
with the Euclidean volume form

\mathrm{d}v:=\frac{1}{(-2i)^{n}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{n},

a distribution
T
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
can be viewed as either a current of bidegree
(0,0)
or
(n,n)
: for
h
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
smooth and compactly supported,

\langle T,h\rangle=\langle T,h\mathrm{d}v\rangle.

Differential operators acts on currents by duality and are continuous with respect to the weak* topology.

Definition 7.3
.

For multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, define

H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\bigg(\frac{z^{\alpha}\bar{z}^{\beta}}{|z|^{2|\beta|}}k_{BM}(z)\bigg)\bigg|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\epsilon\mathbb{B}_{n}},

(7.4)

in the sense of current.

In the case when
|\beta|\leq|\alpha|
,
H_{\alpha,\beta}
has locally integrable distributions. So we can simply write

H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{z^{\alpha}\bar{z}^{\beta}}{|z|^{2|\beta|}}k_{BM}(z).

Only this case will be used in this paper. For completeness and future reference, we include the case when
|\beta|>|\alpha|
. In this case, the current
H_{\alpha,\beta}
has coefficient distributions which are not locally integrable, and we need to define it in the style of a principal value. For any
1\leq j\leq n
and
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
, compactly supported function
h
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, take the Taylor expansion

h(z)=\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|\leq|\beta|-|\alpha|}\frac{\partial^{\gamma_{1}}\bar{\partial}^{\gamma_{2}}h(0)}{\gamma_{1}!\gamma_{2}!}z^{\gamma_{1}}\bar{z}^{\gamma_{2}}+O(|z|^{|\beta|-|\alpha|+1}).

For each
\epsilon>0
, the current inside the limit sign of (
7.4
) vanishes on every term except for
O(|z|^{|\beta|-|\alpha|+1})
. Thus the current
H_{\alpha,\beta}
for
|\beta|>|\alpha|
is well-defined.

The standard Bochner-Martinelli formula follows from Stoke’s Theorem and the following identity.

\bar{\partial}k_{BM}=\delta_{0},

(7.5)

where
\delta_{0}
is the point mass at
0
. Standard arguments show that the following holds.

Lemma 7.4
.

We have

\bar{\partial}H_{\alpha,\beta}=(-1)^{l-k}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}\delta_{0}.

(7.6)

Here

a_{\alpha,\beta}=\begin{cases}\frac{(n-1)!\alpha!}{(n-1+|\beta|)!},&\text{if }\alpha\leq\beta,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
\mathscr{C}^{1}
boundary and
0\in\Omega
. Then for multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,

\int_{\partial\Omega}v(z)H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}v(0)+\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}v(z)\wedge H_{\alpha,\beta}(z).

(7.7)

Proof of Proposition
7.5
.

By Lemma
7.4
and Stokes’ Theorem for currents, we have the following equations

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}v(z)H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\bigg(v(z)H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\bigg)=\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}\bigg(v(z)H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v(z)\bar{\partial}H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)+\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}v(z)\wedge H_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}v(0)+\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}v(z)\wedge H_{\alpha,\beta}(z).

This completes the proof of Proposition
7.5
.
∎

Taking
\Omega=r\mathbb{B}_{n}
and assuming
|\alpha|\geq|\beta|
in Proposition
7.5
gives Lemma
7.1
.

In Section

, the integral operations
\mathcal{F}^{(t)},\mathcal{G}^{(t)}

Definition 8.1
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, denote

\phi_{t}(s)=(1-s)^{t}.

Suppose
\phi:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
is a measurable function. For a positive integer
m

\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(s)=\frac{1}{s^{m}\phi_{t+1}(s)}\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(s)=\frac{\int_{s}^{1}r^{m-1}\phi(r)(1-r)^{t}\mathrm{d}r}{s^{m}(1-s)^{t+1}}\in[0,\infty].

(8.2)

For any
t>-1
, inductively define the functions

\Phi_{n,0}^{(t)}\equiv 1,\quad\Phi_{n,k+1}^{(t)}=M_{\phi_{1}}\big(\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{n+k}\big)^{2}\Phi_{n,k}^{(t)}.

Equivalently,

\Phi_{n,k}^{(t)}=M_{\phi_{1}}\big(\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{n+k-1}\big)^{2}\ldots M_{\phi_{1}}\big(\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{n}\big)^{2}1.

(8.3)

It is straightforward to verify that the following estimates hold.

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose
a>m
is not an integer and
b\geq 0
. Suppose
\phi:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
is measurable and

\phi(s)\lesssim s^{-a}(1-s)^{b}.

Then

\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(s)\lesssim s^{-a}(1-s)^{b}.

As a consequence, the following hold.

Lemma 8.3
.

For any
t>-1
and integers
n>0,k\geq 0
,

\Phi_{n,k}^{(t)}(s)\lesssim s^{-n-k+\frac{1}{2}}(1-s)^{k},

(8.4)

and

\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{n+k}\Phi^{(t)}_{n,k}(s)\lesssim s^{-n-k-\frac{1}{2}}(1-s)^{k}.

(8.5)

Lemma 8.4
.

For any
t>-1
and positive integers
m,k
and
\phi:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
we have

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}M_{\phi_{1}}\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(0)=\frac{1}{k}\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}\phi(0),

(8.6)

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(0)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+j}\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k+j}\phi(0),

(8.7)

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}\phi=\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}M_{\phi_{1}}\phi(0)+\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+1}\phi,

(8.8)

and

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}1(0)=B(m,t+1).

(8.9)

Proof.

The proof is a simple application of Fubini’s Theorem. By definition, we have the following computation for
\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}M_{\phi_{1}}\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(0)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}M_{\phi_{1}}\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{m+k-1}(1-r)^{t+1}\mathcal{G}^{(t)}_{m}\phi(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{k-1}\int_{r}^{1}s^{m-1}(1-s)^{t}\phi(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}r^{k-1}\mathrm{d}rs^{m-1}(1-s)^{t}\phi(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{1}s^{m+k-1}(1-s)^{t}\phi(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m+k}\phi(0).

This proves (
8.6
). We prove (
8.7
) by Lemma
8.3
using the expansion

\frac{1}{1-s}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}s^{j}.

Finally, we arrive at the following equation,

\mathcal{F}^{(t)}_{m}1(0)=\int_{0}^{1}r^{m-1}(1-r)^{t}\mathrm{d}r=B(m,t+1).

This completes the proof of Lemma
8.4
.
∎

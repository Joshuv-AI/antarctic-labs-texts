Towers and elementary embeddings in toral relatively hyperbolic groups
Christopher Perez

Abstract
In a remarkable series of papers, Zlil Sela classified the first-order theories of free groups and torsion-free hyperbolic groups using geometric structures he called towers. It was later proved by Chloé Perin that if H H is an elementarily embedded subgroup (or elementary submodel) of a torsion-free hyperbolic group G G , then G G is a tower over H H . We prove a generalization of Perin’s result to toral relatively hyperbolic groups using JSJ and shortening techniques.

Introduction

Tarski’s problem concerns the
elementary theory

\operatorname{Th}(G)
of a group
G
, the set of all first-order sentences in the language of groups which are valid over
G
. The problem asks if all finitely generated, non-abelian free groups have the same elementary theory. This question was answered in the affirmative in 2006 by Zlil Sela
[
11
]
, and independently by Olga Kharlampovich and Alexei Myasnikov
[
9
]
. Sela went on to generalize the techniques he used to solve Tarski’s problem and prove the following:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 7.10]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free hyperbolic group, and let
G
be a finitely generated group. If
\operatorname{Th}(G)=\operatorname{Th}(\Gamma)
, then
G
is a hyperbolic group.

This result was later generalized by Simon André to apply to hyperbolic groups with torsion, as well, making hyperbolicity a first-order invariant among finitely generated groups
[
1
]
.

A concept closely related to elementary equivalence is that of elementary embeddings: Let
H
be a subgroup of a group
G
. The inclusion of
H
into
G
is an
elementary embedding
, denoted
H\hookrightarrow_{\operatorname{Th}}G
, if for any first-order formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and any
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in H^{n}
with
n\geq 0
,

H\models\varphi(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\iff G\models\varphi(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}),

where
\varphi
is a first-order sentence when
n=0
and
G\models\varphi(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
denotes that
G

models

\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with the assignment
x_{i}=g_{i}
, i.e.,
\varphi(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is true over
G
. In particular,
\operatorname{Th}(H)=\operatorname{Th}(G)
if
H
is elementarily embedded.

Building upon the work of Sela and others, Chloé Perin proved the following:

Theorem 1.2

If

In this paper we prove a generalization of this result to

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G=\langle S\rangle
be a finitely generated group (with

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
be a collection of finitely generated subgroups of

V(\widetilde{X})=G\cup\{c_{gP}:gP\text{ is a coset with }g\in G,P\in\mathcal{P}\},

Definition 1.4
.

A finitely generated group

is

if its Cayley graph is
\delta
-hyperbolic.

A finitely generated group

hyperbolic relative to

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}

A

Definition 1.8

There are four classes of surfaces

Lemma 1.11

([Compare
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
A
and
G
be finitely generated torsion-free groups acting on trees
T_{A}
and
T_{G}
, respectively, with abelian edge stabilizers. Assume that both trees are bipartite with verties of types 0 and 1, and that the actions are 1-acylindrical near vertices of type 1.

Let
f:A\to G
be a homomorphism such that each type 0 vertex stabilizer subgroup of
A
maps injectively into a type 0 vertex stabilizer of
G
, and that each type 1 vertex stabilizer of
A
maps bijectively to a type 1 vertex stabilizer of
G
. If
f
is not injective, then there exist two non-conjugate type 1 vertex stabilizer subgroups of
A
with the same image under
f
.

Proof.

The 1-acylindricity condition ensures that edges which meet at a type 1 vertex are malnormal, so because edge stabilizers are abelian we have that type 1 vertex stabilizers are non-abelian and fix a unique type 1 vertex. Type 0 vertex stabilizers similarly fix unique type 0 vertices. Given a vertex
v\in V(T_{A})
, let
\varphi(v)\in V(T_{G})
be the unique vertex which is of the same type as
v
and is fixed by
f(A_{v})\leq G_{\varphi(v)}
. By 1-acylindricity
\varphi
preserves adjacency, so we can extend to a map
\varphi:T_{A}\to T_{G}
which maps edges to edges. Because
f
is non-injective but is injective on vertex stabilizers, there exist distinct edges
e=(u,v),e^{\prime}=(u^{\prime},v)\in E(T_{A})
such that
\varphi(u)=\varphi(u^{\prime})
and
\varphi(e)=\varphi(e^{\prime})
. In particular, we must have that
v
is type 0 and both
u
and
u^{\prime}
are type 1 by 1-acylindricity.

If
u
and
u^{\prime}
lie in different orbits then the result follows immediately, so suppose that there exists
a\in A
such that
u^{\prime}=a\cdot u
. Then
f(a)\cdot\varphi(u)=\varphi(a\cdot u)=\varphi(u^{\prime})=\varphi(u)
, so
f(a)\in G_{\varphi(u)}=f(A_{u})
and hence there exists
b\in A_{u}
such that
f(a)=f(b)
. Let
c=ab^{-1}
. Then
c\cdot u=a\cdot(b^{-1}\cdot u)=a\cdot u=u^{\prime}
and
c\in\ker(f)
. In particular
c\cdot v\neq v
because
f
is injective on vertex stabilizers. Let
d\in A_{e}
and let
d^{\prime}\in A_{e^{\prime}}\leq A_{u^{\prime}}
be nontrivial. Then
cdc^{-1}\in A_{c\cdot e}\leq A_{u^{\prime}}
, and by 1-acylindricity near
u^{\prime}
we have that
D:=\langle cdc^{-1},d^{\prime}\rangle
is non-abelian. But then we must have that
f(D)=\langle f(cdc^{-1}),f(d^{\prime})\rangle=\langle f(d),f(d^{\prime})\rangle
is abelian because
f(D)
is a subgroup of the abelian group
G_{\varphi(e)}
, which contradicts the injectivity of
f
on
A_{u^{\prime}}
.
∎

Proposition 1.26

[

Let

First-order logic

For
n\geq 0
, we will use the notations
\underline{x}
or
(\underline{x})
to denote finite
ordered
tuples
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. A sequence of elements
x_{n}\in X
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
will be denoted
(x_{n})_{n}
.

In first-order formulas, we will use the notations
\exists\underline{x}
and
\forall\underline{x}
to denote
\exists x_{1}\exists x_{2}\ldots\exists x_{n}
and
\forall x_{1}\forall x_{2}\ldots\forall x_{n}
, respectively.

Given first-order formulas
\varphi_{1}(\underline{x}),\ldots,\varphi_{m}(\underline{x})
we will use
\underline{\varphi}(\underline{x})
to denote
\varphi_{1}(\underline{x})\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi_{m}(\underline{x})
, and similarly given words
\sigma_{1}(\underline{x}),\ldots,\sigma_{m}(\underline{x})\in\mathbb{F}(\underline{x}):=\langle\underline{x}\rangle
we will use
\underline{\sigma}(\underline{x})=1
to denote
(\sigma_{1}(\underline{x})=1)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\sigma_{m}(\underline{x})=1)
.

More generally, if
\Sigma=\Sigma(\underline{x})\subseteq\mathbb{F}=\langle\underline{x}\rangle
is a (possibly infinite) set of words in the variables
\underline{x}
, we denote

\Sigma(\underline{x})=1\iff\sigma(\underline{x})=1\text{ for all }\sigma\in\Sigma

for a system of equations, and similarly

\Sigma(\underline{x})\neq 1\iff\sigma(\underline{x})\neq 1\text{ for all }\sigma\in\Sigma

for a system of inequations.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
G=\langle\underline{s}\mid\underline{\sigma}(\underline{s})=1\rangle
is a finitely presented group, then
G\models\exists\underline{x}\ (\underline{\sigma}(\underline{x})=1)
as witnessed by
\underline{x}=\underline{s}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group. The
elementary theory
of
G
is the set of first-order sentences in the language of groups modeled by
G
,

\operatorname{Th}(G):=\{\varphi:G\models\varphi\}.

Two groups
G
and
H
are said to be
elementarily equivalent
if
\operatorname{Th}(G)=\operatorname{Th}(H)
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a group
G
. The inclusion of
H
into
G
is an
elementary embedding
, denoted
H\hookrightarrow_{\operatorname{Th}}G
, if for any first-order formula
\varphi(\underline{x})
and any
\underline{h}\in H
,

H\models\varphi(\underline{h})\iff G\models\varphi(\underline{h}).

In particular,
\operatorname{Th}(H)=\operatorname{Th}(G)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A group
G
is
commutative-transitive
if for all
x,y,z\in G\setminus\{1\}
we have

[x,y]=[y,z]=1\implies[x,z]=1.

A subgroup
H
of a group
G
is
malnormal
if
gHg^{-1}\cap H=\{1\}
for all
g\in G\setminus H
. A group
G
is
CSA
if every maximal abelian subgroup of
G
is malnormal.

Lemma 2.4
.

CSA groups are commutative-transitive.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in G\setminus\{1\}
and suppose that
[x,y]=[y,z]=1
. Let
M
be the maximal abelian subgroup of
G
containing both
x
and
y
. If
z\notin M
then
y=zyz^{-1}\in zMz^{-1}\cap M=\{1\}
, which contradicts the assumption that
y\neq 1
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Because CSA groups are commutative-transitive, the centralizer
Z_{G}(g)
of an element
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
is the maximal abelian subgroup of
G
containing
g
.

Lemma 2.6
.

A group
G
is commutative-transitive if and only if

G\models\forall x,y,z\ ((y\neq 1)\wedge([x,y]=1)\wedge([y,z]=1))\implies([x,z]=1).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a commutative-transitive group, let
H\hookrightarrow_{\operatorname{Th}}G
be an elementarily embedded subgroup, and let
h\in H\setminus\{1\}
.

(1)

Z_{H}(h)\leq Z_{G}(h)
.

(2)

If
G
is torsion-free and
[Z_{G}(h):Z_{H}(h)]<\infty
, then
Z_{H}(h)=Z_{G}(h)
.

Proof.

(1) is immediate from the definition of centralizers, so to prove (2) suppose that
Z_{H}(h)\lneq Z_{G}(h)
. Let
z\in Z_{G}(h)\setminus Z_{H}(h)
and let
n>1
be the minimal integer such that
z^{n}\in Z_{H}(h)
. Then

G\models\exists x\ (x^{n}=z^{n})\wedge([x,h]=1)\wedge([x,z]=1),

so

H\models\exists x\ (x^{n}=z^{n})\wedge([x,h]=1)\wedge([x,z]=1)

and hence there exists
y\in Z_{H}(h)
such that
y^{n}=z^{n}
and
[y,z]=1
. Then
1=y^{n}z^{-n}=(yz^{-1})^{n}
, so because
G
is torsion-free we must have
yz^{-1}=1
and
z=y\in Z_{H}(h)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that

Note that the centralizer

\mathbb{Z}^{r}\models\exists t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}\forall x\exists y\ \bigvee_{(\epsilon_{i})\in\{0,1\}^{s}}x=y^{2}t_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots t_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}}

. This follows because the formula states that

Then we may define the formula

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{r}(g)\ :\

\displaystyle\exists t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\forall x\ \left(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq r}[g,t_{i}]=1\right)

\displaystyle\wedge\left(([g,x]=1)\implies\left(\exists y\ ([g,y]=1)\wedge\left(\bigvee_{(\epsilon_{i})\in\{0,1\}^{r}}x=y^{2}t_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{\epsilon_{r}}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle:\ \widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(g)\wedge(g\neq 1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{r}(g)\

\displaystyle:\ \neg\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{r-1}(g)\wedge\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{r}(g)\wedge(g\neq 1)

4
Modular automorphisms, bending, and shortening

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with a splitting
\Lambda
over abelian edge groups. If

is an abelian vertex subgroup of

in

be the subgroup generated by the incident edge stabilizers in

. The

is the minimal direct factor of

containing

. We say that an abelian vertex subgroup

is

\overline{I}(Z)=Z
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. A

is an automorphism of one of the following types:

•

If

and
c\in Z(C)
, then define
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
such that
\sigma(a)=a
for

and
\sigma(b)=cbc^{-1}
for

.

•

If

with stable letter

and
c\in Z(C)
, then define
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
such that
\sigma(a)=a
for
a\in A
and
\sigma(t)=tc
.

If
G
has a splitting
\Lambda
over abelian groups and

is an abelian vertex subgroup of

in
\Lambda
, a
generalized Dehn twist
is an automorphism
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
which restricts to the identity on
\overline{I}(Z)
and all other vertex groups.

The

of

is the subgroup
\operatorname{Mod}(G)
of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
generated by Dehn twists, generalized Dehn twists, and inner automorphisms. If

is a subgroup of

of

Definition 4.3
.

Let

be a finitely generated group which is freely indecomposable relative to a subgroup

, and let

. Two morphisms

over an abelian subgroup

(ii)

Remark 4.4
.

These are the type (B2) bending moves of

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a toral relatively hyperbolic group which is freely indecomposable relative to a non-abelian torally complete subgroup

, and let

be the JSJ splitting of

Cyclic vertex groups are trivially incidental because edge groups are nontrivial, so let

be a non-cyclic abelian vertex subgroup of

, and let

is non-abelian and

is conjugate into

is torally complete in

. Choose lifts

∎

.

Let

Theorem 4.7

(Shortening Argument)
.

Let

be a finitely generated group (with

finite) which is freely indecomposable relative to a non-abelian subgroup

, and let
\Gamma
be a toral relatively hyperbolic group with a fixed embedding

such that

is torally complete in

.

Suppose that
(f_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of distinct morphisms in
\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Gamma)
fixing

in the limit. If
(f_{n})_{n}
converges to a faithful isometric

-action on an
\mathbb{R}
-tree

, then all but finitely many

are not short.

Proof.

We will use the Rips machine to analyze the action of

on

. Because

is fixed in the limit,

fixes the basepoint point

by Theorem

, and further we can shorten using elements of

and bending moves. Because

is freely indecomposable relative to

for

as in

, Theorem 3.7]
.
∎

Definition 5.1

. Furthermore, no
j_{n}
will admit non-trivial bending moves by Corollary
5.8
. The sequence will converge to a faithful isometric
G
-action on an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
, so not all
j_{n}
could have been short by
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
.
∎

5.2
Not the co-Hopf property

Definition 5.13
.

A group
G
is
co-Hopf
(or has the co-Hopf property) if any injective morphism
G\hookrightarrow G
is an isomorphism. Similarly, if
H
is a subgroup of
G
, then
G
is
co-Hopf relative to
H
if any injective morphism
G\hookrightarrow G
which restricts to the identity on
H
is an isomorphism.

Remark 5.14
.

If
G
has a splitting
\Lambda
over abelian groups which contains a non-incidental abelian vertex subgroup, then
G
is not co-Hopf: Suppose
Z=\overline{I}(Z)\times B
is a non-incidental abelian vertex subgroup. We can define a monomorphism
G\hookrightarrow G
which is not an epimorphism by mapping
B
into a proper finite-index subgroup of itself and restricting to the identity map elsewhere.

Unfortunately we were unable to prove the more general relative co-Hopf property we had hoped for. The co-Hopf property for torsion-free hyperbolic groups was used in
[
10
]
to rule out the existence of injective preretractions, but we found a workaround using the property
*_{H,A,G}
. We do not know if there exist injective morphisms
A\hookrightarrow G
which fix
H
but do not satisfy
*_{H,A,G}
, but it is clear that the usual embedding
i:A\hookrightarrow G
satisfies
*_{H,A,G}
, and hence so would
i\circ\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\operatorname{Mod}_{H}(G)
. Then any morphism which is
\Lambda
-related to
i
also satisfies
*_{H,A,G}
, and hence preretractions satisfy
*_{H,A,G}
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
G
be a toral relatively hyperbolic group, let
H
be a non-abelian, torally complete subgroup of
G
, and let
A
be a toral relatively hyperbolic retract of
G
which contains and is freely indecomposable relative to
H
. There exists a finite set
H_{0}\subseteq H
such that any embedding
A\hookrightarrow G
satisfying
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,G}
fixes
H
.

Proof.

Suppose that no such subset
H_{0}
exists and let
S
be the finite set consisting of generators from representatives of each of the conjugacy classes of non-cyclic abelian subgroups of
H
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
(r_{n})_{n}
such that
r_{n}\to\infty
and a sequence
(i_{n})_{n}
of distinct embeddings in
\operatorname{Hom}(A,G)
such that each
i_{n}
fixes the finite subset
B_{n}:=(B_{A}(r_{n})\cup S)\cap H
but not
H
and satisfies
*_{\langle B_{n}\rangle,A,G}
, and such that
\langle B_{n}\rangle
is torally complete in
G
. Then
H
is fixed in the limit, and furthermore we may assume each
i_{n}
is short relative to
\langle B_{n}\rangle
because any morphism
f^{\prime}\sim i_{n}
is also an embedding. Then
\underrightarrow{\ker}\ (i_{n})=\{1\}
is trivial, so this sequence converges to a faithful action on an
\mathbb{R}
-tree (possibly after passing to a subsequence) by Theorem
3.6
, so by Theorem
4.7
not all
i_{n}
could have been short, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
H
be a proper torally complete subgroup of a toral relatively hyperbolic group
A
which is freely indecomposable relative to
H
, and let
\Lambda
be the JSJ splitting of
A
relative to
H
. Then there exists a finite subset
H_{0}\subseteq H
and a finite set
K_{0}\subseteq A\setminus\{1\}
such that for any morphism
f:A\to H
which fixes
H_{0}
and satisfies
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,H}
, there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Mod}_{H}(A)
and
k\in K_{0}
such that
f\circ\sigma(k)=1
.

Proof.

Choose
H_{0}
to satisfy the conditions of Proposition
5.10
and Lemma
5.15
for morphisms
A\to A
satisfying
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,A}
and choose
K_{0}
to consist of a single non-trivial element from the kernel of each of the proper quotients of Proposition
5.10
. If
f
is non-injective the result follows immediately by Proposition
5.10
, so suppose that there exists an injective
f:A\hookrightarrow H
as above and let
i:H\hookrightarrow A
be the usual embedding. Then
g:=i\circ f:A\hookrightarrow A
fixes
H_{0}
and satisfies
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,A}
. Then
g
and hence
f
must both fix
H
by Lemma
5.15
, so
f
is surjective and could not have been injective because
H
is a proper subgroup fixed by
f
.
∎

6
Preretractions from elementary embeddings

Lemma 6.1

(Compare
[
10
, Lemma 5.18]
)
.

Let
A=\langle\underline{a}\rangle
be a finitely generated group with a JSJ-like splitting
\Lambda
such that all vertex and edge groups are finitely generated. There exists a first-order formula
\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{x},\underline{y})
such that for any morphisms
f,f^{\prime}:A\to G
given by
\underline{a}\mapsto\underline{s}
and
\underline{a}\mapsto\underline{t}
,
f
and
f^{\prime}
are
\Lambda
-related if and only if
G\models\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{s},\underline{t})
.

Proof.

Given a tuple
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, define

\alpha(\underline{x})\ :\ \bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq n}[x_{i},x_{j}]=1.

Then
G\models\alpha(\underline{x})
if and only if the subgroup
\langle\underline{x}\rangle
is abelian.

For each
v\in V(\Lambda)
and each
e\in E:=E(\Lambda)
choose finite generating sets
S_{v}:=\{\underline{b}=\underline{b}(\underline{a})\}
and
S_{e}:=\{\underline{c}=\underline{c}(\underline{a})\}
so that
A_{v}=\langle S_{v}\rangle
and
A_{e}=\langle S_{e}\rangle
. Partition
V(\Lambda)=U\amalg V
so that
V
consists of the non-exceptional surface-type vertices of
\Lambda
and
U
consists of all other vertices.

For each
u\in U
and
e\in E
define

\displaystyle\rho_{u}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\

\displaystyle:\ \exists z\bigwedge_{b\in S_{u}}b(\underline{x})=zb(\underline{y})z^{-1},

\displaystyle\rho_{e}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\

\displaystyle:\ \exists z\bigwedge_{c\in S_{e}}c(\underline{x})=zc(\underline{y})z^{-1},

and for each
v\in V
let
S_{v}=\{\underline{b}=\underline{b}(\underline{a})\}
and define

\rho_{v}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\ :\ \neg\alpha(\underline{b}(\underline{x}))\implies\neg\alpha(\underline{b}(\underline{y})).

Then the result follows if we define

\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\ :\ \left(\bigwedge_{u\in U}\rho_{u}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{v\in V}\rho_{v}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{e\in E}\rho_{e}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\right).

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
A=\langle\underline{a}\rangle
be a finitely generated group with the JSJ-like splitting
\Lambda
and let
G
be a torsion-free commutative-transitive group with all abelian subgroups finitely generated. There exists a first-order formula
\epsilon_{\Lambda}(\underline{x})
such that for any morphism
f:A\to G
given by
\underline{a}\mapsto\underline{s}
with
f(Z_{A}(A_{e}))\neq\{1\}
for all
e\in E(\Lambda)
,
f
satisfies

\operatorname{rank}(Z_{A}(A_{e}))=\operatorname{rank}(Z_{G}(f(A_{e})))

for all
e\in E(\Lambda)
if and only if
G\models\epsilon_{\Lambda}(\underline{s})
.

Proof.

For each
e\in E(\Lambda)
let
r_{e}=\operatorname{rank}(Z_{A}(A_{e}))
and choose a basis

\{b_{e,i}=b_{e,i}(\underline{a})\}_{i=1}^{r_{e}}
for
Z_{A}(A_{e})
. Since
E(\Lambda)
is finite by Lemma
2.8
we can define

\displaystyle\epsilon_{e}(\underline{x})\

\displaystyle:\ \left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{r_{e}}b_{e,i}(\underline{x})\neq 1\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{r_{e}}\left((b_{e,i}(\underline{x})\neq 1)\implies\mathcal{Z}_{r_{e}}(b_{e,i}(\underline{x}))\right)\right),

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\Lambda}(\underline{x})\

\displaystyle:\ \bigwedge_{e\in E(\Lambda)}\epsilon_{e}(\underline{x}).

Then
f(b_{e,i})=b_{e,i}(\underline{s})
, so
G\models\epsilon_{e}(\underline{s})
if and only if
f(Z_{A}(A_{e}))\neq\{1\}
and

\operatorname{rank}(Z_{G}(f(A_{e})))=\operatorname{rank}(Z_{G}(f(b_{e,i})))=r_{e}=\operatorname{rank}(Z_{A}(A_{e})),

so the result follows.
∎

\mainpropa

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be the JSJ splitting of
A
relative to
H
, let
A=\langle\underline{a}|\Sigma(\underline{a})=1\rangle
be a finite presentation for
A
, and let
H_{0}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}\subseteq H
and
K_{0}=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\}\subseteq A
as in Proposition
5.16
with
h_{i}=h_{i}(\underline{a})
and
k_{j}=k_{j}(\underline{a})
, where we are concerned with maps
A\to H
which satisfy
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,H}
.

Define the first-order formula

\displaystyle\varphi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\ :\

\displaystyle\forall\underline{x}\exists\underline{y}\ \left((\Sigma(\underline{x})=1)\wedge\epsilon_{\Lambda}(\underline{x})\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}=h_{i}(\underline{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\implies\left((\Sigma(\underline{y})=1)\wedge\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\wedge\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(\underline{y})=1\right)\right)

Then by Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
,
\varphi(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
can be interpreted as meaning that, for any morphism
f:A\to H
given by
\underline{a}\mapsto\underline{x}
which satisfies
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,H}
, there exists a
\Lambda
-related morphism
f^{\prime}:A\to H
given by
\underline{a}\mapsto\underline{y}
such that
f^{\prime}(k_{i})=1
for some
i
. Then
H\models\varphi(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
by Proposition
5.16
, so because
H\hookrightarrow_{\operatorname{Th}}G
we have
G\models\varphi(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
. Interpreted over
G
, this implies that because the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow G
satisfies
*_{\langle H_{0}\rangle,A,G}
, there exists a non-injective morphism
p:A\to G
which is
\Lambda
-related to the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow G
. Then
p:A\rightsquigarrow G
is a non-injective preretraction with respect to
\Lambda
.
∎

\mainpropb

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be the JSJ splitting of
C
, let
C=\langle\underline{c}|\Sigma(\underline{c})=1\rangle
be a finite presentation for
C
, let
\{\underline{u}\}=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(C,H)
be the finite set of embeddings as in Proposition
5.12
, and choose
K_{0}=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\}\subseteq C
to consist of a single non-trivial element from the kernel of each of the proper quotients of Proposition
5.11
with
k_{j}=k_{j}(\underline{c})
, where in both cases we are concerned with maps
C\to H
which satisfy
*_{\{1\},C,H}
.

Define the first-order formula with constants
u_{i}(\underline{c})\in H

\displaystyle\varphi(\underline{x})\ :\

\displaystyle((\Sigma(\underline{x})=1)\wedge\epsilon_{\Lambda}(\underline{x}))

\displaystyle\wedge\forall\underline{y}\ \left(((\Sigma(\underline{y})=1)\wedge\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{x},\underline{y}))\implies\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\underline{y}\neq u_{i}(\underline{c})\right)\right)

Then
H\models\varphi(\underline{x})
if and only if
\underline{c}\mapsto\underline{x}
defines a morphism
f:C\to H
which satisfies

\operatorname{rank}(Z_{A}(A_{e}))=\operatorname{rank}(Z_{H}(f(A_{e})))

for all
e\in E(\Lambda)
and is not
\Lambda
-related to any of the embeddings
u_{i}
. By Proposition
5.12
, this is sufficient to ensure that morphisms satisfying
*_{\{1\},C,H}
are non-injective, i.e., if
H\models\varphi(\underline{x})
then
\underline{c}\mapsto\underline{x}
defines a non-injective morphism
C\to H
.

Define the first-order sentence with constants in
H

\displaystyle\psi\ :\

\displaystyle\forall\underline{x}\ \varphi(\underline{x})

\displaystyle\implies\exists\underline{y}\ \left((\Sigma(\underline{y})=1)\wedge\rho_{\Lambda}(\underline{x},\underline{y})\wedge\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(\underline{y})=1\right)\right)

Then
H\models\psi
because any non-injective morphism
C\to H
given by
\underline{c}\mapsto\underline{x}
and satisfying
*_{\{1\},C,H}
is
\Lambda
-related to a morphism
\underline{c}\mapsto\underline{y}
with some
k_{i}
in its kernel by Proposition
5.11
. By the elementary embedding we have
G\models\psi
.

Because
C
is neither cyclic nor a non-exceptional surface group,
\Lambda
has at least one non-surface-type vertex subgroup
B
. Any
f:C\to G
which is
\Lambda
-related to the embedding
C\hookrightarrow G
given by
\underline{c}\mapsto\underline{c}
restricts to conjugation on
B
. Then
f(B)
cannot be in
H
by hypothesis, so the tuple
f(\underline{c})\neq u_{i}(\underline{c})
for all
i
and hence
G\models\varphi(\underline{c})
. Then there exists a non-injective morphism
p:C\to G
which is
\Lambda
-related to the inclusion
C\hookrightarrow G
, so
p:C\rightsquigarrow G
is a non-injective preretraction with respect to
\Lambda
.
∎

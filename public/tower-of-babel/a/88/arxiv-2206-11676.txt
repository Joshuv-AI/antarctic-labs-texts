Probing Neutral Triple Gauge Couplings
at the LHC and Future Hadron Colliders

Abstract
We study probes of neutral triple gauge couplings (nTGCs) at the LHC and the proposed 100 TeV p ​ p pp colliders, and compare their sensitivity reaches with those of the proposed e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders. The nTGCs provide a unique window to the new physics beyond the Standard Model (SM) because they can arise from SM effective field theory (SMEFT) operators that respect the full electroweak gauge group SU ​ ( 2 ) L ⊗ U ​ ( 1 ) Y \mathrm{SU(2)}_{\mathrm{L}}\!\otimes\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{U(1)}_{\mathrm{Y}} of the SM only at the level of dimension-8 or higher. We derive the neutral triple gauge vertices (nTGVs) generated by these dimension-8 operators in the broken phase and map them onto a newly generalized form factor formulation, which takes into account only the residual U(1) em gauge symmetry. Using this mapping, we derive new relations between the form factors that guarantee a truly consistent form factor formulation of the nTGVs and remove large unphysical energy-dependent terms. We then analyze the sensitivity reaches of the LHC and future 100 TeV hadron colliders for probing the nTGCs via both the dimension-8 nTGC operators and the corresponding nTGC form factors in the reaction p ​ p ​ ( q ​ q ¯ ) → Z ​ γ \hskip 0.85358ptp{\hskip 0.85358pt}p{\hskip 0.85358pt}(q{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{q})\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt with Z → ℓ + ​ ℓ − , ν ​ ν ¯ Z\hskip-0.85358pt\!\rightarrow\!\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\!,\hskip 0.85358pt\nu{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{\nu}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.85358pt . We compare their sensitivities with the existing LHC measurements of nTGCs and with those of the high-energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders. In general, we find that the prospective LHC sensitivities are comparable to those of an e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider with center-of-mass energy ⩽ 1 \leqslant\!1 TeV, whereas an e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider with center-of-mass energy ( 3 − 5 ) (3-5) TeV would have greater sensitivities, and a 100 TeV p ​ p pp collider could provide the most sensitive probes of the nTGCs. 4.1
Setup for the Analyses at Hadron Colliders

The distributions of quark and antiquark momenta in protons
are given by parton distribution functions (PDFs).
At leading order,
the total cross section of
\,p{\hskip 0.85358pt}p\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt

at the LHC is calculated by integrating
the convolved product of the quark and antiquark PDFs
and the parton-level cross section of
the
\hskip 0.85358ptq\hskip 0.85358pt\bar{q}\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt
subprocess:

\displaystyle\sigma\penalty\ =\penalty\ \sum_{q,\bar{q}\,}\!\int\!\!\text{d}x_{1}\hskip 0.85358pt\text{d}x_{2}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\!q/p}(x_{1},\mu)\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{F}_{\!\bar{q}/p}(x_{2},\mu)\hskip 1.42262pt{\sigma}_{\!q\bar{q}}(\hat{s})+(q\leftrightarrow\bar{q})\right]\!,

(4.1)

where the functions
\mathcal{F}_{\!q/p}
and

\mathcal{F}_{\!\bar{q}/p}
are the PDFs of the quark and
antiquark in the proton beams, and

\,\hat{s}\!=\!x_{1}\hskip 0.85358ptx_{2}\hskip 0.85358pts\,
with the collider energy

\sqrt{s\,}\hskip-1.42262pt\!=\hskip-1.42262pt\!13\,\text{TeV}\hskip 0.85358pt
. The PDFs depend on the factorization scale
\mu
,
which is set to be
\,\mu\hskip-0.85358pt\!=\hskip-1.42262pt\!\sqrt{\hat{s}\,}/2\,

in our leading-order analysis. We use the PDFs of the quarks

\hskip 0.85358ptq\hskip-0.85358pt=u,d,s,c,b\hskip 0.85358pt
and their antiquarks
determined by the CTEQ collaboration
[
23
]
.

During LHC Run-2 the ATLAS measurements of the

\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma
and
\bar{\nu}\nu\gamma

final states reached a maximum
value of
M_{\ell\ell\gamma}\!\sim\!3
TeV.

\hat{s}\hskip-1.42262pt\lesssim\hskip-1.42262pt3

pp

We compute the production cross section
of
\hskip 0.85358ptq\hskip 0.85358pt\bar{q}\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt
at leading order (LO)
in QCD and
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
for the SM, and

O(\alpha^{1.5}\tilde{c}_{j})
or

O(\alpha\tilde{c}_{j}^{2})
for the nTGCs,
where
\alpha\!=\!\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}

or
\alpha_{\mathrm{w}}
,
as the possible
high-order contributions are not important
for our study. There are next-to-leading-order (NLO) QCD corrections from
the gluon-induced loop diagrams for
\hskip 0.85358ptq\hskip 0.85358pt\bar{q}\rightarrow Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt

and the real emission of a gluon:
\hskip 0.85358ptq\hskip 0.85358pt\bar{q}\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358ptg\hskip 0.85358pt
, and there are also NLO QCD contributions from

g\hskip 0.85358ptq\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358ptq

(\hskip 0.85358ptg\hskip 0.85358pt\bar{q}\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip-0.85358pt+\bar{q}\hskip 0.85358pt)\hskip 0.85358pt
. In these cases the NLO/LO ratio is
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s})
, and
it was found numerically that the effect of adding the full
NNLO corrections is less than 10%
[
24
]
[
25
]
[
26
]
. We define a QCD
K
-factor for the nTGC signal by

\hskip 0.85358ptK_{\mathrm{S}}\!\hskip-0.85358pt\equiv\!S\hskip 0.85358pt/S_{\mathrm{LO}}\!\hskip-0.85358pt=\!\hskip-0.85358pt1\!+\!\Delta K_{\mathrm{S}}\hskip 0.85358pt

and for the SM background by

\hskip 0.85358ptK_{\mathrm{B}}\!\equiv\!B/B_{\mathrm{LO}}\!=\!1\!+\!\Delta K_{B}\hskip 0.85358pt
. We have checked the
K
-factors for
\hskip 0.85358ptpp\!\rightarrow\!Z\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt

by using
Madgraph5@NLO

[
27
]
,
and find that they depend on the kinematic cuts. The corrections
\Delta K
can be larger than one
if only basic cuts are made, but we find that adding a cut to remove the small
P_{T}(\gamma)
region and vetoing extra jets in the final state
reduces
\Delta K\hskip 0.85358pt

to only a few percent, which may be neglected.

We note in addition that
Z\gamma
production by the gluon fusion
process is formally a next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) contribution,
and is found to be generally less than 1%
[
28
]
. The nTGC contributions via gluon fusion is also found to be
negligible
[
28
]
.

Next, we discuss the statistical significance and its optimization
for our present analysis of sensitivity reaches on the nTGCs. Since the SM contribution
\sigma_{0}
could be small, the ratio

\hskip 0.85358ptS/\hskip-0.85358pt\sqrt{B\,}\hskip 0.85358pt
is not an optimal measure of the
statistical significance. We use instead the following formula for the
background-with-signal hypothesis
[
32
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\,=\,\sqrt{2\!\left(\!B\ln\!\frac{\,B\,}{\,B\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358ptS\,}+S\hskip-0.85358pt\right)\,}\,=\,\sqrt{2\!\left(\!\sigma_{0}\ln\!\frac{\,\sigma_{0}\,}{\,\sigma_{0}\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358pt\Delta\sigma\,}+\Delta\sigma\!\right)\,}\times\sqrt{\mathcal{L}\times\epsilon\penalty\ },

(4.2)

where

\hskip 0.85358pt\Delta\sigma\hskip-0.85358pt=\sigma\hskip-0.85358pt-\hskip-0.85358pt\sigma_{0}\hskip 0.85358pt

denotes the part of the cross section beyond the SM contribution,

\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{L}\hskip 0.85358pt
is the integrated luminosity, and

\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon\hskip 0.85358pt
is the detection efficiency. When
\hskip 0.85358ptB\!\gg\!S\hskip 0.85358pt
, we can expand (
4.12
)
in terms of
\,S/B\,
and
find that it reduces to the form

\,\mathcal{Z}\!\simeq\!S/\!\sqrt{B\,}
,
whereas for
\hskip 0.85358ptS\!\gg\!B\hskip 0.85358pt
it reduces to

\,\mathcal{Z}\!\simeq\!\sqrt{2S\,}\hskip 0.85358pt
. If the signal
S
is dominated by the interference contribution
of
O(\Lambda^{-4})
,
we can deduce that the sensitivity reach on the new physics scale:

\displaystyle\Lambda\propto(\mathcal{L}\hskip-1.42262pt\times\hskip-1.42262pt\epsilon)^{1/8},\,\hskip 39.83385pt(\text{for}\penalty\ B\!\gg\!S\hskip 0.85358pt),

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\Lambda\propto(\mathcal{L}\hskip-1.42262pt\times\hskip-1.42262pt\epsilon)^{1/4},\,\hskip 39.83385pt(\text{for}\penalty\ S\!\gg\!B\hskip 0.85358pt).

(4.3b)

If the signal
S
is dominated by the squared contribution
of
O(\Lambda^{-8})
,
we can deduce that the sensitivity reach on the new physics scale:

\displaystyle\Lambda\propto(\mathcal{L}\hskip-1.42262pt\times\hskip-1.42262pt\epsilon)^{1/16},\,\hskip 39.83385pt(\text{for}\penalty\ B\!\gg\!S\hskip 0.85358pt),

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\Lambda\propto(\mathcal{L}\hskip-1.42262pt\times\hskip-1.42262pt\epsilon)^{1/8},\,\hskip 44.10176pt(\text{for}\penalty\ S\!\gg\!B\hskip 0.85358pt).

(4.4b)

In either case, we see that the bound on the
new physics scale
\Lambda
is
not very sensitive to the integrated luminosity
\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{L}\hskip 0.85358pt

and the detection efficiency
\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon\hskip 0.85358pt
.
For instance, in the case of
B\!\gg\!S
,
if the integrated luminosity
\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{L}\,
increases
by a factor of
10\hskip 1.42262pt
,
we find that the sensitivity reach of
\hskip 0.85358pt\Lambda\hskip 0.85358pt
is enhanced
by about 33% when the interference contribution dominates the signal
and 15% when the squared contribution dominates the signal. If the detection efficiency
\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon\hskip 0.85358pt
is reduced from the
ideal value of
\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon\!=\!1\hskip 0.85358pt
to
\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon\!=\!0.5\hskip 0.85358pt
,
we find that the sensitivity reach of
\Lambda
is weakened
by only about 8% when the interference contribution dominates the signal
and 4% when the squared contribution dominates the signal.

In order to achieve higher sensitivity,
we can discriminate between the signal and background
by using the photon
P_{T}
distribution, employing the following measure of significance:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{total}}\,=\,\sqrt{\,\sum\!\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{bin}}^{2}\penalty\ }\,.

(4.5)

In the above, we impose the optimal cut on the photon

P_{T}
for each bin
and compute the corresponding significance

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{bin}}
of each bin. By doing so, we maximize the significance

\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{total}}\hskip 0.85358pt
given in
Eq.(
4.5
).

Conclusions

Neutral triple-gauge couplings (nTGCs) provide an important window
for probing new physics beyond the SM. In this work, we have studied systematically
the prospective experimental
sensitivities to nTGCs at the 13 TeV LHC
and a future 100 TeV
pp
collider,
using the SMEFT approach to classify and characterize the
nTGCs that can arise from gauge-invariant dimension-8 operators.

In Section
2.1
we first considered a set of
CP-conserving dimension-8 nTGC operators and the related contact
operators in Eq.(
2.2
). Then, in Section
2.2

we derived their contributions to the scattering amplitudes
of the partonic process
\bar{q}{\hskip 0.85358pt}q\hskip-0.85358pt\!\rightarrow\!\hskip-0.85358ptZ\gamma\hskip 0.85358pt

in Eqs.(
2.7
) and (
2.8
). With these, we computed the corresponding total cross sections
including the SM contribution, the interference term of

O(1/\Lambda^{4})
, and the squared term of

O(1/\Lambda^{8})
, as in
Eqs.(
2.12
) and (
2.15
),
where
\Lambda
is the new physics cutoff scale
defined in Eq.(
2.1
). We further presented in
Eq.(
2.14
), Eq.(
2.17
),
and Fig.
3

their contributions to the differential angular distributions,
in comparison with that of the SM. In Section
2.3
we analyzed the perturbative
unitarity bounds on the nTGCs, as shown in Table
1

and Fig.
1
, which are much weaker than the
collider limits presented in Sections
4
-
5
. Hence, the perturbation expanison is well justified for the current
collider analyses.

In Section
3
we presented a new form factor formulation
of the neutral triple gauge vertices (nTGVs)
Z\gamma V^{*}

(with
V\hskip-0.85358pt\!=\hskip-0.85358pt\!Z,\gamma
),
by mapping them to the dimension-8 nTGC operators
of the SMEFT that incorporate the spontaneously-broken
electroweak gauge symmetry
SU(2)
{}_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes
U(1)
Y
of the SM.
This differs from the conventional form factor parametrization
of nTGCs that takes into account only the unbroken
U(1)
em

[
3
]
[
4
]
. Using the SMEFT approach, we have found that
a new momentum-dependent
nTGC term with form factor

has to be added and the mapping
with the dimension-8 SMEFT interactions enforces
new nontrivial relations (
3.4a
)-(
3.4b
)
between the form factors
(h_{4}^{V}\hskip-1.42262pt,\hskip 0.85358pth_{5}^{V})

and between the form factors
(h_{4}^{Z}\hskip-0.85358pt,\,h_{4}^{\gamma})
.
The new form factor
h_{5}^{V}
was not included in all the previous
form factor analyses of nTGVs. We have demonstrated that
including the new form factor
h_{5}^{V}
is crucial for a fully consistent

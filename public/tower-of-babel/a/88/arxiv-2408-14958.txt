Note on 𝒩 = 8 \mathcal{N}=8 supersymmetric mechanics with dynamical and semi-dynamical multiplets
Erik Khastyan, Sergey Krivonos, Armen Nersessian

Abstract
We give a Hamiltonian formulation of the new model of 𝒩 = 8 \mathcal{N}=8 supersymmetric mechanics recently proposed by S. Fedoruk and E. Ivanov [ 1 ] and show that it possesses the dynamical 𝒩 = 8 \mathcal{N}=8 superconformal symmetry o ​ s ​ p ​ ( 8 | 2 ) osp(8|2) . The bosonic part of the Hamiltonian is just a free particle on eight-dimensional cone embedded in nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space, while the fermionic part can be interpreted as a spin-orbit interaction term.



I
Introduction

The natural framework for constructing and dealing with supersymmetric mechanics is the superfield approach. It works perfectly in the case of lower,
{\cal{N}}\leq 4
supersymmetries, but acquires a lot of complications for theories with
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetry. The reason is simple - the measure of the corresponding
{\cal{N}}=8
superspace is too large to be used to write superfield actions. To resolve this problem, the evident way is to construct the action in terms of several
{\cal{N}}=4
superfields so as to have another implicit
{\cal{N}}=4
supersymmetry which together with the explicit supersymmetry would form
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetry [1-9]. Clearly, such a construction of
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetric systems is much more complicated than
{\cal{N}}=4
supersymmetric ones because
the number and content of
{\cal{N}}=4
superfields involved is unknown a priori. Moreover, in some cases to have an appropriate component action, one has to use the so called oxidation procedure to avoid the second-order Lagrangian for fermions. Thus, the final component action, with excluded auxiliary
fields, is related to the superfield Lagrangian in an indirect and highly non-trivial way. The natural way to clarify the properties of the final component action and its possible (hidden) symmetries is to construct a Hamiltonian formulation and perform a proper redefinition of the fields involved.

Another approach to the
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetric mechanics has been developed in
[
7
]
, where
the most general
{\cal{N}}=8
invariant action was constructed based on the
\mathbf{(1,8,7)}
supermultiplet. It depends on two real parameters:
a,b
and gives an interaction term; by setting
a=0
one recovers the action of ref.
[
3
]
. Later on it was shown in
[
8
]

that the
F(4)
superconformal action of Delduc-Ivanov
[
10
]
is recovered
by setting
b=0
in the action in
[
7
]
. Finally, in 2018, the quantum superconformal Hamiltonian of the
F(4)
model was constructed in
[
9
]
.

Recently, S. Fedoruk and E. Ivanov have presented the new model of
\mathcal{N}=8
supersymmetric mechanics constructed by using the so-called dynamical and semi-dynamical
\mathcal{N}=4
multiplets
[
1
]
. The final multiplet content of the model is
\mathbf{(1,8,7)}\bigoplus\mathbf{(8,8,0)}
.
After eliminating auxiliary variables, the authors arrived the Lagrangian depending on the single real bosonic variable, the eight complex bosonic variables, and the eight real fermionic variables. Only the real bosonic variable appears in the Lagrangian with the second-order kinetic term, while the kinetic terms corresponding to complex bosonic variables are of the first order.
Hence, the system could be directly formulated on the
(18|8)_{\mathbb{R}}
dimensional phase space.
The authors interpreted the model as a
\mathcal{N}=8
supersymmetric extension of the one-dimensional isospin particle.

In this paper, we give the Hamiltonian formulation of this model
and find two sets of constants of motion: the odd constants of motion - supercharges forming the
d=1,\mathcal{N}=8
Poincare superalgebra and the even constants of motion forming
so(8)
algebra. The bosonic and fermionic parts of these
so(8)
-generators separately define the constants of motion and form the same
so(8)
algebra.
We also demonstrate that the supercharges have the structure
“
so(8)-generators\,\times\,fermions
”.

Moreover, we indicate two unexpected properties of the model:

•

It possesses dynamical
osp(8|2)
superconformal symmetry.

•

It describes the specific
\mathcal{N}=8
supersymmetric extension of the free particle on
eight-dimensional cone
embedded in nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space, with the
fermionic part, which can be interpreted as a spin-orbit coupling term.

Thus, the proposed model is distinguished among the conventional supersymmetric mechanics models, where supersymmetry arises due to the Pauli term defining the interaction of spin with external fields.

The paper is organized as follows:

In
Section 2
, we give the Hamiltonian formulation of the model,
construct its supercharges and even constants of motion. Then we show that this variant of
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetric mechanics possesses
so(8|2)
dynamical symmetry.

In
Section 3
, we show that the bosonic part of the model is a free particle on eight-dimensional cone embedded in nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space, while the fermionic part can be interpreted as a spin-orbital interaction term.

In
Conclusion
we review obtained results and mention possible future developments

In the
Appendix A
we present the explicit expressions for the superconformal algebra
osp(8|2)
written in the notation used.

III
Geometry and dynamics

Since the system under consideration has the canonical symplectic structure (
9
),
we can interpret the pairs
(z_{1}^{iA},z_{2\;iA})
and
(B^{i}_{1},\bar{B}_{2\;i})

as canonically conjugated momenta and coordinates.
For visualization of this observation (and of the structure of the respective Hamiltonian system) we suggest the following notation:

\begin{array}[]{c}z_{1}^{iA}:=x^{4}\delta^{iA}+\imath x^{\alpha}(\sigma_{\alpha})^{iA},\quad z_{2\;iA}:=p_{4}\delta_{iA}+\imath p_{\alpha}(\sigma_{\alpha})_{iA},\quad\chi^{iA}:=\chi^{4}\delta^{iA}+\imath\chi^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\alpha})^{iA},\qquad\alpha=1,2,3\\[8.53581pt]
B_{1}^{i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x^{2i+3}+\imath x^{2i+4}),\quad\bar{B}_{2\;i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(p_{2i+3}-\imath p_{2i+4}),\quad\phi_{i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\chi^{2i+3}+\imath\chi^{2i+4}),\qquad i=1,2.\end{array}

(24)

In these terms, the Poisson brackets (
10
) read

\{p_{r},r\}=1,\qquad\{p_{\mu},x^{\nu}\}=\delta_{\mu}^{\nu},\qquad\{\chi^{\mu},{\chi}^{\nu}\}=\imath\delta^{\mu\nu},

(25)

while the bosonic and fermionic parts of
so(8)
generators (
16
)-(
20
) result, respectively in
L_{\mu\nu}:=p_{\mu}x_{\nu}-p_{\nu}x_{\mu}
, and
R_{\mu\nu}:=\imath\chi_{\mu}\chi_{\nu}
.
The bosonic part of (
22
) acquires now the form

\mathcal{I}_{bos}=\left.{\cal I}\right|_{\chi^{\mu}\to\;0}=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{8}L^{2}_{\mu\nu},.

(26)

Here, the indices
\mu,\nu
in
L_{\mu\nu}
are raised/lowered by the use of Euclidian metrics
\delta_{\mu\nu}
.
Being expressed in terms of spherical coordinates and conjugated momenta the angular part
becomes independent of the radius
R=\sqrt{\sum_{\mu=1}^{8}{x}^{\mu}{x}^{\mu}}
and conjugated momentum
p_{R}={\sum_{\mu=1}^{8}{p}_{\mu}{x}^{\mu}}/R
.

Performing the canonical transformation of the bosonic coordinates

(x^{\mu},p_{\mu},r,p_{r},)\to(y^{\mu},\pi_{\mu},R,p_{R})
with

R=\sqrt{\sum_{\nu=1}^{8}x^{\nu}x^{\nu}}\;:=|\mathbf{x}|,\quad p_{R}=\frac{\sum_{\mu=1}^{8}p_{\mu}x^{\mu}}{|\mathbf{x}|},\quad y^{\mu}={r}\frac{x^{\mu}}{|\mathbf{x}|},\quad\pi_{\mu}=\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{r}p_{\mu}+\left(p_{r}-\frac{\sum_{\lambda=1}^{8}p_{\lambda}x^{\lambda}}{r}\right)\frac{x^{\mu}}{|\mathbf{x}|},

(27)

we bring the bosonic part of the Hamiltonian to the following form

\mathcal{H}_{bos}=\left.{\cal H}\right|_{{\chi}\to\;0}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{8}g^{\mu\nu}\pi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu},\quad{\rm with}\quad g^{\mu\nu}=\delta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{3y^{\mu}y^{\nu}}{\sum_{\lambda=1}^{8}y^{\lambda}y^{\lambda}},\quad L_{\mu\nu}=\pi_{\mu}y^{\nu}-\pi_{\nu}y^{\mu}.

(28)

The Poisson brackets are defined by the relations

\{p_{R},R\}=1
,
\{\pi_{\mu},y^{\nu}\}=\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}
.
So, the variables
p_{R},R
do not appear in the Hamiltonian anymore, i.e. we deal with an eight-dimensional system.

From the above expression of Hamiltonian we immediately get the metric of configuration space:

(ds)^{2}=\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{8}g_{\mu\nu}dy^{\mu}dy^{\nu},\qquad g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{y^{\mu}y^{\nu}}{{\sum_{\lambda=1}^{8}y^{\lambda}y^{\lambda}}},\qquad g^{\mu\lambda}g_{\lambda\nu}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(29)

It is just the induced metrics of the cone in the nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidian space
\mathbf{R}^{8.1}
,

ds^{2}=-dy^{0}dy^{0}+(d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{y}),\qquad{\rm where}\quad 4y_{0}=3|\mathbf{y}|.

(30)

Performing conformal transformation we can represent this metrics to the conformal-flat form

\tilde{y}^{\mu}=\frac{y^{\mu}}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{y}|}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad(ds)^{2}=|\mathbf{\tilde{y}}|^{2}d\mathbf{\tilde{y}}d\mathbf{\tilde{y}}

(31)

Thus, the bosonic part of the Hamiltonian is a free particle on the eight-dimensional cone embedded in nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidian space.

Let us also notice that the fermionic part of Hamiltonian,
({\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{8}L_{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}})/({\sum_{\lambda=1}^{8}y^{\lambda}y^{\lambda}})
, can be interpreted as a spin-orbit coupling term.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we give the Hamiltonian formulation to the
{\cal{N}}=8
supersymmetric mechanic constructed recently in
[
1
]
. The Hamiltonian description reveals some interesting properties of the model hidden in the Lagrangian description. In particular, we demonstrate that

•

The system possesses the dynamical conformal
osp(8|2)
supersymmetry,

•

The Poincaré supercharges have a nice structure, being just the product of
R
-symmetry generators to the fermions,

•

The bosonic sector of the system describes a free particle on
eight-dimensional cone
in nine-dimensional pseudo-Euclidian space . The fermionic part can be interpreted as a spin-orbit coupling term.

This kind of a supersymmetric mechanics is quite unusual and has been out of focus in the supersymmetric mechanics community. For nontrivial examples of similar systems we refer to
[
15
]
where the three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric mechanics with a spin-orbit coupling term were constructed by the Hamiltonian reduction of the appropriate four-dimensional system.
We have no doubts that the earlier introduced
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric mechanics with ”semi-dynamical” (isospin) variables
[
14
]
can be interpreted in a similar way.
These systems can be directly obtained from the action (
8
), (
22
) and supercharges
(
12
), (
13
) in the limit
\left\{\chi^{i^{\prime}A},z_{a}^{jA}\right\}\rightarrow 0
or

\left\{\phi^{i},B_{a}^{i^{\prime}}\right\}\rightarrow 0
. In both cases we will end up with
{\cal{N}}=4
supersymmetric mechanics with
osp(4|2)
dynamical superconformal symmetry.

Another interesting issue is the reduction of the present system by the
SU(2)
group action.
As a result of such reduction, we will arrive at the five-dimensional system specified by an additional interaction with
SU(2)
Yang monopole (cf.
[
16
]
). Also, it would be interesting to construct similar systems on the curved background as well as to construct their higher-dimensional (”multi-particle”) analogs.

It seems that the presented system can be elegantly described in terms of octonions.
The point is that two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
and four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric systems with spin-orbit interaction term can be surprisingly formulated in a unified way in terms of complex and quaternionic variables, respectively.
We are planning to present these systems elsewhere, hopefully with their eight-dimensional octonionic
\mathcal{N}=8
counterpart.

Appendix A: Superconformal algebra
\mathbf{Osp(8|2)}

Here we present the Poisson brackets relations of the
\mathcal{N}=8
conformal superalgebra
osp(8|2)
. The
osp(8|2)
superalgebra contains 31 bosonic generators forming the
sl(2)\times so(8)
algebra and 16 fermionic generators - 8 generators
\left\{Q^{i},{\bar{Q}}_{i},Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}
forming the
d=1,\;\mathcal{N}=8
Poincaré superalgebra and 8 generators
\left\{S^{i},{\bar{S}}_{i},S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}
of conformal supersymmetry.

The
sl(2)
generators span the
d=1
conformal algebra, while
so(8)
generators form the

R
-symmetry algebra. We identify the
sl(2)
generators
{\cal H},D,K
with the time-translation, dilatation and conformal boost, respectively. We divided the
so(8)
generators
into four mutually commuting
su(2)
algebras
\left\{J_{3},J,{\bar{J}}\right\},X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},W^{ij},Y^{AB}
and the generators
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
belonging to the coset
so(8)/su(2)^{4}
. The commutators of the
sl(2)\times so(8)
algebra in our setup look as follows:

IV.1
Bosonic sector

•

The conformal
sl(2)
algebra contains the generators
{\cal H},D,K
which obey the following Poisson brackets

\left\{D,{\cal H}\right\}=-{\cal H},\;\left\{D,K\right\}=K,\;\left\{{\cal H},K\right\}=2D.

(A.1)

The generators
{\cal H},D,K
are Hermitian and have trivial Poisson brackets with the generators of the
so(8)
algebra.

•

First
su(2)

\left\{J,{\bar{J}}\right\}=2\imath J_{3},\;\left\{J_{3},J\right\}=\imath J,\;\left\{J_{3},{\bar{J}}\right\}=-\imath{\bar{J}}.

(A.2)

The generators
J,{\bar{J}},J_{3}
have trivial Poisson brackets with the generators
X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},W^{ij},Y^{AB}
, while
the brackets with
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
read

\left\{J,{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}\right\}=V^{ij^{\prime}A},\;\left\{{\bar{J}},V^{ij^{\prime}A}\right\}=-{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},\;\left\{J_{3},V^{ij^{\prime}A}\right\}=\frac{\imath}{2}V^{ij^{\prime}A},\;\left\{J_{3},{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{\imath}{2}{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}.

(A.3)

•

Second
su(2)

\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},X^{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\right\}=-\epsilon^{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}X^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}-\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}X^{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}.

(A.4)

The generators
X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
have trivial Poisson brackets with the generators
W^{ij},Y^{AB}
, while the brackets with
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
read

\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},V^{km^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{i^{\prime}m^{\prime}}V^{kj^{\prime}A}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}V^{ki^{\prime}A},\quad\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},{\bar{V}}^{km^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{i^{\prime}m^{\prime}}{\bar{V}}^{kj^{\prime}A}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}{\bar{V}}^{ki^{\prime}A}.

(A.5)

•

Third
su(2)

\left\{W^{ij},W^{kl}\right\}=-\epsilon^{ik}W^{jl}-\epsilon^{jl}W^{ik}.

(A.6)

The generators
W^{ij}
have trivial Poisson brackets with the generators
Y^{AB}
, while
the brackets with
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
read

\left\{W^{ij},V^{km^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ik}V^{jm^{\prime}A}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{jk}V^{im^{\prime}A},\quad\left\{W^{ij},{\bar{V}}^{km^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ik}{\bar{V}}^{jm^{\prime}A}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{jk}{\bar{V}}^{im^{\prime}A}.

(A.7)

•

Fourth
su(2)

\left\{Y^{AB},Y^{CD}\right\}=-\epsilon^{AC}Y^{BD}-\epsilon^{BD}Y^{AC}.

(A.8)

The Poisson brackets with the generators
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
read

\left\{Y^{AB},V^{ij^{\prime}C}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{AC}V^{ij^{\prime}B}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{BC}V^{ij^{\prime}A},\quad\left\{Y^{AB},{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}C}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{AC}{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}B}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{BC}{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}.

(A.9)

•

Finally, the generators
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}A}
, from the coset

so(8)/su(2)^{4}
, obey the following brackets:

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},V^{kl^{\prime}B}\right\}=-2\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}J,\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{V}}^{kl^{\prime}B}\right\}=-2\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}{\bar{J}},

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{V}}^{kl^{\prime}B}\right\}=2\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}W^{ik}+2\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}Y^{AB}+2\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{AB}X^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}-2\imath\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{j^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}J_{3}.

(A.10)

The Poisson brackets between the fermionic generators look like

IV.2
Fermionic sector

•

the
d=1,\;\mathcal{N}=8
Poincaré superalgebra

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i},{\bar{Q}}^{j}\right\}=-2\imath\epsilon^{ij}{\cal H},\quad\left\{Q^{i},Q^{j}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{Q}}^{i},{\bar{Q}}^{j}\right\}=\left\{Q^{i},{\cal H}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{Q}}^{i},{\cal H}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},Q^{j^{\prime}B}\right\}=4\imath\epsilon^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}\,{\cal H},\quad\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},Q^{j}\right\}=\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},{\bar{Q}}^{j}\right\}=\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},{\cal H}\right\}=0.

(A.11)

•

Superconformal

\displaystyle\left\{S^{i},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=-2\imath\epsilon^{ij}K,\quad\left\{S^{i},S^{j}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{S}}^{i},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=\left\{S^{i},K\right\}=\left\{{\bar{S}}^{i},K\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{S^{i^{\prime}A},S^{j^{\prime}B}\right\}=4\imath\epsilon^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}K,\quad\left\{S^{i^{\prime}A},S^{j}\right\}=\left\{S^{i^{\prime}A},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=\left\{S^{i^{\prime}A},K\right\}=0.

(A.12)

•

Cross commutators

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i},S^{j}\right\}=\imath\epsilon^{ij}J,\;\left\{{\bar{Q}}^{i},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=\imath\epsilon^{ij}{\bar{J}},

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=-2\imath\epsilon^{ij}D-\imath W^{ij}-\epsilon^{ij}J_{3},\;\left\{{\bar{Q}}^{i},S^{j}\right\}=2\imath\epsilon^{ij}D+\imath W^{ji}-\epsilon^{ij}J_{3},

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i},S^{j^{\prime}A}\right\}=\imath V^{ij^{\prime}A}\;,\left\{{\bar{Q}}^{i},S^{j^{\prime}A}\right\}=\imath{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},S^{j},\right\}=-\imath V^{ji^{\prime}A},\;\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},{\bar{S}}^{j}\right\}=-{\bar{V}}^{ji^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{i^{\prime}A},S^{j^{\prime}B}\right\}=4\imath\epsilon^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}D+2\imath\epsilon^{AB}X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2\imath\epsilon^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}Y^{AB}.

(A.13)

Finally, the cross boson-fermion brackets have the following structure.

IV.3
Boson-Fermion Poisson brackets

•

sl(2)\times\mbox{Supercharges}

\displaystyle\left\{{\cal H},Q^{i}\right\}=\left\{{\cal H},{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=\left\{{\cal H},Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,\quad\left\{{\cal H},S^{i}\right\}=Q^{i},\;\left\{{\cal H},{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}={\bar{Q}}^{i},\;\left\{{\cal H},S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=Q^{i^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{D,Q^{i}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}Q^{i},\;\left\{D,{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}{\bar{Q}}^{i},\;\left\{D,Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}Q^{i^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{D,S^{i}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}S^{i},\;\left\{D,{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}{\bar{S}}^{i},\;\left\{D,S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}S^{i^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{K,S^{i}\right\}=\left\{K,{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=\left\{K,S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,\quad\left\{K,Q^{i}\right\}=-S^{i},\;\left\{K,{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=-{\bar{S}}^{i},\;\left\{K,Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=-S^{i^{\prime}A}.

(A.14)

•

First
su(2)\times\mbox{Supercharges}

\displaystyle\left\{J,Q^{i}\right\}=\left\{J,S^{i}\right\}=\left\{J,Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=\left\{J,S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,\quad\left\{J,{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=Q^{i},\;\left\{J,{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=S^{i},

\displaystyle\left\{J_{3},Q^{i}\right\}=\frac{\imath}{2}Q^{i},\;\left\{J_{3},{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=-\frac{\imath}{2}{\bar{Q}}^{i},\;\left\{J_{3},S^{i}\right\}=\frac{\imath}{2}S^{i},\;\left\{J_{3},{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=-\frac{\imath}{2}{\bar{S}}^{i},\quad\left\{J_{3},Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=\left\{J_{3},S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{{\bar{J}},{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{J}},{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{J}},Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=\left\{{\bar{J}},S^{i^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,\quad\left\{{\bar{J}},Q^{i}\right\}=-{\bar{Q}}^{i},\;\left\{{\bar{J}},S^{i}\right\}=-{\bar{S}}^{i},

(A.15)

•

Second
su(2)\times\mbox{Supercharges}

\displaystyle\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},Q^{k}\right\}=\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},{\bar{Q}}^{k}\right\}=\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},S^{k}\right\}=\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},{\bar{S}}^{k}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},Q^{k^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}Q^{j^{\prime}A}+\epsilon^{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right),\;\left\{X^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},S^{k^{\prime}A}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}S^{j^{\prime}A}+\epsilon^{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}S^{i^{\prime}A}\right).

(A.16)

•

Third
su(2)\times\mbox{Supercharges}

\displaystyle\left\{W^{ij},Q^{k}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{ik}Q^{j}+\epsilon^{jk}Q^{i}\right),\;\left\{W^{ij},{\bar{Q}}^{k}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{ik}{\bar{Q}}^{j}+\epsilon^{jk}{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right),\quad\left\{W^{ij},Q^{k^{\prime}A}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{W^{ij},S^{k}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{ik}S^{j}+\epsilon^{jk}S^{i}\right),\;\left\{W^{ij},{\bar{S}}^{k}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{ik}{\bar{S}}^{j}+\epsilon^{jk}{\bar{S}}^{i}\right),\quad\left\{W^{ij},S^{k^{\prime}A}\right\}=0.

(A.17)

•

Fourth
su(2)\times\mbox{Supercharges}

\displaystyle\left\{Y^{AB},Q^{i}\right\}=\left\{Y^{AB},{\bar{Q}}^{i}\right\}=\left\{Y^{AB},S^{i}\right\}=\left\{Y^{AB},{\bar{S}}^{i}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\left\{Y^{AB},Q^{i^{\prime}C}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{AC}Q^{i^{\prime}B}+\epsilon^{BC}Q^{i^{\prime}A}\right),\;\left\{Y^{AB},S^{i^{\prime}C}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon^{AC}S^{i^{\prime}B}+\epsilon^{BC}S^{i^{\prime}A}\right).

(A.18)

•

The brackets of
V^{ij^{\prime}A}
and
{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A}
with Supercharges

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},Q^{k}\right\}=0,\;\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{Q}}^{k}\right\}=\epsilon^{ik}Q^{j^{\prime}A},\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{Q}}^{k}\right\}=0,\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},Q^{k}\right\}=-\epsilon^{ik}Q^{j^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},S^{k}\right\}=0,\;\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{S}}^{k}\right\}=\epsilon^{ik}S^{j^{\prime}A},\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},{\bar{S}}^{k}\right\}=0,\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},S^{k}\right\}=-\epsilon^{ik}S^{j^{\prime}A},

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ij^{\prime}A},Q^{k^{\prime}B}\right\}=2\epsilon^{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}Q^{i},\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ij^{\prime}A},Q^{k^{\prime}B}\right\}=2\epsilon^{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}{\bar{Q}}^{i},

\displaystyle\left\{V^{ijA},S^{kB}\right\}=2\epsilon^{jk}\epsilon^{AB}S^{i},\;\left\{{\bar{V}}^{ijA},S^{kB}\right\}=2\epsilon^{jk}\epsilon^{AB}{\bar{S}}^{i}.

(A.19)

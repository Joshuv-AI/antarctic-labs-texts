On blow up of C 1 \displaystyle C^{1} solutions of isentropic Euler system
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, Animesh Jana

Abstract
In this article, we study the break-down of smooth and continuous solutions to isentropic Euler system in multi dimension. Sideris [Comm. Math. Phys. 1985] proved the blow up of smooth solutions when initial data satisfies an ‘integral condition’. We show that a C 1 \displaystyle C^{1} solution of isentropic Euler equation breaks down if (i) gradient of initial velocity has a negative real eigenvalue at some point x 0 ∈ ℝ d \displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and (ii) Hessian of initial density satisfies a smallness condition in Sobolev space. Our proof also works for the data which fails to satisfy the above-mentioned ‘integral condition’. Furthermore, we prove the global existence of smooth solution when (i) eigenvalues of gradient of initial velocity have non-negative real-part and (ii) initial density satisfies a smallness condition. This extends the global existence result of [Grassin, Indiana Univ. Math. J. 1998]. Another goal of this article is to study the breakdown of continuous weak solutions of isentropic Euler equations. We are able to show that the ‘integral condition’ of Sideris can cause the breakdown of continuous solutions in finite time. This improves the blow up result of Sideris from C 1 \displaystyle C^{1} to continuous space.

Introduction

In this article, we are interested in regularity and breakdown of smooth solution to the isentropic Euler equations which reads as follows,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\dv_{x}(\varrho\textbf{u})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\textbf{u})+\dv_{x}(\varrho\textbf{u}\otimes\textbf{u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,

(1.2)

where
\displaystyle\varrho,\textbf{u}
denote the density and velocity of a compressible fluid and the function
\displaystyle p:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
represents the pressure. Here we consider
\displaystyle p(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma}
for
\displaystyle\gamma>1
.

Despite of the importance of PDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in the physical world, very little is known about well-posedness. For sufficiently smooth initial data, the local-in-time existence of smooth solutions is known due to
[
9
,
30
,
40
]
. In one dimension, the
\displaystyle C^{1}
–norm of solution blows up in finite time for compact support velocity (see
[
29
,
33
,
35
,
40
]
). In his seminal work
[
45
]
, Sideris proposed a sufficient condition for multi-dimension to show that the smooth solution breaks down in finite time. See also
[
7
,
12
,
13
,
28
,
31
,
38
,
46
]
for results on blow up and lifespan of smooth solution to isentropic Euler system and related hyperbolic system of conservation laws. In this article, we first prove that for a certain restriction on initial data, the continuity of a solution breaks down in finite time if it also satisfies the entropy inequality in the sense of distribution,

\partial_{t}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho\left|\textbf{u}\right|^{2}+P(\varrho)\right]+\dv_{x}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho\left|\textbf{u}\right|^{2}+P(\varrho)+p(\varrho)\right)\textbf{u}\right]\leq 0,\mbox{ where }P(\varrho)=\varrho\int\limits_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{p(r)}{r^{2}}dr.

(1.3)

We note that the initial data for which the result of Sideris
[
45
]
is valid also satisfies the assumption of our result. Hence, for the initial data considered in
[
45
]
, the corresponding solution not only loses its
\displaystyle C^{1}
regularity in finite time, it also becomes discontinuous.

Recently, there has been a growing interest in existence of continuous solutions for fluid flow equations (see
[
15
,
23
,
27
]
). For isentropic Euler equation, it has been shown
[
23
]
that there are infinitely many Hölder continuous weak solutions from a specific initial data. It is then natural to ask whether these solutions are globally defined or not. We show that if a continuous solution satisfies entropy inequality (
1.3
) and an ‘integral condition’ (as in (
2.4
) below), then their continuity breaks down in finite time.

On the other hand, global smooth solution exists
[
33
]
in 1-D for a class of initial data. Chemin
[
9
]
proved the global existence of smooth solution in multi-D when initial density has finite second spatial moment. Later, Serre
[
43
]
showed that global-in-time solution exists when the initial density and velocity,
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
are sufficiently close to
\displaystyle(0,Ax)
in an appropriate Sobolev norm and
\displaystyle A
is a positive definite constant matrix. The theory of global existence for isentropic Euler system has been extended by Grassin
[
25
]
for a larger class of initial data. In the article
[
25
]
, it has been shown that smooth solution exists for all time
\displaystyle t>0
if (i) the initial density is sufficiently small in Sobolev norm and (ii) eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}
belong to a compact set of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
. In this article, we establish a relation between the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}
and the global existence of smooth solution. We consider the background velocity
\displaystyle\overline{\textbf{u}}
solving the following Cauchy problem

\left.\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\overline{\textbf{u}}+\overline{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{\textbf{u}}&=0\,\,\,\,\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,\\
\overline{\textbf{u}}(0,x)&=\overline{\textbf{u}}_{0}\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right\}

(1.4)

For the vectorial Burgers equation (
1.4
), global smooth solution exists if and only if the initial data
\displaystyle\overline{\textbf{u}}_{0}
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\overline{\textbf{u}}_{0}
belong to a compact set of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
, equivalently, we have finite time blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions of (
1.4
) if and only if
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\overline{\textbf{u}}_{0}
has at least one negative real eigenvalue. In this article, we want to show an analogous result for isentropic Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). We first prove the finite time blow up of smooth solution when
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}{\textbf{u}}_{0}(x_{0})
has at least one negative real eigenvalue for some
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the spirit of
[
25
]
, we also show that global smooth solution exists for
\displaystyle\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi\geq 0
where
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
and initial density satisfies a smallness condition. Combining these two results, we conclude the characterization of initial velocity providing global smooth solutions.

Though our main concern of this article is to find the optimal characterization of initial data causing blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
or continuous solution, it is also an interesting subject to study the description and stability of the shock and how the solution behaves after the blow up time. Majda
[

,
39

studied the existence and stability of shock for multi-D system of conservation laws. Description of shock formation has been studied in
[

,

]
. We also refer recent results
[

]
in this direction. For spherically symmetric smooth initial data, the blow up of
\displaystyle L^{\infty}

[

In a forthcoming paper
[

]

2
Main results

2.1
Blow up of continuous solution

Before we state our result on break-down of continuous solution we provide the definition of weak solution.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle T^{*}>0
. We say
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})\in C([0,T^{*});L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) if the following holds.

•

For all
\displaystyle 0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<T^{*}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([t_{1},t_{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left[\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho(t,\cdot)\varphi(t,\cdot)\,dx\right]_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}=\int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\,dxdt.

(2.1)

•

For all
\displaystyle 0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<T^{*}
and
\displaystyle\psi\in C^{1}_{c}([t_{1},t_{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left[\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\cdot\psi(t,\cdot)\,dx\right]_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}=\int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\varrho\textbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\psi+\varrho\textbf{u}\otimes\textbf{u}:\nabla_{x}\psi+p(\varrho)\dv_{x}\psi\right]\,dxdt.

(2.2)

A weak solution
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})
will be called as admissible solution if it satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T^{*}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left(\frac{\varrho\left|\textbf{u}\right|^{2}}{2}+P(\varrho)\right)\partial_{t}\varphi+\left(\frac{\varrho\left|\textbf{u}\right|^{2}}{2}+P(\varrho)+p(\varrho)\right)\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]dxdt

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\varrho_{0}\left|\textbf{u}_{0}\right|^{2}}{2}+P(\varrho_{0})\right)\varphi(0,\cdot)\,dx\geq 0,

(2.3)

for all
\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
\displaystyle P(\varrho)=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}
.

The well-posedness of admissible solution to isentropic Euler system is largely open. In one dimension the well-posedness for admissible weak solution is known in BV setting
[
3
,
4
,
24
]
. But, in multi-D even for Riemann type planar initial data it has been shown
[
10
]
that infinitely many
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
admissible weak solutions exist. In three dimension, infinitely many continuous admissible weak solution has been constructed
[
23
]
so that they satisfy same initial data. On the other hand, uniqueness has been shown
[
17
]
when solution has sufficient Hölder regularity and the velocity gradient satisfies a one-sided bound condition. See also
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
for recent results on uniqueness of Hölder continuous solutions for compressible Euler system and relation hyperbolic conservation laws.

To study the breakdown of continuity of weak solutions, we impose the following condition on initial data as in
[
45
]
,

\frac{1}{\omega_{d}R^{d+1}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{0}\textbf{u}_{0}\cdot x\,dx\geq(d+1)\sigma\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\varrho_{0}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.4)

where
\displaystyle\sigma=\sqrt{\gamma}(\overline{\varrho})^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
and
\displaystyle\omega_{d}
denotes the area of
\displaystyle d
dimensional sphere. Furthermore, we additionally assume that
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
satisfies

(\varrho_{0}(x),\textbf{u}_{0}(x))=(\overline{\varrho},\textbf{0})\mbox{ for }\left|x\right|\geq R\mbox{ and }\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\varrho_{0}-\overline{\varrho})\,dx\geq 0,

(2.5)

for some
\displaystyle\overline{\varrho}>0
and
\displaystyle R>0
. We show that a continuous admissible solution breaks down in finite time if the initial data satisfies above two conditions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})\in C([0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be an admissible solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with initial data
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
according to Definition
2.1
. We assume that the initial data
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
satisfies (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). Then,
\displaystyle T^{*}
is finite.

Sideris
[
45
]
proved that
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions break down in finite time if the initial data satisfies the conditions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). With a similar condition, blow up of classical solution has been shown
[
49
]
Navier-Stokes. We refer to
[
48
,
50
]
and references therein for more results on break-down of
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions with a condition like (
2.4
).

We note that the entropy condition (
1.3
) and the continuity of solution plays key role to prove that characteristics generated from
\displaystyle\{\left|x\right|\leq R\}
are confined inside the cone
\displaystyle\{\left|x\right|\leq R+\sigma t\}
. For
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions we do not additionally need to assume (
1.3
) as it can be shown by using (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) (see
[
14
]
) that equality holds in (
1.3
) for
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions. For isentropic Euler equations, equality in (
1.3
) can still hold even for weak solutions which may not be
\displaystyle C^{1}
(see
[
2
,
18
,
26
]
). Furthermore, we would like to mention a recent result
[
23
]
on existence of infinitely many continuous admissible solutions of isentropic Euler equations. For some specific choice of continuous initial data, this
[
23
]
gives an existential results for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). We wish to remark that due to our result in Theorem

the solutions constructed in
[

]
can not be extended globally (in time) if the initial data satisfies the integral condition (
2.4
).

2.2
Blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
solution

In the view of blow up result of Sideris
[
45
]
, it is natural to ask whether global
\displaystyle C^{1}
solution exists if a
\displaystyle C^{1}
initial data does not satisfy the condition (
2.4
). We answer to this question in our next result. To prove a finite time blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
solution, we impose the following conditions on the initial data.

(ND)

Negative definiteness of velocity gradient:
There exists
\displaystyle\lambda_{max}>0
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})\mbox{ is symmetric and }\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})\xi_{0}=-\lambda_{max}\xi_{0}\mbox{ for some }\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

(2.6)

We now state our result on blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions of isentropic Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})\in C^{1}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a solution of isentropic Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) corresponding to the initial data
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
satisfying
(ND)
and
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}+r\xi_{0})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})=-\lambda_{0}r\xi_{0}
for some
\displaystyle r>0,\lambda_{0}>0,\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define
\displaystyle M:=\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. If
\displaystyle\varrho,\textbf{u}
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}
satisfy the following estimate,

\left|\lambda_{max}-\lambda_{0}\right|+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\pi(\cdot)\nabla_{x}\pi(\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,1/\lambda_{max}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon_{0}:=\frac{\lambda_{0}r}{2}\left[\frac{rM}{\lambda_{max}}+\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}\right]^{-1},

(2.7)

where
\displaystyle\pi=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
, then we have
\displaystyle T^{*}\leq 1/\lambda_{max}
.

Remark 2.4
.

1.

We would like to mention that in order to prove the blow up result, the smallness assumption on
\displaystyle\pi\nabla_{x}\pi
as in (
2.7
) is not essentially required on whole
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
. One can possibly replace it by a locally smallness assumption on the domain of dependence.

2.

We note that the condition on
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}
of having a direction such that
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}+r\xi_{0})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})=-\lambda_{0}r\xi_{0}
holds can be replaced by assuming that
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x)
has
\displaystyle d
real eigenvalues in a neighborhood of
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}
has
\displaystyle d
real eigenvalues then it is guaranteed that there exists a direction such that
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}+r\xi_{0})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})=-\lambda_{0}r\xi_{0}
holds. This can be shown in a similar manner as in Lax theory for solving Riemann problem of
\displaystyle n\times n
hyperbolic system in 1-D (see
[
5
,
32
]
).

Since the smallness condition (
2.7
) in Proposition
2.3
is given on the solution, it does not directly serve our purpose to classify the initial data causing finite time blow up of
\displaystyle C^{1}
. We want to replace (
2.7
) by considering smallness on
\displaystyle H^{m}
-norm of
\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{x}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}_{0}
and
\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}
and the blow up result holds true.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})\in C^{1}([0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a solution of isentropic Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) corresponding to the initial data
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
satisfying

\left(\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}_{0}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\cdot\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}_{0}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon_{0}\mbox{ for some integer }m>1+\frac{d}{2},\epsilon_{0}>0.

(2.8)

We assume that
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}
satisfies
(ND)
for some
\displaystyle\lambda_{max}>0
. Then,
\displaystyle T^{*}
is finite provided
\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}<\frac{\lambda^{2}_{max}}{5(\gamma-1)}
.

Remark 2.6
.

1.

We remark that there exists initial data
\displaystyle\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0}
such that it satisfies
(ND)
and (
2.8
) but it fails to satisfy (
2.4
). See Example
5.2
in section

. Therefore, for the
\displaystyle C^{1}
solutions arsing from initial data as in Example

, we can not invoke blow up result of Sideris
[

but Theorem
2.3

).

2.

Similar to (1) of Remark
2.4
, we again mention that one can obtain the blow up result of Theorem
2.5
by using a local smallness condition with a proper use of finite speed of propagation.

We prove Theorem

in a similar technique as Proposition

with the help of an energy estimate of Kato

for
\displaystyle C^{1}

2.3
Global existence of smooth solution

We remark that the result of Theorem
2.5
complements the global existence theory developed in
[
9
,
25
,
43
]
. We recall that if initial data satisfies (i)
\displaystyle\min\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi\geq c>0
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and (ii)
\displaystyle\pi_{0}
is sufficiently small then the global smooth solution exists (see
[
25
]
). As Theorem
2.5
rules out the possibility of global solution when velocity gradient
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}
has at least one negative real eigenvalue, now it is natural to ask whether global solution when
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We answer this question in our next result.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\displaystyle m>1+\frac{d}{2}
and
\displaystyle\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0}
be satisfying

(G-1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{2}\textbf{u}_{0}\in H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(G-2)

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi;\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}\geq 0
where
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\,\left|x\right|=1\}
.

(G-3)

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}
has compact support and furthermore,

\mbox{supp}(\varrho_{0})\subset\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\,\inf\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\left|\xi\right|=1}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi\geq\alpha\right\}\mbox{ for some }\alpha>0.

(2.9)

Then there exists
\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}>0
such that the following holds: for
\displaystyle\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\epsilon_{0}
, there exists a global smooth solution
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})
of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) corresponding to the initial data
\displaystyle(\varrho_{0},\textbf{u}_{0})
and it satisfies,

\left(\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},\textbf{u}-\textbf{v}\right)\in C^{1}([0,\infty);H^{m-1}((\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C([0,\infty);H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(2.10)

where
v
is the global solution to (
1.4
) with initial data
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}
.

Remark 2.8
.

We note that the hypothesis
(G-3)
restricts support of density
\displaystyle\varrho
in a set where
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi
is strictly away from
\displaystyle 0
for all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Therefore, if
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}u_{0}\equiv\textbf{0}_{d}
or
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}u_{0}(x)
is anti-symmetric matrix for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the hypothesis
(G-3)
forces
\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\equiv 0
and in this case the conclusion of Proposition
2.7
holds true trivially.

2.4
Comparison with previous results

As we mentioned earlier Grassin
[
25
]
proved the global existence of smooth solutions if the initial data satisfies (i)
\displaystyle\inf\left\{\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi;\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}\geq c_{0}
for some
\displaystyle c_{0}>0
and (ii)
\displaystyle\varrho_{0}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
is small in Sobolev space
\displaystyle H^{m}
for some
\displaystyle m>1+\frac{d}{2}
. When (i) holds, global solution
\displaystyle\overline{\textbf{u}}
exists for the Cauchy problem (
1.4
). The proof is based on the energy estimate on
\displaystyle\textbf{u}-\overline{\textbf{u}}
and
\displaystyle\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
. This extends the previous results of Serre
[
43
]
and Chemin
[
9
]
. We also refer to
[
42
]
where author studied the smooth solution in negative time as well for finite mass and the solution is surrounded by vacuum. Global existence smooth solutions have been shown in
[
44
]
when initial velocity is close to a linear function in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In all these articles, it has been crucially used the strict positiveness of
\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi
. In Theorem
2.7
, we relax this positiveness by non-negativeness. We could do this by restricting the support of initial density in the set when
\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x):\xi\otimes\xi>0
.

Sideris
[
45
]
showed the finite time breakdown of smooth solution when initial data satisfies the integral condition (
2.4
). As in Theorem
2.2
we show that finite time blow up happens even if we replace the
\displaystyle C^{1}
solution by a continuous one. Note that when we talk about continuous solution it is no longer satisfying the equation in the classical way. We consider a weak solution which is continuous and satisfying the entropy inequality (
1.3
). Theorem
2.2
not only extends the result of Sideris
[
45
]
for continuous entropy solutions, it also tells us that the
\displaystyle C^{1}
solution considered in
[
45
]
loses its smoothness in finite time and besides, it fails to be continuous as well. Our result also tells that the continuous solutions constructed in
[
23
]
can not be global if it the data satisfies (
2.4
).

In the spirit of Sideris
[

In this article, we use the following notations.

•

The notation
\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}^{n}
stands for
\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}dx^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}\cdots dx^{\alpha_{d}}_{d}}
where
\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
and
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{d}=n
.

•

For multi index
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
, we use
\displaystyle\left|\alpha\right|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.

•

For
\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots_{n}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{1}),\tilde{\beta}=(\tilde{\beta}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\beta}_{1})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

\displaystyle c(\beta,\tilde{\beta};\alpha)
as follows

c(\beta,\tilde{\beta};\alpha)=\frac{\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!}{\beta_{1}!\beta_{2}!\cdots\beta_{n}!\tilde{\beta}_{1}!\tilde{\beta}_{2}!\cdots\tilde{\beta}_{n}!}\mbox{ where }\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}+\tilde{\beta}_{i}\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq n.

(3.1)

•

We often use the following inequality which we refer as “
\displaystyle\epsilon
-inequality"

ab\leq\epsilon a^{2}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}b^{2}\mbox{ for }a,b\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }\epsilon>0.

(3.2)

•

Let
\displaystyle p>1,q>1
and
\displaystyle a,b\geq 0
, Then the following inequality holds,

ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{q}}{q}\quad\mbox{ if $\displaystyle p,q$ satisfy }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

This is known as ‘Young’s inequality’.

•

By
\displaystyle W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we denote the standard Sobolev spaces for
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle p\geq 1
. We use the notation
\displaystyle H^{m}=W^{m,2}
.

In the following lemma, we recall the equivalence of various norms in finite dimension.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle m\geq 1,n\geq 1
and
\displaystyle d\geq 1
. There exists
\displaystyle C_{m,n,d}>1
such that the following holds,

\frac{1}{C_{m,n,d}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\left|x_{k}\right|^{n}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\left|x_{k}\right|^{m}\right)^{\frac{n}{m}}\leq C_{m,n,d}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\left|x_{k}\right|^{n}\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.4)

we refer to

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\psi\in C^{3}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the following holds,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla_{x}^{2}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx\leq C_{d}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\psi\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla_{x}^{3}\psi\right|^{2}\,dx.

(4.2)

Proof.

Throughout the proof we use
\displaystyle C_{d}
as a generic constant depending only on dimension
\displaystyle d
. By using integration by parts, we can see that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi(\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi)^{3}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-C_{d}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}\psi(3\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi)^{2}\partial^{3}_{ijj}\psi\,dx.

Applying
\displaystyle\epsilon
-inequality (
3.2
) with
\displaystyle\epsilon=1/2
we obtain,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx+C_{d}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\partial_{i}\psi\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\left|\partial^{3}_{ijj}\psi\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx+C_{d}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\psi\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla_{x}^{3}\psi\right|^{2}\,dx.

Hence, we have

\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi\right|^{4}\,dx\leq C_{d}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\psi\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla_{x}^{3}\psi\right|^{2}\,dx.

By using the inequality (
3.4

4.2

\displaystyle\displaystyle-C_{1}(\pi\nabla_{x}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi+\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi)-C_{1}\sum\limits_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\alpha,\left|\beta_{1}\right|\geq 1,\left|\beta_{2}\right|\geq 1}c(\beta_{1},\beta_{2};\alpha)\partial^{\left|\beta_{1}\right|}_{\beta_{1}}\pi\nabla_{x}\partial^{\left|\beta_{2}\right|}_{\beta_{2}}\pi,

(4.19)

where
\displaystyle c(\alpha_{1},\beta_{2};\alpha)
is as in (
3.1
). Multiplying (
4.19
) with
\displaystyle\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}
and then integrating over
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{d,n}\left(1+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{d,n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\pi\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\pi\right|^{2}\,dx+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx-C_{1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi\nabla_{x}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi\cdot\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}.

(4.20)

Applying integration by parts we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{d,n}\left(1+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{d,n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\pi\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\pi\right|^{2}\,dx+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi\cdot\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\,dx+C_{1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi\dv_{x}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi\,dx.

We further estimate,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{d,n}\left(1+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{d,n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\pi\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\pi\right|^{2}\,dx+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\pi\dv_{x}\partial^{n}_{\alpha}\textbf{u}\,dx.

(4.21)

Combining (
4.18
), (
4.21
) along with the help of (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{d,n}\left(1+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{d,n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\pi\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\pi\right|^{2}\,dx+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C_{d,n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\pi\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\,dx+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla_{x}^{n-k+1}\pi\right|^{2}\right]\,dx.

(4.22)

By using Lemma
4.2
and
4.3
with appropriate choice of
\displaystyle\psi,\phi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{d,n}\left(1+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx.

By using Grönwall’s inequality, we get

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi\right|^{2}\right]\,dx\leq e^{M(t)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\textbf{u}_{0}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{n}\pi_{0}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx,

(4.23)

where

is defined as

(4.24)

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition

In this subsection, we prove the blow up result of Proposition
2.3
. Here, we do not derive the Riccati equation directly. Rather, we compare the velocity
\displaystyle\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)
with the solution of vectorial Burgers equation (
1.4
) with initial data
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}
.

Proof Proposition
2.3
:

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\displaystyle T^{*}>\frac{1}{\lambda_{max}}
. Let us consider
v
to be the solution to the following Cauchy problem,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\textbf{v}+\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{v}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\textbf{v}(0,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\textbf{u}_{0}(x)\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5.2)

Set
\displaystyle\pi:=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
and we consider the following symmetrization of the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\pi+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\pi+C_{1}\pi\dv_{x}\textbf{u}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\textbf{u}+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}+C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(5.4)

where
\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}
. From (
5.1
) and (
5.4
) we have

\partial_{t}(\textbf{u}-\textbf{v})+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}-\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{v}+C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi=0.

Let
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\textbf{u}-\textbf{v}
. Then, we get

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\textbf{u}+\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi=0.

Multiply by
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}
to get

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|^{2}+\nabla\textbf{u}:\boldsymbol{\varphi}\otimes\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}\left|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|^{2}+C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0,

(5.5)

or equivalently,

(\partial_{t}+\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{x})\left(\frac{\left|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|^{2}}{2}\right)=-\nabla\textbf{u}:\boldsymbol{\varphi}\otimes\boldsymbol{\varphi}-C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}.

(5.6)

Now we consider
\displaystyle X(t,x_{0})
defined as

\frac{dX(t,x_{0})}{dt}=\textbf{v}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\mbox{ and }X(0,x_{0})=x_{0}.

We define
\displaystyle g(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left|\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\right|^{2}
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{dg(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\otimes\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t,X(t,x_{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle-C_{1}\pi(t,X(t,x_{0}))\nabla_{x}\pi(t,X(t,x_{0}))\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t,X(t,x_{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\right|a(t)+C_{1}\pi(t,X(t,x_{0}))\left|\nabla_{x}\pi(t,X(t,x_{0}))\right|\sqrt{g(t)}.

Since
\displaystyle T^{*}>1/\lambda_{max}
, we have
\displaystyle M\geq\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,1/\lambda_{max}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and from (
2.7
) it follows that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}\geq\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\pi\nabla_{x}\pi\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}([0,1/\lambda_{max}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Then, we can write

g^{\prime}(t)\leq Mg(t)+\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{g(t)}.

Let
\displaystyle f(t)=\sqrt{g}(t)
. We have

f^{\prime}(t)\leq Mf(t)+\epsilon_{0}\mbox{ or equivalently, }\left(f(t)e^{-Mt}\right)^{\prime}\leq\epsilon_{0}e^{-Mt}.

Subsequently, we get

f(t)\leq e^{Mt}\left[f(0)+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{M}\left(1-e^{Mt}\right)\right]=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{M}\left(e^{Mt}-1\right).

From the assumption on
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}
we have
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}+r\xi)-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})=-\lambda_{0}r\xi
. Let
\displaystyle x_{\xi}=x_{0}+r\xi
,
\displaystyle X(t)=x_{\xi}+t\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{\xi})
and
\displaystyle Y(t)=x_{0}+t\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|X(t)-Y(t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}+t(\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{\xi})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}+2t(\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{\xi})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}))\cdot(x_{\xi}-x_{0})+t^{2}\left|\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{\xi})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}-2\lambda_{0}t\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}+\lambda_{0}^{2}t^{2}\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(1-t\lambda_{0})^{2}\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\textbf{v}=\textbf{u}-\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, we note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{\xi})-\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\textbf{v}(X(t))-\textbf{v}(Y(t)))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\textbf{u}(X(t))-\textbf{u}(Y(t)))-(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\varphi}(X(t))-\boldsymbol{\varphi}(Y(t))).

As
\displaystyle\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{Mt}\epsilon_{0}
for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,1/\lambda_{max}]
, we get

\left|(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\varphi}(X(t))-\boldsymbol{\varphi}(Y(t)))\right|\leq\frac{2e^{Mt}}{M}\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|\epsilon_{0}\leq\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}r\epsilon_{0}.

(5.7)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\textbf{u}(X(t))-\textbf{u}(Y(t)))\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|\int\limits_{0}^{1}(X(t)-Y(t))\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\theta X(t)+(1-\theta)Y(t))\cdot(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\,d\theta\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left|X(t)-Y(t)\right|\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{0}t)\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{0}t)r^{2}M.

Subsequently,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{0}r^{2}=\lambda_{0}\left|x_{\xi}-x_{0}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|(x_{\xi}-x_{0})\cdot(\textbf{v}_{0}(x_{\xi})-\textbf{v}_{0}(x_{0}))\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{0}t)r^{2}M+\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}r\epsilon_{0}.

Therefore, for
\displaystyle t=\frac{1}{\lambda_{max}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{0}r\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{max}}\right)rM+\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{max}-\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{max}}rM+\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{rM}{\lambda_{max}}+\frac{2e^{\frac{M}{\lambda_{max}}}}{M}\right]\epsilon_{0}.

This contradicts with the assumption (
2.7
) on
\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}
. Hence, we prove Proposition
2.3
.
∎

\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla_{x}^{l}(\partial_{k}a_{ij})\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\mathcal{O}(1)R^{-1-l}\mbox{ for }l=0,1,2,3.

Further, we note that
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(R,0)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
. Hence, the initial velocity satisfies (
2.6
) with
\displaystyle\lambda_{max}=1
and we can choose an appropriate
\displaystyle\varrho_{0}
and
\displaystyle R>1
such that (

) is satisfied.

\displaystyle C^{1}

Proof of Theorem
2.5
:

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{u})\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
\displaystyle T>2/\lambda_{max}
. Similar to Proposition
2.3
, we consider the following symmetrization of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in the spirit of
[
40
]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\pi+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\pi+C_{1}\pi\dv_{x}\textbf{u}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\textbf{u}+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}+C_{1}\pi\nabla_{x}\pi

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(5.16)

where
\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}
and
\displaystyle\pi=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
. Let us consider the flow
\displaystyle X(t,x_{0})
defined as follows,

\frac{dX(t,x_{0})}{dt}=\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\mbox{ and }X(0,x_{0})=x_{0}.

By using energy estimate (
4.23
) we obtain

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\textbf{u}(t,\cdot)\right|^{2}\right]\,dx\leq e^{C_{2}t}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\pi_{0}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\textbf{u}_{0}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx.

(5.17)

Then from assumption (
2.8
) and Lemma
3.2
we have the smallness of
\displaystyle\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
as

\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|\nabla^{2}_{x}\pi(t,\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{C_{2}t}\kappa\mbox{ where }\kappa=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\pi_{0}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}^{k+2}\textbf{u}_{0}\right|^{2}\right]\,dx.

(5.18)

Subsequently, from (
5.15
), we obtain

0\leq\pi(t,X(t,x_{0}))\leq e^{C_{2}t}\pi(x_{0}).

(5.19)

From (
2.6
), there exists
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\displaystyle\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

\sup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left[-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}):\xi\otimes\xi\right]=-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0}):\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}=\lambda_{max}.

Applying
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}
on both side of (
5.16
), we have

\partial_{t}\nabla\textbf{u}+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x}\textbf{u}+\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}+C_{1}\pi\nabla^{2}_{x}\pi+C_{1}\nabla_{x}\pi\otimes\nabla_{x}\pi=0.

(5.20)

First we observe that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}]+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}[\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}]+[\nabla^{T}_{x}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}]\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}.

(5.22)

Along the curve
\displaystyle X(t,x_{0})
we have
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}](t,X(t))\right|\leq C_{1}\left|[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}](t,X(t))\right|
. By Grönwall’s lemma we conclude that
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t))=-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}(t,X(t))
since
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})=-\nabla_{x}^{T}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})
. Now, for any
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}[-\nabla\textbf{u}:\xi\otimes\xi]+\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}[-\nabla\textbf{u}:\xi\otimes\xi]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}]:\xi\otimes\xi+C_{1}\pi\nabla^{2}_{x}\pi:\xi\otimes\xi+C_{1}\left|\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}\pi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}]:\xi\otimes\xi+C_{1}\pi\nabla^{2}_{x}\pi:\xi\otimes\xi.

(5.23)

We note that
\displaystyle[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})]:\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}=\lambda_{max}^{2}
since
\displaystyle-\nabla\textbf{u}_{0}(x_{0})\xi_{0}=\lambda_{max}\xi_{0}
. Let
\displaystyle\delta>0
. Since
\displaystyle\textbf{u}\in C^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists
\displaystyle t_{1}>0
such that the following holds

[\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0}))\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0}))]:\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}\geq\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}\right|^{2}-\delta^{2}.

(5.24)

Using (
5.18
), (
5.23
) and (
5.24
) we get

\frac{d}{dt}[-\nabla\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}]\geq\left|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}\right|^{2}-\delta^{2}-C_{1}\kappa\pi(x_{0})e^{2C_{2}t}.

(5.25)

Define
\displaystyle a(t):=[-\nabla\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi_{0}\otimes\xi_{0}]
. For sufficiently small
\displaystyle\delta>0
we have

a^{\prime}(t)\geq a^{2}(t)-2C_{1}\kappa\pi(x_{0})e^{2C_{2}t}.

(5.26)

From the assumption (
2.8
), we have
\displaystyle\kappa\pi_{0}(x_{0})\leq\epsilon_{0}
. Hence,

a^{\prime}(t)\geq a^{2}(t)-2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}.

(5.27)

Next, we consider
\displaystyle b(t)=a^{m}(t)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle b^{\prime}(t)=ma^{m-1}(t)a^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq ma^{m-1}(t)[a^{2}(t)-2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=ma^{m+1}(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}a^{m-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=mb^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}b^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t).

We define
\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=b(t)e^{-At}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=b^{\prime}(t)e^{-At}-Ab(t)e^{-At}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq mb^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-At}-Ab(t)e^{-At}-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}b^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-At}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=mb^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-At-\frac{At}{m}}e^{\frac{At}{m}}-A\Lambda(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}b^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-At}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=m\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{\frac{At}{m}}-A\Lambda(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}e^{2C_{2}t}b^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-At}.

We choose large
\displaystyle A
such that
\displaystyle A\geq 2C_{2}
. Then we have

\Lambda^{\prime}(t)\geq m\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{\frac{At}{m}}-A\Lambda(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}b^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t).

Using
\displaystyle b(t)=\Lambda(t)e^{At}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq m\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{\frac{At}{m}}-A\Lambda(t)-2C_{1}m\epsilon_{0}\Lambda^{1-\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{At-\frac{At}{m}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=m\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{\frac{At}{m}}\left[1-2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}\Lambda^{-\frac{2}{m}}(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}\right]-A\Lambda(t).

We note that
\displaystyle\Lambda^{\frac{2}{m}}(t)=b^{\frac{2}{m}}(t)e^{-\frac{2At}{m}}=a^{2}(t)e^{-\frac{2At}{m}}
. We choose large enough
\displaystyle m>1
such that
\displaystyle e^{\frac{At}{m}}\leq\frac{5}{4}
for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. From the assumption on initial data, we have

2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}\Lambda^{-\frac{2}{m}}(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}=2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}a^{-2}(t)e^{\frac{At}{m}}\leq\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\epsilon_{0}a^{-2}(t).

Since
\displaystyle a^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
, we have
\displaystyle a(t)\geq a(0)=\lambda_{max}-\delta
, from the choice of
\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}
(as in Theorem
2.5
) we get

2C_{1}\epsilon_{0}\Lambda^{-\frac{2}{m}}(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}\leq\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\epsilon_{0}(\lambda_{max}-\delta)^{-2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\mbox{ for small enough }\delta>0.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{3m}{4}\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)-A\Lambda(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{3m}{4}\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t)\left[1-\frac{4A}{3m}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{m}}(t)\right].

For large enough
\displaystyle m>1
, we have
\displaystyle\frac{A}{m}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{m}}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Hence,

\Lambda^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{m}{2}\Lambda^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(t).

(5.28)

Therefore we have

m\left[\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(0)}-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(t)}\right]\geq\frac{mt}{2},

(5.29)

or equivalently,

\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(0)}-\frac{t}{2}.

(5.30)

Recall that
\displaystyle\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(t)=(b(t)e^{-At})^{\frac{1}{m}}=b^{\frac{1}{m}}(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}=a(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}
and
\displaystyle\Lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}(0)=b^{\frac{1}{m}}(0)=a(0)
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{a(t)e^{-\frac{At}{m}}}\leq\frac{1}{a(0)}-\frac{t}{2}\mbox{ for }t\in[0,t_{1}].

(5.31)

Since (
5.31
) remains true for all
\displaystyle m
sufficiently large, we obtain

\frac{1}{a(t)}\leq\frac{1}{a(0)}-\frac{t}{2}\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,t_{1}].

(5.32)

Therefore,

\frac{1}{a(t_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{a(0)}-\frac{t_{1}}{2}.

(5.33)

Let
\displaystyle\nu(t)
be defined as
\displaystyle\nu(t):=\sup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}[-\nabla\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi\otimes\xi]
. We observe that
\displaystyle\nu(0)=a(0)
and
\displaystyle\nu(t_{1})\geq a(t_{1})
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{\nu(t_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{a(t_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{a(0)}-\frac{t_{1}}{2}=\frac{1}{\nu(0)}-\frac{t_{1}}{2}.

(5.34)

Now, we apply the same argument at time
\displaystyle t_{1}
and
\displaystyle x_{1}=X(t_{1},x_{0})
. In this case, we reuse the previous method for direction
\displaystyle\xi_{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfying
\displaystyle\nu(t_{1})=[-\nabla\textbf{u}(t,X(t,x_{0})):\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{1}]
. We note that
\displaystyle\nu(t_{1})\geq\nu(0)
. Hence, there exists
\displaystyle t_{2}\geq 2t_{1}
such that (
5.24
) holds. We repeat the same argument for time
\displaystyle t_{1},t_{2}
we can obtain

\frac{1}{\nu(t_{2})}\leq\frac{1}{\nu(t_{1})}-\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{2}.

(5.35)

Since
\displaystyle\textbf{u},\pi
belong to
\displaystyle C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any
\displaystyle t^{*}\in(0,T)
, we have finitely many points
\displaystyle 0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}=t^{*}
such that

\frac{1}{\nu(t_{j})}\leq\frac{1}{\nu(t_{j-1})}-\frac{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}{2}\mbox{ for all }1\leq j\leq k.

(5.36)

Hence, we get

\frac{1}{\nu(t_{*})}\leq\frac{1}{\nu(0)}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\frac{t_{j}-t_{j-1}}{2}=\frac{1}{\nu(0)}-\frac{t^{*}}{2},

(5.37)

or equivalently,

\nu(t^{*})\geq\frac{2\nu(0)}{2-\nu(0)t^{*}}.

(5.38)

Hence,
\displaystyle\nu(t)\rightarrow\infty
as
\displaystyle t\rightarrow 2/\nu(0)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

On the fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} : Embedding results & applications
Anouar Bahrouni Hlel Missaoui, Hichem Ounaies

Abstract
This paper deals with new continuous and compact embedding theorems for the fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . As an application, using the variational methods, we obtain the existence of nontrivial weak solution for the following Schrödinger equation ( − Δ ) g x , y s ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ g ​ ( x , x , u ) = b ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | p ⁡ ( x ) − 2 ​ u , for all ​ x ∈ ℝ d , (-\Delta)_{g_{x,y}}^{s}u+V(x)g(x,x,u)=b(x)|u|^{p(x)-2}u,\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, where ( − Δ ) g x , y s (-\Delta)_{g_{x,y}}^{s} is the fractional Museilak g x , y g_{x,y} -Laplacian, V V is a potential function, b ∈ L δ ′ ( x ) ( ℝ d ) b\in L^{\delta^{{}^{\prime}}(x)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , and p , δ ∈ C ⁡ ( ℝ d , ( 1 , + ∞ ) ) ∩ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ d ) p,\delta\in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},(1,+\infty)\right)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . We would like to mention that the theory of the fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces is in a developing state and there are few papers in this topic, see [ 6 , 11 , 12 ] . Note that, all these latter works dealt with bounded case and there are no results devoted for the fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Since the embedding results are crucial in applying variational methods, this work will provide a bridge between the fractional Mueislak-Sobolev theory and PDE’s.

Introduction

Fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces (see
[
25
]
) and fractional
Sobolev spaces with variable exponent (see
[
30

) are two
distinct extensions of classical fractional Sobolev spaces (see

[
24

), and they are two special kinds of fractional
Musielak-Sobolev spaces (see
[
11

). The importance of the
Sobolev-type embedding theorems is well known. We refer the reader
to
[
20
,
5
]
for the embedding of fractional
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and to
[
16
,
30
]
for the embeddings of
fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent. The aim of the
present paper is to establish a continuous and a compact embedding
theorems (Strauss theorem) for the fractional Musielak-Sobolev
spaces
\left(W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)\right)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This
is a new research topic.

Precisely, our main contributions are the
following:

(1)

We prove a continuous embedding theorem for the fractional Musielak-Sobolev space in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(2)

We prove a
compact embedding theorem for the space
W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with a weight function.

(3)

We prove a Lions-type lemma to the modular funtion.

(4)

We prove a Strauss compact embedding theorem for the radial fractional Musielak-Sobolev space.

(5)

We obtain the existence of a nontrivial weak solution for a class of this new
kind of nonlocal problems.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work dealing with embedding theorems for the fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Recently, the study of nonlinear equations involving the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
,
0<s<1
, has gained
tremendous popularity due to their intriguing analytic structure and in view of several applications
in different subjects, such as Optimization, Finance, Anomalous Diffusion, Phase
Transition, Flame propagation, Minimal surface. Note that, the adequate framework for these types of
nonlinear equations ( involving the fractional Laplace operator ) is the well-known fractional Sobolev space. For the basic
properties of fractional Sobolev spaces and the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
with applications to partial differential equations, we
refer the interested reader to
[
24

34

and the references therein.

As we mentioned at the begging, the fractional Sobolev space has two distinct extensions. The first one is the fractional Sobolev space
with variable exponent which was firstly introduced in 2017 by Kaufmann et al. in

with variable exponent and the fractional p(x)-Laplace operator. The second extension is the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. These new
spaces built a bridge between the fractional order theory and the Orlicz-Sobolev theory. As far as we know, J. Fernàndez. Bonder et al. firstly introduced
the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
and the new fractional Orlicz g-Laplace operator, see
[
25
]
. After that in 2020, S. Bahrouni, A. M. Salort, A. Cianchi, et al. proved some basic results as

[
11

12

W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)

-Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}_{g_{x,y}}

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{g_{x,y}}u(x):

\displaystyle=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{x,y}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(u)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+s}},\ \ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

uniform convexity, Radon-Riesz property with respect to the modular

Definition 2.1

Let

(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega;

(4)

\displaystyle{G_{x,y}(t)>0}

2.2

Let

be a generalized N-function. In correspondence to

J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{G}_{x}(|u|)dx.

Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda:\ J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

(2.5)

We would like to mention that our assumptions
(g_{1})-(g_{4})
ensure
that
\displaystyle{\left(L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega)}\right)}

reflexive Banach space.

G_{x,y}
be a generalizd N-function,
s\in(0,1)
and
\Omega
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The fractional Musielak-Sobolev space is defined as follows

W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega):\ J_{s,G_{x,y}}(\lambda u)\leq+\infty,\ \text{for some}\ \lambda>0\right\},

where

J_{s,G_{x,y}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G_{x,y}\left(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{d}}.

(2.7)

The space
W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
is endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega)}+\left[u\right]_{s,G_{x,y}},\ \ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega),

(2.8)

with
\left[u\right]_{s,G_{x,y}}
is the so called
(s,G_{x,y})
-Gagliardo seminorm defined by

\left[u\right]_{s,G_{x,y}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda:\ J_{s,G_{x,y}}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

(2.9)

.

Since assumption
(g_{4})
implies that the functions

and
\widetilde{\widehat{G}}_{x}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, the space

is a reflexive and separable Banach space.

Let
\widehat{G}_{x}
be defined as in (
2.1

Definition 2.15
.

be a generalized N-function and

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{g_{x,y}}u(x):

\displaystyle=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{x,y}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(u)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+s}}

\displaystyle=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{x,y}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(u)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+s}},\ \ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle{G_{x,y}(t)=\int_{0}^{|t|}g_{x,y}(\tau)d\tau}
.

Under the assumptions
(g_{1})-(g_{3})
, the operator

and its topological dual space

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)^{s}_{g_{x,y}}u,v\rangle

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove a continuous embedding result for the
fractional Musielak-Sobolev space in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

First, we establish some notions and technical lemmas which are useful in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For each
u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
, we set

\rho(u):=J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}(u)+J_{s,G_{x,y}}(u)

(3.1)

and

\|u\|_{(\Omega)}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0\ :\ \rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\lambda}\bigg)\leq 1\bigg\}.

(3.2)

Remark 3.1
.

For all
u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
, Fatou’s lemma gives that

\rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)\leq 1.

Lemma 3.2
.

\|\cdot\|_{(\Omega)}
is a norm in
W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\|\cdot\|_{(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}
are equivalents, with the relation

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{(\Omega)}\leq 2\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)},\ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega).

(3.3)

Proof.

First, Let’s prove that
\|\cdot\|_{(\Omega)}
is a norm in
W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
. To this end, we show that
\|\cdot\|
verifies the well-known three axioms of the norm:

(\mathbf{i})
It is clear that, if
\|u\|_{(\Omega)}=0
, then
u=0,\ a.a
.

(\mathbf{ii})
For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\alpha u\|_{(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\ \rho\bigg(\frac{\alpha u}{\lambda}\bigg)\leq 1\bigg\}=\inf\bigg\{|\alpha|\lambda>0:\ \rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\lambda}\bigg)\leq 1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=|\alpha|\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\ \rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\lambda}\bigg)\leq 1\bigg\}=|\alpha|\|u\|_{(\Omega)}.

(\mathbf{iii})
Finally for the triangle inequality, let
u,v\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
, we compute

\displaystyle\rho\bigg(\frac{u+v}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho\bigg(\frac{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}+\frac{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\frac{v}{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\rho\bigg(\frac{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)+\rho\bigg(\frac{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\frac{v}{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)+\frac{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\rho\bigg(\frac{v}{\|v\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Thus,

\|u+v\|_{(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{(\Omega)}\ \ ,\ \text{for all}\ u,v\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega).

Second, we show the inequality (
3.3
). On account of this, we prove the left side of the inequality (
3.3
). In this way, by using Remark
3.1
anyone can check that for each
u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)
, we have

J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)\leq\rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)\leq 1\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ J_{s,G_{x,y}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)\leq\rho\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{(\Omega)}}\bigg)\leq 1.

It follows, by (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), that

\|u\|_{L^{\widehat{G}_{x}}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{(\Omega)}\ \ \text{and}\ \ [u]_{s,G_{x,y}}\leq\|u\|_{(\Omega)}.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{(\Omega)},\ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega).

For the right side of the inequality (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\rho\bigg(\frac{u}{2\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}J_{s,G_{x,y}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}J_{\widehat{G}_{x}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{L^{G_{x}}(\Omega)}}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}J_{s,G_{x,y}}\bigg(\frac{u}{[u]_{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1.

Thus,

\|u\|_{(\Omega)}\leq 2\|u\|_{W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega)},\ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{s,G_{x,y}}(\Omega).

This ends the proof Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
(Strauss radial embedding)

Let

. Since

is radially symmetric for all

,

,
|y|>r
, we denote by

the maximum of the integers

such that there exist

Let

,
|y|>r
and choose

as above. Thus, by (

), (

) and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{G}_{x}(u_{n})dx\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma(y)}\int_{B_{r}(y_{i})}\widehat{G}_{x}(u_{n})dx

(6.7)

Putting together (

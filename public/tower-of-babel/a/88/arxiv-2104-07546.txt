Multi-objective Herglotz’ variational principle and cooperative Hamilton-Jacobi systems
Wei Cheng, Kai Zhao and Min Zhou

Abstract
. We study a multi-objective variational problem of Herglotz’ type with cooperative linear coupling. We established the associated Euler-Lagrange equations and the characteristic system for cooperative weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations. We also established the relation of the value functions of this variational problem with the viscosity solutions of cooperative weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations. Comparing to the previous work in stochastic frame, this approach affords a pure deterministic explanation of this problem under more general conditions. We also showed this approach is valid for general linearly coupling matrix for short time.

Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations using a variational approach based on a vectorial form of the Herglotz’ principle. For technical reason, we will mainly discuss this problem under a general setting but a model with wide interests. We suppose our Lagrangians has the form

(1.1)

L^{i}(x,v)-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}u_{j},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

where
\boldsymbol{A}=(a^{i}_{j})
is an
n\times n
real matrix and each
L^{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Lagrangian, i.e., each
L^{i}
is of class
C^{2}
and each
L^{i}(x,\cdot)
is strictly convex and uniformly superlinear. We impose the following conditions on the coupling matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
:

(C1)

a^{i}_{j}\leqslant 0
for all
i\not=j
.

(C2)

The matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
is irreducible.

We remark that Condition (C1) means the matrix
-\boldsymbol{A}
satisfies so called
Kamke-Müller condition
or
cooperativeness condition
in the literature (see
[
21
,
25
,
26
,
9
,
27
]
). We give the definition of irreducible matrix in Appendix. Please refer to Remark
2.1
and Section 5 for the discussion on more general conditions about
\boldsymbol{A}
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, we set

\displaystyle\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}=\{\xi\in W^{1,1}([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{n}):\xi(0)=x,\xi(t)=y\}.

From the theory of ordinary differential equation in the sense of Carathéodory (
[
8
]
), the following equation

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\dot{u}_{1}(\xi,s)=L^{1}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{1}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\\
\dot{u}_{2}(\xi,s)=L^{2}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{2}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\dot{u}_{n}(\xi,s)=L^{n}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{n}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\end{matrix}\right.\qquad\qquad s\in[0,t],

with initial conditions
u_{i}(0)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, admits a unique solution
(u_{1}(\xi,,\cdot),\ldots,u_{n}(\xi,,\cdot))
for each
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
. Similar to the problem of the scalar case considered in
[
6
,
5
]
, we impose the variational problem

\displaystyle\min\big\{u_{i}(\xi,t):\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}\big\}.

It is convenient to write the problem in a compact form. Let

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\\
\vdots\\
u_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{x}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{v}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
\vdots\\
v_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{a}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi\\
\xi\\
\vdots\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v})=\begin{pmatrix}L^{1}(x_{1},v_{1})\\
L^{2}(x_{2},v_{2})\\
\vdots\\
L^{n}(x_{n},v_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore (
1.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}})-\boldsymbol{A}\cdot\boldsymbol{u},\quad\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{a}.

The uniqueness of the solution allows us to solve the equation above into

(1.3)

\boldsymbol{u}(t)=e^{-\boldsymbol{A}t}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{\boldsymbol{A}(s-t)}\cdot\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\ ds,\quad\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{a},

or

\displaystyle u_{i}(\xi,t)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)a_{j}+\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{k,j=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(s)L^{j}(\xi,\dot{\xi})\ ds

where
e^{-\boldsymbol{A}s}=(b^{i}_{j}(s))
and
e^{\boldsymbol{A}s}=(c^{i}_{j}(s))
. Our conditions (C1) and (C2) ensure that each integrand is a time-dependent Tonelli Lagrangian. Thus, we can deduce the existence and regularity of the minimizers for the functional
u^{i}(\xi,t)
by classical Tonelli’s theorem. Notice under our conditions there is no Lavrentiev phenomenon.

Now, we formulate main results of this paper. We always suppose conditions (C1) and (C2) except (d).

(a)

For each
i
, the functional
u_{i}(\xi,t)
admits a solution and each minimizer
\xi_{i}
is of class
C^{2}
. Moreover, the system of minimizers
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equations (Herglotz’ equations)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=L^{i}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

(b)

Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
, the space of bounded uniformly continuous functions, then the value functions of the Bolza problem of Herglotz’ type, defined by

\displaystyle u^{i}(t,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\bigg\{\phi_{i}(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}\big(L^{i}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s)\big)\ ds\bigg\},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

satisfies the weakly coupled system of Hamilton-Jacobi equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D_{t}u^{1}(t,x)+H^{1}(x,D_{x}u^{1}(t,x))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{1}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)=0,\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
D_{t}u^{n}(t,x)+H^{n}(x,D_{x}u^{n}(t,x))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{n}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)=0,\end{cases}

with initial conditions
u^{i}(0,x)=\phi_{i}(x)
, in the sense of viscosity.

(c)

Each value function above also has the representation

\displaystyle u^{i}(t,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\bigg\{\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{k,j=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(s)L^{j}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\ ds\bigg\}.

(d)

If
\boldsymbol{A}
is an arbitrary real matrix with condition (C3) in Section 5, then all the statements in (a), (b) and (c) hold true for small time (see Theorem
5.1
).

[
20
]
also affords some new observation from the recent development of scalar Hamilton-Jacobi equations of contact type.

In the recent work on the weak KAM theory of contact type, there are mainly two ways to study the associated scalar Hamilton-Jacobi equation of contact type. One is the implicit variational principle developed in
[

). In
[
20

, the authors obtained a representation formula for the solution of the Cauchy problem with very general non-linear coupling, using the idea from implicit variational principle in the scalar case. But, one cannot find dynamical systems behind this problem there.

However, the dynamics behind may help us to understand the problem in a much deeper way, which is the main theme of this paper. We developed the characteristic system for the weakly coupled systems and gave a very clear connection between the Hamilton-Jacobi theory and the Lagrange-Hamilton theory in a complete deterministic way. We emphasize this approach indeed tells us some new observation from the viewpoint of dynamical system, geometry, mathematical physics and dynamic game theory under general conditions. The general theory of multi-contact system will be our future work.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we raise a multi-objective variational problem of Herglotz’ type and obtain the regularity and some necessary conditions of optimality such as Euler-Lagrange equations. We also established the characteristic system of this problem. In Section 3, we consider the relevant Bolza problem and prove the value functions of the associated problem is the unique viscosity solutions of the associated weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations. In Section 4, We introduce the Lax-Oleinik evolution of this cooperative Hamilton-Jacobi systems and discuss the relation between positive and negative type Lax-Oleinik evolutions. In Section 5, we obtained similar results on small time interval, removing condition (C1) and (C2) but adding condition (C3) on the Lagrangians. There is a short appendix on some basic knowledge on the cooperativeness condition of the coupling matrix.

2.
A Herglotz-type variational problem for systems

For each
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
L^{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be of class
C^{2}
satisfying the following conditions:

(L1)

L^{i}(x,v,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
is a strictly convex in the
v
-variable for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(L2)

There exist two superlinear nondecreasing functions
\overline{\theta},\theta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
,
\theta(0)=0
and
c_{1}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}_{i}(|v|)\geqslant L^{i}(x,v,0,\ldots,0)\geqslant\theta_{0}(|v|)-c_{1},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(L3)

There exists
K>0
such that

\displaystyle|L^{i}_{u_{j}}(x,v,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})|\leqslant K,\quad(x,v,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},j=1,\ldots,n.

2.1.
A multi-objective variational problem of Herglotz type

Consider an
n
-tuple
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\prod\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
and the following ordinary differential equation in the sense of Carathéodory

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\dot{u}_{1}(s)=L^{1}(\xi_{1}(s),\dot{\xi}_{1}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\\
\dot{u}_{2}(s)=L^{2}(\xi_{2}(s),\dot{\xi}_{2}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\dot{u}_{n}(s)=L^{n}(\xi_{n}(s),\dot{\xi}_{n}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\end{matrix}\right.\qquad\qquad s\in[0,t],

with initial conditions
u_{i}(0)=a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Equation (
2.1
) admits a unique solution
(u_{i},\ldots,u_{n})
where

\displaystyle u_{i}(s)=u_{i}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},s),\quad s\in[0,t],\ i=1,\ldots n.

We denote
L^{i}_{0}(x,v)=L^{i}(x,v,0,\ldots,0)
. It is clear that
L^{i}_{0}(x,v)
is a Tonelli Lagrangian. Each entry of Carathéodory equation (
2.1
) has the form

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{i}(s)=

\displaystyle\,L^{i}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,L^{i}_{0}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}\widehat{L^{i}_{u_{j}}}(s)\cdot u_{j}(s),

where

\displaystyle\widehat{L^{i}_{u_{j}}}(s)=\int^{1}_{0}L^{i}_{u_{j}}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s),\lambda u_{1}(s),\ldots,\lambda u_{n}(s))\ d\lambda.

Let

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{L}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v})=\begin{pmatrix}L^{1}_{0}(x_{1},v_{1})\\
L^{2}_{0}(x_{2},v_{2})\\
\vdots\\
L^{n}_{0}(x_{n},v_{n})\end{pmatrix},\quad\widehat{\boldsymbol{L}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{L^{1}_{u_{1}}},\cdots,\widehat{L^{1}_{u_{n}}}\\
\widehat{L^{1}_{u_{2}}},\cdots,\widehat{L^{2}_{u_{n}}}\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{L^{1}_{u_{n}}},\cdots,\widehat{L^{n}_{u_{n}}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore (
2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{L}_{0}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}})+\widehat{\boldsymbol{L}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{u},\quad\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{a}.

By solving this equation we have

(2.2)

\boldsymbol{u}(t)=e^{\int^{t}_{0}\widehat{\boldsymbol{L}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}\ ds}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{\int^{t}_{s}\widehat{\boldsymbol{L}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}\ ds}\cdot\boldsymbol{L}_{0}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\ ds,\quad\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{a}.

We remark the matrix
\widehat{\boldsymbol{L}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}
is implicitly determined by Carathéodory equation (
2.1
) in general. But the problem becomes much easier when
\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u})
is linear in
\boldsymbol{u}
.

For any
i=1,\ldots,n
we define

\displaystyle J^{*}_{i}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\int^{t}_{0}L^{i}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s))\ ds,

where
u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)
are uniquely determined by (
2.1
). Our task is to find a way to understand the multi-objects optimization problem with respect to the functional
\{J^{*}_{i}\}
for
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
.

Keep in mind the dynamic programming principle for Bolza problem. So, it is natural to use a reduction of the functionals
\{J^{*}_{i}\}
by setting

\displaystyle J_{i}(\xi)=J^{*}_{i}(\xi,\ldots,\xi),\qquad\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}.

Now, our problem becomes minimizing the functional

(2.3)

J_{i}(\xi)\quad\text{for}\quad\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y},

for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\text{minimize}\quad u_{i}(\xi,t)

where
u_{i}
is determined by

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\dot{u}_{1}(s)=L^{1}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\\
\dot{u}_{2}(s)=L^{2}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\dot{u}_{n}(s)=L^{n}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{1}(s),\ldots,u_{n}(s)),\end{matrix}\right.\qquad\qquad s\in[0,t],

with initial data
u_{i}(0)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

2.2.
Linearly coupled systems

The existence and regularity of the minimizer of problem (
2.3
) are absolutely nontrivial in general, not only because of technical difficulty. We will handle this general problem of contact type in the forthcoming paper.

In the current paper, we will deal with a widely studied system with Lagrangians as in (
1.1
), i.e.,

(2.5)

\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}.

where
\boldsymbol{A}=\{a^{i}_{j}\}
is an
n\times n
-matrix with real entries satisfying conditions (C1) and (C2) in the introduction.

For the Lagrangians in the form (
2.5
), by solving Carathéodory equation (
2.4
) in a compact form we have that

(2.6)

\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{\xi},t)=e^{-\boldsymbol{A}t}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{\boldsymbol{A}(s-t)}\cdot\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\ ds,\quad\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{\xi},0)=\boldsymbol{a}.

We denote
e^{-\boldsymbol{A}s}=\{b^{i}_{j}(s)\}
,
e^{\boldsymbol{A}s}=\{c^{i}_{j}(s)\}
and
e^{\boldsymbol{A}(s-t)}=\{d^{i}_{j}(s)\}
. Thus, for each
i
,

\displaystyle J_{i}(\xi)=u_{i}(\xi,\ldots,\xi,t)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)a_{j}+\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,\xi,\dot{\xi})\ ds.

where
\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v)
is a time-dependent Lagrangian defined by

(2.7)

\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v)=\sum^{n}_{k,j=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(s)L^{j}(x,v)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}(x,v).

Remark 2.1
.

We have to verify some points to ensure this variational problem can deduce the original one and we can solve it. Under conditions (C1) and (C2) we ensure that all the entries of
e^{-\boldsymbol{A}t}
are postive for all
t>0
.

–

Under conditions (C1) and (C2) we also ensure that each
\mathbb{L}^{i}
is a time-dependent Tonelli Lagrangian. In particular,
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{L}^{i}(t,x,v)
is dominated by
\mathbb{L}^{i}(t,x,v)
. So, we can have full regularity of the minimizers.

–

Condition (C2) also ensures that our Carathéodory system (
2.4
) cannot be split into sub-systems. Indeed, this condition is not necessary and it just guarantees all the coefficients of the weighted Lagrangians do not vanish.

–

For arbitrary coupling matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
,
e^{\boldsymbol{A}t}\sim I
for
t\ll 1
. Then, we can deal with the problem in a small time interval for the family
\{L^{i}\}
with certain uniform superlinear growth and uniform convexity similarly (see Section 5).

2.2.1.
Herglotz equation

In view of Remark
2.1
, we always suppose conditions (C1) and (C2).

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose conditions
(C1)
and
(C2)
. Then, for each
i
,
u_{i}(\xi,t)
admits a solution and each minimizer
\xi_{i}
of
u_{i}(\xi,t)
is of class
C^{2}
. Moreover, the system of extremals
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equations

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\mathbb{L}_{v}^{i}(s,\xi_{i},\dot{\xi}_{i})=\mathbb{L}_{x}^{i}(s,\xi_{i},\dot{\xi}_{i}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

More precisely, the Euler-Lagrange equations have the form of Herglotz

(2.8)

\frac{d}{ds}L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=L^{i}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

Proof.

Indeed, the associated Euler-Lagrange equations have the form

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)\frac{d}{ds}c^{k}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

Multiplying each
c^{m}_{i}(t)
to the
m
th-line in the equality above and summing up, we obtain

(2.9)

\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{d}{ds}c^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

Recall
(c^{i}_{j}(s))=e^{\boldsymbol{A}s}
and
((c^{i}_{j})^{\prime}(s))=e^{\boldsymbol{A}s}A
. That is

\displaystyle(c^{i}_{j})^{\prime}(s)=\sum^{n}_{k=1}c^{i}_{k}(s)a^{k}_{j}.

It follows

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{d}{ds}c^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)\frac{d}{ds}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}(c^{i}_{j}(s))^{\prime}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)\frac{d}{ds}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}c^{i}_{k}(s)a^{k}_{j}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)).

Thus (
2.9
) becomes

(2.10)

\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)\frac{d}{ds}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}c^{i}_{k}(s)a^{k}_{j}L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)).

This leads to (
2.8
) by multiplying each
b^{m}_{i}(s)
to
m
th-line in (
2.10
) and summing up. This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.2.
Inf-convolution of convex functions

To understand the Hamiltonians
\{\mathbb{H}^{i}\}
’s associated to
\{\mathbb{L}^{i}\}
’s, we need recall more facts from convex analysis (see, for instance,
[
15
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k},g
are
C^{2}
, strictly convex functions with superlinear growth. Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k},\lambda>0
and
f=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}f_{j}
.

(a)

f
is also a
C^{2}
strictly convex function with superlinear growth as well as
f^{*}
, the convex dual of
f
.

(b)

(f^{*})^{*}=f
.

(c)

(\lambda g)^{*}(y)=\lambda g^{*}(y/\lambda)
.

(d)

(f_{1}+\cdots+f_{k})^{*}(y)=(f_{1}^{*}\uplus\cdots\uplus f_{k}^{*})(y)

where
\uplus
stands for the inf-convolution of convex functions defined by

(2.11)

(f_{1}\uplus\cdots\uplus f_{k})(x)=\inf\{f_{1}(x_{1})+\cdots+f_{k}(x_{k}):x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=x\}.

(e)

\nabla(f_{1}\uplus\cdots\uplus f_{k}))(x)=\nabla f_{1}(x_{1}^{*})=\cdots=\nabla f_{k}(x_{k}^{*})
where
(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{k}^{*})
is the unique minimizer in (
2.11
) such that
x_{1}^{*}+\cdots+x_{k}^{*}=x
.

We introduce the Hamiltonians with respect to the Lagrangians
\{L^{i}\}
in (
2.5
). Let for each
i

\displaystyle H^{i}(x,p)=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{p\cdot v-L^{i}(x,v)\}.

Due to Proposition
2.3
, for each
i
, the Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}^{i}
associated to
\mathbb{L}^{i}
in (
2.7
) is

(2.12)

\mathbb{H}^{i}(s,x,p)=\inf\bigg\{\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}(x,q_{j}/d^{i}_{j}(s)):q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\sum^{n}_{j=1}q_{j}=p\bigg\}.

In the definition of
\mathbb{H}^{i}
, we conclude the infimum can be achieved since
\sum^{n}_{j=1}c^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}(x,q_{j}/c^{i}_{j}(s))
as a function of
(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
is superlinear, and the minimizer is unique because
\mathbb{H}^{i}
is smooth. Given
(s,x,p)
, there exists a unique
(q^{1}_{i}(s),\ldots,q^{n}_{i}(s))
such that

(2.13)

\begin{split}p=\sum^{n}_{j=1}q^{i}_{j}(s),\quad\mathbb{H}^{i}(s,x,p)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}(x,q^{i}_{j}(s)/d^{i}_{j}(s)),\\
\mathbb{H}^{i}_{p}(s,x,p)=H^{1}_{p}(x,q^{1}_{i}(s)/d^{i}_{1}(s))=\cdots=H^{n}_{p}(x,q^{n}_{i}(s)/d^{i}_{n}(s))\\
\mathbb{H}^{i}_{x}(s,x,p)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}_{x}(x,q^{j}_{i}(s)/d^{i}_{j}(s)).\end{split}

2.2.3.
Lie equation and characteristic systems

We also write down the Hamiltonian equations with respect to
\mathbb{H}^{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)=\mathbb{H}^{i}_{p}(s,\xi_{i}(s),p_{i}(s)),\\
\dot{p}_{i}(s)=-\mathbb{H}^{i}_{x}(s,\xi_{i}(s),p_{i}(s)).\end{cases}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p_{i}(s):=\mathbb{L}^{i}_{v}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))
be the dual arc. Then

\displaystyle p_{i}(s)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)p^{j}_{i}(s),\qquad i=1,\cdots,n,

where
p^{j}_{i}(s)=L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,n
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{i}(s,\xi_{i}(s),p_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}(\xi_{i}(s),p^{j}_{i}(s)),\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

Remark 2.5
.

The curves
\{d^{i}_{j}(s)p^{j}_{i}(s)\}_{j=1}^{n}
are exactly the minimizers for
\mathbb{H}^{i}(s,\xi(s),p_{i}(s))
in the inf-convolution representation (
2.12
) by (
2.13
), for each
i
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathbb{L}^{i}
, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{i}(s)=\mathbb{L}^{i}_{v}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)p^{j}_{i}(s),

Therefore

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{H}^{i}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\mathbb{L}^{i}_{v}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{L}^{i}_{v}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)))\cdot\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)-\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)\big\{L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))\cdot\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)-L^{j}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)H^{j}(\xi_{i}(s),p^{j}_{i}(s)).

This completes our proof.
∎

By the observation above the associated Hamiltonian systems of (
2.8
) are more complicated. Similar to Theorem
2.2
we have the following characteristic system in Hamiltonian form.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
be the minimizers of (
2.3
) for
\boldsymbol{L}
as in (
2.5
). For each
i,j=1,\cdots,n
, let
p^{j}_{i}(s)=L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(s),\dot{\xi}_{i}(s))
. Then, the curves

and

satisfy

(2.14)

\begin{split}\dot{\xi}_{i}(s)=H_{p}^{j}(\xi_{j}(s),p^{j}_{i}(s)),\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,n,\\
\dot{p}^{i}_{i}(s)=-H^{i}_{x}(\xi_{i}(s),p^{i}_{i}(s))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}p^{j}_{i}(s),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.\\
\end{split}

To obtain a clear picture of the characteristic system, we also need consider the
\boldsymbol{u}
-curves. Comparing to the scalar case, the
\boldsymbol{u}
-curves are much complicated as well as
\boldsymbol{p}
-curves explained in the last subsection. Suppose

(i,j)

In the Hamiltonian formalism, they have the form

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{j}_{i}=p^{j}_{i}\cdot H^{j}_{p}(\xi_{i},p^{j}_{i})-H(\xi_{i},p^{j}_{i})+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{j}_{k}u^{k}_{i},

3.
Weakly coupled system of Hamilton-Jacobi equations

Let
\phi_{i}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. For any
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the set of accessible arcs by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t,x}=\{\xi\in W^{1,1}([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{n}):\xi(t)=x\}.

Consider the following Bolza problem for systems: for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.1)

u^{i}(t,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\left\{\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}(\xi,\dot{\xi})\ ds\right\}

Theorem 3.1
.

Each
u^{i}
defined in (
3.1
) is locally semiconcave,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and
(u^{1},\ldots,u^{n})
satisfies the following weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations

(HJ
{}_{S}-
)

\begin{cases}D_{t}u^{1}(t,x)+H^{1}(x,D_{x}u^{1}(t,x))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{1}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)=0,\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
D_{t}u^{n}(t,x)+H^{n}(x,D_{x}u^{n}(t,x))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{n}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)=0\end{cases}

on
(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the sense of viscosity. Moreover, if
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a point of differentiability for all
u_{i}
’s, then for each
i
we have

\displaystyle D_{x}u^{i}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t))

\displaystyle D_{t}u^{i}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,-H^{i}(\xi_{i}(t),L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t))-\sum^{n}_{k=1}a^{i}_{k}u^{k}(t,x),

where the
C^{2}
curve
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}
is the unique minimizer for
u^{i}(t,x)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

One can rewrite (
3.1
) as

(3.2)

u^{i}(t,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\left\{\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(z)+A_{0,t}(z,x)\right\},

where

\displaystyle A_{0,t}(x,y)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\ ds,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0.

Invoking Lemma 3.1 in
[
4
]
, the infimum in (
3.2
) can be achieved. Observe we represent each
u^{i}
as a family of functions
\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(z)+A_{0,t}(z,x)
(
z
as a parameter). We conclude
u^{i}
is locally semiconcave since for each
z
the function
(t,x)\mapsto\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(z)+A_{0,t}(z,x)
is uniformly semiconcave on any compact subset of
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Theorem 3.4.4 in
[
7
]
.

Now, suppose
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a point of differentiability for all
u_{i}
’s. Applying Theorem 3.4.4 in
[
7
]
again we know there exists a unique
z
such that

\displaystyle u^{i}(t,x)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(z)+A_{0,t}(z,x),

and

\displaystyle D_{t}u^{i}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}(b^{i}_{j}(t))^{\prime}\phi_{j}(z)-\mathbb{H}^{i}(t,\xi_{i}(t),\mathbb{L}^{i}_{v}(t,\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t)))+\sum^{n}_{j=1}(b^{i}_{j}(t))^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{k=1}c^{j}_{k}(s)L^{k}(\xi_{i},\dot{\xi}_{i})\ ds

\displaystyle D_{x}u^{i}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}b^{i}_{j}(t)c^{j}_{k}(t)L^{k}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t)),

where
\xi^{t}_{z,x}
is the unique minimal curve for
A_{0,t}(z,x)
. Observe
\frac{d}{dt}e^{-\boldsymbol{A}t}=-\boldsymbol{A}\cdot e^{-\boldsymbol{A}t}
, i.e.

(3.3)

(b^{i}_{j}(t))^{\prime}=-\sum^{n}_{k=1}a^{i}_{k}b^{k}_{j}(t),\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Thus,

\displaystyle\,\sum^{n}_{j=1}(b^{i}_{j}(t))^{\prime}\phi_{j}(z)+\sum^{n}_{j=1}(b^{i}_{j}(t))^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{k=1}c^{j}_{k}(s)L^{k}(\xi_{i},\dot{\xi}_{i})\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}\bigg\{\sum^{n}_{k=1}b^{j}_{k}(t)\phi_{k}(z)+\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{j}_{k}(t)\int^{t}_{0}\sum^{n}_{k=1}c^{k}_{m}(s)L^{m}(\xi_{i},\dot{\xi}_{i})\ ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)

Thus, by Lemma
2.4
we conclude

(3.4)

D_{x}u^{i}(t,x)=\sum^{n}_{j,k=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(t)L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t))=L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t))

and

(3.5)

\begin{split}D_{t}u^{i}(t,x)=&\,-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)-\sum^{n}_{k,j=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(t)H^{k}(\xi_{k}(t),L^{j}_{v}(\xi_{k}(t),\dot{\xi}_{k}(t)))\\
=&\,-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{i}_{j}u^{j}(t,x)-H^{i}(\xi_{i}(t),L^{i}_{v}(\xi_{i}(t),\dot{\xi}_{i}(t))),\end{split}

since
\sum^{n}_{k=1}b^{i}_{k}(t)c^{k}_{j}(t)=\delta^{i}_{j}
, the Kronecker symbol,
i,j=1,\ldots,n
. Recall that
u^{i}
’s are all locallly semiconcave. Then, the combination of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) together with Proposition 5.3.1 in
[
7
]
completes the proof.
∎

4.
Lax-Oleinik evolution of weakly coupled systems

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>0
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
we will consider two kinds of problem of the following Carathéodory system

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\dot{u}_{1}(\xi,s)=L^{1}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{1}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\\
\dot{u}_{2}(\xi,s)=L^{2}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{2}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\dot{u}_{n}(\xi,s)=L^{n}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{n}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s),\end{matrix}\right.\qquad\qquad s\in[0,t].

Definition 4.1
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>0
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

(4.2)

h_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})=\inf_{\xi}\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s)\big\}\ ds,

where
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
and
\{u_{i}(\xi,\cdot)\}
are determined by (
4.1
) under initial conditions
u_{i}(\xi,0)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. The functions
\{h_{i}(\boldsymbol{L},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})\}
are called the
negative type fundamental solutions
of the weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations.

Similar to the problem in (
4.2
) we can also consider a terminal conditions problem.

Definition 4.2
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>0
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

(4.3)

\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})=\inf_{\xi}\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s)\big\}\ ds,

where
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
and
\{u_{i}(\xi,\cdot)\}
are determined by (
4.1
) under terminal conditions
u_{i}(\xi,t)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. The functions
\{\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})\}
are called the
positive type fundamental solutions
of the weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations.

The existence and regularity of the minimizers for
h_{i}(\boldsymbol{L},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})
have been obtained in Section 2 by reducing the problem to the classical theorem of Tonelli. In fact, we can deal with
\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})
in a similar way. One can also find the direct relations between
h_{i}
and
\breve{h}_{i}
which is useful for our further analysis. For each
i
define
\breve{L}^{i}(x,v)=L^{i}(x,-v)
and denote by
\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}
in vectorial form of the Lagrangians
\{\breve{L}^{i}\}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})=h_{i}(\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,y,x,-\boldsymbol{a}).

Moreover, for any
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}
and
\{u_{i}(\xi,\cdot)\}
determined by (
4.1
) with respect to
\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}
, with initial conditions
u_{i}(\xi,0)=-a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, we define

\displaystyle\eta(s)=\xi(t-s),\quad u_{i}(\eta,s)=-u_{i}(\xi,t-s),\quad s\in[0,t],\ i=1,\ldots,n.

Then, for each
i
,
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}
is a minimal curve for
h_{i}(\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,y,x,-\boldsymbol{a})
with
\{u_{i}(\xi,\cdot)\}
determined by (
4.1
) with initial conditions
u_{i}(\xi,0)=-a_{i}
if and only if
\eta\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
is a minimal curve for
\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})
with
\{u_{i}(\eta,\cdot)\}
determined by (
4.1
) with initial conditions
u_{i}(\eta,t)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>0
. Let
\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}
and let
\{u_{i}(\xi,\cdot)\}
be determined by (
4.1
) with respect to
\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u}
, with initial conditions
u_{i}(\xi,0)=-a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, i.e.,

\displaystyle h_{i}(\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,-\boldsymbol{a})=\inf_{\xi}\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\xi(s),-\dot{\xi}(s))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,s)\big\}\ ds

Notice that
\dot{\eta}(s)=-\dot{\xi}(t-s)
,
u_{i}(\eta,s)=-u_{i}(\xi,t-s)
with
\{u_{i}(\eta,\cdot)\}
determined by (
4.1
) with initial conditions
u_{i}(\eta,t)=a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore

\displaystyle\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\boldsymbol{a})\leqslant

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\eta(s),\dot{\eta}(s))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\eta,s)\big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\eta(t-\tau),\dot{\eta}(t-\tau))-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\eta,t-\tau)\big\}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{0}\big\{L^{i}(\xi(\tau),-\dot{\xi}(\tau))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{i}_{j}u_{j}(\xi,\tau)\big\}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,h_{i}(\breve{\boldsymbol{L}}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,y,x,-\boldsymbol{a}).

The opposite inequality can be obtained similarly.
∎

Now we can define the Lax-Oleinik evolution.

Definition 4.4
.

For any
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and
t>0
, we define

(4.4)

\begin{split}T^{i}_{t}\phi_{i}(x)=&\,\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\phi_{i}(y)+h_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,x,y,\phi_{1}(y),\ldots,\phi_{n}(y))\},\\
\breve{T}^{i}_{t}\phi_{i}(x)=&\,\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\phi_{i}(y)-\breve{h}_{i}(\boldsymbol{L}-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{u},t,y,x,\phi_{1}(y),\ldots,\phi_{n}(y)).\}\end{split}

In a compact form, we set
\boldsymbol{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n})
and write

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}T^{1}_{t}\phi_{1}(x)\\
\cdots\\
T^{n}_{t}\phi_{n}(x)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\breve{\mathbb{T}}_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\breve{T}^{1}_{t}\phi_{1}(x)\\
\cdots\\
\breve{T}^{n}_{t}\phi_{n}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

We call
\mathbb{T}_{t}
and
\breve{\mathbb{T}}_{t}
the
negative
and
positive type Lax-Oleinik evolution
respectively.

As an easy consequence of Lemma
4.3
and the definition of
\mathbb{T}_{t}
and
\breve{\mathbb{T}}_{t}
we have the following consequence.

Corollary 4.5
.

For any
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle-\breve{T}^{i}_{t}\phi_{i}=T^{i}_{t}(-\phi_{i}).

In Section 3, we have already verified that, if all
\phi_{i}
’s are bounded and uniformly continuous, then
u^{i}=T^{i}_{t}\phi_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, are indeed the viscosity solutions of weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations (
HJ

S

-

). This implies the following result with wide interests in the literature.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
. Then
u_{i}(t,x)=-\breve{T}^{i}_{t}\phi_{i}(x)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, are solutions of the following weakly coupled systems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations

(HJ
S_{+}
)

\begin{cases}D_{t}u_{1}(t,x)+H^{1}(x,-D_{x}u_{1}(t,x))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{1}_{j}u_{j}(t,x)=0,\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
D_{t}u_{n}(t,x)+H^{n}(x,-D_{x}u_{n}(t,x))-\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{n}_{j}u_{j}(t,x)=0,\end{cases}

on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with initial conditions
u_{i}(0,x)=-\phi_{i}(x)
, in the sense of viscosity.

5.
Concluding Remark

The method developed in this paper can also be applied for arbitrary real coupling matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
. For arbitrary coupling matrix
A
,
e^{At}\sim I
for
t\ll 1
. Then, we can deal with the problem in a small time interval. We impose a condition as follows.

(C3)

There exist two superlinear functions
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and
c_{0}>0
such that

(i)

\theta_{1}(|v|)\geqslant L^{i}(x,v)\geqslant\theta_{0}(|v|)-c_{0}
for all
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
i=1,\ldots,n
, and there exists
C_{1}>0
such that
\theta_{1}(\nu)\leqslant C_{1}(1+\theta_{0}(\nu))
for all
\nu\in[0,\infty)
.

(ii)

There exists
C_{2}>0
such that
\sum^{n}_{j=1}D^{2}L^{j}(x,\cdot)\leqslant C_{2}D^{2}L^{i}(x,\cdot)
in the sense of distribution for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Recall that
\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v)=\sum^{n}_{j=1}d^{i}_{j}(s)L^{j}(x,v)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\boldsymbol{A}
is arbitrary and condition
(C3)
is satisfied, then there exists
C,\bar{t}>0
such that each
\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, is a time-dependent Lagrangian with
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v)\leqslant C(1+\mathbb{L}^{i}(s,x,v))
on
(0,\bar{t})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In addition the following statements hold true.

(1)

For any
i
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in(0,\bar{t}]
,
u_{i}(\xi,t)
admits a minimizer
\xi_{i}\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
. Any such a minimizer
\xi_{i}
is of class
C^{2}
and satisfies Herglotz equation (

Lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space
Shyuichi IZUMIYA

Abstract
Anti-de Sitter space is the Lorentzian space form with negative curvature. In this paper we consider lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space with general codimension. In particular, we investigate the singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces as an application of the theory of Legendrian singularities.

1
Introduction

Anti-de Sitter space is one of the Lorentz space forms which has rich geometric properties.
It is defined as a pseudo-sphere with negative curvature in semi-Euclidean space with index 2 which admits the biggest symmetry in Riemannian or Lorentz space forms.
Anti-de Sitter space can be naturally considered as a Lorentzian version (generalization) of Hyperbolic space. Recently we discovered interesting geometric properties of submanifolds in Hyperbolic space as an
application of the theory of Legendrian/Lagrangian singularities
[
7
,
12
,
9
,
10
]
.
Therefore anti-de Sitter space might have rich geometric properties comparing with Hyperbolic space.
This is one of the motivations for the investigation of submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space from a mathematical viewpoint.

On the other hand, anti-de Sitter space plays important roles in theoretical physics such as the theory of general relativity, the
string theory and the brane world scenario etc
[
2
,
22
,
30
,
33
]
.
It is well known that Lorentzian space forms are classified into three types depending on the value of the scalar curvature.
One of them is Lorentz-Minkowski space which has zero curvature.
The Lorentz space form with positive curvature is de Sitter space.
Anti-de Sitter space is a Lorentzian space form with negative curvature.
Recently, submanifolds in Lorentz-Minkowski space or de Sitter space have been well investigated (cf.,
[
8
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
19
,
20
]
).
However, there are not so many results on submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space, in particular from the
viewpoint of singularity theory.
The lightlike hypersurfaces (i.e. the light-sheets in physics) are important objects in theoretical physics because they provide good models for different types of horizons
[
4
,
26
]
.
A lightlike hypersurface is generally a ruled hypersurface along a spacelike submanifold with codimension two whose rulings are lightlike geodesics. In this paper we consider lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds with general codimension in anti-de Sitter space.
Moreover, the lightlike hypersurface in anti-de Sitter
5
-space is a very important subject in the brane world scenario
[
17
,
2
,
30
]
.

In the meantime, tools in the theory of singularities
have proven to be useful
description of geometrical properties of submanifolds immersed in
different ambient spaces, from both the local and global viewpoint

[
7
,
8
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
21
]
.
The natural connection between geometry and singularities relies on
the basic fact that the contacts of a submanifold with the models of
the ambient space can be described by means of the analysis of the
singularities of appropriate families of contact functions, or
equivalently, of their associated Legendrian maps
(
[
1
,
27
,
34
]
).
When working in a Lorentz space form, the properties associated to the
contacts of a given submanifold with lightcones have a
special relevance.
In
[
8
,
13
,
18
]
, it was constructed a framework for the study of
spacelike submanifolds with codimension two in Lorentz-Minkowski space or de Sitter space and discovered a Lorentz invariant
concerning their contacts with models related to lightlike hyperplanes.
The geometry described in this framework is called the
lightlike geometry
of spacelike submanifolds
with codimension two.
By using the invariants of lightlike geometry,
the singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds with codimension two in Lorentz-Minkowski space
or de Sitter space
were investigated in
[
11
,
15
,
18
]
.
However, the situation is rather complicated for the general codimensional case.
The main difference from the Euclidean space (or, Hyperbolic space) case is the fiber of the canal hypersurface of a spacelike submanifold is neither connected nor
compact.
In order to avoid the above difficulty, we arbitrarily choose a timelike future directed unit normal vector field
along the spacelike submanifold which always exists for an orientable manifold (cf., §3).
Then we construct the unit spherical normal bundle relative to the above timelike unit normal vector field, which can be considered as a codimension two spacelike canal submanifold of
the ambient space.
Therefore, we can apply the idea of the lightlike geometry of spacelike submanifolds of
the ambient space with codimension two.
In this paper we apply the idea of this framework and the theory of Legendrian singularities to
investigate the singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space with general codimension.

In §3 we construct the framework of the lightlike geometry of spacelike submanifolds with
general codimension analogous to
[
10
]
.
The notion of lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike submanifolds is introduced and the basic properties are investigated in §4.
The notion of the anti-de Sitter height functions families
is useful for the study of lightlike hypersurfaces (cf., §4).
The critical value set of the lightlike hypersurface along a spacelike submanifold is called
the
lightlike focal set
of the submanifold.
In §5 we show that the lightlike focal set of a spacelike submanifold is a point
if and only if the lightlike hypersurface along the submanifold is a subset of a lightcone (Proposition 5.1). Therefore, an anti-de Sitter lightcone is
a model hypersurface of lightlike hypersurfaces.
The geometric meaning of the singularities of lightlike hypersurface is described by
the theory of contact of submanifolds with model hypersurfaces.
Moreover, as an application of the theory of Legendrian singularities, we show that
two lightlike hypersurfaces are locally diffeomorphic if and only if the types of the
contact of spacelike submanifolds with lightcones are the same in the sense of Montaldi
[
27
]
under some generic conditions (Theorem 5.5).
In §6 we describe the case of codimension two as a special case.
We describe the detailed properties of lightlike focal sets of spacelike surfaces in
anti-de Sitter
4
-space.
We also investigate spacelike curves in anti-de Sitter
4
-space as
the simplest case of a higher codimension in §7.

We shall assume throughout the whole paper that all the
maps and manifolds are
C^{\infty}
unless the contrary is explicitly stated.

2
Basic facts and notations on semi-Euclidean space with index 2

In this section we prepare the basic notions on semi-Euclidean
(n+2)-space with index 2. For details of semi-Euclidean geometry,
see
[
29
]
.
Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}=\{(x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})|x_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\ (i=-1,0,\cdots,n)\ \}
be an (n+2)-dimensional vector space. For
any vectors
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=(y_{-1},y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2},
the
pseudo scalar product
of
x
and
y
is defined to be

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\rangle=-x_{-1}y_{-1}-x_{0}y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
. We call
(\mathbb{R}^{n+2},\langle,\rangle)

semi-Euclidean
(n+2)-
space with index 2
and write
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}
instead of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+2},\langle,\rangle)
.
We say that a non-zero vector
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}
is
spacelike
,
null
or
timelike
if

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle>0,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=0
or

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle<0
, respectively. The norm of the vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}
is defined to be
\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|=\sqrt{|\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle|}
.
We define the signature of
x
by

{\rm sign}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1\qquad\quad$\mbox{\boldmath$x$}$\ \mbox{is\ spacelike,}\\
\mbox{}0\qquad\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\ \mbox{is\ null,}\\
-1\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\ \mbox{is\ timelike.}\end{array}\right.

For a non-zero vector
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}
and a real number
c
, we define the

hyperplane with pseudo-normal
n
by

HP(\mbox{\boldmath$n$},c)=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}|\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\rangle=c\}.

We call
HP(\mbox{\boldmath$n$},c)
a
Lorentz hyperplane
, a
semi-Euclidean hyperplane with index 2
or a
null
hyperplane
if
n
is
timelike, spacelike or null

respectively.

We now define an
anti-de Sitter
(n+1)
-space
(briefly,
AdS

(n+1)
-space
) by

AdS^{n+1}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}_{2}^{n+2}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=-1\}=H^{n+1}_{1},

a
unit pseudo
(n+1)
-sphere with index 2
by

S^{n+1}_{2}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{2}^{n+2}\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\rangle=1\}

and a (
closed
)
nullcone
with vertex
a
by

\Lambda_{a}^{n+1}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{2}^{n+2}|\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}-\mbox{\boldmath$a$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}-\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\rangle=0\}.

In particular we denote that
\Lambda^{*}=\Lambda^{n+1}_{0}\setminus\{\mbox{\boldmath$0$}\}
and also call it
the (
open
)
nullcone
. Our main subject in this paper is
AdS^{n+1}
.
Since there are timelike closed curves in
AdS^{n+1}
, the causality of
AdS^{n+1}
is violated.
In order to avoid such a situation, it is usually considered the universal covering
space
\widetilde{AdS}^{n+1}
of
AdS^{n+1}
in physics which is called the
universal Anti de Sitter space
.
We remark that the local structure of these spaces are the same.

For any
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1},\cdots,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}
. We define a
vector
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}
by

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{-1}&-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{0}&\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1}&\cdots&\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{n}\vskip 5.69054pt\\
x^{1}_{-1}&x^{1}_{0}&x^{1}_{1}&\cdots&x^{1}_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x^{n}_{-1}&x^{n}_{0}&x^{n}_{1}&\cdots&x^{n}_{n}\end{vmatrix},

where
\{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{-1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1},\cdots,\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{n}\}
is the canonical
basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{2}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}=(x^{i}_{-1},x^{i}_{0},x^{i}_{1},\cdots,x^{i}_{n})
. We can easily check that

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\ \mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}\rangle=\textrm{det}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1},\cdots,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}),

so that
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{n}
is pseudo-orthogonal to
any
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}
(for
i=1,\cdots,n
).

3
Spacelike submanifolds in anti-de Sitter space

We introduce in this section the basic geometrical
tools for the study of spacelike submanifolds in
the anti-de Sitter
(n+1)
-space.

Consider the orientation of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}

provided by the condition that
\textrm{det}(\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{-1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1},\cdots,\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{n})>0.

This orientation induces the orientation of
x_{-1}x_{0}
-plane,
so that it gives a time
orientation on
AdS^{n+1}
.
If we consider the universal Anti de Sitter space
\widetilde{AdS}^{n+1},
we can determine the
future direction.

We consider a spacelike embedding
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}:U\rightarrow AdS^{n+1}
from an open subset
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{s}

with
s+k=n+1.
We write

M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
and identify
M
and
U
through the embedding
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}.
We
say that
X
is
spacelike
if the tangent space
T_{p}M

consists only spacelike vectors (i.e. spacelike subspace) for any point
p\in M
. In this case, the pseudo-normal
space
N_{p}(M)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}
is a
k+1
-dimensional semi-Euclidean space with index
2

(cf.
[
29
]
). We write
N(M)
as the pseudo-normal bundle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}

over
M.

On the pseudo-normal space
N_{p}(M),
we have two kinds of pseudo spheres:

\displaystyle N_{p}(M;-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in N_{p}(M)\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle=-1\ \}

\displaystyle N_{p}(M;1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in N_{p}(M)\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle=1\ \},

so that we have two unit spherical normal bundles over
M
:

N(M;-1)=\bigcup_{p\in M}N_{p}(M;-1)\ \mbox{and}\ N(M;1)=\bigcup_{p\in M}N_{p}(M;1).

Then we have the Whitney sum decomposition

T{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}|M=TM\oplus N(M).

By definition
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
is one of the timelike unit normal vectors of
M
at
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),
so that

\mbox{\boldmath$X$}\in N_{p}(M).
Since
AdS^{n+1}
is time oriented, we can arbitrarily choose an
adopted unit timelike normal section
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)\in N_{p}(M)
pseudo-orthogonal to

\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
even globally. Here, we say that
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
is
adopted

if

\textrm{det}(\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{n})>0.

Therefore we have the pseudo-orthonormal complement

(\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}})^{\perp}
in
N_{p}(M)

which is a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
N_{p}(M).

We define a
(k-2)
-dimensional spacelike unit sphere in
N_{p}(M)
by

N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]=\{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\in N_{p}(M;1)\ |\ \langle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(p)\rangle=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$},\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)\rangle=0,p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)\ \}.

Then we have a
spacelike unit
k-2
-spherical bundle over
M
with respect to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
defined by

N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]=\bigcup_{p\in M}N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}].

Since we have

T_{(p,\xi)}N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]=T_{p}M\times T_{\xi}N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}],

we have the canonical Riemannian metric on
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}].

We denote the Riemannian metric on
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
by
(G_{ij}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}))_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}.

We now arbitrarily choose (at least locally) a unit
spacelike normal vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}
with
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u)\in N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
, where
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u).

We call
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
an
adopted pair of normal vector fields
along
M.

Clearly, the vectors

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)\pm\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u)
are null.
We define a mapping

\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}):U\longrightarrow\Lambda^{*}

by
\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u).

We call it the
nullcone Gauss image
of
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
with respect to

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}).

With the identification of
M
and
U
through
\mbox{\boldmath$X$},
we have the
linear mapping provided by the derivative of the nullcone Gauss image
\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
at each point
p\in M
as follows:

d_{p}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}):T_{p}M\longrightarrow T_{p}{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{1}=T_{p}M\oplus N_{p}(M).

Consider the orthogonal projections
\pi^{t}:T_{p}M\oplus N_{p}(M)\rightarrow T_{p}(M).
We define

d_{p}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})^{t}=\pi^{t}\circ d_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}).

We call the
linear transformation
S_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})=-d_{p}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})^{t}
the
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-shape
operator
of
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
at
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u).

Let
\{\kappa_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(p)\}_{i=1}^{s}
be the eigenvalues of
S_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
, which are called the
nullcone
principal curvatures with respect to
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
at
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
.
Then the
nullcone Gauss-Kronecker curvature with respect to

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
at
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
is defined by

K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(p)={\rm det}S_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}).

We say that a point
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
is an
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-umbilical
point
if

S_{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})=\kappa(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(p)1_{T_{p}M}.

We say that
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
is
totally

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-umbilical
if all points on
M
are

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-umbilical.
Moreover,
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
is said to be
totally nullcone umbilical
if
it is totally
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-umbilical for any adopted pair
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}).

We deduce now the nullcone Weingarten formula. Since
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}}

(i=1,\dots s)
are spacelike vectors, we have a Riemannian metric
(the
first fundamental form
) on
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)

defined by
ds^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{ij}du_{i}du_{j}
, where

g_{ij}(u)=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}(u)\rangle
for any

u\in U.
We also have the
nullcone second fundamental invariant
with respect to the normal vector field
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
defined
by
h_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u)=\langle-(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{i}}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}(u)\rangle
for any
u\in U.

By similar arguments to those in the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have
the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1

We choose a pseudo-orthonormal frame
\{\mbox{\boldmath$X$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}\}
of
N(M)
with
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}.
Then we have the following nullcone Weingarten formula
:

(a)

\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{i}}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{u_{i}},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}\rangle(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-2}\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{i}},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{\ell}\rangle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{\ell}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}h_{i}^{j}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}

(b)

\pi^{t}\circ\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{i}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{s}h_{i}^{j}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}.

Here
\displaystyle{\left(h_{i}^{j}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\right)=\left(h_{ik}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\right)\left(g^{kj}\right)}

and
\displaystyle{\left(g^{kj}\right)=\left(g_{kj}\right)^{-1}}.

As a consequence of the above proposition, we have an explicit
expression of the nullcone curvature
by

K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})=\frac{\displaystyle{{\rm det}\left(h_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\right)}}{\displaystyle{{\rm det}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\right)}}.

Since
\langle-(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}(u)\rangle=0,
we have

h_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u)=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}}(u)\rangle.
Therefore the nullcone second fundamental
invariant at a point
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
depends only on the values

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0})+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u_{0})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}}(u_{0})
, respectively.
Thus, the nullcone curvatures also depend only on

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0})+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u_{0})
,
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}}(u_{0})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}}(u_{0})
, which are independent of the derivations of the vector fields

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}.
We write
\kappa_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})(p_{0})

(i=1,\dots,s)

and
K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})(u_{0})
as the nullcone curvatures at
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})

with respect to
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})=(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u_{0})).
We
might also say that a point
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
is

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})
-umbilical
because the nullcone
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})
-shape
operator at
p_{0}
depends only on the normal vectors
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0}).

So we denote that
h_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})(u_{0})=h_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})
and

K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})(p_{0})=K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})(p_{0})
,
where
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u_{0})
for some local extension
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)
of
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}.

Analogously, we say that a point
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
is an
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})
-parabolic point
of
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}:U\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{1}
if

K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})(u_{0})=0.
We also say that a point
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})
-flat point
if it is an

(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})
-umbilical point and
K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{0})(u_{0})=0.

On the other hand, we define a map

\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}):N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\longrightarrow\Lambda^{*}

by

\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$},

which we call the
nullcone Gauss image
of
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}].

This map leads us to the notions of curvatures.
Let
T_{(p,\xi)}N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
be the tangent space of
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
at
(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}).

Under the canonical identification
(\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})^{*}T{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2})_{(p,\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$})}=T_{(\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$n$}^{T}(p)+\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$})}{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}\equiv T_{p}{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2},

we have

T_{(p,\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$})}N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]=T_{p}M\oplus T_{\xi}S^{k-2}\subset T_{p}M\oplus N_{p}(M)=T_{p}{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2},

where
T_{\xi}S^{k-2}\subset T_{\xi}N_{p}(M)\equiv N_{p}(M)
and
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u).

Let

\Pi^{t}:\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})^{*}T{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}=TN_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}\longrightarrow TN_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]

be the canonical projection.
Then
we have a linear transformation

S_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{(p,\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$})}=-\Pi^{t}_{\mathbb{LG}(n^{T})(p,\xi)}\circ d_{(p,\xi)}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}):T_{(p,\xi)}N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\longrightarrow T_{(p,\xi)}N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}],

which is called the
nullcone shape operator
of
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
at
(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}).

Let
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
be the eigenvalues of
S_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{(p,\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$})}
,
(i=1,\dots,n-1)
.
Here, we denote
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
,
(i=1,\dots,s)
as the eigenvalues belonging to
the eigenvectors on
T_{p}M

and
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
,
(i=s+1,\dots n-1)
as the eigenvalues belonging to the eigenvectors on
the tangent space of the fiber
of
N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}].

Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2

We choose a
(
local
)
pseudo-orthonormal frame
\{\mbox{\boldmath$X$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}\}
of

with
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}.

For
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})

we have

\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})=\kappa_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})

(i=1,\dots,s)

\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})=-1

(i=s+1,\dots n-1).

Proof.
Since
\{\mbox{\boldmath$X$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame of
N(M),
we have
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\rangle=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\rangle=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{i}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\rangle=0.

Therefore, we have
T_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$}}S^{k-2}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1}(u_{0}),\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-2}(u_{0})\rangle.

By this orthonormal basis of
T_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}}S^{k-2},

the canonical Riemannian metric
G_{ij}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})
is represented by

(G_{ij}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}))=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{ij}(p_{0})&0\\
0&I_{k-2}\end{array}\right),

where
g_{ij}(p_{0})=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}(u_{0})\rangle
.

On the other hand, by Proposition 3.1, we have

-\sum_{j=1}^{s}h^{j}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}=\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{i}}(u_{0})=d_{p_{0}}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\right),

so that we have

Therefore, the representation matrix of

with respect to the basis

\{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{1}}(u_{0}),\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{s}}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1}(u_{0}),\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-2}(u_{0})\}

of

is of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h^{j}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})&*\\
0&-I_{k-2}\end{array}\right).

Thus, the eigenvalues of this matrix are
\lambda_{i}=\kappa_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})
,
(i=1,\dots,s)
and

\lambda_{i}=-1
,
(i=s+1,\dots,n-1)
.
This completes the proof.

(i=1,\dots,s)

of

4
Lightlike hypersurfaces in anti-de Sitter space

We define a hypersurface

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}):N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow AdS^{n+1}

by

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})((p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),\mu)=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\mu(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})(u)=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\mu\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),

where
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),
which is called the
lightlike hypersurface
along
M
relative to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}.

In general, a hypersurface
H\subset AdS^{n+1}
is called a
lightlike hypersurface
if it is tangent to
the lightcone at any regular point.
We remark that
\mathbb{NH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})
is a lightlike hypersurface.

We introduce the notion of height
functions on spacelike submanifold, which is useful for the study of
singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces.
We define a family of functions

H:M\times AdS^{n+1}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

on a spacelike submanifold
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)

by

H(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})=H(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}\rangle+1,

where
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u).

We call

H
the
anti-de Sitter height function
(briefly,
AdS-height function
) on the spacelike submanifold

M.

For any fixed
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1},
we write
h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(p)=H(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})

and have
the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1

Let

be a spacelike submanifold
and

Suppose that

(3)
With condition

(1) For

Since

(i=1,\dots,s)

We set

\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}=\frac{-1}{\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}).

Then we have

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{-1}{\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle-1=1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle+1=0.

This means that
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\in N_{1}(M)_{p_{0}}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}].

we have

{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})+\mu_{0}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})
, where

p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
and
\mu_{0}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle.

For the converse assertion, suppose that
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})+\mu_{0}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}).

Then
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})\in N_{p_{0}}(M)\cap\Lambda^{*}

Thus we have
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})\in N_{p_{0}}(M)\cap\Lambda^{*}\cap T_{p_{0}}AdS^{n+1}.

By the previous arguments, these conditions are equivalent to the condition that

h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(p_{0})=\partial h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}/\partial u_{i}((p_{0})=0

(i=1,\dots,s)
.

(2) By a straightforward calculation, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}(u)=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\rangle.

{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})+\mu_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0})+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})
,
we have

\frac{\partial^{2}h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}(u_{0})=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}}(u_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})\rangle+\mu_{0}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}}(u_{0}),(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0})+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})\rangle.

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}u_{j}},\mbox{\boldmath$X$}\rangle=-\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{i}},\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{j}}\rangle.

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{\ell}}(u_{0})\right)\left(g^{j\ell}(u_{0})\right)=\left(\mu_{0}h^{j}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})(u_{0})-\delta^{j}_{i}\right),

It follows that

In order to understand the geometric meaning of the assertions of Proposition 4.1, we briefly review the theory of Legendrian singularities.
For detailed expressions, see
[
1
,
34
]
.
Let

\pi:PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be the projective cotangent bundle with its canonical contact structure.
We next review the geometric properties of this bundle.
Consider the tangent bundle

\tau:TPT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+})\rightarrow PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})

and the differential map

d\pi:TPT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\rightarrow T{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

of
\pi.

For any
X\in TPT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}),
there exists an element

\alpha\in T^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{1}
such that

\tau(X)=[\alpha].
For an element
V\in T_{x}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}),

the property
\alpha(V)=0
does not depend on the choice of
representative of the class
[\alpha].
Thus we can define the canonical
contact structure on
PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
by

K=\{X\in TPT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\ |\ \tau(X)(d\pi(X))=0\}.

We have a trivialization

PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times P^{n}({\mathbb{R}})^{*},

and call

((v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}),[\xi_{0}:\xi_{1}:\cdots:\xi_{n}])

the
homogeneous coordinates
of
PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}),
where

[\xi_{0}:\xi_{1}:\cdots:\xi_{n}]

are the homogeneous coordinates of the dual projective space

P^{n}({\mathbb{R}})^{*}.

It is easy to show that
X\in K_{(x,[\xi])}
if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mu_{i}\xi_{i}=0,

where

d\tilde{\pi}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mu_{i}\partial/\partial v_{i}.

An immersion
i:L\rightarrow PT^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
is said to be

a Legendrian immersion
if
\text{dim}\,L=n
and
di_{q}(T_{q}L)\subset K_{i(q)}

for any
q\in L.

The map
\pi\circ i
is also called
the Legendrian map
and the set

W(i)=\text{image}\,\pi\circ i
, the
wave front set
of
i.

Moreover,
i
(or, the image of
i
) is called the
Legendrian lift

of
W(i).

Let
F:({\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})\longrightarrow({\mathbb{R}},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})
be a
function germ.
We say that
F
is
a Morse family of hypersurfaces
if the map germ

\Delta^{*}F=\left(F,\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_{k}}\right):({\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})\longrightarrow({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})

is submersive, where
(q,x)=(q_{1},\dots,q_{k},x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$0$}).

In this case we have a smooth
n
-dimensional submanifold

\Sigma_{*}(F)=\Bigl\{(q,x)\in({\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})\ \Bigm|\ F(q,x)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_{1}}(q,x)=\cdots=\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_{k}}(q,x)=0\ \Bigr\}

and the map germ
\mathscr{L}_{F}:(\Sigma_{*}(F),\mbox{\boldmath$0$})\longrightarrow PT^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
defined by

\mathscr{L}_{F}(q,x)=\left(x,\left[\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{0}}(q,x):\cdots:\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{n}}(q,x)\right]\right)

is a Legendrian immersion.
We call
F

a generating family
of
\mathscr{L}_{F}(\Sigma_{*}(F)),

and the wave front set is given by

W(\mathscr{L}_{F})\!=\pi_{n}(\Sigma_{*}(F)),

where
\pi_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the canonical projection.
In the theory of unfoldings of function germs, the wave front set
W(\mathscr{L}_{F})
is called a

discriminant set
of
F,
which we also denote
\mathcal{D}_{F}.

Therefore, Proposition 4.1 asserts that the discriminant set of the
AdS-height function
H
is given by

{\mathcal{D}}_{H}=\Bigl\{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in AdS^{n+1}\Bigm|\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\mu(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}\pm\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})(u),\ p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)\in M,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\in N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}],\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}\ \Bigr\},

which is the image of the lightlike hypersurface along
M
relative to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}.

By the assertion (2) of Proposition 4.1, a singular point of the lightlike hypersurface is
a point
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})+\mu_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})(u_{0})

for

p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})
and

\mu_{0}=1/\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}),

i=1,\dots.s).

Then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2

The critical value of
\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})
is the point

\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}\mathbb{LG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),

where

p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)

\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\not=0.

For a non-zero nullcone principal curvature
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})\not=0,
we
have an open subset

\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\not=0

Therefore, we have a non-zero nullcone principal curvature function

.
We define a mapping

\mathbb{LF}_{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}}:O_{i}\longrightarrow AdS^{n+1}

\mathbb{LF}_{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),

We also define

\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})=\bigcup\left\{\mathbb{LF}_{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\ |\ (p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\in N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\not=0,i=1,\dots,s\right\}.

We call
\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})
the

of
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)

relative to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}.

By definition, the lightlike focal set of
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)

relative to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
is the critical values set of the lightlike hypersurface

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})
along
M
relative to
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}.

We can show that the image of the lightlike hypersurface along
M

timelike normal vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
as a corollary of Proposition 4.1.

Corollary 4.3

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
and
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}

.

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})=\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})=\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{T}).

\Box

Proposition 4.4

Let

For any point

H
at

Proof.

We now show that
the rank of

\smash{\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{\bg B}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{0}&-X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+X_{1}&\cdots&-X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{n}\\

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{0u_{s}}&-X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+X_{1u_{s}}&\cdots&-X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{nu_{s}}\end{array}\right)

is
s+1
at
(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\in\Sigma_{*}(H).

Since
(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\in\Sigma_{*}(H),
we have

\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\mu\left(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(u)\right)

with
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi^{2}_{i}=1,
where

\{\mbox{\boldmath$X$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}_{k-1}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal (local) frame of
N(M).

Without the loss of generality, we assume that
\mu\not=0
and
\xi_{k-1}\not=0.

We denote that

\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)=^{t}\!\!(n^{T}_{-1}(u),n^{T}_{0}(u),\dots n^{T}_{n}(u)),\ \mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(u)=^{t}\!\!(n^{i}_{-1}(u),n_{0}^{i}(u),\dots n^{i}_{n}(u)).

It is enough to show that the rank of
the matrix

\smash{\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{\bg C}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{0}&-X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+X_{1}&\cdots&-X_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{n}\\
X_{-1u_{1}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{0u_{1}}&-X_{-1u_{1}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+X_{1u_{1}}&\cdots&-X_{-1u_{1}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{nu_{1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{0u_{s}}&-X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+X_{1u_{s}}&\cdots&-X_{-1u_{s}}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-X_{nu_{s}}\\

n^{1}_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-n^{1}_{0}&-n^{1}_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}}+n^{1}_{1}&\cdots&-n^{1}_{-1}\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}}-n^{1}_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

is
n+1
at
(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\in\Sigma_{*}(H).

We denote that

\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{i}=^{t}\!\!(x_{i}(u),x_{iu_{1}}(u),\dots x_{iu_{s}}(u),n^{T}_{i}(u),n^{1}_{i}(u),\dots,n^{k-2}_{i}(u)).

\smash{\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{\bg C}}=\left(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1}\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}-\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{0},-\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1}\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}+\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\dots,-\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}+\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\det\smash{\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{\bg C}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{-1}}{\lambda_{-1}}\det(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n})+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}}\det(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{-1}}(-1)\det(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{2},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n})-\cdots-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}(-1)^{n-1}\det(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n-1}).

\delta_{i}=\det(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{-1},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{i-1},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{i+1},\dots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{n})
for

and

\mbox{\boldmath$a$}=(-\delta_{-1},-\delta_{0},-\delta_{1},(-1)^{2}\delta_{2},\dots,(-1)^{n-1}\delta_{n}).

Then we have

\mbox{\boldmath$a$}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$X$}_{u_{s}}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{k-2}.

We remark that
\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\not=0
and
\mbox{\boldmath$a$}=\pm\|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\|\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{k-1}.

By the above calculation, we have

\displaystyle\det\smash{\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{\bg C}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\lambda{-1}}{\lambda_{-1}},\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{-1}},\dots,\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{-1}}\right),\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{-1}}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\mu\left(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(u)\right),\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{-1}}\times\pm\mu\xi_{k-1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\|=\pm\frac{\mu\xi_{k-1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\|}{\lambda_{-1}}\not=0.

Therefore the Jacobi matrix of
\Delta^{*}H

is non-singular at
(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\in\Delta^{*}H^{-1}(0).

For other local coordinates of
AdS^{n+1}
, we can apply the same method for the proof as the above case.
This completes the proof.

\Box

Here we also consider the local coordinate

U^{+}_{-1}

\mathscr{L}_{H}:\Sigma_{*}(H)\longrightarrow PT^{*}(AdS^{n+1})|U^{+}_{-1}

\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{0}}(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\\

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{0}}(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}):\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{1}}(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}):\cdots:\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{n}}(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\right]

Therefore, we have

\mathscr{L}_{H}(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})=(\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$},[X_{-1}(u)\lambda_{0}-X_{0}(u)\lambda_{-1}:X_{1}(u)\lambda_{-1}-X_{-1}(u)\lambda_{1}:\cdots:X_{n}(u)\lambda_{-1}-X_{-1}(u)\lambda_{n}]),

where

\Sigma_{*}(H)=\{(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})\ |\ \mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$},t)\ ((p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),t)\in N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}}\}.

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})
.
Therefore we say that the AdS-height

For other local coordinates of

5
Contact with lightcones

In this section we consider the geometric meaning of the singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces in Anti-de Sitter space from the view point of the theory of contact of submanifolds with model hypersurfaces in
[
27
]
.
We begin with the following basic observations.

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1}
and
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
a spacelike submanifold without points
satisfying
K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=0.

Then
M\subset\Lambda^{n+1}_{\lambda_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}

if and only if
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\}=\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})
is the lightcone focal set.
In this case we have
\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}])\subset\Lambda^{n+1}_{\lambda_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}

and
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
is totally nullcone umbilical.

Proof.
By Proposition 3.1,
K_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})\not=0
if and only if

\{(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}),(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{1}},\dots,(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}+\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S})_{u_{s}}\}

is linearly independent for
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})\in M
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u_{0}),

where
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}:U\longrightarrow N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
is a local section.
By the proof of the assertion (1) of Proposition 4.1,

M\subset\Lambda^{n+1}_{\lambda_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}

if and only if
h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(u)=0
for any
u\in U,

where
h_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(u)=H(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})
is the AdS-height function on
M.

It also follows from Proposition 4.1 that there exists a smooth function
\eta:U\times N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and section
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}:U\longrightarrow N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]
such that

\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)=\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}+\eta(u,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u))(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)\pm\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u)).

In fact, we have
\eta(u,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u))=-1/\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})

i=1,\dots,s
, where

p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u).

It follows that
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{j}(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),
so that

M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
is totally nullcone umbilical.
Therefore we have

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u),\mu)=\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}+(\mu+\eta(u,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u))(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u)\pm\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{S}(u)).

Hence we have

\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})\subset\Lambda^{n+1}_{\lambda_{0}}.

By Corollary 4.2, the critical value set of
\mathbb{LH}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]\times{\mathbb{R}})
is the lightlike focal set

\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}).

However, it is equal to
\lambda_{0}
by the previous arguments.

For the converse assertion, suppose that
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mathbb{LF}_{M}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}).

Then we have

\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})}\mathbb{LG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}),

for any
i=1,\dots,s
and
(p,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\in N_{1}(M)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}],
where
p=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u).

Thus, we have

\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})=\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{j}(\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})

for any
i,j=1,\dots,s,
so that
M
is totally nullcone umbilical.
Since
\mathbb{LG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(u,\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})
is null, we have
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u)\in\Lambda^{n+1}_{\lambda_{0}}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

According to the above proposition,
\Lambda^{n+1}_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}
is regarded as a model lightlike hypersurface in
AdS^{n+1}.

We define

T(AdS^{n+1})_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+2}_{2}\ |\ \ \mbox{\boldmath$x$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in T_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}AdS^{n+1}\ \},

where
T_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}AdS^{n+1}
is the tangent space of
AdS^{n+1}
at
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1}.

We call
T(AdS^{n+1})_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}
a
tangent affine space
of
AdS^{n+1}
at
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1}.

It is easy to show that

\Lambda^{n+1}_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}=T(AdS^{n+1})_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}.

We denote that
LC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(AdS^{n+1})=\Lambda^{n+1}_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}=T(AdS^{n+1})_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}\cap AdS^{n+1}
,
which is called an
AdS-lightcone
with the vertex
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1}.

Therefore, the model lightlike hypersurface is an AdS-lightcone.

We consider the contact of
spacelike submanifolds with AdS-lightcones.
Let

{\mathcal{H}}:AdS^{n+1}\times AdS^{n+1}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

be a function defined
by
{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$})=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}\rangle+1.

Given
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}\in AdS^{n+1},

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\lambda_{0}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})={\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})
,
so that we have
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\lambda_{0}}^{-1}(0)=LC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(AdS^{n+1}).

For any
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})\in M
,
\mu_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}\in N_{1}(M)_{p}[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}],
we consider the point

\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0})+\mu_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(u_{0})+\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}).

Then we have

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\lambda_{0}}\circ\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0}))={\mathcal{H}}\circ(\mbox{\boldmath$X$}\times 1_{AdS^{n+1}})(u_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})=H(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})=0,

where
\mu_{0}=1/\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}),

i=1,\dots,s.

We also have relations

\frac{\partial{\mathfrak{h}}_{\lambda_{0}}\circ\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}{\partial u_{i}}(u_{0})=\frac{\partial H}{\partial u_{i}}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0})=0,\ i=1,\dots,s.

These imply that the AdS-lightcone
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\lambda_{0}}^{-1}(0)=LC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(AdS^{n+1})
is tangent to
M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
at
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0}).

In this case, we call
LC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(AdS^{n+1})
a
tangent AdS-lightcone
of

M=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(U)
at
p_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$X$}(u_{0}),
which is denoted by
TLC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(M)_{p_{0}}.

Moreover, the tangent AdS-lightcone
TLC_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}}(M)_{p_{0}}
is called an

osculating AdS-lightcone
if
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}=\mathbb{LF}_{\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\xi$}_{0})}(u_{0})\in\mathbb{LF}_{M},
for one of the nullcone principal curvature
\kappa_{N}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})_{i}(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{0}).

In this case, we call
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}_{0}
the
center of the nullcone principal curvature

7
Spacelike curves in
AdS^{4}

In this section we consider lightlike hypersurfaces along spacelike curves in
AdS^{4}
as
the simplest case of higher codimension.
Let
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}:I\longrightarrow AdS^{4}
be a spacelike curve with
\|\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)\|\neq-1.
In this case we write
C=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(I)
instead of
M=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(I).
Since
\|\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s)\|>0,
we can reparameterize it by the arc-length s. So we have the unit tangent vector
\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s)
of
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s).
Moreover we have two unit normal vectors
\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)=\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)}{\|\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)\|}},

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s)=\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{\prime}_{1}(s)+\delta\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)}{\|\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{\prime}_{1}(s)+\delta\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)\|}}
under the conditions that
\kappa_{1}(s)=\|\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)\|\neq 0,

\kappa_{2}(s)=\|\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{\prime}_{1}(s)+\delta k_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)\|\neq 0,
where
\delta_{i}={\rm sign}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(s))
and
{\rm sign}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(s))
is the signature of
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{i}(s)

(i=1,2,3).
Then we have another unit normal vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s)
defined by
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s).
Therefore we can construct a pseudo-orthogonal frame
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s)\},
which satisfies the
Frenet-Serret type formulae
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$t$}^{\prime}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)+\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}^{\prime}(s)=-\delta_{1}\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\kappa_{2}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}^{\prime}(s)=\delta_{3}\kappa_{2}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)+\kappa_{3}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}^{\prime}(s)=\delta_{1}\kappa_{3}(s)n_{2}(s),\end{array}\right.

where
\kappa_{2}(s)=\delta_{2}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s)\rangle
and
\kappa_{3}(s)=\delta_{3}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s)\rangle.

Since
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)
is timelike and
\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)
is spacelike, we distinguish the following three cases:

Case 1:
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)
is timelike, that is,
\delta_{1}=-1
and
\delta_{2}=\delta_{3}=1.

Case 2:
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s)
is timelike, that is,
\delta_{2}=-1
and
\delta_{1}=\delta_{3}=1.

Case 3:
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s)
is timelike, that is,
\delta_{3}=-1
and
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1.

We consider the lightlike hypersurface along
C,
and calculate the anti-de Sitter height function on
C
which is useful for the study the singularities of lightlike hypersurfaces in the each case.

7.1
Case 1

Suppose that
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)
is timelike. In this case we adopt
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s)
and denote that
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{3}(s).
Then we have the pseudo-orthogonal frame

\{\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s)\},

\delta_{1}=-1
and
\delta_{2}=\delta_{3}=1
, which satisfies the following Frenet-Serret type formulae:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$t$}^{\prime}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)+\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}}^{\prime}(s)=\kappa_{1}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\kappa_{2}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\kappa_{2}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s)+\kappa_{3}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}^{\prime}(s)=-\kappa_{3}(s)\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s).\end{array}\right.

Since
N_{1}(C)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]

N_{1}(C)[\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}]=\{(\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$})\in\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{*}T{\mathbb{R}}^{5}_{1}\ |\ \mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}=\cos\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s)+\sin\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s)\in N_{\mbox{\scriptsize\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)}(C),\ s\in I\},

\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(s,\theta)=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s)+\cos\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s)+\sin\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s).

Then we have the lightlike hypersurface along
C

\mathbb{LH}_{C}((s,\theta),\mu)=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)+\mu(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}(s)+\cos\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{1}(s)+\sin\theta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{2}(s))=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)+\mu\mathbb{NG}(\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T})(s,\theta).

We remark that the image of this lightlike hypersurface along
C
is independent of the choice of the future directed timelike normal vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{T}
by Corollary 4.3.

H:I\times AdS^{4}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
on a spacelike curve
C=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(I)

-X^{\prime}_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{1}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{2}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{3}^{\prime}(s_{0})\\

-X^{\prime\prime\prime}_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})\end{array}\right).

Here we consider the following matrix B,

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-X_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}(s_{0})&X_{1}(s_{0})&X_{2}(s_{0})&X_{3}(s_{0})\\
-X^{\prime}_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{1}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{2}^{\prime}(s_{0})&X_{3}^{\prime}(s_{0})\\
-X^{\prime\prime}_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{2}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{3}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})\\
-X^{\prime\prime\prime}_{-1}(s_{0})&-X_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})&X_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})\\
-n_{-1}(s_{0})&-n_{0}(s_{0})&n_{1}(s_{0})&n_{2}(s_{0})&n_{3}(s_{0})\\
\end{array}\right),

where we denote
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}(s_{0})=(n_{-1}(s_{0}),n_{0}(s_{0}),n_{1}(s_{0}),n_{2}(s_{0}),n_{3}(s_{0})).

In fact,

\displaystyle{\rm det}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{t}(\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{t}(\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{t}(\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)-\kappa_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-\kappa_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}),

where
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}_{1}(s)}{\kappa_{1}(s)}\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)+(\delta_{1}\kappa^{2}_{1}(s)-1)\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s)+(1-\frac{\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(s)}{\kappa_{1}})\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s),

and
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}(s)-\kappa_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1},

\mbox{\boldmath$b$}-\kappa_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{1}
are linearly independent each other in all Case 1,2,3, respectively. Therefore we have detB
\neq 0
. This means that
{\rm rankA}=4.

This completes the proof.

\Box

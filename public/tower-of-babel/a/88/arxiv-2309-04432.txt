Nonlinear stability of static Néel walls in ferromagnetic thin films
Antonio Capella, Christof Melcher, Lauro Morales, Ramón G. Plaza

Abstract
. The paper establishes the nonlinear (orbital) stability of static 180 180 -degree Néel walls in ferromagnetic films under the reduced wave-type dynamics for the in-plane magnetization proposed by Capella, Melcher, and Otto [ CMO07 ] . The result follows from the spectral analysis of the linearized operator, which features a challenging non-local operator. As part of the proof, we show that the linearized non-local linearized operator is a compact perturbation of a suitable non-local linear operator at infinity, a result interesting in itself.



1.
Introduction

In order to study the motion of magnetization vectors in ferromagnetic materials, in 1935 Landau and Lifshitz
[
LL35
]
introduced a model system of equations, later reformulated and re-derived by Gilbert
[
Gil55
,
Gil04
]
, which constitutes the fundamental and best accepted mathematical model that describes the magnetization in ferromagnets. Since ferromagnetic thin films exhibit a wide range of applications to the design and manufacturing of magnetic storage devices, the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) model has attracted a great deal of attention from physicists and mathematicians alike in the last decades. A great variety of patterns of magnetization vectors appear in ferromagnetic films. For instance, narrow transition regions between opposite magnetization domains are called
domain walls
. Some of the most common wall types in such materials are called Néel walls, separating two opposite magnetization regions by an in-plane rotation, oriented along an axis; Bloch walls, for which the magnetization rotates about the normal of the domain wall, pointing along the domain wall plane in a 3D system; or Walker walls, which are formed under the presence of an external magnetic field (see, e.g., Hubert and Schäfer
[
HS98
]
for further information).

One of the main objectives of recent mathematical studies is to understand the behavior of these dynamical coherent structures developed by the magnetization of a ferromagnet. The stability under small perturbations of these microstructures is important, not only to validate the mathematical model but also to enhance the numerical simulations performed by physicists and engineers to optimize and design new ferromagnetic materials (see, e.g.,
[
LB99
]
). Up to our knowledge, the literature on the dynamical stability theory for magnetic domain walls is scarce. The stability of one-dimensional Bloch walls has been addressed by Krukowski
[
Kru87
]
using a spectral (linearized) calculation of energies of ground states, and by Carbou and Labbé
[
CL06
]
, under the nanowire, one-dimensional approximation by Sanchez
[
San09
]
. Takasao
[
Tak11
]
improved this last result for Walker walls, also in one dimension and in the presence of an external magnetic field. Carbou
[
Car10
]
proved the stability of a Walker wall in the three-dimensional model using the energy method and under a simplifying assumption that gets rid of the non-local part of the operator. Most of these works employ energy methods to conclude stability, that is, the analyses are based on performing
a priori
energy estimates on the equations of magnetization evolution and relying on their intrinsic structure.

This paper is devoted to studying the dynamical stability of static Néel walls. Our departure point is the one-dimensional thin film reduction of the micromagnetic energy proposed by Capella, Melcher, and Otto
[
CMO07
]
(outlined previously in
[
MO06
]
for numerical purposes), which establishes an effective system for the in-plane magnetization by taking the thin film layer limit. The resulting system underlies a wave-type dynamics for the Néel wall’s phase. The authors prove the existence and uniqueness of a static Néel wall’s phase profile in the absence of external fields, as well as the emergence of traveling wave solutions near the static profile under the influence of a small constant external forcing. The authors also outline the stability of these structures under small one-dimensional perturbations. The present analysis constitutes a follow-up of such formulation and a full study of the nonlinear stability of the static Néel wall under small, one-dimensional perturbations of the phase itself. As far as we know, this problem has not been studied before in the literature.

One of the main technical difficulties pertains to the non-locality of the dynamical equation, even at a linear level. In contrast to previous studies, we adopt a spectral approach to the problem. Motivated by the ideas in
[
CMO07
]
, in which the linearized operator around the static phase is defined and previously studied, we profit from this information and perform a full spectral stability analysis of this operator, that includes a proof of its relative compactness with respect to an asymptotic operator. In contrast with standard techniques, which are usually applied to local differential operators with bounded coefficients and which are based on truncating such coefficients with their asymptotic limits (see, e.g.,
[
KP13
]
, Section 3.1), in this work and by necessity (because we are studying a non-local operator) we develop a novel procedure that focuses on describing totally bounded sets in terms of
L^{2}
-equicontinuity and uniform decay in Fourier space (see Theorem
4.3
below). This relative compactness plays a crucial role in the location of the essential spectrum of a block matrix operator matrix that encodes the linearization of the nonlinear wave equation for perturbations of the static wall. It is proved that both essential and point spectra are stable, that is, they belong to the stable half-plane of complex numbers with negative real part, except for the origin, which is associated with translations of the Néel wall (see Theorem
5.1
). An important feature is the presence of an
spectral gap
, which is a positive distance from the eigenvalue zero to the rest of the spectrum. This allows us to establish the exponential decay of the solutions to the spectral problem when projected outside the one-dimensional vector space generated by translations of the static profile. Upon application of the well-known Gearhart-Prüss theorem
[
CL03
,
EN00
]
and after the establishment of uniform resolvent estimates, we conclude that the semigroup generated by the linear block matrix operator is exponentially decaying in the appropriate subspace. This information is then used to prove nonlinear stability. For that purpose, we apply an abstract result, due originally to Sattinger
[
Sat76
]
and adapted to a Hilbert space setting by Lattanzio
et al.

[
LMPS16
]
, that establishes nonlinear stability from spectral stability by controlling the growth of nonlinear terms and profiting from the fact that the manifold generated by the wave is one-dimensional (the group of translations). We regard our contributions not only new in the context of ferromagnetic wall stability analysis, but also of methodological nature: we advocate for spectral and nonlinear analysis as a feasible and effective method in the study of this type of problems. The unpublished note by Huber
[
Hub
]
warrants note as the only work (as far as we know) that performs a rigorous spectral analysis of the linearized operator around a Néel wall for a layer of small (but positive) thickness,
\epsilon>0
. Huber does not prove the spectral stability of this structure but employs the spectral information to obtain time-periodic solutions in a vicinity of it. (We note that in layers with positive thickness, the linearized operators are sectorial, in contrast with the present case of a thin-film limit.)

Plan of the paper

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
contains a brief description of the thin-film dynamical model in
[
CMO07
]
, recalls some of the main properties of the static Néel wall’s phase, and states the main result of this paper. Section
3
presents a general overview of the proof’s strategy. Section
4
is devoted to study of the linearized (scalar) operator around the static Néel wall defined in
[
CMO07
]
. In particular, it is shown that it is relatively compact to an asymptotic operator, a feature that plays a key role in the stability analysis. Section
5
establishes the spectral stability of the Néel wall’s phase. The spectral problem is posed in terms of a block operator and the stability of both its essential and point spectra is established. Section
6
is devoted to proving the existence of an associated semigroup to the dynamic problem and showing the exponential decay of solutions to the linearized equations outside a one-dimensional space related to profile translations. The final Section
7
contains the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

2.
Preliminaries and main result

2.1.
The micromagnetic model

The Landau and Lifshitz continuum theory of ferromagnetic materials
[
LL35
]
is based on a magnetization field
{\bf m}:\tilde{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, that represents the local average magnetic moment, and a variational principle in terms of the
micromagnetic energy
. In the absence of an external field, the micromagnetic energy is given by

\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(d^{2}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|\nabla\mathbf{m}|^{2}\,dx+Q\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\Phi(\mathbf{m})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla U|^{2}\Big).

where
d>0
and
Q>0
are constants. The first term is known as the
exchange energy
and it quantifies the energy due to magnetic-dipole interactions in the sample. So,
d
is called as the
exchange length
. The second term penalizes crystalline anisotropy via
\Phi
which usually has the form of an even polynomial in
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Then,
Q
measures the relative strength of anisotropy penalization. Finally, the last term in the expression of
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m})
is called the
stray energy
. The term
\nabla U
is called the
stray field
and it is uniquely defined via the distribution equation
\Delta U=\textrm{div}\,(\mathbf{m}\boldsymbol{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}})
(
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function of the set
A
).
The stray-field energy favors vanishing distributional divergence, namely,
\textrm{div}{\bf m}=0
in
\tilde{\Omega}
and
{\bf m}\cdot n=0
on
\partial\tilde{\Omega}
, where
n
is the outward normal to the boundary.
The combination of the stray-field energy (which is a non-local term) and the non-convex saturation constraint
|{\bf m}|=1
gives rise to pattern formation among magnetic domains where the magnetization is almost constant. Thin transition layers separating the magnetic domains are known as domain walls and may form complex patterns
[
HS98
]
.

2.2.
Stationary Néel wall profile in soft magnetic thin films

A thin film is an infinitely extended magnetic material
\tilde{\Omega}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\delta)
where
\delta\ll d
. In this regime, it is safe to assume that the magnetization is independent of the
x_{3}
variable. We assume further that the magnetization is
\ell
-periodic in the
{\bf e}_{2}
direction, namely,

{\bf m}(x_{1},x_{2}+\ell)=\mathbf{m}(x_{1},x_{2})\quad\text{for any }x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

that the material has a uniaxial anisotropy in the
e_{2}
direction, with
\Phi({\bf m})=1-m_{2}^{2}
. We consider transition layers connecting antipodal states on the easy axis

{\bf m}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\quad\text{ with }\mathbf{m}(\pm\infty,x_{2})=(0,\pm 1,0)\quad\text{for any }x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

In this case, the stray energy is approximated at leading order by

E_{stray}({\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{--}\hskip-9.0pt\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\left||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{H}}(m)\right|^{2}+m_{3}^{2}\right)dx,

where
{\bf m}=(m,m_{3})
with
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
and formally
{\mathcal{H}}(m)=\nabla\Delta^{-1}\text{div }m
see
[
CMO07
]
. Thus, the micromagnetic energy becomes

\mathbb{E}({\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{--}\hskip-9.0pt\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(d^{2}|\nabla m|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\left||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{H}}(m)\right|^{2}+Q(1-m_{2})^{2}+m_{3}^{2}\right)dx.

Néel walls are one-dimensional transition layers observed in soft ferromagnetic thin films, that is, magnetic materials with relatively weak anisotropic energy. Here, we consider a parameter regime of soft thin films so that the anisotropy and relative thickness are balanced, more precisely

(2.1)

Q\ll 1,\quad\kappa^{-1}=\delta/d\ll 1\quad\text{while }{\mathcal{Q}}=4\kappa^{2}Q.

Therefore, it is feasible to introduce the small parameter
\varepsilon=\sqrt{Q}
. By rescaling the length
x
by
w=\delta/(2Q)
, and the energy by
\delta/2
, the micromagnetic energy becomes

(2.2)

E_{\varepsilon}({\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{--}\hskip-9.0pt\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left({\mathcal{Q}}|\nabla m|^{2}+\left||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{H}}(m)\right|^{2}+(1-m_{2})^{2}+\left(\frac{m_{3}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\right)dx,

where
L=\ell/w
and we assumed
\varepsilon\ll{\mathcal{Q}}\lesssim 1
.
Assuming further that
m=m(x_{1})
then
{\mathcal{H}}(m)=m_{1}{\bf e}_{1}
is independent of
x_{2}
and the reduced variational principle for the one-dimensional wall transition is

(2.3)

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}({\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\Big({\mathcal{Q}}|{\bf m}^{\prime}|^{2}+||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}m_{1}|^{2}+(1-m_{2})^{2}+\left(\frac{m_{3}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\Big)dx\to\min,

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle m}:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\qquad\text{with}\;\;{\bf m}(\pm\infty)=(0,\pm 1,0),

where
{\bf m}^{\prime}=\frac{d{\bf m}}{dx_{1}}
.
In
[
GC04
]
it is shown that for
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
there exists a sequence of minimizers
{\bf m}_{\varepsilon_{k}}
of
(
2.3
) with a locally convergent subsequence to
{\bf m}=(m,0)
and satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle E_{0}(m)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left({\mathcal{Q}}\|m^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|m_{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|m_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)\to\min,

\displaystyle m:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\qquad\text{with}\;\;m(\pm\infty)=(0,\pm 1).

Since the left translation is an

-isometry, the expression of

is invariant spatial translations. This invariance is inherited by the energy, yielding that minimizers of (

. We refer to

For our analysis, we introduce the phase
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
so that
m=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big({\mathcal{Q}}\|\theta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\cos\theta\|^{2}_{{\dot{H}}^{1/2}}+\|\cos\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\;\rightarrow\;\min

\displaystyle\theta:\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to(-\pi/2,\pi/2),\qquad\text{with}\;\;\theta(\pm\infty)=\pm\pi/2.

Since we are interested in Néel wall’s dynamic, we refer to minimizers of (

) as the

. From now on, we assume

. Despite

2.3.
LLG dynamics

The time evolution of the magnetization distribution on a ferromagnetic body
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is governed by the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation
[
LL35
,
Gil55
,
Gil04
]
:

(2.9)

\mathbf{m}_{t}+\alpha\mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{m}_{t}-\gamma\mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=0,

where
\mathbf{m}:\widetilde{\Omega}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the magnetization field,
\alpha>0
is a non-dimensional damping coefficient (Gilbert factor), and
\gamma>0
is the (constant) absolute value of the gyromagnetic ratio with dimensions of frequency (see, e.g.,
[
Gil04
]
). The effective field,
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathbf{h}-\nabla\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m})
, is the applied field
\mathbf{h}
and the negative functional gradient of the micromagnetic energy
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m})
. If we consider a single magnetic spin
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}(t)
under a constant magnetic field
\mathbf{h}
and neglect damping then, the magnetization
m
will precess about the applied field
\mathbf{h}
with a frequency given by
\omega=\gamma|\mathbf{h}|
. When the damping is turned on, the vector
\mathbf{m}
will spiral down around
\mathbf{h}
until
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{h}
become parallel. The typical relaxation time is
1/(\alpha\omega)
.

In bulk materials and up to translations, there exists a one-dimensional optimal path connecting antipodal magnetization states known as the Bloch wall. Bloch walls are such that
m_{1}=0
and the transition is perpendicular to the transition axis. In this case, the magnetization
\mathbf{m}
is divergence-free and the stray field energy vanishes. Now, for an applied external magnetic field
\mathbf{h}=H{\bf e}_{2}
the landscape changes because under the same initial conditions, explicit dynamic solutions are found and they show that the magnetization
\mathbf{m}
rotates to develop a non-vanishing
m_{1}
component. The latter component implies a rotation of the other magnetization components advancing the domain wall
[
HS98
,
Mel04
]
.

2.4.
LLG wave-type dynamic thin film limit

Thin films are incompatible with gyrotropic wall motion due to the incompatibility constraint of the in-plane magnetization imposed by the stray field. In this configuration, the competition between energy and dynamic forces becomes singular in the thin field limit. In
[
CMO07
]
, an effective suitable limit is considered under the appropriate regime where the oscillatory features of the LLG dynamics are preserved in the limit. It turns out that the effective dynamics depend on the asymptotic regime as
\alpha
and the relative thickness
\delta/d
tend to zero.

For the precise scaling and regime in
[
CMO07
]
let
\varepsilon=\sqrt{Q}
and consider (
2.1
) when
\varepsilon\ll{\mathcal{Q}}
while
{\mathcal{Q}}=(2\varepsilon d/\delta)^{2}\lesssim 1

is small but bounded from below. That is,
\varepsilon\sim\delta/d
can be regarded as the relative thickness. Under these assumptions, we rescale space, time, and energy by

x\mapsto wx,\quad t\mapsto t/(\gamma\varepsilon),\quad\text{and }\quad E_{\varepsilon}=(2/\delta)\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m}),

where
w=\delta/(2\varepsilon^{2})
. In this scaling, the mean effective field
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
becomes

\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\varepsilon^{2}\nabla E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m}).

Notice that
E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m})
is given by (
2.2
). Therefore, the LLG equation (
2.9
) becomes,

(2.10)

\mathbf{m}_{t}+\alpha\mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{m}_{t}+\varepsilon\mathbf{m}\times\nabla E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m})=0.

To derive the effective equation for the in-plane magnetization it is necessary to write down
E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m})
in terms of
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
and
m_{3}
, that is,

E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{m})=E_{0}(m)+\frac{1}{2}\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{--}\hskip-9.0pt\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big({\mathcal{Q}}|\nabla m_{3}|^{2}+\left(\frac{m_{3}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\Big)dx

where

(2.11)

E_{0}(m)=\frac{1}{2}\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{--}\hskip-9.0pt\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big({\mathcal{Q}}|\nabla m|^{2}+||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{H}}(m)|^{2}+(1-m_{2}^{2})\Big)dx.

Notice that for one-dimensional transition layers the energy
E_{0}
coincides with the reduced Néel wall energy (
2.4
).

In
[
CMO07
]
it is shown that as

\varepsilon\to 0\quad\text{while}\quad\alpha(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\to\nu

for some positive
\nu
, while keeping
{\mathcal{Q}}=1
for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a sequence of solution
\mathbf{m}_{\varepsilon}
of (
2.10
)
L
-periodic in the
x_{2}
direction such that the in-plane magnetization
m_{\varepsilon}
weakly converges to
m\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, (in the appropriate spaces) a weak solution of

(2.12)

[\partial_{t}^{2}m+\nu\partial_{t}m+\nabla E_{0}(m)]\perp{T_{m}\mathbb{S}}^{1}.

Because
E_{0}(m)
coincides with the Néel wall energy, it is clear that under the appropriate boundary conditions at infinity (e.g. (
2.4
)) the static Néel wall profile
\bar{m}=(\cos\bar{\theta},\sin\bar{\theta})
is a static solution of (
2.12
).

2.5.
Main result

The static Néel wall solution and the wave-type dynamic equation (
2.12
) are the starting point of the present work.
We state our main result in terms of the magnetic phase
\theta:\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. As function of
\theta(x,t)
, equation (
2.12
) with the boundary conditions given by (
2.5
) becomes

(2.13)

\left\{\ \ \begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{2}\theta+\nu\partial_{t}\theta+\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)=0,\\
&\theta(-\infty,t)=-\pi/2,\quad\theta(\infty,t)=\pi/2,\\
&\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}\theta(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where
(\theta_{0},v_{0})
are some initial conditions and the energy
{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)
is as in (
2.5
). After these definitions, we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 2.3

(Orbital stability of the static Néel wall)
.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of initial conditions such that the Cauchy problem (
2.13
) has a global solution. There exists
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that if the pair
(\theta_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathcal{J}}
satisfies

\|\theta_{0}-\overline{\theta}\|_{H^{1}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon,

then, the solution to (
2.13
) with initial condition
(\theta(x,0),\partial_{t}\theta(x,0))=(\theta_{0},v_{0})
satisfies

\|\theta(\cdot,t)-\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\exp(-\omega t),

for any
t>0
, where
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
is some shift and
C,\omega>0
are some constants that may depend on
(\theta_{0},v_{0})
and
\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is to be noticed that we are not proving the global existence of the solution for a given small initial perturbation. Theorem
2.3
states that any eventual initial small perturbation of the static Néel profile, if exists, must decay to a translation of it. This type of behavior is also called
orbital
stability (or stability
in shape
), as initial perturbations decay to an element of the orbit or manifold generated by the static wave which, in this case, is the one-dimensional manifold of translations. The existence of global solutions can be studied using standard semigroup techniques and with the help of the decaying estimates performed in this work; we do not pursue such analysis here. Instead, we focus on the stability problem alone.

Remark 2.5
.

The perturbations considered in Theorem
2.3
only affect the in-plane magnetization
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
through the phase
\theta
. In the regime where equation (
2.13
) is derived, perturbations on the out-of-plane component
m_{3}
have infinite energy ( see equation (
2.2
) and recall that
\epsilon\to 0
) and are not part of the model.

3.
Strategy of the proof

This work aims to establish the orbital stability of the Neel wall profile, i.e., Theorem
2.3
. We address this problem by rephrasing
equation (
2.13
) as a two-dimensional ODE system

\partial_{t}W=F(W),

where
\varphi=\partial_{t}\theta
,
W=(\theta,\varphi)
,
W_{0}=(u_{0},v_{0})
, and
F(W)=(\varphi,-\nu\varphi-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\theta))
.
In this setting, we apply the result of Lattanzio
et al.

[
LMPS16
]
(Theorem
7.1
) to prove orbital stability. The conditions of Lattanzio’s theorem are the following:

(a)

The flow
F
vanishes on a one-dimensional manifold
\phi
that contains the origin.

(b)

The difference between
\phi
and its linearization at the origin has a supralinear growth in a neighborhood of the latter.

(c)

The difference between
F
and its linearization at
\phi
has a supralinear growth in a neighborhood of the one-dimensional manifold.

(d)

The linearization of the nonlinear ODE system around any point on the one-dimensional manifold
\phi
has a solution whose projection on a suitable subspace of codimension one decay exponentially.

Neel walls’s translation define the one-dimensional manifold in condition (a), namely
\phi(\delta)=(\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta),0)
. Because the Neel wall energy is invariant under translation, the zero-level set of
F
contains the manifold
\phi
. Condition (b) holds because the Néel wall’s phase inherited its smoothness to the one-dimensional manifold
\phi
.
In addition,
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}
is regular enough to grant that condition (c) holds. The complete proof of (a), (b), and (c) are presented in Section
7
.

The proof that condition (d) holds is the most challenging; most of the paper is devoted to it (sections
4
to
6
).
In the proof of condition (d), we are only concerned with the linearized ODE system,

\partial_{t}U={\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}U,\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{I}\\
-\opl^{\delta}&-\nu\mathrm{I}\end{pmatrix},\qquad

and
\opl^{\delta}
is the linearization of
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}
around
\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta)
. Because we rely on the semigroup theory for the existence of solutions, we will concentrate on the operator
{\mathcal{L}}:={\mathcal{L}}^{0}
for the untranslated Néel wall phase
\overline{\theta}
and its associated block operator
{\mathcal{A}}:={\mathcal{A}}^{0}
, since the whole operator family
\{{\mathcal{L}}^{\delta}\ |\ \delta\in\mathbb{R}\}
is isospectral
[
KP13
]
.

Although the semigroup theory holds under rather general conditions, the nonlocal character of
{\mathcal{A}}
poses challenging problems.
The proof of condition (d) follows by showing, first, that block matrix operator
{\mathcal{A}}
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup, and second, that the dynamics perpendicular to the central manifold is exponentially decaying. We have at least two strategies for the former step: apply the generalized
Hille-Yosida theorem
, see
[
EN00
]
, or the classical
Lumer-Phillips theorem
, see e.g.,
[
RR04
]
.
We take the latter approach(Section
6
) because the Hille-Yosida theorem requires bounds on the norm for all powers of the resolvent, but in our case, these bounds are hard to prove due to the nonlocal term.

For a linear and closed operator,
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to Y
, between two Banach spaces, the resolvent set is defined as

\rho({\mathcal{A}}):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,{\mathcal{A}}-\lambda\,\text{ is injective and onto, and }({\mathcal{A}}-\lambda)^{-1}\,\text{is bounded}\,\},

and the spectrum
\sigma({\mathcal{A}})=\mathbb{C}\backslash\rho({\mathcal{A}})
is the complex complement of the resolvent
[
Kat95
]
. By Weyl’s spectrum splitting
[
Wey10
]
,
\sigma({\mathcal{A}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})\cup\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})
where

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\;{\mathcal{A}}-\lambda\,\text{ is Fredholm with index zero and non-trivial kernel}\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\;{\mathcal{A}}-\lambda\,\text{ is either not Fredholm or
has index different from zero}\}.

In this splitting the point spectrum
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})
is a discrete set of
isolated eigenvalues

[
Kat95
,
KP13
]
.

Because the spectral mapping theorem is not valid for
C_{0}
-semigroups, see
[
EN00
]
, we use
Gearhart-Prüss theorem
to prove the second step in the proof of condition (d). We recall that Gearhart-Prüss theorem states that any
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{t{\mathcal{T}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space
H
is exponentially decaying if and only if
{\mathcal{T}}
’s spectral bound is negative and its resolvent satisfies
\sup_{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0}\|({\mathcal{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}\|<\infty
. We present the proof of these condition in Lemma
6.12
and Lemma
6.22
for a suitable restriction of the operator
{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively.
In our problem,
\sigma({\mathcal{A}})
and
\sigma({\mathcal{L}})
are linked. Furthermore, if
\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})
, then
-\lambda(\lambda+\nu)\in\sigma({\mathcal{L}})
, (Subsection
5.3
). Therefore, we only need to characterize the spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}
to localize
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})
. Although
{\mathcal{L}}
is a nonlocal operator, its spectrum is easily localized since it is self-adjoint and coercive on the
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement of
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
(Theorem
4.1
).
Despite
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})
also depends on
\sigma({\mathcal{L}})
, its localization is not straightforward. We rely on Weyl’s essential spectrum theorem,
[
KP13
]
, that implies
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})
provided
{\mathcal{A}}
is a relatively compact perturbation of an auxiliary operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
, (Definition
4.4

(ii)
).
As
{\mathcal{A}}
, the auxiliary operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
has a block structure written in terms of a positive-defined and self-adjoint linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
. In addition, since
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
is an invertible operator, the localization of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
’s complete spectrum is possible. As expected,
\sigma(A_{\infty})
is contained in the negative real part complex semi-plane.

Ultimately, due to the structure of the block matrix operators, the relative compactness between
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
follows from the operator’s
({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}):H^{2}\to L^{2}
compactness. Therefore, we present the spectral analysis of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
and
{\mathcal{L}}
in Section
4
.

4.
The linearized operator around the static Néel wall’s phase

Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the
L^{2}
-linearization of the
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}
around the static Néel wall’s phase
\overline{\theta}

{\mathcal{L}}
and locate its spectrum. In addition, we analyze the auxiliary asymptotic operator
\opli

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}:H^{2}\to L^{2}

The main results in this section are the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\overline{\theta}
be the Néel wall phase and the operator
{\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
with domain
D({\mathcal{L}})=H^{2}

(4.1)

{\mathcal{L}}u:=-\partial^{2}_{x}u+\mathcal{S}u-c_{\theta}u,\qquad u\in D({\mathcal{L}}),

where

is a nonlocal operator, with domain
D({\mathcal{S}})=H^{1}
, defined as

(4.2)

{\mathcal{S}}u:=\sin\overline{\theta}(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})(u\sin\overline{\theta}),\quad u\in D({\mathcal{S}}),

and

(4.3)

c_{\theta}:=\cos\overline{\theta}(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})\cos\overline{\theta}.

Then,
{\mathcal{L}}
is a closed densely defined self-adjoint linear operator whose
L^{2}
-spectrum satisfies

\sigma({\mathcal{L}})\subset\{0\}\cup[\Lambda_{0},\infty),

for some fixed positive constant
\Lambda_{0}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
with domain
D({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})=H^{2}
be given by

(4.4)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}u:=-\partial^{2}_{x}u+(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})u.

Then,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
is a closed densely defined self-adjoint invertible linear operator whose
L^{2}
-spectrum satisfies

\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})\subset[1,\infty).

Theorem 4.3
.

The linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}:H^{2}\to L^{2}
is continuous and compact.

Theorem
4.3
is a consequence of the Kolmogorov-Riesz Theorem
[
Kol91
]
,
[

that establishes the equivalence between the

-uniform decaying, and the precompactness (or totally boundedness) on bounded sets in

The

-bounded sets under

to

. The

By introducing a smooth function

.

4.1.
Basic properties

We begin this section with the definitions of relative boundedness and relatively compact perturbations.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
be two linear operator on a Banach space
X
. We say that:

(i)

The operator
{\mathcal{S}}
is relatively bounded with respect to
{\mathcal{P}}
(or
{\mathcal{P}}
-bounded) if
D({\mathcal{P}})\subset D({\mathcal{S}})
and
for every
u\in D({\mathcal{P}})

\|{\mathcal{S}}u\|\leq a\|u\|+b\|{\mathcal{P}}u\|,

where
a,b
are non negative constants. The greatest lower bound
b_{0}
of all possible constants
b
is called the relative bound of
{\mathcal{S}}
with respect to
{\mathcal{P}}
(or the
{\mathcal{P}}
-bound of
{\mathcal{S}}
).

(ii)

The linear operator
{\mathcal{S}}
is a relatively compact perturbation of
{\mathcal{P}}
if for some
\lambda\in\rho\left({\mathcal{P}}\right)
the operator
({\mathcal{P}}-{\mathcal{S}})(\lambda\mathrm{I}-{\mathcal{P}})^{-1}:X\to X
is compact.

For a function
u\in L^{2}
, we define the Half-laplacian in terms of the Fourier transform as follows,

(-\Delta)^{1/2}u:=(|\xi|\widehat{u}(\xi))^{\vee}.

Reagarded as an operator from
H^{1}
to
L^{2}
, the Half-laplacian is a bounded linear operator.

From harmonic analysis, we know that the Half-laplacian is related to the Hilbert transform. The precise relationship is presented in the following lemma, whose proof can be found in many references(see, e.g.,
[
CCdlH21
,
Kin09
,
Ner75
]
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
be the Hilbert transform given by

u\mapsto\mathrm{P.V.}\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{u(s)}{x-s}\,ds.

Then,
{\mathcal{H}}
is an isometry on
L^{2}
. Moreover, if
u\in H^{1}
we have that

(-\Delta)^{1/2}u={\mathcal{H}}(\partial_{x}u)=\partial_{x}{\mathcal{H}}u.

The following proposition summarizes the basic properties of the linearized operator
{\mathcal{L}}
and the Néel wall’s phase, which have already been proved in
[
CMO07
]
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The operator
{\mathcal{L}}
and the static Néel wall’s phase
\overline{\theta}
satisfy:

(a)

\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in D({\mathcal{L}})
with
{\mathcal{L}}\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}=0
.

(b)

For all
f\in L^{2}
such that
f\perp\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
in
L^{2}
there exists a solution
u\in H^{2}
to the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
. The solution is unique up to a constant multiple of
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
.

(c)

There exists a uniform constant
\Lambda_{0}>0
such that if
u\in H^{1}
and
\langle u,\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
, then

(4.5)

\langle{\mathcal{L}}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\Lambda_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(d)

Let
f\in\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}^{\perp}\subset L^{2}
. Then the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
has a strong solution
u\in H^{2}
, unique up to a constant multiple of
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
. Moreover, if
u\in\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}^{\perp}
, then

(4.6)

\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}},

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemmata 4 and 5, together with Proposition 1 in
[
CMO07
]
.
∎

For notational convenience, we denote

s_{\theta}:=\sin\overline{\theta}
.
It can be shown
[
CMO07
]
, that
s_{\theta}
and
c_{\theta}
both are real, smooth and bounded for in all
\mathbb{R}
with
c_{\theta}\in H^{2}
. Moreover,

Corollary 4.7
.

There exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(4.7)

\|c_{\theta}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}<C,\qquad\|s_{\theta}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}<C.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from Corollary
2.2
and the regularity of
c_{\theta}
and
s_{\theta}
.
∎

The following lemma shows that the nonlocal operator
{\mathcal{S}}
is symmetric and its is related with the sesquilinear form (
2.8
).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
with
D({\mathcal{S}})=H^{1}
be defined as in
4.2
. Then,
{\mathcal{S}}
is a symmetric operator and

\left\langle{\mathcal{S}}u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=b[s_{\theta}u,s_{\theta}v],

for every
u,v\in D({\mathcal{S}})
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in H^{1}
.
By Plancherel’s theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{S}}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|)\widehat{(s_{\theta}u)}(\xi)\widehat{(s_{\theta}v)}(\xi)^{*}\,d\xi=b[s_{\theta}u,s_{\theta}v].

The symmetry of
{\mathcal{S}}
follows because
H^{1}\subset L^{2}
densely and by the hermiticity of
b
, we get that

\left\langle{\mathcal{S}}u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=b[s_{\theta}u,s_{\theta}v]=b[s_{\theta}v,s_{\theta}u]^{*}=\left\langle{\mathcal{S}}v\,,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}}^{*}=\left\langle u\,,{\mathcal{S}}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

∎

We finish this subsection by showing self-adjointness of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

The operator
{\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
with domain
D({\mathcal{L}})=H^{2}
is self-adjoint and closed.

Proof.

First, note that
{\mathcal{L}}
is clearly a symmetric operator, because its domain is dense in
L^{2}
and there holds

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{L}}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,-\partial_{x}^{2}v\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle u,{\mathcal{S}}v\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle u,c_{\theta}v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,{\mathcal{L}}v\rangle_{L^{2}},

for all
u,v\in H^{2}
which follows
by an integration by parts, an application of Lemma
4.8
and the fact that
c_{\theta}
is real.

It is well-known that for every
u\in H^{2}
there holds the estimate

(4.8)

\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq k\|\partial^{2}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{2}{k}\|u\|_{L^{2}},

for any arbitrary
k>0
(see Kato
[
Kat95
]
, p. 192). Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}:L^{2}\to L^{2},\\
&D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}})=H^{1},\\
&\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}u:=s_{\theta}(-\Delta)^{1/2}(s_{\theta}u),\quad u\in D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}),\end{aligned}\right.

so that
{\mathcal{S}}=s_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{I}+\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}
. Following the arguments of Lemma
4.8
, it follows that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}
is a symmetric operator. By Corollary
4.7
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such
\|\partial_{x}s_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(-\Delta)^{1/2}(s_{\theta}(x)u)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{(s_{\theta}u)}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}(s_{\theta}u)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{x}s_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|s_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}},

and by (
4.8
) we get

\|\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq k\|-\partial^{2}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}+\Big(C_{0}+\frac{2}{k}\Big)\|u\|_{L^{2}},

for all
u\in H^{2}
and any arbitrary
k>0
. Because
D(-\partial_{x}^{2})=H^{2}\subset D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}})=H^{1}

we conclude that the symmetric operator
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}
is relatively bounded with respect to
-\partial_{x}^{2}
, and its relative bound is zero. Consequently, we may apply Kato-Rellich’s theorem (see
[
RS75
]
, Theorem X.12, p. 162) to conclude that the operator
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}-\partial_{x}^{2}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
with domain
D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}-\partial_{x}^{2})=D(-\partial_{x}^{2})=H^{2}
is self-adjoint.

Now, let us write
{\mathcal{L}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\mathcal{S}}-c_{\theta}\mathrm{I}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}+\beta\mathrm{I}
, where
\beta:=s_{\theta}^{2}-c_{\theta}
is a bounded real smooth coefficient. Clearly,

\|\beta u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\beta\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\beta\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+k\|(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}-\partial_{x}^{2})u\|_{L^{2}},

for all
u\in H^{2}
and for any
k>0
. Since
D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}-\partial_{x}^{2})=H^{2}\subset D(\beta\mathrm{I})=L^{2}
, we conclude that the symmetric operator
\beta\mathrm{I}
is
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}-\partial_{x}^{2})-
bounded with relative bound equal to zero. Upon application of Kato-Rellich’s theorem, we conclude that the operator
{\mathcal{L}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}+\beta\mathrm{I}
with domain
D({\mathcal{L}})=H^{2}
is self-adjoint.

Finally, the closeness of
{\mathcal{L}}
follows because every self-adjoint operator is closed. ∎

4.2.
The spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}

The operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is a self-adjoint operator by Lemma
4.9
, and its
L^{2}
-spectrum is real. From Proposition
4.6
,
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in L^{2}
is an eigenfunction of
{\mathcal{L}}
associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda=0
.
Moreover, the kernel of
{\mathcal{L}}
is spanned by
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
and has dimension one. Otherwise, there exists
u\in H^{2}\cap\ker({\mathcal{L}})
,
u\neq 0
, with
u=u_{\perp}+\alpha\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
where
\left\langle u_{\perp}\,,\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
. By Proposition
4.6

(c)
,

0=\langle{\mathcal{L}}(u_{\perp}+\alpha\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}),u_{\perp}+\alpha\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{\mathcal{L}}u_{\perp},u_{\perp}\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\Lambda_{0}\|u_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

so that
u_{\perp}=0
, a contradiction. Therefore, the geometric multiplicity of
\lambda=0
is one. Since in a Hilbert, the algebraic and geometric multiplicities coincide for self-adjoint (see Kato
[
Kat95
]
, p. 273), and we get the following result:

Lemma 4.10
.

\lambda=0
is a simple eigenvalue of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
, with eigenfunction
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in D({\mathcal{L}})=H^{2}
. Moreover,
0\in\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{L}})
.

Now, we give:

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Theorem
4.9

{\mathcal{L}}
is a closed densely defined self-adjoint linear operator. It remains to localize its
L^{2}
-spectrum.

Let
L^{2}=\lspan{\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}}\oplus L^{2}_{\perp}
where
L^{2}_{\perp}
is
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
’s
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement.
The operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is self-adjoint; hence its spectrum is real. In addition, by Lemma
4.10
,
\lambda=0
is an isolated simple eigenvalue, and from
the spectral decomposition theorem
\sigma({\mathcal{L}})_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}=\sigma({\mathcal{L}})\backslash\{0\}
(see Theorem III-6.17, p. 178, in Kato
[
Kat95
]
).

Now, we claim that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}:L^{2}_{\perp}\to L^{2}_{\perp},\\
&D({\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}})=D({\mathcal{L}})\cap L^{2}_{\perp}=H^{2}\cap L^{2}_{\perp},\\
&{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}u:={\mathcal{L}}u,\quad u\in D({\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}).\end{aligned}\right.

is also self-adjoint.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}
is symmetric because
{\mathcal{L}}
is symmetric and
D({\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}})\subset L^{2}_{\perp}
densely.
In order to show that
{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}
is self-adjoint it suffices to show that
({\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}\pm i)(D({\mathcal{L}})\cap L^{2}_{\perp})=L^{2}_{\perp}
(see, e.g., Theorem VIII.3, p. 256, in
[
RS80
]
). Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}
to
\lspan\{\partial\overline{\theta}\}
. We already know that
{\mathcal{L}}\pm i:D({\mathcal{L}})\to L^{2}
is surjective because
{\mathcal{L}}
is self-adjoint, and
{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
commute. Therefore, for
v\in L^{2}_{\perp}
there exist elements
u_{\pm}\in D({\mathcal{L}})=H^{2}
such that
({\mathcal{L}}\pm i)u_{\pm}=v
. Thus,
(\mathrm{I}-{\mathcal{P}}_{0})u_{\pm}\in L^{2}_{\perp}
, and

({\mathcal{L}}\pm i)(\mathrm{I}-{\mathcal{P}}_{0})u_{\pm}=v-{\mathcal{P}}_{0}({\mathcal{L}}\pm i)u_{\pm}\\
=(\mathrm{I}-{\mathcal{P}}_{0})v=v

That is,
({\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}\pm i):D({\mathcal{L}})\cap L^{2}_{\perp}\to L^{2}_{\perp}
is surjective, and
{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}
is self-adjoint as claimed.

Finally, from Rayleigh’s formula for semi-bounded self-adjoint operators (
[
Kat95
]
, p. 278) and Proposition
4.6

(c)
, we have

\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{|L^{2}_{\perp}}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{\mathcal{L}}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\Lambda_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for all
u\in D({\mathcal{L}})\cap L^{2}_{\perp}=H^{2}\cap\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}^{\perp}_{L^{2}}
. Therefore,
\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{L^{2}_{\perp}})\subset[\Lambda_{0},\infty)
and Kato’s decomposition theorem yields
\sigma({\mathcal{L}})_{L^{2}}\subset\{0\}\cup[\Lambda_{0},\infty)
as claimed.

∎

4.3.
The asymptotic operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}

This subsection examines the operator
\opli
defined in Theorem
4.3
. By replacing
{\mathcal{L}}
’s coefficients with their limits at
x\to\pm\infty
, we obtain two limiting operators
{\mathcal{L}}_{+\infty}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}
. In the present case,
{\mathcal{L}}_{+\infty}={\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}=\opli
.

Let
a_{\infty}[\cdot,\cdot]:H^{1}\times H^{1}\to\mathbb{C}
be the sesquilinear form

a_{\infty}[u,v]:=\langle\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}v\rangle_{L^{2}}+b[u,v],

where
b[\cdot,\cdot]
is as in (
2.8
).
For
f\in L^{2}
, the weak formulation of the nonhomogeneous linear problem

(4.9)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}u=f,

is given by

a_{\infty}[u,v]=\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle f,v\rangle_{L^{2}}
for every
v\in H^{1}
. In terms of the Fourier transform, it is straightforward that
the bilinear form
a_{\infty}[\cdot,\cdot]
is uniformly elliptic and continuous in
H^{1}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

For every
f\in L^{2}
there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{2}
to (
4.9
). Moreover,

(4.10)

\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
a_{\infty}[\cdot,\cdot]
is uniformly elliptic and continuous in
H^{1}
, for each
f\in L^{2}
there exists a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}
to (
4.9
) by the Lax-Milgram theorem. Hence,

\langle\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})u,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}.

for any test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and by Plancherel’s identity we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(1+|\xi|+\xi^{2})\widehat{u}(\xi)-\widehat{f}(\xi)\big]\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)^{*}\,d\xi=0,

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
(1+|\xi|+\xi^{2})\widehat{u}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)
a.e. in
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1+\xi^{2}}{1+|\xi|+\xi^{2}}\Big)^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

Now, we prove Theorem
4.2

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

By definition,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
is densely defined in
L^{2}
, and its invertibility follows from Lemma
4.11
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.9
, the self-adjointness of
\opli
follows by Kato-Rellich Theorem, and
\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})\subset\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
is semi-bounded since
\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}=a_{\infty}[u,u]\geq\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\geq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
for

. Finally, using Lemma
4.11
, we get

\inf_{0\neq v\in D({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})}\frac{\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}=\inf_{0\neq v\in D({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})}\frac{a_{\infty}[v,v]}{\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\geq 1,

and by Rayleigh’s spectral bound for semi-bounded self-adjoint operators in Hilbert spaces (cf.
[
Kat95
]
, p. 278) we conclude that
\inf\sigma({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})>1
.
∎

Remark 4.12

4.4.
Relative compactness

The goal of this subsection is the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
We begin by establishing the continuity of
\opli-\opl
as an operator from
H^{2}
to
H^{1}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}
continuously maps
H^{2}
into
H^{1}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{2}
then, we have that

(4.12)

({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}})u=(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})u-s_{\theta}(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})(s_{\theta}u)+c_{\theta}u.

By Corollary (
4.7
), we have the bounds

\|c_{\theta}u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq 2\|c_{\theta}\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2},

for some
C>0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})\big|\big((1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})u\big)^{\wedge}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=2\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

From the later inequality and Corollary (
4.7
), there exists
\widetilde{C}>0
such that

\|s_{\theta}(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})(s_{\theta}u)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq 2\|s_{\theta}\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}\|(1+(-\Delta)^{1/2})(s_{\theta}u)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\widetilde{C}\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

We combine all estimates above to conclude that there exists a constant

such that

(4.13)

\|({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}})u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}},

for all

. This shows the result.
∎

At this point, we cite two theorems, one due to Kolmogorov
[
Kol91
]
and Riesz
[
Rie88
]
(see, for example,
[
HOH10
]
and the references therein) and the other due to Pego
[
Peg85
]
, describing totally bounded sets in
L^{p}
and
L^{2}
, respectively. We recall that precompactness and totally boundedness are equivalent concepts for sets contained in complete metric spaces.

Theorem 4.14

(Kolmogorov-Riesz
[
Kol91
,
Rie88
]
)
.

A bounded set
{\mathcal{F}}\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
is totally bounded if and only if

(a)

(
L^{p}
-equicontinuity)
\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{p}\,dx=0
uniformly for
u\in{\mathcal{F}}
, and

(b)

(
L^{p}
-uniform decay)
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{|x|>R}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx=0
uniformly for
u\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Theorem 4.15

(Pego
[
Peg85
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a bounded set of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}:=\{\widehat{u}\,|\,u\in{\mathcal{F}}\}
. The functions for
{\mathcal{F}}
are
L^{2}
-equicontinuous
if and only if the functions for
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}
decay uniformly in
L^{2}
and vice versa.

Before using Theorems
4.14
and
4.15
in our proofs, we need the following preparatory results.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a bounded set in
H^{1}
and
\phi\in H^{1}
. Then the set
\phi{\mathcal{F}}:=\{\phi u\,|\,u\in{\mathcal{F}}\}
is precompact in
L^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\phi{\mathcal{F}}
. By definition, there exists a bounded sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
such that
v_{k}=\phi u_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The sequence,
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
contains an
H^{1}
-weakly convergent subsequence
\{v_{k_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that, without loss of generality, has limit
u=0
.

We claim that
v_{k_{n}}=\phi u_{k_{n}}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}
. Let
\epsilon>0
,
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}
be a standard cut-off function such that
\supp\eta\subset B_{1}
, and
\eta_{R}(x):=\eta(x/R)
. Now, we split
u_{k_{n}}
as the sum of
u_{k_{n}}\eta_{R}
and
u_{k_{n}}(1-\eta_{R})
. Because
\phi\in L^{2}
and
u_{k_{n}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
(by Sobolev inequality), there exists
R>0
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|\phi(1-\eta_{R})u_{k_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon/2.

Moreover,
H^{1}
-weak convergence implies locally uniform convergence, therefore there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
n>N_{0}

\left\|\phi\eta_{R}u_{k_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon/2.

Combining both inequalities
v_{k_{n}}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

In the proof of the following results, if a set
{\mathcal{K}}
belongs to an operator
\mathcal{G}
’s domain, we denote by
\mathcal{G}{\mathcal{K}}
the image of
{\mathcal{K}}
under the action of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\delta>0
,
g_{\delta}\in H^{1}\cap C^{\infty}
be a bounded monotonic function such that
\supp\partial_{x}g_{\delta}\subset[-\delta,\delta]
, and
{\mathcal{H}}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
be the Hilbert transform. Then, the linear operator
[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}:H^{2}\to H^{1}
is compact in
L^{2}
.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
4.16
by showing that for any bounded
{\mathcal{F}}\subset H^{2}
the set
[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}{\mathcal{F}}
is
L^{2}
-equicontinuous and uniformly
L^{2}
-decaying.
We present the proof in two steps.

First, let
\mathcal{G}\subset H^{1}
be bounded. Then, for any
v\in\mathcal{G}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|\xi|>R\}}|\hat{v}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1+R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})|\hat{v}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\frac{\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}}{1+R^{2}}\leq\frac{M^{2}}{1+R^{2}}.

Therefore,
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is
L^{2}
-uniformly decaying, and
\mathcal{G}
is
L^{2}
-equicontinuous by Theorem
4.15
.

Thus, the
L^{2}
-equicontinuity
of
[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}{\mathcal{F}}
follows by showing its boundedness in
H^{1}
.
For any
u\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we get that

\left\|[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|g_{\delta}{\mathcal{H}}\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}}(g_{\delta}\partial_{x}u)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|g_{\delta}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

by the continuity of
{\mathcal{H}}
in
L^{2}
. Analogously, we obtain the estimate

\left\|\partial_{x}([g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}u)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|(\partial_{x}g_{\delta}){\mathcal{H}}\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|g_{\delta}{\mathcal{H}}\partial^{2}_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}}\partial_{x}(g_{\delta}\partial_{x}u)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq 2\|g_{\delta}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Therefore,
\left\|[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2}}
for some positive constant
C
, and we conclude by the boundedness of
{\mathcal{F}}
in
H^{2}
.

Second, we show the
L^{2}
-uniform decay of
[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
R>2\delta
be a fixed constant. We claim that

(4.14)

\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}u(x)=C\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{\partial_{x}u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)y)}{y+|x|}\,dy+\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{g_{\delta}(y)-g_{\delta}(\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\delta)}{y-x}\,\partial_{x}u(y)\,dy,

for
|x|>R
.

We postpone the proof of the claim and prove the result.
By integration by parts on former term on the right hand side of (
4.14
) we get

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{\partial_{x}u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)y)}{y+|x|}\,dy=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)y)-u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\delta)}{(y+|x|)^{2}}\,dy.

Thus, if
M>0
is a uniform bound for
{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M
for every
u\in{\mathcal{F}}
, and

\left|\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{\partial_{x}u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)y)}{y+|x|}\,dy\right|\leq 2M\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{1}{(y+|x|)^{2}}\,dy=\frac{2M}{\delta+|x|}.

By integration on
x
over
\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]
, we get

(4.15)

\int_{|x|>R}\left(\int^{\infty}_{\delta}\frac{\partial_{x}u(-\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)y)}{y+|x|}\,dy\right)^{2}dx\leq 4M^{2}\int_{|x|>R}\frac{dx}{(\delta+|x|)^{2}}\leq\frac{8M^{2}}{\delta+R}.

Hence, the former term on the right hand side of (
4.14
) is
L^{2}
-uniformly decaying.

Now we consider the latter term on the right hand side of (
4.14
).
Let
C_{0}:=|g_{\delta}(-\delta)|+|g_{\delta}(\delta))|
. By recalling that
g_{\delta}
is monotone, from Jensen’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}\left(\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{g_{\delta}(y)-g_{\delta}(\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\delta)}{y-x}\,\partial_{x}u(y)\,dy\right)^{2}\!dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\int_{|x|>R}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{\left(g_{\delta}(y)-g_{\delta}(\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\delta)\right)^{2}}{\left(y-x\right)^{2}}\,(\partial_{x}u(y))^{2}\,dydx

\displaystyle\leq 8C_{0}^{2}\delta\int_{|x|>R}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\left(y-x\right)^{2}}\,(\partial_{x}u(y))^{2}\,dydx.

Since
\partial_{x}u\in L^{2}
and
(y-x)^{2}\geq(|x|-\delta)
for every
y\in(-\delta,\delta)
, we obtain

(4.16)

\rint_{|x|>R}\left(\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{g_{\delta}(y)-g_{\delta}(\mathrm{sgn}\,(x)\delta)}{y-x}\,\partial_{x}u(y)\,dy\right)^{2}dx\leq\rint_{|x|>R}\frac{8C_{0}^{2}\delta\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dx}{\left(|x|-\delta\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{16C_{0}^{2}\delta M^{2}}{R-\delta}.

Combining Young’s inequality with equations (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}([g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}u)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16M^{2}(C^{2}+2C_{0}^{2}\delta)}{\pi(R-\delta)}.

Therefore,
[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]\partial_{x}{\mathcal{F}}
is
L^{2}
-uniformly decaying, and the result follows.

Finally, we prove claim (
4.14
). Let
v=\partial_{x}u
for notational convenience.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus and Lemma
4.5
,

\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|h|>\epsilon}\frac{g_{\delta}(x+h)-g_{\delta}(x)}{h}v(x+h)\,dh=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|h|>\epsilon}\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh.

Assuming that
x>R
and letting
\epsilon<\delta
, the expression
\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)
becomes

\displaystyle\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-x+\delta}\!\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}\!g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh+

\displaystyle+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left[\int_{-x+\delta}^{-\epsilon}\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}\!g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}\!g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh\right].

In the latter equality, the last two integrals vanish since
\supp g^{\prime}\subset[-\delta,\delta]
and
\delta<x+h
for
h>\delta-x
. Furthermore, the first term is given by

\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{-x-\delta}\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh+\int_{-x-\delta}^{-x+\delta}\frac{1}{h}\int_{x}^{x+h}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh.

Since
\supp g^{\prime}_{\delta}\subset[x+h,x]
for
h\leq-x-\delta
, the integral
\int_{|x|\leq\delta}g^{\prime}(x)dx
is independent of
h
. Hence,

\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)=-C\int_{-\infty}^{-x-\delta}\frac{v(x+h)}{h}\,dh+\int_{-x-\delta}^{-x+\delta}\frac{1}{h}\int_{\delta}^{x+h}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(x+h)\,dh,

where
C=\int_{|x|\leq\delta}g^{\prime}(x)dx
.

Now, by letting
y=x+h
, the fundamental theorem of calculus yields

\displaystyle\pi[g_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}]v(x)=

\displaystyle-C\int_{-\infty}^{-\delta}\frac{v(y)}{y-x}\,dy+\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{1}{y-x}\int_{\delta}^{y}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\,v(y)\,dy,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C\int_{-\infty}^{-\delta}\frac{v(y)}{y-x}\,dy-\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\frac{g_{\delta}(\delta)-g_{\delta}(y)}{y-x}\,v(y)\,dy.

Using an analogous argument for
x<-R
we conclude (
4.14
) and the proof is finished.

∎

5.1.

In order to establish the perturbation equations, we consider a solution

In view of (

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}u\\

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}u\\

We specialize the linearized equation (

Remark 5.3

Under the substitution

In the present stability analysis, we are interested in the spectral properties of the block matrix operator,

transpose. We have that

({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{A}})(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})^{-1}U_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{j}\\
g_{j}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}})f_{j}\end{pmatrix}.

Now, because
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}}:H^{2}\to L^{2}
is compact by Theorem
4.3
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
H^{2}
, the sequence
\{({\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{L}})f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}
is bounded and has a convergent subsequence in
L^{2}
. Therefore,
\{({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{A}})(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})^{-1}U_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
has a convergent subsequence in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
, and
({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}-{\mathcal{A}})(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})^{-1}
is compact operator on
H^{1}\times L^{2}
for every
\lambda\in\rho({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})
, as claimed.

Now, since
{\mathcal{A}}
is a relatively compact perturbation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
,
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})
by
Weyl’s essential spectrum theorem (see
[
KP13
]
, p. 29). Hence, from Lemma
5.8
we get

\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{1}_{[2,\infty)}(\nu)\sqrt{\nu^{2}-4}\}.

Finally, we combine
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{ess}}}({\mathcal{A}})
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{\tiny{pt}}}({\mathcal{A}})
’s bounds to conclude that

\sigma({\mathcal{A}})
satisfies (
5.2
).
∎

6.
Semigroup generation and decay

In this section, we use semigroup theory to show that solutions to Cauchy problem (
5.8
) generate a semigroup of quasi-contractions. Moreover, by restricting operator
{\mathcal{A}}
to a codimension-one subspace of
H^{1}\times L^{2}
, we obtain an exponentially decaying
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\nu>0
and
{\mathcal{A}}
as in (
5.10
) with
D({\mathcal{A}})=H^{2}\times H^{1}
. Then,
{\mathcal{A}}
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
of quasicontractions, namely there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq e^{\omega t}\|U\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}},

for all
t\geq 0
and every
U\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
.

From basic semigroup theory (cf.
[
EN00
,
Paz83
]
), the last theorem implies

\frac{d}{dt}\big(e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U\big)=e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{A}}U={\mathcal{A}}(e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U)

for
U\in H^{2}\times H^{1}
.

Before presenting our next result, we must define the following relevant spaces and sets.
Let
\Phi_{0}:=(\nu\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},\partial_{x}\overline{\theta})
, then
H^{1}\times L^{2}=\lspan\{\Phi_{0}\}\oplus(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
where
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
is
\Phi_{0}
’s
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement, namely

(6.1)

(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}:=\{F\in H^{1}\times L^{2}\ |\ \left\langle F\,,\Phi_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\}.

For
\nu>0
fixed and
{\mathcal{A}}
is as in (
5.10
) with
D({\mathcal{A}})=H^{2}\times H^{1}
, let

(6.2)

D_{\perp}:=\{U\in D({\mathcal{A}})\cap(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\ |\ {\mathcal{A}}U\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\}.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
{\mathcal{A}}
as in (
5.10
), let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}:(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
be its restriction to
D_{\perp}
given by

(6.3)

{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}U:={\mathcal{A}}U,\qquad U\in D_{\perp}.

Then,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
is the generator of an exponentially decaying
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, namely, there exists uniform constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega_{1}>0
, such that

(6.4)

\|e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}}U\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq Me^{-\omega_{1}t}\|U\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}},

for all
t\geq 0
and every
U\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
.

6.1.
Generation of the semigroup

In this subsection, we present the proof of Theorem
6.1
. The proof strategy is to apply the classical Lumer-Phillips theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 12.22, p. 407, in
[
RR04
]
). To this end, we need to show that
D({\mathcal{A}})
is densely defined,
{\mathcal{A}}-\nu\mathrm{I}
is onto, and a resolvent estimate on
{\mathcal{A}}
, namely there exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{Re}\,\!\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,U\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\eta_{0}\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}
.
The remainder of this subsection is devoted to proving the latter three.

We begin by presenting some preparatory results. The following result is necessary because the intersection does not distribute the direct sum.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
L^{2}_{\perp}
be
\lspan\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}
’s
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement, and
H^{k}_{\perp}:=H^{k}\cap L^{2}_{\perp}
for
k\in\{1,2\}
. Then, for every
u\in H^{k}
there exist unique
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
u_{\perp}\in H^{k}_{\perp}
such that
u=u_{\perp}+\alpha\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
. Moreover,
for every
U\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
there exist unique
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
U_{\perp}\in H^{k}_{\perp}\times L^{2}
such that
U=U_{\perp}+\alpha\Theta
where
\Theta=(\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},0)
.

Proof.

Let
k
be fixed and
\bar{u}\in H^{k}
.
Because
L^{2}=L^{2}_{\perp}\oplus\lspan\{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\}
, there exists unique
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
u_{\perp}\in L^{2}_{\perp}
such that

u=u_{\perp}+\alpha\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
.
Moreover, since
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in H^{k}
by Proposition
2.1

(c)
,
u_{\perp}\in H^{k}
,
and the first follows. Now, the splitting for
U\in H^{k}\times L^{2}
is a straight forward consequence of the
H^{k}
case and the result follows.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

The bi-linear form
a:H^{1}_{\perp}\times H^{1}_{\perp}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
associated to the elliptic operator
{\mathcal{L}}
, namely
\left\langle\opl u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=a[u,v]
for every
u\in H_{\perp}^{2}
and
v\in H_{\perp}^{1}
, is given by

(6.5)

a\left[u,v\right]=\left\langle\partial_{x}u\,,\partial_{x}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+b[s_{\theta}u,s_{\theta}v]-\left\langle c_{\theta}u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}},

where
b
is as in (
2.8
).
By definition,
a[\cdot,\cdot]
is a sesquilinear Hermitian form, and its positivity follows from Proposition
4.6
.

By the arguments in
[
CMO07
]
, the inner products
a[\cdot,\cdot]
and
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}}
are equivalent in
H_{\perp}^{1}
. Denote by
\|u\|_{a}=\sqrt{a[u,u]}
the induced norm, then there exist two constants
0<k_{0}<K_{0}
such that
k_{0}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|u\|_{a}\leq K_{0}\|u\|_{H^{1}}
for every
u\in H_{\perp}^{1}
.
In what follows, we also will consider the functional space
Z_{\perp}=H_{\perp}^{1}\times L^{2}
with two norms,
\|(u,v)\|_{Z_{\perp}}=\sqrt{\|u\|_{a}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}
and
\|(u,v)\|_{2}=\|u\|_{a}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}
.
By elementary algebra,
\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\leq a+b\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}
for
a,b\geq 0
; therefore both norms are equivalent, that is

\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}

for every
U_{\perp}\in H_{\perp}^{1}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The space
Z_{\perp}=H_{\perp}^{1}\times L^{2}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
, and there exist constants
0<k<1<K
such that

k\|U_{\perp}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}\leq K\|U_{\perp}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}

for every
U_{\perp}\in Z_{\perp}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{\perp}}
is as in the latter paragraph. Moreover,
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{\perp}}
is induced by the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}:Z_{\perp}\times Z_{\perp}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\langle U_{\perp},V_{\perp}\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}:=a[u,w]+\left\langle v\,,z\right\rangle_{L^{2}},

for
U_{\perp}=(u,v)
and
V_{\perp}=(w,z)
in
Z_{\perp}
. Henceforth,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}
and
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{X}
are equivalent in
Z_{\perp}
.

Lemma
6.5
is relevant because the constants on the norm equivalence satisfy
k<1<K
.

Proof of Lemma
6.5
.

The Cartesian product
H_{\perp}^{1}\times L^{2}
is also a Hilbert space with respect to the induced inner product
\|\cdot,\cdot\|_{Z_{\perp}}
since
a[\cdot,\cdot]
and
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}}
are equivalent in
H^{1}_{\perp}
which is a Hilbert space equipped with the latter inner product. Now, because
k_{0}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|u\|_{a}\leq K_{0}\|u\|_{H^{1}}
for every
u\in H_{\perp}^{1}
, and
\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}
for every
U_{\perp}\in H_{\perp}^{1}
, we have that

\min\{1/\sqrt{2},k_{0}/\sqrt{2}\}\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\max\{1,K_{0}\}\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}.

The rest of the lemma’s claims follow directly from the latter equivalence between the norms.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

For
U,V\in Z=H^{1}\times L^{2}
, let
U=U_{\perp}+\alpha\Theta
and
V=V_{\perp}+\beta\Theta
be the decomposition of Lemma
6.3
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}
as in Lemma
6.5
.
We define

\langle U,V\rangle_{Z}:=\langle U_{\perp},V_{\perp}\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}+\left\langle U\,,\beta\Theta\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\left\langle\alpha\Theta\,,V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\alpha\beta^{*}\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Then,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z}:Z\times Z\to\mathbb{C}
is an inner product equivalent to
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
.

Proof.

The form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z}:Z\times Z\to\mathbb{C}
is Hermitian and sesquilinear because it is the sum of four sesquiliner Hermitian inner products in the corresponding functional spaces. Next, for
U=U_{\perp}+\alpha\Theta\in Z
, we have that

\langle U,U\rangle_{Z}=\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}\,\!\!\left\langle U\,,\alpha\Theta\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\left\|\alpha\Theta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Adding and subtracting
\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}
to the latter equation, yields

(6.6)

\langle U,U\rangle_{Z}=\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}^{2}+\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Now, by Lemma
6.5
, there exists positive constants
k<1<K
such that

k\|U_{\perp}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}\leq K\|U_{\perp}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}.

Combining this equivalence between norms with (
6.6
), we get

\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}-(1-k^{2})\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}\leq\langle U,U\rangle_{Z}\leq\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}+(K^{2}-1)\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2},

and because
\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}\geq\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}
with equality only if
\alpha=0
, we conclude

k^{2}\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}\leq\langle U,U\rangle_{Z}\leq K^{2}\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z}
and
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
are equivalent inner products in
Z
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

There exists
\eta_{0}>0
depending only on
\overline{\theta}
, such that

\mathrm{Re}\,\!\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,U\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\eta_{0}\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2},

for every
U\in D({\mathcal{A}})
.

Proof.

Recall that
D({\mathcal{A}})=H^{2}\times H^{1}
, denote
Z_{\perp}=H^{1}_{\perp}\times L^{2}
, and let
U=U_{\perp}+\alpha\Theta\in D({\mathcal{A}})
as in Lemma
6.3
. Regarding
U_{\perp}
’s components,
U_{\perp}=(u_{\perp},v)
where
u_{\perp}\in H^{1}_{\perp}
and
v=v_{\perp}+\beta\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in H^{1}
with
v_{\perp}\in H^{1}_{\perp}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.

By Lemma
5.5
,

\Theta
is an eigenfunction with zero eigenvalue
{\mathcal{A}}
. Thus,

{\mathcal{A}}U={\mathcal{A}}U_{\perp}=V_{\perp}+\beta\Theta,\quad\text{where }\quad V_{\perp}=(v_{\perp},-\nu v-\opl u_{\perp})\in Z_{\perp},

and

(6.7)

\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U,U\right\rangle_{Z}=\langle V_{\perp},U_{\perp}\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}+\left\langle V\,,\alpha\Theta\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\left\langle\beta\Theta\,,U\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\alpha^{*}\beta\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2},

for
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{Z}
as given in Lemma
6.6
and
V=V_{\perp}+\beta\Theta
.
By Lemma
6.5
,
we have that

\langle V_{\perp},U_{\perp}\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}=a[v_{\perp},u_{\perp}]-\langle\opl u_{\perp},v_{\perp}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\nu\langle v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}-\beta^{*}\langle\opl u_{\perp},\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\rangle,

and since
\langle\opl u_{\perp},v_{\perp}\rangle_{L^{2}}=a[u_{\perp},v_{\perp}]
,
\opl
is self adjoint, and
\opl\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}=0
by Proposition
4.6
,
we get

(6.8)

\langle V_{\perp},U_{\perp}\rangle_{Z_{\perp}}=2i\,\mathrm{Im}\,a[w,u]-\nu\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using integration by parts, and that

\Theta=(\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},0)
, we find

(6.9)

\langle V,\alpha\Theta\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\langle\partial_{x}v_{\perp},\alpha\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle v_{\perp},\alpha\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Thus, by substitution of (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) into (
6.7
), we get

\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U,U\right\rangle_{Z}=2i\,\mathrm{Im}\,a[w,u]-\nu\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle v_{\perp},\alpha\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\langle\beta\Theta\,,U\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\alpha^{*}\beta\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2},

and, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the real part of the latter equation is bounded as

2\mathrm{Re}\,\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U,U\right\rangle_{Z}\leq\|v_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(|\beta|^{2}+2\alpha^{*}\beta)\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
and
\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}\neq 0
by Proposition
2.1
, we define the constants

C_{1}:=\left\|\Theta\right\|_{X}^{2}/\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
C_{2}:=\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}/\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, to get

2\mathrm{Re}\,\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U,U\right\rangle_{Z}\leq\|v_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}+(|\alpha|^{2}C_{1}+(|\beta|^{2}+2\alpha^{*}\beta)C_{2})\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Because
\max\{\|v_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}},|\beta|^{2}\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}\}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}
,

\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}
, we find

2\mathrm{Re}\,\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U,U\right\rangle_{Z}\leq(3+2C_{2}+2C_{1})\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Finally, the result follows because
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Z}
and
\left\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
are equivalent inner products by Lemma
6.6
, and the resulting constant
\eta_{0}=K^{2}(3/2+C_{2}+C_{1})>0
only depends on
\overline{\theta}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

From Lemma
6.7
, there exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that

\mathrm{Re}\,\!\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,U\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\eta_{0}\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2},

for every
U\in D({\mathcal{A}})
. In addition,
\mathbb{R}^{+}\subset\rho({\mathcal{A}})
by Theorem
5.1
. Thus, by choosing
\tau>\eta_{0}
with
\eta_{0}
as above, we conclude that
{\mathcal{A}}-\tau
is onto. Finally,

{\mathcal{A}}
is densely defined in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
by definition.
Therefore, the result follows by the classical Lumer-Phillips theorem.
∎

6.2.
The adjoint operator

Our proof of the semigroup’s exponentially decay requires the analysis of
{\mathcal{A}}
’s formal adjoint operator
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
. This subsection is devoted to presenting this analysis.

In the present context, because
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
are reflexive Hilbert spaces, then
{\mathcal{A}}:H^{1}\times L^{2}\to H^{1}\times L^{2}
with
D({\mathcal{A}})=H^{2}\times H^{1}
has a formal adjoint which is also densely defined and closed. Moreover,
{\mathcal{A}}^{**}={\mathcal{A}}
(cf.
[
Kat95
]
, Theorem 5.29, p. 168).

Lemma 6.8
.

The formal adjoint
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
, restricted to the domain
D({\mathcal{A}})
, is given by

(6.10)

\left.{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\right|_{D({\mathcal{A}})}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathcal{F}}\\
-\partial_{xx}+\mathrm{I}&-\nu\end{pmatrix}

where the operator
{\mathcal{F}}:H^{1}\to H^{-1}
is formally defined as the map

v\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}v=-({\mathcal{S}}v-c_{\theta}v,\partial_{x}v).

Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}|_{H^{2}}=[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}\opl
, where
[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}u
denotes the convolution of
u
with the Bessel potential of order
2
.

Proof.

Denote
Z=H^{1}\times L^{2}
, and
let
U=(u,v)
and
V=(w,z)
be both in
D({\mathcal{A}})=H^{2}\times H^{1}
. By definition of the inner product in
Z
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle v\,,w\right\rangle_{H^{1}}-\left\langle\opl u+\nu v\,,z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle v\,,w-\nu z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle\opl u\,,z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\langle\partial_{x}v\,,\partial_{x}w\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Because of
w\in H^{2}
, integration by parts on
\left\langle\partial_{x}v\,,\partial_{x}w\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
yields

(6.11)

\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle v\,,-\partial^{2}_{x}w+w-\nu z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle\opl u\,,z\right\rangle_{L^{2}},

and by the symmetry of
{\mathcal{S}}
(see Lemma
4.8
), we get

\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle v\,,-\partial^{2}_{x}w+w-\nu z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{x}u\,,\partial_{x}z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle u\,,{\mathcal{S}}z-c_{\theta}z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Therefore,
\left\langle{\mathcal{A}}U\,,V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle U\,,{\mathcal{A}}^{*}V\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
for
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
as in (
6.10
) where
{\mathcal{F}}z=-({\mathcal{S}}z-c_{\theta}z,\partial_{x}z)\in H^{-1}
.

The Bessel potential’s Fourier symbol of order
k=2
is
\hat{{\mathcal{K}}}=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-1}
. Since
\opl
is self adjoint, by Plancherel’s identity applied twice to (
6.11
)’s last term, we get

\left\langle\opl u\,,z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle u\,,\opl z\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\hat{u}\,,\widehat{\opl z}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\hat{u}\,,(1+|\xi|^{2})\widehat{{\mathcal{K}}\opl z}(\xi)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle u\,,{\mathcal{K}}\opl z\right\rangle_{H^{1}},

where
z\in H^{2}
and last equality holds because
{\mathcal{K}}\opl z\in H^{1}
with
\left\|{\mathcal{K}}\opl z\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\left\|\opl z\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
be the formal adjoint of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Also, let
\left.{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\right|_{D({\mathcal{A}})}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
be as in Lemma
6.8
and define

(6.12)

\Phi:=(\nu[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}\ \partial_{x}\overline{\theta},\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}).

Then
\Phi\in X^{*}
is an eigenvector of the adjoint
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}:X^{*}\to X^{*}
, associated to the isolated, simple eigenvalue
\lambda=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
, the operator
{\mathcal{A}}:H^{1}\times L^{2}\to H^{1}\times L^{2}
has
\lambda=0
as a simple eigenvalue, therefore
\lambda=0
is also eigenvalue of
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
with the same geometric and algebraic multiplicities (see Kato
[
Kat95
]
, Remark 6.23, p. 184).

By the Plancherel’s identity we have

(6.13)

\left\|[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}\ \partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\nu^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}\left|\widehat{\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}}\right|^{2}d\xi=\nu^{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus,
[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}\ \partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\in H^{2}
, and

\Phi\in H^{2}\times H^{2}\subset D({\mathcal{A}})
by
(c)
in Proposition
2.1
. Now, because
H^{2}\subset H^{-1}
and
(H^{1}\times L^{2})^{*}=H^{-1}\times L^{2}
in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
, we have that
\Phi\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})^{*}
.
Finally, from Lemma
6.8
,

{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\Phi=\left.{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\right|_{D({\mathcal{A}})}\Phi=({\mathcal{F}}\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},0)=({\mathcal{K}}\opl\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},0)=(0,0),

where last equality holds due to Bessel potential’s
L^{2}
invertibility, and that
\opl\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}=0
.
∎

The following result gives the explicit representation of the functional
\left\langle\cdot\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})^{*}
in the dual of
L^{2}\times L^{2}
, and the projection operator
{\mathcal{P}}:H^{1}\times L^{2}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\Phi
as in (
6.12
),
\Phi_{0}:=(\nu\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},\partial_{x}\overline{\theta})\in L^{2}\times L^{2}
and
\Theta=(\partial_{x}\overline{\theta},0)
. Then,

(6.14)

\left\langle F\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle F\,,\Phi_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}},

for every
F\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
. Moreover, the operator
{\mathcal{P}}:H^{1}\times L^{2}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
given by

{\mathcal{P}}U:=U-\left\langle\Theta\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{-1}\left\langle U\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\Theta,

is a projector with

{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{P}})=\left\{F\in H^{1}\times L^{2}\ \middle|\ \left\langle F\,,\Phi_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\right\}.

Proof.

We argue in Fourier space; let
F=(f,g)\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
, then

\left\langle F\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle f\,,\nu[1+(-\Delta)]^{-1}\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\rangle_{H^{1}}+\left\langle g\,,\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle f\,,\nu\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\langle g\,,\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Hence,
\left\langle F\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle F\,,\Phi_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
, as claimed.
Next, we note that

\left\langle\Theta\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\langle\Theta\,,\Phi_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\nu\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0
so that
{\mathcal{P}}
is well defined. Since,
{\mathcal{P}}
is obviously linear and
{\mathcal{P}}F=F
for every
F\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
, it is a projection. The
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{P}})
characterization is a consequence of (
6.14
).
∎

6.3.
Exponential decay of the semigroup

In this subsection, we show that the
the restricted operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
is the generator of a exponentially decaying
C_{0}
-semigroup, namely we prove Theorem
6.2
. The proof strategy is to apply Gearhart-Prüss theorem. To this end,
we need to show that

(6.15)

\sup_{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0}\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}\|_{(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}}<\infty.

Condition (
6.15
) is satisfied provided that any solution
U\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
to the linear equation
(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{1})U=F
for
F\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
satisfies a resolvent estimate of the form
\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq C(\lambda)\left\|F\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}
, in which the constant
C(\lambda)
is bounded for every
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0
.

We begin with some preparatory results.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}:H^{1}\times L^{2}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
be the projection operator as in Lemma
6.10
. Then,

e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{P}}e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

In the reflexive Banach space
H^{1}\times L^{2}
, weak and weak
∗
topologies coincide. Therefore, the family of dual operators
\{(e^{t{\mathcal{A}}})^{*}\}_{t\geq 0}
, consisting of all the corresponding formal adjoints in
(H^{1}\times L^{2})^{*}
, is also
C_{0}
-semigroup (cf.
[
EN00
]
, p. 44), and the infinitesimal generator of this semigroup is
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
(see Corollary 10.6 in
[
Paz83
]
). Hence,

(6.16)

(e^{t{\mathcal{A}}})^{*}=e^{t{\mathcal{A}}^{*}}.

Next, let
U\in H^{1}\times L^{2}
, we have

{\mathcal{P}}e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U=e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U-\left\langle\Theta\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{-1}\left\langle e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\Theta.

Because of (
6.16
), we note that

\left\langle e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\Theta=\left\langle U\,,e^{t{\mathcal{A}}^{*}}\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\Theta=\left\langle U\,,\Phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}\Theta,

where we used that
e^{t{\mathcal{A}}^{*}}\Phi=\Phi
and
e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}\Theta=\Theta
for the last equality.
Therefore, combining the above equations we find
{\mathcal{P}}e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U=e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{P}}U
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{\perp}
be as in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), respectively.
Assume
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
is as in Theorem
6.2
,
then

\sigma({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq-\zeta_{0}(\nu)<0\},

with
\zeta_{0}(\nu)
given by (
5.3
).
In particular,
\lambda=0
is not spectrum of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
.

Proof.

By the spectral decomposition theorem
\sigma({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})\subset\sigma({\mathcal{A}})
(see
[
Kat95
]
), but
0\notin\sigma({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})
because
{\mathcal{P}}\Theta=0
and
\Theta\neq 0
so that
\Theta\notin(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
. Therefore, by Theorem
5.1
, we have that
\sigma({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})\subset\sigma({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})\setminus\{0\}\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq-\zeta_{0}(\nu)<0\}
as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.13
.

The family of operators
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, defined as

e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}}U:=e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}U,

for
U\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
and
t\geq 0
, is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of quasicontractions in the Hilbert space
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
with infinitesimal generator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
6.11
we concludes that
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
is an
e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}
-invariant closed Hilbert subspace of
H^{1}\times L^{2}
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}
is the restricted semigroup infinitesimal generator of
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}}\}_{t\geq 0}
(see Section 2.3 of
[
EN00
]
), where the semigroup properties are inherited from those of
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
.
∎

In the next result, we show the resolvent estimate needed to show (
6.15
).

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
be two positive constants, and
k
and
K
the constants in Lemma
6.5
. For
\lambda\in\rho\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)
, assume that
f,g,u,v\in L^{2}_{\perp}
are such that
F_{\perp}=(f,g)\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
,
U_{\perp}=(u,v)\in D_{\perp}
and

(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})U_{\perp}=F_{\perp}.

Then, if
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>C_{0}
or
|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda|>C_{1}
, the following estimate holds

\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq C(C_{0},C_{1},\nu)\left\|F_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}},

where

C(C_{0},C_{1},\nu)=\frac{2\sqrt{2}K}{kC_{0}}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{2K\sqrt{8C_{1}^{2}+2\nu^{2}}}{k\nu C_{1}},

whenever
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>C_{0}
or
|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda|>C_{1}
, respectively.

Proof.

First, we claim that, under the lemma’s assumptions,

(6.17)

\left|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}\|F_{\perp}\|_{2}.

Indeed, in terms of its components
(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})U_{\perp}=F_{\perp}
is given by

\lambda u-v=f\quad\text{and}\quad\opl u+(\lambda+\nu)v=g.

Take the
L^{2}
-product of latter equations, with
\opl u
and
v
, respectively. The result is

\lambda^{*}\left\langle\opl u\,,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle\opl u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\opl u\,,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\langle\opl u\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\langle g\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

By Definition
6.4
, we write the latter equations in terms of the sesquilinear form
a[\cdot,\cdot]
as

\lambda^{*}a[u,u]-a[u,v]=a[u,f],\quad\text{and}\quad a[u,v]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\langle g\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Then, adding both equations, taking its complex modulus, and using triangle inequality yields

\left|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq|a[u,f]|+|\left\langle g\,,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}|.

Because
a
is a Hermitian sesquilinear nonnegative form, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds for
a
in
H^{1}_{\perp}
. Hence,

\left|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq a^{1/2}[u,u]a^{1/2}[f,f]+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}},

and inequality (
6.17
) follows since the right hand side is bounded by

\left[\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}+a^{1/2}[u,u]\right]\left[\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}+a^{1/2}[f,f]\right]=\|U\|_{2}\|F\|_{2}.

Second, let
a,b,\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
By elementary algebra,

(6.18)

|\lambda^{*}a+(\lambda+\nu)b|^{2}=(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\ a+(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda+\nu)b)^{2}+(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}(a-b)^{2}.

Thus, assuming
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0
, we get

|\lambda^{*}a+(\lambda+\nu)b|^{2}\geq(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda)^{2}(a+b)^{2}
.
By letting
a=a[u,u]
and
b=\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in the last inequality, we find

\left|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\geq\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}^{2}.

Now, by the equivalence between the the norms
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{\perp}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
(see paragraph before Lemma
6.5
), we get

(6.19)

\left|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\geq\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}{2}\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}^{2}.

Combining (
6.17
) and (
6.19
), we obtain

(6.20)

\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}\|F_{\perp}\|_{2}\leq K(C_{0})\|F_{\perp}\|_{2}

whenever
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>C_{0}
and
K(C_{0})=2/C_{0}
.

Now, from (
6.18
) we also get

|\lambda^{*}a+(\lambda+\nu)b|^{2}\geq\nu^{2}b^{2}+(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}(a-b)^{2}.

Let
a=r^{2}\sin^{2}t
and
b=r^{2}\cos^{2}t
for
t\in[0,\pi/2]
. Thus, after this change of variables and some computations, we get

|\lambda^{*}a+(\lambda+\nu)b|\geq\left[\left((\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{4}\right)\cos^{2}(2t)+\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}\cos(2t)+\frac{\nu^{2}}{4}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{2}\geq\left[\frac{\nu^{2}(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}}{4(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}+\nu^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{2},

where the last inequality follows by computing the quadratic equation minimum for
\cos(2t)
, which is attained at
\cos(2t)=-\nu^{2}/(4\mathrm{Im}\,(\lambda)^{2}+4\nu^{2})
.
Therefore, by letting
a=a[u,u]
and
b=\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we have that
r^{2}=a[u,u]+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}^{2}
. In addition, from the last inequality, we get

|\lambda^{*}a[u,u]+(\lambda+\nu)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}|\geq\frac{\nu|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda|}{\sqrt{4(\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda)^{2}+\nu^{2}}}\|U_{\perp}\|_{Z_{\perp}}^{2}.

Arguing as before, we conclude that

(6.21)

\|U_{\perp}\|_{2}\leq K(C_{1},\nu)\|F_{\perp}\|_{2},

whenever
|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda|\geq C_{1}
and

K(C_{1},\nu)=2\sqrt{4C_{1}^{2}+\nu^{2}}/\nu C_{1}
.
Finally, the results follows from (
6.20
), (
6.21
) and the equivalence between norms in Lemma
6.5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

By Lemma
6.13
, the operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}:(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}
is the infinitesimal generator of
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\}_{t\geq 0}
of quasicontractions.
By Lemma
6.12
,
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq-\zeta_{0}(\nu)<0\}\subset\rho(A_{\perp})
. Therefore, the result follows from the Gearhart-Prüss theorem provided we show that

(6.22)

\sup_{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0}\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}\|_{(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}}<\infty

for every
\lambda\in\rho({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})
holds. The rest of the proof is devoted to show (
6.22
).

Let
\lambda\in\rho({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})
so that
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0
, and split
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0\}
into two disjoint sets:

\begin{array}[]{l}S_{0}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ 0\leq\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq C_{0},\ |\textrm{Im}\left(\lambda\right)|\leq C_{1}\}\quad\text{ and }\\
S_{1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ 0\leq\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\leq C_{0},\ C_{1}<|\textrm{Im}\left(\lambda\right)|\}\cup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ C_{0}<\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\}.\end{array}

First, we consider the set
S_{0}
. Because,
\lambda\to(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}
is continuous and the reversed triangle’s inequality,

\left|\ \|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{1})^{-1}\|-\|(\mu-{\mathcal{A}}_{1})^{-1}\|\ \right|\leq\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{1})^{-1}-(\mu-{\mathcal{A}}_{1})^{-1}\|.

holds for every
\lambda,\ \mu\in\rho({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})
,
it follows that the map
\lambda\to\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}\|
is continuous.
Thus, since
S_{0}\subset\rho({\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})
is compact, there exists
K>0
such that
\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}\|\leq K
for every
\lambda\in S_{0}
.

Second, we consider the set
S_{1}
. Let,
u_{\perp}\in H^{2}_{\perp}
,
v_{\perp},f_{\perp}\in H^{1}_{\perp}
,
g_{\perp}\in L_{\perp}^{2}
and
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
. Assume that
U=(u,v)+\alpha(1,-\nu)\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
and
F=(f,g)+\gamma(1,-\nu)\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
are such that

(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})U=F.

From the last equation we get

(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})U_{\perp}=F_{\perp}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha(\lambda+\nu)=\gamma,

where
U_{\perp}=(u,v)
and
F_{\perp}=(f,g)
.
Thus, by Lemma
6.14
,

\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq C(C_{0},C_{1},\nu)\left\|F_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}},

for every
\lambda\in S_{1}
.
Hence,

\left\|U\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\left\|U_{\perp}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}+\frac{\left\|\gamma(1,-\nu)\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}}{|\lambda+\nu|}\leq\left(C(C_{0},C_{1},\nu)+\frac{1}{|\lambda+\nu|}\right)\left\|F\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}.

Therefore,
\|(\lambda-{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp})^{-1}\|
is bounded for every
\lambda\in S_{1}
, and (
6.22
) follows from the latter and the estimates for
S_{0}
.
∎

7.
Nonlinear (orbital) stability

In this section, we study the stability of solutions
\theta(x,t)
to the Cauchy problem (
2.13
), provided they exist, for initial conditions close to the static Néel wall profile
\overline{\theta}
, and we present the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

By letting
v=\partial_{t}u
, problem (
2.13
) can be written as a the following nonlinear system of equations

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}W&=F(W),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,\\
W(x,0)&=W_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{aligned}\right.

where
W=(\theta,\varphi)^{\top}
,
F(W)=(\varphi,-\nu\varphi-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\theta))^{\top}
,
and
W_{0}=(u_{0},v_{0})^{\top}
.

The nonlinear term in
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)
is invariant under space translations (see Lemma 2.6 in
[
Mel03
]
). Thus, if
\overline{\theta}
denotes the phase of the static Néel wall, then

\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta))=0
for every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Equation (
7.1
) inherits this translation symmetry as

(7.2)

F(\phi(\delta))=0,\quad\mbox{for }\quad\phi(\delta)=(\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta),0)^{\top}\quad\text{ and every }\delta\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, the derivative
F
with respect to
\delta
is
DF(\phi(\delta))\phi^{\prime}(\delta)=0
. Therefore, zero is an eigenvalue of the
DF(\phi(\delta))
with eigenfunction
\phi^{\prime}(\delta)
.

The linearization of (
7.1
) around the time-independent solution
\phi(\delta)
is

(7.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}V&={\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}V\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,\\
V(x,0)&=V_{0}(x)\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{aligned}\right.

where,

{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{I}\\
-\opl^{\delta}&-\nu\mathrm{I}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\opl^{\delta}u=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\nabla{\mathcal{E}}\left(\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta)+\epsilon u\right)\right|_{\epsilon=0}.

The base spaces of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{\delta}
are

H^{1}\times L^{2}
and
L^{2}
, respectively. For
delta=0
, we identify

{\mathcal{A}}^{0}:={\mathcal{A}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\opl^{0}:=\opl.

Translations by
\delta
in the arguments of
\overline{\theta}

correspond to the left translation operator
T_{l}(\delta)
, which is an
L^{2}
-isometry and a
C_{0}
-semigroup with generator (see
[
EN00
]
). Because
\overline{\theta}\in H^{1}
, we have that

\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}(x+\delta)\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\partial_{x}T_{l}(\delta)\overline{\theta}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|T_{l}(\delta)\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus,
T_{l}(\delta)
is also a
H^{1}
-isometry.
Therefore, all results presented in the previous sections for
\delta=0
hold for any
\delta\neq 0
. To keep track of the
\delta
dependence, we use the notation
\Phi_{0}(\delta)=(\nu\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta),\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta))
,
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}^{\delta}=\{F\in H^{1}\times L^{2}\ |\ \left\langle F\,,\Phi_{0}(\delta)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\}
,
{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}
for the projection operator into
\{\Phi_{0}(\delta)\}^{\perp}
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}^{\delta}:(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}^{\delta}\to(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}^{\delta}
, etcetera.

In particular, due to Theorem
6.1
,
there exists a unique solution to (
7.3
) in
(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}^{\delta}
given by the action of
a
C_{0}
-semigroup of quasicontraction generated by
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}
. Moreover, because of Theorem
6.2
, there exist constants
M\geq 1
and
\tilde{\omega}>0
such that

\|e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\perp}^{\delta}}V_{0}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq Me^{-\tilde{\omega}t}\|V_{0}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}

for every
V_{0}\in(H^{1}\times L^{2})_{\perp}^{\delta}
. It is important to point out that the spectral and growth bounds
are independent of
\delta
because they depend only on the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms of
\overline{\theta}(\cdot+\delta)
.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
nonlinear stability result follows from an implicit function theorem in Hilbert spaces given by Lattanzio
et al.

[
LMPS16
]
based on a similar result for Banach spaces presented by Sattinger
[
Sat76
]
. We present the former result here to ease the reading.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open neighborhood of
\delta=0
. Assume that
F:{\mathcal{D}}\subset X\rightarrow X
and
\phi:I\subset\mathbb{R}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies
F(\phi)=0
. If
{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}
is the projector onto
\{\phi^{\prime}(\delta)\}^{\perp}_{X}
and there exist positive constants
C_{0},\delta_{0},M,\omega,
and
\gamma
such that

(H1)

for every solution
V=V(t,V_{0},\delta)
to (
7.3
),

\|{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}V(t,V_{0},\delta)\|_{X}\leq C_{0}e^{-\omega t}\|{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}V_{0}\|_{X},

(H2)

\phi
is differentiable at
\delta=0
with

\|\phi(\delta)-\phi(0)-\phi^{\prime}(0)\delta\|_{X}\leq C_{0}|\delta|^{1+\gamma},

for
|\delta|<\delta_{0}
, and

(H3)

F
is differentiable at
\phi(\delta)
for every
\delta\in(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})
with

(7.4)

\|F(\phi(\delta)+W)-F(\phi(\delta))-DF(\phi(\delta))W\|_{X}\leq C_{0}\|W\|_{X}^{1+\gamma},

for
\|W\|_{X}\leq M
.

Then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for any
W_{0}\in B_{\epsilon}(\phi(0))\subset X
there exists
\delta\in I
and a positive constant C for which the solution
W(t;W_{0})
to the nonlinear system (

) satisfies

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Let
X=H^{1}\times L^{2}
,
{\mathcal{D}}:=H^{2}\times H^{1}
, and

F(W)=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi\\
-\nu\varphi-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}.

Now, the proof of Theorem
2.3
follows from Theorem
7.1
by verifying its assumption.

Let
\phi(\delta)=(\overline{\theta}_{\delta},0)
, we have that
F(\phi(\delta))=0
for every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
by (
7.2
). Denote by
V(t,V_{0},\delta)
the solution of (
7.3
) with initial condition
V_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}
. Assume
{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}
is the projection operator into
\{\Phi_{0}(\delta)\}^{\perp}
. Then, condition
(H1)
follows from Theorem
6.2
.

Because
\overline{\theta}\in H^{2}
is a real-valued smooth function, we have that

\|\phi(\delta)-\phi(0)-\phi^{\prime}(0)\delta\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\|\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\overline{\theta}-\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\delta\|_{H^{1}}.

By the Taylor series’ remainder integral representation, for the latter term we estimated

|\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\overline{\theta}-\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\delta|^{2}=\delta^{4}\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}(x+t\delta)\,dt\right|^{2}\leq\delta^{4}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2}\,\left(\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}(x+t\delta)\right)^{2}\,dt,

where the last inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality. Integration in
x
over
\mathbb{R}
leads to

\left\|\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\overline{\theta}-\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\delta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\delta^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2}\,\left(\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}(x+t\delta)\right)^{2}\,dt\,dx.

Now, by the positivity of the last equation’s integrand, we change the integration order, using that
\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}(x+t\delta)=T_{l}(t\delta)\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}
and that
T_{l}(t\delta)
is an
L^{2}
-isometry, to get

\left\|\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\overline{\theta}-\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}\delta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\delta^{4}\left\|\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2}\,dt.

A similar argument for
\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}
gives

\left\|\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\partial_{x}\overline{\theta}-\partial^{2}_{x}\overline{\theta}\delta\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\delta^{4}\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2}\,dt.

Therefore,

\left\|\phi(\delta)-\phi(0)-\phi^{\prime}(0)\delta\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\frac{\left\|\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\theta}\right\|_{H^{1}}}{\sqrt{3}}\,\delta^{2},

and
(H2)
follows for
\gamma=1
.

Let
W=(w_{1},w_{2})\in H^{2}\times H^{1}
, then

F(\phi(\delta))=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}\left(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right)\end{pmatrix},\quad F(\phi(\delta)+W)=\begin{pmatrix}w_{2}\\
-\nu w_{2}-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}\left(w_{1}+\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right)\end{pmatrix},

and

DF(\phi(\delta))W={\mathcal{A}}^{\delta}W=\begin{pmatrix}w_{2}\\
-\nu w_{2}-{\mathcal{L}}^{\delta}w_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, for (
7.4
)’s right hand side, we get

\left\|F(\phi(\delta)+W)-F(\phi(\delta))-DF(\phi(\delta))W\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}=\left\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta})-\opl^{\delta}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}},

where, by Proposition
2.1

(b)
,

\displaystyle\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-\partial_{x}^{2}w_{1}-\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})\,{\mathcal{T}}\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1}),

\displaystyle-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta})

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\,{\mathcal{T}}(\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}),

\displaystyle-\opl^{\delta}w_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{2}w_{1}-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}{\mathcal{T}}(\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}w_{1})+w_{1}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}{\mathcal{T}}(\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}),

and we use the notation
{\mathcal{T}}:=1+(-\Delta)^{1/2}
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}:=\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\theta}_{\delta})-\opl^{\delta}w_{1}
, thus

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=-\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})\,{\mathcal{T}}(\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1}))+\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\,{\mathcal{T}}(\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta})+

\displaystyle-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}{\mathcal{T}}(\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}w_{1})+w_{1}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}{\mathcal{T}}(\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}).

By adding and subtracting
\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})\,{\mathcal{T}}(\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta})-w_{1}\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}))
to the latter, we get
{\mathcal{K}}=A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}
where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})\,{\mathcal{T}}\left(\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right),

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle:=(\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}){\mathcal{T}}(w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}),

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle:=-(\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-w_{1}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}){\mathcal{T}}(\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}).

By standard calculus arguments, we have that

(7.5)

\displaystyle|\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}|

\displaystyle\leq|w_{1}|,

\displaystyle|\sin(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}-w_{1}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}w_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle|\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}w_{1}^{2}.

Because
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a Banach algebra and
w_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that
w_{1}^{2}\in H^{1}
.
Moreover, by the Sobolev embedding theorem,

\left\|w_{1}^{2}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left(\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4\left\|\partial_{x}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq 4\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{4}.

Thus, by (
7.5
), we get

(7.6)

\left\|A_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|\cos(\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1})-\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}+w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

where we use that
\left\|{\mathcal{T}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}}
for every
u\in H^{1}
in the first inequality. Analogously,

\left\|A_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\||w_{1}|{\mathcal{T}}(w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C^{2}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

\left\|A_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{1}^{2}{\mathcal{T}}\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}C^{2}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\left\|\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

and because
\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}
and
\left\|w_{1}\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\|\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
,
we get

(7.7)

\left\|A_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\sin\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|A_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}C\left\|\cos\overline{\theta}_{\delta}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Finally, by combining (
7.6
) and (
7.7
), we conclude that

\left\|{\mathcal{K}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|A_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|A_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|A_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\left\|W\right\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,
(H3)
holds for
\gamma=1
and the proof is complete.

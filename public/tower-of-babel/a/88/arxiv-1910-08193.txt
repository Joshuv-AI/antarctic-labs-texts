Abstract
To the best of our knowledge, there are very few results on how Heyting-valued models are affected by the morphisms on the complete Heyting algebras that determine them: the only cases found in the literature are concerning automorphisms of complete Boolean algebras and complete embeddings between them ( i.e ., injective Boolean algebra homomorphisms that preserve arbitrary suprema and arbitrary infima). In the present work, we consider and explore how more general kinds of morphisms between complete Heyting algebras ℍ \mathbb{H} and ℍ ′ \mathbb{H}^{\prime} induce arrows between V ( ℍ ) \text{V}^{(\mathbb{H})} and V ( ℍ ′ ) \text{V}^{(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})} , and between their corresponding localic topoi 𝐒𝐞𝐭 ( ℍ ) \mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)} ( ≃ 𝐒𝐡 ⁡ ( ℍ ) \simeq\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}\right) ) and 𝐒𝐞𝐭 ( ℍ ′ ) \mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)} ( ≃ 𝐒𝐡 ⁡ ( ℍ ′ ) \simeq\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right) ). More specifically: any geometric morphism f ∗ : 𝐒𝐞𝐭 ( ℍ ) → 𝐒𝐞𝐭 ( ℍ ′ ) f^{*}:\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}\to\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)} (that automatically came from a unique locale morphism f : ℍ → ℍ ′ f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime} ), can be “lifted” to an arrow f ~ : V ( ℍ ) → V ( ℍ ′ ) \tilde{f}:\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H})}\to\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})} . We also provide some semantic preservation results concerning this arrow f ~ : V ( ℍ ) → V ( ℍ ′ ) \tilde{f}:\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H})}\to\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})} .

Proposition 3.7
.

For all

3.2

Theorem 3.8
.

For all
\left\langle x,x^{\prime}\right\rangle,\left\langle y,y^{\prime}\right\rangle,\left\langle z,z^{\prime}\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}
,

f\left(\left\llbracket y\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\leq^{\prime}\left\llbracket y^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad f\left(\left\llbracket x=z\right\rrbracket\right)\leq^{\prime}\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the well-founded relation

\left\langle u,x\right\rangle\prec\left\langle v,y\right\rangle\iff(u=v\text{ and }x\in\text{dom}\left({y}\right))\text{ or }(u\in\text{dom}\left({v}\right)\text{ and }x=y)

Let
\varepsilon:\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
satisfying the conditions of
3.1
. Then:

\displaystyle f\left(\left\llbracket y\in x\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(u)\wedge\left\llbracket u=y\right\rrbracket\right)

(by definition)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\wedge^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket u=y\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle\left(\text{since $f$ preserves $\wedge,\bigvee$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\left(\text{induction hypothesis $\left\langle u,\varepsilon(u)\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}$)}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\left(\text{using that $\left\langle x,x^{\prime}\right\rangle,\left\langle u,\varepsilon(u)\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}$}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(u^{\prime})\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket u^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket

(since
\varepsilon
is surjective)

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket y^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(by definition)

Now, since
\mathbb{H}
is a Heyting algebra, note that the fact that
f
preserves meets implies that
f
is increasing, and also implies that
f(a\to b)\leq f(a)\to f(b)
, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{H}
. With that, let
\tau:\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({z^{\prime}}\right)
satisfying the conditions of
3.1
. Then:

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\left(\left\llbracket x=z\right\rrbracket\right)=

\displaystyle=f\left(\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\\
v\in\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\end{subarray}}\left(x(u)\to\left\llbracket u\in z\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge\left(z(v)\to\left\llbracket v\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\right)

(by definition)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\\
v\in\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\end{subarray}}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f\left(x(u)\to\left\llbracket u\in z\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}f\left(z(v)\to\left\llbracket v\in x\right\rrbracket\right)

(
f
is increasing)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\\
v\in\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\end{subarray}}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}\left(f(x(u))\to^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket u\in z\right\rrbracket\right)\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left(f(z(v))\to^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket v\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\right)

(comments above)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\\
v\in\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\end{subarray}}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}\left(f(x(u))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)\in z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left(f(z(v))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\tau(v)\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)

(comments below)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\\
v\in\text{dom}\left({z}\right)\end{subarray}}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}\left(x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)\in z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left(z^{\prime}(\tau(v))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\tau(v)\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)

(elements of
\tilde{f}
)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)\\
v^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({z^{\prime}}\right)\end{subarray}}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}\left(x^{\prime}(u^{\prime})\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket u^{\prime}\in z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left(z^{\prime}(v^{\prime})\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket v^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\right)

(
\varepsilon,\tau
are surjections)

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(by definition)

In the fourth step, we use that the implication is increasing in the second coordinate, and that by the induction hypothesis we have:

f\left(\left\llbracket u\in z\right\rrbracket\right)\leq\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)\in z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\quad\text{ and }\quad f\left(\left\llbracket v\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\leq\left\llbracket\tau(v)\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

∎

This result easily extends to positive formulas (with only
\wedge,\vee
) with bounded quantifiers (of the form

and
\forall u\in x
), using that corollary 1.18 from

\left\llbracket\forall u\in x\ \varphi(u)\right\rrbracket=\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(u)\to\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\varphi
be a positive formula with bounded quantifiers. Then, for all
\left\langle a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime}\right\rangle,...,\left\langle a_{n},a_{n}^{\prime}\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}
, we have:

f\left(\left\llbracket\varphi(a_{1},...,a_{n})\right\rrbracket\right)\leq^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(a_{1}^{\prime},...,a_{n}^{\prime})\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

Proof.

By induction in the complexity of the formula. The initial case, for atomic sentences, was shown in the previous theorem, and the cases with
\wedge
and
\vee
are immediate from the fact that
f
preserves finite meets and joins. For quantifiers, the proof is similar to last theorem’s proof. Let
\left\langle x,x^{\prime}\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}
with witness
\varepsilon:\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
. Then:

\displaystyle f\left(\left\llbracket\exists u\in x\ \varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(u)\wedge\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

(by definition)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\wedge^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle\left(\text{since $f$ preserves $\wedge,\bigvee$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(\varepsilon(u))\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\left(\text{by hypothesis and $\left\langle u,\varepsilon(u)\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}$)}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(\varepsilon(u))\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\left(\text{using that $\left\langle x,x^{\prime}\right\rangle,\left\langle u,\varepsilon(u)\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}$}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(u^{\prime})\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(u^{\prime})\right\rrbracket

(since
\varepsilon
is surjective)

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket\exists u^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\ \varphi(u^{\prime})\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(by definition)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle f\left(\left\llbracket\forall u\in x\ \varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(u)\to\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

(by definition)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f\left(x(u)\to\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

(since
f
is increasing)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\to^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket\varphi(u)\right\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle\left(\text{since $f(a\to b)\leq f(a)\to f(b)$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}f(x(u))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(\varepsilon(u))\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(\varepsilon(u))\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(using that
\left\langle x,x^{\prime}\right\rangle\in\tilde{f}
)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\limits_{u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}^{\hskip 18.0pt\prime}x^{\prime}(u^{\prime})\to^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varphi(u^{\prime})\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(since
\varepsilon
is surjective)

\displaystyle=\left\llbracket\forall u^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\ \varphi(u^{\prime})\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

(by definition)

∎

3.3
Functorial properties

Another consequence of the previous theorem is that, if
\left\llbracket x=z\right\rrbracket=1_{H}
, then, since
1_{H}=\bigwedge\emptyset
, we obtain:

f\left(\left\llbracket x=z\right\rrbracket\right)=f(1_{H})=1_{H^{\prime}}\leq\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

that is,
\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}=1_{H^{\prime}}
. Therefore, when we take the quotient by
\equiv
, the semi-function
\tilde{f}
defines an object mapping
\overline{f}:\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}\to\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

1.

\overline{id_{\mathbb{H}}}=id_{\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}}:\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}\to\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}
;

2.

if
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime}
preserves finite meets and arbitrary joins, then
\overline{f^{\prime}}\circ\overline{f}=\overline{f^{\prime}\circ f}:\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}\to\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime}\right)}
.

Proof.

1.

We show that, for all
\alpha\in\operatorname{On}
, if
\left\langle x,y^{\prime}\right\rangle\in(\widetilde{id_{\mathbb{H}}})_{\alpha}
, then
\left\llbracket x=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1
. Suppose, inductively, that this is the case for all
\beta<\alpha
, and let
\varepsilon:\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
(with
\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1
) witness
\left\langle x,y^{\prime}\right\rangle\in(\widetilde{id_{\mathbb{H}}})_{\alpha}
. Then,
x^{\prime}\circ\varepsilon=id_{\mathbb{H}}\circ x=x
, and for all
u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)
,
\left\langle u,\varepsilon(x)\right\rangle\in(\widetilde{id_{\mathbb{H}}})_{\varrho(x)}
, thereby
\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=u\right\rrbracket=1
(using the induction hypothesis). Thus, for all
u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)
, since
\varepsilon
is surjective we have:

x(u)=x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))=x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(u))\wedge\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=u\right\rrbracket\leq\bigvee\limits_{w^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}x^{\prime}(w^{\prime})\wedge\left\llbracket w^{\prime}=u\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket u\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket

Similarly, for all
v^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
, there exists
v\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)
such that
\varepsilon(v)=v^{\prime}
, and

x^{\prime}(v^{\prime})=x^{\prime}(\varepsilon(v))=x(v)=x(v)\wedge\left\llbracket\varepsilon(v)=v\right\rrbracket\leq\bigvee\limits_{w\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(w)\wedge\left\llbracket w=v^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket v^{\prime}\in x\right\rrbracket

Observe that
x(u)\leq\left\llbracket u\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
if, and only if,
1\leq x(u)\to\left\llbracket u\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
, for all
u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)
; that is,
\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}x(u)\to\left\llbracket u\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1
. Analogously,
x^{\prime}(v^{\prime})\leq\left\llbracket v^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
for all
v^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
is equivalent to
\bigwedge\limits_{v^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}x^{\prime}(v^{\prime})\to\left\llbracket v^{\prime}\in x\right\rrbracket=1
. Therefore:

\left\llbracket x=x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=\bigwedge\limits_{u\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}\left(x(u)\to\left\llbracket u\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{v^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)}\left(x^{\prime}(v^{\prime})\to\left\llbracket v^{\prime}\in x\right\rrbracket\right)=1

Finally, since
\left\llbracket x=x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1=\left\llbracket x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket
, we may conclude that
\left\llbracket x=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1
, as desired.

Now, by the definition of
\equiv
,
\left\llbracket x=y^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=1
if, and only if,
[x]=[y^{\prime}]
. As a result, taking the quotient,
\left\langle[x],[y^{\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{id_{\mathbb{H}}}
if, and only if,
[x]=[y^{\prime}]
, hence
\overline{id_{\mathbb{H}}}
is the identity in
\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}
.

2.

Let
\left\langle[x],[z^{\prime\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f^{\prime}}\circ\overline{f}
,
i.e.
, there exists
[y^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{Set}^{\left(\mathbb{H}\right)}
such that
\left\langle[x],[y^{\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f}
and
\left\langle[y^{\prime}],[z^{\prime\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f^{\prime}}
. Consider
\varepsilon:\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
, with
x^{\prime}\in[y^{\prime}]
, a witness of
\left\langle[x],[y^{\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime}:\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)\twoheadrightarrow\text{dom}\left({y^{\prime\prime}}\right)
, with
y^{\prime\prime}\in[z^{\prime\prime}]
, a witness of
\left\langle[y^{\prime}],[z^{\prime\prime}]\right\rangle=\left\langle[x^{\prime}],[z^{\prime\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f^{\prime}}
. Then,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\circ\varepsilon:\text{dom}\left({x}\right)\to\text{dom}\left({y^{\prime\prime}}\right)
witnesses
\left\langle[x],[z^{\prime\prime}]\right\rangle\in\overline{f^{\prime}\circ f}
. That is, we have shown that
\overline{f^{\prime}}\circ\overline{f}\subseteq\overline{f^{\prime}\circ f}
, and since both are functions, we obtain
\overline{f^{\prime}}\circ\overline{f}=\overline{f^{\prime}\circ f}
.

∎

At last, using that
f\left(\left\llbracket y\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\leq^{\prime}\left\llbracket y^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}
, it can be shown that, if
\left\llbracket\text{fun}\left({h:x\to y}\right)\right\rrbracket=1

\left\llbracket\text{fun}\left({\tilde{f}(h):\tilde{f}(x)\to\tilde{f}(y)}\right)\right\rrbracket=1^{\prime}

\tilde{f}(id_{x})=id_{\tilde{f}(x)}:\tilde{f}(x)\to\tilde{f}(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\llbracket\text{fun}\left({\tilde{f}(id_{x}):\tilde{f}(x)\to\tilde{f}(x)}\right)\right\rrbracket^{\prime}=1^{\prime}

\left\llbracket\text{fun}\left({g:y\to z}\right)\right\rrbracket=1

Let

Remark
.

The idea now would be to show that such morphisms
\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}
are isomorphisms, which could be used to build a natural isomorphism between
\overline{f}
and
\varphi_{f}
. To show that, a possibility would be to use the characterization of monomorphisms and epimorphisms in
\mathbb{H}\text{-}\mathbf{Set}
(see
[
Bor08c
]
, Propositions 2.8.8 and 2.8.7), that is, to show that for all
u,v\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)
and
u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}\left({x^{\prime}}\right)
:

•

\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}(u,u^{\prime})\wedge\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}(v,u^{\prime})\leq(f\circ\delta_{x})(u,v)
(which is equivalent to
\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}
being monic);

•

\bigvee\limits_{w\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}(w,u^{\prime})=\delta_{x^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},u^{\prime})
(which is equivalent to
\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}
being epic);

and, since
\mathbb{H}\text{-}\mathbf{Set}
is a topos,
\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}
would be an isomorphism.

Now, expanding the definitions,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}

\displaystyle(u,u^{\prime})\wedge\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}(v,u^{\prime})=

\displaystyle=f\left(\left\llbracket u\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=u^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket u^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}f\left(\left\llbracket v\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(v)=u^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket u^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq f\left(\left\llbracket u\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=u^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(v)=u^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq f\left(\left\llbracket u\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(u)=\varepsilon(v)\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ and we want to show that }\leq f\left(\left\llbracket u\in x\right\rrbracket\wedge\left\llbracket u=v\right\rrbracket\right)=(f\circ\delta_{x})(u.v)

\displaystyle\bigvee\limits_{w\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}\varepsilon^{H^{\prime}}(w,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{w\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}f\left(\left\llbracket w\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(w)=u^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket u^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{w\in\text{dom}\left({x}\right)}f\left(\left\llbracket w\in x\right\rrbracket\right)\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(w)=\varepsilon(t)\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\wedge^{\prime}\left\llbracket\varepsilon(t)\in x^{\prime}\right\rrbracket^{\prime}\text{($\varepsilon$ is surjective)}

Remark
.

In a different direction, another aspect that could be analysed is if the “lifting property” through
\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H})}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}\right)
also holds for other natural topoi morphisms, such as the logical functors. Since logical functors and (the left part of) geometric morphism coincide only trivially (
i.e.
iff when both are equivalences of categories), this will be in fact a new direction to pursue.

Note that the “conceptual orthogonality” between the two kind of functors

\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}\right)\to\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)
occurs already in the algebraic level of arrows
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
. More precisely, given a non-trivial complete Boolean algebra
(\mathbb{B},\leq)
and the unique injective morphism
i:\mathbf{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}
(where
\mathbf{2}=\{0,1\}
), we get three kinds of morphisms
\mathbb{B}\to\mathbf{2}
:

•

l:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbf{2}
is the left adjoint of
i
(given by
l(x)=0\Leftrightarrow x=0
): it preserves only the suprema;

•

r:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbf{2}
is the right adjoint of
i
(given by
r(x)=1\Leftrightarrow x=1
): it preserves only the infima;

•

U:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbf{2}
is the quotient by an ultrafilter
U
, that preserves
0
,
1
, negation, implication, finite sups and finite infs.

On the other hand, note that a logical functor
\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}\right)\to\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)
induces a Heyting algebra morphism
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
(since
\mathbb{H}\cong Subobj(\mathbf{1})
). Therefore, we would expect to be able to establish a correspondence between other kind of morphisms
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
and the logical functors
\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}\right)\to\mathbf{Sh}\left(\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\right)
and ask how they are related to some alternative notion of induced arrow
\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H})}\to\text{V}^{(\mathbb{H}^{\prime})}
.

In particular, it seems be natural to consider the connections between the various “forcing relations” (according the previous remark), classical and intuitionistic, related to the canonical morphisms between complete Boolean algebras associated to a complete Heyting algebra
Reg(\mathbb{H})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}\twoheadrightarrow\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{H}}{\langle x\leftrightarrow\neg\neg x\rangle}
.

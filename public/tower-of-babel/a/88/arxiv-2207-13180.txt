Hermite trace polynomials and chaos decompositions for the Hermitian Brownian motion Thanks: This work was supported in part by a Simons Foundation Collaboration Grant.
Michael Anshelevich, David Buzinski

Abstract
. For a non-zero parameter q q , we define Hermite trace polynomials, which are multivariate polynomials indexed by permutations. We prove several combinatorial properties for them, such as expansions and product formulas. The linear functional determined by these trace polynomials is a state for q = 1 N q=\frac{1}{N} for N N a non-zero integer. For such q q , Hermite trace polynomials of different degrees are orthogonal. The product formulas extend to the closure with respect to the state. The state can be identified with the expectation induced by the N × N N\times N Hermitian Brownian motion. Hermite trace polynomials are martingales for this Brownian motion, while the elements in the closure can be interpreted as stochastic integrals with respect to it. Using the grading on the algebra, we prove several chaos decompositions for such integrals, as well as analyse corresponding creation and annihilation operators. In the univariate, pure trace polynomial case, trace Hermite polynomials can be identified with the Hermite polynomials of matrix argument.

1.
Introduction

Let
\left\{B(t):t\geq 0\right\}
be the Brownian motion. It is well known that there exist polynomials
H_{n}(x,t)
(the Hermite polynomials) such that for each
n
,
H_{n}(B(t),t)
is a martingale with respect to the filtration induced by
\left\{B(t)\right\}
. These polynomials have numerous other familiar properties. The list of properties relevant to this article includes:

•

Orthogonality:
\mathbb{E}[H_{n}(B(t),t)H_{k}(B(t),t)]=0
for
n\neq k
;

•

Three-term recursion:
xH_{n}(x,t)=H_{n+1}(x,t)+ntH_{n-1}(x,t)
;

•

Expansion:
H_{n}(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{[n/2]}(-1)^{j}\frac{n!}{(n-2j)!2^{j}j!}t^{j}x^{n-2j}
, where the coefficients are related to the number of incomplete matchings;

•

Product formulas:
H_{n}(x,t)H_{k}(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n\wedge k}\binom{n}{j}\binom{k}{j}j!t^{j}H_{n+k-2j}(x,t)
, where the coefficients are related to the number of inhomogeneous incomplete matchings;

•

Solution of the heat equation:
\partial_{t}H_{n}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}H_{n}(x,t)=0
with the initial condition
H_{n}(x,0)=x^{n}
;

•

Stochastic integral representation:
H_{n}(B(t),t)=\int_{[0,t)^{n}}\,dB(t_{1})\ldots\,dB(t_{n})
.

Moreover, some of these results, notably the product formulas, extend to more general stochastic integrals
\int_{[0,t)^{n}}f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\,dB(t_{1})\ldots\,dB(t_{n})
for
f\in L^{2}
.

Now let
\left\{X(t):t\geq 0\right\}
be the
N\times N
Hermitian Brownian motion: Hermitian random matrices with jointly complex Gaussian entries and the covariance function
\mathbb{E}\left[X(s)_{ij}X(t)_{k\ell}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i=\ell}\delta_{j=k}(s\wedge t)
. Then as is also well-known by now, there is no polynomial
P(x,t)
of degree greater than
2
such that
P(X(t),t)
is a martingale. For example, a simple calculation shows that for
s<t
,

\mathbb{E}\left[X(t)^{3}|\leq s\right]=X(s)^{3}+(t-s)\left(2X(s)+\frac{1}{N}\Tr[X(s)]I_{N}\right).

It is therefore natural to replace the algebra of polynomials by a larger algebra of trace polynomials. Here a trace monomial is, roughly speaking, a product of a regular monomial and traces of regular monomials. See Section
6.1
for details. An incomplete but representative list of related work involving trace polynomials includes the study of random matrices
[
Céb13
]
,
[
DHK13
,
Kem16
,
Kem17
]
, noncommutative functions
[
KŠ17
,
KPV21
]
, and operator algebras
[
DGS21
,
Jek20
,
JLS22
]
. Other related work includes
[
GV06
,
Lév08
,
Hub21
]
.

In this article, we study the
\ast
-algebra of trace polynomials, the state on it induced by the Hermitian Brownian motion, the corresponding basis of Hermite trace polynomials, and the larger algebra obtained as its completion. However, most of the results in the article are stated in terms of permutations (rather than trace monomials), a general parameter
q
(rather than
\frac{1}{N}
), and a general Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(rather than
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dx)
). Thus, denote by
S_{0}(n)
the symmetric group on the set
\left\{0,1,\ldots,n\right\}
. For each
n
,
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we will consider symbols
\mathrm{T}\left(\alpha\otimes(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})\right)
subject to the symmetry relation

\mathrm{T}\left(\alpha\otimes_{s}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})\right)=\mathrm{T}\left((\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1})\otimes_{s}U_{\sigma}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})\right)

for any
\sigma\in S(n)
, where

U_{\sigma}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})=h_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}.

Extending linearly in both arguments, we obtain

\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=\left\{\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right):n\geq 0,\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)],F\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\otimes n}\right\},

where for now we are considering the algebraic tensor product of Hilbert spaces. We now define a star-algebra structure on
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
by

\mathrm{T}\left(\alpha\otimes_{s}F\right)\mathrm{T}\left(\beta\otimes_{s}G\right)=\mathrm{T}\left((\alpha\cup\beta)\otimes_{s}(F\otimes G)\right)

and

\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)^{\ast}=\mathrm{T}\left(\eta^{\ast}\otimes_{s}F\right).

Here for
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
,
\beta\in S_{0}(k)
, the permutation
\alpha\cup\beta\in S_{0}(n+k)
is obtained by shifting the cycles of
\beta
by
n
, and merging the cycles of
\alpha
and
\beta
containing
0
. See Notation
3.2
.

Next, let
q
be a non-zero parameter. For a transposition
\tau
, we define the contraction
C_{\tau}
as follows. For
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
,
C_{\tau}(\alpha)
is obtained by: multiplying
\tau\alpha
; erasing the support of
\tau
and shifting to obtain a permutation in
S_{0}(n-2)
; and multiplying by a weight depending on
q
and the number of cycles in
\tau\alpha
. See Definition
3.9
. Then as usual, we define the Laplacian as the sum over transpositions
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\tau}C_{\tau}
, and the Hermite trace polynomial
\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)=\mathrm{T}\left(e^{-\mathcal{L}}(\eta\otimes_{s}F)\right)
. Here the contraction on the tensor part of the argument is the usual tensor contraction. Hermite trace polynomials satisfy several properties which parallel those of ordinary Hermite polynomials.

We may now define a linear functional
\varphi
on
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
by requiring it to be unital and zero on each
\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)
. If we use
\left\{\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)\right\}
as a spanning set for
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
, multiplication does not depend on
q
, while the state
\varphi
does. On the other hand, if we use
\left\{\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)\right\}
as a spanning set, the state does not depend on
q
, but multiplication does.
\varphi
is positive semi-definite if and only if
q
is zero or of the form
\pm\frac{1}{N}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For such
q
, we can define the GNS Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
as the completion of the quotient
\mathcal{TP}_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
of
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
with respect to the seminorm induced by
\varphi
. The inner product takes the form

\left\langle(\eta\otimes_{s}F),(\zeta\otimes_{s}G)\right\rangle_{q}=\delta_{n=k}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}\chi_{q}[\eta\sigma\zeta^{\ast}\sigma^{-1}]\left\langle F,U_{\sigma}G\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}.

Here
\chi_{q}
is the normalized character
\chi_{q}(\alpha)=q^{\left|\alpha\right|}
, where
\left|\alpha\right|
is the standard length function on the symmetric group.

Using the seminorm induced by
\varphi
allows us to extend the star-algebra structure to

\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=\left\{\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right):n\geq 0,\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)],F\in\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\otimes n}}\right\},

where
F
may now lie in the Hilbert space tensor product. In the case
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dx)
,
\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)
may then be interpreted as a stochastic integral of
F
. We thus obtain contraction, product, and conditional expectation formulas for such integrals. Note that
\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)
does not in general allow for such an extended argument.

Since
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
is naturally graded, we may interpret
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
as a Fock space. There are several natural choices of creation (and annihilation) operators which we describe. The corresponding field operators live in smaller subalgebras of
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
. There is an interesting connection with a different Fock space construction from
[
BG02
]
which we describe in some detail. Incidentally, another article exploring the connection between characters of the symmetric groups and GUE matrices is
[
NK20
]
. We have not elucidated the connection between their work and ours.

Finally, we show that, as expected, for
q=\pm\frac{1}{N}
the structures above are isomorphic to the algebra of trace polynomials, and equivariant square-integrable matrices, in a Hermitian Brownian motion. Various corollaries follow. In particular, we obtain several versions of the chaos decomposition for this process. For
q=0
, with a different scaling, one obtains objects related to free probability.

To the best of our knowledge, the Hermite trace polynomials
\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)
are new even in the univariate case
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
. However if we further restrict to
\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S(n)]
(rather than
S_{0}(n)
), the corresponding objects have appeared in the literature. Indeed, denoting
\chi^{\lambda}
the character of the irreducible representation indexed by the partition
\lambda
,
\mathrm{I}\left(\chi^{\lambda}\right)
is closely related to the corresponding Hermite polynomial of matrix argument. In particular, these elements are all orthogonal with respect to
\varphi
. Moreover, one can form a more general set of characters
\chi^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
indexed by pairs of partitions which differ by one box, such that
\left\{\mathrm{I}\left(\chi^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\right\}
form an orthogonal basis for
\mathcal{F}_{1/N}(\mathbb{C})
. This collection of trace polynomials is clearly deserving of additional study.

The paper is organized as follows. After the introduction and a background section, in Section
3
we discuss the kernel of the character
\chi_{q}
, define the multiplication and contractions on the tensor algebra of symmetric groups, and study their properties. In Section
4
, we define the Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
, describe the kernel of the inner product, and list three chaos decompositions for this space for different choices of
\mathcal{H}
. In Section
5
, we upgrade the algebra structure from
\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
to
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
, define the Hermite trace polynomials
\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes_{s}F\right)
and the functional
\varphi
, and prove conditional expectation and product formulas. We also study creation and annihilation decompositions on the Fock space, and three subalgebras which arise: the Gaussian subalgebra, the commutative subalgebra corresponding to pure trace polynomials, and the subalgebra corresponding to pure polynomials, which is not closed under conditional expectations. We finish the section by describing the relation to a construction by Bożejko and Guţă. Finally, in Section
6
, we give some background on trace polynomials and the Hermitian Brownian motion, prove the isomorphism with the random matrix picture for
q=\frac{1}{N}
, and list several corollaries. We show that in the
q=0
case, there is an isomorphism involving the free Fock space, and the case
q=-\frac{1}{N}
is isomorphic to that for
q=\frac{1}{N}
. We also describe the relation with Hermite polynomials of matrix argument.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Permutations and partitions

Denote
[n]=\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
and
[0,n]=\left\{0,1,\ldots,n\right\}
.

A permutation in
S(n)
induces a (cycle) set partition in
\Part(n)
. Conversely, a partition
\pi\in\Part(n)
will be identified with a permutation the elements of whose cycles are ordered in increasing order. In particular, a partition whose blocks are pairs and singletons will be identified with the corresponding involutive permutation. For such a partition, we denote by
\mathrm{Sing}\left(\pi\right)
its single-element blocks, and
\mathrm{Pair}\left(\pi\right)
its two-element blocks.

Similarly, a set partition
\pi
induces a (number) partition whose parts are the sizes of blocks of
\pi
. Conversely, a partition
\lambda\in\Par(n;k)
with parts
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{k}
induces a set partition whose blocks are intervals of size
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
.

Denote by
S_{0}(n)
the permutations of
[0,n]
.
S(n)
is a subgroup of
S_{0}(n)
, which acts on it by conjugation. The equivalence classes under this action are subsets of the standard conjugacy classes where the number of elements of the cycle containing
0
is preserved. So they are in a natural bijection with number partitions of
n+1
with a marked element. Equivalently, they are indexed by pairs of partitions
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
, where
\lambda\in\Par(n)
,
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Par(n+1)
, and the corresponding diagrams differ by one box.

For
m>n
, we will identify the element
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
with the corresponding element of
S_{0}(m)
under the natural inclusion
[0,n]\subset[0,m]
.

For
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
, denote
\cyc_{0}(\alpha)=\cyc(\alpha)-1
, where
\cyc(\alpha)
is the number of cycles of
\alpha
. In other words,
\cyc_{0}(\alpha)
is the number of cycles of
\alpha
not containing
0
. Denote

\left|\alpha\right|=(n+1)-\cyc(\alpha)=n-\cyc_{0}(\alpha),

which is the usual length function on the symmetric group on
[0,n]
.

To make the paper more readable, we will write the elements of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
as linear combinations
\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}c_{\alpha}\alpha
rather than the more standard
\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}c_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}
.

A
partial permutation

\alpha\in\mathcal{PS}_{0}(n)
is a bijection from a subset of
[0,n]
onto a subset of
[0,n]
; proper subsets and the empty subset are allowed. Orbits of a partial permutation fall into two types. Cyclic orbits are cycles in the usual permutation sense. Linear orbits have the initial and the final element. Note that a linear orbit has at least two elements. It is convenient to abuse the terminology and consider elements of
[0,n]
which do not belong to any orbit of
\alpha
as single-element linear orbits of
\alpha
. Denote
\mathcal{PS}_{0}(n,N)
the set of partial permutations of
[0,n]
with
N
linear orbits.

2.2.
Structure theory of the symmetric group

For a partition
\lambda\in\Par(n+1)
, denote by
\chi^{\lambda}
the character of the irreducible representation of
S_{0}(n)
corresponding to
\lambda
. We will identify
\chi^{\lambda}
with the element

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(n)}\chi^{\lambda}(\sigma)\sigma\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]

(recall that the characters of the symmetric group are real-valued). These elements span the center
Z(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)])
, and are orthogonal, in the sense that for
\lambda\neq\mu
,

\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(n)}\chi^{\lambda}(\sigma)\sigma\right)\left(\sum_{\tau\in S_{0}(n)}\chi^{\mu}(\tau)\tau\right)=\sum_{\rho\in S_{0}(n)}(\chi^{\lambda}\ast\chi^{\mu})(\rho)\rho=0.

In particular, for any character
\chi
,
\chi^{\lambda}
and
\chi^{\mu}
are orthogonal with respect to the inner product induced by
\chi
.

The centralizer of
\mathbb{C}[S(n)]
in
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
is

Z(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:\mathbb{C}[S(n)])=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:{\sigma^{-1}}\eta\sigma=\eta\text{ for all }\sigma\in S(n)\right\}.

The following are well-known
[
VOk04
]
,
[
Gil05
]
.

•

Z(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:\mathbb{C}[S(n)])
is generated (as an algebra) by
Z(\mathbb{C}[S(n)])
and the Jucys–Murphy element
{(01)}+\ldots+{(0n)}
.

•

For
\lambda\in\Par(n)
, write
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\square
if the Young diagram for
\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained by adding one box to the Young diagram for
\lambda
. Denote
\chi^{\lambda^{\prime}:\lambda}
the character of the compression of the
\lambda^{\prime}
-irreducible representation of
S_{0}(n)
to the (unique) component giving a
\lambda
-irreducible representation of
S(n)
. Then
\left\{\chi^{\lambda^{\prime}:\lambda}:\lambda\in\Par(n),\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\square\right\}
are orthogonal and span
Z(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:\mathbb{C}[S(n)])
.

Let
W
be the isomorphism

W:\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1)}M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)],

where
d_{\lambda}
is the dimension of the irreducible representation of
S_{0}(n)
corresponding to
\lambda
. Then
W(M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C}))
is the ideal generated by (any one of) the Young symmetrizer(s)
c_{\lambda}
, and is spanned by these symmetrizers (for different choices of the tableau corresponding to
\lambda
). In particular,
W(I_{M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})})=\frac{d_{\lambda}}{(n+1)!}\chi^{\lambda}
, which can be characterized as minimal central projections.

Denote

\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]_{\leq N}=W\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1;\leq N)}M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})\right)

and

\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]_{>N}=W\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1;\geq N+1)}M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})\right).

Let
\chi
be a character of
S_{0}(n)
. Then
\chi\circ W
is a trace on
\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1)}M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})
, and so has the form

\chi\circ W=\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1)}n_{\lambda}\Tr_{M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})}.

Here

n_{\lambda}=\frac{\sum_{\alpha}\chi[\alpha]\chi^{\lambda}[\alpha]}{\sum_{\alpha}\chi^{\lambda}[\alpha]\chi^{\lambda}[\alpha]}=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}\chi[\alpha]\chi^{\lambda}[\alpha].

Denote
E_{ij}^{\lambda}
the matrix units in
M_{d_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C})
. Then for any
\chi
,

\left\{W(E_{ij}^{\lambda}):\lambda\in\Par(n+1),1\leq i,j\leq d_{\lambda}\right\}

span
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
, and are orthogonal with respect to the (typically degenerate) inner product induced by
\chi
,

\chi[W(E_{ij}^{\lambda})W(E_{ij}^{\mu})^{\ast}]=\delta_{\lambda=\mu}\chi[W(E_{ii}^{\lambda})]=\delta_{\lambda=\mu}n_{\lambda}.

See for example
[
BMP16
]
for a detailed exposition and explicit expressions for
W(E_{ij}^{\lambda})
.

2.3.
Algebraic conditional expectation

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital star-algebra,
\mathcal{B}
a unital star-subalgebra, and
\varphi
a faithful, tracial state on
\mathcal{A}
, where positivity means that
\varphi[a^{\ast}a]\geq 0
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\varphi)
the corresponding GNS Hilbert spaces, with the common state vector
\Omega
. Suppose
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is a function such that the map
a\Omega\mapsto F(a)\Omega
extends to the orthogonal projection
P:L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\varphi)
. Then

•

\varphi[F(a)]=\varphi[a]
.

•

F
is a
\mathcal{B}
-bimodule map.

•

For any
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the operator on
L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\varphi)
induced by
F(a)
is
PaP
. In particular, the operator induced by
F(a^{\ast}a)
is positive.

We call such a map
F
an algebraic conditional expectation.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{\ast}
algebra, it follows that
F
is a genuine
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation.

Proof.

By assumption, for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
,

\varphi[b^{\ast}a]=\varphi[b^{\ast}F(a)],

and
F(a)
is uniquely determined by this condition. By taking
b=1
, we get the first property. Next, for
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
, using the fact that
\varphi
is tracial,

\varphi[b^{\ast}F(ab^{\prime})]=\varphi[b^{\ast}ab^{\prime}]=\varphi[b^{\prime}b^{\ast}a]=\varphi[b^{\prime}b^{\ast}F(a)]=\varphi[b^{\ast}F(a)b^{\prime}],

so
F
is a right
\mathcal{B}
-module map. The proof for the left action is similar, and does not require the tracial property. Finally,

\left\langle b\Omega,F(a)b^{\prime}\Omega\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle b\Omega,ab^{\prime}\Omega\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle b\Omega,PaPb^{\prime}\Omega\right\rangle_{\varphi}

and

\left\langle b\Omega,F(a^{\ast}a)b\Omega\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\left\langle ab\Omega,ab\Omega\right\rangle_{\varphi}\geq 0.\qed

3.
The tensor algebra of symmetric groups

3.1.
A function on the symmetric group

On the symmetric group
S_{0}(n)
, consider the function
\chi^{n+1}_{q}:\alpha\mapsto q^{\left|\alpha\right|}
, and extend it to a function on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
. As is well-known (see, for example,
[
GGK13
,
NK20
]
), this function is positive semi-definite for

q\in\mathcal{Z}_{n+1}=\left[-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}\right]\cup\left\{\pm\frac{1}{N}:1\leq N\leq n-1\right\}

and is not positive semi-definite for other values of
q
. It follows that these functions are positive for all
n
if

q\in\mathcal{Z}=\cap_{n}\mathcal{Z}_{n}=\left\{0\right\}\cup\left\{\pm\frac{1}{N}:N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

We will typically omit the superscript on
\chi^{n+1}_{q}
. For
q\in\mathcal{Z}
, the positivity of
\chi_{q}
follows from the fact that
\chi_{1/N}
is the normalized character of the standard representation

\pi_{n,q}:\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\rightarrow\End\left((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{\otimes(n+1)}\right),

while
\chi_{0}
is the normalized character of its regular representation (and also the standard trace on
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
).

It is well-known
[
GGK13
]
(see also
[
Bia01
,
Ker03
]
) that

\chi_{1/N}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{N^{n+1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1)}\left|SS_{N}(\lambda)\right|\chi^{\lambda},

where
SS_{N}(\lambda)
is the number of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
with entries belonging to the set
\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
. In particular, the coefficients are zero if
\lambda
has more than
N
parts, and non-zero if it has at most
N
parts. Also

\chi_{0}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{\lambda\in\Par(n+1)}d_{\lambda}\chi^{\lambda}.

We now discuss the kernel of the normalized character
\chi_{q}
. See Section 4 of
[
Pro76
]
for a related discussion.

Proposition 3.1
.

Denote

\mathcal{N}_{gr,q,n}=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:\chi_{q}[\eta\eta^{\ast}]=0\right\}=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]:\pi_{n,q}(\eta)=0\right\}

and

\mathcal{N}_{gr,q}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}_{gr,q,n}\subset\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)].

(a)

\mathcal{N}_{gr,q,n}=\left\{0\right\}
for
q=0
or for
q=\frac{1}{N}
,
n+1\leq N
.

(b)

The following are equivalent descriptions of
\mathcal{N}_{gr,1/N,n}
for
n+1>N
.

•

\mathcal{N}_{gr,1/N,n}
is the ideal of the group algebra of
S_{0}(n)
spanned by the Young symmetrizers corresponding to diagrams with at least
N+1
rows.

•

\mathcal{N}_{gr,1/N,n}=\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]_{>N}
, so that on
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]_{\leq N}
,
\chi_{q}
is faithful.

•

More explicitly,
\mathcal{N}_{gr,1/N,n}
is the ideal generated by

\sigma_{n}^{(1/N)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(N-1)}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}\sigma

under the natural embedding of
S_{0}(N-1)
into
S_{0}(n)
. Similarly,
\mathcal{N}_{gr,-1/N,n}
is the ideal generated by

\sigma_{n}^{(-1/N)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(N-1)}\sigma.

•

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{PS}_{0}(n)
be a partial permutation of
[0,n]
with
N
linear orbits. Denote
a_{0},\ldots,a_{N-1}
and
b_{0},\ldots,b_{N-1}
the initial, respectively, final elements of these orbits (recall that if an element does not belong to any actual orbit of
\pi
, we consider it as a single-element linear orbit, in which case
a_{i}=b_{i}
). Here
a_{0},b_{0}
belong to the orbit containing
0
. A permutation
\sigma\in S_{0}(N-1)
naturally acts on the linear orbits of
\pi
by concatenating them. Denote the result of this action by
\sigma\circ\pi\in S_{0}(n)
. Thus,
(\sigma\circ\pi)(b_{i})=a_{\sigma(i)}
, and
(\sigma\circ\pi)(x)=\pi(x)
otherwise. Then

\mathcal{N}_{gr,1/N}=\mathrm{Span}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(N-1)}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}\sigma\circ\pi:\pi\in\mathcal{PS}_{0}(n,N)\right)

and

\mathcal{N}_{gr,-1/N}=\mathrm{Span}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{0}(N-1)}\sigma\circ\pi:\pi\in\mathcal{PS}_{0}(n,N)\right).

Proof.

(a) and the equivalence between the first three entries in (b) are well-known. For the final entry, denote by
\bar{\pi}
the permutation obtained by “closing” the orbits of
\pi
; that is,
\bar{\pi}(b_{i})=a_{i}
and
\bar{\pi}(x)=\pi(x)
otherwise. Also let
\alpha\in S(n)
be defined by
\alpha(a_{i})=i
and arbitrarily otherwise; thus,
\alpha
maps
\left\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{N-1}\right\}
bijectively onto
[0,N-1]
. Then a calculation shows that
\alpha^{-1}\sigma\alpha\bar{\pi}=\sigma\circ\pi
. Therefore

\sum_{\sigma\in S(N-1)}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}\sigma\circ\pi=\sum_{\sigma\in S(N-1)}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}\alpha^{-1}\sigma\alpha\bar{\pi}\in\mathcal{N}_{gr,q}.

The argument for the opposite inclusion is very close to the proof of Theorem 4.5 in
[
Pro76
]
, in a somewhat different language. Start with
\alpha^{-1}\sigma_{n}^{(1/N)}\beta\alpha
in the ideal. Possibly by replacing
\beta
by its multiple by appropriate transpositions, we may assume that
0,1,\ldots,N-1
lie in different cycles of
\beta
. Denote
a_{i}=\alpha^{-1}(i)
for
0\leq i\leq N-1
. Then
a_{0},\ldots,a_{N-1}
lie in different cycles of
\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
. So
\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha=\bar{\pi}
, where linear orbits of
\pi
are cycles of
\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
with initial elements
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{N-1}
, and cyclic orbits of
\pi
are the remaining cycles of
\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
. It follows that

(\alpha^{-1}\sigma_{n}^{(1/N)}\alpha)(\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha)=\sum_{\sigma\in S(N-1)}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}\sigma\circ\pi.

The argument for
q=-1/N
is similar.
∎

Lemma 3.11

Proposition 3.14
.

(a)

\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\right)=\mathrm{I}\left(e^{\mathcal{L}}\eta\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\mathrm{I}\left(\mathcal{L}^{\ell}(\eta)\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}\mathrm{I}\left(C_{\pi}(\eta)\right).

(b)

For

,

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}\left(\alpha\right)\mathrm{I}\left(\beta\right)&=\mathrm{I}\left({\alpha\cup\beta}\right)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\min(n,k)}\frac{1}{\ell!}\mathrm{I}\left(\mathcal{L}_{n,k}^{(\ell)}(\alpha\cup\beta)\right)\\

Proof.

(a) follows by composing (terminating) power series in
\mathcal{L}
. For (b), the argument is very similar to the standard one for Hermite polynomials

. We expand

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}\left(\alpha\right)\mathrm{I}\left(\beta\right)&=\sum_{{\sigma_{1}}\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}(-1)^{n-\left|{\sigma_{1}}\right|}\mathrm{T}\left(C_{\sigma_{1}}(\alpha)\right)\sum_{{\sigma_{2}}\in\Part_{1,2}(k)}(-1)^{k-\left|{\sigma_{2}}\right|}\mathrm{T}\left(C_{\sigma_{2}}(\beta)\right)\\

The final sum is zero unless both

4.
Fock space

Construction 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real Hilbert space, and
\mathcal{H}
its complexification. We will denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
the
algebraic
tensor product, which is spanned by simple tensors, and by
\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
the Hilbert space tensor product. For each
n
, form the algebraic Fock space

\mathbb{C}{(0)}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\right).

On each component of this space, we have a natural action of
S(n)
: for
\sigma\in S(n)

\alpha\otimes F\mapsto{\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1}}\otimes U_{\sigma}F,

where

U_{\sigma}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})=h_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}

extends to
\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
as an isometry. We denote by

\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{C}{(0)}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes_{s}\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\right)

the vector space quotient under this action.
\overline{\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H})}
may be identified with the fixed point subspace of this action, which is the image of the direct sum of the projections

(2)

P_{n}:\alpha\otimes F\mapsto\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}{\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1}}\otimes U_{\sigma}F

Thus

(3)

\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes_{s}\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}\alpha\otimes F_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\ :\ \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}U_{\sigma}F_{\sigma^{-1}\alpha\sigma}=F_{\alpha}\right\}.

To simplify notation, we will denote

\alpha\otimes_{s}F=P_{n}(\alpha\otimes F)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}{\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1}}\otimes U_{\sigma}F.

On
\mathbb{C}{(0)}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\right)
, we have the canonical inner product

(4)

\left\langle\alpha\otimes F,\beta\otimes G\right\rangle_{0}=\delta_{n=k}\chi_{0}[\alpha\beta^{-1}]\left\langle F,G\right\rangle_{\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}}=\delta_{n=k}\delta_{\alpha=\beta}\left\langle F,G\right\rangle_{\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}},

where
\chi_{0}
is the canonical trace on
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
. On this space, define the operator

(5)

\mathcal{K}_{q}\left(\alpha\otimes F\right)=\sum_{\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\chi_{q}[\alpha\beta^{-1}]\beta\otimes F.

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is the identity operator. It is easy to check that for
q\in\mathcal{Z}
,
\mathcal{K}_{q}
is a positive semi-definite operator. Moreover,

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{q}P_{n}(\alpha\otimes F)&=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}\chi_{q}[\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1}\beta^{-1}]\beta\otimes U_{\sigma}F\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}\chi_{q}[\alpha\beta^{-1}]{\sigma\beta\sigma^{-1}}\otimes U_{\sigma}F\\
&=P_{n}\mathcal{K}_{q}(\alpha\otimes F),\end{split}

so
\mathcal{K}_{q}
restricts to the subspace
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
. The resulting inner product on
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
is

(6)

\left\langle\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}\alpha\otimes_{s}F_{\alpha},\sum_{\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\beta\otimes_{s}G_{\beta}\right\rangle_{q}=n!\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\chi_{q}(\alpha\beta^{-1})\left\langle F_{\alpha},G_{\beta}\right\rangle.

On the unsymmetrized Fock space, it is more natural to use the inner product coming from
\mathcal{K}_{q}P
, that is,

(7)

\left\langle\alpha\otimes F,\beta\otimes G\right\rangle_{q}=\delta_{n=k}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}\chi_{q}(\alpha\sigma\beta^{-1}\sigma^{-1})\left\langle F,U_{\sigma}G\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}.

For this inner product,
P_{n}
is an isometric projection.

The inner product (
6
) is positive semi-definite on
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
for
q\in\mathcal{Z}
. It is not in general positive definite. However, for
q=0
,

(8)

\left\langle\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}\alpha\otimes_{s}F_{\alpha},\sum_{\beta\in S_{0}(n)}\beta\otimes_{s}G_{\beta}\right\rangle_{0}=n!\sum_{\alpha\in S_{0}(n)}\left\langle F_{\alpha},G_{\alpha}\right\rangle.

and so the
0
-inner product on
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
is positive definite.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
GM02
]
, Guţă and Maassen considered a general Fock space construction. Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\left\{V_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be Hilbert spaces, such
S(n)
acts unitarily on
V_{n}
. Then one can define
V_{n}\otimes_{s}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
as the fixed point subspace of the action

v\otimes F\mapsto(\sigma\cdot v)\otimes U_{\sigma}F,

and a symmetrized Fock space as the orthogonal sum of these subspaces. In our case,
V_{n}=\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
, with the inner product induced by
\chi_{q}
, on which
S(n)
acts by conjugation (which preserves
\chi_{q}
). One can then define the creation operator based on a sequence of maps
j_{n}:V_{n}\rightarrow V_{n+1}
which commute with the action of
S(n)
; the annihilation operator as its adjoint; and study the algebra generated by field operators. In our setting, there are several natural equivariant choices of the map
j_{n}
. One possibility is the standard embedding
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n+1)]
. Another possibility is the linear extension of the map
\alpha\mapsto(0\ n+1)\alpha
. See Sections
5.1.1
and
5.1.3
. The algebra considered throughout most of the article is much larger than the subalgebras generated by these field operators; in fact, the vacuum vector is cyclic for it.

Proposition 4.3
.

For

\mathcal{F}_{f}(\mathcal{H})
is the full Fock space of
\mathcal{H}
.

The kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{q}
as an operator on
\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
with the (tensor) inner product (
4
) is clearly
\mathcal{N}_{gr,q,n}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
. The kernel of the inner product on
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
is the intersection of the kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{q}
and
\overline{\mathcal{TP}}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

.

the completion of

\mathcal{TP}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
is not

from Proposition

Lemma 4.5
.

(a)

If
F_{i},F\in\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}

5.2.
The relation to a construction by Bożejko and Guţă

We contrast the algebra
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
with a construction from
[
BG02
]
. In section 5 of that paper, Bożejko and Guţă considered the Fock space with the inner product

\left\langle f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n},g_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes g_{k}\right\rangle_{q}=\delta_{n=k}\sum_{\sigma\in S(n)}\chi_{q}[\sigma]\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle f_{i},g_{\sigma(i)}\right\rangle

for
q=\pm\frac{1}{N}
. On this space, they defined the creation operator
a^{+}(h)
in the usual way, and the annihilation operator as its adjoint, which comes out to be

a^{-}(h)(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})=\left\langle h_{1},h\right\rangle+q\sum_{k=2}^{n}\left\langle h_{k},h\right\rangle(h_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{i-1}\otimes h_{1}\otimes h_{i+1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})

(compare with Theorem
5.28
). Then (Lemma 5.1) the operators
\omega(h)=a^{+}(h)+a^{-}(h)
satisfy (with our notation)

\left\langle\Omega,\omega(h_{1})\ldots\omega(h_{2n})\right\rangle=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(2n)}q^{n-c(\pi)}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})

and the corresponding expression is zero for an odd number of factors (compare with equation (
10
)). Here
c(\pi)
is again the number of cycles of a permutation corresponding to a partition
\pi
, but this correspondence is more subtle. For a pair partition
\pi
, there is a unique non-crossing partition
\tilde{\pi}
with the same openers and closers as
\pi
. If
i\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\sim}}j
and
i\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\pi}}}{{\sim}}k
, then for the corresponding permutation
\sigma
,
\sigma(i)=j
if
i<j
, and
\sigma(i)=k
if
k<i
(it is easy to check that this is an alternative). In other words,
\sigma
is a permutation with an upper partition
\pi
and the lower partition
\tilde{\pi}
in the sense of Corteel
[
Cor07
]
. Then
c(\pi)
is the number of cycles of
\sigma
.

Moreover (Lemma 5.1), the creation and annihilation operators satisfy a commutation relation

a^{-}(f)a^{+}(g)=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle+q\ d\Gamma(|g\rangle\langle f|),

where
d\Gamma(A)
is the standard second quantization operator. We prove an analog of this relation in our context below. Note however that in other aspects, our construction behaves quite differently. For example, there is no simple commutation relation between
a^{+}_{(01)}
and
d\Gamma(A)
below. Also, for
q=-\frac{1}{N}
, the distribution of
\omega(h)
only has finite support, in contrast to Theorem
5.13
and Propositions
6.17
.

6.
Trace polynomials in GUE matrices

6.1.
Background

Let
\left\{x_{i}:i\in S\right\}
be a collection of non-commuting variables. Informally, a trace polynomial in these variables is a polynomial in these variables as well as in a tracial functional
\tr
applied to these variables. See
[
Céb13
]
for a formal definition using a universal property.

For the purposes of this paper, we will use a more constructive definition. Let
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
. Denote

\tr_{\alpha}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]=\prod_{i\text{ in the cycle starting with $0$}}x_{i}\prod_{\text{other cycles}}\tr\left[\prod_{i\text{ in the cycle}}x_{i}\right].

For example, for
\alpha=(024)(137)(56)
,

\tr_{\alpha}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{7}]=x_{2}x_{4}\tr[x_{1}x_{3}x_{7}]\tr[x_{5}x_{6}].

Compare with Notation 22.29 in
[
NS06
]
. Then
\tr_{\alpha}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a trace monomial, and a trace polynomial is a linear combination of trace monomials.

Next, let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital algebra,
\mathcal{C}
its center, and
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
a unital
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule linear map. For any
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
, we can similarly form
F_{\alpha}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{A}
, and consider it as the application of the trace monomial
\tr_{\alpha}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
to the elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. See
[
Céb13
]
.

We extend the notation to
F_{\eta}
for
\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
by linearity.

6.1.1.
Invariant theory of
N\times N
matrices

Let
\left\{x_{ij}^{(k)}:k\in S,1\leq i,j\leq N\right\}
be formal commuting variables subject to the relation
x_{ji}^{(k)}=(x_{ij}^{(k)})^{\ast}
. For each
k
, form a matrix
X^{(k)}=(x_{ij}^{(k)})_{i,j=1}^{N}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{N,S}
be the collection of all matrices with polynomial entries

\mathcal{A}_{N,S}=M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}[x_{ij}^{(k)}:k\in S,1\leq i,j\leq N].

Let
Y\in\mathcal{A}_{N,S}
,
Y=P(X^{(k)}:k\in S)
, where each entry
P_{ab}
is a polynomial in the entries of its argument. We say that
Y
is equivariant if for any
U\in U_{N}(\mathbb{C})
,

P(UX^{(k)}U^{\ast}:k\in S)=UYU^{\ast}.

Denote

\mathcal{A}_{N,S}^{\text{equiv}}=\left\{\text{equivariant }Y\in\mathcal{A}_{N,S}\right\}.

Then

(11)

\mathcal{A}_{N,S}^{\text{equiv}}=\mathrm{Span}\left(\Tr_{\alpha}(X^{(k(1))},\ldots,X^{(k(n))}):\alpha\in S_{0}(n),n\geq 0,k(1),\ldots,k(n)\in S\right),

where
\Tr
is the (un-normalized) trace on
M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Indeed, since for the purposes of this expansion,
x_{ij}^{(k)}
and
(x_{ij}^{(k)})^{\ast}
can be considered as independent variables, this follows directly from the first Procesi-Razmyslov theorem
[
Pro76
]
. The result is usually formulated using
GL(n)
-invariance. However, the argument ultimately reduces to Schur-Weyl duality, for which (in the case of inner product spaces) unitary invariance is sufficient.

6.1.2.
Hermitian Brownian motion

Let
\left\{b_{ij}(h):h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\right\}
be
N^{2}
standard independent Gaussian processes indexed by the same real Hilbert space, represented on the same probability space. Define the
N\times N
Hermitian Gaussian process
\left\{X(h):h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\right\}
by

X(h)_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}(b_{ij}(h)+\sqrt{-1}b_{ji}(h)),&i<j,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}b_{ij}(h),&i=j,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}(b_{ij}(h)-\sqrt{-1}b_{ji}(h)),&i>j.\\
\end{cases}

Equivalently, each
X(h)
is a Hermitian random matrix, whose entries are centered Gaussian variables with the joint covariance

\mathbb{E}\left[X(f)_{ij}X(g)_{k\ell}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i=\ell}\delta_{j=k}\left\langle f,g\right\rangle,

so that

(I\otimes\mathbb{E})[X(f)X(g)]=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle I_{N}.

Note that
(I\otimes\mathbb{E})[\Tr_{\alpha}(X(h_{1}),\ldots,X(h_{n}))]
is always a scalar. Indeed, since the distribution of this random matrix is unitarily invariant, so is the distribution of its entry-wise expectation, which then has to be a multiple of identity. By a slight abuse of notation, we will denote this scalar-valued functional by
\mathbb{E}
again.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\left\{D_{i}:i\in I\right\}
be non-random
N\times N
matrices. For even
n
,

D^{(0)}(I\otimes\mathbb{E})\left[\Tr_{\alpha}[X(h_{1})D^{(1)},X(h_{2})D^{(2)},\ldots,X(h_{n})D^{(n)}]\right]\\
=\frac{1}{N^{n/2}}\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})D^{(0)}\Tr_{\pi\alpha}[D^{(1)},D^{(2)},\ldots,D^{(n)}].

In particular,

\mathbb{E}\left[\Tr_{\alpha}[X(h_{1}),X(h_{2}),\ldots,X(h_{n})]\right]=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}\frac{1}{N^{n/2-\cyc_{0}(\pi\alpha)}}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n}).

Proof.

\begin{split}&\left(D^{(0)}(I\otimes\mathbb{E})\left[\Tr_{\alpha}[X(h_{1})D^{(1)},X(h_{2})D^{(2)},\ldots,X(h_{n})D^{(n)}]\right]\right)_{\ell r}\\
&\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in[0,n]^{N}\\
u(0)=r,v(0)=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}D_{v(i),u(\alpha(i))}^{(i)}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{u(i),v(i)}(h_{i})\right]\\
&\quad=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in[0,n]^{N}\\
u(i)=v(\pi(i))\\
u(0)=r,v(0)=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}D_{v(i),u(\alpha(i))}^{(i)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\pi}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{v(\pi(i)),v(i)}(h_{i})X_{v(i),v(\pi(i))}(h_{\pi(i)})\right]\\
&\quad=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in[0,n]^{N}\\
u(i)=v(\pi(i))\\
u(0)=r,v(0)=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}D_{v(i),u(\alpha(i))}^{(i)}\frac{1}{N^{n/2}}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})\\
&\quad=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in[0,n]^{N}\\
u(0)=r,v(0)=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}D_{v(i),v(\pi\alpha(i))}^{(i)}\frac{1}{N^{n/2}}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})\\
&\quad=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(n)}\left(D^{(0)}\Tr_{\pi\alpha}[D^{(1)},D^{(2)},\ldots,D^{(n)}]\right)_{\ell r}\frac{1}{N^{n/2}}C_{\pi}(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n}).\qed\end{split}

\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes(\mathbf{1}_{J_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbf{1}_{J_{n}})\right)=\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes(\mathbf{1}_{J_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbf{1}_{J_{n}})\right)

6.2.1.
Hermite polynomials of matrix argument.

Remark 6.8
.

If
\alpha\in S(n)
rather than
S_{0}(n)
, in the case of a single variable,
\Tr_{\alpha}[X]
depends only on the conjugacy class of
\alpha
, in other words on the number partition
\lambda\in\Par(n)
. Moreover

\Tr_{\lambda}[X]=p_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}),

where
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\right\}
are (random) eigenvalues of
X
and
p_{\lambda}
is the power sum symmetric polynomial. For
X=X(h)
, we also get non-homogeneous symmetric polynomials

h_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})=\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{I}\left(\lambda\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)].

With respect to the inner product induced from
\mathcal{F}_{1/N}(\mathbb{C})
, these polynomials are orthogonal for different
n
but not necessarily for different
\lambda\in\Par(n)
. We now recall a different and more familiar basis of polynomials which are fully orthogonal with respect to this inner product.

Definition 6.9
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and denote

D^{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)

and

E^{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

For
\lambda\in\Par(n)
, the Hermite polynomial of matrix argument (for
\beta=2
) is the symmetric polynomial in
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\right\}
with leading term
\frac{\left|\lambda\right|!}{c_{\lambda}}s_{\lambda}
which is an eigenfunction of the operator
D^{\ast}-E^{\ast}
with eigenvalue
-n
(note a misprint in
[
DES07
]
). Here (see Corollary 7.1.7.4 in
[
Sta99
]
)

s_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\nu\in\Par(n)}\frac{n!}{z_{\nu}}\chi^{\lambda}(\nu)p_{\nu}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\alpha\in S(n)}\chi^{\lambda}(\alpha)p_{\alpha}

is the Schur polynomial and
c_{\lambda}=\prod(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i-j+1)
is the hook length. See
[
BF97
,
DES07
,
For10
]
for more details.

Proposition 6.10
.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{C}[S_{0}(n)]
,

D^{\ast}\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]=2N\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{T}\left(\mathcal{L}(\eta)\otimes h^{\otimes(n-2)}\right)].

and

E^{\ast}\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]=\mathcal{E}[E\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)].

Proof.

For a partition
\mu=1^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}\ldots m^{k_{m}}
, a (long) computation (see
[
AGH+17
]
for a similar computation in the unitary case) shows that

D^{\ast}(p_{\mu})=p_{\mu}\Biggl[2k_{2}\frac{N^{2}}{p_{2}}+2N\sum_{l=3}^{m}k_{l}l\frac{p_{l-2}}{p_{l}}+\sum_{l=2}^{m}k_{l}(k_{l}-1)l^{2}\frac{p_{2l-2}}{p_{l}^{2}}+k_{1}(k_{1}-1)\frac{N}{p_{1}^{2}}\\
+\sum_{2\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}2k_{l_{1}}k_{l_{2}}l_{1}l_{2}\frac{p_{l_{1}+l_{2}-2}}{p_{l_{1}}p_{l_{2}}}+2\sum_{l=2}^{m}k_{1}k_{l}l\frac{p_{l-1}}{p_{1}p_{l}}+\sum_{l=3}^{m}k_{l}l\sum_{a=1}^{l-3}\frac{p_{a}p_{l-2-a}}{p_{l}}\Biggr]

On the other hand, for a permutation
\alpha
of cycle type
\mu=1^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}\ldots m^{k_{m}}
, a contraction by a transposition

•

Eliminates a cycle of length
2
: in
k_{2}
cases, with weight
q^{-1}
.

•

Turns a cycle of length
l>2
into a cycle of length
l-2
: in
k_{l}l
cases, with weight
1
.

•

Splits a cycle of length
l
into those of different lengths
a<l-2-a
: in
k_{l}l
cases, with weight
q
.

•

Splits a cycle of length

into those of equal lengths
(l-2)/2
: in
k_{l}l/2
cases, with weight
q
.

•

Eliminates two cycles of length

: in
k_{1}(k_{1}-1)/2

.

•

Eliminates a cycle of length

and turns a cycle of length

•

Glues together two cycles of different lengths

Comparing these two results, we see that

corresponds to

∎

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
\left\|h\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{N}
. Then
\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{I}\left(\chi^{\lambda}\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]
is a multiple of the Hermite polynomial of matrix argument.

Proof.

Since their leading terms differ by a factor of
\frac{\left|\lambda\right|!}{c_{\lambda}n!}
, it suffices to verify that
\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{I}\left(\chi^{\lambda}\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]
is an eigenfunction of
D^{\ast}-E^{\ast}
with eigenvalue
-n
. Indeed, for
\left\|h\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{N}
,

(D^{\ast}-E^{\ast})\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]=\mathcal{E}[(2\mathcal{L}-E)\mathrm{T}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)].

So using Proposition
5.11
,

(D^{\ast}-E^{\ast})\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]=\mathcal{E}[(2\mathcal{L}-E)\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)]=-n\mathcal{E}[\mathrm{I}\left(\eta\otimes h^{\otimes n}\right)].\qed

6.2.2.
q=0

The scaling used throughout most of the article (corresponding to the un-normalized trace) gives well-defined inner products and Fock space structure for
q=0
, see equation (
8
). However the contractions, and so the operators
\mathrm{T}\left(\alpha\otimes F\right)
, may not be defined. In this section we consider a different scaling, corresponding to the normalized trace. Under this normalization, we get well-defined contractions, but a more degenerate Fock space structure.

Definition 6.12
.

We will now denote
\alpha\otimes_{s}F
by
\tilde{I}(\alpha\otimes_{s}F)
. Define the normalized contractions

\begin{split}\tilde{C}_{\pi}(\alpha)&=q^{\cyc_{0}(\alpha)-\cyc_{0}((\pi\alpha)|_{\mathrm{supp}\left(\pi\right)^{c}})}C_{\pi}(\alpha)\\
&=q^{\cyc_{0}(\alpha)-\cyc_{0}(\pi\alpha)+\ell}{P^{[0,n]\setminus\mathrm{supp}\left(\pi\right)}_{[0,n-2\ell]}(\pi\alpha)|_{\mathrm{supp}\left(\pi\right)^{c}}},\end{split}

and the operators

(12)

\tilde{T}(\alpha\otimes_{s}F)=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}\tilde{I}(\tilde{C}_{\pi}(\alpha)\otimes_{s}C_{\pi}(F)),

so that

\tilde{I}(\alpha\otimes_{s}F)=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}(-1)^{n-\left|\pi\right|}\tilde{T}(\tilde{C}_{\pi}(\alpha)\otimes_{s}C_{\pi}(F))

and

\tilde{T}(\alpha\otimes_{s}F)\tilde{T}(\beta\otimes_{s}G)=\tilde{T}((\alpha\cup\beta)\otimes_{s}(F\otimes G)).

Finally, define

\varphi\left[\tilde{I}(\alpha\otimes_{s}F)\right]=0,\quad\varphi\left[\tilde{I}({(0)})\right]=1.

Remark 6.13
.

If we assume that
\tilde{T}(\alpha\otimes F)=q^{\cyc_{0}(\alpha)}\mathrm{T}\left(\alpha\otimes F\right)
, then for
q=0
, such a multiple is zero unless
\alpha
is a single cycle (containing
0
). As seen below, this need not be the case in general.

Remark 6.14
.

Let
\lambda
be an interval partition of
[0,n]
. As discussed in Section
2.2
, each permutation
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
is conjugate, under the action of
S(n)
, to a permutation with cycle structure

in which the elements in each cycle, as well as the cycles, appear in increasing order. Such permutation is not unique. In the results below, we will only consider permutations of this type; the results are easily extended to general
\alpha\in S_{0}(n)
, but the notation gets heavier.

Remark 6.15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{f}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
be the full Fock space of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. For

, denote by
S(f)
the semicircular element corresponding to

in its standard representation on
\mathcal{F}_{f}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
. Denote by
\Gamma_{f}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
the algebra generated by

, and by
\Phi
the vacuum state on it. Denote by

the multivariate Chebyshev polynomials, which are the elements of

which satisfy

S(f)U(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})=U(f\otimes h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})+\left\langle f,h_{1}\right\rangle U(h_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n}).

More generally, for

,

is the unique element of the weak closure of
\Gamma_{f}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
satisfying

.

Proposition 6.16
.

Let
q=0
, and
\lambda,\mu\in S_{0}(n)
as in Remark

.

(a)

\tilde{C}_{\pi}({\lambda})=0

(b)

Define the map

by

\mathcal{E}_{0}[\tilde{T}(\alpha\otimes(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n}))]=\Phi_{\alpha}[S(h_{1}),\ldots,S(h_{n})].

Then
\mathcal{E}_{0}
extends to a homomorphism from

, and to an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
to
L^{2}(\Gamma_{f}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}),\Phi)\simeq\mathcal{F}_{f}(\mathcal{H})
.

(c)

If
\lambda
is not a single cycle (containing

), then
\tilde{I}({\lambda}\otimes F)=0
. Otherwise,

\mathcal{E}_{0}[\tilde{I}({(01\ldots n)}\otimes(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n}))]=U(h_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{n})

and more generally

\mathcal{E}_{0}[\tilde{I}({(01\ldots n)}\otimes F)]=U(F).

Proof.

Note first that

\cyc_{0}(\alpha)-\cyc_{0}(\pi\alpha)+\ell=-\left|\alpha\right|+\left|\pi^{-1}\alpha\right|+\left|\pi\right|\geq 0.

Moreover, for
\alpha=\lambda

\pi\leq\lambda

\lambda

\pi\leq{\lambda}\end{subarray}}\tilde{I}(\tilde{C}_{\pi}({\lambda})\otimes C_{\pi}(F)),

and so for

\varphi\left[\tilde{T}({\lambda}\otimes F)\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{NC}_{2}(n)\\
\pi\leq{\lambda}\end{subarray}}C_{\pi}(F)=\Phi[\Phi_{\lambda}[S(h_{1}),\ldots,S(h_{n})]].

The homomorphism property follows as before, so
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is an isometric isomorphism. In particular, if
(0)
is a cycle in
\lambda

(13)

\tilde{T}({\lambda}\otimes F)=\varphi\left[\tilde{T}({\lambda}\otimes F)\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{NC}_{2}(n)\\
\pi\leq{\lambda}\end{subarray}}C_{\pi}(F)

is a scalar. Next,

\tilde{I}(\lambda\otimes F)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{NC}_{1,2}(n)\\
\pi\leq\lambda\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n-\left|\pi\right|}\tilde{T}(\tilde{C}_{\pi}({\lambda})\otimes C_{\pi}(F)).

Suppose
\lambda
contains a cycle
V

\begin{split}\tilde{I}(\alpha\otimes F)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{NC}_{1,2}([n]\setminus V)\\

Proposition 6.17
.

Define the map
R:\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow\mathcal{TP}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
by

so that

•

\mathrm{T}^{(-q)}(R(\alpha\otimes F))=R(\mathrm{T}^{(q)}(\alpha\otimes F))

•

is a star-isomorphism from

•

R
is an isometry from
\mathcal{F}_{1/N}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
onto
\mathcal{F}_{-1/N}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
.
R
is clearly a bijection. We first compute

\begin{split}\mathrm{T}^{(-q)}(\alpha)&=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}\mathrm{I}\left(C_{\pi}^{(-q)}(\alpha)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}(-1)^{\cyc_{0}((\pi\alpha)|_{\mathrm{supp}\left(\pi\right)^{c}})-\cyc_{0}(\pi\alpha)+\ell}\mathrm{I}\left(C_{\pi}^{(q)}(\alpha)\right)\\

&=(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|}\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{1,2}(n)}\mathrm{I}\left(R(C_{\pi}^{(q)}(\alpha))\right)\\
&=(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|}R(\mathrm{T}^{(q)}(\alpha))\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}R(\mathrm{T}^{(q)}(\alpha))\cdot_{-q}R(\mathrm{T}^{(q)}(\beta))&=(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|+\left|\beta\right|}\mathrm{T}^{(-q)}(\alpha)\cdot_{-q}\mathrm{T}^{(-q)}(\beta)\\

\begin{split}\varphi_{q}\left[(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|}\mathrm{T}^{(q)}(\alpha\otimes F)\right]&=\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(2n)}(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|}q^{\left|\pi\alpha\right|}C_{\pi}(F)\\

&=(-1)^{n}\sum_{\pi\in\Part_{2}(2n)}(-q)^{{\pi\alpha}}C_{\pi}(F)\\
&=(-1)^{n}\varphi_{-q}\left[\mathrm{T}^{(-q)}(\alpha\otimes F)\right].\qed\end{split}

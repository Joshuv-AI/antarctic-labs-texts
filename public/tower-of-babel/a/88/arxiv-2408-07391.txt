Matrix models for extremal and integrated correlators of higher rank
Alba Grassi, and Cristoforo Iossa

Abstract
We study extremal and integrated correlators of half-BPS operators in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 SQCD and 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM with S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) gauge group. We focus on the large R-charge sector where the number of operators insertions becomes very large. In this regime, we demonstrate that the correlators are described by a combination of Wishart and Jacobi matrix models, coupled in a non-trivial way. The size of the matrices in each model corresponds to the maximal number of insertions for each of the two single trace generators. This dual matrix model representation allows us to extract the behavior of the correlators at weak and strong coupling in a ’t Hooft-like double scaling limit, including nonperturbative corrections. Although this work focuses on S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) , we expect that our techniques can be extended to S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) for N > 3 N>3 as well.

Extremal correlators

In this paper, we study extremal correlators in four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories (SCFTs). The underlying superconformal algebra is
SU(2,2|2)
, which extends the standard Poincaré and conformal generators by including additional supersymmetry generators. Specifically, this algebra has eight Poincaré supercharges, denoted as
Q_{\alpha}^{a}
and
\overline{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{a}
, and eight conformal supercharges, denoted as
S_{\alpha}^{a}
and
\overline{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{a}
where
a=1,2
and
\alpha,\dot{\alpha}=1,\dots,4
. Furthermore, the algebra incorporates an
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{R}
R-symmetry. See
(
25
, Sec. 1)
for a concise review and a list of references.
Here we focus on a particular class of operators known as (anti-) chiral primary operators, or Coulomb branch operators. Specifically, chiral primary operators
\mathcal{O}_{I}
are those annihilated by
S,\overline{S},
and
Q
, while anti-chiral primary operators
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{I}
are annihilated by
S,\overline{S},
and
\overline{Q}
.
These operators are Lorentz scalars and
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}
singlets.
It follows from unitarity and supersymmetry that the scaling dimension
\Delta
and the
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{R}
R-charge
R
of these operators are related by

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{2},

\displaystyle\text{for chiral}~,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=-\frac{R}{2},

\displaystyle\text{for anti-chiral}~.

An important property of these operators is the fact that their OPE is non-singular

\mathcal{O}_{I}(x)\mathcal{O}_{I}(0)=\sum_{K}C^{K}_{IJ}\mathcal{O}_{K}(0)+\cdots

(2.12)

where
\cdots
denote regular terms when
x\to 0
. Therefore (anti-) chiral primaries endowed with the OPE form a ring, often dubbed chiral ring, which is commutative and freely generated
2
2

2

For non-Lagrangian theories this is still conjectural, but in this paper we focus on Lagrangian theories.
. Thanks to this property it is always possible to choose a basis of operators and a normalization such that the structure constants are trivial and the OPE reads

\mathcal{O}_{I}(x)\mathcal{O}_{J}(x)=\mathcal{O}_{I}\mathcal{O}_{J}(x)~.

(2.13)

Within this normalization the two point function of chiral and anti-chiral operators becomes however non-trivial

\langle\mathcal{O}_{I}(x)\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{J}(0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}_{4}}={G_{IJ}\over|x|^{2\Delta_{I}}}\delta_{\Delta_{I}\Delta_{J}},

(2.14)

where
G_{IJ}
is a non-trivial function of the marginal couplings.

Outlook

In this paper we studied extremal and integrated correlators in four dimensional
SU(3)
SCFT in the regime where we have a large number of insertions, i.e. large
R
-charge. We found that a new description emerge involving a set of two coupled matrix models: a Wishart model and a Jacobi model.
The size of the matrices in these models corresponds to the maximal number of insertions for each of the two single trace operators:
\phi_{2}
and
\Phi_{2}(x,y)
insertions are controlled by the Wishart model, while
\phi_{3}
and
\Phi_{3}(x,y)
insertions are controlled by the Jacobi model.
This description gives us a new analytic handle into these regimes and the corresponding non-perturbative effects.

A.1

Let

Schematically, a Wishart matrix model with potential

Z={1\over{\rm Vol}(U(n))}\int{\rm d}W{\rm e}^{V(W)}.

(A.280)

Here we are interested in a particular example of such matrix model where the potential is

V(W)=-n{\rm Tr}W-(3m+3){\rm Tr}\log W~.

(A.281)

After gauge-fixing, we can express the matrix integral as an ordinary multidimensional integral over the eigenvalues
z_{i}
of

n

Z_{\rm MP}^{(m)}(n)={1\over n!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}}{\rm d}^{n}z\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{-nz_{i}}z_{i}^{3m+3}~.

(A.282)

This model is exactly solvable and is commonly known as the Wishart-Laguerre model, see for instance
[
60
,
61
,
62
,
63

Z_{\rm MP}^{(m)}(n)={1\over n!}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{n\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n+3m+3}}\right)}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(j+2)\Gamma(j+3m+4)

Whether we orthogonalize with respect to operators of the same dimension only, or if we also include operators of lower dimensions.

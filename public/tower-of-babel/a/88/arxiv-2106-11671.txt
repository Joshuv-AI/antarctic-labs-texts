Representation Formula for Viscosity Solutions to a class of Nonlinear Parabolic PDEs
Marco Pozza

Abstract
We provide a representation formula for viscosity solutions to a class of nonlinear second order parabolic PDEs given as a sup–envelope function. This is done through a dynamic programming principle derived from [ 7 ] . The formula can be seen as a nonlinear extension of the Feynman–Kac formula and is based on the backward stochastic differential equations theory.

Introduction

It is well known that viscosity solutions were conceived by Crandall and Lions (1982) in the framework of optimal control theory. The goal was to show well posedness of Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations in the whole space, and to prove, via dynamic programming principle, the value function of a suitable optimal control problem being the unique solution.

When trying to extend viscosity methods to the analysis of second order
parabolic partial differential equations
, PDEs for short, and getting representation formulas, it appeared clear that some stochastic dynamics must be brought into play. Not surprisingly, this has been first done for stochastic control models. The hard work of a generation of mathematicians,
[
15
,
14
,
1
,
13
,
10
,
18
]
among the others, allowed making effective dynamic programming approach to stochastic control problems.

Prompted by this body of investigations, a stream of research arose in the probabilistic community ultimately leading to the theory of
backward stochastic differential equations
, BSDEs for short, which was introduced by Pardoux and Peng in
[
20
]
(1990). Since then, it has attracted a great interest due to its connections with mathematical finance and PDEs, as well as with stochastic control. This theory has been in particular used to extend the classical Feynman–Kac formula, which establishes a link between linear parabolic PDEs and
stochastic differential equations
, SDEs for short, to semilinear and quasilinear equations, see for example
[
6
,
5
,
16
,
19
]
. See also
[
22
]
for a rather complete overview of the semilinear case.

For sake of clarity, let us consider the following semilinear parabolic PDE problem coupled with final conditions,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\left\langle\sigma\sigma^{\dagger},D^{2}_{x}u\right\rangle(t,x)+(\nabla_{x}ub)(t,x)&\\
+\partial_{t}u(t,x)+f(t,x,u,\nabla_{x}u\sigma)&=0\end{aligned}&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\\
u(T,x)=g(x),&x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(1)

then its viscosity solution can be written as
u(t,x)=\mathds{E}\left(Y^{t,x}_{t}\right)
, where
Y
is given by the following system, called
forward backward stochastic differential equation
or FBSDE in short, which is made in turn of two equations, the first one is a SDE, and the second one a
backward stochastic differential equation
, BSDE for short, depending on the first one:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \!X^{t,x}_{s}\!=&x+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma\left(r,X^{t,x}_{r}\right)dW_{r}+\int_{t}^{s}b\left(r,X^{t,x}_{r}\right)dr,\\
\!Y^{t,x}_{s}\!=&g\left(X^{t,x}_{T}\right)\!+\!\!\int_{s}^{T}\!\!f\!\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x},Y_{r}^{t,x},Z_{r}^{t,x}\right)\!dr\!-\!\!\int_{s}^{T}\!\!Z_{r}^{t,x}dW_{r},\end{aligned}\right.s\!\in\![t,T],x\!\in\!\mathds{R}^{N}.

As can be intuitively seen, the SDE takes care of the linear operator defined by
\sigma
and
b
, also called the
infinitesimal generators
of the SDE, while the BSDE depends on
f
and
g
. In other words, this extension of the Feynman–Kac formula basically does not modify the treatment of the second order linear operator with respect to the completely linear case.

Subsequently, Peng introduced in
[
23
]
(2006) the notion of
G
–expectation
, a nonlinear expectation generated by a fully nonlinear second order operator
G
via its viscosity solutions. This work has originated an active research field, with relevant applications to Mathematical Finance.

Peng has improved this theory in several papers and has given a comprehensive account of it in the book
[
24
]
, where he highlights the role of the so–called
sublinear expectations
, namely
G
–expectations generated by sublinear operators given as a sup–envelope function. Finally in
[
7
]
, Peng provides representation formulas for viscosity solutions using these expectations. More precisely, given a sublinear operators
G
and the
G
–heat equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+G\left(D_{x}^{2}u(t,x)\right)=0,&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\\
u(T,x)=g(x),&x\in\mathds{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

(2)

he represents the viscosity solution as

u(t,x)=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}}\mathds{E}\left(g\left(x+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right),

where
\mathcal{A}
is a family of stochastic process associated to
G
and
W
is a Brownian motion.
The key to prove it is a
dynamic programming principle
that we will illustrate in the paper, see
section

3
. We point out that this formula has been established in
[
15
]
using a different method and that the novelty with respect to the Feynman–Kac formula is essentially given by the sublinearity of the operator
G
.

The purpose of this article is to apply a generalized version of the dynamic programming principle of
[
7
]
in order to give representation formulas of solutions to PDE problems of the type

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+F\left(t,x,u,\nabla_{x}u,D^{2}_{x}u\right)=0,&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\\
u(T,x)=g(x),&x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(3)

where
F
is a sup–envelope function of semilinear operators as in (
1
). This problem is clearly a blend between (
1
) and (
2
) which generalize both.

This is hopefully just a first step to further extend the Feynman–Kac formula to fully nonlinear problems. In this regard the method here employed has been successfully used in
[
28
]
to provide a representation formula for a particular Dirichlet type problem. We also point out that there is close connection between this method and second order BSDEs, 2BSDEs for short. 2BSDEs were introduced by Cheridito, Soner, Touzi and Victoir in
[
3
]
(2007). Then, in 2011, Soner, Touzi and Zhang
[
29
]
provided a complete theory of existence and uniqueness for 2BSDEs under Lipschitz conditions. In those papers is also analyzed the connection between 2BSDEs and fully nonlinear PDEs. Among the subsequent developments of this theory we cite
[
27
,
29
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
12
]
and in particular
[
26
]
, which performs its analysis replacing the Lipschitz condition on
y
with monotonicity as we do here.

Setting of the Problem

Here we introduce the problem, which is object of investigation, as well as basic preliminary facts.

We start giving the assumption of the PDE operator which we will analyze in this paper.

Assumptions 2.1
.

We fix a
T>0
, hereafter called
terminal time
, the constants
\ell\geq 0
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
\mu\in\mathds{R}
and let
K_{F}
be a set made up by functions

(b,\sigma):[0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathds{R}^{N\times M}

and which satisfies, for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}
, the following conditions:

i)

(local equiboundedness)
\sup\limits_{(b,\sigma)\in K_{F}}|b(t,x)|+|\sigma(t,x)|<\infty
;

ii)

(equicontinuity) for each
\varepsilon>0
there is a neighborhood
U
of
(t,x)
such that
|b(t,x)-b(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|+|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon
for any
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in U
and
(b,\sigma)\in K_{F}
;

iii)

(equiLipschitz continuity) for any
(b,\sigma)\in K_{F}
and
t\in[0,T]

|b(t,x)-b(t,x^{\prime})|\leq\ell|x-x^{\prime}|,\qquad|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime})|\leq\ell|x-x^{\prime}|.

Now let

f:[0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathds{R}^{N\times M}\times\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathds{R}

be a continuous function such that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,
x,x^{\prime},q,q^{\prime},z,z^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}^{N\times M}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}
,

iv)

|f(t,x,q,A,y,z)-f(t,x^{\prime},q^{\prime},A^{\prime},y,z^{\prime})|\leq\ell(|x-x^{\prime}|+|q-q^{\prime}|+|A-A^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|)
;

v)

|f(t,x,q,A,y,z)|\leq\ell(1+|x|+|q|+|A|+|y|+|z|)
;

vi)

(y-y^{\prime})(f(t,x,q,A,y,z)-f(t,x,q,A,y^{\prime},z))\leq\mu|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}
;

vii)

fixed a
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathds{R}
, a compact set
K\subset\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathds{R}^{N\times M}\times\mathds{R}^{N}
and a positive
\varepsilon
there is a
\delta>0
such that

|f(t,x,q,A,y,z)-f(t^{\prime},x,q,A,y^{\prime},z)|<\varepsilon

for any
(t^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B_{\delta}((t,y))\cap[0,T]\times\mathds{R}
and
(q,A,z)\in K
.

In what follows we will focus on nonlinear operator of this type

F(t,x,y,p,S):=\sup_{(b,\sigma)\in K_{F}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\sigma\sigma^{\dagger}(t,x),S\right\rangle+p^{\dagger}b(t,x)+f_{(b,\sigma)}\left(t,x,y,p^{\dagger}\sigma\right),

where
f_{(b,\sigma)}\left(t,x,y,p^{\dagger}\sigma\right):=f\left(t,x,b(t,x),\sigma(t,x),y,p^{\dagger}\sigma(t,x)\right)
.

It is a simple verification that
F
is continuous. Furthermore we have that
F
is an elliptic and convex (for the second order term) operator in the sense that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,
y\in\mathds{R}
,
p\in\mathds{R}^{N}
and
S\in\mathds{S}^{N}
,

i)

(Convexity)
for any
\delta\in[0,1]

F(t,x,y,p,\delta S+(1-\delta)S^{\prime})\leq\delta F(t,x,y,p,S)+(1-\delta)F(t,x,y,p,S^{\prime});

ii)

(Ellipticity)
there is a
\lambda\geq 0
such that, for any
S^{\prime}\geq 0
,

F(t,x,y,p,S+S^{\prime})-F(t,x,y,p,S)\geq\lambda|S^{\prime}|.

The
\lambda
in
ii)
is actually the lowest eigenvalue of the matrix
\sigma\sigma^{\dagger}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We point out that
ii)
and
i)
in
2.1
and the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem imply that
K_{F}
is relatively compact in the compact–open topology and it is easy to see that its closure
\overline{K_{F}}
satisfies
ii)
,
iii)
and
i)
. Moreover the map

(b,\sigma)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\sigma\sigma^{\dagger}(t,x),S\right\rangle+p^{\dagger}b(t,x)+f_{(b,\sigma)}\left(t,x,y,p^{\dagger}\sigma\right)

is continuous, thus we have that

F(t,x,y,p,S)=\max_{(b,\sigma)\in\overline{K_{F}}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\sigma\sigma^{\dagger}(t,x),S\right\rangle+p^{\dagger}b(t,x)+f_{(b,\sigma)}\left(t,x,y,p^{\dagger}\sigma\right).

For this reason we will always assume that
K_{F}
is closed.

Problem 2.3
.

Let

be a terminal time,

a sublinear operator as in

,

where

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+F\left(t,x,u,\nabla_{x}u,D^{2}_{x}u\right)=0,&t\in(0,T),x\in\mathds{R}^{N},\\

Definition 2.4
.

Given an upper semicontinuous function

An upper semicontinuous function

is called a

if, for any suitable

and

For viscosity sub and supersolutions to

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

, which is based on a construction on a broader set than

. This set, which we call

, is made up of the controls

By

, for any bounded subset

3
Dynamic Programming Principle

The scope of this
section
is to provide the necessary instruments to prove the
4.4
, which will be used to derive representation formulas for the viscosity solution to
2.3
. The dynamic programming principle is, in this contest, an instrument that permit us to break a stochastic trajectory in two or more part. In particular for the problem (
1
) it means that

\mathds{E}\left(u\left(s,X^{t,x}_{s}\right)+\int_{s}^{T}f\left(r,X_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}},Y_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}},Z_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}}\right)dr\right)\\
\begin{aligned} =&\mathds{E}\left(Y^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}}_{s}+\int_{s}^{T}f\left(r,X_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}},Y_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}},Z_{r}^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}}\right)dr\right)\\
=&\mathds{E}\left(Y^{t,x}_{s}+\int_{s}^{T}f\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x},Y_{r}^{t,x},Z_{r}^{t,x}\right)dr\right)\\
=&\mathds{E}\left(Y^{t,x}_{t}\right)=u(t,x),\end{aligned}

which is just a simple consequence of the uniqueness of the solutions to the FBSDE, while for the
G
–heat equation (
2
) this means that

\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{G}}\mathds{E}\left(u\left(s,x+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(r)dW_{r}\right)\right)\\
\begin{aligned} =&\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{G}}\sup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{G}}\mathds{E}\left(g\left(x+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(r)dW_{r}+\int_{s}^{T}\sigma^{\prime}(r)dW_{r}\right)\right)\\
=&\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{G}}\mathds{E}\left(g\left(x+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma(r)dW_{r}\right)\right)\\
=&u(t,x).\end{aligned}

(4)

The proof of (
4
) is contained in
[
7
, Subsection 3.1]
. This also intuitively explain why we ask to the elements of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
to be cadlag in time. We point out that in
[
7
]
the authors ask to the elements of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
to only be measurable in time, but for the analysis of the more general
2.3

The dynamic programming principle exposed in theorem
4.4
is a generalization of the one presented by Denis, Hu and Peng in
[
7
]
and will be obtained in a similar way.

We start endowing
\mathcal{A}_{F}
with the topology of the

–convergence on compact sets, which is to say that a sequence converges in
\mathcal{A}_{F}
if and only if it converges in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega\times K)

Let
\varphi
be a continuous map from
[0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{A}_{F}
into
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T};\mathds{R})
such that, for any stopping time
\tau
with values in
[0,T]
,

and
(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}_{F}

\varphi_{\tau}(x,b,\sigma)
is affected only by the values that
(b,\sigma)
assumes on
[\tau,T]
, i.e.

\varphi_{\tau}:\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{A}_{F}|_{[\tau,T]}\longrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T};\mathds{R}),

and if
(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}_{F}^{\tau}
, then
\varphi_{\tau}(x,b,\sigma)
is
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}_{T}
–measurable. We further assume that, for any stopping time
\tau
with value in
[0,T]
and
\zeta
in
L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathds{R}^{N}\right)
,

\sup_{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}_{F}}\mathds{E}(|\varphi_{\tau}(\zeta,b,\sigma)|)<\infty,

(5)

thus the function

\Phi_{\tau}(\zeta):=\esss_{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}_{F}}\mathds{E}(\varphi_{\tau}(\zeta,b,\sigma)|\mathcal{F}_{\tau})

is well defined, as we will prove in

In this section the function
\Phi
represents, roughly speaking, the viscosity solution,

is the first part of a stochastic trajectory broken off at
\tau
(this is why we restrict
\varphi_{\tau}
on
\mathcal{A}_{F}|_{[\tau,T]}

Appendix A
Connection with 2BSDEs

In
[
3
]
it is established a connection between second order BSDEs, 2BSDEs for short, and parabolic PDEs similar to the ones studied here, which can be used to obtain representation formulas. The link between this approach and this method can be seen, in particular, in the dynamic programming principle we have developed. We emphasize, on the other side, starting from the dynamic programming principle, we can provide a new angle for looking at 2BSDE. We present this point of view in the
appendix
. This is clearly just a short note and not a complete analysis.

We start giving the formulation of 2BSDE as in
[
29
,
26
]
. Assume that
\Omega:=\left\{\omega\in C\left([0,T];\mathds{R}^{N}\right):\omega_{0}=0\right\}
and let
\mathds{P}_{0}
be the Wiener measure. Note that in this space the Brownian motion
W
is a projection, i.e.
W_{t}(\omega)=\omega_{t}
. Denote with
[W]_{t}
the quadratic variation of the projection and with

\hat{a}_{t}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{[W]_{t}-[W]_{t-\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}

its variation. We will then denote with
\mathcal{P}_{W}
the set of the probability measures
\mathds{P}
such that
[W]
is absolutely continuous in
t
and
\hat{a}\in\mathds{S}^{N}_{+}
,
\mathds{P}
–a.e.. In particular
\mathds{P}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{W}
because
[W]_{t}=tI_{N}
and
\hat{a}_{t}=I_{N}
,
\mathds{P}_{0}
–a.e., where
I_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix. Moreover, let
\mathcal{P}_{S}
be the subset of
\mathcal{P}_{W}
composed by the probability measures
\mathds{P}^{\alpha}:=\mathds{P}_{0}\circ(X^{\alpha})^{-1}
, where

X^{\alpha}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\alpha^{1/2}_{s}dW_{s},\qquad\mathds{P}_{0}\text{--a.e.},

and
\alpha
is a progressive process in
\mathds{S}^{N}_{+}
such that, for two fixed
\underline{a},\overline{a}
in
\mathds{S}^{N}_{+}
,
\underline{a}\leq\alpha\leq\overline{a}
,
\mathds{P}_{0}
–a.e.. It is then apparent a link between
\mathcal{P}_{S}
and the control set
\mathcal{A}_{F}
.

Now, given a function

h:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{N}\times D_{h}\to\mathds{R},

where
D_{h}
is a subset of
\mathds{R}^{N\times N}
containing 0, define for any

f(t,\omega,y,z,a):=\sup_{\gamma\in D_{h}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle\gamma,a\rangle-h(t,\omega,y,z,\gamma)\right).

Furthermore let
\hat{f}(t,y,z):=f(t,y,z,\hat{a}_{t})
,

\mathcal{P}_{h}^{2}:=\left\{\mathds{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{S}:\mathds{E}^{\mathds{P}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\hat{f}(t,0,0)\right|^{2}dt\right)<\infty\right\}

and
\mathcal{P}_{h}^{2}(t,\mathds{P}):=\left\{\mathds{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{h}^{2}:\mathds{P}^{\prime}=\mathds{P}\text{ on }\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}
.

A pair of progressive processes
(Y,Z)
is solution to the 2BSDE

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\hat{f}(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int^{T}_{t}Z_{s}dW_{s}+K_{T}-K_{t}

(21)

if, for any
\mathds{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{h}^{2}

i)

Y_{T}=\xi
,
\mathds{P}
–a.e.;

ii)

the process

defined below has non decreasing path
\mathds{P}
–a.e.,

satisfies the minimum condition

z,z^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,

ii)

|f(t,y,z)|\leq|f(t,0,0)|+\ell(1+|y|+|z|)
;

iii)

|f(t,y,z)-f(t,y,z^{\prime})|\leq\ell|z-z^{\prime}|
;

iv)

(y-y^{\prime})(f(t,y,z)-f(t,y^{\prime},z))\leq\mu|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}
;

v)

v\mapsto f(t,v,z)
is continuous.

A solution to the BSDE
(\xi,f,T)
, where
\xi
and
T
have respectively the role of a final condition and a terminal time, is a pair
(Y,Z)
of continuous progressive processes belonging to
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
such that

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T].

(25)

The followings are classical results of BSDE theory, see for example theorems 1.2 and 1.3, and proposition 1.3 in
[
21
]
.

Theorem B.6
.

Under the
B.5
the BSDE (
25
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)
. Furthermore, if
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
is the solution to the BSDE
(\xi^{\prime},f^{\prime},T)
, there exists a constant
c
, which depends on

,
\mu
and
\ell
, such that

\mathds{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{t}-Z^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}dt\right)\\
\leq c\mathds{E}\left(|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|f(t,Y^{\prime}_{t},Z^{\prime}_{t})-f^{\prime}(t,Y^{\prime}_{t},Z^{\prime}_{t})|^{2}dt\right).

Appendix C
Comparison Theorem

Consider the parabolic problem

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+G\left(t,x,u,\nabla_{x}u,D_{x}^{2}u\right)=0,\quad t\in(0,T),x\in\mathds{R}^{N},

(26)

under the following
appendix
.

Assumptions C.1
.

G
is a continuous elliptic operator which admits, for any
t\in[0,T]
,
x,p,p^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}
and
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathds{S}^{N}
, a positive
\ell
and a
\mu\in\mathds{R}
such that

i)

|G(t,x,r,p,S)-G(t,x,r,p,S^{\prime})|\leq\ell\left(1+|x|^{2}\right)|S-S^{\prime}|
;

ii)

|G(t,x,r,p,S)-G(t,x,r,p^{\prime},S)|\leq\ell(1+|x|)|p-p^{\prime}|
;

iii)

(G(t,x,r,p,S)-G(t,x,r^{\prime},p,S))(r-r^{\prime})\leq\mu|r-r^{\prime}|^{2}
;

iv)

for each
R>0
there is a modulus of continuity
\omega_{R}
for which

G(t,x,r,\alpha(x-y),S)-G(t,y,r,\alpha(x-y),S^{\prime})\leq\omega_{R}\left(\alpha|x-y|^{2}+|x-y|\right)

for any
\alpha>0
,
t\in[0,T]
,
x,y\in B_{R}(0)
and
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathds{S}^{N}
such that

-3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&0\\
0&I_{N}\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\
0&-S^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\leq 3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&-I_{N}\\
-I_{N}&I_{N}\end{pmatrix},

(27)

where
I_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.

For this problem holds a comparison result, which is a parabolic version of
[
11
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Theorem C.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be respectively a sub and a supersolution to (
26
) under
C.1
. If
u(T,x)\leq v(T,x)
for any
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
and both
u
and
v
have polynomial growth, then
u\leq v
on
(0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

First of all let
\gamma
be a constant that we will fix later. If we define
u_{\gamma}(t,x):=e^{\gamma t}u(t,x)
and
v_{\gamma}(t,x):=e^{\gamma t}v(t,x)
we have that they are a sub and a supersolution, respectively, to

\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\gamma u(t,x)+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,u,\nabla_{x}u,D^{2}_{x}u\right)=0,

(28)

where
G_{\gamma}(t,x,r,p,S):=e^{\gamma t}G\left(t,x,e^{-\gamma t}r,e^{-\gamma t}p,e^{-\gamma t}S\right)
. It is a simple verification that
G_{\gamma}
still satisfies
C.1
and in particular

(r-r^{\prime})(G_{\gamma}(t,x,r,p,S)-\gamma r-G_{\gamma}(t,x,r^{\prime},p,S)+\gamma r^{\prime})\leq(\mu-\gamma)|r-r^{\prime}|^{2}.

(29)

Since we assumed that
u
and
v
have polynomial growth, there is a constant
k\geq 2
such that, for any
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{\gamma}(t,x)-v_{\gamma}(t,x)-2\varepsilon(1+|x|)^{k}=-\infty,

thus we set
\phi(x):=(1+|x|)^{k}
,
u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}:=u_{\gamma}-\varepsilon\phi
,
v_{\gamma,\varepsilon}:=v_{\gamma}+\varepsilon\phi
and we get

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-v_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)=-\infty.

(30)

Notice that

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}(t,x)-\gamma u_{\gamma}(t,x)+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,u_{\gamma},\nabla_{x}u_{\gamma},D^{2}_{x}u_{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-\gamma(u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)+\varepsilon\phi(x))

\displaystyle+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\phi(x),\nabla_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\nabla\phi(x),D^{2}_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon D^{2}\phi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-\gamma u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-(\gamma-\mu)\varepsilon\phi(x)

\displaystyle+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,u_{\gamma,\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\nabla\phi(x),D^{2}_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon D^{2}\phi(x)\right)

and
k-2

i)
and
ii)

yield that there exists a constant

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-\gamma u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-(\gamma-\mu)\varepsilon\phi(x)

\displaystyle+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,u_{\gamma,\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon},D^{2}_{x}u_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\right)+C\varepsilon\phi(x).

Similarly we can prove that

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)-\gamma v_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(t,x)+(\gamma-\mu)\varepsilon\phi(x)

\displaystyle+G_{\gamma}\left(t,x,v_{\gamma,\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}v_{\gamma,\varepsilon},D^{2}_{x}v_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\right)-C\varepsilon\phi(x).

Choosing

(0,T]\times\mathds{R}^{N}

Fractional Integral and Generalized Stieltjes Transforms for Hypergeometric Functions as Transmutation Operators This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Exact Solvability and Symmetry Avatars in honour of Luc Vinet. The full collection is available at http://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/ESSA2014.html

Abstract
For each of the eight n n -th derivative parameter changing formulas for Gauss hypergeometric functions a corresponding fractional integration formula is given. For both types of formulas the differential or integral operator is intertwining between two actions of the hypergeometric differential operator (for two sets of parameters): a so-called transmutation property. This leads to eight fractional integration formulas and four generalized Stieltjes transform formulas for each of the six different explicit solutions of the hypergeometric differential equation, by letting the transforms act on the solutions. By specialization two Euler type integral representations for each of the six solutions are obtained.

Preliminaries about hypergeometric functions

The
Gauss hypergeometric function

[

, Chapter 2]
,

[

, Chapter 2]
,
[

is defined by its power series

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};z\right)=F(a,b;c;z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}k!}z^{k}\qquad(|z|<1).

(2.1)

The function (

It satisfies the

(

)
and it is uniquely determined as the function regular near 0 and equal to 1

Important transformation formulas, due to Pfaff and Euler, respectively,

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};z\right)=(1-z)^{-a}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,c-b}{c};\frac{z}{z-1}\right)\qquad

\displaystyle(z\notin[1,\infty)),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};z\right)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{c-a,c-b}{c};z\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{b};z\right)=(1-z)^{-a}\qquad(z\notin[1,\infty)).

The main ideas illustrated in a special case

3.1
Transmutation property of a differentiation operator

In
[
7
, 2.8(20)–(27)]
or

[
18
, § 15.5]
there is a list of eight parameter changing

n
-th derivative formulas for Gauss hypergeometric functions.
One of these is
[
18
, (15.5.4)]
:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{n}\left(z^{c-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};z\right)\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(c-n)}\,z^{c-n-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c-n};z\right).

(3.1)

Formula (
3.1
) can be proved immediately by power series expansion (
2.1
). Note also that the
n
-th derivative case follows by iteration of
the case
n=1
. The case
n=1
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle D_{c-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};\,.\,\right)=(c-1)\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c-1};\,.\,\right),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle(D_{a}f)(z):=zf^{\prime}(z)+af(z)=z^{-a+1}\frac{d}{dz}\big(z^{a}f(z)\big).

(3.3)

Clearly, if
f
is analytic at 0 then
D_{a}f
is analytic at 0 and

(D_{a}f)(0)=af(0)
.

Straightforward computation followed by iteration gives the following
transmutation property:

\displaystyle L_{a,b,c-1}D_{c-1}=D_{c-1}L_{a,b,c},

(3.4)

\displaystyle L_{a,b,c-n}D_{c-n}\cdots D_{c-2}D_{c-1}=D_{c-n}\cdots D_{c-2}D_{c-1}L_{a,b,c}.

(3.5)

Formula (
3.2
) is also a consequence of (
3.4
).
Indeed, by (
3.4
)
L_{a,b,c-1}
annihilates the left-hand side
of (
3.2
).
Since this left-hand side is regular at 0, it must be a constant times

F(a,b;c-1;\,.\,)
with the constant obtained by evaluating both sides
at 0.
Similarly, (
3.1
) is a consequence of (
3.5
).

Because of the above argument, it is natural to consider

D_{c-1}w_{j}(\,.\,;a,b,c)
not just for
j=1
but also for the other
explicit solutions of the hypergeometric differential equation
(
j=2,\ldots,6
). It will turn out that for all

we get
some constant factor times
w_{j}(\,.\,;a,b,c-1)
. For instance,
with
w_{2}
given by (
2.6
),

\displaystyle D_{c-1}w_{2}(\,.\,;a,b,c)=\frac{(a-c+1)(b-c+1)}{2-c}w_{2}(\,.\,;a,b,c-1).

(3.6)

This is equivalent to the prototypical differentiation formula for
the hypergeometric function

[
4
, (15.5.4)]

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};z\right)=\frac{ab}{c}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a+1,b+1}{c+1};z\right).

(3.7)

Corresponding to each of the eight
n
-th derivative formulas

[
18
, (15.5.2)–(15.5.9)]
one can write down a transmutation formula
like (

), and corresponding to each of these transmutation
formulas one can write down an

-th derivative formula for each of
the six solutions

. These will not be included in the present paper. The transmutation formulas for the first derivative operators
were earlier given by Dereziński

He calls them commutation relations and he relates them to

(or equivalently

This way to associate a Lie algebra (the so-called dynamical

This was later understood in the framework of

3.4
Generalized Stieltjes transforms as transmutation operators

We already observed in Section
1
that variants of
fractional integral transforms and the Euler integral representation
for hypergeometric functions naturally lead to formulas
involving a generalized Stieltjes transform.
In the definition by
Widder
[
26
, Section 8]
the generalized Stieltjes transform
sends a suitable measure
\alpha
or function
\phi

(with
d\alpha(t)=\phi(t)\,dt
)
to a function
f
analytic on
\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0]
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\alpha(t)}{(z+t)^{\rho}}=f(z).

The special case
\rho=1
gives the classical Stieltjes transform.
In order to have analytic expressions similar to the ones in fractional
integral transforms, we will work with transforms (
1.7
).

Transforms of
generalized Stieltjes type have transmutation properties.
For instance, from (
3.14
) we have, associated with (
3.18
),
the intertwining property

\displaystyle L_{a,b,c+\mu;x}\left(x^{1-c-\mu}\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{c-1}f(y)(y-x)^{\mu-1}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=x^{1-c-\mu}\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{c-1}(L_{a,b,c}f)(y)(y-x)^{\mu-1}\,dy\qquad(0<x<1).

Noteworthy is the case
\mu=0
. Then we have the same hypergeometric differential operator on both sides. The Stieltjes transform
will then map solutions of the differential equation to other solutions.

[

(3.22)

If we replace

Stieltjes transform sending a

8
Generalized Stieltjes transform

and fractional integral transform combined

As observed in
[
8
, p. 213]
, generalized Stieltjes transforms
of different order are connected with each other by fractional integration.
Formula (
8.1
) below was essentially given there,
and later given with proof in
[
10
, Theorem 9]

Some related identities can also be proved:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
{\rm Re}\,(1-\nu)>{\rm Re}\,\mu>0
. Assume that
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}((m,M))
and
that the integrals on the right-hand side
of the four identities below converge
absolutely. Then

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x}\left(\int_{m}^{M}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\nu)}{(z-y)^{1-\nu}}\,dz\right)\frac{(x-y)^{\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\,dy=\int_{m}^{M}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\mu-\nu)}{(z-x)^{1-\mu-\nu}}\,dz\quad(x<m),\!\!\!

(8.1)

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\left(\int_{m}^{M}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\nu)}{(y-z)^{1-\nu}}\,dz\right)\frac{(y-x)^{\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\,dy=\int_{m}^{M}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\mu-\nu)}{(x-z)^{1-\mu-\nu}}\,dz\quad(M<x),\!\!\!

(8.2)

\displaystyle\int_{m}^{\infty}\left(\int_{m}^{y}f(z)\frac{(y-z)^{\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\,dz\right)\frac{\Gamma(1-\nu)}{(x-y)^{1-\nu}}\,dy=\int_{m}^{\infty}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\mu-\nu)}{(z-x)^{1-\mu-\nu}}\,dz\quad(x<m),

(8.3)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{M}\left(\int_{y}^{M}f(z)\frac{(z-y)^{\mu-1}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\,dz\right)\frac{\Gamma(1-\nu)}{(y-x)^{1-\nu}}\,dy=\int_{-\infty}^{M}f(z)\frac{\Gamma(1-\mu-\nu)}{(x-z)^{1-\mu-\nu}}\,dz\quad(x>M).\!\!\!\!

(8.4)

The proofs are immediate, by the Fubini theorem and by a version

[

8.2
)
is an immediate consequence of (
8.1

8.4

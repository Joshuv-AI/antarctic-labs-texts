On the Ergodic Capacity of Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS)-Aided MIMO Channels
Chongjun Ouyang ∗† , Hao Xu ∗ , Xujie Zang ∗ , and Hongwen Yang ∗

Abstract
Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) have emerged as a promising technique to enhance the system spectral efficiency. This paper investigates the ergodic channel capacity (ECC) of an RIS-aided multiple-input multiple-output channel under the assumption that the transmitter-RIS, RIS-receiver, and transmitter-receiver channels contain deterministic line-of-sight paths. Novel expressions are derived to characterize the upper and lower bounds of the ECC. To unveil more system insights, asymptotic analyses are performed to the system ECC in the limit of large signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and number of reflecting elements (REs). Theoretical analyses suggest that the RIS’s deployment can shape the ECC curve by influencing its high-SNR power offset and the ECC can get improved by increasing the number of REs.

I
Introduction

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) can enhance the system spectral efficiency (SE) with low energy consumption and deployment costs, which is believed to be a promising technology in next-generation networks
[
1
]
. Specifically, by properly programming its adopted phase shifts, an RIS can control the wireless physical layer channel intelligently and then give it a performance boost
[
2
]
. One of the key scenarios where RIS shines, is for SE improvements in multi-antenna systems
[
3
]
.

Recently, there has been an increasing interest in performing theoretical analyses on the ergodic SE (ESE) of RIS-aided multi-antenna systems
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
7
]
. For example, a closed-form lower bound was derived to characterize the ESE of an RIS-aided multiuser multiple-input single-output (MISO) channel under Rayleigh fading
[
4
]
. Yet, the RIS is usually deployed on the facade of a high building in the vicinity of transceivers, which yields deterministic line-of-sight (LoS) paths in the RIS-related channels. Thus, it makes more sense to characterize the ESE of RIS-aided systems under fading models that consider the influence of deterministic LoS paths, such as Rician fading. Particularly, a tight upper bound of the ESE of a single-user RIS-aided MISO Rician channel was formulated in
[
5
]
, which was further extended to the multiuser case
[
6
]
. Moreover, the ergodic channel capacity (ECC) of an RIS-aided multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) Rician channel was investigated in
[
7
]
under the assumption of single-stream transmissions. In fact, the MIMO channel therein can be considered equivalent to a MISO one. As a supplement of this work, a large-system approximation of ECC of an RIS-aided MIMO Rician channel under multi-stream transmission was derived by the replica method
[
8
]
. Unfortunately, the ECC’s calculation therein requires the solutions of a series of fixed-point equations and it is challenging to unveil more insights on the effect of RIS from the analytical results in
[
8
]
.

To portray the effect of RIS on the ECC and thus fill the aforementioned research gap, this paper evaluates the SE performance of an RIS-aided MIMO Rician channel under multi-stream transmission by formulating upper and lower bounds of the ECC. For gleaning further insights, we perform asymptotic analyses to the ECC in the limit of large signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and number of reflecting elements (REs). We observe that the RIS can influence the asymptotic ECC through its high-SNR power offset. Moreover, it is also found that increasing the number of REs can reduce the high-SNR power offset and thus enhance the ECC.

III
Ergodic Channel Capacity

Assume that the instantaneous channel state information (CSI) of
G
is unknown at the transmitter and perfectly known at the receiver. We comment that this assumption has been widely used in the MIMO literature, especially for the frequency division duplex (FDD) systems. Then, the ECC can be written as

\displaystyle C={\mathbbmss{E}}\left\{\log_{2}\det\left({{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}+\bar{\rho}{\textbf{G}}{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}}\right)\right\},

(6)

where
\bar{\rho}=\frac{\rho}{N_{\text{t}}}
with
\rho=\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}}
being the SNR and the expectation is taken with respect to
G
. Note that the phase shifts used at the RIS should be computed by either transmitter or receiver, because the RIS has no computation power. Also, it is challenging to estimate
R
or
T
, since the RIS cannot send or detect pilot signals. Recalling the previous assumption, it is clear that neither the transmitter nor receiver has access to the instantaneous CSI of
R
and
T
. As a result, if no more other information of
R
and
T
is available at the transceivers, random phase shifts are the only choices. In contrast to this, if the statistical CSI of
H
,
T
, and
R
is available at the transmitter or receiver, then the phase shifts may be designed more properly by utilizing this information. Yet, in both these two cases, we can treat
\theta_{i}
(
\forall i
) as some constants when analyzing the ECC. We now intend to evaluate the ECC of the considered channel.

C\geq N_{\text{r}}\log_{2}\left(1+\bar{\rho}\exp\left({N_{\text{r}}^{-1}}{\mathbbmss{E}}\left\{\ln\det\left({\textbf{G}}{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right)\right\}\right)\right).

(26)

It follows from
{\textbf{G}}=\bar{\textbf{G}}+{\textbf{X}}{\bm{\Psi}}^{1/2}
that
\ln\det\left({\textbf{G}}{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right)=\ln\det\left(\tilde{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}{\bm{\Psi}}\tilde{\textbf{G}}\right)
, which together with (
21
), yields

\ln\det\left({\textbf{G}}{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right)\geq\ln\det\left(\tilde{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\tilde{\textbf{G}}\right)+\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N_{\text{r}}}\ln{\zeta_{N_{\text{t}}-i+1}},

(27)

where
\zeta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\zeta_{N_{\text{t}}}>0
are the eigenvalues of
\bm{\Psi}
. Note that
{\text{vec}}\left(\tilde{\textbf{G}}\right)\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left({\text{vec}}\left({\bm{\Psi}}^{-1/2}\bar{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right),{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}\otimes{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{t}}}\right)
. By substituting (
27
) into (
26
) and calculating the resultant expectation based on (
15
), we arrive at the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

When
N_{\text{t}}>N_{\text{r}}
, the ECC of the RIS-aided MIMO channel is lower bounded by

\begin{split}&C\geq N_{\text{r}}\log_{2}\left(1+\bar{\rho}\exp\left({N_{\text{r}}^{-1}}\left(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N_{\text{r}}}\ln{\zeta_{N_{\text{t}}-i+1}}\right.\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.\left.+\sum\nolimits_{k=0}^{N_{\text{r}}-1}\psi\left(N_{\text{t}}-k\right)+{\mathsf{F}}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\alpha}_{\bar{L}}\right)\right)\right)\right)\triangleq C_{4},\end{split}

(28)

where
\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\bar{L}}
are the non-zero eigenvalues of
\bar{\textbf{G}}{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}\bar{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}
.

Remark 2
.

When the statistical CSI including
\bar{\textbf{G}}
and
\bm{\Psi}
is available at the transmitter or receiver, Theorems
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
can provide insightful guidelines for designing the reflecting matrix to improve the ECC of the considered system.

More specifically, since the ECC defined in (
6
) lacks any closed-form expressions, it makes sense to solve the problems given by
{\text{P}}_{i}:{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}=\arg\max\nolimits_{\bm{\Phi}}\left\{C_{i}\right\}
(
i\in\left\{1,2,3,4\right\}
) for the sake of finding a suboptimal
\bm{\Phi}
in terms of maximizing the ECC. Generally, problems (
{\text{P}}_{i}
) can be solved by using the classical genetic algorithm (GA)
[
17
]
. Yet, since this paper focuses more on the theoretical analyses of the ECC instead of the design of
\bm{\Phi}
, we do not provide more discussions.

IV
Asymptotic Analyses

We now intend to apply some asymptotic analyses to the previously derived bounds to unveil more system insights. Specifically, two limiting cases including
\rho\rightarrow\infty
and
M\rightarrow\infty
will be considered.

IV-A

Asymptotic Analyses in the High-SNR Regime

IV-A
1
N_{\text{r}}\geq N_{\text{t}}

It can be derived from (
10
) that

\displaystyle\lim\nolimits_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left(C-N_{\text{t}}\log_{2}\bar{\rho}\right)=\frac{1}{\ln{2}}{\mathbbmss{E}}\left\{\ln\det\left({\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}{\textbf{G}}\right)\right\}.

(29)

Interestingly, it can be found that (
29
) can be also derived from (
23
) by setting
\rho
as infinity, which suggests that this lower bound is exact in the high-SNR regime. The ECC in the high-SNR regime can be generally approximated as
[
18
]

C\approx{\mathcal{S}}_{\infty}\left(\log_{2}{\rho}-{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}\right),

(30)

where
{\mathcal{S}}_{\infty}=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C}{\log_{2}{\rho}}
denotes the high-SNR slope in bits/s/Hz/(3 dB), and
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left(\log_{2}{\rho}-\frac{C}{{\mathcal{S}}_{\infty}}\right)
denotes the high-SNR power offset in 3 dB units. Based on (
15
) and (
29
), we can evaluate
S_{\infty}
and
L_{\infty}
in as follows.

Theorem 5
.

When
N_{\text{r}}\geq N_{\text{t}}
, the high-SNR slope and high-SNR power offset of the RIS-aided MIMO channel are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\infty}=

\displaystyle N_{\text{t}},

(31)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\log_{2}{N_{\text{t}}}-\left({N_{\text{t}}\ln{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(\ln\det\left(\bm{\Psi}\right)+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum\nolimits_{k=0}^{N_{\text{t}}-1}\psi\left(N_{\text{r}}-k\right)+{\mathsf{F}}\left(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{\tilde{L}}\right)\right).

(32)

Remark 3
.

The results shown in (
31
) suggest that the high-SNR slope of the ECC curve over logarithmic SNR is
N_{\text{t}}
. In other words, when
N_{\text{r}}\geq N_{\text{t}}
, the ECC increases by approximately
N_{\text{t}}
bits/s/Hz for each 3 dB increase in SNR.

It is clear that
N_{\text{t}}
is independent of
\bm{\Phi}
while
\left\{\alpha_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\tilde{L}}
are functions of
\bm{\Phi}
, which leads to the following remark.

Remark 4
.

Equations (
31
) and (
32
) imply that the RIS’s deployment can shape the ECC curve over SNR by controlling its high-SNR power offset. Moreover, the RIS’s deployment cannot influence the high-SNR slope.

IV-A
2
N_{\text{t}}>N_{\text{r}}

By following the approach in obtaining (
31
) and (
32
), we find that when
N_{\text{t}}>N_{\text{r}}
, the high-SNR slope and high-SNR power offset can be written, respectively, as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=N_{\text{r}},

(33)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\log_{2}{N_{\text{t}}}-\frac{1}{N_{\text{r}}\ln{2}}{\mathbbmss{E}}\left\{\ln{\det{\left(\tilde{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}{\bm{\Psi}}\tilde{\textbf{G}}\right)}}\right\}.

(34)

Equation (
33
) implies that when
N_{\text{t}}>N_{\text{r}}
, the ECC increases by approximately
N_{\text{r}}
bits/s/Hz for each 3 dB increase in SNR. Moreover, by leveraging
Lemma

5
and
Lemma

6
to calculate the expectation in (
34
), we can get the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

When
N_{\text{t}}>N_{\text{r}}
, the high-SNR power offset of the RIS-aided MIMO channel is upper bounded by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}\leq\log_{2}{N_{\text{t}}}-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{r}}}\ln{\zeta_{N_{\text{t}}-i+1}}+{\mathsf{L}}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\alpha}_{\bar{L}}\right)}{N_{\text{r}}\ln{2}}

(35)

and lower bounded by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}\geq\log_{2}{N_{\text{t}}}-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{r}}}\ln{\zeta_{i}}+{\mathsf{L}}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\alpha}_{\bar{L}}\right)}{N_{\text{r}}\ln{2}},

(36)

where
{\mathsf{L}}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\alpha}_{\bar{L}}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\text{r}}-1}\psi\left(N_{\text{t}}-k\right)+{\mathsf{F}}\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\alpha}_{\bar{L}}\right)
.

IV-B

Asymptotic Analyses in the Limit of Many REs

We first rewrite (
6
) as

\displaystyle C={\mathbbmss{E}}\left\{\log_{2}\det\left({{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}+\bar{\rho}M\left(\frac{{\textbf{G}}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)\left(\frac{{\textbf{G}}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)^{{\dagger}}}\right)\right\}.

(37)

Since
{\text{vec}}\left({\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right)\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left({\text{vec}}\left(\bar{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}\right),{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}\otimes{\bm{\Psi}}\right)
, we can obtain
{\text{vec}}\left(\frac{{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left({\text{vec}}\left(\frac{\bar{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{M}}\right),{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}\otimes\frac{{\bm{\Psi}}}{M}\right)
. Based on
[
6
, Page 9]
,
\left|{\textbf{a}}_{M_{\text{h}},M_{\text{v}}}\left(\varphi_{{\text{d}}_{2,i}},\theta_{{\text{d}}_{2,i}}\right)^{\intercal}{\bm{\Phi}}{\textbf{a}}_{M_{\text{h}},M_{\text{v}}}\left(\varphi_{{\text{a}}_{1,j}},\theta_{{\text{a}}_{1,j}}\right)\right|
(
\forall i,j
) is a bounded value under random phase shifts, which together with (
2
), (
4
), and (
5
), yields
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\bar{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{M}}={{\textbf{0}}_{N_{\text{t}}\times N_{\text{r}}}}
. Besides,

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\bm{\Psi}}}{M}={\beta_{\text{t}}\beta_{\text{r}}N_{\textbf{R}}}\sum_{i=1}^{P_{\textbf{T}}}\frac{\left|t_{i}\right|^{2}}{P_{\textbf{T}}}{\textbf{a}}_{N_{\text{t}}}\left(\theta_{{\text{d}}_{1,i}}\right)^{\ast}{\textbf{a}}_{N_{\text{t}}}\left(\theta_{{\text{d}}_{1,i}}\right)^{\intercal}\triangleq{\bm{\Upsilon}}.

Thus,
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}{\text{vec}}\left(\frac{{\textbf{G}}^{{\dagger}}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left({\textbf{0}},{\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}\otimes{\bm{\Upsilon}}\right)
. It follows that as
M\rightarrow\infty
, the ECC satisfies
C-\dot{C}\left(\rho\right)\xrightarrow[M\rightarrow\infty]{a.s.}0
, where

\displaystyle\dot{C}\left(\rho\right)={\mathbbmss{E}}_{\textbf{X}}\left\{\log_{2}\det\left({\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}+M{\rho}/{N_{\text{t}}}{\textbf{X}}{\bm{\Upsilon}}{\textbf{X}}^{{\dagger}}\right)\right\}

(38)

with
{\textbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{\text{r}}\times N_{\text{t}}}
containing independent
{\mathcal{CN}}\left(0,1\right)
elements. We now intend to consider the case where the transmit power is scaled down by a factor of
1/M^{\alpha}
. From (
38
), we have
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\dot{C}\left({\rho}{M^{-\alpha}}\right)=\infty
for
\alpha<1
,
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\dot{C}\left({\rho}{M^{-\alpha}}\right)=0
for
\alpha>1
, and
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\dot{C}\left({\rho}/{M}\right)={\mathbbmss{E}}_{\textbf{X}}\left\{\log_{2}\det\left({\textbf{I}}_{N_{\text{r}}}+\rho/N_{\text{t}}{\textbf{X}}{\bm{\Upsilon}}{\textbf{X}}^{{\dagger}}\right)\right\}
.

Remark 5
.

It is clear that
\dot{C}\left({\rho}{M^{-\alpha}}\right)
is a non-zero constant if and only if
\alpha=1
, which suggests that in the large limit of
M
, with no degradation in the ECC, the total SNR or transmit power can be cut down at most
{1}/{M}
.

It is challenging to derive a close-form expression for
\tilde{C}=\dot{C}\left(\rho/M\right)
. Fortunately, by following similar steps outlined in Section
III-B
, a closed-form upper bound of
\tilde{C}
is available. For the sake of brevity, we omit the details here.

VI
Conclusion

Novel expressions were derived to characterize the upper and lower bounds of the ECC of RIS-aided MIMO channels with deterministic LoS paths. To unveil more system insights, asymptotic analyses were performed to the system ECC in the large limit of the SNR and number of REs. It was found that the RIS can shape the ECC curve via influencing its high-SNR power offset and the ECC increases with the number of REs.

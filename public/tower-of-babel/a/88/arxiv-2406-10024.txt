On Fridman invariant, injectivity radius function and squeezing function
Akhil Kumar, Sanjay Kumar Pant

Abstract
. We give a class of domains for which Fridman invariant and injectivity radius function coincide with respect to Carathéodory metric. We give explicit expressions of the squeezing functions for these domains and investigate some of their properties.

32F45, 32H02

1.
Introduction

In
[
19
]
, Ng, Tang and Tsai raised a question: “For which
c
-hyperbolic complex manifold
X
we have
H_{X}^{c}(z)\leq i_{X}^{c}(z)
for all
z\in X
?” In one complex dimension, they have already shown that
H_{X}^{k}(z)=i_{X}^{k}(z)
for all
z\in X
, where
X
is any 1-dimensional
k
-hyperbolic complex manifold. In the context of this question, we state our main result

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{D}\setminus K
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{D}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:|\xi|<1\}
and
K
is a removable set in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, we have

H_{\Omega}^{c}(z)=i_{\Omega}^{c}(z)=\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}\ \mbox{for all}\ z\in\Omega.

Here,
H_{\Omega}^{c}(z)
and
i_{\Omega}^{c}(z)
refer to the Fridman invariant and the injectivity radius function with respect to the Carathéodory metric.

In
[
6
,
7
]
, Fridman introduced a holomorphic invariant known as the Fridman invariant. Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional
d
-hyperbolic complex manifold. For
z\in X
, the Fridman invariant denoted by
h_{X}^{d}
, is defined as

h_{X}^{d}(z)=\inf\left\{\dfrac{1}{r}:B_{X}^{d}(z,r)\subseteq f(\mathbb{B}^{n}),f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to X\right\},

where
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to X
is an injective holomorphic map and
B_{X}^{d}(z,r)
is a ball centred at
z
with radius
r
with respect to
d
-metric. The Fridman invariant is very useful in decoding some geometrical properties of the manifold. For example, Fridman
[
7
]
has shown that if Fridman invariant with respect to Kobayashi metric
h_{X}^{k}(z)=0
for some
z\in X
, where
X
is any connected
k
-hyperbolic complex manifold, then
X
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. For comparison purposes, Nikolov and Verma
[
20
]
considered a modification in
h_{X}^{d}
, which is denoted by
H_{X}^{d}
and defined as

H_{X}^{d}(z)=\sup\{\tanh r:B_{X}^{d}(z,r)\subseteq f(\mathbb{B}^{n}),f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to X\},

where
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to X
is an injective holomorphic map.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional
d
-hyperbolic complex manifold. For
z\in X
, the injectivity radius function with respect to
d
-metric denoted by
i_{X}^{d}
, is defined as

i_{X}^{d}(z)=\sup\{\tanh r:B_{X}^{d}(z,r)\ \mbox{is simply connected}\}.

For Carathéodory and Kobayashi metrics, we denote injectivity radius functions by
i_{X}^{c}
and
i_{X}^{k}
respectively. The injectivity radius with respect to
d
-metric is defined as

i_{X}^{d}=\inf_{z\in X}i_{X}^{d}(z).

A Riemann Surface
X
is said to be uniformly thick if the injectivity radius function
i_{X}^{k}(z)
on it admits a positive lower bound. In
[
19
]
, Ng, Tang and Tsai extended the definition of uniformly thickness to higher dimension. An
n
-dimensional
d
-hyperbolic complex manifold is said to be
d
-uniformly thick if Fridman invariant
H_{X}^{d}(z)
on it, admits a positive lower bound. For example, all simply connected bounded domains in
\mathbb{C}
are both
c
-uniformly thick and
k
-uniformly thick. For other important properties of injectivity radius function, one can refer to the following papers:
[
11
,
12
,
25
]
.

Let us recall the definition of a removable set. A compact set
K
in
\mathbb{C}
is said to be removable for bounded holomorphic functions, or simply removable, if for every open superset
U
of
K
in
\mathbb{C}
, each bounded and holomorphic function on
U\setminus K
extends holomorphically on the whole of
U
. One can see that a removable set
K
in
\mathbb{C}
is totally disconnected.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
implies that for the class of domains
\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}\setminus K
, where
\Omega^{\prime}
is any simply connected domain other than
\mathbb{C}
and
K
is any removable set in
\Omega^{\prime}
, the Fridman invariant and the injectivity radius function coincide with respect to the Carathéodory metric.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.1
, we take the domain obtained by deleting a removable set from the unit disc. It is natural to ask that what happens to the Fridman invariant and the injectivity radius function if we replace the unit disc by an arbitrary domain in Theorem
1.1
? In Theorem
1.4
, we show that one side of the inequality holds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
and
K
be a removable set in
\Omega
. Then for
D=\Omega\setminus K
, we have

i_{D}^{c}(z)\leq H_{D}^{c}(z)\leq\min_{w\in K}\tanh[c_{\Omega}(z,w)].

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.4
implies that the domain
D=\Omega\setminus K
, where
\Omega
is any domain in
\mathbb{C}
and
K
is any removable set in
\Omega
, is not
c
-uniformly thick. Theorem
1.6
is about a domain where the above inequality does not hold in general.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
D=A_{r}\setminus\{p\}
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
, where
A_{r}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:r<|\xi|<1,r>0\}
and
p\in(-1,-\sqrt{r})
. Then for
z\in(r,1)

i_{D}^{c}(z)\neq\tanh[c_{A_{r}}(z,p)]\ \mbox{and}\ H_{D}^{c}(z)\neq\tanh[c_{A_{r}}(z,p)].

Our next result gives the explicit expression of the squeezing function for the domain given in the Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{D}\setminus K
be an open connected set. Then the squeezing function on
\Omega
is given by

S_{\Omega}(z)=\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

The
S_{\Omega}(z)
refer squeezing function which is defined as follows. In 2012, Deng, Guan and Zhang in their paper
[
2
]
, developed the idea of squeezing function by following the work of Liu, Sun and Yau
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
and Yeung
[
26
]
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain. For
z\in{\Omega}
and a injective holomorphic map
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
f(z)=0
, define

S_{\Omega}(z,f)=\sup\{r:\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)\subseteq f(\Omega)\},

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)
denotes the ball entered at origin with radius
r
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The squeezing function on
\Omega
, denoted by
S_{\Omega}
, is defined as

S_{\Omega}(z)=\sup_{f}\{S_{\Omega}(z,f)\},

where supremum is taken over all injective holomorphic maps
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
f(z)=0.

It is clear that the Fridman invariant, the injectivity radius function and the squeezing function are biholomorphic invariants. It is interesting to note that any domain in class
\Omega^{\prime}\setminus K
, where
\Omega^{\prime}
is any simply connected domain other than
\mathbb{C}
and
K
is any removable set in
\Omega^{\prime}
, is not a holomorphic homogeneous regular domain. A domain is said to be a holomorphic homogeneous regular (HHR) domain if the squeezing function on it admits a positive lower bound. For other important properties of squeezing functions, see, for example:
[
4
,
13
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
and references therein.

Remark 1.8
.

Now the same question arises for the squeezing function: what about the squeezing function if we replace an arbitrary domain instead of the unit disc in Theorem
1.7
? In Theorem
1.9
, we see that the one-sided inequality still holds.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}
and
K
be a removable set in
\Omega
. Then for
D=\Omega\setminus K
, we have

S_{D}(z)\leq\min_{w\in K}\tanh[c_{\Omega}(z,w)].

Remark 1.10
.

Theorem
1.9
implies that the domain
D=\Omega\setminus K
, where
\Omega
is any bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}
and
K
is any removable set in
\Omega
, is not a HHR domain. In Theorem
1.11
, we give an example of domain to illustrate that the above inequality does not hold in general.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
D=A_{r}\setminus\{-\sqrt{r}\}
be a domain, where
A_{r}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:r<|\xi|<1,r>0\}
. Then for
z\in(r,\sqrt{r})

S_{D}(z)\neq\tanh[c_{A_{r}}(z,-\sqrt{r})].

2.
Some notations and Observations

The Carathéodory pseudo distance between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
on a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, denoted by
c_{\Omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
, is defined as

c_{\Omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\displaystyle\sup_{f}\{\tanh^{-1}|\alpha|:f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}\ \text{holomorphic},f(\xi_{1})=0,f(\xi_{2})=\alpha\}.

Let
d_{\rho}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
denote the Poincaré distance between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mu:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a real-valued function defined as

\mu(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\log\dfrac{1+x}{1-x}
. It is clear that
\mu
is a strictly increasing function, and for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
,
\mu(|\xi|)
is the Poincaré distance between 0 and
\xi
. One can observe that the inverse of this function can be expressed as
\tanh(x)
.

In
[
24
]
, Simha given the explicit formula for Carathéodory distance for the annulus
D=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:1/R<|\xi|<R\}
. Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in D
, then

(2.1)

c_{D}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\tanh^{-1}c^{\prime}_{D}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

where

(2.2)

c^{\prime}_{D}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\dfrac{1}{R|\xi_{2}|}|f(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\cdot|f(|\xi_{1}|^{-1},-|\xi_{2}|)|,

and for
\xi_{1}>0
,

f(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\left(1-\dfrac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\right)\dfrac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\dfrac{\xi_{2}}{R^{4n}\xi_{1}}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{\xi_{1}}{R^{4n}\xi_{2}}\right)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\dfrac{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{R^{4n-2}}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{1}{R^{4n-2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}\right)}.

After some computation, the expression can be reformulated in terms of the Schottky-Klein prime function
\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for annulus. Let
\xi_{1}>0,\xi_{2}\in A_{r}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:r<|\xi|<1,r>0\}
, then the Carathéodory distance between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
on
A_{r}
is given by

(2.3)

c_{A_{r}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\tanh^{-1}c^{\prime}_{A_{r}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

where

(2.4)

c^{\prime}_{A_{r}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\xi_{1}}\left|\dfrac{\omega(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}\omega(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}^{-1})}\right|\left|\dfrac{\omega\left(\xi_{1},-\dfrac{r}{|\xi_{2}|}\right)}{\dfrac{r}{|\xi_{2}|}\omega\left(\xi_{1},-\dfrac{|\xi_{2}|}{r}\right)}\right|,

and
\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is expressed as

\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\xi_{1}-r^{2n}\xi_{2})(\xi_{2}-r^{2n}\xi_{1})}{(\xi_{1}-r^{2n}\xi_{1})(\xi_{2}-r^{2n}\xi_{2})}\ \mbox{for}\ \xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

The Schottky-Klein prime function satisfies the following identities see
[
1
, Section 5.3]
.

(2.5)

\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=-\omega(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})

(2.6)

\omega(\overline{\xi_{1}},\overline{\xi_{2}})=\overline{\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}

(2.7)

\omega(1/\xi_{1},1/\xi_{2})=-\dfrac{\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}

(2.8)

\omega(r^{2}\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=-\dfrac{\xi_{2}\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}{\xi_{1}}

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
z>0\in A_{r}
. Then we have
c_{A_{r}}(z,\overline{w})=c_{A_{r}}(z,w)\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in A_{r}.

Proof.

Since by (
2.4
)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{A_{r}}(z,\overline{w})=\dfrac{1}{z}\left|\dfrac{\omega(\overline{w},z)}{z\omega(\overline{w},z^{-1})}\right|\left|\dfrac{\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{r}{|\overline{w}|}\right)}{\dfrac{r}{|\overline{w}|}\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{|\overline{w}|}{r}\right)}\right|=\dfrac{1}{z}\left|\dfrac{\omega(\overline{w},\overline{z})}{z\omega(\overline{w},\overline{z}^{-1})}\right|\left|\dfrac{\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{r}{|w|}\right)}{\dfrac{r}{|w|}\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{|w|}{r}\right)}\right|.

By (
2.6
)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{A_{r}}(z,\overline{w})=\dfrac{1}{z}\left|\dfrac{\overline{\omega(w,z)}}{z\overline{\omega(w,z^{-1})}}\right|\left|\dfrac{\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{r}{|w|}\right)}{\dfrac{r}{|w|}\omega\left(z,-\dfrac{|w|}{r}\right)}\right|=c^{\prime}_{A_{r}}(z,w)

It follows that
c_{A_{r}}(z,\overline{w})=c_{A_{r}}(z,w)\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in A_{r}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

[
19
, Lemma 1]

Let
r<z<1
and
-1<w<-r
. Then we have the following.

(1)

c_{A_{r}}(z,w)=c_{A_{r}}\left(z,\dfrac{r}{w}\right)
.

(2)

Fix
z
and let
c_{A_{r}}(z,w)
be a function of
w
, where
w
varies between
(-1,-r)
. Then the minimum value of
c_{A_{r}}(z,w)
is attained at
w=-\sqrt{r}
i.e.

c_{A_{r}}(z,-\sqrt{r})<c_{A_{r}}(z,w)\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in(-1,-r)\setminus\{-\sqrt{r}\}.

3.
Proof of theorems

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us assume
p=\displaystyle\min_{w\in K}c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)
. First we prove that the ball
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)=\{\xi\in\Omega:c_{\Omega}(z,\xi)<p\}
is simply connected. Since
K
is removable set, it is easy to see that
c_{\Omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=c_{\mathbb{D}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\Omega
. This implies
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)=\{\xi\in\Omega:c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi)<p\}
. We claim that
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)=B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,p)
. It is obvious that
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)\subseteq B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,p)
. For other side containment, let us assume there is some
\xi^{\prime}\in B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,p)
with
\xi^{\prime}\notin B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)
. This implies
\xi^{\prime}\in K
such that
c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi^{\prime})<p
. This contradict the fact that
p=\displaystyle\min_{w\in K}c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)
. Therefore,
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)=B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,r)
which is just a Poincaré ball in the unit disc. Hence
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,p)
is simply connected. Thus

(3.1)

i_{\Omega}^{c}(z)\geq\tanh p=\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

For
z\in\Omega
, let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be any injective holomorphic function with
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r)\subseteq f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\Omega
. This implies

(3.2)

B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r)=\{\xi\in\Omega:c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi)<r\}\subseteq f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\Omega.

Since
f(\mathbb{D})
is simply connected domain in
\Omega
, we have
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r)=B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,r)
. By (
3.2
)

(3.3)

B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,r)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{D}:c_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi)<r\}\subseteq f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\Omega.

Consider a conformal map
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
such that

g(\xi)=\dfrac{\xi-z}{1-\overline{z}\xi}.

Note that
g
is an isometry, it maps
B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(z,r)
onto the ball with center zero and radius
r
in the unit disc. Therefore, by (
3.3
)

B_{\mathbb{D}}^{c}(0,r)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{D}:c_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\xi)<r\}\subseteq g(\Omega).

Thus, we get

(3.4)

H_{\Omega}^{c}(z)\leq\tanh r\leq\displaystyle\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

Hence by
[
19
, Theorem 1(1)]
, (
3.1
) and (
3.4
)

\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}\leq i_{\Omega}^{c}(z)\leq H_{\Omega}^{c}(z)\leq\displaystyle\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For
z\in D
, let
H_{D}^{c}(z)=c
. Then by definition there exist a sequence of positive numbers
\{r_{n}\}
converging to
\tanh^{-1}c
and a sequence of injective holomorphic functions
f_{n}:\mathbb{D}\to D
such that
B_{D}^{c}(z,r_{n})\subseteq f_{n}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq D
for all
n
. It is easy to see that
c_{D}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=c_{\Omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in D
. This implies

(3.5)

B_{D}^{c}(z,r_{n})=\{\xi\in D:c_{\Omega}(z,\xi)<r_{n}\}\subseteq f_{n}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq D\ \mbox{for all}\ n.

Since
f_{n}(\mathbb{D})
is simply connected domain in
D
, we have
B_{D}^{c}(z,r_{n})=B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r_{n})
for all
n
. By (
3.5
)

(3.6)

B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r_{n})=\{\xi\in\Omega:c_{\Omega}(z,\xi)<r_{n}\}\subseteq f_{n}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq D\ \mbox{for all}\ n.

If
c>\displaystyle\min_{w\in K}\tanh[c_{\Omega}(z,w)]
, then there is some
w^{\prime}\in K
such that
w^{\prime}\in B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r_{n})
for some large
n
. This is not possible because
B_{\Omega}^{c}(z,r_{n})\subseteq D
for all
n
. Thus

(3.7)

H_{\Omega}^{c}(z)\leq\min_{w\in K}\tanh[c_{\Omega}(z,w)].

Hence the result follows from
[
19
, Theorem 1(1)]
and (
3.7
).
∎

1.6
.

For
z\in(r,1)
, let us assume
i_{D}^{c}(z)=\tanh[c_{A_{r}}(z,p)]
. Then by definition, there exists a sequence of positive numbers
\{r_{n}\}
converging to
c_{A_{r}}(z,p)

. By Lemma

,
c_{A_{r}}(z,-\sqrt{r})<c_{A_{r}}(z,\xi)

- axis. This implies there exist two paths
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

z
and
-\sqrt{r}

z\in(r,1)
, let us assume

. Then by definition, there exist a sequence of positive numbers

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

For
z\in\Omega
, by using the automorphism of the unit disc, it is easy to see that

(3.8)

S_{\Omega}(z)\geq\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

By
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, there is an injective holomorphic function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
with
f(z)=0
such that
\mathbb{D}(0,S_{\Omega}(z))\subseteq f(\Omega)
. Since
K
is removable set, there exists a holomorphic function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\tilde{f}(\xi)=f(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\Omega
. Note that
K
have no interior. This implies
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and by the maximum modulus principle
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
. It is easy to see that
\tilde{f}(w)\notin f(\Omega)
for all
w\in K
. This implies

(3.9)

S_{\Omega}(z)\leq|\tilde{f}(w)|\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in K.

By distance decreasing property of Poincaré metric

\displaystyle d_{\rho}(0,\tilde{f}(w))=d_{\rho}(\tilde{f}(z),\tilde{f}(w))\leq d_{\rho}(z,w)=d_{\rho}\left(0,\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mu(|\tilde{f}(w)|)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right).

Thus, we get

(3.10)

|\tilde{f}(w)|\leq\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in K.

By (
3.9
) and (
3.10
),

(3.11)

S_{\Omega}(z)\leq\min_{w\in K}\left\{\left|\dfrac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|\right\}.

Hence the result follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.11
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

For
z\in\Omega
, by
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

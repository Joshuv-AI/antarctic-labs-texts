An updated review on crosscorrelation of m-sequences This is an invited chapter to Series on Coding Theory and Cryptology, World Scientific
Tor Helleseth, Chunlei Li

Abstract
Maximum-length sequences (m-sequences for short) over finite fields are generated by linear feedback shift registers with primitive characteristic polynomials. These sequences have nice mathematical structures and good randomness properties that are favorable in practical applications. During the past five decades, the crosscorrelation between m-sequences of the same period has been intensively studied, and a particular research focus has been on investigating the crosscorrelation spectra with few possibles values. In this chapter we summarize all known results on this topic in the literature and promote several open problems for future research.

Crosscorrelation of m-sequences

Assume
p

\mathbb{F}_{p}

p

Given an initial state
(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1})

\{s_{t}\}

c(x)=x^{n}+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}x+c_{0}.

For a linear recursion of degree
n

\mathbb{F}_{p}

p^{n}-1
since
they are determined by the initial states and there are in total
p^{n}-1
distinct
n

\mathbb{F}_{p}

Suppose

\{s_{t}\}

\mathbb{F}_{p}

Let

With the one-to-one correspondence between the absolute trace functions and the linear functions from

All nontrivial cyclic shifts of the m-sequence

\{s_{t+\tau}\}\text{ with }s_{t+\tau}=\Tr(\alpha^{\tau}\alpha^{t}),\quad 0<\tau\leq p^{n}-2.

These shifts are also m-sequences, and they are called the shift equivalence of

Golomb in

Lemma 1

Definition 1

(Crosscorrelation between m-sequences)
.

Let

C_{d}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{p^{n}-2}\omega^{s_{t+\tau}-s_{dt}}.

(2)

\{C_{d}(\tau)\,|\,\tau=0,1,\dots,p^{n}-2\}.

If the spectrum of

Definition 2

(Walsh Transform)
.

Let
f
be a function from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

W_{f}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\mapsto\mathbb{C}

W_{f}(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}{\omega^{f(x)-\Tr(ax)}}.

(4)

The Walsh spectrum of

is the set of values
\{W_{f}(a)\,|\,a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}.

The notion of Walsh transform can be also defined for a function from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
to itself as follows.

Definition 3
.

Let
F

\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

W_{F}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{2}\mapsto\mathbb{C}

The Walsh spectrum of

\{W_{F}(b,a)\,|\,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*},a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\}.

From the above definitions, when

C_{d}(\tau)=-1+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(az-z^{d})}=-1+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(x^{d}-ax)}=-1+W_{f}(a),

The crosscorrelation function
C_{d}(\tau)
also has a close connection to the weight distribution of cyclic codes. We briefly recall some basics of linear codes and cyclic codes for completeness.
A linear
[\texttt{n},\texttt{k}]

\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a
k

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\texttt{n}}
.
Let

denote the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}
with Hamming weight
i

The weight distribution of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
(A_{0},A_{1},\cdots,A_{\texttt{n}})
. A linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is called

if any cyclic shift of a codeword
is another codeword of
\mathcal{C}
.
It is well known that any cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
corresponds to an ideal of the polynomial residue class ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{\texttt{n}}-1)
and can be expressed as
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
, where
g(x)

is monic and has the least degree. This polynomial is called the
generator polynomial
and
h(x)=(x^{\texttt{n}}-1)/g(x)
is referred to as the

of
\mathcal{C}
.

Consider the
p

\mathcal{C}
of length

with parity-check polynomial

, where
m_{i}(x)
is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha^{i}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The Hamming weight of a nonzero codeword

can be expressed as

\begin{split}wt(\mathbf{c}_{a,b})&=p^{n}-1-\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}\,|\,\Tr(ax+bx^{d})=0\}\\
&=p^{n}-1-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\omega^{y\Tr(ax+bx^{d})}\\
&=p^{n}-1-\frac{1}{p}\left(p^{n}-p+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{y\Tr(ax+bx^{d})}\right)\\
&=p^{n-1}(p-1)-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(yax+ybx^{d})}\\
&=p^{n-1}(p-1)-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(x^{d}+\frac{ya}{\sqrt[d]{yb}}x)}\\

(6)

Three-valued crosscorrelation

A decimation integer

When

Moreover, with the identities (4)-(6) in ?THM?

Four-valued crosscorrelation

One of the main contributions leading to decimations with four-valued crosscorrelation is due to Niho. In his thesis
[
19

Niho studied a class of decimations of the form

for m-sequences with period
2^{2m}-1

This type of decimations were later generalized to the odd prime
p
case, namely, the decimation is defined as
d=s(p^{m}-1)+1
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2m}}
.
Note that the power functions

with such decimations are linear over the subfield
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}

The decimation of the form

was later referred to as

in the construction of low-correlated sequences, highly nonlinear functions and permutations.
In the context of crosscorrelation, the main idea of using Niho exponent

is to reduce the problem to computing the number of solutions of certain equations in a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
More concretely, the transformation and its proof are recalled below
[
29
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n=2m
and
d=s(p^{m}-1)+1
with a nonnegative integer
s
.
Let
C_{d}({\tau})
be the crosscorrelation of the m-sequence
\{s_{t}\}
and its
d
-decimation
\{s_{dt}\}
, where
s_{t}=\Tr(\alpha^{t})
and

\alpha
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
Then for each

W_{d}(a)=C_{d}(\tau)+1=(N(a)-1)p^{m},

where

is the number of distinct solutions to the equation

x^{2s-1}-ax^{s}-\bar{a}x^{s-1}+1=0

(10)

in the unit circle

, where
\bar{x}=x^{p^{m}}
for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
q=p^{m}
and denote

.
Then each element of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
is uniquely represented as
hy
for some

and
y\in Y
. Note that
h^{d}=h
for every
h\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
. Thus we can write the crosscorrelation as

\displaystyle W_{d}(a)

\displaystyle=1+C_{d}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(x^{d}-ax)}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{h\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}}\sum_{y\in Y}\omega^{\Tr(h(y^{d}-ay))}

\displaystyle=-p^{m}+\sum_{h\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}}\sum_{y\in Y}\omega^{\Tr^{m}_{1}(h\Tr_{m}^{n}(y^{d}-ay))}

\displaystyle=-p^{m}+\sum_{y\in Y}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}}\omega^{\Tr^{m}_{1}(h(y^{d}+\bar{y}^{d}-ay-\bar{a}\bar{y}))}

\displaystyle=p^{m}(Z(a)-1),

where

is the number of distinct solutions of
y^{d}+\bar{y}^{d}-ay-\bar{a}\bar{y}=0
in
Y

Note that

for any

counts the number of

which (by dividing by

y\in Y

y^{-(2s-1)(q-1)}-ay^{-s(q-1)}-\bar{a}y^{-(s-1)(q-1)}+1=0.

Taking

is the same as the number of distinct

satisfying the equation

x^{2s-1}-ax^{s}-\bar{a}x^{s-1}+1=0.

That is to say,
Z(a)
is equal to

We now move on to the decimations with six-valued crosscorrelation in nonbinary cases. As a matter of fact, the following decimation is a union of both binary case and nonbinary cases.

Theorem 18
.

[
17
, Theorem 4.11]

Let
p
be a prime satisfying
p\equiv 2\mod{3}
and
n=2m
. Let
d=\frac{1}{3}(p^{n}-1)+p^{i}
, where
0\leq i<n
and
f=\frac{p^{n}-1}{3}p^{i}\not\equiv 2\mod{3}
. Then
C_{d}(\tau)
takes on the following values

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{9}(4p^{n}+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}-29)\text{ times},\\
-1+(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{9}(2p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m}-4)\text{ times},\\
-1+(-1)^{m}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{27}(8p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m}-10)\text{ times},\\
-1+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{27}(p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}+1)\text{ times},\\
-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m})&\quad\text{occurs}&1\text{ time},\\
-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{2m}+(-1)^{m+1}p^{m})&\quad\text{occurs}&2\text{ times}\end{array}

when
f\equiv 0\mod{3}
; and takes on the following values

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{9}(4p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}-20)\text{ times},\\
-1+(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{9}(2p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m}-4)\text{ times},\\
-1+(-1)^{m}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{27}(8p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m}-28)\text{ times},\\
-1+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}&\quad\text{occurs}&\frac{1}{27}(p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}-8)\text{ times},\\
-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{2m}+2(-1)^{m}p^{m})&\quad\text{occurs}&2\text{ times},\\
-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{2m}+4(-1)^{m+1}p^{m})&\quad\text{occurs}&1\text{ time}\end{array}

when
f\equiv 1\mod{3}
.

The proof of the above theorem used a similar technique as in ?THM?
. Choose
N=3
, since
(d-p^{i})N\equiv 0\mod{p^{n}-1}
, it follows that

C_{d}(\tau)+1=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr((\beta^{j}x^{3})^{d}-\alpha^{t}(\beta^{j}x^{3}))}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(x^{3}(\beta^{jd}-\alpha^{t}\beta^{j}))},

where
\alpha
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
\beta=\alpha^{(p^{n}-1)/3}
. Denote
A=(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}
. It is easily checked that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{\Tr(ax^{3})}=\begin{cases}p^{n}&\text{ when }a=0,\\
A&\text{ when }a\in C_{0},\\
-\frac{1}{2}A&\text{ when }a\not\in C_{0},\end{cases}

where
C_{0}
is the set of cubes in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
. More detailed analysis of the triple
(1-\alpha^{t},\beta^{d}-\alpha^{t}\beta,\beta^{2d}-\alpha^{t}\beta^{2})
indicates that

C_{d}(\tau)
takes on the following values for
f\equiv 0\mod{3}
:

-1,-1+\frac{1}{2}A,-1-\frac{1}{2}A,-1+A,-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{n}-A),-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{n}+\frac{1}{2}A)

and takes on the following values for
f\equiv 1\mod{3}
:

-1,-1+\frac{1}{2}A,-1-\frac{1}{2}A,-1+A,-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{n}-A),-1+\frac{1}{3}(p^{n}+2A).

The calculation of the distribution of
C_{d}(\tau)
relies on the investigation of the first three power moments.

The following decimation is the
p
-ary variant of ?THM?
. It is
also similar to the three-valued
decimation of the form
d=p^{2k}-p^{k}+1
under the condition that
n/\gcd(n,k)
is odd. As this condition is not satisfied,
the crosscorrelation has six possible values.

Theorem 19
.

[
44
, Theorem 1]

Let
n=4m
,
p^{m}\not\equiv 2\mod{3}
and let
d=p^{2m}-p^{m}+1
. Then
C_{d}(\tau)
takes on the following values

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1-2p^{2m}&\quad\text{occurs}&(p^{4m}-3p^{3m}+3p^{2m}-p^{m})/6\text{ times},\\
-1-p^{2m}&\quad\text{occurs}&p^{3m}-p^{2m}\text{ times},\\
-1&\quad\text{occurs}&(p^{4m}-p^{3m}+p^{2m}-p^{m}-4)/2\text{ times},\\
-1+p^{2m}&\quad\text{occurs}&(p^{4m}-p^{m})/3\text{ times},\\
-1+p^{3m}-p^{2m}&\quad\text{occurs}&p^{m}\text{ times},\\
-1+p^{3m}&\quad\text{occurs}&1\text{ time}.\end{array}

The proof adopted a similar technique as in ?THM?
. Take
N=p^{m}+1
. Firstly, the exponential sum

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{4m}}}\omega^{\Tr(ax^{N})}=\begin{cases}p^{4m}&\text{ when }a\in C_{\infty},\\
-p^{3m}&\text{ when }a\in C_{0},\\
p^{2m}&\text{ when }a\in C_{1},\end{cases}

where
C_{\infty}=\{0\}
,
C_{0}=\{x^{N}\,|\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{4m}}^{*}\}
and
C_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{4m}}^{*}\setminus C_{0}.

Denote
d_{1}=dp^{m}
. Since
(d_{1}-1)N\equiv 0\mod{p^{4m}-1}
, the crosscorrelation
C_{d}(\tau)
can be expressed as

\begin{split}C_{d}(\tau)&=-1+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{4m}}}\omega^{\Tr(x^{p^{m}+1}(\alpha^{dj}-\alpha^{\tau}\alpha^{j}))}\\
&=-1+\frac{1}{N}\left(p^{4m}n_{\infty}(c)-p^{3m}n_{0}(c)+p^{2m}n_{1}(c)\right),\end{split}

where
n_{i}(c)
for
i=0,1,\infty
and
c=\alpha^{\tau}
is defined by

n_{i}(c)=\#\{j\,|\,\alpha^{jd_{1}}-\alpha^{\tau+j}\in C_{i},\,0\leq j<N\}.

As in the proof of ?THM?
, the remaining proof relies on the analysis of possible values of
(n_{\infty}(c),n_{0}(c),n_{1}(c))
and
the number of solutions of the equations

\begin{split}x_{1}+x_{2}+1&=0,\\
x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d}+1&=0,\end{split}

which is shown to be
p^{m}
. The first three power moments yield the complete distribution of
C_{d}(\tau)
.

7
Conclusion

The crosscorrelation of m-sequences is a fascinating and challenging research topic of both theoretical and applied interest.
Recent years have witnessed some advances on the topic, meanwhile, many problems still remain open. This chapter provides an updated overview of the topic with the focus on
reviewing known and new techniques in the literature.

The Convexity Condition of Density-Functional Theory Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Andrew C. Burgess, Edward Linscott, David D. O’Regan

Abstract
It has long been postulated that within density-functional theory (DFT) the total energy of a finite electronic system is convex with respect to electron count, so that 2 ​ E v ​ [ N 0 ] ≤ E v ​ [ N 0 − 1 ] + E v ​ [ N 0 + 1 ] 2E_{v}[N_{0}]\leq E_{v}[N_{0}-1]+E_{v}[N_{0}+1] . Using the infinite-separation-limit technique, this article proves the convexity condition for any formulation of DFT that is (1) exact for all v − v- representable densities, (2) size-consistent, and (3) translationally invariant. An analogous result is also proven for one-body reduced density matrix functional theory. While there are known DFT formulations in which the ground state is not always accessible, indicating that convexity does not hold in such cases, this proof nonetheless confirms a stringent constraint on the exact exchange-correlation functional. We also provide sufficient conditions for convexity in approximate DFT, which could aid in the development of density-functional approximations. This result lifts a standing assumption in the proof of the piecewise linearity condition with respect to electron count, which has proven central to understanding the Kohn-Sham band-gap and the exchange-correlation derivative discontinuity of DFT.

Illustrative Example
We claim that all formulations of DFT that satisfy conditions (1) to (3) must obey the convexity condition. In order to show this, suppose that there exists some
N_{0}
-electron finite system with external potential
v({\bf r})
, which breaks the convexity condition. Then we have

2E_{v}[N_{0}]>E_{v}[N_{0}-1]+E_{v}[N_{0}+1].

(9)

In order to proceed, we employ the infinite-separation-limit technique
Yang, Zhang, and Ayers 2000
;
Ayers 2008
and consider the external potential
v^{\prime}({\bf r})
, which is composed of two copies of the same external potential
v({\bf r})
, infinitely separated in space, such that

v^{\prime}({\bf r})=\sum_{l=1}^{2}v_{{\bf R}_{l}}({\bf r}).

(10)

In this illustrative example (only), we assume that the
2N_{0}
-electron ground-state wavefunction
\Psi_{1}
of the system, with external potential
v^{\prime}({\bf r})
, is the anti-symmetric product of
\Phi_{N_{0}-1}({\bf{R}}_{1})
and
\Phi_{N_{0}+1}({\bf{R}}_{2})
, where
\Phi_{N_{0}-1}({\bf{R}}_{1})
and
\Phi_{N_{0}+1}({\bf{R}}_{2})
are the ground-state wavefunctions of the
N_{0}-1
- and
N_{0}+1
-electron systems in the external potential
v({\bf{r}})
. Specifically, we write

\Psi_{1}=\hat{A}\bigg(\Phi_{N_{0}-1}({\bf{R}}_{1})\Phi_{N_{0}+1}({\bf{R}}_{2})\bigg).

(11)

Eq.
11
is an assumption as one cannot rule out the possibility that the true ground-state wavefunction is for example, the anti-symmetric product of
\Phi_{N_{0}-2}({\bf{R}}_{1})
and
\Phi_{N_{0}+2}({\bf{R}}_{2})
. In the complete proof, this assumption is not invoked because the site electron counts of the wavefunctions
\Phi({\bf{R}}_{l})
are not specified. We will first show that the convexity condition cannot be violated in this simple case, after which a full proof of the convexity condition will be given.

One may construct the wavefunction
\Psi_{2}
by reversing the position vectors
{\bf{R}_{1}}
and
{\bf{R}_{2}}
,

\Psi_{2}=\hat{A}\bigg(\Phi_{N_{0}-1}({\bf{R}}_{2})\Phi_{N_{0}+1}({\bf{R}}_{1})\bigg).

(12)

The averaged wavefunction
defined by

\Psi_{\rm avg}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}]

(13)

will be degenerate with
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
and its corresponding density will be given by

\rho({\bf r})=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}+1),

(14)

where
\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}\pm 1)
is the ground-state electron density of site
l
with an electron count
N_{0}\pm 1
. To deduce an expression for the total energy of the system with electron density
\rho({\bf r})
, we make three assumptions about the nature of the total energy functional that were previously discussed, namely that it is (1) exact for all
v-
representable densities, (2) size-consistent, and (3) translationally
invariant. For the purposes of the proof, these three assumptions are treated as axioms.

Given that
\Psi_{1}
of Eq.
11
is a ground-state wavefunction of the system, we know that
\rho({\bf r})
of Eq.
14
is a ground-state density for the system with external potential
v^{\prime}({\bf r})
and is thus

representable. From assumption 1 it follows that the total energy functional is exact for the electron density
\rho({\bf r})
. Its total energy should be equal to that of
\Psi_{1}
, which is simply the sum of the ground-state energies of two infinitely separated sites with electron counts
N_{0}\pm 1

E_{v^{\prime}}[\rho({\bf r})]=E_{v_{{\bf R}_{l}}}[N_{0}-1]+E_{v_{{\bf R}_{l}}}[N_{0}+1].

(15)

Here,
E_{v}[N]
is the ground-state energy of the
N
electron system with external potential
v({\bf r})
,

E_{v}[N]=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\rightarrow\int\rho(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}=N\end{subarray}}E_{v}[\rho({\bf r})].

(16)

From assumption 2, the total energy functional should be size-consistent, whereby

E_{v^{\prime}}[\rho({\bf r})]=\sum_{l=1}^{2}E_{v_{{\bf R}_{l}}}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}+1)\bigg].

(17)

Eq.
17
can be simplified by application of the translational invariance assumption to give

E_{v^{\prime}}[\rho({\bf r})]=2E_{v_{{\bf R}_{l}}}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l}({\bf r};N_{0}+1)\bigg],

(18)

where the site electron count is

N=\frac{1}{2}(N_{0}-1)+\frac{1}{2}(N_{0}+1)=N_{0}.

(19)

Eq.
18
enforces an upper bound on the total energy of the
N_{0}
electron system in external potential
v_{{\bf R}_{l}}({\bf r})

E_{v^{\prime}}[\rho({\bf r})]\geq 2E_{v_{{\bf R}_{l}}}[N_{0}].

(20)

Equality in Eq.

is achieved if there exists no wavefunction with a lower total energy per site, for any external potential composed of more than two copies of
v({\bf r})
that are infinitely separated. Combining Eqs.

and suppressing the site index label

2E_{v}[N_{0}]\leq E_{v}[N_{0}-1]+E_{v}[N_{0}+1].

(21)

This is in direct contradiction to our original assumption in Eq.
9
. We now proceed to give a complete proof by contradiction of the convexity condition in the general case.

In conclusion, the convexity condition of the total energy of a finite electronic system with respect to electron count, within density-functional theory, is derived from using the infinite-separation-limit technique based on three minimal,
conditions, with sufficient
conditions adapted also

Our result nonetheless confirms a stringent constraint on the exact exchange-correlation functional. This proof also
lifts an outstanding assumption in the proof of the piecewise linearity condition with respect to electron count, which
is central to understanding the Kohn-Sham band-gap and the exchange-correlation derivative discontinuity of DFT.

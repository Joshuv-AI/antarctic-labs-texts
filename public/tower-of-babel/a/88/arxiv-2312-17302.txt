Abstract
This paper consists of three parts: (I) To develop general theory of a (large) class of central simple finite dimensional algebras and answering some natural questions about them (that in general situation it is not even clear how to approach them, and the Brauer group is a step in the right directions), (II) To introduce and develop general theory of a large class of rings, PLM-rings, (intuitively, they are the most general form of Quillen’ Lemma), and (III) to apply these results for the following four classic algebras that turned out to be PLM-rings. Let K K be an arbitrary field and q ∈ K \ { 0 , 1 } q\in K\backslash\{0,1\} be a primitive n n ’th root of unity. Classifications of prime, completely prime, maximal and primitive ideals, and simple modules are obtained for the quantum Weyl algebra A 1 ( q ) = K ⟨ x , y | x y − q y x = 1 ⟩ A_{1}(q)=K\langle x,y\,|\,xy-qyx=1\rangle , the skew polynomial algebra 𝔸 = K ​ [ h ] ​ [ x ; σ ] \mathbb{A}=K[h][x;\sigma] , the skew Laurent polynomial algebras 𝒜 := K ⁡ [ h ] ​ [ x ± 1 ; σ ] {\cal A}:=K[h][x^{\pm 1};\sigma] , and ℬ := K ⁡ [ h ± 1 ] ​ [ x ± 1 ; σ ] {\cal B}:=K[h^{\pm 1}][x^{\pm 1};\sigma] where σ ⁡ ( h ) = q ​ h \sigma(h)=qh . The quotient rings (of fractions) of prime factor algebras of the algebras A 1 A_{1} , 𝔸 \mathbb{A} , 𝒜 {\cal A} , and ℬ {\cal B} are explicitly described. Each quotient ring is a central simple finite dimensional algebra, i.e. isomorphic to the matrix algebra M d ​ ( D ) M_{d}(D) for some d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and a central simple division algebra D D . The division algebra D D is either a finite field extension of K K or a cyclic algebra. These descriptions are a key fact in the classifications of prime ideals, completely prime ideals, and simple modules for the algebras above. For each simple module its basis, dimension, and endomorphism algebra are given. Explicit descriptions are obtained for the automorphism groups of the algebras A 1 A_{1} , 𝔸 \mathbb{A} , 𝒜 {\cal A} , and ℬ {\cal B} . Key Words: quantum Weyl algebra, division algebra, cyclic algebra, skew polynomial ring, skew Laurent polynomial ring, Galois group, norm, matrix norm, prime ideal, primitive ideal, maximal ideal, completely prime ideal, simple module, automorphism group. For an algebra

4.1
.(3) is a general result about containments of primes.
It partitions the prime spectrum of a ring into two disjoint subsets such that elements of one are not contained in elements of the other.

The Zariski topology on the prime spectrum of a ring.

Let
R
be a ring. For an ideal
I
of

are open sets in the

on the prime spectrum

of the ring

or the

. Clearly, the sets

are precisely all the closed sets in the Zariski topology. If the ring

where the closed sets are (by definition) finite intersection of the sets

where

. The algebras

The group

is a normal subgroup of

is isomorphic to the abelian group

(
\gamma(\lambda)=\lambda

\lambda\in K^{\times}

{\rm Aut}_{{\rm gr},K^{\times}}({\cal B}^{\times})=\{\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}\,|\,A\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\lambda,\mu\in K^{\times}\}\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}:h\mapsto\lambda h^{a}x^{b},\;\;x\mapsto\mu h^{c}x^{d}

\tau

induces a group automorphism of the factor group

for some

and
\lambda,\mu\in K^{\times}

\tau(x)\tau(h)=q\tau(h)\tau(x)

A\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 6.5
.

{\rm Aut}_{K}({\cal B})={\rm Aut}_{{\rm gr},K^{\times}}({\cal B}^{\times})=\{\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}\,|\,A\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\lambda,\mu\in K^{\times}\}

where
\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}:h\mapsto\lambda h^{a}x^{b}
,
x\mapsto\mu h^{c}x^{d}
.

Proof.

The second equality of the theorem has been proven above. Clearly,
{\rm Aut}_{K}({\cal B})\supseteq{\rm Aut}_{{\rm gr},K^{\times}}({\cal B}^{\times})
, as we checked already that every
\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}
respects the defining relation
xh=qhx
of the algebra
{\cal B}
. Since every automorphism of the algebra
{\cal B}
induces a group automorphism of
{\cal B}^{\times}
, we must have
{\rm Aut}_{K}({\cal B})={\rm Aut}_{{\rm gr},K^{\times}}({\cal B}^{\times})
.
∎

The group
{\rm Aut}_{K}({\cal B})
contains the subgroup

\mathbb{T}^{2}=\{t_{\lambda,\mu}=\tau_{E,\lambda,\mu}\,|\,\lambda,\mu\in K^{\times}\},\;\;t_{\lambda,\mu}:h\mapsto\lambda h,\;\;x\mapsto\mu x.

Notice that the subset
\{\tau_{A,1,1}\,|\,A\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\}
is not subgroups of
{\rm Aut}_{K}({\cal B})
.

Recall that the group of inner automorphisms

{\rm Inn}({\cal B})\simeq{\cal B}^{\times}/K^{\times}
of the algebra
{\cal B}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
via
K^{\times}h\mapsto(1,0),\;\;K^{\times}x\mapsto(0,1)
. So,
{\rm Inn}({\cal B})=\langle\omega_{h}\rangle\times\langle\omega_{x}\rangle=\{t_{q^{i},q^{j}}\,|\,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The set
\Omega:=\{(q^{i},q^{j})\,|\,(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
is a subgroup of
\Big(K^{\times}\Big)^{2}
.

Corollary 6.6
.

{\rm Out}({\cal B})={\rm Aut}_{\rm gr}({\cal B}^{\times})/{\rm Inn}({\cal B})=\{\tau_{A,\lambda,\mu}\,|\,A\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),(\lambda,\mu)\in\Big(K^{\times}\Big)^{2}/\Omega\}.

Proof.

The corollary follows from Theorem
6.5
.
∎

On Tamagawa numbers of CM tori
Pei-Xin Liang, Yasuhiro Oki, Hsin-Yi Yang, Chia-Fu Yu

Abstract
. In this article we investigate the problem of computing Tamagawa numbers of CM tori. This problem arises naturally from the problem of counting polarized abelian varieties with commutative endomorphism algebras over finite fields, and polarized CM abelian varieties and components of unitary Shimura varieties in the works of Achter–Altug–Garcia–Gordon and of Guo–Sheu–Yu, respectively. We make a systematic study on Galois cohomology groups in a more general setting and compute the Tamagawa numbers of CM tori associated to various Galois CM fields. Furthermore, we show that every (positive or negative) power of 2 2 is the Tamagawa number of a CM tori, proving the analogous conjecture of Ono for CM tori.

1.
Introduction

In his two fundamental papers
[
21
,
23
]
Takashi Ono investigated the arithmetic of algebraic tori. He introduced and explored the class number and Tamagawa number of
T
, which will be denoted by
h(T)
and
\tau(T)
respectively (also see Section 2 for the definitions). One arithmetic significant of these invariants is that
the class number
h(\mathbb{G}_{{\rm m},k})
is equal to the class number
h_{k}
of the number field
k
, and the analytic class number formula for
k
can be reformulated by the simple statement

\tau(\mathbb{G}_{{\rm m},k})=1
. Thus, the class numbers of algebraic tori can be viewed as generalizations of class numbers of number fields, while Tamagawa numbers play a key role in the extension of analytic class number formulas.

Ono showed
[
23
]
that
\tau(T)=|H^{1}(k,X(T))|/|\Sha^{1}(k,T)|
, where
X(T)
is the group of characters of
T
and
\Sha^{1}(k,T)
is the Tate-Shafarevich group of
T
. Kottwitz
[
13
]
generalized Ono’s formula to reductive groups and proved
[
14
]
the celebrated conjecture of Weil for the Tamagawa number of semi-simple simply connected groups. Ono constructed a
15
-dimensional algebraic torus with Tamagawa number
1/4
, showing that
\tau(T)
can be non-integral and conjectured in
[
22
]
that every positive rational number is equal to
\tau(T)
for some torus
T
. Ono’s conjecture was proved by S. Katayama
[
10
]
for the number field case.
For some later studies of class numbers and Tamagawa numbers of algebraic tori we refer to J.-M. Shyr
[
33
, Theorem 1]
,
S. Katayama
[
11
]
, M. Morishita
[
19
]
, C. González-Avilés
[
6
,

]
and M.-H. Tran
[

]
and references within.

In this article we are mainly concerned with the problem of computing the Tamagawa numbers of complex multiplication (CM) algebraic tori. CM tori are closely related to the arithmetic of CM abelian varieties and computing their Tamagawa numbers itself is a way of exploring the structure of CM fields. This problem directly contributes to recent works of Achter, Altug, Garcia and Gordon
[
1
]
and of J. Guo, N. Sheu and the fourth named author
[
8
]
.
In the former one the authors computed the size of an isogeny class of principally polarized abelian varieties over a finite field with commutative endomorphism algebra, and express the number in terms of a discriminant, the Tamagawa number, and the product of Frobenius local densities. In the latter one the authors computed formulas for certain CM abelian varieties and certain polarized abelian varieties over finite fields with commutative endomorphism algebras upon the results of
[
37
]
. Using the class number formula for CM tori, they also computed the numbers of connected components of complex unitary Shimura varieties.
In the appendix of
[
1
]
, W.-W. Li and T. Rüd have obtained several initial results of the values of

. Our goals are to prove more cases of CM tori and to determine the range of the values of Tamagawa numbers of all CM tori.
With a similar goal but using different methods, T. Rüd obtains several results along this direction
[
30

Though our original motivation of investigating Tamagawa numbers of CM tori comes from counting certain abelian varieties and exploring the structure of CM fields, the main part of the problem itself was to compute the Tate-Shafarevich group.
We explain in Remark

how the Tate-Shafarevich group of a CM torus also comes into play in the theory of Shimura varieties of PEL-type.
Tate-Shafarevich groups measure the failure of the local-global principle that is one of main interests in number theory and has been actively studied. Hürlimann
[
9
]
proved that the multiple norm principle holds for any etale

in which one component is cyclic and the other one is Galois.
Prasad and Rapinchuk
[

settled the problem of the local-global principle for embeddings of fields with involution into simple algebras with involution, where they also investigated the multiple norm principle. The multiple norm principle has been investigated further by Pollio and Rapinchuk
[
26
,
25
]
, Demarche and D. Wei
[
4
]
and D. Wei and F. Xu
[

]

[

]
studied the Tate-Shafarevich group of general multinorm one tori in which one of factors is a cyclic extension. They give a simple rule for determining the
\Sha
-group in the case of products of extensions of prime degree

computes explicitly the

-group for the cases where every factor is cyclic of degree

of highest degree defined over
k
.
To each place
v
, one associates a Haar measure
\omega_{v}
on

T(k_{v})
. Then the product of the Haar measures

\prod_{v\mid\infty}\omega_{v}\cdot\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\left(L_{v}(1,K/k,\chi_{T})\cdot\omega_{v}\right)

converges absolutely and defines a Haar measure on
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})
.

Write (N) for the number field case and (F) for the function field case.
For (N), let
d_{k}
be the discriminant of
k
. For (F),
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}(C)
is the function field of a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
and let
g(C)
be the genus of
C
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T
be an algebraic torus over a global field
k
and
\omega
be a nonzero invariant differential form on
T
defined over
k
of highest degree. Then

\omega_{\mathbb{A},can}:=\frac{\prod_{v\mid\infty}\omega_{v}\cdot\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\big(L_{v}(1,K/k,\chi_{T})\cdot\omega_{v}\big)}{\mu_{k}^{(\dim{T})}\cdot\rho_{T}},

defines a Haar measure on
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})
, which is called
the
Tamagawa measure on
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})
,
where

\mu_{k}:=\begin{cases}|d_{k}|^{1/2},&\text{for (N);}\\
q^{g(C)},&\text{for (F),}\end{cases}

\rho_{T}:=\lim_{s\to 1}(s-1)^{a}L(s,K/k,\chi_{T}),

and
a
is the order of the pole of the Artin
L
-function

L(s,K/k,\chi_{T})
at
s=1
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{a}
be a basis of
X(T)^{G}
. Define

\xi:T(\mathbb{A}_{k})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{a},\quad x\mapsto(||\xi_{1}(x)||,\dots,||\xi_{a}(x)||),

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}:x>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\times}
is the connected open subgroup.
Let
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}
denote the kernel of
\xi
; one has an isomorphism

T(\mathbb{A}_{k})/T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}\simeq\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{a},&\text{for (N);}\\
(q^{\mathbb{Z}})^{a},&\text{for (F)}.\end{cases}

Let
d^{\times}t:=\prod_{i=1}^{a}dt_{i}/t_{i}
be the canonical measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{a}
for (N) and
d^{\times}t
be the counting measure on
(q^{\mathbb{Z}})^{a}
with measure
(\log q)^{a}
on each point for (F). Let
\omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can}
be the unique Haar measure on
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}
such that

(2.4)

\omega_{\mathbb{A},can}=\omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can}\cdot d^{\times}t,

that is, for any measurable function

on

one has

\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{k})/T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}}\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}}F(xt)\,\omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can}\cdot d^{\times}t=\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{k})}F(x)\,\omega_{\mathbb{A},can}.

By a well-known theorem of Borel and Harish-Chandra
[
24
, Theorem
5.6]
, the quotient
space
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}/T(k)
has finite volume with respect to every Haar
measure. In fact
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}/T(k)
is the unique maximal
compact subgroup of
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})/T(k)
, because
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{a}

has no nontrivial compact subgroup.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
T
be an algebraic torus over a global field
k
.
The
Tamagawa number
\tau_{k}(T)
of
T

is defined by

(2.5)

\tau_{k}(T):=\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}/T(k)}\ \omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can},

the volume of
T(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{1}/T(k)
with respect to
\omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can}
, where

\omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A},can}
is the Haar measure on

defined in (

).

One has the following properties (
[
21
, Theorem 3.5.1]

[

, Section 3.2, p. 57]
):

(i)

For any two algebraic

-tori

and

, one has
\tau_{k}(T\times T^{\prime})=\tau_{k}(T)\cdot\tau_{k}(T^{\prime})

(ii)

For any finite extension

(iii)

Note that the last statement (iii) is equivalent to the analytic class number formula

2.2.
Values of Tamagawa numbers

Theorem 2.4

(Ono’s formula)
.

Let
K/k
be a finite Galois extension with Galois group
\Gamma
, and
T
be an algebraic torus over
k
with splitting field
K
. Then

(2.6)

\tau_{k}(T)=\frac{|H^{1}\big(\Gamma,X(T)\big)|}{|\Sha^{1}(\Gamma,T)|},

where

(2.7)

\Sha^{i}(\Gamma,T):=\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits\big(H^{i}(K/k,T)\to\prod_{v}H^{i}(K_{w}/k_{v},T)\big)

is the Tate-Shafarevich group associated to
H^{i}(\Gamma,T)
for
i\geq 0
and
w
is a place of
K
over
v
.

Proof.

See
[
23
, Section 5]
.

Remark 2.5
.

The cohomology groups
H^{1}\big(\Gamma,X(T)\big)
and

\Sha^{1}(\Gamma,T)
are independent of the choice of the splitting
field
K
; see
[
23
, Sections 3.3 and 3.4]
.

According to Ono’s formula, the Tamagawa number of any algebraic
k
-torus is a positive rational number. Ono constructed an infinite family of algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-tori
T
with
\tau(T)=\tau_{\mathbb{Q}}(T)=1/4
, which particularly shows that
\tau(T)
needs not to be an integer. Ono conjectured
[

]

that every positive rational number can be realized as
\tau_{k}(T)
for some algebraic
k
-torus

.
Ono’s conjecture was proved by S. Katayama
[
10
]
for the number field case.

For any abelian group
G
, the Pontryagin dual of
G
is defined to be

(2.8)

G^{\vee}:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The Poitou-Tate duality (
[
24
, Theorem 6.10]
and
[
20
, Theorem 8.6.8]
)
says that there is a natural isomorphism
\Sha^{1}(\Gamma,T)^{\vee}\simeq\Sha^{2}(\Gamma,X(T))
. Thus,

(2.9)

\tau_{k}(T)=\frac{|H^{1}\big(\Gamma,X(T)\big)|}{|\Sha^{2}(\Gamma,X(T))|}.

To simply the notation we shall often
suppress the Galois group from Galois
cohomology groups and write
H^{1}(X(T))
and
\Sha^{2}(X(T))
for

H^{1}(\Gamma,X(T))
and
\Sha^{2}(\Gamma,X(T))
etc.,
if there is no risk of confusion.

3.
Transfer maps, corestriction maps and extensions

3.1.
Transfer maps

Let
G
be a group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
of finite index. Let
X:=G/H
, and let
\varphi:X\to G
be a section. If
g\in G
and
x\in X
, the elements
\varphi(gx)
and
g\varphi(x)
belong to the same class of mod
H
; hence there exists a unique element
h^{\varphi}_{g,x}\in H
such that
g\varphi(x)=\varphi(gx)h^{\varphi}_{g,x}
. Let
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}(g)\in H^{\rm ab}
be defined by:

\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}(g):=\prod_{x\in X}h^{\varphi}_{g,x}\mod D(H),

where
D(H)=[H,H]
is the commutator group of
H
and the product is computed in
H^{\rm ab}=H/D(H)
.

By
[
32
, Theorem 7.1]
, the map
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}:G\to H^{\rm ab}
is a group homomorphism and it does not depend on the choice of the section
\varphi
. This homomorphism is called the
transfer
of
G
into
H^{\rm ab}
(originally from the term “Verlagerung” in German). One may also view it as a homomorphism
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}:G^{\rm ab}\to H^{\rm ab}
.

In the literature one also uses the right coset space
X^{\prime}:=H\backslash G
but this does not effect the result. One can easily show that if
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}^{\prime}
is the transfer map defined using
X^{\prime}
, then
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}^{\prime}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}
.
One has the following functorial property (see
[
32
, p. 89]
).

.

Let
H\subset G
and
H^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be subgroups of finite index.
If
\sigma
be a group homomorphism from the pair
(G,H)
to
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
which induces a bijection

, then the following diagram

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{\rm ab}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{{}^{\prime}\rm ab}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\rm ab}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{{}^{\prime}\rm ab}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G^{\prime}.H^{\prime}}}

commutes.

Lemma 3.2

Let

(2) By definition

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

be two subgroups of finite index in

with

and cyclic quotient

of order

of

.

(1)
Let

and

be double coset representatives of

\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}(g)=\sigma^{\sum_{i=1}^{r}m(g,x_{i})},

where

and

is the unique integer such that

(2)
Let

be the element defined by

,
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}^{\vee}:N^{\vee}\to\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

. Then

.

Proof.

(1) We choose the set of representatives

of

, which defines a section
\widetilde{\varphi}
of the natural projection
G\to G/\widetilde{N}
. Then the image of the element
n^{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{g,x}
in
H
is given by

n^{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{g,x}\mod H=\begin{cases}\sigma^{m(g,x_{i})}&\text{if $x=g^{f_{i}-1}x_{i}H$ for some $1\leq i\leq r$;}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2) By definition,
f(g)=\sigma^{*}(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}(g))=\sigma^{*}(\sigma^{\sum_{i}m(g,x_{i})})=\left(\sum_{i}m(g,x_{i})\right)/n\mod\mathbb{Z}
.

One can show that the integer

\langle g\rangle x_{i}H
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}
be a product of groups

and let

, for each

, be a subgroup of finite index. Put

Let

be the

\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,\widetilde{N}_{i}/H_{i}}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G/H_{i},\widetilde{N}_{i}/H_{i}}\circ\,{\rm pr}_{i}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G_{i},N_{i}}\circ\,{\rm pr}_{i},

{\rm pr}_{i}(D_{{\mathfrak{P}}})=D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}
. We show that
{\rm pr}_{i}(D_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\widetilde{N}_{i})=D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}
. The inclusion
\subseteq
is obvious because
{\rm pr}_{i}(D_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\widetilde{N}_{i})\subset{\rm pr}_{i}(D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}})\cap{\rm pr}_{i}(\widetilde{N}_{i})=D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}
. For the other inclusion, since
{\rm pr}_{i}:D_{\mathfrak{P}}\to D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}
is surjective, for each
y\in D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}
there exists an element
x=(x_{i})\in D_{\mathfrak{P}}
such that
x_{i}=y
. Since
x_{i}\in N_{i}
,
x
also lies in
\widetilde{N}_{i}
. This proves the other inclusion. Therefore,
\overline{D}_{i}=D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}
.

The map
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{D}_{i}}^{\vee}
is dual to the map

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}:D\to\prod_{i=1}^{r}(D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}).

Since each
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}},D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}\circ\,{\rm pr}_{i}
(cf. (
3.6
)),
the above map factorizes into the following composition

\begin{CD}D@>{({\rm pr}_{i})_{i}}>{}>\prod_{i=1}^{r}D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}@>{\prod_{i}{\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}},D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}}}>{}>\prod_{i=1}^{r}(D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}).\end{CD}

Consider the restriction
f|_{\prod_{i}D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}}=(f_{i}|_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}})
of
f
to
\prod_{i}D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}
, and put
D_{i}^{\prime}:=D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}
.
By condition (b), there exists an element
h_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
such that
f_{i}|_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits^{\vee}_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}},D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}(h_{i})
. Then
f|_{\prod_{i}D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}}=\left(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits^{\vee}_{D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}},D_{{\mathfrak{P}}_{i}}\cap N_{i}}(h_{i})\right)
.
Restrict this function to
D
, we obtain
f|_{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{D}_{i}}^{\vee}(h_{i})
. This shows that
f|_{D}
lies in the image of
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{D}_{i}}^{\vee}
.

(2) By (1), we have
\prod_{i=1}^{r}H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
. To prove the other inclusion, we must show that each
f_{i}
satisfies condition (b) provided
f
satisfies condition (a), which we assume from now on. For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
be a decomposition group of
G
over
D_{i}^{\prime}
and
s_{i}:D_{i}^{\prime}\to D
be a section such that
{\rm pr}_{k}\circ s_{i}
is trivial for
k\neq i
. Then (a) implies

f|_{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{D}_{k}}^{\vee}(h_{k})

for some
h_{k}\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\overline{D}_{k},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Pulling back to
D_{i}^{\prime}
via
s_{i}
, we have

f_{i}|_{D_{i}^{\prime}}=s_{i}^{*}f|_{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}s_{i}^{*}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{D}_{k}}^{\vee}(h_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{r}s_{i}^{*}{\rm pr}_{k}^{*}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D^{\prime}_{k},\overline{D}_{k}}^{\vee}(h_{k}),

where
D^{\prime}_{k}:={\rm pr}_{k}(D)
if
k\neq i
. Since
s_{i}^{*}{\rm pr}_{k}^{*}=1
if
k=i
and
s_{i}^{*}{\rm pr}_{k}^{*}=0
otherwise, we get
f_{i}|_{D_{i}^{\prime}}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D^{\prime}_{i},\overline{D}_{i}}^{\vee}(h_{i})
.

Proposition 5.7
.

With the notation and assumptions be as above. Suppose further that for each
1\leq i\leq r
, every decomposition group of
G_{i}
is cyclic. Then we have
\tau(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\tau(T_{i})
.

Proof.

We shall show that the assumption in Lemma
5.6
(2) holds. Let
D^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
be a decomposition group of
G_{i}
; by our assumption
D^{\prime}_{i}
is cyclic and let
a_{i}
be a generator.
Let
D
be a cyclic subgroup of
G
generated by
\tilde{a}_{i}=(1,\dots,1,a_{i},1,\dots,1)
with
a_{i}
at the
i
-th place.
Clearly
D
is the decomposition group of some prime and let
s_{i}:D_{i}^{\prime}\to D
be the section sending
a_{i}
to
\tilde{a}_{i}
. Clearly for
k\neq i
,
{\rm pr}_{k}\circ s_{i}:D_{i}^{\prime}\to D_{k}^{\prime}
is trivial. By Lemma
5.6
, we have
H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have

(5.9)

\tau(T)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}|N_{i}|}{|H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}|}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(T_{i})=\frac{|N_{i}|}{|H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}|}.

Therefore,
\tau(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\tau(T_{i})
.

In this section, we assume that

6.1.
Cyclotomic extensions

Proposition 6.1
.

(1)

(2)

In other cases,

Proof.
(1) In this case the Galois group

is cyclic and Proposition

(3) shows that

.

(2)
Suppose first that

is not a power of a prime. By Proposition

divides

.
Proposition

(1) then shows

.
Now suppose the other case that

2.7
, we have

(1).

6.2.
Abelian extensions

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume that

(1)

We have
\tau(T)\in\{1,2\}
.

(2)

If
g
is odd, then
\tau(T)=1
.

(3)

If
g

(1) Write

.

in the projection

(3) Since

6.3.

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume the short exact sequence
(*)
splits. Let

(2)
When

(ii)

Since
(*)

(2) We have

),

(ii) Suppose

Remark 6.4
.

When
G
is non-abelian and the short exact sequence
(*)
does not split,
we do not know the value of
\tau(T)
in general. However,
if the involution
\iota
is non-trivial on the maximal abelian extension

K_{ab}
over

in

then

is a CM abelian field and it follows from Proposition

It remains to determine the Tamagawa number when

and the dihedral groups, for which we treat in the next subsections.

6.4.
Q_{8}
-extensions

The quaternion group
Q_{8}=\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}
is the group of
8
elements generated by
i,j
with usual relations
i^{2}=j^{2}=-1
and
ij=-ji=k
.
We have the following well-known properties of
Q_{8}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

(1)

The group
Q_{8}
contains
6
elements of order
4
, one element of order
2
, and the identity. It does not has a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, every proper subgroup is cyclic.

(2)

Every non-trivial subgroup contains
\{\pm 1\}
, and only subgroup which maps onto
Q_{8}/\{\pm 1\}
is
Q_{8}
.

(3)

The center
Z(Q_{8})=\{\pm 1\}=D(Q_{8})
, where
D(Q_{8})=[Q_{8},Q_{8}]
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two odd positive integers such that

(6.2)

P-1=a^{2},\quad Q-1=Pb^{2}

for some integers
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
Q
is not a square. Let
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
be the simple extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\beta
which satisfies
\beta^{2}=\alpha:=-(P+\sqrt{P})(Q+\sqrt{Q})
. Then

K
is a Galois CM field with Galois group
Q_{8}
with maximal totally real field
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P},\sqrt{Q})
. The Galois group
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/\mathbb{Q})
is generated by
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
given by

(6.3)

\tau_{1}(\beta)=\frac{(\sqrt{P}-1)}{a}\beta,\quad\tau_{2}(\beta)=\frac{(\sqrt{Q}-1)}{\sqrt{P}a}\beta.

Moreover, for each prime
\ell
, the decomposition group
D_{\ell}
is cyclic except when
\ell|Q
. For
\ell|Q
, one has

(6.4)

D_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $\left(\frac{P}{\ell}\right)=1$;}\\
Q_{8},&\text{if $\left(\frac{P}{\ell}\right)=-1$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
\gcd(P,Q)=1
and
P
is not a square. Then
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P},\sqrt{Q})
is a totally real biquadratic field and its Galois group is generated by
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, where

\sigma_{1}:\begin{cases}\sqrt{P}\mapsto-\sqrt{P}\\
\sqrt{Q}\mapsto\sqrt{Q}\end{cases},\quad\sigma_{2}:\begin{cases}\sqrt{P}\mapsto\sqrt{P}\\
\sqrt{Q}\mapsto-\sqrt{Q}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\alpha
is a totally negative, and hence
K
is a CM field. The maximal totally real subfield
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\subset\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P},\sqrt{Q})
.
Since the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
is of degree
4
, one has
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P},\sqrt{Q})
.
Let
\tau_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})
,
i=1,2
, be elements such that
\tau_{i}|_{K^{+}}=\sigma_{i}
. One computes

\frac{\tau_{1}(\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}=\frac{\tau_{1}(\alpha)}{\alpha}=\frac{-\sigma_{1}(P+\sqrt{P})\sigma_{1}(Q+\sqrt{Q})}{-(P+\sqrt{P})(Q+\sqrt{Q})}=\frac{(P-\sqrt{P})^{2}}{P^{2}-P}=\frac{(\sqrt{P}-1)^{2}}{P-1}=\frac{(\sqrt{P}-1)^{2}}{a^{2}},

and obtains

\tau_{1}(\beta)=\pm\frac{(\sqrt{P}-1)}{a}\beta,\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{1}(K)\subset K.

Similarly, one computes
\tau_{2}(\beta)^{2}/\beta^{2}=(\sqrt{Q}-1)^{2}/Pb^{2}
and obtains

\tau_{2}(\beta)=\pm\frac{(\sqrt{Q}-1)}{\sqrt{P}a}\beta,\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{2}(K)\subset K.

It follows that
K/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois.
Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/\mathbb{Q})
be defined as in (
6.3
).
One easily computes
\tau_{1}^{2}(\beta)=-\beta,\tau_{2}^{2}(\beta)=-\beta,\tau_{1}\tau_{2}(\beta)=\iota\tau_{2}\tau_{1}(\beta)
and obtains the relations

\tau_{1}^{4}=\tau_{2}^{4}=1,\quad\tau_{1}^{2}=\tau_{2}^{2},\quad\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{1}^{-1}=\tau_{2}^{-1},

showing that
K
is a
Q_{8}
-CM field.

Denote by
D_{\ell}^{+}
the decomposition group at
\ell
in
G^{+}=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q})
. Then
D_{\ell}=Q_{8}
if and only if
D_{\ell}^{+}=G^{+}
. If
\ell\nmid PQ
, then
\ell
is unramified in
K^{+}
and
D_{\ell}^{+}
is cyclic. Thus,
D_{\ell}^{+}
cannot be
G^{+}
,
D_{\ell}\neq Q_{8}
and
D_{\ell}
is cyclic.
If
\ell|P
, then
(\frac{Q}{\ell})=(\frac{1}{\ell})=1
. So
\ell
is ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P})
and splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{Q})
.
Thus,
D_{\ell}^{+}\neq G^{+}
and hence
D_{\ell}
is cyclic. It remains to treat the case
\ell|Q
. If
(\frac{P}{\ell})=1
, then
D_{\ell}^{+}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and hence
D_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
. Otherwise,
\ell
is ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{Q})
and inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P})
. One has
D_{\ell}^{+}=G^{+}
and
D_{\ell}=Q_{8}
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
K
be a CM field which is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
Q_{8}
. Then

(6.5)

\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}1/2,&\text{if every decomposition group of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is cyclic;}\\
2,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Put
G=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/\mathbb{Q})\simeq Q_{8}
and
N=\langle\iota\rangle
. We shall show that

(6.6)

H^{1}(\Lambda)=H^{1}(G,\Lambda)=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

and

(6.7)

\Sha^{2}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\text{if every decomposition group of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is cyclic;}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then (
6.5
) follows from (
6.6
) and (
6.7
).

By Proposition
4.1
and (
4.18
), we have an exact sequence

(6.8)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ H^{1}(\Lambda^{1})\simeq N^{\rm ab,\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})=G^{\rm ab,\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}^{\vee}}

noting that
H^{2}(G,\Lambda^{1})=0
when
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois CM field. Since the
2
-Sylow subgroup of
Q_{8}
is not cyclic, the transfer
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}
is not surjective by Proposition
3.3
. Therefore,
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{G,N}^{\vee}
is not injective and is zero.
This proves
H^{1}(\Lambda)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\simeq\Sha^{2}(\Lambda)
from (
4.25
).

If there is a finite place of
K
whose decomposition group is not cyclic, then we have
\Sha^{2}(\Lambda)=0
. Therefore,
\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=2
.
On the other hand, suppose that every decomposition group of
K/\mathbb{Q}
is cyclic. One has
G^{\rm ab}=G/N\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Recall

H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=\{f\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}):(*)\ f|_{D}\in{\rm Im}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,D\cap N}^{\vee},\ \forall\,D\in{\mathcal{D}}\}.

Note that
f|_{N}=0
, so the restriction to
D
map
f|_{D}
is contained in
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(D/N,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
whenever
N\subset D
(also noting that
N\subset D
if
D
is not trivial). So if
D=0
or
D\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
then
f|_{D}=0
and condition
(*)
above is satisfied automatically. Suppose
D\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
. Since the
2
-Sylow subgroup of
D
is cyclic, the map
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,N}^{\vee}:N^{\vee}\to D^{\vee}
is injective and
{\rm Im}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,N}^{\vee}
the unique subgroup of
D^{\vee}
of order
2
. Then
f|_{D}\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(D/N,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})={\rm Im}\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,N}^{\vee}
and condition
(*)
is also satisfied automatically. Therefore,
\Sha^{2}(\Lambda)\simeq H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. This proves the proposition.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two odd positive integers, and

with
\alpha=-(P+\sqrt{P})(Q+\sqrt{Q})
be the Galois CM with group

. Then

\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}1/2,&\text{if $\left(\frac{P}{q}\right)=1$ for all primes $q|Q$;}\\

Proof.

By Proposition
6.6
, the cyclicity of all decomposition groups of

is equivalent to the condition
\left(\frac{P}{q}\right)=1
for all primes

. Hence the assertion follows from Proposition
6.7
.

6.5.
The dihedral case

Let
D_{n}
denote the dihedral group of order

.

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose the CM-field
K
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G
,
and let

If
|S|\geq|G|/2
, then
n_{K}\leq 2
. If the inequality is strict holds, then

Proof.

This is due to Jiangwei Xue. Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be the class field corresponding to the open subgroup
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}N(T(\mathbb{A}))\subset\mathbb{A}^{\times}
by class field theory. Then
n_{K}=[\mathbb{A}^{\times}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{A}_{L}^{\times})]=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
. Suppose

Proof.

We first describe the transfer map

.
We may regard the CM fields

as subfields of

. Following the definition of

.
Then (cf. Lemma

Thus, it remains to show

. Let

\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}:=\widetilde{\varphi}(\Sigma_{K_{i}^{+}})
. Then we have bijections
\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Phi_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Sigma_{K_{i}^{+}}

, and let

be the corresponding elements. Put

\widetilde{\phi}^{\prime}
. Then

are elements lying over

and we have

. So

if and only if

. On the other hand, since

and two elements

are lying over the same element

Therefore, the bijection

gives the bijection of subsets:

\{\phi\in\Phi_{i}:g\phi\not\in\Phi_{i}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\{x\in\Sigma_{K_{i}^{+}}:{n}^{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{g,x}\not\in H_{i}\}.

This shows the desired equality

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{g\mapsto(g,\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})})}\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/\mathbb{Q})\times\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})

is an isomorphism by the definition of
K^{\prime}
.

For a number field
E
which is finite Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, we write for
\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(E)
the set of prime numbers which ramify in
E
.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
r
be a positive integer, and
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
be number fields which are Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
. For each
i
, we denote by
E_{i}
the maximal abelian subfield of
K_{i}
. Assume the following:

(i)

[K_{i}:E_{i}]
is a prime number for any
i\geq 1
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Ram}(E_{i})\cap\mathrm{Ram}(E_{j})=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.

Let
L
be the compositum of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
. Then there is an isomorphism

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/\mathbb{Q})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

We give a proof by induction on
r
. It is trivial if
r=1
. Next, assume that the assertion holds for
r-1
, that is, there is an isomorphism

(8.1)

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}),

where
L^{\prime}:=K_{1}\cdots K_{r-1}
. We first prove the equality

(8.2)

E_{r}\cap L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}.

Since
E_{r}
is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
, it is contained in the maximal abelian subfield
E^{\prime}
of
L^{\prime}
. On the other hand, the induction hypothesis (
8.1
) implies the equality
E^{\prime}=E_{1}\cdots E_{r-1}
. Hence
E_{r}\cap L^{\prime}
is contained in
E_{r}\cap(E_{1}\cdots E_{r-1})
. However, we have
E_{r}\cap(E_{1}\cdots E_{r-1})=\mathbb{Q}
by the assumption (ii) and the global class field theory, and hence (
8.2
) holds.

Now we prove the equality
K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}
, which gives the desired assertion. If
K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}\neq\mathbb{Q}
, then we have
E_{r}\cap(K_{r}\cap L^{\prime})=\mathbb{Q}
and
E_{r}\subsetneq E_{r}\cdot(K_{r}\cap L^{\prime})\subset K_{r}
by (
8.2
). Therefore
K_{r}
is equal to the compositum of
E_{r}
and
K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}
by the assumption (i). Consequently, there is an isomorphism

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{r}/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(E_{r}/\mathbb{Q})\times\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}).

In particular,
[K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}]=[K_{r}:E_{r}]
is a prime number, and hence
K_{r}/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian. This contradicts the assumption (i), which implies the desired equality
K_{r}\cap L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be the set of unordered pairs of prime numbers
\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
satisfying the following:

\ell\equiv\ell^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod 4,\quad\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=1.

Proposition 8.6
.

For any positive integer
r
, there are
r
-pairs
\{\ell_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}\},\ldots,\{\ell_{r},\ell^{\prime}_{r}\}
in
\mathbf{L}
such that

|\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r},\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\ell^{\prime}_{r}\}|=2r.

Proof.

By the Dirichlet prime number theorem, there are
r
distinct prime numbers
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
which are congruent to
1
modulo
4
. Moreover, the Dirichlet prime number theorem implies the existence of prime numbers
\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\ell^{\prime}_{r}
satisfying the following:

\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}_{i}\equiv 1\bmod 4,\,\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}_{i}}{\ell_{i}}\right)=1&\text{if }i=1,\\
\ell^{\prime}_{i}\equiv 1\bmod 4,\,\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}_{i}}{\ell_{i}}\right)=1,\,\ell^{\prime}_{i}\notin\{\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\ell^{\prime}_{i-1}\}&\text{if }i\geq 2.\end{cases}

Therefore the assertion holds.

For
\lambda:=\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\in\mathbf{L}
, put
K_{\lambda}^{+}:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell},\sqrt{\ell^{\prime}})
.

Lemma 8.7
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{L}
, the decomposition groups of
K_{\lambda}^{+}/\mathbb{Q}
at all finite places are cyclic.

Proof.

Write
\lambda=\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
. Let
v
be a finite place of
K_{\lambda}^{+}
which lies above a prime number
\ell_{0}
. If
\ell_{0}\notin\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
, then
K_{\lambda}^{+}/\mathbb{Q}
is unramified at
v
, and hence the assertion holds for
v
. The assertion for
\ell_{0}=\ell
follows from the assumption
\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=1
. Finally, in the case
\ell_{0}=\ell^{\prime}
, the statement is a consequence of the equality
\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=1
.

Proposition 8.8
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{L}
, there is a CM field
K
containing
K_{\lambda}^{+}
such that
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a
Q_{8}
-extension.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.4
(2), it suffices to prove the existence of
Q_{8}
-extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
containing
K_{\lambda}^{+}
. Write
\lambda=\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
. It is equivalent to the equality

(\ell,\ell)_{0}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{0}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{0}=1,

which is a consequence of Theorem
8.2
. Since the image of the class
(\ell,\ell)_{0}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{0}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{0}
in
\mathop{\rm Br}\nolimits(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
is equal to
(\ell,\ell)_{v}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{v}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{v}
, by
the Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem
[
16
, Chapter IX, Section 6, p. 195]

it is equivalent to prove the following for any place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
:

(8.3)

(\ell,\ell)_{v}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{v}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{v}=1.

Case 1.
v
is infinite.

In this case, (
8.3
) follows from Proposition
8.3
(1) since
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are positive.

Case 2.
v=\ell_{0}\notin\{2,\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
.

The condition
\ell_{0}\notin\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
derives that
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are units in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell_{0}}
. Hence, since
\ell_{0}\neq 2
, one has

(\ell,\ell)_{\ell_{0}}=(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{\ell_{0}}=(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{\ell_{0}}=1

by Proposition
8.3
(2). This implies equality (
8.3
).

Case 3.
v=2
.

Since
\ell\equiv\ell^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod 4
, Proposition
8.3
(4) implies

(\ell,\ell)_{2}=(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{2}=(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{2}=1.

Hence (
8.3
) holds.

Case 4.
v=\ell
.

The assumption
\ell\equiv 1\bmod 4
is equivalent to the equality
\left(\frac{-1}{\ell}\right)=1
. Hence

(\ell,\ell)_{\ell}=(\ell,-1)_{\ell}=1.

On the other hand, the assumption
\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=1
implies

(\ell,\ell^{\prime})_{\ell}=(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})_{\ell}=1,

which is a consequence of Proposition
8.3
(2) and (3). Therefore we obtain the desired equality (
8.3
).

Case 5.
v=\ell^{\prime}
.

Since
\ell^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod 4
and

\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=1,

the assertion (
8.3
) follows from the same argument as Case 4.

In the following, we give a proof of Theorem
7.8
.
By Corollary
7.6
, we may assume
n=-r<0
. Take
\lambda_{1}=\{\ell_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}\},\ldots,\lambda_{r}=\{\ell_{r},\ell^{\prime}_{r}\}\in\mathbf{L}
satisfying

(8.4)

|\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r},\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\ell^{\prime}_{r}\}|=2r,

which is possible by Proposition
8.6
. Then, Proposition
8.8
implies that there is a
Q_{8}
-CM field containing
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+}
for any
1\leq i\leq r
.

The following immediately implies the desired assertion.

Theorem 8.9
.

Under the above notations, let
K_{\lambda_{i}}
be a
Q_{8}
-CM field containing
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
. Then the CM fields
K_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,K_{\lambda_{r}}
satisfy the conditions (a), (b) and (c) in Proposition
7.7
.

Proof.

From the definition of
K_{\lambda_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, condition (a) holds.

We shall show that the assumptions (i) and (ii) in Lemma
8.5
hold. By Lemma
6.5
(3), for any
1\leq i\leq r
,
K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+}
is the maximal abelian subfield of

and
[K_{\lambda_{i}}:K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+}]=2
. In particular, assumption (i) holds. On the other hand, since
\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+})=\lambda_{i}
for any

, the condition (
8.4
) implies the equality
\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(K_{\lambda_{i}}^{+})\cap\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(K_{\lambda_{j}}^{+})=\emptyset
for

. Hence we obtain the assumption (ii), which verifies condition (b).

Take a finite places
w
of
K_{\lambda_{i}}
and
v
of

satisfying

. Then Lemma

implies the cyclicity of the decomposition group of

9.
Products of two linearly disjoint Galois CM fields

In this section we show the following result.

Theorem 9.1
.

There are infinitely many CM algebras
K=K_{1}\times K_{2}
with linearly disjoint Galois CM fields
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
such that

(9.1)

\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\tau(T^{K_{i},\mathbb{Q}}).

This theorem shows that the conclusion of Proposition
5.7
is no longer true if one drops the cyclicity of decomposition groups of
G_{i}
for all
i
.
We shall use the notations in Section
5.2
. In particular,
L=K_{1}K_{2}
,
G:=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/\mathbb{Q})
,
G_{i}:=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{i}/\mathbb{Q})
,

H_{i}:=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/K_{i})
,
\widetilde{N}_{i}:=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/K_{i}^{+})
and
N_{i}=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{i}/K_{i}^{+})
for

First, we give a sufficient condition on

for which Theorem
9.1
holds. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
be the sets of cyclic subgroups of
G
and
G_{i}
respectively. Then put

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in G^{\vee}\mid f\!\mid_{D}\in{\rm Im}(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D,\overline{\mathbf{D}}}^{\vee})\text{ for all }D\in{\mathcal{C}}\},

\displaystyle H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in G_{i}^{\vee}\mid f\!\mid_{D^{\prime}}\in{\rm Im}(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D^{\prime},D^{\prime}\cap N_{i}}^{\vee})\text{ for all }D^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\}.

Lemma 9.2
.

If
G\cong G_{1}\times G_{2}
, then
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}=H^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}\times H^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}

Proof.

The proof is the same as Lemma
5.6
.

We define a subgroup
D_{0}
of
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
as follows:

D_{0}:=\langle(\bar{1},\bar{0},\bar{1}),(\bar{0},\bar{1},\bar{0})\rangle.

Here we denote by
(\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{c})
the element
(a\bmod 4,b\bmod 2,c\bmod 2)
in
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
for
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Assume that Galois CM fields
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
satisfy the following:

(i)

G_{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
N_{1}\cong\langle(\bar{2},\bar{0})\rangle\subset G_{1}
and

(ii)

G\cong G_{1}\times G_{2}
, that is,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are linearly disjoint;

(iii)

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{C}}\cup\{D_{0}\}
.

Put
K:=K_{1}\times K_{2}
. Then we have the following:

\tau(T^{K_{1},\mathbb{Q}})=2,\quad\tau(T^{K_{2},\mathbb{Q}})=1,\quad\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=1.

In particular, the equality (
9.1
) holds.

Proof.

By definition, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{1}=(2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\{\bar{0}\})\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\quad H_{1}=\{(\bar{0},\bar{0})\}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{2}=G,\quad H_{2}=(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\{\bar{0}\}.

Moreover, Proposition
4.3
implies

\tau(T^{K_{i},\mathbb{Q}})=\frac{2}{|H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}|},\quad\tau(T^{K_{1}\times K_{2},\mathbb{Q}})=\frac{4}{|H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}|}.

Since
G_{1}
is
D_{0}
modulo
G_{2}
, by (iii)
G_{1}
itself is a decomposition group of
G_{1}
. By this and that
G_{1}
is not cyclic, one computes that
H^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=0
, which implies
\tau(T^{K_{1},\mathbb{Q}})=2/1=2
. Moreover, the equality
\tau(T^{K_{2},\mathbb{Q}})=1
follows from Proposition
2.8
(2) as
[K_{2}:\mathbb{Q}]=2
. In particular, we obtain
|H^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}|=2
, that is,

(9.2)

H^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=H^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}=G_{2}^{\vee}.

On the other hand, for the equality
\tau(T^{K,\mathbb{Q}})=1
, it suffices to prove

(9.3)

H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=\{f\in G^{\vee}\mid f((2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\{\bar{0}\})=0\}.

By direct computation, we have

H^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}=\{f_{1}\in G_{1}^{\vee}\mid f(2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0\}.

Combining this equality, (
9.2
) and Lemma
9.2
, one has

H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}=H^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}\times H^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime}=\{f\in G^{\vee}\mid f((2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\{\bar{0}\})=0\}.

For another inclusion, it suffices to prove

(9.4)

{\rm Im}(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{0},\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{0}}^{\vee})=\{f\in D_{0}^{\vee}\mid f((2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\{\bar{0}\})=0\}.

Recall that
\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{0},\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{0}}=(\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{0},\overline{D}_{0,i}})_{1\leq i\leq 2}
and

\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{0},\overline{D}_{0,i}}=\mathop{\rm Ver}\nolimits_{D_{0}/D_{0}\cap H_{i},\overline{D}_{0,i}}\circ\pi_{i},

where
\pi_{i}\colon D_{0}\rightarrow D_{0}/D_{0}\cap H_{i}
is the canonical surjection. Since one has

D_{0,1}=D_{0}\cap\widetilde{N}_{1}=2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\{\bar{0}\}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\quad D_{0}\cap H_{1}=\{0\},

we obtain that

is not cyclic and

. Hence Proposition 3.3 implies

. On the other hand, we have

by

. Consequently, the homomorphism

can be written as the composite

Therefore, (
9.4
) follows from the equality

Now we construct pairs of CM fields

satisfying Proposition

.

Lemma 9.4
.

Third, if
\ell=\ell_{2}
, then by (b) we have

\displaystyle D_{\ell_{2}}(K_{1}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{1},\quad I_{\ell_{2}}(K_{1}/\mathbb{Q})\cong\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

One computes
\left(\frac{-\ell_{1}\ell_{3}}{\ell_{2}}\right)=-1
and then

D_{\ell_{2}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{2},\quad I_{\ell_{2}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=\{0\}.

Since
I_{\ell_{2}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=\{0\}
, one has
I_{\ell_{2}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=\langle(\bar{0},\bar{1},\bar{0})\rangle
. By
D_{\ell_{2}}(K_{1}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{1}
, one sees
D_{\ell_{2}}(L/\mathbb{Q})=\langle(\bar{0},\bar{1},\bar{0}),((\bar{1},\bar{0},\bar{c})\rangle
for some
\bar{c}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Because
D_{\ell_{2}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{2}
, we see
\bar{c}=\bar{1}
and hence
D_{\ell_{2}}(L/\mathbb{Q})=D_{0}
.
Finally, suppose
i=3
. Since
\ell_{3}
splits completely in
K_{1}
, then

D_{\ell_{3}}(L/\mathbb{Q})=D_{\ell_{3}}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{2},

and hence it is cyclic. This completes the proof of (iii).

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})
and
(\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{3})
be ordered triples of prime numbers satisfying (a), (b) and (c) in Lemma
9.5
. Put

K:=K_{(\ell_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}})\times\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell_{1}\ell_{3}}),\quad K^{\prime}:=K_{(\ell^{\prime}_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell^{\prime}_{2}})\times\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell^{\prime}_{1}\ell^{\prime}_{3}}).

Then we have
K\simeq K^{\prime}
if and only if
\ell_{i}=\ell^{\prime}_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq 3
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
K\simeq K^{\prime}
implies
\ell_{i}=\ell^{\prime}_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq 3
. Assume
K\simeq K^{\prime}
, which is equivalent to
K_{(\ell_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}})\simeq K_{(\ell^{\prime}_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell^{\prime}_{2}})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell_{1}\ell_{3}})\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell^{\prime}_{1}\ell^{\prime}_{3}})
. Then, by Lemma
9.4
(2), one has

\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}=\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(K_{(\ell_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}}))=\mathop{\rm Ram}\nolimits(K_{(\ell^{\prime}_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell^{\prime}_{2}}))=\{\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{2}\}.

Moreover, the following hold:

\displaystyle|I_{\ell_{1}}(K_{(\ell_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})|=|I_{\ell^{\prime}_{1}}(K_{(\ell^{\prime}_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell^{\prime}_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})|=4,

\displaystyle|I_{\ell_{2}}(K_{(\ell_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})|=|I_{\ell^{\prime}_{2}}(K_{(\ell^{\prime}_{1})}(\sqrt{\ell_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})|=2.

Hence we must have
\ell_{1}=\ell^{\prime}_{1}
and
\ell_{2}=\ell^{\prime}_{2}
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell_{1}\ell_{3}})\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell^{\prime}_{1}\ell^{\prime}_{3}})
implies
\ell_{1}\ell_{3}=\ell^{\prime}_{1}\ell^{\prime}_{3}
since they are square-free integers. Combining this equality with
\ell_{1}=\ell^{\prime}_{1}
, we obtain
\ell_{3}=\ell^{\prime}_{3}
. This completes the proof.

Appendix A
Ono’s Conjecture on Tamagawa numbers of algebraic tori

Jianing Li and Chia-Fu Yu

Theorem A.1
.

For any global field
k
and any positive rational number
r
, there exists a
k
-torus
T
such that
\tau(T)=r
.

Proof.

We follow the idea of Ono. It suffices to construct for each prime
\ell
(a) a
k
-torus
T_{1}
with
\tau(T_{1})=\ell
and (b) a
k
-torus
T_{2}
with
\tau(T_{2})=\ell^{-2}
. Take a cyclic extension
K/k
of degree
\ell
with Galois group
G
and let
T_{1}=R_{K/k}^{(1)}\mathbb{G}_{{\rm m},K}
. Then
H^{1}(G,X(T_{1}))\simeq H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
and
\Sha^{2}(K/k,X(T))=0
by Chebotarev’s density theorem. Thus,
\tau(T_{1})=\ell
and (a) is done.
For (b), we take an abelian extension
K/k
with Galois group
G\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{4}
such that every decomposition group is cyclic. Take
T_{2}:=R_{K/k}^{(1)}\mathbb{G}_{{\rm m},K}
. Since every decomposition group of
G
is cyclic,
\Sha^{2}(G,X(T_{2}))=H^{2}(G,X(T_{2}))\simeq H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. By Lyndon’s formula
[
18
, Theorem 6]
,
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{6}
. On the other hand,
H^{1}(G,X(T))\simeq H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{4}
. Thus,
\tau(T_{2})=\ell^{-2}
and (b) is done. A construction of such an abelian extension
K/k
when
k
is a number field is given by Katayama
[
10
]
. For the function field case, a construction, which is more involved, is given in Theorem
A.2
.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
k
be a global function field of
char
p>0
. For any prime
\ell
and any positive integer
n
, there exists an abelian extension of
k
with Galois group
G\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{n}
in which every decomposition group is cyclic.

We shall use the cyclotomic function field and we recall the basic facts following from class field theory. For an explicit construction of these fields by Drinfeld modules, we refer to
[
28
, Chapter 12]
. Let
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
be the function field of the projective line over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}

and let
A={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}[t]
. In what follows
P,P_{i}
always denote monic irreducible polynomials of
A
. Let
\infty
denote the place of the infinite point and set
V_{\infty}=\langle t,1+t^{-1}{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}[[t^{-1}]]\rangle\subset k^{\times}_{\infty}={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}((t^{-1}))
.
For a monic polynomial
M=\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{n_{i}}_{i}\in A
, let
K(M)
be the finite abelian extension of
k
corresponding to
the following open subgroup (with finite index) of
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}

U(M)=k^{\times}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1+P^{n_{i}}_{i}O_{P_{i}})\times V_{\infty}\times\prod_{v\nmid M\infty}O^{\times}_{v}\right).

(The field
K(M)
is called the cyclotomic function field for
M
.) Thus, we have
\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}/U(M)\simeq\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K(M)/k)
via the Artin map. Clearly,
P
is unramified in
K(M)
if
P\nmid M
. The decomposition group of
\infty
in
K(M)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{p}
, which is cyclic. There is an isomorphism
(A/M)^{\times}\cong\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{k}/U(M)
induced by
P\mod M\mapsto(1,\cdots,P,\cdots,1)\mod U(M)
(
P
sitting on the place
P
) for
P\nmid M
, and
a\mod M\mapsto(1,\cdots,1,\cdots,a)\mod U(M)
for
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{p}\subset(A/M)^{\times}
(
a
sitting on the place
\infty
). So we have the following isomorphism which maps
P\mod M
(
P\nmid M
) to its Frobenius element in
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K(M)/k)

(A/M)^{\times}\simeq\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K(M)/k).

We will primarily be concerned with the fields
K(P)
and
K(P^{2})
, in which
P
is totally ramified.
If
\ell
divides
|(A/P)^{\times}|
, let
F(P)
denote the subfield of
K(P)
fixed by
((A/P)^{\times})^{\ell}
so that
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F(P)/k)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. If
a\in A/P
,
then
1+aP\bmod P^{2}\in(A/P^{2})^{\times}
is well defined and it generates the subgroup
H(a)=\{1+aiP\bmod P^{2}:i=0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
of order
p
when
a\neq 0
. We let
F(P,a)
denote the subfield of
K(P^{2})
fixed by
\{x\in(A/P^{2})^{\times}:x^{q-1}\in H(a)\}
where
q=p^{\deg P}=|A/P|
. This group is isomorphic to
H(a)\times(A/P)^{\times}
; since
(A/P^{2})^{\times}\simeq A/P\times(A/P)^{\times}
, we have
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F(P,a)/k)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\deg P-1}
when
a\neq 0
.

Proof.

We first prove the theorem when
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
and
\ell\neq p
. The argument of this case is entirely similar to the case of number fields given in
[
10
]
.
Choose
P_{1}
such that it splits in
k(\mu_{\ell})
where
\mu_{\ell}
is the group of
\ell
-th roots of unity. We inductively choose
P_{r}
(
1\leq r\leq n
) such that
P_{r}
splits completely in the composite field
k(\mu_{\ell},\sqrt[\ell]{P_{1}},\cdots,\sqrt[\ell]{P_{r-1}})F(P_{1})\cdots F(P_{r-1})
. Clearly, each
P_{i}
has infinitely many ways to choose by density theorems. Now let
K
be the composite field
F(P_{1})\cdots F(P_{n})
. The decomposition group of
\infty
in
K/k
is cyclic, since
K
is a subfield of the cyclotomic function field
K(P_{1}\cdots P_{r})
. Moreover, if
P
is not one of the
P_{i}
,
P
is unramified and hence its decomposition group is cyclic. Therefore, to show that
K/k
is the desired extension, it suffices to show that
P_{i}
splits in
F(P_{j})
whenever
i\neq j
. Assume
i<j
. Then
P_{j}
splits in
F(P_{i})
by the construction. Conversely, since
P_{j}
splits in
k(\sqrt[\ell]{P_{i}})
,
P_{i}
is an
\ell
-power in the completion
k_{P_{j}}
which implies that
P_{i}\in((A/P_{j})^{\times})^{\ell}
; hence
P_{i}
splits in
F(P_{j})
. This proves the theorem when
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
and
\ell\neq p
.

Assume next
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
and
\ell=p
. Take an arbitrary
P_{1}\in A
with
a_{1}=0\in A/P_{1}
. Let
k_{1}
be a subfield of
F(P_{1},a_{1})
with
[k_{1}:k]=p
. We inductively choose
(P_{r},a_{r},k_{r})

(1\leq r\leq n)
with
a_{r}\in A/P_{r}
such that
\deg P_{r}\geq r
and

P^{q_{i}-1}_{r}\equiv 1+a_{i}P_{i}\mod P^{2}_{i}\quad\text{ for }i=1,\cdots,r-1,\text{ where }q_{i}=|A/P_{i}|.

Let
a_{ri}\in A/P_{r}
(
i=1,\cdots,r-1
) such that

P^{q_{r}-1}_{i}\equiv 1+a_{ri}P_{r}\mod P^{2}_{r}\quad\text{ for }i=1,\cdots,r-1.

Let
a_{r}:=a_{r1}
, and let
k_{r}
be a subfield of
\cap{{}_{i=1}^{r-1}}F(P_{r},a_{ri})
with
[k_{r}:k]=p
. Put
K:=k_{1}\cdots k_{r}
and let us show that
K/k
is the desired extension. We have
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/k)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{n}
by considering the ramification. Assume
i<j
. Then
P_{j}
splits completely in
F(P_{i},a_{i})
by the first congruences and hence splits in

; conversely,
P_{i}
splits completely in

by the second congruences and hence also splits in its subfield

. It follows that the decomposition subgroup of
P_{i}
(

) in
K/k
is cyclic. This property also holds for the place
\infty
, since
K\subset K(P^{2}_{1}\cdots P^{2}_{n})
. This proves the theorem when
k={{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
and
\ell=p
.

Now assume that
k
is any finite extension of
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
and
\ell
is any prime. Choose
P_{1},\cdots,P_{n}
as above but we additionally require that
P_{i}
is unramified in

as above. Then
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(Kk/k)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Since each decomposition subgroup for
Kk/k
is a subgroup of that of
K/{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(t)
, the field
Kk
is the desired extension. This completes the proof of Theorem
A.2
.

Detecting Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering in non-Gaussian spin states from conditional spin-squeezing parameters
Jiajie Guo, Feng-Xiao Sun, Daoquan Zhu, Manuel Gessner, Qiongyi He, Matteo Fadel

Abstract
We present an experimentally practical method to reveal Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering in non-Gaussian spin states by exploiting a connection to quantum metrology. Our criterion is based on the quantum Fisher information, and uses bounds derived from generalized spin-squeezing parameters that involve measurements of higher-order moments. This leads us to introduce the concept of conditional spin-squeezing parameters, which quantify the metrological advantage provided by conditional states, as well as detect the presence of an EPR paradox.



Introduction.–
Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) steering was first termed by Schrödinger
Schrodinger1935
to describe the contradiction to local complementarity in the EPR paradox
EPR1935
. As an intermediate correlation, EPR steering is stronger than entanglement but not as general as Bell nonlocality
WisemanPRL2007
. Being easier to generate and detect than nonlocality renders EPR steering a valuable resource for a variety of quantum information tasks
ReidRMP2009
;
CavalcantiRPP2017
;
GuehneRMP2020
, such as quantum teleportation
QiongyiPRL2015
;
ChiuNPJQI2016
, one-side device independent QKD
BranciardPRA2012
;
GehringNC2015
;
WalkOpt2016
;
GallegoPRX2015
, quantum secret sharing
ArmstrongNP2015
;
XiangPRA2017
;
KogiasPRA2017
and assisted quantum metrology
YadinNC2021
.

Typically, EPR steering is revealed from the violation of a criterion based on a local uncertainty relation
ReidPRA1989
;
ReidRMP2009
. For this reason, such criteria are often expressed in terms of variances of linear operators, and therefore best suited to reveal steering in Gaussian states, where the correlations are fully described by first and second-order moments. Recently, non-Gaussian states were shown to have more competitive advantages in several quantum information protocols
GuoPRA2019
;
TakahashiNP2010
;
LeeNPJQI2019
;
MariPRL2012
. However, their nontrivial correlations appear in higher-order moments of physical operators, leading to the failure of steering criteria limited to linear observables. To detect non-Gaussian steering, some approaches have taken higher-order moments into account

WalbornPRL11
;
WalbornPRA13
. For example, nonlinear correlations in a three-photon down-conversion process with quadratic steerability index were considered in Ref.
ShenPRL2015
. A steering criterion derived from Hillery and Zubairy’s multimode entanglement criterion
HilleryPRL2006
has been investigated to detect steering in multipartite scenario
CavalcantiPRA2011
and further extended to a higher-order version in a two-well BEC ground state
QiongyiPRA2012
.
Nevertheless, these methods are specifically tailored to particular states, and a general steering criterion for non-Gaussian states is still highly desirable to further unlock their potential applications.

Nonclassical spin states are many-body quantum states of great interest for fundamental studies as well as for practical applications. For example, squeezed spin states have attracted increasing attention in quantum metrology for precision improvements to overcome the standard quantum limit and are nowadays routinely prepared in a variety of platforms, from solid state systems to atomic ensembles
PezzeRMP2018
. Recent studies have in particular explored the metrological potential of non-Gaussian spin states, both in theory
ManuelPRL2019
;
BaamaraArxiv
and experiment
StrobelScience2014
;
BohnetSCIENCE2016
;
EvrardPRL2019
;
XuArXiv2021
;
ColomboArXiv2021
.

Methods derived from quantum metrology
WinelandPRA1992
;
SorensenNL2001
;
SorensenPRL2001
;
PezzePRL2009
;
RenPRL2021
, already allow for the efficient detection of multiparticle entanglement without addressing individual spins. In particular, the quantum Fisher information (QFI) constitutes a powerful tool for capturing even strongly non-Gaussian features of quantum states by probing them for their sensitivity under small perturbations
StrobelScience2014
.
Very recently, the QFI was also used to formulate a criterion for EPR correlations
YadinNC2021
, thus providing us with a powerful method for detecting EPR steering in non-Gaussian states.

However, accessing the QFI is often challenging. Determining the QFI of arbitrary mixed states requires full knowledge of the quantum state. On the other hand, efficient approximations based on the full counting statistics demand that a carefully chosen observable is measured with high resolution, which is also difficult in multipartite systems. Spin-squeezing parameters
WinelandPRA1992
have proven to be efficient alternatives with high practical relevance, especially for Gaussian spin systems; suitable generalizations are also able to capture non-Gaussian features from higher-order moments
ManuelPRL2019
. But so far they have been limited to the detection of entanglement in a many-spin ensemble with collective measurements.

Here, we introduce the concept of conditional spin-squeezing parameters, and based on that we propose a practical and convenient witness for EPR steering in split nonclassical spin states. For the purpose of detecting non-Gaussian steering, spin observables involving higher-order moments are taken into consideration. An optimization of the measurement within these accessible higher-order observable ensembles leads to conditional nonlinear spin-squeezing parameters, whose potential to detect steering in a wider class of non-Gaussian states is explored. We demonstrate that conditional spin-squeezing parameters approximate the conditional QFI criterion
YadinNC2021
and as we increase the order of the measured moments this approximation ultimately converges to the QFI criterion. In addition, we also prove that the conditional spin squeezing parameters detect a larger class of steerable correlations than Reid’s criterion
ReidPRA1989
;
ReidRMP2009
. As a detailed study, we analyze their performance using analytical results for split one-axis-twisted states, where a hierarchy of criteria is clearly shown. Our work provides an experimentally practical tool to witness non-Gaussian steering, which helps to further investigate the quantum information of non-Gaussian spin states and paves a way to exploiting their promising potential.

Phase estimation and the spin-squeezing parameter.–

In a typical phase estimation protocol, a generator
H
imprints an unknown parameter
\theta
on quantum state
\rho
. An observable
M
is then measured on the probe state
\rho(\theta)
and an estimator
\theta_{\mathrm{est}}
for
\theta
is constructed as a function of the measurement results. This protocol is repeated
m
times and, for unbiased cases, the variance of the estimator
\text{Var}[\theta_{\mathrm{est}}]
represents the deviation of the estimate
\theta_{\mathrm{est}}
to the parameter
\theta
. A simple estimator known as the method of moments is constructed from the average value of
M
and yields, in the limit
m\gg 1
the phase uncertainty
\text{Var}[\theta_{\mathrm{est}}]=\chi^{2}[\rho,H,M]/m
, where

\chi^{2}[\rho,H,M]:=\dfrac{\text{Var}[\rho,M]}{\lvert\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho}\rvert^{2}}

(1)

is the spin-squeezing parameter
WinelandPRA1992
.

For an unbiased estimation, a fundamental limit to the sensitivity is given by the Cram
\acute{\text{e}}
r-Rao bound
\text{Var}[\theta_{\mathrm{est}}]\geq(mF[\rho,H,M])^{-1}
, where
F[\rho,H,M]
is the Fisher information (FI)
Helstrom1976
;
Holevo1982
. By optimizing over all observables
M
, the maximum value of the FI defines the quantum Fisher information (QFI),
i.e.
F_{Q}[\rho,H]=\max_{M}F[\rho,H,M]
, which determines the optimal sensitivity potential of the probe state
\rho

ParisIJQI2009
. In conclusion, we have
\chi^{-2}[\rho,H,M]\leq F[\rho,H,M]\leq F_{Q}[\rho,H]

BraunsteinPRL1994
;
PezzePRL2009
. For practical experiments, the achievable sensitivity can be optimized by maximizing
\chi^{-2}[\rho,H,M]
over a set of measurement operators
M
that can be realistically implemented
ManuelPRL2019
. Denoting with
\mathbf{X}
a basis for such measurements, we can achieve the maximal sensitivity
\max_{M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\chi^{-2}[\rho,H,M]
.

Assisted phase estimation with conditional squeezing parameter.–
In the assisted phase-estimation protocol
YadinNC2021
, Bob’s estimation of
\theta
is improved by communication from Alice about her measurement setting and result,
Y
and
b
. This information allows Bob to choose a measurement observable
M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})
that is optimally tailored to the conditional state
\rho^{B}_{b|Y}
. This way, Bob can achieve on average an estimation sensitivity given by the conditional spin-squeezing parameter
footnote

(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]:=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\max_{M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\chi^{-2}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H,M]\;.

(2)

Here, we introduced the definition of assemblages
\mathcal{A}(b,Y)=p(b|Y)\rho_{b|Y}^{B}
, which are determined by the local probability distribution
p(b|Y)
for results
b
conditioned on Alice’s measurement observable
Y
and Bob’s conditional state
\rho_{b|Y}^{B}
.
Note that the ultimate limit in phase estimation for a specific measurement
Y
for Alice is expressed by the conditional Fisher information
YadinNC2021

F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]:=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)F_{Q}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H]\;,

(3)

In fact, we can obtain a chain of inequalities (see Supplementary Information Sec. IA)

F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]\geq\,(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]\geq\frac{\lvert\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho^{B}}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M,Y]},

(4)

which results in a hierarchy of EPR steering criteria that will be discussed below.

Connection to EPR steering.–
Besides the estimation of the phase
\theta
, we could be interested in estimating its generator
H
. As
\theta
and
H
are conjugate variables, the uncertainty principle prevents their simultaneous knowledge with arbitrary precision
BraunsteinAnnPhys1996
. By making use of EPR steering from Alice to Bob in the assisted metrology protocol, an inference of these properties can be realized below the local uncertainty limit, which is given by
\text{Var}[\theta_{\text{est}}]\text{Var}[H_{\text{est}}]\geq(4m)^{-1}

YadinNC2021
. Here,
\text{Var}[\theta_{\text{est}}]
and
\text{Var}[H_{\text{est}}]
are inference variances. Based on the additional information of Alice’s measurement setting
X
and result
a
, Bob uses the estimator
h_{\text{est}}(a)
to predict the result
h
of his local measurement
H
with the inference variance
\text{Var}[H_{\text{est}}]:=\sum_{a,h}p(a,h|X,H)(h_{\text{est}}(a)-h)^{2}
. A lower bound for
\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]
is given by the conditional variance
ReidRMP2009

\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]:=\sum_{a}p(a|X)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{a|X},H]\;.

(5)

Analogously, we can obtain
\text{Var}[\theta_{\text{est}}]
with a different choice for Alice’s measurement settings (denoted by
Y
) and Bob’s measurement
M
. The uncertainty bound is a witness of EPR steering, whose violation implies that Alice’s measurements can steer Bob’s states to overcome Bob’s local phase-generator uncertainty relation.

Consider now a local hidden state (LHS) model
WisemanPRL2007
, described by a classical random variable
\lambda
with probability distribution
p(\lambda)
. The assemblage in this case can be written as
\mathcal{A}(a,X)=\sum_{a,\lambda}p(a|X,\lambda)p(\lambda)\sigma_{\lambda}^{B}
. In Ref.
YadinNC2021
, a steering criterion based on the QFI is proposed. For any LHS model, it holds

\Delta_{1}:=F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;,

(6)

independently of the choices of Alice’s measurement.

As the conditional squeezing parameter provides a lower bound to the conditional FI (
4
), this allows us to formulate the following steering criterion: For any assemblage
\mathcal{A}
that admits a LHS model, we have

\Delta_{2}:=(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;.

(7)

This criterion is one of the main results of this work. The violation of (
7
) reveals useful EPR steering in the assisted metrological protocol.

Reduction to Reid’s criterion.–

From the previous chain of inequalities (
4
), it is immediate to recover Reid’s criterion
ReidPRA1989
;
ReidRMP2009
(here expressed in a linearized form)

\Delta_{3}:=\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M,Y]}-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;.

(8)

As defined in Eq. (
5
), the conditional variance represents the average of individual variances for Bob’s conditional states, determined as the minimized inference variance
ReidRMP2009
. For linear observables, Reid’s criterion is very powerful for Gaussian states; in a continuous variable setting, it can be shown to be necessary and sufficient for steering detection by Gaussian measurements
WisemanPRL2007
, while it may fail to detect steering in non-Gaussian cases. Because of the right-hand side in Eq. (
4
), we obtain
\Delta_{2}\geq\Delta_{3}
, that means the criterion
\Delta_{2}
we proposed contains all LHS models based on Reid’s uncertainty relations. A crucial advantage of
\Delta_{2}
over Reid’s criterion
\Delta_{3}
is the possibility to adapt the measurement observable
M
to each conditional state
\rho_{b|Y}
individually [see the maximization in Eq. (
2
)], while in (
8
) only a single
M
is used for the entire assemblage. As we will see below, this leads in particular to an increased potential to reveal non-Gaussian EPR steering in a wider class of states, especially when the set
\mathbf{X}
contains higher-order moments of the collective spins.

Reduction to linear-estimate Reid’s criterion.–

If Bob’s estimator
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)
depends linearly on Alice’s measurement result
a
and takes the form
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)=ga+d
, optimal estimates are obtained by minimizing the inference variance
\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]
. Based on that, a well-known linear-estimate Reid’s criterion commonly used in experiments is

\displaystyle\Delta_{4}:=\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{\text{Var}[Y+g^{\prime}M]}-4\text{Var}[X-gH]\leq 0\;.

(9)

Note that in a Gaussian system, where quantum correlations are well characterized with first and second-order moments, the best estimator equals the optimized linear estimator
ReidRMP2009
. In this case, we have
\text{Var}[X-gH]=\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]
, which leads to
\Delta_{3}=\Delta_{4}
for Gaussian states.

We define the maximum value of the left-hand side of the above criteria as
\delta_{i}=\max_{H\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{H}),M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\Delta_{i}
, respectively (see Supplementary Information Sec. IB,IIB,IIIC,IIID). As a result, a hierarchy of criteria reads

\displaystyle\delta_{1}\geq\delta_{2}\geq\delta_{3}\geq\delta_{4}\;.

(10)

In the following, we will compare these criteria for a scenario of experimental relevance.

Split spin-squeezed state.–
Squeezed states play a key role in measurement sensitivity enhancement, overcoming the standard quantum limit in quantum metrology

WinelandPRA1992
;
PezzeRMP2018
;

Initially, the atomic ensemble is in a coherent spin state, which consists of
N

can be parametrized by

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{A}\rangle}_{N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}\;.

The properties of split spin states can be characterized by local collective spin observables, that is

The sharpest formulations of the above criteria are obtained by optimizing the measurement observables

Conclusions.–
We have proposed a non-Gaussian steering criterion based on conditional spin squeezing parameters. By introducing nonlinear operators and optimizing measurement within accessible higher-order observables, the criterion shows an improved ability to reveal EPR steering with a larger range of non-Gaussian states. This approach is related by a hierarchy to other criteria such as Reid’s criteria and metrological complementarity based on the QFI. The key advantage of metrology-based steering criteria is the ability to adjust the measurement observable to each conditional state individually. Our steering criterion is experimentally feasible and constitutes a general method to reveal non-Gaussian steering in a bipartite scenario. This work provides a powerful approach to further investigate nonlinear EPR correlations in non-Gaussian states and takes a step further to unlock the promising applications for non-Gaussian systems.

I. Fisher criterion

II.1
A. Definition: Fisher criterion

As proposed in
YadinNC2021
, a formulation of an EPR criterion can be derived from the
quantum conditional variance

\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]:=\min_{X}\,\sum_{a}p(a|X)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{a|X},H]\;.

(14)

and the
quantum conditional Fisher information

\displaystyle F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]

\displaystyle:=\max_{Y}\,\sum_{b}p(b|Y)F_{Q}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H]\;.

(15)

For any assemblage
\mathcal{A}
that admits a LHS model, the following bound holds:

\displaystyle F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\leq 4\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\;.

(16)

Also here, note that the quantum conditional variance (
14
) and the quantum conditional Fisher information (
15
) are defined as an optimisation over Alice’s measurement. For simplicity, however, we always compute a conditional Fisher Information by choosing a specific measurement
Y
for Alice

F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]:=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)F_{Q}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H]\;,

(17)

and also identify the conditional variance as calculated based on a specific measurement
X
for Alice with the symbol

\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]:=\sum_{a}p(a|X)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{a|X},H]\;,

(18)

In summary, we then have
F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\geq F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]
and
\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\leq\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]
. So after choosing specific measurement setting for Alice, the Fisher criterion (
16
) can be written as:

\Delta_{1}:=F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;,

(19)

its violation indicates EPR steering from Alice to Bob.

II.2
B. Calculation: Fisher criterion

Note that in our case, the split spin state is a pure global state and therefore the conditional states
\rho^{B}_{a|X}
of Bob will be pure, too, i.e.,
\rho^{B}_{a|X}=\mathinner{\lvert\phi^{B}_{a|X}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle\phi^{B}_{a|X}\rvert}
. Using
F_{Q}[|\psi\rangle,H]=4\text{Var}[|\psi\rangle,H]

BraunsteinPRL1994
(for simplicity we write
F_{Q}[|\psi\rangle,H]
instead of
F_{Q}[|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,H]
for pure states and similarly for the variance), we obtain from (
17
) that

F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]=4\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\text{Var}[\mathinner{\lvert\phi^{B}_{a|X}\rangle},H]=4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]\;.

(20)

Decomposing the Hamiltonian
H
in terms of a vector of observables
\mathbf{H}
as
H=\mathbf{n}^{T}\mathbf{H}
with real coefficient vector
\mathbf{n}
, the conditional variance can be written as

\displaystyle\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]=\mathbf{n}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},Y]\mathbf{n}\;,

(21)

where we defined the conditional covariance matrices by introducing

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}[\rho,\mathbf{H}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle H_{i}H_{j}+H_{j}H_{i}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle H_{i}\rangle_{\rho}\langle H_{j}\rangle_{\rho}\;,

(22)

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},Y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\bm{\Gamma}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\mathbf{H}]\;.

(23)

And analogously, we also have
\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]=\mathbf{n}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X]\mathbf{n}
, which allows us to optimize the witness over the Hamiltonian
H
analytically:

\displaystyle\max_{H\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{H})}\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=4\max_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]-\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}(\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},Y]-\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X]).

(24)

Since the squeezing direction lies in the yz-plane, we also restrict Alice’s measurement direction to this plane, and represent it by the angle
\phi
on the yz-plane:
\cos(\phi)\textbf{y}+\sin(\phi)\textbf{z}
. We label the measurement direction
\phi_{X}
for
X
,
\phi_{Y}
for
Y
. After optimizing also over Alice’s measurements, we thus obtain the optimized witness

\displaystyle\delta_{1}:=4\max_{\phi_{X},\phi_{Y}}\,\lambda_{\max}(\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},Y]-\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X])\;.

(25)

For the comparison of the hierarchy (
4
), we limit the optimization to the first term, i.e.,
4\max_{\phi_{Y}}\lambda_{\max}(\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},Y])
.

II.3
C. A chain of inequalities

It holds for arbitrary measurements
M
that
PezzePRL2009

\displaystyle F_{Q}[\rho,H]\geq\frac{\lvert\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}[\rho,M]}\;.

(26)

In analogy to results in
YadinNC2021
, based on the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definitions of the conditional Fisher information and the conditional variance, we obtain the chain of inequalities

\displaystyle F^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]

\displaystyle\geq\,\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\frac{\lvert\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho^{B}_{b|Y}}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},M]}

\displaystyle\geq\,\frac{\lvert\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho^{B}_{b|Y}}\rvert^{2}}{\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},M]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho^{B}}\rvert^{2}}{\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},M]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert\mathinner{\langle[H,M]\rangle}_{\rho^{B}}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M,Y]}.

(27)

We will use this result to lower bound the Fisher criterion when using specific measurements for Alice. Note that this relation (
II.3
) leads to the hierarchy of criteria.

III
II. Conditional spin-squeezing parameter based criterion

III.1
A. Definition: Conditional spin-squeezing parameter based criterion

Rather than using the QFI, which corresponds to the maximal sensitivity achievable with an optimal estimation strategy and observable, we can quantify Bob’s sensitivity assuming the estimation of the phase parameter with the method of moments based on the data obtained from a specific collective spin observable. In this case, we arrive at the quantum conditional spin-squeezing parameter (where we consider the same measurement
M
for different results of Alice, even if in full generality one can have
M_{a|X}
)

(\chi^{-2}_{Q})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X}]:=\max_{Y}\,\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\max_{M\in\mathrm{span(\mathbf{X})}}\,\chi^{-2}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H,M]\;,

(28)

where the (generalized) spin-squeezing parameter for rotations generated by
H
, and measurements of
M
, is defined as

\chi^{2}[\rho,H,M]:=\dfrac{\text{Var}[\rho,M]}{\lvert\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho}\rvert^{2}}\;.

(29)

The bound
F_{Q}[\rho,H]\geq\chi^{-2}[\rho,H,M]
for all
M
implies that

\displaystyle F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\geq(\chi^{-2}_{Q})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X}]\;.

(30)

This can be used to introduce a steering criterion weaker than the Fisher criterion but in general stronger than the Reid’s criterion, namely

(\chi^{-2}_{Q})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X}]\leq 4\text{Var}^{B|A}_{Q}[\mathcal{A},H]\;.

(31)

For a specific choice of the measurement
Y
, we define conditional spin-squeezing parameters

(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]:=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\max_{M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\,\chi^{-2}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H,M]\;,

(32)

such that, according to the first line in Eq. (
II.3
) we have

\displaystyle F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,Y]\geq(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]\;.

(33)

This can be used to introduce the conditional spin-squeezing parameter based steering criterion

\Delta_{2}:=(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;.

(34)

Furthermore, the criterion
\Delta_{2}
can be extended to a higher-order version
\Delta_{2}^{(n)}
, if nonlinear measurement settings
\textbf{X}^{(n)}
are taken into consideration

\Delta_{2}^{(n)}:=(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X}^{(n)},Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;.

(35)

With higher-order measurement settings,
\Delta_{2}^{(n)}
will get closer to the Fisher criterion
\Delta_{1}
, and will be eventually equal to
\Delta_{1}
when
\mathbf{X}
becomes a complete basis of operators
ManuelPRL2019
.

III.2
B. Calculation: Conditional spin-squeezing parameter based criterion

Expanding

\displaystyle\chi^{-2}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H,M]=\frac{\lvert\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}_{b|Y}}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},M]}=\frac{|\mathbf{n}^{T}\mathbf{C}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\mathbf{m}|^{2}}{\mathbf{m}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\mathbf{X}]\mathbf{m}}\;,

(36)

where
\textbf{C}[\rho,\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]
is the commutator matrix with elements
(\mathbf{C}[\rho,\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}])_{ij}=-i\langle[X_{j},H_{i}]\rangle_{\rho}

\bm{\Gamma}[\rho,\mathbf{X}]

(\bm{\Gamma}[\rho,\mathbf{X}])_{ij}=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j})_{\rho}

\displaystyle\max_{M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\,\chi^{-2}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},H,M]=\max_{M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\,\dfrac{\lvert\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}_{b|Y}}\rvert^{2}}{\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},M]}=\textbf{n}^{T}\textbf{M}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\textbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\textbf{n}\;,

(37)

Here, the moment matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}[\rho,\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]:=\textbf{C}^{T}[\rho,\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}[\rho,\mathbf{X}]\mathbf{C}[\rho,\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\;,

(38)

\displaystyle(\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\left(\textbf{n}^{T}\textbf{M}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\textbf{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\textbf{n}^{T}\textbf{M}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},Y]\textbf{n}\;,

where we introduced the conditional moment matrix

\displaystyle\textbf{M}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},Y]=\sum_{b}p(b|Y)\textbf{M}[\rho^{B}_{b|Y},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X}]\;.

(40)

Next, we choose an optimal Hamiltonian

to maximize the steering witness. Together with (

\displaystyle\max_{H\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{H}),M\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{X})}\,\left((\chi^{-2})^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,\mathbf{X},Y]-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\textbf{n}^{T}\textbf{M}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},Y]\textbf{n}-4\mathbf{n}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X]\mathbf{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}(\textbf{M}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},Y]-4\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X])\;.

(41)

At last we choose optimal measurements for Alice. In analogy to our previous derivation we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{2}:=\max_{\phi_{X},\phi_{Y}}\,\lambda_{\max}(\textbf{M}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},Y]-4\bm{\Gamma}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},\mathbf{H},X])\;.

(42)

For Alice, the optimal measurement direction

can be found by searching in the yz-plane (

Note that here we optimize

III. Reid’s criterion

IV.1
A. Definition: Reid’s criterion

In Reid’s criterion
ReidRMP2009
, based on the information of Alice’s measurement
X
and result
a
, Bob chooses an estimator
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)
to predict the value and measures
H
to yield the result
h
. The average deviation is called inference variance

\displaystyle\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]:=\sum_{a,h}p(a,h|X,H)(h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)-h)^{2}\;,

(44)

where
p(a,h|X,H)
is the joint probability. The other inference variance
\text{Var}[M_{\mathrm{est}}]
can be obtained analogously by measurement settings
Y
and
M
.

The sharpest formulation of Eq. (
44
) is thus obtained by minimizing the estimation error. The optimal estimator
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}\{\rho^{B}_{a|X}H\}
attains the lower bound
\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]\geq\sum_{a}p(a|X)\text{Var}[\rho^{B}_{a|X},H]
, where
\text{Var}[\rho,H]=\langle H^{2}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle H\rangle_{\rho}^{2}
is the variance with
\langle O\rangle_{\rho}=\mathrm{Tr}\{\rho O\}
.
Optimising over Alice’s measurement setting
X
leads to the quantum conditional variance
\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]
(
14
), and any fixed choice of
X
yields its upper bound
\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]
(
18
).
In summary, we then have (for any
H_{\mathrm{est}}
)

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\leq\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]\;.

(45)

Based on the quantum conditional variance, Reid’s criterion for an EPR paradox consists of a violation of the local uncertainty limit

\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H]\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M]\geq\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{4}\;.

(46)

If we chose specific measurement settings
X
,
Y
for Alice, we have

\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M,Y]\geq\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{4}\;,

(47)

this can be written in the linearised form as

\Delta_{3}:=\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},M,Y]}-4\text{Var}^{B|A}[\mathcal{A},H,X]\leq 0\;.

(48)

The inequality
\Delta_{3}\leq 0
holds for all non-steerable assemblages, i.e., those that admit an LHS model, thus steering is detected by a violation of this inequality.

In the simplest situations, in particular for Gaussian states, it is optimal to consider a linear estimator, i.e.,
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)=ga+d
, This yields an extensively used linear-estimate Reid’s criterion
ReidPRA1989

\Delta_{4}:=\dfrac{|\langle[H,M]\rangle_{\rho^{B}}|^{2}}{\text{Var}[Y+g^{\prime}M]}-4\text{Var}[X-gH]\leq 0\;.

(49)

IV.2
B. Discussion on estimators and variances in Reid’s criterion.

In this section, we will distinguish between MSE (mean squared error) and the variance. We consider the remote estimation of measurement results
h
performed on Bob’s system from the results
a
that are acquired from local measurements on Alice’s system. For each estimator
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)
we define the mean squared error

\displaystyle\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]:=\sum_{a,h}p(a,h|X,H)\left(h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)-h\right)^{2}\;,

(50)

and the variance

\displaystyle\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]:=\sum_{a,h}p(a,h|X,H)\left(h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)-\langle h_{\mathrm{est}}\rangle\right)^{2}\;,

(51)

where
\langle h_{\mathrm{est}}\rangle=\sum_{a}p(a|X)h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)
is the average of the estimator. For unbiased estimators, we have
\langle h_{\mathrm{est}}\rangle=h
and
\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]=\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]
. In general we have the relation

\displaystyle\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]

\displaystyle=\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]+\langle H_{\mathrm{est}}\rangle^{2}-2\langle H_{\mathrm{est}}H\rangle+\langle H^{2}\rangle\;.

(52)

Let us now focus on a linear estimator constructed from the eigenvalues
a
of Alice’s measurement observable
X
for the estimation of the result
h
of Bob’s measurement of
H
. We thus take
h_{\mathrm{est}}(a)=ga
, where
g\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. We obtain

\displaystyle\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]=\langle(gX-H)^{2}\rangle_{\rho^{AB}}\;,

(53)

and

\displaystyle\text{Var}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,h}p(a,h|X,H)\left(ga-g\langle X\rangle\right)^{2}=\text{Var}[\rho^{A},gX]\;,

(54)

where
\langle h_{\mathrm{est}}\rangle=g\langle X\rangle
.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\text{Var}[\rho^{AB},gX-H]\leq\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]\;,

(55)

with equality when
g\langle X\rangle=\langle H\rangle
.

Specifically, we focus on the observables
H=S^{B}_{y}
and
M=S^{B}_{z}
and we denote by
y^{A/B}
and
z^{A/B}
the measurement results, i.e., the eigenvalues of
S^{A/B}_{y}
and
S^{A/B}_{z}
. We estimate Bob’s result
z^{B}
for
S^{B}_{z}
from Alice’s result
z^{A}
for
S^{A}_{z}
using the linear estimator
z^{B}_{\mathrm{est}}(z^{A})=-g_{z}z^{A}
. This will lead to

\displaystyle\text{MSE}[H_{\mathrm{est}}]=\langle(g_{z}S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B})^{2}\rangle_{\rho^{AB}}\;,

(56)

and if
\langle g_{z}S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{AB}}=0
, i.e., if the estimator is unbiased, this coincides with
\text{Var}[\rho^{AB},g_{z}S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}]
.

In our cases, since the squeezing direction is on the yz-plane, the spin expectation for reduced states is

\displaystyle\langle S_{y}\rangle_{\rho^{A(B)}}=\langle S_{z}\rangle_{\rho^{A(B)}}=0\;.

(57)

so we have

\displaystyle\langle g_{z}S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{AB}}=g_{z}\langle S_{z}^{A}\rangle_{\rho^{A}}+\langle S_{z}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{B}}=0\;.

(58)

Similarly, we also have
\langle g_{y}S_{y}^{A}+S_{y}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{AB}}=0
. So it is proved that the estimator is unbiased in our case.

IV.3
C. Calculation: general Reid’s criteiron.

The general Reid’s criterion

(63)

V.1
A. EPR criterion

Let us consider a measurement of
S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}
by Alice, which yields the results
N_{A}
(the number of particles in Alice side) and
l_{A}
(the result of the spin measurement along the chosen direction). Let
\mathinner{\lvert l_{A}\rangle}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}
denote the corresponding eigenstates of
S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}
in the spin-
N_{A}/2
subspace, such that we can write

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}=\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\left(l_{A}-\frac{N_{A}}{2}\right)\mathinner{\lvert l_{A}\rangle}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle l_{A}\rvert}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}.

(70)

The event of obtaining measurement results
(l_{A},N_{A})
for Alice occurs with probability

\displaystyle p(l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A})=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}\sqrt{\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k^{\prime}_{A}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}e^{i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k^{\prime}_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\langle l_{A}\rvert}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{A}\rangle}_{N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle k^{\prime}_{A}\rvert}_{N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert l_{A}\rangle}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}

(71)

when
0\leq l_{A}\leq N_{A}
and zero otherwise,
and produces the conditional states

\displaystyle\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}}=\frac{p(l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A})^{-1/2}}{2^{N}}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\langle l_{A}\rvert}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{A}\rangle}_{N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}.

(72)

for Bob’s system.

Let us consider a phase shift generated on Bob’s subsystem by the observable
S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}
. Writing

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}=\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)\mathinner{\lvert l_{B}\rangle}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}},

(73)

we obtain the following first and second moments for the conditional states

\displaystyle\langle S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}\rangle_{l_{A},N_{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)|\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}}|^{2},

(74)

\displaystyle\langle(S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B})^{2}\rangle_{l_{A},N_{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)^{2}|\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}}|^{2},

(75)

with

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}}=\frac{p(l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A})^{-1/2}}{2^{N}}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\langle l_{A}\rvert}_{\mathbf{n},N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{A}\rangle}_{N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}.

(76)

This allows us to obtain the variance of the conditional states

\displaystyle\text{Var}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]=\langle(S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B})^{2}\rangle_{l_{A},N_{A}}-\langle S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}\rangle_{l_{A},N_{A}}^{2},

(77)

and an upper bound for the quantum conditional variance

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)\rangle},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]\leq\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}p(l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A})\text{Var}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}].

(78)

Since the conditional states are pure, we have

\displaystyle F_{Q}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]=4\text{Var}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]

(79)

and we immediately obtain also a lower bound for the conditional quantum Fisher information as

\displaystyle F_{Q}^{B|A}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)\rangle},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]\geq 4\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}p(l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A})\text{Var}[\mathinner{\lvert\Phi(\mu)^{B}\rangle}_{l_{A},N_{A}|S_{\mathbf{n}}^{A}},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}].

(80)

V.2
B. Reduced quantum Fisher information and variance

The reduced state for Bob’s system reads

\displaystyle\rho^{B}=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k^{\prime}_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}e^{i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k^{\prime}_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle k^{\prime}_{B}\rvert}_{N-N_{A}}.

(81)

This yields first and second moments as

\displaystyle\langle S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{B}}=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}

\displaystyle\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k^{\prime}_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}e^{i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k^{\prime}_{B})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle k^{\prime}_{B}\rvert}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert l_{B}\rangle}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}

(82)

and

\displaystyle\langle(S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B})^{2}\rangle_{\rho^{B}}=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}

\displaystyle\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k^{\prime}_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}e^{i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k^{\prime}_{B})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)^{2}\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle k^{\prime}_{B}\rvert}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert l_{B}\rangle}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}},

(83)

leading to the variance

\displaystyle\text{Var}[\rho^{B},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]=\langle(S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B})^{2}\rangle_{\rho^{B}}-\langle S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}\rangle_{\rho^{B}}^{2}.

(84)

To determine the quantum Fisher information, we must diagonalize the state
\rho^{B}
:

\displaystyle\rho^{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}\binom{N-N_{A}}{k^{\prime}_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}e^{i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k^{\prime}_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\langle k^{\prime}_{B}\rvert}_{N-N_{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}\frac{1}{2^{N_{A}}}\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rangle}\mathinner{\langle\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{k_{A}=0}^{N_{A}}p(N_{A},k_{A})\mathinner{\lvert\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rangle}\mathinner{\langle\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rvert},

(85)

where the eigenstates read

\displaystyle\mathinner{\lvert\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N-N_{A}}}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}},

(86)

and the corresponding eigenvalues are

\displaystyle p(N_{A},k_{A})=\frac{1}{2^{N+N_{A}}}\binom{N}{N_{A}}\binom{N_{A}}{k_{A}}.

(87)

We can now use the expression
F_{Q}[\rho^{B},S_{z}^{B}]=2\sum_{i,j}\frac{(p_{i}-p_{j})^{2}}{p_{i}+p_{j}}|\langle\psi_{i}|S_{z}^{B}|\psi_{j}\rangle|^{2}
to write

\displaystyle F_{Q}[\rho^{B},S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{k_{A},k_{A}^{\prime}=0}^{N_{A}}\frac{(p(N_{A},k_{A})-p(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A}))^{2}}{p(N_{A},k_{A})+p(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A})}\left|\mathinner{\langle\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rvert}S_{\mathbf{m}}^{B}\mathinner{\lvert\Psi(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A})\rangle}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{l_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sum_{k_{A},k_{A}^{\prime}=0}^{N_{A}}\frac{(p(N_{A},k_{A})-p(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A}))^{2}}{p(N_{A},k_{A})+p(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A})}\left(l_{B}-\frac{N-N_{A}}{2}\right)^{2}|\langle\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})|l_{B}\rangle_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}|^{2}|\langle\Psi(N_{A},k^{\prime}_{A})|l_{B}\rangle_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}|^{2},

(88)

with

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert\Psi(N_{A},k_{A})\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{N-N_{A}}}}\sum_{k_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}\sqrt{\binom{N-N_{A}}{k_{B}}}e^{-i\frac{\mu}{2}(N/2-k_{A}-k_{B})^{2}}\mathinner{\langle l_{B}\rvert}_{\mathbf{m},N-N_{A}}\mathinner{\lvert k_{B}\rangle}_{N-N_{A}}.

(89)

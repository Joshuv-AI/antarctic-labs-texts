Abstract:
In this paper, we study the Dirichlet problem of Hessian quotient equations in exterior domains. By estimating the eigenvalues of the solution, the necessary and sufficient conditions on existence of radial solutions are obtained. Applying the solutions of ODE, the viscosity subsolutions and supersolutions are constructed and then the existence of viscosity solutions for exterior problems is established by the Perron’s method.

\sigma_{k}(a)=\sigma_{k;i}(a)+a_{i}\sigma_{k-1;i}(a)
for all
i,
we get that

\displaystyle\sigma_{l-1;i}(a)a_{i}-\sigma_{l-1;j}(a)a_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)+\sigma_{l-2;ij}(a)a_{j}]a_{i}-[\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)+\sigma_{l-2;ij}(a)a_{i}]a_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)(a_{i}-a_{j}).

Therefore (
3.8
) becomes

\overline{g}(r)J(r)^{l-1}\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)(a_{i}-a_{j})=J(r)^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)(a_{i}-a_{j}).

If
k=n
, then
\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)=0
for any
i\not=j
. But
\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)>0
, so we get that

a_{1}=\dots=a_{n}=\hat{a}.

If
1\leq l<k\leq n-1
, then
\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)>0
and
\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)>0
. Suppose on the contrary that
a_{i}\not=a_{j}
, then

\overline{g}(r)J(r)^{l-1}\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)=J(r)^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a).

Thus

\frac{\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)}{\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)}=\frac{\overline{g}(r)J(r)^{l-1}}{J(r)^{k-1}}=\overline{g}(r)J(r)^{l-k}.

(3.9)

Since the left side is independent of
r
, then
\overline{g}(r)J(r)^{l-k}
is a constant
c_{0}>0.
So
\overline{g}(r)=c_{0}J(r)^{k-l}.
Substituting into (
3.7
), we have that

\frac{J(r)(1-c_{0})}{c_{0}J^{\prime}(r)}=\frac{\sigma_{l-1;i}(a)a_{i}-\sigma_{k-1;i}(a)a_{i}}{\sigma_{k}(a)}.

(3.10)

Since the left side of the above equality is independent of
i
, then for any
i\not=j
,

\sigma_{l-1;i}(a)a_{i}-\sigma_{k-1;i}(a)a_{i}=\sigma_{l-1;j}(a)a_{j}-\sigma_{k-1;j}(a)a_{j},

so

\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)(a_{i}-a_{j})=\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)(a_{i}-a_{j}).

However
a_{i}\not=a_{j}
, thus
\sigma_{l-1;ij}(a)=\sigma_{k-1;ij}(a)
. Therefore by (
3.9
), we can have
c_{0}=1
. Then by (
3.10
), we can get that for all
i
,

\sigma_{l-1;i}(a)a_{i}=\sigma_{k-1;i}(a)a_{i}.

Recalling the equality

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\sigma_{k-1;i}(a)=k\sigma_{k}(a),

we know that
k\sigma_{k}(a)=l\sigma_{l}(a)
. Since
A\in\mathcal{A}_{k,l}
, then
\sigma_{k}(a)=\sigma_{l}(a)
, so
k=l
. This is a contradiction.
∎

So in virtue of Proposition
3.2
, we can only find the generalized subsolutions of (
1.1
). We will construct the generalized symmetric subsolution
W(x)=w(r)
of (
1.1
). First we discuss the function
h(r)
which actually equals to
w^{\prime}(r)/r
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0\leq l<k\leq n,n\geq 3,A\in\mathcal{A}_{k,l},a:=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}):=\lambda(A),0<a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dots\leq a_{n}
and
\delta>\sup_{r\in[1,+\infty)}\overline{g}^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(r).
Then the problem

\begin{cases}\dfrac{h(r)^{k}+\overline{t}_{k}rh(r)^{k-1}h^{\prime}(r)}{h(r)^{l}+\underline{t}_{l}rh(r)^{l-1}h^{\prime}(r)}=\overline{g}(r),\ r>1,\\
h(1)=\delta,\\
h(r)^{k}+\overline{t}_{k}rh(r)^{k-1}h^{\prime}(r)>0,\end{cases}

(3.11)

has a smooth solution
h(r)=h(r,\delta)
on
[1,+\infty)
satisfying

(i)

\overline{g}^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(r)\leq h(r,\delta)\leq\delta,
and
\partial_{r}h(r,\delta)\leq 0
.

(ii)

h(r,\delta)
is continuous and strictly increasing in
\delta
and

\lim_{\delta\to+\infty}h(r,\delta)=+\infty,\ \forall\ r\geq 1.

Proof.

For brevity, sometimes we write
h(r)
or
h(r,\delta)
,
\overline{t}_{k}(a)
or
\overline{t}_{k}
and
\underline{t}_{l}(a)
or
\underline{t}_{l}
, when there is no confusion. Due to (
3.11
), we have

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{d}h}{\mbox{d}r}=-\frac{1}{r}\dfrac{h}{\overline{t}_{k}}\dfrac{h(r)^{k-l}-\overline{g}(r)}{h(r)^{k-l}-\overline{g}(r)\frac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}},\ r>1,\\
h(1)=\delta.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Since
\delta>\sup_{r\in[0,+\infty)}\overline{g}^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(r)
and
\underline{t}_{l}/\overline{t}_{k}<1
, then by the existence, uniqueness and extension theorem for the solution of the initial value problem of the ODE, we obtain that the problem (
3.11
) has a smooth solution
h(r,\delta)
such that
\overline{g}^{\frac{1}{k-l}}(r)\leq h(r,\delta)\leq\delta,
and
\partial_{r}h(r,\delta)\leq 0
. Then assertion (i) of the lemma is proved. In addition, the existence of solution can be proved by the following.

By (
3.11
), we can know that

\dfrac{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-1}(h(r)^{k}+\overline{t}_{k}rh(r)^{k-1}h^{\prime}(r))}{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}-1}(h(r)^{l}+\underline{t}_{l}rh(r)^{l-1}h^{\prime}(r))}=\dfrac{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-1}}{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}-1}}\overline{g}(r),\ r>1.

That is

\dfrac{(r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r))^{\prime}}{(r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r))^{\prime}}=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(r).

So

(r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r))^{\prime}=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(r)(r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r))^{\prime}.

Integrating the above equality from
1
to
r
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}(s^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(s))^{\prime}ds=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}s^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(s)(s^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(s))^{\prime}ds.

Then

r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\delta^{k}=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}s^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(s)(s^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(s))^{\prime}ds.

(3.13)

Since
h(r)^{k}+\overline{t}_{k}rh(r)^{k-1}h^{\prime}(r)>0
, then
h(r)^{l}+\underline{t}_{l}rh(r)^{l-1}h^{\prime}(r)>0
, so also
(r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r))^{\prime}>0.
According to the mean value theorem of integrals, we have that there exists
1\leq\theta_{1}\leq r
such that

r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\delta^{k}=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}(s^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(s))^{\prime}ds,

i.e.,

r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\delta^{k}=\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})[r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)-\delta^{l}].

As a result,

r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\delta^{k}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)+\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\delta^{l}=0.

(3.14)

Denote the left side of the above equality as
F(h,\delta,\theta_{1},r)
, that is,

F(h,\delta,\theta_{1},r)=r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\delta^{k}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)+\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\delta^{l}.

Then we assert

\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial h}=k\left[r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k-1}-\dfrac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l-1}\right]>0.

(3.15)

In fact, by (
3.14
), we can get that

\displaystyle r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)=

\displaystyle\delta^{k}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\delta^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{l}\left[\delta^{k-l}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\right].

(3.16)

Since

\underline{t}_{l}\leq\frac{l}{n}<\frac{k}{n}\leq\overline{t}_{k},

then

\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\leq 1\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}\leq 0.

Combining with
\delta^{k-l}>\sup_{r\in[0,+\infty)}\overline{g}(r)
, we have that

\delta^{k-l}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})>0.

(3.17)

Therefore by (
3.16
),

r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)>0.

However,

-\dfrac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}>-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}},

then we conclude that

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k}(r)-\dfrac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l}(r).

Thus

\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial h}>0.

By the implicit function theorem, (
3.14
) can determine a unique function

. Moreover,

\frac{\partial h}{\partial\delta}=-\frac{\partial F}{\partial\delta}/\frac{\partial F}{\partial h}.

So due to (
3.15
) and (
3.17
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial\delta}=

\displaystyle\frac{k\delta^{l-1}\left[\delta^{k-l}-\frac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\right]}{kr^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{l-1}[r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}h^{k-l}-\frac{\underline{t}_{l}}{\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})]}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

(3.18)

In virtue of (
3.16
), we deduce that

\displaystyle h^{l}(r)\left[r^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}}h^{k-l}(r)-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})r^{\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\right]=\delta^{l}\left[\delta^{k-l}-\dfrac{k\underline{t}_{l}}{l\overline{t}_{k}}\theta_{1}^{\frac{k}{\overline{t}_{k}}-\frac{l}{\underline{t}_{l}}}\overline{g}(\theta_{1})\right].

If

then the right side of the above equality tends to
+\infty
. Since

is increasing in
\delta
by (

h(r,\delta,\theta_{1})\to+\infty,\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ \delta\to+\infty.

The lemma is proved.
∎

\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}.
And thus by the definition of
u
,
u(y)\geq\tilde{w}(y)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
and so
u(y)\geq\tilde{u}(y)
in
B_{\varepsilon}.
Hence

u(y)\equiv\tilde{u}(y),~\forall~y\in B_{\varepsilon}.

But
\tilde{u}
satisfies (
4.14
), then we have in the viscosity sense,

\dfrac{S_{k}(D^{2}u(y))}{S_{l}(D^{2}u(y))}=g(y),~\forall~y\in B_{\varepsilon}.

In particular, we have

\dfrac{S_{k}(D^{2}u(x))}{S_{l}(D^{2}u(x))}=g(x).

Because
x
is arbitrary, we know that
u
is a viscosity solution of (
1.1
). Theorem
1.3
is proved.

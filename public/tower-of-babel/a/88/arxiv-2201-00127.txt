Extremal sequences related to the Jacobi symbol
Santanu Mondal, Krishnendu Paul, Shameek Paul

Abstract
For a weight-set A ⊆ ℤ n A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n} , the A A -weighted zero-sum constant C A ​ ( n ) C_{A}(n) is defined to be the smallest natural number k k , such that any sequence of k k elements in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} has an A A -weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. A sequence of length C A ​ ( n ) − 1 C_{A}(n)-1 in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} which does not have any A A -weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms will be called a C C -extremal sequence for A A . Let ( x n ) \big(\frac{x}{n}\big) denote the Jacobi symbol of x ∈ ℤ n x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n} . We characterize the C C -extremal sequences for the weight-set S ⁡ ( n ) = { x ∈ U ⁡ ( n ) : ( x n ) = 1 } S(n)=\big\{\,x\in U(n):\big(\frac{x}{n}\big)=1\,\big\} and for the weight-set L ⁡ ( n , p ) = { x ∈ U ⁡ ( n ) : ( x n ) = ( x p ) } L(n;p)=\big\{\,x\in U(n):\big(\frac{x}{n}\big)=\big(\frac{x}{p}\big)\,\big\} where p p is a prime divisor of n n . We can define D D -extremal sequences for these weight-sets in a way analogous to the definition of C C -extremal sequences. We also characterize these sequences.



Keywords: Extremal sequences, Zero-sum constants, Jacobi symbol

Let

. Then

-weighted zero-sum sequence.

3
D
-extremal sequences for
S(n)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n
be squarefree and every prime divisor of

be at least 7. Suppose

is a

-extremal sequence for

. If
\Omega(n)\geq 3
, then

is a

-extremal sequence for

. If

, then

is either a

-extremal sequence for

or

is a permutation of a sequence
(x_{1},x_{2})
where

and
-x_{2}\in U(n)\setminus S(n)
.

Proof.

Let

be a
D
-extremal sequence for

. Then all terms of
S
are non-zero. As

is squarefree,
\Omega(n)\geq 2
and every prime divisor of

is at least 7, by Theorem

we have

. Hence

must be
\Omega(n)
.

Case 3.1.1
.

There is a prime divisor
p
of
n
such that at most one term of

is coprime to
p
.

Suppose all terms of
S
are divisible by
p
. Let
n^{\prime}=n/p
and let

be the image of

under

. By Theorem
1.6
we have
D_{U(n^{\prime})}(n^{\prime})=\Omega(n^{\prime})+1
. As
S^{\prime}
has length
\Omega(n)=\Omega(n^{\prime})+1
, we see that

has a
U(n^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. As

is squarefree, it follows that

is coprime to

and so by Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we get the contradiction that

has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence.

So there is exactly one term of

which is not divisible by

. Let us assume that that term is

. Let

and let
T^{\prime}
be the image of
T
under
f_{n|n^{\prime}}
. Suppose
T^{\prime}
has a
U(n^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we get the contradiction that
S
has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Thus,
T^{\prime}
is a sequence of length
\Omega(n^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n^{\prime}}
which does not have any

-weighted zero-sum subsequence. As

, it follows that

is a
D
-extremal sequence for

. So from Theorem 5 of
[
2
]
we see that

is a
D
-extremal sequence for
U(n)
.

Case 3.1.2
.

For every prime divisor
p
of
n
, exactly two terms of

are coprime to
p
.

If
\Omega(n)=2
, then
S=(x_{1},x_{2})
. For every prime divisor
p
of
n
, as both

and
x_{2}
are coprime to

, it follows that
x_{1},x_{2}\in U(n)
. Suppose either
x_{1},-x_{2}\in S(n)
or

. As

is squarefree, from Proposition 1 of
[

]
we get that
S(n)
has index two in
U(n)
. Hence,
a\in S(n)
where

. As

and

, it follows that
S
is an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Thus, the sequence
S
is a permutation of a sequence
(x_{1},x_{2})
where
x_{1}\in S(n)
and
-x_{2}\in U(n)\setminus S(n)
.

Let us now assume that
\Omega(n)\geq 3
. Suppose

has at most one unit. By Lemma
2.5
we get the contradiction that
S
is an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So we can assume that

has at least two units. By the assumption in this subcase, we see that
S
will have exactly two units and the other terms of

will be zero. As
S
has length
k\geq 3
, we get the contradiction that some term of
S
is zero.

Case 3.1.3
.

For every prime divisor
p
of
n
at least two terms of
S
are coprime to
p
, and there is a prime divisor
p
of

such that at least three terms of

are coprime to

In this case, by Lemma
2.6
we get the contradiction that

is an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n
be a squarefree number which is not a prime and suppose every prime divisor of
n
is at least 7. When
\Omega(n)=2
, a sequence is a

-extremal sequence for

if and only if either it is a

-extremal sequence for

or a permutation of a sequence
(x_{1},x_{2})
where

and
-x_{2}\in U(n)\setminus S(n)
. When

, a sequence is

-extremal sequence for

if and only if it is a

-extremal sequence for

.

Proof.

Let

be as in the statement of the theorem. We have already proved one implication in Theorem

. Hence, as

-extremal sequence for

, then

is a
D
-extremal sequence for

.

Let

and

where

and
-x_{2}\in U(n)\setminus S(n)
. Suppose

-weighted zero-sum subsequence of

. Then

must be

such that

such that

. As both

and

are in

, we get the contradiction that

. Thus

does not have any

-weighted zero-sum subsequence. From Theorem

-extremal sequence for

.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be squarefree number which is not a prime and assume that every prime divisor of

is at least 7. Then a sequence in

is a
C
-extremal sequence for

if and only if it is a

-extremal sequence for

Proof.

As

is a squarefree number which is not a prime and every prime divisor of

is at least 7, by Theorems

and

we get

. Hence, as

, it follows that any

-extremal sequence for

is a

-extremal sequence for
S(n)
. Suppose a sequence
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a
C
-extremal sequence for
S(n)
. By Theorem
2.7
we have

and so

must be
2^{\Omega(n)}-1
. Also all the terms of

must be non-zero.

Case 4.1.1
.

There is a prime divisor
p
of
n
such that at most one term of
S
is not divisible by
p
.

Suppose all the terms of
S
are divisible by
p
. Let
n^{\prime}=n/p
and
S^{\prime}
be the image of
S
under
f_{n|n^{\prime}}
. By Theorem
1.6
we have

. As
S^{\prime}
has length

and as
\Omega(n)=\Omega(n^{\prime})+1
, we get that

. Hence,
S^{\prime}
has a
U(n^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Thus by Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we get the contradiction that
S
has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms.

So exactly one term
x^{*}
of
S
is coprime to
p
. Suppose
x^{*}\neq x_{k+1}
where
k+1=(l+1)/2
. Then there is a subsequence
T
of consecutive terms of
S
of length at least
k+1
such that
p
divides every term of
T
. Also
k+1=2^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}
as
l+1=2^{\Omega(n)}
. So by a similar argument as in the previous paragraph, we get the contradiction that
T
(and hence

) has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Thus the term

must be
x_{k+1}
.

Let
S_{1}^{\prime}
and
S_{2}^{\prime}
be the images of the sequences
S_{1}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and
S_{2}=(x_{k+2},\ldots,x_{l})
under
f_{n|n^{\prime}}
. Suppose
S_{1}^{\prime}
has an
S(n^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. By Lemma
2.3
, we have
U(n^{\prime})\subseteq f_{n|n^{\prime}}\big(S(n)\big)
. As
p
divides every term of

, by Lemma
2.4
we see that
S_{1}
has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Now as
S_{1}
is itself a subsequence of consecutive terms of
S
, we get the contradiction that
S
has an
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms.

By Theorem
1.6
, we have
C_{U(n^{\prime})}(n^{\prime})=2^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}
. As
S_{1}^{\prime}
does not have any
U(n^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms and
S_{1}^{\prime}
has length

, it follows that
S_{1}^{\prime}
is a
C
-extremal sequence for
U(n^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n^{\prime}}
. A similar argument shows that
S_{2}^{\prime}
is also a
C
-extremal sequence for
U(n^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n^{\prime}}
. So from Theorem 5 of
[
6
]
, we see that
S
is a
C
-extremal sequence for
U(n)
.

Case 4.1.2
.

Given any prime divisor of
n
, exactly two terms of

are not divisible by it.

If

has at most one unit, by Lemma

we get the contradiction that

is an

, we see that

.

such that at least three terms of

are not divisible by it.

As

, and there is a prime divisor
p
of
n/p^{\prime}
, such that at least three terms of
S
are coprime to
p
.

In this case, by Lemma
5.6
we get the contradiction that
S
is an
L(n;p^{\prime})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
n=p^{\prime}q
where
p^{\prime}
and
q
are distinct primes which are at least 7. Suppose
S=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
is a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then
S
is a
C
-extremal sequence for
L(n;p^{\prime})
if and only if
S
has either of the following two forms.

•

The terms
x_{1},x_{3}
and
x_{5}
are non-zero multiples of
q
and the image of the sequence
(x_{2},x_{4})
under
f_{n|q}
is a
C
-extremal sequence for
Q_{q}
.

•

The only term of
S
which is coprime to
p^{\prime}
is
x_{3}
and the images of the sequences
(x_{1},x_{2})
and
(x_{4},x_{5})
under
f_{n|q}
are
C
-extremal sequences for
Q_{q}
.

8
Concluding remarks

Let
n
and
p^{\prime}
be as in the statement of Theorem
7.3
. From Theorems
1.6
and
5.8
, we have
D_{U(n)}(n)=3
and
D_{L(n;p^{\prime})}(n)=4
, and so we cannot compare the
D
-extremal sequences for
U(n)
with the
D
-extremal sequences for
L(n;p^{\prime})
. Also as
C_{U(n)}(n)=4
and
C_{L(n;p^{\prime})}(n)=6
, we cannot compare the corresponding
C
-extremal sequences.

When
\Omega(n)=2
, from Theorem
3.2
we see that there exist
D
-extremal sequences for
S(n)
which are not
D
-extremal sequences for
U(n)
and when
\Omega(n)=3
, from Theorem
6.4
we see that there exist
D
-extremal sequences for
L(n;p^{\prime})
which are not
D
-extremal sequences for
U(n)
. The following questions can be investigated.

•

Can we determine the value of
D_{S(n)}(n)
and characterize the
D
-extremal sequences for
S(n)
for general
n
.

•

If
n
is such that
D_{U(n)}(n)=D_{S(n)}(n)
, can we say that a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
which does not have any
S(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence will not have any
U(n)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence.

•

We can ask analogues of the above questions for the constants
C_{S(n)}(n)
and
C_{U(n)}(n)
.

A

, where

and an edge set consisting of all pairs
\{A,B\}
, where

and
|A|=|B|-1

For a graph

, let
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},H)
be the largest number of edges in a subgraph
G
of a hypercube
Q_{n}
such that there is no subgraph of
G
isomorphic to
H
. The behaviour of the function
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},H)
is not well understood in general and it is not even known for what graphs
H
,
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},H)=\Omega(||Q_{n}||)
, where
||Q_{n}||=n2^{n-1}
denotes the number of edges of
Q_{n}
. See the following papers on the topic:
[
4

,
2
,
3

17
,
16
]
. Conlon

observed a connection between extremal numbers in the hypercube and classical extremal numbers for uniform hypergraphs. That allowed to determine a large class of graphs

such that
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},H)=o(||Q_{n}||)
, i.e., the class of graphs with zero Turán density.

In this note we focus on the case when
H
is an even cycle. Here, a
2\ell
-cycle, denoted
C_{2\ell}
, is a graph on
2\ell
vertices and
2\ell
edges such that the edges are pairs of consecutive vertices with respect to some circular ordering of the vertices. It is known that
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4})=\Omega(||Q_{n}||)
and
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{6})=\Omega(||Q_{n}||)
, see Chung
[
8
]
, Conder
[

]
, and Brass et al.
[
6
]
. Chung
[

]
showed that
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4k})=o(||Q_{n}||)
, for any integer

.
Füredi and Özkahya
[
15
,

]
extended Chung’s results by showing that
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4k+2})=o(||Q_{n}||)

\ell\not\in\{2,3,5\}

{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{2\ell})=o(||Q_{n}||)

{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{2\ell})=\Omega(||Q_{n}||)

Conlon

Considering more specific upper bounds for cycles with zero Turán density, Conlon

{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4k})\leq c_{k}n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2k}}||Q_{n}||.
Füredi and Özkahya
[

]
,
[

{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4k+2})=O(n^{-q_{k}}||Q_{n}||),

q_{k}=\frac{1}{2k+1}
for

, and

for any other integer

. Note that for large

{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{4k+2})
behaves as

Theorem 1
.

Let
\ell
be an odd integer,
\ell\geq 7
. Then
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{2\ell})=O\left(n^{\frac{5}{6}+\frac{1}{3(\ell-3)}}2^{n}\right)
.

The main ingredient of the proof is also due to Conlon
[
10
]
using partite representation. Our contribution here is finding a suitable representation and bounding the respective extremal hypergraph function.

We need some definitions and notations. For a set
V
and a non-negative integer

the set of all

, where

-graph. We denote the number of edges in an

. If

-graph, then

denotes the largest number of edges in an

-graph not containing

An

-graph is
k
-partite
if its vertex set could be partitioned into
k

Edges
\{x,y\}
and
\{x,y,z\}
are written simply as

and
xyz

3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and its vertex

link graph

L(x)
is the graph with no isolated vertices whose edge set consists of all pairs

such that

is an edge of
\mathcal{H}
. Let

be a bipartite graph. Let the

of

be the

\mathcal{H}
with vertex set

For a non-negative

Lemma 1
.

A cycle
C_{2\ell}
, where
\ell\geq 7
is an odd integer, has a
3
-partite representation
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
is a subgraph of a two-lift of a cycle of length
\ell-3
with a pendant edge.

Proof.

We shall construct a cycle

for odd

. We shall define
A_{\ell}
to be the ordered set of vertices of

in the third layer,
B_{C}
to be a respective set of vertices in the second layer, and let

be built by taking vertices from
A_{\ell}
and
B_{\ell}
alternatingly in order.
Let

\displaystyle A_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ax_{1}y_{1},ax_{2}y_{1},ax_{2}y_{2},\ldots,ax_{k-1}y_{k-2},ax_{k-1}y_{k-1},~~bx_{k-1}y_{k-1},bx_{k-1}y_{0},bx_{1}y_{0},bx_{1}y_{1}),

\displaystyle B_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ay_{1},ax_{2},ay_{2},\ldots,ax_{k-1},~~x_{k-1}y_{k-1},bx_{k-1},by_{0},bx_{1},x_{1}y_{1}).

Then

has a

-partite representation
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
with the edge set equal to the underlying unordered set of

.
Indeed, we see that

is a three-partite

-graph with parts

,
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}

One can visualise

Lemma 2
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph and
\mathcal{H}
be a two-lift of
H
. If
{\rm ex}(n,H)=O(n^{\gamma})
, then
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=O(n^{\frac{\gamma+4}{2}})
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma,c^{\prime}>0
,
n
sufficiently large, and
{\rm ex}(n,H)<c^{\prime}n^{\gamma}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
3
-graph with vertex set
V
,
|V|=n
and
cn^{\sigma}
edges, where
\sigma=\frac{\gamma+4}{2}
and
c
is a constant,
c^{\prime}=c^{2}
.
We shall show that
\mathcal{G}
contains a two-lift
\mathcal{H}
of
H
.

Let
U=\binom{V}{2}
and
G
be a bipartite graph with partite sets
V
and
U
and
edge set
\{\{x,zz^{\prime}\}:x\in V,zz^{\prime}\in U,xzz^{\prime}\in E(\mathcal{G})\}
.
Then
||G||=3||\mathcal{G}||=3cn^{\sigma}
.
Let
q
be the largest integer such that
G
contains a complete bipartite subgraph with two vertices in
V
and
q
vertices in
U
.
We shall show that
q\geq n^{2\sigma-4}
.

To do this, we shall apply a standard double counting and reduction to smaller uniformity, already used by Erdős
[
13
]
in bounding the extremal function for complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs. Let
X
be the number of stars in
G
with
2
edges and center in
U
. Then
X\geq\sum_{u\in U}\binom{deg_{G}(u)}{2}\geq|U|\binom{||G||/|U|}{2}\geq c^{2}n^{2}n^{2(\sigma-2)}
.
On the other hand, since every pair of vertices from
V
belongs to at most
q
such stars,
X\leq q\binom{n}{2}\leq qn^{2}
. Thus
q\geq c^{2}n^{2\sigma-4}=c^{\prime}n^{\gamma}
.
Let
Q\subseteq U
,
|Q|=q
,
a,b\in V
, such that
a,b,
and
Q
induce
K_{2,q}
in
G
. Then

a,b
and
Q
correspond to a two-lift
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of a graph
H^{\prime}
with the edge-set
Q
.
Since
|Q|=q=c^{\prime}n^{\gamma}>{\rm ex}(n,H)
, we see that
H
is a subgraph of
H^{\prime}
. Thus
\mathcal{G}
contains a two-lift of
H
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

It was shown by Erdős
[
12
]
as well as by Bondy and Simonovits
[
7
]
, that
{\rm ex}(n,C_{2m})=O(n^{1+\frac{1}{m}})
.
This implies that
{\rm ex}(n,H)\leq O(n^{1+\frac{2}{\ell-3}})
, where
H
is a cycle of length
\ell-3
with a pendant edge, for odd
\ell\geq 7
. Now, using Theorem
2
and Lemmas
1
and
2
,
with
\gamma=1+\frac{2}{\ell-3}
,
\frac{\gamma+4}{2}=\frac{5+\frac{2}{\ell-3}}{2}
, and
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\ell-3}
, we have
{\rm ex}(Q_{n},C_{2\ell})\leq O(\alpha^{1/3}n2^{n})=O(n^{1-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{3(\ell-3)}}2^{n})
.
∎

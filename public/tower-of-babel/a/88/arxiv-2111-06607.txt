Generalized orthotoric Kähler surfaces
Włodzimierz Jelonek

Abstract
In this paper we describe QCH Kähler surfaces ( M , g , J ) (M,g,J) of generalized orthotoric type type. We introduce a distinguished orthonormal frame on ( M , g ) (M,g) and give the structure equations for ( M , g , J ) (M,g,J) . In the case when I I is conformally Kähler and ( M , g , J ) (M,g,J) is not hyperkähler we integrate these structure equations and rediscover the orthotoric Kähler surfaces. We also investigate the hyperkäler case. We prove in a simple way that if ( M , g , J ) (M,g,J) is a hyperkähler surface with a degenerate Weyl tensor W − W^{-} (i.e. QCH hyperkähler surface) then among all hyperkäler structures on ( M , g ) (M,g) there exists a Kähler structure J 0 J_{0} such that ( M , g , J 0 ) (M,g,J_{0}) is of Calabi type or of orthotoric type.



1. Introduction.

QCH Kähler surfaces
are Kähler surfaces
(M,g,J)

admitting a global,
2
-dimensional,
J
-invariant distribution

K(\pi)=R(X,JX,JX,X)

|X_{D}|=\sqrt{g(X_{D},X_{D})}
, where

X_{D}
is the orthogonal projection of
X
on
D
(see [J-1]). Every QCH Kähler surface admits an opposite almost Hermitian structure
I
such that the Ricci tensor
\rho

(M,g,J)

I

(M,g,I)
satisfies the second Gray condition:

R(X,Y,Z,W)-R(IX,IY,Z,W)=R(IX,Y,IZ,W)+R(IX,Y,Z,IW).

[formula]

In [J-5], [J-6], [J-2], [J-3] the author has studied QCH semi-symmetric Kähler surfaces
(M,g,J)
which admit an opposite Hermitian structure and such that one of distributions
D,D^{\perp}
is integrable. A general method of constructing such manifolds was given there. The aim of the present paper is to investigate the case where none of the distributions
D,D^{\perp}
is integrable. In [J-1] we have proved that a QCH Kähler surface whose opposite structure
I
is Hermitian is of Calabi type or orthotoric type, or is hyperkähler. In the present paper we find the distinguished orthonormal frame
\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}\}
and the structure equations with respect to this frame for the QCH Kähler surfaces with Hermitian opposite structure
I
which are not of generalized Calabi type. We call such QCH surfaces the generalized orthotoric surfaces. In the case when
I
is conformally Kähler and
(M,g,J)
is not hyperkähler we integrate these structure equations and rediscover the orthotoric Kähler surfaces (see [A-C-G], [J-1]). We also investigate the hyperkäler case. We prove that if
(M,g,J)
is a hyperkähler surface with a degenerate Weyl tensor
W^{-}
(i.e. QCH hyperkähler surface) then among all hyperkäler structures on
(M,g)
there exists a Kähler structure
J_{0}
such that
(M,g,J_{0})
is of Calabi type or of orthotoric type. We also describe the cases of hyperkähler surfaces with only one Calabi structure, with only one orthotoric structure, with all structures orthotoric. The Kähler surfaces are studied by many authors see for example [D-T].

are constant.
\diamondsuit

Assume now that
(M,g,J)
is a hyperkähler orthotoric surface with
\phi\neq const
. Then
\sin\phi=\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}},\cos\phi=-\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}
,
\alpha=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}h^{3}
and
tan\phi=-\frac{b}{a}
. Thus
\ln|\tan\phi|=\ln b-\ln a
. It means
d^{I}\ln\tan\phi=hb^{\prime}(y)\theta_{4}+ha^{\prime}(x)\theta_{3}
and
d^{I}\ln\tan\phi=\frac{bb^{\prime}}{x-y}(dz-xdt)+\frac{aa^{\prime}}{x-y}(dz-ydt)
. Let
F=a^{2},G=b^{2}
. Then
d^{I}\ln\tan\phi=\frac{G^{\prime}}{2(x-y)}(dz-xdt)+\frac{F^{\prime}}{2(x-y)}(dz-ydt)=\frac{G^{\prime}+F^{\prime}}{2(x-y)}dz+\frac{-G^{\prime}x-F^{\prime}y}{2(x-y)}dt
. Let
\gamma=\cos\psi(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2}-\theta_{3}\wedge\theta_{4})+\sin\psi(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{4}+\theta_{3}\wedge\theta_{2})
be Kähler.
Since
d\psi=d^{I}\ln\tan\phi
it means that
\psi
is a function depending only on
z,t
and consequently
G^{\prime}+F^{\prime}=2c(x-y),-G^{\prime}x-F^{\prime}y=2d(x-y)
for some
d,c\in\mathbb{R}
. It implies

G^{\prime}=-2cy-2a,F^{\prime}=2cx+2a,a\in\mathbb{R}
and
G(y)=-cy^{2}-2ay+b_{1},F(x)=cx^{2}+2ax+b_{2}
. It means
d=-a
. Thus

d\psi=cdz-adt
and
\psi=cz-at+\phi_{0}
where
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. The Killing vector field
\xi
related with conformally Kähler structure
I
is
\xi=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. This field is triholomorphic if and only if
d\psi(\xi)=c=0
. It follows that for
G(y)=-2ay+b_{1},F(x)=2ax+b_{2}
all Kähler structures from the sphere of Kähler structures are orthotoric. If
c\neq 0
then only the original one given by
G(y)=-cy^{2}-2ay+b_{1},F(x)=cx^{2}+2ax+b_{2}
is orthotoric, the other are not orthotoric or Calabi type. If
c=0
then all the other structures are orthotoric. If
c\neq 0
then only this one is orthotoric the other are not of Calabi type or orthotoric.

In passing we also proved that an orthotoric structure is hyperKähler if and only if
G(y)=-cy^{2}-2ay+b_{1},F(x)=cx^{2}+2ax+b_{2}
.(it follows also from [A-C-G]).

Let
(M,g,J)
be hyperkähler QCH surface. Then

E_{3}(\alpha\sin\phi)=E_{4}(\alpha\cos\phi)

and

E_{3}(\alpha\cos\phi)=E_{4}(\alpha\sin\phi)=0
. Let
\theta=-d\ln u
.

When the field
\xi
is
J
-holomorphic and when is triholomorphic?

We have
\beta(x)=g(\xi,X)=-\frac{\alpha\cos\phi}{u}\theta_{3}+\frac{\alpha\sin\phi}{u}\theta_{4}
. Hence

d(-\frac{\alpha\cos\phi}{u}\theta_{3}+\frac{\alpha\sin\phi}{u}\theta_{4})=-\frac{1}{u}d(\alpha\cos\phi)\wedge\theta_{3}+\frac{du}{u^{2}}\wedge(\alpha\cos\phi\theta_{3})-\frac{\alpha\cos\phi}{u}d\theta_{3}+\frac{1}{u}d(\alpha\sin\phi)\wedge\theta_{4}-\frac{du}{u^{2}}\wedge(\alpha\sin\phi\theta_{4})+\frac{\alpha\sin\phi}{u}d\theta_{4}

It implies
d\beta=\frac{-2}{u}((E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi)\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{3}+\frac{2}{u}((E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi)\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{4}-\frac{2}{u}(E_{2}(\alpha\cos\phi)+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi)\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{3}+\frac{2}{u}(E_{1}(\alpha\sin\phi)+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi)\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{4}+\frac{2}{u}E_{4}(\alpha\cos\phi)(\theta_{3}\wedge\theta_{4}-\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2})
.

d\beta=\frac{2}{u}(-E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi)(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{3}+\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{4})-\frac{2}{u}(E_{2}(\alpha\cos\phi)+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi)(\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{3}-\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{4})+\frac{2}{u}E_{4}(\alpha\cos\phi)(\theta_{3}\wedge\theta_{4}-\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2})-\frac{2}{u}((E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)+E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2})(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{3}-\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{4})

Hence
\xi
is
J
-holomorphic if
E_{2}(\alpha\cos\phi)=-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi=E_{1}(\alpha\sin\phi)
and
E_{4}(\alpha\cos\phi)=E_{3}(\alpha\sin\phi)=0
i.e. if

(M,g,J)
is orthotoric.

The first three forms are self-dual, the last one is anti-self-dual. In an orthotoric case we have

d\beta=\frac{2}{u}(-E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi)(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{3}+\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{4})-\frac{2}{u}(E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)+E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2})(\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{3}-\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{4})

which means that
\xi
is
I,J
-holomorphic.
\xi
is triholomorphic if

-E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi=0
.

In a general hyperkähler generalized orthotoric case
\xi
is triholomorphic if:

-E_{1}(\alpha\cos\phi)+E_{2}(\alpha\sin\phi)-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\phi+\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\phi=0

E_{2}(\alpha\cos\phi)=E_{1}(\alpha\sin\phi)=-\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi

E_{4}(\alpha\cos\phi)=E_{3}(\alpha\sin\phi)=0
thus
(M,g,J)
is orthotoric with
G(y)=-2ay+b_{1},F(x)=2ax+b_{2}
.

Theorem 4.2

If
\xi
is Killing on a hyperkähler surface
(M,g)
then there exists a complex structure
J
on
M
such that
\xi
is
J
-holomorphic.

Proof.
(see also [L], p. 226) Let
V=span\{J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\}
be a bundle of Kähler structures on
M
. If
\phi_{t}
is a one-parametric group of transformations of
\xi
then

(\phi_{t})_{*}(J)\in\Gamma(V)
. Hence
S^{2}\ni J\rightarrow(\phi_{t})_{*}(J)\in S^{2}
is a local group of isometries of
S^{2}
(a geodesic in
SO(3)
) which extends to a one-parametric group in
SO(3)
. Hence after the changing a base in
V

\phi_{t}(J)=\exp tAJ
where
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\cr 0&0&a\\
\cr 0&-a&0\end{pmatrix}
. It follows that this group has a fixed point,
(\phi_{t})_{*}(J)=J
and
L_{\xi}J=0
, which means that
\xi
is
J
-holomorphic.
\diamondsuit

Hence we have:

Theorem 4.3

If
(M,g,J)
is a hyperkähler surface with a degenerate Weyl tensor
W^{-}
then among Kähler structures on
M
there exists one of orthotoric or of Calabi type.

Proof.
Let
W^{-}\neq 0
. Then there exists a Hermitian structure
I
in an opposite orientation such that
\omega_{I}
is a simple eigenform of
W^{-}
. The Lee form
\theta
of
(M,g,I)
is closed, locally
d\theta=-d\ln u
and the field
\xi=\frac{1}{u}I\theta^{\sharp}
is Killing. From Th.4.2 it follows that
\xi
is
J_{0}
holomorphic for some Kähler structure
J_{0}
. If
\phi
is non-constant it follows that
(M,g,J_{0})
is orthotoric. If
\phi
is constant then there exists a Kähler structure
J
such that
(M,g,J)
is of Calabi type.

Now we give simple proof of the following theorem generalizing well known result of Gibbens and Ruback (see [G-R]).

Theorem 4.4.

If a hyperkähler surface admits a two-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathfrak{i}so(M)
then it admits a triholomorphic Killing vector field.

Proof.
Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
be an orthogonal basis of Kähler forms on
M
. Then ( since
L_{\xi}(\nabla_{X}\omega_{i})=\nabla_{X}(L_{\xi}\omega_{i})=0
if
\nabla\omega_{i}=0
) we have

L_{\xi}\omega_{i}=\sum a_{ij}\omega_{j}
,
L_{\eta}\omega_{i}=\sum b_{ij}\omega_{j}

where
a_{ij},b_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
we get

L_{\xi}\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}+\omega_{i}\wedge L_{\xi}\omega_{j}=0
and
\sum a_{ip}\omega_{p}\wedge\omega_{j}+\sum a_{jp}\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{p}=0
which implies
a_{ij}+a_{ji}=0
. Similarly
L_{\xi}\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{i}=L_{\xi}vol=0
i
a_{ii}=b_{ii}=0
.

Hence
A,B\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
where

A=[a_{ij}],B=[b_{ij}]
. Let
\Phi:\mathfrak{iso}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(3)
be defined by
\Phi(\eta)=B=[b_{ij}]
, where

L_{\eta}\omega_{i}=\sum b_{ij}\omega_{j}
. Note that

L_{\eta}L_{\xi}\omega_{i}=\sum a_{ij}L_{\eta}\omega_{j}=\sum a_{ij}b_{jp}\omega_{p}
i
L_{\xi}L_{\eta}\omega_{i}=\sum b_{ij}L_{\xi}\omega_{j}=\sum b_{ij}a_{jp}\omega_{p}
. Hence
\Phi([\xi,\eta)=[\Phi(\xi),\Phi(\eta)]
. Thus
\Phi
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras and
im\Phi
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(3)
hence it has a dimension
0,1
or
3
and
\ker\Phi=\mathfrak{Tri}(M)
where
\mathfrak{Tri}(M)
is a subalgebra of triholomorphic Killing vector fields. Consequently if there exists a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset dim\mathfrak{iso}(M)
such that
dim\mathfrak{h}=2
then there exists a triholomorphic Killing vector field on
M
. This field is a linear combination of two fields from
\mathfrak{h}
. In fact if
X,Y\in\mathfrak{h}
then for example
\Phi(X)=a\Phi(Y)
and the field
X-aY
is triholomorphic.
It holds if there exist two fields
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{iso}(M)
such that
[\xi,\eta]=0
. It is enough to take
\mathfrak{h}=span(\xi,\eta)
. Note that an algebra
\mathfrak{Tri}(M)=ker\Phi
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{iso}(M)
.
\diamondsuit

Remark
From the proof of Th.4.4 follows another proof of Th.4.2. Let
\omega=\sum\alpha_{i}\omega_{i}
. Then
L_{\xi}\omega=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{i}a_{ij}L_{\eta}\omega_{j}
. Hence
L_{\xi}\omega=0
if
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}a_{ij}=0
for
j=1,2,3
. It means that
\Phi(\xi)(\alpha)=0
where
\alpha=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}]\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Note that
ker\Phi(\xi)\neq\{0\}
. If we take
\alpha\in ker\Phi(\xi)
and
||\alpha||=1
then
J=\sum\alpha_{i}J_{i}
, where
\omega_{i}(X,Y)=g(J_{i}X,Y)
, is a Kähler structure such that
L_{\xi}J=0
.

Corollary.

Hyperkahler QCH surface admits local triholomorphic Killing vector field. It means that every hyperkähler surface with degenerate Weyl tensor
W^{-}
admits locally triholomorphic Killing vector field.

Proof.
Let
(M,g)
be a hyperkähler QCH surface. If
(M,g,J)
is an orthotoric surface then from Th.4.4 it admits a triholomorphic Killing vector field.
The case of Calabi type hyperkähler surfaces we solve in the case of Eguchi-Hanson metric. The other cases are similar.

Let
M=\mathbb{R}\times SO(3)
and
g=h(t)^{2}dt^{2}+f^{2}(t)\theta_{1}^{2}+g(t)^{2}(\theta_{2}^{2}+\theta_{3}^{2})
where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
are left-invariant 1-forms on
SO(3)
and
d\theta_{i}=2\epsilon_{ijk}\theta_{j}\wedge\theta_{k}
.
Define two complex structures on
(M,g)

J(hdt)=f\theta_{1},J(\theta_{2})=\theta_{3}
i
J_{-}(hdt)=-f\theta_{1},J_{-}(\theta_{2})=\theta_{3}
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{iso}(S^{2})
where
p:M\rightarrow S^{2}
and
\theta_{2}^{2}+\theta_{3}^{2}=p^{*}m
where
m
is the metric on
S^{2}
. We will find conditions on
\eta=k\xi+X^{-}
to be Killing.
We have
g=h(t)^{2}dt^{2}+f^{2}(t)\theta_{1}^{2}+g(t)^{2}p^{*}m
and
L_{X}m=0
.
L_{\eta}(f^{2}\theta_{1})=f^{2}d(k)\odot\theta_{1}+f^{2}(X\lrcorner d\theta_{1})\odot\theta_{1}
.

Hence we get an equation
dk+X\lrcorner\omega=0
where
k
is a function on
S^{2}
. But
d(X\lrcorner\omega)=L_{X}\omega=0
hence the equation has a solution (
H^{1}(S^{2})=0
). Hence
a field
\eta
is Killing if
dk=-X\lrcorner\omega
. Note that
[\xi,\eta]=0
since
g(\xi,X^{-})=0
,
g(\xi,[\xi,X^{-}])=0
and the field
[\xi,X^{-}]
is vertical.

Since
\mathfrak{iso}(S^{2})
is three-dimensional we get three fields
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}
. Note that since
[\xi,\eta_{i}]=0
we get
\Phi(\eta_{i})=\alpha_{i}\Phi(\xi)
for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence the image of the subalgebra generated by

\mathfrak{h}=span\{\xi,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\}

is one-dimensional, dim im
\Phi(\mathfrak{h})=1
. Thus we have three triholomorphic vector fields on
M
. Note also that Th.4.4 implies that if
(M,g)
does not admit a triholomorphic then
dim\mathfrak{iso}(M)=0,1
or
\mathfrak{iso}(M)
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(3)
. None of these cases hold for a Calabi type Kähler surface.
\diamondsuit

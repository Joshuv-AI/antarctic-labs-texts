Conformal properties of soft-operators - 2 : Use of null-states
Shamik Banerjee, Pranjal Pandey

Abstract
Representations of the (Lorentz) conformal group with the soft operators as highest weight vectors have two universal properties, which we clearly state in this paper. Given a soft operator with a certain dimension and spin, the first property is about the existence of "(large) gauge transformation" that acts on the soft operator. The second property is the decoupling of (large) gauge-invariant null-states of the soft operators from the S S -matrix elements. In each case, the decoupling equation has the form of zero field-strength condition with the soft operator as the (gauge) potential. Null-state decoupling effectively reduces the number of polarisation states of the soft particle and is crucial in deriving soft-theorems from the Ward identities of asymptotic symmetries. To the best of our understanding, these properties are not directly related to the Lorentz invariance of the S S -matrix or the existence of asymptotic symmetries. We also verify that the results obtained from the decoupling of null-states are consistent with the leading and subleading soft-theorems with finite energy massive and massless particles in the external legs.

Universal properties of representation of the conformal group with soft-operator as the highest weight vector

In
Banerjee:2019aoy
it was shown that Lorentz invariance of the
S
-matrix, together with the Ward-identities of the asymptotic symmetries, require the soft-operators to satisfy certain differential equations. For example, the leading soft-photon operator
S_{a}(x)(=S^{1}_{a}(x))
was shown to satisfy the (Euclidean) Maxwell’s equation,
\partial_{a}(\partial_{a}S_{b}-\partial_{b}S_{a})=0
. Similarly, the leading soft-graviton operator
h_{ab}(x)(=S^{1}_{ab}(x))
, in
D=6
, satisfies the equation,

\partial^{2}h_{ab}-\frac{2}{3}\bigg(\partial_{a}\partial_{c}h_{cb}+\partial_{b}\partial_{c}h_{ca}\bigg)+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ab}\partial_{c}\partial_{d}h_{cd}=0

(3)

These equations are additional constraints which need to be satisfied if we want to derive the soft-theorems from Ward-Identities of the asymptotic symmetries.

Now, the curious fact is that both these equations are equations of (Euclidean) gauge theories. Maxwell’s equations have obvious gauge invariance whereas the second equation (
3
) is invariant under the transformation
Erdmenger:1997wy
,

h_{ab}(x)\rightarrow h_{ab}(x)+\bigg(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{ab}\partial^{2}\bigg)\phi(x)

(4)

where
\phi(x)
is a
(\Delta=-1,l=0)
primary.

In this paper,
instead of Lorentz invariance and Ward-identity, we take this "gauge-structure" as the starting point.
It turns out that the conformal representation built on any soft-operator
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
obeys two simple
universal
rules stated below,

1)
Symmetry transformation
:

Given a
(\Delta,l)
primary field
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
, we look for another primary field
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
which transforms in the representation
R
of the rotation group
SO(D-2)
and has dimension
\delta<\Delta
. The
defining property of the primary
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
is that it should have a primary descendant of weight
\Delta
and spin
l
. The primary field
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
may or may not exist depending on the quantum numbers
(\Delta,l)
. Let us consider the interesting case where it exists and denote the corresponding primary descendant by
N^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
.

The existence of the
(\Delta,l)
primary descendant

allows us to define a "global symmetry transformation" by,

\boxed{S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)\rightarrow S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)+N^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)}

(5)

Since
N^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
can be written as a differential operator acting on the primary
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
, one can think of
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
as the transformation function or parameter.

2)
Decoupling of Null-states
:
The next step is to construct the conformal representation with the soft operator
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
as the highest-weight vector. For our purpose, the interesting states in the representation space are the primary descendants. The primary descendants have two possible fates, which we now describe:

Case-A

(\Delta,l)
is such that the primary
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
does
not
exist. In that case,
we cannot set any of the primary descendants of
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
to zero.

Case-B

(\Delta,l)
is such that the primary field
O^{\delta}_{R}(x)
exists and the symmetry transformation (
5
) is defined. In this case, we can
set
only
those primary descendants of
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
to zero which are
invariant
under the symmetry transformation (
5
).
In other words, the primary descendants of
S^{\Delta}_{a_{1}....a_{l}}(x)
, which are invariant under the symmetry transformation (
5

decouple from the
S
-matrix element
.

Lorentz invariance of the

Leading soft-photon or infinite-dimensional

The leading soft-photon

F_{ab}=\partial_{a}S_{b}-\partial_{b}S_{a}

3.2

Leading soft-graviton or Supertranslation

3.2.1

n=D-2>2

The leading soft-graviton is a

\boxed{O_{abc}(x)=\partial_{a}S_{bc}-\partial_{b}S_{ac}+\frac{1}{n-1}\big(\delta_{bc}\partial_{d}S_{da}-\delta_{ac}\partial_{d}S_{db}\big)=0}

Proof

We have to solve the equation

\partial_{a}V_{b}-\partial_{b}V_{a}=0\Rightarrow\boxed{V_{a}=\partial_{b}S_{ab}=\partial_{a}\phi(x)}

\partial_{a}O_{abc}(x)=\partial^{2}S_{bc}-\frac{n}{n-1}\big(\partial_{b}\partial_{c}-\frac{1}{n}\delta_{bc}\partial^{2}\big)\phi(x)=0

\boxed{S_{ab}=\frac{n}{n-1}\big(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\frac{1}{n}\delta_{ab}\partial^{2}\big)\psi(x)+\tilde{S}_{ab}(x)}

Now substituting (

\boxed{\partial_{a}\tilde{S}_{bc}-\partial_{b}\tilde{S}_{ac}=0}

(24)

where we have used
\partial_{a}\tilde{S}_{ab}=0

\tilde{S}_{ab}=\partial_{a}\xi_{b}(x)

where

Tracelessness of

\boxed{S_{ab}(x)=\big(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\frac{1}{n}\delta_{ab}\partial^{2}\big)F(x)}

So

So again we can see that the decoupling of the null-state effectively reduces the number of polarization states of soft-graviton to

can be written as,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i,Q_i\},out}S_{a}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j,Q_j\},in}\\
=\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}Q_{k}\frac{p(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\cdot\epsilon_{a}(\vec{w})}{p(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\cdot\hat{q}(\vec{w})}\bigg]\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i,Q_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j,Q_j\},in}\end{gathered}

Here
Q_{k}
is the charge of the
k
-th particle and
\epsilon_{a}(\vec{w})
is the polarization vector of the soft photon.

The soft-factor can be calculated to be,

\begin{gathered}\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}Q_{k}\frac{p(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\cdot\epsilon_{a}(\vec{w})}{p(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\cdot\hat{q}(\vec{w})}\bigg]=\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{a}}\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\ln(y_k^2 + |\vec w - \vec x_k|^2)\bigg]\\
\end{gathered}

(45)

Therefore the matrix element of the soft-photon operator can be written as,

\boxed{\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i,Q_i\},out}S_{a}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j,Q_j\},in}=\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{a}}\Lambda(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},Q\})}

(46)

where,

\begin{gathered}\Lambda(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},Q\})=\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}\ln(y_k^2 + |\vec w - \vec x_k|^2)\bigg]\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i,Q_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j,Q_j\},in}\end{gathered}

(47)

(
46
) matches with the form (
10
) of the leading soft photon theorem obtained from the decoupling of the null-state.

4.2

Leading soft graviton or Supertranslation

In terms of the leading soft graviton operator
S_{ab}(\vec{w})
, Weinberg’s soft graviton theorem
Weinberg:1964ew
;
Weinberg:1965nx
can be written as,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}S_{ab}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}=\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\frac{p_{\mu}(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})p_{\nu}(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\epsilon^{\mu\nu}_{ab}(\vec{w})}{p(y_{k},\vec{x}_{k})\cdot\hat{q}(\vec{w})}\bigg]\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}\end{gathered}

where
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}_{ab}(\vec{w})
is the graviton polarization vector.

Now the soft-factor can be computed to be,

\begin{gathered}\gamma\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\frac{m_{k}}{y_{k}}\bigg(\delta_{ab}-n\frac{(w_{a}-x_{ka})(w_{b}-x_{kb})}{y_{k}^{2}+|\vec{w}-\vec{x}_{k}|^{2}}\bigg)\bigg]=\bigg(\partial_{a}^{w}\partial_{b}^{w}-\frac{1}{n}\delta_{ab}\partial_{w}^{2}\bigg)F(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},m\})\end{gathered}

(48)

where,

\boxed{\begin{gathered}F(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},m\})=\frac{-n\gamma}{2}\bigg[\sum_{k}\eta_{k}m_{k}\frac{y_{k}^{2}+|\vec{w}-\vec{x}_{k}|^{2}}{2y_{k}}\ln(y_k^2 + |\vec w - \vec x_k|^2)\bigg]\end{gathered}}

(49)

Here
m_{k}
is the mass of the
k
-th particle and
\gamma
is a numerical constant whose precise value is not important for us. Now, using this we can write,

\boxed{\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}S_{ab}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}=\bigg(\partial_{a}^{w}\partial_{b}^{w}-\frac{1}{n}\delta_{ab}\partial_{w}^{2}\bigg)\Lambda(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},m\})}

(50)

where we have defined,

\Lambda(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},m\})=F(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x},m\})\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}

(51)

(
50
) matches with the form (
32
) of the leading soft graviton theorem obtained from the decoupling of the null-state

4.3

Subleading soft graviton theorem in
D=6(n=4)
or Superrotation

In terms of the subleading soft graviton operator - again denoted by
S_{ab}(\vec{w})
- Cachazo-Strominger subleading soft graviton theorem
Cachazo:2014fwa
can be written as,

\begin{gathered}\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}S_{ab}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}\\
=-i\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\frac{\epsilon_{ab}^{\mu\nu}(\vec{w})p^{k}_{\mu}q^{\rho}}{p^{k}\cdot q}J^{k}_{\rho\nu}\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}\end{gathered}

(52)

Since all the external particles are scalars, the (orbital) angular momentum operator is given by,

J^{k}_{\rho\nu}=-i\left(p^{k}_{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}^{\nu}}-p^{k}_{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}^{\rho}}\right)

(53)

Now a straightforward calculation gives,

\boxed{\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}S_{ab}(\vec{w})\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}=\partial^{w}_{a}\zeta_{b}+\partial^{w}_{b}\zeta_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}\partial^{w}.\zeta}

(54)

where the vector
\zeta
is given by,

\begin{gathered}\zeta_{a}(\vec{w},\{y,\vec{x}\})=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\bigg[\ln(y_k^2+|\vec{w}-\vec{x_k}|^2)\bigg\{-(w_{a}-x_{ka})y_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\\
+\left((w_{a}-x_{ka})(w_{c}-x_{kc})-\frac{1}{2}(y_{k}^{2}+|\vec{w}-\vec{x_{k}}|^{2})\delta_{ac}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{kc}}\bigg\}\bra{\{y_i,\vec x_i\},out}\ket{\{y_j,\vec x_j\},in}\bigg]\end{gathered}

(55)

Here
\gamma
is a numerical constant whose exact value is not important for us.

(
54
) matches with the form (
44
) of the subleading soft-graviton theorem obtained from the decoupling of the null-state.

Comments on the construction of the soft-charge

In
even
space-time dimensions the
soft-charge

Strominger:2013lka
;
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He
;
Strominger:2014pwa
;
He:2014cra
;
Kapec:2015ena
;
Kapec:2014zla
;
Kapec:2015vwa
;
Pate:2017fgt
;
Kapec:2016jld
;
Campiglia:2015qka
;
Campiglia:2015kxa
;
Bondi:1962px
;
Strominger:2017zoo
corresponding to a particular symmetry transformation can always be written as,

Q_{S}[\xi]=\int d^{D-2}{x}\ \xi(x)\cdot O(x)

(56)

where
\xi(x)
is the transformation parameter and
O(x)
is a local operator, which is also a
primary
of the (Lorentz) conformal group
Banerjee:2019aoy
. The tensor indices of
O(x)
are dual to those of
\xi(x)
, so that the
contraction
\xi(x)\cdot O(x)
is a scalar of dimension
(D-2)
. In even space-time dimension
O(x)
can be constructed as a descendant of the soft operator. Let us now illustrate this with an example.

Consider the case of subleading soft-graviton in
D=6
. In this case we know that the transformation parameter is a
(\Delta=-1,l=1)
primary which we denote by
\xi_{a}(x)
. So the soft-charge can be written as,

Q_{S}[\xi]=\int d^{4}{x}\ \xi_{a}(x)O_{a}(x)

(57)

Since
\xi_{a}(x)O_{a}(x)
should have dimension
4
and
\xi(x)
has dimension
\Delta=-1
,
O_{a}(x)
should be a vector primary with dimension
\Delta=5
. Now
O_{a}(x)
can be constructed as a descendant of the subleading soft-graviton
S_{ab}(x)
and is given by,

\boxed{O_{a}(x)\sim\partial^{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\partial_{c}S_{bc}-\frac{3}{4}\partial^{4}\partial_{b}S_{ab}(x)}

(58)

Here we have used
\sim
because the overall normalization of the charge cannot be determined in this way. Now since the subleading soft graviton is a
(\Delta=0,l=2)
primary,
O_{a}(x)
constructed in this way clearly has dimension
\Delta=5
and spin
l=1
. One can check by straightforward calculation that
O_{a}(x)
, given in (
58
), is a primary operator as long as the decoupling equation (
42
) for the subleading soft graviton
S_{ab}(x)
holds.

6

Comparison with
Banerjee:2019aoy

Before we conclude we would like to compare the results of
Banerjee:2019aoy
with the current paper. For the sake of concreteness let us compare the decoupling equations for the leading soft-graviton
S_{ab}(x)
which is a
(\Delta=1,l=2)
primary.

In our current approach the decoupling equation (
15
) is given by,

O_{abc}(x)=\partial_{a}S_{bc}-\partial_{b}S_{ac}+\frac{1}{3}\big(\delta_{bc}\partial_{d}S_{da}-\delta_{ac}\partial_{d}S_{db}\big)=0

(59)

where we have specialized to the case of
D=6
or
n=4
.

Now in
Banerjee:2019aoy
, starting from the Lorentz invariance of the
S
-matrix and the Ward identity for the supertranslation, we arrived at a somewhat different decoupling equation,

\tilde{O}_{ab}=\partial^{2}S_{ab}-\frac{2}{3}\bigg(\partial_{a}\partial_{c}S_{cb}+\partial_{b}\partial_{c}S_{ca}\bigg)+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ab}\partial_{c}\partial_{d}S_{cd}=0

(60)

Now one can easily check that,

\boxed{\tilde{O}_{bc}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{a}O_{abc}+\partial_{a}O_{acb})}

(61)

Therefore
\tilde{O}_{ab}
is a descendant of
O_{abc}
– in fact a primary descendant – and the decoupling equation

O_{abc}=0\Rightarrow\tilde{O}_{ab}=0

(62)

But,

\tilde{O}_{ab}=0\nRightarrow\tilde{O}_{abc}=0

(63)

Therefore (
59
) is a stronger condition than (
60
).

Now, in the case of leading soft graviton or supertranslation, the operator
O(x)
appearing in the soft charge must be a
(\Delta=5,l=0)
primary in
D=6
. Therefore a potential candidate for
O(x)
is the unique scalar operator
\partial^{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}S_{ab}
of dimension
\Delta=5
. In
Banerjee:2019aoy
, the decoupling equation (
60
) was obtained by demanding that the operator
\partial^{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}S_{ab}
be a primary. Now the same thing can also be achieved by using the decoupling equation (
59
). To be more precise, under an infinitesimal special conformal transformation,
O(x)=\partial^{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}S_{ab}
transforms as
Banerjee:2019aoy
,

O^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\left(1+10\,\epsilon\cdot x\right)O(x)+\boxed{8\,\epsilon_{a}\partial_{c}\left(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}S_{bc}(x)-\partial_{b}\partial_{c}S_{ab}(x)\right)}

(64)

Now,

O_{abc}=0\Rightarrow\partial_{c}O_{abc}(x)=0\Rightarrow\partial_{a}\partial_{c}S_{cb}-\partial_{b}\partial_{c}S_{ca}=0

(65)

Therefore, owing to the decoupling equation (
59
), the inhomogeneous term in (
64
) vanish and
\partial^{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}S_{ab}
indeed transforms like a primary.

Although the two formulations seem to be mutually consistent, the current one is more general and cannot be simply derived from Lorentz invariance and the existence of infinite dimensional asymptotic symmetries. For example, the decoupling equation (
59
) of this paper is
sufficient
for the Lorentz invariance of the supertranslation Ward identity. Now, it is natural to conjecture that this is also a
necessary
condition, although the reason may not be Lorentz invariance and asymptotic symmetry. In fact, the current formulation is very natural from a dual CFT perspective, if there is one. We hope to return to these interesting questions in future.

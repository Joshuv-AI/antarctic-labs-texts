Ultra-Fast Accurate AoA Estimation via Automotive Massive-MIMO Radar
Bin Li, Shusen Wang, Jun Zhang, Xianbin Cao, Chenglin Zhao

Abstract
Massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) radar, enabled by millimeter-wave virtual MIMO techniques, provides great promises to the high-resolution automotive sensing and target detection in unmanned ground/aerial vehicles (UGA/UAV). As a long-established problem, however, existing subspace methods suffer from either high complexity or low accuracy. In this work, we propose two efficient methods, to accomplish fast subspace computation and accurate angle of arrival (AoA) acquisition. By leveraging randomized low-rank approximation, our fast multiple signal classification (MUSIC) methods, relying on random sampling and projection techniques, substantially accelerate the subspace estimation by orders of magnitude. Moreover, we establish the theoretical bounds of our proposed methods, which ensure the accuracy of the approximated pseudo-spectrum. As demonstrated, the pseudo-spectrum acquired by our fast-MUSIC would be highly precise; and the estimated AoA is almost as accurate as standard MUSIC. In contrast, our new methods are tremendously faster than standard MUSIC. Thus, our fast-MUSIC enables the high-resolution real-time environmental sensing with massive MIMO radars, which has great potential in the emerging unmanned systems.

Popular subspace methods, such as MUSIC

Recently, a single-snapshot MUSIC was developed

Assume there are
K

{\bf S}\>=\>{\bf U}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}{\bf V}^{H}\>=\>{\bf U}_{K}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}{\bf V}_{K}^{H}+\epsilon_{n}^{2}{\bf U}_{-K}{\bf V}_{-K}^{H},

where

{\bf U}_{-K}=[{\bf u}_{K+1},\cdots,{\bf u}_{M}]\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times(M-K)}

For a Hermitian matrix
{\bf S}^{H}={\bf S}
, we further have
{\bf V}={\bf U}
.
As common, we assume
\sigma_{1}({\bf S})>\sigma_{2}({\bf S})>\cdots>\sigma_{M}({\bf S})

\sigma_{m}({\bf S})=\epsilon_{n}^{2}~~(m>K)

Then, we uniformly partition the spatial angle range

For each angle

{\bf a}(\theta)\>=\>\big[a_{0}(\theta),\,a_{1}(\theta),\,\cdots,\,a_{M-1}(\theta)\big]^{H}

Finally, we are able to evaluate the standard MUSIC pseudo-spectrum, based on the computed subspaces, i.e.

\displaystyle P_{\text{music}}(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{{\bf a}(\theta)^{H}{\bf U}_{-K}{\bf U}_{-K}^{H}{\bf a}(\theta)}=\tfrac{1}{{\bf a}(\theta)^{H}({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}){\bf a}(\theta)}.

(5)

And then, each peak in the pseudo-spectrum

So, unknown AoAs,

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\big\{\theta_{k},~\theta_{k}=\texttt{peak}\{P_{\text{music}}(\theta)\}\big\}.

(6)

In practice, the pseudo-spectrum

The computation of the whole pseudo-spectrum

requires a time complexity

Fortunately,

can be evaluated in a highly efficient manner

]

As seen, the involved complexity mainly comes from the decomposition of large covariance

Then, the sketching matrix is abstracted as
{\bf C}={\bf S}(\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}},{\mathcal{I}})={\bf S}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}
, whereby
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times p}
is the equivalent sampling matrix.
As seen,
{\bf C}~\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times p}
contains
p
columns of
{\bf S}
indexed by
{\mathcal{I}}
.
In the equivalent sampling matrix
\Pi
, there are
p
columns contains only one non-zero entry
\sqrt{M/p}
(the remaining
(M-p)
columns are all zeros).

By minimizing the approximation error, we further obtain another weight matrix
{\bf W}_{\text{opt}}
, i.e.

{\bf W}_{\text{opt}}=\arg\min_{{\bf W}}\|{\bf S}-{\bf C}{\bf W}{\bf C}^{H}\|_{F}^{2}={\bf C}^{\dagger}{\bf S}{{\bf C}^{\dagger}}^{H}.

(7)

Since the involved pseudo-inverse and matrix multiplication incur a high complexity, we tend to the other scheme to compute
{\bf W}\simeq{\bf W}_{\text{opt}}
.
To be specific, we solve the above over-determined system by sketching both
{\bf S}
and its random approximation.
This leads to the so-called Nyström approximation
[
52
,
47
]
; and the weight matrix is determined by:

{\bf W}=\arg\min_{{\bf W}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}^{H}({\bf S}-{\bf C}{\bf W}{\bf C}^{H})\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\|_{F}^{2}=(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}^{H}{\bf S}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath})^{\dagger}.

(8)

Finally, a large covariance matrix
{\bf S}
is approximated by:

{\bf S}\simeq\tilde{{\bf S}}\>\triangleq\>{\bf C}{\bf W}{\bf C}^{H}.

(9)

Relying on this low-rank representation of
{\bf S}
, its SVD can be efficiently computed via multiple SVDs of these small sketches.
Assume
{\bf C}={\bf U}_{c}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}{\bf V}_{c}^{H}
, the covariance matrix
{\bf S}
would be further approximated by:

{\bf S}\simeq{\bf U}_{c}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}{\bf V}_{c}^{H}{\bf W}({\bf U}_{c}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}{\bf V}_{c}^{H})^{H}.

After defining
\textbf{B}\triangleq\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}{\bf V}_{c}^{H}{\bf W}{\bf V}_{c}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}^{H}

and computing its SVD, i.e.
\textbf{B}={\bf U}_{B}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B}{\bf V}_{B}^{H}\in{\mathbb C}^{p\times p},

we would obtain the rank-restricted approximate SVD of
{\bf S}
, i.e.

{\bf S}\simeq{\bf U}_{c}{\bf U}_{B}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B}{\bf V}_{B}^{H}{\bf U}_{c}^{H}=\bar{{\bf U}}_{K}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B}\bar{{\bf U}}_{K}^{H},

whereby
\bar{{\bf U}}_{K}\triangleq{\bf U}_{c}\textbf{U}_{B}
is one unitary matrix.
Relying on it, we finally obtain an approximated pseudo-spectrum, i.e.

\tilde{P}_{\textrm{{music}}}(\theta)\>=\>\frac{1}{{\bf a}(\theta)^{H}({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}_{K}\tilde{{\bf U}}_{K}^{H}){\bf a}(\theta)}.

(10)

The schematic flow of our fast-MUSIC method 1 is summarized in
Algorithm
1
, which actually obtains the rank-restricted SVD of a large covariance and thus reduces the time complexity significantly.
Based on the above analysis, we easily see that:

{\mathcal{O}}(pM)
time.

to

{\mathcal{O}}(p^{2}M)
time.
Combined together, the overall complexity of our fast method 1 in estimating the signal subspace is
{\mathcal{O}}(p^{2}M)

III-B

Fast MUSIC Method – 2

The above random sampling method enables a highly efficient approximation of large covariance as well as the pseudo-spectrum.
In practice, this approximated pseudo-spectrum in eq. (10) is almost accurate, which suffices to attain the high-resolution DoAs estimation.
Nevertheless, one potential risk is that its error bound would be relatively weak in the worst case; see Section
IV-C
for the theoretical analysis.

If the precise matrix approximation was emphasized, we may resort to the other random projection technique
[
53
]
.
Here, the main difference with regard to our fast method 1 is that, when abstracting a sketch
{\bf C}
, a more informative projection matrix
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times p}
is adopted, i.e.
{\bf C}={\bf S}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}
, which will be computed in an
iterative
manner.
Compared with the random sampling based matrix sketch as in
Algorithm 1
, this random projection based sketch incorporates more information of
{\bf S}
and permits the more accurate approximation.

To begin with, we first initialize one
M\times p
random matrix
\Pi
, whose entries are i.i.d. values from the Normal distribution
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
.
Then, we obtain one randomly projected sketch
{\bf C}^{(1)}={\bf S}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\in{\mathbb C}^{M\times p}
.
To improve the quality of this sketching matrix, we further compute the orthonormal basis of
{\bf C}^{(1)}
,

[{\bf Q}_{c},{\bf R}_{c}]=\texttt{qr}({\bf S}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}),~~{\bf V}^{(1)}\triangleq{\bf Q}_{c},

where
\texttt{qr}(\cdot)
denotes the QR decomposition with the time complexity
\mathcal{O}(p^{2}M)
; and therefore, the
M\times p
matrix
{\bf V}
gives another improved projection matrix.
Subsequently, we obtain one updated sketch
{\bf C}^{(2)}={\bf S}{\bf V}^{(1)}\in{\mathbb C}^{M\times p}
, and further obtain its orthonormal basis
{\bf V}^{(2)}
, i.e.

[{\bf Q}_{c},{\bf R}_{c}]=\texttt{qr}\left({\bf S}{\bf V}^{(1)}\right),~~{\bf V}^{(2)}\triangleq{\bf Q}_{c}.

(11)

The above process would be repeated for
t
times, and finally we achieve a sufficiently good matrix sketch
{\bf C}^{(t)}={\bf S}{\bf V}^{(t)}\in{\mathbb C}^{M\times p}
.
Similar to eq. (8), the
p\times p
weight matrix is:

{\bf W}=({{\bf V}^{(t)}}^{H}{\bf S}{\bf V}^{(t)})^{\dagger}.

With
{\bf C}={\bf C}^{(t)}
and
{\bf W}
, we are able to obtain the projection-based matrix approximation, i.e.
{\bf S}\simeq{\bf C}{\bf W}{\bf C}^{H}
.
In a similar way, the SVD of
{\bf S}
is approximately attained by multiple SVDs on the small sketches, i.e.
{\bf S}\simeq\tilde{{\bf U}}_{K}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B}\tilde{{\bf U}}_{K}^{H}
.
Finally, the pseudo-spectrum
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{{music}}}(\theta)
is estimated, as in eq. (10).

The time complexity of our fast method 2 is measured by
{\mathcal{O}}(tpM^{2})
, see
Algorithm 2
.
Note that, despite the similar random projection as in the Lanczos method, here our method targets at deriving small sketches and a matrix approximation in eq. (9), which is faster than the block-Lanczos method, as shown in Table I.
In our fast method 2, the user-specific parameter
t
trades off the complexity and the accuracy.
E.g., a large
t
improves the projection matrix and the matrix approximation accuracy, but also increases the complexity.

One practical concern is that the noise may become non-Gaussian.
In this case, a generalized eigen-decomposition problem can be similarly formulated
[
54
]
, by resorting to high-order cumulates techniques.
As a result, EVD/SVD can be still used to acquire unknown AoA.
In this regard, our new methods are readily applicable to reduce the time complexity of subspace computation in the case of non-Gaussian noises.

IV
Performance Bounds for Fast-MUSIC

Our primary concern is then the accuracy of the approximated pseudo-spectrum
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{{music}}}(\theta)
relative to its exact version
P_{\textrm{{music}}}(\theta)
.
In the following, we analyze the theoretical performance of our fast-MUSIC methods, giving the relative error between the approximated pseudo-spectrum in eq. (10) and the exact pseudo-spectrum in eq. (
5
) as standard MUSIC.

It should be noted that, from a theoretical perspective, the existing theories or error bounds developed for randomized matrix sketching are inapplicable to our concerned problem.
This is to say, the ultimate aim of our fast-MUSIC method is to approximate an exact pseudo-spectrum
P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta)
, and then to acquire the high-resolution AoAs estimation.
Unfortunately, most existing theories only bound the matrix approximation errors
[
48
,
53
,
47
]
, e.g.
\|{\bf S}-\tilde{{\bf S}}\|_{F}
or
\|{\bf S}-\tilde{{\bf S}}\|_{2}
.
Accordingly, such matrix norm bounds should not lend any support to our fast-MUSIC methods.

In this section, we show the relative error of our estimated pseudo-spectrum
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta)
would be also bounded, after approximating
{\bf S}
with
\tilde{{\bf S}}
, which is more challenging than to derive the classical matrix norm bounds.
Our Theorems
1
and
2
establish the theoretical lower bounds for
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{{music}}}(\theta)
.
Theorem
3
establishes the theoretical upper bound for it.

\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta)

lower bound
in the following form:

\alpha_{l}

IV-E

Accurate DoA Estimation

As shown latterly, in the context of massive-MIMO radars, the target peaks of pseudo-spectrum, which are located exactly at target AoAs, are significantly higher than noise baseline,

P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})\gg P_{0},

where
P_{0}\triangleq\max\left\{P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\}
denotes the maximum amplitude of non-target regions, i.e.
\theta^{\prime}\notin\boldsymbol{\theta}_{K\times 1}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume it almost satisfies
P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})/P_{0}>\gamma
, when the SNR is not very small (e.g.
\gamma\thicksim M
; see the following Figure 3).
If the approximated pseudo-spectrum misses one target peak located at
\theta_{k}
, we should have

\sqrt{\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})}\leq\sqrt{P_{0}}.

(17)

At the same time, according to our theoretical lower bounds in eq. (13), we are supposed to have

\tfrac{1}{1+\alpha_{l}}\sqrt{P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})}\leq\sqrt{\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})}\leq\sqrt{P_{0}},

and therefore,

\sqrt{\gamma}\leq\sqrt{P_{\textrm{music}}(\theta_{k})/P_{0}}\leq 1+\alpha_{l}.

(18)

On the other hand, according to the following numerical analysis (e.g. in Figure 5), we would have
1+\alpha_{l}\rightarrow 1
and

1+\alpha_{l}<\sqrt{\gamma}.

(19)

By checking eq. (18) and (19), we immediately conclude this contradiction result indicates eq. (17) should be invalid.
I.e., the approximated pseudo-spectrum
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta)
should not miss the true peaks of targets.
Similarly, from the upper bound in eq. (14), we show the approximated pseudo-spectrum
\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}(\theta)
should not mistake non-target AoA as one target peak, thus preventing the false alarm in automotive sensing.

VI
Conclusion

We investigate the high-resolution target detection and AoA estimation problem in the automotive massive-MIMO radar.
Existing subspace methods (e.g. MUSIC and ESPRIT) are computationally expensive, due to the complex decomposition of a large covariance matrix.
Other simplified schemes, e.g. matrix-inverse and propagator methods, lead to the degraded accuracy.
As one long-standing dilemma in MIMO radar signal processing, the high-resolution estimation and the real-time computation remain mutually exclusive.

To enable real-time and high-resolution environmental sensing, we leverage on randomized matrix approximation and develop two fast-MUSIC methods, whereby a large covariance was approximated by three small sketches that are abstracted via random sampling/projection.
We theoretically show that, in high SNRs, our fast-MUSIC is almost as good as the near-optimal MUSIC.
The numerical study also demonstrates that, whilst our method is nearly as accurate as MUSIC in acquiring unknown AoA, it is dramatically faster than standard MUSIC by orders-of-magnitude.
As such, our fast-MUSIC enjoys both the linear complexity and the high accuracy, breaking the theoretical bottleneck in MIMO radar signal processing.
It would provide the great potential to high-resolution and real-time massive-MIMO radars in the emerging automotive applications.
In the future study, we may consider the extension of this new concept of randomized matrix sketching to the other methods, e.g. subspace methods (e.g. Capton method) and the optimal ML method.

{\bf V}

{\bf S}^{t}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}
,

{\bf C}

{\bf S}^{t+1}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}

\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf S}^{t+1}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\>=\>{\bf S}^{t+1}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}.

(30)

In the same way as the proof of Lemma
5
,
we can prove that for all

:

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}\leq\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf x}}\big\|_{2}.

(31)

We artificially construct

\displaystyle=\>\big({\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}+{\bf S}^{t+1}-{\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}\big)\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}.

In the same way as the proof of Lemma

, we can use

\displaystyle{\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}

\displaystyle=\>{\bf U}_{K}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}^{t+1}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}

VI-D

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf x}}\>=\>{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}+\>\big({\bf S}^{t+1}-{\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}\big)\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}.

Complete the proof of Theorem
2
:

Proof.

Lemma
3
shows that the spectral norm of the
M\times K
standard Gaussian matrix
\Pi

\big\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big\|_{2}\>\leq\>\sqrt{M}+\sqrt{K}+{\mathcal{O}}(1),\qquad\textrm{almost surely}.

{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}

It follows from Lemma
4
that

\sigma_{K}^{-1}({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath})\>\leq\>\tfrac{\sqrt{K}}{\delta}

with probability at least
1-\delta-o(1)
.

6
that

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big(\tfrac{\sigma_{K+1}({\bf S})}{\sigma_{K}({\bf S})}\big)^{t+1}\,\big\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big\|_{2}\,\big\|({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath})^{\dagger}\big\|_{2}\,\big\|{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big(\tfrac{\sigma_{K+1}({\bf S})}{\sigma_{K}({\bf S})}\big)^{t+1}\,\frac{\sqrt{MK}+K+{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{K})}{\delta}\big\|{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}.

The proof follows from the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\sqrt{\tfrac{P_{\textrm{music}}}{\tilde{P}_{\textrm{music}}}}\>=\>\tfrac{\big\|({\bf I}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}}{\big\|({\bf I}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}}\leq\>1+\tfrac{\big\|\big({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}\big){\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}}{\big\|({\bf I}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}}.

by incorporating eq. (

Proof of Theorem
3
:

Proof.

It can be shown that for all
\tilde{{\bf X}}
:

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\big\|_{2}\leq\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf X}}\big\|_{2}.

We artifically construct

\tilde{{\bf X}}\>=\>\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf S}^{t+1}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}.

In the same way as the proof of Lemma
6
, we can show that

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf X}}\>=\>{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}+\>\big({\bf S}^{t+1}-{\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}\big)\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}.

Thus

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big\|\big({\bf S}^{t+1}-{\bf S}^{t+1}_{K}\big)\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big)^{\dagger}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{K}^{-t-1}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big(\tfrac{\sigma_{K+1}({\bf S})}{\sigma_{K}({\bf S})}\big)^{t+1}\,\big\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath}\big\|_{2}\,\big\|({\bf U}_{K}^{H}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$\unboldmath})^{\dagger}\big\|_{2}.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
2
that

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big[1+o(1)\big]\,\tfrac{\sqrt{MK}}{\delta}\,\big(\tfrac{\sigma_{K+1}({\bf S})}{\sigma_{K}({\bf S})}\big)^{t+1}

holds with probability at least
1-\delta
.
Since
{\bf U}_{K}
and
\tilde{{\bf U}}
are both
M\times K
,
[
63
, Eqn 2.54]
shows that

\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}){\bf U}_{K}\big\|_{2}\>=\>\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H})\tilde{{\bf U}}\big\|_{2}.

It follows that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H})\tilde{{\bf U}}\big\|_{2}\>=\>\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}){\bf U}_{K}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big[1+o(1)\big]\,\tfrac{\sqrt{MK}}{\delta}\,\big(\tfrac{\sigma_{K+1}({\bf S})}{\sigma_{K}({\bf S})}\big)^{t+1}.

(33)

We have that

\displaystyle\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H})\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H})({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\>\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-{\bf U}_{K}{\bf U}_{K}^{H})\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}{\bf a}\big\|_{2}+\big\|({\bf I}_{M}-\tilde{{\bf U}}\tilde{{\bf U}}^{H}){\bf a}\big\|_{2}.

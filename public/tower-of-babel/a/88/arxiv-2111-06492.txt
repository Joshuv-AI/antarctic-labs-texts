Invariant Measure for Neutral Stochastic Functional Differential Equations with Non-Lipschitz Coeffiecients
Andriy Stanzhytskyi, Oleksandr Stanzhytskyi, Oleksandr Misiats

Abstract
In this work we study the long time behavior of nonlinear stochastic functional-differential equations of neutral type in Hilbert spaces with non-Lipschitz nonlinearities. We establish the existence of invariant measures in the shift spaces for such equations. Our approach is based on Krylov-Bogoliubov theorem on the tightness of the family of measures.



1
Introduction

In this work we study the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions of neutral type stochastic functional-differential equations of the form

\displaystyle d[u(t)+g(u_{t})]=[Au+f(u_{t})]dt+\sigma(u_{t})dW(t)\text{ for }t>0;

(1)

\displaystyle u(t)=\varphi(t),t\in[-h,0),\ h>0.

Here
A
is an inifinitesimal generator of a strong continuous semigroup
\{S(t),t\geq 0\}
of bounded linear operators in real separable Hilbert space
H
. The noise
W(t)
is a
Q
-Wiener process on a separable Hilbert space
K
. For any
h>0
denote
C_{h}:=C([-h,0],H)
to be a space of continuous
H
-valued functions
\varphi:[-h,0]\to H
, equipped with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{C_{h}}:=\sup_{t\in[-h,0]}\|\varphi(t)\|_{H},

where
\|\cdot\|_{H}
stands for the norm in
H
. Throughout this paper,
\|\cdot\|_{H}
will be denoted with
\|\cdot\|
. The solution
u(t)
of (
1
) is sometimes referred as a
state process
. We also denote
u_{t}:=u(t+\theta)
, where
\theta\in[-h,0)
. The functionals
f
and
g
map
C_{h}
to
H
, and
\sigma:C_{h}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}=\mathcal{L}(Q^{1/2}K,H)
is the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
Q^{1/2}K
to
H
. Finally,
\varphi:[-h,0]\times\Omega\to H
is the initial condition, where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is the probability space.

Differential equations of neutral type arise in modeling the memory effects in various dynamical processes (e.g. heat conduction). While the classic heat equation
u_{t}=\Delta u
accurately describes the heat transfer
by conduction in many materials, this model has two important drawbacks. First, the classic heat equation does not take into account the memory effects which may be present in some materials, especially at low temperatures. Most importantly, the heat equation predicts an unrealistic result: that a
thermal disturbance at one point of the body is instantly felt everywhere in the body,
although not uniformly. In other words in Fourier heat conductors, finite thermal discontinuities must propagate with infinite speed. These observations lead one to
believe that in materials with memory, the Fourier’s law of heat conduction may be an approximation (perhaps for sufficiently steady temperature fields) to a more general nonlinear constitutive assumption relating the heat flux to the material’s thermal history. Gurtin and Pipkin
[
10
]
have proposed a memory theory of heat
conduction which is independent of the present value of the temperature gradient, generalizes constitutive relations deduced from kinetic theory by Maxwell
[
20
]

and Cattaneo
[
3
]
, and has finite heat propagation speeds. The linearized version of Gurkin-Pipkin’s model reads as

\dot{u}(x,t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(s)\dot{u}(x,t-s)\,ds=C\Delta u(x,t),

which is a particular example of a neutral type differential equation. Similar memory effects emerge in Hodgkin-Huxley model, Dawson-Fleming model of population genetics
[
6
]
and other models.

The natural first step in the analysis of (
1
) is to establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions and their continuous dependence on the initial data. The question of well-posedness was addressed, e.g. in
[
18
]
under the Lipschitz conditions. This condition was somewhat relaxed in
[
13
]
. The work
[
1
]
addressed similar questions for the equations with impulsive action, as well as with infinite delays. However, in our opinion, the proof of the main result in aforementioned works is not quite complete. The reason is that this proof is based on a successive approximation scheme, which is implicit. In particular, the existence of a solution at each iteration step is highly nontrivial and requires a separate proof.

On the other hand,
[
8
]
correctly establishes the existence and uniqueness of mild solution under the Lipschits and linear growth assumptions. Under the similar condition the works
[
26
,
2
]
establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions if the equation is perturbed with Brownian motion, as well as fractional Brownian motion. However, the continuous dependence on the initial conditions was not established there.

To the best of our knowledge, the question of the existence of invariant measures for nonlinear equations of type (
1
) has not been addressed in literature. One exception is the work
[
17
]
, which established the existence of a stationary solution for a linear equation of type (
1
).

The main goal of this paper is to establish the existence of invariant measure for the equation (
1
) using Krylov-Bogoliubov theory on the tightness of a family of measures
[
15
]
. More precisely, we will implement the compactness approach of Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
5
]
. This approach was used in establishing the existence of invariant measure for a large class of stochastic partial differential equations with no delay
[
24
,
23
,
22
,
12
]
. The results on long time behavior of ordinary differential equations of similar nature were obtained in
[
29
,
28
]
. The existence of periodic solutions for neutral functional differential equations in deterministic case were derived in
[
16
]
. Analogous results for non-neutral type stochastic functional differential equations were established in
[
21
]
.

In order to apply the compactness approach, we need to establish that the solutions are both Markovian and Feller. These properties were established for (
1
) in
[
14
]
in case when
A
is Laplacian. The analog of comparison theorem for such equations was established in
[
30
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary notation and preliminary results, as well as formulate the main results. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the main result. In Section
4
we discuss the application of the main results of the paper.

Suppose

-x\ln(x),\ 0<x\leq e^{-2};\\
x+e^{-2},\ x>e^{-2}.\end{cases}

(32)

2.4

which, in turn, yields
(H2)
(b) for two
constant

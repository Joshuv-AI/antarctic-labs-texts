On the representations and ℤ 2 {{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}} -equivariant normal form for solenoidal Hopf-zero singularities
Fahimeh Mokhtari

Abstract
In this paper, we deal with the solenoidal conservative Lie algebra associated to the classical normal form of Hopf-zero singular system. We concentrate on the study of some representations and ℤ 2 {{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}} -equivariant normal form for such singular differential equations. First, we list some of the representations that this Lie algebra admits. The vector fields from this Lie algebra could be expressed by the set of ordinary differential equations where the first two of them are in the canonical form of a one-degree of freedom Hamiltonian system and the third one depends upon the first two variables. This representation is governed by the associated Poisson algebra to one sub-family of this Lie algebra. Euler’s form, vector potential, and Clebsch representation are other representations of this Lie algebra that we list here. We also study the non-potential property of vector fields with Hopf-zero singularity from this Lie algebra. Finally, we examine the unique normal form with non-zero cubic terms of this family in the presence of the symmetry group ℤ 2 . {{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}. The theoretical results of normal form theory are illustrated with the modified Chua’s oscillator. Key words. Hopf-pitchfork singularity; Conservative and solenoidal vector field; Clebsch representation; Euler’s form; Vector potential; Normal form. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 34C20; 34A34.

2.3
Euler’s form for
\mathscr{L}

Any solenoidal vector field may be represented by Euler’s form as

\displaystyle v=h(g_{1},g_{2})\nabla g_{1}\times\nabla g_{2},

(2.11)

where
h,g_{1},
and
g_{2}
are scalar valued functions. We remark that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
may not be defined everywhere on the domain of

. The functions

g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are
first integrals or vector sheets of vector field
v

and named Euler’s potentials.
The geometrical meaning of Euler’s form is that
the vector lines of solenoidal vector fields are the intersection of level surfaces of Euler’s potentials, see
[
37
, page 22]
,
Euler’s Theorem

[
38
, page 48]
, and
[
23
]
.

In the following result, we express the vector fields in
\mathscr{L}
by local Euler’s potentials.

Theorem 2.5
.

The Euler’s form of
{F}^{l}_{k}
and
\Theta^{l}_{k}
are given as follows

\displaystyle{F}^{l}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\arctan({\frac{z}{y}})\right)\times\nabla\left(x^{l+1}(y^{2}+z^{2})^{k-l+1}\right),\qquad\hbox{for}\quad y\neq 0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle{{\Theta}^{l}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\left(x^{l+1}\right)\times\nabla\left({\frac{-\left({y}^{2}+{z}^{2}\right)^{k-l+1}}{2\left(l+1\right)\left(k-l+1\right)}}\right).

(2.13)

Proof.

We prove this theorem only for
{F}^{l}_{k}.

The proof
for
\Theta^{l}_{k}

can be done analogously.
Due to the solenoidal property of vector fields in
\mathscr{L}
and Euler’s Theorem

[

, page 48]
,

may be written as (

).
Setting the first integrals of

as local Euler’s potentials, in fact,
g_{1}:=x^{l+1}(y^{2}+z^{2})^{k-l+1}
and

Now, we show that

given by (
2.11
) equals one. By straightforward calculation, one has

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\arctan({\frac{z}{y}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{-z}{2({y}^{2}+{z}^{2})}}\cdot\mathbf{{e}_{y}}+{\frac{y}{2({y}^{2}+{z}^{2})}}\cdot\mathbf{{e}_{z}},

\displaystyle\nabla\left(x^{l+1}(y^{2}+z^{2})^{k-l+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{l}(y^{2}+z^{2})^{k-l+1}\Big((l+1)(y^{2}+z^{2})\cdot\mathbf{{e}_{x}}+2(k-l+1)xy\cdot\mathbf{{e}_{y}}

\displaystyle+2(k-l+1)xz\cdot\mathbf{{e}_{z}}\Big).

By taking the cross product of the

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\arctan({\frac{z}{y}})\right)\times\nabla\left(x^{l+1}(y^{2}+z^{2})^{k-l+1}\right)=F^{l}_{k}.

This implies that

2.5

\mathscr{L}

In this part, we shall study the following problems: namely, we shall study
the non-potential property of Hopf-zero singularities from
\mathscr{L},
and we shall present an alternative representation that is the Clebsch representation of any vector fields from
\mathscr{L}.

Based on this representation
for a given vector field
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

in which

\nabla\times v\neq 0,

one can find three
scalar valued functions

and

such that

\displaystyle v=f_{1}\nabla f_{2}+\nabla f_{3}.

(2.19)

These scalar valued functions are called Clebsch potentials of
v.
Noting that
f_{1},f_{2}
and
f_{2}
may not be defined everywhere on the domain of

Moreover,
this representation shows that any vector fields may be presented by summation of the lamellar field and complex lamellar field,
see
Definition
2.2
.
For those interested in knowing that how this representation is constructed, we include the following discussion from the proof of Clebsch’s Theorem of
[
38
, Section 2.4]
.

Since
\nabla\times v
is a solenoidal vector field then, there exist local Euler’s potentials
f_{1}
and
f_{2}

such that
\nabla\times v=\nabla f_{1}\times\nabla f_{2}.

Making use of the vector calculus identity
we have

\nabla\times(v-f_{1}\nabla f_{2})=0.
Hence,

is
irrotational vector field, then there exists potential function
f_{3}
such that

see Definition
2.2
. Then, the relation (
2.19
) follows.

For the Euler’s form (and consequently the representation given by vector potential) to be possible,
the vector field

has to be solenoidal.
We remark that
deriving the vector field

v
from Clebsch potentials
f_{1},f_{2},
and
f_{3}
through the relation
(
2.19
), is not related to the solenoidal property of vector field

In fact,
any vector field may be represented
in the Clebsch representation, see
[
38
, Section 2.4]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Any
v\in\mathscr{L}
with Hopf-zero singularity is the non-potential vector field.

Proof.

Recall from
[
16
]
, the grading function for generators of
\mathscr{L}

given by
\delta({\rm F}^{l}_{k})=\delta({\Theta}^{l}_{k})=k.

Suppose that the non-zero Hopf-zero vector field

where

is given.
Assume that the claim of the theorem does not hold.
Similar to argument given in

]
,
since the vector fields with different grades do not have any monomial in common, it implies that

if and only if
\nabla\times v_{j}=0,
for all

Thus without loss of generality, it suffices to show that

Taking into account the defined grading function, the vector field

may be represented by

v_{j}:=\sum_{i=-1}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}a_{i}{F}^{i}_{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}b_{i}\Theta^{i}_{j},

where

are real constants for all

Applying the curl operator to

\displaystyle\nabla\times v_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=-1}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}(0,0,a_{i}\frac{i({i}+1)}{2}{x}^{{i}-1}{\rho}^{2(j-{i})+1}+2a_{i}(j-i)(j-i+1){x}^{{i}+1}{\rho}^{2(j-{i})-1})

This implies that either

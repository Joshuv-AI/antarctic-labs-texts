Goodwillie Towers of ∞ \infty -categories and Desuspension
Daniel Fuentes-Keuthan

Abstract
. We reconceptualize the process of forming n n -excisive approximations to ∞ \infty -categories, in the sense of Heuts, as inverting the suspension functor lifted to A n A_{n} -cogroup objects. We characterize n n -excisive ∞ \infty -categories as those ∞ \infty -categories in which A n A_{n} -cogroup objects admit desuspensions. Applying this result to pointed spaces we reprove a theorem of Klein-Schwänzl-Vogt: every 2-connected cogroup-like A ∞ A_{\infty} -space admits a desuspension.



Introduction

Given a suitably nice
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
one may form the stablization
Sp(\mathcal{C})
by formally inverting the suspension functor
\Sigma:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
, as given by the filtered colimit

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\dots\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\dots\to Sp(\mathcal{C})

This new category
Sp(\mathcal{C})
is easier to work with: it is the homotopic analogue of the category of abelian group objects in
\mathcal{C}
and satisfies formal properties which make it into a sort of linearization of
\mathcal{C}
. This stabilization procedure however loses a lot of information about
\mathcal{C}
, since, for instance, in
Sp(\mathcal{C})
we can recover an object from only its suspension. One might remedy this harsh truncation by requiring additional structure on the suspension in order to recover the object. In particular the suspension functor lifts to a functor landing in
A_{\infty}
-cogroup objects in
\mathcal{C}
.

\Sigma:\mathcal{C}\to coGrp_{\infty}(\mathcal{C})

By forgetting down to the
A_{n}
-cogroup structures for each
n
one obtains a collection of functors

\Sigma:\mathcal{C}\to coGrp_{n}(\mathcal{C})

which can be formally inverted by taking the filtered colimit of

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}coGrp_{n}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\Sigma}coGrp_{n}(coGrp_{n}(\mathcal{C}))\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\cdots

to give a tower of approximations to
\mathcal{C}
which capture more information as
n
grows larger. In particular
coGrp_{1}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{C}
so the first stage of this process is just the stabilization
Sp(\mathcal{C})
.

In
[
Heu15
]
Heuts introduces another tower of
\infty
-categories which interpolates between
\mathcal{C}
and
Sp(\mathcal{C})

\dots\xrightarrow{\Sigma_{n+2,n+1}}P_{n+1}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma_{n+1,n}}P_{n}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\Sigma_{n,n-1}}\dots P_{1}\mathcal{C}\simeq Sp(\mathcal{C})

via the
n
-excisive approximations
P_{n}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathcal{C}
. We refer to this tower as the Goodwillie tower of
\mathcal{C}
. The
\infty
-category
P_{n}(\mathcal{C})
is built by formally inverting the suspension functor viewed as landing in an
\infty
-category
T_{n}(\mathcal{C})
of certain “special” punctured
(n+1)
-cubes.

The main goal of this paper is to prove that the two constructions above are the same by showing that the
\infty
-category
T_{n}(\mathcal{C})
gives a new model for
A_{n}
-cogroup objects in
\mathcal{C}
. Namely we have Theorem
1.22
:

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed
\infty
-category with finite colimits, then there is an equivalence

coGrp_{n}(\mathcal{C})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}T_{n}(\mathcal{C})

As a corollary of this result we obtain a characterization of
n
-excsisive
\infty
-categories as those
\infty
-categories in which one can desuspend
A_{n}
-cogroup objects. This is Theorem
2.5
:

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed, compactly generated
\infty
-category. Then
\mathcal{C}
is
n
-excisive if and only if the suspension functor induces an equivalence

\Sigma:\mathcal{C}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}coGrp_{n}(\mathcal{C})

Passing to the limit as
n
goes to infinity of the Goodwillie tower, one obtains an
\infty
-category
P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
which best approximates
\mathcal{C}
from the point of view of Goodwillie calculus. Theorem
2.5
extends to this
\infty
-category to obtain an
A_{\infty}
desuspension theorem, stated as Theorem
2.14
:

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed, compactly generated
\infty
-category. The suspension functor induces an equivalence

\Sigma:P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}coGrp_{\infty}(P_{\infty}\mathcal{C})

These results together motivate a philosophical claim that Goodwillie calculus attempts to reconstruct
\infty
-categories and functors via the
A_{\infty}
cogroup structure on the suspension functor.

As an application, in this new light we may translate certain desuspension problems into problems of the convergence of the Goodwillie tower. For example, from knowing that the Goodwillie tower convergences on simply connected spaces, we are able to recover the following theorem of Klein-Schwänzl-Vogt, which we state as Theorem
3.2

Theorem
.

The suspension functor induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\Sigma:\mathcal{S}_{*}^{\geq 2}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}coGrp_{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{*}^{\geq 3})

In particular every 2-connected
A_{\infty}
-comonoid admits a desuspension to a 1-connected space.

A brief outline of this article is as follows:

(1)

In Section

we develop the punctured cube model of

-cogroup objects and prove Theorem

which states that our model is equivalent to the existing model developed by Lurie in

.

(2)

and Theorem

which establishes the connection between Goodwillie calculus and cogroup objects.

(3)

In Section

to the special case of pointed spaces in order to recover Theorem

: that all 2-connected
A_{\infty}
-cogroup objects admit a desuspension.

1.
Punctured Cubes as coGroups

In this section we develop a new model for
A_{n}
-cogroup objects in an
\infty
-category in terms of certain punctured cubical diagrams, and prove that this model is equivalent to the model dual to the
A_{n}
-group model developed in
[
Lur20
, Section 4.1]
. The dual story holds for group objects as well, but we will not need this. We begin by recalling the established formalism of
A_{n}
-comonoid objects.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Delta^{inj}
be the wide subcategory of the category
\Delta
containing only the injections. Let
\Delta_{n}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{inj}
be the full subcategory on the objects
[0],[1],...,[n]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Throughout this paper when we discuss maps in
\Delta
we only ever consider the injections.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed
\infty
-category with finite colimits. A (unital)
A_{n}
-comonoid object
in
C
is a functor
A:\Delta_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
satisfying the conditions

(1)

A[0]
is a zero object.

(2)

For each
t\leq n
the maps
p_{k}:[1]\rightarrow[t]
sending 0 to 0 and 1 to
k
induce an equivalence

\coprod_{i=1}^{n}A[1]\xrightarrow{\sim}A[t]

(3)

The following composites are the identity on
A[1]

A[1]\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}A[2]\simeq A[1]\amalg A[1]\rightarrow A[1]\amalg 0\simeq A[1]

A[1]\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}A[2]\simeq A[1]\amalg A[1]\rightarrow 0\amalg A[1]\simeq A[1]

where the right hand arrow is the coproduct of the identity of
A[1]
and the zero map.

Let
coMon_{n}(\mathcal{C})\hookrightarrow Fun(\Delta_{n},\mathcal{C})
denote the full subcategory spanned by the
A_{n}
-comonoid objects in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 1.4
.

For
n=0,1
conditions
(2)
and
(3)
do not make sense. By convention an
A_{0}
-comonoid object is a zero object, and an
A_{1}
-comonoid object is simply an object of
\mathcal{C}
. The second condition is dual to the usual Segal condition for a monoid, the third condition says that the comultiplication induced by the Segal conditions is counital. Because we are in a pointed setting we have only one choice of counit, greatly simplifying the data needed to specify counitality.

Definition 1.5
.

An
A_{n}
-comonoid object
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is cogroup-like if it satisfies conditions 1 and 3 above, and the stronger condition
(2^{\prime})
.

(2’)

For each
t\leq n
and partition of
[n]
into subsets
S,S^{\prime}
agreeing on at most one element the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A[S\cap S^{\prime}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A[S]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A[S^{\prime}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A[n]}

is a pushout square in
\mathcal{C}
.

In particular condition
(2^{\prime})
implies the usual Segal condition
(2)
. We will denote by

the
\infty
-category of cogroup-like

-comonoids in
\mathcal{C}
. We will refer to these objects as

-cogroup objects.

.

A functor

(2”)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\cap S^{\prime}}

.

Lemma 2.15
.

In the diagram
\mathcal{J}_{\bullet,\bullet}
above there are natural equivalences

\hat{P}_{\infty}\mathcal{C}\simeq colim_{n}lim_{m}\mathcal{J}_{n,m}\text{ and }P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}\simeq lim_{n}colim_{m}\mathcal{J}_{n,m}

In particular the natural functor

induces a functor
\hat{P}_{\infty}\mathcal{C}\rightarrow P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
which factors the comparison functor
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma

and the proof of Theorem

.
∎

Because filtered colimits and filtered limits do not commute in general in
\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{at}^{\omega}
it seems unlikely that this map is an equivalence. However one does have this result in the case that the Goodwillie tower converges.

Lemma 2.16
.

Suppose the comparison functor
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow P_{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence, then the functor
\hat{P}_{\infty}\mathcal{C}\rightarrow P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}P_{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence then
\Sigma:\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}coGrp_{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence by Theorem
2.14
and so
\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\hat{P}_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence. The result then follows by the two out of three property for equivalences.
∎

It does not however appear that
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\hat{P}_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
being an equivalence implies that
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow P_{\infty}\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence. Convergence of the Goodwillie tower remains a stronger result.

in this case gives us the following result.

Remark 3.4
.

that the suspension functor

We end with a discussion on
n
-excisive approximations to functors. A reduced functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is 1-excisive if it takes a suspension diagram to a pullback. This tells us that the value of
F
on
X
can be recovered in
\mathcal{D}
from the suspension
F(X)\simeq\Omega F(\Sigma X)
. In light of Theorem
2.5
, we see that a reduced functor is
n
-excisive when it can recover its values not from the suspension alone, but from the suspension equipped with its
A_{n}
-cogroup structure. Examining the construction of the excisive approximation in Goodwillie’s original work
[
Goo03
]
we see this is exactly the property being forced. A functor can be recovered from its tower when the value at any object can be recovered from the functors value on the entire
A_{\infty}
-cogroup structure on the suspension.

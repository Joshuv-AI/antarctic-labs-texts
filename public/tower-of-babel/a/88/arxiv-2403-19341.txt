The Green’s function of polyharmonic operators with diverging coefficients: Construction and sharp asymptotics. Thanks: This publication is supported by the French Community of Belgium as part of the funding of a FRIA grant.
Lorenzo Carletti

Abstract
. We show existence, uniqueness and positivity for the Green’s function of the operator ( Δ g + α ) k (\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k} in a closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) , of dimension n > 2 ​ k n>2k , k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , with Laplace-Beltrami operator Δ g = − div g ( ∇ ⋅ ) \Delta_{g}=-\operatorname{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot) , and where α > 0 \alpha>0 . We are interested in the case where α \alpha is large : We prove pointwise estimates with explicit dependence on α \alpha for the Green’s function and its derivatives. We highlight a region of exponential decay for the Green’s function away from the diagonal, for large α \alpha .



1.
Introduction and statement of the result

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth connected Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, compact and without boundary. Let
k\geq 1
be an integer, we assume that
n>2k
. Let
\alpha>0
be a fixed real number, we consider the elliptic partial differential operator of order
2k
,
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, where we define
\Delta_{g}:=-\operatorname{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
the Laplace-Beltrami operator. In this article we construct the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, we show uniqueness and positivity, as well as sharp pointwise asymptotics. The main goal is to obtain asymptotics that explicitly depend on
\alpha
to understand the behavior of the Green’s function when
\alpha
is large.

The Green’s function for polyharmonic problems has been extensively studied in the Euclidean setting, and sharp bounds from above and below have been obtained, see for instance to
[
5
,
9
,
13
]
.
It is also worth mentioning that there exists an extensive literature for the construction of Green’s functions for standard operators of second order on common domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in particular in
\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\mathbb{R}^{3}
. See for instance
[
6
]
for an undergraduate-level textbook on the matter.

Polyharmonic operators on manifolds have been studied because of their connections with the so-called prescribed
Q
-curvature equations. These equations involve a special family of conformally invariant operators, called
GJMS operators

[
12
]
, for which a Green’s function was investigated in
[
21
]
. Green’s functions for GJMS operators have proven fundamental to obtain existence results for the prescribed
Q
-curvature equations, we refer to
[
24
]
for the conformal Laplacian, see also
[
20
]
and the references therein for higher-order
Q
-curvature equations.
Moreover, the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
can be seen as a toy-model for the GJMS operator of order
2k
, which, on an Einstein manifold, can be written as a product of
k
operators of the form
\Delta_{g}+c_{j}
(see
[
7
]
).

For any
p\geq 1
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
, let us define the norms

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{l,p}(M)}^{p}:=\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\|\Delta_{g}^{m/2}u\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{p},

where we write
\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{m/2}u\right\rvert:=\begin{cases}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{i}u\right\rvert&\text{if $m=2i$ is even,}\\
\left\lvert\nabla\Delta_{g}^{i}u\right\rvert_{g}&\text{if $m=2i+1$ is odd}\end{cases}
.
Let us also define the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{l,p}(M)
as the closure of
C^{\infty}(M)
in
L^{p}(M)
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{l,p}}
. We write
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)
for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)=\mathcal{H}^{k,2}(M)
, for
k\geq 1
integer.

In this paper, we study the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
, with
\alpha>0
, in
M
. Our main motivation to consider these specific operators comes from their importance in the study of the optimal constant for the critical Sobolev embeddings in compact manifolds. We refer to
[
18
]
for the case
k=1
, and
[
16
]
for the biharmonic case
k=2
, where the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
, for
\alpha\to\infty
, naturally appears in a contradiction argument. In a companion article
[
4
]
, we consider the general case
k\geq 1
. In that case, our proof makes an extensive use of the explicit behavior of the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
, when
\alpha\to\infty
. We require a precise description of the Green’s function depending explicitly on the coefficient
\alpha
. The present article thus provides technical results that are crucial in
[
4
]
. The geometrical context explains that no boundary conditions need to be considered here, the presence of a diverging coefficient being the main challenge.

Operators of the form
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
also naturally appear in other contexts, as they are simple toy-models to understand the effect of a large parameter in the lower-order terms. We refer to
[
2
,
22
,
26
]
for instances where concentration phenomena for sequences of solutions to critical non-linear equations are investigated in the case
k=1
. See also
[
8
]
, where the energy of sequences of solutions is studied, in the case
k=2
.

Observe that, for
\alpha>0
, the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
is coercive, since if
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}u\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\alpha^{k-l}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}u\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}=\left\langle(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-k},\mathcal{H}^{k}},

and if
\alpha<1

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{k}}\left\langle(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-k},\mathcal{H}^{k}}.

If
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the existence and uniqueness of a solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
to the linear equation

(1.1)

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u=\varphi\quad\text{on $M$}

follows from the coercivity of the operator, and from standard elliptic theory. See for instance
[
10
]
for standard existence and regularity results in the case
k=1
, which can be iterated in the case of our operator. This allows us to define a Green’s function for this operator.

Definition 1.1

(Green’s function)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
k\geq 1
and
n>2k
, and let
(M,g)
be a connected compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, without boundary, with Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}=-\operatorname{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
. A Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
is a function
G:M\times M\setminus\{(x,x)\,:\,x\in M\}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, writing
G_{x}(y):=G(x,y)
for all
x\neq y
in
M
, we have
G_{x}\in L^{1}(M)
for all
x\in M
, and for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and all
x\in M
,

\int_{M}G_{x}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,dv_{g}=\varphi(x).

This is equivalent to saying that
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}G_{x}=\delta_{x}
in the distributional sense on
M
, where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac’s delta distribution centered at
x\in M
.

In this work, we are interested in explicit
\alpha
-dependent estimates for the Green’s function and its derivatives, in particular as
\alpha
gets large. Our main result is the following Theorem. Note that a closed Riemannian manifold has a positive injectivity radius,
i_{g}>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, of dimension
n\geq 3
, let
k\geq 1
with
n>2k
, and
\alpha>0
. The operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
has a unique Green’s function
G_{g,\alpha}
, which is positive, symmetric, and is in
C^{\infty}(M\times M\setminus\{(x,x)\,:\,x\in M\})
.
Moreover, there exists
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
such that we have the following :

•

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and all
x\neq y
in
M
with
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
, we have

(1.2)

G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)=c_{n,k}\,d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}\left(1+\eta_{\alpha}(x,y)\right)

with

\left\lvert\eta_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\begin{cases}\sqrt{\alpha}\,d_{g}{(x,y)}&n=2k+1\\
\alpha\,d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2}\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}\,d_{g}{(x,y)}\right\rvert\big)&n=2k+2\\
\alpha\,d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2}&n\geq 2k+3\end{cases},

and where
c_{n,k}
is an explicit positive constant given by (
2.1
) below.

•

For all
0<\varepsilon<1
, there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and all
x,y\in M
with
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
,

G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\text{if }d_{g}{(x,y)}<i_{g}/2\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}&\text{if }d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2.\end{cases}

This Theorem highlights that
when
d_{g}{(x,y)}
is small in comparison to
1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
behaves to first order as the Green’s function for the poly-Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
, up to a remainder term on which we prove explicit bounds. On the other hand, when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, we obtain an exponential decay. In particular, any region of
M
situated at a fixed distance from a given point
x\in M
will lie in this regime as
\alpha
becomes large. Note also that most of the construction of the Green’s function does not rely on the fact that
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. It is only at
\alpha\to\infty
, however, that exponential estimates at finite distance are of interest.

We point out that in the construction of the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
that we give in Theorem
1.1
, the main difficulty resides in the presence of the diverging coefficient
\alpha
, rather than on the terms that could appear in a domain with boundary conditions. In this case, since we work on a closed manifold, there are no boundary conditions to consider. This follows from the geometrical context in which the operator appears. A new contribution of this work is the derivation of estimates on
G_{g,\alpha}
which are explicit in the lower-order terms’ coefficient. This novel consideration is mainly handled in section
3.3
. The methods developed in this article are flexible and could be adapted to construct and obtain sharp estimates on Green’s functions for more general elliptic operators with one or more diverging coefficients.

Green’s function for polyharmonic operators of order
2k
in
n
-dimensional domains or manifolds, with
n>2k
, and with bounded coefficients, have been known to satisfy estimates of the following type: There exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert G(x,y)\right\rvert\leq Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}

for all
x\neq y
(see
[
9
, Section 4]
,
[
23
]
). Theorem
1.1
improves these estimates for the specific polyharmonic operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, as
\alpha\to\infty
. The new highlighted dependence in
\alpha
for the decay of
G_{g,\alpha}
draws parallel to the well-known behavior of the Helmholtz kernel for the operator
-\Delta-\lambda^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see for instance
[
6
]
).

The article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we construct a fundamental solution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
, and prove precise estimates using a modified Giraud’s Lemma which is proved in the Appendix
A
. Here, we let
\Delta_{\xi}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
be the non-negative Laplace operator in the Euclidean space. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Based on the method of Robert
[
23
]
, we iteratively construct an approximation of the Green’s function in
M
preserving the estimates of the Euclidean case. We then conclude the proof of the Theorem thanks to a self-improving argument that allows to estimate the remainder term. This is where we need to handle the presence of the diverging coefficient, which requires a particular attention in this crucial step, see Remark
3.1
. Finally, we show estimates on the derivatives of the Green’s function, in Proposition
3.9
.

2.
The Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Throughout this Section, we let
\Delta_{\xi}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
be the non-negative Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We adopt the notation
\Delta_{\xi}
instead of
-\Delta
to maintain consistency with the manifold case introduced below.
In this Section, we prove uniqueness and pointwise bounds for the Green’s function of the elliptic polyharmonic operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.1.
Green’s function of the poly-Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

We start by gathering basic results for the fundamental solution of the poly-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
, and
n>2k
, then define

(2.1)

c_{n,k}=\frac{1}{4^{k}\pi^{n/2}(k-1)!}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\right),

where
\Gamma(t)
is the well-known Gamma function. This constant
c_{n,k}
is chosen such that

(2.2)

H^{(k)}(x,y)=c_{n,k}\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}}

is a fundamental solution for the poly-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
9
, Section 2.6]
. This means in particular that

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}H^{(k)}_{x}=0\quad\text{in the weak sense on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$}.

2.2.
Construction and uniqueness.

Fix
\alpha>0
, the purpose this Section is to show the existence and study the behavior of the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, we are interested in its dependence on the coefficient
\alpha>0
. We start by observing that the Green’s functions for different values of
\alpha
are related by a simple scaling property. Then, we obtain an exact expression for the Green’s function of the operator
\Delta_{\xi}+1
. In a second step, we will use properties of the convolutions of distributions to retrieve expressions for the polyharmonic operators
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
,
k\geq 1
. Finally, we use a modified version of Giraud’s Lemma, proved in
A.1
, to obtain sharp pointwise bounds on the Green’s function.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>2k
and
\alpha>0
, we say that
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)
is a fundamental solution for the polyharmonic operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\,dy=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for all $\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$.}

Remark 2.1
.

It is straightforward to compute that
H^{(k)}_{1}(x,y)
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if

(2.3)

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y):=\alpha^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}H^{(k)}_{1}(\sqrt{\alpha}x,\sqrt{\alpha}y)

is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

With this observation, we only study the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}
in a first step, and then retrieve the general case
\alpha>0
using relation (
2.3
).

Recall the definition of the Bessel function of the second kind of order
\nu>0
,
K_{\nu}(r)
, which is singular at the origin, and solution to the second order ordinary differential equation

u^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}u^{\prime}(r)-\left(1+\frac{\nu^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)u(r)=0\qquad\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}$}.

These are well-known functions with explicit behavior (see
[
1
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
n\geq 3
, then

H_{1}(x,y):=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}K_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)

is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Start by observing that, thanks to the asymptotics for
K_{\nu}
found in
[
1
]
, we have the following :

•

When
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\ll 1
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle H_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}}{4}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2)}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2-n}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}H_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{(x_{i}-y_{i})}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n}}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\left\lvert\nabla H_{1}\right\rvert(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-n}(1+o(1));

•

When
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\gg 1
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle H_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}}{2}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}(1+o(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-1}))

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla H_{1}\right\rvert(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}}{2}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}(1+o(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-1})).

We now show that

(2.6)

(\Delta_{\xi}+1)H_{1;x}(y)=0\qquad\text{for all $y\neq x$.}

By the expression of
H_{1}
, we write
r=\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
and define

H(r):=H_{1}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}K_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(r).

Using the expression of the Laplacian in spherical coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, (
2.6
) re-writes as

\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}H(r)+\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}H(r)-H(r)=0.

Now
H(r)
satisfies this last equation for
r>0
, by the definition of the Bessel function of the second kind
K_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(r)
. Thus, we conclude that
H_{1}
solves (
2.6
) for all
x\neq y
.

For the second part of the proof, take
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we show that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H_{1;x}(y)(\Delta_{\xi}+1)\varphi(y)\,dy=\varphi(x).

We have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{\xi}\varphi+\varphi)H_{1;x}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{B_{0}(\delta)^{c}}(\Delta_{\xi}\varphi+\varphi)H_{1;x}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\big[\,a(\delta)+b(\delta)+c(\delta)\,\big],

where

a(\delta):=\int_{B_{x}(\delta)^{c}}(\Delta_{\xi}H_{1;x}+H_{1;x})\,\varphi\,dy=0

since
H_{1;x}
satisfies (
2.6
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}
, and with (
2.4
),

(2.8)

\displaystyle b(\delta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}H_{1;x}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,d\sigma(y)\sim\delta^{-(n-2)}\delta^{n-1}=\mathcal{O}(\delta),

\displaystyle c(\delta)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}\partial_{\nu}H_{1;x}\varphi\,d\sigma(y)=\frac{\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\omega_{n-1}\,\varphi(x)+o(1)

as
\delta\to 0
. Using the expression for the surface area of the sphere, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{\xi}\varphi+\varphi)H_{1}\,dy=\varphi(x).

We can conclude that
H_{1}(x,y)
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We additionally observe that, for
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\ll 1
, the Green’s function
H_{1}
and its gradient are equal to first order to the standard Green’s function for the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its gradient, respectively. Moreover,
H_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
only depends on
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
.

The following result is technical and establishes improved bounds for fundamental solutions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be a function satisfying
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}u=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and such that there exist
C>0
,
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, with

\left\lvert u(x)\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}C\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2k-n}&\text{if }0<\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 1\\
C\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert}&\text{if }\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}.

Then for
l=0,\ldots 2k
, there is
C_{l}>0
such that

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u(x)\right\rvert\leq C_{l}\begin{cases}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&0<\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert}&\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}.

Proof.

Let
x\neq 0
be fixed. Notice that there is
C>0
such that
t^{\rho}e^{-t}\leq Ct^{2k-n}
for all
t\geq 1
, so that

(2.9)

\left\lvert u(x)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2k-n}\qquad\forall\penalty\ x\neq 0.

When
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 2
, define
v(y)=u(\left\lvert x\right\rvert y)
on a ball
B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2)\not\ni 0
, and write
\lambda=\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}\neq 0
. We compute

(\Delta_{\xi}+\lambda)^{k}v(y)=\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2k}\left((\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}u\right)(\left\lvert x\right\rvert y)=0\qquad\forall\,y\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2).

By standard elliptic theory, since
\lambda\leq 4
, there is
C>0
independent of
x
such that
v\in C^{2k}(B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/4))
, and for all
y\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/4)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}v(y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\left\|(\Delta_{\xi}+\lambda)^{k}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2))}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2))}\Big)

\displaystyle=C\sup_{z\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2)}\left\lvert u(\left\lvert x\right\rvert z)\right\rvert\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2)}\frac{C}{\left\lvert\left\lvert x\right\rvert z\right\rvert^{n-2k}}\leq\frac{C}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{n-2k}}

using (
2.9
), and since
\left\lvert z\right\rvert\geq 1/2
for all
z\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/2)
. Now,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}v(y)\right\rvert=\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{l}\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u\right\rvert(\left\lvert x\right\rvert y),

we evaluate inequality (
2.10
) at
y=\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\in B_{\frac{x}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}}(1/4)
to obtain

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u\right\rvert(x)\leq\frac{C}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{n-2k+l}}\qquad\forall\,\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 2.

On the other hand, when
\left\lvert x\right\rvert>2
, we use elliptic theory for
u
on a ball
B_{x}(1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{0}(1)
. This shows that there is
C>0
independent of
x
such that for all
y\in B_{x}(1/2)
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u\right\rvert(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\left\|(\Delta_{\xi}+\lambda)^{k}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}B_{x}(1)}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{x}(1))}\Big)\leq C\sup_{z\in B_{x}(1)}\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert z\right\rvert},

where this last inequality follows from the assumption on
u
.
Observe that, when
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq 2
,
\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}\leq\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq\frac{3\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}
and
\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert x\right\rvert-1
for all
y\in B_{x}(1)
, so that no matter if
\rho
is positive or negative,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u\right\rvert(y)\leq C\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert}.

Evaluating this inequality at
y=x\in B_{x}(\varepsilon)
gives the result for
\left\lvert x\right\rvert>2
.

For the intermediate values
1\leq\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 2
, the two regimes coincide, up to a constant. The first part of the proof gives

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u\right\rvert(x)\leq\frac{C}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{n-2k+l}}.

But now for
l=0,\ldots 2k
, we have
C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
x
such that, if
1\leq\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 2
,

\begin{aligned} \left\lvert x\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&\leq C_{1}\\
C_{1}\leq\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert}&\leq C_{2}\end{aligned},

and we conclude.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Fix
k\geq 1
and
n>2k
, we define the space
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
, of all functions
u\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
such that the following holds.

•

There exists
C>0
such that
\left\lvert u(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2k)}
when
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1
;

•

For all
p\geq 1
, there is
C_{p}>0
such that for
l=0,\ldots 2k
,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}u(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{p}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-p}\quad\text{when $\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1$}.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a unique fundamental solution of
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that with this result, the function
H_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag\to\mathbb{R}
defined in Proposition
2.1
is the unique fundamental solution of the operator
\Delta_{\xi}+1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathfrak{H}_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
H,\tilde{H}\in\mathfrak{H}_{k}
be two fundamental solutions for
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define
f_{x}(y):=H(x,y)-\tilde{H}(x,y)
. Then
f_{x}\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\})
with

(1)

\displaystyle\left\lvert f_{x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}
when
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1
;

(2)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}f_{x}\right\rvert\leq C_{p}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-p}
for all
p
, when
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1
, and for
l=0,\ldots 2k
;

(3)

f_{x}
satisfies
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}f_{x}=0
in the weak sense on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We start by proving that the singularity of
f_{x}
at
x
is removable. Note that
f_{x}\in L^{p}(B_{x}(1))
for all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-2k}
. Elliptic theory gives that
f_{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{2k,p}(B_{x}(1/2))
and there is
C>0
independent of
x
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{x}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2k,p}(B_{x}(1/2))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|f_{x}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{x}(1))}+\left\|(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}f_{x}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{x}(1))}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left\|f_{x}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{x}(1))}.

Iterating, and by elliptic theory, we similarly find that
f_{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{l,p}(B_{x}(1/2))
for any
l\geq 0
. By Sobolev embeddings, using a big enough
l
in the previous argument, then
f_{x}\in C^{0,\delta}(B_{x}(1/4))
for some
\delta>0
.
We conclude that
f_{x}
has no singularity at 0, and
f_{x}\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

(2.11)

(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}f_{x}=0\qquad\text{ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ in the classical sense.}

Using the decay of
f_{x}
at infinity, we have
f_{x}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\geq 1
. Fix
R>0
, and take
\chi_{R}
a cutoff function supported in
B_{x}(R)
such that
\chi_{R}\equiv 1
on
B_{x}(R/2)
. We compute

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}(\chi_{R}f_{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\left(\chi_{R}(\Delta_{\xi})^{l}f_{x}+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{2l}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}\chi_{R}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{2l-m}f_{x}\right\rvert\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{2l}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}\chi_{R}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{2l-m}f_{x}\right\rvert\right)

with (
2.11
). Testing this equation against
\chi_{R}f_{x}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have by integration by parts on the left-hand side, for all
p>1
and
R>2
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\left\lvert(\Delta_{\xi})^{l/2}(\chi_{R}f_{x})\right\rvert^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{2l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}\chi_{R}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{2l-m}f_{x}\right\rvert\chi_{R}\left\lvert f_{x}\right\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{B_{x}(R)\setminus B_{x}(R/2)}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-p}dy

where the latter follows from the decay of
f_{x}
at infinity. Now
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{x}(1))
for
p>n
, and thus the right-hand side vanishes as
R\to\infty
provided we choose a fixed
p>n
. On the other hand since all the terms in the left-hand side are positive,

\displaystyle\left\|f_{x}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{l/2}f_{x}\right\rvert^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{l/2}(\chi_{R}f_{x})\right\rvert^{2}dy=0

by Fatou’s Lemma. Thus
f=0
everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

With the same strategy of proof as in the previous Lemma
2.3
, we can show the following. The function
H^{(k)}(x,y)
defined in (
2.2
) is the unique fundamental solution for the poly-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class of functions
u\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
such that there exists
C>0
and

\left\lvert u(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}\quad\forall\,x\neq y.

Remark 2.5
.

A fundamental solution
h
for
(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}
is always smooth away from its singularity: Let
h
be a distribution that satisfies

(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}h_{x}=0\qquad\text{weakly on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}$}.

Now for all
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
x\not\in\overline{\Omega}
,
h
satisfies

(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{k}h_{x}=0\qquad\text{weakly on $\overline{\Omega}$}

and by elliptic theory we can conclude
h_{x}\in C^{\infty}(U)
for an open set
U\subset\subset\Omega
. This gives in turn
h_{x}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\})
.

We have everything we need to construct the Green’s function of
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and describe its exact behavior. Define
H^{(1)}_{1}:=H_{1}
and for
k\geq 1
, iteratively

(2.12)

H^{(k+1)}_{1}(x,y):=H^{(k)}_{1}*H^{(1)}_{1}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(k)}_{1}(x,z)H^{(1)}_{1}(z,y)\,dz,

which is well-defined provided
2k+2<n
, as easily seen by iteratively applying Giraud’s Lemma (see Lemma
A.1
below).

Theorem 2.4
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>2k
, and
\alpha>0
, then

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y):=\alpha^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}H^{(k)}_{1}(\sqrt{\alpha}x,\sqrt{\alpha}y)

is the unique Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
defined in
2.2
, where
H^{(k)}_{1}
is as defined in (
2.12
). Moreover, there exists
C>0
independent of
\alpha>0
such that for all
x\neq y
,

(2.13)

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq\begin{cases}C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}&\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
C\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{(k-2)n+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}.

Finally, the Green’s function is radial,
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)
only depends on
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We then get explicit
\alpha
-dependent estimates for
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
, and deduce its uniqueness.

Since
n>2k
,
H^{(l)}_{1}
is defined for all
l=1,\ldots k
. We prove the first statement by induction. First,
H^{(1)}_{1}(x,y)=H_{1}(x,y)
is a Green’s function for
\Delta_{\xi}+1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as we showed in Proposition
2.1
. Assume that we have proven that
H^{(l)}_{1}
is a Green’s function for some
1\leq l\leq k-1
. Then let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

(2.14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(l+1)}_{1;x}(y)(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{l+1}\varphi(y)dy\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(l)}_{1}(x,z)H^{(1)}_{1}(z,y)dz\right)(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{l+1}\varphi(y)dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(l)}_{1;x}(z)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(1)}_{1;z}(y)(\Delta_{\xi}+1)\left[(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{l}\varphi\right](y)dy\right)dz\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H^{(l)}_{1;x}(z)(\Delta_{\xi}+1)^{l}\varphi(z)dz\\
&=\varphi(x),\end{aligned}

where the last line is the induction assumption. Now using (
2.3
), we obtain that
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\alpha^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}H^{(k)}_{1}(\sqrt{\alpha}x,\sqrt{\alpha}y)
is a Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

To prove pointwise estimates on
H^{(k)}_{1}(x,y)
, we use an exponential version of the so-called
Giraud’s Lemma
, whose standard proof can be found in
[
11
]
. We prove this result in appendix
A
in the generalized setting of a manifold, following a similar reasoning. With the behavior of
H^{(1)}_{1}
in (
2.4
), (
2.5
), and Lemma
A.1
, we get iteratively for
l=1,\ldots k
,

H^{(l)}_{1}(u,v)\leq\begin{cases}C\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert^{2l-n}&\text{when }\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert\leq 1\\
C\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert^{-l\frac{n-1}{2}+(l-1)n}e^{-\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert}&\text{when }\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert\geq 1.\end{cases}

We then observe that
H^{(k)}_{1}\in\mathfrak{H}_{k}
using Lemma
2.2
, it is thus the only Green’s function in this class by Lemma
2.3
. Similarly, by relation (
2.3
), we conclude that
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
is the unique Green’s function for
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
. The previous estimates now become

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq\begin{cases}C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
C\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1.\end{cases}

Finally, this Green’s function inherits its symmetry from fact that
H^{(1)}_{1}
only depends on
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
and that the convolution of two radial functions is itself radial.
∎

Note that the exponent
\frac{(k-2)n+k}{2}
in (
2.13
) becomes positive for
k\geq 2
.

2.3.
Refined asymptotics.

We now prove more precise pointwise estimates on the Green’s function
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
and its derivatives. When
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
is small, we show that
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
and its first
2k-1
derivatives are equal to first order to the standard Green’s function for the poly-Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and its derivatives, respectively.

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>2k
and
\alpha>0
. Then
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
, where
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
is defined in Theorem
2.4
, and for all
l=0,\ldots 2k
, there exists
C_{l}>0
independent of
\alpha
such that for all
x\neq y
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}C_{l}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
C_{l}\,\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+\frac{l}{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{(k-2)n+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first part
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
comes from the Remark
2.5
. The estimates for
l\geq 1
are then a direct consequence of Lemma
2.2
. For
l=1,\ldots 2k
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)\right\rvert=

\displaystyle\,\alpha^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\alpha^{\frac{l}{2}}\left(\nabla^{l}H^{(k)}_{1;\sqrt{\alpha}x}\right)(\sqrt{\alpha}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C_{l}\alpha^{\frac{n-2k}{2}+\frac{l}{2}}\begin{cases}\big(\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\big)^{-(n-2k+l)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\big(\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\big)^{\frac{(k-2)n+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}

\displaystyle=C_{l}\begin{cases}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+\frac{l}{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{(k-2)n+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}.

∎

We now prove precise estimates for the behavior of
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
, when
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
is small. To simplify the notation, define

(2.15)

\eta(t)=\begin{cases}t&\text{when $n=2k+1$}\\
t^{2}\big(1+\left\lvert\log t\right\rvert\big)&\text{when $n=2k+2$}\\
t^{2}&\text{when $n\geq 2k+3$}\end{cases},\qquad\text{for $0<t\leq 1$.}

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>2k
,
\alpha>0
, and let
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
be the unique Green’s function in
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, when
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1
,

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)=c_{n,k}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\eta(\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)\big)\right),

where
\eta
is defined in (
2.15
) and
c_{n,k}
is the constant in (
2.1
).

Remark 2.6
.

Here and in the following, the notation
f(x,y)=\mathcal{O}(u(x,y))
, for a positive function
u
, is used to mean that there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
\alpha
, such that for all
x,y
,

\frac{\left\lvert f(x,y)\right\rvert}{u(x,y)}\leq C.

Proof.

We begin by defining
R_{\alpha}:=H^{(k)}_{\alpha}-H^{(k)}_{0}
, where we write
H^{(k)}_{0}(x,y)=c_{n,k}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}
the Green’s function for
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Now, we compute

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}^{k}R_{\alpha,x}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}-\Delta_{\xi}^{k}H^{(k)}_{0,x}-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\tbinom{k}{l}\,\alpha^{k-l}\Delta_{\xi}^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\tbinom{k}{l}\,\alpha^{k-l}\Delta_{\xi}^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}

in the distributional sense on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
h_{\alpha}(x,y):=-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\tbinom{k}{l}\,\alpha^{k-l}\Delta_{\xi}^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)
, straightforward computations with (
2.13
) then show that

(2.17)

\left\lvert h_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\begin{cases}\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k\frac{n+1}{4}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1.\end{cases}.

We now claim that, for all
y\neq x
,

(2.18)

R_{\alpha}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\alpha}(x,z)\,c_{n,k}\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert^{2k-n}dz.

This follows from the fact that first,

\left\lvert R_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert+\left\lvert H^{(k)}_{0}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2k-n}\quad\forall\penalty\ x\neq y.

Moreover, the right-hand side of (
2.18
) defines a function in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

Z(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\alpha,x}(z)\,c_{n,k}\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert^{2k-n}dz,

which satisfies
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}Z_{x}=h_{\alpha,x}
in the distributional sense on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Remark
2.4
, we conclude that for all
x\neq y
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
R_{\alpha}(x,y)=Z(x,y)
.
We now have, by Lemma
A.1
together with (
2.17
), when
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1
,

\left\lvert R_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2k-2)}&&\text{when $2k+2<n$}\\
&C\alpha\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\right\rvert\big)&&\text{when $2k+2=n$}\\
&C\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}&&\text{when $2k+1=n$}\end{aligned}\right.,

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
\alpha>0
. Finally, coming back to
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}=H^{(k)}_{0}+R_{\alpha}
, we have the conclusion.
∎

We obtain similar pointwise estimates for the derivatives of
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
. The next Corollary shows that the estimates in Proposition

can be differentiated.

.

is defined in (
2.15
).

Proof.

With notations from the previous proof of Proposition
2.6
, we have

\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)=c_{n,k}+\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}R_{\alpha,x}(y),

and thus for
l\geq 1
,

(2.19)

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}\,H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)\right)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}\,R_{\alpha,x}(y)\right)\right\rvert.

To estimate the derivatives of
R_{\alpha}
, we go back to (
2.18
): Since
l\leq 2k-1
, we can differentiate under the integral sign, and we obtain

\nabla^{l}R_{\alpha,x}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\alpha}(x,z)\nabla^{l}_{(y)}H^{(k)}_{0}(y,z)\,dz.

By Proposition
2.5
, and since
l<2k
, we can use as before Lemma
A.1
to estimate the derivatives of
R_{\alpha,x}
when
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
:

•

When
n-2k=1
, we have to consider several cases. For
l=1
we get

\left\lvert\nabla_{(y)}R_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\right\rvert\big),

and for
2\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}_{(y)}R_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-l}.

•

When
n-2k\geq 2
, we obtain for all
1\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}_{(y)}R_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-(n-2k-2+l)}.

By (
2.19
) and by Leibniz’s formula, we now have, for
l=1,\ldots 2k-1

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}\,H^{(k)}_{\alpha;x}(y)\right)\right\rvert\leq C\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k-m}\left\lvert\nabla^{l-m}R_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\\
\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-l}&&\text{when $n-2k=1$}\\
&C\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2-l}\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\right\rvert\big)&&\text{when $n-2k=2$}\\
&C\alpha\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2-l}&&\text{when $n-2k\geq 3$}\end{aligned}\right..

∎

3.
Extending the construction to a Riemannian manifold

In this Section we construct the Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
on a manifold
M
. We follow the construction from Robert
[
23
]
.
We prove uniqueness, positivity, as well as estimates that explicitly depend on
\alpha
.
In the following, we will always consider
(M,g)
to be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, of dimension
n>2k
and with injectivity radius
i_{g}>0
. We let
\Delta_{g}:=-\operatorname{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
M
. The notation
B_{x}(R)
will represent a ball of radius
R>0
and center
x
either in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or on the manifold, depending on the context.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in several steps.
We first define an approximate fundamental solution for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
which is modelled on the Euclidean fundamental solution of
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
. It satisfies the equation
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}G_{x}=\delta_{x}
up to error terms. We then iteratively improve the precision of these terms until we obtain a bounded error, which is finally controlled in subsection
3.3
.
Subsequently, we prove bounds on the derivatives of the Green’s function of the same kind as Proposition
2.5
and Corollary
2.7
. We finish this section with a remark on the mass of the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
when the dimension of the manifold
n=2k+1
.

We start with an observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
, if
G
is a Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, as defined in Definition
1.1
, then it is unique.

Proof.

Start by noting that, by the same arguments as in Remark
2.5
, any Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
is smooth away from its singularity, so that
G_{x}\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\{x\})
for any
x\in M
.

Let
\tilde{G}
be another Green’s function for
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
. Take
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and define
u\in C^{\infty}
the unique solution to the equation

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u=\varphi\qquad\text{on $M$.}

We then have, for all
x\in M
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(x)&=\int_{M}G(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
u(x)&=\int_{M}\tilde{G}(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\end{aligned}\right..

For all
x\in M
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have obtained

\int_{M}\left(G(x,y)-\tilde{G}(x,y)\right)\varphi(x)\,dv_{g}=u(x)-u(x)=0.

Thus, we can conclude that
G_{x}(y)=\tilde{G}_{x}(y)
for almost every
y\in M
, and by continuity
G_{x}(y)=\tilde{G}_{x}(y)
on
M\setminus\{x\}
.
∎

3.1.
Step 1: An approximate fundamental solution.

We start the proof of Theorem
1.1
by pulling back the function
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
onto the manifold. Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>2k
, and let
0<\tau_{0}<i_{g}/2
that will be chosen later. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a cut-off function with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
, such that
\chi(t)\equiv 1
on
[0,\tau_{0}/2)
and
\chi(t)\equiv 0
on
(\tau_{0},+\infty)
. We define

(3.1)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y):=\chi(d_{g}{(x,y)})H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(0,\exp^{-1}_{x}(y))

for all
x\neq y
in
M
, where
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
is defined in Theorem
2.4
. This function only depends on
d_{g}{(x,y)}
.

Assume that
\alpha
is large enough so that
1/\sqrt{\alpha}<\tau_{0}/2
. Using Theorem
2.4
we obtain

(3.2)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)\leq\begin{cases}Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
C\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\end{cases}.

In particular, when
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\to 0
, we have that

(3.3)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)=c_{n,k}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\eta(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\big)\right),

this function behaves to first order as a Riemannian version of the Green’s function for the poly-Laplacian
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\eta
is defined in (
2.15
).

The following Proposition estimates the error term between
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}
and a true fundamental solution in the distributional sense.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\tau_{0}<i_{g}/2
and let
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}
be as defined in (
3.1
). There exist
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
such that, for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
x\in M
, there is
l_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M\setminus\{x\})
satisfying

(3.4)

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}=\varphi(x)+\int_{M}\varphi(y)\,l_{\alpha,x}(y)\,dv_{g}(y),

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. The function
l_{\alpha,x}
is
L^{1}(M)
, has support in
B_{x}(\tau_{0})
, and

\left\lvert l_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
C\alpha^{k\frac{n+1}{2}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\frac{n(k-2)+k+4}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\end{cases}

for all
x\neq y
in
M
.

Proof.

We compute
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)
and precisely estimate the error terms. Let
\tilde{g}:=\exp_{x}^{*}g
, it is a metric on
B_{0}(\tau_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounded geometry since
\tau_{0}<i_{g}
. In particular, for
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(3.5)

\Delta_{\tilde{g}}f(u)=\Delta_{\xi}f(u)+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla f(u)\right\rvert)+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\nabla^{2}f(u)\right\rvert),

where
u:=\exp_{x}^{-1}(y)\in B_{0}(\tau_{0})
. Since
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\chi(d_{g}{(x,y)})H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(0,\exp^{-1}_{x}(y))
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau_{0})
, we can write, when
x\neq y
with
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq\tau_{0}
,

\Delta_{g}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)=\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\left(H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)\right)\Big\lvert_{u=\exp_{x}^{-1}(y)}.

We observe that
\left(H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}\,\chi(\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert)\right)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is supported in
B_{0}(\tau_{0})
. Now for
u\neq 0
such that
\left\lvert u\right\rvert<\tau_{0}/2
, using Proposition
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\nabla^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\begin{cases}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+\frac{l}{2}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}

for
l=0,\ldots 2k
. Take
\alpha_{0}
such that
1/\sqrt{\alpha_{0}}<\tau_{0}/2
, then we have
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\geq 1
for all
\left\lvert u\right\rvert\geq\tau_{0}/2
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla^{l-m}\chi\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{m}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+\frac{l}{2}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert}.

We have thus

(3.6)

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert\leq C\begin{cases}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{-(n-2k+l)}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+\frac{l}{2}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert}&\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\geq 1\\
0&\left\lvert u\right\rvert>\tau_{0}\end{cases}.

We now show that, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

(3.7)

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}=\varphi(x)+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(\delta)}\varphi\,(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}.

Start by observing that since
\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\in L^{1}(M)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(\delta)}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\alpha^{k-l}\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(\delta)}\Delta_{g}^{l}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}\right].

Integration by parts gives, as in (
2.7
), for
l=1,\ldots k
,

(3.8)

\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(\delta)}\Delta_{g}^{l}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}=\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(\delta)}\varphi\,\Delta_{g}^{l}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}\\
+\sum_{m=0}^{l-1}\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{l-1-m}\varphi\Delta_{g}^{m}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}d\sigma_{g}\\
-\sum_{m=0}^{l-1}\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}\Delta_{g}^{l-1-m}\varphi\,\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{m}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}d\sigma_{g},

where
\partial_{\nu}
is the covariant derivative along the normal direction to
\partial B_{x}(\delta)
in
M
. Using (
3.6
), we obtain for
l=1,\ldots k
,
m=0,\ldots l-1
,

(3.9)

\left\lvert\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{l-1-m}\varphi\Delta_{g}^{m}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}d\sigma_{g}\right\rvert\leq C\delta^{-n+2k-2m}\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}d\sigma_{g}=o(1)

as
\delta\to 0
. On the other hand, when
l=k
and
m=k-1
, we compute

(3.10)

-\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}\varphi(y)\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)d\sigma_{g}(y)\\
=\varphi(x)\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)d\sigma_{g}(y)+o(1)\quad\text{as $\delta\to 0$}

since
\varphi
is
C^{\infty}(M)
. Using (
3.5
) iteratively, we have

\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)d\sigma_{g}(y)\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\left(\Delta_{\xi}^{k-1}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)d\sigma+\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}\delta^{2}\left\lvert\nabla^{2k-1}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert d\sigma\right)\\
&\qquad+\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}\delta\left\lvert\nabla^{2k-2}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert d\sigma\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{m=2}^{2k-3}\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert d\sigma\right)\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\left(\Delta_{\xi}^{k-1}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)d\sigma+o(1),\end{aligned}

as
\delta\to 0
, estimating the terms with (
3.6
). Here in the right-hand side,
\partial_{\nu}
is now the derivative normal to the sphere in the Euclidean space. Using Corollary
2.7
, one has

\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\left(\Delta_{\xi}^{k-1}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)d\sigma=\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\left(\Delta_{\xi}^{k-1}\frac{c_{n,k}}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{n-2k}}\right)d\sigma+o(1),

and thus by definition of
c_{n,k}
in (
2.1
), we obtain in the end

(3.11)

\int_{\partial B_{x}(\delta)}-\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{k-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}d\sigma_{g}=1+o(1)\qquad\text{when $\delta\to 0$}.

Finally, all the remaining terms are estimated with (
3.6
),

(3.12)

\left\lvert\int_{\partial B_{0}(\delta)}\Delta_{g}^{l-1-m}\varphi\,\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{g}^{m}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}d\sigma_{g}\right\rvert\leq C\delta^{2k-m-1}=o(1)

as
\delta\to 0
, when
m\neq k-1
,
1\leq l\leq k
. Coming back to (
3.8
), putting together (
3.10
), (
3.11
) in (
3.9
), and with (
3.12
), we obtain (
3.7
).
Note that all the terms
\Delta_{g}^{l}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}
are integrable by (
3.6
) except for the term
\Delta_{g}^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}
which is only bounded by
d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-n}
when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
.

Write
l_{\alpha,x}(y):=(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(y)
for all
x\neq y
, then
l_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M\setminus\{x\})
with support in
B_{x}(\tau_{0})
by the definition of
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}
. Now for
x\neq y
such that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq\tau_{0}
, write
u:=\exp_{x}^{-1}(y)
, and compute

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(\exp_{x}(u))\begin{aligned} &=(\Delta_{\tilde{g}}+\alpha)^{k}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\\
\end{aligned}\\
=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\alpha^{k-l}\Bigg[\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)\Delta_{\xi}^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{2l-2}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert\right)\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{2l-1}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\nabla^{2l}\left(\chi(\left\lvert u\right\rvert)H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right)\right\rvert\right)\Bigg],

using again (
3.5
) iteratively. By (
3.6
) and since
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have when
0<\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(\exp_{x}(u))\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-l}\Bigg[\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\nabla^{2l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla^{2l-1}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{2l-2}\left\lvert\nabla^{m}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(u)\right\rvert\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{l=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-l}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2k-n-2l+2}\leq\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2-n}.

Similarly, when
\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\geq 1
,

\left\lvert(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}(\exp_{x}(u))\right\rvert\leq C\sum_{l=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-l}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}\alpha^{k\frac{n-3}{4}+l}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{n(k-2)+k}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert}.

We have thus shown that, for
x\neq y
,

(3.13)

\left\lvert l_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k\frac{n+1}{4}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\frac{(k-2)n+k+4}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
0&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq\tau_{0}\end{cases}.

In particular,
l_{\alpha,x}\in L^{1}(M)
so that (
3.7
) becomes

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}=\varphi(x)+\int_{M}\varphi\,l_{\alpha,x}\,dv_{g}

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Note that
k\frac{n+1}{4}-\frac{(k-2)n+k+4}{2}=\frac{n-2}{2}
, so that the two regimes in (
3.13
) are of order
\alpha^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\sim 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
.
∎

3.2.
Step 2: The induction step.

In this step, we define a sequence of functions to iteratively improve the estimates on the error term.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau_{0}>0
,
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
such that, for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, there is a function
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}\in C^{\infty}(M\times M\setminus Diag)
and for all
x\in M
, a function
\gamma_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M)
such that

(3.14)

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)+\int_{M}\gamma_{\alpha,x}(y)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)=\varphi(x)

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Moreover, both
\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}^{*}
and
\gamma_{\alpha,x}
are supported in
B_{x}(N\tau_{0})
,
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}
satisfies

(3.15)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)=c_{n,k}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\eta(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\right)\right)

for
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
, and there is a constant
C>0
independent of
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
such that writing
p_{n,k}:=\frac{k(n+1)+4}{2}N-n
,

(3.16)

\left\lvert\gamma_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{-N+\frac{n}{2}}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\left(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\right)^{p_{n,k}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
0&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq N\tau_{0}\end{cases},

for all
x,y\in M
.

Proof.

Fix
\tau_{0}>0
such that
\tau_{0}<i_{g}/(n+2)
, and take
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
given by Proposition
3.2
. We define, for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and all
x\neq y
in
M
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{1}_{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=-l_{\alpha,x}(y)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{i+1}_{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)\Gamma^{1}_{\alpha}(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\qquad\forall\penalty\ i\geq 2.

By the exponential version of Giraud’s Lemma, Lemma
A.2
, and by (
3.13
) we see that, as long as
2i<n
and
i\tau_{0}<i_{g}
, we have
\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha,x}\in L^{1}(M),\,\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}\in C^{0}(M\times M\setminus Diag)
, and

\left\lvert\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{i}\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2i)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{ki\frac{n+1}{4}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\frac{k(n+1)+4}{2}i-n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
0&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i\tau_{0}\end{cases}.

Now take
N=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
2N>n
. By the choice of
\tau_{0}
, we have
N\tau_{0}<i_{g}/2
. Lemma
A.2
then shows that
\Gamma^{N}_{\alpha}\in C^{0}(M\times M)
and that

(3.17)

\left\lvert\Gamma^{N}_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\\
\leq C\begin{cases}\alpha^{-N+\frac{n}{2}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{kN\frac{n+1}{4}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\frac{k(n+1)+4}{2}N-n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
0&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq N\tau_{0}\end{cases}.

Let, for
i=1,\ldots N-1
and
x\neq y
,

(3.18)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y):=\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z).

If
x\neq y
are such that
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
, we again have by Lemma
A.2

(3.19)

\left\lvert\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{i}\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k-2i)}&\text{when $2k+2i<n$}\\
1+\left\lvert\log(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\right\rvert&\text{if $2k+2i=n$}\\
\alpha^{-\frac{2k+2i-n}{2}}&\text{when $2k+2i>n$}\end{cases}.

While, if
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
, writing for simplicity

(3.20)

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle:=k\tfrac{i(n+1)+n-3}{4}

and

\displaystyle\rho_{i}

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{k(n+1)(i+1)-2n+4i}{2}

satisfying
2p_{i}-\rho_{i}=n-2k-2i
, we have

(3.21)

\left\lvert\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{i}\alpha^{p_{i}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho_{i}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}

with
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y)=0
when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq(i+1)\tau_{0}
.

In some sense, the
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}
are successive error terms in the expression of the Green’s function. Let us define for all
x\neq y
in
M
,

(3.22)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y):=\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y).

Then we have, for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
x\in M
, by Proposition
3.2
and since
\Gamma^{1}_{\alpha,x}=-l_{\alpha,x}
,

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}\,dv_{g}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{M}\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(z,y)(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(z)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
&=\penalty\ \varphi(x)+\int_{M}l_{\alpha,x}(y)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)\left(\int_{M}\tilde{G}_{\alpha;z}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,dv_{g}\right)\,dv_{g}(z)\\
&=\penalty\ \varphi(x)+\int_{M}l_{\alpha,x}\,\varphi\,dv_{g}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)\left[\varphi(z)+\int_{M}l_{\alpha,z}\,\varphi\,dv_{g}\right]\,dv_{g}(z)\end{aligned}

where we used Fubini. Therefore, we obtain with the definition of
\Gamma^{i+1}_{\alpha}
that

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=\penalty\ \varphi(x)-\int_{M}\Gamma^{1}_{\alpha,x}\varphi\,dv_{g}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha,x}\varphi\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\hfil+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{M}\left[\int_{M}\Gamma^{i}_{\alpha}(x,z)l_{z}(y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right]\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=\penalty\ \varphi(x)-\int_{M}\Gamma^{N}_{\alpha,x}\varphi\,dv_{g}

We now let
\gamma_{\alpha,x}(y):=\Gamma^{N}_{\alpha}(x,y)
, then (
3.14
) follows, and
\gamma_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M)
satisfies (
3.16
) thanks to (
3.17
). Finally, for
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
, and again by Lemma
A.2
, we have

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\alpha^{-1/2}&\text{if $n-2k=1$,}&\\
&C\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\right\rvert\big)&\text{if $n-2k=2$,}&\\
&Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k-2)}&\text{if $n-2k\geq 3$,}&\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=C\alpha^{-1}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}\eta(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}),

recalling the definition (
2.15
) for
\eta
. This means that, with (
3.3
), we still have

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)=c_{n,k}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\eta(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\big)\right)

when
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
.
∎

3.3.
Step 3: Estimates on the remainder term.

With Proposition
3.3
, we have modified the starting function
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}
to get closer to a real fundamental solution to the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
. The remainder
\gamma_{\alpha,x}
is now uniformly bounded in
\alpha
by (
3.16
) and continuous in
M\times M
.

Fix
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
given by the Proposition
3.3
, and
x\in M
. We let
u_{\alpha,x}
be the unique solution of

(3.24)

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u_{\alpha,x}=\gamma_{\alpha,x}\qquad\text{weakly in $M$,}

where
\gamma_{\alpha,x}=\Gamma^{N}_{\alpha,x}
as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
Such
u_{\alpha,x}
exists and is unique since
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
is coercive for any
\alpha>0
, and since
\gamma_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M)
.

Remark 3.1
.

By standard elliptic theory and Sobolev’s embeddings, we have
u_{\alpha,x}\in C^{2k-1,\theta}(M)
for all
\theta\in(0,1)
, with

(3.25)

\left\|u_{\alpha,x}\right\|_{C^{l}(M)}\leq C_{l,\alpha}\left\|\gamma_{\alpha,x}\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}\leq C_{l,\alpha}\qquad l=0,\dots 2k-1

by (
3.16
).
The constants
C_{l,\alpha}
depend on
\alpha
, but we want pointwise estimates on
u_{\alpha,x}
and its derivatives with an explicit dependence in
\alpha
, so that (
3.25
) is not enough, unlike in
[
23
]
where (
3.25
) leads to the conclusion. In particular, in this step, we aim at recovering some exponential decay for
u_{\alpha,x}(y)
, when
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
given by Proposition
3.3
. For
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
x\in M
, let
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}
be as in (
3.22
), and let
u_{\alpha,x}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)
be the unique weak solution to (
3.24
). Define

(3.26)

G_{g,\alpha}(x,y):=\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(x,y)+u_{\alpha,x}(y)

for all
x\neq y
in
M
. Then
G_{g,\alpha}
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
. Moreover, if
f\in L^{q}(M)
for some
q>n/2k
, and
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{k,q}(M)
solves
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{u}=f
in
M
, then
\tilde{u}\in C^{0}(M)
and we have the following representation formula,

\tilde{u}(x)=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,z)f(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\quad\text{for all $x\in M$}.

Proof.

For the first part, go back to (
3.14
). For all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

(3.27)

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi(y)\,G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
\begin{aligned} &=\varphi(x)-\int_{M}\gamma_{\alpha,x}\varphi\,dv_{g}+\int_{M}u_{\alpha,x}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\varphi\,dv_{g}\\
&=\varphi(x)\end{aligned}

since
u_{\alpha,x}
is a weak solution to (
3.24
). By (
3.15
), and since
u_{\alpha,x}
is continuous in
M
, we have
G_{g,\alpha;x}\in L^{1}(M)
and
G_{g,\alpha}
is a fundamental solution for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
.

Now take
f\in L^{q}(M)
,
q>n/2k
and
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)
satisfying
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{u}=f
weakly in
M
. Standard elliptic theory gives
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{2k,q}(M)
, and then with Sobolev’s embeddings we have
\tilde{u}\in C^{0,\theta}(M)
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
(f_{m})_{m\geq 1}
be a sequence of functions in
C^{\infty}(M)
such that
f_{m}\to f
in
L^{q}
, and take
\tilde{u}_{m}\in C^{\infty}(M)
the respective solutions to
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\tilde{u}_{m}=f_{m}
. By elliptic estimates and Sobolev’s embedding, for any fixed
\alpha\geq 1
,
(\tilde{u}_{m})_{m\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence in
C^{0,\theta}(M)
. Then, by compactness of the inclusion
C^{0,\theta}(M)\subset C^{0}(M)
and since the solution is unique,
\tilde{u}_{m}\to\tilde{u}
in
C^{0}(M)
up to a subsequence. Since
u_{m}\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have, testing (
3.27
) against
u_{m}
,

\tilde{u}_{m}(x)=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)f_{m}(y)\,dv_{g}(y).

And, for all
x\in M
,
\tilde{u}_{m}(x)\to^{m\to\infty}\tilde{u}(x)
. Finally, for all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-2k}
, we have
G_{g,\alpha;x}\in L^{p}(M)
since
\tilde{G}_{\alpha;x}^{*}\in L^{p}(M)
by (
3.15
) and
u_{\alpha,x}\in C^{0}(M)\subset L^{p}(M)
. Thus, since
f_{m}\to f
in
L^{q}(M)
, choosing
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-2k}
such that
\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}=1
, Hölder’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)f_{m}(y)\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)f(y)\,dv_{g}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\|G_{g,\alpha;x}\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\left\|f_{m}-f\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}

\displaystyle\to 0.

We have thus shown, for all
x\in M
,

\tilde{u}(x)=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)f(y)\,dv_{g}(y).

∎

Thanks to Proposition
3.4
and (
3.24
), there is a representation formula for
u_{\alpha,x}
itself, given by

(3.28)

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,x}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(y,z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(y,z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)+\int_{M}u_{y}(z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z),

where
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}
and
\gamma_{\alpha,x}
are introduced in Proposition
3.3
. We now use this formula to self-improve the estimates on
u_{\alpha,x}
.
We prove exponential decay on
u_{\alpha,x}
, when
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
is large enough.
This is a striking difference with the case of operators with bounded coefficients.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
such that the following holds. For all
0<\varepsilon<1
, there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and all
x,y\in M
,

(3.29)

\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-k}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}\,i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2\end{cases},

where
u_{\alpha,x}
is the unique weak solution to (
3.24
).

Proof.

We define, for all
\alpha\geq 1
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, a function
\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\in L^{1}(M\times M)
as

(3.30)

\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y)=\begin{cases}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}\,i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2\end{cases}.

We prove the Proposition by using the representation formula (
3.28
) for
u_{\alpha,x}
. The first term is estimated with Lemma
A.2
, the estimates (
3.19
), (
3.21
) and (
3.16
) give

(3.31)

\left\lvert\int_{M}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(y,z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\
\leq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha^{-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}}&&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha^{\tilde{p}_{i}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\tilde{\rho}_{i}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
&0&&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 2N\tau_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

where

\tilde{p}_{i}:=k\tfrac{n+1}{4}(N+i)+k\tfrac{n-3}{4}\text{ and }\tilde{\rho}_{i}:=\tfrac{k(n+1)(N+i+1)}{2}+2(N+i)-n,

with
N\tau_{0}<i_{g}/2
and
N>n/2
,
N
and
\tau_{0}
are as defined in the proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Fix any
0<\varepsilon<1
and
x\in M
, there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
independent of
\alpha
and
x
such that, for
\alpha
large enough, we can write (
3.31
) as

(3.32)

\left\lvert\int_{M}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(y,z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
0&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 2\tau\end{cases}\\
&\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}}\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y),\end{aligned}

for all
y\in M
.
Note that, by integration by parts and the fact that
u_{x}
solves (
3.24
), one obtains

(3.33)

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{y}(z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}u_{y}(z)\,(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u_{x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u_{y}(z)\,u_{x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\gamma_{\alpha,y}(z)\,u_{x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z).

We now claim that
u_{\alpha,x}=o(\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha})
in the sense that

\left\|\frac{u_{\alpha,x}(\cdot)}{\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\to 0\qquad\text{as $\alpha\to\infty$, when $x\in M$ is fixed}.

For this, define for all
\alpha
,

\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}:=\left\|\frac{u_{\alpha,x}}{\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\\

and let
y_{\alpha}\in M
be such that
\frac{\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y_{\alpha})\right\rvert}{\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha})}=\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}
.
We know
\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}
and
y_{\alpha}
exist since
u_{\alpha,x}
,
\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,\cdot)
are continuous, and
\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}>0
in
M
. Applying (
3.28
) at the point
y_{\alpha}
and using (
3.32
) and (
3.33
) we now have, for all
\alpha
large enough,

(3.34)

\displaystyle\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y_{\alpha})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\int_{M}\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{*}(y_{\alpha},z)\gamma_{\alpha,x}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{M}u_{x}(z)\gamma_{\alpha,y_{\alpha}}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}}\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha})+\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}\int_{M}\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,z)\left\lvert\gamma_{\alpha,y_{\alpha}}(z)\right\rvert\,dv_{g}(z).

Using the bounds (
3.16
) on
\gamma_{\alpha,x}
and Lemma
A.3
below, which is a modified version of the exponential Giraud’s Lemma, we obtain that

(3.35)

\left\lvert\int_{M}\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,z)\gamma_{\alpha,y_{\alpha}}(z)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\alpha^{-N}\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha}).

Going back to (
3.34
) and dividing by
\Psi_{\varepsilon,\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha})>0
, we have thus proven that

(3.36)

\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}}+C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\alpha^{-N}\Upsilon_{\alpha,x},

with
N>n/2
by definition of
N
. Hence,
\Upsilon_{\alpha,x}\to 0
for all
x\in M
.

We have thus shown, since
M
is compact, that there exists
\alpha_{0}\geq 0
and a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
independent of
x
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
,
x,y\in M
,

(3.37)

\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2.\end{cases}

We now use this estimate to compute again the second term of (
3.28
): Using Lemma
A.3
with (
3.37
), and with (
3.32
), we finally get

\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-k}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2,\end{cases}

since
-N+\frac{n-2k}{2}\leq-k
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The assumption
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, with
\alpha_{0}
large, is crucial to obtain (
3.37
) from (
3.36
), and thus exponential decay for
u_{\alpha,x}
when
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
.

Remark 3.3
.

With our approach, we cannot expect to obtain the exact decay
e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}
for
u_{\alpha,x}
when
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
. Successive convolutions in the second term of the representation formula (
3.28
) add positive exponents of
d_{g}{(x,y)}
that we cannot get rid of, see Remark
A.2
. We are thus forced to reduce the exponential decay of
u_{\alpha,x}
to
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}
. This is what allows us to obtain (
3.35
).

3.4.
Step 4: End of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We can now proceed to conclude the proof of the main Theorem, putting the several pieces together.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and let
G_{g,\alpha}
be the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
defined in Proposition
3.4
. Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
x\neq y
, we have

(3.38)

\left\lvert G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2\end{cases}.

Proof.

Fix any
0<\varepsilon<1
. First, let
x,y\in M
be such that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
and
x\neq y
. Going back to the definition of
G_{g,\alpha}
in (
3.26
), we use (
3.2
), (
3.19
) and the fact that
u_{x}\in C^{0}(M)
satisfies (
3.25
), and we obtain

\left\lvert G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}

for all
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
.

Now let
1\sqrt{\alpha}\leq d_{g}{(x,y)}<i_{g}/2
. We use (
3.2
) and (
3.21
), there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
independent of
\alpha
,
x,y
such that

\left\lvert\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-i}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}

for
i=0,\ldots N-1
, writing
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{0}:=\tilde{G}_{\alpha}
. Up to taking a slightly smaller
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
, we also have for all
y\in M

\displaystyle\left\lvert u_{\alpha,x}(y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\alpha^{-k}e^{-(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}},

using (
3.29
).
We have thus obtained

\left\lvert G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C_{\varepsilon}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-2k)}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}.

Finally, when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2
, the estimate follows from (
3.29
).
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Fix
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
and let
G_{g,\alpha}
be the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
defined in Proposition
3.4
. Then for all
x\neq y
in
M
,
G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)=G_{g,\alpha}(y,x)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, define for
y\in M

h(y):=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(z,y)\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z).

Then since
G_{g,\alpha}(\cdot,y)\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\{y\})\cap L^{1}(M)
,
h
is well-defined. We claim that
h
is continuous on
M
. To prove the latter, fix
y\in M
and take a sequence of points in
M
,
y_{m}\to y
as
m\to\infty
. Let
\delta_{m}:=d_{g}{(y_{m},y)}
, we have

h(y_{m})=\int_{B_{y_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}G_{g,\alpha}(z,y_{m})\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z)+\int_{M\setminus B_{y_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}G_{g,\alpha}(z,y_{m})\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z).

On the one hand, by (
3.38
), we have

\int_{B_{y_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}G(y_{m},z)\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z)=o(1)

as
m\to\infty
. On the other hand, for

, we have
d_{g}{(z,y_{m})}\geq\frac{1}{3}d_{g}{(z,y)}
, so that using (
3.38
),

\left\lvert G(y_{m},z)\varphi(z)\right\rvert\leq Cd_{g}{(z,y)}^{2k-n}.

By dominated convergence, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}h(y_{m})

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{M\setminus B_{y_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}G_{g,\alpha}(z,y_{m})\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(z,y)\varphi(z)\,dv_{g}(z),

and
h
is continuous at
y\in M
.

Let now
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
be the unique solution to
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}g=\varphi
on
M
. Since
G_{g,\alpha}
is the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, we have, for all
x\in M
,

g(x)=\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y).

Now since
h\in C^{0}(M)
,
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, one has
h-g\in L^{\infty}(M)
and there is a unique
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)
such that

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}=h-g\quad\text{in the weak sense on $M$.}

Moreover, elliptic theory gives
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2k,p}(M)
for all
p\geq 1
. By Fubini’s theorem, and since
G_{g,\alpha;x}\in L^{1}(M)
by Proposition
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}h\,(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)\varphi(x)\,dv_{g}(x)\right)(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}(y)\,dv_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\int_{M}G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right)\varphi(x)\,dv_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\psi_{0}(x)\varphi(x)\,dv_{g}(x).

Use the definition of
g
and integrate by parts: Since
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2k,p}(M)
for any
p\geq 1
,

\int_{M}\psi_{0}\varphi\,dv_{g}=\int_{M}\psi_{0}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}g\,dv_{g}=\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}g\,dv_{g}.

By definition of
\psi_{0}
, we have thus shown in the end that

\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}\psi_{0}\big(h-g\big)\,dv_{g}=0=\int_{M}\big(h-g\big)^{2}\,dv_{g},

so that
h(x)=g(x)
for almost every
x\in M
, and thus
h\equiv g
on
M
by continuity of
h,g
. This shows that for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and all
x\in M
,

\int_{M}\left(G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)-G_{g,\alpha}(y,x)\right)\varphi(y)\,dv_{g}(y)=0.

Since
G_{g,\alpha}(x,\cdot),\,G_{g,\alpha}(\cdot,x)\in L^{1}(M)
for all
x\in M
, we deduce that

G_{g,\alpha}(x,y)=G_{g,\alpha}(y,x)

for almost every
y\in M
, and we conclude with the continuity of
G_{g,\alpha}
in
M\times M\setminus Diag
.
∎

\mu_{x}(y)=\tilde{G}_{\alpha}(x,y)-\frac{c_{n,k}}{d_{g}{(x,y)}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{-1/2})

using (
3.19
), (
3.21
) and Proposition
3.5
. Now with the definition (
3.1
), we have for
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq\tau_{0}/2
given in Proposition
3.3
,

(3.48)

\mu_{x}(y)=\left(H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(0,u)-\frac{c_{n,k}}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}\right)\Big\lvert_{u=\exp_{x}^{-1}(y)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{-1/2}).

Using the notations of Proposition
2.6
, recall that

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(0,u)-\frac{c_{n,k}}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}=R_{\alpha}(0,u),

where
R_{\alpha}
satisfies (
2.18
),

R_{\alpha}(0,u)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}c_{n,k}\left\lvert u-z\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha^{k-l}\Delta_{\xi}^{l}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(z)dz

for all
u\neq 0
. By integration by part, and using Proposition
2.5
, we obtain

(3.49)

R_{\alpha}(0,u)=-c_{n,k}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha^{k-l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{\xi}^{l})_{(z)}\left(\left\lvert u-z\right\rvert^{-1}\right)\,H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(z)dz.

Simple calculations show that for
l=0,\ldots k-1
, there is a constant
d_{n,k}>0
depending only on
n,k
such that

\Delta_{\xi}^{l}\big(\tfrac{1}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}\big)=d_{n,k}\frac{1}{\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{1+2l}}.

Since
H^{(k)}_{\alpha}>0
, (
3.49
) gives

-R_{\alpha}(0,u)\geq\tilde{c}_{n,k}\,\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert u-z\right\rvert^{2k-1}}H^{(k)}_{\alpha;0}(y)\,dy.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
whose value will be fixed later. Proposition
2.6
shows that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\alpha
, such that

H^{(k)}_{\alpha}(x,y)\geq\frac{c_{n,k}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}(1-C\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)

for all
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
. Thus,

\displaystyle-R_{\alpha}(0,u)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{c}_{n,k}\alpha\int_{B_{0}(\delta/\sqrt{\alpha})}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y-u\right\rvert^{2k-1}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert}(1-C\delta)dy

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{c}_{n,k}\alpha\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha}}(1-\delta C)

for all
\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq\tfrac{2\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
. Finally, fix
\delta>0
small enough so that
1-\delta C\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, we obtain that there exists
C>0
independent of
\alpha
such that for all
\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq\tfrac{2\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
,

(3.50)

-R_{\alpha}(0,u)\geq C\sqrt{\alpha}.

The continuity of
R_{\alpha}
follows from elliptic theory, since
R_{\alpha}
satisfies

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}R_{\alpha,x}=h_{\alpha,x}

and
h_{\alpha,x}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p>\frac{n}{n-2}
using (
2.17
), with
n-2<2k
. This implies the continuity of
\mu_{x}(y)
, since all the terms
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}^{i}
and
u_{\alpha,x}
are also continuous by Lemma
A.2
. Putting (
3.48
) and (
3.50
) together and evaluating them at
u=0
gives

\mu_{x}(x)=R_{\alpha}(0,u)\Big\lvert_{u=0}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{-1/2})\leq-C_{1}\sqrt{\alpha}

for some
C_{1}>0
and for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. In particular,
\mu_{x}(x)\to-\infty
as
\alpha\to\infty
.
∎

Note that the terms due to the presence of the metric contribute only in
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{-1/2})
whereas the main contribution leading to the divergence of the mass comes from the lower-order terms in the operator
(\Delta_{\xi}+\alpha)^{k}
on the Euclidean space.

Appendix A
Generalized Giraud’s Lemmas

In this Appendix, we compute some convolution estimates that are needed in the previous Sections to obtain the bounds on the Green’s functions. These are modified versions of results known as Giraud’s Lemma in the literature (see
[
3
, Proposition 4.12]
,
[
17
, Lemma 7.5]
), proved in
[
11
, p. 150]
.

Lemma A.1

(Exponential Giraud’s)
.

Let
X,Y\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
. Assume that there exist
\beta,\gamma\in(0,n]
, and
\rho,\nu>-n
such that

\displaystyle\left\lvert X(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\beta-n}&\text{if }\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\rho}e^{-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\text{if }\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases},

\displaystyle\left\lvert Y(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\gamma-n}&\text{if }\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1\\
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\nu}e^{-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}&\text{if }\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}

for all
x\neq y
. Let
Z(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dz
for
x\neq y
. Then
Z\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Diag)
and there exists
C>0
such that for all
x\neq y
:

•

If
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 1
,

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\beta+\gamma-n}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma<n\\
C\big(1+\left\lvert\log\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\right\rvert\big)&\text{when }\beta+\gamma=n\\
C&\text{when }\beta+\gamma>n.\end{cases}

•

If
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq 1
,

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}.

Moreover, when
\beta+\gamma>n
,
Z
is continuous on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following Proposition extends this result on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
with injectivity radius
i_{g}>0
, and with a scale parameter
\alpha\geq 1
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and
\tau,\sigma>0
such that
\tau+\sigma<i_{g}
. Let
X,Y\in C^{0}(M\times M\setminus Diag)
, such that for all
x,y\in M
,
X(x,\cdot)
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau)
and
Y(\cdot,y)
in
B_{y}(\sigma)
. Assume that there exist
\beta,\gamma\in(0,n]
,
p,q\geq 0
and
\rho,\nu>-n
satisfying

(A.1)

\begin{cases}2p-\rho\leq n-\beta\\
2q-\nu\leq n-\gamma\end{cases}

and such that, for all
x\neq y
,
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert X(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\beta-n}&\text{if }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{p}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\text{if }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left\lvert Y(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\gamma-n}&\text{if }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{q}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\nu}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\text{if }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let, for all
x\neq y
,
Z(x,y):=\int_{M}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)
. Then
Z\in C^{0}(M\times M\setminus Diag)
and, for all
x\in M
,
Z(x,\cdot)
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau+\sigma)
. There exists
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
such that for all
x\neq y
and
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, we have the following :

•

If
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1
,

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\beta+\gamma-n}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma<n\\
\left(1+\left\lvert\log(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\right\rvert\right)&\text{when }\beta+\gamma=n\\
\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma>n.\end{cases}

•

If
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1
,

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{n-\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}+\frac{\rho+\nu}{2}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}.

Moreover, when
\beta+\gamma>n
,
Z\in C^{0}(M\times M)
.

Since the proofs of these two Lemmas are the same, we only show the second one. The proof of Lemma
A.1
is identical setting formally
\tau,\sigma=\infty
and
\alpha=1
, and taking the integrals on the Euclidean space.

Proof.

Up to choosing
\alpha_{0}
large enough, we can assume that
5/\sqrt{\alpha}<\tau+\sigma<i_{g}
.

For the first part of the proof, let
x,y\in M
with
x\neq y
, and assume that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
. We have

(A.2)

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\int_{B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(z,y)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
+\left\lvert\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert,

this comes from fact that for
z\in B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert X(x,z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{p}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}\leq Cd_{g}{(x,z)}^{\beta-n}

\displaystyle\text{when }d_{g}{(x,z)}\geq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}

\displaystyle\left\lvert Y(z,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{q}d_{g}{(z,y)}^{\nu}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}\leq Cd_{g}{(z,y)}^{\gamma-n}

\displaystyle\text{when }d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}

for a constant
C>0
independent of
\alpha,x,y,z
, by (
A.1
).

Write
r:=d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
, we first claim that

(A.3)

\int_{B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(z,y)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
\leq C\begin{cases}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-\beta-\gamma)}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma<n\\
1+\left\lvert\log(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\right\rvert&\text{when }\beta+\gamma=n\\
\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma>n\end{cases}.

To prove (
A.3
), we decompose
B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})
in three parts:
B_{x}(r/2)
,
B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus B_{x}(r/2)
and
B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})\setminus B_{x}(3r/2)
. First, when
z\in B_{x}(r/2)
, we have
d_{g}{(y,z)}\geq r/2
, so that

(A.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{x}(r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(z,y)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\gamma-n}\int_{B_{x}(r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\gamma+\beta-n}

since
r/2\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}
and
\beta>0
.
Similarly, when
z\in B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus B_{x}(r/2)
, we have
d_{g}{(x,z)}\geq r/2
, so that

(A.5)

\int_{B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus B_{x}(r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(z,y)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq Cr^{\beta-n}\int_{B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus B_{x}(r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(y,z)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
&\leq Cr^{\beta+\gamma-n}\end{aligned}

since
5r/2\leq 5/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}
and
\gamma>0
.
Finally, when
z\in B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})\setminus B_{x}(3r/2)
, we have
\frac{1}{3}d_{g}{(x,z)}\leq d_{g}{(z,y)}\leq\frac{5}{3}d_{g}{(x,z)}
, so that

\int_{B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})\setminus B_{x}(3r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(z,y)}^{n-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\int_{B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})\setminus B_{x}(3r/2)}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{2n-\beta-\gamma}}\,dv_{g}\\
&\leq C\begin{cases}r^{\beta+\gamma-n}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma<n\\
1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha}r\right\rvert&\text{when }\beta+\gamma=n\\
\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma>n\end{cases}\end{aligned}

since
3/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}
. This concludes the proof of (
A.3
) when
r\leq 2\sqrt{\alpha}
, realizing that when
\beta+\gamma>n
,
r^{\beta+\gamma-n}\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}
in (
A.4
) and (
A.5
).

We now claim that

(A.6)

\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}\left\lvert X(x,z)\right\rvert\left\lvert Y(z,y)\right\rvert\,dv_{g}(z)\leq C\begin{cases}r^{\beta+\gamma-n}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma<n\\
\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}&\text{when }\beta+\gamma\geq n\end{cases}.

To prove (
A.6
), note that, by assumption on
X,Y
, the integral in (
A.6
) has non-zero contribution only on the support of
X
and
Y
, i.e. on
B_{x}(\tau)\cap B_{y}(\sigma)
. Moreover, when
z\in M\setminus B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have
d_{g}{(x,z)}\geq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
,
d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{3}d_{g}{(x,z)}\leq d_{g}{(z,y)}\leq\frac{5}{3}d_{g}{(x,z)}
, so that

\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}\left\lvert X(x,z)\right\rvert\left\lvert Y(z,y)\right\rvert\,dv_{g}(z)\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\int_{\left(B_{x}(\tau)\cap B_{y}(\sigma)\right)\setminus B_{x}(3/\sqrt{\alpha})}\alpha^{p+q}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho+\nu}e^{-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,z)}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
&\leq C\alpha^{p+q}\int_{3/\sqrt{\alpha}}^{\tau}t^{\rho+\nu+n-1}e^{-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\alpha}t}dt\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}},\end{aligned}

where the last inequality follows from (
A.1
). This concludes the proof of (
A.6
) for
r\leq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
, realizing that when
\beta+\gamma<n
,
\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}\leq Cr^{\beta+\gamma-n}
.

Combining (
A.3
) and (
A.6
) with (
A.2
), we have proven that, for
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
,

(A.7)

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{-(n-\beta-\gamma)}&\beta+\gamma<n\\
C(1+\left\lvert\log(\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)})\right\rvert)&\beta+\gamma=n\\
C\alpha^{-\frac{\beta+\gamma-n}{2}}&\beta+\gamma>n\end{cases}.

For the second part of the proof, assume now that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
. Write again
r:=d_{g}{(x,y)}
, we split the domain
M
in the integral that defines
Z(x,y)
in several parts:
B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
,
B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
,
B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha}))
and
M\setminus B_{x}(3r/2)
. As before, the integral has non-zero contributions only in
\Omega_{0}:=B_{x}(\tau)\cap B_{y}(\sigma)
, and
\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset B_{x}(i_{g}),\,\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset B_{y}(i_{g})
.
It is then clear that
Z(x,\cdot)
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau+\sigma)
. Without loss of generality, we can therefore assume that
\Omega_{0}\neq{\text{\Large\o}}
, i.e. we can restrict to the case where
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq\sigma+\tau
.

For
z\in B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have
d_{g}{(y,z)}\geq r-1/\sqrt{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{2}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq d_{g}{(y,z)}\leq\frac{3}{2}d_{g}{(x,y)}
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}\alpha^{q}\frac{d_{g}{(y,z)}^{\nu}}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,y)}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{q}r^{\nu}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}\frac{1}{d_{g}{(x,z)}^{n-\beta}}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{q-\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{\nu}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}r}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{n-\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}+\frac{\rho+\nu}{2}}r^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}r}

since
1/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}
, where the last inequality follows from (
A.1
) and
n+\rho\geq 0
. The same arguments on
B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
similarly show that

\left\lvert\int_{B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{n-\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}+\frac{\rho+\nu}{2}}r^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}r}.

Now for
z\in B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)
, we have
d_{g}{(x,z)}+d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq d_{g}{(x,y)}
, so that

(A.8)

\left\lvert\int_{B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\
\leq C\alpha^{p+q}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}d_{g}{(z,y)}^{\nu}\,dv_{g}(z).

(A.9)

\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}d_{g}{(z,y)}^{\nu}\,dv_{g}(z)\leq Cr^{\rho+\nu+n}

for

.

B_{x}(r/2)\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})

\Omega_{0}\cap B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(r/2)\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)

since
r/2<i_{g}
and
\rho+n>0
. For analogous reasons, we have

\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(r/2)\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}d_{g}{(z,y)}^{\nu}\,dv_{g}(z)\leq Cr^{\rho+\nu+n}.

This concludes the proof of (
A.9
). Putting (
A.8
) with (
A.9

r\geq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
,

\left\lvert\int_{B_{x}(3r/2)\setminus\left(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\right)}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\

using (
A.1
).

\left\lvert\int_{M}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{\frac{n-\beta-\gamma}{2}}.

C>0
independent of

,

\tfrac{1}{C}\alpha^{\frac{n-\beta-\gamma}{2}}\leq\alpha^{n-\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}+\frac{\rho+\nu}{2}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}\leq C\alpha^{\frac{n-\beta-\gamma}{2}}.

We can then conclude, when
1\leq\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 2
we have

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{n-\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}+\frac{\rho+\nu}{2}}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho+\nu+n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}.

Regarding the continuity, fix
x,y\in M
with
x\neq y
, and take any sequence
((x_{m},y_{m}))_{m}
such that
x_{m}\to x
,
y_{m}\to y
. Let
\delta_{m}:=d_{g}{(x_{m},x)},\,\tilde{\delta}_{m}:=d_{g}{(y_{m},y)}
, we assume without loss of generality that
d_{g}{(x_{m},x)}\leq\tfrac{1}{3}d_{g}{(x,y)}
and
d_{g}{(y_{m},y)}\leq\tfrac{1}{3}d_{g}{(x,y)}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we compute

(A.10)

Z(x_{m},y_{m})=\int_{B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z)\\
+\int_{B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2)}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z)\\
+\int_{M\setminus(B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)\cup B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}/2))}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z).

On the one hand, when
z\in B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)
, we have
d_{g}{(z,y_{m})}>r/2
, writing once again
r=d_{g}{(x,y)}>0
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\gamma-n}\int_{B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)}d_{g}{(x_{m},z)}^{\beta-n}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{m}^{\beta}r^{\gamma-n}.

Similarly, when
z\in B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2)
, we have
d_{g}{(x_{m},z)}>r/2
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2)}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\beta-n}\int_{B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2)}d_{g}{(z,y_{m})}^{\gamma-n}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{\delta}_{m}^{\gamma}r^{\beta-n}.

On the other hand, when
z\not\in(B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)\cup B_{x_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2))
, we have
d_{g}{(x_{m},z)}\geq\tfrac{1}{3}d_{g}{(x,z)}
and
d_{g}{(z,y_{m})}\geq\tfrac{1}{3}d_{g}{(z,y)}
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert X(x_{m},z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{g}{(x,z)}^{\beta-n},

\displaystyle\left\lvert Y(z,y_{m})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{g}{(z,y)}^{\gamma-n}

By dominated convergence, we obtain

\int_{M\setminus(B_{x_{m}}(\delta_{m}/2)\cup B_{y_{m}}(\tilde{\delta}_{m}/2))}X(x_{m},z)Y(z,y_{m})\,dv_{g}(z)\\
\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}\int_{M}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z).

Coming back to (
A.10
), we have shown that for all
x\neq y
in
M
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}Z(x_{m},y_{m})=\int_{M}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)+0+0=Z(x,y),

and
Z
is continuous at
(x,y)\in M\times M\setminus Diag
.
Additionally, when
\beta+\gamma>n
, for all
w\in M
, and for all
0<\delta<1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in M
,

\left\lvert\int_{B_{w}(\delta)}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\leq C\delta^{\beta+\gamma-n},

this holds true even when
x=y
. We conclude that
Z\in C^{0}(M\times M)
.
∎

Remark A.1
.

The assumption (
A.1
) is a compatibility condition. If
2p-\rho=n-\beta
, we know that the two regimes of
X
are equivalent when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\sim 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
.

Remark A.2
.

Observe that the convolution
Z
decreases less quickly than either
X
and
Y
at large distances. This is due to the term
d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho+\nu+n}
that comes from (
A.8
). This term becomes larger than
\alpha^{-\frac{\rho+\nu+n}{2}}
when
d_{g}{(x,y)}\gg 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, which happens at finite distance as
\alpha\to\infty
.

We conclude this Appendix with variant of Lemma
A.2
, which is used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, where
X
and
Y
are allowed to have slightly different exponential decay.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
and let
X,Y\in C^{0}(M\times M\setminus Diag)
be such that
X(x,\cdot)
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau)
,
\tau<i_{g}/2
. Suppose that there are
p\geq 0
and
\rho>-n
with
2p-\rho\leq 0
, and
0<\varepsilon<1
such that for all
x\neq y
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert X(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{p}d_{g}{(x,y)}^{\rho}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left\lvert Y(x,y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}\,i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2.\end{cases}

Let, for all
x,y\in M
,
Z(x,y):=\int_{M}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)
. There exists
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in M
and
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
,

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}\begin{cases}1&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}}&\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq 1\\
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}\,i_{g}/2}&d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2\end{cases}.

Remark A.3
.

Classically, the exponential decay of
Z
exactly matches that of the least decreasing term
Y
.

The proof follows the same steps as the proof of Lemma
A.2
, and we just explain the modifications.

Proof.

Assume first that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq 2/\sqrt{\alpha}
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
A.2
in the case
\beta=\gamma=n
, we obtain

\left\lvert Z(x,y)\right\rvert\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

Assume now that
2/\sqrt{\alpha}\leq d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq i_{g}/2
. We adapt the proof of Lemma
A.2
. Write
r=d_{g}{(x,y)}
and split the domain
M
in the integral that defines
Z(x,y)
between
B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
,
B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, and
M\setminus(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha}))
.

When
z\in B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have
d_{g}{(y,z)}\geq r-1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}.

Now when
z\in B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have

\left\lvert X(x,z)\right\rvert\leq Ce^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r},

since
d_{g}{(x,z)}\geq r-1/\sqrt{\alpha}
and
p-\rho/2<0
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}.

Finally, when
z\in M\setminus(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha}))
, we have
d_{g}{(x,z)}+d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq r
and
d_{g}{(x,z)}+i_{g}/2\geq i_{g}/2\geq r
, so that since
X(x,\cdot)
is supported in
B_{x}(\tau)
,

(A.11)

\left\lvert\int_{M\setminus(B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\cup B_{y}(1/\sqrt{\alpha}))}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\alpha^{p}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{B_{x}(\tau)\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}e^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,z)}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
&\leq C\alpha^{p-\frac{\rho}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}t^{\rho+n-1}e^{-t}dt\\
&\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}r}\end{aligned}

since
p-\rho/2<0
. This concludes the proof for the case
2/\sqrt{\alpha}\leq d_{g}{(x,y)}\leq i_{g}/2
.

For the last case, assume that
d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2
, and split the domain
M
in the integral that defines
Z(x,y)
in two parts:
B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
and
M\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
. When
z\in B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have
d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq d_{g}{(x,y)}-1/\sqrt{\alpha}\geq i_{g}/2-1/\sqrt{\alpha}
, so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}\int_{B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}\,dv_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}.

On the other hand, when
z\in M\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, we have

d_{g}{(x,z)}+d_{g}{(z,y)}\geq d_{g}{(x,y)}\geq i_{g}/2,

so that, as in (
A.11
),

\left\lvert\int_{M\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dv_{g}(z)\right\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\alpha^{p}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}\int_{B_{x}(\tau)\setminus B_{x}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})}d_{g}{(x,z)}^{\rho}e^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(x,z)}}\,dv_{g}(z)\\
&\leq C\alpha^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\alpha}i_{g}/2}\end{aligned}

using
p-\rho/2<0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

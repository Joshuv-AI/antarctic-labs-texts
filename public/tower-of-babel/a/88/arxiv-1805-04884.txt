Introduction

Several recent works (e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
), motivated by mathematical physics and probability, have used explicit central elements of various quantum groups. For the applications in those paper, the central elements need to be explicitly written in terms of the generators of the quantum group. In particular, in a probabilistic perspective, the probabilities are required to be non-negative, which requires explicit expressions for the central elements for calculations. Furthermore, in an asymptotic analysis, the analysis tools also require explicit expressions for the central elements.

The explicit generators of the center of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
was first constructed for generic
q

in
[
7
]
without proof (see
[
1
]
). Previous work of
[
24
]
applies Drinfeld’s central element construction
[
6
]
to universal
R
-matrices in order to construct central elements of quantum groups and to determine their eigenvalues on irreducible highest weight modules.

Jimbo in
[
9
]
provided a method to construct a polynomial
z(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N+1}z_{i}x^{i}
in variable
x
such that all
z_{i}
’s are central. Tanisaki in
[
21
]
proved that all these
z_{i}
’s generate the whole centre by quantized Harish-Chandra isomorphism. However, the expression of
z_{i}
’s in
[
9
]
are not explicit enough for applications cited in the first paragraph.

By using Jimbo’s
[
9
]
formula for the
R
-matrix of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
, further work by the same authors
[
8
]
explicitly writes a quantum Casimir element of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
with a formula for its eigenvalues. Using an explicit formula for the universal
R
-matrix in
[
11
]
, the authors
[
23
]
write an explicit (but somewhat complicated) expression for general Casimir elements in quantum groups.

In this paper, we apply Drinfeld’s central element construction to the fused
R
-matrices of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
in
[
9
]
, rather than the universal
R
-matrices of
[
11
]
. The resulting central elements appear to be slightly simpler than the previous expressions. The proof requires some new ingredients, notably relations between the root vectors
[
10
,
18
,
22
]
and some elementary knowledge of coset representatives of symmetric groups.

We also note the work in
[
16
]
, which shows that the Casimir elements (and a trivial central element) generate the entire center of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
, although this had been known as early as
[
20
]
. Additionally, the paper
[
17
]
explicitly writes two algebraically independent central elements in
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
. Recently, Dai
[
5
]
provided the explicit generators of the center of all (simply-connected) quantum groups of finite type by following
[
24
]
. In principle, it should be possible to match those central elements to the ones here, but we do not pursue this direction here.

Notations and backgrounds

2.1
Symmetric groups

Recall that the symmetric group is the Weyl group of the special linear group. Define the usual action of the symmetric group
S_{m}
on
\mathbb{N}^{m}
by
\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(m)})
. For any
A\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
, define
H_{A}\leq S_{m}
to be the subgroup
\{\sigma\in S_{m}\colon\sigma(A)=A\}
. Let
D_{A}\subset S_{m}
be the set of (left) coset representatives of
H_{A}
with the fewest inversions: in other words,
\sigma\in D_{A}
if and only if
\inv(\sigma)\leq\inv(\tau)
for every
\tau\in\sigma H_{A}
. Here,
\inv
is the length function defined as the fewest number
of simple reflections constituting the Weyl group element.

Example 2.1
.

Take
A=(3,3,3,2,2,1).
Then
H_{A}=S_{3}\times S_{2}\times S_{1}\leq S_{6}
. We have
(34)\in D_{A}
with
(34)\cdot A=(3,3,2,3,2,1)
, and
(35)\in D_{A}
with
(35)\cdot A=(3,3,2,2,3,1)
. However,
(134)\cdot A=(34)\cdot A
and
\inv((134))>\inv((34))
, so
(134)\notin D_{A}
.

We recall (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) that each coset of
H_{A}
has a unique representative
\sigma\in D_{A}
, and that
\inv(\sigma\tau)=\inv(\sigma)+\inv(\tau)
for every
\tau\in H_{A}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in D_{A}
. Then there exist a sequence of elements
\tau_{0},\dots,\tau_{l}\in D_{A}
such that
\tau_{0}=\tau^{\prime}
,
\tau_{l}=\tau
and every
\tau_{j+1}\tau_{j}^{-1}
is a transposition for
j=0,\dots,l-1
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this statement when either
\tau
or
\tau^{\prime}
is the identity permutation
e
, because the two sequences can be concatenated. So suppose that
\tau
is an arbitrary element of
D_{A}
and
\tau^{\prime}=e
. Let
s_{k}\dots s_{1}
be a minimal word representation of
\tau
and set
\tau_{j}=s_{j}\dots s_{1}
. Then
\tau_{j+1}\tau_{j}^{-1}=s_{j+1}
, which is a transposition. So it remains to show that
\tau_{j}\in D_{A}
. If it were not, then there would exist a transposition
s\in H_{A}
such that
\inv(\tau_{j}s)=\inv(\tau_{j})-1
. But then
\inv(\tau s)=\inv(\tau)-1
, contradicting the assumption that
\tau\in D_{A}
.

Now suppose that
\tau=e
and
\tau^{\prime}
is an arbitrary element of
D_{A}
. By the previous paragraph, there exist a sequence of elements
\tilde{\tau}_{0}=e,\tilde{\tau}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\tau}_{l}=\tau^{\prime}
in
D_{A}
such that every
\tilde{\tau}_{j+1}\tilde{\tau}_{j}^{-1}
is a transposition. Setting
\tau_{j}=\tilde{\tau}_{l-j}
, we have that
\tau_{0}=\tau^{\prime},\dots,\tau_{l}=e
is a sequence of elements in
D_{A}
and
{\tau}_{l-j-1}{\tau}_{l-j}^{-1}
is a transposition for every
j
. The latter equality is equivalent to the condition that for every
k
,
\tau_{k}=s\tau_{k+1}
for some transposition
s
. Since this is also equivalent to the condition that
\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k}^{-1}
is a transposition for every
k
, this finishes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{(N)}
denote the set of sequences
\mu=(\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{N})
of non-negative integers such that
\mu_{0}+\dots+\mu_{N}=m
. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{B}_{m}^{(N)}
, let
H^{\mu}\leq S_{m}
denote the subgroup
S_{\mu_{0}}\times\dots\times S_{\mu_{N}}
, and likewise let
D^{\mu}
denote the set of left coset representatives with the fewest inversions. Define
\mathcal{B}_{m}
to be the union
\bigcup_{N\geq 1}\mathcal{B}_{m}^{(N)}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{m}\subset\mathbb{N}^{m}
denote the subset of elements
(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
satisfying
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{m}
. For
{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}
, and assuming that
N\geq i_{m}
, let
\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}})\in\mathcal{B}_{m}^{(N)}
be defined by

\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}})_{k}=|\{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\colon i_{l}=k\}|.

For
{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}
satisfying
i_{m}\leq N
, we have a natural isomorphism between the subgroups
H_{{\mathbf{i}}}
and
H^{\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}})}
. Thus there is also a natural bijection between
D_{{\mathbf{i}}}
and
D^{\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}})}
.

Given
{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}
, define the equivalence relation
\sim_{{\mathbf{i}}}
on
S_{m}
by

\tau\sim_{{\mathbf{i}}}\sigma\text{ if and only if }\sigma^{-1}\tau\in H_{{\mathbf{i}}}.

In words,
\tau\sim_{{\mathbf{i}}}\sigma
if and only if they are in the same left coset of
H_{{\mathbf{i}}}
. For any
\tau\in S_{m}
, there is a unique
\sigma\in D_{{\mathbf{i}}}
such that
\tau\sim_{{\mathbf{i}}}\sigma
. Define
d_{{\mathbf{i}}}(\tau)=\inv\big(\sigma^{-1}\tau\big)
. In other words, if
\tau
is written uniquely as
\tau=\sigma\xi
for
\sigma\in D_{{\mathbf{i}}}
and
\xi\in H_{{\mathbf{i}}}
, then
d_{{\mathbf{i}}}(\tau)=\inv(\xi)
. We will also let
\sigma_{{\mathbf{i}}}(\tau)
and
\xi_{{\mathbf{i}}}(\tau)
denote the two permutations in the unique expression
\tau=\sigma\xi
.

Finally, given
\tau\in S_{m}
, let
\bar{\tau}
denote the reversed permutation
\bar{\tau}(k)=\tau(m+1-k)
.

We conclude this section by noting the following identity.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}
, set
\mu=\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}})
. Then

-N\mu_{0}-(N-2)\mu_{1}+\dots+N\mu_{N}=-Nm+2(i_{1}+\dots+i_{m}).

Proof.

By definition,

\mu_{k}=|l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\colon i_{l}=k|.

We thus rewrite

\displaystyle-N\mu_{0}-(N-2)\mu_{1}+\dots+N\mu_{N}

\displaystyle=-N(\mu_{0}+\dots+\mu_{N})+2\mu_{1}+4\mu_{2}+\dots+2N\mu_{N}

\displaystyle=-Nm+2\mu_{1}+4\mu_{2}+\dots+2N\mu_{N}

and note that

2(i_{1}+\dots+i_{m})=2\sum_{k=1}^{N}k\cdot|l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\colon i_{l}=k|=2\sum_{k=1}^{N}k\cdot\mu_{k},

which shows the identity.
∎

2.2
Quantum groups

We use the following notation modified from
[
9
]
.
Define
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(N+1))
to be the associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by the symbols
q^{\pm h_{i}/2}
,
\hat{e}_{\pm,i}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, under the following relations:

\displaystyle q^{h_{i}/2}\cdot q^{-h_{i}/2}=q^{-h_{i}/2}\cdot q^{h_{i}/2}=1,\qquad q^{h_{i}/2}\cdot q^{h_{i^{\prime}}/2}=q^{h_{i^{\prime}}/2}\cdot q^{h_{i}/2},

\displaystyle q^{h_{i}/2}\hat{e}_{\pm,i^{\prime}}q^{-h_{i}/2}=q^{\pm a_{ii^{\prime}}/2}\hat{e}_{\pm,i^{\prime}},

\displaystyle[\hat{e}_{+,i},\hat{e}_{-,i^{\prime}}]=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\frac{q^{h_{i}}-q^{-h_{i}}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{\pm,i}\hat{e}_{\pm,i^{\prime}}=\hat{e}_{\pm,i^{\prime}}\hat{e}_{\pm,i},\qquad|i-i^{\prime}|\geq 2,

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{\pm,i}^{2}\hat{e}_{\pm,i\pm 1}-\big(q+q^{-1}\big)\hat{e}_{\pm,i}\hat{e}_{\pm,i\pm 1}\hat{e}_{\pm,i}+\hat{e}_{\pm,i\pm 1}\hat{e}_{\pm,i}^{2}=0,\qquad 1\leq i,i\pm 1\leq N.

Here,
(a_{i,i^{\prime}})_{1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq N}
denotes the Cartan matrix of type
A_{N}
, i.e.,

a_{ii^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}2,&i=i^{\prime},\\
-1,&i=i^{\prime}\pm 1,\\
\hphantom{-}0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then define
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))
by adjoining to
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(N+1))
the elements
q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2}
,
0\leq i\leq N
, so that
q^{h_{i}}=q^{\epsilon_{i-1}-\epsilon_{i}}
and that
q^{\epsilon_{0}+\dots+\epsilon_{N}}
belongs to the center.

The
m
-fold co-product is the algebra homomorphism

\Delta^{(m)}\colon\ {U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\rightarrow\underbrace{{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\dots\otimes{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}}_{m}

such that

\displaystyle\Delta^{(m)}\big(q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2}\big)=q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2},\qquad\Delta^{(m)}(\hat{e}_{\pm,i})=\sum_{v=1}^{m}\hat{e}^{(v)}_{\pm,i},

(2.1)

where

\hat{e}^{(v)}_{\pm,i}=\underbrace{q^{h_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{h_{i}/2}}_{v-1}\otimes\hat{e}_{\pm,i}\otimes\underbrace{q^{-h_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{-h_{i}/2}}_{m-v}.

We also have the reversed co-product

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}^{(m)}\big(q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2}\big)=q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{\pm\epsilon_{i}/2},

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}^{(m)}(\hat{e}_{\pm,i})=\sum_{v=1}^{m}\underbrace{q^{-h_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{-h_{i}/2}}_{v-1}\otimes\hat{e}_{\pm,i}\otimes q^{h_{i}/2}\otimes\dots\otimes q^{h_{i}/2}.

We write
\Delta
,
\bar{\Delta}
for
\Delta^{(2)}
,
\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}
. The map
\Delta
endows
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
with a structure of a bi-algebra. It is also a Hopf algebra, but we will not need the counit and antipode.

Consider the following relations for
R
:

\displaystyle R\Delta(u)=\bar{\Delta}(u)R\qquad\forall u\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}.

(2.2)

This admits a unique (up to a multiplicative constant) solution

R\in\End(V\otimes V)
, where
V:=\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
is the defining representation of
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
.

Let us consider (
2.2
) in
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End\big({\mathbb{C}}^{N+1}\big)
. Then a family of solutions is given by (see
[
9
]
)
R(x)=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq N}\hat{E}_{ij}^{\prime}(x)\otimes e_{ji}
, where

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{\prime}_{ij}(x)=\begin{cases}\big(xq^{(\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}-1)/2}\big)^{\mp 1}{E}^{\prime}_{ij},&i\lessgtr j,\\
\big(xq^{\epsilon_{i}}-x^{-1}q^{-\epsilon_{i}}\big)/\big(q-q^{-1}\big),&i=j.\end{cases}

For this paper, only the value at
x=1
will be used in the proofs, but for completeness Jimbo’s result is stated in its full generality. Here,
E^{\prime}_{ij}
are the root vectors defined recursively by

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{ij}=E_{ik}^{\prime}E_{kj}^{\prime}-q^{\pm 1}E_{kj}^{\prime}E_{ik}^{\prime},\qquad i\lessgtr k\lessgtr j,\qquad E^{\prime}_{i-1,i}=\hat{e}_{+,i},\qquad E^{\prime}_{i,i-1}=\hat{e}_{-,i}

and
e_{ji}
is the usual matrix which acts on the canonical basis
\{I_{0},\dots,I_{N}\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
by

e_{ji}I_{k}=\delta_{ik}I_{j}.

In
[
8
]
, the authors define

\hat{E}_{ij}=\begin{cases}q^{(E_{ii}+E_{jj}-1)/2}E_{ij},&i\neq j,\\
q^{E_{ii}}/\big(q-q^{-1}\big),&i=j,\end{cases}

where the modified root vectors are

E_{ij}=E_{ik}E_{kj}-q^{-1}E_{kj}E_{ik},\qquad i\lessgtr k\lessgtr j,\qquad E^{\prime}_{i-1,i}=\hat{e}_{+,i},\qquad E^{\prime}_{i,i-1}=\hat{e}_{-,i}.

Using Jimbo’s results, they show that

\displaystyle R=\sum_{N\geq i\geq j\geq 0}\hat{E}_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End(V),

\displaystyle R^{T}=\sum_{N\geq i\geq j\geq 0}\hat{E}_{ji}\otimes e_{ij}\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End(V)

satisfy

\displaystyle R\Delta(u)=\bar{\Delta}(u)R,\qquad R^{T}\bar{\Delta}(u)={\Delta}(u)R^{T}.

We can solve (
2.2
) even more generally. Consider the
m
-fold tensor of the defining representation
V^{\otimes m}
, and let
P_{m}V^{\otimes m}
be the symmetric projection. In other words,
P_{m}V^{\otimes m}
is isomorphic to the module of highest weight
m\nu
, where
\nu
is the highest weight of
V
.

Then the solution of (
2.2
) in
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End(P_{m}V^{\otimes m})
is given by the fused
R
-matrix
[
9
]

\displaystyle R_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}(x)=R_{0m}(x)R_{0m-1}(xq)\cdots R_{01}\big(xq^{m-1}\big)P_{m}\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End\big(P_{m}V^{\otimes m}\big).

Therefore, the fused
R
-matrices

\displaystyle R_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}=R_{0m}R_{0m-1}\cdots R_{01}P_{m}\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N+1})\otimes\End\big(P_{m}V^{\otimes m}\big),

\displaystyle R^{T}_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}=R^{T}_{0m}R^{T}_{0m-1}\cdots R^{T}_{01}P_{m}\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N+1})\otimes\End\big(P_{m}V^{\otimes m}\big)

satisfy

\displaystyle R_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}\Delta(u)=\bar{\Delta}(u)R_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}},\qquad R^{T}_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}\bar{\Delta}(u)={\Delta}(u)R^{T}_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}

(2.3)

in
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End(P_{m}V^{\otimes m})
for all
u\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
.

Therefore,

\Gamma_{m}:=R^{T}_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}R_{0,\{1,2,\dots,m\}}\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}\otimes\End\big(P_{m}V^{\otimes m}\big)

commutes with
\Delta(u)
for all
u\in{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
. By Drinfeld’s central element construction
[
6
]
, the element

C_{m}=\id\otimes\tr_{q}(\Gamma_{m})

is central in
{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(N+1))}
, where the quantum trace
\tr_{q}
of an operator
A
is defined by

\tr_{q}(A)=\tr\big(q^{-2h_{\rho}}A\big):=\tr\big(q^{-N\epsilon_{0}-(N-2)\epsilon_{1}-\dots+N\epsilon_{N}}A\big).

Lemma 2.4
.

1.

For every
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in D_{A}
, the following identity holds:

\sum_{\sigma\in D_{B}}q^{\mp(\inv(\sigma)-\inv(\tau))}\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\tau(A)\sigma(B)}=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{B}}q^{\mp(\inv(\sigma)-\inv(\tau^{\prime}))}\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\tau^{\prime}(A)\sigma(B)}.

2.

Likewise, for every
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in D_{B}
,

\sum_{\tau\in D_{A}}q^{\mp(\inv(\tau)-\inv(\sigma))}\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\tau(A)\sigma(B)}=\sum_{\tau\in D_{A}}q^{\mp(\inv(\tau)-\inv(\sigma^{\prime}))}\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\tau(A)\sigma^{\prime}(B)}.

Proof.

For
m=2
, both cases are equivalent to the relations in (

) and (
2.5
).

Now suppose that
m>2

\tau^{\prime}=s\tau

\displaystyle D_{B}^{(1)}=\{\sigma\in D_{B}\colon s\sigma(B)=B\},\qquad D_{B}^{(2)}=\{\sigma\in D_{B}\colon s\sigma(B)\neq B\}.

\sum_{\sigma\in D_{B}^{(1)}}q^{\inv(\sigma)-\inv(\tau)}\hat{E}^{-}_{\tau(A)\sigma(B)}=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{B}^{(1)}}q^{\inv(\sigma)-\inv(s\tau)}\hat{E}^{-}_{s\tau(A)\sigma(B)}.

Remark 3.4
.

In

, the central element

is defined, where

is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional irreducible module

. The construction there is similar to the one here, with the major difference being the use of explicit universal
R
-matrices in place of fused
R
-matrices. Although it is not necessarily simple to check directly that
C_{m}
equals (up to a constant)
C^{\Lambda_{0}}
for
V(\Lambda_{0})=P_{m}V^{\otimes m}
, it is straightforward to check that their eigenvalues are the same.

If

has distinct weights
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
with multiplicities
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}
, then the eigenvalue of
C^{\Lambda_{0}}
on an irreducible module with highest weight
\Lambda
is given by

\sum_{k=1}^{r}d_{k}q^{2(\Lambda+\rho,\lambda_{k})}.

Here,
\rho
is half the sum of the positive roots, and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the usual invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Now take
\Lambda_{0}
to be the highest weight of
P_{m}V^{\otimes m}
; then the distinct weights are elements of
\mathcal{B}^{(N)}_{m}
with multiplicity

. Therefore the eigenvalue is

\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{B}^{(N)}_{m}}q^{(2\rho,\mu)}q^{2(\Lambda,\mu)}.

If
C_{m}
acts on the same module
V(\Lambda)
, then its eigenvalue can be found by evaluating on the lowest weight vector, because then only the diagonal terms
({\mathbf{i}}={\mathbf{j}})
have a nonzero contribution. So the eigenvalue is

\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{-2m}q^{-Nm}\sum_{{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{W}_{m}}q^{2i_{1}+\dots+2i_{m}}q^{(2\mu^{(N)}({\mathbf{i}}),\Lambda)}.

By Lemma
2.3
, this is
\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{-2m}q^{-Nm}
times the eigenvalue of
C^{\Lambda_{0}}
.

Theorem 3.6

The set

Proof.

Note that
\big|\mathcal{B}^{(N)}_{m}\big|=\dimension P_{m}V^{\otimes m}

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{B}^{(N)}_{m}

\Delta^{(m)}(\hat{e}_{-,i})(M(\mu))=q^{\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{i-1}+\mu_{i}-1)}\big(1+q^{-2}+q^{-4}+\dots+q^{-2\mu_{i}}\big)\cdot M\big(\mu-\hat{i}\big),

where

with the

) and (

),
\Delta^{(m)}(\hat{e}_{-,i})(M(\mu))
is in the span of
\{\tau(v(\mu-\hat{i}))\}_{\tau\in S_{m}}

be the coefficients in the expansion

\Delta^{(m)}(\hat{e}_{-,i})(M(\mu))=\sum_{\tau\in D^{\mu-\hat{i}}}A(\tau)\tau\big(v\big(\mu-\hat{i}\big)\big).

It suffices to show that

for all
\tau\in D^{\mu-\hat{i}}
. In fact, we show something stronger: for all
\tau\in D^{\mu-\hat{i}}
, there exist elements
\sigma^{(0)},\dots,\sigma^{(\mu_{i})}\in D^{\mu}
such that for

\hat{e}_{-,i}^{(a_{j})}\big(q^{-\inv(\sigma^{(j)})}\sigma^{(j)}(v(\mu))\big)=q^{\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{i-1}+\mu_{i}-1)}q^{-2j}q^{-\inv(\tau)}\tau\big(v\big(\mu-\hat{i}\big)\big).

\inv(\tau)
, using Lemma
2.2
. The base case is when
\tau

\sigma^{(j)}=(\mu_{[0,i-1]}\ \ \mu_{[0,i-1]}+1\ \ \dots\ \ \mu_{[0,i-1]}+j),

where
\mu_{[0,i-1]}=\mu_{0}+\dots+\mu_{i-1}

Now fix
\tau\in D^{\mu-\hat{i}}

satisfies
\tilde{\tau}=s\tau

\inv(\tilde{\tau})=\inv(\tau)+1

\sigma^{(j)},&\text{else. }\end{cases}

We now aim to prove that

\hat{e}^{(s(a_{j}))}_{-,i}\big(q^{-\inv(\tilde{\sigma}^{(j)})}\tilde{\sigma}^{(j)}(v(\mu))\big)=q^{\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{i-1}+\mu_{i}-1)}q^{-2j}q^{-\inv(\tilde{\tau})}\tilde{\tau}\big(v\big(\mu-\hat{i}\big)\big).

(3.4)

If

) and the induction hypothesis. Now assume that

\tilde{\sigma}^{(j)}(v(\mu))=\underbrace{I_{*}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{*}}_{a_{j}-2}\otimes I_{i-1}\otimes I_{i-1}\otimes\underbrace{I_{*}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{*}}_{m-a_{j}}.

Bose-Einstein Condensation with Optimal Rate for Trapped Bosons in the Gross-Pitaevskii Regime
Christian Brennecke, Benjamin Schlein, Severin SchravenDepartment of Mathematics, Harvard University, One Oxford Street, Cambridge MA 02138, USAInstitute of Mathematics, University of Zurich, Winterthurerstrasse 190, 8057 Zurich, Switzerland

Abstract
We consider a Bose gas consisting of N N particles in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , trapped by an external field and interacting through a two-body potential with scattering length of order N − 1 N^{-1} . We prove that low energy states exhibit complete Bose-Einstein condensation with optimal rate, generalizing previous work in [ 1 , 4 ] , restricted to translation invariant systems. This extends recent results in [ 15 ] , removing the smallness assumption on the size of the scattering length.



1
Introduction and Main Results

We consider a system of
N\in\mathbb{N}
bosons trapped by an external potential in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime; the particles interact through a repulsive two-body potential with scattering length of order
N^{-1}
. The Hamilton operator as the form

H_{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[-\Delta_{x_{j}}+V_{ext}(x_{j})\right]+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}N^{2}V(N(x_{i}-x_{j})).

(1.1)

and it acts on a dense subspace of the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
, the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
consisting of functions that are symmetric with respect to permutations of the
N
particles. The confining potential
V_{ext}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
diverge to infinity, as
|x|\to\infty
(more precise conditions on
V_{ext}
will be introduced later on). Furthermore, we assume
V\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to be pointwise non-negative, spherically symmetric and compactly supported (but our results could easily be extended to potentials decaying sufficiently fast at infinity).

The scattering length of
V
is defined through the zero-energy scattering equation

\left[-\Delta+\frac{1}{2}V(x)\right]f(x)=0

(1.2)

with the boundary condition
f(x)\to 1
, as
|x|\to\infty
. For
|x|
large enough (outside the support of
V
) we find

f(x)=1-\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}{|x|}

where the constant
\mathfrak{a}_{0}>0
is known as the scattering length of
V
. A simple computation shows that

8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)f(x)dx.

(1.3)

Moreover, by scaling, (
1.2
) implies that

\left[-\Delta+\frac{1}{2}N^{2}V(Nx)\right]f(Nx)=0

so that the scattering length of
N^{2}V(N.)
is given by
\mathfrak{a}_{0}/N
.

From
[
14
]
, it is known that the ground state energy
E_{N}
of the Hamilton operator (
1.1
) satisfies

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{E_{N}}{N}=\inf_{\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|\psi\|_{2}=1}\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\psi),

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{E}_{GP}
denotes the Gross-Pitaevskii energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}+V_{ext}(x)|\psi(x)|^{2}+4\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\psi(x)|^{4}\right)dx.

(1.5)

For the rest of the paper, we will lighten the notation and write
\int
instead of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
. Furthermore, we will write
\|\cdot\|
for the
L^{2}
-norm and indicate other
L^{p}
-norms by a suitable subscript.
The Gross–Pitaevskii functional
\mathcal{E}_{GP}
admits a unique normalized, strictly positive minimizer
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. It satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta\varphi+V_{ext}\varphi+8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\varphi=\varepsilon_{GP}\varphi

(1.6)

with the Lagrange multiplier
\varepsilon_{GP}:=\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+4\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}\|\varphi\|_{4}^{4}
. As first shown in
[
12
]
, the ground state of (
1.1
) exhibits complete Bose-Einstein condensation in the state
\varphi
. More precisely, if
\gamma^{(1)}_{N}=\operatorname{tr}_{2,\dots,N}|\psi_{N}\rangle\langle\psi_{N}|
denotes the one-particle reduced density associated with the ground state of (
1.1
), then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle\varphi,\gamma_{N}^{(1)}\varphi\rangle=1.

(1.7)

This implies that, in the ground state of (
1.1
), the fraction of particles in the state
\varphi
approaches one, as
N\to\infty
. The convergence in (
1.7
) was later extended in
[
13
,
16
]
to any sequence
\psi_{N}
of approximate ground states, satisfying

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\langle\psi_{N},H_{N}\psi_{N}\rangle=\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi).

For translation invariant systems (particles trapped in the box
\Lambda=[0;1]^{3}
, with periodic boundary conditions), (
1.4
) (stating, in this case, that
E_{N}/N\to 4\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}
) and (
1.7
) (establishing Bose-Einstein condensation in the zero-momentum mode
\varphi(x)=1
for all
x\in\Lambda
) have been proved in
[
1
,
4
,
10
]
to hold with the optimal rate of convergence. This result was recently generalised in
[
15
]
(extending the approach of
[
6
,
8
]
) to trapped systems described by the Hamilton operator (
1.1
), under the assumption of sufficiently small scattering length
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
.

Our goal in this paper is to obtain optimal bounds on the rate of convergence in (
1.4
) and (
1.7
), with no restriction on the size of the scattering length. To reach this goal, we adapt and extend the approach developed in
[
4
]
for the translation invariant case. To this end, we will impose, throughout the rest of this paper, the following conditions:

\begin{split}(1)\;&V\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),V(x)\geq 0\text{ for a.e.}\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},V\text{ spherically symmetric, }\operatorname{supp}(V)\text{ compact},\\
(2)\;&V_{ext}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}),V_{ext}(x)\to\infty\text{ as }|x|\to\infty,\\
&\exists\;C>0\;\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:V_{ext}(x+y)\leq C(V_{ext}(x)+C)(V_{ext}(y)+C),\\
&\nabla V_{ext},\Delta V_{ext}\text{ have at most exponential growth as }|x|\to\infty.\end{split}

(1.8)

Note in particular that all polynomials in
x^{2}
with positive leading coefficient satisfy condition
(2)
in (
1.8
). The assumptions that
V_{ext}(x+y)\leq C(V_{ext}(x)+C)(V_{ext}(y)+C)
and that
\nabla V_{ext},\Delta V_{ext}
grow at most exponentially allow for certain simplifications of our analysis and are needed for technical reasons only.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.8
) and let
E_{N}
denote the ground state energy of (
1.1
). Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

E_{N}\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)-C

(1.9)

and

H_{N}\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+C^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(1-|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|_{i}\big)-C.

In particular, if
\psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
with
\|\psi_{N}\|=1
is an sequence of approximate ground states such that

\langle\psi_{N},H_{N}\psi_{N}\rangle\leq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+\zeta,

for a
\zeta>0
, then the reduced density
\gamma_{N}^{(1)}
associated with
\psi_{N}
satisfies

1-\langle\varphi,\gamma_{N}^{(1)}\varphi\rangle\leq\frac{(C+\zeta)}{N}.

Remark:
Our techniques could also be used to prove an upper bound for
E_{N}
matching (
1.9
), implying that
|E_{N}-N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)|\leq C
. We do not show it, because it would require some non-trivial additional work (this is a consequence of our choice, leading to some technical simplifications, to work on the Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
rather than on
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\perp\varphi}
, where we impose orthogonality to
\varphi
; we will explain this point in the next section) and because it is already established in
[
15
]
(the upper bound there does not require restrictions on the size of the
potential)

Remark:
We plan to apply Theorem
1.1
in a separate paper to determine the low-energy spectrum of (
1.1
) and to establish the validity of the predictions of Bogoliubov theory, extending recent results obtained in
[
2
,
3
]
for the translation invariant setting.

2
Excitation Hamiltonians

We introduce the bosonic Fock space

\mathcal{F}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\otimes_{s}n}

On
\mathcal{F}
, we consider creation and annihilation operators, satisfying the
canonical commutation relations

[a(g),a^{*}(h)]=\langle g,h\rangle,\quad[a(g),a(h)]=[a^{*}(g),a^{*}(h)]=0

(2.1)

for all
g,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We also introduce position and momentum-space operator-valued distributions
a_{x},a_{x}^{*}
and
\hat{a}_{p},\hat{a}^{*}_{p}
, for
x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, so that

a(f)=\int\bar{f}(x)\,a_{x}\,dx=\int\bar{\hat{f}}(p)\,\hat{a}_{p}\,dp,\qquad a^{*}(f)=\int f(x)\,a_{x}^{*}\,dx=\int\hat{f}(p)\,\hat{a}_{p}^{*}\,dp.

In terms of these operator-valued distributions, the number of particles operator
\mathcal{N}
, defined by
(\mathcal{N}\Psi)^{(n)}=n\Psi^{(n)}
for every
\Psi\in\mathcal{F}
, takes the form

\mathcal{N}=\int a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\,dx=\int\hat{a}_{p}^{*}\hat{a}_{p}\,dp.

More generally, given an operator
A:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with kernel
A(x;y)
, we define its second quantization
d\Gamma(A)
acting in
\mathcal{F}
through

d\Gamma(A)=\int dxdy\,A(x;y)a^{*}_{x}a_{y}.

In particular,
\mathcal{N}=d\Gamma(1)
is the second quantization of the identity operator.

It is simple to check that creation and annihilation operators are bounded, with respect to
\mathcal{N}^{1/2}
. In fact, we find that

\|a(f)\Psi\|\leq\|f\|\ \|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\Psi\|,\quad\|a^{*}(f)\Psi\|\leq\|f\|\ \|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|.

(2.2)

To describe excitations of the Bose-Einstein condensate, we also define the truncated Fock spaces

\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{N}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\otimes_{s}n},\qquad\text{and }\quad\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\perp\varphi}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{N}L_{\perp\varphi}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\otimes_{s}n}

defined over
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and, respectively, over
L^{2}_{\perp\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the orthogonal complement of the condensate wave function
\varphi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(
\varphi
is the unique minimizer of (
1.5
)). Since they do not preserve the number of particles, creation and annihilation operators are not well-defined on
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\perp\varphi}
(but notice that products of a creation and an annihilation operators are well-defined). They are replaced by modified creation and annihilation operators

b^{*}(f)=a^{*}(f)\,\sqrt{\frac{N-\mathcal{N}}{N}},\qquad\quad b(f)=\sqrt{\frac{N-\mathcal{N}}{N}}\,a(f).

For every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
b(f)
and
b^{*}(f)
map
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
to
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
. If moreover
f\perp\varphi
, we also find
b(f),b^{*}(f):\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\perp\varphi}\to\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\perp\varphi}
. From (
2.2
) we have

\|b(f)\Psi\|\leq\|f\|\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\Psi\|,\quad\|b^{*}(f)\Psi\|\leq\|f\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|.

Also here, it is convenient to introduce operator-valued distributions
b_{x},b_{x}^{*}
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and, in momentum space
\hat{b}_{p},\hat{b}^{*}_{p}
, for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. They satisfy the commutation relations (focussing here on position space operators)

\begin{split}[b_{x},b_{y}^{*}]&=\left(1-\frac{\mathcal{N}}{N}\right)\delta(x-y)-\frac{1}{N}a_{y}^{*}a_{x},\hskip 28.45274pt[b_{x},b_{y}]=[b_{x}^{*},b_{y}^{*}]=0\end{split}

(2.3)

and

\begin{split}[b_{x},a_{y}^{*}a_{z}]&=\delta(x-y)b_{z},\qquad[b_{x}^{*},a_{y}^{*}a_{z}]=-\delta(x-z)b_{y}^{*}.\end{split}

(2.4)

In particular, it follows that
[b_{x},\mathcal{N}]=b_{x}
and
[b_{x}^{*},\mathcal{N}]=-b_{x}^{*}
.

We factor out the Bose-Einstein condensate applying a unitary map
U_{N}:L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})\to\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
, first introduced in
[
11
]
. To define
U_{N}
, we observe that any
\psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
can be uniquely decomposed as

\psi_{N}=\alpha_{0}\varphi_{0}^{\otimes N}+\alpha_{1}\otimes_{s}\varphi_{0}^{\otimes(N-1)}+\dots+\alpha_{N}

with
\alpha_{j}\in L^{2}_{\perp\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\otimes_{s}j}
for every
j=0,\dots,N
. Thus, we can set
U_{N}\psi_{N}=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
. It is then possible to show that
U_{N}
is unitary; see
[
11
]
for details.

With
U_{N}
, we can define the excitation Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{N}=U_{N}H_{N}U_{N}^{*}
, acting on a dense subspace of
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
.
The action of
U_{N}
on creation and annihilation operators is given by

\begin{split}U_{N}a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)U_{N}^{*}&=N-\mathcal{N},\\
U_{N}a^{*}(f)a(\varphi)U_{N}^{*}&=a^{*}(f)\sqrt{N-\mathcal{N}}=\sqrt{N}\,b^{*}(f),\\
U_{N}a^{*}(\varphi)a(g)U_{N}^{*}&=\sqrt{N-\mathcal{N}}a(g)=\sqrt{N}\,b(g),\\
U_{N}a^{*}(f)a(g)U_{N}^{*}&=a^{*}(f)a(g)\end{split}

(2.5)

for all
f,g\in L^{2}_{\perp\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the number of particles operator in
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
. Writing
H_{N}
in second quantized form and using (
2.5
), we proceed as in
[
11
, Section 4]
and find that

\mathcal{L}_{N}=\mathcal{L}^{(0)}_{N}+\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{N}+\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{N}+\mathcal{L}^{(3)}_{N}+\mathcal{L}^{(4)}_{N},

where, in the sense of forms in
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
, we have that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(0)}&=\big\langle\varphi,\big[-\Delta+V_{ext}+\frac{1}{2}\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\big]\varphi\big\rangle(N-\mathcal{N})\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\varphi,\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\varphi\big\rangle(\mathcal{N}+1)(1-\mathcal{N}/N),\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(1)}&=\sqrt{N}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{\sqrt{N}}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)+\text{h.c.},\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(2)}&=\int dx\;\left(\nabla_{x}a_{x}^{*}\nabla_{x}a_{x}+V_{ext}(x)a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\right)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\int dxdy\;N^{3}V(N(x-y))|\varphi(y)|^{2}\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{x}-\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\int dxdy\;N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{y}-\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{y}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\;N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\varphi(x)\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{y}^{*}+\text{h.c.}\Big),\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(3)}&=\int N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\big(b_{x}^{*}a^{*}_{y}a_{x}+\text{h.c.}\big)dxdy,\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(4)}&=\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\;N^{2}V(N(x-y))a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{y}a_{x}.\end{split}

(2.6)

While
\mathcal{L}_{N}
maps
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
to itself, the operators
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(j)}
,
j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
are also well defined as operators on
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
. In the following, it will be technically convenient to identify the
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(j)}
, through Eq. (
2.6
), as acting in
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
and in this case we will denote them by
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(j)}
. Moreover, we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(0)}_{N}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(1)}_{N}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(2)}_{N}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(3)}_{N}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(4)}_{N}

(2.7)

as a self-adjoint operator acting on a dense subspace in
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
. In particular, if
\Gamma(q)
is the orthogonal projection from
\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
onto
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
, defined by
(\Gamma(q)\Psi)^{(n)}=q^{\otimes n}\Psi^{(n)}
for every
n\in\{0,1,\dots,N\}
, with
q=1-|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|
, then
\mathcal{L}_{N}=\Gamma(q)\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Gamma(q)
in
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
.

The vacuum expectation of
\mathcal{L}_{N}
is given, up to terms of order one, by

N\int\left[|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}+V_{ext}(x)|\varphi(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{V}(0)|\varphi(x)|^{4}\right]dx

Since
\hat{V}(0)>8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}
, the difference between
\langle\Omega,\mathcal{L}_{N}\Omega\rangle
and the ground state energy of
\mathcal{L}_{N}
(or of
H_{N}
) is very large, of order
N
, in the limit
N\to\infty
. The point is that, through the map
U_{N}
, we expand
H_{N}
around the energy of the pure condensate wave function
\varphi^{\otimes N}\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
. In the Gross-Pitaevskii regime, however, it is well-known that short scale correlations among particles play a crucial role; they even affect the energy to leading order. To extract the missing correlation energy, we will conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{N}
with two unitary operator, given by the exponential of a quadratic and a cubic operator in (modified) creation and annihilation operators. We follow here the basic strategy of
[
4
]
. Loosely speaking, through unitary conjugation, we renormalize the singular interaction, producing a soft, mean-field potential whose ultraviolet behavior is easy to control. This procedure extracts the missing correlation energy from the higher order terms in
\mathcal{L}_{N}
and, at the same time, it generates the coercivity needed to show Theorem
1.1
.

In our first renormalization step, we will conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{N}
with a generalized Bogoliubov transformation. To define the kernel of the quadratic phase, we consider the ground state
solution of the Neumann problem

\left[-\Delta+\frac{1}{2}V\right]f_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}f_{\ell}

(2.8)

on the ball
|x|\leq N\ell
, for some
0<\ell<1
. For simplicity, we omit here the
N
-dependence in the notation for
f_{\ell}
and for
\lambda_{\ell}
. By radial symmetry of the interaction
V
,
f_{\ell}
is radially symmetric and we normalize it such that
f_{\ell}(x)=1
if
|x|=N\ell
. By scaling,
f_{\ell}(N.)
solves

\left[-\Delta+\frac{N^{2}}{2}V(Nx)\right]f_{\ell}(Nx)=N^{2}\lambda_{\ell}f_{\ell}(Nx)

on the ball where
|x|\leq\ell
. Later in our analysis, we will choose the parameter
\ell>0
to be sufficiently small, but it will always be of order one, independent of
N
. We then extend
f_{\ell}(N.)
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, by setting
f_{N,\ell}(x)=f_{\ell}(Nx)
if
|x|\leq\ell
and
f_{N,\ell}(x)=1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|x|>\ell
. Thus,
f_{\ell}(N.)
solves the equation

\left(-\Delta+\frac{N^{2}}{2}V(N.)\right)f_{N,\ell}=N^{2}\lambda_{\ell}f_{N,\ell}\chi_{\ell}

(2.9)

where
\chi_{\ell}
denotes the characteristic function of the ball
B_{\ell}(0)
of radius
\ell
, centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Finally, we denote by
w_{\ell}
the function
w_{\ell}=1-f_{\ell}
. Notice that by scaling,
w_{\ell}(N.)
has compact support in
B_{\ell}(0)
, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. Defining the Fourier transform of
w_{\ell}
through

\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p)=\int dx\;w_{\ell}(x)e^{-2\pi ipx},

we see that
w_{\ell}(N.)
has Fourier transform

\int dx\;w_{\ell}(Nx)e^{-2\pi ipx}=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p/N)

and we record that (
2.9
) implies that

\begin{split}-4\pi^{2}p^{2}\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p/N)+\frac{N^{2}}{2}(\widehat{V}(./N)\ast\widehat{f}_{N,\ell})(p)=N^{5}\lambda_{\ell}(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}\ast\widehat{f}_{N,\ell})(p).\end{split}

The next lemma collects important properties of
f_{\ell},w_{\ell}
and the Neumann eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be non-negative, compactly supported and spherically symmetric. Fix
\ell>0
and let
f_{\ell}
denote the solution of (
2.8
).

i)

We have that

\lambda_{\ell}=\frac{3\mathfrak{a}_{0}}{(\ell N)^{3}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}{\ell N}\right)\right)

(2.10)

ii)

We have
0\leq f_{\ell},w_{\ell}\leq 1
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)f_{\ell}(x)dx-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}\right|\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{a}_{0}}{\ell N}\,

(2.11)

for all
\ell\in(0;1)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

iii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

w_{\ell}(x)\leq\frac{C}{|x|+1}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla w_{\ell}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2}+1}.

(2.12)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\ell\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. Moreover,

\Big|\frac{1}{(N\ell)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\text{w}_{\ell}(x)dx-\frac{2}{5}\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}\Big|\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{2}}{N\ell}

for all
\ell\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough.

iv)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p)|\leq\frac{C}{|p|^{2}}

(2.13)

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\ell\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough.

Proof.

This has already been proved in
[
3

, based on
[
7

and
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Let us now define the correlation kernel for the generalized Bogoliubov transformation that we will be using. We denote by
G:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
the rescaled function

G(x)=-Nw_{\ell}(Nx)

(2.14)

which has compact support in
B_{\ell}(0)
and which, by (
2.13
), satisfies for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

|\widehat{G}(p)|\leq\frac{C}{|p|^{2}}.

(2.15)

We denote by
\chi_{H}
the characteristic function of
\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|p|\geq\ell^{-\alpha}\}
, for fixed
\alpha>0
, and by
\widecheck{\chi}_{H}
its inverse Fourier transform. Finally, we define
\eta_{H}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

Lemma 2.6

\displaystyle[(\mathcal{N}+1)^{k},A]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}\big((\mathcal{N}+2)^{k}-(\mathcal{N}+1)^{k}\big)+\text{h.c.}

&\geq\frac{1}{2}f_{\delta N}e^{B}e^{A}d\Gamma(-\Delta_{x}+V_{ext}(x)+8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi(x)|^{2}-\varepsilon_{GP})e^{-A}e^{-B}f_{\delta N}\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-C\big(\ell^{\kappa_{1}}+\ell^{-\kappa_{2}}\delta\big)\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}-C\ell^{-\kappa_{2}}.\end{split}

From (
1.6
) and under the assumptions (
1.8
), the operator
h_{GP}=-\Delta+V_{ext}(x)+8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi(x)|^{2}-\varepsilon_{GP}
has the simple eigenvalue 0 at the bottom of its spectrum (with eigenvector
\varphi
) and a positive gap
\lambda_{1}>0
on top of it; see, for example,
[
17
, Theorem XIII.47]
. Applying once more Lemmas
2.3
and
2.6
, we therefore find that

\begin{split}&f_{\delta N}e^{B}\big(\mathcal{G}_{N}^{\text{eff}}-N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)\big)e^{-B}f_{\delta N}\\
&\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}f_{\delta N}e^{B}e^{A}(\mathcal{N}-a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi))e^{-A}e^{-B}f_{\delta N}-C\big(\ell^{\kappa_{1}}+\ell^{-\kappa_{2}}\delta\big)\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}-C\ell^{-\kappa_{2}}\end{split}

(3.5)

With the commutators

\begin{split}\Big[a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi),A\Big]=\;&\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\,\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\varphi(x)b^{*}(\varphi)a_{y}^{*}a_{z}\\
&+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\,\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\varphi(y)b_{x}^{*}a^{*}(\varphi)a_{z}\\
&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\,\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\varphi(z)b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a(\varphi)+\text{h.c.}\\
\Big[a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi),B\Big]=\;&\int dxdy\,\eta_{H}(x;y)\varphi(y)b^{*}_{x}b^{*}(\varphi)+\text{h.c.}\end{split}

and with Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
, we find

-e^{B}e^{A}a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)e^{-A}e^{-B}\geq-a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)-C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1).

Thus, inserting in (
3.5
) and then in (
3.4
), and using again
Lemmas
2.3
and
2.6
, we arrive at

\begin{split}f_{\delta N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}f_{\delta N}&\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}+\widetilde{c}\,(1-C\ell^{\kappa_{1}})\,\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}(1-C\ell^{\kappa_{1}})a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-C\big(\ell^{\kappa_{1}}+\ell^{-\kappa_{2}}\delta\big)\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}-C\ell^{-\kappa_{2}},\end{split}

where the constants
\widetilde{c},C>0
are independent of
\ell\in(0;1)
,
\delta\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

We now set
\delta=\ell^{2\kappa_{2}}
and choose
\ell
sufficiently small, so that the last bound implies

\begin{split}f_{\delta N}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}f_{\delta N}&\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}+c_{1}\,\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}-C.\end{split}

(3.6)

Here, the positive constant
c_{1}>0
is independent of
\ell\in(0;1)
,
\delta\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For the rest of the proof, we fix this choice of
\delta=\ell^{4\kappa_{2}}
and this (sufficiently small) value of
\ell\in(0;1)
so that (
3.6
) holds true. Then, substituting (
3.6
) into (
3.3
), we get

\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}\Gamma(q)+c_{1}\,\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}\Gamma(q)+g_{\delta N}\Gamma(q)\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Gamma(q)g_{\delta N}-C\,\Gamma(q)\frac{\mathcal{V}_{N}}{\delta^{2}N^{2}}\Gamma(q)-C

in the sense of forms in
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi}^{\leq N}
. Notice that we used that
\Gamma(q)a^{*}(\varphi)a(\varphi)\Gamma(q)=0
and
[\Gamma(q),\mathcal{N}]=0
. Since
\Gamma(q)\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}\Gamma(q)=\mathcal{L}_{N}
in the sense of forms in
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi}^{\leq N}
and since
\Gamma(q)_{|\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\bot\varphi}}=\operatorname{id}_{|\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}_{\bot\varphi}}
, the previous bound translates to

\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)f_{\delta N}^{2}+c_{1}\,\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}+g_{\delta N}\mathcal{L}_{N}g_{\delta N}-C\,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{N}}{\delta^{2}N^{2}}-C

(3.7)

in the sense of forms in
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}

Next, we notice that the contribution proportional to
g_{\delta N}\mathcal{L}_{N}g_{\delta N}
in (
3.7
) can be controlled as explained in
[
4
]
, using the results from
[
13
,
16
]
, i.e. (
1.7
). This argument shows that there exists some constant
c_{2}>0
such that for all sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}

\begin{split}g_{\delta N}\mathcal{L}_{N}g_{\delta N}&\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)g_{\delta N}^{2}+\left[\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\geq\delta N/2}:\|\xi\|=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\langle\xi,\mathcal{L}_{N}\xi\rangle-\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)\Big)\right]Ng_{\delta N}^{2}\\
&\geq N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)g_{\delta N}^{2}+c_{2}Ng_{\delta N}^{2}\end{split}

(3.8)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\geq\delta N/2}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}:\chi(\mathcal{N}\geq\delta N/2)\xi=\xi\}
. Hence, plugging (
3.8
) into (
3.7
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq&\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+c_{1}\,\mathcal{N}f_{\delta N}^{2}+c_{2}Ng_{\delta N}^{2}-C\,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{N}}{\delta^{2}N^{2}}-C\geq\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+\widetilde{c}\,\mathcal{N}-C\,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{N}}{\delta^{2}N^{2}}-C\end{split}

in the sense of forms in
\mathcal{F}_{\bot\varphi}^{\leq N}
, where
\widetilde{c}=\min(c_{1},c_{2})>0
.

Finally, to control the error that contains the potential energy
\mathcal{V}_{N}
, we recall the definitions (
2.6
) and a simple computation involving Cauchy-Schwarz shows that

\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}_{N}-CN\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}_{N}-CN

for some constant
C>0
, independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq&\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+\widetilde{c}\,\mathcal{N}-C\,\frac{\mathcal{L}_{N}}{\delta^{2}N^{2}}-C,\end{split}

so that, by choosing
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, we have proved that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{N}\geq&\,N\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+c\,\mathcal{N}-C\end{split}

for some constant
c>0
that is independent of
N
.    ∎

4
Analysis of
\mathcal{G}_{N}

In this section we analyse the operator
\mathcal{G}_{N}=e^{-B}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}e^{B}
, as defined in (
2.21
). To compute the action of the generalized Bogoliubov transform
e^{B}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
, we are going to compare it with the action of a standard Bogoliubov transformation. Interpreting (
2.16
) as the integral kernel of a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we define

\sinh_{\eta_{H}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{H}^{(2k+1)}}{(2k+1)!},\hskip 14.22636pt\cosh_{\eta_{H}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{H}^{(2k)}}{(2k)!}.

In addition, we define the Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\text{p}_{\eta_{H}}
and
\text{r}_{\eta_{H}}
by

\text{p}_{\eta_{H}}=\sinh_{\eta_{H}}-\,\eta_{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{H}^{(2k+1)}}{(2k+1)!},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{r}_{\eta_{H}}=\cosh_{\eta_{H}}-\,\text{id}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{H}^{(2k)}}{(2k)!}.

(4.1)

Using (
2.17
) one obtains for
\ell\in(0;1)
small enough

\|\text{p}_{\eta_{H}}\|,\|\text{r}_{\eta_{H}}\|\leq C\ell^{\alpha},\quad|\text{p}_{\eta_{H}}(x,y)|,|\text{r}_{\eta_{H}}(x,y)|\leq C\ell^{\alpha}\varphi(x)\varphi(y).

(4.2)

The following lemma, whose proof is an adaptation of the translation-invariant case
[
4
, Lemma 3.4]
, shows that, on states with few excitations,
e^{B}
acts approximately like a standard Bogoliubov transformation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
d_{\eta_{H}}(f)
as well as
d_{\eta_{H},x}
be defined as

e^{-B}b(f)e^{B}=b(\cosh_{\eta_{H}}(f))+b^{*}(\sinh_{\eta_{H}}(\overline{f}))+d_{\eta_{H}}(f),

(4.3)

respectively

e^{-B}b_{x}e^{B}=b(\cosh_{\eta_{H},x})+b^{*}(\sinh_{\eta_{H},x})+d_{\eta_{H},x}.

(4.4)

Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{split}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{n/2}d_{\eta_{H}}(f)\xi\|&\leq\frac{C\ell^{\alpha/2}}{N}\|f\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+3)/2}\xi\|,\\
\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{n/2}d_{\eta_{H}}(f)^{*}\xi\|&\leq\frac{C\ell^{\alpha/2}}{N}\|f\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+3)/2}\xi\|\end{split}

(4.5)

and such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have that

\begin{split}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{n/2}d_{\eta_{H},x}\xi\|&\leq\frac{C}{N}\Big[\ell^{\alpha/2}\|a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+2)/2}\xi\|+\|\eta_{H,x}\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+3)/2}\xi\|\Big].\end{split}

(4.6)

Furthermore, if we set
\overline{d}_{\eta_{H},x}=d_{\eta_{H,x}}+(\mathcal{N}/N)b^{*}(\eta_{H,x})
, it holds true that

\begin{split}&\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{n/2}a_{y}\overline{d}_{\eta_{H},x}\xi\|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{N}\Big[\|\eta_{H,x}\|\|\eta_{H,y}\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+2)/2}\xi\|+\ell^{\alpha/2}|\eta_{H}(x;y)|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+2)/2}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+\|\eta_{H,y}\|\|a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+1)/2}\xi\|+\ell^{\alpha/2}\|\eta_{H,x}\|\|a_{y}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+3)/2}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+\ell^{\alpha/2}\|a_{x}a_{y}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+2)/2}\xi\|\Big]\end{split}

(4.7)

and, finally, we have that

\begin{split}&\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{n/2}d_{\eta_{H},x}d_{\eta_{H},y}\xi\|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\Big[\|\eta_{H,x}\|\|\eta_{H,y}\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+6)/2}\xi\|+\ell^{\alpha/2}|\eta_{H}(x;y)|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+4)/2}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 34.14322pt+\ell^{\alpha/2}\|\eta_{H,y}\||\|a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+5)/2)}\xi\|+\ell^{\alpha/2}\|\eta_{H,x}\|\|a_{y}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+5)/2}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 34.14322pt+\ell^{\alpha}\|a_{x}a_{y}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(n+4)/2}\xi\|\Big].\end{split}

(4.8)

From the decomposition (
2.7
), we can write

\mathcal{G}_{N}=\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(0)}+\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(1)}+\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(2)}+\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(3)}+\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(4)},

where, for
j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
, we set

\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(j)}=e^{-B}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(j)}e^{B}.

In the following subsections, we will analyze the main contributions
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(j)}
separately and, in Section
4.6
, we combine these results to conclude Proposition
2.4
.

4.1
Analysis of
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(0)}

From (
2.6
), we recall that

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(0)}&=\big\langle\varphi,\big[-\Delta+V_{ext}+\frac{1}{2}\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\big]\varphi\big\rangle(N-\mathcal{N})\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\varphi,\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\varphi\big\rangle(\mathcal{N}+1)(1-\mathcal{N}/N).\end{split}

(4.9)

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(0)}=&\,\big\langle\varphi,\big[-\Delta+V_{ext}+\frac{1}{2}\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\big]\varphi\big\rangle(N-\mathcal{N})\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\varphi,\big(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\big)\varphi\big\rangle(\mathcal{N}+1)(1-\mathcal{N}/N)+\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(0)},\end{split}

where the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(0)}
satisfies

\pm\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(0)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)

for all
\alpha>0
and
\ell\in(0;1)
.

Proof.

We start with the observation that

\begin{split}e^{-B}\mathcal{N}e^{B}-\mathcal{N}=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\bigg(\int dxdy\,\eta_{H}(x;y)e^{-sB}b^{*}_{x}b^{*}_{y}e^{sB}\bigg)+\text{h.c.}\end{split}

This implies together with Lemma
2.3
and Cauchy-Schwarz that

\pm\big(e^{-B}\mathcal{N}e^{B}-\mathcal{N}\big)\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1).

Similarly, it is straight-forward to prove that

\pm\big(e^{-B}\mathcal{N}^{2}e^{B}-\mathcal{N}^{2}\big)\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}.

If we use these two observations together with the bounds

|\langle\varphi,(-\Delta+V_{ext})\varphi\rangle|\leq\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)\leq C,\;\langle\varphi,(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2})\varphi\big\rangle|\leq\|V\|_{1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq C,

the proposition follows directly from the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(0)}
in Eq. (
4.9
).
∎

4.2
Analysis of
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(1)}

From (
2.6
), we recall that

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(1)}&=\sqrt{N}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{\sqrt{N}}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)+\text{h.c.}\end{split}

(4.10)

For the statement of the next proposition, let us define
h_{N}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

h_{N}=\big(N^{3}(Vw_{\ell})(N.)\ast|\varphi|^{2}\big)\varphi.

(4.11)

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(1)}=&\,\big[\sqrt{N}b(\cosh_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N}))+\sqrt{N}b^{*}(\sinh_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N}))+\emph{h.c.}\big]+\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(1)},\end{split}

where the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(1)}
satisfies

\pm\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(1)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)+C\ell^{-\alpha/2}N^{-1}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}+C\ell^{-1}.

for all
\alpha>0
and
\ell\in(0;1)
.

Proof.

First of all, we notice that a simple application of Cauchy-Schwarz and the fact that
\|(N^{3}V(N\cdot)*|\varphi|^{2})\varphi\|_{2}\leq C
imply that

\pm\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{\sqrt{N}}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)+\text{h.c.}\bigg)\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)+C\ell^{-\alpha/2}N^{-1}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we therefore obtain that

\begin{split}&\pm e^{-B}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{\sqrt{N}}b\left(\left(N^{3}V(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)+\text{h.c.}\bigg)e^{B}\\
&\hskip 128.0374pt\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)+C\ell^{-\alpha/2}N^{-1}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}.\end{split}

This controls the conjugation of the second contribution to
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(1)}
in (
4.10
). To deal with the first term on the right hand side of (
4.10
), we first observe that

\begin{split}\left(N^{3}V(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi=h_{N}+\left(N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\end{split}

and we find with Lemma
2.1

ii)
that

\begin{split}\|N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\|_{\infty}\leq&\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int dy\,(Vf_{\ell})(y)\big|\varphi^{2}(x-y/N)-\varphi^{2}(x)\big|+\frac{C}{\ell N}\\
\leq&\,C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|\|V\|N^{-1}+C\ell^{-1}N^{-1}\leq C\ell^{-1}N^{-1}.\end{split}

Note that
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C
by Appendix
A
. By Cauchy-Schwarz, this implies that

\pm e^{-B}\big(\sqrt{N}b\left(\left(N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N.)*|\varphi|^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\varphi\right)+\text{h.c.}\big)e^{B}\leq C\ell^{-1}

and it only remains to control

e^{-B}\sqrt{N}b(h_{N})e^{B}+\text{h.c.}=\sqrt{N}b(\cosh_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N}))+\sqrt{N}b^{*}(\sinh_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N}))+\sqrt{N}d_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N})+\text{h.c.}

However, by Eq. (
4.5
) from Lemma
4.1
, we know that for all
\xi\in\mathcal{F}^{\leq N}
we have that

|\langle\xi,\sqrt{N}d_{\eta_{H}}(h_{N})\xi\rangle|\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}N^{-1/2}\|h_{N}\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\xi\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)\xi\|\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+1)\xi\rangle,

and hence, collecting the previous estimates, we conclude the proposition.
∎

4.3
Analysis of
\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(2)}

From (
2.6
), we recall that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2)}=\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{ext}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2,V)}
, where

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}&=\int dx\;\nabla_{x}a_{x}^{*}\nabla_{x}a_{x},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{V}_{ext}=\int dx\,V_{ext}(x)a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2,V)}&=\int dx\,\big(N^{3}V(N.)\ast|\varphi|^{2}\big)(x)\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{x}-\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\int dxdy\;N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{y}-\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{y}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\;N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\varphi(x)\Big(b_{x}^{*}b_{y}^{*}+\text{h.c.}\Big).\end{split}

In the following, we will analyze the contributions
e^{-B}\mathcal{K}e^{B}
,
e^{-B}\mathcal{V}_{ext}e^{B}
and
e^{-B}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2,V)}e^{B}
separately. The analysis of the kinetic energy is the most involved so let us first treat the contributions
e^{-B}\mathcal{V}_{ext}e^{B}
and
e^{-B}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2,V)}e^{B}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{split}e^{-B}\mathcal{V}_{ext}e^{B}=\mathcal{V}_{ext}+\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(ext)},\end{split}

where the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(ext)}
satisfies

\pm\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(ext)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1).

for all
\alpha>0
and
\ell\in(0;1)
.

Proof.

We start with the observation that

e^{-B}\mathcal{V}_{ext}e^{B}-\mathcal{V}_{ext}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\bigg(\int dx\,V_{ext}(x)e^{-sB}b^{*}_{x}b^{*}(\eta_{H,x})e^{sB}\bigg)+\text{h.c.}

In the appendix we show that the assumptions (2) in (
1.8
) imply that the external potential
V_{ext}(x)
has at most exponential growth as
|x|\to\infty
while, by (
A.2
), the minimizer
\varphi(x)
has exponential decay as
|x|\to\infty
with arbitrary rate. This implies in particular that
\int dx\,V_{ext}^{2}(x)|\varphi(x)|^{2}\leq C.
As a consequence, Cauchy-Schwarz, Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.2
imply that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\int dx\,V_{ext}(x)\langle\xi,e^{-sB}b^{*}_{x}b^{*}(\eta_{H,x})e^{sB}\xi\rangle\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg(\int dx\,V_{ext}^{2}(x)\|\eta_{H,x}\|^{2}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}e^{sB}\xi\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int dx\,\|a_{x}e^{sB}\xi\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\bigg(\int dx\,V_{ext}^{2}(x)|\varphi(x)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+1)\xi\rangle\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+1)\xi\rangle\end{split}

uniformly in
s\in[0;1]
, which proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{split}&e^{-B}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N}^{(2,V)}e^{B}\\
&=\int dx\big(N^{3}V(N.)\ast|\varphi|^{2}\big)(x)b_{x}^{*}b_{x}+\int dxdy\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)b_{x}^{*}b_{y}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\big(b_{x}^{*}b_{y}^{*}+b_{x}b_{y}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\int dxdy\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\eta_{H}(x;y)\left(\frac{N-\mathcal{N}}{N}\right)\left(\frac{N-\mathcal{N}-1}{N}\right)+\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(2,V)},\end{split}

where the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(2,V)}
satisfies

\pm\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(2,V)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+1).

for all
\alpha>0
,
\ell\in(0;1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We split
e^{-B}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(2,V)}e^{B}=\sum_{j=1}^{5}F_{j}
, setting

&\hskip 42.67912pt\leq C\ell^{(\alpha-3)/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)+C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N})+C\ell^{-5\alpha/2}N^{-1}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}.\end{split}

With very similar arguments, one can show that

\pm\text{G}_{24}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)

so that, in summary, we have

\text{G}_{2}=-\|\nabla_{1}\eta_{H}\|^{2}\,\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{N}\frac{N-\mathcal{N}}{N}+\mathcal{E}_{2}

(4.29)

for an error
\mathcal{E}_{2}
that satisfies

Going back to (

and
4.7

(4.30)

Hence, collecting (

), (
4.29
) and (

) proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

There exists a constant

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(3)}=&\,\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\big(b_{x}^{*}a^{*}_{y}a_{x}+\emph{h.c.}\big)\\

\pm\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{(3)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+1)+CN^{-1/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}+C\ell^{-\alpha}.

for all

,

Proof.

We use the identity

, where

\begin{split}\text{J}_{1}&:=\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)e^{-B}b_{x}^{*}e^{B}a^{*}_{y}a_{x},\\
\text{J}_{2}&:=\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)e^{-B}b_{x}^{*}e^{B}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,e^{-sB}b(\eta_{H,y})b_{x}e^{sB},\\
\text{J}_{3}&:=\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)e^{-B}b_{x}^{*}e^{B}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,e^{-sB}b^{*}_{y}b^{*}(\eta_{H,x})e^{sB}.\end{split}

(4.31)

We start with the analysis of
\text{J}_{1}
. Using (
4.3

\begin{split}\text{J}_{1}&=\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)b^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a_{x}\\

(4.32)

and, by (

Note that we used

for all

).

Going back to (

) and recalling the definition (

\begin{split}\text{J}_{13}&=\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\big[\eta_{H}(y;x)b_{x}+a^{*}_{y}a_{x}b(\eta_{H,x})\big]\\

where

denotes the inverse Fourier transform of the characteristic function of the set

. Using that

, by (

\pm\big(\text{J}_{13}+\sqrt{N}b(h_{N})\big)\leq C\ell^{-\alpha}+C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)

\begin{split}&\pm\bigg(J_{1}-\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)b^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a_{x}+\sqrt{N}b(h_{N})\bigg)\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+1)+C\ell^{-\alpha}N^{-1/2}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}+C\ell^{-\alpha}.\end{split}

(4.33)

Next, we bound

, defined in (

&\hskip 14.22636pt+C\ell^{\alpha}\int dxdy\,N^{5/2}V(N(x-y))\\

, where

Proposition 4.11

Using the identity

we have that

\displaystyle G_{N,\ell}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=e^{-B({\eta_{H}})}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(4)}e^{B}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{N}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}ds\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))e^{-sB}\big[a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{y}a_{x},B\big]e^{sB}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{N}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}ds\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))\eta_{H}(x;y)\left(e^{-sB}b_{x}b_{y}e^{sB}+\text{h.c.}\right)

so that

&\hskip 91.04872pt\times\left(e^{-\tau B}b(\eta_{H,y})b(\eta_{H,x})e^{\tau B}e^{-sB}b_{x}b_{y}e^{sB}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\

.

Finally, we have that

\text{W}_{13}=-\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))\eta_{H}(x;y)^{2}\left(1-\frac{\mathcal{N}}{N}\right)\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{N}+\mathcal{E}_{13}^{(4)}

(4.35)

where, by the bound (
4.7
), it is simple to see that

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,\mathcal{E}_{13}^{(4)}\xi\rangle|&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}ds\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))|\eta_{H}(x;y)|\cdot\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\Big[\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}a^{*}(s\eta_{H,y})a_{x}\xi\|+\ell^{\alpha/2}|\varphi(x)|\|d_{s\eta_{H},y}\xi\|\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}a_{x}\overline{d}_{s\eta_{H},y}\xi\|\Big]\\
&\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+1)\xi\rangle.\end{split}

In summary, the analysis from above proves that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{N}^{(4)}=&\,\mathcal{V}_{N}+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))\eta_{H}(x;y)\big(b_{x}b_{y}+b_{x}^{*}b_{y}^{*}\big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))\eta_{H}(x;y)^{2}(1-\mathcal{N}/N)(1-\mathcal{N}/N-1/N)+\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N,\ell}^{(4)},\end{split}

for an error
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N,\ell}^{(4)}
satisfies

\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N,\ell}^{(4)}\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+1).

Replacing finally
\eta_{H}(x;y)
by
G(x-y)\varphi(x)\varphi(y)=-Nw_{\ell}(N(x-y))\varphi(x)\varphi(y)
in the first two contributions on the right hand side of the last equation for

, we conclude the proposition, using that
\|G\ast\widecheck{\chi}_{H^{c}}\|_{\infty}\leq C\ell^{-\alpha}
and
N^{-1}|\eta_{H}(x;y)|\leq C
, by (
2.19
).
∎

5.1
Preliminary estimates

First of all, with the next lemma we control the growth of the expectation of the external potential.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume (
1.8
) and let
\alpha>\beta>0
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
,

,

and

\Big|\langle\xi,e^{-tA}\mathcal{V}_{ext}e^{tA}\xi\rangle-\langle\xi,\mathcal{V}_{ext}\xi\rangle\Big|\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\big\langle\xi,\big[\mathcal{V}_{ext}+\mathcal{N}+1\big]\xi\big\rangle.

(5.3)

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle[\mathcal{V}_{ext},A]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ [V_{ext}(x)+V_{ext}(y)-V_{ext}(z)]\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.

Using (
2.27
) and the fact that, by (
A.3
),
\|V_{ext}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C
we get

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ V_{ext}(y)\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\langle\xi,b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}\xi\rangle\right|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ |(G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)|\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}a_{y}\xi\|\|a_{z}\xi\|\leq C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|^{2}.\end{split}

(5.4)

Furthermore, recalling the assumption
V_{ext}(x+y)\leq C(V_{ext}(x)+C)(V_{ext}(y)+C)
from (
1.8
), and using (
2.24
) as well as (
2.27
), we find that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ V_{ext}(x)\nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\langle\xi,b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}\xi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\int dxdydz\ V_{ext}(x)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)|\nu_{H}(x;y)|^{2}\|a_{z}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\int dxdydz\ V_{ext}(x)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}a_{y}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\int dxdz\ V_{ext}(x+z)\check{g}_{L}(x)\|a_{z}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathcal{V}_{ext}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|

grows at most exponentially (see Appendix

Next, we need to control the growth of the kinetic and potential energy. To estimate contributions arising from the kinetic energy, we will often need to switch to momentum space. We will use the formal notation

\int dy\,e^{2\pi ip\cdot y}\varphi(y)a_{y}=\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})

(5.5)

where
\hat{\varphi}_{p}(q):=\hat{\varphi}(p-q)
.

We will often encounter operators as in (
5.5
), with
\varphi
the minimizer (or the square of the minimizer) of the Gross-Pitaevskii energy functional. Switching to position space, we can bound

\int dp\ \|\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\xi\|^{2}=\int dx|\varphi(x)|^{2}\|a_{x}\xi\|^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq C\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}

and, as already discussed in (

\int dp\ p^{2}\|\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\xi\|^{2}\leq C\|(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{N})^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

(5.7)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\alpha>4

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nabla_{x}\nu_{H}(x;y)\nabla_{x}\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.],

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.],

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ [(N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N\cdot))*\widecheck{\chi}_{H^{c}}](x-y)\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]

\begin{split}\pm T_{2}&\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\mathcal{K}\,,\qquad\pm S_{1}\leq C(\mathcal{V}_{N}+\mathcal{N}+1)\,,\qquad\pm S_{2}\leq C\mathcal{K}+C\ell^{-\alpha}(\mathcal{N}+1)\,,\end{split}

\pm[\mathcal{K},A]\leq C(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N})+C\ell^{-\alpha}(\mathcal{N}+1).

(5.10)

Proof.

With the commutation relations (
2.1

2.4
)
and integration by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}[\mathcal{K},A]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[-\Delta_{x}b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}-b_{x}^{*}\Delta_{y}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}\Delta_{z}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ [\Delta_{x}\nu_{H}(x;y)+\Delta_{y}\nu_{H}(x;y)]\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nabla_{x}\nu_{H}(x;y)\nabla_{x}\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&=:T_{1}+T_{2}.\end{split}

We used here the identity
-\Delta_{x}\check{g}_{L}(x-z)+\Delta_{z}\check{g}_{L}(x-z)=0
. We rewrite

as

\begin{split}T_{1}&=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ (\Delta G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ (G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\,\Delta\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ (G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\,\nabla\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}\nabla_{y}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&=:T_{11}+T_{12}+T_{13}.\end{split}

Using Young’s inequality and (
2.27
) we obtain

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,T_{12}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\|G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H}\|\int dxdz\ \check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\|a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|^{2}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,T_{13}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\|G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H}\|\int dxdz\ \check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{x}\xi\|\|a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\|\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|.\end{split}

We are left with

. For this term we use the scattering equation (
2.9

\begin{split}T_{11}&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ [(N^{3}(Vf_{\ell})(N\cdot))*\widecheck{\chi}_{H^{c}}](x-y)\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \left[N^{3}\lambda_{\ell}(f_{\ell}(N\cdot)\chi_{\ell})*\widecheck{\chi}_{H}\right](x-y)\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&=:S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.\end{split}

An explicit calculation shows that
|\hat{\chi}_{\ell}(p)|=\ell^{3}|\hat{\chi}_{1}(\ell p)|\leq C\ell|p|^{-2}
. With (
2.15
), we find
\|(f_{\ell}(N.)\chi_{\ell})*\check{\chi}_{H}\|\leq\ell^{1+\alpha/2}
(for
N
large enough). From (
2.10
),
(
2.27
), we obtain

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,S_{3}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}N^{3}\lambda_{\ell}\|(f_{\ell}(N\cdot)\chi_{\ell})*\widecheck{\chi}_{H}\|\int dxdz\ \check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\|a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\ell^{\frac{(\alpha-4)}{2}}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|^{2}.\end{split}

This proves (
5.8
). To show (
5.9
), we observe that, integrating by parts,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ (G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\nabla\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}\nabla_{z}a_{z}+h.c.]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ (G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)[b_{x}^{*}\nabla_{y}a_{y}^{*}\nabla_{z}a_{z}+h.c.].

With
\|G*\check{\chi}_{H}\|=\|\hat{G}\chi_{H}\|\leq\ell^{\alpha/2}
and (

) we get

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,T_{2}\xi\rangle|&=\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdz\ |\check{g}_{L}(x-z)|\|\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{x}\xi\|\|\nabla_{z}a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\quad+\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdz\ |\check{g}_{L}(x-z)|\|\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{x}\xi\|\|\nabla_{z}a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\|\check{g}_{L}\|_{1}\|\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\|\mathcal{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\leq C\ell^{\alpha/2}\|\mathcal{K}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.\end{split}

By (
2.27
), we have

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,S_{1}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\varphi(y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}a_{y}\xi\|\|a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\left(\int dxdydz\ N^{2}V(N(x-y))\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{x}a_{y}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\int dxdydz\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{z}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|.\end{split}

(5.11)

For
S_{2}
we change to momentum space to get, using (

)

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,S_{2}\xi\rangle|&=\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dpdq\,\widehat{(Vf_{\ell})}(p/N)\chi_{H^{c}}(p)g_{L}(q)\langle\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\hat{b}_{q-p}\xi,\hat{a}_{q}\xi\rangle\right|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\int dpdq\,p^{2}\|\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\hat{a}_{q-p}\xi\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{|p|\leq\ell^{-\alpha}}dpdq\,|p|^{-2}\|\hat{a}_{q}\xi\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\ell^{-\alpha/2}\|(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{N})^{1/2}\xi\|\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\xi\|.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of (
5.9
) and thus of (
5.10
). ∎

To bound the commutator of
A
with the interaction energy operator
\mathcal{V}_{N}

\mathcal{K}_{\theta}=\int_{|p|\leq\theta}p^{2}\,\hat{a}_{p}^{*}\hat{a}_{p}

(5.12)

for the kinetic energy of particles having momentum below

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\alpha\geq\beta>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-\beta)
. Then there exists

such that for

sufficiently small we have

[\mathcal{V}_{N},A]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)N^{2}V(N(x-y))[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]+\delta_{V},

(5.13)

where

\pm\delta_{V}\leq C\ell^{\frac{(\alpha-\beta)}{2}}\big[\mathcal{K}_{\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+\mathcal{N}+1\big].

Estimating the term on the r.h.s. of (
5.13
), we conclude that

\pm[\mathcal{V}_{N},A]\leq C\big[\mathcal{K}_{\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+\mathcal{N}+1\big].

(5.14)

Proof.

With the commutation relations (
2.1
), (
2.4
), we find

\begin{split}[\mathcal{V}_{N},A]&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydzdu\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)N^{2}V(N(u-z))[b_{u}^{*}a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}a_{u}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydzdu\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)N^{2}V(N(u-y))[b_{u}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}^{*}a_{u}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydzdu\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)N^{2}V(N(x-u))[b_{x}^{*}a_{u}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{u}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dxdydz\ \nu_{H}(x;y)\check{g}_{L}(x-z)N^{2}V(N(x-y))[b_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}+h.c.]\\
&=:\sum_{j=1}^{4}V_{j}.\end{split}

Switching partially to momentum space and using (
5.6
), (
5.7
), we have that

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,V_{1}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\int dpdqdudz\,N^{2}V(N(u-z))|\widehat{G}(p)|\chi_{H}(p)g_{L}(q)\|a_{u}\hat{a}_{p+q}\hat{a}(\widehat{\varphi_{p}})\xi\|\|a_{z}a_{u}\xi\|\\
&\leq\frac{C\ell^{3\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\int dpdq\,p^{2}\,\|\hat{a}_{p+q}\hat{a}(\widehat{\varphi_{p}})\xi\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int dq\,|g_{L}(q)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{1/2}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\ell^{3(\alpha-\beta)/2}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+\mathcal{N})\xi\rangle.\end{split}

We have
\|\nu_{H,x}\|_{1}\leq\|G*\widecheck{\chi}_{H}\|\cdot\|\varphi\|\leq C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. Thus, we get using (
2.27
)

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,V_{1}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\|\check{g}_{L}\|_{1}\int dzdu\ N^{2}V(N(u-z))\|a_{u}\xi\|\cdot\|a_{z}a_{u}\xi\|\\
&\leq\frac{C\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{N}\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\cdot\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|.\end{split}

Writing
\nu_{H}(x,y)=(G*\check{\chi}_{H})(x-y)\varphi_{0}(y)
and expanding
(G*\check{\chi}_{H})
and also
\check{g}_{L}
in Fourier space we can estimate

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,V_{2}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\int d\sigma dpdudy\,N^{2}V(N(u-y))\,\frac{g_{L}(p)}{p^{2}}\,\|a_{u}a_{y}\hat{a}_{\sigma+p}\xi\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left[\int d\sigma dpdudy\,|\hat{G}(\sigma)\chi_{H}(\sigma)|^{2}\,N^{2}V(N(u-y))g_{L}(p)\,p^{2}\|a_{u}\hat{a}_{p}\xi\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\ell^{(\alpha-\beta)/2}\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{1/2}\xi\|\,\|(\mathcal{K}_{\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}+\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\xi\|,\end{split}

where, to bound the parenthesis in the second line, we divided the
p
-integral in the two domains
|p|<\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}
(where we can use the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}
) and
|p|\geq\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}
, where we use the estimate
\sup_{|p|>\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}p^{2}e^{-p^{2}\ell^{2\beta}}<C
.
The term
V_{3}
can be bounded similarly. This proves (
5.13
). To show (
5.14
), we use
|\nu_{H}(x;y)|\leq CN\varphi(y)
,
(
2.27
)
and (

) to estimate

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,V_{4}\xi\rangle|&\leq C\int dxdydz\ N^{\frac{5}{2}}V(N(x-y))\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\varphi(y)\|a_{x}a_{y}\xi\|\cdot\|a_{z}\xi\|\\
&\leq C\|\check{g}_{L}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\left(\int dxdydz\ N^{3}V(N(x-y))\check{g}_{L}(x-z)\|a_{z}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|\cdot\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\|.\end{split}

∎

Combining the last three lemmas, we obtain a bound for the growth of the Hamilton operator
\mathcal{H}_{N}=\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{ext}+\mathcal{V}_{N}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume (
1.8
). Let
\alpha>\beta>0
and
\alpha\geq 4
. There exists
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0;1]
, all
\ell\in(0;1)
and all
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough

e^{-tA}\mathcal{H}_{N}e^{tA}\leq C\mathcal{H}_{N}+C\ell^{-\alpha}(\mathcal{N}+1).

(5.15)

Proof.

We define
f_{\xi}(s):=\langle\xi,e^{-sA}[\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N}]e^{sA}\xi\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{s}f_{\xi}(s)=\langle\xi,e^{-sA}[\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N},A]e^{sA}\xi\rangle.

Inserting the bounds (
5.10
), (
5.14
) (with
\mathcal{K}_{\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}\leq\mathcal{K}
) and applying (
2.29
), we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{s}f_{\xi}(s)&\leq Cf_{\xi}(s)+C\ell^{-\alpha}\langle\xi,e^{-sA}(\mathcal{N}+1)e^{sA}\xi\rangle\\
&\leq Cf_{\xi}(s)+C\ell^{-\alpha}\langle\xi,e^{-sA}(\mathcal{N}+1)e^{sA}\xi\rangle\leq Cf_{\xi}(s)+C\ell^{-\alpha}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+1)\xi\rangle\end{split}

for all
s\in[0;1]
. With Gronwall’s Lemma and using (
5.3
) to estimate the growth of the external potential, we arrive at (
5.15
).
∎

The estimate (

) is still not optimal (because of the large factor

in front of

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume (
1.8
). Let
0<\beta<\alpha\leq 4\beta
,
\alpha\geq 4
,
\varepsilon\in(0;\alpha-\beta)
. Then there exists a constant

such that for all

all

small enough and all

Proof.

In momentum space, we have

\begin{split}A&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int dudvdw\,\widehat{\nu}_{H}(v;w)g_{L}(u)[\hat{b}_{u+v}^{*}\hat{a}_{w}^{*}\hat{a}_{u}-h.c.]\\

(5.17)

Thus, we find

\begin{split}[\mathcal{K}_{\theta},A]&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{|u|\leq\theta}dudvdw\ \widehat{\nu}_{H}(v;w)g_{L}(u)|u|^{2}[\hat{b}_{u+v}^{*}\hat{a}_{w}^{*}\hat{a}_{u}+h.c.]\\

&=:A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon\in(0;\alpha-\beta)
. For
|u|\leq\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}
and

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,A_{1}\xi\rangle|&\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{|u|\leq\theta,\ |v|\geq\ell^{-\alpha}}dudvdw\ \frac{1}{|v|^{2}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(v+w)|g_{L}(u)|u|^{2}\|\hat{a}_{w}\hat{a}_{u+v}\xi\|\|\hat{a}_{u}\xi\|\\

&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left[\int_{|u|\leq\theta,|v|\geq\ell^{-\alpha}}dudvdw\frac{1}{|v|^{4}}|\hat{\varphi}_{0}(v+w)|g_{L}(u)\,u^{4}\|\hat{a}_{u}\xi\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\\

where, in the last step, we separated the
u
-integral in the second parenthesis between

(where we control
|u|^{4}
with
g_{L}(u)
) and
|u|<\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}

where we divided the

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume (

). Let

Proof.

, with

Switching to Fourier space, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\xi,S_{2}\xi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{|p|\leq\ell^{-\alpha}}dpdq\,g_{L}(q)\|\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\hat{a}_{q-p}\xi\|\|\hat{a}_{q}\xi\|

\displaystyle\leq C\ell^{3\alpha}\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}+\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{|p|\leq\ell^{-\alpha},|q|<\ell^{-\beta-\varepsilon}}dpdq\,\|\hat{a}(\hat{\varphi}_{p})\hat{a}_{q-p}\xi\|\|\hat{a}_{q}\xi\|,

\ell

\begin{split}|\langle\xi,S_{2}\xi\rangle|\leq\;&C\ell^{3\alpha}\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\\

(5.35)

Appendix A
Properties of the Gross-Pitaevskii Functional

In this appendix we collect several well-known results about the Gross-Pitaevskii functional
\mathcal{E}_{GP}
, defined in equation (
1.5
). Let us recall that
\mathcal{E}_{GP}:\mathcal{D}_{GP}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}+V_{ext}(x)|\varphi(x)|^{2}+4\pi a_{0}|\varphi(x)|^{4}\right)dx

with domain

\mathcal{D}_{GP}=\big\{\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ V_{ext}|\varphi|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big\}.

Recall, moreover, assumption
(2)
in Eq. (
1.8
) on the external potential
V_{ext}
. The following was proved in
[
14
, Theorems 2.1, 2.5 & Lemma A.6]
.

Theorem A.1
.

There exists a minimizer
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{GP}
with
\|\varphi\|_{2}=1
such that

\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{GP}\ :\|\psi\|_{2}=1}\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\psi)=\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi).

The minimizer
\varphi
is unique up to a complex phase, which can be chosen so that
\varphi
is strictly positive. Furthermore, the minimizer
\varphi
solves the Gross-Pitaevskii equation

-\Delta\varphi+V_{ext}\varphi+8\pi a_{0}|\varphi|^{2}\varphi=\varepsilon_{GP}\varphi,

(A.1)

with
\mu
given by

\varepsilon_{GP}=\mathcal{E}_{GP}(\varphi)+4\pi a_{0}\|\varphi\|_{4}^{4}.

Moreover,
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and for every
\nu>0
there exists
C_{\nu}
(which only depends on
\nu
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
) such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
it holds true that

|\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\nu}e^{-\nu|x|}.

(A.2)

We denote by
\varphi
in the following the unique, strictly positive minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{GP}
, subject to the contraint
\|\varphi\|_{2}=1
. In addition to Theorem
A.1
, we need to collect a few additional facts about the regularity of
\varphi
. Before we do so, notice that the assumption

V_{ext}(x+y)\leq C(V_{ext}(x)+C)(V_{ext}(y)+C)

implies that
V_{ext}
has at most exponential growth, as
|x|\to\infty
. Indeed, by (
1.8
), we find
R>0
such that
V_{ext}(x)>0
for all
|x|\geq R
. Let
\tilde{C}
be the maximum of
V_{ext}
in the ball of radius
2R
around the origin. For
|x|\geq R
, we pick
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
nR\leq|x|<(n+1)R
and obtain

\displaystyle|V_{ext}(x)|\leq C^{n}(V_{ext}(x/n)+C)^{n}\leq(C(\tilde{C}+C))^{n}\leq(C(\tilde{C}+C))^{|x|/R}.

Hence,
V_{ext}
grows at most exponentially. In particular, by (
A.2
), this implies that

\|V_{ext}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C.

(A.3)

Lemma A.2
.

Let
V_{ext}
satisfy the assumptions in (
1.8
). Then
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and for every
\nu>0
there exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have

|\nabla\varphi(x)|,\ |\Delta\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\nu}e^{-\nu|x|}.

(A.4)

Moreover, if
\widehat{\varphi}
denotes the Fourier transform of
\varphi
, we have for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
that

|\widehat{\varphi}(p)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|p|)^{4}}.

(A.5)

Proof.

By the previous Theorem
A.1
, the Gross-Pitaevskii equation (
A.1
), Eq. (
A.2
) and the fact that
V_{ext},\nabla V_{ext}
grow at most exponentially (by the assumptions (
1.8
) and the previous remark), we obtain the exponential decay of
\Delta\varphi
. Moreover, since
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and local Hölder continuity of
V_{ext}
, we get the local Hölder continuity of
\Delta\varphi
. Elliptic regularity then implies that
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
.

Next, by
[
9
, Theorem 3.9]
, if
u\in C^{2}(B_{2}(y))
solves
\Delta u=f
, then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(y))}\leq C\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}(y))}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}(y))}\big).

Here,
B_{1}(y)
and
B_{2}(y)
denote the open balls of radius one and two, respectively, centered at
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Applying this last bound to
\varphi
and using the exponential decay of
\varphi,\Delta\varphi
implies that also
\nabla\varphi
has exponential decay.

Finally, let us prove the decay estimate (
A.5
). By Theorem (
A.1
), Eq. (
A.1
) and elliptic regularity theory, we conclude
\varphi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and that

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\varphi=-\Delta(\varphi V_{ext})+\varepsilon_{GP}\Delta\varphi+8\pi a_{0}(3\varphi^{2}\Delta\varphi+6\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^{2}).

Since, on the one hand,
\varphi,\nabla\varphi
and
\Delta\varphi
all have exponential decay with arbitrary rate while
V_{ext},\nabla V_{ext},\Delta V_{ext}
grow at most exponentially by assumption (
1.8
), we conclude that
\Delta^{2}\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This implies the estimate (
A.5
) by switching to Fourier space.

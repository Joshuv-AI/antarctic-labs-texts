Abstract
. We consider Rumin’s filtration on the de Rham complex of a Carnot group. Although Pansu pullback by a Sobolev map is filtration preserving, it need not be a chain mapping. Nonetheless, we show that Pansu pullback induces a mapping of the associated spectral sequences. This gives an alternate interpretation of the Pullback Theorem from [ KMX20 ] .

Introduction

This is a continuation of a series of papers on geometric mapping theory

Preliminaries

2.1.
Filtrations on the de Rham complex and its variants

The distributional and rough de Rham complexes

Let
M
be an oriented smooth
n
-manifold.

We denote by
\Omega^{*}M:=\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}}M
and
\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
the de Rham and compactly supported de Rham complexes on

Definition 2.1
.

The
rough de Rham complex
is the cochain complex

(\hat{\Omega}^{*}M,\hat{d})
where

\hat{\Omega}^{j}M:=(\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{*}\,,\quad(\hat{d}T)(\eta):=T((-1)^{j+1}d\eta)

for all
T\in\hat{\Omega}^{j}M=(\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{*}
,
\eta\in\Omega^{n-(j+1)}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M

to denote the (algebraic) dual space of the vector space

, at the risk of confusion with the degree index on the complex.)
Thus, up to reindexing and the sign convention,
\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
is the algebraic dual of the compactly supported de Rham complex.

We will often denote the differential
\hat{d}
generically as
d
, although this has the potential to be confused with the exterior derivative.

Definition 2.2
.

The
distributional de Rham complex

(\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M,d^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})
is the subcomplex of
\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
where

\Omega^{j}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M:=\{T\in\hat{\Omega}^{j}M=(\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{*}\mid T\;\text{continuous}\}\,.

Here continuity refers to the standard distributional type condition, i.e.

is continuous iff for every compact subset
K\subset M
there exists
j
and
C
such that

|T(\eta)|\leq C\|\eta\|_{C^{j}}

for every
\eta\in\Omega^{n-k}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
supported in
K
. As with the rough de Rham complex, we will often denote
d^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
generically by
d
.

Note that
\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M
is the same thing as the chain complex of de Rham currents, modulo the reindexing which converts the chain complex to a cochain complex
[
dR84
]
.

With the above definitions the embedding

\Phi:\Omega^{*}M\hookrightarrow\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M

given by

(2.3)

\Phi(\omega)(\eta)=\int_{M}\omega\wedge\eta

for all
\omega\in\Omega^{k}M
,
\eta\in\Omega^{n-k}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
is a chain mapping.

Filtrations

Let
(G,\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j}V_{j})
be a Carnot group of dimension
n
and homogeneous dimension
\nu
. Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a graded basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the dual basis; for
J\subset I
we let
\theta_{J}=\wedge_{i\in J}\theta_{i}
where the wedge factors are taken in increasing order with respect to a fixed linear order on the index set
I
. We define the weight of an element
i\in I
to be
j
if
X_{i}\in V_{j}
, and the weight of a subset
J\subset I
to be the sum
\operatorname{wt}(J):=\sum_{i\in J}\operatorname{wt}(i)
. Carnot dilation
\{\delta_{r}\}_{r>0}
acts diagonalizably on the exterior algebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and we have a decomposition

\Lambda^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\oplus_{0\leq p\leq\nu}\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

where
\delta_{r}^{*}\alpha=r^{p}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
r>0
; let
\Lambda^{*,\geq p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}:=\oplus_{\bar{p}\geq p}\Lambda^{*,\bar{p}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. In terms of the graded basis, we have
\alpha\in\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
iff
\alpha=\sum_{\operatorname{wt}J=p}a_{J}\theta_{J}
. Carnot dilation commutes with wedging and exterior differentiation, and therefore

(2.4)

\Lambda^{*,p_{1}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\wedge\Lambda^{*,p_{2}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\subset\Lambda^{*,p_{1}+p_{2}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,,\quad d(\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*})\subset\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,.

Using the left invariant trivialization of the exterior bundle
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}G
, we let
\Omega^{*,p}G
and
\Omega^{*,\geq p}G
be the differential forms taking values in
\Lambda^{*,p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\Lambda^{*,\geq p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, respectively. Thus if
\omega\in\Omega^{*}G
, then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\omega\in\Omega^{*,p}G\;\iff\;\omega=\sum_{\operatorname{wt}J=p}a_{J}\theta_{J}\quad\text{for some}\quad a_{J}\in C^{\infty}G\,.

\displaystyle\omega\in\Omega^{*,\geq p}G\;\iff\;\omega=\sum_{\operatorname{wt}J\geq p}a_{J}\theta_{J}\quad\text{for some}\quad a_{J}\in C^{\infty}G\,.

Given an open subset
M\subset G
, we denote by
\Omega^{*,p}M
,
\Omega^{*,\geq p}M
, etc the corresponding objects on
M
.
Using (
2.4
) it is easy to check that the sequence
F^{p}\Omega^{*}M:=\Omega^{*,\geq p}M
is a descending filtration of
\Omega^{*}M
by graded differential ideals, and that this is the same as the filtration defined by Rumin
[
Rum99
]
. We define a decreasing filtration on
\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
by

F^{p}\hat{\Omega}^{j}M:=(F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{\perp}

where
F^{p}\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M:=\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M\cap F^{p}\Omega^{*}M
and
\perp
denotes the annihilator; thus
F^{p}\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
consists of the
T\in\hat{\Omega}^{j}M
such that

T(\eta)=0
for all
\eta\in F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
. We filter
\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M\subset\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
by restricting the filtration on
\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The embedding
\Phi:\Omega^{*}M\hookrightarrow\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M
from (
2.3
) is filtration preserving.

Proof.

Suppose

\alpha\in\Omega^{j}M
,
\beta\in\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
, with

\alpha=\sum_{J_{1}}a_{J_{1}}\theta_{J_{1}}
and
\beta=\sum_{J_{2}}b_{J_{2}}\theta_{J_{2}}
.
If
\alpha\in F^{p}\Omega^{*}M
,
\beta\in F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
then by (

) we have
\alpha\wedge\beta\equiv 0
since
\theta_{J_{1}}\wedge\theta_{J_{2}}=0
whenever
\operatorname{wt}(J_{1})+\operatorname{wt}(J_{2})>\nu
. Hence
\Phi(F^{p}\Omega^{j}M)\subset(F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{\perp}=F^{p}\Omega^{*}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}M
.

On the other hand, if
\alpha\not\in F^{p}\Omega^{j}M
, then
a_{J}(x)\neq 0
for some

with
\operatorname{wt}J<p
,

. Then taking

where

is a nonnegative function supported near

\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\beta=\pm\int_{M}a_{J}b\theta_{I}\neq 0

Hence
\Phi(\alpha)\not\in(F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{\perp}

∎

It will be useful to reinterpret the above structures slightly, as follows.

Given a chain complex
(C_{*},\partial)
with an increasing filtration
\{F_{p}C_{*}\}
by subcomplexes, the dual chain complex
(C^{*},\partial^{*})
inherits a dual descending filtration
\{F^{p}C^{*}:=(F_{p-1}C_{*})^{\perp}\}
by subcomplexes. This makes sense for complexes of modules over an arbitrary commutative ring, but we will always work with complexes of vector spaces over
\mathbb{R}
.

In our setting we let
(C_{*},\partial)
be the chain complex where
C_{j}:=\Omega^{n-j}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
and the boundary operator
\partial_{j}:C_{j}\rightarrow C_{j-1}
is given by
\partial_{j}\eta=(-1)^{j}d
, where
d
denotes the exterior derivative, and we define an increasing filtration on
C_{*}
by
F_{p}C_{*}:=\Omega^{*,\geq\nu-p}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M
. Then
(\hat{\Omega}^{*},\hat{d})
is the cochain complex dual to the chain complex
(C_{*},\partial)
, and the dual filtration is

F^{p}C^{*}:=(F_{p-1}C_{*})^{\perp}=(F^{\nu-(p-1)}\Omega^{*}_{C^{\infty}_{c}}M)^{\perp}

which is precisely
F^{p}\hat{\Omega}^{*}M
as defined before.

.

A
(cohomological) spectral sequence
is a sequence
\{(E_{r},d_{r})\}_{r\geq r_{0}}
of cochain complexes, together with isomorphisms
E_{r+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{*}(E_{r},d_{r})

between spectral sequences
\{(E_{r},d_{r})\}_{r\geq r_{0}}
and
\{(E^{\prime}_{r},d^{\prime}_{r})\}_{r\geq r_{0}}
is a collection of chain mappings
\{\phi_{r}:(E_{r},d_{r})\rightarrow(E^{\prime}_{r},d^{\prime}_{r})\}_{r\geq r_{0}}
which are compatible with taking cohomology, i.e. the following diagram commutes:

(2.8)

\begin{diagram}

for all
r\geq r_{0}
, and
H(\phi_{r})
is the map on cohomology induced by
\phi_{r}
.

Let
(C^{*},d)
be a cochain complex and
\{F^{p}C^{*}\}

be a descending filtration by subcomplexes. The
standard cohomological spectral sequence
\{(E^{*,*}_{r},d_{r})\}_{r\geq 0}
is defined as follows.
Let

\displaystyle Z^{p,q}_{r}

\displaystyle:=F^{p}C^{p+q}\cap d^{-1}(F^{p+r}C^{p+q+1})\,,\quad r\geq 0

\displaystyle B^{p,q}_{r}

\displaystyle:=d(F^{p-r}C^{p+q-1})\cap F^{p}C^{p+q}=d(Z^{p-r,q+r-1}_{r})\,,\quad r\geq 0\quad

(2.9)

\displaystyle D^{p,q}_{0}

\displaystyle:=F^{p+1}C^{p+q}\,,\quad D^{p,q}_{r}:=Z^{p+1,q-1}_{r-1}+B^{p,q}_{r-1}\,,\;r\geq 1

(2.10)

\displaystyle E^{p,q}_{0}

\displaystyle:=F^{p}C^{p+q}/F^{p+1}C^{p+q}=Z^{p,q}_{0}/D^{p,q}_{0}

(2.11)

\displaystyle E^{p,q}_{r}

\displaystyle:=\frac{Z^{p,q}_{r}}{Z^{p+1,q-1}_{r-1}+B^{p,q}_{r-1}}=Z^{p,q}_{r}/D^{p,q}_{r}\,,\quad r\geq 1

and let the differential
d_{r}:E^{p,q}_{r}\longrightarrow E^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}
be the map of bidegree
(r,-r+1)
induced by

; finally, we let
\eta_{r}:E^{p,q}_{r+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{p,q}(E^{*,*}_{r},d_{r})
be the isomorphism induced by

Z^{p,q}_{r+1}\hookrightarrow Z^{p,q}_{r}\rightarrow Z^{p,q}_{r}/D^{p,q}_{r}=E^{p,q}_{r}
.

Likewise, given a chain complex
(C_{*},\partial)
with an increasing filtration
\{F_{p}C_{*}\}
by subcomplexes, there is a homological spectral sequence

\displaystyle Z^{r}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=F_{p}C_{p+q}\cap\partial^{-1}(F_{p-r}C_{p+q-1})\,,\quad r\geq 0

\displaystyle B^{r}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=\partial(F_{p+r}C_{p+q+1})\cap F_{p}C_{p+q}=\partial(Z^{r}_{p+r,q-r+1})\,,\quad r\geq 0\quad

(2.12)

\displaystyle D^{0}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=F_{p-1}C^{p+q}\,,\quad D^{r}_{p,q}:=Z^{r-1}_{p-1,q+1}+B^{r-1}_{p,q}\,,\;r\geq 1

(2.13)

\displaystyle E^{0}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=F_{p}C_{p+q}/F_{p-1}C_{p+q}

(2.14)

\displaystyle E^{r}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=Z^{r}_{p,q}/(Z^{r-1}_{p-1,q+1}+B^{r-1}_{p,q})=Z^{r}_{p,q}/D^{r}_{p,q}\,,\quad r\geq 1

and the mapping
\partial_{r}:E^{r}_{p,q}\rightarrow E^{r}_{p-r,q+r-1}
is induced by the boundary operator
\partial
.

Lemma 2.15

(Duality of spectral sequences)
.

Let
(C_{*},\partial)
be a chain complex with an increasing filtration
F_{p}C_{*}
, and let
(C^{*},d:=\partial^{*})
be the dual cochain complex, with the dual filtration
F^{p}C^{*}:=(F_{p-1}C_{*})^{\perp}
. Then:

(1)

The restriction of the duality pairing

to
Z^{p,q}_{r}\times Z^{r}_{p,q}\subset C^{*}\times C_{*}
descends to a well-defined pairing

(2.16)

for all

.

(2)

The pairing (

, and

Kostka numbers and Fourier duality
Michael Finkelberg, Alexander Postnikov, Vadim Schechtman

Abstract
. We relate the Fourier transform of perverse sheaves smooth along the coordinate hyperplane configuration in a complex vector space to the Deligne-Lusztig duality of unipotent representations of a general linear group over a finite field. A similar relation is established for arbitrary finite Coxeter groups.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

As soon as perverse sheaves were discovered about 40 years ago, there appeared a problem of concrete
linear algebraic description of abelian categories of perverse sheaves smooth along some particular
stratifications (“How to glue perverse sheaves”). One important case is a complex affine space
stratified by a hyperplane arrangement. In case such an arrangement is
real
, a solution of
this problem was suggested in
[
KS
]
(
hyperbolic sheaves
).

In the present note we study a most trivial example of a hyperplane arrangement: namely the one of
coordinate hyperplanes. In this case another description of the category of perverse sheaves was
proposed in
[
GGM
]
(
iterated vanishing/nearby cycles
). It turns out that already in this
simplest example, the interplay between the two different descriptions of the same abelian category
leads to some nontrivial combinatorial consequences.

Namely, we go even further along the route of simplification and consider semisimple perverse sheaves
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with trivial monodromy, however with coefficients not in the category of vector spaces,
but in the category
{\mathcal{C}}
of representations of the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
(over a field
{\mathsf{k}}
of
characteristic 0). We consider a perverse sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}
whose generic stalk is the irreducible

S_{n+1}
-module
V_{\mu}
(for a partition
\mu
of
n+1
), and whose hyperbolic stalks are
equal to induction of
V_{\mu}
to
S_{n+1}
from appropriate parabolic subgroups. When

\mu=(n+1)
, so that
V_{(n+1)}
is trivial, we call the multiplicities of simple perverse
sheaves in
{\mathcal{F}}_{(n+1)}

small Kostka numbers

\kappa_{\lambda,I}
.
According to Theorem
3.5.1
,
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
is equal to the number of standard Young tableaux
of shape
\lambda
with descent set
I
.
We also discuss an extension of (small)
Kostka numbers to an arbitrary finite Coxeter group
W

related to
[
So2
]
.

The
hyperbolic calculus
developed in
[
FKS
]
allows to compute the Fourier-Sato transform
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}
. Namely, we have
\operatorname{FT}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}\cong{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu^{t}}
(transposed partition). Also, the
hyperbolic calculus provides a
master complex

{\mathcal{M}}as^{\bullet}_{\mu}
computing the vanishing
cycles
\Phi_{0}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}
at the origin. It turns out that
\Phi_{0}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}\cong V_{\mu^{t}}
,
and the master complex goes back at least to
[
So1
]
.

For a prime power
q
, there is a Benson-Curtis isomorphism

{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n+1}
with the Iwahori-Hecke algebra. Thus
{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent to the category

{\mathcal{C}}_{q}
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n+1}
-modules. Under this equivalence, the master complex
{\mathcal{M}}as^{\bullet}_{\mu}

corresponds to a complex
{}_{q}{\mathcal{M}}as^{\bullet}_{\mu}
going back at least to
[
Kat
]
. According to

loc.cit.
, a similar complex
exists for the Hecke algebra of an arbitrary finite Coxeter group.

Furthermore,
{\mathcal{C}}_{q}
is equivalent to the category of unipotent representations of

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm GL}}}(n+1,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, and under this equivalence,
{}_{q}{\mathcal{M}}as^{\bullet}_{\mu}
corresponds to the complex
of
[
DL
]
defining the Alvis-Curtis-Deligne-Lusztig-Kawanaka duality
[
A
,
C
,
Kaw
,
DL
]
.

2.
Hyperbolic sheaves over coordinate arrangements

If
(X,\leq)
is a poset, we call a
bisheaf
on
X
with values in a category
{\mathcal{C}}

a couple
B=(\gamma,\delta)
of functors

\gamma\colon X\to{\mathcal{C}},\ \delta\colon X^{\operatorname{op}}\to{\mathcal{C}}

such that for all
x\in X,\ \gamma(x)=\delta(x)=:B(x)
. Thus, for all

x\leq y
we have two arrows

\gamma_{x,y}\colon B(x)\leftrightarrows B(y)\ :\delta_{y,x}.

2.1.
Real coordinate arrangement

Let
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
;

{\mathcal{H}}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\},

where
H_{i}\subset V
is given by the equation
x_{i}=0
.

{\mathcal{S}}
is the corresponding stratification of
V
.

The strata of
{\mathcal{S}}
are in bijection with sequences

s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n},

where
C_{s}
is given by
n
equalities and inequalities:

x_{i}=0
if
s_{i}=0
, and
s_{i}x_{i}>0
if
s_{i}=\pm 1
. So all the strata are cells, and in what
follows we will refer to the strata as cells.

To put it differently, the cells are numbered by couples
(I,\epsilon)
where

I
is a subset of
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{j})\in\{1,-1\}^{[n]\smallsetminus I}

is a collection of signs on the complement. We denote the corresponding cell by
C_{(I,\epsilon)}
.

We have
C_{(I,\epsilon)}\leq C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}
(i.e.
C_{(I,\epsilon)}
lies in the closure of

C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}
) if and only if

(a)
I\supset I^{\prime}
, whence
\overline{I}:=[n]\smallsetminus I\subset\overline{I^{\prime}}
,

and

(b)
\epsilon_{j}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{j}
for all
j\in\overline{I}
.

In other words,
(I,\epsilon)
is obtained from
(I,\epsilon^{\prime})
by replacing some elements

\epsilon^{\prime}_{j}
in the sequence
\epsilon^{\prime}
by
0
’s.

Consider two cells
C_{s}
and
C^{\prime}=C_{s^{\prime}}
where the sequence
s^{\prime}
is obtained
from
s
by replacing some element
s_{i}=\pm 1
by
s^{\prime}_{i}=-s_{i}
. We call such two cells

neighbours
. Let
C^{\prime\prime}=C_{s^{\prime\prime}}
where
s^{\prime\prime}
is obtained
from
s
by replacing the element
s_{i}
by
0
. This
C^{\prime\prime}
is called
the wall
between

C
and
C^{\prime\prime}
.

Following Tits, let us call
a gallery
a sequence

C_{0},\ldots,C_{r}

where all
C_{i},C_{i+1}
are neighbours. We call
C_{0}
and
C_{r}

faraway neighbours
.

Each cell
C
of dimension
p
has
2^{p}
faraway neighbours (including itself), whose union
is dense in
L(C)
:
the flat
of
C
, the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space spanned by
C
.

We denote by
C_{I}
the positive cell
C_{(I,\epsilon)}
with all
\epsilon_{j}=1
.
So, there are
2^{n}
positive cells.

2.2.
Complex coordinate arrangement

Let

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}=\{H_{1}^{\mathbb{C}},\ldots,H_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\}

be the complex coordinate arrangement in
V_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and let

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
be the corresponding stratification of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Its strata are
S_{I},I\subset[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where

S_{I}=\{x\in V_{\mathbb{C}}|x_{i}=0\text{\ for}\ i\in I,x_{i}\neq 0\text{\ for}\ i\notin I\}.

We write
S\leq S^{\prime}
iff
S\subset\overline{S^{\prime}}
.

In the complex case
S_{I}\leq S_{J}
iff
J\subset I
.

A complex stratum
S_{I}
with
|I|=p
is of real codimension
2p
, and contains

2^{n-p}
real cells
C_{(I,\epsilon)}
.

2.3.
GGM sheaves

We have two linear algebra descriptions of the category

{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})={\operatorname{Perv}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

The first one is due to
[
GGM
]
. Namely,
{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is equivalent
to a category
{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}
whose objects are certain
bisheaves
\Phi
on the poset
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}
with values in
{\operatorname{Vect}}
, i.e.

— collections of
2^{n}
spaces

\Phi(I)=\Phi(S_{I})\in{\operatorname{Vect}},\ I\subset[n],

— and
canonical
maps

u_{IJ}\colon\Phi(I)\to\Phi(J),\ I\subset J,

and
variation
maps

v_{JI}\colon\Phi(J)\to\Phi(I),\ I\subset J,

satisfying the transitivity for triples
I\subset J\subset K
.

They must satisfy the following further conditions.

For all
I,i\in I
denote

v_{i}=v_{I,I\smallsetminus\{i\}}\colon\Phi(I)\leftrightarrows\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i\})\ :u_{I\smallsetminus\{i\},I}=u_{i}.

Then the
mixed commutativity
condition:
uv=vu
must hold, that is, for any

i,j\in I,i\neq j
, the square

\begin{CD}\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i\})@>{v_{j}}>{}>\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i,j\})\\
@V{u_{i}}V{}V@V{u_{i}}V{}V\\
\Phi(I)@>{v_{j}}>{}>\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{j\})\end{CD}

must commute.

Also, the
invertibility
condition of
1+vu
must hold, that is for all

i\in I\subset[n]
, the
monodromy
operator

T_{i}:=1+v_{i}u_{i}\colon\Phi_{I}\to\Phi_{I}

must be invertible.

Note that
1+v_{i}u_{i}
is invertible if and only if
1+u_{i}v_{i}
is invertible.

This definition makes sense if we replace the coefficient category
{\operatorname{Vect}}
by an
arbitrary
additive
category
{\mathcal{C}}
; let us denote the corresponding GGM category

{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
.

2.4.
Fourier-Sato transform

The
Fourier-Sato transform

{\operatorname{Perv}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Perv}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
induces
the same named auto-equivalence
{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}
. More generally, for an arbitrary additive
category
{\mathcal{C}}
, we have an auto-equivalence

\operatorname{FT}\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})

that sends an object
\Phi=(\Phi(I),u,v)
to
\operatorname{FT}(\Phi)=(\Phi^{\prime}(I):=\Phi(\overline{I}),u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
Here we set for
i\not\in I\subset[n]
,

u^{\prime}_{i}:=-v_{i}\colon\Phi(\overline{I})\to\Phi(\overline{I}\smallsetminus\{i\}),\ \operatorname{and}\ v^{\prime}_{i}:=u_{i}(1+v_{i}u_{i})^{-1}\colon\Phi(\overline{I}\smallsetminus\{i\})\to\Phi(\overline{I}),

cf.
[
BK
, Proposition 4.5]
and
[
Mal
, (II.2.1), (II.3.2)]
.

2.5.
Hyperbolic sheaves

The second, “Möbius dual”, linear algebraic description of
{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})

is a version of
[
KS
]
. The category
{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})

is equivalent to a category
{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
of
hyperbolic sheaves
.

An object of
{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
is a
bisheaf
E
with values in
{\operatorname{Vect}}
on the poset of real strata
{\mathcal{S}}
, i.e.

— a collection of
3^{n}
spaces
E(C)=E(I,\epsilon)\in{\operatorname{Vect}},\ C\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

— maps
\gamma_{CC^{\prime}}\colon E(C)\to E(C^{\prime}),\ \delta_{C^{\prime}C}\colon E(C)\to E(C^{\prime}),\ C\leq C^{\prime}
.

These maps must satisfy the following conditions:

Idempotence
: For
C\leq C^{\prime}
,

\gamma_{CC^{\prime}}\delta_{C^{\prime}C}={\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Id}}}_{E(C^{\prime})}.

Let
C,C^{\prime}
be any pair of cells. There exists a cell
C^{\prime\prime}\leq C
and
C^{\prime\prime}\leq C
, for example

C^{\prime\prime}=\{0\}
. Define a map

\phi_{CC^{\prime}}=\gamma_{C^{\prime\prime}C}\delta_{CC^{\prime\prime}}.

Due to the idempotence axiom this map does not depend on a choice of
C^{\prime\prime}
.

Invertibility
: If
\dim C=\dim C^{\prime}=p
, and
C,C^{\prime}
belong to the same linear subspace

L\subset V
of dimension
p
then
\phi_{CC^{\prime}}
is invertible.

The invertibility axiom is equivalent to a seemingly weaker requirement:

Weak invertibility
: The map
\phi_{CC^{\prime}}=\gamma_{C^{\prime\prime}C}\delta_{CC^{\prime\prime}}
is an isomorphism for all
neighbours.

Mixed commutativity
:
\gamma\delta=\delta\gamma
. That is,
for
C\leq D,\ C^{\prime}\leq D^{\prime},\ C\leq C^{\prime},\ D\leq D^{\prime}
, the square

\begin{CD}E(C^{\prime})@>{\delta}>{}>E(D^{\prime})\\
@V{\gamma}V{}V@V{\gamma}V{}V\\
E(C)@>{\delta}>{}>E(D)\\
\end{CD}

must commute.

This definition makes sense if we replace the coefficient category
{\operatorname{Vect}}
by an
arbitrary category
{\mathcal{C}}
; let us denote the corresponding category

{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
.

2.6.
Morita equivalence

We have equivalences of categories

L_{h}\colon{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
(“hyperbolic, or big localization”) and

L_{g}\colon{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}
(“vanishing cycles, or small localization”),
so there should exist equivalences of quiver categories

P\colon{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n},\ Q\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}.

Below we will construct equivalences

(2.6.1)

P\colon{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}}),\ Q\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})

for an arbitrary additive category
{\mathcal{C}}
.

2.7.
Example
n=1

The construction below is a version of
[
KS
, 9.A]
.

An object of
{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}})
is a triple
E=(E_{0},E_{\pm})
of objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
, and
4
maps

\gamma_{\pm}\colon E_{0}\to E_{\pm},\ \delta_{\pm}\colon E_{\pm}\to E_{0}

such that
\gamma_{\pm}\delta_{\mp}
are isomorphisms.

An object of
{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}})
is a couple
\Phi,\Psi\in({\mathcal{C}})
and
2
maps

v\colon\Phi\leftrightarrows\Psi\ :u

such that
t:=1+vu
is an isomorphism.

Let
E\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}})
; we set
\Psi:=E_{+},\ \Phi:={\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Ker}}}\gamma_{-}
, and
v
is the composition

\Phi\hookrightarrow E_{0}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{+}}\Psi,

while
u
is the composition

\Psi\xrightarrow{\delta_{+}}E_{0}\xrightarrow{1+\delta_{-}\gamma_{-}}\Phi.

This way we get an object

P(E)=(\Phi,\Psi,v,u)\in{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}}).

In the opposite direction, given
G=(\Phi,\Psi,v,u)\in{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}})
with

t:=1+vu
, we define

Q(G)=(E_{0},E_{\pm},\gamma_{\pm},\delta_{\pm})\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{1}({\mathcal{C}})

by

E_{+}=E_{-}=\Psi;\ E_{0}=\Phi\oplus\Psi;

where
\delta_{-}
(resp.
\gamma_{-}
) is given by the obvious inclusion (resp., projection),
whereas

\gamma_{+}(x,y)=-v(x)+y,

and

\delta_{+}(y)=(u(y),t(y)).

2.8.
General
n

We note that

{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\operatorname{GGM}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{C}})),\ \text{and}\ {\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{1}({\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{C}})).

Whence we define
P_{n},Q_{n}
by induction:
P_{n}
being given as the composition

(2.8.1)

{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{1}({\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{C}}))\xrightarrow{P_{1}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{C}}))\\
\xrightarrow{P_{n-1}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{1}({\operatorname{GGM}}_{n-1}({\mathcal{C}}))\cong{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}}),

and similarly for
Q_{n}
.

2.9.
Vanishing cycles at the origin: the Master complex

We will give an explicit description of the equivalence
P\colon{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}

via vanishing cycles. Given
E=(E(C),\gamma,\delta)\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
,
we are going to describe the corresponding GGM-sheaf
P(E)=(\Phi(I),u,v)
.

First let us describe its vanishing cycles at the origin,
\Phi_{0}:=\Phi([n])
.
Geometrically, let
M\in{\mathcal{M}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the perverse sheaf corresponding to
E
;
the sheaf of vanishing cycles
\Phi_{f}(M)
corresponding to a function

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
is a scyscraper concentrated at
0
, and
\Phi_{0}
is its stalk at
0
:

\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{f}(M)_{0}.

Algebraically the procedure of computing
\Phi_{f}(M)
is described in
[
FKS
]
, for an arbitrary
real hyperplane arrangement. For the coordinate arrangement it reduces to the following.

We consider the positive cells
C_{I}
, and denote
E(I):=E(C_{I})
. Let

{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
denote the subset of positive cells.

Let

\operatorname{Sub}_{n}^{i}=\{I\subset[n]\ :\ |I|=n-i\}\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{+}:=\{C\in{\mathcal{S}}^{+}|\ \dim C=i\}.

We consider a complex

(2.9.1)

\Phi^{\bullet}(E)\colon 0\to E([n])\to\oplus_{I\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}^{+}}E(I)\to\ldots\to E(\emptyset)\to 0,

concentrated in degrees
0,\ldots,n
.

The differentials are
\pm\gamma
. More precisely, if
J=I\sqcup j
, then the
JI
matrix coefficient
of the differential is
(-1)^{s}\gamma_{C(J)C(I)}
, where
s=|\{i\in I\ :\ i<j\}|
.

Proposition 2.9.1
.

The complex
\Phi^{\bullet}(E)
is acyclic in positive degrees, and

\Phi_{0}=H^{0}(\Phi^{\bullet}(E)).

This is a particular case of
[
FKS
, Theorem 2.3]
.
This complex
\Phi^{\bullet}(E)
will be called the
Master complex of vanishing cycles

for a hyperbolic sheaf
E\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
.

2.10.
Explicit description of
P

Similarly, for an arbitrary
I\subset[n]
,
|I|=p
, let

f_{I}(x)=\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}
. The sheaf of vanishing cycles

\Phi_{f_{I}}(M)

is supported on the closure of the stratum
\overline{S(I)}
, and
\Phi(I)
is the stalk
of
\Phi_{f_{I}}(M)
at
C_{I}
. It is computed via an exact sequence

0\to\Phi(I)\to E(I)\to\oplus_{I^{\prime}\subset I,|I|=p-1}E(I^{\prime})\to\ldots\to E(\emptyset)\to 0,

where the differentials are
\pm\gamma
.

In particular,
\Phi(I)\subset E(I),\ \Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i\})\subset E(I\smallsetminus\{i\})
,
and the variation map
v_{i}\colon\Phi(I)\to\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i\})
(notation of §
2.3
)
is induced by

\gamma_{C_{I}C_{I\smallsetminus\{i\}}}\colon E(I)\to E(I\smallsetminus\{i\}).

Finally, the canonical map
u_{i}\colon\Phi(I\smallsetminus\{i\})\to\Phi(I)
is induced by the
composition

E(I\smallsetminus\{i\})\xrightarrow{\delta_{C_{I\smallsetminus\{i\}}C_{I}}}E(I)\xrightarrow{1+\delta_{-}\gamma_{-}}E(I),

where
E(I)=E(C_{I})=E(C_{I,\epsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma_{-}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E(C_{I\smallsetminus\{i\},\epsilon^{\prime}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{-}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E(C_{I,\epsilon})=E(C_{I})=E_{I}
, and
\epsilon^{\prime}
consists of all
positive signs except for one negative sign of
i
.

Remark 2.10.1
.

We will not give an explicit description of the equivalence
Q\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
;
we just mention that for
{\mathcal{G}}=(\Phi(I),u,v)
, the
positive
hyperbolic stalks of
the corresponding
E=Q({\mathcal{G}})=(E(C),\gamma,\delta)\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}
are given by

E(I)=\oplus_{J\subset I}\Phi(J)

(“Takeuchi formula”, cf.
[
KS
, 4.C.(1)]
and
[
T
]
).

2.11.
Amonodromic semisimple sheaves

Fix a semisimple abelian category
{\mathcal{C}}
. The category of perverse sheaves

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})={\operatorname{Perv}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
with coefficients in
{\mathcal{C}}
contains a full semisimple
subcategory
{\operatorname{Perv}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
formed by the direct sums of constant sheaves along the
strata closures. Under the equivalences
{\operatorname{Perv}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
and

{\operatorname{Perv}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
the subcategory

{\operatorname{Perv}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset{\operatorname{Perv}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{C}})
corresponds
to the full semisimple subcategories
{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\subset{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
and

{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\subset{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
.

Namely,
{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\subset{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
consists of all GGM sheaves
(\Phi(I),u,v)
as
in §
2.3
with all
u_{IJ}=v_{JI}=0
.

The description of
{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\subset{\operatorname{Hyp}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
is a little bit more elaborate.
Given two cells
C_{(I,\epsilon)}\leq C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}
as in §
2.1
, we define the

reflected
cell
C_{(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})}
as follows. Recall from §
2.1
that
(I,\epsilon)
is
obtained from
(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})
be replacing some elements
\epsilon^{\prime}_{j}
in the sequence
\epsilon^{\prime}

by

(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})
we replace these elements by the opposite ones (i.e. change their signs). In particular,
I^{\prime\prime}=I^{\prime}
, and
\dim C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}=\dim C_{(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})}
.

Finally, a hyperbolic sheaf
(E(C),\gamma,\delta)
lies in
{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
if for any cells

C_{(I,\epsilon)}\leq C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}
and the corresponding reflected cell
C_{(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})}
we have

(2.11.1)

{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Ker}}}\gamma_{C_{(I,\epsilon)}C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}}={\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Ker}}}\gamma_{C_{(I,\epsilon)}C_{(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})}}\ \operatorname{and}\ \operatorname{Im}\delta_{C_{(I^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})}C_{(I,\epsilon)}}=\operatorname{Im}\delta_{C_{(I^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime})}C_{(I,\epsilon)}}.

The equivalence
P\colon{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
takes
(E(C),\gamma,\delta)
to

(\Phi(I),0,0)
, where
\Phi(I):=\bigcap_{J\subsetneqq I}{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Ker}}}\gamma_{C_{I}C_{J}}
.

The inverse equivalence
Q\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}
takes
(\Phi(I),0,0)
to

(E(C),\gamma,\delta)
, where
E(C_{(I,\epsilon)}):=\bigoplus_{J\subset I}\Phi(J)
, while
\gamma
are
given by the natural embeddings of direct summands, and
\delta
are given by the natural projections
onto direct summands.

Remark 2.11.1
.

The autoequivalence of §
2.4

\operatorname{FT}\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})

induces an involutive autoequivalence

(2.11.2)

\operatorname{FT}\colon{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}}),\ (\Phi(I),0,0)\mapsto(\Phi(\overline{I}),0,0).

The corresponding involutive autoequivalence

(2.11.3)

\operatorname{FT}\colon{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\mathcal{C}})

is a particular case of

3.3.
Kostka numbers

For a partition
\lambda
and a composition
\beta
, the
Kostka number

K_{\lambda,\beta}
is defined as
the number of semi-standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda

and weight
\beta
:

K_{\lambda,\beta}:=\#\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda,\beta).

The Kostka numbers
K_{\lambda,\beta}

are known to be
invariant under permutations of parts of
\beta
.
Thus, without loss of generality,
the Kostka numbers can be labelled by a pair of partitions

\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{P}}
,

K_{\lambda\mu}=K_{\lambda,\iota(\mu)}
,
where
\iota
denotes the inclusion (
3.1.1
).
If
\mu=\lambda
, the only semi-standard Young tableau of the same shape and weight

\lambda
is obtained by filling the
i
th row of
\lambda
all all
i
’s, for
i=1,2,\dots
.
So
K_{\lambda\lambda}=1
.

3.4.
Small Kostka numbers

Let us fix a nonnegative integer
n
. Let
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
and let
\operatorname{Sub}_{n}:=2^{[n]}
denote the set of subsets
I\subset[n]
.
The set
\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
is in bijection
with the set
{\mathfrak{Q}}_{n+1}
of compositions of
n+1
.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

The following two maps

\varrho=\varrho_{n}\colon\operatorname{Sub}_{n}\overset{\sim}{\to}{\mathfrak{Q}}_{n+1}

and

\varrho^{-1}\colon{\mathfrak{Q}}_{n+1}\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{Sub}_{n}

are bijective and inverse to each other:

\begin{array}[]{l}\varrho:I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r}\}\mapsto\beta=(i_{1},i_{2}-i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}-i_{r-1},n+1-i_{r}),\\[7.22743pt]
\varrho^{-1}:\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})\mapsto I=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{k-1}\}.\end{array}

For example,
\varrho([n])=(1,1,\ldots,1)
and
\varrho(\emptyset)=(n+1)
.

Definition 3.4.2
.

For
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}
and
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, the
small Kostka number

\kappa_{\lambda,I}

is defined by

\kappa_{\lambda,I}:=\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}K_{\lambda,\varrho(J)}.

Proposition 3.4.3
.

For
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}
and

I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
,
we have

(3.4.1)

K_{\lambda,\varrho(I)}=\sum_{J\subset I}\kappa_{\lambda,J},

or, equivalently,

K_{\lambda,\beta}=\sum_{J\subset\varrho^{-1}(\beta)}\kappa_{\lambda,J}
.
This formula defines the numbers
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
uniquely.

Proof.

The claim follows from the Möbius inversion formula.
∎

Example 3.4.4
.

Let
n=2
and
\lambda=(2,1)\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{3}
.
We have
K_{\lambda,\rho(\emptyset)}=K_{\lambda,(3)}=0
,

K_{\lambda,\rho(\{1\})}=K_{\lambda,(1,2)}=1
,

K_{\lambda,\rho(\{2\})}=K_{\lambda,(2,1)}=1
,

K_{\lambda,\rho(\{1,2\})}=K_{\lambda,(1,1,1)}=2
.
Thus the small Kostka numbers are

\kappa_{\lambda,\emptyset}=0
,

\kappa_{\lambda,\{1\}}=1-0=1
,

\kappa_{\lambda,\{2\}}=1-0=1
,

\kappa_{\lambda,\{1,2\}}=2-1-1+0=0
.

Remark 3.4.5
.

For
n\leq 4
, all small Kostka numbers
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
are either 0 or 1.
For
n\geq 5
, the numbers
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
can be greater than 1.
For example, for
n=3k-1
, we have

\kappa_{(2k-1,k,1),\{k,2k\}}=k
.

For
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}
, let
\lambda^{t}
denote the
conjugate partition

of
\lambda
, i.e., the partition whose Young diagram is obtained
from
\lambda
by transposition.
For
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, let
\overline{I}:=[n]\smallsetminus I
.

Theorem 3.4.6
.

(1)

The small Kostka numbers
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
are non-negative integers.

(2)

The small Kostka numbers have the symmetry

\kappa_{\lambda,I}=\kappa_{\lambda^{t},\overline{I}}
.

This theorem follows from a combinatorial and a representation-theoretical
interpretations of the small Kostka numbers
\kappa_{\lambda,I}
given below,
see Theorem
3.5.1
and Corollary
3.8.3
.

3.5.
Descents in Young tableaux

For a standard Young tableau

3.7.
Young symmetrizers

Let
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
be the group algebra of the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
.
Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew Young diagram with
n+1
boxes. Let
T
be any
filling of boxes of
\lambda/\mu
by

1,\dots,n+1
(without repetitions), i.e.,
T
is any bijection

[n+1]\overset{\sim}{\to}\text{\lx@text@lbrace boxes of
$\lambda/\mu$\lx@text@rbrace}
. (Here
T
is not necessarily a standard Young tableau.)
Let
R(T)
(resp.,
C(T)
) denote the subgroup of
S_{n+1}
consisting of all permutations
preserving the rows (resp., the columns) of
T
.

The
Young symmetrizer

y_{T}\in{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
is defined as

a_{T}=\sum_{w\in R(T)}w\,,\quad b_{T}=\sum_{w\in C(T)}\epsilon(w)\,w\,,\textrm{ \ and \ }y_{T}=b_{T}\,a_{T}\,.

Here
\epsilon(w)\in\{1,-1\}
denotes the sign of permutation
w\in S_{n+1}
.

The left ideal

(3.7.1)

L_{T}:={\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]\cdot y_{T}\subset{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]

is a representation of
S_{n+1}
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
have the same shape
\lambda/\mu
,
then
y_{T}=y_{T^{\prime}}\cdot u
, for some
u\in S_{n+1}
. Thus

L_{T}\cong L_{T^{\prime}}
are isomorphic
S_{n+1}
-modules.

The
Specht module

V_{\lambda/\mu}
is defined (up to an isomorphism) as

V_{\lambda/\mu}:=L_{T},

for any filling
T
of the shape
\lambda/\mu
. A choice of
T
gives an
embedding of the Specht module
V_{\lambda/\mu}

as a left submodule
L_{T}
of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
.

For
\mu=\emptyset
, the Specht modules
V_{\lambda}
,

\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}
, are exactly (the isomorphism classes of)
all irreducible representations of the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
.
We have the following decomposition of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]

into a direct sum of left submodules:

(3.7.2)

{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]=\bigoplus_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}_{n+1}}L_{T}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}}\left(\bigoplus_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}L_{T}\right),

where
\operatorname{SYT}_{n+1}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}}\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)
,
see
[
We
, Theorem 1.3.J]
.

K_{\lambda,\varrho(I)}=\sum_{J\subset I}\tilde{\kappa}_{\lambda,J}
,
which is exactly equation (
3.4.1
) that defines the small Kostka
numbers.
Thus
\kappa_{\lambda,I}=\tilde{\kappa}_{\lambda,I}
, as needed.
∎

Remark 3.8.4
.

For any skew Young diagram
\lambda/\mu
,
the multiplicities of irreducible representations
V_{\nu}
of the symmetric group
in the Specht module
V_{\lambda/\mu}

are called the
Littlewood-Richardson coefficients

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
:

V_{\lambda/\mu}\cong\bigoplus_{\nu}V_{\nu}^{\otimes c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}}.

The Littlewood-Richardson rule is a combinatorial rule for the
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
.
The combinatorial interpretation of the small Kostka numbers
(Theorem
3.5.1
) can be shown to be equivalent
to the special case of the Littlewood-Richardson rule
when
\lambda/\mu
is a ribbon.

Theorem
3.5.1
, decomposition (
3.7.2
), and
Corollary
3.8.3
imply the following claim.
For
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, let
\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda,I)
be the set of all standard-Young
tableaux
T
of shape
\lambda
with
n+1
boxes with descent set

\operatorname{Des}(T)=I
. Also let
\operatorname{SYT}_{I}:=\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}}\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda,I)
.
According to Theorem
3.5.1
,
\kappa_{\lambda,I}=\#\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda,I)
.

Corollary 3.8.5
.

Let
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
. The ribbon module
R_{I}
is
isomorphic to the direct sum of irreducible submodules of
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
:

R_{I}\cong\bigoplus_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}_{I}}L_{T}.

The induced module
M_{I}
is isomorphic to the direct sum of irreducible submodules
of
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
:

M_{I}\cong\bigoplus_{J\subset I}\left(\bigoplus_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}_{J}}L_{T}\right).

3.9.
Kostka numbers for Coxeter groups

The symmetric group
S_{n+1}
is a Coxeter group.
Some of the above constructions can be extended
to an arbitrary finite Coxeter group
W
.

Let
W
be a finite Coxeter group of rank
n
with Coxeter generators
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
.
Let
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
be the group algebra of
W
over a field
{\mathsf{k}}
of characteristic 0.

For a subset
I\subset[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
,
let
W_{I}
denote the parabolic subgroup of
W

generated by
s_{i}
, for
i\in I
.
In particular,
W_{[n]}=W
and
W_{\emptyset}=\{1\}
.

For
I\subset[n]
, define the
induced module

M_{I}
as
the following left
W
-submodule of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[W]

(3.9.1)

M_{I}:={\mathsf{k}}[W]\left(\sum_{w\in\overline{I}}w\right)\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{W_{\overline{I}}}^{W}{\mathsf{k}}\,,

where, as usual,
\overline{I}:=[n]\smallsetminus I
.

If
W=S_{n+1}
, then the induced module
M_{I}

is exactly the module
M_{\beta}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{S_{\beta_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{\beta_{k}}}^{S_{n+1}}{\mathsf{k}}
,
where
\beta=\varrho(I)
, from §
3.8
.

By analogy with (
3.8.2
) and
Definition
3.4.2
, we define the
W
-Kostka numbers and the small
W
-Kostka numbers,
as follows.

Definition 3.9.1
.

Let
V
be any irreducible representation of
W
,
and let
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
be any subset of
[n]
.
The
W
-Kostka number

K_{V,I}

is the multiplicity of
V
in the induced module
M_{I}
:

(3.9.2)

K_{V,I}:=\dim{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathsf{k}}[W]}(M_{I},V).

The
small
W
-Kostka number

\kappa_{V,I}
is
the alternating sum of
K
-Kostka numbers:

(3.9.3)

\kappa_{V,I}:=\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}K_{V,J}.

Using Möbius inversion,
we can express the
K
-Kostka numbers in
terms of the small
W
-Kostka numbers as

(3.9.4)

K_{V,I}=\sum_{J\subset I}\kappa_{V,J}.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}
be the
sign representation
of
W
, which is the
1-dimensional
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
-module, where each Coxeter generator as

s_{i}:x\mapsto-x
. For a
W
-module
V
, let

V^{t}:=V\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}\,.

The following claim generalizes Theorem
3.4.6
.

Theorem 3.9.2
.

(1)

The small
W
-Kostka numbers
\kappa_{V,I}
are non-negative integers.

(2)

The small Kostka numbers have the symmetry

\kappa_{V,I}=\kappa_{V^{t},\overline{I}}
.

This theorem follows from Corollary
3.11.4
and
Lemma
3.11.5
below.
It would be nice to find a simple
combinatorial rule like Theorem
3.5.1
for calculation of the small
W
-Kostka numbers
(e.g. when
W
is a Weyl group of type
B
or
D
).

3.10.
Solomon’s decomposition of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]

Define the following elements of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[W]

a_{I}:=\sum_{w\in W_{I}}w\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad b_{I}:=\sum_{w\in W_{I}}\epsilon(w)\,w\,,

where
\epsilon(w)=(-1)^{\ell(w)}
is the sign of
w\in W

and
\ell(w):=\min\{l\mid w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}\}

is the length of
w
.

Notice the induced module
M_{I}
defined by (
3.9.1
) is

M_{I}={\mathsf{k}}[W]\,a_{\overline{I}}
.

Define the
ribbon
W
-module

R_{I}
as

(3.10.1)

R_{I}:={\mathsf{k}}[W]\,b_{I}a_{\overline{I}}.

If
W=S_{n+1}
, then the ribbon
W
-module
R_{I}

is exactly the ribbon module (
3.8.3
)
from §
3.8
.

Theorem 3.10.1
.

[
So2
, Theorem 2]

The group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
decomposes into a direct sum of
2^{n}
ribbon
modules:

{\mathsf{k}}[W]=\bigoplus_{I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}}R_{I}.

3.11.
Descent basis

Let us give a
{\mathsf{k}}
-linear basis of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
which agrees with Solomon’s decomposition.

The (right)
descent set

\mathrm{Des}(w)
of an element
w\in W

is

\operatorname{Des}(w):=\{i\in I\mid\ell(w\,s_{i})<\ell(w)\}.

For
w\in W
, define the following element of the group algebra

(3.11.1)

d_{w}:=w\,b_{\operatorname{Des}(w)}a_{[n]\smallsetminus\operatorname{Des}(w)}\in{\mathsf{k}}[W].

Example 3.11.1
.

For
W=S_{3}=\{1,s_{1},s_{2},s_{1}s_{2},s_{2}s_{1},s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}\}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}d_{1}=1+s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{1}s_{2}+s_{2}s_{1}+s_{1}s_{2}s_{1},\\[0.0pt]
d_{s_{1}}=s_{1}(1-s_{1})(1+s_{2})=-1+s_{1}-s_{2}+s_{1}s_{2},\\[0.0pt]
d_{s_{2}}=s_{2}(1-s_{2})(1+s_{1})=-1-s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{2}s_{1},\\[0.0pt]
d_{s_{1}s_{2}}=s_{1}s_{2}(1-s_{2})(1+s_{1})=-1-s_{1}+s_{1}s_{2}+s_{1}s_{2}s_{1},\\[0.0pt]
d_{s_{2}s_{1}}=s_{2}s_{1}(1-s_{1})(1+s_{2})=-1-s_{2}+s_{2}s_{1}+s_{1}s_{2}s_{1},\\[0.0pt]
d_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}}=-1+s_{1}+s_{2}-s_{1}s_{2}-s_{2}s_{1}+s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}.\end{array}

Theorem 3.11.2
.

(1)

The set of elements
\{d_{w}\mid w\in W\}
is a
{\mathsf{k}}
-linear basis
of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
.

(2)

For any
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, the set of elements

\{d_{w}\mid w\in W\textrm{ such that }\mathrm{Des}(w)=I\}
is a
{\mathsf{k}}
-linear
basis of the ribbon module
R_{I}
.
In particular,

\dim R_{I}=\#\{w\in W\mid\operatorname{Des}(w)=I\}.

(3)

For any
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, the set of elements

\{d_{w}\mid w\in W\textrm{ such that }\mathrm{Des}(w)\subset I\}
is a
{\mathsf{k}}
-linear
basis of the induced module
M_{I}
.
In particular,

\dim M_{I}=\#\{w\in W\mid\operatorname{Des}(w)\subset I\}.

A proof of this theorem is given in §
3.12
below.
It is immediate from the definitions that
d_{w}\in R_{I}
if
\mathrm{Des}(w)=I
,
and that
d_{w}\in M_{I}
if
\mathrm{Des}(w)\subset I
.

Corollary 3.11.3
.

For
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
, we have the decomposition of the induced module
M_{I}

into a direct sum of ribbon modules:

M_{I}=\bigoplus_{J\subset I}R_{J}.

Corollary 3.11.4
.

For an irreducible
W
-representation
V
and
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
,
the small
W
-Kostka number
\kappa_{V,I}
is the multiplicity of
V

in the ribbon module
R_{I}
:

\kappa_{V,I}=\dim{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathsf{k}}[W]}(V,R_{I}).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\kappa}_{V,I}
be the multiplicity of
V
in
R_{I}
.
By Corollary
3.11.3
,

K_{V,I}:=\dim{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathsf{k}}[W]}(V,M_{I})=\sum_{J\subset I}\dim{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathsf{k}}[W]}(V,R_{J})=\sum_{J\subset I}\tilde{\kappa}_{V,I},

which is exactly equation (
3.9.4
) defining
the small Kostka numbers. Thus
\tilde{\kappa}_{V,I}=\kappa_{V,I}
.
∎

Recall that
{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}
is the sign representation of
W
.

Lemma 3.11.5
.

We have the isomorphism of
W
-modules:

R_{I}\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}\cong R_{\overline{I}}.

Proof.

By
[
So2
, Lemma 12]
, we have the isomorphism

{\mathsf{k}}[W]\,b_{J}a_{I}\cong{\mathsf{k}}[W]\,a_{I}b_{J}
.
Thus
R_{I}\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}={\mathsf{k}}[W]\,a_{I}b_{\overline{I}}\cong{\mathsf{k}}[W]\,b_{\overline{I}}a_{I}=R_{\overline{I}}
.
∎

Now Theorem
3.9.2
follows
from Corollary
3.11.4

and Lemma
3.11.5
.

3.12.
Proof of Theorem
3.11.2

For
I\subset[n]
, define the (right)

symmetrization
and the
antisymmetrization
operators

\mathrm{Sym}_{I}
and
\mathrm{Asym}_{I}
acting on
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
by

\mathrm{Sym}_{I}\colon f\mapsto f\,a_{I}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathrm{Asym}_{I}\colon f\mapsto f\,b_{I}

Let
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I}),\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I}),\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I}),\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})\subset{\mathsf{k}}[W]
be the images
and kernels of these operators.

Lemma 3.12.1
.

The ribbon module
R_{I}
is isomorphic to the quotient module

R_{I}\cong\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})/(\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}})\cap\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})).

More precisely, the map
\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}}

restricted to
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})

induces the isomorphism
between
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{{I}})/(\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}})\cap\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I}))

and
R_{I}
.

Proof.

We have
R_{I}:={\mathsf{k}}[W]\,b_{I}\,a_{\overline{I}}=\{f\,a_{\overline{I}}\mid f\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})\}=\{\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}}(f)\mid f\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})\}
.
∎

For
i\in[n]
, define the subspaces

S^{i}:=\{f\in{\mathsf{k}}[W]\mid fs_{i}=f\}

and
A^{i}:=\{f\in{\mathsf{k}}[W]\mid fs_{i}=-f\}
of the group algebra
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
.
Clearly,
S^{i}=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Sym}_{\{i\}})=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Asym}_{\{i\}})
and

A^{i}=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{\{i\}})=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{\{i\}})
.

Lemma 3.12.2
.

The image of the symmetrization operator
\mathrm{Sym}_{I}

is the intersection of subspaces:

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I})=\bigcap_{i\in I}S^{i},

and the kernel of
\mathrm{Sym}_{I}
is the linear span of subspaces:

\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I})=\underset{i\in I}{\mathrm{Span}}\,A^{i}.

Similarly, the image and the kernel of the antisymmetrization
operator
\mathrm{Asym}_{I}
are

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})=\bigcap_{i\in I}A^{i}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})=\underset{i\in I}{\mathrm{Span}}\,S^{i}.

Proof.

An element
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[W]
belongs to the image of
\mathrm{Sym}_{I}

if and only if
f\,w=f
, for any
w\in W_{I}
.
Equivalently, we should have
f\,s_{i}=f
, for all
i\in I
,
that is,
f\in\bigcap_{i\in I}S^{i}
.

An element
g=\sum_{w\in W}g_{w}\,w\in{\mathsf{k}}[W]
belongs
to the kernel of
\mathrm{Sym}_{I}
if and only if
the sum of its coefficients
g_{w}
over any
right
W_{I}
-coset
C
in
W
is zero:
\sum_{w\in C}g_{w}=0
.
Such
g
should be a linear combination of the elements

w-ws_{i}
, for
w\in W
and
i\in I
.
This follows from the fact that the right
W_{I}
-cosets in
W

are exactly the connected components of the graph on set of
vertices
W
with the set of edges

\{(w,ws_{i})\mid w\in W,\,i\in I\}
.
For fixed
i\in I
, the elements
w-w\,s_{i}
, for
w\in W
,
span the subspace
A^{i}
. Thus
g
should belong to the linear
span of the subspaces
A^{i}
over all
i\in I
.

The claim about the image and the kernel of
\mathrm{Asym}_{I}
is proved
analogously.
∎

For
w\in W
, let

c_{w}:=w\,b_{\mathrm{Des}(w)}\in{\mathsf{k}}[W].

Lemma 3.12.3
.

The set
\{c_{w}\mid w\in W\}
is a
{\mathsf{k}}
-linear basis of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
.

Proof.

Notice that
w
is the unique maximal by length element in the
expansion of
c_{w}
.
Thus the set of elements
\{c_{w}|w\in W\}
is related to the standard linear
basis
\{w\mid w\in W\}
of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
by a triangular matrix with 1’s
on the diagonal. Thus
\{c_{w}\mid w\in W\}
is a linear basis of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
.
∎

Lemma 3.12.4
.

In the linear basis
\{c_{w}\mid w\in W\}
,
the subspace
A^{i}
is the coordinate subspace of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]

spanned by the basis elements
c_{w}
, for
all
w
such that
i\in\mathrm{Des}(w)
:

A^{i}=\underset{w\,\mid\,i\in\mathrm{Des}(w)}{\mathrm{Span}}\,c_{w}.

Moreover, for any
I\subset[n]
,

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})
and
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I})
are the coordinate subspaces given by

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})=\underset{w\,\mid\,I\subset\mathrm{Des}(w)}{\mathrm{Span}}\,c_{w}\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I})=\underset{w\,\mid\,I\cap\mathrm{Des}(w)\neq\emptyset}{\mathrm{Span}}\,c_{w}.

Proof.

Clearly,
c_{w}\in A^{i}
if
i\in\mathrm{Des}(w)
.
Since
\#\{w\in W\mid i\in\mathrm{Des}(w)\}=|W|/2=\dim A^{i}
,
we deduce that the basis elements
c_{w}
such that
i\in\mathrm{Des}(w)

span the subspace
A^{i}
.
Now the claims about
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Asym}_{I})
and
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{Sym}_{I})

follow from Lemma
3.12.2
.
∎

Now we can prove part (2) of Theorem
3.11.2
.
By Lemmas
3.12.1
and
3.12.4
,
the map
\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}}
,
restricted to
\operatorname{Span}\left\{c_{w}\mid I\subset\mathrm{Des}(w)\right\}
,
induces the isomorphism

\left(\underset{w\,\mid\,I\subset\mathrm{Des}(w)}{\mathrm{Span}}\,c_{w}\right)/\left(\underset{w\,\mid\,I\subset\mathrm{Des}(w),\ \overline{I}\cap\mathrm{Des}(w)\neq\emptyset}{\mathrm{Span}}\,c_{w}\right)\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}R_{I}.

Thus the elements
\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}}(c_{w})
,
for all
w\in W
such that
\mathrm{Des}(w)=I
, form a linear basis of
R_{I}
.
This is exactly the claim of Theorem
3.11.2
(2),
because
\mathrm{Sym}_{\overline{I}}(c_{w})=w\,b_{\mathrm{Des}(w)}a_{\overline{I}}=:d_{w}
.

Part (1) of Theorem
3.11.2
follows from part (2) and
Solomon’s decomposition (Theorem
3.10.1
).

To prove part (3) Theorem
3.11.2
, notice that

d_{w}\in M_{I}
, for all
w\in W
such that
\mathrm{Des}(w)\subset I
.
Such
w
’s are exactly the minimal length coset representatives for the

.
So we get

.

4.
Kostka sheaves

Let
{\mathcal{C}}={\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})={\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]{\operatorname{-mod}}
denote the category of finite dimensional representations of

S_{n+1}
over
{\mathsf{k}}
, that is the category of finite dimensional left
{\mathsf{k}}[S_{n+1}]
-modules.
The key object of this section is a semisimple amonodromic perverse sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
smooth along the
coordinate stratification of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Namely, it is a direct sum of constant sheaves
{\mathcal{F}}_{I}
on
the strata closures
\overline{S}_{I},\ I\subset\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
. Finally,

{\mathcal{F}}_{I}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{P}}_{n+1}}V_{\lambda}^{\oplus\kappa_{\lambda,I}}[n-|I|].

4.1.
The sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
in GGM realization

The corresponding amonodromic semisimple GGM sheaf with values in
{\mathcal{C}}
is

{\mathscr{P}}={\mathscr{P}}^{(n)}\in{\operatorname{GGM}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})).

By definition, for any
I\in\operatorname{Sub}_{n}
we have
{\mathscr{P}}(I)=R_{I}
,
the ribbon module defined by
(
3.8.3
) and (
3.8.4
).

4.2.
The sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
in hyperbolic realization

The corresponding amonodromic semisimple hyperbolic sheaf with values in
{\mathcal{C}}
is

{\mathscr{Q}}={\mathscr{Q}}^{(n)}\in{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})).

Explicitly, by §
2.11
its hyperbolic stalks are

{\mathscr{Q}}(C_{(I,\epsilon)})=\oplus_{J\subset I}R_{J}.

Therefore,
by Theorem
3.8.2
,
they are isomorphic to the induced modules

(4.2.1)

{\mathscr{Q}}(C_{(I,\epsilon)})={\mathscr{Q}}(I)\simeq M_{I}=M_{\varrho(I)}

We recall that
\varrho
denotes the isomorphism

\varrho\colon\operatorname{Sub}_{n}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}{\mathfrak{Q}}_{n+1}
of Lemma
3.4.1
,
so that
\varrho(I)=\alpha
is a composition of
n+1
, and

M_{\alpha}
is the
S_{n+1}
-module induced from the trivial representation
of the subgroup
S_{\alpha}\subset S_{n+1}
.

4.3.
A functor
{\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})\to{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1}))

More generally, for any
M\in{\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})
we define a hyperbolic sheaf

{\mathscr{Q}}_{M}=(M\otimes{\mathscr{Q}}(C_{(I,\epsilon)}),{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Id}}}_{M}\otimes\gamma,{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Id}}}_{M}\otimes\delta).

This way we get a “localization” functor

{\mathscr{Q}}\colon{\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})\to{\operatorname{Hyp}}^{\operatorname{ass}}_{n}({\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})).

Theorem 4.3.1
.

For
M\in{\operatorname{Rep}}(S_{n+1})
, we have an isomorphism
\operatorname{FT}{\mathscr{Q}}_{M}\simeq{\mathscr{Q}}_{M\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to construct an isomorphism
\operatorname{FT}{\mathscr{Q}}\simeq{\mathscr{Q}}\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}
.
Equivalently, in the GGM realization, we have to construct an isomorphism

\operatorname{FT}{\mathscr{P}}\simeq{\mathscr{P}}\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}
. The existence of the desired isomorphism follows
immediately from the isomorphism

V_{\lambda}\otimes{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}\simeq V_{\lambda^{t}}
,
Theorem
3.4.6
, and
the formula for Fourier-Sato transform in §
2.4
.
∎

4.4.
Induced from parabolics as functions on flags

Let us call a
type

a sequence of integers
\chi=(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{p})
with

1\leq\chi_{1}<\ldots<\chi_{p}\leq n+1.

Notation:
\{\chi\}=\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{p}\}
.

The number
p=\ell(\chi)
will be called the length of
\chi
. The set of types of length
p

will be denoted
{\mathcal{T}}yp_{p}
; this set contains
\binom{n+1}{p}
elements.

To each type corresponds a composition of
n+1

(4.4.1)

\alpha(\chi)=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}-\chi_{1},\ldots,n+1-\chi_{p})\in{\mathfrak{Q}}_{n+1}

A
flag of type
\chi
is a chain
I_{\bullet}
of subsets

I_{1}\subset\ldots\subset I_{p}\subset[n+1]
with
|I_{i}|=\chi_{i}
, and
p
is the length of
I_{\bullet}
.

We denote by
F\ell_{\chi}
the set of all flags of type
\chi
,
and by
F\ell_{p}
the set of all flags of length
p
.

The set
F\ell_{\chi}
is acted upon from the left by
S_{n+1}
and is isomorphic to

F\ell_{\chi}\cong S_{n+1}/S_{\chi},

where a parabolic subgroup
S_{\chi}
is the stabilizer of the standard flag

[\chi_{1}]\subset\ldots\subset[\chi_{p}].

We have

S_{\chi}\cong S_{\alpha_{1}}\times S_{\alpha_{2}}\times\ldots\times S_{\alpha_{p+1}}=S_{\alpha},

where

\alpha=\alpha(\chi)
, cf. (

4.7.

Recall the complex

Theorem 4.7.1

Proof.

We know already that the individual terms of the above complexes are isomorphic,

). Both of them start with the regular representation

On the other hand,

. Indeed, the Euler characteristic of

\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})
is
{\mathsf{k}}_{\epsilon}
e.g. by
[
So1
, Theorem 2]
, and

H^{>0}(\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}}))=0
by Proposition

We will apply the following elementary

Lemma 4.7.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a semisimple category,
A\subset B,A^{\prime}\subset B^{\prime}

objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
. If
A\cong A^{\prime}
and
B\cong B^{\prime}
then
A/B\cong A^{\prime}/B^{\prime}

\Box

We can reformulate the lemma as follows. Choose an isomorphism

\phi_{A}\colon A\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}A^{\prime}

is semisimple, there are objects

such that

and

. According to the lemma,

Whence there exists an isomorphism

making the square

@V{\phi_{A}}V{}V@V{\phi_{B}}V{}V\\

Now we can construct the desired isomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}as^{\bullet}\mathbin{\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}}\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})
inductively from

4.8.
Relation to the permutohedron

We present a geometric interpretation of the master complex of vanishing cycles

\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})
, cf.
[
Kat
,
So1
,
Wi
]
.

Proposition 4.8.1
.

The complex
\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})
is isomorphic, as a complex of

{\mathsf{k}}
-vector spaces, to the complex
C^{\bullet}(\operatorname{Perm}_{n})
of cochains of the

n
-th permutohedron.

For instance, let
n=2
. The complex
\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})=\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}}^{(2)})
has the form

0\to{\mathsf{k}}[S_{3}]\to{\mathsf{k}}[S^{\prime}]\oplus{\mathsf{k}}[S^{\prime\prime}]\to{\mathsf{k}}\to 0.

Here
S^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime}\cong S_{2}
are the subgroups of
S_{3}
generated by transpositions
s_{1}=(12)

and
s_{2}=(23)
respectively.
So the dimensions of its terms are
6,6,1
, and it is isomorphic to the complex of cochains of the
hexagon
\operatorname{Perm}_{2}
.

Proof.

Our complex
\Phi^{\bullet}({\mathscr{Q}})
has the form

0\to{\mathsf{k}}[W]\to\oplus_{i\in S}{\mathsf{k}}[W/W_{\{i\}}]\to\oplus_{\{i,j\}\subset S}{\mathsf{k}}[W/W_{\{i,j\}}]\to\ldots\to{\mathsf{k}}\to 0,

where
W=S_{n+1}
, and for a subset
I\subset S
of the set of Coxeter generators,
W_{I}\subset W

denotes the subgroup generated by
s_{i},\ i\in I
.

On the other hand, consider the root arrangement in
{\mathbb{R}}^{S}
; the permutohedron

P_{W}
is a polygon dual to this arrangement. Thus, its vertices are in one-to-one correspondence
with the chambers that form a set on which
W
acts simply transitively, i.e. after choosing a
fundamental chamber
C
we can identify this set with
W
, i.e. with a base of
{\mathsf{k}}[W]
.

Next, the edges of
P_{W}
are in bijection with walls; there are
n
walls
M_{i}
on the boundary of

C
which correspond to simple reflections
s_{i}
; the stabilizer of
M_{i}

being the subgroup
W_{\{i\}}
. So the set of walls is in bijection with

\coprod_{i\in S}W/W_{\{i\}}
, etc.
∎

4.9.
Warning

One might think that the maps
i_{\chi\theta}
and
r_{\theta\chi}
give rise to a hyperbolic sheaf,
but this is not so: the collinearity axiom does not hold, as the example of
S_{3}
already shows.

Namely, let
n=2
. Consider the square

\begin{CD}M_{(1)}@>{r}>{}>M_{\emptyset}\\
@V{i}V{}V@V{i}V{}V\\
M_{(12)}@>{r}>{}>M_{(2)}.\end{CD}

Let
f\in M_{(1)}
, i.e.
f
is a function from one-element subsets of
[3]
to
{\mathsf{k}}
.
Then
r(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(i)
, and
ir(f)
is the constant function with value
r(f)
.

On the other hand,
i(f)(\{i\}\subset\{i,j\})=f(i)
,
and
ir(f)(\{i,j\})=f(i)+f(j)
.
Thus,
ir\neq ri
, the square is not commutative.

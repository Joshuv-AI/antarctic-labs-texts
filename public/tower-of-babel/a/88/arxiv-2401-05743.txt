Consistent Query Answering for Existential Rules with Closed Predicates
Lorenzo Marconi marconi@diag.uniroma1.it, Riccardo Rosati rosati@diag.uniroma1.it

Abstract
Consistent Query Answering (CQA) is an inconsistency-tolerant approach to data access in knowledge bases and databases. The goal of CQA is to provide meaningful (consistent) answers to queries even in the presence of inconsistent information, e.g. a database whose data conflict with meta-data (typically the database integrity constraints). The semantics of CQA is based on the notion of repair , that is, a consistent version of the initial, inconsistent database that is obtained through minimal modifications. We study CQA in databases with data dependencies expressed by existential rules. More specifically, we focus on the broad class of disjunctive embedded dependencies with inequalities (DED ≠ s), which extend both tuple-generating dependencies and equality-generated dependencies. We first focus on the case when the database predicates are closed , i.e. the database is assumed to have complete knowledge about such predicates, thus no tuple addition is possible to repair the database. In such a scenario, we provide a detailed analysis of the data complexity of CQA and associated tasks (repair checking) under different semantics (AR and IAR) and for different classes of existential rules. In particular, we consider the classes of acyclic, linear, full, sticky and guarded DED ≠ s, and their combinations.

Introduction

Consistent query answering
(?) is an approach to inconsistency-tolerant reasoning in databases and knowledge bases (KBs).
The central notion of consistent query answering is the one of

is a consistent database or KB that is “maximally close” to

In general, many different repairs for the same database or KB may exist.
The problem of consistent query answering as originally formulated was: given a query

Also the

\mathcal{D}

CQA was originally defined for relational databases (?),
but it has then been studied also in the context of Description Logics KBs (see e.g. (?, ?, ?)) and rule-based KBs, in particular knowledge bases with
existential rules
(?, ?), where an extensional database (set of ground atoms) is coupled with a set of first-order implications.

In this paper,
we consider a very expressive class of existential rules that comprises and extends both disjunctive tuple-generating dependencies (DTGDs) and equality-generating dependencies (EGDs), which are the most studied forms of rules studied in the literature.
Specifically, the rules that we consider in this paper

≠
s

We also consider five subclasses of these dependencies, corresponding to some of the most important ones studied in the literature on existential rules, i.e. the classes of

full

Concerning the semantics, we first adopt a

Example 1

A hospital facility has complete knowledge about its domain of interest. Suppose that patients are modeled through a predicate
P

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\forall ssn,f,l\,(&P(ssn,f,l)\rightarrow\exists ph,d\,V(ssn,ph,d)\lor\exists dep,ts\,H(ssn,dep,ts)),\\

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\mathcal{D}=\{&P(\mathsf{123},\mathsf{kim},\mathsf{lee}),P(\mathsf{456},\mathsf{lou},\mathsf{smith}),H(\mathsf{456},\mathsf{icu},\mathsf{s}_{1}),R(\mathsf{123},\mathsf{icu},\mathsf{s}_{1}),R(\mathsf{456},\mathsf{icu},\mathsf{s}_{1})\}\end{array}

Despite consistent query answering is well-known to be a computationally hard task, our results identify many interesting classes of existential rules in which the problems of consistent query answering and repair checking are tractable in data complexity. Moreover, for most of these tractable classes we prove the problems studied to enjoy the
first-order (FO) rewritability

This property is very interesting not only from the theoretical viewpoint, but also towards the definition of practical algorithms for consistent query answering based on FO rewriting methods, in a way similar to the case of classical query answering (see e.g. (?, ?)).

The closest work to the present paper is (?), which presents an exhaustive study of the complexity of consistent query answering over existential rules. That paper inherits the open-world assumption (OWA) adopted by the recent approaches to existential rules, in which the interpretation of all predicates is open, thus repairing an inconsistency between the database and the rules is preferably done by (virtually) adding tuples to the initial database (that is, tuple deletions are considered only if tuple additions can not repair the data).
Conversely, our approach adopts a closed-world assumption (CWA), in which all predicates are closed and the only way to repair an inconsistency is through tuple deletions. Given this semantic discrepancy, the problems studied in the two papers are actually different, so
our findings can be seen as complementary to the ones presented in the aforementioned work (see also the conclusions).
We also remark that both the language for existential rules and the query language considered there are less expressive than the ones (disjunctive dependencies and BUCQ
\neq
s) studied in the present paper.

Related work

Consistent Query Answering was originally proposed for relational databases in (?), which introduced the notions of repairs and consistent answers, and repairs were intended as the the ones minimizing the symmetric difference with the original database.
Ever since, many works considered various kinds of integrity constraints and chose different repairing approaches.
Other types of repairs include the maximal consistent subsets of the original database (?), as well as the minimal consistent supersets (?). The notion of maximality is also sometimes based on the cardinality (?).
Here we define a repair as the maximal consistent superset of the original database, i.e. we adopt the so-called tuple-deletion semantics.

For what concerns the problem of repair checking, the most relevant papers that are strictly related to our investigation are (?, ?, ?, ?, ?), which deeply explored the problem for many classes of dependencies.

Also the problem of finding consistent answers for a given query under tuple-deletion semantics has been extensively studied by (?) and (?), with a particular focus on (some forms of) tuple-generating dependencies.
As shown in the conclusions, some of our results extend the ones provided in these two papers.
Special attention should also be paid to (?) and (?), which presented FO-rewritable techniques for solving the CQA problem, though limiting the set of integrity constraints to (primary) key dependencies and making some assumptions on the user query.

In the mentioned works, all of which best reviewed in (?, ?, ?), the most common entailment semantics adopted corresponds to the one that here we call AR-entailment.
The IAR semantics, instead, was previously studied in the context of ontologies by (?), and further investigated for multiple DL languages in (?, ?, ?).
In particular, the latter proved IAR-entailment of CQs to be FO rewritable for the language
\text{DL-Lite}_{R,den}
.
Afterward, (?) proposed a new semantics named ICR (“intersection of closed repairs”), which IAR is a sound approximation of, showing its FO expressibility for simple ontologies.

Both the entailment problems that we consider have also been studied in the field of existential rules (?, ?, ?, ?), and systematically revised in (?).
However, all such results are not straightforwardly transposable under CWA, which is our case study.
Indeed, in this paper we aim to provide a complementary analysis to the “open” case.

3.3
Complexity classes

In this article we refer to the following computational complexity classes:

•

\mathrm{AC}^{0}
, i.e. the class of decision problems solvable by circuit families of constant (i.e. independent of the input size) depth, polynomial size, and whose gates have unbounded fanin (each gate can handle and arbitrarily-large number of inputs);

•

PTIME, i.e. the class of decision problems solvable in polynomial time by a deterministic Turing machine;

•

NP, i.e. the class of decision problems solvable in polynomial time by a non-deterministic Turing machine;

•

coNP, i.e. the class of decision problems whose complement is solvable in polynomial time by a non-deterministic Turing machine;

•

\mathrm{\Pi}^{p}_{2}
, i.e. the class of decision problems whose complement is solvable in polynomial time by a non-deterministic Turing machine augmented by an oracle for some NP-complete problem.

These classes are such that
\mathrm{AC}^{0}\subseteq\text{PTIME}\subseteq\text{NP}\cup\text{coNP}\subseteq\mathrm{\Pi}^{p}_{2}

Given a complexity class

C

The names of these entailment semantics come from the field of Description Logics. In fact, AR and IAR stand for “ABox repair” and “intersection of ABox repairs”, respectively (?).
As anticipated in the introduction, IAR-entailment is a well-known sound approximation of AR-entailment. This is illustrated in the subsequent example.

Example 2
.

In a university, every lecturer (predicate

\mathsf{c}_{2}

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l@{~~~~}r@{}l}\mathcal{D}=\{&A(\mathsf{bob},\mathsf{c}_{1}),L(\mathsf{tom},\mathsf{c}_{1}),T(\mathsf{tom},\mathsf{bob}),&\Sigma=\{&\forall l,c,s\,(L(l,c)\land A(s,c)\rightarrow T(l,s)),\\

The second dependency of

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\mathsf{rep}_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{D})=\{&\{A(\mathsf{bob},\mathsf{c}_{1}),L(\mathsf{tom},\mathsf{c}_{1}),T(\mathsf{tom},\mathsf{bob}),A(\mathsf{ann},\mathsf{c}_{2})\},\\

5
Recoverability

In what follows, we present some auxiliary outcomes that are helpful in proving certain complexity results presented further on.
In particular, we introduce the
recoverability
property, that is related to the notion of consistency of a database.

Definition 7

(Recoverability)
.

Given a set of dependencies
\Sigma
, we say that a set of facts
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is
recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
if there exists a subset
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
.

Intuitively, a set of facts
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
if it is a subset of
\mathcal{D}
that can be “repaired” adding facts belonging to the original database
\mathcal{D}
. Obviously, if
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
then it is contained in some repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.
For some classes of dependencies, we then study the associated decision problem of checking whether a set of facts
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.

We now turn our attention to full dependencies.
We recall that the chase of a database w.r.t. full dependencies
can be computed in polynomial time. Consequently, the following property holds.

Lemma 1
.

Checking recoverability is in PTIME with respect to data complexity in the case of full dependencies.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that a database
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\mathit{Chase}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\Sigma)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Then, the thesis follows from the fact that, for full dependencies,
\mathit{Chase}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\Sigma)
is finite and can be computed in polynomial time.
∎

Consider now the following crucial property.

Definition 8

(CQ-FO-rewritability)
.

We call a set of dependencies
\Sigma

CQ-FO-rewritable
if, for every CQ

(where
\mathbf{x}
are the free variables of the query) there exists an FO query
\phi_{q}(\mathbf{x})
such that, for every database
\mathcal{D}
and for every tuple of constants
\mathbf{t}
of the same arity as
\mathbf{x}

\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle\models q(\mathbf{t})
iff
\phi_{q}(\mathbf{t})
evaluates to true in
\mathcal{D}
.

It is easy to see that CQ-FO-rewritability is enjoyed by any set of full dependencies if such a set is also acyclic, or linear, or sticky, as shown below.

Proposition 5
.

Every set of acyclic+full dependencies, every set of full+linear dependencies and every set of full+sticky dependencies is CQ-FO-rewritable.

Proof.

To prove the proposition, we introduce some auxiliary definitions.
Given a single-head full dependency
\tau
, we define
\tau_{t}

as the TGD obtained from
\tau
deleting all the inequality atoms in its body.
Moreover, given a set of full dependencies
\Sigma
, we denote by
\mathit{TGD}(\Sigma)
the set of full TGDs
\{\tau_{t}\mid\tau\in\Sigma\}
.

Now, the key observation is that it is very easy to modify existing rewriting techniques for conjunctive queries over TGDs to deal with single-head full dependencies without inequalities. E.g. the algorithm shown in (?) can very easily be adapted to such single-head full dependencies with inequality atoms in rule bodies. Indeed, since the dependencies are full, no variable is deleted by the rewriting steps, which implies that the rewriting of every CQ over
\Sigma
can be obtained essentially in the same way as in the case of the corresponding
\mathit{TGD}(\Sigma)
: the additional inequality atoms can just be collected along the rewriting steps, and returned in the final rewritten query (which is thus a UCQ with inequalities rather than a simple UCQ).
This immediately implies the following property.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of full dependencies. If
\mathit{TGD}(\Sigma)
is CQ-FO-rewritable, then
\Sigma
is CQ-FO-rewritable.

Now, since acyclic TGDs, linear TGDs and sticky TGDs have the CQ-FO-rewritability property (see e.g. (?, ?)),
the above lemma immediately implies that the CQ-FO-rewritability property holds for every set of dependencies that is either acyclic+full, or full+linear, or full+sticky.
∎

We now prove that, for the above classes of dependencies, checking recoverability is FO rewritable, i.e. it is always possible to build an FO formula (depending only on

6
Repair checking

In this section we study the data complexity of repair checking in the classes of dependencies previously mentioned.

Lower bounds

We remark that all the lower bounds shown in this section also hold if the dependencies are restricted to be single-head and without inequalities in the head.

First, we focus on the lower bounds of the problem of checking if a given database is a repair.
We prove the repair checking

Theorem 1

There exists a set of linear+sticky dependencies for which repair checking is PTIME-hard with respect to data complexity.

We prove the thesis by showing that the HORN SAT problem can be reduced to repair checking.

\phi^{\prime}

\phi

\phi^{\prime}
.

Now, we associate an id

Now, the (unique) repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
is the set of facts built starting from
\mathcal{D}
and deterministically removing some atoms according to the dependencies of
\Sigma
.
Observe that:

•

because of the first dependency of
\Sigma
, if for a rule there are no more body atoms, then the head atom of that rule is deleted;

•

because of the second dependency, if a head atom is deleted, then all the head atoms with that variable are deleted;

•

because of the third dependency, if there are no head atoms for a variable, then all the body atoms for that variable are deleted;

•

because of the fourth and fifth dependencies, if the atom
H(r,u,1,2)
is deleted, then all the atoms are deleted.

Therefore, it can be verified that for every variable
x
that belongs to the minimal model of
\phi^{\prime}
, no fact of the form
H(\_,x,\_,\_)
or
B(x,\_)
belong to the repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
. Consequently, the minimal model of
\phi^{\prime}
contains
u
(i.e.
\phi
is unsatisfiable) iff the fact
H(r_{1},u,1,2)
does not belong to the repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
, which implies (because of the fourth and fifth rules of
\Sigma
) that
\phi
is unsatisfiable iff the empty database is the repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.
∎

Then, we extend the PTIME lower bound shown in (?, Theorem 5) for full dependencies to the case of full+guarded dependencies.

Theorem 2
.

There exists a set of full+guarded dependencies for which repair checking is PTIME-hard with respect to data complexity.

Proof.

We prove the thesis using a modification of the reduction from Horn 3-CNF satisfiability shown in the proof of (?, Theorem 5) for the case of full TGDs.

Let
\phi
be a Horn 3-CNF formula. We denote by
\mathit{CL}(\phi)
the set of clauses of
\phi
, and by
\mathit{PV}(\phi)
the set of propositional variables of
\phi
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be the following sets of facts:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=&\{U(x,y)\mid x\in\mathit{CL}(\phi),y\in\mathit{PV}(\phi)\}\,\cup\\
&\{P(x,y,z)\mid x,y\rightarrow z\in\mathit{CL}(\phi)\}\,\cup\\
&\{N(x,y,z)\mid x,y,z\rightarrow\bot\in\mathit{CL}(\phi)\}\\
\mathcal{D}=&\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{T(x),V(x)\mid x\in\mathit{PV}(\phi)\}\end{array}

It is crucial to observe that both
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
contain a fact
U(x,y)
if the
x
is a propositional variable belonging to a
unit clause
of
\phi
and
y
is
any
variable of
\phi
.
Then, let
\Sigma
be a set consisting of the following full+guarded dependencies:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}\forall x,y,z&(N(x,y,z)\land T(x)\land T(y)\land T(z)\rightarrow F)\\
\forall x,y,z&(P(x,y,z)\land T(x)\land T(y)\rightarrow T(z))\\
\forall x,y&(V(y)\land U(x,y)\rightarrow T(x))\\
\forall x&(T(x)\rightarrow V(x))\end{array}

We now show that
\phi
is unsatisfiable iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.

(\Rightarrow)

\phi
is unsatisfiable only if
\mathcal{D}
violates at least one of the first three dependencies. Then, starting from
\mathcal{D}
, we can build a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
as follows.

1.

For each violation of the first or second dependency, we choose to delete one
T
-fact in the image of the body of such dependency which causes the violation. Since this is not sufficient for achieving consistency (otherwise
\phi
would be satisfiable), we enter a loop, and eventually the third dependency will be violated. Then, go to the next step.

2.

If the third dependency is violated, we choose to delete all the
V
-facts. Then, because of the fourth dependency, we also have to remove all the
T
-facts.

The obtained repair coincides with
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

(\Leftarrow)
On the other hand, if
\phi
is satisfiable, then
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is not a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
because the database
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{T(x),V(x)\mid x\in\mathit{MM}(\phi)\}
, where
\mathit{MM}(\phi)
is the minimal model of
\phi
, is consistent with
\Sigma
.
∎

Furthermore, the results provided in (?) imply that, in the general case, repair checking is coNP-hard. We establish the same lower bound for the case of guarded+sticky dependencies.

Theorem 3
.

There exists a set of guarded+sticky dependencies for which repair checking is coNP-hard with respect to data complexity.

Proof.

We prove the thesis by showing a reduction from CNF SAT. The reduction is obtained by modifying the reduction from 2-QBF shown in Theorem 4.7 of (?).

Let
\Sigma
be a set consisting of the following guarded+sticky dependencies:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}\forall x,y,z,w&(R(x,y,z,w)\rightarrow\exists x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\,R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},z))\\
\forall x,y,z,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}&(R(x,0,y,z)\land R(x,1,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\land R_{\mathit{aux}}(x,y,z,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\rightarrow U(x,y,z,y^{\prime},z^{\prime}))\end{array}

Now let
\phi=\psi_{1}\land\ldots\land\psi_{n}
be a CNF formula, where every
\psi_{i}
is a clause. Let
\mathit{PV}(\phi)
be the set of propositional variables occurring in
\phi
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
be the following sets of facts:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=&\{R(a,1,i,(i\bmod n)+1)\mid a\textrm{ occurs positively in }\psi_{i}\}\,\cup\\
&\{R(a,0,i,(i\bmod n)+1)\mid a\textrm{ occurs negatively in }\psi_{i}\}\\
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}=&\{R_{\mathit{aux}}(v,i,j,(i\bmod n)+1,(j\bmod n)+1)\mid v\in\mathit{PV}(\phi)\text{\ and\ }i,j\in\{1,...,n\}\}\end{array}

and let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
.
We prove that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\phi
is unsatisfiable.

First, it is immediate to see that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
.
Now, due to the first dependency of
\Sigma
, if there exists
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
, then for every conjunct
\psi_{i}
there must exist at least one fact from
R
of the form
R(\_,\_,i,\_)
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime\prime}
. But, due to the second dependency of
\Sigma
, this is possible if and only if
\phi
is satisfiable. Consequently,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\phi
is unsatisfiable.
∎

Upper bounds

As shown in (?, Proposition 4), in the general case (actually, for «every finite set of first-order constraints»), repair checking is in coNP.
We show that the problem can be solved in PTIME if the set of dependencies is either linear or full, extending the same complexity result proved in (?, ?) for less expressive dependencies.
Moreover, we show that it is in
\mathrm{AC}^{0}
for acyclic sets of dependencies, as well as for full+linear and full+sticky dependencies.

In order to exploit the previous results for recoverability, we start by noticing the following property (whose proof is straightforward):

Proposition 7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of dependencies, and let
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be two databases such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Then,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
and, for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is not recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.

Consequently,
whenever deciding recoverability is in PTIME with respect to data complexity, also repair checking is in PTIME with respect to data complexity.

Thus, from Lemma
1
and Lemma
4
we can prove the following complexity result.

Theorem 4
.

Repair checking is in PTIME with respect to data complexity in the case of either linear or full dependencies.

We now focus on the case of acyclic dependencies, for which we prove what follows.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of acyclic dependencies, and let
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be two databases such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Then,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
and, for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is inconsistent with
\Sigma
.

Proof.

We prove that, if
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is not a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
, then there exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is consistent with
\Sigma
(the other direction of the proof is straightforward).
So let us assume that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is not a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
.

First, observe that, since
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is not a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
, then
there must exist an
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.

Then, let us stratify the set of predicates according to the dependencies: layer
1
contains predicates not occurring in the body of any dependency; layer
i
contains predicates occurring only in the body of dependencies whose head contains only predicates of layers lower than
i
.
Moreover, we say that a dependency
\tau
is of layer
i
if no predicate of layer greater than
i
occurs in the head of
\tau
.

Now, let
i
be the minimum layer such that there exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
and the predicate of
\alpha
belongs to layer
i
.
Let
\alpha
be such a fact whose predicate is of layer
i
and suppose that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is not consistent with
\Sigma
. Then, since
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
, it follows that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
violates a dependency of layer
i-1
.
Now observe that, for every layer
j
such that
j<i
, and for every
\beta\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\beta\}
is not consistent with
\Sigma
and the predicate of
\beta
belongs to layer
j
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\beta\}
is not recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.
This implies that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is not recoverable from
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
, giving rise to a contradiction. Thus,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\}
is consistent with
\Sigma
.
∎

We now show how to decide repair checking by means of an FO sentence. As done in Section
5
, we use an auxiliary predicate
\accentset{\ast}{p}
for every
p\in\textit{Pred}(\Sigma)
.

First, given any set
\Phi
of domain-independent FO sentences, we define the formula
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Phi)
as follows:

\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Phi)=\bigwedge_{\phi\in\Phi}\textit{aux}(\phi)

Clearly, the
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Phi)
evaluates to true in a database
\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D})
iff each
\phi\in\Phi
does. In particular, given a set of dependencies
\Sigma
, the evaluation of
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma)
over a database
\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D})
is true iff the latter is consistent with
\Sigma
.

Then, we define the auxiliary formula
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma,\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x}))
, where
\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x})
is an atom: it is obtained from
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma)
by replacing every atom
\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{t})

with the subformula
(\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{t})\vee\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{x})
.

Definition 9

(Repair checking sentence 1)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of acyclic dependencies. We define the formula
\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
as follows:

\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)=\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma)\land\bigwedge_{p\in\textit{Pred}(\Sigma)}\forall\mathbf{x}\,\big(p(\mathbf{x})\land\neg\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\neg\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma,\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x})\big)

where
\mathbf{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
if
m
is the arity of
p
.

Based on this definition and Proposition
8
, it is possible to prove the following property.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of acyclic dependencies, and let
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be two databases. Then, the sentence
\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in
\mathcal{D}\cup\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.

Proof.

First, it is immediate to verify that, for every instantiation
\sigma
of
p(\mathbf{x})
, the sentence
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma,\sigma(\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x})))
evaluates to true in
\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
if and only if
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in
\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\cup\{\sigma(\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{x}))\}
.
Then, from Proposition
8
and from the fact that
\Psi^{\mathit{cons}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in
\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is consistent with
\Sigma
, it follows that
\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in
\mathcal{D}\cup\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Repair checking is in
\mathrm{AC}^{0}
with respect to data complexity in the case of acyclic dependencies.

Example 5
.

IUCN is an organization studying the conservation status of species worldwide. If a taxon is a species (predicate
S
), and it has been described (predicate
D
) by IUCN, then we know its conservation status (predicate
C
). Moreover, our dataset contains the fact that both species Mammuthus primigenius and Elephas maximus have been described by IUCN, and that the first species is extinct.
This situation can be modeled through the following database and acyclic set of dependencies:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ }l}\mathcal{D}=\{&S(\mathsf{e}),D(\mathsf{e},\mathsf{iucn}),S(\mathsf{m}),D(\mathsf{m},\mathsf{iucn}),C(\mathsf{m},\mathsf{ex})\ \},\\
\Sigma=\{&\forall x\,(S(x)\land D(x,\mathsf{iucn})\rightarrow\exists y\,C(x,y))\ \}.\end{array}

Then, the sentence
\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
is equivalent to:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}l}\neg\exists x&\big(\accentset{\ast}{S}(x)\land\accentset{\ast}{D}(x,\mathsf{iucn})\land\neg\exists y\,\accentset{\ast}{C}(x,y)\big)\land\\
\forall x&\big(S(x)\land\neg\accentset{\ast}{S}(x)\rightarrow\exists x^{\prime}\,((\accentset{\ast}{S}(x^{\prime})\lor x^{\prime}=x)\land\accentset{\ast}{D}(x^{\prime},\mathsf{iucn})\land\neg\exists y^{\prime}\,\accentset{\ast}{C}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\big)\land\\
\forall x,y&\big(D(x,y)\land\neg\accentset{\ast}{D}(x,y)\rightarrow\exists x^{\prime}\,(\accentset{\ast}{S}(x^{\prime})\land(\accentset{\ast}{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\lor(x^{\prime}=x\land\mathsf{iucn}=y))\land\neg\exists y^{\prime}\,\accentset{\ast}{C}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\big)\land\\
\forall x,y&\big(C(x,y)\land\neg\accentset{\ast}{C}(x,y)\rightarrow\exists x^{\prime}\,(\accentset{\ast}{S}(x^{\prime})\land\accentset{\ast}{D}(x^{\prime},\mathsf{iucn})\land\neg\exists y^{\prime}\,(\accentset{\ast}{C}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\lor(x^{\prime}=x\land y^{\prime}=y)))\big).\end{array}

One can verify that
\mathsf{rep}_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{D})=\{\mathcal{D}\setminus\{S(\mathsf{e})\},\mathcal{D}\setminus\{D(\mathsf{e},\mathsf{iucn})\}\}
and that these are the only two possible subsets of
\mathcal{D}
such that
\Psi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in
\mathcal{D}\cup\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
.

\triangledown

Finally, we address the cases of full+sticky and full+linear dependencies. To this aim, we exploit the formulas for checking recoverability in case
\Sigma
is a set of CQ-FO-rewritable dependencies.
Indeed, we define the FO sentence
\Phi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
as follows:

Definition 10

(Repair checking sentence 2)
.

Given a CQ-FO-rewritable set of dependencies
\Sigma
, we define
\Phi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
as the FO sentence:

\Phi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)=\Phi^{\mathit{rec}}(\Sigma)\land\bigwedge_{p\in\textit{Pred}(\Sigma)}\neg\exists\mathbf{y}\,\big(p(\mathbf{y})\land\neg\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{y})\land\Phi^{\mathit{rec}}(\{\accentset{\ast}{p}(\mathbf{y})\},\Sigma)\big)

The intuition behind the formula is straightforward: we check if the database is recoverable (which is a necessary condition for it to be a repair) and no further atom not already present in it can be added while preserving its recoverability.

It is now possible to show that
\Phi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
constitutes a sound and complete FO-rewriting for the repair checking problem, i.e. such a formula is evaluated to true over the database
\mathcal{D}\cup\textit{aux}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
iff
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
.
Therefore, the following property holds.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a CQ-FO-rewritable set of full dependencies. For every pair of databases
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, deciding whether
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of

is in
\mathrm{AC}^{0}
with respect to data complexity.

Proof.

First, we state the following property, whose proof immediately follows from the definition of chase and the definition of repair.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of full dependencies, and let
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be two databases. Then,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a repair of
\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle
iff
\mathit{Chase}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\Sigma)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and, for every fact

,
\mathit{Chase}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cup\{\alpha\},\Sigma)\not\subseteq\mathcal{D}
.

Now, given a CQ-FO-rewritable set of full dependencies
\Sigma
, we can encode the conditions of the above lemma in terms of an FO sentence.
Indeed, Lemma
3
and Lemma

imply that, for every set of dependencies
\Sigma

\Phi^{\mathit{rc}}(\Sigma)
evaluates to true in

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

\langle\Sigma,\mathcal{D}\rangle

9
Conclusions

This article falls within the scope of research on consistent query answering. We employ a notion of repair based on tuple-deletion, and consistency of databases is checked with respect to disjunctive embedded dependencies with inequalities, a very expressive language for schema constraints. We studied different decision problems related to the notion of database repair under tuple-deletion semantics for the whole class of DED
≠
s, for the linear, acyclic, full, sticky and guarded subclasses, and for all the possible combinations thereof. We have shown tractability with respect to data complexity of the examined decision problems for several of such classes of dependencies.

Another very interesting future research direction of this work is extending our framework towards the combination of open-world assumption (OWA) and closed-world assumption (CWA). Many studies have proposed forms of combination of OWA and CWA in knowledge bases (see e.g. (?, ?, ?, ?, ?)). Specifically, for consistent query answering in existential rules the present paper has studied a “purely closed” approach, in which all predicates have a closed interpretation, while previous work has considered “open” approaches, as explained in the introduction. It would be very interesting to study a hybrid approach, in which the predicates can be partitioned into an “open” and a “closed” class.
This kind of combination of OWA and CWA is usually very challenging from the computational viewpoint, however both our results and the results for consistent query answering in existential rules under OWA presented in (?) (although for a more restricted language for existential rules than ours) are quite promising.

Finally, consistent query answering has important connections with the problem of
controlled query evaluation
(?), i.e. the problem of evaluating queries on a database (or knowledge base) in the presence of a logical specification of a privacy policy that should not be violated by the query answers. We are very interested in investigating the consequences of our results for such a problem.
In this context, handling partially-complete knowledge is of pivotal importance. As well-explained in (?, ?), there are several situations where, if an attacker is aware that the system has a complete knowledge over part of the domain, she can exploit this form of
meta-knowledge
to employ non-standard forms of reasoning for inferring sensitive information.

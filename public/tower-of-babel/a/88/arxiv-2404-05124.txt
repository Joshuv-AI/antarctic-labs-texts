Semistable reduction for complex analytic spaces
Makoto Enokizono, Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. Semistable reduction theorem for projective morphisms in the category of complex analytic spaces is established.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14M25, 14D05

1.
Introduction

Hironaka’s resolution theorem stands as a cornerstone in both algebraic and analytic geometry.
Semistable reduction theorem, which guarantees
the existence of relative resolutions for morphisms between varieties, is also crucial.
In essence, the semistable reduction problem seeks to find an alteration
B^{\prime}\to B
and a modification
X^{\prime}\to X\times_{B}B^{\prime}
from smooth varieties for a given surjective morphism
f\colon X\to B
between varieties such that the induced morphism
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
has fibers with mild singularities.
For the case where
\dim B=1
, the semistable reduction theorem was initially established in
[
17
]
for both algebraic and analytic varieties.
For higher dimensional bases
B
, Abramovich and Karu
[
2
]
proved a weakly semistable reduction theorem within the category of algebraic varieties in characteristic
0
.
They also showed the full semistable reduction theorem can be deduced from a purely combinatorial conjecture known as the polyhedral conjecture.
Recently, Adiprasito, Liu and Temkin
[
4
]
proved the polyhedral conjecture and hence the semistable reduction theorem holds in the category of algebraic varieties in characteristic
0
.
In this paper, we prove the semistable reduction theorem in the category of complex analytic varieties:

Theorem 1.1

(Simplified version of Theorem
4.7
, see also Remark
2.3
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a projective surjective morphism between complex analytic varieties.
Assume that
B
is projective over a Stein space and fix any compact subset
K
of
B
.
Then after shrinking
B
to an open neighbourhood of
K
, there exist a projective alterations
B^{\prime}\to B
from a smooth variety
B^{\prime}
, a projective modification
X^{\prime}\to X\times_{B}B^{\prime}
from a smooth variety
X^{\prime}
onto the main component of
X\times_{B}B^{\prime}
such that the projection
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is semistable.

Here a morphism
f\colon X\to B
between smooth varieties is said to be
semistable
(with respect to
\Delta_{X}
and
\Delta_{B}
) if there exist reduced divisors
\Delta_{X}\subseteq X
and
\Delta_{B}\subseteq B
with simple normal crossing supports such that
f
can locally be written as

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}),\quad t_{i}=\prod_{j=l_{i-1}+1}^{l_{i}}x_{j}

for some
0=l_{0}<l_{1}<\cdots<l_{m}\leq n
,
where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
are respectively local coordinates on
X
and
B
such that
\Delta_{X}=\{x_{1}\cdots x_{l}=0\}
and
\Delta_{B}=\{t_{1}\cdots t_{k}=0\}
for some
k
and
l\geq l_{k}
satisfying
l_{i+1}=l_{i}+1
for
i\geq k
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
heavily relies on the arguments in
[
2
]
and employs toroidal geometry.
Building upon the results of
[
4
]
, the semistable reduction problem is reduced to the toroidal reduction problem for morphisms.
By the argument of
[
2
]
, it suffices to establish the semistable reduction theorem for morphisms of relative dimension one, which is the complex analytic counterpart of de Jong’s results
[
9
]
.
However, there are some obstacles to discussing in the complex analytic category.
Indeed, de Jong obtained the semistable reduction for families of curves
f\colon X\to B
by resolving the indeterminacy of a certain moduli map
B^{\prime}\dasharrow M
, where
M
is a fine moduli space of curves with additional structure and
B^{\prime}\to B
an alteration such that the base change of
f
to some open subspace of
B^{\prime}
admits the moduli map.
However, in the complex analytic category, there does not always exist a resolution of indeterminacy (for instance, the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}
is not extended to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
).
To avoid this difficulty, we propose an alternate approach.
Let
f\colon X\to B
be a projective surjective morphism between complex analytic varieties
and assume for simplicity that all irreducible components of all fibers of
f
are curves of geometric genus at least
2
.
We first take a resolution of singularities
B^{\prime}\to B
, ensuring that the discriminant locus of the base change of
f
to
B^{\prime}
is simple normal crossings.
Next we directly construct a “semistable reduction in codimension one”.
Consequently, we may assume that
f\colon X\to B
defines a family of stable curves over some Zariski open subspace
U\subseteq B
the complement of which has codimension at least
2
and the discriminant locus of
f
is simple normal crossings.
Finally, we apply the extension theorem of de Jong–Oort
[
10
]
to obtain a family of stable curves
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to B
, which is a desired semistable reduction.
Notably, our proof circumvents the use of the moduli spaces of curves and morphisms.
After the paper was completed, Dan Abramovich taught the authors a reduction of Theorem
1.1
to the algebraic case.
For details, see Subsection
4.5
.

The shrinking assumption and the projectivity assumption in Theorem
1.1
are necessary for technical reasons.
Hence the following question naturally arises:

Question 1.2
.

Can the semistable reduction theorem (Theorem
4.7
) and the toroidal reduction theorem (Theorem
4.1
) be established without assuming the shrinking or the projectivity condition, while allowing for base changes by infinite discrete coverings?

This question leads a more natural question:

Question 1.3
.

Can the semistable reduction theorem (Theorem
4.7
) and the toroidal reduction theorem (Theorem
4.1
) be extended functorially?

In the category of algebraic varieties (or more broadly, Noetherian schemes) in characteristic
0
,
the functorial semistable and toroidal reduction problem have been developed (e.g.,
[
19
]
,
[
3
]
).
Other developments in a wide category containing complex analytic varieties are explored in
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
.

The contents of this paper are as follows:
Section
2
provides some notions on toroidal geometry and finite coverings of complex analytic spaces used in this paper.
In Section
3
, we prove the semistable reduction theorem for families of curves in the complex analytic setting.
In Section
4
, we prove the main results in this paper.
We also give a reduction of Theorem
1.1
to the algebraic case.

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper,
complex analytic spaces
are always assumed to be Hausdorff and second countable.

Analytic varieties
are reduced and irreducible complex analytic spaces.
For the notations and definitions, see
[
12
]
(see also
[
11
]
).

Definition 2.1

(Projective morphism)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper morphism of complex analytic spaces and
\mathcal{L}
an invertible
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module on
X
.
Then
\mathcal{L}
on
X
is said to be
relatively very ample over
Y
if the natural map
f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
is surjective and the induced morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}_{Y}(f_{*}\mathcal{L})
is a closed immersion.
The invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
on
X
is called
relatively ample over
Y
if for any point
y\in Y
, there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
y
and a positive integer
m
such that the restriction
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}|_{f^{-1}(U)}
is relatively very ample over
U
.
The morphism
f
is said to be
projective
if there exists a relatively ample sheaf on
X
over
Y
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a projective morphism of complex analytic spaces and assume that
B
is projective over a Stein space
S
.
Let
W
be any Stein compact subset of
S
.
Then after shrinking
S
around
W
, there is a closed immersion
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}\times B
over
B
for some
N
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a relatively ample sheaf on
X
over
B
.
Since
W
is compact and
B
is projective over
S
, there exists a positive integer
m
such that
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}
is relatively very ample over
B
after shrinking
S
around
W
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a relatively ample sheaf on
B
over
S
.
By the compactness of
W
and
[
5
, Chapter IV, §2, Theorem 2.1]
, there exists a positive integer
n
such that the natural map

\pi^{*}\pi_{*}(f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\to f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}

is surjective along
\pi^{-1}(W)
, where
\pi\colon B\to S
is the structure morphism.
Since
S
is Stein, the coherent sheaf
\pi_{*}(f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})
is globally generated by Cartan’s Theorem A.
Hence
f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
is globally generated after shrinking
S
around
W
.
Since
\pi^{-1}(W)
is compact and
f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
is coherent, there exists a finite dimensional vector subspace
V
of
H^{0}(B,f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})
such that

V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}\to f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}

is surjective after shrinking
S
around
W
.
Thus the composition

X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{B}(f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})=\mathbb{P}_{B}(f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)\times B

is a desired closed immersion.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Shrinking assumption)
.

In this paper, we consider the situation that the base space
B
is projective over a Stein space
S
and fix any Stein compact subset
W
of
S
.
Almost all results in this paper are of the form that after shrinking
S
around
W
, the given statement holds.
The arguments of this form lead to the arguments of the form that after shrinking
B
around any compact subset
K
of
B
, the given statement holds.
Indeed, let
\pi\colon B\to S
be the structure morphism.
Then
\pi(K)
is also compact.
We take the holomorphically convex hull
W
of
\pi(K)
.
Then
W
is a Stein compact subset of
S
from
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
and
K\subseteq\pi^{-1}(W)
.

2.1.
Toroidal geometry

In this subsection, we recall the notion of toroidal embeddings introduced in
[
17
]
with reference to
[
2
, Section 1]
.

Definition 2.4

(Toroidal pair)
.

Let
X
be a normal analytic variety and
U\subseteq X
a Zariski open subset.
Then the inclusion
U\subseteq X
is called a
toroidal embedding

if for any point
x\in X
, there exist a germ of a toric variety
t\in X_{\sigma}

and an isomorphism
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}\cong\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\sigma},t}
of complete local
\mathbb{C}
-algebras which sends the reduced ideal sheaf of
X\setminus U
in
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
to that of
X_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma}
in
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\sigma},t}
,
where we write
X_{\sigma}
as an affine toric variety associated to a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma
with the open dense torus
T_{\sigma}
.
We call the germ
t\in X_{\sigma}
a
local model at
x
.
Replacing
X_{\sigma}
by a neighbourhood of
t
if necessary, we may assume that the orbit of
t
is the unique closed orbit in
X_{\sigma}
and hence we can replace
t
with the distinguished point
x_{\sigma}
.
Let
\Delta
be the complement of
U
in
X
.
Then
\Delta
is a reduced divisor since
X_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma}
is the sum of torus-invariant divisors for any local model
t\in X_{\sigma}
.
The pair
(X,\Delta)
is called a
toroidal pair
.
A toroidal pair
(X,\Delta)
is
strict
(or
without self-intersection
) if each irreducible component of
\Delta
is normal.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the boundary divisor
\Delta
may have infinite irreducible components in general.
However, from the next section onwards, we will only deal with the case of finitely many irreducible components since we shrink the base Stein space
S
around any Stein compact subset
W
.

Definition 2.6

(Strata)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a strict toroidal pair and
\Delta=\sum_{i\in I}E_{i}
the irreducible decomposition. Then all the connected components
Y_{\lambda}
of
\cap_{j\in J}E_{j}\setminus\cup_{i\in I\setminus J}E_{i}
for each subset
J\subseteq I
form a stratification
X=\sqcup_{\lambda}Y_{\lambda}
.
For a stratum
Y=Y_{\lambda}
, we define

\mathrm{Star}(Y)
as the union of strata
Z
such that the closure of
Z
contains
Y
, which is Zariski open in
X
and contains
Y
as an analytic subset.
Let
M^{Y}
denote the group of Cartier divisors on
\mathrm{Star}(Y)
whose supports are contained in
\Delta
, and put
M^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}:=M^{Y}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
,
N^{Y}:=\Hom(M^{Y},\mathbb{Z})
and
N^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}:=N^{Y}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
Let
M^{Y}_{+}
denote the submonoid of
M^{Y}
generated by effective divisors and put

\sigma^{Y}:=(M^{Y}_{+})^{\vee}=\{\gamma\in N^{Y}_{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ \langle D,\gamma\rangle\geq 0\text{ for any $D\in M^{Y}_{+}$}\}.

According to
[
17
, Corollary 1, p.61]
, for any local model
x_{\sigma}\in X_{\sigma}
at a point
x\in Y
where
\sigma
is the cone in
N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
, there are natural identifications

M^{Y}\cong M_{\sigma}/\sigma^{\bot}\cap M_{\sigma},\quad N^{Y}\cong N_{\sigma}\cap\mathrm{Span}(\sigma),\quad\sigma^{Y}\cong\sigma.

Definition 2.7

(Toroidal morphism)
.

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})
and
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
be toroidal pairs.
Then a dominant morphism
f\colon X\to Y
is
toroidal
if
for any point
x\in X
, there exist a local model
s\in X_{\sigma}
at
x
, a local model
t\in X_{\tau}
at
f(x)
and a toric morphism
g\colon X_{\sigma}\to X_{\tau}
which maps
s
to
t
such that the formal isomorphisms
(x\in X)\cong(s\in X_{\sigma})
and
(f(x)\in Y)\cong(t\in X_{\tau})
are compatible with
f
and
g
.

The following results are useful.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
2
, Proposition 1.5]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a toroidal morphism and
x
a point of
X
.
Then for any local model
x_{\tau}\in X_{\tau}
at
f(x)
, there exist a local model
x_{\sigma}\in X_{\sigma}
at
x
and a toric morphism
g\colon X_{\sigma}\to X_{\tau}

which maps
x_{\sigma}
to
x_{\tau}
such that the formal isomorphisms
(x\in X)\cong(x_{\sigma}\in X_{\sigma})
and
(f(x)\in Y)\cong(x_{\tau}\in X_{\tau})
are compatible with
f
and
g
.

Corollary 2.9

(
[
2
, Corollary 1.6]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
be toroidal morphisms.
Then
g\circ f\colon X\to Z
is also toroidal.

Definition 2.10

(Rational conical polyhedral complex)
.

A
rational conical polyhedral complex
is the data
\Sigma=(|\Sigma|,\{(\sigma,M_{\sigma})\})
as follows:

(1)

|\Sigma|
is a topological space.

(2)

\{(\sigma,M_{\sigma})\}
is a set of pairs
(\sigma,M_{\sigma})
such that
\sigma
is a subset of
|\Sigma|
, which is called a
cone in
\Sigma
, and
M_{\sigma}
is a finitely generated subgroup of the vector space of continuous
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions on
\sigma
, which is called the
lattice of functions on
\sigma
, satisfying as follows:

(a)

For any
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{\sigma}}
of
M_{\sigma}
,
the continuous map

\varphi_{\sigma}:=(f_{1}\ldots,f_{n_{\sigma}})\colon\sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{\sigma}}

is a homeomorphism onto the image
\sigma^{\prime}:=\varphi_{\sigma}(\sigma)
which is a full-dimensional strongly convex rational polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\sigma}}
.
Moreover, for any face
\tau^{\prime}\preceq\sigma^{\prime}
, there exists a pair
(\tau,M_{\tau})
such that
\tau=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}(\tau^{\prime})
.
In this case, we denote by
\tau\preceq\sigma
and
\tau
is called a
face of
\sigma
.

(b)

For any
\tau\preceq\sigma
, the group
M_{\tau}
coincides with the set of restricted functions
f|_{\tau}\colon\tau\to\mathbb{R}
for
f\in M_{\sigma}
.
In particular, there is a natural surjective homomorphism
M_{\sigma}\to M_{\tau}
.

(c)

|\Sigma|
is the union of cones
\sigma
and the intersection
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
for any cones
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
is a finite union of faces of both
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
.

Put
N_{\sigma}:=\Hom(M_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
and call it the
lattice of
\sigma
.
Note that the cone
\sigma
naturally embeds into
N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}:=N_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{R}
by the composite map
\sigma\cong\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{\sigma}}\cong N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
, where the last map is determined by the basis
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{\sigma}}
.
This embedding
\sigma\subseteq N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
does not depend on the choice of the basis
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n_{\sigma}}
.
The condition (b) is equivalent to
N_{\tau}=N_{\sigma}\cap\mathrm{Span}(\tau)\subseteq N_{\sigma}
.
Hence we can describe
\Sigma
as the pairs
(\sigma,N_{\sigma})
with
\sigma\subseteq N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
satisfying certain conditions instead of the pairs
(\sigma,M_{\sigma})
.

Example 2.11
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a toric variety associated to a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the associated fan
\Sigma
can be considered as a rational conical polyhedral complex as

|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma,\quad N_{\sigma}=N\cap\mathrm{Span}(\sigma),\quad M_{\sigma}=\Hom(N_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\cong M/\sigma^{\bot}\cap M.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
17
, p.71]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a strict toroidal pair.
Then the data

\Sigma_{(X,\Delta)}:=(|\Sigma_{(X,\Delta)}|,\{(\sigma^{Y},M^{Y})\}_{Y})

is a rational conical polyhedral complex, where
Y
runs through all the strata of
(X,\Delta)
,
the restriction map
M^{Y}\to M^{Z};\ D\mapsto D|_{\mathrm{Star}(Z)}
is surjective for any strata
Y
and
Z
with
Y\subseteq\overline{Z}
and

|\Sigma_{(X,\Delta)}|
is the gluing of
\sigma^{Y}
for all strata
Y
.

Definition 2.13

(Morphism of polyhedral complexes)
.

Let
\Sigma=(|\Sigma|,\{(\sigma,M_{\sigma})\})
and
\Sigma^{\prime}=(|\Sigma^{\prime}|,\{(\sigma^{\prime},M_{\sigma^{\prime}})\})
be rational conical polyhedral complexes.
We define a
morphism of rational conical polyhedral complexes

\varphi\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
as a continuous map
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to|\Sigma^{\prime}|
satisfying that for any cone
\sigma
in
\Sigma
, there exists a cone
\sigma^{\prime}
in
\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
and the composite function
f\circ\varphi|_{\sigma}\colon\sigma\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
M_{\sigma}
for each
f\in M_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
Note that in this case, the dual
\varphi_{\sigma}\colon N_{\sigma}\to N_{\sigma^{\prime}}
of the induced homomorphism
M_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to M_{\sigma}
satisfies

\varphi_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}|_{\sigma}=\varphi|_{\sigma}
as a map from
\sigma
to
\sigma^{\prime}
.
If, moreover,
|\Sigma^{\prime}|=|\Sigma|
holds and the continuous map
\varphi
and all homomorphisms between lattices are identity maps, then
\Sigma^{\prime}
is called a
subdivision of
\Sigma
.

For a toroidal morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between strict toroidal pairs
(X,\Delta_{X})
and
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
, the induced morphism
\varphi_{f}\colon\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}\to\Sigma_{(Y,\Delta_{Y})}
of the associated polyhedral complexes can be defined as follows (cf.
[
2
, Lemma 1.8]
):
Let
x\in X
be a point,
x_{\sigma}\in X_{\sigma}
and
x_{\tau}\in X_{\tau}
local models at
x
and
f(x)
, and
g\colon X_{\sigma}\to X_{\tau}
a toric morphism compatible with their local models.
Let
Z\subseteq X
and
W\subseteq Y
denote the strata containing
x
and
f(x)
, respectively.
Since the toric morphism
g
sends the orbit of
x_{\sigma}
into the orbit of
x_{\tau}
, we have
f(Z)\subseteq W
and hence
f(\mathrm{Star}(Z))\subseteq\mathrm{Star}(W)
.
Then we can define the homomorphism
f^{*}\colon M^{W}\to M^{Z}
of lattices by the pullback of Cartier divisors, which is compatible with
g^{*}\colon M_{\tau}\to M_{\sigma}
.
It induces the maps
f^{*}\colon M_{+}^{W}\to M_{+}^{Z}
and
\varphi^{Z}=(f^{*})^{\vee}\colon\sigma^{Z}\to\sigma^{W}
.
These maps
\{\varphi^{Z}\}_{Z}
are glued together to define the continuous map
\varphi_{f}\colon|\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}|\to|\Sigma_{(Y,\Delta_{Y})}|
,
which defines a morphism of their polyhedral complexes.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})
be a strict toroidal pair.
Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the isomorphism classes of proper toroidal morphisms
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
from a toroidal pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})
with
X^{\prime}\setminus\Delta_{X^{\prime}}\cong X\setminus\Delta_{X}

and subdivisions
\Sigma^{\prime}
of the associated polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper toroidal morphism from a toroidal pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})
satisfying
X^{\prime}\setminus\Delta_{X^{\prime}}\cong X\setminus\Delta_{X}
.
Note that
(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})
is automatically strict since so is
(X,\Delta_{X})
and each exceptional prime divisor of
f
is normal which is due to the toric case. Let
\varphi_{f}\colon\Sigma_{(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})}\to\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
denote the associated morphism of polyhedral complexes.
For any cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
with
\varphi_{f}(\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq\sigma
, the corresponding toric morphism
g\colon X_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to X_{\sigma}
between local models is étale over the torus of
X_{\sigma}
by the assumption
X^{\prime}\setminus\Delta_{X^{\prime}}\cong X\setminus\Delta_{X}
.
It follows that
N_{\sigma^{\prime},\mathbb{R}}=N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
and hence
\varphi_{f}
is injective.
Since
f
is proper,
\varphi_{f}
is surjective. Thus
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma_{(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})}
is a subdivision of
\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a subdivision of
\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
.
For each cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{Y}\in\Sigma_{(X,\Delta_{X})}
with
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma^{Y}
, we define an analytic variety

X_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}:=\mathrm{Specan}_{\mathrm{Star}(Y)}\left(\sum_{D\in\sigma^{\prime\vee}\cap M^{Y}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Star}(Y)}(-D)\right).

Let
f_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}\colon X_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}\to\mathrm{Star}(Y)
be the natural projection and put
\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}:=f_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}^{-1}(\Delta_{X})
.
One can easily check that these data
\{f_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}\colon X_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}\to\mathrm{Star}(Y),\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime},Y}\}_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{Y}}
are glued together to a proper toroidal morphism

from a strict toroidal variety
(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})
with
\Sigma_{(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})}=\Sigma^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 2.16

(Resolution, projective subdivision and alteration)

Definition 2.22

(Toroidal action and Pre-toroidal action
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a toroidal pair.
Let
\rho\colon G\curvearrowright(X,\Delta)
be a faithful finite group action on the pair
(X,\Delta)
, that is, a finite group
G
acts on

faithfully such that
\Delta
is
G
-invariant.

(1)
\rho
is

at

and a group homomorphism
\varphi\colon G_{x}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)\to\mathrm{Stab}_{T_{\sigma}}(x_{\sigma})
from the stabilizer subgroup of

is

-equivariant.

.
In this case, its quotient
(X/G,\Delta/G)
is also a toroidal pair since the quotient

is toric.

(2)
\rho
is

if there exist a germ of a toric variety
x_{\sigma}\in X_{\sigma}
, a group homomorphism
\varphi\colon G_{x}\to\mathrm{Stab}_{T_{\sigma}}(x_{\sigma})
, a non-trivial character
\psi
of
G_{x}
and a
G_{x}
-equivariant formal isomorphism

(x\in X,\Delta)\cong((x_{\sigma},0)\in X_{\sigma}\times\mathbb{C},(X_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma})\times\mathbb{C}),

where the action of
G_{x}
on

is defined by the diagonal action
(\varphi,\psi)
.
Note that the character
\psi
depends only on the germ

.
We say that

is

if it is toroidal or pre-toroidal for any

.

(3) Assume that
\rho
is pre-toroidal.
Then the

\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
of

is defined as follows:
For any

, let
\psi_{x}
define the character

of

Since

is abelian, the stalk

2.2.
Abhyankar’s lemma

In this subsection, we will prove an analytic version of Abhyankar’s lemma which is used later and discuss finite coverings of analytic varieties.

Lemma 2.24

(Uniqueness of finite coverings)
.

Let
X
be a normal analytic variety of dimension
n
and
Y\subseteq X
a non-empty Zariski open subspace.
Let
\pi_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X
,
i=1,2
be finite surjective morphisms from normal analytic varieties.
Let
\varphi\colon\pi_{1}^{-1}(Y)\cong\pi_{2}^{-1}(Y)
be an isomorphism over
Y
.
Then it extends uniquely an isomorphism
X_{1}\cong X_{2}
over
X
.

Proof.

Let
Y_{i}:=\pi_{i}^{-1}(Y)
.
We first show that the map
\varphi\colon X_{1}\dasharrow X_{2}
is bimeromorphic.
Let
\Gamma\subseteq Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
be the graph of
\varphi
and show that the closure of
\Gamma
in
X_{1}\times X_{2}
is an analytic subset.
Note that
\Gamma
is an irreducible component of
Y_{1}\times_{Y}Y_{2}
of dimension
n
and
Y_{1}\times_{Y}Y_{2}\subseteq X_{1}\times_{X}X_{2}
is Zariski open whose complement has dimension less than
n
.
Thus applying Remmert–Stein’s theorem (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4.6]
), the closure
\overline{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
in
X_{1}\times_{X}X_{2}
is an analytic subset. Let
X_{3}
denote the normalization of
\overline{\Gamma}
.
Then the decompositions
X_{3}\to X_{i}\to X
,
i=1,2
are both the Stein factorizations.
Hence the uniqueness of the Stein factorization says that
X_{1}
is isomorphic to
X_{2}
over
X
which is an extension of
\varphi
.
∎

The following is an analytic version of Abhyankar’s lemma (cf.
[
13
]
).

Proposition 2.25

(Analytic version of Abhyankar’s lemma)
.

Let
X
be a smooth analytic variety of dimension
n
,
\Delta
a reduced normal crossing divisor on
X
and
Y:=X\setminus\Delta
.
Let
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be a finite étale covering.
Then there exist a toroidal pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
with
X^{\prime}\setminus\Delta^{\prime}\cong Y^{\prime}
and a finite toroidal morphism
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
compatible with
\pi
such that
the following hold:

(i) If
\Delta
is a smooth divisor, then
X^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are smooth.

(ii) If
\pi
is Galois with the Galois group
G
, then so is
X^{\prime}\to X
and the action of
G
on
X^{\prime}
is toroidal.

Proof.

For any point
p\in X
, we take an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq X
such that
U\cong\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the unit open disc in
\mathbb{C}
.
We may assume that
p
is the origin and
\Delta\cap U
is defined by
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k}=0
for some
0\leq k\leq n
.
Let

V:=U\setminus\Delta\cong\mathbb{D}^{*k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k},\quad\mathbb{D}^{*}:=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}

and consider any connected component
V^{\prime}
of
\pi^{-1}(V)
.
The restriction map
V^{\prime}\to V
of
\pi
is also finite étale.
Let
\widetilde{V}
denote the universal cover of
V
.
Then we have the decomposition
\widetilde{V}\to V^{\prime}\to V
.
Note that the exponential map
\bm{e}\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}^{*};z\mapsto e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}
gives the universal covering of
\mathbb{D}^{*}
with the covering transformation group
\mathbb{Z}
which naturally extends to the universal covering
\bm{e}\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Thus the covering
\widetilde{V}\to V
is isomorphic to

\bm{e}^{k}\times\mathrm{id}\colon\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{D}^{*k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}

with the covering transformation group
\pi_{1}(V)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
which naturally extends to

\bm{e}^{k}\times\mathrm{id}\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{C}^{*k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k}.

Since
\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})
is a subgroup of the abelian group
\pi_{1}(V)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, the covering
V^{\prime}\to V
is Galois with the finite Galois group
\pi_{1}(V)/\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})
.
Note that
\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})
is (abstractly) isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and so
V^{\prime}\cong\widetilde{V}/\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{*k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}
.
Now we regard
V
as an open subspace of the toric variety
X_{\sigma,N}=\mathbb{C}^{*k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
with the same fundamental group,
where
N:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the lattice and the cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
is generated by
e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}
, where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is the standard basis of
N
.
Let
N_{\sigma}
be the sublattice of
N
spanned by
\sigma
, which has the basis
e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}
.
By
[
8
, Proposition 12.1.9]
, we have a natural isomorphism
\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma,N})\cong N/N_{\sigma}
induced by the natural identification
\pi_{1}(T_{N})\cong N
.
The subgroup
\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})\subseteq\pi_{1}(V)=\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma,N})
corresponds to an intermediate lattice
N_{\sigma}\subseteq N^{\prime}\subseteq N
with
\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})\cong N^{\prime}/N_{\sigma}
.
The inclusion
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
induces the toric morphism
X_{\sigma,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\sigma,N}
which is finite étale and corresponds to the inclusion
\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma,N^{\prime}})=N^{\prime}/N_{\sigma}\subseteq\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma,N})=N/N_{\sigma}
.
Thus
\widetilde{V}\to V^{\prime}\to V
extends to the sequence of covering maps

\bm{e}^{k}\times\mathrm{id}\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k}\to X_{\sigma,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\sigma,N}.

Let
\tau
denote the cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
and
\sigma
.
Then the inclusion
V=U\setminus\Delta\subseteq X_{\sigma,N}
extends to
U\subseteq X_{\tau,N}=\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
.
Moreover, the finite étale morphism
X_{\sigma,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\sigma,N}
naturally extends to a finite toric morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon X_{\tau,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\tau,N}
.
Note that
\tau
is simplicial in
N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and smooth if
k=0
or
k=1
.
Let
U^{\prime}:=\pi^{\prime-1}(U)
.
Then
V^{\prime}
is a Zariski open subspace in
U^{\prime}
and the restriction map
U^{\prime}\to U
is an extension of the covering
V^{\prime}\to V
.
The pair
(U^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{U^{\prime}}:=U^{\prime}\setminus V^{\prime})
is toroidal and
\pi^{\prime}\colon(U^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{U^{\prime}})\to(U,\Delta|_{U})
is a toroidal morphism since it is the restriction of the toroidal embedding
X_{\sigma,N^{\prime}}\subseteq X_{\tau,N^{\prime}}
and
\pi^{\prime}\colon X_{\tau,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\tau,N}
is a toric morphism.
By Lemma
2.24
, the above
(U^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{U^{\prime}})
constructed for each
p\in U\subseteq X
and
V^{\prime}
can naturally glued to obtain a toroidal pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
with
X^{\prime}\setminus\Delta^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}
and a finite toroidal morphism
\pi\colon(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
which is an extension of
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
.

If
\Delta
is smooth, then
k=0
or
k=1
for the above proof and hence
X^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are smooth by construction, whence (i) holds.

If
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
is Galois with the Galois group
G
, then
V^{\prime}\to V
as above is a
G_{V^{\prime}}
-covering, where
G_{V^{\prime}}
is the stabilizer group of the connected component
V^{\prime}
.
Then
U^{\prime}\to U
is also a
G_{V^{\prime}}
-covering and
\pi^{-1}(U)\to U
becomes a
G
-covering (this can also be seen by Lemma
2.24
).
For any
x\in X^{\prime}
, we put
p:=\pi(x)\in X
and take
U
and
V^{\prime}
as above such that
x\in U^{\prime}
.
Then the stabilizer group
G_{x}
at
x
equals to
G_{V^{\prime}}\cong\pi_{1}(V)/\pi_{1}(V^{\prime})\cong N/N^{\prime}
.
Hence the action of
G_{x}
on the germ
(x\in X^{\prime})\cong(x_{\tau}\in X_{\tau,N^{\prime}})
factors through the torus action
T_{N^{\prime}}
on
X_{\tau,N^{\prime}}
, whence (ii) holds.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain the following Grauert–Remmert’s theorem.

Corollary 2.26

(Extension of finite coverings
[

)
.

Let
X
be a normal analytic variety and
Y\subseteq X
a non-empty Zariski open subspace.
Let
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be a finite covering from a normal analytic variety
Y^{\prime}
.
Assume that the branch locus
B_{Y}
of
\pi
extends to an analytic subset
B_{X}
of
X
.
Then there exists a finite covering
X^{\prime}\to X
from a normal analytic variety
X^{\prime}
which is an extension of
\pi
.

Proof.

We take a resolution
\widetilde{X}\to X
of
X
such that the inverse image of the union of
X\setminus Y
and
B_{X}
has simple normal crossing support, which is denoted by
\Delta
.
Applying Proposition
2.25
to
(\widetilde{X},\Delta)
and the normalized base change
\widetilde{Y^{\prime}}\to\widetilde{Y}\subseteq\widetilde{X}
of
Y^{\prime}\to Y\subseteq X
,
we have a finite covering
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}\to\widetilde{X}
which is compatible with
\pi
.
Taking the Stein factorization
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}\to X
of the composite map
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}\to\widetilde{X}\to X
, we have the desired morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
.
∎

Lemma 2.27

(Existence of the Galois closure)
.

Let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a finite covering between normal analytic varieties.
Then there exists a finite Galois covering
\varphi\colon X^{\prime\prime}\to X
from a normal analytic variety
X^{\prime\prime}
which factors through
\pi
which is universal in the following sense:
For any finite Galois covering
\psi\colon Y\to X
from a normal analytic variety which factors through
\pi
, there exists uniquely a Galois covering
Y\to X^{\prime\prime}
over
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.26
and Lemma
2.24
, we may assume that
X
is smooth and
\pi
is étale.
Let
\Pi:=\pi_{1}(X)
and
\Pi^{\prime}:=\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
.
Then
\pi
corresponds to the inclusion
\pi_{*}\colon\Pi^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\Pi
.
Let
\Pi^{\prime\prime}:=\cap_{\gamma\in\Pi}\gamma\Pi^{\prime}\gamma^{-1}
denote the maximal normal subgroup of
\Pi
contained in
\Pi^{\prime}
.
By the Galois theory of covering maps, the desired variety
X^{\prime\prime}
can be obtained by the étale quotient
X^{\prime\prime}:=\widetilde{X}/\Pi^{\prime\prime}
of the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
by the action of
\Pi^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.28

(Existence of Galois alterations)
.

Let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be an alteration between normal analytic varieties.
Then there exists a Galois alteration
\varphi\colon X^{\prime\prime}\to X
which factors through
\pi
.

Proof.

We take the Stein factorization
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}X
of
\pi
.
By Lemma
2.27
, there exists the Galois closure
\varphi^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime\prime}\to X
of
\pi^{\prime}
.
Let
G
denote the Galois group of
\varphi^{\prime}
.
For each
g\in G
, we define a meromorphic map
\psi_{g}\colon Y^{\prime\prime}\dasharrow X^{\prime}
by the composite of meromorphic maps

Y^{\prime\prime}\xrightarrow{g}Y^{\prime\prime}\to Y^{\prime}\leftarrow X^{\prime}.

The product
\prod_{g\in G}\psi_{g}\colon Y^{\prime\prime}\dasharrow\prod_{g\in G}X^{\prime}
is also a meromorphic map.
Let
\overline{\Gamma}
denote the closure of the graph
\Gamma
of
\prod_{g\in G}\psi_{g}
in
Y^{\prime\prime}\times\prod_{g\in G}X^{\prime}
.
Then
G
acts on
\overline{\Gamma}
which is equivariant with respect to the projection
\overline{\Gamma}\to Y^{\prime\prime}
.
Indeed, we define the action by
g\cdot(x,(x_{h})_{h\in G}):=(gx,(x_{hg})_{h\in G})
for
g\in G
and
(x,(x_{h})_{h\in G})\in\overline{\Gamma}
.
Let
X^{\prime\prime}
denote the normalization of
\overline{\Gamma}
.
Then
G
acts on

4.1.

Theorem 4.1

(Toroidal reduction)
.

Let

be a proper analytic subset of

of

(B^{\prime},\Delta_{B^{\prime}})

(X^{\prime},\Delta_{X^{\prime}})

For the readers’ convenience, we prove this.

Let

In the case where

Replacing

by its projective modification, we may assume that

From Lemma

, We can take a Galois alteration

which factors through

and denote

Let

denote the normalization of the base change

Then

\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}/G

.
We take a projective resolution

around

by

By Lemma

over

around

we may assume that it defines a morphism

Let

Applying Theorem
3.1
to the morphism

and

around

,

,

4.3.
Weakly semistable reduction

Lemma 4.4

(cf.
[
2
, Proposition 5.10]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon\Sigma\to T
be a morphism of rational conical polyhedral complexes.
Then there exists a projective alteration
T^{\prime}\to T
such that the induced morphism
\Sigma\times_{T}T^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
is weakly semistable.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, replacing
\varphi
with its base change to a projective resolution of
T
,
we may assume that
\varphi
is equidimensional.
For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the image
\varphi(\sigma)
is a cone in
T
and the image of the lattice homomorphism is a sublattice
\varphi_{\sigma}(N_{\sigma})\subseteq N_{\varphi(\sigma)}
.
We define a rational conical polyhedral complex
T^{\prime}
by the data
\{(\tau,N^{\prime}_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in T}
, where the lattice
N^{\prime}_{\tau}
is defined by the intersection of
\varphi_{\sigma}(N_{\sigma})
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
with
\tau=\varphi(\sigma)
.
Then the natural morphism
T^{\prime}\to T
is a lattice alteration and the induced morphism
\Sigma\times_{T}T^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
is weakly semistable.
∎

The problem for translating Lemma
4.4
into the language of toroidal morphisms is to realize a toroidal morphism corresponding to a lattice alteration.
One (weak) answer is to use Kawamata’s covering trick (Lemma
2.30
, Lemma
2.31

(Weak semistable reduction)
.

.

over

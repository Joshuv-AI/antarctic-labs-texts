Comment on: “Interacting quantum and classical waves: Resonant and non-resonant energy transfer to electrons immersed in an intense electromagnetic wave” [Phys. Plasmas 29, 022107 (2022)]
A. Macchi

Abstract




In Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
, a theory of the interaction of a particle with an electromagnetic (EM) wave in a plasma based on the quantum Klein-Gordon equation was presented. In this Comment we show that the basic results of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
are recovered by a simple classical calculation, and we add remarks on the (un)suitability of the proposed mechanism for particle energization in both laser and astrophysical plasmas.

Following Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
, we consider in a reference frame
S
a transverse, monochromatic wave in a tenuous plasma of density
n_{0}
represented by the vector potential

\displaystyle{\bf A}_{\perp}={\bf A}_{\perp}(\omega t-kz)=A_{0}\mbox{Re}(\mbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}\mbox{e}^{-ikz+i\omega t})\;,

(1)

corresponding to a four-potential
A_{\mu}=(0,{\bf A}_{\perp},0)
. The dispersion relation is

\displaystyle\omega^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}+k^{2}c^{2}\;,

(2)

where
\omega_{p}=(4\pi e^{2}n_{0}/m_{e})^{1/2}
is the plasma frequency and
\omega>\omega_{p}
is assumed for propagation.
Corrections due to both thermal and relativistic dynamics effects may be incorporated in a redefinition of
\omega_{p}
, see Eq.(33) in Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
.

Let us consider the interaction of the wave (
1
) with a particle of charge
q
and mass
m
which at
(z,t)=(0,t_{i})
has a 4-momentum

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{i0},{\bf p}_{i})=(p_{i0},{\bf p}_{i\perp},p_{iz})\,,

(3)

\displaystyle p_{i0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m^{2}c^{2}+{\bf p}_{i}^{2})^{1/2}\;.

(4)

The initial conditions are fully general. Now it is advantageous to switch to a Lorentz frame
S^{\prime}
moving in the
z
-direction at the group velocity
v_{g}=\partial\omega/\partial k
with boost parameters

\displaystyle\beta_{g}=\frac{v_{g}}{c}=\frac{kc^{2}}{\omega}\;,\qquad\gamma_{g}=(1-\beta_{g}^{2})^{-1/2}=\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}\;.

(5)

In
S^{\prime}
, the vector potential depends only on time
t^{\prime}
and there is no magnetic field, as it is easily found from the Lorentz transformations of the wave phase and the four-potential:

\displaystyle\omega t-kz=\omega_{p}t^{\prime}\;,\qquad A^{\prime}_{\mu}=(0,{\bf A}_{\perp}(\omega_{p}t^{\prime}),0)\;.

(6)

The solution to the equation of motion for
t^{\prime}>t^{\prime}_{i}=\gamma_{g}t_{i}

is thus straightforward. From

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}{\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp}}{\mbox{d}t^{\prime}}=q{\bf E}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})=-\frac{q}{c}\partial_{t}^{\prime}{\bf A}_{\perp}\;,\qquad\frac{\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}p^{\prime}_{z}}{\mbox{d}t^{\prime}}=0\;,

(7)

one obtains
p^{\prime}_{z}(t^{\prime})=p^{\prime}_{iz}=\gamma_{g}(p_{iz}-\beta_{g}p_{i0})
and

\displaystyle{\bf p}_{\perp}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})+\frac{q}{c}{\bf A}(\omega_{p}t^{\prime})={\bf p}^{\prime}_{i\perp}+\frac{q}{c}{\bf A}_{i}\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}\;,

(8)

where
{\bf A}_{i}\equiv{\bf A}_{\perp}(\omega_{p}t^{\prime}_{i})
; note that
{\bf p}^{\prime}_{i\perp}={\bf p}_{i\perp}
. Eq.(
8
) gives the conservation of the canonical momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}={\bf p}_{\perp}+{\bf A}_{\perp}
. The 4-momentum in the boosted frame is

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{\mu}=\left(p^{\prime}_{0},{\bf p}^{\prime}_{\perp},p^{\prime}_{iz}\right)\,,

(9)

where

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{0}=\left({\bf p}^{{}^{\prime}2}_{\perp}+p^{{}^{\prime}2}_{iz}+m^{2}c^{2}\right)^{1/2}\;.

(10)

By transforming back in the
S
frame we obtain for the energy

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}(p^{\prime}_{0}+\beta_{g}p^{\prime}_{iz})

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}(p^{\prime}_{0}+\beta_{g}\gamma_{g}(p_{iz}-\beta_{g}p_{i0}))\;.

(12)

In Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
it is stated that energization occurs when the initial particle velocity along
v_{z}=p_{z}c/p_{0}=v_{g}
, the group velocity. Actually Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
reports “the phase velocity of the EM wave equals to the axial (in the propagation direction) phase velocity of the relativistic quantum particle” which is an obvious mistake (presumably a misprint) since the EM phase velocity
v_{p}=\omega/k>c
, so there cannot ever be “phase matching” between the particle and the wave. Looking at Eq.(
12
) we see instead that
p_{iz}\geq\beta_{g}p_{i0}
is necessary to make the second term in Eq.(
12
) positive. When
p_{iz}=\beta_{g}p_{i0}
(corresponding to the erroneous “resonant” condition of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
) the particle velocity along
z
is null in
S^{\prime}
(
p^{\prime}_{z}=p^{\prime}_{iz}=0
) and constant
(v_{z}=v_{g})
in
S
(note that this does not imply
\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}p_{z}/\mbox{d}t=0
in
S
; it is a consequence that, being
B/E=\beta_{g}
,
\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}p_{z}/\mbox{d}t=\beta_{g}\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}p_{0}/\mbox{d}t
), and the second term in the time-dependent energy (
12
) vanishes. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle p_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}\left(\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}-\frac{q}{c}{\bf A}_{\perp}(\xi)\right)^{2}+m^{2}c^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(p^{2}_{i0}+\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}({\bf A}^{2}_{\perp}(\xi)+{\bf A}^{2}_{i})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-2\frac{q}{c}\left(A_{\perp}(\xi)\Pi_{i}-A_{i}p_{i\perp}\right)\right)\right)^{1/2}

where

\displaystyle\xi=\omega t-k(v_{g}t)=\omega t(1-\beta_{g}^{2})=\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega}t\;,

(14)

and we used Eqs.(
8
) and (
5
).

For the case
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}=0
, Eq.(
13
) is identical to Eq.(33) of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
, namely Eq.(33) for the case
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}=0
. For
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}\neq 0
, Eq.(44) of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
yields
p_{0}=\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle/c
where

\displaystyle\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle=\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}^{2}+\left(\frac{q}{c}A_{\perp}\right)^{2}+m^{2}c^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,,

(15)

after recognizing that in such equations
K_{\perp}c
is the conserved canonical momentum
\Pi_{i}
. The difference with our Eq.(
13
) comes from the mistake in Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
of calculating the energy as the expectation value

\displaystyle\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle=\left\langle i\hbar\Psi^{*}\partial_{t}\Psi\right\rangle\,,

(16)

with
\psi
the wavefunction. However, as it fully evident in Schrödinger equation (2) of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
, the operator
i\hbar\partial_{t}
does not correspond to the particle energy
{\bf p}^{2}/2m
but rather to
({\bf p}+q{\bf A}/c)^{2}/2m=(p_{z}+\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$})^{2}/2m
. The same mistake occurs for the Klein-Gordon equation where the coupling with the classical EM field is also made by the replacement
{\bf p}\to{\bf p}+q{\bf A}/c
. Hence in the results of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
the canonical momentum
K_{\perp}c=\Pi_{i}
should be replaced by the transverse kinetic momentum
{\bf p}_{\perp}
. Looking at the first expression of Eq.(
13
) we see that
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}-({q}/{c}){\bf A}_{\perp}(\xi)={\bf p}_{\perp}
, thus (
13
) is identical to the quantum result when the expectation value of the energy is calculated correctly.

Recovering the results of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
via a classical calculation shows that a quantum approach is not necessary. This could be expected
a priori
since the de Broglie length of high-momentum particle is very small, so that the particle behaves classically.
The statement in Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
“the wave–particle coupling is fully contained in the term proportional to
K_{\perp}
” [i.e the term containing
A_{\perp}\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}
in Eq.(
13
)] might suggest that such term is of quantum nature, but indeed it has a simple classical origin in the
{\bf v}\times{\bf B}
force: when
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{i}\neq 0
the particle has an initial velocity in the perpendicular direction which yields a force term
\propto p_{\perp}B/p_{0}
.

The “rate of change of particle energy” can be trivially and explicitly calculated as
\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}p_{0}/\mbox{d}t
from Eq.(
13
). However, Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
rather considers the root mean square of
\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}}{\mbox{d}t}\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle\propto\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}\right)^{2}\frac{A_{\perp}\Pi_{\perp}}{\left\langle{\cal E}\right\rangle}\frac{\sin kL}{kL}\;,

(17)

where the factor
{\sin kL}/{kL}
originates from the particle being now assumed to be localized in a region of length
L
[Eq.(E5) of Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
]. While admittedly the need for and meaning of (
17
) are not fully clear, because of its definition (
17
) should represent a sort of uncertainty in the value of
{\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}}{\cal E}/{\mbox{d}t}
; actually because of Heisenberg’s principle the uncertainty in the longitudinal momentum should be proportional to
1/L
. Moreover, the values of the energy may have zero uncertainty only if the particle is in a steady state after “localization”, which is possible only for “standing” wavefunctions with
\sin kL=0
so that the region contains an integer number of half de Broglie wavelengths. This would be the only truly quantum effect described in Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
. Clearly, for the scenarios discussed below
kL
has enormous values so that
{\mbox{$\mathrm{d}$}}{\cal E}/{\mbox{d}t}\to 0
; quantum uncertainty effects are completely negligible.

The conceptual transparency of the classical description provides insight on the possibility of transverse EM waves in a low-density plasma to yield ultra-high energization of particles. First of all, modeling the EM field as a plane, monochromatic wave of infinite width and duration is at best a local approximation for an EM field of finite extension in space and time. Assuming an adiabatic rise and fall of the field from
A_{i}=0
to its peak value and than back to zero, no energy is eventually left to the particle (this is similar to the “no acceleration” or Lawson-Woodward theorem for a plane wave in vacuum
Gibbon 2005
;
Macchi 2013
). This is why we write “energization” instead of “acceleration” which would suggest that the ultra-high energy particles can still be detected out of the interaction region.
A net energy only appears for
A_{i}\neq 0
which may occur for a particle released from rest when the EM field is already on (a field-ionized electron, for example).

If a quasi-monochromatic wavepacket is considered, the initial condition
v_{iz}=v_{g}
requires the particle to be “born” inside the wavepacket which otherwise would never be reached. Even if the particle is efficiently injected (by some unspecified mechanism) into the wavepacket with
v_{iz}=v_{g}
, reaching the maximum possible energy gain would require in
S^{\prime}
a time
t^{\prime}_{m}=\pi/(2\omega_{p})
(equal to a quarter of the oscillation period) which, due to Lorentz time dilation, corresponds to
t_{m}=\gamma_{g}t^{\prime}_{m}=(\pi\omega/2\omega_{p}^{2})
in the laboratory frame
S
as also indicated by the temporal dependence of
A
in Eqs.(
13
). This greatly increased energization time may be not compatible with the assumption of a quasi-plane, quasi-monochromatic field. Picking up an example from Ref.
Mahajan and Asenjo 2022
, we observe that using high-intensity femtosecond laser pulses laser-plasma interaction experiments are nowadays routinely performed with parameters such as
a_{0}=eA/mc^{2}=10
and
\omega/\omega_{p}=10^{2}
. An energy gain factor of
\sim a_{0}(\omega/\omega_{p})
would require an interaction time of
\sim(\omega/\omega_{p})^{2}=10^{4}
laser cycles which is typically
\sim 10^{2}
times the pulse duration, and would imply particles to move over
\sim
mm distances which are much larger than both the Rayleigh length and focal width of the laser beam. These numbers largely justify the lack of any evidence of ultrahigh energization in laser-plasma experiments. For what concerns an astrophysical scenario with
\omega/\omega_{p}=10^{5}
, assuming a frequency of
\omega/2\pi=10^{11}
Hz the EM field should keep a constant amplitude over a distance of
\sim 5\times 10^{6}
km in both transverse and longitudinal directions.

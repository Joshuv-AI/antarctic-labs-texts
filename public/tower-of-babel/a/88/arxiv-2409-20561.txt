Covariant Quantum Error-Correcting Codes with Metrological Entanglement Advantage
Cheng-Ju Lin, Zi-Wen Liu, Victor V. Albert, Alexey V. Gorshkov

Abstract
We show that a subset of the basis for the irreducible representations of a tensor-product SU(2) rotation forms a covariant approximate quantum error-correcting code with transversal U(1) logical gates. Generalizing previous work on “thermodynamic codes” to general local spin and different irreducible representations using only properties of the angular momentum algebra, we obtain bounds on the code inaccuracy under generic noise on any known d d sites, under independent and identically distributed noise, and under heralded d d -local erasures. We demonstrate that this family of codes protects a probe state with quantum Fisher information surpassing the standard quantum limit when the sensing parameter couples to the generator of the U(1) logical gate.

Code words from SU(2) irrep states—
Consider a system of
N
spin-
s
degrees of freedom (i.e. qudits of dimension
2s\!+\!1
), where
s
can be a half integer or an integer.
The total SU(2)-rotation unitary is a tensor product of the SU(2) rotations on the individual spins, generated by the su(2) algebra

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{z}\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{q}^{z}_{j}~,~~~

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{+}\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{q}^{+}_{j}~,

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{-}\!=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{q}^{-}_{j}~,

(1)

where
j
labels the spin and
[\hat{q}_{j}^{+},\hat{q}_{j}^{-}]=2\hat{q}^{z}_{j}
,
[\hat{q}_{j}^{z},\hat{q}_{j}^{\pm}]=\pm\hat{q}_{j}^{\pm}
. This yields
[\hat{Q}^{+},\hat{Q}^{-}]=2\hat{Q}^{z}
and
[\hat{Q}^{z},\hat{Q}^{\pm}]=\pm\hat{Q}^{\pm}
.

The irreps of the total SU(2)-rotation unitary are labeled by
J\leq sN
, with a possible multiplicity, and each irrep has dimension
2J\!+1
with the basis labeled by
|J\!,\!M\rangle
,
M\in\{-J,\cdots,J\}
.
The state
|J\!,\!M\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle Q^{z}|J\!,\!M\rangle=M|J,M\rangle~,~~Q^{\pm}|J\!,\!M\rangle=c_{M}^{\pm}|J,{M\!\pm\!1}\rangle~,

(2)

where
c^{\pm}_{M}=\sqrt{(J\mp M)(J\pm M+1)}
.

We will show that the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, equipped with transversal U(1) logical gates generated by
\hat{Q}^{z}
, forms an AQECC under generic noise on any known
d
sites, i.i.d. error with sufficiently low error probability, and heralded
d
-local erasures for some parameters—provided the spacing
\Delta
between magnetic quantum numbers is large enough.
For the maximum
J=sN
,
|J\!,\!M\rangle
is totally permutation symmetric, and the irrep multiplicity is one.
For a general
J<sN
, one can have multiple irreps with the same
J
, and we consider
|J\!,\!M\rangle
in any irrep or from any linear superposition of different irreps with the same
J
, as long as the ladder algebra in Eq. (
2
) holds among the resulting
|J\!,\!M\rangle
.
As such, we suppress the multiplicity index.

Code inaccuracy against generic errors—
We use the notation
(\!(N,k)\!)
and
\epsilon(\mathcal{N})
for an AQECC, where
N
denotes the number of qudits,
k
denotes the number of logical qubits, and
\epsilon(\mathcal{N})
denotes the optimal recovery inaccuracy against noise channel
\mathcal{N}
(with encoding):

\displaystyle\epsilon(\mathcal{N})=\min_{\mathcal{R}}D_{P}(\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})~,

(3)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the recovery channel and
D_{P}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is the purified distance between channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
, defined as

\displaystyle D_{P}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=\max_{|\psi\rangle}\sqrt{1-f^{2}[\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id}(\psi),\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{id}(\psi)]}~,

(4)

where
f(\rho,\sigma):=\mathrm{Tr}[\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}]
is the fidelity,
\psi:=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
,
|\psi\rangle
is a purification of
\rho
, and
\mathrm{id}
is the identity channel on the auxiliary space whose input has the same Hilbert space dimension as the input of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
(see Supplemental Material S1
[
40
]
for a list of definitions used throughout the paper).
As we only consider noise on at most
d
sites, we use
\epsilon(d)
to stress its dependence on
d
, while suppressing
\mathcal{N}
whose specific choices should be clear from the context.

We bound the code inaccuracy
\epsilon(\mathcal{N})
using a generalization
[
18
]
of the Knill-Laflamme conditions
[
41
]
. If we let
\Pi
be the projector onto the code space and
\mathcal{N}(\bullet)=\sum_{j}K_{j}(\bullet)K_{j}^{\dagger}
be the noise channel, then
\epsilon(\mathcal{N})\leq\delta
if and only if

\Pi K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}\Pi=\lambda_{ij}\Pi+\Pi B_{ij}\Pi~,

(5)

where
\lambda_{ij}
are the components of a density operator, and
D_{P}(\Lambda+\mathcal{B},\Lambda)\leq\delta
, where
\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)|i\rangle\langle j|
and
(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)=\Lambda(\rho)+\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B_{ij})|i\rangle\langle j|
.

To this end, we start with the following two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Assume
\hat{F}
is a
d
-local operator, then
\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle=0
if
|n-m|\geq 2sd+1
.

Physically, this lemma holds since a
d
-local operator can change the magnetic quantum number
M
by at most
2sd
.
It ensures the “off diagonal” part of the Knill-Laflamme conditions to be exactly zero if the code words have enough spacing in
M
.
The proof is simple and given in Supplemental Material S2
[
40
]
.

To bound the “diagonal part” of the Knill-Laflamme conditions, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Assume
\hat{F}
is a
d
-local operator, then
|\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle-\langle J,m|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle|\leq dq_{0}\|\hat{F}\|_{op}C(n,m)
, where
C(n,m):=\sum_{M=n}^{m-1}[c^{+}_{M}]^{-1}
and
q_{0}
is a constant.
In particular, for
0\leq b<a\leq 1
, if we take
J\sim N^{a}
and
|m-n|\sim N^{b}
, then we have
|\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle-\langle J,m|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle|=\|\hat{F}\|_{op}\cdot O(dN^{-(a-b)})
.

Note that
\|\hat{F}\|_{\text{op}}:=\sup_{|\psi\rangle}\{\|\hat{F}|\psi\rangle\|:\||\psi\rangle\|\!=\!1\}
is the operator norm and
\||\psi\rangle\|:=\sqrt{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
.
Relegating the proof to Supplemental Material S3
[
40
]
, we comment that this bound is obtained by bounding
|\langle J,n+1|\hat{F}|J,n+1\rangle-\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle|
and using the triangle inequality. We emphasize that the proof only uses the ladder algebra Eq. (
2
) and the locality of the operator
Q^{\pm}
without the explicit expression for
|J\!,\!M\rangle
.

Using these two lemmas, we obtain the following upper bound on the code inaccuracy for generic noise on any known
d
sites
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{d}}K_{j}(\rho)K^{\dagger}_{j}
whose Kraus operators
K_{j}
are at most
d
local, acting on a fixed set of
d
qudits, and
n_{d}=(2s+1)^{2d}
is the number of independent Kraus operators. The proof is presented in Supplemental Material S4
[
40
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, where
J\!\sim\!N^{a}
,
M_{\mathrm{max}}\!-\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\!\sim\!N^{b}
, and
b\!<\!a\!\leq 1
. The code
\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O[b\log_{2}N])\!)
AQECC against
d
-local noise on known sites, with inaccuracy
\epsilon(d)=O[\sqrt{d}(2s+1)^{d}N^{-(a-b)/2}]
if
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
.

(a) For our SU(2) code with total spin
J\!\sim\!N^{a}
and magnetic quantum number range
M_{\mathrm{max}}\!-\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
, under heralded
d
-local noise channel (

), the

(blue and red areas), and the region with metrological advantage (red area). (b) For

, and

-local erasures (

), the
(b,c)

\epsilon\to 0
(blue and red areas), and the region with metrological advantage (red area), all independent of

.

We focus on parameters where
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in the thermodynamic limit
N\rightarrow\infty
.
For error size
d\!\sim\!c\log_{2}N
, we have
\epsilon=O(\sqrt{\log_{2}N}N^{-[a-b-2c\log_{2}(2s+1)]/2})
, which vanishes as

provided

. This is illustrated in Fig.

(a) as the shaded area.

Our code-inaccuracy bounds generalize and improve the results in Ref.
[
26
]
, which gives
\epsilon^{\prime}=\tilde{O}(n_{d}N^{(5b-1)/2})
for special cases
(s,J)=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{N}{2})
and
(s,J)=(1,N)
, while we achieve
\epsilon=\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n_{d}}N^{(b-1)/2})

Our improvements from

to

and from

come from setting the “off-diagonal” Knill-Laflamme conditions to zero and a different proof technique.

Moreover, the derivation of the bounds in Ref.
[
26
]
relies on the

assumption; our approach allows

, which enables a probe state with a metrological entanglement advantage, as we will discuss below.
Notably, using the ladder algebra of SU(2), we obtain a general
s
- and
J
-dependent bound on the code inaccuracy, explaining the coincidence of code-inaccuracy bounds for
(s,J)=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{N}{2})
and
(s,J)=(1,N)
in Ref.
[
26
]
.

The reason to consider the noise on a fixed set of
d
sites in Theorem
1
is to control the number of Kraus operators
n_{d}
. For generic
d
-local noise
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{d}}K_{j}(\rho)K^{\dagger}_{j}
, where
K_{j}
can act on
different
sets of
d
qudits,
\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O[b\log_{2}N])\!)
AQECC with
\epsilon(d)=O(\sqrt{d}n_{d}N^{-(a-b)/2})
if we take
\Delta\geq 4sd+1
. (See Supplemental Material
[
40
]
S4 for Corollary 1.)

Physically, the above result implies that the code can tolerate any error with the number of Kraus operators growing slower than
O(\sqrt{d}N^{-(a-b)/2})
, assuming each has operator norm
O(1)
.
However, in practice, more nonlocal errors tend to occur with lower probability, resulting in smaller operator norms for their corresponding Kraus operators.
This structure allows the code to tolerate noise channels with a larger number of Kraus operators.
Indeed, under i.i.d. errors at unknown locations, we show in Supplemental Material
[
40
]
S5 (Corollary 2) that our code can achieve vanishing code inaccuracy by correcting
d
-local errors with
d\sim c\log_{2}N
, provided the error rate is sufficiently low
p\lesssim N^{-2}\log^{2}N
.
Although this error rate implies a vanishing expected number of errors
Np\lesssim N^{-1}\log^{2}N
, it is chosen to enable a rigorous analytical bound on the code inaccuracy.
It remains an interesting open question whether the code can still achieve low inaccuracy when the number of errors becomes nonvanishing.

Code inaccuracy against heralded erasures—
The inaccuracy of the “thermodynamic code” against heralded
d
-local erasures for
d=O(1)
and
|M|=O(1)
was found in Refs.
[
20
,
25
]
to be
\epsilon=O(N^{-1})
.
The heralded
d
-local erasure channel is
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}(\rho):=\mathrm{Tr}_{\alpha}[\rho]
, where
\alpha
denotes the set of
d
erased qudits.

We now generalize the code-inaccuracy bound against heralded erasures to
s
,
d\sim N^{c}
and
|M|=O(N^{b})
.
In this case, we consider codes with
J=sN
only, where
|J\!,\!M\rangle
is totally permutation symmetric.
Owing to the irrep multiplicity being one, the
J=sN
assumption allows us to express each codeword uniquely in terms of a basis for two subsystems using Clebsch–Gordan coefficients.
To bound the code inaccuracy, we follow Ref.
[
18
]
by bounding the channel distance via the complementary channel
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\alpha}(\bullet)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bullet)
, where
\bar{\alpha}
denotes the set of qudits not in
\alpha
(the complement of
\alpha
). This can be achieved by the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Consider the irrep of SU(2) formed by
|J\!,\!M\rangle
, where
J=sN
, and the reduced density matrix
\rho_{M}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}[|J\!,\!M\rangle\langle J\!,\!M|]
, where
\bar{\alpha}
is the complement of the set of erased qudits
\alpha
.
We have the fidelity
f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})=1-O(\frac{dM^{2}}{sN^{2}})=1-O(N^{2b+c-2})
asymptotically for
M\sim N^{b}
,
d\sim N^{c}
, and
1>b\geq c
.

Physically, the above lemma states that the environment can barely tell the code words apart from each other.
In the proof (presented in Supplemental Material S6
[
40
]
), we use Clebsch-Gordan coefficients without writing the code words out explicitly, enabling a general
s
-dependent bound on the fidelity for
J\!=\!sN
.
In particular, we can rewrite
|J\!,\!M\rangle=\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}|j_{1}\!,\!m_{1}\rangle_{\alpha}\otimes|j_{2}\!,\!m_{2}\rangle_{\bar{\alpha}}
, where
m_{2}=M-m_{1}
,
j_{1}=sd
,
j_{2}=J-j_{1}
, and
C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}=(\langle j_{1},m_{1}|\langle j_{2},m_{2}|)|J,M\rangle
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient.
The fidelity between
\rho_{M}
and
\rho_{M=0}
can therefore be calculated accordingly.

Our proof for a general
s
has essentially the same asymptotic scaling as the proof of Lemma 13 in Ref.
[
20
]
for
s=\frac{1}{2}
, which reported
f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})=1-D_{d,M}/N^{2}+O(N^{-3})
, where
D_{d,M}=d^{2}/8+[d(1+M^{2}+2M)+2d^{3}+d^{2}(2M-1)]/4
.
This indeed becomes our result in the asymptotic regime
M\sim N^{b}
,
d\sim N^{c}
, and
1>b\geq c
, making the leading-order term
\sim dM^{2}
.

The following theorem bounds the code inaccuracy against a heralded

-local erasure error.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, where
J=sN
,
M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
and

.
\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O[(b\!-\!c)\log_{2}N])\!)
AQECC against heralded
d
-local erasures of size
d\sim N^{c}
, where
0\leq c\leq b<1
, with
\epsilon(d)=O(N^{-(1-b-c/2)})
if we take

.

Theorem
2
can be obtained by combining our Lemma
1
, our Lemma
3
, and Theorem 3 of Ref.
[
20
]
.
To make our presentation self-contained, we present the proof of Theorem

tailored to our case in Supplemental Material S7
[

]
.
References
[

,

]
showed that
\epsilon=\Omega(N^{-1})
for any U(1) covariant code.
Thus, this family of SU(2) AQECCs is asymptotically optimal for general

when

.
Although Theorem

also applies to heralded erasure errors, the improvement in Theorem 2 arises from the assumption that

For general

, to have

, we need

.
Additionally, we need

.

Metrological entanglement advantage—
We now apply our codes to quantum metrology and show that they can have an entanglement advantage with noise resistance.
In the local parameter estimation problem
[
10
]
, we would like to estimate the parameter
\theta
imprinted on the probe state
|\psi_{0}\rangle\in\mathfrak{C}
as
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=e^{-i\hat{Q}^{z}\theta}|\psi_{0}\rangle\in\mathfrak{C}
.
Abbreviating
\psi_{\theta}:=|\psi_{\theta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\theta}|
, the noisy probe state is then
\rho_{\theta}:=\mathcal{N}(\psi_{\theta})
for the noise channel
\mathcal{N}
.

In quantum metrology, QFI is a central figure of merit, which characterizes how well a parameter can be estimated via the quantum Cramér-Rao bound
[
42
]
.
For the states
\rho_{\theta}
parametrized by
\theta
, the QFI is defined as

\mathfrak{F}(\rho_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}):=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\theta}R^{2})~,

(6)

where
R
satisfies
\rho_{\theta}R+R\rho_{\theta}=2\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}
.

If
\theta
is generated from
e^{-i\theta\hat{Q}^{z}}
, then without entanglement, the QFI is upper bounded by the standard quantum limit, scaling as
\mathfrak{F}\!\sim\!N
.
Achieving
\mathfrak{F}\!\sim\!N^{\gamma}
for
1\!<\!\gamma\!\leq\!2
indicates a metrological entanglement advantage, with
\gamma\!=\!2
as the Heisenberg limit.
Since
\mathfrak{F}(\psi_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})=4[\langle\psi_{\theta}|(\hat{Q}^{z})^{2}|\psi_{\theta}\rangle-\langle\psi_{\theta}|\hat{Q}^{z}|\psi_{\theta}\rangle^{2}]
for a pure state, our code can have an entanglement advantage if it contains a logical state whose variance of
\hat{Q}^{z}
scales superlinearly with
N
.
This is possible with the probe state
|\psi_{0}\rangle\propto|J,M\rangle+|J,-M\rangle
, whose QFI is

Discussion—
We establish code-inaccuracy bounds for a family of covariant approximate quantum error-correcting codes whose codewords house irreducible representations of SU(2).
We show that a particular (entangled) codeword can be used for quantum sensing in a way that outperforms classical strategies, even in the presence of certain noise.

References
[
64
,
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
]
show protocols to prepare
|J\!,\!M\rangle
states for
J=sN
.
One can then prepare the probe state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
using an ancilla qubit and a controlled version of the circuit that prepares
|J\!,\!M\rangle
.

We have proposed some measurement schemes for the ideal and partially-erased probe states in Supplemental Material S8
[
40
]
.
It is worth investigating whether simpler measurements can saturate the Cramér-Rao bound for our noisy state
[
69
]
. While the recovery channel
[
18
,
70
]
cannot increase quantum Fisher information
[
62
,
10
]
, it may aid in devising a measurement protocol for any noisy probe.
Refs.
[
18
,
71
]
proposed a near-optimal recovery channel for AQECCs, which we outline in Supplemental Material S9
[
40
]
.
Although an efficient circuit implementation is not guaranteed, it would be interesting to explore whether the structure of the
J=sN
code, such as its total permutation symmetry, can be leveraged for this purpose.

With modifications, our approximate quantum error-correcting code results can be applied to other SU(2) code words, like the quantum many-body scar states in the spin-1 XY-Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya model
[

,

(see Supplemental Material S10

Accordingly, the SU(2)-scar probe states could be noise-resilient, complementing the results using scar states for sensing

For states with approximate SU(2) symmetry

, some exact codes
[

,

]
, or approximately prepared states
[
82
]
, further study is warranted to determine if they also provide a noise resilient entanglement advantage.

Our result suggests a new way of finding approximate quantum error-correcting codes with continuous transversal logical gates by using symmetry irreps, complementing existing methods
[
32
,
14
,
33
,
29
,
31
]

Here, one selects a subset of the SU(2)-irrep basis to form an approximate quantum error-correcting code, reducing the symmetry to U(1) and giving rise to continuous transversal logical gates.
This raises the question of whether one can also devise approximate quantum error-correcting codes by using irreps from other continuous symmetries [say, SU(3)], which could potentially yield transversal gates generated by multiple (possibly noncommuting) generators and an entanglement advantage for multiparameter sensing.

S1
A List of definitions

In this section, we list the definitions used throughout the paper.

Definition 1
.

(Operator norm)

\|\hat{F}\|_{\text{op}}:=\sup_{|\psi\rangle}\{\|\hat{F}|\psi\rangle\|:\||\psi\rangle\|\!=\!1\}

is the operator norm, where
\||\psi\rangle\|:=\sqrt{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
is the vector norm.

Definition 2
.

(Trace norm)

\|\rho\|_{1}:=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\rho^{\dagger}\rho}
,

which induces the trace distance between two density matrices
\rho
and
\Gamma
as
\|\rho-\Gamma\|_{1}
.
The trace distance then induces a distance measure between quantum channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
:

Definition 3
.

(Diamond distance)

\|\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}\|_{\diamond}:=\max_{|\psi\rangle}\|\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)-\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\|_{1}
,

where
|\psi\rangle
is a purification of
\rho
and
\mathrm{id}
is the identity channel on the auxiliary space whose input has the same Hilbert space dimension as the input of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 4
.

(Fidelity)

f(\rho,\sigma):=\|\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}
,

which induces the entanglement fidelity between channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
:

Definition 5
.

(Entanglement fidelity)

F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}):=\min_{|\psi\rangle}f(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))
,

where again
|\psi\rangle
is a purification of
\rho
and
\mathrm{id}
is the identity channel on the auxiliary space whose input has the same Hilbert space dimension as the input of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
.
The purified channel distance is defined as follows:

Definition 6
.

(Purified channel distance)

D_{P}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}):=\sqrt{1-F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})^{2}}
.

Note that one can also define another channel distance measure, called Bures distance as follows:

Definition 7
.

(Bures channel distance)

D_{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}):=\sqrt{1-F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
.

In Ref.
[
18
]
, the code inaccuracy is quantified by the Bures distance. On the other hand, in this work, we opt for using purified distance. These channel distances are related, however, via the following Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality
[
63
]
:

1-f(\rho,\sigma)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{1-f(\rho,\sigma)^{2}}~.

(S1)

This immediately gives us

D_{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}\|_{\diamond}\leq D_{P}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})~.

Lemma 1: Assume
\hat{F}
is a
d
-local operator, then
\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle=0
if
|n-m|\geq 2sd+1
.

Proof.

Recall that
\hat{Q}^{z}|J,n\rangle=n|J,n\rangle
, where
\hat{Q}^{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{q}^{z}_{j}
. Since we consider a system of spin-
s
degrees of freedom, the charge
\hat{q}^{z}
can have eigenvalues
q^{z}=-s,-s+1,\cdots,s
. Therefore, a
d
-local operator can change the total charge by at most
2sd
. So we have
\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle=0
, if
|n-m|\geq 2sd+1
.

S3
Proof of Lemma 2

In this section, we present the proof of Lemma 2, which bounds the “diagonal” part of the Knill-Laflamme conditions.

Lemma 2: Assume
\hat{F}
is a
d
-local operator, then
|\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle-\langle J,m|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle|\leq dq_{0}\|\hat{F}\|_{op}C(n,m)
, where
C(n,m):=\sum_{M=n}^{m-1}[c^{+}_{M}]^{-1}
and
q_{0}
is a constant.
In particular, for
0\leq b<a\leq 1
, if we take
J\sim N^{a}
and
|m-n|\sim N^{b}
, then we have
|\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle-\langle J,m|\hat{F}|J,m\rangle|=\|\hat{F}\|_{op}\cdot O(dN^{-(a-b)})
.

Proof.

Assume
I
is the set of qudits
\hat{F}
acts on. We then have

\|[\hat{F},\hat{Q}^{+}]\|_{op}\leq\|[\hat{F},\sum_{j\in I}\hat{q}^{+}_{j}]\|_{op}\leq dq_{0}\|\hat{F}\|_{op}~,

(S3)

where we denoted
q_{0}=2\|\hat{q}^{+}_{j}\|_{op}
for convenience, which is a constant.
To bound
|\langle J,{n+1}|\hat{F}|J,{n+1}\rangle-\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle|
, note that

\langle J,{n+1}|\hat{F}|J,{n+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{(c^{+}_{n})^{2}}\langle J,{n}|\hat{Q}^{-}\hat{F}\hat{Q}^{+}|J,{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{(c^{+}_{n})^{2}}\langle J,{n}|\hat{Q}^{-}\hat{Q}^{+}\hat{F}|J,{n}\rangle+\frac{1}{c^{+}_{n}}\langle J,{n+1}|[\hat{F},\hat{Q}^{+}]|J,{n}\rangle~.

(S4)

However, recall
c_{n}^{\pm}=\sqrt{(J\mp n)(J\pm n+1)}
, we also have

\frac{1}{(c^{+}_{n})^{2}}\langle J,{n}|\hat{Q}^{-}\hat{Q}^{+}\hat{F}|J,{n}\rangle=\frac{c^{-}_{n+1}}{c^{+}_{n}}\langle J,{n}|\hat{F}|J,{n}\rangle=\langle J,{n}|\hat{F}|J,{n}\rangle~,

(S5)

which gives us

|\langle J,{n+1}|\hat{F}|J,{n+1}\rangle-\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle|\leq dq_{0}\|\hat{F}\|_{op}\cdot(c^{+}_{n})^{-1}~,

(S6)

or

\displaystyle|\langle J,{m}|\hat{F}|J,{m}\rangle-\langle J,n|\hat{F}|J,n\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\ell=n}^{m-1}\langle J,{\ell+1}|\hat{F}|J,{\ell+1}\rangle-\langle J,\ell|\hat{F}|J,\ell\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=n}^{m-1}|\langle J,{\ell+1}|\hat{F}|J,{\ell+1}\rangle-\langle J,\ell|\hat{F}|J,\ell\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq dq_{0}\|\hat{F}\|_{op}\cdot C(m,n)~,

(S7)

where
C(m,n)=\sum_{\ell=n}^{m-1}[c^{+}_{\ell}]^{-1}=\sum_{\ell=n}^{m-1}[(J-\ell)(J+\ell+1)]^{-1/2}
and we used the triangle inequality in the first inequality.

We can obtain a bound on
C(n,m)
as follows.
Consider the function
g(x):=[(J-x)(J+x+1)]^{-1/2}
in the domain
x\in(-J-1,J)
. It is easy to see that the minimum of
g(x)
is at
x^{*}=-1/2
, and
g(x)
is a decreasing function from
x=-J-1
to
x=x^{*}
and an increasing function from
x=x^{*}
to
x=J
. It is also easy to check that
g(x)=g(2x^{*}-x)
.
We first consider the case
n<0<m
.
We separate
C(n,m)
into two pieces,
C(n,m)=C_{1}(n)+C_{2}(m)
, where
C_{1}(n):=\sum_{\ell=n}^{\ell=-1}[c^{+}_{\ell}]^{-1}
and
C_{2}(m):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}[c^{+}_{\ell}]^{-1}
.
These two pieces can be bounded as

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{0}g(x)dx

\displaystyle<C_{1}(n)<\int_{n}^{0}g(x-1)dx~,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{m}g(x-1)dx

\displaystyle<C_{2}(m)<\int_{0}^{m}g(x)dx~.

(S8)

The integrals of the upper bounds are given by
\int_{0}^{m}g(x)dx=2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J}{2J+1}}\right)-2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J-m}{2J+1}}\right)
and

\int_{n}^{0}dxg(x-1)=\int_{n}^{0}dxg(-x)=\int_{0}^{-n}dyg(y)=2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J}{2J+1}}\right)-2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J+n}{2J+1}}\right)
.

We are interested in the regime where
n/J\rightarrow 0
,
m/J\rightarrow 0
, and
J\sim N^{a}
(
a\leq 1
) when
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Consider the function

f(\tilde{x})=2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\tilde{x}}{2+J^{-1}}}\right)~,

(S9)

where
\tilde{x}=x/J
.
Its Taylor series is
f(\tilde{x})-f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)\tilde{x}+O(f^{\prime\prime}(0)\tilde{x}^{2})
, where
f(0)=2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J}{2J+1}}\right)

f^{\prime}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{J}{1+J}}
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\frac{J^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2(1+J)^{\frac{3}{2}}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J}{2J+1}}\right)-2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{J+x}{2J+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{J(J+1)}}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{[J(1+J)]^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{J}(1+J^{-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{J^{3}}(1+J^{-1})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{J}+O(xJ^{-2})~.

(S10)

Therefore, for
n<0<m
, we have

C(m,n)=\frac{|m-n|}{J}+O(|m-n|J^{-2})~.

(S11)

If
0<n<m
, then we only need to bound
\sum_{\ell=n}^{m-1}[c^{+}_{\ell}]^{-1}<\int_{n}^{m}g(x)dx=\int_{0}^{m}g(x)dx-\int_{0}^{n}g(x)dx=|m-n|J^{-1}+O(|m-n|J^{-2})

(S12)

Therefore, for
J\sim N^{a}
and

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem 1, which gives an upper bound on the code inaccuracy against a generic

Theorem 1: Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}

(\!(N,O[b\log_{2}N])\!)

\epsilon(d)=O[\sqrt{d}(2s+1)^{d}N^{-(a-b)/2}]

\Delta\geq 2sd+1

Proof.

The proof of this theorem uses the Knill-Laflamme condition for the AQECC developed by Bény and Oreshkov in Ref.
[
18

(Bény and Oreshkov) Consider a noise channel with the Kraus representation
\mathcal{N}(\bullet)=\sum_{j}K_{j}(\bullet)K_{j}^{\dagger}

\Pi
be the projector onto the code space.
Then the code is
\epsilon

\mathcal{R}
such that
D_{P}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},id)\leq\epsilon
) if and only if

\Pi K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}\Pi=\lambda_{ij}\Pi+\Pi B_{ij}\Pi~,

(S14)

where
\lambda_{ij}
are the components of a density operator, and
D_{P}(\Lambda+\mathcal{B},\Lambda)\leq\epsilon
, where
\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)|i\rangle\langle j|
and
(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)=\Lambda(\rho)+\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B_{ij})|i\rangle\langle j|
.

This theorem in fact has an interpretation from the complementary channel point of view, as described in Ref.
[
18
]
. More specifically, consider the noise channel
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{i}K_{i}(\rho)K_{i}^{\dagger}
, whose complementary channel is
\hat{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Tr}(K_{j}\rho K_{i}^{\dagger})|i\rangle\langle j|=(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)
, where
|i\rangle\langle j|
acts on the environment Hilbert space.
If we have perfect Knill-Laflamme conditions, the complementary channel of the noise becomes
\hat{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|:=\lambda_{0}=\Lambda(\rho)
, which is a fixed density matrix
\lambda_{0}
independent of the input
\rho
. In other words, the condition for having a perfect quantum error-correcting code is equivalent to the condition that the environment gains no information from the code space.
And we indeed see that the AQECC generalization of the Knill-Laflamme conditions is a perturbation from the complementary channel point of view, where the environment can gain some
\epsilon
-small information from the code space.
Note that, in the original theorem in Ref.
[
18
]
, Bures distance
D_{B}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
is used as the measure of channel distance, while here we opt for using the purified distance
D_{P}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
, where
D_{B}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})^{2}\leq D_{P}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
can be obtained using Eq. (
S2
).

Here we consider the noise channel
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{d}}K_{i}(\rho)K_{i}^{\dagger}
, where
n_{d}=(2s+1)^{2d}
is the number of independent Kraus operators.
We assume
K_{j}
is at most
d
-local, and that all
K_{j}
act on the same set of
d
qudits, so
K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}
is at most
d
-local as well.
We are also interested in the regime
J\sim N^{a}
and
(M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}})\sim N^{b}
.
From
Lemma 1
, for
|J,n\rangle,|J,m\rangle\in\mathfrak{C}
and
n\neq m
, we have
\langle J\!,\!{n}|K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}|J\!,\!{m}\rangle=0
if
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
.
Now assuming we take
\lambda_{ij}=\langle J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}|K^{\dagger}_{i}K_{j}|J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\rangle
,we then have
\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho]|i\rangle\langle j|
and
(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\mathrm{Tr}[K_{j}\rho K_{i}^{\dagger}]|i\rangle\langle j|
, where the inputs of both channels are from the logical space.

Our goal now is to bound the entanglement fidelity,
F(\Lambda,\Lambda+\mathcal{B}):=\min_{|\psi\rangle}f((\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))
.
Recall that the inputs of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda+\mathcal{B}
are from the logical space, and the auxiliary space is isomorphic to the input space. Let us use
|x\rangle:=V^{\dagger}|J\!,\!M_{x}\rangle
for
x=1,\cdots,2^{k}
to denote the basis of the logical space for convenience, where
|J\!,\!M_{x}\rangle\in\mathfrak{C}
, and
V
is the isometric encoding such that
VV^{\dagger}=\Pi
is the projection onto the code space.

For any state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|x\rangle\otimes|y\rangle
, if we consider the operators
A=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|x\rangle\langle y|
and
B:=A^{T}=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|y\rangle\langle x|
and the state
|\Phi\rangle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}|x\rangle\otimes|x\rangle
, then we can express
|\psi\rangle=(A\otimes\mathrm{id})|\Phi\rangle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes B)|\Phi\rangle
.
We can therefore rewrite
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id}[(V\otimes I)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)(V^{\dagger}\otimes I)]=(\mathrm{id}\otimes B)(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Phi}|)(\mathrm{id}\otimes B^{\dagger})
for
\mathcal{E}=\Lambda
and
\mathcal{E}=\Lambda+\mathcal{B}
, where we abbreviate
|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle:=(V\otimes I)|\Phi\rangle
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Phi}|)=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\langle J\!,\!M_{x}|K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}|J\!,\!M_{x}\rangle\cdot|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|:=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma^{x}_{ij}\cdot|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|,

(S15)

\displaystyle\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Phi}|)=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\langle J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}|K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}|J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\rangle\cdot|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}\cdot|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|~,

(S16)

where we have used Lemma
1
and denoted
\Gamma^{x}_{ij}=\langle J\!,\!M_{x}|K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}|J\!,\!M_{x}\rangle
.

From the normalization condition
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_{x}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle=1
, we can interpret
\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle
as a probability.
The fidelity can be rewritten as

\displaystyle f((\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))

\displaystyle=f\left(\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma_{ij}^{x}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle},\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma_{ij}^{x}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle},\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma_{ij}^{x}|i\rangle\langle j|,\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\right)~,

(S17)

where we have used the joint concavity of the fidelity in the first inequality.

We will use the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality Eq. (
S1
) to bound the fidelity via the trace norm.
To this end, we would like to bound
\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|_{1}
, where
\Gamma_{x}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma_{ij}^{x}|i\rangle\langle j|
and
\lambda_{0}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|
.
Note that since
\Gamma_{x}^{\dagger}=\Gamma_{x}
and
\lambda_{0}^{\dagger}=\lambda_{0}
, for each
x
, we can find a unitary transformation
U_{x}
that diagonalizes
\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}
, giving us
\gamma_{i}^{x}\delta_{i\ell}=\sum_{j,k}[U^{\dagger}_{x}]_{ij}(\Gamma^{x}_{jk}-\lambda_{jk})[U_{x}]_{k\ell}
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}[U_{x}^{\dagger}]_{ij}(\langle J\!,\!M|K_{j}^{\dagger}K_{k}|J\!,\!M\rangle-\langle J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}|K_{j}^{\dagger}K_{k}|J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\rangle)[U_{x}]_{ki}

\displaystyle:=\langle J\!,\!M|F_{i}^{\dagger}F_{i}|J\!,\!M\rangle-\langle J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}|F_{i}^{\dagger}F_{i}|J\!,\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\rangle~,

(S18)

where
F_{i}:=\sum_{j}K_{j}[U_{x}]_{ji}
is at most
d
-local and
\sum_{i}F_{i}^{\dagger}F_{i}=I
. (Note that
F_{i}
depends on
x
in general, though here we suppress it to simplify the notation.)
We therefore have
|\gamma_{i}^{x}|=\|F_{i}\|_{\text{op}}\cdot O(dN^{b-a})=O(dN^{b-a})
from Lemma
2
and the fact that
\|F_{i}\|_{op}\leq 1
.
This enables us to bound
\|\Gamma_{x}-\rho_{0}\|_{1}=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n_{d}}\gamma_{i}^{x}|i\rangle\langle i|\|_{1}=O(dn_{d}N^{b-a})
, which is independent of
x
.
Using the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality Eq. (
S1
), or
f(\Gamma_{x},\lambda_{0})\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|_{1}=1-O(n_{d}dN^{b-a})
, we have

\displaystyle f((\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\Gamma_{ij}^{x}|i\rangle\langle j|,\sum_{i,j=1}^{n_{d}}\lambda_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot[1-O(dn_{d}N^{b-a})]

\displaystyle=1-O(dn_{d}N^{b-a})~.

(S19)

Since the above inequality is independent of
|\psi\rangle
, we have
F(\Lambda,\Lambda+\mathcal{B})=1-O(dn_{d}N^{b-a})
.
We therefore have

\epsilon=D_{P}(\Lambda,\Lambda+\mathcal{B})=\sqrt{1-F(\Lambda,\Lambda+\mathcal{B})^{2}}=O(\sqrt{dn_{d}}N^{-(a-b)/2})=O(\sqrt{d}(2s+1)^{d}N^{-(a-b)/2})~,

(S20)

where we have used
n_{d}=(2s+1)^{2d}
.
Since
k=\log_{2}[\frac{M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}}{\Delta}+1]
, and
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
, we have
k=O(b\log_{2}N)
for
|M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}|\sim N^{b}
.

∎

We see that the reason to assume the Kraus operators
K_{i}
all act on the same set of qudits is so that the number of Kraus operator
n_{d}=(2s+1)^{2d}
can be controlled.
In general, if we consider the noise channel
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{d}}K_{i}\rho K_{i}^{\dagger}
, where
K_{i}
can act on different sets of at most
d
qudits, then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=span\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, where
J\sim N^{a}
,
M_{\mathrm{max}}\!-\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
, and
b<a\leq 1
.
\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O(\log_{2}\frac{N^{b}}{\Delta}))\!)
AQECC against a
d
-local noise channel with
\epsilon(d)=O(\sqrt{d}n_{d}N^{(b-a)/2})
if we take
\Delta\geq 4sd+1
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the proof of Theorem
1
. The difference is that, here,
K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{j}
is at most
2d
-local instead of
d
-local, so we need to take
\Delta\geq 4sd+1
.
Moreover, since
F_{i}=\sum_{j}K_{j}[U_{x}]_{ji}
is not necessary
d
-local any more, we can only bound

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=O(dn_{d}^{2}N^{-(a-b)})~,

(S21)

since there are
n_{d}^{2}
terms.
This gives us

D_{P}(\Lambda,\Lambda+\mathcal{B})=O(\sqrt{d}n_{d}N^{-(a-b)/2})~.

(S22)

The above result illustrate the tradeoff between the parameters
d
,
n_{d}
, and the suppression factor
N^{-(a-b)/2}

∎

S5
Code Performance Against i.i.d. errors

While our Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 characterize the code’s performance against general errors, they assume knowledge of the error locations.
This assumption effectively limits the number of Kraus operators.
In particular, the code has a “room for error” of order
O(N^{-(a-b)/2})
.
This means the code cannot effectively correct errors while achieving an asymptotically vanishing inaccuracy if the number of Kraus operators grows faster than this “room for error”, as indicated by Corollary 1.
The assumption of knowing the locations of the error in Theorem 1 is therefore a way to restrict the number of Kraus operators of the error channel.
However, we emphasize that, in the proofs of both Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, we assumed that each Kraus operator has an operator norm of order one, which is a worst-case assumption.
On the other hand, one often expects that error processes involving more qudits occur with a suppressed probability, and thus the corresponding Kraus operators would have smaller operator norms.
This consideration should allow for more Kraus operators, as long as the number of Kraus operators, weighted by their operator norms, grows more slowly than the “room for error.”

With this motivation, we analyze the code performance under the following i.i.d. error model.
For simplicity, we consider the noise channel
\mathcal{N}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{j}
, where each local noise channel acts as

\mathcal{N}_{j}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pA_{j}\rho A_{j}^{\dagger}~,

(S23)

with
A_{j}
a unitary operator acting on site
j
, and
p
the error probability.

This noise channel admits a Kraus representation

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{\ell}}E_{\vec{\ell}}\rho E_{\vec{\ell}}^{\dagger}~,

(S24)

where
\vec{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{N})\in\{0,1\}^{N}
is a binary string, and we use
\ell
to denote its Hamming weight, defined as the number of entries equal to
1
in
\vec{\ell}
.

The corresponding Kraus operator takes the form

E_{\vec{\ell}}:=\sqrt{p_{\ell}}E_{\ell_{1}}E_{\ell_{2}}\cdots E_{\ell_{N}}~,

(S25)

with
p_{\ell}=p^{\ell}(1-p)^{N-\ell}
, where
E_{\ell_{j}=0}=I_{j}
is the identity operator on site
j
, and
E_{\ell_{j}=1}=A_{j}
is the error operator on site
j
.

It is useful to decompose the channel into contributions from errors acting on at most
d
sites, and those acting on more than
d
sites:

\mathcal{N}=P_{d}\mathcal{N}_{d}+P_{>d}\mathcal{N}_{>d}~,

(S26)

where
P_{d}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{N}{\ell}p^{\ell}(1-p)^{N-\ell}
and
P_{>d}=1-P_{d}=\sum_{\ell=d+1}^{N}\binom{N}{\ell}p^{\ell}(1-p)^{N-\ell}
are the respective probabilities. The maps
\mathcal{N}_{d}
and
\mathcal{N}_{>d}
are completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP), describing the
d
-qudit errors and the remaining higher-weight errors, respectively.
More specifically,
\mathcal{N}_{d}(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{\ell}:\ell\leq d}K_{\vec{\ell}}\rho K_{\vec{\ell}}^{\dagger}
, where
K_{\vec{\ell}}=E_{\vec{\ell}}/\sqrt{P_{d}}
. For later convenience, we set
K_{\vec{\ell}}=0
if
\ell>d
.
As we will show, our code can protect against this noise model by correcting
d
-qudit errors
\mathcal{N}_{d}
, with
d\leq(c+o(1))\log N
, provided the error probability
p
satisfies
p\leq(c_{0}^{2}+o(1))N^{-2}\log^{2}N
. We now state this result.

Corollary 2
.

Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=span\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, where
J\sim N^{a}
,
M_{\mathrm{max}}\!-\!M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
,
b<a\leq 1
. Under the i.i.d. noise channel

\mathcal{N}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{j}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{j}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pA_{j}\rho A_{j}^{\dagger}
for a unitary matrix
A_{j}
on site
j
,

\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O(b\log_{2}N))\!)
AQECC by correcting
d
-qudit errors with
d=(c+o(1))\log N
, achieving a code inaccuracy
\epsilon=O(\sqrt{\log N}N^{-\beta/2})
, if
p=(c_{0}^{2}+o(1))(N^{-2}\log^{2}N)
,
\Delta\geq 4sd+1
, and
\beta=a-b-c_{0}-2c>0
.

Proof.

We aim to lower bound the entanglement fidelity
\max_{\mathcal{R}}F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})
, in order to demonstrate that the purified distance is small.
Note that, using the concavity of the fidelity, we have

f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi))\geq P_{d}f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi))+P_{>d}f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{>d}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi))

(S27)

for any state
|\psi\rangle
and channel
\mathcal{R}
.
Suppose
|\psi^{*}\rangle
minimizes
f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))
. We then have
f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi^{*}\rangle\langle\psi^{*}|))\geq\min_{\psi}f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))=F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})
, and similarly
f(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{>d}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi^{*}\rangle\langle\psi^{*}|))\geq F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{>d},\mathrm{id})
.
We therefore have

F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})\geq P_{d}F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})+P_{>d}F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}_{>d},\mathrm{id})

(S28)

for any quantum channel
\mathcal{R}
.

Suppose we can lower bound
\max_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})\geq 1-\epsilon_{d}
, and assume
\mathcal{S}^{*}
is the channel that minimizes the left-hand side. Then we have

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{R}}F(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})

\displaystyle\geq F(\mathcal{S}^{*}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})

\displaystyle\geq P_{d}F(\mathcal{S}^{*}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})+P_{>d}F(\mathcal{S}^{*}\mathcal{N}_{>d},\mathrm{id})

\displaystyle\geq P_{d}(1-\epsilon_{d})=1-(P_{>d}+P_{d}\epsilon_{d})~,

(S29)

where we have used the concavity of entanglement fidelity Eq. (
S28
),
F(\mathcal{S}^{*}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})\geq(1-\epsilon_{d})
, and
F(\mathcal{S}^{*}\mathcal{N}_{>d},\mathrm{id})\geq 0
.
The purified distance is therefore

\min_{\mathcal{R}}D_{P}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})\leq\sqrt{1-(1-P_{>d}-P_{d}\epsilon_{d})^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2(P_{>d}+P_{d}\epsilon_{d})}~.

(S30)

Physically, this inequality indicates that the code inaccuracy can be made small as long as the probability
P_{>d}
is small, and the code achieves small inaccuracy
\epsilon_{d}
when correcting
d
-local errors
\mathcal{N}_{d}
.
As we will show below, we can obtain an upper bound for
\min_{\mathcal{R}}D_{P}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})
by bounding
P_{>d}
and
P_{d}\epsilon_{d}
from above.

First, we lower bound
\max_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})
.
We follow the same strategy as the one used in the proofs of Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 by examining Knill-Laflamme conditions for the noise channel
\mathcal{N}_{d}(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{\ell}}K_{\vec{\ell}}\rho K_{\vec{\ell}}^{\dagger}
, where, for convenience, we set
K_{\vec{\ell}}=0
if
\ell>d
.
Considering the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, from Lemma 1, we have
\langle J\!,\!m|K_{\vec{r}}^{\dagger}K_{\vec{t}}|J\!,\!n\rangle=0
if
|m-n|\geq 4sd+1
, which is satisfied if
\Delta\geq 4sd+1
.
For the diagonal Knill-Laflamme conditions, using Lemma 2, we have

|\langle J\!,\!m|K^{\dagger}_{\vec{r}}K_{\vec{t}}|J\!,\!m\rangle-\langle J\!,\!M_{\min}|K^{\dagger}_{\vec{r}}K_{\vec{t}}|J\!,\!M_{\min}\rangle|=\frac{\sqrt{p_{r}p_{t}}}{P_{d}}\cdot O(dN^{-(a-b)})~,

(S31)

where we have used
\|K_{\vec{\ell}}\|_{\text{op}}=\sqrt{p_{\ell}}
, where
p_{\ell}=(1-p)^{N-\ell}p^{\ell}
and
\ell
is the Hamming weight of the binary vector
\vec{\ell}
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem 1, we take
\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}=\langle J\!,\!M_{\min}|K^{\dagger}_{\vec{r}}K_{\vec{t}}|J\!,\!M_{\min}\rangle
, and the channels
\Lambda
and
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{d}=\Lambda+\mathcal{B}
to be
\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho]|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|
and
(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\mathrm{Tr}[K_{\vec{t}}\rho K^{\dagger}_{\vec{r}}]|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|
, respectively.
To bound the entanglement fidelity
F(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{d},\Lambda)
, consider the state
|\Phi\rangle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}|x\rangle\otimes|x\rangle
. We then have

\displaystyle(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Phi}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\Gamma^{x}_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}\cdot|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|

(S32)

\displaystyle\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Phi}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}\cdot|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|\otimes|x\rangle\langle x|~,

(S33)

where
\Gamma^{x}_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}=\langle J\!,\!M_{x}|K^{\dagger}_{\vec{r}}K_{\vec{t}}|J\!,\!M_{x}\rangle
.
Analogous to Eq. (
S17
), we have

\displaystyle f((\Lambda+\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f\left(\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\Gamma_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}^{x}|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|,\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|\right)

(S34)

due to the joint concavity of fidelity.
We then proceed to bound the fidelity between the density matrices
\Gamma_{x}:=\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\Gamma_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}^{x}|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|
and
\lambda_{0}:=\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}|\vec{r}\rangle\langle\vec{t}|
using the trace distance, obtaining

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\vec{r},\vec{t}}|\Gamma_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}^{x}-\lambda_{\vec{r}\vec{t}}|=\sum_{r=0}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{d}\binom{N}{r}\binom{N}{t}\frac{1}{P_{d}}(1-p)^{N-\frac{1}{2}(r+t)}p^{\frac{1}{2}(r+t)}\cdot O(dN^{-(a-b)})

\displaystyle=P_{d}^{-1}(1-p)^{N}\mathbf{F}_{d}^{2}\cdot O(dN^{-(a-b)})~,

(S35)

where

\mathbf{F}_{d}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{N}{\ell}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{N}{\ell}p_{1}^{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{N}{\ell}p_{2}^{\ell}(1-p_{2})^{N-\ell}\cdot(1+p_{1})^{N}~,

(S36)

where
p_{1}:=\sqrt{p/(1-p)}
and
p_{2}:=p_{1}/(1+p_{1})
.
We are interested in the regime
d=(c+o(1))\log N
. Since we assume
p=(c_{0}^{2}+o(1))N^{-2}\log^{2}N
, we have
p_{2}=\sqrt{p}/(\sqrt{1-p}+\sqrt{p})=(c_{0}+o(1))N^{-1}\log N
, or
Np_{2}=(c_{0}+o(1))\log N
.
Assuming
d=(1-\delta)Np_{2}
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, the Chernoff bound for the binomial distribution gives

\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{N}{\ell}p_{2}^{\ell}(1-p_{2})^{N-\ell}\leq\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}Np_{2})=O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{0}\delta^{2}})~.

(S37)

Since
p_{1}=p_{2}/(1-p_{2})
, we have

(1+p_{1})^{N}=\exp(N\log(1+p_{1}))=\exp(-N\log(1-p_{2}))=O(\exp(Np_{2}))=O(N^{c_{0}})~.

(S38)

Similarly, we have

(1-p)^{N}=\exp(N\log(1-p))=O(\exp(-Np))=O(\exp(-c_{0}^{2}N^{-1}\log^{2}N))~,

(S39)

which is sub-leading compared to
N^{\alpha}
for any exponent
\alpha
.
We therefore have

\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|_{1}=P_{d}^{-1}\cdot O(dN^{-\alpha})~,

(S40)

where
\alpha=a-b+c_{0}(\delta^{2}-2)=a-b-c_{0}-2c+c^{2}/c_{0}+o(1)
, since
\delta=1-d/(Np_{2})=1-c/c_{0}+o(1)
.

Using Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality Eq. (
S1
), we have
f(\Gamma_{x},\lambda_{0})\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\|\Gamma_{x}-\lambda_{0}\|
, and therefore

\max_{\mathcal{S}}F(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{N}_{d},\mathrm{id})=\max_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}F(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{d},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\circ\text{Tr})\geq F(\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{d},\Lambda)\geq 1-P_{d}^{-1}\cdot O(dN^{-\alpha})~,

(S41)

where we used the result in Ref.
[
18
]
for the first equality and the fact that
\text{Tr}[\bullet]
is the complimentary channel of the identity channel
\mathrm{id}
.

Finally, to bound
P_{>d}=\sum_{\ell=d+1}^{N}\binom{N}{\ell}p^{\ell}(1-p)^{N-\ell}
, we have

P_{>d}\leq\binom{N}{d+1}p^{d+1}<\left(\frac{Nep}{(d+1)}\right)^{d+1}<\left(\frac{Nep}{d}\right)^{d+1}=O(\exp(d\log\frac{Nep}{d}))=O(N^{-c\log N})~,

(S42)

which approaches zero faster than
N^{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
, and we have used
\binom{N}{m}<(\frac{Ne}{m})^{m}
.

Substituting the results from Eq. (
S41
) and Eq. (
S42
) into Eq. (
S30
), we have

\epsilon=\min_{\mathcal{R}}D_{P}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},\mathrm{id})=O(\sqrt{\log N}N^{-\beta/2})~,

(S43)

where
\beta:=a-b-c_{0}-2c<\alpha
.
We therefore obtain the result of Corollary 2 as long as
\beta=a-b-c_{0}-2c>0
.

∎

S6
Proof of Lemma 3

In this section, we present the proof of Lemma 3, which bounds the fidelity between the reduced density matrices from code words of different magnetic quantum numbers. This proof is in fact mostly parallel to the proof of Lemma 14 in Ref.
[
20
]
but with a different and generalized physical interpretation, enabled by the usage of Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.

Lemma 3: Consider the irrep of SU(2) formed by
|J\!,\!M\rangle
, where
J=sN
, and the reduced density matrix
\rho_{M}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}[|J\!,\!M\rangle\langle J\!,\!M|]
, where
\bar{\alpha}
is the complement of the set of erased qudits
\alpha
.
We have the fidelity
f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})=1-O(\frac{dM^{2}}{sN^{2}})=1-O(N^{2b+c-2})
asymptotically for
M\sim N^{b}
,
d\sim N^{c}
, and
1>b\geq c
.

Proof.

Note that, since
|J\!,\!M\rangle=\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}|j_{1}\!,\!m_{1}\rangle\otimes|j_{2}\!,\!m_{2}\rangle
, where
C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}=(\langle j_{1},m_{1}|\otimes\langle j_{2},m_{2}|)|J\!,\!M\rangle
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient, we have
\rho_{M}=\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}(C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},M-m_{1}})^{2}|j_{1}m_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}m_{1}|
, where
j_{1}=sd
and
j_{2}=J-j_{1}
, since
J=sN
is the irrep with the highest possible total spin quantum number with multiplicity one.
The fidelity between
\rho_{M}
and
\rho_{M=0}
is therefore
f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})=\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},M-m_{1}}C^{JM=0}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},-m_{1}}
.

For
J=j_{1}+j_{2}
, the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient is

C^{JM}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}=\delta_{M=m_{1}+m_{2}}\left[\frac{\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}}{\binom{2J}{J+M}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}~,

(S44)

where
\binom{N}{k}=\frac{N!}{k!(N-k)!}
is the binomial coefficient.
The fidelity is therefore

f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})=\left[\binom{2J}{J+M}\binom{2J}{J}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}\left[\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}-m_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}~.

(S45)

We are after the asymptotic behavior of the fidelity in the powers of
J
(equivalently
N
), expecting
M\sim J^{b}
and
j_{1}\sim J^{c}
.
Using Stirling’s approximation
\ln(N!)=N\ln N-N+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi N)+\frac{1}{12N}+O(N^{-3})
, we have

\displaystyle\ln\binom{2R}{R+\Delta}

\displaystyle=2R\ln(2R)-2R+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2R)+\frac{1}{24R}+O(R^{-3})

\displaystyle-(R+\Delta)\ln(R+\Delta)+(R+\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(R+\Delta)-\frac{1}{12(R+\Delta)}+O((R+\Delta)^{-3})

\displaystyle-(R-\Delta)\ln(R-\Delta)+(R-\Delta)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(R-\Delta)-\frac{1}{12(R-\Delta)}+O((R-\Delta)^{-3})~.

(S46)

Expecting
\Delta/R\rightarrow 0
when
R\rightarrow\infty
, but also the possibility that
R\sim N
and
\Delta\sim N^{b}
(
b<1
), we use

\displaystyle\ln(R+\Delta)

\displaystyle=\ln R+\ln(1+\frac{\Delta}{R})=\ln R+\frac{\Delta}{R}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2R^{2}}+\frac{\Delta^{3}}{3R^{3}}+O(\Delta^{4}R^{-4})

(S47)

\displaystyle\ln(R-\Delta)

\displaystyle=\ln R+\ln(1-\frac{\Delta}{R})=\ln R-\frac{\Delta}{R}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2R^{2}}-\frac{\Delta^{3}}{3R^{3}}+O(\Delta^{4}R^{-4})

(S48)

\displaystyle(R\pm\Delta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}\mp\frac{\Delta}{R^{2}}+O(\Delta^{2}R^{-3})~,

(S49)

and obtain

\displaystyle\ln\binom{2R}{R+\Delta}

\displaystyle=(2\ln 2)R-\frac{1}{2}\ln R-\frac{1}{2}\ln\pi-\frac{1}{8R}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{R}+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2R^{2}}+O(\Delta^{4}R^{-3})~,

(S50)

or

\displaystyle\binom{2R}{R+\Delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2R}}{\sqrt{\pi R}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8R}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{R}+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2R^{2}}+O(\Delta^{4}R^{-3})\right)~.

(S51)

Using Eq. (
S51
), we have

\displaystyle\left[\binom{2J}{J+M}\binom{2J}{J}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2^{2J}}{\sqrt{\pi J}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8J}-\frac{M^{2}}{J}+\frac{M^{2}}{J^{2}}+O(M^{4}J^{-3})\right)~.

(S52)

Applying Eq.(
S50
) to
\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}
and
\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}-m_{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\ln\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}

\displaystyle=(2\ln 2)(J-j_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\ln(J-j_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\ln\pi-\frac{1}{8(J-j_{1})}-\frac{(M-m_{1})^{2}}{(J-j_{1})}+\frac{(M-m_{1})^{2}}{2(J-j_{1})^{2}}+O(J^{-3}),

(S53)

\displaystyle\ln\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}-m_{1}}

\displaystyle=(2\ln 2)(J-j_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\ln(J-j_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\ln\pi-\frac{1}{8(J-j_{1})}-\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{(J-j_{1})}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{2(J-j_{1})^{2}}+O(J^{-3})~.

(S54)

Using

\displaystyle(J-j_{1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=J^{-1}\left(1+\frac{j_{1}}{J}+O(J^{-2})\right)~,

\displaystyle(J-j_{1})^{-2}

\displaystyle=J^{-2}+O(J^{-3})~,

(S55)

we have

\displaystyle\left[\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}-m_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2(J-j_{1})}}{\sqrt{\pi(J-j_{1})}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8J}-\frac{j_{1}}{8J^{2}}-\frac{(M-m_{1})^{2}+m_{1}^{2}}{2J}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{(M-m_{1})^{2}+m_{1}^{2}}{4J^{2}}(1-2j_{1})+O(J^{-3})\vphantom{\int_{1}^{2}}\right)~.

(S56)

Combining the above equations together, using
(1-j_{1}/J)^{-1/2}=(1+j_{1}/2J+3j_{1}^{2}/8J^{2}+O(J^{-3}))
, and expanding the equation up to
O(J^{-3})
, we have

\displaystyle f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-2j_{1}}}{\sqrt{1-j_{1}/J}}\exp\left(-\frac{j_{1}}{8J^{2}}-\frac{j_{1}M^{2}}{2J^{2}}+O(J^{-3})\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}\exp\left(\frac{Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2}}{J}-\frac{Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2}}{2J^{2}}(1-2j_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-2j_{1}}\left(1+\frac{j_{1}}{2J}+\frac{3j_{1}^{2}}{8J^{2}}+O(J^{-3})\right)\left(1-\frac{j_{1}(1+4M^{2})}{8J^{2}}+O(J^{-3})\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}\left(1+\frac{Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2}}{J}+\frac{(Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2})^{2}}{2J^{2}}-\frac{(Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2})}{2J^{2}}(1-2j_{1})+O(J^{-3})\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-2j_{1}}\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}(1+J^{-1}A_{M,j_{1},m_{1}}+J^{-2}B_{M,j_{1},m_{1}})~,

(S57)

where

\displaystyle A_{M,j_{1},m_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}j_{1}+Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2}~,

\displaystyle B_{M,j_{1},m_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3j_{1}^{2}}{8}-\frac{j_{1}}{8}-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}j_{1}+\frac{1}{2}j_{1}(Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(Mm_{1}-m_{1})^{2}+(j_{1}-\frac{1}{2})(Mm_{1}-m_{1}^{2})~.

(S58)

Upon the change of variables
r=j_{1}+m_{1}
, using equations

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}

\displaystyle=2^{2j_{1}}~,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}r

\displaystyle=j_{1}2^{2j_{1}}~,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}r^{2}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}j_{1}+j_{1}^{2})2^{2j_{1}}~,

(S59)

we have
\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}A_{M,j_{1},m_{1}}=0
.

In addition, using equations

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}r^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}j_{1}^{2}(2j_{1}+3)2^{2j_{1}}~,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}r^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}j_{1}(1-3j_{1}+16j_{1}^{2}+4j_{1}^{3})2^{2j_{1}}~,

(S60)

we have

\displaystyle 2^{-2j_{1}}\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{r}B_{M,j_{1},m_{1}}=-\frac{1}{4}j_{1}M^{2}-\frac{7}{8}j_{1}^{2}+2j_{1}^{2}M^{2}+4j_{1}^{3}M+\frac{1}{2}j_{1}^{3}+\frac{3j_{1}}{8}~.

(S61)

So we have

\displaystyle f(\rho_{M},\rho_{M=0})

\displaystyle=1-J^{-2}\left(\frac{1}{4}j_{1}M^{2}+\frac{7}{8}j_{1}^{2}-2j_{1}^{2}M^{2}-4j_{1}^{3}M-\frac{1}{2}j_{1}^{3}-\frac{3j_{1}}{8}\right)+O(J^{-3})

\displaystyle=1-O\left(\frac{j_{1}M^{2}}{J^{2}}\right)=1-O\left(\frac{dM^{2}}{sN^{2}}\right)=1-O(N^{2b+c-2})~,

(S62)

where we have used
J=sN
,
M\sim J^{b}
and
j_{1}=sd\sim J^{c}
with
1>b\geq c
.
∎

S7
Proof of Theorem 2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem 2, which bounds the code inaccuracy against the
d
-local erasure error. This theorem in fact can be obtained by combining our Lemma 1, our Lemma 3, and Theorem 3 of Ref.
[
20
]
. Here we present the proof tailored to our case to make our presentation self-contained.

Theorem 2: Consider the code space
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, where
J=sN
,
M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
and
b<1
.
\mathfrak{C}
forms an
(\!(N,O[(b\!-\!c)\log_{2}N])\!)
AQECC against heralded
d
-local erasures of size
d\sim N^{c}
, where
0\leq c\leq b<1
, with
\epsilon(d)=O(N^{-(1-b-c/2)})
if we take
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
.

Proof.

The bound of the code inaccuracy against erasure errors can be obtained using Lemma
3
and Theorem 3 in Ref.
[
20
]
. (See also Ref.
[
25
]
.)
A potential concern is the applicability of the aforementioned theorem in Ref.
[
20
]
when
d\sim N^{c}
for some power
c<1
, as the theorem in Ref.
[
20
]
is only addressing the case with
d=O(1)
.
To alleviate the above concern and to make our presentation self-contained, we prove our code-inaccuracy bound against erasure errors at known locations.

We consider the erasure errors at known locations expressed as
\mathcal{N}(\bullet)=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|_{C}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}(\bullet)
, where
\alpha
denotes the set of qudits that are erased whose cardinal number is at most
d
,
C
is the classical registry that records the set of lost qudits, and
{\cal N}^{\alpha}(\bullet):=\mathrm{Tr}_{\alpha}(\bullet)
.
To bound the code inaccuracy, following Bény and Oreshkov
[
18
]
, we bound the entanglement fidelity between the complementary error channel
\widehat{N}(\rho)=(\Lambda+\mathcal{B})(\rho)
and some channel
\Lambda(\rho)
, which needs to be independent of the input
\rho
.
Note that the complementary channel of
\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}
is
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}(\bullet)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}(\bullet)
, where
\bar{\alpha}
is the complement of
\alpha
.
The complementary channel
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
to the full erasure channel is just the original channel, but with each
\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}\to\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}
and with a different reference system
C^{\prime}
.
We consider the channels
\Lambda(\rho):=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|_{C^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}(\rho)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}(\rho):=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho]\cdot\tau^{0}_{\alpha}
, both independent of the input
\rho
and
\tau^{0}_{\alpha}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}(|J,M\!=\!0\rangle\langle J,M\!=\!0|)
.
The goal now is to bound the entanglement fidelity
F(\mathcal{\hat{N}}=\Lambda+\mathcal{B},\Lambda)
.

Recall that the code space is
\mathfrak{C}=span\{|J\!,\!M\rangle;M\!=\!M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!\Delta,M_{\mathrm{min}}\!+\!2\Delta,\dots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
, and we use
|x\rangle_{L}=V^{\dagger}|J,M_{x}\rangle_{A}
,
x=1,\cdots,2^{k}
, to denote the basis in the logical space for
|J,M_{x}\rangle\in\mathfrak{C}
, where
V
is the isometric encoding such that
VV^{\dagger}=\Pi
is the projection onto the code space.
Consider
\rho_{\alpha}^{x,x^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}[|J,M_{x}\rangle\langle J,M_{x^{\prime}}|]
, which is an at-most
d
-local operator. If we pick
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
, from Lemma
1
, we should have
\rho_{\alpha}^{x,x^{\prime}}=0
if
x\neq x^{\prime}
, assuming the size of
\alpha
is at most
d
. We therefore will only consider the case
x=x^{\prime}
and denote
\rho^{x}_{\alpha}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bar{\alpha}}[|J,M_{x}\rangle\langle J,M_{x}|]
for later convenience.

First, using the joint concavity of fidelity for the probability
p_{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))

\displaystyle=f\left(\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|_{C}^{\prime}\otimes(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|_{C}^{\prime}\otimes({\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}f[(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),({\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)]~.

(S63)

To bound
f[(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),({\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)]
, recall that the auxiliary space of
[\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id}]
and
[{\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id}]
is isomorphic to the input space, which is the logical space.
For any state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|x\rangle\otimes|y\rangle
,
consider the operators
A=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|x\rangle\langle y|
and
B:=A^{T}=\sum_{x,y}A_{xy}|y\rangle\langle x|
and the state
|\Phi\rangle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}|x\rangle\otimes|x\rangle
, we have
|\psi\rangle=A\otimes\mathrm{id}|\Phi\rangle=\mathrm{id}\otimes B|\Phi\rangle
.
Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes B)\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)(\mathrm{id}\otimes B^{\dagger})=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\rho_{\alpha}^{x}\otimes B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes B)\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)(\mathrm{id}\otimes B^{\dagger})=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\tau_{\alpha}^{0}\otimes B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}~.

(S64)

Recall that the normalization condition
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_{x}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle=1
, so we can interpret
\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle
as a probability.
Using the joint concavity of fidelity again, we have

\displaystyle f[(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),({\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)]

\displaystyle=f\left(\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot\rho^{x}_{\alpha}\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle},\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot\tau^{0}_{\alpha}\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f\left(\rho^{x}_{\alpha}\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle},\tau^{0}_{\alpha}\otimes\frac{B|x\rangle\langle x|B^{\dagger}}{\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{k}}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\cdot f(\rho^{x}_{\alpha},\tau^{0}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x}\langle x|B^{\dagger}B|x\rangle\left(1-O\left(\frac{j_{1}M_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4J^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1-O\left(\frac{j_{1}M_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4J^{2}}\right)=1-O\left(\frac{|\alpha|M_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4sN^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-O\left(\frac{dM_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4sN^{2}}\right)~,

(S65)

where we have used Lemma
3
,
j_{1}=s|\alpha|
,
J=sN
,
|\alpha|\leq d
, and assumed
|M_{\mathrm{max}}|\geq|M_{\mathrm{min}}|
, without loss of generality.
We therefore have

f(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|),\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))=1-O\left(\frac{dM_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4sN^{2}}\right)~.

(S66)

Since it holds for arbitrary
|\psi\rangle
, we have
F(\widehat{N},\Lambda)=1-O\left(\frac{dM_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}{4sN^{2}}\right)
, or
\epsilon=D_{P}(\mathcal{\hat{N}},\Lambda)=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2s}}\frac{M_{\mathrm{max}}}{N}\right)
.
In the scaling regime
M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}\sim N^{b}
and
d\sim N^{c}
, we have
\epsilon=O(N^{b+c/2-1})
.
The number of logical qubits is
k=\log_{2}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{max}}-M_{\mathrm{min}}}{\Delta}+1\right)
. Since
d\sim N^{c}
, we need to take the spacing of the magnetic quantum number
\Delta=\Omega(N^{c})
. We therefore have
k=O((b-c)\log_{2}N)
as claimed.

∎

Calculation of QFI loss and measurements

In this section, we present the details of the numerical calculation for the QFI loss shown in the main text. In addition, we propose a simple
\theta
-independent local measurement for the noiseless probe state and a
\theta
-independent global measurement for the partially-erased probe state that can saturate the quantum Cramér-Rao bound. We also propose a local measurement for the partially-erased probe state, which can give a scaling better than the standard quantum limit in some parameter regime, though it does not saturate the quantum Cramér-Rao bound.

We consider a system with
N
spin-
s
degrees of freedom and the ideal probe state
|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|J,M\rangle+|J,-M\rangle)
, where
J=sN
, and the quantum metrology problem where the sensing parameter
\theta
couples to
\hat{Q}_{z}
,
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=e^{-i\theta\hat{Q}_{z}}|\psi\rangle
, or
\psi_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(|J,M\rangle\langle J,M|+|J,-M\rangle\langle J,-M|+e^{-i2M\theta}|J,M\rangle\langle J,-M|+e^{i2M\theta}|J,-M\rangle\langle J,M|)
.
Since
\hat{Q}_{z}|J,M\rangle=M|J,M\rangle
, we see that the QFI of this probe state is
\mathfrak{F}(\psi_{\theta})=4(\langle\hat{Q}_{z}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{Q}_{z}\rangle^{2})=4M^{2}
.

Consider the probe state going through an erasure channel
\rho_{\theta}=\mathcal{N}(\psi_{\theta}):=\mathrm{Tr}_{d}[\psi_{\theta}]
, where
\mathrm{Tr}_{d}[...]
means tracing out
d
sites. Note that, since the
J=sN
irrep is totally permutationally symmetric, we can just assume that we trace out the first
d
sites.
To calculate the QFI of
\rho_{\theta}
, we need to find its eigendecomposition. This can be achieved as follows.
First, note that we can rewrite

|J,M\rangle=\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}C^{J,M}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},M-m_{1}}|j_{1},m_{1}\rangle_{d}\otimes|j_{2},M-m_{1}\rangle_{\bar{d}}~,

(S67)

where
C^{J,M}_{j_{1},m_{1},j_{2},m_{2}}
are the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients,
j_{1}=sd
,
j_{2}=J-j_{1}
, and
\bar{d}
is the complement of the
d
sites to be traced out.
Defining
a_{m}=C^{J,M}_{j_{1},m,j_{2},M-m}
and
b_{m}=C^{J,-M}_{j_{1},m,j_{2},-M-m}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(|J,M\rangle\langle J,M|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}^{2}|j_{2},M-m\rangle\langle j_{2},M-m|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(|J,-M\rangle\langle J,-M|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}b_{m}^{2}|j_{2},-M-m\rangle\langle j_{2},-M-m|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(|J,M\rangle\langle J,-M|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}|j_{2},M-m\rangle\langle j_{2},-M-m|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(|J,-M\rangle\langle J,M|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}|j_{2},-M-m\rangle\langle j_{2},M-m|~.

(S68)

Note that we consider the case
M>j_{1}
so that
|j_{2},M-m\rangle
and
|j_{2},-M-m^{\prime}\rangle
are always distinct states for
m,m^{\prime}=-j_{1}\dots j_{1}
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\bigoplus_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}a_{m}^{2}&e^{-i2M\theta}a_{m}b_{m}\\
e^{i2M\theta}a_{m}b_{m}&b_{m}^{2}\end{pmatrix}:=\bigoplus_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}B_{m}~,

(S69)

where the matrix
B_{m}
is represented by the basis
|j_{2},M-m\rangle
and
|j_{2},-M-m\rangle
.
We also see that
\rho_{\theta}
can be interpreted as an ensemble labeled by
m=-j_{1},\cdots,j_{1}
, where the
\theta
information is encoded in the coherence between the states
|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle
and
|j_{2},-M\!-\!m\rangle
, which have a magnetic quantum number separation
2M
.
The eigenvalues of the
2\times 2
matrix
B_{m}
are
\frac{1}{2}(a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2})
and
0
, and the corresponding eigenvectors are

\displaystyle|m\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda_{m}}}(e^{-iM\theta}a_{m}|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle+e^{iM\theta}b_{m}|j_{2},-M\!-\!m\rangle)~,

(S70)

\displaystyle|\bar{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda_{m}}}(e^{-iM\theta}b_{m}|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle-e^{iM\theta}a_{m}|j_{2},-M\!-\!m\rangle)~,

(S71)

respectively.
Note that
\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}=-iM\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}(e^{-2iM\theta}|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle\langle j_{2},-\!M\!-\!m|-e^{2iM\theta}|j_{2},-\!M\!-\!m\rangle\langle j_{2},M\!-\!m|)
.
Therefore, we obtain
\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m^{\prime}\rangle=0
for both
m\neq m^{\prime}
and
m=m^{\prime}
.
On the other hand,
(\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})^{2}=\oplus_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}M^{2}a_{m}^{2}b_{m}^{2}I_{m}
, where
I_{m}
is the identity matrix in the
|J,M\!-\!m\rangle,|J,-M\!-\!m\rangle
subspace.
Denoting the set of
m
such that
\lambda_{m}\neq 0
as
\Lambda
(it is possible that some of
\lambda_{m}
are zero), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(\rho_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}:\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}}>0}\frac{2|\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m^{\prime}\rangle|^{2}}{\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\frac{2|\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m^{\prime}\rangle|^{2}}{\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m\in\Lambda}\frac{4}{\lambda_{m}}\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}(I-\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\Lambda}|m^{\prime}\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|)\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}}|\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{m\in\Lambda}\frac{4}{\lambda_{m}}\langle m|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}(I-\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\Lambda}|m^{\prime}\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|)\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|m\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\Lambda}\frac{4a_{m}^{2}b_{m}^{2}}{\lambda_{m}}M^{2}=\sum_{m\in\Lambda}\left(\frac{2a_{m}^{2}b_{m}^{2}}{a_{m}^{2}+b_{m}^{2}}\right)4M^{2}~.

(S72)

Finally, we note that
\mathfrak{F}(\psi_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})=4M^{2}
and
\Delta\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{F}(\psi_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})-\mathfrak{F}(\rho_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})
, and the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients can be calculated efficiently via a recursive formula or an explicit formula, which gives us an efficient method to calculate the QFI loss.
We calculate the QFI by picking values of
J
and
j_{1}
. So the result is applicable to different
s
with the corresponding
N=J/s
and
d=j_{1}/s
.

It is interesting to examine the QFI loss if the probe state is a GHZ(-like) state
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|J,J\rangle+|J,-J\rangle)
, which would achieve the Heisenberg scaling in the absence of noise.
We can express
|J,J\rangle=|j_{1},j_{1}\rangle\otimes|j_{2},j_{2}\rangle
and
|J,-J\rangle=|j_{1},-j_{1}\rangle\otimes|j_{2},-j_{2}\rangle
, which gives us
\rho_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(|j_{2},j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2},j_{2}|+|j_{2},-j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2},-j_{2}|)
independent of
\theta
. This implies that
\mathfrak{F}(\rho_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})=0
.
This can also be obtained from Eq. (
S72
). Note that, in this case, the only nonzero
\lambda_{m}
is
m=j_{1}
or
m=-j_{1}
, and we have
(a_{m},b_{m})=(1,0)
if
m=j_{1}
and
(a_{m},b_{m})=(0,1)
if
m=-j_{1}
, respectively, so
\mathfrak{F}(\rho_{\theta};\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})=0
.
As expected, noise obstructs us from using GHZ-like states for optimal sensing.

We propose a simple local measurement for the ideal probe state
|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|J,M\rangle+|J,-M\rangle)
which saturates the quantum Cramér-Rao bound. Specifically, we consider the observable
\mathbf{D}:=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}D_{j}
, where
D_{j}
satisfies
D_{j}\hat{q}^{z}_{j}D_{j}=-\hat{q}^{z}_{j}
.
This is a local measurement since one can measure
\mathbf{D}
by measuring
D_{j}
locally and multiplying the outcomes.
For example, in the basis
\hat{q}^{z}_{j}|\Gamma\rangle=\Gamma|\Gamma\rangle
, where
\Gamma=-s,\cdots s
, one can pick
D_{j}=\sum_{\Gamma=-s}^{s}|s\rangle\langle-s|
, which would give
\mathbf{D}^{2}=I
.
The unknown parameter
\theta
can therefore be obtained by measuring
\langle\mathbf{D}\rangle=\langle\psi_{\theta}|\mathbf{D}|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=\cos(2M\theta)
, and we have
\theta=\arccos(\langle\mathbf{D}\rangle)/(2M)
.
The variance is
\Gamma_{\mathbf{D}}^{2}=\langle\mathbf{D}^{2}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{D}\rangle^{2}=1-\cos^{2}(2M\theta)
, which gives the standard error of the measurement as
\Delta\mathbf{D}=\Gamma_{\mathbf{D}}/\sqrt{\nu}
, where
\nu
is the number of samples.
So we have
\Delta\theta=\frac{\Delta\mathbf{D}}{2M}\frac{\arccos(x)}{dx}|_{x=\langle\mathbf{D}\rangle}=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\nu}}
, which indeed saturates the quantum Cramér-Rao bound since QFI
\mathfrak{F}(\psi_{\theta})=4M^{2}
.

For the partially-erased probe state
\rho_{\theta}=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\lambda_{m}|m\rangle\langle m|
, recall that the
\theta
information is encoded in the coherence between the states
|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle
and
|j_{2},-M\!-\!m\rangle
.
A global measurement such as
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}|j_{2},M\!-\!m\rangle\langle j_{2},-M\!-\!m|+\text{h.c.}
would be a candidate to estimate the unknown
\theta
.
Indeed, we see
\langle\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\theta}\mathbf{D}^{\prime}]=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}\cos(2M\theta)=A\cos(2M\theta)
, where
A:=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}
.
Since
\mathbf{D}^{\prime 2}=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}I_{m}
, we have
\Gamma_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime}}^{2}=1-A^{2}\cos^{2}(2M\theta)
, and

\Delta\theta=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\nu}2MA\sin(2M\theta)}\sim\frac{1}{2MA}~.

(S73)

Note that

A=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}a_{m}b_{m}=\left[\binom{2J}{J+M}\right]^{-1}\sum_{m_{1}=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\binom{2j_{1}}{j_{1}+m_{1}}\left[\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}+M-m_{1}}\binom{2J-2j_{1}}{J-j_{1}-M-m_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}~,

(S74)

and the asymptotic expansion for
A
can be carried out like in Sec.
S6
. We expect
A=1-O(j_{1}M^{2}/J^{2})
, which gives us
\Delta\theta\sim M^{-1}=N^{-b}
.
This scaling matches with the QFI scaling in the red-shaded parameter regime of Fig.
1
(b) in the main text.

A global measurement is generally hard to implement so it is usually more desirable to use a local measurement.
Below, we show that the local measurement
\mathbf{D}_{\bar{d}}:=\bigotimes_{j=d+1}^{N}D_{j}
can be used to obtain
\theta
with a
\Delta\theta
scaling beyond the standard quantum limit, although it does not saturate the scaling given by the QFI.
Note that, for
\rho_{\theta}=\sum_{m=-j_{1}}^{j_{1}}\lambda_{m}|m\rangle\langle m|
, if
j_{1}
is a half-integer, then we can throw out one more qudit so that
j_{1}
is an integer.
We thus see that
\mathbf{D}_{\bar{d}}
will pick up the signal from the coherence between states
|j_{2}\!,\!M\!-\!m\rangle
and
|j_{2}\!,\!-M-m\rangle
only when
m=0
.
More explicitly, we have
\langle\mathbf{D}_{\bar{d}}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\theta}\mathbf{D}_{\bar{d}}]=a_{0}b_{0}\cos(2M\theta)
.
Comparing this result with Eq. (
S73
) and noting that
a_{0}b_{0}\sim(j_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
we obtain
\Delta\theta\sim\sqrt{j_{1}}/M\sim N^{-(b-c/2)}
if
j_{1}\sim N^{c}
and
M\sim N^{b}
, exhibiting a scaling beyond the standard quantum limit if
2b-c>1
, together with the conditions
b>c
and
c<2(1-b)
. For
c=0
, the local measurement would give a variance scaling matching the QFI scaling.

S9
A Recovery Channel for AQECC

While a recovery operation is not required for metrological applications, it is nevertheless insightful to understand how a recovery channel can be constructed for our AQECC in the context of quantum error correction.
Refs.
[
18
,
71
]
provide a general framework for constructing nearly optimal recovery channels, which we briefly outline below.

First, we calculate
\lambda_{ij}
in the Knill-Laflamme condition. While in our earlier analysis, we used
\lambda_{ij}=\langle J,M_{\mathrm{min}}|K_{i}^{\dagger}{K_{j}}|J,M_{\mathrm{min}}\rangle
, in fact, any
M
satisfying
M_{\mathrm{min}}\leq M\leq M_{\mathrm{max}}
yields an equally good choice for
\lambda_{ij}=\langle J,M|K_{i}^{\dagger}{K_{j}}|J,M\rangle
for the purpose of constructing a recovery channel.
One then considers the following minimization problem

F_{\rho}=\min_{\rho}\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\Phi_{\rho}(\mathbf{I})}~,

(S75)

where

\Phi_{\rho}(\mathbf{I})=\sum_{ij}K_{i}\rho^{2}K_{j}^{\dagger}\lambda_{ij}~,

(S76)

and the minimization is over the density matrix
\rho=\sum_{a,b=1}^{2^{k}}\rho_{ab}|J\!,\!M_{a}\rangle\langle J\!,\!M_{b}|
in the logical space.

Suppose
\rho_{0}
is the density matrix which minimizes the above equation.
If
\rho_{0}
is unique and of full rank, then the following recovery channel is nearly optimal:

\mathcal{R}(\tau)=\Phi_{\rho_{0}}^{\dagger}[\Phi_{\rho_{0}}(\mathbf{I})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau\Phi_{\rho_{0}}(\mathbf{I})^{-\frac{1}{2}}]+\mathcal{T}(\tau)~,

(S77)

where

\Phi_{\rho}^{\dagger}(\bullet)=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}[\rho E_{j}^{\dagger}(\bullet)E_{i}\rho]~,

(S78)

and
\mathcal{T}(\tau)
is some completely positive map chosen to ensure that
\mathcal{R}(\tau)
is trace-preserving.

S10
Spin-1 XY-Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Scar States as AQECC

In this appendix, we show that the quantum many-body scar states in the spin-1 XY-Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya model
[
72
,
73
,
74
]
can form an AQECC.
While the original model considers a system with
N
spin-
1
degrees of freedom, here we generalize it to
N
spin-
s
degrees of freedom.
Specifically, we consider the spin-
s
local Hilbert space with the basis
\{|m\rangle,m=-s,\cdots,s\}
.
We define the operators

\displaystyle J^{z}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|s\rangle\langle s|-|-s\rangle\langle-s|)_{j}~,~~

\displaystyle J^{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\phi_{j}}|s\rangle\langle-s|_{j}~,~~~~J^{-}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-i\phi_{j}}|-s\rangle\langle s|_{j}~,

(S79)

where
\phi_{j}
is some site-dependent phase.
It is easy to verify that these operators generate the
SU(2)
algebra
[J^{+},J^{-}]=2J^{z}
and
[J^{z},J^{\pm}]=\pm J^{\pm}
.
The quantum many-body scar states are
|S_{M}\rangle:=Z_{M}(J^{+})^{M}|S_{0}\rangle
, where
|S_{0}\rangle=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}|-\!s\rangle_{j}
and
Z_{M}
is the normalization constant.
The scar states in the spin-1 XY-Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya model correspond to
s=1
with some specific choice of
\phi_{j}
.

In fact, these scar states are Dicke states in disguise. This can be seen by redefining
|s\rangle_{j}\rightarrow e^{-\phi_{j}/2}|s\rangle_{j}
and
|-s\rangle_{j}\rightarrow e^{\phi_{j}/2}|s\rangle_{j}
, which can be achieved by applying a tensor product of single-site unitaries
U=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}
, where
u_{j}=|s\rangle\langle s|_{j}e^{-\phi_{j}/2}+|\!-\!s\rangle\langle\!-\!s|_{j}e^{\phi_{j}/2}+\sum_{m=-s+1\cdots s-1}|m\rangle\langle m|_{j}
.
We therefore see that
|S_{M}\rangle
are Dicke states
|J\!,\!M\rangle
(
s=\frac{1}{2}
,
J=\frac{N}{2}
) embedded in a larger local Hilbert space, up to a tensor product of single-site unitary.
Therefore, we conclude that the quantum many-body scar states
\mathfrak{C}=\text{span}\{|S_{M}\rangle;M=M_{\mathrm{min}},M_{\mathrm{min}}+\Delta,\cdots,M_{\mathrm{max}}\}
form an AQECC against
d
-local noise or erasures at known locations if we take
\Delta\geq 2sd+1
.
The code parameters and the inaccuracy bound against different errors can be readily obtained from Theorems
1
and
2
with
s=\frac{1}{2}
,
J=\frac{N}{2}
.

The Regularity of difference divisors
Baiqing Zhu

Abstract
. For a prime number p > 2 p>2 and a finite extension F / ℚ p F/\mathbb{Q}_{p} , we explain the construction of the difference divisors on the unitary Rapoport-Zink spaces of hyperspecial level over 𝒪 F ˘ \mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}} , and the GSpin Rapoport-Zink spaces of hyperspecial level over ℤ ˘ p \breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p} associated to a minuscule cocharacter μ \mu and a basic element b b . We prove the regularity of the difference divisors, find the formally smooth locus of both the special cycles and the difference divisors, by a purely deformation-theoretic approach.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer. Let
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}
be the unitary Rapoport-Zink space of signature
(1,n-1)
with hyperspecial level. Kudla-Rapoport special divisors
\mathcal{Z}(x)
on
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}
have been defined and extensively studied in the works of Kudla and Rapoport
[
KR11
]
. Then Terstiege introduced the difference divisors on the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}
in
[
Ter13a
]
, he also proved the regularity of difference divisors in
[
Ter13b
]
. A key step in the proof is a previous result in his joint work with Rapoport and Zhang
[
RTZ13
, Theorem 10.7]
, whose proof is based on the windows theory developed by Zink in
[
Zin01
]
and
[
Zin02
]
.

For a self-dual quadratic lattice
V
of rank
m=n+1
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, a Hodge type Rapoport-Zink space
\mathcal{N}(V)
associated to a minuscule cocharacter
\mu
and a basic element
b
has been constructed and studied in the works of Kim
[
Kim18
]
and Howard-Pappas
[
HP17
]
. When
V
is one of the self-dual lattices of rank 4, Terstiege defined difference divisors on the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}(V)
and studied the intersection numbers of them in
[
Ter11
]
, where he also proved the regularity of these difference divisors. The notion of difference divisors can be defined similarly on
\mathcal{N}(V)
for general
V
, but the regularity of these difference divisors was previously not known to us. It’s difficult to prove a similar result as
[
RTZ13
, Theorem 10.7]
since the
p
-divisible groups parameterized by the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}(V)
have dimension
2^{n}
and height
2^{n+1}
, so the windows theory becomes much more complicated in this case.

Main results

p

\mathbb{F}

E/F
be an unramified extension. The unitary Rapoport-Zink space
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}
is a formally smooth formal scheme over
\textup{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}

\mathcal{O}_{E}
-modules of signature

\lx@sectionsign
2.1.1

\mathcal{N}^{u}

\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}
for simplicity. Let
\overline{\mathbb{Y}}
be the unique (up to isomorphism) hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-module of signature
(0,1)

\mathbb{F}

\mathbb{X}

\mathcal{O}_{E}
-module of signature
(1,n-1)
over
\mathbb{F}
. The space of space quasi-homomorphisms
\mathbb{V}^{u}\coloneqq\textup{Hom}^{\circ}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}(\overline{\mathbb{Y}},\mathbb{X})
carries a natural
E/F
-hermitian form
(\cdot,\cdot)
(
\lx@sectionsign
2.2.1
).

In the GSpin case, let
V
be a self-dual quadratic lattice of rank
m=n+1
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Associated to
V
we have a local unramified Shimura-Hodge datum
(G,b,\mu,C(V))
, where
G=\textup{GSpin}(V)
,
b\in G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is a basic element,
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow G
is a certain cocharacter, and

p

\mathbb{X}_{V}

\textup{RZ}(G,b,\mu,C(V))
of Hodge type, parametrizing
p
-divisible groups quasi-isogenous to
\mathbb{X}_{V}
with crystalline Tate tensors . It is a formally smooth formal scheme over
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
(
\lx@sectionsign
2.1.2
). Let
\mathcal{N}(V)
be a connected component of the formal scheme
\textup{RZ}(G,b,\mu,C(V))
. The space of special quasi-homomorphisms
\mathbb{V}^{o}\coloneqq(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})^{\overline{b}\circ\sigma}
can be identified as a subspace of
\textup{End}^{\circ}(\mathbb{X}_{V})
(
\lx@sectionsign
2.2.2
), where
\overline{b}
is the image of

under the homomorphism
G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\rightarrow\textup{SO}(V)(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
,
\sigma\in\textup{Gal}(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the arithmetic Frobenius automorphism on
\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. The
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
\mathbb{V}^{o}
carries a natural quadratic form
q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}
induced from
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Denote by
\mathcal{N}^{o}
the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}(V)
.

For any subset
L\subset\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
L\subset\mathbb{V}^{o}
). The special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is a closed formal subscheme of
\mathcal{N}^{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}^{o}
), over which each quasi-homomorphism
x\in L
deforms to homomorphisms. When
L=\{x\}
for a nonzero element
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
). The special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(x)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(\{x\})
is a Cartier divisor on the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}^{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}^{o}
) (
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
). There is a closed immersion of divisors
\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}(x)
(resp.
\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}(x)
), define the difference divisor associated to
x
to be the following Cartier divisor on
\mathcal{N}^{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}^{o}
),

\mathcal{D}(x)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(x)-\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x)\,\,\,\,\textup{(resp. $\mathcal{D}(x)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(x)-\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)$)}.

This definition recovers Terstiege’s definition of difference divisors in
[
Ter13b
]
(unitary case,
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
), and in
[
Ter11
]
(GSpin case,
V
is a specific rank
4
self-dual lattice over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
).
The main results of this article are stated in the following theorem (Theorem
5.4.1
and Corollary
5.4.3
).

Theorem 1.2.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
) be a nonzero special quasi-homomorphism such that
(x,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
(resp.
q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
).

(i)

There is an equality
\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
.

(ii)

The difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is a regular formal scheme. It is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
(resp.
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) of relative dimension
n-2
at a point
z\in\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})
if and only if
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
(resp.
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
) and there exists a lift of
z
to
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}/\varpi^{2})
(resp.
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/p^{2})
).

(iii)

Let
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
be a point. Let
\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{D}(x)\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}\mathbb{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{Z}(x)\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}\mathbb{F}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{D}(x)\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{Z}(x)\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
). Then

\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})=\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}),

where
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})
and
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})
are the tangent spaces of the formal schemes
\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}
at the point
z
, respectively.

We give some examples of the equality
\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
in Theorem
1.2.1
(i). See also Example
5.4.2
.

Example 1.2.2
.

(a)

The orthogonal case: Let
H
be the unique rank
4
self-dual quadratic lattice over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
whose discriminant is not a square in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
be a non-isotropic vector and
n=\nu_{p}(q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x))
. Let
x^{\prime}=p^{-[n/2]}\cdot x
. Let
\mathscr{B}
be the Bruhat-Tits building of
\textup{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
. The element
x^{\prime}
induces an automorphism of
\mathscr{B}
. Let
\mathscr{B}^{x^{\prime}}
be the fixed set. For a vertex
[\Lambda]
in the Bruhat-Tits building
\mathscr{B}
of
\textup{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
, let
d_{[\Lambda]}
be the distance of
[\Lambda]
to
\mathscr{B}^{x^{\prime}}
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{Z}(x)\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
and
\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{D}(x)\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
. By
[
Ter11
, Theorem 3.6]
, we have the following equality of effective Cartier divisors on
\mathcal{N}^{o}_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}^{o}\times_{W}\mathbb{F}
,

\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\sum\limits_{[\Lambda]:d_{[\Lambda]}\leq(n-1)/2}(1+p+\cdots+p^{(n-1)/2-d_{[\Lambda]}})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{[\Lambda]}+p^{n/2}\cdot s,

where
\mathbb{P}_{[\Lambda]}
is a divisor associated to the vertex
[\Lambda]
and isomorphic to the projective line
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}
. The symbol
s
represents another divisor which only depends on the element
x^{\prime}
and is nonempty only if
n
is even. Since
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{Z}(x)-\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)
, we have

\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{[\Lambda]:d_{[\Lambda]}=(n-1)/2}\mathbb{P}_{[\Lambda]}+\sum\limits_{[\Lambda]:d_{[\Lambda]}\leq(n-3)/2}p^{(n-1)/2-d_{[\Lambda]}}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{[\Lambda]}+(p^{n/2}-p^{(n-2)/2})\cdot s,&\textup{if $n\geq 1$;}\\
s,&\textup{if $n=0$.}\end{cases}

Therefore
\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
.

(b)

The unitary case: Let
n=3
and
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. For an element
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
, the proof of (
[
Ter13a
, Proposition 2.13]
) implies that if
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
, then the local equation of the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is not a unit at the point
z
, hence
\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})\subset\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})
. Therefore
\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
.

1.3.
Strategy and novelty of the proof

We prove Theorem
1.2.1
by a purely deformation-theoretic approach.

In the unitary case, we have the Grothendieck-Messing deformation theory of hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-modules of signature
(1,n-1)
. In the GSpin case, similar deformation results (Lemma
3.2.1
) are also available in Madapusi’s work
[
MP16
, Proposition 5.16]
(see also the works of Li and Zhang
[
LZ22b
, Lemma 4.6.2]
).

We prove a commutative algebra result Lemma
5.1.2
which helps us to identify a sequence of regular elements in a formally finitely generated and formally smooth algebra over a discrete valuation ring.

Our final proof combines the deformation theory and the commutative algebra results. In stead of analyzing one single difference divisor, we study a sequence of difference divisors
\{\mathcal{D}(\varpi^{a}x)\}_{a\geq 0}
(resp.
\{\mathcal{D}(p^{a}x)\}_{a\geq 0}
). We show that the local equations of difference divisors in this sequence satisfy the assumptions in Lemma
5.1.2
. The verification is a series of deformation-theoretic computations based on Lemma
3.1.5
and Lemma
3.2.1
, the computations are much easier compared to windows theory and works for general finite extension
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
since both two lemmas are of Grothendieck-Messing type.

1.4.
Applications

1.4.1.
Linear invariance

Li and Zhang
[
LZ22a
, Lemma 2.8.1]
proved the linear invariance of the derived intersections on the Rapoport-Zink space
\mathcal{N}^{u}
based on the regularity of difference divisors, which guarantees that the local arithmetic intersection numbers on
\mathcal{N}^{u}
are well-defined.

The parallel result in the GSpin case is proved by Terstiege
[
Ter11
, Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.2]
(see also
[
LZ22b
, Lemma 4.11.1]
) using globalization. Our result can be applied to give an alternative proof of the linear invariance of the derived intersections on the Rapoport-Zink space
\mathcal{N}^{o}
along the lines of
[
LZ22a
, Lemma 2.8.1]
(see
[
LZ22b
, foodnote 3]
), which guarantees that the local arithmetic intersection numbers on
\mathcal{N}^{o}
are well-defined.

1.4.2.
Rapoport-Zink spaces with level structures

In some cases, difference divisors can be identified with Rapoport-Zink spaces with certain level structures. We give several examples.

(a)

When the valuation of the special quasi-homomorphism
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
) has valuation
0
, i.e.,
\nu_{\varpi}((x,x))=0
(resp.
\nu_{p}(q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x))=0
). Then the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{Z}(x)
is isomorphic to the unitary (resp. GSpin) Rapoport-Zink space of hyperspecial level of one dimension lower (
[
RSZ18
, Remark 4.5]
and
[
LZ17
, Lemma 3.2.2]
).

In the GSpin case, when the valuation of the special quasi-homomorphism
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
has valuation
1
, i.e.,
\nu_{p}(q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x))=1
. Then the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{Z}(x)
is isomorphic to the GSpin Rapoport-Zink space of almost self-dual level of one dimension lower (
[
Oki20
, Theorem 4.5]
).

(b)

In the unitary case, let
n=2
. Then
\mathcal{N}^{u}\simeq\textup{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}[[t]]
. Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
be a special quasi-homomorphism such that
\nu_{\varpi}((x,x))=k\geq 0
. Then the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is isomorphic to the quasi-canonical lifting of level
k
with respect to the unramified extension
E/F
(
[
KR11
, Proposition 8.1]
for the case
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which also works for general field
F
).

In the GSpin case, we take
V=\textup{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\textup{tr}=0}
equipped with the quadratic form given by the determinant. Then
\mathcal{N}^{o}\simeq\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[t]]
. Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
be a special quasi-homomorphism such that
\nu_{p}(q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x))=k\geq 0
. Then the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is isomorphic to the quasi-canonical lifting of level
[k/2]
with respect to an unramified extension (when
k
is even) or ramified extension (when
k
is odd) (
[
GK93
, (5.10)]
, see also
[
LZ22b
,
\lx@sectionsign
5.1]
).

(c)

Let
V=\textup{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with the quadratic form given by the determinant. It is a self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice of rank
4
. In this case, we have
\mathcal{N}^{o}\simeq\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[[t_{1},t_{2}]]
,
\mathbb{V}^{o}
is the unique division quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
be an element such that
\nu_{p}(q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x))=k
for some integer
k\geq 0
. The difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
on
\mathcal{N}^{o}
is isomorphic to the Rapoport-Zink space associated with the modular curve
\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{k})
(cf.
[
Zhu23
, Theorem 6.2.3]
). A key ingredient in the proof is the regularity of the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
. Notice that the definition of difference divisor is relatively simple, while the Drinfeld level structure defining the modular curve
\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{k})
(cf.
[
KM85
]
, see also
[
Dri74
]
) is much more subtle.

2.
Special cycles on Rapoport-Zink spaces

2.1.
Rapoport-Zink spaces

2.1.1.
Unitary Rapoport-Zink space
\mathcal{N}^{u}

Let
E
be the unramified quadratic extension of
F
, and
\mathcal{O}_{E}
be the integer ring of
E
. Fix an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra embedding
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{O}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
and denote by
\phi_{1}
the embedding
\sigma\circ\phi_{0}:\mathcal{O}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
. The embedding
\phi_{0}
induces an embedding between the residue fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, which we shall think of as the natural embedding.

Let
n
be a positive integer. For an
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
-scheme
S
, a hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-module of signature
(1,n-1)
over an object
S
in
\textup{Nilp}_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}
is a triple
(X,\iota,\lambda)
where

(1)

X
is a formal
\varpi
-divisible group over
S
of relative height
2n
and dimension
n
;

(2)

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{E}\rightarrow\textup{End}(X)
is an action satisfying the signature
(1,n-1)
condition, i.e., for
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
,

\textup{char}(\iota(\alpha):\textup{Lie}\,X)(T)=(T-\phi_{0}(\alpha))(T-\phi_{1}(\alpha))^{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{S}[T];

(3)

\lambda:X\rightarrow X^{\vee}
is a principal polarization such that the associated Rosati involution induces
\alpha\mapsto\sigma(\alpha)
on
\mathcal{O}_{E}
via
\iota
.

Up to
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-linear quasi-isogeny compatible with the polarizations, there is a unique such triple
\mathbb{X}=(\mathbb{X},\iota_{\mathbb{X}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}})
over
S=\textup{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}
. The unitary Rapoport-Zink space of signature
(1,n-1)
is the following functor
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}:\textup{Nilp}_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}\rightarrow\textbf{Sets}
: for a scheme
S
in the category
\textup{Nilp}_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}
, the set of
\mathcal{N}_{1,n-1}(S)
is the isomorphism classes of quadruples
(X,\iota,\lambda,\rho)
, where
(X,\iota,\lambda)
is a hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-module of signature
(1,n-1)
over
S
and
\rho:X\times_{S}\overline{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}\times_{\mathbb{F}}\overline{S}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-linear quasi-isogeny of height zero such that
\rho^{\ast}((\lambda_{\mathbb{X}})_{\overline{S}})=\lambda_{\overline{S}}
. Two quadruples

Let

\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module of rank

for all

=

Let

be an integer, and

be a self-dual quadratic lattice of rank

over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

of

(1)

\psi_{\delta}(c_{1},c_{2})=\textup{Trd}(c_{1}\delta c_{2}^{\ast})

The spinor similitude group

is a reductive group over

-algebra

The group

(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
be a finite set of tensors

in the total tensor algebra

cutting out

from
\textup{GL}(C(V))

\boldsymbol{\pi}

1.3]

\mathbf{F}_{z}^{(2,2)}:\textup{End}_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\mathbb{D}(X_{z})_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})\rightarrow\textup{End}_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\mathbb{D}(X_{z})_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}),\,\,f\mapsto\mathbf{F}_{z}\circ f\circ\mathbf{F}_{z}^{-1}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{N}^{o}
in
\lx@sectionsign
2.1.2
, the crystalline Tate tensor
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\textup{crys},z}
is Frobenius invariant. Hence
\mathbf{F}_{z}^{(2,2)}
acts on
\mathbf{V}_{z,\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}=\textup{im}\,\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\textup{crys},z}(\textup{End}_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\mathbb{D}(X_{z})_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}))
as a Frobenius-linear isomorphism. We use
\Phi_{z}
to denote this action.

Let
\tilde{z}\in\mathcal{N}^{o}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
be a lift of
z
to
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. By discussion in
\lx@sectionsign
3.2.1
, the point
\tilde{z}
induces an isotropic line
L^{1}=\varprojlim\limits_{n}\textup{Fil}^{1}\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/p^{n})\subset\mathbf{V}_{z}
. Let
L^{0}\coloneqq(L^{1})^{\bot}\subset\mathbf{V}_{z}
, we have a filtration
0\subset L^{1}\subset L^{0}\subset\mathbf{V}_{z}
, the following lemma proves that this filtration is strongly divisible in the sense of
[
Laf80
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.1]
(see also
[
MP16
,
\lx@sectionsign
4.8]
).

Lemma 3.2.4
.

The Frobenius-linear map
\Phi_{z}
on
\mathbf{V}_{z,\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
induces the following isomorphism of
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattices,

\Phi_{z}:p^{-1}L^{1}+L^{0}+p\mathbf{V}_{z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{V}_{z}

Proof.

The proof is basically the same as that of
[
MP16
,
\lx@sectionsign
4.8]
. Here we give a self-contained argument without referring to results of
[
Laf80
,
\lx@sectionsign
4.2]
.
For simplicity, we use
H
to denote the Dieudonne module
\mathbb{D}(X_{z})(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\simeq\mathbb{D}(X_{\tilde{z}})(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. Let
H_{\mathbb{F}}=H\otimes_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{z}=p\cdot\mathbf{F}_{z}^{-1}
be the Verschiebung morphism on
H
. Then the supersinglarity of
X_{z}
implies that
\mathbf{F}_{z}^{2}(H)=pH
, hence
\mathcal{V}_{z}(H)=\mathbf{F}_{z}(H)
.

Let
0\subset\textup{Fil}^{1}H_{\mathbb{F}}\subset H_{\mathbb{F}}
be the Hodge filtration given by
X_{z}
. It is well-known that this filtration is equal to
0\subset\mathcal{V}_{z}(H)/pH\subset H_{\mathbb{F}}
(see Oda’s work
[
Oda69
, Corollary 5.11]
). By Grothendieck-Messing theory, the Hodge filtration
0\subset\textup{Fil}^{1}H\subset H
determined by the
p
-divisible group
X_{\tilde{z}}
lifts the filtration
0\subset\textup{Fil}^{1}H_{\mathbb{F}}\subset H_{\mathbb{F}}
. Therefore we have
\mathbf{F}_{z}(H)=\mathcal{V}_{z}(H)=\textup{Fil}^{1}H+pH
. Combining this with
\mathbf{F}_{z}^{2}(H)=pH
, we get

(5)

\mathbf{F}_{z}\left(p^{-1}\textup{Fil}^{1}H+H\right)=H.

We still use
\mathcal{V}_{z}
to denote the base change morphism
\mathcal{V}_{z}\otimes_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\rightarrow H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
. It’s a Frobenius-conjugate linear isomorphism. Then
\mathcal{V}_{z}^{\vee}:H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}^{\vee}\rightarrow H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}^{\vee},\,\,f\mapsto f\circ\mathcal{V}_{z}
is a Frobenius-linear isomorphism, we define
\mathbf{F}_{z}^{\vee}
similarly. The filtration
0\subset\textup{Fil}^{1}H\subset H
induces a filtration on
H^{\vee}:0\rightarrow\textup{Fil}^{0}H^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{\vee}
where
\textup{Fil}^{0}H^{\vee}\simeq(H/\textup{Fil}^{1}H)^{\vee}
. The equality (
5
) induces

(6)

\mathcal{V}_{z}^{\vee}\left(p^{-1}\textup{Fil}^{0}H^{\vee}+H^{\vee}\right)=H^{\vee}.

It’s easy to check that the Frobenius-linear action
\mathbf{F}_{z}^{(2,2)}
on
\textup{End}_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})\simeq H_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\otimes_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}H^{\vee}_{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
we defined before equals to
p^{-1}\mathbf{F}_{z}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{z}^{\vee}
. Therefore (
5
) and (
6
) together imply that

\mathbf{F}_{z}^{(2,2)}\left(p^{-1}\textup{Fil}^{1}\textup{End}(H)+\textup{Fil}^{0}\textup{End}(H)+p\textup{End}(H)\right)=\textup{End}(H).

Applying the projector
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\textup{crys},z}
, notice that it is Frobenius invariant and respects the Hodge filtration, we get

\Phi_{z}\left(p^{-1}L^{1}+L^{0}+p\mathbf{V}_{z}\right)=\mathbf{V}_{z}.

∎

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Let
\nu_{p}:\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the
p
-adic valuation of
\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
\mathbf{V}
be a free
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-module of finite rank equipped with a non-degenerate bilinear pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
L^{1}\subset\mathbf{V}
be an isotropic line, and
L^{0}=(L^{1})^{\perp}\subset\mathbf{V}
. Let
l\in\mathbf{V}
be a generator of the isotropic line
L^{1}
, then

L^{0}+p\mathbf{V}=\{x\in\mathbf{V}:\nu_{p}((x,l))\geq 1\}.

Proof.

By the definition of
L^{0}
, we have
\nu_{p}((x,l))\geq 1
for any
x\in L^{0}+p\mathbf{V}
. Now if
x\in\mathbf{V}
satisfies the condition that
\nu_{p}((x,l))\geq 1
, there exists
x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{V}
such that
(l,x^{\prime})=p^{-1}(l,x)
since
l\notin p\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{V}
is a self-dual quadratic lattice. Therefore
x-p\cdot x^{\prime}\in L^{0}
, hence
x\in L^{0}+p\mathbf{V}
.
∎

4.
Formally smoothness locus of the special cycle

4.1.
Formally smoothness over a discrete valuation ring

Lemma 4.1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Let
R=\mathcal{O}[[t_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,t_{n-1}]]
where
\mathcal{O}
is a discrete valuation ring of characteristic
(0,p)
with uniformizer
\pi
and residue field
\mathbf{k}
. The ring
R
has maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{R}\coloneqq(\pi,t_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,t_{n-1})
. Let
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}
be
r
elements in
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
. The quotient ring
\overline{R}=R/(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r})
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
if and only if its base change
\overline{R}_{\mathbf{k}}=\overline{R}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{k}
is formally smooth over
\mathbf{k}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{0}:R\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
be the continuous homomorphism which sends all the
t_{j}
to
0
. Let
J=(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}))_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
1\leq j\leq n-1\end{subarray}}
be the Jacobian matrix of
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{r}
at
\mathbf{0}
. The quotient ring
\overline{R}
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
if and only if the matrix
J
has a
r\times r
-minor which is invertible in
\mathcal{O}
, because in this case we can rearrange a system of parameters
t_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,t_{n-1}^{\prime}
of
R
such that
t_{i}^{\prime}=f_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

Let
J_{\mathbf{k}}=J\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{k}
be the base change of
J
to
\mathbf{k}
, the matrix
J
has a
r\times r
-minor which is invertible in
\mathcal{O}
is equivalent to the rank of
J_{\mathbf{k}}
is
r
, which is further equivalent to
\overline{R}_{\mathbf{k}}
is formally smooth over
\mathbf{k}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
, hence the quotient ring
\overline{R}=R/(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r})
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
if and only if
\overline{R}_{\mathbf{k}}
is formally smooth over
\mathbf{k}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
.
∎

\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}
be the completion of the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}^{u}
at
z
. Recall that the Dieudonne module
\mathbb{D}(X_{z})
of

has a decomposition

\lx@sectionsign
3.1.2

. Let

\mathcal{O}_{E}

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{V}^{u}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-lattice of rank

and

, then
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
at
z
if and only if the map
i_{\textup{crys},z}
is primitive at
z
.

Proof.

The special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is cut out by
r
equations in the deformation space
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}\simeq\textup{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}^{u},z}
by Proposition
2.3.3
. Therefore the special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
at
z
if and only if
\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(L)\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}\mathbb{F}
is formally smooth over
\mathbb{F}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
at
z
by Lemma
4.1.1
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{u}_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{N}^{u}\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}}\mathbb{F}
. Let
l\in\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}
be an element such that its image
\overline{l}
in
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}/\varpi\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}\simeq\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})
is a generator of the line
\textup{Fil}^{1}\mathbb{D}(X_{z})(\mathbb{F})\cap\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})
. Let
\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]=\mathbb{F}[X]/X^{2}
.

By Schlessinger’s criterion in
[
Sch68
, Theorem 2.11]
, the tangent space
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{N}^{u}_{\mathbb{F}})
of
\mathcal{N}^{u}_{\mathbb{F}}
at

can be identified with the set
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
. This set has a natural bijection to the set of lines in
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]
which lifts the line
\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}
in
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})

3.1.5
. Therefore any such line has a generator of the form
\overline{l}+\epsilon\cdot w
for some element

. Two elements
\overline{l}+\epsilon\cdot w
and
\overline{l}+\epsilon\cdot w^{\prime}
generate the same line if and only if

\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{N}^{u}_{\mathbb{F}})=n-1
.

The tangent space
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})
of
\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}}
at
z
can be identified with the set
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(L)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
. For all
x\in L
, let
\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}}
be the image of
x_{\textup{crys},z}
in
\mathbf{D}_{z}(\mathbb{F})\simeq\mathbf{D}_{z}/\varpi\mathbf{D}_{z}
. The set
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(L)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
has a natural bijection to the set of lines in
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})\otimes_{W}\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]
which lifts the line
\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}
in
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})
and orthogonal to
\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}}
for all
x\in L
. Let
\overline{l}+\epsilon\cdot w
be a generator of a line in the set
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(L)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
, then
\langle\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}},\overline{l}+\epsilon\cdot w\rangle=\epsilon\langle\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}},w\rangle=0
, hence
\langle\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}},w\rangle=0
. Therefore
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})
is the subspace of
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathbb{F}}/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}
orthogonal to the image of
L
in
\mathbf{D}_{z}(\mathbb{F})
under the perfect pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbf{D}_{z}(\mathbb{F})\times\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{z}(\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
. Let
\overline{L}\subset\mathbf{D}_{z}/\varpi\mathbf{D}_{z}
be the
\mathbb{F}
-linear subspace spanned by the image of

in
\mathbf{D}_{z}(\mathbb{F})
, we have

(7)

\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})=n-1-\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(\overline{L}).

therefore
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
of relative dimension

at

if and only if

, which is equivalent to the fact that the map

is injective, i.e., the map

is primitive.
∎

4.3.
The GSpin case

For a point
z\in\mathcal{N}^{o}(\mathbb{F})
, let
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}
be the completion of the formal scheme
\mathcal{N}^{o}
at

. Let
\mathbf{V}_{z}=\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}

\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{V}^{o}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice of rank
r\geq 1
and

, then
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of relative dimension

at

if and only if the following two assertions hold,

The map

is primitive at

;

(ii)

There exists a lift of
z
to

.

Proof.

The special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is cut out by
r
equations in the deformation space
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}\simeq\textup{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N},z}
by Proposition
2.3.3
. Therefore the special cycle
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
at
z
if and only if
\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(L)\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
is formally smooth over
\mathbb{F}
of relative dimension
n-r-1
at
z
by Lemma
4.1.1
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{o}_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathcal{N}^{o}\times_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\mathbb{F}
. Let
l\in\mathbf{V}_{z}
be an isotropic element such that its image
\overline{l}\in\mathbf{V}_{z}/p\mathbf{V}_{z}\simeq\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})
generates the line
\textup{Fil}^{1}\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})
. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the bilinear form on
\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})
. Let
\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]=\mathbb{F}[X]/X^{2}
.

By Schlessinger’s criterion in
[
Sch68
, Theorem 2.11]
, the tangent space
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{N}^{o}_{\mathbb{F}})
(resp.
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})
) of
\mathcal{N}^{o}_{\mathbb{F}}
(resp.
\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}}
) at

can be identified with the set
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{o}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
. This set has a natural bijection to the set of isotropic lines in
\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys}}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])\simeq\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]
which lifts the line
\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}
in

3.2.1
. Therefore any such line has a generator of the form

for some

, note that

\overline{l}

, we denote it by

is non-degenerate.

The tangent space
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})
of
\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}}
at
z
can be identified with the set
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(L)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])

x\in L

\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}}

in

which lifts the line
\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}
in
\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})
and orthogonal to
\overline{x_{\textup{crys},z}}
for any

. Let

be a generator of a line in the set

, then

, hence

\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})
is the subspace of
\{\overline{l}\}^{\bot}/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}

L
in
\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})

\overline{(\cdot,\cdot)}
. Let
\overline{L}\subset\mathbf{V}_{z}/p\mathbf{V}_{z}
be the

-linear subspace spanned by the image of

in
\mathbf{V}_{\textup{crys},z}(\mathbb{F})

\{\overline{l}\}^{\bot}
of

, we have

\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})=\left((\overline{L}+\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l})/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}\right)^{\bot}\subset\{\overline{l}\}^{\bot}/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}.

\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\,\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(L)_{\mathbb{F}})=n-1-\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\,\left((\overline{L}+\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l})/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}\right)

is non-degenerate on

. Therefore
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is formally smooth over
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of relative dimension

at

\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\,\left((\overline{L}+\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l})/\mathbb{F}\cdot\overline{l}\right)=r
, which is further equivalent to
\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\,\overline{L}=r

. The condition

.

\mathcal{Z}(L)

-point

\overline{l}
doesn’t belong to the space
\overline{L}
. Let

be a lift of

to

t=m
.

We first show that the number
m
is always nonzero. If
m=0
, we have
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
by similar arguments in the unitary case. By Lemma
3.2.2
, the assumption
x_{\textup{crys},z}\in\mathbf{V}
implies that
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
, which is a contradiction. Hence
m
is nonzero.

If
m=\infty
, i.e., the point
\tilde{z}:\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}
factors through
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
. If
t
is a finite number. The morphism
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is a surjection in
\textup{Art}_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
whose kernel admits a nilpotent divided power structure. The isotropic line
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t+1})\cdot l
corresponds to the point
\tilde{z}_{t+1}:\textup{Spec}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t+1})\rightarrow\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{z}}}{{\rightarrow}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}
under the bijection (i) in Lemma
3.2.1
. It also factors through
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
. By definition, the image of
x_{\textup{crys},z}
in
\mathbf{V}/p^{t+1}\mathbf{V}
equals to
x_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t+1}))
. Therefore
(x_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t+1})),l)\equiv(x_{\textup{crys},z},l)\equiv 0\,\,\textup{mod}\,p^{t+1}
by Lemma
3.2.1
. This contradicts to the fact that
\nu_{p}((x_{\textup{crys},z},l))=t
. Hence
t=\infty=m
.

If
m<\infty
. The morphism
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is a surjection in
\textup{Art}_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
whose kernel admits a nilpotent divided power structure. The isotropic line
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m})\cdot l
corresponds to the point
\tilde{z}_{m}:\textup{Spec}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m})\rightarrow\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{z}}}{{\rightarrow}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}
under the bijection (i) in Lemma
3.2.1
, which factors through
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
. By definition, the image of
x_{\textup{crys},z}
in
\mathbf{V}/p^{m}\mathbf{V}
equals to
x_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m}))
. Therefore
(x_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m})),l)\equiv(x_{\textup{crys},z},l)\equiv 0\,\,\textup{mod}\,p^{m}
by Lemma
3.2.1
. Hence
t\geq m
.

On the other hand, the morphism
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is a surjection in
\textup{Art}_{\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
whose kernel admits a nilpotent divided power structure. The isotropic line
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p)^{t}\cdot l
corresponds to the point
\tilde{z}_{t}:\textup{Spec}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t})\rightarrow\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{z}}}{{\rightarrow}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}
under the bijection (i) in Lemma
3.2.1
. The point
\tilde{z}_{t}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t}))
factors through
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
because
(x_{\textup{crys},z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t})),l)\equiv(x_{\textup{crys},z},l)\equiv 0\,\,(\textup{mod}\,\,p^{t})
by Lemma
3.1.5
. Therefore we have a morphism
\textup{Spec}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{t})\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}\times_{\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}}\textup{Spf}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\simeq\textup{Spec}\,\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{m})
by the universal property of fiber product, hence
m\geq t
. Therefore we conclude that
m=t
.

∎

5.3.
Comparison of several quantities

Definition 5.3.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
) be a nonzero special quasi-homomorphism such that
(x,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
(resp.
q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
). Let
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
be a point. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{o}
) be the formal completion of
\mathcal{N}^{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}^{o}
) at
z
. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
be the formal completion of
\mathcal{Z}(x)
at
z
. Define

\displaystyle l_{z,x}

\displaystyle=\textup{the largest number $l$ such that $z\in\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-l+1}x)(\mathbb{F})$ (resp. $z\in\mathcal{Z}(p^{-l+1}x)(\mathbb{F})$).}

\displaystyle m_{z,x}

\displaystyle=\textup{the largest number $m$ such that $x_{\textup{crys},z}\in\varpi^{m}\mathbf{D}$ (resp. $x_{\textup{crys},z}\in p^{m}\mathbf{V}$).}

\displaystyle n_{z,x}

\displaystyle=\textup{the largest number $n$ (could be infinity) such that there exists a point }

z^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}/(\varpi^{n}))
(resp.
z^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/(p^{n}))
).

For a point
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
(resp.
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{o}(\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
), let
l\in\overline{\mathbf{D}}
(resp.
l\in\mathbf{V}
) be a generator of the line (resp. isotropic line)
L^{1}\subset\overline{\mathbf{D}}
(resp.
L^{1}\subset\mathbf{V}
) corresponding to the point
\tilde{z}
. Define

\displaystyle t_{x}(\tilde{z})=\nu_{\varpi}(\langle x_{\textup{crys},z},l\rangle)\,\,\textup{(resp. $\nu_{p}((x_{\textup{crys},z},l))$)}.

Remark 5.3.2
.

In the set up of the above definition, the smoothness criterion (Lemma
4.2.1
and Lemma
4.3.1
) of the formal scheme
\mathcal{Z}(x)
at a point
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
can be interpreted in the following way:

\bullet

In the unitary case, the formal scheme
\mathcal{Z}(x)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
of relative dimension
n-2
at the point
z
if and only if
m_{z,x}=0
.

\bullet

In the GSpin case, the formal scheme
\mathcal{Z}(x)
is formally smooth over
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of relative dimension
n-2
at the point
z
if and only if
m_{z,x}=0
and
n_{z,x}\geq 2
.

Proposition 5.3.3
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{V}^{u}
(resp.
x\in\mathbb{V}^{o}
) be a nonzero special quasi-homomorphism such that
(x,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
(resp.
q_{\mathbb{V}^{o}}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
). Let
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x)(\mathbb{F})
be a point. We have

\displaystyle l_{z,x}

\displaystyle=\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}\,\,\textup{(resp. $\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{o}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}$)}.

\displaystyle n_{z,x}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}\,\,\textup{(resp. $\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{o}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}$)}.

Moreover, we have
l_{z,x}=m_{z,x}+1
in the GSpin case, while
l_{z,x}-m_{z,x}
can be
0
or
1
in the unitary case.

Proof.

We first prove the equality about
n_{z,x}
. Then we prove the equality about
l_{z,x}
while which the statements about the relationships between
l_{z,x}
and
m_{z,x}
will be clear. We separate the unitary and the GSpin case.

In the unitary case. For a point
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
, denote by
l_{\tilde{z}}\in\overline{\mathbf{D}}
be a generator of the line
L^{1}\subset\overline{\mathbf{D}}
corresponding to the point
\tilde{z}
. For an integer
n
, let
f_{\varpi^{n}x,z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{u},z}
be the local equation of the special divisor
\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{n}x)
at
z
. By Proposition
5.2.1
, we have

(\tilde{z}^{\sharp}(f_{\varpi^{n}x}))=(\langle\varpi^{n}x_{\textup{crys},z},l_{\tilde{z}}\rangle)=(\varpi^{n+t_{x}(\tilde{z})})\subset(\varpi)

as long as
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{n}x)(\mathbb{F})
. Notice that the point
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-l_{z,x}+1}x)(\mathbb{F})
. Let
n=-l_{z,x}+1
, we get
-l_{z,x}+1+t_{x}(\tilde{z})\geq 1
, i.e.,
l_{z,x}\leq t_{x}(\tilde{z})
. Let
n=0
, the fiber product
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}\times_{\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z},\tilde{z}}\textup{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}\simeq\textup{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}/(\varpi^{t_{x}(\tilde{z})})
defines a point
z^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\varpi^{t_{x}(\tilde{z})})
, hence
n_{z,x}\geq t_{x}(\tilde{z})
. Therefore we have proved that
l_{z,x}\leq t_{x}(\tilde{z})\leq n_{z,x}
for all points
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
. Hence

l_{z,x}\leq\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}\leq\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}\leq n_{z,x}.

We first prove
n_{z,x}=\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
. Let
M=\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
. If
n_{z,x}=\infty
. For all integer
n
, there exists a point
z^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}/(\varpi^{n}))
. Let
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
be a lift of
z^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
, the existence of
\tilde{z}
follows from the fact that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{u},z}
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
. Then we have
\langle l_{\tilde{z}},x_{\textup{crys},z}\rangle\equiv 0
mod
\varpi^{n}
by Lemma
3.1.5
. Hence
t_{x}(\tilde{z})=\nu_{\varpi}(\langle l_{\tilde{z}},x_{\textup{crys},z}\rangle)\geq n
. Therefore
M=\infty=n_{z,x}
. If
n_{z,x}<\infty
, there exists a point
z^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}/(\varpi^{n_{z,x}}))
. Let
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
be a lift of
z^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
. Then we have
\langle l_{\tilde{z}},x_{\textup{crys},z}\rangle\equiv 0
mod
\varpi^{n_{z,x}}
by Lemma
3.1.5
. Hence
M\geq n_{z,x}
. We already have
M\leq n_{z,x}
. Hence
n_{z,x}=M=\max\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
.

Next we prove
l_{z,x}=\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
. Let
N=\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
. Let
x^{\prime}=\varpi^{-l_{z,x}+1}x
. Then we have
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
but
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
. There are two possibilities.

(a)

The element
\varpi^{-1}x_{\textup{crys},z}^{\prime}\notin\mathbf{D}
. Let
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})
be a point. Notice that

t_{x^{\prime}}(\tilde{z})=1
, then
t_{x}(\tilde{z})=t_{x^{\prime}}(\tilde{z})+l_{z,x}-1=l_{z,x}
, hence
l_{z,x}\geq N
. If
t_{x^{\prime}}(\tilde{z})\geq 2

be an element such that
\nu_{\varpi}(\langle l,x_{\textup{crys},z}\rangle)=0

x_{\textup{crys},z}\notin\varpi\mathbf{D}

. We give an example of case (a) in Example

(b)

be a point, then

. Hence

, then

.

In this case we can conclude that

implies that

, hence
m_{z,x}\geq l_{z,x}
. If

, we have
\varpi^{-1}x_{\textup{crys},z}^{\prime}\in\varpi\mathbf{D}
. Then
\nu_{\varpi}(\langle l_{\tilde{z}},\varpi^{-1}x^{\prime}_{\textup{crys},z}\rangle)\geq 1
. Proposition
5.2.1
implies that

, which is a contradiction. Therefore

. We give an example of case (b) in Example
5.3.4
.

In both cases, we have
l_{z,x}\geq N
. We already have
N\geq l_{z,x}
, hence
l_{z,x}=N=\min\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{z}^{u}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})}\{t_{x}(\tilde{z})\}
. Notice that
l_{z,x}-m_{z,x}
can be
0
or
1
.

and
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})
are the tangent spaces of the formal scheme
\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}}
at the point
z
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
x^{\prime}=\varpi^{-l_{z,x}+1}x
throughout this proof. Notice that
z\in\mathcal{Z}(x^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
but
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
(resp.
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
).

We first prove (i). Let
z\in\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})
be a point. By Theorem
5.4.1
(iii) and (iv), the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{D}(\varpi^{l_{z,x}-1}x^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{D}(p^{l_{z,x}-1}x^{\prime})
) is regular at
z
. Since
z
is an arbitrary point, we conclude that the formal scheme
\mathcal{D}(x)
is regular.

Now we prove (ii). Suppose that the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
(resp.
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) at the point
z\in\mathcal{D}(x)(\mathbb{F})
. If
l_{z,x}\geq 2
, the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{D}(\varpi^{l_{z,x}-1}x^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathcal{D}(x)=\mathcal{D}(p^{l_{z,x}-1}x^{\prime})
) is not smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
(resp.
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) by Theorem
5.4.1
(ii), hence we must have
l_{z,x}=1
. In the case
l_{z,x}=1
, we get
n_{z,x}\geq 2
by Theorem
5.4.1
(iii). For the converse direction. Let’s assume
l_{z,x}=1
and
n_{z,x}\geq 2
. Theorem
5.4.1
(iii) implies that the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
(resp.
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) at
z
. This concludes the whole proof.

Finally we prove (iii). Let
\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
be the completion of the formal scheme
\mathcal{D}(x)
and
\mathcal{Z}(x)
at the point
z
respectively. By Schlessinger’s criterion in
[
Sch68
, Theorem 2.11]
,

\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})=\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]),\,\,\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])

where
\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]=\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{2})
. The closed immersion
\mathcal{D}(x)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}(x)
implies that there is an injection
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})
. We want to show that this is bijective. We separate the proof into two cases.

(a)

The point
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
(resp.
z\notin\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
). Then we have
\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}
. The bijitivity is obvious.

(b)

The point
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\varpi^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
(resp.
z\in\mathcal{Z}(p^{-1}x)(\mathbb{F})
). Let
d_{x,z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{u},z}
(resp.
d_{x,z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{o},z}
) be the local equation of the difference divisor. Since
l_{z,x}\geq 2
, the difference divisor
\mathcal{D}(x)
is not formally smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
(resp.
\breve{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) at the
z
, hence
d_{x,z}\equiv\varpi
mod
\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2}
(resp.
d_{x,z}\equiv p
mod
\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2}
). Let
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
(resp.
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
) be a point. Let
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{u},z}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]
(resp.
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{N}^{o},z}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[\epsilon]
) be the corresponding ring homomorphism. We have
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}(d_{x,z})=0
because
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}(\varpi)=0
(resp.
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}(p)=0
) and
\tilde{z}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})\subset(\epsilon^{2})=0
. Hence
\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{u}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])\supset\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
(resp.
\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}^{o}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])\supset\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
). Therefore
\widehat{\mathcal{D}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}(x)}_{z}(\mathbb{F}[\epsilon])
.

Therefore we have proved that
\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{D}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})=\textup{Tgt}_{z}(\mathcal{Z}(x)_{\mathbb{F}})
.
∎

Some Identities in Quantum Torus Arising from Ringel-Hall Algebras
Jiuzhao Hua

Abstract
We define two classes of representations of quivers over arbitrary fields, called monomorphic representations and epimorphic representations. We show that every representation has a unique maximal nilpotent subrepresentation and the associated quotient is always monomorphic, and every representation has a unique maximal epimorphic subrepresentation and the associated quotient is always nilpotent. The uniquenesses of such subrepresenations imply two identities in the Ringel-Hall algebra. By applying Reineke’s integration map, we obtain two identities in the corresponding quantum torus.

2
Two Identities in Quantum Torus

Each representation
M=(V_{i},f_{h})_{i\in Q_{0},h\in Q_{1}}
gives rise to two linear maps
\sigma_{i}
and
\tau_{i}
for each
i\in Q_{0}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}:V_{i}

\displaystyle\,\,\to\,\,\bigoplus_{h^{\prime}=i}V_{h^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle{}\!\!\!\!\!\!\tau_{i}:\bigoplus_{h^{\prime\prime}=i}V_{h^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\,\,\to\,\,V_{i},

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle\,\,\mapsto\,\,(f_{h}(v_{i}))_{h^{\prime}=i},

\displaystyle{}(v_{h^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\,\,\mapsto\,\,\sum_{h^{\prime\prime}=i}f_{h}(v_{h^{\prime}}).

The following lemma is a direct consequence of the definitions of monomorphic and epimorphic representations.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M=(V_{i},f_{h})_{i\in Q_{0},h\in Q_{1}}
be a representation of
Q
. Then we have

1.

M
is monomorphic if and only
\sigma_{i}
is injective for all
i\in Q_{0}
.

2.

M
is epimorphic if and only
\tau_{i}
is surjective for all
i\in Q_{0}
,

Lemma 2.2
.

For any representation
M
of
Q
over a field
k
, there exists a unique maximal nilpotent subrepresentation
N\subset M
. Moreover,
M/N
is monomorphic.

Proof.

Since
M
is finite dimensional, chain (
2
) must be stable in finite steps, i.e., there exists an integer
s
such that
im^{-t}(0)=im^{-s}(0)
for all
t>s
.
We claim that
im^{-s}(0)
is the unique maximal nilpotent subrepresentation of
M
.

im^{-s}(0)
is obviously nilpotent. Let
N=(W_{i},f_{h}|_{W_{h^{\prime}}})_{i\in Q_{0},h\in Q_{1}}
be any nilpotent subrepresentation of
M
, we claim that
N\subset im^{-s}(0)
. Since
N
is nilpotent, there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}

such that each vector space
W_{i}
(for
i\in Q_{0}
) is mapped to the zero vector space by any path starting from vertex
i
as long as the length of the path is greater than
r
. This
implies that
N\subset im^{-(s+r)}(0)
. Since
im^{-(s+t)}(0)=im^{-s}(0)
, we have
N\subset im^{-s}(0)
.

To show that
M/im^{-s}(0)
is monomorphic, we only need to show the
\sigma_{i}
is injective for each
i\in Q_{0}
when
\sigma_{i}
is acting on vector spaces in
M/im^{-s}(0)
, which is an easy consequence of the definition of
im^{-}
.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any representation
M
of
Q
over a field
k
, there exists a unique maximal epimorphic subrepresentation

. Moreover,
M/E
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Since
M
is finite dimensional, chain (
3
) must be stable in finite steps, i.e., there exists an integer
s
such that
im^{+t}(M)=im^{+s}(M)
for all
t>s
.
We claim that
im^{+s}(M)
is the unique maximal epimorphic subrepresentation of
M
.

im^{+s}(M)
is obviously epimorphic. Let
E
be any epimorphic subrepresentation of
M
, then
im^{+1}(E)=E
and hence
im^{+s}(E)=E
.

implies that

im^{+s}(E)\subset im^{+s}(M)
and hence

. Thus
im^{+s}(M)
is the unique maximal epimorphic subrepresentation.

M/im^{+s}(M)
is nilpotent because
im^{+i}(M)/im^{+(i+1)}(M)
is nilpotent for all
i\geq 1
.
∎

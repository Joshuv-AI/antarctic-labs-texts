AN INVERSE PROBLEM FOR THE MINIMAL SURFACE EQUATION IN THE PRESENCE OF A RIEMANNIAN METRIC
Janne Nurminen

Abstract
. In this work we study an inverse problem for the minimal surface equation on a Riemannian manifold ( ℝ n , g ) (\mathbb{R}^{n},g) where the metric is of the form g ​ ( x ) = c ​ ( x ) ​ ( g ^ ⊕ e ) g(x)=c(x)(\hat{g}\oplus e) . Here g ^ \hat{g} is a simple Riemannian metric on ℝ n − 1 \mathbb{R}^{n-1} , e e is the Euclidean metric on ℝ \mathbb{R} and c c a smooth positive function. We show that if we know the associated Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps corresponding to metrics g g and c ~ ​ g \tilde{c}g , then the Taylor series of the conformal factor c ~ \tilde{c} at x n = 0 x_{n}=0 is equal to a positive constant. Introduction

This article focuses on an inverse problem for the minimal surface equation (MSE), which is a quasilinear elliptic PDE. In particular we consider MSE on a manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g),n\geq 3
, where

(1.1)

g(x^{\prime},x_{n})=c(x^{\prime},x_{n})\begin{pmatrix}\hat{g}(x^{\prime})&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Here
(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\hat{g}
is a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
c\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

c(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Inverse problems for the MSE were considered in
[
Nur23
]
where
\hat{g}
was Euclidean and in
[
CLLO22
]
where
c=1
. Now we consider metrics of the form (
1.1
) which includes both of these cases. Metrics with local coordinates of the form (
1.1
) were introduced for example in
[
DKSU09
]
,
[
DKLS16
]
where the Calderón problem was studied.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be a bounded domain. In order to define a minimal surface, we introduce notation for Riemannian Hessian and norm of the gradient

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{2}u=\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u\right)_{i,j=1}^{n},\quad|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}=g^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{j}}u.

We use the Einstein summation convention where there is no chance to misinterpret. Also here
\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}
are the standard Christoffel symbols.
Now define the Laplace-Beltrami operator to be the trace of the Hessian

\Delta_{g}u=\Tr(\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=g^{ij}\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}u-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u\right).

Then for the metric (
1.1
) the MSE has the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\dive_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{g}u(1-c^{-1})+\frac{1}{2c}(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}c(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}-c^{-2})+\frac{n-1}{2c^{3}}\partial_{x_{n}}c(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})}{\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}=0.

For the derivation of this equation, see Section
2
. When
c=1
, one gets the MSE from
[
CLLO22
]
. The graph of a function
u\colon\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
minimal surface
if it satisfies (
1.2
) for all
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
.

We will look at the boundary value problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega\\
u=f,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

where
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u):=

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

(this is an equivalent formulation of (
1.2
), see Section
2
).
One can show that (
1.3
) is well-posed for small enough boundary data
f
for example by following the arguments in
[
LLLS21
]
. In particular one can show that there exist
C,\delta>0
and
s>3
,
s\notin\mathbb{N}
, such that for all

f\in U_{\delta}:=\{h\in C^{s}(\partial\Omega):||h||_{C^{s}(\partial\Omega)}<\delta\}

there is a unique small solution
u_{f}
in
\{v\in C^{s}(\bar{\Omega}):||v||_{C^{s}(\bar{\Omega})}<C\delta\}
. In addition to full data we will be considering partial data and for this let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be a nonempty open subset of the boundary. We can now define a (partial) Dirichlet-to-Neumann map (DN map)
\Lambda_{g}^{\Gamma}\colon C_{c}^{s}(\Gamma)\to C^{s-1}(\Gamma),

(1.4)

\Lambda_{g}^{\Gamma}f\mapsto\partial_{\nu}u_{f}|_{\Gamma}

where
u_{f}
is the unique small solution corresponding to the boundary value
f
,
\partial_{\nu}u=\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}u\nu_{j}
and
\nu
is the unit normal of
\partial\Omega
. When
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
, we will denote
\Lambda_{g}=\Lambda_{g}^{\partial\Omega}
.

The inverse problem we study in this work is the following: Given the knowledge of the partial DN map for two metrics in the same conformal class, does it hold that the metrics are the same? We note that when trying to recover the metric from boundary measurements one expects an obstruction to uniqueness by a boundary fixing diffeomorphism. When the metrics are in the same conformal class such an obstruction is not present, since the only diffeomorphism that leaves a conformal class invariant is the identity. In this article we will give some partial answers to this inverse problem.

Before stating the results, we will introduce the class of
admissible metrics

[
DKSU09
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A Riemannian metric
g
is called admissible if on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
it is of the form (
1.1
) and
(\Omega,\hat{g})
is a simple manifold.

Definition 1.2
.

A compact manifold
(\hat{M},\hat{g})
with boundary is called simple if for any
p\in\hat{M}
the exponential map
\exp_{p}
with its maximal domain of definition in
T_{p}\hat{M}
is a diffeomorphism onto
\hat{M}
, and if
\partial\hat{M}
is strictly convex in the sense that its second fundamental form is positive definite.

The first result is for the full data case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
,
n\geq 3
, be a Riemannian manifold where
g
is as in (
1.1
) with
\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},0)=0
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be a bounded domain with
C^{\infty}
boundary. When
n=3
assume that
\Omega
is simply connected and when
n>3
assume that
\hat{g}
is admissible. Let
\tilde{c}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\partial_{x_{n}}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
and let
\partial_{x_{n}}^{k}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)|_{\partial\Omega}=0
for
k\geq 3
. Assume that
\Lambda_{g}(f)=\Lambda_{\tilde{c}g}(f)
for all
f\in U_{\delta}
, where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small. Then

\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=\lambda,\quad\partial_{x_{n}}^{k}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0

for some
\lambda>0
and for all
k>2
.

Thus, if
\tilde{c}
is real analytic with respect to
x_{n}
, then
\tilde{c}(x)=\lambda
for all
x\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

For the partial data we will only consider the case
n=3
. For
n>3
we would be able to get some results, but the partial data results for the magnetic Schrödinger equation (see
[
SY23
]
) are more restrictive and hence we do not record these results here.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g)
,
\tilde{c}
and
\Omega
be as in Theorem
1.3
and let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
,
\Gamma\neq\emptyset
, be open. Assume that
\Lambda_{g}^{\Gamma}(f)=\Lambda_{\tilde{c}g}^{\Gamma}(f)
for all
f\in U_{\delta}
, where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small. Then

\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=\lambda,\quad\partial_{x_{3}}^{k}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0

for some
\lambda>0
and for all
k>2
.

Thus, if
\tilde{c}
is real analytic with respect to
x_{3}
, then
\tilde{c}(x)=\lambda
for all
x\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

These results extend the results from
[
Nur23
]
.

We make some comments about the assumptions made in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. First of all, the assumption
\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{x_{n}}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
is needed for the well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.3
) for small data, that is we want it to be well-posed for both the metric
g
and the metric
\tilde{c}g
. The assumption
\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
is needed in order for the method used in the proof to work and it is not yet known if this could be removed. Lastly, there is a small gauge invariance in (
1.2
) and also in the DN map. To be more precise, if one replaces the conformal factor
c
in (
1.1
) by
\mu c
,
\mu\neq 0
, then both the equation (
1.2
) and the DN map remain the same. Thus we cannot have
\lambda=1
in Theorem
1.3
.

There are also some assumptions made when
n=3
. The assumption that
\Omega
is simply connected is needed to use the Poincaré Lemma. This is something that one might be able relax with some amount of work by looking at the so called gauge isomorphism mentioned in
[
GT11
]
that relates two connection 1-forms. The other assumption that
\partial_{x_{n}}^{k}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)|_{\partial\Omega}=0
for
k\geq 3
could possibly be removed by doing a boundary determination result.

The proof will use the method of higher order linearization. We will begin by looking at the first linearization of (
1.3
) and the DN map for that. The first linearization of the DN map corresponds to an advection diffusion type equation. From this it is possible to determine the advection term (
[
GT11
]
for
n=3
,
[
KU18
]
for
n>3
) and this will then imply that
\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=\lambda
for some
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. Now fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
and construct a solution
v^{(0)}
to the adjoint of the linearized equation so that
v^{(0)}(x_{0})\neq 0
. Then using the higher order linearizations one can obtain an integral identity

\int_{\Omega}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)v^{(0)}\prod_{k=1}^{N+1}v^{l_{k}}\,dx^{\prime}=0,

where
N+1
is the order of linearization and
v^{l_{k}}
are solutions to the linearized equation. Then choosing
v^{l_{k}}\equiv 1
for
k>2
and using that a product of these solutions form a complete set in
L^{1}
(see Lemma
4.1
) we can conclude that

\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)v^{(0)}=0.

From this, using
v^{(0)}(x_{0})\neq 0
and that
x_{0}
was arbitrary, we get
\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
. Then one proceeds by induction.

For the partial data, the proof is very similar and has only a few modifications. One of the more significant changes is that instead of using the results of
[
GT11
]
to determine the advection term, we use the results for partial data in
[

]
.

2.
Deriving the minimal surface equation

In this section we derive the equation (
1.2
). This is done similarly as in
[
Nur23
, Section 3]
. Let
(M,g),

M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a Riemannian manifold with the metric

(2.1)

g(x^{\prime},x_{n})=c(x^{\prime},x_{n})\begin{pmatrix}\hat{g}(x^{\prime})&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

where

(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},\;c\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;c(x)>0\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
\hat{g}
is a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
We use the standard notations
g_{ij}
for the matrix
g
and
g^{ij}
for the inverse
g^{-1}
.
These assumptions are valid for the rest of the article, unless otherwise stated.

Let
u\colon\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R},

u\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, and consider the graph of the function
u

\text{Graph}_{u}=\{(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))\colon x^{\prime}\in\Omega\}\subset M.

This graph is a minimal surface if and only if its mean curvature
H
is equal to zero at all points on the graph. By defining

f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad f(x^{\prime},x_{n})=x_{n}-u(x^{\prime}),

the graph of
u
is the surface

\Sigma:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:f(x^{\prime},x_{n})=0\}.

The mean curvature of
\Sigma
at
x\in\Sigma
is the sum of principal curvatures. We omit the normalizing factor
\frac{1}{n-1}
when calculating the mean curvature and we use the Einstein summation convention when it does not cause confusion. In order to calculate the principal curvatures, we introduce the Riemannian gradient and Hessian of a function
f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}f=g^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}f\partial_{x_{j}},\quad\nabla_{g}^{2}f=\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}f\right)_{i,j=1}^{n},

where
g^{ij}
is the inverse of
g_{ij}
and
\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ml}(\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jl}+\partial_{x_{j}}g_{il}-\partial_{x_{l}}g_{ij})
is the Christoffel symbol related to the metric
g
.
Define also the Laplace-Beltrami operator, which is the trace of the Hessian (this is one way of defining it), and the norm of the gradient:

\Delta_{g}f=\Tr(\nabla_{g}^{2}f)=g^{ij}\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}f-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}f\right),\quad|\nabla_{g}f|_{g}^{2}=g^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}f\partial_{x_{j}}f.

Now the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
at
x\in\Sigma
are the eigenvalues of
\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)
restricted to the tangent space
T_{x}\Sigma
at
x
. Since
\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}}
is a normal to
\Sigma
at the point
x
, we have
T_{x}\Sigma=\{\nabla_{g}f(x)\}^{\perp}
, or in other words, the tangent space
T_{x}\Sigma
is the orthogonal complement of the vector
\nabla_{g}f(x)
.

Let
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1}\}
be an
g
-orthonormal basis of
T_{x}\Sigma
. Then
\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1},\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}}\right\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now the mean curvature of
\Sigma
at
x\in\Sigma
is the trace of
\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)|_{\{\nabla_{g}f(x)\}^{\perp}}
:

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\langle\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)E_{i},E_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\langle\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)E_{i},E_{i}\rangle+\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}},\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}},\frac{\nabla_{g}f(x)}{|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x))-|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{-2}\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}f(x)-|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{-2}\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right).

Thus
\text{Graph}_{u}
is a minimal surface if and only if

(2.2)

|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{2}\Delta_{g}f(x)-\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right)=0\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptx\in\text{Graph}_{u}.

Next we will calculate the minimal surface equation more explicitly using the metric (
2.1
). Now

g^{-1}=c^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{g}^{-1}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

is the inverse matrix of (
2.1
). Let us calculate the first term of (
2.2
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle c^{2}|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{2}\Delta_{g}f(x)

\displaystyle=c^{2}g^{ij}(\delta_{in}-\partial_{x_{i}}u)(\delta_{jn}-\partial_{x_{j}}u)g^{kl}\left(-\partial^{2}_{x_{k}x_{l}}u-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}kl}(\delta_{mn}-\partial_{x_{m}}u)\right)

\displaystyle=c^{2}g^{ij}(\delta_{in}\delta_{jn}-\delta_{in}\partial_{x_{j}}u-\delta_{jn}\partial_{x_{i}}u+\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{j}}u)g^{kl}(-\partial^{2}_{x_{k}x_{l}}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}kl}+\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}kl}\partial_{x_{m}}u)

\displaystyle=c\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)\left(-\Delta_{g}u-g^{kl}\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}kl}\right).

The second term of (
2.2
) becomes

(2.4)

\displaystyle c^{2}\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=c^{2}(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}f-\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}f)g^{ai}\partial_{x_{a}}fg^{bj}\partial_{x_{b}}f

\displaystyle=c^{2}(-\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}ij}+\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u)g^{ai}g^{bj}(\delta_{an}\delta_{bn}-\delta_{an}\partial_{x_{b}}u-\delta_{bn}\partial_{x_{a}}u+\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u)

\displaystyle=c^{2}(-\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}ij}+\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u)(g^{ni}g^{nj}-g^{ni}g^{bj}\partial_{x_{b}}u-g^{ai}g^{nj}\partial_{x_{a}}u+g^{ai}g^{bj}\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u)

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nn}\partial_{x_{m}}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nn}-2(\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nj}\partial_{x_{m}}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj})cg^{bj}\partial_{x_{b}}u

\displaystyle+c^{2}(-\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}ij}+\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u)g^{ai}g^{bj}\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nn}\partial_{x_{m}}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nn}-2(\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nj}\partial_{x_{m}}u-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj})\left(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u\right)^{j}

\displaystyle-\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)-\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}ij}\left(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u\right)^{i}\left(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u\right)^{j}.

In order to simplify further we will calculate the Christoffel symbols more explicitly:

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}

\displaystyle=g^{mr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{ij}),

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nn}

\displaystyle=g^{mr}(2\partial_{x_{n}}g_{nr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{nn})=2g^{mn}\partial_{x_{n}}g_{nn}-g^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}g_{nn}

\displaystyle=2g^{mn}\partial_{x_{n}}c-g^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c,

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}ij}

\displaystyle=g^{nr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{ij})=g^{nn}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{in}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jn}-\partial_{x_{n}}g_{ij})

\displaystyle=c^{-1}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{in}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jn}-\partial_{x_{n}}g_{ij}),

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{m}_{\hphantom{m}nj}

\displaystyle=g^{mr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{nr}+\partial_{x_{n}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{nj}),

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj}

\displaystyle=g^{nr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{nr}+\partial_{x_{n}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{nj})=g^{nn}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{nn}+\partial_{x_{n}}g_{jn}-\partial_{x_{n}}g_{nj})

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}c.

Inserting these into (

) we have

\displaystyle c^{2}|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{2}\Delta_{g}f(x)

\displaystyle=c\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)(-\Delta_{g}u-\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}c^{-1}(\partial_{x_{l}}g_{kn}+\partial_{x_{k}}g_{ln}-\partial_{x_{n}}g_{kl}))

\displaystyle=\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)(-c\Delta_{g}u-\frac{1}{2}(g^{nl}\partial_{x_{l}}c+g^{kn}\partial_{x_{k}}c-g^{kl}\partial_{x_{n}}g_{kl}))

\displaystyle=\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)(-c\Delta_{g}u-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}(2-n)\partial_{x_{n}}c).

For calculating the linearizations in Section

it is useful to transform

into

:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u

\displaystyle=g^{ij}\left(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}g^{mr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{ij})\partial_{x_{m}}u\right)

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\frac{1}{2c}\sum_{m,r=1}^{n-1}\hat{g}^{mr}g^{ij}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{ij})\partial_{x_{m}}u

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\frac{1}{2c}\sum_{m,r=1}^{n-1}\hat{g}^{mr}\left(\hat{g}^{ij}((\partial_{x_{j}}\hat{g}_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}\hat{g}_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}\hat{g}_{ij}))+\frac{2-n}{c}\partial_{x_{r}}c\right)\partial_{x_{m}}u

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\Delta_{\hat{g}}u-\frac{2-n}{2c^{2}}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u.

Thus

\displaystyle c^{2}|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{2}\Delta_{g}f(x)

\displaystyle=\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{2-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}(2-n)\partial_{x_{n}}c\right).

Now the second part (
2.4
) becomes

\displaystyle c^{2}\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u-\frac{1}{2}g^{nn}\partial_{x_{n}}c

\displaystyle-(g^{mr}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{nr}+\partial_{x_{n}}g_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{nj})\partial_{x_{m}}u-c^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}c)(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{j}

\displaystyle-\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}(\partial_{x_{j}}g_{in}+\partial_{x_{i}}g_{jn}-\partial_{x_{n}}g_{ij})(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{i}(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\sum_{m,r=1}^{n-1}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}c

\displaystyle-\sum_{m,r=1}^{n-1}c^{-1}\hat{g}^{mr}\hat{g}_{jr}\partial_{x_{n}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{j}+c^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}c(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{j}

\displaystyle-\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)+\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}c\hat{g}_{ij}(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{i}(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\sum_{m,r=1}^{n-1}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u-\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}c(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})-\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u).

As before we would like to modify
\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)
to have
\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}g^{mr}(\partial_{x_{i}}g_{rj}+\partial_{x_{j}}g_{ri}-\partial_{x_{r}}g_{ji})\partial_{x_{m}}u)\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{a}}u\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{b}}u.

Since
\partial_{x_{n}}u=0
, all the indices run from
1
to
n-1
. Hence

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\frac{1}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}(\partial_{x_{i}}c\hat{g}_{rj}+c\partial_{x_{i}}\hat{g}_{rj}+\partial_{x_{j}}c\hat{g}_{ri}+c\partial_{x_{j}}\hat{g}_{ri}

\displaystyle-\partial_{x_{r}}c\hat{g}_{ij}-c\partial_{x_{r}}\hat{g}_{ij})\partial_{x_{m}}u)\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{a}}u\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{b}}u

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u-\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u-\frac{1}{2c}(\delta^{m}_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}c+\delta^{m}_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}c-\hat{g}^{mr}\hat{g}_{ij}\partial_{x_{r}}c)\partial_{x_{m}}u)\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{a}}u\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{b}}u

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)-\frac{1}{2c}(\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{i}}c\partial_{x_{a}}u\hat{g}^{mb}\partial_{x_{m}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u+\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{j}}c\partial_{x_{b}}u\hat{g}^{mb}\partial_{x_{m}}u\partial_{x_{a}}u

\displaystyle-\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{j}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)-\frac{1}{2c}\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{i}}c\partial_{x_{a}}u|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2},

where
2\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}=\hat{g}^{mr}(\partial_{x_{j}}\hat{g}_{ir}+\partial_{x_{i}}\hat{g}_{jr}-\partial_{x_{r}}\hat{g}_{ij})
.

Putting these together gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c^{2}|\nabla_{g}f(x)|_{g}^{2}\Delta_{g}f(x)-c^{2}\left(\nabla_{g}^{2}f(x)\right)\left(\nabla_{g}f(x),\nabla_{g}f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u).

Thus
\text{Graph}_{u}
is a minimal surface if and only if the function
u
satisfies the following minimal surface equation

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{1-n}{2c(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))}\hat{g}^{mr}(x^{\prime})\partial_{x_{r}}c(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))}\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)=0

for all
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
.

We will modify this a bit in order to see the more familiar Euclidean version of the equation directly. For this, we start with the divergence of a vector field defined in local coordinates as

\dive_{g}a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}a^{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{j}\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ij}\right).

Let us denote
\eta:=\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and expand the following for a CTA metric and a function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as before:

\displaystyle\dive_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{i}}{\eta}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{\eta}\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ij}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}g^{ik}\partial_{x_{k}}u+g^{ik}\partial_{x_{i}x_{k}}^{2}u}{\eta}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{i}}{\eta^{3}}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}g^{ab}\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u+g^{ab}\left(\partial_{x_{i}x_{a}}^{2}u\partial_{x_{b}}u+\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{i}x_{b}}^{2}u\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{\eta}\left(\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ij}+\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj}\right).

Now we will use that
\partial_{x_{l}}g^{ik}=-(\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ml}g^{mk}+\Gamma^{k}_{\hphantom{k}ml}g^{im})
to have

\displaystyle\dive_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{-(\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}il}g^{lk}+\Gamma^{k}_{\hphantom{k}il}g^{il})\partial_{x_{k}}u+g^{ik}\partial_{x_{i}x_{k}}^{2}u}{\eta}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{i}}{\eta^{3}}\left(-(\Gamma^{a}_{\hphantom{a}il}g^{lb}+\Gamma^{b}_{\hphantom{b}il}g^{al})\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{b}}u+g^{ab}\left(\partial_{x_{i}x_{a}}^{2}u\partial_{x_{b}}u+\partial_{x_{a}}u\partial_{x_{i}x_{b}}^{2}u\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{\eta}\left(\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ij}+\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{g}u}{\eta}-\sum_{i,l=1}^{n-1}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{l}}{\eta}\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}il}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{i}}{\eta^{3}}\left(-(\nabla_{g}u)^{l}\Gamma^{a}_{\hphantom{a}il}\partial_{x_{a}}u-(\nabla_{g}u)^{l}\Gamma^{b}_{\hphantom{b}il}\partial_{x_{b}}u+(\nabla_{g}u)^{a}\partial_{x_{i}x_{a}}^{2}u+(\nabla_{g}u)^{b}\partial_{x_{i}x_{b}}^{2}u\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{\eta}\left(\Gamma^{i}_{\hphantom{i}ij}+\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj}\right).

After renaming some of the indices, using the definition of the Riemannian Hessian of a function and
\Gamma^{n}_{\hphantom{n}nj}=\frac{1}{2}c^{-1}\partial_{x_{j}}c
we get

(2.6)

\dive_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{\eta}\right)=\frac{\Delta_{g}u}{\eta}-\frac{\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}u)}{\eta^{3}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{2c\eta}\partial_{x_{j}}c.

Let us now go back to (
2.5
) and modify it to have (
2.6
) visible. Now

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

\displaystyle=\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{2-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u)

\displaystyle=\left(-c\Delta_{g}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})-\frac{1}{2}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u).

Then, we will multiply both sides of (
2.5
) by
c^{-2}
to get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(-\Delta_{g}u+\frac{n-1}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(c^{-1}+|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}_{g})-\frac{1}{2}c^{-3}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}u)

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{g}u|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}_{g}-c^{-1}\Delta_{g}u+\Delta_{g}u-\Delta_{g}u+\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{2c}\partial_{x_{j}}c\eta^{2}-\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{2c}\partial_{x_{j}}c\eta^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{2c^{3}}\partial_{x_{n}}c(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})-\frac{1}{2}c^{-3}(\nabla_{g}u)^{r}\partial_{x_{r}}c+\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}u)

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{g}u\eta^{2}+\nabla_{g}^{2}u(\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}u)-\frac{(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}}{2c}\partial_{x_{j}}c\eta^{2}+\Delta_{g}u(1-c^{-1})

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{2c^{3}}\partial_{x_{n}}c(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})+\frac{1}{2c}(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}c(\eta^{2}-c^{-2}).

Dividing both sides by
\eta^{3}=\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}
gives

\displaystyle\dive_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{g}u(1-c^{-1})+\frac{1}{2c}(\nabla_{g}u)^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}c(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}-c^{-2})+\frac{n-1}{2c^{3}}\partial_{x_{n}}c(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})}{\left(1+|\nabla_{g}u|_{g}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}=0.

When
g
is the Euclidean metric, we will have the familiar Euclidean minimal surface equation. Moreover, when
c=1
we get the minimal surface equation in
[
CLLO22
]
:

\dive_{\hat{g}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\hat{g}}u}{\left(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|_{\hat{g}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)=0.

3.
First and second order linearizations

Let
g
be as in (
2.1
) and define
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}\to\mathbb{R},

(3.1)

\displaystyle F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u):=

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u,\nabla_{\hat{g}}u).

We want to use the well-posedness result from
[
Nur23
]
to say that the boundary value problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega\\
u=f,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

has a unique small solution. For this we need that
F(x^{\prime},0,0,0)=0
which guarantees that
u\equiv 0
is a solution to (
3.3
) with
f=0
. From (
3.1
) we can see that this happens if and only if
\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},0)=0
for all
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
.

To use the above mentioned well-posedness, we will need to calculate the first linearization of the equation
F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=0
. Let us do a formal calculation to get the first linearization. Let
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})
where
\varepsilon_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
small and assume that
u_{\varepsilon}:=u(x,\varepsilon)
depends smoothly on
\varepsilon
and solves
F(x^{\prime},u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{g}u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{g}^{2}u_{\varepsilon})=0
. We will differentiate this in three parts with respect to
\varepsilon_{l}
and evaluate at
\varepsilon=0
(We could have calculated the first linearization as in
[
Nur23
]
, but it would be a longer calculation). Denote by
u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}:=\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}u_{\varepsilon}
. Just to make the calculations a bit nicer we will start with

(3.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left(\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-n}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}(\partial_{x_{r}x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)})

\displaystyle:=Q_{l}.

Now the first term of (
3.1
) becomes

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left(\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}_{\hat{g}})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}-\frac{n-1}{2c^{-2}}(\partial_{x_{n}}c)^{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}+Q_{l}\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}_{\hat{g}})

\displaystyle+\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)\hat{g}^{ij}(\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}).

The second term is

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left(\nabla_{\hat{g}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left((\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}-\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon})\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{a}}u_{\varepsilon}\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{b}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}-\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)})\hat{g}^{ia}\partial_{x_{a}}u_{\varepsilon}\hat{g}^{jb}\partial_{x_{b}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+(\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}-\hat{\Gamma}^{m}_{\hphantom{m}ij}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon})\hat{g}^{ia}\hat{g}^{jb}(\partial_{x_{a}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{b}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{a}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x_{b}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)})

\displaystyle:=P_{1}+P_{2}.

where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are for future reference.
When evaluating at
\varepsilon=0
we have
u_{0}=0
, also
\nabla_{g}u_{0},\nabla_{g}^{2}u_{0}
vanish, and thus when combining the above, denoting
v^{l}:=u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}|_{\varepsilon=0}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}F(x^{\prime},u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{g}u_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{g}^{2}u_{\varepsilon})|_{\varepsilon=0}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\hat{g}}v^{l}+\frac{n-1}{2c(x^{\prime},0)}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},0)v^{l}+\frac{1-n}{2c(x^{\prime},0)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}c(x^{\prime},0)\partial_{x_{j}}v^{l}=0.

Now this first linearization satisfies the assumptions of
[
Nur23
, Proposition 2.1]
and thus we have the existence and uniqueness of small solutions to (
3.3
).

Next we will calculate the second linearization for the equation
F(x^{\prime},u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=0
. Denote
u_{\varepsilon}^{(kl)}:=\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{l}}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}
. We will differentiate (
3.4
)-(
3.6
) with respect to
\varepsilon_{k}
,
k\neq l
, starting with (
3.4
):

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left(\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-n}{c^{3}}(\partial_{x_{n}}c)^{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\hat{g}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1-n}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\hat{g}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\frac{1-n}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(kl)}\hat{g}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\frac{1-n}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\hat{g}^{mr}(\partial_{x_{r}x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})

\displaystyle-\frac{1-n}{2c^{2}}\partial_{x_{n}}c\hat{g}^{mr}(\partial_{x_{r}x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)})

\displaystyle+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\big(\partial_{x_{r}}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{r}x_{n}}^{2}c(u_{\varepsilon}^{(kl)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{k})

\displaystyle+\partial_{x_{r}x_{n}}cu_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(l)}+\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}^{(kl)}\big).

Then we differentiate (
3.5
) to have

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\left(\left(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1-n}{2c}\hat{g}^{mr}\partial_{x_{r}}c\partial_{x_{m}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n-1}{2c}\partial_{x_{n}}c\right)(1+|\nabla_{\hat{g}}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}_{\hat{g}})\right)

v^{3}=\ldots=v^{K+1}=1
) gives
\partial_{x_{n}}^{K+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
.

Subtracting the equations (
4.9
) and (
4.11
) we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{\hat{g}}\left(\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}u(x^{\prime},0)-\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)\right)\\
-X\left(\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}u(x^{\prime},0)-\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)\right)=0,&\text{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}u(x^{\prime},0)-\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)=0,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

This is true, since by induction assumptions for all
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
the other terms agree for
k\leq K
. Again, by the uniqueness of solutions,
\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}u(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{K+1}}^{K+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)
,
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
, which ends the subinduction.

Returning to the original induction, the linearization of order
N+1
at
\varepsilon_{1}=\dots=\varepsilon_{N+1}=0
for the metric
g
is

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\hat{g}}\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}u(x^{\prime},0)-X\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}u(x^{\prime},0)+R_{N}(u,g(x^{\prime},0),0)

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{2c(x^{\prime},0)}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}c(x^{\prime},0)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N+1}v^{(l_{k})}\right)=0,

x^{\prime}\in\,\,\Omega

and for the metric
\tilde{c}g
we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\hat{g}}\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)-X\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)+R_{N}(\tilde{u},g(x^{\prime},0),0)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n-1}{2c(x^{\prime},0)}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}c(x^{\prime},0)+\frac{n-1}{2\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N+1}v^{(l_{k})}\right)=0.

Now the subinduction implies that
R_{N}(u,g(x^{\prime},0),0)=R_{N}(\tilde{u},g(x^{\prime},0),0)
. Thus by subtracting, integrating against
v^{(0)}
(the solution of (
4.7
)), using integration by parts and that
\partial_{\nu}\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}u(x^{\prime},0)|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}\partial_{l_{1}\dots l_{N+1}}^{N+1}\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)|_{\partial\Omega}
we get

\int_{\Omega}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)^{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)v^{(0)}\prod_{k=1}^{N+1}v^{l_{k}}\,dx^{\prime}=0.

Choosing all but two of the functions

to be equal to

, then by the completeness of such solutions (Lemma
4.1
) implies that

\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x_{0}^{\prime},0)=0.

Since
x_{0}^{\prime}
was arbitrary, we have
\partial_{x_{n}}^{N+2}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=0
for all
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
.
∎

We will next prove Theorem
1.4
. The proof will be very similar to the one above and we will only point out the differences.

Let
\varepsilon
be, as in the previous proof, small enough so that (
3.3
) has a unique small solution for

, where

with

and

.

The first linearizations are (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and for the corresponding partial DN maps we have

(4.12)

(D\Lambda_{g}^{\Gamma})_{0}=(D\Lambda_{cg}^{\Gamma})_{0}.

Now using Lemma
4.2
together with a boundary determination
[

, Proposition
4.1
]
we get from the first linearization that for all

\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)}{\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)}=0,\ \text{for}\ x^{\prime}\in\Omega.

This then implies two things. Firstly,
\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)=\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. Secondly, since solutions to an advection diffusion equation are unique, we have that
v^{l}=\tilde{v}^{l}
.

Let us move to the second order linearization and let
x_{0}^{\prime}\in\Omega
. We will subtract equations (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and integrate against the function
v^{(0)}
that solves

(4.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\hat{g}}v^{(0)}-Xv^{(0)}-qv^{(0)}=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega\\
v^{(0)}=g,&\text{on}\,\,\Gamma\\
v^{(0)}=0,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

for

,
g\geq 0
,
g\not\equiv 0
and that

. This function can be constructed exactly as before. We now do the integration, where the difference will be on how to deal with the boundary terms:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{n-1}{2\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)}\partial_{x_{n}}^{3}\tilde{c}(x^{\prime},0)v^{k}v^{l}v^{(0)}\,dV_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-(w^{kl}-\tilde{w}^{kl})(-\Delta_{\hat{g}}v^{(0)}+Xv^{(0)}+qv^{(0)})\,dV_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega}(w^{kl}-\tilde{w}^{kl})\partial_{\nu}v^{(0)}-v^{(0)}\partial_{\nu}(w^{kl}-\tilde{w}^{kl})\,dS

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}(w^{kl}-\tilde{w}^{kl})\frac{1-n}{2c(x^{\prime},0)}\hat{g}^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}c(x^{\prime},0)|\hat{g}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{(0)}\nu_{j}\,dS.

Proceeding with induction as before and taking further care about the boundary terms in the integrals will conclude the proof.

Existence of solutions for a singular double phase in Sobolev-Orlicz spaces with variable exponents in a complete manifold
Ahmed AberqiJaouad BennounaOmar Benslimane, Maria Alessandra Ragusa

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to study a class of double phase problems, with a singular term and a superlinear parametric term on the right-hand side. Using the method of Nehari manifold combined with the fibering maps, we prove that for all small values of the parameter λ > 0 , \lambda>0, there exist at least two non-trivial positive solutions. Our results extend the previous works Papageorgiou, Repovš, and Vetro papageorgiou2020positive and Liu, Dai, Papageorgiou, and Winkert liu2021existence , from the case of Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space, when exponents p p and q q are constant, to the case of Sobolev-Orlicz spaces with variable exponents in a complete manifold.

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth complete compact Riemannian N-manifold. In this article, we are addressed in proving the existence of at least two weak solutions to the following singular double phase problem.

(\mathcal{P})\,\begin{cases}-\,\mbox{div}(|D\mathrm{u}(z)|^{p(z)-2}D\mathrm{u}(z)+\mu(z)|D\mathrm{u}(z)|^{q(z)-2}D\mathrm{u}(z)\,)\\
\hskip 17.07182pt=\frac{g(z)}{\mathrm{u}(z)^{\gamma(z)}}+\lambda\,|\mathrm{u}(z)|^{r(z)-1}&\text{in \,\,M },\\[8.5359pt]
\,\mathrm{u}\,>\,0&\text{in \,\,M },\\
\,\mathrm{u}\,=\,0&\text{on \,$\partial$M }.\end{cases}

We suppose the subsequent assumptions:

(i)

The variables exponents
p,q,r\in C(\overline{M})
and satisfy the following assumptions:

1<q^{-}\leq q^{+}<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<r^{-}\leq r^{+}<+\infty,

(1)

where
q^{+}=\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\overline{M}}q(z),\,\,q^{-}=\inf_{z\in\overline{M}}q(z),\,\,p^{+}=\sup_{z\in\overline{M}}p(z),\,\,p^{-}=\inf_{z\in\overline{M}}p(z),\\
r^{+}=\sup_{z\in\overline{M}}r(z),\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,r^{-}=\inf_{z\in\overline{M}}r(z).

\frac{p^{-}}{q^{+}}<1+\frac{1}{N}.

(2)

(ii)

\mu:\overline{M}\rightarrow[1,+\infty)
is Lipschitz continuous.

(iii)

g(z)\in L^{\infty}(M)
and
g(z)\geq 0
for a.a
z\in M
with
g\not\equiv 0
.

(iv)

0<\gamma(z)\in C(\overline{M}),\,\,0<\gamma^{-}\leq\gamma^{+}<1.

(v)

\lambda
is a positive parameter.

In this section, we are going to prove our main result stated as Theorem
1.1
in section
1

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{\lambda}(\mathrm{u})=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{p(z)}\,|D\mathrm{u}(z)|^{p(z)}\,\,dv_{g}(z)+\int_{M}\frac{\mu(z)}{q(z)}\,|D\mathrm{u}(z)|^{q(z)}\,\,dv_{g}(z)

From the presence of the singular term

we known that

is not

In order to overcome this, we will make use the Nehari manifold corresponding to the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}=\{\mathrm{u}\in W_{0}^{1,q(z)}(M)\backslash\{0\};\,

\displaystyle||D\mathrm{u}||_{p(z)}^{p(z)}+\int_{M}\mu(z)\,|D\mathrm{u}(z)|^{q(z)}\,\,dv_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}g(z)|\mathrm{u}(z)|^{1-\gamma(z)}\,\,dv_{g}(z)+\lambda||\mathrm{u}||_{r(z)}^{r(z)}\,\}.

It is easy to see that

For further considerations we decompose the set

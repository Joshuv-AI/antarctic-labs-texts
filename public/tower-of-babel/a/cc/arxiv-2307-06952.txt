Non-Hermitian propagation in equally-spaced Hermitian waveguide arrays
Ivan Bocanegra, Héctor M. Moya-Cessa

Abstract
A non-unitary transformation leading to a Hatano-Nelson problem is performed on an array of equally-spaced optical waveguides. Such transformation produces a non-reciprocal system of waveguides, as the corresponding Hamiltonian becomes non-Hermitian. This may be achieved by judiciously choosing an attenuation (amplification) of the injected (or exciting) field. The non-Hermitian transport induced by such transformation is studied for several cases and closed analytical solutions are obtained. The corresponding non-Hermitian Hamiltonian may represent an open system that interacts with the environment, either loosing to or being provided with energy from the exterior.

2.1
Non-Hermitian transport in the Hermitian equally-spaced system

When the Hamiltonian (
6
) is
z
-independent, the solution of (
5
) is given by

|\psi(z)\rangle=e^{-iHz}|\psi(0)\rangle,

(9)

with
|\psi(0)\rangle
a given initial condition, for instance at
z=0
.

A transformation

|\psi(0)\rangle=e^{-\gamma\hat{n}}|\phi(0)\rangle,\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

(10)

is considered, with
\hat{n}|k\rangle=k|k\rangle
,
|k\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
.

By replacing (
10
) into (
9
), it is obtained

|\psi(z)\rangle=e^{-\gamma\hat{n}}e^{\gamma\hat{n}}e^{-iHz}e^{-\gamma\hat{n}}|\phi(0)\rangle=e^{-\gamma\hat{n}}|\phi(z)\rangle,

(11)

with

|\phi(z)\rangle:=e^{\gamma\hat{n}}e^{-iHz}e^{-\gamma\hat{n}}|\phi(0)\rangle=e^{-i\tilde{H}z}|\phi(0)\rangle,

(12)

the solution of the non-Hermitian (non-conservative) problem

i\frac{\partial|\phi(z)\rangle}{\partial z}=\tilde{H}|\phi(z)\rangle,

(13)

where

\tilde{H}=\alpha(e^{\gamma}V^{\dagger}+e^{-\gamma}V)=k_{1}V^{\dagger}+k_{2}V,

(14)

is non-Hermitian, this is
\tilde{H}^{\dagger}\neq\tilde{H}
, and
k_{1}=\alpha e^{\gamma},k_{2}=\alpha e^{-\gamma}
. We have used the commutation relations
[\hat{n},V]=-V
and
[\hat{n},V^{\dagger}]=V^{\dagger}
to obtain the last expression in (
12
). From now on we focus on the non-Hermitian problem (
13
)-(
14
).

In the particular case of the infinite array the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian (
14
) coincides with Eq. (2) of Ref.
[
29
]
. Thus, the problem (
13
) for the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in (
14
),
i.e.
the Hatano-Nelson problem, is rather interesting by itself. Moreover, a detailed proposal of its implementation in optical devices by means of coupled resonator optical waveguides (CROW’s) is finely described in
[

]
(also see
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
for alternative implementations). In a different approach, here we analyze (
13
)-(
14
) as modelling the non-Hermitian transport of the actual Hermitian system (
5
)-(
6
) in the finite, semi-infinite and infinite regimes.

Equation (
13
) has a partner equation

i\dot{d}_{n}(z)=k_{1}d_{n-1}(z)+k_{2}d_{n+1}(z),

(15)

by means of

|\phi(z)\rangle=\sum_{n}d_{n}(z)|n\rangle,

(16)

where

d_{k}(z)=\langle k|\phi(z)\rangle=e^{\gamma k}c_{k}(z),\qquad k\in S,

(17)

represents the amplitude of the electric field propagating in the
k
-th waveguide of the non-conservative (non-Hermitian) array. To obtain the last equality in (
17
), (
8
) and (

) have been used.

For general
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, the solution
|\phi(z)\rangle
of (

) can be cast in terms of the solution

of the corresponding Hermitian problem (
5
)-(
6
) as

|\phi(z)\rangle=\left(\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}}\right)^{\displaystyle\frac{\hat{n}}{2}}|\psi(z)\rangle.

For

and

as given in (

). Then, the non-Hermitian transport is obtained from the Hermitian one by means of the rather simple relation

Semi-infinite array

In the semi-infinite case the operators
V
and

(the Susskind-Glogower operators) appearing in the Hamiltonian in (

V^{{\dagger}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|k+1\rangle\langle k|,

[\hat{n},V^{\dagger}]=V^{\dagger}.

And the relation between

In parallel, we have

The ladder operators above may be written in terms of annihilation and creation operators as

As we have

4
Finite array

In the case of a finite array, the operators
V
and
V^{\dagger}
in (
6
), together with
\hat{n}
, are defined by

V_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|k\rangle\langle k+1|,

(33a)

V_{N}^{{\dagger}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|k+1\rangle\langle k|,

(33b)

\hat{n}_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}k|k\rangle\langle k|,

(33c)

where the subindex
N
has been added to emphasize that they correspond to the finite case, where we have a total of
N
waveguides in the array. The commutation relations are

[V_{N},V_{N}^{\dagger}]=|0\rangle\langle 0|,

(34a)

[\hat{n}_{N},V_{N}]=-V_{N},

(34b)

[\hat{n}_{N},V_{N}^{\dagger}]=V_{N}^{\dagger}.

(34c)

Indeed, these have exactly the same form of those in (
21c
). Thus, along this section we only call
V
,
V^{\dagger}
and
\hat{n}
, respectively, the operators defined in (
33c
).

We connect
(
4
) and (
5
) through

|\psi(z)\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}c_{k}(z)|k\rangle,

(35)

with
|k\rangle\in\mathcal{H},k=0,1,\dots,N-1
. As now we have two edge or ending waveguides, besides (
4
) we have

i\dot{c}_{0}(z)=\alpha c_{1}(z),\qquad i\dot{c}_{N-1}(z)=\alpha c_{N-2}(z),

(36)

and

i\dot{d}_{0}(z)=k_{2}d_{1}(z),\qquad i\dot{d}_{N-1}(z)=k_{1}d_{N-2}(z),

(37)

besides (
15
).
In order to follow
[
21
]
, we change to a normalized variable
Z:=\alpha z
, such that (
4
) and (
36
) become

i\dot{c}_{n}(Z)=c_{n-1}(Z)+c_{n+1}(Z),

(38)

i\dot{c}_{0}(Z)=c_{1}(Z),

(39)

i\dot{c}_{N-1}(Z)=c_{N-2}(Z).

(40)

The Hamiltonian (
6
), for
V
and
V^{\dagger}
as given in (

), then takes the simple form of a square tri-diagonal matrix of size

(see also

]
)

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\end{array}\right).

(41)

A similarity transformation

\Lambda=S^{-1}HS,

(42)

is to be performed, with
S
an orthonormal matrix whose columns are the normalized eigenvectors
|\psi_{j}\rangle
of
H
, this is

H|\psi_{j}\rangle=\lambda_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle,\qquad j=0,1,\dots,N-1.

(43)

The
N
eigenvalues
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N-1}
are as usual obtained from the condition

D_{N}=0
,
with
D_{N}\equiv
det
(H-\lambda\mathbb{I})
and
\mathbb{I}
the identity matrix of size
N
. In turn,
D_{N}
can be obtained by the three-terms recurrence relation

D_{0}=1,

(44a)

D_{1}=\lambda,

(44b)

D_{s}=\lambda D_{s-1}-D_{s-2},\qquad s=2,3,\dots,N,

(44c)

and the corresponding normalized eigenvectors turn out to be

|\psi_{j}\rangle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[D_{k}(\lambda_{j})\right]^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}D_{0}(\lambda_{j})\\
D_{1}(\lambda_{j})\\
\vdots\\
D_{N-1}(\lambda_{j})\par\end{array}\right).

(45)

The recurrence relations (
44c
) are those of the Chebyshev polynomials
[
40
]
of the second kind, such that the condition to find out the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of
H
is given by

D_{N}(\lambda)=U_{N}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)=0,

(46)

with
U_{k}(x)
the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind of order
k
. Thus, obtaining (for details, the reader is referred to
[
21
]
)

\lambda_{j}=2\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right],\qquad j=0,1,\dots,N-1.

(47)

Note that unlike Ref.
[
21
]
we label the eigenvalues from
0
to
N-1
.
The eigenvectors thus become

|\psi_{j}\rangle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[U_{k}(\lambda_{j}/2)\right]^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{0}(\lambda_{j}/2)\\
U_{1}(\lambda_{j}/2)\\
\vdots\\
U_{N-1}(\lambda_{j}/2)\par\end{array}\right).

(48)

By inverting (
42
) and replacing into (
9
) we obtain

|\psi(Z)\rangle=Se^{-i\Lambda Z}S^{-1}|\psi(0)\rangle\equiv R|\psi(0)\rangle,

where

is a diagonal matrix with diagonal elements
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N-1}
, and

is a square matrix of size

with elements

R_{p,q}(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{U_{p-1}(\lambda_{j}/2)U_{q-1}(\lambda_{j}/2)\exp(-iZ\lambda_{j})}{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[U_{k}(\lambda_{j}/2)\right]^{2}},

(50)

with
p,q=1,2,\dots,N
. From (
49
) and (
50
) we obtain

c_{n}(Z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}R_{n+1,\ell+1}(Z)c_{\ell}(0),\qquad n=0,1,\dots N-1,

(51)

or explicitly

c_{n}(Z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{c_{\ell}(0)\exp\left(-2iZ\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[U_{k}\left(\cos\left[\displaystyle\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)\right]^{2}}

\times\left\{U_{n}\left(\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)U_{\ell}\left(\cos\left[\displaystyle\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)\right\}.

(52)

For a single input as initial state
|\psi(0)\rangle
, at the
m

, with
\delta_{ij}
the Kronecker delta, thus giving

\times\left\{U_{n}\left(\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)U_{m}\left(\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)\right\}.

(53)

Therefore, the impulse response of the non-conservative system is simply

d_{n}(Z)=e^{\gamma n}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{\exp\left(-2iZ\cos\left[\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[U_{k}\left(\cos\left[\displaystyle\frac{\left(j+1\right)\pi}{N+1}\right]\right)\right]^{2}}

(54)

5
Infinite array

In the infinite case a slight modification of equation (
4
) is considered:

i\dot{c}_{n}(z)=\omega(-1)^{n}c_{n}(z)+\alpha\left[c_{n+1}(z)+c_{n-1}(z)\right],\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R},

(58)

with
n=\dots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\dots
The equation (
58
) rules the propagation of the electromagnetic field in a binary waveguide that, in turn, represents the optical analogue of the rapid trembling motion (
Zitterbewegung
) of free relativistic (Dirac) electrons, caused by the interference between positive and negative energy states in relativistic quantum mechanics, see for instance
[
15
]
. Also, a minor modification of (
58
) serves to simulate Bloch-Zener oscillations in lattices of curved waveguides
[
16
]
. The corresponding Hamiltonian is then

H=\omega(-1)^{\hat{n}}+\alpha(V^{\dagger}+V),

(59)

with the operators in (
59
) defined by

V_{\infty}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|k\rangle\langle k+1|,

(60a)

V_{\infty}^{{\dagger}}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|k+1\rangle\langle k|,

(60b)

\hat{n}_{\infty}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}k|k\rangle\langle k|.

(60c)

The subindex
\infty
has been added to remark that they correspond to the infinite case. Their commutation relations are thus

[V_{\infty},V_{\infty}^{\dagger}]=[V_{\infty}^{\dagger},V_{\infty}]=0,

(61a)

[\hat{n}_{\infty},V_{\infty}]=-V_{\infty},

(61b)

[\hat{n}_{\infty},V_{\infty}^{\dagger}]=V_{\infty}^{\dagger}.

(61c)

Note that again the second and third expressions in (
61c
) preserve the form of the corresponding expressions in (
21c
). Nonetheless, in this case
V_{\infty}
and
V^{\dagger}_{\infty}
commute. As previously, along this section the subindices are dropped by keeping in mind the definitions given in (
60c
).

The relation between
(
58
) and (
5
), with
H
in (
59
), is given by

|\psi(z)\rangle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{k}(z)|k\rangle,

(62)

with
|k\rangle,k=\dots,-1,0,1,\dots
, a basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Namely,

\langle m|n\rangle=\delta_{mn},\qquad\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}|m\rangle\langle m|=\mathbb{I},

(63)

with
\mathbb{I}
the identity operator and
m,n=\dots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\dots
By defining
\cos{\hat{\Phi}}=\displaystyle\frac{V+V^{\dagger}}{2}
, the Hamiltonian (
59
) becomes
H=\omega\left(-1\right)^{\hat{n}}+2\alpha\cos\hat{\Phi}
. By using the relation

(-1)^{\hat{n}}V=-V(-1)^{\hat{n}},

(64)

it is obtained

H^{2}=\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}.

(65)

Therefore (compare to
[
37
]
)

H^{2k}=\left(\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}\right)^{2k},

(66)

and

H^{2k+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}}

\times\left(\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}\right)^{2k+1}\left(\omega(-1)^{\hat{n}}+2\alpha{\cos{\hat{\Phi}}}\right).

(67)

The propagator in (
9

e^{-iHz}=\cos\left(z\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}\right)-i\sin\left(z\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}\right)

\times\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}{\cos^{2}{\hat{\Phi}}}}}\left(\omega(-1)^{\hat{n}}+2\alpha{\cos{\hat{\Phi}}}\right).

(68)

Also, as we desire to obtain the impulse response of the system we choose
|\psi(0)\rangle=|m\rangle
. Nevertheless, due to the symmetry of the problem, we can set
m=0
without any loss of generality. Thus, from (

(69)

where

\mathbb{I}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\eta|\eta\rangle\langle\eta|,

(70)

with
|\eta\rangle
the phase states (see Ref.
[
37
]
) defined as

|\eta\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{in\eta}|n\rangle.

(71)

By using

(-1)^{\hat{n}}|\eta\rangle=|\eta+\pi\rangle,\quad V^{{\dagger}}|\eta\rangle=e^{i\eta}|\eta\rangle,\quad V|\eta\rangle=e^{-i\eta}|\eta\rangle,

(72)

expression (
69
) results into

|\psi(z)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\eta\left\{\cos(\xi(\eta)z)|\eta\rangle\right.

\left.-i\Omega(\eta,z)(2\alpha\cos\eta|\eta\rangle+\omega|\eta+\pi\rangle)\right\},

(73)

with

\Omega(\eta,z):=\frac{\sin(\xi(\eta)z)}{\xi(\eta)},\qquad\xi(\eta):=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\eta}.

(74)

Also, since

\langle k|\eta\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ik\eta},\qquad\langle k|\eta+\pi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ik\eta}(-1)^{k},

(75)

we obtain

\left.\left.-i\Omega(\eta,z)(2\alpha\cos\eta+\omega(-1)^{n})\right]\right\}.

(76)

Therefore

d_{n}(z)=\frac{e^{\gamma n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\eta\left\{\cos(\eta n)\left[\cos(\xi(\eta)z)\right.\right.

\left.\left.-i\Omega(\eta,z)(2\alpha\cos\eta+\omega(-1)^{n})\right]\right\}.

(77)

In the particular case

(78)

6
Conclusions

The non-Hermitian propagation induced by a non-unitary transformation, leading to a Hatano-Nelson type problem of an actual equally-spaced Hermitian waveguide array, was discussed. Three regimes were addressed: the finite, semi-infinite and completely infinite waveguide arrays. Quite generally, such transformation enhances the propagation towards one (transversal) direction in the array while inhibiting propagation towards the opposite direction.

In the semi-infinite and finite regimes, when one edge waveguide is initially excited, the intensity
|d_{n}(z)|^{2}
of the electromagnetic field is damped (
\gamma<0
) as it propagates (upper middle panels in Figure
4
and Figure
5
), thus describing a more accurate situation of actual propagation in comparison with the Hermitian
\gamma=0
case (upper left panels in the same Figures). On the other hand, the non-Hermitian
\gamma>0
case models external providing of power, shown for instance in the upper right panels of Figure
4
and Figure
5
. Thus, the (open) non-Hermitian system
\gamma\neq 0
describes better the actual physical propagation phenomena in the finite and semi-infinite arrays when an edge waveguide is initially excited.

Also, from lower rows in Figure
4
and Figure
5
,

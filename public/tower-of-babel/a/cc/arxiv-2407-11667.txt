Cumulants and Limit Theorems for q q -step walks on random graphs of long-range percolation radius model
O. Khorunzhiy

Abstract
We study cumulants of q q -step walks and 3 3 -step closed walks on Erdős-Rényi-type random graphs of long-range percolation radius model in the limit when the number of vertices N N , concentration c c , and the interaction radius R R tend to infinity. These cumulants represent terms of cumulant expansion of the free energy of discrete analogs of matrix models widely known in mathematical and theoretical physics. Using a diagram technique, we show that the limiting values of k k -th cumulants ℱ k ( q ) {\cal F}_{k}^{(q)} exist and can be associated with one or another family of tree-type diagrams, in dependence of the asymptotic behavior of parameters c ​ R / N cR/N for q q -step non-closed walks and c 2 ​ R / N 2 c^{2}R/N^{2} for 3-step closed walks, respectively. These results allow us to prove Limit Theorems for the number of non-closed walks and for the number of triangles in large random graphs. Adapting the Prüfer codification procedure to the tree-type diagrams obtained, we get explicit expressions for their numbers. This allows us to get upper bounds for ℱ k ( q ) {\cal F}_{k}^{(q)} as k → ∞ k\to\infty and, in the limit of infinite q q , to get upper bounds in terms of high moments of Compound Poisson distribution.

3.1
Cumulants and semi-invariants

We start with well-known semi-invariant representation of cumulants

,

and where by definition,

cum\left\{{\langle}Y^{(q)}{\rangle}_{1},\dots,{\langle}Y^{(q)}{\rangle}_{k}\right\}={d^{k}\over dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k}}\log{\mathbb{E}}\left(\exp\left\{z_{1}{\langle}Y^{(q)}{\rangle}_{1}+\dots+z_{k}{\langle}Y^{(q)}{\rangle}_{k}\right\}\right)|_{z_{i}=0}.

with given values of variables
that belong to the set

[formula]

It is known that the right-hand side of (3.2) can be computed

+\ \sum_{s=2}^{k}\ \sum_{\pi_{s}\in\Pi_{k}}\,(-1)^{s-1}\,(s-1)!\ {\mathbb{E}}\left({\langle}Y^{(q)}(\rho_{1}){\rangle}\right)\,\cdots{\mathbb{E}}\,\left({\langle}Y^{(q)}(\rho_{s}){\rangle}\right),

[formula]

where

into

To compute the mean values of the right-hand side of (3.5), we use a version of the diagram method
widely known in random matrix theory and its applications.
Let us briefly describe the main ingredients of this method,
some of its elements
date back to the original works by
E. Wigner
[
25
]
. The starting point is to use a natural
graphical representation of a random variable
a_{ij}
by
two vertices joined by an edge, the vertices being attributed by
values of
i

{\langle}i{\rangle}_{q+1}={\langle}i_{1},\dots,i_{q+1}{\rangle}_{N}

the product of random variables

{\mathbb{E}}{\langle}Y^{(q)}{\rangle}=\prod_{e(j,l)\in{\cal E}({\rm g}({\langle}i{\rangle}_{q+1}))}{\mathbb{E}}\left(a^{(\alpha)}_{i_{j}i_{l}}\right)^{m(j,l)},

[formula]

where according to (2.1) and (2.2),

{\mathbb{E}}\left(a^{(\alpha)}_{i_{j}i_{l}}\right)^{m(j,l)}={c\over N}\,\,h^{(\alpha)}_{m(i,j)}\left({s_{i}-s_{j}\over R}\right),\quad h_{m(i,j)}^{(\alpha)}\big(t\big)=\big(1+\alpha\psi^{2}(t)\big)^{m(i,j)}e^{-\psi^{2}(t)},

and

of
D_{k}^{(q)}

into the sum over classes of equivalence

and finally, to compute the contribution of a class by multiplying this average value by the cardinality of
the class. In the newt subsection, we give rigorous definition of the

Lemma 4.2.

In all of three asymptotic regimes of Lemma 4.1, the following relation is true,

[formula]

[formula]

where
V_{m}^{(0)}=V_{0}
(2.7) and
V_{m}^{(1)}
is determined by (2.14).

Proof.

Let us consider an auxiliary tree-type diagram

{\cal T}^{(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})}={\rm T}_{r}

constructed with the help of
r

\lambda_{q_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{q_{r}}

We will also use denotation

for such tree-type diagrams.
Using recurrence by
l=|\bar{q}_{r}|\geq 1

A_{l}

based on (3.10),

{\rm A}_{l}:\lim_{(N,R)_{0}\to\infty}{1\over N(p_{N}R)^{E}}W_{N,R}({\cal T}^{(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})})=w^{(\alpha)}({\cal T}^{(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})})

[formula]

The initial step is given by the diagram

we restrict the sum over
s_{1}
to the sum over interval

s_{1}
from this interval we replace the

{1\over cR}W_{N,c,sR}({\cal T}^{(1,1,\dots,1)})={1\over N}\sum_{s_{1}\in[-N+RL,N-RL]}{1\over R}\sum_{s_{2}\in[-N,N]}h_{m(1,2)}\left({s_{1}-s_{2}\over R}\right)+\Sigma_{1}^{(N,R,L)},

[formula]

where

where we denoted

6

Convergence of cumulants of random variables
X^{(\alpha,3)}_{N,c,R}
and

Y^{(\alpha,q)}_{N,c,R}

of (2.4)
makes possible to prove limiting theorems
for their centered and normalized versions. In particular, it is known from probability theory
that
if there exists
a sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
cumulants of random variables
{\Upsilon}_{n}
verify the following property,

b_{n}\to\infty\quad{\hbox{and}}\quad{1\over b_{n}}{\rm Cum}_{k}({\Upsilon}_{n})\to\phi_{k},\ k\geq 1,\ n\to\infty,

[formula]

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Cum}_{k}\left({{\Upsilon}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}{\Upsilon}_{n}\over\sqrt{b_{n}}}\right)=\begin{cases}\phi_{2},&\text{if \ $k=2$};\\
0,\quad&\text{if \ $k\neq 2$}.\end{cases}

[formula]

{\cal N}(0,\phi^{2})

\chi_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\cal L}}}{{\to}}\chi,\quad\chi\sim{\cal N}(0,\phi^{2}),\quad n\to\infty.

[formula]

when centered and properly normalized, or to a random variable
with Poisson probability distribution, in dependence of the asymptotic behavior

Regarding a tree-type diagram

Lemma 7.1.

The number of

-type Prüfer sequences

|{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{(q)}(\sigma_{k})|={(k-1)!}\,\,{(k(q-1)+1)!\over(k(q-1)+1-u)!}\,\ \prod_{i=1}^{k-1}{1\over\,(i!)^{s_{i}}\,s_{i}!},

[formula]

Proof of Lemma 7.1.

It is known that

Thus

8
Upper bounds for cumulants and transition
q\to\infty

This section is of a speculative character because some arguments
and assumptions are non-rigorous.
In mathematical physics studies of cumulant expansions of free energy of the form

Z^{(Y^{(q)})}_{n}
(1.5), it is important to get the upper bounds
for the cumulants because this knowledge can help to determine
the domains of parameters with no phase transitions.
In this section, we study the upper bounds of cumulants of
random variable
Y^{(q)}_{N,c,R}
(2.4) that counts the number of
q
-step walks
in random graphs in the asymptotic regime of full graphs,
when
{\rm d}_{N,c,R}=cR/N\gg 1
. Let us note that in the ensemble of random graphs we have (2.1),
one can consider the value
c/N
as the edge probability and
R
as an effective
size of graph around a vertex, so
{\rm d}_{N,c,R}
can be regarded as
an average vertex degree of the graphs.

Regarding Theorem 2.1, we deduce from (2.11) that the cumulants

\Phi_{k}^{(q,1)}
obviously have factorial behavior as
k\to\infty
,

\tilde{\Phi}_{k}^{(q,1)}={1\over(2q^{2}V_{0}^{q-1})^{k}}\Phi_{k}^{(q,1)}=2q^{2}V_{0}\left(k+{1\over q-1}\right)^{k-2}\left(1-{1\over q}\right)^{k-2}

[formula]

and the series
\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{k}\tilde{\Phi}_{k}^{(q,1)}/k!

converges for all
t
from the domain

D_{q}=\left\{t\in{\mathbb{C}}:|t|<\rho_{q}={q\over(q-1)e^{1/(q-1)}}\right\}.

[formula]

Remembering (1.13),
one could expect that the function

Z^{(\alpha,q)}(t)=\log{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{t\tilde{Y}^{(\alpha,q)}_{N,c,R}\right\},\quad\tilde{Y}^{(\alpha,q)}_{N,c,R}={1\over 2q^{2}V_{0}^{q-1}}\left({N\over cR}\right)^{q-1}Y^{(\alpha,q)}_{N,c,R}

[formula]

with
\alpha=0
is analytical for
t\in D_{q}
, in the limit
N,c,R\to\infty
,
{\rm d}_{N,c,R}\to\infty
,
and
therefore
D_{q}
could be considered as free from phase transition points.
In what follows, we assume for simplicity that
V_{0}=1
. The last remark
to do is that with the additional normalization by
2q^{2}
(8.3),
one can consider the limiting transition
q\to\infty

and the domain
D_{q}
(8.5) obviously becomes
the inner area of the unit circle.

8.1
Estimates by moments of sums of Bernoulli random variables

Let us consider the sum of
k(q-1)+1
i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables

\xi_{i}
with mean value
p
.
and write down its
(k-1)
-th moment as follows,

{\bf E}\left(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\dots+\xi_{k(q-1)+1}\right)^{k-1}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k-1}\leq k(q-1)+1}{\bf E}\big(\xi_{i_{1}}\xi_{i_{2}}\cdots\xi_{i_{k}}\big),

[formula]

where
{\bf E}
denotes the mathematical expectation with respect to the measure generated by

\{\xi_{i},1\leq i\leq k(q-1)+1\}
.
Considering
a realization
{\langle}i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}{\rangle}
such that there are

s_{j}
subsets of
j
elements,
1\leq j\leq k-1
that have equal value,
we get

{\bf E}\big(\xi_{i_{1}}\xi_{i_{2}}\cdots\xi_{i_{k-1}}\big)=p^{s_{1}}p^{s_{2}}\cdots p^{s_{k-1}}=p^{u},\quad u=s_{1}+s_{2}+\dots+s_{k-1}.

Remembering definitions of Section 7,
we can write that

{\bf E}\left(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\dots+\xi_{k(q-1)+1}\right)^{k-1}=\sum_{u=1}^{k-1}p^{u}\,\Upsilon_{k,u}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{k}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})}}{{|\sigma_{k}|=u,\,\|\sigma_{k}\|=k-1}}}\ {\cal N}(\sigma_{k})

=\sum_{u=1}^{k-1}{(k-1)!}\ \,u!\,{{k(q-1)+1}\choose{u}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{k}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})}}{{|\sigma_{k}|=u,\,\|\sigma_{k}\|=k-1}}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{k-1}{p^{s_{j}}\over s_{j}!\,(i!)^{s_{j}}},

[formula]

where the sum runs over
\sigma_{k}
of (2.19).
Comparing the right-hand side of (8.5) with that of (2.18), we observe
that moments (8.4) can be useful in the studies of
cumulants of
Y^{(q)}
-model.

Indeed, regarding (2.18) with
V_{i+1}=1
and using (8.5),
we can write that

\Phi_{k}^{(q,1)}={q^{k}\,(k-1)!\over k(q-1)+1}\,\ \sum_{u=1}^{k-1}\,{(k(q-1)+1)\cdots(k(q-1)-u+2)\over p^{u}}\ \sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{k}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})}}{{|\sigma_{k}|=u,\,\|\sigma_{k}\|=k-1}}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k-1}{1\over s_{i}!}\left({p\over i!}\right)^{s_{i}}

\leq{q^{k}\over p^{k-1}\big(k(q-1)+1\big)}\,{\bf E}\left(\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{k(q-1)+1}\right)^{k-1}.

Taking
p=1/(q-1)
, we obtain the following upper bound,

\Phi_{k}^{(q,1)}\leq{q^{2k-1}\over k(q-1)+1}\,{\bf E}\big(\hat{\xi}_{1}+\dots+\hat{\xi}_{k(q-1)+1}\big)^{k-1},

[formula]

where

\hat{\xi}_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{with probability \ $1/(q-1)$},\\
0,\quad&\text{with probability \ $1-1/(q-1)$}.\end{cases}

[formula]

In the limit
q\to\infty
,
the right-hand side of (8.6), when properly normalized,
is asymptotically close
to the
k
-th moment of the Poisson random variable.
This observation can be used in the studies of the upper bounds
of
\Phi_{k}^{q,1)}
and, what is more important, in the studies of
cumulants
\Xi_{k}^{(q,1)}
(2.18), for which explicit expressions of the form (2.11) leading to (8.1) are unknown.

Let us rewrite the right-hand side of (2.17) as follows

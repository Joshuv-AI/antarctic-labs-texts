Evaluating the V V -band Photometric Metallicity with Fundamental Mode RR Lyrae in the Kepler Field astropy ( Astropy Collaboration et al. 2013 ; Astropy Collaboration et al. 2018 ) , astrometry.net ( Lang et al. 2010 ) , IRAF ( Tody 1986 ; Tody 1993 ) , SExtractor ( Bertin & Arnouts 1996 ) , Matplotlib ( Hunter 2007 ) , NumPy ( Harris et al. 2020 ) , SciPy ( Virtanen et al. 2020 ) .
Chow-Choong Ngeow

Abstract
The aim of this work is to evaluate the performance of photometric metallicity [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} , determined based on V V -band light-curves photometrically transformed from the g ​ r gr -band light-curves. We tested this by using a set of homogeneous sample of fundamental mode RR Lyrae located in the Kepler field. It was found that the color-term is necessary in such photometric transformation. We demonstrated that including the color-term the determined photometric [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} are in good agreement to the spectroscopic [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} , either based on the calibrated or the transformed V V -band light-curves. We also tested the impact of Blazhko RR Lyrae in determining the photometric [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} , and found that Blazhko RR Lyrae can give consistent photometric [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} . Finally, we derived independent g ​ V ​ r gVr -band [ Fe / H ] \mathrm{[Fe/H]} - ϕ 31 \phi_{31} - P P relations (where ϕ 31 \phi_{31} and P P are Fourier parameter and pulsation period, respectively) using our light-curves. The V V -band relation is in good agreement with the most recent determination given in the literature.



I
Introduction

One of the important observed quantity, besides the pulsation periods
P
, for RR Lyrae is metallicity, commonly denoted as
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
. This is because the
V
-band absolute magnitude (
M_{V}
) for RR Lyrae is correlated with
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
. Also,
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
is one of the independent parameters in the period-luminosity-metallicity (PLZ) relations (especially in the infra-red filters). For exemplary reviews on
M_{V}
-
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
relation or PLZ relations, see
Sandage & Tammann 2006
,
Beaton et al. 2018
,
Bhardwaj 2020
, and reference therein.

The best way to measure
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
is via spectroscopic observations. Errors on the spectroscopically measured
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
fall in the range of
\sim 0.1
to
\sim 0.3
dex
(
Nemec et al. 2013
, e.g., see)
, in some cases these errors can even reach to
\sim 0.01
dex level
(
Crestani et al. 2021
, e.g., SX For in)
. However, spectroscopic observations for RR Lyrae can be expensive or time-consuming. Alternatively,
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
can be estimated based on the stellar systems (for examples, in globular clusters or dwarf galaxies) or environment (for example, in Galactic halo) at which the targeted RR Lyrae are belong to. Another way to estimate
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
is using light-curve properties of RR Lyrae, such as amplitudes
(
Alcock et al. 2000
;
Sandage 2004
;
Fabrizio et al. 2021
, for examples, see)
or Fourier parameter
\phi_{31}
.

Since the seminal paper of
Jurcsik & Kovacs 1996
, who derived the
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
-
\phi_{31}
-
P
relation in the
V
-band for ab-type (fundamental mode) RR Lyrae (hereafter RRab), a number of publications has derived a similar relation (some with additional parameters in such relation) in other filters, as well as for the c-type (first overtone) RR Lyrae. These works include:
Sandage 2004
and
Morgan et al. 2007
in the
V
-band;
Smolec 2005
and
Dékány et al. 2021
in the
I
-band;
Watkins et al. 2009
,
Sesar et al. 2010
, and
Oluseyi et al. 2012
in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS)
g
- and/or
r
-band;
Nemec et al. 2011
and
Nemec et al. 2013
in the
Kepler

K_{p}
-band;
Ngeow et al. 2016
in the
R_{PTF}
-band;
Iorio & Belokurov 2021
in the
Gaia

G
-band;
Mullen et al. 2021
in the
WISE

W1
- and
W2
-band; and
Wu et al. 2006

\sim 0.1
dex
(
Nemec et al. 2013
)
to
\sim 0.5
dex
(
Mullen et al. 2021

.

Recently, the
V
-band
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
-
\phi_{31}
-

relation was updated from two works.
Martinez-Vazquez et al. 2016
updated the
Jurcsik & Kovacs 1996

Mullen et al. 2021
re-derived the

-band
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
-
\phi_{31}
-
P
relation based on a sample of
\sim 10^{3}
field RRab with spectroscopic determined
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
. It is foreseen that the
Mullen et al. 2021

V
-band relation will be widely applied in various studies on using ab-type RR Lyrae as distance tracers. On the other hand, the SDSS-like
(u)griz
filters are becoming more popular in major synoptic sky surveys, including (but not limited to) the Pan-STARRS1
(
Chambers et al. 2016
)
, the Zwicky Transient Facility
(
Bellm et al. 2019
;
Graham et al. 2019

, the SkyMapper Southern Survey
(
Onken et al. 2019
)
, the Dark Energy Survey
(

)
, the HyperSuprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program
(

)

(

\mathrm{[Fe/H]}

-

III
Photometric Calibration

For each of our targeted RR Lyrae, we constructed a reference catalog by merging the Pan-STARRS1 Data Release 1 (DR1) photometric data
(
Chambers et al. 2016
;
Flewelling et al. 2020
)
and the
UBV
photometric catalog published in
Everett et al. 2012
. A search area with a size of
27\arcmin\times 27\arcmin
centered at each targeted RR Lyrae was adopted to query the Pan-STARRS1 DR1 photometric data. We applied a number of selection criteria to select only the non-varying stellar sources in the merged reference catalogs. Further details of the adopted selection criteria were given in the Appendix
A
. These merged reference catalogs were then cross-matched to the catalogs generated from the
SExtractor
3
3

3

https://www.astromatic.net/software/sextractor/
or
https://ascl.net/1010.064

(
Bertin & Arnouts 1996
, version 2.25.0,)
on all reduced images. The popular
MAG\_AUTO
implemented in
SExtractor
was adopted for measuring the instrumental magnitudes. Hence, each images we have a catalog containing both of the
gr
-band and
BV
-band photometry from the Pan-STARRS1 and the
UBV
catalog, respectively, for the reference stars, as well as their instrumental magnitudes.

The photometric calibration was done using the following set of equations
(
Masci et al. 2019
, for example, see)
:

\displaystyle g^{PS1}-g^{\mathrm{instr}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ZP_{g}+C_{g}(g^{PS1}-r^{PS1}),

(1)

\displaystyle r^{PS1}-r^{\mathrm{instr}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ZP_{r}+C_{r}(g^{PS1}-r^{PS1}),

(2)

\displaystyle V-V^{\mathrm{instr}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ZP_{V}+C_{V}(B^{EHK}-V^{EHK}),

(3)

m^{PS1\ \mathrm{or}\ EHK}

and

Since our SLT observations did not include the

\displaystyle(B-V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.207+1.113(g^{PS1}-r^{PS1}).

(4)

Finally, we can transform the calibrated

\displaystyle VT

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{PS1}+0.006+0.474(g^{PS1}-r^{PS1}).

(5)

In general, light-curve for a periodic variable star can be fitted with an
n
-order Fourier expansion in the following form
(
Petersen 1986
;
Deb & Singh 2009
, for example, see)
:

\displaystyle m(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{0}+\sum^{n}_{i=1}\left[a_{i}\cos(2\pi i\Phi)+b_{i}\sin(2\pi i\Phi)\right],

(8)

where
\Phi=t/P-INT(t/P)
4
4

4

INT(x)
is a function that returns the integer of
x
. Normally
t
will be subtracted with a reference epoch
t_{0}
(for example, at maximum light). For simplicity we assume
t_{0}=0
.
is the pulsational phases (between 0 and 1) after folding a time-series
t
with
P
. With trigonometric identities, equation (8) can be re-written either as a sine series or a cosine series, i.e. in the form of
m(\Phi)=m_{0}+\sum^{n}_{i=1}A_{i}\sin(2\pi i\Phi+\phi^{s}_{i})
or
m(\Phi)=m_{0}+\sum^{n}_{i=1}A_{i}\cos(2\pi i\Phi+\phi^{c}_{i})
. Following
Simon & Lee 1981
, the Fourier parameter
\phi_{31}
is either defined as
\phi^{s}_{31}=\phi^{s}_{3}-3\phi^{s}_{1}
or
\phi^{c}_{31}=\phi^{c}_{3}-3\phi^{c}_{1}
, with a conversion of
\phi^{c}_{31}=\phi^{s}_{31}-\pi

(
Ngeow et al. 2016
)
. To be consistent with
Mullen et al. 2021
, we adopted the sine series and
n=5
for obtaining the Fourier parameter
\phi^{s}_{31}
. Since the error on
\phi_{31}
is the same for either the sine series or the cosine series, we calculated the error on
\phi^{s}_{31}

VII
Discussions and Conclusions

In this work, we tested the performance of
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
V
from
Mullen et al. 2021
using a set of homogeneous ab-type RR Lyrae located in the
Kepler
field. The most important conclusion from our work is good agreement was found between
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
V
and
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
spec
. This conclusion also holds if the
V
-band light-curves are photometrically transformed from the
gr
-band light-curves, as long as the color-terms are properly taken into account when calibrating the photometry. On the other hand, the transformed
V
-band (i.e. the
VT
-band) light-curves should not be used to derive
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
V
if the color-terms are ignored or set to a constant. Modern synoptic time-series surveys often will not acquire near simultaneous
g
- and
r
-band photometry
5
5

5

Recall that RR Lyrae are short period pulsating stars, even the
g
- and
r
-band photometry were taken in the same night but separated in few hours cannot be treated as “near simultaneous”.
, hence estimating the
(g-r)
colors at certain pulsational phases can be a non-trivial task. In Appendix
B
, we provide a template color-curve such that the
(g-r)
colors can be estimated at various pulsational phases.

Our sample also contains
\sim 40\%
Blazhko RR Lyrae, providing an opportunity to test the inclusion of Blazhko RR Lyrae in deriving
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}
V
. Our test results suggested Blazhko RR Lyrae can be used even the Blazhko modulations are not removed. This implies RR Lyrae found in the synoptic time-series surveys

Hernitschek & Stassun 2022

Finally, we derive independent

-
\phi_{31}
-
P
relations, for the ab-type RR Lyrae, in the
gVr
bands based on our data. Good agreement between the coefficients can be seen in the

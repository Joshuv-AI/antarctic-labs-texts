Reciprocity of the Chern-Simons invariants of 3-manifolds


Abstract
We pose reciprocity conjectures of the Chern-Simons invariants of 3-manifolds, and discuss some supporting evidence on the conjectures. Especially, we show that the conjectures hold if Galois descent of a certain K 3 K_{3} -group is satisfied.



Takefumi Nosaka

Keywords

3-manifolds, Chern-Simons invariants, dilogarithm identities,

Subject Codes

57K31, 58J28, 11G55, 19J10, 11G55

1
Introduction

Let us review the Chern-Simons invariant. Let
M
be an oriented closed 3-manifold, and
G
be a complex simple Lie group, which is simply connected. Let
\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)
be the character variety of
M
, that is, the set of the conjugacy classes of all irreducible representations
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow G.
For
[\rho]\in\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)
, consider the associated principal
G
-bundle
P\rightarrow M
with a flat connection
A_{\rho}
, and choose a smooth section
\mathfrak{s}:M\rightarrow P
. Then, as in
[
CS
]
, with a choice of a (normalized) Killing form
P:\mathfrak{g}^{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, the Chern-Simons invariant of
\rho
is defined to be

{\rm CS}(\rho)=\int_{M}\mathfrak{s}^{*}(P(A_{\rho}\wedge[A_{\rho},A_{\rho}]))\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}},

(1)

modulo
{\mathbb{Z}}
. For example, if
G={\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, then
P(A_{\rho}\wedge[A_{\rho},A_{\rho}])
is the familiar formula
\mathrm{Tr}(A_{\rho}\wedge{\rm d}A_{\rho}+\frac{2}{3}A_{\rho}\wedge A_{\rho}\wedge A_{\rho})/8\pi^{2}
. Since the map
{\rm CS}:\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
is known to be locally constant, it can be regarded as a map from
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M))
, where
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M))
is the connected components of
\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)
and is of finite order. However, in many papers, the Chern-Simons invariant is studied in situations with a fixed
\rho
. For example, if
M
is hyperbolic and
G={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, there is an associated holonomy
\rho_{M}:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow G
such that the quadrupled invariant
4{\rm CS}(\rho_{M})
equals the complex volume of
M
, with relation to the algebraic
K
-theory, Beilinson regulator,
\eta
-invariant, etc.

Meanwhile, motivated by physical predictions from M5-branes in string theory, the sums of some topological invariants with respect to all
\rho\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M))
have recently been discussed. For example, it is conjectured that the sum of adjoint Reidemeister torsions (or, generally, some perturbative invariants of odd order) vanishes; see
[
BGZ
,
CGK
,
CQW
,
Y
]
. Analogously, in this paper, we focus on the sum of the Chern-Simons invariants and discuss the vanishing of the sum. Precisely, the main problem can be described as follows:

Problem 1.1
.

Is there an integer
c_{G}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that the vanishing of the following sum holds for any closed 3-manifold
M
?

c_{G}\sum_{[\rho]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M))}{\rm CS}(\rho)\in\mathbb{C}/{\mathbb{Z}}

(2)

If it does vanish, provide a topological interpretation of
r_{G,M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/c_{G}{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\sum_{[\rho]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M))}{\rm CS}(\rho)=\frac{r_{G,M}}{c_{G}}\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

(3)

If this problem is multi-directionally proved/improved in terms of, e.g.,
K
-theory, differential geometry, or mathematical physics, it might further our understanding of the intersections of the above sums.

In this paper, we restrict ourselves to the case
G={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
and establish a conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
G={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
and
|\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)|<\infty
, then
c_{G}=24
and the sum in (
2
) is zero.

This paper mainly examines supporting evidence of Conjecture
1.2
as follows: In Section
2
, we show (Proposition
2.2
) that Conjecture
1.2
is correct, if
\mathrm{R}_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)
satisfies a Galois descent of
K_{3}
shown in
[
Z1
]
, which implies that the sum (
2
) factors through
K_{3}^{\rm ind}({\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/24
. As a corollary, we show many examples of hyperbolic 3-manifolds satisfying the conjecture (Theorem
2.3
). We also examine relations between the conjecture and the dilogarithm function and approximately give supporting evidence of the conjecture (Corollary
2.4
). Section
3
proves the conjecture for some non-hyperbolic 3-manifolds, and Section
4
proposes another conjecture for hyperbolic 3-manifolds with a torus boundary and discusses further problems.

2
Galois action on the sum (
2
), and dilogarithm identities

In this section, we show the key propositions and give supporting evidence of Conjecture
1.2
from the viewpoint of the Galois descent above.

To begin, we should notice that the imaginary part of Problem
1.1
is almost clear:

Proposition 2.1
.

The imaginary part of the sum (
2
) is zero.

Proof.

For a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow G
, we denote by
\bar{\rho}
the conjugate representation. We can choose representatives
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m},\overline{\rho_{1}},\dots,\overline{\rho_{m}},\rho^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\rho_{n}^{\prime}
of
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M))
such that
[\rho^{\prime}_{i}]=[\overline{\rho^{\prime}_{i}}]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)).
If
A_{\rho}
is a flat connection associated with
\rho
, the conjugate
\overline{A_{\rho}}
equals
A_{\overline{\rho}}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Im}({\rm CS}(\rho))=-\mathrm{Im}({\rm CS}(\overline{\rho}))
by definition. In particular,
\mathrm{Im}({\rm CS}(\rho_{i}))=-\mathrm{Im}({\rm CS}(\overline{\rho_{i}}))
and
\mathrm{Im}({\rm CS}(\rho_{j}^{\prime}))=0
. Hence, the imaginary part of the sum (
2
) is zero as required.
∎

In conclusion, we shall focus on only the real part of the Chern-Simons invariants. For simplicity, this paper focuses on the case
G={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. From the viewpoint of Galois groups, let us give a brief proof of Conjecture
1.2
in some situations. For a subfield,
F\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, let
\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}(F)}^{\rm irr}(M)
be the set of the conjugacy classes of all irreducible representations
\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}(F).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
be a Galois extension of finite degree with an embedding
F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. Assume that the inclusion
\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}(F)}^{\rm irr}(M)\subset\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\rm irr}(M)
is bijective as a finite set, and is closed under the action of the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then, Conjecture
1.2
is true.

Proof.

This short proof is essentially due to Zickert
[
Z2
]
in a private communication. Zickert
[
Z1
]
defines “the extended Bloch group
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(F)
” such that the Chern-Simons invariant can be described as the composite map
\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}(F)}^{\rm irr}(M)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widehat{L}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Here, it is shown that
\widehat{L}
is a homomorphism, and the former map is equivariant under the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})
, and that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(F)
is isomorphic to the indecomposable part of the algebraic
K
-group
K_{3}^{\rm ind}(F)
; see Section 5 and Theorem 7.13 in
[
Z1
]
. By assumption, the sum (
2
) lies in the invariant subgroup
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(F)^{\mathrm{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})}
. Since
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(F)^{\mathrm{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{Q}})
is a Galois descent (
[
Z1
, Corollary 7.15]
) and
K_{3}^{\rm ind}({\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/24
is well-known to be true, the sum (
2
) with
c_{G}={\mathbb{Z}}/24
vanishes.
∎

Now let us examine some examples. For a knot
K
in the 3-sphere and
r=p/q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, let
M_{p/q}(K)
be the closed 3-manifold obtained by the
(p/q)
-surgery along
K
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K
be a twist knot. Suppose that
p
is even and relatively prime to
q
. Then, Conjecture
1.2
holds in the case where
M
is
M_{p/q}(K)
.

Proof.

We may check that
M_{p/q}(K)
satisfies the condition in Proposition
2.2
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
n\neq 0
such that
K
is the twist knot having
|n|
right-handed half twists (left-handed, if
n
is negative). Then, the fundamental group of
S^{3}\setminus K
has a presentation,

\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)=\langle a,b\mid w^{n}a=bw^{n}\rangle,\ \ \ \textrm{where }w=ba^{-1}b^{-1}a.

From
[
Y
]
, let us review explicitly the set
\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\rm irr}(M)
. Take the Chebyshev polynomials
S_{k}(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
defined by
S_{k+1}(z)=zS_{k}(z)-S_{k-1}(z)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
inductively, where the initial conditions are defined by
S_{0}(z)=0,S_{1}(z)=1.
As in
[
Y
]
, we write
S_{k}(z)
simply as
S_{k}
for short. From Remarks 6 and 7 in
[
Y
]
, take the Laurent polynomials of the form,

F(m,z)=S_{n}(S_{n}-S_{n-1})(m^{2}+m^{-2})-zS_{n}(S_{n}-S_{n-1})+1,

E_{\gamma}(m,z)=-(z-2)S^{2}_{n}m^{4}-(z-2)(S_{n}-S_{n-1})m^{2}+(S_{n}-S_{n-1})^{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}].

Then, as shown in §2.2 in
[
Y
]
, the set
\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\rm irr}(M)
is in a 1:1-correspondence with

\{(m,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times{\mathbb{C}}\mid F(m,z)=0,m^{p}E_{\gamma}(m,z)^{q}=1\}_{(m,z)\sim(m^{-1},z)}.

(4)

Here, for
(m,z)
in this set, the associated representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
is given by

\rho(a)=\begin{pmatrix}m&1\\
0&m^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \rho(b)=\begin{pmatrix}m&0\\
-z&m^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

Since
p
is even, the equation
F(m,z)=0
implies that
m^{2}=e\pm\sqrt{g}
and the equation
m^{p}E_{\gamma}(m,z)^{q}=1
reduces to
hm^{2}-k=0
for some rational functions
e,g,h,k\in{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
. Thus,
hm^{2}-k=h(e\pm\sqrt{g})-k=0
is equivalent to
\pm\sqrt{g}=k/h-e
: that is,
g=(k/h-e)^{2}
. Let
f(z)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[z^{\pm 1}]
be a numerator of the common denominator of
g-(k/h-e)^{2}.
Let
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
be the field extension by
f(z),\sqrt{g}
, and
F/E
be that by
(e\pm\sqrt{g})^{1/2}
. Then, by construction, the number field
F
satisfies the condition in Proposition
2.2
, as desired.
∎

As an application of Theorem
2.3
, we can point to a non-trivial identity of the dilogarithm function (Corollary
2.4
below). Let
K
be the figure eight knot, for simplicity. From
[
Mu
]
, we review the resulting computation of the Chern-Simons invariants from the viewpoint of saddle point methods. Consider the classical dilogarithm
{\rm Li}_{2}(z)=-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{{\rm Log}(1-t)}{t}{\rm d}t
. The codomain is
{\mathbb{C}}/\pi^{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Define the (potential) function
\tilde{V}
on the area
\{(z,w)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\mid zw\neq 0\}
by

\tilde{V}(z,w):=-\mathrm{Li}_{2}(zw)+\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z/w)+\frac{p}{4}({\rm Log}z)^{2}-{\rm Log}z{\rm Log}w\in{\mathbb{C}}/\pi^{2}{\mathbb{Z}},

and the following equation in
[
Mu
, (4.2)]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w=\frac{z+z^{p/2}}{z^{p/2}z+1},\\
z^{2}-(\frac{z+z^{p/2}}{z^{p/2}z+1}+1+\frac{1+z^{p/2}z}{z^{p/2}+z})z+1=0.\end{array}\right.

(5)

If
p
is even and
|p|\geq 6
, it is shown that there is a bijection,

\mathcal{B}:R_{G}^{\rm irr}(M_{p/1}(K))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1:1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\{(z,w)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\mid\eqref{sum29}\textrm{ is satisfied, and }\mathrm{Im}(z)\geq 0\}.

Let
(\zeta_{1},\omega_{1}),\dots,(\zeta_{p},\omega_{p})
be the solutions of (
5
). For
i\leq p
, choose integers
c_{i},d_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that

\frac{\partial\tilde{V}}{\partial z}(\zeta_{i},\omega_{i})+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\frac{c_{i}}{\zeta_{i}}=\frac{\partial\tilde{V}}{\partial w}(\zeta_{i},\omega_{i})+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\frac{d_{i}}{\omega_{i}}=0.

Then, as shown in
[
Mu
, Remark 5.7]
(see also
[
O
]
for mathematical estimates on the saddle point method), the Chern-Simons invariant can be computed as follows:

4\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}(\zeta_{i},\omega_{i}))=\tilde{V}(\zeta_{i},\omega_{i})+2\pi\sqrt{-1}(c_{i}\log\zeta_{i}+d_{i}\log\omega_{i}).

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
p
is even and
|p|\geq 6
. Then,

\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\tilde{V}(\zeta_{i},\omega_{i})+2\pi\sqrt{-1}(c_{i}\log\zeta_{i}+d_{i}\log\omega_{i})=0\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

In general, if Conjecture
1.2
is solved for every closed 3-manifold, then the concrete computation of Chern-Simons invariants might produce dilogarithm identities. Furthermore, if the conjugacy class
\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)
as an algebraic variety is of dimension
>0
, we might get new dilogarithm identities with parameters.

In addition, we can numerically check Conjecture
1.2
for other hyperbolic 3-manifolds. In fact, in terms of group (co)-homology and the extended Bloch group
[
Z1
,
DZ
]
, it is not so hard to establish a computer program for computing the Chern-Simons invariants of easy 3-manifolds; see Appendix
A
. By using such a program, the author numerically checked the following.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
K
be a hyperbolic knot with crossing number
<8
. Let
p
satisfy
|p|<7
and that
M_{p/1}(K)
is hyperbolic. Then, the absolute value of the sum (
2
) with
c_{G}=24
is bounded by
10^{-6}
.

3
The conjectures of some Seifert 3-manifolds and torus bundles

While the preceding section concentrates on only hyperbolic manifolds, this section solves Conjecture
1.2
for some non-hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Here, the point is that the set
\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)
might not be closed under any Galois action. Precisely,

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}),
and let
M
be either a Seifert 3-manifold with three singular fibers over
S^{2}
or a torus bundle over the circle. Then, the conjecture is true.

Proof.

If
M
is a torus bundle over the circle, then
\mathrm{CS}(\rho)\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
is known for any
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
; see, e.g.,
[
CQW
, §4.2]
. Thus, the conjecture is obviously true. We may suppose that
M
is the Seifert 3-manifold of type
M(0;(o,0);(p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2}),(p_{3},q_{3}))
, where
p_{i},q_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Choose integers
s_{j}
and
r_{j}
satisfying
p_{j}s_{j}-q_{j}r_{j}=1
.

Let us briefly review the set
\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)
and the Chern-Simons invariants from
[
CQW
, §3.1]
. For an integer
n>0
, let
[0\cdots n]
be the set
\{0,1,\dots,,n\}
, and
[0\cdots n]^{e}
(resp.
[0\cdots n]^{o}
) is the subset of even (resp. odd) integers in
[0\cdots n]
. Then, we can define a bijection
\mathcal{B}
from the set
\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)
to

\Bigl\{(\frac{j_{1}+1}{2},\frac{j_{2}+1}{2},\frac{j_{3}+1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\in(\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}})^{4}\mid j_{k}\in[0\cdots p_{k}-2]^{\epsilon_{k}}\Bigr\}

\cup\Bigl\{(\frac{j_{1}+1}{2},\frac{j_{2}+1}{2},\frac{j_{3}+1}{2},0)\in(\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}})^{4}\mid j_{k}\in[0\cdots p_{k}-2]^{o}\Bigr\}

where
\epsilon_{k}=e
if
q_{k}
is odd, and
\epsilon_{k}=o
otherwise. It follows from
[
CQW
, Proposition 2.1]
that, for
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\lambda)
in the set, the fourfold Chern-Simons invariant is computed as
4\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\lambda))=4\sum_{j=1}^{3}(r_{j}n_{j}^{2}/p_{j})\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Thus, the computation of the sum (
2
) can be done in some cases according to parity of
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
. For example, if the
p_{k}
’s are odd and
q_{k}
’s are even, the sum can be computed as

\sum_{\rho\in\mathrm{R}^{\rm irr}_{G}(M)}4\mathrm{CS}(\rho)=4\sum_{i=1}^{(p_{1}-1)/2}\sum_{j=1}^{(p_{2}-1)/2}\sum_{k=1}^{(p_{3}-1)/2}2(\frac{r_{1}i^{2}}{p_{1}}+\frac{r_{2}j^{2}}{p_{2}}+\frac{r_{3}k^{2}}{p_{3}})=\frac{4}{3}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{p_{j}-1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}r_{k}(\frac{p_{k}+1}{2}),

which lies in
\frac{1}{3}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, the sum (
2
) with
c_{G}=24
is zero. It is left to the reader to check the other cases.
∎

4
Reciprocity for 3-manifolds with boundary, and other gauge groups

Now let us discuss the situation where
M
has a torus boundary. The integral (
1
) ever converges with respect to any homomorphism
\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
; however, given a boundary-parabolic representation
\rho
, we can define the Chern-Simons invariant
\mathrm{CS}\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
; see
[
Z1
, Sections 2–5]
for the detailed definition. For instance, if
M
is hyperbolic and
\rho
is the holonomy representation, the fourfold invariant is known to be equal to the complex volume of
M
. Let
\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M)
be the set of conjugacy classes of boundary-parabolic representations
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, and
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M))
be the connected components. The Chern-Simons invariant is locally constant. Thus, similarly to the main conjecture
1.2
, it is sensible to pose the following problem:

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
M
be an oriented compact 3-manifold with a torus boundary. If
|\mathrm{R}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\rm para}(M)|<\infty
, the similar sum
24\sum_{\rho\in\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M)}{\rm CS}(\rho)
is zero.

By the same discussion as in Proposition
2.2
, we can readily show the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

Take a Galois extension
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
of finite degree. Assume a complete set of representatives,
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m},
of
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M))
such that
\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{j})\subset{\rm SL}_{2}(F)
and the set is closed under the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(F/{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then, the sum
24\sum_{[\rho]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M))}{\rm CS}(\rho)
is zero.

As a corollary, we get supporting evidence:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
K
be a 2-bridge knot.
If
M
is the exterior of
K
, Conjecture
4.1
holds.

Proof.

The knot group
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)
has a presentation of the form
\langle x,y\mid Wx=yW\rangle
, where
W
is a word in
x
and
y
. Riley
[
R
, Sect. 3]
defines a polynomial
\Phi_{K}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t^{\pm 1}]
such that any zero
\zeta
of
\Phi(K)
gives rise to a representation
\gamma_{\zeta}:\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
of the form,

\rho(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \rho(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\zeta&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Riley shows that any parabolic representation of
K
is conjugate to such a representation. Let
F/{\mathbb{Q}}
be the field extension by
\Phi_{K}
. Then, we rearch at the condition of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

We can get similar results if we replace
K
with a link of crossing number
<7
. However, Conjecture
4.1
remains mysterious if
\mathrm{R}^{\rm para}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}(M)
is not of finite order.

Finally, we conclude this paper by discussing further problems with other gauge groups. In the introduction, we supposed that
G
is simply connected and simple over
{\mathbb{C}}
. In general, through a discussion as in
[
CS
, Section 6]
, if
\pi_{1}(G)={\mathbb{Z}}
and the homotopy groups
\pi_{2}(G)=\pi_{3}(G)=0
or if
\pi_{1}(G)=\pi_{2}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0
and
\pi_{3}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}
, we can define the Chern-Simons invariant whose codomain is
{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, if
G
is a Lie group and 3-connected as in
G=\widetilde{{\rm SL}_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})
, the Chern-Simons invariant is defined as an
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued function. Thus, in the situations on
G
, it might be interesting to investigate a problem similar to Problem
1.1
even if
R_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)
is not of finite order. However, if when
M
is a rational homology 3-sphere and
G=U(1)
is taken as the simplest case, the problem becomes almost trivial. In fact,
R_{G}^{\rm irr}(M)
is identified with
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}),U(1))
, and the Chern-Simons invariant
\mathrm{CS}:\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}),U(1))\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
is known to be a quadratic form; see, e.g.,
[
MOO
]
. Thus, the sum (
2
) with
c_{U(1)}=48
must be zero.

Appendix A
Computation of the Chern-Simons invariants of 3-manifolds

Take a closed 3-manifold
M
with orientation homology 3-class
[M]\in H_{3}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
and a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. Following
[
No
]
, we establish a procedure to compute the Chern-Simons invariant. As is known (see
[
CS
, Proposition 2.8]
), the invariant is equal to the pairing
\langle\widehat{C}_{2},\rho_{*}[M]\rangle\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
for some group 3-cocycle
\widehat{C}_{2}
of
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. Thus, if
2\widehat{C}_{2}
and the pushforward
\rho_{*}[M]
are concretely described in terms of group cohomology, the doubled Chern-Simons invariant can be computed with a help of a computer.

We start by reviewing the group homology of
G={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
Y
be a set acted on by
G
, and
C_{n}(Y)
be the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by
(n+1)
-tuples of
Y
. Consider the boundary map
\partial_{n}:C_{n}(Y)\rightarrow C_{n-1}(Y)
by

\partial_{n}(y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(y_{0},\dots,y_{i-1},y_{i+1},\dots,y_{n}).

If
Y={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
with canonical action, the homology of
C_{*}(Y)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})]}{\mathbb{Z}}
is called
the group homology of
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. Since
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
acts on the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
transitively, we have a surjection
h:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
as a
G
-equivariant map, which is called a Hopf map. Consider the subcomplex of the form,

C_{n}^{h\neq}({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})):={\mathbb{Z}}\langle(y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))^{n+1}\mid h(y_{i})\neq h(y_{j})\ \ (\textrm{if }i\neq j)\rangle

Then, as is known (see
[
DZ
, §3]
), the homology of
C_{n}^{h\neq}({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})]}{\mathbb{Z}}
is isomorphic to the group homology of
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, and any 3-cycle in
C_{*}(Y)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})]}{\mathbb{Z}}
with
Y={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
is homologous to a 3-cycle in the subcomplex
C_{n}^{h\neq}({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})]}{\mathbb{Z}}
. In the paper
[
DZ
, Theorem 4.1]
, the doubled of the Chern-Simons 3-cocycle,
2\widehat{C}_{2}
, is explicitly described as a map
\frac{-1}{2\pi^{2}}\widehat{L}\circ\widehat{\lambda}:C_{3}^{h\neq}({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
in terms of the extended Bloch group and dilogarithms; see
[
DZ
, Section 3]
for details.

Next, we will describe the pushforward
\rho_{*}[M]
. We choose a genus
g
Heegaard decomposition of
M.
Then,
M
consists of a single 0-handle,
g
pieces of 1-, 2-handles, and a single 3-handle. By the van Kampen theorem, we have a group presentation
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{g}\mid r_{1},\dots,r_{g}\rangle
of
\pi_{1}(M)
. Moreover, the (reduced) cellular complex of the universal covering space of
M
can be described as

C_{*}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}}):0\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(M)]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(M)]^{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(M)]^{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(M)]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow 0,

(6)

where
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(M)]
is the group ring of
\pi_{1}(M)
. As is known, the boundary maps
\partial_{2}
(resp.
\partial_{3})
can be described in terms of Fox derivative (resp. the “taut identity”). For example, if
M
is the closed 3-manifold obtained as the
(p/1)
-surgery along a knot in
S^{3}
, i.e.,
M=M_{p/1}(K)
, we can explicitly describe
\partial_{2}
and
\partial_{3}
; see
[
No
, Corollary 3.5]
for details.

Following
[
No
, §5]
, the
\pi_{1}(M)
-equivariant chain map
\rho_{*}:C_{*}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow C_{*}(G)
is constructed as follows. Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{g}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{g}
be a canonical basis of
C_{1}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
and
C_{2}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively. Let
c_{0}
be the identity. For
A\in\pi_{1}(M)
, define
c_{1}(Aa_{i}):=(\rho(A),\rho(Ax_{i}))
. If
r_{i}
is expanded as
x_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}^{\epsilon_{n}}
for some
\epsilon_{k}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we define

c_{2}(Ab_{i})=\sum_{m:1\leq m\leq n}\epsilon_{m}(\rho(A),\rho(Ax_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m-1}}^{\epsilon_{m-1}}x_{i_{m}}^{(\epsilon_{m}-1)/2}),\rho(Ax_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m-1}}^{\epsilon_{m-1}}x_{i_{m}}^{(\epsilon_{m}+1)/2}))\in C_{2}(G).

Then, the commutativity
\partial_{1}^{\Delta}\circ c_{1}=c_{0}\circ\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}^{\Delta}\circ c_{2}=c_{1}\circ\partial_{2}
is true. Let
\mathcal{O}_{M}
be the canonical basis of
C_{3}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
. Notice that
\partial^{\Delta}_{2}\circ c_{2}\circ\partial_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{M})=c_{1}\circ\partial_{2}\circ\partial_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{M})=0
, that is,
c_{2}\circ\partial_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{M})
is a 2-cycle. If we expand
c_{2}\circ\partial_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{M})
as
\sum n_{i}(g_{0}^{i},g_{1}^{i},g_{2}^{i})
for some
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}},g_{j}^{i}\in G
, then with a choice of
v_{0}\in G
such that
v_{0}
is different from all
g_{k}^{\ell}
,
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\prime}:=-\sum n_{i}(v_{0},g_{0}^{i},g_{1}^{i},g_{2}^{i})
satisfies
\partial_{3}^{\Delta}(\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\prime})=c_{2}\circ\partial_{3}(\mathcal{O}_{M})
. Therefore, the correspondence
\mathcal{O}_{M}\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\prime}
gives rise to a chain map
c_{3}:C_{*}(\widetilde{M})\rightarrow C_{*}(G)
, as desired. Here,
c_{3}
up to homotopy is independent of the choice of
v_{0}.
In conclusion, the class
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\prime}\otimes 1
in
C_{3}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}
is a representative of the pushforward
\rho_{*}[M]
.

In our experience, if
M
is hyperbolic, fortunately, the 3-class
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\prime}
often lies in the subcomplex
C_{n}^{h\neq}({\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
. Thus, it is not so hard to compute numerically the pairing
\langle 2\widehat{C}_{2},\rho_{*}[M]\rangle\in{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. As stated in Proposition
2.5
, for instance, if
M=M_{p/1}(K)
and
|p|<8
and
\mathrm{cr}(K)<8
, we can easily compute the pairings numerically.

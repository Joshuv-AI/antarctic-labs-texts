Norio Iwase

Abstract
. We are trying to look over the Lusternik-Schnirelmann theory (L-S theory, for short) and the Topological Complexity (TC, for short) as a natural extension of the L-S theory. In particular, we focus on the impact of the ideas originated from E. Fadell and S. Husseini on both theories. More precisely, we see how their ideas on a category weight and a relative category drive the L-S theory and the TC.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 55M30; Secondary 18M75, 55P05, 55P10, 55P45, 55P48, 55Q25, 55R35, 55R70, 55S10

In this article, we work in the category of CW-complexes and maps between them, but we often restrict ourselves into the full subcategory of pointed CW-complexes.
The pointed and unpointed theories are very close in the usual homotopy theory, but we find that they are far apart, if we discuss the higher associativity of an H-space fibrewise, or parametrized.
Before starting the main part, we discuss about the higher associativity of an H-space, which is closely related to both (fibrewise) L-S theory and TC.

1.
Higher Associativity

In 1941, H. Hopf introduced, in
[
Hop41
]
, a notion of an H-space, as a space with a multiplication with a homotopy unit.
The idea attracted many authors such as A. Borel, I. James, J. F. Adams, H. Toda, W. Browder, J. Hubbuck, M. Sugawara, A. Zabrodsky, R. Kane, J. Lin, M. Mimura and many others including the author. But an H-space such as the unit sphere of Octanions
S^{7}
fails to satisfy (higher) homotopy associativity.

In 1957, M. Sugawara gave a criteria for a space to be an H-space or a homotopy associative H-space in
[
Sug57b
,
Sug57a
]
.
Refining extensively the idea employed by M. Sugawara, J. D. Stasheff introduced a homotopy-theoretical version of the Milnor filtration
[
Mil56a
,
Mil56b
]
as
A_{\infty}
-forms and
A_{\infty}
-structures in
[
Sta63
]
and a few years later in
[
Sta70
]
in a more sophisticated form, while the definitions in
[
Sta63
]
and in
[
Sta70
]
are slightly different:
In
[
Sta63
]
, an
A_{\infty}
-form requires a strict unit with higher coherency conditions, while in
[
Sta70
]
it requires only a homotopy unit as an H-space.
Because the induction step of the proof in
[
Sta70
]
claiming the equality of the two definitions stops at the stage
3
, it puzzled us for a long time, and finally is resolved in
[
Iwa12
]
, and so the two definitions are not the same but
equivalent, up to homotopy
.

One goal of
A_{\infty}
theory is described as Theorem
2.5
which is due to J. D. Stasheff.

See Appendix for explanations on Milnor filtrations of the classifying space of a topological group, definitions of
A_{\infty}
-forms and
A_{\infty}
-structures for both spaces and maps, and Ganea’s fibre-cofibre constructions for connected spaces.

2.
L-S theory

Let
M
be a differentiable closed manifold and
f
a smooth function on
M
.
Then, one basic question arises: how many critical points does
f
have?
Let us denote by
\operatorname{Crit}(f)
the number of critical points of a smooth function
f
on
M
, and by
\operatorname{Crit}(M)
the minimum among all the number
\operatorname{Crit}(f)
where
f
runs over all smooth functions on
M
.

In 1934, L. Lusternik and L. Schnirelmann, who worked on variation theory, gave a lower bound for
\operatorname{Crit}(M)
as their ‘category’ number
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(M)\geq 1
, in other words,
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(M)\leq\operatorname{Crit}(M)
in
[
LS34
]
.
However, among people working on L-S theory, it became popular to define
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
one less than the original:
the Lusternik-Schnirelmann (L-S, for short) category of a connected pointed space
X
, denoted by
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
, is the smallest integer
n\!\geq\!0
such that
X
is covered by
n{+}1
categorical open subsets, where a subset
U\subset X
is said to be categorical if the inclusion map
U\hookrightarrow X
is homotopic to the constant map at the base point.
If no such integer exists, we write
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\infty
.

Theorem 2.1

(L. Lusternik and L. Schnirelmann)
.

Let
M
be a manifold.
Then we have

\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(M)\leq\operatorname{Crit}(M)\!-\!1\leq\dim{M}.

In 1939-41, R. H. Fox introduced, in
[
Fox39
,
Fox41
]
, a series of new ideas related to L-S theory.
Firstly, for a manifold
M
, a ball category is denoted by
\operatorname{Ball}(M)
as the smallest integer
n\!\geq\!0
such that
M
is covered by
n{+}1
open balls in
M
.
Secondly, for a connected pointed space
X
, his version of strong category or a geometric category is denoted by
\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)
, as the smallest integer
n\!\geq\!0
such that
X
is covered by
n{+}1
contractible open subsets, which is later reformulated by T. Ganea as his homotopy-invariant version of strong category.
We remark that
\operatorname{Ball}(M)
as well as
\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)
are not homotopy invariant (see
[
CLOT03
]
).
Thirdly, for a connected pointed space
X
, a finite sequence
\{A_{0},\ldots,A_{k}\}
of closed subsets of
X
a categorical sequence of length
k
, if
\{\ast\}\subset A_{0}\subset A_{1}\subset\cdots\subset A_{k-1}\subset A_{k}{=}X
such that
\{A_{0},A_{1}\!\smallsetminus\!A_{0},\ldots,A_{k}\!\smallsetminus\!A_{k-1}\}
are all categorical in
X
.
Then
\operatorname{catlen}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
is the minimal length of all such sequences, which can also be reformulated (see
[
Iwa09
]
) using Fadell-Husseini relative L-S category which will be introduced later.

Theorem 2.2

(R. H. Fox)
.

Let
M
be a manifold, and
X
a space.
Then we have

(1)

\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(M)\leq\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(M)\leq\operatorname{Ball}(M)\leq\operatorname{Crit}(M){-}1\leq\dim{M}
, and

(2)

\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\operatorname{catlen}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)\leq\dim{X}
.

To determine L-S category, we need some computable lower bound.
Let
h
be a multiplicative cohomology theory.
Classically, the cup-length of
X
w.r.t.
h
, denoted by
\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;h)
, is the supremum of
k\!\geq\!0
such that there exists a non-zero
k
-fold product in the reduced theory
\tilde{h}^{\ast}(X)
.
When
h
is the ordinary cohomology with coefficients in
R
a ring with unit, then we denote
\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;R)=\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;h)
.
The following theorem is well known, while
\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;h)
is much less than
\dim{X}
in a number of examples.

Theorem 2.3
.

\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;h)\leq\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)\leq\dim{X}
.

In 1960, I. Berstein and P. J. Hilton
[
BH60
]
gave a criterion for
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(C_{f})=2
where
C_{f}
is the mapping cone of a map
f:X\to Y
, in terms of their version of a Hopf invariant
H_{1}(f)\in[\Sigma X,\Omega\Sigma Y{\ast}\Omega\Sigma Y]
for a map
f:\Sigma X\to\Sigma Y
, where
A{*}B
denotes the join of spaces
A
and
B
.
In addition, its higher version

is used to disprove the Ganea conjecture on L-S category (see
[
Iwa98
,
Iwa02
]
).
They also introduced a new lower bound called weak category, denoted by
\operatorname{{\it w}cat}(X)
, which is the supremum of
k\!\geq\!0
such that the reduced iterated diagonal
\overline{\Delta}^{k}:X\to\wedge^{k}X
is non-trivial.
Similarly, we denote by
\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
the supremum of
k\!\geq\!0
such that the reduced iterated diagonal
\overline{\Delta}^{k}:X\to\wedge^{k}X
is stably non-trivial.
For example, M. Mimura and the author showed in
[
IM04
]
that
\overline{\Delta}^{n+2}:\mathrm{Sp}(n)\to\wedge^{n+2}\mathrm{Sp}(n)
is non-trivial and
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{Sp}(n))\!\geq\!n{+}2
for

by showing that
\overline{\Delta}^{4}

:

\mathrm{Sp}(3)\overset{q}{\twoheadrightarrow}S^{18}\xrightarrow{\nu^{2}}S^{12}\overset{j}{\hookrightarrow}\wedge^{4}\mathrm{Sp}(3)
is non-trivial, using Toda secondary composition
[
Tod62
]
which is obtained independently by L. Fernández-Suárez, A. Gómez-Tato, J. Strom and D. Tanré in
[
FSGTST04
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;h)\leq\operatorname{cup}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq\operatorname{{\it w}cat}(X)\leq\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
.

In 1962, I. Berstein and T. Ganea defined in
[
BG62
]
a L-S category of a map.
For a map
f
from a space
K
to a connected pointed space
X
, they defined
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(f)
as the smallest integer
n\!\geq\!0
such that
K
is covered by
n{+}1
open subsets on which the restriction of
f
is homotopic to the constant map at the base point.
If no such integer exists, we write
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(f)=\infty
.
For an inclusion
i:K\hookrightarrow X
, let us denote
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;K)=\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(i)
.

In 1963, J. D. Stasheff introduced a notion of an
A_{m}
-space using
A_{m}
-forms and
A_{m}
-structures to amount the higher associativity for an H-space in
[
Sta63
]
.
The existence of an

-structure implies the ‘standard’

-structure which enjoys the following three properties (see
[
Sta63
]
).
The advantage to consider Stasheff’s construction is that the cell structure of his ‘standard’
A_{m}
-structure is quite understandable.

(Z)

\displaystyle P^{0}(G)=\{\ast\},

(F’)

\displaystyle G\hookrightarrow E^{n}(G)\twoheadrightarrow P^{n-1}(G)\ \ \text{is a fibre sequence, $m\!\geq\!n\!\geq\!1$,}

(C)

\displaystyle E^{n}(G)\twoheadrightarrow P^{n-1}(G)\hookrightarrow P^{n}(G)\ \ \text{is a cofibre sequence, $m\!\geq\!n\!\geq\!1$.}

When
m\!=\!\infty
, they enjoys the following three properties (see
[
Sta63
,
Sta70
]
, for details).

(Z)

\displaystyle P^{0}(G)=\{\ast\},

(F)

\displaystyle E^{n}(G)\twoheadrightarrow P^{n-1}(G)\hookrightarrow P^{\infty}(G)\ \ \text{is a fibre sequence up to homotopy, $n\!\geq\!1$,}

(C)

\displaystyle E^{n}(G)\twoheadrightarrow P^{n-1}(G)\hookrightarrow P^{n}(G)\ \ \text{is a cofibre sequence, $n\!\geq\!1$.}

Using
A_{\infty}
-maps between
A_{\infty}
-spaces (see §
B
), J. D. Stasheff showed in
[
Sta63
,
Sta70
]
the following fundamental result saying that his

gives the inverse functor to
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.5

(J. D. Stasheff)
.

For a connected CW complex
X
,
\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(X)
is an
A_{\infty}
-space and
P^{\infty}\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(X)\simeq X
.
For an
A_{\infty}
-space
G
, which has the homotopy type of a connected CW complex,
\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(P^{\infty}G)
is
A_{\infty}
-homotopy equivalent to
G
.

From now on,
\{(E^{n+1},P^{n},p_{n+1},i_{n},e_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
consisting of spaces
E^{n+1}
and
P^{n}
, and maps
p_{n+1}:E^{n+1}\to P^{n}
,
i_{n}:P^{n}\to P^{n+1}
and
e_{n}:P^{n}\to X
for all
n\!\geq\!0
is called a fibre-cofibre construction for a pointed space
X
, if it satisfies the following three conditions.

(Z)

\displaystyle P^{0}=\{\ast\},

(F)

\displaystyle E^{n}\xrightarrow{p_{n}}P^{n-1}\xrightarrow{e_{n\!-\!1}}X\ \ \text{is a fibre sequence up to homotopy for $n\geq 1$,}

(C)

\displaystyle E^{n}\xrightarrow{p_{n}}P^{n-1}\xrightarrow{i_{n\!-\!1}}P^{n}\ \ \text{is a cofibre sequence up to homotopy for $n\geq 1$.}

In 1967, T. Ganea introduced in
[
Gan67
]
a strong category
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
as a homotopy invariant version of a Fox strong category or a geometric category
\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)
.
More precisely,
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
is the minimum among all the number
\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(Y)\!\geq\!0
where
Y
runs over all spaces with the same homotopy type of
X
.
T. Ganea also showed that
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
is characterized as follows:
for a connected pointed space
X
,
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
is
0
if
X
is contractible and, otherwise, is equal to the smallest integer
m
such that there is a series of cofibre sequences

\{K_{i}\to F_{i-1}\hookrightarrow F_{i}\;|\;1\!\leq\!i\!\leq\!m\}
with
F_{0}=\{\ast\}
and
F_{m}\simeq X
.
Such cofibre sequence is called a cone-decomposition of
X
, and the smallest integer
m
is called the cone-length of
X
denoted here by
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
, which is a similar idea to Fox’s categorical length (see Fox
[
Fox39
,
Fox41
]
) with category replaced by Ganea’s strong category.

Theorem 2.6

(T. Ganea)
.

\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\!-\!1\!\leq\!\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\!\leq\!\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\!=\!\operatorname{Cl}(X)\!\leq\!\operatorname{{\it g}Cat}(X)
.

We know that
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq 1
if and only if
X
is a co-H-space, while
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq 1
if and only if
X
is desuspendable, that is,
X=\operatorname{\Sigma}\hskip 1.00006pt(Y)
for some
Y
.
Since there are lots of co-H-spaces which are not desuspendable,
\operatorname{cat}
and
\operatorname{Cat}
are actually different, in general.
For instance, a series of counter examples to the Ganea conjecture for a co-H-spaces in
[
Iwa01
]
gives such examples.
This reminds us a result on discrete groups by S. Eilenberg and T. Ganea
[
EG57
]
.

Theorem 2.7

(S. Eilenberg and T. Ganea)
.

Let
G
be a discrete group. Then we have

\operatorname{cd}{G}\!\leq\!\operatorname{gd}{G}
, where the equality holds except for the case when
(\operatorname{gd}{G},\operatorname{cd}{G})=(3,2)
.

Because there is a space
X
with
\operatorname{Cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\!+\!1
, there could be a group
G
with
\operatorname{gd}{G}=\operatorname{cd}{G}\!+\!1=3
.
But at this moment, the author doesn’t have any idea on it.

In 1967, T. Ganea gave a fibre-cofibre construction
\{(E_{n},G_{n},p_{n},i_{n-1},e_{n})\}
for a space

[

(T. Ganea)
.

\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq m
if and only if

To examine the existence of the right homotopy inverse of

In 1966-1976, an interesting result was obtained independently by A. S. Švarc
[
Š66
]
and by I. Berstein
[
Ber76
]
as Theorem
2.9
below in the case when

.
The result was based on the study of I. Berstein

In 2009, A. Dranishnikov and Y. Rudyak
[

For a cell complex
X
, we denote the universal covering space of
X
by
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
and
\widetilde{X}_{0}=p^{-1}(\ast)\approx\pi=\pi_{1}(X)
.
Then the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi
acts on
C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0})
, and so we have a cochain group
C^{*}(X,\ast;M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}\pi}(C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0}),M)
of

-homomorphisms for
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi
-module

.
Let
I(\pi)
be the kernel of the augmentation
\varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}\pi\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then we have the ‘fundamental class’
\bar{\mathfrak{b}}

\in

H^{1}(X,\ast;I(\pi))\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_{1}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0}),I(\pi))\cong\operatorname{Hom}(I(\pi),I(\pi))
corresponding to the identity homomorphism.
The element

is often called the Berstein-Švarc class, where

is the canonical inclusion.

Theorem 2.9

(I. Berstein, A. S. Švarc, A. Dranishnikov-Y. Rudyak)
.

For a space satisfying
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\dim{X}=n\geq 2
,
\mathfrak{b}^{n}\not=0
in
H^{n}(X,I(\pi)^{n})
, where
I(\pi)^{n}=I(\pi)\otimes\cdots\otimes I(\pi)
.

While the definition of L-S category is fairly simple, it is not quite easy to determine the number of a given space.
As for an upper bound, strong category or cone-length gives a candidate for
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
.
As for a lower bound, the cup-length gives a candidate for
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
.
If the two candidates are fortunately the same, we can conclude that is the one we want.
But in general, the two candidates are far apart from each other.

In 1975-76, W. Singhof
[
Sin75
,
Sin76
]
determined the L-S category of a series of Lie groups by using cup-length arguments together with an open ball covering as
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{U}(n))=n
and
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{SU}(n))=n{-}1
.
But unfortunately, this method can not be applied to other types of compact connected Lie groups, because we do not have general machinery for obtaining an open ball covering whose cardinality is one more than the cup-length.

In 1992, E. Fadell and S. Husseini
[

introduced a new lower bound for

.
That means the invariant is topological but not homotopical.

\operatorname{cwgt}(u)=\operatorname{Min}\{m\geq 0\mid\forall\,A\underset{\text{closed}}{\subset}X\ u|_{A}=0\ \text{if}\ \operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;A)<m\},\quad u\in\tilde{h}^{*}(X),

where
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X;A)=\operatorname{cat^{BG}}\hskip 0.20004pt(X,A)
denotes the L-S category of

in

, which is the same as
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(i)
the L-S category of the inclusion map

.

The homotopy invariant version of ‘category weight’ is introduced independently by J. Strom and Y. Rudyak, and is applied to determine L-S category of various spaces (see Y. Rudyak and J. Oprea
[

]
, for example) and to solve Arnold conjecture (see Y. Rudyak
[

]
and/or K. Fukaya and K. Ono

]

Defninition 2.11

(Y. Rudyak

\operatorname{wgt}\hskip 0.59998pt(u;h)=\operatorname{Min}\{m\geq 0\mid f^{*}(u)\!=\!0\ \text{if}\ \operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(f)\!<\!m\}

In 1998, L-S category is described in terms of an
A_{\infty}
-structure of the loop space of a well-pointed space or a space with non-degenerate base point, using the Whitehead definition of L-S category, which is performed in
[
Iwa98
]
.
The Whitehead definition says
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq m
if and only if the
m{+}1
-fold diagonal
\Delta^{m+1}:X\to\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}X
is compressible into the fat wedge
\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}X
, where
\overset{k+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}X
,

is defined by induction on

as follows:

\displaystyle(\overset{1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}X,\overset{1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}X)=(X,\ast)\quad\text{and}\quad(\overset{k+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}X,\overset{k+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}X)=(\overset{k}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}X\times X,\overset{k}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}X\times X\cup\overset{k}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}X\times\ast).

The homotopy pull-back of

3.
Sectional Category and Topological Complexity

We all know that the L-S category is representing a kind of complexity of a given space.
From the view point of Robot Motion Planning, we may think it is actually the same as the complexity for a Robot Motion Planning with a fixed Robot station.

Let us first recall the definition of a genus (see A. S. Švarc
[
Š61
,
Š62
]
)
or a sectional category (see I. M. James
[
Jam78
]
): In 1959-62, A. S. Švarc introduced a homotopy invariant for a fibration
\varpi:P\to W
.
Following I. M. James
[
Jam78
]
, we denote by
\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(\varpi)
the smallest integer
n\!\geq\!1
such that
W
is covered by
n
sectional open subsets; that is, open subsets over which there is a section of
\varpi
.
For a map
f:Y\to W
, the sectional category of
f
is defined as
\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(f)=\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(f^{*}\varpi)
the sectional category of
f^{*}\varpi:f^{*}P\to Y
the fibration induced from
\varpi:P\to W
by
f
.

For two fibrewise spaces
p:E\to X
and
q:F\to X
over a space
X
, we can topologise
E\,\ast_{B}\,F=\underset{x\in X}{\bigcup}\,(E_{x}\,\ast\,F_{x})
as a subspace of
E\,\ast\,F
(see I. M. James
[
Jam78
]
) with a natural projection
E\,\ast_{B}\,F\to X
.
Then by taking an iterated fibrewise join construction, we obtain
J^{m}(p):J^{m}(E)=E\,\ast_{B}\,\cdots\,\ast_{B}\,E\to X
(
m
-fold fibrewise join)

Theorem 3.1

(A. S. Švarc)
.

\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(\varpi)\leq m
if and only if the
m
-fold fibrewise join
J^{m}(\varpi):J^{m}(P)\to W
of
\varpi:P\to W
admits a section.

In 2003, M. Farber
[
Far03
]
introduced an idea of Topological Complexity for Robot Motion Planning (without a Robot Station).
Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
be the space of all paths on
X
and
\pi:\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\to X\!\times\!X
the projection designating initial and terminal points, which is often called a Serre path fibration.
Then the original Topological Complexity (TC, for short) of a connected space
X
, denoted by
\operatorname{TC}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
, is defined to be
\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(\pi)
.

Just as in L-S category, people working on TC (including M. Farber) have come to define TC as one less than the original definition as the minimum number of open sets with sections.
To avoid confusion, we denote
\operatorname{genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(\varpi)=\operatorname{Genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(\varpi){-}1
and
\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\operatorname{TC}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)-1
for the new definition.
The following result by M. Farber gave a strong impact not only to algebraic topologists but also to differential topologists.

Theorem 3.2

(M. Farber
[
Far03
]
)
.

For all
n\!\geq\!1
, we have

\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(\operatorname{{{\mathbb{R}}}P}^{n})=\operatorname{Imm}(\operatorname{{{\mathbb{R}}}P}^{n})-\delta_{n},\quad\delta_{n}=\left\{\vphantom{\bigg(}\!\!\!\!\right.\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&n=1,3,7\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}

where
\operatorname{Imm}(M)
is the Euclidean immersion dimension of a closed manifold
M
.

This motivated many people to study TC more seriously.
Also the theoretical similarity with L-S theory drew attention of people working on L-S theory including the author.

In 2008, M. Farber and M. Grant
[
FG08
]
introduced a TC version of ‘category weight’ called TC-weight, and show its strength determining TC.

Defninition 3.3

(M. Farber and M. Grant)
.

Let
h
be a cohomology theory and
Z^{*}_{\varpi}=\ker\{\Delta^{*}:h^{*}(X\!\times\!X)\to h^{*}(X)\}
the zero-divisors ideal.

(1)

\operatorname{wgt}_{\varpi}(u;h)=\operatorname{Min}\{m\!\geq\!0\mid f^{*}(u)\!=\!0\ \text{if}\ \operatorname{genus}\hskip 0.59998pt(f)\!<\!m\}
,
u\!\in\!Z^{*}_{\varpi}
,

(2)

\operatorname{wgt}_{\varpi}(X;h)=\operatorname{Max}\{\operatorname{wgt}_{\varpi}(u;h)\mid 0\!\not=\!u\!\in\!Z^{*}_{\varpi}\}
.

The original motivation of Robot Motion Planning leads us to consider various kinds of configuration spaces where robots are moving around. We can also consider a robot of a special shape which forces us to consider some advanced featured TCs including sequential or higher versions of TC.
More recently, leading researchers of TC are shifting to a more applied-side, introducing a parametrized TC (which takes account of obstacles that robots must avoid) and etc.

As for the original TC, there looks still a lot of things to learn from E. Fadell and S. Husseini.
In particular, we want to know about a relative TC and TC module weight.

4.
Fibrewise L-S theory

In 2010, M. Sakai and the author learned a fibrewise L-S theory from I. M. James and J. R. Morris
[
JM91
]
and M. C. Crabb and I. M. James
[
CJ98
]
, and found that a fibrewise L-S theory and TC are almost the same.
The difference lies in the motion of a base point: James’s fibrewise L-S category
\operatorname{cat}^{B}_{B}
corresponds to a ‘pointed’ version of TC which we named ‘monoidal’ TC denoted by
\operatorname{tc^{M}}
, requiring that a robot does not move in the case when the initial and terminal states are the same. The original TC corresponds to an ‘unpointed’ version of a fibrewise L-S category which we named as fibrewise unpointed L-S category
[
IS10
,
IS12
]
.
In other words, the original TC does allow a robot to move around, even if the given initial and terminal states of the robot are the same.

As is seen in the original paper by E. Fadell and S. Husseini
[
FH92
]
, there is a strong connection between the category weight and the bar resolution of a group.
In fact,
[
Iwa02
,
Iwa07
]
showed the connection can be applied to every topological space.
Going into fibrewise, we see the same idea can be applied to every fibrewise pointed space.

A fibrewise space is a surjection
p:E\to X
and is denoted by
E=(E,p,X)
, where
E
,
X
,
p
are called the total space, the base space, and the projection, respectively.
Also a fibrewise pointed space is a pair of a fibrewise space
E=(E,p,X)
and a cross-section
s
of
p
, which is often denoted by
(E,s)=(E,p,X,s)
.
For a given fibrewise pointed space
(E,s)
, its fibrewise loop space
\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E,s)
can be constructed naturally in
[
JM91
]
, which has a natural fibrewise
A_{\infty}
-form by
[
Sak10
]
.
A section
s:X\hookrightarrow E
for
E=(E,p,X)
is said to be a non-degenerate fibrewise base point, if it is a closed cofibration.
A fibrewise space with a non-degenerate fibrewise base point is called a fibrewise well-pointed space.

For example, the product space
E=X\!\times\!Y
is a fibrewise space
E=(E,\operatorname{pr}_{1},X)
over
X
. Further, for any given map
f:X\to Y
, we obtain a cross-section
s_{f}:X\to E
to
\operatorname{pr}_{1}
by
s_{f}=(\operatorname{id}\!\times\!\,f)\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}\Delta:X\to X\!\times\!Y
.
On the one hand, if
Y
has a non-degenerate base point
\ast
, then
s_{*}
is a non-degenerate fibrewise base point for
E
where
*:X\to\{\ast\}\subset Y
denotes the trivial map.
In this case, the associated fibrewise loop space of
E
is nothing but the product space
X\!\times\!\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(Y)
.
On the other hand, if
(Y,X)
is a CW-pair with closed cofibration
i:X\hookrightarrow Y
, then
(X\!\times\!Y,\Delta{X})
is an NDR-pair and
s_{i}=(\operatorname{id}\!\times\!\,i)\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}\Delta:X\to X\!\times\!Y
is a non-degenerate fibrewise base point for
E
.
In this case, the associated fibrewise loop space of
E
is nothing but the restriction to
X
of the free loop space
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}\hskip 1.00006pt(Y)
over
Y
.

The Whitehead definition of an L-S category for a well-pointed space can be extended to that for a fibrewise well-pointed space.
The fibrewise fat wedge
\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}_{\!\!\!\!B}E
of a fibrewise pointed space
E=(E,s)
over
X
is given by induction on
k\!\geq\!1
by
(\overset{1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{B}E,\overset{1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}_{\!B}E)

=

(E,s(X))
and

\displaystyle(\overset{k+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{\!\!B}E,\overset{k+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}_{\!\!\!B}E)=(\overset{k}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{B}E\times_{B}E,\overset{k}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}_{\!B}E\times_{B}E\cup\overset{k}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{B}E\times_{B}s(X)).

Then the Whitehead definition of a fibrewise pointed L-S category
\operatorname{cat}^{B}_{B}(E,s)
for a fibrewise well-pointed space
E=(E,s)
is given as follows:

Defninition 4.1
.

\operatorname{cat}^{B}_{B}(E,s)\leq m
if the diagonal map
\Delta^{m+1}:E\to\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{\!\!\!B}E
is compressible into the fibrewise fat wedge by a fibrewise pointed homotopy.

The fibrewise
A_{\infty}
-structure associated to the fibrewise loop space is fibrewise homotopy equivalent to the fibrewise version of the Ganea construction
[
Gan67
]
as in
[
Sak10
]
, using the fibrewise version of fibre-cofibre lemma
[
Mat76
,
Iwa98
]
.
More precisely, for a fibrewise well-pointed space
E=(E,s)
, the associated fibrewise projective
m
-space
P^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)
together with a fibrewise fibration
E_{B}^{m+1}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\hookrightarrow P_{B}^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\overset{e^{E}_{m}}{\longrightarrow}E
and a fibrewise cofibration
E_{B}^{m+1}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\rightarrow P_{B}^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\hookrightarrow P_{B}^{m+1}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)
is nothing but the
m
-th fibrewise Ganea construction, up to homotopy, and we obtain the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

\operatorname{cat}^{B}_{B}(E,s)\leq m
if and only if
e^{E}_{m}:P_{B}^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\to E=(E,s)
has a right homotopy inverse in the category of fibrewise pointed spaces and maps.

We can define a fibrewise version of category and module weights as well, while they are more difficult to calculate than the usual category and module weights.
For a space

, we denote by

the fibrewise space over

IS12

Theorem 4.3

(M. Sakai and I)

\operatorname{tc^{M}}(X)=\operatorname{cat}^{B}_{B}(X\!\times\!X,\Delta)

Theorem 4.4

\operatorname{tc^{M}}(X)=\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)

5.
Fibrewise unpointed L-S theory

Let us see TC from the fibrewise view point.
If there is a section of
\pi:\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\to X\!\times\!X
on a subset
F\subset X\!\times\!X
, which gives a motion from the first point to the second point.
By reversing the orientation, we may think that a section gives a motion from the second point to the first point.
Thus by taking adjoint, we obtain a compression of
F
into the diagonal
\Delta{X}\subset X\!\times\!X
.
Now we assume that the inclusion
F\hookrightarrow X\!\times\!X
is a closed cofibration, and we obtain a deformation
h:X\!\times\!X\!\times\!I\to X\!\times\!X
of the identity whose restriction to
F\!\times\!I
coincides with the above compression of
F
into
\Delta{X}\subset X\!\times\!X
.
In particular,
h
gives a motion of an element
(x,x)\in\Delta{X}
to somewhere in
\Delta{X}
.

Let
E=(E,s)
be a fibrewise pointed space over
X
.
If
E
is covered by
m{+}1
closed cofibrations each of which is equipped with a fibrewise compression into
s(X)
, then we obtain a fibrewise compression
H:E\times\!I\to\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{\!\!\!B}E
of the fibrewise diagonal
\Delta^{m+1}
into the fibrewise fat wedge
\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\text{T}$}}_{\!\!\!\!B}E
, where
H
is a fibrewise unpointed homotopy.

Defninition 5.1
.

\operatorname{cat}_{B}(E,s)\leq m
if the fibrewise diagonal map
\Delta^{m+1}:E=(E,s)\to\overset{m+1}{\text{\large$\textstyle\Pi$}}_{\!\!\!B}E
is fibrewise compressible into the fibrewise fat wedge by a fibrewise unpointed homotopy.

Then, M. Sakai and the author obtained the following in
[
IS10
,
IS12
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(M. Sakai and I.)
.

\operatorname{cat}_{B}(E,s)\leq m
if and only if
e^{E}_{m}:P_{B}^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(E)\to E=(E,s)
has a right homotopy inverse in the category of fibrewise unpointed spaces and maps.

For a space
X
, we denote by
X\!\times\!X=(X\!\times\!X,\operatorname{pr}_{1},X)
the fibrewise space over
X
, and
(X\!\times\!X,\Delta)
is a fibrewise pointed space.
Then we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.3

(M. Sakai and I.)
.

\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)=\operatorname{cat}_{B}(X\!\times\!X,\Delta)
.

It might be possible for us to extend Theorem
5.3
to an equality of the
r
-th higher TC of a space
X
and the fibrewise L-S category of a fibrewise space
(X^{r},\operatorname{pr}_{1},X)
with the
r
-fold diagonal
\Delta_{r}:X\to X^{r}
as its fibrewise base point.

Similarly to Theorem
4.2
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq m
if the natural map
e^{X\!\times\!X}_{m}:P_{B}^{m}\operatorname{\Omega}^{B}_{B}(X\!\times\!X)\to X\!\times\!X=(X\!\times\!X,\Delta)
has a right homotopy inverse in the category of fibrewise unpointed spaces and maps.

If we look at the fibrewise resolution of
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}}\hskip 1.00006pt(BG)
for the fibrewise L-S category, the cell-structure could be less complicated for a group

than the fibrewise join for the sectional category.
For instance, for a Klein bottle

[

But the author has come to believe now that the fibrewise join of a projection is homotopy equivalent to the fibrewise projective space in some sense, and the fibrewise L-S theory and obstruction theory due to A. We will explain briefly the construction introduced by J. Milnor

For a family of spaces

\displaystyle(t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+1}\ldots,t_{n};x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\sim(t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+1}\ldots,t_{n};x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x^{\prime}_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}),\quad 0\!\leq\!i\!\leq\!n.

So, we may assume that

When

T. Ganea introduced his (fibre-cofibre) construction to show the characterization of the L-S category
[
Gan67
, Proposition 2.2]
.
Ganea began with (Z) below and inductively constructed a series of cofibrations (C) and fibrations (F) concretely.

(Z)

\displaystyle E_{0}(X)=\{\ast\}\overset{p_{0}}{\longrightarrow}X,

(F)

F_{n}(X)
is the mapping fibre of
p_{n}:E_{n}(X)\to X
,

(C)

E_{n+1}(X)
is the mapping cone of
i_{n}:F_{n}(X)\hookrightarrow E_{n}(X)
.

Here,
p_{n+1}
is the extension of
p_{n}:E_{n}(X)\to X
by annihilating whole
C(F_{n}(X))
, which is well-defined since
p_{n}(F_{n}(X))=\{\ast\}
.
This construction is, however, depending on the choice of
p_{n+1}
, since we know there are many choices for
p_{n+1}
extending
p_{n}
. In any case, Exercise
2.13
tells us that such choices does not affect the homotopy type of fibre-cofibre constructions. But this is not so obvious as we have seen in the fact that
[
CLOT03
, Exercise 2.16]
can not be verified as is.

So, it would be possible to say that the space
E_{n}(X)
, which is often referred as
G_{n}(X)
the Ganea space, is homotopy equivalent to
\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(X)
-projective
n
-space
P^{n}\operatorname{\Omega}\hskip 1.00006pt(X)
, which is obtained by using Exercise
2.13
and Theorem
2.5
.

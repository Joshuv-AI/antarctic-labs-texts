The convergence analysis of an accelerated iteration for solving algebraic Riccati equations
Chun-Yueh Chiang

Abstract
The discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation (DARE) have extensive applications in optimal control problems. We provide new theoretical supports to the stability properties of solutions to the DARE and reduce the convergence conditions under which the accelerated fixed-point iteration (AFPI) can be applied to compute the numerical solutions of DARE. In particular, we verify that the convergence of AFPI is R-superlinear when the spectral radius of the closed-loop matrix is greater than 1, which is shown by mild assumption and only using primary matrix theories. Numerical examples are shown to illustrate the consistency and effectiveness of our theoretical results.

The discrete algebraic Riccati equation (DARE) arising in the field of applied mathematics and many classical problems of control theory has been a subject of study for a long time, see

In this paper, we are concerned with the discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation (DARE)

In the past few decades, there have developed fruitful theoretical results as well as a variety of numerical algorithms for the DARE such
as the famous direct method, the Schur method, and some iterative methods including matrix disk function method, matrix sign function method, Newton iterations (NM) and structure-preserving doubling algorithm (SDA), as well as those developed in

Recently, the semigroup property for some binary matrix operations is investigated in

A Hermitian solution

The stability property plays important role in the analysis of the convergence behaviour of SDA. In the previous works, the convergence analysis of SDA are concerned with “the regular case”, that is,
\rho(T_{X})<1

4
,
5
,
7

14
,

that the SDA converges linearly to an almost stabilizing solution

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}-\lambda\mathcal{L}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
-H&I\end{bmatrix}-\lambda\mathcal{L}\begin{bmatrix}I&-G\\

As mentioned above the convergence assumptions in the critical case are highly related to the distribution of the partial multiplicity of the unimodular eigenvalue of

In the practical implementations it is expected to avoid computing the elementary divisors corresponding to the eigenvalues of

on the unit circle. By the way, we are interested in the case where some eigenvalues of

The main contribution of this paper is to show that, under ceratin conditions, the quadratic convergence of AFPI still holds in the critical case and even
\rho(T_{X_{+}})>1

4.1
Acceleration of fixed-point iteration

The following definition characterizes the semigroup property of the iteration associated a binary operator.

Definition 4.1
.

[
13
]

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
and
F:D\times D\rightarrow D
be a binary matrix operator. We call that an iteration

\mathbb{X}_{k+1}=F(\mathbb{X}_{k},\mathbb{X}_{1}),\quad k\geq 1,

(16)

has the semigroup property if the operator
F
satisfies the following associative rule:

\displaystyle F(F(Y,Z),W)=F(Y,F(Z,W)),

(17)

for any
Y,Z
and
W
in
D
.

It is interesting to point out that the sequence
\{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}\}
satisfies the so-called discrete flow property
[
13
]
[Theorem 3.2], that is,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{X}}_{k+\ell}=F({\mathbb{X}}_{k},{\mathbb{X}}_{\ell}),

(18)

for any two positive integers
k
and
\ell
.
Now, we construct a fixed point iteration that has the semigroup property so that the fixed point iteration can be accelerated by applying the procedure as in
[
13
]
[Algorithm 3.1]. To this end, we have

\displaystyle X=R_{\rm d}^{(k)}(R_{\rm d}(X))=R_{\rm d}^{(k+1)}(X)=H_{k+1}+A_{k+1}^{\ast}{X}(I+G_{k+1}{X})^{-1}A_{k+1},

A_{k}
,
G_{k}
, and
H_{k}
, for
k=1,2,\ldots
, are matrices given by the following iteration

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}A_{k+1}\\
G_{k+1}\\
H_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}=F(\begin{bmatrix}A_{k}\\
G_{k}\\
H_{k}\end{bmatrix}):=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}\Delta_{G_{k},{H_{1}}}A_{k}\\
G_{1}+A_{1}\Delta_{G_{k},{H_{1}}}G_{k}A_{1}^{\ast}\\
H_{k}+A_{k}^{\ast}H_{1}\Delta_{G_{k},{H_{1}}}A_{k}\end{bmatrix},

where
G_{1}=G\geq 0
,
H_{1}=H\geq 0
,
A_{1}=A
, and
\Delta_{G_{k},{H_{1}}}=I+G_{k}H_{1}
. An induction argument proves that
G_{k}\geq 0
and
H_{k}\geq 0
and thus
I+G_{k}H_{1}
is
invertible. Therefore, the sequence of matrices
\mathbb{X}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{k}^{\top}&G_{k}^{\top}&H_{k}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
generated by

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{k+1}=F(\mathbb{X}_{k},\mathbb{X}_{1}),

(19)

is well defined with
\mathbb{X}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}^{\top}&G_{1}^{\top}&H_{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
for
k\geq 1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}_{n}:=\mathbb{C}_{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{n}
.
We consider the mapping
F:\mathbb{K}_{n}\times\mathbb{K}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}_{n}
as an action defined by

\displaystyle F(Y,Z)=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{1}\Delta_{Y_{2},Z_{3}}Y_{1}\\
Z_{2}+Z_{1}\Delta_{Y_{2},Z_{3}}Y_{2}Z_{1}^{\ast}\\
Y_{3}+Y_{1}^{\ast}Z_{3}\Delta_{Y_{2},Z_{3}}Y_{1}\end{bmatrix},

(20)

where
Y=\begin{bmatrix}Y_{1}^{\top}&Y_{2}^{\top}&Y_{3}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
,
Z=\begin{bmatrix}Z_{1}^{\top}&Z_{2}^{\top}&Z_{3}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
and
\Delta_{Y_{2},Z_{3}}=(I+Y_{2}Z_{3})^{-1}
. Note that
\Delta_{Y_{2},Z_{3}}
is well defined since all eigenvalues of
I+Y_{2}Z_{3}
are positive. It has been proved in
[
13
, Example 4.4]
that the binary operator
F
satisfies the associative rule (
17
) holds, that is, the iteration
\mathbb{X}_{k+1}=F(\mathbb{X}_{k},\mathbb{X}_{1})
has the semigroup property. As a consequence,
{\mathbb{X}}_{k+1}=F(\mathbb{X}_{1},\mathbb{X}_{k})
for any positive integers
k
and we deduce that

H_{k+1}=H_{1}+A_{1}^{\ast}H_{k}\Delta_{G_{1},{H_{k}}}A_{1}.

from which we see that the sequence
\{H_{k}\}
coincides with the sequence
\{X_{k}\}
generated by the fixed point iteration
X_{k+1}=R_{\rm d}(X_{k})
since
X_{1}=H_{1}=H
. Applying the discrete flow property (
18
) to the iteration (
19
) we obtain the following accelerated fixed point iteration (AFPI).

Algorithm 4.1
.

(An accelerated of fixed-point iteration (AFPI) to solve DARE. (
1
))

1.

Given a positive integer
r>1
, let
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{1}={\mathbb{X}}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}^{\top}&G_{1}^{\top}&H_{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
;

2.

For
k=1,\ldots,
iterate

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=F(\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{k},\mathbb{X}_{k}^{(r-1)}),

until convergence, where
F
is defined in (
20
) and
\mathbb{X}_{k}^{(r-1)}
is defined in step 3.

3.

For
\ell=1,\ldots,r-2
, iterate

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{k}^{(\ell+1)}

\displaystyle=F(\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{k},\mathbb{X}_{k}^{(\ell)}),

with
\mathbb{X}_{k}^{(1)}=\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{A}_{k}^{\top}&\widehat{G}_{k}^{\top}&\widehat{H}_{k}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
.

Proof.

From the above discussion, the sequence

5
Concluding remarks

This paper concerns comprehensive convergence analysis of the most recent and advanced algorithms including SDA and its variants AFPI for solving DARE (
1
). Our contribution fills in an existing gap in the minimal positive semidefinite solution
X_{\star}
of the DARE (
1
), concerning the magnitude
\rho(T_{X_{\star}})\geq 1
. More precisely, we have proved the convergence for the AFPI, when the eigenvalues of
T_{X_{\star}}
are inside, on or outside the open unit circle. The theoretical result is confirmed by a randomness numerical example. Consequently, our results are more general than those in the past works, which considered only eigenvalues lies in the closed unit disk. The techniques of Proposition 2.1 and Theorem
3.2
can be adopted in the convergence analysis of AFPI. We believe the results we obtain are novel on this topic and could provide considerable insights into the study of other nonlinear matrix equations.

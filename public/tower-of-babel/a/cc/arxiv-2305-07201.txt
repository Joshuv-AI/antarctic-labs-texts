The obstacle problem for a higher order fractional Laplacian
Donatella Danielli, Alaa Haj Ali & Arshak Petrosyan

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the obstacle problem for the fractional Laplace operator ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} in the Euclidian space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} in the case where 1 < s < 2 1<s<2 . As first observed in [ 13 ] , the problem can be extended to the upper half-space ℝ + n + 1 \mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1} to obtain a thin obstacle problem for the weighted biLaplace operator Δ b 2 ​ U \Delta^{2}_{b}U , where Δ b U = y − b ∇ ⋅ ( y b ∇ U ) \Delta_{b}U=y^{-b}\nabla\cdot(y^{b}\nabla U) . Such a problem arises in connection with unilateral phenomena for elastic, homogenous, and isotropic flat plates. We establish the well-posedness and C l ​ o ​ c 1 , 1 ​ ( ℝ n ) ∩ H 1 + s ​ ( ℝ n ) C_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{1+s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) -regularity of the solution. By writing the solutions in terms of Riesz potentials of suitable local measures, we can base our proofs on tools from potential theory, such as a continuity principle and a maximum principle. Finally, we deduce the regularity of the extension problem to the higher dimensional upper half space. This gives an extension of Schild’s work in [ 9 ] and [ 10 ] from the case b = 0 b=0 to the general case − 1 < b < 1 -1<b<1 .

Introduction

Obstacle-type problems for second order uniformly elliptic operators have attracted great interest over the years, and they continue to be explored, due the wide range of applications in elasticity, biology, and engineering, just to name a few. In the past two decades there has been a resurgence of interest in obstacle problems associated with a class of nonlocal operators, whose prototype is the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
with order
s\in(0,1)
, driven by their relevance in financial mathematics. On the other hand, much less is known on obstacle-type problems associated with the bi-harmonic operator, or with higher order of the fractional Laplacian.

In this work we are concerned with the regularity of solutions to an obstacle problem associated with the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}u
for
s\in(1,2)
, in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 1
. In its simplest formulation, given a sufficiently regular function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, known as the
obstacle
, we want to find a function
u
that satisfies

(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq 0,\quad u\geq\psi,\quad(-\Delta)^{s}u(u-\psi)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

with zero boundary condition at infinity

u(x),\ \nabla u(x)\to 0\quad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.

These conditions have to be understood in appropriate weak sense. The unique solution is obtained by minimizing the energy functional

I_{0}[v]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n-2+2s}}dxdz,

(1.2)

(which is the square of the seminorm
[v]_{\dot{H}^{s}}
, see Section
2
for more details) over the space of functions

\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\{v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid v\geq\psi\},

(1.3)

where
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space, which is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to
[\cdot]_{\dot{H}^{s}}
.

We also consider another version of the problem, where besides the obstacle
\psi
, we are given a bounded domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sufficiently regular boundary. We want to find a function
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq 0,\quad u\geq\psi,\quad(-\Delta)^{s}u\ (u-\psi)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.4)

together with “boundary” conditions

u=g\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

where
g\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the compatibility condition
g\geq\psi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. If we additionally assume that
g\in C^{2s+\epsilon}_{\rm loc}\cap\dot{H}^{2s}
, then replacing
u
with
u-g
and
\psi
with
\psi-g
, and letting
f=(-\Delta)^{s}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can equivalently state the above problem as

(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq f(x),\quad u\geq\psi,\quad((-\Delta)^{s}u-f)(u-\psi)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.5)

with boundary conditions

u=0\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

The solution of (
1.5
) can be obtained by minimizing the functional

I[v]:=\frac{1}{2k(n,s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n-2+2s}}dxdz-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)v(x)dx

(1.6)

over the set

\mathcal{B}:=\{v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid v=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\text{ and }v\geq\psi\}.

(1.7)

The constant
k(n,s)
comes from the equivalency (
2.3
) below.

In

(-\Delta)^{s}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{a}U=0&\text{ for }(x,y)\in B_{1}^{+}\\

\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{a}U_{y}(x,y)\leq 0&\text{ in }\bar{B_{1}^{\prime}}\\
\big(U(x,0)-\psi(x)\big)\lim_{y\to 0}y^{a}U_{y}(x,y)=0&\text{ in }\bar{B_{1}^{\prime}}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

Here
B_{1}

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

.
The thin obstacle problem has been extensively studied. Just to mention a few salient results, in
[
3
]
, Caffarelli proves that a solution of the Signorini problem is

[
1

, where they achieved the optimal

-regularity of the solution. In the case of vanishing obstacle, Athanasopoulos, Caffarelli and Salsa in
[
2

analyzed the regularity of the free boundary near the so-called regular free boundary points. In 2008, Caffarelli, Salsa and Silvestre in
[
4

extended the optimal regularity results and the analysis of the regular part of the free boundary to all
0<s<1

The Caffarelli-Silvestre characterization of the fractional Laplacian as a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map was generalized in
[
13

to positive, non-integer orders of the fractional Laplace operator. More specifically, for
1<s<2
, let

\Delta_{b}U=y^{-b}\nabla\cdot(y^{b}\nabla U).

For

(1.10)

We recall the following definition of a weak solution of the

\int_{\Omega}y^{b}\ \Delta_{b}U\Delta_{b}\phi=0\text{ for all functions }\phi\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega,y^{b}).

The following result holds.

Fractional Sobolev spaces and the fractional Laplacian of order
1<s<2

For
1<s<2
, the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the seminorm

\displaystyle[v]_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n-2+2s}}dxdz\right)^{1/2}

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\left(k(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2s}|\hat{v}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2},

(2.2)

where
\hat{v}(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
v
and
k(n,s)
is a positive constant depending on the dimension
n
and the exponent
s
only.
The space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space when endowed with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(z))\cdot(\nabla g(x)-\nabla g(z))}{|x-z|^{n-2+2s}}dxdz

(2.3)

\displaystyle=k(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi)\overline{\hat{g}(\xi)}d\xi

(2.4)

For a function
v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
1<s<2
we define the
fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}v
as a tempered distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}v}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{v}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.5)

in terms of Fourier transforms. Equivalently, it can be given in the weak form as

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}v,\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{k(n,s)}\langle v,\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\text{for any }\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.6)

In our proofs, we will also use the following Sobolev embedding theorems. If
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1<s<2
, then
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and moreover
u\in L^{\frac{2n}{n-2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\nabla u\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2-2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2-2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,s}[u]_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

(2.7)

For a domain
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)
if its extension

\tilde{u}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u&x\in\Omega\\
0&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.

is in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(resp.

(resp.

Theorem 3.1
(Existence and Uniqueness of Minimizers)

There exists a unique minimizer
u_{0}
(resp.
u
) of the functional
I_{0}[\cdot]
(resp.
I[\cdot]
) over the admissible set
\mathcal{B}_{0}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}
).

Proof.

Concerning
u_{0}
, first we note that the admissible set
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is non-empty since
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
. Now let

be a minimizing sequence for

. Then,
\{u_{m}\}
is bounded in the

-norm, and therefore it has a subsequence which converges weakly in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to a function
u_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
By the property of weak convergence, we immediately conclude that
u_{0}
is a minimizer of
I_{0}[\cdot]
over the set
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

An analogous argument shows that the minimizing sequence of
I[\cdot]
(still denoted by
\{u_{m}\}
), has a subsequence which converges weakly in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to a function

. The Sobolev inequality (
2.7
) implies that
\{u_{m}\}
is bounded in

. Moreover, since

in

, we apply the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem for

to conclude that

converges strongly in

. Passing to the weak limit in
I[\cdot]
we conclude that

is indeed a minimizer of
I[\cdot]
. The uniqueness of both of minimizers follows from the convexity of the functionals
I_{0}[\cdot]
and
I[\cdot]
.

In the next two lemmas, we obtain the variational inequalities satisfied by the minimizers
u_{0}
and
u
.

Lemma 3.2

The minimizer
u_{0}
of the functional
I_{0}[\cdot]
over the set
\mathcal{B}_{0}
satisfies the variational inequality

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{k(n,s)}\langle u_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\geq 0,\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{B}_{0}.

(3.1)

In particular,

\mu^{0}:=(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}\geq 0

is a nonnegative Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

For
v\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, write
v=u_{0}+\phi
. Then
u_{0}+\epsilon\phi
is an admissible function for all
\epsilon>0
.
By the minimality of

we have

0\leq\langle u_{0}+\epsilon\phi,u_{0}+\epsilon\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}-\langle u_{0},u_{0}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=2\epsilon\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon^{2}\langle\phi,\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Dividing by
\epsilon
and then letting

we obtain

\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq 0.

We can derive the following variational inequality for the minimizer
u
of
I[\cdot]
in a similar way .

Lemma 3.3

The minimizer
u
of the functional
I[\cdot]
over the set
\mathcal{B}
satisfies the variational inequality

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u-f,v-u\rangle=\frac{1}{k({n,s)}}\langle u,v-u\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\langle f,v-u\rangle\geq 0,\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{B}.

In particular,

\mu:=(-\Delta)^{s}u-f

is a nonnegative Radon measure on
\Omega
.

4
The problem without boundary values - Problem (
1.1

In the remainder of the paper,
\phi_{s}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\phi_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2s}},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}&\text{ when $n\neq 1,2,3$ or $s\neq\frac{3}{2}$},\\

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1

The identity

Proof.

First of all, it is not hard to see that

(4.3)

Moreover, applying Fubini’s theorem we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi_{s}*\zeta)(x)d\mu^{0}(x)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{s}(y-x)\zeta(x)dx\right)d\mu^{0}(y)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{s}(y-x)d\mu^{0}(y)\right)\zeta(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi_{s}*\mu^{0})(x)\,\zeta(x)dx.\\
\end{split}

(4.4)

Combining (
4.3
) with (
4.4
) yields

u_{0}=\phi_{s}*\mu^{0}\quad\text{a.e.\ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

5
The extension problem

To study the regularity of problem (
1.5
), we extend the minimizer
u
to the upper half-space with a function
U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},y^{b})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{b}^{2}U=0&\text{ for }(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\\
\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{b}U_{y}(x,y)=0&\text{ for }(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\\
U(x,0)=u(x)&\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

HJere
b=3-2s
, and the PDE is satisfied in the weak sense (
1.11
). We say that
U
is the
b-biharmonic extension
of
u
to the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. We begin with deriving the fundamental solution of the b-biLaplace equation
\Delta_{b}^{2}U=0
at the origin.

5.1
Fundamental solution at the origin

For
s\in(1,2)
let
b=3-2s\in(-1,1)
, or equivalently
s=\frac{3-b}{2}
. First, we make the following observation. Suppose
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{1+b}\to\mathbb{R}
is radially symmetric in the second variable
y
, e.g.
w(x,y_{1})=w(x,y_{2})
whenever
|y_{1}|=|y_{2}|
. Denoting
r:=|y|
, a direct computation shows that

\Delta^{2}w(x,y)=\Delta_{b}^{2}w(x,r).

Thus, following the line of thought in
[
5
]
, we can think of the problem (
5.1
) as the biharmonic extension problem of
u
in
1+b
additional dimensions.

With this observation, we see that the fundamental solution at the origin of the biLaplace equation in
n+1+b
dimensions is indeed a fundamental solution for the b-biLaplace equation. From the Appendix in
[
9
]
, we know that the latter reads, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}E_{s}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2s}{2}}},&\quad n\neq 1,2,3\text{ or }s\neq{3}/{2},\\
E_{{3}/{2}}(x,y)=-\log\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},&\quad n=3\\
E_{{3}/{2}}(x,y)=-\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},&\quad n=2,\\
E_{{3}/{2}}(x,y)=\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)\left(\log\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{3+n}{2n+2}\right),&\quad n=1.\\
\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

Recalling (
4.1
), we notice that
E_{s}(x,0)
is the fundamental solution of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
.

5.2
Poisson Formula

Let
b=3-2s
as before. Now we derive the Poisson kernel for the b-biharmonic operator
\Delta_{b}^{2}
on the upper half space, and therefore we obtain a Poisson representation formula for the extension problem (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.1

The function

P_{s}(x,y)=-C_{n,s}\frac{y^{2s}}{(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{n+2s}{2}}}

(5.3)

is the Poisson kernel for the b-biLaplace equation
\Delta_{b}^{2}u=0
. That is,
\Delta_{b}^{2}P_{s}(x,y)=0
for all
(x,y)
such that
y>0
, and
\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}P_{s}(x,y)=-\delta_{0}(x)
, where
\delta_{0}
denotes Dirac’s delta. Moreover,
P_{s}(x,y)
satisfies

P_{s}(\cdot,y)=C_{n,s}(-\Delta)^{s}E_{s}(\cdot,y)\quad\text{for all $y>0$}.

(5.4)

In particular, we have the following Poisson formula

U(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P_{s}(x-\xi,y)u(\xi)d\xi=\left(E_{s}(\cdot,y)*(-\Delta)^{s}u\right)(x)\quad\text{for all $y>0$}.

(5.5)

Proof.

By direct computations we obtain

\begin{split}\Delta_{b}\left(\frac{y^{m}}{r^{k}}\right)&=(m^{2}+bm-m)\frac{y^{m-2}}{r^{k}}+k(k-n-2m+1-b)\frac{y^{m}}{r^{k+2}}\\
\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}_{b}\left(\frac{y^{m}}{r^{k}}\right)&=(m^{2}+bm-m)\left((m^{2}+bm-5m-2b+6)\frac{y^{m-4}}{r^{n+m}}+(n+m)(-2m+6-2b)\frac{y^{m-2}}{r^{n+m+2}}\right)\\
&+(n+m)(-m+1-b)(n+m+2)(-m+3-b)\frac{y^{m}}{r^{n+m+4}}.\\
\end{split}

Setting
k=m+n
we get

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}_{b}\left(\frac{y^{m}}{r^{k}}\right)&=(m^{2}+bm-m)\left((m^{2}+bm-5m-2b+6)\frac{y^{m-4}}{r^{n+m}}+(n+m)(-2m+6-2b)\frac{y^{m-2}}{r^{n+m+2}}\right)\\
&+(n+m)(-m+1-b)(n+m+2)(-m+3-b)\frac{y^{m}}{r^{n+m+4}}.\\
\end{split}

Now we notice that for
m=3-b
, we have
\Delta^{2}_{b}\left(\frac{y^{m}}{r^{k}}\right)=0
. This shows that
P_{s}(x,y)
as defined in (
5.3
) satisfies
\Delta_{b}^{2}P_{s}=0
.
Moreover, we can easily check that
\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}P_{s}(x,y)=-\delta_{0}(x)
, where
\delta_{0}(x)
is the Dirac delta function at 0. Therefore,
P_{s}(x,y)
is indeed the Poisson kernel.

It remains to prove (
5.4
). For this, we write

E_{s}(\cdot,y)=\phi_{s}*P_{s}(\cdot,y).

Thus,

\widehat{E_{s}(.,y)}=\widehat{\phi_{s}}\widehat{P_{s}(\cdot,y)}=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2s}}\widehat{P_{s}(\cdot,y)}.

This shows that

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}E_{s}(.,y)}=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{E_{s}(\cdot,y)}=\widehat{P_{s}(\cdot,y)}\quad\text{for all }y>0,

and yields (
5.4
).

Proof.

Lemma 7.8

8
H^{1+s}
-regularity of Problem (

)

Following the approach in
[
9

, next we show that the solution to Problem (

H^{1+s}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 8.1

Let
u
be the minimizer of
I[\cdot]
. Then,
u\in H_{\rm loc}^{1+s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,

\|u\|_{H^{1+s}(B^{\prime}_{{\rho}/{4}})}\leq C(\rho)\left(\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{2\rho})}\right).

(8.1)

Proof.
We define

Q_{\beta}(x):=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\beta}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(8.2)

which is equal (up to a constant) to the Riesz kernel of order
\beta
. It is a classical fact from potential theory (see, for example,
[
7
, Theorem 1.20]
) that the following relation between the
L^{2}
-norm of the Riesz potential of order
\beta/2
of a compactly supported measure
\nu
and the Riesz energy of order
\beta
of the same measure holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Q_{\beta/2}*\nu|^{2}dx=C(n,\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(Q_{\beta}*\nu)d\nu\quad\text{for all }0<\beta<{n}/{2}.

(8.3)

Setting
\nu=\mu_{\rho/4}
and
\beta=2(s-1)
in (
8.3
) we obtain

\begin{split}\|Q_{s-1}*&\mu_{\rho/4}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q_{2(s-1)}*\mu_{\rho/4}d\mu_{\rho/4}\\
&=-C(n,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta\phi_{s}*\mu_{\rho/4}d\mu_{\rho/4}\\
&\leq C(n,s,\rho)\|\Delta\phi_{s}*\mu_{\rho/4}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/2})}\\
&\leq C(n,s,\rho)\|\Delta\phi_{s}*\mu_{\rho/2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/2})}\\
&\leq C(n,s,\rho)\left(\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{2\rho})}\right),\\
\end{split}

(8.4)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
7.5
.
Noticing that

\begin{split}-\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\Delta\phi_{s}}&=C(n,s)\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}Q_{2(s-1)}}\\
&=C(n,s)|\xi|^{s-1}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2s-2}}=C(n,s)\frac{1}{|\xi|^{s-1}}=C(n,s)\widehat{Q_{s-1}},\end{split}

we conclude that

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\Delta\phi_{s}*\mu_{{\rho}/{4}}\in L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/2}),

and

\begin{split}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\Delta\phi_{s}*\mu_{{\rho}/{4}}\|_{L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/2})}\leq C(\rho)&\Bigl(\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&+\|y^{{b}/{2}}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{2\rho})}\Bigr).\end{split}

(8.5)

Combining (
8.5
) with the representation (
6.5
), we infer that

\Delta u\in H^{s-1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|\Delta u\|_{H^{s-1}(B^{\prime}_{{\rho}/{4}})}\leq C(\rho)\left(\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{2\rho})}\right).

(8.6)

Moreover, we notice that, for all
i,j:1,..,n
,

\int_{B^{\prime}_{\rho/8}}\left|\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}(\phi_{s})_{x_{i}x_{j}}}\right|^{2}=\int_{B^{\prime}_{\rho/8}}|\xi|^{2(s-1)}\left|\widehat{(\phi_{s})_{x_{i}x_{j}}}\right|^{2}\leq\int_{B^{\prime}_{\rho/8}}|\xi|^{2(s-1)}\left|\widehat{\Delta\phi_{s}}\right|^{2},

(8.7)

where the last inequality follows from Remark
7.7
and the fact that
\Delta\phi_{s}\leq 0
.
Combining (
8.7
) with (
8.5
) and the representation (
7.12
) with
y=0
, we reach the desired conclusion.

Theorem 9.3

Let
u
be the minimizer of

U
be the extension of

to the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{n+1}

\begin{split}\|\nabla(\Delta_{b}U)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho/8},y^{b})}\leq C(\rho)&\left(\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+\|y^{{b}/{2}}\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}\right.\\
&\left.+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{\prime}_{2\rho})}\right).\end{split}

(9.10)

Proof.

From Theorem
8.1

\Delta u\in H^{s-1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{b}\ \nabla W\cdot\nabla\zeta=\langle\Delta u,\zeta\rangle_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(9.11)

for all

Notice that, with the regularity result of Theorem

For the second part of the theorem, set

&\leq C(n,\rho)\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{H^{s-1}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/4})}.\\

Expanding the left hand side we get

\int_{B_{\rho/4}^{+}}y^{b}\ \eta^{2}|\nabla(\Delta_{b}U)|^{2}\leq C(n,\rho)\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{H^{s-1}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/4})}-2\int_{B_{\rho/4}}y^{b}\eta(\Delta_{b}U)\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla(\Delta_{b}U).

Applying Young’s inequality we infer

\|\nabla(\Delta_{b}U)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho/8},y^{b})}\leq C(n,\rho)\left(\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{H^{s-1}(B^{\prime}_{\rho/4})}+\|\Delta_{b}U\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho/4},y^{b})}\right).

Finally, employing Theorem
8.1
, we reach (
9.10
).

(9.16)

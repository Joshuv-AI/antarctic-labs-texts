For the Stirling numbers of the second kind S ⁡ ( n , k ) S(n,k) and the ordered Bell numbers B ⁡ ( n ) B(n) , we prove the identity ∑ k = 1 n / 2 S ⁡ ( n , 2 ​ k ) ​ ( 2 ​ k − 1 ) ! = B ⁡ ( n − 1 ) \sum_{k=1}^{n/2}S(n,2k)(2k-1)!=B(n-1) . An analogous identity holds for the sum over odd k k ’s.



1
Introduction

For integers
0\leq k\leq n
, we denote by
S(n,k)
the Stirling numbers of the second kind and by
B(n)
the ordered Bell numbers. These sequences can be found in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS)
[
3
]
as
A008277
and
A000670
. They are related by

\displaystyle B(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!S(n,k)\text{.}

(1)

In this short note, we will give four different ways to express the ordered Bell numbers as a weighted sum of odd or even Stirling numbers of the second kind, where the mentioned parity refers to the second argument.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle B(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}+2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k)!S(n,2k)=(-1)^{n}+2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k+1)!S(n,2k+1)

(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}(2k-1)!S(n+1,2k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}(2k)!S(n+1,2k+1)\text{.}

(3)

We could not find the Identities (
3
) (respectively (
8
) below) in the literature. The Identities (
2
) are direct consequences of known other equations, as explained below.

2
Proof of the Theorem

The formal (exponential) generating functions (gf’s) of
B(n)
and
S(k,n)
(for fixed
k
) are given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}B(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2-e^{x}}=:\mathcal{B}(x)

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}S(n,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{x}-1)^{k}}{k!}\text{,}

(5)

see Quaintance and Gould
[
2
]
. The following identities are known

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k!S(n,k)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}(k-1)!S(n,k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-1,&\text{if $n=1$;}\\
0,&\text{if $n\geq 2$, }\end{cases}\text{,}

(7)

see Boyadzhiev
[
1
, Appendix A]
. Combining identities (
1
) and (
6
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k)!S(n,2k)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(B(n)+(-1)^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k+1)!S(n,2k+1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(B(n)-(-1)^{n}\right)\text{.}

This shows Equation (
2
) of Theoreom
1
.

We show below the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Put
H(n):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k-1)!S(n,k)
for
n\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle H(n)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n=1$;}\\
2B(n-1),&\text{if $n\geq 2$. }\end{cases}

(8)

Combining identities (
7
) and (
8
) gives, for
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k-1)!S(n,2k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k)!S(n,2k+1)=B(n-1)\text{,}

(9)

and therefore (
3
) of Theoreom
1
.
It remains to prove (
8
).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The gf
\mathcal{H}(x)
of
H(n)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k-1)!S(n,k)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}(e^{x}-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=-\log(2-e^{x})\text{,}

where we used (
5
) and the Taylor series

\log(1-z)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}z^{k}
for
\lvert z\rvert<1
.
Further, defining
G(1)=2
and
G(n)=H(n)
for
n\geq 2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}G(n)=x-\log(2-e^{x})\text{,}

noticing that
H(1)=1
.
By elementary differentiation rules and (
4
), it follows that

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{e^{x}-1}=2\mathcal{B}(x)
.
Hence, by Wilf
[
5
, Formula 2.3.1]
, we get
H(n)=G(n)=2B(n-1)
for
n\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 3
.

We put

H_{e}(n):=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k-1)!S(n,2k)

and

H_{o}(n):=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2k)!S(n,2k+1)
.
Using the same tools as in the proof above, we can also show that the corresponding gf’s are given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}H_{e}(n)

\displaystyle=\artanh(e^{x}-1)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{e^{x}}{2-e^{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}H_{o}(n)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(e^{x}(2-e^{x})\right)\text{.}

With these gf’s, we can deduce (
9
) directly, without using (
7
).

Remark 4
.

The quantities
W(n,k):=k!S(n+1,k+1)
are called the
Worpitzky numbers
, see Vanderfelde
[
5
]
(OEIS
A130850
). With this notation, (
9
) can be written as (
n\geq 1
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}W(n,2k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}W(n,2k+1)=B(n)\text{.}

Regularisation by Hamiltonian extension
Andreas Knauf

Abstract
We consider the Kepler potential and its relatives q ↦ − ‖ q ‖ − 2 ​ ( 1 − 1 / n ) q\mapsto-\|q\|^{-2(1-1/n)} , n ∈ ℕ n\in{\mathbb{N}} in arbitrary dimension d d . We derive a unique real-analytic symplectic extension of phase space on which the Hamiltonian flow is complete and still real-analytic.



1
Introduction

There has been a long tradition to regularise the two-body problem of celestial mechanics,
in order to deal with (near) collision orbits.
Here we present a method that works not only for any spatial dimension, but also for
a known discrete family of homogeneous potentials.
Specifically, we consider for dimension
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

(negative) potentials of (negative) homogeneity

\alpha_{n}:=2(1-1/n)
with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
:

U_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{q}\backslash\{0\}\to{\mathbb{R}}\quad\mbox{,}\quad U_{n}(q)=Z\|q\|^{-\alpha_{n}}\,,

with strength of interaction
Z>0
. For reasons of comparison,
we also look at the free case with
n=1
and
U_{1}
constant.

These potentials were discussed by
McGehee
in
[
McG
]
.
For them a regularisation of Levi-Civita type in dimension

d=2
, that is,
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{q}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
,
is of the form
q=Q^{n}
,
n=2
being Kepler’s case.
Not knowing a name for them, we tentatively call them
McGehee potentials
.

Some typical trajectories for motion in these potentials at energy 0 are shown in
Figure
2.1
.
The solution curves for
U_{3}(q)=Z\|q\|^{-4/3}
at total energy zero are Tschirnhaus
cubics.

1.1 Theorem

(completion by analytic extension)

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
d\geq 2

there exists a unique Hamiltonian system
(P,\omega,H)

which is a real-analytic extension
of
\big(\widehat{P},\widehat{\omega},\widehat{H}\big)
,
having a complete vector field
X_{H}
.

1.2 Remark

(existence and uniqueness)

For dimension
d=1
, too there exists such a Hamiltonian system
(P,\omega,H)
,
but it is not uniquely defined (see Remark
2.7
.1).

For
d\geq 2
uniqueness up to isomorphisms is granted because every collision orbit
must contain exactly one collision point, and there is only one
continuous

way to continue it after collision, comparing with nearby non-collision orbits.
So the article basically shows

of such a
(P,\omega,H)
.

\Diamond

Although the solutions of the Hamiltonian equations only have an essential
dependence on
n
, we kept the constants
m
and
Z
for later applications.

Two basic ideas lead to our construction:

\bullet

We consider (physical) time
t
as a phase space function
T
near collision and
complement it by suitable constants of motion to obtain a symplectic chart.
These functions on phase space are simply total energy,
angular momentum and a generalised Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector.

\bullet

We deal with the fact that – unlike for free and Kepler motion – for
n\geq 3
the Hamiltonian
flow is not super-integrable. This follows from Bertrand’s Theorem and means that for
negative energies the typical orbit is not closed. So a generalised
Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector can only exist
locally
in phase space.
But it turns out that this suffices for our construction.

2.1
Levi-Civita-like regularisation

Because of conservation of angular momentum, for any dimension
d
the
solution curves lie in a plane.
So we assume that
\widehat{M}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

so that
\widehat{P}\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

Up to time parameterisation, the curves that correspond to Hamiltonian orbits
at energy
E
(and do not touch the boundary circle
U_{n}\equiv-E
of Hill’s
region for
E<0
) coincide with the unit speed
geodesics for the Jacobi-Maupertuis metric
\widehat{g}_{E}
on
\widehat{M}
that
equals
W:=\tfrac{2}{m}(E+U_{n})
times the Euclidean metric.

Although the hypersurfaces

(\widehat{H}-E)^{-1}(0)
and
\big((\widehat{H}-E)/W)\big)^{-1}(0)
of
\widehat{P}
coincide, the factor of time reparameterisation is non-trivial:

\textstyle\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}=W(q(t))\quad\mbox{or conversely}\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}s}=\frac{1}{W(Q(s))}=\tfrac{m}{2}\frac{|Q(s)|^{2(n-1)}}{Z+E\,|Q(s)|^{2(n-1)}}\,.

(2.1)

When, as in
[
McG
, Section 8]
, we consider the
n
-fold branched covering

\pi:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\quad\mbox{,}\quad\pi(Q)=Q^{n}\qquad\mbox{and }\ \hat{\pi}:=\pi|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}\,,

then
\hat{\pi}
is unbranched.
The lift of
U_{n}
has the form
U_{n}\circ\hat{\pi}(Q)=Z|Q|^{2(1-n)}
, so that the lift
of the Jacobi metric
\widehat{g}_{E}
equals, for
{\rm Re}(Q)=Q_{1}
and
{\rm Im}(Q)=Q_{2}

\hat{\pi}^{*}\,\widehat{g}_{E}(Q)=2n^{2}m\big(Z+E(Q\bar{Q})^{2n-2}\big)(dQ_{1}\otimes dQ_{1}+dQ_{2}\otimes dQ_{2})\,.

(2.2)

Since
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, this uniquely extends to
a real-analytic metric
\mathbf{g}_{E}
on
{\mathbb{C}}
if
E\geq 0
, respectively
on lifted Hill’s region
\{Q\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid|Q|^{2(n-1)}<-Z/E\}
if
E<0
.

So the geodesic flow on the covering surface with metric
\mathbf{g}_{E}

is real-analytic in the time parameter
s
and the initial conditions.

The factor
\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}s}
of time reparameterisation is real-analytic too, see (
2.1
).

In particular, for
E=0
, the geodesics are straight lines. Their projections to the

q
-plane via

are curves of degree

.

2.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We will begin with the definition of an open phase space neighbourhood

\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}\subseteq\widehat{P}
.
Then we will prove analyticity properties of phase space functions defined on it.

2.2.1
The domain
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}

The domain
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
of a phase space chart,
to be introduced in the following lemma, will be the intersection
of a domain
U^{\varepsilon}\subseteq P
of a chart on extended phase space
P

and the domain

\widehat{P}\subseteq P
of the
(q,p)
chart, with

P=\widehat{P}\cup U^{\varepsilon}
.

2.2 Lemma

We consider a maximal solution
(q,p):(t_{-},t_{+})\to\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}

of the Hamiltonian equation for (
1.1
), on
the open subset near the excluded fiber
T^{*}_{0}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{q}

\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{(q,p)\in\widehat{P}\,\left|\ \|q\|<\varepsilon\,,\,\textstyle\widehat{H}(q,p)>\frac{-Z}{2n\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}\right.\right\}\qquad(\varepsilon>0).

(2.5)

Then
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle q,p\right\rangle>\frac{Z}{n\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}}
.
This implies that the length
t_{+}-t_{-}>0
of the time interval
has a uniform upper bound
2\varepsilon^{2-1/n}\sqrt{n/Z}
and

\bullet

the solution either intersects the real-analytic hypersurface

\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}:=\big\{(q,p)\in\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}\;\big|\,\left\langle q,p\right\rangle=0\big\}

(2.6)

exactly once and transversally, in a
pericenter
(meaning
\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle q,p\right\rangle>0
),

\bullet

or it is a
collision solution
, that is,
\lim_{t\searrow t_{-}}q(t)=0\,
or

\,\lim_{t\nearrow t_{+}}q(t)=0
.

\bullet

Every collision solution
(q,p):(t_{-},t_{+})\to\widehat{P}
intersects
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
.

Proof:

Note that
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
is open in
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, too since

\widehat{P}\subseteq T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is open.

\bullet

\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
is a real-analytic hypersurface, since
q,p\neq 0
for

(q,p)\in\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
.

\bullet

The squared distance from the origin is strictly convex as a function of time
t
:

\textstyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\|q\|^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle q,p\right\rangle=\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{m}-\frac{\alpha_{n}Z}{\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}=2\widehat{H}(q,p)+\frac{2Z}{n\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}>\frac{Z}{n\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}>\frac{Z}{n\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}},

(2.7)

using Definition (
2.5
) in the last two inequalities.

So if
t\mapsto\|q(t)\|
is strictly increasing on
(t_{-},t_{+})
, then

\lim_{t\searrow t_{-}}q(t)=0
and

\varepsilon^{2}\geq\lim_{t\nearrow t_{+}}\|q(t_{+})\|^{2}\geq\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}(t-t_{-})\textstyle\frac{2Z}{n\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}}\,\mathrm{d}t=(t_{+}-t_{-})^{2}\frac{Z}{n\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}}\,.

(2.8)

Equation (
2.8
) leads to the upper bound

\varepsilon^{2-1/n}\sqrt{n/Z}
for total time
t_{+}-t_{-}
,
and
\lim_{t\searrow t_{-}}q(t)=0
implies collision at
t_{-}
.

By reversibility of the flow, the strictly decreasing case is similar.

\bullet

Otherwise there is a
t_{0}\in(t_{-},t_{+})
, with

\big(q(t_{0}),p(t_{0})\big)\in\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
.
Then (
2.8
) implies

\varepsilon^{2}\geq(t_{\pm}-t_{0})^{2}\frac{Z}{n\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}}
,
q(t_{0})
is a pericenter,
and the only time of intersection with
\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
. In both cases we get
the upper bound
2\,\varepsilon^{2-1/n}\sqrt{n/Z}
for
t_{+}-t_{-}
.

\bullet

For a collision solution in
\widehat{P}
, by preservation of total energy
\widehat{H}
,
in the limit of collision time

\|p\|^{2}/(2m)\sim\frac{Z}{\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}
.
But the condition
\widehat{H}(q,p)>\frac{-Z}{2n\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}

in (
2.5
) is equivalent to

\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{2m}>(1-\frac{1}{2n})\frac{Z}{\|q\|^{\alpha_{n}}}
.

So the collision solution intersects
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
.

\Box

2.2.2
Hamiltonian and angular momentum

We now regularise the motion by introducing new canonical coordinates
on
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
and then extending
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
to
U^{\varepsilon}\subseteq P
.
These coordinates will be shown to be real-analytic and, taken together, will
rectify the flow.

Total energy and angular momentum are treated first. They
are real-analytic from the outset.
We denote the restriction of the real-analytic
Hamiltonian
(
1.1
)
to
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
by
\widehat{H}
, too.

Angular momentum

\hat{L}:\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}\to\Lambda^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

equals
\hat{L}(q,p)=q\wedge p
. As a function with values in the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{so}(d,{\mathbb{R}})\cong\Lambda^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
it has entries

\hat{L}_{i,j}(q,p)=q_{j}p_{i}-q_{i}p_{j}\,,

and we set

\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}:\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}\to[0,\infty)\quad\mbox{,}\quad\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\rm tr}\big(\widehat{L}\widehat{L}^{*}\big)\,.

(2.10)

Thus
\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}
generalises the squared norm of the
angular momentum vector from
d=3
to arbitrary dimensions.
Both
\hat{L}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}
are real-analytic.

Depending on the value
E
of the energy, we can bound the
image of
\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}
in (
2.10
) from above.
If
\|q\|=\varepsilon
, then
\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}(q,p)
is maximal
if
\langle p,q\rangle=0
and then takes the value

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|^{2}\varepsilon^{2}
.
In terms of
E=\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{2m}-U_{n}(\varepsilon)
we conclude that then

\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}(q,p)=m(E+U_{n}(\varepsilon))\varepsilon^{2}
.
For
E<0
,
\|q\|=\varepsilon
may lie outside Hill’s region,
but still the upper bound
\hat{\mathfrak{L}}^{2}(q,p)\leq m(E+U_{n}(\varepsilon))\varepsilon^{2}
is valid.

2.2.3
Time as a phase space variable

Let
\widehat{T}:\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the

was at its pericenter in
\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
, respectively since collision. By (

)
there is only one such pericenter or collision of the orbit.
So the phase space function
\widehat{T}
is defined.

\bullet

It is obvious that
\widehat{T}
is

on the open subset of
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}

where the angular momentum does not vanish. This follows from transversality
of the real-analytic flow to the real-analytic hypersurface

.

\bullet

To see that
\widehat{T}
has this property everywhere, including on collision orbits,
we first consider dimension

.
The lift of the domain
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
defined in (

)
via

from (

) equals

\mathbf{\widehat{U}}^{\varepsilon}:=\widehat{\Pi}^{-1}(\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon})=\big\{(Q,P)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\times{\mathbb{C}}\ \mid\|P\|^{2}>2m(1-\tfrac{1}{2n})Z\big\}\,.

The defining equation is independent of

, and we add the points with

,
obtaining

.

\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\varepsilon}:=\widehat{\Pi}^{-1}\big(\hat{S}^{\varepsilon}\big)\subseteq\mathbf{\widehat{U}}^{\varepsilon}

As

, we can drop the non-vanishing

Noticing that the factor

Its restriction to
\mathbf{\widehat{U}}^{\varepsilon}
is the lift

This shows that
\widehat{T}
is real-analytic, since the Jacobi-Maupertuis
metric

, see (

), is real-analytic in
the position

and the parameter

.

\bullet

The generalisation to arbitrary dimensions

is immediate, since
\widehat{T}

is invariant under the symplectic lift of the

action on
\widehat{M}

to
\widehat{P}
.

2.5 Example

(Laplace-Runge-Lenz for the Kepler problem)

For

this flow-invariant, real-analytic

Note that for

2.8 Lemma

(symplectic structure)

For all

is a diffeomorphism onto its image.
On the completed phase space region

for all

Proof of Lemma
2.8
:

\bullet

We noticed that
\widehat{\Psi}
from (
2.19
) is real-analytic.
As a by-product of the calculation of the
Poisson brackets, it is a local diffeomorphism.
\widehat{\Psi}
is injective, since
the vectors
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
determine the plane or line of motion in
configuration space, and then energy, time to and direction of the pericenter
together with (scalar) angular momentum allow to reconstruct the particle position
on the trajectory. So
\widehat{\Psi}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

\bullet

Along solutions of the Hamiltonian equations
\widehat{T}
is their time parameter,
up to an additive constant. So
\{\widehat{H},\widehat{T}\}=1
.

As
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
are constants of the motion, their
Poisson brackets with
\widehat{H}
vanish.

\bullet

To show that
\{\widehat{A}_{i},\widehat{A}_{j}\}=0
, like in Remark
2.9

we need only consider the case of
d=2
dimensions.
Then
\widehat{A\,}_{1}^{2}+\widehat{A\,}_{2}^{2}=1
, so that

\{\widehat{A}_{1},\widehat{A}_{2}\}=0
.

\bullet

The Poisson brackets
\{\widehat{T},\widehat{A}_{i}\}
vanish for the
following reason: The Hamiltonian vector field of the function

(q,p)\mapsto\left\langle p,q\right\rangle

is tangential to the hypersurface

\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}=\widehat{T}^{-1}(0)
. It acts as a dilation and thus does
not change the vector
\widehat{A}
on
\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
. It follows that

\{\widehat{T},\widehat{A}_{i}\}
vanishes on
\widehat{S}^{\varepsilon}
.
On the other hand

\frac{d}{dt}\{\widehat{T},\widehat{A}_{i}\}=\big\{\widehat{H},\{\widehat{T},\widehat{A}_{i}\}\big\}=0
,
using the Jacobi identity,
\{\widehat{H},\widehat{T}\}=1
and

\{\widehat{H},\widehat{A}_{i}\}=0
.

\bullet

Since
\widehat{A}
transforms like a vector,

\{\widehat{L}_{r,m},\widehat{A}_{n}\}=\delta_{m,n}\widehat{A}_{r}-\delta_{r,n}\widehat{A}_{m}
.
So the Poisson brackets
\{\widehat{A}_{i},\widehat{B}_{j}\}
equal

\delta_{i,j}-\widehat{A}_{i}\widehat{A}_{j}
.

\bullet

The commutation relations for angular momentum are

\big\{\widehat{L}_{i,j},\widehat{L}_{k,\ell}\big\}=\delta_{i,\ell}\widehat{L}_{j,k}-\delta_{i,k}\widehat{L}_{j,\ell}-\delta_{j,\ell}\widehat{L}_{i,k}+\delta_{j,k}\widehat{L}_{i,\ell}\,.

(2.24)

To show that

\{\widehat{B}_{i},\widehat{B}_{j}\}=\widehat{L}_{i,j}
, one substitutes the definition
(
2.16
) of
\widehat{B}
, uses (
2.24
) and
\|\widehat{A}\|=1
.

By real-analyticity of the variables and the symplectic form,
and since
\widehat{U}^{\varepsilon}
is dense in
U^{\varepsilon}
,
we can omit the hats in the Poisson brackets.

\bullet

The flow of the Hamiltonian system

(P,\omega,H)
is complete, since the only incomplete trajectories of

(\widehat{P},\widehat{\omega},\widehat{H})
are the collision trajectories.

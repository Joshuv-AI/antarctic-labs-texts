]
,

]
and

]
for example. For the Hardy spaces in the upper half plane

3
Preliminaries to the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})

We first consider the point-evaluation of functions in the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
or
[
16
]
)

If
f
is
\lambda
-harmonic in
{\mathbb{D}}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, then its integral mean
r\mapsto M_{p}(f;r)
is nondecreasing over
[0,1)
; and if
f
is
\lambda
-analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
and
p_{0}\leq p<1
, then
M_{p}(f;r^{\prime})\leq 2^{2/p-1}M_{p}(f;r)
for
0\leq r^{\prime}<r<1
.

Lemma 3.2

Let
p_{0}\leq p<\infty
and let
w
be a nonzero weight function. If
f\in A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
, then

M_{p}(f;r)=o\left(\left(\int_{r}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\right)
as
r\rightarrow 1-
, and

M_{p}(f;r)\lesssim\left(\int_{r}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}},\qquad 0\leq r<1.

Proof.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle M_{p}(f;r)^{p}\int_{r}^{1}w(s)ds\lesssim\int_{r}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds,\qquad r\in[0,1).

It remains to show that for
r\in[0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds\lesssim\int_{r}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)s^{2\lambda+1}ds.

(17)

Choose fixed
r_{0}\in(0,1)
so that
\int_{r_{0}}^{1}w(s)ds>0
. For
r\in[r_{0},1)
, (
17
) is obvious, and for
r\in[0,r_{0})
,
since

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{r_{0}}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds\lesssim M_{p}(f;r_{0})^{p}\int_{r}^{r_{0}}w(s)ds\lesssim\frac{\int_{r}^{r_{0}}w(s)ds}{\int_{r_{0}}^{1}w(s)ds}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds,

it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds\lesssim\left(\frac{\int_{r}^{r_{0}}w(s)ds}{\int_{r_{0}}^{1}w(s)ds}+1\right)\int_{r_{0}}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)s^{2\lambda+1}ds,

which concludes (
17
).

Lemma 3.3

(
[
18
, Theorem 4.10]
)

Let
p\geq p_{0}
and
f\in H_{\lambda}^{p}({\mathbb{D}})
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\lesssim\frac{(1-|z|)^{-1/p}}{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda/p}}\|f\|_{H_{\lambda}^{p}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Theorem 3.4

Let
p_{0}\leq p<\infty
and let
w
be a nonzero weight function. If
f\in A_{\lambda,w}^{p}({\mathbb{D}})
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\lesssim\frac{(1-|z|)^{-1/p}}{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda/p}}\left(\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(18)

Proof.

For
0<\rho<1
set
f_{\rho}(z)=f(\rho z)
. We apply Lemma
3.3
to
f_{\rho}
to get

\displaystyle|f(\rho se^{i\theta})|\lesssim\frac{(1-s)^{-1/p}}{|1-s^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda/p}}\|f_{\rho}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{p}},\qquad\rho,s\in[0,1),

but from (
16
) and Lemma
3.2
,

\|f_{\rho}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{p}}\lesssim M_{p}(f;\rho)\lesssim\left(\int_{\rho}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}.

Combining the above two estimates and letting
\rho=s=r^{1/2}
for
r\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|\lesssim\frac{(1-r^{1/2})^{-1/p}}{|1-re^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda/p}}\left(\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}},\qquad z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}},

that is identical with (
18
) in view of the fact

\displaystyle|1-z^{2}|\asymp 1-r+|\sin\theta|\asymp|1-re^{2i\theta}|,\qquad z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(19)

Next we turn to the completeness of the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
18
, Lemma 5.4]
)

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a sequence of
\lambda
-analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
\{f_{n}\}
converges uniformly on each compact subset of
{\mathbb{D}}
, then its limit function is also
\lambda
-analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Theorem 3.6

Let
p_{0}\leq p<\infty
and let
w
be a nonzero radial weight function. Then the space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
is complete.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
. For
r\in(0,1)
, it follows from Theorem
3.4
that

\displaystyle|f_{m}(z)-f_{n}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-r)^{(2\lambda+1)/p}}\left(\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{-1/p}\|f_{m}-f_{n}\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}},\qquad z\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r},

which shows that
\{f_{n}\}
converges to a function
f
uniformly on each compact subset of
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Lemma
3.5
asserts that
f
is
\lambda
-analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
. The locally uniform convergence implies, for
r\in[0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}|f_{n}(z)-f(z)|^{p}w(|z|)d\sigma_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}|f_{n}(z)-f_{m}(z)|^{p}w(|z|)d\sigma_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|^{p}_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}},

so that
\|f_{n}-f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}\leq\liminf\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}
. Hence
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
is complete.

The above theorem shows that for a radial weight function
w
, the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
is a Banach space when
1\leq p<\infty
, and a Fréchet space with the quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|^{p}_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}
when
p_{0}\leq p<1
.

Finally we come to the density of
\lambda
-analytic polynomials in the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman spaces
f\in A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
. For a
\lambda
-analytic function
f
on
{\mathbb{D}}
, we denote by
S_{n}:=S^{f}_{n}(z)
the
n
th partial sum of the series (
12
).

Lemma 3.7

(
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
)

If
f(z)
is
\lambda
-analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
, then for fixed
0<r_{0}<1
,
S_{n}(z)
converges uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r_{0}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{0}}=\{z:\,|z|<r_{0}\}
.

Theorem 3.8

Let
p_{0}\leq p<\infty
and let
w
be a nonzero weight function. Then the set of
\lambda
-analytic polynomials is dense in the
\lambda
-Bergman space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
; and in particular, the system

\displaystyle\left\{1,\,\,\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(z)/\|\phi_{n}\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{2}},\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}

is an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space
A^{2}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
, where

\|\phi_{n}\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{2}}=\left(2(\lambda+1)\int_{0}^{1}s^{2n+2\lambda+1}w(s)ds\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

For
f\in A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
, set
f_{s}(z)=f(sz)
for
0\leq s<1
.
We assert that
\|f_{s}-f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}\rightarrow 0
as
s\rightarrow 1-
, namely,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 1-}\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}(f_{s}-f;r)^{p}\,w(r)r^{2\lambda+1}dr=0.

(20)

Indeed, applying (
15
) yields
\lim_{s\rightarrow 1-}M_{p}(f_{s}-f;r)=0
for
0\leq r<1
(indeed, for all
p\geq p_{0}
and fixed
r
this follows from the uniform continuity of
f
on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
), and for all
0\leq r,s<1
,
M_{p}(f_{s}-f;r)\lesssim M_{p}(f;rs)+M_{p}(f;r)\lesssim M_{p}(f;r)
by Lemma
3.1
. Thus (
20
) follows immediately by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. In addition, by Lemma
3.7
, for fixed
s\in(0,1)

\|(S_{n})_{s}-f_{s}\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus the density in Theorem
3.8
is proved.
The last assertion in the theorem follows from the fact
\|\phi_{n}\|_{L_{\lambda}^{2}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}=1
.

4
Operators mapping
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
into a general Banach space

4.1
The main theorems and lemmas

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space and
T
a linear operator mapping a
\lambda
-analytic polynomial into an element in
X
. In this section, we give a characterization of the boundedness of the operator
T
from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
X
by the behaviour of a single vector-valued
\lambda
-analytic function related to
T
.

We define the function
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\zeta)
for
|z\zeta|<1
, called the
(\lambda,\alpha)
-Bergman kernel, by

\displaystyle K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\zeta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(n+\lambda+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)\Gamma(n+\lambda+1)}\phi_{n}(z)\phi_{n}(\zeta).

(21)

It is obvious that
K_{\lambda,-1}(z,\overline{\zeta})
is identical with the
\lambda
-Cauchy kernel
C(z,\zeta)
, and
K_{\lambda,0}(z,\overline{\zeta})
with the
\lambda
-Bergman kernel
K_{\lambda}(z,\zeta)
defined in
[
18
]
.

Letting
x_{n}=T\phi_{n}
(
\in X
) for
n=0,1,\cdots
, we define

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(n+\lambda+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)\Gamma(n+\lambda+1)}\,x_{n}\phi_{n}(z),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(22)

Our main theorems in this section are stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1

Let
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1
and
0<q<\infty
, and let
w
be a nonzero weight function satisfying the condition (
3
). If
F_{\lambda,\alpha}
is an
X
-valued
\lambda
-analytic function and satisfies

\displaystyle\|F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)\|_{X}^{p}\lesssim\frac{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-|z|)^{(\alpha+2)p-1}}\int_{|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}w(s)ds,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

(23)

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{q+2\lambda+1}{p}-2\lambda-2,

(24)

then
T
can be extended to a bounded operator from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
X
.

Theorem 4.2

Let
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1
and
0<q<\infty
, and let
w
be a weight function satisfying the condition (
4
). If the operator
T
has a bounded extension from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
X
, then
F_{\lambda,\alpha}
is an
X
-valued
\lambda
-analytic function and satisfies (
23
) with
\alpha
given by (
24
).

Combining Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3

Let
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1
and
0<q<\infty
, and let
w
be a nonzero weight function satisfying the conditions (
3
) and (
4
). Then
T
can be extended to a bounded operator from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
X
if and only if
F_{\lambda,\alpha}
is an
X
-valued
\lambda
-analytic function and satisfies (
23
) with
\alpha
given by (
24
).

Remark.
We point out that the number
1/2
on the right hand side of (
23
) could be replaced by arbitrary fixed number
\delta\in(0,1)
for
p_{0}\leq p<1
, and by
1
for
p=1
; and in particular one may choose
\delta=p
for all
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1
. This could be seen from the proofs of these theorems below. We have to use such a choice in Section 9 for characterizing sequence multipliers on the weighted Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Lemmas
4.4
and
4.6
below are the key in the proof of the above three theorems.

The
p
-integral means
M_{\tau,p}(f;r)
, involving the parameter
\tau\geq 0
, of functions
f
in the
\lambda
-Hardy space
H_{\lambda}^{p}({\mathbb{D}})
is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\tau,p}(f;r)=\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau p}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right]^{1/p},\qquad r\in[0,1).

(25)

Lemma 4.4

Let
p_{0}\leq p<1
,
\delta=p^{-1}-1
and
0\leq\tau\leq\delta
. Then for
f\in H_{\lambda}^{p}({\mathbb{D}})
,

\displaystyle M_{\tau,1}(f;r)\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\|f\|_{H_{\lambda}^{p}},\qquad r\in[0,1).

The proof of the lemma is postponed to the next section (see Corollary
5.4
).

Lemma 4.5

For
0<p\leq 1
and
\alpha>(2\lambda+1)(p^{-1}-1)-1
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\frac{|1-s^{2}z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-s|z|)^{(2+\alpha)p-1}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},\,\,\,s\in[0,1).

The proof of Lemma
4.5
is postponed to Sections six and seven (see Theorem
7.5
), but we now use it to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
0<q<\infty
, and let
w
be a weight function satisfying the condition (
4
). Then

\displaystyle\|{K_{\lambda,\alpha}}(z,\cdot)\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\frac{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-|z|)^{(2+\alpha)p-1}}\int_{|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}w(s)ds,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

(26)

where
\alpha
is given by (
24
).

Proof.

Note that for
q>0
, (
24
) implies
\alpha>(2\lambda+1)(p^{-1}-1)-1
, and then, by Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w(s)\frac{|1-s^{2}z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-s|z|)^{(2+\alpha)p-1}}\,ds.

Since
(2+\alpha)p-1=q+2\lambda(1-p)
and

\displaystyle|1-s^{2}z^{2}|\asymp 1-rs+|\sin\theta|\asymp 1-s+1-r+|\sin\theta|,\qquad z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}},\,\,s\in[0,1],

(27)

we have

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w(s)\frac{(1-s+1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-sr)^{q+2\lambda(1-p)}}\,ds,\qquad z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Furthermore, dividing the above integral into two parts gives

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)\|_{A_{\lambda,\sigma}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{1/2}}w(s)\frac{(1-s+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-s)^{q+2\lambda(1-p)}}\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-r)^{q+2\lambda(1-p)}}\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)\,ds.

(28)

Since
(1-s+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p)}\asymp(1-s)^{2\lambda(1-p)}+|\sin\theta|^{2\lambda(1-p)}
, the first integral above is dominated by
a multiple of

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{|\sin\theta|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-r)^{2\lambda(1-p)}}\right)\int_{0}^{r^{1/2}}\frac{w(s)}{(1-s)^{q}}\,ds.

Substituting this into (
4.1
) and applying (
4
) yields

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)\|_{A_{\lambda,\sigma}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-r)^{q+2\lambda(1-p)}}\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)\,ds,\qquad z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}},

that is identical with (
26
) on account of (
27
) with
s=1
.

4.2
Proofs of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By the density theorem, Theorem
3.8
, it suffices to show that
\|Tf\|_{X}\leq C\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}
for all
\lambda
-analytic polynomials under the conditions (
3
) and (
23
).

Assume that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}c_{n}\phi_{n}(z)
. It follows that
Tf=\sum_{n=0}^{m}c_{n}x_{n}
, where
x_{n}=T\phi_{n}
for
n=0,1,\cdots
. We now express
Tf
into an integral in terms of
F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)
.

Since, using the notation
\tau_{n}
given by (
32
),

\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)}\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha}r^{n+\lambda}dr=\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}r^{2n+2\lambda+1}dr,

we have

\displaystyle Tf=\sum_{n=0}^{m}c_{n}x_{n}=\frac{2\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}\tau_{n}c_{n}x_{n}r^{2n+2\lambda+1}\right)dr.

But by the orthogonality,

\displaystyle\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}f(re^{i\theta})F_{\lambda,\alpha}(re^{-i\theta})\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\tau_{n}c_{n}x_{n}r^{2n},

and hence

\displaystyle Tf=\frac{2\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{1}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}f(re^{i\theta})F_{\lambda,\alpha}(re^{-i\theta})(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}r^{2\lambda+1}\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta)dr.

Now applying the condition (
23
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{X}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|\|F_{\lambda,\alpha}(re^{-i\theta})\|_{X}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}r^{2\lambda+1}\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|\frac{|1-r^{2}e^{-2i\theta}|^{2\lambda(p^{-1}-1)}}{(1-r)^{\alpha+2-p^{-1}}}\left(\int_{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{1/p}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}r^{2\lambda+1}\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2\lambda+1}}{(1-r)^{2-p^{-1}}}\left(\int_{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{1/p}M_{p^{-1}-1,1}(f;r)dr,

(29)

where
M_{p^{-1}-1,1}(f;r)
is given by (
25
).

Again set
f_{\rho}(z)=f(\rho z)
for
0\leq\rho<1
. We apply Lemma
4.4
to
f_{\rho}
with
\tau=p^{-1}-1
to get

\displaystyle M_{p^{-1}-1,1}(f_{\rho};s)\lesssim(1-s)^{1-p^{-1}}\|f_{\rho}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{p}},\qquad s\in[0,1).

Choosing
\rho=s=r^{1/2}
for
r\in[0,1)
and appealing to (
16
) gives

\displaystyle\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}|1-re^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda(p^{-1}-1)}|f(re^{i\theta})|\,dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\lesssim(1-r)^{1-p^{-1}}M_{p}(f;r^{1/2}),\qquad r\in[0,1),

which shows, on account of (
19
),
M_{p^{-1}-1,1}(f;r)\lesssim(1-r)^{1-p^{-1}}M_{p}(f;r^{1/2})
for
r\in[0,1)
. Inserting this into (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2\lambda+1}}{1-r}\left(\int_{r^{1/2}}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{1/p}M_{p}(f;r^{1/2})dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2\lambda+1}}{1-t}\left(\int_{t}^{1}w(s)ds\right)^{1/p}M_{p}(f;t)dt.

If we set

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)=\sup_{0\leq t<1}M_{p}(f;t)^{p}\int_{t}^{1}w(s)ds,

then

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{X}\lesssim\Lambda(f)^{p^{-1}-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2\lambda+1}}{1-t}\left(\int_{t}^{1}w(s)ds\right)M_{p}(f;t)^{p}\,dt.

(30)

By Lemma
3.1
and (
17
), one has

\displaystyle\Lambda(f)\lesssim\sup_{0\leq t<1}\int_{t}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)ds\lesssim\sup_{0\leq t<1}\int_{t}^{1}M_{p}(f;s)^{p}w(s)s^{2\lambda+1}ds\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p};

and using the condition (
3
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2\lambda+1}}{1-t}\left(\int_{t}^{1}w(s)ds\right)M_{p}(f;t)^{p}\,dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}(f;t)^{p}\,w(t)t^{2\lambda+1}dt\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p}.

Substituting the two estimates above into (
30
) gives

\|Tf\|_{X}\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{1-p}\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}^{p}=\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,w}^{p}}
.
In view of the density of
\lambda
-analytic polynomials in
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
, the proof of Theorem
4.1
is finished.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Assume that the operator
T
has a bounded extension from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
X
. For
M>N\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=N}^{M}\tau_{n}x_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\|T\left(\sum_{n=N}^{M}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\phi_{n}(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\left\|\sum_{n=N}^{M}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\phi_{n}(\cdot)\right\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

(31)

where

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(n+\lambda+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\lambda+\alpha+2)\Gamma(n+\lambda+1)}\asymp(n+1)^{\alpha+1}.

(32)

However from (
7
),
\|\phi_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}^{p}\lesssim(n+1)^{\lambda p}
, and so

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=N}^{M}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\phi_{n}(\cdot)\right\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=N}^{M}\tau_{n}^{p}|\phi_{n}(z)|^{p}\|\phi_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{M}(n+1)^{(\alpha+2\lambda+1)p}|z|^{np},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{D}}.

In view of completeness of the space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
,
this shows that for
|z|<1
, the series
\zeta\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\phi_{n}(\zeta)
converges in the space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
, namely, for
|z|<1
the
(\lambda,\alpha)
-Bergman kernel
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)
defined in (
21
) is in
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
; and moreover, in conjunction with (
4.2
), it is concluded that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tau_{n}x_{n}\phi_{n}(z)
defines an
X
-valued
\lambda
-analytic function of
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, denoted by
F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)
as in (
22
). Now letting
N=0
and
M\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)\right\|_{X}\leq\|T\|\left\|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\cdot)\right\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Direct application of Lemma
4.6
proves that
F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)
satisfies (
23
).
The proof of Theorem
4.2
is completed.

5
Estimation of the
p
-integral means
M_{\tau,p}(f;r)

We define the
p
-integral means
M_{\tau,p}(f;r)
involving the parameter
\tau\geq 0
by

\displaystyle M_{\tau,p}(f;r)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau p}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right]^{1/p},\qquad 0<p<\infty,

\displaystyle M_{\tau,\infty}(f;r)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\theta}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}|f(re^{i\theta})|.

We are going to give some estimates of the
p
-integral means
M_{\tau,p}(f;r)
of functions
f
in the
\lambda
-Hardy space
H_{\lambda}^{p}({\mathbb{D}})
. We first consider the case when
1\leq p\leq\infty
. In this case we shall work with the
\lambda
-Poisson integral
P(f;z)
of
f\in L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
. Similarly, for
\tau\geq 0
we define

\displaystyle M_{\tau,p}(f;r)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau p}|P(f;re^{i\theta})|^{p}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right]^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle M_{\tau,\infty}(f;r)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\theta}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}|P(f;re^{i\theta})|,

and in the same way,
M_{\tau,p}(d\nu;r)
for
d\nu\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
16
, Corollary 4.3]
)

For
\theta,\varphi\in[-\pi,\pi]
and
r\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\lesssim\frac{(1-r)(1-r+|\sin(\theta-\varphi)/2|)^{-2}}{(1-r+|\sin\theta|+|\sin(\theta-\varphi)/2|)^{2\lambda}}\ln\Big(\frac{|\sin(\theta-\varphi)/2|}{1-r}+2\Big).

Theorem 5.2

Let
1\leq p\leq\ell\leq\infty
,
\delta=p^{-1}-\ell^{-1}
and
0\leq\tau\leq\delta
, and let
f\in L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
. Then

(i)
for
1\leq p\leq\ell\leq+\infty
,
M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}
for
r\in[0,1)
;

(ii)
for
1\leq p<\ell\leq+\infty
,

M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)=o\left((1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\right)
as
r\rightarrow 1-
;

(iii)
for
1<p<\ell\leq+\infty
,
p\leq k<+\infty
and
0\leq\tau<\delta
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{k\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau k-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{k}dr\right)^{1/k}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})};

(33)

(iv)
for
d\nu\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
,
1\leq\ell\leq+\infty
,
\delta=1-\ell^{-1}
and
0\leq\tau\leq\delta
,

M_{\tau,\ell}(d\nu;r)\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-\delta(1+2\lambda)}\|d\nu\|_{{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})},\qquad r\in[0,1).

Proof.

Choose
q
so that
q^{-1}=1-(p^{-1}-\ell^{-1})
. Then
1\leq q\leq p^{\prime}
, or equivalently
1\leq p\leq q^{\prime}\leq\infty
. For
r\in[0,1)
, define the operator
U_{r}
by

\displaystyle U_{r}f(\theta)=|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}P(f;re^{i\theta}),\qquad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi).

(34)

We shall prove

\displaystyle\|U_{r}f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\sup_{\theta,\varphi}\mid 1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}\mid^{2\lambda\tau}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{1-q^{-1}}.

(35)

At first, for
p_{1}=q^{\prime}
and
\ell_{1}=+\infty
, from (
13
), (
34
) and Hölder’s inequality one has

\displaystyle\|U_{r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q^{\prime}}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\sup_{\theta}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}\|P(re^{i\theta},\cdot)\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}.

Writing

\displaystyle\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{q}=\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{q-1}P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})

and noting that
\|P(re^{i\theta},\cdot)\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}=1
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\|U_{r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q^{\prime}}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\sup_{\theta,\varphi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{1-q^{-1}}.

(36)

Secondly, for
p_{2}=1
and
\ell_{2}=q
, it follows from (
13
) and Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle|P(f,re^{i\theta})|\leq\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\||f|^{\frac{1}{q}}P(re^{i\theta},\cdot)\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})},

and then, from (
34
) and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|U_{r}f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau q}\||f|^{\frac{1}{q}}P(re^{i\theta},\cdot)\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{q}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(e^{i\varphi})|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau q}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{q}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Again using
\|P(r\cdot,e^{i\varphi})\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}=1
, the inner integral above is dominated by

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta,\varphi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau q}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{q-1},

(37)

and hence

\displaystyle\|U_{r}f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{q}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\sup_{\theta,\varphi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{1-q^{-1}}.

(38)

Finally (
35
) is yielded by applying the Riesz-Thörin interpolation theorem to (
36
) and (
38
).

Now by Lemma
5.1
and on account of (
27
) with
s=1
, we have

\displaystyle P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\lesssim\frac{(1-r)^{-1}}{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda}}\asymp\frac{(1-r)^{-1}}{|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda}},\qquad\theta,\varphi\in[-\pi,\pi],\,\,\,r\in[0,1),

and so

\displaystyle|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{\delta}\lesssim\frac{(1-r)^{-\delta}}{|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda(\delta-\tau)}}\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}.

(39)

Inserting this into (
35
) and noting
1-q^{-1}=p^{-1}-\ell^{-1}=\delta
, part (i) of the theorem is proved.

Next we come to showing part (ii). For
\epsilon>0
, we write

f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}
is bounded and
\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}<\epsilon
. Since for
1\leq p<\ell\leq\infty
and
0\leq\tau\leq\delta
, one has
2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta\leq-\delta<0
,
there exists
\eta>0
such that
\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\epsilon
whenever

1-r<\eta
. Note that
M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)\leq M_{\tau,\ell}(f_{1};r)+M_{\tau,\ell}(f_{2};r)
, but by the definition of
M_{\tau,\ell}(f_{1};r)
and Proposition
2.5
(iv),

\displaystyle M_{\tau,\ell}(f_{1};r)\lesssim\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|P(f_{1};re^{i\theta})|^{\ell}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right]^{1/\ell}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\leq(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\epsilon.

Moreover by part (i) just proved,

M_{\tau,\ell}(f_{2};r)\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}<(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\epsilon.

Thus it is concluded that
M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)\lesssim(1-r)^{2\lambda\tau-(2\lambda+1)\delta}\epsilon
. Part (ii) is proved.

In order to prove part (iii), we fix
\ell\in(1,\infty)
and
\tau\geq 0
satisfying
\ell^{-1}+\tau<1
. Consider the operator
T
defined by, for
f\in L_{\lambda}^{1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
,

Tf(r)=(1-r)^{-2\lambda\tau-(1+2\lambda)/\ell}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r),\qquad r\in[0,1).

It is clear that
T
is quasi-linear. We are going to prove that for
1\leq p\leq 1/(\tau+\ell^{-1})
, the operator
T
is of weak type
(p,p)
from
L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
to the measure space
\left([0,1),d\nu\right)
with
d\nu=(1-r)^{2\lambda}dr
.

Since
1\leq p\leq 1/(\tau+\ell^{-1})
implies
0\leq\tau\leq p^{-1}-\ell^{-1}
, from part (i) it follows that

Tf(r)\leq c(1-r)^{-(1+2\lambda)/p}\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})},\qquad r\in[0,1).

Now for
s>0
,
\{r:\,\,Tf(r)>s\}\subseteq[1-s^{\prime},1)
, where
s^{\prime}=\left(c\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}/s\right)^{p/(1+2\lambda)}
, and then

\displaystyle\nu\left(\{r:\,\,Tf(r)>s\}\right)\leq\int_{1-s^{\prime}}^{1}(1-r)^{2\lambda}dr=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\left(\frac{c}{s}\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\right)^{p}.

Hence
T
is of weak type
(p,p)
for all
1\leq p\leq 1/(\tau+\ell^{-1})
.

By Marcinkiewicz’s interpolation theorem,
T
is of strong type
(p,p)
for all
1<p<1/(\tau+\ell^{-1})
, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{1}Tf(r)^{p}d\nu(r)\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{p}
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{p\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau p-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{p}dr\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{p}.

(40)

This is the case for
k=p
in (
33
).

For
p<k<\infty
, we write
(1-r)^{k\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau k-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{k}
into the form

\displaystyle\left[(1-r)^{\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)\right]^{k-p}(1-r)^{p\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau p-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{p}.

By part (i), the first factor above is dominated by a multiple of
\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{k-p}
, and so

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{k\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau k-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{k}dr\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{k-p}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{p\delta(2\lambda+1)-2\lambda\tau p-1}M_{\tau,\ell}(f;r)^{p}dr.

In view of (
40
), this is dominated by a multiple of
\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{k}
, and part (iii) is proved.

Part (iv) can be verified in a similar way to part (i). By

14

|P(d\nu;re^{i\theta})|^{\ell}\leq\|d\nu\|_{{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{\ell/\ell^{\prime}}c_{\lambda}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})|^{\ell}|\sin\varphi|^{2\lambda}\left|d\nu(\varphi)\right|.

Taking integration over
[-\pi,\pi]
with respect to

yields

M_{\tau,\ell}(d\nu;r)^{\ell}\leq\|d\nu\|_{{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}^{\ell/\ell^{\prime}}c_{\lambda}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau\ell}|P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})|^{\ell}dm_{\lambda}(\theta)\right]|\sin\varphi|^{2\lambda}\left|d\nu(\varphi)\right|.

The inner integral above is dominated by the quantity (
37
) with
\ell
instead of
q
, and so

\displaystyle M_{\tau,\ell}(d\nu;r)\leq\|d\nu\|_{{\mathfrak{B}}_{\lambda}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})}\sup_{\theta,\varphi}|1-r^{2}e^{2i\theta}|^{2\lambda\tau}\left[P(re^{i\theta},e^{i\varphi})\right]^{1-\ell^{-1}}.

Finally appealing to (
39
) with
\delta=1-1/\ell
proves part (iv) immediately.

(

and

p_{0}\leq p\leq\ell\leq\infty
,

Proof.

In this section we give a sharp estimate for the derivatives of the

-Bergman kernel

From
[

]

Lemma 6.1

(
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
)

For

, we have

\displaystyle|C(z,w)|\lesssim\frac{(|1-zw|+|1-z\bar{w}|)^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}|}\ln\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}+2\right).

(43)

To evaluate the derivatives of

Lemma 6.2

For
k=0,1,\cdots
and for
z=re^{i\theta}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k}\left[\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda+k}}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the following equality

\displaystyle\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k}\left[\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}\frac{a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j)}}+\sum_{j=[k/2]+1}^{k}\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k}a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j)}}

(44)

for
k=1,2,\cdots
, where
z=re^{i\theta}
,
w=se^{i\varphi}
, and
a_{k,j}
are some polynomials of one variable.

It is easy to find, for
z=re^{i\theta}
,

\displaystyle r\frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}\right]=\lambda\frac{1-|zw|^{2}-|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda+2}},

that can be written into the form (
44
) with
k=1
.

Assume that (
44
) holds for
k
. We are going to prove that it holds too for
k+1
instead of
k
.
From (
44
) we have

\displaystyle\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k+1}\left[\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}\frac{\tilde{a}_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j)}}+\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}(\lambda+j)\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j+1)}}

(45)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{j=[k/2]+1}^{k}\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k-1}\tilde{a}_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j)}}+\sum_{j=[k/2]+1}^{k}(\lambda+j)\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k+1}a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j+1)}},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k,j}(t)

\displaystyle=ta^{\prime}_{k,j}(t)-(\lambda+j)a_{k,j}(t),\qquad 0\leq j\leq[k/2],

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k,j}(t)

\displaystyle=(1-t^{2})\left[ta^{\prime}_{k,j}(t)-(\lambda+j)a_{k,j}(t)\right]-2(2j-k)t^{2}a_{k,j}(t),\qquad[k/2]+1\leq j\leq k.

If
k
is odd,
[k/2]=(k-1)/2
and
[(k+1)/2]=[k/2]+1=(k+1)/2
, and the third sum on the right hand side of (
45
) can be rewritten into

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{a}_{k,[k/2]}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+(k+1)/2)}}+\sum_{j=[(k+1)/2]+1}^{k}\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k-1}\tilde{a}_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+j)}}.

Combining this with the other sums on the right hand side of (
45
) proves (
44
) for
k+1
instead of
k
when
k
is odd.

If
k
is even,
[k/2]=[(k-1)/2]+1=k/2
and
[(k+1)/2]=[k/2]=k/2
, and the last term of the second sum on the right hand side of (
45
) can be written into

\displaystyle\left(\lambda+\frac{k}{2}\right)\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})a_{k,k/2}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(\lambda+(k/2)+1)}}.

The equality (
44
) for
k+1
instead of
k
is yielded by inserting this into (
45
) when
k
is even.

Let

\displaystyle A(r)=\frac{4({\rm Im}z)({\rm Im}w)}{|1-zw|^{2}},\qquad z=re^{i\theta}.

(46)

Lemma 6.3

For

and for

,
w=se^{i\varphi}
,

\displaystyle\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k}A(r)=A(r)\left[\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\frac{b_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2j}}+\sum_{j=[k/2]+1}^{k}\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k}a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2j}}\right],

(47)

where

,

are some polynomials of one variable.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of (

).
It is easy to find, for

\displaystyle r\frac{d}{dr}A(r)=A(r)\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}},

(48)

that can be written into the form (

) with

. The general case of (

) is proved by induction.

Assume that (

) holds for

. We are going to prove that it holds too for

.
Similarly to (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A(r)}\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k+1}A(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left[\frac{\tilde{a}_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2j}}+(j+1)\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2(j+1)}}\right]

(49)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=[k/2]+1}^{k}\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2j-k-1}}{|1-zw|^{2j}}\left[\tilde{a}_{k,j}(rs)+(j+1)\frac{(1-r^{2}s^{2})^{2}a_{k,j}(rs)}{|1-zw|^{2}}\right],

where

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k,j}(t)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k,j}(t)

\displaystyle=(1-t^{2})\left[ta^{\prime}_{k,j}(t)-ja_{k,j}(t)\right]-2(2j-k)t^{2}a_{k,j}(t),\qquad[k/2]+1\leq j\leq k.

By considering

, the expression on the right hand side of (

)

Lemma 6.4

Let
F
be a smooth function on
(-1,1)
, and
A=A(r):\,[0,1)\mapsto(-1,1)
a smooth mapping. Then
for
k=1,2,\cdots

\displaystyle\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k}F(A)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=k\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq k-j+1\end{subarray}}a^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)\partial_{A}^{j}F(A)

(50)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=2j\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq j+1\end{subarray}}b^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)(D_{r}A)^{k-2j}\,\partial_{A}^{k-j}F(A),

where

D_{r}=r(\partial/\partial_{r})
,
D^{0}_{r}A=1
,
\partial_{A}=d/dA
, and
a^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}
,
b^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}

Proof.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(A)=D_{r}A\cdot\frac{d}{dA}F(A),

(51)

so that (
50
) is true for
k=1
.

Assuming that (
50
) holds for
k
, one has

\displaystyle\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{k+1}F(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=k\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq k-j+1\end{subarray}}a^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)D_{r}A\,\partial_{A}^{j+1}F(A)

(52)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=k\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq k-j+1\end{subarray}}a^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}\left(D_{r}^{i_{\ell}}A\right)\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)\partial_{A}^{j}F(A)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=2j\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq j+1\end{subarray}}b^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)(D_{r}A)^{k+1-2j}\,\partial_{A}^{k+1-j}F(A)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=2j\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq j+1\end{subarray}}b^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)(k-2j)(D_{r}^{2}A)(D_{r}A)^{k-2j-1}\partial_{A}^{k-j}F(A)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}=2j\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq j+1\end{subarray}}b^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}\left(D_{r}^{i_{\ell}}A\right)\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j}}A\right)(D_{r}A)^{k-2j}\,\partial_{A}^{k-j}F(A).

If
k
is odd,
[k/2]=(k-1)/2
and
[(k+1)/2]=[k/2]+1=(k+1)/2
, and the last term in the fourth sum on the right hand side of (

) is

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{\frac{k+1}{2}}=k+1\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{\frac{k+1}{2}}\leq\frac{k+1}{2}+1\end{subarray}}\tilde{b}^{(k,\frac{k+1}{2})}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{\frac{k+1}{2}}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{\frac{k+1}{2}}}A\right)\partial_{A}^{\frac{k+1}{2}}F(A),

which is identical with the last term of the first sum on the right hand side of (

), with
k+1
instead of

and
j=(k+1)/2=[k/2]+1
,
and the remaining terms in the fourth sum on the right hand side of (

) can be rewritten into

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j+1}=2(j+1)\\
0\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j+1}\leq j+2\end{subarray}}\tilde{b}^{(k,j)}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{j+1}}D_{r}^{i_{1}}A\cdots D_{r}^{i_{j+1}}A\right)(D_{r}A)^{k+1-2(j+1)}\partial_{A}^{k+1-(j+1)}F(A)

), with

instead of

.
For the last sum on the right hand side of (

,
and then, treat its last term and remaining terms by the same way as above to arrive at similar assertions. It is easy to find that, the first two sums on the right hand side of (

instead of

) for

instead of

If

and

instead of

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{\frac{k}{2}}=k\\

Theorem 6.5

For
m=1,2,\cdots
and for
z=re^{i\theta},w\in{\mathbb{D}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{(|1-z\overline{w}|+|1-zw|)^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\overline{w}|}\left(\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{w}|^{m}}+\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{m}}\right).

(53)

Proof.

Case I:

|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}<2|1-zw|^{2}
.

For this case, it follows from (
10
) and the first equality in (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle C(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z\bar{w}}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}F(A(r)),\qquad|zw|<1,

where

\displaystyle F(A)={}_{2}\!F_{1}(\lambda,\lambda;\,2\lambda+1;\,A),

and
A=A(r)
is given by (
46
).
Since

\displaystyle 1-A(r)=\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}},

(54)

it follows that
-1<A(r)<1
for
|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}<2|1-zw|^{2}
, and
F(A(r))
is bounded.

With the notation
D_{r}=r(\partial/\partial_{r})
, Leibniz’s rule gives

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|\lesssim\sum_{j+\ell+k=m}\left|D_{r}^{j}\left[\frac{1}{1-z\bar{w}}\right]\right|\left|D_{r}^{\ell}\left[\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda}}\right]\right|\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|,\qquad|zw|<1.

By induction, one has

D_{r}^{j}\left[\frac{1}{1-z\bar{w}}\right]=\sum_{\tau=1}^{j+1}\frac{a_{j,\tau}}{(1-z\bar{w})^{\tau}}.

Appealing to this and Lemma
6.2
, we get

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|\lesssim\sum_{j+\ell+k=m}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{j+1}}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda+\ell}}\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|,\qquad|zw|<1.

(55)

Since (cf.
[
13
, 2-1(7)]
)

\displaystyle\partial_{A}F(A)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\lambda+1}\,{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda+1,\lambda+1;2\lambda+2;A\right)

(56)

and

\displaystyle{}_{2}\!F_{1}(a,b;a+b;t)\asymp\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-t}+2\right),\qquad-1\leq t<1,

(57)

from (
48
), (
51
) and (
54
) we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|1-zw|}\ln\left(\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}+2\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{w}|},\qquad z=re^{i\theta},

(58)

where the last inequality is based on the fact
s\ln\left(s^{-2}+2\right)\lesssim 1
for
s\in(0,2]
.
Thus from (
55
) we get

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|1-zw|^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}+\frac{|1-zw|^{-2\lambda-1}}{|1-z\bar{w}|}+\frac{|1-zw|^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}|}\left|D_{r}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|1-zw|^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}},\qquad|zw|<1,

so that (
53
) is true for
m=1
and for
|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}<2|1-zw|^{2}
.

In order to show (
53
) for
m\geq 2
, at first by Lemma
6.3
,
\left|D_{r}^{k}A(r)\right|\lesssim|1-zw|^{-k}
, and then, by Lemma
6.4
and (
48
),

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left|\partial_{A}^{j}F(A)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2j}}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}\right)^{k-2j}\left|\partial_{A}^{k-j}F(A)\right|.

(59)

For
j\geq 2
, again by
[
13
, 2-1(7)]
) we have

\partial_{A}^{j}F(A)=\frac{(\lambda)_{j}(\lambda)_{j}}{(2\lambda+1)_{j}}{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda+j,\lambda+j;2\lambda+j+1;A\right),

and further, by
[
13
, 2-1(23)]
),

\displaystyle\partial_{A}^{j}F(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda)_{j}(\lambda)_{j}}{(2\lambda+1)_{j}}(1-A)^{1-j}{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda+1,\lambda+1;2\lambda+j+1;A\right)

\displaystyle\asymp(1-A)^{1-j}.

Thus for
k\geq 2
, from (
54
), (
56
), (
57
) and (
6
) we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[F(A(r))\right]\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{k}}\left[\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}\right)^{1-[k/2]}+\ln\left(\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}+2\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2j}}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}\right)^{k-2j}\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}\right)^{j+1-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{k}}\left[\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|}{|1-zw|}\right)^{2-k}+\ln\left(\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}+2\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|}\right)^{k-2j}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}.

(60)

Applying (
58
) and (
6
) to (
55
) yields

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j+\ell+k=m}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{j+1}}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{2\lambda+\ell}}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|1-zw|^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{m+1}},\qquad|zw|<1.

Thus (
53
) is proved for
|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}<2|1-zw|^{2}
.

Case II:

|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}\geq 2|1-zw|^{2}
.

For this case, it follows from (
10
) and the second equality in (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle C(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z\bar{w}}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2\lambda}}\tilde{F}(\tilde{A}(r)),\qquad|zw|<1,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\tilde{A})={}_{2}\!F_{1}\Big({\lambda,\lambda+1\atop 2\lambda+1};\tilde{A}\Big),\qquad\tilde{A}:=\tilde{A}(r)=-\frac{4({\rm Im}z)({\rm Im}w)}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}},\qquad z=re^{i\theta}.

Since

\displaystyle 1-\tilde{A}(r)=\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}},

(61)

it follows that
1/2\leq\tilde{A}(r)<1
for
|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}\geq 2|1-zw|^{2}
, and from (
57
),

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\tilde{A}(r))\asymp\ln\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}+2\right),\qquad z=re^{i\theta},\,w\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(62)

Similarly to (
55
), one has

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|\lesssim\sum_{j+\ell+k=m}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2\lambda+j+\ell+1}}\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[\tilde{F}(\tilde{A}(r))\right]\right|,\qquad|zw|<1.

(63)

Again by Lemma
6.3
,
\left|D_{r}^{k}\tilde{A}(r)\right|\lesssim|1-z\bar{w}|^{-k}
, and then, by Lemma
6.4
and (
48
),

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[\tilde{F}(\tilde{A}(r))\right]\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{[k/2]}\left|\partial_{\tilde{A}}^{j}\tilde{F}(\tilde{A})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2j}}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}\right)^{k-2j}\left|\partial_{\tilde{A}}^{k-j}\tilde{F}(\tilde{A})\right|.

(64)

For
j\geq 1
, also by
[
13
, 2-1(7) and 2-1(23)]
) we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{A}}^{j}\tilde{F}(\tilde{A})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda)_{j}(\lambda+1)_{j}}{(2\lambda+1)_{j}}{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda+j,\lambda+j+1;2\lambda+j+1;\tilde{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda)_{j}(\lambda+1)_{j}}{(2\lambda+1)_{j}}(1-\tilde{A})^{-j}{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda+1,\lambda;2\lambda+j+1;\tilde{A}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp(1-\tilde{A})^{-j}.

Thus for
k\geq 1
, from (
61
) and (
6
) we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{k}\left[\tilde{F}(\tilde{A}(r))\right]\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}\left(\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}\right)^{-[k/2]}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2j}}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}\right)^{k-2j}\left(\frac{|1-zw|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}\right)^{j-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{k}}\left(\frac{|1-zw|}{|1-z\bar{w}|}\right)^{-k}+\sum_{j=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\left(\frac{1-|zw|^{2}}{|1-zw|}\right)^{k-2j}\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{k}}.

Applying this and (
62
) to (
63
) yields

\displaystyle\left|D_{r}^{m}\left[C(z,w)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2\lambda+m+1}}\ln\left(\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}}{|1-zw|^{2}}+2\right)+\sum_{j+\ell+k=m}\frac{|1-zw|^{-k}}{|1-z\bar{w}|^{2\lambda+j+\ell+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|1-z\bar{w}|^{-2\lambda}}{|1-z\bar{w}||1-zw|^{m}},\qquad|zw|<1.

This proves (
53
) for
|1-z\bar{w}|^{2}\geq 2|1-zw|^{2}
. The proof of Theorem
6.5
is completed.

7
Estimates of the
(\lambda,\alpha)
-Bergman kernel
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\zeta)

In comparison to the
(\lambda,\alpha)
-Bergman kernel
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,w)
, we often use the following simpler form

\displaystyle h_{\lambda,\beta}(z,w)=\sum(n+1)^{\beta}\phi_{n}(z)\overline{\phi_{n}(w)}.

(65)

But in what follows we work with the following variant of
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,w)

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\lambda,\beta}(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+2\lambda+2)}{\Gamma(n+2\lambda+2)}\phi_{n}(z)\overline{\phi_{n}(w)}.

(66)

By means of (
7
), the series in defining
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,\zeta)
,
h_{\lambda,\beta}(z,w)
and
\tilde{K}_{\lambda,\beta}(z,w)

Theorem 7.5

For
0<p\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\frac{|1-s^{2}z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p)}}{(1-s|z|)^{(2+\alpha)p-1}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},\,\,\,s\in[0,1).

Proof.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
with
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. If
\theta\in[-\pi,0]
, the conclusion follows from the equality
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})=\overline{K_{\lambda,\alpha}(\bar{z},se^{-i\varphi})}
.
We first have

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|+|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{-i\varphi})|\right)^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi),

and then, by Theorem
7.4
, this is further dominated by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{w}|^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{1}{|1-zw|^{\alpha+2}}\right)^{p}\frac{dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)}{(|1-z\overline{w}|+|1-zw|)^{2\lambda p}},

where
w=se^{i\varphi}
.
Since for
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
,

\displaystyle|1-re^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{2}=1-2r\cos(\theta-\varphi)+r^{2}\leq|1-re^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|1-rse^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{-2\lambda p}}{|1-rse^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{(\alpha+2)p}}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi).

(77)

In what follows, we consider the case for
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
. If
\theta\in(\pi/2,\pi]
, the same procedure works by setting
\theta^{\prime}=\pi-\theta
and
\varphi^{\prime}=\pi-\varphi
since
K_{\lambda,\alpha}(re^{i\theta},se^{i\varphi})=\overline{K_{\lambda,\alpha}(re^{i\theta^{\prime}},se^{i\varphi^{\prime}})}
.

From (
77

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\frac{|1-rse^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{-2\lambda p}}{|1-rse^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{(\alpha+2)p}}+\frac{|1+rse^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{-2\lambda p}}{|1+rse^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{(\alpha+2)p}}\right)\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi),

and since, for

\displaystyle|1-re^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{2}\leq|1+re^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad|1-re^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{2}\leq|1+re^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{2},

it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{|1-rse^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{-2\lambda p}}{|1-rse^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{(\alpha+2)p}}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi).

Furthermore, since for
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi/2]

\displaystyle|1-re^{i(\theta-\varphi)}|^{2}\asymp(1-r+|\theta-\varphi|)^{2},\qquad|1-re^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|^{2}\asymp(1-r+\theta+\varphi)^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z,se^{i\varphi})|^{p}\,dm_{\lambda}(\varphi)\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{(1-rs+\theta+\varphi)^{-2\lambda p}\varphi^{2\lambda}}{(1-rs+|\theta-\varphi|)^{(\alpha+2)p}}\,d\varphi.

Now We split the last integral above into three parts, that are over
[0,\theta/2]

[\theta/2,3\theta/2]
and

the second part by

Collecting these estimates yields, for

8
) we have

\displaystyle(D_{z}\circ z)^{m}g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(n+\lambda+1)^{m}\phi_{n}(z),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Now using a similar expansion to (
75
), with
m-1
replacing
\alpha
and an appropriate large
M
, gives

\displaystyle G_{\lambda,m-1}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}\tilde{a}_{m-1,j}(D_{z}\circ z)^{m-j}g(z)+g_{M}(z),

where
g_{M}
is a bounded
\lambda
-analytic function on
{\mathbb{D}}
. The estimate (
81
) of
G_{\lambda,m-1}
follows from (
80
) and Corollary
8.3
immediately.

Conversely, if
G_{\lambda,m-1}
satisfies (
81
), one can obtain the estimate (
80
) for the function
g
by the same process. The proof of the theorem is finished.

8.2
Multiplication operators on
A^{p}_{\lambda,\alpha}({\mathbb{D}})
for
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1

For a given
\lambda
-analytic function
g
on
{\mathbb{D}}
, we consider the associated multiplication operator
T_{g}
defined by

T_{g}f(z)=g(z)f(z).

In this subsection we shall give a sufficient condition of
g
in terms of a Carleson type measure so that the multiplication operator
T_{g}
becomes a bounded mapping from the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman spaces
A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha}({\mathbb{D}})
into the unweighted space
L_{\lambda}^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{D}})

for
p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq 1\leq p_{2}<\infty
.

As usual, for given
\theta_{0}\in[0,2\pi)
and
0<h<1
define

S_{\theta_{0}}(h)=\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}:\,\,\,|z-e^{i\theta_{0}}|\leq 2h\}.

Definition 8.5

Let
d\mu
be a finite Borel measure on the disc
{\mathbb{D}}
. For
\lambda\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
, we say
d\mu
to be a
(\lambda,\alpha)
-Carleson measure if

\displaystyle\mu\left(S_{\theta}(h)\right)=O\left((h+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda}h^{\alpha}\right),\qquad 0\leq\theta<2\pi,\,\,0<h<1.

If
\alpha=1
,
d\mu
is called a
\lambda
-Carleson measure.

Theorem 8.6

Let
p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq 1\leq p_{2}<\infty
and
\alpha>0
.
If the
\lambda
-analytic function
g
on
{\mathbb{D}}
satisfies the condition that
d\mu_{g}(z):=|g(z)|^{p_{2}}d\sigma_{\lambda}(z)
is a
(\lambda p_{2}/p_{1},(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1})
-Carleson measure, then
the multiplication operator
T_{g}
is bounded from
A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha}({\mathbb{D}})
into
L_{\lambda}^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{D}})
.

Proof.

What we need to prove is
\|gf\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{D}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\lambda,\alpha}^{p_{1}}}
for
f\in A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha}({\mathbb{D}})
, that is,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}|f(z)|^{p_{2}}d\mu_{g}(z)\right)^{1/p_{2}}\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}|f(z)|^{p_{1}}(1-|z|)^{\alpha-1}d\sigma_{\lambda}(z)\right)^{1/p_{1}},\qquad f\in A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha}({\mathbb{D}}).

(82)

Again,
w(s)=(1-s)^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha>0
satisfies the conditions (
3
) and (
4
) for all
q>\alpha
. We take

q=(m+2\lambda+1)p_{1}-2\lambda-1,

where

m=\left[\frac{\alpha+2\lambda+1}{p_{1}}-2\lambda\right].

Obviously
q>\alpha
, and since
\left(T_{g}\phi_{n}\right)(z)=g(z)\phi_{n}(z)
, by Theorem
4.2
proving (
82
) is equivalent to showing

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}|K_{\lambda,m-1}(z,w)|^{p_{2}}d\mu_{g}(w)\right)^{1/p_{2}}\lesssim\frac{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda(p_{1}^{-1}-1)}}{(1-|z|)^{m+1-(\alpha+1)/p_{1}}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(83)

By Theorem
7.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}|K_{\lambda,m-1}(z,w)|^{p_{2}}d\mu_{g}(w)\lesssim J_{1}(z)+J_{2}(z),

(84)

where

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|1-z\overline{w}|^{-(m+1)p_{2}}\,d\mu_{g}(w)}{(|1-z\overline{w}|+|1-zw|)^{2\lambda p_{2}}},\qquad J_{2}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|1-zw|^{-(m+1)p_{2}}\,d\mu_{g}(w)}{(|1-z\overline{w}|+|1-zw|)^{2\lambda p_{2}}}.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
with
3/4\leq r<1
and define the sets
\{E_{k}\}
by
E_{-1}=\emptyset
, and for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle=S_{\theta}(2^{k}(1-r)).

Take a positive integer
N
so that
2^{N}(1-r)\geq 2
, and write

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{N}\int_{E_{k}\setminus E_{k-1}}\frac{|1-z\overline{w}|^{-(m+1)p_{2}}}{(|1-z\overline{w}|+|1-zw|)^{2\lambda p_{2}}}\,d\mu_{g}(w).

Since, with
w=se^{i\varphi}
,

\displaystyle|1-z\overline{w}|^{2}+|1-zw|^{2}

\displaystyle=2(1-rs)^{2}+4rs(1-\cos\theta\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(1-rs+|\sin\theta|+|\sin\varphi|\right)^{2},

we have

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\leq 1-r+|\sin\theta|}\int_{E_{k}\setminus E_{k-1}}\frac{|1-z\overline{w}|^{-(m+1)p_{2}}}{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}}}\,d\mu_{g}(w)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)>1-r+|\sin\theta|}\int_{E_{k}\setminus E_{k-1}}\frac{d\mu_{g}(w)}{|1-z\overline{w}|^{(m+1)p_{2}+2\lambda p_{2}}}.

Note that for
w\in E_{k}\setminus E_{k-1}
(
k\geq 1
),

|1-z\overline{w}|\geq 2^{k-1}(1-r),

and so

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1-r)^{-(m+1)p_{2}}}{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}}}\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\leq 1-r+|\sin\theta|}\frac{\mu_{g}\left(E_{k}\right)}{2^{(m+1)p_{2}k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)>1-r+|\sin\theta|}\frac{\mu_{g}\left(E_{k}\right)}{(2^{k}(1-r))^{(m+1)p_{2}+2\lambda p_{2}}}.

By the assumption that
d\mu_{g}(z):=|g(z)|^{p_{2}}d\sigma_{\lambda}(z)
is a
(\lambda p_{2}/p_{1},(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1})
-Carleson measure, it follows that

\mu_{g}\left(E_{k}\right)\lesssim(2^{k}(1-r)+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}/p_{1}}(2^{k}(1-r))^{(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1}},

and hence

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}(p_{1}^{-1}-1)}}{(1-r)^{(m+1)p_{2}-(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1}}}\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\leq 1-r+|\sin\theta|}\frac{1}{2^{[m+1-(\alpha+1)/p_{1}]p_{2}k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)>1-r+|\sin\theta|}\frac{(1-r)^{-[m+2\lambda+1-(\alpha+2\lambda+1)/p_{1}]p_{2}}}{2^{[m+2\lambda+1-(\alpha+2\lambda+1)/p_{1}]p_{2}k}}.

(85)

But since

\displaystyle m+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{p_{1}}\geq m+2\lambda+1-\frac{\alpha+2\lambda+1}{p_{1}}>0,

the first sum in (
8.2
) is bounded, and
the second sum is controlled by a multiple of

\displaystyle(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{-[m+2\lambda+1-(\alpha+2\lambda+1)/p_{1}]p_{2}}\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}(p_{1}^{-1}-1)}}{(1-r)^{(m+1)p_{2}-(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1}}}.

Thus we conclude

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda p_{2}(p_{1}^{-1}-1)}}{(1-r)^{(m+1)p_{2}-(\alpha+1)p_{2}/p_{1}}}.

Similarly one can get the same estimate for
J_{2}(z)
as above, and substituting these into (
84
) proves (
83
) in view of (
27
).

9
Sequence multipliers on the weighted Bergman spaces

In this section we consider the operators mapping the weighted
\lambda
-Bergman space
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
for
p_{0}\leq p\leq 1
into the sequence space
\ell^{s}
for
1\leq s<\infty
with norm
\|\{c_{n}\}\|_{\ell^{s}}:=\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}|c_{n}|^{s}\right)^{1/s}
. A given sequence
\eta=\{\eta_{n}\}
of complex numbers is said to a sequence multiplier from
A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
\ell^{s}
if
T_{\eta}f:=\{\eta_{n}a_{n}\}\in\ell^{s}
whenever
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(z)\in A^{p}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
. By the closed graph theorem, this is equivalent to the boundedness of the operator
T_{\eta}
, that is,

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\eta_{n}a_{n}|^{s}\right)^{1/s}\lesssim\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\lambda,w}}.

We shall need several lemmas.

Lemma 9.1

Let
w
be a weight function satisfying the condition (
4
) for some
q>0
. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{(1-rt)^{q}}dt\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-r)^{q}}\int_{r}^{1}w(s)ds,\qquad r\in(0,1).

Indeed, dividing the integral on the left hand side above into two parts over
[0,r]
and
[r,1)
respectively, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{(1-rt)^{q}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{r}\frac{w(t)}{(1-t)^{q}}dt+\frac{1}{(1-r)^{q}}\int_{r}^{1}w(s)ds.

The lemma follows by applying the condition (
4
) to the first integral on the right hand side.

Lemma 9.2

Let
w
be a nonzero weight function satisfying the condition (
4
) for some
q>0
, and let
a,b
satisfy
b-q+1\geq a\geq 0
. Then for a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of nonnegative numbers, the following are equivalent:

(i)
for
r\in[0,1)
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}r^{n}\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{a}}{(1-r)^{b}}\int_{r}^{1}w(t)dt;

(ii)
for
N\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}N^{b}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

.

Proof.

Assuming that part (i) holds and choosing
r=1-N^{-1}
for
N\geq 2
, part (ii) follows immediately since

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}(1-N^{-1})^{n}
.

Conversely assume that part (ii) holds. It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-r}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}r^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right)r^{n}\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}n^{b}\left(\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt\right)r^{n}.

Noting that
\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w(t)t^{n}dt
and
(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\asymp n^{-a}+|\sin\theta|^{a}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-r}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w(t)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{b-a}(rt)^{n}+|\sin\theta|^{a}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{b}(rt)^{n}\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}w(t)\left(\frac{1}{(1-rt)^{b-a+1}}+\frac{|\sin\theta|^{a}}{(1-rt)^{b+1}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{(1-r)^{b-a-q+1}}+\frac{|\sin\theta|^{a}}{(1-r)^{b-q+1}}\right)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{(1-rt)^{q}}dt.

This concludes part (i) by Lemma
9.1
.

Lemma 9.3

Let
w
be a nonzero weight function satisfying the conditions (
3
) and (
4
) for some
q>0
, and let
a,b
satisfy
b-q\geq a\geq 0
. Then for a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of nonnegative numbers, the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)
for
N\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{b}\alpha_{n}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}N^{b}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt;

(ii)
for
N\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt;

(iii)
for
N\geq 1
,

\sum_{n=N}^{2N}\alpha_{n}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

.

Proof.

Assuming that part (i) holds and setting
s_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{b}\alpha_{k}
, for
M>N\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{M}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N}^{M-1}s_{n}(n^{-b}-(n+1)^{-b})+M^{-b}s_{M}-N^{-b}s_{N-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{M-1}n^{-1}(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt+(M^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-M^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

Letting
M\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}n^{-1}\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

But since

\displaystyle n^{-1}\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt\lesssim\int_{1-(n-1)^{-1}}^{1-n^{-1}}\frac{1}{1-r}\int_{r}^{1}w(t)dtdr,

(86)

by the condition (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\left(N^{-1}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\int_{1-(n-1)^{-1}}^{1-n^{-1}}w(r)dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

Thus part (ii) is proved under the assumption in part (i).

Conversely assume that part (ii) holds, and let
\tilde{s}_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}
.
For
N\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{b}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{s}_{n}(n^{b}-(n-1)^{b})-N^{b}\tilde{s}_{N+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{b-1}(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{a}\int_{1-n^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

In a similar way to (
86
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{b}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha_{0}+\sum_{n=2}^{N}\int_{1-(n-1)^{-1}}^{1-n^{-1}}\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{a}}{(1-r)^{b+1}}\int_{r}^{1}w(t)dtdr,

and then, applying the conditions (
3
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{b}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1-N^{-1}}\left(\frac{1}{(1-r)^{b-a}}+\frac{|\sin\theta|^{a}}{(1-r)^{b}}\right)w(r)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(N^{-a}+|\sin\theta|^{a}\right)N^{b-q}\int_{0}^{1-N^{-1}}\frac{w(r)}{(1-r)^{q}}dr.

Thus part (i) follows by means of the condition (
4
).

That part (ii) implies part (iii) is obvious. It remains to show part (ii) under the assumption in part (iii).

For
N\geq 1
, by part (iii) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2^{k}N}^{2^{k+1}N}\alpha_{n}\lesssim\left(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{a}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{1-(2^{k}N)^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

Again in a similar way to (
86
),

\displaystyle\int_{1-(2^{k}N)^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt\lesssim\int_{1-(2^{k-1}N)^{-1}}^{1-(2^{k}N)^{-1}}\frac{1}{1-r}\int_{r}^{1}w(t)dtdr,

and appealing to the conditions (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{a}\left(\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{1-(2^{k-1}N)^{-1}}^{1-(2^{k}N)^{-1}}w(r)dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{a}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt.

Thus part (ii) is proved. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Lemma 9.4

Assume that
\lambda>0
. We have

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\asymp\left(|\sin\theta|+n^{-1}\right)^{-\lambda}\qquad\hbox{for}\,\,\,\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,n\geq 1.

Proof.

From Szegö
[
25
, (7.33.6)]
,

\displaystyle\left|P_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta)\right|\lesssim n^{\lambda-1}\left(\sin\theta+n^{-1}\right)^{-\lambda},\qquad\theta\in[0,\pi].

Applying this to (
5
) and in view of (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\lesssim\left(|\sin\theta|+n^{-1}\right)^{-\lambda}\qquad\hbox{for}\,\,\,\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,n\geq 1.

The proof of the converse estimate needs a delicate analysis.
From
[
25
, (4.7.1) and (8.21.18)]
, for a given positive number
c_{1}
we have

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta)=\frac{2^{1-\lambda}}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\frac{n^{\lambda-1}}{(\sin\theta)^{\lambda}}\left[\cos\left((n+\lambda)\theta-\frac{\lambda}{2}\pi\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{n\sin\theta}\right)\right],\,\,\,\theta\in\left[\frac{c_{1}}{n},\pi-\frac{c_{1}}{n}\right].

Using this in (
5
) yields

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|\sin\theta|^{-\lambda}}{\sqrt{2\pi\tilde{c}_{\lambda}}}\left[e^{i\left((n+\lambda)\theta-\frac{\lambda}{2}({\rm sign}\,\theta)\pi\right)}+O\left(\frac{1}{n|\sin\theta|}\right)\right],\qquad\frac{c_{1}}{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi-\frac{c_{1}}{n}.

Choosing
c_{1}
appropriately large but fixed, we get

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\gtrsim|\sin\theta|^{-\lambda}\asymp\left(|\sin\theta|+n^{-1}\right)^{-\lambda},\qquad\frac{c_{1}}{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi-\frac{c_{1}}{n}.

(87)

To obtain a similar estimate to (
87
) in the “complementary intervals” of
[-\pi,\pi]
, we appeal to a different method. Consider the function
h
on
[-1,1]
defined by, for
n\geq 1
,

h(x)=n(n+2\lambda)\left(P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)\right)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}(1-x^{2})\left(P_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(x)\right)^{2},\qquad x\in[-1,1].

In view of (
2
) and (
5
), it is obvious that

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\asymp n^{-\lambda}\sqrt{h(\cos\theta)},\qquad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

(88)

Noting that
2\lambda P_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(x)=P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime}
(cf.
[
25
, (4.7.14)]
), we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)=2P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime}\left[n(n+2\lambda)P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)+(1-x^{2})P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime\prime}\right]-2x\left(P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime}\right)^{2};

but by
[
25
, (4.7.5)]
, the expression in the square bracket above is identical with
(2\lambda+1)xP_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime}
, so that

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)=4\lambda x\left(P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)^{\prime}\right)^{2}\geq 0\qquad\hbox{for}\,\,\,x\in[0,1].

Consequently the function
h(x)
is nondecreasing on
[0,1]
.

Let
\theta^{*}
and
\theta^{**}
be the two adjacent zeros of
P_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta)
such that
\theta^{**}\leq c_{1}/n<\theta^{*}
. It then follows that, for
0\leq\theta\leq c_{1}/n
,

\displaystyle h(\cos\theta)\geq h(\cos\theta^{*})=4\lambda^{2}\sin^{2}\theta^{*}\left(P_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(\cos\theta^{*})\right)^{2};

(89)

but by
[
25
, Theorem 8.9.1]
,
c_{1}/n<\theta^{*}\leq c_{2}/n
for some constant
c_{2}
(
>c_{1}
) independent of
n
, and by
[
25
, (4.7.1), (4.7.14) and (8.9.2)]
,

2\lambda\left|P_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(\cos\theta^{*})\right|=\left|\left.\frac{d}{dx}P_{n}^{\lambda}(x)\right|_{x=\cos\theta^{*}}\right|\asymp n^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot n^{\lambda+\frac{3}{2}}=n^{2\lambda+1}.

Combining these with (
89
) gives us

\displaystyle h(\cos\theta)\geq h(\cos\theta^{*})\asymp n^{4\lambda},\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq c_{1}/n,

and from (
88
), we get
\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\gtrsim n^{\lambda}
for
0\leq\theta\leq c_{1}/n
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(e^{i\theta})\right|\gtrsim\left(|\sin\theta|+n^{-1}\right)^{-\lambda}.

(90)

It is easy to check that (
90
) is true also for
-c_{1}/n\leq\theta<0
and
\pi-c_{1}/n\leq|\theta|\leq\pi
, and on account of (
87
), we have proved (
90
) for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
and for
n\geq 1
.
The proof of Lemma
9.4
is finished.

We now come to our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 9.5

Let
p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq 1\leq p_{2}<\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, and let
w
be a nonzero weight function satisfying the conditions (
3
) and (
4
). Then a sequence
\eta=\{\eta_{n}\}
of complex numbers is a sequence multiplier from
A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
\ell^{p_{2}}
, if and only if
\eta=\{\eta_{n}\}
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=N}^{2N}|\eta_{k}|^{p_{2}}\right)^{p_{1}/p_{2}}\lesssim N^{(\lambda+1)p_{1}-2\lambda-1}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt,\qquad N=1,2,\cdots.

(91)

Proof.

Assume that
\eta_{0}=0
without loss of generality. Define the operator by
T_{\eta}f:=\{\eta_{n}a_{n}\}
whenever
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\phi_{n}^{\lambda}(z)\in A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
; and in particular
T\phi_{n}^{\lambda}=\eta_{n}e_{n}
, where
\{e_{0},e_{1},\cdots\}
is the canonic basis of
\ell^{p_{2}}
. Thus the associated
\ell^{p_{2}}
-valued function
F_{\lambda,\alpha}
defined by (
22
) becomes

\displaystyle F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)\,e_{n},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

where
\tau_{n}
is given by (
32
) and

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{q+2\lambda+1}{p_{1}}-2\lambda-2,

so that
\|F_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)\|_{\ell^{p_{2}}}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\eta_{n}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)|^{p_{2}}\right)^{1/p_{2}}
. Now by Theorem
4.3
and the remark following it, the sequence
\eta=\{\eta_{n}\}
is a sequence multiplier from
A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
\ell^{p_{2}}
, if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\eta_{n}\tau_{n}\phi_{n}(z)|^{p_{2}}\right)^{p_{1}/p_{2}}\lesssim\frac{|1-z^{2}|^{2\lambda(1-p_{1})}}{(1-|z|)^{(\alpha+2)p_{1}-1}}\int_{|z|^{p_{1}}}^{1}w(s)ds,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}};

and by Lemma
9.4
and in view of (
27
) and (
32
), this is equivalent to say

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{n}n^{(\alpha+1)p_{1}}|\eta_{n}|^{p_{1}}}{\left(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{\lambda p_{1}}}r^{p_{1}n}\lesssim\frac{(1-r+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p_{1})}}{(1-r)^{(\alpha+2)p_{1}-1}}\int_{r^{p_{1}}}^{1}w(s)ds,\qquad r\in[0,1),

(92)

for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
and for all
\{\beta_{n}\}\in\left(\ell^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\right)^{*}
satisfying
\|\{\beta_{n}\}\|_{\left(\ell^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\right)^{*}}\leq 1
.

But according to Lemma
9.2
, with
a=2\lambda(1-p_{1})
and
b=(\alpha+2)p_{1}-1
, (
92
) can be rephrased by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{n}n^{(\alpha+1)p_{1}}|\eta_{n}|^{p_{1}}}{\left(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{\lambda p_{1}}}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p_{1})}N^{(\alpha+2)p_{1}-1}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt,\qquad N\geq 1,

and furthermore, according to Lemma
9.3
(iii), by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{2N}\frac{\beta_{n}n^{1-p_{1}}|\eta_{n}|^{p_{1}}}{\left(n^{-1}+|\sin\theta|\right)^{\lambda p_{1}}}\lesssim(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{2\lambda(1-p_{1})}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt,\qquad N\geq 1,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{2N}\beta_{n}|\eta_{n}|^{p_{1}}\lesssim N^{p_{1}-1}(N^{-1}+|\sin\theta|)^{\lambda(2-p_{1})}\int_{1-N^{-1}}^{1}w(t)dt,\qquad N\geq 1.

Finally, taking the supremum over all
\{\beta_{n}\}\in\left(\ell^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\right)^{*}
satisfying
\|\{\beta_{n}\}\|_{\left(\ell^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\right)^{*}}\leq 1
, and the infimum over
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
,
we conclude that
\{\eta_{n}\}
is a sequence multiplier from
A^{p_{1}}_{\lambda,w}({\mathbb{D}})
to
\ell^{p_{2}}
if and only if (
91
) holds for
\{\eta_{n}\}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

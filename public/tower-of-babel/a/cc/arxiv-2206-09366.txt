Abstract
. The classical Cauchy–Davenport inequality gives a lower bound for the size of the sum of two subsets of ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} , where p p is a prime. Our main aim in this paper is to prove a considerable strengthening of this inequality, where we take only a small number of points from each of the two subsets when forming the sum. One of our results is that there is an absolute constant c > 0 c>0 such that if A A and B B are subsets of ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} with | A | = | B | = n ⩽ p / 3 |A|=|B|=n\leqslant p/3 then there are subsets A ′ ⊂ A A^{\prime}\subset A and B ′ ⊂ B B^{\prime}\subset B with | A ′ | = | B ′ | ⩽ c ​ n |A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\leqslant c\sqrt{n} such that | A ′ + B ′ | ⩾ 2 ​ n − 1 |A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant 2n-1 . In fact, we show that one may take any sizes one likes: as long as c 1 c_{1} and c 2 c_{2} satisfy c 1 ​ c 2 ⩾ c ​ n c_{1}c_{2}\geqslant cn then we may choose | A ′ | = c 1 |A^{\prime}|=c_{1} and | B ′ | = c 2 |B^{\prime}|=c_{2} . We prove related results for general abelian groups.

Introduction

The Cauchy–Davenport theorem
[

Over the years, this classical result
was followed by a host of important contributions about sums of subsets of groups, including other abelian groups such as

itself. For these

, Kneser

, Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa
[

,

[

, Stanchescu
[

]
, Breuillard, Green and Tao
[

]
, as well as the books of
Nathanson
[

]
, Tao and Vu
[
24
]
and Grynkiewicz
[
15
]
.

Recently, a new direction of research was started in
[
2
]
: can we get similar bounds for the size of the sum
if
A+B
is replaced by
A+B^{\prime}
, where
B^{\prime}
is a
small
subset of
B
? Among other results, it was proved that if

A
and
B
are finite non-empty subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|A|\geqslant|B|
then
B^{\prime}
can be taken to be really small:
there are
three
elements
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in B
such that
|A+\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}|\geqslant|A|+|B|-1
. For

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, it was shown that if

A,B\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|=n\leqslant p/3
then
B
has a subset
B^{\prime}
with at most
c
elements
such that
|A+B^{\prime}|\geqslant 2n-1
, where
c
is an absolute constant. (Here again
p
is prime, and for the rest of this paper
p
will always denote a prime.)

Our aim in this paper is to prove that actually one can replace
both

A
and
B
by appropriate small subsets.
In the result just mentioned, the product of the sizes of our two subsets in the sum, namely
A
and
B^{\prime}
, is
cn
,
and trivially we cannot ever get a sum of size linear in
n
without the product of the sizes of the two sets being
linear in
n
. But, remarkably, one can indeed always choose subsets
A^{\prime}
of
A
and
B^{\prime}
of
B
, of any desired
sizes, as long as the product of these sizes is linear in
n
.

The result mentioned in the Abstract has both sizes being a constant times
\sqrt{n}
, and the sets
A
and
B

themselves have size bounded away from
p/2
. The general form of our result is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

For all
\alpha,\beta>0
there exists
c>0
such that the following holds.
Let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with

\alpha|B|\leqslant|A|\leqslant\alpha^{-1}|B|
and
|A|+|B|\leqslant(1-\beta)p
.
Then, for any integers
1\leqslant c_{1}\leqslant|A|
and
1\leqslant c_{2}\leqslant|B|
such that
c_{1}c_{2}\geqslant c\max(|A|,|B|)
,
there exist subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
, of sizes
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant|A|+|B|-1
.

It is rather surprising that this holds with no other restrictions on the values of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, whatever the
form of the sets
A
and
B
. We remark that the constant
c
does have to depend on
\beta
: if our two sets
are allowed to have sizes whose sum approaches
p
then taking random subsets shows that
c
has to grow. Also, it is
easy to see that
c
depends on
\alpha
. For example, if
B
is far larger than
A
then trivially taking
c
points
from
B
to be summed with all of
A
will yield a sumset that is too small.

Our main tool is a similar result that is valid in general abelian groups. The reader will note that it is ‘worse’
than the above result in that there is an error term, but it is also far ‘better’ because the lower bound comes from
the sum of two actual sets (the sets
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
below) rather than merely from the lower bound that comes from the
sum of their sizes (or, more precisely, the general Kneser lower bound that generalises the Cauchy–Davenport theorem).

Theorem 2
.

For all
K
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
c
such that the
following holds.
Let
A
and
B
be finite non-empty subsets of an abelian group, and let
1\leqslant c_{1}\leqslant|A|
and

1\leqslant c_{2}\leqslant|B|
be integers satisfying
c_{1}c_{2}\geqslant c\max(|A|,|B|)
. Then there are subsets
A^{*}\subset A

and
B^{*}\subset B
, with
|A^{*}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|A|
and

|B^{*}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|B|
, such that
if we select points
a_{1},\ldots,a_{c_{1}}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{c_{2}}
uniformly at random from
A^{*}
and

B^{*}
then, writing
A^{\prime}
for
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{c_{1}}\}
and
B^{\prime}
for
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{c_{2}}\}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant\min\big((1-\varepsilon)|A^{*}+B^{*}|,\ K|A^{*}|,\ K|B^{*}|\big).

We remark that the terms
K|A^{*}|
and
K|B^{*}|
are only present to deal with unimportant cases: the key term is

(1-\varepsilon)|A^{*}+B^{*}|
. Thus the result is informally somehow saying that, in terms of sumsets,
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
may really be approximated by very small subsets of themselves (and indeed most subsets will do).

Interestingly, while this theorem is for general abelian groups, Theorem
1
is only about
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The passage
between these does require quite a lot of work.

In Section 4 we prove Theorem

Our notation is standard.
Sometimes we write ‘
x\mod d
’ as shorthand for the infinite arithmetic progression

\{y\in\mathbb{Z}:y\equiv x\mod d\}
, and refer to it as a
fibre
mod
d
.
When
S
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
we often write
S^{x}
for the intersection of this fibre with

S
– when the value of
d
is clear. (We sometimes write
S^{x}
as
S^{x}_{d}
when we want to stress the value of
d
.)
Thus
S^{x}=S\cap\pi^{-1}(x)
, where
\pi=\pi_{d}
denotes
the natural projection from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

When we write a probability or an expectation over a finite set, we always assume that the elements
of the set are being sampled uniformly. Thus, for example,
for a finite set
X
we denote the expectation and probability when we sample uniformly over
all
x\in X
by
{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in X}\text{ and }\Prob_{x\in X}
.
We also often sample uniformly over all
c
-sets of a given set
X
. In most of those cases,
we could instead sample
c
elements uniformly and independently, but the notation would tend to get
unwieldy, and this is why we use the sampling over all
c
-sets instead.

Before we turn to the next section, let us draw attention to the superficial similarity of
our problems to a beautiful result of Ellenberg
[
8
]
. Given a prime
p
and a positive
integer

d
, let
f(p^{d})
be the smallest integer such that for any sets
S,T\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{d}

there are subsets
S^{\prime}\subset S
and
T^{\prime}\subset T
satisfying
(S+T^{\prime})\cup(S^{\prime}+T)=S+T

and
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|\leqslant f(p^{d})
. Ellenberg proved that
f(p^{d})\leqslant(cp)^{d}
, where
c<1
is an absolute
constant.

2.
Prerequisites

In this section we collect together the various prerequisites that we will need. Each of these may be treated
as a ‘black box’: knowledge of their proofs will not be required.

The first of the three theorems we shall need is due to Shao
[
22
]
, and concerns
restricted sums. Let
A
and
B
be subsets of an abelian group, and let

\Gamma\subset A\times B
. The
\Gamma
-restricted sum
of
A
and
B
is

A+_{\Gamma}B=\{a+b:\ a\in A,\ b\in B,(a,b)\in\Gamma\}
. Here is the result of Shao.

Theorem 3
.

For all
\varepsilon,K>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an
abelian group and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A,B\subset G

be two subsets with
|A|,|B|\geqslant N
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
be a subset with

|\Gamma|\geqslant(1-\delta)|A||B|
. If
|A+_{\Gamma}B|\leqslant KN
, then there
exist
A_{0}\subset A
and
B_{0}\subset B
such that

|A_{0}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|A|\text{ and }|B_{0}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|B|\text{ and }|A_{0}+B_{0}|\leqslant|A+_{\Gamma}B|+\varepsilon N.

The second theorem is an easy corollary of a theorem of Grynkiewicz
[
15
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Given
\beta,\gamma>0
there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that the following holds.
Let
A
and
B
be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Suppose that

2\leqslant\min(|A|,|B|)\text{ and }|A|+|B|\leqslant(1-\beta)p

and
|A+B|\leqslant|A|+|B|-1+\varepsilon\min(|A|,|B|)
.
Then there are arithmetic progressions
P
and
Q
with the same common difference that contain
A
and
B
and satisfy
|P\Delta A|\leqslant\gamma\min(|A|,|B|)\text{ and }|Q\Delta B|\leqslant\gamma\min(|A|,|B|)
.

The last theorem we need is a somewhat technical result from
[
2
]
. It gives a strengthening of the result from
[
2
]

mentioned above about sums in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, when the sets
A
and
B
‘relate nicely’ to intervals.

Theorem 5
.

For all
\beta>0
there exists
\gamma>0
such that for every
\alpha>0
there is a value of
c
for which the
following
holds. Let
A
and
B
be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
I=[p_{l},p_{r}]
and
J=[q_{l},q_{r}]

be intervals in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
satisfying
\alpha|J|\leqslant|I|\leqslant\alpha^{-1}|J|
,
|I|+|J|\leqslant(1-\beta)p
,

\max(|A\Delta I|,|B\Delta J|)\leqslant\gamma\min(|I|,|J|)
and
\{q_{l},q_{r}\}\subset B\subset J
.
Then there is a family
\mathcal{F}\subset B^{(c)}
, depending only on
I
,
J
and
B
(but not on

), such that

\E_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}|A+B^{\prime}|\geqslant|A|+|J|-1\geqslant|A|+|B|-1.

Lemma 10
.

Let

,

and

be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

and

5.
Open problems

One very natural question to ask is as follows. Suppose that as usual we are choosing
c_{1}
points of
A
and
c_{2}

points of
B
, where
|A|=|B|=n
and
c_{1}c_{2}
is a fixed multiple of
n
. Are there phenomena that may not occur in
the regime where say we are
choosing
c_{1}=n
(in other words, we choose the whole of
A
) and
c_{2}
bounded, but might possibly
always hold when both
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are of order
\sqrt{n}
?
One example is the following.

Question 1.
Is there a constant
c
such that the following is true? If
A
and
B
are non-empty subsets of

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|=n\leqslant(p+1)/2
then there are subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and

B^{\prime}\subset B
with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\leqslant c\sqrt{n}
such that
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant 2n-1
.

As remarked in the Introduction, this is not true for
c_{1}=n
and
c_{2}
bounded, in other words for
A^{\prime}=A
and

|B^{\prime}|
bounded, as may be seen by taking
A
and
B
to be random subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of size approaching

p/2
. But it might conceivably be true when we force both
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
to be large.

In a similar vein, one might ask whether the case of both
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
being of order
\sqrt{n}
is
in fact always the ‘best’ case (where
A
and
B
are set of size
n
, say). Thus for Theorem
1

we would be asking the following.

Question 2.
Let
c>0
and
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)
be such that whenever
A
and
B
are subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with

|A|=|B|=n\leqslant p/3
there exist subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and

B^{\prime}\subset B
, with
|A^{\prime}|\leqslant c_{1}
and
|B^{\prime}|\leqslant cn/c_{1}
, such that
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant 2n-1
. Does it follow that
whenever
A
and
B
are subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with

|A|=|B|=n\leqslant p/3
there exist subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and

B^{\prime}\subset B
with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\leqslant\sqrt{cn}
such that
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geqslant 2n-1
?

One could also ask what the ‘worst’ case is: is it when
c_{1}=c
and
c_{2}=n
? More generally, is there
‘monotonicity’ as
c_{1}
varies from
c
to
\sqrt{cn}
?

It would also be very interesting to obtain good bounds on the constants appearing in our various results.
For example, in Theorem
1
, what is the form of the dependence of
c
on
\alpha
and
\beta
?

Finally, we consider what happens for discrete versions of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality. Green and
Tao
[
14
]

showed
that, given a dimension
k
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
t
such that if
A
is a subset of

{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
of size
n
that is not
contained inside
t
parallel hyperplanes (intuitively,
A
‘does not look lower-dimensional’),
then
|A+A|\geqslant(2^{k}-\varepsilon)n
. We wonder if the following might be true. Although this is a question
about
\mathbb{Z}
rather than
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we feel that the methods in this paper are likely to be
relevant.

Question 3.
For a given dimension
k
, does there exist a constant
c
such that the
following holds? For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
t
such that if
A
is a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
of
size
n
that is not contained in

t
parallel hyperplanes, then there exists a subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
of size at most
c\sqrt{n}
such
that
|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\geqslant(2^{k}-\varepsilon)n
.

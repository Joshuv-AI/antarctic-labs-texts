Variations on the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism
Markus Frembs, Eric G. Cavalcanti

Abstract
We address various aspects of a widely used tool in quantum information theory: the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism [A. Jamiołkowski, Rep. Math. Phys., 3, 275 (1972)]. We review different versions of the isomorphism, their properties and propose a unified description that combines them all. To this end, we identify the physical reason for the appearance of the (basis-dependent) operation of transposition in the isomorphism as used in Choi’s theorem [M.-D. Choi, Lin. Alg. Appl., 10, 285 (1975)]. This requires a careful distinction between Jordan algebras and the different C ∗ C^{*} -algebras they arise from, which are distinguished by their order of composition. Physically, the latter encodes a choice of time orientation in the respective algebras, which relates to a number of recent results, including a characterisation of quantum from more general non-signalling bipartite correlations [M. Frembs and A. Döring, arXiv:2204.11471] and a classification of bipartite entanglement [M. Frembs, arXiv:2207.00024].

Choi and Jamiołkowski - two isomorphisms, two theorems

Let

I.2
Choi’s isomorphism and theorem

The most commonly used version of the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism is motivated by a theorem due to Choi

3

C:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho^{C}_{\phi}=C(\phi)&:=\sum_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\phi(|i\rangle\langle j|)\;,\\
C^{-1}(\rho)(a)&:=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}[\rho(a^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]\;,\end{split}

(3)

for all
a\in{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}

\{|i\rangle\langle j|\}_{ij}
denotes the orthonormal basis in
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}
built from an orthonormal basis
\{|i\rangle\}_{i}\in\mathrm{ONB}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
, and
T
denotes transposition in that basis.
3
3

3

This is sometimes written

2

Unlike Eq. (

4
4

4

(Choi

I.3
A basis-independent variant of the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism

The basis dependence in Eq. (
3

1

3

is generally not positive (cf. Ref.
LeiferSpekkens2013
;
PaulsenShultz2013
;
Kye2022

AllenEtAl2017
;
BarrettLorenzOreshkov2020
(see also Ref.
Ozawa2014
), which define a map
{JC}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
by
5
5

5

Ref.
AllenEtAl2017
defines
a_{A^{*}}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}[\tau^{\mathrm{id}}_{A}a]
for
\tau^{\mathrm{id}}_{A}:=\sum_{ij}|i\rangle_{A^{*}}\langle j|\otimes|i\rangle_{A}\langle j|
; hence,
(a_{A})_{mn}=(a_{A^{*}})_{nm}
as matrices. In this way, the transposition in Eq. (
3
) is absorbed into the mapping between
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and its dual
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho^{{JC}}_{\phi}={JC}(\phi)&:=\sum_{ij}|i\rangle_{A^{*}}\langle j|\otimes\phi(|i\rangle\langle j|)\;,\\
{JC}^{-1}(\rho)(a)&:=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}}[\rho(a_{A^{*}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]\;,\quad a_{A^{*}}:=a^{T}\quad\quad\forall a\in{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\;.\end{split}

(4)

Here,
\{|i\rangle_{A^{*}}\in\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}\}_{i}
corresponds to the dual basis of
\{|i\rangle_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\}_{i}
,
6
6

6

Recall that the dual basis
\{\langle j|_{A}\}_{j}
of
\{|i\rangle_{A}\}_{i}
is uniquely defined by the condition
\langle j|i\rangle_{A}=\delta_{ij}
.
yet the elements
|i\rangle_{A*}
are thought of as vectors (rather than dual vectors) such that
\langle j|i\rangle_{A^{*}}=\langle i|j\rangle_{A}=\overline{\langle j|i\rangle}_{A}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}}[\rho^{{JC}}_{\phi}(a_{A^{*}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]&=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}}[(\sum_{ij}|i\rangle_{A^{*}}\langle j|\otimes\phi(|i\rangle\langle j|))(\sum_{mn}|m\rangle(a_{A^{*}})_{mn}\langle n|\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]\\

&=\sum_{ijkmn}\langle k|n\rangle_{A}(a_{A})_{nm}\langle m|j\rangle_{A}\langle i|k\rangle_{A}\ \phi(|i\rangle\langle j|)\\
&=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}[(\sum_{ij}|j\rangle\langle i|\otimes\phi(|i\rangle\langle j|))(\sum_{mn}|m\rangle a_{mn}\langle n|\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}[\rho^{J}_{\phi}(a\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})]\;.\end{split}

Consequently, Eq. (
4

2

3

\phi
to positive matrices

In the next section, we explicitly identify the physical significance of the transposition in Eq. (

and

2

3
, and recovers Eq. (
4

II
A unified version of the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism

II.1
Background - Jordan algebras, complex structure and inner product

At the heart of the the different versions and properties of the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism lies the subtle distinction between
Jordan and
C^{*}
-algebras. We will only need the bare essentials here, and liberally refer to Ref.
Hanche-OlsenStormer1984JordanOA
;
AlfsenShultz1998a
;
AlfsenShultz1998b
for many more details.

Jordan algebras.
The operator product on
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
admits the decomposition,

ab=\frac{1}{2}\{a,b\}-\frac{1}{2}[a,b]\;,

(5)

where
[a,b]=ab-ba
denotes the commutator and
\{a,b\}:=ab+ba
denotes the anti-commutator.
({\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})},[\cdot,\cdot])
defines a Lie algebra while
{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H})}:=({\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
defines a Jordan algebra.
7
7

7

A Jordan algebra
(J,\circ)
is commutative (but generally non-associative) and satisfies the Jordan identity:
(a\circ b)\circ(a\circ a)=a\circ(b\circ(a\circ a))
for all
a,b\in J
. For a classification of Jordan algebras, see Ref.
JordanVNWigner1934
;
Hanche-OlsenStormer1984JordanOA
;
McCrimmon2004
.
8
8

8

Overloading notation slightly, we write
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
for the linear space and for the algebra of operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
Importantly, already
{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H})}_{\mathrm{sa}}
defines a real Jordan algebra, in particular, the anti-commutator closes on self-adjoint (Hermitian) elements—unlike the operator product in
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
. Indeed, this was the original motivation to study Jordan algebras in Ref.
Jordan1933
;
JordanVNWigner1934
, viz. as generalisations of observable algebras in quantum mechanics.

Not every real Jordan algebra
\mathcal{J}
arises as a self-adjoint part (closed under the anti-commutator) from a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{J}\not\subseteq(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sa}},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
in general.
8
If it does,
\mathcal{J}
is called
special
, otherwise it is called an
exceptional
Jordan algebra. The key to identify special Jordan algebras is the double role played by self-adjoint operators in
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
: as observables and as generators of symmetries

;
AlfsenShultz1998b
.
9
9

9

Jordan algebras whose elements mimic this dichotomy are characterised by the existence of a
dynamical correspondence

AlfsenShultz1998a
;
AlfsenShultz1998b
(see also Ref.
Baez2022
). In turn, for every dynamical correspondence on
\mathcal{J}
, there is an associative
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
from which
\mathcal{J}
arises as a self-adjoint part
\mathcal{J}\subseteq(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sa}},\{\cdot,\cdot\})

AlfsenShultz1998a
).
In the case of
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathrm{sa}}
, there are exactly two such associative products that extend the anti-commutator in
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathrm{sa}}
(cf.
AlfsenShultz1998a

a\cdot_{+}b:=\frac{1}{2}\{a,b\}+\frac{1}{2}[a,b]=ab\quad\quad\quad a\cdot_{-}b:=\frac{1}{2}\{a,b\}-\frac{1}{2}[a,b]=ba\;,

(6)

for all

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathrm{sa}}
arises as the self-adjoint part of both
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}=({\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})},\cdot_{+})
and its opposite algebra
{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}^{\mathrm{op}}}=({\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})},\cdot_{-})

{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
.
8
Since transposition

is an order-reversing involution on
{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}

{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
and
{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}^{\mathrm{op}}}

{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}^{\mathrm{op}}}

6

Time orientations and complex structure.
Note that the two (associative) products in Eq. (
6

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\Psi(t,a)=i[a,\cdot]\;.

10

10

Inner product and Born rule.
The Hilbert-Schmidt inner product on

Decomposable and completely positve maps.
We can study the isomorphism under the restriction to Jordan algebras for maps with even more structure. Recall that a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
\Phi:{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}

{}^{*}\Phi=*\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ*=\Phi^{*}

\Phi(\{a,a^{\prime}\})=\{\Phi(a),\Phi(a^{\prime})\}

\phi:{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}

\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})_{\mathrm{D}}

\phi=v^{*}\Phi v

\Phi:{\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{K})

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})_{\mathrm{D}}

What about completely positive (CP) maps? Can we capture the set of CP maps (and thus the set of operators under Eq. (
3
)) by studying their restriction to real
Jordan algebras? This is not the case! To see this, it is helpful to consider the classification of completely positive maps due to Stinespring

\phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}

\phi=v^{*}\Phi v

\Phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})

-algebra homomorphism, i.e.,
{}^{*}\Phi=\Phi^{*}
and
\Phi(aa^{\prime})=\Phi(a)\Phi(a^{\prime})

.

Of course, every
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism restricts to a Jordan

\Phi(aa^{\prime})=\Phi(a)\Phi(a^{\prime})

\Phi(aa^{\prime})=\Phi(a^{\prime})\Phi(a)

\Phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})

\Phi:{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})

-algebra homomorphism

Recall (from Eq. (

{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}

\phi:{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}
,

16

16

;
Horodeckisz1996
(see also Ref.
Frembs2022a

Lemma 1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Then
\phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}
is completely positive if and only if
\phi\circ*:{{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}^{\mathrm{op}}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}
is completely positive.

Proof.

By Stinespring’s theorem
Stinespring1955
, every completely positive map
\phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}
is of the form
\phi=v^{*}\Phi v
for a linear map
v:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
and
\Phi:{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism. It follows that
\Phi\circ*:{{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}^{\mathrm{op}}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
is also a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism,

(\Phi\circ*)(a\cdot_{-}a^{\prime})=\Phi((a^{\prime}a)^{*})=\Phi(a^{*}a^{\prime*})=\Phi(a^{*})\Phi(a^{\prime*})=(\Phi\circ*)(a)(\Phi\circ*)(a^{\prime})\;.

Hence,
\phi\circ*=v^{*}(\Phi\circ*)v:{{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A})}^{\mathrm{op}}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})}
is completely positive
Stinespring1955
.
∎

Note that a similar result holds for

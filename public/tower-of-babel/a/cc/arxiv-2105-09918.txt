Four-Loop Gauge and Three-Loop Yukawa Beta Functions in a General Renormalizable Theory
Alexander Bednyakov, Andrey Pikelner

Abstract
We present the beta functions of gauge and Yukawa couplings in general four-dimensional quantum field theory, at four and three loops, respectively. The essence of our approach is fixing unknown coefficients in the most general ansatz for beta functions by direct calculation in several simplified models. We apply our results to the Standard Model and its extension with an arbitrary number of Higgs doublets and provide expressions for all four-loop gauge couplings beta functions with matrix Yukawa interactions.



I
Introduction

Renormalization group equations (RGE) find their numerous applications in many
physical problems formulated in the language of quantum field theory (QFT).
Being a convenient tool to improve perturbation theory results, RGE allow
one to obtain high-precision predictions for various quantities ranging from
critical exponents in the theory of critical phenomena to observables in the
Standard Model (SM) and its extensions.

In the early 1980s, Machacek and
Vaughn
Machacek and Vaughn 1984
;
Machacek and Vaughn 1983
;
Machacek and Vaughn 1985
presented their
two-loop RG functions in
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme for all dimensionless couplings in general
four-dimensional renormalizable QFT. Several misprints have been corrected
during the subsequent 35 years, and the classical result has been extended to
include RG functions for dimensionful
parameters
Luo et al. 2003
;
Bednyakov 2018
;
Schienbein et al. 2019
;
Sartore 2020
.
The two-loop general expressions became highly demanded in studies of the SM
extensions. After the appearance of computer codes aimed to generate RGE for
user-specified models, two-loop RG studies have become
de facto
standard in a
New Physics analysis.

After discovering the Higgs boson at the
LHC
Aad et al. 2012
;
Chatrchyan et al. 2012
and a burst of activity on the vacuum
stability problem (see, e.g.,
Bednyakov et al. 2015
), it became clear that
three-loop RG
functions
Pickering et al. 2001
;
Mihaila et al. 2012
;
Bednyakov et al. 2013a
;
Bednyakov et al. 2013b
;
Chetyrkin and Zoller 2013
;
Bednyakov et al. 2013c

can play an essential role in precision studies of the SM and its extensions.
Partial four-loop results in the
SM
Bednyakov and Pikelner 2016
;
Zoller 2016
;
Chetyrkin and Zoller 2016
and three-loop beta
functions in the Two-Higgs-Doublet Model(THDM)
Herren et al. 2018
became
available during the past few years.

Recently, two major steps toward general high-order results have been made.
First of all, RG functions in general theories were represented by a linear
combination of independent tensor structures (TS) corresponding to contractions
of various indices. One can match these “template” expressions to known
results in specific models and extract model- independent
coefficients
Steudtner 2020
;
Steudtner 2021
. Second, new ideas based
on the so-called Weyl Consistency Conditions
(WCC)
Jack and Osborn 2014
;
Antipin et al. 2013
;
Poole and Thomsen 2019a
allow one to find
relations between known and unknown TS coefficients, thus, putting constraints
on to-be-computed numbers. In particular, WCC relate gauge, Yukawa, and
self-coupling beta functions computed at four, three, and two loops, respectively.

Let us also mention that WCC allows to resolve
Poole and Thomsen 2019b
a well-known
issue with
\gamma_{5}
ambiguity (see, e.g.,
Jegerlehner 2001
) in
{\rm\overline{MS}}

RG functions. Poole and Thomsen in Ref.
Poole and Thomsen 2019b
use WCC to relate
the ambiguous four-loop terms in strong coupling beta
function
Bednyakov and Pikelner 2016
;
Zoller 2016
to unambiguous three-loop
contributions to Yukawa beta functions
Chetyrkin and Zoller 2012
;
Bednyakov et al. 2013b

and confirm the prescription advocated in Ref.
Bednyakov and Pikelner 2016
. The
relation holds for any four-loop gauge beta function in a general QFT, and it
was immediately used for the gauge sector of the SM with diagonal Yukawa
matrices
Davies et al. 2020
.

There is also an (unphysical) ambiguity in RG functions due to possible unitary
contributions
Fortin et al. 2013
;
Bednyakov et al. 2014
;
Herren et al. 2018
to field renormalization in models with flavor symmetries. In what follows, we assume a
natural choice of Hermitian anomalous dimension and refer to recent
work
Herren and Thomsen 2021
for more details.

In the current study, we present for the first time complete results for all TS
coefficients in gauge and Yukawa beta functions at four and three loops, respectively.
Partial results are already available in the
literature
Steudtner 2020
;
Steudtner 2021
;
Poole and Thomsen 2019a
, and we want
to emphasize the difference between our approach and methods used in previous
studies. In the latter, given WCC, the unknown beta-function coefficients are
fixed from
known
results for specific (usually physical) models. One of
the most powerful constraints of this type is provided by the three-loop
calculation in THDM
Herren et al. 2018
.

On the contrary, we, for the first time,
design simple toy models
with specific
gauge group structure, each giving constraints on different unknown
coefficients. We use diagrams for TS provided in Supplementary Materials of
Ref.
Poole and Thomsen 2019a
as a guide, but it is fair to say that we found the
required models by trial and error. We use TSs implemented in the prominent code

RGBeta

Thomsen 2021
to speed up our investigation.

II
Models

We emphasize two different sources of constraints on beta-function coefficients.
One type is coming from direct calculations and fixes independently

\mathfrak{g}^{(4)}
and
\mathfrak{y}^{(3)}
. Another one is WCC providing
relations between these coefficients. Most of the first type constraints, which
one can obtain from SM and THDM, are already included in the analysis by Poole
and Thomsen
Poole and Thomsen 2019a
. We extend these results by direct calculations
in toy

gauge models described below. Calculation with arbitrary
n

Cvitanovic 1976
gives an additional handle on unknown coefficients.

Due to the nature of constraints from WCC, it is natural to consider Gauge and
Yukawa together. Since constraints from different sources are independent, the
combined system becomes overdetermined, and we have a large number of
equalities. The latter allows us not only to fix the TS coefficients but also to
make extensive crosschecks of the
RGBeta
code and the validity of our
results.

IV
Results and discussion

We combine the constraints from WCC
Jack and Osborn 2014
and our explicit
computations in toy models to fix all the coefficients in the ansatz for the
4-3-2 ordering given in Ref.
Poole and Thomsen 2019a
. As an application of our
general expressions, we derive all four-loop gauge beta functions in the SM
extension with the arbitrary number
n_{d}
of Higgs doublets (NHDM). We keep
matrix Yukawa couplings, and by setting
n_{d}=1
or
n_{d}=2

THDM result. Direct computation in such a scenario would be very complicated.

V
Conclusion

The calculation of four-loop Gauge and three-loop Yukawa beta functions
performed in this Letter complement recent six-loop results in general pure
scalar theory
Bednyakov and Pikelner 2021
, and represents the most advanced
achievement in this field. The obtained TS coefficients can be incorporated into
modern computer codes, giving access to a new precision level for model
building. The dummy-field method (see, e.g., Ref.
Staub 2014
) applied
to our results provides us with scale dependence of such important quantities as
fermion mass matrices.

We make all our results, including the TS coefficients and four-loop
gauge-coupling beta functions in the SM, THDM, and NHDM, available as
Supplementary Material. We also provide a modified version of the RGBeta package
with all our findings included
rgb
.

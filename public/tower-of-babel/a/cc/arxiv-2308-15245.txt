Efficient time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster computations with time-dependent basis sets at the two-mode coupling level: full and hybrid TDMVCC[2]
Andreas Buchgraitz Jensen, Mads Greisen Højlund, Alberto Zoccante, Niels Kristian Madsen, Ove Christiansen

Abstract
Abstract The computation of the nuclear quantum dynamics of molecules is challenging, requiring both accuracy and efficiency to be applicable to systems of interest. Recently, theories have been developed for employing time-dependent basis functions (denoted modals) with vibrational coupled cluster theory (TDMVCC). The TDMVCC method was introduced along with a pilot implementation, which illustrated good accuracy in benchmark computations. In this paper we report an efficient implementation of TDMVCC covering the case where the wave function and Hamiltonian contain up to two-mode couplings. After a careful regrouping of terms, the wave function can be propagated with a cubic computational scaling with respect to the number of degrees of freedom. We discuss the use of a restricted set of active one-mode basis functions for each mode, as well as two interesting limits: i) the use of a full active basis where the variational modal determination amounts essentially to the variational determination of a time-dependent reference state for the cluster expansion; and ii) the use of a single function as active basis for some degrees of freedom. The latter case defines a hybrid TDMVCC/TDH approach which can obtain an even lower computational scaling. The resulting computational scaling for hybrid and full TDMVCC[2] are illustrated for polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) with up to 264 modes. Finally, computations on the internal vibrational redistribution on benzoic acid (39 modes) are used to show the faster convergence of TDMVCC/TDH hybrid computations towards TDMVCC compared to simple neglect of some degrees of freedom.

Classical molecular dynamics methods have the advantage of being easily applicable to large systems using simple coordinates,

time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals seeks to incorporate the benefits of multiconfiguration time-dependent Hartree and the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster Ansatz.
By introducing a small active space of time-dependent basis functions as in multiconfiguration time-dependent Hartree and leveraging the fast convergence in configuration
space offered by the exponentially parameterized vibrational coupled cluster methodology,
truncated time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals variants present a potential solution to the exponential scaling problem.
Still, the development of time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals is in its infancy and much further research is necessary to fully explore its capabilities.

The previous pilot implementation of time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals could not establish
the true low-order polynomial computational scaling of time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals methods,
as it relied on very general, but exponentially scaling steps. It was made
solely with the aim of investigating accuracy and convergence in simple benchmarks.
In this paper, we present an efficient implementation of the
TDMVCC method covering the case where the wave function and Hamiltonian contain up to two-mode couplings (henceforth denoted TDMVCC[2]).
The two-mode coupling case has a manageable number of terms that can be programmed by hand,
something that has been utilized in similar papers in time-independent
seidler2008tfc
;
zoccante154101
and time-dependent
hansen_timedependent_2019
cases.
We demonstrate how the TDMVCC[2] method can be implemented to efficiently
handle many-mode computations with many-term Hamiltonians, achieving a computational scaling of third order with respect to the system size.
In particular, we address the challenge of efficiently computing the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals mean fields.
By manually deriving and implementing all the necessary terms, we establish a highly efficient code and lay the groundwork for future general implementations of time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals.
In addition, we discuss two interesting limits of using
a restricted set of active one-mode basis functions for each mode:
i) the use of a full active basis where the variational
modal determination amounts essentially to the variational determination of a time-dependent
reference state for the cluster expansion; and ii) the use of a single function as active basis
for some degrees of freedom. The latter case defines a hybrid TDMVCC/TDH approach, where some modes are treated at the TDH level.
This approach can obtain an even lower computational scaling, where the equations of motion for the time-dependent Hartree modes are similar to
those of the simple time-dependent Hartree theory, only with some additional mean-field contributions due to the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals modes.
This work thereby initiates a new way of dealing with large systems
with different levels of time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals theory.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Sec.

the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals method and derive the expressions in a form suitable for efficient implementation.
In Sec.

polyaromatic hydrocarbons with up to 264 modes, as well as TDMVCC and TDMVCC/TDH

Finally, in Sec.

Theory

II.1
The many-mode second quantization formulation for biorthogonal bases

The time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals method is formulated using the second quantization framework introduced in Ref.
50
.
In an extension to Ref.
50
and following
Refs.
16
and
17
, we are working in a biorthogonal framework with the main points repeated in this paper for completeness.
The biorthogonality is indicated by using
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}
and
\tilde{b}
for creation and annihilation operators, respectively. The tilde is used to denote the time-dependence of the operators.

Relative to the vacuum state,
\ket{{\mathrm{vac}}}
, single-mode creation and annihilation operators act as,

\displaystyle\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{s^{m}}\ket{{\mathrm{vac}}}=\ket{\tilde{\varphi}^{m}_{s^{m}}},

(1)

\displaystyle\bra{{\mathrm{vac}}}\tilde{b}^{m}_{r^{m}}=\bra{\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime m}_{r^{m}}}.

(2)

In first quantization coordinate representation, the right-hand sides would be written as
\tilde{\varphi}^{m}_{s^{m}}(q_{m},t)
and
\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime m}_{r^{m}}(q_{m},t)
.
The explicit time-dependence will usually be omitted in the following.
One-mode basis functions will generally be denoted modals (in analogy to orbitals for electronic structure theory).

To describe the biorthogonality of the basis, we define the commutator relations

\Big[\tilde{b}^{m}_{r^{m}},\tilde{a}^{m^{\prime}\,\dagger}_{s^{m^{\prime}}}\Big]=\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta_{r^{m}s^{m^{\prime}}},\quad\Big[\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{r^{m}},\tilde{a}^{m^{\prime}\,\dagger}_{s^{m^{\prime}}}\Big]=\Big[\tilde{b}^{m}_{r^{m}},\tilde{b}^{m^{\prime}}_{s^{m^{\prime}}}\Big]=0.

(3)

The time-dependent biorthogonal creation and annihilation operators
will be defined through a linear combination of time-independent orthonormal creation (
{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}
) and annihilation (
{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}}
) operators with a set of time-dependent expansion coefficients:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{p^{m}}=\sum_{\alpha^{m}}{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}p^{m}},

(4)

\displaystyle\tilde{b}^{m}_{p^{m}}=\sum_{\alpha^{m}}W^{m}_{p^{m}\alpha^{m}}{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}}.

(5)

We use an index convention with Greek letters
\alpha^{m}
for the primitive set of time-independent modals
and roman letters
p^{m}
,
q^{m}
,
r^{m}
,
s^{m}
for generic time-dependent modals.
The full set of time-dependent modals is divided into
an active set of modals indexed by
u^{m}
,
v^{m}
,
w^{m}
and a set of secondary
modals indexed by
x^{m}
,
y^{m}
. The active set of modals are those actually present
in the wave functions. The number of time-independent modals will later be denoted
N
while the number of time-dependent modals will be denoted
A

The biorthonormality of the time-dependent modals is captured by the modal expansion coefficients,

\sum_{\alpha^{m}}W^{m}_{p^{m}\alpha^{m}}U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}q^{m}}=\delta_{p^{m}q^{m}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathbf{W}^{m}\mathbf{U}^{m}=\mathbf{I}^{m},

(6)

where we have used matrix notation in the latter equation.

From the creation and annihilation operators we define the shift operator as

\tilde{E}^{m}_{r^{m}s^{m}}=\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{r^{m}}\tilde{b}^{m}_{s^{m}}.

(7)

This type of operator is used extensively in Sec.
II.4
either as written above in Eq. (
7
) or as the one-mode excitation and deexcitation operators,

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}^{m}_{a^{m}}=\tilde{E}^{m}_{a^{m}i^{m}},

(8)

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}=\tilde{E}^{m}_{i^{m}a^{m}}.

(9)

Here, excitation and deexcitation refer to a reference Hartree product which is given as a string of
M
creation operators acting on the vacuum:

\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{i^{m}}\ket{{\mathrm{vac}}}{.}

(10)

Here,

denotes the number of modes.
The index

is reserved for the occupied active modal of mode
m

in the reference state while
a^{m}
,
b^{m}
,
c^{m}
denote unoccupied (virtual) active modals.

We will at times use the compound index
\mu
to mean some collection of mode and modal indices defining a general excitation.
Other single Hartree product ket or bra states can, for example, be obtained as

\displaystyle\ket{\mu}=\tilde{\tau}_{\mu}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}{,}

(11)

\displaystyle\bra{\mu^{\prime}}={\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}\tilde{\tau}^{\ddagger}_{\mu}.

(12)

In addition, we will use the nomenclature that
\mu_{j}
denotes a
j
-mode excitation. In our case the two-mode excitations,
\mu_{2}
, are of particular interest.
Such excitations are constructed as products of one-mode excitations, i.e.

II.3
Polar parameterization of the modals

In Ref.
17
we described how instabilities can occur in the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals equations of motion when employing a secondary modal basis (i.e. when the active basis is not full),
and that such instabilities can also occur for other types of wave functions when a biorthogonal basis is used.
This phenomenon was analyzed in detail for general bivariational time-dependent wave functions, and it was shown that the issue occurs because the bra and ket modal bases tend to span different spaces when given enough time in a numerical computation.
A solution was proposed based on the reparameterization of the modals through polar decomposition.
Fully variational equations of motion were derived and subsequently modified in order to lock the bra and ket modal bases together such that they span the same space at any given time. This restricted polar representation corresponds to a modal parameterization of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{m}_{\!\textsc{A}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}_{\!\textsc{A}}^{m}\mathbf{P}^{m},

(28a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{m}_{\!\!\textsc{A}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{P}^{m})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}_{\!\textsc{A}}^{m})^{\dagger},

(28b)

where
\mathbf{V}^{m}_{\!\textsc{A}}
is semi-unitary (has orthonormal columns) and
\mathbf{P}^{m}
is Hermitian.
Eqs. (
28
) clearly imply that the columns of
\mathbf{U}^{m}_{\!\textsc{A}}
and
\mathbf{W}^{m}_{\!\!\textsc{A}}

span the same space. Although the modified equations of motion are not strictly variational,
we found an attractive combination of stability and accuracy.

II.4
Two-mode TDMVCC implementation

In the two-mode specific case we truncate the cluster expansion to include at most two-mode excitations. As mentioned, one-mode excitations are redundant
and can be excluded as derived in Sec. II.C.3 of Ref.
16
.
This means that unlike in TDVCC
[2]
(with static modals), we do not need one-mode excitations in TDMVCC
[2]
, since
these degrees of freedom are covered by the time-dependent modals.
TDMVCC[2] is defined by letting
T\rightarrow T_{2}
,
L\rightarrow L_{2}
and
\mu\rightarrow\mu_{2}
.
In this study we further restrict ourselves to Hamiltonians containing at most two-mode couplings, i.e.
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{1}+\hat{H}_{2}
, where
\hat{H}_{j}
contains only
j
-mode couplings.
To be specific, let us write the cluster operators in detail:

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}<m_{2}}T^{m_{1}m_{2}}=\sum_{\mu_{2}}s_{\mu_{2}}\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}=\sum_{m_{1}<m_{2}}\sum_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}s^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}\tilde{\tau}^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}},

(32)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}<m_{2}}L^{m_{1}m_{2}}=\sum_{\mu_{2}}l_{\mu_{2}}\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}^{\ddagger}=\sum_{m_{1}<m_{2}}\sum_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}l^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}\tilde{\tau}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ddagger}_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}.

(33)

Note that the summations over modes in the
T_{2}
and
L_{2}
operators runs over unique pairs of modes.
In many summations later in this paper we will use a notation allowing us to utilize permutation
symmetry, i.e. we set
T^{m_{1}m_{2}}=T^{m_{2}m_{1}}
and

{s}^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{a^{m_{1}}a^{m_{2}}}={s}^{m_{2}m_{1}}_{a^{m_{2}}a^{m_{1}}}

and similarly for
L
.
The reasoning is that excitations in two modes
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
commute, as the mode indices are different by definition.
We can use this in detailed expressions including commutation, e.g.

\displaystyle[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{0}>m}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T^{mm_{0}}]+\sum_{m_{0}<m}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T^{m_{0}m}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{0}>m}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T^{mm_{0}}]+\sum_{m_{0}<m}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T^{mm_{0}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},T^{mm_{0}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}\sum_{a^{m}a^{m_{0}}}s^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}a^{m_{0}}}[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},\tilde{E}^{m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}]\tilde{E}^{m_{0}}_{a^{m_{0}}i^{m_{0}}}.

(34)

We express the Hamiltonian in a sum-of-products format with factors
containing one-mode operators,
\hat{h}^{mO^{m}}
,
where the
O^{m}
index refers to a specific operator.
Correspondingly, we have one-mode integrals of this
operator in the time-dependent basis denoted as
\tilde{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}
.
We can write the
\hat{H}_{1}
and
\hat{H}_{2}
operators as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\hat{H}_{1}^{m}=\sum_{m}\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\hat{h}^{mO^{m}}=\sum_{m}\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\sum_{p^{m}q^{m}}\tilde{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m<m_{0}}\hat{H}_{2}^{mm_{0}}=\sum_{m<m_{0}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\hat{h}^{mO^{m}}\hat{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m<m_{0}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{m}q^{m}\\
t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}\end{subarray}}\tilde{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}\tilde{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m_{0}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}{.}

(36)

Here,
C^{m}_{O^{m}}
and
C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}
are possible fitting coefficients.

The two-mode implementation presented in this paper is obtained by explicitly deriving and hand-coding every single non-zero term.
The overall idea and implementation follows that of Refs.
4
,
48
and
49
,
but the mean fields contain other types of terms. As the equations of motion for the
s_{\mu_{2}}
and
l_{\mu_{2}}
amplitudes presented in
Sec.
II.2
are equivalent to the ones derived explicitly in Ref.
4

(apart from the lack of the
\hat{g}
operator), we re-use the
\bm{\omega}^{\hat{H}}
and
\bm{\eta}^{\hat{H}}
transformers,
as well as the phase evaluation algorithm. The only terms that are left for us to implement are thus the constraint contributions, the modal derivatives,
and in particular the efficient evaluation of the
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector, the density matrix (
\bm{\rho}^{m}
) and the mean-field matrices (
\check{\mathbf{F}}^{m}
and
\check{\mathbf{F}}^{\prime m}
).
Of these terms, the mean-field matrices are the most complex to evaluate. They contain many demanding terms, and would be strongly time dominating unless
care is taken in reducing operation count and scaling by analysis and use of intermediates.
Naively, the computation of the mean-field matrices would scale as
M^{4}
, but this can be reduced to
M^{3}
as explored in later sections.

Including only two-mode excitations in the excitation space and in the Hamiltonian greatly reduces the number of terms from thousands to tens.
A first indication of this is given later in Tables
2
and
3
.

II.4.1
The density matrix

We will start out with an explicit derivation of the TDMVCC[2] density matrix, as it showcases general ideas that can be applied to many of the following equations.
Starting from the definition of the density matrix, we simply expand the bra and ket states and then apply a Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion which terminates after only two terms, because the
T
and
L
operators only contain two-mode excitations/deexcitations.
This procedure yields

\displaystyle\rho^{m}_{w^{m}v^{m}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}(1+L_{2})e^{-T_{2}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}e^{T_{2}}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}\big(1+L_{2}\big)\big(\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}+\big[\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}},T_{2}\big]\big){\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i^{m}w^{m}}\delta_{i^{m}v^{m}}+{\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}L_{2}\big[\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}},T_{2}\big]{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}.

(37)

Specifying the modals as either reference or virtual, only four combinations are possible:

1.

Passive: (
v^{m},w^{m})=(i^{m},i^{m}
)

2.

Up: (
v^{m},w^{m})=(a^{m},i^{m}
)

3.

Down: (
v^{m},w^{m})=(i^{m},a^{m}
)

4.

Forward: (
v^{m},w^{m})=(a^{m},b^{m}
).

It is rather easy to see that the up and down elements vanish as the shift operator becomes either an excitation or deexcitation operator, i.e.
\rho^{m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}=\rho^{m}_{i^{m}a^{m}}=0
. The remaining cases; passive and forward give non-zero contributions. The passive indices results in

\displaystyle\rho^{m}_{i^{m}i^{m}}

\displaystyle=1+{\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}L_{2}\left[\tilde{E}^{m}_{i^{m}i^{m}},T_{2}\right]{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}\sum_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}s^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}{\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}L_{2}\left[\tilde{E}^{m}_{i^{m}i^{m}},\tilde{\tau}^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}\right]{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}\sum_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}s^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}{\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}L_{2}\tilde{\tau}^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{i^{m}i^{m}}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}\sum_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}s^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}l^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}b^{m_{0}}}.

(38)

Following the same logic we can get an expression for the forward contribution, where we obtain a sum over matrix products given as

\rho^{m}_{b^{m}a^{m}}=\sum_{m_{0}\neq m}\sum_{c^{m_{0}}}s^{mm_{0}}_{b^{m}c^{m_{0}}}l^{mm_{0}}_{a^{m}c^{m_{0}}}.

(39)

II.4.2
The
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector

Another important quantity to compute is the
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector; see Eq. (A29) in Ref.
16
.
Utilizing the fact that
\mu
is strictly a two-mode excitation (
\mu_{2}
) simplifies this expression significantly. Equation (A29) reads

u^{m}_{a^{m}}=\tilde{F}^{{}^{\prime}m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}-\tilde{F}^{m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}+\sum_{\mu}\left({\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu}\big]{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}(i\dot{s}_{\mu})+(i\dot{l}_{\mu})\bra{\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}}e^{-T}\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}\right).

(40)

Assuming that the fully transformed mean-field matrices as well as
\dot{\mathbf{l}}
and
\dot{\mathbf{s}}

are calculated and saved elsewhere, we are left with the summation over
\mu
and write this out with explicit two-mode cluster operators as

u^{m}_{a^{m}}\leftarrow\sum_{\mu_{2}}\left({\bra{\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}}}(1+L_{2})e^{-T_{2}}\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}\big]e^{T_{2}}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}(i\dot{s}_{\mu_{2}})+(i\dot{l}_{\mu_{2}})\bra{\tilde{\mu}_{2}^{\prime}}e^{-T_{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}e^{T_{2}}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}\right).

(41)

The two terms are expanded using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion and examined one at a time. The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion in the first term evaluates to

e^{-T_{2}}\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}\big]e^{T_{2}}=\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}\big]+\Big[\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}\big],T_{2}\Big]+\bigg[\Big[\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}\big],T_{2}\Big],T_{2}\bigg].

(42)

It is seen that the resulting three terms contains an even number of excitations (2, 4, 6) due to
\tilde{\tau}_{\mu_{2}}
and
T_{2}
, and a single deexcitation due to
\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}
. In the full expression we also have the
(1+L_{2})
term containing 0 and 2 deexcitations. This means that in total we have an even number of excitations (2, 4, 6) and an odd number of deexcitations (1, 3) resulting in orthogonal bra and ket states. The first term in the
\mu_{2}
sum in Eq. (
40
) therefore always contributes 0 to the
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector.
The same procedure can be applied to the second term, again resulting in a vanishing contribution

\bra{\tilde{\mu}_{2}^{\prime}}e^{-T_{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}e^{T_{2}}{\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}=\bra{\tilde{\mu}_{2}^{\prime}}\left(\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}}+\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},T_{2}\big]+\Big[\big[\tilde{\tau}^{m\ddagger}_{a^{m}},T_{2}\big],T_{2}\Big]\right){\ket{\tilde{\Phi}}}=0.

(43)

Thus, the
u^{m}_{a^{m}}
vector elements for the two-mode couplings case is simply the difference of the first columns in the mean-field matrices, i.e.

u^{m}_{a^{m}}=\tilde{F}^{{}^{\prime}m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}-\tilde{F}^{m}_{a^{m}i^{m}}.

(44)

Again, this encourages an efficient implementation of the mean-field matrices which will be presented in Sec.
II.4.3
.

II.4.3
Mean-field equations

As mentioned in Sec.
II.2
an efficient implementation of the mean-field matrices is key to an efficient implementation of the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals method.
We will thus elaborate on the specifics of the equations for the mean fields in this and the next section.
The half-transformed mean fields defined in Ref.
16
are given as

\displaystyle\check{F}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}v^{m}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{v^{m}}{}[{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}},\hat{H}]{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}},

(45)

\displaystyle\check{F}^{\prime m}_{v^{m}\alpha^{m}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}[\hat{H},{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}]\tilde{b}^{m}_{v^{m}}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}.

(46)

To obtain computationally convenient expressions for the two-mode part we rewrite the primitive creation and annihilation operators in the biorthogonal basis using Eqs. (
4
), (
5
) and (
6
), giving the following two expressions

\displaystyle{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}}=\sum_{p^{m}}U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}p^{m}}\tilde{b}^{m}_{p^{m}},

(47)

\displaystyle{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}=\sum_{p^{m}}\tilde{a}^{m\dagger}_{p^{m}}W^{m}_{p^{m}\alpha^{m}}.

(48)

This means that

\displaystyle[{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}},\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}}]=U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}p^{m}}\tilde{b}^{m}_{q^{m}},

(49)

\displaystyle[\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}},{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}]=W^{m}_{q^{m}\alpha^{m}}\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{p^{m}}.

(50)

We can therefore rewrite the essential commutator part of the mean-field expressions using

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{mO^{m}}_{(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}=\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{v^{m}}[{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}},\hat{h}^{mO^{m}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{v^{m}}\sum_{p^{m}q^{m}}{\tilde{h}}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}[{a}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}},\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}q^{m}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{v^{m}}\sum_{p^{m}q^{m}}{\tilde{h}}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}p^{m}}\tilde{b}^{m}_{q^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{m}}{{}^{U\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{\alpha^{m}q^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}q^{m}}

(51)

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{\prime mO^{m}}_{(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}=[\hat{h}^{mO^{m}},\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{\alpha^{m}}]{a}^{m}_{v^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p^{m}}{{}^{W\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}\alpha^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{p^{m}v^{m}}

(52)

Here, we have introduced one-mode integrals that are transformed halfway into the primitive basis:

\displaystyle{{}^{U\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{\alpha^{m}q^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p^{m}}U^{m}_{\alpha^{m}p^{m}}\tilde{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}},

(53)

\displaystyle{{}^{W\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{w^{m}\alpha^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{m}}W^{m}_{q^{m}\alpha^{m}}\tilde{h}^{mO^{m}}_{p^{m}q^{m}}.

(54)

Now, if Eqs. (
35
), (
36
), (
49
) and (
50
) are inserted into Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
),
we can write the one- and two-mode Hamiltonian contributions to the mean fields as expectation values of effective operators. We thus obtain

\displaystyle{{}^{\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}v^{m}}={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{m}_{1,(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}+{\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{m}_{2,(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}},

(55)

\displaystyle{{}^{\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{\prime m}_{v^{m}\alpha^{m}}={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{\prime m}_{1,(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}+{\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{\prime m}_{2,(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}},

(56)

with the mode-locked operators defined as

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{m}_{1,(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\breve{H}^{mO^{m}}_{(\alpha^{m}v^{m})},

(57)

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{\prime m}_{1,(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\breve{H}^{\prime mO^{m}}_{(v^{m}\alpha^{m})},

(58)

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{m}_{2,(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}<m\\
m_{0}>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\breve{H}^{mO^{m}}_{(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}\tilde{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}},

(59)

\displaystyle\breve{H}^{\prime m}_{2,(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}<m\\
m_{0}>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\breve{H}^{\prime mO^{m}}_{(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}\tilde{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}}.

(60)

In accord with Ref.
16
, the contributions to the mean fields from
\hat{H}_{1}
can be computed efficiently from the density matrix as

\displaystyle{{}^{1M\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}v^{m}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{m}_{1,(\alpha^{m}v^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}=\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\sum_{w^{m}}{{}^{U\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{\alpha^{m}w^{m}}\rho^{m}_{w^{m}v^{m}}=\Big[{{}^{U\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{\mathbf{H}}^{m}_{1}\,\bm{\rho}^{m}\Big]_{\alpha^{m}v^{m}},

(61)

\displaystyle{{}^{1M\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{\prime m}_{v^{m}\alpha^{m}}

\displaystyle={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\breve{H}^{\prime m}_{1,(v^{m}\alpha^{m})}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}=\sum_{O^{m}}C^{m}_{O^{m}}\sum_{w^{m}}{{}^{W\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{w^{m}\alpha^{m}}\rho^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}=\Big[\bm{\rho}^{m}\,\,{{}^{W\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{\mathbf{H}}^{m}_{1}\Big]_{v^{m}\alpha^{m}}.

(62)

The two-mode contributions to the mean fields can be written as

\displaystyle{{}^{2M\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{m}_{\alpha^{m}v^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}<m\\
m_{0}>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\sum_{w^{m}}{{}^{U\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{\alpha^{m}w^{m}}\sum_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}\tilde{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}{\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m_{0}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}},

(63)

\displaystyle{{}^{2M\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{F}^{\prime m}_{v^{m}\alpha^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}<m\\
m_{0}>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}C^{mm_{0}}_{O^{m}O^{m_{0}}}\sum_{w^{m}}{{}^{W\mkern-2.0mu}}\check{h}^{mO^{m}}_{w^{m}\alpha^{m}}\sum_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}\tilde{h}^{m_{0}O^{m_{0}}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}{\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}\tilde{E}^{m}_{w^{m}v^{m}}\tilde{E}^{m_{0}}_{t^{m_{0}}u^{m_{0}}}{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}}.

(64)

II.4.5
The full active basis approach

The use of an active space smaller that the full space has been decisive for MCTDH and obviously anticipated
to be similarly advantageous for time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals.
However, it is of course an additional approximation that may give a basis set truncation error,
and it gives rise to additional numerical steps, projections etc.
In the full active basis limit the time-evolving basis has solely the purpose of making the reference optimal, which is obviously a theoretically interesting limit.
For these reasons we consider the efficient implementation of using the full space
as active space to study advantages and disadvantages.
In the limit where the primitive and time-dependent modal bases are equal in size, the equations of motion simplify significantly, and we only need to compute the parts of the mean-field matrices necessary for computing the
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector. We see in Eq. (
44
) that this is simply the first column of both the matrices. Expanding the right-hand side of Eq. (112) from Ref.
16
,

\tilde{F}^{\prime m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}-\tilde{F}^{m}_{v^{m}w^{m}}={\bra{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}}}[\hat{H},\tilde{E}^{m}_{w^{m}v^{m}}]{\ket{\tilde{\Psi}}},

(73)

as was done in the previous sections results in terms with structure similar to the ones in Eq. (
II.4.4
) and (
II.4.4
), but with fully transformed mean-field matrices rather than half-transformed ones. These two approaches are of course equivalent in the limit of no secondary modal space, but the implementation differs a bit as new efficient intermediates/transformations might become available.

Initially we had hoped for a very fast specialized implementation of this limit, but despite the apparent simplicity of the
\mathbf{u}^{m}
vector equations, many expensive terms remain to be computed, and the approach still scales as
M^{3}
.

II.5
Hybrid TDMVCC/TDH scheme

In this section we present a computational scheme introduced for obtaining an important computational limit which opens up for efficient calculations of large systems. The idea is to apply time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals in the usual way for a selected set of important modes and then only have a single active time-dependent modal for the rest.
Thus, we restrict these modes to stay in the reference modal. In this way we obtain a TDMVCC/TDH hybrid scheme where we do not ignore less important modes, as may be a more standard approach. Instead, they are included in a simplified way which makes the computations significantly faster compared to TDMVCC[2].
We denote the modes with the simplified treatment as TDH modes but emphasize that their mean fields will
be different from the pure TDH mean fields due to the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals modes.
Thus, one can state that the ket state of the system can be written like

{\ket{\bar{\Psi}}}=e^{-i{\epsilon}}e^{T^{\mathrm{TDMVCC}}}\prod_{m\in\mathrm{MC}^{\mathrm{TDMVCC}}}\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{i^{m}}\prod_{m\in\mathrm{MC}^{\mathrm{TDH}}}\tilde{a}^{m\,\dagger}_{i^{m}}\ket{{\mathrm{vac}}}

(74)

The speedup achieved by TDMVCC/TDH is seen by the fact that we can skip certain parts of the calculations involved in solving the equations of motion as these TDH modes do not have any virtual modals. For example, we see this effect in the density matrices and the mean-field matrices. The density matrix for a TDH mode reduces to a 1-by-1 matrix with the element
1
, as can be seen in Eqs. (
II.4.1
) and (
39
).
The mean-field matrices for the TDH modes reduce to a vector with very few contributions as seen in Eqs. (
II.4.4
) and (
II.4.4
), where the intermediates with a virtual modal index does not contribute.

This approach will be explored further with numerical results in Sec.
III.3
where we utilize it to do computations on a 39-mode potential-energy surface (coupled at the two-mode level) describing benzoic acid.
We will also denote this method as TDMVCC[2|X] where the X denotes the number of modes propagated at the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals level. The X notation will be used both with absolute numbers, but also with relative quantities indicating a certain percentage of the systems modes which have been propagated at the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals level.
We note that TDMVCC[2|0] will occasionally be named the TDH limit.

The described methods have been implemented in the Molecular Interactions, Dynamics And Simulations Chemistry Program Package
MidasCpp

IVR for Benzoic acid

To explore further the numerical behavior of TDMVCC[2] and the quality of the TDMVCC[2|X] method we present some results on the benzoic acid molecule (39 modes).
The potential-energy surface was obtained using the adaptive density-guided approach
sparta_adaptive_2009
procedure in Molecular Interactions, Dynamics And Simulations Chemistry Program Package
MidasCpp
and the GFN2-xTB method
gfn2

in the xTB program for computing cheap single points in these exploratory computations.
adaptive density-guided approach settings are given in the supporting information and the final potential-energy surface has 35,956 terms.
The dynamics of the benzoic acid molecule is initialized by using a VSCF state with the mode describing the acid O–H stretch in the first excited state (described by the
v=1
modal) computed on an uncoupled potential-energy surface and then letting this state relax in the coupled potential-energy surface.

It is clear that increasing X ensures that the expectation values converge towards the
time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals[2] limit, where all modes at treated at the coupled cluster level.
We also note that if one wants to do computations on a very large system the computational cost can be significantly reduced by the hybrid approach, and the results are qualitatively very similar. Another observation is that the hybrid approach is much better than time-dependent Hartree.

IV
Summary and outlook

This work presents the first efficient implementation of the time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals method. Focusing
on the two-mode coupling level, all detailed equations were derived and implemented
by hand.
We showed that the computational cost of TDMVCC[2] can be reduced to cubic scaling
with respect to the number of degrees of freedom by introducing appropriate intermediates.
Specializations were made, allowing efficient and flexible usage of both
full active basis sets and basis sets limited to only a few active basis function per mode.
The latter allows for a hybrid TDMVCC/TDH approach
that has an even lower quadratic computational scaling
as long as the set of TDMVCC modes is kept constant and
only the number of TDH modes is extended.
The resulting low order scaling was illustrated
for polyaromatic hydrocarbons with up to 264 modes,
and the gain of TDMVCC/TDH hybrid computations compared to simply
freezing some degrees of freedom
was illustrated in computations on a 39-mode benzoic acid potential-energy surface.

The ability to perform these high-dimensional computations shows how
TDMVCC may open for accurate quantum dynamical studies
on systems that were previously out of reach.
The hybrid methods introduced further opens a new line of research where TDMVCC computations
can be tailored to balance accuracy, efficiency, and feasibility in quantum dynamical computations.

The work presented here is only the first step in making TDMVCC more generally
applicable. In many cases the present two-mode version will not be sufficiently accurate,
and higher-level TDMVCC and higher-level couplings in the Hamiltonian will be important.
This includes flexible multi-reference schemes along the lines
of recent work on MR-MCTDH
[n]
madsen_mrmctdh_2020
.
Implementing such higher-level TDMVCC methods must generally be considered
as a very hard problem as higher-order TDMVCC methods with higher-order Hamiltonians include numerous terms (on the order of many thousands)

madsen_general_timedependent_2020
.
For time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster (with static modals), it has been possible to make a general implementation
madsen_general_timedependent_2020

with well-defined low-order polynomial scaling of the computational effort by utilizing existing machinery for
automatic derivation, analysis and computation of all terms that appear in the equations. However, time-dependent vibrational coupled cluster with time-dependent modals
contains new types of terms (particularly in the mean fields) that fall outside the scope of our current code base.
The present work has shown that it is possible and important to construct certain
appropriate intermediates to obtain low-order computational scaling.
This work therefore paves the way for and encourages the laborious work
on general machinery for implementing general order TDMVCC.

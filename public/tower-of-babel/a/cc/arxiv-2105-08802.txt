Stratonovich solution for the wave equation
Raluca M. Balan

Abstract
In this article, we construct a Stratonovich solution for the stochastic wave equation in spatial dimension d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 , with time-independent noise and linear term σ ⁡ ( u ) = u \sigma(u)=u multiplying the noise. The noise is spatially homogeneous and its spectral measure satisfies an integrability condition which is stronger than Dalang’s condition. We give a probabilistic representation for this solution, similar to the Feynman-Kac-type formula given in [ 5 ] for the solution of the stochastic wave equation with spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise, that is white in time. We also give the chaos expansion of the Stratonovich solution and we compare it with the chaos expansion of the Skorohod solution from [ 1 ] .

Introduction

In this article, we study the stochastic wave equation with time-independent noise:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta v(t,x)+v(t,x)\dot{W}(x)\quad\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad(d\leq 2)\\
v(0,x)=1,\quad\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}(0,x)=0\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(1)

The noise is given by a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, with covariance

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)\gamma(x-y)dxdy:=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
is a non-negative-definite function. By the Bochner-Schwartz theorem, there exists a tempered measure
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
in the sense of distributions, and hence

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi)}\mu(d\xi)\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
W=\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{H}\}
is an isonormal Gaussian process, and we can use Malliavin calculus with respect to
W
to analyze the solution of (
1
).
We recall briefly some basic facts from Malliavin calculus which are needed in this paper. We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for more details.
For any
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{n}
the
n
-th Wiener chaos space corresponding to
W
, i.e. the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
generated by
\{H_{n}(W(\varphi));\varphi\in\mathcal{H},\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1\}
, where
H_{n}(x)
is the Hermite polynomial of order
n
.
Any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is measurable with respect to
W
, has the Wiener chaos expansion:

F=E(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n})

where
I_{n}:\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the multiple Wiener integral of order
n
.
We let
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
and
I_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the identity map.
Moreover,

\mathbb{E}|F|^{2}=\big(\mathbb{E}(F)\big)^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

where
\widetilde{f}_{n}
is the symmetrization of
f_{n}
. Moreover,

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.

We impose the following assumption, which is needed for Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
below:

Assumption A.

(a)
\gamma
is continuous, symmetric, and
\gamma(x)<\infty
if and only if
x\not=0
;

(b)
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

A basic example is the Riesz kernel
\gamma(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,d)
, in which case
\mu(d\xi)=C_{d,\alpha}|\xi|^{-(d-\alpha)}d\xi
and
C_{d,\alpha}>0
is a constant depending on
d
and
\alpha
.

We are interested in the Stratonovich solution of (
1
), whose definition is given below.

Definition 1.1
.

The random field
\{v(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is a (mild)
Stratonovich solution
of equation (
1
) if for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability 1,

v(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)v(s,y)W^{\circ}(dy)\right)ds,

where the
W^{\circ}(dy)
denotes the Stratonovich integral.

We recall that the Stratonovich integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(x)W^{\circ}(dx)
of the random field
\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is defined as the following limit in probability, if it exists:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(x)W^{\circ}(dx)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(x)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)dx,

where
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)=W(p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot))
is a mollification of
W
and
p_{\varepsilon}(x)=(2\pi\varepsilon)^{-d/2}e^{-|x^{2}|/(2\varepsilon)}
.

We denote by
G
the fundamental solution of the wave equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle G(t,x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}1_{\{|x|<t\}}&\text{if $d=1$},\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}1_{\{|x|<t\}}&\text{if $d=2$},\\
\end{cases}

(2)

Note that
G(t,\cdot)
is integrable and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t,x)dx=t
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\varphi(x)dx

the Fourier transform of a function
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot)(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\quad\mbox{for all}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The following inequality will play an important role in the present article:

|\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot)(\xi)|\leq C_{t}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

(3)

with
C_{t}=2\sqrt{2}(t\vee 1)
. To see this, use
\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\leq 2(t\vee 1)\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}
and
\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{2}{1+|\xi|^{2}}
.

The first goal of this article is to prove the existence of a Stratonovich solution to equation (
1
).
This will be achieved under the following condition:

\displaystyle K_{\mu}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

(C)

which, due to (
3
), implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot)(\xi)|\mu(d\xi)\leq C_{t}K_{\mu}.

(4)

The Stratonovich solution
v
is different than the Skorohod solution
u
of equation (
1
), which satisfies (by definition) the following integral equation:

u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)u(s,y)W(\delta y)ds,

(5)

where
W(\delta y)
denotes the Skorohod integral (see Section
6
below).
In
[
1
]
, it was shown that under
Dalang’s condition
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

(D)

the Skorohod solution exists and has the chaos expansion
u(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\big)
, where

f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x;t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n},

(6)

and
T_{n}(t)=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,t]^{n};t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}\}
. Here

t_{n+1}=t
and
x_{n+1}=x
.

If
\gamma
satisfies the scaling property

\gamma(cx)=c^{-\alpha}\gamma(x)
for all
c>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
for some
\alpha>0
, then
\mu(cA)=c^{\alpha}\mu(A)
for any
c>0
and
A\in{\cal B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and (C) is equivalent to
\alpha\in(0,1)

while (D) is equivalent to
\alpha\in(0,2)
.

In the case of the heat equation:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{W}(x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ (d\geq 1)\\
\quad u(0,x)=1\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(7)

it was proved in
[
6
]
that the Skorohod solution
u_{h}
and the Stratonovich solution
v_{h}
exist under Dalang’s condition, and they admit the following Feynman-Kac representations:

u_{h}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{B}[e^{V(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}M(t)}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{h}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{B}[e^{V(t,x)}].

Here
B=(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion independent of
W
and
B_{t}^{x}=B_{t}+x
. The functional
V(t,x)
, defined formally by

V(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta_{0}(B_{r}^{x}-y)W(dy)dr,

is the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-limit of
V^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\varepsilon}(B_{r}^{x}-y)W(dy)dr
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,
and the correction term

M(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{s}-B_{r})dsdr

is the conditional variance of
V(t,x)
given
B
. If in addition,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{1-\delta}\mu(d\xi)<\infty\quad\mbox{for some $\delta\in(0,1)$},

then, by Proposition 5.28 of
[
6
]
,
W
has a version with values in a weighted Besov space, and equation (
7
) has a pathwise solution which coincides with the Stratonovich solution.
The construction of the Stratonovich solution of equation (
7
) (given by Theorem 5.7 of
[
6
]
) relies heavily on the Feynman-Kac representation of this solution, and cannot be extended to the wave equation.

In this article, we develop a method for constructing a Stratonovich solution based on chaos expansions, which seems to be new in the literature. We implement this method for the wave equation, but we believe that the method is so robust that can be applied to a large class of SPDEs with time-dependent noise and linear term
\sigma(u)=u
multiplying the noise.
(We postpone the study of the wave equation with time-dependent noise for future work.)
We believe that this method can also be applied to show the existence of a Stratonovich solution for the heat equation under condition (D), using inequality:

\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}G_{h}(t,\cdot)(\xi)dt\leq 4(T\vee 1)\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}},

instead of (
3
),
where
\mathcal{F}G_{h}(t,\cdot)(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}/2}
is Fourier transform in the space variable of the fundamental solution
G_{h}(t,x)=p_{t}(x)
of the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The idea is to use the classical method of Picard’s iterations for solving the equation with mollified noise
W^{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}v^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x),\quad\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad(d\leq 2)\\
v^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=1,\quad\dfrac{\partial v^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}(0,x)=0\end{cases}

(8)

and then apply the product formula from Malliavin calculus for writing the
n
-th Picard approximation
v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
of
v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
as a finite sum of multiple Wiener integrals. The delicate part is to let
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Finally, we let
n\to\infty
.
This method involves some non-trivial combinatorial arguments, which appeared for the first time in the earlier works
[
7
,
8
]
of Hu and Meyer on multiple Stratonovich integrals with respect to Brownian motion.

We are now ready to state the first main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

If Assumption A holds and
\mu
satisfies condition (C), then there exists a process
v=\{v(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
such that for any
T>0
and
p\geq 2
,

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-v(t,x)|^{p}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$},

(9)

where
v^{\varepsilon}
is the solution of (
8
). The random variable
v(t,x)
can be represented as

v(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}H_{n}(t,x),

(10)

where
H(t,x)=I_{n}^{\circ}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\big)
is the multiple Stratonovich integral of
f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)
, and is given by relation (
32
) below.
Moreover,
v
is a Stratonovich solution of equation (
1
).

The series (
10
) can be used to derive the Wiener chaos expansion of
v(t,x)
, but the explicit expression of the projection on each Wiener chaos space is quite involved, since
H_{n}(t,x)
is a sum of multiple Wiener integrals of order
n-2k
, for
k=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Note that the term of this sum which corresponds to
k=0
is the
n
-th chaos term appearing in the Wiener chaos expansion of the Skorohod solution
u(t,x)
(see Section
6
below).

The second goal of this article is to give a Feynman-Kac-type formula for the Stratonovich solution of equation (
1
), similar to the one given by Dalang, Mueller and Tribe in
[
5
]
for Itô-Skorohod solution of the wave equation with spatially-homogeneous Gaussian noise that is white in time. Unlike the Feynman-Kac formula for the solution of the heat equation (which uses the paths of Brownian motion), this formula is based on a process whose paths are obtained by linear interpolation between the jump times of a Poisson process.

More precisely, let
N=(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Poisson process of rate
1
, with jump times
(\tau_{i})_{i\geq 1}
. Denote
\tau_{0}=0
. Let
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. random variables with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with density
G(1,\cdot)
. Note that if
d=1
,
U_{1}
has a uniform distribution on
(-1,1)
. Assume that
N
,
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and
W
are independent.

Based on
N
and
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, we define the linearly interpolated process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as follows:

X_{t}=X_{\tau_{i}}+(t-\tau_{i})U_{i+1}\quad\mbox{for $\tau_{i}<t\leq\tau_{i+1}$, $i\geq 0$,}

with
X_{0}=0
. Let
X_{t}^{x}=x+X_{t}
.

The next theorem is the second main result of this paper, which gives the Feynman-Kac-type representation for
v
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If Assumption A holds,
\mu
satisfies condition (C), and
v
is the Stratonovich solution of equation (
1
) given by Theorem
1.2
, then for any
t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, almost surely,

\displaystyle v(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N,X}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{N_{t}}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})\prod_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\dot{W}(X_{\tau_{i}}^{x})\Big]

(11)

\displaystyle:=e^{t}\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}^{N,X}\Big[1_{\{N_{t}=n\}}\prod_{i=1}^{N_{t}}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})\prod_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\dot{W}(X_{\tau_{i}}^{x})\Big],

where the terms of the series are defined by relation (
51
) below if
n\geq 1
, and we use the convention
\prod_{i=1}^{0}=1
if
n=0
(so that the first term of the series is
e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N,X}[1_{\{N_{t}=0\}}]=1
).

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we analyze equation (
8
) with mollified noise. The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
are given in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.
In Section

. In Section

2
Equation with mollified noise

In this section, we study equation (
8
) with mollified noise
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}
.

2.1
Existence of solution

The mollified noise
\{\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a zero-mean Gaussian process with stationary increments (in Yaglom sense) and covariance function:

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)})^{2}e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi).

Under condition (D),

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}=\|p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)=:C_{\mu,\varepsilon}<\infty

and

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}=z_{p}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2})^{p/2}\leq z_{p}C_{\mu,\varepsilon}^{p/2}\quad\mbox{for any $p>0$},

(12)

where

z_{p}=E|Z|^{p}=\frac{2^{p/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)\quad\mbox{with $Z\sim N(0,1)$}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the random field
v^{\varepsilon}=\{v^{\varepsilon}(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is a
solution
of equation (
8
) if for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability
1
,

v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)dyds.

(13)

Intuitively,
v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
should be given by the series:

v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i})d{\bf x}d{\bf t},

(14)

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and we let
t_{n+1}=t
and
x_{n+1}=x
.

Note that (
14
) cannot be obtained by a pathwise application of Proposition 2.2 of
[
5
]
, since the potential
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)
is not bounded.
We will justify below relation (
14
) for fixed
\varepsilon>0
. The fact that the series (
14
) converges
uniformly
in
\varepsilon
will be proved in Section
3.3
.

Let
v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
be the
n
-th Picard iteration, where

H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i})d{\bf x}d{\bf t}.

(15)

Note that
H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
is well-defined due to
inequality (
16
) below.
Let
H_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1
. We have the following recurrence relation: for any
n\geq 0
,

H_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)dyds.

The next result shows that the series (
14
) converges, for any
\varepsilon>0
fixed.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under condition (D), for any
\varepsilon>0
fixed,
\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for any
p\geq 1
and
T>0
, i.e.

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{p}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

Moreover,
v^{\varepsilon}
is a solution of equation (
8
).

Proof.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
be the norm in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Using Minkowski inequality, the generalized Hölder inequality and inequality (
12
), we obtain that for any
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i})\Big\|_{p}d{\bf x}d{\bf t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i})\|_{pn}d{\bf x}d{\bf t}

\displaystyle\leq z_{pn}^{1/p}C_{\varepsilon}^{n/2}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})d{\bf x}d{\bf t}

\displaystyle=z_{pn}^{1/p}C_{\mu,\varepsilon}^{n/2}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})d{\bf t}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{2^{pn/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{pn+1}{2}\right)\right\}^{1/p}C_{\mu,\varepsilon}^{n/2}\frac{t^{2n}}{(2n)!}.

Since
\Gamma(\frac{pn+1}{2})\leq C_{p}^{n}(n!)^{p/2}
and
(2n)!\geq c_{0}^{n}(n!)^{2}
(due to Stirling’s formula), we obtain:

\|H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\frac{2^{n/2}}{\pi^{1/(2p)}}C_{p}^{n/p}C_{\mu,\varepsilon}^{n/2}c_{0}^{n}\frac{t^{2n}}{(n!)^{3/2}}.

(16)

Hence,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\|_{p}<\infty

and the sequence
\{v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\}_{n}
is Cauchy in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To prove that
v^{\varepsilon}
is a solution to (
8
), we let
n\to\infty
in the recurrence relation:

v_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)dyds.

(17)

The left-hand side converges in
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
. The right-hand side converges in
L^{1}(\Omega)
to
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)dyds
, by the dominated convergence theorem. To justify the application of this theorem, we use the fact that

\mathbb{E}|\big(v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,y)-v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\big)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|v_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,y)-v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The last term is bounded by a constant (that depends on
\varepsilon
), due to (
12
) and the uniform convergence of
(v_{n}^{\varepsilon})_{n}
.
∎

2.2
Feynman-Kac-type formula

In this section, we give a Feynman-Kac-type representation for
v^{\varepsilon}
. Note that this cannot be obtained directly by a pathwise application of Theorem 3.2 of

First, note

\langle p_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-\cdot),p_{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=(p_{2\varepsilon}*\gamma)(x_{1}-x_{2}),

(25)

in the sense of distributions, where

\mu_{\varepsilon}(d\xi)=e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi).

(26)

We need to introduce some notation.

Definition 3.1

The

of order

Using Theorem

for expressing the product

and (

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}I_{n-2k}\big(\bigotimes_{j\in J}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}-\cdot)\big)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p_{2\varepsilon}*\gamma)(x_{\ell_{i}}-x_{m_{i}}).

(27)

This shows that

exists if
I_{n-2k}(f_{k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}I_{n-2k}(f_{k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}})
as

, for any

, for any set

and for any partition

as above,
where

\displaystyle f_{k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((y_{j})_{j\in J}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\prod_{j\in J}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}-y_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p_{2\varepsilon}*\gamma)(x_{\ell_{i}}-x_{m_{i}})dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}

\displaystyle f_{k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2k}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\gamma(x_{\ell_{i}}-x_{m_{i}})d\big((x_{j})_{j\in J^{c}}\big).

Moreover, in this case,

|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}I_{n-2k}(f_{k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}).

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\circ}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}f(x_{1},x_{2})W(dx_{1})W(dx_{2})+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}f(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(x_{1}-x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2},

For

\displaystyle W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})W^{\circ}(dx_{3})

\displaystyle=W(dx_{1})W(dx_{2})W(dx_{3})+W(dx_{1})\gamma(x_{2}-x_{3})dx_{2}dx_{3}+

\displaystyle\quad W(dx_{2})\gamma(x_{1}-x_{3})dx_{1}dx_{3}+W(dx_{3})\gamma(x_{1}-x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}.

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{\circ}(dx_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\

(29)

H_{n}(t,x)
. By Minkowski’s inequality and hypercontractivity,

\displaystyle\|H_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}(p-1)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\big((n-2k)!\big)^{1/2}\|A_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}}.

We now use (
38
). Since there are
\binom{n}{2k}
sets
J\subset[n]
with
|J|=n-2k
, and for each set
J
there are

\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!}
(unordered)
partitions
\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}
of
J^{c}
with
|I_{i}|=2
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle\|H_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{n!}{(n-2k)!\,2^{k}k!}(p-1)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\big((n-2k)!\big)^{1/2}\left[\frac{t^{n}}{n!}K_{\mu}^{n}C_{t}^{2(n-k)}\frac{2^{k}t^{n+2k}}{(n+2k)!}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\mu,t}^{n/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{(2n)!}{(n-2k)!\,k!\,(n+k)!}\cdot\frac{n!}{(2n)!}\cdot\left(\frac{(n-2k)!}{n!}\right)^{1/2}\big((n+k)!\big)^{1/2},

where
C_{p,\mu,t}=(p-1)K_{\mu}C_{t}^{2}t(t\vee 1)^{3/2}
and we used the fact that
(n+2k)!\geq(n+k)!
. By Stirling’s formula,
(2n)!\geq c_{1}^{-n}(n!)^{2}
and
(n+k)!=\Gamma(n+k+1)\leq\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}n+1)\leq c_{2}^{n}(n!)^{3/2}
, where
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
are positive constants. Since
(n-2k)!\leq n!
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|H_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\mu,T}^{n/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{2n}{n-2k,k,n+k}\cdot\frac{c_{1}^{n}}{n!}\cdot c_{2}^{n/2}(n!)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\mu,T}^{n/2}\,c_{1}^{n}c_{2}^{n/2}\frac{1}{(n!)^{1/4}}3^{2n}.

This proves (
45
).
∎

We now prove the uniform convergence in
\varepsilon
which was mentioned in Section
2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

If Assumption A and condition (C) holds, then for any

and

,

and
\varepsilon>0
, where

is the solution of equation (

Proof.

As in Theorem
3.3
, it is enough to prove that

Recall decomposition (
34
) of
H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
and the fact that

Note that

has the same expression as

(defined by (

)), but with

replaced by
h_{{\bf t},(x_{j})_{j\in J^{c}}}^{(t,x)}*p_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes(n-2k)}
. Similarly to

\displaystyle\|A_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}}^{2}\leq\frac{t^{n}}{n!}C_{t}^{2(n-k)}K_{\mu}^{k}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(t_{m_{i}+1}-t_{m_{i}})^{2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k}}e^{-\varepsilon\sum_{j\in J}|\xi_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\prod_{j\in J}\frac{1}{1+|\sum_{s=1}^{j}\xi_{s}|^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\sum_{s=1}^{\ell_{i}}\xi_{s}|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{\xi_{\ell_{i}}=-\xi_{m_{i}}=\eta_{i}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\varepsilon}(d\eta_{i})\prod_{j\in J}\mu(d\xi_{j})d{\bf t}.

Using the bound

for

The rest of the proof is the same as for Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Proof.

Step 1.
Since

where

(47)

Step 2.

We use relations (
32
) and (
34
) for expressing

and

. We obtain:

\displaystyle v(s,y)-v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\

\displaystyle\big(v(s,y)-v^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\big)\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\

(48)

We apply the product formula (
60
) for computing the product of the two Wiener integrals:

\displaystyle I_{n-2k}\big(B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)\big)I_{1}\big(p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle=I_{n-2k+1}\big(\widetilde{B}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)\otimes p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle+(n-2k)I_{n-2k-1}\big(\widetilde{B}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)\otimes_{1}p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\big),

\widetilde{B}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)

B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)

Note that for any functions
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}

I_{m}(\widetilde{f}\otimes g)=I_{m}(f\otimes g),

since

mI_{m-1}(\widetilde{f}\otimes_{1}g)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m-1}}\langle f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},\cdot,x_{\ell+1},\ldots,x_{m}),g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}}^{m}W(dx_{j}).

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{n-2k}\big(B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)\big)I_{1}\big(p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\big)=I_{n-2k+1}\big(B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,s,y)\otimes p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\big)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sum_{\ell\in J}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k-1}}\langle B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},\cdot,s,y),p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}}^{m}W(dx_{j}),

(49)

where
B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},\cdot,s,y)
denotes the function
x_{\ell}\mapsto B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}((x_{j})_{j\in J},s,y)

We introduce expression (
49

48

on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using stochastic Fubini’s theorem for interchanging the multiple Wiener integral with the

\displaystyle L^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}\Big(\mathcal{I}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\mathcal{J}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\Big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k+1}}F_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},z,t,x\big)\prod_{j\in J}W(dx_{j})W(dz)

\displaystyle F_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},z,t,x\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}((x_{j})_{j\in J},s,y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)dyds

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in J}\mathcal{J}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k-1}}Q_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},t,x\big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}}W(dx_{j})

\displaystyle Q_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},t,x\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)\langle B_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},\cdot,s,y\big),p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}dyds.

Since

(see (

and

, where

\displaystyle G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},z,t,x\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)T_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},s,y\big)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)dyds

\displaystyle R_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},t,x\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)\langle T_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},\cdot,s,y\big),p_{\varepsilon}(y-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}dyds,

and

have the same form as
G^{\varepsilon}
, respectively
R^{\varepsilon}
, but with

replaced by

.

We obtain the decomposition:

\displaystyle L^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}\Big(\mathcal{G}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\mathcal{G}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\prime\varepsilon}(t,x)+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\mathcal{R}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\mathcal{R}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\prime\varepsilon}(t,x)\Big)

\displaystyle=:L_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+L_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+L_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+L_{4}^{\varepsilon}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k+1}}G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},z,t,x\big)\prod_{j\in J}W(dx_{j})W(dz)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in J}\mathcal{R}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k-1}}R_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},t,x\big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}}W(dx_{j}),

\mathcal{G}^{\prime\varepsilon}
has the same form as
\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}
but with
G^{\varepsilon}
replaced by
G^{\prime\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime\varepsilon}=\sum_{\ell\in J}\mathcal{R}^{\prime\varepsilon,\ell}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime\varepsilon,\ell}
has the same form as
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon,\ell}
but with
R^{\varepsilon,\ell}
replaced by
R^{\prime\varepsilon,\ell}
.

We will prove that

\mathbb{E}|L_{i}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\downarrow 0,\quad\mbox{for}\ i=1,2,3,4.

(50)

Step 3.
We treat
L_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
.
We proceed similarly to Step 3 of the proof of Theorem
3.2
. Note that the function
G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\in L^{1}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k+1})
. Letting
t_{n+1}=s
and
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})
, it can be proved that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\big((\xi_{j})_{j\in J},\eta\big)=e^{-i(\sum_{j\in J}\xi_{j})\cdot x}e^{-i\eta\cdot x}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\eta|^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j\in J}|\xi_{j}|^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{T_{n+1}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\mathcal{F}G(t-t_{n+1},\cdot)\big(\sum_{j\in J}\xi_{j}+\eta\big)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G(t_{j+1}-t_{j},\cdot)\big(\sum_{s=1}^{j}\xi_{s}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{\xi_{\ell_{i}}=-\xi_{m_{i}}=\eta_{i}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{\varepsilon}(d\eta_{i})d{\bf t}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality as above, and using the estimate (
37

\displaystyle\|G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(n-2k+1)}}^{2}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k+1}}|\mathcal{F}G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\big((\xi_{j})_{j\in J},\eta\big)|^{2}\prod_{j\in J}\mu(d\xi_{j})\mu(d\eta)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{t}^{k}K_{\mu}^{k}\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\int_{T_{n+1}(t)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(t_{m_{i}+1}-t_{m_{i}})^{2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-2k+1}}e^{-\varepsilon|\eta|^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j\in J}|\xi_{j}|^{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}|\mathcal{F}G(t-t_{n+1},\cdot)(\sum_{j\in J}\xi_{j}+\eta)|^{2}\prod_{j\in J}|\mathcal{F}G(t_{j+1}-t_{j},\cdot)(\sum_{s=1}^{j}\xi_{s})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\mathcal{F}G(t_{\ell_{i}+1}-t_{\ell_{i}},\cdot)(\sum_{s=1}^{\ell_{i}}\xi_{s})|\prod_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{\xi_{\ell_{i}}=-\xi_{m_{i}}=\eta_{i}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu(d\eta_{i})\prod_{j\in J}\mu(d\xi_{j})d{\bf t}.

The last integral converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, by the dominated convergence theorem. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|L_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\
I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,k\end{subarray}}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{G}_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\
|J|=n-2k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}\ {\rm partition}\ {\rm of}\ J^{c}\\

The last series converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, again by the dominated convergence theorem. The application of this theorem is justified as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Step 4.
We treat
L_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
. It can be proved that the function

and its Fourier transform is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}G_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\prime\varepsilon}(\cdot,t,x)\big((\xi_{j})_{j\in J},\eta\big)=e^{-i(\sum_{j\in J}\xi_{j})\cdot x}e^{-i\eta\cdot x}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\eta|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\int_{T_{n+1}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\mathcal{F}G(t-t_{n+1},\cdot)\big(\sum_{j\in J}\xi_{j}+\eta\big)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G(t_{j+1}-t_{j},\cdot)(\sum_{s=1}^{j}\xi_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{\xi_{\ell_{i}}=-\xi_{m_{i}}=\eta_{i}\}}\Big(1-e^{-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\eta_{i}|^{2}}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu(d\eta_{i})d{\bf t},

. As in Step 4, one can show that

as

.

Step 5.
We treat

. Expressing the

\displaystyle R_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}\big((x_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
j\not=\ell\end{subarray}},t,x\big)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi_{\ell}\cdot y}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\xi_{\ell}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi_{\ell}\cdot x_{l}}T_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\big((x_{j})_{j\in J},s,y\big)dx_{\ell}\right)\mu(d\xi_{\ell})dyds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}R_{n,k,J,I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}}^{\varepsilon,\ell}(\cdot,t,x)\big((\xi_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\

as

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{F}G(t-t_{n+1},\cdot)\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\

4
Feynman-Kac-type formula

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
First, we introduce the notation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{N,X}\Big[1_{\{N_{t}=n\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(X_{\tau_{i}}^{x})\Big]:=

(51)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\mathbb{E}^{N}\Big[1_{\{N_{t}=n\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{G(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1},x_{i}-x_{i-1})}{\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{\circ}(dx_{i})\Big],

where
\tau_{0}=0
,
x_{0}=x
.
This notation is motivated by the formal relation

\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{\circ}(dx_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(x_{i})dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}
(see
(
29
)) and a formal application of Lemma
2.4
(similar to the proof of Lemma
2.3
), with
X=(X_{\tau_{1}}^{x},\ldots,X_{\tau_{n}}^{x})
,
Y=N
,
Z=W
,

g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},W)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(x_{i})\quad\mbox{and}\quad h(N)=1_{\{N_{t}=n\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}),

We need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any random variable
Y:\Omega\to\mathcal{Y}
and a non-negative measurable function
f
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\mathcal{Y}
, such that
f(\cdot,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
for any
y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathbb{E}[f(\cdot,Y)]\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
, we have:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}[f(x_{1},\ldots,y_{n},Y)]\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{\circ}(dx_{i})=\mathbb{E}^{Y}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},Y)\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{\circ}(dx_{i})\right].

(52)

Proof.

We first examine the case
n=1
. Recall that (for deterministic integrands),
W^{\circ}(dx)=W(dx)
.
Let
\mu_{Y}
be the law of
Y
. By the stochastic Fubini theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{Y}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,Y)W(dx)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,y)W(dx)\right)\mu_{Y}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{Y}}f(x,y)\mu_{Y}(dy)\right)W(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[f(x,Y)]W(dx).

(53)

The general case is proved in the same way, using linearity. For instance, for
n=2
, we use the fact that
W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})=W(dx_{1})W(dx_{2})+\gamma(x_{1}-x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.3
follows from the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

If Assumption A and condition (C) hold, for any
n\geq 1
,
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability 1,

H_{n}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{N,X}\Big[1_{\{N_{t}=n\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(X_{\tau_{i}}^{x})\Big],

where the right-hand-side is defined by (
51
).

Proof.

We treat only the cases
n=1
and
n=2
. The general case is similar.

Let
n=1
. Using the Poisson representation (
19
) and relation (
53
) (with
Y=N
),

\displaystyle H_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-t_{1},x-x_{1})W(dx_{1})dt_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}G(s_{1},x_{1}-x)ds_{1}\right)W(dx_{1})

\displaystyle=e^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\big[G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)1_{\{N_{t}=1\}}\big]W(dx_{1})=e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)1_{\{N_{t}=1\}}W(dx_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[1_{\{N_{t}=1\}}\tau_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)}{\tau_{1}}W(dx_{1})\right]

If
n=2
, we use the change of variables
s_{1}=t-t_{2},s_{2}=t-t_{1}
,
x_{1}^{\prime}=x_{2}
,
x_{2}^{\prime}=x_{1}
, followed by Poisson representation (
19
) and relation (
52
) (with

):

\displaystyle H_{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{2}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}G(t-t_{2},x-x_{2})G(t_{2}-t_{1},x_{2}-x_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})d{\bf t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\int_{T_{2}(t)}G(s_{1},x_{1}^{\prime}-x)G(s_{2}-s_{1},x_{2}^{\prime}-x_{1}^{\prime})d{\bf s}\right)W^{\circ}(dx_{2}^{\prime})W^{\circ}(dx_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbb{E}\big[G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)G(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1},x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{N_{t}=2\}}\big]W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})

\displaystyle=e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)G(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1},x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{N_{t}=2\}}W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{t}\mathbb{E}^{N}\left[1_{\{N_{t}=2\}}\tau_{1}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{G(\tau_{1},x_{1}-x)}{\tau_{1}}\frac{G(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1},x_{2}-x_{1})}{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}W^{\circ}(dx_{1})W^{\circ}(dx_{2})\right].

∎

5
Smooth noise case

In this section, we consider the case of a smooth noise
W
with a finite spectral measure
\mu
. In this case,
\gamma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\mu(d\xi)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we do not need Assumption A.

Since
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})<\infty
, the sequence
\{p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is Cauchy in
\mathcal{H}
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\|p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)-p_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x-\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}/2}-e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}|\xi|^{2}/2}\right)^{2}\mu(d\xi)\to 0\quad\mbox{as $\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0$}.

We denote its limit by
A(x)
. Then
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)-A(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\|A(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)=\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The random variable

\dot{W}(x):=W(A(x))

is well-defined and
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)\to\dot{W}(x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, uniformly in
x
.
For any
p>0
,

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}=z_{p}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2})^{p/2}\leq z_{p}(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{p/2}

(54)

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}(x)|^{p}=z_{p}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}(x)|^{2})^{p/2}=z_{p}(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{p/2}

Remark 5.1
.

The Gaussian process
\{\dot{W}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
has stationary increments (in Yaglom sense) and covariance function:

\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}(x)-\dot{W}(y)|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)})^{2}\mu(d\xi).

This process is
not the same
as the mean-square derivative process
W^{\prime}
of the random field
W(x)=W(1_{[0,x]})
, which is defined as the limit
W^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{W(x+h)-W(x)}{h}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. (This limit exists if
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})<\infty
.)
To see this, it suffices to note that
\{W^{\prime}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a stationary Gaussian process with zero-mean and covariance

\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}(x)W^{\prime}(y)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot(x-y)}\mu(d\xi).

Let
\overline{v}_{n}(t,x)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\overline{H}_{k}(t,x)
, where

\overline{H}_{n}(t,x)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(x_{i})d{\bf x}d{\bf t}.

Since
\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}
is bounded by a constant that does not depend on
\varepsilon
, similarly to (
16
), we have:

\|\overline{H}_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\frac{2^{n/2}}{\pi^{1/(2p)}}C_{p}^{n/p}\big(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)^{n/2}c_{0}^{n}\frac{t^{2n}}{(n!)^{3/2}}.

and the convergence in Lemma
2.2
is uniform in
\varepsilon
.
Moreover, the proof of Lemma
2.2
can be repeated with
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}
replaced by
\dot{W}
, to infer that
\{\overline{v}_{n}(t,x)\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The next lemma deals with the issue of convergence when
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})<\infty
, then for any
p\geq 1
,
T>0
and
n\geq 1
,

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\overline{H}_{n}(t,x)|^{p}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$}.

(55)

Consequently, for any
t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
H_{n}(t,x)=\overline{H}_{n}(t,x)
a.s. and
v_{n}(t,x)=\overline{v}_{n}(t,x)
a.s.

Proof.

By Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|H_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\overline{H}_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\quad\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(s_{i}-s_{i-1},y_{i}-y_{i-1})\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(x+y_{i})\Big\|_{p}d{\bf y}d{\bf s},

where
s_{0}=0
and
y_{0}=0
.
Using the inequality

\Big|\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\Big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{1}\ldots a_{i-1}b_{i+1}\ldots b_{n}||a_{i}-b_{i}|.

for
a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, followed by the generalized Hölder inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}(x+y_{i})\Big|^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\quad n^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{1})|^{pn})^{1/n}\ldots(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i-1})|^{pn})^{1/n}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}(x+y_{i+1})|^{pn})^{1/n}\ldots

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}(x+y_{n})|^{pn})^{1/n}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i})-\dot{W}(x+y_{i})|^{pn})^{1/n}\leq

\displaystyle\quad n^{p-1}z_{pn}\Big(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big)^{\frac{p(n-1)}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}|\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x+y_{i})-\dot{W}(x+y_{i})|^{2})^{p/2}.

Since the right-hand-side above converges to
0
when
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the conclusion follows by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

6.2
Approximation for the Skorohod solution

In this section, we present an approximation procedure for
u(t,x)
, which turns out to be quite different than the approximation of
v(t,x)
. This section is included only for the sake of comparison, and it is not needed for the results in this paper.

We consider the equation with mollified noise
\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}
and Wick product
\diamond
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)+u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\diamond\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(x),\quad\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad(d\leq 2)\\
u^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=1,\quad\dfrac{\partial u^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}(0,x)=0\end{cases}

(56)

Recall that
F\diamond W(h)=\delta(Fh)
where
\delta
is the divergence operator and
F
is a random variable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
which is Malliavin differentiable with respect to
W
(see
[
11
]
).

Definition 6.1
.

A process
u^{\varepsilon}=\{u_{\varepsilon}(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is a
solution
to (
56
) if it satisfies

u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)u^{\varepsilon}(s,y)\diamond\dot{W}^{\varepsilon}(y)dyds.

(57)

By the definition of the Wick product and stochastic Fubini theorem, (
57
) is equivalent to

u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)u^{\varepsilon}(s,y)dydz\right)W(\delta z).

We define the kernel

f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x;t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{i}-y_{i})d{\bf x}d{\bf t}.

It is not difficult to prove that
f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
, using the fact that
f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
and

\mathcal{F}f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}).

Lemma 6.2
.

Under condition (D), for any
T>0
and
p\geq 2
, the series

u^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t))\quad\mbox{converges in $L^{p}(\Omega)$}

(58)

uniformly in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover,

\{u^{\varepsilon}(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is the unique solution of equation (
56
).

Proof.

Note that

\mathcal{F}\widetilde{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}).

Hence
\|\widetilde{f}_{n,\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
and the

L^{2}(\Omega)
-convergence of the series (
58
) (uniform in
(t,x)
) follows from the uniform convergence of
\sum_{n\geq 0}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
. The
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence follows by hypercontractivity.

Next, we prove that
u^{\varepsilon}
is a solution of (
56
). We have to show that

u^{\varepsilon,t,x}\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
and

\delta(u^{\varepsilon,t,x})=u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-1
, where

u^{\varepsilon,t,x}(z)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)u^{\varepsilon}(s,y)dyds.

First, we deduce the chaos expansion of
u^{\varepsilon,t,x}(z)
:

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon,t,x}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}(f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,y;s))\Big)dyds

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-z)f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,y;s)dyds\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}\big(f_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,z,x;t)\big).

Using an analogue of Proposition 1.3.7 of
[
11
]
,
we infer that

u^{\varepsilon,t,x}\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
and

\delta(u^{\varepsilon,t,x})=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n+1}\big(f_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\big)=u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-1.

Uniqueness follows by classical arguments.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Under condition (D), for any
T>0
and
p\geq 2
,

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{p}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

Proof.

By hypercontractivity, it suffices to consider the case
p=2
.
Note that

u(t,x)-u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(h_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t))
,
where
h_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)=f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)-f_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)
,
and

\mathcal{F}\widetilde{h}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{2}\right)\right]\mathcal{F}\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots\xi_{n}).

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\|\widetilde{h}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\widetilde{h}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover,

\|\widetilde{h}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)-u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{h}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}.

and the series converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, uniformly in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}<\infty
.
∎

Appendix A
Parseval-type identities

In this section, we give two Parseval-type identities which are used in this paper.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
be arbitrary and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
be non-negative-definite function satisfying Assumption A. Let
\mu
be the tempered measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
.
If
\varphi
is a non-negative integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\gamma(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\mu(d\xi).

Proof.

By relation (5.37) of
[
9
]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma(x-y)\nu_{1}(dx)\nu_{2}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\nu_{1}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\nu_{2}(\xi)}\mu(d\xi),

for any finite measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\nu_{i}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
for
i=1,2
. The conclusion follows taking
\nu_{1}(dx)=\varphi(x)dx
and
\nu_{2}=\delta_{0}
.
∎

Appendix B
Products of Wiener integrals

In this section, we develop a formula for the product of
n
Wiener integrals with respect to the noise
W
, which plays a crucial role in the present paper. This formula may be known but we could not find a reference for it.

We recall the
product formula
from Malliavin calculus: for any integer
p\geq 1
, for any symmetric function
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}
and for any
g\in\mathcal{H}
,

I_{p}(f)I_{1}(g)=I_{p+1}(f\otimes g)+pI_{p-1}(f\otimes_{1}g),

(60)

where

f\otimes_{1}g
is the first contraction of
f
and
g
, defined by:

(f\otimes_{1}g)(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p-1})=\langle f(\cdot,y_{1},\ldots,y_{p-1}),g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

(see e.g. Theorem 2.7.10 of
[
10
]
, or Proposition 1.1.3 of
[
11
]
).

We will use the fact that
I_{p}(f)=I_{p}(\widetilde{f})
for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}
, where
\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of
f
.
We let
f\otimes_{0}g=f\otimes g
and we denote by
f\widetilde{\otimes}_{r}g
the symmetrization of
f\otimes_{r}g
.

.

For any
n\geq 2
and for any functions

(61)

with the convention that

and

.

We use induction on
n
. The case
n=2
is clear.

Suppose that the result is true for
n

n
is even,

for

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[n]\\

I_{i}=\{\ell_{i},m_{i}\}\forall i=1,\ldots,K-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{j\in J}I_{1}\big(\bigotimes_{\ell\in J-\{j\}}f_{\ell}\big)\langle f_{j},f_{n+1}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}\langle f_{\ell_{i}},f_{m_{1}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=A_{2}^{\prime}+A_{2}^{\prime\prime}.

We notice that:

(a)\ A_{1}+A_{2}^{\prime}=B_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(b)\ A_{2}^{\prime\prime}+A_{3}=B_{2}.

(a) We use the decomposition
B_{1}=B_{1}^{\prime}+B_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, where
B_{1}^{\prime}
and
B_{1}^{\prime\prime}
correspond to the cases
n+1\in J^{\prime}
, respectively
n+1\not\in J^{\prime}
. We observe that
A_{1}=B_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
A_{2}^{\prime}=B_{1}^{\prime}
.

(b) We use the decomposition
B_{2}=B_{2}^{\prime}+B_{2}^{\prime\prime}
, where
B_{2}^{\prime}
and
B_{2}^{\prime\prime}
correspond to the cases
j=n+1
, respectively
j\leq n
. We observe that
A_{3}=B_{2}^{\prime}
and
A_{2}^{\prime\prime}=B_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.

∎

Appendix C
Semigroup-type property of
G

In this section, we prove a property of
G
which was used in Step 3.(a) of the proof of Theorem
3.2
. We denote
G_{t}(x)=G(t,x)
.

Lemma C.1

(Lemma 4.3 of
[
3
]
)
.

Suppose that
d=2
. Let
q\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
be arbitrary. For any
0<r<t
and
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\int_{r}^{t}\big(G_{t-s}^{2q}*G_{s-r}^{2q}\big)^{1/q}(x-z)ds\leq A_{q}(t-r)^{\frac{1}{q}-1}G_{t-r}^{2-\frac{1}{q}}(x-z),

where
A_{q}>0
is a constant depending on
q
.

As an immediate consequence of the previous lemma, we obtain the following semigroup-type property of
G
.

Lemma C.2
.

For any
0<r<t
and
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)G_{s-r}(y-z)dyds\leq C_{t}^{(1)}G_{t-r}(x-z),

where
C_{t}^{(1)}=Ct^{2}
and
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

If
d=1
, this follows by relation (2.6) of
[
12
]
.

Suppose that
d=2
. Let
q\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
be arbitrary. Note that
G_{t}(x)\leq C_{t,q}G_{t}^{2q}(x)
, where
C_{t,q}=(2\pi t)^{2q-1}
. By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
C.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)G_{s-r}(y-z)dydr\leq C_{t,q}^{2}\int_{r}^{t}\big(G_{t}^{2q}*G_{s}^{2q}\big)(x-z)dr\leq

\displaystyle\qquad C_{t,q}^{2}(t-r)^{1-q}\left[\int_{r}^{t}\big(G_{t}^{2q}*G_{s}^{2q}\big)^{1/q}(x-z)ds\right]^{q}\leq C_{t,q}^{2}(t-r)^{2(1-q)}A_{q}^{q}G_{t-r}^{2q-1}(x-z).

The conclusion follows using the fact that
G_{t-r}^{2q-1}(x-z)\leq(2\pi t)^{2-2q}G_{t-r}(x-z)
.

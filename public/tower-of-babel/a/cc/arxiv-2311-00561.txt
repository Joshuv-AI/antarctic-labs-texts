Two-colour laser cooling for K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} - Rb 87 {}^{87}{\text{Rb }} quantum gas mixtures Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yann Kiefer, Max Hachmann, Andreas Hemmerich

Abstract
We present an efficient cooling scheme for fermionic K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} atoms, using laser light red and blue detuned with respect to the 𝒟 ​ 2 \mathcal{D}2 and 𝒟 ​ 1 \mathcal{D}1 principle flourescence lines, respectively. The cooling scheme is found to significantly increase the saturation level for loading of a K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} magneto-optical trap (MOT), resulting in increased atom numbers or decreased cycle times. While the attainable K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} atom number is approximately doubled if exclusively K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} atoms are cooled, the scheme is particularly powerful for dual-species MOTs, for example, if K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} and Rb 87 {}^{87}{\text{Rb }} atoms are cooled simultaneously in the same MOT configuration. The typical atom losses due to light-assisted hetero-nuclear collisions between K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} and Rb 87 {}^{87}{\text{Rb }} seem to be reduced giving rise to a threefold improvement of the K 40 {}^{40}{\text{K }} atom number as compared to that in a conventional dual-species MOT, operating merely with 𝒟 ​ 2 \mathcal{D}2 light. Our scheme can be a useful extension to most dual-species experiments, aiming to reach simultaneous degeneracy of both species.



I
Introduction

The recent advances in quantum simulation platforms based on ultracold neutral atoms
Sherson et al. 2010
;
Mazurenko et al. 2017
and molecules
Marco et al. 2019
;
Schindewolf et al. 2022
;
Stevenson et al. 2023
;
Kiefer et al. 2023
have triggered renewed interest in refined laser cooling techniques enabling rapid cycle times and high fidelity sample preparation
Bernien et al. 2017
;
Brown et al. 2023
. To study fundamental low-temperature phases of matter
Schäfer et al. 2020
;
Chomaz et al. 2022
, degenerate quantum gases are typically employed, requiring temperatures on the 100 nK scale, well below the degeneracy temperature
T_{deg}
, where the behaviour of matter is governed by quantum mechanics. The usual initial step for preparation of a single-species quantum degenerate gas is laser-cooling to temperatures below the Doppler temperature
T_{D}
(on the order of
100\,\mu
K) in a three-dimensional (3D) magneto-optical trap (3D-MOT)
Raab et al. 1987
, which combines an optical molasses
Dalibard and Cohen-Tannoudji 1989
with a 3D magnetic quadrupole field. If more than a single atomic species is required, for example, for the production of ultracold hetero-nuclear molecules, the simultaneous, spatially overlapped operation of multiple 3D-MOTs is necessary referred to as a dual-species 3D-MOT. This typically comes with undesirable density and atom number limitations due to two-body loss processes such as molecule association via light assisted collisions in presence of photons of the cooling light
Anderson et al. 1994
. In this article we present a novel cooling scheme in a dual-species 3D-MOT, which mitigates such losses for the specific case of a
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
-
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
mixture
Goldwin et al. 2002
;
Ospelkaus-schwarzer 2006
.

A possible way to decrease such losses is the use of the
Spontaneous Force Optical Trap
(SPOT) technique discussed in Ref.
Ketterle et al. 1993
. This method requires to place a shadow into the repumping beam blocking the light from irradiating the atoms in the trap center. As a result since the cooling cycling transition for alkali atoms is typically not completely closed, the atoms in the trap center naturally accumulate in a dark state and exit the cooling cycle. Hence, the overall fluorescence and the rate of light-assisted collisions decrease. Unfortunately, the SPOT technique is only efficient for rather incompletely closed cycling transitions as in the case of sodium. Hence, it also does not apply for many dual-species mixtures of interest. For single-species scenarios, more recently, laser cooling using multiple atomic transitions was employed and found favourable for improved 3D-MOT performance in experiments of alkaline
Salomon et al. 2013a
and alkaline earth isotopes
Höschele et al. 2023
. In these works, the improved performance is linked to shielding effects due to short lived meta-stable intermediate states.

In the present work, we explore a novel two-colour scheme for cooling a dual-species
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
-
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
mixture, where in addition to the conventional laser radiation, detuned to the red side of the
\mathcal{D}2
-transition of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
at
767\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
, we include a second spectral component, detuned to the blue side of the
\mathcal{D}1
-transition of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
at
770\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
. The second species,
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
, is cooled by the conventional single-colour method using red-detuned light on the
\mathcal{D}2
-transition of
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
at
780\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
. After introducing the baseline scenario of a single-colour, single-species
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
3D-MOT, we benchmark the performance of the two-colour cooling scheme in a single-species
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
3D-MOT in the saturated regime. Here, we find an approximately twofold improvement of the number of cooled atoms, as compared to the conventional single-colour laser cooling scheme
DeMarco and Jin 1999
. Subsequently, by adding a conventional single-colour 3D-MOT for
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
, we investigate the dual-species (
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
-
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
) 3D-MOT performance when the novel two-colour cooling scheme is applied, finding an up to threefold improvement of the final potassium atom number, which we attribute to the suppression of light-assisted hetero-nuclear collisions.

II
Experimental setup

General setup:

The experimental setup consists of a dual-species 3D-MOT formed by the superposition of single-species MOTs for
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
and
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
inside an ultra-high vacuum glass cell. The dual-species 3D-MOT is loaded by cold atomic beams of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
and
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
atoms obtained from two separated source regions, which connect to the glass cell via differential pumping stages. The preparation of the cold beams is achieved in so-called
\textrm{2D}^{+}
- MOTs, loaded from dispensers. Essentially, this is a 2D-MOT using a magnetic field with linear quadrupole geometry and two additional beams along the zero magnetic field axis, which is referred to by the ”+” sign in the acronym. A detailed description of this technique is found in Ref.
Dieckmann et al. 1998
.

{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
3D-MOT:

The
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
3D-MOT uses the same single-colour cooling scheme as the
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
\textrm{2D}^{+}
-MOT. Similarly as the parameters of both
\textrm{2D}^{+}
- MOTs, the parameters of the
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
3D-MOT are not varied for all experiments reported here, in order to reduce complexity.

{}^{40}{\text{K }}
3D-MOT:

To realise the two-colour cooling scheme, the
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
3D-MOT beams are derived from three tapered amplifiers (TA), divided into six beams of equal intensity delivered by polarisation maintaining optical fibers. The cooling and repumping light on the
\mathcal{D}2
-transition is provided by two separate TAs. The cooling/repumping scheme on the
\mathcal{D}1
-transition is realised by a single TA, where the phase-coherent repumping light is imprinted by an electro-acoustic modulator operating at
f_{HFS,^{40}K}=$1.285\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}$

Tiecke 2019
. The magnetic quadrupole field centered around the position of the atoms is realised with a pair of water-cooled copper coils placed outside the vacuum system.

in the single-colour MOT is saturated after
\sim$15\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
, while the reach of saturation of the potassium number in the two-colour cooling scheme is significantly shifted towards later times (cf. Appendix B).

VI
Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate a novel, powerful cooling scheme for preparing mixtures of cold
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
and
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
atoms in a dual-species MOT. In addition to the conventional cooling of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
atoms using light tuned to the red side of the
\mathcal{D}2
-transition, a second light component, tuned to the blue side of the
\mathcal{D}1
-transition, is used. We have started with benchmarking the performance of a conventional single-species MOT for
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
atoms using single frequency cooling light with a negative detuning
\delta_{D2}<0
with respect to the
\mathcal{D}2
-line and an intensity
I_{D2}
. Two domains in the parameter space
\{\delta_{D2},I_{D2}\}
are identified, dubbed
conventional branch
and
side branch
, where the cooling efficiency attains a relative maximum, while the global maximum is reached in the
conventional branch
. In a second step, discussed in
section III
, we demonstrate that the inclusion of additional light beams, blue-detuned with respect to the
\mathcal{D}1
-transition, can increase the potassium atom number in a single-species MOT for long loading times. In a third step, we investigate MOT loading in a dual-species scenario of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
and
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
. In
section IV
, we benchmark the dual-species MOT using conventional cooling on the
\mathcal{D}2
-transition. Here, we find significant atomic losses due to LAHNCs and the disappearance of the
side branch
. Finally, in
section V
, we investigate the influence of the two-colour cooling scheme in a dual-species MOT scenario. We observe a significant improvement of the
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
atom number
N_{K}
, with the
side branch
forming the optimal parameter region. For long MOT loading times we demonstrate a threefold improvement of
N_{K}
. We attribute the significant reduction of saturation of
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
loading, observed in the dual-colour MOT, to a suppression of light-assisted hetero-nuclear collisions between
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
and
{}^{87}{\text{Rb }}
atoms. As a consequence, longer loading times or an increase of the flux of the cold
{}^{40}{\text{K }}
beam source allow for larger
N_{K}
. Due to the similar level structure of alkali atomic species, we suspect that the discussed cooling scheme can be a useful extension for most modern dual-species alkali quantum gas experiments, which require high fidelity preparation of large atomic samples with high repetition rates.

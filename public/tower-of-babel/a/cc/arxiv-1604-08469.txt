Bounds of Trilinear and Quadrilinear Exponential Sums
Giorgis Petridis, Igor E. Shparlinski

Abstract
. We use an estimate of Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and Shkredov (2016) on the number of solutions of certain equations involving products and differences of sets in prime finite fields to give an explicit upper bound on trilinear exponential sums which improves the previous bound of Bourgain and Garaev (2009). We also obtain explicit bounds for quadrilinear exponential sums.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B30, 11L07, 11T23

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
p
be a prime and let
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
be the finite field of
p
elements.
Now given three sets
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and three sequences of weights

\alpha=(\alpha_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}
,
\beta=\left(\beta_{y}\right)_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}
and
\gamma=\left(\gamma_{z}\right)_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}

supported on

{\mathcal{X}}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
and

{\mathcal{Z}}
, respectively, we consider exponential sums

(1.1)

S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\alpha_{x}\beta_{y}\gamma_{z}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(xyz),

where
{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(z)=\exp(2\pi iz/p)
.
We recall that the bilinear analogues of these sums are classical and have been studied in several papers, in which case
for any sets
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and any
\alpha=(\alpha_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}
,
\beta=\left(\beta_{y}\right)_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}
,
with

\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}|\alpha_{x}|^{2}=A\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}|\beta_{y}|^{2}=B,

we have

(1.2)

\left|\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\alpha_{x}\beta_{y}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(xy)\right|\leq\sqrt{pAB},

see, for example,
[
4
, Equation 1.4]
or
[
15
, Lemma 4.1]
.

The trilinear sums (
1.1
) have been introduced and estimated by Bourgain and Garaev
[
4
]
.
In particular, for and sets
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of cardinalities

\#{\mathcal{X}}=X,\qquad\#{\mathcal{Y}}=Y,\qquad\#{\mathcal{Z}}=Z,

and weights with

\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}|\alpha_{x}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}|\beta_{y}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}|\gamma_{z}|\leq 1,

by
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
we have

(1.3)

\left|S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\right|\leq(XYZ)^{13/16}p^{5/18+o(1)},

as
p\to\infty
.
The bound (
1.3
) has been generalised and extended in various directions,
see
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
15
,
32
]
. In particular, Bourgain
[
2
]
has obtained
a bound on multilinear sums

(1.4)

\left|\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})\right|\leq X_{1}\ldots X_{n}p^{-\eta}

under an optimal condition

\min_{i=1,\ldots,n}X_{i}\geq p^{\delta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\geq p^{1+\delta},

of the sizes
X_{i}={\mathcal{X}}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, of the sets involved, where
\eta>0

depends only on the arbitrary parameter
\delta>0
.
The interest to multilinear exponential sums partially comes from applications to
exponential sums over subgroups of small order over finite fields, which has been used in the celebrated work of
Bourgain, Glibichuk and Konyagin
[
7
]
.
We also note that several consecutive applications of the Cauchy inequality allow us to
reduce general multilinear sums to sums without weights, see, for example, Lemma
2.10

below.
Furthermore, the bound (
1.4
) has been extended to arbitrary finite fields
by Bourgain and Glibichuk
[
6
]
, see also
[
3
,
32
]
.
However, prior to the present work, the bound (
1.3
) has been the best
known explicit bound on the sums (
1.1
).

We also note that Hegyvári
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
has given estimates of
some multilinear sums, without weights, over sets
of special additive structure (for sets with small difference sets).

Here we use a different approach to estimating the sums (
1.1
)
and a recent result of Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
]
to obtain a different bound, which, in particular, improves (
1.3
) when
p\to\infty
.

Furthermore, using a slightly different approach, based on another result of Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
]
(see also
[
33
]
) we consider sums
with more general weights to which the above approach does not apply. We also consider their multivariate versions.
In particular, given
n
sets
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,
and also
n
sequences of weights

\omega_{i}=(\omega_{i}(\mathbf{x}))_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}}
such that
\omega_{i}(\mathbf{x})
does not
depend on the
i
th coordinate of the vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
we consider multilinear exponential sums

(1.5)

\begin{split}T({\mathcal{X}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1}&,\ldots,\omega_{n})\\
&=\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\ldots\omega_{n}(\mathbf{x}){\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(x_{1}\ldots x_{n}).\end{split}

As we have mentioned, if the strength of the bound is not of concern but only the range
of non-triviality is important, then Bourgain
[
2
]
provides an optimal
result (
1.4
) for sums with constant weights, which can be extended to
sums (
1.5
) with several consecutive applications of the Cauchy inequality
as in Lemma
2.10
below. However the saving in such a bound is rather
small, while here we are interested in stronger and more explicit bounds.

Although we estimate the sums (
1.5
) only for
n=3
and
n=4
we develop some
tools in Section
2.2
in full generality, which may be of use if the results
of Section
2.1
get eventually extended to equations with more sets and variables.

Our method is based on a upper bound of Rudnev
[
38
]
on the number of incidences between a set of points and a set of planes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{3}
. Rudnev’s paper, which undoubtedly will find many more applications, stems from the Guth and Katz
[
18
]
solution to the Erdős distinct distance problem for planar sets and the Klein–Plücker line geometry formalism
[
34
, Chapter 2]
. So Rudnev’s work
[
38
]
indirectly depends on classical techniques such as the polynomial method (see
[
16
]
and
[
46
, Chapter 9]
) and properties of ruled surfaces (see
[
26
]
) and the Klein quadric (see
[
39
]
). A more detailed discussion can be found in the beginning of
Section
2.1
.

We also illustrate potential applications of our results on a example of a certain question from additive
combinatorics complementing those of
Sárközy
[
41
]
on nonlinear equations with variables from
arbitrary sets and of Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
]
on sizes of polynomial images.
In fact both are closely related and also both can be approached via the idea
of Garaev
[
14
]
which links multilinear exponential sums, equation
with variables from arbitrary sets and sums-product type results (see
also
[
20
]
for some other applications of this idea). So it is not surprising
that our results fit well into this approach, see Section
1.4
. More precisely,
our bounds of multilinear exponential sums, allow to derive results for multifold sums
and products, which have recently become a subject of quite active investigation.
For example, to put our results of Section
1.4
in a proper context, we present
one of the bounds of Roche-Newton, Rudnev and Shkredov
[
36
]
.
Namely, by
[
36
, Corollary 12]
we have

\left(\#({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{A}})\right)^{4}\left(\#({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}})\right)^{9}\geq\left(\#{\mathcal{A}}\right)^{16}

for any set
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
\#{\mathcal{A}}=O\left(p^{18/35}\right)

(we refer to (
1.8
) for the definition sum and
product sets); see also (
1.9
), (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) below.

Finally, we recall that the bound (
1.2
) has a full analogue for sums
with a nontrivial multiplicative characters
\chi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
with two sets

{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Chang
[
9
]
has given a better bound if one of these sets has a small sum set.
More recently,
Hanson
[
19
]
, also using methods of additive
combinatorics,
has obtained a series of results which apply to trilinear character sums.
Shkredov and Volostnov
[
43
]
have given further
improvements of the results of
[
9
,
19
]
. Unfortunately our approach does
not seem to apply to multiplicative character sums.

1.2.
Notation

We always use the letter
p
to denote a prime number and use the letter
q
to denote a prime power.

Before we formulate our results, we recall that the notations
U=O(V)
,
U\ll V
and
V\gg U
are all
equivalent to the assertion that the inequality
|U|\leq c|V|
holds for some
constant
c>0
.
We also write
U\asymp V
if
U\ll V\ll U
.
Throughout the paper, the implied constants in the symbols ‘
O
’, ‘
\ll
’
and ‘
\gg
’ are absolute.

1.3.
New bounds of exponential sums

We note that to simplify the results and exposition we assume that
0
is
excluded from the sets under consideration.
This changes the absolute value of, say, the sum

S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
by at most
O(XY+XZ+YZ)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
X,Y,Z
, respectively, with

X\geq Y\geq Z

and any weights

\alpha=(\alpha_{x})
,
\beta=(\beta_{y})
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{z})

with

\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}|\alpha_{x}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}|\beta_{y}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}|\gamma_{z}|\leq 1,

we have

S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ll p^{1/4}X^{3/4}Y^{3/4}Z^{7/8}.

Theorem
1.1
in nontrivial and improves the bound

(1.6)

|S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)|\leq p^{1/2}X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}Z,

which is instant from (
1.2
), if

X^{1/4}Y^{1/4}Z^{1/8}\gg p^{1/4}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad X^{1/4}Y^{1/4}Z^{-1/8}\ll p^{1/4}.

We rewrite these inequalities as

pZ^{1/2}\gg XY\gg pZ^{-1/2}.

In particular if
X=Y=Z
, then

S({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ll p^{1/4}X^{19/8},

which is nontrivial for
X\geq p^{2/5}
. This range of non-triviality is inferior to that obtained by Bourgain
[
2
]
.

It is easy to see that Theorem
1.1
improves (
1.3
) for all
cardinalities
X,Y,Z
when
p\to\infty
, since without loss of generality we can assume that

X\geq Y\geq Z
and then we have

\displaystyle p^{1/4}X^{3/4}Y^{3/4}Z^{7/8}

\displaystyle\leq p^{5/18}X^{3/4}Y^{3/4}Z^{7/8}

\displaystyle=p^{5/18}(XYZ)^{13/16}(Z/(XY))^{1/16}

\displaystyle\leq p^{5/18}(XYZ)^{13/16}.

Also, to indicate the strength of Theorem
1.1
we compare it with the bound of Hegyvári
[
20
, Corollary 2]
.
First we note that the bound of multilinear sums from
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
makes use of only three
sets and does not improve with the number of sets. Now for
X\asymp Y\asymp p^{1/2}
and
Z\geq p^{3/8}
,
ignoring very restrictive additive conditions on the sets
{\mathcal{X}},
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
, the bound of
[
20
, Corollary 2]

takes form
O\left(p^{3/16}XYZ^{1/2}\right)=O\left(p^{19/16}Z^{1/2}\right)
. Unlike Theorem
1.1
, the set
{\mathcal{Z}}
need not be the smallest set. Once
{\mathcal{Z}}
is assumed to be the smallest set, then Theorem
1.1
gives the bound
O\left(pZ^{7/8}\right)
, which is stronger in the range
Z<p^{1/2}
(in which case
{\mathcal{Z}}
is the smallest set anyway).

We also compare Theorem
1.1
with the bound of
Garaev
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
,
which work trilinear sums over arbitrary sets (but without weights). More precisely,
by
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
,
if
XY\gg p
then

\left|\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(xyz)\right|\leq XYZ^{539/540+o(1)},

while Theorem
1.1
applies to weighted sums and yields (under the same condition
XY\gg p
) the bound of the form

O\left(p^{1/4}X^{3/4}Y^{3/4}Z^{7/8}\right)=O\left(XYZ^{7/8}\right)
.

As yet another evidence of the efficiency of our approach and the strength of Theorem
1.1

we note that one can easily recover the second bound of
[
36
, Corollary 19]
.

We now obtain a bound of quadrilinear analogues of sums (
1.1
)

S({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\alpha_{w}\beta_{x}\gamma_{y}\delta_{z}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(wxyz)

with four sets
{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
, and weights

\alpha=(\alpha_{w})_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}
,
\beta=\left(\beta_{x}\right)_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}
,
\gamma=\left(\gamma_{y}\right)_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}

and
\delta=\left(\delta_{z}\right)_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}

supported on
{\mathcal{W}}
,

{\mathcal{X}}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
and

{\mathcal{Z}}
, respectively.

To simplify the exposition we now assume that all cardinalities are less than
p^{2/3}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
W,X,Y,Z
, respectively, with

p^{2/3}\geq W\geq X\geq Y\geq Z,

and any weights

\alpha=(\alpha_{x})
,
\beta=(\beta_{y})
,
\gamma=(\gamma_{z})
and
\delta=\left(\delta_{z}\right)

with

\max_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}|\alpha_{w}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}|\beta_{x}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}|\gamma_{y}|\leq 1,\qquad\max_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}|\delta_{z}|\leq 1,

we have

S({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)\ll p^{1/8}W^{7/8}X^{7/8}Y^{15/16}Z^{15/16}.

If
W=X=Y=Z
, the bound in Theorem
1.2
becomes

S({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)\ll p^{1/8}W^{29/8},

which is nontrivial for
W\geq p^{1/3}
. Once again, the range of non-triviality is
inferior to that obtained by Bourgain
[
2
]
.

Next we move to the case of sums (
1.5
) with more complicated weights.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
X,Y,Z
, respectively, with

X\geq Y\geq Z

and weights

\rho=(\rho_{x,y})
,
\sigma=(\sigma_{x,z})
and
\tau=(\tau_{y,z})

with

\max_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}}|\rho_{x,y}|\leq 1,\quad\max_{(x,z)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}}|\sigma_{x,z}|\leq 1,\quad\max_{(y,z)\in{\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}}|\tau_{y,z}|\leq 1,

we have

T({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\rho,\sigma,\tau)\ll p^{1/8}X^{7/8}Y^{29/32}Z^{29/32}.

For
X=Y=Z
, Theorem
1.3
is nontrivial in the same range

X\geq p^{2/5}
like Theorem
1.1
.

We also present an explicit bound for multilinear sums with four sets.
Again, we make a simplifying assumption that all cardinalities are less than
p^{2/3}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
W,X,Y,Z
, respectively, with

p^{2/3}\geq W\geq X\geq Y\geq Z,

and weights
\vartheta=(\vartheta_{w,x,y})
,

\rho=(\rho_{w,x,z})
,
\sigma=(\sigma_{w,y,z})
and
\tau=(\tau_{x,y,z})

with

\displaystyle\max_{(w,x,y)\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}}|\vartheta_{w,x,y}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\quad\max_{(w,x,z)\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}}|\rho_{w,x,z}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\max_{(w,y,z)\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}}|\sigma_{w,y,z}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\quad\max_{(x,y,z)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}}|\tau_{x,y,z}|\leq 1,

we have

T({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\vartheta,\rho,\sigma,\tau)\ll p^{1/16}W^{15/16}(XY)^{61/64}Z^{31/32}.

For
W=X=Y=Z
, the bound of Theorem
1.4
becomes

T({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}},{\mathcal{Z}};\vartheta,\rho,\sigma,\tau)\ll p^{1/16}W^{61/16},

which is nontrivial for
W\geq p^{1/3}
which is the same range as for
Theorem
1.4
in the case of sets of equal cardinalities.

Note that although the bound of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
are
weaker than that of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, they however
apply to more general sums, including, for example, to sums of the form

(1.7)

\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\ {\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(F(x,y,z)),\quad\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\ {\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(G(w,x,y,z)),

for any cubic polynomial
F(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x,y,z]
and
quartic polynomial

G(w,x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w,x,y,z]

that contain a term of the form
axyz
and
awxyz
,
respectively, with
a\neq 0
.

1.4.
Applications

Given two set
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
we define the sum, difference and product sets

(1.8)

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}=\{a+b~:~a\in{\mathcal{A}},\ b\in{\mathcal{B}}\},\\
&{\mathcal{A}}-{\mathcal{B}}=\{a-b~:~a\in{\mathcal{A}},\ b\in{\mathcal{B}}\},\\
&{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}=\{ab~:~a\in{\mathcal{A}},\ b\in{\mathcal{B}}\}.\end{split}

These notations naturally extend to operations with any number of sets.

First we recall that by a result Sárközy
[
41
]
, for any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

of cardinalities
A,B,C
, we have

(1.9)

\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}}\right)=q+O\left(\frac{q^{3}}{ABC}\right),

see also
[
44
, Equation (9)]
. This immediately implies that there is an absolute constant
c_{0}

such that for any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

of cardinalities
A,B,C,D
with

ABCD\geq c_{0}q^{3}
,
we have

(1.10)

{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

Indeed, if there is
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\setminus({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}})
then

({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})\cap(\lambda-{\mathcal{D}})=\emptyset
and thus

D=\#(\lambda-{\mathcal{D}})<q-\#({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})=O\left(\frac{q^{3}}{ABC}\right)

by (
1.9
).
Furthermore, over a prime field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, Roche-Newton, Rudnev and Shkredov
[
36
, Theorem 1]
give a
lower bound

(1.11)

\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}}\right)\gg\min\{p,(ABC)^{1/2},ABC/M\}

where
M=\max\{A,B,C\}
.

We now consider the related question, involving triple products and four sets.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
A,B,C,D
, respectively, with

A\geq B\geq C,

we have

\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)=p+O\left(p^{5/2}A^{-1/2}B^{-1/2}C^{-1/4}D^{-1}\right)

and

\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)\gg\min\{p,p^{-1/2}A^{1/2}B^{1/2}C^{1/4}D\}.

In particular, we immediately derive a version of the property (
1.10
) for five sets
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Corollary 1.6
.

There is an absolute constant
c_{0}

such that for any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}

of cardinalities
A,B,C,D,E
with

ABC^{1/2}D^{2}E^{2}\geq c_{0}p^{5},

we have

{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}+{\mathcal{E}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

Furthermore, we also define

{\mathcal{A}}^{k}=\{a^{k}~:~a\in{\mathcal{A}}\}

(note that
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}
is not the
k
-fold product set of
{\mathcal{A}}
which sometimes is
also denoted by
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}
).

Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
, Corollary 2.13(1)]

have shown that
for any set
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of cardinality
A<p^{7/12}
we have

(1.12)

\#\left(({\mathcal{A}}-{\mathcal{A}})^{3}+({\mathcal{A}}-{\mathcal{A}})^{3}\right)\gg A^{36/35}.

Here we obtain a related result involving four sets from
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of cardinalities
A,B,C,D
, respectively, with

p^{2/3}\geq A\geq B\geq C,

we have

\#\left(({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})^{3}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)=p+O\left(p^{9/4}A^{-1/4}B^{-3/16}C^{-3/16}D^{-1}\right)

and

\#\left(({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})^{3}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)\gg\min\{p,p^{-1/4}A^{1/4}B^{3/16}C^{3/16}D\}.

In particular, if
A=B=C=D
the lower bounds of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
are
nontrivial for
A\geq p^{2/5}
. We also obtain yet another analogue of (
1.10
):

Corollary 1.8
.

There is an absolute constant
c_{0}

such that for any sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}

of cardinalities
A,B,C,D,E
with

AB^{3/4}C^{3/4}D^{4}E^{4}\geq c_{0}p^{9},

we have

({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})^{3}+{\mathcal{D}}+{\mathcal{E}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
are based on bounds of exponential sums
of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, respectively. Using Theorems
1.2
and
1.4

one can obtain versions of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7

for more complicated sets such as
{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}+{\mathcal{E}}
and

({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}})^{4}+{\mathcal{E}}
.

Our final application is an extension of the following inequality of Garaev
[
14
, Theorem 1]
to triple products

\#({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}})\#({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{A}})\gg\min\left\{pA,\frac{A^{4}}{p}\right\}.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
be sets of cardinalities
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
, respectively.
Consider the sets

{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{D}}

of cardinalities
U
and
V
, respectively.
Then we have

UV\gg pA\qquad\text{or}\qquad U^{3}V^{2}\gg A^{4}BC^{1/2}D^{2}p^{-1}.

In particular Theorem
1.9
implies that

\max\{U,V\}\gg\min\{p^{1/2}A^{1/2},A^{4/5}B^{1/5}C^{1/10}D^{2/5}p^{-1/5}\}.

Furthermore, if
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
is a multiplicative subgroup
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of order
T
then for any set
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
with
\#{\mathcal{S}}=S
, by
Theorem
1.9
we have

(1.13)

\#\left({\mathcal{G}}+{\mathcal{S}}\right)\gg\min\{p,ST^{5/4}p^{-1/2}\},

which improves the trivial universal lower bound
S
for
T\geq Cp^{2/5}
with any a sufficiently large constant
C
.

As before, we note that using Theorem
1.2

one can obtain analogues of
Theorem
1.9

for more complicated sets. For example, Theorem
1.2
allows to
deal with the sets

{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{E}}

with
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
. In turn one can obtain the following
version of (
1.13
)

\#\left({\mathcal{G}}+{\mathcal{S}}\right)\gg\min\{p,ST^{3/4}p^{-1/4}\},

which is now nontrivial for
T\geq Cp^{1/3}
with any a sufficiently large constant
C
.

1.5.
Recent developement

Our results depend on the forthcoming bounds on the quantity
T({\mathcal{U}})
, c.f. Lemma
2.8
. The recent work
[
30
]
contains an improvement when

, which leads to improved bounds for the exponential sum we consider in certain ranges of the cardinalities

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Background from arithmetic combinatorics

Some of the results of this section apply to arbitrary fields
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
q
elements
so we formulate them in this form.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
come down to non-trivial upper bounds on the number of solutions to equations with variables in prescribed sets in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
. Of particular importance in our considerations is the following such quantity.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Then
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
denotes the number of solutions to

u_{1}(v_{1}-w_{1})=u_{2}(v_{2}-w_{2})

with
u_{1},u_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}
,
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{W}}
.

A trivial upper bound for
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
in terms terms of the cardinalities
U=\#{\mathcal{U}},V=\#{\mathcal{V}},W=\#{\mathcal{W}}
is

N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})\leq UV^{2}W^{2}+U^{2}VW.

This is because for each 5-tuple
(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2})
where
v_{1}\neq w_{1}
and
v_{2}\neq w_{2}
there is at most one
u_{2}
satisfying
u_{1}(v_{1}-w_{1})=u_{2}(v_{2}-w_{2})
; while if
v_{1}=w_{1}
there is no solution unless
v_{2}=w_{2}
and in this case all
u_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}
work. Our method for obtaining non-trivial upper bounds for the modulus of exponential sums relies on non-trivial upper bounds for
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
. The first non-trivial upper bound (for the special case
U=V=W\leq p^{2/3}
) can be traced back to the breakthrough paper of Bourgain, Katz and
Tao
[
8
]
on sum-product questions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Bourgain, Katz and Tao have shown
[
8
, Theorem 6.2]
that, under mild conditions, a set of
n
points in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
and a set of
n
lines in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
determine
O(n^{3/2-c})
points-lines incidences for an absolute albeit small
c>0
. From this a non-trivial upper bound for
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
follows easily, for example, by modifying
[
10
, Lemma 3.5.1]
in Dvir’s survey
[
10
]
. Progress over the years to the Bourgain-Katz-Tao result, see
[
4
,
13
,
21
,
23
,
27
,
29
,
31
,
37
]
and references therein, leads implicitly to improved upper bounds for
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
.

The most recent and significant progress in estimating
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
has its
roots in a bound of Rudnev
[
38
]
on the number of incidences between a set of points in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{3}
and a set of planes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{3}
.
Rudnev’s work
[
38
]
is based on a theorem of Guth and Katz
[
18
, Theorem 2.10]
from their solution to the Erdős distinct distance conjecture for planar sets and on the 19th century Plücker-Klein formalism for projective line geometry
[
34
]
. Applying the incidence theorem of Rudnev
[
38
]
to
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
requires an elegant trick and has been done by Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and Shkredov
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.
Given the importance of Rudnev’s points-planes incidence theorem to our results, it should be noted that the theorem is essentially sharp. Existing points-lines incidence results in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{3}
(see
[
12
,
28
]
and also
[
17
]
) do not seem to work as well for bounding quantities as
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
.

We begin our thorough examination of
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
with an easy upper bound based on multiplicative characters in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
with cardinalities
U
,
V
,
W
, respectively. The following inequality holds:

\left|N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})-\frac{U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}}{q-1}\right|\leq qUVW.

Proof.

Clearly, the number of
zero-solutions
, that is, solutions with
u_{1}(v_{1}-w_{1})=u_{2}(v_{2}-w_{2})=0
is at most
U^{2}VW
, because we must have
v_{1}=w_{1}
and
v_{2}=w_{2}
. We use standard properties of multiplicative characters to bound the number
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})^{*}
of non-zero solutions.
Let
\Omega
denote the set of all
q-1
multiplicative characters of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

and let
\Omega^{*}
be the set of nonprincipal characters;
we refer to
[
22
, Chapter 3]
for a background on characters. In particular,
using the orthogonality of characters, we write

\displaystyle N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Omega}\sum_{u_{1},u_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{W}}}\chi\left(\frac{u_{1}(v_{1}-w_{1})}{u_{2}(v_{2}-w_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Omega}\left|\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\chi(u(v-w))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}}{q-1}+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Omega^{*}}\left|\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\chi(u)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathcal{V}}\\
w\in{\mathcal{W}}\end{subarray}}\chi(v-w)\right|^{2}.

Recalling the well-known analogue of (
1.2
):

(2.1)

\max_{\chi\in\Omega^{*}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathcal{V}}\\
w\in{\mathcal{W}}\end{subarray}}\chi(v-w)\right|\leq\sqrt{qVW},

we obtain

\left|N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})^{*}-\frac{U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}}{q-1}\right|\leq\frac{qVW}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Omega^{*}}\left|\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\chi(u)\right|^{2}.

Using the orthogonality of characters again, we derive

\displaystyle\left|N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})^{*}-\frac{U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}}{q-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{qVW}{q-1}\left(\sum_{\chi\in\Omega}\left|\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\chi(u)\right|^{2}-U^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq qUVW-U^{2}VW,

which concludes the proof.

\sqcap
\sqcup

The next step is to obtain a complementary bound for small sets
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
in a prime field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
It is based on the points-planes incidence bound of Rudnev
[
38
]

and in particular on its application described by Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
with cardinalities
U
,
V
,
W
, respectively,
and set
M=\max\{U,V,W\}
. Suppose that

UVW\ll p^{2}
. The following inequality holds:

N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})\ll U^{3/2}V^{3/2}W^{3/2}+MUVW.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
1
, Theorem 11]
by setting
A={\mathcal{U}}
and

L\simeq{\mathcal{V}}\times{\mathcal{W}}

to be the set of lines
\{y=vx+vw~:~v\in{\mathcal{V}},\ w\in{\mathcal{W}}\}
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Combining the two results gives an upper bound on
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
with cardinalities
U
,
V
,
W
, respectively, and set
M=\max\{U,V,W\}
. The following inequality holds.

N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})\ll\frac{U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}}{p}+U^{3/2}V^{3/2}W^{3/2}+MUVW.

Proof.

When
UVW\geq p^{2}
we apply Lemma
2.2
. It is easy to see that
in this range the
U^{2}V^{2}W^{2}/p
term dominates the other two.
When
UVW<p^{2}
we apply Lemma
2.3
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

In particular, we see from Corollary
2.4
that when
U=V=W
we have

N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})\ll\frac{U^{6}}{p}+U^{9/2}.

We also need two other quantities similar to
N({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}})
. We recall that the
multiplicative energy

E_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})
of a set
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, is defined
as the number of solutions to the equation

u_{1}u_{2}=u_{3}u_{4},\qquad u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

It is known to play a crucial role in bounds of various exponential sums,
see, for example,
[
15
]
.
However, here our argument relies on bounds of the multiplicative energy
of the difference set with multiplicities counted.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Then
D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})
denotes the number of solutions to the equation

(u_{1}-v_{1})(u_{2}-v_{2})=(u_{3}-v_{3})(u_{4}-v_{4}),\quad u_{i},v_{i},\in{\mathcal{U}},\ i=1,2,3,4.

An essentially optimal upper bound for
D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})
over the real numbers has been obtained by Roche-Newton and Rudnev
[
35
]
by an application of the Guth–Katz theorem
[
18
]
. The quantity
D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})
does not seem to have been studied in the finite field context until recently (see
[
33
]
). We will present a different bound, which depends on a third quantity that, moreover, features an autonomous role in the proofs of the exponential sum estimates is the following.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Then
T({\mathcal{U}})
denotes the number of solutions to the equation

\frac{u_{1}-v}{u_{2}-v}=\frac{u_{3}-w}{u_{4}-w},\quad u_{i},v,w\in{\mathcal{U}},\ i=1,2,3,4.

The notation reflects the fact that the function
T({\mathcal{U}})
counts the number of collinear triplets of points in
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. A more or less optimal upper bound for
T({\mathcal{U}})
over the real numbers has been obtained by Elekes and Ruzsa in
[
11
]
by an application of the Szemerédi–Trotter theorem
[
45
]
. In the finite field context,
T({\mathcal{U}})
is studied by Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and Shkredov in
[
1
]
.

We begin our examination of
D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})
and
T({\mathcal{U}})
with the following elementary estimate that relates the two quantities.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any set
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of cardinality
\#{\mathcal{U}}=U
, we have

D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})\ll U^{2}T({\mathcal{U}})+U^{6}.

Proof.

Clearly
D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})=D_{\times}^{*}({\mathcal{U}})+O(U^{6})

where
D_{\times}^{*}({\mathcal{U}})
is the number of solutions to the equation

\frac{u_{1}-v_{1}}{u_{2}-v_{2}}=\frac{u_{3}-v_{3}}{u_{4}-v_{4}}\neq 0,\quad u_{i},v_{i},\in{\mathcal{U}},\ i=1,2,3,4.

Let
J(\lambda)
be the number of quadruples
(u_{1},u_{2},v,w)\in{\mathcal{U}}^{4}
with

(2.2)

\frac{u_{1}-v}{u_{2}-w}=\lambda

and let
J_{v,w}(\lambda)
be the number of pairs
(u_{1},u_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}^{4}

for which (
2.2
) holds.

Then, by the Cauchy inequality, we have

\displaystyle D_{\times}^{*}({\mathcal{U}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}J(\lambda)^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\left(\sum_{v,w\in{\mathcal{U}}}J_{v,w}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq U^{2}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\sum_{v,w\in{\mathcal{U}}}J_{v,w}(\lambda)^{2}=U^{2}\sum_{v,w\in{\mathcal{U}}}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}J_{v,w}(\lambda)^{2}.

Using that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}J_{v,w}(\lambda)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\#\left\{(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\in{\mathcal{U}}^{4}~:~\frac{u_{1}-v}{u_{2}-w}=\frac{u_{3}-v}{u_{4}-w}=\lambda\right\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\in{\mathcal{U}}^{4}~:~\frac{u_{1}-v}{u_{3}-v}=\frac{u_{2}-w}{u_{4}-w}\neq 0\right\},

and renaming the variables
(v,w)\to(u_{3},u_{2})
, we immediately obtain the desired result.

\sqcap
\sqcup

We now need the bound on
T({\mathcal{U}})
given by Petridis
[
33
]
, which (for
U\leq p^{2/3}
) is based on a result of Aksoy Yazici, Murphy, Rudnev and
Shkredov
[
1
, Proposition 2.5]

(see also
[
42
, Theorem 10]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

For any set
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of cardinality
U
, we have

T({\mathcal{U}})\ll\frac{U^{6}}{p}+U^{9/2}.

Thus combining Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
we obtain

Corollary 2.9
.

For any set
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of cardinality
U
, we have

D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})\ll\frac{U^{8}}{p}+U^{13/2}.

In particular, we see from Corollary
2.9
that when
U\leq p^{2/3}
then
the second term dominates and we derive

D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})\ll U^{13/2}
. We also remark that
Rudnev, Shkredov and Stevens
[
40
, Theorem 6]
have given a related result.

2.2.
Exponential sums and differences

We now link multilinear exponential sums (
1.5
) to exponential sums with differences. For a technical reason
it is also convenient for us to prove this in the setting of arbitrary finite fields and additive characters. This may also be
useful in further applications.

Namely,
for sets
{\mathcal{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, weights
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}
and an additive character
\psi

of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
we define

T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})=\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\ldots\omega_{n}(\mathbf{x})\psi(x_{1}\ldots x_{n}).

Note that our bound is uniform in
\psi
(and is actually trivial when
\psi=\psi_{0}
is the principal character).

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For any additive character
\psi

of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, sets
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of cardinalities
\#{\mathcal{X}}_{i}=X_{i}
, and weights

\omega_{i}=(\omega_{i}(\mathbf{x}))_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}}
such that
\omega_{i}(\mathbf{x})
does not
depend on the
i
th coordinate of
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

and

\max_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}}|\omega_{i}(\mathbf{x})|\leq 1,

for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\begin{split}|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1}&,\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})|^{2^{n-1}}\\
&\leq X_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1}(X_{2}\ldots X_{n})^{2^{n-1}-2}\sum_{x_{2},y_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n},y_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\left|\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\psi(x_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2})\ldots(x_{n}-y_{n}))\right|.\end{split}

Proof.

For every complex number
\xi
we write
|\xi|^{2}=\xi\overline{\xi}
.

We first establish the result for
n=2
. Let us begin by eliminating
\omega_{2}
by applying the triangle inequality.

\displaystyle|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1},{\mathcal{X}}_{2};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\omega_{2}(x_{1})\sum_{x_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\omega_{1}(x_{2})\psi(x_{1}x_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\left|\omega_{2}(x_{1})\right|\left|\sum_{x_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\omega_{1}(x_{2})\psi(x_{1}x_{2})\right|.

Squaring both sides and applying the Cauchy inequality gives

\displaystyle|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1},{\mathcal{X}}_{2};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq X_{1}\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\left|\sum_{x_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\omega_{1}(x_{2})\psi(x_{1}x_{2})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=X_{1}\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\sum_{x_{2},y_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\omega_{1}(x_{2})\overline{\omega_{1}(y_{2})}\psi(x_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2}))

\displaystyle=X_{1}\sum_{x_{2},y_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\omega_{1}(x_{2})\overline{\omega_{1}(y_{2})}\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\psi(x_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq X_{1}\sum_{x_{2},y_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{2}}\left|\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\psi(x_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2}))\right|.

This proves the result for
n=2
. For
n>2
the argument is similar, though it requires the Hölder inequality. Using that
\omega_{n}(\mathbf{x})
does not depend on
x_{n}
, we get

\begin{split}|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1}&,\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})|\\
&\leq\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n-1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n-1}}\left|\sum_{x_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\ldots\omega_{n-1}(\mathbf{x})\psi(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})\right|.\end{split}

Hence, by the Cauchy inequality we derive

\begin{split}|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1}&,\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})|^{2}\\
&\leq X_{1}\ldots X_{n-1}\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n-1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n-1}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left|\sum_{x_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\ldots\omega_{n-1}(\mathbf{x})\psi(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})\right|^{2}\\
&=X_{1}\ldots X_{n-1}\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n-1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n-1}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{x_{n},y_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\overline{\omega}_{1}(\mathbf{y})\ldots\omega_{n-1}(\mathbf{x})\overline{\omega}_{n-1}(\mathbf{y})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\psi\left(x_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}(x_{n}-y_{n})\right),\end{split}

where
\mathbf{y}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n})
(and as before
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
).
By changing the order of summation, we obtain

|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})|^{2}\leq X_{1}\ldots X_{n-1}\sum_{x_{n},y_{n}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n}}\mathfrak{T}(x_{n},y_{n}),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{x_{n-1}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{n-1}}\omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\overline{\omega}_{1}(\mathbf{y})\ldots\omega_{n-1}(\mathbf{x})\overline{\omega}_{n-1}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\psi\left(x_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}(x_{n}-y_{n})\right).

Now, raising both sides to the
2^{n-2}
th power and using the Hölder inequality, we obtain

(2.3)

\begin{split}|T_{\psi}({\mathcal{X}}_{1}&,\ldots,{\mathcal{X}}_{n};\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})|^{2^{n-1}}\\

4.
Proofs of expansion of polynomial images

4.1.
General approach

Our proof strategy is modeled from that of Garaev
[
14
]
. Namely, each time
we consider the number of solutions to an appropriately chosen equation
and use our new bounds of exponential sums to obtain an asymptotic formula for the number
of its solutions. On the other hand,
using the specially designed structure of this equation,
we are able to explicitly produce a large family of solutions. Both the asymptotic formula
and the size of the family of explicit solutions depend on the cardinalities of the image sets
of interest and lead to the desired results.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We let
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}\setminus\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)
be of cardinality
E
and
define

as the number of solutions to the equation

for which we obviously have
N=0
. On the other hand, using the orthogonality of exponential functions
we express

as

N=\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{(a,b,c,d,e)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{E}}}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(\lambda(abc+d-e)\right).

Changing the order of summation and separating the term
ABCDE/p
corresponding to

,
we obtain

Hence, applying Theorem
1.1
, we derive

N-\frac{ABCDE}{p}\ll p^{-3/4}A^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda e)\right|.

Now applying the Cauchy inequality and then analogues of the orthogonality relation (

), we obtain

(4.1)

\begin{split}&N-\frac{ABCDE}{p}\\
&\quad\ll p^{-3/4}A^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|^{2}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda e)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad=p^{-3/4}A^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}(pD)^{1/2}(pE)^{1/2}\\
&\quad=p^{1/4}A^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}D^{1/2}E^{1/2}.\end{split}

Recalling that

which concludes the proof of the asymptotic formula.

For the lower bound, we consider the number

of solutions to the equation

Clearly

Furthermore, by the Cauchy inequality

(4.3)

\left(\sum_{\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}J(\eta)\right)^{2}\leq\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)\sum_{\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}J(\eta)^{2}=\#\left({\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)J,

where
J
is the number of solutions to the equation

a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}+d_{1}=a_{2}b_{2}c_{2}+d_{2},\quad(a_{\nu},b_{\nu},c_{\nu},d_{\nu})\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}},\ \nu=1,2.

As before, we write

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a_{\nu},b_{\nu},c_{\nu},d_{\nu})\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\\
\nu=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(\lambda(a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}c_{2}+d_{1}-d_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\left|\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{(a,b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(\lambda abc\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}B^{2}C^{2}D^{2}}{p}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\left|\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{(a,b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(\lambda abc\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|^{2}.

We now recall Theorem
1.1
and derive

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\ll\frac{A^{2}B^{2}C^{2}D^{2}}{p}+p^{-1/2}A^{3/2}B^{3/2}C^{7/4}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}B^{2}C^{2}D^{2}}{p}+p^{1/2}A^{3/2}B^{3/2}C^{7/4}D.

Substituting this bound in (
4.3
) and recalling (
4.2
)
we obtain the desired lower bound.

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}\setminus\left(({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{C}})^{3}+{\mathcal{D}}\right)
be of cardinality
E
.
Then for the number
N
of solutions to the equation

(a+b+c)^{3}+d-e=0,\qquad(a,b,c,d,e)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{E}}

we obviously have
N=0
.
On the other hand, using the orthogonality of exponential functions we express
N
as

N=\mathop{\sum\ldots\sum}_{(a,b,c,d,e)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{E}}}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda((a+b+c)^{3}+d-e).

Changing the order of summation and separating the term
ABCDE/p
corresponding to
\lambda=0
,
we obtain

\displaystyle N=

\displaystyle\frac{ABCDE}{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(\lambda(a+b+c)^{3}\right)\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(-\lambda e).

We now note that the sum over triples
(a,b,c)
is of the same types as in (
1.7
)
hence Theorem
1.3
applies.
Recalling that
N=0
we arrive to the bound

ABCDE\ll p^{1/8}A^{7/8}B^{29/32}C^{29/32}\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}}\left|\sum_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda d)\right|\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(\lambda e)\right|.

Now applying the Cauchy inequality and then analogues of (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle ABCDE

\displaystyle\ll p^{1/8}A^{7/8}B^{29/32}C^{29/32}(pD)^{1/2}(pE)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=p^{9/8}A^{7/8}B^{29/32}C^{29/32}D^{1/2}E^{1/2}.

The asymptotic formula follows.

For the lower bound, we consider the number
J(\eta)
of solutions to the equation

(a+b+c)^{3}+d=\eta,\qquad(a,b,c,d)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}},

and again proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
however using
Theorem
1.3
instead of Theorem
1.1
.
In particular, for

J=\sum_{\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}J(\eta)^{2}

we obtain

J\ll\frac{A^{2}B^{2}C^{2}D^{2}}{p}+p^{1/4}A^{7/4}B^{29/16}C^{29/16}D,

from which the desired result follows.

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.9

Let
N
be the number of solutions to the equation

(4.4)

u/(bc)+d=v,\qquad(b,c,d,u,v)\in{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{V}}.

Clearly the quintuples
(b,c,d,abc,a+d)
with

(a,b,c,d)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{D}}
are pairwise
distinct solutions to (
4.4
).
Hence

(4.5)

N\geq ABCD.

On the other hand, similarly to (
4.1
), we infer from Theorem
1.1
that

(4.6)

N=BCDUV/p+O\left(p^{1/4}U^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}D^{1/2}V^{1/2}\right).

Hence, we see from comparing (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), that either

ABCD\ll BCDUV/p

or

ABCD\ll p^{1/4}U^{3/4}B^{3/4}C^{7/8}D^{1/2}V^{1/2}

and we obtain the result.

5.
Comments

We now recall a result of Karatsuba
[
24
]

(see also
[
25
, Chapter VIII, Problem 9]
)
that for the character sums in (
2.1
)
we have

\max_{\chi\in\Omega^{*}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathcal{V}}\\
w\in{\mathcal{W}}\end{subarray}}\chi(v-w)\right|\ll V^{1-1/2r}Wq^{1/4r}+V^{1-1/2r}W^{1/2}q^{1/2r},

where the implied constant depends only on the
arbitrary parameter
r=1,2,\ldots
.
Used instead of (
2.1
), this allows us to improve
Lemma
2.2
for some ranges of
U,V,W
, however not
in ranges which are relevant for our applications to the trilinear sums (
1.1
).

Furthermore,
[
33
]
also contains the bound

D_{\times}({\mathcal{U}})\ll\frac{U^{8}}{p}+p^{2/3}U^{16/3},

which is better than that of Corollary
2.9
when
U\geq p^{4/7}
. It turns out,
however, that in this range applying the classical bound works better.

Finally, we note that some of our bounds can be refined and expressed in terms of
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}
-norms
of some weights instead of their
{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}
-norms.

Abstract

We also explore some examples of this decomposition for the particular case of the dihedral groups D 2 ​ n D_{2n} with n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 an even integer.

Introduction

In the past few years there has been a growing interest in studying
twisted
K
-theory motivated by its appearance in string theory and also due
to the celebrated theorem of Freed, Hokpins and Teleman (see
[
5
, Theorem 1]
).
In this article we study
G
-equivariant twisted
K
-theory when
G
is a finite group.
Our main goal is to show that, under suitable hypothesis, the canonical decomposition
theorem for projective representations can be used to obtain a decomposition for twisted

G
-equivariant
K
-theory as a direct sum of other twisted equivariant
K
-theories,
thus generalizing the work in
[
6
]
, where a similar decomposition was obtained for
equivariant
K
-theory.

Suppose that we have a short exact sequence of finite groups

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow A\rightarrow G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}Q\rightarrow 1.

Let
X
be a compact and Hausdorff
G
-space on which
A
acts trivially and fix

\alpha
a normalized
2
-cocycle on
G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Associated to the
cocycle
\alpha
we have a central extension

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1

and the action of
G
on
X
can be extended to an action of
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on
X

in such a way that the central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts trivially. The
\alpha
-twisted

G
-equivariant
K
-theory of
X
,
{}^{\alpha}K^{\ast}_{G}(X)
, is constructed

using

\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant vector bundles on which the central factor

\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of scalars. The main object of study in this work are
the twisted
K
-groups
{}^{\alpha}K^{\ast}_{G}(X)
.

Representations of the group

\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on which the central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}

acts by multiplication of scalars are in a one to one correspondence
with
\alpha
-projective representations of
G
. Via this correspondence we can use
the classical tools of projective representations to study the twisted
K
-groups

{}^{\alpha}K^{\ast}_{G}(X)
. To this end we show in Section
2
that,
if
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of
\alpha
-projective
representations of
A
, then there is an action of
G
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

and this action factors through an action of
Q
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
.
Given an isomorphism class
[\tau]\in\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
let

Q_{[\tau]}
denote the isotropy subgroup at
[\tau]
. Using Lemma
2.8

we show that we can associate to each
[\tau]\in\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

a normalized
2
-cocycle defined on
Q_{[\tau]}
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. This is
precisely the data needed to construct a twisted version of
Q_{[\tau]}
-equivariant

K
-theory. With this in mind, the main result of this article is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
. Let
\alpha

be a normalized
2
-cocycle on
G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
X
a
compact
G
-space on which
A
acts trivially.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}\colon\ \phantom{i}^{\alpha}K_{G}^{\ast}(X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bigoplus_{[\tau]\in G\backslash\Irr^{\alpha}(A)}\phantom{i}^{\beta_{\tau,\alpha}}K^{\ast}_{Q_{[\tau]}}(X),

\displaystyle[E]

\displaystyle\mapsto\bigoplus_{[\tau]\in G\backslash\Irr^{\alpha}(A)}\big[\Hom_{\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{V}_{\tau},E)\big].

Explicit formulas for the cocycles
\beta_{\tau,\alpha}
that appear in the previous theorem
are described in Section
2
. Observe that when
\alpha
is the trivial cocycle
then
\phantom{i}{}^{\alpha}K_{G}^{\ast}(X)
agrees
with
K_{G}^{*}(X)
and the previous decomposition agrees with
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]

in this case. Therefore Theorem
1.1
generalizes
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
. In fact,
Theorem
1.1
is proved using ideas similar to the ones used to prove

[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
.

We remark that the main idea used to prove Theorem
1.1
is well known and is often referred
to in the literature as “Mackey decomposition” or the “Mackey machine”. Moreover, similar decomposition
theorems have been obtained using these ideas for the case of equivariant K-theory. To the knowledge of
the authors the first instance where such a decomposition appears is
[
10
]
.

In 1981 in his Ph.D. Thesis Wassermann used the “Mackey machine” in

-algebras to derive a decomposition for equivariant K-theory in the particular

with one orbit type.
This means that for every

is conjugated to a fixed subgroup

(see

[

[

]

to obtain a similar decomposition result for equivariant K-theory in the case of proper actions of a compact Lie
group
G
.

Theorem
1.1
could also be proved using the work of Freed, Hopkins and
Teleman
[
5
]
. However, we have chosen to prove it directly as to obtain an explicit

Obtaining explicit formulas for such cocycles is one of the main contributions of this

[

]
.

The outline of this article is as follows. In Section

for the particular case of the dihedral group

with

2
Projective representations

In this section we recall some basic definitions and properties of projective representations that
will be used throughout this article.

2.1
Basic definitions

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
V
a finite dimensional complex
vector space. A map
\rho\colon G\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
is called a

projective representation
of
G
if there exists a
function

\displaystyle\alpha\colon\ G\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}

such that

\displaystyle\rho(g)\rho(h)=\alpha(g,h)\rho(gh)

(2.1)

for all
g,h\in G
and
\rho(1)={\rm Id}_{V}
.

Note that if
\rho
satisfies equation (
2.1
) then the function
\alpha
defines a

\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-valued normalized 2-cocycle on
G
; that is, for all
g,h,k\in G
we have:

\displaystyle\alpha(gh,k)\alpha(g,h)=\alpha(g,hk)\alpha(h,k),

\displaystyle\alpha(g,1)=\alpha(1,g)=1.

To stress the dependence of
\rho
on
V
and
\alpha
, we shall
often refer to
\rho
as an
\alpha
-representation of
G

on the space
V
or, simply as an
\alpha
-representation of

G
, if
V
is not pertinent to the discussion.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\alpha
is the trivial cocycle; that is, if
\alpha(g,h)=1
for all
g,h\in G
, then

\alpha
-representations of
G
are simply ordinary representations of
G
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that
\rho_{i}\colon G\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V_{i})
with
(i=1,2)
are two
\alpha
-representations.

1.

A linear map
\varphi\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
is said to be a map of projective representations or a

G
-map if for any
g\in G
and any
v\in V_{1}
we have
\varphi(\rho_{1}(g)v)=\rho_{2}(g)\varphi(v)
.
We write
\Hom_{G}(V_{1},V_{2})
for the set of
G
-morphisms from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
.

2.

The
\alpha
-representations
V_{1}
and
V_{2}

are said to be
linearly equivalent
or
isomorphic
if
there exists a
G
-map
f\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
that is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
In other words,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic if there is a vector space
isomorphism
f\colon V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that
\rho_{2}(g)=f\rho_{1}(g)f^{-1}
for all
g\in G
.

Just as in the case of the ordinary theory of representations of groups
we have similar notions for projective representations such as irreducible representations
and unitary representations. Given a
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-valued normalized 2-cocycle
\alpha
on

G
we denote by
\Irr^{\alpha}(G)
the set of isomorphism classes of
complex irreducible
\alpha
-representations of
G
.
If
\rho\colon G\to{\rm GL}(V)
is an irreducible
\alpha
-representation of
G
then
[\rho]\in\Irr^{\alpha}(G)

denotes the corresponding isomorphism class. Observe that the direct sum of two
\alpha
-representations
is also an
\alpha
-representation. Thus we can form the monoid of isomorphism classes of
\alpha
-representations.
The
\alpha
-twisted representation group of
G
, denoted by
R^{\alpha}(G)
,
is defined as the associated Grothendieck group. As an abelian group
R^{\alpha}(G)
is a free abelian
group with one generator for each element in
\Irr^{\alpha}(G)
.
We remark that the classical results of representation theory
such as complete reducibility and Schur’s lemma also hold for the case of projective representations.
We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for
the basic theory of projective representations.

Example 2.4
.

Consider the dihedral group
D_{2n}
of order
2n
defined by

\displaystyle D_{2n}=\big\langle a,b\mid a^{n}=b^{2}=1,\,bab=a^{-1}\big\rangle.

For such groups we have

\displaystyle H^{2}\big(D_{2n},\mathbb{S}^{1}\big)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad n\text{ is odd},\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&\text{if}\quad n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

In this example we only consider the case where
n
is even as otherwise we will obtain
usual representations. Let
n\geq 2
be an even integer,
\epsilon
a primitive
n
-th root of
unity in
\mathbb{C}
and let

\displaystyle\alpha\colon\ D_{2n}\times D_{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}

be the function defined by

\displaystyle\alpha\big(a^{j},a^{k}b^{l}\big)=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha\big(a^{j}b,a^{k}b^{l}\big)=\epsilon^{k}\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j,k\leq n-1\quad\text{and}\quad l=0,1.

The function
\alpha
defines a normalized
2
-cocycle on
D_{2n}
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
whose
corresponding cohomology class is a generator in
H^{2}\big(D_{2n},\mathbb{S}^{1}\big)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.
For each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n/2\}
put

\displaystyle A_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{i}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{1-i}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\!.

Consider the map

\displaystyle\tau_{i}\colon\ D_{2n}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

defined by

\displaystyle\tau_{i}\big(a^{k}b^{l}\big)=A_{i}^{k}B_{i}^{l}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1\quad\text{and}\quad l=0,1.

These assignments determine the irreducible, non-equivalent
\alpha
-representations of
D_{2n}

so that
\Irr^{\alpha}(D_{2n})=\big\{[\tau_{1}],\dots,[\tau_{n/2}]\big\}
and as an abelian group we
have an isomorphism
R^{\alpha}(D_{2n})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n/2}\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{i}]

(see for example
[
8
, Chapter 5, Theorem 7.1]
).

A key feature of projective representations is that we also have the following canonical
decomposition whose proof can be obtained in a similar way as in the case of regular
representations.

Theorem 2.5

(canonical decomposition)
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is a
normalized
2
-cocycle of a finite group
G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Let
W
be a
finite-dimensional
\alpha
-representation. Then the assignment

\displaystyle\gamma\colon\ \bigoplus_{[\rho]\in\Irr^{\alpha}(G)}V_{\rho}\otimes\Hom_{G}(V_{\rho},W)

\displaystyle\rightarrow W,

\displaystyle v\otimes f

\displaystyle\mapsto f(v)

defines an isomorphism of
\alpha
-representations.

Suppose now that
\alpha
is a normalized 2-cocycle on
G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
We can associate to
\alpha
a central extension of
G
by
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in the following way.
As a set define

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}=\big\{(g,z)\mid g\in G,z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\big\}.

The product structure in
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
is given by
the assignment

\displaystyle(g_{1},z_{1})(g_{2},z_{2}):=\left(g_{1}g_{2},\alpha(g_{1},g_{2})z_{1}z_{2}\right).

This way
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
is a compact Lie group that fits
into a central extension

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1.

Let
\rho\colon G\to{\rm GL}(V)
be an
\alpha
-representation of
G
. If we define

\tilde{\rho}\colon\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}\to{\rm GL}(V)

by
\tilde{\rho}(g,z)=z\rho(g)
then
\tilde{\rho}
defines a representation of

\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on which the central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of scalars.
Conversely, if
\tilde{\rho}\colon\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}\to{\rm GL}(V)
is a representation of

\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on which the central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of scalars
then the function
\rho\colon G\to{\rm GL}(V)
given by
\rho(g)=\tilde{\rho}(g,1)
defines an

\alpha
-representation of
G
. The above assignment defines a one to one correspondence between

\alpha
-representations of
G
and representations of
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on which the
central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of scalars. Via this correspondence we will
switch back and forth between
\alpha
-representations of
G
and representations of

\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
on which the central factor
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of scalars
without explicitly mentioning it. In addition, if
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are two

\alpha
-representations of
G
then having a map of projective representations

f\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
is equivalent to having a linear map

f\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
that is
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant. With this correspondence
in mind we will also identify
\Hom_{G}(V_{1},V_{2})
with

\Hom_{\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}}(V_{1},V_{2})
without explicitly mentioning it.

2.2
Cocycles and projective representations

Suppose now that
A
is a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
so that we have a short exact
sequence

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow A\rightarrow G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}Q\rightarrow 1.

Assume that
\alpha
is a normalized
2
-cocycle of
G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
by restriction we can also see
\alpha
as a cocycle defined on
A
.
Let us define an action of
G
on the set
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
in the following way.
Given
\rho\colon A\rightarrow U(V_{\rho})
an
\alpha
-representation and
g\in G
we define

g\cdot\rho\colon A\rightarrow U(V_{\rho})
so that if
a\in A
we have:

\displaystyle g\cdot\rho(a)=\alpha(g^{-1}a,g)\alpha(g,g^{-1}a)^{-1}\rho(g^{-1}ag)\in U(V_{\rho}).

(2.2)

Proposition 2.6
.

The above assignment defines a left action of
G
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
. Furthermore, for all

b\in A
, we have that
b\cdot\rho\cong\rho
so that the action of
G
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

factors to an action of
Q=G/A
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
.

Proof.

First we show that
g\cdot\rho
is an
\alpha
-representation
of
A
. Indeed, for all
g\in G
and
a,b\in A
we have

\displaystyle(g\cdot\rho(a))(g\cdot\rho(b))

\displaystyle=\big(\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\rho\big(g^{-1}ag\big)\big)\big(\alpha\big(g^{-1}b,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}b\big)^{-1}\rho\big(g^{-1}bg\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\alpha\big(g^{-1}b,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}b\big)^{-1}\alpha\big(g^{-1}ag,g^{-1}bg\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,\times\rho\big(g^{-1}abg\big)=\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\alpha\big(g^{-1}ab,g\big)\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,b\big)\rho\big(g^{-1}abg\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha(a,b)\alpha\big(g^{-1}ab,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}ab\big)^{-1}\rho\big(g^{-1}abg\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha(a,b)(g\cdot\rho(ab)).

The above equalities are obtained using the 2-cocycle equation for
\alpha
.
Now, we show that this definition satisfies the axioms of an action. If

\rho\colon A\rightarrow U(V_{\rho})
is an
\alpha
-representation,
as
\alpha
is a normalized cocycle,

\displaystyle 1\cdot\rho(a)=\alpha(a,1)\alpha(1,a)^{-1}\rho(a)=\rho(a).

Moreover, given
g,h\in G
and
a\in A
, we have

\displaystyle g\cdot(h\cdot\rho)(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}(h\cdot\rho)\big(g^{-1}ag\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\alpha\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}ag,h\big)\alpha\big(h,h^{-1}g^{-1}ag\big)^{-1}\rho\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}agh\big)

and

\displaystyle(gh)\cdot\rho(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}a,gh\big)\alpha\big(gh,h^{-1}g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\rho\big((gh)^{-1}a(gh)\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}a,gh\big)\alpha\big(gh,h^{-1}g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\rho\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}agh\big).

Manipulating the cocycle equation for
\alpha
it can be proved that

\displaystyle\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)

\displaystyle\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\alpha\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}ag,h\big)\alpha\big(h,h^{-1}g^{-1}ag\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(h^{-1}g^{-1}a,gh\big)\alpha\big(gh,h^{-1}g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}.

This implies that for all
a\in A

\displaystyle g\cdot(h\cdot\rho)(a)=(gh)\cdot\rho(a).

Finally, for
a,b\in A
expanding and using the cocycle equation we obtain

\displaystyle b\cdot\rho(a)=\rho(b)^{-1}\rho(a)\rho(b)

and thus
b\cdot\rho\cong\rho
as
\alpha
-representations.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The action of
G
on the set
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
given by equation (
2.2
) can be
described in an alternative way as follows. Suppose that
\alpha
is a normalized
2
-cocycle of

G
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and let
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}
be the central extension corresponding
to the cocycle
\alpha
. If
\rho\colon A\rightarrow U(V_{\rho})

\tilde{\rho}\colon\tilde{A}_{\alpha}\to U(V_{\rho})

Given
(g,z)\in\widetilde{G}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle(g,z)\cdot\tilde{\rho}(a,w)=\tilde{\rho}\big((g,z)^{-1}(a,w)(g,z)\big).

-representation as an

\alpha

). Explicitly,
for

\displaystyle g\cdot\rho(a):\!

\displaystyle=(g,1)\cdot\tilde{\rho}(a,1)=\tilde{\rho}\big(g^{-1}ag,\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(g^{-1}a,g\big)\alpha\big(g,g^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\rho\big(g^{-1}ag\big).

As above assume that
A
is a normal subgroup of a group
G
and
Q=G/A
. Fix an assignment

\sigma\colon Q\rightarrow G
such that
\pi(\sigma(q))=q
for all
q\in Q
with
\sigma(1)=1
. We remark that the map
\sigma

not necessarily have to be a group homomorphism.

Suppose that
\rho\colon A\rightarrow U(V_{\rho})
is a complex irreducible
\alpha
-representation
with the property that

is isomorphic to
\rho
for every
g\in G

(under the action defined in equation (

\displaystyle\sigma(q)\cdot\rho(a)=M_{q}^{-1}\rho(a)M_{q}.

This means that

\displaystyle\alpha\big(\sigma(q)^{-1}a,\sigma(q)\big)\alpha\big(\sigma(q),\sigma(q)^{-1}a\big)^{-1}\rho\big(\sigma(q)^{-1}a\sigma(q)\big)=M_{q}^{-1}\rho(a)M_{q}

. We can choose

\sigma(1)\cdot\rho=\rho
.
Let
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}
be the central extension of

\rho(a)=i
.
For the action of
D_{8}
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
we have

\displaystyle b\cdot\rho(a)=\alpha(ba,b)\alpha(b,ba)^{-1}\rho(bab)=\alpha\big(a^{3}b,b\big)\alpha\big(b,a^{3}b\big)^{-1}\rho(a^{3})=\big(i^{3}\big)^{-1}i^{3}=1

therefore
b\cdot\rho=1
. Moreover,

\displaystyle b\cdot\rho^{2}(a)=\alpha(ba,b)\alpha(b,ba)^{-1}\rho^{2}(bab)=\alpha\big(a^{3}b,b\big)\alpha\big(b,a^{3}b\big)^{-1}\rho^{2}\big(a^{3}\big)=\big(i^{3}\big)^{-1}\big(\rho^{2}(a)\big)^{3}=-i

so that
b\cdot\rho^{2}=\rho^{3}
. We conclude that orbits of the
D_{8}
action on

\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
are
\{[1],[\rho]\}
and
\big\{\big[\rho^{2}\big],\big[\rho^{3}\big]\big\}
. Thus we can choose

[1]
and
\big[\rho^{2}\big]
as representatives for the elements in
D_{8}\backslash\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

and
G_{[1]}=G_{[\rho^{2}]}=A
.
In this case Theorem
3.6
gives us an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})=^{\alpha}K^{\ast}_{D_{8}}(\ast)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[1]})_{\alpha},1}(\ast)\oplus K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{2}]})_{\alpha},\rho^{2}}(\ast).

As
G_{[1]}=G_{[\rho^{2}]}=A
and
\alpha
restricted to
A
is trivial we have that

\big(\widetilde{G}_{[1]}\big)_{\alpha}=\big(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{2}]}\big)_{\alpha}=A\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Therefore
K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[1]})_{\alpha},1}(\ast)\cong\mathbb{Z}\tilde{1}
and

K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{2}]})_{\alpha},\rho^{2}}(\ast)\cong\mathbb{Z}\tilde{\rho^{2}}
.
(Recall that
\tilde{\rho^{2}}
denotes the representation of

\big(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{2}]}\big)_{\alpha}
on which
\mathbb{S}^{1}
acts by multiplication of
scalars corresponding to
\rho^{2}
and similarly for
\tilde{1}
).
For the representations
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(a)=\left(\,\begin{matrix}\framebox{$i$}&0\\[-0.86108pt]
0&\framebox{1}\end{matrix}\,\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tau_{2}(a)=\left(\,\begin{matrix}\framebox{$-1$}&0\\[-0.86108pt]
0&\framebox{$-i$}\end{matrix}\,\right)\!.

Thus as
A
-representations
\tau_{1}
is isomorphic to
1\oplus\rho
and

\tau_{2}
is isomorphic to
\rho^{2}\oplus\rho^{3}
.
Moreover, in the isomorphism given by Theorem
3.6
we have

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})\cong\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[1]})_{\alpha},1}(\ast)\oplus K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{2}]})_{\alpha},\rho^{2}}\cong\mathbb{Z}{\tilde{1}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tilde{\rho^{2}},

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{1},

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\rho^{2}}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.7
we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}\phantom{i}^{\beta_{1,\alpha}}K^{\ast}_{Q_{[1]}}(\ast)\oplus\phantom{i}^{\beta_{\rho^{2},\alpha}}K^{\ast}_{Q_{[\rho^{2}]}}(\ast).

As
G_{[1]}=G_{[\rho^{2}]}=A
we have that
Q_{[1]}=Q_{[\rho^{2}]}=\{1\}
is the
trivial group. Therefore
\beta_{1,\alpha}
and
\beta_{\rho^{2},\alpha}
are
the trivial cocycles and Theorem
3.7
gives us the isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})\cong\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ast}_{\{1\}}(\ast)\oplus K^{\ast}_{\{1\}}(\ast)\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}.

Example 5.2
.

Suppose now that
G=D_{8}
and
A=Z(D_{8})=\big\langle a^{2}\big\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.
Therefore in this case we have

\displaystyle Q=G/A=\{[1],[b],[a],[ab]\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2.

As in the previous example
\alpha|_{A}
is trivial and

\displaystyle\Irr^{\alpha}(A)=\Irr(A)=\{[1],[\sigma]\},

\sigma\colon A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the representation defined by
\sigma\big(a^{2}\big)=-1
.
For the action of
D_{8}
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

\displaystyle b\cdot\sigma\big(a^{2}\big)=\alpha\big(ba^{2},b\big)\alpha\big(b,ba^{2}\big)^{-1}\sigma\big(ba^{2}b\big)=\alpha\big(a^{2}b,b\big)\alpha\big(b,a^{2}b\big)^{-1}\sigma\big(a^{2}\big)=(-1)(-1)=1

. In particular the action of

D_{8}
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)

[1]

as a representative for the set

. For the

1

.

X=\ast
then Theorem
3.6

gives us an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})\cong\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[1]})_{\alpha},1}(\ast).

As
\alpha
is trivial on

\rho\colon\langle a\rangle\to\mathbb{C}

\rho(a)=i

then
\tilde{1}
and
\tilde{\rho^{2}}

\big(\big(\widetilde{G}_{[1]}\big)_{\alpha},1\big)

\ast

\displaystyle K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[1]})_{\alpha},1}(\ast)\cong\mathbb{Z}\tilde{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tilde{\rho^{2}}.

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(a^{2})=\left(\,\begin{matrix}\framebox{$-1$}&0\\[-0.86108pt]
0&\framebox{1}\end{matrix}\,\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tau_{2}(a^{2})=\left(\,\begin{matrix}\framebox{1}&0\\[-0.86108pt]
0&\framebox{$-1$}\end{matrix}\,\right)\!.

-representations we have

\langle a\rangle

3.6
we have

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{8})\cong\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{2}

Example 5.3
.

Example
5.1
can easily be generalized to the dihedral groups
D_{2n}

with
n
an even number. Suppose then that
n
is an even number and let
D_{2n}

be the group generated by the elements
a,b
subject to the relations
a^{n}=b^{2}=1

and
bab=a^{-1}
. Fix
\epsilon
a primitive
n
-th root of unity and let

\displaystyle\alpha\colon\ D_{2n}\times D_{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}

be the function defined by

\displaystyle\alpha\big(a^{j},a^{k}b^{l}\big)=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha\big(a^{j}b,a^{k}b^{l}\big)=\epsilon^{k}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j,k\leq n-1\quad\text{and}\quad l=0,1.

The function
\alpha
defines a normalized
2
-cocycle on
D_{2n}
with values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
whose
corresponding cohomology class is the generator in
H^{2}\big(D_{2n},\mathbb{S}^{1}\big)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.
By Example
2.4
we know that the irreducible projective
\alpha
-representations of

D_{2n}
are
\tau_{i}\colon D_{2n}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for
i=1,\dots,n/2
, defined by

\displaystyle\tau_{i}\big(a^{k}b^{l}\big)=A_{i}^{k}B_{i}^{l}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1\quad\text{and}\quad l=0,1,

where

\displaystyle A_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{i}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{1-i}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\!.

If we take
X=\ast
endowed with the trivial
D_{2n}
action we have

\phantom{i}{}^{\alpha}K^{\ast}_{D_{2n}}(\ast)\cong R^{\alpha}(D_{2n})=\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{n/2}.

Take
A=\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/n
so that
Q=D_{2n}/A=\{[1],[b]\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.
Observe that
\alpha|_{A}
is trivial and thus

\displaystyle\Irr^{\alpha}(A)=\Irr(A)=\big\{[1],[\rho],\big[\rho^{2}\big],\dots,\big[\rho^{n-1}\big]\big\},

where
\rho(a)=\epsilon
. For the action of
D_{2n}
on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
we have that

\displaystyle b\cdot 1=\rho,\qquad b\cdot\rho^{2}=\rho^{n-1},\qquad\dots,\qquad b\cdot\rho^{n/2}=\rho^{n/2+1}

so that orbits of the
D_{2n}
action on
\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
are

\big\{[1],[\rho]\big\},\big\{\big[\rho^{2}\big],\big[\rho^{n-1}\big]\big\},\dots,\big\{\big[\rho^{n/2}\big],\big[\rho^{n/2+1}\big]\big\}
.
We can choose
[\rho],\big[\rho^{2}\big],\dots,\big[\rho^{n/2}\big]
as representatives for the set

D_{2n}\backslash\Irr^{\alpha}(A)
and we have
G_{[\rho]}=G_{[\rho^{2}]}=\cdots=G_{[\rho^{n/2}]}=A
. As
\alpha|_{A}
is trivial we have

\big(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{i}]}\big)_{\alpha}=A\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
for
i=1,\dots,n/2
.
In this case Theorem
3.6
gives us the isomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{2n})=\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{n/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n/2}K^{\ast}_{(\widetilde{G}_{[\rho^{i}]})_{\alpha},\rho^{i}}(\ast)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n/2}\mathbb{Z}\tilde{\rho^{i}},

\displaystyle\tau_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\rho}^{i}.

On the other hand, for
i=1,\dots,n/2
we have
Q_{[\rho^{i}]}=\{1\}
and

\beta_{\rho^{i},\alpha}
is the trivial cocycle. In this case
Theorem
3.7
gives us an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ R^{\alpha}(D_{2n})\cong\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau_{n/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n/2}K^{\ast}_{\{1\}}(\ast)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n/2}.

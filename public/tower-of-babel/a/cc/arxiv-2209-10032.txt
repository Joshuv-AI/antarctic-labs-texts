Robust Dynamic State Estimation of Multi-Machine Power Networks with Solar Farms and Dynamics Loads
Muhammad Nadeem, Ahmad F. Taha

Abstract
Conventional state estimation routines of electrical grids are mainly reliant on dynamic models of fossil fuel-based resources. These models commonly contain differential equations describing synchronous generator models and algebraic equations modeling power flow/balance equations. Fuel-free power systems that are driven by inertia-less renewable energy resources will hence require new models and upgraded estimation routines. To that end, in this paper we propose a robust estimator for an interconnected model of power networks comprised of a comprehensive ninth order synchronous generator model, advanced power electronics-based models for photovoltaic (PV) power plants, constant power loads, constant impedance loads, and motor loads. The presented state estimator design is based on Lyapunov stability criteria for nonlinear differential algebraic equation (DAE) models and is posed as a convex semi-definite optimization problem. Thorough simulations studies have been carried out on IEEE-39 bus test system to showcase the robustness of the proposed estimator against unknown uncertainty from load demand and solar irradiance.



I
Introduction and Paper Contributions

P
ower systems are moving rapidly toward a greener and more sustainable future through the widespread installation of renewable energy resources (RERs).
The intermittent and volatile nature of RERs has created challenges in modern power systems and made the secure and reliable operation of the grid more difficult. The solution to these problems can be found in dynamic state estimation (DSE) for the next generation of smart power grids. In particular DSE can provide accurate realtime estimates of the physical states of electrical grid which can be highly beneficial for the realtime feedback control and health monitoring of electric grid
[
1
]
.

In this regard, thorough research has been carried out in the past two decades in power system DSE mainly focusing on estimating generator internal states (rotor angle and frequency) and algebraic states (voltage and current phasors). All of the current DSE algorithms can be divided into two broad categories: (1) deterministic observers and (2) Kalman filters (also known as stochastic estimators).
In deterministic observers the basic idea is as follows: First, a dynamical model for the error dynamics is computed (this model explains the evolution of error between original and estimated state variables), then observer gain is designed based on Lyapunov stability notion such that it drives the error model near zero
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

On the other hand Kalman filters (KF) are iterative and they primarily exploit the statistical properties (such as normal distribution) of the noise/disturbances to minimize their impact on the performance of DSE
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

A summary of all the techniques used for power system DSE can be found in recent survey paper
[
1
]
, while a thorough comparative study between different derivatives of Kalman filter can be found in
[
8
]
. Similarly, a comparative analysis between stochastic estimators and deterministic observers can also be seen in
[
9
]
. An extensive summary of these methods is beyond the scope of this paper.

In the current literature, the DSE algorithms are primarily based on a simple power system model capturing only dynamical model of generators and few algebraic constraints showcasing power balance equations. Thus the monitoring scope of DSE is mainly limited to the estimation of algebraic states and generator internal states. However, with the rapid deployment of solar PV power plants and dynamic loads in the electrical grid these traditional power systems models are gradually becoming obsolete and out of context
[
1
]
. Hence to achieve grid-wise situational awareness in the future power systems it is important to model solar PV power plants and dynamic loads in the nonlinear descriptor (NL-D) model of power system while performing DSE.

Unfortunately, the literature on state estimation of multi-machine power network models with synchronous machines, solar PV power plants, and motor loads while considering PMU measurements models and algebraic constraints is virtually non-existent. In the recent study
[
10
]
DSE has been extended and state estimation for a detailed PV power plant model has been reported, however, the study is limited to distribution network only and does not estimate algebraic variables and generators’ internal states.

Based on the above discussion the contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We propose DSE technique for a holistic model of a power system having ninth order synchronous generator model, advanced power electronics-based models of solar farms, motor loads, constant power loads, and constant impedance loads. The proposed DSE algorithm can simultaneously estimate all the states of the power system that include dynamic states of solar farm, loads and algebraic states.

•

To design a robust estimator that can handle load fluctuations and uncertainty from RERs, we propose an
H_{\infty}
based state estimation technique. The main advantage of
H_{\infty}
based state estimator over Kalman filters is that no prior knowledge about the statistic of uncertainty is required.

•

We showcase the performance of the proposed estimator on IEEE case39 test power system which is widely used for DSE studies in power systems.

III
Estimator for NL-D Model of Power Systems

In this section, we propose a Luenberger type observer design for the NL-D model depicted in (
11
). First, we focus on modeling the uncertainties in loads and renewables and present a technique to parameterize the nonlinear function
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
in the power system dynamics.

III-A

Modeling uncertainty from loads and renewables

In Eq. (
11
) the vector
\boldsymbol{w}
encapsulates load demand and irradiance, note that both of these quantities are time-varying and fluctuating. To that end, herein we assume that hour- or minute-ahead predictions of loads and irradiance are available while the fluctuation/disturbances in these quantities are unknown. Notice that this is realistic as power systems operators record and publish these quantities on a daily basis (see daily hour- and minute-ahead predictions of load and renewables published by California independent system operator
[
14
]
). However, the prediction may be inaccurate. In particular, high fidelity estimate of RERs are difficult to obtain. Accordingly, we can write
\boldsymbol{w}=\bar{\boldsymbol{w}}+\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
, where
\bar{\boldsymbol{w}}
is the predicted/known values and
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
defines all the disturbances/uncertainties in these quantities. The goal of the estimator is to provide accurate state estimation results under unknown uncertainty
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
. To that end, the NL-D model (
11
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{E}\dot{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\hskip-0.28436pt&=\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{A}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\hskip-0.2275pt\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{f}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{w}\right)\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{B}}{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{B}}_{w}\bar{\boldsymbol{w}}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{B}}_{w}\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\end{split}

(11a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{y}&=\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{v}.\end{split}

(11b)

The main reason for splitting the vector
\boldsymbol{w}
into known/predicted and unknown parts is because later on in the estimator dynamics to achieve robust performance the estimator only has access to the predicted value of
\boldsymbol{w}
and disturbances
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
is kept unknown to the estimator.

In the following section we discuss Lipschitz continuity condition to classify the nonlinear vector valued function
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
in the system dynamics.

III-B

Parameterizing nonlinearities in the NL-D model

To synthesize robust observers it is crucial to identify and parameterize the nonlinearities in the dynamical system.
Hence, to express the nonlinear function
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
in a better way in the NL-D model we assume that
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
is Lipschitz continuous, which means
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
is differentiable everywhere and the rate of change is bounded above by a real number. Smallest such real number is called Lipschitz constant. With that in mind we assume
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathcal{X}
define the operating region of state vector
\boldsymbol{x}
, then the Lipschitz bounding condition for
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})-\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})\rVert_{2}\leq\alpha\lVert(\boldsymbol{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\rVert_{2}

(12)

where
\alpha
is the Lipschitz constant. In the case of power systems the Lipschitz constant can be assumed based on operator knowledge and by looking at the overall size/topology of the grid or it can also be computed using much more sophisticated ways as shown in
[
15
,
16
]
. Also, since the Lipschitz constant provides an operational bound on grid state variables, its value can be varied to consider anomalies and deviations from steady state values and vice versa
[
2
]
. Showcasing methods to compute Lipschitz constant is beyond the scope of this paper.

III-C

H_{\infty}
based state estimator design

In this study we are designing a Luenberger type state estimator for NL-D model of power systems depicted in (
11
). The overall estimator design is primarily based on Lyapunov stability criterion and
H_{\infty}
estimation concept is used to achieve robust performance under unknown fluctuations/disturbances from loads and RERs.

In state estimation literature, the
H_{\infty}
concept was initially introduced in
[
17
]
to synthesize a robust estimator for linear systems subject to unknown uncertainty. The main advantage of
H_{\infty}
based state estimation is that no prior knowledge or assumptions are required about the statistical properties of uncertainty. In
H_{\infty}
based state estimation the uncertainty is considered as a random bounded signal and then the observer is designed such that it ensures a particular
H_{\infty}
performance for the error dynamics for all such bounded uncertainty, such that:
\lVert{\boldsymbol{e}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}\leq\gamma^{2}\lVert{\Delta\boldsymbol{w}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}
with performance level
\gamma
.

That being said, we now focus on presenting the structure of our observer design for the NL-D model of power system depicted in (
11
). To begin, let
\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}
be the estimated outputs and
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
be the estimated states variables. Then Luenberger type state estimator for Eq. (
11
) can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{E}\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}}\hskip-0.28436pt&=\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{A}}{\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt{\boldsymbol{f}}\left({\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\bar{\boldsymbol{w}}}\right)+\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{y}-{\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})}+{\boldsymbol{B}}{{\boldsymbol{u}}}+{\boldsymbol{B}_{w}}{{\bar{\boldsymbol{w}}}}\end{split}

(13a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}&=\boldsymbol{C}\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\end{split}

(13b)

where
\boldsymbol{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is the estimator gain matrix. Even if the estimator starts from different initial conditions, using measurement provided by PMUs
\boldsymbol{y}
, the gain matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
ensures the convergence of estimated states
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
to their true values
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
. The main objective of this paper is to design an appropriate gain matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
such that robust state estimation can be achieved from the estimator dynamics (
13
).

To that end, we define the error between actual and true values of state variables as
\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
. Then using (
11
) and (
13
) the model for the error dynamics can be computed as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}\dot{{\boldsymbol{e}}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}_{c}\boldsymbol{e}+\Delta\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{B}_{w}\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\vskip-5.69046pt

(14)

where
\boldsymbol{A}_{c}=(\boldsymbol{A}-\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{C})
and
\Delta\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{f}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{w}}\right)-{\boldsymbol{f}}\left({\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}},{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{w}}\right)
. The primary objective of the estimator is to drive the differential equation of the error (
14
) near zero under unknown uncertainty
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
. Before we proceed to the theory of designing the gain matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
, we present key assumption that is critical in developing the proposed methodology.

Assumption 1

The effect of uncertainty
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
on the norm of
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
is negligible, meaning:

\displaystyle\lVert\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},\boldsymbol{u},\bar{\boldsymbol{w}})\rVert_{2}\approx\lVert\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},\boldsymbol{u},{\boldsymbol{w}})\rVert_{2}.

(15)

Assumption
1
is crucial in developing the theory of the observer design as it is required while applying the S-procedure lemma
[
18
]
in the derivation of estimator gain in a tractable way. Notice that Assumption
1
is mild and holds in the case of power networks as the nonlinear function in the power systems models are commonly independent of uncertainties and are mostly dependent only on state vector, see
[
19
]
and references therein. To validate this on the NL-D model (
13
) we used different IEEE test power systems such as WECC-9 bus system and IEEE-39 bus system and we note that by varying
\boldsymbol{P}_{d}
in
\boldsymbol{w}
the norm of
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
does not change while by adding uncertainty in
\boldsymbol{I}_{r}
the norm of
\boldsymbol{f}(.)
changes slightly, such as by decreasing the irradiance by
40\%
the percentage change in
\lVert\boldsymbol{f}(.)\rVert_{2}
is
0.09\hskip-1.42271pt\times\hskip-1.42271pt10^{-3}\%
. Hence Assumption
1
is empirically satisfied. Proving Assumption
1
theoretically is an interesting research question beyond the scope of this paper.

With that in mind, we can find necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of observer gain matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
. To that end, let us assume a Lyapunov candidate function
V(\boldsymbol{e})=\boldsymbol{e}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{e}
, where
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and
\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}=\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}\succeq 0
, then its derivative along the trajectories of Eq. (
14
) yields:

\displaystyle{\dot{V}}(\boldsymbol{e})

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{E}\dot{\boldsymbol{e}})^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{e}+(\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{e})^{\top}(\boldsymbol{E}\dot{\boldsymbol{e}}).

For any bounded disturbances
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}
the
H_{\infty}
stability condition can be satisfied as:
{\dot{V}}(\boldsymbol{e})+\boldsymbol{e}^{\top}\boldsymbol{e}-\gamma^{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{w}^{\top}\Delta\boldsymbol{w}<0

\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}<0

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{e}\\
\Delta\boldsymbol{f}\\
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\end{bmatrix},\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{1}=\hskip-0.28436pt\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}_{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}_{c}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt\boldsymbol{I}&\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{B}_{w}\\

\boldsymbol{B}_{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}&\boldsymbol{O}&-{\gamma}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\end{bmatrix}.

, which can be written as
\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{2}\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}\leq 0

,
where

\displaystyle\hskip-0.28436pt\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}_{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}_{c}\hskip-0.28436pt+\hskip-0.28436pt\boldsymbol{I}+\epsilon\alpha^{2}\boldsymbol{I}&\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{B}_{w}\\
\boldsymbol{P}&-\epsilon\boldsymbol{I}&\boldsymbol{O}\\
\boldsymbol{B}_{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{P}&\boldsymbol{O}&-{\gamma}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0.

(16)

Finally, by rewriting

\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}_{++}

20

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{11}&*&*\\
\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}+\boldsymbol{E}^{\perp\top}\boldsymbol{Y}&-{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{I}&*\\
\boldsymbol{B_{w}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}+\boldsymbol{B_{w}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}^{\perp\top}\boldsymbol{Y}&\boldsymbol{O}&-{\gamma}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\end{bmatrix}\prec 0

(17)

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{11}
is given as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{11}=\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}+\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}^{\perp\top}\boldsymbol{Y}+\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{Y}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}^{\perp}\boldsymbol{A}-\\
\boldsymbol{C}^{\top}\boldsymbol{N}-\boldsymbol{N}^{\top}\boldsymbol{C}+\epsilon\alpha^{2}\boldsymbol{I}.\end{split}

If LMI (
17
) is solved and there exist matrices
\boldsymbol{N}
,
\boldsymbol{Y}
,
\boldsymbol{X}
, and
\gamma
and
\epsilon>0
, then the estimator gain can be retrieved as
\boldsymbol{L}=\left({\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{P}^{-1}}\right)^{\top}
.

Notice that, since
\gamma
denotes performance level, then one can minimize
\gamma
to achieve robust performance. Also, minimizing
\lVert\boldsymbol{N}\rVert_{2}
can provide estimator gain of reasonable magnitude. Furthermore, as our Lyapunov candidate function can be written as
V(e)=\boldsymbol{e}^{\top}\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{e}
then to achieve quick convergence of
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
to
\boldsymbol{x}
one can minimize the maximum eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}
which can be written as a convex SDP optimization problem:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\left(P_{1}\right)}\;\minimize_{\kappa,\boldsymbol{X}}\;\;\;

\displaystyle a_{3}\kappa\;\;\;\;\mathrm{subject\;to}\,\,\;\;\;\kappa\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}\succ 0,\kappa>0.

Hence, the necessary and sufficient condition of computing
\boldsymbol{L}
given in Eq. (
17
) can be converted to a convex semidefinite optimization problem expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\left(P_{2}\right)}\;\minimize_{\kappa,{\epsilon},\gamma,\boldsymbol{N},\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{Y}}\;\;\;

\displaystyle a_{1}\lVert\boldsymbol{N}\rVert_{2}+a_{2}\gamma+a_{3}\kappa

\displaystyle\subjectto\;\;\;

\displaystyle\kappa\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{E}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{E}\succ 0,\;\mathrm{LMI}\;\eqref{eq:LMI_Hinf},\;

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{X}\succ 0,\;\kappa>0,\;{\epsilon}>0,\;\gamma>0

where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}

\in\mathbb{R_{++}}
are predefined weighting constants. Calculating estimator gain matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
by solving
\mathbf{P_{2}}
always ensures that the performance of state error dynamics (
14
) is bounded in
H_{\infty}
sense, such that
\lVert{\boldsymbol{e}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}\leq\gamma^{2}\lVert{\Delta\boldsymbol{w}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}
. In other words, solving
\mathbf{P_{2}}
guarantees that
\lVert{\boldsymbol{e}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}
always lies in a tube of circle with center at the origin and having a radius of
\gamma^{2}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}\rVert^{2}_{L_{2}}
.

V
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we proposed a robust estimator for an interconnected model of power systems. The observer design is posed as a convex optimization and work as a one step predictor. Using a few measurements provided by PMUs the proposed estimator can provide an accurate estimate of all the states of a power system including dynamic states of PV power plants and motor loads. The presented estimator does not require any prior knowledge about the statistical properties of the uncertainty and can provide accurate estimation results as long as the uncertainty/disturbance is bounded.

The limitations of this study are twofold: First, the proposed estimator performs DSE in a centralized fashion, thus for a very big power network it needs to be extended to a decentralized framework. Second, the theory of the proposed estimator is based on continuous models, however PMUs measurements are commonly transmitted via a digitized network, thus a discrete time version of this estimator will be more appropriate. To that end, as a future work the proposed estimator will be extended to a discretized and decentralized framework.

Linear rates of asymptotic regularity for Halpern-type iterations
Horaţiu Cheval, Laurenţiu Leuştean, [2mm] LOS, Faculty of Mathematics, Computer Science, University of Bucharest

Abstract
In this note we apply a lemma due to Sabach and Shtern to compute linear rates of asymptotic regularity for Halpern-type nonlinear iterations studied in optimization and nonlinear analysis. Keywords: Rates of asymptotic regularity; Halpern iteration; Proximal point algorithm. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 47H05, 47H09, 47J25.



1
Introduction

Sabach and Shtern proved in
[
22
]
a lemma that can be used to show the linear convergence towards
0

of sequences of nonnegative reals satisfying certain conditions.
They used this lemma to get linear rates of asymptotic regularity for the sequential
averaging method (SAM), an iteration defined by Xu
[
26
]
as a viscosity approximation
of the Halpern iteration. As an immediate consequence, linear rates for the Halpern iteration
are obtained. The lemma of Sabach and Shtern has since been employed to obtain linear rates of asymptotic
regularity for the Tikhonov-Mann and modified Halpern iterations in
[
6
]
and
in
[
20
]
for the alternating Halpern-Mann iteration, introduced recently by Dinis and Pinto
[
9
]
.

The following lemma is a slight reformulation of
[
22
, Lemma 3]
, proved in
[
20
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
L>0
,
J\geq N\geq 2
,
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
(c_{n})
be a sequence bounded above by
L
, and

a_{n}=\frac{N}{\gamma(n+J)}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that
(s_{n})
is a sequence of nonnegative
reals such that
s_{0}\leq L
and, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle s_{n+1}\leq(1-\gamma a_{n+1})s_{n}+(a_{n}-a_{n+1})c_{n}.

(1)

Then, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle s_{n}\leq\frac{JL}{\gamma(n+J)}.

(2)

In this paper we show that Lemma
1.1
can be applied to compute linear
rates of asymptotic regularity for other Halpern-type nonlinear iterations studied in optimization and nonlinear analysis.

2
Preliminaries

A
W
-space is a structure
(X,d,W)
, where
(X,d)
is a metric space and

W:X\times X\times[0,1]\to X
. We think of
W(x,y,\lambda)
as an abstract convex
combination of the points
x,y\in X
. That is why we write
(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y

instead of
W(x,y,\lambda)
.

A
W
-hyperbolic space
[
15
]
is a
W
-space satisfying for all
x,y,w,z\in X
,
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{(W1)}&d(z,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\leq(1-\lambda)d(z,x)+\lambda d(z,y),\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W2)}&d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y,(1-\tilde{\lambda})x+\tilde{\lambda}y)=|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(x,y),\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W3)}&(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x,\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W4)}&d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\lambda)y+\lambda w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w).\end{array}

It is obvious that (convex subsets of) normed spaces are
W
-hyperbolic spaces. Other examples of
W
-hyperbolic spaces
are Busemann spaces (as proved in
[
1
, Proposition 2.6]
) and
CAT(0) spaces (as proved in
[
16
, pp. 386-388]
).

We usually write
X
instead of
(X,d,W)
. A nonempty subset
C
of
X
is said to be convex if
for all
x,y\in C
, we have that
(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\in C
for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a
W
-hyperbolic space. For all
x,y,w,z\in X
,
\lambda,\widetilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle d(x,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=\lambda d(x,y)\text{~and~}d(y,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=(1-\lambda)d(x,y),

(3)

\displaystyle d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\widetilde{\lambda})y+\widetilde{\lambda}w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)+\left|\lambda-\widetilde{\lambda}\right|d(y,w).

(4)

Proof.

(
3
) holds even in the more general setting of convex metric spaces, introduced
by Takahashi
[
25
]
as
W
-spaces satisfying (W1). For the proof of (
4
) see

[
7
, Lemma 2.1(iv)]
.
∎

Asymptotic regularity is a very important notion in nonlinear analysis and optimization that was defined by
Browder and Petryshyn
[
5
]
for the Picard iteration of a nonexpansive mapping and extended, subsequently, by
Borwein, Reich and Shafrir
[
4
]
to general nonlinear iterations. It turns out that
two notions of asymptotic regularity for a sequence
(a_{n})
in a metric space
(X,d)
arise naturally.
Firstly, we say that
(a_{n})
is asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},a_{n+1})=0
.
If, furthermore,
C
is a nonempty subset of
X
and
T:C\to C
is a mapping, then
(a_{n})
is said to be

T
-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},Ta_{n})=0
.

If
(a_{n})
is asymptotically regular, then a rate of convergence of
(d(a_{n},a_{n+1}))
towards
0

is called a rate of asymptotic regularity of
(a_{n})
. Similarly, if
(a_{n})
is

T
-asymptotically regular, then a rate of convergence of
(d(a_{n},Ta_{n}))
towards
0
is
called a rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity of
(a_{n})
.

As in
[
19
]
, we can extend the notion of
T
-asymptotic regularity to countable families of mappings. Thus,
if
(T_{n}:C\to C)
is a countable family, then we say that
(x_{n})
is
(T_{n})
-asymptotically regular with rate
\varphi
if

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})=0
with rate of convergence
\varphi
.

Recall that if
(b_{n})
is a sequence of nonnegative reals such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}b_{n}=0
, then
a rate of convergence of
(b_{n})
(towards
0
) is a function
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall n\geq\varphi(k)\left(b_{n}\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

We denote by
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
the set
{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
of positive natural numbers.

3
Linear rates of (
T
-)asymptotic regularity

In the sequel, if not otherwise stated,
X
is a
W
-hyperbolic space,

C
is a convex subset of
X
, and
T:C\to C
is a nonexpansive mapping, that is

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in C
. We denote by
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
the set of fixed points of
T
and we assume that
T
has fixed points,
i.e.
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\neq\emptyset
.

3.1
Halpern iteration

Let us recall that the well-known Halpern iteration is defined as follows:

x_{0}=x,\quad x_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n})u+\alpha_{n}Tx_{n},

(5)

where
x,u\in C
and
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
.

In
[
6
]
, the authors and Kohlenbach computed linear rates of (
T-
)asymptotic regularity,
for a special choice of the parameter sequences,
for the modified Halpern iteration, a generalization of the Halpern iteration.
One gets immediately linear rates for this iteration too.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha_{0}=0
and
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{n+1}
for
n\geq 1
. Define

\Phi(k)=8M(k+1)-1\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi^{*}(k)=16M(k+1)-1,

(6)

where
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is such that
M\geq\max\{d(x,p),d(u,p)\}
for some fixed point
p
of
T
.

Then
\Phi
is a rate of asymptotic regularity for
(x_{n})
and
\Phi^{*}
is a rate of
T
-asymptotic
regularity for
(x_{n})
.

Proof.

Apply
[
6
, Proposition 4.7]
with
\lambda_{n}=1
and
\beta_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}
.
∎

Remark that if
C
is a bounded set, then one can take
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
to be an upper bound on the diameter of
C
.

For Hilbert spaces,
u=x
and
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{1}{n+2}
, Lieder
[
21
]
proved the
following inequality: for all
n\geq 1
and all fixed points
p
of
T
,

\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\leq\frac{2\|x_{0}-p\|}{n+1}.

(7)

Furthermore, Lieder showed that the bound in (
7
) is tight. As a consequence, one
gets that
\Phi_{0}(k)=2M(k+1)-1
, where
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is such that
M\geq\|x_{0}-p\|
, is a
rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity that improves by a factor of
8
the one for
W
-hyperbolic spaces.

In a recent paper
[
12
]
, He et al. defined the following Halpern iteration with adaptive anchoring parameters:

\displaystyle x_{0}\in X,\quad x_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\varphi_{n+1}+1}x_{0}+\frac{\varphi_{n+1}}{\varphi_{n+1}+1}Tx_{n},

(8)

where
X
is a Hilbert space,
T:X\to X
is nonexpansive with
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\neq\emptyset
, and, for every
n\geq 1
,

\varphi_{n}=\dfrac{2\langle x_{n-1}-Tx_{n-1},x_{0}-x_{n-1}\rangle}{\|x_{n-1}-Tx_{n-1}\|^{2}}+1.

They proved (see
[
12
, Lemma 3.1.(i)]
and
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
) that for
n\geq 1
and any fixed point
p
of
T
,

\varphi_{n}\geq n\quad\text{and}\quad\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|\leq\frac{2\|x_{0}-p\|}{\varphi_{n}+1},

(9)

that is in general an impovement of (
7
). Furthermore,
[
12
, Remark 3.3]
provides an example showing that the rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity given by (
9
) is better than the one from
[
21
]
.

As the definition of
\varphi_{n}
uses essentially the Hilbert space structure, the proof of (
9
) from
[
12
]
cannot be generalized to Banach spaces or to
W
-hyperbolic spaces. Furthermore, the proof does not use Sabach and Shtern’s lemma. A problem for future research is to find an adaptive selection method for
\varphi_{n}
that can be expressed in a more general setting (Banach spaces, classes of
W
-spaces) and to compute rates of
T
-asymptotic regularity for the corresponding adaptive Halpern iteration having a similar form with the ones from
[
12
]
.

3.2
Sequential averaging method

Xu
[
26
]
introduced, in the setting of Banach spaces, a viscosity version of the Halpern
iteration that was called the sequential averaging method (SAM) by Sabach and Shtern
[
22
]
,
who studied it in Hilbert spaces. Sabach and Shtern obtained, as an application of
[
22
, Lemma 3]
,
linear rates of asymptotic regularity for SAM. In what follows, we show that we can obtain such linear rates in the much more general setting
of
W
-hyperbolic spaces, too.

Let
\rho\in[0,1)
and
f:C\to C
be a
\rho
-contraction, that is for all

x,y\in C
,
d(f(x),f(y))\leq\rho d(x,y)
.
The sequential averaging method (SAM) is defined by

x_{0}=x,\qquad x_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n})f(x_{n})+\alpha_{n}Tx_{n},

(10)

where
x\in C
and
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
.

The following lemma extends to
W
-hyperbolic spaces results obtained for Banach spaces in
[
26
, proof of Theorem 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-(1-\rho)(1-\alpha_{n+1}))d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n}),

(11)

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n}).

(12)

(ii)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any fixed point
p
of
T
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq M,

(13)

\displaystyle d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 2M,

(14)

where
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is such that
M\geq\max\left\{d(x,p),\frac{d(p,f(p))}{1-\rho}\right\}
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We have, by (
4
), that

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{n+1})d(f(x_{n+1}),f(x_{n}))+\alpha_{n+1}d(Tx_{n+1},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n}).

As
f
is a
\rho
-contraction and
T
is nonexpansive, we get (
11
).
Moreover,

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+1},Tx_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dxlambday}}}{{=}}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n}).

(ii)

We prove (
13
) by induction on
n
. The case
n=0
is obvious.

n\Rightarrow n+1
:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},p)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(x_{n}),p)+\alpha_{n}d(Tx_{n},p)\quad\text{by (W1)}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(x_{n}),f(p))+(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(p),p)+\alpha_{n}d(x_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{n})\rho d(x_{n},p)+\alpha_{n}d(x_{n},p)+(1-\alpha_{n})d(f(p),p)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{n})\rho M+\alpha_{n}M+(1-\alpha_{n})(1-\rho)M=M,

by the definition of
M
and the induction hypothesis. Furthermore, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(f(x_{n}),f(p))+d(f(p),p)+d(p,Tx_{n})\leq\rho d(x_{n},p)+d(f(p),p)+d(x_{n},p)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{xnp-SAM}}}{{\leq}}(1+\rho)M+d(f(p),p)\leq 2M.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{(1-\rho)(n+J)}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
J=2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil
.
Then for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2MJ}{(1-\rho)(n+J)},

(15)

\displaystyle d(Tx_{n},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M(J+2)}{(1-\rho)(n+J)},

(16)

where
M
is as in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle L=2M,\quad N=2,\quad\gamma=1-\rho,\quad s_{n}=d(x_{n+1},x_{n}),\quad a_{n}=1-\alpha_{n},\quad c_{n}=d(f(x_{n}),Tx_{n}).

One can easily see that the assumptions of Lemma
1.1
are met, so we can apply it
to get (
15
).

(
16
) follows easily:

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+2M(1-\alpha_{n})\quad\text{by~}\eqref{main-ineq-SAM-2}\text{~and~}\eqref{fxn-Txn-SAM}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M(J+2)}{(1-\rho)(n+J)},

by (
15
) and the definition of
(\alpha_{n})
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.3
, we get, for
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{(1-\rho)(n+J)}
,
linear rates of (
T
-)asymptotic regularity. Thus,

(x_{n})
is asymptotically regular with rate

\Phi(k)=4M\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil^{2}(k+1)-2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil

(17)

and
T
-asymptotically regular with rate

\Phi^{*}(k)=\left(4M\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil^{2}+4M\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil\right)(k+1)-2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\rho}\right\rceil.

(18)

Let
u\in C
and take
f:C\to C,\,f(x)=u
. Then
\rho=0
,
J=2
,
M\geq\max\{d(x_{0},p),d(u,p)\}
.
Furthermore,
(x_{n})
becomes the Halpern iteration. We get the following:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{n+2}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and define

\Phi_{0}(k)=4M(k+1)-2,\qquad\Phi^{*}_{0}(k)=8M(k+1)-2.

(19)

Then
\Phi_{0}
is
a rate of asymptotic regularity and
\Phi^{*}_{0}
is a rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity for the Halpern iteration.

These rates are better by a factor of
2
than the ones obtained in Proposition
3.1
.

Very recently
[
28
]
, Zaslavski extended the above results by weakening the hypothesis that
T
has fixed points to
T
having approximate fixed points in a bounded neighborhood of
x
, as guaranteed by results in proof mining (see, for example
[
11
,
16
]
), or that
T
has bounded orbits.

3.3
Alternative iterative method

Let
(\alpha_{n})
be a sequence in
[0,1]
and
f:C\to C
be a
\rho
-contraction for some
\rho\in[0,1)
.
The alternative iterative method
(x_{n})
is defined as follows:

x_{0}=x\in C,\qquad x_{n+1}=T((1-\alpha_{n})f(x_{n})+\alpha_{n}x_{n}).

(20)

This iteration was studied in
[
8
]
in the setting of Banach spaces.
By letting
f(x)=u\in C
in (
20
), one obtains, as a particular case, the alternative regularization method, introduced by Xu
[
27
]
.
The second author and Nicolae remarked in
[
17
]
that results on the asymptotic
regularity and the strong convergence of the alternative regularization method can be immediately
obtained from the ones for the Halpern iteration.

The next lemma extends in a straightforward way
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
from normed spaces to
W
-hyperbolic
spaces and is very similar to Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(i)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-(1-\rho)(1-\alpha_{n+1}))d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+\left|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\right|d(x_{n},f(x_{n})),

(21)

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})d(x_{n},f(x_{n})).

(22)

(ii)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any fixed point
p
of
T
,

d(x_{n},p)\leq M
and
d(x_{n},f(x_{n}))\leq 2M
,

with
M
defined as in Lemma
3.2
.(
ii
).

One can easily see, by using the previous lemma, that Proposition
3.3
holds for the alternative iterative method, too.
Furthermore, one gets for the alternative iterative method exactly the same rates of
(T-)
asymptotic regularity as for SAM.

3.4
Halpern-type abstract proximal point algorithm

Let
(T_{n}:X\to X)
be a family of self-mappings of
X
that have common fixed points.
Hence, if we denote by
F
the set
\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n})
, then
F\neq\emptyset
.

The
Halpern-type abstract proximal point algorithm
was defined by Sipoş
[
23
]
as follows:

\displaystyle x_{0}=x,\qquad

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n})u+\alpha_{n}T_{n}x_{n},

(23)

where
x,u\in C
and
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
.

Throughout this section,
p\in F
and
M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is such that
M\geq\max\left\{d(x,p),d(u,p)\right\}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
d(x_{n},p)\leq M
.

Proof.

An easy induction on
n
.
∎

From now on we assume that
(\gamma_{n})
is a sequence of positive reals such that for all
y\in X
and
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(T_{n}y,T_{m}y)\leq\frac{\left|\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{m}\right|}{\gamma_{n}}d(y,T_{n}y).

(24)

This is condition (C1) from
[
18
]
, used to obtain abstract versions of the proximal point algorithm.
As proved in
[
18
, Proposition 3.10]
, (
24
) holds if
X
is a CAT(0) space and the family
(T_{n})
is
jointly
(P_{2})
with respect to
(\gamma_{n})
, a notion introduced in
[
18
]
as a generalization of
the so-called
(P_{2})
property
[
2
]
. Families of mappings
(T_{n})
that are jointly
firmly nonexpansive with respect to
(\gamma_{n})
are also defined and studied in
[
18
]
as an extension of the definition of
a firmly nonexpansive mapping in
W
-hyperbolic spaces, given in
[
1
]
.
Jointly firmly nonexpansive families of mappings are jointly
(P_{2})
with respect to
(\gamma_{n})
, hence they also satisfy (
24
) in CAT(0) spaces.
We refer to
[
18
,
24
]
for more information about these families of mappings.

The following result establishes the essential inequality that allows us to apply Lemma
1.1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+\frac{2M\alpha_{n+1}\left|\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}\right|}{\gamma_{n}}+2M\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|.

(25)

Proof.

We have that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d((1-\alpha_{n+1})u+\alpha_{n+1}T_{n+1}x_{n+1},(1-\alpha_{n})u+\alpha_{n}T_{n}x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n+1}d(T_{n+1}x_{n+1},T_{n}x_{n})+\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|d(u,T_{n}x_{n})\quad\text{by \eqref{prop:arbitrary-w-ineq}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n+1}d(T_{n+1}x_{n+1},T_{n+1}x_{n})+\alpha_{n+1}d(T_{n+1}x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})+\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|d(u,T_{n}x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+\frac{\alpha_{n+1}\left|\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}\right|}{\gamma_{n}}d(x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})+\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|d(u,T_{n}x_{n}),

by (
24
) and the nonexpansiveness of
T_{n+1}
. Apply Lemma
3.6

to obtain that

3.5
Halpern-type proximal point algorithm

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
A:X\to 2^{X}
be a maximally monotone operator such that the set

\operatorname{zer}(A)=\{x\in X\mid 0\in Ax\}
of zeros of
A
is nonempty. Recall that for any
\gamma>0
,
the resolvent of order
\gamma
of
A
is defined by
J_{\gamma A}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma A)^{-1}
,
where
\operatorname{Id}
is the identity mapping of
X
. As
A
is maximally monotone,
J_{\gamma A}:X\to X
is
a single-valued firmly nonexpansive (in particular, nonexpansive) mapping such that

\operatorname{Fix}(J_{\gamma A})=\operatorname{zer}(A)
for
every
\gamma>0
. We refer to
[
3
]
for details.

The
Halpern-type proximal point algorithm
was introduced by Kamimura and Takahashi
[
14
]
as a combination
between the Halpern iteration and the proximal point algorithm:

\displaystyle x_{0}=x,\qquad

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n})u+\alpha_{n}J_{\gamma_{n}A}x_{n},

(29)

where
x,u\in C
,
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
, and
(\gamma_{n})
a sequence in
(0,\infty)
. A generalization of the iteration
(
29
) to
m
-accretive operators in Banach spaces was studied by Dominguez-Benavides, López-Acedo, and Xu
[
10
]
.

If
\alpha_{n}=1
, then
(x_{n})
becomes the proximal point algorithm, while if
\gamma_{n}=\gamma
is a constant sequence, then

(x_{n})
is the Halpern iteration with
T=J_{\gamma A}
.

By
[
18
, Proposition 3.21]
, the family
(J_{\gamma_{n}A})
is jointly
firmly nonexpansive with respect to
(\gamma_{n})
, hence (
24
) holds with
T_{n}=J_{\gamma_{n}A}
. Furthermore,

\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{Fix}(J_{\gamma_{n}A})=\operatorname{zer}(A)\neq\emptyset
.

It follows that we can apply Proposition
3.10
with
T_{n}=J_{\gamma_{n}A}
to
get, for
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{n+2}
and
\gamma_{n}=\frac{n+2}{n+1}
, that
(x_{n})
is (
(T_{n})
,
T_{m}
)-asymptotically
regular with linear rates given by (
28
). This is a significant
improvement of the polynomial rates obtained by the second author and Pinto
in
[
19
, Proposition 9]
for another choice of the parameter sequences.

3.6
Minimizers of proper convex lsc functions

Let
X
be a complete CAT(0) space and
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper convex
lower semicontinuous (lsc) function that has minimizers, that is the set

\operatorname{Argmin}(f)=\{x\in X\mid f(x)\leq f(y)\text{~for all~}y\in X\}
of minimizers of
f
is nonempty.
For any
\gamma>0
, the resolvent
J_{\gamma f}
of
f
of order
\gamma

was defined in a nonlinear setting by Jost
[
13
]
:

J_{\gamma f}:X\to X,\quad J_{\gamma f}(x)=\operatorname{argmin}_{y\in X}\left(f(y)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}d^{2}(x,y)\right).

Jost proved in
[
13
]
that for all
\gamma>0
,
J_{\gamma f}
is nonexpansive and

\operatorname{Fix}(J_{\gamma f})=\operatorname{Argmin}(f)
. In fact,
J_{\gamma f}
is even firmly nonexpansive, as pointed
out in
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
.

We have that
\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{Fix}(J_{\gamma_{n}f})=\operatorname{Argmin}(f)\neq\emptyset
and that
(
24
) holds with
T_{n}=J_{\gamma_{n}f}
, since the family
(J_{\gamma_{n}f})
is jointly
firmly nonexpansive with respect to
(\gamma_{n})
, as proved in
[
18
, Proposition 3.17]
.

It follows that we can apply Proposition
3.10
with
T_{n}=J_{\gamma_{n}f}
to
get linear rates for
\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{2}{n+2}
and
\gamma_{n}=\frac{n+2}{n+1}
.

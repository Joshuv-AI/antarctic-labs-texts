Ensemble minimaxity of James-Stein estimators
Yuzo Maruyama, Lawrence D. Brown, Edward I. George

Abstract
This article discusses estimation of a multivariate normal mean based on heteroscedastic observations. Under heteroscedasticity, estimators shrinking more on the coordinates with larger variances, seem desirable. Although they are not necessarily minimax in the ordinary sense, we show that such James-Stein type estimators can be ensemble minimax , minimax with respect to the ensemble risk, related to empirical Bayes perspective of Efron and Morris.

Let us assume

\sigma_{1}^{2}>\sigma^{2}_{2}>\dots>\sigma^{2}_{p}.

We are interested in the estimation of
\bm{\theta}

with respect to the ordinary squared error loss function

(1.2)

where the risk of an estimator
\bm{\delta}(\bm{X})

.
The MLE
\bm{X}
with constant risk

.

In the homoscedastic case
\sigma_{1}^{2}=\dots=\sigma^{2}_{p}
,

showed that

\left(1-\frac{c}{\bm{X}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{X}}\right)\bm{X}\text{ for }c\in\left(0,2(p-2)\right)

(1.3)

dominates the MLE
\bm{X}
for
p\geq 3
.

shrinkage estimators under heteroscedasticity.

Brown 1975

showed that the James-Stein estimator (
1.3
) is not necessarily minimax
when the variances are not equal.
Specifically, it is not minimax for any

when

\left(\bm{I}-\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\frac{c}{\bm{X}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-2}\bm{X}}\right)\bm{X}\text{ for }c\in\left(0,2(p-2)\right)

(1.4)

and any
\bm{\Sigma}
.
However,

)

Ordinary minimax estimators, as in (

),
typically shrink most on the coordinates with smaller variances.
From

one of the most natural Jame-Stein variants is

\left(\bm{I}-\bm{\Sigma}\frac{c}{\|\bm{X}\|^{2}}\right)\bm{X}\text{ for }c>0,

(1.5)

which we are going to rescue, by providing some minimax properties related to Bayesian viewpoint.

In many applications,
\theta_{i}
are thought to follow some exchangeable prior
distribution
\pi
. It is then natural to consider the compound risk function
which is then the Bayes risk with respect to the prior
\pi

\displaystyle\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\pi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}R(\bm{\theta},\bm{\delta})\pi(\mathrm{d}\bm{\theta}).

(1.6)

Efron and Morris 1971
;
Efron and

;

;
Efron and

In particular, they considered a prior distribution

By introducing a set of ensemble risks

\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}R(\bm{\delta},\bm{\theta})\frac{1}{(2\pi\tau)^{p/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{\theta},

(1.7)

\mathcal{P}_{\star}=\{N_{p}(\bm{0},\tau\bm{I}_{p}):\tau\in(0,\infty)\},

(1.8)

that is, an estimator
\bm{\delta}
is said to be ensemble minimax with respect to

if

\sup_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)=\inf_{\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\sup_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta}^{\prime},\tau).

(1.9)

As a matter of fact, the second author in his unpublished manuscript,

as well as the generalized Bayes estimator with respect to

2
Minimaxity, Ensemble Minimaxity and Casella’s viewpoint

If the prior
\pi(\bm{\theta})
were known,
the resulting posterior mean
E[\bm{\theta}\,|\,\bm{x}]
would then be the
optimal estimate under the sum of the squared error loss.
However, it is typically not feasible to exactly specify the prior.
One approach to avoid excessive dependence on the choice of prior,
is to consider a set of priors
\mathcal{P}
on
\Theta
and
study the properties of estimators based on the corresponding set
of ensemble risks.
As in classical decision theory, there rarely exists an
estimator that achieves the minimum ensemble risk uniformly for all
\pi\in\mathcal{P}
.
A more realistic goal as pursued in this paper is to study the ensemble
minimaxity of James-Stein type estimators.

Recall that with ordinary risk
R(\bm{\delta},\bm{\theta})
,

\bm{\delta}
is said to be minimax if

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta}R(\bm{\delta},\bm{\theta})=\inf_{\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\sup_{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta}R(\bm{\delta}^{\prime},\bm{\theta}).

(2.1)

Similarly for the case of ensemble risk we have the following definition.
Note the Bayes risk of
\bm{\delta}
under the prior
\pi
is given by (
1.6
).
The estimator
\bm{\delta}
is said to be ensemble minimax with respect to
\mathcal{P}
if

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\pi)=\inf_{\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta}^{\prime},\pi).

(2.2)

The motivation for the above definitions comes from the use of the empirical Bayes method
in simultaneous inference.
Efron and
Morris 1972b
,
derived the James-Stein estimator through the parametric empirical Bayes model with

\bm{\theta}\sim N_{p}(\bm{0},\tau\bm{I}_{p})
.
Note that in such an empirical Bayes model,
\tau
is the unknown non-random parameter.
Given the family
\mathcal{P}_{\star}=\{N_{p}(\bm{0},\tau\bm{I}_{p}):\tau\in(0,\infty)\}
,
the Bayes risk is a function of
\tau
as follows,

\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}R(\bm{\delta},\bm{\theta})\frac{1}{(2\pi\tau)^{p/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{\theta}.

(2.3)

Hence, with
\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)
, the estimator
\bm{\delta}
is said to be ensemble minimax with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{\star}
if

\sup_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)=\inf_{\bm{\delta}^{\prime}}\sup_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\bar{R}(\bm{\delta}^{\prime},\tau),

(2.4)

which may be seen as the counterpart of ordinary minimaxity in the empirical Bayes model.

Clearly the usual estimator
\bm{X}
has constant risk, has constant Bayes risk and hence

\bm{X}
is ensemble minimax. Then the ensemble minimaxity of
\bm{\delta}
follows if

\displaystyle\bar{R}(\bm{\delta},\tau)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sigma^{2}_{i},\ \forall\tau\in(0,\infty).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
\phi(z)
is non-negative, non-decreasing and concave.
Also
\phi(z)/z
is assumed non-increasing.
Then

\bm{\delta}_{\phi}=\left(\bm{I}-\bm{G}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right)\bm{x},\ \mbox{ for }z=\bm{x}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{x}=\sum\frac{g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}

is ensemble minimax if

\phi(p\min_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\leq 2(p-2)\frac{\min_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}{\max_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})},\quad\forall\tau\in(0,\infty).

(3.1)

Proof.

Recall, for
i=1,\dots,p
,

\displaystyle x_{i}\,|\,\theta_{i}\sim N(\theta_{i},\sigma^{2}_{i}),\text{ and }\theta_{i}\sim N(0,\tau).

Then the posterior and marginal are given by

\displaystyle\theta_{i}\,|\,x_{i}\sim N\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}x_{i},\frac{\tau\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\right)\text{ and }x_{i}\sim N(0,\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}),

respectively, where
\theta_{1}\,|\,x_{1},\dots,\theta_{p}\,|\,x_{p}
are mutually independent and

x_{1},\dots,x_{p}
are mutually independent. Then the Bayes risk is given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}(\bm{\delta}_{\phi},\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{\theta}}E_{\bm{x}\,|\,\bm{\theta}}\left[\left\{\left(1-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right)x_{i}-\theta_{i}\right\}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{x}}E_{\bm{\theta}\,|\,\bm{x}}\left[\left\{\left(1-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right)x_{i}-\theta_{i}\right\}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{x}}E_{\bm{\theta}\,|\,\bm{x}}\left[\left\{\left(1-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right)x_{i}-E[\theta_{i}\,|\,x_{i}]+E[\theta_{i}\,|\,x_{i}]-\theta_{i}\right\}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{x}}\left[\left\{\left(1-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right)x_{i}-E[\theta_{i}\,|\,x_{i}]\right\}^{2}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mathrm{Var}(\theta_{i}\,|\,x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{x}}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}x_{i}-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}x_{i}\right)^{2}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\tau\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}.

Since the first term of the r.h.s. of the above equality is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{\bm{x}}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}x_{i}-g_{i}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}x_{i}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\right)^{2}E_{\bm{x}}[x_{i}^{2}]-2E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right]+E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{4}_{i}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}-2E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right]+E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right],

we have

\bar{R}(\bm{\delta}_{\phi},\tau)-\sum\sigma^{2}_{i}=-2E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right]+E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right].

(3.2)

Let

\displaystyle w_{i}=\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau},\ w=\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{i}\ \text{ and }\ t_{i}=\frac{w_{i}}{w}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,p.

Then

\displaystyle w\sim\chi^{2}_{p},\quad\bm{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(1/2,\dots,1/2),

and
w
and
\bm{t}
are mutually independent.
With the notation, we have

\displaystyle x_{i}^{2}=wt_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)\text{ and }z=\bm{x}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}=w\sum_{i=1}^{p}t_{i}g_{i}\left(1+\frac{\tau}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}\right)

and hence

\displaystyle E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=E_{w,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g^{2}_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\frac{\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))^{2}}{w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right]

\displaystyle=E_{\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g^{2}_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))^{2}}{w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right]\right].

Since
\phi(w)/w
is non-increasing and
\phi(w)
is non-decreasing,
by the correlation inequality, we have

\displaystyle E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))^{2}}{w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]E_{w}\left[\frac{\phi(w\min g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{w\min g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right],

and hence

\begin{split}&E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right]\leq E_{w}\left[\frac{\phi(w\min g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{w\min g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\times E_{\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g^{2}_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]\right].\end{split}

(3.3)

In the first part of the r.h.s. of the inequality (
3.3
), we have

\begin{split}E_{w}\left[\frac{\phi(w\min g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{w}\right]&\leq E_{w}[1/w]E_{w}\left[\phi(w\min_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]\\
&\leq\frac{\phi(E_{w}\left[w\right]\min_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{p-2}\\
&=\frac{\phi(p\min_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))}{p-2},\end{split}

(3.4)

where the first and second inequality follow from the correlation inequality and Jensen’s inequality, respectively.
In the second part of the r.h.s. of the inequality (
3.3
),

we have

\begin{split}&E_{\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g^{2}_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}E_{w\,|\,\bm{t}}\left[\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]\right]\\
&=E_{w,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g^{2}_{i}(\sigma^{2}_{i}+\tau)}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]\\
&\leq\max_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma_{i}^{2})E_{w,\bm{t}}\left[\frac{\sum t_{i}g_{i}\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i})}\phi(w\sum t_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma^{2}_{i}))\right]\\
&=\max_{i}g_{i}(1+\tau/\sigma_{i}^{2})E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum\frac{\sigma^{2}_{i}g_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{\tau+\sigma^{2}_{i}}\frac{\phi(z)}{z}\right].\end{split}

(3.5)

By (

) and (

\begin{split}&E_{\bm{x}}\left[\sum g^{2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\phi^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right]\\

(3.6)

and, by (

is ensemble minimax if

\phi(p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1})\leq 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau}{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau}\quad,\forall\tau\in(0,\infty).

(3.7)

3.2
An ensemble minimax James-Stein variant

As an example of Corollary
3.1
, we consider

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{c_{1}z}{c_{2}+z}

(3.8)

for
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}\geq 0
,
which is motivated by
Stein 1956
and
James and Stein 1961
.
Under
\bm{\Sigma}=\bm{I}_{p}
,
Stein 1956
suggested that
there exist estimators dominating the usual estimator
\bm{x}

among a class of estimators
\bm{\delta}_{\phi}
with
\phi
given by (
3.8
)
for small
c_{1}
and large
c_{2}
.
Following
Stein 1956
,
James and Stein 1961
showed that

\bm{\delta}_{\phi}
with
0<c_{1}<2(p-2)
and
c_{2}=0
is ordinary minimax.
The choice
c_{2}=0
is, however, not good since, by Corollary
3.1
,
c_{1}
cannot be larger than

2(p-2)\sigma^{2}_{p}/\sigma^{2}_{1}
. With positive
c_{2}
, we can see that
c_{1}
can be much larger as follows.

Note that
\phi(z)
given by (
3.8
)
is non-negative, increasing and concave and that
\phi(z)/z
is decreasing.
Then the sufficient condition in (
3.7
)
is

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1}}{c_{2}+p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1}}\leq 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau}{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau}\quad\forall\tau\in(0,\infty),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2(p-2)\left\{\sigma^{2}_{1}c_{2}+p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)\right\}-c_{1}p(\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau)\geq 0\quad\forall\tau\in(0,\infty)

or

\displaystyle p\tau\left\{2(p-2)-c_{1}\right\}+2(p-2)\sigma^{2}_{1}\left\{c_{2}-p\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2(p-2)}-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}}{\sigma^{2}_{1}}\right)\right\}\geq 0\quad\forall\tau\in(0,\infty).

Hence we have a following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

1.

When

0<c_{1}\leq 2(p-2)\text{ and }c_{2}\geq\max\left(0,p\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2(p-2)}-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}}{\sigma^{2}_{1}}\right)\right),

(3.9)

the shrinkage estimator

\displaystyle\left(\bm{I}-\bm{\Sigma}\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}\sigma^{2}_{1}+\|\bm{x}\|^{2}}\right)\bm{x}

is ensemble minimax.

2.

It is ordinary minimax if

2\left(\sum\sigma_{i}^{4}/\sigma_{1}^{4}-2\right)\geq c_{1}.

Part
2
above follows from Theorem
A.1
.

It seems to us that one of the most interesting estimators with ensemble minimaxity
from Part
1

is

\left(\bm{I}-\bm{\Sigma}\frac{p-2}{(p-2)\sigma^{2}_{1}+\|\bm{x}\|^{2}}\right)\bm{x}

(3.10)

with the choice
c_{1}=c_{2}=p-2
satisfying (
3.9
).
It is clear that the
i
-th shrinkage factor

\displaystyle 1-\frac{(p-2)\sigma^{2}_{i}}{(p-2)\sigma^{2}_{1}+\|\bm{x}\|^{2}}

is nonnegative for any
\bm{x}
and any
\bm{\Sigma}
, which is a nice property.

3.3
A generalized Bayes ensemble minimax estimator

In this subsection, we provide a generalized Bayes ensemble minimax estimator. Following
Strawderman 1971
,
Berger 1976
and

Maruyama and
Strawderman 2005
,
we consider the generalized harmonic prior

\bm{\theta}\,|\,\lambda\sim N_{p}(\bm{0},\lambda^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{G}^{-1}-\bm{\Sigma}),\ \pi(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{-2}I_{(0,1)}(\lambda)

(3.11)

where
\bm{G}=\mbox{diag}(g_{1},\dots,g_{p})
satisfies
0<\forall g_{i}\leq 1
.
Note that for
\bm{\Sigma}=\bm{G}=\bm{I}_{p}
, the density of
\bm{\theta}
is exactly
\pi(\bm{\theta})=\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2-p}
,
since
\lambda^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{G}^{-1}-\bm{\Sigma}=\{(1-\lambda)/\lambda\}\bm{I}_{p}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p/2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)^{p/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2}}{2(1-\lambda)}\right)\lambda^{-2}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{p/2-2}\exp\left(-g\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2}/2\right)\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(p/2-1)2^{p/2-1}}{(2\pi)^{p/2}}\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2-p}.

The prior
\pi(\bm{\theta})=\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2-p}
is called the harmonic prior and
was originally investigated by
Baranchik 1964
and
Stein 1974
.

Berger 1980
and
Berger and
Strawderman 1996
recommended the use of the prior
(
3.11
) mainly because it is on the boundary of admissibility.

By the way of
Strawderman 1971
,
the generalized Bayes estimator with respect to the prior is given by

\bm{\delta}_{*}=\left(\bm{I}-\bm{G}\frac{\phi_{*}(z)}{z}\right)\bm{x},\ \mbox{ for }z=\bm{x}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{x}

(3.12)

with

\phi_{*}(z)=z\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{p/2-1}\exp(-z\lambda/2)\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{p/2-2}\exp(-z\lambda/2)\mathrm{d}\lambda},

where
\phi_{*}(z)
satisfies the following properties

H1

\phi_{*}(z)
is increasing in
z
.

H2

\phi_{*}(z)
is concave.

H3

\lim_{z\to\infty}\phi_{*}(z)=p-2
.

H4

\phi_{*}(z)/z
is decreasing in
z
.

H5

The derivative of
\phi_{*}(z)
at
z=0
is
(p-2)/p
.

Under the choice

\bm{G}=\bm{\Sigma}/\sigma_{1}^{2}
and with the condition of
Corollary
3.1
, we have a following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

1.

The estimator
\bm{\delta}_{*}
is ensemble minimax.

2.

The estimator
\bm{\delta}_{*}
is ordinary minimax when

2\left(\sum\sigma_{i}^{4}/\sigma_{1}^{4}-2\right)\geq p-2.

3.

The estimator
\bm{\delta}_{*}
is conventional admissible.

Proof.

[Part
1
]
Recall that the sufficient condition for ensemble minimaxity is given by
Corollary
3.1
.
By
H1
–
H5
, we have only to check (
3.7
) in Corollary
3.1
.

For
\tau\geq\max(0,\sigma^{2}_{1}-2\sigma^{2}_{p})
, we have

\displaystyle 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma_{p}^{2}+\tau}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\tau}\geq p-2.

By the properties
H1
and
H3
,

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1})\leq p-2.

for
\tau\in(0,\infty)
. Hence for
\tau\geq\max(0,\sigma^{2}_{1}-2\sigma^{2}_{p})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1})\leq 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma_{p}^{2}+\tau}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\tau}.

So it suffices to show

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1})\leq 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma_{p}^{2}+\tau}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\tau}

when
\sigma^{2}_{1}-2\sigma^{2}_{p}>0
and
0<\tau<\sigma^{2}_{1}-2\sigma^{2}_{p}
.
By the properties
H2
and
H5
, we have
\phi_{*}(z)\leq\{(p-2)/p\}z
for all
z\geq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau}{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau}-\phi_{*}(p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)/\sigma^{2}_{1})

\displaystyle\geq 2(p-2)\frac{\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau}{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{p(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)}{\sigma^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle=(p-2)(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)\left(\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau}-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2)(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)}{\sigma^{2}_{1}(\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau)}\left(\sigma^{2}_{1}-\tau\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p-2)(\sigma^{2}_{p}+\tau)}{\sigma^{2}_{1}(\sigma^{2}_{1}+\tau)}2\sigma^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

which completes the proof.

[Part
2
] It follows from Theorem
A.1
in Appendix
A
.

[Part
3
] It follows from Theorem 6.4.2 of
Brown 1971
.
∎

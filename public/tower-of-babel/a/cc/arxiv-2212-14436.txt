Fractional dimension related to badly approximable matrices associated with higher successive minima
Hao Xing

Abstract
In this article we introduce the notion of badly approximable matrices of higher order using higher sucessive minima in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We will prove that for order less than d d , they have Lebesgue measure zero and the gaps between them still have full Hausdorff dimension.

Introduction

Throughout, all norms of vectors in an Euclidean space without specification are assumed to be the maximum Euclidean norm.

1.1
Dirichlet’s Theorem and Diophantine Approximation

Diophantine approximation is a branch of number theory studying the approximation of real numbers by rational numbers. The first known result of this kind is due to Adrien-Marie Legendre, which can be proved by the Pigeonhole Principle:

Theorem 1.1

(Legendre, 1808)
.

For any real number
x
and every
Q>1
, there exists an integer vector
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

such that

|xq-p|<\frac{1}{Q}\text{ and }0<q<Q.

The Classical multidimensional version of approximation theorem, Due to Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet, states that

Theorem 1.2

(Dirichlet’s approximation theorem
[
Dir42
]
, 1842)
.

For every real
m\times n
matrix
A
and every

Q>1
, there exists an integer vector

\textbf{p}\in Z^{m}
and
\textbf{q}\in Z^{n}
such that

\|A\textbf{q}-\textbf{p}\|<\frac{1}{Q^{\frac{n}{m}}}\text{ and }0<\textbf{q}<Q.

The modern proof of Dirichlet’s approximation theorem often uses a generalized version of ‘‘Pigeonhole Principle” for lattices in Euclidean spaces, called the (first) convex body theorem, due to Hermann Minkowski, applied to an appropriately chosen convex set

To further investigate the sets with various Diophantine properties, we will look into their fractional dimensions. In order to compute the Hausdorff dimension of badly approximable numbers and matrices, Schmidt invented the following topological game
[
Sch66
]

[

]
called

Choose two parameters

and
0<\beta<1
. Two players, called Alice and Bob, will play the following game:

•

First Bob choose a closed ball

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

•

Then Alice choose closed ball
A_{1}\subset B_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose radius is
\alpha
times the radius of
B_{1}

•

Next Bob chooses a closed ball
B_{2}\subset A_{1}
whose radius is
\beta
times the radius of
A_{1}

•

Then Alice chooses a closed ball
A_{2}\subset B_{2}
whose radius is
\alpha
times the radius of
B_{2}

•

\cdots\cdots

We call a sequence of choices by Alice (resp. Bob) depending on the choices of Bob (resp. Alice) a
strategy
. A set

is called

if Alice has a strategy to make sure (no matter how Bob chooses his strategy), we have

\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\subset S.

Schmidt proved the following

(Theorem 2,
[

]
)

]

By introducing a modified version of Schmidt’s game, Kleinbock and Weiss

J=\cup_{Q\geq Q_{\epsilon}}J_{Q},

where
J_{Q}:=\left[n\log\left(\frac{Q}{\delta}\right),m\log\left(\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}Q^{\frac{n}{m}}\right)\right]
. It follows that the
T_{\delta}
we need can be taken as

T_{Q}:=n\log\left(\frac{Q_{\epsilon}}{\delta}\right).

∎

1.5
Hausdorff and packing dimensions and the statement of main results

The
s-dimensional Hausdorff measure
of a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
is defined to be

\mathscr{H}^{s}(S):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\operatorname{diam}U_{i})^{s}:\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}\supseteq S,\operatorname{diam}U_{i}<\delta\right\}

(1.10)

The
s-dimensional packing measure
of a set
S
is defined as

\mathscr{P}^{s}(S)=\inf\left\{\left.\sum_{j\in J}\mathscr{P}_{0}^{s}(S_{j})\right|S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in J}S_{j},J\text{ countable}\right\},

(1.11)

where
\mathscr{P}^{S}_{0}
, called the
s-dimensional packing pre-measure
, is defined as

\mathscr{P}_{0}^{s}(S)=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\left\{\left.\sum_{i\in I}\mathrm{diam}(B_{i})^{s}\right|\begin{matrix}\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{ is a countable collection}\\
\text{of pairwise disjoint closed balls with}\\
\text{diameters }\leq\delta\text{ and centres in }S\end{matrix}\right\}.

(1.12)

Given the measures defined above, we define the Hausdorff dimension and packing dimension of a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
as follows:

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(S)=\inf\{d\geq 0:\mathscr{H}^{d}(S)=0\}=\sup\left(\{d\geq 0:\mathscr{H}^{d}(S)=\infty\}\cup\{0\}\right),

(1.13)

\dim_{\mathrm{P}}(S)=\sup\{s\geq 0|\mathscr{P}^{s}(S)=+\infty\}=\inf\{s\geq 0|\mathscr{P}^{s}(S)=0\}.

(1.14)

Our first result is on the measure of badly approximable matrices of higher orders:

Theorem 1.17
.

\textbf{BA}_{d}(m,n)=\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. For all
r=1,2,\dots,d-1
,
\textbf{BA}_{r}(m,n)
is Lebesgue null.

Since
\textbf{BA}_{1}(m,n)\subset\textbf{BA}_{2}(m,n)\cdots\subset\textbf{BA}_{d}(m,n)=\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, a natural question to ask next is how big the Gaps
BA_{r+1}(m,n)-BA_{r}(m,n)
are in terms of fractional dimensions. We have

Theorem 1.18
.

For
1\leq r\leq d-1
, we have the Hausdorff dimension for the gaps between the badly approximable matrices of order
r
and
r+1
is full:

\dim_{H}\big(\textbf{BA}_{r+1}(m,n)-\textbf{BA}_{r}(m,n)\big)=\dim_{P}\big(\textbf{BA}_{r+1}(m,n)-\textbf{BA}_{r}(m,n)\big)=mn.

Remark 1.19
.

As of now, we are not able to obtain an explicit formula for the Hausdorff and packing dimensions for the singular matrices of order
r
. This is a work in progress.

2.1
Templates

Recall the definition of successive minina (Definition
1.13
). The key idea of variational principles is to approximate successive minina function

Definition 2.1

(
[
DFSU20
]
)
.

An
m\times n

template

\textbf{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the
following properties:

(I)

f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq f_{d}
.

(II)

-\frac{1}{n}\leq f_{i}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{m}

(III)

For all

F_{j}:=\sum_{0<i\leq j}f_{i}

(2.1)

Here
d_{+}:=m,d_{-}:=n,[a,b]_{\mathbb{Z}}:=[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

We use the convention that
f_{0}=-\infty
and
f_{d+1}=+\infty
. We will call the assertion that
F_{j}
is convex
the
convexity condition
, and the assertion that its slopes are in

the
quantized
slope condition
. We denote the space of
m\times n
templates by
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}

Observe that
F_{d}=f_{1}+...+f_{d}
is always a constant due to the property (III) above. A template
f
is called
balanced
if
F_{d}=0
. A
partial template
is a piecewise linear map
f
satisfying (I)-(III) whose

.

The fundamental relation between templates and successive minima functions is given
as follows:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
DFSU20
]
)
.

(i)

For every

\textbf{h}_{A}\asymp_{+}\textbf{f}
.

(ii)

For every
m\times n
matrix

, there exists an
m\times n

f

\textbf{h}_{A}\asymp_{+}\textbf{f}

Theorem

2.2
The lower and upper contraction rates

The next important notion we need to introduce for the statement of the variational principal is the lower and upper average contraction rate of a template.

We define the lower and upper average contraction rate of a template
f
as follows. Let
I
be an open interval on which
f
is linear. For each
q=1,\dots,d
such that
f_{q}<f_{q+1}
on
I

L_{\pm}=L_{\pm}(\textbf{f},I,q)\in[0,d_{\pm}]_{\mathbb{Z}}
be chosen to satisfy
L_{+}+L_{-}=q
and

F_{q}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1^{q}}f_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{L_{+}}{m}-\frac{L_{-}}{m},

(2.2)

as guaranteed by (III) of the definition of templates. An interval of equality for
f
on
I
is an interval
(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
0\leq p<q\leq d

f_{p}<f_{p+1}=\cdots=f_{q}<f_{q+1}~\text{on}~I,

(2.3)

As before, we use the convention that
f_{0}=-\infty
and
f_{d+1}=\infty
. Note that the collection of intervals of equality forms a partition of
[1,d]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}
is an interval of equality for
f
on
I
, then we let
M_{\pm}(p,q)=M_{\pm}(\textbf{f},I,p,q)
, where

M_{\pm}(\textbf{f},I,p,q)=L_{\pm}(\textbf{f},I,q)-L_{\pm}(\textbf{f},I,p),

(2.4)

or equivalently,
M_{\pm}(p,q)
are the unique integers such that

M_{+}+M_{-}=q-p~\text{and}~\sum_{i=p+1}^{q}f_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{M_{+}}{m}-\frac{M_{-}}{n}~\text{on}~I.

(2.5)

It can be shown from the definition of template that
M_{\pm}\geq 0
by (II) of the definition of templates. Next, let

S_{+}=S_{+}(\textbf{f},I)=\cup_{(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}}(p,p+M_{+}(p,q)]_{\mathbb{Z}}

(2.6)

S_{-}=S_{-}(\textbf{f},I)=\cup_{(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}}(p+M_{+}(p,q),q]_{\mathbb{Z}}

(2.7)

where the unions are taken over all intervals of equality for
f
on
I
. Note that
S_{+}
and
S_{-}

are disjoint and satisfy

, and that

and

.

Next, let

\delta(\textbf{f},I)=\#\{(i_{+},i_{-})\in S_{+}\times S_{-}:i_{+}<i_{-}\}\in[0,mn]_{\mathbb{Z}},

(2.8)

and note that

mn-\delta(\textbf{f},I)=\#\{(i_{+},i_{-})\in S_{+}\times S_{-}:i_{+}>i_{-}\}

(2.9)

Definition 2.3

The

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.10
.

For
d=m+n
,
G=\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
, let
P_{-}
denote the subgroup of lower diagonal block matrices

P_{-}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A&O\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\in\text{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})\;\middle|\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$A,C,D$ are any real matrices of size $m\times m,n\times m,n\times n$ respectively,\\
and $O$ is a zero $m\times n$ matrix\end{tabular}\right\}

and
U^{+}
denote the subgroup of unipotent upper diagonal matrices

U^{+}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&B\\

then the complement
G-P_{-}U^{+}

Proof.

The classical proof of this involves the theory of algebraic geometry and algebraic groups. By looking at the orbit
P_{-}\backslash P_{-}U^{+}
in the irreducible projective variety
P_{-}\backslash G
. By the theory of algebraic groups,
P_{-}\backslash P_{-}U^{+}

P_{-}\backslash G

P_{-}\backslash G
has strictly non-full dimension. So
P_{-}U^{+}

For the self-containedness purpose,we shall give an elementary proof here that only involves linear algebra and manifold theory. We will study the set of matrices
\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Z&W\end{bmatrix}
in
\text{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
that can be (and cannot be) represented by matrices in
P_{-}
and
U^{+}
.

Suppose
\begin{bmatrix}A&O\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\in P_{-}
and
\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&B\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in U^{-}
and we have the following equation of block matrices:

\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Z&W\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A&O\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&B\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A&AB\\
C&BC+D\end{bmatrix}

(2.16)

We have the system of equations:

\begin{cases}X=A\\
Z=C\\
Y=AB\\
W=BC+D\end{cases}\,

(2.17)

which can be simplified to

By the last term is a subvariety of

(2)

On the intervals of linearity

For

From the derivative restrictions:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{\tau_{2}}{r}\leq\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{\tau_{2}}{d-r}\leq\frac{1}{m}

we have
\tau\leq\min\{\frac{r}{m},\frac{d-r}{n}\}
, but
\tau_{2}\leq\frac{r}{m}
is automatic and the condition

(\tau_{2})_{\min}=-\frac{m+n}{mn}\left\lceil\frac{rn}{m+n}-1\right\rceil+\frac{r}{m}\leq\frac{d-r}{n}

is equivalent to

Summarizing what we have done so far, we have demonstrated that it is possible to find slopes
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
such that the
d
-component piecewise linear function
\textbf{f}=\textbf{q}[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
on
[a_{k},b_{k}]=[b_{k}-k,b_{k}]
as constructed is indeed a partial template as long as
r\leq\max(m,n)
.

Now we will show that

\delta(\textbf{f})=mn.

Indeed,

\delta(\textbf{f}):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Delta(\textbf{f},T):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt,

where
0<\delta(\textbf{f},[a_{k},b_{k}])\leq mn
and
\delta(\textbf{f},[b_{n-1},a_{k}])=mn
.

Since

0=:b_{0}<b_{1}<\cdots<b_{k}=n^{n}\to\infty,

any
T>0
lies in the dilating period
(b_{n-1},b_{k}]
of
f
for some
n\geq 0
. In each of such period, the average of integral
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,T])dt
reaches its maximum at
T=a_{k}
and its minimum at
T=b_{k}
:

\frac{1}{a_{k}}\int_{0}^{a_{k}}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt\leq\frac{1}{b_{k}}\int_{0}^{b_{k}}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt\leq mn.

But

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{k}}\int_{0}^{a_{k}}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{b_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}\delta(\textbf{f},[0,t])dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{b_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}mn~dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mn\frac{1}{k^{k}-k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(i^{i}-i-(i-1)^{i-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mn\frac{k^{k}}{k^{k}-k}-mn\frac{k(k+1)}{2(k^{k}-k)}

which converges to zero as
k\to\infty
.

Therefore, by the variational principle,

\dim_{P}(\textbf{BA}(r+1)-\textbf{BA}(r))=\dim_{H}(\textbf{BA}(r+1)-\textbf{BA}(r))=mn,

for all
r\leq\max(m,n)
. Since
\frac{d}{2}\leq\max(m,n)
, we have proved the theorem for at least half of the orders.

For the computation of cases when
r>\max(m,n)
, we will study the dual of the orbit of lattices
(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{t\geq 0}
.

Recall that for a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with basis
\{\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}\}
, since
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
are linearly independent, from linear algebra we know there exist vectors
\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}
, call
dual vectors
to

\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
, such that

\langle\textbf{b}_{i},\textbf{b}_{i}^{*}\rangle=\begin{cases}0&i\neq j\\
1&i=j\end{cases}.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-span of dual basis vectors, namely
\Lambda^{*}:=\text{Span}\{\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}\}
, is called the
dual (or polar or reciprocal) lattice
to the lattice
\Lambda
.

Although defined through basis, it turns out that the dual lattices are independent of the choice of basis of the original lattice.

Proposition 2.17
.

The dual lattice
\Lambda^{*}
consists of all vectors
\textbf{b}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}\rangle
is an integer for all
b
in
\Lambda
. As a consequence,
\Lambda^{*}
is also the dual of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
be a basis of the lattice
\Lambda
and their duals be
\textbf{b}_{1}^{*},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}
. For any
\textbf{b}\in\Lambda
and any
\textbf{c}\in\Lambda^{*}
, suppose

\textbf{b}=s_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}\textbf{b}_{d},\text{ and }\textbf{c}=t_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+t_{d}\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}

with integer coefficients
s_{i},t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,d.
We have immediately that

\langle\textbf{b},\textbf{c}\rangle=s_{1}t_{1}\cdots+s_{d}t_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}.

On the other hand, if
\textbf{b}^{*}=u_{1}\textbf{b}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+u_{d}\textbf{b}_{d}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z},
for any
\textbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then in particular this holds for
\textbf{b}=\textbf{b}_{i}
, for any
i=1,2,\cdots,d
and thus

u_{i}=\langle\textbf{b}^{*},\textbf{b}_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore
\textbf{b}^{*}\in\Lambda^{*}
.

∎

The dual lattice operator commutes nicely with an invertible linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an invertible linear transformation, then we have

(T\Lambda)^{*}=T^{*}\Lambda^{*},

where
T^{*}={}^{t}T^{-1}
is the inverse of the transpose of
T
and
\Lambda^{*}
is the dual lattice to
\Lambda
.

Proof.

If
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,\textbf{b}_{d}
is a basis of
\Lambda
, then
\textbf{b}_{1},\cdots,T\textbf{b}_{d}

is a basis of
T\Lambda
. The corresponding dual basis

(T\textbf{b}_{1})^{*},\cdots,(T\textbf{b}_{d})^{*}

satisfy

\begin{bmatrix}{}^{t}(T\textbf{b}_{1})^{*}\\
\vdots\\
{}^{t}(T\textbf{b}_{1})^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T\textbf{b}_{1}&\cdots&T\textbf{b}_{d}\end{bmatrix}=I_{d}

But on the other hand,

\begin{bmatrix}{}^{t}({}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
{}^{t}({}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{d})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T\textbf{b}_{1}&\cdots&T\textbf{b}_{d}\end{bmatrix}=I_{d}

So by the uniqueness of inverse matrix,
T^{*}\textbf{b}_{i}={}^{t}T^{-1}\textbf{b}_{i}=(Tb_{i})
, for any
i=1,2\cdots,d
. Therefore
(T\Lambda)^{*}=T^{*}\Lambda^{*}.

∎

We are able to use the idea of dual lattice to address the issue of higher
r
’s, thanks to the following theorem:

Theorem 2.19

(
[
Cas97
]
Chapter VIII, Theorem VI)
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d}
be the successive minima of lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for a lattice
\Lambda
and its dual
\Lambda^{*}
, we have

1\leq\lambda_{r}(\Lambda)\lambda_{d+1-r}(\Lambda^{*})\leq d!

for any
r=1,2,\cdots d
.

Now let us return to our proof, for the flow of lattices
(g_{t}u_{A}\Lambda)
, the proposition above gives its dual as:

\displaystyle(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*}=

\displaystyle g_{t}^{*}u_{A}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{t}g_{t}^{-1}\cdot{}^{t}u_{A}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}e^{t/m}I_{m}&0\\
0&e^{-t/n}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\cdot{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&A\\
0&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t/m}I_{m}&0\\
0&e^{t/n}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&0\\
-{}^{t}A&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Now observe that for
r>\frac{d}{2}
,
d+1-r<d+1-\frac{d}{2}=\frac{d}{2}+1\leq\max(m,n)+1
(which is the same as

), and we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\textbf{h}_{A,r}=-\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{r}(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{r}(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{d+1-r}((g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*})=\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{d+1-r}\left(\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t/m}I_{m}&0\\

-{}^{t}A&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)=\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\textbf{h}_{-^{t}A,d+1-r}(t)=\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}-\textbf{h}_{-^{t}A,d+1-r}(t)=-\infty

and that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\textbf{h}_{A,r+1}(t)>-\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{r+1}(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})>0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{r+1}(g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})>0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{d-r}((g_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*})<\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{d-r}\left(\begin{bmatrix}e^{-t/m}I_{m}&0\\
0&e^{t/n}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&0\\
-{}^{t}A&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)<\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\textbf{h}_{-^{t}A,d-r}(t)<\infty

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}-\textbf{h}_{-^{t}A,d-r}(t)>-\infty

Therefore, by replacing templates

\dim_{P}(\textbf{BA}(r+1)-\textbf{BA}(r))=\dim_{H}(\textbf{BA}(r+1)-\textbf{BA}(r))=mn,

for

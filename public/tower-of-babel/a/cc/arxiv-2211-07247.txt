Controlling the magnetic state of the proximate quantum spin liquid α \alpha -RuCl 3 with an optical cavity
Emil Viñas Boström, Adithya Sriram, Martin Claassen, Angel Rubio

Abstract
Harnessing the enhanced light-matter coupling and quantum vacuum fluctuations resulting from mode volume compression in optical cavities is a promising route towards functionalizing quantum materials and realizing exotic states of matter. Here, we extend cavity quantum electrodynamical materials engineering to correlated magnetic systems, by demonstrating that a Fabry-Pérot cavity can be used to control the magnetic state of the proximate quantum spin liquid α \alpha -RuCl 3 . Depending on specific cavity properties such as the mode frequency, photon occupation, and strength of the light-matter coupling, any of the magnetic phases supported by the extended Kitaev model can be stabilized. In particular, in the THz regime, we show that the cavity vacuum fluctuations alone are sufficient to bring α \alpha -RuCl 3 from a zigzag antiferromagnetic to a ferromagnetic state. By external pumping of the cavity in the few photon limit, it is further possible to push the system into the antiferromagnetic Kitaev quantum spin liquid state.

Discussion

The results of Fig.
2
and
3
show that the magnetic ground state of
\alpha
-RuCl
3
is sensitive to the interaction with the quantum fluctuations of the cavity field, and depending on the photon frequency, average cavity occupation and strength of the light-matter coupling, the equilibrium magnetic order can be transformed into any of the magnetic states supported by the extended Kitaev model of Eq.
1
. In particular, our results show that the cavity vacuum fluctuations alone are indeed sufficient to change the magnetic order of the photo ground state when the light-matter coupling is sufficiently strong (but within the range of present experimental light-matter couplings). However, as the value of the light-matter coupling is strongly dependent on the cavity size, and scales as
g\sim 1/L
with the linear size of the system, it seems as though the effect of the cavity vanishes quickly in the thermodynamic limit. This naive scaling argument might fail for several reasons, the most important being the neglect of additional cavity modes with momenta
{\bf q}\approx 0
. In fact, the number of modes with momenta smaller than some given cut-off
q_{\rm max}
scales as
N(q_{\rm max})\sim L
,
Latini2021
;
Latini2021b
;
Fausti2022
and can potentially cancel the volume scaling of
g
. For the effective single mode approximation employed here, the mode volume appearing in the light-matter coupling should therefore be interpreted as an effective mode volume to be fixed either by additional calculations or by comparison to experiment.
Choi2017
;
Hubener2020
;
Latini2021

An additional subtlety of the thermodynamic limit is the potential coupling of the cavity modes to collective fluctuations of the magnetic system. In particular, close to a magnetic phase boundary, collective fluctuations with a correlation length
\xi\sim L
are expected to appear. Such collective modes can be expected to play a crucial role in stabilizing the magnetic order, and to couple more strongly to the cavity field.
Weber2022
However, an explicit treatment of such macroscopic effects, while simultaneously retaining an exact description of spin and photon quantum fluctuations, requires a more advanced methodology going beyond the current work.

For practical purposes, we note that for a given light-matter coupling
g
the effective coupling
\bar{g}
can be enhanced by increasing the photon occupation of the cavity. In particular, since
\bar{g}=2g\sqrt{n_{\rm av}}
, a substantial effective coupling (and corresponding modification of the magnetic interactions in the spin-photon Hamiltonian of Eq.
1
) can be obtained with relatively few photons. Although the present work neglects any cavity dissipation as might be relevant in a non-equilibrium setting or for a dark cavity with lossy mirrors, the qualitative conclusions are expected to be robust against such effects. This includes in particular the identification of a zigzag antiferromagnetic to ferromagnetic transition induced by the cavity vacuum fluctuations, and the presence of a large Kitaev QSL domain in the magnetic phase diagram in the few photon regime. Together these results demonstrate the feasibility of c-QED engineering of the magnetic state of the proximate spin liquid
\alpha
-RuCl
3
in the few photon limit. Looking ahead, we note that the cavity photon momentum constitutes a tuning parameter in addition to the light-matter coupling, and we therefore envisage that c-QED can be used to also modify phases with finite-
{\bf q}
ordering vectors (such as charge density waves). Our work thereby paves the way for utilizing c-QED to control exotic magnetic states in real cavity-embedded quantum materials.
Ruggenthaler2018
;
Hubener2020

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\bigg[\Big(\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}^{\dagger}\Big)\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{2}&t_{4}\\

t_{4}&t_{4}&t_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}\\

\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+t_{pd}\big(\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}+\hat{c}_{jyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.6491pt+\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}+\hat{c}_{jxz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}\big)+H.c.\bigg].

(5)

Here,
t_{i}
with
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
parameterize direct
d-d
hopping processes, while
t_{pd}
determines the strength of ligand mediated hopping via the Cl
p_{z}
-orbitals.

Light-matter coupling

In presence of the cavity the total Hamiltonian in the dipole approximation is given by
H=H_{U}+H_{t}+\sum_{\lambda}\hbar\Omega_{\lambda}\hat{n}_{\lambda}
. Here,
\hbar\Omega_{\lambda}
is the photon energy of a mode described by the label
\lambda
and
\hat{n}_{\lambda}
is the corresponding number operator. Inside the cavity the Hamiltonian
H_{t}
is modified by the replacements
\hat{c}_{i\alpha\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i\beta\sigma}\to e^{i\phi_{ij}}\hat{c}_{i\alpha\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i\beta\sigma}
, where the Peierls phases are
\phi_{ij}=(ea/\hbar){\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}
,
{\bf d}_{ij}={\bf r}_{j}-{\bf r}_{i}
is the vector between atomic sites
i
and
j
measured in units of the Ru-Ru distance
a
, and the quantum vector potential is

\displaystyle\hat{\bf A}=\sum_{\lambda}(A_{\lambda}{\bf e}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}^{\dagger}+A_{\lambda}^{*}{\bf e}^{*}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}).

(6)

For a given mode
\lambda
this defines the light-matter coupling
g_{\lambda}=(ea/\hbar)A_{\lambda}=ea/\sqrt{2\epsilon_{0}\hbar\Omega_{\lambda}V}
. Thus, the Peierls phases can be written as
\phi_{ij}={\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}
with

\displaystyle\hat{\bf A}=\sum_{\lambda}(g_{\lambda}{\bf e}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}^{\dagger}+g_{\lambda}^{*}{\bf e}^{*}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}),

(7)

and the modified hopping Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{t}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\bigg[\Big(\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}^{\dagger}\Big)e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{2}&t_{4}\\
t_{2}&t_{1}&t_{4}\\
t_{4}&t_{4}&t_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}\\
\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}\\
\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+t_{pd}\big(e^{i{\bf d}_{i^{\prime}i}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}+e^{i{\bf d}_{ji^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}\hat{c}_{jyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.6491pt+e^{i{\bf d}_{j^{\prime}i}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}+e^{i{\bf d}_{jj^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}\hat{c}_{jxz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}\big)+H.c.\bigg].

(8)

Hamiltonian in the photon number basis

The Hamiltonian is expanded in the photon number basis
|{\bf n}\rangle=|n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{N}\rangle
according to
Sentef2020
;
Li2020
;
Li2021

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bf nm}({\bf 1}_{e}\otimes|{\bf n}\rangle\langle{\bf n}|)H({\bf 1}_{e}\otimes|{\bf m}\rangle\langle{\bf m}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bf nm}H_{\bf nm}\otimes|{\bf n}\rangle\langle{\bf m}|.

(9)

Here
{\bf 1}_{e}
is the identity operator in the electronic Hilbert space, and the Hamiltonian
H_{\bf nm}
is given by

\displaystyle H_{\bf nm}

\displaystyle=\big(H_{U}+\sum_{\lambda}\hbar\Omega_{\lambda}n_{\lambda}\big)\delta_{\bf nm}+H_{t,\bf nm}.

(10)

The matrix elements
\langle{\bf n}|e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}|{\bf m}\rangle
are obtained by noting that since
[\hat{a}_{\lambda}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}]=0
, the Peierls phases factorize over different modes and

\displaystyle\langle{\bf n}|e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}|{\bf m}\rangle=\prod_{\lambda}\langle n_{\lambda}|e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}_{\lambda}}|m_{\lambda}\rangle,

(11)

where
\hat{\bf A}_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}{\bf e}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}^{\dagger}+g_{\lambda}^{*}{\bf e}^{*}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\lambda}
.

The single-mode expressions are calculated by introducing the variables
\eta_{ij\lambda}=g_{\lambda}({\bf d}_{ij}\cdot{\bf e}_{\lambda})
. Using the Baker-Hausdorff formula to expand the exponential, the matrix elements are
\langle n_{\lambda}|e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}_{\lambda}}|m_{\lambda}\rangle=i^{|n_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}|}j_{n_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}}^{ij}
where

\displaystyle j_{n_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\eta_{ij\lambda}|^{2}/2}\hskip 103.85237pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69046ptn_{\lambda}\geq m_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m_{\lambda}}\frac{(-1)^{k}|\eta_{ij\lambda}|^{2k}(\eta_{ij\lambda})^{n_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}}}{k!(k+n_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda})!}\sqrt{\frac{n_{\lambda}!}{m_{\lambda}!}}\frac{m_{\lambda}!}{(m_{\lambda}-k)!}

(12)

\displaystyle j_{n_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\eta_{ij\lambda}|^{2}/2}\hskip 103.85237pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69046ptm_{\lambda}\geq n_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\lambda}}\frac{(-1)^{k}|\eta_{ij\lambda}|^{2k}(\eta_{ij\lambda}^{*})^{m_{\lambda}-n_{\lambda}}}{k!(k+m_{\lambda}-n_{\lambda})!}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\lambda}!}{n_{\lambda}!}}\frac{n_{\lambda}!}{(n_{\lambda}-k)!}.

(13)

Using the notation
{\bf g}=\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\}
to denote the full set of light-matter couplings, the light-matter coupling is described by the function

\displaystyle J_{\bf nm}^{ij}({\bf g})=\langle{\bf n}|e^{i{\bf d}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bf A}}|{\bf m}\rangle=\prod_{\lambda}j_{n_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}}^{ij}.

(14)

With this function, the hopping Hamiltonian in presence of the cavity can be written as

\displaystyle H_{t,\bf nm}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\bigg[\Big(\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}^{\dagger}\Big)

\displaystyle\times J_{\bf nm}^{ij}({\bf g})\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{2}&t_{4}\\
t_{2}&t_{1}&t_{4}\\
t_{4}&t_{4}&t_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}\\
\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}\\
\hat{c}_{ixy\sigma}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+t_{pd}\big(J_{\bf nm}^{i^{\prime}i}({\bf g})\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{ixz\sigma}+J_{\bf nm}^{ji^{\prime}}({\bf g})\hat{c}_{jyz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{i^{\prime}\sigma}

(15)

\displaystyle\hskip 15.6491pt+J_{\bf nm}^{j^{\prime}i}({\bf g})\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{iyz\sigma}+J_{\bf nm}^{jj^{\prime}}({\bf g})\hat{c}_{jxz\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{j^{\prime}\sigma}\big)+H.c.\bigg].

Single-mode light-matter coupling and polarization dependence

For a single photon mode the index
\lambda
can be dropped, and the function
J_{\bf nm}^{ij}({\bf g})=J_{nm}^{ij}(g)=j_{n,m}^{ij}
. Writing the intersite vector on a given bond as
{\bf d}_{ij}=d(\cos\theta_{ij}\hat{\bf x}+\sin\theta_{ij}\hat{\bf y})
, the parameter
\eta_{ij}
is given for a circularly polarized field by
\eta_{ij}=g({\bf d}_{ij}\cdot{\bf e})=gde^{\pm i\theta_{ij}}
, where
\pm
denotes a left-handed/right-handed polarization. Noting that
|\eta_{ij}|=|\eta|=gd
so that the spatial dependence of the function
j_{n,m}^{ij}
factorizes, we have
j_{n,m}^{ij}=j_{n,m}e^{\pm i(n-m)\theta_{ij}}
with

\displaystyle j_{n,m}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\eta|^{2}/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{|\eta|^{2k+n-m}}{k!(k+n-m)!}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{m!}}\frac{m!}{(m-k)!}

(16)

for
n\geq m
and similarly but with
n\leftrightarrow m
for
m\geq n
.

For linear polarization the polarization vector can be written as
{\bf e}=\cos\phi\hat{\bf x}+\sin\phi\hat{\bf y}
, and we have
\eta_{ij}=gd\cos(\theta_{ij}-\phi)
.

Connection to Floquet Hamiltonian

As a consistency check, the Hamiltonian
H_{t,\bf nm}
for a single circularly polarized photon mode is shown to reduce to the Floquet Hamiltonian of Ref.
Sriram2022
in the semi-classical limit. For a single photon mode, we can drop the mode index, and the function
J_{\bf nm}^{ij}({\bf g})=J_{nm}^{ij}(g)=j_{n,m}^{ij}
. Taking the polarization vector of the photon mode to be
{\bf e}=(\hat{\bf x}\pm i\hat{\bf y})/\sqrt{2}
, and writing
{\bf d}_{ij}=d(\cos\theta_{ij}\hat{\bf x}+\sin\theta_{ij}\hat{\bf y})
, we have
\eta_{ij}=g({\bf d}_{ij}\cdot{\bf e})=gde^{\pm i\theta_{ij}}
.

To establish the connection to the Floquet Hamiltonian, what remains is now to show that the function
j_{n,m}(g)
reduces to the Bessel function
J_{n-m}(A)
in the semi-classical limit. To do this, we write
n=m+l
and take the limit
m\to\infty
and
g\to 0
while keeping
A=2gd\sqrt{m}
constant. Noting that

\displaystyle j_{m+l,m}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\eta|^{2}/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{|\eta|^{2k+l}}{k!(k+l)!}\sqrt{\frac{(m+l)!}{m!}}\frac{m!}{(m-k)!}

\displaystyle=e^{-A^{2}/8n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)^{2k+l}}{k!(k+l)!}\sqrt{\frac{(m+l)!}{m!m^{l}}}\frac{m!}{(m-k)!m^{k}},

(17)

and that
(m+l)!/m!\to m^{l}
and
m/(m-k)!\to m^{k}
as
m\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}j_{m+l,m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)^{2k+l}}{k!(k+l)!}=J_{l}(A).

(18)

Since
l=n-m
the Hamiltonian
H_{t,\bf nm}
reproduces the correct Floquet Hamiltonian in the semi-classical limit.

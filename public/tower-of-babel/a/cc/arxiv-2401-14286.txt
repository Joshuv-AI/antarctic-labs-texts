Equivalence of Applicative Functors and Multifunctors
Andreas Abel Department of Computer Science, Gothenburg University, Sweden

Abstract
McBride and Paterson ( McBride and Paterson 2008 ) introduced Applicative functors to Haskell, which are equivalent to the lax monoidal functors (with strength) of category theory. Applicative functors F F are presented via idiomatic application _ ​ ⊛ ​ _ : F ⁡ ( A ​ → ​ B ) ​ → ​ F ​ A ​ → ​ F ​ B \_{⊛}\_:F\,(A\textrightarrow B)\textrightarrow FA\textrightarrow FB and laws that are a bit hard to remember. Capriotti and Kaposi 2014 observed that applicative functors can be conceived as multifunctors, i. e., by a family 𝗅𝗂𝖿𝗍𝖠 n : ( A 1 → … → A n → C ) → F ​ A 1 → … → F ​ A n → F ​ C \mathsf{liftA}_{n}:(A_{1}\to\dots\to A_{n}\to C)\to FA_{1}\to\dots\to FA_{n}\to FC of zipWith -like functions that generalize pure ( n = 0 ) (n=0) , fmap ( n = 1 ) (n=1) and liftA2 ( n = 2 ) (n=2) . This reduces the associated laws to just the first functor law and a uniform scheme of second (multi)functor laws, i. e., a composition law for 𝗅𝗂𝖿𝗍𝖠 \mathsf{liftA} . In this note, we rigorously prove that applicative functors are in fact equivalent to multifunctors, by interderiving their laws.



1
Introduction

McBride and Paterson 2008
introduce applicative functors as a
Haskell type constructor class
\mathsf{Applicative}\,F
with two methods

\begin{array}[]{lll@{\qquad}l}\mathsf{pure}&:&A\to FA&\mbox{embedding}\\
\_⊛\_&:&F\,(A\to B)\to FA\to FB&\mbox{idiomatic application, left associative}\\
\end{array}

satisfying four laws:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}lll}\mbox{{identity}}&\mathsf{pure}\,(\lambda x\to x)⊛u&=&u\\
\mbox{{composition}}&\mathsf{pure}\,(\lambda fgx\to f\,(g\,x))⊛u⊛v⊛w&=&u⊛(v⊛w)\\
\mbox{{interchange}}&\mathsf{pure}\,(\lambda f\to fx)⊛u&=&u⊛\mathsf{pure}\,x\\
\mbox{{homomorphism}}&\mathsf{pure}\,(f\,x)&=&\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛\mathsf{pure}\,x\\
\end{array}

Using the usual definitions of identity
\mathsf{id}
and composition
(\_{\circ}\_)

the first two laws can be presented as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}lll}\mbox{{identity}}&\mathsf{pure}\,\mathsf{id}⊛u&=&u\\
\mbox{{composition}}&\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)⊛u⊛v⊛w&=&u⊛(v⊛w)\end{array}

Functoriality of
F
is recovered via
\mathsf{fmap}\,f\,u=\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛u

where
identity
acts as the first functor law. The second
functor law can be derived via
composition
and

homomorphism
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}\mathsf{fmap}\,f\,(\mathsf{fmap}\,g\,u)&=&\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛(\mathsf{pure}\,g⊛u)&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)⊛\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛\mathsf{pure}\,g⊛u\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(f\circ\_)⊛\mathsf{pure}\,g⊛u&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(f\circ g)⊛u\\
&=&\mathsf{fmap}\,(f\circ g)\,u\end{array}

Unfortunately,
McBride and Paterson 2008
’s laws are not easy
to remember, especially the
composition
and
interchange

laws. They do not follow simple patterns like the functor laws which
can be seen as actions of the function category, or the monad laws,
which can be conceived as generalization of the monoid laws. It is
also not intuitively clear at a glance that these laws are complete.

Starting with GHC 8.2 (2017),
\mathsf{Applicative}
s can also be given via

\mathsf{liftA}_{2}:(A\to B\to C)\to FA\to FB\to FC

rather than idiomatic application, which are
interdefinable:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,u\,v&=&\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛u⊛v\\
h⊛u&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\lambda fx\to fx)\,h\,u\\
\end{array}

However, to this date (2024-01-24) the documentation
1
1

1

\url
https://hackage.haskell.org/package/base-4.19.0.0/docs/Control-Applicative.html

of
\mathsf{Applicative}
does not spell out the type class laws in terms of

\mathsf{liftA}_{2}
.

Note that
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}
appears to be the
binary
generalization
of the
unary

\mathsf{fmap}=\mathsf{liftA}_{1}:(A\to B)\to FA\to FB
.
In the same way, we get the
nullary

\mathsf{pure}=\mathsf{liftA}_{0}:A\to FA
.

In this note, we show that the further generalization to arbitrary
arities
\mathsf{liftA}_{n}
gives very elegant laws for the family

\mathsf{liftA}_{n}
, which are just generalizations of the two functor laws.

The infinite family
\mathsf{liftA}_{n}
can be truncated to
n\leq 2
,
yielding the following composition laws in addition to the functor
laws (for
\mathsf{liftA}_{1}
):

\begin{array}[]{lll@{\quad}l}\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,(f\,x)&\mbox{$\mbox{{homomorphism}}$}\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(f\,x)&\mbox{$\mbox{{homomorphism}}$}\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,y)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\lambda x\to f\,x\,y)\,u&\mbox{$\mbox{{exchange}}$}\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,g\,u)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(f\circ g)\,u&\mbox{2nd functor law}\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,h\,v)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\lambda x\to f\,x\circ h)\,u\,v\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,g\,u\,v)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\lambda x\to f\circ g\,x)\,u\,v\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,g\,u\,v)\,w&=&\lx@intercol\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\lambda x(y,z)\to f\,(g\,x\,y)\,z)\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\_{,}\_)\,v\,w)\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,f\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,g\,v\,w)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\lambda(x,y)\to f\,x\circ g\,y)\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\_{,}\_)\,u\,v)\,w\\
\end{array}

2
Applicative Functors as Multifunctors

Preliminaries: generalized composition.

If
f:A_{1..n}\to C\to D
and
g:B_{1..m}\to C
then let

f\circ^{m}_{n}g:A_{1..n}\to B_{1..m}\to D
be
defined by

(f\circ^{m}_{n}g)\;a_{1..n}\;b_{1..m}=f\,a_{1..n}\,(g\,b_{1..m}).

Herein,
h\,x_{1..n}
is to be understood as curried application

h\,x_{1}\,\dots\,x_{n}
. A sequence
x_{1..n}
may more succinctly be
written as
\vec{x}^{n}
or just
\vec{x}
.

Note that
(f\circ^{1}_{0}g)\,x=f\,(g\,x)
is ordinary unary function composition.
Further,
(f\circ^{0}_{n}y)\,a_{1..n}=f\,a_{1..n}\,y
is partial application of
f
to its
n+1
st argument, which for
n=0
is just plain application:
f\circ^{0}_{0}y=f\,y
.

Multifunctors.

To distinguish our concept of applicative functors from that of

McBride and Paterson 2008
, we temporarily call them

multifunctor
.
2
2

2

The name
multi-functor
is taken from

Capriotti and Kaposi 2014
and already motivated there as means
to “naturally arrive at the definition of the
\mathsf{Applicative}
clase
via an obvious generalization of the notion of functor.”

A multifunctor
F
shall be witnessed by a family of functions (
n\geq 0
)

\mathsf{liftA}_{n}:(A_{1}\to\dots\to A_{n}\to C)\to FA_{1}\to\dots\to FA_{n}\to FC

satisfying the following laws:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l@{~~}l@{~~}l}\mbox{{identity}}&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,\mathsf{id}&=&\mathsf{id}\\
\mbox{{composition}}&\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1+m}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}^{k})&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n+k+m}\,(f\circ^{k}_{n}g)\,\vec{u}\,\vec{v}\end{array}

We may drop the index to
\mathsf{liftA}
when it is
generic or clear from the context of discourse.

Just functoriality of
F
can be recovered by
\mathsf{fmap}=\mathsf{liftA}_{1}
with
identity
being the first functor law and
composition
specializing to the second functor law with
n=m=0
and
k=1
:

\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,g\,v)=\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(f\circ^{1}_{0}g)\,v

Pure computations are represented via
\mathsf{pure}=\mathsf{liftA}_{0}
, with the composition law specializing to:

\mathsf{liftA}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)=\mathsf{liftA}\,(f\circ^{0}_{n}x)\,\vec{u}

For
\mathsf{fmap}
(
n=m=0
) this yields
\mathsf{fmap}\,f\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)=\mathsf{pure}\,(f\,x)
.
For just
n=0
we get law
\mathsf{liftA}_{1+m}\,f\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)\,\vec{w}=\mathsf{liftA}_{m}\,(f\,x)\,\vec{w}
. This can be iterated to

\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x_{1})\,\dots\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x_{n})=\mathsf{pure}\,(f\,x_{1..n})
corresponding to the intuition that composition
of effect-free computations is again an effect-free computation.

2.1
Multifunctors are applicative

Idiomatic application can be obtained as a special case of

\mathsf{liftA}_{2}
:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\_⊛\_&:&F(A\to B)\to FA\to FB\\
u⊛v&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,u\,v\end{array}

We easily derive its laws:

1.

identity
:

\begin{array}[]{lllllll@{\qquad}l}\mathsf{pure}\,\mathsf{id}⊛u&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,\mathsf{id})\,u&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\mathsf{id}\circ^{0}_{0}\mathsf{id})\,u&&&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\mathsf{id}\,\mathsf{id})\,u&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,\mathsf{id}\,u&=&u&\mbox{by $\mbox{{identity}}$}\\
\end{array}

2.

composition
.

\begin{array}[]{lllll@{\qquad}l}\lx@intercol\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)⊛u⊛v⊛w\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u⊛v⊛w&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u⊛v⊛w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u)\,v⊛w&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u\,v⊛w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u\,v)\,w&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{3}\,(\_{\circ}\_)\,u\,v\,w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{3}\,(\lambda fgx\to f\,(gx))\,u\,v\,w&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{3}\,(\lambda fgx\to\mathsf{id}\,f\,(\mathsf{id}\,g\,x))\,u\,v\,w\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{3}\,(\mathsf{id}\circ^{2}_{1}\mathsf{id})\,u\,v\,w&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,v\,w)&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&u⊛(v⊛w)\end{array}

3.

homomorphism
. This has been shown before, here again step-by-step:

\begin{array}[]{lllll@{\qquad}l}\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛\mathsf{pure}\,x&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,f)\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\mathsf{id}\,f)\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,(f\,x)&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,\,(f\,x)\end{array}

4.

interchange
:

\begin{array}[]{lllll@{\qquad}l}u⊛(\mathsf{pure}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{2}\,\mathsf{id}\,u\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,x)&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\mathsf{id}\circ^{0}_{1}x)\,u&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,(\lambda f\to f\,x)\,u&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(\lambda f\to fx)⊛u&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\end{array}

2.2
Applicative functors are multifunctors

Following
McBride and Paterson 2008
, the family
\mathsf{liftA}_{n}
can be
defined for each applicative functor:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathsf{liftA}_{0}\,x&=&\mathsf{pure}\,x\\
\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}\,v&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}⊛v\end{array}

The
identity
is just
identity
. We establish

composition
by a series of inductions.

Lemma 1

(Frame)
.

If
\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}=\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}^{k}
then

\mathsf{liftA}_{n+m}\,f\,\vec{u}\,\vec{w}^{m}=\mathsf{liftA}_{k+m}\,g\,\vec{v}\,\vec{w}
.

Proof.

By induction on
m
.
∎

As a consequence of
Lemma

1
, we only need to show the composition law for
m=0
:

\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}^{k})=\mathsf{liftA}_{n+k}\,(f\circ^{k}_{n}g)\,\vec{u}\,\vec{v}

We first show the case
n=0
:

Lemma 2

(Composition for
n=0
)
.

\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v})=\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,(f\circ^{k}_{0}g)\,\vec{v}

Proof.
By induction on
k
.

Case

k=0
: This is
homomorphism
.

Case

k\to k+1
.

\begin{array}[]{lll@{\qquad}l}\lx@intercol\mathsf{liftA}_{1}\,f\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k+1}\,g\,\vec{v}\,w)\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w)\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)⊛\mathsf{pure}\,f⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(f\circ\_)⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{homomorphism}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,((f\circ\_)\circ^{k}_{0}g)\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by ind.hyp.}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{k+1}\,(f\circ^{k+1}_{0}g)\,\vec{v}\,w\end{array}

For the last step, note that

(f\circ\_)\circ^{k}_{0}g=\lambda\vec{x}^{k}\to(f\circ\_)(g\,\vec{x})=\lambda\vec{x}\to f\circ(g\,\vec{x})=\lambda\vec{x}y\to f(g\,\vec{x}\,y)=f\circ^{k+1}_{0}g
.
∎

Corollary 3

(Composition for
k=0
)
.

\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)=\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,(f\circ^{0}_{n}x)\,\vec{u}

Proof.

\begin{array}[]{lllll@{\qquad}l}\lx@intercol\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}\,(\mathsf{pure}\,x)\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}⊛\mathsf{pure}\,x&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(\lambda k\to k\,x)⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}&\mbox{by $\mbox{{exchange}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,((\lambda k\to k\,x)\circ^{n}_{0}f)\,\vec{u}&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,(f\circ^{0}_{n}x)\,\vec{u}\end{array}

The last step is justified by

(\lambda k\to k\,x)\circ^{n}_{0}f=\lambda\vec{y}^{k}\to(\lambda k\to k\,x)\,(f\vec{y})=\lambda\vec{y}^{k}\to f\vec{y}\,x=f\circ^{0}_{n}x
.
∎

Theorem 4

(Composition)
.

\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}^{k})=\mathsf{liftA}_{n+k}\,(f\circ^{k}_{n}g)\,\vec{u}\,\vec{v}

Proof.

By induction on
k
.

Case

k=0
: This is
Corollary

3
.

Case

k\to k+1
:

\begin{array}[]{lll@{\qquad}l}\lx@intercol\mathsf{liftA}_{n+1}\,f\,\vec{u}^{n}\,(\mathsf{liftA}_{k+1}\,g\,\vec{v}^{k}\,w)\hfil\lx@intercol\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}⊛(\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w)\\
&=&\mathsf{pure}\,(\_{\circ}\_)⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,f\,\vec{u}⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by $\mbox{{composition}}$}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n}\,((\_{\circ}\_)\circ^{n}_{0}f)\,\vec{u}⊛\mathsf{liftA}_{k}\,g\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:compnzero}}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n+k}\,(((\_{\circ}\_)\circ^{n}_{0}f)\circ^{k}_{n}g)\,\vec{u}\,\vec{v}⊛w&\mbox{by ind.hyp.}\\
&=&\mathsf{liftA}_{n+k+1}\,(f\circ^{k+1}_{n}g)\,\vec{u}\,\vec{v}\,w\end{array}

For the last step, we calculate

((\_{\circ}\_)\circ^{n}_{0}f)\circ^{k}_{n}g=\lambda\vec{x}^{n}\vec{y}^{k}\to(\lambda\vec{x}^{n}\to(f\vec{x})\circ\_))\,\vec{x}\,(g\,\vec{y})=\lambda\vec{x}^{n}\vec{y}^{k}\to(f\vec{x})\circ(g\,\vec{y})=\lambda\vec{x}^{n}\vec{y}^{k}z\to f\vec{x}\,(g\,\vec{y}\,z)=f\circ^{k+1}_{n}g.

∎

Q.E.D.

Global weak solutions of an initial-boundary value problem on a half-line for the higher order nonlinear Schrödinger equation Thanks: This paper has been supported by Russian Science Foundation grant 23-11-00056.
Andrei V. Faminskii

Abstract
. An initial-boundary value problem with one boundary condition is considered for the higher order nonlinear Schrödinger equation. It is assumed that either the boundary condition is homogeneous or the nonlinearity in the equation is quadratic. Results on existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence on input data of global weak solutions are obtained.

35Q55, 35Q53

1.
Introduction. Notation. Description of main results

In the paper we consider the higher order nonlinear Schrödinger equation (HNLS)

(1.1)

iu_{t}+au_{xx}+ibu_{x}+iu_{xxx}+\lambda|u|^{p}u+i\beta\bigl(|u|^{p}u\bigr)_{x}+i\gamma\bigl(|u|^{p}\bigr)_{x}u=f(t,x),

posed on the semiaxis
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
.
Here
a
,
b
,
\lambda
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are real constants,
p\geq 1
,
u=u(t,x)
and
f
are complex-valued functions (as well as all other functions below, unless otherwise stated).

For an arbitrary
T>0
in a half-strip
\Pi_{T}^{+}=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
we study for this equation an initial-boundary value problem with an initial condition

(1.2)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\geq 0,

and a boundary condition

(1.3)

u(t,0)=\mu(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

We consider two cases: either
\mu\equiv 0
or
p=1
,
\gamma=0
. In both cases results on existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence on the input data
u_{0}
,
f
,
\mu
(in the second case) of global weak solutions are established.

Equation (
1.1
) is a generalized combination of the well-known nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) in one spatial dimension

iu_{t}+au_{xx}+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0

and the Korteweg–de Vries equation (KdV)

u_{t}+bu_{x}+u_{xxx}+\beta uu_{x}=0.

It has various physical applications, in particular, it models propagation of femtosecond optical pulses in a monomode optical fiber, accounting for additional effects such as third order dispersion, self-steeping of the pulse, and self-frequency shift (see
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein).

Similarly to the NLS and KdV equations it can be shown that the norm in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
of a solution to the initial value problem for the HNLS equation is preserved, that is

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx=0.

It is well known, that for the NLS and KdV equations the following quantities, which are usually referred to as the energy, are also preserved by the solution flow:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(|u_{x}|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda}{a(p+2)}|u|^{p+2}\Bigr)\,dx,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(u_{x}^{2}-\frac{\beta}{3}u^{3}\Bigr)\,dx.

On the contrary, if one tries to derive the analogue of the conservation law for the energy in the case of the HNLS equation, the following identity is obtained:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl[|u_{x}|^{2}+\frac{i}{\beta+\gamma}\Bigl(\lambda-\frac{a(3\beta+2\gamma)}{3}\Bigr)u\bar{u}_{x}-\frac{2(3\beta+2\gamma)}{3(p+2)}|u|^{p+2}\Bigr]\,dx\\
-\frac{\gamma}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(|u|^{p}\bigr)_{x}\bigl(|u|^{2}\bigr)_{xx}\,dx=0.

Therefore, the integral of the expression in the square brackets is preserved only if either
\gamma=0
or
p=2
,
since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(|u|^{2}\bigr)_{x}\bigl(|u|^{2}\bigr)_{xx}\,dx=0.

Another serious obstacle in the study of such an equation is the non-smoothness of the function
|u|^{p}
except the special cases of even natural values of
p
. As a result, just in the case
p=2
, that is for the equation

iu_{t}+au_{xx}+ibu_{x}+iu_{xxx}+\lambda|u|^{2}u+i\beta\bigl(|u|^{2}u\bigr)_{x}+i\gamma(|u|^{2})_{x}u=0,

for the first time were obtained results on local and global well-posedness of the initial value problem. In particular, in
[
20
]
local well-posedness was proved for the initial data
u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>3/4
, and global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 1
, if
\beta+\gamma\neq 0
. In
[
22
]
the local result was improved up to
s\geq 1/4
. In
[
8
]
the same result was obtained for the analogous equation with variable (depending on
t
) coefficients. Under certain relation between the coefficients the local result was extended to the global one in
[
7
]
if
s>1/4
. In
[
9
]
global well-posedness was proved in certain weighted subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where power weights were effective at
\pm\infty
; the argument used global estimates from
[
20
]
. A unique continuation property was obtained in
[
10
]
. Local well-posedness of the periodic initial value problem in

was established in
[
23
]
. For the truncated NHLS equation (

) local well posedness in

[

]
.

For other values of

the initial value problem for the HNLS equation was considered in the recent paper
[
15

in the case

,
\gamma=0
. Results on existence and uniqueness of global weak solutions in certain weighted subspaces of

for power weights effective at

In

an initial-boundary value problem on a bounded interval

and the initial function

The initial-boundary value problem on the semiaxis

The notion of a weak solution of the considered problem is understood in the following sense.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L_{2,+}
,
\mu\in L_{2}(0,T)
,
f\in L_{1}(0,T;L_{2,+})
. A function
u\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2,+})
,
u_{x}\in L_{2}((0,T)\times(0,r))

\forall r>0
, is called a weak solution of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) if for any function
\phi\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{+})
,
\phi\big|_{t=T}=0
,
\phi\big|_{x=0}=\phi_{x}\big|_{x=0}\equiv 0
, the functions
|u|^{p}u\phi,|u|^{p}u\phi_{x},\gamma|u|^{p}u_{x}\phi\in L_{1}(\Pi_{T}^{+})
and the following equality holds:

(1.4)

\iint_{\Pi_{T}^{+}}\big[u(i\phi_{t}-a\phi_{xx}+ib\phi_{x}+i\phi_{xxx})-\lambda|u|^{p}u\phi+i\beta|u|^{p}u\phi_{x}+i\gamma|u|^{p}(u\phi)_{x}+f\phi\bigr]\,dxdt\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u_{0}\phi\big|_{t=0}\,dx+i\int_{0}^{T}\mu\phi_{xx}\big|_{x=0}\,dt=0.

In fact, the constructed in the paper solutions possess certain additional properties than the ones from this definition. Introduce certain notation.

We say that
\psi(x)
is an admissible weight function if
\psi
is an infinitely smooth positive function on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, such that

(1.5)

|\psi^{(j)}(x)|\leq c(j)\psi(x)\quad\text{for each natural\ }j\text{\ and\ }\forall x\geq 0.

Note that such a function obviously satisfies an inequality

(1.6)

\psi(x)\leq\psi(0)e^{c(1)x}\quad\forall x\geq 0.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that
\psi^{s}
for any

satisfies (
1.5
), so
\psi^{s}
is an admissible weight function. Any functions
\psi(x)\equiv e^{2\alpha x}
as well as
\psi(x)\equiv(1+x)^{2\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, are admissible weight functions, moreover,
\psi^{\prime}(x)
are also admissible weight functions if
\alpha>0
. As an another important example of admissible functions, we define
\rho_{0}(x)\equiv 1+\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan x
. Note that both
\rho_{0}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{0}
are admissible weight functions.

For an admissible weight function
\psi(x)
let

(1.7)

L_{2,+}^{\psi(x)}=\{\varphi(x):\varphi\psi^{1/2}(x)\in L_{2,+}\}.

Obviously,
L_{2,+}^{\rho_{0}(x)}=L_{2,+}
.

Let both
\psi(x)
and
\psi^{\prime}(x)
be admissible weight functions. We construct solutions of the considered problem in spaces
X_{w}^{\psi(x)}(\Pi_{T}^{+})
, consisting of functions

such that

(1.8)

u\in C_{w}([0,T];L_{2,+}^{\psi(x)}),\quad u_{x}\in L_{2}(0,T;L_{2,+}^{\psi^{\prime}(x)}).

For auxiliary linear results we also use spaces
X^{\psi(x)}(\Pi_{T}^{+})
, where in comparison with
X_{w}^{\psi(x)}(\Pi_{T}^{+})
the weak continuity with respect to

in (

) is substituted by the strong one.

Let also

(1.9)

\sigma^{+}(f;T)=\sup\limits_{x_{0}\geq 0}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{0}+1}|f(t,x)|^{2}\,dxdt\Bigr)^{1/2}.

The main results of the paper are the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

,

2.
Preliminaries

The following interpolating inequality for weighted Sobolev spaces is crucial for the study.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\psi_{1}(x)
,
\psi_{2}(x)
be two admissible weight functions,
q\in[2,+\infty]
,

(2.1)

s=s(q)=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2q}.

Then for every function
\varphi(x)
satisfying
\bigl(|\varphi^{\prime}|+|\varphi|\bigr)\psi_{1}^{1/2}(x)\in L_{2,+}
,
\varphi\psi_{2}^{1/2}(x)\in L_{2,+}
, the following inequality holds:

(2.2)

\bigl\|\varphi\psi_{1}^{s}\psi_{2}^{1/2-s}\bigr\|_{L_{q,+}}\leq c\bigl\|\bigl(|\varphi^{\prime}|+|\varphi|\bigr)\psi_{1}^{1/2}\bigr\|^{2s}_{L_{2,+}}\bigl\|\varphi\psi_{2}^{1/2}\bigr\|_{L_{2,+}}^{1-2s},

where the constant
c
depends on
q
and the properties of the functions
\psi_{j}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\varphi
is a smooth decaying at
+\infty
function (for example,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{exp,+}
).

First consider the case
q=+\infty
, then the proof is based on a simple inequality for functions
f\in W_{1,+}^{1}

\sup\limits_{x>0}|f(x)|\leq\int|f^{\prime}(x)|\,dx,

applied to
f\equiv\varphi^{2}\psi_{1}^{1/2}\psi_{2}^{1/2}
.
In fact, the properties of admissible weight functions imply that

\sup\limits_{x>0}|\varphi^{2}\psi_{1}^{1/2}\psi_{2}^{1/2}|\leq 2\int|\varphi\varphi^{\prime}|\psi_{1}^{1/2}\psi_{2}^{1/2}\,dx+c\int|\varphi|^{2}\psi_{1}^{1/2}\psi_{2}^{1/2}\,dx\\
\leq c_{1}\Bigl(\int\bigl(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\varphi|^{2}\bigr)\psi_{1}\,dx\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\int|\varphi|^{2}\psi_{2}\,dx\Bigr)^{1/2},

whence the desired result follows.

If
q<+\infty
then

\|\varphi\psi_{1}^{s}\psi_{2}^{1/2-s}\|_{L_{q,+}}\leq\Bigl(\sup\limits_{x>0}|\varphi|\psi_{1}^{1/4}\psi_{2}^{1/4}\Bigr)^{(q-2)/q}\Bigl(\int|\varphi|^{2}\psi_{2}\,dx\Bigr)^{1/q}\\
\leq\Bigl(\int\bigl(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\varphi|^{2}\bigr)\psi_{1}\,dx\Bigr)^{(q-2)/(4q)}\Bigl(\int|\varphi|^{2}\psi_{2}\,dx\Bigr)^{(q+2)/(4q)}.

∎

Inequality (
2.2
) is the generalization for weighted spaces on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the well-known interpolating inequality

(2.3)

\|\varphi\|_{L_{q}(I)}\leq c(q,I)\bigl(\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2s}\|\varphi\|_{L_{2}(I)}^{1-2s}+\|\varphi\|_{L_{2}(I)}\bigr),

where
s
is given by (
2.1
), valid for
q\in[2,+\infty]
and any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Consider a linear problem in
\Pi_{T}^{+}

(2.4)

\displaystyle iu_{t}+au_{xx}+ibu_{x}+iu_{xxx}=f(t,x),

(2.5)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u(t,0)=\mu(t).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{exp,+}
,
\mu\in C^{\infty}[0,T]
,
f\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{exp,+})
and

(2.6)

\mu^{(l)}(0)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}(0)\quad\forall l,

where

(2.7)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{0}(x)\equiv u_{0}(x),\quad\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}(x)\equiv-i\partial_{t}^{l-1}f(0,x)+\bigl(ia\partial_{x}^{2}-b\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}^{3}\bigr)\widetilde{\Phi}_{l-1}(x),\ l\geq 1.

Then there exists a unique solution to problem (
2.1
), (
2.2
)
u\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{exp,+})
.

Proof.

Extend the functions
u_{0}
and
f
to the the whole real line such that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,
f\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S})
and consider the initial value problem

(2.8)

iw_{t}+aw_{xx}+ibw_{x}+iw_{xxx}=f(t,x),\quad w(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

The unique solution of this problem
w(t,x)
in the space
C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S})
is constructed via the Fourier transform and is given by a formula

(2.9)

\widehat{w}(t,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi)e^{i(\xi^{3}-a\xi^{2}-b\xi)t}-i\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi)e^{i(\xi^{3}-a\xi^{2}-b\xi)(t-\tau)}\,d\tau,

where

\widehat{w}(t,\xi)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}w(t,x)\,dx

with the similar notation for
\widehat{u}_{0}
and
\widehat{f}
.

Next, let
w_{j}(t,x)\equiv\partial_{x}^{j}w(t,x)
for some
j
. Then the function
w_{j}
is the solution of the problem of (
2.8
) type, where
u_{0}
,
f
are replaced by
u_{0}^{(j)}
,
\partial_{x}^{j}f
. Let
m\geq 3
,
\psi(x)\equiv x^{m}
(note that this function is not an admissible weight one). Multiplying the corresponding equation for
w_{j}
by
2\bar{w}_{j}(t,x)\psi(x)
, integrating over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and taking the imaginary part, we derive an equality (here we use that
\psi^{(l)}(0)=0
for
l\leq 2
)

(2.10)

\frac{d}{dt}\int|w_{j}|^{2}\psi\,dx+3\int|w_{jx}|^{2}\psi^{\prime}\,dx=2a\operatorname{{Im}}\int w_{jx}\bar{w}_{j}\psi^{\prime}\,dx\\
+\int|w_{j}|^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\,dx+b\int|w_{j}|^{2}\psi^{\prime}\,dx+2\operatorname{{Im}}\int\partial_{x}^{j}f\bar{w}_{j}\psi\,dx.

Since

\Bigl|2a\operatorname{{Im}}\int w_{jx}\bar{w}_{j}\psi^{\prime}\,dx\Bigr|\leq\int|w_{jx}|^{2}\psi^{\prime}\,dx+a^{2}\int|w_{j}|^{2}\psi^{\prime}\,dx,

it follows from (
2.10
) that

(2.11)

\frac{d}{dt}\int|w_{j}|^{2}\psi\,dx\leq\int|w_{j}|^{2}\bigl((a^{2}+|b|)\psi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\bigr)\,dx+2\operatorname{{Im}}\int\partial_{x}^{j}f\bar{w}_{j}\psi\,dx.

Fix
\alpha>0
and let
n\geq 3
. For any
m\in[3,n]
multiplying the corresponding inequality (
2.11
) by
(2\alpha)^{m}/(m!)
and summing by
m
we obtain that for

z_{n}(t)\equiv\int\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\frac{(2\alpha x)^{m}}{m!}|w_{j}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx

because of the special choice of the functions
\psi
inequalities

z^{\prime}_{n}(t)\leq cz_{n}(t)+c,\quad z_{n}(0)\leq c,

hold uniformly with respect to

, whence it follows that

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\int e^{2\alpha x}|\partial_{x}^{j}w(t,x)|^{2}\,dx<\infty.

As a result,
w\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{exp,+})
.

Next, let
\mu_{0}(t)\equiv\mu(t)-w(t,0)
. Note that
\mu_{0}\in C^{\infty}[0,T]
and
\mu_{0}^{(l)}(0)=0

\forall l
because of the compatibility conditions (
2.6
), (
2.7
). Consider in
\Pi_{T}^{+}
an initial-boundary value problem

(2.12)

iv_{t}+av_{xx}+ibv_{x}+iv_{xxx}=0,\quad v(0,x)=0,\quad v(t,0)=\mu_{0}(t).

Let
\Psi(t,x)\equiv\mu_{0}(t)\eta(1-x)
,
F(t,x)\equiv-(i\Psi_{t}+a\Psi_{xx}+ib\Psi_{x}+i\Psi_{xxx})(t,x)
. It is obvious that
F\in C^{\infty}([0,T]:\mathcal{S}_{exp,+})
and
\partial_{t}^{l}F(0,x)\equiv 0

\forall l
. Problem (
2.12
) is equivalent to a problem

(2.13)

iV_{t}+aV_{xx}+ibV_{x}+iV_{xxx}=F(t,x),\quad V(0,x)=0,\quad V(t,0)=0

for the function
V(t,x)\equiv v(t,x)-\Psi(t,x)

Apply the Galerkin method. Let
\{\varphi_{j}(x):j=1,2\dots\}
be a set of linearly independent functions complete in the space
\{\varphi\in H^{3}_{+}:\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0\}
. Construct an approximate solution of problem (

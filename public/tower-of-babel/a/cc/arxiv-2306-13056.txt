Gain-loss-induced non-Abelian Bloch braids
Bikashkali Midya

Abstract
Onsite gain-loss-induced topological braiding principle of non-Hermitian energy bands is theoretically formulated in multiband lattice models with Hermitian hopping amplitudes. Braid phase transition occurs when the gain-loss parameter is tuned across exceptional point degeneracy. Laboratory realizable effective-Hamiltonians are proposed to realize braid groups 𝔹 2 \mathbb{B}_{2} and 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}_{3} of two and three bands, respectively. While 𝔹 2 \mathbb{B}_{2} is trivially Abelian, the group 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}_{3} features non-Abelian braiding and energy permutation originating from the collective behavior of multiple exceptional points. Phase diagrams with respect to lattice parameters to realize braid group generators and their non-commutativity are shown. The proposed theory is conducive to synthesizing exceptional materials for applications in topological computation and information processing.

Before describing the gain-loss-induced braiding mechanism, we first brief about general ideas of band spectrum braids. A braid is a disjoint collection of interlacing trajectories of
N
non-degenerate complex energy bands
\{E_{1},E_{2},\cdots,E_{N}\}
as
k
evolves in the Brillouin zone (BZ)
k_{0}\leq k\leq k_{0}+2\pi
; bands are ordered here according to their real parts at an arbitrary
k_{0}
. This can be mathematically defined by the mapping
E_{n}(k):BZ\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\times BZ
for
n\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
; here,
\mathbb{C}
represents the complex energy plane. Every braid in
\mathbb{B}_{N}
can be obtained as a product of
(N-1)
generators
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots,\tau_{N-1}
and their inverses
\tau_{1}^{-1},\tau_{2}^{-1},\cdots,\tau_{N-1}^{-1}
. These elementary braids
\tau_{n}
or
\tau_{n}^{-1}
consist of only a single twist between neighboring bands
E_{n}
and
E_{n+1}
in the positive or negative direction. The identity braid with no twist is denoted here by
\tau^{0}
. Collection of braids forms a braid group
\mathbb{B}_{N}
under concatenation (the braid
\tau_{n}\tau_{\ell}
is obtained by joining end points of
\tau_{n}
with initials of
\tau_{\ell}
), such that
\tau_{n}\tau_{\ell}=\tau_{\ell}\tau_{n}
for
|n-\ell|>1
and
\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1}\neq\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n}

Kauffman2013
. The braid group formed by
N>2
bands is thus non-Abelian. A link or knot is formed when a braid is closed.

Gain-loss-induced braid group
\mathbb{B}_{2}

A two-band non-Hermitian lattice is considered with
\alpha
and
(\beta,\delta)
being intra-dimer and
m

\pm i\gamma

H(k)=\begin{pmatrix}2\delta\sin mk+i\gamma&\alpha+\beta e^{-imk}\\
\alpha+\beta e^{imk}&-i\gamma\end{pmatrix},\quad

(1)

with two energy bands

\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-10.00002ptE_{1,2}(k)=\delta\sin mk\\
\quad\mp\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+2\alpha\beta\cos mk+(i\gamma+2\delta\sin mk)^{2}}.\end{array}

(2)

In general,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
either braid
m

(E_{1},E_{2})\rightarrow(E_{2},E_{1})

m
is odd, and trivial permutation
(E_{1},E_{2})\rightarrow(E_{1},E_{2})
occurs for even
m

\mathbb{B}_{2}

2

\mbox{det}(H(k)-E)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma=\pm(\beta-\alpha)\quad\mbox{at}\quad k=\pi/m,\\
\gamma=\pm(\beta+\alpha)\quad\mbox{at}\quad k=0,\end{array}

(3)

for
\delta\neq 0

and

Different braid diagrams are distinguished by the topological integer invariant
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
defined by the spectral winding number with respect to the energy at an EP

\nu=\int\limits_{k\in BZ}\frac{dk}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{dk}\ln\det\left(H(k)-\mbox{EP}\right).

(4)

The braid degree
\nu
describes how many times the two bands wind around an EP, with the sign indicating the handedness of braids. We obtain
\nu\neq 0~(=0)
for a non-trivial (trivial) braid with
\nu(\tau_{1}^{\pm m})=\pm m
. Phase diagram in the
(\beta,\gamma)

1

3
). While zero gain loss always results in a trivial braid, a non-zero critical gain loss is required to induce non-trivial braids. Nontrivial braids are also absent whenever the long-range coupling, either
\delta

\beta

Gain-loss induced non-Abelian braid group
\mathbb{B}_{3}
.
We now consider a minimal model of non-Hermitian lattice of trimers with Bloch Hamiltonian given by

H(k)=\begin{pmatrix}-2\delta\sin 2mk+i\gamma&\alpha&\beta e^{-imk}\\
\alpha&\mbox{v}&\alpha\\
\beta e^{imk}&\alpha&-i\gamma\end{pmatrix}.\quad

(6)

Here
\alpha

\beta
is the
m

\pm i\gamma

E_{3}

One of the remarkable features of three-bands braiding is that the concatenation of two braids is non-Abelian. For example,
\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\neq\tau_{2}\tau_{1}
as the braid diagrams for these two products are different. (Particular examples are shown in Fig.
4

\nu(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})=\nu(\tau_{2}\tau_{1})=2
. Inequivalence of
\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{2}\tau_{1}

Zhong2018
;
Pap2018
;
Guo2023
). Note the different orders in which two EPs are encircled for the two braids: anticlockwise loop
\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
encircles EP
1

2
, the order is reversed for
\tau_{2}\tau_{1}
(see Fig.
4
). Non-Abelian braiding has an important observable effect in terms of energy permutations; one obtains
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})\rightarrow(E_{2},E_{3},E_{1})

\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
and
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})\rightarrow(E_{3},E_{1},E_{2})
for
\tau_{2}\tau_{1}

4

can be generated by considering long-range coupling
\pm i\delta

(2m)
-th nearest-neighbor unit cells and coupling

m

3

connection

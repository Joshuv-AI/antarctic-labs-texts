Abstract
. We develop local forms of Ramsey-theoretic dichotomies for block sequences in infinite-dimensional vector spaces, analogous to Mathias’ selective coideal form of Silver’s theorem for analytic partitions of [ ℕ ] ∞ [\mathbb{N}]^{\infty} . Under large cardinals, these results are extended to partitions in 𝐋 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R}) and 𝐋 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R}) -generic filters of block sequences are characterized. Variants of these results are also established for block sequences in Banach spaces and for projections in the Calkin algebra.

1.
Introduction

Ramsey-theoretic techniques have a long history of use in Banach space theory, see e.g.,
[
4
]
. Most relevant for the present work is Gowers’ dichotomy for infinite block sequences in Banach spaces:

Theorem

(Gowers
[
18
]

[
19

)
.

Let
B
be an infinite-dimensional Banach space with a Schauder basis. If
\mathbb{A}
is an analytic set of normalized block sequences, then for any
\Delta>0

such that either

(i)

every normalized block subsequence of
Y
is in
\mathbb{A}^{c}
, or

(ii)

II has a strategy in the Gowers game
G^{*}[Y]
for playing into
\mathbb{A}_{\Delta}
.

Loosely speaking, this result says that for
\mathbb{A}
as described, there is a block sequence

such that either all of

\mathbb{A}
, or there is a wealth of block subsequences of
Y
which are within a small perturbation of
\mathbb{A}
. This was used, together with work of Komorowski and Tomczak-Jaegerman
[
23
]
, to solve (affirmatively) the homogeneous space problem.

In the setting of a discrete countably infinite-dimensional vector space

Theorem

(Rosendal

If
\mathbb{A}

(i)

for playing into
\mathbb{A}^{c}

(ii)

\mathbb{A}

These dichotomies are analogues, in the Banach space and vector space settings, respectively, of the following result for partitions of
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}

Theorem

(Silver
[
38
]
)
.

If
\mathbb{A}\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}

y\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}

\mathbb{A}
.

While the theory of topological Ramsey spaces, in the sense of
[
40
]
, encompasses many variations on this result, the dichotomies of Gowers and Rosendal highlighted above do not fall into this framework.

An important generalization of Silver’s theorem is the following “local” Ramsey theorem, showing that the witness

Theorem

(Mathias
[
30

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
be a selective coideal. If
\mathbb{A}\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}

with either all of its further infinite subsets disjoint from, or contained in,
\mathbb{A}

By passing to a forcing extension resulting from the Lévy collapse of a Mahlo cardinal, Mathias extended these results to all partitions
\mathbb{A}
which are “reasonably definable”, that is, in the definable closure of the reals
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})

[

37

[
31
]

[
12

The upshot of obtaining these local results is two-fold: We clearly isolate the combinatorial properties which enable the original dichotomies, and we obtain greater control over the witnesses to said dichotomies.

This latter point was used by Todorcevic

to characterize, under large cardinal hypotheses, selective ultrafilters as being exactly those which are generic for

2.
Families of block sequences

Fix a countable field
F
, a countably infinite-dimensional
F
-vector space
E
, and an Hamel
F
-basis
(e_{n})
for
E
. Typically we will think of
F
as a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
, but this is not necessary;
F
may even be finite. Given
v\in E
, say with
v=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}e_{n}
, let
\mathrm{supp}(v)=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:a_{n}\neq 0\}
, the
support
of
v
. We write
n<v
if
n<\min(\mathrm{supp}(v))
and
v<w
if
\max(\mathrm{supp}(v))<\min(\mathrm{supp}(w))
.

We say that a (finite or infinite) sequence
(x_{n})
of non-zero vectors in
E
is a
block sequence
(with respect to
(e_{n})
) if for all
n
,
x_{n}<x_{n+1}
. If
\vec{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
is a finite block sequence, let
\mathrm{supp}(\vec{x})=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathrm{supp}(x_{i})
, and for
X
any block sequence, let
\langle X\rangle=\linspan(X)\setminus\{0\}
. We will abuse notation and write
E
for
E\setminus\{0\}
, and use “vector” to mean non-zero vector.

Let
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
be the collection of all infinite block sequences in
E
, which we consider as a subspace of
E^{\mathbb{N}}
, where
E
has the discrete topology. It is easy to check that
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
E^{\mathbb{N}}
, and thus a Polish space. Let
\mathrm{bb}^{<\infty}(E)
be the collection of all finite block sequences in
E
.

For
X=(x_{n})
and
Y=(y_{n})
in
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
, we write
X\preceq Y
if
(x_{n})
is a block sequence with respect to
(y_{n})
, sometimes called a
block subsequence
of
Y
, or equivalently (for
block
sequences),
\langle X\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
. We write
X\preceq^{*}Y
if for some
m
,
X/m\preceq Y
, where
X/m
is the tail of
X
with supports above
m
. For
\vec{x}\in\mathrm{bb}^{<\infty}(E)
, write
X/\vec{x}
for
X/\max(\mathrm{supp}(\vec{x}))
. Note that the orderings
\preceq
and
\preceq^{*}
fail to be antisymmetric, but are reflexive and transitive.

We will make repeated use of the following order-theoretic notions: A subset
D
of a pre-order
(P,\leq)
(that is,
\leq
is reflexive and transitive) is
dense
if for all
p\in P
, there is a

with
q\leq p
. It is, moreover,

, if whenever
q\leq p\in D
, then
q\in D
. Elements
p
and
q
in
P
are
compatible
if they have a common lower bound in
P
, and
incompatible
otherwise.

Compatibility in
(\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E),\preceq)
is equivalent to that in
(\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E),\preceq^{*})
and we write
X\bot Y
when
X
and
Y
are incompatible. The following observation shows that
(\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E),\preceq)
can be identified with a dense suborder of the lattice of all infinite-dimensional subspaces of
E
. In particular,
X
and
Y
are compatible if and only if
\langle X\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
is infinite-dimensional.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X
is an infinite-dimensional subspace of
E
, then
X
contains an infinite block sequence.

Proof.

By taking appropriate linear combinations, one can show that for any
N
,
X
contains an infinite-dimensional subspace whose supports are above
N
. From this, it is easy to inductively construct a block sequence in
X
.
∎

Throughout, when we speak of a
family

\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
, we mean a non-empty subset which is closed upwards with respect to
\preceq^{*}
. For

, we denote by
\mathcal{H}\upharpoonright X=\{Y\in\mathcal{H}:Y\preceq X\}
. A

is a family such that for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}
, there is a
Z\in\mathcal{F}
with
Z\preceq X
and
Z\preceq Y
.

.

(a)

Given a descending sequence

A family

-family

3.
Games with vectors and a local Rosendal dichotomy

The
Gowers game
played below
X\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
, denoted
G[X]
, is defined as follows: Two players, I and II, alternate with I going first and playing block sequences
X_{k}\preceq X
, and II responding with vectors
y_{k}\in\langle X_{k}\rangle
subject to the constraint
y_{k}<y_{k+1}
. The block sequence
(y_{k})
is the
outcome
of a play of the game. Given
\vec{x}\in\mathrm{bb}^{<\infty}(E)
and

Proof.

It is easy to see that strategies

5.
Constructions of filters in

In this section we show how to construct filters

Definition 5.1

(a)

A

We use the following notational conventions for versions of Martin’s Axiom: for

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 5 in
[

]

(

Theorem 5.3
.

(\mathfrak{t}^{*}=2^{\aleph_{0}})
There exists a strategic

-filter in

Proof.

Fix enumerations:

Lemma 5.4
.

For

(p^{+})
-family, forcing with

(p)
-property, respectively).

\mathcal{H}

is

-closed, and thus adds no new reals. We use this fact implicitly in what follows. Let
\mathcal{G}
be as described. To see that

-dense below some

6.
Connections to filters on a countable set

We would like to relate the filters discussed thus far to filters of subsets of a countable set. In our case, the countable set will be
E\setminus\{0\}
, but we will call these filters on
E
.

Definition 6.1
.

A filter
\mathcal{F}
on
E
is a
block filter
if it has a base consisting of sets of the form
\langle X\rangle
, for
X\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
.

It is tempting to define a
block ultrafilter
on
E
to be a block filter on
E
which is also an ultrafilter. However, unless
|F|=2
, such objects do not exist: Let
\mathcal{F}
be a block filter on
E
. For
A_{0},A_{1}\subseteq E
given in Example
2.4
, note that
E=A_{0}\cup A_{0}
. But, for every
X\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
,
\langle X\rangle\cap A_{0}\neq\emptyset
and
\langle X\rangle\cap A_{1}\neq\emptyset
, so neither set can be in
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
\mathrm{FIN}
be the set of nonempty finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. An ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathrm{FIN}
is said to be an
ordered union ultrafilter

[
11
]
if it has a base consisting of sets of the form
\langle X\rangle=\{x_{n_{0}}\cup\cdots\cup x_{n_{k}}:n_{0}<\cdots<n_{k}\}
, where
X=(x_{n})
is a block sequence in
\mathrm{FIN}
(that is, for all
n
,
\max(x_{n})<\min(x_{n+1})
). The set of infinite block sequences in
\mathrm{FIN}
is denoted by
\mathrm{FIN}^{[\infty]}
. We have, perhaps, overloaded the notation
\langle X\rangle
, but its intended interpretation should be clear from context. If
X=(x_{n})\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
, denote by
\widetilde{X}=(\mathrm{supp}(x_{n}))\in\mathrm{FIN}^{[\infty]}
.

If
|F|=2
, then
E\setminus\{0\}
can be identified with
\mathrm{FIN}
via each vector’s support. Sums of vectors in block position corresponds to unions of their supports. As a consequence of Hindman’s Theorem (Corollary 3.3 in
[
20
]
), one can construct (under hypotheses such as
\mathsf{CH}
or
\mathsf{MA}
) ordered union ultrafilters on
\mathrm{FIN}
; these will correspond to block ultrafilters on
E
.

For the remainder of this section we will consider a general countable field
F
. The map which takes a vector to its support will provide the connection between this general setting and
\mathrm{FIN}
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a block filter on
E
.

(a)

A subset
D\subseteq E
is
\mathcal{F}
-dense
if for every
\langle X\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
, there is a
Z\preceq X
with
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D
.

(b)

\mathcal{F}
is
full
if whenever
D\subseteq E
is
\mathcal{F}
-dense, we have that
D\in\mathcal{F}
.

As in the case for filters in
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
, every full block filter on
E
is maximal with respect to containment amongst block filters.

The map
s:X\mapsto\langle X\rangle
takes block sequences to subsets of
E
. It is straightforward to show that the image of a (full) filter in
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
under
s
generates a (full) block filter on
E
and that the inverse image of a (full) block filter on
E
is a (full) filter in
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
. By Theorem
5.3
(or Lemma
5.4
), it is consistent that such filters exist.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a full block filter on
E
, and let

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})=\{A\subseteq\mathrm{FIN}:\exists F\in\mathcal{F}(A\supseteq\{\mathrm{supp}(v):v\in F\})\}.

Then,
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
is an ordered union ultrafilter on
\mathrm{FIN}
.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
, say with
A\supseteq\{\mathrm{supp}(v):v\in F\}
and
B\supseteq\{\mathrm{supp}(v):v\in G\}
, for
F,G\in\mathcal{F}
. Then,

\displaystyle A\cap B

\displaystyle\supseteq\{s:\exists v\in F\exists w\in G(s=\mathrm{supp}(v)=\mathrm{supp}(w))\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\{\mathrm{supp}(v):v\in F\cap G\},

which is in
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
, as
F\cap G\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
is upwards closed by definition, we have that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
is a filter on
\mathrm{FIN}
. As
\mathcal{F}
is a block filter, it follows that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
has a base consisting of sets
\langle\widetilde{X}\rangle
for
X\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
.

It remains to show that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
is an ultrafilter. Take
A\subseteq\mathrm{FIN}
such that for all
B\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
,
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Let

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle=\{v\in E:\mathrm{supp}(v)\in A\}

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\{v\in E:\mathrm{supp}(v)\notin A\}.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that for all
\langle X\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
, there is a
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\langle X\rangle
with
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is full, there is a
\langle Z\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
with
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{1}
. Then
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
, but
A\cap\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

Thus, there is some
\langle X\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
such that for no
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\langle X\rangle
is
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{1}
. Take
\langle Y\rangle\in\mathcal{F}\upharpoonright\langle X\rangle
. By Hindman’s Theorem applied to
\langle\widetilde{Y}\rangle
, there is a
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathrm{FIN}^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\subseteq\langle\widetilde{Y}\rangle
and either (i)
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\subseteq A
, or (ii)
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\subseteq\langle\widetilde{Y}\rangle\setminus A
.

Take any
Z\preceq Y
in
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
whose supports agree with
\widetilde{Z}
, then if (ii) holds,
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{1}
, contrary to what we know about
\langle X\rangle
. Thus,
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\subseteq A
and
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{0}
. Since
\langle Y\rangle\in\mathcal{F}\upharpoonright\langle X\rangle
was arbitrary, we have that
D_{0}
is
\mathcal{F}
-dense. As
\mathcal{F}
is full, we can find a
\langle Z\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
with
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq D_{0}
. Then,
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
and
\langle\widetilde{Z}\rangle\subseteq A
, so
A\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
6.3
and the Corollary on p. 87 of
[
11
]
we have:

Corollary 6.4
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a full filter on
E
, then

\displaystyle\min(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\{\{n=\min(\mathrm{supp}(v)):v\in F\}:F\in\mathcal{F}\},

\displaystyle\max(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\{\{n=\max(\mathrm{supp}(v)):v\in F\}:F\in\mathcal{F}\}

are selective ultrafilters on
\mathbb{N}
.

As it is consistent that there are no selective ultrafilters
[
24
]
, we have:

.

, and thus full filters in

\mathsf{ZFC}
.

An ordered union ultrafilter

]

The ordering

For

[\vec{x},A,\sigma]=\{Y\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E):Y\text{ is an outcome of $F[\vec{x},A]$ where I follows $\sigma$}\}.

We collect some basic properties of

in the following lemma:

Lemma 7.5
.

(a)

If
(\vec{y},B,\tau)\leq(\vec{x},A,\sigma)
in
\mathbb{P}
, then
[\vec{y},B,\tau]\subseteq[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
. Conversely, if
[\vec{y},B,\tau]\subseteq[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
, then
(\vec{y},B,\tau)
is below
(\vec{x},A,\sigma)
in the separative quotient of
\mathbb{P}
.

(b)

If
(\vec{x},A,\sigma)\in\mathbb{P}
, then the set
[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
is (topologically) closed.

(c)

If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E)
is a filter, then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})
is
\sigma
-centered.

Proof.

(a) The first part follows from our observations about the ordering on strategies for I. For the converse, suppose that
[\vec{y},B,\tau]\subseteq[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
. Then, every outcome of
F[\vec{y},B]
where I follows
\tau
is an outcome of
F[\vec{x},A]
where I follows
\sigma
. In particular,
\vec{y}=\vec{x}^{\smallfrown}(y_{0},\ldots,y_{k-1})
where
y_{0},\ldots,y_{k-1}
are the first
k
moves by II in a round of
F[\vec{x},A]
where I follows
\sigma
.

We claim that
B/m\subseteq A
, where
m=\max\{\mathrm{supp}(\vec{y}),\tau(\emptyset)\}
and
B/m=\{y\in B:y>m\}
. To see this, note that for any
y\in B/m
, there is an outcome
\vec{y}^{\smallfrown}y^{\smallfrown}Z\in[\vec{y},B,\tau]
and thus in
[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
. In particular,

.

By our choice of
m
,
\tau\upharpoonright B/m=\tau
. So,
(\vec{y},B/m,\tau)\leq(\vec{x},A,\sigma)
and the sets of extensions of
(\vec{y},B/m,\tau)
and
(\vec{y},B,\tau)
coincide. Thus, their images in the separative quotient of
\mathbb{P}
coincide.

(b) If
Y=(y_{n})\notin[\vec{x},A,\sigma]
, then either
\vec{x}\not\sqsubseteq Y
, or there is some least

such that

is not a valid response to

or

(c) Suppose that
(\vec{x},A,\sigma)
and
(\vec{x},B,\tau)
are both in

. There are

with

and
\langle Y\rangle\subseteq B
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a filter, there is a

below both. Let
\rho
be the strategy for I in

given by

. Then,
(\vec{x},Z,\rho)\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})
and extends both
(\vec{x},A,\sigma)
and
(\vec{x},B,\tau)
. Since there are only countably many such

, this shows that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})
is
\sigma
-centered.
∎

Let

By Lemma

Let

In what follows, we strengthen the hypotheses on the basis

, asserting that there is some

such that for all

being a Schauder basis of the completion

of

, cf. Proposition 1.1.9

. The infimum of all such

as above is called the

of

. The following Lemma about perturbations of blocks sequences appears to be well-known.

Lemma 8.16
.

For any
\Delta>0
, there is a
\Gamma>0
such that whenever
X=(x_{n}),X^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{n})\in\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}_{1}(E)
satisfy
d(X^{\prime},X)\leq\Gamma
, then
[X^{\prime}]\subseteq[X]_{\Delta}
. In fact, if
Y^{\prime}\in[X^{\prime}]
, then
\widetilde{Y}\in[X]
and
d(Y^{\prime},\widetilde{Y})\leq\Delta
, where
\widetilde{Y}
is the normalization of the image of
Y^{\prime}
under the linear map extending
x^{\prime}_{n}\mapsto x_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta>0
. If
K
is the basis constant of
(e_{n})
, then by Lemma 1.3.5 in
[
2
]
, the basis constant of
X
is
\leq K
. Pick
\Gamma>0
with
\sum_{n\geq m}\gamma_{n}\leq\min\{1/6K,\delta_{m}/8K\}
. For
X^{\prime}=(x_{n}^{\prime})
with
d(X^{\prime},X)\leq\Gamma
, consider the map on the completions
T:\overline{\langle X\rangle}\to\overline{\langle X^{\prime}\rangle}
extending
x_{n}\mapsto x_{n}^{\prime}
.
T
is a bounded linear isomorphism, as whenever
v=\sum a_{n}x_{n}\in\overline{\langle X\rangle}
,

\displaystyle\|Tv\|-\|v\|\leq\|Tv-v\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sum a_{n}x_{n}^{\prime}-\sum a_{n}x_{n}\|\leq\sup_{n}|a_{n}|\sum\|x_{n}^{\prime}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2K\|v\|\sum\|x_{n}^{\prime}-x_{n}\|\leq 1/3\|v\|,

and so
\|T\|\leq 4/3
. Using
1/\|T^{-1}\|=\inf_{\|v\|=1}\|Tv\|
, we have
\|T^{-1}\|\leq 3/2
.

As the basis constant for
X^{\prime}
is also
\leq K
, for

, we have that
\|T^{-1}v^{\prime}-v^{\prime}\|\leq\delta_{m}/4\|v^{\prime}\|
by a similar argument as above.

If

is a unit vector, then we also have that

|1-\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}v^{\prime}\|}|\leq\|T\|\|T^{-1}v^{\prime}-v^{\prime}\|\leq(4/3)(\delta_{m}/4)\leq\delta_{m}/3.

For

, we claim

, where
\widetilde{Y}
is the normalization of
Y=(y_{m})=(T^{-1}(y_{m}^{\prime}))
. Observe that

\displaystyle\|y_{m}-\frac{1}{\|y_{m}\|}y_{m}\|\leq\left|1-\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}(y^{\prime}_{m})\|}\right|\|T^{-1}(y^{\prime}_{m})\|\leq(\delta_{m}/3)(3/2)=\delta_{m}/2.

Thus, for all

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}_{m}-\frac{1}{\|y_{m}\|}y_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\|y_{m}^{\prime}-y_{m}\|+\|y_{m}-\frac{1}{\|y_{m}\|}y_{m}\|\leq\delta_{m}.

We focus on a particular variant of this which is of significant interest.

Let
H

Denote by

\mathcal{P}(H)
.

Definition 9.1
.

(a)

For projections

Lemma 9.3

We conclude this section by describing a hoped-for application of our machinery and its limitations. A
state

\tau
on
\mathcal{B}(H)
is a linear functional on
\mathcal{B}(H)
which is
positive
, that is,
\tau(T^{*}T)\geq 0
for all
T
, and satisfies
\tau(I)=1
, where
I
is the identity operator. The set of states forms a weak*-compact convex subset of the dual of
\mathcal{B}(H)
and thus has extreme points, called
pure states
. These definitions generalize to any unital C*-algebra, including
\mathcal{C}(H)
.

A state on
\mathcal{B}(H)
is
singular
if it vanishes on
\mathcal{K}(H)
. Composing with the quotient map
\pi:\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{C}(H)
yields a bijective correspondence between singular pure states on
\mathcal{B}(H)
and pure states on
\mathcal{C}(H)
.

For any choice of orthonormal basis
(f_{k})
for
H
, and any ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, the functional defined by
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}(T)=\lim_{k\to\mathcal{U}}\langle Tf_{k},f_{k}\rangle
is a pure state which is singular if and only if
\mathcal{U}
is non-principal (cf. Theorem 4.21 and Example 6.1 in
[
15
]
). Such pure states are said to be
diagonalizable
. On an abelian C*-algebra, pure states coincide with characters, so the aforementioned
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}
restricts to a pure state on the atomic maximal abelian self-adjoint subalgebra (or
masa
) generated by the rank-one projections corresponding to the
f_{k}
. The following problem asks to what extent this is true of all pure states:

Problem

(Kadison–Singer
[
21
]
)

Does every pure state on
\mathcal{B}(H)
restrict to a pure state on some (atomic or continuous) masa?

Anderson conjectured that not only is the answer to this question “yes”, but that every pure state is of the form
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}
, for some choice of orthonormal basis
(f_{k})
and ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
:

Conjecture

(Anderson
[
3
]
)
.

Every pure state on
\mathcal{B}(H)
is diagonalizable.

Akemann and Weaver
[

showed that the above problem of Kadison and Singer has a negative answer, and thus Anderson’s conjecture is false, assuming
\mathsf{CH}

By the recent positive solution

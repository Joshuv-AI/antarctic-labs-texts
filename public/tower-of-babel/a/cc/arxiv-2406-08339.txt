Microstructures in a two-dimensional frustrated spin system: Scaling regimes and a discrete-to-continuum limit
Janusz Ginster, Melanie Koser, Barbara Zwicknagl

Abstract
We study pattern formation within the J 1 J_{1} - J 3 J_{3} - spin model on a two-dimensional square lattice in the case of incompatible (ferromagnetic) boundary conditions on the spin field. We derive the discrete-to-continuum Γ \Gamma -limit at the helimagnetic/ferromagnetic transition point, which turns out to be characterized by a singularly perturbed multiwell energy functional on gradient fields. Furthermore, we study the scaling law of the discrete minimal energy. The constructions used in the upper bound include besides rather uniform or complex branching-type patterns also structures with vortices. Our results show in particular that in certain parameter regimes the formation of vortices is energetically favorable.



1
Introduction

We study the formation of complex magnetization patterns in certain magnetic compounds. Such structures can often be explained in the framework of statistical mechanics as a result of competing ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interactions (see e.g.
[
Die13
]
). We focus on an anisotropic version of the classical
J_{1}-J_{3}
-spin model on regular square lattices which arises from the classical
J_{1}-J_{2}-J_{3}
-model in the case
J_{2}=0
.
Precisely, we set
\Omega\coloneqq[0,1)^{2}
and consider the square lattice
\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of side length
\varepsilon>0
. To each atom
(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\in\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we associate a spin vector
u_{i,j}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{1}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The configurational energy of a spin field
u\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)=u_{i,j}
, is given by

\begin{split}I_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(u)\coloneqq&\alpha^{hor}\sum_{(i,j)}u_{i,j}\cdot u_{i+1,j}-\alpha^{ver}\sum_{(i,j)}u_{i,j}\cdot u_{i,j+1}+\beta^{hor}\sum_{(i,j)}u_{i,j}\cdot u_{i+2,j}+\sum_{(i,j)}u_{i,j}\cdot u_{i,j+2}\end{split}

(1.1)

for interaction parameters
\alpha^{hor},\alpha^{ver},\beta^{hor}>0
. Here
x\cdot y
denotes the scalar product of two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The first two terms in the energy are ferromagnetic and favor aligned neighboring spin vectors, whereas the other two terms are anti-ferromagnetic and favor antipodal next-to-nearest neighboring spins.
Throughout this work, we set for simplicity
\beta^{hor}=1
, and consider the case of (incompatible) Dirichlet boundary conditions on the spin field. In this sense, our work complements the more local results from
[
CS15
]
in the one-dimensional and from
[
CFO19
]
in the two-dimensional setting, and studies explicitly the influence of boundary conditions, which is a step towards the understanding of the influence of external fields. Our two main results are the following. We first present the variational continuum limit at the helimagnetic/ferromagnetic transition point of the anisotropic model (
1.1
) in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence (see Theorem
2.1
), which turns out to be a singularly perturbed multi-well functional for gradient fields with diffuse interfaces. Additionally, we consider the scaling behaviour of the minimal energy (
1.1
) in the case
\alpha^{hor}=\alpha^{ver}
(see Theorem
2.5
), and show in particular that there is a parameter regime in which optimal structures have topological defects.

Let us briefly explain our results in some more detail and how they complement the literature, the precise statements can be found in Section
2.6
after the notation is introduced. The results of this paper are essentially contained in the Ph.D. thesis
[
Kos24
]
where also more discussion of related literature can be found.
If no boundary conditions are assigned, the minimizers of the energy (
1.1
) with
\beta^{hor}=1
are well-known (see
[
CS15
,
CFO19
]
). As the energy decouples into a contribution of vertical interactions and one of horizontal interactions, minimizing configurations are characterized by their behaviour in vertical and horizontal direction, respectively, given as minimizers of one-dimensional energies as studied in
[
CS15
]
. Precisely, if
\alpha^{hor}\geq 4
or
\alpha^{ver}\geq 4
then minimizers are (up to boundary effects),
ferromagnetic
in horizontal or vertical direction, respectively, i.e. the spin field is constant in the respective direction. If, however,
\alpha^{hor}
or
\alpha^{ver}\in(0,4)
then minimizers are (up to boundary effects)
helical
in the respective direction, i.e., spin vectors rotate with the optimal angle
\theta_{opt}^{hor}\coloneqq\pm\arccos(\alpha^{hor}/4)
and
\theta_{opt}^{ver}\coloneqq\pm\arccos(\alpha^{ver}/4)
between neighboring lattice points in horizontal and vertical direction, respectively. Note that if both,
\alpha^{hor},\alpha^{ver}\in(0,4)
then there are (up to a global rotational invariance) four optimal structures, corresponding to clockwise or counterclockwise rotations with the optimal angle in vertical and horizontal direction, respectively, see Figure
1
.

Gamma-convergence.

We first focus on the helimagnetic regime
\alpha^{hor},\alpha^{ver}\in(0,4)
and study the energy (
1.1
) with incompatible boundary conditions on the spin field
u
, i.e., setting
u(0,j\varepsilon)=(0,1)^{T}

for all
j\varepsilon\in[0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
. In this case, the explicit characterization of minimizers is very challenging as one expects the formation of complex magnetization patterns near the boundary, mixing regions with optimal helical structures to approximate the boundary condition. We first focus on the regime where
\varepsilon\to 0
, i.e., the continuum limit, and
\alpha\nearrow 4
, i.e., approaching the helimagnetic/ferromagnetic transition point. Following
[
CS15
,
CFO19
]
, we reformulate the problem in terms of the order parameters
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta^{hor/ver}}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor/ver}}{2}\right)
, where
\delta^{hor/ver}=\frac{4-\alpha^{hor/ver}}{4}
and

\theta^{hor/ver}_{i,j}
is the oriented angle between neighboring spin vectors in horizontal and vertical direction, respectively. The normalization is chosen such that for
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\in\{(\pm\theta_{opt}^{hor},\pm\theta_{opt}^{ver})\}
, the order parameters take values
(\pm 1,\pm 1)
, for details see Section
3
.

As minimizers for (
1.1
) are explicitly known, one can proceed in the spirit of
\Gamma
-development (see
[
BT08
]
). A heuristic computation from
[
CFO19
]
based on
[
CS15
]
suggests that in the above mentioned parameter regime, the behaviour of a properly renormalized version of (
1.1
) behaves like a Modica-Mortola type functional in the order parameters, with a typical transition length between regions of different chirality being of order
\varepsilon/\sqrt{\delta^{hor/ver}}
. We recall the heuristics for the reader’s convenience in the appendix. In the case without boundary conditions, this behaviour has been confirmed rigorously in
[
CS15
]
in the one-dimensional setting for
\varepsilon\to 0
, and in
[
CFO19
]
in the two-dimensional setting in the regime
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\varepsilon/\sqrt{\delta}\to 0
for
\delta:=\delta^{hor}=\delta^{ver}
. Here, as expected from the heuristics, the continuum model takes the form of an anisotropic perimeter functional subject to a differential inclusion (see Remark
2.3
). We point out that a major difficulty in the two-dimensional setting arises from a compatibility condition for the horizontal and vertical angle changes. More precisely, reformulating the energy in terms of the order parameters induces a discrete curl condition, which essentially ensures that if one starts at the spin vector at a given lattice point, then going along a lattice cell, the angular changes have to add up to a multiple of
2\pi
, i.e., yielding the same spin vector at the beginning and the end of the closed path.
For related asymptotic results on this and other variants of the
J_{1}-J_{2}-J_{3}
-model in different parameter regimes, we refer, e.g., to
[
CFO22
,
KL23
,
SV18
,
AC09
,
BCDLP18
,
ACR15
,
COR23
]
and the references therein.

We consider here the situation with Dirichlet boundary conditions on the spin field, for which the above described energy becomes infinite. Therefore, we study the regime
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\delta^{hor/ver}\to 0
and
\lim\varepsilon/\sqrt{2\delta^{ver}}>0
in the anisotropic setting

\delta^{hor}/\delta^{ver}\in[0,\infty)
. We confirm the expectation from the heuristics by rigorously deriving the
\Gamma
-limiting functional, which is of Modica-Mortola type for gradient fields, i.e., a singularly perturbed multiwell energy with incompatible Dirichlet boundary conditions (see Theorem
2.1
for the precise statement). An important step in the proof is to show that the limiting field is curl-free and hence a gradient field (c.f. also
[
CFO19
]
). On the discrete level, this requires to control the number of
vortices
, i.e., lattice cells in which the angular changes do not add up to
0
but to
\pm 2\pi
.

Scaling laws.
Singularly perturbed problems related to the limit problem have been studied in
[

expected. Qualitatively similar behaviour is well-known also in related singularly perturbed models for other pattern forming systems (see

[

,

,

,

-convergence result with associated compactness, we expect the analogue scaling behaviour (at least asymptotically) in the respective parameter domain for the discrete energies (

The mathematical model and main results

2.1
Notation

Let
\Omega\coloneqq[0,1)^{2},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1/2),\ \alpha^{hor},\alpha^{ver}\in(0,4)
,
\delta^{hor/ver}\coloneqq(4-\alpha^{hor/ver})/4
,
\Delta\coloneqq(\delta^{hor},\delta^{ver})
and
\gamma\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\delta^{hor}}{\delta^{ver}}}
. Furthermore, let
\textup{sign}(x)\in\{\pm 1\}
be the sign of
x\in\mathbb{R}
with the convention
\textup{sign}(0)=-1
. We define for vectors
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
the Euclidean scalar product by
a\cdot b\coloneqq a^{T}b
and the cross product by
a\times b\coloneqq a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}.
We introduce several sets of indices, namely

\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\in\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\},\\
&\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}\coloneqq\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\colon(i+1,j)\in\mathcal{I}\right\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}\coloneqq\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\colon(i,(j+1))\in\mathcal{I}\right\},\\
&\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}\coloneqq\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\colon(i+2,j)\in\mathcal{I}\right\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}\coloneqq\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\colon(i,j+2)\in\mathcal{I}\right\}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Furthermore, let
Q_{\varepsilon}(i,j)\coloneqq[i\varepsilon,(i+1)\varepsilon)\times[j\varepsilon,(j+1)\varepsilon)
for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}.

Note that we identify a lattice point
(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\in\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with its indices
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Accordingly, for a function
g\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we write
g_{i,j}\coloneqq g(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)
for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
.
Moreover, we write for the discrete derivatives of a function
g\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
(with
m\in\mathbb{N}
)

\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}g_{i,j}\coloneqq\frac{g_{i+1,j}-g_{i,j}}{\varepsilon}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}g_{i,j}\coloneqq\frac{g_{i,j+1}-g_{i,j}}{\varepsilon}

(2.2)

for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}
and
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}
, respectively. Additionally, we define the discrete curl of a discrete vector field
(g,h)\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
via

\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((g,h))\coloneqq\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}h-\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}g\textup{\quad on\quad}\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}.

Note that for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}
, we have

\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((g,h)_{i,j})=h_{i+1,j}-h_{i,j}-g_{i,j+1}+g_{i,j}.

2.2
Spin fields and their angular velocity field

In this section, we clarify the relation between a spin field and its angular velocity field. Moreover, we give an estimate of the number of large angles of an angular velocity field in terms of its energy. Note that throughout this manuscript we consider a static situation. The terminology ’angular velocity’ refers to the angular changes of the spin field between neighboring points in
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
not
to angular changes of the spin field in time. We can describe a spin field
v\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
by its angular velocities

\theta^{hor}_{i,j}\coloneqq\textup{sign}(v_{i,j}\times v_{i+1,j})\arccos(v_{i,j}\cdot v_{i+1,j})\text{\qquad for $(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}$, and}

(2.3)

\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\coloneqq\textup{sign}(v_{i,j}\times v_{i,j+1})\arccos(v_{i,j}\cdot v_{i,j+1})\text{\qquad for $(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}$.}

(2.4)

Here,
\arccos:[-1,1]\to[0,\pi]
denotes as usual the inverse of
\cos:[0,\pi]\to[-1,1]
.
We can retrieve the spin field by setting for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}

v_{i,j}=\textup{Rot}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{i-1}\theta^{hor}_{k,j}\right)v_{0,j}\quad\textup{ with }\quad\textup{Rot}(\theta)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{array}\right)\quad\textup{ for }\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

However, not for every angular velocity field
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
there exists an associated spin field
v\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}.
A necessary and sufficient condition to recover a spin field from an angular velocity field
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
is the discrete curl condition

\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver}))\in\{-2\pi,0,2\pi\}.

(2.5)

This condition ensures that rotating a spin vector with angular changes according to the field
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})
within an
\varepsilon
-cell
Q_{\varepsilon}(i,j)\subseteq\Omega
yields the starting spin vector i.e.,

\textup{Rot}(\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver}))v=v.

Let

V\coloneqq\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}\colon|\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})_{i,j})|=2\pi\right\}

(2.6)

be the set of
vortices
of an angular velocity field
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
.
If it holds
|\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})_{i,j})|=2\pi

for some
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}
we know that at least one of the four angular velocities
\theta^{hor}_{i,j},\theta^{hor}_{i,j+1},\theta^{ver}_{i,j}
, and
\theta^{ver}_{i+1,j}
can in absolute value not be smaller than
\pi/2
i.e.,

\max\left\{|\theta^{hor}_{i,j}|,|\theta^{hor}_{i,j+1}|,|\theta^{ver}_{i,j}|,|\theta^{ver}_{i+1,j}|\right\}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}.

(2.7)

Hence, an upper bound on the number of large angular velocities also implies an upper bound on the number of vortices.

2.3
The chirality parameters

Next, we introduce the so-called chirality order parameters for an angular velocity field and to reformulate the two-dimensional lattice energy with respect to these parameters. This will be useful in the formulation of the
\Gamma
-convergence result, see Theorem
2.1
.
Let
u\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a spin field and
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
the associated angular velocity field, see Section
2.2
. The chirality order parameters are then given by

w^{\Delta}\coloneqq w^{\Delta}(u)\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta^{hor}}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}}{2}\right)\quad\textup{ and }\quad z^{\Delta}\coloneqq z^{\Delta}(u)\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta^{ver}}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}}{2}\right)

(2.8)

on
\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The signs of the chirality order parameters determines if a spin field rotates clockwise or counterclockwise in horizontal and vertical direction, respectively. Due to the trigonometric identity
2\sin^{2}(\frac{\theta}{2})=1-\cos(\theta)
for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi),
one has
(w,z)\in\left\{(\pm 1,\pm 1)\right\}
if and only if the associated angular velocity field satisfies

(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\in\{(\pm\theta_{opt}^{hor},\pm\theta_{opt}^{ver})\}\quad\textup{ with }\quad\theta_{opt}^{hor}=\arccos(1-\delta^{hor})\textup{ and }\theta_{opt}^{ver}=\arccos(1-\delta^{ver}).

Moreover, we associate to a spin field
u:\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
the mapping

T^{\Delta}(u):=(\gamma w^{\Delta}(u),z^{\Delta}(u))\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}.

2.5
Reformulation of the energy

Let
u:\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
We define the energy
E_{\varepsilon,\Delta}(u,\Omega):=E^{hor}_{\varepsilon,\Delta}(u,\Omega)+E^{ver}_{\varepsilon,\Delta}(u,\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle E^{hor}_{\varepsilon,\Delta}(u,\Omega):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}|u_{i,j}-2(1-\delta^{hor})u_{i+1,j}+u_{i+2,j}|^{2}

(2.11)

and

\displaystyle E^{ver}_{\varepsilon,\Delta}(u,\Omega):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}}|u_{i,j}-2(1-\delta^{ver})u_{i,j+1}+u_{i,j+2}|^{2}.

(2.12)

If
\delta=\delta^{hor}=\delta^{ver}
we also denote the energies
E_{\varepsilon,\Delta},E^{hor}_{\varepsilon,\Delta},
and
E^{ver}_{\varepsilon,\Delta}
by
E_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,
E^{hor}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, and

\leq&C\left(\|Fu\|_{W^{1,2}((-1,2)^{2})}+\|\partial_{11}Fu\|_{L^{2}((-1,2)^{2})}+\|\partial_{22}F^{L}u\|_{L^{2}((-1,2)^{2})}\right)\|\phi\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{D})}\\
\leq&C\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}}\|\phi\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{D})}.\end{split}

(3.9)

∎

Lemma 3.3

The function

Proof.

The proof can be found in

We sketch it here for the reader’s convenience.
Applying several trigonometric identities, one obtains

p(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\begin{cases}\frac{\cos^{2}((\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})/4)\cos(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})}{\cos^{2}((\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})/4)}\quad&\textup{ if }\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2},\\
1&\textup{ else.}\end{cases}

Since

Similarly, one estimates for

&\leq 4\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{8(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq\frac{8(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}K.\end{split}

(4.7)

The right hand side of (
4.7
) is bounded since
\varepsilon_{n}/(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\rightarrow\sigma\in(0,\infty)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Step 4.2: Boundedness of
\bm{(\partial_{1}u_{n})_{n}}
in
\bm{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
First, note that it holds
|\theta|\leq 2|\sin(\theta)|
for
\theta\in[-\pi/3,\pi/3]
. Using that
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{n})=0
, the reformulation of the energy (
2.25
) and Hölder’s inequality, we estimate

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{1}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\delta_{n}^{ver}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}|\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}|^{2}\leq\frac{4\cdot 16\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\delta_{n}^{ver}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}=16\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left|w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq 16\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left(\left|w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}-\gamma_{n}^{2}\right)\right)+16\gamma_{n}^{2}\leq 16\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left(\left|w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}-\gamma^{2}_{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}+16\gamma_{n}^{2}\\
&\leq 16\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_{n}}{(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\right)^{1/2}+16\gamma_{n}^{2}\leq 23\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{(2\delta_{n}^{hor})^{1/2}}K\right)^{1/2}+16\gamma_{n}^{2},\end{split}

(4.8)

which is uniformly bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
since
\varepsilon_{n}/(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\rightarrow\sigma\in(0,\infty)
and
\gamma_{n}^{2}\to\gamma^{2}\in[0,\infty)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Step 5: Conclusion.

By Step 4.1 and Step 4.2 the sequence

is bounded in

. By Theorem

. As

and a (not relabeled) subsequence such that

in
W^{2,2}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\to u
in

.
∎

.

Proposition 4.2

(Local compactness and liminf inequality)
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
hold. Then, the statements (i) and (ii) are true for
H_{n}\colon L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
and the limit functional
H_{\sigma,\gamma}\colon L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts. First, we prove the local compactness. Subsequently, we prove the desired liminf inequality for the periodic problem.

Local compactness.
Let
(w_{n},z_{n})\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be uniformly bounded in energy, i.e., there is
K>0
such that

H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq K\quad\textup{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.9)

As in the proof of Lemma
4.1
we denote by

the corresponding spin fields and by
(\theta^{hor}_{n},\theta^{ver}_{n})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
the associated angular velocity fields. Again, we identify the functions
w_{n},z_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with the discrete functions
\gamma_{n}w^{\Delta_{n}}(v_{n})
and
z^{\Delta_{n}}(v_{n})
as defined in (
2.8
). The strategy will then be to use the potentials
u_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
satisfying the assertions of Lemma
4.1
to conclude the proof of the compactness by showing

\left\|\nabla u_{n}-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w_{n}\\
z_{n}\end{array}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\longrightarrow 0\quad\textup{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.10)

Step 1. Definition of a suitable candidate
\bm{(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma}).}
Let

4.1

(4.11)

\partial_{11}u_{n}=(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}\theta^{hor}_{n},\ \partial_{22}u_{n}=(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}\theta^{ver}_{n}\quad\textup{ on }\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

In particular, as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we obtain for

\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}\colon\max\{|\theta^{hor}_{i,j}|,|\theta^{hor}_{i,j+1}|\}\geq\frac{\pi}{8}\right\}\bigcup\left\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}\colon\max\{|\theta^{ver}_{i,j}|,|\theta^{ver}_{i+1,j}|\}\geq\frac{\pi}{8}\right\}

that

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{n})=0.

Furthermore, there is
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
and a subsequence (not relabeled) such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
strongly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W^{2,2}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Then define
(w,z)^{T}\coloneqq\nabla u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).
It follows
\textup{curl}((w,z))=0
in
\Omega
. Next, we will argue that it holds

z(0,\cdot)=0,\ |z(\cdot,0)|=|z(\cdot,1)|,\quad\textup{ and }\quad|w(0,\cdot)|=|w(1,\cdot)|.

(4.12)

Let
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
with
\varphi(\cdot,x)\equiv 0
for

and
\varphi(1,\cdot)\equiv 0.
Using integration by parts, the weak convergence
\nabla u_{n}\rightharpoonup(w,z)^{T}
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\partial_{2}u_{n}(0,\cdot)=0
, we conclude

\int_{0}^{1}z(0,y)\varphi(0,y)\textup{d}y=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\partial_{1}z+z\partial_{1}\varphi\dx\textup{d}y=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{n}+\partial_{2}u_{n}\partial_{1}\varphi\dx\textup{d}y=0.

(4.13)

Thus,
z(0,\cdot)=0.
Next, we verify the periodicity condition from (
4.12
). The uniform boundedness of
H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})
implies that the associated spin fields
v_{n}\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfy for all

v_{n,0,k}\cdot v_{n,1,k}=v_{n,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor-1,k}\cdot v_{n,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor,k}\quad\textup{ and }\quad v_{n,k,0}\cdot v_{n,k,1}=v_{n,k,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor-1}\cdot v_{n,k,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor}.

Hence,

\cos(\theta^{hor}_{n,0,k})=\cos\left(\theta^{hor}_{n,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor-1,k}\right)\quad\textup{ and }\quad\cos(\theta^{ver}_{n,k,0})=\cos\left(\theta^{ver}_{n,k,\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\rfloor-1}\right)\quad\textup{ for all }k=0,\dots,\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}_{n}\right\rfloor,

which yields
|\partial_{1}u_{n}(0,\cdot)|=|\partial_{1}u_{n}(1,\cdot)|
and
|\partial_{2}u_{n}(\cdot,0)|=|\partial_{2}u_{n}(\cdot,1)|
in the sense of traces.
By the weak convergence of
\nabla u_{n}
to
(w,z)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the previous observations imply
(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma}).

Step 2. Convergence of
\bm{((w_{n},z_{n}))_{n}}
towards
\bm{\nabla u}
in
\bm{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}
.
We only prove the strong convergence

as

. The convergence

in

\begin{split}&\|w_{n}-\partial_{1}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{n}-(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\mathcal{PA}({\theta}_{n}^{hor})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\mathcal{PA}(\theta_{n}^{hor})-\partial_{1}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&=:A_{n}+B_{n}.\end{split}

(4.14)

We estimate

and

for

.
By the definition of

, (
2.10
), and the identity (

), we find for

\begin{split}&(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\left|\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}-\mathcal{PA}(\theta_{n}^{hor})(x,y)\right|\leq(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\left|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}(x-i\varepsilon_{n})+\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}(y-j\varepsilon_{n})\right|\\

(4.15)

and for

\begin{split}&(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\left|\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}-\mathcal{PA}(\theta_{n}^{hor})(x,y)\right|\\

&\quad+\varepsilon_{n}\left|\partial_{11}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}u_{n}(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\right|+\varepsilon_{n}\left|\partial_{21}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}u_{n}((i+1)\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\right|.\end{split}

Consequently,

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\|(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\mathcal{PA}(\theta_{n}^{hor})-\partial_{1}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}(i,j))}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{n}\left(\|\partial_{11}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{21}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(4.17)

As the sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{2,2}(\Omega)
, see Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

as
n\rightarrow\infty.

It remains to show

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. First, observe that for all

and
\arccos(1-\delta)<\pi/3

(4.18)

Note that in Step 1 we argued that
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{n})=0

such that

. Moreover, there is a further subsequence that satisfies the condition

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By inequality (
4.18
) and

, we conclude

\begin{split}&\sup\left\{\left|(\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j})^{2}-(\arccos(1-\delta_{n}^{hor}))^{2}\right|^{2}\colon(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}\right\}\\

=&\frac{4}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left|2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right)-\delta_{n}^{hor}\right|^{2}\\
=&\frac{4}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}|\gamma_{n}^{2}-(w_{n,i,j})^{2}|^{2}\\
=&\frac{4}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}(\delta_{n}^{hor})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}|1-(\gamma_{n}^{-1}w_{n,i,j})^{2}|^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{4\sqrt{2}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}K\eqqcolon\beta_{n}\delta^{ver}_{n}.\end{split}

Since
\beta_{n}\rightarrow 4/\sigma
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, there is
C>0
such that
|\beta_{n}|\leq C<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the triangle inequality, we find

s_{n}^{hor}\coloneqq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\theta^{hor}_{n}|^{2}\leq\beta_{n}^{1/2}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}+4\delta_{n}^{hor}\rightarrow 0\quad\textup{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

(4.19)

where we used that
\arccos(1-\delta)\leq 2\delta^{1/2}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
. Using the estimate

for

, we conclude

\begin{split}A_{n}^{2}&\leq\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}|w_{n,i,j}-(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}|^{2}=\frac{2\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right)-\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}\left|\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}\right|^{6}\leq C\left(\beta_{n}^{3/2}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}+\gamma_{n}^{2}(\delta_{n}^{hor})^{2}\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\textup{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

(4.20)

This concludes the proof of (
4.10
).

Liminf inequality.
We show that for
(w,z)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and any sequence
(w_{n},z_{n})\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
for

converging strongly to

in

as

, we have

H_{\sigma,\gamma}(w,z)\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n}).

The proof of the liminf-inequality can be done separately for the horizontal and vertical contributions of the energy.
We will only present the proof for the horizontal terms.
Precisely, we show

\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\Omega}(\gamma^{2}-w^{2})^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y+\sigma\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{2}w)^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}^{hor}(w_{n}).

(4.21)

We may assume without loss of generality that
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})<\infty
,
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})
and
H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq C
for all

.
By the local compactness, there exists a (not relabeled) subsequence of
(w_{n},z_{n})
that converges to some
(w,z)^{T}\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})
strongly in

satisfies the properties

,

z(0,\cdot)=0,\ |z(\cdot,0)|=|z(\cdot,1)|,\quad\textup{ and }\quad|w(0,\cdot)|=|w(1,\cdot)|.

(4.22)

Moreover, let
u_{n}\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
be the associated sequence of potentials from Step 1 of the proof of the local compactness.
Recall, that there it holds
(\nabla u_{n})_{n}
converges to
(w,z)^{T}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
First, we observe that it follows by the strong convergence
w_{n}\to w
in
L^{2}
and
\sigma_{n}:=\varepsilon_{n}/\sqrt{2\delta_{n}^{ver}}\to\sigma
that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\Omega}(\gamma^{2}-w^{2})^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y=&\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n}^{2}-w_{n}^{2})^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{ d}y\\
=&\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{n}}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}(\gamma_{n}^{2}-w_{n,i,j}^{2})^{2}\\
=&\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{n}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}(\gamma_{n}^{2}-w_{n,i,j}^{2})^{2}\\
=&\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{n}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}2(\delta_{n}^{hor})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{\textup{hor}}_{\textup{N}}}(1-(\gamma_{n}^{-1}w_{n,i,j})^{2})^{2}.\end{split}

(4.23)

Next, the weak lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm, the weak convergence
\partial_{1}u_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and the definition of

(recall Figure

) yield

\begin{split}&\sigma\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{1}w|^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{11}u_{n}|^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{3}}{(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{-1/2}\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}\\
&=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}}{(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i+1,j}}{2}-\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.24)

By Young’s inequality and (
4.19
)

\displaystyle\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}}{(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i+1,j}}{2}-\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right|^{2}

(4.25)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}}{(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\int^{\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i+1,j}}{2}}_{\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}}1\textup{ d}s\right|^{2}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}}{(\cos((s_{n}^{hor})^{1/2}))^{2}(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i+1,j}}{2}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}

(4.27)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2}{(\cos((s_{n}^{hor})^{1/2})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\delta^{hor}_{n}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}\gamma_{n}^{-1}w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}.

(4.28)

Since

as

, we have

as

. Together with the uniform convergence
p(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\rightarrow 1

, we conclude

\begin{split}\sigma\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{1}w|^{2}\textup{ d}x\textup{d}y&\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{(\cos((s_{n}^{hor})^{1/2})^{2}\varepsilon_{n}(2\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\delta^{ver}_{n}\varepsilon_{n}^{4}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}\left|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{n}(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{3/2}}\delta^{ver}_{n}\varepsilon_{n}^{4}\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{hor}}}p(\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j},\theta^{hor}_{n,i,j+1})\left|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{k}}w_{n,i,j}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.29)

This finishes the proof of (
4.21
).
Arguing similarly for the vertical contribution proves the liminf inequality.
∎

4.2
Upper bound: Existence of a recovery sequence

In this section, we prove the upper bound stated in (iii) of Theorem
2.1
. We start with the construction of recovery sequences for a dense subset of
\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})
. Then a standard diagonal argument will yield the existence of a recovery sequence for all
(w,z)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Lemma 4.3

(Existence of a recovery sequence for a dense subset)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,1/2),\Delta_{n}=(\delta_{n}^{hor},\delta_{n}^{ver})
satisfy

\quad\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0,\quad\Delta_{n}=(\delta^{hor}_{n},\delta^{ver}_{n})\rightarrow(0,0),\quad\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta^{ver}_{n}}}\rightarrow\sigma\in(0,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{n}^{2}=\frac{\delta^{hor}_{n}}{\delta^{ver}_{n}}\rightarrow\gamma^{2}\in[0,\infty).

(4.30)

For every
(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H)\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
there is a sequence
(w_{n},z_{n})\in\textup{dom}(H_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq H_{\sigma,\gamma}(w,z).

(4.31)

Proof.

Let
(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.
By definition of
\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})
the field
(w,z)
satisfies

z(0,\cdot)=0,\ |z(\cdot,0)|=|z(\cdot,1)|,\quad\textup{ and }\quad|w(0,\cdot)|=|w(1,\cdot)|.

We start by introducing a discretization of
(w,z)
on
\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Note that it is not clear that this can be interpreted as the angular velocity field of a spin field.
Hence, we will have to modify this discretization to obtain an angular velocity field
(\theta^{hor}_{n},\theta^{ver}_{n})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
that induces a spin field
v_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)(\Omega;\varepsilon_{n}).

The actual recovery sequence will then be given by the sequence of chiralities
(w_{n},z_{n})
associated with this spin field
v_{n}
.

Step 1. The discretization
\bm{(\tilde{w}_{n},\tilde{z}_{n})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{2}}
of
\bm{(w,z)}
on
\bm{\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
.
Define for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}

\tilde{w}_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}w(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\quad&\textup{ if }i\leq\lfloor 1/\varepsilon_{n}\rfloor-1,\\
w(1,j\varepsilon_{n})&\textup{ if }i=\lfloor 1/\varepsilon_{n}\rfloor\end{cases}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\tilde{z}_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}z(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon_{n})\quad&\textup{ if }j\leq\lfloor 1/\varepsilon_{n}\rfloor-1,\\
z(i\varepsilon_{n},1)\quad&\textup{ if }j=\lfloor 1/\varepsilon_{n}\rfloor.\end{cases}

(4.32)

Step 2. Lifting
\bm{(\tilde{w}_{n},\tilde{z}_{n})}
to a spin field
\bm{u_{n}\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}}
.
First, note that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N

(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\max\{\|z\|_{L^{\infty}},\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\}\leq 1.

(4.33)

We set
\phi_{n}^{hor}:\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
as

\phi_{n}^{hor}\coloneqq\begin{cases}2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}\tilde{w}_{n}\right)\quad&\textup{ if }n\geq N,\\
0&\textup{ else}.\end{cases}

(4.34)

We define the spin field
u_{n}\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
as

u_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(0,1)^{T}&\textup{ if }i=0,\\
\textup{Rot}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\phi_{n,i,j}^{hor}\right)(0,1)^{T}\quad&\textup{ else}.\end{cases}

(4.35)

Step 3. Determining the vertical angular velocity
\bm{\theta^{ver}_{n}\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)}
of
\bm{u_{n}.}
Let us now define

\phi^{ver}_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}(\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j+1}-\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j})\quad&\textup{ if }i\geq 1,\\
0&\textup{ else},\end{cases}

(4.36)

which satisfies

u_{n,i,j+1}=\textup{Rot}(\phi^{ver}_{n,i,j})u_{n,i,j}\quad\textup{ for all }(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{N}}^{\textup{ver}}.

Note that by definition, the angular vertical velocity fields
(\phi_{n}^{hor},\phi_{n}^{ver})
is curl-free in a discrete sense.
Moreover, the bound (
4.33
) and
w\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
imply

\begin{split}|\phi^{ver}_{n,i,j}|&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j+1}-\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j}|=2\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left|\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}\tilde{w}_{n,k,j+1}\right)-\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}\tilde{w}_{n,k,j}\right)\right|\phantom{halloha}\\
&\leq 2(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|w(k\varepsilon_{n},(j+1)\varepsilon_{n})-w(k\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})|\leq 2(\delta_{n}^{ver})^{1/2}\|\partial_{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}},\end{split}

(4.37)

where we used that
|\arcsin(s)-\arcsin(t)|\leq\sqrt{2}|t-s|
for all
t,s\in(-2^{-1/2},2^{-1/2})
.
Since the right-hand side of (
4.37
) vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
, there is
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi^{ver}_{n}\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
for all
n\geq M.
Then the field

(\theta^{hor}_{n},\theta^{ver}_{n})\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\phi_{n}^{hor},\phi^{ver}_{n})\quad&\textup{if }n\geq M,\\
(0,0)&\textup{else}\end{cases}

induces the spin field
u_{n}\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}((\theta^{hor}_{n},\theta^{ver}_{n}))=0
for
n\geq M.

Step 4. Definition of the recovery sequence
\bm{(w_{n},z_{n})}
.
Define
(\hat{w}_{n},\hat{z}_{n})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

\hat{w}_{n}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{n}}{2}\right)\quad\textup{ and }\quad\hat{z}_{n}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{n}}{2}\right),

(4.38)

and set
(w_{n},z_{n})\coloneqq(\mathcal{PC}(\hat{w}_{n}),\mathcal{PC}(\hat{z}_{n}))\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note, that
(w_{n},z_{n})\in\textup{dom}(H_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the following, we shall identify
(w_{n},z_{n})
with its discrete version
(\hat{w}_{n},\hat{z}_{n}).

Step 5. Strong convergence
\bm{(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)}
in
\bm{L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm I\!R}^{2})}
as
\bm{n\rightarrow\infty}
.
We only prove the convergence
z_{n}\rightarrow z
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
since the convergence
w_{n}\to w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is actually easier to show due to the simpler form of
\phi_{n}^{hor}
.
Let
(x,y)\in Q_{\varepsilon_{n}}(i,j)
. We estimate

\begin{split}&|z_{n}(x,y)-z(x,y)|\leq|z_{n,i,j}-z(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})|+|z(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})-z(x,y)|.\end{split}

(4.39)

The second term on the right-hand side of (
4.39
) is bounded by
\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
For the first term of (
4.39
), using Lipschitz continuity of the
\sin
-function, we obtain

\begin{split}&\left|z_{n,i,j}-z(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}}\left|\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left(\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j+1}-\phi^{hor}_{n,k,j}\right)\right)-\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}z(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\right)\right|\\
&\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left|\arcsin\left(\Psi^{w}_{n,k,j+1}\right)-\arcsin\left(\Psi^{w}_{n,k,j}\right)-\left(\arcsin\left(\Psi^{z}_{n,k+1,j}\right)-\arcsin\left(\Psi^{z}_{n,k,j}\right)\right)\right|,\end{split}

(4.40)

where

\Psi^{w}_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}w(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\quad\textup{ and }\quad\Psi^{z}_{n,i,j}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{n}^{ver}}{2}}z(i\varepsilon_{n},j\varepsilon_{n})\quad\textup{ on }\Omega\cap\varepsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

For a function
g\in C^{1}([l\varepsilon_{n},(l+1)\varepsilon_{n}];(-1,1))
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds

\begin{split}&\arcsin(g((l+1)\varepsilon_{n}))-\arcsin(g(l\varepsilon_{n}))=\int_{l\varepsilon_{n}}^{(l+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-g^{2}(s)}}g^{\prime}(s)\textup{ d}s.\end{split}

(4.41)

Hence,

\begin{split}&\sqrt{\frac{2}{\delta_{n}^{ver}}}\left(\arcsin\left(\Psi^{w}_{n,k,j+1}\right)-\arcsin\left(\Psi^{w}_{n,k,j}\right)-\left(\arcsin\left(\Psi^{z}_{n,k+1,j}\right)-\arcsin\left(\Psi^{z}_{n,k,j}\right)\right)\right)\\
&=\int_{j\varepsilon_{n}}^{(j+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}^{ver}w^{2}(k\varepsilon_{n},y)/2}}\partial_{2}w(k\varepsilon_{n},y)\textup{ d}y-\int_{k\varepsilon_{n}}^{(k+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}^{ver}z^{2}(x,j\varepsilon_{n})/2}}\partial_{1}z(x,j\varepsilon_{n})\textup{ d}x\\
&=\int_{j\varepsilon_{n}}^{(j+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}^{ver}w^{2}(k\varepsilon_{n},y)/2}}-1\right)\partial_{2}w(k\varepsilon_{n},y)\textup{ d}y+\int_{j\varepsilon_{n}}^{(j+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{2}w(k\varepsilon_{n},y)\textup{ d}y\\
&\phantom{=}-\int_{k\varepsilon_{n}}^{(k+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}^{ver}z^{2}(x,j\varepsilon_{n})/2}}-1\right)\partial_{1}z(x,j\varepsilon_{n})\textup{ d}x-\int_{k\varepsilon_{n}}^{(k+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{1}z(x,j\varepsilon_{n})\textup{ d}x.\end{split}

(4.42)

The first and third term of the right hand-side can each be bounded by

\varepsilon_{n}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{n}^{ver}\max\{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}},\|z\|_{L^{\infty}}\}^{2}/2}}-1\right|\max\{\|\partial_{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\partial_{1}z\|_{L^{\infty}}\}:=\varepsilon_{n}h_{n}.

From the boundedness of
w
,
z
,
\partial_{2}w
and
\partial_{1}z
it follows that
h_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Moreover,
\textup{curl}((w,z))=0
implies
\partial_{2}w=\partial_{1}z
and thus

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{j\varepsilon_{n}}^{(j+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{2}w(k\varepsilon_{n},y)\textup{ d}y-\int_{k\varepsilon_{n}}^{(k+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{1}z(x,j\varepsilon_{n})\textup{ d}x\right|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{k\varepsilon_{n}}^{(k+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{j\varepsilon_{n}}^{(j+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\left|\partial_{1}z(k\varepsilon_{n},y)-\partial_{1}z(x,j\varepsilon_{n})\right|\textup{ d}y\textup{ d}x\\
\leq&\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\|\partial_{12}z\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{11}z\|_{L^{\infty}}).\end{split}

(4.43)

Combining the estimates above we find that

\begin{split}|z_{n,i,j}-z(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)|\leq 2h_{n}+2\varepsilon_{n}\left(\|\partial_{12}z\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{11}z\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\to 0.\end{split}

(4.44)

Thus, by (
4.39
) we have
(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which implies strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Similarly, one can show that

(\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}w_{n},\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}z_{n})\rightarrow(\partial_{1}w,\partial_{2}z)\quad\textup{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\textup{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.45)

Step 6. Conclusion.
Using the strong convergences

(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)\textup{ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\textup{ and }\quad(\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}w_{n},\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}z_{n})\rightarrow(\partial_{1}w,\partial_{2}z)\textup{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{\ as $n\rightarrow\infty$,}

the boundedness of
((w_{n},z_{n}))_{n}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and the boundedness of
((\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}w_{n},\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon_{n}}w_{n}))_{n}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the estimate (
4.31
) follows.
∎

We now prove the existence of recovery sequences for general admissible functions.

Proposition 4.4

(Existence of a recovery sequence and limsup inequality)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,1/2)
and
\Delta_{n}=(\delta_{n}^{hor},\delta_{n}^{ver})
satisfy

\quad\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0,\quad\Delta_{n}=(\delta^{hor}_{n},\delta^{ver}_{n})\rightarrow(0,0),\quad\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\sqrt{2\delta^{ver}_{n}}}\rightarrow\sigma\in(0,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{n}^{2}=\frac{\delta^{hor}_{n}}{\delta^{ver}_{n}}\rightarrow\gamma^{2}\in[0,\infty).

(4.46)

Then for every
(w,z)\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
there is a sequence
(w_{n},z_{n})\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and the limsup inequality holds

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq H_{\sigma,\gamma}(w,z).

Proof.

Let
(w,z)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. There are two cases.

Case A: Assume
\bm{H_{\sigma,\gamma}(w,z)=\infty}
.
We simply set
(w_{n},z_{n})\coloneqq(w,z)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then
(w_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(w,z)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{n}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq\infty=H_{\sigma,\gamma}(w,z).

Case B: Assume
\bm{(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma}).}

By Lemma
4.3
there is a recovery sequence for every
(w,z)\in D\coloneqq\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma}^{per})\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. By mollification, for every
(w,z)\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})
there exists a sequence
(w_{n},z_{n})\in\textup{dom}(H_{\sigma,\gamma})\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
(w_{n},z_{n})\to(w,z)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then the existence of a recovery sequence follows from Lemma
4.3
and a diagonal argument.
∎

Upper Bound

(Upper bound)

(5.5)

This extension to

satisfies
\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\tilde{\Psi}_{N}^{hor},\tilde{\Psi}_{N}^{ver}))=0

([2^{-N},\infty)\times\mathbb{R})\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

5.2
Lower Bound

In this section, we aim to prove the lower bound of the scaling law. For this, we will treat the situation of large
\delta\in[\overline{\delta},1)
and the case of small
\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta})
for some
\overline{\delta}\in(0,1)
separately. The critical value
\overline{\delta}
will be chosen so that Lemma
3.4
holds.
We build on the strategy from the continuum setting (see
[
GZ23
]
and the references given there). As the continuum argument was developed for gradient fields this strategy can only be successfully applied in the absence of vortices. The main result of this section reads as followes.

Proposition 5.3

(Lower bound)
.

There is
\overline{\delta}\in(0,1)
such that the following is true. There is a constant
c>0
, only depending on
\overline{\delta}
, such that the following two statements are true:

(i)

For all
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times[\overline{\delta},1)
it holds

\min\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\}\geq c\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}.

(ii)

For all
\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta})
and
\delta\leq\varepsilon
it holds

\min\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\}\geq c\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
be the constant introduced in Lemma
5.6
and
\overline{\delta}\coloneqq\min\{\eta^{2}/16,1-\cos(\pi/16)\}
. The first statement (i) follows from Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.5
as follows. Let
\tilde{c}
be the maximal constant from both Lemmas. For
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times(63/64,1)
we obtain from Lemma
5.4
the lower bound

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}\quad\textup{ for all }u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon).

(5.24)

Lemma
5.5
implies for
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times[\overline{\delta},63/64)

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon\delta^{2}\geq\tilde{c}\overline{\delta}^{3/2}\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}.

For the second statement we separately discuss the case large and small
\varepsilon
i.e.,
\varepsilon\in[\eta/4,1/2)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\eta/4).
If
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in[\eta/4,1/2)\times(0,\overline{\delta})
we apply Lemma
5.5
to obtain

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon\delta^{2}\geq\tilde{c}\frac{\eta}{4}\delta^{2}\geq\tilde{c}\frac{\eta}{4}\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}.

with

for a constant constant

. Then (i) and (ii) follow for

.
∎

5.2.1

\delta\in[\overline{\delta},1)

Using the definition of the energy
E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\cdot,\Omega)
and the boundary condition
u_{0,\cdot}=(0,1)^{T}
, one easily obtains from the energy on indices of the form
(0,j)
the estimate

Lemma 5.4
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for all
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times(63/64,1)
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[0,1])\geq c\varepsilon

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
. Recall, for any unit vectors
u,v,w\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\theta_{u,v}=\textup{sign}(u\times v)\arccos(u\cdot v)
and
\theta_{v,w}=\textup{sign}(v\times w)\arccos(v\cdot w)
the identity

\begin{split}&|u-2(1-\delta)v+w|^{2}=2+2\cos(\theta_{u,v}+\theta_{v,w})-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta_{u,v})-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta_{v,w})+4(1-\delta)^{2}\\
&\quad=4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\theta_{u,v}+\theta_{v,w}}{2}\right)-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta_{u,v})-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta_{v,w})+4(1-\delta)^{2},\end{split}

(5.25)

where we used that
\cos(2\theta)=2\cos^{2}(\theta)-1
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
the angular velocity field associated to

and
j\varepsilon\leq 1-2\varepsilon\in[0,1)
. We will show that

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[j\varepsilon,(j+2)\varepsilon])\geq\frac{1}{16}\varepsilon^{2}.

(5.26)

First we argue that we may assume that

\frac{|\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}|}{2}\in\left(\frac{5\pi}{12},\frac{7\pi}{12}\right)\quad\textup{ for all }k\in\{0,1,2\}.

(5.27)

Otherwise, there is
k\in\{0,1,2\}
such that the preceding assumption does not hold. Assume that
|\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}|\leq 5\pi/6
. Then, we can estimate the energy on
[0,2\varepsilon]\times[j\varepsilon,(j+2)\varepsilon]
by

\begin{split}&E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[j\varepsilon,(j+2)\varepsilon])\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}}{2}\right)-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k})-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k})+4(1-\delta)^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right)-8(1-\delta)\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-8(1-\delta)\right)\geq\frac{1}{16}\varepsilon^{2},\end{split}

(5.28)

where we used
4\cos^{2}(5\pi/12)\geq 1/4
and
1-\delta\leq 1/64
. If
|\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}|\geq 7\pi/6
, one argues similarly. Now, assume that (
5.27
) holds. Since
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})
is the angular velocity field of a spin field, we have for
k=0,1

\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}=\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k+1}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k+1}-\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}-\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})_{0,j+k})-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})_{1,j+k}),

(5.29)

where we used
\theta^{ver}_{0,\cdot}\equiv 0
. We claim that it holds

|\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}|\leq\frac{\pi}{3}\quad\textup{ for }k=0,1.

(5.30)

Define
\theta^{u}_{k}\coloneqq\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k+1}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k+1}
and
\theta^{l}_{k}\coloneqq\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k}+\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k}
.
First, assume that
\textup{sgn}(\theta^{u}_{k})=\textup{sgn}(\theta^{l}_{k})
and estimate using (
5.27
)

\displaystyle|\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}|

\displaystyle\geq|\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))+\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))|-|\theta_{k}^{u}-\theta_{k}^{l}|

\displaystyle\geq|\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))+\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))|-\frac{\pi}{6}.

Since
\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}\in[-\pi,\pi)
and
\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k})),\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))\in\{0,\pm 2\pi\}
it follows that
\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))+\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))=0
.
In turn, this implies by (
5.29
) that it holds
|\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}|\leq|\theta_{k}^{u}-\theta_{k}^{l}|\leq\pi/6\leq\pi/3
.
Now, assume
\textup{sgn}(\theta^{u}_{k})\neq\textup{sgn}(\theta^{l}_{k})
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
\textup{sgn}(\theta^{u}_{k})=1.
Then,

\begin{split}\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}&=\theta^{u}_{k}-\theta^{l}_{k}-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))\\
&\leq\frac{14}{6}\pi-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))\end{split}

(5.31)

and

\begin{split}\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}&=\theta^{u}_{k}-\theta^{l}_{k}-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))\\
&\geq\frac{10}{6}\pi-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))-\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k})).\end{split}

(5.32)

As
\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}\in[-\pi,\pi)
and
\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k})),\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))\in\{0,\pm 2\pi\}
it follows that
\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{0,j+k}))+\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{1,j+k},\theta^{ver}_{1,j+k}))=2\pi
.
Thus, it holds
|\theta^{ver}_{2,j+k}|\leq\pi/3
.
Then we estimate using (
5.30
)

\begin{split}&E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[j\varepsilon,(j+2)\varepsilon])\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{1,j}+\theta^{ver}_{1,j+1}}{2}\right)-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta^{ver}_{1,j})-4(1-\delta)\cos(\theta^{ver}_{1,j+1})+4(1-\delta)^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)-8(1-\delta)\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(1-8(1-\delta)\right)\geq\frac{1}{16}\varepsilon^{2},\end{split}

(5.33)

where we used
\cos(\pi/3)=1/2.
This shows (
5.26
).
Eventually, we sum estimate (
5.26
) to find

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[0,1])

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor 1/\varepsilon\right\rfloor-2}E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[j\varepsilon,j\varepsilon+2\varepsilon])

\displaystyle\geq\left(\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor-1\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{48}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{192}.

∎

We continue by discussing the case of
\delta
being bounded away from one and away from zero.

Lemma 5.5
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for all
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times(0,63/64)
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
it holds

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,[0,2\varepsilon]\times[0,1])\geq c\varepsilon\delta^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\eta:=1/(32\cdot 64^{2})
and assume that
E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\leq\eta\varepsilon
(otherwise, there is nothing to show).
We introduce the set of vortices along the left boundary

\begin{split}V_{0}\coloneqq&\left\{(0,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon j\varepsilon\in(0,1-\varepsilon)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\textup{ and }\left|\varepsilon\textup{curl}^{d,\varepsilon}((\theta^{hor}_{0,j},\theta^{ver}_{0,j}))\right|=2\pi\right\}\\
\subseteq&\left\{(0,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon j\varepsilon\in(0,1-\varepsilon)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\textup{ and }\max\{|\theta^{hor}_{0,j}|,|\theta^{hor}_{0,j+1}|,|\theta^{ver}_{1,j}|\}\geq\frac{2}{3}\pi\right\}=:B_{0}.\end{split}

(5.34)

Lemma
3.5
and Remark
3.7
for
\beta=2\pi/3
yield the estimate

\mathcal{H}^{0}(V)\leq\mathcal{H}^{0}(B_{0})\leq\frac{8E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)}{\varepsilon^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}}\leq\frac{8\eta\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}.

(5.35)

Let
\mathcal{I}_{0}
be the set of cells on the left boundary without vortices i.e.,

\mathcal{I}_{0}\coloneqq\{(0,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon j\varepsilon\in(0,1)\textup{ and }j\notin B_{0}\}.

By (
5.35
) and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{0})\geq\frac{3}{4\varepsilon}-1\geq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}.

(5.36)

Now, let
(0,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
. Since
V_{0}\subseteq B_{0}
, it holds

0=\theta^{hor}_{0,j+1}-\theta^{ver}_{1,j}-\theta^{hor}_{0,j}=:\theta_{1}^{j}-\theta_{2}^{j}-\theta_{3}^{j}.

(5.37)

We claim that there exists
k\in\{1,2,3\}
such that

|\theta_{k}^{j}|\notin\left[\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt},\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt}\right].

(5.38)

Assuming (
5.38
) is true for every
j\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
, we estimate the energy as in (
3
) in the proof of Lemma
3.5

\begin{split}&E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2\cdot 64^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(0,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\left[(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{hor}_{0,j}))^{2}+(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{hor}_{0,j+1}))^{2}+(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{1,j}))^{2}\right]\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2\cdot 64^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{0})\min\left\{\left|\cos(\theta_{opt})-\cos\left(\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt}\right)\right|^{2},\left|\cos(\theta_{opt})-\cos\left(\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt}\right)\right|^{2}\right\}\\
\geq&\frac{\varepsilon}{8\cdot 64^{2}}\min\left\{\left|\int_{\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt}}^{\theta_{opt}}\sin(s)\textup{ d}s\right|^{2},\left|\int_{\theta_{opt}}^{\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt}}\sin(s)\textup{ d}s\right|^{2}\right\}\\
\geq&\sin^{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt}\right)\frac{\varepsilon\theta_{opt}^{2}}{8\cdot 16\cdot 64^{2}}\\
\geq&\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}\theta_{opt}^{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{8\cdot 16\cdot 64^{2}}\geq\frac{9}{4\cdot 8\cdot 16^{2}\cdot 64^{2}}\varepsilon\delta^{2},\end{split}

(5.39)

where we used
\delta^{1/2}\leq\theta_{opt}=\arccos(1-\delta)
. This yields the lower bound with
c\coloneqq 9/(4\cdot 8\cdot 16^{2}\cdot 64^{2})
.

Hence, it is left to prove that there is
k\in\{1,\dots,3\}
such that (
5.38
) holds.
For the sake of a contradiction, assume
|\theta^{j}_{k}|\in[3\theta_{opt}/4,5\theta_{opt}/4]
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,3\}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
\theta_{1}^{j}\geq 0
.
Since
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})
is curl-free, we have

|\theta_{1}^{j}|=\theta_{1}^{j}=\theta_{2}^{j}+\theta_{3}^{j}
.
If
\textup{sgn}(\theta_{2}^{j})=\textup{sgn}(\theta_{3}^{j})
, we estimate

\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt}\geq\theta_{1}^{j}\geq\frac{6}{4}\theta_{opt}>\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt},

which is a contradiction.
Next, assume
\textup{sgn}(\theta_{2}^{j})\neq\textup{sgn}(\theta_{3}^{j}).

Then

\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt}\leq\theta_{1}^{j}=\theta_{2}^{j}+\theta_{3}^{j}\leq-\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt}+\frac{5}{4}\theta_{opt}=\frac{1}{2}\theta_{opt}<\frac{3}{4}\theta_{opt},

which is again a contradiction.
∎

5.2.2
Lower bound for small
\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta})

In this section we prove the lower bound for small interaction parameters
\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta})
. In this setting Lemma
3.4
allows us to exclude the presence of vortices. The remainder of the proof is inspired by the strategy from
[
GZ23
, Lemma 4]
for a related continuum model, see also
[
GZ24
,
GRTZ24
]
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\eta:=\frac{1}{2\cdot 4\cdot 12^{2}\cdot 256}
. Then there is a constant
c_{l}>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\eta^{2}/16)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\eta/4)
with
\delta\leq\varepsilon
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
it holds

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq c_{l}\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}.

Remark 5.7
.

Note, that

\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}=\begin{cases}\delta^{2}\quad&\textup{ if }\delta^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon,\\
\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\quad&\textup{ if }\varepsilon\leq\delta^{1/2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
,
c_{1}\coloneqq 1/(32\cdot 12^{2}\cdot 256)
and
c_{2}\coloneqq\eta
.

Step 1: Preparation.
We may assume that
u
satisfies

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\leq c_{1}\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}.

(5.40)

Otherwise there is nothing to show.
Next, using (
2.26
) and Lemma
3.4
yields

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq&\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}}(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j}))^{2}+2\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}}p(\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1},\theta^{ver}_{i,j})\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i+1,j}}{2}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\\
\geq&\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}}(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j}))^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}}{2}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\\
&-2\pi^{2}(1-2c_{p})\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver}),\end{split}

(5.41)

where
A^{ver}=\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{NN}}^{\textup{ver}}\colon\max\{|\theta^{ver}_{i,j}|,|\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}|\}\geq\pi/8\}
and
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
is the associated angular velocity field of
u
. Analogously, the same is true for the horizontal energy contribution, replacing
A^{ver}
by the corresponding set
A^{hor}.

Step 2: Reduction to estimate on slices.

We claim that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

c_{2}\geq i\varepsilon\geq\frac{c_{2}}{4}\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}=:a(\varepsilon,\delta)

one of the following holds

\begin{split}\textup{(i)}&\ \varepsilon\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{N}}(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j}))^{2}\geq c_{2}\delta^{2}\\
\textup{(ii)}&\ \varepsilon\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{N}}\max\{|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})|,|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1})|\}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}}{2}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{2}\right)\right|\geq\frac{c_{2}}{i\varepsilon}\varepsilon\delta^{3/2},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{J}_{N}\coloneqq[0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
.
We will show first how to conclude from the claim. The rest of the proof will then be devoted to proving the claim.

By Young’s inequality, (i) and (ii) from above, and (
5.41
), we have

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq\varepsilon\sum_{i\varepsilon\geq a(\varepsilon,\delta)}\min\left\{c_{2}\delta^{2},\frac{c_{2}}{i\varepsilon}\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\right\}-2\pi^{2}(1-2c_{p})\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver}).

(5.42)

By Lemma
3.5
we estimate

\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver})\leq\frac{2E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)}{(1-\delta)^{2}\varepsilon^{2}(1-\delta-\cos(\frac{\pi}{8}))^{2}}\leq\frac{4\cdot 20^{2}c_{1}s(\varepsilon,\delta)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 4\cdot 20^{2}c_{1}\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\leq 4\cdot 20^{2}c_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2},

where we used
1-\delta-\cos(\pi/8)=\cos(\theta_{opt})-\cos(\pi/8)\geq\cos(\pi/16)-\cos(\pi/8)\geq 1/20
, and
\delta\leq\varepsilon
. Consequently,
\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver})=0
. Similarly, one obtains
\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{hor})=0
. Now, we discuss the cases
\delta^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon
and
\delta\leq\varepsilon\leq\delta^{1/2}
separately.

Step 2.1:
\delta^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon
.
In this case, inequality (
5.42
) reduces to

E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq\varepsilon\sum_{c_{2}\geq i\varepsilon\geq a(\varepsilon,\delta)}\min\left\{c_{2}\delta^{2},\frac{c_{2}}{i\varepsilon}\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\right\}\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2}\delta^{2}\left(c_{2}-\frac{c_{2}}{4}-2\varepsilon\right)\geq\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{8}\delta^{2},

(5.43)

where we used
a(\varepsilon,\delta)=c_{2}/4
,
c_{2}\leq 1
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,c_{2}/4).

Step 2.2:
\delta\leq\varepsilon\leq\delta^{1/2}
.
By Remark
5.7
and the assumption
\delta^{1/2}\leq\eta/4=c_{2}/4
, we have

a(\varepsilon,\delta)=\frac{c_{2}}{4}s(\varepsilon,\delta)=\frac{c_{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{\varepsilon}\right)+1\right)\geq\frac{c_{2}}{4}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\geq\varepsilon.

(5.44)

It follows that

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{c_{2}\geq i\varepsilon\geq a(\varepsilon,\delta)}c_{2}\min\left\{\delta^{2},\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{i\varepsilon}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{c_{2}\geq i\varepsilon\geq a(\varepsilon,\delta)}c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{i\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{4}\int_{a(\varepsilon,\delta)+\varepsilon}^{c_{2}}\frac{1}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{4}\left(\ln(c_{2})-\ln(a(\varepsilon,\delta)+\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{4}\left(\ln(c_{2})-\ln(2a(\varepsilon,\delta))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}}{4}\left(\ln(2)-\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}(\ln(\delta^{1/2}/\varepsilon)+1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}}{4}\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\ln\left(2\right)-\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{\varepsilon}\right)+1\right)\right)\right)\geq\frac{c_{2}}{4\cdot 50}\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right),

where we used
\ln(|\ln\sigma|+1)\leq\max\{24\ln(2)/25,3|\ln\sigma|/4\}
and
\ln(2)\geq 1/2
in the last estimate.

Step 3: Proof of the claim.
Assume that (i) and (ii) do not hold for
i\varepsilon
. Define the sets

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{+}&\coloneqq\left\{j\varepsilon\in(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\colon\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\in\left(\frac{1}{2}\theta_{opt},\frac{3}{2}\theta_{opt}\right)\right\}\text{\qquad and}\\
\mathcal{M}_{-}&\coloneqq\left\{j\varepsilon\in(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\colon\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\in\left(-\frac{3}{2}\theta_{opt},-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{opt}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

(5.45)

Note that for
0\leq\theta<\pi/3
it holds

|\arccos(1-\delta)^{2}-\theta^{2}|=\left|\int_{\arccos(1-\delta)}^{\theta}\frac{1}{2}s\,ds\right|\leq\left|\int_{\arccos(1-\delta)}^{\theta}\sin(s)\,ds\right|=|1-\delta-\cos(\theta)|.

(5.46)

Similarly, the same estimate holds for
-\pi/3<\theta\leq 0
.
Together with
\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver})=0
and the assumption that (i) does not hold it follows for the set
\mathcal{M}^{c}\coloneqq((0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})\setminus(\mathcal{M}_{+}\cup\mathcal{M}_{-})
that

\begin{split}\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M}^{c})=&\varepsilon\sum_{j\varepsilon\in\mathcal{M}_{c}}1\leq\frac{16}{9\theta_{opt}^{4}}\varepsilon\sum_{j\varepsilon\in\mathcal{M}^{c}}(\theta_{opt}^{2}-(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})^{2})^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{4\cdot 16}{9\theta_{opt}^{4}}\varepsilon\sum_{j\varepsilon\in\mathcal{M}^{c}}\left(1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4\cdot 16c_{2}\delta^{2}}{9\theta_{opt}^{4}}\leq\frac{64}{9}c_{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}.\end{split}

(5.47)

In particular, since
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)

\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M}_{+}\cup\mathcal{M}_{-})\geq\frac{2}{3}-\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{6}.

(5.48)

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M}_{+})\geq 1/12.

We introduce the piecewise affine interpolation of
\theta^{ver}_{i,\cdot}
on
(0,1)
by
(see Figure
14
)

\theta_{i}(y)\coloneqq\frac{y-j\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}+\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon-y}{\varepsilon}\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\quad\textup{ for }y\in[j\varepsilon,(j+1)\varepsilon).

Note, that
\theta_{i}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz-continuous on
(0,1)
and
|\theta_{i}|\leq\pi/3
since
\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver})=0
.

Figure 14:
Sketch of the function
\theta_{i}
. The black dots are the values of
\theta^{ver}_{i,\cdot}
, the red dots mark the discrete boundary points of
\mathcal{M}_{+}
, and the blue rectangles indicate where the interpolated function
\theta_{i}
leaves the interval
(\theta_{opt}/2,3\theta_{opt}/2)
.

Furthermore, we introduce the function

F(\theta)\coloneqq\int_{-\frac{\pi}{3}}^{\theta}|\theta_{opt}^{2}-\gamma^{2}|\textup{ d}\gamma\quad\textup{ for }\theta\in[-\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{3}].

By the Lipschitz continuity of
F\circ\theta_{i}
, the coarea formula (see
[
EG92
, Theorem 1 in Section 3.4.2]
), estimate (
5.46
),
|s-t|/2\leq|\sin(s)-\sin(t)|
for all
|s|,|t|\leq\pi/3
and the fact that we assume that (ii) does not hold, we estimate

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>s\right\}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial\{F\circ\theta_{i}<s\})\textup{ d}s\\
\leq&2\int_{0}^{1}|(F\circ\theta_{i})^{\prime}(y)|\,dy\\
\leq&2\sum_{j}\int_{j\varepsilon}^{(j+1)\varepsilon}|\theta_{opt}^{2}-\theta_{i}^{2}(y)|\left|\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}-\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{\varepsilon}\right|\textup{ d}y\\
\leq&2\sum_{j}\int_{j\varepsilon}^{(j+1)\varepsilon}\max\{|\theta_{opt}^{2}-(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})^{2}|,|\theta_{opt}^{2}-(\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1})^{2}|\}\left|\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}-\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{\varepsilon}\right|\textup{ d}y\\
\leq&2\varepsilon\sum_{j}\max\{|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1})|,|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})|\}\left|\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}-\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{\varepsilon}\right|\textup{ d}y\ \\
\leq&8\sum_{j}\max\{|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1})|,|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{ver}_{i,j})|\}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j+1}}{2}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\theta^{ver}_{i,j}}{2}\right)\right|\textup{ d}y\\
\leq&\frac{8c_{2}\delta^{3/2}}{i\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(5.49)

Next, by Fubini, there exists
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\theta_{opt}/4)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{\theta_{i}>\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\right\}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{\theta_{i}<\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\right\}\right)

(5.50)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>F\left(\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\right)\right\}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}<F\left(\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\right)\right\}\right)

(5.51)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{8}{\theta_{opt}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}\theta_{opt}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>F\left(\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-s\right)\right\}\right)\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}<F\left(\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+s\right)\right\}\right)\textup{ d}s.

(5.52)

Consider the functions

g_{1}(s)\coloneqq F\left(\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-s\right)\quad\textup{ and }\quad g_{2}(s)\coloneqq F\left(\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+s\right).\quad

Then,
\min\{|g_{1}^{\prime}(s)|,|g_{2}^{\prime}(s)|\}\geq 3\theta_{opt}^{2}/4
for all
s\in(0,\theta_{opt}/4).

Combining (
5.49
), (
5.51
) and the estimate
\delta^{1/2}\leq\arccos(1-\delta)=\theta_{opt}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{\theta_{i}>\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\right\}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{\theta_{i}<\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\right\}\right)\\
\leq&\frac{8}{\theta_{opt}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}\theta_{opt}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>g_{1}(s)\right\}\right)\textup{ d}s+\frac{8}{\theta_{opt}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}\theta_{opt}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}<g_{2}(s)\right\}\right)\textup{ d}t\\
\leq&\frac{8}{\theta_{opt}}\left(\int_{F\left(\frac{1}{4}\theta_{opt}\right)}^{F\left(\frac{1}{2}\theta_{opt}\right)}\frac{4\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>s\right\}\right)}{3\theta_{opt}^{2}}\textup{ d}s+\int_{F\left(\frac{3}{2}\theta_{opt}\right)}^{F\left(\frac{7}{4}\theta_{opt}\right)}\frac{4\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial\{F\circ\theta_{i}<s\})}{3\theta_{opt}^{2}}\textup{ d}s\right)\\
\leq&\frac{32}{3\theta_{opt}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\partial\left\{F\circ\theta_{i}>s\right\}\right)+\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial\{F\circ\theta_{i}<s\}\textup{ d}s\leq\frac{256c_{2}}{i\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(5.53)

Hence, the set

M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\coloneqq\left\{y\in(0,1)\colon\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\leq\theta_{i}(y)\leq\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\right\}

(5.54)

has at most
\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)\leq 256c_{2}/(i\varepsilon)
boundary points in
(0,1)
. We want to show that the number of discrete boundary points
\partial^{d,\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}
of the set

\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\coloneqq M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}=\left\{j\varepsilon\in(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\colon\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\leq\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\leq\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\right\}

(5.55)

is bounded by
\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)
. If
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})=0
we either have
\theta_{i}\in(\theta_{opt}/2-\hat{\theta},\theta_{opt}/2+\hat{\theta})
or
\theta_{i}\notin(\theta_{opt}/2-\hat{\theta},\theta_{opt}/2+\hat{\theta})
which implies the same property for
\theta^{ver}_{i,\cdot}
in
(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}.
Thus, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial^{d,\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})=0
. If
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})=1
, there is
t\in(0,1)
such that either
(0,t)=M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{t\}
or
(t,1)=M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{t\}
. Hence, there is
l\varepsilon\in(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
such that either
(0,l\varepsilon)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}=M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{l\varepsilon\}
or
(l\varepsilon,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}=M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{l\varepsilon\}
, which yields
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})=1
. Finally, let us turn to the case
M\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})\geq 2
. Let
0\eqqcolon y_{0}<y_{1}<\dots<y_{M}<y_{M+1}\coloneqq 1
be the boundary points of
M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}
and
l=0,\dots,M-1
. By the definition of
M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}
we either have
(y_{l},y_{l+1})\subseteq M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{y_{l},y_{l+1}\}
and
(y_{l+1},y_{l+2})\subseteq(M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})^{c}\setminus\{y_{l+1},y_{l+2}\}
or vice versa. Therefore,
(y_{l},y_{l+1})\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\setminus\{y_{l},y_{l+1}\}
and
(y_{l+1},y_{l+2})\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\subseteq(\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})^{c}\setminus\{y_{l+1},y_{l+2}\}
holds. If
(y_{l},y_{l+1}]\cap\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}=\emptyset
we have
\partial^{d,\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\cap(y_{l},y_{l+2})=\emptyset.
Otherwise, we have
\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial^{d,\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}}\cap(y_{l},y_{l+2}))=1
, and thus obtain

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial^{d,\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})\leq\mathcal{H}^{0}(\partial M_{+}^{\hat{\theta}})\leq\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)\leq\frac{256c_{2}}{i\varepsilon}.

(5.56)

Hence, there exist pairwise disjoint, non-empty discrete intervals of the form
I_{l}\coloneqq[k_{l}\varepsilon,m_{l}\varepsilon]\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
, for
l=0,\dots,\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)
and
k_{l},m_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying

\bigcup_{l=1}^{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}I_{l}\subseteq\{j\varepsilon\in(0,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}:\frac{\theta_{opt}}{2}-\hat{\theta}\leq\theta^{ver}_{i,j}\leq\frac{3\theta_{opt}}{2}+\hat{\theta}\}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\bigcup_{l=1}^{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}I_{l}\right)\geq\frac{1}{12}.

Further, we introduce a discrete potential
u\colon\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the angular velocity field
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})
via

u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\coloneqq\frac{\varepsilon}{\theta_{opt}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\theta^{hor}_{k,j}\quad\textup{ for }(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\in[\varepsilon,1)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and

Since
\mathcal{H}^{0}(V)\leq\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{ver})+\mathcal{H}^{0}(A^{hor})=0
for the set
V
of vortices of
(\theta^{hor},\theta^{ver})
, we have

\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\theta_{opt}}\theta^{hor}_{i,j}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\theta_{opt}}\theta^{ver}_{i,j}.

(5.57)

Furthermore, we have
\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,\cdot)\geq 1/4
for all
j\varepsilon\in I_{l}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
and
l=1,\dots,\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon).

Consider the piecewise affine interpolation
\tilde{u}\colon[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\tilde{u}(y)\coloneqq\frac{y-j\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,(j+1)\varepsilon)+\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon-y}{\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)\quad\textup{ for }y\in[j\varepsilon,(j+1)\varepsilon)

for
j\varepsilon\in[0,1-\varepsilon)\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}.
The function
\tilde{u}
satisfies
\partial_{2}\tilde{u}=\partial_{2}^{d,\varepsilon}u\geq 1/4
for all
y\in\cup_{l=1}^{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}I_{l}.
Using Hölder’s inequality we find for all
l=1,\dots,\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)

\frac{1}{12\cdot 16}|I_{l}|^{3}\leq\int_{I_{l}}|\tilde{u}(y)|^{2}\textup{ d}y\leq 2\varepsilon\sum_{j\varepsilon\in I_{l}}\left(\varepsilon\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|\partial_{1}^{d,\varepsilon}u(i\varepsilon,j\varepsilon)|\right)^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{j\varepsilon\in I_{l}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left|\frac{\theta^{hor}_{i,j}}{\theta_{opt}}\right|^{2}\cdot i\varepsilon.

(5.58)

Summing over
l
leads to

\begin{split}\frac{(i\varepsilon)^{2}}{(256c_{2})^{2}16\cdot 12^{4}}\leq&\frac{1}{(\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon))^{2}16\cdot 12\cdot 12^{3}}\\
=&\frac{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}{16\cdot 12}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}|I_{l}|\right)^{3}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{16\cdot 12}\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{K}(i\varepsilon)}|I_{l}|^{3}\\
\leq&\sum_{l=1}^{K}\int_{I_{l}}|\tilde{u}(y)|^{2}\textup{ d}y\\
\leq&2\varepsilon\sum_{l=1}^{K}\sum_{j\varepsilon\in I_{l}}\left(\varepsilon\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left|\frac{\theta^{hor}_{k,j}}{\theta_{opt}}\right|^{2}\right)i\varepsilon\\
\leq&\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}}{\theta_{opt}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{NN}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|\theta_{opt}^{2}-(\theta^{hor}_{k,j})^{2}|\cdot i\varepsilon\right)+2(i\varepsilon)^{2}.\end{split}

(5.59)

Using (
5.46
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{(i\varepsilon)^{2}}{(256c_{2})^{2}16\cdot 12^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}}{\theta_{opt}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{NN}}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|\theta_{opt}^{2}-(\theta^{hor}_{k,j})^{2}|\cdot i\varepsilon\right)+2(i\varepsilon)^{2}

(5.60)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}}{\theta_{opt}^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{NN}}\sum_{k\leq i}|1-\delta-\cos(\theta^{hor}_{k,j})|\cdot i\varepsilon+2(i\varepsilon)^{2}

(5.61)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)}{\theta_{opt}^{4}}\right)^{1/2}(i\varepsilon)^{3/2}+2(i\varepsilon)^{2}

(5.62)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)}{\delta^{2}}\right)^{1/2}(i\varepsilon)^{3/2}+2(i\varepsilon)^{2},

(5.63)

where we used
\delta^{1/2}\leq\arccos(1-\delta)
for
\delta\in(0,1)
in the last line.
Since
(256c_{2})^{2}16\cdot 12^{4}=1/4
we obtain

\frac{(i\varepsilon)^{2}}{2(256c_{2})^{2}16\cdot 12^{4}}=\frac{(i\varepsilon)^{2}}{(256c_{2})^{2}16\cdot 12^{4}}-2(i\varepsilon)^{2}\leq 2\left(\frac{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)}{\delta^{2}}\right)^{1/2}(i\varepsilon)^{3/2}.

(5.64)

Rearranging (
5.64
), using
E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\leq c_{1}s(\varepsilon,\delta)
and
4(4\cdot 256^{2}\cdot 16\cdot 12^{4})^{2}c_{2}^{3}c_{1}=1
yields that
i\varepsilon\leq a(\varepsilon,\delta)
. Hence, (i) or (ii) is satisfied for

.

Remark 5.8

Recalling that the number of vortices of

Remark 5.9

(Existence of vortices)
.

If a minimizer
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
is curl-free, we can use the strategy of Lemma
5.6
to obtain the same lower bound

\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}\lesssim E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\lesssim s(\varepsilon,\delta)\leq\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}.

5.3
Scaling law for the periodic problem

In this section, we discuss the periodic problem

\min\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u,\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\},

(5.66)

where
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
consists of all spin fields
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
that additionally satisfy the periodicity condition (
2.24
).
Note that

\argmin_{\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)}(E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\cdot,\Omega))=\argmin_{\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)}I_{\alpha,\varepsilon}.

We prove the following partial scaling law.

Theorem 5.10

(Scaling Law of the discrete model with periodic boundary conditions)
.

Consider the function
s\colon(0,1/2)\times(0,1)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by

s(\varepsilon,\delta)\coloneqq\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right),\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}\right\}.

Then following statements are true for
\delta:=\delta^{ver}=\delta^{hor}
:

(i)

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times(0,1)
there holds

\min\left\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u;\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\right\}\leq Cs(\varepsilon,\delta).

(ii)

There is
\overline{\delta}\in(0,1)
and a constant
c>0
, only depending on
\overline{\delta},
such that for all

(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1/2)\times[\overline{\delta},1)
it holds

c\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}\leq\min\left\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u;\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\right\}

and for all
\delta\in(0,\overline{\delta})
and
\varepsilon\in[\delta,1/2)
it holds

c\min\left\{\delta^{2},\varepsilon\delta^{3/2}\left(\left|\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{1/2}}\right)\right|+1\right)\right\}\leq\min\left\{E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u;\Omega)\colon u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{|per|}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\right\}.

Proof.

The lower bound follows immediately from the lower bound on
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega,\varepsilon)
. For the upper bound we need to adjust the construction in such a way that the associated spin fields satisfy the periodicity condition (
2.24
). The constructions are very similar to the ones used in Proposition
5.1
, and we sketch them only for the readers’ convenience.

As in the proof of Proposition
5.1
we choose
u^{(1)}\coloneqq(0,1)^{T}
on
\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
This spin field satisfies (
2.24
) and
E_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u^{(1)})\leq 4\delta^{2}.

We follow the lines of the proof of the upper bound of Proposition
5.1
. Recall Steps 2 and 3 from the branching-type construction. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

on

Let

for a sketch)

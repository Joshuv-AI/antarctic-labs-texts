Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals of small graphs Thanks: Antonio Macchia was supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – project number 454595616.
Davide Bolognini, Antonio Macchia, Giancarlo Rinaldo, Francesco Strazzanti

Abstract
. A combinatorial property that characterizes Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals has long been elusive. A recent conjecture ties the Cohen-Macaulayness of a binomial edge ideal J G J_{G} to special disconnecting sets of vertices of its underlying graph G G , called cut sets . More precisely, the conjecture states that J G J_{G} is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if J G J_{G} is unmixed and the collection of the cut sets of G G is an accessible set system. In this paper we prove the conjecture theoretically for all graphs with up to 12 12 vertices and develop an algorithm that allows to computationally check the conjecture for all graphs with up to 15 15 vertices and all blocks with whiskers where the block has at most 11 11 vertices. This significantly extends previous computational results.

1.
Introduction

Finding a combinatorial characterization of an algebraic property of a class of ideals in a polynomial ring is an interesting and challenging task. A prominent example is Fröberg’s theorem according to which the (monomial) edge ideal of a graph
G
has a linear resolution if and only if the complement of
G
is chordal
[
8
]
.

In this paper we study a graph-theoretical characterization of Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals, a class of binomial ideals naturally defined starting from simple graphs. Fix a field
K
. Given a finite simple graph
G
with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
, the
binomial edge ideal
of
G
is

J_{G}=(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}],

where
m=|V(G)|
. Binomial edge ideals have been introduced independently in
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
and generalize the well-studied ideals of
2
-minors of a generic
2\times m
matrix.

Crucial in the theory of binomial edge ideals is the notion of cut sets, special subsets of vertices that disconnect the graph. More precisely, given a subset
S\subset V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus S
the subgraph induced by
G
on the vertices
V(G)\setminus S
and by
c_{G}(S)
the number of connected components of
G\setminus S
. A subset
S\subset V(G)
is called a
cut-point set
, or simply
cut set
, of
G
if either
S=\emptyset
or
c_{G}(S)>c_{G}(S\setminus\{i\})
for every
i\in S
. We call
cut vertex
or
cut-point
a vertex
v
of
G
such that
\{v\}
is a cut set of
G
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}(G)
the collection of cut sets of
G
. Cut sets are important because there is a one-to-one correspondence between the cut sets of
G
and the minimal primes of
J_{G}
, see
[
9
, Corollary 3.9]
.

The study of Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals mainly focused on the search of classes of graphs
[
2
,
7
,
17
,
18
]
and of constructions preserving this property
[
10
,
16
]
. On the other hand, in
[
1
]
it is described a topological characterization in terms of reduced homology groups of a certain poset, but there are no general combinatorial characterizations so far. In
[
3
]
the first, second and fourth authors propose the following conjecture in terms of the structure of the cut sets of the associated graph.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
3
, Conjecture 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
R/J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
G
is accessible.

Following
[
3
]
, recall that a graph
G
is called
accessible
if
J_{G}
is unmixed and
\mathcal{C}(G)
is an
accessible set system
(see
[
11
, p. 360]
), i.e., for every non-empty
S\in\mathcal{C}(G)
there exists
s\in S
such that
S\setminus\{s\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. This notion first emerged in
[
2
]
, where Conjecture
1.1
was proved for bipartite graphs.
Accessibility is a completely combinatorial notion since the unmixedness of
J_{G}
can also be expressed in terms of the cut sets of
G
, see
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
:
J_{G}
is unmixed if and only if
c_{G}(S)=|S|+c
for every
S\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, where
c
is the number of connected components of
G
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds for chordal, bipartite and traceable graphs, see
[
3
, Theorems 6.4 and 6.8, Corollary 6.9]
, as well as for other classes of graphs described in
[
12
]
and
[
19
]
.
The proof of these results relies on a further condition, the
strong unmixedness
of
J_{G}
, introduced in
[
3
]
, that only depends on
G
(and not on the field
K
). For every graph
G
, the following implications hold as a consequence of
[
3
, Theorem 5.11]
and
[
12
, Theorem 2]
:

(1)

J_{G}\text{ strongly unmixed}\Rightarrow R/{J_{G}}\text{ Cohen-Macaulay}\Rightarrow R/{J_{G}}\text{ satisfies Serre's condition }(S_{2})\Rightarrow G\text{ accessible}.

In this article we provide both theoretical and computational evidence for Conjecture
1.1
, proving that it holds for new classes of graphs. To this aim, we develop a series of theoretical tools that allows us to limit the search space to a special family of graphs.

Recall that a
block
, or biconnected graph, is a graph that does not have cut vertices and
adding a whisker
to a vertex
v
of a graph means attaching a pendant edge
\{v,f\}
, where

is a new vertex. In particular,

is a

, which means that it belongs to a unique maximal clique. The first important reduction to prove that

, i.e., graphs constructed by adding a whisker to some vertices of a block. This follows from
[

[

[

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

(a)

a block with

More precisely,

•

we prove case (a) in Theorem

Let

2.
Strongly unmixed binomial edge ideals

Let
G
be a finite simple graph, i.e., a graph without loops and multiple edges, with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
.
Given
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
G_{v}
the graph with vertex set
V(G_{v})=V(G)
and edge set
E(G_{v})=E(G)\cup\{\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\mid\{v,w_{1}\},\{v,w_{2}\}\in E(G)\}
. We recall the following definition from
[
3
, Definition 5.6]
:

Definition 2.1
.

A binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
is called
strongly unmixed
if the connected components of
G
are complete graphs or if
J_{G}
is unmixed and there exists a cut vertex
v
of
G
such that
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
,
J_{G_{v}}
, and
J_{G_{v}\setminus\{v\}}
are strongly unmixed.

We notice here that in the above definition it is enough to require that only one between
J_{G_{v}}
and
J_{G_{v}\setminus\{v\}}
is strongly unmixed.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
J_{G}
be unmixed. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

J_{G}
is strongly unmixed;

(2)

There exists a cut vertex
v
of
G
such that
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
and
J_{G_{v}}
are strongly unmixed;

(3)

There exists a cut vertex
v
of
G
such that
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
and
J_{G_{v}\setminus\{v\}}
are strongly unmixed.

Proof.

Clearly (1) implies (2) and (3). Since
J_{G}
and
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
are unmixed,
[
19
, Lemma 3.16]
says that if (2) holds, then
J_{G_{v}\setminus\{v\}}
is strongly unmixed. Assume now that (3) holds. Since
J_{G}
is unmixed, also
J_{G_{v}}
is unmixed by
[
3
, Lemma 4.5]
. Moreover,
v
is a free vertex of
G_{v}
and then
[

, Lemma 11]
implies that
J_{G_{v}}
is strongly unmixed.
∎

Note that, as for accessibility, the strong unmixedness of
J_{G}
is also a purely combinatorial property, i.e., it depends only on
G
and not on the field
K

[
3
, Theorem 5.11]
and
[
12

for any graph

(

J_{G}\text{ strongly unmixed}\Rightarrow R/{J_{G}}\text{ Cohen-Macaulay}\Rightarrow R/{J_{G}}\text{ satisfies Serre's condition }(S_{2})\Rightarrow G\text{ accessible.}

It is not known whether any of these implications can be reversed. In order to show that

strongly unmixed, i.e., the above conditions are all equivalent, as a consequence of

Question 2.3

If

3.
Blocks with whiskers

In this section we show that to answer Question
2.3
we can restrict to consider blocks with whiskers. Moreover, given a block with whiskers
G
, we find several sufficient conditions on the structure of
G
for the existence of a cut vertex
v
such that
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed. Combining these results, we prove that
G
accessible implies
J_{G}
strongly unmixed, and hence Conjecture
1.1
holds true, for new classes of graphs and for all graphs with up to
12
vertices.

Recall that, for a connected graph
G
,
J_{G}
is unmixed if and only if
c_{G}(S)=|S|+1
for every
S\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, see
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
. For unmixed binomial edge ideals we characterize the cut sets of
G
as the subsets
S
of
V(G)
satisfying a simple bound on the number of components of
G\setminus S
. This result will be useful throughout the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
J_{G}
unmixed and
S
be a subset of
V(G)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

S
is a cut set of
G
;

(2)

c_{G}(S)=|S|+1
;

(3)

c_{G}(S)\geq|S|+1
.

Proof.

Let
m=|V(G)|
. The implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
are clear. Recall that, for every
S\subseteq V(G)
, there is a prime ideal
P_{S}(G)
containing
J_{G}
with
{\rm ht}(P_{S}(G))=m-c_{G}(S)+|S|
, see
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
. We refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 3]
for more details. Thus, condition (3) implies that

{\rm ht}(P_{S}(G))=m-c_{G}(S)+|S|\leq m-1=m-c_{G}(\emptyset)+|\emptyset|={\rm ht}(P_{\emptyset}(G))={\rm ht}(J_{G}).

Since
P_{S}(G)
contains
J_{G}
, it follows that
{\rm ht}(P_{S}(G))={\rm ht}(J_{G})
. Therefore
P_{S}(G)
is a minimal prime of
J_{G}
and
[
9
, Corollary 3.9]
implies that
S
is a cut set of
G
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
J_{G}
unmixed. If
S
is a cut set of
G
and
v\in V(G)\setminus S
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
S\cup\{v\}
is a cut set if and only if
v
is a cut vertex of
G\setminus S
.

3.1.
Reduction to block with whiskers

Recall that a
block
is a graph with no cut vertices. A block of a graph
G
is a maximal induced subgraph of
G
that is a block. Any graph can be decomposed into blocks, where each two blocks share at most one cut vertex.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
B
a block of
G
. Denote by
W=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
the set of cut vertices of
G
that are vertices of
B
. Then

(2)

G=B\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}\right),

where
V(G_{i})\cap V(B)=\{v_{i}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, and the connected components of
G\setminus W
are
G_{1}\setminus\{v_{1}\},\dots,G_{r}\setminus\{v_{r}\}
and
B\setminus W
if it is non-empty.

If
B
is a block and
W=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\subseteq V(B)
, we denote by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{W}
the graph obtained by adding a whisker to each vertex in
W
. More precisely:

(1)

V(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{W})=V(B)\cup\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\}
, where
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
are new vertices;

(2)

E(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{W})=E(B)\cup\{\{v_{i},f_{i}\}:i=1,\dots,r\}
, where
\{v_{i},f_{i}\}
is the whisker attached to
v_{i}
.

We call
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{W}
a
block with whiskers
. Whenever we do not need to specify the set
W
, we simply write
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

The next result implies that if Question
2.3
has a positive answer for all blocks with whiskers, then Conjecture
1.1
holds for all graphs.

Theorem 3.3
.

If in every accessible block with whiskers
G
there is a cut vertex
v
such that
J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed, then the binomial edge ideal of every accessible graph is strongly unmixed.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exist accessible graphs whose binomial edge ideal is not strongly unmixed and among them choose a graph
H
with the minimum number of vertices. Clearly,
H
is connected and not complete.

We claim that there are no cut vertices of
H
such that
J_{H\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed. In fact if there is a cut vertex
v
such that
J_{H\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed,
[
3
, Corollary 5.16]
ensures that both
H\setminus\{v\}
and
H_{v}\setminus\{v\}
are accessible. Since they have fewer vertices than
H
, it follows that
J_{H\setminus\{v\}}
and
J_{H_{v}\setminus\{v\}}
are strongly unmixed. By Proposition
2.2
, this implies that
J_{H}
is strongly unmixed, a contradiction.

By
[
3
, Proposition 6.1]
, we have that there is a block
B
of
H
such that for every cut vertex
v
of
H
contained in

there exists a cut set
S_{v}
of

.

By
[

, the graph
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is accessible. By assumption there exists a cut vertex

of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

are exactly the cut vertices of

of

containing

. Let

. Since

cannot be a cut set of

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

is a cut set of

. Hence, by definition of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
,

is adjacent to a leaf in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and then
c_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}(T_{v})\leq c_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}(T_{v}\setminus\{w\})
implies that

is contained in

. Let
H_{1}
and

be the connected components of

, where

contains

. Consider

, where

We also know that there exists a cut set

; in particular,
U_{2}
is also a cut set of
H
. Finally, consider
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
. By construction of
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, it follows that
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
is a cut set of
H
. However, since
U_{1}\subseteq V(H_{1})\cup\{w\}
and
U_{2}\subseteq V(H_{2})
are cut sets of
H
,
w\in U_{1}
, and
N_{B}(w)\subseteq U_{2}
, we have that

|U_{1}\cup U_{2}|=c_{H}(U_{1}\cup U_{2})-1=(c_{H}(U_{1})-1)+(c_{H}(U_{2})-1)-1=|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|-1=|U_{1}\cup U_{2}|-1,

where the first and third equalities follow by the unmixedness of
J_{H}
(and the second equality holds because
U_{1}
leaves one component in
H_{2}
and
U_{2}
leaves one component in
H[V(H_{1})\cup\{w\}]
).
This yields a contradiction.
∎

In the rest of the paper we use the following notation:

In this subsection we may always assume that

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
be accessible. If
B
has a free vertex
w
, then there exists a cut vertex
v
of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
such that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 4.12]

w
is adjacent to some cut vertex
v
of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
(since
k\geq 3
). By contradiction
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}
is not unmixed. Since
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
is unmixed, by Proposition
2.4
there exists a cut set
S
of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}
containing

.

If
w
is not a cut vertex of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}
. Since

and cut sets only contain non-free vertices by
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have that

If

is a cut vertex of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
is unmixed and let
v
be a cut vertex of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. If
N_{B}(v)
contains only cut vertices of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, then
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed.

Proof.

By assumption,
N_{B}(v)
is a cut set of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
because every vertex in
N_{B}(v)
is adjacent to
v
and to a leaf. Since
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
is unmixed, the connected components of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus N_{B}(v)
are
|N_{B}(v)|
isolated vertices and the whisker containing
v
. This implies that
N_{B}[v]=V(B)
. Note that
N_{B}(v)\notin\mathcal{C}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\})
, and every vertex of
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\})\setminus N_{B}(v)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus V(B)
is isolated. Thus there is no cut set of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}
containing

, and by Proposition
2.4
it follows that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed.
∎

Given a graph

and
S\subset V(G)
, when we say that a vertex

reconnects
some connected components

of

, we mean that if we add back

to

, together with all edges of

incident in

, then

are in the same connected component.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
be accessible and assume that there is a cut vertex
v
of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
such that
|N_{B}(v)|=2
. Then there exists a cut vertex

of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
for which
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v^{\prime}\}}
is unmixed.

Proof.

Let
N_{B}(v)=\{v_{1},w\}
, where by
[
3
, Theorem 4.12]
and Lemma
3.7
we may assume that
v_{1}
is a cut vertex (since
k\geq 3
), while
w
is not. Assume by contradiction that neither

nor

are unmixed. Hence,
[
3
, Lemma 6.2 (1)]
says that
N_{B}(v)\nsubseteq N_{B}[v_{1}]
and then

.

Consider

and
N^{\prime}=\{u\in N\mid N_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}(u)\nsubseteq N\}\setminus\{v,v_{1}\}
. By construction

is a cut set of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and we call

the connected components of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus N^{\prime}

. Clearly

. We show the reverse inclusion.

By Proposition
2.4
,
N_{B}(v_{1})
is contained in a cut set of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}

is adjacent to at least two vertices of

. We claim that also

. Indeed, if

, then

has at least three connected components: the one containing

, the one containing

, and an isolated vertex. Therefore,

, which contradicts Lemma

. Hence,

.

Since

, the vertices of
G_{1}
are
v,v_{1}
and the two leaves adjacent to them. Both

and

have two connected components, hence Remark

implies that both

and

are cut sets of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Moreover, the accessibility of

such that

is a cut set of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

that

is adjacent to exactly two connected components of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus N^{\prime}

If

, then

does not reconnect any component of

.
∎

Let

Assume by contradiction that

In the next lemma we use an argument similar to the proof of

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

for exactly one

Without loss of generality, we may assume

. Let

J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}}

In this section, in order to answer Question

to restrict the search space to certain blocks with whiskers. The key idea of the algorithm is to generate all blocks with

This allows us to verify Conjecture

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G
be one of the following:

(a)

a graph with
|V(G)|\leq 15
;

(b)

G=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
a block with whiskers, where
n=|V(B)|\leq 11
.

Then the conditions below are equivalent:

(1)

J_{G}
is strongly unmixed;

(2)

R/J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay;

(3)

R/J_{G}
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
;

(4)

G
is accessible.

Proof.

We know that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)
for every graph
G
, hence we only need to show that
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
.

(a)
Let
G
be a graph with
|V(G)|\leq 15
. By Remark
3.4
, it is enough to show that every block with whiskers
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
with at most
15
vertices has a cut vertex
v
such that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v\}}
is unmixed. If
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
has at most
3
whiskers, then the claim follows by Proposition
3.12
. Otherwise,
B
is a block with at most
11
vertices and we conclude by Remark
4.3
.

(b)
Let
G=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
be a block with whiskers, where
n=|V(B)|\leq 11
. We proceed by induction on
|V(B)|\geq 1
. The case
|V(B)|=1
is trivial. By Remark
4.3
, there exists a cut vertex, say
v_{1}
, such that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}}
is unmixed. Moreover, in light of Proposition
2.2
, it is enough to show that
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}}
and
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{v_{1}}\setminus\{v_{1}\}}
are strongly unmixed. Notice that the graphs
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{v_{1}}\setminus\{v_{1}\}
are accessible by
[
3
, Corollary 5.16]
. Furthermore, for each block
C
of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}
,
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
is strongly unmixed by induction since
|V(C)|<|V(B)|
, and hence
J_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{v_{1}\}}
is strongly unmixed by
[
19
, Theorem 3.17]
.

Let now
G_{1}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{v_{1}}\setminus\{v_{1}\}
, which is an accessible block
C
with
k-1
whiskers. We only need to show that
J_{G_{1}}
is strongly unmixed. Notice that
V(C)=(V(B)\cup\{f_{1}\})\setminus\{v_{1}\}
, where
f_{1}
is the leaf adjacent to
v_{1}
in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.0muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Therefore,
|C|=|B|\leq 11
and by assumption there exists a cut vertex, say
v_{2}
of
G_{1}
such that
J_{G_{1}\setminus\{v_{2}\}}
is unmixed. As above,
J_{G_{1}\setminus\{v_{2}\}}
is strongly unmixed and
G_{2}=(G_{1})_{v_{2}}\setminus\{v_{2}\}
is an accessible block with
k-2
whiskers. Hence, it is enough to prove that
J_{G_{2}}
is strongly unmixed. Repeating the same argument we obtain the graph
G_{k}=(G_{k-1})_{v_{k}}\setminus\{v_{k}\}
and we only need to show that
J_{G_{k}}
is strongly unmixed. However,
G_{k-1}
is an accessible block with one whisker, and then its unique cut vertex is adjacent to all the other vertices of
G_{k-1}
by
[
3
, Lemma 4.9]
. Therefore,
G_{k}
is a complete graph and
J_{G_{k}}
is strongly unmixed.
∎

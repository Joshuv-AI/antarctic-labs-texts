How baryons appear in low-energy QCD: Domain-wall Skyrmion phase in strong magnetic fields Preprint: YGHP-23-01, KEK-TH-2513
Minoru Eto 1,2 , Kentaro Nishimura 3,2 , Muneto Nitta 2,4,5

Abstract
Low-energy dynamics of QCD can be described by pion degrees of freedom in terms of the chiral perturbation theory(ChPT). A chiral soliton lattice(CSL), an array of solitons, is the ground state due to the chiral anomaly in the presence of a magnetic field larger than a certain critical value at finite density. Here, we show in a model-independent and fully analytic manner (at the leading order of ChPT) that the CSL phase transits to a domain-wall Skyrmion phase when the chemical potential is larger than the critical value μ c = 16 ​ π ​ f π 2 / 3 ​ m π ∼ 1.03 ​ GeV \mu_{\rm c}=16\pi f_{\pi}^{2}/3m_{\pi}\sim 1.03\;\;{\rm GeV} with the pion’s decay constant f π f_{\pi} and mass m π m_{\pi} , which can be regarded as the nuclear saturation density. There spontaneously appear stable two-dimensional Skyrmions or lumps on a soliton surface, which can be viewed as three-dimensional Skyrmions carrying even baryon numbers from the bulk despite no Skyrme term. They behave as superconducting rings with persistent currents due to a charged pion condensation, and areas of the rings’ interiors are quantized. This phase is in scope of future heavy-ion collider experiments.



I
Introduction

We are all made of baryons,
that is, nucleons such as protons and neutrons,
and nucleons are composed of quarks and gluons,
particles gluing quarks.
Theoretically, these particles are
described by
Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD),
fundamental theory of the strong interaction.
It is, however,
quite difficult to prove by first-principles calculation
of QCD that
quarks are all confined to form
hadrons,
i. e.

baryons (three quark bound states
such as nucleons)
and mesons (quark–anti-quark bound states such as pions).
Nevertheless, low-energy dynamics of QCD
can be described in terms of
symmetry:
chiral symmetry,
a symmetry mixing different species of
quarks (up-quarks, down-quarks and so on)
in QCD,
is spontaneously broken with
resulting in light scalar
(Nambu-Goldstone) bosons, that is
pions.
Thus, low-energy dynamics of QCD can be described
by these pion degrees of freedom
in a model-independent manner in terms of
a chiral Lagrangian or
more generally within the chiral perturbation theory (ChPT)
up to some constants, the pion’s decay constant,
quark masses and so on

Scherer and Schindler 2012
;
Bogner et al. 2010
.

However,
one of big questions is
how baryons
can be described at low energy.
One of old ideas by Skyrme was
that nucleons can be identified with
topological solitons,
called Skyrmions,
of the chiral Lagrangian
supplemented by the so-called four derivative
Skyrme term stabilizing Skyrmions

Skyrme 1962
;
Witten 1983a
.
However, one of the drawbacks of the Skyrme model
is the necessity of the Skyrme term,
a particular choice of four derivative term;
all quantities depend on
the coupling constant of
the Skyrme term,
and thus all predictions are
model-dependent in this sense.

One of breakthroughs to overcome the problem was made by
Son and Stephanov
Son and Stephanov 2008
.
The chiral Lagrangian
contains the anomalous coupling of the neutral pion
\pi_{0}
to the magnetic field via the chiral anomaly

Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
;
Son and Stephanov 2008

in terms of
the Goldstone-Wilczek current
Goldstone and Wilczek 1981
;
Witten 1983b
.
It has been pointed out that, due to this anomalous term, the ground state of QCD with two flavors (up and down quarks) at a finite baryon chemical potential
\mu_{\textrm{B}}
under a sufficiently strong magnetic field is a stack of sheet-type baryons, which is called chiral soliton lattice (CSL).
Focusing only on the neutral pion
\pi_{0}
,
the ChPT is mathematically equivalent to the sine-Gordon model and has the domain wall solution corresponding to the sheet-type baryons
Son and Stephanov 2008
;
Eto et al. 2013
;
Brauner and Yamamoto 2017
.
Recently, CSL phases have been paid great interests:
CSLs appear also
under thermal fluctuation
Brauner and Kadam 2017a
;
Brauner and Kadam 2017b
;
Brauner et al. 2021
;
Brauner and Kolešová 2023

or rapid rotation

Huang et al. 2018
;
Nishimura and Yamamoto 2020
;
Eto et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2021
.
Other topics include
the instability of CSLs via a charged pion condensation
Brauner and Kadam 2017a
,
a possibility of an Abrikosov’s vortex lattice
Evans and Schmitt 2022
,
relations between Skyrmions and CSLs
Kawaguchi et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023
,
and quantum nucleation of CSLs

Eto and Nitta 2022
;
Higaki et al. 2022

(see also
Yamada and Yamamoto 2021
;
Brauner et al. 2019a
;
Brauner et al. 2019b
).
Furthermore, apart from QCD, CSLs
universally appear in various condensed matter systems;
chiral magnets
with nanotechnological application
to magnetic memory storage devices and magnetic sensors
Togawa et al. 2016
,
and chiral liquid crystals.

In this Letter, we find
a new baryonic phase
in a model-independent and analytic manner,
that is a

domain-wall Skyrmion phase

appearing inside higher density region of the CSL
with the baryon chemical potential

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm B}\geq\mu_{\textrm{c}}=\frac{16\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{3m_{\pi}}\sim 1.03\;\;{\rm GeV}\,,

(1)

where we have used the vacuum values of the physical quantities
f_{\pi}\approx 93\,\textrm{MeV}
and
m_{\pi}\approx 140\,\textrm{MeV}
.
This phase boundary coincides with
the instability of CSLs via a charged pion condensation

Brauner and Kadam 2017a
.
In this phase,
two-dimensional Skyrmions
Polyakov and Belavin 1975

spontaneously appear on a soliton surface,
which can be viewed
as three-dimensional Skyrmions
from the bulk,
thereby called domain-wall Skyrmions

Nitta 2013a
;
Nitta 2013b
;
Gudnason and Nitta 2014a
;
Gudnason and Nitta 2014b
.

II
Chiral solitons lattices

We focus on the phase in which the chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken down.
The low-energy dynamics can thus be described by an effective field theory of the pions – ChPT.
The pion fields are represented by a
2\times 2
unitary matrix

\displaystyle\Sigma=\text{e}^{\text{i}\tau_{a}\chi_{a}}\,,

(2)

where
\tau_{a}
(
a=1,2,3
) are the Pauli matrices with the normalization
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\tau_{a}\tau_{b})=2\delta_{ab}
.
This field
\Sigma
transforms under
\text{SU}(2)_{\textrm{L}}\times\text{SU}(2)_{\textrm{R}}
chiral symmetry as

\Sigma\to L\Sigma R^{{\dagger}}
,
where
L
and
R
are
2\times 2
unitary matrices.
Then, the effective Lagrangian
at the
{\cal O}(p^{2})
order is
(
\mu=0,\cdots,3
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}=\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{4}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left(D_{\mu}\Sigma D^{\mu}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\right)-\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}}{4}(2-\Sigma-\Sigma^{{\dagger}})\,,

(3)

where
f_{\pi}
and
m_{\pi}
are pion’s decay constant and mass, respectively, and

D_{\mu}
is a covariant derivative defined by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\Sigma\equiv\partial_{\mu}\Sigma+\text{i}eA_{\mu}[Q,\Sigma]\,,\quad Q=\frac{1}{6}\bm{1}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{3}\,.

(4)

The
U(1)_{\rm EM}
transformation is

\Sigma\to\text{e}^{i\lambda\frac{\tau_{3}}{2}}\Sigma\text{e}^{-i\lambda\frac{\tau_{3}}{2}}
and
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\frac{1}{e}\partial_{\mu}\lambda
.
The external
\text{U}(1)_{\textrm{B}}
gauge field
A^{\textrm{B}}_{\mu}
can couple to
\Sigma
through the Goldstone-Wilczek (GW) current
Goldstone and Wilczek 1981
;
Witten 1983b
.
The conserved and gauge-invariant baryon current in the external magnetic field is calculated in refs.
Goldstone and Wilczek 1981
;
Son and Stephanov 2008
:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359ptj_{\textrm{GW}}^{\mu}=-\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}}{24\pi^{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\{L_{\nu}L_{\alpha}L_{\beta}-3\text{i}e\partial_{\nu}\left[A_{\alpha}Q(L_{\beta}+R_{\beta})\right]\}\hskip-8.5359pt

(5)

where
A^{\textrm{B}}_{\mu}=(\mu_{\textrm{B}},\bm{0})
,
and we have introduced the standard notation
L_{\mu}\equiv\Sigma\partial_{\mu}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}
and
R_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\Sigma
.
Then, the effective Lagrangian coupling to
A^{\textrm{B}}_{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{WZW}}=-A^{\textrm{B}}_{\mu}j_{\textrm{GW}}^{\mu}\,.

(6)

which is known as
the Wess-Zumino-Witten(WZW) term

Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
;
Son and Stephanov 2008
.
The total Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}+\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{WZW}}
.

An important remark is in order here.
To construct an effective Lagrangian,
we choose a modification of the standard power counting scheme of ChPT
Brauner et al. 2021
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\,,m_{\pi}\,,A_{\mu}=\mathcal{O}(p^{1})\,,\qquad A^{\textrm{B}}_{\mu}=\mathcal{O}(p^{-1})\,.

(7)

We note that, in this power counting, eq. (
6
) has the order
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
and is of the same order as eq. (
3
).
The fact that
\mu_{\textrm{B}}
appears only in the WZW term in eq. (
6
) enables us to assign the negative power counting to
\mu_{\rm B}
.
The effective field theory up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
has to contain both terms in eq. (
5
); however, the first term in eq. (
5
) has not been considered in the previous researches of the CSLs.
We emphasize that we do not need
an
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
order such as a Skyrme term to
obtain our results,
and thus our analysis is model-independent.

We note that our effective theory admits the sine-Gordon soliton solutions,
and they are stable under a sufficiently large magnetic field as shown in
Son and Stephanov 2008
.
If we consider the case of
no charged pions

\Sigma=\text{e}^{\text{i}\tau_{3}\chi_{3}}
, the effective Lagrangian reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\chi_{3})^{2}-f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}(1-\textrm{cos}\chi_{3})+\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{4\pi^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\chi_{3}\,.

(8)

The sine-Gordon Lagrangian consists of
the first and second terms, while
the third term is a topological term
that does not contribute to the EOM.
Nevertheless
it stabilizes an inhomogeneous configuration of
\chi_{3}
.
Solutions of
\chi_{3}
are sine-Gordon solitons winding around
\text{U}(1)_{3}\subset\text{SU}(2)_{\textrm{V}}
.
A single soliton solution is

\displaystyle\chi_{3}=4\tan^{-1}e^{m_{\pi}(z-Z)}\,,

(9)

where
Z
is the position of the center
(or the translational modulus).
The tension, that is
the energy density per unit area, can be analytically obtained:

\displaystyle E=8m_{\pi}f_{\pi}^{2}-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{2\pi}\,.

(10)

When
E<0
,
the sine-Gordon soliton is energetically more stable than the QCD vacuum (
\chi_{3}
=0).
The critical magnetic field at which the transition happens is

\displaystyle B_{\textrm{c}}=\frac{16\pi m_{\pi}f_{\pi}^{2}}{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}\,.

(11)

The ground state at
B>B_{\textrm{c}}
is a stack of parallel
\chi_{3}
solitons,
which is the so-called CSL
Son and Stephanov 2008
;
Eto et al. 2013
;
Brauner and Yamamoto 2017
.

\mu_{\textrm{B}}
.

The situation is drastically different

28

(30)

V
Summary

We have reported the existence of
the domain-wall Skyrmion phase
in QCD matter at finite density with magnetic field.
Our results were obtained at the leading

{\cal O}(p^{2})
order of the ChPT,
without a help of higher order corrections
such as a Skyrme term.
Thus our results are model-independent and robust.
The lumps on the domain wall are Skyrmions(baryons) in the bulk,
for which one lump corresponds to two baryons.
Skyrmions exist in a pair
in a Macarons shape
with sandwiching the domain wall.
The boundary
n_{3}=0
of the lumps are superconducting rings,
yielding the flux quantization condition or
the area quantization of the lumps, eq. (
28
).
While single
k=1
lumps always have positive energy,

k(\geq 2)
lumps have negative energy in
the domain-wall Skyrmion phase.
As a byproduct, we have obtained
the nuclear saturation density
in terms of the pion’s decay constant and mass.
The magnetic field
10^{19}{\rm G}
at the tricritical point
is about only 10 times stronger than the ability of current heavy-ion colliders and so it is in scope of future experiments.

An Abrikosov’s vortex lattice
was proposed
Evans and Schmitt 2022

as a possible fate of the charged pion condensation

Brauner and Kadam 2017a
.
However they used
{\mathcal{O}}(p^{4})
order
(but not all terms)
of the ChPT.
In contrast, we have considered the leading

{\mathcal{O}}(p^{2})
order consistently
with all the terms.

Appendix A
Derivation of the effective action of a non-Abelian sine-Gordon soliton

Here,
we derive the EFT of the non-Abelian sine-Gordon soliton
for the both orientational moduli
\phi
and the translational modulus
Z
,
following Ref.
Eto and Nitta 2015

in the moduli approximation
Manton 1982
;
Eto et al. 2006
.

We first calculate the effective action coming from eq. (
3
).
Substituting eq. (
15
) into eq. (
3
),
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{2}\left\{\partial_{\alpha}\theta\partial^{\alpha}\theta+|1-u^{2}|^{2}[(\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial_{\alpha}\phi)^{2}+\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial^{\alpha}\phi]\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{e}{2}f_{\pi}^{2}A^{\alpha}|u^{2}-1|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi\cdot\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi-\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\partial_{\alpha}\phi)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{8}A^{2}|u^{2}-1|^{2}\left[-1+(\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{2}(\partial_{z}\theta)^{2}-f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}(1-\textrm{cos}\theta)\,,

(31)

where
x^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=0,1,2
) are world-volume coordinates.
Since
\theta
depends on
x^{\alpha}
through
Z(x^{\alpha})
,

\partial_{\alpha}\theta
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\theta=-\partial_{\alpha}Z\partial_{z}\theta\,,

(32)

then
f_{\pi}^{2}\partial_{\alpha}\theta\partial^{\alpha}\theta/2
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{2}(\partial_{z}\theta)^{2}\partial_{\alpha}Z\partial^{\alpha}Z\,.

(33)

Integrating over
z
, the effective action stemming from eq. (
3
) can be calculated as

\displaystyle\int\text{d}z\,\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}

\displaystyle=4m_{\pi}f_{\pi}^{2}(\partial_{\alpha}Z)^{2}+\frac{16f_{\pi}^{2}}{3m_{\pi}}[(\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial_{\alpha}\phi)^{2}+\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial^{\alpha}\phi]

\displaystyle+\frac{16{\text{i}}e}{3m_{\pi}}f_{\pi}^{2}A^{\mu}\left[\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi\cdot\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial_{\mu}\phi+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi-\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\partial_{\mu}\phi)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{4e^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{3m_{\pi}}A^{2}\left[-1+(\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)^{2}\right]-8m_{\pi}f_{\pi}^{2}\,,

(34)

where we have used the integrals,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}z\,(\partial_{z}\theta)^{2}=8m_{\pi}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}z\,|1-u^{2}|^{2}=\frac{32}{3m_{\pi}}\,.

(36)

The gauge field in the above Lagrangian can be stored
in the standard covariant derivative:

D_{\alpha}\phi=(\partial_{\alpha}+\text{i}eA_{\alpha}\tau_{3}/2)\phi
.
This comes from the original gauge transformation

\Sigma\to\text{e}^{i\lambda\frac{\tau_{3}}{2}}\Sigma\text{e}^{-i\lambda\frac{\tau_{3}}{2}}
and
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\frac{1}{e}\partial_{\mu}\lambda

with the definition
\Sigma=[{\bm{1}}_{2}+(u^{2}-1)\phi\phi^{{\dagger}}]u^{-1}
and
\phi\to\text{e}^{\text{i}\frac{\lambda\tau_{3}}{2}}\phi
. Then,
eq. (
34
) is simplified into

\displaystyle\int\text{d}z\,\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}

\displaystyle=-8f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}+4m_{\pi}f_{\pi}^{2}(\partial_{\alpha}Z)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{16f_{\pi}^{2}}{3m_{\pi}}[(\phi^{{\dagger}}D_{\alpha}\phi)^{2}+D_{\alpha}\phi^{{\dagger}}D^{\alpha}\phi]\,.

(37)

The first term is the tension of the non-Abelian sine-Gordon soliton,
and the second and third terms are the kinetic terms of
Z
and
\phi
, respectively.

We next calculate the effective action coming from the WZW term in eq. (
6
).
Due to the eq. (
20
),
the first term can be expressed as
\mu_{\textrm{B}}{\cal B}
.
Integration of
{\cal B}
over
z
equals twice
q
.
Then, we get

\displaystyle\int\text{d}z\,\mu_{\textrm{B}}{\cal B}=2\mu_{\textrm{B}}q\,.

(38)

The second term in eq. (
6
) is divided into two terms as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{0ijk}\partial_{i}[A_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\tau_{3}L_{k}+\tau_{3}R_{k})]

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{0ijk}\partial_{i}A_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\tau_{3}L_{k}+\tau_{3}R_{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{0ijk}A_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}(\partial_{i}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}+\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\partial_{i}\Sigma)\,.

(39)

We consider the uniform external magnetic field along the
z
-axis,
\bm{B}=(0,0,B)
.
Then, the first term in eq. (
39
) becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}B\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}(L_{3}+R_{3})\,.

(40)

In terms of the projection operator

P\equiv\phi\phi^{{\dagger}}
,
R_{k}
and
L_{k}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle L_{k}=(1-2P)\text{i}\partial_{k}\theta+(u^{-2}-1)\partial_{k}P+|u^{2}-1|^{2}P\partial_{k}P\,,

(41)

\displaystyle R_{k}=(1-2P)\text{i}\partial_{k}\theta+(u^{-2}-1)\partial_{k}P+|u^{2}-1|^{2}\partial_{k}P\cdot P\,.

(42)

Since
\phi
does not depend on
z

the second and third terms in
L_{3}
and
R_{3}
vanish.
Therefore, eq. (
40
) becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi^{2}}(\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)\partial_{3}\theta\,,

(43)

and integrating over

, we get

\displaystyle\int\text{d}z\,\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{0ijk}\partial_{i}A_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\tau_{3}L_{k}+\tau_{3}R_{k})=-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{2\pi}\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi\,,

(44)

where we have used the boundary condition of
\theta
for a single soliton,
\theta(\infty)-\theta(-\infty)=2\pi
.

We next calculate the second term in eq. (
39
).
Substituting eq. (
15
) into
\partial_{i}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}
and
\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\partial_{i}\Sigma
, these two quantities can be calculated as

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}=\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}\theta+[(1-u^{-2})-(u^{2}-u^{-2})P]\text{i}\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}P

\displaystyle-[(1-u^{2})+(u^{2}-u^{-2})P]\text{i}\partial_{k}\theta\partial_{i}P+|1-u^{2}|^{2}\partial_{i}P\partial_{k}P\,,

and

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\partial_{k}\Sigma=\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}\theta+[(1-u^{-2})-(u^{2}-u^{-2})P]\text{i}\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}P

\displaystyle-[(1-u^{2})+(u^{2}-u^{-2})P]\text{i}\partial_{k}\theta\partial_{i}P+|1-u^{2}|^{2}\partial_{k}P\partial_{i}P\,.

(46)

Hence, we can represent

u

\phi

\displaystyle\epsilon^{0ijk}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}(\partial_{i}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}+\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\partial_{i}\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{0ijk}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}\{2\text{i}\left[-1+(1+u^{2})P\right](u^{-2}-1)\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}P

\displaystyle\quad+2\text{i}\left[-1+(1+u^{-2})P\right](u^{2}-1)\partial_{i}\theta\partial_{k}P\}

\displaystyle=2\text{i}|1-u^{2}|^{2}\epsilon^{0ijk}\partial_{i}\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}\partial_{k}P

\displaystyle=2\text{i}|1-u^{2}|^{2}\epsilon^{03jk}\partial_{3}\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}\partial_{k}P\,.

Inserting this expression into the second term in eq (

\displaystyle-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{8\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{03jk}|1-u^{2}|^{2}\partial_{3}\theta A_{j}\partial_{k}(\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)\,.

z

\displaystyle\int\text{d}z\,\frac{\text{i}e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{0ijk}A_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_{3}(\partial_{i}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}+\partial_{k}\Sigma^{{\dagger}}\partial_{i}\Sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{2\pi}\epsilon^{03jk}A_{j}\partial_{k}(\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)\,,

(49)

where we have used the integral,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}z(u-u^{-1})^{2}\partial_{3}\theta=-4\pi\,.

(50)

Summing up eqs. (
44

\displaystyle-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{2\pi}\epsilon^{03jk}\partial_{j}(A_{k}\phi^{{\dagger}}\tau_{3}\phi)\,.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{DW}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\text{d}z\,(\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{ChPT}}+\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{WZW}})

Here, we derive the correspondence between
the topological charge in three dimensions and two dimensions
by using the decomposition
of the topological charge density,
the Baryon number density,
into the sine-Gordon soliton number density
and the lump topological charge density.
The baryon charge density

\displaystyle{\cal B}=\frac{-1}{24\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{ijk}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(L_{i}L_{j}L_{k}),

(53)

can be factorized as

\displaystyle{\cal B}=-(u-u^{-1})^{2}{\cal W}(z)q(x,y),

(54)

where
{\cal W}(w)
is the domain wall charge density

\displaystyle{\cal W}(z)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{3}\theta}{2\pi}=\frac{m}{\pi\cosh mz},

(55)

and the lump topological charge density

\displaystyle q(x,y)\equiv-\frac{\text{i}}{2\pi}\epsilon^{ij}\partial_{i}\phi^{{\dagger}}\partial_{j}\phi=\frac{1}{8\pi}\epsilon^{ij}{\bm{n}}\cdot(\partial_{i}{\bm{n}}\times\partial_{j}{\bm{n}}).

(56)

The sine-Gordon soliton number and the lump number are given by

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dz\,{\cal W}=1,\quad\int d^{2}x\,q=k,

(57)

respectively.
The dressing factor in
{\cal B}
is given by

\displaystyle-(u-u^{-1})^{2}=\frac{16\sinh^{2}mw}{\cosh^{4}mw}.

(58)

Thus, the domain wall number density together with the dressing factor
determines
z
-dependence of
{\cal B}

\displaystyle-(u-u^{-1})^{2}{\cal W}=\frac{16m\sinh^{2}mw}{\pi\cosh^{5}mw}.

(59)

This has two peaks as shown in Fig.
4
and its integration reads

\displaystyle-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dz\,(u-u^{-1})^{2}{\cal W}=2.

(60)

Hence, the
z
integration of the baryon number density is

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dz\,{\cal B}=2q(x,y).

(61)

Appendix C
The WZW term for
k
lumps

Here, we calculate the WZW term.
for
k
-lump configuration.
Let us take the constant magnetic flux background with

\displaystyle A_{i}=-\frac{B}{2}\epsilon_{ij}x^{j},\quad F_{12}=B.

(62)

Then, the WZW term includes

\displaystyle\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{2\pi}\epsilon^{03jk}\partial_{j}[A_{k}(1-n_{3})]

\displaystyle~=-\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\epsilon^{jk}\epsilon_{kl}\partial_{j}\left(x^{l}(1-n_{3})\right)

\displaystyle~=\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\partial_{j}\left(x^{j}(1-n_{3})\right),

(63)

where our notation is
\epsilon^{12}=\epsilon_{12}=1
.
Next we integrate this over the
xy
plane

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x~\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}}{2\pi}\epsilon^{03jk}\partial_{j}[A_{k}(1-n_{3})]

\displaystyle~=\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\int d^{2}x~\partial_{j}\left(x^{j}(1-n_{3})\right)

\displaystyle~=\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\oint dS_{j}x^{j}(n_{3}-1).

(64)

The lump solution can be easily described by
the inhomogeneous coordinate
f\in\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|f|^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
f\end{array}\right),

providing

\displaystyle\phi\phi^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{1+|f|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\bar{f}\\
f&|f|^{2}\end{array}\right).

Then, the equation of motion reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{w}}f=0,

(71)

with
w\equiv x+iy
.
Hence
f
can be an arbitrary meromorphic function

\displaystyle f(w)=\frac{b_{k-1}w^{k-1}+\cdots+b_{1}w+b_{0}}{w^{k}+a_{k-1}w^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{1}w+a_{0}}.

(72)

The highest power
k
is the lump charge.
The
n_{3}
component for the
k
lumps is given by

\displaystyle n_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\dagger}\tau_{1}\phi=\frac{f+\bar{f}}{1+|f|^{2}}\,,

(73)

\displaystyle n_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\dagger}\tau_{2}\phi=\frac{-i(f-\bar{f})}{1+|f|^{2}}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle n_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\dagger}\tau_{3}\phi=\frac{1-|f|^{2}}{1+|f|^{2}}.

(75)

We have
n_{3}=1
for zeros of
f
,
whereas
n_{3}=-1
for poles of
f
.
In order to evaluate the WZW term, it is enough
for us to know the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle n_{3}-1\to\frac{r^{2}-|b_{k-1}|^{2}}{r^{2}+|b_{k-1}|^{2}}-1=\frac{-2|b_{k-1}|^{2}}{r^{2}+|b_{k-1}|^{2}}.

(76)

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\oint dS_{j}x^{j}(n_{3}-1)

\displaystyle~=\frac{e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B}{4\pi}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\theta~r^{2}\frac{-2|b_{k-1}|^{2}}{r^{2}+|b_{k-1}|^{2}}

\displaystyle~=-e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B|b_{k-1}|^{2}.

(77)

Thus, this term contributes to the energy

\displaystyle E\supset e\mu_{\textrm{B}}B|b_{k-1}|^{2}.

(78)

This implies that
b_{k-1}=0
is energetically favored.

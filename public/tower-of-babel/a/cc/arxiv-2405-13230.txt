q q -Analogs of divisible design graphs and Deza graphs
Dean Crnković, Maarten De Boeck, Francesco Pavese, Andrea Švob

Abstract
Divisible design graphs were introduced in 2011 by Haemers, Kharaghani and Meulenberg. In this paper, we introduce the notion of q q -analogs of divisible design graphs and show that all q q -analogs of divisible design graphs come from spreads, and are actually q q -analogs of strongly regular graphs. Deza graphs were introduced by Erickson, Fernando, Haemers and Hardy in 1999. In this paper, we introduce q q -analogs of Deza graphs. Further, we determine possible parameters, give examples of q q -analogs of Deza graphs and characterize all non-strongly regular q q -analogs of Deza graphs with the smallest parameters.

Introduction

A graph
\Gamma
can be interpreted as a design by taking the vertices of
\Gamma
as points, and the neighborhoods of the vertices as blocks. Such a design is called the
neighborhood design
of
\Gamma
. The adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
is the incidence matrix of its neighborhood design.

A
k
-regular graph on
v
vertices with the property that any two distinct vertices have exactly
\lambda
common neighbors is called a
(v,k,\lambda)
-graph (see
[
15
]
) or
edge-regular
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda)
. The neighborhood design of a
(v,k,\lambda)
-graph is a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design. Haemers, Kharaghani and Meulenberg have defined divisible design graphs (DDGs for short) as a generalization of
(v,k,\lambda)
-graphs (see
[
12
]
).

An incidence structure with
v
points and the constant block size
k
is a (group)
divisible design
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n)
whenever the point set can be partitioned into
m
classes of size
n
, such that two points from the same class are incident with exactly
\lambda_{1}
common blocks, and two points from different classes are incident with exactly
\lambda_{2}
common blocks. A divisible design
D
is said to be
symmetric
(or to have the dual property) if the dual of
D
is a divisible design with the same parameters as
D
.

A
k
-regular graph with
v
vertices is a
divisible design graph (DDG)
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n)
if the vertex set can be partitioned into
m
classes of size
n
such that two vertices of the same class have precisely
\lambda_{1}
common neighbors, and two vertices from different classes have precisely
\lambda_{2}
common neighbors. Note that a DDG with
m=1
, or
n=1
, or
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
is a
(v,k,\lambda)
-graph. If this is the case, we call the DDG
improper
, otherwise it is called
proper
. The definition of a DDG yields the following theorem (see
[
12
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

If
D
is a divisible design graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n)
then its neighborhood design is a symmetric divisible design
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n)
.

Conversely, a symmetric divisible design with a polarity with no absolute points is the neighborhood design of a DDG (see
[
12
]
). We also have the following counting result (
[
12
, Section 2]
).

Lemma 1.2
.

For a divisible design graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n)
we have
k^{2}=k+\lambda_{1}(n-1)+\lambda_{2}n(m-1)
and
v=mn
.

Now we look at a second class of regular graphs, generalizing the DDG’s.
Deza graphs
were introduced by Erickson, Fernando, Haemers and Hardy in
[
9
]
, as a generalization of strongly regular graphs. Let
v
,
k
,
b
and
a
be integers such that
0\leq a<b\leq k<v
. A
k
-regular graph on
v
vertices is called a Deza graph with parameters
(v,k,b,a)
if any two distinct vertices have either
a
or
b
common neighbors. Recently, Deza graphs attended a lot of attention of researchers, see e.g.
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
.

Given a vertex of a Deza graph we know with how many other vertices it has precisely
b
(or precisely
a
) common neighbors (
[
9
, Proposition 1.1]
).

Lemma 1.3
.

For a Deza graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
with parameters
(v,k,b,a)
with
b>a
, and for
u\in V
we have that

\displaystyle|\{v\in V\mid|N_{\Gamma}(u)\cap N_{\Gamma}(v)|=b\}|=\frac{k(k-1)-a(v-1)}{b-a}.

In this statement, we can interchange the roles of
b
and
a
. Note that the result is independent of the vertex
u
.

In
[
8
]
, Delsarte introduced the notion of
q
-analogs of designs
or
subspace design
. The interest in this subject has been renewed recently due to applications of
q
-analog of designs to network coding and distributed storage, (see
[
4
]
). Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
be a vector space of dimension
v
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A subspace of vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
of dimension
k
will be called a
k
-subspace, or
k
-space. A (simple)
t
-
(v,k,\lambda;q)
subspace design consists of a set
B
of
k
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
, called blocks, such that each
t
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
is contained in exactly
\lambda
blocks. Subspace designs are also called designs over finite fields, designs in vector spaces, designs in the
q
-Johnson scheme, and geometric designs (see e.g.
[
13
]
). The set of all
k
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
is always a design, called the
trivial design
. Recently, in
[
5
]
the authors introduced
q
-analogues of group divisible designs and in
[
6
]
gave more results on this topic.

In
[
3
]
, the following definitions of
q
-ary graph, regular
q
-ary graph and strongly regular
q
-ary graph are given. We use the notation
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W}{s}
for the set of
s
-dimensional subspaces of the vector space
W
.

Definition 1.4
.

A
q
-ary graph

{\mathcal{E}}
is a pair
(V,E)
where
V=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W}{1}
and
E\subseteq\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W}{2}
, with
W
a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The elements of
V
and
E
are called
vertices
and
edges
, respectively. If
\langle X,Y\rangle
is an edge, then we say that the vertices
X
and
Y
are
adjacent
.

A
q
-ary graph is thus a hypergraph whose vertices are the vector lines of a vector space
W
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and whose edges correspond to vector planes of
W
. A
q
-ary graph over
W
is naturally embedded in the projective geometry
\PG(W)
. Its vertices are the points of the projective space
\PG(W)
and the edge set is a subset of the line set. Based on a
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}
we can define a corresponding simple graph whose vertices are the vertices of
{\mathcal{E}}
and with two vertices adjacent if they are adjacent in
{\mathcal{E}}
(see also
[
3
]
). A
q
-ary graph will be called (dis)connected if its corresponding simple graph is (dis)connected.

Definition 1.5
.

For a vertex
X
of a
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}
we define its
neighborhood

N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)
as the set of all vertices adjacent to it, and itself.

Definition 1.6
.

A
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}
is
k
-regular
if for any vertex
X
its neighborhood
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)
is a
(k+1)
-dimensional subspace. The set of all
k
-regular
q
-ary graphs with underlying vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
will be denoted by
\RG(v,k;q)
.

It is a basic result in graph theory that a
k
-regular graph has
\frac{vk}{2}
edges. In particular,
vk
is even. For regular
q
-graphs a similar result holds. For a prime power
q
and an integer
i
we denote
\frac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}
by
[i]_{q}
; this is the number of vector lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{i}
.

Lemma 1.7
.

A
k
-regular
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}
has
\frac{[v]_{q}[k]_{q}}{[2]_{q}}
edges.

Proof.

Count the incident vertex-edge pairs.
∎

Definition 1.8
.

A regular
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}\in\RG(v,k;q)
is a strongly regular
q
-ary graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu;q)
if and only if for all vertices
X,Y
, with
X\neq Y
, we have that

|(N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y))\setminus\{X,Y\}|=\begin{cases}\lambda&\text{if $X$ and $Y$ are adjacent,}\\
\mu&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The set of all strongly regular
q
-ary graphs with these parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
is denoted by
\SRG(v,k,\lambda,\mu;q)
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors show the following classification:

Result 1.9
.

[
3
]

If
{\mathcal{E}}\in SRG(v,k,\lambda,\mu;q)
, then

1.

{\mathcal{E}}
is the disjoint union of
\frac{q^{v}-1}{q^{k+1}-1}
complete
q
-graphs on the elements of a
(k+1)
-spread, or

2.

{\mathcal{E}}
has parameters
(v,v-2,\mu-2,\mu;q)
with
\mu=\frac{q^{v-2}-1}{q-1}
and arises from a symplectic polarity
\varphi
on the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
,
v
even, in the following way:
{\mathcal{E}}
is the
q
-ary graph on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
whose edges are the totally isotropic vector planes of
\varphi
.

In Section
2
, we will now introduce and discuss the
q
-analog for divisible design graphs, and in Section
3
, we will discuss the
q
-analog for Deza graphs.

2
q
-Analogs of divisible design graphs

In this section, we introduce the notion of a
q
-analog of divisible design graphs. Throughout the section
v
,
k
, and
n
will denote positive integers and
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
non-negative integers. We first recall the definition of a vector space partition or spread.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
\mathcal{G}
of
n
-spaces of a vector space
W
forms a
vector space partition into subspaces of dimension
n
or
n
-spread
if every vector in
W
different from the zero vector, is in contained in precisely one of the
n
-spaces in
\mathcal{G}
.

It is immediate that an
n
-spread of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
has
\frac{q^{v}-1}{q^{n}-1}
elements.

Remark 2.2
.

Vector space partitions have often been discussed via their interpretation in a projective geometry context. The names ‘spreads’ arises from this context. It is classic result by Segre that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
admits a vector space partition into subspaces of dimension
n
if and only if
n\mid v
(
[
16
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
n
-spread of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
. A
k
-regular
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}\in\RG(v,k;q)
is a
q
-ary divisible design graph
(shortly
q
-DDG) with respect to
\mathcal{G}
if for all vertices
X
and
Y
with
X\neq Y
we have that

|(N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y))\setminus\{X,Y\}|=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}&\text{if a $G\in\mathcal{G}$ exists such that $X,Y\subseteq G$,}\\
\lambda_{2}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In this case
\mathcal{E}
is said to have parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
. The set of all
q
-ary divisible design graphs (for some
n
-spread) with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
is denoted by
\DDG(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},m,n;q)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that a
q
-DDG with
n=v
or
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
is a
q
-SRG with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1})
.

In the following theorem, we show that a
\DDG(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
yields a classical DDG.

Theorem 2.5
.

If a
q
-DDG with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
exists, then a classical DDG with parameters
\left([v]_{q},[k+1]_{q}-1,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},[n]_{q}\right)
exists.

Proof.

Consider a
q
-DDG
{\mathcal{E}}
with parameters
\left(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q\right)
with respect to a vector space partition
\mathcal{G}
. Now define a (simple) graph
\Gamma
with as vertex set the vertices of
{\mathcal{E}}
(i.e. the vector lines of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{v}
) and such that two vertices of
\Gamma
are adjacent if and only if the corresponding 1-subspaces belong to an edge of
{\mathcal{E}}
(every hypergraph edge is replaced by edges consisting of two vertices in it). It is straightforward to check that
\Gamma
is a DDG with parameters
([v]_{q},[k+1]_{q}-1,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},[n]_{q})
, where the classes correspond to the vertex sets of the elements of
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

The following are trivial examples of
q
-DDG’s.

1.

For any
n
-spread, the complete graph is a
q
-DDG with parameters
(v,v-1,\lambda,\lambda,n;q)
, with
\lambda=\frac{q^{v}-1}{q-1}-2
and
n\mid v
.

2.

For any
n
-spread
S
, the disjoint union of the complete graphs on the elements of
S
is a
q
-DDG with parameters
(v,n-1,\lambda,0,n;q)
, with
\lambda=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-2
and
n\mid v
.

3.

For any
n
-spread the empty graph is a
q
-DDG with parameters
(v,0,0,0,n;q)
, with
n\mid v
.

Recall Lemma
1.2
that for classical DDG’s we have
k^{2}=k+\lambda_{1}(n-1)+\lambda_{2}n(m-1)
with
v=mn
. Or in other words,
k^{2}=k+\lambda_{1}(n-1)+\lambda_{2}(v-n)
.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a q-DDG with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
, then

\displaystyle\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)^{2}=\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)+\lambda_{1}\left([n]_{q}-1\right)+\lambda_{2}\left([v]_{q}-[n]_{q}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the
n
-spread with respect to which
{\mathcal{E}}
is defined. We count the triples
(X_{1},X_{2},Y)
with
X_{1},X_{2},Y
vertices of
{\mathcal{E}}
, such that
Y\in N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X_{1})\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X_{2})
and
X_{1}\neq Y\neq X_{2}
. On the one hand, there are
[v]_{q}
choices for
Y
. Given
Y
, we have
[k+1]_{q}-1
choices for both
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
.

On the other hand, there are
[v]_{q}
choices for
X_{1}
. Given
X_{1}
, we have three options for
X_{2}
; let
G
bet the element of
\mathcal{G}
in which
X_{1}
is contained. If
X_{1}=X_{2}
, then we have
[k+1]_{q}-1
choices for
Y
. If
X_{1}\neq X_{2}
and
X_{2}
is contained in
G
, then there are
\lambda_{1}
choices for
Y
. Note that there are
[n]_{q}-1
vertices
X_{2}
in this case. If
X_{2}
is not contained in
G
, then there are
\lambda_{2}
choices for
Y
. Note that there are
[v]_{q}-[n]_{q}
vertices
X_{2}
not in
G
.

We find that

\displaystyle[v]_{q}\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)^{2}=[v]_{q}\left(\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)+\lambda_{1}\left([n]_{q}-1\right)+\lambda_{2}\left([v]_{q}-[n]_{q}\right)\right)\>,

from which the statement immediately follows.
∎

We first look at a particular kind of
q
-DDG’s.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a q-DDG with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},v;q)
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial, namely complete or empty.

Proof.

It immediately follows that
{\mathcal{E}}\in\SRG(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1};q)
. From Theorem
1.9
the statement follows (given the parameters of these graphs). ∎

Note that
\lambda_{2}
is actually not defined in the case described by the previous lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}\in\DDG(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
, then

•

any two vertices in the same spread element are adjacent, or

•

no two vertices in the same spread element are adjacent, or

•

\lambda_{1}=1
and
q=2
.

Proof.

We assume neither the first nor the second possibility in the statement of the lemma is fulfilled. So, there are non-adjacent vertices
X,X^{\prime}
, with
X^{\prime}\neq X
, belonging to the same spread element. Also there are adjacent vertices
Y,Y^{\prime}
belonging to the same spread element.

Since
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)
and
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X^{\prime})
are subspaces, also
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X^{\prime})
is a subspace. Note that
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X^{\prime})
contains the 2-subspace
\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle
. It follows that
\lambda_{1}=\frac{q^{a}-1}{q-1}-2
for some integer
a\geq 2
. Since
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y)
and
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y^{\prime})
are subspaces, also
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y^{\prime})
is a subspace. Note that
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y^{\prime})
contains neither
Y
nor
Y^{\prime}
. It follows that
\lambda_{1}=\frac{q^{b}-1}{q-1}
for some integer
b\geq 0
. We find that

\displaystyle\frac{q^{a}-1}{q-1}-2=\frac{q^{b}-1}{q-1}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad q^{a}-q^{b}=2(q-1),

which implies that
a>b
. We rewrite the previous expression once more and find that

\displaystyle q^{b}\frac{q^{a-b}-1}{q-1}=2.

It follows that
a=2
,
b=1
and
q=2
. Hence,
\lambda_{1}=1
.
∎

Similarly we can also prove.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}\in\DDG(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
, then

•

any two vertices in a different spread element are adjacent, or

•

no two vertices in a different spread element are adjacent, or

•

\lambda_{2}=1
and
q=2
.

Theorem 2.11
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a q-DDG with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},n;q)
with
2\leq n<v
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
we can distinguish between three cases. If any two vertices in a different spread element are adjacent, then the neighborhood of each vertex must equal
\mathbb{F}^{v}_{q}
, since the whole space is the only subspace containing all spread elements but one. So,
{\mathcal{E}}
is the complete graph.

If no two vertices in a different spread element are adjacent,
{\mathcal{E}}
is the disjoint union of
q
-graphs
{\mathcal{E}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
, with
m=\frac{q^{v}-1}{q^{n}-1}
, each defined on an element of the spread. Each of the
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is a
q
-graph fulfilling the conditions of Lemma
2.8
, so is either complete or empty. However, if one of them is the empty graph, then
\lambda_{1}=0
, so they all must be the empty graph. Hence, either
{\mathcal{E}}
is the empty graph, or the disjoint union of complete graphs on the elements of the spread.

Finally we look at the case with
\lambda_{2}=1
and
q=2
. From Theorem
2.7
we find that

\displaystyle\left(2^{k+1}-2\right)^{2}=\left(2^{k+1}-2\right)+\lambda_{1}\left(2^{n}-2\right)+\left(2^{v}-2^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}+3=\lambda_{1}\left(2^{n-1}-1\right)+2^{v-1}-2^{n-1}\;.

(1)

We now distinguish two cases based on the results of Lemma
2.9
, combining the second and third case of this lemma. We can assume without loss of generality that
v\geq k+2
since for
v=k+1
we get the complete graph, and also that
k\geq 1
since for
k=0
we get the empty graph.

•

If any two vertices in the same spread element are adjacent, then
\lambda_{1}=(2^{a}-1)-2
for some integer
a
with
n\leq a\leq k+1
, since
N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X^{\prime})
is a subspace containing both
X
and
X^{\prime}
, for any vertices
X,X^{\prime}
in the same spread element. Moreover, if
k+1=n
, then two vertices from different spread elements cannot be adjacent, contradicting
\lambda_{2}=1
. So
k\geq n
. Substituting
\lambda_{1}=2^{a}-3
in Equation
1
we find

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}=2^{v-1}+2^{a+n-1}-2^{a}-2^{n+1}\;.

(2)

We distinguish between three subcases.

–

If
a=n
then Equation (
2
) reduces to

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}=2^{v-1}+2^{2n-1}-2^{n}-2^{n+1}\;.

From reducing modulo
2^{n+1}
it follows that
k=n
, and thus that
2^{2k}+2^{2k-1}=2^{v-1}+2^{k+1}
. We find that
(v,k)=(5,2)
. However, then
n\nmid v
, a contradiction.

–

If
a=n+1
then Equation (
2
) reduces to

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}=2^{v-1}+2^{2n}-2^{n+2}\;.

Recall that
n\geq 2
, and note that for
n=2
we immediately have a contradiction. So,
n\geq 3
. It follows that
k=n+2
or
v-1=n+2
, since
v\geq k+2
. If
k=n+2
, then
2^{2k+1}-2^{2k-4}-2^{k+2}=2^{v-1}
, a contradiction. If
v-1=n+2
, then
2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}=2^{2n}
, also a contradiction.

–

If
a\geq n+2
, then reducing Equation (
2
) modulo
2^{a}
learns that
k=n+1
; recall that
v-1\geq k+1\geq a
. Equation (
2
) reduces to

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}=2^{v-1}+2^{a+k-2}-2^{a}\;.

It follows that
2^{a+k-2}-2^{a}\equiv 0\pmod{2^{k+1}}
. Since
a\geq n+2
we have that
a\geq 4
, so
k\geq 3
, hence
a+k-2>a
. Therefore it follows from
2^{a+k-2}-2^{a}\equiv 0\pmod{2^{k+1}}
that
a=k+1
. We find that

. Exactly as in the first case we find a contradiction.

•

If no two vertices in the same spread element are adjacent, then
\lambda_{1}=2^{a}-1
for some integer
a
with
a\geq 0
, since

is a subspace, for any vertices
X,X^{\prime}
. If
\lambda_{1}=1
, then
\lambda_{1}=2^{a}-1
for
a=1
. Substituting
\lambda_{1}=2^{a}-1
in Equation
1
we find

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}+2=2^{v-1}+2^{a+n-1}-2^{a}-2^{n}\;.

(3)

Since
n\geq 2
and
v-1\geq k+1\geq 2
, we must have that
a=1
or
k=1
. We distinguish between these two subcases.

–

If

, then Equation (
3
) reduces to

\displaystyle 2^{2k+1}-2^{k+2}-2^{k}+4=2^{v-1}\;.

Recall that

is a non-trivial multiple of

, so

. Hence, reducing the above expression modulo 8, learns that

. Then,
v=5
a prime. However

is a nontrivial divisor of

, a contradiction.

–

If

\displaystyle 0=2^{v-1}+2^{a+n-1}-2^{a}-2^{n}\;,

a contradiction since

and

We conclude that in each of the cases we find no examples, or only the trivial examples.

3

-Analogs of Deza graphs

In the following definition of

-analogs of strongly regular graphs in a similar way as Deza graphs generalize strongly regular graphs.

Definition 3.1
.

A
k
-regular
q
-ary graph
{\mathcal{E}}\in\RG(v,k;q)
is a
q
-ary Deza graph
(shortly
q
-Deza graph
) with parameters
(v,k,b,a;q)
if for all vertices

with
X\neq Y

|(N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y))\setminus\{X,Y\}|\in\{a,b\}

We now prove an analog for Lemma
1.3
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}=(V,E)
be a q-Deza graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2};q)
, with
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. For any vertex

we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{Y\in V\mid|N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y)|=\lambda_{1}\right\}\right|=q\frac{\left(q^{k+1}-2q+1\right)\left(q^{k}-1\right)-\lambda_{2}\left(q^{v-1}-1\right)(q-1)}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(q-1)^{2}}\;.

In this statement, we can interchange the roles of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
. Note that the result is independent of the vertex
X
.

Proof.

We denote
|\{Y\in V\mid|N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(X)\cap N_{{\mathcal{E}}}(Y)|=\lambda_{i}\}|
by
n_{i}
. It follows immediately from the definition of a
q
-ary Deza graph that

\displaystyle n_{1}+n_{2}=[v]_{q}-1\;.

(4)

Counting the triples
(Y_{1},Z,Y_{2})
with
Y_{1},Y_{2},Z
vertices of
\Gamma
and
Z\in N_{\Gamma}(Y_{1})\cap N_{\Gamma}(Y_{2})
and
Y_{1}\neq Z\neq Y_{2}
in two ways, we find that

\displaystyle\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)^{2}=\left([k+1]_{q}-1\right)+\lambda_{1}n_{1}+\lambda_{2}n_{2}.

(5)

Solving the system of Equations (
4
) and (
5
) for

and
n_{2}
gives

\displaystyle n_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{([k+1]_{q}-1)([k+1]_{q}-2)-\lambda_{2}([v]_{q}-1)}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=q\frac{(q^{k+1}-2q+1)(q^{k}-1)-\lambda_{2}(q^{v-1}-1)(q-1)}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(q-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle n_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{([k+1]_{q}-1)([k+1]_{q}-2)-\lambda_{1}([v]_{q}-1)}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=q\frac{(q^{k+1}-2q+1)(q^{k}-1)-\lambda_{1}(q^{v-1}-1)(q-1)}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(q-1)^{2}}

and the statement directly follows.
∎

(v,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2};q)

Assume that any two vertices that have

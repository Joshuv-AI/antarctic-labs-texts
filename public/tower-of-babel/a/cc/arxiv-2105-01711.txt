Categorifications of rational Hilbert series and characters of 𝐅𝐒 op {\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}} modules Thanks: This work was supported by NSF grant DMS-1903040.
Philip Tosteson

Abstract
. We introduce a method for associating a chain complex to a module over a combinatorial category, such that if the complex is exact then the module has a rational Hilbert series. We prove homology–vanishing theorems for these complexes for several combinatorial categories including: the category of finite sets and injections, the opposite of the category of finite sets and surjections, and the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over a finite field and injections. Our main applications are to modules over the opposite of the category of finite sets and surjections, known as 𝐅𝐒 op {\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}} modules. We obtain many constraints on the sequence of symmetric group representations underlying a finitely generated 𝐅𝐒 op {\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}} module. In particular, we describe its character in terms of functions that we call character exponentials. Our results have new consequences for the character of the homology of the moduli space of stable marked curves, and for the equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of the braid matroid.



1.
Introduction

Prompted by the work of Snowden
[
25
]
and Church–Ellenberg–Farb
[
5
]
, there have been many recent applications of the representation theory of categories to sequences of group representations arising in topology, algebra, and combinatorics.

Let
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of representations of groups
G_{n}
over a field
k
. In these applications,
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
can be lifted to a representation of a category
\mathcal{C}
whose objects (or isomorphism classes) are indexed by natural numbers, such that the automorphism group of the
n^{\rm th}
object of
\mathcal{C}
is
G_{n}
. A
\mathcal{C}
–representation
, or
\mathcal{C}
–module
, is a functor
\mathcal{C}\to\Mod k
.
One attempts to use the representation theory of
\mathcal{C}
to show that the whole sequence of representations is determined by a finite amount of data, and to discover universal patterns which the sequence must satisfy. Often, these patterns are expressed in terms of generating functions.

One coarse invariant of a sequence of representations is its sequence of dimensions:
(\dim V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.
The data can be recorded as a generating function, the
Hilbert Series
of
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

h_{V}(t):=\sum_{n}\dim_{k}V_{n}~t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]].

Often, theorems from the representation theory of
\mathcal{C}
imply that
h_{V}(t)
is rational with denominator of a specific form. We list three important instances.

•

Let
\mathbf{FI}
be the category of finite sets and injections. If
V
is a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
module then
h_{V}(t)
is a rational function, with denominator
(1-t)^{d},
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Equivalently, the sequence
(\dim V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
eventually agrees with a polynomial in
n
, proved in characteristic
0
in
[
5
,
22
]
and for all fields by Church–Ellenberg–Farb–Nagpal
[
6
]
.

•

Let
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
be the opposite of the category of finite sets and surjections. If
V
is a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module, then
h_{V}(t)
is a rational function with denominator a power of
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-jt),
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
. This was proved by Sam–Snowden
[
23
]
.

•

Let
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
be the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and linear injections between them. If
V
is a finitely generated
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
module and
{\rm char~}k\neq{\rm char~}\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
h_{V}(t)
is rational with denominator
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-q^{j}t)
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Equivalently,
(\dim V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
eventually agrees with a polynomial in
q^{n}
, proved by Nagpal
[
14
]
.

In this paper we
categorify
these three results, in a uniform way.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{FI},~{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}},
or
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
, and let
w_{d}(t)=(1-t)^{d}
,
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-jt)
, or
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-q^{j}t)
, respectively. Let
V
be a
\mathcal{C}
module. Using methods from poset topology, we construct a chain complex of
\mathcal{C}
modules
\mathrm{K}_{d}(V)
for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
, such that exactness of
\mathrm{K}_{d}(V)
categorifies the equation
w_{d}(t)h_{V}(t)=p(t)
for
p
a polynomial. In each case, we prove a theorem showing if
V
is finitely generated, either
\mathrm{K}_{d}
or a power of
\mathrm{K}_{d}
applied to
V
is exact (modulo torsion).

In particular, our categorification explains why the denominators
(1-t)^{d},~\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-jt),
and
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-q^{j}t)
appear. They are the
Whitney polynomials
of the poset of subsets of a
d
-element set, of set partitions of a
d
-element set, and of subspaces of a
d
-dimensional vector space, respectively (see Definition
2.6
).

1.1.
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules

Our main applications are in the case where
\mathcal{C}
is the opposite of the category of finite sets and surjections,
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
.

In practice,
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules have been used to study sequences of symmetric group representations
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\dim(V_{n})
grows at an exponential rate—too quickly for representation stability in the sense of Church–Farb
[
7
]
to hold. Proudfoot–Young used
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
to study the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of the braid matroid, by constructing an action of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
on the intersection homology of the reciprocal plane of the braid arrangement
[
17
]
. In
[
28
]
, we showed that the homology of the moduli space of stable curves is a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module. In forthcoming work, we extend this result to the Kontsevich space of stable maps. Proudfoot–Ramos constructed finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules in the cohomology of the resonance arrangement
[
20
]
.

In other directions,
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
was used by Sam–Snowden
[
23
]
to prove the Lannes–Schwartz Artinian conjecture: for a finite ring
R
any finitely generated
{\mathbf{VI}}_{R}
module (Definition
3.10
) restricts to a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module. The category of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules is the category of right modules over the commutative operad, and thus is closely tied to homology theories for commutative algebras, and
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
may be used to compute the homology groups of pointed mapping spaces
[
16
]
. In forthcoming work with Sam and Snowden, we relate
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules to representations of the Witt Lie algebra and the monoid of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

Despite these applications, the representation theory of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
is not well understood. For finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
modules and
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
modules in non-describing characteristic, Nagpal has proved structure theorems
[
13
,
14
]
, known as shift theorems, which we use to establish our results. But
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules do not satisfy a shift theorem, and are qualitatively different: the category of finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules has infinite Krull–Gabriel dimension, as opposed to dimension
1
. Little has been known about the sequence of symmetric group representations underlying a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module.

1.2.
Categorification for
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules

For an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module
M
, the complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
we construct takes the form

\Sigma^{d}M\leftarrow\Sigma^{d-1}M^{\oplus{d\choose 2}}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow\Sigma^{1}M^{\oplus(d-1)!}.

Here
\Sigma^{n}M
denotes the
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module
x\mapsto M_{x\sqcup[n]}
, where
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
is the distinguished finite set with
n
elements. In general, the degree
i
term of
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
is
\Sigma^{d-i}M^{\oplus s(d,d-i)}
, where
s(d,d-i)
is the unsigned
Stirling number of the first kind
: by definition
(-1)^{i}s(d,d-i)
is the coefficient of
t^{i}
in the product
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-jt)
. We may extend the definition of
\mathrm{K}_{d}
to chain complexes of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules, by applying
\mathrm{K}_{d}
degreewise and forming the total complex.

To state our categorification for
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules, we recall the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
M
is
finitely generated in degree
\leq d
if
M_{x}
is a finitely generated
k
module for all
x\in{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
with
|x|\leq d
, and for any
y\in{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
,
M_{y}
is spanned by elements of the form
M_{f}(v)
, where
v\in M_{x},f:y\mathchar 13328\relax x
and
|x|\leq d
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module over a field, which is a subquotient of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module that is finitely generated in degree
\leq d
. Then there exists
s\in\mathbb{N},
such that for all
r\geq s
and all
(\ell_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
satisfying
\ell_{t}\geq d
for all
t
and
\ell_{r}\geq d+1
, the complex

\mathrm{K}_{\ell_{1}}\circ\mathrm{K}_{\ell_{2}}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{K}_{\ell_{r}}(M)

is exact.

In particular there exists an
s\geq 1
such that
\mathrm{K}_{d}^{\circ s-1}(\mathrm{K}_{d+1}(M))
is exact. This categorifies the theorem of Sam–Snowden that
h_{M}(t)
is rational with denominator a power of
\mathrm{W}_{d+1}(t):=\prod_{j=0}^{d}(1-jt)
, by categorifying the equation
\mathrm{W}_{d}(t)^{s-1}\mathrm{W}_{d+1}(t)h_{M}(t)=p(t)
for
p
a polynomial. We prove Theorem
1.2
by using Sam–Snowden Gröbner theory to reduce to a combinatorial statement about regular languages associated to
M
. The value of
s
is related to the minimal number of states in the DFAs accepting these languages.

We list two cases in which Theorem
1.2
applies.

Example 1.3
.

For
i,g\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
representation
n\mapsto H_{i}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}},\mathbb{Q})
, where
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}
is the moduli space of marked stable curves, can be extended to an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module. In
[
28
]
we showed that this
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module is a subquotient of one that is finitely generated in degree
\leq 8g^{2}i^{2}+29g^{2}i+16gi^{2}+21g^{2}+10gi
.

Example 1.4
.

The
i^{\rm th}
coefficient of the equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of the
n^{\rm th}
braid arrangement is the character of an
\mathrm{S}_{n}
representation. For fixed
i
, Proudfoot–Young extended this sequence of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
representations to an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module and proved it is a subquotient of one that is finitely generated in degree
\leq 2i

[
17
]
.

1.3.
Characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules

For each
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
the group
\mathrm{S}_{d}\times\mathrm{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)_{n}
. The action of
\mathrm{S}_{d}
is closely related to its action on the Whitney homology of the partition lattice. Via this action, Theorem
1.2
decategorifies to a system of differential equations for the Frobenius character of
M
. By solving these equations, we obtain new results about the character of a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module.
To state them, we take
k=\mathbb{Q}
.

Associated to every
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module
M
is its
character

\sqcup_{n}\mathrm{S}_{n}\to\mathbb{Q}
, given by
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{n}\mapsto\Tr(\sigma,M_{[n]})
. Our first result describes the character of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module in terms of character polynomials and new functions that we call character exponentials.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 1
. Then
\mathbb{X}_{d}:\sqcup_{n}\mathrm{S}_{n}\to\mathbb{Q}
is the class function

\mathbb{X}_{d}(\sigma):=\#\{\text{$d$-cycles of }\sigma\}.

A
character polynomial
is any class function that is a polynomial in the functions
\{\mathbb{X}_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
be an integer partition. We define the
character exponential
of
A
to be class function

A^{\mathbb{X}}:=\prod_{n\geq 1}(\sum_{d|n}da_{d})^{\mathbb{X}_{n}},

where sum is over all natural numbers
d\geq 1
which divide
n
. The product is well defined because any
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{m}
has finitely many cycles, so
A^{\mathbb{X}}(\sigma)
is a natural number.

In this definition, it is important that
0^{0}=1
and
0^{n}=0
for all
n>0
. We give examples to clarify:

Example 1.7
.

If
a_{i}=0
for
i>1
, then
A^{\mathbb{X}}=(a_{1})^{\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{n}}
.

Example 1.8
.

If
a_{1}=0
and
a_{i}=0
for
i>2
, then

A^{\mathbb{X}}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}(2a_{2})^{\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{2n}(\sigma)}&\text{ if all cycles of $\sigma$ have even length}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

In fact, to state our results we need a more general function, which is similar to (but not equal to) a product of a character polynomial and a character exponential.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
and
\nu=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
be integer partitions. We define

{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu}:=\prod_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{X}_{n}\choose m_{n}}(\sum_{d|n}da_{d})^{\mathbb{X}_{n}-m_{n}},

where
{\mathbb{X}_{i}\choose m_{i}}(\sigma)={\mathbb{X}_{i}(\sigma)\choose m_{i}}.
We define the
rank of
\nu
to be
\rank(\nu):=\sum_{i\geq 1}m_{i}
so that
{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}
is a character polynomial of degree
\rank(\nu)
.

The function
{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu}
specializes to the character polynomial
{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}
when
A=1
and to the character exponential
A^{\mathbb{X}}
when
\nu=0
.

Example 1.10
.

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i
and
\nu=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
then

{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $\sigma$ is in the conjugacy class corresponding to $\nu$ }\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Let
d,s\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module has
class
(d,s)
if it satisfies the hypothesis and the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
for
d
and
s
. Then our first result on characters is:

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of class
(d,s)
. Then the character function of
M_{n}
takes the form:

\sum_{\nu,A~{\rm partitions}}c(\nu,A){\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu},

where
c(\nu,A)\in\mathbb{Q}
and
c(\nu,A)=0
if
|A|>d
; or
\rank(\nu)\geq s
; or
|A|=d
and
\rank(\nu)>1
.

In the case
s=1
this sum is finite and Theorem
1.11
sharp, in the sense that the characters of degree
d
projective
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules span the space of character functions. We emphasize that the sum in Theorem
1.11
is not finite when
s>1
. So by itself Theorem
1.11
does not reduce the computation of the character of
M
to a finite problem. However if we restrict the character of
M
to elements in
\mathrm{S}_{n}
whose cycles all have length
\leq C
(which corresponds to setting
\mathbb{X}_{i}=0
for
i>C
), then there are only finitely many terms in the sum. In this case Theorem
1.11
can be rephrased as a rationality theorem for a multivariate generating function (left to the reader). When
C=1
, we precisely recover Sam–Snowden’s rationality theorem.

Example 1.12
.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of class
(2,2)
. Theorem
1.11
states that the character of
M
takes the form

c_{1}~2^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{i}}~+~c_{2}~~\delta_{\rm even}~2^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{2i}}~+c_{3}+~\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}~\mathbb{X}_{n}~+~\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}~\delta_{\text{$n$-cycle}},

where
c_{1},c_{2},b_{n},f_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Here
\delta_{\rm even}(\sigma)
(resp.
\delta_{\text{$n$-cycle}}(\sigma)
) is
1
if all of the cycles of
\sigma
have even length (resp. if
\sigma
is an
n
-cycle) and zero otherwise.

There are further constraints on
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules of class
(d,s)
: if
M
is a subquotient of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module generated in degree
d
we show the character of
M
is
uniquely determined
by its restriction to elements of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
whose cycles have length
\leq d
. Using this, we can prove the following.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
\hat{\Lambda}
be the completed ring of symmetric functions.
There is a finite dimensional subspace
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}\subseteq\hat{\Lambda},
such that if
M
is an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of class
(d,s)
, then its Frobenius character
\ch(M)
is an element of
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
. The dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
is
p(d)+{d+s-1\choose s-1}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}p(i)
where
p(i)
is the number of integer partitions of
i
.

This reduces the computation of the character of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of class
(d,s)
to a finite problem. We describe (refined versions of) the space
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
in more detail in §
10
and §
11
. In particular, we construct bases for
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
. We also show that if
F
is a (restricted) class function, defined on elements of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
whose cycles have length
\leq d
, which takes the form of Theorem
1.11
, then there is a unique lift of
F
to a (full) class function taking the same form. This lift corresponds to an element of
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
, and we describe how to compute it.

Using these methods, we can refine Example
1.12
.

Example 1.14
.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of class
(2,2)
. Then its character takes the form

(1.15)

c_{1}~2^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{i}}~+~c_{2}~2^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{X}_{2i}}~\delta_{\rm even}~+~c_{3}~+~c_{4}~\mathbb{X}_{1}~+~c_{5}\sum_{n\geq 2}n~\mathbb{X}_{n}+~\sum_{n=0}^{2}f_{n}~\delta_{n-\text{cycle}}

where
c_{n},f_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
. This behavior is representative: in general the coefficients
c(\nu,A)
of Theorem
1.11
are governed by recurrence relations. There are class
(2,2)

{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules whose characters span the space of characters of the form (
1.15
) with
c_{5}=0
. We do not know an example of a class
(2,2)

{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module with
c_{5}\neq 0
, but heuristic comparisons with
\mathbf{FI}_{d}
modules suggest that they may exist.

Our final result on
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module characters is an analog of multiplicity stability for
\mathbf{FI}
modules.

Definition 1.16
.

For
\lambda
an integer partition
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\dots
and
n\geq\lambda_{1}
, we let
(n,\lambda)
be the integer partition
n\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots
. We write
|\lambda|=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
. Given an
\mathrm{S}_{|\lambda|}
representation
V
over a field of characteristic zero, we write
\mult_{\lambda}(V)
to denote the multiplicity of the irreducible representation corresponding to
\lambda
in
V
.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
\lambda
be an integer partition. Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module over a field of characteristic zero. Then the generating function

\sum_{n\geq|\lambda|}\mult_{(n,\lambda)}(M_{|\lambda|+n})~t^{n+|\lambda|}

is rational. If
M
is of class
(d,s)
, then the roots of the denominator are roots of unity of order
\leq d
and
\mult_{(n,\lambda)}(M_{|\lambda|+n})
is eventually a quasi-polynomial of degree
\leq ds
.

Whereas for
\mathbf{FI}
modules the multiplicities in Theorem
1.17
are eventually constant, for
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules they are eventually quasi-polynomials.

1.4.
Construction of chain complexes

Let
(\mathcal{C},\oplus)
be a small monoidal category. Let
d\in\mathcal{C}
be an object, and
\mathcal{C}/d
be the over-category. To construct functors
\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
from
\mathcal{C}
modules to chain complexes of
\mathcal{C}
modules, we proceed in the following simple way. First we restrict along
\oplus:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}/d\to\mathcal{C}
to obtain a functor
\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Mod(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}/d)
. Then we apply a functor
F:\Mod(\mathcal{C}/d)\to\Ch(\Mod k)
in the second factor to obtain
\Mod(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}/d)\to\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
. The functors we use are the composite,
(\mathrm{id}\times F)\circ\Res^{\oplus}:\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
.

Of course, there are many possible choices for
F
: up to quasi-isomorphism they are parameterized by chain complexes of
(\mathcal{C}/d){{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules. When
\mathcal{C}/d
is equivalent to a poset
P

there is a particularly natural choice: a bar construction
\mathrm{B}_{P}:\Mod P\to\Mod k
, which we recall in §
2
. When
P
satisfies a Cohen–Macaulay property, the functor
\mathrm{B}_{P}
can be replaced by a smaller functor
\mathrm{K}_{P}
, which is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}_{P}
. Corresponding to these two constructions, there are functors
\mathrm{K}_{d},\mathrm{B}_{d}:\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
. The conditions for
\mathrm{B}_{d}
and
\mathrm{K}_{d}
to exist are satsified for any object
d\in\mathcal{C}
when
\mathcal{C}
is one of the categories
\mathbf{FI},{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}},{\mathbf{VI}}_{L},
or
{\mathbf{VI}}\mathbf{C}_{L}
. Here
L
is a field and
{\mathbf{VI}}_{L}
(resp.
{\mathbf{VI}}\mathbf{C}_{L}
) denotes the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over
L
and with injections (resp. injections together with a splitting, see
[
19
]
).

When
\mathcal{C}
is an EI-category, there is a natural notion of
\mathcal{C}
-module homology, which in the case of
\mathbf{FI}
modules reduces to
\mathbf{FI}
module homology
[
4
]
. The complexes we use admit a more general definition in terms of
\mathcal{C}
module homology which we discuss in §
4
, although we do not use it elsewhere in the paper.

One feature of our arguments is that, for the most part, we do not deal with the complexes
\mathrm{K}_{d}
or
\mathrm{B}_{d}
directly. Instead, we work with poset representations before applying the functors
\mathrm{K}_{d}
or
\mathrm{B}_{d}
, and use a couple of general facts to prove exactness. So although, when it exists, the complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}
is more elegant than
\mathrm{B}_{d}
, for the first half of the paper the two are interchangeable. The applications to
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules in the second part use
\mathrm{K}_{d}
and the homology of the partition lattice.

1.5.
Relation to other work

Beyond Sam–Snowden’s theorem on the rationality of Hilbert series, we are only aware of two previously-known statements about the characters of finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules in the literature. They are both elementary: they follow from decomposition of the free
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module generated in degree
d
into irreducibles. The first states that if
M
is a subquotient of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module generated in degree
d
, then the irreducible representations appearing in its decomposition have at most
d
rows. The second is that the multiplicity of
(n,\lambda)
in
M_{n+|\lambda|}
is bounded above by a polynomial.

There is a long history of using poset topology for what we now call categorification, going at least back to Rota
[
21
]
. Our only novelty is in the specific application of these methods. The complexes associated to poset representations that we use were first introduced by Baclawski
[
2
,
1
]
, in terms of the cohomology of constructible sheaves on the Alexandroff space of the poset. Baclawski also gave explicit Bar constructions, and the smaller complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}
in the case of Cohen–Macaulay posets. They may also be thought of as computing derived functors for the tensor product with a representation of the opposite poset, as discussed in §
4
. To minimize technical overhead, we use explicit complexes.

In the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{FI}
the complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
is simply the complex obtained by iterating the functor

M\mapsto\cone(M\to\Sigma M),

d
times. This construction and its categorification of rationality is well-known. The complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
appears implicitly in many of arguments about the structure theory of
\mathbf{FI}
-modules, which proceed inductively using
M
,
\Sigma M
and the cokernel of
M\to\Sigma M
.

The way we construct chain complexes is, heuristically, adjoint to the central stability complexes which appear in the representation stability literature, introduced in
[
19
]
and axiomatized in
[
15
]
. Whereas central stability complexes are constructed by induction along the monoidal functor
-\oplus-:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
, ours are constructed via restriction. We do not know of a direct relationship between these two types of complexes, although there is one with
\mathbf{FI}
-module homology
[
4
,
18
]
(which goes under the name central stability homology in Putman’s original paper on central stability).

In
[
24
, §5]
Sam–Snowden prove that the Hilbert series of a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}_{r}
module (see
[
23
, §7.1]
for definition) is a sum of functions of the form
{X\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu}
for
A=1^{d}
and
d\leq r
. They also prove a statement that categorifies the rationality of Hilbert series of finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}_{r}
modules. Our categorifications are in the same spirit as Sam–Snowden’s, but their complexes do not fit into our framework. Given a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}_{r}
module
M
, the complex Sam–Snowden use to categorify the rationality of
h_{M}(t)
depends on the choice of subspaces of
k^{\oplus r}
specifically adapted to
M
. In contrast, the complexes we use depend on a discrete set of choices. By generalizing our definitions slightly, it is possible to obtain Sam–Snowden’s complexes corresponding to the subspaces of
k^{\oplus r}
that are linearizations of subsets
s\subseteq[r]
.

Sam–Snowden’s Gröbner theory, developed in
[
23
]
, is the source of many results about Hilbert series of representations of combinatorial categories. Our proof of Theorem
1.2
relies heavily on their ideas: we reduce to the case of monomial
{\mathbf{OS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules, and eventually to proving a combinatorial statement about poset ideals associated to certain ordered DFAs (concepts they introduced). In
[
23
, §11.2]
they ask whether it is possible to prove rationality results for enhanced Hilbert series for the combinatorial categories they consider. We view our results as an affirmative answer in the case of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules, and the framework of this paper as a new strategy for approaching other cases.

Chain complexes categorifying rationality

2.
Poset homology

In this section, following Baclawski
[
1
]
, we associate chain complexes to poset representations. Then we discuss several examples, and recall elementary properties of these complexes. Throughout, we let
k
be a commutative ring.

Definition 2.1
.

For any poset
Q
, we write
\mathrm{N}Q
for the
order complex of
Q
, the simplicial complex whose
r
simplices are the set of chains

q_{0}<q_{1}<\dots<q_{r}\in Q.

Equivalently
\mathrm{N}Q
is the geometric realization of the simplical set
[r]\mapsto\{q_{0}\leq\dots\leq q_{r}\in Q\}.

Definition 2.2
.

When
Q
has top and bottom elements
\hat{1},\hat{0}
, we define
Z_{Q}\subseteq\mathrm{N}Q
to be the subcomplex
\mathrm{N}(Q-\hat{0})\cup\mathrm{N}(Q-\hat{1})
. In other words,
Z_{Q}
is the union of all simplices corresponding to chains
q_{0}<\dots<q_{r}
where either
q_{0}\neq\hat{0}
or
q_{1}\neq\hat{1}
. When
\hat{0}=\hat{1}
,
Z_{Q}=\emptyset
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a poset
P
is
graded
if for any
p\leq q\in P
all maximal chains
p=p_{0}\prec p_{1}\prec\dots\prec p_{\ell}=q
have the same length. When
P
is graded, we define
\mathrm{r}(p,q)
to be
\ell
. When
P
has a top element, we put
\mathrm{r}(p):=\mathrm{r}(p,\hat{1}).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a finite poset, with top element
\hat{1}
. For
p\in P
we define the
Möbius number
of
p
, denoted
\mu(p)
, to be the Euler characteristic of the pair
(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]})
. When
P
is graded, we define the
unsigned Möbius number
to be
\mathaccent 869{\mu}(p)=(-1)^{\mathrm{r}(p)}\mu(p)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Our definition of
\mu(p)
agrees with
\mu(p,\hat{1})
as usually defined (as for example in
[
29
, §1.2]
). Instead of using the pair
(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]})
, it is more common to interpret the Möbius function topologically in terms of the reduced homology of
\mathrm{N}(p,\hat{1})
. Whenever
p\neq\hat{1}
, the homology groups of these complexes are related by

\mathaccent 869{H}_{s-2}(\mathrm{N}(p,\hat{1}))=H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]})\text{ for all }s\geq 0,

as can be seen by writing the simplicial chains for both. In this paper, we prefer the latter homology groups because they lead to cleaner formulas and behave more simply for products of posets.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
P
be a finite graded poset with top element
\hat{1}
. The
Whitney Polynomial
of

is

W_{P}(t):=\sum_{p\in P}\mu(p)~t^{\mathrm{r}(p)}.

Now we define the Bar complex associated to a

representation.

Definition 2.7
.

A

is a functor

. For a representation

for the value of

at

, and if

for the associated morphism

.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
P
be a poset with top element
\hat{1}
, and let

be a
P
representation. We define
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
be the following chain complex. In degree

\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)_{s}:=\bigoplus_{\hat{1}>p_{1}>p_{2}>\dots>p_{s}}M_{p_{s}}.

The differential of

is given by an alternating sum
d_{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i}
, where if we represent an element of
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)_{s}
as a tuple

for

, the boundary

\partial_{i}(\hat{1}>\dots>p_{s},m)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $i=0$}\\
(\hat{1}>\dots>p_{i-1}>p_{i+1}>\dots>p_{s},m)&\text{if $s>i>0$ }\\
(\hat{1}>\dots>p_{s-1},M_{p_{s}p_{s-1}}(m))&\text{if $i=s$}.\par\end{cases}

Definition 2.9
.

Let

be a poset, and

a group acting on

-equivariant

is a

When

2.1.
Koszul complexes

Following Baclawski
[
1
]
, when the poset
P
satisfies a connectivity condition it is possible to replace
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
by a smaller complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
, which we define in this subsection.

Definition 2.10
.

We say that a poset
P
is
upper CM over
k
if it is graded, has a top element
\hat{1}
, and satisfies the following conditions for every
p\in P
:

H_{j}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)=0,~\text{ for all }0\leq j<\mathrm{r}(p),

and
H_{\mathrm{r}(p)}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)
is a free
k
module.

Example 2.11
.

If
P
is Cohen–Macaulay over
k
then by defintion
\mathaccent 869{H}_{s-2}(\mathrm{N}(x,y))=0
for
s<\rank(x,y)

[
3
, Defn 3.1]
. Therefore
P
is upper CM over
k
. Examples include arbitrary geometric lattices
[
9
]
.

Since
\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}]
is
\mathrm{r}(p)
dimensional, when
P
is upper CM the unsigned Möbius number computes the rank of the the top nonvanishing homology group of
(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,1]})
. In other words we have

H_{\mathrm{r}(p)}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)\cong k^{\oplus\mathaccent 869{\mu}(p)}.

Definition 2.12
.

When
P
is upper CM over
k
, there is a functor from
P
representations to chain complexes of
k
modules,
\mathrm{K}_{P}
, defined as follows.

Let
M
be a
P
-representation.
We filter
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
by defining

F_{i}(\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)_{s}):=\bigoplus_{\hat{1}>\dots>p_{s}~|~\mathrm{r}(p_{s})\leq i}M_{p_{s}}.

The complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
is defined to be the
E_{1}
page of the spectral sequence associated to this filtration.

Let us describe
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
more concretely, by computing the
E_{1}
page. The associated graded complex of the filtration
F_{i}
has the same chain groups as
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
but
\partial_{s}
acts by zero: in other words the differential of the
E_{0}
page is
d_{0}^{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i}.
Since
d_{0}^{s}
preserves the grading by
p\in P
, the
E_{0}
page splits as a direct sum of complexes
\bigoplus_{p}C(p)\otimes M_{p}
for
p\in P
where

C(p)_{s}:=\bigoplus_{\hat{1}>p_{1}>\dots>p_{s-1}>p}k.

By definition, we see that
C(p)
is the chain complex computing the simplicial homology of
(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]})
. Because
P
is upper CM the homology of
C(p)
is concentrated in degree
\mathrm{r}(p)
, and it has rank
\mathaccent 869{\mu}(p)
. Therefore the spectral sequence associated to the filtration degenerates after the
E_{1}
page, and on the
E_{1}
page it takes the form

M_{\hat{1}}\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p,~\mathrm{r}(p,\hat{1})=1}M_{p}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p,~\mathrm{r}(p,\hat{1})=s}H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)\otimes M_{p}\leftarrow\dots

This is the complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
. Non-canonically it takes the form

M_{\hat{1}}\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p\in P,~r(p)=1}M_{p}\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p\in P,~\mathrm{r}(p)=2}M_{p}^{\oplus\mathaccent 869{\mu}(p)}\leftarrow\dots.

The differential is induced by the action of
(-1)^{s}\partial_{s}
.

Both
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
and
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
compute the same homology groups, but
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
depends more closely on the combinatorics of
P
and is much smaller than
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
.

Proposition 2.13
.

There is an edge map
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
which induces a canonical isomorphism
H_{\bullet}(\mathrm{K}_{P}(M))\cong H_{\bullet}(\mathrm{B}_{P}(M))
.

Proof.

The complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
can be identified with the subcomplex of
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
which in degree
s
is

\ker(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i})\subseteq\bigoplus_{\hat{1}\succ p_{1}\succ\dots\succ p_{s}}M_{p_{s}}.

Since the spectral sequence degenerates, this inclusion is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

When
P
is an upper CM poset,
G
is a group acting on
P
, and
M
is a
G
-equivariant
P
representation, the
G
action on
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
induces one on
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
. The degree
s

\bigoplus_{p,~\mathrm{r}(p,\hat{1})=s}H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)\otimes M_{p},

is a
G
representation in the obvious way:
g\in G
acts by tensoring and summing the maps
g:M_{p}\to M_{g(p)}
and
g:H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]};k)\to H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[g(p),\hat{1}],Z_{[g(p),\hat{1}]};k)
induced by the functoriality of homology.

Remark 2.14
.

From an abstract perspective both
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
and
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
compute the derived functors
of tensoring with a

representation

(see Definition

). The complex
\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)
arises from the Bar resolution of

, and the complex
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
arises from a minimal resolution (assuming that

is a field). Essentially, the complex

is a Koszul complex: there is a generalization of the theory of Koszul duality from graded algebras to graded linear categories, such that the category

is Koszul if

is Cohen–Macaulay
[

,
12
]
. In this case,
\mathrm{K}_{P}(M)
is a Koszul complex.

Example 2.16
.

The partition lattice

[

.

Definition 2.17
.

For a finite set

the

\mathbf{B}(x)
is the poset of subsets of

. We write
\mathbf{B}(n):=\mathbf{B}([n])

Example 2.18
.

The Boolean lattice

\mathaccent 869{\mu}(s)=1
for every

Its Whitney polynomial is

[

.

Definition 2.19
.

Let

for the poset of subspaces of

Example 2.20
.

The poset fon
\mathrm{B}(V)
is

\mathaccent 869{\mu}(w)=q^{c\choose 2},
where

Let

2.3.
Complexes associated to product posets

Let
P\times Q
be a product of two posets with top elements
\hat{1}_{P}
and
\hat{1}_{Q}
. Suppose we are given a
P\times Q
module
M
. We may consider
M
as a functor
P\to\Mod Q
, and post-composing with
\mathrm{B}_{Q}
we obtain a chain complex of
P
modules,
p\mapsto\mathrm{B}_{Q}(M_{(p,-)})
. We may apply
\mathrm{B}_{P}
to this complex, as described in §
1.8
, to obtain a chain complex of
k
modules.

Definition 2.22
.

We define
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(M)
to be the chain complex
\mathrm{B}_{P}(p\mapsto\mathrm{B}_{Q}(M_{(p,-)}))
and
\mathrm{B}_{(Q,P)}(M)
to be the chain complex
\mathrm{B}_{Q}(q\mapsto\mathrm{B}_{P}(M_{(-,q)}))
.

There is an isomorphism of complexes
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(M)\cong\mathrm{B}_{(Q,P)}(M)
. More concretely, their degree
n
term is

\bigoplus_{i+j=n}\bigoplus_{p_{i}<\dots<\hat{1}_{P}}\bigoplus_{q_{j}<\dots<\hat{1}_{Q}}M_{(p_{i},q_{j})},

and their differentials agree up to a sign introduced by the definition of the total complex.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
P,Q
be finite posets with top elements. Let
M
and
N
be
P
and
Q
representations, respectively. Let
M\mathchar 9218\relax N
be the
P\times Q
representation
(p,q)\mapsto M_{p}\otimes N_{q}
. Then
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(M\mathchar 9218\relax N)\cong\mathrm{B}_{P}(M)\otimes\mathrm{B}_{Q}(N)
. In particular if
\mathrm{B}_{Q}(N)
is exact, then
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(M\mathchar 9218\relax N)
is exact.

Proof.

This is true by the definition of the tensor product of chain complexes.
∎

We may extend the definition of
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}
to a finite product of posets with top elements
P_{1}\times P_{2}\times\dots\times P_{r}
in the obvious way to obtain a functor
\mathrm{B}_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})}
. Proposition
2.23
carries over to this setting, and we also have the following.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
be finite posets with top elements and let
L
be a
P_{1}\times\dots\times P_{r}
representation. We put
Q:=P_{2}\times\dots\times P_{r}
, and for
p\in P_{1}
write

for the
Q
representation

. Suppose that that for every

we have that
\mathrm{B}_{(P_{2},\dots,P_{r})}(L|_{p\times Q})
is exact. Then
\mathrm{B}_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})}(L)
is exact.

Proof.

Applying
\mathrm{B}_{(P_{2},\dots,P_{r})}
to

, by hypothesis we obtain an exact complex of
P_{1}
representations
p\mapsto\mathrm{B}_{(P_{2},\dots,P_{r})}(L|_{p\times Q})
. Denote this complex by

. Since
\mathrm{B}_{P_{1}}
is exact,
\mathrm{B}_{P_{1}}(C)\cong\mathrm{B}_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})}(L)

∎

Remark 2.25
.

If the definition of
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}
seems ad hoc, we remark that for any
P\times Q
representation

there is a canonical quasi-isomorphism
EZ:\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(L)\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}}\mathrm{B}_{P\times Q}(L)
. To construct it, note that
\mathrm{B}_{(P,Q)}(L)
is the totalization of a normalized bicomplex of a bisimplicial abelian group, and
\mathrm{B}_{P\times Q}(L)
is the normalized chain complex of that bisimpicial group’s diagonal. Then apply the Eilenberg–Zilber theorem stated in
[
30
, §8.5]
. In fact, if
P
and

are upper CM, then so is

and

induces an

between the subcomplexes
\mathrm{K}_{(P,Q)}(L)
and
\mathrm{K}_{P\times Q}(L)
. We use

rather than

, because all the properties we need are immediate for it.
∎

2.4.
Poset homology of ideals

Definition 2.26
.

Associated to any upward ideal
I
in a poset
P
, there is a functor
\mathrm{S}I:P\to\Set
given by

p\mapsto\begin{cases}*&\text{if $p\in I$}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
*
denotes the terminal set
\{\{\emptyset\}\}
. (The action of
\mathrm{S}I
on morphisms is uniquely determined by its value on objects). Postcomposing with the free functor from sets to
k
modules we obtain a
P
representation, denoted
kI
, such that
kI_{p}=k
if
p\in I
and
kI_{p}=0
otherwise.

The order complex
\mathrm{N}(I-\hat{1})
is a subcomplex of
\mathrm{N}(I)
, and by definition
\mathrm{B}(kI)
is the chain complex computing the relative simplicial homology
(\mathrm{N}I,\mathrm{N}(I-\hat{1}))
with coefficients in
k
.
We need to know that the homology of the bar complex applied to a principal ideal is zero.

Proposition 2.27
.

Let
P
be a finite poset with top element
\hat{1}
. Let
x\in P,x\neq\hat{1}
, and let
P_{\geq x}=\{p\in P~|~p\geq x\}
be the principal ideal generated by
x
. Then the homology of
\mathrm{B}(kP_{\geq x})
vanishes.

Proof.

Since both
I
and
I-\hat{1}
contain
x
as their smallest element,
\mathrm{N}I
and
\mathrm{N}(I-\hat{1})
are both contractible, and so the relative homology vanishes.
∎

We will also need a simple fact about
P
-sets.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
P
be a poset with top element
\hat{1}
. Let
F:P\to\Set
be a functor such that
F_{pq}
is an injection for all
p\leq q\in P
. Given
x\in F_{\hat{1}}
, let
F\langle x\rangle_{p}:=\{y\in F_{p}~|~F_{p\hat{1}}(y)=x\}
. Then
F\langle x\rangle
is a subfunctor, and there are isomorphisms
\bigsqcup_{x\in F_{\hat{1}}}F\langle x\rangle\cong F

and
F\langle x\rangle\cong\mathrm{S}I\langle x\rangle
where
I\langle x\rangle
is the ideal

I\langle x\rangle:=\{p\in P~|~\exists y\in F_{p},F_{p\hat{1}}(y)=x\}.

Proof.

This is straightforward. The map
\bigsqcup_{x\in F_{\hat{1}}}F\langle x\rangle\to F
is induced by the inclusions
F\langle x\rangle\subseteq F
and it is an injection because if
x,y
are distinct elements of
F_{\hat{1}}
the fact that the transition maps are inclusion implies that
F\langle x\rangle\cap F\langle y\rangle=\emptyset
. The isomorphism
\mathrm{S}I\langle x\rangle\to F\langle x\rangle
is given by defining the image of
*
in
F\langle x\rangle_{p}
to be the unique element
y
such that
F_{p\hat{1}}(y)=x
, if it exists.
∎

3.
Chain complexes associated to categories

In this section, we apply the definitions of
\mathrm{B}_{P}
and
\mathrm{K}_{P}
to construct chain complexes associated to modules over combinatorial categories.

3.1.
Poset vs Posetal

In our main examples,
\mathbf{FI},{\mathbf{VI}}_{q},
and
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
, the over categories of objects are equivalent to posets, but
not
isomorphic to posets. Although this may seem to be a fussy distinction, we want our complexes to be canonical so that they carry group actions. Accordingly, we recall the following notion.

Definition 3.1
.

A small category
\mathcal{J}
is
posetal
if there is at most one morphism between any two objects of
\mathcal{J}
.

When
\mathcal{J}
is posetal, its set of isomorphism classes
|\mathcal{J}|
forms a poset, by defining
[j]\leq[j^{\prime}]
if there is a morphism
j\to j^{\prime}
. There is a canonical functor

\mathcal{J}\to(|\mathcal{J}|,\leq),~j\mapsto[j]

which is an equivalence of categories. Inducing and restricting along this functor, we obtain an equivalence of categories between
\Mod\mathcal{J}
and
\Mod|\mathcal{J}|
. Given a
\mathcal{J}
module
M
, the associated
|\mathcal{J}|
module takes an equivalence class
x\in|\mathcal{J}|
to

|M|_{x}:=\frac{\bigsqcup_{j\in\mathcal{J},~[j]=x}M_{j}}{\text{ $(j,m)\sim(j^{\prime},m^{\prime})$ if for the unique map $f:j\to j^{\prime}$, $M_{f}(m)=m^{\prime}$}}.

For any
j\in\mathcal{J}
such that
[j]=x
there is a canonical isomorphism
M_{j}\to|M|_{x}
. And if
x\leq y\in|\mathcal{J}|
there is a unique
|M|_{x}\to|M|_{y}
such for any
j,k\in\mathcal{J}
with
[j]=x,[k]=y
the obvious diagram commutes.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a posetal category. For any functor
F_{|\mathcal{J}|}:\Mod|\mathcal{J}|\to\mathcal{D}
then we define
F_{\mathcal{J}}
to be the functor
F_{\mathcal{J}}(M):=F_{|\mathcal{J}|}(|M|)
.

Thus when
\mathcal{J}
is posetal and has a terminal object (equivalently
|\mathcal{J}|
has a top element), then we have a definition of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)
. And when
|\mathcal{J}|
is upper CM we have a definition of
\mathrm{K}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)
. Similarly, given a product of posetal categories with terminal objects
\mathcal{J}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{J}_{r}
, we may define
\mathrm{B}_{(\mathcal{J}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{J}_{r})}
.

Finally, suppose that
G
is a group which acts on
\mathcal{J}
by isomorphisms. Then
G
acts on
|\mathcal{J}|
, and if
M
is a
G
-equivariant
\mathcal{J}
representation,
|M|
is a
G
-equivariant
|\mathcal{J}|
representation. If
x\in|\mathcal{J}|,j\in\mathcal{J}
and
[j]=x
, then for all
g\in G
we have
g(x)=g([j])
and
g:M_{x}\to M_{g(x)}
is the unique morphism compatible with
g:M_{j}\to M_{g(j)}
.

3.2.
Construction of complexes

Let
(\mathcal{C},\oplus)
be a monoidal category, and
d\in\mathcal{C}
. Restricting along the functor
\oplus:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}/d\to\mathcal{C}
,
(c,c^{\prime}\to d)\mapsto c\oplus c^{\prime}
, we obtain a functor

\Mod\mathcal{C}\to(\Mod\mathcal{C}/d)^{\mathcal{C}},

taking a
\mathcal{C}
module
M
to the functor
c\mapsto(M_{c\oplus-})|_{\mathcal{C}/d}
.
Note that
\mathrm{id}:d\to d
is always a terminal object of
\mathcal{C}/d
, so if
\mathcal{C}/d
is posetal contains a top element.

Definition 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{C}/d
is posetal, we define
\mathrm{B}_{d}:\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
to be the composite

\Mod\mathcal{C}\to(\Mod\mathcal{C}/d)^{\mathcal{C}}\overset{\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}}{\longrightarrow}\Ch(\Mod k)^{\mathcal{C}}=\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C}).

Thus for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
, the complex
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)_{c}\in\Ch(\Mod k)
is
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}(M_{c\oplus-}|_{\mathcal{C}/d})
.

We define the complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}
similarly.

Definition 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{C}/d
is equivalent to an upper CM poset, then we define
\mathrm{K}_{d}:\Mod\mathcal{C}\to\Mod\mathcal{C}
to be the composite functor
\Mod\mathcal{C}\to(\Mod\mathcal{C}/d)^{\mathcal{C}}\overset{\mathrm{K}_{\mathcal{C}/d}}{\longrightarrow}\Ch(\Mod\mathcal{C})
.

The following elementary proposition shows that
\mathrm{B}_{d}
is always defined for categories in which all morphisms are monomorphisms.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. Then
\mathcal{C}/d
is posetal for all
d\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if every morphism of
\mathcal{C}
is a monomorphism.

For
d\in\mathcal{C}
, the group
\Aut(d)
acts on the category
\mathcal{C}/d
by postcomposition. For
N
a
\mathcal{C}
module, the
\mathcal{C}/d
module
N|_{\mathcal{C}/d}
is
\Aut(d)
equivariant: an element
g\in\Aut(d)
acts by
(f,n)\mapsto(g\circ f,n)
for
f:c\to d
and
n\in N_{f}
. Therefore, when
\mathcal{C}/d
is posetal (resp.
\mathcal{C}/d
is equivalent to an upper
CM
poset) the group
\Aut(d)
acts on
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
(resp.
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
) for all
\mathcal{C}
modules
M
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}/d
is posetal. Instead of passing through
|\mathcal{C}/d|
, we may obtain a complex that is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
by restricting
(M_{c\oplus-})|_{\mathcal{C}/d}
to a skeleton
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{C}/d
, and applying
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{S}}
(since
\mathcal{S}
is necessarily a poset). Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is
a skeleton of
\mathcal{C}/d
if any two isomorphic objects of
\mathcal{S}
are equal, and every object of
\mathcal{C}/d
is isomorphic to one in
\mathcal{S}
.

3.3.
Examples

Now we discuss the complexes arising in our main three examples.

Definition 3.6
.

When
\mathcal{C},\oplus
is a monoidal category, for any
c\in\mathcal{C}
we define
\Sigma^{c}M
to be the
\mathcal{C}
module
(\Sigma^{c}M)_{c^{\prime}}:=M_{c\oplus c^{\prime}}
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
d\in{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
. Then
|{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}/d|
is
\mathrm{P}(d)
, the lattice of set partitions of
d
. A surjection
f:d\mathchar 13328\relax y
corresponds to the following partition of
d
:

are in the same block of
p
if
f(i)=f(j)
. Two surjections are isomorphic if and only if their associated partitions agree.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module. Then
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
takes the form

\Sigma^{[d]}M\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{P}(d),~r(p)=1}\Sigma^{p}M\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{P}(d),~r(p)=s}\Sigma^{p}M\otimes H_{s}(\mathrm{N}[p,\hat{1}],Z_{[p,\hat{1}]})\leftarrow\dots

(Recall that for us the partition
p
equals its set of blocks). The first differential is given by summing the maps induced by the surjection
d\mathchar 13328\relax p
for each partition
p
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathbf{FI}
be the category of finite sets and injections.

Example 3.9
.

Let
d\in\mathbf{FI}
. Then
|\mathbf{FI}/d|
is the poset of subsets of
d
, and for an
\mathbf{FI}
module

\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
takes the form

\Sigma^{d}M\leftarrow\bigoplus_{i\in d}\Sigma^{d-i}M\bigoplus_{\{i,j\}\subseteq d}\leftarrow\Sigma^{d-\{i,j\}}M\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow M,

where, up to signs, the differentials are induced by the inclusions of subsets. Notice that the action by
\mathrm{S}_{d}
is nontrivial: for instance
\mathrm{S}_{d}
acts on the highest term of the complex by the
\mathrm{id}_{M}\otimes\sgn
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
{\mathbf{VI}}_{R}
is the category of

for some

be a prime power. We define
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}:={\mathbf{VI}}_{R}
.

Example 3.11
.

For
w\in{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
the poset
|{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}/w|
is the poset of subspaces of

. Then
\mathrm{K}_{w}(M)
takes the form

\Sigma^{w}M\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow M\otimes{\rm Stein}(w),

where

\bigoplus_{v\subseteq w,~\codim(v)=s}\Sigma^{v}M\otimes{\rm Stein}(w/v).

More generally, the complex
\mathrm{K}_{d}

, the poset of submodules of a finitely generated module is Cohen–Macaulay.

Example 3.12
.

Let

3.4.
Rational Hilbert series

We discuss how exactness of the complex
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
categorifes rationality, in an axiomatic setting. (In our three examples, it is easy to see the categorification directly using
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
). The key is the following general identity.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small monoidal category, and let
d\in\mathcal{C}
be an object with
\mathcal{C}/d
equivalent to a finite poset. Let
M
be a
\mathcal{C}
module over a commutative Noetherian ring
k
, such that
M_{c}
is finitely generated for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
. Then for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
we have

[\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)_{c}]=\sum_{[f:c^{\prime}\to d]\in|\mathcal{C}/d|}\mu([f])[M_{c\oplus c^{\prime}}]

in the Grothendieck group of finitely generated
k
modules.

Proof.

We may neglect the differentials of
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
. Then, as in §
2.1
, the complex
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
splits as a sum of
C([f])\otimes M_{c\oplus c^{\prime}}
over
[f:c^{\prime}\to d]\in|\mathcal{C}/d|
. By the definition of
\mu
as an Euler characteristic,
[C([f])]=\mu([f])[k]
, so the result follows.
∎

We specialize to the following situation, which holds in our main examples.

Let
k
be a field, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a monoidal category, all of whose morphisms are monomorphisms. Its set of isomorphism classes
|\mathcal{C}|
is a monoid, suppose that we have an isomorphism
s:(|\mathcal{C}|,\oplus)\to(\mathbb{N},+)
, which we use to identify isomorphism classes of objects with natural numbers. Suppose that if there is a morphism from the
n^{\rm th}
object to the
m^{\rm th}
one, then
n\leq m
. For any
f:c\to d\in\mathcal{C}/d
define
r(f)=s(d)-s(c)
.

Definition 3.14
.

The
Hilbert series
of a pointwise finite dimensional
\mathcal{C}
module (relative to
s
) is

H_{M}(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\dim(M_{n})~t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]].

Definition 3.15
.

We define the
Whitney polynomial
of
\mathcal{C}/d
to be

W_{\mathcal{C}/d}(t):=\sum_{p\in|\mathcal{C}/d|}\mu(p)~t^{r(p)}\in\mathbb{Z}[t].

In the cases
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{FI},{\mathbf{VI}}_{q},
and
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
, this definition of the Whitney polynomial agrees with the previous one. The following statement shows that when
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
is exact the Hilbert series of
M
is rational.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
M
be a
\mathcal{C}
module, and suppose that
\mathrm{B}_{d_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{r}}(M)_{c}
is exact for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
with
s(c)\gg 0
. Then
H_{M}(t)
is rational, with denominator
\prod_{i=1}^{r}W_{\mathcal{C}/d_{i}}(t)
.

Proof.

We consider the special case
r=1
, and put
d:=d_{1}
. We write
h_{1}(t)\approx h_{2}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]]
if their difference is a polynomial. Then we have that by hypothesis

\displaystyle 0\approx\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi(\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)_{n})t^{n+s(d)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{[f:c\to d]\in|\mathcal{C}/d|}\mu(f)\chi(M_{n+s(c)})~t^{n+s(d)}

\displaystyle=~\sum_{[f:c\to d]\in|\mathcal{C}/d|}~\sum_{m\geq s(c)}\chi(M_{m})\mu(f)t^{m+r(f)}\approx H_{M}(t)W_{\mathcal{C}/d}(t),

where the first equality follows from Proposition
3.13
. (Here
\chi(\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)_{n})
denotes
\chi(\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)_{c})
for some
c\in\mathcal{C}
with
s(c)=n
). Therefore
H_{M}(t)W_{\mathcal{C}/d}(t)
is a polynomial, and so
H_{M}(t)
is rational with denominator
W_{\mathcal{C}/d}(t)
. The general case is similar, iterating applications of Proposition
3.13
.
∎

3.5.
Composites and products

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a small monoidal category and every morphism is a monomorphism. Given
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\in\mathcal{C}
we may iterate
\mathrm{B}_{d_{i}}
to obtain a complex

\mathrm{B}_{d_{1}}\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{2}}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{r}}(M)

for every
M\in\Mod\mathcal{C}
. When the over categories of
\mathcal{C}
are equivalent to upper CM posets, we may do the same with
\mathrm{K}_{d_{i}}
. Alternately, we may construct this complex in one step, by using the functor

\oplus:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}/d_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{C}/d_{r}\to\mathcal{C}

to restrict a
\mathcal{C}
module to an object of
\Mod(\mathcal{C}/d_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{C}/d_{r})^{\mathcal{C}}
and applying the construction
\mathrm{B}_{(\mathcal{C}/d_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}/d_{r})}
. The complex we obtain is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}_{d_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{r}}(M)
.

Similarly, we note the following elementary proposition.

Proposition 3.17
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C},\oplus
is a symmetric monoidal small category, and every morphism is a monomorphism. Then there are natural isomorphisms
\mathrm{B}_{d}(\Sigma^{c}M)\cong\Sigma^{c}\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
and
\mathrm{B}_{d_{1}}\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{2}}(M)\cong\mathrm{B}_{d_{2}}\circ\mathrm{B}_{d_{1}}(M)
for every
c,d,d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
.

3.6.
Categorifications for
\mathbf{FI}
and
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}

Let
k
be a commutative ring. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category such that every morphism is a monomorphism, and every endomorphism is an isomorphism. Because every endomorphism is an isomorphism, the isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{C}
inherit a natural partial order defined by
[c]\leq[d]
if there is a map from
c
to
d
. We write
\mathbf{P}(c)
for the projective
\mathcal{C}
module
d\mapsto k\mathcal{C}(c,d)
. Then we have the following general result.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
c\in\mathcal{C}
. Suppose that
\Res^{\oplus}\mathbf{P}(c)
splits as a sum of
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
modules of the form
\mathbf{P}(c_{1})\otimes_{k}\mathbf{P}(c_{2})
where
c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
and
[c_{i}]\leq[c]
. Then
\mathrm{B}_{d}(\mathbf{P}(c))
is exact for all
d\in\mathcal{C}
such that
[d]>[c]
.

Proof.

Fix
c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. To show that
\mathrm{B}_{d}(\mathbf{P}(c))_{c^{\prime}}
is exact, we consider the representation
\mathcal{C}/d\to\Mod k

(b\to d)\mapsto\mathbf{P}(c)_{c^{\prime}\oplus b},

from which
\mathrm{B}_{d}(\mathbf{P}(c))_{c^{\prime}}
is constructed by applying
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}
. By our hypothesis, this representation splits as a sum of representations of the form
(\mathbf{P}(c_{1})_{c^{\prime}})\otimes_{k}\mathbf{P}(c_{2})|_{\mathcal{C}/d}
for some choice of
c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
with
[c_{i}]\leq[c]<[d]
. Since
(\mathbf{P}(c_{1})_{c^{\prime}})
is a free
k
module and
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}(-)
preserves sums and tensor products with
k
-modules, it suffices to show that the homology of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}(\mathbf{P}(c_{2})|_{\mathcal{C}/d})
vanishes for all
c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
with
[c_{2}]<[d]
.
Further, note that

\mathbf{P}(c_{2})|_{\mathcal{C}/d}=\bigoplus_{f:c_{2}\to d}k(\mathcal{C}/d)(f,-).

Under the equivalence between
\mathcal{C}/d
modules and
|\mathcal{C}/d|
modules,
k(\mathcal{C}/d)(f,-)
corresponds to the
|\mathcal{C}/d|
module associated to the principal ideal
\{x\in|\mathcal{C}/d|,~x\geq[f]\}
. Since
[c_{2}]<[d]
,
f
is never an isomorphism, and so
[f]<\hat{1}_{|\mathcal{C}/d|}
. So by Proposition
2.27
, it follows that
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}/d}(k\mathcal{C}/d(f,-))
is exact. Thus
\mathrm{B}_{d}(\mathrm{P}(c))
is exact.
∎

Theorem
3.18
is formal, and can be extended to a general setting where we no longer assume every morphism of
\mathcal{C}
is a monomorphism, as discussed in §
4
. Using it, we may prove our categorification theorems for
\mathbf{FI}
and
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
modules.

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
k
be a commutative noetherian ring and let
q
be a prime power that is invertible in
k
. Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
module over
k
. Then there exists
D\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
such for all
d\geq D
and
W\in{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
with
\dim W\geq n
the homology of

(\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(M))_{W}

vanishes.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.13
we may prove the statement for
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(M)
. Since the direct sum functor is symmetric monoidal, by Proposition
3.17
have that
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(\Sigma^{V}M)\cong\Sigma^{V}\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(M)
. Thus to prove the statement for
M
it suffices to prove that
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(\Sigma^{V}M)
is exact for
\dim V\gg 0
and
d\gg 0
.
Theorem
1.2
of Nagpal
[
14
]
states that if
q
is invertible in
k
there is
V\in{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
such that
\Sigma^{V}M
admits a finite filtration whose associated graded modules are induced. The spectral sequence for this filtration reduces us to the case where
M
is an induced module. Recall that
M
is
induced
if there is a sequence of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
representations,
(W_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
W_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
such that

M\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}\otimes_{\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}).

We have that

\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(M)\cong\bigoplus_{n}W_{n}\otimes_{\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})).

For every
V\in{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
the
k[\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
module
\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})_{V}
is flat, because
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts freely on the set of linear injections
{\mathbf{VI}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},V)
. Thus
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}))
is a complex of flat
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
modules, and so it suffices to prove that the homology of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}(\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}))
vanishes for
d>n
.

To complete the proof, we verify the hypothesis of Theorem
3.18
for
({\mathbf{VI}}_{q},\oplus)
. Indeed we have that for
U,V_{1},V_{2}\in{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}

{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}(U,V_{1}\oplus V_{2})=\bigsqcup_{A_{1},A_{2}\subseteq U,~A_{1}\cap A_{2}=0}{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}(U/A_{1},V_{1})\times{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}(U/A_{2},V_{2}).

Thus

\Res^{\oplus}(\mathbf{P}(U))\cong\bigoplus_{A_{1},A_{2}\subseteq U,A_{1}\cap A_{2}=0}\mathbf{P}(U/A_{1})\otimes\mathbf{P}(U/A_{2}),

and
U/A_{i}\leq U
in the order on the set of objects of
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
.
∎

The analog for
\mathbf{FI}
modules has an identical proof, using the identity

\mathbf{FI}(x,a\sqcup b)=\bigsqcup_{x=r\sqcup s}\mathbf{FI}(r,a)\times\mathbf{FI}(s,b),

to apply Theorem
3.18
and Nagpal’s shift theorem for
\mathbf{FI}
modules
[
13
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
k
be a commutative noetherian ring. Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
module over
K
. Then there exist
D\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
such that for every
d\geq D
the complex
(\mathrm{K}_{[d]}(M))_{x}
is exact for all
x\in\mathbf{FI}
of size
\geq n
.

Remark 3.21
.

Theorem
3.19
is a categorification of the result, due to Nagpal, that if
M
is a finitely generated
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
module in non-describing characteristic then
h_{M}(t)
is rational with denominator
\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(1-q^{d}t)
for some
d>0
. However, when
k
has the same characteristic as
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this is false. For instance, consider the case

. Then if
M
is the
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
module defined by the identity functor
V\mapsto V
, we have

h_{M}(t)=\sum_{i}it^{i}=\frac{t}{(1-t)^{2}}.

Therefore Theorem
3.19
cannot extend to representations of
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
in equal characteristic. However, in analogy with Theorem
1.2
, we make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.22
.

Let
k
be an arbitrary field. Let
M
be a
{\mathbf{VI}}_{q}
module which is a subquotient of a module generated in degree
d
. Then there exists an
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that the homology of
(\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}})^{\circ r}(M)

4.
Extension to EI categories

In this section, we explain how the complexes
\mathrm{B}_{d}
can be interpreted using
\mathcal{C}
module homology. This allows us to define the complexes
\mathrm{B}_{d}
more generally when
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal EI category. The material in this section is not used elsewhere in the paper, and it is also more technical. We include it since it may be of interest to some readers, but others may prefer to skip it.

4.1.
Homology of EI categories

First we recall the definition of the homology of EI categories, paralleling the discussion for
\mathbf{FI}
module homology in
[
4
, §5]
.

Definition 4.1
.

An
EI category
is a category in which every endomorphism is an isomorphism. If
\mathcal{D}
is an EI category, then the set of isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{D}
carries a natural partial order: define
[d_{1}]\leq[d_{2}]
if there is a morphism
d_{1}\to d_{2}
in
\mathcal{D}
.

For the remainder of this section,
\mathcal{D}
denotes a small EI category.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\Mod k
be a
\mathcal{D}
module. Define

H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(M)_{d}:=\frac{M_{d}}{\{M_{f}(m)~|~f:c\to d,~[c]<[d],~m\in M_{c}\}},

the left derived functors of
H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
are denoted by
H_{i}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
and called the
\mathcal{D}
module homology in degree
d
.

The functor
H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
is naturally isomorphic to
S(d)\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}-
for the following
\mathcal{D}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}
. We let
S(d):\mathcal{D}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}\to\Mod k
be the
\mathcal{D}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
representation

c\mapsto\frac{k\mathcal{D}(c,d)}{k\{f~|~f:c\to d,~[d]<[c]\}}.

Concretely it takes
c
to
k\mathcal{D}(c,d)
if
c
is isomorphic to
d
, and zero otherwise.

For any category
\mathcal{C}
, using
H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}:\Mod\mathcal{D}\to\Mod k
we may construct a functor
\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}

\Mod(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D})=\Mod(\mathcal{D})^{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow^{H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)}(\Mod k)^{\mathcal{C}}=\Mod{\mathcal{C}}.

The following proposition interprets
\mathrm{B}_{d}
in terms of
\mathcal{D}
module homology.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a monoidal EI category, and
d\in D
. If
\mathcal{D}/d
is equivalent to a poset, then
M\mapsto\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
is a model for the derived functor

(\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}))\circ\Res^{\oplus}:\mathbf{D}^{-}(\Mod\mathcal{D})\to\mathbf{D}^{-}(\Mod\mathcal{D}).

Concretely if
\mathrm{C}_{d}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)
is a functorial complex computing
\mathcal{D}
module homology in degree
d
, coming from a projective
\mathcal{D}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
resolution of
S(d)
, then there is a quasi-isomorphism

(\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{C}_{d}^{\mathcal{D}}(-))\circ\Res^{\oplus}(M)\simeq\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)

for any
\mathcal{D}
module
M
.

Remark 4.5
.

The reason we must derive the product functor
\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
in the statement of Proposition
4.4
instead of using the functor

\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{L}H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}:\mathbf{D}(\Mod\mathcal{D})^{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathbf{D}(\Mod(k))^{\mathcal{D}},

is that for ordinary (unenhanced) derived categories there is no equivalence
\mathbf{D}(\Mod\mathcal{D})\simeq\mathbf{D}(\Mod(k))^{\mathcal{D}}
.
However, if
N
is a
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
module such that the
\mathcal{D}
module
N_{(c,-)}
is
\mathrm{L}H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
acyclic for all
c\in\mathcal{D}
then
N
is
\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d})
acyclic. So we may compute
\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d})
exactly how we would compute
\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{L}H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
.

We also note that from a more sophisticated point of view, Proposition
4.4
follows immediately by interpreting
\mathcal{D}
module homology as the homology of

\cone(\left(\mathrm{L}j_{!}j^{*}M)_{d}\to M_{d}\right),

where
j:\mathcal{D}_{<d}\to\mathcal{D}
is the inclusion of the subcategory spanned by objects
<d
and
j^{*},j_{!}
are the restriction and left Kan extension functors. Then
(\mathrm{L}j_{!}j^{*}M)_{d}
can be computed as a homotopy colimit over
\mathcal{D}_{<d}/d
, which is modelled by a bar construction.
∎

Before proving Proposition
4.4
we introduce some definitions.

4.2.
General Bar complexes

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a small category containing a terminal object, and such that there are no morphisms in
\mathcal{J}
from a terminal object to a non-terminal object.

Definition 4.6
.

Define
S(\hat{1}_{\mathcal{J}})
to be the
\mathcal{J}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module,

S(\hat{1})_{x}=\begin{cases}k&\text{if }x\text{ is terminal }\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Where for
f\in\mathcal{J}
,
S(\hat{1})_{f}
is
\mathrm{id}_{k}
if
f
is a morphism between two terminal objects, and the
0
map otherwise.

Write
\mathrm{O}\mathrm{b}(\mathcal{J})
for the subcategory of
\mathcal{J}
consisting of the set of objects of
\mathcal{J}
and identity maps. Note
\mathrm{O}\mathrm{b}(\mathcal{J})=\mathrm{O}\mathrm{b}(\mathcal{J}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}})
.
Via the adjunction

(\Mod k)^{\mathrm{O}\mathrm{b}(\mathcal{J})}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\halign{\hbox to\dimexpr\@tempdima+1em{#}\cr 27.01393pt{\rightarrowfill\cr\kern 2.15277pt\cr 27.01393pt{\leftarrowfill\cr}}}\limits^{\!\rm Free}_{\!\Res}}(\Mod k)^{\mathcal{J}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}},}}

we may resolve
S(\hat{1}_{\mathcal{J}})
using the associated monad
\bot={\rm Free}\circ{\rm Res}
as in
[
30
, §8.6]
. We obtain a simplicial abelian
\mathcal{J}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module
F
whose
r
simplices are

x\in\mathcal{J}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mapsto k\{x\to x_{r}\to x_{r-1}\to\dots\to^{f_{1}}x_{0}~|~x_{0}\text{ is terminal}\}.

Let
\mathaccent 869{F}
be the normalized chain complex associated to
F
. Then
\mathaccent 869{F}\to S(\hat{1})
is a projective resolution, and so the derived functor of
S(\hat{1}_{\mathcal{J}})\otimes_{\mathcal{J}}-
is modelled by
\mathaccent 869{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{J}}-
. For any
\mathcal{J}
module
M
, we have that
\mathaccent 869{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}M
is a chain complex, in degree
r
it is

(\mathaccent 869{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}M)_{r}=\bigoplus_{x_{r}\to^{f_{r}}x_{r-1}\to\dots\to x_{0}~|~x_{0}\text{ terminal, }f_{i}\neq\mathrm{id}_{x_{i}}}M_{x_{r}}

Definition 4.7
.

We define
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}}(M):=(\mathaccent 869{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}M)
.

It is clear that when
\mathcal{J}
is a poset with top element
\hat{1}
, we have
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)\cong\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)
. As a warning, note that if there is an equivalence of categories
f:\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}}\mathcal{J}
, we do not have
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)\cong\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}(f^{*}M)
. However, because there is an isomorphism of
(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}){{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules
f^{*}S(\hat{1}_{\mathcal{J}})\cong S(\hat{1}_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}})
, both complexes compute the same derived functor, hence there is a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}}(M)\simeq\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}(f^{*}M).

Definition 4.8
.

Using
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{J}}
we may extend our construction of chain complexes to any small monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the retraction-isomorphism property:

(\bullet)

if
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
c\to^{f}c^{\prime}\to^{g}c
is a retraction (i.e.
g\circ f=\mathrm{id}
), then
f
is an isomorphism,

by defining

\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{d}(N):=(\mathrm{id}\times\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{C}/d}(-))\circ\Res^{\oplus}(N)

for any
C
module
N
. Indeed, the over-category
\mathcal{C}/c
always contains
\mathrm{id}_{c}:c\to c
as a terminal object, and the condition
(\bullet)
corresponds to the statement that any morphism from
\mathrm{id}_{c}
to
c^{\prime}\to^{g}c
in
\mathcal{C}/c
is an isomorphism.

When the over-categories of
\mathcal{C}
are posetal, the discussion above implies that there is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes of
\mathcal{C}
modules
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}_{d}(N)\simeq\mathrm{B}_{d}(N)
for every

.

4.3.
Proof of Proposition
4.4

With the general definitions of Bar complexes in hand, we may prove Proposition
4.4
. In fact we we may weaken our assumption that the overcategories of
\mathcal{D}
are posets, to the assumption that
\mathcal{D}
satsifies property

.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
F:\Ch^{-}(\Mod(\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}))\to\Ch^{-}(\Mod\mathcal{D})
be an exact sum-preserving functor from bounded below chain complexes
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
modules to bounded below chain complexes of
\mathcal{D}
modules. To show that

\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d})
it suffices to prove that there is a natural isomorphism
H_{0}(F(N))\cong\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d}
for all
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}

, and that for every
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
we have that
H_{i}(F(k(\mathcal{D}(c_{1},-)\times\mathcal{D}(c_{2},-)))=0
for

. This follows from the fact that
\{k(\mathcal{D}(c_{1},-)\times\mathcal{D}(c_{2},-))\}_{c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{D}}
form a collection of projective generators of
\Mod(\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D})
, by the Yoneda lemma.

The equivalence between the abstract statement of the proposition and the concrete one follows from the fact that if
P_{\bullet}\to S(d)
is a projective resolution of
S(d)
and we define
C(-):=P_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}-
, then

satisfies the above criteria.

Let
r:\mathcal{D}/d\to\mathcal{D}
be the functor that forgets the morphism to

\mathcal{D}

M
there is a natural isomophism

S(\hat{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{D}/d}r^{*}M\cong\frac{M_{d}}{\{M_{g}(m)~|~g:c\to d,m\in M_{c}\text{ $g$ not an isomorphism}\}}\cong H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(M)_{d}.

Further we have that

r^{*}(k\mathcal{D}(c,-))=\bigoplus_{f:c\to d}k(\mathcal{D}/d)(f,-).

Because
{\rm Bar}_{\mathcal{D}/d}(-)
models
S(\hat{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{L}}-
and

is a projective

H_{i}({\rm Bar}_{\mathcal{D}/d}(r^{*}(k\mathcal{D}(c,-))=0
for all

.
Therefore
\mathrm{id}\times{\rm Bar}_{\mathcal{D}/d}(-)
satisfies the criteria to model
\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{id}\times H_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)_{d})
. When
\mathcal{D}/d
is equivalent to a poset

, the quasi-isomorphism between
{\rm Bar}_{\mathcal{D}/d}
and

finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Using the material in this section, we may extend the definition of the complexes
\mathrm{B}_{d}(-)
to an arbitrary small monoidal EI–category
\mathcal{D}
, by taking Proposition
4.4
as a definition. It is natural to ask: which results from §
3
continue to hold in this level of generality?

The answer is that Theorem
3.18
holds for formal reasons. However, at this level of generality, exactness of complexes does not imply rationality of Hilbert series. Of course, exactness always encodes
some
relation between the virtual
\Aut(c)
representations
[M_{c}]
. But this relation only implies a relation between the dimensions
\dim M_{c}
if for every
d\in\mathcal{D}
the over-category
\mathcal{D}/d
is
directed
: every endomorphism of an object in
\mathcal{D}/d
is the identity. (Directedness of
\mathcal{D}/d
is equivalent to
\Aut(c)
acting freely on
\mathcal{D}(c,d)
for all
c
). In fact, all of §
2
and §
3
generalize easily if we replace “posetal” by “directed” and “poset” by “skeletal directed category.” We chose not to work at that level of generality because posets are more familiar, and all of the examples we consider have posetal over-categories. ∎

5.
Gröbner theory

In this section, we use Sam–Snowden’s Gröbner theory of categories to give a criterion for chain complexes such as
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
and
\mathrm{B}_{d}(M)
to be exact. For background see
[
23
, §4]
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\sqcup)
be a small monoidal category. In this paper, we will only consider the case
\mathcal{C}={\mathbf{OS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
(see Definition
7.1
), but in the interest of future applications work in a more general setting. Fix
d\in\mathcal{C}
, and let
\mathbf{S}(d):\mathcal{C}\to\Set
be the functor
c\mapsto\mathcal{C}(d,c)
. We assume that
\mathcal{C}(d,c)
is finite.

Definition 5.1
.

An
ordering
on
\mathbf{S}(d)
consists of a total ordering
\leq

\mathcal{C}(d,c)

Definition 8.1
.

We write
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}
to denote the groupoid
\sqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{S}_{n}
. Any
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}

The category

\oast:\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}}\times\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}}\to\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}},

Concretely, if

and

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}

Finally there is a composition product

\odot:\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}}\times\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}}\to\Ch(\Mod R)^{\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}}.

defined by

M\odot N:=\bigoplus_{r}M_{r}\otimes_{\mathrm{S}_{r}}N^{\oast r}

where
\mathrm{S}_{r}
acts on

in the way induced by the Koszul sign rule.
\hfill\mathchar 1027\relax

Our first step will be to describe the underlying representation of
\mathrm{K}_{d}(M)
in terms of these operations and the top Whitney homology of the partition lattice.

Definition 8.2
.

For

Definition 8.5

Let be

We use standard notation for symmetric functions. Let

We set

\mathrm{S}_{|\lambda|}

The Hall inner product on

\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},p_{\nu}\rangle=\delta(\lambda,\nu)=\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\nu}\rangle,

\langle-,-\rangle:\Lambda\times\hat{\Lambda}\to\mathbb{Q}

\hat{\Lambda}\to\Lambda^{*}

Theorem 8.9

In terms of symmetric functions Proposition

The brackets

To calculate this expression, note that since

\partial_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i}\partial_{i}^{m_{i}}
.
Therefore we have

\Delta(\ch(M))=\sum_{\lambda\text{ integer partition }}\frac{\partial_{\lambda}(\ch(M))}{\lambda!}\otimes p_{\lambda}.

Since
p_{\lambda}[w]=\prod_{i}(w[p_{i}])^{m_{i}}
and
w[p_{i}]=\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log(1+p_{id})
we have

\sum_{d\geq 1}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{h}(\mathrm{K}_{d}(M))=\sum_{\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots}~\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{\partial_{i}^{m_{i}}(\ch(M))}{m_{i}!}\otimes\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log(1+p_{id})\right)^{m_{i}}.

To rearrange this sum, we introduce formal variables
t_{i}
which commute with
p_{i}
and consider the formal expression

\sum_{\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots}~\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{t_{i}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}!}\left(\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log(1+p_{id})\right)^{m_{i}}=\exp\left(\sum_{i}t_{i}\left(\sum_{d}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log(1+p_{id})\right)\right)

=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\log(1+p_{n})\sum_{d|n}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}t_{n/d}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1+p_{n})^{T_{n}}

where
T_{n}:=\sum_{d|n}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}t_{n/d}
and the final expression is interpreted as a power series via the binomial theorem
(1+p_{n})^{T_{n}}=\sum_{k}{T_{n}\choose k}p_{n}^{k}
. Expanding the product, we see that this expression equals

\sum_{\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots}p_{\lambda}\prod_{i}{T_{i}\choose m_{i}}.

Now, we may interpret the formal variables
t_{i}
(resp.
p_{i}
) as the operators
\mathaccent 1371{\Lambda\otimes\Lambda}\to\mathaccent 1371{\Lambda\otimes\Lambda}
defined by
a\otimes b\mapsto\partial_{i}(a)\otimes b
(resp.
a\otimes b\mapsto a\otimes p_{i}b
), because these operators commute with each other. Applying the operator defined by our formal expression in
p_{i},t_{i}
to
\ch(M)\otimes 1,
we obtain an equality

\sum_{d}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{h}(\mathrm{K}_{d}(M))=\sum_{\lambda}{D\choose\lambda}\ch(M)\otimes p_{\lambda}.

Equating degree
d
pieces in the second factor, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Differential equations and characters of bounded type

In this section, motivated by Theorem

of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}

9.1.
Solution space to a system differential equations

It is convenient to introduce generators of
\hat{\Lambda}
adapted to
D_{i}
.

Definition 9.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

y_{n}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{p_{nk}}{k}=\sum_{i}-\log(1-x_{i}^{n}).

The elements

satisfy several elementary identities: for example
y_{1}=\log(\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n})
and
y_{n}=y_{1}[p_{n}]
. The most important one follows from Möbius inversion:

Proposition 9.2
.

For all
n,m\geq 1
we have
D_{n}(y_{m})=\delta(n,m),
where
\delta(n,m)
is the Kronecker delta.

Proof.

We have

y_{m}=\sum_{e,~m|e}(m/e)p_{e}.

So

D_{n}(y_{m})=\sum_{d|n}\frac{\mu(n/d)}{n/d}\partial_{d}\sum_{e,~m|e}(m/e)p_{e}=\sum_{e,~m|e|n}\frac{\mu(n/e)}{(n/e)}(m/e).

Factoring out
m/n
and applying Möbius inversion for the poset
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1},|)
, we see that this sum is
1
if
n=m
and zero otherwise.
∎

A similar Möbius inversion to Proposition
9.2
yields

p_{n}=\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}y_{nd}.

Definition 9.3
.

Let
(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm pow}_{i}
denote the closure of the span of the degree

monomials in the power sum symmetric functions. Thus an arbitrary element of
(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm pow}_{i}
takes the form

\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},\dots),\sum m_{j}=i}a_{(m_{1},\dots)}\prod_{j}p_{j}^{m_{j}},

for some choice of
a_{(m_{1},\dots)}\in\mathbb{Q}
. We also write
(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm pow}_{\leq i}:=\bigoplus_{j\leq i}(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm pow}_{j}
.

Our main theorem of this section describes the space of solutions to a system of differential equations.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
j_{1}\geq j_{2}\geq\dots\geq j_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the space of solutions in
\hat{\Lambda}
to the system of linear differential equations

(9.5)

{D\choose\lambda_{1}}\dots{D\choose\lambda_{r}}s=0,\text{ for all }(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\text{ integer partitions such that }|\lambda_{t}|\geq j_{t},

is the subspace

\sum_{t=1}^{r}(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm power}_{t-1}\left\{\exp(\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}y_{i})~\bigg|~A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots,~|A|<j_{t}\right\}.

Example 9.6

Let

,
j_{1}=3
and
j_{2}=2
. Then Theorem
9.4
states that the space of solutions to (

\{1,\exp(y_{1}),\exp(y_{2}),\exp(2y_{1})\}\cup\{p_{k},p_{k}\exp(y_{1})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

The proof of Theorem

is straightforward, but occupies some space. We defer the the proof until the end of this section, and first explain the consequences of Theorem

9.2.

9.3.
From symmetric functions to class functions

We show how to recover the results of the introduction, stated in terms of class functions, from the results of the body of the paper, stated in terms of symmetric functions.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between elements of
\hat{\Lambda}
and class functions on
\sqcup_{n}\mathrm{S}_{n}
, under which
\ch(M)
corresponds to the character of
M
,
\sigma\mapsto\Tr(\sigma,M)
. If
s\in\hat{\Lambda}
, then
s
corresponds to the class function
\sigma_{\lambda}\mapsto\langle p_{\lambda},s\rangle
, where
\sigma_{|\lambda|}\in\mathrm{S}_{|\lambda|}
is an element in the conjugacy class of the integer partition
\lambda.

The following fact allows us to translate into the language of the introduction.

Proposition 9.12
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
and
\nu=1^{\ell_{1}}2^{\ell_{2}}\dots
integer partitions. Then symmetric function
\frac{p_{\nu}}{z_{\nu}}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
corresponds to the class function

{\mathbb{X}\choose\nu}A^{\mathbb{X}-\nu}=\prod_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{X}_{n}\choose\ell_{n}}\left(\sum_{d|n}da_{d}\right)^{\mathbb{X}_{n}-\ell_{n}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be an integer partition. We wish to determine
\langle p_{\lambda},\frac{p_{\nu}}{z_{\nu}}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})\rangle,
in other words the coefficient of
p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda}
in the power series expansion of
\frac{p_{\nu}}{z_{\nu}}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}).
To do this, it is convenient to introduce the variables
t_{n}:=p_{n}/n
, so that if
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
we have
\frac{p_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}=\prod_{i}\frac{t_{i}^{m_{i}}}{m_{i}!},
and

\exp(\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}y_{i})=\prod_{n\geq 1}\exp(t_{n}\sum_{d|n}da_{d})=\left(\prod_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t_{n})^{m}}{m!}(\sum_{d|n}da_{d})^{m}\right).

Now, if
\nu=1^{\ell_{1}}2^{\ell_{2}}\dots
we have

\frac{p_{\nu}}{z_{\nu}}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{\ell_{n}}}{\ell_{n}!}\frac{(t_{n})^{m}}{m!}(\sum_{d|n}da_{d})^{m}\right).

So the coefficient of
\prod_{n}\frac{t_{n}^{m_{n}}}{m_{n}!}
is

\prod_{n}{m_{n}\choose\ell_{n}}\left(\sum_{d|n}da_{d}\right)^{m_{n}-\ell_{n}},

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Let

be an

(D-b_{k})\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})=(b_{k}-a_{k})\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
we see that
\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
is an eigenvector of
D^{\prime}
with nonzero eigenvalue, establishing the first claim.

To see the second claim, notice that
{D\choose\nu}
is a nonzero scalar times a product of operators of the form
(D_{i}-b_{i})
for
b_{i}<a_{i}
.

For the converse, let
\prod_{i}y_{i}^{d_{i}}
be a monomial of degree
d
and suppose that
\sum_{i}ia_{i}<j_{d+1}
. (Note that this implies
d<r
). Let
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d+1})
be a sequence of integer partitions with
|\lambda_{t}|\geq j_{t}
. We show that

(9.17)

\left(\prod_{t=1}^{d+1}{D\choose\lambda_{t}}\right)\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})(\prod_{i}y_{i}^{d_{i}})=0.

The converse follows from this statement via linearity and the fact that if the product of the first
d
operators annihilates
s
then so does the full product.

To see that equation (
9.17
) holds, note that for each
\lambda_{t}=1^{m_{1,t}}2^{m_{2,t}}\dots
there must exist an
i
such that
m_{i,t}>a_{i}
, since otherwise we would have

|\lambda_{t}|=\sum_{i}im_{i,t}\leq\sum_{i}ia_{i}<j_{d+1}\leq j_{t},

contradicting the hypothesis on
\lambda_{t}
. For each
t
choose such an
i
, and denote it by
i_{t}
. Then
\prod_{t=1}^{d+1}(D_{i_{t}}-a_{t})
is a factor of
\left(\prod_{t=1}^{d+1}{D\choose\lambda_{t}}\right)
. Because
(D_{k}-a_{k})(m\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})=\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})D_{k}(m)
, we see that each factor in the product
\prod_{t=1}^{d+1}(D_{i_{t}}-a_{t})
reduces the degree of the coefficent of
\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
by one. Since there are
d+1
factors and
\prod_{i}y_{i}^{d_{i}}
has degree
d
we have that

\left(\prod_{t=1}^{d+1}(D_{i_{t}}-a_{t})\right)\exp(\sum_{a_{i}}y_{i})(\prod_{i}y_{i}^{d_{i}})=0,

finishing the proof of the converse.
∎

10.
Characters of bounded rank

In §
8
and §
9
we examined the constraints on the characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules of type
<J
for
J
an integer partition. The purpose of this section is to study an additional constraint on the characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
M
be an
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}
module over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
\rank(M)\leq k
if, in the decomposition of
M_{n}
into irreducibles, every irreducible representation appearing corresponds to a Young diagram with
\leq k
rows. We say that an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module has rank
\leq k
when its underlying
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}
representation does.

Definition 10.2
.

We write
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\subseteq\hat{\Lambda}
for the closure of the span of Schur functions
s_{\lambda}
such that the Young diagram of
\lambda
has
\leq k
rows.

Notice that an
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}
representation
M
has rank
\leq k
if and only if
\ch(M)\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
. The reason that the rank is useful is the following folklore proposition, which appears in
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
M
be an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
M
is a subquotient of a module generated in degree
\leq d
, then
\rank(M)\leq d
.

Therefore, by Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
9.10
, if
M
is a subquotient of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module generated in degree
d
there exists an
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ch(M)\in\hat{\Lambda}
lies in the subspace

\mathcal{F}_{\leq d}\cap\left(\bigoplus_{A\vdash d}\mathcal{V}_{A,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{A\text{ integer partition, }|A|<d}\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\right).

In this section, we prove that this intersection is finite dimensional, and characterize it using the Hall inner product.

Definition 10.4
.

Write
\epsilon_{k}:\hat{\Lambda}\to\mathaccent 1371{\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},\dots,p_{k}]}
for the specialization homomorphism
p_{i}\mapsto p_{i}
for
i\leq k
and
p_{i}\mapsto 0
for
i>k
.

We prove the following theorem over the course of this section.

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
A_{i},i\in I
be a finite collection of distinct integer partitions, and
r_{i}\in\mathbb{N},i\in I
be a finite collection of natural numbers (not necessarily distinct). Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\geq\max_{i}(|A_{i}|)
. Then we have

\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}.

Further, given any element
s\in\hat{\Lambda}
, there is a unique element
\pi_{k}(s)\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
such that

\epsilon_{k}(\pi_{k}(s))=\epsilon_{k}(s).

And if
s\in\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
, then
\pi_{k}(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
.

Because the space
\epsilon_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{A,r})
is finite dimensional, Theorem
10.5
implies that
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
is finite dimensional, since the elements of this intersection are uniquely determined by their image in
\epsilon_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{A,r})
. In particular the elements
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))
where
p_{\nu}
ranges over degree
<r
monomials in
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
form a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
, because
\epsilon_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))
is a basis for
\epsilon_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{A,r})
.

Under the correspondence between class functions and symmetric functions, the specialization
\epsilon_{k}
corresponds to restricting the class function to elements
\sigma\in\mathrm{S}_{n}
whose cycles all have length
\leq k
. So Theorem
10.5
provides unique lift from restricted class functions to class functions.

Assuming Theorem
10.5
we can prove Theorem
1.13
.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

Let
(d,s)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. If
M
has type
(d,s)
then by Proposition
10.3
we have that the rank of
M
is
\leq d
and
M
has type
<J
for
J=d^{s-1}(d+1)
. Define

\mathcal{U}_{d,s}:=\bigoplus_{A\vdash d}\mathcal{F}_{\leq d}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{A\text{ integer partition, }|A|<d}\mathcal{F}_{\leq d}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,s}.

By Theorem
10.5
and Theorem
9.10
we have that
\ch(M)\in\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
. Since
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))
where
p_{\nu}
ranges over degree
<r
monomials in
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
form a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
, we see that
\mathcal{U}_{d,s}
has dimension
p(d)+{d+s-1\choose s-1}\sum_{i<d}p(i)
where
p(i)
is the number of integer partitions of
i
.
∎

To prove Theorem
10.5
and describe the projection operator
\pi_{k}:\hat{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, we characterize the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
and
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
using the Hall inner product.

Definition 10.6
.

If
W
is a subspace of
\Lambda
, we write
W^{\perp}\subseteq\hat{\Lambda}
for the
perpendicular space
with respect to the Hall inner product, characterized by the property
t\in W^{\perp}
if and only if
\langle w,t\rangle=0
for all
w\in W
.

We record the following general fact from linear algebra.

Proposition 10.7
.

Let
W\subseteq\Lambda
. The Hall inner product induces an isomorphism
W^{\perp}\to(\Lambda/W)^{*}
, by
t\mapsto\langle-,t\rangle
. In particular, there is a unique element of
W^{\perp}
corresponding to every linear function
L:\Lambda/W\to\mathbb{Q}
namely

\sum_{\lambda\text{ integer partition}}L([p_{\lambda}])~p_{\lambda}.

where
[p_{\lambda}]
denotes the equivalence class of
p_{\lambda}
mod
W
.

The next two propositions characterize
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
and
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
as the perpendicular spaces to two different ideals in
\Lambda
. The first is certainly known, but we do not have a reference so we include the proof.

Proposition 10.8
.

Let
(e_{k+1},e_{k+2}\dots)\Lambda
be the ideal of
\Lambda
generated by
e_{i}
for
i>k
. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}=((e_{k+1},e_{k+2}\dots)\Lambda)^{\perp},

with respect to the Hall inner product.

Proof.

For
\lambda
an integer partition, we write
\rank(\lambda)
for the number of rows in the Young diagram of
\lambda
.
Write
t=\sum_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}
for
b_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
w\in\Lambda
and
i>k
. By
[
26
, 7.15.4]

\langle we_{i},t\rangle=\sum_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}\langle we_{i},s_{\lambda}\rangle=\sum_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}\langle w,s_{\lambda/1^{i}}\rangle.

If
t\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
then
b_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda
of rank
>k
. By the (transpose of) the Pieri rule
[
26
, 7.15.9]
we have that
s_{\lambda/1^{i}}
is zero if
\lambda
does not have rank
\geq i
. Hence
\langle we_{i},t\rangle=0
, and so
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\subseteq(e_{k+1},\dots)\Lambda^{\rm\perp}
.

For the converse note that the degree
n
component of the ideal
(e_{k+1},\dots)\Lambda
is spanned by the set of
e_{\lambda}
where
\lambda\vdash n
is an integer partition with
>k
columns in its Young diagram. Thus its perpendicular space in
\hat{\Lambda}_{n}
has the same dimension as the number of integer partitions of
n
with
\leq k
columns. Taking a transpose, we see that this is the same as the dimension of the degree
n
component of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
. Thus the containment
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\subseteq(e_{k+1},\dots)\Lambda^{\rm\perp}
is an equality.
∎

Proposition 10.9
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
r>1
. Define
u_{n}:=p_{n}-\sum_{i|n}ia_{i}
for all
n\geq 1
. Then Let
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}\Lambda
be the
r^{\rm th}
power of the ideal generated by the
u_{n}
. Then

\mathcal{V}_{A,r}=\left((u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}\Lambda\right)^{\perp},

with respect to the Hall inner product.

Proof.

Any element
t\in\hat{\Lambda}
can be expanded uniquely as

t=\sum_{\lambda\text{ integer partition}}c_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})

for
c_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}
, because the lowest degree term of
p_{\lambda}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
is
p_{\lambda}
. Then
t\in\hat{\Lambda}
if and only if
c_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda
whose Young diagrams have
\geq r
rows.

For
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
put
u_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i}u_{i}^{m_{i}}.

Lemma 10.10
.

We have that

\left\langle\frac{u_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}},~p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}))\right\rangle=\delta(\lambda,\nu),

where
\delta(\lambda,\nu)
is the Kronecker delta.

Proof.

First notice that for any
\lambda,\nu
we have

\langle\partial_{i}(p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda}),p_{\nu}\rangle=\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},(p_{i}/i)p_{\nu}\rangle,

using the fact that
\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},p_{\nu}\rangle=\delta(\lambda,\nu)
. By linearity, it follows that the operator
\partial_{i}
and multiplication by
p_{i}/i
are adjoint. Thus for any pair of symmetric functions
f\in\Lambda,g\in\hat{\Lambda}
we have that

\langle f,g\exp(\sum_{d\geq 1}a_{d}y_{d})\rangle=\langle f,g\exp(\sum_{d\geq 1}a_{d}d\sum_{d|k}p_{k}/k)\rangle=\langle\exp(\sum_{d}a_{d}d\sum_{d|k}\partial_{k})f,g\rangle.

Taylor expanding with respect to
p_{k}
, we see that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator
\exp(\partial_{k})
acts on
\Lambda
by the ring homomorphism
p_{k}\mapsto p_{k}+1
and
p_{n}\mapsto p_{n}
for
d\neq k
. Thus we have that
\exp(\sum_{d}da_{d}\sum_{k}\partial_{k})
acts on
\Lambda
by the substitution
p_{n}\mapsto p_{n}+\sum_{d|n}da_{d}
. In particular we have that
\exp(\sum_{d}da_{d}\sum_{k}\partial_{k})u_{\lambda}=p_{\lambda}
. So

\langle u_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{d}a_{d}y_{d})\rangle=\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},p_{\nu}\rangle=\delta(\lambda,\nu),

as desired.
∎

Applying Lemma
10.10
to the expansion of
t
, we see that
t\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
if and only if
\langle u_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},t\rangle=0
for all
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
such that
\sum_{i}m_{i}\geq r
. Rescaling by
z_{\lambda}
we see that
t\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
if and only if every degree
\geq r
monomial in the
u_{i}
pairs to zero with it. Taking linear combinations of monomials finishes the proof.
∎

Since sums of subspaces correspond to intersections of perpendicular spaces, Propositions
10.8
and
10.9
imply.

Proposition 10.11
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
,
let
u_{n}=p_{n}-\sum_{i|n}ia_{i}
, and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}={((u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}+(e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\dots))}^{\perp}.

Next we need

Proposition 10.12
.

The ring homomorphism

\iota_{k}:\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},\dots,p_{k}]\to\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots),

\iota_{k}(p_{i}):=p_{i}
is an isomorphism.
In particular, the images of
\{p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda=1^{m_{1}}\dots k^{m_{k}}}
in
\frac{\Lambda}{(e_{k+1},\dots)}
form a basis. Here
\lambda
ranges over partitions whose associated Young diagrams have
\leq k
columns.

Proof.

We have that
\frac{\Lambda}{(e_{k+1},\dots)}=\mathbb{Q}[e_{1},\dots,e_{k}]\cong\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]^{\mathrm{S}_{k}}
is isomorphic to ring of symmetric functions in
k
variables. It is well known that the power sums
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
form an independent set of generators of this ring.
∎

Now Propositions
10.7
,
10.8
and
10.12
allow us to define
\pi_{k}(s)
.

Definition 10.13
.

Let
s\in\hat{\Lambda}
. We define
\pi_{k}(s)\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
to be the element corresponding (as in Proposition
10.7
) to the linear function,
\frac{\Lambda}{(e_{k+1},\dots)}\to\mathbb{Q}
defined by
p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda}\mapsto\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},s\rangle
for all integer partitions
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}\dots k^{m_{k}}
, whose Young diagrams have
\leq k
columns.

Because
\langle p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda},t\rangle
is precisely the coefficient of
p_{\lambda}
in the power-sum expansion of
t
, it is clear that
s
and
\pi_{k}(s)
have the same image under
\epsilon_{k}
. And by Propositions
10.7
,
10.8
and
10.12
,
\pi_{k}(s)
is the only element of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
with this property.

To actually compute
\pi_{k}(s)
as a sum of power functions, as in Proposition
10.7
, we need to know how to write
p_{\nu}
as a sum of monomials in
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
under the specialization
\Lambda\to\frac{\Lambda}{(e_{k+1},\dots)}\cong\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]^{\mathrm{S}_{k}}
.

Proposition 10.14
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
be an integer partition and
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
u_{n}
denote
p_{n}-\sum_{d|n}da_{d}
. Suppose that
k\geq n
. Let
\overline{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}}
be the image of
(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)^{r}
in
\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots)
. Then
\iota_{k}^{-1}\left(\overline{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}}\right)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\iota_{k}^{-1}\left(\overline{(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)}\right)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
, where
\overline{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)}
is the image of
{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)}
in
\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots)
.

Now
\iota_{k}^{-1}\left(\overline{(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)}\right)
clearly contains
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
which is a maximal ideal. So it suffices to prove that
\overline{(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)}
is a proper ideal of
\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots)
. To do this, we construct a nontrivial homomorphism
\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots)\to\mathbb{Q}
under which which
u_{i}
vanishes.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-set
S:=\bigsqcup_{d\geq 1}(\mathbb{Z}/d\times[a_{d}])
. Linearizing it we obtain an
|A|
dimensional vector space
\mathbb{Q}S
and an operator
T:\mathbb{Q}S\to\mathbb{Q}S
defined to be the action of
+1\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there is a specialization homomorphism
\kappa:\Lambda\to\mathbb{Q}
defined by

p_{n}\mapsto\Tr(T^{n},\mathbb{Q}S).

Since
\mathbb{Q}S
has dimension
|A|\leq k
we have that
\wedge^{i}\mathbb{Q}S=0
for all
i>k
. Thus
\kappa(e_{i})=\Tr(T,\wedge^{i}\mathbb{Q}S)=0
for all
i>k
. So
\kappa
factors through
\Lambda/(e_{k+1},\dots)
. Finally,
\kappa(p_{n})
is the number of fixed points of the action of
+n\in\mathbb{Z}
on
S
. If
d|n
then
+n
fixes all of the points of
\mathbb{Z}/d
, otherwise it fixes none of them. So we see that the number of fixed points of
+n
is
\sum_{d|n}da_{d}
. Therefore
\kappa(u_{n})=\kappa(p_{n}-\sum_{d|n}da_{d})=0
.

Thus
\kappa
is the desired homomorphism with kernel
(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)+(e_{k+1},\dots)
and so we are done.
∎

Remark 10.15
.

The homomorphism
\kappa
constructed in the proof of Proposition
10.14
can also be directly by
p_{n}\mapsto p_{n}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\dots,0,0,\dots)
where the
\zeta_{i}
are the roots of unity obtained by diagonalizing
T
.
∎

Proposition
10.14
has the following immediate consequence.

Proposition 10.16
.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
and let
u_{n}=p_{n}-\sum_{i|n}ia_{i}
.
There is an isomorphism

\frac{\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]}{(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}}\to\frac{\Lambda}{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}+(e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\dots)}

induced by the map
u_{i}\mapsto u_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

The morphism is surjective because its image equals the image of
\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},\dots,p_{k}]
, and it is injective by Proposition
10.14
.
∎

Proposition 10.17
.

Let
A
be an integer partition such that
|A|\leq k
. If
s\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
then
\pi_{k}(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
.

Proof.

Let
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
and let
u_{i}=p_{i}-\sum_{i}ia_{i}
. By Proposition
10.9
, it suffices to show that
\langle b,\pi_{k}(s)\rangle=0
for every
b\in(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)^{r}
. Consider
\iota_{k}^{-1}(b)\in\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},\dots,p_{k}]
. By Proposition
10.14
it lies in the ideal
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}
. By Propositions
10.9
and
10.7
, it suffices to show that
\langle m,\pi_{k}(s)\rangle=0
, where
m
is any degree
>r
monomial in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
. Because
m
is a linear combination of monomials in
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
, by the definition of
\pi_{k}(m)
we have that
\langle m,\pi_{k}(s)\rangle=\langle m,s\rangle=0
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
10.5
.

We have already constructed
\pi_{k}
and shown that it is unique. Proposition
10.17
states that if
\pi_{k}
preserves the subspace
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
when
k\geq|A|
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
. Suppose that we have
s_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
such that
\sum_{i\in I}s_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
. The operator
\pi_{k}
is projection onto the subspace
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, so we have
\sum_{i\in I}s_{i}=\pi_{k}(\sum_{i\in I}s_{i})=\sum_{i}\pi_{k}(s_{i})
. By linear independence of
\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
and the fact that
\pi_{k}(s_{i})\in\mathcal{V}_{A_{i},r_{i}}
it follows that
\pi_{k}(s_{i})=s_{i}
for all
i
. Thus all of the
s_{i}
are in
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally we include an alternate description of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, that can be used to compute
\pi_{k}
quickly some cases.

Proposition 10.18
.

Let
(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm homog}_{\leq k}
denote the closure of the span of monomials of degree
\leq k
in the homogenous symmetric functions. Then
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}=(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm homog}_{\leq k}
.

Proof.

We need to show that the closure of the span
s_{\lambda}
where
\lambda
are integer partitions with
\leq d
rows equals
(\hat{\Lambda})^{\rm homog}_{\leq d}
. By the Pieri rule
[
26
, 7.15.9]
, we have that every monomial
h_{i}
of length
\leq d
is a sum of such
s_{\lambda}
, showing one direction of the containment. To prove equality, observe that in degree
n
the two subspaces have the same dimension: the number of integer partitions of
n
with
\leq d
rows.
∎

We conclude this section with an example.

Example 10.19
.

Consider the space
\mathcal{V}_{1,2}
, which is the closure of the span of
\exp(y_{1})
and
\{p_{n}\exp(y_{1})\}_{n\geq 1}
. Then because

\exp(y_{1})=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n},

we have that
\pi_{2}(\exp(y_{1}))=\exp(y_{1})
and
\pi_{2}(p_{1}\exp(y_{1}))=p_{1}\exp(y_{1})
. We also have that
\pi_{2}(p_{n}\exp(y_{1}))=0
for
n\geq 3
, because
\epsilon_{2}(p_{n}\exp(y_{1}))=0
. So the interesting case is
\pi_{2}(p_{2}\exp(y_{1}))
. Observe that we have

\sum_{n\geq 2}p_{n}\exp(y_{1})=\sum_{n\geq 2}nh_{n}

by Newtons identity. Hence this element lies in
\mathcal{F}_{\leq 2}
and its image under
\epsilon_{2}
agrees with
p_{2}\exp(y_{1})
. So it must be
\pi_{2}(p_{2}\exp(y_{1}))
.

It is also possible to compute
\pi_{2}(p_{2}\exp(y_{1}))
directly from Definition
10.13
. We describe a systematic method for computing
\pi_{k}
in §
11.4
.

11.
Generating functions valued in the dual character space

We begin with a summary of what we know so far. First Theorem
10.5
and Theorem
9.10
immediately imply the following.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
J=j_{1}\geq\dots\geq j_{r}
be an integer partition, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
k\geq j_{1}
.
Then if
M
is an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of type
<J
and rank
\leq k
, we have

(11.2)

\ch(M)\in\bigoplus_{A\text{ integer partition }}\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,t(A,J)},

where
t(A,J)=\#\{i\in\{1,\dots r\}~|~j_{i}>|A|\}
.

In this section, we study each summand of (
11.2
) individually.
Thus, for the remainder of this section, we fix an integer partition
A=1^{a_{1}}2^{a_{2}}\dots
, and
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|A|\leq k
. We set
u_{n}:=p_{n}-\sum_{d|n}da_{d}
and
u_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i}u_{i}^{m_{i}}
for
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}\dots
an integer partition. We will consider
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
and the projection operator
\pi_{k}:\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\to\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
.

Now, Propositions
10.11
and
10.16
show that there are isomorphisms.

(11.3)

\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cong\left(\frac{\Lambda}{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}+(e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\dots)}\right)^{*}\cong\left(\frac{\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]}{(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}}\right)^{*},

which take an element
s\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{a,r}
to the function
\langle-,s\rangle:\Lambda\to\mathbb{Q},
and to the restriction of this function to degree
<r
monomials in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
.

11.1.
An interpretation

We can interpret the isomorphisms of (
11.3
) in the following way, which we find clarifying. Let
\ch(V)\in\hat{\Lambda}
be the character of some sequence of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
representations
V
. Then elements
m\in\Lambda
correspond to different
measurements
that we can perform on
V
, by taking the inner product
\langle m,\ch(V)\rangle
. For example:

•

The element
m=p_{\lambda}
measures the trace of an element of
\mathrm{S}_{|\lambda|}
with cycle type
\lambda
,

\langle p_{\lambda},\ch(V)\rangle=\Tr(\sigma_{\lambda},V_{|\lambda|}).

•

The element
m=h_{n}
measures the dimension of the space of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
invariants:

\langle h_{n},\ch(V)\rangle=\dim(V_{n})^{\mathrm{S}_{n}}.

•

The element
m=u_{\lambda}
measures the coefficient
c_{\lambda}
in the unique expansion

\ch(V)=\sum_{\lambda\text{ integer partition}}c_{\lambda}\frac{p_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}\exp(\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}y_{i}),

by Lemma
10.10
.

In these terms, the first isomorphism states that if
\ch(V)\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
then the value of the measurement
m\in\Lambda
only depends on its image in the quotient
\frac{\Lambda}{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}+(e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\dots)}
. (Measurements that were independent for arbitrary characters are correlated for elements of
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
). And the second isomorphism shows that to determine the value of an arbitrary measurement
m\in\Lambda
we can proceed in two steps: (1) compute the measurements which are monomials in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
, (2) express the image of
m
in the quotient ring as a linear combination of monomials in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
.

Step (2) is nontrivial, since we have to actually compute the isomorphism which Proposition
10.16
provides abstractly. One can use Gröbner bases to algorithmically reduce
m
into a normal form, but this does not yield explicit formulas. The purpose of this section is to introduce a strategy for accomplishing step (2) in the cases
m=u_{\lambda}
and
m=h_{n}
, using generating functions. We use this strategy to construct an explicit basis for
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, to describe how to compute
\pi_{k}
, and to prove Theorem
1.17
.

11.2.
A generating function identity

We let
R:=\frac{\Lambda}{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)^{r}+(e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\dots)}
be the quotient ring, and write
f:\Lambda\to R
for the quotient homomorphism.

Let
\lambda
be an integer partition. We wish to express
f(u_{\lambda})
as a linear combination of
f(u_{\nu})
where
\nu=1^{\ell_{1}}\dots k^{\ell_{k}}
has
\leq k
columns (in other words
u_{\nu}
is a monomial in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
).
Our strategy is to first compute this expression for
f(u_{n})
and then use the homomorphism property to compute it for
f(u_{\lambda})
. Accordingly, we compute an expression for the
R
-valued generating function

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}\in R[[t]].

We will also consider generating functions valued in
\Lambda
(i.e. elements of
\Lambda[[t]]
). There is a specialization homomorphism
f:\Lambda[[t]]\to R[[t]]
, given by applying
f:\Lambda\to R
coefficient wise. Now, consider

\exp(-\sum_{n}(u_{n}t^{n})/n)\in\Lambda[[t]].

We have

\displaystyle\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}(u_{n}t^{n})/n)

\displaystyle=\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}t^{n}/n)\exp(\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i|n}ia_{i}t^{n}/n)=\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}t^{n}/n)\exp(\sum_{e,i\geq 1}a_{i}\frac{(t^{i})^{e}}{e})

(11.4)

\displaystyle=(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}e_{n}t^{n})\exp(\sum_{i\geq 1}-a_{i}\log(1-t^{i}))=\frac{1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}e_{n}t^{n}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}

Where we have used the well-known identity,

(11.5)

(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}e_{n}t^{n})=\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}t^{n}/n),

which can be proved by writing
p_{n}=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{n}
and using the power series expansion of
\log
.

Applying the specialization homomorphism
f:\Lambda[[t]]\to R[[t]]
we obtain the identity:

\exp\left(-\sum_{n}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}\right)=\frac{1-f(e_{1})t+\dots+(-1)^{k}f(e_{k})t^{k}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}

Now, we wish to write express the numerator in terms of the variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in R
. Before doing this, we introduce some notation.

Definition 11.6
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
c_{m}
be the coefficient of
t^{m}
in
\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}.

Concretely
c_{m}
is an alternating sum of products of binomial coefficients. (We may also interpret it as the trace of an operator constructed as in the proof of Proposition
10.14
).

Definition 11.7
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
we define
E_{m}\in\Lambda
to be
E_{m}:=-(-1)^{m}e_{m}+c_{m}
. Notice that
E_{0}=0
and
E_{m}=-(-1)^{m}e_{m}
for
m>|A|
. We extend this definition to integer partitions, by defining
E_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i}E_{i}^{m_{i}}
if
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
.

By definition, we have that

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}e_{n}t^{n}=-\sum_{m\geq 0}E_{m}t^{m}+\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}.

Therefore:

\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}t^{n}/n)=1-\frac{\sum_{m\geq 1}E_{m}t^{m}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}.

Proposition 11.8
.

We have that

E_{m}=-\sum_{n=1}^{m}(-1)^{n}c_{m-n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sgn(\lambda)\frac{u_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}.

In particular
E_{m}\in(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)\Lambda
.

Proof.

By (
11.4
) we have:

\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}t^{n}/n)\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}=1-\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}e_{n}t^{n}.

We have the well-known identity

\exp(-\sum_{n\geq 1}v_{n}t^{n}/n)=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sgn(\lambda)\frac{v_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}},

for any family of independent variables
v_{n}
. (In particular, for
v_{n}=p_{n}
this identity follows from (
11.5
) and the character of the sign representation).

Thus the coefficient of
t^{m}
in
\exp(-\sum u_{n}t^{n}/n)\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}
is

c_{m}+\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{m-n}(-1)^{n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sgn(\lambda)\frac{u_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}.

Subtracting
c_{m}
and multiplying by
-1
, we obtain the result.
∎

Since
|A|\leq k
we have that
E_{m}=\pm e_{m}
for all
m>k
. Hence
f(E_{m})=0
for all
m\geq k
, and specializing we obtain:

\exp\left(-\sum_{n}f(u_{n})t^{n}/n\right)=1-\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}.

Therefore we have that

\sum_{n}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}=-\log\left(1-\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}E_{m}t^{m}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}\right).

Because the numerator
\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m}
is nilpotent of order
r
we obtain a finite Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}+\frac{(\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m})^{2}}{2\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{2a_{i}}}+\dots+\frac{(\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m})^{r-1}}{(r-1)\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{(r-1)a_{i}}}

(11.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in{\rm Part}(r,k)}f(E_{\lambda})t^{|\lambda|}\frac{(\rank(\lambda)-1)!}{\lambda!\left(\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}\right)^{\rank(\lambda)}},

where the sum in the second line is over all integer partitions whose Young diagrams have
\leq k
columns and
<r
rows. In the passage to the second line we have used the multinomial identity

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)^{n}=\sum_{\lambda=1^{m_{1}}\dots k^{m_{k}},~\sum_{i}m_{i}=n}{n\choose m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}x_{\lambda}.

Equation (
11.9
) is our desired expression for
\sum_{n}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}
. The remaining subsections are independent of each other. In the next two subsections we apply (
11.9
) to construct a basis for
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, then describe how to use (
11.9
) to compute the operator
\pi_{k}
. After that, we prove Theorem
1.17
using a related identity. Finally, we discuss the relationship between characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
and
\mathbf{FI}_{r}
modules.

11.3.
A convenient basis for
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}

Definition 11.10
.

Let
{\rm Part}(r,k)
be the set of integer partitions whose Young diagrams have
\leq k
columns and
<r
rows.

From the point of view of character functions, the elements
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))
for
\nu\in{\rm Part}(r,k)
are a natural basis for
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
. However, we do not know a simple expression for these elements. In this subsection we construct a different basis, better adapted to Equation (
11.9
).

Proposition 11.11
.

There is an isomorphism

R\cong\frac{\mathbb{Q}[E_{1},\dots,E_{k}]}{(E_{1},\dots,E_{k})^{r}}

defined by
E_{i}\mapsto f(E_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Consequently, the set
\{f(E_{\nu})\}_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(r,k)}
is a basis for
R
.

Proof.

We already know, by Proposition
10.16
, that
u_{i}\mapsto f(u_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,k
gives an isomorphism
\frac{\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]}{(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}}\to R
. Since
E_{m}\in(u_{1},\dots)
we have that
f(s)=0
for all
s\in(E_{1},\dots,E_{k})^{r}
thus the homomorphism is well defined.

To see that it is surjective, note that by Proposition
11.8

E_{m}\equiv u_{m}/m+\sum_{1\leq n<m}c_{m-n}u_{n}/n\mod(u_{1},u_{2}\dots)^{2}.

Using this equation, by upper-triangularity we may write any
f(u_{m})
as a linear combination of
f(E_{m})
modulo
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{2}
. Since any collection of elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]/(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{r}
satisfying
x_{i}\equiv u_{i}\mod(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})^{2}
generate the ring, it follows that these linear combinations of
f(E_{m})
generate. Thus the homomorphism is surjective, and comparing dimensions it is also injective.
∎

Since by Proposition
11.11
the elements
\{f(E_{\nu})\}_{\nu\in{\rm Part}(r,k)}
form a basis of
R
, we may make the following definition.

Definition 11.12
.

We define
\{L_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(r,k)}
to be the basis of
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
which is dual to
\{f(E_{\nu})\}_{\lambda\in{\rm Part}(r,k)}
.

Thus
L_{\nu}
is the unique element of
\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
satisfying
\langle E_{\lambda},L_{\nu}\rangle=\delta(\lambda,\nu)
for all integer partitions
\lambda,\nu\in{\rm Part}(r,k)
. We will now an explicit epxression for
L_{\nu}
.

Definition 11.13
.

Let
B=1^{b_{1}}2^{b_{2}}\dots
be an integer partition. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we define
g_{B}(n)
be the coefficient of
t^{n}
in
\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{-b_{i}}
. In particular
g_{B}(n)=0
for
n<0
. For
n\geq 0
we have

g_{B}(n)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n,~\lambda=1^{m_{2}}2^{m_{2}}\dots}~\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(\kern-3.00003pt\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{ b_i}{m_i }\right)\kern-3.00003pt\right).

The function
g_{B}(n)
equals a quasi-polynomial of degree
\rank(B)
, for all
n\geq 0
.

Definition 11.14
.

If
\nu=1^{\ell_{1}}2^{\ell_{2}}\dots
and
\nu^{\prime}=1^{\ell_{1}^{\prime}}2^{\ell_{2}^{\prime}}\dots
we write
\nu^{\prime}+\nu
for the partition
1^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{1}^{\prime}}2^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{2}^{\prime}}\dots
, making the set of partitions into a commutative monoid (corresponding to multiplication of monomials). For
n\in\mathbb{N}
write
n\cdot\nu
for the
n
-fold addition of
\nu
in this monoid.

Definition 11.15
.

Define
H_{\nu}(n):=n~g_{\rank(\nu)\cdot A}(n-|\nu|)\frac{(\rank(\nu)-1)!}{\nu!}

By Equation (
11.9
), we have that
f(u_{n})=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(r,k),|\nu|>0}H_{\nu}(n)f(E_{\nu}).
So for
\lambda=n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots
we have that

f(u_{\lambda})=\prod_{i}\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(r,k)}H_{\nu}(n_{i})f(E_{\nu})\right)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(r,k)}f(E_{\nu})\sum_{\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i},~|\nu_{i}|>0}\prod_{i\geq 1}H_{\nu_{i}}(n_{i}),

Pairing with
L_{\nu}
we get a formula for
\langle u_{\lambda},L_{\nu}\rangle
. By Lemma
10.10
we obtain:

Proposition 11.16
.

There is an explicit formula for
L_{\nu}
.

L_{\nu}=\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})\sum_{\lambda=n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots}\frac{p_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}\ \sum_{\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i},|\nu_{i}|>0}\prod_{i}H_{\nu_{i}}(n_{i}),

where the last sum is over all finite tuples of integer partitions
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{l})
with
|\nu_{i}|>0
and
\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}
.

Example 11.17
.

Let
\nu
be the partition
3\geq 2
. Then
L_{\nu}
is
\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})
times

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}}{n}H_{3\geq 2}(n)+\sum_{n>m\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}p_{m}}{nm}(H_{3}(n)H_{2}(m)+H_{2}(n)H_{3}(m))+\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{p_{n}}{n}\right)^{2}H_{3}(n)H_{2}(n).

11.4.
Computing the operator
\pi_{k}

Let
\lambda
be an integer partition. We discuss how to compute
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))
.

We assume that
p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k})\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
, or equivalently that
\rank(\nu)<r
. If the Young diagram of
\lambda
has
>k
columns, then
\epsilon_{k}(p_{\nu})=0
and thus
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))=0.

Therefore, suppose that
\nu\in{\rm Part}(r,k)
. By Lemma
10.10
the coefficients
b_{\lambda,\nu}
in the expansion

\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}))=\sum_{\lambda}b_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{p_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}),

are given by
\langle u_{\lambda},\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))\rangle
. Let

(11.18)

f(u_{\lambda})=\sum_{\nu\in{\rm Part}(r,k)}\overline{b}_{\lambda,\nu}f(u_{\nu})

be the unique expansion
f(u_{\lambda})
in
R
. By Proposition
10.11
, since
\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}))\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{A,r}
, we have that
\langle u_{\lambda},\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{k}))\rangle
only depends on
f(u_{\lambda})
. And by the definition of
\pi_{k}
, we have that for every
\psi\in\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{t}(n,k)
that

\langle u_{\psi},\pi_{k}(p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i}))\rangle=\langle u_{\psi},p_{\nu}\exp(\sum_{i}a_{i}y_{i})\rangle=\delta(\psi,\nu).

Applying this to (
11.18
) we obtain

b_{\lambda,\nu}=\overline{b}_{\lambda,\nu}.

The coefficients
\overline{b}_{\lambda,\nu}
can be computed from
\{\overline{b}_{n,\nu}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by multiplication. And Equation (
11.9
) can be used to compute
\overline{b}_{n,\nu}
. However, the equation for
\overline{b}_{\lambda,\nu}
one obtains in this way is unwieldy. So we will just carry out this computation in an example.

Example 11.19
.

Suppose that
k=2,r=3
and
a_{1}=1,a_{i}=0
for
i>1
. Then
c_{0}=1
,
c_{1}=-1
and
c_{i}=0
for
i>1
. So
f(E_{1})=-f(u_{1})
,
f(E_{2})=f(u_{2})/2-f(u_{1})^{2}/2+f(u_{1})
, and
f(E_{i})=0
for
i>3
. Then we have

\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}f(E_{m})t^{m}}{\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}=-f(u_{1})t+\frac{(f(u_{2})+f(u_{1})^{2})t^{2}}{2(1-t)}.

So by (
11.9
) we compute that
\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{f(u_{n})}{n}t^{n}
is

f(u_{1})(-t)+f(u_{2})\frac{t^{2}}{2(1-t)}+f(u_{1}^{2})\left(t^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{2(1-t)}\right)+f(u_{1}u_{2})\frac{-t^{2}}{2(1-t)}+f(u_{2}^{2})\frac{t^{4}}{4(1-t)^{2}}

Thus for
n\geq 4
we have that

f(u_{n})=\frac{n}{2}f(u_{2})+\frac{n}{2}f(u_{1}^{2})-\frac{n}{2}f(u_{1}u_{2})+\frac{n(n+1)}{4}f(u_{2}^{2}).

Which computes
\overline{b}_{n,\nu}
for all
\nu
and
n\geq 4
. To compute
\overline{b}_{\lambda,\nu}
for the remaining cases,
\lambda=n\geq m
, simply multiply
f(u_{n})f(u_{m})
.

11.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.17

We use a generating function argument to prove Theorem
1.17
.

Proof of Theorem
1.17
.

Fix an integer partition
\lambda
and consider the generating fuction

F_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}s_{(k,\lambda)}~t^{k+|\lambda|}\in\Lambda[[t]].

The following identity appears in the theory of
\mathbf{FI}
modules:

(11.20)

F_{\lambda}(t)=q_{\lambda}(t)+\sum_{\nu~|~\lambda-\nu\in\mathrm{V}\mathrm{S}}(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\nu|}s_{\nu}t^{|\nu|}\sum_{m\geq 0}h_{m}t^{m},

where the sum is over all
\nu
such that the Young diagram of
\lambda
minus the Young diagram of
\nu
is a vertical strip, and
q_{\lambda}(t)\in\Lambda[t]
has degree
\leq|\lambda|
(in both
\Lambda
and
t
). In
[
22
, §5]
these polynomials also go under the name
q_{\lambda}
, although they use the coordinates
t_{i}:=p_{i}/i
for the ring of symmetric functions. (They show that this identity corresponds to a resolution in the quotient of
\Mod\mathbf{FI}/\Mod\mathbf{FI}^{\rm tors}
and interpret
q_{\lambda}
in terms of local cohomology in
[
22
, §7.4]
).

Now an integer partition
A
and
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq|A|
. We use the notation
R,E_{\lambda}
from above, relative to these choices if parameters. From here on we consider the image of our generating functions under the specialization
\Lambda[[t]]\to R[[t]].
We have that

s_{\nu}t^{|\nu|}\sum_{m\geq 0}h_{m}t^{m}=\frac{s_{\nu}t^{|\nu|}}{1+\sum_{m\geq 1}(-1)^{m}e_{m}t^{m}}=\frac{s_{\nu}t^{|\nu|}}{\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}-\sum_{m=1}^{k}E_{m}t^{m}}.

factoring out
\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}
and applying a geometric series expansion we have that this equals

\frac{s_{\nu}t^{|\nu|}}{\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}\sum_{n=0}^{r-1}\left(\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}E_{m}t^{m}}{\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}}\right)^{n}.

For any
u\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
, applying
\langle-,u\rangle
to this expression produces a rational function in
\mathbb{Z}[[t]]
. Thus
\sum_{m\geq 0}\langle s_{\nu}h_{m},u\rangle t^{m+|\nu|}
is rational and therefore so is
\sum_{m\geq|\nu|}\langle s_{(m,\nu)},u\rangle t^{m+|\nu|}
by (
11.20
). The denominator of this function is of degree
\leq r|A|
and its roots are roots of unity of order
\leq|A|
.

Finally if
M
is an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module of type
(d,s)
, then
\ch(M)
is a finite sum of
u_{A,r,k}\in\mathcal{V}_{A,r}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}
for
|A|\leq d
and
r\leq s
. So the generating function
\sum_{m\geq|\nu|}\langle s_{(m,\nu)},\ch(M)\rangle t^{m+|\nu|}
is rational and its denominator has degree
\leq sd
, and the roots of the denominator are roots of unity of order
\leq d
.
∎

Remark 11.21
.

In practice, the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.17
can be used to make computations. For example, fix
A
an integer partition, and
d,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
d>|A|
. Then the proof of Theorem
1.17
yields a formula for
\sum_{n}\langle h_{n},L_{\lambda}\rangle t^{n}
. Namely it is

\sum_{n}\langle h_{n},L_{\lambda}\rangle t^{n}=\frac{t^{|\lambda|}\rank(\lambda)!}{\lambda!\left(\prod_{i}(1-t^{i})^{a_{i}}\right)^{\rank(\lambda)+1}}.

∎

11.6.
Relationship with
\mathbf{FI}_{d}
module characters

Sam–Snowden
[
24
]
characterized the Hilbert series of modules over the category
\mathbf{FI}_{d}
. They express these characters as polynomials in the elements
\sigma_{n}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}{n\choose k}h_{n}
, and observe that
\sigma_{n}
takes the form
q_{n}(T_{1},T_{2},\dots)\exp(y_{1})
where
q_{n}
is a polynomial in
T_{k}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}{n\choose k}\frac{p_{n}}{n}.
In fact,
q_{n}
is the
n^{\rm th}
complete ordinary Bell polynomial. For
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots
, define
\sigma^{\lambda}:=\prod_{i}\sigma_{i}^{m_{i}}
. Then if
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda
satsifies
\rank(\lambda)\leq k
we have that

\sigma^{\lambda}\sigma_{0}^{k-\rank(\lambda)}\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1^{k},|\lambda|+1}

Sam–Snowden show that these elements are linearly independent. Thus as
\lambda
ranges over partitions with
\leq k
rows and
|\lambda|<r
, we obtain a collection of linearly independent elements in
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1^{k},r}.
Since the dimension of this space is the number of partitions with
\leq k
rows and
<r
columns, which is strictly larger than the number of
\sigma^{\lambda}\sigma_{0}^{k-\rank(\lambda)},
this collection of elements does not span.

Sam–Snowden compute the character of an
\mathbf{FI}_{d}
module to be a sum of
s_{\nu}\sigma^{\lambda}\sigma_{0}^{k-\rank(\lambda)}
. We do not know if it is possible to use such elements to construct a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{\leq k^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1^{k},r^{\prime}}
for some
r^{\prime},k^{\prime}
.

12.
Further Questions

We list some questions related to our work. Many of these questions stem from the fact that we still do not understand the representation theory of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules.

12.1.
Behavior of the type under kernels and cokernels

For applications, it would be useful to have more sophisticated methods of proving that a specific
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module is of class
(d,s)
.

There are two numerical invariants associated to an
\mathbf{FI}
module
M
, the stable degree
\delta(M)
and the local degree
h^{\rm max}(M)
, introduced and used to great effect in
[
8
]
. The type of an
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module is roughly analogous to these invariants. Given a morphism of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules
f:N_{1}\to N_{2}
, is it possible to bound the type of
\ker f
and
\coker f
in terms of the type of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
(or other invariants)?

12.2.
Grothendieck group of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules

When do two finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules have the same character? In other words, what is the kernel of the map of Grothendieck groups:
\mathrm{K}_{0}({\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}})\to\prod_{n}\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{S}_{n})
?

12.3.
Are
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module characters cycle bounded?

We say that a symmetric function is
cycle bounded
if it is a linear combination of finitely many functions of the form

\exp(\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}y_{i})\sum_{\lambda\text{ integer partition}}c_{\lambda}p_{\lambda},

where only finitely many
c_{\lambda}
are non-zero and
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\oplus\infty}
. Is the character of every finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module cycle bounded? If not, what is an example of a finitely generated
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
module that is not cycle bounded?

We note that projective
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules are cycle bounded, and cycle bounded characters are closed under sums, induction product and Kronecker product. And if
t
is cycle bounded, then
s_{\lambda}[t]
is also cycle bounded. On the other hand, Sam–Snowden show in
[
24
]
that when
d>1
there are
\mathbf{FI}_{d}
modules that are not cycle-bounded.

12.4.
Characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules in characteristic
>0

What are the consequences of Theorem
1.2
for fields of positive characteristic? Is it possible to carry out a similar combinatorial analysis for the characters of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
modules in this case?

12.5.
Other combinatorial categories

The arguments we use to prove Theorem
1.2
are mostly general. The specific combinatorics of
{\mathbf{FS}}{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}
only enters in §
6
. We are interested in extensions of the method of proof of Theorem
1.2
, to other categories studied by Sam–Snowden
[
23
]
. See for instance Conjecture
3.22
. For which combinatorial categories does the method of proof of Theorem
1.2
work?

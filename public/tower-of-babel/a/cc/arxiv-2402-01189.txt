On Malle’s conjecture for the product of symmetric and nilpotent groups
Hrishabh Mishra, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite nilpotent group and n ∈ { 3 , 4 , 5 } n\in\{3,4,5\} . Consider S n × G S_{n}\times G as a subgroup of S n × S | G | ⊂ S n ​ | G | S_{n}\times S_{|G|}\subset S_{n|G|} , where G G embeds into the second factor of S n × S | G | S_{n}\times S_{|G|} via the regular representation. Over any number field k k , we prove the strong form of Malle’s conjecture (cf. [ Mal02 , p.316] ) for S n × G S_{n}\times G viewed as a subgroup of S n ​ | G | S_{n|G|} . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical context

Let
k
be a number field and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a transitive subgroup of
S_{n}
. Given a number field extension
K/k
, denote by
\widetilde{K}
its Galois closure over
k
. Suppose that
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n
. We enumerate the embeddings
\iota_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\hookrightarrow\bar{K}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, and for
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
, consider the composite

\iota_{\sigma(j)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\xrightarrow{\iota_{j}}\bar{K}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\bar{K}.

This gives rise to a natural permutation representation and we realize
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)
as a transitive subgroup of
S_{n}
. Set
\operatorname{disc}(K/k)
to denote the relative discriminant of
K/k
and take

\operatorname{Disc}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\operatorname{Norm}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{disc}(K/k))|.

For
X>0
, let
N_{k}(\mathcal{G};X)
be the number of extensions
K/k
with
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n
for which
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}
as a permutation subgroup of
S_{n}
, and
\operatorname{Disc}(K)\leq X
. The quantity
N_{k}(\mathcal{G};X)
depends not only on
\mathcal{G}
and
k
, but also on the embedding of
\mathcal{G}
in
S_{n}
, and this is suppressed in our notation. For
g\in S_{n}
, set

(1.1)

\operatorname{ind}(g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=n-\text{number of orbits for }g.

We set
1=1_{\mathcal{G}}
to denote the identity element in
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{G}\backslash\{1\}
.

Malle made predictions about the asymptotic growth of
N_{k}(\mathcal{G};X)
as
X\rightarrow\infty
. We define

a(\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\operatorname{min}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\mid g\in\mathcal{G}^{\ast}\}\right)^{-1}.

The absolute Galois group
G_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/k)
of
k
acts on the set of conjugacy classes
C(\mathcal{G})
of
\mathcal{G}
via the action of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-characters of
\mathcal{G}
. We define

b(k,\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\{C\in C(\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{ind}(C)=\operatorname{ind}(\mathcal{G})\}/G_{k}.

The following conjecture (cf.
[
Mal04
, Conjecture 1.1]
) is referred to as the
strong form of Malle’s conjecture
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

We have that

N_{k}(\mathcal{G};X)\sim c(k,\mathcal{G})X^{a(\mathcal{G})}(\log X)^{b(k,\mathcal{G})-1},

for some constant
c(k,\mathcal{G})>0
.

We also have the following weak conjecture (cf.
[
Mal02
, p.316]
) which is referred to as the
weak form of Malle’s conjecture

Conjecture 1.2
.

For all
\epsilon>0
,

X^{a(\mathcal{G})}\ll_{k,\mathcal{G}}N_{k}(\mathcal{G};X)\ll_{k,\mathcal{G},\epsilon}X^{a(\mathcal{G})+\epsilon}.

The strong form of Malle’s conjecture has been shown to be false, and Klüners provided an explicit counterexample, cf.
[
Klü05
]
. However, the weak version of the conjecture is still widely believed to be true. The strong form is however known for various families of groups, some of the well-known cases are listed below.

•

The conjecture has been proven for the abelian groups by Maki
[
Mäk85
]
and Wright
[
Wri89
]
.

•

For the groups
S_{n}

[
DH71
]
for
n=3
, for
n=4,5

[
Bha05

]
and for general number fields

by Bhargava–Shankar–Wang

]

•

For the dihedral group

•

Let

be a finite nilpotent group,

. Assume that all elements of order

via the regular representation. The strong form of Malle’s conjecture in this context was recently resolved by Koymans and Pagano

]
. The weak version of the conjecture for all finite nilpotent groups

1.2.
Main result

Let
G
be a finite nilpotent group and
\ell_{G}
the smallest prime factor of
|G|
. We view
G
as a subgroup of
S_{|G|}
via the regular representation. On the other hand,
S_{n}
is in its natural degree
n
representation (thus contained in
S_{n}
via the identity map). We recall that
a(G)=\frac{\ell_{G}}{(\ell_{G}-1)|G|}
,
a(S_{n})=1
, and
b(k,S_{n})=1
for all
n\geq 2
. It is easy to see that
a(S_{n}\times G)=\frac{1}{|G|}
. Since
{a(S_{n})}{n}>{a(G)|G|}
we deduce that
b(k,S_{n}\times G)=b(k,S_{n})
, cf.
[
Wan21
, p.91 l.-3 – p. 92, l.2]
. We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial finite nilpotent group and
k
a number field. Consider the regular representation
\operatorname{reg}_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\hookrightarrow S_{|G|}
. Here,
S_{n}\times G
embeds in
S_{n|G|}
via natural inclusions

S_{n}\times G\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\times\operatorname{reg}_{G}}S_{n}\times S_{|G|}\hookrightarrow S_{n|G|}.

Then there exists a constant
c(k,S_{n}\times G)>0
such that

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)\sim c(k,S_{n}\times G)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}

in the following cases:

(1)

n=3
, if
2\nmid|G|
,

(2)

n=4
, if
2,3\nmid|G|
,

(3)

n=5
, if
2,3,5\nmid|G|
.

Thus, Conjecture
1.1
is true in this setting.

This extends the main result of
[
Wan21
]
. The result is proven via a synthesis of the methods of Klüners–Malle
[
KM04
]
, Wang
[
Wan21
]
and Koymans–Pagano
[
KP23
]
. The conditions on
G
are consistent with those in
[
Wan21
]
.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

We closely follow the method in
[
Wan21
]
. Two complications have to be addressed. First, the method requires strong uniformity estimates for nilpotent groups. We address this in Section
3
. It is worth noting that although a weaker uniformity estimate suffices for proving the main theorem, we pursue a sharp bound to attain the best possible uniformity estimate (see Proposition
3.6
). This result stands as a potentially independent point of interest. Proposition
4.6
gives us a crucial ingredient for proving precise asymptotics for
S_{n}\times G
-extensions. This technical result is the analogue of
[
Wan21
, p. 117 (5.6)]
, however, it is proven via a different strategy. For more details, see Section
4
.

1.4.
Organization

Including the Introduction the paper consists of four sections. In section
2
, several preliminary results are proven on the discriminants of the compositum of two number fields. Furthermore, some analytic tools are introduced here. In section
3
, using the parameterization for finite nilpotent extensions of
k
with fixed Galois group
G
given by Koymans and Pagano
[
KP23
]
we prove a local uniformity estimate for nilpotent groups (cf. Proposition
3.6
). The proof of the main theorem is given in section
4
.

1.5.
Outlook

It is possible that the inductive methods used in this article could eventually lead to the proof of the strong form of Malle’s conjecture in other similar cases. We have been apprised of ongoing efforts by Alberts, Lemke-Oliver, Wang, and Wood, who are expected to develop more generalized methods similar to Wang and ours to establish the strong form of Malle’s conjecture for various groups.

Lemma 2.4

Let

Proof.

The result is essentially the same as

to apply.
∎

A lower bound for

is given below.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose there exist real constants

such that

c_{1}X\leq F_{1}(X)\text{ and }c_{2}X^{\alpha}\leq F_{2}(X)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

then, for all large enough values of

, we have that

(2.11)

cX^{1/a}\leq P_{a,b}(X),

where

.

Proof.

The proof of our result adapts the first part of
[
Wan21
, Lemma 3.2]
. Given

, we set

to be the number of times

occurs in the set
S_{i}
. From the definition of

, we deduce that

(2.12)

\begin{split}P_{a,b}(X)&=\sum_{r_{1}^{a}r_{2}^{b}\leq X}n_{1}(r_{1})n_{2}(r_{2})=\sum_{r^{b}\leq X}n_{2}(r)F_{1}((X/r^{b})^{1/a})\\
&\geq c_{1}X^{1/a}\sum_{r^{b}\leq X}\frac{n_{2}(r)}{r^{b/a}}.\end{split}

Using Abel’s summation formula,

\sum_{r\leq X^{1/b}}\frac{n_{2}(r)}{r^{b/a}}\geq\frac{c_{2}\underline{X}^{\alpha}}{\underline{X}^{b/a}}+c_{2}\int_{1}^{\underline{X}}\frac{t^{\alpha}}{t^{b/a+1}}dt,

where
\underline{X}
denotes the largest integer smaller or equal to

such that

. Noting that the integral
\int_{1}^{\underline{X}}\frac{t^{\alpha}}{t^{b/a+1}}dt=a/(b-a\cdot\alpha)\cdot(1-\underline{X}^{\alpha-b/a})
. Hence,

\int_{1}^{\underline{X}}\frac{t^{\alpha}}{t^{b/a+1}}dt\geq a/(b-a\cdot\alpha)\cdot(1-2^{\alpha-b/a})

∎

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}
. We can write

\begin{split}&v^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{G-H(G)}(v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}(v_{g}(1),v_{g}(2));\\
&v^{\prime\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{I(G)}(v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{h\in I(G)}(v_{h}(1),v_{h}(2)).\end{split}

The elements
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}
belong to
\operatorname{Prim}\left(\mathcal{S}^{G-H(G)}\right)
and
\operatorname{Prim}\left(\mathcal{S}^{I(G)}\right)
respectively. We set
N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)
to denote the number of
v^{\prime\prime}=\prod_{h\in I(G)}(v_{h}(1),v_{h}(2))\in\operatorname{Prim}\left(\mathcal{S}^{I(G)}\right)
such that

•

\mathfrak{q}_{2}\mid\prod_{h\in I(G)}v_{h}(2)^{a(G)^{-1}}
,

•

|\prod_{h\in I(G)}v_{h}(2)^{a(G)^{-1}}|\leq\left(\frac{X}{|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|}\right)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})\right)
be the set of all squarefree ideals
\mathcal{I}
supported in
\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})
. We note that for
h\in I(G)
, the only prime that divides the order of
h
is
\ell_{G}
. All primes
\mathfrak{p}
that divide
v_{h}(2)
must satisfy the congruence

\#\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\right)\equiv 1\mod{\ell_{G}}.

Therefore for
h\in I(G)
, the ideal
v_{h}(2)
belongs to
\mathcal{S}\left(\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})\right)
.

Since we prove an upper bound, we may as well neglect the coprimality conditions between
(v_{g}(1),v_{g}(2))
for
g\in G-H(G)
. It suffices to bound the following sum

(3.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime},|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|\leq X\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mid\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}\end{subarray}}N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X).

Set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\mathfrak{p}
to be the squarefree part of
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
and write
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}I_{2}=\prod_{h\in I(G)}v_{h}(2)
. For
h\in I(G)
, recall that the ideals
v_{h}(2)
are all squarefree and mutually coprime. Furthermore, they are supported on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})
. Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}I_{2}
is squarefree and supported in
\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})
. It is easy to see that

(3.5)

N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{2}\\
|\mathfrak{q}_{2}\left(I_{2}\right)^{a(G)^{-1}}|\leq\frac{X}{|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|},\\
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}I_{2}\text{ supported in }\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(I_{2})\#I(G)^{\omega(\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2})\omega(I_{2})},

cf. the proof of
[
KP23
]
[Theorem 5.1] for further details.

Set
L
to be the cyclotomic extension
k(\mu_{\ell_{G}})
and
\mathfrak{S}=\{1\}\subset\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)
. Note that an ideal
I
is supported on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{K}(\ell_{G})
if and only if all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}|I
split in
L
. Setting
z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#I(G)
, rewrite (
3.5
) as

N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\leq z^{\omega(\mathfrak{q}_{2})}A_{z}\left(Y\right),

where

Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{X^{a(G)}}{|\mathfrak{q}_{2}|^{a(G)}|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}a(G)}|}\right).

Using the estimate (
3.2
) we obtain the following bound

N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\ll_{G,k,\epsilon}Y(\log Y)^{\frac{z\#\mathfrak{S}}{[k(\mu_{\ell_{G}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]}-1}.

Noting that

i(G,k)=\frac{\#I(G)}{[k(\mu_{\ell_{G}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]}=\frac{z\#\mathfrak{S}}{[k(\mu_{\ell_{G}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]},

and that

\log(Y)\sim a(G)\log(X),

we obtain the following upper bound

(3.6)

N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\ll_{G,k,\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{q}_{2}|}\right)^{a(G)}\frac{X^{a(G)}}{|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}a(G)}|}(\log X)^{i(G,K)-1}.

For any
\epsilon>0
, the integral ideal
\mathfrak{q}
can be written as the product of two proper ideals in at most
O_{\epsilon}(|\mathfrak{q}|^{\epsilon})
ways.
From (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime},|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|\leq X\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mid\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}\end{subarray}}N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\\
\ll_{G,k,\epsilon}&|\mathfrak{q}|^{\epsilon/2}\operatorname{max}_{(\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2})}\left(\left(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}\right)^{a(G)}(\log X)^{i(G,k)-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\\
|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|\leq X,\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mid\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}a(G)}|}\right).\end{split}

Observe that
e_{g}a(G)>1
for
g\in G-H(G)
. Setting
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|G|-\#H(G)
, enumerate the elements of
G-H(G)
as
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
. Setting,
e_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=e(g_{i})
, we find that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\\
|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|\leq X,\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mid\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}a(G)}|}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\\
|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}}|\leq X\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}|},

where
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}
are coprime integral ideals. Since
g_{i}\notin I(G)
, we find that
e_{i}a(G)>1
. We write
I_{i}=\mathfrak{q}_{1,i}J_{i}
, where
J_{i}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, and
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
divides
\prod_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{1,i}^{e_{i}}
. Then, the product
\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}
can be written as

\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}=\prod_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{1,i}^{e_{i}a(G)}\prod_{i}J_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}.

Note that the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}|}

converges and in fact, is bounded above by

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{I}\frac{1}{|I|^{e_{i}a(G)}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\zeta_{K}(e_{i}a(G)).

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{k}
be the prime divisors of
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, which factorizes as
\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots\mathfrak{p}_{k}^{a_{k}}
. Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the set of all tuples

\mathfrak{T}=\{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{t})\mid\mathfrak{a}_{i}\text{ are coprime, }\prod_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\dots\mathfrak{p}_{k}\}.

Writing
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}_{i_{1}}\dots\mathfrak{p}_{i_{r}}
, set

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathfrak{p}_{i_{j}}^{\lceil a_{i_{j}}/e_{i}\rceil}.

We write

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}|}\\
=&\sum_{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{k})\in\mathfrak{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{i}\\
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}|I_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|I_{i}|^{e_{i}a(G)}}\right)\\
=&\sum_{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{k})\in\mathfrak{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(\sum_{J_{i}}\frac{1}{|\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}|^{e_{i}a(G)}|J_{i}|^{e_{i}a(G)}}\right).\end{split}

We note that
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{i}}^{e_{i}}
is divisible by
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, and therefore,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{i}I_{i}^{e_{i}a(G)}|}\leq\left(\frac{\#\mathfrak{T}}{|\mathfrak{q}_{1}|^{a(G)}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(\sum_{J_{i}}\frac{1}{|J_{i}|^{e_{i}a(G)}}\right)=\left(\frac{\#\mathfrak{T}}{|\mathfrak{q}_{1}|^{a(G)}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\zeta_{K}(e_{i}a(G)).

We note that
\#\mathfrak{T}=t^{\omega(\mathfrak{q}_{1})}=o(|\mathfrak{q}|^{\epsilon/2})
.
Combining the above estimates with (
3.7
), we thus obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime},|\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}|\leq X\\
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mid\prod_{g\in G-H(G)}v_{g}(2)^{e_{g}}\end{subarray}}N_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}(v^{\prime},X)\\
\ll_{G,K,\epsilon}|\mathfrak{q}|^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X}{|\mathfrak{q}|}\right)^{a(G)}(\log X)^{i(G,K)-1}.

This concludes the proof of the result.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem
1.3

In this section,
G
denotes a non-trivial finite nilpotent group. The results in this section are analogous to those in
[
Wan21
, Section 5]
. We also suppose that
G
satisfies the appropriate conditions of Theorem
1.3
for
S_{3},S_{4},
or
S_{5}
. We set

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{&(K,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\simeq S_{n},\\
&\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}(KL)\leq X\}\end{split}

and prove asymptotics for
\#\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
as
X\rightarrow\infty
. We shall prove that the same asymptotics hold for
N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
. We first establish a lower bound.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
and
k
be as in Theorem
1.3
, then,

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)\gg_{G,k}X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Proof.

Using the upper bound on
\mathrm{Disc}(KL)
from Proposition
2.1
, we note that

\displaystyle N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)\geq

\displaystyle\#\{(K,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G,\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}\leq X\}.

Malle’s conjecture is known for
S_{n}
(
n\in\{3,4,5\})

[
DH71
,
Bha05
,
Bha10
]
. For nilpotent groups, the weak form of the conjecture is known in generality, cf.
[
KM04
]
. Thus, we find that

\begin{split}&N_{k}(S_{n};X)\gg_{n,k}X,\text{ and }\\
&N_{k}(G;X)\gg_{G,k}X^{a(G)}.\end{split}

The result then follows from the above bounds and Corollary
2.8
setting
\operatorname{inv}_{1}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Disc}(K)
and
\operatorname{inv}_{1}(L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Disc}(L)
.
∎

Next, we examine a set of invariants denoted by
\{\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}\}_{Y>0}
for
S_{n}\times G
-extensions, defined below.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
Y>0
, and suppose
(K,L)\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
we define

\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega_{k}}\mathrm{Disc}_{Y,\mathfrak{p}}(KL),

where

\mathrm{Disc}_{Y,\mathfrak{p}}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)\text{ }\mathrm{if}\text{ }|\mathfrak{p}|\leq Y,\\
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}\text{ }\mathrm{if}\text{ }|\mathfrak{p}|>Y.\end{cases}

We set

(4.1)

\begin{split}N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\#\{(K,L)\mid[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\\
&\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n},\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)<X\}.\end{split}

Remark 4.3
.

We note here that a similar definition arises in Wang’s work. The above definition generalizes
[
Wan21
, equation (5.4)]
.

We remark that
\mathrm{Disc}(KL)\leq\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)
for all
Y>0
using Proposition
2.1
. Hence

N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)\leq N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)

for all
Y>0
. The invariants
\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}
approximate
\mathrm{Disc}
as
Y\to\infty
. The counting function associated with
\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}
is denoted as
N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)
. We establish asymptotics for
N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)
for
Y>0
and leverage these results to derive the intended asymptotics for
N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
.

Let
S_{Y}
be the finite set of all primes of
k
with absolute norm
|\mathfrak{p}|\leq Y
. Let
\Sigma_{Y}
denote the set of tuples
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}
of pairs of local étale extensions over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
of degree
n
and
|G|
respectively. We will think of these tuples as specifying local conditions at all
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}
for
S_{n}
and
G
-extensions. This argument is indeed an analogue of
[
Wan21
, p.116, l. -12]
. Accordingly, given a pair
(K,L)\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
and a tuple
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}\in\Sigma_{Y}
we write
(K,L)\in((\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}))_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}
, if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}
,

(1)

the local étale algebra
(K)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and

(2)

the local étale algebra
(L)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Next, we have the following Lemma relating
\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}
and
\mathrm{Disc}
under given local specifications.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
Y>0
and
\Pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=((\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}),\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}))_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}\in\Sigma_{Y}
. If
(K,L)\in\Pi
then,

\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}}{d_{\Pi}}

where
d_{\Pi}
depends only on
\Pi
.

Proof.

The results is similar to Wang’s argument (cf.
[
Wan21
, equation (5.5)]
), which applies in the case when
G
is abelian. From the definition of
\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}
, it follows that,

\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{Y}}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}.

Hence, it follows that

\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}}{d_{\Pi}}

with
d_{\Pi}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)^{-1}
. From Lemma
2.3
we deduce that
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)
,
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
and
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)
are determined by
(K)_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
(L)_{\mathfrak{p}}
and hence
d_{\Pi}
depends only on
\Pi
.
∎

Before proceeding we state the following easy Lemma from analytic number theory which will be used in the proof of the next Proposition.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}
be a sequence indexed by the positive integers for which
b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
. For a fixed positive real number
a>0
the following are equivalent:

(1)

for all
\epsilon>0

\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}\ll X^{a+\epsilon}\,,

(2)

the Dirichlet series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
converges for all real numbers
s>a
.

The above Lemma is also stated in
[
Alb20
, Lemma 3.1]
. We refer to
[
Ten15
]
for a proof. We state the next Proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
Y>0
then we have that,

N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G,X)\sim c(k,S_{n}\times G,Y)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Proof.

We count extensions by specifying local conditions at each prime
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{Y}
. Let
\Pi\in\Sigma_{Y}
, we consider the counting function

\displaystyle N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=

\displaystyle\#\{(K,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n},\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G,

\displaystyle\text{ and }(K,L)\in\Pi,\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\leq X\}.

Using Lemma
4.4
we obtain the following equality,

\displaystyle N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=

\displaystyle\#\{(K,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n},\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G,

\displaystyle\text{ and }(K,L)\in\Pi,\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}\leq d_{\Pi}X\}.

We use Corollary
2.8
along with the bounds from
[
BSW15
]
and
[
KP23
]

(4.2)

\begin{split}&N_{k}(S_{n};X)\ll_{n,k}X,\text{ and }\\
&N_{k}(G;X)\ll_{G,k}X^{a(G)}(\log X)^{i(G,k)-1},\end{split}

to get that

N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\Pi}X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Given a
G
-extension
L/k
, we write
L\in\Pi
to mean that there exists some number field
K
with
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n
and
\widetilde{K}
an
S_{n}
-extension of
k
, for which
(K,L)\in\Pi
. Given any
G
-extension
L\in\Pi
, we also consider the counting function,

\displaystyle N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X)=

\displaystyle\#\{K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n},(K,L)\in\Pi,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}\leq d_{\Pi}X\}.

Since we know precise asymptotics for counting
S_{n}
degree
n
extensions
[
BSW15
]
with finitely many local conditions, we deduce that for any
L\in\Pi
the asymptotic estimate holds

N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X)\sim\frac{c(k,S_{n},\Pi)}{\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n/|G|}}\cdot X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

We also note that,

N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=\sum_{L\in\Pi}N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X).

Given
Z>0
, it is clear that,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\Pi,\\
\mathrm{Disc}(L)\leq Z\end{subarray}}N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X)\sim C_{Y}^{Z}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Note that
C_{Y}^{Z}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)
is an increasing function of
Z>0
and it is bounded. Hence,

\lim_{Z\to\infty}C_{Y}^{Z}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)

exists and we denote it by
C_{Y}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)
. Clearly,

C_{Y}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)\leq\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)}{X^{1/|G|}}.

We consider the sum,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\Pi,\\
\mathrm{Disc}(L)>Z\end{subarray}}N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X)\ll_{k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi,Y}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\Pi,\\
\mathrm{Disc}(L)>Z\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n/|G|}}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Using Lemma
4.5
and the bound
4.2
we deduce that the sum,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\Pi,\\
\mathrm{Disc}(L)>Z\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{s}}

converges when
s>a(G)
. Since
\frac{n}{|G|}>a(G)
we obtain that

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\Pi,\\
\mathrm{Disc}(L)>Z\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{k,Y,\Pi}^{L}(S_{n}\times G;X)}{X^{1/|G|}}=0.

Therefore, we have that,

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)}{X^{1/|G|}}\leq C_{Y}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi).

Hence,
N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)\sim C_{Y}(k,S_{n}\times G,\Pi)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}
. Since the set
\Sigma_{Y}
is finite and

N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G,X)=\sum_{\Pi\in\Sigma_{Y}}N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X),

the Proposition follows.
∎

We have determined precise asymptotics for
N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)
across all
Y>0
mirroring the result we aim to establish for
N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)
. Next, we analyze the difference

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)

and obtain an upper bound for this expression, thereby concluding the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Before proceeding, we state the following important observation from index computation in Proposition
2.4
and local uniformity estimates in Theorem
3.1
.
Given a square-free ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
we denote by
N_{k,\mathfrak{q},\sigma}(S_{n};X)
the counting function for number of
S_{n}
degree
n
extensions
K
of
k
such that
\widetilde{K}
is tamely ramified at all primes
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}
with inertia groups equal to
\langle\sigma\rangle
up to conjugacy. We consider the set,

S_{\sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{s\geq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N_{k,\mathfrak{q},\sigma}(S_{n};X)\ll_{\epsilon}X/|\mathfrak{q}|^{s-\epsilon}\text{ for all squarefree }\mathfrak{q}\text{ in }k\},

and we define
s_{\sigma}=\sup S_{\sigma}
. The Theorem
3.1
gives explicit lower bounds on
s_{\sigma}
for certain
\sigma\in C(S_{n})
when
n=3,4,
or
5
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
n\in\{3,4,5\}
, let
G
be a finite nilpotent group. Then,

\mathrm{ind}(\sigma,g)/m-\mathrm{ind}(\sigma)+s_{\sigma}>1,

for all
(\sigma,g)\in(S_{n}\times G)\setminus\{(1,1)\}
.

Proof.

In the case when
\sigma
is totally ramified (or overramified), the result follows from Theorem
3.1
. In all other cases, the result is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.5
by simply employing the trivial lower bound
s_{\sigma}\geq 0
. Note that when
n=5
, and
\sigma
is conjugate to
(12345)
, then one happens to be in the totally ramified case and Theorem
3.1
applies.
∎

4.0.1.
The difference
N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)

We fix
Y>0
. From definition
4.2
it is obvious that this difference is given by,

(4.3)

\begin{split}&N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)\\
=&\#\{(K,L)\mid[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n},\\
&\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G,\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}(KL)<X<\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)\}.\end{split}

Our method for bounding the above counting function will closely resemble the proof outlined in Proposition
4.6
, wherein we express this counting function as the aggregate of various counting functions parameterized by specific local conditions at various primes in
k
. We proceed by describing these local conditions of our interest, we call such specification of local conditions a
standard tuple
.

Definition 4.8
.

A standard tuple is a tuple
(S,\mathcal{L})
satisfying following properties:

(1)

S
is a finite set of primes in
k
,

(2)

\mathcal{L}
is a tuple
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}
such that:

(a)

for each
\mathfrak{p}\mid n!|G|
the pair
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}
is a pair of ramified local étale algebras over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
of degree
n
and
|G|
respectively,

(b)

for each
\mathfrak{p}\nmid n!|G|
the pair
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}
is a pair of conjugacy classes of inertia generators in
S_{n}
and
G
respectively.

Let
\Sigma_{k}
denote the set of all
standard tuples
for the number field
k
. Given
(K,L)
and a specification of local conditions
\Pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(S,\mathcal{L})\in\Sigma_{k}
as above, we write
(K,L)\in\Pi
if following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega_{k}
is such that both
K
,
L
are ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
then
\mathfrak{p}\in S
.

(2)

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
such that for each
\mathfrak{p}\nmid n!|G|
, we have that
(I_{\mathfrak{p}}(K),I_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))=(\langle h_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle,\langle g_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle)
.

(3)

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
such that for each
\mathfrak{p}\mid n!|G|
, we have that
((K)_{\mathfrak{p}},(L)_{\mathfrak{p}})=(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Remark 4.9
.

We note that the notion of standard tuples arises in Wang’s work;
\Pi
used in this context is the exact analogue of the set considered in
[
Wan21
, equation (5.10)]
.

Write
K\in\Pi
if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
we have that,
(K)_{\mathfrak{p}}=h_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ or }I_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)=\langle h_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle
and we write
L\in\Pi
if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
we have that,
(L)_{\mathfrak{p}}=g_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ or }I_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)=\langle g_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle
. Following the notation in
[
Wan21
]
, we write
\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
to denote the exponent of
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)
where
(K)_{\mathfrak{p}}=h_{\mathfrak{p}}
or
I_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)=\langle h_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle
and
(L)_{\mathfrak{p}}=g_{\mathfrak{p}}
or
I_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)=\langle g_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle
. The following result shows that
\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
is completely determined by
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Lemma 4.10
.

In the above setting,
\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
depends only on the tuple
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{p}\nmid n!|G|
then using the
[
Wan21
, Theorem 2.3]
, the indices
\mathrm{ind}(h_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\mathrm{ind}(g_{\mathfrak{p}})
completely determine
\mathrm{ind}(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
and hence
\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
. When
\mathfrak{p}\mid n!|G|
, the Lemma
2.3
implies that
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)
is completely determined by the pair
((K)_{\mathfrak{p}},(L)_{\mathfrak{p}})
which is the pair
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

As a consequence of the above and our definition of a

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\Pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(S,\mathcal{L})\in\Sigma_{k}

\mathrm{Disc}(KL)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}}{d_{\Pi}}

where

Proof.

This result generalizes
[
Wan21
, equation (5.11)]
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
then either

or

is not ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
K/k
is not ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. We deduce from the upper bound in Proposition
2.1
that the
\mathrm{disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL/k)\mid\mathrm{disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L/k)^{n}
. We recall that in the tower
KL/L/k
we have the following formula,

\mathrm{disc}(KL/k)=\mathrm{Disc}(KL/L)\mathrm{disc}(L/k)^{n}.

Therefore,
\mathrm{disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL/k)=\mathrm{disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L/k)^{n}
. Hence,
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)=\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}
or
\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)=\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
depending upon whether
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified in
K
or
L
. We obtain that,

\mathrm{Disc}(KL)=\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|^{\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})-n\cdot\exp(g_{\mathfrak{p}})-|G|\cdot\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}})}.

Lemma
4.10
implies that the quantity

d_{\Pi}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|^{n\cdot\exp(g_{\mathfrak{p}})+|G|\cdot\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}})-\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})},

depends only on
\Pi
.
∎

For
\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}
, we set

(4.4)

\displaystyle N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\{(K,L)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(K,L)\in\Pi\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}(KL)<X\},

where

(4.5)

\mathcal{E}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(K,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/k)\simeq S_{n}\text{ and }\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G\}.

From Lemma
4.11
we deduce that

(4.6)

\begin{split}N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=&\#\{(K,L)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(K,L)\in\Pi\text{ and }\\
&\mathrm{Disc}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}(L)^{n}<Xd_{\Pi}\}\\
=&\#\{(K,L)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(K,L)\in\Pi\text{ and }\\
&\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}<{X}/{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|^{\exp(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})}}\}\end{split}

We also consider the same counting function with invariants
\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}
for
Y>0
. We denote these by
N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)
. Then we have the following expression for any

(4.7)

\begin{split}N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}&(S_{n}\times G;X)\\
&=\sum_{\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}}N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X).\end{split}

Suppose that
(K,L)\in\Pi
. We recall from the conditons on

that any prime

must be unramified in either

or

. Therefore, we find that for

\operatorname{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(KL)=\operatorname{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)^{|G|}\operatorname{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)^{n}.

Thus, if

, then we have that

\mathrm{Disc}(KL)=\mathrm{Disc}_{Y}(KL)

for all

.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}
be such that
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|\leq Y
. Then,

N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=N_{k,Y,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X).

Proof.

The condition that
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|\leq Y
implies that
|\mathfrak{p}|\leq Y
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
. Consequently, we get that
S\subseteq S_{Y}
, and the conclusion follows from (
4.8
).
∎

Therefore, only
\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}
with
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|>Y
have a positive contribution in the above sum (
4.6
). This motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
(K,L)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
(cf. (

. Assume that
(K,L)\in\Pi

of

\mathrm{Disc}_{res}^{\Pi}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\mathrm{Disc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K).

in the same way.

We now prove asymptotic upper bounds for the number of
S_{n}
degree

-extensions when ordered by

. We now define the relevent counting functions. Given

, we set

Set

to be the set of conjugacy classes in

. Given

Lemma 4.14
.

With respect to above notation, we have that

N_{k,\Pi,res}(S_{n};X)\ll_{k,S_{n},\epsilon}\left(\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{-r_{\sigma}+\operatorname{ind}(\sigma)+\epsilon}\right)X.

Proof.

This result is adaptation of
[
Wan21
, equation (5.13)]

(4.9)

\begin{split}N_{k,\Pi,res}(S_{n};X)&\leq\#\{K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\simeq S_{n},K\in\Pi,\text{ and }\\

&\leq\#\{K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=n,\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\simeq S_{n},K\in\Pi,\text{ and }\\
&\mathrm{Disc}(K)\leq CX\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{\mathrm{ind}(\sigma)}\},\end{split}

is an absolute constant. This inequality holds because there are only finitely many primes that can be wildly ramified in

, and there are finitely many local étale algebras over

with a fixed degree for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
in

and

can be uniformly bounded for all such primes. By recalling the definition of

and applying Theorem

to (

\mathfrak{q}=\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n}),r_{\sigma}\neq 0}\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}

and counting extensions with

N_{k,\Pi,res}(S_{n};X)\ll_{k,S_{n},\epsilon}\left(\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{-r_{\sigma}+\mathrm{ind}(\sigma)+\epsilon}\right)X.

This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Next, we use the local uniformity estimates for

Lemma 4.15

The following asymptotic bound holds

N_{k,\Pi,res}(G;X)\ll_{k,G,\epsilon}\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|^{A\epsilon}\right)X^{a(G)}(\log X)^{i(G,k)-1}.

for some constant

and

Proof.

Our result is proven as a generalization of
[

. Note that the following inequality holds,

\displaystyle N_{k,\Pi,res}(G;X)\leq\#\{L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]=|G|,\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\simeq G,\text{ and }\mathrm{Disc}(L)\leq X\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|^{\exp(g_{\mathfrak{p}})}\}.

Applying Proposition
3.6
with
\mathfrak{q}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}\mathfrak{p}^{\exp(g_{\mathfrak{p}})}
and counting extensions with
\mathrm{Disc}
upto

we deduce the following bound for the quantity on the right-hand side above and same bound holds for
N_{k,\Pi,res}(G;X)

N_{k,\Pi,res}(G;X)\ll_{k,G,\epsilon}\left(\frac{X|\mathfrak{q}|}{|\mathfrak{q}|^{1-\epsilon}}\right)^{a(G)}(\log(X|\mathfrak{q}|)^{i(G,k)-1}

In the result that follows, set

.

Lemma 4.16

Given

N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G,X)\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\epsilon}\left(\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{\delta+\epsilon}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

\displaystyle N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G;X)=

We now give the proof of our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First, we recall that Theorem
4.1
asserts that

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)\gg_{G,k}X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

Recall that for
Y>0
, the definition of
N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)
is given in (
4.1
). Proposition
4.6
asserts that

N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G,X)\sim c(k,S_{n}\times G,Y)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

We remark that
c(k,S_{n}\times G,Y)
is monotonically increasing as a function of
Y
. We note that,

(4.10)

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G,X)}{X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}}\leq\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k}(S_{n}\times G,X)}{X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}}.

Hence,
c(k,S_{n}\times G,Y)\leq\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k}(S_{n}\times G,X)}{X^{1/|G|}}
for all
Y>0
and therefore

\lim_{Y\to\infty}c(k,S_{n}\times G,Y)

exists and we denote this limit by
c(k,S_{n}\times G)
.
Next, we consider the difference,

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X).

From (
4.7
) we deduce that

(4.11)

\begin{split}N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Pi\in\Sigma_{k},\\
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|>Y\end{subarray}}N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G,X),\end{split}

(see (
4.4
) for the definition of
N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G,X)
). Let
\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}
be a
standard tuple
. Set
\mathcal{T}(\Pi)
to be the associated tuple
(\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}
, where
C(S_{n})
denotes the set of conjugacy classes of
S_{n}
and

\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S,h_{\mathfrak{p}}=\sigma}\mathfrak{p}.

We claim that for any tuple of ideals
(\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}
there are at most
O_{\epsilon}(\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})^{\epsilon}

standard tuples

\Pi\in\Sigma_{k}
such that

(4.12)

\mathcal{T}(\Pi)=(\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}.

In greater detail,
\Pi
is determined by the set of primes
S
and the tuple
\mathcal{L}
of pairs
(h_{\mathfrak{p}},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
. The equation (
4.12
) implies that there are at most
2^{\omega(\prod_{\sigma}\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})}
choices for the set
S
and at most
C(n!|G|)^{\omega(\prod_{\sigma}\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})}
choices for the tuple
\mathcal{L}
for some absolute constant
C>0
. Hence, we deduce that at most
O_{\epsilon}(\prod_{\sigma\in C(S_{n})}\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma})^{\epsilon}
choices for
\Pi
.

Combining (
4.11
) with Lemma
4.16
, we find that

\begin{split}N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Pi\in\Sigma_{k},\\
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|>Y\end{subarray}}N_{k,\Pi}(S_{n}\times G,X)\\
&\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Pi\in\Sigma_{k},\\
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}|\mathfrak{p}|>Y\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{\sigma}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{\delta+\epsilon}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}\\
&\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\epsilon}\left(\sum_{(\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}),\prod_{\sigma}|\mathfrak{q}|_{\sigma}>Y}\prod_{\sigma}|\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma}|^{\delta+\epsilon}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}\\
&\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\epsilon}\left(\sum_{|\mathfrak{q}|>Y}|\mathfrak{q}|^{\delta+\epsilon}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.\end{split}

As
\delta<-1
, we can select
\epsilon>0
to ensure convergence of the series
\sum_{|\mathfrak{q}|>Y}|\mathfrak{q}|^{\delta+\epsilon}
for all
Y>0
. Consequently, we infer that

N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)\ll_{k,S_{n},G,\epsilon}\left(\sum_{|\mathfrak{q}|>Y}|\mathfrak{q}|^{\delta+\epsilon}\right)X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}.

for all
Y>0
. Since the sum
\sum_{|\mathfrak{q}|>Y}|\mathfrak{q}|^{\delta+\epsilon}
approaches zero as
Y\to\infty
for the chosen
\epsilon>0
, we deduce that

\lim_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)-N_{k,Y}(S_{n}\times G;X)}{X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}}=0.

Using (
4.10
) we conclude that

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{k}(S_{n}\times G;X)}{X^{\frac{1}{|G|}}}=c(k,S_{n}\times G).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

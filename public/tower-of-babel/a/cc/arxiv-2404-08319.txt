Abstract
. A classical inequality by Grünbaum provides a sharp lower bound for the ratio vol ⁡ ( K − ) / vol ⁡ ( K ) \mathrm{vol}(K^{-})/\mathrm{vol}(K) , where K − K^{-} denotes the intersection of a convex body with non-empty interior K ⊂ ℝ n K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} with a halfspace bounded by a hyperplane H H passing through the centroid g ⁡ ( K ) \mathrm{g}(K) of K K . In this paper we extend this result to the case in which the hyperplane H H passes by any of the points lying in a whole uniparametric family of r r -powered centroids associated to K K (depending on a real parameter r ≥ 0 r\geq 0 ), by proving a more general functional result on concave functions. The latter result further connects (and allows one to recover) various inequalities involving the centroid, such as a classical inequality (due to Minkowski and Radon) that relates the distance of g ⁡ ( K ) \mathrm{g}(K) to a supporting hyperplane of K K , or a result for volume sections of convex bodies proven independently by Makai Jr. & Martini and Fradelizi.

1.
Introduction

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set with positive volume
\mathrm{vol}(K)
, i.e., with positive

n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure (along the paper, the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
M
, provided that
M
is measurable, is denoted by
\mathrm{vol}_{k}(M)
and we will omit the index
k
when it is equal to the dimension
n
of the ambient space; furthermore, when integrating
\mathrm{d}x
will stand for
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}(x)
).
The centroid of
K
is the affine-covariant point

\mathrm{g}(K):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\int_{K}x\,\mathrm{d}x.

According to a classical result by Grünbaum
[
9
]
, if
K
is convex (from now on a compact convex set will be referred to as a
convex body
) with centroid at the origin, then

(1.1)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n},

where
K^{-}=K\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\leq 0\}
and
K^{+}=K\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\geq 0\}

represent the parts of
K
which are split by the (vector) hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0\}
,
for any given
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

There exists a classical inequality similar in spirit to Grünbaum’s result, attributed to Minkowski for
n=2,3
and Radon for general
n
, which bounds the distance from
\mathrm{g}(K)
to a supporting hyperplane of the convex body
K
(see
[
1
, p. 57-58]
).
This result asserts that when
K
has centroid at the origin then
K\subset-nK
, a fact that is equivalent to the following statement (here
M|E
denotes the orthogonal projection of the subset
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto a vector subspace
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
):

Theorem A
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with non-empty interior and let
H
be a hyperplane. If
K
has centroid at the origin then

(1.2)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}\bigl(K^{-}|H^{\perp}\bigr)}{\mathrm{vol}_{1}\bigl(K|H^{\perp}\bigr)}\geq\frac{1}{n+1}.

Another inequality of this type, but now involving volume sections instead of projections, is the following inequality (
1.3
). It was shown (independently) by
[
11
]
, and later by
[
6
]
, who further proved this result when considering sections by planes of arbitrary dimension.

Theorem B

(
[
6
,
11
]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with non-empty interior, let
H
be a hyperplane and let
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the function given by
f(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl({K\cap(tu+H)}\bigr)
.
If
K
has centroid at the origin then

(1.3)

\frac{f(0)}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\geq\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n-1}.

Grünbaum’s result was extended to the case of sections
[
7
,
15
]
and projections
[
17
]
of compact convex sets, and generalized to the analytic setting of
log-concave
functions
[
14
]
(see also
[
4
, Lemma 2.2.6]
) and
p
-concave functions
[
15
]
, for
p>0
. Other Grünbaum type inequalities involving volumes of sections of compact convex sets through their centroid, later generalized to the case of classical and dual
quermassintegrals
in
[
16
]
, can be found in
[
6
,
11
]
.

Exploiting the original proof of Grünbaum in
[
9
]
, the following extension of Grünbaum’s inequality (
1.1
) to the case of compact sets with some concavity for the function which gives the volumes of cross-sections parallel to a given hyperplane (having Grünbaum’s inequality as the particular case
p=1/(n-1)
) was shown in
[
12
]
.

Theorem C

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set with non-empty interior and with centroid at the origin. Let
H
be a hyperplane such that the function
f:H^{\bot}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by
f(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(x+H)\bigr)
is
p
-concave, for some
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(1.4)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{p+1}{2p+1}\right)^{(p+1)/p}.

The inequality is sharp.

Following the idea of the proof of the previous result, we will first show that an analogous statement holds true when one replaces the centroid by the midpoint in a direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, namely, the point
\bigl[(a+b)/2\bigr]\cdot u
, where
[a,b]
is the support of the function
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by

f(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(tu+H)\bigr)

(see Proposition
2.1
).

In view of these results (Theorem
C
and Proposition
2.1
), here we ask about the possibility of finding other particular points that ensure a large enough amount of mass in both subsets that are obtained when cutting the given compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a hyperplane passing through them.
To figure out such a possible family of points we notice that, fixed a unit direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the corresponding components w.r.t.
u
of both the centroid and the midpoint have a similar nature. Indeed, the component of
\mathrm{g}(K)
w.r.t.
u
is given by (see (
2.2
))

[\mathrm{g}(K)]_{1}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\int_{a}^{b}tf(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}tf^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}f^{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t},

whereas the corresponding component of the midpoint is

\frac{a+b}{2}=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}tf^{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}f^{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}.

Thus, with the above-mentioned aim in mind, it seems reasonable to consider the points
\mathrm{g}_{r}\cdot u
, where

(1.5)

\mathrm{g}_{r}:=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}tf^{r}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}f^{r}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}

for any
r\geq 0
. Here we show that such a uniparametric class of points allows us to extend Grünbaum’s inequality (or more generally Theorem
C
) to the case in which one replaces the classical centroid by any of them.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r\in[0,\infty)
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set with non-empty interior having the point
\mathrm{g}_{r}\cdot u
, with respect to some direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, at the origin. Let
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0\}
be the hyperplane with normal vector
u
and assume that the function
f:H^{\bot}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by
f(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(x+H)\bigr)
is
p
-concave, for some
p\in(0,\infty)
. If
r\geq 1
then

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{p+1}{2p+r}\right)^{(p+1)/p},

whereas if
0\leq r\leq 1
then

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{p+r}{2p+r}\right)^{(p+1)/p}.

Notice that the cases
r=1
and
r=0
correspond to Theorem
C
and Proposition
2.1
, respectively.

Taking into account that, once a unit direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is fixed, the above geometric results are reduced to the study of one variable functions with certain concavity, here we deal with the corresponding functional counterpart
of these statements (from which the latter result will be obtained as a consequence of such an equivalent functional one). To this aim, first we need to define the notion of functional
\alpha
-centroid: given a non-negative function
h:[a,b]\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
with positive integral, for any
\alpha>0
we will write

(1.6)

\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h):=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}th^{\alpha}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}h^{\alpha}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}.

Now, the statement of our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
h:[a,b]\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
be a non-negative concave function, and let
\alpha,\beta>0
.
If
\beta\leq\alpha
then

(1.7)

\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{b}h^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}h^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\beta+1},

whereas if
\alpha\leq\beta
then

(1.8)

\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{b}h^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}h^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\beta+1}.

Remark 1.1
.

We observe that Theorem
1.1
is directly obtained from the previous result by just taking

h=f^{p}
,
\beta=1/p
and
\alpha=r\beta
(where the case
r=0
is derived when doing
\alpha\to 0^{+}
).
Moreover, Theorem
1.2
can be shown from Theorem
1.1
by just considering the set of revolution
K
associated to the radius function
r=(1/\kappa_{n-1})f^{1/(n-1)}
, where
\kappa_{n-1}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume of the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
f=h^{\beta}
,
p=1/\beta
and
r=\alpha/\beta
.
Therefore, in fact, both results (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) are equivalent.

We would also like to point out that, apart from the already mentioned Theorem
1.1
, and thus in particular Theorem
C
and Grünbaum’s inequality, both Theorems
A
and
B
can be derived as direct applications of Theorem
1.2
.

Indeed, on the one hand, applying Theorem
1.2
with
h=f^{1/(n-1)}
, which is concave because of
Brunn’s concavity principle
(see e.g.
[
4
, Section 1.2.1]
and also
[
13
, Theorem 12.2.1]
), and taking
\alpha=n-1
and
\beta\to 0^{+}
, one gets

b/(b-a)\geq 1/(n+1)
, which is exactly (
1.2
).

On the other hand, applying Theorem
1.2
with
h=f^{1/(n-1)}
and
\alpha=n-1
, and then raising both sides of (
1.8
) to the power
1/\beta
and taking
\beta\to\infty
, one has

(1.9)

\left(\frac{\max_{t\in[0,b]}f(t)}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\right)^{1/(n-1)}\geq\frac{n}{n+1}.

Since we may assume without loss of generality that
\|f\|_{\infty}>\max_{t\in[0,b]}f(t)
(considering otherwise the function
t\mapsto f(-t)
), we then get
\max_{t\in[0,b]}f(t)=f(0)
(since
f
is
(1/(n-1))
-concave and thus quasi-concave), and therefore (
1.9
) is nothing but (
1.3
).

2.
Preliminaries and first results

We recall that a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is

p
-concave, for
p\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, if

\varphi\bigl((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\bigr)\geq\bigl((1-\lambda)\varphi(x)^{p}+\lambda\varphi(y)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\varphi(x)\varphi(y)>0
and any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, where the cases
p=0
,
p=\infty
and
p=-\infty
must be understood as the corresponding expressions that are obtained by continuity, namely, the geometric mean, the maximum and the minimum (of
\varphi(x)
and
\varphi(y)
), respectively.
Note that if
p>0
, then
\varphi
is
p
-concave if and only if
\varphi^{p}
is concave on its support

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(x)>0\}
and thus, in particular,
1
-concave is just concave (on its support) in the usual sense.
A
0
-concave function is usually called

log-concave
whereas a
(-\infty)
-concave function is referred to as
quasi-concave
.
Moreover, Jensen’s inequality for means (see e.g.
[
10
, Section 2.9]
and
[
5
, Theorem 1 p. 203]
) implies that a
q
-concave function is also
p
-concave, whenever
q>p
.

For the sake of simplicity, in the following we consider

H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0\}
, for a given direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
that
we extend to an orthonormal basis
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n})
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
u_{1}=u
.
Given a set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and a real number
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we will write
M^{-}(u,c)=M\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\leq c\}
and

M^{+}(u,c)=M\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\geq c\}
. When
dealing with the above-mentioned prescribed hyperplane
H
we will just write
H^{-}
and
H^{+}
for the corresponding halfspaces determined by
H
.
Moreover, for a compact set with non-empty interior
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
K(t)=K\cap(tu+H)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We notice that, if
K|H^{\bot}\subset[au,bu]
, Fubini’s theorem
applied to the function
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by

f(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(tu+H)\bigr)

yields (provided that
a\leq 0
)

(2.1)

\mathrm{vol}(K)=\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})=\int_{a}^{0}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where, as usual, we are identifying the linear subspace spanned by
u
with
\mathbb{R}
.
Since the set
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:f(t)>0\}
is convex whenever
f
is quasi-concave, from now on we will assume, without loss of generality, that
f(t)>0
for all
t\in(a,b)
. Furthermore, by Fubini’s theorem, we get

(2.2)

[\mathrm{g}(K)]_{1}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\int_{a}^{b}tf(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

and thus, in particular,
a<[\mathrm{g}(K)]_{1}<b
(cf. (
2.1
)).

It seems natural to wonder about the possibility of getting an analogue of Theorem
C
by considering the midpoint in the direction
u
of
K
instead its centroid, that is, the point
\bigl[(a+b)/2\bigr]\cdot u
.
This is the content of the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set with non-empty interior and with midpoint, with respect to some direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, at the origin. Let
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0\}
be the hyperplane with normal vector
u
and assume that the function
f:H^{\bot}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by
f(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(x+H)\bigr)
is
p
-concave, for some
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{(p+1)/p}.

Proof.

In the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
it is shown that
there exists a
p
-affine function
g_{p}:[-\gamma,\delta]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by

g_{p}(t)=c(t+\gamma)^{1/p}
, for some
\gamma,\delta,c>0
,
such that
g_{p}(0)=f(0)
,

\int_{-\gamma}^{0}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{a}^{0}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{\delta}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{b}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

and further that
-\gamma\leq a<0<\delta\leq b
.

Here, since
K
has its midpoint (w.r.t.
u
) at the origin, we have that
a=-b
and then we get
-\gamma+\delta\leq 0
. Thus

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}=\frac{\int_{-\gamma}^{0}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{-\gamma}^{\delta}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\frac{\int_{-\gamma}^{(-\gamma+\delta)/2}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{-\gamma}^{\delta}g_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{(p+1)/p},

as desired.
∎

We collect here the following result, originally proved in

[
2
]
and
[
3
]
(see also
[
8
]
for a detailed presentation), which can be regarded as the
functional counterpart of the
Brunn-Minkowski inequality
.

Theorem D

(The Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality)
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
-1/n\leq p\leq\infty
and let
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be measurable
functions with positive integrals such that

h((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq\bigl((1-\lambda)f(x)^{p}+\lambda g(y)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
f(x)g(y)>0
. Then

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\left[(1-\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}+\lambda\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q}\right]^{1/q},

where
q=p/(np+1)
.

Now, considering the points
\mathrm{g}_{r}\cdot u
, for any
r>0
, where
\mathrm{g}_{r}
is given by (
1.5
),
and following the same idea as in
[
6
, Theorem 3]
, we can get a first result concerning this family of points. The statement reads as follows.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
r\in(0,\infty)
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set with non-empty interior having the point
\mathrm{g}_{r}\cdot u
, with respect to some direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, at the origin. Let
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0\}
be the hyperplane with normal vector
u
and assume that the function
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by
f(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(tu+H)\bigr)
is
p
-concave, for some
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(2.4)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\geq\left(\frac{p}{2p+r}\right)^{(p+1)/p}.

We will derive Corollary
2.1
as a simple application of the following (slightly more general) functional result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Let
g:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a concave function and let
f:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a
p
-concave function, with
p>0
. Then

g\left(\frac{\int_{K}xf(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)\geq\left(\frac{p}{(n+1)p+1}\right)\|g\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be the probability measure whose density function is given by

\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\frac{f(x)}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}\mathrm{d}x.

Since
g
is concave, using Jensen’s inequality we get that

\begin{split}g\left(\frac{\int_{K}xf(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)=g\left(\int_{K}x\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)&\geq\int_{K}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
&=\int_{0}^{\|g\|_{\infty}}\mu\bigl(\{x\in K:g(x)\geq t\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where in the last identity we have used Fubini’s theorem. Now, since the density of
\mu
, with respect to the Lebesgue measure, is
p
-concave, from the Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
2.3
) we have that
the function
\varphi(t):=\mu\bigl(\{x\in K:g(x)\geq t\}\bigr)
is
\bigl(p/(np+1)\bigr)
-concave.
Indeed, it is enough to apply Theorem
D
with the functions

\frac{f\cdot\chi_{{}_{\{x\in K\,:\,g(x)\geq t_{1}\}}}}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x},\quad\frac{f\cdot\chi_{{}_{\{x\in K\,:\,g(x)\geq t_{2}\}}}}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{f\cdot\chi_{{}_{\{x\in K\,:\,g(x)\geq(1-\lambda)t_{1}+\lambda t_{2}\}}}}{\int_{K}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}

for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\chi_{{}_{M}}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
M
, one easily obtains the desired concavity of
\varphi
.
Hence, and taking into account that
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi\bigl(\|g\|_{\infty}\bigr)\geq 0
, we may assure that
\varphi(t)^{p/(np+1)}\geq(1-t/\|g\|_{\infty})
for all
t\in[0,\|g\|_{\infty}]
. So, by integrating we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\|g\|_{\infty}}\mu\bigl(\{x\in K:g(x)\geq t\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t&\geq\int_{0}^{\|g\|_{\infty}}(1-t/\|g\|_{\infty})^{(np+1)/p}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\left(\frac{p}{(n+1)p+1}\right)\|g\|_{\infty},\end{split}

from where the result immediately follows.
∎

We conclude this section by showing Corollary
2.1
.

Proof of Corollary
2.1
.

Denoting by
[a,b]
the support of
f
, let
\bar{f},g:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the functions given by

g(t)=\mathrm{vol}\bigl(K\cap(tu+H^{-})\bigr)^{p/(p+1)}=\left(\int_{a}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/(p+1)}

and

\bar{f}(t)=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\bigl(K\cap(tu+H)\bigr)^{r}=f(t)^{r}.

By hypothesis, it is clear that
\bar{f}
is
(p/r)
-concave, whereas from the Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
2.3
) we have that
g
is concave on
[a,b]
. So, from Proposition
2.2
applied to the functions
\bar{f}
and
g
, and taking into account that
\mathrm{g}_{r}=0
, we get that

\begin{split}\mathrm{vol}(K^{-})=g\left(\frac{\int_{a}^{b}t\bar{f}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}\bar{f}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{(p+1)/p}&\geq\left(\frac{p}{2p+r}\right)^{(p+1)/p}\|g\|_{\infty}^{(p+1)/p}\\
&=\left(\frac{p}{2p+r}\right)^{(p+1)/p}\mathrm{vol}(K),\end{split}

as desired.
∎

In the following section, the tighter inequalities collected in Theorem

, which improve the one that was obtained in Corollary

More precisely, we will enhance the constant given by (

We devote this section to proving the first part of Theorem

We will first prove that there exists a non-negative and concave function
\varphi:[\gamma,\delta]\longrightarrow[0,\|h\|_{\infty}]
such that

(3.5)

\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)\leq\frac{\int_{L_{g}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g}}\varphi(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}.

To this aim, we consider the function

given by

W(s)=\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr),

which is clearly both strictly decreasing and surjective.
We may then define the non-negative function

that satisfies

W(s)=\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq w(s)\}\bigr)

for any
s\in[0,\|h\|_{\infty}]
.
Indeed, since

W(s)=\frac{c^{\beta}(\delta-w(s))^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)},

we get that

w(s)=\delta-\left(\frac{\beta(\beta+1)}{c^{\beta}}W(s)\right)^{1/(\beta+1)}.

Notice that, from the Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
2.3
), we have that
the function

is concave (since the density of
\mu_{\beta}
, with respect to the Lebesgue measure, is
\bigl(1/(\beta-1)\bigr)
-concave).

is strictly increasing, surjective and convex, and then there exists the function
\varphi=w^{-1}:[\gamma,\delta]\longrightarrow[0,\|h\|_{\infty}]
, which is further (strictly increasing and) concave.

Now, we will start by bounding from above the right-hand side of (

).

) and (
3.4
), we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}&\int_{L}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\

\leq&\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{\|h\|_{\infty}}s_{2}^{\alpha-\beta-1}\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{g}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{2},s_{2})\bigr)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\

) we get

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}&\int_{L}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\

Now we will prove that for any concave function

we have that

(3.9)

\frac{\int_{L_{g}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g}}\varphi(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}\leq\frac{\int_{L_{g}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g}}\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}.

\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}\left(\bigl(C_{1}(t-\gamma)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}-\varphi(t)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

we may assert, taking into account that

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(i)}\;C_{1}(t-\gamma)\leq\varphi(t)\text{ for every }\gamma\leq t\leq t_{0},\quad\text{and}\\[17.07164pt]

), yields (

).

). On the one hand,

\begin{split}\int_{L_{g}}\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)=&\frac{c^{\beta}}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}(t-\gamma)^{\alpha-\beta}(\delta-t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t\\

=&\frac{c^{\beta}}{\beta}(\delta-\gamma)^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha-\beta+1\right)\Gamma\left(\beta+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+2\right)},\end{split}

whereas, on the other hand, we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\quad\int_{L_{g}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)=\frac{c^{\beta}}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}t(t-\gamma)^{\alpha-\beta}(\delta-t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t\\

\frac{\int_{L_{g}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g}}\left(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle-\gamma\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}=\gamma+(\delta-\gamma)\frac{\alpha-\beta+1}{\alpha+2},

and therefore, this together with (

) and (

)

\begin{split}\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{b}h(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{a}^{b}h(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}&=\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\frac{\int_{g_{0}}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}=\left(\frac{\delta-g_{0}}{\delta-\gamma}\right)^{\beta+1}\\
&=\left(1-\frac{\alpha-\beta+1}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\beta+1}=\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\beta+1},\end{split}

as desired. This finishes the proof of (

).

, we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\frac{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}&<\frac{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}\\[5.69054pt]
&+\frac{b\delta\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x}.\end{split}

To this aim, we will observe that, denoting by
b_{\varepsilon}=\max\bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}:(x,y)\in L_{\varepsilon}\bigr\}
,
for any
0\leq s\leq b_{\varepsilon}
we have that

(3.14)

\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{\varepsilon}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)\leq\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g,\varepsilon}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)+W_{1}(\varepsilon).

Indeed, taking into account that if
s\leq w_{1}(\varepsilon)
then

\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)=\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g,\varepsilon}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)+W_{1}(\varepsilon),

and if
s>w_{1}(\varepsilon)
then

\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)\leq W_{1}(\varepsilon),

we get, from (
3.4
), that

\begin{split}\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{\varepsilon}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)&\leq\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)\\
&\leq\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)\\
&\leq\mu_{\beta}\bigl(\{x\in L_{g,\varepsilon}\,:\,\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq s\}\bigr)+W_{1}(\varepsilon)\end{split}

for all
0\leq s\leq b_{\varepsilon}
, which shows (
3.14
).
This, together with

W_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}y^{\beta-1}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{b}{\beta}\varepsilon^{\beta}

and (ii) in (
3.12
),
implies that

\begin{split}\min&\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\mathrm{e}_{2},1/s_{2})\bigr)\right\}\\
&\leq\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr)+W_{1}(\varepsilon),\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\right\}\\
&\leq W_{1}(\varepsilon)+\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\right\}\\
&\leq\frac{b}{\beta}\varepsilon^{\beta}+\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\right\}\end{split}

for all
0\leq s_{1}\leq b_{\varepsilon}
and all
0<1/s_{2}\leq\|h\|_{\infty}
, where again the notations
L_{\varepsilon}^{+}(u,c)
and
L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(u,c)
represent the sets

\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}\bigr)^{+}(u,c)
and
\bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{+}(u,c)
, respectively, for a given direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and a real number
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

So, defining
w_{1}(s):=\gamma
if
s\geq\|h\|_{\infty}
, and taking into account that
b_{\varepsilon}\leq b\leq\delta
(by (iii) in (
3.3
)), from the fact that
\alpha<\beta
and using Fubini’s theorem we have on the one hand that

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\!\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\
&\quad\,=\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\int_{0}^{1/\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\
&\quad\,=\int_{0}^{b_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\cap L_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\mathrm{e}_{2},1/s_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\
&\quad\,<\int_{0}^{b_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\mathrm{e}_{2},1/s_{2})\bigr)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\
&\quad\,\leq\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\frac{b}{\beta}\varepsilon^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\
&\quad\,+\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\min\left\{\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\bigr),\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\
&\quad\,=\frac{b\delta}{\beta-\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s_{2}^{\beta-\alpha-1}\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}(\mathrm{e}_{1},s_{1})\cap L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\\
&\quad\,=\frac{b\delta}{\beta-\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x),\end{split}

where in the last equality above we have also used that
\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq w_{1}(1/s_{2})
if and only if
1/\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)

\geq s_{2}
(since
\varphi_{1}
is decreasing).

On the other hand, since
\alpha<\beta
, from Fubini’s theorem (jointly with (ii) in (
3.12
))
we have that

(3.16)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)&=\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{1/\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle}s^{\beta-\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s^{\beta-\alpha-1}\mu_{\beta}\bigl(L_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\mathrm{e}_{2},1/s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}s^{\beta-\alpha-1}\mu_{\beta}\Bigl(L_{g,\varepsilon}^{+}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{1},w_{1}(1/s)\bigr)\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x),\end{split}

where in the last equality above we have used again that
\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\geq w_{1}(1/s)
if and only if
1/\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)

\geq s
.
Hence, from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we obtain (
3.13
), as desired.

Now we will prove that for any concave function
\varphi_{1}:[\gamma,\delta]\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
we have that

(3.17)

\frac{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}\leq\frac{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}.

To this aim, let
C_{1}>0
be such that

(3.18)

\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)=\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x).

Since the latter identity is equivalent (by Fubini’s theorem) to

\int_{\gamma}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}\left(\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}-\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

we may assert, taking into account that
\varphi_{1}
is concave, that
there exists
t_{0}(\varepsilon)\in(\gamma,\delta)
such that

(3.19)

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(i)}\;C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\geq\varphi_{1}(t)\text{ for every }\gamma\leq t\leq t_{0}(\varepsilon),\quad\text{and}\\[17.07164pt]
\text{(ii)}\;C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\leq\varphi_{1}(t)\text{ for every }t_{0}(\varepsilon)\leq t\leq w_{1}(\varepsilon).\end{array}

Then, from (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) (taking into account that
\alpha<\beta
), and using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle&\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)-\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\\
=&\,\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}t\Bigl(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\Bigr)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\,\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}t\Bigl(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\Bigr)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+&\,\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{t_{0}}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}t\Bigl(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\Bigr)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq&\,\frac{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}\left(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+&\,\frac{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}{\beta}\int_{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}\left(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\,\frac{t_{0}(\varepsilon)}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}\left(\varphi_{1}(t)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-t)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)g^{\beta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\,t_{0}(\varepsilon)\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\left(\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}-\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)=0.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\varphi_{1}(\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)\leq\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\bigl(C_{1}(\varepsilon)(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle)\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x),

which, together with (
3.18
), yields (
3.17
).

Hence, from (
3.13
) and (
3.17
), for every
0<\varepsilon\leq\|h\|_{\infty}
we get

\begin{split}\frac{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}&<\frac{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}\\[5.69054pt]
&+\frac{b\delta\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x}.\end{split}

Now, taking limits as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in the above inequality, we have that the left-hand side, namely,

\frac{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}=\frac{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}x},

tends to

\frac{\int_{L}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{L}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}x}=\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
(see (
3.1
)).
Furthermore, the first term in the right-hand side,

\frac{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}{\int_{L_{g,\varepsilon}}\left(\delta-\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{1}\rangle\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\beta}(x)}=\frac{\frac{c^{\beta}}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}t(\delta-t)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\frac{c^{\beta}}{\beta}\int_{\gamma}^{w_{1}(\varepsilon)}(\delta-t)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t},

tends to

\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(g)=\frac{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}t(\delta-t)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}(\delta-t)^{\alpha}dt}=\left(\delta-\frac{(\alpha+1)(\delta-\gamma)}{\alpha+2}\right)

for
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, whereas
the second term in the right-hand side,

\frac{b\delta\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{\int_{L_{\varepsilon}}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x},

clearly tends to

\frac{0}{\int_{L}\langle x,\mathrm{e}_{2}\rangle^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x}=0

for
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Therefore, we have that

\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)\leq\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(g).

Finally, the latter relation jointly with (ii) and (iii) in (
3.2
) gives us

\begin{split}\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{b}h(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{b}h(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}&=\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(h)}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}\geq\frac{\int_{\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(g)}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{\gamma}^{\delta}g(t)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t}=\left(\frac{\delta-\mathrm{g}_{\alpha}(g)}{\delta-\gamma}\right)^{\beta+1}\\
&=\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}\right)^{\beta+1},\end{split}

as desired. This finishes the proof of (
1.8
), and hence also that of Theorem
1.2
.

Mildly Flavoring domain walls in SU(N) SQCD: baryons and monopole superpotentials
Sergio Benvenuti, Paolo Spezzati

Abstract
We study supersymmetric domain walls of four dimensional S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) SQCD with N N and N + 1 N+1 flavors. In 4 ​ d 4d we analyze the BPS differential equations numerically. In 3 ​ d 3d we propose the 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 Chern-Simons-Matter gauge theories living on the walls. Compared with the previously studied regime of F < N F<N flavors, we encounter a couple of novelties: with N N flavors, there are solutions/vacua breaking the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) baryonic symmetry; with N + 1 N+1 flavors, our 3 ​ d 3d proposal includes a linear monopole operator in the superpotential.

BPS domain walls of
SU(N)
with
N
flavors

In this section we consider
\mathcal{N}=1
SQCD with gauge group
SU(N)
and
N
flavors of quarks
Q_{I}
,
\widetilde{Q}^{I}
in the (anti)-fundamental representation. The UV massless model has zero superpotential.

Let us review the well known IR behavior of the model
Taylor:1982bp
;
Seiberg:1994bz
;
Seiberg:1994pq
. The non-anomalous continuous global symmetry group is
SU(F)\times SU(F)\times U(1)_{B}\times U(1)_{R}
. The gauge-invariant operators which describe the moduli space of the massless theory (at the classical level) are the mesons
M_{I}^{J}=Q_{I}^{\alpha}\tilde{Q}^{J}_{\alpha}
, the baryon
B=\epsilon^{I_{1}\dots I_{N}}\epsilon_{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{N}}Q^{\alpha_{1}}_{I_{1}}\cdots Q^{\alpha_{N}}_{I_{N}}
and anti-baryon
\tilde{B}=\epsilon_{I_{1}\dots I_{N}}\epsilon^{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{N}}\tilde{Q}_{\alpha_{1}}^{I_{1}}\cdots\tilde{Q}_{\alpha_{N}}^{I_{N}}
.
The massless theory has classical moduli space which is singular at the origin when
M_{I}^{J}=B=\widetilde{B}=0
. However at the quantum mechanical level there is a constraint

\det M-B\tilde{B}=\Lambda^{2N}.

(1)

in terms of the dynamically generated scale
\Lambda
. This constraint smooths out the singularity at the origin, yielding a smooth moduli space. This smooth manifold will be called
\mathcal{M}_{N,N}
.
4
4

4

In the special case of
SU(2)
, for which the fundamental representation coincides with the anti-fundamental, this is precisely the constraint
\Pf M_{Sp(1)}=\Lambda^{2N}
which appears in the
Sp(1)
model with
2
flavors. This can be seen reorganizing the baryons and the mesons into the matrix

M_{Sp(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&B&M_{11}&M_{12}\\
-B&0&M_{21}&M_{22}\\
-M_{11}&-M_{21}&0&\tilde{B}\\
-M_{12}&-M_{22}&-\tilde{B}&0\end{pmatrix}

(2)

that maps the
Sp(1)
mesons to the
SU(2)
mesons and baryons.

We then turn on a diagonal mass term for the quarks,

W_{m}=m_{4d}\Tr M.

(3)

This breaks the flavor symmetry
SU(F)\times SU(F)\rightarrow SU(F)
and it leaves only a discrete R-symmetry unbroken, i.e.
\mathbb{Z}_{2N}
. We can implement the quantum constraint on the would-be moduli space using a Lagrange multiplier
A
. The low-energy physics is described by the effective superpotential

W=m_{4d}\Tr M+A(\det M-B\tilde{B}-\Lambda^{2N}),

(4)

The solutions of the F-term equations are
N
gapped vacua with R-symmetry breaking
\mathbb{Z}_{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
:

M=\tilde{M}\mathbb{I}_{N},\quad\tilde{M}^{N}=\Lambda^{2N},\quad B=\tilde{B}=0,\quad A=\frac{m_{4d}\tilde{M}}{\Lambda^{2N}}.

(5)

We can see that the moduli space is lifted when
m_{4d}\neq 0
.
When the quark mass is large
\absolutevalue{m_{4d}}\gg\Lambda
, one can integrate out the quarks and remain with pure SYM, which has, in turn,
N
vacua. On the other hand, if
\absolutevalue{m_{4d}}\ll\Lambda
the effective description as a WZ model on the moduli space is reliable.

In the following we will not use the formulation with the Lagrange multiplier field
A
, we will instead study the NLSM with target space
\mathcal{M}_{N,N}
, embedded in the flat
(N^{2}+2)
-dimensional complex space parameterized by
M,B,\tilde{B}
. As in the
Sp(N)

F=N+1
case
Benvenuti:2021yqv
, we do not really need complete information about the Kähler potential, except for the fact that it is smooth, it is natural to use the canonical, quadratic, Kähler potential for the ambient space

\mathcal{K}=M_{I}^{J}(M^{\ast})_{J}^{I}+BB^{\ast}+\tilde{B}\tilde{B}^{\ast}\,.

(6)

2.1
Analysis of the BPS equations

In this paper we study domain walls of
\mathcal{N}=1

4d
massive SQCD that preserve half of the supersymmetry, these are called BPS domain walls
Dvali:1996xe
;
Abraham:1990nz
;
Cecotti:1992rm
. These walls interpolate between two vacuum configuration at the two ends of the Universe, at
x=\pm\infty
. When the low energy physics of the model is described by a Wess-Zumino model with superpotential
W
, the tension of the walls is fixed by supersymmetry and it is given by

T=2\absolutevalue{W(v_i)-W(v_j)},

(7)

the difference of the superpotential being evaluated at the two vacua
v_{i}
,
v_{j}
at the two ends of the Universe. Moreover, if we are considering a Wess-Zumino model there are first order differential equations to study the trajectory of such domain walls
Fendley:1990zj
;
Abraham:1990nz
:

\partial_{x}\Phi^{a}=e^{i\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{a\bar{b}}\overline{\partial_{b}W},

(8)

where
\Phi^{a}
are the chirals of the WZ model,
\mathcal{K}^{a\bar{b}}
is the inverse Kähler metric and
e^{i\gamma}=\frac{\Delta W}{\absolutevalue{\Delta W}}
. The trajectory of the domain wall in the W-space, that is the image of
W(\Phi^{a})
along the domain wall solution, is a straight line

\partial_{x}W=e^{i\gamma}\absolutevalue{\partial W}^{2}.

(9)

Let us point out that the very existence of the domain walls does not depend on the D-terms
Cecotti:1992rm
. In other words, it is insensitive to the choice of the Kähler metric. This will allow us, in the following, in order to find domain wall solutions, to choose a Kähler metric as we like, provided that the Kähler metric does not have singularities along the domain wall solution.

Let us apply this general formalism to our case of interest, the Wess-Zumino model described at the beginning of this Section. In order to solve the BPS equations (
8
) for a constrained system we need to choose a set of coordinates for the target manifold
\mathcal{M}_{N,N}
of the NLSM and rewrite the equations (
8
) in term of these coordinates.
5
5

5

When choosing the set of coordinates one has to make sure that the domain wall solutions and the vacua of the model can be described in those coordinates. For example if we solve (
1
) like
B=\frac{1}{\tilde{B}}(\text{det}(M)\Lambda^{2N})
we could not see the vacua because, on the vacua (which have
\tilde{B}=B=0
), this expression is not valid.

At this point we use the
SU(F)\times U(1)
flavor symmetry. The mesonic
SU(F)
symmetry allows to diagonalize the meson matrix
M=\text{diag}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})
. Since the equations (
8
) transform covariantly under the flavor symmetry, if at some point along the domain wall solution we set the off-diagonal components of
M
to zero, they remain zero along all the solution. Similarly, using the baryonic
U(1)
flavor symmetry we set
\tilde{B}=B
. In this way we reduce the number of independent functions from
N^{2}+2
to
N+1
.

We thus study a reduced system with variables
\xi_{i}
and
B
(we express
\xi_{i}
in units of
\Lambda^{2}
,
B
in units of
\Lambda^{N}
), satisfying the constraint

\prod_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}=B^{2}+1\,,

(10)

providing the embedding into a
N+1
dimensional target space with flat metric, i.e. with Kähler potential

\mathcal{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\xi_{i}|^{2}+2|B|^{2}\,.

(11)

The superpotential on the reduced target space is just given by the mass term

\mathcal{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}

(12)

The next step to take is to solve the constraint (
10
). We write

\xi_{1}=\frac{1+B^{2}}{\prod_{i\neq 1}\xi_{i}}.

(13)

This particular choice of solving the constraint allow us to have well defined coordinates on the vacua of the theory, where the baryons vanish.
Then, projecting the canonical Kähler potential and flat metric of the ambient space
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
on the manifold determined by the constraint, we get the induced Kähler potential

\mathcal{K}=\sum_{i=2}^{N}\absolutevalue{\xi_i}^{2}+\absolutevalue{B }^{2}+\frac{\absolutevalue{1+B^2}^{2}}{\prod_{i\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_i}^{2}},

(14)

whereas the metric reads

\mathcal{K}_{a\bar{b}}=\sum_{i=2}^{N}\absolutevalue{d\xi_i}^{2}\biggl(1+\frac{\absolutevalue{1+B^2}^{2}}{\prod_{j\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_j}^{2}\absolutevalue{\xi_i}^{2}}\biggr)+\sum_{i\neq j}d\xi_{i}^{\ast}d\xi_{j}\frac{\absolutevalue{1+B^2}^{2}}{\prod_{h\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_h}^{2}\xi_{j}\xi_{i}^{\ast}}+\\
+2\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{B^{\ast}(1+B^{2})dB^{\ast}d\xi_{i}}{\prod_{h\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_h}^{2}\xi_{i}}+2\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{B(1+B^{\ast 2})dBd\xi_{i}^{\ast}}{\prod_{h\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_h}^{2}\xi^{\ast}_{i}}+\absolutevalue{dB}^{2}\biggl(1+\frac{4\absolutevalue{B}^{2}}{\prod_{h\neq 1}\absolutevalue{\xi_h}^{2}}\biggr).

(15)

The final expression for the differential equations (
8
) is involved and we will not write it down here, however, all the elements to write an efficient code to solve the differential equation have been given. What we have just described is the general procedure to solve the differential (
8
) in case of a WZ model with non-trivial target manifold for the chiral fields. We now turn to describing the solutions we have found.

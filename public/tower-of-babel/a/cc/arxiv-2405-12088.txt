Product representation of perfect cubes
Zsigmond György Fleiner, Márk Hunor Juhász, Blanka Kövér, Péter Pál Pach, Csaba Sándor

Abstract
. Let F k , d ​ ( n ) F_{k,d}(n) be the maximal size of a set A ⊆ [ n ] {A}\subseteq[n] such that the equation a 1 ​ a 2 ​ … ​ a k = x d , a 1 < a 2 < … < a k a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}=x^{d},\;a_{1}<a_{2}<\ldots<a_{k} has no solution with a 1 , a 2 , … , a k ∈ A a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\in{A} and integer x x . Erdős, Sárközy and T. Sós studied F k , 2 F_{k,2} , and gave bounds when k = 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 k=2,3,4,6 and also in the general case. We study the problem for d = 3 d=3 , and provide bounds for k = 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 k=2,3,4,6 and 9 9 , furthermore, in the general case, as well. In particular, we refute an 18 years old conjecture of Verstraëte. We also introduce another function f k , d f_{k,d} closely related to F k , d F_{k,d} : While the original problem requires a 1 , … , a k a_{1},\ldots,a_{k} to all be distinct, we can relax this and only require that the multiset of the a i a_{i} ’s cannot be partitioned into d d -tuples where each d d -tuple consists of d d copies of the same number.



1.
Introduction

The problem of the solvability of equations of the form

a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}=x^{2},\;a_{1}<a_{2}<\ldots<a_{k}

in a set
A\subseteq[n]
first appeared in a 1995 paper of Erdős, Sárközy and T. Sós
[
10
]
.
They investigated the maximal size of a set
A
such that the equation cannot be solved in
A
, that is, there are no distinct
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A
whose product is a perfect square. This motivates the following definitions:

Let
F_{k,d}(n)
be the maximal size of a set
A\subseteq[n]
such that

(1)

a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}=x^{d},\;a_{1}<a_{2}<\ldots<a_{k}

has no solution with
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\in{A}
and integer
x
. If
k\geq 2
and
A
is a set of positive integers such that equation (
1
) cannot be solved in
A
, then
A
is said to have property
P_{k,d}
.
\Gamma_{k,d}
denotes the family of sets of positive integers which have property
P_{k,d}
. Similarly, let
f_{k,d}(n)
be the maximal size of a set
A\subseteq[n]
such that

(2)

a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}=x^{d}

has no solution with
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\in{A}
and integer
x
, except
trivial
solutions that we specify below. If we allow some of the
a_{i}
’s in equation (
2
) to coincide, some trivial solutions do arise: It is clear, for instance, that
a_{1}=\ldots=a_{d}
will yield a solution to the equation
a_{1}\ldots a_{d}=x^{d}
. Let us call a solution trivial if the multiset of the
a_{i}
’s can be partitioned into
d
-tuples where each
d
-tuple consists of
d
copies of the same number: see for example
(a_{1}a_{1}a_{1})(a_{2}a_{2}a_{2})(a_{3}a_{3}a_{3})=x^{3}
for
k=9
,
d=3
. Note that trivial solutions arise only if
d\mid k
. Let
\gamma_{k,d}
denote the family of sets
A
of positive integers which have the property that equation (
2
) cannot be solved in
A
with the exception of trivial solutions of this kind. Note that
f_{k,d}\leq F_{k,d}
.

With our notation, Erdős, Sárközy and T. Sós
[
10
]
proved the following results:

Theorem 1

(Erdős, Sárközy, T. Sós)
.

For every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(1)

F_{2,2}(n)=\left(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}+o(1)\right)n
;

(2)

\frac{n^{3/4}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}\ll F_{4,2}(n)-\pi(n)\ll\frac{n^{3/4}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}
;

(3)

\frac{n^{2/3}}{(\log n)^{4/3}}\ll F_{6,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)\ll n^{7/9}\log n
;

(4)

\frac{n^{\frac{2\ell}{4\ell-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{4\ell}{4\ell-1}}}\ll F_{4\ell,2}(n)-\pi(n)\ll\frac{n^{3/4}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}
;

(5)

\frac{n^{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\ell+1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{4\ell+2}{4\ell+1}}}\ll F_{4\ell+2,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)\ll n^{7/9}\log n
.

Later Győri
[
11

and the fourth named author

improved the upper bound for

F_{6,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)\ll n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2^{1/3}-1/3+o(1)}.

The fourth named author

proved the lower bound

\frac{n^{3/5}}{(\log n)^{6/5}}\ll F_{8,2}(n)-\pi(n).

For general cases, the current best lower bound estimates have been proved recently by the fourth named author and Vizer

Theorem 2

(Pach, Vizer)
.

For every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

(1)

\frac{n^{3/5}}{(\log n)^{6/5}}\ll F_{10,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)
;

(2)

\frac{n^{6/11}}{(\log n)^{12/11}}\ll F_{22,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)

(4)

\frac{n^{\frac{3\ell}{6\ell-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{6\ell}{6\ell-1}}}\ll F_{8\ell+2,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)

(5)

\frac{n^{\frac{6\ell-1}{12\ell-4}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{6\ell-1}{6\ell-2}}}\ll F_{8\ell+6,2}(n)-\left(\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)
.

is closely related to (generalized) multiplicative Sidon sets. However, the case

seems to be much more difficult. The following is known:

Theorem 3

(Erdős, Sárközy, T. Sós)

For every

(1)

\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\varepsilon}}\ll n-F_{3,2}(n)\leq n-f_{3,2}(n)\ll n(\log n)^{\frac{e\log 2}{2}-1+\varepsilon}

(2)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{2\ell+1,2}(n)}{n}\geq\log 2=0.69\dots
;

(3)

\frac{n}{(\log n)^{2}}\ll n-F_{2\ell+1,2}(n)
.

So already for
F_{5,2}
the right shape of the function is not yet determined and remains an interesting open problem to find it.

Theorem 4

(3)

(4)

Based on the work of Erdős, Sárközy, and T. Sós, Verstraëte
[
21
]

studied a similar problem: He aimed to find the maximal size of a set
{A}\subseteq[n]
such that no product of
k
distinct elements of
{A}
is in the value set of a given polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. He showed that for a certain class of polynomials the answer is
\Theta(n)
, for another class it is
\Theta(\pi(n))
, and conjectured that these are the only two possibilities:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and let
k

be a positive integer. Then, for some constant
\rho=\rho(k,f)

depending only on
k
and
f
, the maximal size of
a set
{A}\subseteq[n]
such that no product of
k
distinct elements of
{A}
is in the value set of
f
is either
(\rho+o(1))n
or
(\rho+o(1))\pi(n)
as
n\to\infty
.

For further related results, see
[
14
,
18
]
.

We investigated the original problem in the case
d=3

F_{k,3}
and
f_{k,3}

d=2

For
k=2

Theorem 5
.

There exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

c_{1}n^{2/3}<n-F_{2,3}(n)\leq n-f_{2,3}(n)<c_{2}n^{{2}/{3}}.

For the case
k=3

f_{3,3}(n)/n

c_{3,3}\in(0,1)

Theorem 6

There exists a constant
0.6224\leq c_{3,3}\leq 0.6420

f_{3,3}(n)=(c_{3,3}+o(1))n.

An analogous result holds for

Theorem 7

There exists a constant
0.6919\leq C_{3,3}\leq 0.7136

Notations.
Throughout this paper, we denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. The standard notation

,
\gg

and
O
is applied to positive quantities in the usual way.
That is,

all mean that

,
for some absolute constant

. If the constant

. Analogously to squarefree numbers, we call an integer

2.
Combinatorial and arithmetic lemmas

Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r}
be arbitrary graphs. The Turán number
\mathrm{ex}(n;G_{1},\dots,G_{r})
is the maximum number of edges in a graph on
n
vertices not containing any copy of
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, … or
G_{r}
. Let
C_{\ell}
denote the cycle of length
\ell
. A complete bipartite graph with partite sets of size
u
and
v
is denoted
K_{u,v}
.

For the proof of Theorem
14
we need the following graph-theoretic lemma.

Lemma 18
.

For every sufficiently large
n
, there exists a
K_{3,3}
-free bipartite graph
G=(S,T,E)
with
|S|=|T|=n
such that
|E|>cn^{5/3}
for some
c>0
.

Proof of Lemma
18
.

Brown
[
3
]
proved that there exists a graph on
2n
vertices with
c^{\prime}n^{5/3}
edges that does not contain
K_{3,3}
as a subgraph, where
c^{\prime}>0
. Let us denote this graph by
G_{0}
. We use a standard probabilistic argument to show that there exists a bipartite graph
G
that satisfies the requirements of the lemma. Let us divide the vertices of
G_{0}
into two sets of equal sizes with uniform distribution, and delete every edge within the two sets. We delete every edge with probability
\frac{n-1}{2n-1}
, so the expected value of the number of remaining edges is at least
\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime}n^{5/3}
. Hence, there exists such a bipartite graph with at least
\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime}n^{5/3}
edges as we claimed.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
k\geq 4
be a positive integer.
Let

be the set of primes. Let us suppose that the positive integers

are the products of two primes, where the canonical form of

E_{u}=\{(P_{i},P_{j}):p_{i},p_{j}\in P,\ p_{i}p_{j}=a_{h}\text{ for some $h$}\}

If

is
\{C_{3},C_{4},\dots,C_{k}\}
-free, then
a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{3k}
is not a perfect cube.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, let us assume that
a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{3k}=x^{3}

G
contains a cycle

u\leq 2k
. Let us suppose that the shortest length of a cycle in the graph

Lemma 25
.

Suppose that
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\left[\frac{n}{\log n},n\right]
such that
d^{2}|a_{i}
implies
d\leq\log n
. If
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}
is a perfect cube, then there exist integers
u_{i},v_{i},w_{i}\in\left(\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{\log^{16}n},\sqrt[3]{n}\log^{16}n\right)

a_{i}=u_{i}v_{i}w_{i}

Proof.

Suppose that the conditions of the lemma hold for
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
, and let
a_{i}=b_{i}c_{i}^{2}d_{i}^{3}
, where
b_{i}
and
c_{i}
are squarefree and
\gcd(b_{i},c_{i})=1
. Then
c_{i}d_{i}\leq\log n
and

n\geq b_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{c_{i}^{2}d_{i}^{3}}\geq\frac{\frac{n}{\log n}}{\log^{3}n}=\frac{n}{\log^{4}n}

Clearly,

\frac{n^{4}}{\log^{4}n}\leq a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}^{2}d_{1}^{3}d_{2}^{3}d_{3}^{3}d_{4}^{3}=x^{3}\leq n^{4}

for some

. Let

m=c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}^{2}d_{1}^{3}d_{2}^{3}d_{3}^{3}d_{4}^{3},

where
m\leq\log^{12}n
. Let us define the positive integers
e_{i}
as
e_{i}=\frac{b_{i}}{\gcd(b_{i},m)}
. Since
b_{i}
is squarefree we have

\gcd(b_{i},m)\leq c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}d_{4}\leq\sqrt{m}\leq\log^{6}n

and

n\geq e_{i}=\frac{b_{i}}{\gcd(b_{i},m)}\geq\frac{\frac{n}{\log^{4}n}}{\log^{6}n}=\frac{n}{\log^{10}n}.

Using that
\gcd(e_{i},m)=1
and
\gcd(e_{i},\gcd(b_{j},m))=1
for every
1\leq i,j\leq 4
we get that

\gcd(e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4},\gcd(b_{1},m)\gcd(b_{2},m)\gcd(b_{3},m)\gcd(b_{4},m)m)=1.

It follows from

a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}=e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}\gcd(b_{1},m)\gcd(b_{2},m)\gcd(b_{3},m)\gcd(b_{4},m)m=x^{3}

that

e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}=y^{3}

for some

. Since
e_{i}
is squarefree we have

e_{1}=\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4})\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4}),

e_{2}=\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4})\gcd(e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}),

e_{3}=\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4})\gcd(e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}),

and

e_{4}=\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4})\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4})\gcd(e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}).

Hence

y^{3}=e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}=\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})^{3}\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4})^{3}\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4})^{3}\gcd(e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})^{3},

that is

It follows from
\frac{n^{4}}{\log^{40}n}\leq e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}\leq n^{4}

\frac{n^{4/3}}{\log^{40/3}}n\leq y\leq n^{4/3}

Similarly,

\frac{n^{1/3}}{\log^{40/3}n}\leq\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4}),\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4}),\gcd(e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})\leq n^{1/3}\log^{10}n.

Let

a_{1}=\left(\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\right)\left(\gcd(e_{1},e_{2},e_{4})\gcd(b_{1},m)\right)\left(\gcd(e_{1},e_{3},e_{4})c_{1}^{2}d_{1}^{3}\right)=u_{1}v_{1}w_{1},

where

\frac{n^{1/3}}{\log^{16}n}\leq u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}\leq n^{1/3}\log^{16}n,

which completes the proof.

Lemma 26
.

Let
B\subseteq[n]
be the set of those positive integers
b
that can be written in the form
b=uvw
with
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
u,v,w\in\left[\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{\log^{16}n},\sqrt[3]{n}\log^{16}n\right]
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that

|B|\leq\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1-\frac{e\log 3}{2\sqrt{3}}-\varepsilon}}

for
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
B_{1}
be the set of all
b=uvw\in B
such that

\min\{\Omega(u),\Omega(v),\Omega(w)\}>\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}\log\log n,

and let
B_{2}
be the set of all
b=uvw\in B
such that

\Omega(w)\leq\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}\log\log n.

Then
B=B_{1}\cup B_{2}
, so
|B|\leq|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|
.

Let
n\geq n_{1}(\varepsilon)
as per Lemma
23
. Now if
b\in B_{1}
, then

\Omega(b)=\Omega(u)+\Omega(v)+\Omega(w)>\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}\log\log n,

so it follows from Lemma
23
that

|B_{1}|\leq\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}\log\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{e}{\sqrt{3}}-\varepsilon}}=\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1-\frac{e\log 3}{2\sqrt{3}}-\varepsilon}}.

For the estimation of the size of
B_{2}
, let

S_{n}=\left\{(u,v):\ \frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{(\log n)^{16}}\leq u,v\leq\sqrt[3]{n}(\log n)^{16}\right\}

and for each

let

W_{u,v}:=\left\{w:\ w\leq\frac{n}{uv},\ \Omega(w)\leq\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}\log\log n\right\},

W_{u,v}^{*}:=\left\{w:\ w\leq\frac{n}{uv},\ \Omega(w)\leq\left(\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}+\frac{1}{\log\log n}\right)\log\log\frac{n}{uv}\right\}.

A simple calculation shows that, for

\log\log n-\log\log\frac{n}{uv}=\log 3+o(1)

\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}\log\log n\leq\left(\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}+\frac{1}{\log\log n}\right)\log\log\frac{n}{uv}

Hence,

|B_{2}|\leq\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in S_{n}}|W_{u,v}|\leq\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in S_{n}}|W_{u,v}^{*}|

From Lemma

, it follows that

|W_{u,v}^{*}|\leq\frac{\frac{n}{uv}}{\left(\log\frac{n}{uv}\right)^{1+\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}\log\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}-\frac{e}{3^{1.5}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}}=\frac{\frac{n}{uv}}{\left(\log\frac{n}{uv}\right)^{1-\frac{e\log 3}{2\sqrt{3}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}}

for

\begin{split}|B_{2}|&\leq\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in S_{n}}|W_{u,v}^{*}|\leq\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in S_{n}}\frac{\frac{n}{uv}}{\left(\log\frac{n}{uv}\right)^{1-\frac{e\log 3}{2\sqrt{3}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}}\\

Proof of Theorem

.

First, we show that

.

If
A\subseteq[n]
is a set such that
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
has no solution in
A
with
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
, then there is at most one perfect cube in

. If we omit this perfect cube from the set, there will be no solution for
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
in
A
(where
a_{1},a_{2}
are not necessarily different): if there was a solution, it would have to be of the form
a_{1}^{2}=x^{3}
, so
a_{1}
would have to be yet another perfect cube. Hence
F_{2,3}(n)-1\leq f_{2,3}(n)
. Vice versa, if
A\subseteq[n]
is such that
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
has no solution in
A
(with
a_{1},a_{2}
not necessarily different), then
1\notin A
, and
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
has no solution in
A\cup\{1\}
with
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
. Hence,
f_{2,3}(n)+1\leq F_{2,3}(n)
.

We prove the theorem for
f_{2,3}(n)
. Let us consider a set
A\subseteq[n]
of maximal size avoiding solutions to
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
, by definition,
|A|=f_{2,3}(n)
. We will estimate at least how many elements we have to leave out from
[n]
to get such a set.

Suppose we have a solution
a_{1}a_{2}=x^{3}
. Let us write
a_{1}=uv^{2}w^{3}
, where
u,v
are squarefree and
\gcd(u,v)=1
, so
uv^{2}
is the cubefree part of
a_{1}
. Notice that the cubefree part of
a_{2}
must be
vu^{2}
, hence
a_{2}\in\{vu^{2}t^{3}:\ t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
. We will call
a
and
b
equivalent if their cubefree parts are the same. We will call
a
and
b

opposites
if their product is a perfect cube. Clearly, if a number is in
A
, then there is no number in
A
from the opposite equivalence class. Therefore,
A
can be partitioned into equivalence classes, and it contains exactly one (the larger) class from every pair of opposite equivalence classes.

Hence, the minimum number of elements we have to leave out is the sum of the sizes of the smaller equivalence classes (from each opposite pair). The size of the class containing integers with cubefree part
uv^{2}
is
\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{\frac{n}{uv^{2}}}\right\rfloor
, since for the elements
uv^{2}t^{3}\leq n
the bound

must hold. For a fixed pair
u,v
(satisfying
u<v
and
\gcd(u,v)=1
) we have the following pair of opposite equivalence classes:
\{uv^{2}t^{3}:\;t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}\cap[n]
and
\{vu^{2}t^{3}:\;t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}\cap[n]

u<v
, the first class will be smaller (or of the exact same size), so the minimum number of elements we have to leave out is exactly

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v\\
\gcd(u,v)=1\\
uv^{2}\leq n\\
u,v\ \text{squarefree}\end{subarray}}\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{\frac{n}{uv^{2}}}\right\rfloor=n-f_{2,3}(n).

First we give an upper bound for

using this formula.

Observe that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv\ \text{squarefree}\end{subarray}}\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{\frac{n}{uv^{2}}}\right\rfloor=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv\ \text{squarefree}\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{\frac{n}{uv^{2}}}\right\rfloor}1=\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{n}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv\>\text{squarefree},\\
uv^{2}\leq\frac{n}{t^{3}},\end{subarray}}1<\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{n}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv^{2}\leq\frac{n}{t^{3}}\end{subarray}}1.

The inner sum can be rewritten as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv^{2}\leq\frac{n}{t^{3}}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{u=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{t}\right\rfloor}\sum_{v=u+1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{n}{ut^{3}}}\right\rfloor}1\leq\sum_{u=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{t}\right\rfloor}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{ut^{3}}}-u\right).

As

\sum_{u=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{t}\right\rfloor}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{ut^{3}}}-u\right)<\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\sqrt[3]{n}}{t}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{ut^{3}}}-u\right)\,du=1.5\,\frac{n^{2/3}}{t^{2}}.

Hence,

\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{n}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v,\\
uv^{2}\leq\frac{n}{t^{3}}\end{subarray}}1<\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor\sqrt[3]{n}\right\rfloor}1.5\,\frac{n^{2/3}}{t^{2}}<1.5\,{n^{2/3}}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}n^{2/3},

which proves the upper bound with

.

Now we provide a lower bound for the number of elements that we have to leave out.

We will construct a set

of disjoint pairs

is always a perfect cube. Then we have to leave out at least one element from each pair in

Let

Then

is indeed a perfect cube. The squarefree parts of

and

are
a
and

respectively, which implies that the pairs are disjoint. It is well-known that the number of squarefree numbers less than

is

|A_{n}|\geq\frac{1}{2}{\left(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}+o(1)\right)^{2}n^{2/3}}>0.18n^{2/3}

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let us take a set

such that the equation

has no solution with
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
and integer

, except the trivial case
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}
. Note that distinct elements of
A
have distinct cubefree parts, since

would yield the solution
a_{1}=a_{2}=uv^{3},a_{3}=uw^{3}
. Therefore, we can restrict our attention to sets of cubefree numbers, since each element of
A
may be replaced by its cubefree part. From now on, it is assumed that

contains only cubefree integers.

First, we give an upper bound for the size of
A
. Let
r
be a fixed positive integer and let
p_{i}
denote the
i
th prime. Each cubefree

can be written as

where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\{0,1,2\}
and

is cubefree satisfying
\gcd(a^{\prime},p_{1}p_{2}\dots p_{r})=1

is the
p_{r}
-smooth
and

is the
p_{r+1}
-rough
part of the number

. Observe that the product of three integers is a perfect cube if and only if so are the product of their
p_{r}
-smooth parts and the product of their
p_{r+1}
-rough parts.
In particular, for a fixed
a^{\prime}
there cannot be three elements in
A
with
p_{r+1}
-rough part
a^{\prime}
such that the product of their
p_{r}
-smooth parts is a perfect cube. Note that the product of three
p_{r}
-smooth numbers is a cube if and only if the sum of their exponent vectors
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{r})
add up to
(0,0,\dots,0)
calculating coordinate-wise modulo 3. Alternatively, if we consider the exponent vectors as elements of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{r}
, they form a nontrivial 3-term arithmetic progression (3AP). Let
L_{r}(i)
be the set of
p_{r}
-smooth cubefree integers up to

and let

denote the largest possible size of a subset of

avoiding nontrivial solutions to
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=x^{3}
. Note that

is the size of the largest 3AP-free subset of

\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\{0,1,2\}^{r}:\ \alpha_{1}\log p_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{r}\log p_{r}\leq\log i\},

if we consider this set as a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{r}
. For brevity, we will say that
s_{r}(i)
is the size of the largest 3AP-free subset of
L_{r}(i)
. Clearly, for every
i\geq p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2}
, we have
s_{r}(i)=s_{r}(p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2})
(whose common value is
r_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{3}^{r})
, the largest possible size of a 3AP-free subset of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{r}
).

For a
p_{r+1}
-rough number

, there are at most
s_{r}\left(\lfloor n/a^{\prime}\rfloor\right)

[n]

(where

. Note that the number of
p_{r+1}
-rough elements of

|I_{i}|\cdot\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)+O_{r}(1)
, where

denotes the size of

, since in each set of

-rough numbers is exactly

|A|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2}}|I_{i}|\cdot\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)s_{r}(i)+O_{r}(1).

\gamma_{r}:=\left(\frac{s_{r}(p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2})}{p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p_{1}^{2}\dots p_{r}^{2}-1}\frac{s_{r}(i)}{i(i+1)}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right),

it is obtained that

(3)

In the previous upper bound we used only that there is no nontrivial solution to

same

p_{r+1}
-rough part. However, if we consider only squarefree numbers as possible

-rough parts

.
Indeed, in the prime factorization of a product of three squarefree numbers each exponent is 1, 2, or 3, so in order to get a perfect cube, the three numbers must have exactly the same prime factors, meaning they coincide. Let us define

Proof of Theorem

&=n\left(-\log\alpha+\sum_{n^{\alpha}<p\leq n^{1-\alpha}}\frac{\log\alpha-\log\left(1-\frac{\log p}{\log n}\right)}{p}+o(1)\right)\\
&=n(-\log\alpha+\left(\log(1-\alpha)-\log\alpha\right)\log\alpha-\\
&-\sum_{n^{\alpha+o(1)}\leq k\leq n^{1-\alpha+o(1)}}\frac{\log\left(1-\frac{\log(k\log k)}{\log n}\right)}{k\log k}+o(1))\\
&=n\left(-\log\alpha+\left(\log(1-\alpha)-\log\alpha\right)\log\alpha-\sum_{n^{\alpha}\leq k\leq n^{1-\alpha}}\frac{\log\left(1-\frac{\log k}{\log n}\right)}{k\log k}+o(1)\right)\\

as

|B|=\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)-O\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}\right).

Let
P=\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t}\}
denote the set of primes
p
with
2<p\leq\sqrt{n}
, so that by the Prime Number Theorem
t=(2+o(1))\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}
. Let
G_{t}
be a
\{C_{3},\dots,C_{12\ell}\}
-free graph on the vertex set
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{t}\}
with the maximal number of edges. Let
C
denote the set of elements of the form
p_{i}p_{j}
(where
1\leq i<j\leq t
) such that
p_{i}p_{j}\in C
if and only if
P_{i}P_{j}
is an edge in
G_{t}
. By Lemma
19
(4) we have

|C|\gg\frac{n^{\frac{9\ell}{18\ell-2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{9\ell}{9\ell-1}}}.

Thus, for the size of
A=B\cup C
we have the lower bound

|A|=|B|+|C|\geq\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)+c\frac{n^{\frac{9\ell}{18\ell-2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{9\ell}{9\ell-1}}}

for some
c>0
.
Now we will prove that
A\in\Gamma_{36\ell,3}
. Assume, to the contrary, that we have
a_{1}\dots a_{36\ell}=x^{3}
for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{36\ell}\in A
such that
a_{1}<\dots<a_{36\ell}
.

Assume that
q
is a prime with
q>\sqrt{n}
and
q\mid x^{3}
. Then
q^{3}\mid x^{3}
, which is impossible, since at most two multiples of
q
are contained in
A
. Therefore, if
p
is a prime number with
p\mid x^{3}
, then
p\leq\sqrt{n}
, that is,
a_{i}\in C
for all
1\leq i\leq 36\ell
. Hence,
a_{1}\dots a_{36\ell}=x^{3}
, which is impossible by Lemma
22
.

The upper bound is a direct consequence of Lemma
27
.

(2) First we prove the lower bound. Let

\begin{split}B=&\{p:\sqrt{n}<p\leq n,\ p\hbox{ prime}\}\cup\{2p:\sqrt{n}<p\leq\frac{n}{2},\ p\hbox{ prime}\}\cup\\
&\cup\{3p:\sqrt{n}<p\leq\frac{n}{3},\ p\hbox{ prime}\}.\end{split}

By the Prime Number Theorem,

|B|=\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)-O\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}\right).

Let
P=\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t}\}
denote the set of primes
p
with
3<p\leq\sqrt{n}
, by the Prime Number Theorem
t=(2+o(2))\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}
. Let
G_{t}
denote a
\{C_{3},\dots,C_{12\ell+1}\}
-free graph on vertex set
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{t}\}
with the maximal number of edges. Let
C
denote the set of elements of the form
p_{i}p_{j}
(where
1\leq i<j\leq t
) such that
p_{i}p_{j}\in C
if and only if
P_{i}P_{j}
is an edge in
G_{t}
. By Lemma
19
(4) we have

|C|\gg\frac{n^{\frac{9\ell}{18\ell-2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{9\ell}{9\ell-1}}}.

Thus, for the size of
A=B\cup C
we have the lower bound

|A|=|B|+|C|\geq\pi(n)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)+\pi\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)+c\frac{n^{\frac{9\ell}{18\ell-2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{9\ell}{9\ell-1}}}

with some
c>0
. Now we will prove that
A\in\Gamma_{36\ell+3,3}
. Assume, to the contrary, that we have
a_{1}\dots a_{36\ell+3}=x^{3}
for some distinct elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{36\ell+3}\in A
.

Let
s=|\{a_{1},\dots,a_{36\ell+3}\}\cap C|
, we may assume that
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in C
. Observe that
a_{1}\dots a_{s}=z^{3}
and
a_{s+1}\dots a_{36\ell+3}=u^{3}
for some
u,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
as
a_{1}\dots a_{s}
and
a_{s+1}\dots a_{36\ell+3}
are coprime. Clearly,
\Omega(a_{1}\dots a_{s})=2s
. Hence we have
3\mid 2s
, that is,
3\mid s
.
If
s=0
, then there exist prime numbers
\sqrt{n}<p_{1}<\dots<p_{12\ell+1}\leq n
such that
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{36\ell+3}\}=\{p_{i},2p_{i},3p_{i}:1\leq i\leq 12\ell+1\}

a_{1}\dots a_{36\ell+3}=6^{12\ell+1}(p_{1}\dots p_{12\ell+1})^{3}=u^{3}

s=3

a_{7}\dots a_{36\ell+3}=6^{12\ell-1}(p_{1}\dots p_{12\ell-1})^{3}=u^{3}

22

The upper bound is a direct consequence of Lemma
27

4.
Concluding remarks and open problems

In this paper we gave bounds for the functions
F_{k,3}(n)
and
f_{k,3}(n)
for every
k
.

Finally, we pose some problems for further research. Clearly,
F_{1,d}(n)=f_{1,d}(n)=n-n^{1/d}+O(1)
. For
1<k<d
, the set

\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus\{m:m\in\{1,2,\dots,n\},\exists\ell\leq n^{k/d},\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\hbox{ s.t. }m\mid\ell^{d}\}

shows that

n-F_{k,d}(n)\leq\sum_{\ell\leq n^{k/d}}d(\ell^{d})\leq n^{k/d+\varepsilon}.

Problem 28
.

Let us suppose that
1<k<d
. Is it true that

n^{k/d}\ll n-F_{k,d}(n)\leq n-f_{k,d}(n)\ll n^{k/d}?

Problem 29
.

Is it true that there exists a constant
c
such that

f_{2,3}(n)=n-(c+o(1))n^{2/3}?

Problem 30
.

Let
d\geq 4
. Is it true that

f_{d+1,d}(n)=(1-o(1))n?

As a corollary of the above theorems we get the following result:

Corollary 31
.

For
d=2,3
and
k>d
,
d\mid k
, there exist constants
c_{k,d}>0
and
C_{k,d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

F_{k,d}(n)=(c_{k,d}+o(1))\pi_{C_{k,d}}(n).

Problem 32
.

Is it true that for any
d\geq 4
and
k>d
,
d\mid k
, there exist constants
c_{k,d}>0
and
C_{k,d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

F_{k,d}(n)=(c_{k,d}+o(1))\pi_{C_{k,d}}(n)?

Maximal green sequences of quivers with multiple edges
Kiyoshi Igusa, Ying Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper we completely describe maximal green sequences (MGS) of acyclic quivers with multiple edges in terms of maximal green sequences of their multiple edge-free (ME-free) versions. In particular we establish that any MGS of a quiver must be an ME-free MGS of its ME-free version. For quivers with oriented cycles our results for acyclic quivers do not apply. However a weaker result is available under certain conditions.

2.3.
MGS-finiteness

In this subsection let’s review the basics about what kind of quivers have finitely many maximal green sequences.

Definition 2.12
.

A quiver

Here are some results that are either already known or easily proven about MGS-finiteness of quivers.

Theorem 2.13
.

[
1
]
Any acyclic quiver

of finite type or tame type as well as any acyclic quiver

of wild type with three vertices are MGS-finite.

Theorem 2.14
.

Any quiver

mutation equivalent to an acyclic quiver of finite or tame type is MGS-finite.

Proof.

Due to
[
2
]
the result is already proven in the mutation-equivalent to tame type case. For the mutation-equivalent to finite type case using the Rotation Lemma in
[
2
]
it is obvious that any MGS in such a quiver must be an
k
-reddening sequence of an acyclic quiver of finite type for a fixed

-reddening sequence can only repeat a cluster

times as shown in
[
2
]
and

10
]
and in an acyclic quiver of finite type there are only finitely many cluster-tilting objects and hence clusters.
∎

Lemma 2.15

If

Any MGS of

Since we can obtain all MGSs of

On the other hand if all

Let
Q
be an ME-ful quiver. Any MGS of an ME-ful quiver
Q
must not contain any
Q
-ME-ful
c
-vector of
Q^{\prime}
or any vector
c
which is an imaginary root of
Q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Due to
3.10
(3) we only need to prove the second part. In that case
\langle c,c\rangle_{kQ}\leq\langle c,c\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}<1
. Hence
c
is not a
c
-vector of
Q
.
∎

Now we can easily establish the following theorem.

Theorem 3.12
.

MGSs of an acyclic quiver
Q
are a subset of the set of
Q
-ME-free MGSs of its ME-free version,
Q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If the statement is incorrect along an MGS of
Q
pick the first
C
-matrix that isn’t shared by
Q^{\prime}
assuming that such an MGS exists.

In this case either at least one
c
-vector is
Q
-ME-ful or none is. If some
c
-vector is
Q
-ME-ful it must be formed by extending one
Q
-ME-free exceptional module by another
Q
-ME-free exceptional module in
Q
(i.e.
dimExt_{kQ}(A,B)=1
because it can not be larger due to Lemma
2.11
. Let’s label the indecomposable module formed by the extension
M
. We need
Ext_{kQ}(B,A)=0
so that
\langle M,M\rangle_{kQ}=1
) while in
Q^{\prime}
there are no such extensions (i.e.
dimExt_{kQ^{\prime}}(A,B)=0
). However this is impossible because
A,B
are rigid,
Hom
-orthogonal and indecomposable because
\langle M,M\rangle_{kQ}-\langle M,M\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}\leq-2
which causes
\langle M,M\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}
to be at least 3 which is impossible because
\langle M,M\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}=\langle A,A\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}+\langle A,B\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}+\langle B,A\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}+\langle B,B\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}=2-dimExt_{kQ^{\prime}}(A,B)-dimExt_{kQ^{\prime}}(B,A)
is at most 2.

If no
c
-vector is
Q
-ME-ful then in
kQ
,
kQ^{\prime}
the relevant
Hom
and
Ext
groups shouldn’t differ because neither of them involve the multiple edges that are absent in
kQ^{\prime}
. As a result that can’t happen either.

Hence the
C
-matrices corresponding to
Q,Q^{\prime}
in the MGS are all the same. Any MGS of
Q
must be an MGS of
Q^{\prime}
with the same
C
-matrices. Since all the
C
-matrices of the two quivers are the same they have the same associated permutation.

∎

Now we can prove a stronger result. In order to do so we will use a sequence of
C
-matrices as MGSs.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
Q
be an ME-ful acyclic quiver and
Q^{\prime}
be its ME-free version. The MGSs of
Q
are exactly the
Q
-ME-free MGSs
(C_{0},C_{1},\cdots C_{m})
of
Q^{\prime}
such that for any multiple edge from
i
to
j
in
Q
for any
C
-matrix
C_{i}
in the MGS such that there exists a negative
c
-vector with support containing
i
the mutation on
C_{i}
in the MGS isn’t done on any negative
c
-vector with support containing
j
.

Proof.

Let’s compare
\langle M,N\rangle_{kQ}
and
\langle M,N\rangle_{kQ^{\prime}}
. They differ if and only if there exists some multiple edge from
i
to
j
such that
i
is in the support of
M
and
j
is in the support of
N
. In this case since
Hom_{kQ}(M,N)=Hom_{kQ^{\prime}}(M,N)=Ext_{kQ^{\prime}}(M,N)=0

dimExt_{kQ}(M,N)>0
. Repeating the argument in
3.12
we can show that this is the only possible scenario for a
Q
-ME-free MGSs of
Q^{\prime}
to not be identical to an MGS in
Q
.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

The number of maximal green sequences of a quiver
Q
is no greater than that of its ME-free version.

(2)

All quivers with an MGS-finite ME-free version must themselves be MGS-finite.

(3)

No minimally MGS-infinite quiver can contain multiple edges.

(4)

Any two ME-equivalent quivers are MGS-equivalent to each other.

Proof.

Only (4) needs to be proven even though it is still obvious. For ME-equivalent quivers
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
the conditions of
3.13
are identical which is why the number of MGS are identical.
∎

Measurement of the \PZ / γ ∗ → τ ​ τ \PZ/\gamma^{*}\to\tau\tau cross section in pp collisions at s = \sqrt{s}= 13 \TeV and validation of τ \tau lepton analysis techniques


Abstract
A measurement is presented of the \PZ / γ ∗ → τ ​ τ \PZ/\gamma^{*}\to\tau\tau cross section in pp collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV , using data recorded by the CMS experiment at the LHC, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 2.3 \fbinv . The product of the inclusive cross section and branching fraction is measured to be σ ⁡ ( pp → \PZ / γ ∗ ​ +X ) ​ ℬ ​ ( \PZ / γ ∗ → τ ​ τ ) = 1848 ± 12 ​ \stat ± 67 ​ (syst+lumi) \unit ​ p ​ b \sigma(\textrm{pp}\to\PZ/\gamma^{*}\text{+X})\,\mathcal{B}(\PZ/\gamma^{*}\to\tau\tau)=1848\pm 12\stat\pm 67\text{~(syst+lumi)}\unit{pb} , in agreement with the standard model expectation, computed at next-to-next-to-leading order accuracy in perturbative quantum chromodynamics. The measurement is used to validate new analysis techniques relevant for future measurements of τ \tau lepton production. The measurement also provides the reconstruction efficiency and energy scale for τ \tau decays to hadrons + ν τ +\nu_{\tau} final states, determined with respective relative uncertainties of 2.2% and 0.9%.

With a lifetime of

\Pgt

Approximately two thirds of
\Pgt

\Pgt

Constrained by the
\Pgt
lepton mass of
1.777\GeV

the hadronic system is characterized by low particle multiplicities, typically consisting of either one or three charged pions or kaons, and up to two neutral pions.
The hadrons produced in
\Pgt

The
\Pgt

35\%

We denote the electron and muon produced in
\Pgt\to\Pe\PGn\PGn

\Pgt\to\Pgm\PGn\PGn

\Pgt_{\Pe}

The hadronic system produced in a
\Pgt\to\mbox{hadrons}+\PGn_{\Pgt}

\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

The Drell–Yan (DY)
[
36

\Pgt

\Pq\bar{\Pq}\to\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt

First, the process
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
represents a reference signal to study the efficiency to reconstruct and identify
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
as well as to measure the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
energy scale.
Moreover,
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
production constitutes the dominant irreducible background to analyses of SM
\textrm{H}\to\Pgt\Pgt
events,
and to searches for new resonances decaying to
\Pgt
lepton pairs.
The cross section for DY production exceeds the one for SM
H
production by about two orders of magnitude.
Signals from new resonances are expected to be even more rare.
It is therefore important to control with a precision reaching the sub-percent level
the rate for
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
production, as well as its distribution in kinematic observables.
In addition, the reducible backgrounds relevant for the study of
\PZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt

are also relevant for studies of SM
H
production and to searches for new resonances.

This paper reports a precision measurement of the inclusive
\Pp\Pp\to\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\text{+X}\to\Pgt\Pgt\text{+X}
cross section.
The measurement demonstrates that
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
production is well understood,
and provides ways to validate techniques relevant in future analyses of
\Pgt
lepton production.
Most notably, a method based on control samples in data is introduced for determining background contributions
arising from the misidentification of quark or gluon jets as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
Measurements of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
identification (ID) efficiency and of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
energy scale
[
37

are obtained as byproducts of the analysis.

The cross section for DY production of
\Pgt
lepton pairs
was previously measured by the CMS and ATLAS experiments
in proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collisions at
\sqrt{s}=7\TeV
at the LHC
[
38

39

and in proton-antiproton collisions at

42

In this study, we present the
\Pp\Pp\to\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\text{+X}\to\Pgt\Pgt\text{+X}
cross section measured at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
,
using data recorded by the CMS experiment, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 2.3
\fbinv
.
Events are selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}

\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}

The

The

0.2
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
A silicon pixel and strip tracker,
a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL), and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter (HCAL),
each composed of a barrel and two endcap sections,
are positioned within the solenoid volume.
The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.5
.
Trajectories of isolated muons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}=100\GeV
, emitted at
\abs{\eta}<1.4
, are reconstructed with an efficiency close to 100%
and resolutions of 2.8% in
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, and with uncertainties of 10 and 30
\mum
in their respective transverse and longitudinal impact parameters relative to their points of origin
[
44
]
.
The ECAL is a fine-grained hermetic calorimeter with quasi-projective geometry,
segmented in the barrel region of
\abs{\eta}<1.48
, as well as in the two endcaps that extend up to
\abs{\eta}<3.0
.
Similarly, the HCAL barrel and endcaps cover the region
\abs{\eta}<3.0
.
Forward calorimeters extend the coverage up to
\abs{\eta}<5.0
.
Muons are measured and identified in the range
\abs{\eta}<2.4
using
gas-ionization detectors embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
A two-level trigger system is used to reduce the rate of recorded
events to a level suitable for data acquisition and storage.
The first level (L1) of the CMS trigger system, composed of specialized hardware processors,
uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select the most interesting events in a fixed time interval of less than 4
\mus
.
The high-level trigger processor farm decreases the event rate from around 100
\unit
kHz to less than 1
\unit
kHz before storage and subsequent analysis.
Details of the CMS detector and its performance, together with a definition of the coordinate system and kinematic variables, can be found in Ref.
[
45
]
.

Data and Monte Carlo simulation

The data were recorded in
\Pp\Pp
collisions at 25
\unit

The analysed data correspond to an integrated luminosity of 2.3

The background events are complemented with SM

A reweighting is applied to MC-generated

All generated events are passed through a detailed simulation of the CMS apparatus, based on

0.4
Event reconstruction

The information provided by all CMS subdetectors is employed in a
particle-flow (PF)
algorithm
[
69
]

to identify and reconstruct individual particles in the event, namely muons, electrons, photons, charged and neutral hadrons.
These particles are then used to reconstruct jets,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates and the vector imbalance in missing transverse momentum in the event, referred to as
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
,
as well as to quantify the isolation of leptons.

Electrons are reconstructed using an algorithm
[
70
]
that matches trajectories in the silicon tracker to energy depositions in the ECAL.
Trajectories of electron candidates are reconstructed using a dedicated algorithm that accounts for the emission of bremsstrahlung photons.
The energy loss due to bremsstrahlung is determined by searching for energy depositions in the ECAL emitted tangentially to the track.
A multivariate (MVA) approach based on boosted decision trees (BDT)
[
71
]

is employed to distinguish electrons from hadrons that mimic electron signatures.
Observables that quantify the quality of the electron track,
the compactness of the electron cluster in directions transverse and longitudinal relative to the electron motion,
and the matching of the track momentum and direction to the sum and positions of energy depositions in the ECAL are used as inputs to the BDT.
The BDT is trained on samples of genuine and false electrons,
produced in MC simulation.
Additional requirements are applied to remove electrons originating from photon conversions.

The identification of muons is based on
linking track segments reconstructed in the silicon tracking detector and in the muon system
[
72
]
.
The matching is done both by starting from a track in the muon system and
starting from a track in the inner detector.
When a link is established, the track parameters are refitted using the combination of hits in the inner and outer detectors,
and the reconstructed trajectory is referred to as a global muon track.
Quality criteria are applied on the multiplicity of hits, the number of matched segments, and the quality of the fit to a global muon track, the latter being quantified through a
\chi^{2}
criterion.

Electrons and muons in signal events are expected to be isolated,
while leptons from heavy flavour (charm and bottom quark) decays,
as well as from in-flight decays of pions and kaons,
are often reconstructed within jets.
Isolated leptons are distinguished from leptons in jets through a sum, denoted by the symbol
I_{\ell}
, of the scalar
p_{\mathrm{T}}
values of additional charged particles, neutral hadrons,
and photons reconstructed using the PF algorithm within a cone in
\eta
and azimuth
\phi
(in radians)
of size
\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}=0.3
, centred around the lepton direction.
Neutral hadrons and photons within the innermost region of the cone,
\Delta R<0.01
, are excluded from the isolation sum for muons
to prevent the footprint of the muon in ECAL and HCAL from causing the muon to fail isolation criteria.
When computing the isolation of electrons reconstructed in the ECAL endcap region,
we exclude photons within
\Delta R<0.08
and charged particles within
\Delta R<0.015
of the direction of the electron,
to avoid counting photons emitted in bremsstrahlung and tracks originating from the conversion of such photons.
As the amount of material that electrons traverse in the barrel region before reaching the ECAL is smaller,
the resulting probability for bremsstrahlung emission and photon conversion is sufficiently reduced
so as not to require exclusion of particles in the innermost cone from the isolation sum.
Efficiency loss due to pileup is kept minimal
by considering only charged particles originating from the lepton production vertex (“charged from PV”).
The contribution from the neutral component of pileup to the isolation of the lepton is taken into account by means of
\Delta\beta
corrections
[
69
]
,
which enter the computation of the isolation
I_{\ell}
, as follows:

I_{\ell}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textrm{charged}\\
\textrm{from PV}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathrm{T}}+\textrm{max}\left\{0,\sum_{\textrm{neutrals}}p_{\mathrm{T}}-\Delta\beta\right\},

(1)

where
\ell
corresponds to either
\Pe
or
\Pgm
, and the sums extend over, respectively,
the charged particles that originate from the lepton production vertex and the neutral particles.
The “
max
” function represents taking the largest of the two values within the brackets.
The
\Delta\beta
corrections are computed by summing the scalar
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of charged particles
that are within a cone of size
\Delta R=0.3
around the lepton direction, but do not originate from the lepton production vertex,
(“charged from PU”) and scaling that sum by a factor of one-half:

\Delta\beta=0.5\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textrm{charged}\\
\textrm{from PU}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathrm{T}}.

(2)

The factor of
0.5
approximates the phenomenological ratio of neutral-to-charged hadron production in the hadronization of inelastic
\Pp\Pp
collisions.

Collision vertices are reconstructed using a deterministic annealing algorithm
[
73
,
74
]
,
with the reconstructed vertex position required to be compatible with the location of the LHC beam in the
x
–
y
plane.
The primary collision vertex (PV) is taken to be the vertex that has the maximum
\sum p_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}
of tracks associated to it.
Electrons, muons, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates are required to be compatible with originating from the PV.

Hadronic
\Pgt
decays are reconstructed using the “hadrons+strips” (HPS) algorithm
[
37
]
, which is used to separate the
individual decay modes of the
\Pgt
into

\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGn_{\Pgt}
,
\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\Pgpz\PGn_{\Pgt}
,
\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\Pgpz\Pgpz\PGn_{\Pgt}
, and
\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\mathrm{h}^{+}\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGn_{\Pgt}
,
where
\mathrm{h}^{\pm}
denotes either a charged pion or kaon (the decay modes of
\Pgt^{+}
are assumed to be identical to their partner
\Pgt^{-}
modes through charge conjugation invariance).
The
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates are constructed by combining the charged PF hadrons with neutral pions.
The neutral pions are reconstructed by clustering the PF photons within rectangular strips,
narrow in the
\eta
, but wide in the
\phi
directions,
to account for the non-negligible probability for photons produced in
\Pgpz\to\PGg\PGg
decays
to convert into electron-positron pairs when traversing the all-silicon tracking detector of CMS
and the broadening of energy depositions in the ECAL that occurs when this happens.
For the same reason, electrons and positrons reconstructed through the PF algorithm are considered in the reconstruction of the neutral pions
besides photons.
The momentum of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate is taken as the vector sum over the momenta of the charged hadrons and neutral pions used in reconstructing the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
decay mode, assuming the pion-mass hypotheses.
We do not use the strips of
0.20\times 0.05
size in the
\eta
–
\phi
plane, used in previous analyses
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
33
,
34
,
38
,
43
]
,
but an improved version of the strip reconstruction developed during the
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
run.
In the improved version, the size of the strip is adjusted as function of
p_{\mathrm{T}}
,
taking into consideration the bending of charged particles in the magnetic field increasing inversely with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
.
More details on strip reconstruction and validation of the algorithm with data are given in Ref.
[
75
]
.
The main handle for distinguishing
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from the large background of quark and gluon jets relies on the use of tight isolation requirements.
The sums of scalar
p_{\mathrm{T}}
values from photons and from charged particles originating from the PV
within a cone of
\Delta R=0.5
centred around the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
direction, are used as input to an MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant.
The set of input variables is complemented with the scalar
p_{\mathrm{T}}
sum of charged particles not originating from the PV,
by the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
decay mode, and by observables that are sensitive to the lifetime of the
\Pgt
.
The transverse impact parameter of the “leading” (highest
p_{\mathrm{T}}
) track of each
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate relative to the PV
is used for
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates reconstructed in any decay mode.
For
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates reconstructed in the
\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\mathrm{h}^{+}\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGn_{\Pgt}
decay mode,
a fit of the three tracks to a common secondary vertex (SV) is attempted, and the distance between SV and PV is used as additional input to the MVA.
The MVA is trained on genuine
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and jets generated in MC simulation.
Four working points (WP), referred to as barely, minimally, moderately, and tightly constrained,
are defined through changes made in the selections on the MVA output.
The thresholds are adjusted as functions of the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate,
such that the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
identification efficiency for each WP is independent of
p_{\mathrm{T}}
.
The moderate and tight WP used to select events in different channels
provide efficiencies of
55
and
45\%
, and misidentification rates for jets of typically
1
and
0.5\%
, depending on the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the jet
[
75
]
.
Additional discriminants are employed to separate
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from electrons and muons.
The separation of
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from electrons is performed via another MVA-based discriminant
[
75
]
that utilizes input observables that quantify the matching between the sum of energy depositions in the ECAL and the momentum of the leading track of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate,
as well as variables that distinguish electromagnetic from hadronic showers.
The cutoff-based discriminant described in Ref.
[
37
]
is used to separate
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from muons.
It is based on matching the leading track of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate with energy depositions in the ECAL and HCAL, as well as with track segments in the muon detectors.

Jets within the range
|\eta|<4.7
are reconstructed
using the anti-
\textrm{k}_{\text{t}}
algorithm
[
76
,
77
]
with a distance parameter
R=0.4
.
Reconstructed jets are required not to overlap with identified electrons, muons, or
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates within
\Delta R<0.5
,
and to pass a set of minimal identification criteria that aim to reject jets arising from calorimeter noise
[
78
]
.
The energy of reconstructed jets is calibrated as function of jet
p_{\mathrm{T}}
and
\eta

[
79
]
.
Average energy density corrections calculated using the
FastJet
algorithm
[
80
,
81
]

are applied to compensate pileup effects.
Jets originating from the hadronization of
\PQb
quarks
are identified using the “combined secondary vertex” (CSV) algorithm
[
82
]
,
which exploits observables related to the long lifetime of
\PQb
hadrons and the higher particle multiplicity and mass
of
\PQb
jets compared to light-quark and gluon jets.

The vector
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
, with its magnitude referred to as
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
, is reconstructed using an MVA regression algorithm
[
83
]
.
To reduce the impact of pileup on the resolution in
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
,
the algorithm utilizes the fact that pileup produces jets predominantly of low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
,
while leptons and high-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
jets are almost exclusively produced through hard scattering processes.

The
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal is distinguished from backgrounds by means of the mass of the
\Pgt
lepton pair.
The mass, denoted by the symbol
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
, is reconstructed using the
SVfit
algorithm
[
84
]
.
The algorithm is based on a likelihood approach and uses as inputs the measured momenta of the visible decay products of both
\Pgt
leptons,
the reconstructed
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
, and an event-by-event estimate of the
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
resolution.
The latter is computed as described in Refs.
[
85
,
83
]
.
The inputs are combined with a probabilistic model for leptonic and hadronic
\Pgt
decays
to estimate the momenta of the neutrinos produced in these decays.
The algorithm achieves a resolution in
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
of
{\approx}15\%
relative to the mass of the
\Pgt
lepton pairs at the generator level.

0.5
Event selection

The events selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
, and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels
are recorded by combining single-electron and single-muon triggers,
triggers that are based on the presence of two
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates in the event,
and triggers based on the presence of both an electron and a muon.

The
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels utilize single-electron and -muon triggers
with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
thresholds of
23
and
18\GeV
, respectively.
Selected events are required to contain
an electron of
p_{\mathrm{T}}>24\GeV
or a muon of
p_{\mathrm{T}}>19\GeV
, both with
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.1
,
and a
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>20\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.3
.
The electron or muon is required to pass an isolation requirement of
I_{\ell}<0.10\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
, computed according to Eq. (
1
).
The
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate is required to pass the moderate WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant,
and to have a charge opposite to that of the electron or muon.
The
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate is further required to pass a tight or minimal requirement on the discriminant that separates hadronic
\Pgt
decays from electrons,
and a minimal or tight selection on the discriminant that separates
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from muons.
Background arising from
\PW
+jets and
\cPqt\cPaqt
production is reduced
by requiring the transverse mass of electron or muon and
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
to satisfy
m_{\mathrm{T}}<40\GeV
.
The transverse mass is defined by:

m_{\mathrm{T}}=\sqrt{2\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}\,\left(1-\cos\Delta\phi\right)}\,,

(3)

where the symbol
\ell
refers to the electron or muon,
and
\Delta\phi
denotes the angle in the transverse plane between the lepton momentum and the
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
vector.
Events containing additional electrons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.5
, or muons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.4
,
passing minimal identification and isolation criteria,
are rejected to reduce backgrounds from
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pe\Pe
and
\Pgm\Pgm
events, and from diboson production.

A trigger based on the presence of two
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates is used to record events in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel.
The trigger selects events containing two isolated calorimeter energy deposits at the L1 trigger stage,
which are subsequently required to pass a simplified version of the PF-based offline
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
reconstruction at the high-level trigger stage.
The latter applies additional isolation criteria.
The
p_{\mathrm{T}}
threshold for both
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates is
35\GeV
.
The trigger efficiency increases with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
because different algorithms are used to reconstruct the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
at the L1 trigger stage and in the offline reconstruction.
The trigger reaches an efficiency plateau of
{\approx}80\%
for events in which both
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates have
p_{\mathrm{T}}>60\GeV
.
Selected events are required to contain two
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>40\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.1

that have opposite charge and satisfy
the tight WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant,
as well as the minimal criteria on the discriminants used to separate
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from electrons and muons.
Events containing electrons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.5
or muons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.4
,
passing minimal identification and isolation criteria,
are rejected to avoid overlap with the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels.

Events in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channel are recorded with the triggers based on the presence of an electron and a muon.
The acceptance for the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal is increased by using two complementary triggers.
The first trigger selects events that contain an electron with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>12\GeV
and a muon with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>17\GeV
,
while events containing an electron with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>17\GeV
and a muon with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>8\GeV
are recorded through the second trigger.
The offline event selection demands the presence of an electron with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>13\GeV
and
|\eta|<2.5
,
in conjunction with a muon of
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.4
.
Either the electron or the muon is required to pass a threshold of
p_{\mathrm{T}}>18\GeV
,
to ensure that at least one of the two triggers is fully efficient.
Electrons and muons are further required to satisfy isolation criteria of
I_{\ell}<0.15\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
, and to have opposite charge.
Background from
\cPqt\cPaqt
production is reduced through a cutoff on a topological discriminant
[
86
]
based on the projections:

P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{miss}}={\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}\cdot\hat{\zeta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{vis}}=\left({\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \mathrm{e}}+{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \mu}\right)\cdot\hat{\zeta}\,,

(4)

where the symbol
\hat{\zeta}
denotes a unit vector in the direction of the bisector of the electron and muon
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}
vectors.
The discriminator takes advantage of the fact that the angle between the neutrinos and the visible
\Pgt
lepton decay products is typically small,
causing the
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
vector in signal events to point in the direction of the visible
\Pgt
decay products,
which is often not true for
\cPqt\cPaqt
background.
Selected events are required to satisfy the condition
P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{miss}}-0.85\,P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{vis}}>-20\GeV
.
The reconstruction of the projections
P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
and
P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{vis}}
is illustrated in Fig.
1
.
The figure also shows the distribution in the observable
P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{miss}}-0.85\,P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{vis}}
for events selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channel
before that condition is applied.

Figure 1:

(Left) Construction of the projections
P_{\zeta}^{\,\textrm{miss}}

The sum of background contributions from

The symbols
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \PGn(\Pe)}
and
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \PGn(\Pgm)}
refer to the vectorial sum of transverse momenta
of the two neutrinos produced in the respective
\Pgt\to\Pe\PGn\PGn
and
\Pgt\to\Pgm\PGn\PGn

The events selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channel are recorded using a single-muon trigger with a
p_{\mathrm{T}}
threshold of
18\GeV
.
The two muons are required to be within the acceptance of
|\eta|<2.4
, and to have opposite charge.
The muons of higher and lower
p_{\mathrm{T}}
are required to satisfy the conditions of
p_{\mathrm{T}}>20
and
>10\GeV
, respectively.
Both muons are required to pass an isolation criterion of
I_{\Pgm}<0.15\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\Pgm}
.
The large background arising from DY production of
\Pgm
pairs is reduced by requiring the mass of the two muons to satisfy
m_{\Pgm\Pgm}<80\GeV
,
and through the application of a cutoff on the output of a BDT trained to separate the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal from the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgm\Pgm
background.
The following observables are used as BDT inputs:
the ratio of the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the dimuon system to the scalar
p_{\mathrm{T}}
sum of the two muons (
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\Pgm\Pgm}/\sum p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\Pgm}
),
the pseudorapidity of the dimuon system (
\eta_{\Pgm\Pgm}
),
the
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{miss}}
,
the topological discriminant
P_{\zeta}
, computed according to Eq. (
4
),
and the azimuthal angle between the muon of positive charge and the
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
vector, denoted by the symbol
\Delta\phi(\Pgm^{+},{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}})
.
The angle between the muon of negative charge and the
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
vector,
\Delta\phi(\Pgm^{-},{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}})
,
is not used as BDT input, as it is strongly anticorrelated with
\Delta\phi(\Pgm^{+},{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}})
.

We refer to the events passing the selection criteria detailed in this Section
as belonging to the “signal region” (SR) of the analysis.

0.6
Background estimation

The accuracy of the background estimate is improved by determining from data
the contributions from the main backgrounds, as well as from backgrounds that are difficult to model through MC simulation.
In particular, the background from multijet production falls into the latter category.
In the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels, the dominant background
is from events in which a quark or gluon jet is misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
The estimation of background from these “false”
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
sources is discussed in Section
0.6.1
.
It predominantly arises from multijet production in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel
and from
\PW
+jets events, as well as from multijet production in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels.
A small additional background contribution in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels
arises from
\cPqt\cPaqt
events with quark or gluon jets misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
The multijet background is also relevant in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels.
The estimation of the multijet background in these channels is described in Section
0.6.2
.
The contribution to the SR from the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels
arising from backgrounds with misidentified leptons other than multijet production is small and
not distinguished from background contributions with genuine leptons.
Significant background contributions arise from
\cPqt\cPaqt
production in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channel
and from the DY production of muon pairs in the
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channel.
The normalization of the
\cPqt\cPaqt
background in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels is determined from data,
using a control region that contains events with one electron, one muon, and one or more
\PQb
-tagged jets.
Details of the procedure are given in Section
0.6.3
.
The
\cPqt\cPaqt
normalization factor obtained from this control region is also applied to the
\cPqt\cPaqt
background events
selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels,
in which the reconstructed
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
is either due to a genuine
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
or due to the misidentification of an electron or muon.
The background rate from
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pe\Pe
and
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgm\Pgm
production is determined from the data
through a maximum-likelihood (ML) fit of the
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
distributions in the SR,
described in Section
0.8
.
The contributions of minor backgrounds from single top quark and diboson production,
as well as a small contribution from
\PW
+jets background in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels,
are obtained from MC simulation.
The sum of these minor backgrounds is referred to as “electroweak” background.
A Higgs boson with a mass of
m_{\textrm{H}}=125\GeV
, produced at the rate and with branching fractions predicted in the SM, is considered as background.
Nevertheless, this contribution is found to be negligible.

0.6.1
Estimation of false-
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
background in
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels

The background arising from events in which a quark or gluon jet is misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels
is estimated via the “fake factor” (
F_{\textrm{F}}
) method.
The method is based on selecting events that pass altered
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria,
and weighting the events through suitably chosen extrapolation factors (the
F_{\textrm{F}}
).
The events passing the altered
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria are referred to as belonging to the “application region” (AR) of the
F_{\textrm{F}}
method.
Except for modifying the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria, the same selections are applied to events in the AR and in the SR.
The
F_{\textrm{F}}
are measured in dedicated control regions in data.
These are referred to as “determination regions” (DR) of the
F_{\textrm{F}}
method,
and are chosen such that they neither overlap with the SR nor with the AR.

The
F_{\textrm{F}}
are determined in bins of decay mode and
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate, and as a function of jet multiplicity.
In each such bin, the
F_{\textrm{F}}
is given by the ratio:

F_{\textrm{F}}=\frac{N_{\textrm{nominal}}}{N_{\textrm{altered}}}\,,

(5)

where
N_{\textrm{nominal}}
corresponds to the number of events with
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates that pass the nominal WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant in a given channel,
and
N_{\textrm{altered}}
is the number of events with
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates that satisfy the altered
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria.
To satisfy the altered
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates must satisfy the barely constrained WP, but fail the nominal WP.
The multiplicity of jets that is used to parametrize the
F_{\textrm{F}}
is denoted by
N_{\textrm{jet}}
, and is defined by the jets that satisfy the conditions
p_{\mathrm{T}}>20\GeV
and
|\eta|<4.7
,
and do not overlap with
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates passing the barely constrained WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant,
nor with electrons or muons within
\Delta R<0.5
.
In each bin, the contribution from processes with genuine
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and with electrons or muons misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
are estimated through MC simulation, and subtracted from the numerator as well as from the denominator in Eq. (
5
).

As the probabilities for jets to be misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
depend on the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria, and the latter differ in different channels,
the
F_{\textrm{F}}
are measured separately in each one of them.
Moreover, the misidentification rates differ for multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
events,
necessitating a measurement of the
F_{\textrm{F}}
in the DR enriched in contributions from multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds.
The relative fractions of multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
background processes in the AR, denoted by
R_{\textrm{p}}
,
are determined through a fit to the distribution in
m_{\mathrm{T}}
,
and are used to weight the
F_{\textrm{F}}
determined in the DR when computing the estimate of the false-
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
background in the SR.
The procedure is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

R_{\textrm{p}}

\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
background in the SR (bottom left).

p
on the symbol

indicates that the

depend on the background process
p
,
where

refers to either multijet,

\cPqt\cPaqt

\PZ/\gamma^{*}\to\tau\tau

R_{\textrm{p}}
are determined by a fit of the
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distribution in the AR (bottom right),
described in more detail in Section

.

includes a small contribution from DY events in which the reconstructed

is due to the misidentification of a quark or a gluon jet.

The
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria applied in the AR are identical to the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria applied in the denominator of Eq. (
5
).
More specifically, the criteria on
p_{\mathrm{T}}
and
\eta
, as well as the requirements on the discriminators that distinguish
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
from electrons and muons, are the same as in the SR.
The
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates selected in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels are required to pass the barely constrained,
but fail the moderately constrained WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant.
In the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel, one of the two
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates must pass the tight WP,
while the other
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate is required to pass the barely constrained, but fail the tight WP,
precluding overlap of the AR with the SR.

The DR enriched in contributions from multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds
contain specific mixtures of gluon, light-quark (
\cPqu
,
\cPqd
,
\cPqs
),
and heavy-flavour (
\cPqc
,
\cPqb
) quark jets,
with different probabilities for misidentification as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
as illustrated for simulated events in Fig.
3
.
The misidentification rates are shown for jets passing
p_{\mathrm{T}}>20\GeV
and
|\eta|<2.3
,
and for jets satisfying in addition the barely constrained WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant.
In general, the misidentification rates are higher in quark jets compared to gluon jets,
as the former typically have lower particle multiplicity and are more collimated than the latter,
thereby increasing their probability to be misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
As it can be seen in the figure, the requirement for jets to pass minimal
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
selection criteria significantly reduce the flavour dependence of the misidentification rates.
This in turn lowers
the systematic uncertainty that arises from the limited knowledge of the flavour composition in the AR.
Residual flavour dependence of the
F_{\textrm{F}}
is taken into account
by measuring separate sets of
F_{\textrm{F}}
in each DR,
and determining the relative fraction
R_{\textrm{p}}
of multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds in the AR of the respective channel.
Given the
F_{\textrm{F}}
and the fractions
R_{\textrm{p}}
,
the estimate of the background from misidentified
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
in the SR is obtained
by applying the weights

w=\sum_{\textrm{p}}\,R_{\textrm{p}}\,F_{\textrm{F}}^{\textrm{p}}

(6)

to events selected in the AR,
where the sum extends over the above three background processes
p
.
The
F_{\textrm{F}}
refer, as usual, to Eq. (
5
).
The symbol
F_{\textrm{F}}^{\textrm{p}}
indicates that, in addition to their dependence on
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
decay mode,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, and jet multiplicity,
the
F_{\textrm{F}}
depend on the background process
p
,
where the superscript
p
refers to either multijet,
\PW
+jets, or
\cPqt\cPaqt
background.
In the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel, the
F_{\textrm{F}}^{\textrm{p}}
is determined by the decay mode and
p_{\mathrm{T}}

of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

Measurement of
F_{\textrm{F}}

The
F_{\textrm{F}}
are measured in DR chosen such that one particular background process is enhanced in each DR.
The selection criteria applied in the DR are similar to those applied in the SR.
In the following, we describe only the differences relative to the SR.

In the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels,
three different DR are used to measure the
F_{\textrm{F}}
for multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds.
The DR dominated by multijet background contains events
in which the charges of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate and of the light lepton candidates are the same,
and the electron or muon satisfies a modified isolation criterion of
0.05<I_{\ell}/p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}<0.15
.
Depending on whether the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate passes or fails the moderate WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant,
the event contributes either to the numerator or to the denominator of Eq. (
5
).
The DR dominated by
\PW
+jets background is defined by
modifying the requirement for the transverse mass of lepton and
{\vec{p}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ \textrm{miss}}
to
m_{\mathrm{T}}>70\GeV
.
The contamination arising from
\cPqt\cPaqt
background is reduced by vetoing events containing jets that
pass the
\PQb
tagging criteria described in Section
0.4
.
A common
\cPqt\cPaqt
DR is used for the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels.
The events are required to contain an electron, a muon, at least one
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate,
and pass triggers based on the presence of an electron or a muon.
The offline event selection demands that
the electron satisfy the conditions
p_{\mathrm{T}}>13\GeV
and
|\eta|<2.5
,
the muon
p_{\mathrm{T}}>10\GeV
and
\lvert\eta\rvert<2.4
,
and that both pass an isolation criterion of
I_{\ell}<0.10\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
.
The event is furthermore required to contain at least one jet that passes the
\PQb
tagging criteria
described in Section
0.4
.
In case events contain multiple
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates, the candidate used for the
F_{\textrm{F}}
measurement is chosen at random.

In the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel, a single DR is used,
which selects a high purity sample of multijet events, the dominant background in this channel.
The multijet DR is identical to the SR of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel,
except that the two
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates are required to have the same rather than opposite charge.
One of the jets is chosen to be the “tag” jet, and required to pass the tight WP of the MVA-based
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID discriminant,
while the measurement of the
F_{\textrm{F}}
is performed on the other jet, referred to as the “probe” jet.
The tag jet is chosen at random.
The
\PW
+jets and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds are small in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel,
making it difficult to define a DR that is dominated by these backgrounds,
or that provides sufficient statistical information for the
F_{\textrm{F}}
measurement.
The
F_{\textrm{F}}
in the multijet DR of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel are therefore used to weight all events selected in the AR of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel.
Differences in the
F_{\textrm{F}}
between
\PW
+jets,
\cPqt\cPaqt
, and multijet events are accounted for by adding a systematic uncertainty of
30\%

on the part of the background from misidentified
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
expected
from the contribution of
\PW
+jets and
\cPqt\cPaqt
background processes.
This contribution is estimated using MC simulation,
and the magnitude of the systematic uncertainty is motivated by the difference found in the
F_{\textrm{F}}
measured in multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
DR
in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels.

The
F_{\textrm{F}}
determined in the various DR are shown in Figs.
4

5

The decay modes
\Pgt^{-}\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGn_{\Pgt}

Determination of
R_{\textrm{p}}

In the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels,
the relative fractions
R_{\textrm{p}}
of multijet,
\PW
+jets, and
\cPqt\cPaqt
backgrounds in the AR are determined through
a fit to the distribution in
m_{\mathrm{T}}
.
The distribution in
m_{\mathrm{T}}
(“template”) used to represent the multijet background in the fit
is obtained from a sample of events selected in data,
in which the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate and the electron or muon have same charge, and where at least one of the leptons satisfies
a modified isolation criterion of
0.05<I_{\ell}/p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}<0.15
.
The contributions from other backgrounds to this control region are subtracted, based on MC simulation.
The distribution representing the other backgrounds in the fit are also taken from simulation.
The templates for
\cPqt\cPaqt
, diboson, and DY events are split into three components:
events in which the reconstructed
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
is due to a genuine
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
events in which the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
is due to the misidentification of an electron or muon,
and events in which a quark or gluon jet is misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
The normalization of each component is determined independently in the fit.
The relative fractions of the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal and all individual background processes are left unconstrained in the fit.
Finally, the fractions
R_{\textrm{p}}
are parametrized as function of
m_{\mathrm{T}}
and are normalized
such that the contribution of all processes
p
in which the reconstructed
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

is due to a misidentified jet sums to unity,
\sum_{\textrm{p}}\,R_{\textrm{p}}=1
.

In the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel,
the AR is dominated by multijet background.
The contributions from the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal and all background processes, except multijet production, are small
and taken from simulation.
The fraction of multijet background in the AR is determined by subtracting the sum of all processes modelled in the MC simulation
from the data in the AR, without performing a fit in this channel.

A small fraction of events in the AR of the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels
arises from DY events in which quark or gluon jets are misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates.
These events are treated as background and are included in the false-
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
estimate using the
F_{\textrm{F}}
method.
As the analysed data do not provide a way of determining
F_{\textrm{F}}
in DY events with sufficient statistical accuracy,
the
F_{\textrm{F}}
measured in
\PW
+jets events are used instead
for the fraction of DY events with jets misidentified as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels.
The validity of this procedure is justified by studies of
F_{\textrm{F}}
in simulated
\PW
+jets and DY events,
which indicate that the flavour composition of jets and the
F_{\textrm{F}}
are very similar in these events.
In the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel, the
F_{\textrm{F}}
measured in multijet events are used and the systematic uncertainty
on the DY background with misidentified
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
is increased by
30\%
.

0.6.2
Estimation of multijet background in
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels

The contributions from multijet background in the SR of the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
or
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
channels
are estimated using control regions containing events with an electron and muon or two muons of same charge, respectively.
An estimate for the contribution from multijet events in the SR is obtained
by scaling the yield of the multijet background in the SS control region by a suitably chosen extrapolation factor, defined
by the ratio of
\Pe\Pgm
or
\Pgm\Pgm
pairs with opposite charge
to those with same charge.
The ratio is measured in events in which at least one lepton passes an inverted isolation criterion of
I_{\ell}>0.15\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
.
We refer to this event sample as an isolation sideband region (SB).
The requirement
I_{\ell}>0.15\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
ensures that the SB does not overlap with the SR.
A complication arises from the fact that the ratio of OS to SS pairs depends on
the lepton kinematics and the isolation criterion used in the SB.
The nominal OS/SS ratio is measured in an isolation sideband (SB1)
defined by requiring both leptons to satisfy a relaxed isolation criterion of
I_{\ell}<0.60\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
,
with at least one lepton passing the condition
I_{\ell}>0.15\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
.
The systematic uncertainty in the OS/SS ratio that arises from the choice of the upper limit on
I_{\ell}
applied in SB1
is estimated by taking the difference between the OS/SS ratio computed in SB1 and the ratio computed in a different isolation sideband region (SB2).
The latter is defined by requiring at least one lepton to pass the condition
I_{\ell}>0.60\,p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\ell}
, without setting an
upper limit on
I_{\ell}
in the SB2 region.
The criteria to select events in the isolation sidebands are optimized to ensure high statistical accuracy in
the measurement of the OS/SS extrapolation factor
and at the same time the minimization of differences in lepton kinematic distributions between the SR and the SB.
In both isolation sidebands,
the OS/SS ratio is measured as function of
p_{\mathrm{T}}
of the two leptons
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}

and of their separation
\Delta R(\ell,\ell^{\prime})=\sqrt{(\eta_{\ell}-\eta_{\ell^{\prime}})^{2}+(\phi_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell^{\prime}})^{2}}
in the
\eta
-
\phi
plane.
The contributions to the SS control region, as well as to SB1 and SB2, from backgrounds other than multijet production
are subtracted, based on results from MC simulation.

0.6.3
Estimation of
\cPqt\cPaqt
background

While the
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
distribution for
\cPqt\cPaqt
background is obtained from MC simulation,
the event yield in the
\cPqt\cPaqt
background in the SR is determined from data, using a control region dominated by
\cPqt\cPaqt
background.
Events in the
\cPqt\cPaqt

\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}

.
The main differences are that the cutoff on

,
and a condition

is added to the event selection in the
\cPqt\cPaqt
control region.
The
\cPqt\cPaqt
event yield observed in the control region is a

multiple of the expectation from the MC simulation.
The ratio of the
\cPqt\cPaqt
event yield measured in data to the MC prediction is applied as a scale factor to simulated
\cPqt\cPaqt
events,
to correct the
\cPqt\cPaqt
background yield in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}

as well as to correct the part of the
\cPqt\cPaqt

\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

that is either due to genuine
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
or due to the misidentification of an electron or muon as
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

The latter is not included in the background estimate obtained through the
F_{\textrm{F}}
method, but modelled in the MC simulation.

0.7
Systematic uncertainties

Imprecisely measured or imperfectly simulated effects can alter the normalization and distribution of the
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
mass spectrum in
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal or background processes.
These systematic uncertainties can be categorized into theory-related and experimental sources.
The latter can be further subdivided into those associated with the reconstruction of physical objects of interest
and with estimated backgrounds.
The uncertainties related to the reconstruction of physical objects apply to the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal and to backgrounds
modelled in the MC simulation.
The main background contributions are determined from data, as described in Section
0.6
,
and are largely unaffected by the accuracy achieved in modelling data in the MC simulation.

The main experimental uncertainties are related to the reconstruction and identification of electrons, muons, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, as follows.
The efficiency to reconstruct and identify
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
and the energy scale of
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
(
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES)
is measured using
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt\to\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
events.
The former is done by comparing the number of
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt\to\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
events
with
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates passing and failing the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID criteria,
and the latter by comparing the distributions in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidate mass,
as well as the visible mass of the muon and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
system in data and in MC simulation
[
75
]
,
measured with respective uncertainties of
{\approx}6
and
{\approx}1\%
.
The events selected for the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID efficiency and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES measurements overlap with the events in the
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel.
We account for the overlap by assigning a
3\%
uncertainty to
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES.
A
3\%
change in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES affects the acceptance in
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal by
3
,
3
, and
17\%

in the
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels, respectively.
The impact on the signal acceptance and on the distribution in
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}

is illustrated in Fig.
7
.
It has been checked that the overlap and the choice in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES uncertainty have little impact on the final results.
The ML fit performed to measure the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
cross section, described in Section
0.8
, reduces the uncertainties
in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ID efficiency and in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES to
2.2
and
0.9\%
, respectively.
The efficiency of the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
trigger used in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel is measured in
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt\to\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
events
with an uncertainty of
{\approx}4.5\%
per
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

[

.

0.8
Signal extraction

The cross section
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\rightarrow\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\text{+X})\,\mathcal{B}(\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt)
for DY production of
\Pgt
pairs
is obtained through a simultaneous ML fit to the observed
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
distributions in the five decay channels:

\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
, and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
.
The likelihood function
\mathcal{L}\left(\text{data}\,|\,\xi,\Theta\right)

depends on the value of the cross section, denoted by the symbol
\xi
, which defines the parameter of interest (POI) in the fit,
and it also depends on the values of nuisance parameters
\theta_{k}

that represent the systematic uncertainties discussed in Section
0.7
:

\mathcal{L}\left(\text{data}\,|\,\xi,\Theta\right)=\prod_{i}\,\mathcal{P}\left(n_{i}|\xi,\Theta\right)\,\prod_{k}\,\rho\left(\tilde{\theta}_{k}|\theta_{k}\right).

(7)

The index
i
refers to individual bins of the
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
distribution in each of the five final states.
The set of all nuisance parameters
\theta_{k}
is denoted by the symbol
\Theta
.
Correlations among decay channels as well as between the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal and background processes
are taken into account through relationships among
channels, processes, and nuisance parameters in the ML fit.
The probability to observe
n_{i}
events in a given bin
i
, when
\nu_{i}(\xi,\Theta)
events are expected in that bin
is given by the Poisson distribution:

\mathcal{P}\left(n_{i}|\xi,\Theta\right)=\frac{\left(\nu_{i}(\xi,\Theta)\right)^{n_{i}}}{n_{i}!}\,\exp\left(-\nu_{i}(\xi,\Theta)\right).

(8)

The number of events expected in each bin corresponds to the sum of the number of signal (
\nu_{i}^{\textrm{S}}
) and background (
\nu_{i}^{\textrm{B}}
) events:

\nu_{i}(\xi,\Theta)=\nu_{i}^{\textrm{S}}(\xi,\Theta)+\nu_{i}^{\textrm{B}}(\Theta)
.
The estimate in the number of background events is obtained as described in Section
0.6
.
The number of signal events is proportional to
\xi
, with the
coefficient of proportionality depending on the signal acceptance and on the signal selection efficiency, with both obtained from MC simulation.

The function
\rho\left(\tilde{\theta}_{k}|\theta_{k}\right)
represents the probability to
observe a value
\tilde{\theta}_{k}
in an auxiliary measurement of the nuisance parameter,
given that the true value is
\theta_{k}
.
The nuisance parameters are treated via the frequentist paradigm,
as described in Refs.
[
95
,
96
]
.
Systematic uncertainties that affect only the normalization, but not the distribution in
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}
,
are represented by the Gamma function if they are statistical in origin,
e.g. corresponding to the number of events observed in a control region,
and otherwise by log-normal probability density functions.
Systematic uncertainties that affect the distribution in
m_{\Pgt\Pgt}

are incorporated into the ML fit via the technique detailed in Ref.
[
97
]
,
and represented by Gaussian probability density functions.
Nuisance parameters representing systematic uncertainties of the latter type
can also affect the normalization of the
\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt
signal or of its backgrounds.
The nuisance parameters corresponding to the cross sections for DY production of electron and muon pairs are left unconstrained in the fit.

The best fit value
\hat{\xi}
of the POI is the value that maximizes the likelihood
\mathcal{L}\left(\text{data}\,|\,\xi,\Theta\right)

in Eq. (
7
).
A
68\%
confidence interval (CI) on the POI is obtained using the profile likelihood ratio (PLR)
[
95
,
96
,
98
]
:

\lambda\left(\xi\right)=\frac{\mathcal{L}\left(\text{data}\,|\,\xi,\hat{\Theta}_{\xi}\right)}{\mathcal{L}\left(\text{data}\,|\,\hat{\xi},\hat{\Theta}\right)}.

(9)

The symbol
\hat{\Theta}_{\xi}
denotes the values of nuisance parameters that maximize the likelihood
for a given value of
\xi
.
The combination of
\hat{\xi}
and
\hat{\Theta}
correspond to the values of
\xi
and
\Theta
for which the likelihood function reaches its maximum.
The
68\%
CI is defined by the values of
\xi
for which
-2\ln\lambda\left(\xi\right)
increases by one unit relative to its minimum.
To quantify the effects from individual statistical uncertainties,
the uncertainty in the integrated luminosity,
and other systematic uncertainties,
we ignore some single source of uncertainties at a time, and recompute the
68\%
CI.
The nuisance parameters
\theta_{k}
corresponding to uncertainties that are ignored are fixed at the values
\hat{\theta}_{k}
that yield the best fit to the data.
The square root of the quadratic difference between the CI,
computed for all sources of uncertainties in the fit,
and for the case that some given source is ignored,
reflects the estimate of the uncertainty in the POI resulting from a single source.
The procedure is illustrated in Fig.
9
for the combined fit of all five final states.
Correlations among different sources of uncertainty are estimated through this procedure.

\Pgt
pairs, the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}

\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
ES are left unconstrained in the fit,

0.10
Summary

The cross section for inclusive Drell–Yan production of
\Pgt
pairs has been measured
using
\Pp\Pp
collisions recorded by the CMS experiment at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
at the LHC.
The analysed data correspond to an integrated luminosity of
2.3~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
.
The signal yield was determined in a global fit to the mass distributions in five
\Pgt\Pgt
decay channels:

\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\Pgt_{\Pe}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
, and
\Pgt_{\Pgm}\Pgt_{\Pgm}
.
The measured cross section times branching fraction

\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\text{+X})\,\mathcal{B}(\cPZ/\PGg^{*}\to\Pgt\Pgt)=1848\pm 12\stat\pm 57\syst\pm 35\lum\unit{pb}

is in agreement with the standard model expectation, computed at next-to-next-to-leading order accuracy in perturbation theory.
As a byproduct of the global fit,
the efficiency for reconstructing and identifying the decays of
\Pgt
leptons to hadrons (
\Pgt\to\mbox{hadrons}+\PGn_{\Pgt}
),
as well as the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
energy scale, have been determined.
The results from data agree with Monte Carlo simulation
within the uncertainties of the measurement,
amounting to
2.2\%
relative uncertainty in the
\Pgt_{\mathrm{h}}
identification efficiency, and
0.9\%
in the energy scale.

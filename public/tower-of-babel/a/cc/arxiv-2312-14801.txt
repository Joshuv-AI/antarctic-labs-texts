Stabilized SQP Methods in Hilbert Spaces
Andrian Uihlein 1 and Winnifried Wollner 2

Abstract
Based on techniques by (S.J. Wright 1998) for finite-dimensional optimization, we investigate a stabilized sequential quadratic programming method for nonlinear optimization problems in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. The method is shown to achieve fast local convergence even in the absence of a constraint qualification, generalizing the results obtained by (S.J. Wright 1998 and W.W. Hager 1999) in finite dimensions to this broader setting.

, give rise to nonlinear optimization problems of infinite dimension. Due to the prominence of such problems, a variety of solution techniques has been developed over the years, e.g., projected gradient descent methods

, sequential quadratic programming (SQP) algorithms
[
14

, or penalty and barrier methods
[

. An overview of various general first and second order methods is given in
[

.

Given two real Hilbert spaces
X
,
Y
and a subset
K\subseteq Y
, the general infinite-dimensional optimization problem can be stated as

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle f(x)

(P)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle G(x)\in K\subseteq Y,

where
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the objective function and
G:X\to Y
encapsulates the optimization constraints.

In this contribution, we want to focus on SQP methods, which aim to find a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point
[
16
,
17
]
of (
P
) by iteratively solving quadratic subproblems. Due to the intrinsic connection of the KKT conditions and constraint qualifications, convergence results concerning SQP methods mostly rely on the Robinson constraint qualification
[
18
]
, i.e., it is required that a KKT point
x_{\ast}
of (
P
) satisfies

0\in\interior\big(G(x_{\ast})+G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})X-K\big),

(RCQ)

where
G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})
denotes the Fréchet derivative of
G
at
x_{\ast}
. In the special case
K=\{0\}
, which corresponds to purely equality constrained optimization problems, Robinson’s constraint qualification is satisfied if and only if
G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})
is surjective. This, however, is a harsh condition and is violated in many applications
[
11
,
19
,
20
]
.

In the finite-dimensional setting, the stabilized SQP algorithm proposed in
[
21
]
reformulates the quadratic subproblems as saddle point problems and augments them by an additional term for the Lagrange multiplier. The proposed method was proven to converge superlinearly under the assumption that MFCQ
[
22
]
is satisfied. Subsequently, it was shown in
[
23
]
that the method in fact converges quadratically without the need for a constraint qualification, as long as the weight for the augmenting term is chosen appropriately. Estimates for said parameter were stated in
[
24
]
. Recently, in
[
25
]
, a generalized version of the stabilized SQP method was proposed for Banach spaces and global convergence results were established.

In contrast, following the ideas presented in
[
23
]
, we are able to prove an infinite-dimensional analogue of the local quadratic convergence result, provided that
G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})
is a Fredholm operator. In the case of purely equality constrained optimization problems, this assumption is much weaker than (
RCQ
) and in fact holds trivially in the finite-dimensional setting. Thus, the main result presented in this contribution can be seen as the direct generalization of
[
23
, Theorem 1]
to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Furthermore, we generalize the estimates provided for the augmentation parameter as well.

Note that many of the proofs presented in this contribution follow key arguments made in
[
23
]
. While some intermediate results require only minor changes for their generalization to our infinite-dimensional setting, for the sake of self-containedness, we still include the respective arguments and refrain from excessively referencing the reader to
[
23
]
.

Assumptions and notation

For real Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, we denote by
\mathfrak{L}(X,Y)
and
\mathfrak{F}(X,Y)
the space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
and Fredholm operators respectively. The dual space to
X
is defined as
X^{\ast}\defas\mathfrak{L}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{N}(A)
and
\mathcal{R}(A)
denote the kernel and range of
A\in\mathfrak{L}(X,Y)
.

For
A\in\mathfrak{L}(X,Y)
, we denote by
A^{\ast}
the adjoint of
A
, i.e.,
A^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{L}(Y^{\ast},X^{\ast})
satisfies

\left\langle y^{\ast},Ax\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}=\left\langle A^{\ast}y^{\ast},x\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\quad\text{for all }(x,y^{\ast})\in X\times Y^{\ast},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}
denote the respective duality pairings.
Furthermore, we denote by
\R:Y^{\ast}\to Y
the Riesz isomorphism between
Y^{\ast}
and
Y
, i.e.,

\left(\R\lambda,y\right)_{Y}=\left\langle\lambda,y\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}\quad\text{for all }(\lambda,y)\in Y^{\ast}\times Y,

with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{Y}
on
Y
.
Observe that, for all
\lambda\in Y^{\ast}
, we have

\|\lambda\|_{Y^{\ast}}^{2}=\|\R\lambda\|_{Y}^{2}=\left(\R\lambda,\R\lambda\right)_{Y}=\left\langle\lambda,\R\lambda\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}.

Given a subset
M\subseteq X
, we introduce the annihilator

M^{\text{An}}\defas\left\{x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}\,:\,\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}=0\text{ for all }x\in M\right\},

orthogonal complement

M^{\perp}\defas\left\{\bar{x}\in X\,:\,(x,\bar{x})_{X}=0\text{ for all }x\in M\right\}

and polar cone

M^{\circ}\defas\left\{x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}\,:\,\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\leq 0\text{ for all }x\in M\right\}.

Henceforth,
c
denotes a generic constant that might take a
different value in each appearance, but does not depend on the
relevant quantities.

Our first set of general assumptions for the optimization problem (
P
) can then be stated as follows:

Assumption 2.1
.

(A1)

X
and
Y
are real Hilbert spaces.

(A2)

There exist
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\in Y
such that
K=\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}y_{i}\,:\,\mu_{i}\geq 0\}
.

(A3)

f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
G\colon X\to Y
are (locally) twice Lipschitz continuously Fréchet differentiable.

Furthermore, if we want to guarantee convergence for the stabilized SQP method, we need to impose additional conditions for an optimal solution
x_{\ast}
to (
P
):

Assumption 2.2
.

(A4)

For a local solution
x_{\ast}
to (
P
), we have

G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})\in\mathfrak{F}(X,Y)
and
G(x_{\ast})=0
.

(A5)

We have
K\subseteq\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1})
.

Remark 1
.

As an important special case to (
P
), consider an equality constrained setting, i.e.,
K=\{0\}
. Thus,
(A2)
and
(A5)
hold trivially and the set of associated multipliers simplifies to

\Lambda(x_{\ast})=\left\{\lambda\in Y^{\ast}\,:\,G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})^{\ast}\lambda+f^{\prime}(x_{\ast})=0\right\}.

Introducing the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}:X\times Y^{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda)\defas f(x)+\left\langle\lambda,G(x)\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y},

we recall the following second-order sufficient condition
[
26
, Theorem 5.6]
:

Definition 2.3
.

A local solution
x_{\ast}\in X
to (
P
) satisfies the second-order sufficient condition, if there exist
\alpha,\eta>0
and an associated multiplier
\lambda_{\ast}\in\Lambda(x_{\ast})
such that

\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{xx}(x_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})d,d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\geq\alpha\|d\|_{X}^{2}\quad\text{for all }d\in\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(x_{\ast}),

(SOSC)

where

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(x_{\ast})\defas\left\{d\in X\,:\,G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})d\in\mathcal{T}\big(K,G(x_{\ast})\big),\text{ }\langle f^{\prime}(x_{\ast}),d\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\leq\eta\|d\|_{X}\right\}

is the approximate critical cone and

\mathcal{T}(K,G(x_{*}))\defas\left\{d\in X\,:\,d=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{x_{n}-G(x_{\ast})}{t_{n}},\;t_{n}\searrow 0,\;(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in K\right\}

denotes the (Bouligand) tangent cone.

Remark 2
.

Assuming
(A1)
-
(A5)
and (
SOSC
), it holds for all
d\in\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast}))

d\in\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(x_{\ast})\Longleftrightarrow\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{\ast}),d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\leq\eta\|d\|_{X}.

Now, by definition of
\Lambda(x_{\ast})
, we have

0=\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{\ast}),d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}+\left\langle G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})^{\ast}\lambda_{\ast},d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}=\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{\ast}),d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}+\left(\lambda_{\ast},G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})d\right)_{X}.

Thus,
\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{\ast}),d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}=0
for all
d\in\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast}))
, showing that
\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast}))\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(x_{\ast})
for all
\eta>0
. Hence,
(A1)
-
(A5)
and (
SOSC
) imply the existence of
\alpha>0
, such that

\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{xx}(x_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})d,d\right\rangle_{X^{\ast},X}\geq\alpha\|d\|_{X}^{2}\quad\text{for all }d\in\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast})).

(1)

In fact, we could work with the weaker condition (
1
) instead of (
SOSC
) for the remainder of this contribution. However, since (
SOSC
) is commonly used in the literature, we refrain from doing so. Also, note that (
SOSC
) and (

) are equivalent if
K=\{0\}
.

The stabilized SQP subproblem

Assuming
(A1)
-
(A3)
, the generalized auxiliary problem considered in the stabilized SQP algorithm is given by

\min_{x\in Z}\max_{\lambda\in K^{\circ}}\quad L_{k}(x,\lambda).

(2)

where

\displaystyle L_{k}(x,\lambda)\defas

\displaystyle\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{k},\lambda_{k})(z-z_{k}),z-z_{k}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\lambda,G(z_{k})+G^{\prime}(z_{k})(z-z_{k})\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}-\frac{\rho_{k}}{2}\|\lambda-\lambda_{k}\|^{2}_{Y^{\ast}}

for given
z_{k}\in Z
,
\lambda_{k}\in K^{\circ}
and
\rho_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
The basic outline of the local stabilized SQP method, generalized to an infinite-dimensional setting, is given in
Algorithm

1
.

Algorithm 1

Generalized local stabilized SQP method

Choose
z_{0}\in Z
and
\lambda_{0}\in Y
.

for

k=0,1,2,\ldots

do

if

(z_{k},\lambda_{k})
is a KKT point of (
P
)
then

STOP

else

Calculate

(z_{k+1},\lambda_{k+1})
as a solution to (
2
);

end

if

end

for

Our analysis of
Algorithm

1
is based on the first-order optimality conditions
[
27
, Proposition VI.1.6]

of (
2
).
To be precise, every local solution
(z_{k+1},\lambda_{k+1})
to (
2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{k},\lambda_{k+1})+\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{k},\lambda_{k})(z_{k+1}-z_{k})

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle G(z_{k})+G^{\prime}(z_{k})(z_{k+1}-z_{k})-\rho_{k}\mathfrak{R}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\in K,

(4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}

\displaystyle\in K^{\circ},

(5)

\displaystyle G(z_{k})+G^{\prime}(z_{k})(z_{k+1}-z_{k})-\rho_{k}\mathfrak{R}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\in\{\lambda_{k+1}\}^{\text{An}}.

(6)

Conversely, if
(z_{k+1},\lambda_{k+1})
satisfies
(
3
)-(

,
\lambda_{k+1}

, though

need not be a local minimum of

.

We will therefore reformulate these conditions as a variational
inclusion problem, which allows
us to switch our viewpoint according to what is more convenient for a
given situation.
Based on (
3

T(z,\lambda,\underline{z},\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2})\defas\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(\underline{z},\lambda)+\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(\underline{z},\underline{\lambda}_{1})(z-\underline{z})\\

z,\underline{z}\in Z

Find
(z,\lambda)\in Z\times Y^{\ast}
such that for
p\defas(\underline{z},\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2})

T(z,\lambda,p)\in\begin{pmatrix}0\\
K\cap\{\lambda\}^{\text{An}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda\in K^{\circ}.

(7)

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
(A1)
. Let
B\in\mathfrak{F}(Z,Y)
. Suppose
Q\in\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})
satisfies

\left\langle Qz_{1},z_{2}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}=\left\langle Qz_{2},z_{1}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\quad\textnormal{for all
}z_{1},z_{2}\in Z

and

\left\langle Qz,z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\geq\alpha\|z\|_{Z}^{2}\quad\textnormal{for all }z\in\mathcal{N}(B)

(11)

for some

Then
\mathcal{A}\colon Z\times Y^{\ast}\to Z^{\ast}\times Y

\mathcal{A}(z,y)\defas\begin{pmatrix}Qz+B^{\ast}y\\
Bz\end{pmatrix},

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{A}\in\mathfrak{L}(Z\times Y^{\ast},Z^{\ast}\times Y)

Let
(z_{0},y_{0})\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle Qz_{0},z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\left\langle B^{\ast}y_{0},z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}=0

\displaystyle\left\langle y^{\ast},Bz_{0}\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}=0

\displaystyle\textnormal{for all }y^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}.

Especially, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle Qz_{0},z_{0}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\left\langle B^{\ast}y_{0},z_{0}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Qz_{0},z_{0}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\left\langle y_{0},Bz_{0}\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A})=\{0\}\times\mathcal{N}(B^{\ast}),

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
(A1)
. Suppose that
B\in\mathfrak{F}(Z,Y)
and let
Q\in\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})
satisfy

\left\langle Qz_{1},z_{2}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}=\left\langle Qz_{2},z_{1}\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\quad\text{for all }z_{1},z_{2}\in Z

and

\left\langle Qz,z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\geq\alpha\|z\|_{Z}^{2}\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathcal{N}(B).

(12)

Then given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist neighborhoods
\mathcal{B}
of
B
and
\mathcal{Q}
of
Q
and
\sigma>0
such that

\left\langle\Big(\tilde{Q}+\frac{1}{\rho}(\tilde{B})^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}\tilde{B}\Big)z,z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\geq(\alpha-\varepsilon)\|z\|_{Z}^{2}

for all

,

,
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}
, and
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

The proof follows the ideas presented in
[

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{N}(B)
is a closed subspace of
Z
, we can use the decomposition

Z=\mathcal{N}(B)\oplus\mathcal{N}(B)^{\perp}.

Therefore, we may split each
z\in Z
uniquely into
z=u+v
, where
u\in\mathcal{N}(B)
and
v\in\mathcal{N}(B)^{\perp}
. For
u\in\mathcal{N}(B)
we have

\left\langle\Big(Q+\frac{1}{\rho}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}B\Big)u,u\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\geq\alpha\|u\|_{Z}^{2}

by (
12
). Since
B
is Fredholm, there exists
\tau>0
such that

\|Bz\|_{Y}\geq\tau\|z\|_{Z}\quad\text{for all
}z\in\mathcal{N}(B)^{\perp},

see, e.g.,
[
29
, page 181, (17)]
.
Now, for
v\in\mathcal{N}(B)^{\perp}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(Q+\rho^{-1}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}B\big)v,v\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle=\langle Qv,v\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left\langle\mathfrak{R}^{-1}Bv,Bv\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}

\displaystyle=\langle Qv,v\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\frac{\|Bv\|_{Y}^{2}}{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\|v\|_{Z}^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho}\|v\|_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho}-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\right)\|v\|_{Z}^{2}.

Therefore, for arbitrary
z\in Z
, we have

\displaystyle\big\langle\big(Q+\rho^{-1}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}

\displaystyle B\big)z,z\big\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\big(Q+\rho^{-1}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}B\big)(u+v),(u+v)\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle=\langle Qu,u\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+\left\langle\big(Q+\rho^{-1}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}B\big)v,v\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}+2\langle Qv,u\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|u\|_{Z}^{2}+\left(\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho}-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\right)\|v\|_{Z}^{2}-2\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\|u\|_{Z}\|v\|_{Z}.

Using a variant of Young’s inequality

ab\leq\delta a^{2}+b^{2}/4\delta\quad\textnormal{for all $\delta>0$ and
all $a,b\geq 0$},

with
a=\|u\|_{Z}
and
b=2\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\|v\|_{Z}
yields

2\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}\|u\|_{Z}\|v\|_{Z}\leq\delta\|u\|_{Z}^{2}+\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}^{2}\|v\|_{Z}^{2}/\delta.

Inserting into the above inequality gives

\displaystyle\big\langle\big(Q+\rho^{-1}B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}

\displaystyle B\big)z,z\big\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha-\delta)\|u\|_{Z}^{2}+\left(\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho}-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}-\frac{\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}^{2}}{\delta}\right)\|v\|_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\alpha-\delta,\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho}-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}-\frac{\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}^{2}}{\delta}\right\}\big(\|u\|_{Z}^{2}+\|v\|_{Z}^{2}\big).

Choosing
\sigma>0
small enough that

\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sigma}-\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}-\frac{\|Q\|_{\mathfrak{L}(Z,Z^{\ast})}^{2}}{\delta}\geq\alpha-\delta,

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(Q+B^{\ast}\mathfrak{R}^{-1}B/\rho\big)z,z\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha-\delta)\big(\|u\|_{Z}^{2}+\|v\|_{Z}^{2}\big)

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{k},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})=\underbrace{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{\ast},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})}_{=0}+\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{\ast},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})(z_{k}-z_{\ast})+\mathcal{O}(\|z_{\ast}-z_{k}\|_{Z}^{2}).

Substituting into (
53
), we obtain

F(z_{\ast})=\begin{pmatrix}\big[\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{k},\lambda_{k})-\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{\ast},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})\big](z_{\ast}-z_{k})\\
\big[G^{\prime}(z_{k})-G^{\prime}(z_{\ast})\big](z_{\ast}-z_{k})-\rho_{k}\mathfrak{R}(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\lambda_{k})\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\|z_{\ast}-z_{k}\|_{Z}^{2}).

Taking norms and utilizing the Lipschitz continuity of all occurring derivatives yields

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle=\|F(z_{\ast})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\|z_{k}-z_{\ast}\|_{Z}^{2}+\|\lambda_{k}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\|z_{k}-z_{\ast}\|_{Z}+\rho_{k}\|\lambda_{k}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\Big)

\displaystyle=c\overline{E}_{k},

showing (
41
). Now, applying Young’s inequality gives

E_{k}\leq c\overline{E}_{k}\leq c\Big(\|z_{k}-z_{\ast}\|_{Z}^{2}+\|\lambda_{k}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}^{2}+\rho_{k}\|\lambda_{k}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\Big)=c\overline{\overline{E}}_{k},

which proves (
42
).
On the other hand, estimating
E_{k}
with the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{k},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})\|_{Z^{\ast}}+\|\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{k},\lambda_{k})(z_{\ast}-z_{k})\|_{Z^{\ast}}+\|G(z_{k})-G(z_{\ast})\|_{Y}

\displaystyle+\|G^{\prime}(z_{\ast})(z_{\ast}-z_{k})\|_{Y}+\rho_{k}\|\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\lambda_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}.

By local Lipschitz continuity of
G(\cdot)
,
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{k},\lambda_{k})\cdot
and
G^{\prime}(z_{\ast})\cdot
, we get

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{k},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})\|_{Z^{\ast}}+c\big(\|z_{\ast}-z_{k}\|_{Z}+\|\widehat{\lambda}-\lambda_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{z}(z_{k},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})-\underbrace{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(z_{\ast},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})}_{=0}\|_{Z^{\ast}}+c\big(\|z_{\ast}-z_{k}\|_{Z}+\|\widehat{\lambda}-\lambda_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\|z_{\ast}-z_{k}\|_{Z}+\|\widehat{\lambda}-\lambda_{k}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\big).

Therefore, we also have
E_{k}^{2}\leq c\overline{\overline{E}}_{k}
.
∎

As (

) shows, choosing

according to the total error in the current step is needed to guarantee local quadratic convergence. To obtain a suitable bound to said quantity, we follow the ideas presented in
[
24
]
to show that

can be chosen proportional to the known quantity

.

Notation 1
.

Under the assumptions of

4.1
, we set
Q\defas\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(z_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})
and

. Furthermore, we define
\mathcal{F}\colon Z\times Y^{\ast}\to Z^{\ast}\times Y

Bz\end{pmatrix}

(54)

\text{For $\psi\in Z^{\ast}\times Y$, find $(z,\lambda)$ such that

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a solution to (

with the property that if

Proof.

Given

and

to obtain

\|\psi-\bar{\psi}\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}=\|\mathcal{F}(z,\lambda)-\mathcal{F}(\bar{z},\bar{\lambda})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}=\|\mathcal{F}(z-\bar{z},\lambda-\bar{\lambda})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}.

For

(56)

and write
\lambda-\bar{\lambda}=y+y^{\perp}.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(z-\bar{z},\lambda-\bar{\lambda})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{F}(z-\bar{z},y+y^{\perp})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{F}(z-\bar{z},y^{\perp})+\mathcal{F}(0,y)\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}.

Since

\|\mathcal{F}(z-\bar{z},y^{\perp})\|_{Z^{\ast}\times Y}\geq\tau\big(\|z-\bar{z}\|_{Z}+\|y^{\perp}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\big)\geq\tau\|z-\bar{z}\|_{Z}

6
Application

As an application for our setting, we consider the following optimal control problem:

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle J(u,q)\defas\frac{1}{2}\|u-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|q-q_{d}|^{2},

(69)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle-\Delta u+qu=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Here,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is a bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary,
q\in\mathbb{R}
is the control variable and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is the state variable. Note that we do not require
q
to be positive, and hence
-q
can be an eigenvalue of
-\Delta
. Therefore, in an optimal solution
(u_{\ast},q_{\ast})
to (
69
), the state equation need not be uniquely solvable. In this case, (
RCQ
) can not be satisfied.

From standard results concerning elliptic equations it is known that for each
q\in\mathbb{R}
, the state equation in (
69
) admits at least one solution and every solution lies in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence, we set
Z\defas\big(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\times\mathbb{R}
,
Y\defas L^{2}(\Omega)
,
K=\{0\}
and define
G:Z\to Y
,

G(u,q)\defas-\Delta u+qu.

It is obvious that
(A1)
-
(A3)
are satisfied. Furthermore, since
K=\{0\}
,
Remark

1
yields that
(A5)
holds as well. Thus, we will proceed by showing that (
69
) has at least one local solution and that each local solution satisfies
(A4)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For arbitrary
(u_{d},q_{d})\in Z
, there exists at least one local solution to (
69
).

Proof.

We start by proving that
G:Z\to Y
is weakly continuous. For this purpose, let
(u_{n},q_{n})\rightharpoonup(\bar{u},\bar{q})
, i.e.,
q_{n}\rightarrow\bar{q}
in
\mathbb{R}
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Now, for arbitrary
y^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}
it holds

\displaystyle\big\langle y^{\ast},G(u_{n},q_{n})

\displaystyle-G(\bar{u},\bar{q})\big\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}

\displaystyle=\big\langle y^{\ast},-\Delta(u_{n}-\bar{u})\big\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}+q_{n}\left\langle y^{\ast},u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}+(q_{n}-\bar{q})\left\langle y^{\ast},\bar{u}\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}.

Since
-\Delta
is linear and
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}
is bounded, the expression on the right hand side tends to 0, showing that
G:Z\to Y
is weakly continuous.

Now, since
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous,
K
is weakly closed and
G:Z\to Y
is weakly continuous, it suffices to show that the level set

\mathfrak{N}(0,0)\defas\left\{(u,q)\in Z\,:\,G(u,q)\in K,J(u,q)\leq J(0,0)\right\}

is bounded in
Z
.

For every sequence
(q^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
|q^{k}|\to\infty
, we have
J(u,q^{k})\to\infty
for all
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, showing that the
q
-component is bounded. Analogously, the
u
-component is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, every
(u,q)\in\mathfrak{N}(0,0)
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta u=-qu

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Therefore, elliptic regularity yields

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\big(1+|q|\big)\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

showing that the
u
-component is also bounded in
H^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Next up, we show that
G^{\prime}(u,q):Z\to Y
, given by

G^{\prime}(u,q)[d_{u},d_{q}]=-\Delta d_{u}+qd_{u}+ud_{q},

is Fredholm.

Lemma 6.2
.

For all
(u,q)\in Z
, we have
G^{\prime}(u,q)\in\mathfrak{F}(Z,Y)
.

Proof.

For the range condition, we use
[
32
, Theorem 7.3.3]
to see that

\codim\mathcal{R}\big(G^{\prime}(u,q)[\cdot,d_{q}]\big)<\infty

for all
(u,q)\in Z
and every
d_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,

\codim\mathcal{R}\big(G^{\prime}(u,q)\big)\leq\codim\mathcal{R}\big(G^{\prime}(u,q)[\cdot,d_{q}]\big)<\infty.

For the kernel condition, we distinguish two cases. First, assume that

\displaystyle-\Delta d_{u}+qd_{u}=-u

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(70)

\displaystyle d_{u}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

admits no solution.
In this case,

\mathcal{N}\big(G^{\prime}(u,q)\big)=\text{Eig}(-\Delta;q)\times\{0\},

where
\text{Eig}(-\Delta;q)
denotes the set of solutions to

\displaystyle-\Delta u+qu=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

It is known that this set is of finite dimension, showing the kernel condition in this case.

On the other hand, if (
70
) admits at least one solution
\bar{d}_{u}
, then the solution set of (
70
) is given by
\bar{d}_{u}+\text{Eig}(-\Delta;q)
. Therefore, we have

\text{dim }\mathcal{N}\big(G^{\prime}(u,q)\big)=\text{dim }\left\{(d_{u},d_{q})\in Z\,:\,d_{u}=d_{q}\bar{d}_{u}+\text{Eig}(-\Delta;q)\right\}<\infty,

finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For each local solution
(u_{\ast},q_{\ast})\in X
to (
69
) and all
\lambda_{\ast}\in\Lambda\big((u_{\ast},q_{\ast})\big)
, there exists
\alpha>0
large enough, such that (
SOSC
) is fulfilled.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(x_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})d,d\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}

\displaystyle=\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha|d_{q}|^{2}+2\left\langle\lambda_{\ast},d_{q}d_{u}\right\rangle_{Y^{\ast},Y}

(71)

\displaystyle\geq\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha|d_{q}|^{2}-2\|\lambda_{\ast}\|_{Y^{\ast}}\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|d_{q}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left(\alpha-2\|\lambda_{\ast}\|_{Y^{\ast}}^{2}\right)|d_{q}|^{2}.

Let
(d_{u},d_{q})\in\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(x_{\ast}))
, i.e.,
(d_{u},d_{q})
solves

\displaystyle-\Delta d_{u}+q_{\ast}d_{u}=-u_{\ast}d_{q}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle d_{u}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

By standard results for elliptic regularity, we obtain

\|d_{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\big(\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q_{\ast}d_{u}+u_{\ast}d_{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

Squaring and utilizing Young’s inequality yields

c\|d_{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\big(|q_{\ast}|^{2}+1\big)\|d_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u_{\ast}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}|d_{q}|^{2}.

Inserting into (
71
) gives

\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}_{zz}(x_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})d,d\right\rangle_{Z^{\ast},Z}\geq\frac{c}{2(|q_{\ast}|^{2}+1)}\|d_{u}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left(\alpha-2\|\lambda_{\ast}\|_{Y^{\ast}}^{2}-\frac{\|u_{\ast}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\left(|q_{\ast}|^{2}+1\right)}\right)|d_{q}|^{2}

for all
d\in\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime}(z))
. Thus, for
\alpha
large enough, (
1
) is satisfied. Since
K=\{0\}
, this also yields (
SOSC
), see
Remark

2
.
∎

Abstract
. Any vertex algebra has a canonical decreasing filtration, called Li filtration, whose associated graded space has a natural structure of a vertex Poisson algebra. In this note, we introduce an analogous filtration for any SUSY vertex algebra, which was introduced by Heluani and Kac as a superfield formalism of a supersymmetric vertex algebra. We prove that the associated graded superspace of our filtration has a structure of SUSY vertex Poisson algebras. We also introduce and discuss related notions, such as Zhu’s C 2 C_{2} -Poisson superalgebras, associated superschemes and singular supports, for SUSY vertex algebras.

Introduction

Li filtration

A basic philosophy in the theory of vertex algebras is that they are quantized objects.
This phrase can be understood in various ways,
and one of them is given by the work of Haisheng Li
[
L05
]
.
He introduced a canonical decreasing filtration for an arbitrary vertex algebra
V
,
nowadays called the
Li filtration
of
V
.
The associated graded space has a natural structure of a vertex Poisson algebra

[
FBZ04
, Chap. 16]
, i.e., a combined structure of a commutative vertex algebra
and a vertex Lie algebra.
A vertex Poisson algebra can be considered as a Poisson object in the world of vertex algebras,
and in this sense, the existence of the Li filtration shows that
we can view any vertex algebra as a quantization of the associated graded vertex Poisson algebra.

For explicitness, let us recall the definition of the Li filtration (see also
section

3.2
).
Let
V
be a vertex algebra, and for
a\in V
we denote by
a_{(n)}\in\End V

the
(n)
-operator in the expansion

Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)}
of the state-field correspondence.
Then the Li filtration of
V
is a decreasing sequence of subspaces

V=E_{0}(V)\supset E_{1}(V)\supset\dotsb\supset E_{n}(V)\supset\dotsb

consisting of

\displaystyle E_{n}(V)\coloneqq\spn\left\{a^{1}_{(-1-k_{1})}\dotsm a^{r}_{(-1-k_{r})}b\,\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\,a^{i},b\in V,\,k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\\
\text{satisfying $k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{r}\geq n$}\end{array}\right\}.

Besides the philosophical reason explained above,
there have been increasing interest in and importance of
the Li filtration and the associated graded vertex Poisson algebra.
In particular, there have been increasing studies with a geometric viewpoint
where one sees the spectrum of the associated graded vertex Poisson algebra
as a vertex algebra (a.k.a. chiral, or semi-infinite) analogue of a Poisson scheme.
Such geometric studies have a strong point that the complicated structure of
the original vertex algebra can be treated with less complexity
by using Poisson geometry arguments or by making appropriate analogue of them.
Among various studies, let us here only name the work of Arakawa and Moreau
[
AM21
]
,
where the notion of chiral symplectic cores is introduced and studied
as a vertex Poisson analogue of symplectic leaves in Poisson varieties.

SUSY vertex algebras

This note is written under the influence of those Poisson-geometric studies of vertex algebras.
We focus on
SUSY vertex algebras
introduced by Heluani and Kac
[
HK07
]
.
It is a superfield formulation of vertex algebras equipped with supersymmetry.
The detailed explanation will be given in §
2
, but here let us give a brief account.
There are two classes of structures,
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras

and
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebras
, for a positive integer
N
.
Both structures are given by a linear superspace
V
, an element
\ket{0}\in V

and a linear operator
Y(\cdot,Z)
on
V
whose value for
a\in V
is
taken in a
superfield
of the form

(0.0.3)

\displaystyle Y(a,Z)=\sum_{(j|J)}Z^{j|J}a_{(j|J)},\quad Z^{j|J}\coloneqq z^{j}\zeta^{j_{1}}\dotsb\zeta^{j_{r}},\quad a_{(j|J)}\in\End V.

Here
Z=(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})
denotes a
supervariable

with an even
z
and odd
\zeta^{i}
’s,
and the index
(j|J)
runs over integers
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and ordered subsets

J=\{j_{1}<\dotsb<j_{r}\}
of the ordered set
[N]\coloneqq\{1<\dotsb<N\}
.

V
is called the superspace of states,
\ket{0}
is called the vacuum,
and
Y(\cdot,Z)
is called the state-superfield correspondence.
These should satisfy the vacuum axiom and the locality axiom,
similarly as in the even case (i.e., the case of ordinary vertex algebras).

The difference between
N_{W}=N
and
N_{K}=N
cases lies in the translation invariance.
An
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra is equipped with an even endomorphism
T

and odd endomorphism
S^{i}
’s for
i\in[N]
which satisfy

[T,Y(a,Z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,Z),\quad[S^{i},Y(a,Z)]=\partial_{\zeta^{i}}Y(a,Z).

Here
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the supercommutator
(see §
1.1
for a formal definition).
On the other hand, an
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra is equipped with
odd operations
S_{K}^{i}
’s for
i\in[N]
satisfying

[S_{K}^{i},Y(a,Z)]=D^{i}_{Z}Y(a,Z),\quad D_{Z}^{i}\coloneqq\partial_{\zeta^{i}}+\zeta^{i}\partial_{z}.

In this
N_{K}=N
case, we automatically have
(S_{K}^{1})^{2}=\dotsb=(S_{K}^{N})^{2}

corresponding to
(D_{Z}^{i})^{2}=\partial_{z}
,
and setting
T\coloneqq(S_{K}^{i})^{2}
, we have
[T,Y(a,Z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,Z)
.

Similarly as the superfield formalism in supersymmetric quantum field theories,
a SUSY vertex algebra has a compact description of the structure.
Besides of such formal convenience, it has also much importance.
Here we name only a few studies which have geometric flavor:
Heluani’s construction
[
H07
]
of chiral algebras over super curves
and superconformal curves, his another work
[
H09
]

of SUSY structure on the chiral de Rham complex of a Calabi-Yau manifold,
and the study of super-modular property of SUSY conformal blocks for
N_{W}=1
case
by Heluani and Van Ekeren
[
HV16
]
.

Summary of results

Now we can explain the main theme of this note.
For any
N_{W}=N
or
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra
V
,
we introduce a canonical decreasing filtration

V=E_{0}(V)\supset E_{1}(V)\supset\dotsb\supset E_{n}(V)\supset\dotsb

where, using the
(j|J)
-operator in (
0.0.3
), we set

\displaystyle E_{n}(V)\coloneqq\spn\left\{a^{1}_{(-1-k_{1}|K_{1})}\dotsm a^{r}_{(-1-k_{r}|K_{r})}b\,\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\,a^{i},b\in V,\,k_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\,K_{i}\subset[N]\\
\text{satisfying $k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{r}\geq n$}\end{array}\right\}.

We call it the
Li filtration
of
V
(
3.2.2
and
3.3.2
).

Our definition is just neglecting the odd index
J
and
using the even case definition (
0
).
Despite of its simple-mindedness, we need some careful arguments on SUSY vertex algebras to
obtain the following SUSY analogue of Li’s theorem in
[
L05
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Theorem 1

(
3.2.6
and
3.3.6
)
.

Let
V
be an
N_{W}=N
(resp.
N_{K}=N
) SUSY vertex algebra, and
E_{n}=E_{n}(V)

be the linear sub-superspaces of
V
in (
0
).
Then the associated graded space

\gr_{E}V\coloneqq{\textstyle\bigoplus}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}/E_{n+1}

has the following structure of an
N_{W}=N
(resp.
N_{K}=N
) SUSY vertex Poisson algebra
(see
3.1.6
and
3.1.17
). Let
a\in E_{r}
and
b\in E_{s}
.

•

The commutative multiplication
\cdot
is

(a+E_{r+1})\cdot(b+E_{s+1})\coloneqq a_{(-1|N)}b+E_{r+s+1}.

•

The unit is
1\coloneqq\ket{0}+E_{1}
.

•

The even operator
\partial
is
\partial(a+E_{r+1})\coloneqq Ta+E_{r+2}
,
and the odd operator
\delta^{i}
for
i\in[N]
is

\delta^{i}(a+E_{r+1})\coloneqq S^{i}a+E_{r+2}

(resp. the odd operator
\not{\partial}^{i}
is

\not{\partial}^{i}(a+E_{r+1})\coloneqq S_{K}^{i}a+E_{r+2}
).

•

The SUSY vertex Lie structure
Y_{-}
is

Y_{-}(a+E_{r+1},Z)(b+E_{s+1})\coloneqq\sum_{(j|J),\,j\geq 0}Z^{-1-j|[N]\setminus J}(a_{(j|J)}b+E_{r+s-j+1}).

Once we have obtained the Li filtration of SUSY vertex algebras,
we can discuss various related notions which are already established in the even case,
and can expect to develop a Poisson-geometric theory of SUSY vertex algebras.
In this note, as a first step toward such a geometric theory,
we study a SUSY analogue of
associated schemes
and
singular supports

introduced by Arakawa
[
Ar12
]
.
Our definitions and the arguments are straightforward analogue of loc. cit.
As a result, we have the following SUSY analogue of the equivalence between
the
C_{2}
-cofiniteness and the lisse condition
[
Ar12
, Theorem 3.3.3]
.

Theorem 2

(
4.2.9
)
.

Let
V
be an
N_{W}=N
or
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra which is finitely strongly generated
and has a lower-bounded grading (see
4.2.3
for the detail). Then we have

\text{$V$ is $C_{2}$-cofinite (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{dfn:C2cof})}\iff\text{$V$ is lisse (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{dfn:lisse})}.

n\geq m+1
, we have
a morphism of commutative superalgebras
\mathrm{HS}_{A/k}^{m}\to\mathrm{HS}_{A/k}^{\infty}=A_{\infty}
,
which induces another morphism

(1.3.4)

\displaystyle A^{O_{m}}\longrightarrow A^{O}.

We call it the
projection
of superjet algebras.

1.3.2.
Superconformal jet algebras

Let us recollect the
super Riemann surface
structure or
the
SUSY structure
on the superdisk
O=O^{1|N}
.
We denote the topological Lie superalgebra of
k
-derivations on
O
(
1.2.2
) as

(1.3.5)

\displaystyle\Der O\coloneqq\Der_{k}(O)=\mathfrak{X}_{O/k}^{1}.

By
[
FBZ04
, §6.2]
and
[
H07
, §3.1.2]
, the set of automorphisms

\Aut O\coloneqq\Aut_{\TSCom k}(O)

is a group superscheme over
k
, and the set of
k
-valued points is described as

(\Aut O)(k)=\Bigl\{Z=(z,\zeta^{i})\mapsto\Bigl(\sum_{n+\left|{J}\right|\geq 1}a_{n,J}Z^{n|J},\sum_{n+\left|{J}\right|\geq 1}b^{i}_{n,J}Z^{n|J}\Bigr)\,|\,\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1,0}&a_{0,e_{i}}\\
b^{i}_{1,0}&b^{i}_{0,e_{i}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\GL_{k}(1|N)\Bigr\}.

The topological Lie superalgebra of
\Aut O
is denoted by

\Der_{0}O\coloneqq\Lie(\Aut O),

which is linearly compact and has a topological basis

\{z^{n}\partial_{z},z^{n}\partial_{\zeta^{i}}\mid n\geq 1,i\in[N]\}\cup\{z^{m}\zeta^{i}\partial_{z},z^{m}\zeta\partial_{\zeta^{i}}\mid m\geq 0,i\in[N]\}
.
It is actually a Lie sub-superalgebra of
\Der O
given in (
1.3.5
).

Definition 1.3.12
.

Consider the even differential 1-form on
O

\omega_{Z}\coloneqq dz+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\zeta^{i}d\zeta^{i}\in(\Omega^{1}_{A/R})_{\overline{1}},

where
d=d_{\textup{ev}}
denotes the even universal differential (
1.2.6
).
We define
\Asc O
to be the subgroup of
\Aut O
consisting of automorphisms
preserving
\omega_{Z}
up to multiplication by a function.
Its element is called a
superconformal transformation
of
\mathbb{D}
.
The topological Lie superalgebra of
\Asc O
is denoted by
\Dsc O\coloneqq\Lie\Asc O
,
and its element is called a
superconformal vector field
.

Let us recall basic properties of the Lie superalgebra
\Dsc O
, following

[
H07
, §3.1.3]
and
[
HK07
, Example 2.12]
.
For
i\in[N]
, we define an odd derivation
D_{Z}^{i}
of
O
by

(1.3.6)

\displaystyle D_{Z}^{i}\coloneqq\partial_{\zeta}+\zeta\partial_{z}^{i}.

Then
\Dsc O
is equal to the Lie subalgebra of
\Der_{0}O

\Dsc O=\{W\in\Der_{0}O\mid[W,D_{Z}^{i}]=fD_{Z}^{i},\ \exists f\in O\}.

Also,
\Dsc O
consists of the derivations of the form

D^{f}=f\partial_{z}+\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{p(f)}{\textstyle\sum}_{i=1}^{N}(D_{Z}^{i}f)D_{Z}^{i}

with

of pure parity.
In particular, it contains

\displaystyle l_{n}\coloneqq-z^{n+1}\partial_{z}-\tfrac{n+1}{2}z^{n}{\textstyle\sum}_{i=1}^{N}\zeta^{i}\partial_{\zeta^{i}}

\displaystyle(n\in\mathbb{N}),

\displaystyle g_{r}^{j}\coloneqq-z^{r+\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{\zeta^{i}}-\zeta^{i}\partial_{z})+\bigl(r+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)z^{r-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{j}{\textstyle\sum}_{i=1}^{N}\zeta^{i}\partial_{\zeta^{i}}

\displaystyle(r\in\mathbb{N}+\tfrac{1}{2},\,j\in[N]).

In the case
N=1
, the elements
l_{n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) and
g_{r}\coloneqq g_{r}^{1}
(

)
are topological generators of
\Dsc O
, subject to the relations

\displaystyle[l_{m},l_{n}]=(m-n)l_{m+n},\quad[l_{n},g_{r}]=(\tfrac{n}{2}-r)g_{n+r},\quad[g_{r},g_{s}]=2l_{r+s}.

Thus, in the case
N=1
,
\Dsc O
is a Lie sub-superalgebra of
the
Neveu-Schwarz algebra
of central charge
0
(see (
2.3.9
)).

Recall that the superjet algebra
A^{O}
enjoys a universal property:

\Hom_{\SCom k}(A^{O},k)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\Hom_{\SCom k}(A,O).

Now we would like to find the odd derivations
S_{K}^{i}
on
A^{O}
which corresponds to
the action of
D_{Z}^{i}=\partial_{\zeta^{i}}+\zeta^{i}\partial_{z}
on
O
.
Let
\phi(Z)=\sum_{(n|J),\,n\geq 0}Z^{n|J}\phi_{n|J}
be the series corresponding to
an element
\phi\in\Hom_{\SCom k}(A,O)
. Then, we have

(D_{Z}^{i}\phi)(Z)=\sum_{(n|J),\,n\geq 0,\,i\notin J}Z^{n|J}\sigma(e_{i},J)\phi_{n|J\cup e_{i}}+\sum_{(n|J),\,n\geq 0,\,i\in J}Z^{n|1}(n+1)\sigma(e_{i},J\setminus e_{i})\phi_{n+1|J\setminus e_{i}},

where we used the same symbols as in
1.3.7
.
This calculation indicates that the desired
S_{K}^{i}
is given by

(1.3.7)

\displaystyle S_{K}^{i}(a^{[n|J]})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sigma(e_{i},J)a^{[n|J\cup e_{i}]}&(i\notin J)\\
(n+1)\sigma(e_{i},J\setminus e_{i})a^{[n+1|J\setminus e_{i}]}&(i\in J)\end{cases}.

Using this action twice, we see that the square
T_{K}=(S_{K}^{i})^{2}
is independent of
i
,
and its action is given by

(1.3.8)

\displaystyle T_{K}(a^{[n|J]})=(n+1)a^{[n+1|J]}.

Then, we immediately have:

Lemma 1.3.13
.

The operators
S_{K}^{i}
’s and
T_{K}
form a Lie superalgebra with commutation relation

[S_{K}^{i},S_{K}^{j}]=2\delta_{i,j}T_{K},\quad[T_{K},S_{K}^{i}]=0.

Recalling
1.3.8
, we find that

(A^{O},S_{K}^{i})
is a differential superalgebra for any
A\in\SCom k
.
Consulting
1.3.12
, we may name:

Definition 1.3.14
.

For
A\in\SCom k
, the differential superalgebra
(A^{O},S_{K}^{i})
is called
the
superconformal jet algebra
of
A
, and denoted by
A^{O_{\textup{sc}}}
.

By
1.3.6
, we have:

Lemma 1.3.15
.

For any
A\in\SCom k
, the superconformal jet algebra
A^{O_{\textup{sc}}}

is generated by
A
as a differential superalgebra.

Then we have the following analogous of
1.3.10
:

Lemma 1.3.16
.

Let
A,B\in\SCom k
, and assume the following conditions.

•

B
contains
A
as a linear sub-superalgebra.

•

B
has
N
odd derivations
\not{\partial}^{i}
(

)
which form the Lie superalgebra in
1.3.13
.

•

The differential superalgebra
(B,\not{\partial}^{i})
is generated by

.

Then the injection

can be extended to a surjective morphism

(A^{O},S_{K}^{i})\twoheadrightarrow(B,\not{\partial}^{i})
of differential superalgebras.

2.1.
Superfields

Here we explain the formalism of superfields used throughout the rest of this note.
The original reference is
[
HK07
, §2.6]
.

Let us continue to use
1.3.4
. So
Z=(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})
is
a
1|N
-supervariable with even
z
and odd
\zeta^{i}
’s, and denote

Z^{j|J}\coloneqq z^{j}\zeta^{J}=z^{j}\zeta^{j_{1}}\zeta^{j_{2}}\dotsm\quad(j\in\mathbb{Z},\,J=\{j_{1},j_{2},\dotsc\}\subset[N]).

We also use a simplified notation

Z^{j|N}\coloneqq Z^{j|[N]}=z^{j}\zeta^{1}\dotsm\zeta^{N}.

Below we will repeatedly use the sign notation given in
[
HK07
, §3.1.1]
.
For disjoint ordered sets
I,J\subset[N]
, we define
\sigma(I,J)\in\{\pm 1\}
by

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle\zeta^{I}\zeta^{J}=\sigma(I,J)\zeta^{I\cup J},

where
I\cup J
denotes the re-ordered set.
If
I\cap J\neq\emptyset
, then we define
\sigma(I,J)\coloneqq 0
.
Also, for
i\in[N]
and
J\subset[N]
, we use the following abbreviations.

(2.1.2)

\displaystyle e_{i}\coloneqq\{i\},\quad N\setminus J\coloneqq[N]\setminus J.

For example, we denote
\sigma(N\setminus J,e_{i})\coloneqq\sigma([N]\setminus J,\{i\})
.

For a linear superspace
V
, we define
V\llbracket Z\rrbracket
and
V\llbracket Z^{\pm 1}\rrbracket

to be the linear spaces of series with coefficients in
V
:

V\llbracket Z\rrbracket\coloneqq\Biggl\{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N},\,J\subset[N]}Z^{n|J}v_{n|J}\ \Bigg|\ v_{n|J}\in V\Biggr\},\quad V\llbracket Z^{\pm 1}\rrbracket\coloneqq\Biggl\{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\,J\subset[N]}Z^{j|J}v_{j|J}\ \Bigg|\ v_{j|J}\in V\Biggr\}.

Here the summations are possibly infinite, and the index
J
runs over
ordered subset in
[N]
.
These are linear superspaces by setting the parity as

p(Z^{j|J}v)\coloneqq\left|{J}\right|+p(v)\bmod 2
for
v\in V
of pure parity. We also denote

V(\!(Z)\!)\coloneqq\Biggl\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\,J\subset[N]}Z^{j|J}v_{j|J}\in V\llbracket Z^{\pm 1}\rrbracket\ \Bigg|\ v_{j|J}=0\ \forall j\ll 0\Biggr\}.

Let
\iEnd(V)\coloneqq\iHom(V,V)
be the internal end in the category of linear superspaces
(see (
1.1.3
)). In particular, it is a linear superspace.
An
\iEnd(V)
-valued superfield
of
1|N
-supervariable
Z
is a series of the form

(2.1.3)

\displaystyle a(Z)=\sum_{(j|J)}Z^{-1-n|N\setminus J}a_{(j|J)}\quad(a_{(j|J)}\in\iEnd(V)),

where the indices run over the range
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
J\subset[N]
,
and
N\setminus J\coloneqq[N]\setminus J
,
such that for each
v\in V
we have
a(Z)v\in V(\!(Z)\!)
.
We denote the linear superspace of
\iEnd(V)
-valued superfields of supervariable
Z
by

\SF(V,Z)=\SF(V).

Next, we recall the locality of superfields
[
HK07
, §3.1.2]
.
Let us given two (super-)commuting
1|N
-supervariables
Z=(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})

and
W=(w,\omega_{1},\dotsc,\omega_{N})
, and let
A
be a superalgebra.
An
A
-valued formal distribution in two variables

Z
and
W

is a (possibly infinite) series of the form

a(Z,W)=\sum_{(i|I),(j|J)}Z^{i|I}W^{j|J}a_{i|I,j|J}\quad(a_{i|I,j|J}\in A)

where the running index means
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
I,J\subset[N]
, and we wrote

Z^{i|I}W^{j|J}\coloneqq z^{i}\zeta^{I}w^{j}\omega^{J}=z^{i}w^{j}\zeta^{I}\omega^{J}
.
We denote by
A\llbracket Z^{\pm 1},W^{\pm 1}\rrbracket

the linear superspace of
A
-valued formal distributions.
A formal distribution
a(Z,W)\in A\llbracket Z^{\pm 1},W^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
is called
local

if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2.1.4)

\displaystyle(z-w)^{n}a(Z,W)=0.

Note that the left hand side is well-defined in
A\llbracket Z^{\pm 1},W^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
.

2.2.
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras

In this subsection, we cite from
[
HK07
, §3]
the notion of
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras.
We continue to use the notation in §
2.1
. In particular,

Z=(z,\zeta^{i})=(z,\zeta^{1},\dotsc,\zeta^{N})
, and
W=(w,\omega^{i})
are
1|N
-supervariables.

2.2.1.
Definition and basic properties

Definition 2.2.1

(
[
HK07
, Definition 3.3.1]
)
.

An
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra
(
N_{W}=N
SUSY VA
for short)
is a data
(V,\ket{0},T,S^{i},Y)
consisting of

•

a linear superspace
V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}
, called the
state superspace
,

•

an even vector
\ket{0}\in V_{\overline{0}}
, called the
vacuum
,

•

an even operator
T\in\End(V)_{\overline{0}}
,

•

odd operators
S^{i}\in\End(V)_{\overline{1}}
for
i\in[N]
, and

•

an even linear map
Y(\cdot,Z)\colon V\to\SF(V,Z)
,
called the
state-superfield correspondence
,

which satisfies the following axioms.

•

Vacuum axiom
: For any
a\in V
and
i\in[N]
, we have

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle Y(a,Z)\ket{0}=a+O(Z),\quad T\ket{0}=S^{i}\ket{0}=0,

where
O(Z)
is an element of
V\llbracket Z\rrbracket
which vanishes at
Z=0
(i.e.,
z=\zeta^{i}=0
).

•

Translation invariance
: For each
a\in V
, we have

(2.2.2)

\displaystyle[T,Y(a,Z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,Z),\quad[S,Y(a,Z)]=\partial_{\zeta^{i}}Y(a,Z)\quad(i\in[N]),

where we denoted
\partial_{z}\coloneqq\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z}
and
\partial_{\zeta^{i}}\coloneqq\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta^{i}}
,
and used the supercommutator (
1.1.2
).

•

Locality axiom
: For any
a,b\in V
, there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2.2.3)

\displaystyle(z-w)^{n}[Y(a,Z),Y(b,W)]=0.

The identity is regarded as that of
(\End V)\llbracket Z^{\pm 1},W^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
(see (
2.1.4
)).

We abbreviate
V=(V,\ket{0},T,S^{i},Y)
if no confusion may arise.

For
a\in V
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and an ordered subset
J\subset[N]
,
we define the
Fourier mode
or the
(j|J)
-operator

a_{(j|J)}\in\End(V)
by the expansion

(2.2.4)

\displaystyle Y(a,Z)=\sum_{(j|J)}Z^{-1-j|N\setminus J}a_{(j|J)},

where we used similar symbols as in (
2.1.3
).
We also denote
a_{(j|N)}\coloneqq a_{(j|[N])}
.

Using
(j|J)
-operators, the vacuum axiom (
2.2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2.5)

\displaystyle a_{(-1|N)}\ket{0}=a,\quad a_{(n|J)}\ket{0}=0\quad(n\in\mathbb{N},\,J\subset[N]),

and by
[
HK07
, (3.3.1.5)]
, the translation invariance (
2.2.2
) is equivalent to

(2.2.6)

\displaystyle[T,a_{(j|J)}]=-ja_{(j-1|J)},\quad[S^{i},a_{(j|J)}]=\begin{cases}\sigma(N\setminus J,e_{i})a_{(j|J\setminus e_{i})}&(i\in J)\\
0&(i\notin J)\end{cases},

where we used the sign
\sigma
in (
2.1.1
) and the abbreviations (
2.1.2
).

As in the even case, the locality axiom (
2.2.3
) implies
operator product expansion (OPE for short). To explain it, let us cite some notation
on formal delta functions for
N_{W}=N
SUSY VAs from
[
HK07
, §2.3]
.
Let
Z=(z,\zeta^{i})
and
W=(w,\omega^{i})
be two commuting
1|N
-supervariables.
We denote by

(2.2.7)

\displaystyle i_{z,w}\colon k(\!(Z,W)\!)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow k(\!(Z)\!)(\!(W)\!),\quad i_{w,z}\colon k(\!(Z,W)\!)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow k(\!(W)\!)(\!(Z)\!)

the embeddings obtained by the expansions with respect to
\frac{w}{z}

(in the domain
\left|{z}\right|>\left|{w}\right|
),
and with respect to
\frac{z}{w}
(in the domain
\left|{z}\right|<\left|{w}\right|
), respectively.
For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and an ordered subset
J=\{j_{1}<\dotsb<j_{r}\}\subset[N]
, we set

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle(Z-W)^{j|J}\coloneqq(z-w)^{j}(\zeta-\omega)^{J}=(z-w)^{j}(\zeta^{j_{1}}-\omega^{j_{1}})\dotsm(\zeta^{j_{r}}-\omega^{j_{r}}).

The formal
\delta
-function for
N_{W}=N

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle\delta(Z,W)\coloneqq(i_{z,w}-i_{w,z})(Z-W)^{-1|N}=(i_{z,w}-i_{w,z})\frac{(\zeta-\omega)^{N}}{z-w}=(i_{z,w}-i_{w,z})\frac{(\zeta^{1}-\omega^{1})\dotsm(\zeta^{N}-\omega^{N})}{z-w}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
J=\{j_{1}<\dotsb<j_{r}\}\subset[N]
, let

(2.2.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{W}^{n|J}\coloneqq\partial_{w}^{n}\partial_{\omega^{j_{1}}}\dotsm\partial_{\omega^{j_{r}}},\quad\partial_{W}^{(n|J)}\coloneqq\frac{(-1)^{\binom{r+1}{2}}}{j!}\partial_{W}^{j|J}.

Then, by
[
HK07
, §3.1.4 (4)]

(2.2.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{W}^{(n|J)}\delta(Z,W)=(i_{z,w}-i_{w,z})(Z-W)^{-1-j|N\setminus J}.

Finally, for
f(Z)=\sum_{(j|J)}Z^{j|J}f_{j|J}\in V\llbracket Z^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
, we denote

(2.2.12)

\displaystyle\res_{Z}f(Z)\coloneqq f_{-1|N}.

Then, by
[
HK07
, Lemma 3.1.3]
, any local distribution
a(Z,W)

is decomposed in the following finite sum.

(2.2.13)

\displaystyle a(Z,W)=\sum_{(j|J),\,j\geq 0}\bigl(\partial_{W}^{(j|J)}\delta(Z,W)\bigr)c_{j|J}(W),\quad c_{j|J}(W)\coloneqq\res_{Z}(Z-W)^{j|J}a(Z,W).

Now, let
V
be an
N_{W}=N
SUSY VA, and
a,b\in V
of pure parity.
Then the locality axiom (
2.2.3
) and the expansion (
2.2.13
) imply
the following OPE formula
[

, Theorem 3.3.8 (5)]
:

(2.2.14)

\displaystyle[Y(a,Z),Y(b,W)]=\sum_{(j|J),\,j\geq 0}\bigl(\partial_{Z}^{(j|J)}\delta(Z,W)\bigr)Y(a_{(j|J)}b,W).

As in the even case, an
N_{W}=N
SUSY VA

is called

by a subset

if

is spanned by the elements
of the form
a^{i_{1}}_{(-n_{1}|J_{1})}\dotsm a^{i_{r}}_{(-n_{r}|J_{r})}\ket{0}

with

,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
J_{i}\subset[N]
.

We also have the notion of a

over an
N_{W}=N
SUSY VA
V
.
We omit the detailed definition, and only give the notation: Let
M
be a
V
-module.
For
a\in V
and
m\in M
, we denote the
V
-action on
M
by

(2.2.15)

\displaystyle Y^{M}(a,Z)m=\sum_{(j|J)}Z^{-1-j|N\setminus J}a^{M}_{(j|J)}m\in M(\!(Z)\!).

In the rest of this

Example 2.2.3

(
[

)
.

V(\mathscr{W}_{N})
is an

SUSY vertex algebra
strongly generated by an element

of parity

and elements

,

, of parity

whose OPEs are

(2.2.16)

\displaystyle[L_{\Lambda}L]=(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[Q^{i}_{\Lambda}Q^{j}]=(S^{i}+\chi^{i})Q^{j}-\chi^{j}Q^{i},\quad[L_{\Lambda}Q^{i}]=(T+\lambda)Q^{i}+(-1)^{N}\chi^{i}L.

\displaystyle[L_{\Lambda}L]=(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[Q_{\Lambda}Q]=SQ+\frac{\lambda\chi}{3}C,\quad[L_{\Lambda}Q]=(T+\lambda)Q-\chi L+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{6}C.

For

(2.2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[L_{\Lambda}L]=(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[L_{\Lambda}Q^{i}]=(T+\lambda)Q^{i}+\chi^{i}L\\
&[Q^{i}_{\Lambda}Q^{j}]=S^{i}Q^{j},\quad[Q^{1}_{\Lambda}Q^{2}]=(S^{1}+\chi^{1})Q^{2}-\chi^{2}Q^{1}+\frac{\lambda}{6}{C}.\end{split}

V(\mathscr{W}_{N})
is an

Lemma 2.2.10
.

Let

\displaystyle-(-1)^{k+p(a)p(b)+(p(b)+N-\char 12\relax{J})\char 12\relax{N\setminus J^{\prime}}}b_{(k+l-j|J^{\prime})}a_{(j|J)}u\Bigr)

with

and

\displaystyle\alpha\coloneqq\left|{K\setminus J}\right|(1+\left|{N\setminus J}\right|),\quad\widetilde{\sigma}\coloneqq\sigma(J,K\setminus J)\sigma(J,N\setminus J)\sigma(L,K\setminus J)\sigma(J^{\prime},N\setminus J^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
a,b\in V
be elements of pure parity, and recall the OPE (
2.2.14
):

[Y(a,Z),Y(b,W)]=\sum_{(j|J),\,j\geq 0}\bigl(\partial_{Z}^{j|J}\delta(Z,W)\bigr)Y(a_{(j|J)}b,W).

Following the argument in the even case
[
FBZ04
, §3.3.10]
,
we multiply both sides of this equality by some

f(Z,W)\in k\bigl[Z^{1|J},W^{1|J},(Z-W)^{-1|J}\mid J\subset[N]\bigr]
.
Then, using (

), we have

(2.2.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\res_{W}\res_{Z}f(Z,W)a(Z)b(W)-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\res_{W}\res_{Z}f(Z,W)b(W)a(Z)\\
&=\res_{W}\res_{Z-W}\sum_{(j|J),\,j\geq 0}f(Z,W)(Z-W)^{-1-j|N\setminus J}Y(a_{(j|J)}b,W).\end{split}

To show
2.2.10
, we set
f(Z,W)\coloneqq W^{l|L}(Z-W)^{k|K}
.
Let us calculate the first term in the left hand side. We have

\displaystyle\res_{Z}(Z-W)^{k|K}a(Z)

\displaystyle=\res_{Z}(z-w)^{k}(\zeta-\omega)^{K}\sum_{(j|J)}z^{-1-j}\zeta^{N\setminus J}a_{(j|J)}

\displaystyle=\res_{Z}\Bigl[(z-w)^{k}\cdot\sum_{M\subset K}(-1)^{\left|{K\setminus M}\right|}\sigma(M,K\setminus M)\zeta^{M}\omega^{K\setminus M}\cdot\sum_{(j|J)}z^{-1-j}\zeta^{N\setminus J}a_{(j|J)}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\res_{Z}\Bigl[\sum_{M\subset K}\sum_{(j|J)}(-1)^{\beta}\tau\cdot(z-w)^{k}z^{-1-j}\zeta^{M\cup(N\setminus J)}\omega^{K\setminus M}a_{(j|J)}\Bigr]

with
\beta\coloneqq\left|{K\setminus M}\right|+\left|{K\setminus M}\right|\left|{N\setminus J}\right|
and

\tau\coloneqq\sigma(M,K\setminus M)\sigma(M,N\setminus J)
.
By definition of
\res_{Z}
, the terms with
J=M
survive, and

\displaystyle\res_{Z}(Z-W)^{k|K}a(Z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j|J),\\
j\geq 0,\,J\subset K\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\beta+k-j}\tau\cdot\binom{k}{j}w^{k-j}\omega^{K\setminus J}a_{(j|J)}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\res_{W}\res_{Z}W^{l|L}(Z-W)^{k|K}a(Z)b(W)

\displaystyle=\res_{W}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j|J),\\
j\geq 0,\,J\subset K\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\beta+k-j}\tau\cdot\binom{k}{j}W^{l|L}w^{k-j}\omega^{K\setminus J}a_{(j|J)}\sum_{(p|P)}W^{-1-p|N\setminus P}b_{(p|P)}

(2.2.25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j|J),\\
j\geq 0,\,J\subset K\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-j+\beta}\upsilon\cdot\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{(p(a)+N-\char 12\relax{J})\char 12\relax{N\setminus J^{\prime}}}a_{(j|J)}b_{(l+k-j|J^{\prime})}

with
J^{\prime}\coloneqq L\cup(K\setminus J)
and

\beta=\left|{K\setminus J}\right|+\left|{K\setminus J}\right|\left|{N\setminus J}\right|=\alpha,\quad\upsilon\coloneqq\tau\cdot\sigma(L,K\setminus J)\sigma(J^{\prime},N\setminus J^{\prime})=\widetilde{\sigma}.

Replacing
(j,k-j)\mapsto(k-j,j)
, we have
k-j+\alpha\mapsto j+\alpha
,
and (
2.2.25
) is equal to the first term in the right hand side of the statement.
By a similar calculation, we see the second terms in (
2.2.24
) and the statement coincide.
For the right hand side of (
2.2.24
), we have by (
2.2.11
) that

\res_{Z-W}\partial_{W}^{(j|J)}\delta(Z,W)=\delta_{j,0}\delta_{J,\emptyset}
, which yields

\res_{W}\res_{Z-W}\sum_{(j|J)}W^{l|L}(Z-W)^{k|K}(Z-W)^{-1-j|1-J}Y(a_{(j|J)}b,W)=(a_{(k|K)}b)_{(l|L)}.

Hence we have the consequence.

2.4.1.

Recall that, in the even case, a

[

Definition 2.4.1

A SUSY VA

n=0

in the locality axiom, i.e.,

[Y(a,Z),Y(b,W)]=0

Similarly as in the even case, we have the following restatement:

Lemma 2.4.2
.

A SUSY VA

is commutative if and only if

Y(a,Z)\in(\End V)\llbracket Z\rrbracket
for any

.

Proof.

The argument is quite similar to that for the even case
[
FBZ04
, §1.4]
,
but let us write down it for completeness.
Assume that
V
is commutative. Then, for any
a,b\in V
of pure parity, we have

(2.4.1)

\displaystyle Y(a,Z)b=\left.\vphantom{1^{(}}Y(a,Z)Y(b,W)\ket{0}\right|_{W=0}=\left.(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}Y(b,W)Y(a,Z)\ket{0}\right|_{W=0}.

The right hand side has no negative powers of

by the vacuum axiom,
and hence
Y(a,Z)b\in V\llbracket Z\rrbracket
. Thus we have
Y(a,Z)\in(\End V)\llbracket Z\rrbracket

Conversely, assume

for any

Then, for any
a,b\in V
of pure parity, both of the series

f_{1}(Z,W)\coloneqq Y(a,Z)Y(b,W)
and

are in
(\End V)\llbracket Z,W\rrbracket
.
Now the locality axiom says that there exists

such that

(z-w)^{n}f_{1}(Z,W)=(z-w)^{n}(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}f_{2}(Z,W)
.
The last equality yields

,
and thus
V
is commutative.
∎

Mathematical study of conformal blocks of a SUSY vertex algebra
seems to be still in infancy.
One remarkable study in this direction is, as mentioned in
section

0
,
the work of Heluani and Van Ekeren
[
HV16
]
, which shows that
the normalized character of a positively graded module over a charge cofinite
N=2

topological vertex algebra can be regarded as a solution of a flat connection
over the moduli space of elliptic supercurves, and actually are Jacobi forms.
An
N=2
topological vertex algebra has a structure of
a strongly conformal
N_{W}=1
SUSY vertex algebra by the result of Heluani and Kac
[
HK07
]
,
and by the work of Heluani
[
H07
]
, we have the vector bundle with flat connection
of conformal blocks on the moduli space.
It is shown in
[
HV16
, A.2. Lemma]
that the charge cofiniteness is equivalent to
the
C_{2}
-cofiniteness for the underlying vertex superalgebra.
We can show that it is also equivalent to the
C_{2}
-cofiniteness of the associated
N_{W}=1

SUSY vertex algebra in the sense of
4.1.1
.
Thus, the result of
[
HV16
]
implies that modules over a
C_{2}
-cofinite
N_{W}=1

SUSY vertex algebra satisfying
4.2.3
would enjoy super-modularity.

(2)

We also expect an analogous result of the item (
1
) for the
N_{W}=N>1
case.
The argument of
[
HV16
]
is based on a SUSY analogue
[
H07
, Theorem 3.4]
of Huang’s
coordinate change formula, applied to a natural
N_{W}=1
SUSY analogue

(z,\zeta)\mapsto(e^{2\pi iz}-1,e^{2\pi iz}\zeta)
of Zhu’s coordinate change

z\mapsto e^{2\pi iz}-1
, and based on
N=1
super analogue of Weierstrass elliptic functions.
Both have straightforward
N>1
analogue, but the analysis gets much complicated,
and at this moment the author has no clear picture.

(3)

Along the line of (
1
) and (
2
),
the
N_{K}=N
case is mysterious for the author.
The
N_{K}=N
SUSY analogue of Huang’s formula is given in
[
H07
, Theorem 3.6]
.
Mimicking the argument in
[
HK07
]
, the author tried to find a clean coordinate change
which preserves the superconformal structure (also called SUSY structure or
super Riemann surface structure), but has failed until this moment.

(4)

Also related to super-modularity, let us recall the notion of

quasi-lisse vertex algebras
introduced in the work of Arakawa and Kawasetsu
[
AK18
]
.
It is a finitely strongly generated vertex algebra whose associated variety
(the reduced part of the associated scheme in our terminology)
has only finitely many symplectic leaves.
In
[
AK18
, Theorem 5.1]
, it is shown that the normalized character of a module
over a quasi-lisse vertex operator algebra enjoys modularity.
An
N_{W}=1
SUSY analogue of this result might be obtained
by borrowing some arguments in
[
HV16
]
, but one must take care to establish
a super (non-reduced) analogue of the results of Poisson geometry used in
[
AK18
]
.
The other
N_{W}=N>1
and
N_{K}=N
cases are much more unclear at this moment.

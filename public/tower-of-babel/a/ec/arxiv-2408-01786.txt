Introduction

Our starting point is the following
(M+N)
-body Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\partial_{t}\Psi=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M+N}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\Delta\Psi+\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi\varepsilon_{0}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1,\text{ }j\neq k}^{M+N}\frac{Z_{j}Z_{k}}{|x_{j}-x_{k}|}\Psi,

where
\Psi:\mathbb{R\times R}^{3(M+N)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the
constants
eZ_{j}
’s are the charges and in particular the charge numbers
Z_{j}
’s are positive for the nuclei and
-1
for the electrons. Such system
originates from a molecular system made of
M
nuclei interacting via the
Coulomb potential with
N
electrons.

Its complexity led to use various approximations to describe the stationary
states with simpler models. Historically, the first effort made in this
direction began from Hartree
[
17
]
by choosing some particular test
functions without considering the antisymmetry (i.e. the Pauli principle).
Subsequently, Fock
[
16
]
and Slater
[
30
]
, to take into account the
Pauli principle, proposed another class of test functions, i.e. the class of
Slater determinants, obtaining the following Hartree-Fock type system:

\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\psi_{k}+V_{\text{ext}}\psi_{k}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x-y|^{-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(y)|^{2}dy\right)\psi_{k}+(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}=E_{k}\psi_{k},&\text{ }\forall k=1,2,...,N,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
\psi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

V_{\text{ext}}
is
a given external potential,
(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}
is the
k
’th
component of the crucial exchange potential defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\psi_{k}(y)\bar{\psi}_{j}(y)}{|x-y|}dy,&\text{ }\forall k=1,2,...,N,\end{array}

and
E_{k}
is the
k
’th eigenvalue.

In
[
31
]
, a further relevant free-electron approximation for the
k
’th
component of the exchange potential
V_{\text{ex}}\psi
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}=-C\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/3}\psi_{k},&\text{ }\forall k=1,2,...,N,\end{array}

(1.2)

where
C
is a positive constant. Such approximation is used more frequently
when the qualitative analysis of system (
1.1
) is studied.

When
N=1,
the exchange potential (
1.2
) becomes
V_{\text{ex}}\psi=-C|\psi_{1}|^{2/3}\psi_{1}
. If we consider
\psi_{1}
as a real
function, renaming it as
u,
and take, for simplicity,
C=1
, then system (
1.1
) becomes Schrödinger–Poisson–Slater equation as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u+\mu\phi_{u}(x)u=|u|^{2/3}u&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}

(1.3)

where
\mu>0
is a parameter and

\phi_{u}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dy.

Sánchez and Soler
[
28
]
used a minimization procedure in an
appropriate manifold to find a positive solution of equation (
1.3
). If
the nonlinearity
|u|^{2/3}u
is replaced with
|u|^{p-2}u

(2<p<6)
(or,
more generally,
f(u))
, then equation (
1.3
) becomes the Schrödinger–Poisson equation (also called Schrödinger–Maxwell equation),
where the existence and multiplicity of various solutions has been
extensively studied depending on the parameter
\mu
, see e.g.
[
2
,
4
,
26
,
27
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
42
]
. More precisely, Ruiz
[
26
]
considered the
Schrödinger–Poisson equation with the nonlinearity
|u|^{p-2}u

(2<p<6),
and concluded that if
2<p\leq 3,
one positive radial solutions
is found for
\mu>0
sufficiently small and no nontrivial solution admits
for
\mu\geq 1/4
while if
3<p<6,
one positive radial solution admits for
all
\mu>0.

Comparing with the case of
N=1,
Hartree-Fock type system (
1.1
) is
much more complicated when
N=2
. In order to further simplify it, we assume
the exchange potential

V_{\text{ex}}\psi=-C\binom{(\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{p-2}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}})\psi_{1}}{(\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{p-2}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2})\psi_{2}},

(1.4)

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
2<p<6
. Clearly, if
p=\frac{8}{3},
then (
1.4
) is written as

V_{\text{ex}}\psi=-C\binom{(\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{4}{3}})\psi_{1}}{(\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{4}{3}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}})\psi_{2}},

which can be viewed as an approximation of the exchange potential (
1.2
) proposed by Slater. Considering
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
real
functions, renaming them as
u,v,
and taking, for simplicity,
C=1
, system
(
1.1
) becomes the following autonomous Hartree-Fock type system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u+\mu\phi_{u,v}u=\left|u\right|^{p-2}u+\beta\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta v+v+\mu\phi_{u,v}v=\left|v\right|^{p-2}v+\beta\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}v&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi_{u,v}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(y)+v^{2}\left(y\right)}{|x-y|}dy.
For a solution
\left(u,v\right)
of system (
1.5
), we introduce some concepts of its triviality and
positiveness.

Definition 1.1

A vector function
\left(u,v\right)
is said to be

\left(i\right)
nontrivial if either
u\neq 0
or
v\neq 0;

\left(ii\right)
semitrivial if it is nontrivial but either
u=0
or
v=0;

\left(iii\right)
vectorial if both of
u
and
v
are not zero;

\left(iv\right)
nonnegative if
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0;

\left(v\right)
positive if
u>0
and
v>0.

If
\mu=0,
then system (
1.5
) is deduced to the local weakly coupled
nonlinear Schrödinger system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\left|u\right|^{p-2}u+\beta\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta v+v=\left|v\right|^{p-2}v+\beta\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}v&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

which arises in various physical phenomena, such as the occurrence of
phase-separation in Bose-Einstein condensates with multiple states, or the
propagation of mutually incoherent wave packets in nonlinear optics, see
e.g.
[
1
,
14
,
15
,
25
,
38
]
. The parameter
\beta
is the interaction
between the two components. the positive sign of
\beta
stays for
attractive interaction, while the negative sign stays for repulsive
interaction. In recent years, the existence and multiplicity of positive
vectorial solutions for system (
1.6
) have been extensively studied,
depending on the range of
\beta
, see e.g.
[
3
,
5
,
9
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
29
]
. Furthermore, for the study of nonlinear Schrödinger systems with an
external potential
V(x),
we refer the reader to
[
20
,
22
,
41
]
.

If
\mu>0,
then the typical characteristic of system (
1.5
) lies on
the presence of the double coupled terms, including a Coulomb interacting
one and a cooperative pure power one. Very recently, d’Avenia, Maia and
Siciliano
[
11
]
firstly studied the existence of radial vectorial
solutions to system (
1.5
) when either
3<p<4
and
\beta>0,
or
4\leq p<6
and
\beta\geq 2^{2q-1}-1.
The proof is based on the method of
Nehari-Pohozaev manifold defined in
\mathbf{H}_{r}:=H_{rad}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H_{rad}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
developed by Ruiz
[
26
]
. Later,
we
[
33
]
considered another interesting case, i.e.
2<p<3,
and proved
the existence of radial vectorial solutions for
\left(HF_{\beta}\right)

when either
\mu>0
small enough or
\beta>\beta(\mu).
In addition, for

\beta>\beta(\mu),
we also obtained vectorial ground states in
\mathbf{H}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
when either
3\leq p<4

and
0<\mu<\mu_{0},
or
\frac{1+\sqrt{73}}{3}\leq p<4
and
\mu>0.

In this paper we study the existence of vectorial solutions for the
Hartree-Fock type system with potentials

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V\left(x\right)u+\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}u=\left|u\right|^{p-2}u+\beta\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta v+V\left(x\right)v+\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}v=\left|v\right|^{p-2}v+\beta\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}v&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

\left(HF_{\beta}\right)

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
2<p<6
and

\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho\left(x\right)(u^{2}(y)+v^{2}\left(y\right))}{|x-y|}dy.

In addition,
V(x)
and
\rho(x)
represents an external potential and a
charge potential, respectively, which satisfy the following assumptions:

(D1)

V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
with
\lambda:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}V\left(x\right)>0
and
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)=V_{\infty}>0;

(D2)

\rho\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
with
\rho_{\min}:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)>0
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}

and
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\rho(x)=\rho_{\infty}>0.

Our approach is variational, and we look for solutions of system
\left(HF_{\beta}\right)
as critical points of the associated energy functional
J_{\beta}:\mathbf{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

J_{\beta}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p}+2\beta\left|u\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)dx,

where
\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}+V(x)v^{2})dx\right]^{1/2}
is
the standard norm in
\mathbf{H}.
Clearly,
J_{\beta}
is a well-defined
and
C^{1}
functional on
\mathbf{H}.

Observe that the case of
4\leq p<6
is trivial, and we only consider the
case of
2<p<4
in the whole paper.

We point out that the geometric properties of
J_{\beta}
have not been
studied in detail in existing papers
[
11
,
33
]
. One of the motivations
of this paper is to shed some light on the behavior of
J_{\beta}
on
\mathbf{H}
when
2<p<3
. As we shall see, under conditions
(D1)-(D2),

system
\left(HF_{\beta}\right)
does not admits any nontrivial solutions
for
\beta
sufficiently small, even positive, while
J_{\beta}
is not
bounded from below on
\mathbf{H}\
for
\beta>0
sufficiently large.
However, under additional assumptions on
V,\rho
(condition
(D3)
below),

J_{\beta}
is bounded from below on
\mathbf{H}
when
\beta
lies in a
finite interval
,
and we prove the existence of a global minimizer
with negative energy, which seems to be an interesting result. In addition,
we also find that the existence of vectorial solutions is subject to the
parameter
\beta
, regardless of size of the charge potential, which is
totally different from the Schrödinger–Poisson equation, e.g.
[
26
]
.

For the autonomous system (
1.5
) with
3\leq p<4
, we have concluded
that the existence of vectorial ground states depends on both
\mu>0

sufficiently small and
\beta>0
sufficiently large in
[
33
]
. For the
nonautonomous system
\left(HF_{\beta}\right)
with
3\leq p<4
, in this
paper we are likewise interested in finding vectorial ground states by
developing a new analytic method and exploring the conditions on
V,\rho.

However, unlike the autonomous system, we shall focus on relaxing the
restriction on the parameters, which is the second aim of our paper.

Note that nonradial ground states have been found in other frameworks
previously, such as in the Schrödinger–Poisson equation, see
[
27
,
40
]
, where the Coulomb interacting term need to be controlled. However,
to the best of our knowledge, there seems no any related result for the
Hartree-Fock type system like
\left(HF_{\beta}\right)
. In view of this,
the last object of this paper is to study the existence of nonradial ground
states for a class of Hartree-Fock type systems with potentials. As we shall
see, symmetry breaking phenomenon could occur when the cooperative pure
power term is controlled, not the Coulomb interacting one.

1.1
Main results

First of all, we consider the following minimization problem:

\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right):=\inf_{u\in\mathbf{H}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}.

(1.7)

where
\theta,k>0
are constants and
\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\theta u^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+\theta v^{2}\right)dx.
Then we have the following
proposition.

Proposition 1.2

Assume that
2<p<3
and
\theta,k
are positive constants. Then
we have

(i)

\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right)\geq\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{k}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}-1;

(ii)

\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right)
is achieved, i.e. there exists
\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\in\mathbf{H}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}
such that

\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right)=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u_{0}\right|^{p}+\left|v_{0}\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u_{0}\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v_{0}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}>0.

Then we have the following results.

Theorem 1.3

Assume that
2<p<3
and conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D2\right)
hold. Then we have the following statements.

\left(i\right)
there exists
\overline{\Lambda}_{0}\geq 2^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{\rho_{\min}}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}-1
such that for every
\beta\in\left(-\infty,\overline{\Lambda}_{0}\right),
system
(HF_{\beta})
does not admits any
nontrivial solutions;

\left(ii\right)
for every
\beta>\Lambda\left(V_{\max},\rho_{\max}\right),
there holds
\inf_{\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}}J_{\beta}(u,v)=-\infty,
where
V_{\max}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}V\left(x\right)
and
\rho_{\max}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right).

Theorem 1.4

Assume that
2<p<3
and conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D2\right)
hold. In addition, the following assumption also holds:

\left(D3\right)
there exist two positive constants
\lambda_{0},k_{0}

such that

V_{\infty}^{3-p}\rho_{\infty}^{p-2}>\frac{2^{\frac{6-p}{2}}}{p}\left(p-2\right)^{p-2}\left(3-p\right)^{3-p}\left(1+\Lambda\left(\lambda_{0},k_{0}\right)\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V\left(x\right)\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lambda_{0}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx+\frac{k_{0}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k_{0},\left(u,v\right)}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx,

where
\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\in\mathbf{H}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}
is a minimizer of the minimization problem (
1.7
) in Proposition
1.2
with
\theta=\lambda_{0}
and
k=k_{0}.

Then for every

\Lambda\left(\lambda_{0},k_{0}\right)<\beta<\frac{p}{2^{\frac{6-p}{2}}}\left(\frac{V_{\infty}}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}-1,

system
(HF_{\beta})
admits a vectorial ground state solution
\left(u_{\beta}^{\left(1\right)},v_{\beta}^{\left(1\right)}\right)\in\mathbf{H}
satisfying
J_{\beta}\left(u_{\beta}^{\left(1\right)},v_{\beta}^{\left(1\right)}\right)=\inf_{\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}}J_{\beta}(u,v)<0.

Remark 1.5

By conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D3\right),
for every

\Lambda\left(\lambda_{0},k_{0}\right)<\beta<\frac{p}{2^{\frac{6-p}{2}}}\left(\frac{V_{\infty}}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{\rho_{\infty}}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}-1,

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:V^{3-p}\left(x\right)\rho^{p-2}\left(x\right)<\frac{2^{\frac{6-p}{2}}}{p}\left(p-2\right)^{p-2}\left(3-p\right)^{3-p}\left(1+\beta\right)\right\}

is nonempty and

Theorem 1.6

Assume that
2<p<4
and conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D2\right)
hold. In addition, we assume that

\left(D4\right)

Then there exists

\left(D5\right)

V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

for

\left(ii\right)

then for every

system
(HF_{\beta})

Lemma 2.3

Lemma 2.4

Assume that
2<p<4,\beta>0
and conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D2\right)
hold. Then for each

, there exists
s_{z}\in\left(0,1\right)
such that

J_{\beta}\left(\sqrt{s_{z}}z,\sqrt{1-s_{z}}z\right)<J_{\beta}\left(z,0\right)=J_{\beta}\left(0,z\right)=I_{0}\left(z\right),

where

I_{0}(z):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla z|^{2}+V(x)z^{2}\right)dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,z}z^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|z\right|^{p}dx.

Proof.
Let
\left(u,v\right)=\left(\sqrt{s}z,\sqrt{1-s}z\right)
for
z\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and
s\in[0,1].

A direct calculation shows that

\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}=s\left\|z\right\|_{V}^{2}+\left(1-s\right)\left\|z\right\|_{V}^{2}=\left\|z\right\|_{V}^{2}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(sz^{2},\left(1-s\right)z^{2}\right)}\left(sz^{2}+\left(1-s\right)z^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,z}z^{2}dx,

\left\|u\right\|_{V}:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2})dx\right]^{1/2}\text{ and }\phi_{\rho,z}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho\left(y\right)z^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dy.

2.2

Thus, we have

\displaystyle J_{\beta}\left(\sqrt{s_{z}}z,\sqrt{1-s_{z}}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|z\right\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,z}z^{2}dx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|z\right\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,z}z^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|z\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle J_{\beta}\left(z,0\right)=J_{\beta}\left(0,z\right)=I_{0}\left(z\right).

The proof is complete.

\square

Lemma 2.5

Assume that
2<p<4,\beta>0
and conditions
\left(D1\right)-\left(D2\right)
hold. Let
\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\in\mathbf{H}

with

J_{\beta}\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)=\inf_{\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}}J_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)<\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I_{0}\left(u\right)<0.

Then

Proof.
Suppose that

Let
\left(u,v\right)=\left(\sqrt{s}u_{0},\sqrt{1-s}u_{0}\right)
for

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)}\left(su_{0}^{2}+\left(1-s\right)u_{0}^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{\rho,u_{0}}u_{0}^{2}dx.

\displaystyle J_{\beta}\left(\sqrt{s_{0}}u_{0},\sqrt{1-s_{0}}u_{0}\right)

3
Construction of natural constraints

Define the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{N}_{\beta}:=\{\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}\backslash\{\left(0,0\right)\}:\left\langle J_{\beta}^{\prime}\left(u,v\right),\left(u,v\right)\right\rangle=0\}.

Then
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}
if and only if

\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)dx=0.

It follows the Sobolev and Young inequalities that there exists
C_{\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\beta}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{p}\text{ for all
}u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}.

So it leads to

\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|\geq C_{\beta}^{-1/\left(p-2\right)}\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}.

(3.1)

The Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{\beta}
is closely linked to the behavior
of the function of the form
\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}:t\rightarrow J_{\beta}\left(tu,tv\right)
for
t>0.
Such maps are known as fibering
maps introduced by Drábek-Pohozaev
[
12
]
. For
\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H},
we find that

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+\frac{t^{4}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{t^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)dx,

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+t^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-t^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)dx,

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|^{2}+3t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\left(p-1\right)t^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(u,v\right)dx.

A direct calculation shows that

t\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime}\left(t\right)=\left\|\left(tu,tv\right)\right\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,\left(tu,tv\right)}\left(t^{2}u^{2}+t^{2}v^{2}\right)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\beta}\left(tu,tv\right)dx

and so, for
\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}
and
t>0,

\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime}\left(t\right)=0
holds if and only if
\left(tu,tv\right)\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}
. In particular,
\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime}\left(1\right)=0
holds if and only if
\left(u,v\right)\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}.
It becomes natural to split
\mathcal{N}_{\beta}
into three
parts corresponding to the local minima, local maxima and points of
inflection. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\beta}^{+}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}:\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)>0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\beta}^{0}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}:\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\beta}^{-}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}:\phi_{\beta,\left(u,v\right)}^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)<0\}.

By (
3.6
), there exists
\bar{\beta}_{0}=\bar{\beta}_{0}\left(\lambda,V_{\max},\rho_{\max}\right)\geq\beta_{1}
such that for every
\beta>\bar{\beta}_{0},

\displaystyle J_{\beta}\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\sqrt{s_{\beta}}w_{\beta},t_{\beta}^{-}\sqrt{1-s_{\beta}}w_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{2}}{2}\left\|w_{\beta}\right\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{4}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,w_{\beta}}w_{\beta}^{2}dx-\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{\beta}\left(s_{\beta}\right)w_{\beta}^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{2}}{2}\left\|w_{\beta}\right\|_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{4}\lambda}{4V_{\max}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\left(x\right)\phi_{\rho,w_{\beta}}w_{\beta}^{2}dx-\frac{\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\right)^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\lambda g_{\beta}\left(s_{\beta}\right)}{V_{\max}}w_{\beta}^{p}dx\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{V_{\max}}{\lambda}\left(\alpha_{\beta}^{\infty}+\frac{4\sqrt[3]{2}\rho_{\max}^{2}}{3\sqrt{3}\pi\lambda^{3/2}}\left(\frac{2}{4-p}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-2}}\left\|w_{\beta}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\left(p-2\right)V_{\max}}{p\lambda}\left(\frac{V_{\max}S_{\lambda,p}^{p}}{\lambda\left(1+\beta\right)}\right)^{2/\left(p-2\right)}+\frac{16\sqrt[3]{2}\rho_{\max}^{2}V_{\max}}{3\sqrt{3}\pi\lambda^{5/2}}\left(\frac{2}{4-p}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-2}}\left(\frac{V_{\max}S_{\lambda,p}^{p}}{\lambda\left(1+\beta\right)}\right)^{4/\left(p-2\right)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{3\sqrt{3}\pi\left(p-2\right)^{2}\lambda^{3/2}}{64\sqrt[3]{2}p(4-p)\rho_{\max}^{2}},\frac{p-2}{2p}S_{\lambda,p}^{2p/\left(p-2\right)}\right\},

which implies that
\left(t_{\beta}^{-}\sqrt{s_{\beta}}w_{\beta},t_{\beta}^{-}\sqrt{1-s_{\beta}}w_{\beta}\right)\in\mathcal{N}_{\beta}^{\left(1\right)}\cap\mathbf{H}_{r}.
The proof is complete.

\square

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\theta\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx+\frac{k}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{3}+\left|v\right|^{3})dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{d_{\theta,k}}{2p}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}=\frac{d_{\theta,k}}{2}-1,

where
d_{\theta,k}:=p\left(\frac{\theta}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{k}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}.
This shows that

\displaystyle\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\inf_{u\in\mathbf{H}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{3-p}\right)^{3-p}\left(\frac{k}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}-1.

\left(ii\right)
Let
\left\{\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\}\subset\mathbf{H}_{r}\setminus\{\left(0,0\right)\}
be a minimum sequence of (
1.7
). First of all, we claim that
\{\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\}
is
bounded in
H_{r}
. Suppose on the contrary. Then
\left\|(u_{n},v_{n})\right\|_{\theta}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
\Lambda\left(\lambda,k\right)>-\infty
and

\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u,v\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{p}+\left|v\right|^{p})dx}{\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx}=\Lambda\left(\lambda,k\right)+o\left(1\right),

there exists
C_{1}>\max\left\{-1,\Lambda\left(\theta,k\right)\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}\left(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u_{n}\right|^{p}+\left|v_{n}\right|^{p})dx

\displaystyle-\frac{2C_{1}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|u_{n}\right|^{\frac{{}^{p}}{2}}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx<0

for
n
sufficiently large. Similar to (
4.1
)–(
4.2
), we also have

\frac{k}{\sqrt{8}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\left|u\right|^{3}+v^{2}\left|u\right|)dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx

(7.4)

and

\frac{k}{\sqrt{8}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{2}\left|v\right|+\left|v\right|^{3})dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}dx+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u,v\right)}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)dx

(7.5)

for all
\left(u,v\right)\in\mathbf{H}_{r}.
Then it follows from (
7.3
)–(
7.5
) that

\widetilde{J}\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}\left(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{\theta,k}\left(u_{n}\right)+f_{\theta,k}\left(v_{n}\right))dx,

where
f_{\theta,k}\left(s\right):=\frac{\theta}{4}s^{2}+\frac{k}{\sqrt{8}}s^{3}-\frac{1+C_{1}}{p}s^{p}
for

It is clear that
f_{\theta,k}

is positive for
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
or
s\rightarrow\infty,
since
2<p<3

and
\theta,k>0.
Define

m_{\theta,k}:=\inf_{s>0}f_{\theta,k}(s).

If
m_{\theta,k}\geq 0,
then by (
7.3
) we have

0\geq\widetilde{J}\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2})dx>0,

which is a contradiction. We now assume that

Then the set

\left\{s>0:f_{\theta,k}\left(s\right)<0\right\}
is an open interval
\left(s_{1},s_{2}\right)

Note that constants

and

. Thus, there

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{\theta,k}\left(u_{n}\right)+f_{\theta,k}\left(v_{n}\right))dx

(7.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2})dx+\int_{D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}f_{\beta}\left(u_{n}\right)dx+\int_{D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}f_{\beta}\left(v_{n}\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{k,\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)}(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2})dx-\left|m_{\theta,k}\right|\left(\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\right),

where the sets
D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:u_{n}\left(x\right)\in\left(s_{1},s_{2}\right)\right\}
and
D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:v_{n}\left(x\right)\in\left(s_{1},s_{2}\right)\right\}.
It follows from (

) and (

) that

\left|m_{\theta,k}\right|\left(\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\right)>\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)\right\|_{\theta}^{2},

(7.7)

which implies that
\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
since
\left\|(u_{n},v_{n})\right\|_{\theta}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Moreover, since
D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}
and
D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}
are spherically
symmetric, we define
\rho_{n}^{\left(i\right)}:=\sup\left\{\left|x\right|:x\in D_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\right\}
for
i=1,2.
Then we
can take

such that
\left|x^{\left(i\right)}\right|=\rho_{n}^{\left(i\right)}.

Clearly,
u_{n}\left(x^{\left(1\right)}\right)=v_{n}\left(x^{\left(2\right)}\right)=s_{1}>0.
Recall the following Strauss’s inequality by

[
32
]
:

\left|z\left(x\right)\right|\leq c_{0}\left|x\right|^{-1}\left\|z\right\|_{H^{1}}\text{ for all }z\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(7.8)

for some

Thus, by (
7.7
)–(
7.8
), we have

0<s_{1}=u_{n}\left(x^{\left(1\right)}\right)<c_{0}\left(\rho_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right)^{-1}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq 2c_{0}\left|m_{\theta,k}\right|^{1/2}\left(\rho_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right)^{-1}\left(\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\right)^{1/2}

These imply that

c_{i}\rho_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\leq\left(\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\right)^{1/2}\text{ for some }c_{i}>0\text{ and }i=1,2.

(7.9)

On the other hand, since

we

\displaystyle\frac{8}{k^{2}}\left|m_{\beta}\right|\left(\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|+\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|\right)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{(u_{n},v_{n})}\left(u_{n}^{2}+v_{n}^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{n}^{2}(x)v_{n}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(x)v_{n}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}\int_{D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy+\int_{D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}\int_{D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}\frac{v_{n}^{2}(x)v_{n}^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy+2\int_{D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}v_{n}^{2}(y)\left(\int_{D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(x)}{|x-y|}dx\right)dy

\displaystyle s_{1}^{4}\left(\frac{\left|D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}\right|^{2}}{2\rho_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}+\frac{\left|D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}\right|^{2}}{2\rho_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}\right)+2\int_{D_{n}^{\left(2\right)}}v_{n}^{2}(y)\left(\int_{D_{n}^{\left(1\right)}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}(x)}{|x|+\left|y\right|}dx\right)dy

which is a contradiction. Hence, we conclude that

Walter A. Kehowski

Abstract

Fixed points

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N=2n-1
,
n\geq 1
. Then

F_{N-i}^{2}+F_{i}^{2}=F_{N-(2j+1)}F_{N+1}+F_{2j+1},

(4.1)

where

and
0\leq i\leq n-1
. Observe that

is always even, and that the indices
N-(2j+1)
and
2j+1
sum to

. If

Proof.

Let us assume that

\displaystyle F_{N-i}^{2}+F_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{N-j}^{2}+F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{(N+1)-2(j+1)}F_{(N+1)}+(-1)^{(N+1)-2(j+1)}F_{j+1}^{2}+F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{N-(2j+1)}F_{N+1}+F_{j+1}^{2}+F_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle F_{N-i}^{2}+F_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{N-(2j+1)}F_{N+1}+F_{2j+1},\quad(0\leq i\leq n-1).\qed

Let

This section shows that the fundamental cycles of type I admit extension to an arithmetic sequence of cycles in which the common differences are all Fibonacci numbers.

.

Let

Lemma 7.2

\displaystyle=F_{2i+2}

\displaystyle F_{N+1}F_{N-2i}

\displaystyle=F_{N+2}F_{N-2i-1}+F_{2i+2}.\qed

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

,

where

\displaystyle=F_{N-i}F_{N-i+1}+F_{i}F_{i+1}.\qed

7.1

Let

Definition 9.1

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)

Theorem 9.3

(Pell polynomial identities,
[
3
]
)
.

The Pell polynomials satisfy the following identities:

Pell-Cassini identity:

p_{n}^{2}(x)=p_{n+1}(x)p_{n-1}(x)+(-1)^{n+1}

(9.3)

Pell-Catalan identity:

p_{n}^{2}(x)=p_{n+r}(x)p_{n-r}(x)+(-1)^{n-r}p_{r}^{2}(x)

(9.4)

Pell-Vajda identity:

p_{n+r}(x)p_{n+s}(x)=p_{n}(x)p_{n+r+s}(x)+(-1)^{n}p_{r}(x)p_{s}(x)

(9.5)

Pell-Lucas identity:

p_{2n+1}(x)=p_{n+1}^{2}(x)+p_{n}^{2}(x)

(9.6)

Pell-d’Ocagne identity:

2x\,p_{2n}(x)=p_{n+1}^{2}(x)-p_{n-1}^{2}(x)

(9.7)

Remark 9.4
.

The “Pell” prefix is intended as descriptive and is unrelated to the historical discovery of the identities.

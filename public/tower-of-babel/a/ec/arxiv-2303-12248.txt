On two conserved quantities in the inviscid electron and Hall magnetohydrodynamic equations
Yanqing Wang Jing Yang and Yulin Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the energy and magnetic helicity conservation of weak solutions for both the electron and Hall magnetohydrodynamic equations. Various sufficient criteria to ensure the energy and magnetic helicity conservation in Onsager’s critical spaces B ¯ p , V ​ M ​ O α \underline{B}^{\alpha}_{p,VMO} and B p , c ⁡ ( ℕ ) α B^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})} in these systems are established. Moreover, for the E-MHD equations, we observe that the conservation criteria of energy and magnetic helicity to the E-MHD equations correspond to the helicity and energy to the ideal incompressible Euler equations, respectively.

Introduction

The magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations,
the electron (E-MHD) and Hall (H-MHD)
magnetohydrodynamic equations play an central role in the theory of plasma physical (see e.g.
[
22
,
19
,
8
]
).
The inviscid H-MHD equations may be written as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u-h\cdot\nabla h+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&h_{t}+u\cdot\nabla h-h\cdot\nabla u+\text{d}_{\text{I}}\nabla\times\Big[(\nabla\times h)\times h\Big]=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,u=\mathrm{div}\,h=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here
u
represents the velocity field,
h
stands for the magnetic field and
\Pi=\pi+\frac{1}{2}|h|^{2}
denotes the magnetic pressure with
\pi
being the fluid pressure, respectively.
We hereafter denote
\vec{j}=\text{curl}h
as the electric current.
Without loss of generality, we set the iron inertial length
\text{d}_{\text{I}}=1
. In the present paper, we consider the case of a bounded domain with periodic boundary condition in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, namely
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d=2,3
.
Formally, when the velocity
u
in the H-MHD equations (
1.1
) is zero, this system becomes the following E-MHD equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h_{t}+\nabla\times\Big[(\nabla\times h)\times h\Big]=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,h=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

For the derivation of the E-MHD system and its background, we refer the readers to
[
22
]
.
Moreover, if ignoring the Hall effect, namely letting
\text{d}_{I}=0
, then the Hall-MHD equations (
1.1
) reduce to the standard incompressible MHD equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u-h\cdot\nabla h+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&h_{t}+u\cdot\nabla h-h\cdot\nabla u=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,u=\mathrm{div}\,h=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

As known to all, in physics, the common conserved quantities in these three systems are the total energy and magnetic helicity (see
[
19
,
8
]
).
Precisely,
the energy in the E-MHD system (
1.2
) and H-MHD (MHD) equations (
1.1
) for smooth solutions are

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|h|^{2}dx
and
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|v|^{2}+|h|^{2}dx.

The magnetic helicity in these system is
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H\cdot{\rm curl\,}H\ dx,
where
H={\rm curl}^{-1}h
represents the magnetic vector potential.

The mathematical study of conserved quantities is very close to the Onsager’s conjecture on the energy conservation of weak solutions for the ideal Euler equations in
[
27
]
. The initial Onsager’s conjecture is that the critical regularity for weak solutions conserving the energy in the Euler equations is 1/3 in Hölder spaces.
As the initial Onsager’s conjecture for the Euler equations, two parts of the analogous Onsager’s conjecture for the usual MHD system (
1.3
) are well studied. For the positive part, the sufficient conditions of the weak solutions implying the energy and magnetic helicity conservation for the standard MHD system (
1.3
) can be found in
[
35
,
20
,
17
,
4
,
29
]
. Precisely, a weak solution
(u,h)
of the ideal MHD equations (
1.3
) satisfies the total energy conservation if one of the following conditions holds

[

u\in C(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha_{1}}),h\in C(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha_{2}}),\alpha_{1}>1/3,\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}>1;

•

Kang and Lee

u\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha_{1}}),h\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha_{2}}),\alpha_{1}\geq 1/3,\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\geq 1;

Yu
[
35

u\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha_{1}}),h\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha_{2}}),\alpha_{1}\geq 1/3,\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\geq 1;

•

Wang-Huang-Ye-Liu
[

\alpha\geq 1/3,\alpha+2\beta\geq 1,1/q_{1}+2/q_{2}=1,1/p_{1}+2/p_{2}=1,1/p_{2}+1/q_{1}\leq 1.

The magnetic helicity of weak solutions for the ideal MHD equations (

u\in C(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha_{1}}),h\in C(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha_{2}}),\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}>0;

Now, we turn our attentions back to the ideal E-MHD and H-MHD equations.
Compared with nonlinear term in terms of convection type of both the ideal Euler and the standard MHD equations, the Hall term
\nabla\times\Big[(\nabla\times h)\times h\Big]
in the E-MHD and H-MHD equations
involves the second order derivative rather than the first order derivative.
To the knowledge of authors,
there are only two works for positive part of
Onsager type results in the inviscid E-MHD and H-MHD equations.
Precisely, in
[
21
]
,
Kang-Deng-Bie showed that if

u,h\in L^{p}\cap B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}),~\vec{j}=\text{curl}h\in B^{\beta}_{p,\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}),\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,2\alpha+\beta>1,

(1.5)

then the total energy of weak solutions for the ideal H-MHD equations (
1.1
) is conserved.
On the other hand, Dai-Krol-Liu in
[
12
]
showed that the weak solutions for the ideal E-MHD equations (
1.2
) preserve
the energy if
h\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\frac{2}{3}})
and
magnetic helicity if

h\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\frac{1}{3}}),

(1.6)

respectively.
The intention of this paper is to establish new energy and
magnetic helicity conservation criterion of weak solutions for both the inviscid E-MHD and
H-MHD equations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d=2,3
and
h\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be a weak solution of the incompressible E-MHD equations (
1.2
). Then for any
t\in[0,T]
, the energy
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|h|^{2}dx
is invariant provided that one of the following four conditions is satisfied

(1)

h\in L^{3}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
;

(2)

\vec{j}\in L^{r_{1}}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\alpha}_{p,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),h\in L^{r_{2}}(0,T;B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),
\alpha+2\beta\geq 1,\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{2}{r_{2}}=1,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1
;

(3)

\vec{j}\in L^{r_{1}}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),h\in L^{r_{2}}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\beta}_{q,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),
\alpha+2\beta\geq 1,\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{2}{r_{2}}=1,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1
;

(4)

\vec{j}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

Remark 1.1
.

According to inclusion relations (
1.4
) for Besov spaces, we know that this theorem improves the corresponding results in
[
12
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Since there holds

B^{\gamma}_{\ell,c(\mathbb{N})}\subseteq\underline{B}^{\gamma}_{\ell,VMO}
, for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\ell\in[1,\infty]
, the results in (2) and (3) are still valid by replacing
\underline{B}^{\gamma}_{\ell,VMO}
by
B^{\gamma}_{\ell,c(\mathbb{N})}
.

Remark 1.3
.

It seems that the energy conservation criteria for the E-MHD equations is by the analogy of
the helicity conservation roles of the ideal Euler equations due to Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy
[
7
]
.
And the results (2)-(4) are partial motived for the investigation of the helicity of the ideal Euler equations by Chae in
[

Before we state the result concerning the magnetic helicity for E-MHD equations (
1.2
), we recall that the magnetic vector potential equations take the form below

H_{t}+(\nabla\times h)\times h+\nabla p=0,~\text{div}H=0,

(1.7)

where

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d=3
and
\vec{j}\in C(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

be a weak solution of the inviscid E-MHD equations (
1.2
). If
h\in L^{3}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),

then for any

, the magnetic helicity is invariant, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}H\cdot{\rm curl\,}H\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}H_{0}\cdot{\rm curl\,}H_{0}\ dx,

where
H={\rm curl}^{-1}h
.

Remark 1.4
.

Owing to
inclusion relations (
1.4
), we see that this theorem generalizes the sufficient condition (
1.6
) for the conservation of magnetic helicity.

Remark 1.5
.

It seems that the magnetic helicity conservation criteria for the E-MHD equations corresponds to
the energy conservation roles of the ideal Euler equations Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy
[
7
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

It seems that this is the first result for weak solutions keeping magnetic helicity for the inviscid Hall-MHD equations (

Notations and some auxiliary lemmas

First, we introduce some notations used in this paper.
For
p\in[1,\,\infty]
, the notation
L^{p}(0,\,T;X)
stands for the set of measurable functions
f
on the interval
(0,\,T)
with values in
X
and
\|f\|_{X}
belonging to
L^{p}(0,\,T)
.

Second,
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the Besov semi-norm
\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
and Besov norm
\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\||y|^{-s}\|f(x-y)-f(x)\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|_{L_{y}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

In the spirit of
[
18
]
, we recall
the Besov-VMO space
L^{p}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\alpha}_{q,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
of function
f
if it satisfies

\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,\,~\,~\,|f(x)-f(y)|^{q}dydx\Big]^{\frac{p}{q}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|f(x)-f(x-y)|^{q}dydx\Big]^{\frac{p}{q}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=0.

Eventually, we let
\eta_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a standard mollifier, i.e.
\eta(x)=C_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}}
for
|x|<1
and
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
, where

is a constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(x)dx=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define the rescaled mollifier by
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
, and for any function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its mollified version is defined by

f^{\varepsilon}(x)=(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

The key point of this lemma is that the following
Constantin-E-Titi identity in
[
9
]

\displaystyle(fg)^{\varepsilon}(x)-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}(x)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\Big[f(x-y)-f(x)\Big]\Big[g(x-y)-g(x)\Big]dy-(f-f^{\varepsilon})(g-g^{\varepsilon})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II.

(1) It follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|f(x-y)-f(x)|^{q_{1}}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\Big[\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|g(x-y)-g(x)|^{q_{2}}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}.

Performing a space-time integration and using the definition of Besov-VMO space, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we note that

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|f(x-y)-f(x)|^{q_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\Big\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T)}\Big\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|g(x-y)-g(x)|^{q_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta}),

where we need to require
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
.

We deduce from the Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.1
that, as

\displaystyle\|II\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|g-g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta}).

(2.5)

Then substituting (

(2) The Hölder inequality implies that

|I|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\Big[\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\big|\eta\big(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\big)[g(x-y)-g(x)]\big|^{\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}dy\Big]^{1-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\Big[\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}|f(x-y)-f(x)|^{q_{1}}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}.

Combining this, the Hölder inequality again and Minkowski inequality, we find

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\big\|g(x-y)-g(x)\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}dy\Big]^{1-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|f(x-y)-f(x)|^{q_{1}}dydx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}},

where we used
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
and
q_{2}\geq\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}
.
Thereby, by utilizing Hölder inequality and Minkowski inequality once again, as
\varepsilon\to 0

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\|g(x-y)-g(x)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}dy\Big]^{1-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-~\,~\,~\,~\,|f(x-y)-f(x)|^{q_{1}}dydx\Big\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta}).

Plugging (

First, we show the energy conservation of weak solutions for the E-MHD equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In what follows, for the sake of simplicity, we assume that the weak solution
h
of the inviscid E-MHD equations (
1.2

[

Multiplying

3.1
)
by

) as

. For the convenience of proof, we denote the second term on the left-hand side of (
3.2

.
First, in light of (
2.15

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\times\Big[(\nabla\times h)\times h\Big]^{\varepsilon}h^{\varepsilon}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla\times\Big[\text{div}(h\otimes h)\Big]^{\varepsilon}h^{\varepsilon}dxdt

\displaystyle C\|(h\otimes h)^{\varepsilon}-h^{\varepsilon}\otimes h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\|\nabla h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}

Since

and

\displaystyle\|(h\otimes h)^{\varepsilon}-h^{\varepsilon}\otimes h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|h\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q}(0,T;\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\|h\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(0,T;\mathbb{T}^{3}))},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla h^{\varepsilon}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))},

(5.11)

) as

(5.12)

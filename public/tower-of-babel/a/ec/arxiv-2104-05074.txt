Compactness and Large-Scale Regularity for Darcy’s Law
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the quantitative homogenization of the steady Stokes equations with the Dirichlet condition in a periodically perforated domain. Using a compactness method, we establish the large-scale interior C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} and Lipschitz estimates for the velocity as well as the corresponding estimates for the pressure. These estimates, when combined with the classical regularity estimates for the Stokes equations, yield the uniform Lipschitz estimates. As a consequence, we also obtain the uniform W k , p W^{k,p} estimates for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . Keywords : Stokes equations; perforated domain; large-scale regularity; Darcy law. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35Q35; 35B27; 76D07.

Introduction

In this paper we continue the study of the quantitative homogenization of the steady Stokes equations
for an incompressible viscous fluid,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=f,\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with a no-slip (Dirichlet) boundary condition on solid pores,
in a periodically perforated domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
In (
1.1
),
\mu>0
is the viscosity constant, and
we have normalized the velocity vector by a factor
\varepsilon^{2}
, where
\varepsilon>0
is the period.
It is well known
that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the effective equations for (
1.1
)
are given by a Darcy law
[
19
,
23
,
1
,
15
,
2
,
17
,
3
]
.
In
[
21
]
we established the sharp
O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
convergence rate in a bounded domain
by constructing some boundary correctors.
In this paper we will investigate the large-scale regularity problem for solutions
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
.

To describe the porous domain, we let
Y=(0,1)^{d}
be an open unit cube and

Y_{s}
(solid part) an open subset of
Y
with Lipschitz boundary.
Throughout the paper we assume that dist
(\partial Y,\partial Y_{s})>0
and that

Y_{f}=\overline{Y}\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}
(the fluid part) is connected.
Let

\omega=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(Y_{f}+z)

(1.2)

be the periodic repetition of
Y_{f}
. For
R>0
, let

Q_{R}=(-R,R)^{d}\quad\text{ and }\quad Q_{R}^{\varepsilon}=Q_{R}\cap\varepsilon\omega.

(1.3)

The following are the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(Q_{R}^{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(Q_{R}^{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=f&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{R}^{\varepsilon},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{R}^{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }Q_{R}\cap\partial(\varepsilon\omega),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
0<\varepsilon<R/2
and
f\in C^{\alpha}(Q_{R};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+R^{\alpha}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(Q_{R})}\right\}

(1.5)

for any
\varepsilon\leq r<R/2
, where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\alpha
, and
Y_{s}
.

In (
1.5
) (and thereafter) we have extended
u_{\varepsilon}
to
Q_{R}
by zero.
In the next theorem,
W(y)=(W_{j}^{i}(y))
is a 1-periodic
d\times d
matrix-valued function, defined by the cell problem
(
2.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then

\displaystyle\inf_{E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}\nabla W(x/\varepsilon)E|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\inf_{E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\beta}\left\{\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+R^{\alpha}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(Q_{R})}\right\}

for any
0<\varepsilon\leq r<R/2
,
where
0<\beta<\alpha
and
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
Y_{s}
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
give the large-scale interior Lipschitz and
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates
for the Stokes equations (
1.1
) in a periodically perforated domain.
We also obtain the corresponding large-scale estimates for the pressure
p_{\varepsilon}
.
See Section
6
.
We remark that the large-scale estimates for
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
hold under the assumption that
Y_{s}
is an open set with Lipschitz boundary.
If the boundary of
Y_{s}
is smooth,
we may combine the classical regularity estimates for the Stokes equations (with
\varepsilon=1
) in
Y\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}
with these large-scale estimates to
obtain regularity estimates that are uniform in
\varepsilon>0
.
In particular, this yields

\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R/2})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R/2})}\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+R^{\alpha}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(Q_{R})}\right\}

(1.7)

for
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
R>0
,
where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
\alpha
, and
Y_{s}
.
See Remark
5.7
.

Our approach to Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is based on a compactness method, originated in the study of regularity problems
for nonlinear PDEs and minimal surfaces.
The method was introduced in a seminal work
[
7
]
by M. Avellaneda and F. Lin to the study of the quantitative homogenization theory
(see
[
14
]
for the use of the compactness method for the Stokes equations with periodic coefficients in a fixed domain).
Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon_{j}},p_{\varepsilon_{j}})\}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.4
) with
R=4
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
.
Assume that
\{u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(Q_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
To apply the compactness method to the Stokes equations in perforated domains with the Dirichlet condition,
the key is to extract a subsequence, still denoted by
\{(u_{\varepsilon_{j}},p_{\varepsilon_{j}})\}
, such that

P_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to p_{0}
in
L^{2}(Q_{1})
, where
P_{\varepsilon}
is a suitable extension of
p_{\varepsilon}
defined by (
2.6
), and that

u_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon_{j})(f-\nabla p_{0})\to 0\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.8)

While the strong convergence
P_{\varepsilon_{j}}
in
L^{2}
may be proved as in the classical work

[
23
,
1
,
15
,
2
,
17
,
3
]

on Darcy’s law, the strong convergence for
u_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.8
) was only known previously in the case
when the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}
has the same Dirichlet data on a fixed boundary
[
2
,
3
]
.
One of the main technical contributions of this work is establishing the compactness property (
1.8
)
for a sequence of solutions with a uniform
L^{2}
bound for
u_{\varepsilon}
.
This is done by first proving a boundary layer estimate,

\left(\int_{Q_{1+\delta}\setminus Q_{1-\delta}}|\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\delta^{\sigma}\big\{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{4})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{4})}\big\}

(1.9)

for
\varepsilon\leq\delta<1/2
, where
C
and
\sigma>0
depend only on
d
,
\mu
, and
Y_{s}
.
The proof of (
1.9
) uses the self-improving property of the (weak) reverse Hölder inequalities as well as an energy estimate in
[
21
]

and the nontangential-maximal-function estimates in
[
9
]
for the Stokes equations in a bounded (unperforated) Lipschitz domain.
With (
1.9
) at our disposal, (
1.8
) is proved by applying the two-scale convergence method.

The large-scale regularity estimates in the homogenization theory have been studied extensively in recent years.
Besides the compactness method, there is another approach
that is based on the convergence rate and is effective in both the periodic and non-periodic settings
for second-order elliptic systems with oscillating coefficients (see
[
6
,
10
,
5
,
20
]
for references).
In a recent work
[
21
]
the present author was able to establish the sharp convergence rate for the Stokes equations
(
1.1
) in a periodically perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.
However, since the results are proved by energy estimates, the bounds for solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
and their divergences cannot be separated.
As a result, the error bound in
[
21
]
requires a strong condition for the normal component of
u_{\varepsilon}
on the fixed boundary
\partial\Omega
,
which is difficult to handle in the approximation scheme.

For second-order elliptic equations and systems in perforated domains, the large-scale regularity estimates
may be found in
[
24
,
27
,
26
,
25
,
18
,
4
,
22
,
8
]
,
where the Neumann type conditions are imposed on the boundaries of
the solid obstacles. In this case, the effective equations are of the same type
and the effective solutions share the same boundary data as
u_{\varepsilon}
on the fixed boundary.
To the best of the author’s knowledge, the paper
[
16
]
by N. Masmoudi seems
to be the only one that treats the Stokes equations with the Dirichlet condition
on the boundaries of solid pores.
In particular, the uniform
W^{k,p}
estimates for
the Stokes equations (
1.1
) in
\varepsilon\omega
with smooth boundary were stated in
[
16
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
without proof
(no proof has appeared since).
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we are able to provide a proof for the uniform
W^{k,p}
estimates.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\partial Y_{s}
is of
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
.
Let

and

Then there exist a unique

(1.10)

for some

.
Moreover,

\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\varepsilon\omega)}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\varepsilon\omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|\nabla p_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{-1,q}(\varepsilon\omega)}\leq C\big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\varepsilon\omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\varepsilon\omega)}\big\},

(1.11)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
q
, and
Y_{s}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\partial Y_{s}
is of
C^{k,\alpha}
for some
k\geq 2
and
0<\alpha<1
.
Let
F\in W^{k-2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some

.
Then there exists a unique

such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}&=F&\quad&\text{ in }\ \varepsilon\omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\ \varepsilon\omega,\\

Lemma 2.2

(v,\tau)\in H^{1}(Q_{t};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(Q_{t})

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta v+\nabla\tau&=0&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{t},\\
\text{\rm div}(v)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{t},\\
v&=h&\quad&\text{ on }\partial Q_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

for some
t>0
, where
h\in H^{1}(\partial Q_{t};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the compatibility condition

\int_{\partial Q_{t}}h\cdot n\,d\sigma=0.

Then there exist
q_{0}\in(1,2)
and
C>0
, depending only on
d
, such that

\left(\fint_{Q_{t}}|v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{\partial Q_{t}}|h|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}},

(3.4)

and

\left(\fint_{Q_{t}}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{\partial Q_{t}}|\nabla_{\tan}h|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}.

(3.5)

Proof.

By dilation we may assume
t=1
.
To prove (
3.5
), we use the energy estimates to obtain

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial Q_{1})}\leq C\|h\|_{W^{1,q_{0}}(\partial Q_{1})},

where
\frac{2(d-1)}{d}<q_{0}<2
, and we have used the Sobolev imbedding on
\partial Q_{1}
for the last inequality.
Replacing
v
be
v-E
, with
E=\fint_{\partial Q_{1}}h
, we obtain (
3.5
) by a
Poincaré inequality on
\partial Q_{1}
.

To see (
3.4
), we use the nontangential-maximal-function estimate,

\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\partial Q_{1})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\partial Q_{1})}.

(3.6)

The estimate (
3.6
) was proved in
[
9
]
for the Stokes equations in bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
, where

|q_{0}-2|<\sigma
and
\sigma>0
depends only on
d
and the Lipschitz characters of
\Omega
.
As a result, (
3.6
) holds for some
\frac{2(d-1)}{d}<q_{0}<2
, depending only on
d
.
This, together with the estimate,

\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\leq C\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\partial Q_{1})},

(3.7)

gives (
3.4
).

Finally, to see
3.7
, we use the observation

|v(x)|\leq C\int_{\partial Q_{1}}\frac{(v)^{*}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-1}}\,d\sigma(y)

for any
x\in Q_{1}
. It follows that

\Big|\int_{Q_{1}}v(x)g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq C\int_{\partial Q_{1}}(v)^{*}(y)G(y)\,d\sigma(y),

where

G(y)=\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x-y|^{d-1}}\,dx.

Since

\|G\|_{L^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(\partial Q_{1})}\leq C\|G\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial Q_{1})}\leq C\|G\|_{H^{1}(Q_{1})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})},

we obtain (
3.7
) by a duality argument.
∎

In the proof of the next lemma, we will use the following observation: there exists
c_{0}>0
, depending only on
d
and

Y_{s}
, such that

\text{\rm dist}(\partial Q_{t},\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon\omega)\geq c_{0}\varepsilon\quad\text{ if }\ \ \text{\rm dist}(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{N})\leq c_{0}\varepsilon.

(3.8)

The case
\varepsilon=1
follows from the assumption that dist
(\partial Y,\partial Y_{s})>0
, while the general case follows by dilation.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(Q_{2R}^{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(Q_{2R}^{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{2R}^{\varepsilon}
with

u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
Q_{2R}\cap\partial(\varepsilon\omega)
, where
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
R\in\varepsilon\mathbb{N}
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{2R}}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
q_{0}\in(1,2)
is given by Lemma
3.2
, and
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and
Y_{s}
.

Proof.

By dilation we may assume
R=1
and
\varepsilon^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
.
We first observe that by Fubini’s Theorem, there exists
t\in[1,2]
such that
dist
(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{N})\leq c_{0}\varepsilon
and

\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{\partial Q_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial Q_{t}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,d\sigma\leq C_{0}\left\{\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{Q_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx+\int_{Q_{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right\},

(3.10)

where
C_{0}
depends on
d
and
Y_{s}
.
For otherwise, suppose that for any
t\in[1,2]
with dist
(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{N})\leq c_{0}\varepsilon
,

\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{\partial Q_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial Q_{t}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,d\sigma>C_{0}\left\{\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{Q_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx+\int_{Q_{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right\}.

By integrating the inequality above with respect to
t
over the set

E_{\varepsilon}=\big\{t\in(1,2):\text{\rm dist}(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{N})\leq c_{0}\varepsilon\big\},

and using the observation that
|E_{\varepsilon}|\geq c>0
,
we obtain

\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{Q_{2}\setminus Q_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx+\int_{Q_{2}\setminus Q_{1}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx\geq C_{1}C_{0}\left\{\varepsilon^{q_{0}}\int_{Q_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx+\int_{Q_{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right\},

where
C_{1}
depends only on
d
and
c_{0}
. This gives a contradiction if we choose
C_{0}=(2C_{1})^{-1}
.

Next, let
(v,\tau)
be a weak solution of (
3.3
) in
Q_{t}
with Dirichlet data
h=u_{\varepsilon}
on
\partial Q_{t}
.
Since dist
(\partial Q_{t},\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon\omega)\geq c_{0}\varepsilon
,
by the energy estimates for the Stokes equations in periodically perforated domains
in
[
21
, Inequality (3.9)]
, we deduce that

\varepsilon^{2}\int_{Q_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{Q_{t}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq C\left\{\varepsilon^{2}\int_{Q_{t}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{Q_{t}}|v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{Q_{t}}|f|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

This, together with (
3.4
) and (
3.10
), gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t})}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon\|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\partial Q_{t})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\partial Q_{t})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{t})}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(Q_{2})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(Q_{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}\Big\},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
Q^{\varepsilon}(x_{0},4R)

with
u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
Q(x_{0},4R)\cap\partial(\varepsilon\omega)
,
where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
R\geq 2\varepsilon
.
Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},R)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},R)}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},4R)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},4R)}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},4R)}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
q_{0}\in(1,2)
is given by Lemma
3.3
.
Indeed, by (
3.9
) and translation, (
3.11
) holds if
x_{0}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

R\in\varepsilon\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, in this case,
Q(x_{0},4R)
in the right-hand side is replaced by
Q(x_{0},2R)
.
For the general case, we choose
y_{0}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
R_{1}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{N}
such that

Q(x_{0},R)\subset Q(y_{0},R_{1})\quad\text{ and }\quad Q(y_{0},2R_{1})\subset Q(x_{0},4R),

which is possible under the assumption
R\geq 2\varepsilon
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By rescaling we may assume
R=1
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
.
We also assume
0<\varepsilon<c
, where
c>0
is sufficiently small; the case
c\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
is trivial.

Let
q_{0}\in(1,2)
be given by Lemma
3.3
. Define

G_{\varepsilon}(y)=\sup\left(\fint_{Q(z,r)}\big(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|\big)^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}},

(3.12)

where the supremum is taken over all
Q(z,r)
with the properties that

y\in Q(z,r)
,
r\geq 2\varepsilon
, and
Q(z,r)\subset Q_{2}
.
We will show that

\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{2}}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{2}}|f|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

(3.13)

for some
q>2
, depending only on
d
,
\mu
, and
Y_{s}
.
Note that by the
L^{2/q_{0}}
boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator,

\left(\fint_{Q_{2}}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{2}}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Also, observe that by (
3.11
),

\left(\fint_{Q(x,2\varepsilon)}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq CG_{\varepsilon}(x)+C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8\varepsilon)}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for
x\in Q_{1}
.
It follows that

\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|g_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{2}}|f|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

As a result, the estimate (
3.2
) follows from (
3.13
).

Finally, to prove (
3.13
),
we use the well-known self-improving property of (weak) reverse Hölder inequalities
[
12
]
.
Consequently,
it suffices to show that

\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.14)

for any
x\in Q_{1}
and
0<t<c
.
We divide the proof of (
3.14
) into two cases.

Case 1. Suppose
0<t<4\varepsilon
. Observe that

G_{\varepsilon}(y)\sim G_{\varepsilon}(z)\quad\text{ for }y,z\in Q(x,t).

This implies that

\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}.

Case 2. Suppose
4\varepsilon\leq t<c
. For
y\in Q(x,t)
, write

G_{\varepsilon}(y)=\max\left(G^{(1)}_{\varepsilon}(y),G_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(y)\right),

where
G_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}
is defined as in (
3.12
), but with the supremum being taken over all

Q^{\varepsilon}(z,r)
with the properties that

y\in Q(z,r)
,
r\geq 2\varepsilon
, and
Q(z,r)\subset Q(x,2t)
.
By the
L^{2/q_{0}}
boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator,
we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G^{(1)}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,2t)}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{Q(x,8t)}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where we have used (
3.11
) for the second inequality.
Since

G_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(y)\sim G_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(z)\quad\text{ for }y,z\in Q(x,t),

we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}|G_{\varepsilon}|^{q_{0}}\right)^{1/q_{0}}.

As a result, we have proved (
3.14
) for Case 2.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(Q_{3}^{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(Q_{3}^{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{3}^{\varepsilon}
with

u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
Q_{3}\cap\partial(\varepsilon\omega)
,
where

. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Q_{1+\delta}\setminus Q_{1-\delta}}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{\sigma}\left\{\left(\int_{Q_{3}}(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|+|u_{\varepsilon}|)^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3})}\right\},

for any

, where

and

depend only on

,

, and

.

; for otherwise the estimate is trivial.

.

∎

4
Compactness

The goal of this section is to establish the compactness in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon_{j}},p_{\varepsilon_{j}})\}
be a sequence of weak solutions of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon_{j}}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon_{j}}&=f_{\varepsilon_{j}}&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{4}^{\varepsilon_{j}},\\
\text{\rm div}(u_{\varepsilon_{j}})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{4}^{\varepsilon_{j}},\\
u_{\varepsilon_{j}}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }Q_{4}\cap\partial(\varepsilon_{j}\omega),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where
\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
.
Assume that

\fint_{Q_{4}}|u_{\varepsilon_{j}}|^{2}\leq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{4})}\leq 1

(4.2)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{(u_{\varepsilon_{j}},p_{\varepsilon_{j}})\}
, and

f\in C^{\alpha}(Q_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p_{0}\in H^{1}(Q_{2})
, such that

f_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to f
uniformly in
Q_{4}
,

P_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\fint_{Q_{2}}P_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to p_{0}\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{2}),

(4.3)

u_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon_{j})(f-\nabla p_{0})\to 0\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(4.4)

and

\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\mu^{-1}\nabla W(x/\varepsilon_{j})(f-\nabla p_{0})\to 0\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),

(4.5)

where
P_{\varepsilon_{j}}
denotes the extension of
p_{\varepsilon_{j}}
defined by (
2.6
).

Proof.

We divide the proof of Theorem
4.1
into several steps.

Step 1.

By subtracting a constant we may assume
\int_{Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}}}p_{\varepsilon_{j}}\,dx=0
.
It follows from Caccioppoli’s inequality (
2.9
) and (
4.2
) that

\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}+\|P_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\fint_{Q_{2}}P_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}\leq C.

(4.6)

Thus, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\fint_{Q_{2}}{P_{\varepsilon_{j}}}\to p_{0}\ \ \text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q_{2}),\\
&u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\ \text{ two-scale converges to }u_{0}(x,\xi),\\
&\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\ \text{ two-scale converges to }\nabla_{\xi}u_{0}(x,\xi),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.7)

for some
p_{0}\in L^{2}(Q_{2})
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(Q_{2};H^{1}_{per}(Y;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Moreover, since
u_{\varepsilon_{j}}=0
in
Q_{2}\setminus(\varepsilon_{j}\omega)
and div
(u_{\varepsilon_{j}})=0
in
Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}
,
the limit
u_{0}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{0}(x,\xi)=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}\times Y_{s},\\
&\text{\rm div}_{\xi}u_{0}(x,\xi)=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}\times Y,\\
&\text{\rm div}_{x}\int_{Y}u_{0}(x,\xi)\,d\xi=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.8)

Clearly, by passing to a subsequence, we may also assume that
f_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to f
uniformly in
Q_{4}

for some
f\in C^{\alpha}(Q_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})

with
\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{4})}\leq 1
.

Step 2.

We show that

P_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\fint_{Q_{2}}{P_{\varepsilon_{j}}}\to p_{0}\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{2}).

(4.9)

The proof is the same as in the case with boundary value
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial Q_{2}
.
See e.g.
[
3
]
. We sketch a proof here for the reader’s convenience.
The key is to show that for any
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle|<\nabla P_{\varepsilon_{j}},\psi>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2})}|

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{2})}\Big\}\Big\{\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}\Big\}.

To see (
4.10
), let
R_{\varepsilon_{j}}:H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the restriction operator defined in
[
3
, Lemma 1.7]
.
Then

\displaystyle|<\nabla P_{\varepsilon_{j}},\psi>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2})}|

\displaystyle=|<\nabla p_{\varepsilon_{j}},R_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\psi)>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}|

\displaystyle=|<\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon_{j}}+f_{\varepsilon_{j}},R_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\psi)>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\mu\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}\|\nabla R_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\psi)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}\|+\|f_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}\|R_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\psi)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2}^{\varepsilon_{j}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{2})}+\|f_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Q^{\varepsilon_{j}}_{2})}\Big\}\Big\{\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}\Big\}.

The estimate (
4.10
) implies (
4.9
).
For otherwise,
\nabla P_{\varepsilon_{j}}
does not converge to
\nabla p_{0}
in
H^{-1}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It follows that there exists a sequence
\{\psi_{j}\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

|<\nabla P_{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}}-\nabla p_{0},\psi_{j}>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2})}|\geq c_{0}>0

for a subsequence
\{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}\}
.
By passing to a subsequence we may assume
\psi_{j}\to\psi_{0}
weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
thus strongly in
L^{2}(Q_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since

<\nabla P_{\varepsilon_{j}}-\nabla p_{0},\psi_{0}>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2})}\to 0,

we see that

|<\nabla P_{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}},\psi_{j}-\psi_{0}>_{H^{-1}(Q_{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(Q_{2})}|\geq c_{0}/2

if

is sufficiently large. This leads to a contradiction if we take
\psi=\psi_{j}-\psi_{0}
in (
4.10
).

Step 3.
We show that

u_{0}(x,\xi)=\mu^{-1}W(\xi)(f-\nabla p_{0})\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}.

(4.11)

By using the Stokes equations in
Q_{2}^{\varepsilon}

and the two-scale convergence of
\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}
, we have

\mu\int_{Q_{2}\times Y}\nabla_{\xi}u_{0}(x,\xi)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi=\int_{Q_{2}\times Y}f(x)\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi

(4.12)

for any
\psi=\psi(x,\xi)\in L^{2}(Q_{2};H_{per}^{1}(Y;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
satisfying the conditions,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{\rm div}_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi)=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}\times Y,\\
&\psi(x,\xi)=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{2}\times Y_{s},\\
&\text{\rm div}_{x}\int_{Y}\psi(x,\xi)\,d\xi=0\quad\text{ for }x\in Q_{2},\\
&n\cdot\int_{Y}\psi(x,\xi)\,d\xi=0\quad\text{ for }x\in\partial Q_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.13)

where
n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial Q_{2}
.
See
[
3
, p.48-89]
.
Let
p_{*}\in H^{1}(Q_{2})
be a weak solution of the Neumann problem,

and

It remains to show that

in
Q_{2}
. To this end, we note that
by using the Stokes equations in

, (

) and the two-scale convergence of

\displaystyle\mu\int_{Q_{2}\times Y}\nabla_{\xi}u_{0}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi-\int_{Q_{2}\times Y}p_{0}(x)\,\text{\rm div}_{x}\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{2}\times Y}f(x)\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi,

if

Step 4.

We show that

Let

Observe that

\displaystyle I_{j}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\mu^{2}\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}|^{2}\,dx-2\mu\int_{Q_{1}}\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\nabla W(x/\varepsilon_{j})(f-\nabla p_{0})\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla W(x/\varepsilon_{j})(f-\nabla p_{0})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=I_{j}^{1}+I_{j}^{2}+I_{j}^{3}.

Since
\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{j}}
two-scale converges to

in

\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{1}\times Y}|\nabla W(\xi)(f-\nabla p_{0})|^{2}\,dxd\xi

|E(k)|\leq C\Big\{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{1})}\Big\},

depends only on

,
\mu
, and

.

Proof.

The proof uses the following observation,

|E|\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(5.13)

for any
r\geq\varepsilon
and

, where
C
depends only on
d
,
\mu
, and
Y_{s}

Let

\displaystyle|E(\ell)-E(\ell-1)|\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{\theta^{\ell}}}|\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)(E(\ell)-E(\ell-1))|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{\theta^{\ell}}}|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E(\ell)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{\theta^{\ell}}}|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E(\ell-1)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{\theta^{\ell}}}|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E(\ell)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{\theta^{\ell-1}}}|u_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{-1}W(x/\varepsilon)E(\ell-1)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

where we have used (
5.8

It follows that

\displaystyle|E(k)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|E(\ell)-E(\ell-1)|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{1})}\big\}.

∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(u,p)\in H^{1}_{loc}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}_{loc}(\omega)
be a weak solution of the Stokes
equations

-\mu\Delta u+\nabla p=f\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm div}(u)=0\qquad\text{ in }\omega,

with
u=0
on
\partial\omega
, where
f
is constant.
Assume that there exist some
C>0
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
such that

\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq CR^{\sigma}

(6.8)

for any
R>1
. Then

u=\mu^{-1}W(x)E\quad\text{ and }\quad p=(\pi(x)-x)\cdot E+x\cdot f(0)+\gamma,

(6.9)

for some
E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Choose
\alpha
,
\beta
so that
\sigma<\beta<\alpha<1
.
We apply the estimate (
5.9
) with
\varepsilon=1
to
(u,p)
and let
R\to\infty
.
It follows that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

u=\mu^{-1}W(x)E(k)\text{ in }Q_{k}\cap\omega

for some
E(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since

\mu\int_{Y_{f}}u\,dx=\int_{Y_{f}}W(x)E(k)\,dx=KE(k),

and
K=(K_{j}^{i})
is invertible, we see that
E(k)=E(k+1)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
This implies that
u=\mu^{-1}W(x)E
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
E\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It follows that

\nabla\{p-(\pi(x)-x)\cdot E-x\cdot f(0)\}=0\quad\text{ in }\omega.

Since
\omega
is connected, we conclude that

p=(\pi(x)-x)\cdot E+x\cdot f(0)+\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

7

In this section we give the proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.
By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Step 1.

The case
q=2
.

Le
V
denote the closure of
\mathcal{V}
in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

\mathcal{V}=\big\{\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \text{\rm div}(\psi)=0\text{ in }\omega\big\}.

(7.1)

Using the inequality

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

for any
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\omega)
, and the Lax-Milgram Theorem, one may show that for each
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, there
exists a unique
u\in V
such that

\mu\int_{\omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\int_{\omega}F\cdot\psi\,dx-\int_{\omega}f\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

(7.2)

for any
\psi\in V
. Moreover,
u
satisfies the estimate (
1.11
) with
q=2
and
\varepsilon=1
, and

-\mu\Delta u+\nabla p=F+\text{div}(f)
in
\omega
for some

p\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\omega)
.

Step 2.

Let
(u,p)
be the weak solution of (
1.10
) with
\varepsilon=1
, given by Step 1, where
F\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.
We prove the estimate (
1.11
) for
2<q<\infty
by a real variable method.

Consider the linear operator,

T(F,f)=u,

where
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, and
u
is the solution of (
1.10
) with
\varepsilon=1
, given by Step 1.
Clearly,
\|T(F,f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|(F,f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
We claim that if
supp
(F)
, supp
(f)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q(x_{0},4R)
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0
, then

\|T(F,f)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q(x_{0},R))}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},4R)}|T(F,f)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(7.3)

Indeed, since
F=0
and
f=0
in
Q(x_{0},4R)
, we have

-\mu\Delta u+\nabla p=0
and div
(u)=0
in
Q^{1}(x_{0},4R)
, and
u=0
on
\partial\omega
.
If
0<R<2
, by the classical
L^{\infty}
estimates for the Stokes equations
in
C^{1,\alpha}
domains, we obtain

\max_{Q(x_{0},R)}|u|\leq C\left(\fint_{Q(x_{0},4R)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

If
R>2
, in view of (
5.15
), the inequality above continues to hold.
As a result, by
[
20
, Theorem 4.2.5]
, we deduce that

\|T(F,f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{q}\|(F,f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for any
q>2
and
F\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
where
C_{q}
depends only on
d
,
\mu
,
q
, and
Y_{s}
.
This gives the desired estimate for
u
. To bound
\nabla u
, we use the local estimate
[
11
]
,

\int_{Y_{f}+z}|\nabla u|^{q}\,dx\leq C\left\{\int_{\widetilde{Y_{f}}+z}|u|^{q}\,dx+C\int_{\widetilde{Y_{f}}+z}|F|^{q}\,dx+\int_{\widetilde{Y_{f}}+z}|f|^{q}\,dx\right\}

(7.4)

for
1<q<\infty
,
where
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\widetilde{Y_{f}}=(1+\delta)Y\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}
.
It follows from (
7.4
) by summing over
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|u\|_{L^{q}(\omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\}

(7.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\}.

Step 3.
Let
(u,p)
be the weak solution of (
1.10
), given by Step 1, where
F\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

1.11
) for
1<q<2

Let
(v,\tau)
be the weak solution of (
1.10
), given by Step 1, with
G\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the place of
F
and

in the place of

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\omega}G\cdot u\,dx-\int_{\omega}g\cdot\nabla u\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

By duality we obtain
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\}

Step 4.

The existence of solutions
u

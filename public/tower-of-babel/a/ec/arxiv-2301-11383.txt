Tensor products and intertwining operators between two uniserial representations of the Galilean Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n} Thanks: This research was partially supported by a CONICET grant PIP 11220210100597CO, SeCyT-UNC grant 33620180100983CB
Leandro Cagliero, Iván Gómez Rivera

Abstract
. Let 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , be the Galilean Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero, here 𝔥 n \mathfrak{h}_{n} is the Heisenberg Lie algebra of dimension 2 ​ n + 1 2n+1 , and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) acts on 𝔥 n \mathfrak{h}_{n} so that 𝔥 n ≃ V ⁡ ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) ⊕ V ⁡ ( 0 ) \mathfrak{h}_{n}\simeq V(2n-1)\oplus V(0) as 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -modules (here V ⁡ ( k ) V(k) denotes the irreducible 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -module of highest weight k k ). In this paper, we study the tensor product of two uniserial representations of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n} . We obtain the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -module structure of the socle of V ⊗ W V\otimes W and we describe the space of intertwining operators Hom 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n ​ ( V , W ) \text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(V,W) , where V V and W W are uniserial representations of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n} . The structure of the radical of V ⊗ W V\otimes W follows from that of the socle of V ∗ ⊗ W ∗ V^{*}\otimes W^{*} . The result is subtle and shows how difficult is to obtain the whole socle series of arbitrary tensor products of uniserials. In contrast to the serial associative case, our results for 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n} reveal that these tensor products are far from being a direct sum of uniserials; in particular, there are cases in which the tensor product of two uniserial ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔥 n ) \big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big) -modules is indecomposable but not uniserial. Recall that a foundational result of T. Nakayama states that every finitely generated module over a serial associative algebra is a direct sum of uniserial modules. This article extends a previous work in which we obtained the corresponding results for the Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ⋉ 𝔞 m \mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m} where 𝔞 m \mathfrak{a}_{m} is the abelian Lie algebra of dimension m + 1 m+1 and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) acts so that 𝔞 m ≃ V ⁡ ( m ) \mathfrak{a}_{m}\simeq V(m) as 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -modules.

1.
Introduction

This article is part of a project whose general goal
is to understand to what extent there is a
category consisting of certain finite-dimensional representations of
(non-semisimple) Lie groups such that, on the one hand,
it includes many (most) representations that are relevant to problems of interest and, on the other hand,
is small enough
so that its members can be described and handled in a reasonable simple way.
Clearly, for semisimple Lie groups, the whole category
of finite-dimensional representations works thanks to
the highest weight theorem and Weyl’s theorem on complete reducibility.
In the non-semisimple case, the whole category
of finite-dimensional representations is wild even for abelian Lie algebras of dimension greater than or equal to
2
(see
[
22
]
).
This is also discussed in
[
34
]

for the 3-dimensional euclidean Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{e}(2)
, and in
[
29
]
for virtually any complex Lie algebra other than semisimple
or 1-dimensional.

Instead of considering the whole category
of finite-dimensional representations, we and other authors have been working on the idea of describing or classifying
a special class of indecomposable representations of (non-semisimple) Lie algebras whose members might be used as building blocks for describing more general representations.
For instance, A. Piard
[
32
]
analyzed thoroughly the indecomposable modules
U
,
of the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
such that
U/\text{rad}(U)
is irreducible.
More recently, various families of indecomposable modules over various types of non-semisimple Lie algebras have been constructed and/or
classified, see for instance
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
20
,
18
,
19
,
24
]
.
Among these attempts, the class of uniserial representations stands out.
In the articles
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
6
,
5
,
7
,
16
]

all finite-dimensional
uniserial representations have been classified
for some different families of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
.
These classifications show that the class of uniserial representations of
\mathfrak{g}

is rather small and treatable in the universe of all
indecomposable modules.
Also, in
[
21
]
, it is shown how the infinite dimensional uniserial representations of
certain special linear groups obtained in

[
35
]

appear naturally in cohomology spaces.
We think that it is natural to consider
the class of uniserial representations as building blocks
for an ‘interesting’ category of representations, as described
at the beginning of this paper.
We recall that, for associative algebras, the class of uniserial modules
is very relevant, a foundational result here
is due to T. Nakayama
[
30
]
(see also
[
2
]
or
[
3
]
) and it states that every finitely generated module over a serial ring is a direct sum of uniserial modules. For more information
in the associative case we refer the reader mainly to
[
2
,
3
,
33
]
, and also
[
4
,
23
,
31
]
.
On the other hand, in the Lie algebra case, very little is known about uniserial representations.

The idea of considering
uniserial modules as building blocks of an interesting monoidal category naturally leads to the study of tensor products of uniserial representations and intertwining operators.
Additionally, another motivation for this project is to find a better way to thoroughly describe certain cohomology spaces associated to
an algebra (associative or Lie)
viewed as a module over its entire Lie algebra of derivations, which is, in general, non-semisimple. Therefore, tensor products of
uniserial modules are of particular relevance to us, especially when the cohomology space possesses a Gerstenhaber or a Poisson structure.

In this article and
[
8
]
we address the problem of describing
the tensor product of two uniserial
\mathfrak{g}
-modules in terms of uniserials for certain families of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
.
In contrast to the Nakayama case, these tensor products are not at all a direct sum of uniserials, in particular
there are many cases where the tensor product of
two uniserial
\mathfrak{g}
-modules is an indecomposable
\mathfrak{g}
-module but not uniserial.

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, all Lie algebras and representations considered
are assumed to be finite dimensional over a field
\mathbb{F}

of characteristic zero.
For
n\geq 0
, we denote
by
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
the abelian Lie algebra of dimension
n+1
,
by
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
the Heisenberg Lie algebra of dimension
2n+1
,
and
by
V(n)
the irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module with highest weight
n
(
\dim V(n)=n+1
).
In addition, for
n\geq 1
,

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{n}
denotes
the Lie algebra obtained by letting
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
act on
\mathfrak{a}_{n}

so that

\mathfrak{a}_{n}\simeq V(n)
; and, similarly,

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

denotes
the Lie algebra where

\mathfrak{h}_{n}\simeq V(2n-1)\oplus V(0)
as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules.
We notice that
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{2n-1}
is isomorphic to
the quotient

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
mod its 1-dimensional center

\mathfrak{z}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)\simeq V(0)
.
The Lie algebras

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{2n-1}
(note that
\dim\mathfrak{a}_{2n-1}
is even) are known as
conformal Galilei Lie algebras, and the
1-dimensional central extensions
of them,
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, are the extended conformal Galilei Lie algebras.
For
n=1
,
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is also known as
Schrödinger algebra.
Galilei algebras and their representations
attract considerable attention, see for instance

[
1
,
26
,
27
,
25
]
and
references therein.
It is worth mentioning that

\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
is contained in
\text{Der}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})

and thus,
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
may act on

\mathfrak{h}_{n}
in many different ways. In our case,
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
acts on
\mathfrak{h}_{n}

as a principal
\mathfrak{s}
-triple in
\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
.

In this work, we obtain
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module structure of
the socle
of the tensor product
V\otimes W
and, as an application, we compute
the space of intertwining
operators
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(V,W)

where
V
and
W
are
(almost arbitrary) uniserial representations of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
.

To describe more precisely our results, we need to recall
the classification of all the isomorphism classes of
uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules.
This was obtained in

[
5
]
.
This classification is reviewed with details in
§
3
and a
rough description of it is given below.
In what follows, and for the rest of the paper,

m=2n-1
.

\bullet

Non-faithful uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules.

Since

\mathfrak{z}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
acts trivially on them,
they are in correspondence with the
uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}\big)
-modules.
In turn, these were classified in
[
9
]
:

–

A general family
E(a,b)
, where
a,b
are non-negative integers with certain restrictions (depending on
n
).
The composition length
of
E(a,b)
is
2
.

–

A general family
Z(a,\ell)
and its duals. Here

a
and
\ell
are a non-negative integers. The composition length
of
Z(a,\ell)
is
\ell+1
.

–

Some exceptional modules
with composition lengths
3
and
4
.

The modules
Z(a,\ell)
and their duals are referred to as
modules of type
Z
.

\bullet

Faithful uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules
. All of them have composition length
3
.

–

For
n=1
: Two families denoted by
FU_{a}^{+}
and
FU_{a}^{-}
, with
a
an integer that satisfies
a\geq 0
and
a\geq 1
, respectively.

–

For
n=2
: Only four equivalence classes, they are denoted by
FU_{(0,3,0)}
,

FU_{(1,4,1)}
,

FU_{(1,2,1)}
and

FU_{(4,3,4)}
.

–

For any
n\geq 3
: Only three equivalence classes, they are denoted by

FU_{(0,m,0)}
,

FU_{(1,m+1,1)}
and

FU_{(1,m-1,1)}
(recall that
m=2n-1
).

All faithful uniserial modules (except
FU_{(4,3,4)}
,
n=2
)
are, in some sense, of a similar type and
are referred to as
standard faithful modules.

The
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{2}\big)
-module
FU_{(4,3,4)}
is quite exceptional.

The modules
E(a,b)
(which have composition length equal to 2) constitute the building blocks of all the other uniserial
modules: all uniserials can be obtained by
combining the modules
E(a,b)
in a subtle way governed by the zeros of the 6
j
-symbols (see
[
6
,
9
]
).
As a consequence, the
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-module structure of the tensor product
of two uniserial representations of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
depends strongly on the

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-module structure of
E(a,b)\otimes E(c,d)
which is already quite involved.

In
[
8
]
we stated a conjecture that provides the description of the socle of
E(a,b)\otimes E(c,d)
for any
a,b,c,d
(see Conjecture
4.2
below) and we proved the part of it
that was necessary to obtain the socle of

V\otimes W

and the intertwining
operators
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(V,W)

where
V
and
W
are
uniserial representations of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

of type
Z

(in fact, we dealt in
[
8
]

with
uniserial representations of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}
, instead of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, recall that,
for modules of type
Z
,
the action of
\mathfrak{z}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
is trivial).
In particular, we proved that the socle of

V\otimes W
is multiplicity free as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules.
As an application of these results, we proved in
[
8
]
that if
V
and
W
are

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules
of type
Z
, then
V
and
W
are determined from
V\otimes W
.
Moreover, we
provided a procedure to identify the corresponding
parameters
a
and
\ell
of
V
and
W
from
V\otimes W
.
This is a rare property, even if the factors are irreducible, it is not frequent that the factors
V
and
W
are determined
from
V\otimes W
(see
[
28
]
and references within).

In this paper, we extend the results of

[
8
]
obtaining
the socle of

V\otimes W

and the intertwining
operators
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(V,W)

when both
V
and
W
are
standard faithful uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules,
or when one of them is standard faithful and the other one is uniserial of type
Z
.
In contrast to the non-faithful case, in the standard faithful case it may happen that
the socle of

V\otimes W
is not multiplicity free as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module
(this occurs when
V\simeq W
).
As a consequence, if

V
and
W
are isomorphic standard faithful uniserials,
then the space of

\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}}(V,W)

The main step toward these results
requires making a considerable advance
in the proof of Conjecture
4.2
.
See the comments after it to know what is
still open about this conjecture.

The paper is organized as follows.
In §
2
we review some basic facts about
uniserial representations of Lie algebras and recall all the

In §

we review the classification of all

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}

]
)

(obtained in

]

The main section of the paper is §

-module structure of the socle of the tensor product of

-modules

and
W
:
Theorem

recalls the case when

and
W
are of type

(obtained in
[

]
),
Conjecture

deals with all the possible cases of

Theorem

confirms the part of Conjecture

needed to

4.6
and
4.7
,

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Clebsch-Gordan coefficients

Recall that
\mathbb{F}
is a field of characteristic zero and that all Lie algebras and representations are assumed to be finite dimensional
over
\mathbb{F}
.
Let

(2.1)

e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
.
Let
V(a)
be the irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module with highest weight
a\geq 0
.
We fix a basis
\{v_{0}^{a},\dots,v_{a}^{a}\}
of
V(a)
relative to which the basis
\{e,h,f\}
acts as follows:

\displaystyle e\,v_{k}^{a}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{a}{2}+1\right)-\left(\frac{a}{2}-k+1\right)\left(\frac{a}{2}-k\right)}v_{k-1}^{a},

(2.2)

\displaystyle h\,v_{k}^{a}=

\displaystyle(a-2k)v_{k}^{a},

\displaystyle f\,v_{k}^{a}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{a}{2}+1\right)-\left(\frac{a}{2}-k-1\right)\left(\frac{a}{2}-k\right)}v_{k+1}^{a},

where
0\leq k\leq a
and
v_{-1}^{a}=v_{a+1}^{a}=0
.
The basis
\{v_{0}^{a},\dots,v_{a}^{a}\}
has been chosen in a convenient way to
introduce below the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.
Note that if we denote by
(x)_{a}
the matrix of

x\in\mathfrak{sl}(2)
relative to the basis
\{v_{0}^{a},\dots,v_{a}^{a}\}
, then

\{(e)_{1},(h)_{1},(f)_{1}\}

are as in (
2.1
),
and

(e)_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{2}&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{2}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad(h)_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad(f)_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\sqrt{2}&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

This means
that we may assume that

\{v_{0}^{2},v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2}\}=\{-e,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}h,f\}
.

We know that
V(a)\simeq V(a)^{*}
as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules.
More precisely, if
\{(v_{0}^{a})^{*},\dots,(v_{a}^{a})^{*}\}

is the dual basis of
\{v_{0}^{a},\dots,v_{a}^{a}\}
then
the map

(2.3)

\begin{split}V(a)&\rightarrow V(a)^{*}\\
v_{k}^{a}&\mapsto(-1)^{a-k}(v_{a-k}^{a})^{*}\end{split}

gives an explicit
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-isomorphism.

It is well known that the decomposition of the tensor product
of two irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules
V(a)
and
V(b)

is

(2.4)

V(a)\otimes V(b)\simeq V(a+b)\oplus V(a+b-2)\oplus\cdots\oplus V(|a-b|).

This is the well known Clebsch-Gordan formula.

The

Clebsch-Gordan coefficients

CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}\mid j_{3},m_{3})

are defined below and they
provide an explicit
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-embedding

V(c)\rightarrow V(a)\otimes V(b)

which is the following

\displaystyle V(c)

\displaystyle\rightarrow V(a)\otimes V(b)

\displaystyle v_{k}^{c}

\displaystyle\mapsto v_{k}^{a,b,c}

where, by definition,

(2.5)

v_{k}^{a,b,c}=\sum_{i,j}CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{c}{2},\tfrac{c}{2}-k)\,v_{i}^{a}\otimes v_{j}^{b},

where the sum runs over all
i,j
such that

\tfrac{a}{2}-i+\tfrac{b}{2}-j=\tfrac{c}{2}-k

(in fact, we could let
i,j
run freely since the Clebsch-Gordan
coefficient involved is zero if
\tfrac{a}{2}-i+\tfrac{b}{2}-j\neq\tfrac{c}{2}-k
).
Since

(2.6)

\text{Hom}(V(b),V(a))\simeq V(b)^{*}\otimes V(a)\simeq V(a)\otimes V(b)

it follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) that
the map
V(c)\rightarrow\text{Hom}(V(b),V(a))

given by

\displaystyle v_{k}^{c}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i,j}CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{c}{2},\tfrac{c}{2}-k)\,v_{i}^{a}\otimes v_{j}^{b},

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{b-j}CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{c}{2},\tfrac{c}{2}-k)\,v_{i}^{a}\otimes(v_{b-j}^{b})^{*},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{j}CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},-\tfrac{b}{2}+j\,|\,\tfrac{c}{2},\tfrac{c}{2}-k)\,\,(v_{j}^{b})^{*}\otimes v_{i}^{a}

is an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module homomorphism.

We now recall briefly the basic definitions and
facts about the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.
We will mainly follow
[
36
]
.

Given three non-negative integers or half-integers
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
, we say that they
satisfy
the triangle condition
if

j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}
is an integer and
they can be the side lengths of a (possibly degenerate)
triangle (that is

|j_{1}-j_{2}|\leq j_{3}\leq j_{1}+j_{2}
).
We now define (see
[
36
, §8.2, eq.(1)]
)

\Delta(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})=\sqrt{\frac{(j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3})!(j_{1}-j_{2}+j_{3})!(-j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3})!}{(j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+1)!}}

if
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
satisfies the triangle condition;
otherwise, we set
\Delta(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})=0
.

If, in addition,
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
are three integers or half-integers, then
the corresponding
Clebsch-Gordan coefficient

CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}|j_{3},m_{3})

is zero unless
m_{1}+m_{2}=m_{3}
and
|m_{i}|\leq j_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. In this case, the following formula is valid for
m_{3}\geq 0
and
j_{1}\geq j_{2}
(see
[
36
, §8.2, eq.(3)]
)

CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}\mid j_{3},m_{3})=\Delta(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})\,\sqrt{(2j_{3}+1)}\\[2.84526pt]
\times\sqrt{(j_{1}+m_{1})!(j_{1}-m_{1})!(j_{2}+m_{2})!(j_{2}-m_{2})!(j_{3}+m_{3})!(j_{3}-m_{3})!}\\[2.84526pt]
\times\sum_{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}}{r!(j_{1}\!+\!j_{2}\!-\!j_{3}\!-\!r)!(j_{1}\!-\!m_{1}\!-\!r)!(j_{2}\!+\!m_{2}\!-\!r)!(j_{3}\!-\!j_{2}\!+\!m_{1}\!+\!r)!(j_{3}\!-\!j_{1}\!-\!m_{2}\!+\!r)!},

where the sum runs through all integers

r
for which the argument of every factorial is non-negative.
If either
m_{3}<0
or
j_{1}<j_{2}

we have

\displaystyle CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}\mid j_{3},m_{3})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}}\;CG(j_{1},-m_{1};j_{2},-m_{2}\mid j_{3},-m_{3})

(2.8)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}}\;CG(j_{2},m_{2};j_{1},m_{1}\mid j_{3},m_{3}).

In addition, it also holds

(2.9)

CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}\mid j_{3},m_{3})=(-1)^{j_{1}-m_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{2j_{3}+1}{2j_{2}+1}}\;CG(j_{1},m_{1};j_{3},-m_{3}\mid j_{2},-m_{2}).

In the following sections, we will need the following particular values of the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.
Here,
a,b
are integers and
i=0,\dots,a
,

j=0,\dots,b
.

(2.10)

CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{a+b}{2},\tfrac{a+b}{2}-i-j)=\sqrt{\frac{a!b!(a+b-i-j)!(i+j)!}{i!j!(a+b)!(a-i)!(b-j)!}},

(2.11)

CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},j-\tfrac{b}{2}\,|\,\tfrac{a-b}{2},\tfrac{a-b}{2}-i+j)\\
=(-1)^{j}\sqrt{\frac{(a-i)!\;i!\;b!\;(a-b+1)!}{(a+1)!\;j!\;(b-j)!\;(a-b-i+j)!\;(i-j)!}},

(2.12)

CG(\tfrac{a}{2},i-\tfrac{a}{2};\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{b-a}{2},\tfrac{b-a}{2}+i-j)\\
=(-1)^{a}CG(\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j;\,\tfrac{a}{2},i-\tfrac{a}{2}\,|\,\tfrac{b-a}{2},\tfrac{b-a}{2}+i-j)\\
=(-1)^{j}\sqrt{\frac{(b-j)!\;j!\;a!\;(b-a+1)!}{(b+1)!\;i!\;(a-i)!\;(b-a-j+i)!\;(j-i)!}},

(2.13)

CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},\tfrac{b}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{a+b}{2}-i-j,\tfrac{a+b}{2}-i-j)\\
=(-1)^{i}\sqrt{\frac{(a+b-2i-2j+1)!\;(i+j)!\;(a-i)!\;(b-j)!}{(a+b-i-j+1)!\;(a-i-j)!\;(b-i-j)!\;i!\;j!}}.

2.2.
Uniserial representations

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is

uniserial
if it admits a unique composition series.
In other words,
V
is uniserial if the socle series

0=\text{soc}^{0}(V)\subset\text{soc}^{1}(V)\subset\cdots\subset\text{soc}^{n}(V)=V

is a composition series of
V
, that is, the socle factors
\text{soc}^{i}(V)/\text{soc}^{i-1}(V)
are irreducible for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Recall that
\text{soc}^{1}(V)=\text{soc}(V)

is the sum of all irreducible

\mathfrak{g}
-submodules of
V
and

\text{soc}^{i}(V)/\text{soc}^{i-1}(V)=\text{soc}(V/\text{soc}^{i-1}(V))
.
Note that for uniserial modules,
the
composition length
of
V
coincides with
its
socle length
.

If the
Levi decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{r}
, (with
\mathfrak{r}
the solvable radical and
\mathfrak{s}
semisimple) we may choose irreducible

\mathfrak{s}
-submodules
V_{i}\subset V
,
1\leq i\leq n
, such that

(2.14)

V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{n}

with

V_{i}\simeq\text{soc}^{i}(V)/\text{soc}^{i-1}(V)

as
\mathfrak{s}
-modules and

\mathfrak{r}V_{i}\subset V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i}.

In fact, if

[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{r}]=\mathfrak{r}
, then
\mathfrak{r}V_{i}\subset V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i-1}
,
see Lemma
2.2
below.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that (
2.14
) is the

socle decomposition
of
V
.
We point out that, in the socle decomposition of a

\mathfrak{g}
-module, the order of the summands is relevant.

The proof of the following lemma can be found in

[
8
, Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{r}=[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{r}]
and
let
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Then

(1)

\text{soc}(V)=V^{\mathfrak{r}}
.

(2)

If
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{n}
is a vector space
decomposition such that
\text{soc}(V)=V_{1}
and

\mathfrak{r}V_{k}\subset V_{k-1}
for all
k=2,\dots,n
, then

\text{soc}^{k}(V)=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}

for all
k=1,\dots,n
.

3.
Uniserial representations of

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

3.1.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

Let us introduce some notation, recall some basic facts about
the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and define
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
.
Let us fix
n\geq 1
and, for the rest of the paper, set
m=2n-1
.
We know that
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
acts by derivations on
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
in such a way that

\mathfrak{h}_{n}\simeq V(m)\oplus V(0)

as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules.
In order to have this action described in harmony with §
2.1
,
we:

\bullet

assume, by definition, that
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=V(m)\oplus V(0)
, (
m=2n-1
),

\bullet

denote by
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(m)
the subspace
V(m)
of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
,

\bullet

denote by

\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{m}\}

the basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(m)
corresponding to the basis
\{v_{0}^{m},\ldots,v_{m}^{m}\}
of
V(m)
chosen in (
2.2
)
(that is
e_{k}=v_{k}^{m}
when
V(m)
is viewed inside
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
),

\bullet

denote by
z\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}
the vector
v_{0}^{0}\in V(0)
chosen in (
2.2
).

In this context, the non-zero brackets in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
are

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{m-i}]

\displaystyle=CG(\tfrac{m}{2},\,\tfrac{m}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{m}{2},-\tfrac{m}{2}+i\,|\,0,\,0)\,z

(3.1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\,\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{m+1}}\,z.

The center of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}

is

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})=\mathbb{F}z
and

\mathfrak{h}_{n}/\mathbb{F}z\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{m}
as Lie algebras.
It is straightforward to see that (
2.2
)
provides
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
with an action of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
by derivations.
This action allow us to define the Lie algebras

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}

(
\mathfrak{a}_{m}
is abelian of dimension
m+1=2n
).
It is clear that
\mathbb{F}z
is also the center of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)/\mathbb{F}z\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}

as Lie algebras.

In
[
5
]
, it is obtained the classification, up to isomorphism, of all uniserial representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
.
It is straightforward to see that a uniserial
representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is faithful
if and only if
z
acts non-trivially.
Therefore, the classification was given in two stages: the non-faithful and the faithful ones.
The non-faithful ones are the same as those of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes V(m)
which were classified earlier in

[
9
, Theorem 10.1]
.
We now recall this classification.

3.2.
The non-faithful

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules
E(a,b)

If

it follows from (

) and (

that, up to scalar, there is a unique

\mathfrak{sl}(2)

Recall that the radical
\mathfrak{r}

\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}
is
\mathfrak{a}_{m}\simeq V(m)

as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)

Thus, the above

(3.2)

e_{s}\,v_{j}^{b}=\sum_{i=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\,CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},-\tfrac{b}{2}+j\,|\,\tfrac{m}{2},\tfrac{m}{2}-s)\,v_{i}^{a}.

Note that (
3.2

2.7

Note also that the above sum has, in fact, at most one summand, that is

(3.3)

e_{s}\,v_{j}^{b}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $i\neq j+s+\frac{a-b-m}{2}$;}\\[5.69054pt]
(-1)^{j}CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},-\tfrac{b}{2}+j\,|\,\tfrac{m}{2},\tfrac{m}{2}-s)\,\,v_{i}^{a},&\text{if $i=j+s+\frac{a-b-m}{2}$.}\end{cases}

Consequently, this also defines a uniserial

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-module structure
(of composition length 2) on

on which the center
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
acts trivially.

In the following example,

and thus

.

on the module

3.3.
Non-faithful

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules of type

The above construction can be extended to arbitrary composition length

\{a_{i}\}

|a_{i}-a_{i-1}|=m

More precisely, for the “increasing case”

\alpha

\ell

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}\big)

that is, the

-arrow

e_{s}\,v_{j}^{b}=\sum_{i=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\,CG(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b}{2},-\tfrac{b}{2}+j\,|\,\tfrac{m}{2},\tfrac{m}{2}-s)\,v_{i}^{a},

of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

(We point out that the above sequence serves as an indication
of the action of

We notice that

(

acts trivially) and

See Theorem

]

3.6.

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)

The faithful uniserial

It turns out that there are no faithful uniserial

and

Let us start with the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module

We now indicate how to obtain an action of

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)

Although we know that

(i)

4.1.
The non-faithful case and the crucial conjecture

Let us recall some of the results obtained in
[
8
]
, mainly

the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module structure of the socle of the tensor product of two uniserial
\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{a}_{m}\big)
-modules of type
Z
.

If
U

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-module,

(4.1)

Therefore, if

\displaystyle\text{soc}(V\otimes W)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}}\left(\text{soc}(V\otimes W)\cap\bigoplus_{i+j=t}V(a_{i})\otimes V(b_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}}\left(\bigoplus_{i+j=t}V(a_{i})\otimes V(b_{j})\right)^{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}.

For

Hence, as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules,

\text{soc}(V\otimes W)=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}}S_{t}

and

S_{0}=\text{soc}(V)\otimes\text{soc}(W)=V(a_{0})\otimes V(b_{0}).

The following theorem is the same as
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
but stated in terms of non-faithful uniserial

\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\big)
-modules.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

(ii)

Lemma 4.4

\displaystyle(zv_{0})_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=\tfrac{a_{i_{0}}+b_{j_{0}}-c}{2}}CG(\tfrac{a_{i_{0}}}{2},\,\tfrac{a_{i_{0}}}{2}-i;\,\tfrac{b_{j_{0}}}{2},\;\tfrac{b_{j_{0}}}{2}-j\,|\,\tfrac{c}{2},\tfrac{c}{2})\,zv_{i}^{a_{i_{0}}}\otimes v_{j}^{b_{j_{0}}}.

Rainbow powers of a Hamilton cycle in G n , p G_{n,p}
Tolson Bell and Alan Frieze

Abstract
We show that the threshold for having a rainbow copy of a power of a Hamilton cycle in a randomly edge colored copy of G n , p G_{n,p} is within a constant factor of the uncolored threshold. Our proof requires ( 1 + ε ) (1+\varepsilon) times the minimum number of colors.



1
Introduction

There has recently been great progress in our understanding of thresholds for monotone properties in the random graph
G_{n,p}
. Inspired by the work of Alweiss, Lovett, Wu and Zhang
[
1
]
on the Sunflower Conjecture, Frankston, Kahn, Narayanan and Park
[
4
]
showed that under fairly general conditions, the threshold for the existence of combinatorial objects is within a factor
O(\log n)
of the point where the expected number of such objects begins to take off. Great though these results are, this is not the end of the story. In a paper remarkable for the strength of its result and for the simplicity of its proof, Park and Pham
[
10
]
proved the so-called Kahn-Kalai conjecture
[
8
]
which implies the result of
[
4
]
.

Kahn, Narayanan and Park
[
7
]
tightened their analysis for the case of the square of a Hamilton cycle, removing the
O(\log n)
factor and solving the existence problem up to a constant factor; a remarkable achievement, given the complexity of the proofs of earlier weaker results. Their result was generalized by Espuny Díaz and Person
[
3
]
and Spiro
[
12
]
, both of whom defined more generalized conditions under which the
O(\log n)
factor can be removed. Espuny Díaz and Person asked whether a rainbow generalization of their result could be proven
[
3
]
. Our main theorem here proves a rainbow version in a setting that is more general than the Kahn–Narayanan–Park result but less general than Espuny Díaz–Person and Spiro results. It is likely that our result could be extended to the full generality of the Espuny Díaz–Person and Spiro results with some additional effort.

Some notation

Given a set

|{\mathcal{H}}\cap\langle S\rangle|\leq\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}|}{\kappa^{|S|}},\quad\forall S\subseteq X.

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}
is called

-bounded if

for all

and

. The following theorem was proved in

Theorem 1
.

Let

be an

\kappa
-spread hypergraph and let

. There is an absolute constant

then w.h.p.

or

respectively contains an edge of

. More precisely,
\mathbb{P}(X_{p}\text{ contains an edge of ${\mathcal{H}}$ })\geq 1-\varepsilon_{r}

.

To apply this theorem to, say, Hamilton cycles, we let

and we let

be the edge sets of the Hamilton cycles of

.

In the special case of

corresponding in this way to the squares of Hamilton cycles,
[

]
removed the

-factor from the bounds in (

We now turn to the main topic of this note. We suppose that each

is uniformly and independently given a random color from a set

. Given a set

we refer to

as the set after its elements have been colored. We say that

[

]
attempted to extend the results of
[

]
to rainbow colorings. They proved

.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be an

-bounded,
\kappa
-spread hypergraph and let

The constraint

. The aim of this note is to tackle this case while also removing the extra

-factor. Unfortunately, we have to increase the number of colors slightly, by a factor
(1+\varepsilon_{1})
for arbitrary positive

. Chapter 15 of
[

]
extracts a property used in

]
to make the following extra assumption about the hypergraph

. For

The assumption now is that there exist constants

independent of

such that

f_{t,A}\leq\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}|{\mathcal{H}}|\text{ for all $A\in{\mathcal{H}}$ and }1\leq t\leq\alpha r.

(4)

As
{\mathcal{H}}
remains
\kappa
-spread, it follows from (

f_{t,A}\leq\frac{2^{r}}{\kappa^{t}}|{\mathcal{H}}|\text{ for all $A\in{\mathcal{H}}$ and }t>\alpha r

(5)

Let a hypergraph

be

2
Proof of Theorem
3

The proof will proceed in three stages. First, we will color all elements of
X
independently and uniformly at random from
[q]
, and will remove all sets in
{\mathcal{H}}
that are not rainbow. We show that the number of remaining sets is with high probability close to its expectation.

Then, let
N=|X|
and
m=\frac{CN}{\kappa}
for sufficiently large
C=C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1})
. Let
W_{0}
be chosen randomly from
\binom{X}{m}
. Let
p_{1}=\frac{m}{N}
and let
W_{1}
be obtained from
X\setminus W_{0}
by including each element with probability
p_{1}
. Proving Theorem
3
on
W_{0}\cup W_{1}
suffices to prove it for
X_{O(m)}
by standard concentration bounds. The second stage (succeeding with high probability) will deal with
W_{0}
while the third stage (succeeding with probability
1-\varepsilon
) will deal with
W_{1}
.

We will use the notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate that
A\leq(1+o(1))B
as
r\to\infty
. We will also assume that
q=(1+\varepsilon_{1})r
. This assumption comes without loss of generality because
C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1})
will be strictly decreasing in
\varepsilon_{1}
, so if
q>(1+\varepsilon_{1})r
, we could set
\varepsilon_{2}
such that
q=(1+\varepsilon_{2})r
and use
\varepsilon_{2}
in the proof instead.

2.1
The size of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}

Let
{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{M}\right\}
and let
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
denote the rainbow edges of
{\mathcal{H}}
after a uniform and independent random coloring. Similarly, let
X^{*}
denote
X
after it has been randomly colored. Let
(a)_{b}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)
for positive integers
a,b
.

We use the Chebyshev inequality to prove concentration of
Z=|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|
around its mean. We have

\mathbb{E}(Z)=\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}|(q)_{r}}{q^{r}}\to\infty.

as
r\to\infty
, because spread (with
S\in{\mathcal{H}}
in (
1
)) implies that
|{\mathcal{H}}|\geq\kappa^{r}
and we have assumed that
\kappa
is sufficiently large.

Using the edge transitivity of
{\mathcal{H}}
to obtain (
7

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{r}\sum_{A_{i},A_{j}:|A_{i}\cap A_{j}|=t}\frac{(q)_{t}((q-t)_{r-t})^{2}}{q^{2r-t}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(Z)\left(1+\mathbb{E}(Z)+\sum_{t=1}^{r-1}\sum_{A_{i}:|A_{i}\cap A_{1}|=t}\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(Z)\left(1+\mathbb{E}(Z)+|{\mathcal{H}}|\left(\sum_{t=1}^{\alpha r}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}+\sum_{t=\alpha r+1}^{r-1}\frac{\binom{r}{t}}{\kappa^{t}}\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}\right)\right).

(8)

Explanation for (
8
):
For the first sum we use (
4
) on

and for the second sum we use spread by summing over all
\binom{r}{t}

t
-subsets of

So,

\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(Z)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(Z)}+1+\sum_{t=1}^{\alpha r}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}q^{r}}{q^{r-t}(q)_{r}}+\sum_{t=\alpha r+1}^{r-1}\frac{2^{r}}{\kappa^{t}}\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}q^{r}}{q^{r-t}(q)_{r}}=1+o(1)

as long as

. It follows that w.h.p.

|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\sim\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}|(q)_{r}}{q^{r}}.

(9)

Thus, for the rest of the proof we will assume
|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\geq(1-o(1))\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}|(q)_{r}}{q^{r}}
.

2.2
Random sample from
X

Given a set
A^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, we define

f^{*}_{t,A^{*}}=|\left\{B^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}:|B^{*}\cap A^{*}|=t\right\}|

so that for

, we have by (

\mathbb{E}(f^{*}_{t,A^{*}})=\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}f_{t,A}\leq\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}|{\mathcal{H}}|.

(10)

and for

, we have by (
5

\mathbb{E}(f_{t,A^{*}}^{*})=\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}f_{t,A}\leq\frac{(q-t)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}\frac{2^{r}}{\kappa^{t}}|{\mathcal{H}}|

(11)

For
A^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
and
W_{0}^{*}\subseteq X^{*}
with

, let

be

for some

Let

. For

is

. Otherwise

. Let

have a relatively small

Lemma 4

\mathbb{P}(\text{success})\geq 1-c_{0}^{\omega}

Proof.

Let
\nu_{bad}
denote the number of bad pairs

. Fix a function
\phi:2^{X^{*}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, where
\phi(S^{*})\subseteq S^{*}

contains a set in
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
. We claim that

\displaystyle\nu_{bad}\leq\sum_{t\geq\omega}\sum_{|Z^{*}|=m+t}\sum_{t^{\prime}\geq t}2^{t^{\prime}}f^{*}_{t^{\prime},\phi(Z^{*})}.

(12)

Explanation for (
12
):
This equation follows from the key observation of recent threshold papers
[
7
,
10
]
. We count the number of

with
|T^{*}(A^{*},W_{0}^{*})|=t
for a given
t\geq\omega
. We first fix
Z^{*}=T^{*}\cup W_{0}^{*}
, which as these are disjoint has size

. Then, we let

, noting that
\phi(Z^{*})\subseteq Z^{*}
as

does contain a set in

. Since

is chosen to minimize
|B^{*}\setminus W_{0}^{*}|,B^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*},B^{*}\subseteq A^{*}\cup W_{0}^{*}
, and

is a valid choice of

, we must have

, and so

. Given

, we can specify one of the at most

possibilities for

as a superset of
\phi(Z^{*})\cap A^{*}
. We then specify
T^{*}\subseteq\phi(Z^{*})\cap A^{*}
in at most

ways, which uniquely gives
W_{0}^{*}=Z^{*}\setminus T^{*}
.

By linearity of expectation and Equations (
10
), (
11
), and (
12
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\nu_{bad})

\displaystyle\leq{\sum_{t\geq\omega}}\sum_{|Z^{*}|=m+t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^{\alpha r}\frac{(q-t^{\prime})_{r-t^{\prime}}}{q^{r-t^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{2K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{H}}|+\sum_{t^{\prime}>\alpha r}\frac{(q-t^{\prime})_{r-t^{\prime}}}{q^{r-t^{\prime}}}\frac{2^{r+t^{\prime}}}{\kappa^{t^{\prime}}}|{\mathcal{H}}|\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\sum_{t\geq\omega}\sum_{|Z^{*}|=m+t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^{\alpha r}\frac{(q-t^{\prime})_{r-t^{\prime}}}{q^{r-t^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{2K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t^{\prime}}\frac{q^{r}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|}{(q)_{r}}+\sum_{t^{\prime}>\alpha r}\frac{(q-t^{\prime})_{r-t^{\prime}}}{q^{r-t^{\prime}}}\frac{2^{r+t^{\prime}}}{\kappa^{t^{\prime}}}\frac{q^{r}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|}{(q)_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\sum_{t\geq\omega}\sum_{|Z^{*}|=m+t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^{\alpha r}\left(\frac{2eK_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|+\sum_{t^{\prime}>\alpha r}\frac{e^{t^{\prime}}2^{r+t^{\prime}}}{\kappa^{t^{\prime}}}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\right).

(14)

Continuing, and using (
14
),

\displaystyle(1-o(1))~\mathbb{E}(\nu_{bad})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t\geq\omega}\binom{N}{m+t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^{\alpha r}\left(\frac{2eK_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|+\sum_{t^{\prime}>\alpha r}\frac{2^{r+t^{\prime}}e^{t^{\prime}}}{\kappa^{t^{\prime}}}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{N}{m}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\sum_{t\geq\omega}\left(\frac{\kappa}{C}\right)^{t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}=t}^{\alpha r}\left(\frac{2eK_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t^{\prime}}+\sum_{t^{\prime}>\alpha r}\frac{2^{r+t^{\prime}}{e^{t^{\prime}}}}{\kappa^{t^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{N}{m}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|c^{\omega}\text{ for some }0<c<1.

Now, let
w_{bad}=|\left\{W_{0}^{*}:\text{there are at least $|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|/2$ bad $(A^{*},W_{0}^{*})$}\right\}|
. Then the above equation gives that

(1-o(1))~\mathbb{E}(w_{bad})\leq 2\binom{N}{m}c^{\omega}

and thus

\mathbb{P}(failure)=\frac{\mathbb{E}(w_{bad})}{\binom{N}{m}}\leq(1+o(1))~2c^{\omega}.

∎

By taking
\omega\to\infty
as

, this means that success will happen with high probability.

2.3
Finishing the proof

Suppose now that
W_{0}^{*}
is a success and then let
{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
denote the multi-hypergraph

\left\{{T^{*}(A^{*},W_{0}^{*})}:A^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*},(A^{*},W_{0}^{*})\text{ is good}\right\}

where each good

contributes one element. Then let

{\mathcal{R}}_{\ell}^{*}=\left\{R^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}:|R^{*}|=\ell\right\}\text{ for }0\leq\ell<\omega.

We can assume that
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{*}=\emptyset
, as otherwise

contains an edge of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
and we have already succeeded. Now, generate

where
W_{1}^{*}
is distributed as
(X^{*}\setminus W_{0}^{*})_{p_{1}}
. If
R^{*}\subseteq W_{1}^{*}
for some

, then the

for which

satisfies Theorem

. Thus, we just need to show that with probability at least

there exists such an

.

To aid in the calculations below, for each
R^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\ell}^{*}
with
R^{*}\subseteq W_{1}^{*}
, take

to be an independent random variable with distribution Bernoulli
((\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{\omega-\ell})
.
R^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
is

if
R^{*}\subseteq W_{1}^{*}
and

. Let
\nu_{R}
denote the number of accepted sets. It suffices to show
\mathbb{P}(\nu_{R}=0)\leq\varepsilon
, which we will do by Chebyshev’s inequality. Then

\mathbb{E}(\nu_{R})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\omega}|{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{\ell}|\frac{p_{1}^{\ell}(q-r+\ell)_{\ell}}{q^{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{\omega-\ell}\sim|{\mathcal{R}}^{*}|(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{\omega}\to\infty.

(15)

The claims in (
15
) follow from the fact that

\omega=o(r^{1/2})
and the fact that
|{\mathcal{R}}^{*}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\gtrsim\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-r}|{\mathcal{H}}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\kappa/e)^{r}
.

(16)

Now

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar(\nu_{R})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\omega}\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{\omega}\mathbb{E}\left(|\left\{(R^{*},S^{*}):R^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\ell_{1}}^{*},S^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell_{2}}^{*},|R^{*}\cap S^{*}|=t\right\}|\right)\times

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\frac{p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}(q-r+\ell_{1})_{\ell_{1}}}{q^{\ell_{1}}}\frac{p_{1}^{\ell_{2}-t}(q-r+\ell_{2}-t)_{\ell_{2}-t}}{q^{\ell_{2}-t}}\cdot(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{2\omega-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{t=1}^{\omega}\mathbb{E}\left(|\left\{R^{*},S^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}:|R^{*}\cap S^{*}|=t\right\}|\right)(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{2\omega-t}.

(17)

(The same assumptions (
16
) suffice to obtain (
17
).)

Fix
R^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
and then for
1\leq t\leq\omega
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|\left\{S^{*}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}:|R^{*}\cap S^{*}|=t\right\}|)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=t}^{r}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{s}\frac{(q)_{r-s}}{q^{r-s}}|{\mathcal{H}}|

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{(q)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}|{\mathcal{H}}|\sum_{s=t}^{r}\left(\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{1})K_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}\kappa}\right)^{s-t}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{(q)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}|{\mathcal{H}}|\lesssim 2\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{(q)_{r-t}}{q^{r-t}}\frac{q^{r}}{(q)_{r}}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|\leq 2\left(\frac{eK_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}|.

Explanation for (

):

R^{*}
appears several times in
{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
as
A^{*}\setminus W_{0}^{*}
for some
A^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
. For each such
A^{*}
we count the number of sets
B^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
for which

. This will bound the number of choices for

in the LHS of (

). For the sum we use (

) which is only valid for

. For larger

, we proceed as in (

) and

by

So

\mathbb{V}ar(\nu_{R})\lesssim 2|{\mathcal{H}}^{*}||{\mathcal{R}}^{*}|\sum_{t=1}^{\omega}\left(\frac{eK_{0}}{\kappa}\right)^{t}(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{2\omega-t}\\
\leq 4|{\mathcal{R}}^{*}|^{2}(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{2\omega}\sum_{t=1}^{\omega}\left(\frac{eK_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}\kappa p_{1}}\right)^{t}\leq 4|{\mathcal{R}}^{*}|^{2}(\varepsilon_{1}p_{1})^{2\omega}\sum_{t=1}^{\omega}\left(\frac{eK_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}C}\right)^{t}\leq\frac{12K_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}C}\mathbb{E}(\nu_{R})^{2}.

\mathbb{P}(\nu_{R}=0)\leq\frac{\mathbb{V}ar(\nu_{R})}{\mathbb{E}(\nu_{R})^{2}}\lesssim\frac{12K_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}C}.

3
Powers of Hamilton cycles

We verify (
4
) for the hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
whose edges correspond to the
k
th power of a Hamilton cycle. As in
[
7
]
we split this into two propositions and modify their proof for

.

Proposition 1
.

For
T\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}
, with
t\leq n/3k
edges, inducing
c
components,

|{\mathcal{H}}\cap\langle T\rangle|\leq(2k)^{2t}\left(n-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c-1\right)!.

Proof.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{c}
be the components of the subgraph induced by the edges
T
and let
v=|V(T)|
where (
V(A),E(A)
) is the set of (vertices, edges) used by a subgraph
A
. The upper bound on
t
implies that no
T_{j}
can “wrap around,” and so
|E(T_{j})|\leq k|V(T_{j})|-(2k-1)
for each
j
and so

t\leq kv-(2k-1)c.

(19)

We designate a root vertex
v_{j}
for each
T_{j}
and order
V(T_{j})
by some order
\prec_{j}
that begins with
v_{j}
and in which each
v\neq v_{j}
appears later than at least one of its neighbors. We may then bound
|{\mathcal{H}}\cap\langle T\rangle|
as follows. To specify an
S\in{\mathcal{H}}
containing
T
, we first specify a cyclic permutation of
\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{c}\right\}\bigcup([n]\setminus V(T))
. By (
19
), the number of ways to do this (namely,
(n-v+c-1)!)
is at most
\left(n-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c-1\right)!
. We then extend to a full cyclic ordering of
[n]
(thus determining
T
) by inserting, for
j=1,\ldots,c
, the vertices of
V(T_{j})\setminus\left\{v_{j}\right\}
in the order
\prec_{j}
. This allows at most
2k
places to insert each vertex (since one of its neighbours has been inserted before it and the edge joining them must belong to
T
), so the number of possibilities here is at most
(2k)^{v}\leq(2k)^{2t}
, and the proposition follows.
∎

Proposition 2
.

For
T\subseteq S\in{\mathcal{H}},|T|=t\leq n/3k
, the number of subgraphs of
T
with
c
components is at most
(4ke)^{t}\binom{2t}{c}
.

Proof.

To specify a subgraph
T
of
S
we proceed as follows. We first choose root vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{c}
for the components, say
T_{1},\ldots,T_{c}
, of
T
, the number of possibilities for this being at most
\binom{2t}{c}
.
We then choose the sizes, say
t_{1},\ldots,t_{c}
, of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{c}
; here the number of possibilities is at most
\sum_{u=c}^{t}\binom{u-1}{c-1}
. (For
u<t
, the summand is the number of positive integer solutions to
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{c}=u
.) Finally, we specify for each
i
a connected
S_{i}
of size
t_{i}
rooted at
v_{i}
in at most
\prod_{i=1}^{c}(2ke)^{t_{i}}
ways. This comes for the fact that there are at most
(\Delta e)^{t-1}
rooted subtrees of the infinite
\Delta
-regular tree, see Knuth
[
9
]
, p396, Ex11. Combining these estimates (with
\sum_{u=c}^{t}\binom{u-1}{c-1}=\binom{t}{c}<2^{t}
) yields the proposition.
∎

It follows from these two propositions that if
S\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
1\leq t\leq n/3k
then

\displaystyle{\frac{f_{t,S}}{|{\mathcal{H}}|}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{c=1}^{t}(2k)^{2t}\left(n-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c-1\right)!\times(4ke)^{t}\binom{2t}{c}\times\frac{1}{(n-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{c=1}^{t}(16k^{3}e)^{t}\binom{2t}{c}\left(\frac{e}{n-1}\right)^{{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}-c}\left(\frac{n-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c-1}{n-1}\right)^{n-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c-1}

\displaystyle\leq e^{O(t)}\sum_{c=1}^{t}n^{-{\left\lceil\frac{t+(2k-1)c}{k}\right\rceil}+c}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{O(1)}{n^{1/k}}\right)^{t}.

So the
k
th power of a Hamiltonian cycle satisfies the conditions with
r=kn
,
\kappa=O(n^{1/k})
,
\alpha=1/3k
.

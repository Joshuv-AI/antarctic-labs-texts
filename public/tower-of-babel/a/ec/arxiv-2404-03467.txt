Exponential decay of solutions to linear evolution equations with time-dependent time delay
Elisa Continelli, &, Cristina Pignotti

Abstract
In this note, we analyze an abstract evolution equation with time-dependent time delay and time-dependent delay feedback coefficient. We assume that the operator corresponding to the nondelayed part of the model generates an exponentially stable semigroup. Under an appropriate assumption on the delay feedback, we prove the well-posedness and an exponential stability estimate for our model. Applications of our abstract results to concrete models are also illustrated.



Keywords and Phrases:
evolution equations, wave equation, stability estimates, time delay.

1
Introduction

The study of evolution equations in presence of delay or memory terms attracted, in recent years, the interest of many researchers. The presence of a time delay makes the problems more difficult to deal with and, of course, it is important to include in the models time delays/memory terms to take into account time lags, such as reaction times, maturation times, times needed to receive some information, etc., commonly present in real life phenomena.

On the other hand, it is well-known that a time delay can produce instability phenomena. In particular, for the damped wave equation, it has been proven that an arbitrarily small delay can make the model unstable even if it is uniformly asymptotically stable in absence of delay effects (see e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
22
,
33
]
).

Nevertheless, suitable feedback laws can ensure the delayed model has the same stability properties as the undelayed one (see
[
22
,
33
]
).
Here, we are interested in proving exponential stability estimates for abstract linear evolution equations in presence of a time delay feedback. In particular, we will consider time-variable time delays, that is time delays that are functions of the time variable.

Let
\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty),
be the time delay function belonging to
C(0,+\infty)
.
We assume that

0\leqslant\tau(t)\leqslant\bar{\tau},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0,

(1.1)

for a suitable positive constant
\bar{\tau}
.

We consider the following abstract model:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+k(t)BU(t-\tau(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{U(t)=f(t)\quad t\in[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(1.2)

where the operator
A
generates an exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
in a Hilbert space
H
, and
B
is a continuous linear operator of
H
into itself. Moreover, the delay damping coefficient
k:[-\bar{\tau},+\infty)\to{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty);{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
, and
\tau
is a continuous time delay function satisfying (
1.1
). We denote with
U_{0}:=f(0)
.

By the assumptions on the operator
A,
there exist two positive constants
M
and
\omega
such that

\|S(t)\|\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\,.

(1.3)

Under some mild assumptions on the involved functions and parameters, we will establish the well-posedness of the problem (
1.2
), and we will obtain exponential decay estimates for its solutions.

Moreover, on the delay feedback coefficient, we assume that the integrals on intervals of length
\bar{\tau}
are uniformly bounded, namely,

\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|ds\leqslant K,\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0,

(1.4)

for some
K>0.

Stability results for abstract evolution equations with delay have been already studied in
[
24
,
25
,
18
]
. In
[
24
,
25
]
it is analyzed the case of a single constant delay and also the delay damping coefficient is assumed to be constant. The analysis has then been extended in
[
18
]
by considering, as here, (multiple) time dependent time delays.
However, here we work in a more general setting. Indeed, in
[
18
]
, the classical set of assumptions usually employed to deal with wave-type equations in presence of time variable time delays (see e.g.
[
26
,
9
,
14
]
) is used. In particular, in addition to (
1.1
), it is required that

\tau\in W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)
and that

\tau^{\prime}(t)\leqslant c<1.

On the contrary, in the present note, we only assume that the time delay is a function bounded from above. Therefore, our results significantly
extend and improve the ones in
[
18
]
.

A concrete model that can be rewritten in the form (
1.2
) is, e.g., the wave equation with frictional damping and delay feedback. In the case of constant delay feedback coefficient and constant time delay, this model has been first studied in
[
30
]
. Under a suitable smallness condition on the delay term coefficient, an exponential decay estimate has been proven. This result has then been extended to linear wave equations with internal delay feedback and boundary dissipative condition in
[
5
]
. In
[
2
,
15
]
it is instead analyzed the case of the wave equations with delay feedback and viscoelastic damping.

For other stability estimates in the presence of time delay effects, for specific models, mainly in the case of constant time delay and constant delay damping coefficient, we quote, among the others,
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
3
,
10
,
27
,
31
,
20
]
. We mention also the recent papers
[
17
]
and
[
8
]
dealing with delayed Korteweg-de Vries-Burgers and higher-order dispersive equations,
respectively.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we prove the well-posedness of the model. We give two different proofs: the first, via a step by step procedure, under the additional assumption that the time delay function is bounded from below by a positive constant; the second, in the general setting, using a fixed point theorem. In section
3
, we deduce the exponential stability estimate for the linear model. In section
4
, we extend the well-posedness and exponential stability results to a nonlinear model with a Lipschitz continuous nonlinearity. Finally, section
5
illustrates some concrete examples for which the abstract theory is applicable: namely the damped wave equation with delay and the elasticity system with delay.

2
Well-posedness

Now, we prove the following well-posedness result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f:[-\bar{\tau},0]\to H
be a continuous function. Then, the problem (
1.2
) has a unique (weak) solution given by Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\,k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))\ ds,

(2.1)

for all
t\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Let
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
. We give two different proofs, the first one only valid under the additional assumption that the time delay is bounded from below by a positive constant.

Case 1.
Assume that

\tau(t)\geqslant\tau_{0}>0,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

(2.2)

for a suitable positive constant
\tau_{0}.
We can argue step by step, as in the proof of Proposition 2.1 of
[
18
]
, by restricting ourselves each time to time intervals of length
\tau_{0}.

First we consider
t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
. Then, from (
2.2
),
t-\tau(t)\in[-\bar{\tau},0].
So,
setting
F(t)=k(t)BU(t-\tau(t)),

t\in[0,\tau_{0}],
we have that
F(t)=k(t)Bf(t-\tau(t)),

t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
.
Then, problem (
1.2
) can be rewritten, in the interval
[0,\tau_{0}]
, as a standard inhomogeneous evolution problem:

\begin{array}[]{l}U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+F(t)\quad\mbox{in}\ (0,\tau_{0}),\\
U(0)=U_{0}.\end{array}

(2.3)

Since
k\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty);{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
,
B
is a bounded linear operator and
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
, we have that
F\in\mathcal{L}^{1}((0,\tau_{0});H)
.
Therefore, applying
[
29
, Corollary 2.2]
there exists a unique solution
U\in C([0,\tau_{0}];H)
of (
2.3
) satisfying the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)F(s)ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau_{0}].

Therefore,

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau_{0}].

Next, we consider the time interval
[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]
and also define

F(t)=k(t)BU(t-\tau(t)),
for
t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}].
Note that, if
t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}],
then
t-\tau(t)\in[-\bar{\tau},\tau_{0}]
and so
U(t-\tau(t))
is known from the first step.
Then
F_{|_{[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]}}
is a known function and it belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{1}((\tau_{0},2\tau_{0});H)
.
So we can rewrite our model (
1.2
) in the time interval
[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]
as the inhomogeneous evolution problem

\begin{array}[]{l}U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+F(t)\quad\mbox{for }\ t\in(\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}),\\
U(\tau)=U(\tau_{0}^{-}).\end{array}

(2.4)

Then, by the standard theory of abstract Cauchy problems, we have a unique continuous solution
U:[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]\rightarrow H
satisfying

U(t)=S(t-\tau_{0})U(\tau_{0}^{-})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}S(t-s)F(s)ds,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}],

and so

U(t)=S(t-\tau_{0})U(\tau_{0}^{-})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))ds,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}].

Putting together the partial solutions obtained in the first and second steps we have a unique continuous solution
U:[0,2\tau_{0}]\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}

satisfying the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))ds,\quad t\in[0,2\tau_{0}].

Iterating this procedure we can find a unique solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
satisfying the representation formula (
2.1
).

Case 2.
Let
\tau(\cdot)
be a continuous function satisfying (
1.1
). In this case, since assumption (
2.2
) does not necessarily hold, we cannot use a step by step procedure in order to get the existence of a solution to (
1.2
) with the initial datum
f
, as we did in Case 1. We have rather to employ a different method, based on the use of the Banach’s fixed point Theorem.

Now, since
k\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty);{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
, there exists
T>0
such that

\lVert k\rVert_{{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}=\int_{0}^{T}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds<\frac{1}{M\lVert B\rVert}.

(2.5)

We define the set

C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H):=\{U\in C([-\bar{\tau},T];H):U(s)=f(s),\,\forall s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]\}.

Let us note that
C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)\neq\emptyset
, since it suffices to take

U(t)=\begin{cases}U_{0},\quad&t\in(0,T],\\
f(t),\quad&t\in[-\bar{\tau},0],\end{cases}

to have that
U\in C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
.

It is immediate to see that

C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
with the norm

\lVert U\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}=\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},T]}\lVert U(r)\rVert,\quad\forall U\in C([-\bar{\tau},T];H),

is a Banach space.

Now, we define the map

\Gamma:C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)\rightarrow C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
given by

\Gamma U(t)=\begin{cases}S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))\,ds,\quad&t\in[0,T],\\
f(t),\quad&t\in[-\bar{\tau},0).\end{cases}

We claim that
\Gamma
is well-defined. Indeed, let
U\in C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
. Then,
from the semigroup theory,
t\mapsto S(t)U_{0}
is continuous. Also, since
U(\cdot)
is continuous in
[-\bar{\tau},T]
,
\tau(\cdot)
is a continuous function and
B
is a bounded linear operator from
H
into itself,
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto BU(t-\tau(t))
is continuos. Moreover,
k\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
. So
k(\cdot)BU(\cdot-\tau(\cdot))\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,T];H)
. Hence, the map
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))ds
is continuous in
[0,T]
. Thus,
\Gamma U\in C([0,T];H)
. Finally, since
\Gamma U=f
in
[\bar{\tau},0]
with
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
and
f(0)=U_{0}
,
\Gamma U\in C_{f}([\bar{\tau},T];H)
and
\Gamma
is well-defined.

Now, let
U,V\in C_{f}([\bar{\tau},T];H)
. For all
t\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
,

\lVert\Gamma U(t)-\Gamma V(t)\rVert=0.

On the other hand, for all
t\in(0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\lVert\Gamma U(t)-\Gamma V(t)\rVert\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S(t-s)\rVert\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert BU(s-\tau(s))-BV(s-\tau(s))\rVert ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant M\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert U(s-\tau(s))-V(s-\tau(s))\rVert ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant M\lVert B\rVert\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}\int_{0}^{T}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt=MB\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}.}\end{array}

Thus,

\lVert\Gamma U(t)-\Gamma V(t)\rVert\leqslant MB\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)},\quad\forall t\in[-\bar{\tau},T],

from which

\lVert\Gamma U-\Gamma V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}\leqslant M\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}.

Now, from (
2.5
) we have that
M\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}<1
. Hence,
\Gamma
is a contraction. Then, from the Banach’s Theorem,
\Gamma
has a unique fixed point
U\in C_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
, i.e. (
1.2
) has a unique solution
U\in C([0,T];H)
given by the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))\,ds,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Now, let us note that the solution
U
is bounded. Indeed, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\lVert U(t)\rVert\leqslant M\lVert U_{0}\rVert+M\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert U(s-\tau(s))\rVert ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant M\lVert U_{0}\rVert+M\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert f(r)\rVert+M\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert\max_{r\in[0,s]}\lVert U(r)\rVert ds.}\end{array}

Then,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\max_{r\in[0,t]}\lVert U(r)\rVert\leqslant M\left(\lVert U_{0}\rVert+\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert f(r)\rVert\right)+M\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert\max_{r\in[0,s]}\lVert U(r)\rVert ds.}\end{array}

Hence, the Gronwall’s estimate yields

\max_{r\in[0,t]}\lVert U(r)\rVert\leqslant M\left(\lVert U_{0}\rVert+\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert f(r)\rVert\right)e^{M\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds,}

from which, taking into account of (
2.5
),

\lVert U(t)\rVert\leqslant e\left(M\lVert U_{0}\rVert+\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert f(r)\rVert\right),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Thus, the solution
U
is bounded and we can extend it up to some maximal interval
[0,\delta)
,
\delta>0
. We claim that
\delta=+\infty
. Indeed, assume by contradiction that
\delta<+\infty
. Then, being
U
bounded, we can consider the following problem

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{V^{\prime}(t)=AV(t)+k(t)BV(t-\tau(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{V(t)=U(t)\quad t\in[\delta-\bar{\tau},\delta),}\\
\displaystyle{V(\delta)=U(\delta^{-}).}\end{array}

(2.6)

Arguing as before, there exists
T^{\prime}>0
such that

\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([\delta,T^{\prime}],{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}=\int_{\delta}^{T^{\prime}}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds<\frac{1}{M\lVert B\rVert}.

(2.7)

We then set

C_{U}([\delta-\bar{\tau},T^{\prime}];H)=\{V\in C([\delta-\bar{\tau},T^{\prime}];H):V(s)=U(s),\forall s\in[\delta-\bar{\tau},\delta),V(\delta)=U(\delta^{-})\},

which is a nonempty closed subset of
C([\delta-\bar{\tau},T^{\prime}];H)
.

Next, we define the map
\Gamma:C_{U}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)\rightarrow C_{U}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
given by

\Gamma V(t)=\begin{cases}S(t-\delta)U(\delta^{-})+\int_{\delta}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BV(s-\tau(s)),\quad&t\in[\delta,T^{\prime}],\\
U(t),\quad&t\in[\delta-\bar{\tau},\delta).\end{cases}

We have that
\Gamma
is well-defined and, using the same arguments employed at the beginning of Case 2, (
2.7
) implies that
\Gamma
is a contraction. So,
\Gamma
has a unique fixed point, i.e. (
2.6
) has a unique continuous
V
solution given by the Duhamel formula

V(t)=S(t-\delta)U(\delta^{-})+\int_{\delta}^{t}S(t-s)k(s)BV(s-\tau(s)),\quad t\in[\delta,T^{\prime}].

Thus, putting together the solutions
U
,
V
, we get the existence of a unique continuous solution to (
1.2
) that satisfies the Duhamel’s formula (
2.1
) and that is defined in
[0,\delta^{\prime})
, with
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
. This contradicts the maximality of
\delta
. Hence,
\delta=+\infty
, i.e. (
1.2
) has a unique global solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
given by (
2.1
).

3
Exponential stability

Under an appropriate relation between the problem’s parameters the system (
1.2
) is exponentially stable.
In particular, we assume that

M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds\leqslant\gamma+\omega^{\prime}t,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

(3.1)

for suitable constants
\gamma\geqslant 0
and
\omega^{\prime}\in[0,\omega).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (
3.1
).
For every
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H),
the solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
to (
1.2
) with the initial datum
f
satisfies the exponential decay estimate

||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(\|U_{0}\|+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\lVert B\rVert\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},

(3.2)

for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
. Let
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
be the solution to (
1.2
) with the initial condition
f
. From Duhamel’s Formula, we have that

\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}||U_{0}||+Me^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||BU(s-\tau(s))||ds,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.}

Then, for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}||U_{0}||+Me^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||{B}U(s-\tau(s))||ds}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\displaystyle{+Me^{-\omega t}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||BU(s-\tau(s))||ds}\\
\hskip 34.14322pt\displaystyle{\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}||U_{0}||+M||B||e^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||ds}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\displaystyle{+M||B||e^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||ds.}\end{array}

(3.3)

Now, observe that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot\|U(s-\tau(s))\|ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\left(\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|f(r)\|\right\}+\max_{r\in[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|U(r)\|\right\}\right)ds}\\
\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\displaystyle{K\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|f(r)\|\right\}+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|U(r)\|\right\}ds.}\end{array}

(3.4)

Then, using (
3.4
) in (
3.3
), we deduce

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\overline{\tau}}K\|B\|\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|f(r)\|\right\}\right)}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\displaystyle{+M\|B\|e^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}ds,}\end{array}

(3.5)

for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
. On the other hand, for all
t\in[0,\bar{\tau}]
, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}||U_{0}||+M\|B\|e^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||ds.}\end{array}

Then, arguing as before,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot\|U(s-\tau(s))\|ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\left(\max_{r\in[-\overline{\tau},0]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|f(r)\|\right\}+\max_{r\in[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|U(r)\|\right\}\right)ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant K\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|f(r)\|\right\}+\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}\|U(r)\|\right\}ds}\end{array}

for all
t\in[0,\bar{\tau}]
. Hence, (
3.5
) holds also for
t\in[0,\bar{\tau}]
. So, we deduce

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{e^{\omega t}||U(t)||\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\|B\|\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}ds,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.}\end{array}

Now, we note that it also holds

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\max_{s\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\left\{e^{\omega s}||U(s)||\right\}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\|B\|\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}ds,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.}\end{array}

Hence, if we denote

\tilde{u}(t):=\max_{s\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\left\{e^{\omega s}||U(s)||\right\},

Gronwall’s estimate implies

{\tilde{u}}(t)\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{M\|B\|e^{\omega\overline{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

where

\tilde{M}:=M\left(\|U_{0}\|+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\|B\|\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right).

Then,

e^{\omega t}\|U(t)\|\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Finally, assumption (
3.1
), yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\|U(t)\|\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds}e^{-\omega t}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{\gamma+\omega^{\prime}t}e^{-\omega t}=\tilde{M}e^{\gamma}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},}\end{array}

for all
t\geqslant 0
, which proves the exponential decay estimate (
3.2
).

4
A nonlinear model

As an easy generalization of previous results, we can consider the nonlinear model

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+k(t)BU(t-\tau(t))+G(U(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{U(t)=f(t)\quad t\in[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(4.1)

where
A,
B,

k(\cdot),\tau(\cdot)
are as before, and we denote
U_{0}:=f(0)
. Moreover,
G:H\rightarrow H
is Lipschitz continuous, namely there exists
L>0
such that

\|G(U_{1})-G(U_{2})\|\leqslant L\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|,\quad\forall\ U_{1},U_{2}\in H,

(4.2)

and we assume that
G(0)=0
.

Analogously to before, one can first give a well-posedness result. See
[
25
]
for the proof in the case of constant time delay.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:[-\bar{\tau},0]\to H
be a continuous function. Then, the problem (
4.1
) has a unique (weak) solution given by Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]\ ds,

(4.3)

for all
t\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Let
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0],H)
. As before, we can give two different proofs.

Case 1
Assume that (
2.2
) holds tue. We can argue step by step, as before, by restricting ourselves each time to time intervals of length
\tau_{0}.

First we consider
t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
. Then, from (
2.2
),
t-\tau(t)\in[-\bar{\tau},0].
So,
setting
F(t)=k(t)BU(t-\tau(t)),

t\in[0,\tau_{0}],
we have that
F(t)=k(t)Bf(t-\tau(t)),

t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
.
Then, problem (
4.1
) can be rewritten, in the interval
[0,\tau_{0}]
, as a standard inhomogeneous evolution problem:

\begin{array}[]{l}U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+G(U(t))+F(t)\quad\mbox{in}\ (0,\tau_{0}),\\
U(0)=U_{0}.\end{array}

(4.4)

Since
k\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty);{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
and
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
, then we have that
F\in\mathcal{L}^{1}((0,\tau_{0});H)
.
Therefore, applying the standard theory for nonlinear evolution equations (see e.g.
[
29
]
), there exists a unique solution
U\in C([0,\tau_{0}];H)
of (
4.4
) satisfying the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+F(s)]ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau_{0}].

Therefore,

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau_{0}].

Next, we consider the time interval
[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]
and also define

F(t)=k(t)BU(t-\tau(t)),
for
t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}].
Note that, if
t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}],
then
t-\tau(t)\in[-\bar{\tau},\tau_{0}]
and so
U(t-\tau(t))
is known from the first step.
Then
F_{|_{[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]}}
is a known function and it belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{1}((\tau_{0},2\tau_{0});H)
.
So we can rewrite our model (
4.1
) in the time interval
[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]
as the inhomogeneous evolution problem

\begin{array}[]{l}U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+G(U(t))+F(t)\quad\mbox{for }\ t\in(\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}),\\
U(\tau)=U(\tau_{0}^{-}).\end{array}

(4.5)

Then, we have a unique continuous solution
U:[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}]\rightarrow H
satisfying

U(t)=S(t-\tau_{0})U(\tau_{0}^{-})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+F(s)]ds,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}],

and so

U(t)=S(t-\tau_{0})U(\tau_{0}^{-})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]ds,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}].

Putting together the partial solutions obtained in the first and second steps we have a unique continuous solution
U:[0,2\tau_{0})\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}

satisfying the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]ds,\quad t\in[0,2\tau_{0}].

Iterating this procedure we can find a unique solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
satisfying the representation formula (
4.3
).

Case 2
Let
\tau(\cdot)
be a continuous function satisfying (
1.1
).

Now, since
k\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty);{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
, there exists
T>0
sufficiently small such that

LT+\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}=LT+\lVert B\rVert\int_{0}^{T}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds<\frac{1}{M},

(4.6)

where
L
is the Lipschitz constant in (
4.2
).
We define the set

\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H):=\{U\in C([-\bar{\tau},T];H):U(s)=f(s),\,\forall s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]\}.

Let us note that
\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
is a nonempty and closed subset of
C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
.
Hence,
(\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)})
is a Banach space.

Next, we define the map

\tilde{\Gamma}:\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)\rightarrow\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
given by

\tilde{\Gamma}U(t)=\begin{cases}S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]\,ds,\quad&t\in[0,T],\\
f(t),\quad&t\in[-\bar{\tau},0).\end{cases}

Let us note that
\tilde{\Gamma}
is well-defined.

Moreover,
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a contraction. Indeed, let
U,V\in\tilde{C}_{f}([\bar{\tau},T];H)
. Then, for all
t\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
,

\lVert\tilde{\Gamma}U(t)-\tilde{\Gamma}V(t)\rVert=0.

On the other hand, for all
t\in(0,T]
, since
G
is Lipschitz continuous we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\lVert\tilde{\Gamma}U(t)-\tilde{\Gamma}V(t)\rVert\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S(t-s)\rVert G(U(s))-G(V(s))\rVert ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S(t-s)\rVert\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert BU(s-\tau(s))-BV(s-\tau(s))\rVert ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant M(LT+\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}.}\end{array}

Thus,

\lVert\tilde{\Gamma}U-\tilde{\Gamma}V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}\leqslant M(LT+\lVert B\rVert\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},T];H)}.

As a consequence, from (
4.6
)
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a contraction. Thus, from the Banach’s Theorem,
\tilde{\Gamma}
has a unique fixed point
U\in\tilde{C}_{f}([-\bar{\tau},T];H)
, i.e. (
1.2
) has a unique solution
U\in C([0,T];H)
given by the Duhamel formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[G(U(s))+k(s)BU(s-\tau(s))]\,ds,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Now, note that the solution
U
is bounded. So, arguing as in
Case 2
of Theorem
2.1
, we can conclude that (
4.1
) has a unique continuous global solution that satisfies the Duhamel’s formula (
4.3
).

Under an appropriate relation between the problem’s parameters, the system (
4.1
) is exponentially stable.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume (
3.1
) and
L<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{M}.
Then, for every
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H),
the solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);H)
to (
4.1
) with the initial datum
f
satisfies the exponential decay estimate

||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(\|U_{0}\|+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\lVert B\rVert\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime}-ML)t},

(4.7)

for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H)
and let
U
be the unique global solution to (
4.1
) with initial datum
f
. Then, from Duhamel’s formula (
4.3
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}||U_{0}||+Me^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}||G(U(s))||ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+M\lVert B\rVert e^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||)ds,}\end{array}

for all
t\geqslant 0
. Now, using the same arguments employed in Theorem

3.1
, we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||\,ds\leqslant e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||\,ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||\,ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 71.13188pt\leqslant e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}}\{||e^{\omega r}f(r)||\}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\}ds,\end{array}

(4.8)

for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
. Also, for all
t\in[0,\bar{\tau}]
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||\,ds\leqslant e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||\,ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{||e^{\omega r}f(r)||\}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\}ds,}\end{array}

i.e. (
4.8
) holds true for all
t\geqslant 0
. Hence, from (
4.8
) we get

\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K\lVert B\rVert\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{||e^{\omega r}f(r)||\}\right)}\hskip 142.26378pt

\hskip 28.45274pt\displaystyle{+Me^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}||B||\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\}ds+Me^{-\omega t}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}||G(U(s))||ds.

Now, let us note that, being
G(0)=0
and being
G
Lipschitz continuous of constant
L
,

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}||G(U(s))||ds\leqslant L\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}||U(s)||\leqslant L\int_{0}^{t}\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\}ds.}

As a consequence, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||U(t)||\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K||B||\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{||e^{\omega r}f(r)||\})}\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+Me^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}(e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}||B|||k(s)|+L)\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\}ds.}\end{array}

Then, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{e^{\omega t}||U(t)||\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K||B||\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega r}f(r)\|\right\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+M\int_{0}^{t}(e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}||B|||k(s)|+L)\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}ds,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.}\end{array}

Now, we note that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\max_{r\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K||B||\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega s}f(s)\|\right\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+M\int_{0}^{t}(e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}||B|||k(s)|+L)\cdot\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\}ds,\quad\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.}\end{array}

Hence, if we denote with

\tilde{u}(t):=\max_{r\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\left\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||\right\},

Gronwall’s estimate yields

{\tilde{u}}(t)\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds+MLt},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0

where

\tilde{M}:=M\left(\|U_{0}\|+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}K||B||\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\left\{\|e^{\omega r}f(r)\|\right\}\right).

Then,

e^{\omega t}\|U(t)\|\leqslant{\tilde{M}}e^{M\|B\|e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds+MLt}.

Finally, by the assumption (
3.1
) and the assumption on the Lipschitz constant
L
, we get the exponential decay estimate (
4.7
).

5
Examples

In this section we consider, as concrete examples for which previous abstract well-posedness and stability results hold, the wave equation with localized frictional damping and delay feedback and an elasticity system with analogous feedback laws.

5.1
The damped wave equation

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
, with boundary
\partial\Omega
of class
C^{2},
and let
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
be an open subset which satisfies the geometrical control property in
[
7
]
.
For instance,
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
can be a neighborhood of the whole boundary
\partial\Omega
or, denoting by
m
the standard multiplier
m(x)=x-x_{0},

x_{0}\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d},
as in
[
19
]
,
\mathcal{O}
can be the intersection of
\Omega
with an open neighborhood of the set

\Gamma_{0}=\left\{\,x\in\Gamma\ :\ m(x)\cdot\nu(x)>0\,\right\}.

Moreover, let
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\subset\Omega
be another open subset. Denoting by
\chi_{\mathcal{O}}
and
\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}
the characteristic functions of the sets
{\mathcal{O}}
and
\tilde{{\mathcal{O}}}
respectively, we consider the following wave equation

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)+a\chi_{\mathcal{O}}(x)u_{t}(x,t)}\\
\hskip 108.12054pt\displaystyle{+k(t)\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}(x)u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),\quad u_{t}(x,s)=u_{1}(x,s),\quad(x,s)\in{\Omega}\times[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(5.1)

where
a
is a positive constant,
\tau(t)
is the time delay function satisfying
0\leqslant\tau(t)\leqslant\bar{\tau},
and the delayed damping coefficient
k(\cdot):[-\bar{\tau},+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty))
function satisfying (
1.4
).
Denoting
v(t)=u_{t}(t)
and
U(t)=(u(t),v(t))^{T},
for any
t\geqslant 0,
, we can rewrite system (
5.1
) in the abstract form (
1.2
), with
{\mathcal{H}}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
,

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&Id\\
\Delta&-a\chi_{\mathcal{O}}\end{pmatrix}

and

B\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}v\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0.

We know that
{A}
generates an exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
(see e.g.
[
16
]
), namely there exist
\omega,M>0
such that

||S(t)||_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leqslant Me^{-\omega t},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0.

Hence, under the assumption (
3.1
), the stability estimate of Theorem
3.1
holds for such a model. Then, we can deduce an exponential decay estimate for the
energy functional

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}|k(s)|\cdot|u_{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under assumption (
3.1
), for all initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)),
the solution to
(
5.1
) satisfies the energy decay estimate

E(t)\leqslant C_{*}e^{-\beta t},\quad t\geqslant 0,

(5.2)

where
C_{*}
is a constant depending on the initial data and
\beta>0.

Proof.

From the energy’s definition,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|U(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\overline{\tau}}^{t}\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}|k(s)|\cdot|u_{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 65.44142pt\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|U(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\|U(s)\|^{2}ds.}\end{array}

(5.3)

Then, from Theorem
3.1
,

\|U(t)\|\leqslant C_{0}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

for a suitable constant
C_{0}
depending on the initial data.
So, we can estimate

\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\|U(s)\|^{2}ds\leqslant C_{0}Ke^{(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\bar{\tau}}\,e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

By using the last two inequalities in (
5.3
), we obtain the exponential decay estimate (
5.2
).

Remark 5.2
.

As another example, we could consider the damped plate equation (see e.g.
[
23
]

for the model details). The analysis is analogous to the wave case above.
Then, under suitable assumptions, the exponential stability result holds for that model.

5.2
A damped elasticity system

Let
\Omega\subset{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d},
and let
\tilde{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
be as in the previous example.
We consider the following elastodynamic system

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(x,t)-\mu\Delta u(x,t)-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\,\mbox{\rm div}\ u+a\chi_{\mathcal{O}}(x)u_{t}(x,t)}\\
\vskip 2.0pt\hskip 99.58464pt\displaystyle{+k(t)\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}(x)u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\vskip 2.0pt\displaystyle{u(x,t)=0,\ \ \quad\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),\quad u_{t}(x,s)=u_{1}(x,s),\quad(x,s)\in{\Omega}\times[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(5.4)

where
a
is a positive constant,
\tau(t)
is the time delay function satisfying
0\leqslant\tau(t)\leqslant\bar{\tau},
and the delayed damping coefficient
k(\cdot):[-\bar{\tau},+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty))
function satisfying (
1.4
).
Note that, in this case, the function
u
is vector-valued and takes values in
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
while
\lambda
and
\mu
are positive constants usually called Lamé coefficients.
Denoting
v(t)=u_{t}(t)
and
U(t)=(u(t),v(t))^{T},
for any
t\geqslant 0,
, we can rewrite system (
5.4
) in the abstract form (
1.2
), with
{\mathcal{H}}={H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}^{d}\times{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{d}
,

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&Id\\
\mu\Delta+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\mbox{\rm div}\ &-a\chi_{\mathcal{O}}\end{pmatrix}

and

B\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}v\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0.

We know that
{A}
generates an exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
(see e.g.
[
13
]
), namely there exist
\omega,M>0
such that

||S(t)||_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leqslant Me^{-\omega t},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0.

Hence, under the assumption (
3.1
), the stability estimate of Theorem
3.1

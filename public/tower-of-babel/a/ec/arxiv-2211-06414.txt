Radiative electron attachment to rotating C 3 ​ N \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} through dipole-bound states
Joshua Forer 1,2 , Viatcheslav Kokoouline 1 , Thierry Stoecklin 2

Abstract
The role of a large dipole moment in rotating neutral molecules interacting with low-energy electrons is studied using an accurate ab initio approach accounting for electronic and rotational degrees of freedom. It is found that theory can reproduce weakly-bound (dipole-bound) states observed in a recent photodetachment experiment with C 3 ​ N – \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}} [Phys. Rev. Lett. 127 , 043001 (2021)]. Using a similar level of theory, the cross section for radiative electron attachment to the C 3 ​ N \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} molecule, forming the dipole-bound states, was determined. The obtained cross section is too small to explain the formation of C 3 ​ N – \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}} in the interstellar medium, suggesting that it is likely formed by a different process.



Introduction

Despite the large amount of molecules detected in interstellar and circumstellar environments, only six molecular anions have been detected
Thaddeus et al. 2008
;
Cernicharo et al. 2007
;
Cernicharo et al. 2008
;
McCarthy et al. 2006
;
Brünken et al. 2007
:
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
, and
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
. Their formation is not yet well understood. Initially, it was proposed that such anions are formed by the process of radiative electron attachment (REA), in which a free electron can attach to the neutral molecule
M
and release excess energy via photon emission

M+e^{-}\to M^{-}+\hbar\omega\,.

(1)

However, previous calculations
Khamesian et al. 2016
;
Harrison and Tennyson 2011a
;
Lara-Moreno et al. 2019a
;
Lara-Moreno et al. 2019b
have shown that REA rates are too low to explain the observed abundance of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
, and
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
.
It was then suggested that the REA mechanism could take place via a “doorway” weakly bound dipole state (DBS)
Simpson et al. 2021
, first described by Fermi and Teller in 1947
Fermi and Teller 1947
.
For a point dipole interacting with a charge, the existence of an infinite number of DBSs was predicted for dipole values larger than a critical value of approximately 0.65 a.u.
Crawford 1967
(a.u. here and below are atomic units).
Including additional molecular effects, however, modifies this value while reducing the number of DBSs to just a few.
Other contributions to the long-range potential being associated with the electrostatic and polarization potentials need to be considered when modeling a neutral polar molecule interacting with a point charge and, for these reasons, some authors have also investigated the possible existence of quadrupole and polarization bound states
Desfrançois et al. 1994
;
Abdoul-Carime, H. and Desfrançois, C. 1998
.
Experimentally, the minimum dipole moment required for the formation of stable dipole-bound anions of common closed-shell molecules has been measured to range between 2-2.5 D. Over the years, the effect of vibration
Anstöter et al. 2020
and rotation of the dipolar molecule on the DBS was also investigated
Brinkman et al. 1993
;
Walthall et al. 2005
, showing that the critical moment for a rotating dipole is larger than that of a stationary dipole.
However, a quantum study of REA through a dipole-bound state including both the rotation of the molecule and the short range part of the electron-molecule potential is missing.
In this study we consider the REA process via the DBSs of one of the anions detected in the ISM:
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
.
This molecule is a good candidate because the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
dipole is supercritical and the existence of a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
DBS was demonstrated in a recent ion trap experiment.
Simpson et al. 2021

Theoretical model

The theoretical model of the present study is based on wave functions (bound and continuum states) of the
\text{e}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}

\text{e}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}

(r,\theta)

To highlight the effect of the short range contribution, two different potentials will be used.
The first potential,

V_{\text{dip}}=-\dfrac{\mu e}{r^{2}}P_{1}(\cos\theta)\,,

(2)

(

1.3 a.u.) and

The second interaction potential

3

3
N

V_{\text{ai}}(\psi^{e})=V^{e}_{\text{Stat}}(\psi^{e})+V_{\text{nuc}}+V_{\text{Ex}}^{\text{HFEGE}}(\psi^{e})+V_{\text{copol}}(\psi^{e})\,,

(3)

where

is the electronic part of the static potential,

The latter two contributions are obtained in the usual density-functional forms of Morrison and Collins

The perpendicular

The same geometry is used within the fixed nuclei approximation for performing the

Electron-molecule scattering and radiative attachment

For REA calculations,
\text{e}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
continuum-state wave functions are also needed. A key difference between our approach and that of Lara-Moreno
et al.

Lara-Moreno et al. 2019a
is that the effect of rotation on the REA process is accounted for. The space-fixed frame is then more appropriate than the body-fixed frame used in that previous study. We furthermore take advantage of using a local model of the
\text{e}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
potential and use a method initially developed for radiative association
Ayouz et al. 2011
;
Stoecklin et al. 2013
and later adapted to REA
Douguet et al. 2013
;
Douguet et al. 2015
.

We do not resolve molecular vibration and write the rigid rotor scattering wave function of the system in Jacobi coordinates in the following form

\psi^{i}(\vec{r},\hat{Z})=\frac{1}{r}\sum\limits_{jl}\chi^{JM}_{jl}(r)\;Y^{JM}_{jl}(\hat{r},\hat{Z})\,,

(5)

where
\vec{r}
and
\hat{Z}
are respectively the electron coordinate vector and the orientation angles of the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
axis in the space-fixed frame. The indices
j
and
l
are, respectively, the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
rotational and electron orbital quantum numbers, while the total angular momentum of the system is
\vec{J}=\vec{j}+\vec{l}
.
The
Y^{JM}_{jl}(\hat{R},\hat{r})
angular functions take the usual form

Y^{JM}_{jl}(\hat{r},\hat{Z})=\sum\limits_{m_{j}m_{l}}\braket{jm_{j}lm_{l}|JM}Y^{m_{l}}_{l}(\hat{r})Y^{m_{j}}_{j}(\hat{Z})\,,

(6)

where
M
,
m_{j}
, and
m_{l}
are, respectively, the projections of
\vec{J}
,
\vec{j}
, and
\vec{l}
on the space-fixed
\hat{z}
-axis.

For a given value of
J
, the radial part of the scattering wave function
\chi^{J}_{jl}(r)
is the solution of the driven differential equation
Stoecklin et al. 2013

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}+k_{j}^{2}(E)-2m_{r}U_{j^{\prime}l^{\prime},jl}^{J}(r)\right)\chi_{j^{\prime}l^{\prime},jl}^{J}(r)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{jl}^{\alpha J}(r)\,,

(7)

where
U_{j^{\prime}l^{\prime},jl}^{J}(r)
is the potential matrix in basis set (
6
). The right-hand-side term is called
the driving term for a given initial state of the
\text{e}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
system and final state of
{}{}\mathrm{C}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{3}}}\mathrm{N}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}^{\mathrm{-}}
, each respectively characterized by the sets of quantum numbers
(j,l)
and
(\alpha,J^{\prime})
.
It is real-valued and results from the dipolar coupling of the initial (scattering) and final (bound) states within the dipolar approximation.
It is given by

\lambda_{jl}^{\alpha J}(r)=-2m_{r}\int d\hat{r}\;d\hat{Z}\;Y^{JM}_{jl}(\hat{r},\hat{Z})\mu(\vec{r},\hat{Z})\psi^{f}_{\alpha J^{\prime}}(\vec{r},\hat{Z})\,,

(8)

where
m_{r}
is the reduced mass of the system.
The final DBS functions
\psi^{f}_{\alpha J^{\prime}}(\vec{r},\hat{Z})
, presented in the previous section, were also calculated in the space fixed frame and expanded in the basis given by equation (
6
), i.e.,

\psi^{f}_{\alpha J^{\prime}}(\vec{r},\hat{Z})=\frac{1}{r}\sum\limits_{jl}\omega^{J^{\prime}M^{\prime}}_{jl}(r)\;Y^{J^{\prime}M^{\prime}}_{jl}(\hat{r},\hat{Z})\,.

(9)

The two sets of DBSs obtained using the potentials
V_{\text{ai}}
and
V_{\text{dip}}
are then used to calculate the REA cross section with a rotational basis of 40 states.
The REA cross section is given by the following equation

\sigma^{\text{REA}}_{f}=\frac{g_{a}}{g_{n}}\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3k_{e}^{2}c^{3}}\sum\limits_{J,J^{\prime},\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}^{3}|M_{j,J}^{\alpha J^{\prime}}|^{2}\,,

(10)

where the transition dipole moment
M_{j,J}^{\alpha J^{\prime}}
is obtained from the driving term
Stoecklin et al. 2013
,
g_{a}
and
g_{n}
are respectively the degeneracy factors of the anion and the neutral, and
\omega_{\alpha}
is the frequency of the emitted photon.

Concluding
, we would like to stress the following findings of the present study.
Energies of dipole-bound states of rotating
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
molecular ion were obtained using an accurate computational approach. In total, six weakly-bound states were found for
J=0-5
. Cross sections for radiative attachment to the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
molecule forming the weakly-bound states of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
were obtained for several initial rotational states
j=0-6
of the neutral molecule and for a large interval of collision energies. The obtained REA cross sections are significantly smaller than those previously obtained for the process towards the ground electronic state of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
, which was, in principle, expected.

•

It was concluded that the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
abundance observed in the ISM cannot be explained by formation of these ions by REA towards weakly or deeply bound electronic states of the ion.
The present results obtained at the rigid rotor level suggest that the effect of the formation of long-lived resonances via vibrationally excited bending vibrations should be considered in future studies. The alternative formation mechanism of large molecular anions via reactions of carbon anions with N atoms should also be considered for C
3
N
-
as suggested by
Bierbaum 2011
and
Agúndez et al. 2010
. Five other negative ions observed in the ISM:
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
, and
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
, deserve separate studies of this kind, as their formation mechanisms are probably not the same.

•

A technical result, imporant for theorists: we have found that the short-range part of the electron-molecule interaction significantly influences the REA cross sections for energies below a few eV.

Null Infinity and Unitary Representation of The Poincare Group
Shamik Banerjee

Abstract
Following Pasterski-Shao-Strominger we construct a new basis of states in the single-particle Hilbert space of massless particles as a linear combination of standard Wigner states. Under Lorentz transformation the new basis states transform in the Unitary Principal Continuous Series representation. These states are obtained if we consider the little group of a null momentum direction rather than a null momentum. The definition of the states in terms of the Wigner states makes it easier to study the action of space-time translation in this basis. We show by taking into account the effect of space-time translation that the dynamics of massless particles described by these states takes place completely on the null-infinity of the Minkowski space . We then second quantize the theory in this basis and obtain a manifestly Poincare invariant (field) theory of free massless particles living on null-infinity. This theory has unitary time evolution. The null-infinity arises in this case purely group-theoretically without any reference to bulk space-time. Action of BMS is particularly natural in this picture. As a by-product we generalize the conformal primary wave-functions for massless particles in a way which makes the action of space-time translation simple. Using these wave-functions we write down a modified Mellin(-Fourier) transformation of the S-matrix elements. The resulting amplitude is Poincare covariant. Under Poincare transformation it transforms like products of primaries of inhomogeneous S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) ( I ​ S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) ISL(2,\mathbb{C}) ) inserted at various points of null-infinity. I ​ S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) ISL(2,\mathbb{C}) primaries are defined in the paper.

In

SL(2,\mathbb{C})
primaries inserted at various points of the celestial sphere at null-infinity. This suggests a relation between 4-D scattering amplitudes and a 2-D Euclidean CFT defined on the celestial sphere. This 4D-2D correspondence is a hint of flat-space holography

In this paper we further explore this construction by looking at the single-particle Hilbert space of

SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(Lorentz group). Geometrically speaking these new states are obtained if, instead of a null momentum vector, we consider the
little-group of a null momentum direction
. A null momentum direction is defined as the
set of all null momentum vectors which point in the same direction
and so can be represented by a
single point of the celestial sphere in the momentum space
. These states are naturally written as
\ket{h,\bar h, z,\bar z}
where

are the stereographic coordinates of the celestial sphere in the momentum space.
(h,\bar{h})
are little-group indices defined as,

h=\frac{1+i\lambda-\sigma}{2},\ \bar{h}=\frac{1+i\lambda+\sigma}{2}

where

U(\Lambda)\ket{h, \bar h, z, \bar z}=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\ket{h \ , \bar h \ ,\frac{az+b}{cz+d} \ , \frac{\bar a \bar z + \bar b}{\bar c \bar z + \bar d}},\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

These states are somewhat analogous to position eigenkets in non-relativistic quantum mechanics. In the state
\ket{h,\bar h,z,\bar z}
the massless particle can be thought of as sitting at the point
(z,\bar{z})
of the celestial sphere in the momentum space and
(h,\bar{h})
are internal quantum numbers. These states form a complete set.

Now the definition of the states
\ket{h,\bar h,z,\bar z}
in terms of the Wigner-states makes it easier to study the action of
space-time translation
on them. It turns out that the simplest way to represent the action of the space-time translation on these states is to first consider the "position eigenket in Heisenberg-picture" defined as,

\Lambda(u,z,\bar{z})=\bigg(\frac{u\ (1+z\bar{z})}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}\ ,\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\ ,\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)

II
Lorentz Group In (2+1) Dimensions

In
(2+1)
dimensions Lorentz group is
SO(2,1)
. In this paper we shall consider
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
which is the double cover of
SO(2,1)
.
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is the group of real two by two matrices with determinant
1
. The group elements will be denoted by,

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\ ad-bc=1,\ (a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{R}

(1)

Let us now consider a momentum vector
P=(P^{0},P^{1},P^{2})
and associate a real symmetric two by two matrix, denoted by the same letter
P
, defined as

P=\begin{pmatrix}P^{0}-P^{2}&P^{1}\\
P^{1}&P^{0}+P^{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \det P=(P^{0})^{2}-(P^{1})^{2}-(P^{2})^{2}=-P^{2}

(2)

So the determinant gives the norm of the three vector
P
.

The
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
matrix
\Lambda
acts on
P
according to the following rule,

P^{\prime}=\Lambda P\Lambda^{T}

(3)

Under this transformation
P^{\prime}
is again a real symmetric matrix and,
\det P=\det P^{\prime}
. Therefore
\{\pm\Lambda\}
induces a Lorentz transformation of the three vector
P
.

II.1
Null Momenta

Let us now specialize to the case of null momenta. When
P
is null we can define a real variable
z
as,

z=\frac{P^{1}}{P^{0}+P^{2}}

(4)

The variable
z
has the property that when
P
transforms under Lorentz transformation as
P\rightarrow P^{\prime}=\Lambda P\Lambda^{T}
,
z
transforms as,

z\rightarrow z^{\prime}=\Lambda z=\frac{P^{1^{\prime}}}{P^{0^{\prime}}+P^{2^{\prime}}}=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

(5)

The geometrical interpretation of the variable
z
is that it parametrizes the
space of null momentum directions
rather than a specific null vector. To see this let us consider the set of all null vectors which point in the positive
2
-direction. These vectors have the form
k^{\mu}=(E,0,E)
where
E>0
. It is easy to check that the set of all such null vectors get mapped to the point
z=0
.

Now let us make a rotation in the
1-2
plane through an angle
\theta
. Under this transformation
k^{\mu}\rightarrow k^{\prime\mu}=(E,E\sin\theta,E\cos\theta)
. This is the most general form of a null vector which makes an angle
\theta
with the positive
2
-axis. As a result of this rotation,

z=0\rightarrow z^{\prime}=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}

(6)

So we can see that the parameter
z
depends only on the direction of the null ray and the set of all null vectors that point in the same direction correspond to a
single
value of
z
. In fact, once we choose the set
\{k^{\mu}=(E,0,E),E>0\}
as the
reference null direction with
(z=0,\theta=0)
then there is an invertible correspondence between the space of null directions and values of
z
given by Eq-(
6
).

Geometrically the space of null momentum directions in (2+1) dimensions is a circle parametrized by the angle
\theta
and
z
is the stereographic coordinate on the circle. The Lorentz group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
acts on the
z
coordinate according to Eq-
5
.

II.2
Standard Rotation

A standard rotation,
R(z)
, is a rotation in the
1-2
plane which takes the
reference null direction
,
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,0,E),E>0\big|z=0\bigg\}
to the null direction
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,E\sin\theta,E\cos\theta),E>0\big|z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg\}
. In matrix form,

R(z)=\pm\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}&\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\\
-\sin\frac{\theta}{2}&\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}

(7)

II.3
Little Group of A Null Momentum
Direction

In the standard Wigner construction
little group
of a
null vector
plays a central role. We want to consider instead the
little group of a null direction
. This is the subgroup of the Lorentz group which does not change the
direction
of a null vector. As a result,
the set of all null vectors which point in the same direction, is closed under the action of this subgroup
. Let us now explicitly write down the subgroup in (2+1) dimensions.

The generators of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
can be written as
(J_{0},K_{1},K_{2})
, where
J_{0}
is the generator of rotation in the
1-2
plane,
K_{1}
is the generator of boost in the
1
-direction and
K_{2}
is the generator of boost in the
2
-direction. Algebra satisfied by these generators is,

[J_{0},K_{1}]=iK_{2},\ [J_{0},K_{2}]=-iK_{1},\ [K_{1},K_{2}]=-iJ_{0}

(8)

Let us now consider the little group of the
reference null direction
given by the set
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,0,E),E>0\big|z=0\bigg\}
. The little group of the
null vector
,
(1,0,1)
, is generated by the element,
A=-J_{0}-K_{1}
. It is well known that the one-parameter subgroup generated by
A
is non-compact and isomorphic to the group of translation. Now in order to get the little group of the reference null direction we just have to add the boost generator
K_{2}
to this. Therefore,
the little group,
D(z=0)
, of the reference null direction,
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,0,E),E>0\big|z=0\bigg\}
, is generated by two elements,
A=-J_{0}-K_{1}
and
K_{2}
. One can check that,
[K_{2},A]=iA
. Similarly the little group of the null direction,
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,E\sin\theta,E\cos\theta),E>0\big|z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg\}
, is given by conjugation,
D(z)=R(z)D(z=0)R(z)^{-1}
.

II.4
Hilbert Space Representation

Let us consider massless single particle quantum states in
(2+1)
dimensions. The single particle states are denoted by
\ket{p}
with
p^{2}=0
. The Lorentz-invariant inner product between the states is given by,
\bra{p_1}\ket{p_2}=(2\pi)^{2}\ 2|\vec{p}_{1}|\ \delta^{2}(\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{p}_{2})
.

Let
\Lambda
be an arbitrary Lorentz transformation and
U(\Lambda)
its unitary representation in the Hilbert space which acts on
\ket{p}
as,

U(\Lambda)\ket{p}=\ket{\Lambda p}

(9)

If we take
\Lambda
to be the boost in the
2
-direction with velocity
v
then

U(\Lambda)\ket{E,0,E}=\ket{e^{-\eta}E,0,e^{-\eta}E}

(10)

where
\eta
is the rapidity defined as,
\tanh\eta=v
.

Now the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
matrix which generates boost in the 2-direction is given by ,

B_{2}(\eta)=\pm\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\eta}{2}}&0\\
\\
0&e^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(11)

The transformation of the
z
variable under boost in the
2
-direction is,
z\rightarrow e^{\eta}z
. So it is a scale transformation for which
z=0
is a fixed point. This is another way of seeing that
K_{2}
is a generator of the little group of the reference null direction
\bigg\{k^{\mu}=(E,0,E),E>0\big|z=0\bigg\}
.

Let us mention one more important point. Here we are considering single particle states
\ket{p}
annihilated by the single non-compact generator
A=-J_{0}-K_{1}
of the
little group of a null vector
, i.e,

A\ket{E,0,E}=0,E>0

(12)

II.4.1
Change of Basis

The works of
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Cheung:2016iub
;
deBoer:2003vf
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
suggest the introduction of the following states parametrized by a complex number
\Delta
,

\ket{\Delta, z=0}\ =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{\Delta-1}\ket{E,0,E}

(13)

Now if we apply a boost in the
2
-direction with rapidity
\eta
we get,

U\big(B_{2}(\eta)\big)\ket{\Delta, z=0}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{\Delta-1}\ket{e^{-\eta}E,0,e^{-\eta}E}\ =e^{\eta\Delta}\ket{\Delta, z=0}

(14)

Let us now define states
\ket{\Delta,z}
which are related to
\ket{\Delta, z=0}
by standard rotation
R(z)
in the
1-2
plane, i.e,

\boxed{\ket{\Delta, z}:=\frac{1}{(1+z^{2})^{\Delta}}\ U\big(R(z)\big)\ket{\Delta, z=0}=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(z))\ket{\Delta, z=0}}

(15)

We can write down the states explicitly as,

\ket{\Delta,z}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{1}{(1+z^{2})^{\Delta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dEE^{\Delta-1}\ket{E, E\sin\theta,E \cos\theta}

(16)

The inner product between two such states can be written as,

\displaystyle\bra{\Delta_2,z_2}\ket{\Delta_1,z_1}

(17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{(1+z_{2}^{2})^{\Delta_{2}^{*}}(1+z_{1}^{2})^{\Delta_{1}}}\times

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE_{2}dE_{1}{E_{2}}^{\Delta_{2}^{*}-1}{E_{1}}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\bra{E_2,E_2\sin\theta_2,E_2\cos\theta_2}\ket{E_1,E_1\sin\theta_1,E_1\cos\theta_1}

where
*
denotes complex conjugation. This integral is convergent if we choose
\Delta
to be of the form
(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R})

Pasterski:2017kqt
. With this choice of
\Delta
the states that we have defined are delta function normalizable, i.e,

\bra{\Delta_2, z_2}\ket{\Delta_1, z_1}=\delta(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\delta(z_{2}-z_{1})

(18)

where
\Delta_{i}=\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda_{i}
.

II.4.2
Action of
U(\Lambda)

Let us now calculate
U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z}
where
\Lambda
is an arbitrary Lorentz transformation. If we use the definition of the state given in Eq-(
15
) we get,

\displaystyle U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z}

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(\Lambda)\ U(R(z))\ket{\Delta, z=0}

(19)

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ U^{-1}(R(\Lambda z))\ U(\Lambda)\ U(R(z))\ket{\Delta, z=0}

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ W(\Lambda,z)\ket{\Delta, z=0}

where
W(\Lambda,z)
is the Lorentz transformation given by,

W(\Lambda,z)=U^{-1}(R(\Lambda z))\ U(\Lambda)\ U(R(z))=U(R^{-1}(\Lambda z)\Lambda R(z))

(20)

Now using the definition of the state
\ket{\Delta, z=0}
we get,

W(\Lambda,z)\ket{\Delta, z=0}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{\Delta-1}W(\Lambda,z)\ket{E,0,E}

(21)

So we have to find out the effect of the transformation
R^{-1}(\Lambda z)\Lambda R(z)
on the set of null vectors of the form
(E,0,E)
, pointing in the positive
2
-direction which is our reference direction. Since Lorentz transformation is linear it is sufficient to consider its effect on the vector
k^{\mu}=(1,0,1)
. The transformations act on
k^{\mu}
in the following way :

1)
R(z)
sends the vector
k^{\mu}=(1,0,1)
to the vector
k^{\prime\mu}=(1,\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
. The corresponding
z
transforms from
z=0
to
z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}
.

2) Now we make an arbitrary Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
given by the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
matrix,

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\ ad-bc=1

(22)

This transformation sends
k^{\prime}
to
\Lambda k^{\prime}
and
z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}
to

\Lambda z=\frac{a\tan\frac{\theta}{2}+b}{c\tan\frac{\theta}{2}+d}=\tan\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}

(23)

where
\Lambda\theta
is the angle made by
\Lambda k^{\prime}
with the positive
2
-direction.

The components of
\Lambda k^{\prime}
are given by,

(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{0}=\frac{(cz+d)^{2}+(az+b)^{2}}{1+z^{2}},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}

(24)

(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{1}=\frac{2(az+b)(cz+d)}{1+z^{2}},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}

(25)

(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{2}=\frac{(cz+d)^{2}-(az+b)^{2}}{1+z^{2}},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}

(26)

We also have the relation,

(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{2}=(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{0}\cos\Lambda\theta,\ (\Lambda k^{\prime})^{1}=(\Lambda k^{\prime})^{0}\sin\Lambda\theta

(27)

3) Now the final rotation
R^{-1}(\Lambda z)
rotates the vector
\Lambda k^{\prime}
in 1-2 plane through an angle
-\Lambda\theta
and the resulting vector points again in the positive 2-direction. Similarly the rotation brings
\Lambda z=\tan\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}
back to
z=0
which is the reference null direction
\bigg\{(E,0,E),E>0\bigg|z=0\bigg\}
. The form of the matrix
R^{-1}(\Lambda z)
is ,

R^{-1}(\Lambda z)=\pm\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}&-\sin\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}\\
\\
\sin\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}&\cos\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \Lambda z=\tan\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}

(28)

Therefore the resulting vector can be written as,

\bigg(R^{-1}(\Lambda z)\Lambda R(z)\bigg)(1,0,1)=\bigg(\frac{(cz+d)^{2}+(az+b)^{2}}{1+z^{2}},\ 0,\ \frac{(cz+d)^{2}+(az+b)^{2}}{1+z^{2}}\bigg)

(29)

Here we have made use of the facts that the
0
-th component of a vector does not change under rotation in the
1-2
plane and the transformed vector is a null vector.

For a general vector
k^{\mu}
of the form
(E,0,E)
we can write,

\bigg(R^{-1}(\Lambda z)\Lambda R(z)\bigg)(E,0,E)=B_{2}(\eta)(E,0,E)=(e^{-\eta}E,0,e^{-\eta}E),\ E>0

(30)

where we have defined the boost factor
e^{-\eta}
as,

\boxed{e^{-\eta}=\frac{(cz+d)^{2}+(az+b)^{2}}{1+z^{2}}=(cz+d)^{2}\ \frac{1+(\Lambda z)^{2}}{1+z^{2}},\ \Lambda z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}}

(31)

With this information we can now derive the transformation law as,

\displaystyle U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z}

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ \underbrace{U^{-1}(R(\Lambda z))\ U(\Lambda)\ U(R(z))}\ket{\Delta, z=0}

(32)

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ W(\Lambda,z)\ket{\Delta,z=0}

\displaystyle=N_{\Delta}(z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ e^{\eta\Delta}\ket{\Delta,z=0},\ \text{We have used Eq-$\ref{LG}$, Eq-$\ref{LT}$, Eq-$\ref{beigenvalue}$}

\displaystyle=e^{\eta\Delta}\frac{N_{\Delta}(z)}{N_{\Delta}(\Lambda z)}\ \underbrace{N_{\Delta}(\Lambda z)U(R(\Lambda z))\ket{\Delta,z=0}}

\displaystyle=e^{\eta\Delta}\frac{N_{\Delta}(z)}{N_{\Delta}(\Lambda z)}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda z},\ \text{We have used Eq-$\ref{define}$}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta}}\ \frac{(1+z^{2})^{\Delta}}{(1+(\Lambda z)^{2})^{\Delta}}\ \frac{(1+(\Lambda z)^{2})^{\Delta}}{(1+z^{2})^{\Delta}}\ \ket{\Delta, \Lambda z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda z}

So a general Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
acts on the states
\ket{\Delta,z}
like,

\boxed{U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta, z}=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda z},\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})}

(33)

Now using this it is easy to see that the states
\big\{\ket{\Delta,\Lambda}\big\}
, for a fixed
\Delta
, form a representation of the Lorentz group. In order to see that let us consider two Lorentz transformations
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
.

\displaystyle U(\Lambda_{2})U(\Lambda_{1})\ket{\Delta,z}

\displaystyle=U(\Lambda_{2})\bigg(\frac{1}{(c_{1}z+d_{1})^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda_1 z}\bigg)

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(c_{1}z+d_{1})^{2\Delta}}\ U(\Lambda_{2})\ket{\Delta, \Lambda_1 z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(c_{1}z+d_{1})^{2\Delta}}\ \frac{1}{(c_{2}(\Lambda_{1}z)+d_{2})^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda_2 \Lambda_1 z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(c_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}}z+d_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}})^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda_2\Lambda_1 z}

\displaystyle=U(\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1})\ket{\Delta, z}

Where,

\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}}&b_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}}\\
c_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}}&d_{\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\ \begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}

(35)

Therefore,

U(\Lambda_{2})U(\Lambda_{1})=U(\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{1})

(36)

and so
U(\Lambda)
form a representation of the Lorentz group when acting on the states
\big\{\ket{\Delta,z}\big\}
according to Eq-
33
. So we have representations of the Lorentz group parametrized by
{
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\big\}
.

This is also a unitary representation as one can easily check that,

\big(U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta',z'},U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z}\big)=\big(\ket{\Delta',z'},\ket{\Delta,z}\big)

(37)

The representation labelled by a fixed
\Delta
is known as the "Unitary principal continuous series" representation of the Lorentz group
bargman
which has also appeared in several recent investigations of CFT
Gadde:2017sjg
;
Hogervorst:2017sfd
;
Simmons-Duffin:2017nub
.

III
Representation on Wave Functions (Packets)

The inner product between the states is given by,

\bra{\Delta',z'}\ket{\Delta,z}=\delta(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\delta(z^{\prime}-z),\ \Delta=\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}

(38)

So we can write the completeness relation as,

\ket{\Psi}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ dz\ \ket{\Delta,z}\bra{\Delta,z}\ket{\Psi}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ dz\ \ket{\Delta,z}\Psi(\Delta,z)

(39)

where
\ket{\Psi}
is an arbitrary massless one-particle state in the QFT. Using this the inner product between two states can be written as,

\bra{\Phi}\ket{\Psi}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\ dz\ \Phi^{*}(\Delta,z)\ \Psi(\Delta,z)

(40)

where
*
denotes complex conjugation. We want to find out the effect of an arbitrary Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
on the wave function
\Psi(\Delta,z)
. In order to do that we can write,

\displaystyle\big(U(\Lambda^{-1})\Psi\big)(\Delta,z)

\displaystyle=\big(\ket{\Delta,z},U(\Lambda^{-1})\ket{\Psi}\big)

(41)

\displaystyle=\big(U(\Lambda^{-1})^{\dagger}\ket{\Delta,z},\ket{\Psi}\big)=\big(U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z},\ket{\Psi}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(U(\Lambda)\ket{\Delta,z},\ket{\Psi}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta}}\ket{\Delta, \Lambda z},\ket{\Psi}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta^{*}}}\ \Psi(\Delta,\Lambda z),\ \Delta^{*}=\frac{1}{2}-i\lambda

Therefore the action of the Lorentz group on the wave-functions is,

\boxed{\big(U(\Lambda^{-1})\Psi\big)\big(\Delta,z\big)=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2\Delta^{*}}}\ \Psi\bigg(\Delta,\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\bigg),\ \Delta^{*}=\frac{1}{2}-i\lambda}

(42)

III.1
Differential Operators and Casimir

The Lorentz group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is the group of conformal transformations of the line (or circle) parametrized by
z
. So let us consider dilatation, special conformal transformation and translation separately.

III.1.1
Translation

Let us consider translation given by,

T(\alpha)z=z^{\prime}=z+\alpha,\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

(43)

Now after a straightforward calculation, which we describe in the Appendix, we get,

U\big(T(\alpha)\big)=e^{i\alpha\big(J_{0}-K_{1}\big)}=e^{\alpha L_{-1}}

(44)

So,

(L_{-1})^{\dagger}=-L_{-1}

(45)

Now using Eq-
42
, we get,

L_{-1}=i(J_{0}-K_{1})=-\frac{d}{dz}

(46)

III.1.2
Special Conformal Transformation (SCT)

Let us consider the SCT given by,

S(\alpha)z=z^{\prime}=\frac{z}{1-\alpha z}\ ,\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}

(47)

After a starightforward calculation we get,

U\big(S(\alpha)\big)=e^{i\alpha(J_{0}+K_{1})}=e^{\alpha L_{1}}

(48)

So,

(L_{1})^{\dagger}=-L_{1}

(49)

In differential operator form we get,

L_{1}=i(J_{0}+K_{1})=-z^{2}\frac{d}{dz}-2\Delta^{*}z

(50)

III.1.3
Scale Transformation (ST)

Consider the scale transformation given by,

D(\alpha)z=z^{\prime}=e^{\alpha}z\ ,\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(51)

It is easy to check that,

U\big(D(\alpha)\big)=e^{i\alpha K_{2}}=e^{\alpha L_{0}}

(52)

So,

{L_{0}}^{\dagger}=-L_{0}

(53)

So in differential operator form,

L_{0}=iK_{2}=-z\frac{d}{dz}-\Delta^{*}

(54)

III.1.4
Value of the Casimir for
\Delta
-Representation

The Casimir of the Lorentz group is given by,

C=-(J_{0})^{2}+(K_{1})^{2}+(K_{2})^{2}

(55)

Let us now consider a representation with a specific value of
\Delta
. According to our previous calculation, in such a representation,

J_{0}=\frac{i}{2}(z^{2}+1)\frac{d}{dz}+i\Delta^{*}z

(56)

K_{1}=\frac{i}{2}(z^{2}-1)\frac{d}{dz}+i\Delta^{*}z

(57)

K_{2}=iz\frac{d}{dz}+i\Delta^{*}

(58)

Now one can easily check that,

C\Psi(\Delta,z)=\bigg(-(J_{0})^{2}+(K_{1})^{2}+(K_{2})^{2}\bigg)\Psi(\Delta,z)=\Delta\Delta^{*}\ \Psi(\Delta,z)=\big(\lambda^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\big)\Psi(\Delta,z)

(59)

So,

\boxed{C=\Delta^{*}\Delta=\frac{1}{4}+\lambda^{2}}\ ,\ \Delta=\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}

(60)

This is the value of the Casimir obtained in
bargman
.

III.1.5
Comments on The Representation of The Conformal Algebra

The algebra of Lorentz generators in
(2+1)
dimensions can be written as,

\big[L_{0},L_{1}\big]=-L_{1},\ \big[L_{0},L_{-1}\big]=L_{-1},\ \big[L_{1},L_{-1}\big]=2L_{0}

(61)

This is also the algebra of global conformal group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
in one dimension. But as we have seen the reality properties of the generators are different if we think of the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
as acting unitarily on the Hilbert space of massless single particle states of a
(2+1)
dimensional QFT. For example in the standard highest weight representation of the conformal group,

L_{0}^{\dagger}=L_{0},\ L_{1}^{\dagger}=L_{-1},\ L_{-1}^{\dagger}=L_{1}

(62)

whereas in our case unitarity requires that,

L_{0}^{\dagger}=-L_{0},\ L_{1}^{\dagger}=-L_{1},\ L_{-1}^{\dagger}=-L_{-1}

(63)

IV
(3+1) Dimensions

In four dimensions the same argument goes through unchanged except that the states now acquire an extra
helicity
index. For the sake of convenience we will collect the necessary formulas.

In four dimensions we can associate a hermitian matrix with a four momentum
P^{\mu}=(P^{0},P^{1},P^{2},P^{3})
as,

P=\begin{pmatrix}P^{0}-P^{3}&P^{1}+iP^{2}\\
P^{1}-iP^{2}&P^{0}+P^{3}\end{pmatrix},\ \det P=-P^{2}

(64)

In
(3+1)
dimensions
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
is the double cover of the Lorentz group
SO(3,1)
and an
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrix
\Lambda
acts on
P
as,

P\rightarrow P^{\prime}=\Lambda P\Lambda^{\dagger},\ \Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\ ad-bc=1

(65)

In four dimensions the space of null
directions
is a two-sphere (a space-like cross section of the future light-cone in the momentum space). The stereographic coordinate of the two-sphere can be defined as,

z=\frac{P^{1}+iP^{2}}{P^{0}+P^{3}}

(66)

Here the projection is from the south-pole of the sphere so that the north-pole has coordinate
z=0
, which corresponds to the family of null vectors,
\big\{(E,0,0,E)\big|E>0\big\}
, pointing in the positive
3
-direction. One can also check that under Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
,
z
transforms as,

z\rightarrow z^{\prime}=\frac{{P^{\prime}}^{1}+i{P^{\prime}}^{2}}{{P^{\prime}}^{0}+{P^{\prime}}^{3}}=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

(67)

Now if we introduce spherical polar coordinates in momentum space then we can write a null vector
P^{\mu}
as,

P^{\mu}=(E,E\sin\theta\cos\phi,E\sin\theta\sin\phi,E\cos\theta)

(68)

In this prametrization
(\theta,\phi)
become coordinates on the two-sphere. Its relation to the stereographic coordinates
z
is given by,

z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\phi}

(69)

IV.1
Little Group of a Null Momentum Direction

In four dimensions the little group of the standard null direction
\bigg\{(E,0,0,E),E>0\big|z=0\bigg\}
is generated by the elements
\big(J_{3},K_{3},A,B\big)
where
A=J_{2}-K_{1}
and
B=-J_{1}-K_{2}
. The commutators are given by,

[A,B]=0\ ,[J_{3},A]=iB,\ [J_{3},B]=-iA,\ [J_{3},K_{3}]=0,\ [K_{3},A]=iA,\ [K_{3},B]=iB

(70)

IV.2
Construction Of New Basis

Let us define the following state,

\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi^{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\ E^{i\lambda}\ket{E,0,0,E \ ; \sigma},\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}

(71)

where
\sigma
is the helicity. The momentum states have the standard normalisation given by,

\bra{p_1,\sigma_1}\ket{p_2,\sigma_2}=(2\pi)^{3}2|\vec{p}_{1}|\delta^{3}(\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{p}_{2})\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

(72)

The action of the little group on the state
\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z=0, \bar z=0}
is given by,

A\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}=B\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}=0

(73)

U\big(R_{3}(\phi)\big)\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}=e^{i\sigma\phi}\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}

(74)

U\big(B_{3}(\eta)\big)\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0}=e^{\eta\Delta}\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z= 0,\bar z= 0},\ \Delta=1+i\lambda

(75)

where
\phi
is the angle of rotation around the 3-axis and
\eta
is the rapidity of the boost in the 3-direction.

Now let us define the states
\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z,\bar z}
as,

\boxed{\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z , \bar z}=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\ U\big(R(z,\bar{z})\big)\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z=0,\bar z=0}=N(z,\bar{z})\ U\big(R(z,\bar{z})\big)\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z=0,\bar z=0}}

(76)

where
U\big(R(z,\bar{z})\big)
is a unitary rotation operator defined as,

U\big(R(z,\bar{z})\big)=e^{-i\phi J_{3}}\ e^{-i\theta J_{2}}\ e^{i\phi J_{3}},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\phi}

(77)

The rotation operator we have defined takes the standard null direction
\big\{(E,0,0,E)\big|z=0\big\}
to the direction given by
\bigg\{(E,E\sin\theta\cos\phi,E\sin\theta\sin\phi,E\cos\theta)\bigg|z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\phi}\bigg\}
.

Now with our choice of normalization the inner product between the states is given by,

\bra{\lambda_1,\sigma_1,z_1, \bar z_1}\ket{\lambda_2,\sigma_2,z_2,\bar z_2}=\delta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2})\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

(78)

where
\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2})=\delta(\real z_{1}-\real z_{2})\delta(\imaginary z_{1}-\imaginary z_{2})
.

IV.3
Action of The Lorentz Group

Now we want to compute
U(\Lambda)\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z, \bar z}
. The computation is straightforward and we will summarize only the essential elements with obvious notation. Just as before,

\displaystyle U(\Lambda)\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z, \bar z}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(\Lambda)U(R(z,\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z =0, \bar z =0}

(79)

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(\Lambda R(z,\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z =0, \bar z=0}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))U(R^{-1}(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})\Lambda R(z,\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z =0, \bar z=0}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))U(W(\Lambda,z,\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z =0, \bar z=0}

Now the Lorentz transformation
W(\Lambda,z,\bar{z})
belongs to the little group of the standard null direction
\big\{(E,0,0,E)\big|z=0\big\}
and we need to calculate this.

We will evaluate this by multiplying the corresponding
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
matrices. Please see the appendix for our convention.

The matrices are given by,

R(z,\bar{z})=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}&e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
-e^{-i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}&\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+|z|^{2})}}\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\
-\bar{z}&1\end{pmatrix},\ z=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\phi}

(80)

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})

(81)

R^{-1}(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+|\Lambda z|^{2})}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\Lambda z\\
\Lambda\bar{z}&1\end{pmatrix},\

(82)

where,

\Lambda z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\ \Lambda\bar{z}=\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}

(83)

If we multiply these matrices the little group element
W(\Lambda,z,\bar{z})
can be written as,

W(\Lambda,z,\bar{z})=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\beta&1\end{pmatrix}=D(\alpha)S(\beta)

(84)

where

e^{\alpha}=\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1+|\Lambda z|^{2}}\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2}}

(85)

We do not need the expression of
\beta
. So we get,

U(W)=U(D(\alpha))U(S(\beta))

(86)

Now it is easy to check that ,

U(D(\alpha))=e^{\alpha L_{0}+\bar{\alpha}\bar{L}_{0}},\ D(\alpha)z=e^{\alpha}z

(87)

where

L_{0}=\frac{iK_{3}-J_{3}}{2},\ \bar{L}_{0}=-{L_{0}}^{\dagger}

(88)

Similarly,

U(S(\beta))=e^{\beta L_{1}+\bar{\beta}\bar{L}_{1}},\ S(\beta)z=\frac{z}{1-\beta z}

(89)

where,

L_{1}=\frac{iK_{1}-J_{1}}{2}+i\frac{iK_{2}-J_{2}}{2},\ \bar{L}_{1}=-{L_{1}}^{\dagger}

(90)

For later use let us also write down the generator of translation in
z
. It is given by,

U(T(\gamma))=e^{\gamma L_{-1}+\bar{\gamma}\bar{L}_{-1}},\ T(\gamma)z=z+\gamma

(91)

where

L_{-1}=\frac{J_{1}-iK_{1}}{2}-i\frac{J_{2}-iK_{2}}{2},\ \bar{L}_{-1}=-{L_{-1}}^{\dagger}

(92)

Now let us take note of the fact that the generator of the special conformal transformations on
z
can be written as,

L_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(B-iA),\ \bar{L}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}(B+iA)

(93)

where
A
and
B
are the elements of the little group of the null vector
(E,0,0,E)
. These are the generators which are set to zero on the states
\ket{E,0,0,E ; \sigma}
to satisfy the requirement of a finite number of polarization states of a massless particle. Using these facts we get,

\displaystyle U(\Lambda)\ket{\lambda, \sigma, z, \bar z}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))U(W(\Lambda,z,\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda, \sigma, 0,0}

(94)

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))U(D(\alpha))U(S(\beta))\ket{\lambda, \sigma, 0,0}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))U(D(\alpha))\ket{\lambda, \sigma, 0,0}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))(e^{\alpha})^{L_{0}}(e^{\bar{\alpha}})^{\bar{L}_{0}}\ket{\lambda, \sigma, 0,0}

\displaystyle=N(z,\bar{z})U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))\bigg[\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1+|\Lambda z|^{2}}\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2}}\bigg]^{\frac{\Delta-\sigma}{2}}\bigg[\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1+|\Lambda z|^{2}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2}}\bigg]^{\frac{\Delta+\sigma}{2}}\ket{\lambda, \sigma, 0,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\Delta}}\frac{(1+|z|^{2})^{\Delta}}{(1+|\Lambda z|^{2})^{\Delta}}\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}U(R(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z}))\ket{\lambda, \sigma,0,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\ket{\lambda, \sigma, \Lambda z, \Lambda\bar z}

where we have used the definition of the states
\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z,\bar z}
and have defined,

\boxed{h=\frac{\Delta-\sigma}{2}=\frac{1+i\lambda-\sigma}{2},\ \bar{h}=\frac{\Delta+\sigma}{2}=\frac{1+i\lambda+\sigma}{2}}

(95)

Here
\bar{h}
is
not
the complex conjugate of
h
.

Therefore, if we rename the state
\ket{\lambda,\sigma,z,\bar z}
as
\ket{h,\bar h, z, \bar z}
, we can write,

\boxed{U(\Lambda)\ket{h, \bar h, z, \bar z }=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\ket{h,\bar h, \Lambda z, \Lambda\bar z}=\bigg(\frac{d\Lambda z}{dz}\bigg)^{h}\bigg(\frac{d\Lambda\bar{z}}{d\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\bar{h}}\ket{h,\bar h, \Lambda z, \Lambda\bar z},}

(96)

where
\Lambda\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
. Using this transformation law one can easily check that,

\bigg(U(\Lambda)\ket{h',\bar h', z', \bar z'},U(\Lambda)\ket{h,\bar h, z, \bar z}\bigg)=\bigg(\ket{h',\bar h', z', \bar z'},\ket{h,\bar h, z, \bar z}\bigg)

(97)

So the basic kets form a unitary representation of the Lorentz group and transform
like (Lorentz)
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
primary operators of weight
(h,\bar{h})
on the plane
.

IV.4
Reality Condition on The Generators

The Lorentz group acts on the two-sphere as the group of global conformal transformation. In the last section we decomposed the six generators as
\big(L_{-1},L_{0},L_{1}\big)
and
\big(\bar{L}_{-1},\bar{L}_{0},\bar{L}_{1}\big)
following the standard convention of 2-D CFT. Then we saw that the unitarity imposes the reality condition,

{L_{n}}^{\dagger}=-\bar{L}_{n},\ {\bar{L}_{n}}^{\dagger}=-L_{n},\ n=-1,0,1

(98)

The commutator of these generators are given by,

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\ [\bar{L}_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n},\ [L_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=0

(99)

So in this case unitarity imposes a reality condition on the conformal generators which is different from the reality condition in standard highest-weight representation.

V
Action of The Space-time Translation Operators

Let us now discuss the action of the space-time translations on the states
\ket{h,\bar h, z, \bar z}
. This will also give us the physical/geometrical interpretation of these states.

There are four space-time translation operators given by,
P^{\mu}=\big(P^{0}=H,P^{1},P^{2},P^{3}\big)
. Here
H
is the Hamiltonian - the generator of time translation. The generators transform as four-vector under Lorentz transformation, i.e,

U(\Lambda)^{-1}P^{\mu}U(\Lambda)={\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}P^{\nu}

(100)

Let us consider the following family of time dependent (Heisenberg-Picture) states defined as,

\ket{h,\bar h, u, z, \bar z}=e^{iHu}\ket{h, \bar h, z, \bar z}=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{1+i\lambda}\ e^{iHu}\ U(R(z,\bar{z}))\ket{h, \bar h, 0, 0}

(101)

where
u
is a real number. These states are somewhat analogous to the Heisenberg picture states
\ket{t,\vec x}
in the non-relativistic quantum mechanics. But now we also have the "internal degrees of freedom" parametrized by
(h,\bar{h})
or
(\lambda,\sigma)
. The completeness relation can be rewritten as,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\lambda\int d^{2}z\ket{h,\bar h, u, z, \bar z}\bra{h, \bar h, u, z, \bar z}=1

(102)

This is analogous to the completeness relation
\int d^{n}\vec{x}\ket{t,\vec x}\bra{t,\vec x}=1
in non-relativistic quantum mechanics. We can calculate the matrix elements of the unitary translation operators in the basis
\big\{\ket{h, \bar h, z, \bar z}\big\}
using its definition given in terms of the standard Wigner states. But for physical interpretation it will be more useful to have the transformation law of the time dependent states
\{\ket{h,\bar h,u,z,\bar z}\}
under Poincare transformations. In other words we first construct the three parameter family of states and then see how they look like from a different frame of reference. Let us first state the results.

Under a Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
,

\boxed{U(\Lambda)\ket{h, \bar h, u, z, \bar z}=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\ket{h \ , \bar h \ , \frac{u \ (1+ z \bar z)}{|az+b|^2 + |cz+d|^2}, \frac{az+b}{cz+d} \ , \frac{\bar a \bar z + \bar b}{\bar c \bar z + \bar d}}}

(103)

and under space-time translation through a vector
l^{\mu}
, we get

\boxed{e^{-il.P}\ket{h, \bar h, u, z, \bar z}=\ket{h, \bar h, u+ f(z, \bar z, l), z, \bar z}}

(104)

where

V.1.1
A Geometric Argument

Let us now give a geometric argument which makes it clear that why we obtained this result, i.e, why we get an action of the Poincare group on a three dimensional space instead of the bulk four dimensional Minkowski space-time. This is related to the nature of the basis states and the geometry of the (conformally compactified) Minkowski space-time.

So let us consider the state
\ket{h,\bar h, z=0 , \bar z=0}
but now from the point of view of the Poincare group. We know that the little group of a null direction in the momentum space is generated by four elements,
\big\{J_{3},K_{3},A=J_{2}-K_{1},B=-J_{1}-K_{2}\big\}
and as has already been discussed the state
\ket{h,\bar h, z=0 , \bar z=0}
can be associated with the null direction given by,
\big\{(E,0,0,E),E>0\big|z=0,\bar{z}=0\big\}
. Now when space-time translations are included one can check from the definition that the three translations
\big\{e^{ia(H-P^{3})},e^{iaP^{1}},e^{iaP^{2}}\big\}
keep the state
\ket{h,\bar h, z=0 , \bar z=0}

invariant
. So let us adjoin the three generators to the little (algebra) group of a null direction. As a result we get a subgroup of the Poincare group generated by seven elements -
\big\{J_{3},K_{3},A=J_{2}-K_{1},B=-J_{1}-K_{2},H-P^{3},P^{1},P^{2}\big\}
. The seven generators are closed under commutation. Let us denote the subgroup generated by them by
NH
.

The geometric significance of
NH
is that it is isomorphic to the little group of a null-hyperplane in Minkowski space
. It is easy to check this.

For example let us consider the null-hyperplane passing through the origin of the Minkowski space. The equation of this null-hyperplane is,

X.P=0,\ P^{2}=0

(110)

Here
P
is a representative from the equivalence class
\big\{P\sim\alpha P\big|\alpha>0\big\}
specifying the direction of the null normal. The null geodesic generators of the null hyperplane are also parallel to
P
.

Now let us consider a Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
, in the active sense, which maps the point
X
lying on the null hyperplane to
\Lambda X
. Now if
\Lambda X
also belongs to the null hyperplane then,
(\Lambda X).P=0=X.(\Lambda^{-1}P)
. Therefore we must have,

\Lambda^{-1}P\sim P\rightarrow\Lambda^{-1}P=\alpha\ P

(111)

for some
\alpha>0
. If we impose
\alpha=1
and choose
P
to be
(1,0,0,1)
then we get the standard little group of massless particles generated by
\big\{J_{3},A,B\big\}
. But since any other positive value of
\alpha
is allowed, boost in the 3-direction is also an element of the little group. In this way we get four Lorentz generators,
\big\{J_{3},K_{3},A,B\big\}
as elements of the little group of a null-hypersurface.

Now for translations given by,
X\rightarrow X^{\prime}=X+a
, the condition that
X^{\prime}
belongs to the null-hyperplane gives

a.P=0

(112)

This has three solutions corresponding to translations in the direction of
P
itself and in the two space-like directions orthogonal to
P
. These are generated by
\big\{H-P^{3},P^{1},P^{2}\big\}
if we take
P
to be
(1,0,0,1)
.

So altogether we get seven generators
\big\{J_{3},K_{3},A,B,H-P^{3},P^{1},P^{2}\big\}
which
map the null-hyperplane to itself
and we can associate the state
\ket{h,\bar h, z=0 , \bar z=0}
with the null-hypersurface
X.P=0
with
P\sim(1,0,0,1)
.

The remaining three generators of the Poincare group act non-trivially on the space of null-hyperplanes. Now a null-hyperplane can be thought of as the past light-cone of a point of the future null-infinity (or the future light-cone of a point of the past null-infinity)
1
1

1

See for example
Penrose:1967wn
.
. So the space of null-hyperplanes in Minkowski space can either be thought of as the future null-infinity or the past null-infinity. The number of parameters also match. There are three-parameter family of null hyperplanes corresponding to the three Poincare generators which act non-trivially on the space of null hyperplanes and the null infinity is also three dimensional. This shows that we can think of the massless particle, described by a quantum state associated with a null-hyperplane,
as sitting at one point at null infinity
.

Let us now analyse the state
\ket{h,\bar h, u, z, \bar z}=e^{iHu}U(R(z,\bar{z}))\ket{h,\bar h,z=0,\bar z=0}
. As we have already described, we can associate the state
\ket{h,\bar h,z=0,\bar z=0}
with the null-hyperplane
X.P=0
with
P\sim(1,0,0,1)
which passes through the origin. Now

rotates the state and the rotated state corresponds to the null-hyperplane
X.P^{\prime}=0
where
P^{\prime}=R(z,\bar{z})P
. In this way we generate the 2-parameter family of null-hyperplanes all of which pass through the origin. They represent the family of states
\big\{\ket{h,\bar h,z,\bar z}\big\}
with varying
(z,\bar{z})
. Now the rest of the null hyperplanes are generated by time translating this two parameter family of null hyperplanes. This is essentially the action of
e^{iHu}
on the states
\ket{h,\bar h,z,\bar z}
. This explains that why the states
\ket{h,\bar h, u, z,\bar z}
transform under the asymptotic Poinacre group.

In passing we would like to point out that the generators
\big\{J_{3},K_{3},A=J_{2}-K_{1},B=-J_{1}-K_{2},H-P^{3},P^{1},P^{2}\big\}
of the Poincare group are also the
kinematic generators in the Light-front quantization

\ket{h,\bar h,u,z,\bar z}
by some non-local transformation.

Creation And Annihilation Fields At Null-Infinity

In this section we change our notation a little bit and define,

\ket{\lambda,\sigma, z, \bar z}=\ket{h,\bar h, z, \bar z},\ h=\frac{1+i\lambda-\sigma}{2},\ \bar{h}=\frac{1+i\lambda+\sigma}{2}

Now the question is how are these creation/annihilation operators related to the standard creation/annihilation operators in the momentum eigenstate basis ? The simplest anwer is given by,

(122)

in second-quantized notation.

The (anti) commutator between the creation and annihilation operators is given by,

\displaystyle{[A_{\lambda,\sigma}(u,z,\bar{z}),A^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})]_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\bra{\lambda,\sigma, u, z, \bar z}\ket{\lambda',\sigma', u', z', \bar z'}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\big(i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)}{(1+z\bar{z})^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}\frac{\delta^{2}(z^{\prime}-z)}{\big(-i(u^{\prime}-u+i0+)\big)^{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}}

Eq-(
\ref{amc1},\ref{amc2}

VIII.0.1

We denote the 4-point function by

The fact that we get a hint of supertranslation in a
purely non-gravitational setting
is perhaps an indication of
holography in asymptotically flat space-time
. This is somewhat similar to the situation in AdS
3
-CFT
2
correspondence. The infinite dimensional Virasoro symmetry of a two-dimensional conformal field theory can be understood in a purely field theory setting without dynamical gravity. But in asymptotically AdS
3
spaces Virasoro can also be understood as the asymptotic symmetry group of the bulk gravity theory. These two facts nicely fit together in AdS
3
-holography. Similarly the goal here is to understand
BMS in a purely non-gravitational setting
. If such an understanding can be reached in a consistent manner then that will perhaps be an indication of flat-space holography.

Conformal Primary Wave-Functions With Translational Invariance

For simplicity we consider the massless scalar conformal primary wave functions
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Cheung:2016iub
;
deBoer:2003vf
. Scalar conformal primary wave functions in (3+1) dimensions are given by,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|z,\bar{z})=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\Delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}dEE^{\Delta-1}e^{\pm iEQ\cdot X}e^{-\epsilon E}=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\Delta}\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(-Q\cdot X\mp i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}\ ,\ \Delta=1+i\lambda

(145)

where
X^{\mu}
is an arbitrary point in the Minkowski space and
Q^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})
is a standard null vector given by

Q^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})=\bigg(1,\frac{z+\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{-i(z-\bar{z})}{1+z\bar{z}},\frac{1-z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)

(146)

which under a
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation transforms as ,

Q^{\mu}(\Lambda z,\Lambda\bar{z})=\frac{1+z\bar{z}}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}}\ {\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}Q^{\nu}(z,\bar{z}),\ \Lambda z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\ \Lambda\bar{z}=\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}

(147)

where
{\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}
is the corresponding Lorentz transformation matrix. It was shown in
Pasterski:2017kqt
that the wave-functions given in Eq-
145
form a complete set of delta function normalizable solutions of the massless Klein-Gordan equation if
\Delta=1+i\lambda
.

Under Lorentz transformation the scalar conformal primary wave functions transform as,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}\bigg({\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}X^{\nu}\bigg|\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)=(cz+d)^{\Delta}(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{\Delta}\ \Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|z,\bar{z})

(148)

To see the action of the space-time translation in a simple manner let us introduce an extra parameter
u
and new wave functions labelled by
\Delta(=1+i\lambda)
and three parameters
(u,z,\bar{z})
. The new wave functions are given by,

\boxed{\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\Delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}dEE^{\Delta-1}e^{\pm iE(Q\cdot X+u)}e^{-\epsilon E}=\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z\bar{z}}\bigg)^{\Delta}\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(-Q\cdot X-u\mp i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}}\

(149)

This still solves the massless Kelin-Gordon equation and for a
fixed value of
u
the family of wave functions parametrized by
(\Delta,u,z,\bar{z})
form a complete set just like the wave functions given in Eq-
145
. Now one can easily check that under Lorentz transformation,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}\bigg({\Lambda^{\mu}}_{\nu}X^{\nu}\bigg|\frac{u(1+z\bar{z})}{|az+b|^{2}+|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\bigg)=(cz+d)^{\Delta}(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{\Delta}\ \Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})

(150)

and under spacetime translation by four vector
l^{\mu}
,

\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}+l^{\mu}\big|u+f(z,\bar{z},l),z,\bar{z})=\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})

(151)

where

f(z,\bar{z},l)=\frac{(l^{0}-l^{3})-(l^{1}-il^{2})z-(l^{1}+il^{2})\bar{z}+(l^{0}+l^{3})z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}

(152)

So the wave functions
\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})
transform in a simple manner under the Poincare group and it is natural to identify the parameters
(u,z,\bar{z})
as the Bondi coordinates of the null infinity in Minkowski space.

The above procedure has a simple geometric interpretation. As we have described in section-(
V.1.1
) the states
\ket{h,\bar h, u,z, \bar z}
are associated with null-hyperplanes in the Minkowski space. The original 2-parameter family of conformal primary wave functions, given by
\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|z,\bar{z})
(Eq-
145
), are singular along the null hyperplanes
X\cdot Q(z,\bar{z})=0
passing through the origin. They correspond to the states
\ket{h,\bar h,u=0,z,\bar z}
. So to get the rest of the states with nonzero
u
we have translate the family
X\cdot Q(z,\bar{z})=0
along the time axis. This gives rise to the wave functions
\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})
(Eq-
149
) singular along the null-hyperplanes
X\cdot Q(z,\bar{z})+u=0
. This 3-parameter family exhausts all the null-hyperplanes in the Minkowski space and correspond to the family of states
\ket{h,\bar h,u,z,\bar z}
located on null-infinity. This makes it clear that why the Poincare group has simple action on the wave-functions
\Phi_{\Delta}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u,z,\bar{z})
.

Now suppose instead of plane-waves we use the wave functions
\big\{\Phi_{\Delta_{i}}^{\pm}(X^{\mu}|u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\big\}
as the wave functions of the external particles then we can write down a modified Mellin-transform as,

\boxed{\tilde{A}(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+z_{i}\bar{z}_{i}}\bigg)^{\Delta_{i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dE_{i}\ E_{i}^{\Delta_{i}-1}e^{-i\epsilon_{i}E_{i}u_{i}}A(\{E_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\})}

(153)

where
h_{i}=\bar{h}_{i}=\frac{\Delta_{i}}{2}
and we have also defined
\epsilon_{i}=+1
for an outgoing particle and
\epsilon_{i}=-1
for an incoming particle. This is a slightly modified form of the Mellin amplitude defined in
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017ylz
.

\tilde{A}
amplitude behaves like
correlation function of
ISL(2,\mathbb{C})
primaries inserted at points at null infinity
and transform under the
Asymptotic Poincare Group
as,

\tilde{A}\big(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},\Lambda u_{i},\Lambda z_{i},\Lambda\bar{z}_{i}\}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(cz_{i}+d)^{2h_{i}}(\bar{c}\bar{z}_{i}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}_{i}}\ \tilde{A}\big(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\}\big)

(154)

\tilde{A}\big(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},u_{i}+f(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},l),z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\}\big)=\tilde{A}\big(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\}\big)

(155)

where

{}\Lambda u_{i}=\frac{(1+z_{i}\bar{z}_{i})u_{i}}{|az_{i}+b|^{2}+|cz_{i}+d|^{2}}

and
f(z,\bar{z},l)
is given by Eq-
152
.

It will be interesting to compute the
\tilde{A}
amplitude for some field theories along the lines of
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Cardona:2017keg
;
Banerjee:2017jeg
;
Schreiber:2017jsr
.

The
\tilde{A}
amplitudes can be thought of as the "scattering amplitude with asymptotic states" given by
\ket{h_i,\bar h_i,u_i,z_i,\bar z_i}
. It is very likely that a relation of the type,

\tilde{A}(\{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i},u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\})\sim\bra{\Omega}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{(h_{i},\bar{h}_{i})}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\ket{\Omega}

(156)

exists where
\phi_{(h_{i},\bar{h}_{i})}
are
ISL(2,\mathbb{C})
primaries at null-infinity and the correlator in the R.H.S is
computed in a theory defined at null-infinity
. The correlation function should perhaps be
u-ordered
because, as we have seen, the coordinate
u
has time-like character. It will be interesting to make these things more precise.

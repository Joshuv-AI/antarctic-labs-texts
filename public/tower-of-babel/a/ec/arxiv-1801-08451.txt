Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to provide a construction to model shared-variable systems using higher-dimensional automata which is compositional in the sense that the parallel composition of completely independent systems is modeled by the standard tensor product of HDAs and nondeterministic choice is represented by the coproduct.

1.
Introduction

Higher-dimensional automata provide a rich model for concurrent systems. A higher-dimensional automaton (HDA) is a precubical set with an initial state, a set of final states, and a labeling on 1-cubes such that opposite edges of 2-cubes have the same label. An HDA is thus a transition system (or an ordinary automaton) with incorporated squares and cubes of higher dimensions. An
n
-cube in an HDA indicates that the
n
actions starting at its origin are independent in the sense that they may be executed in any order, or even simultaneously, without any observable difference. The concept of higher-dimensional automaton goes back to Pratt
[
Pra91
]
. The notion defined here is due to van Glabbeek
[
Gla06
]
.

A natural approach to constructing an HDA from a transition system, which has been described in various places in the literature (see e.g.
[
Gau08
,
Gau10
,
Gla06
,
Gou02
,
GM12
]
), is to fill in empty squares and higher-dimensional cubes. In order to realize this strategy concretely, one needs to specify not only which cubes to fill in but also how to fill in a cube. To make this more precise, suppose that we consider the actions
a
and
b

Since there is no canonical way to attach a 2-cube to an empty square, most methods to construct HDAs from transition systems are based on the symmetric approach to fill in squares always in both possible ways, and it has been shown that this is well suited to many purposes
[
Gau10
,
Gla06
,
Gou02
,
GM12
]
. A drawback of this symmetric way to proceed is, however, that it leads to a certain redundancy in the representation of the independence relation: the independence of
n
actions is represented by not just one but a significantly larger number of
n
-cubes. Moreover, the symmetric construction does not explain very well the intuition that the usual tensor product of HDAs models the parallel composition of completely independent concurrent systems.

In this paper we focus on transition systems and HDAs modeling finite systems of asynchronously executing processes which communicate through shared variables. Given a transition system representing the state space of such a concurrent system, a canonical choice between the two possibilities to fill in an empty independence square is possible if process IDs are taken into account: we may attach a 2-cube always in such a way that the process behind the action of the first front edge has a higher process ID than the one associated with the second front edge. Based on this attaching rule for 2-cubes, one can set up a filling procedure to construct HDAs from transition system models of shared-variable programs. In an HDA constructed using this method the independence of
n
actions at a state is represented by a unique
n\mbox{-}
cube (at least if the system under consideration consists of deterministic processes). Thus the independence relation is described in a nonredundant fashion. Moreover, the construction of the HDA is compositional in the sense that the tensor product of HDAs models independent parallel composition and the coproduct represents nondeterministic choice. The main objective of this paper is to establish these compositionality properties of the construction.

Process IDs are a means to order pairs of independent actions. In other words, they turn independence relations asymmetric. It turns out that instead of using process IDs, we may more abstractly work with asymmetric or even plain relations. To do so, we introduce
\ltimes
-transition systems, which are transition systems with a relation on labels that does not need to have any properties. The state space of a shared-variable system can be modeled as a
\ltimes
-transition system with an asymmetric relation, and we shall provide more details for systems of program graphs
[
BK08
,
MP92
]
later on in the paper.

The fundamental material on
\ltimes
-transition systems is presented in Section
3
. The earlier Section
2
contains preliminaries on precubical sets and higher-dimensional automata. For every
\ltimes
-transition system, we construct an HDA model by filling in empty cubes. The construction is functorial and has a left adjoint. HDA models are the subject of Section
4
. In Section
5
we define an interleaving operator for
\ltimes
-transition systems and show that it models the parallel composition of shared-variable systems without common variables. Our main result is Theorem
20
, which states that the HDA model of the interleaving of two
\ltimes
-transition systems is the tensor product of their HDA models. In Section
6
we turn our attention to nondetermistic choice and show that our HDA model construction preserves coproducts. The final section contains a few concluding remarks.

2.
Precubical sets and higher-dimensional automata

This section presents some well-known material about precubical sets and higher-dimensional automata.

2.1.
Precubical sets

A
precubical set
is a graded set
P=(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
boundary
or
face operators

d^{k}_{i}\colon P_{n}\to P_{n-1}

(n>0,\;k=0,1,\;i=1,\dots,n)
satisfying the relations
d^{k}_{i}\circ d^{l}_{j}=d^{l}_{j-1}\circ d^{k}_{i}

(k,l=0,1,\;i<j)
. If
x\in P_{n}
, we say that
x
is of
degree

n
and write
\deg(x)=n
. The elements of degree
n
are called the
n
-cubes
of
P
. The elements of degree
0
are also called the
vertices
of
P
, and the
1
-cubes are also called the
edges
of
P
. A
precubical subset
of a precubical set
P
is a graded subset of
P
that is stable under the boundary operators. A
morphism
of precubical sets is a morphism of graded sets that is compatible with the boundary operators. The category of precubical sets will be denoted by
{{\mathsf{PCS}}}
.

The category of precubical sets can be seen as the presheaf category of functors
\Box^{\mbox{\tiny op}}\to{\mathsf{Set}}
where
\Box
is the small subcategory of the category of topological spaces whose objects are the standard
n
-cubes
[0,1]^{n}

(n\geq 0)
and whose nonidentity morphisms are composites of the maps
\delta^{k}_{i}\colon[0,1]^{n}\to[0,1]^{n+1}
(
k\in\{0,1\}
,
n\geq 0
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
) given by
\delta_{i}^{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},k,u_{i}\dots,u_{n})
.

2.2.
Tensor product of precubical sets

Given two precubical sets
P
and
Q
, the
tensor product

P\otimes Q
is the precubical set defined as follows: The underlying graded set is given by

(P\otimes Q)_{n}=\coprod\limits_{p+q=n}P_{p}\times Q_{q}.

For an
n
-cube
(x,y)\in P_{p}\times Q_{q}\subseteq(P\otimes Q)_{n}
, the boundary operators are defined by

d_{i}^{k}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(d_{i}^{k}x,y),&1\leq i\leq p,\\
(x,d_{i-p}^{k}y),&p<i\leq n.\end{array}\right.

With respect to this tensor product, the category
{{\mathsf{PCS}}}
is a monoidal category.

2.3.
Precubical intervals

Let
k
and
l
be two integers such that
k\leq l
. The
precubical interval

\llbracket k,l\rrbracket
is the precubical set defined by
\llbracket k,l\rrbracket_{0}=\{k,\dots,l\}
,
\llbracket k,l\rrbracket_{1}=\{{[k,k+1]},\dots,{[l-1,l]}\}
,
d_{1}^{0}[j-1,j]=j-1
,
d_{1}^{1}[j-1,j]=j
, and
\llbracket k,l\rrbracket_{n}=\emptyset
for
n>1
.

2.4.
Precubical cubes

The
precubical
n\mbox{-}
cube
is the
n
-fold tensor product
{\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes n}}
. Here, we use the convention that
{\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes 0}}
is the precubical set
\llbracket 0,0\rrbracket=\{0\}
. The only element of degree
n
in
\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes n}
will be denoted by
\iota_{n}
. We thus have
\iota_{0}=0
and
\iota_{n}=(\underbracket{[0,1],\dots,[0,1]}_{n\;{\text{times}}})
for
n>0
. Given an element
x
of degree
n
of a precubical set
P
, there exists a unique morphism of precubical sets
x_{\sharp}\colon\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes n}\to P
that sends
\iota_{n}
to
x
. Indeed,
by the Yoneda lemma, there exist unique morphisms of precubical sets
f\colon\Box(-,[0,1]^{n})\to P
and
g\colon\Box(-,[0,1]^{n})\to\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes n}
such that
f(id_{[0,1]^{n}})=x
and
g(id_{[0,1]^{n}})=\iota_{n}
. The map
g
is an isomorphism, and
x_{\sharp}=f\circ g^{-1}
.

2.5.
Truncated precubical sets

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. An
n
-truncated
precubical set is a precubical set
P
such that
P_{m}=\emptyset
for all
m>n
. The full subcategory of
{{\mathsf{PCS}}}
given by the
n
-truncated precubical sets is denoted by
{{\mathsf{PCS}}_{n}}
. The
n\mbox{-}
skeleton
of a precubical set
P
is the
n
-truncated precubical set
P_{\leq n}
defined by
(P_{\leq n})_{m}=P_{m}

(m\leq n)
. The association
P\mapsto P_{\leq n}
defines the

n
-truncation functor

T_{n}\colon{{\mathsf{PCS}}}\to{{\mathsf{PCS}}_{n}}
. The functor
T_{n}
has both a left and a right adjoint (cf. e.g.
[
Rie16
, 6.2.6]
). The left adjoint is the inclusion
{{\mathsf{PCS}}_{n}}\hookrightarrow{{\mathsf{PCS}}}
. The right adjoint, which we will denote by
\varGamma_{n}
, sends an
n
-truncated precubical set
P
to its
n
-coskeleton
, i.e., the unique (up to isomorphism) maximal precubical set such that the
n
-skeleton is
P
and no two different cubes of degree
>n
have the same boundary. We remark that natural bijections
(\varGamma_{n}P)_{m}\to\mathsf{PCS}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes m}_{\leq n},P)
are given by
x\mapsto(x_{\sharp})_{\leq n}
.

2.6.
Paths

A
path
of
length
l

(l\geq 0)
from a vertex
v
of a precubical set
P
to a vertex
w
is a morphism of precubical sets
\omega\colon\llbracket 0,l\rrbracket\to P
such that
\omega(0)=v
and
\omega(l)=w
. The set of paths in
P
is denoted by
P^{\mathbb{I}}
. The
concatenation

\omega\cdot\nu
of two paths
\omega\colon\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket\to P
and
\nu\colon\llbracket 0,l\rrbracket\to P
with
\omega(k)=\nu(0)
is defined in the obvious way. Note that every path in
P
can be written as a finite concatenation of paths of the form
x_{\sharp}
where
x\in P_{\leq 1}
.

Given two precubical sets
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
, there are unique maps
\pi^{i}\colon(P^{1}\otimes P^{2})^{\mathbb{I}}\to(P^{i})^{\mathbb{I}}

(i\in\{1,2\})
that are compatible with concatenation and satisfy
\pi^{i}((x^{1},x^{2})_{\sharp})=x^{i}_{\sharp}
for all
(x^{1},x^{2})\in(P^{1}\otimes P^{2})_{\leq 1}
. If
\omega\in(P^{1}\otimes P^{2})^{\mathbb{I}}
is a path from
(v^{1},v^{2})
to
(w^{1},w^{2})
, then
\pi^{i}(\omega)
is a path in
P^{i}
from
v^{i}
to
w^{i}
. The map
(\pi^{1},\pi^{2})\colon(P^{1}\otimes P^{2})^{\mathbb{I}}\to(P^{1})^{\mathbb{I}}\times(P^{2})^{\mathbb{I}}
is not bijective in general but has a right inverse. If for
i\in\{1,2\}
,
\omega^{i}
is a path in
P^{i}
from
v^{i}
to
w^{i}
, then this right inverse sends the pair
(\omega^{1},\omega^{2})
to a path from
(v^{1},v^{2})
to
(w^{1},w^{2})
.

2.7.
Higher-dimensional automata

A
higher-dimensional automaton
(HDA) is a tuple

{\mathcal{A}}=(P_{{\mathcal{A}}},I_{{\mathcal{A}}},F_{{\mathcal{A}}},\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}},\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}})

where
P_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is a precubical set,
{I_{{\mathcal{A}}}\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{0}}
is a vertex, called the
initial state
,
{F_{{\mathcal{A}}}\subseteq(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{0}}
is a possibly empty set of
final
or
accepting states
,
\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is a set of
labels
, and
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}\colon(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{1}\to\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is a map, called the
labeling function
, such that
\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}(d_{i}^{0}x)=\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}(d_{i}^{1}x)
for all
x\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{2}
and
i\in\{1,2\}

[
Gla06
]
. A
morphism
from an HDA
{\mathcal{A}}
to an HDA
{\mathcal{B}}
consists of a morphism of precubical sets
f\colon P_{\mathcal{A}}\to P_{\mathcal{B}}
and a map
\sigma\colon\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}\to\Sigma_{{\mathcal{B}}}
such that
f(I_{{\mathcal{A}}})=I_{{\mathcal{B}}}
,
f(F_{{\mathcal{A}}})\subseteq F_{{\mathcal{B}}}
, and
\lambda_{{\mathcal{B}}}(f(x))=\sigma(\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}(x))
for all
x\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{1}
. The category of HDAs is denoted by
\sf HDA
.

Given two HDAs
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
, we say that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
subautomaton
of
{\mathcal{A}}
and write
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
if
P_{\mathcal{B}}
is a precubical subset of
P_{\mathcal{A}}
,
\Sigma_{B}\subseteq\Sigma_{A}
,
I_{{\mathcal{B}}}=I_{{\mathcal{A}}}
,
F_{{\mathcal{B}}}\subseteq F_{{\mathcal{A}}}
, and
\lambda_{{\mathcal{B}}}(x)=\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}(x)
for all
x\in{(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{1}}
.

An
n
-truncated
HDA is an HDA
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is an
n
-truncated precubical set. The
n
-skeleton
of an HDA
{\mathcal{A}}
is the
n
-truncated HDA
{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}
defined by
P_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}}=(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{\leq n}
,
I_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}}=I_{{\mathcal{A}}}
,
F_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}}=F_{{\mathcal{A}}}
,
\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}}=\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}
, and
\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}}=\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}|_{(P_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq n}})_{1}}
.

3.
\ltimes
-Transition systems

We introduce
\ltimes
-transition systems, which are transition systems with a relation on the set of labels. We make precise how shared-variable systems can be modeled by
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition systems and relate
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition systems to 2-truncated higher-dimensional automata.

3.1.
Deterministic and extensional HDAs

An HDA is said to be
deterministic
if its
1
-skeleton is deterministic in the sense of automata theory, i.e., if any two edges with the same label and the same initial vertex are equal. The following weaker condition on HDAs is essential in the sequel (see Section
5.4
): we say that an HDA is
extensional
if any two edges with the same label and the same initial and final vertices are equal. The full subcategory of
\mathsf{HDA}
given by the extensional HDAs will be denoted by
\mathsf{HDA_{ext}}
.

3.2.
Transition systems and
\ltimes
-transition systems

A
transition system
is a
1\mbox{-}
truncated extensional HDA. A
\ltimes
-transition system
is a tuple

{\mathcal{T}}=(P_{{\mathcal{T}}},I_{{\mathcal{T}}},F_{{\mathcal{T}}},\Sigma_{{\mathcal{T}}},\lambda_{{\mathcal{T}}},\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}})

where
U({\mathcal{T}})=(P_{{\mathcal{T}}},I_{{\mathcal{T}}},F_{{\mathcal{T}}},\Sigma_{{\mathcal{T}}},\lambda_{{\mathcal{T}}})
is a transition system and
\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}
is a relation on
\Sigma_{T}
. The transition system
U({\mathcal{T}})
is called the
underlying transition system
. A
morphism
from a
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition system
{\mathcal{S}}
to a
\ltimes
-transition system
{\mathcal{T}}
is a morphism of HDAs
(f,\sigma)\colon U({\mathcal{S}})\to U({\mathcal{T}})
such that
\sigma
is compatible with the relations
\ltimes_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}
. The category of
\ltimes
-transition systems will be denoted by
\mathsf{TS_{\ltimes}}
. A
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition system is called
deterministic
if the underlying transition system is a deterministic HDA.

3.3.
Asynchronous transition systems

One may view
\ltimes
-transition systems as generalized asynchronous transition systems in the sense of
[
WN95
]
(see also
[
Bed87
,
Shi85
]
). These are transition systems with an irreflexive and symmetric relation that has to satisfy certain conditions and represents independence of actions.

3.4.
Shared-variable systems given by program graphs

Let
Var
be a set of
variables
. The
domain
of a variable
x
, i.e., the set of its possible values, will be denoted by
D_{x}
. A
program graph
over
Var
is a tuple

(V,L,A,T,g,\imath)

the components of which are described as follows (cf.
[
BK08
,
MP92
]
):

•

V\subseteq Var
is a set of
declared variables
.

•

L
is a set of control-flow
locations
.

•

A
is a set of
actions
. An action is a function
Ev(V)\to Ev(V)
where
Ev(W)

(W\subseteq Var)
is the set of
evaluations
of the variables in
W
, i.e.,

\quad\quad\quad Ev(W)=\{\eta\colon W\to\cup_{x\in Var}D_{x}\,|\,\forall\,x\in W:\eta(x)\in D_{x}\}\cong\prod\limits_{x\in W}D_{x}.

•

T\subseteq L\times A\times L
is a set of
transitions
. A transition is thus a triple

t=(s_{t},a_{t},e_{t})

consisting of a start location
s_{t}
, an action
a_{t}
, and an end location
e_{t}
.

•

g\colon T\to{\mathfrak{P}}(Ev(V))
is a function that associates with every transition a
guard condition
on the values of the variables, which is represented by the subset of
Ev(V)
containing the evaluations for which the condition is true and therefore the transition is enabled.

•

\imath\in L
is the
initial location
of the program graph.

We say that a program graph is
deterministic
if any two transitions with the same start location and the same action are equal. A
shared-variable system
over
Var
is a tuple
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
of program graphs over
Var
.

3.5.
The
\ltimes
-transition system model of a shared-variable system

Consider the shared-variable system
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
over
Var
given by the program graphs

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}=(V_{i},L_{i},A_{i},T_{i},g_{i},\imath_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

and fix an
initial evaluation

\eta_{0}\in Ev(Var)
.
Define the set of
declared variables
of the system by

V=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
. Note that
V
will normally consist of shared variables and not be a disjoint union.
The
states
of the system
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
are the elements of the set

Q_{0}=L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{n}\times Ev(V).

The
initial state
of the system is the state

I=(\imath_{1},\dots,\imath_{n},\eta_{0}|_{V})
. The set
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices of a 1-truncated precubical set
Q
, which we call the
state graph
of
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
. The set of edges of
Q
is given by

Q_{1}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\\
t\in T_{i}\end{subarray}}L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{i-1}\times\{t\}\times L_{i+1}\times\cdots\times L_{n}\times g_{i}({t})\times Ev(V\setminus V_{i}).

Before we define the boundary operators of
Q
, let us note that for any two disjoint subsets
W,W^{\prime}\subseteq V
, we have a bijection
Ev(W\cup W^{\prime})\to Ev(W)\times Ev(W^{\prime})
,
\eta\mapsto(\eta|_{W_{1}},\eta|_{W_{2}})
. We denote the inverse bijection by
\ast
. The starting point of an edge

x=(l_{1},\dots,l_{i-1},t,l_{i+1},\dots,l_{n},\gamma,\eta)

is the state

d^{0}_{1}x=(l_{1},\dots,l_{i-1},s_{t},l_{i+1},\dots,l_{n},\gamma\ast\eta),

and its endpoint is the state

d^{1}_{1}x=(l_{1},\dots,l_{i-1},e_{t},l_{i+1},\dots,l_{n},a_{t}(\gamma)\ast\eta).

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be the
\ltimes
-transition system defined as follows:

•

P_{\mathcal{T}}
is the largest precubical subset of
Q
the vertices of which are the
reachable
states, i.e., the states to which there exists a path from
I
.

•

I_{\mathcal{T}}=I
.

•

F_{\mathcal{T}}=\emptyset
.

•

\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{i\}\times A_{i}
.

•

Given an edge
x=(l_{1},\dots,l_{i-1},t,l_{i+1},\dots,l_{n},\gamma,\eta)\in(P_{\mathcal{T}})_{1}
, we say that
i
is the
process ID
of
x
and set
\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=(i,a_{t})
.

•

For labels
(i,a),(j,b)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
, we set
(i,a)\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}(j,b)
if
i<j
.

The
\ltimes
-transition system
{\mathcal{T}}
is the
\ltimes
-transition system model
of
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
with respect to the initial evaluation
\eta_{0}

3.6.
Independence squares

An

\ltimes
-transition system

is a family of edges

(1)

4.
HDA models of
\ltimes
-transition systems

In this section we introduce HDA models of
\ltimes
-transition systems. We show that the HDA model of an
\ltimes
-transition system exists functorially and is unique up to isomorphism. We also establish that in the HDA model of a deterministic
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition system with an asymmetric relation the independence of
n
transitions is modeled by a unique
n
-cube.

4.1.
HDA models

We say that an HDA
{\mathcal{A}}
is an
HDA model
of a
\ltimes
-transition system
{\mathcal{T}}
if the following conditions hold:

HM1:

{\mathcal{A}}_{\leq 1}=U({\mathcal{T}})
.

HM2:

For all
x\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{2}
,
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}(d^{0}_{2}x)\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}(d^{0}_{1}x)
.

HM3:

For all
m\geq 2
and
x,y\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}
, if
d^{k}_{r}x=d^{k}_{r}y
for all
r\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
, then
x=y
.

HM4:

{\mathcal{A}}
is maximal with respect to the properties HM1-HM3, i.e.,
{\mathcal{A}}
is not a proper subautomaton of any HDA satisfying HM1-HM3.

Condition HM1 says that
{\mathcal{A}}
is built on top of
{\mathcal{T}}
by filling in empty cubes. By condition HM2, an empty square may only be filled in if it is an independence square. Condition HM3 ensures that no empty cube is filled in twice in the same way. By condition HM4, all admissible empty cubes are filled in.

Condition HM3 has the following equivalent form, which says that a cube is determined by the family of its vertices and edges (like in a 1-coskeleton):

Proposition 3
.

An HDA
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies condition HM3 if and only if for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map

(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}\to\mathsf{PCS}(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes m}_{\leq 1},(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{\leq 1}),\quad x\mapsto(x_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}

is injective.

Proof 4.1
.

Suppose first that
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies HM3. We show by induction that the map of the statement is injective for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
m\leq 1
, it is actually bijective. Suppose that
m\geq 2
and that the assertion holds for
m-1
. Let
x,y\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}
be elements such that
(x_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}=(y_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}
. Let
r\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
. We have
(d^{k}_{r}x)_{\sharp}(\iota_{m-1})=d^{k}_{r}x=d^{k}_{r}x_{\sharp}(\iota_{m})=x_{\sharp}(d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})=x_{\sharp}((d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}(\iota_{m-1}))={x_{\sharp}\circ(d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}(\iota_{m-1})}
and therefore
(d^{k}_{r}x)_{\sharp}=x_{\sharp}\circ(d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}
. For the same reason,
(d^{k}_{r}y)_{\sharp}={y_{\sharp}\circ(d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}}
. For
w\in\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes{m-1}}_{\leq 1}
,
(d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}(w)\in\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket^{\otimes{m}}_{\leq 1}
and hence

(d^{k}_{r}x)_{\sharp}(w)=x_{\sharp}((d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}(w))=y_{\sharp}((d^{k}_{r}\iota_{m})_{\sharp}(w))=(d^{k}_{r}y)_{\sharp}(w).

Thus
((d^{k}_{r}x)_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}=((d^{k}_{r}y)_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}
. By the inductive hypothesis, it follows that
d^{k}_{r}x=d^{k}_{r}y
. Hence, by HM3,
x=y
.

Suppose now that the map of the statement is injective for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
m\geq 2
, and consider elements
x,y\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}
such that
d^{k}_{r}x=d^{k}_{r}y
for all
r\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
. Then all iterated boundaries of
x
and
y
coincide. Therefore
x_{\sharp}
and
y_{\sharp}
agree on all iterated boundaries of
\iota_{m}
. In particular,
(x_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}=(y_{\sharp})_{\leq 1}
. Hence
x=y
.

\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{0}_{2})=\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(f(d^{0}_{2}x))=\sigma(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(d^{0}_{2}x))\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(d^{0}_{1}x))=\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(f(d^{0}_{1}x))=\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{0}_{1}).

By Proposition

such that

for all

and

f
extends uniquely to degree
m

Corollary 7
.

Given two HDA models
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
, there exists a unique morphism of HDAs

that restricts to the identity on

, and this morphism is an isomorphism.

5.
Parallel composition

The parallel composition of completely independent shared-variable systems can be modeled using the interleaving operator for
\ltimes
-transition systems, which we introduce in this section. We show that an HDA model of the interleaving of two
\ltimes
-transition systems is given by the tensor product of their HDA models.

5.1.
Tensor product of HDAs

The
tensor product
of two HDAs
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is the HDA
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}
defined by
P_{{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}}=P_{{\mathcal{A}}}\otimes P_{{\mathcal{B}}}
,
I_{{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}}=(I_{{\mathcal{A}}},I_{{\mathcal{B}}})
,
F_{{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}}=F_{{\mathcal{A}}}\times F_{{\mathcal{B}}}
,
\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}}=\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}\amalg\Sigma_{{\mathcal{B}}}
, and

\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}}(x),&(x,y)\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{1}\times(P_{{\mathcal{B}}})_{0},\\
\lambda_{{\mathcal{B}}}(y),&(x,y)\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}})_{0}\times(P_{{\mathcal{B}}})_{1}.\end{array}\right.

We remark that the categories
\mathsf{HDA}
and
\mathsf{HDA_{ext}}
are monoidal categories with respect to this tensor product.

5.2.
Interleaving of
\ltimes
-transition systems

We define the
interleaving
of two
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition systems
\mathcal{S}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
to be the
\ltimes\mbox{-}
transition system
\mathcal{S}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}
where

U(\mathcal{S}\interleave{\mathcal{T}})=(U({\mathcal{S}})\otimes U({\mathcal{T}}))_{\leq 1}

and
\alpha\ltimes_{{\mathcal{S}}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}}\beta
if either

(1)

\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\alpha\ltimes_{\mathcal{S}}\beta
;

(2)

\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\alpha\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\beta
; or

(3)

\alpha\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\beta\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
.

With respect to the interleaving operator
\interleave
, the category of
\ltimes
-transition systems is a monoidal category.

5.3.
Parallel composition of shared-variable systems

Let
{\mathcal{P}}^{1}=({\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{n})
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{2}=({\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{m})
be two shared-variable systems given by program graphs over the set of variables

parallel composition
of

and

is the shared-variable system

given by

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{i},&1\leq i\leq n,\\

We shall write

, and

to denote the sets of declared variables of the systems
{\mathcal{P}}^{1}
,
{\mathcal{P}}^{2}

Theorem 12
.

If

{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n+m}}\cong{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{n}}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{m}}.

Proof 5.1

Remark 13

Although this will, of course, not happen in general, one may have an isomorphism

({\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2})
of Example

although the variable

and

5.4.
HDA models of interleavings

Let
\mathcal{S}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
be
\ltimes
-transition systems, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be HDA models of
\mathcal{S}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
, respectively. We establish that the tensor product
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}
is an HDA model of the interleaving
\mathcal{S}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}
. Given its intuitive plausibility, it is perhaps surprising that this result does not hold in either the nonextensional or the precubical setting. Consider, for example, a 1-truncated HDA with two vertices and two identically labeled edges from one to the other. While the tensor product of this nonextensional HDA with an HDA representing an interval has two 2-cubes, its 1-skeleton has four independence squares. Hence the HDA corresponding to the HDA model of the interleaving would have four
2\mbox{-}
cubes. This shows that the result that
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}
is an HDA model of
\mathcal{S}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}
does not hold without extensionality. It does not hold in the precubical setting either, as is shown by the same example without labels or the even simpler one of two intervals. As a consequence, this result cannot be established by a general abstract argument that also would apply to precubical sets or nonextensional HDAs.

-transition systems models the nondeterministic sum of shared-variable systems. We show that the coproduct of the HDA models of two
\ltimes
-transition systems is an HDA model of their coproduct.

6.1.
Coproduct of HDAs

The coproduct of two HDAs
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is the HDA
{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
where

•

P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}
is the precubical subset of
P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{B}}
defined by

(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{m}={(\{I_{\mathcal{A}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{m})}\cup((P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}\times\{I_{\mathcal{B}}\})\quad(m\geq 0);

•

I_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}=(I_{\mathcal{A}},I_{\mathcal{B}})
;

•

F_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}=(\{I_{\mathcal{A}}\}\times F_{\mathcal{B}})\cup(F_{\mathcal{A}}\times\{I_{\mathcal{B}}\})
;

•

\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}=\Sigma_{\mathcal{A}}\amalg\Sigma_{\mathcal{B}}
; and

•

\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y),&(x,y)\in\{I_{\mathcal{A}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{1},\\
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}(x),&(x,y)\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{B}}\}.\end{array}\right.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are extensional, then so is
{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
. Moreover, in this case
{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
is the coproduct of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\mathsf{HDA_{ext}}
.

6.2.
Coproduct of
\ltimes
-transition systems

The coproduct of two
\ltimes
-transition systems
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
is the
\ltimes
-transition system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}
where
U({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})=U({\mathcal{S}})+U({\mathcal{T}})
and
\alpha\ltimes_{{\mathcal{S}}\interleave{\mathcal{T}}}\beta
if either

(1)

\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\alpha\ltimes_{\mathcal{S}}\beta
; or

(2)

\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\alpha\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\beta
.

6.3.
Nondeterministic sum of shared-variable systems

Let
Var
be a set of variables, and let
\eta_{0}\in Ev(Var)
be an initial evaluation. We say that a shared-variable system
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n})
over
Var
is the
nondeterministic sum
with respect to
\eta_{0}
of two shared-variable systems
({\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{n_{1}})
and
({\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{n_{2}})
over
Var
if
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{n}}\cong{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{1}_{n_{1}}}+{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{n_{2}}}
.

{exa}

Consider the shared-variable system
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2})
of Example
3.5
and modify the function
g
of
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}
by setting
g(0,x{\scriptstyle{++}},1)=\{0\}
. While this has no effect on
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{2}}
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2}}
is altered in that the state
(1,1,2)
and the transitions involving it disappear and
\ltimes_{{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2}}}
becomes the empty relation. Therefore one now has
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2}}\cong{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{2}}
, and so
({\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2})
is the nondeterministic sum of the systems
({\mathcal{P}}_{1})
and
({\mathcal{P}}_{2})
.

6.4.
HDA models of coproducts

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
be
\ltimes
-transition systems, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be HDA models of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
, respectively.

Theorem 22
.

{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
is an HDA model of
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof 6.1
.

(HM1)
({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}})_{\leq 1}={\mathcal{A}}_{\leq 1}+{\mathcal{B}}_{\leq 1}=U({\mathcal{S}})+U({\mathcal{T}})=U({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
.

(HM2) Let
(x,y)\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{2}
. If
(x,y)\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{2}
, then

\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}(d^{0}_{2}(x,y))=\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(d^{0}_{2}y)\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(d^{1}_{2}y)=\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}(d^{1}_{2}(x,y)).

Hence
\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}(d^{0}_{2}(x,y))\ltimes_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}}\lambda_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}}(d^{1}_{2}(x,y))
. A similar argument shows that this also holds if
(x,y)\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{2}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
.

(HM3) Let
m\geq 2
and
(a,b),(x,y)\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{m}
such that
d^{k}_{r}(a,b)=d^{k}_{r}(x,y)
for all
r\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
. Suppose first that
(a,b)\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{m}
. Then also
(x,y)\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{m}
. Indeed, if this was not the case, then we would have
(x,y)\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
and
(I_{\mathcal{S}},d^{0}_{1}b)=d^{0}_{1}(a,b)=d^{0}_{1}(x,y)=(d^{0}_{1}x,I_{\mathcal{T}})
. Since
\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{m-1}
and
(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
are disjoint subsets of
(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{m-1}
, this is impossible. Thus
(x,y)\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{m}
. It follows that
d^{k}_{r}b=d^{k}_{r}y
for all
r\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
k\in\{0,1\}
. By property HM3 for
{\mathcal{B}}
,
b=y
. Thus
(a,b)=(x,y)
. A similar argument shows that
(a,b)=(x,y)
if
(a,b)\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{m}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
.

(HM4) Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an HDA that satisfies HM1-HM3 for
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}
and contains
{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
as a subautomaton. We show by induction that for all
r\geq 1
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}=({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}})_{\leq r}
. We have
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 1}=U({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})=({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}})_{\leq 1}
.
Let
r\geq 2
, and suppose that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r-1}=({\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}})_{\leq r-1}
. Let
c\in(P_{\mathcal{C}})_{r}
. We show that
c\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{r}
. By the induction hypothesis, we have
d^{k}_{i}c\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{r-1}
for all
k\in\{0,1\}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

We show first that either for all
k
and
i
,
d^{k}_{i}c\in{\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}}
or for all
k
and
i
,
d^{k}_{i}c\in{(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}}
. If
r=2
, then this follows from the fact that, by property HM2,
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{0}_{2}c)\ltimes_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{0}_{1}c)
. Indeed, this implies that either all
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{k}_{i}c)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}}
or all
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{k}_{i}c)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
, which is only possible if either all
d^{k}_{i}c\in{\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}}
or all
d^{k}_{i}c\in{(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}}
. Suppose that
r>2
. If
d^{k}_{1}c\in{\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}}
, then for all
j\in\{2,\dots,r\}
and
l\in\{0,1\}
,
d^{k}_{1}d^{l}_{j}c=d^{l}_{j-1}d^{k}_{1}c\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-2}
and therefore
d^{l}_{j}c\in\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}
. Similarly, if
d^{k}_{1}c\in{(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}}
, then for all
j\in\{2,\dots,r\}
and
l\in\{0,1\}
,
d^{l}_{j}c\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
. It follows that either
d^{k}_{i}c\in{\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}}
for all
k,i
or
d^{k}_{i}c\in{(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}}
for all
k,i
.

Suppose that all
d^{k}_{i}c\in{\{I_{\mathcal{S}}\}\times(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r-1}}
. Write
d^{k}_{i}c=(I_{\mathcal{S}},y^{k}_{i})
. For
k,l\in\{0,1\}
and
1\leq i<j\leq r
,
(I_{\mathcal{S}},d^{k}_{i}y^{l}_{j})=d^{k}_{i}d^{l}_{j}c=d^{l}_{j-1}d^{k}_{i}c=(I_{\mathcal{S}},d^{l}_{j-1}y^{k}_{i})
and hence
d^{k}_{i}y^{l}_{j}=d^{l}_{j-1}y^{k}_{i}
. If
r=2
, we also have
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{0}_{i})=\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{0}_{i}c)=\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{1}_{i}c)=\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{1}_{i})
for all
i\in\{1,2\}
. Moreover,
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{0}_{2}c)\ltimes_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}}}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}(d^{0}_{1}c)
and therefore
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{0}_{2})\ltimes_{\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{0}_{1})
. By Proposition
5
, there exists an element
y\in(P_{\mathcal{B}})_{r}
such that
d^{k}_{i}y=y^{k}_{i}
for all
k,i
. Hence for all
k,i
,
d^{k}_{i}c=(I_{\mathcal{S}},y^{k}_{i})=d^{k}_{i}(I_{\mathcal{S}},y)
. By property HM3, it follows that
c=(I_{\mathcal{S}},y)\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{r}
. An analogous argument shows that
c\in(P_{{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}})_{r}
if all
d^{k}_{i}c\in(P_{\mathcal{A}})_{r-1}\times\{I_{\mathcal{T}}\}
.

Remark 23
.

By the universal property of the coproduct, there exists a unique morphism of HDAs
\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}})+\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{T}})\to\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
that restricts to the (obvious) morphisms
\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
and
\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
. The morphism
(\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}})+\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{T}}))_{\leq 1}\to\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})_{\leq 1}
is the identity on
U({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
. By Theorem
22
and Corollary
7
, it follows that
\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}})+\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{T}})\to\mathscr{A}({\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{T}})
is an isomorphism. Therefore the functor
\mathscr{A}
preserves (binary) coproducts.

7.
Concluding remarks

We have introduced
\ltimes
-transition systems to model shared-variable programs and presented a method to construct higher-dimensional automata from them. We have established that the construction is compositional in the sense that interleaving and nondeterministic choice are modeled by the tensor product and the coproduct of HDAs, respectively.

The squares and higher-dimensional cubes in an HDA represent independence of actions. The difference between a filled and an empty cube is a topological one, and this strongly suggests to use topological methods to analyze the independence structure of HDAs. An approach to this based on homology is described in
[
Kah18
]
. In the HDA models considered in the present paper the independence concept for actions is induced by a binary relation. Since this does not extend to sequences of actions, these HDAs have usually nonetheless a nontrivial higher independence structure and topology.

The main objective of this paper was to provide a framework in which one can actually prove the intuitively obvious fact that the tensor product of HDAs models the parallel composition of independent concurrent systems. Unfortunately, the tensor product of HDAs has the undesirable property not to be symmetric, i.e., normally,
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}\not\cong{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
. One way to overcome this problem is to consider a larger class of morphisms in the category of HDAs. In previous work this has already been advocated as a means to compare HDAs of different refinement levels
[
Kah14
,
Kah16
]
. It turns out that the
weak morphisms
considered in those articles are still too rigid to make the tensor product symmetric. The more flexible
cubical dimaps
introduced in
[
Kah18
]
seem, however, to be a suitable choice of morphisms for a symmetric monoidal category of higher-dimensional automata. This issue will be a subject of future work.

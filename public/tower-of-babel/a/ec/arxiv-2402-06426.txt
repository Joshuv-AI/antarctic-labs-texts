Random multiplicative functions and typical size of character in short intervals
Rachid Caich

Abstract
We examine the conditions under which the sum of random multiplicative functions in short intervals, given by ∑ x < n ⩽ x + y f ⁡ ( n ) \sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n) , exhibits the phenomenon of better than square-root cancellation . We establish that the point at which the square-root cancellation diminishes significantly is approximately when the ratio log ⁡ ( x y ) \log\big(\frac{x}{y}\big) is around log ⁡ log ⁡ x \sqrt{\log\log x} . By modeling characters by random multiplicative functions, we give a sharp bound of 1 r − 1 ​ ∑ χ mod r | ∑ x < n ⩽ x + y χ ⁡ ( n ) | \frac{1}{r-1}\sum_{\chi\!\!\!\mod r}\big|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}\chi(n)\big| , where r r is a large prime and x + y ⩽ r x+y\leqslant r . This extends the result of Harper [ 6 ] . Keywords: Random multiplicative functions, mean values of multiplicative functions, Rademacher functions, Steinhaus functions, martingales, multiplicative chaos, Dirichlet character. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11N37, (11K99, 60F15).



1
Introduction

The aim of this article is to investigate the typical size of random multiplicative functions in short intervals. Recent years have witnessed significant interest directed towards the study of random multiplicative functions. Within the realm of number theory and probability, two prominent models of random multiplicative functions have gained considerable attention.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of prime numbers. One of these models is referred to as the
Steinhaus random multiplicative function
. It is constructed by defining
(f(p))_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
as a sequence of independent Steinhaus random variables, which are uniformly distributed on the unit circle
|z|=1
. Subsequently,
f(n)
is defined for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
as follows:
f(n):=\prod_{p^{a}||n}f(p)^{a}
, where
p^{a}||n
indicates that
p^{a}
is the highest power of the prime
p
dividing
n
.

Another model is the
Rademacher multiplicative function
, obtained by letting
(f(p))_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
be independent Rademacher random variables, which take values
\pm 1
with equal probability of
\tfrac{1}{2}
each, and we set

f(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}ll@{}}\prod_{p|n}f(n),&\text{if}\ n\text{ is squarefree}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The Rademacher model was introduced by Wintner
[
16
]
in 1944 as a heuristic model for the Möbius function
\mu
(see the introduction
[
12
]
). With a slight modification, one can derive a probabilistic model for a real primitive Dirichlet character (see Granville–Soundararajan
[
3
]
). The Steinhaus random multiplicative functions serve as models for randomly chosen Dirichlet characters
\chi
or continuous characters
n\mapsto n^{it}
; for more details, refer to section 2 of Granville–Soundararajan
[
2
]
and Harper
[
6
]
.

Given such a random multiplicative function, an important goal
is to understand the partial sums
\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)
. Since
\mathbb{E}\big[f(n)\overline{f(m)}\big]=\mathbb{1}_{n=m}
, we have then

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)\bigg|^{2}\bigg]\asymp x.

One of the most remarkable results in the study of random multiplicative functions can arguably be attributed to Harper’s work
[
8
]
, which is: when
x\to+\infty

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)\bigg|\bigg]\asymp\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log_{2}x)^{1/4}}.

Here and in the sequel
\log_{k}
denotes the k-fold iterated logarithm. Harper’s theorem established Helson’s conjecture
[
10
]
, which says that the partial sums of random multiplicative functions generally manifest more than square-root cancellation than what is expected from square-roots. The veracity of Helson’s conjecture appeared considerably uncertain during that period. In fact, prior research conducted by Harper, Nikeghbali, and Radziwiłł
[
9
]
had suggested the opposite Helson’s conjecture.

More recently, Harper
[
6
]
proved an interesting
result about character sums, in fact, he proved, if
x
and
r/x
tend to infinity with
r
, then

\frac{1}{r-1}\sum_{\chi\!\!\!\!\!\mod r}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\chi(n)\bigg|=o(\sqrt{x}).

The proof relies on establishing a link between the sum of characters and the sum of Steinhaus multiplicative functions. After this connection is established, the phenomenon of
better than square-root cancellation
arises.

The aim of this paper is to study the typical size of random multiplicative functions in short intervals
]x,x+y]
. In suitable ranges, we shall establish that the sum of a random multiplicative function has more than square-root cancellation. In a prior work by Chatterjee and Soundararajan
[
1
]
, they established, in the case of Rademacher functions, that when
y=o\big(\frac{x}{\log x}\big)
, the sum
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n)
satisfies to a central limit theorem. More recently, Soundararajan and Xu
[
15
]
improved this result
[
1
]
to a range
y\leqslant\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}
where
\alpha=2\log 2-1
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Thus, in this range, one can easily deduce that when
y
tends to infinity, we have for any
0\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{(A(x,y))^{q}}\bigg|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n)\bigg|^{2q}\bigg]\sim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2q+1}{2})2^{\frac{2q+1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

where
A(x,y):=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n)\bigg|^{2}\bigg]
in particular
A(x,y)
is equal to
y+O(1)
in Steinhaus case. In Rademacher case
A(x,y)
is equal to
|\{n\in[x,x+y]:\,\,n\,\,\,\text{ square-free integer}\}|
. Alternatively, according to Harper’s
[
8
]
, it is proven that the probability distribution
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n)
deviates from a Gaussian distribution for
y\geqslant\frac{x}{(\log_{2}x)^{1/2-\varepsilon}}
.

For the typical size problem, Xu
[
17
]
had investigated the phenomena of the better than square-root cancellation when the sum is restricted to
n\leqslant x
of
R
rough numbers. For
\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)=\{n\leqslant x:p|n\implies p>R\}
, he showed that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}}\bigg|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}f(n)\bigg|\bigg]=o(1)

when
\log_{2}R=o(\sqrt{\log_{2}x})
and when
\log_{2}R\gg\sqrt{\log_{2}x}

and

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}}\bigg|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}_{R}(x)}f(n)\bigg|\bigg]\asymp 1,

which means that
R\approx\sqrt{\log\log x}
is the threshold.

In our case :
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n)
, we anticipate observing the following behavior at certain thresholds. When the value of
y
is sufficiently large, the range under consideration approximates
[x,2x]
and since
\sum_{x<n\leqslant 2x}f(n)=\sum_{n\leqslant 2x}f(n)-\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)
, the better than square-root cancellation appears as done by Harper
[
8
]
. On the other hand, when
y
takes on sufficiently small values, the presence of weak dependence might even lead to the emergence of a central limit theorem. This phenomenon is explored, as mentioned before, in the works of Chatterjee and Soundararajan as
[
1
]
and Soundararajan and Xu
[
15
]
. Our results in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
provide a conclusive response to this inquiry. We establish that the threshold for this behavior is characterized by
\log\big(\frac{x}{y}\big)\approx\sqrt{\log_{2}x}
.
Define

M(x,y;f):=\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}f(n).

(1)

The main results of this paper are the following three theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a Rademacher or Steinhaus random multiplicative function. Let
y\leqslant x
large numbers, we define
\theta
by
\frac{x}{y}=\log^{\theta}x
. We have uniformly for
\theta>0
and
0\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\ll\bigg(y\min\bigg\{1,\theta\sqrt{\log_{2}x}+\frac{1}{(1-q)\sqrt{\log_{2}x}}\bigg\}\bigg)^{q}.

In particular for
\theta=o(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log_{2}x}})
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|\bigg]=o(\sqrt{y}).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be a Rademacher or Steinhaus random multiplicative function and
x
be large. For
\sqrt{\log_{2}x}\ll\log(\frac{x}{y})
, we have uniformly for
0\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\asymp y^{q}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r
be a large prime. Let
x,y
be such that
x+y\leqslant r
and
\log(\frac{x}{y})\ll\penalty\ \log\log x
, and let
\theta
as in Theorem
1.1
. We set
L:=\min\{x+y,r/(x+y)\}+3
. We have for
L
large enough and uniformly for
\theta>0
and
0\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\frac{1}{r-1}\sum_{\chi\!\!\!\!\!\mod r}\big|M(x,y;\chi)\big|^{2q}\ll\bigg(y\min\bigg\{1,\theta\sqrt{\log_{2}x}+\frac{1}{(1-q)\sqrt{\log_{2}L}}\bigg\}\bigg)^{q}.

(2)

Remark 1
.

Note that from Theorem
1.1
, when
\theta=o(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log_{2}x}})
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|\bigg]=o(\sqrt{y}).

In this domain, the quantity
M(x,y;f)
doesn’t behave as gaussian. This extends Soundararajan and Xu’s results
[
15
]
of limiting distribution of

\frac{M(x,y;f)}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[|M(x,y;f)|^{2}\big]}}

when
y
is large comparing to
x
(For more details, see
[
8
, Some corollaries 1.2.]
).

Sketch of the proof

There are some technical distinctions between the Steinhaus and Rademacher cases. However the majority of their behavior and essential proof concepts align. Therefore, in this introductory discussion, we focus on the Steinhaus case. Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are established through the application of Harper’s resilient approach in
[
8
]
to short intervals problem. The initial stage involves transforming the
L^{1}
norm estimation into a specific average of squared random Euler products. Essentially, let
f
be a Steinhaus multiplicative function and set
\delta:=\frac{x}{y}
. We prove that roughly speaking

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}M(x,y;f)\bigg|^{2q}\bigg]\approx\bigg(\frac{1}{\log x}\bigg)^{q}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

(3)

where

F_{z}(s):=\prod_{p\leqslant z}\bigg(1-\frac{f(p)}{p^{1/2+s}}\bigg)^{-s}.

(4)

The difficult aspect lies in providing a sharp bound for right side hand of (
3
). Let’s start with the upper bound. It’s clear the straight application of Hölder’s inequality doesn’t give the required bound. In
[
8
]
, the problem was reduced roughly speaking to
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]
. The importance difference between our problem (
3
) and Harper’s work is that the range of our integral in the right hand side of (
3
) is larger. Indeed, we prove that as long as
\delta
does not exceed a certain critical value,
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]
provides a “good” saving factor. However when
\delta
becomes larger, we show that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\gg 1.

In the prior studies (Harper
[
8
]
and Xu
[
17
]
),
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
. In the present investigation,
\delta
takes large values, constituting a primary distinction between our work and the previous works.
Harper’s approach initiates by introducing “barrier events” that restrict the growth rate of every random partial Euler product across all values of
|t|\leqslant\delta
. In other words, Harper define an event that control, for each
k
, the quantity
\prod_{x^{{\rm e}^{-{k+1}}}<p\leqslant x^{{\rm e}^{-k}}}\big|1-\frac{f(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\big|^{-1}
by some “expected growth”. In addition to Harper’s “expected growth”, in our case, we introduce an extra element arising from the extensive range of values within
|t|\leq\delta
:
\theta\sqrt{\log_{2}x}
where
\theta
as defined in Theorem
1.1
.

Denote
\mathcal{G}
to be this “good” event, and
q^{\prime}:=\frac{1+q}{2}
. It is clear to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)|^{2}\right)^{q}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left(1_{\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)|^{2}\right)^{q}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left(1_{\mathcal{G}\text{ fails }}\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it)|^{2}\right)^{q}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{q}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\bigg[(\mathcal{G}\text{ fails })^{\frac{q^{\prime}-q}{q^{\prime}}}\bigg]\left(\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}.

Here we applied Hölder’s inequality in the second line. Following Harper
[
8
]
, we prove first that the probability of “
\mathcal{G}
fails” is “small” and secondly the above expectation under the condition “
\mathcal{G}
holds” provides a saving factor:
\theta\log_{2}x+\frac{1}{(1-q)\sqrt{\log_{2}x}}
. Following an iterative procedure, we get the required bound. Note that when
\theta
is larger than
1/\sqrt{\log_{2}x}
, our bound is useless, since we can get a better bound by using a Hölder’s inequality.

For the lower bound, the strategy is relatively simple. we use the same approach as
[
8
]
: the problem is reduced to give an upper bound
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{\mathcal{L}}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it\right)\right|^{2}\right]^{2}
for a chosen random set in
[-\delta,\delta]
that verifies some “good” barriers. We set
L(t)
the event: those “good” barriers occurs at point
t\in[-\delta,\delta]
. The idea is that when we expend this expectation to
\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{1})}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{2})}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\big]
, we want this quality to behave as
\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{1})}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{2})}\left|F_{\sqrt{2x}}\left(it_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\big]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant K}\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}M_{k}(x,y;f)\big\|_{2q}\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant K}\bigg\|\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{k}(it-k/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg\|_{q}^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant K}\bigg\|\frac{\delta}{\log x}\int_{\delta<|t|\leqslant\delta^{5}}\frac{\big|F_{k}(it-k/\log x)\big|^{2}}{\big|1/2+it-k/\log x\big|^{2}}{\rm d}t\bigg\|_{q}^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{1+(1-q)\sqrt{\log_{2}x}}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be a

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{k}\bigg[\big|M_{k}(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\bigg]\bigg)^{1/2q}

\sum_{d>1}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\big|N_{k}\big(\frac{x}{d},\frac{y}{d};f\big)\big|^{2}
by a
smoothed version. Set

. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{d>1}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\bigg|N_{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{d},\frac{y}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d>1}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|N_{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{d},\frac{y}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{d>1}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|S_{k}\bigg(\frac{x+y}{t},\frac{x+y}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{d>1}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|S_{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{x}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t.

Using Hölder’s inequality and a mean square calculation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|S_{k}\bigg(\frac{x+y}{t},\frac{x+y}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\big(1+\frac{x+y}{dX}\big)\bigg)^{q}.

(14)

\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}1\ll 2^{-{\rm e}^{k}}\frac{x}{\log x}\ll 2^{-{\rm e}^{k}}\frac{y}{(\log x)^{0.98}}.

We have also

Recall that

\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant K}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|S_{k}\bigg(\frac{x+y}{t},\frac{x+y}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\Bigg)^{1/2q}\ll\frac{\sqrt{y}}{(\log x)^{0.49}}.

We prove, following the same steps, that

\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant K}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}\bigg|S_{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{x}{d};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\Bigg)^{1/2q}\ll\frac{\sqrt{y}}{(\log x)^{0.49}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leqslant x+y}^{x_{k},x_{k-1}}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+\frac{1}{X})}

\displaystyle\bigg|N_{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

Given a probability space

, we can introduce other probability measures and obtain other probability spaces with the same sample space
\Omega
and the same
\sigma
-algebra of events
\mathcal{F}
. One “easy” way to do that is to start with a nonnegative random variable
\Theta
such that
\mathbb{E}[\Theta]=1
. Then we can obtain another probability measure

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[A]:=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}_{A}\Theta]\text{ for all }A\in\mathcal{F}.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
denote the expectation associated to
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
(i.e for any
Y
random variable
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[Y]:=\mathbb{E}[\Theta Y]
).
With the same way, we define the following probability.

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}(A):=\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbb{1}_{A}\prod_{p\leqslant x^{{\rm e}^{-1}}}\left|1+a_{f}\frac{f(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2a_{f}}\bigg]}{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{p\leqslant x^{{\rm e}^{-1}}}\left|1+a_{f}\frac{f(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2a_{f}}\bigg]},

where
A
is an event from
\mathcal{F}
. The next lemma provides an upper bound of
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}\big[\mathcal{G}_{q}(k,t)\big]
uniformly for all
\frac{2}{3}\leqslant q\leqslant 1
,
\frac{1}{\log_{2}x}\leqslant|t|\leqslant\delta^{5}
and
0\leqslant k\leqslant K
.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{1}...r_{R}\in\mathcal{R}_{R}\\
\sum_{m=1}^{j}r_{m}\leqslant\tilde{v}_{j}\forall k\leqslant j\leqslant R\end{subarray}}\bigg\{r_{m}\leqslant N_{m}\leqslant r_{m}+1/m^{2}\text{ for all }1\leqslant m\leqslant R\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\bigg[\tilde{u}_{j}-2\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}N_{m}\leqslant\tilde{v}_{j}+2\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg].

Recall that we have
|t-v_{m}(t)|\ll\frac{1}{\log x_{u_{m}}\log_{2}x_{u_{m}}}
. In the Rademacher case, we have

\displaystyle 2\cos\big(t\log p\big)\cos\big(v_{m}(t)\log p\big)

\displaystyle=\cos\big((t+v_{m}(t))\log p\big)+\cos\big((t-v_{m}(t))\log p\big)

\displaystyle=\cos\big((t+v_{m}(t))\log p\big)+1+O(1/m^{2}).

Since
|t|\log x_{R}\gg{\rm e}^{B}
(recall that
R
is defined in (
24
)), we get from Prime Number Theorem

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{\cos\left(\left(t+v_{m}(t)\right)\log p\right)}{p}\,\,,\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/e}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{\cos\left(2v_{m}(t)\log p\right)}{p}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{|t|\log x_{u_{m}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{m}|t|\log x_{R}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{B{\rm e}^{m}}.

Since

\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{1}{p}=1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{b_{0}{\rm e}^{m/2}}}\bigg)

where
b_{0}
is an absolute constant, we finally get

\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{c(t,v_{m}(t),p)}{p}=1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{m^{2}}\bigg).

By taking

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{k}

\displaystyle=a_{k}(q,t)

in (
36
),
and

G_{m}=N_{m}-\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{c(t,v_{m}(t),p)}{p},

we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}\log\bigg|I_{u_{m}}\bigg(\frac{k}{\log x},v_{m}(t)\bigg)\bigg|\leqslant a_{j}(q,t)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant j-\sum_{m=1}^{j}\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{c(t,v_{m}(t),p)}{p}+a(q,t)+2\log j\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j+O(1)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg].

The proof of the corresponding lower bound of (
36
) is exactly similar. By following the same steps, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}\bigg[-a_{j}(q,t)\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}\log\big|I_{u_{m}}\big(\frac{k}{\log x},v_{m}(t)\big)\big|\leqslant a_{j}(q,t)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\gg\mathbb{P}\bigg[-j-\sum_{m=1}^{j}\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{c(t,v_{m}(t),p)}{p}\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg].

\displaystyle\gg\mathbb{P}\bigg[-2j+O(1)\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

For Steinhaus case, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}\log\big|I_{u_{m}}\big(\frac{k}{\log x},v_{m}(t)\big)\big|\leqslant a_{j}(q,t)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant-\sum_{m=1}^{j}\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{1}{p}+a_{j}(q,t)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j+O(1)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg].

For the lower bound in the Steinhaus case

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}[-a_{j}(q,t)\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}\log\big|I_{u_{m}}\big(\frac{k}{\log x},v_{m}(t)\big)\big|\leqslant a_{j}(q,t)\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R]

\displaystyle\gg\mathbb{P}\bigg[-j-\sum_{m=1}^{j}\sum_{x_{u_{m}}^{1/{\rm e}}<p\leqslant x_{u_{m}}}\frac{1}{p}\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg]

\displaystyle\gg\mathbb{P}\bigg[-2j+O(1)\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{j}G_{m}\leqslant a(q,t)+2\log j\text{ for all }k\leqslant j\leqslant R\bigg].

This ends the proof.
∎

4.2
The proof of the lower bound.

The approach used to prove Theorem
1.2
is once again derived from
[
8
]
. The initial phase of our argument involves a reduction step aimed at narrowing down the initial expression to comprehending specific averages of random Euler products, much like the methodology employed in the upper bound demonstration.

In the following, we will use the notations
M,\,M^{+},\,M^{-}
defined in (
1
) and (
9
). Let first start when
y\ll\frac{x}{\log x}
. We have the following Lemma

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
f
be a Steinhaus or Rademacher multiplicative function. Let
x
and
y
large, with
y\ll\frac{x}{\log x}
. We have uniformly for
0\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\asymp y^{q}.

Proof.

Is a direct result from the fact that for
y\ll\frac{x}{\log x}
the quantity
\frac{M(x,y;f)}{\mathbb{E}\big[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2}\big]}
is distributed like a standard normal random variable with mean
0
and variance
1
(See for instance
[
15
, Corollary 1.2 and Theorem 9.1]
).
∎

Now, we can reduce the problem on studying
M(x,y;f)
when
y\geqslant\frac{x}{\log x}
.
Let
\epsilon
be an auxiliary Rademacher random variable, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2q}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)+M^{+}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)

\displaystyle+M^{-}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)-M^{+}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)+M^{+}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)-M^{+}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg].

Here, we used the fact that the law of
-M^{+}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)=-\sum_{p>\sqrt{2x}}f(p)M(x/p,y/p;f)
conditional on the values
(f(p))_{p\leqslant\sqrt{2x}}
is the same as the law of
M^{+}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)
. Thus we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M(x,y;f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\gg\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg].

In the decomposition

\displaystyle M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant x+y}f(p)M\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{y}{p};f\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y<p\leqslant x+y}f(p)M\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{y}{p};f\bigg)+\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}f(p)M\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{y}{p};f\bigg),

the inner sum in both sums which are determined by
(f(p))_{p>\sqrt{2x}}
are independent from
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x}{p}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{p}\end{subarray}}f(n)
, by applying the Khintchine’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\gg\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{y<p}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leqslant x+y\\
P(n)>y\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{q}\bigg].

Remark 4
.

Note that the left-hand side of the expression above is deterministic. One can prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
and for
\sqrt{x}\leqslant y\leqslant x^{1-\varepsilon}
, the following holds:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leqslant x+y\\
P(n)>y\end{subarray}}1\gg_{\varepsilon}y.

However, this result is irrelevant as we are investigating
M(x,y;f)
in cases where
y\geqslant\penalty\ \frac{x}{\log x}
.

In the following, we will focus on
\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\big|M^{+}\big(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\big)\big|^{2}
. We start with this following Lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
f
be a Rademacher or Steinhaus multiplicative function. There exists a large absolute constant
C_{1}>0
such that the following is true. Let
x
be large enough and assume that
y\geqslant\frac{x}{\log x}
. For any large quantity
V\leqslant(\log x)^{0.1}
. We have uniformly for all
2/3\leqslant q\leqslant 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\gg

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{y}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

(37)

\displaystyle-C_{1}y^{q}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{1}{V{\rm e}^{V}}\bigg)^{q}-\frac{\log_{2}x}{\log x}\bigg).

Proof.

Note first that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]\gg\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\bigg|M^{-}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{q}\bigg].

We follow the same techniques as in Harper
[
8
]
in the “Proof of Proposition 3 and 4”. Let
X\geqslant{\rm e}^{\sqrt{\log x}}
. It can be readily checked that the following inequality consistently holds:
|x+a+b|^{2}\geqslant(\frac{1}{4})|x|^{2}-\min\{2(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}),|x/2|^{2}\}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\frac{X}{p}\int^{p(1+1/X)}_{p}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{p},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\frac{X}{p}\int^{p(1+1/X)}_{p}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\frac{X}{p}\int^{p(1+1/X)}_{p}\min\bigg\{2\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x+y}{t},\frac{x+y}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+2\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{x}{p},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2},\frac{1}{4}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\bigg\}{\rm d}t.

thus for any
2/3\leqslant q\leqslant 1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{-}(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{(\log x)^{q}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{q}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(2\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{q}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(2\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg].

We deal first with the last two terms. By Hölder’s inequality and by using the same techniques as in the inequality (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}\bigg]{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\ll\bigg(\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\bigg(\frac{x}{pX}+1\bigg)\bigg)^{q}\ll\bigg(\frac{x\log x}{X}+y\bigg)^{q}\ll y^{q}\bigg(\log x\frac{\delta}{X}+1\bigg)^{q}.

Recall that
\delta\ll\log x
and
X\geqslant{\rm e}^{\sqrt{\log x}}
. We obtain

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant x+y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\ll\bigg(\frac{y}{\log x}\bigg)^{q}.

We prove similarly that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant x+y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{p}\\
P(n)>\sqrt{2x}\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\ll\bigg(\frac{y}{\log x}\bigg)^{q}.

Let deal now with
\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\big|M^{+}\big(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\big)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\sqrt{2x}<p\leqslant y}\log p\frac{X}{p}\int_{p}^{p(1+1/X)}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\gg\int_{y}^{\sqrt{2x}}\sum_{\frac{t}{1+\frac{1}{X}}<p<t}\log p\frac{X}{p}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\gg\int^{y}_{\sqrt{2x}}\bigg|M^{+}\bigg(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t},\sqrt{2x};f\bigg)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t=y\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{1}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}.

We used the fact that
\sum_{\frac{t}{1+\frac{1}{X}}<p<t}\log p\frac{X}{p}\asymp 1
followed by a change of variable
z=y/t
. Let’s focus on
\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{1}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{1}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}-\int^{1}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}.

Note that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\log x}\int^{1}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}\bigg]\leqslant\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant\delta+1}\frac{1}{n}\asymp\frac{\log\delta}{\log x}\ll\frac{\log_{2}x}{\log x}.

Let focus now on
\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}
.
Let
V
be a large number such that
V<(\log x)^{0.1}
, we have for
x
large enough

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x})\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

(38)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2^{-q}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{y/\sqrt{2x}}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+8V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+8V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{y/\sqrt{2x}}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+8V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+8V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2{\rm e}^{Vq}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+4V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg].

By using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+8V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\gg\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

where
F_{\sqrt{2x}}
is defined in (
4
) for
z=\sqrt{2x}
.
Note that we have as well

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z

\displaystyle,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+4V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|M^{+}(\delta z,z,\sqrt{2x};f)\big|^{2}\bigg]\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2+4V/\log x}}\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\ll\bigg(\frac{\log\delta}{V}+\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{q}\ll\bigg(\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{q}.

At the end, and by gathering all previous inequalities, we deduce that there exists a large constant
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}M^{+}\big(x,y,\sqrt{2x};f\big)\bigg|^{2q}\bigg]\gg

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta\log x}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|F_{\sqrt{2x}}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle-C_{1}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{1}{V{\rm e}^{V}}\bigg)^{q}-\bigg(\frac{\log_{2}x}{\log x}\bigg)^{q}\bigg).

∎

-Ba_{k}(q,x)\leqslant\sum^{k}_{m=1}\log\left|I_{u_{m}}\left(\frac{4V}{\log x},t\right)\right|\leqslant a_{k}(q,x)

(39)

where

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Proposition
4.11
assuming Proposition

.

In this proof, we will focus on Steinhaus case, the proof in the Rademacher case is similar.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\geqslant\frac{\bigg(\mathbb{E}\big[\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\big]\bigg)^{2-q}}{\bigg(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\big)^{2}\big]\bigg)^{1-q}}.

We give first a lower bound on the numerator. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log_{2}x}}<|t|\leqslant\delta\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]{\rm d}t.

By using inequality (
35

4.7
and by taking

,

\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]\big)^{2-q}\gg\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]\big)^{2-q}\gg\bigg(\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{2-q}.

Combining with the (

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{q}\bigg]\gg\bigg(\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{q}.

∎

Proof of Proposition

.

We start with the Steinhaus case.
By using the fact that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{1})}\big|F_{x}(it_{1}+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t_{1}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathbb{1}_{L(t_{2})}\big|F_{x}(it_{2}+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t_{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-2\delta}^{2\delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(0)}\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]{\rm d}t.

Here, we have made a change of variables
(t=t_{1}-t_{2})

We have for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(0)}\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big[\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\ll\exp\bigg\{\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{2+2\cos(t\log p)}{p^{1+8V/\log x}}\bigg\}\ll\exp\bigg\{\sum_{{\rm e}^{1/|t|}<p\leqslant x}\frac{2+2\cos(t\log p)}{p^{1+8V/\log x}}\bigg\}.

3.3

|t|>1

\displaystyle\exp\bigg\{\sum_{{\rm e}^{1/|t|}<p\leqslant x}\frac{2+2\cos(t\log p)}{p^{1+8V/\log x}}\bigg\}\ll\bigg(\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{2}.

\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1<|t|\leqslant 2\delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{1}_{L(0)}\big|F_{x}(4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{L(t)}\big|F_{x}(it+4V/\log x)\big|^{2}\big]{\rm d}t\ll\bigg(\frac{\log x}{V}\bigg)^{2}.

Now let’s deal with the case where

The proof of the Rademacher cases is quite similar to the Steinhaus case, with only a slight difference. When transitioning to the random multiplicative function,

5.2.1
The conditioning argument

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{char}\bigg[\big|M^{+}(x,y,x^{\frac{1}{\log_{2}x}};\chi)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

\mathbb{E}^{char}\bigg[\big|M^{+}(x,y,x^{\frac{1}{\log_{2}x}};\chi)\big|^{2q}\bigg]

(46)

We will focus on studying

Following Harper

Euler product

\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg].

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{rand}(\textbf{j})\bigg(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},rand}\bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leqslant x+y\\
P(d)>x^{\frac{1}{\log_{2}x}}\end{subarray}}^{P,x}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{d}\\
P(n)\leqslant P\\
\end{subarray}}f(n)\bigg|^{2}{\rm d}t\bigg]\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{rand}(\textbf{j})\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},rand}\bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leqslant x+y\\
P(d)>x^{\frac{1}{\log_{2}x}}\end{subarray}}^{P,x}\frac{X}{d}\int_{d}^{d(1+1/X)}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{x+y}{t}<n\leqslant\frac{x+y}{d}\\

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\frac{y}{\log P}\bigg)^{q}\Bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\delta K_{P}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k|\leqslant\delta K_{P}\end{subarray}}\,\,({\log P)^{2.2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{\textbf{j}}\prod_{i\neq k}g_{j_{i}}(S_{i}(f))\bigg]\Bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\ll\bigg(\frac{y}{\log P}\bigg)^{q}\Bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\delta K_{P}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k|\leqslant\delta K_{P}\end{subarray}}\,\,({\log P)^{2.2}}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{-N\leqslant j_{k}\leqslant N+1}1\Bigg)^{q}\ll y^{q}\big(\lambda N(\log P)^{1.2}\big)^{q}.

Periodic frames
Lev D. Beklemishev, Yunsong Wang

Abstract
Polymodal provability logic GLP is incomplete w.r.t. Kripke frames. It is known to be complete w.r.t. topological semantics, where the diamond modalities correspond to topological derivative operations. However, the topologies needed for the completeness proof are highly non-constructive. The question of completeness of GLP w.r.t. natural scattered topologies on ordinals is dependent on large cardinal axioms of set theory and is still open. So far, we are lacking a useable class of models for which GLP is complete. In this paper we define a natural class of countable general topological frames on ordinals for which GLP is sound and complete. The associated topologies are the same as the ordinal topologies introduced by Thomas Icard. However, the key point is to specify a suitable algebra of subsets of an ordinal closed under the boolean and topological derivative operations. The algebras we define are based on the notion of a periodic set of ordinals generalizing that of an ultimately periodic binary omega-word.

One obstacle in the study of

the relations corresponding to all modalities but
[0]

A sufficiently rich class of

Thereafter, attention of researchers was drawn to

Under the standard topological interpretation of provability logic

for the topological interpretation w.r.t. the interval topology on any ordinal
\Omega

[
16

for a comprehensive survey of topological models of provability logic.

Topological interpretation of

turned out to be a rich area of study on its own with a number of significant contributions.
Thomas Icard
[

suggested natural topological models for the variable-free fragment of

In

[

who showed that

The extension of this result to more than two modalities turned out to be a significant problem. The ordinals bear a natural sequence of topologies making it a

-space, the so-called

-space

More exactly, the limit points of the topologies of the ordinal GLP-space correspond to the so-called

The question of consistency strength of

-(simultaneusly)-stationary reflecting cardinals and its generalization to transfinite iterations turned out to be fruitful from a set-theoretic point of view and stimulated a number of papers starting from the work of Joan Bagaria

Despite these set-theoretic complications the topological completeness of

[

In this paper we consider a more general kind of topological semantics for

called

Such structures have been considered in the modal logic literature before, e.g., the authors of
[
20

[
29
]
under the name

[

is a general topological frame satisfying the axioms of

in which the sequence of topologies is increasing and the topologies are scattered.

In this paper we define explicit and natural general

Periodic transfinite binary words, or equivalently periodic subsets of the ordinals, have appeared in the literature before. On the one hand, they are considered in the context of the combinatorics of words, as a natural generalization of periodic
\omega
-words (see, for example,
[

On the other hand, they appeared in the context of the studies of monadic second-order theories of well-orders, starting from the work of J.R. Büchi

. In fact, MSO definable sets on

in the language with more than one modality. So, we will generalize them to what we call

-periodic sets
, for each

, and to arbitrary ordinals.

The main result of this paper is the completeness theorem for
\mathrm{GLP}
w.r.t. the ordinal
\varepsilon_{0}
(or any larger ordinal) equipped with Icard’s topologies and the algebra of

\mathrm{GLP}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present necessary background results on
\mathrm{GLP}
, its algebraic and neighborhood semantics, and general topological frames. In Section 3 we introduce periodic transfinite words and sets of ordinals. We prove that periodic sets form a Magari algebra. In Section 4 we introduce

-periodic sets and the corresponding general topological frames. In Section 5 we borrow some techniques from
[
15

reducing the question of completeness of

w.r.t. periodic frames to a covering construction. Some necessary intermediate lemmas from
[
15
]
are presented in the appendix to this paper. Finally, in Section 6 we prove the covering lemma and thereby complete the proof of our main result.

The authors started working on this topic together in the context a Master’s thesis project of Yunsong Wang at the ILLC of the University of Amsterdam. In his Thesis
[

the notions of hereditarily periodic set and of 1-periodic set were defined, and the corresponding completeness theorem for the bimodal fragment of

was proved. After the completion of this work, the first author of the paper reworked the whole setup and extended the result to the full language of
\mathrm{GLP}
. The current paper is written up by Lev Beklemishev.

2
Preliminaries

Here we introduce some of the background notions for this paper.

2.1
Provability logic
\mathrm{GLP}

\mathrm{GLP}
is a propositional modal logic formulated in the language with infinitely many
modalities
[n]
, for all
n\in\omega
. As usual, we abbreviate
\neg[n]\neg\varphi
by
\langle n\rangle\varphi
.
\mathrm{GLP}
is
given by the following axiom schemata and rules:

Axioms:

\mathrm{(i)}

Boolean tautologies;

\mathrm{(ii)}

[n](\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow([n]\varphi\rightarrow[n]\psi)
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

[n]([n]\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)\rightarrow[n]\varphi
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

\langle m\rangle\varphi\rightarrow[n]\langle m\rangle\varphi
, for
m<n
.

\mathrm{(v)}

[m]\varphi\rightarrow[n]\varphi
, for

m\leqslant n
;

Rules:

modus ponens,
\varphi\vdash[n]\varphi
.

The first three axioms are the well-known axioms of the Gödel–Löb provability logic
\mathrm{GL}
(stated for modality
[n]
). We identify
\mathrm{GL}
with the fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}
restricted to the language with modality
[0]
.

2.2
GLP-algebras

Algebraic models of
\mathrm{GLP}
are called
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebras. These are boolean algebras with operators
\langle n\rangle
, for all
n\in\omega
, satisfying the following modal identities:

\mathrm{(i)}

\langle n\rangle(x\lor y)=\langle n\rangle x\lor\langle n\rangle y
;

\neg\langle n\rangle\bot
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\langle n\rangle x=\langle n\rangle(x\land\neg\langle n\rangle x)
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

\langle n\rangle x\land\langle m\rangle y=\langle n\rangle(x\land\langle m\rangle y)
, for
m<n
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

\langle n\rangle x\land\langle m\rangle x=\langle n\rangle x
, for
m\leqslant n
.

Identities (i) and (ii), for any modality
\langle n\rangle
, define the variety of
Magari algebras
, which are the algebraic models of
\mathrm{GL}
. The last two identities correspond to the remaining axioms of
\mathrm{GLP}
.

A
valuation
on a
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is a map
v
from propositional variables to
{\mathcal{M}}
. It is inductively extended to the set of all
\mathrm{GLP}
-formulas by the rules:
v(\varphi\land\psi)=v(\varphi)\land v(\psi)
,
v(\neg\varphi)=\neg v(\varphi)
, and
v([n]\varphi)=\neg\langle n\rangle\neg v(\varphi)
.

The
logic
of
{\mathcal{M}}
is the set

\mathrm{Log}({\mathcal{M}})
of all formulas
\varphi
such that
v(\varphi)=\top
, for all valuations
v
in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
We say that a logic
L
is
complete
for a class of algebras
\mathbf{C}
, if
L=\bigcap_{{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{Log}({\mathcal{M}})
.

On general grounds, the logic
\mathrm{GLP}
is complete for the class of all
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebras, and
\mathrm{GL}
is complete for the class of all Magari algebras.

2.3
Neighborhood frames and
\mathrm{GLP}
-spaces

Let
L
be a normal modal logic in the language with modalities
[i]
, for
i\in I
. We call a
neighborhood frame for
L
a structure
(X,(\delta_{i})_{i\in I})
where each
\delta_{i}
is an operator
\delta_{i}:{\mathcal{P}}(X)\to{\mathcal{P}}(X)
and the algebra
({\mathcal{P}}(X),(\delta_{i})_{i\in I})
satisfies the axioms of
L
under the interpretation of boolean connectives as the usual set-theoretic operations and
\langle i\rangle
as
\delta_{i}
.

Esakia
[
28
]
has shown that in the case of Gödel–Löb logic
\mathrm{GL}
every such
\delta
coincides with the topological derivative operation of a (uniquely defined) scattered topology on
X
. Recall that a topological space
(X,\tau)
is
scattered
if each nonempty subspace of
X
has an isolated point. Topological derivative operation
d_{\tau}
maps a subset
A\subseteq X
to its set of limit points
d_{\tau}(A)
. Esakia’s result justifies us calling neighborhood frames for
\mathrm{GL}

topological (derivative) frames
.

The same applies to neighborhood frames for
\mathrm{GLP}
which correspond to
GLP-spaces

[
14
]
.

Definition 1

(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in\omega})
is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-space
if, for each
n\in\omega
:

(i)

(X,\tau_{n})
is a scattered topological space;

(ii)

\tau_{n}\subseteq\tau_{n+1}
;

(iii)

For each
A\subseteq X
,
d_{\tau_{n}}(A)
is
\tau_{n+1}
-open.

It is easy to see that
(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in\omega})
is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-space iff
(X,(d_{\tau_{i}})_{i\in\omega})
is a neighborhood frame for
\mathrm{GLP}
iff
({\mathcal{P}}(X),(d_{\tau_{i}})_{i\in\omega})
is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebra.

2.4
General topological frames and ambiences

Ambiences have been introduced by Fernández-Duque in
[
29
]
as a generalization of
\mathrm{GLP}
-spaces. The same notion is called
general topological frame
in
[
43
]
.

Definition 2

An
ambience
is a tuple
(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in I},\mathcal{A})
where
(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in I})
is a poly-topological space and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
is closed under the boolean operations and
d_{\tau_{i}}
, for each
i\in I
. We call
\cal A
the algebra of
admissible sets
of the ambience.

Similarly to neighborhood frames, with each ambience
\mathcal{X}=(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in I},\mathcal{A})
we associate the boolean algebra with operators
({\mathcal{A}},(d_{\tau_{i}})_{i\in I})
.
Thus, an ambience is essentially the same thing as a subalgebra of the algebra associated with a topological derivative frame. By definition, the
logic of
\mathcal{X}
, denoted
\mathrm{Log}(\mathcal{X})
, is the logic of this algebra.

Definition 3

An ambience
(X,(\tau_{n})_{n\in\omega},\mathcal{A})
is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-ambience
(or
provability ambience

[
29
]
) if, for each
n\in\omega
:

(i)

(X,\tau_{n})
is a scattered topological space;

(ii)

\tau_{n}\subseteq\tau_{n+1}
;

(iii)

For each
U\in{\cal A}
,
d_{\tau_{n}}(U)
is
\tau_{n+1}
-open.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-ambience then its algebra is a
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebra and
\mathrm{Log}(\mathcal{X})
contains
\mathrm{GLP}
.
\mathrm{GLP}
-spaces are
\mathrm{GLP}
-ambiences in which
{\mathcal{A}}
is the algebra of all subsets of
X
.

Remark 2.1

The requirements stated in items (i) and (ii) are actually stronger than is necessary for the soundness of
\mathrm{GLP}
. For example, Löb’s axiom will be satisfied if the topologies are ‘scattered’ w.r.t. the admissible subsets only.

Let
(\mathcal{X}_{j})_{j\in J}
be a family of ambiences,
\mathcal{X}_{j}=(X_{j},(\tau_{ij})_{i\in I},{\mathcal{A}}_{j})
. The
sum

\mathcal{X}=\bigsqcup_{j\in J}\mathcal{X}_{j}
is defined in a natural way. We define
\mathcal{X}=(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in I},{\mathcal{A}})
so that
X
is the disjoint union of sets
X_{j}
. A subset
U\subseteq X
is
\tau_{i}
-open if
U\cap X_{j}\in\tau_{ij}
, for all
j\in J
, and
{\mathcal{A}}
consists of all sets
A\subseteq X
such that
A\cap X_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
, for all
j\in J
.

Clearly, in this case
({\mathcal{A}},(d_{\tau_{i}})_{i\in I})
is isomorphic to the product of the derivative algebras
({\mathcal{A}}_{j},(d_{\tau_{ij}})_{i\in I})
. Since varieties of algebras are closed under products, if all
\mathcal{X}_{j}
are
\mathrm{GLP}
-ambiences, then so is
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 4

Let
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
denote the fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}
in the language with modalities
[i]
for
i\leqslant n
. Thus,
\mathrm{GL}
is a notational variant of
\mathrm{GLP}_{0}
. The notions of
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebra,
\mathrm{GLP}
-space,
\mathrm{GLP}
-ambience, etc. have obvious analogues for
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
, they will occasionally be used below.

2.5
Ranks and
d
-maps

Let
(X,\tau)
be a scattered space. The
Cantor–Bendixson sequence
of subsets of
X
is defined by transfinite iteration of the derivative operation
d_{\tau}
on
X
:
d_{\tau}^{0}X=X
;

d_{\tau}^{\alpha+1}X=d_{\tau}(d_{\tau}^{\alpha}X)
and

d_{\tau}^{\alpha}X=\bigcap\limits_{\beta<\alpha}d_{\tau}^{\beta}X
if
\alpha\in\mathrm{Lim}
.

Notice that if
X
is scattered, all
d_{\tau}^{\alpha}X
are closed and hence decreasing. In fact, Cantor proved that

(X,\tau)
is scattered iff
d_{\tau}^{\alpha}X=\varnothing
, for some
\alpha
.

The
rank function

\rho_{\tau}:X\to\mathrm{On}
is defined by

\rho_{\tau}(x):=\min\{\alpha:x\notin d_{\tau}^{\alpha+1}(X)\}.

\rho_{\tau}
maps
X
onto an ordinal
\rho_{\tau}(X)
called the Cantor–Bendixson rank of
X
.

Let
\Omega
be an ordinal. Its
left
topology

\tau_{\leftarrow}
is given by intervals of the form
[0,\alpha)
, for all
\alpha\in\Omega
. In this case we have
\rho(\alpha)=\alpha
, for all
\alpha
.

The
interval topology
on
\Omega
is generated by
\{0\}
and all intervals of the form
(\alpha,\beta)
for
\alpha<\beta\leqslant\Omega
. Its rank function is the function
\ell
defined by

\ell(0)=0;\quad\ell(\alpha)=\beta\text{ if $\alpha=\gamma+\omega^{\beta}$, for some
$\gamma$, $\beta$.}

Next we introduce
d
-maps that are very useful in the study of topological derivative semantics. A map
f:X\to Y
between topological spaces is called a
d-map
if
f
is continuous, open and
pointwise discrete
, that is,

f^{-1}(y)
is a discrete subspace of
X
for each
y\in Y
.

d
-maps satisfy the properties expressed in the
following proposition (see
[
19
]
).

Proposition 2.2

\mathrm{(i)}

f^{-1}(d_{Y}(A))=d_{X}(f^{-1}(A))
, for any
A\subseteq Y
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

f^{-1}:({\mathcal{P}}(Y),d_{Y})\to({\mathcal{P}}(X),d_{X})
is a homomorphism of modal algebras;

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
f
is onto, then
f^{-1}
is an embedding, and hence

\mathrm{(iv)}

\mathrm{Log}(({\mathcal{P}}(X),d_{X}))\subseteq\mathrm{Log}(({\mathcal{P}}(Y),d_{Y}))
.

When considering analogs of
d
-maps between ambiances, the relevant notion is
algebra preserving
d
-map
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X,(\tau_{i})_{i\in I},{\mathcal{A}})
and
\mathcal{Y}=(Y,(\sigma_{i})_{i\in I},{\mathcal{B}})
be ambiances. A map
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is called
algebra preserving
if

f^{-1}(B)\in{\mathcal{A}}
, for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. It is called a
d
-map, if it is a
d
-map between
(X,\tau_{i})
and
(Y,\sigma_{i})
, for all
i\in I
.

Proposition 2.3

If
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is an algebra preserving
d
-map, then

\mathrm{(i)}

f^{-1}:({\mathcal{B}},(d_{\sigma_{i}})_{i\in I})\to({\mathcal{A}},(d_{\tau_{i}})_{i\in I})
is a homomorphism;

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
f
is onto, then
f^{-1}:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is an embedding, and hence

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathrm{Log}(\mathcal{X})\subseteq\mathrm{Log}(\mathcal{Y})
.

The following proposition
[
16
]
states that the rank function, when the ordinals
are equipped with their left topology, becomes a
d
-map. It is also
uniquely characterized by this property.

Proposition 2.4

Let
\Omega:=\rho_{\tau}(X)
taken
with its left topology. Then

\mathrm{(i)}

\rho_{\tau}:X\twoheadrightarrow\Omega
is an onto
d
-map;

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
f:X\to\lambda
is a
d
-map, where
\lambda
is an ordinal with its left topology, then
f(X)=\Omega
and
f=\rho_{\tau}
.

Definition 5

3
Periodic sets of ordinals

3.1
Transfinite words and periodic sets

Here we develop some basic theory of transfinite words. We will only consider binary words in this paper, although the notions can be easily generalized to any alphabet.

Definition 6

A
(transfinite) word
is map
A:\alpha\to\{0,1\}
where
\alpha\in On
. The ordinal
\alpha
is called the length of
A
and denoted
|A|
. We also call
A
an
\alpha
-word
. The empty word (the only word of length
0
) is denoted
\Lambda
.

Concatenation
of transfinite words
A
and
B
is defined in a natural way and is denoted
AB
. This operation obviously satisfies
|AB|=|A|+|B|
,
A(BC)=(AB)C
.

A word
A
is an
initial segment
of
B
if there is a
C
such that
AC=B
. Similarly,
A
is an end segment of
B
if there is a
C
such that
CA=B
.

Iteration

A^{\beta}
of a transfinite word

is defined by transfinite induction on
\beta

A^{0}:=\Lambda
,
A^{\beta+1}:=A^{\beta}A
,
A^{\lambda}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}A^{\beta}
if
\lambda\in\mathrm{Lim}
.

Clearly,
|A^{\beta}|=|A|\cdot\beta
. We also have
A^{\beta}A^{\gamma}=A^{\beta+\gamma}
and
(A^{\beta})^{\gamma}=A^{\beta\gamma}
.

Next we are going to introduce one of the main concepts in this paper, that of a periodic word. We do not intend to treat this notion in full generality, but we deal with a somewhat restricted concept suitable for our purposes. In particular, in our version a periodic word must always contain
\lambda
-many periods, for some limit ordinal
\lambda
. Thus, for example, a word of the form
AAA
is not always periodic, but
A^{\omega}
is.

Definition 7

A word

X
is
periodic
if
X=A^{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathrm{Lim}
and some
A\neq\Lambda
. In this case,
A
is called a
period
of
X
and
\lambda
is called the
exponent
.

X
is
ultimately periodic
(u.p.) if
X=BY
for some
Y,B
with
Y
periodic. Periods of
X
are, by definition, periods of
Y
for any such representation.
B
is called an
offset
of
X
.

We remark that these definitions agree with the standard concepts of periodic and ultimately periodic
\omega
-words.

Given an ordinal
\Omega
, there is a natural one-to-one correspondence (depending on
\Omega
) between transfinite binary words of length
\Omega
and subsets of
\Omega
: We associate with a set
X\subseteq\Omega
its characteristic function
\Omega\to\{0,1\}
, and with a word
A:\Omega\to\{0,1\}
the set
\{\alpha\in\Omega:A(\alpha)=1\}
. Thereby, all the notions introduced so far for transfinite words are also meaningful for sets.

Definition 8

We call a set
X

periodic/u.p. in

\Omega
if so is the
\Omega
-word associated with the subset
X\cap\Omega
of
\Omega
.

Thus, we extend the terminology to sets
X
not necessarily contained in
\Omega
. Clearly, this makes sense only for limit ordinals
\Omega
(otherwise there are no periodic sets in
\Omega
).

In general, period of a word, if it exists, is not unique. For example, we obviously have

5
Logic
J_{n}
and
J_{n}
-morphisms

This section presents the technical notion of
J_{n}
-morphism from
[
15
]
. This notion allows us to map general topological frames to Kripke models of logic
J_{n}
in such a way that the validity of formulas is preserved. It is essentially the same as the notion of
J_{n}
-morphism from
[
15
]
with the exception that we now also require backwards preservation of admissible sets. This requirement is relatively minor, so our presentation is almost literally taken from
[
15
]
and all the results in this section are from that paper.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{n}
denote the modal language with modalities

[0],[1],\dots,[n]
. Recall that we denote by
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
the restriction of
\mathrm{GLP}
to
\mathcal{L}_{n}
.
While
\mathrm{GLP}
and
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
for
n>0
are Kripke incomplete, the following subsystem
\mathrm{J}
serves as a best approximation to
\mathrm{GLP}
which is. An axiomatization of
\mathrm{J}
is obtained by replacing Axiom (
v
) by two theorems of
\mathrm{GLP}
:

\mathrm{(i)}

[m]\varphi\rightarrow[n][m]\varphi
, for
m\leqslant n
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

[m]\varphi\to[m][n]\varphi
, for
m\leqslant n
.

We denote by
\mathrm{J}_{n}
the fragment of
\mathrm{J}
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
.

Definition 10

The class of
J_{n}
-trees
, which are finite Kripke frames of the form
(W,R_{0},\dots,R_{n})
, is defined inductively as follows.

1.

J_{0}
-trees are finite irreflexive transitive tree-like Kripke frames
(W,R_{0})
;

2.

If
{\mathcal{W}}=(W,R_{0},\dots,R_{n})
is a
J_{n}
-tree, then
{\mathcal{W}}^{+}:=(W,\varnothing,R_{0},\dots,R_{n})
is a
J_{n+1}
-tree;

3.

If
{\mathcal{W}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{W}}_{k}
are
J_{n}
-trees and
{\mathcal{V}}
is a
J_{n-1}
-tree, then the structure
{\mathcal{W}}=(W,R_{0},\dots,R_{n})
is a
J_{n}
-tree, where
{\mathcal{W}}
is obtained from the disjoint sum of structures
{\mathcal{V}}^{+}
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{W}}_{k}
by extending
R_{0}
with all pairs
(v,w)
for
v\in V
and
w\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}
.

We call
J
-trees
the structures that are
J_{n}
-trees for some
n\in\omega
. We can view them as Kripke frames
(W,(R_{i})_{i\in\omega})
where all relations
R_{i}
for
i>n
are empty.

It is easy to check that the relations of a
J
-tree are irreflexive partial orders and satisfy the following properties:

•

R_{k}R_{m}\subseteq R_{\min(k,m)}
(polytransitivity);

•

R_{m}^{-1}R_{k}\subseteq R_{k}
, for
k<m
.

These conditions ensure that
\mathrm{J}_{n}
is sound w.r.t.
J_{n}
-trees. The following proposition is from
[
12
]
.

Proposition 5.1

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{J}_{n}
is complete w.r.t. the class of
J_{n}
-trees;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{J}
is complete w.r.t. the class of
J
-trees.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}=(T,R_{0},\dots,R_{n})
be a
J_{n}
-tree and let
R^{*}_{k}
denote
\bigcup_{i=k}^{n}R_{i}
. By the polytransitivity,
R^{*}_{k}
is transitive.
An
R^{*}_{k}
-minimal node

is called a
hereditary
k
-root
. Since
{\mathcal{T}}
is a
J_{n}
-tree, for each
w\in T
and each
k\leqslant n
there exists a hereditary
k
-root
v\in T
such that
v=w
or

vR_{k}w
. We call the
root
of
{\mathcal{T}}
its hereditary
0
-root.

As any Kripke model, we can view
{\mathcal{T}}
as a poly-topological space
{\mathcal{T}}=(T,\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n})
by considering all
R_{i}
-upsets to be
\sigma_{i}
-open.

Definition 11

Given a poly-topological space
{\mathcal{X}}=(X,\tau_{0},\dots,\tau_{n})
and a map

we say that

is a
J_{n}
-morphism
if:

(
j_{1}
)

f:(X,\tau_{n})\to(T,\sigma_{n})
is a
d
-map;

(
j_{2}
)

f:(X,\tau_{k})\to(T,\sigma_{k})
is an open map for all
k\leqslant n
;

(
j_{3}
)

For each
k<n
and each hereditary
(k+1)
-root
w\in T
, the sets
f^{-1}(R_{k}^{*}(w))
and
f^{-1}(R_{k}^{*}(w)\cup\{w\})
are open in
\tau_{k}
;

(
j_{4}
)

For each
k<n
and each hereditary
(k+1)
-root
w\in T
, the set
f^{-1}(w)
is a
\tau_{k}
-discrete subspace of
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Here
R_{k}^{*}(w)
denotes the set
\{u\in T:wR_{k}^{*}u\}
. Notice that the sets
R_{k}^{*}(w)
and
R_{k}^{*}(w)\cup\{w\}
are
\sigma_{i}
-open for all

, therefore condition (

) is a weakening of the continuity of
f:(X,\tau_{k})\to(T,\sigma_{k})
.

If
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is a
J_{n}
-morphism and
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{A}})
is an ambience on
{\mathcal{X}}
, then

is called

if
f^{-1}(A)\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all

. (We consider
{\mathcal{T}}
as an ambience where all subsets are admissible.) Since

is finite, this requirement is equivalent to

, for all

.

Let
\varphi
be a formula in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
. Define:

M(\varphi):=\bigwedge_{i<s}\bigwedge_{k=m_{i}+1}^{n}([m_{i}]\varphi_{i}\rightarrow[k]\varphi_{i}),

where
[m_{i}]\varphi_{i}
,

, are
all subformulas of

of the form

and

. Also, let

.
These formulas provide a well-known translation from
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
to
\mathrm{J}_{n}
. Clearly,

.

The following theorem allows one to reduce the completeness proof for

to a covering construction. It is obtained by the same proof as in
[

]
for the situation of ambiences. For the reader’s convenience, we reproduce the proof of this theorem in the Appendix.

Theorem 1

be an ambience,

a

-tree,

-morphism. Then, for all

-formulas

{\mathcal{X}}\vDash\varphi
implies
{\mathcal{T}}\vDash M^{+}(\varphi)\to\varphi
.

Covering lemma

Recall that
\varepsilon_{0}
is the supremum of
the countable ordinals
\omega_{k}
recursively defined by
\omega_{0}=1
and
\omega_{k+1}=\omega^{\omega_{k}}
. The crucial point of the completeness proof is the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
(covering)

For each
{J}_{n}
-tree
{\mathcal{T}}
there exist an ordinal
\lambda<\omega_{n+2}
and an onto algebra preserving
{J}_{n}
-morphism

f:{\mathcal{X}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
where

{\mathcal{X}}=([1,\lambda],\vartheta_{0},\dots,\vartheta_{n},\mathcal{H}_{n})
.

Of course, the ambience
{\mathcal{X}}
is isomorphic to
(\Omega,\vartheta_{0},\dots,\vartheta_{n},\mathcal{H}_{n})
, where
\Omega=\lambda+1
if
\lambda
is infinite, otherwise
\Omega=\lambda
, but it will be more convenient in the inductive construction below to deal with the intervals of the form
[1,\lambda]
.
For the proof we need a few preparatory lemmas. The first one covers the case
n=0
.

Lemma 6.2

For each finite tree
{\mathcal{T}}=(T,R_{0})
with the root
r
there is an ordinal
\lambda<\omega^{\omega}
and an onto algebra preserving
d
-map
f:{\mathcal{X}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
where
{\mathcal{X}}=([1,\lambda],\vartheta_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0})
and
f^{-1}(r)=\{\lambda\}
.

Proof.
The construction of such a
d
-map is well-known, see e.g.
[
21
]
. The essentially new claim is that the
d
-map is algebra preserving. We construct

by induction on the height of
{\mathcal{T}}
. If
ht({\mathcal{T}})=0
, then we put
\lambda=1
. If

{\mathcal{T}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{T}}_{k}
denote the immediate subtrees of
{\mathcal{T}}

r_{1},\dots,r_{k}

d

g_{i}^{-1}(r_{i})=\lambda_{i}

where

is the disjoint sum of the trees
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}

([1,\mu],\vartheta_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0})
is isomorphic to the sum of ambiences
([1,\lambda_{i}],\vartheta_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0})
, so

is defined as the sum of maps

and is an algebra preserving
d
-map. Then we put
\lambda:=\mu\omega
and define
f(\lambda):=r
and

if

, where

and

Obviously,
f:[1,\lambda]\twoheadrightarrow T
. The interval
[1,\lambda)
is isomorphic to the sum of
\omega

[1,\mu]
, and the restriction of

to each copy is the same as

To verify that

. It follows that

A^{\omega}
is hereditarily periodic in
\lambda
.

, and
\mathcal{H}
.

Further, as in base case, consider the subset

By Lemma

\ell

d

-morphism.

-morphism

\vartheta_{0}
-open in

\vartheta_{1}
-open, hence
\vartheta_{i}

, for all

4.4
applies.)

Claim 1:

f
is open for each
\vartheta_{i}
,
i\leqslant n
.

\vartheta_{i}
-open set

\vartheta_{i}
-open, therefore so is

-morphism,

\sigma_{i}
-open. This holds for all

\sigma_{i}
-open.

\vartheta_{i}
-open, then
A\cap L

\vartheta_{i}

-open, hence so is

.

f:([1,\lambda],\vartheta_{n})\to(T,\sigma_{n})

\sigma_{n}
-open, then so are

and

\sigma_{i}
-open for

-morphism, the set

is
\vartheta_{n}
-open in

. Then
\nu\cdot B
is
\vartheta_{n}
-open in
L
. Since
L
is a
\vartheta_{1}
-open subset of
[1,\lambda]
and

, we conclude that
f^{-1}(A\cap Q)=\nu\cdot B
is also
\vartheta_{n}
-open.

Similarly,

\vartheta_{i}
-open, since

is continuous. Hence, so is the set

\vartheta_{n}
-open. Thus, we have proved that

-open.

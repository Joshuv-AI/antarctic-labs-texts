On bicrossed product of fusion categories and exact factorizations
Monique Müller, Héctor Martín Peña Pollastri, Julia Plavnik

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a matched pair of fusion categories (fusion rings), generalizing the one for groups. Using this concept, we define the bicrossed product of fusion categories (fusion rings) and construct exact factorizations for them. This concept generalizes the bicrossed product of groups, also known as the external Zappa-Szép product. We also show that every exact factorization of fusion rings can be presented as a bicrossed product. With this characterization, we describe the adjoint subcategory and universal grading group of an exact factorization of fusion categories. We give explicit fusion rules and associativity constraints for examples of fusion categories arising as a bicrossed product of combinations of Tambara-Yamagami and pointed fusion categories.



1.
Introduction

Fusion categories are rich mathematical objects with many connections to different areas of mathematics and physics. In the foundational work of Moore and Seiberg
[
11
]
, they proposed that conformal quantum field theory can be viewed as a generalization of group theory and that the correct way to axiomatize quantum field theory is with what we call today modular fusion categories. Other applications of fusion categories involve the construction of knot invariants as done in
[
17
]
by Turaev and Viro and
[
14
]
by Reshetikhin and Turaev.

To study any algebraic (or mathematical) object, it is of major importance to construct new examples from known ones and to understand how complex objects can be decomposed into simpler ones. As fusion categories can be thought of as generalizations of finite groups, a very fruitful strategy is to mimic notions from group theory in the fusion categorical settings. One such notion is that of exact factorization of groups that generalize the notions of direct and semidirect products. A group
G
is said to be an exact factorization of two subgroups
H
and
K
if
G=HK
and
H\cap K=\{e\}
. This notion was defined for fusion categories by Gelaki in
[
7
, Definition 3.4]
and further studied for tensor categories in
[
1
]
.

A fusion category can be thought of as its Grothendieck ring, which is a fusion ring, plus associativity coherence data. In this article, we provide answers to both of the following natural questions.

Question 1
.

What is the fusion ring of an exact factorization of fusion categories?

Question 2
.

How can we construct exact factorizations of fusion categories?

For Question
1
, our approach is to generalize the answer to a similar question for the group case. Exact factorizations of groups
G=H\bullet K
are completely characterized by actions
\blacktriangleright\colon K\times H\to H
and
\blacktriangleleft\colon K\times H\to K
satisfying certain conditions (see Subsection
3.1
). The tuple
(H,K,\blacktriangleright,\blacktriangleleft)
is called a matched pair of groups, and an exact factorization of groups can be recovered from these data using a construction called the bicrossed product
H\bowtie K
of groups, or the Zappa–Szép product. Every exact factorization of groups can be obtained like this. One of the contributions of this work is the generalization of the previously mentioned notions to the context of fusion categories. A key insight that enabled this generalization is the central role played by the universal grading group of a fusion category in controlling the structure of an exact factorization. More specifically, it was possible to define actions that depend only on the graded component in which an object lives, that is, have a (grading) group acting on each of the categories. This can already be seen at the level of the Grothendieck ring, that is, at the level of fusion rings.

We define the notion of matched pair of fusion rings that generalizes the one from groups (Definition
3.10
) using the notion of grading of a fusion ring. In addition, we construct an exact factorization of fusion rings from this data via what we call the bicrossed product of fusion rings (Theorem
3.13
). Moreover, we prove that every exact factorization of fusion rings is obtained like this (Theorem
3.14
). This completes the answer to Question
1
.

As an application of this characterization, we obtain results about every exact factorization of fusion categories related to concepts that depend only on the fusion ring. For example, we prove that an exact factorization
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent if and only if
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
are nilpotent (Corollary
3.26
). In addition, we show that the universal grading group of
\mathcal{B}
is given by an exact factorization of the universal grading groups of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
, and that there is an exact factorization of the adjoint subcategories
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
(Proposition
3.25
) for these last two.

To answer Question
2
, we generalize the notion of a matched pair of fusion rings to get a definition of matched pair of fusion categories, see Definition
4.1
. In analogy to the group setting, with the data of a matched pair of fusion categories, we define the notion of
bicrossed product of fusion categories
, which is an exact factorization of fusion categories. Hence, the following question is natural.

Question 3
.

Is every exact factorization of fusion categories given by a bicrossed product? If not, how much can the associativities differ from the one given by the bicrossed product?

We know that every exact factorization of fusion rings is given by a bicrossed product of fusion rings, and for the fusion categories setting, the question is still open as we pointed out in Remark
4.16
.

As an application of the bicrossed product of fusion categories, we construct exact factorizations of fusion categories between a Tambara-Yamagami fusion category and a pointed one, and between two Tambara-Yamagami fusion categories. We emphasize that explicit formulas for the associator data can be obtained from this construction.

Organization of the article

In Section
2
, we introduce the relevant definitions and notation. Section
3
is about the exact factorization of fusion rings and is divided into three main subsections. Subsection

defines the notion of a matched pair for fusion rings, and from these data we construct exact factorizations of fusion rings; we call this construction the bicrossed product, this is Theorem

. Subsection

is entirely dedicated to the proof of Theorem
3.14
, which shows that every exact factorization of fusion rings is given by a bicrossed product. Finally, Subsection

shows some direct applications of this characterization to the general structure of exact factorizations of fusion categories.
Section

provides categorifications of the ideas presented in Section
3
, we define the notion of a matched pair of fusion categories (Definition
4.1
) and the bicrossed product of fusion categories, see Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
4.9
. We also discuss how pivotal and spherical structures of fusion categories are induced in the bicrossed product, see Propositions
4.11
and
4.12
.
Section
5
is about applications of the construction from Section
4
to generate examples of fusion categories, the fusion rings of exact factorizations between a Tambara-Yamagami fusion category and a pointed fusion category, and between two Tambara-Yamagami fusion categories are fully characterized (Theorems
5.1
and
5.3
). Their categorifications as a bicrossed product of fusion categories are characterized in Theorems
5.2
and

. Explicit fusion rules and associators are computed for these examples.

Preliminaries

In this article, we denote by
\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. If
\ell\leq n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we set
\mathbb{I}_{\ell,n}=\{\ell,\ell+1,\dots,n\}
,
\mathbb{I}_{n}=\mathbb{I}_{0,n}

2.1.
Monoidal and fusion categories

In this subsection, we recall some definitions and fix notation. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for a more detailed exposition.

A
monoidal category

\mathcal{C}
is a category equipped with a bifunctor
\otimes\colon\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
together with an unit object
\mathbf{1}
, and natural isomorphisms
\ell_{X}\colon\mathbf{1}\otimes X\to X
,
r_{X}\colon X\otimes 1\to X
,
\alpha_{X,Y,Z}\colon(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\to X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)
satisfying pentagon and triangle axioms for all objects
X,Y,Z
of
\mathcal{C}
, see
[
2
, Definition 2.1.1]
for details.

A
monoidal functor
between two monoidal categories
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
is a pair
(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{F}^{2})
, where
\mathbf{F}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}
is a functor and
\mathbf{F}^{2}_{X,Y}\colon\mathbf{F}(X)\otimes\mathbf{F}(Y)\to\mathbf{F}(X\otimes Y)
is a natural isomorphism in both variables such that
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{1})\simeq\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{F}^{2}
satisfies a commutative diagram, see
[
2
, Definition 2.4.1]
. A
monoidal natural transformation
between two monoidal functors
(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{F}^{2})
and
(\mathbf{F}^{\prime},(\mathbf{F}^{\prime})^{2})
is a natural transformation
\theta_{X}\colon\mathbf{F}(X)\to\mathbf{F}^{\prime}(X)
such that
(\mathbf{F}^{\prime})^{2}_{X,Y}\theta_{X\otimes Y}=(\theta_{X}\otimes\theta_{Y}){\mathbf{F}^{2}_{X,Y}}
, for all
X
,
Y\in\mathcal{A}
.

In this article, the categories with which we work are fusion, which means they are abelian,
\Bbbk
-linear, monoidal, rigid, finite, and semisimple, and the unit is simple, see
[
2
, Definition 4.1.1]
. The functors are also required to preserve the relevant structure, so they are additive,
\Bbbk
-linear and exact. The functors are monoidal only when indicated.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
Since the category
\mathcal{C}
is semisimple, the tensor product of two objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
can be decomposed as

(1)

\displaystyle X\otimes Y\simeq\bigoplus_{Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}N_{X,Y}^{Z}Z,

where
N_{X,Y}^{Z}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Z,X\otimes Y)
is the multiplicity of the object
Z
in the tensor product of
X
and
Y
.

2.2.
Fusion rings

One fundamental invariant in understanding a fusion category is their Grothendieck ring. This is a special kind of ring, whose properties have been axiomatized by the notion of
fusion ring
. In this subsection, we recall some definitions about this topic from
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
fusion ring
is a pair
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
, where
\mathsf{R}
is a ring with a fixed
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})=\{b_{0},\dots,b_{n}\}
, such that:

(i)

the
structure coefficients
of the multiplication are non-negative integers, that is,
b_{i}b_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}N_{i,j}^{k}b_{k}
for some
N_{i,j}^{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(ii)

b_{0}=1
is the unit of the ring,

(iii)

there is an involution
{}^{*}\colon\mathbb{I}_{n}\to\mathbb{I}_{n}
such that the structure coefficients satisfy

N_{i,j}^{0}=\begin{cases}1&if $j=i^{*}$ ,\\
0&otherwise,\end{cases}

and the induced map given by
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{n}}a_{i}b_{i}\mapsto x^{*}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{n}}a_{i}b_{i^{*}}
is an anti-automorphisms of rings.

Let
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
be a fusion ring with the basis
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})=\{b_{0},\dots,b_{n}\}
. For
i,j,k\in\mathbb{I}_{n}
, we denote by
N_{b_{i},b_{j}}^{b_{k}}\coloneqq N_{i,j}^{k}
.

Example 2.2
.

The
Grothendieck ring
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
and product induced from the tensor product

\displaystyle CC^{\prime}=\sum_{C^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}N_{C,C^{\prime}}^{C^{\prime\prime}}C^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle C,C^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C}),

The involution is induced by the duality of the category. This is a fusion ring and we will denote it as
K_{0}(\mathcal{C)}
.

A
fusion subring

(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{S}))
is a fusion ring such that
\mathsf{S}
is a subring of
\mathsf{R}
and
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{S})\subseteq\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})
. A
based (left) module
for a fusion ring
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
is a
\mathsf{R}
-module
M
, free as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, endowed with a fixed basis
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
such that

\displaystyle b_{i}v_{j}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{J}}d_{i,j}^{k}v_{k},

for some
d_{i,j}^{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
d_{i,j}^{k}=d_{i^{*},k}^{j}
for all
j,k\in\mathcal{J}
and
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n}
.

The following important result proven originally in
[
13
]
about based modules will be used repeatedly in this article.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
13
, Lemma 1]
An indecomposable based module over a fusion ring is irreducible. In other words, based modules have the complete reducibility property, that is, every based module is a direct sum of irreducible based modules.

2.2.1.
Gradings of fusion rings

Let
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
be a fusion ring and
G
a group. We say that
\mathsf{R}
is
graded
by
G
if we have decompositions

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}=\sum_{g\in G}\mathsf{R}_{g},

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})=\bigsqcup_{g\in G}\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})_{g},

where
\mathsf{R}_{g}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-submodules generated by
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})_{g}
satifying
\mathsf{R}_{g}\mathsf{R}_{g^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathsf{R}_{gg^{\prime}}
and
\mathsf{R}_{g}^{*}=\mathsf{R}_{g^{-1}}
. Such
G
-grading is called
faithful
if
\mathsf{R}_{g}\neq 0
for all
g\in G
. A
G
-grading on
\mathsf{R}
induces a map
\operatorname{deg}:\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})\to G
,
b\mapsto\left|b\right|
such that if
xy
has
z
in their
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})
-decomposition one has

\left|z\right|=\left|x\right|\left|y\right|
.

In
[
8
]
, the authors proved that every fusion ring
\mathsf{R}
is faithfully graded by a group
U(\mathsf{R})
called the universal grading group. We first need the following definition to describe this grading in detail.

Definition 2.4
.

The fusion subring of
\mathsf{R}
generated by the elements
xx^{*}
, for
x\in\mathsf{R}
, is called the
adjoint fusion subring
and it is denoted
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
with basis
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}})\subseteq\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})
.

Remark 2.5
.

The object
I(1)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}b_{i}^{*}
generates
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
in the sense that every element of
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}})
is a summand of
I(1)^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A fusion ring
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
can be regarded as a (left) based module over
(\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}))
. This induces a decomposition
\mathsf{R}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathsf{R}_{j}
where the
\mathsf{R}_{j}
are indecomposable based
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
-modules by
[
13
, Lemma 1]
. The index set
\mathcal{J}
has a group structure described in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
8
, Theorem 3.5]

There is a canonical group structure in the index set
\mathcal{J}
with the multiplication defined by the following property for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{J}
:

\displaystyle ab

\displaystyle=c

if and only if

\displaystyle xy\in\mathsf{R}_{c},

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathsf{R}_{a},y\in\mathsf{R}_{b}.

The identity of
\mathcal{J}
is an element
1\in\mathcal{J}
such that
\mathsf{R}_{1}=\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
. The inverse of an element
j\in\mathcal{J}
is an element
j^{-1}
such that
\mathsf{R}_{j^{-1}}=\mathsf{R}_{j}^{*}
.

The index set
\mathcal{J}
with this group structure is called the
universal grading group
of
\mathsf{R}
and is denoted by
U(\mathsf{R})
. The decomposition
\mathsf{R}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathsf{R}_{j}
endows
\mathsf{R}
with a
U(\mathsf{R})
-grading. The following result shows why the adjective universal is appropriate for this group.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
8
, Corollary 3.7]
Every fusion ring
\mathsf{R}
has a canonical faithful grading by the group
U(\mathsf{R})
. Any other faithful grading of
\mathsf{R}
by a group
G
is determined by a surjective group homomorphism
\pi\colon U(\mathsf{R})\to G
.

2.3.
Gradings of fusion categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category and
G
a group. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is graded by
G
if
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{g}
are full abelian subcategories such that
\otimes:\mathcal{C}_{g}\times\mathcal{C}_{h}\to\mathcal{C}_{gh}
and
\mathcal{C}_{g}^{*}=\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}
. Such
G
-grading is called faithful if
\mathcal{C}_{g}\neq 0
for all
g\in G
. As in fusion rings, such
G
-grading on
\mathcal{C}
induces a map
|-|:\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})\to G
such that if
X\otimes Y
contains
Z
one has
\left|Z\right|=\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|
.

We define the
adjoint subcategory

\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
as the tensor subcategory generated by the elements
X\otimes X^{*}
for
X\in\mathcal{C}
. It is clear that
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})=K_{0}(\mathcal{C})_{\operatorname{ad}}
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between (faithful) gradings in
\mathcal{C}
and gradings in the fusion ring
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
.
Therefore every fusion category admits a faithful grading by
U(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq U(K_{0}(\mathcal{C}))
; this is the
universal grading group
of
\mathcal{C}
.

2.5.
Pointed fusion rings and fusion categories

A
pointed fusion ring
is a fusion ring
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B})
such that the Frobenius-Perron dimension of the elements in the basis is one, that is
\operatorname{FPdim}b=1
for all
b\in\mathsf{B}
, see
[
6
, Section 3.2]
for the definition of Frobenius-Perron dimension. A
pointed fusion category
is a fusion category whose fusion ring is a pointed fusion ring. All pointed fusion rings are completely determined by a finite group
G
, and the corresponding fusion ring is
\mathsf{R}(G)=\mathbb{Z}G
the integral group ring, with basis
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}(G))=G
. Pointed fusion categories are parametrized by a finite group
G
and a normalized
3
-cocycle
\omega\in H^{3}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
that determines the associativity, and are denoted as
\operatorname{vec}_{G}^{\omega}
. This category consists of finite dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces, simple objects are parametrized by elements
g\in G
and consist in one-dimensional vector spaces concentrated in degree
g
. The associativity natural isomorphism in the simples is
\alpha_{g,h,k}=\omega(g,h,k)\operatorname{id}_{ghk},
for
g,h,k\in G
.

Given a fusion ring
\mathsf{R}
(respectively fusion category
\mathcal{C}
), we denote the maximal pointed fusion subring (respectively subcategory) by
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{pt}}
(respectively
\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{pt}}
).

2.6.
Tambara-Yamagami fusion rings and fusion categories

In this subsection, we introduce a nice family of examples of fusion rings and fusion categories studied for the first time in
[
16
]
. It is also one of the few families where the pentagon equation for the associativity were solved explicitly. Let
\Gamma
be a group, the Tambara-Yamagami fusion ring
\mathsf{TY}(\Gamma)
is the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with basis
\Gamma\cup\{X\}
and multiplication given by

\displaystyle gX=Xg=X,\quad g\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle X^{2}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}g,

and the multiplication between group elements is the group multiplication. This ring admits a categorification only when
\Gamma
is abelian, the extra data needed is a non-degenerate symmetric bicharacter
\chi\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\Bbbk^{\times}
and
\tau\in\Bbbk
such that
\tau^{2}=1/|\Gamma|
. The corresponding fusion category is denoted as
\mathcal{TY}(\Gamma,\chi,\tau)
. The natural isomorphisms
\ell
and
r

3.
Exact factorization of fusion rings and fusion categories

In this section, we recall the definition and properties of exact factorization of fusion categories given by

3.1.

A

and

are groups,

\blacktriangleright

\blacktriangleleft

\textstyle{K}
\textstyle{K\times H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447pt\blacktriangleright}
\scriptstyle{\blacktriangleleft\hskip 8.19447pt}
\textstyle{H}

(4)

If the action

Remark 3.2

In a bicrossed product of groups

3.2.
Exact factorization of fusion categories

The following definition was first proposed in

Let

,
\mathcal{C}

The definition of exact factorization of fusion categories is symmetric in

The following example shows why this definition is a generalization of the definition of exact factorization for groups.

Exact factorizations

If a fusion category

then there is an induced exact factorization of its pointed subcategory

3.3.
Exact factorization of fusion rings

We propose the following definition for the exact factorization of fusion rings.

Let

Remark 3.8

If

is an exact factorization of fusion categories, then there is an associated exact factorization of its Grothendieck ring, that is,

is an exact factorization of fusion rings.

The next proposition is analogous to Remark

Proposition 3.9

Let
(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
be a fusion ring and
(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}))

(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}))
be fusion subrings. If

Proof.

We first show that the elements

and

. Since

are different for distinct

x\in K_{1},y\in K_{n-1}
such that
c
is a summand of
xy
, that is,
N_{x,y}^{c}\neq 0
. By the inductive hypothesis,
\ell_{x}=\ell_{y}=\operatorname{id}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.16
,
\ell_{c}=\ell_{x}\circ\ell_{y}=\operatorname{id}
.
∎

The next proposition shows that the maps
\ell_{c}
and
r_{a}
only depend on the degrees of
c
and
a
in
U(\mathsf{C})
and
U(\mathsf{A})
, respectively.

Proposition 3.19
.

Consider the universal grading of the fusion rings
\mathsf{A}=\sum_{h\in U(\mathsf{A})}\mathsf{A}_{h}
,
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})=\bigsqcup_{h\in U(\mathsf{A})}\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{h}
, and
\mathsf{C}=\sum_{k\in U(\mathsf{C})}\mathsf{C}_{k}
,
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})=\bigsqcup_{k\in U(\mathsf{C})}\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
.

(i)

If
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
then
\ell_{c}=\ell_{c^{\prime}}
, for every
k\in U(\mathsf{C})
.

(ii)

If
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{h}
then
r_{a}=r_{a^{\prime}}
, for every
h\in U(\mathsf{A})
.

Proof.

We only prove
(i)
as the proof of
(ii)
is similar. Let fix
k\in U(\mathsf{C})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
. Then
\mathsf{C}_{k}
is an indecomposable
\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
-module by definition of the universal grading. Now we define recursively:

\displaystyle L_{1}:=\{x\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}\colon x\text{ is a summand of }yc\text{ for some }y\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})\},

\displaystyle L_{n}:=\{x\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}\colon x\text{ is a summand of }yz\text{ for some }y\in\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}},z\in L_{n-1}\}.

Consider
L:=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}
, and the based
\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
-submodule
E:=\{\sum_{i}n_{i}x_{i}\colon n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},x_{i}\in L\}
of
\mathsf{C}_{k}
. Then
E=\mathsf{C}_{k}
and
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}=L
since based modules have the complete reducibility property
[
13
, Lemma 1]
and
\mathsf{C}_{k}
is indecomposable.

We will prove by induction on
n
that
\ell_{x}=\ell_{c}
, for all
x\in L=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}=\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{h}
. If
n=1
and
x\in L_{1}
, then there exists
y\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})
such that
x
is a summand of
yc
, that is,
N_{y,c}^{x}\neq 0
. Then, by Lemma
3.16
and Proposition
3.18
,
\ell_{x}=\ell_{y}\circ\ell_{c}=\ell_{c}
. If
n>1
and
x\in L_{n}
, then there exist
y\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})
and
z\in L_{n-1}
such that
x
is a summand of
yz
, that is,
N_{y,z}^{x}\neq 0
. The inductive hypothesis implies
\ell_{z}=\ell_{c}
, and by the same argument as in the base case,
\ell_{x}=\ell_{y}\circ\ell_{z}=\ell_{c}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion rings. Then there exist
\mathbb{Z}
-linear group left action
\mathbf{\triangleright}\colon U(\mathsf{C})\times\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}
and right action
\mathbf{\triangleleft}\colon\mathsf{C}\times U(\mathsf{A})\to\mathsf{C}
that map basic elements to basic elements and

(7)

\displaystyle ca=(\left|c\right|\triangleright a)(c\triangleleft\left|a\right|),

\displaystyle c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}),a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}).

Proof.

We define
\left|c\right|\triangleright a\coloneqq\ell_{c}(a)
and
c\triangleleft\left|a\right|\coloneqq r_{a}(c)
, for
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})
and
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})
, and extend them
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly. These functions are well defined by Proposition
3.19
and are group actions by Lemmas
3.15
and
3.16
, and Corollary
3.17
.
∎

We study now the structure of the adjoint subring
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
and the universal grading group
U(\mathsf{R})
in terms of the ones from
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{C}
.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion rings. Then there exist an exact factorization for the adjoint subring
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{A}}
(respectively
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{C}}
) be the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule generated by the elements
ac
with
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})
(respectively
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})
). Let
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}=\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{A}}\cap\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{C}}
.

Claim
.

The element
I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)=\sum_{b\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})}bb^{*}
is in
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}
.

Given that every
b\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})
can be written as

, with
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)=\sum_{b\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})}bb^{*}=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}\sum_{c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})}acc^{*}a^{*}=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}aI_{\mathsf{C}}(1)a^{*}.

Since
I_{\mathsf{C}}(1)=\sum_{j}c_{j}
, for some
c_{j}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})

\displaystyle I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}\sum_{j}ac_{j}a^{*}=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}\sum_{j}a(\left|c_{j}\right|\triangleright a^{*})(c_{j}\triangleleft\left|a^{*}\right|)=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}\sum_{j}aa^{*}(c_{j}\triangleleft\left|a^{*}\right|)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{A}},

where the second equality follows from Equation (
7
) and the third one from
\left|c_{j}\right|=e
and
aa^{*}\in\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}
.
A similar argument
shows that
I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}_{\mathsf{C}}
.

Claim
.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}
with basis
\mathsf{B}(\widetilde{\mathsf{R}})=\left\{ac\colon a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}_{ad}),c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{ad})\right\}
is a fusion subring of
\mathsf{R}_{ad}
.

If
b=ac
and
b^{\prime}=a^{\prime}c^{\prime}
are elements of
B(\widetilde{\mathsf{R}})
with
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}})
and
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}})
then

\displaystyle bb^{\prime}=aca^{\prime}c^{\prime}=a(\left|c\right|\triangleright a^{\prime})(c\triangleleft\left|a^{\prime}\right|)c^{\prime}=aa^{\prime}cc^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{R}},

where the second equality follows from Equation (
7
) and the last one from
\left|c\right|=e=\left|a^{\prime}\right|
.

Claim
.

\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}=\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
.

It follows directly from the definition of
\mathsf{B}(\widetilde{\mathsf{R}})
and from the fact that
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
that
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}=\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
. Since
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
is generated as a fusion ring by the element
I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)
and
I_{\mathsf{R}}(1)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}
, we have that
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}
is a fusion subring of
\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}
. Hence
\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathsf{R}}
and, since the other inclusion is clear, this concludes the proof.
∎

Let
(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A}))\subseteq(\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R}))
be a fusion subring and
H_{\mathsf{A}}:=\{h\in U(\mathsf{R})\colon\mathsf{A}\cap\mathsf{R}_{h}\neq 0\}
subgroup of
U(\mathsf{R})
then
\mathsf{A}=\sum_{h\in H_{\mathsf{A}}}\mathsf{A}_{h}
, where
\mathsf{A}_{h}:=\mathsf{A}\cap\mathsf{R}_{h}
is a faithful grading. See
[
12
, Section 2]
for details.
Because of the universality of
U(\mathsf{A})
(respectively
U(\mathsf{C})
), there is a surjective group homomorphism
\phi_{\mathsf{A}}:U(\mathsf{A})\to H_{\mathsf{A}}
(respectively
\phi_{\mathsf{C}}:U(\mathsf{C})\to H_{\mathsf{C}}
) with kernel denoted by
K_{\mathsf{A}}
(respectively
K_{\mathsf{C}}
).

If
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
is an exact factorization of fusion rings then
U(\mathsf{R})
is a factorization (not necessarily exact) of the groups
H_{\mathsf{A}}
and
H_{\mathsf{C}}
. Now, we will characterize the universal grading group
U(\mathsf{R})
of the exact factorization
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
.

Proposition 3.22
.

If
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
is an exact factorization of fusion rings then:

(1)

The morphisms
\phi_{\mathsf{A}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{C}}
are isomorphisms of groups.

(2)

There is an exact factorization of groups
U(\mathsf{R})\simeq U(\mathsf{A})\bullet U(\mathsf{C})
.

Proof.

The groups
H_{\mathsf{A}}
and
H_{\mathsf{C}}
form a factorization of
U(\mathsf{R})
, since for every
g\in U(\mathsf{R})
there is a basic element
b\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})_{g}
. Since
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
is exact, there exist unique
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})
such that
b=ac
. If
a
has degree
h\in H_{\mathsf{A}}
and
c
has degree
k\in H_{\mathsf{C}}
, then
g=hk
. Therefore,
U(\mathsf{R})=H_{\mathsf{A}}H_{\mathsf{C}}
is a factorization and, by applying the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem to the
H_{\mathsf{A}}
-action on the cosets
hH_{\mathsf{C}}
, we get
|U(\mathsf{R})|=\frac{|H_{\mathsf{A}}||H_{\mathsf{C}}|}{|H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|}
.
Also, since
\phi_{\mathsf{A}}:U(\mathsf{A})\to H_{\mathsf{A}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{C}}:U(\mathsf{C})\to H_{\mathsf{C}}
are epimorphisms with kernels
K_{\mathsf{A}}
and
K_{\mathsf{C}}
respectively, then
|H_{\mathsf{A}}|=\frac{|U(\mathsf{A})|}{|K_{\mathsf{A}}|}
and
|H_{\mathsf{C}}|=\frac{|U(\mathsf{C})|}{|K_{\mathsf{C}}|}
.
By considering Frobenius-Perron dimensions, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{|H_{\mathsf{A}}||H_{\mathsf{C}}|}{|H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{R}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\frac{|U(\mathsf{A})||U(\mathsf{C})|}{|K_{\mathsf{A}}||K_{\mathsf{C}}||H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{A}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{C}}{|K_{\mathsf{A}}||K_{\mathsf{C}}||H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|},

where the first and third equalities follow from
[
4
, Proposition 8.20]
and the second equality follows from Proposition
3.21
.
Since
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
then

\displaystyle\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{R}=\sum_{b\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{R})}(\operatorname{FPdim}b)^{2}=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}\sum_{c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{c})}(\operatorname{FPdim}a)^{2}(\operatorname{FPdim}c)^{=}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{A}\operatorname{FPdim}\mathsf{C}.

Therefore
|K_{\mathsf{A}}||K_{\mathsf{C}}||H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|=1
,
which implies
|K_{\mathsf{A}}|=|K_{\mathsf{C}}|=|H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}|=1
. Hence,
\phi_{\mathsf{A}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{C}}
are isomorphisms and the factorization
U({\mathsf{R}})=H_{\mathsf{A}}H_{\mathsf{C}}\simeq U(\mathsf{A})U(\mathsf{C})
is exact because
H_{\mathsf{A}}\cap H_{\mathsf{C}}\simeq U(\mathsf{A})\cap U(\mathsf{C})
is trivial.
∎

It follows from the previous proposition that every exact factorization of fusion rings
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
has associated an exact factorization of their universal grading groups
U(\mathsf{R})=U(\mathsf{A})\bullet U(\mathsf{C})
and therefore, a matched pair
(U(\mathsf{A}),U(\mathsf{C}),\blacktriangleright,\blacktriangleleft)
.

Proposition 3.23

(
Canonical matched pair of fusion rings
)
.

Let
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion rings.
The data below gives rise to a matched pair of fusion rings in the following way:

(i)

the grading of
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{C}
by their universal grading groups
U(\mathsf{A})
and
U(\mathsf{C})
,

(ii)

the associated matched pair of groups
(U(\mathsf{A}),U(\mathsf{C}),\blacktriangleright,\blacktriangleleft)
defined above,

(iii)

the group actions
\mathbf{\triangleright}\colon U(\mathsf{C})\times\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}
and
\mathbf{\triangleleft}\colon\mathsf{C}\times U(\mathsf{A})\to\mathsf{C}
from Corollary
3.20
.

We call this matched pair the
canonical matched pair
of fusion rings associated to the exact factorization
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

To prove that Definition
3.10
(ii)
holds, it is enough to show that
k\triangleright a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{k\blacktriangleright h}
and
c\triangleleft h\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k\blacktriangleleft h}
, for
h\in U(\mathsf{A})
,
k\in U(\mathsf{C})
,
a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{h}
, and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
. Identifying
U(\mathsf{A})
and
U(\mathsf{C})
as subgroups of
U(\mathsf{R})
via
\phi_{\mathsf{A}}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{C}}
, respectively, the element
ca
has degree
\left|ca\right|=\left|c\right|\left|a\right|=kh=(k\blacktriangleright h)(k\blacktriangleleft h)\in U(\mathsf{R})
.
On the other hand, since
ca=(k\triangleright a)(c\triangleleft h)
then
\left|ca\right|=\left|(k\triangleright a)(c\triangleleft h)\right|=\left|k\triangleright a\right|\left|c\triangleleft h\right|.

By the uniqueness of the decomposition of elements of
U(\mathsf{R})
in product of elements of
U(\mathsf{A})
and
U(\mathsf{C})
, we have
\left|k\triangleright a\right|=k\blacktriangleright h
and
\left|c\triangleleft h\right|=k\blacktriangleleft h
. Hence
k\triangleright\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{h}=\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})_{k\blacktriangleright h}
and
\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}\triangleleft h=\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k\blacktriangleleft h}
as desired.

To check that Definition
3.10

(iii)
holds, we consider

\displaystyle ca_{1}a_{2}

\displaystyle=(k\triangleright a_{1})(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|)a_{2}=(k\triangleright a_{1})(\left|c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\right|\triangleright a_{2})((c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|)\triangleleft\left|a_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle=(k\triangleright a_{1})((k\blacktriangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|)\triangleright a_{2})(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|),

for
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})
,
k\in U(\mathsf{C})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
.

Notice that if

then
\left|a\right|=\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|
and we get the alternative expression

\displaystyle ca_{1}a_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}N_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{a}(k\triangleright a)(c\triangleleft\left|a\right|)=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}N_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{a}(k\triangleright a)(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle=\left(k\triangleright\left(\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{A})}N_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{a}a\right)\right)(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|)=(k\triangleright(a_{1}a_{2}))(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|),

for

,
k\in U(\mathsf{C})
and
c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})_{k}
.

Then
(k\triangleright(a_{1}a_{2}))(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|)=(k\triangleright a_{1})((k\blacktriangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|)\triangleright a_{2})(c\triangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|\left|a_{2}\right|)
.
By uniqueness of the decomposition in basic elements, we have
k\triangleright(a_{1}a_{2})=(k\triangleright a_{1})((k\blacktriangleleft\left|a_{1}\right|)\triangleright a_{2}).

Similarly,
(c_{1}c_{2})\triangleleft h=(c_{1}\triangleleft(\left|c_{2}\right|\blacktriangleright h))(c_{2}\triangleleft h)
, for
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})
and

.

It follows from Lemma
3.15
that the condition in Definition
3.10
(iv)
holds.
∎

Now, we will prove the main theorem from this subsection.

Proof of Theorem
3.14

For the exact factorization
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}
, we consider the canonical matched pair of fusion rings associated to it, as in Proposition
3.23
, and the bicrossed product
\mathsf{A}\bowtie\mathsf{C}
, as in Definition
3.12
. We define the
\mathbb{Z}
-module map

on the basic elements

and

by

, which is an isomorphism since it sends basic elements into basic elements.
To finish the proof, we check below that

\displaystyle\psi\left((a_{1}\bowtie c_{1})(a_{2}\bowtie c_{2})\right)=\psi(a_{1}(\left|c_{1}\right|\triangleright a_{2})\bowtie(c_{1}\triangleleft\left|a_{2}\right|)c_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(A),c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})}N_{a_{1},\left|c_{1}\right|\triangleright a_{2}}^{a}N_{c_{1}\triangleleft\left|a_{2}\right|,c_{2}}^{c}\psi(a\bowtie c)=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{B}(A),c\in\mathsf{B}(\mathsf{C})}N_{a_{1},\left|c_{1}\right|\triangleright a_{2}}^{a}N_{c_{1}\triangleleft\left|a_{2}\right|,c_{2}}^{c}ac

\displaystyle=a_{1}(\left|c_{1}\right|\triangleright a_{2})(c_{1}\triangleleft\left|a_{2}\right|)c_{2}=a_{1}c_{1}a_{2}c_{2}=\psi\left(a_{1}\bowtie c_{1}\right)\psi\left(a_{2}\bowtie c_{2}\right),

for

and

∎

Corollary 3.24
.

Let
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{A}\bullet\mathsf{C}

This follows from Proposition

3.6.
Grothendieck invariants of exact factorizations of fusion categories

In this subsection, we present some results at the level of fusion categories that depend only on the underlying Grothendieck ring and, therefore, are direct consequence of the results in previous sections, since an exact factorization of fusion categories
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
induces an exact factorization of the corresponding fusion rings
K_{0}(\mathcal{B})=K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\bullet K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
.

The next proposition follows from Proposition
3.21
and Proposition
3.22
.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion categories then

(1)

there is an exact factorization of the adjoint subcategory
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
, and

(2)

there is an exact factorization
U(\mathcal{B})\simeq U(\mathcal{A})\bullet U(\mathcal{C})
of the universal grading group.

The
upper central series

\mathcal{B}^{(0)}=\mathcal{B}
of a fusion category is defined recursively as follows:
\mathcal{B}^{(1)}:=\mathcal{B}_{\text{ad}}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{(n)}:=(\mathcal{B}^{(n-1)})_{\text{ad}}
, for
n\geq 1
. This gives rise to a non-increasing sequence of fusion subcategories
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{(0)}\supseteq\mathcal{B}^{(1)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{B}^{(n)}\supseteq\cdots
of
\mathcal{B}
.
A fusion category
\mathcal{B}
is
nilpotent
if there exists
n
such that
\mathcal{B}^{(n)}=\operatorname{vec}
, see
[
8
, Definition 4.4]
.

The following corollary is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.21
.

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
be an exact factorization of fusion categories. Then
\mathcal{B}
is nilpotent if and only if
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
are nilpotent fusion categories.

Remark 3.27
.

The previous results allow us to recover every exact factorization of fusion categories
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
by extension theory (see
[
5
, Theorem 7.7]
). Indeed, by Proposition
3.25
such a factorization induces an exact factorization
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
for the adjoint subcategory with same fusion rules as the Deligne product
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
by Corollary
3.24
. Categories satisfying this last condition are discussed in a paper in preparation by P. Etingof, D. Nikshych, and V. Ostrik
[
3
]
. Then the category
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
is recovered as a
U(\mathcal{B})=U(\mathcal{A})\bullet U(\mathcal{C})
-extension of
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{ad}}=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{ad}}\bullet\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ad}}
. Then, by extension theory, we get exact factorizations
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
by multiplying the associativity of
\mathcal{B}
by a cocycle
\omega\in H^{3}(U(\mathcal{A})\bullet U(\mathcal{C}),\Bbbk^{\times})
. All exact factorizations with trivial component given
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{ad}}
and the same fusion rules as
\mathcal{B}
are of this form. Notice that the fusion rules of
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{C}
correspond to some bicrossed product of the fusion rings of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}

Given a matched pair of fusion categories

(i)

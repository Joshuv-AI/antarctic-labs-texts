A Deep Multi-Modal Cyber-Attack Detection in Industrial Control Systems
Sepideh Bahadoripour, Ethan MacDonald, Hadis Karimipour

Abstract
The growing number of cyber-attacks against Industrial Control Systems (ICS) in recent years has elevated security concerns due to the potential catastrophic impact. Considering the complex nature of ICS, detecting a cyber-attack in them is extremely challenging and requires advanced methods that can harness multiple data modalities. This research utilizes network and sensor modality data from ICS processed with a deep multi-modal cyber-attack detection model for ICS. Results using the Secure Water Treatment (SWaT) system show that the proposed model can outperform existing single modality models and recent works in the literature by achieving 0.99 precision, 0.98 recall, and 0.98 f-measure, which shows the effectiveness of using both modalities in a combined model for detecting cyber-attacks.

I
Introduction

ICS
(
ICS
)s are crucial for monitoring and controlling critical infrastructure such as smart grids, oil and gas industry, and transportation. Traditionally,
ICS
s were placed on isolated communications systems to keep them safe from cyber-attacks, where they were relatively inconspicuous and unknown to most attackers
[
1
]
.

The connection between
ICS
wide area networks facilitates the online access, monitoring, and control of the systems remotely. The drawback of wide area network integration is increased attack surfaces in ICS and increased vulnerability to cyber-criminals
[
2
]
. The Stuxnet campaign that targeted Iranian centrifuges for nuclear enrichment in 2010, and caused severe damage to the equipment is one high-profile example
[
3
]
. Several other prominent examples include: cyber-attacks affecting three U.S. gas pipeline companies in 2018
[
3
]
, and two U.S. water treatment systems in 2021
[
4
]
. Due to the complexity and sensitivity of the ICS environment, a compromise in the system may lead to severe danger to human life or the environment. Moreover, the growing number of cyber-attacks against
ICS
in recent years motivates the need for timely security solutions for these systems
[
5
]
.

Although security solutions developed for
IT
(
IT
) are mature, they are not sufficient due to differences between
ICS
and
IT

[
6
]
. While the
IT
networks mainly focus on managing the high throughput of a network,
ICS
networks have to function more towards carrying out tasks reliably and punctually. When failure occurs, the
ICS
network cannot merely be rebooted, like a typical computer network
[
7
]
. In addition,
ICS
introduces more security vulnerability due to the tight integration between the controlled physical environment and the cyber system
[
8
]
. Compared to the IT protocols, ICS protocols, including Modbus or DNP3, suffer from common security issues such as the absence of authentication, lack of protection or security measures for data transferring over the link, and insufficient control measures to avoid default broadcast approaches
[
7
]
.

Considering the vital role of
ICS
in critical infrastructural operations, various methods for cyber-threat detection and classification have been proposed in the literature to address the above-mentioned challenges. Model-based solutions, such as variants of state estimation techniques and statistical-based models, have been suggested in
[
9
]
. Additionally, the use of Kalman filters for measurement estimation is also proposed to detect cyber-attacks
[
10
]
. However, such model-based techniques are difficult to adapt to larger stochastic systems and various threats. Intelligent systems are thus proposed for detecting and classifying cyber-attacks
[
11
]
.

The current work proposes a multi-modal cyber-attack detection model to analyze sensor and network modalities of an
ICS
and detect cyber-attacks based on their joint abstract representation. Due to the different structures of sensor and network data, a complex deep neural network includes a partially connected stacked
LSTM
(
LSTM
) and neural network model, connected to a fully connected fusion network is proposed in this research. The proposed model is able to analyze each modality separately and then combine the key findings of each modality automatically using a deep neural network with a single training.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
II
introduces some works in the literature. Section
III
explains the proposed model, which is followed by the experimental setup in Section
IV
. Section
V
presents the evaluation results of the proposed model and compares it with some single modality models in the literature. Finally, Section
VI
concludes the paper.

ML
(
ML
)-based attack detection techniques work based on a moving target to continually evolve and learn new vulnerabilities rather than identifying the attack signatures or the regular pattern of the network
[
12
]
. There are different
ML
algorithms available in the literature to detect cyber-attacks compromising data integrity
[
13
]
, availability
[
14
]
, and confidentiality
[
2
]
.
ML
-based methods are categorized into two main categories, conventional and
DNN
(
DNN
).

II-A

Conventional Machine Learning-Based Cyber-Attack Detection

In work by Zolanvari et al.
[
2
]
, the authors compared several traditional
ML
algorithms, including
KNN
(
KNN
),
RF
(
RF
),
DT
(
DT
),
LR
(
LR
),
ANN
(
ANN
),
NB
(
NB
), and
SVM
(
SVM
) to detect cyber-attacks in a water storage system. Their evaluation showed that the
RF
algorithm is the best model with a recall of 0.9744, the
ANN
is the fifth algorithm with a recall of 0.8718, and the
LR
is the worst algorithm with a recall of 0.4744. They also reported that the
ANN
could not detect 12.82% of the attacks but considered 0.03% of the normal samples as an attack due to the imbalanced nature of the data. Based on the results, the authors considered many attack samples as normal samples without labeling so many normal samples as an attack happened for
LR
,
SVM
, and
KNN
. Therefore, these
ML
algorithms are sensitive to imbalanced data and unsuitable for
ICS
attack detection.

In 2019, Sakhnini et al.
[
15
]
proposed three feature selection methods, including binary cuckoo search, a genetic algorithm, and binary particle swarm optimization, which were analyzed to detect false data injection attack samples. The selected features were passed to
SVM
,
KNN
, and
ANN
models for classification. The evaluation shows that the
SVM
model outperforms the other classifiers in detecting cyber-attacks on three smart grid datasets.
Moreover, the evaluation illustrated that the genetic algorithm feature selection was the best at selecting a set of features among the feature selection methods.
In
[
16
]
, the authors proposed a
LAD
(
LAD
) method to extract patterns/rules from the sensor data and used them to design a two-step anomaly detection system. They compared the proposed
LAD
method with
DNN
,
SVM
, and
CNN
(
CNN
) models. The evaluations showed that the
DNN
outperformed the
LAD
method in the precision metric; however, the
LAD
performed better in recall and f-measure.
In
[
17
]
, a
SAE
(
SAE
)-based cyber-attack detection model for smart grid systems was proposed. This model was evaluated using three smart grid datasets and compared with conventional
ML
approaches. The evaluations showed that the proposed method outperformed all other methods in all the metrics.
In 2022, Mittal et al.
[
18
]
proposed a gravitational search algorithm-based clustering to analyze the
ICS
data and detect cyber-attacks. Their model was evaluated on several datasets and compared with some other search-based algorithms in the literature.

II-B

Deep Learning-Based Cyber-Attack Detection

In
[
19
]
, a deep cyber-attack detection method for real-time detection of false data injection attacks was proposed for smart grid systems. In this study, the effect of using different numbers of hidden layers on the model’s performance is analyzed. The evaluations showed that the accuracy of a deeper model is higher than the shallower ones.
Later work by Wang et al.
[
20
]
proposed a
DNN
state estimation method for cyber-attack detection. In this method, the state estimation was done using an
SAE
, and an attack was detected by comparing the estimated state with the actual one.
In
[
21
]
, the authors used autoencoders to detect cyber-attacks and restore injected data. Two types of autoencoders were used in this study. An undercomplete model was used for cyber-attack detection, and a denoising model was used to restore the attacked data. They compared this method to
SVM
-based cyber-attack detection approach and showed that their proposed method outperformed them in attack detection, especially the previous unseen attack samples.
Moreover, in
[
22
]
, the authors proposed a scalable deep cyber-attack detection for smart grids. This method was evaluated on a large-scale smart grid dataset and reported an accuracy of around 99% for all scenarios.

Moreover, in 2021, Althobaiti et al.
[
23
]
proposed a
GRU
(
GRU
)-based cognitive computing intrusion detection for
ICS
. In their proposed method, first, the optimal subset of features was selected using a feature selection approach. Then, a
GRU
model was applied to the selected features to detect cyber-attacks. Moreover,
NADAM
(
NADAM
) optimizer was used to increase the model’s accuracy.
In another manuscript, a novel deep representation learning approach was used to handle the challenge of imbalanced data in detecting cyber-attacks
[
24
]
. This model was evaluated using two
ICS
datasets and the results show the effectiveness of the proposed method.
In addition, in
[
25
]
, the authors proposed a
LSTM
encoder-decoder model to detect cyber-attacks. In this method, a raw network traffic dataset was used, and a feature extraction method was used to extract features from the raw data. The extracted features were then passed through an encoder-decoder model and, the reconstruction error of this model is used to detect cyber-attacks.
This method was evaluated using an
ICS
dataset that shows 97.95% accuracy, 0.98 precision, 0.9663 recall, and 0.9789 f-measure on the gas pipeline dataset.

In another study
[
26
]
, the authors proposed a
DNN
-based method to detect false data injection attacks. The chi-squared test was first performed on the dataset to assign a weight to each feature based on its correlation with the output. The weighted features were then passed through a
SAE
to generate new representations. Finally, the data in the latent space was used to build an ensemble of randomized
DT
s. The majority voting method is used to detect cyber-attacks, and was evaluated on four power system datasets and compared with conventional
ML
methods. The results illustrate that the DNN method outperforms the others in detecting cyber-attacks.
Recently, federated learning-based cyber-attack detection approaches have increased in popularity due to the ability to preserve privacy of
ICS
data by training the models locally inside the clients and share the model parameters with a server to build a global model for all the clients
[
27
,
28
]
. In another study, Kravchik and Shabtai
[
29
]
proposed a lightweight deep model to detect
ICS
cyber-attacks. They used 1D
CNN
and undercomplete
AE
(
AE
) to detect cyber-attacks using sensor data. Their experiments on the
SWaT
(
SWaT
) dataset showed that their
CNN
model achieved 0.90 precision, 0.87 recall, and 0.88 f-measure while their
AE
model gained 0.96 precision, 0.93 recall, and 0.94 f-measure.

While the existing
ML
-based approach can achieve acceptable results in detecting cyber-attacks in
ICS
, to the best of the authors, none of them consider the multi-modality of
ICS
environments in their solutions. To handle this gap, this work proposes a multi-modal cyber-attack detection component that used sensor and network modalities of
ICS
environment to detect cyber-attacks more accurately.

III
The Proposed Deep Multi-Modal Cyber-Attack Detection Model

In the proposed model, a multi-modal
DNN
, including network and sensor modalities, was used to detect cyber-attacks in real time.
For each sample in sensor data, several network samples are available that show the transmitted packets in the
ICS
network between
IT
and
OT
(
OT

LSTM
model is used to analyze the network data sequence and map it to the network latent space. These two new representations are then fused into a fully connected fusion network and finally classified using a single layer. Figure

In the training step, the sensor part of each sample

III-B

Network Model

In each

LSTM

In the training process, the network part of each sample

h_{N}^{t}=f(Ux_{N}^{t},Wh_{N}^{t-1})

o_{N}^{t}=f(Vh_{N}^{t})

(4)

In the proposed network modality model, three

LSTM

III-C

Fusion and Classification Model

After mapping the original features from each modality to a latent space, these two new representations are then passed to another fully connected deep model to fuse them. The output of this model is a joint representation of both modalities that consider the main abstract information in them.

Sensor and network representations,

h=\sigma((h_{S},o_{N}),W)

IV
Experimental Setup

To evaluate the proposed model, the
SWaT
dataset
[
30
]
, which includes both network and sensor modalities, was used. The

In this dataset, 87.9% and 12.1% were normal and attack samples, respectively. Sensor modality includes 51 features, each showing a sensor measurement or an actuator status. On the other hand, network

ICS

The mentioned data is pre-processed before passing it to the model. First, the mean value of each feature is used to impute the missing values. Then, equation

x_{normalized}=\frac{x-min(x)}{max(x)-min(x)}

IV-B

Evaluation Metrics

The proposed model is compared with several recent studies in the literature. Similar to other approaches, we used standard ML metrics to evaluate the performance of the proposed model. The basic
ML
evaluation metrics are:

•

True Positive (TP)– Number of correctly classified samples as attack.

•

True Negative (TN)– Number of correctly classified samples as normal.

•

False Positive (FP)– Number of wrongly classified samples as attack.

•

False Negative (FN)– Number of wrongly classified samples as normal.

Based on these basic metrics, more complicated metrics are defined that are used in this work, including:

•

Precision
indicates the number of samples that are detected correctly as attack over total samples detected as attack (see equation
10
).

•

Recall
indicates the number of samples that are detected correctly as attack over the total samples of the attack in the dataset (see equation
11
).

•

F1-Score
is the harmonic value of precision and recall (see equation
12
).

Due to the use of imbalanced data in this work, accuracy metric is not considered for comparison.

Precision=\dfrac{TP}{TP+FP}

(10)

Recall=\dfrac{TP}{TP+FN}

(11)

F1-Score=\dfrac{2\times Precision\times Recall}{Precision+Recall}

(12)

V
Results and Discussion

The proposed multi-modal cyber-attack detection model was evaluated using an
ICS
dataset consisting of a sensor and network modalities. The use of multiple modalities of data in the proposed model allows for a more comprehensive analysis of the ICS environment, which can improve the robustness and accuracy of cyber-attack detection. However, the utilization of different data modalities also poses certain challenges, such as the need to synchronize the datasets before training the model. This process can be time-consuming, especially when dealing with large datasets, and is considered the main limitation of the proposed model.

Table
I
shows the experimental results of evaluating the proposed model and its comparison with the single modality models and two existing works in the literature. As illustrated in this table, the proposed multi-modal model achieved a precision of 0.99, recall of 0.98, and f-measure of 0.98, which outperformed the other methods in all metrics. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of using both sensor and network modalities in a single model for cyber-attack detection in ICS environments.
While the single modal models in Table
I
gain a high precision, they cannot achieve a good recall due to the use of imbalanced data that caused a high number of evaded attacks from the model. However, the proposed model can handle this challenge and achieve a high recall that shows the effectiveness of using both modalities in a single model. On the other hand, LAD-ADS
[
16

and CNN
[

]
models achieved a reasonable recall score by modifying the model that sacrificed the precision score, resulting in a higher number of false alarms of these models, which is handled in the proposed model.

VI
Conclusion

In this project, a multi-modal cyber-attack detection model is proposed to analyze sensor and network modalities of
ICS
environments and build an abstract joint representation based on these modalities. In the proposed model, a fully connected
DNN
is used to map the sensor modality to the sensor latent space, while a stacked
LSTM
model is used to encode the time-series network data into network latent space.
The evaluation results show that the proposed model outperformed all other models with 0.99 precision, 0.98 recall, and 0.98 f-measure metrics. Moreover, the proposed model has low false alarms and evaded attack samples that show its ability to handle imbalanced data, which shows the effectiveness of using different modalities in the proposed model.

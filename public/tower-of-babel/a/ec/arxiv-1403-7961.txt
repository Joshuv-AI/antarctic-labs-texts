Phase Transitions in Ferromagnetic Ising Models with spatially dependent magnetic fields
Rodrigo Bissacot

Abstract
In this paper we study the nearest neighbor Ising model with ferromagnetic interactions in the presence of a space dependent magnetic field which vanishes as | x | − α |x|^{-\alpha} , α > 0 \alpha>0 , as | x | → ∞ |x|\to\infty . We prove that in dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 for all β \beta large enough if α > 1 \alpha>1 there is a phase transition while if α < 1 \alpha<1 there is a unique DLR state.

Introduction

The Ising Model is one of the most studied subjects in Statistical Physics and will complete a century in a few years
1
1

1

Wilhelm Lenz introduced the model in 1920.
.
The literature about ferromagnetic Ising models on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, is mainly focused on cases where the external magnetic field is constant. We will study ferromagnetic nearest neighbor hamiltonians of the form

\displaystyle H^{w}_{\Lambda}(\sigma)=-J\sum_{|x-y|=1,x,y\in\Lambda}\sigma(x)\sigma(y)-\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)\sigma(x)-J\sum_{|x-y|=1,x\in\Lambda,y\notin\Lambda}\sigma(x)w(y)

\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\Lambda}

\Lambda

When the magnetic field

is constant, that is

the existence of a phase transition. If instead

a unique DLR measure, as it follows from the Lee-Yang Theory and GHS inequalities.
The absence of phase transitions comes from the differentiability
of the free energy with respect to the parameter

Alternating signs fields on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

are considered in
[
15
]
,
constant fields on semi-infinite lattices are studied in
[

11
]
.
The magnetic field in all these models has some spatial symmetry.
The challenging case of i.i.d. random magnetic fields

with zero mean has been studied in

[

,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
]

[

]
.
Some deterministic and not spatially symmetric fields have

.

In this paper we study the hamiltonian (
1
) in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
,
with a non negative,
space dependent magnetic field

of the form

h(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{h^{*}}{|x|^{\alpha}}&x\neq 0\\
h^{*}&x=0\end{cases},\quad\alpha>0,h^{*}>0

(2)

where if

then

.
Calling

the corresponding partition function
one can easily check that (along van Hove sequences)

However surface effects are relevant

The existence of phase transitions at

\alpha<1
at low temperatures is more involved and it is based on an iterative scheme introduced in
[
5
]
. For
\alpha=1
we have partial results but not a complete characterization.

2
Existence of phase transitions

In this section we shall prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
h(\cdot)
be as in (
2
) with
\alpha>1
. Then for
\beta
large enough there is
a phase transition, namely the plus and minus Gibbs measures
\mu_{\beta,h(\cdot),\Lambda}^{\pm}

converge weakly as
\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to mutually distinct DLR measures.

As we shall see the result extends to
\alpha=1

under the additional assumption that
h^{*}
is small enough
and to non negative magnetic fields which are
“local perturbations” of (
2
)
(by this we mean that

norm of the difference is finite). We shall first prove the theorem under a stronger
assumption on the magnetic field, see (
3

allows to reproduce the Peierls’ argument. We need some geometric notation
that will be used extensively throughout the paper.

Definition 1
.

Two sites

\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Given a finite set

its complement,

the sites

to sites in
\bar{K}

denotes the number of connected pairs

with

and

Lemma 2
.

Let

be any non negative magnetic field such that

J|\partial\Delta|>2\sum_{x\in\Delta}h(x)

(3)

\beta

Proof.

We shall use (

of the Peierls bounds, see (

\beta

the weak limits of the Gibbs measures
with plus and minus boundary conditions are distinct DLR measures

phase transition hence the lemma. We shall use later
that

have trivial

at infinity so that they have disjoint support, see for instance the Georgii book,
[

Proof of the Peierls bounds.
Contours are geometric objects in the dual lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{*}
, namely call

,

x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the closed unit cube in

with center

of the faces

.
Given a spin configuration

Let

\gamma

Denote by

,

Then

\displaystyle Z_{I(\gamma);h(\cdot)}^{-}(\sigma_{I(\gamma)}(x)=1,x\in\delta_{\rm in}(I(\gamma)))

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq e^{\beta\sum_{x\in I(\gamma)}h_{x}}\;Z_{I(\gamma);h\equiv 0}^{-}(\sigma_{I(\gamma)}(x)=1,x\in\delta_{\rm in}(I(\gamma)))

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq e^{-2\beta J|\partial I(\gamma)|}e^{\beta\sum_{x\in I(\gamma)}h_{x}}Z_{I(\gamma);h\equiv 0}^{-}(\sigma_{I(\gamma)}(x)=-1,x\in\delta_{\rm in}(I(\gamma)))

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq e^{-2\beta J|\partial I(\gamma)|}e^{2\beta\sum_{x\in I(\gamma)}h_{x}}Z_{I(\gamma);h(\cdot)}^{-}(\sigma_{I(\gamma)}(x)=-1,x\in\delta_{\rm in}(I(\gamma))).

(4)

Same bound holds for the plus contours. ∎

The proof of Theorem
1

reducing to magnetic fields for which (

a task that will be achieved via a few lemmas where we
shall extensively use
the Isoperimetric Inequality (see

]
for a proof):
for any finite
\Delta\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}

|\Delta|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\leq\frac{|\partial\Delta|}{2d}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
h(\cdot)
be as in (
2
) with
\alpha>1
. Then there is
C\equiv C(h^{*},\alpha,d,J)>0
so that
(
3
) holds for all finite regions
\Delta
such that
|\Delta|>C
.

Proof.

Since
h(x)
is a non
increasing function of
|x|
, calling

{B}(0,R):=\{x:|x|\leq R\}
we have

\sum_{x\in\Delta}h(x)\leq\sum_{x\in{B}(0,R)}h(x),\quad\text{ for $R$ such that }\;|{B}(0,R)|\geq|\Delta|

We claim that
the condition
|{B}(0,R)|\geq|\Delta|
is satisfied
if

R=\text{ smallest integer }\geq c|\partial\Delta|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}

(5)

with
c
large enough. In fact, recalling that

|\partial{B}(0,n)|=2d\cdot n^{d-1}
, we have

|{B}(0,R)|\geq aR^{d}
,
a>0
small enough, hence using the isoperimetric inequality

|{B}(0,R)|\geq aR^{d}\geq ac^{d}|\partial\Delta|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\geq ac^{d}(2d)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}|\Delta|\geq|\Delta|

for
c
large enough.

Thus the lemma will be proved once we show that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d-1}}\sum_{|x|\leq R}h(x)=0.

Recalling that

|\partial{B}(0,n)|=2d\cdot n^{d-1}
this is implied by

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{R}\frac{n^{d-1}}{R^{d-1}}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}=0

whose validity follows from the Lebesgue dominated
convergence theorem. The lemma is thus proved.
∎

\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d-1}}\sum_{|x|\leq R}\frac{1}{|x|^{\alpha}}\leq c

Lemma 4
.

Let
h(\cdot)
be as in (

\alpha>1
, then

)

\hat{h}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $|x|\leq R$}\\

Proof.
Suppose

,

2\sum_{x\in\Delta}\hat{h}(x)\leq 2\sum_{x\in\Delta}h(x)\leq J|\partial\Delta|

Suppose next

, then by the Isoperimetric Inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Delta}\hat{h}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Delta;|x|>R}\hat{h}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{h^{*}|\Delta|}{R^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{h^{*}|\partial\Delta|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{R^{\alpha}(2d)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}\leq\frac{h^{*}C^{\frac{1}{d-1}}|\partial\Delta|}{R^{\alpha}(2d)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}

Proof of Theorem
1
.
Let
h(\cdot)
be as in (
2
) with
\alpha>1
. By Lemma
2
and
4
for
\beta
large enough
there is a phase transition for the system with magnetic field
\hat{h}(\cdot)
,
let
\mu_{\beta,\hat{h}(\cdot)}^{\pm}
the corresponding DLR measures obtained as
limit of the Gibbs measures with plus respectively minus boundary conditions.
Call
\phi(x):=h(x)-\hat{h}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{|x|<R}h(x)

and define the probability measures

d\nu_{\beta,h(\cdot)}^{\pm}(\sigma):=C_{\pm}e^{\beta\sum\phi(x)\sigma(x)}d\mu_{\beta,\hat{h}(\cdot)}^{\pm}(\sigma)

(6)

(
C_{\pm}
the normalization constants).
We shall first check that they are DLR measures with magnetic field
h(\cdot)
. To have lighter notation we drop super and subscripts writing just
\nu
,
\mu
and
C
.
We need to show that for any finite cube
\Lambda
large enough
(we need below that
\Lambda\supset B(0,R)
) the
\nu
conditional probability
given
\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}}
is the Gibbs measure with magnetic field
h(\cdot)
. By the DLR property for
\mu
we have

d\nu(\sigma)=Ce^{\beta\sum\phi(x)\sigma(x)}\frac{e^{-\beta\hat{H}(\sigma_{\Lambda}|\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}}{\hat{Z}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}d\mu_{\bar{\Lambda}}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})

where

is the marginal of
\mu
on the

\bar{\Lambda}
. We then have

d\nu(\sigma)=C\frac{e^{-\beta H(\sigma_{\Lambda}|\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}}{Z_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}\frac{Z_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}{\hat{Z}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}d\mu_{\bar{\Lambda}}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})

By integrating over
\sigma_{\Lambda}
we get

d\nu_{\bar{\Lambda}}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})=C\frac{Z_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}{\hat{Z}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}d\mu_{\bar{\Lambda}}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})

hence

d\nu(\sigma)=\frac{e^{-\beta H(\sigma_{\Lambda}|\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}}{Z_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})}d\nu_{\bar{\Lambda}}(\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}})

which proves the DLR property.
Thus

are DLR measures with magnetic field

and are absolutely

\mu_{\beta,\hat{h}(\cdot)}^{\pm}
. Hence they also have
disjoint supports and are therefore distinct. Theorem
1
is proved. ∎

3
Restricted ensembles and contour partition functions

We fix hereafter
h(x)
as in (
2
)
and we shall prove that

Theorem 5
.

Let
h(\cdot)
as in (
2
), then for any
\beta
large enough there is a unique DLR measure.

In this section we shall prove some crucial estimates
which will be used in the next section to prove Theorem
5

but which have an interest in their own right.
Observe that when
h(\cdot)
is given by
(
2
) the condition (
3
) may fail
for some
\Delta
for instance a large ball centered at the origin.

With this in mind we classify the contours
\gamma
by saying that
\gamma
is “slim” if

J|\partial I(\gamma)|>2\sum_{x\in I(\gamma)}h(x)

(7)

see the proof of Lemma
2
for notation.
We call “fat” the contours which do not satisfy (
7
). Following Pirogov-Sinai
we then introduce plus-minus
restricted ensembles where spin configurations are restricted in such a way that
there are only slim contours. We thus define for any bounded region
\Lambda
the plus-minus restricted partition functions

Z^{\pm,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{\sigma_{\Lambda}:\text{all contours are slim}}e^{-\beta H(\sigma_{\Lambda}|\pm\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda^{c}})}.

(8)

Obviously the pressures in the plus and minus ensembles are equal but the Pirogov-Sinai
theory requires for the existence of a phase transition
finer conditions on the finite volume corrections to the pressure namely that the latter differs from the limit
pressure by a surface term. In our case the correction is larger than a surface term
because
\alpha<1
as
shown by the following:

Theorem 6
.

For any
\beta
large enough there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
so that

Z^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}\leq c_{1}e^{-\beta c_{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}Z^{+,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}.

(9)

Proof.
By repeating the proof of Theorem
1
and denoting by
E^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}

the expectation w.r.t. the Gibbs measure in the minus restricted ensemble,
we have for any
x\in\Lambda
:

E^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}(\sigma(x))\leq-1+2\sum_{\gamma:I(\gamma)\ni 0}e^{-\beta J|\partial I(\gamma)|}=-m^{*},\quad m^{*}>0

(10)

for
\beta
large enough. Then

\mu_{\beta,h(\cdot),\Lambda}^{-,{\rm slim}}\Big[\frac{\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)\sigma_{\Lambda}(x)}{\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}\leq-\frac{m^{*}}{2}\Big]\geq\frac{m^{*}}{2-m^{*}}

(11)

To prove (
11
) let
X
be a random variable with values in
[-1,1]
and
P
its law.
Suppose that
E(X)\leq-m^{*}
and call
p:=P[X\geq-m^{*}/2]
, then

-m^{*}\geq-1(1-p)-\frac{m^{*}}{2}p,\quad(1-\frac{m^{*}}{2})p\leq(1-m^{*}),\quad(1-p)\geq\frac{m^{*}}{2-m^{*}}

hence (
11
).

Calling
Z^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}(A)
the partition function with the constraint
A
,
we can rewrite (
11
) as:

\displaystyle Z^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2-m^{*}}{m^{*}}Z^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}\Big(\frac{\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)\sigma_{\Lambda}(x)}{\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}\leq-\frac{m^{*}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2-m^{*}}{m^{*}}e^{-\beta\frac{m^{*}}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}Z^{-,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda,h\equiv 0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2-m^{*}}{m^{*}}e^{-\beta\frac{m^{*}}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}Z^{+,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda,h\equiv 0}.

By repeating the previous argument we get

Z^{+,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda,h\equiv 0}\leq\frac{2-m^{*}}{m^{*}}e^{-\beta\frac{m^{*}}{2}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h(x)}Z^{+,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}

where
Z^{+,{\rm slim}}_{\Lambda}
is the partition function with
the contribution of the magnetic field

h(\cdot)
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5
Concluding remarks

We have proved that when the magnetic field is given by (

for all

while, if
\alpha<1

When

However, our proof of uniqueness does not extend to the case

maybe related to an extension of Minlos-Sinai or the Wulff shape problem.

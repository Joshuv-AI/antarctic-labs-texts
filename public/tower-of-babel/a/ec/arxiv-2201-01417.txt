On the growth of meromorphic solutions of homogeneous and non-homogeneous linear difference equations in terms of (p,q)-order
Chinmay Ghosh, Subhadip Khan, Anirban Bandyopadhyay

Abstract
In this paper we have studied the growth of meromorphic solutions of higher order homogeneous and non-homogeneous linear difference equations with entire and meromorphic coefficients. We have extended and improved some results of Zhou and Zheng ( 2017 ) , \left(2017\right), Belaidi and Benkarouba ( 2019 ) \left(2019\right) by using ( p , q ) − (p,q)- order and ( p , q ) − (p,q)- type. AMS Subject Classification (2010) : 30D35, 39A10, 39B32 Keywords and phrases : entire function, meromorphic function, homogeneous difference equation, non-homogeneous difference equation, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- order, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- type.

Introduction

Recently the properties of meromorphic solutions of complex difference
equations

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=0

(1)

and

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=F(z)

(2)

have become a subject of great interest from the view point of Nevanlinna’s
theory and achieved many valuable results where the coefficients
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\neq 0\
and
F\neq 0

(k\geq 2)
in (
1
)
or (
2
) are entire or meromorphic functions and
c_{k},c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{1}
are distinct nonzero complex numbers.

In
1976
Juneja, Kapoor and Bajpai
[
9
]
introduced the idea of
(p,q)-
order of an entire function and in
2010
Liu, Tu and Shi
[
14
]
modified
the definition of the
(p,q)-
order to make it more suitable. Laine and
Yang, in
2007,
considered the equation (
1
) when more than one
dominant coefficients exist but exactly one has its type strictly greater
than the others (
[
11
]
, Theorem 5.2.). In
2008
Chiang and Feng
[
4
]

investigated meromorphic solutions of (
1
) and established a theorem (
[
4
]
, Theorem 9.2) taking exactly one coefficient of (
1
) with
maximal order
.
In
2013
, Liu and Mao used hyper order to establish the
case when one or more coefficients of (
1
) or (
2
) having
infinite order (
[
13
]
, Theorem 1.4, Theorem 1.6). Finally in
2017
, Zhou
and Zheng (
[
15
]
, Theorem 1.5) and in
2019
, Belaïdi and
Benkarouba (
[
2
]
, Theorem 1.1-Theorem 1.4) used iterated order and
iterated type to investigate the solutions of (
1
) or (
2
) and
obtained some results which improved and generalized those previous results.

In this article we use the concept of
(p,q)-
order to investigate
meromorphic solutions of (
1
) and (
2
). We also extend and
improve some results of Zhou and Zheng
[
15
]
, Belaïdi and
Benkarouba
[
2
]
. Here we consider both cases, when (
1
) and (
2
) have entire coefficients and meromorphic coefficients. We also cover
the cases when, either one of the coefficients have maximal
(p,q)-
order or
more than one coefficients having maximal
(p,q)-
order.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with the
fundamental results and the standard notations of Nevanlinna’s value
distribution theory
[
8
]
.

2
Definitions and Lemmas

In this section we give some basic definitions and lemmas which are used to
prove our main results.

For all
r\in\mathbb{R},
set
\exp_{1}r=e^{r}
and
\exp_{p+1}r=\exp\left(\exp_{p}r\right),

p\in\mathbb{N}.
Also for all sufficiently
large values of
r,

\log_{1}r=\log r
and
\log_{p+1}r=\log\left(\log_{p}r\right),

p\in\mathbb{N}.
Further
\exp_{0}r=\log_{0}r=r,

\exp_{-1}r=\log_{1}r,

\exp_{1}r=\log_{-1}r.

Definition 1

[
12
]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
or
2\leq q=p+1
be integers. The
(p,q)-
order of a transcendental meromorphic function
f
is defined by

\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}M(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

Note that
0\leq\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)\leq\infty.
Also for a
rational function
\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=0.

Definition 2

[
12
]
A transcendental meromorphic function is said to have index pair
\left[p,q\right]
if
0\leq\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)\leq\infty
and
\rho_{f}\left(p-1,q-1\right)
is not a non-zero finite number.

Definition 3

[
12
]
The
(p,q)-
type of a meromorphic function
f
having non-zero
finite
(p,q)-
order
\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)
is defined by

\tau_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\tau_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}M(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

Definition 4

[
12
]
Let
p\geq q\geq 1
or
2\leq q=p+1
be integers. The
(p,q)-
exponent of convergence of the sequence of poles of a meromorphic function
f
is defined by

\lambda_{\frac{1}{f}}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}N(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

Now we recall that the linear measure of a set
S\subset\left(0,+\infty\right)
is defined by

m\left(S\right)=\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\limits_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{S}\left(t\right)dt

and the logarithmic measure of a set
S\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)
is
defined by

lm\left(S\right)=\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{S}\left(t\right)}{t}dt,

where
\chi_{S}\left(t\right)
is the characteristic function of a set
S.

The upper density of a set
S\subset\left(0,+\infty\right)
is defined by

\overline{dens}S=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m\left(S\cap\left[0,r\right]\right)}{r}

and the upper logarithmic density of a set
S\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)
is defined by

\overline{\log dens}S=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{lm\left(S\cap\left[1,r\right]\right)}{\log r}.

Fab-Four cosmography to tackle the Hubble tension
Celia Escamilla-Rivera, José María de Albornoz-Caratozzolo, Sebastián Nájera

Abstract
In the context of the Fab-Four theory of gravity in a Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker background, in this work we use the cosmography approach to study a particular self-tuning filter solution focused on a zero-curvature fixed point to study the H 0 H_{0} tension. In this scheme, the equations restrict the universe’s evolution to certain scenarios, including radiation-like expansion, matter-like expansion, and late-time acceleration. Furthermore, we build the cosmographic series of the Fab-Four theory to obtain the kinematic parameters as the Hubble constant H 0 H_{0} and the deceleration parameter q 0 q_{0} for all the scenarios mentioned. Finally, we compare our results to find that it is possible to alleviate the current discrepancy on H 0 H_{0} by considering specific requirements on the free parameters of the Fab-Four theory through a self-tuning filter.

On the other hand, alternative gravity theories also attempt to solve the Hubble tension and other problems within standard cosmology. These theories change General Relativity’s (GR) Einstein-Hilbert action to introduce different dynamics in the resulting equations of motion, which can explain different phases of expansion of the universe and could explain the Hubble tension

;
Clifton:2011jh

In this work, we focus on a special case of Horndeski’s theory first proposed in

Charmousis_2012

\phi
. The self-tuning filter consisted in requiring that
Charmousis_2012
:

1.

The theory admits a patch of Minkowski vacuum for any value of the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, and for instantaneous changes in its value;

2.

The theory allows non-trivial cosmologies by introducing additional dynamical terms to the standard one.

According to these couple of requirements, in

an initial outlook was provided on the types of cosmology and its dynamics derived from the Fab-Four theory. Although it is a fairly recent proposal, the Fab-Four theory has been explored extensively and in different contexts. Specifically, there have been several studies involving the
\mathcal{L}_{john}

term (see Sec.
II

\mathcal{L}_{5}
term in Horndeski’s theory which adds a dependency on the field kinetic term
X=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi
, finding that the resulting
Fab 5

Fab 5

Starobinsky_2016
;
Starobinsky_2020
;
Galeev_2021

V_{j}(\phi)
and
V_{p}(\phi)

V_{j}(\phi,X)
and
V_{p}(\phi,X)

However, in

;
Ezquiaga:2017ekz

New self-tuning models known as

In this work, we follow closely the derivations performed for the Fab-Four cosmology
Copeland:2012qf
to obtain the corresponding equations of motion for a Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background and to apply a dynamical systems approach to solving the dynamical equations. Under this scheme and considering all four components of Fab-Four theory, we also extend this analysis by using a cosmographic approach, which will be useful to obtain kinematic quantities, such as
H_{0}
and
q_{0}
. Finally, with these quantities at hand, we will be capable to set general requirements on the free parameters to relax the Hubble tension and give a likelihood landscape for future works and analyses of this theory.
The core idea behind this process is the facility to look for a stable cosmological solution through dynamical systems. The advantage of this(these) kind of solution(s) is the possibility to explore the ones that fulfill the cosmological constraint equation of interest for the scheme to analyze. Once this constraint is fulfilled the
analytical
solution at hand can be tested with observations in order to set a cutoff in the cosmological parameters of interest, in our case,
H_{0}
and
q_{0}
. Of course, there could be more analytical solutions for the set of equations, however, since we are considering a cosmographic approach up to second order, this helps us to reduce the number of them and select the ones that fix the late-time cosmic acceleration.

We rewrite the equations in the zero-curvature fixed point of interest and solve them to find viable cosmological scenarios.
In Sec.

In Sec.

H_{0}

V

II
Fab-Four cosmology as a dynamical system

The Fab-Four model comes from a restriction set upon Horndeski’s theory, which simplifies it greatly so that the Lagrangian density is now just the sum of four base Lagrangians
Charmousis_2012
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{Fab-Four}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{john}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{paul}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{george}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{ringo}},

(1)

where each Lagrangian is defined as a combination of derivatives of the scalar field
\phi
, combinations of the metric tensor, and an arbitrary function of the field
V_{i}(\phi)
, with
i=j,p,g,r
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{john}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}V_{john}G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{paul}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}V_{paul}P^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi\nabla_{\beta}\phi,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{george}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}V_{george}R,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ringo}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}V_{ringo}\hat{\mathcal{G}},

(5)

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor,
P_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the double dual of the Riemann tensor,
R
is the Ricci scalar and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is the gauss-Bonnet combination
Copeland:2012qf
.
Given this notation and considering a FLRW metric whose line element is given by

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right),

(6)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor, which denotes the relative size of the co-moving, constant-time hypersurfaces of space-time,
d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}
is the element of solid angle and
k
is the spatial curvature constant. Here,
k=0
gives a flat space, while
k>0
(
k<0
) corresponds to positive (negative) curvature. We can express the Fab-Four Lagrangians as
Copeland:2012qf

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3a^{3}V_{j}(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2}\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3a^{3}V_{p}(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{3}H\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6a^{3}V_{g}(\phi)\left(H^{2}-\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right)-6a^{3}V_{{g},\phi}(\phi)H\dot{\phi},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8a^{3}V_{{r},\phi}(\phi)\dot{\phi}H\left(H^{2}+3\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right).

(10)

Here,
V_{i,\phi}
denotes the derivative of
V_{i}
with respect to the field,
{dV_{i}}/{d\phi}
, and
\dot{\phi}=U^{a}\nabla_{a}\phi
, with
U^{a}=\tensor{\delta}{a}{\;\;t}
the
4
-velocity.
Following
Copeland:2012qf
, we find that in the presence of standard matter and a cosmological constant
\Lambda
, the Hamiltonian density is given by

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{j}+\mathcal{H}_{p}+\mathcal{H}_{g}+\mathcal{H}_{r}+\rho_{\Lambda}=0,

(11)

where, for convenience, we can express each Hamiltonian in conformal time by considering
N=\ln{a}
, where
A^{\prime}
denotes differentiation with respect to
N
, such that
A^{\prime}={dA}/{dN}=\dot{A}(1/H)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3V_{j}{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}H^{2}\left(3H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3V_{p}{\phi^{\prime}}^{3}H^{4}\left(5H^{2}+3\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right),

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6V_{g}\left[\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right)+H^{2}\frac{V_{{g},\phi}\phi^{\prime}}{V_{g}}\right],

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-24V_{{r},\phi}\phi^{\prime}H^{2}\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right).

(15)

The equations for the scalar field
\phi
can be obtained through varying

E_{i}^{(\phi)}=-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i}}{\partial\phi},

(16)

which give the following expressions
Copeland:2012qf
:

\displaystyle E^{(\phi)}_{j}\dot{\phi}H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}\Delta_{2}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{j}a^{6}\Delta_{2}^{2}}{3\Delta_{2}-2\frac{k}{a^{2}}}\right)^{\prime},

(17)

\displaystyle E^{(\phi)}_{p}\dot{\phi}H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{a^{3/2}(H\Delta_{2})^{1/2}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{p}a^{9/2}(H\Delta_{2})^{1/2}\Delta_{2}}{5\Delta_{2}-2\frac{k}{a^{2}}}\right)^{\prime},

(18)

\displaystyle E^{(\phi)}_{g}\dot{\phi}H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3\frac{V_{g}^{\prime}}{a}(\Delta_{2}a^{4})^{\prime},

(19)

\displaystyle E^{(\phi)}_{r}\dot{\phi}H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{r}}{H\Delta_{2}}\left[a^{3}\left(\frac{k}{a^{2}}\Delta_{1}+\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{3}\right)\right]^{\prime},

(20)

with
\Delta_{n}=H^{n}-(\sqrt{-k}/a)^{n}
.
Notice that this function goes to zero as the latter equations approach a self-tuning solution corresponding to a Ricci flat scenario and
the full system of equations that arises is coupled with second-order differential equations which are extremely difficult to solve analytically.
Therefore, a viable path to reach a set of possible solutions is through a dynamical systems approach
Copeland:2012qf
.
In order to implement
this procedure, we consider the following dynamical variables:

h=\frac{H^{\prime}}{H},\qquad\qquad\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{-k}}{Ha},

(21)

notice that if
h=
const. we obtain that

H=H_{0}a^{h}\Rightarrow a\propto t^{-\frac{1}{h}},

where a matter-like expansion would arise from
h=-3/2
, radiation-like expansion from
h=-2
, and an inflationary expansion coming from
-1<h<0
.
To build the dynamical system according to the latter variables, we define the following expressions
Copeland:2012qf
:

x=H^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime},

(22)

\displaystyle y_{j}=H^{4-2\alpha}V_{j},

\displaystyle y_{p}=H^{6-3\alpha}V_{p},

\displaystyle y_{g}=H^{2}V_{g},

\displaystyle y_{r}=H^{4-\alpha}V_{r,\phi},

(23)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=H^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{j,\phi}}{V_{j}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}=H^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{p,\phi}}{V_{p}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}=H^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{g,\phi}}{V_{g}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}=H^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{r,\phi\phi}}{V_{r,\phi}},

(24)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}=\frac{V_{j}V_{j,\phi\phi}}{(V_{j,\phi})^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{p}=\frac{V_{p}V_{p,\phi\phi}}{(V_{p,\phi})^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{g}=\frac{V_{g}V_{g,\phi\phi}}{(V_{g,\phi})^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{r}=\frac{V_{r,\phi}V_{r,\phi\phi\phi}}{(V_{r,\phi\phi})^{2}},

(25)

where
\alpha
is an arbitrary constant that provides freedom in re-defining the scalar field while maintaining equations of motion that do not explicitly depend on
H
. This freedom will later be fixed to a particular value in Sec.
III
.

The evolution equations for these variables are directly obtained by differentiating Eqs. (
21
)-(
25
) with respect to
N
. Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-k}\frac{1}{H^{2}a^{2}}\left(H^{\prime}a+Ha\right)=-\sigma(h+1),

(26)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha H^{\alpha-1}H^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+H^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime\prime}=x\left(\alpha h+\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle y_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{i}H^{\beta_{i}}hV_{i}+H^{\beta_{i}}V_{i,\phi}\phi^{\prime}=y_{i}\left(\beta_{i}h+\lambda_{i}x\right),

(28)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha hH^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{i.\phi}}{V_{i}}+H^{-\alpha}\phi^{\prime}\frac{V_{i,\phi\phi}}{V_{i}}-H^{-\alpha}\frac{V_{i,\phi}^{2}}{V_{i}^{2}}\phi^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}\left(-\alpha h+\lambda_{i}x(\mu_{i}-1)\right),

(29)

where
i=j,p,g,r
and
\beta_{i}
denotes the exponents of
H
in Eq. (
23
). Knowing how these variables evolve, we can easily find their fixed points. Considering constant
\mu_{i}
allows for potentials in a power law form. For the
y_{i}
and the
\lambda_{i}
, these fixed points are those where

\displaystyle\beta_{i}h+\lambda_{i}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(30)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}x(\mu_{i}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha h.

(31)

In this fixed point, combining Eqs. (
30
)-(
31
) yields

\lambda_{i}\left(-\frac{\beta_{i}h}{\lambda_{i}}\right)(\mu_{i}-1)=\alpha h\Rightarrow(1-\mu_{i})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta_{i}},

(32)

which allows us to directly find the exponents for all four potentials, and they become

\displaystyle V_{j}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{j}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2},

(33)

\displaystyle V_{p}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{p}\phi^{\frac{6}{\alpha}-3},

(34)

\displaystyle V_{g}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{g}\phi^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},

(35)

\displaystyle V_{r,\phi}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{r}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-1}.

(36)

Furthermore, the fixed point for
\sigma
corresponds to
\sigma(h+1)=0
, i.e. fixed points solutions exist for
\sigma=0
and
h=-1
. On this line of thought, we are interested in the case where
\sigma=0
, which refers to vanishing spatial curvature, as it is consistent with observations

. In this selected fixed point, the full scalar field equation Eq. (

) can be written as:

(37)

Similarly, the Hamiltonian constraint Eq. (

(38)

,

) should be all independent of

. This assumption constrains the possible values of the terms present in Eqs. (

)-(

) while satisfying the following

\displaystyle(3+h)c_{p}(-\alpha h)^{2-\frac{2}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(39)

\displaystyle-2c_{j}\alpha h(3+h)-8c_{r}(h+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(41)

We can distinguish several values for

that fulfill these requirements, which are associated with different behaviors of

, due to

. Two cases of interest in this article are arbitrary for

and radiation for

Under the arbitrary case, we focus on three cases:
on the case

And finally, the radiation case, due to their applicability in studying the standard cosmological model, which corresponds to an inflationary period followed by a radiation-dominated epoch, then a matter-like expansion where structure formation happens, and finally followed by a late-time inflationary expansion that we are currently observing

. The applicability of such cases is due to the fact that we are looking for a viable cosmological scheme that can reproduce the current cosmic dynamics. Of course, in this case, we are considering solely a particular scalar field dynamic minimally coupled to gravity, so, the possibility that this scalar field can imitate dark matter and the effects of dark energy, could set up a
toy
contemporary universe model.

III
Fab Four cosmography

In what follows, we proceed with the standard cosmographic methodology
Bamba:2012cp
;
Capozziello:2019cav
;
Escamilla-Rivera:2019aol
to derive the kinematic parameters, which will be evaluated at current cosmic times. The advantage to consider this approach is the geometry construction behind it. By assuming the structure of the Cosmological Principle we can factorize the scale factor around the present time. However, we should take into account that we require to relax the issues of such approach: (i) the convergence of the series, which can be attended by using observables in order to restrain the order of it. (ii) the cosmic systematics that could be propagated on the cosmographic parameters. In such case, we require a control of the numerical fit.
Notice that for this analysis we require to select a fixed stable point and two possible cosmological scenarios.

Staying in the fixed point
\sigma=0
, we can start by constructing cosmographic series based on the Taylor series expansion context of the scale factor
a
, defined as

a=1+H_{0}\Delta t-\frac{1}{2}q_{0}H_{0}^{2}\Delta t^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{3}),

(42)

where
q
defined as

q=-\frac{\ddot{a}a}{\dot{a}^{2}}=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}-1=-\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}-1,

(43)

and
q_{0}
is the current deceleration parameter.
To obtain
H_{0}
, we first choose an appropriate scenario as described in Sec.
II
, substitute it into the Hamiltonian constraint Eq. (
11
), and solve the system for the Hubble parameter
H
. Furthermore, with
H
we can find
q
using Eq. (
43
). Finally, we can then evaluate both expressions at the current time to obtain
H_{0}
and
q_{0}
, respectively.

Specifically, we chose to build the cosmographical series because it is a purely kinematic expression where other parameters such as matter density or curvature do not play any role. Therefore, we can focus solely on the kinematic terms without specifying other parameters of the model.

At this point, we consider the radiation-dominant behaviour case (for the early-universe calculation of
H_{0}
), and the arbitrary case (for the late-universe calculation of
H_{0}
).
On one hand, for the arbitrary case, we have only two potential functions different from zero (as can be seen in Eqs. (
39
)-(
41
) by letting
h
be arbitrary):

\displaystyle V_{j}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\text{arb}}(h+1)\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2},

(44)

\displaystyle V_{r,\phi}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{4}h(3+h)c_{\text{arb}}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-1},

(45)

where the subindex

denotes arbitrary.
Therefore, the only Hamiltonian parts considered would be
\mathcal{H}_{j}
and
\mathcal{H}_{r}
, thus we can write Eq. (

\displaystyle 9c_{\text{arb}}(1+h)H^{4}{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2}+6c_{\text{arb}}\alpha h(3+h)H^{4}\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-1}+\rho_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(46)

\displaystyle-3c_{\text{arb}}H^{4}\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-1}\left[3(h+1)\phi^{\prime}\phi^{-1}+2\alpha h(3+h)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda}.

(47)

From the latter we can solve for

V
Conclusions

In this work, we consider the possibility to tackle the Hubble tension using the Fab-Four theory. This scalar-tensor theory of gravity applies a self-tuning filter on the more general Horndeski theory, to solve the cosmological constant fine-tuning problem. This leaves a much more manageable theory, consisting only of four base Lagrangians given by Eqs.(
7
)-(
10
). We follow the procedure discussed in
Copeland:2012qf
to obtain a simpler form of the theory, using a dynamical system approach to find the fixed points of the theory, and we concentrate on the
\sigma=0
fixed-point, which denotes zero curvature (as supported by observations
Planck_2020
). In this fixed point, the equation of motion of the scalar field Eq. (
37
) and the Hamiltonian restriction Eq. (
38
) of the theory are such that we have certain allowed types of cosmological scenarios, among which are the inflationary expansion, radiation-like expansion, and a matter-like phase. This shows the utility of the Fab-Four theory, as
turning on-and-off
certain potentials lead naturally to different types of cosmological behaviors, and the full dynamics of the theory allow for a smooth, continuous shift between these types of cosmological evolution.

Furthermore, we construct the Fab-Four cosmography in the zero-curvature fixed point. In particular, we derive analytical expressions for the current Hubble constant
H_{0}
, and the deceleration parameter
q_{0}
, for the matter, inflation, and radiation scenarios, as well as for different choices of the arbitrary constant
\alpha
. To do this, we take into account the forms of the potentials, substitute them into the Hamiltonian constraint, which results in quartic equations of the Hubble parameter, and then solve for
H
. These equations yield four solutions:
(i)
in the arbitrary case, we discard two complex roots and a negative one;
(ii)
in the radiation case, we can only discard two negative roots, leaving both signs of the expression for
H^{\text{rad}}_{\pm}
. Examining Table
1
, we also notice that the requirements do not restrict the choice of signs in the expressions for
H^{\text{rad}}_{\pm}
and
q^{\text{rad}}_{\pm}
, and both signs can give
\delta_{H_{0}}=0.1
, which is why we have left them written explicitly in all expressions.
Afterward, we differentiate the results for
H
according to Eq. (
43
) to obtain expressions for the deceleration parameter, which all ultimately take the functional form of Eq. (
75
). Using these expressions evaluated at current time, we can compare the Hubble constant in the late universe with the Hubble constant in the early universe (radiation-like expansion).

Finally, we show that it is possible to get the desired discrepancy between the early-universe and the late-universe Hubble constants, of around
\sim 10
%, showing that the Hubble constant could, in reality, be dynamical. Therefore, we have succeeded in our objective to show that it is indeed possible to get a disagreement between the observed Hubble constant if measured through local means, and the one obtained from observations of the CMB, due to the different dynamics put forward by the Fab-Four theory.

The final results contain five arbitrary parameters which are constrained by the requirements in Table
1
. However, using the expressions for the deceleration parameter we can reduce the number of free parameters in these results to only two constants. In future work, we will propose performing an analysis with observational data to obtain confidence limits on the free variables of the theory. For this, extracting other observables from the Fab Four theory, e.g., expressions for the luminosity distance, and even perturbation theory results such as the angular power spectrum from the CMB would be convenient. Afterward, a possible course of action would be to make a Bayesian statistics comparison of this model’s prediction against the standard
\Lambda
CDM to test its plausibility, as well as to further constrain the possible values of the free parameters to match observations
escamilla2023
. Furthermore, the possibility to explore beyond Horndeski theories
Kobayashi:2019hrl
could be a path in order to tackle the cosmological tensions at early cosmic times.

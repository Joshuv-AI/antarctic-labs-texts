Probing New Physics at Cosmic Dawn with 21-cm Cosmology
Omer Zvi Katz, Nadav Outmezguine, Diego Redigolo, Tomer Volansky

Abstract
21-cm cosmology provides an exciting opportunity to probe new physics dynamics in the early universe. In particular, a tiny sub-component of dark matter that interacts strongly with the visible sector may cool the gas in the intergalactic medium and significantly alter the expected absorption signal at Cosmic Dawn. However, the information about new physics in this observable is obscured by astrophysical systematic uncertainties. In the absence of a microscopic framework describing the astrophysical sources, these uncertainties can be encoded in a bottom up effective theory for the 21-cm observables in terms of unconstrained astrophysical fluxes. In this paper, we take a first step towards a careful assessment of the degeneracies between new physics effects and the uncertainties in these fluxes. We show that the latter can be constrained by combining measurements of the UV luminosity function, the Planck measurement of the CMB optical depth to reionization, and an upper bound on the unresolved X-ray flux. Leveraging those constraints, we demonstrate how new physics signatures can be disentangled from astrophysical effects. Focusing on the case of millicharged dark matter, we find sharp predictions, with small uncertainties within the viable parameter space. 1 1 1 This paper is a contribution to the proceeding of the Nobel Symposium on Dark Matter.

Each of the above categories requires detailed analyses to ensure that phenomenologically viable models exist.
More importantly, new physics effects may be degenerate with the inherent astrophysical uncertainties, a prevailing challenge during the Cosmic Dawn era. Within the above categories, certain scenarios exhibit complete degeneracy.
For example, fuzzy or warm DM models lead to a suppressed matter power spectrum at small scales, resulting in a reduced density of Pop-III stars. As this stellar population remains observationally elusive, it is currently impossible to distinctly separate the primary effects of such dark sectors using 21-cm cosmology.
The situation, however, is often more optimistic and in this letter we aim to take an initial stride in quantifying the degeneracies, concentrating specifically on the dark cooling category outlined above.

III.3
The X-ray intensity

Soft X-ray photons (with energy
E\leq 2{{\rm\ keV}}
) continuously interact with the IGM through photoionizations
5
5

5

X-rays with energy greater than approximately
2\text{ keV}
have a mean free path longer than the Hubble distance at
z=10

Oh 2001

McQuinn 2012

. Their intensity can be derived by solving the energy transfer equation in a homogeneously absorbing, non-emitting medium

Based on observations of nearby starburst galaxies

6

Grimm et al. 2003
;
Ranalli et al. 2003

Gilfanov et al. 2004
;
Fabbiano 2006
;
Mineo et al. 2012

Fragos et al. 2013

\left\langle\frac{dN_{X}}{dE}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{E_{0}}\left[\frac{E}{E_{0}}\right]^{\beta_{X}}\!\!\!\!\Theta\left[E-E_{\mathrm{min}}\right]\Theta\left[E_{\mathrm{max}}-E\right]\,,

(7)

where

E_{0}

Cohen et al. 2017

F_{\text{X}}

E_{\text{min}}

Das et al. 2017
;
Fragos et al. 2013
;

An upper limit on the X-ray flux can be derived by assuming that early X-ray sources are responsible for the totality of the unresolved cosmic X-ray background (CXB) in the
\left[0.5,2\right]\text{ keV}

\frac{1}{\left(1+z_{X}^{\text{un}}\right)^{3}}\int_{0.5\,\text{keV}}^{2\,\text{keV}}\!\!\!EJ_{\text{X}}\left(E,z_{X}^{\text{un}}\right)dE<0.5\frac{\rm{keV}}{\rm{cm^{2}\,sec\,sr}}\,.

Since star formation accelerates at lower redshifts, sources at the lowest redshift dominate the CXB so that a lower

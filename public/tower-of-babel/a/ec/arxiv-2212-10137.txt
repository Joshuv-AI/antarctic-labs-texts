Well-Posedness and Stability Analysis of an Epidemic Model with Infection Age and Spatial Diffusion
Christoph Walker

Abstract
. A compartment epidemic model for infectious disease spreading is investigated, where movement of individuals is governed by spatial diffusion. The model includes infection age of the infected individuals and assumes a logistic growth of the susceptibles. Global well-posedness of the equations within the class of nonnegative smooth solutions is shown. Moreover, spectral properties of the linearization around a steady state are derived. This yields the notion of linear stability which is used to determine stability properties of the disease-free and the endemic steady state.

1.
Introduction

We consider a compartment epidemic model for infectious disease spreading. The total population is divided into susceptible and infected individuals which move in space by diffusion. Infectious individuals are structured by infective age keeping track of the time elapsed since an individual first acquires the disease. A logistic growth is assumed for the susceptibles.

Let
S(t,x)
and
I(t,a,x)
be the densities of susceptible and infected individuals, respectively, at time
t\geq 0
, position
x\in\Omega
, and infection age
a\in(0,a_{m})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
is a bounded, smooth domain, and
a_{m}\in[0,\infty)
is the maximal invective age. The population of susceptible individuals is assumed to obey a logistic growth with intrinsic growth rate
\kappa_{1}>0
and carrying capacity
\kappa_{2}>0
. Susceptible individuals are infected at a rate
b(a,x)
by infected individuals of invective age
a
and position
x
. Infectious individuals die naturally and disease-induced at a combined rate
\mu(a,x)\geq 0
. They may recover and enter directly the class of susceptibles at a rate
r(a,x)\geq 0
.
We shall thus focus on the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S(t,x)

\displaystyle=d_{1}\Delta S(t,x)+\kappa_{1}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\kappa_{2}}S(t,x)\right)S(t,x)

\displaystyle\qquad-S(t,x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}r(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle DI(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=d(a)\Delta I(t,a,x)-m(a,x)I(t,a,x)-r(a,x)I(t,a,x)\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle I(t,0,x)

\displaystyle=S(t,x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(1.1c)

for
t>0
,
a\in(0,a_{m})
, and
x\in\Omega
. The differentiation operator
D
in (
1.1b
) is defined as

DI(t,a,\cdot):=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{h}\big(I(t+h,a+h,\cdot)-I(t,a,\cdot)\big)

and is thus, if
I
is continuously differentiable with respect to
t
and
a
, given by

DI(t,a,\cdot)=\partial_{t}I(t,a,\cdot)+\partial_{a}I(t,a,\cdot)\,.

For notational simplicity we will take a susceptible diffusion rate
d_{1}=1
and consider a diffusion coefficient
d=d(a)>0
for infected individuals dependent only upon infection age (though a space dependence does not alter the subsequent results).
The equations are supplemented by the initial conditions

\displaystyle S(0,x)=S_{0}(x)\,,\quad I(0,a,x)

\displaystyle=I_{0}(a,x)\,,\qquad(a,x)\in(0,a_{m})\times\Omega\,,

(1.1d)

and the boundary conditions

\displaystyle(1-\delta)S(t,x)+\delta\partial_{\nu}S(t,x)=0\,,\qquad(1-\delta)I(t,a,x)+\delta\partial_{\nu}I(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=0

(1.1e)

for
(t,a,x)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,a_{m})\times\partial\Omega
. Here,
\delta\in\{0,1\}
is fixed so that
\delta=0
corresponds to Dirichlet boundary conditions, while
\delta=1
yields Neumann boundary conditions with
\partial_{\nu}I=\nabla I\cdot\nu
denoting the derivative in normal direction
\nu
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
(we treat the two cases simultaneously).

Age-structured compartment epidemic models and age-structured population models in general have been investigated since many years
[
26
,
36
,
37
]
. The particular case of
equations (
1.1
) without spatial diffusion (and
r=0
) was studied in
[
8
]
. Therein, criteria for stability and instability of the disease-free and the endemic steady states were obtained in dependence on the corresponding basic reproduction number. Moreover, conditions for the occurrence of Hopf bifurcation were presented.

The inclusion of spatial heterogeneity in age-structured populations leads to additional technical difficulties in the analysis. We refer to the monograph
[
37
]
for a general treatment of and a comprehensive overview on such problems. Regarding SIS- and SIR-models there are various linear and nonlinear variants involving age and spatial structure with Laplace diffusion. The list includes the pioneering works
[
34
,
35
]
on epidemic models including incubation periods, followed by
[
18
,
19
,
22
,
24
]
and, more recently,
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
15
,
21
,
23
]
though these references are non-exhaustive. The cited papers address various questions under different modeling hypotheses, for instance related to well-posedness of the equations, existence and stability properties of disease-free and endemic steady states, disease persistence, traveling wave solutions, or numerical simulations. We also refer to
[
20
]
and the references therein for age-structured epidemic models describing long-distance spreading of diseases by nonlocal diffusion.

In this research we shall focus on the particular model (
1.1
). We first prove the existence and uniqueness of positive, global, smooth solutions by using a semigroup approach relying on results outlined in
[
37
]
. We then show that the linearization of these equations around a steady state yields a strongly continuous semigroup, and we use the spectrum of its generator for determining linear stability properties of steady states. Without further assumptions we prove stability or instability of the disease-free steady state in dependence on the reproduction number.
In a particular case of (
1.1
) assuming spatially homogeneous rates and Neumann boundary conditions we improve the local stability of this steady state to global stability. Moreover, we investigate the stability of the endemic steady state.

In the following Section

we present our main results. Section

5
. The application to a simplified version of equations (
1.1
) with Neumann boundary conditions and spatially homogeneous rates are presented in Section
6
. Some technical results are postponed to the Appendix

2.
Main Results

We first state the result on the well-posedness of (
1.1
) and then investigate linearized stability of steady states.

2.1.
Well-Posedness

In order to present our existence result, we set
J:=[0,a_{m}]
and take without loss of generality
d_{1}=1
. We assume that

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}\,,\kappa_{2}>0

(2.1a)

and

\displaystyle d\in C^{\rho}(J)\,,\qquad d(a)\geq\underline{d}>0\,,\quad a\in J\,,

(2.1b)

Moreover,

\displaystyle m\in C^{\rho}\big(J,L_{\infty}(\Omega)\big)\,,\quad r\in C^{\rho}\big(J,L_{\infty}(\Omega)\big)\cap L_{\infty}(J,C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\big)\,,\qquad m,r\geq 0\,,

(2.1c)

for some
\rho>0
and

\displaystyle b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\big)\,,\quad b\geq 0\,,\quad b\not\equiv 0\,.

(2.1d)

Here,
C^{\rho}
stands for
\rho
-Hölder continuous functions. The regularity assumptions on the data are mainly imposed in order to derive smooth solutions. Denote
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=[0,\infty)
and
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+}:=(0,\infty)
.

We shall prove the following result on the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of global, positive solutions to (
1.1
):

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume (
2.1
) and
p\in\big(\max\{3n/4,2\},\infty\big)
. Then, given initial values
S_{0}\in L_{p}^{+}(\Omega)
and
I_{0}\in L_{1}\big(J,L_{p}^{+}(\Omega)\big)
, there is a unique positive global solution
(S,I)
to (
1.1
) such that

S\in C\big(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}^{+}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+},W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\big)

is a strong solution to (
1.1a
), while

I\in C\big(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{1}(J,L_{p}^{+}(\Omega))\big)\cap C\big(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+},L_{1}(J,W_{p}^{2}(\Omega))\big)

satisfies (
1.1b
) in the sense that

DI(t,a)=\big(d(a)\Delta-m(a,\cdot)-r(a,\cdot)\big)I(t,a,\cdot)\ \text{ in }\ L_{p}(\Omega)

for
t>0
and a.e.
a\in(0,a_{m})
. In fact, the solution map
(t,(S_{0},I_{0}))\mapsto(S,I)(t)
defines a global semiflow on
L_{p}^{+}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}^{+}(\Omega))
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is based on a semigroup representation of solutions in the spirit of
[
37
]
and on Banach’s fixed point theorem. It is performed in several steps in Section
3
. In fact, it is not restricted to the particular nonlinearities in (
1.1
) and may rather be a template for similar problems. That the solution map defines a global semiflow paves the way to consider qualitative aspects of the model.

2.2.
Linearization Around Steady States

Assume (
2.1
) and fix an arbitrary steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
to (
1.1
), i.e. a time-independent solution. The regularizing effects of the Laplacian implies that we may assume without loss of generality the regularity

\begin{split}&S_{*}\in W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\,,\quad S_{*}>0\ \text{ in }\ \Omega\,,\\
&I_{*}\in L_{1}\big(J,W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap W_{1}^{1}\big(J,L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\,,\quad I_{*}\geq 0\ \text{ in }\ J\times\Omega\,,\end{split}

(2.2)

for some
p>n
.
The linearization of (
1.1
) around the steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S(t,x)

\displaystyle=\Delta S(t,x)+\kappa_{1}S(t,x)-\dfrac{2\kappa_{1}S_{*}(x)}{\kappa_{2}}S(t,x)-S(t,x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I_{*}(a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad-S_{*}(x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}r(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle DI(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=d(a)\Delta I(t,a,x)-\big(m(a,x)+r(a,x)\big)I(t,a,x)\,,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle I(t,0,x)

\displaystyle=S_{*}(x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a+S(t,x)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)\,I_{*}(a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(2.3c)

for
(t,a,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,a_{m})\times\Omega
, and subject to the initial conditions

\displaystyle S(0,x)=S_{0}(x)\,,\quad I(0,a,x)

\displaystyle=I_{0}(a,x)\,,\qquad(a,x)\in(0,a_{m})\times\Omega\,,

(2.3d)

and boundary conditions

\displaystyle(1-\delta)S(t,x)+\delta\partial_{\nu}S(t,x)=0\,,\qquad(1-\delta)I(t,a,x)+\delta\partial_{\nu}I(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=0

(2.3e)

for
(t,a,x)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,a_{m})\times\partial\Omega
. We shall show that the solutions
(S,I)
to the linearization (
2.3
) are given by a strongly continuous semigroup on
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
with compact resolvent:

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose (
2.1
) and
p>(2\vee n)
. Let
(S_{*},I_{*})
be a steady state to (
1.1
) satisfying (
2.2
). Then, the solution
(S,I)
to the linearized equation (
2.3
) is given as

(S,I)(t)=\mathbb{S}_{*}(t)(S_{0},I_{0})\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

where
(\mathbb{S}_{*}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
. Its generator has a compact resolvent and thus a pure point spectrum without finite accumulation point.

Theorem
2.2
is a consequence of Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
4.3
from Section
4
. There, we also present more precise information on the semigroup and its generator. Note that the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}_{*}(t))_{t\geq 0}
lacks positivity and thus less information on the spectrum of its generator is available in general. We refer to Remark
4.6
for further details.

2.3.
Linear Stability

Due to Theorem
2.2
, one may characterize stability properties of steady states based on the linearization of (
1.1
) around these steady states. That linearized stability indeed determines the (asymptotic) stability of steady states in certain nonlinear population models including age- and spatial structure has recently been shown in
[
32
,
33
]
. We refrain, however, to prove this for problem (
1.1
).

Herein, we shall just call a steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})

linearly stable
if the generator of the semigroup associated with the linearization (
2.3
) of (
1.1
) (given in Theorem
2.2
) has a spectrum lying entirely in the half plane
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<0
while we call the steady state
linearly unstable
if there is a spectral point in the half plane
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0
(see Definition
4.4
and Remark
4.6
below for more details in this regard).

We assume for simplicity that

r\equiv 0\,.

(2.4)

We provide a stability analysis in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
of the trivial and the disease-free steady states. To state precise results we introduce the principal eigenvalue
\mu_{0}
of the Laplacian
-\Delta
on
\Omega
subject to either Dirichlet boundary conditions (hence
\mu_{0}>0
) or Neumann boundary conditions (hence
\mu_{0}=0
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose (
2.1
), (
2.4
), and let
p>(2\vee n)
.

(a)

The trivial steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
to (
1.1
) is linearly unstable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
if
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
and linearly stable if
\kappa_{1}<\mu_{0}
.

(b)

There is a disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
to (
1.1
) with a smooth function
\tilde{S}_{*}>0
if and only if
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
. In this case, the disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
is unique, and there is a number
\mathsf{R}_{0}>0
such that it is linearly stable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
if
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
and linearly unstable if
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
.

(c)

Let
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
. There is no endemic state
(S_{*},I_{*})
to (
1.1
) with
S_{*},I_{*}\geq 0
and
I_{*}\not\equiv 0
if
\mathsf{R}_{0}\leq 1
.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
is presented in Section
5
.
The reproduction number
\mathsf{R}_{0}>0
is defined in (
5.3a
) and corresponds to the spectral radius of a compact irreducible operator (defined in (
5.3b
)) depending on
\tilde{S}_{*}
. It is open whether there is an endemic state
(S_{*},I_{*})
with
S_{*},I_{*}\geq 0
and
I_{*}\not\equiv 0
if
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
(see Remark
5.5
in this regard). However, the existence of an endemic steady state in case that
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
is easily obtained when assuming Neumann boundary conditions and spatially homogeneous rates
m
and
b
as shown in the next subsection.

2.4.
Linear Stability in a Particular Model with Neumann Boundary Conditions

We give a more detailed account of Theorem
2.3
in the particular case of
Neumann boundary conditions

\displaystyle\delta=1\,,

(2.5a)

so that
\mu_{0}=0
, and spatially homogeneous data

\displaystyle d\in C(J)\,,\quad d(a)\geq\underline{d}>0\,,\quad a\in J\,,

(2.5b)

\displaystyle m\in C(J)\,,\quad m\geq 0\,,\qquad b\in L_{\infty}(J)\,,\quad b>0\,,

(2.5c)

that is, data only depending on age
a
.
In this particular situation, besides the trivial steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
and the disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\kappa_{2},0)
, there is also an
endemic steady state

(\bar{S}_{*},\bar{I}_{*})
provided that
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
, where

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{0}:=\kappa_{2}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\Pi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a

(2.6)

with

\Pi(a):=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{a}m(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,,\quad a\in J\,.

It is given as

\bar{S}_{*}:=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\,,\qquad\bar{I}_{*}(a):=\frac{1}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\right)\Pi(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,.

Linear stability and instability of these steady states is determined by the basic reproduction number
\mathsf{R}_{0}
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume (
2.4
), (
2.5
), let
p>(2\vee n)
, and let
\mathsf{R}_{0}>0
be defined in (
2.6
).

(a)

The trivial steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
is linearly unstable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
.

(b)

If
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
, then the disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\kappa_{2},0)
is globally linearly stable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
; that is, it is linearly stable and attracts any solution starting from positive initial values. If
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
, then
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\kappa_{2},0)
is linearly unstable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
.

(c)

For
1<\mathsf{R}_{0}<3
, the endemic steady state
(\bar{S}_{*},\bar{I}_{*})
is linearly stable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
.

Part (a) and the local stability statements of part (b) of Theorem
2.4
have been observed already in Theorem
2.3
. The proofs of the remaining statements of Theorem
2.4
are given in Section
6
. In fact, when
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
we prove that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\big(S(t),I(t)\big)=(\kappa_{2},0)\ \text{ in }\ L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}\big(J,C(\bar{\Omega})\big)

for any solution
(S,I)
to (
1.1
) corresponding to positive nontrivial initial values
(S_{0},I_{0})
so that there is no further steady state in this case (in accordance with Theorem
2.3
(c)). Clearly, one expects
(\bar{S}_{*},\bar{I}_{*})
to be linearly stable whenever
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
.

3.
Well-Posedness: Proof of Theorem
2.1

We prove Theorem
2.1
in several steps. After introducing some notation we derive the existence of a local solution then establish further properties.

3.1.
Preliminaries
\&
Notation

For two Banach spaces
E
and
F
we write
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
for the Banach space of bounded linear operators from
E
to
F
, and we set
\mathcal{L}(E):=\mathcal{L}(E,E)
. Similarly,
\mathcal{K}(E,F)
and
\mathcal{K}(E)
stand for compact linear operators.

For fixed
\delta\in\{0,1\}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we set

\mathcal{B}u:=u\ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega\ \text{ if }\ \delta=0\,,\qquad\mathcal{B}u:=\partial_{\nu}u\ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega\ \text{ if }\ \delta=1\,,

and introduce the scale of Banach spaces

W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{v\in W_{p}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,;\;\mathcal{B}w=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}\,,&\delta+\frac{1}{p}<2\theta\leq 2\,,\\[3.0pt]
W_{p}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,&0\leq 2\theta<\delta+\frac{1}{p}\,.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

By
\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}
we denote the Laplacian defined on
W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for fixed
a\in J
, also the operator

\displaystyle A(a):=d(a)\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}-m(a,\cdot)-r(a,\cdot)

(3.2)

has domain
W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then
\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}
and
A(a)

are generators of positive analytic contraction semigroups on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for each
p\in(1,\infty)

[
2
,
25
]
. In fact, since

A\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))\big)\,,

it follows from
[
4
, II.Corollary 4.4.2]
that

generates a positive parabolic evolution operator

U_{A}(a,\sigma)\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\leq a_{m}\,,

on

in the sense of

, II.Section 2.1]
. In particular,

v(a):=U_{A}(a,\sigma)v^{0}\,,\quad a\in[\sigma,a_{m}]\,,

is, for given
\sigma\in[0,a_{m})
and

, the unique solution

v\in C\big([\sigma,a_{m}],L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big((\sigma,a_{m}],L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big((\sigma,a_{m}),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\big)

to the Cauchy problem

\partial_{a}v(a)=A(a)v(a)\,,\quad a\in(\sigma,a_{m})\,,\qquad v(\sigma)=v^{0}\,.

The contraction properties

\begin{split}\|e^{t\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}&\leq 1\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\\
\|U_{A}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}&\leq e^{-\int_{\sigma}^{a}\underline{m}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}\,\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\in J\,,\end{split}

are valid, where

Recall the interpolation relations

\big(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,p}\doteq W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\,,\quad 2\theta\in[0,2]\setminus\left\{1,\delta+\frac{1}{p}\right\}\,,

with real interpolation functor

and

\big[L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\big]_{1/2}\doteq W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega)

with complex interpolation functor

(see

). From

and the embedding

we then infer parabolic regularizing properties in the sense that, given

with
2\vartheta,2\theta\notin\{\delta+\frac{1}{p}\}
and

, there are

\displaystyle t^{\theta-\vartheta}\,\|e^{t\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\vartheta}(\Omega),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}+t^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\,\|e^{t\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{q}(\Omega))}\leq M\,e^{\varpi t}\,,\quad t>0\,,

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle(a-\sigma)^{\theta-\vartheta}\,\|U_{A}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\vartheta}(\Omega),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}+(a-\sigma)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\,\|U_{A}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{q}(\Omega))}\leq M\,e^{\varpi(a-\sigma)}\,.

(3.5)

Let
p\in\big(\max\{\frac{3n}{4},2\},\infty\big)
and let
S_{0}\in L_{p}(\Omega)
and
I_{0}\in L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
be fixed in the following.

3.2.
Existence of a Unique Maximal Solution

Given
S\in L_{p}(\Omega)
and
I\in L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
we use the abbreviations (dropping

Setting

u_{j}(a):=\int_{0}^{a}U_{A}^{\lambda}(a,\sigma)I_{0,j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\,,

we next show that
\{u_{j}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in
L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
adopting the arguments from
[
6
]
(there the case of semigroups was considered):

(i)
We first fix
\mu>0
and define

v_{j}^{\mu}(a):=U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,a)u_{j}(a)=\int_{0}^{a}U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)I_{0,j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Since

\|u_{j}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|I_{0,j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\,,

and

U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,a)\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\subset\mathcal{K}\big(L_{p}(\Omega)\big)\,,\quad\quad a\in J\,,

we see that, for every
a\in J
, the sequence
(v_{j}^{\mu}(a))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
L_{p}(\Omega)
. Next, in order to check equi-integrability we recall from
[
4
, II. Equation (2.1.2)]
that

U_{A}^{\lambda}\in C\big(\Delta_{J}^{*},\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))\big)\ \text{ with }\ \Delta_{J}^{*}:=\{(a,\sigma)\,;\,0\leq\sigma<a\leq a_{m}\}\,,

and note for
\xi>0
that the set

K_{\xi}:=\{(a,\sigma)\in\Delta_{J}^{*}\,;\,\sigma+\xi\leq a\}

is compact in
\Delta_{J}^{*}
. We thus find for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\xi>0
some
\eta>0
such that

\|U_{A}(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-U_{A}(a_{2},\sigma_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\leq\varepsilon\,,\qquad(a_{i},\sigma_{i})\in K_{\xi}\,,\quad|(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-(a_{2},\sigma_{2})|\leq\eta\,.

Taking
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary and
\xi=\mu>0
, we use (
3.3
) to derive, for
h\in(0,\eta)
with
0<a\leq a+h\leq a_{m}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-v_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{a+h}\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a+h,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a}\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a+h,\sigma)-U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{a+h}\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{a}\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq h\|I_{0,j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}+\varepsilon a_{m}\|I_{0,j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}\,,

where the tilde refers to the trivial extension. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\,\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}a=0

so that
\{v_{j}^{\mu}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equi-integrable and thus

\displaystyle\{v_{j}^{\mu}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}\ \text{ is relatively compact in $L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))$ for $\mu>0$}\,.

(4.17)

(ii)
We next consider the limit
\mu\to 0
. Given
\varepsilon>0
,
\xi>0
, and using the notation from the previous part we have, for
a\in J
with
a\geq\xi
and
0<\mu<\eta
,

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a)-u_{j}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{a-\xi}\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)-U_{A}^{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{a-\xi}^{a}\big(\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}+\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\big)\,\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{a-\xi}\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+2\int_{a-\xi}^{a}\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,

while for
0\leq a\leq\xi
we have

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a)-u_{j}(a)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\xi}\|I_{0,j}(\sigma)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}-u_{j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq 4\xi\|I_{0,j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}+\varepsilon a_{m}\|I_{0,j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}

so that

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to 0}\,\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\,\|v_{j}^{\mu}-u_{j}\|_{L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))}=0\,.

Together with (
4.17
) we conclude that
\{u_{j}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in
L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
.

(iii)
Finally, since

\|U_{A}^{\lambda}(a+h,0)-U_{A}^{\lambda}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))}\leq ch^{1/4}\,,\quad 0\leq a\leq a+h\leq a_{m}\,,

according to
[
4
, II. Equation (5.3.8]
, it readily follows from (
4.16
) and the Arzelà - Ascoli Theorem that
(U_{A}^{\lambda}(\cdot,0)\psi_{j}(0))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
C(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
. Consequently, we deduce from the previous step
(ii)
and (
4.14
) that
(\psi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
. Therefore,
\mathbb{A}_{*}
has indeed a compact resolvent.

(d) Spectral Bound:
Since
\mathbb{T}_{*}
is eventually compact, it follows from
[
17
, IV. Corollary 3.12]
that
s(\mathbb{A}_{*})=\omega_{0}(\mathbb{T}_{*})
.

∎

Introducing now the perturbation

\mathbb{B}_{*}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-P_{*}+N\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))\big)

with
P_{*}
and
N
defined in (
4.3b
)
and observing from (
4.8
) that
\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*}
is exactly the linearized operator appearing in (
4.4a
) subject to (
4.4b
), we obtain the desired generation result for the linearization:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
be satisfied. Then, the generator

\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*}
of the strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{S}_{*}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
, associated with the linearized problem (
4.4
), has compact resolvent. In particular, the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*})
is a pure point spectrum without finite accumulation point.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
4.2
and
[
17
, III. Proposition 1.12]
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

We call the steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})

linearly stable
in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
, if the generator
\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*}
of the linearized problem satisfies
\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*})<0
, while we call
(S_{*},I_{*})

linearly unstable
if
\sigma(\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*})\cap[\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0]\not=\emptyset
.

Remark 4.5
.

Corollary
4.3
implies that the linear stability of the steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
is determined from (the real parts of) those
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which there is a nontrivial
(S,I)\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times C(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
satisfying

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}A_{1}^{*}&-P_{*}+N\\
0&-\partial_{a}+A(a)\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)=\lambda\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)\,,\qquad I(0)-P_{*}I=q_{*}S\,,

(equality for the second component in the sense of Theorem
4.2
) with notation introduced in (
4.3
).

Remark 4.6
.

Since the semigroup
(e^{t\mathbb{A}_{*}})_{t\geq 0}
is eventually compact and due to the particular (nonlocal) form of the perturbation
\mathbb{B}_{*}
, one can in fact show that the perturbation semigroup
\mathbb{S}_{*}=(e^{t(\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*})})_{t\geq 0}
is also eventually compact. Consequently,

\omega(\mathbb{S}_{*})=s(\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*})\,,

that is, the growth bound of the semigroup
\mathbb{S}_{*}
and the spectral bound of its generator
\mathbb{A}_{*}+\mathbb{B}_{*}
coincide. A steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
is thus linearly stable in the sense of Definition
4.4
if and only if the semigroup
\mathbb{S}_{*}
associated with the linearization around this steady state has an exponential decay. One can further prove that this indeed implies the asymptotic stability of the steady state. The technical details of the proof follow along the lines of
[
32
]
(see also
[
33
]
).

5.
Linearized Stability: Proof of Theorem
2.3

We shall apply the results from the previous section. In the following, we are still imposing (
2.1
) with
p>(2\vee n)
and assume for simplicity (
2.4
).
Recall that
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
. We consider only steady states
(S_{*},I_{*})
to (
1.1
) with regularity as in (
4.1
).

For the investigation of stability of steady states recall that the spectrum of the Laplacian is (counted according to multiplicity)

\sigma(-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}})=\{\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots\}

with
0\leq\mu_{i}\leq\mu_{i+1}
for
i\geq 0
. In fact,
\mu_{0}>0
in the Dirichlet case
\delta=0
and
\mu_{0}=0
in the Neumann case
\delta=1
.

5.1.
The Trivial Steady State

The linear stability of the trivial steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
depends on the sign of
\kappa_{1}-\mu_{0}
(which is always positive for the Neumann case
\delta=1
but may be negative in the Dirichlet case
\delta=0
):

Proposition 5.1
.

The trivial steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
is linearly unstable if
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
and linearly stable if
\kappa_{1}<\mu_{0}
.

Proof.

Using the notation introduced in (
4.3
) for
(S_{*},I_{*})=(0,0)
, we have

q_{*}=0\,,\qquad P_{*}=0\,,\qquad A_{1}^{*}=\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+\kappa_{1}\,,

so that Remark
4.5
leads to investigating the eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+\kappa_{1}&0\\
0&-\partial_{a}+A(a)\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)=\lambda\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)\,,\qquad I(0)=0\,,

for a nontrivial
(S,I)\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times C(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
; that is,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}S

\displaystyle=(\kappa_{1}-\lambda)S\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{a}I(a)

\displaystyle=\big(-\lambda+A(a)\big)I(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad I(0)=0\,,

with mild solution
I
. Hence
I=0
so that
S\not=0
and thus
\kappa_{1}-\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}})
.
Consequently,
s(\mathbb{A}_{*})=\kappa_{1}-\mu_{0}
is an eigenvalue.
∎

5.2.
The Disease-Free Steady State

The existence of a disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
reduces to finding a positive non-trivial solution
\tilde{S}_{*}\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
to the semilinear equation

-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}\tilde{S}_{*}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\tilde{S}_{*}^{2}=\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}\,.

(5.1)

Clearly, in the Neumann case
\delta=1
, the positive (constant) solution to (
5.1
) is
\tilde{S}_{*}=\kappa_{2}
.

To continue let us recall the following result from
[
5
, Theorem 12, Theorem 16]
:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
q\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then the eigenvalue problem

-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}u+qu=\lambda u

has a smallest eigenvalue
\lambda=\lambda_{0}(q)\in\mathbb{R}
(in the sense that
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>\lambda_{0}(q)
for every other eigenvalue
\lambda
). This principal eigenvalue is simple and the only eigenvalue with a positive eigenfunction
u_{0}
, i.e.
u_{0}\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and
u_{0}>0
in
\Omega
. Moreover, if
q_{1},q_{2}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
with
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
and
q_{1}\not\equiv q_{2}
, then
\lambda_{0}(q_{1})<\lambda_{0}(q_{2})
. In fact,
\lambda_{0}(0)=\mu_{0}
.

Now, if
\tilde{S}_{*}\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
is a positive non-trivial solution to (
5.1
), then necessarily

\kappa_{1}=\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)>\lambda_{0}(0)=\mu_{0}\,.

(5.2)

Conversely, it follows from
[
7
]
that if
\kappa_{1}>\lambda_{0}(0)=\mu_{0}
, then (
5.1
) admits a positive solution
\tilde{S}_{*}\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
(see also
[
29
]
). This solution is unique. Indeed, if there was another positive solution
\hat{S}_{*}\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
to (
5.1
), then
z:=\tilde{S}_{*}-\hat{S}_{*}
solves the eigenvalue equation

-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}z+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(\tilde{S}_{*}+\hat{S}_{*})z=\kappa_{1}z

so that

\kappa_{1}\geq\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(\tilde{S}_{*}+\hat{S}_{*})\right)\,.

According to (
5.2
) and the monotonicity of
\lambda_{0}(q)
with respect to
q
this yields the contradiction

\kappa_{1}=\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)<\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(\tilde{S}_{*}+\hat{S}_{*})\right)\leq\kappa_{1}\,.

The linear stability of the disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
is then determined from the value of

\mathsf{R}_{0}:=r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})>0\,,

(5.3a)

where the family of compact operators

Q^{\lambda}=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,e^{-\lambda a}\,U_{A}(a,0)\,\mathrm{d}a\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(\Omega))\,,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,,

(5.3b)

was introduced in (
4.5
) and properties of the spectral radius
r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{\lambda})
are stated in Lemma
4.1
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

There is a disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
with a smooth function
\tilde{S}_{*}>0
if and only if
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
. In this case,
\tilde{S}_{*}
is unique, (
5.2
) holds, and
\mathsf{R}_{0}>0
in (
5.3a
) is well-defined. For Neumann boundary conditions (i.e.
\delta=1
), we have
\tilde{S}_{*}=\kappa_{2}
.

Moreover,
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
is linearly stable in
L_{p}(\Omega)\times L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
if
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
and linearly unstable if
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
.

Proof.

We have already shown that there is a (unique) disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
with
\tilde{S}_{*}>0
(satisfying (
5.2
)) if and only if
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
. Thus, let
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
. According to Remark
4.5
we have to check the real parts of solutions
\lambda
to the eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}A_{1}^{*}&-P_{*}\\
0&-\partial_{a}+A(a)\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)=\lambda\left(\begin{matrix}S\\
I\end{matrix}\right)\,,\qquad I(0)=P_{*}I\,,

with a nontrivial
(S,I)\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times C(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
, where, due to (
4.3
),

q_{*}=0\,,\quad P_{*}I=\tilde{S}_{*}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,\cdot)I(a,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad A_{1}^{*}=\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+\kappa_{1}-\frac{2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\,,\quad A(a)=d(a)\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}-m(a,\cdot)\,.

That is, we have to investigate

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}S+\frac{2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}S

\displaystyle=(\kappa_{1}-\lambda)S-\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{\lambda}I(0)\,,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}I

\displaystyle=(-\lambda+A(a))I\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad I(0)=\tilde{S}_{*}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,\cdot)I(a,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(5.5)

where (
5.5
) entails

\displaystyle I(a)=e^{-\lambda a}U_{A}(a,0)I(0)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad I(0)=\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{\lambda}I(0)\,.

(5.6)

Assume first that
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
. Then either
I(0)=0
so that (
5.4
), the monotonicity of the principal eigenvalue, and (
5.2
) imply

\mathrm{Re}\,(\kappa_{1}-\lambda)\geq\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)>\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)=\kappa_{1}\,,

hence
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<0
.
Or
I(0)\not=0
so that (
5.6
) together with
[
31
, Theorem 2.3 (b)]
imply that
\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})
, where
\mathbb{A}
is the generator of a strongly continuous, positive, eventually compact semigroup on
L_{1}(J,L_{p}(\Omega))
. Its spectral bound
s:=s(\mathbb{A})
is the unique real number with
r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{s})=1
according to
[
31
, Proposition 5.2]
. Since
\mathsf{R}_{0}=r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})<1
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
s=s(\mathbb{A})<0
and hence again
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<0
. Consequently, if
\mathsf{R}_{0}<1
, then
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
is linearly stable.

Conversely, if
\mathsf{R}_{0}=r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})>1
, then there is
\lambda>0
such that
r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{\lambda})=1
by Lemma
4.1
and there is a nontrivial
I(0)\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega)
with
(1-\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{\lambda})I(0)=0
. Then

I(a):=e^{-\lambda a}U_{A}(a,0)I(0)\,,\quad a\in J\,,

satisfies (
5.5
). Moreover, owing to (
5.2
), we have

\kappa_{1}-\lambda<\kappa_{1}=\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)<\lambda_{0}\left(\frac{2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}\right)\,,

and thus
\kappa_{1}-\lambda
belongs to the resolvent set of
-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}/\kappa_{2}
. Hence,

S:=\left(-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+\frac{2\kappa_{1}\tilde{S}_{*}}{\kappa_{2}}-\kappa_{1}+\lambda\right)^{-1}I(0)\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)

is a nontrivial solution to (
5.4
). That is,
\lambda>0
is an eigenvalue
and the disease-free steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})=(\tilde{S}_{*},0)
is thus linearly unstable.
∎

5.3.
Non-Existence of Endemic Steady States for
\mathsf{R}_{0}\leq 1

An endemic steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
is a steady state to (
1.1
) with
S_{*},I_{*}\geq 0
and
I_{*}\not\equiv 0
.
Note that, setting
I_{0}:=I_{*}(0)
, this is equivalent to finding a positive
(S_{*},I_{0})\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega)
with
I_{0}\not=0
satisfying

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}S_{*}+Q^{0}I_{0}S_{*}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}S_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}S_{*}\,,

(5.7a)

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=S_{*}Q^{0}I_{0}\,.

(5.7b)

As shown next,
\mathsf{R}_{0}>1
is a necessary condition for the existence of an endemic state.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\kappa_{1}>\mu_{0}
and let
\tilde{S}_{*}
be as in Proposition
5.3
. If
\mathsf{R}_{0}=r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})\leq 1
, then there is no positive solution
(S_{*},I_{0})\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega)
to (
5.7
) with
I_{0}\not=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{R}_{0}=r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})\leq 1
and assume for contradiction that there was a positive solution
(S_{*},I_{0})\in W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)\times W_{p,\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\Omega)
to (
5.7
) with
I_{0}\not=0
.
It follows from (
5.1
) and (
5.7a
) that
z:=S_{*}-\tilde{S}_{*}
solves

-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}z+Q^{0}I_{0}z+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(S_{*}+\tilde{S}_{*})z=\kappa_{1}z-I_{0}\,.

Moreover, we infer from (
5.7a
) and Lemma
5.2
that

0<\kappa_{1}=\lambda_{0}\left(Q^{0}I_{0}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}S_{*}\right)<\lambda_{0}\left(Q^{0}I_{0}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(S_{*}+\tilde{S}_{*})\right)\,.

Hence,
\kappa_{1}
belongs to the resolvent set of the operator

-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}+Q^{0}I_{0}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(S_{*}+\tilde{S}_{*})

and consequently, since
I_{0}>0
,

z=-\left(\kappa_{1}-\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}z+Q^{0}I_{0}z+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}(S_{*}+\tilde{S}_{*})\right)^{-1}I_{0}\leq 0\quad\text{in }\ \Omega\,,

where we used the maximum principle from
[
5
, Theorem 13]
. That is,
S_{*}\leq\tilde{S}_{*}
in
\Omega
and thus
S_{*}Q^{0}\leq\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0}
. The Krein-Rutman Theorem now yields for the spectral radii

r(S_{*}Q^{0})<r(\tilde{S}_{*}Q^{0})\leq 1\,,

and therefore that the eigenvector equation
p=S_{*}Q^{0}p
has no positive nontrivial solution in contradiction to
I_{0}>0
solving (
5.7b
).
∎

Remarks 5.5
.

(a)
As noted in the proof of Lemma
5.4
, necessary conditions for the existence of an endemic steady state
(S_{*},I_{*})
with
S_{*},I_{*}\geq 0
and
I_{*}\not\equiv 0
are (see (
5.7
))

(b)

6.2.
The Endemic Steady State
(\bar{S}_{*},\bar{I}_{*})

In case that the basic reproduction number satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{0}=\kappa_{2}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\Pi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a>1\,,

there is an endemic steady state
(\bar{S}_{*},\bar{I}_{*})
given by

\bar{S}_{*}:=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\,,\qquad\bar{I}_{*}(a):=\frac{1}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathsf{R}_{0}}\right)\Pi(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,.

It is convenient to set

\bar{S}_{*}=\mathsf{r}_{0}\kappa_{2}\,,\qquad\bar{I}_{*}(a)=\Pi(a)i_{*}\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad i_{*}:=\mathsf{r}_{0}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\left(1-\mathsf{r}_{0}\right)\,.

In (
4.3
) we have

q_{*}=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\bar{I}_{*}(a)\,\mathrm{d}a=\kappa_{1}\left(1-\mathsf{r}_{0}\right)

so that

A_{1}^{*}=\Delta_{N}-\kappa_{1}\mathsf{r}_{0}

and

P_{*}I=\mathsf{r}_{0}\kappa_{2}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)I(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

In the following we still assume
p>(2\vee n)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume (

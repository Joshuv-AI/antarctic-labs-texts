General stabilizer approach for constructing highly entangled graph states
Zahra Raissi, Adam Burchardt, Edwin Barnes

Abstract
Highly entangled multipartite states such as k k -uniform ( k k -UNI) and absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states serve as critical resources in quantum networking and other quantum information applications. However, there does not yet exist a complete classification of such states, and much remains unknown about their entanglement structure. Here, we substantially broaden the class of known k k -UNI and AME states by introducing a method for explicitly constructing such states that combines classical error correcting codes and qudit graph states. This method in fact constitutes a general recipe for obtaining multipartitite entangled states from classical codes. Furthermore, we show that at least for a large subset of this new class of k k -UNI states, the states are inequivalent under stochastic local operations and classical communication. This subset is defined by an iterative procedure for constructing a hierarchy of k k -UNI graph states.

II
Constructing
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states from classical codes

The connection between classical codes and
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states has been shown to provide a systematic method of constructing a large set of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states
Scott2004
,
Zahra-min-support
,
Zahra-non-min-support
.
In this method, starting from a suitable classical code, a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state of
n
qudits with local dimension
q
(
q

|\vec{c}_{i}\rangle
of the code:

|\phi_{n,0}\rangle=\sum_{i=1,\dots,q^{k}}|\vec{c}_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1,\dots,q^{k}}|c^{(i)}_{1},\dots,c^{(i)}_{n}\rangle\ ,

(1)

where we use that in the language of coding theory, a classical linear code encodes messages into a subset of codewords denoted by the vector
\vec{c}_{i}=(c^{(i)}_{1},\dots,c^{(i)}_{n})

[
MacWilliams
, Chapter 1]
(see Appendix
A
for the explicit definition and more details).
In the corresponding classical codes, we only consider
k\leq n/2
as we focus on
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states. The first index in
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
indicates the number of qudits
n
; the purpose of the second index (here equal to 0) will become clear later when we extend each of the states in Eq. (
1
) to a new family of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states. Here are some explicit examples of AME states constructed using the approach shown in Eq. (
1
):

\begin{split}|\phi_{2,0}\rangle&=\sum_{\alpha=1,\dots,q}\,|\alpha,\alpha\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ |\phi_{3,0}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha=1,\dots,q}\,|\alpha,\alpha,\alpha\rangle,\ \end{split}

(2)

which are Bell and GHZ states, respectively.

How can we check if a given pure state is
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
?
For this, we discuss two equivalent approaches.
The first is to check if all the reduced density matrices for up to
k
qudits are maximally mixed.
In this case, further information regarding the code parameters can be used to prove that the state in Eq. (
1
) is a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state (for more details see
Zahra-min-support
,
Zahra-non-min-support
).
Another approach is based on the structure of the stabilizer formalism (see Appendix
B
for an overview of the stabilizer formalism):
It is known that a pure stabilizer state of
n
qudits is a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state if and only if, in all its stabilizer generators and arbitrary products of them, identity operators appear on at most
n-k-1
different qudits (except for the trivial stabilizer operator given by the tensor product of
n
identity operators).
In this method, one needs to check all possible products of the stabilizer generators.
In the following, we will make heavy use of this second method to prove that a given graph state is
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
.
Although at first glance this method would seem to require checking exponentially many stabilizer operators, in practice it suffices to only check the stabilizer generators. This is because multiplying stabilizer operators does not increase the number of identity operators for the states we consider (more details can be found in Appendix
C.1
).

III
Graph states

Graph states are pure quantum states that are defined based on a graph. A graph
G=(V,\Gamma)
is composed of a set
V
of
n
vertices (each qudit is represented by a vertex), and a set of weighted edges specified by the

\Gamma

,
Hein
,
Hein-2006
,
Bahramgiri
.

The graph state associated with a given graph

is the

eigenstate of the following set of stabilizer generators

,
Hein
,
Hein-2006
,
Bahramgiri

S_{i}=X_{i}\prod_{j}(Z_{j})^{\Gamma_{i,j}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n\,,

where the operators
X
and

are generalized Pauli operators acting on qudits with
q
levels.

X
and
Z
are unitary, traceless, and they satisfy the conditions
X^{q}=Z^{q}=\mathbbm{1}
and
ZX=\omega XZ
, where
\omega=\e^{\iu 2\pi/q}
is a
q
-th root of unity (see Appendix
B
).

We first briefly describe how we can convert the

.
In this case, the adjacency matrix is

\Gamma^{(n,0)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&-A^{\phantom{T}}\\
\hline\cr-A^{T}&0\\
\end{array}\right]\ .

(3)

The matrix

is directly related to the codewords in Eq. (
1
). In particular, these codewords are obtained from

\vec{c}_{i}=(\vec{x}_{i}\,G_{k\times n})=(\vec{x}_{i},\,\vec{x}_{i}\,A)
,
where

is a vector of size

,
G_{k\times n}=[\mathbbm{1}_{k}|A]
, and

is a matrix of size

Note that, for a given classical code, several techniques are known for finding a suitable

[
MacWilliams
, Chapter 11]

,

Instead of starting from the

state in Eq. (

) and converting it into a graph state, we could alternatively start from a graph state with an adjacency matrix as in Eq. (
3
) and ask what properties must

It is generally challenging to find

This alternative perspective in which we start from a graph and ask what properties must the adjacency matrix satisfy in order to obtain a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state allows us to uncover a larger class of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states. This larger class is summarized by the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

A graph state of
n
qudits with local dimension
q
defined by the adjacency matrix

\Gamma^{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&-A^{\phantom{T}}\\
\hline\cr-A^{T}&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}B\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,

(4)

where
B
is an arbitrary matrix and all submatrices of

are nonsingular, is a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}

This theorem says that for every
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
graph state defined by the adjacency matrix
\Gamma^{(n,0)}
from Eq. (
3
), there is an infinite family of additional
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states that can be obtained by replacing the lower-right block of zeros by an arbitrary matrix

in the adjacency matrix. In Appendix
D
, we prove Theorem
1
by showing that any product of the stabilizer generators defined by
\Gamma^{n}
has identity operators on at most
n-k-1

B
is, so long as all the submatrices of
A
are nonsingular.
Although Theorem
1
establishes sufficient conditions for a state to be
k
-UNI, we strongly suspect that the requirement that
A
contains only nonsingular submatrices is also necessary for
k

Given this new class of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states, a natural question to ask is whether all these states are locally equivalent or not. While this question is hard to answer for all possible choices of
B

k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states of Eq. (
1
) by applying certain operators. We then demonstrate that these hierarchical graph states are inequivalent under SLOCC.

IV
Hierarchical graph states

Now we are ready to define hierarchical graph states in which we consider particular forms of

V
generalizing the method of constructing states from codes

Next, we show that the state obtained after the first iteration of the hierarchical procedure described in the previous section,

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we presented a general method for constructing highly entangled
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
and AME states using the stabilizer formalism in conjunction with classical error correcting codes. This method significantly expands the set of known
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
and AME states. We further showed that a special subset of this new class of entangled states can be obtained from an iterative procedure that produces a hierarchy of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
graph states. For the first two levels of the hierarchy, we showed that while the states share the same number of qudits and the same
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
property, they cannot be converted into each other by means of SLOCC operations.
We also presented new
sets of AME states that belong to different SLOCC and, equivalently, LU classes. These results constitute a general approach to constructing new examples of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
and AME states that admit graphical representations, providing further instances of these important resource states and an opportunity to shed more light on the entanglement structure of these highly entangled multipartite states.
Although we have only proven that certain conditions on the underlying classical codes are sufficient for
k
-uniformity, we anticipate that future work will demonstrate rigorously that these conditions are also necessary.

Appendix B
Stabilizer formalism

The stabilizer formalism is a useful tool in different branches of quantum information science like quantum error correcting codes
Gottesman-thesis
,
Zahra-min-support
,
ZahraQECC
, one-time or cluster states
Raussendorf-Briegel

We first recall the definition of the generalized Pauli operators acting on a
q
-dimensional Hilbert space:

\displaystyle X|j\rangle

\displaystyle=|j+1\mod q\rangle,

\displaystyle Z|j\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega^{j}\,|j\rangle\,,

where
\omega\coloneqq\e^{\iu 2\pi/q}
is the
q
-th root of unity.

X
and
Z
are unitary, traceless, and they satisfy the conditions
X^{q}=Z^{q}=\mathbbm{1}
and
ZX=\omega XZ
.
For a collection of
n
qudits, we shall use
subscripts to identify the corresponding Pauli operators.
For example,
Z_{i}
and

operate on the space of
i
-th qudit.
Operators of the form

\omega^{\lambda}X_{1}^{w_{1}}Z_{1}^{w^{\prime}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n}^{w_{n}}Z_{n}^{w^{\prime}_{n}}\ ,

(24)

are called
Pauli products
, where
\lambda,w_{i}
, and
w^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
for all

. For a given number of qudits

of all possible Pauli products (
24
) form a group called the Pauli group

Now we are ready to define the stabilizer formalism.
For a given state
|\psi\rangle
of
n

is called a

The set of all stabilizer operators of a state

If

is called a

, and its density matrix has the following representation:
Stabilizers

|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\dfrac{1}{q^{n}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{|\psi\rangle}}S\ .

(25)

Each stabilizer state has exactly
n
independent and commuting stabilizers, called

,
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{|\psi\rangle}
, such that any operator

S=S_{1}^{w_{1}}\cdots S_{n}^{w_{n}}\,,

for some choice of

For a stabilizer state, there is a straightforward method to calculate the form of a reduced density matrix
\rho_{C}
.
Consider any subsystem

of
n
qudits and an element of the Pauli group

we denote the restriction of the operator

to the subsystem

For example, the operator

has the form
S_{|\{2\}}=X_{2}Z_{2}^{2}

are traceless except when

, one can show the following:

Proposition 4
.

Consider a stabilizer state
|\psi\rangle
of
n
qudits with local dimension
q
and the stabilizer operators
\mathcal{S_{|\psi\rangle}}\subset P_{n}
. Consider any subsystem
C\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
of the
n

, where

.
The reduced density matrix

has the following form:

\rho_{C}=\Tr_{C^{c}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\propto\,\Tr_{C^{c}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{S}_{|\psi\rangle}\\
S_{|C^{c}}=\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes|C^{c}|}\end{subarray}}S\ .

In particular,
\rho_{C}
is maximally mixed, i.e.
\rho_{C}\propto\mathbbm{1}
, if and only if for any
S\in\mathcal{S}_{|\psi\rangle}
,
S_{|C^{c}}=\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes|C^{c}|}
implies

.

Observe that the following corollary is an immediate consequence of the above proposition, because the only stabilizer operators

that contribute after the partial trace are those that have identity operators acting on all qudits belonging to the complementary set
C^{c}
(see also

Corollary 1
.

A pure stabilizer state of
n
qudits is a

different qudits.
We note that there is one stabilizer operator that is formed by tensoring

Appendix E
Inequivalence under SLOCC

It is well known that the number of product states needed to specify a pure state is an upper bound to the rank of all possible reduced density matrices.
It is also known that this number cannot be increased by SLOCC operations.
This implies that, for a
k
-UNI state
|\phi_{(n,0)}\rangle
, and for any subset
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
, one has

\text{rank}(\rho_{S})\leq q^{k}\ ,

(57)

where
\rho_{S}=\Tr_{S^{c}}|\phi_{(n,0)}\rangle\langle\phi_{(n,0)}|
.
This means that all the reductions up to
k
parties of the state
|\phi_{(n,0)}\rangle
are maximally mixed, i.e., it is a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state.
However, if one can find a pure state that is a
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state and in addition to this, there exists at least one subset of size larger than
k
parties such that the reduced density matrix are maximally mixed, then these two
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states cannot be converted into one another via SLOCC.

In this section, we show that some reduced states
\rho_{S}=\Tr_{S^{c}}|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|
, for subsystem size
|S|>k
, are maximally mixed.
More precisely, we show that
\rho_{S}
for a subset
S
comprised of two parts
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, is maximally mixed, where
S_{1}
has size
|S_{1}|=k
and is contained entirely in the support of the first
n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}
qudits, i.e., the subset
\{1,\dots,n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}\}
, and
S_{2}
(with size
|S_{2}|=k^{\mathrm{\star}}
) is contained in the last
n^{\mathrm{\star}}
qudits, i.e, the subset
\{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}+1,\dots,n\}
.
Concretely, we want to show

\begin{split}\rho_{S}&=\Tr_{S^{c}}|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\\
&=\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\Tr_{S_{2}^{c}}|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\\
&\propto\mathbbm{1}\ .\end{split}

(58)

To proceed further, let us first review the structure of the state
|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
.
This state is obtained by applying operator
\mathcal{O}_{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}
on the state
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
, Eq. (
10
), and can be expanded as

\begin{split}|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle&=\mathbbm{1}^{(n-n^{\mathrm{\star}})}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\ |\phi_{n,0}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i}|c^{(i)}_{1},\dots,c^{(i)}_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\otimes Z^{-c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}}\otimes\dots\otimes Z^{-c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-k^{\mathrm{\star}})}}\otimes X^{c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-k^{\mathrm{\star}})+1}}\otimes\dots\otimes X^{c^{(i)}_{n}}\ |\phi_{n^{\mathrm{\star}},0}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i}|c^{(i)}_{1},\dots,c^{(i)}_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\ ,\end{split}

(59)

where as above we denote

|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\coloneqq Z^{-c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}}\otimes\dots\otimes Z^{-c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-k^{\mathrm{\star}})}}\otimes X^{c^{(i)}_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-k^{\mathrm{\star}})+1}}\otimes\dots\otimes X^{c^{(i)}_{n}}\ |\phi_{n^{\mathrm{\star}},0}\rangle\ .

(60)

The state
|\phi_{n^{\mathrm{\star}},0}\rangle
is a
k^{\mathrm{\star}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state constructed from classical codes using the method presented in Eq. (
1
).
For every
i
, the state
|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
is also a
k^{\mathrm{\star}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
state, as acting with local unitaries on a given state does not change the entanglement properties.
Moreover, it is proven in
[
Zahra-non-min-support
, Lemma 1]
that the states
|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
for
i\in\{1,\dots,q^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\}
form a complete orthonormal basis of
k^{\mathrm{\star}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states with
n^{\mathrm{\star}}
qudits, i.e.,
\langle\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\psi_{i^{\prime}}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}
.

To prove that the reduced density matrix
\sigma_{S}
is maximally mixed for the given subset of size
|S|=k+k^{\mathrm{\star}}
, i.e, to show that Eq. (
58
) holds, we check two different cases: (i)
k\leq n^{\mathrm{\star}}
and (ii)
k>n^{\mathrm{\star}}
.

case (i)

We first consider the case
k\leq n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, where we have

\begin{split}\rho_{S}&=\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\Tr_{S_{2}^{c}}|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\\
&=\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}|c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle c_{1}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i^{\prime})}|\otimes\Tr_{S_{2}^{c}}|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{i^{\prime}}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\ .\end{split}

(61)

We know that the states
|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
, Eq. (
60
), are a complete basis of
k^{\mathrm{\star}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states.
When
k=n^{\mathrm{\star}}
the set
i=\{1,\dots,q^{k}\}
contains the entire basis, and otherwise in the case of having
k<n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, we have only part of the basis.
It is obvious that in both cases,
\Tr_{S_{2}^{c}}|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\ \mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}
.
Therefore, the reduced density matrix simplifies to

\begin{split}\rho_{S}&=\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}|c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle c_{1}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i^{\prime})}|\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}\ \delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\\
&=\mathbbm{1}_{k}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}\ .\end{split}

(62)

where we used the fact that
\vec{c}_{i}=(\vec{x}_{i}\,G_{k\times n})=(\vec{x}_{i},\ \vec{x}_{i}A)
are codewords of a suitable classical code such that the number of free indices in the code is equal to
k
.

case (ii)

In the case where
k>n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, there can be a repetition in the basis
|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
, because the number of the states that form a complete orthonormal basis in a Hilbert space
\hiH(n^{\mathrm{\star}},q)\coloneqq\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\otimes n^{\mathrm{\star}}}
, is equal to
q^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}
, while
i\in\{1,\dots,q^{k}\}
.
Therefore, to proceed with the proof, we consider the known conditions on the codewords
\vec{c}_{i}=(c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n}^{(i)})
that are used to construct the state
|\phi_{n,n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
and the orthonormal basis
|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle
.

We can have two conditions depending on the exponents of the
X
and
Z
operators in Eq. (
60
).
One corresponds to the case that the two sequences
c_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n}^{(i)}
and
c_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n}^{(i^{\prime})}
differ in at least one position.
In this case we get
\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{i^{\prime}}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|=\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\ \mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}
.
Therefore, as for case (i), we have
\rho_{S}=\mathbbm{1}_{k}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}
.

In the second condition, the two sequences
c_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n}^{(i)}
and
c_{n-(n^{\mathrm{\star}}-1)}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n}^{(i^{\prime})}
that are part of the codewords
\vec{c}_{i}
and
\vec{c}_{i^{\prime}}
, do not differ.
Therefore, these parts of the codewords, with the size
n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, have overlap.

We know that, based on the properties of classical linear codes (see Appendix
A
), in general, every two codewords
\vec{c}_{i}
and
\vec{c}_{i^{\prime}}
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
have a distance at least equal to the Singleton bound (
15
), i.e.,
d_{H}=n-k+1
.
As sequences of these codewords, with size
n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, have overlap, the remaining part is orthogonal

\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}|c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle c_{1}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i^{\prime})}|=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\sum_{i}|c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}|\ .

(63)

In other words, we used the fact that
d_{H}-n^{\mathrm{\star}}\geq|S_{1}^{c}|=n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}-k
, and considering this, there cannot be any overlap with the sequences
c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}
and
c_{1}^{(i^{\prime})},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i^{\prime})}
of the codewords.
Putting all this together, the reduced density matrix
\rho_{S}
simplifies to

\begin{split}\rho_{S}&=\Tr_{S_{1}^{c}}\sum_{i}|c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle c_{1}^{(i)},\dots,c_{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{(i)}|\otimes\Tr_{S_{2}^{c}}|\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}|\\
&=\mathbbm{1}_{k}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{k^{\mathrm{\star}}}\ ,\end{split}

(64)

in which we used the fact that every linear code contains
k
free indices.

Now, we can conclude that for both cases
k\leq n^{\mathrm{\star}}
and
k>n^{\mathrm{\star}}
, the reduced density matrix
\sigma_{S}
, for
S
given by the union of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, is maximally mixed, where
S_{1}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}\}
with
|S_{1}|=k
and
S_{2}\subseteq\{n-n^{\mathrm{\star}}+1,\dots,n\}
with
|S_{2}|=k^{\mathrm{\star}}
.
Obviously, this can only be achieved if
|S|=k+k^{\mathrm{\star}}\leq n/2
.

Here, we have only proven that the two sets of
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states,
|\phi_{(n,0)}\rangle
and
|\phi_{(n,n^{\mathrm{\star}})}\rangle
,
belong to different SLOCC classes, we leave the proof for the more general cases to future, in which we will show that all the
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states
|\phi_{(n,n^{\mathrm{\star}})}\rangle
and
|\phi_{(n,{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{\prime})}\rangle

with
n^{\mathrm{\star}}\neq{n^{\mathrm{\star}}}^{\prime}
as well as those
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states belonging to different hierarchy levels.

Appendix F
Two inequivalent types of AME states under SLOCC

In this section, we examine the problem of SLOCC-discrimination of different AME states with the same number of qudits
n
and local dimension
q
.
Here, we consider two states
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n,{2}}\rangle
of odd numbers of qudits

defined by Eq. (

We demonstrated in

is an AME state, then the second state (i.e., the state

) is also an AME state.
We shall see that they are not SLOCC-equivalent.

Therefore the method used in

where

.
We separate qudits

and

reads

|\phi_{n,0}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle}_{k-1}\otimes\underbrace{|x_{j}\rangle}_{1}\otimes\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j}\rangle}_{1}\otimes\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle}_{n-k-1},

(66)

where the matrix
A
is separated into four submatrices

A=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c|c}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
\hline\cr a&A_{3}\end{array}\end{bmatrix}

(67)

where
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
are of size
(k-1)\times 1
,
(k-1)\times(n-k-1)
, and
1\times(n-k-1)
respectively, while
a\in GF(q)
.
Since
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
is an AME(
2k+1
,
q
) state, the states
|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle
are linearly independent for different values of
\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1},x_{j}\in[q]
.
In fact, the set of states
\{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle\}_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1},x_{j}\in[q]}\in\hiH(k,q)
forms a basis in
\hiH(k,q)\coloneqq\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\otimes k}
.
Therefore the reduced density matrix
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})
, with
S
corresponding to the first
k+1
qudits reads

\rho_{S}\Big(\phi_{n,{0}}\Big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j}\in[q]\end{subarray}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle|x_{j}\rangle|\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\langle x_{j}|\langle\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j}|.

(68)

Secondly, we study the state
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
and its reduction
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})
to the subsystem
S=\{1,\ldots,k+1\}
containing the first
(k+1)
qudits.
Note that the maximally entangled state of two qudits with the local dimension
q
is locally equivalent to the state

|\phi_{2,0}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\ell\in[q]}|\ell,\ell\rangle.

(69)

The state
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
is constructed from
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
and
|\phi_{2,0}\rangle
via the non-local operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
acting on the last two qudits of
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
, as indicated in
eqs.

9
and
10
.
Because of the properties that
k\mbox{-}\mathrm{UNI}
states have, after any permutation of its subsystem, without loss of generality, we can change the subspace of the action of the operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
onto subsystem
\{k,k+1\}
.
In this way, the state
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
reads

|\phi_{n,2}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j},\ell\in[q]\end{subarray}}\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle}_{k-1}\otimes\underbrace{X^{x_{j}}|\ell\rangle}_{1}\otimes\underbrace{Z^{\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j}}|\ell\rangle}_{1}\otimes\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle}_{n-k-1}.

(70)

As we noticed before, states
\{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle\}_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1},x_{j}\in[q]}\in\hiH(k,q)
form a basis of
\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\otimes k}
.
Therefore the state
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
reduced to the subsystem
S
reads

\rho_{S}\Big(\phi_{n,{2}}\Big)=\dfrac{1}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[q]}\omega^{(\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j})(\ell-\ell^{\prime})}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle|x_{j}+\ell\rangle|\ell\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\langle x_{j}+\ell^{\prime}|\langle\ell^{\prime}|,

(71)

where
\omega
is a
q
th root of unity.
Note the difference in the last two positions in

\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})
and
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})
given in
eq.

68
and
eq.

71
, respectively.
We shall show the following.

Lemma 5
.

Both reduced density matrices are LU-equivalent by the following transformation:

U=\underbrace{\mathbbm{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}}_{k-1}\otimes W\otimes V,

(72)

where unitary matrices
W=[w_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{q}
and
V=[v_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{q}
are defined by
w_{ij}:=\frac{1}{q}\omega^{-aij+j^{2}T}
and
v_{ij}:=\frac{1}{q}\omega^{ij-j^{2}T}

where
\omega
is a
q
-th root of unity, while
T
is an element in
GF(q)
such that
2T=a
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\Big(\phi_{n,{0}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\in[q]}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle|\ell\rangle|\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+a\ell\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\langle\ell|\langle\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+a\ell|\xmapsto{\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes W\otimes V}

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\Big(\dfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,m^{\prime},\\
k,k^{\prime}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\sum_{\ell\in[q]}\omega^{\big(-a\ell(m-m^{\prime})+T(m^{2}-{m^{\prime}}^{2})+(\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+a\ell)(k-k^{\prime})-T(k^{2}-{k^{\prime}}^{2})\big)}|m,k\rangle\langle m^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\Big).

(73)

In the above expression, we change the summation indices
m,m^{\prime}
to
j:=m-k
and
j^{\prime}:=m^{\prime}-k^{\prime}
respectively.
In this way, the right-hand side of
eq.

73
reads

\displaystyle\text{(\ref{eq:7})}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime},\\
j,j^{\prime}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in[q]}\omega^{\big(-a\ell(j+k-j^{\prime}-k^{\prime})+T((j+k)^{2}-{(j^{\prime}+k^{\prime})}^{2})+(\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+a\ell)(k-k^{\prime})-T(k^{2}-{k^{\prime}}^{2})\big)}\Bigg)|k+j,k\rangle\langle k^{\prime}+j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime},\\
j,j^{\prime}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{q^{2}}\underbrace{\sum_{\ell\in[q]}\omega^{-a\ell(j-j^{\prime})}}_{=q\delta_{jj^{\prime}}}\omega^{T(j^{2}-{j^{\prime}}^{2})+2T(kj-k^{\prime}j^{\prime})}\omega^{(\vec{x}_{i}A_{1})(k-k^{\prime})}\Bigg)|k+j,k\rangle\langle k^{\prime}+j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime},\\
j,j^{\prime}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\dfrac{1}{q}\omega^{(2Tj+\vec{x}_{i}A_{1})(k-k^{\prime})}\Bigg)|k+j,k\rangle\langle k^{\prime}+j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle\langle\vec{x}_{i}|\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in[q]}\sum_{j\in[q]}\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{q}\omega^{(aj+\vec{x}_{i}A_{1})(k-k^{\prime})}\Bigg)|k+j,k\rangle\langle k^{\prime}+j,k^{\prime}|,

which is equal to
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})
(compare with
eq.

71
).
∎

Now, we recall the notion of a
monomial matrix
, and the
support of a state
.
The
support
of a state
|\psi\rangle
is the number of non-zero coefficients when
|\psi\rangle
is written in the computational basis.

Definition 1
.

A unitary matrix
M
is called a unitary monomial matrix if one of the following holds:

1.

M
has exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each column,

2.

M
is a product of a permutation and diagonal matrix,

3.

M
does not change the support of any quantum state.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We show the statement by contradiction.
Assume that states
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
are LU-equivalent by some unitary matrices:

U_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{k+1}\otimes U_{k+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{n}

We shall keep ”
\otimes
” in the notation in order to distinguish it from matrix multiplication.
Since the (partial) trace is invariant under cycling permutations, one can show that:

\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})=\Big(U_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{k+1}\Big)\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})\Big(U_{1}^{\dagger}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{k+1}^{\dagger}\Big)

(74)

Hence, the operator
\widetilde{U}:=(U_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{k+1})
provides the local equivalence between
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})
and
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})
.
Notice that in
lemma

5
, we pointed out that the local matrices
U=\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes W\otimes V
provide the LU-equivalence between
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})
and
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{2}})
.
Therefore
\widetilde{U}U
provides an LU-equivalence between
\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})
and itself, i.e.

\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})=\widetilde{U}U\rho_{S}(\phi_{n,{0}})U^{\dagger}\widetilde{U}^{\dagger}.

(75)

Note that the operator
\widetilde{U}U
has the following form

\widetilde{U}U=U_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{k-1}\otimes U_{k}W\otimes U_{k+1}V.

(76)

According to
[
BurchardtRaissi20
, Proposition 2]
, all matrices in
eq.

76
, i.e.
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k-1},U_{k}W,U_{k+1}V
are monomial matrices.

We shall see that such a restriction on matrices
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k-1},\ U_{k}W,\ U_{k+1}V
leads to a contradiction.
To sum up the discussion so far, LU-equivalence between states
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
has the following form:

M_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{k-1}\otimes M_{k}W\otimes M_{k+1}V\otimes U_{k+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{n}

where
U_{i}
are arbitrary unitary matrices,
W,V
are defined in
lemma

5
, while
M_{i}
are products of diagonal and permutation matrices.
From the form of
eq.

66
, we have

|\phi_{n,2}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\vec{M}|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle}_{k-1}\otimes\underbrace{B_{\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}}}_{2}\otimes\underbrace{\vec{U}|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle}_{n-k-1},

(77)

where
\vec{M}=M_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{k-1}
, and
\vec{U}=U_{k+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{n}
, and

B_{\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}}:=M_{k}W|x_{j}\rangle\otimes M_{k+1}V|\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j}\rangle.

(78)

Since the matrix
A
is nonsingular, its
k\times k
submatrix
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A_{2}\\
\hline\cr A_{3}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is also non-singular. As a consequence the following
n-k-1=k
states:

|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle

are linearly independent for different values of multi-indices
\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}\in[q^{k}]
. Similarly, their unitary transformation

\vec{U}|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle

remains linearly independent. Furthermore, from the form of matrices
V
and
W
, the support of the state
B_{\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}}
equals
q^{2}
for any multi-index
\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}\in[q^{k}]
. Therefore, we conclude that the support of a state (
77
) equals at least
q^{k+2}
.

On the other hand, from
eq.

70
, we have

|\phi_{n,2}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{x}_{i}\in[q]^{k-1}\\
x_{j}\in[q]\end{subarray}}\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}\rangle}_{k-1}\otimes\underbrace{C_{\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}}}_{2}\otimes\underbrace{|\vec{x}_{i}A_{2}+x_{j}A_{3}\rangle}_{n-k-1},

(79)

where

C_{\vec{x}_{i},x_{j}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\ell\in[q]}|\ell+x_{j}\rangle\otimes\omega^{(\vec{x}_{i}A_{1}+ax_{j})\ell}|\ell\rangle.

(80)

Clearly, the support of a state
eq.

79
equals to
q^{k+1}
. As a consequence, the support of states in
eq.

77
and
eq.

79
differs. Note that both equations present the same state
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
, hence should have the same support. This ends the proof.
∎

In such a way, we have shown that two families of AME(
n
,
q
) states:
|\phi_{n,0}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n,2}\rangle
for
n=2k+1
are not SLOCC-equivalent and hence LU equivalent.

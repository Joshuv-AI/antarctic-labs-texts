Harada’s conjecture II and Gramian determinants
Toshiyuki Abe

Abstract
Let G G be a finite group. Harada’s conjecture II states that the ratio of the product of all the number of elements in conjugacy classes over that of all degrees of irreducible complex characters of G G is an integer. The ratio is called Harada’s number. In this article, we discuss the Harada’s number from the view point of Gramian determinants for suitable inner spaces and introduce an invariants generalizing the square of Harada’s number associated to central characters of G G . We also give a necessary and sufficient condition so that Harada’s conjecture II holds. We calculate explicitly Harada’s numbers in some examples by using the method given in this article.

Introduction

This is an article for the aim of the affirmative solution to Harada’s conjecture II.
The conjecture was presented in
[
Ha
]
by Harada (It was also mentioned to Harada’s conjecture I in
[
Ha
]
).
The conjecture relates between the sizes of conjugacy classes and the degrees of complex irreducible characters, and states that Harada’s number of a finite group is always an integer.
This conjecture has been studied and verified for some concrete models or series of groups (see
[
Ha
]
,
[
Hi
]
,
[
K
]
,
[
Sug
]
and
[
M
]
).
In this article, we consider Gramian determinants on a subspace of the center of the group algebras with suitable basis and give a necessary and sufficient condition to Harada’s conjecture II.
By using the technique, we give alternative calculations of Harada’s numbers explicitly for some known example.

We shall give a statement of Harada’s conjecture II.
Let
G
be a finite group, and consider the set
\{K_{1},\cdots,K_{s}\}
of all conjugacy classes and the set
\{\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{s}\}
of all complex irreducible characters.

Harada’s number
is the ratio

h(G):=\frac{|K_{1}|\cdots|K_{s}|}{\chi_{1}(1)\cdots\chi_{s}(1)},

where
1
denotes the unit of a group.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Harada’s conjecture II)
For any finite group
G
,
h(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In the case
G
is abelian,
h(G)=1
since
|K_{i}|=\chi_{i}(1)=1
for all
i
.
In the non abelian case, calculations of the numbers elements in conjugacy classes and degrees of irreducible characters are sufficient and hence the character table

X=(\chi_{i}(g_{j}))_{i,j=1,\cdots,s},

of
G
gives enough information to determine
h(G)
, where
g_{i}
is a representative of
K_{i}
for each
i
.
In particular, it is verified that every finite simple group
G
in
[
ATLAS
]
satisfies
h(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
by Chigira (as announced in
[
Ha
]
).
It is also proved that
h(G)
is integer if
G
is the symmetric groups and the alternating groups by Hida (
[
Hi
]
) by using the well known correspondence of irreducible modules over
\mathbb{C}
and conjugacy classes via Young diagrams.
In another direction, Kiyota proved that
h(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
for finite groups
G
whose every Sylow subgroup is abelian (see
[
K
]
) in help of Brauer’s height
0
conjecture.
Recently, it has been proved for a some series of finite groups of Lie type (
[
Sug
]
) and some groups of nilpotent class
2
(
[
M
]
).

One of the main result of this article is that Harada’s number of
G
can be expressed by means of the structure of the center
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
of the the usual group algebra
\mathbb{C}G
.
We write
[g]
for the vector in
\mathbb{C}G
corresponding to
g\in G
, and for any subset
U\subset G
, write
[U]=\sum_{x\in U}[x]
.
Then the ordered set of the class sums
[K_{1}],\cdots,[K_{s}]
forms a basis of
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
It is also well known that

e_{\chi_{i}}=\frac{\chi_{i}(1)}{|G|}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\chi_{i}(g_{j}^{-1})[K_{j}]

for
i=1,\cdots,s
are primitive idempotents and that they also forms a basis of
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
For any
i,j
,
[K_{j}]e_{\chi_{i}}=\omega_{ij}e_{\chi_{i}}
with constant

\omega_{ij}=\frac{\chi_{i}(g_{j})|K_{j}|}{\chi_{i}(1)}.

The determinant of the matrix
W=(\omega_{ij})_{i,j=1,\cdots,s}
is
\det W=h(G)\det X
as pointed out by Harada in
[
Ha
]
.

We note that
|\det W|^{2}=\det({}^{t}\overline{W}W)=h(G)^{2}c(G)
, where

c(G)=\det({}^{t}\overline{X}X)=|C_{G}(g_{1})|\cdots|C_{G}(g_{s})|.

Hence
h(G)
appears in the absolute value of a Hermitian matrix
{}^{t}\overline{W}W
, that is Gramian matrix of a certain Hermitian space over
\mathbb{C}
.
As a desired Hermitian space, we can choose the algebra
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
We see that
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
becomes a Hermitian space equipped with a Hermitian form defined by

\langle u|v\rangle:=\tr_{Z(\mathbb{C}G)}\overline{u}v

for
u,v\in Z(\mathbb{C}G)
, where
\tr_{Z(\mathbb{C}G)}u
is the trace of the linear map given by
w\mapsto uw
for
w\in Z(\mathbb{C}G)
and the map
\overline{\cdot}
is a skew-linear map which
[g]
sends to
[g^{-1}]
for
g\in G

For any subset
{\mathcal{B}}
of
Z(\mathbb{C}G)
, we consider the absolute value
\gamma({\mathcal{B}})
of the determinant of the Gramian matrix
(\langle u|v\rangle)_{u,v\in{\mathcal{B}}}
;

\gamma({\mathcal{B}})=|\det((\langle u|v\rangle)_{u,v\in{\mathcal{B}}})|.

Then we can show that

\gamma(G):=\gamma(\{[K_{1}],\cdots,[K_{s}]\})\in\mathbb{Z}

is nonzero and admits with
\det({}^{t}\overline{W}W)
.
Since
\gamma(G)
and
c(G)
are integers, we have the following criterion.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
2.3
)

Let
G
be a finite group.
Then
\gamma(G)=h(G)^{2}c(G)
.
Therefore,
h(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
c(G)
divides
\gamma(G)
.

This theorem implies that Harada’s conjecture II can be verified by using only the group structure of
G
, not using the classification of irreducible characters.
We consider a number
\mu(G):=h(G)^{2}=\gamma(G)/c(G)
for a finite group
G
.
We generalize the number
\mu(G)
to
\mu_{\phi}(G)
for any irreducible character
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
of a central subgroup
Z
of
G
.
For any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
, we consider an idempotent
e_{\phi}=\frac{1}{|Z|}\sum_{z\in Z}\phi(z^{-1})[z]\in Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
Then we take a suitable basis
{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}\subset\{e_{\phi}[K_{1}],\cdots,e_{\phi}[K_{s}]\}
of
e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)
and define
\gamma_{\phi}(G):=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi})
.
We also define
c_{\phi}(G)
as a generalization of

.
Then we define
\mu_{\phi}(G)=\gamma_{\phi}(G)/c_{\phi}(G)
.
The number
\mu_{\phi}(G)
admits

in the case
\phi=\mathbf{1}
is the trivial character of
Z=\{1\}
.
We also prove that

\mu(G)=\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\mu_{\phi}(G)

for any central subgroup
Z
of
G
as in Theorem
3.4
.
Unfortunately, we have examples where
\mu_{\phi}(G)
is not an integer for some faithful
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
We also consider the case
\Ker\phi=N
is not
\{1\}
.
In this case,
\phi
induces a faithful character
\overline{\phi}
of
Z/N
and we show that
\mu_{\phi}(G)=\kappa_{N}(G)^{2}\mu_{\overline{\phi}}(G/N)
for some integer
\kappa_{N}(G)
defined for the subgroup
N
of
Z
.

One of the aim of this article is to demonstrate calculations of
\gamma_{\phi}(G)
and
\mu_{\phi}(G)
for some finite groups
G
.
In fact, we do in the case
G
is an abelian group, a dihedral group, a semi-dihedral group, a quaternion group, a
p
-group which contains a maximal cyclic subgroup for
p>2
, and central products of finite groups.

This article is organized as follows.
In Section 2.1, we give a preliminary of Hermitian spaces and Gramian determinants.
In Section 2.2, we state Harada’s conjecture II.
In Section 2.3, we give a criterion to Harada’s conjecture.
Section 3 is devoted to a generalization of the square of Harada’s number for central characters.
In Section 3.1, we generalize
\mu(G)

\mu_{\phi}(G)
for a characters of a central subgroup.
In Section 3.2, we show the factorization property of
\mu(G)
.
In Section 3.3, we consider the relation of
\mu_{\phi}(G)
and
\mu_{\overline{\phi}})(G/\ker\phi)

\overline{(}\phi)

We give some examples in Section 4.

Harada’s conjecture II and a criterion for the conjecture

Let

A number

Let
G

In this section, we shall define a number
\gamma_{\phi}(G)
and an integer

for a character

As their ratio, we shall define
\mu_{\phi}(G)=\gamma_{\phi}(G)/c_{\phi}(G)

Let

\displaystyle e_{\phi}:=\frac{1}{|Z|}\sum_{z\in Z}\overline{\phi(z)}[z]

\displaystyle[\{e_{\phi}|\phi\in\Irr(Z)\}:\{[z]|z\in Z\}]^{2}=|Z|^{-2|Z|}|\det(\overline{\phi(z)})_{\phi\in\Irr(Z),z\in Z}|^{2}=|Z|^{-|Z|},

We see that

We regard

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\Conj(G)=\{zK_{j}|z\in Z,j\in I_{G/Z}\}.

is a
Z
-subset of
\Conj(G)
.
Let

I(G:Z)_{N}=\{j\in I_{G/Z}|{\rm Ann}_{Z}(K_{j})=N\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
N\leq Z
,
\sum_{X\leq N}|I(G:Z)_{X}|=|I(G/N:Z/N)_{\{1\}}|
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{N}:Z\rightarrow Z/N
be the canonical projection.
We note that
\{\pi_{N}(K_{i})|i\in I_{G/Z}\}
is a complete set of representatives of
Z/N
-orbits in
\Conj(G/N)
.
One also has

{\rm Ann}_{Z/N}(\pi_{N}(K_{i}))=\pi_{N}({\rm Ann}_{Z}(K_{i})N),\quad i\in I_{G/N}.

In particular,
{\rm Ann}_{Z/N}(\pi_{N}(K_{i}))=\{1\}
if and only if
{\rm Ann}_{Z}(K_{i})N=N
and this is equivalent to
{\rm Ann}_{Z}(K_{i})\leq N
.
This implies that

\displaystyle I(G/N,Z/N)_{\{1\}}=\bigsqcup_{X\leq N}I(G,Z)_{X}.

(3.4)

Hence we have the lemma.
∎

We set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}:

\displaystyle=\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|X\leq\Ker\phi,j\in I(G:Z)_{X}\}

(3.5)

for any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

For
K\in\Conj(G)
, if
{\rm Ann}_{Z}(K)
is not a subgroup of
\Ker\phi
, then
e_{\phi}[K]=0
.

(2)

The set
{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}
is a basis of
e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)
and

\dim e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)=\sum_{X\leq\Ker\phi}|I(G:Z)_{X}|=|I(G/\Ker\phi:Z/\Ker\phi)_{\{1\}}|.

Proof.

(1) Let
z\in{\rm Ann}_{Z}(K)-\Ker\phi
.
Then for any
g\in K
, there exists
h\in G
such that
g^{h}=zg
.
Thus
e_{\phi}[g^{h}]=\phi(z)e_{\phi}[g]
.
Therefore,
e_{\phi}[K]=\phi(z)e_{\phi}[K]
.
Since
\phi(z)\neq 1
, we have
e_{\phi}[K]=0
.

(2)
It follows from (
3.3
) that
\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|j\in I_{G/Z}\}
spans
e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
By (1),
e_{\phi}[K_{j}]=0
if
j\in I(G:Z)_{N}
for
N\leq Z
which is not a subgroup of
\Ker\phi
.
Hence
{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}
spans
e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.

On the other hand, we note that

{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm conj}(G)=\bigsqcup_{N\leq Z}\bigsqcup_{j\in I(G:Z)_{N}}\{[zK_{j}]|zN\in Z/N\}

is linearly independent.
We also consider the set

{\mathcal{B}}(G:Z):=\bigsqcup_{N\leq Z}\bigsqcup_{j\in I(G:Z)_{N}}\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|\phi\in\Irr(Z/N)\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{B}}(G:Z)&=\bigsqcup_{N\leq Z}\bigsqcup_{\phi\in\Irr(Z/N)}\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|j\in I(G:Z)_{N}\}\\
&=\bigsqcup_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\bigsqcup_{N\leq\Ker\phi}\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|j\in I(G:Z)_{N}\}\\
&=\bigsqcup_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi},\end{split}

(3.6)

where for any subgroup
N\leq Z
, we identify

\Irr(Z/N)=\{\phi\in\Irr(Z)|N\leq\Ker\phi\}.

As in (
3.2
), we see that

\displaystyle[\{e_{\phi}[K_{j}]|\phi\in\Irr(Z/N)\}:\{[zK_{j}]|zN\in Z/N\}]^{2}=|Z/N|^{-|Z/N|}

for
N\leq Z
and
j\in I(G:Z)_{N}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{B}}(G:Z):{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm conj}(G)\right]^{2}=\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{-|Z/N||I(G:Z)_{N}|}.

(3.7)

Therefore,
{\mathcal{B}}(G:Z)
is linearly independent and so is
{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}
.
Hence it is a basis of
e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)
.
In particular, by Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\dim e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)=|{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}|=\sum_{X\leq\Ker\phi}|I(G:Z)_{X}|=|I(G/\Ker\phi:Z/\Ker\phi)_{\{1\}}|.

∎

For
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
, we define

\displaystyle\gamma_{\phi}(G):=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}).

We also consider the integer

c_{\phi}(G):=\prod_{N\leq\Ker\phi}\prod_{j\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|C_{G}(g_{j})||Z/N|^{-1}.

and define

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}(G):=\frac{\gamma_{\phi}(G)}{c_{\phi}(G)}.

Let
\mathbf{1}
is the trivial character of the unit group
\{1\}
.
We note that
I(G:\{1\})_{\{1\}}
becomes an index set of
\Conj(G)
and hence
|I(G:\{1\})_{\{1\}}|=|\Conj(G)|
.
We also see that
\gamma_{\mathbf{1}}(G)=\gamma(G)
and
c_{\mathbf{1}}(G)=c(G)
.
Hence

\mu_{\mathbf{1}}(G)=\mu(G)=h(G)^{2}.

Therefore,
\mu_{\phi}(G)
is a generalization of the square of Harada’s number.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
Since
\gamma_{\phi}(G)
is an absolute value of a determinant and
|\phi(z)|=1
for any
z\in Z
, it is independent of a choice of a complete set
\{K_{i}|i\in I(G/Z)\}
of representatives of
Z
-orbits in
\Conj(G)
.

3.2
A factorization of
\mu(G)

Let
G
be a finite group and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
.
It follows from (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(G)&=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}_{\rm conj}(G))\\
&=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G:Z))[{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm conj}(G):{\mathcal{B}}(G:Z)]^{2}\\
&=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G:Z))\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|Z/N||I(G:Z)_{N}|}.\end{split}

(3.8)

For distinct
\phi,\psi\in\Irr(Z)
,
\langle{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}|{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\psi}\rangle=0
since
e_{\phi}e_{\psi}=0
.
Therefore, by (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G:Z))=\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\gamma_{\phi}(G).

(3.9)

Therefore, by (
3.8
) we have

\displaystyle\gamma(G)=\left(\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\gamma_{\phi}(G)\right)\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|Z/N||I(G:Z)_{N}|}\right).

(3.10)

On the other hand, we can deduce

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}c_{\phi}(G)\right)\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|Z/N||I(G:Z)_{N}|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\prod_{N\leq\Ker\phi}\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|C_{G}(g_{i})||Z/N|^{-1}\right)\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|I(G:Z)_{N}|}\right)^{|Z/N|}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z/N)}\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|C_{G}(g_{i})||Z/N|^{-1}\right)\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|I(G:Z)_{N}|}\right)^{|Z/N|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{N\leq Z}\left(|Z/N|^{-|I(G:Z)_{N}|}\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|C_{G}(g_{i})|\right)^{|Z/N|}\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}|Z/N|^{|I(G:Z)_{N}|}\right)^{|Z/N|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{N\leq Z}\left(\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|C_{G}(g_{i})|\right)^{|Z/N|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{N\leq Z}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I(G:Z)_{N}\\
zN\in Z/N\end{subarray}}|C_{G}(zg_{i})|

\displaystyle=c(G).

Combining this and (
3.10
), we have

Theorem 3.4
.

For any subgroup
Z
of
Z(G)
,

\displaystyle\mu(G)=\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\mu_{\phi}(G).

(3.11)

3.3
\mu_{\phi}(G)
in the case
\phi
is non-faithful

Let
G
be a finite group and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
.
Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
Then
\phi
induces a faithful irreducible character
\overline{\phi}
of
Z/\Ker\phi
defined by
\overline{\phi}(z\Ker\phi)=\phi(z)
for
z\in Z
.
Recall the canonical projection
\pi_{N}:G\rightarrow G/N
for any subgroup
N
of
Z
.
For the later use, we set

\kappa_{N}(G):=\prod_{X\leq N}|X|^{|I(G:Z)_{X}|}

for any
N\leq Z
.
We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
Then

\gamma_{\phi}(G)=\kappa_{\Ker\phi}(G)^{2}\gamma_{\overline{\phi}}(G/\Ker\phi).

Proof.

Set
N=\Ker\phi
.
We extend
\pi_{N}
to an algebra homomorphism

\pi_{N}:\mathbb{C}G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G/N,\quad[g]\mapsto[\pi_{N}(g)],\quad g\in G.

We then have

\displaystyle\pi_{N}(e_{\phi}[u])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Z|}\sum_{z\in Z}\phi(z)^{-1}[\pi_{N}(zu)]

\displaystyle=\frac{|N|}{|Z|}\sum_{\lambda\in Z/N}\overline{\phi}(\lambda)^{-1}[\lambda\pi_{N}(u)]

\displaystyle=e_{\overline{\phi}}[\pi_{N}(u)].

Thus we have an algebra homomorphism

F_{\phi}:e_{\phi}\mathbb{C}G\rightarrow e_{\overline{\phi}}\mathbb{C}G/N,\quad e_{\phi}[g]\mapsto e_{\overline{\phi}}[\pi_{N}(g)],\quad g\in G

as the restriction of
\pi_{N}
to
e_{\phi}\mathbb{C}G
.
In fact,
F_{\phi}
is an isomorphism since
F_{\phi}
is surjective and

\displaystyle\dim e_{\phi}\mathbb{C}G=[G:Z]=[G/N:Z/N]=\dim e_{\overline{\phi}}\mathbb{C}G/N.

Let
X\leq N
and
i\in I(G:Z)_{X}
.
Then

\displaystyle F_{\phi}(e_{\phi}[K_{i}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in K_{i}}F_{\phi}(e_{\phi}[g])

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in K_{i}}e_{\overline{\phi}}[\pi_{N}(g)]

\displaystyle=|X|\sum_{\overline{g}\in\pi_{N}(K_{i})}e_{\overline{\phi}}[\overline{g}]

\displaystyle=|X|e_{\overline{\phi}}[\pi_{N}(K_{i})].

This shows that
F_{\phi}({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi})
is a basis of
e_{\overline{\phi}}Z(\mathbb{C}G/N)
and

[F_{\phi}({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}):{\mathcal{B}}({G}/N)_{\overline{\phi}}]=\prod_{X\leq N}|X|^{|I(G:Z)_{X}|}=\kappa_{N}(G).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\phi}(G)=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi})

\displaystyle=\gamma(F_{\phi}({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}))

\displaystyle=\gamma({\mathcal{B}}(G/N)_{\overline{\phi}})[F_{\phi}({\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}):{\mathcal{B}}({G}/Z)_{\overline{\phi}}]^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{N}(G)^{2}\gamma_{\overline{\phi}}({G}/N).

∎

We next consider the numbers
c_{\phi}(G)
and
c_{\overline{\phi}}(G/N)
for
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
with
N=\Ker\phi
.
Let
X\leq N
and
i\in I(G:Z)_{X}
.
Since
|K_{i}|=|X||\pi_{N}(K_{i})|
, we have

\displaystyle|C_{G}(g_{i})|=\frac{|G|}{|K_{i}|}=\frac{|G/N||N|}{|X||\pi_{N}(K_{i})|}=|N/X||C_{G/N}(\pi_{N}(g_{i}))|.

Since

I(G/N,Z/N)_{\{1\}}=\bigsqcup_{X\leq N}I(G:Z)_{X}

by (
3.4
),
we deduce that

\displaystyle c_{\phi}(G)

\displaystyle=\prod_{X\leq N}\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{X}}|Z/X|^{-1}|C_{G}({g}_{i})|

\displaystyle=\prod_{X\leq N}\prod_{i\in I(G:Z)_{X}}|N/X||Z/X|^{-1}|C_{G/N}(\pi_{N}(g_{i}))|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I(G/N:Z/N)_{\{1\}}}|Z/N|^{-1}|C_{G/N}(\pi_{N}(g_{i}))|

\displaystyle=c_{\overline{\phi}}(G/N),

where we note that
\overline{\phi}
is faithful.
By Lemma
3.5
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{\phi}(G)}{c_{\phi}(G)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\Ker\phi}(G)^{2}\frac{\gamma_{\overline{\phi}}(G/\Ker\phi)}{c_{\overline{\phi}}(G/\Ker\phi)}.

Now we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
.
Then for any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
,

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}(G)=\kappa_{\Ker\phi}(G)^{2}\mu_{\overline{\phi}}(G/\Ker\phi).

(3.12)

Let
\mathbf{1}_{Z}
be a character of
Z
defined by
\mathbf{1}_{Z}(z)=1
for any
z\in Z
.
Since
\overline{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}=\mathbf{1}
and
\mu(G)=\mu_{\mathbf{1}}(G)
, as a corollary of Proposition
3.6
, we have

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)=(\kappa_{Z}(G)h(G/Z))^{2}.

(3.13)

As a corollary of Theorem
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
.
Then

\displaystyle h(G)^{2}=\left(\prod_{N\leq Z}\kappa_{N}(G)^{|Z/N|}\right)^{2}\left(\prod_{\phi\in\Irr(Z)}\mu_{\overline{\phi}}(G/\Ker\phi)\right).

(3.14)

Finally, we consider the case
Z
is of prime order.
Let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
and
Z
a subgroup of
Z(G)
of order
p
.
Then we have

\kappa_{Z}(G)=\prod_{X\leq Z}|X|^{|I(G:Z)_{X}|}=p^{|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}.

We also see that
\Irr(Z)-\{\mathbf{1}_{Z}\}=\{\phi^{a}|a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\}
for some
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.
Therefore, we have

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that
Z
is of prime order
p
.
Take
\phi\in\Irr(Z)-\{\mathbf{1}_{Z}\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mu(G)=p^{2|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}\mu(G/Z)\left(\prod_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\mu_{\phi^{a}}(G)\right).

(3.15)

If
p>2
then

h(G)=p^{|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}h(G/Z)\left(\prod_{a=1}^{(p-1)/2}\mu_{\phi^{a}}(G)\right).

Proof.

The second assertion is verified by the fact that
\gamma_{\phi}(G)=\gamma_{\phi^{-1}}(G)
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

By Corollary
3.9
, if all of the conditions (i)
p||Z(G)|
for some prime
p>2
, (ii)
\mu_{\phi}(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
and (iii)
h(G/Z)\in\mathbb{Z}
are valid, then
h(G)\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We shall see that (ii) does not hold generally in Section 4.

4
Examples

In this section, we recalculate Harada’s numbers for some known examples by calculating
\mu_{\phi}(G)
for some characters
\phi
.

4.1
Abelian groups

It is clear that Harada’s number of an abelian group
A
is
1
.
We shall prove that
\mu_{\phi}(A)
is also
1
for any subgroup
Z
of
A
and
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a finite abelian group and
Z
its subgroup.
For any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
,
\mu_{\phi}(G)=1
.

Proof.

We take a section
s:A/Z\rightarrow A
.
For simplicity, assign
s(\pi_{Z}(1))=1
.
We may set
I_{A/Z}=A/Z
as

\Conj(A/Z)=\{\{u\}|u\in A/Z\}.

Since
A
is abelian, we have
I(A:Z)_{\{1\}}=I_{A/Z}=A/Z
and
I(A:Z)_{N}=\emptyset
for any
\{1\}\neq N\leq Z
.
In particular,
|I(A:Z)_{\{1\}}|=|A/Z|
and
|I(A:Z)_{N}|=0
for
N\neq\{1\}
.

Let
\alpha:A/Z\times A/Z\rightarrow Z
be a
2
-cocycle defined by

\alpha(u,v)=s(u)s(v)s(uv)^{-1}

for
u,v\in A/Z
.
For any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
, we have
{\mathcal{B}}(A)_{\phi}=\{e_{\phi}[s(u)]|u\in A/Z\}
.
Since
[s(u)][s(v)]=[\alpha(u,v)s(uv)]
we have

e_{\phi}[s(u)]e_{\phi}[s(v)]=\phi(\alpha(u,v))e_{\phi}[s(uv)].

This implies that
\tr_{e_{\phi}\mathbb{C}A}e_{\phi}[s(u)]=\delta_{u,\pi_{Z}(1)}|A/Z|
for
u\in A/Z
.
Therefore,

\langle e_{\phi}[s(u)]|e_{\phi}[s(v)]\rangle=\tr_{e_{\phi}\mathbb{C}A}e_{\phi}[s(u)^{-1}s(v)]=\delta_{u,v}\frac{\phi(\alpha(u^{-1},v))}{\phi(\alpha(u^{-1},u))}|A/Z|

for
u,v\in A/Z
.
Since
|\phi(\alpha(u,v))|=1
for any
u,v\in A/Z
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{\phi}(A)=|A/Z|^{|A/Z|}.

(4.1)

We also see that

c_{\phi}(A)=\prod_{N\leq\Ker\phi}\prod_{j\in I(G:Z)_{N}}|Z/N|^{-1}|A|=(|Z|^{-1}|A|)^{|I(A;Z)_{\{1\}}|}=|A/Z|^{|A/Z|}.

Hence we have
\mu_{\phi}(A)=1
.
∎

4.2
Dihedral groups

In this section we consider the dihedral groups
G={{\rm D}}_{2n}
of order
2n
for
n\geq 3
.
We use the following presentation of the dihedral group

G={{\rm D}}_{2n}=\langle\sigma,\tau|\sigma^{n}=\tau^{2}=1,\quad\sigma^{\tau}=\sigma^{-1}\rangle

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
G={{\rm D}}_{2n}
.Then

h(G)=\begin{cases}n=|G^{\prime}{}|&\text{ if $n$ is odd},\\
\frac{n^{2}}{4}=|G^{\prime}{}|^{2}&\text{ if $n$ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We give a proof by dividing three cases.

The case
n
is odd
:
In this case, the conjugacy classes of
G
is

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\{e\},\quad K_{i}=\{\sigma^{i},\sigma^{-i}\},\quad i=1,\cdots,\tfrac{n-1}{2},

\displaystyle K_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma^{i}\tau|i=0,\cdots,n-1\}.

Set
I=\{0,1,\cdots,\tfrac{n-1}{2},n+1\}
.
We then obtain the following identities:

\displaystyle\begin{split}[K_{0}][K_{i}]&=[K_{i}],\quad i\in I,\\
[K_{i}][K_{j}]&=\begin{cases}[K_{i+j}]+[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if }1\leq i\leq j\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2},\quad i\neq j,\\
[K_{2i}]+2[K_{0}]&\text{if }1\leq 2i\leq\frac{n-1}{2},\quad i=j,\\
[K_{n-(i+j)}]+[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if }\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq i+j\leq n-1,\quad i\neq j,\\
[K_{n-2i}]+2[K_{0}]&\text{if }\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq 2i\leq n-1,\quad i=j,\end{cases}\\
[K_{n+1}][K_{i}]&=\begin{cases}[K_{n+1}]\quad&i=0,\\
2[K_{n+1}]\quad&i=1,\cdots,\tfrac{n-1}{2},\\
n(\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}[K_{i}])&i=n+1.\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.2)

These identities imply that for any
1\leq i\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, there exists
1\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
uniquely such that
[K_{i}][K_{j}]=[K_{j}]
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle\tr_{Z(\mathbb{C}G)}[K_{0}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n+3}{2},\quad\tr_{Z(\mathbb{C}G)}[K_{i}]=3\quad\text{for $i\in I-\{0,n+1\}$},

\displaystyle\tr_{Z(\mathbb{C}G)}[K_{n+1}]

\displaystyle=0.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(G)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{n+3}{2}&3&3&\cdots&3&0\\
3&n+6&6&\cdots&6&0\\
3&6&n+6&\cdots&6&0\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
3&6&6&\cdots&n+6&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&2n^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.3)

We can calculate that
\gamma(G)=4n^{\frac{n+5}{2}}
.
On the other hand,
c(G)=(2n)n^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot 2=4n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle h(G)^{2}=\mu(G)=\gamma(G)/c(G)=n^{2}.

(4.4)

Thus
h(G)=n=|G^{\prime}{}|
.

The case
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
:
Suppose
n=2q^{\prime}{}
with
odd
integer
q^{\prime}{}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then
we have
G\cong\langle z\rangle\times{{\rm D}}_{2q^{\prime}{}}
.
By (
4.4
) and Proposition
2.4
,

h(G)=h({{\rm D}}_{2q^{\prime}{}})^{2}=(q^{\prime}{})^{2}.

Since
|G^{\prime}|=|\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle|=q^{\prime}{}
, we have

\displaystyle h(G)=(q^{\prime}{})^{2}=|G^{\prime}|^{2}.

(4.5)

The case
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
:
Suppose
n=4q
with integer
q\geq 2
.
We see that
z=\sigma^{2q}
generates the center
Z(G)
and that

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{0}&=\{1\},\quad K_{i}=\{\sigma^{i},\sigma^{-i}\},\quad(i=1,\cdots,2q-1),\quad K_{2q}=\{z\},\\
K_{2q+1}&=\{\sigma^{2i}\tau|i=0,1,\cdots,2q-1\},\quad K_{2q+2}=\{\sigma^{2i+1}\tau|i=0,1,\cdots,2q-1\}\end{split}

(4.6)

are the list of all conjugacy classes of
G
.

Set
Z=Z(G)=\langle z\rangle
.
Note that
G/Z\cong{{\rm D}}_{4q}
.
Among of the conjugacy classes of
G
in (
4.6
), only
K_{q},K_{2q+1},K_{2q+2}
satisfy the condition
zK_{i}=K_{i}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\Conj(G)_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\{z^{a}K_{i}|0\leq i\leq q-1,a=0,1\},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Conj(G)_{Z}

\displaystyle=\{K_{q},K_{2q+1},K_{2q+2}\}.

(4.8)

We may set
I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}=\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
and
I(G:Z)_{Z}=\{q,2q+1,2q+2\}
.

Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
be the character defined by

\phi(z)=-1.

We can show that

e_{\phi}[K_{i}]e_{\phi}[K_{j}]=\begin{cases}e_{\phi}[K_{i+j}]+e_{\phi}[K_{|i-j|}],&\text{ if }i\neq j,\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}],&\text{ if }i=j\end{cases}

for
i,j\in I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}-\{0\}
and that
e_{\phi}[K_{0}]e_{\phi}[K_{i}]=e_{\phi}[K_{i}]
for
i\in I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}
.
We note that
e_{\phi}[K_{i}]=-e_{\phi}[K_{2q-i}]
for
0\leq i\leq q
.
Thus for
1\leq i,j\leq q-1
,

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{i}]e_{\phi}[K_{j}]=\begin{cases}e_{\phi}[K_{i+j}]+e_{\phi}[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if }0<i+j<q,i\neq j,\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2j}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if }0<2i<q,i=j,\\
-e_{\phi}[K_{2q-(i+j)}]+e_{\phi}[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if }q\leq i+j<2q,i\neq j,\\
-e_{\phi}[K_{2q-2i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if }q\leq 2i<2q,i=j.\\
\end{cases}

These relations lead that
\tr_{e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)}(e_{\phi}[K_{0}])=q
and
\tr_{e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)}(e_{\phi}[K_{i}])=0
for
1\leq i\leq q-1
.
Since
0\leq i+j<2q
,
0\leq|i-j|<q
for any
0\leq i,j\leq q-1
and
{K_{i^{*}}}=K_{i}
,

\langle e_{\phi}[K_{i}]|e_{\phi}[K_{j}]\rangle=\begin{cases}q&\text{ if }i=j=0,\\
2q&\text{ if }i=j\neq 0,\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j\end{cases}

for
0\leq i,j\leq q-1
.
Hence
\gamma_{\phi}(G)=q(2q)^{q-1}=2^{q-1}q^{q}
.
On the other hand,

c_{\phi}(G)=\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}|Z|^{-1}|C_{G}(\sigma^{i})|=(4q)(2q)^{q-1}=2^{q+1}q^{q}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}(G)=\frac{1}{4}.

(4.9)

On the other hand, we have
\kappa_{Z}(G)=|Z|^{|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}=8
.
Since
G/Z\cong{{\rm D}}_{4q}
for
q\geq 2
, by Corollary
3.7
,

\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)=(8h({{\rm D}}_{4q}))^{2}.

By (
4.9
), we have
h(G)^{2}=\mu(G)=\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)\mu_{\phi}(G)=(4h({{\rm D}}_{4q}))^{2}

and hence

h({{\rm D}}_{8q})=4h({{\rm D}}_{4q}).

Since
|{{\rm D}}_{8q}^{\prime}{}|=q=2|{{\rm D}}_{4q}^{\prime}{}|
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{h({{\rm D}}_{8q})}{|{{\rm D}}_{8q}^{\prime}{}|^{2}}=\frac{h({{\rm D}}_{4q})}{|{{\rm D}}_{4q}^{\prime}{}|^{2}}.

(4.10)

Set
q=2^{e}q^{\prime}{}\geq 2
with odd
q^{\prime}{}
and
e\geq 0
.
Then by (
4.5
),

\displaystyle\frac{h({{\rm D}}_{8q})}{|{{\rm D}}_{8q}^{\prime}{}|^{2}}=\frac{h({{\rm D}}_{4q^{\prime}{}})}{|{{\rm D}}_{4q^{\prime}{}}^{\prime}{}|^{2}}=1.

(4.11)

Consequently, for
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle h({{\rm D}}_{8q})=|{{\rm D}}_{8q}^{\prime}{}|^{2}=4q^{2}.

(4.12)

In the case
q=1
,
Z=Z({{\rm D}}_{8})
and
{{\rm D}}_{8}/Z\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
.
This case will be considered in Section
4.4
, and it follows from Proposition
4.4
with

that

.
Hence (
4.12
) also holds in the case

.
∎

4.3
Generalized quaternions

In this section we consider the generalized quaternions:

G={{\rm Q}}_{4n}:=\langle\sigma,\tau|\sigma^{2n}=1,\sigma^{n}=\tau^{2},\sigma^{\tau}=\sigma^{-1}\rangle

.
The element

generates the center

We also find that

We shall prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq 2
,
h({{\rm Q}}_{4n})=n^{2}=|{{\rm Q}}_{4n}^{\prime}|^{2}
.

Proof.

The case
n
is odd
:
The list of conjugacy classes are given by

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\{1\},\quad K_{i}=\{\sigma^{i},\sigma^{2n-i}\}\quad(i=1,\cdots,n-1),\quad K_{n}=\{z\},

\displaystyle K_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma^{2i}\tau|i=0,\cdots,n-1\},\quad K_{n+2}=\{\sigma^{2i+1}\tau|i=0,\cdots,n-1\}.

Since

zK_{j}=K_{n-j},\text{ for }0\leq j\leq n-1,\quad zK_{n+1}=Z_{n+2},

we have
\Conj(G)_{\{1\}}=\Conj(G)
and
\Conj(G)_{Z}=\emptyset
.
In particular, we may set
I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}=\{0,2,4,\cdots,n-1,n+1\}
.

Let
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
be the character defined by
\phi(z)=-1
.
By direct calculations,

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{2i}]e_{\phi}[K_{2j}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{\phi}[K_{2j}]&\text{if $0=i\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{2}$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2(i+j)}]+e_{\phi}[K_{2|i-j|}]&\text{if $0<j\neq i\leq\frac{n-1}{2}$, $2i+2j\leq n-1$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2n-2(i+j)}]+e_{\phi}[K_{2|i-j|}]&\text{if $0<j\neq i\leq\frac{n-1}{2}$, $2i+2j\geq n+1$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{4i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if $0<i=j\leq\frac{n-1}{2}$, $4i\leq n-1$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2n-4i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if $0<i=j\leq\frac{n-1}{2}$, $4i\geq n+1$},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{0}]e_{\phi}[K_{n+1}]

\displaystyle=e_{\phi}[K_{n+1}],

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{2j}]e_{\phi}[K_{n+1}]

\displaystyle=2e_{\phi}[K_{n+1}]\quad(j=1,\cdots,\frac{n-1}{2}),

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{n+1}]^{2}

\displaystyle=ne_{\phi}[z\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle]=-n\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e_{\phi}[K_{2j}].

These structure constants are similar with the ones in (
4.2
) but not the same.
By noting
K_{i^{*}}=K_{i}
for

and
K_{(n+1)^{*}}=K_{n+2}
, direct calculation gives the same Gramian matrix in (
4.3
) and hence

\gamma_{\phi}(G)=\gamma({{\rm D}}_{2n})=\gamma(G/Z),\quad c_{\phi}(G)=c(G/Z).

Therefore, we have
\mu_{\phi}(G)=\mu(G/Z)
.
On the other hand, since
|I(G:Z)_{Z}|=0
, we have
\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)=\mu(G/Z)
.
Therefore, we have
\mu(G)=\mu(G/Z)^{2}
.
Thus
h(G)=h(G/Z)^{2}=h({{\rm D}}_{2n})^{2}=n^{2}=|G^{\prime}{}|^{2}
.

The case
n
is even
:
In this case, the list of conjugacy classes of
G
are given by

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\{1\},\quad K_{i}=\{\sigma^{i},\sigma^{2n-i}\}\quad(i=1,\cdots,n-1),\quad K_{n}=\{z\},

\displaystyle K_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma^{2i}\tau|i=0,\cdots,n-1\},\quad K_{n+2}=\{\sigma^{2i+1}\tau|i=0,\cdots,n-1\}

and the list is the same one in the case
n
is odd.
The difference is

zK_{j}=K_{j},\text{ for $j=\frac{n}{2},n+1,n+2$}

and the situation is the same as the case
{{\rm D}}_{4n}
.

We set
I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}=\{0,1,2,\cdots,\frac{n}{2}-1\}
and
I(G:Z)_{Z}=\{\frac{n}{2},n+1,n+2\}
.
Then

\displaystyle e_{\phi}[K_{i}]e_{\phi}[K_{j}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{\phi}[K_{j}]&\text{if $0=i\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}-1$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{i+j}]+e_{\phi}[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if $0<j\neq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1$, $i+j\leq\frac{n}{2}$},\\
-e_{\phi}[K_{n-(i+j)}]+e_{\phi}[K_{|i-j|}]&\text{if $0<j\neq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1$, $i+j>\frac{n}{2}$},\\
e_{\phi}[K_{2i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if $0<i=j\leq\frac{n}{2}-1$, $2i\leq\frac{n}{2}$},\\
-e_{\phi}[K_{n-2i}]+2e_{\phi}[K_{0}]&\text{if $0<i=j\leq\frac{n}{2}-1$, $2i>\frac{n}{2}$}.\end{cases}

Hence
\tr_{e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)}e_{\phi}[K_{0}]=\frac{n}{2}
,
\tr_{e_{\phi}Z(\mathbb{C}G)}e_{\phi}[K_{i}]=0
for

.
Therefore, as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we obtain

.
We also easily check that

.
Thus, in this case,

.
On the other hand, since

, we have
\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)=2^{6}\mu({{\rm D}}_{2n})=2^{6}\cdot\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}=4n^{4}
.
Therefore we have
\mu(G)=\mu_{\mathbf{1}_{Z}}(G)\mu_{\phi}(G)=n^{4}
and

.
∎

Proposition 4.4

Remark 4.5
.

This is the case
G
is nilpotent of class

[
M
]

Proof.

Since

and hence we may identify

Let

and assume that

Since

,

{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}=\{e_{\phi}[g_{i}]|i\in I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}\}=\{e_{\phi}[g_{i}]|i\in I(Z(G):Z)_{\{1\}}\}={\mathcal{B}}(Z(G))_{\phi}.

Therefore, we have

).
We also have

Thus

\mu_{\phi}(G)=|G/Z(G)|^{-|Z(G)/Z|}.

One also has

|I(G:Z)_{Z}|=(|G|-|Z(G)|)/|Z|=(|G/Z(G)|-1)|Z(G)/Z|.

\displaystyle\mu(G)

\displaystyle=|Z|^{2|Z(G)/Z|(|G/Z(G)|-1)}\times\left(|G/Z(G)|^{-|Z(G)/Z|}\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\left(p^{2(p^{s}-1)-s(p-1)}\right)^{|Z(G)/Z|}

\displaystyle=\left(p^{(2[s]_{p}-s)(p-1)}\right)^{|Z(G)/Z|}.

Since

we have the desired formula.

4.5
Central products

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite group and
H_{i}\subset Z(G_{i})
a central subgroup of
G_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Assume that
H_{1}\cong H_{2}
and
\theta:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
is a group isomorphism.
Then the quotient
G=(G_{1}\times G_{2})/N
by the subgroup
N=\{(h_{1},\theta(h_{1}^{-1}))|h_{1}\in H_{1}\}
of
H_{1}\times H_{2}
is called a
central product
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with respect to
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
(cf.
[
G
, P29]
,
[
Suz1
, P137]
).

Let
\pi:G_{1}\times G_{2}\rightarrow G
be the canonical projection.
We regard
G_{i}
to be a subgroup of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
in natural way, and set
Z=\pi(H_{1})=\pi(H_{2})
.
We have

G/Z\cong(G_{1}\times G_{2})/(H_{1}\times H_{2})\cong G_{1}/H_{1}\times G_{2}/H_{2}.

For
s=1,2
, let
\Conj(G_{s}/H_{s})=\{\overline{K}^{(s)}_{i}|i\in I_{G_{s}/H_{s}}\}
and take a set of all representatives
\{K_{i}^{(s)}|i\in I_{G_{s}/H_{s}}\}
of the
Z
-orbits of the
Z
-set
\Conj(G_{s})
and element
g_{i}^{(s)}\in K^{(s)}
.
We see that
I_{G/Z}=I_{G_{1}/H_{1}}\times I_{G_{2}/H_{2}}
and that

|(g_{i}^{(1)}g_{j}^{(2)})^{G}|=|K_{i}^{(1)}||K_{j}^{(2)}|\quad\text{for }(i,j)\in I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}\times I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}.

Assume that
Z
is of prime order
p
.
Then we see that

I(G:Z)_{\{1\}}=I(G_{1};H_{1})_{\{1\}}\times I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{\{1\}}.

For any
\phi\in\Irr(Z)
with
\Ker\phi=\{1\}
, we have

{\mathcal{B}}(G)_{\phi}=\{uv|u\in{\mathcal{B}}(G_{1})_{\phi_{1}},v\in{\mathcal{B}}(G_{2})_{\phi_{2}}\},

where
\phi_{i}
is defined by
\phi_{i}(h)=\phi(\pi(h))
for
h\in H_{i}
, and we identify
\mathbb{C}[G_{i}]\subset\mathbb{C}[G]
for
i=1,2
.
Since
\mathbb{C}[G_{1}]
and
\mathbb{C}[G_{2}]
mutually commute, we have

\gamma_{\phi}(G)=\gamma_{\phi_{1}}(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}\gamma_{\phi_{2}}(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}.

Furthermore, one has

|C_{G}(g^{(1)}_{i}g^{(2)}_{j})|=\frac{|G|}{|(g^{(1)}_{i}g^{(2)}_{j})^{G}|}=\frac{|G_{1}||G_{2}|}{p|K^{(1)}_{i}||K^{(2)}_{j}|}=p^{-1}|C_{G_{1}}(g^{(1)}_{i})||C_{G_{2}}(g^{(2)}_{j})|

for
(i,j)\in I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}\times I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle c_{\phi}(G)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}\\
j\in I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}\end{subarray}}|C_{G}((g_{i},g_{j})N)||Z|^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}\\
j\in I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}\end{subarray}}|C_{G_{1}}(g_{i})||C_{G_{2}}(g_{j}))|p^{-2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i\in I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}}|C_{G_{1}}(g_{i})|p^{-1}\right)^{|I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\prod_{j\in I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}}|C_{G_{2}}(g_{j}))|p^{-1}\right)^{|I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle=c_{\phi_{1}}(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}c_{\phi_{2}}(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}

and thus

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}(G)=\mu_{\phi_{1}}(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2},H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}\mu_{\phi_{2}}(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1},H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}.

On the other hand,
by Proposition
2.4
,

\mu(G/Z)=\mu\left(G_{1}/H_{1}\times G_{2}/H_{2}\right)=\mu(G_{1}/H_{1})^{|I_{G_{2}/H_{2}}|}\mu(G_{2}/H_{2})^{|I_{G_{1}/H_{1}}|}.

It follows from (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\mu(G)

\displaystyle=p^{2|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}\mu(G/Z)\left(\prod_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\mu_{\phi^{a}}(G)\right)

\displaystyle=p^{2|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}\mu(G_{1}/H_{1})^{|I_{G_{2}/H_{2}}|}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\mu_{\phi_{1}^{a}}(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu(G_{2}/H_{2})^{|I_{G_{1}/H_{1}}|}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\mu_{\phi_{2}^{a}}(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle=p^{2|I(G:Z)_{Z}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu(G_{1}/H_{1})^{|I_{G_{2}/H_{2}}|}\left(\mu(G_{1})p^{-2|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{H_{1}}|}\mu(G_{1}/H_{1})^{-1}\right)^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu(G_{2}/H_{2})^{|I_{G_{1}/H_{1}}|}\left(\mu(G_{2})p^{-2|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{H_{2}}|}\mu(G_{2}/H_{2})^{-1}\right)^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle=p^{2|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{H_{1}}||I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{H_{2}}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}\mu(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu(G_{1}/H_{1})^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{H_{2}}|}\mu(G_{2}/H_{2})^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{H_{1}}|}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(G)=&p^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{H_{1}}||I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{H_{2}}|}\\
&\times h(G_{1})^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{\{1\}}|}h(G_{2})^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{\{1\}}|}\\
&\times h(G_{1}/H_{1})^{|I(G_{2}:H_{2})_{H_{2}}|}h(G_{2}/H_{2})^{|I(G_{1}:H_{1})_{H_{1}}|}.\end{split}

(4.14)

Now we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite groups containing central subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
of prime order
p
, respectively.
Let
G
be the central product of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with respect to
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
.
Then
h(G)
is given by (
4.14
).
In particular, if all
h(G_{1})
,
h(G_{2})
,
h(G_{1}/H_{1})
and
h(G_{2}/H_{2})
are integer so is
h(G)
.

Remark 4.12
.

In the case
Z=\{1\}
, a central product is a direct product.
This is the case
p=1
, that is
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are trivial, in Proposition
2.4
.

Example 4.13
.

As a trivial case, for
i=1,2
, we consider a finite group
G_{i}=Z\times T_{i}
, where
Z
is a group of prime order
p
and
T_{i}
is a group.
Let
G=(G_{1}\times G_{2})/N
with
H_{1}=H_{2}=Z
and
N=(z,z^{-1})|z\in Z\}
.
Since
G\cong Z\times T_{1}\times T_{2}
we have

h(G)=h(T_{1})^{p|I_{T_{2}}|}h(T_{2})^{p|I_{T_{1}}|}

by Proposition
2.4
.

On the other hand, we have
I(G_{i},Z)_{(1)}=I_{T_{i}}
and
I(G_{i},Z)_{Z}=\emptyset
for
i=1,2
.
Thus (
4.14
) gives

h(G)=h(Z\times T_{1})^{|I_{T_{2}}|}h(Z\times T_{2})^{|I_{T_{1}}|}=h(T_{1})^{p|I_{T_{2}}|}h(T_{2})^{p|I_{T_{1}}|}.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
G
,
G_{1},G_{2},H_{1},H_{2}
be as in Theorem
4.11
.
If
|G_{i}^{\prime}||h(G_{i})
and
|(G_{i}/H_{i})^{\prime}||h(G_{i}/H_{i})
for
i=1,2
, then
|G^{\prime}||h(G)
.

Proof.

Since
H_{i}
is of prime order,
I(G_{i},H_{i})_{H_{i}}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
H_{i}\subset(G_{i})^{\prime}
, and then
|G_{i}^{\prime}|=p|(G_{i}/H_{i})^{\prime}|
.
Hence
Z\subset(G_{1}\times G_{2})^{\prime}
if one of
I(G_{1},H_{1})_{H_{1}}
and
I(G_{2},H_{2})_{H_{2}}
are non-empty.
In the case,

|G^{\prime}|=|Z||G^{\prime}/Z|=p|(G_{1}/H_{1})^{\prime}||(G_{2}/H_{2})^{\prime}|.

Moreover, if
I(G_{1},H_{1})_{H_{1}}\neq\emptyset
or
I(G_{2},H_{2})_{H_{2}}\neq\emptyset
then

|G^{\prime}|=|G_{1}^{\prime}||(G_{2}/H_{2})^{\prime}|\quad\text{or }|G^{\prime}|=|(G_{1}/H_{1})^{\prime}||G_{2}^{\prime}|,

respectively.
By (
4.14
), we have
|G^{\prime}||h(G)
.

In the case
I(G_{1},H_{1})_{H_{1}}=I(G_{2},H_{2})_{H_{2}}=\emptyset
, then
Z\cap G^{\prime}=\{1\}
and

|G^{\prime}|=|(G/Z)^{\prime}|=|(G_{1}/H_{1})^{\prime}||(G_{2}/H_{2})^{\prime}|=|G_{1}^{\prime}||G_{2}^{\prime}|.

Hence (
4.14
) proves
|G^{\prime}||h(G)
.
∎

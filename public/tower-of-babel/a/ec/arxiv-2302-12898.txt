I
Introduction

In the Standard Model (SM),
CP
violation in the quark sector arises from a single irreducible phase in
the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) quark-mixing matrix
Kobayashi:1973fv
.

Measuring decay-time-dependent
CP
-violating rate asymmetries in neutral
B
meson decays to
CP
eigenstates
mediated by a
b\rightarrow c\bar{c}s

tree-level transition gives access to
\phi_{1}\equiv\arg(-V_{cd}V_{cb}^{*}/V_{td}V_{tb}^{*})
,
one of the angles of the CKM unitarity triangle.
The probability of a
B

q
(
q=-1
for
B^{0}
and
q=+1
for
\bar{B}^{0}
) at some time and decaying to
such a

eigenstate after a time
\Delta t

P(\Delta t,q)=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{2\tau_{B^{0}}}\{1+q[S_{\mathit{CP}}\sin(\Dmd\Dt)+A_{\mathit{CP}}\cos(\Dmd\Dt)]\},

(1)

where
S_{\mathit{CP}}
is the mixing-induced, and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
the direct,
CP
-violating parameter;
\tau_{B^{0}}
is the
B^{0}
lifetime;
and
\Delta m_{d}
is the
B^{0}
-
\bar{B}^{0}
oscillation frequency, which corresponds to the mass difference between the two
neutral
B
mass eigenstates.
The SM predicts
A_{\mathit{CP}}=0
and

, where
\eta
is the
CP
eigenvalue of the final state
(
\eta=+1
for a
CP
-odd and
\eta=-1
for a
CP
-even state).
The Belle, BaBar, and LHCb collaborations all measured

and
S_{\mathit{CP}}
using
several

eigenstates
Belle:2012paq
;
BaBar:2009byl
;
LHCb:2015ups
;
LHCb:2017mpa
.
All measurements have comparable precision, with the result of Belle being the most precise
Belle:2012paq
,

\begin{array}[]{l r c l r c l}S_{\mathit{CP}}=&0.667&\pm&0.023\text{(stat)}&\pm&0.012\text{(syst)},\\
A_{\mathit{CP}}=&0.006&\pm&0.016\text{(stat)}&\pm&0.012\text{(syst)}.\end{array}

We present a Belle II measurement of these parameters using decays into
B^{0}\to{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
final states, which are

$190\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}$^{-1}

The

in this mode with an approximation better than
2\%

Frings:2015eva
.
As the data set is four times smaller than that used in the Belle analysis, the statistical precision is not yet competitive.
The analysis, however, yields comparable systematic uncertainties, paving the way to a follow-up
\sin 2\phi_{1}
measurement with
competitive precision.

The
B
mesons are produced in
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Upsilon(4S)\to B\bar{B}
events.
The SuperKEKB collider
Ohnishi:2013fma
accelerates electron and positron beams to 7 GeV and 4 GeV, respectively, producing
\Upsilon(4S)
with a Lorentz boost
\beta\gamma=0.287
.
We fully reconstruct one

, in its decay to
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}

at the time of its decay

.
As the two

flavor at that time. As the two
B

frame, they are boosted

to deduce the time difference
\Delta t
between the

and
B_{\text{tag}}
decays. We fit Eq. (
1

and

II
Experimental setup

The Belle II detector consists of several subsystems arranged in a cylindrical structure around the beam pipe
Belle-II:2010dht
.
The detector is asymmetric with more extensive coverage in the
e^{-}
beam direction to mirror the energy asymmetry of the beams.
The tracking system consists of a two-layer silicon-pixel detector (PXD) surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon-strip detector (SVD) and a 56-layer central drift chamber (CDC).
For the data used in this work, the second PXD layer is partially instrumented and covers only one sixth of the azimuthal angle.
In the case of
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
decays,
the combined PXD and SVD system provides an average
B_{\mathit{CP}}
vertex resolution along the beam direction of approximately
25\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
.
A time-of-propagation counter and an aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov counter that cover the barrel and forward end-cap regions of the detector, respectively, are used for charged-particle identification.
An electromagnetic calorimeter fills the remaining volume inside a superconducting magnet that generates an axial, uniform 1.5 T field.
It measures the energy of photons and supplements particle identification.
A dedicated system to identify
K^{0}_{L}
mesons and muons is installed in the outermost part of the detector.
The
z
axis of the laboratory frame is defined as the central axis of the solenoid, with its positive direction defined by the direction of the electron beam.
The data are processed using the Belle II analysis software framework
Kuhr:2018lps
, which relies on the track reconstruction algorithm described in Ref.
BelleIITrackingGroup:2020hpx
.

Simulated events are used for selection optimization, fit modelling, and validation of the measurement.
They are generated using
KKMC
for quark-antiquark pairs from
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions
Jadach:1999vf
,
PYTHIA8
for hadronization
Sjostrand:2014zea
,
EVTGEN
for the decay of hadrons
Lange:2001uf
, and
GEANT4
for the detector response
GEANT4:2002zbu
.
The simulation includes the effect of beam-induced background

.

III

We reconstruct the

All tracks are required to have a polar angle

Tracks that are not used to form a

than

We require that electrons and muons be identified by their particle identification (PID) likelihoods, which

The
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}

in
[2.95,\;3.15]\;$\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}

in
[3.00,\;3.15]\;$\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}
.
The

meson candidates are reconstructed from pairs of oppositely-charged particles with a
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
mass
in
[480,\;515]\;$\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}

Candidate

(

B_{\mathit{CP}}
candidate.
We require

and

The

We determine the

The

\bar{D}^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\bar{D}^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, is used to calibrate the resolution function
and flavor-tagger parameters for the charged decay modes.
The selection of the charged
B
decay modes is kept as close as possible to that of the neutral
B
modes.
We require that the charged kaon in the
B^{+}\to J/\psi K^{+}
decay satisfy a PID likelihood criterion.
The requirement
R_{2}<0.4
is used to suppress the continuum background to
B^{+}\rightarrow J/\psi K^{+}
and
B^{+}\rightarrow\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{+}
decays.
The total reconstruction and selection efficiencies for
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
and
B^{+}\rightarrow J/\psi K^{+}
decay modes
are listed in Tab.
1
.

After the selection, the fraction of events containing multiple candidate decays is approximately
0.4\%
and
2\%
for
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
, respectively.
All candidates are retained for further analysis.

IV
Determination of the sample composition

We perform an unbinned extended maximum likelihood fit to the
\Delta E
distribution to
measure the signal and background yields.
The signal PDF is modelled by the sum of a Gaussian distribution and a double-sided Crystal Ball
distribution
Gaiser:1982yw
.
Dedicated studies using simulated events
show that
the background consists of random combinations of tracks from

e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}

events, or from
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow B\bar{B}
or
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow B^{+}B^{-}
events in which tracks from the two
B^{(\pm)}
mesons are
combined. No significant background has a peak in
\Delta E
. Therefore, the background shape is
modelled with an exponential distribution.
The observed
\Delta E
distribution is
shown in Fig.
1
with the fitted curve superimposed. The signal yields and purities are listed in Tab.
1
.
Using event-by-event fractions calculated from the fitted
\Delta E
PDFs, we employ the sWeight method
Pivk:2004ty
to obtain a background-subtracted
\Delta t
distribution. We check that
\Delta E
is not correlated to
\Delta t
in simulated
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
signal and background events, hence allowing the use of sWeights.
The sWeight method simplifies the analysis by avoiding the need to
parameterize the
\Delta t
background distribution.

for

candidates reconstructed in the data with the fit

V
Calibration of detector effects

Three effects impact the measured
\Delta t
distribution: the
\Delta t
resolution, incorrect flavor-tagging assignment, and the small motion of the
B
mesons in the
\Upsilon(4S)
frame.
These effects are calibrated by measuring the
\Delta t
distribution for events in which the fully reconstructed
B
decays as
B^{0}\to D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
.

Taking into account the asymmetry
\mu
between the
B^{0}_{\text{tag}}
and
\bar{B}^{0}_{\text{tag}}
reconstruction efficiencies, and the
fractions
w_{B}
and
w_{\bar{B}}
of incorrectly tagged
B^{0}_{\text{tag}}
and
\bar{B}^{0}_{\text{tag}}
mesons,
the
\Delta t
PDF in Eq. (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle P_{\mathit{CP}}(\Delta t,~q)=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{2\tau_{B^{0}}}

\displaystyle\{1-q\Delta w+q\mu(1-2w)

(2)

\displaystyle+[q(1-2w)+\mu(1-q\Delta w)][S_{\mathit{CP}}\sin(\Dmd\Dt)+A_{\mathit{CP}}\cos(\Dmd\Dt)]\},

where
w\equiv(w_{B}+w_{\bar{B}})/2
and
\Delta w\equiv w_{B}-w_{\bar{B}}
.

The events are classified into seven distinct intervals (bins) of
r
, with boundaries
(0.0,0.1,0.25,0.45,0.6,0.725,0.875,1.0)
.
The values of
the flavor-tagger parameters are determined in each
r
bin by performing an unbinned fit to the background-subtracted
\Delta t
distribution
of events in which the fully reconstructed
B
decays as
B^{0}\to D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}

had
.
As the wrong-sign decay
B^{0}\to D^{(*)+}\pi^{-}
occurs with a negligible
branching fraction, the charge of the
\pi
,
q_{\pi}
, identifies the flavor of the fully reconstructed
B
at the time of its decay.
For these events, the
\Delta t
PDF is

\displaystyle P_{\text{flav}}(\Delta t,~q,~q_{\pi})=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}

\displaystyle\{1-q\Delta w+q\mu(1-2w)

(3)

\displaystyle-q_{\pi}[q(1-2w)+\mu(1-q\Delta w)]\cos(\Dmd\Dt)\}.

To model the effect of the
\Delta t
resolution, the PDFs
P_{\text{flav}}
and
P_{\mathit{CP}}
are convolved with a resolution function using the parameterization of Ref.
had
.
The resolution function is a sum of three components, referred to as core, tail, and outlier components, and is a function of the residual
{\text{d}\Delta\tau}=\Delta t-\Delta t_{\text{true}}
.
It reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}({\text{d}\Delta\tau};\sigma_{\Delta t})

\displaystyle=f_{\text{core}}G({\text{d}\Delta\tau};m_{\text{core}}\sigma_{\Delta t},s_{\text{core}}\sigma_{\Delta t})

(4)

\displaystyle+f_{\text{tail}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{tail}}({\text{d}\Delta\tau};m_{\text{tail}}\sigma_{\Delta t},s_{\text{tail}}\sigma_{\Delta t},c/\sigma_{\Delta t},f_{>},f_{<})

\displaystyle+f_{\text{OL}}G({\text{d}\Delta\tau};0,\sigma_{\text{OL}}),

where
G(x;m,s)
is a Gaussian distribution of mean
m
and width
s
, and

f_{\text{core}}+f_{\text{tail}}+f_{\text{OL}}=1
.
The core function accounts for approximately
70\%
of events.
The parameter
s_{\text{core}}
is free in the fit to allow for an overall under- or over-estimate of the uncertainty
\sigma_{\Delta t}
. Its value is close to unity.
The PDF
R_{\text{tail}}
is parameterized as a sum of a Gaussian distribution and two exponentially modified Gaussian distributions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\text{tail}}(x)

\displaystyle\propto G(x;m,s)

(5)

\displaystyle+f_{<}G(x;m,s)\otimes c\exp_{<}(cx)

\displaystyle+f_{>}G(x;m,s)\otimes c\exp_{>}(-cx),

where
\exp_{>}(-cx)=\exp(-cx)
if
x>0
and
\exp_{>}(-cx)=0
otherwise, and
similarly for
\exp_{<}(cx)
.
The component
R_{\text{tail}}
accounts for approximately
30\%
of events.
The exponential tails, as well as
m_{\text{core}}
and
m_{\text{tail}}
that are allowed to take non-zero values, describe the impact of tracks originating from charmed mesons from the
B_{\text{tag}}
decay on the
B_{\text{tag}}
vertex resolution.
The outlier component accounts for approximately
0.1\%
of events that have poorly reconstructed vertices. It has a large width:
\sigma_{\text{OL}}=200\;$\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}$
.
All resolution-function parameters share the same values across all
r
bins,
apart from
m_{\text{core}}
,
m_{\text{tail}}
, and

f_{\text{tail}}
that have independent values in the seventh
r
bin.
This
r
bin is mostly populated with
B_{\text{tag}}
mesons decaying semileptonically and is characterized by a resolution function with means closer to zero.
Simulation studies show that neglecting this effect yields a bias in the measurement of
A_{\mathit{CP}}
.
In the fit to the calibration modes, the following parameters are allowed to vary:
f_{\text{tail}}
,
m_{\text{core}}
, and
m_{\text{tail}}
separately in
r
bins
1
-
6
and
7
, and
s_{\text{core}}
and
s_{\text{tail}}
. The remaining parameters are fixed to their values derived from simulation studies. The corresponding systematic uncertainties are determined to be small.

In addition to the convolution with the resolution function,
the PDFs
P_{\mathit{CP}}
and
P_{\text{flav}}
are further corrected
for the effect of the small motion of the
B
mesons in the
\Upsilon(4S)
frame,
to yield the PDFs
P^{\prime}_{CP}
and
P^{\prime}_{\text{flav}}

had
.
The PDF
P^{\prime}_{\text{flav}}
is fitted to the background-subtracted
\Delta t
distribution in the calibration sample with
\Delta m_{d}
and
\tau_{B^{0}}
fixed to their known values. This fit
has 28 free parameters, all related to the detector response: 21 flavor tagger
parameters and 7
\Delta t
resolution parameters.

VI
Determination of the
CP
asymmetries

The parameters
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
are obtained by maximizing the logarithm of the weighed likelihood
L
,

\log L=\sum_{i}sW_{i}\;\log P^{\prime}_{CP}({\Delta t_{i},q_{i}|A_{\mathit{CP}},S_{\mathit{CP}}}),

(6)

where the sum runs over all events in the sample associated to an sWeight
sW_{i}
,
a flavor
q_{i}
, and a
decay time difference
\Delta t_{i}
.
The
CP
asymmetries

A_{\mathit{CP}}
and
S_{\mathit{CP}}
are the only free parameters in the fit,
\tau_{B^{0}}
and
\Delta m_{d}
are fixed to the known values, and the resolution and flavor-tagging parameters are fixed to the values obtained with the calibration samples.
We check that the fit procedure is unbiased by generating and fitting background-free simplified simulated experiments with various true values of
A_{\mathit{CP}}
and
S_{\mathit{CP}}
.

The statistical uncertainties on the final result are computed by resampling,

i.e.
, bootstrapping
Efron:1979bxm
, the data of the calibration
and signal samples
1000
times
and computing the standard deviations of the fitted parameters across the
bootstrapped replicas.
For consistency checks, statistical uncertainties are estimated
using likelihood-ratio-based confidence regions applied to Eq.
6
. The resulting uncertainties are typically
underestimated by a few percent, as they do not account for the statistical fluctuations of the background, and of the flavor tagger and resolution-function parameters.

The sWeighted
\Delta t
distributions for the
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
sample are shown in Fig.

separately for

and
{B}^{0}_{\text{tag}}
events. The fit shapes,
corresponding to
P^{\prime}_{\mathit{CP}}(\Delta t,q=+1)
and
P^{\prime}_{\mathit{CP}}(\Delta t,q=-1)
,
are superimposed to the distributions and the raw asymmetry is also shown.
The fitted values of
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
are listed Tab.

.
The values of
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
obtained from the fit performed separately to the
B_{\mathit{CP}}\rightarrow J/\psi(\to e^{+}e^{-})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
and
B_{\mathit{CP}}\rightarrow J/\psi(\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
subsamples are also listed.
The uncertainties quoted in Tab.
1
are statistical only and are estimated
based on likelihood-ratios applied to Eq.
6
.
The fit results across the subsamples are in statistical agreement.

As a check of our fitting procedure, we measure
A_{\mathit{CP}}
and

for a sample of

Several systematic uncertainties are related to the calibration of the resolution function and flavor-tagger parameters with
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
decays.
The statistical uncertainties due to the size of the
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
sample are the dominant systematic uncertainty on
S_{\mathit{CP}}
. The statistical uncertainties on
the flavor-tagging parameters have a larger impact on the precision than the statistical uncertainties on the resolution-function parameters.
Another source of systematic uncertainty related to the calibration of detector effects is the signal charge-asymmetry, reflecting the fact that the reconstruction and selection efficiencies for
B^{0}\to D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
and the charge-conjugated
\bar{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)+}\pi^{-}
channels differ by
2.6\%
.
In the nominal configuration of the fit, these efficiencies are assumed to be equal, which induces
a bias on the
B^{0}_{\text{tag}}
detection asymmetries
\mu
.
To evaluate the impact on
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
,
the measurement of the flavor-tagging parameters is repeated after weighing the
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
data such that the event yields for
B^{0}
and
\bar{B}^{0}
decays are identical. The difference between the
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
values
obtained with and without this correction is assigned as a systematic uncertainty.
In the
\Delta t
fit to the calibration modes, the wrong-tag fraction
w_{B^{0}}
in the highest
r
bin is evaluated to be at the lower limit
w_{B^{0}}=0
in
30\%
of the resampled (bootstrapped) replicas obtained from the data.
To estimate the possible bias, we perform an alternative fit where
w_{B^{0}}
is not determined by the fit, but fixed to
0.0
, and the difference of
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
with respect to the values from the nominal fit is assigned as the uncertainty.

Other systematic uncertainties are related to the fit model,
i.e.
, inaccuracies in the parameterization of
P^{\prime}_{CP}
and
P^{\prime}_{\text{flav}}
.
The analysis bias is the difference between the true parameter values used in the
simulation and the value obtained by fitting
500
simulated samples with an average number of events corresponding to that observed in the experimental data.
The systematic uncertainty related to the resolution parameters fixed to the their values in the simulation is evaluated by repeating the fit, while letting the otherwise fixed parameters free to vary one-by-one. Each fit yields additional values for
A_{\mathit{CP}}
and
S_{\mathit{CP}}
whose deviations from the nominal values are
summed in quadrature to yield a systematic uncertainty.
In the implementation of the fitter,
\sigma_{\Delta t}
is used as a conditional
observable. The
\sigma_{\Delta t}
distributions are sampled in each of the
r
bins and separately for
B^{0}
and
\bar{B}^{0}
events.
To assess the uncertainty related to the choice of
\sigma_{\Delta t}
binning, the number of bins in the
\sigma_{\Delta t}
histograms is varied from 30 to 1000 bins.
The largest variation with respect to the nominal fit is assigned as a systematic uncertainty.
The impact of the uncertainty of the
B^{0}
lifetime and the
B^{0}
-
\bar{B}^{0}
oscillation frequency is tested by varying these parameters within their uncertainties
PDG
.

Another category of systematic uncertainties are those related to the measurement of
\Delta t
.
To evaluate the systematic uncertainty related to the detector alignment
Bilka:2021rqj
, simulated
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K_{S}^{0}
events are reconstructed with four alternative misalignment scenarios.
The extraction of
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
, including the calibration with the
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
modes, is repeated using the four misaligned samples,
and the maximal deviation from the nominal configuration is assigned as alignment uncertainty.
Both
B^{0}_{\text{sig}}
and
B^{0}_{\text{tag}}
decay vertices are determined with the
\Upsilon(4S)
production vertex constrained to the beam spot position.
The beam spot position, dimensions, and orientation are continuously monitored using
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
events.
The uncertainties on these parameters originate from the limited size of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
data sample and the systematic uncertainty of the measurement.
To estimate the impact on
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
, the analysis is repeated with the beam-spot parameters shifted by their respective uncertainties.
The difference between the momentum scale of tracks in the data and in the simulation is measured to be less than
0.1\%
and has a very small impact on the analysis.

In addition to the sources of uncertainty mentioned above, several other systematic effects are considered.
The measurement is repeated by changing the parameterization of the
\Delta E
background shape from an exponential to a second-degree polynomial. The deviation with respect to the nominal is assigned as a systematic uncertainty.
To assess a possible bias stemming from multiple candidates,
the fit is repeated by removing multiple candidates at random.
The difference of this fit result with respect to the nominal is taken as a systematic uncertainty.
The expression of
P^{\prime}_{CP}
does not account for
the effect of
CP
violation in
B^{0}_{\text{tag}}
decays
Long:2003wq
.
This yields a systematic uncertainty determined following Ref.
Belle:2012paq
. This is the dominant source of systematic uncertainty on
A_{\mathit{CP}}
.

The total systematic uncertainty is evaluated as the quadratic sum of the individual contributions.
The precision on
S_{\mathit{CP}}
and
A_{\mathit{CP}}
is limited by the sample size. The systematic uncertainty of
0.016
on
S_{\mathit{CP}}
is one fourth of the statistical uncertainty and is comparable to the world-average precision.

VIII
Results and conclusions

A measurement of mixing-induced and direct
CP
violation in
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays is performed using data collected by the Belle II detector.
We find 2755 signal candidates in a sample consisting of
200\times 10^{6}

B\bar{B}
pairs, where both
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}\to e^{+}e^{-}
decay channels are reconstructed.
The results are

\begin{array}[]{l r c l r c l}S_{\mathit{CP}}=&0.720&\pm&0.062\text{(stat)}&\pm&0.016\text{(syst)},\\
A_{\mathit{CP}}=&0.094&\pm&0.044\text{(stat)}&{}^{\displaystyle{+}}_{\displaystyle{-}}&{}^{\displaystyle{0.042}}_{\displaystyle{0.017}}\text{(syst)},\end{array}

with a statistical correlation coefficient of
-6\%
. These results allow the determination of the CKM angle
\phi_{1}

Frings:2015eva
; for negligible penguin pollution, as expected for this final state, our value for
S_{\mathit{CP}}
corresponds to
\phi_{1}=(23.0\pm 2.6\text{(stat)}\pm 0.7\text{(syst)})$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}$
.

These results are consistent with the world-average results. The statistical uncertainty is twice that of the current most precise determination, consistent with a four-times smaller data set. The systematic uncertainties are comparable.

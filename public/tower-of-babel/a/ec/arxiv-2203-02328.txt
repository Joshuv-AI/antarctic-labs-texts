1.1.
Multijoint Inequalities as Boundedness of Operators

We interpret the multijoint inequality as boundedness of a multilinear operator and give a proof of this boundedness by establishing two assertions.

We define the
multijoint operator
by

T[f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}](p)=\sum_{l_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{d}\in\mathcal{L}_{d}}\delta(p,l_{1},\ldots,l_{d})f_{1}(l_{1})\cdots f_{d}(l_{d}),

for
p\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
and arbitrary
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then we can express the multijoint inequality (
1
) as the boundedness of the multilinear operator
T
,

(2)

\norm{T[f_1, \ldots, f_d]^{\frac 1 d}}_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(J)}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{d}\norm{f_j}_{L^{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})}^{\frac{1}{d}},

for arbitrary
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, where we use
\norm{f}_{L^{p}(A)}
to denote
\left(\sum_{a\in A}\absolutevalue{f(a)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
for any discrete set
A
.

Now, consider an arbitrary non-negative test function
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Suppose that we can find a “factorising” function
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
so that

T[f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}](p)S(p)^{d}\leq C^{d}\sum_{l_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{d}\in\mathcal{L}_{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s(p,e(l_{j}))f_{j}(l_{j})

uniformly over all
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
- this is the first assertion mentioned above. Defining positive linear operators
T_{j};L^{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})\rightarrow L^{1}(J)
by

T_{j}[f_{j}](p)=\sum_{l_{j}\in\mathcal{L}_{j}}s(p,e(l_{j}))f_{j}(l_{j}),

for any
p\in J
, let us further suppose that
s
can be chosen so that the operators
T_{j}
each satisfy

\norm{T_j[f_j]}_{L^{1}(J)}\leq\norm{S}_{d}\norm{f_j}_{L^{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})}

for all
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
- this is the second assertion. Then

\sum_{p\in J}S(p)T[f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}](p)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq C\sum_{p\in J}\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{j}[f_{j}](p)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{d}\norm{T_j[f_j]}_{1}^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq C\norm{S}_{d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\norm{f_j}_{1}^{\frac{1}{d}},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality followed by boundedness of each
T_{j}
. Therefore, to prove (
2

1
), it suffices, for arbitrary
S
, to find bounded linear operators
T_{j}
, as above, so that

S(p)T[f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}](p)^{1/d}\leq CT_{1}[f_{1}](p)^{1/d}\cdots T_{d}[f_{d}](p)^{1/d}

for all
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
p\in J
for some constant
C=C(d)
.

This type of analysis is closely related to so-called geometric multilinear duality,
[
CHV20
]

This analysis was motivated by the following theorem, essentially due to Bourgain and Guth, which formed the cornerstone of their proof of the general multilinear Kakeya theorem. The precise formulation of this result was not stated by Bourgain and Guth, but was given in
[
CV13
]
by Carbery and Valdimarsson.

To state these factorisation results we momentarily use
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
to denote the classical unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{d}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we define the

Theorem

(Multilinear Kakeya Factorisation Theorem,
[
Gut10
,
BG11
,
CV13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the lattice of unit cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be finitely supported. Then there exists a function
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Q}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
so that

(\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{d})S(Q)^{d}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{d}s(Q,\omega_{j})

for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
and
\omega_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, where
\wedge
denotes the Euclidean wedge product, and so that

\sum_{Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T\cap Q\neq\emptyset}s(Q,e(T))\leq\norm{S}_{d}

for any tube
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with unit cross sectional area and direction

.

The function
s
that appears in the multilinear Kakeya factorisation theorem is constructed in terms of the so-called visibility and directional surface area of a suitable polynomial hypersurface that can associated to the given configuration of tubes.

1.2.
Results

The multijoint problem is a discrete analogue of the multilinear Kakeya problem. This can be seen from (
1
) by taking
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, replacing the counting measure with the Lebesgue measure, the discrete wedge product (in the definition of
\delta
) with the absolute value of the Euclidean wedge product, and lines with 1-tubes. It was observed in
[
CV13
]
(and implicity in
[
Gut10
,
BG11
]
) that the multilinear Kakeya theorem follows from the multilinear Kakeya factorisation theorem, above. Although the multijoint problem was proved by Zhang,
[
Zha20
]
, and more recently, higher-dimensional generaliations were proved by Tidor, Yu and Zhao,
[
TYZ20
]
, a discrete analogue to the multilinear Kakeya factorisation theorem has remained unproven, until now.

From now on we will fix our notation so that
n
denotes the underlying spacial dimension and
d
denotes the degree of multilinearity.

Suppose
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}
are
k_{1}
-,
\ldots,k_{d}
-dimensional vector spaces in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, respectively, where

. We define the
discrete wedge product
on these

-planes by

where

is a choice of
k_{j}
linearly independent directions contained in

, for each
1\leq j\leq d
. We can now define the (

-plane
)
multijoint kernel
by

\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\chi_{\pi_{j}}(p)\right)e(\pi_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge e(\pi_{d}),

for all
p\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
and all
k_{j}
-planes
\pi_{j}
. Given sets of

-planes,
\Pi_{j}

Let

is the set of all

We may now state our results as follows.

Theorem 1

(Discrete Factorisation Theorem)
.

Let
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=n
. For all finitely supported
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\norm{S}_{d}=1

(V_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge V_{d})S(p)^{d}\lesssim_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j}}(p,V_{j}),

for all

and
V_{j}\in\Gr(k_{j},\mathbb{F}^{n})

1\leq j\leq d

1\leq j\leq d

\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}}s_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))\leq 1

for all affine
k_{j}
-subspaces
\pi_{j}\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}
.

Theorem
1
is precisely an analogue of the Multilinear Kakeya Factorisation Theorem. Restricting our attention to a particular choice of sets of
k_{j}
-planes, we arrive at a multijoint-specific factorisation theorem.

Theorem 2

(Multijoint Factorisation Theorem)
.

Let
\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{d}
be sets of
k_{1}
-
,\ldots,k_{d}
-planes in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, respectively, so that

(3)

\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})S(p)^{d}\lesssim_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j})),

for all

\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}\cap J}s_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))=1

Note that in the case that the sets

Remark 1

Recall the linear operators

from Section

\blacktriangleleft

In closely related work in collaboration with Carbery,

2.
Polynomials, Handicaps and Vanishing Conditions

2.1.
The Polynomial Method

This section introduces the main tools we will employ, and motivates the arguments that follow.

We will work in an arbitrary field,
\mathbb{F}
, and any derivative will be the
Hasse
derivative. All arguments remain valid when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
with the usual derivative operator.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a multi-index. The
\alpha
-th Hasse derivative of
f
is the coefficient of the monomial
z^{\alpha}=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
in the polynomial
p(x+z)\in\left(\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\right)[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
. This is denoted by
D^{\alpha}f
. For further details see
[
DKSS13
]
or
[
CI20
]
.

Recall that for any field
\mathbb{F}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5)

\dim\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]={\lambda+n\choose n},

and hence,
\dim\left(\left(\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\right)^{*}\right)={\lambda+n\choose n}
. From this fact, we can deduce a commonly used consequence, known as the parameter counting lemma. Indeed, checking whether a (Hasse) derivative of a low degree polynomial at a point is zero is equivalent to checking whether
\langle\phi,f\rangle=0
for an appropriately chosen
\phi\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\right)^{*}
. Therefore, the following so-called parameter counting Lemma follows from elementary linear algebra.

Lemma 1

(Parameter Counting)
.

Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m}
be homogeneous linear functionals which act on
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. If
m<{n+\lambda\choose n}
, then there is a non-zero
f\in\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
so that
\langle\phi_{i},f\rangle=0
, for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

We organise our arguments in such a way that the only dimensional constants that appear in our stated results arise directly from the implied constants in the inequalities described by
{n+\lambda\choose n}\sim\lambda^{n}
, or, for any positive
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
,

{k_{1}+\lambda\choose k_{1}}\cdots{k_{d}+\lambda\choose k_{d}}\sim_{n}\lambda^{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}}.

2.2.
Handicaps

For any finite subset
J\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
, a
handicap
is a function
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. We will use
\alpha
to equip
J\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with a linear order so that it has a least element. We will then accumulate vanishing conditions as we increment along this linear order, starting from the least element.

Remark 2
.

The handicap keeps track of degrees of freedom in choosing vanishing conditions. More precisely, given a fixed
p_{0}\in J
, the degrees of freedom are the
(\absolutevalue{J}-1)
entries of
(\alpha_{p_{0}}-\alpha_{p})_{p\in J\setminus\{p\}}
.

\blacktriangleleft

Since
J
is finite, we may equip it with a total order so that for
p,q\in J
, at most one of
p<q
,
p=q
or
p>q
holds.
We define a total order (called the
priority order
in
[
TYZ20
]
), denoted by
\prec
, on
J\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as follows. We say
(p,r)\prec(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})
if

•

r-\alpha_{p}<r^{\prime}-\alpha_{p^{\prime}}
, or

•

r-\alpha_{p}=r^{\prime}-\alpha_{p^{\prime}}
and
p<p^{\prime}
.

Moreover,
(p,r)=(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})
if and only if
p=p^{\prime}
and
r=r^{\prime}
. We write
\preceq
to allow for this equality case.

2.3.
Vanishing Conditions

Let
\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{d}
be finite sets of
k_{1}
-
,\ldots,k_{d}
-dimensional planes in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, respectively, where
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=n
. Let
J=\{p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\exists(\pi_{j})_{j}\text{ so that }\delta(p,\pi_{1}\ldots,\pi_{d})=1\}
be the associated set of multijoints. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
be a parameter and let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a handicap. Choose an ordering of the set
J
and thereafter, define the total order
\prec=\prec_{\alpha}
on
J\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, as in Section
2.2
.

Let
\pi\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a
k
-plane which we fix for the remainder of Section
2.3
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\pi
is spanned by the coordinate vectors
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
, so we make the identification

(6)

\left\{f\rvert_{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f\in\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\right\}=\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}].

For each
(p,r)
, we define
\mathbb{B}_{r}(p,\pi,\lambda)
to be the vector space of linear functionals which act on
\mathbb{F}_{\lambda}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}]
of the form
f\mapsto Df(p)
for some differential operator
D
, of order
r
, acting on
k
-variate polynomials.

With
\alpha
,
\lambda
and
\pi
fixed, we now define sets
B(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)
for
p\in\pi\cap J
. To do so, we perform an iterative procedure starting with the
\prec
-least element of
(\pi\cap J)\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and proceeding to the
\prec
-next element on each iteration. Starting with
(p,0)
, the least element of
(\pi\cap J)\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we choose a set
B_{0}(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)\subset\mathbb{B}_{0}(p,\pi,\lambda)
that is a basis for
\mathbb{B}_{0}(p,\pi,\lambda)
.

Assume for each
(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\prec(p,r)
, we have chosen sets
B_{r^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\alpha,\lambda)
so that the disjoint union,
\cup_{(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\prec(p,r)}B_{r^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\alpha,\lambda)
is a basis for
\lspan_{(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\prec(p,r)}\mathbb{B}_{r^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\lambda)
. Thereafter, choose
B_{r}(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)\subset\mathbb{B}_{r}(p,\pi,\lambda)
so that the disjoint union

\bigcup_{(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\preceq(p,r)}B_{r^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\alpha,\lambda)

is a basis for
\lspan_{(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})\preceq(p,r)}\mathbb{B}_{r^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\lambda)
.

Remark 3

2.4.
The Vanishing Lemma

The implied constant

in our Theorem

A traditional application of the polynomial method would find a non-zero polynomial

It was observed in

]

[

]

Once the vanishing lemma is established, the use of polynomials is concluded. We then show that these vanishing conditions were chosen in a way that satisfies desirable uniform boundedness, monotonicity and continuity properties with respect to these degrees of freedom. This allows for a heuristically simple perturbation argument and we conclude the argument by choosing vanishing conditions in a way that respect the geometry of the particular multijoint configuration with which we are working. In Section

, our results diverge from
[
TYZ20
]

2.5.
Handicap Properties

In this subsection we recall and remark upon the uniform boundedness, monotonicity and continuity of the numbers

The advantages of using the new Tidor–Yu–Zhao approach are twofold. Firstly, the choice of vanishing conditions varies nicely with respect to the handicap. Secondly, interpreting vanishing conditions as elements of a dual space allows us to establish the uniform equality (

The good properties of handicaps are as follows, and are proved in

]

non-zero entries, it suffices to show that

(13)

w_{i}-w_{i+1}\leq\frac{h}{\lambda}

for all
1\leq i<N
. Indeed, summing this inequality over all
1\leq i\leq N
, we deduce
\max_{i}w_{i}-\min_{i}w_{i}\leq\absolutevalue{J}h/\lambda
. That is, the interval containing all
(w_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
has width
\sim_{k_{j},J,S}1/\lambda
, where the implied constant does not depend on
\lambda
.

We now prove that (
13
) holds for all
1\leq i<N
. Suppose for a contradiction that there is some index
1\leq i<N
so that (
13
) does not hold. Let
t
be the least such index, and for this choice of

, define

v=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\\
p_{i}\in\Supp S\end{subarray}}e_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\\
p_{i}\not\in\Supp S\end{subarray}}e_{i}

and let
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-v
.

We say that
w_{i}
is
large
if
i\leq t
, and
w_{i}
is
small
otherwise.

Let
\pi\in\Pi_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq d
. Recall that
\sum_{p\in\pi}\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)={\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}
for any
\alpha
. It follows that, if
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi,\alpha^{\prime},\lambda)>\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)
for some
p\in\pi\cap J
, then there exists some
p^{\prime}\in\pi\cap J
so that
p^{\prime}\neq p
and
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\alpha^{\prime},\lambda)<\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p^{\prime},\pi,\alpha,\lambda)
. For those
i
such that
p_{i}\not\in\Supp S
, we have that
w_{i}(\alpha)=0=w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})
. Moreover, by monotonicity (Lemma
4
), for
i
such that
p_{i}\in\Supp S
, we have that if
i\leq t
then
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})\leq w_{i}(\alpha)
(where the handicap is decreased) and if
i>t
then
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq w_{i}(\alpha)
(where the handicap is unchanged). One inequality is strict if and only if the other is too. By Proposition
2
, the difference
\absolutevalue{w_i(\alpha) - w_i(\alpha^\prime)}
is bounded above by
h/(2\lambda)
for all
1\leq i\leq N
, and by the definition of
t
,

(14)

w_{t}(\alpha)-w_{t+1}(\alpha)>\frac{h}{\lambda}.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{\absolutevalue{J}}
be a permutation such that

(15)

w_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq\cdots\geq w_{{\sigma^{\prime}(\absolutevalue{J})}}(\alpha^{\prime}),

where
\sigma(i)=i
for

.

Claim 1
.

If
1\leq i\leq t
, then
1\leq\sigma^{\prime}(i)\leq t
, and if
t<i^{\prime}\leq N
, then
t<\sigma^{\prime}(i^{\prime})\leq\absolutevalue{J}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
p_{i},p_{i^{\prime}}\in\Supp S
are such that
i\leq t<i^{\prime}
. By Proposition

,

\displaystyle w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})-w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})-w_{i}(\alpha)\right)+\left(w_{i}(\alpha)-w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha)\right)+\left(w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha)-w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{h}{2\lambda}+\left(w_{i}(\alpha)-w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha)\right)-\frac{h}{2\lambda}.

Using inequalities (
14
) and (
15
), this is at least

\left(w_{t}(\alpha)-w_{t+1}(\alpha)\right)-\frac{h}{\lambda}>-\frac{h}{\lambda}+\frac{h}{\lambda}=0.

Hence, if
1\leq i\leq t
, then
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})
for all
i^{\prime}>t
. Therefore
1\leq\sigma^{\prime}(i)\leq t
. Similarly, if
t<i\leq N
, then
w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})
for all
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq t
. Hence,
t<\sigma^{\prime}(i)\leq N
, as desired.
∎

Claim 2
.

With
\alpha^{\prime}
as above,
w(\alpha^{\prime})\in w(A)
. Hence
w(\alpha^{\prime})
was among those considered when the minimiser
w(\alpha)
was chosen.

Proof.

Since
w(\alpha)\in w(A)
, we have that
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\cdot,\alpha,\lambda)=0
for all

and

. We may assume that
\alpha
is such that for all

, and all

so that
\pi\ni q
, if

, then

\alpha_{q}<\alpha_{p}-\lambda.

We will show that
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(q,\cdot,\alpha,\lambda)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq d
and
q\not\in\Supp S
. Let
q\not\in\Supp S
and
1\leq j\leq d
. If
\pi\in\Pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Pi_{d}
is such that
\pi\ni q
, and
p\in\pi\cap\Supp S
, then

\alpha_{q}^{\prime}=\alpha_{q}-1<(\alpha_{p}-\lambda)-1=(\alpha_{p}-1)-\lambda\leq\alpha_{p}^{\prime}-\lambda.

We may assume that every plane in
\Pi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Pi_{d}
intersects
\Supp S
, so such
p
exists. Hence, by uniform boundedness (Lemma
3
),
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(q,\pi,\alpha^{\prime},\lambda)=0
. Hence
w(\alpha^{\prime})\in w(A)
and so
(w_{p}(\alpha^{\prime}))_{p}
was among those tuples considered when
(w_{p}(\alpha))_{p}
was chosen.
∎

Claim 3
.

If
w(\alpha^{\prime})\neq w(\alpha)
then
w(\alpha^{\prime})<w(\alpha)
. Hence
w(\alpha)=w(\alpha^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is some
1\leq i\leq\absolutevalue{J}
so that
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})\neq w_{i}(\alpha)
. Then there is an
i\leq t
so that
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})<w_{i}(\alpha)
and
1\leq\sigma^{\prime}(i)\leq t
. That is,
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})
is strictly smaller than
w_{i}(\alpha)
and
w_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}(\alpha^{\prime})
is among the

largest values of
(w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime}))_{1\leq i\leq\absolutevalue{J}}
by Claim
1
. Hence

(w_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}(\alpha^{\prime}))_{1\leq i\leq t}<_{\text{lex}}(w_{i}(\alpha))_{1\leq i\leq t},

and therefore,
w(\alpha^{\prime})
is of strictly lower lexicographical order than
w(\alpha)
. Moreover, by Claim
2
,
w(\alpha^{\prime})\in w(A)
. This contradicts that
w(\alpha)\in w(A)
was chosen to be minimising.
∎

Thus,
w_{i}(\alpha^{\prime})=w_{i}(\alpha-v)=w_{i}(\alpha)
for all
1\leq i\leq\absolutevalue{J}
. We have not yet contradicted our assumption that (
13
) is false, however we have deduced that the perturbation
\alpha\mapsto(\alpha-v)
must leave
(w_{i})_{i}
unchanged. To conclude, we observe that we may return to (
10
), use
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-v
, and repeat the application of Claim
1
, Claim
2
and Claim

. We may repeat this process
c
times to deduce that

for all

. By connectedness of
\Supp S
, we can find a plane

for some

contributing to distinct multijoints

for some

and

. Taking

) forces

) and hence
w_{i}(\alpha)=0
. That is, one of the large entries of the tuple

is zero. Since
(w_{i})_{i}
is decreasing in

and each
w_{i}
is non-negative,
w_{i^{\prime}}(\alpha)=0
for all
i\leq i^{\prime}\leq\absolutevalue{J}
. This contradicts our assumption that
w_{t}(\alpha)-w_{t+1}(\alpha)>h/\lambda
, and hence (

) holds for all

.
∎

3.2.
Good Vanishing Conditions Yield A Factorisation

We now deduce Theorem
2
in the special case where the sets
\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{d}
are finite.

Let
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be non-negative with
\norm{S}_{d}=1
and let
\lambda
be sufficiently large. By Lemma
6
, we may choose a handicap
\alpha=\alpha(\lambda)
, so that the numbers
\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi_{j},\alpha,\lambda)
, as constructed in Section
2.3
, are such that there is an interval containing

w_{p}(\alpha)=\min_{\delta(p,(\pi_{j})_{j})=1}\frac{1}{S(p)^{d}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\tilde{S}(p,\pi_{j},\alpha,\lambda)}{{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}\right)

for all
p\in\Supp S
with length at most
h^{\prime}/\lambda
. Moreover,
\tilde{S}(p,\cdot,\alpha,\lambda)=0
for all
p\not\in\Supp S
. That is, we can find
w\in\mathbb{R}
so that

w-\varepsilon\leq w_{p}(\alpha)\leq w

for all
{p\in\Supp S}
and so that
\tilde{S}(p,\cdot,\alpha,\lambda)=0
if
S(p)=0
, where
\varepsilon=h/\lambda
, and
h
is the constant given in Lemma
6
. For each
p\in J
, let
(\pi_{j}(p))_{j}
be a choice of
k_{j}
-planes which minimises
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{S}(p,\pi_{j}(p),\alpha,\lambda)
over all tuples
(\pi_{j})_{j}
so that
\delta(p,(\pi_{j})_{j})=1
. Corollary
1
applies, so,

(16)

w=\sum_{p\in J}S(p)^{d}w\geq\sum_{p\in J}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\tilde{S}(p,\pi_{j}(p),\alpha,\lambda)}{{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}\geq\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}{\lambda+n\choose n}\gtrsim_{n}1,

where we have used that
w_{p}(\alpha)=0
if
p\not\in\Supp S
, and
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=n
. Hence,
w\gtrsim_{n}1
.

If
\lambda
is large enough, then the length of the interval
\varepsilon=h^{\prime}/\lambda<w
so that the left endpoint
w-\varepsilon
will be strictly positive. Hence, each
B(p,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)\neq\emptyset
for all
p\in J
and every
\pi
that contributes to
p
. Define

s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\tilde{S}(p,\pi_{j},\alpha,\lambda)}{{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}.

For each
p\in J
, if
\pi_{j}\in\Pi_{j}
is such that
p\not\in\pi_{j}
, then set
s_{k_{j},\varepsilon}(p,\pi_{j})=0
. Thus,

1\lesssim_{n}w\leq\frac{h}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{S(p)^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j}(p)).

for any
p\in\Supp S
. Since
\norm{S}_{d}=1
,
S(p)\leq 1
for all
p\in J
. Hence

S^{d}(p)\lesssim_{n}\frac{h}{\lambda}+\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j}(p)).

Since the tuple of planes
(\pi_{j}(p))_{j}
minimises the right-hand side over all tuples such that
\delta(p,(\pi_{j})_{j})=1
,

(17)

\delta(p,(\pi_{j})_{j})S^{d}(p)\lesssim_{n}\frac{h}{\lambda}+\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j})

for all tuples of planes
(\pi_{j})_{j}
. By construction, for all
1\leq j\leq d
,
\pi_{j}\in\Pi_{j}

(18)

\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}\cap J}s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j})=\frac{1}{{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}\cap J}\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi_{j}(p),\alpha,\lambda)=1.

Both inequality (
17
) and equation (
18
) are uniform in
\varepsilon
and each function

\frac{1}{S(p)}s_{k_{j},\lambda}(p,\pi_{j})=\frac{1}{S(p)}\frac{\tilde{S}_{k_{j}}(p,\pi_{j},\alpha,\lambda)}{{\lambda+k_{j}\choose k_{j}}}

can be realised as an
\mathbb{R}
-valued vector in

. Hence, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may let
s_{k_{j}}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}s_{k_{j},\lambda}
for each
1\leq j\leq d
. Letting
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
in (
17
) and (

) concludes our proof of Theorem
2
under the assumption that the sets
\Pi_{j}
are finite. ∎

3.3.
Multijoint Factorisation to Discrete Factorisation

With Theorem
2
proved for finite sets
\Pi_{j}
, we deduce Theorem
1
. If the field
\mathbb{F}
is finite, then we can apply Theorem
2
with each
\pi_{j}=\Gr(k_{j},\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Hence, we may assume that the field is infinite.

Let
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be finitely supported. Consider

\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left(\pi_{j}\cap\Supp S\right)_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})=1,\,p\in\mathbb{F}^{n},\,\pi_{j}\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}\right\},

where in the definition of
\mathcal{O}
, we consider any tuple of
k_{j}
-planes (of which there may be infinitely many) and any
p\in\Supp S
. However, since
\Supp S
is finite, so too is
\mathcal{O}
. For every
(E_{j})_{j}\in\mathcal{O}
, choose a tuple of
k_{j}
-planes so that
\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})=1
and
\pi_{j}\cap\Supp S=E_{j}
for every
1\leq j\leq d
. We hence define the finite sets
\Pi_{j}
to consist of all such planes
\pi_{j}
. In particular, since the field is infinite, for each
p\in\Supp S
, the tuple
(\{p\})_{j}
belongs to
\mathcal{O}
and hence
\Supp S
is a subset of the multijoints formed by the finite sets
\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{d}
. Hence, there exist factorising functions
\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}
that satisfy the displays described in Theorem
2
.

Recall from Remark
3
that if two
k_{j}
-planes
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are distinct and satisfy
\pi\cap\Supp S=\pi^{\prime}\cap\Supp S
, then
B(\cdot,\pi,\alpha,\lambda)=B(\cdot,\pi^{\prime},\alpha,\lambda)
. Hence, the functions
\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}
satisfy
\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}(\cdot,e(\pi))=\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}(\cdot,e(\pi^{\prime}))
. Now, consider the pairs
(p,V_{j})\in\Supp S\times\Gr(k_{j},\mathbb{F}^{n})
. By construction, there exists
\pi_{j}\in\Pi_{j}
so that
\pi_{j}\cap\Supp S=(p+V_{j})\cap\Supp S
and it is well-defined to set
s_{k_{j}}(p,V_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))
. We additionally set
s_{k_{j}}(p,\cdot)=0
for any
p\not\in\Supp S
, whereby each
s_{k_{j}}
is finitely supported and defined on
\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\Gr(k_{j},\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Each
s_{k_{j}}
automatically satisfies

\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}}s_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))=\sum_{p\in\pi_{j}\cap J}s_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))=1

for any
k_{j}
-plane
\pi_{j}\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}
, establishing the second display of Theorem
1
. Turning to the first display, let
p\in\Supp S
and let
V_{j}\in\Gr(k_{j},\mathbb{F}^{n})
be such that
V_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge V_{d}=1
. For each
j
there exists
\pi_{j}\in\Pi_{j}
so that
(p+V_{j})\cap\Supp S=\pi_{j}\cap\Supp S
and
\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})=1
by construction. Hence

\displaystyle(V_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge V_{d})S(p)^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(p,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})S(p)^{d}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{s}_{k_{j}}(p,e(\pi_{j}))\hskip 10.00002pt=\hskip 10.00002pt\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{k_{j}}(p,V_{j}).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

An alternative presentation of this argument in the case where each
k_{j}=1
can also be found in
[
CT22
, Section 4]
.

k_{j}

\gamma_{j}

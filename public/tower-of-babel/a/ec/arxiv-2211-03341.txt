2.1
Radial Distance and Radial Curves

The

in
G
between two faces

and
f_{2}
of
G
is defined as the minimum length of
a sequence of faces starting from
f_{1}
to

, such that
every two consecutive faces of this sequence share a common vertex.
Similarly, the radial distance between two vertices

and

, denoted by
\textsf{rdist}(u,v)
,
is defined as the minimum radial distance between two faces
F_{u}
and
F_{v}
incident to
u
and

, respectively.
Note that there is a curve connecting
u
and
v

which meets

only at
\textsf{rdist}(u,v)
vertices (excluding

and

) and does not intersect any edges of
G
.
We call such a curve a

connecting

and
v
. See Figure
1
(a).
For two subsets

and

of

as the Hausdorff distance:
\textsf{rdist}(X,Y)=\max\{\max_{x\in X}\min_{y\in Y}\textsf{rdist}(x,y),\max_{y\in Y}\min_{x\in X}\textsf{rdist}(x,y)\}
.
For two subgraphs
P
and

of

, we define their radial distance as

.

An

of

We can compute

in linear time using a variant of the breadth-first search on

Also, we call a simple closed curve a

if it intersects

, and does not intersect any edge of
G
.
The complexity of a noose is defined as the number of vertices of
G
intersected by the noose.
A similar notion we use in this paper is a

in

: an alternating sequence
of faces and edges
such that every face is incident (in
G
) to the edges neighboring in the sequence, and the two end elements of the sequence are faces.

The
radial completion
of
G
, denoted by

, is a subgraph of

constructed as follows. We add one vertex for each face

of

, and connect this vertex and all vertices lying on

. Note that every face of

is a triangle.
See Figure
1

One can consider a radial curve of length

of length

.
We use the radial completion to define the discrete homotopy in the following subsection.

Schrijver

As the algorithm of

Let

The group

.
Then the product

Flow Function and Linkages.

A flow function
\phi:E(G)\rightarrow\Sigma^{*}
is defined as follows.
For a vertex
v

be its incident edges in

Then

\phi(e_{r})^{\mu(e_{r})}
’s for all

where the sign

2
2

2

Note that

depends on the choice of

. To avoid the ambiguity, we choose

A function

,

An edge-disjoint weak

Let

For each edge

We say

But it is possible that a flow function

Discrete Homotopy and Linkages

Instead of working with homology,

[

Two weak linkages

There are three types of face operations:

,
Face Pull
, and
Face Push
.
Since our argument uses
Face Move
and
Face Pull
only, we give the definitions of
Face Move
and
Face Pull
only. The definition of

can be found in
[
21
, Definition 5.3]
. Each operation is applied to a face
F
of

and a walk

of
\mathcal{W}
. Let
\partial F
be the cycle consisting of the three edges incident to
F
.
For illustration, see Figure

3.1.1

The first step is to enumerate

Given an instance

More specifically, a vertex

A single irrelevant vertex can be found in linear time, and thus a naive implementation of this technique yields a quadratic-time algorithm.

3.1.3
Obstacles in Designing a
2^{O(k^{2})}n
-time Algorithm and Our Methods

There are three places where a super-linear dependence on
n
appears:
the application of the irrelevant vertex technique takes
2^{O(k)}n^{2}
time,
the computation of the backbone tree takes
O(n^{2})
time, and the algorithm of
[
25
]
takes
O(n^{6})
time.
Although a fine-grained analysis of the algorithm of
[
25
]
gives the bound of

O(\chi^{2}+n\cdot\eta\cdot\chi\cdot|\Pi|)
, the values of
\chi,\eta
and
|\Pi|
are too large in the case of the weak
T
-linkages
\mathcal{W}

constructed from
[
21
]
.
This is because the number of edges of
R
is
\Theta(n)
, and the maximum radial distance from the outer face to
the vertices of
R
is
\Theta(n)
in the worst case.

One possible direction for obtaining a faster algorithm for the
Planar Disjoint Paths
problem is
to construct weak
T
-linkages with respect to some other backbone structure of complexity of
2^{O(k)}
instead of constructing them with respect to the backbone tree. Then (after modifying
G
slightly), we can show that this decreases
\chi
to
2^{O(k)}
, and thus this leads to an algorithm running in
2^{O(k^{2})}n^{5}
time.
This was our starting point.

New Backbone Structures.

We first construct a backbone structure (a subgraph of
G^{\textsf{rad}}
) of complexity of
2^{O(k)}
in
2^{O(k)}n
time, and then show that all paths of
\mathcal{P}
can be pushed onto the backbone structure (and several precomputed paths) for a
T
-linkage
\mathcal{P}
. In this paper, we call this backbone structure the
frames
and
skeleton forests
, which will be defined later.
In this way, each edge on the backbone structure is traversed by the resulting walks
2^{O(k)}
times in total.
Also, each edge on the precomputed paths is traversed by the resulting walks at most once in total.
This allows us to only consider the weak
T
-linkages using each edge of the backbone structure
2^{O(k)}
times and each edge of the precomputed paths exactly once.
There are
2^{O(k^{2})}
such weak
T
-linkages
\mathcal{W}
, and we can enumerate each of them in
2^{O(k)}n
time.
By slightly modifying
G
with respect to a given weak
T
-linkage, we can make each vertex of the backbone structure
have degree
2^{O(k)}
(without changing the answer of the instance).
Then the number
\chi
of face-edge paths
\pi
of
\Pi
with
\phi(\pi)\notin\Gamma(\pi)
decreases to
2^{O(k)}
.
In this way, we can overcome the second obstacle: we can construct the backbone structure in
2^{O(k)}n
time (although we define the backbone structure in a different way.)
Also, we can partially overcome the third obstacle: now the algorithm of
[
25
]
takes
O(n^{5})
time in our case.

Maintaining a Data Structure.

There remain obstacles to be handled to obtain a
2^{O(k^{2})}n
time algorithm.
First, due to properties of the backbone structure we defined, we can show that
the length of
\bar{f}(F)
of all initial functions
f
constructed from
[
25
]
is
2^{O(k)}
.
But even in our case,
|\Pi|
can be
2^{\Theta(k)}n
as the total number of edges of the walks of
\mathcal{W}
can be
2^{\Theta(k)}n
.
We resolve this issue by designing a suitable data structure and implementing the algorithm of
[
25
]

using this data structure.
In this way, we can fully overcome the third obstacle: Given a
correct
weak
T
-linkage of
G
defined with respect to the backbone structure (frames and skeleton forests),
we can compute a
T
-linkage of
G
in
2^{O(k)}n
time.

Irrelevant Vertex Technique.

The remaining obstacle is to remove irrelevant vertices so that

has treewidth
2^{O(k)}
in
2^{O(k)}n
time in total.
We overcome this obstacle by removing irrelevant vertices while subdividing
G
into several pieces.
This idea was already used in
[
23
]
, but this work focused on designing
2^{O(k^{2})}n
time algorithm for the

Planar Disjoint Paths
problem. We apply the algorithm
[
23
]
first, and then we are given

2^{O(k)}
pieces (subgraphs of

) enclosed by a closed curve such that no terminal is contained in the

of each piece.
Then we remove all vertices in each piece whose radial distance is at least

from the boundary vertices of the piece for a specific constant

in time linear in the complexity of the piece. We show that all such vertices are irrelevant for
(G,T,k)

the resulting graph has treewidth
2^{O(k)}
.

Let

Let

whose terminals lie consecutively along

Now we show that the algorithm returns a feasible solution if there exists a feasible weak

-linkage

We claim that

In each iteration, we find a consecutive terminal pair and a path connecting them along

Merging the Subwalks Obtained from Each Framed Ring.

We simply merge all subwalks obtained from the framed rings.

Flow Function and Linkages.

A weak

We let

A

of two strings

Let

For each edge

to

, and
let

if it is not used by any path of

In general, a flow function

is defined as follows.
For a vertex

be its incident edges in

sorted in the clockwise direction.
Then

be the product of
\psi(e_{r})^{v(e_{r})}

where the sign

is an outgoing edge from

A function

is called a

if

for all

for all

,
and

Homology.

For a directed edge

of

denote the face lying to the left of

denote

We say two flow functions

is the set of all faces of

•

Homology Feasibility Problem.

Our goal in this section is to compute a flow function
\psi
homologous to a given flow function

such that

represents a
T
-

.
To control the number of edges with

[
25

introduced a more general problem setting: the

In this problem, we are given a flow function
\phi
, and a candidate function

,
where
\Pi
is a set of

.
Here, a face-edge path in

is an alternating sequence of edges and faces such that

.
Moreover,

must be

also belong to
\Gamma(\pi)

In the following, for a flow function

denote the product of

\pi

where the sign

is the face lying previous to

\pi

Then the goal is to compute a flow function
\psi
homologous to a given flow function

such that

\psi(\pi)\in\Gamma(\pi)

Schrijver showed that a flow function

with

for all

corresponds to a

-linkage
if
\Pi
is the set of all face-edge paths each consisting of faces and edges sharing a common vertex,
and if

for all

Therefore, it suffices to present an algorithm for the homology feasibility problem.

Recall that our goal is compute a homology function

for a given

Also, a function

For two strings

in

, we define the

of them, denoted by

x\vee y
is infinite. Furthermore, two functions

8.1.1

For a function

The subroutine is based on the following lemma.

Definition 28

We say a face-edge path

We repeat this procedure until

The analysis of

8.3
Analysis of the Subroutine

In this section, we analyze the running time of the algorithm for finding a smallest pre-feasible function

Lemma 33

The length of
\bar{f}_{\pi}(F)

Proof.

Recall that
\phi

First, we show that

The number of edges of

or

Therefore,
|\psi(\gamma_{i})|=2^{O(k)}

|\phi(\gamma_{i})|\in 2^{O(k)}

Then we show that
\bar{f}_{\pi}(F)

Corollary 34

The number of iterations of the subroutine described in

Dynamic Data Structure for Finding a Violating Face-Edge Pair.

We let
\psi

f(L_{e})^{-1}\cdot\phi(e)\cdot f(R_{e})

By definition,

First, the number of face-edge paths in

Therefore, in the following, it suffices to design a data structure for finding a violating face-edge pair

For every vertex

Query and Update Algorithms.

To answer a query, we first check if there is a vertices marked as “un-feasible”.
If a vertex

We select two edges

At least one face-edge path

During the algorithm, we make updating

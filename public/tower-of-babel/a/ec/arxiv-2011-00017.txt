MHV Gluon Scattering Amplitudes from Celestial Current Algebras
Shamik Banerjee, Sudip Ghosh

Abstract
We show that the Mellin transform of an n n -point tree level MHV gluon scattering amplitude, also known as the celestial amplitude in pure Yang-Mills theory, satisfies a system of ( n − 2 ) (n-2) linear first order partial differential equations corresponding to ( n − 2 ) (n-2) positive helicity gluons. Although these equations closely resemble Kniznik-Zamolodchikov equations for S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) current algebra there is also an additional “correction” term coming from the subleading soft gluon current algebra. These equations can be used to compute the leading term in the gluon-gluon OPE on the celestial sphere. Similar equations can also be written down for the momentum space tree level MHV scattering amplitudes. We also propose a way to deal with the non closure of subleading current algebra generators under commutation. This is then used to compute some subleading terms in the mixed helicity gluon OPE.

They are called conformal primaries because under Lorentz transformations, which act on the celestial sphere as global conformal transformations, they transform like primary operators in a conformal field theory. Let us now briefly describe the main results of this paper.

In this paper we focus on the Maximal Helicity Violating (MHV) gluon scattering amplitudes in pure Yang-Mills (YM) theory with gauge group

The first two terms of (

2.

The third term in (

This has no analog in the usual KZ equation and is most likely related to the fact that there is no Sugawara stress tensor in CCFT. This is also very different from pure gravity where the corresponding differential equations for MHV amplitudes do not have any contribution from the subsubleading soft graviton theorem.

(25)

where

T^{a}_{k}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=if^{aa_{i}b}\ \mathcal{O}^{b}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\ \delta_{ki}

(26)

and

P_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i}-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\ \delta_{ki}

(27)

Here
\epsilon_{k}=\pm 1
depending on whether the gluon primary
\mathcal{O}^{a_{k}}_{h_{k},\bar{h}_{k}}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})
is outgoing or incoming. For simplicity of notation we keep the additional label
\epsilon
implicit when we write the correlation functions of the gluons.

Now following
Pate:2019lpp
;
Banerjee:2020zlg
we expand the R.H.S of (
25
) in powers of the coordinate
\bar{z}
of the subleading conformally soft gluon operator
S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
and define two currents
J^{a}(z)
and
K^{a}(z)
whose Ward identities are given by,

\begin{gathered}\bigg\langle{J^{a}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{z-z_{k}}\left(-2\bar{h}_{k}+1-\bar{z}_{k}\bar{\partial}_{k}\right)T^{a}_{k}P_{k}^{-1}\bigg\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\bigg\rangle\end{gathered}

(28)

and

\begin{gathered}\bigg\langle K^{a}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\bigg\rangle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{z-z_{k}}\bar{\partial}_{k}\ T^{a}_{k}P_{k}^{-1}\bigg\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\bigg\rangle\end{gathered}

(29)

This is equivalent to expanding the soft operator
S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
as,

S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})=J^{a}(z)+\bar{z}\hskip 1.42271ptK^{a}(z)

(30)

We can now define the modes of the currents,
J^{a}_{n}(z)
and
K^{a}_{n}(z)
, in the standard way. Their actions on a gluon primary are given by the following commutation relations,

\begin{split}\left[J^{a}_{n},\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]&=-\epsilon z^{n}\left(-2\bar{h}+1-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)T^{a}P^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\\
&=-i\epsilon f^{abc}z^{n}\left(-2\bar{h}+1-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})\end{split}

(31)

and

\begin{gathered}\left[K^{a}_{n},\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=-\epsilon z^{n}\bar{\partial}\ T^{a}P^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})=-i\epsilon f^{abc}z^{n}\bar{\partial}\ \mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})\end{gathered}

(32)

Although the generators
\left(J^{a}_{n},K^{a}_{n}\right)
do not form a Lie algebra under commutation, we will see in section (
5
) that when the commutators
\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]
,
\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]
and
\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]

act on a gluon conformal primary or its descendants
, the results are given by simple expressions which look almost like closure. This is crucial for our purpose in this paper.

4.1
OPE between
S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
and a hard gluon conformal primary

Suppose we want to compute the OPE between
S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
and the gluon primary
\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
. For this we have to expand the R.H.S of (
25
) in powers of
(z-z_{1})
and
(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{1})
. So as
(z,\bar{z})\rightarrow(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
we can write,

\begin{gathered}\bigg\langle S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\bigg\rangle\\
=\left(-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{z-z_{1}}\left(-2\bar{h}_{1}+1\right)T^{a}_{1}P^{-1}_{1}+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(z-z_{1})^{p-1}\mathcal{J}^{a}_{-p}(1)\right)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle\\
+(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{1})\left(-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{z-z_{1}}\bar{\partial}_{1}T^{a}_{1}P_{1}^{-1}+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(z-z_{1})^{p-1}\mathcal{K}^{a}_{-p}(1)\right)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle\end{gathered}

(33)

where the differential operators
\mathcal{J}^{a}_{-p}(1)
and
\mathcal{K}^{a}_{-p}(1)
are defined as,

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{J}^{a}_{-p}(1)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle=-\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n}\epsilon_{j}\frac{2\bar{h}_{j}-1+(\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{1})\bar{\partial}_{j}}{(z_{j}-z_{1})^{p}}T^{a}_{j}P^{-1}_{j}\right)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle,\quad p\geq 1\end{gathered}

(34)

and

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{K}^{a}_{-p}(1)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n}\epsilon_{j}\frac{\bar{\partial}_{j}}{(z_{j}-z_{1})^{p}}T^{a}_{j}P^{-1}_{j}\right)\bigg\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}^{a_{i}}_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\bigg\rangle,\quad p\geq 1\end{gathered}

(35)

From (
33
) the OPE between
S^{a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
and
\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
can be extracted to be,

\begin{gathered}S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\\
=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{z-z_{1}}\left(-2\bar{h}_{1}+1\right)T^{a}_{1}P^{-1}_{1}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(z-z_{1})^{p-1}\left(J^{a}_{-p}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}\right)(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\\
-(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{1})\left(\epsilon_{1}\frac{\bar{\partial}_{1}}{z-z_{1}}T^{a}_{1}P_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(z-z_{1})^{p-1}\left(K^{a}_{-p}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}\right)(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\right)\end{gathered}

(36)

where the correlation functions with the insertion of the descendants
J^{a}_{-p}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
and
K^{a}_{-p}\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
are given by (
34
) and (
35
), respectively.

Now (
36
) acts as a boundary condition on the OPE of two hard gluon primaries one which is positive helicity. Using the definition (
24
) of the subleading conformally soft gluon
S^{+a}_{1}(z,\bar{z})
we can write,

\boxed{\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 0}\Delta\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,+}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})=\eqref{ope1}}

(37)

As we will see (
37
) is a nontrivial constraint on the OPE of two hard gluons. Inside correlation functions (
37
) means that we first take the OPE limit
(z,\bar{z})\rightarrow(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
and then take the subleading conformal soft limit. In that limit we should always get back (
33
).

We also note that the operator product expansion (
36
) leads to the following conditions which are satisfied by any gluon primary
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
,

J^{a}_{n}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=0,\quad J^{a}_{0}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abc}(-2\bar{h}+1)\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0),\quad\forall n>0

(38)

and

K^{a}_{n}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=0,\quad K^{a}_{0}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abc}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0),\quad\forall n>0

(39)

4.2
Commutation relations involving subleading generators

In this section we collect some useful commutation relations. These are the ”classical” commutators which can be easily obtained from the action of the generators on a primary operator. For the convenience of the reader we gather here the actions of the Poincare and current algebra generators on a gluon primary,

\left[L_{1},\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\left(z^{2}\partial+2hz\right)\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z}),\quad\left[L_{-1},\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\partial\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(40)

\left[\bar{L}_{1},\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\left(\bar{z}^{2}\bar{\partial}+2\bar{h}\bar{z}\right)\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z}),\quad\left[\bar{L}_{-1},\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(41)

\left[P_{m,n},\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=\epsilon z^{m+1}\bar{z}^{n+1}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{h+\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

(42)

\left[j^{a}_{n},\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=-iz^{n}f^{abc}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(43)

\left[J^{a}_{n},\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=-i\epsilon f^{abc}z^{n}\left(-2\bar{h}+1-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

(44)

\left[K^{a}_{n},\mathcal{O}^{b}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=-i\epsilon f^{abc}z^{n}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

(45)

where
\epsilon=\pm 1
depending on whether the gluon is outgoing or incoming. Using these we arrive at the following commutation relations between generators,

\left[L_{1},J^{a}_{n}\right]=-(n+1)J^{a}_{n+1},\quad\left[L_{0},J^{a}_{n}\right]=\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)J^{a}_{n},\quad\left[L_{-1},J^{a}_{n}\right]=-nJ^{a}_{n-1}

(46)

\left[\bar{L}_{1},J^{a}_{n}\right]=0,\quad\left[\bar{L}_{0},J^{a}_{n}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}J^{a}_{n},\quad\left[\bar{L}_{-1},J^{a}_{n}\right]=-K^{a}_{n}

(47)

\left[L_{1},K^{a}_{n}\right]=-(n+1)K^{a}_{n+1},\quad\left[L_{0},K^{a}_{n}\right]=\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)K^{a}_{n},\quad\left[L_{-1},K^{a}_{n}\right]=-nK^{a}_{n-1}

(48)

\left[\bar{L}_{1},K^{a}_{n}\right]=J^{a}_{n},\quad\left[\bar{L}_{0},K^{a}_{n}\right]=\frac{1}{2}K^{a}_{n},\quad\left[\bar{L}_{-1},K^{a}_{n}\right]=0

(49)

\left[P_{m,-1},J^{a}_{n}\right]=j^{a}_{m+n+1},\quad\left[P_{m,0},J^{a}_{n}\right]=0,\quad m=0,-1

(50)

\left[P_{m,-1},K^{a}_{n}\right]=0,\quad\left[P_{m,0},K^{a}_{n}\right]=j^{a}_{m+n+1},\quad m=0,-1

(51)

\left[j^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]=-if^{abc}J^{c}_{m+n},\quad\left[j^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]=-if^{abc}K^{c}_{m+n}

(52)

5
How to interpret the commutators of subleading symmetry generators

It is well known
Lysov:2014csa
that the subleading symmetry generators
J^{a}_{n}
and
K^{a}_{n}
do not close under commutation. So they are not the generators of a Lie algebra symmetry in the ordinary sense. To see this we note that the scaling dimensions of
J^{a}_{n}
and
K^{a}_{n}
are given by
(-n-1/2,-1/2)
and
(-n-1/2,1/2)
, respectively. Therefore the antiholomorphic scaling dimension of the commutator amongst these generators must be an integer. But there is no generator with integer antiholomorphic scaling dimension that can appear here and so the generators
(J^{a}_{m},K^{a}_{m})
cannot form a Lie algebra.

Now, at least for the purposes of this paper, what we really need to know is how the commutator of two subleading generators acts on a gluon primary or its descendants. For example, in the OPE of two outgoing gluon primaries of opposite helicities given by,
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta_{1},+}
and
\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{1},-}
, one gets a subleading term of the form (
235
),
J^{a}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}
. In order to calculate the OPE coefficient multiplying this operator, one has to know the structure of the term

J^{c}_{1}J^{a}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}=\left[J^{c}_{1},J^{a}_{-1}\right]\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}

(53)

In (
53
) we have used (
38
) which gives
J^{c}_{1}O^{b}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}=0
. More generally, we will get terms like

\left(\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right],\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]\right)\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(54)

from the OPE and we need to get simplified expressions for them. In order to do this we start by computing the following commutators

\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right],\quad\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right],\quad\left[\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]

(55)

Let us focus on the first commutator. Using (
44
) and the Jacobi identity we get,

\begin{gathered}\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]\\
=-f^{abd}f^{dce}z^{m+n}\left(-2\bar{h}+1-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)\left(-2\bar{h}+2-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)\mathcal{O}^{e}_{h-1,\bar{h}-1}(z,\bar{z})\end{gathered}

(56)

Again using (
44
) we can write this as

\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]=-i\epsilon f^{abd}\left(-2\bar{h}+1-\bar{z}\bar{\partial}\right)\left[J^{d}_{m+n},\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})\right]

(57)

where we have used
\epsilon^{2}=1
. Now we take the limit
(z,\bar{z})\rightarrow(0,0)
and from (
57
) we get

\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)\right]=-i\epsilon f^{abd}\left(-2\bar{h}+1\right)\left[J^{d}_{m+n},\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)\right]

(58)

Since the mode
J^{a}_{n}
of the current
J^{a}
is defined with respect to the point
(0,0)
, (
58
) can be interpreted as the relation between the descendants

\boxed{\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abd}\left(-2\bar{h}+1\right)J^{d}_{m+n}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}

(59)

Now we can apply the same argument to get the other two relations

\boxed{\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abd}\left(-2\bar{h}+1\right)K^{d}_{m+n}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}

(60)

and

\boxed{\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abd}K^{d}_{m+n}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)}

(61)

These are the relations that will be used in the following sections to obtain recursion relations for subleading OPE coefficients and to define null states or primary descendants.

We can see that the L.H.S of (
59
), (
60
) and (
61
) are linear in the subleading symmetry generators. But, they also depend on the scaling dimension of the gluon primary on which the commutators are acting and also the gluon primary appearing on the R.H.S has shifted dimension compared to the one appearing on the L.H.S. This is a signature of the fact that the generators do not form a Lie algebra. This is perhaps the closest one can come towards forming a closed algebra out of
J^{a}_{n}
and
K^{a}_{n}
when they act on states in a Hilbert space and, as we will see, this is sufficient for finding (subleading) OPE coefficients as we will see in section (
9
).

Before we end this section we would like to mention the result when the commutator acts on a level-
1
descendant. They are given by,

\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]J^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)J^{e}_{m+n}J^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(62)

\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]J^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}J^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(63)

\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]J^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}J^{c}_{p}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(64)

\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]K^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)J^{e}_{m+n}K^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(65)

\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]K^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}K^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(66)

\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]K^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}K^{c}_{p}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(67)

\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]j^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)J^{e}_{m+n}j^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(68)

\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]j^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}j^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(69)

\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]j^{c}_{p}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abe}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{e}_{m+n}j^{c}_{p}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(70)

The above relations, say for example (
62
), can be obtained by starting from the commutator

\left[\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right],\left[J^{c}_{p},\mathcal{O}^{d}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\right]\right]

(71)

The above relations have obvious generalisations to a general descendant and are given by,

\left[J^{a}_{m},J^{b}_{n}\right]\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abx}(-2\bar{h}+1)J^{x}_{m+n}\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(72)

\left[J^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abx}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{x}_{m+n}\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(73)

\left[K^{a}_{m},K^{b}_{n}\right]\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h,\bar{h}}(0)=-i\epsilon f^{abx}(-2\bar{h}+1)K^{x}_{m+n}\prod_{i}J^{c_{i}}_{p_{i}}\prod_{j}K^{d_{j}}_{q_{j}}\prod_{k}j^{e_{k}}_{r_{k}}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{O}^{f}_{h-\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}-\frac{1}{2}}(0)

(74)

We do not need these more general relations in this paper but, it will be interesting to check their consistency with explicit calculations performed using scattering amplitudes. For example, a good check of this will be to compute subleading OPE coefficients in the gluon-gluon OPE directly from the (MHV) scattering amplitude and compare them with the results from the recursion relations obtained using (
72
), (
73
) and (
74
). Our derivation of these relations has been somewhat hand waving. We leave a more rigorous derivation to future work.

6
Differential equation for MHV gluon amplitudes in Mellin space

In this section we will derive a differential equation for Mellin transformed tree level
n
-point MHV gluon amplitudes in Yang-Mills theory.

Consider the celestial OPE between two positive helicity outgoing gluon primaries in Yang-Mills theory. We will denote these operators below as
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,+}(z,\bar{z})
and
\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})

8.2
Outgoing-incoming OPE

We will now deal with the case where one of the gluon primaries in the celestial OPE is outgoing and the other is incoming. Here we will take the outgoing gluon primary to have positive helicity and denote it as
\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})

In this case, both an outgoing and an incoming gluon primary can contribute to the OPE at leading order. Then as in the previous subsection we begin by assuming the following general form of the leading OPE

\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\mathcal{O}^{a_{2},-}_{\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\\

\mathcal{O}^{x,+}_{\Delta,\sigma}
and
\mathcal{O}^{x,-}_{\Delta,\sigma}

) respectively denote the outgoing and incoming primaries which contribute to the leading OPE. Their conformal dimension and spin are

\begin{split}\Delta=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+p+q,\quad\sigma=p-q+\sigma_{2}+1\end{split}

(119)

In (
118

Now following exactly the same steps as in the previous subsection (
8.1
) we can obtain a recursion relation for the leading OPE coefficients using the differential equation (
97

\begin{split}(2p+\Delta_{1}+1)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})+(\Delta_{2}-\sigma_{2}-1+q)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1}+1,\Delta_{2}-1,\sigma_{2})=0\end{split}

\begin{split}&(\Delta_{1}+q-2)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1}-1,\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})-(\Delta_{2}-\sigma_{2}-1)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}-1,\sigma_{2})\\
&=\pm 2(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+2q-\sigma_{2}-2)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})\end{split}

Let us also note that invariance of the OPE (

\begin{split}C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})=\pm\bigg[C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1}+1,\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})-C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}+1,\sigma_{2})\bigg]\end{split}

\begin{split}(2p+\Delta_{1}+1)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})=\pm(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+2p+q-\sigma_{2}+1)C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1}+1,\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})\end{split}

\begin{split}&\bigg[\Delta_{1}+q-1+\frac{(\Delta_{1}+2p+1)(\Delta_{2}-\sigma_{2}-1)}{\Delta_{2}-\sigma_{2}+q-1}\bigg]C^{\pm}_{p,q}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\sigma_{2})\\

\begin{split}&p=-1,\quad q=0\end{split}

9
Subleading OPE coefficients from symmetry

In this section we will illustrate how the OPE coefficients of descendants in the celestial OPE between gluon primaries can be determined using the underlying symmetries. For the OPE between positive helicity gluons, some of the descendant OPE coefficients were obtained in
Ebert:2020nqf
using translation, global conformal and leading soft gluon current algebra symmetries. Here we will consider the mixed helicity case, i.e., the OPE between a positive helicity and a negative helicity gluon primary. We will see that the subleading soft gluon symmetry plays a crucial role here.

Let us denote the gluon primaries whose OPE we want to consider as
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})
and
\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{2},-}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})
. We will also consider both of these to be outgoing. Then as shown in Section
C
of the Appendix in this paper, upto the first subleading order this OPE is given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{2},-}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\\
&\sim B(\Delta_{1}-1,\Delta_{2}+1)\bigg[-\frac{if^{abx}}{z_{12}}+\Delta_{1}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta^{bx}\hskip 1.42271ptj^{a}_{-1}+\frac{(\Delta_{1}-1)}{(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})}\hskip 1.13791pt\delta^{bx}J^{a}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\bigg]\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1,-}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\end{split}

(128)

where
P_{-1,-1}\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1,-}=\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}
. In the Appendix, Section
C
we have derived this result from the Mellin transform of the
4
-point MHV gluon amplitude in Yang-Mills theory. Although we have obtained this from the
4
-point Mellin amplitude, the above form of the mixed helicity OPE is expected to hold within any
n
-point tree level MHV gluon amplitude in Yang-Mills.

Now it is important to note that in the OPE (

), at order

and
J^{a}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},-}
respectively in (
128
). We will now show that the OPE coefficients for these descendants can also determined using symmetries as follows.

In general, we can have the following descendants appearing at
\mathcal{O}(1)
in the mixed-helicity OPE

\begin{split}&L_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,-},\quad j^{a}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta,-},\quad J^{a}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta,-}\end{split}

(129)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{2},-}(0)\supset B(\Delta_{1}-1,\Delta_{2}+1)\bigg[\alpha_{1}f^{abx}\hskip 1.42271ptL_{-1}+\alpha_{2}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta^{bx}j^{a}_{-1}+\alpha_{3}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta^{bx}J^{a}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\bigg]\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1,-}(0)\end{split}

(130)

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
are constants which we want to determine. The leading OPE coefficient

89

Now let us apply the subleading soft symmetry mode

\begin{split}&i\hskip 1.13791pt\alpha_{1}f^{cxd}f^{abx}+\left(\alpha_{2}-(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})\alpha_{3}-1\right)f^{cax}f^{xbd}=0\end{split}

\begin{split}&\alpha_{1}=0\end{split}

(132)

Similarly setting

\begin{split}&\alpha_{2}-(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})\alpha_{3}-1=0\end{split}

The Celestial or Mellin amplitude for

\sigma_{i}=\pm 1

i

i

The Lorentz group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

in a

A.1
Comments on notation in the paper

Note that conformal primaries carry an additional index

Similarly in many places in the paper we denote a gluon conformal primary of weight

&\delta\left(\bar{z}_{14}-\frac{t}{\sigma_{1,1}}\left(1+z_{34}\ t\ \frac{\sigma_{1,2}}{\sigma_{1,1}}\right)^{-1}\frac{z_{23}}{z_{12}}\bar{z}_{34}\right)\delta\left(\bar{z}_{24}+\frac{t}{\sigma_{2,1}}\left(1+z_{34}\ t\ \frac{\sigma_{2,2}}{\sigma_{2,1}}\right)^{-1}\frac{z_{13}}{z_{12}}\bar{z}_{34}\right)\end{split}

(219)

In (
217
) we have again set the Yang-Mills coupling
g=2
. Now let us also note the expression of the
3
-point Mellin amplitude in terms of which the
4
-point Mellin amplitude factorises in the
(3^{+},4^{-})
OPE channel. This can be easily derived and is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-x})&=\left\langle\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},-}(1)\mathcal{O}^{a_{2}}_{\Delta_{2},+}(2)\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-1,-}(4)\right\rangle\\
&=f^{a_{1}a_{2}x}\hskip 1.13791pt\mathcal{N}\hskip 1.13791pt\delta(\bar{z}_{14})\delta(\bar{z}_{24})\ \prod_{i=1}^{3}\Theta(\epsilon_{i}(\sigma_{i,1}))\end{split}

(220)

with
\mathcal{N}
defined in (
218
).

C.2
OPE decomposition of
4
-point Mellin amplitude

We can now extract the mixed helicity celestial OPE in the
(3^{+},4^{-})
channel by setting
u_{34}=0
in (
217
) and expanding the integrand
\mathcal{I}(t)
around
z_{34}=0,\bar{z}_{34}=0
. As before, in order to avoid irrelevant contact terms we will simply set
z_{34}=0
inside the argument of the theta functions in the Mellin integral in (
217
).

C.2.1
Leading term

The leading term in (
217
) as
z_{34}\rightarrow 0
is

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},3^{+a_{3}},4^{-a_{4}})&\approx-i\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{N}\int_{0}^{1}dt\hskip 2.84544ptt^{i\lambda_{3}-1}(1-t)^{i\lambda_{4}+1}\hskip 1.42271ptf^{a_{1}a_{2}x}f^{xa_{3}a_{4}}\hskip 1.13791pt\frac{\delta(\bar{z}_{14})\delta(\bar{z}_{24})}{z_{34}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{3}\Theta(\epsilon_{i}(\sigma_{i,1}))\\
&=-\frac{if^{a_{3}a_{4}x}}{z_{34}}\hskip 1.42271ptB(\Delta_{3}-1,\Delta_{4}+1)\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-x})\end{split}

(221)

From the above result we get the leading celestial OPE to be

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}^{a_{3}}_{\Delta_{3},+}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\mathcal{O}^{a_{4}}_{\Delta_{4},-}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})&\sim-\frac{if^{a_{3}a_{4}x}}{z_{34}}\hskip 1.42271ptB(\Delta_{3}-1,\Delta_{4}+1)\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-1,-}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})\end{split}

(222)

This agrees with the result obtained in
Pate:2019lpp
;
Fan:2019emx
.

C.2.2
Subleading terms:
\mathcal{O}(z_{34}^{0}\bar{z}_{34}^{0})

Using (
219
), we find the
\mathcal{O}(z_{34}^{0}\bar{z}_{34}^{0})
term from the
4
-pt Mellin amplitude to be

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},3^{+a_{3}},4^{-a_{4}})\Big|_{\mathcal{O}(z_{34}^{0}\bar{z}_{34}^{0})}\\
&=-i\hskip 1.42271ptB(\Delta_{3}-1,\Delta_{4}+1)\bigg[\frac{i\lambda_{3}}{(i\lambda_{3}+i\lambda_{4}+2)}f^{a_{1}a_{2}x}f^{{x}a_{3}a_{4}}\bigg(-\frac{i\lambda_{1}}{z_{24}}-\frac{(i\lambda_{2}-2)}{z_{14}}+\frac{i\Lambda}{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\hskip 1.42271pt\frac{u_{12}}{z_{12}}\bigg)\\
&+\left(-\frac{f^{a_{1}a_{4}x}f^{{x}a_{2}a_{3}}}{z_{24}}+\frac{f^{a_{1}a_{3}x}f^{{x}a_{2}a_{4}}}{z_{14}}\right)\bigg]\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})\end{split}

(223)

where

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})=\mathcal{N}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta(\bar{z}_{14})\delta(\bar{z}_{24})\ \prod_{i=1}^{3}\Theta(\epsilon_{i}(\sigma_{i,1}))\end{split}

(224)

is the modified Mellin transform of the color-stripped
3
-point amplitude
A(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})
.

Now the R.H.S. of (
223
) can be expressed in terms of a linear combination of
3
-point Mellin amplitudes which involve descendants created by the action of
J^{a}_{-1}
and
j^{a}_{-1}
respectively. In order to show this, let us first consider the correlator

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},-}(1)\mathcal{O}^{a_{2}}_{\Delta_{2},+}(2)\left(J^{a_{3}}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{a_{4}}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4},-}(4)\right)\right\rangle=\mathcal{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(225)

where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\epsilon_{i}\left(2\bar{h}_{i}-1+u_{i4}\partial_{u_{i}}+\bar{z}_{i4}\partial_{\bar{z}_{i}}\right)}{2\hskip 1.42271ptz_{i4}}\hskip 1.42271ptT^{a_{3}}_{i}P^{-1}_{i}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(226)

and

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})=i\partial_{u_{4}}\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(227)

Note that since we are using here the modified Mellin transform, the differential operator representation of
J^{a_{3}}_{-1}
explicitly includes the
u_{i}
coordinates. This is obtained from (
34
) by making the replacement

\begin{split}2\bar{h}_{i}\rightarrow 2\bar{h}_{i}+u_{i4}\partial_{u_{i}}.\end{split}

(228)

In order to evaluate (
226
), it is useful to note that since the derivatives with respect to
\bar{z}_{i}
act only on the anti-holomorphic delta functions in the
3
-point Mellin amplitude, we can use the identity

\begin{split}&\bar{z}_{i4}\hskip 1.42271pt\partial_{\bar{z}_{i}}\left(\delta(\bar{z}_{14})\delta(\bar{z}_{24})\right)=-\delta(\bar{z}_{14})\delta(\bar{z}_{24}),\quad i=1,2\end{split}

(229)

Then it can be easily shown that

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})&=-i\bigg[\bigg(i\lambda_{1}f^{a_{3}a_{1}y}f^{ya_{2}a_{4}}-(i\lambda_{2}-2)f^{a_{3}a_{2}y}f^{a_{1}ya_{4}}\bigg)\frac{z_{12}}{z_{14}z_{24}}\\
&-\frac{i\Lambda}{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\left(\frac{u_{14}}{z_{14}}f^{a_{3}a_{1}y}f^{ya_{2}a_{4}}+\frac{u_{24}}{z_{24}}f^{a_{3}a_{2}y}f^{a_{1}ya_{4}}\right)\bigg]\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})\end{split}

(230)

Now let us consider the following
3
-point correlator

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathcal{O}^{a_{1}}_{\Delta_{1},-}(1)\mathcal{O}^{a_{2}}_{\Delta_{2},+}(2)\left(j^{a_{3}}_{-1}\mathcal{O}^{a_{4}}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-1,-}(4)\right)\right\rangle=\mathscr{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(231)

Using (
21
) this is simply given by

\begin{split}&\mathscr{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})=i\left(\frac{f^{a_{3}a_{1}x}f^{{x}a_{2}a_{4}}}{z_{14}}+\frac{f^{a_{3}a_{2}x}f^{a_{1}xa_{4}}}{z_{24}}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})\end{split}

(232)

Then using (
230
) and (
232
) we get the following relation

\begin{split}&f^{a_{1}a_{2}x}f^{{x}a_{3}a_{4}}\bigg(-\frac{i\lambda_{1}}{z_{24}}-\frac{(i\lambda_{2}-2)}{z_{14}}+\frac{i\Lambda}{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\hskip 1.42271pt\frac{u_{12}}{z_{12}}\bigg)\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(1^{-},2^{+},4^{-})\\
&=i\mathcal{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})+i(2+i\lambda_{3}+i\lambda_{4})\mathscr{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(233)

Gathering the above results, (

) can finally be written as

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},3^{+a_{3}},4^{-a_{4}})\Big|_{\mathcal{O}(z_{34}^{0}\bar{z}_{34}^{0})}\\
&=-i\hskip 1.42271ptB(\Delta_{3}-1,\Delta_{4}+1)\bigg[\frac{\Delta_{3}-1}{(\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4})}\hskip 1.42271pti\mathcal{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\mathcal{P}_{-1,-1}(4)+i\hskip 1.42271pt\Delta_{3}\mathscr{J}^{a_{3}}_{-1}(4)\bigg]\mathcal{M}(1^{-a_{1}},2^{+a_{2}},4^{-a_{4}})\end{split}

(234)

Thus upto the first the subleading order, the celestial OPE between outgoing gluon primaries of opposite helicities is given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}^{a_{3}}_{\Delta_{3},+}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\mathcal{O}^{a_{4}}_{\Delta_{4},-}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})\\
&\sim-i\hskip 1.42271ptB(\Delta_{3}-1,\Delta_{4}+1)\bigg[\frac{f^{a_{3}a_{4}x}}{z_{34}}+\frac{i(\Delta_{3}-1)}{(\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4})}\hskip 1.13791pt\delta^{a_{4}x}J^{a_{3}}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+i\hskip 1.13791pt\Delta_{3}\hskip 1.42271pt\delta^{a_{4}x}\hskip 1.42271ptj^{a_{3}}_{-1}\bigg]\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-1,-}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})\end{split}

(235)

where
P_{-1,-1}\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-1,-}=\mathcal{O}^{x}_{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4},-}
. The above OPE manifestly satisfies both the leading as well as the subleading conformal soft gluon theorem.

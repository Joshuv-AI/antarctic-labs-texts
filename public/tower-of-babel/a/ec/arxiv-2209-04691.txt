Modified symmetrized integral in G G -coalgebras
Nathan Geer, Ngoc Phu HA, Bertrand Patureau-Mirand

Abstract
. For G G a commutative group, we give a purely Hopf G G -coalgebra construction of G G -colored 3 3 -manifolds invariants using the notion of modified integral.



MSC: 57M27, 17B37.

Key words: Quantum group, ribbon Hopf
G
-coalgebra, Hennings type invariant, modified symmetrized integral.

1.
Introduction

In
[
11
]
, Ohtsuki defined the structure of a ribbon colored Hopf algebra and
he reveal such a structure based on
a non-semisimple example coming from
quantum
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
He showed that a ribbon colored Hopf algebra leads to a universal link invariant taking values in some quotient of the colored Hopf algebra. In the example of quantum
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
this universal invariant recovers the ADO link invariant given in
[
1
]
.

A non commutative version of the notion of a ribbon colored Hopf algebra
called a
G
-coalgebra was introduced by Turaev
with the goal of producing Homotopy Quantum Field Theories (HQFTs),
see
[
12
]
. Virelizier showed that certain
G
-coalgebras gives rise to
universal invariants of a
G
-link (i.e. a link with a flat
connection in a principal
G
-bundle over the complement of the link).
Moreover, he shows that if the
G
-coalgebra has a
G
-trace this
universal invariant leads to a
Hennings-Virelizier invariant
of
3 dimensional
G
-manifolds (3-manifolds endowed with a flat

G
-bundle), see
[
13
]
and
[
12
, Appendix 7.2]
.
Moreover, in

[
14
, Theorem 7.4]
, Virelizier shows that a
G
-trace exists
when the
G
-coalgebra has certain conditions (for example, when it is
semisimple and finite type). Constructing non-trivial
G
-manifold
invariants in the non-semisimple and non-finite type setting seems to require new
techniques which we propose here: we
show that in a special case (when
G
is commutative and the

G
-coalgebra has certain conditions including a modified symmetrized integral)
Virelizier’s construction can be re-normalized and leads to
non-trivial
G
-manifold invariants.
In particular, we show that unrolled quantum group associated to
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
gives
rise to
G
-coalgebra with modified symmetrized
integral and so an invariant of
G
-manifolds. The link invariant underlying this example is closely related to the universal link invariant defined by Ohtsuki in
[
11
]
. This link invariant was previously used to produce a representation theoretic Reshetikhin-Turaev type 3-manifold invariants in
[
3
]
.
Here we give an Hopf
G
-coalgebra approach to make a Hennings type 3-manifold invariants.

The main new concept of this paper is the notion of a modified symmetrized integral on a
G
-coalgebra. This notion is related and inspired by the theory of modified traces and symmetrized integrals given in
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
.
We show that modified symmetrized integrals exist in the general context of Theorem
3.5
. This general context is inspired and modeled on topological unrolled quantum groups associated to simple Lie algebras, see
[
4
]
.
However, the work of
[
4
]
requires technical topological completions where this paper uses more straightforward algebraic techniques.

The outline and main results of the paper are described as follows.
In Section
2
, when
G
is commutative, we
describe
Virelizier version of the universal invariant of
G
-links and its corresponding 3-dimensional
G
-manifold invariant which we call the graded Hennings-Virelizier invariant. In this context, a
G
-manifolds
(M,\omega)
is
just the data of a 3-manifold with a cohomology class
\omega\in H^{1}(M,G)
.
In Section
3
we give the notion of a (modified) symmetrized integral and show that such an integral can be used to renormalize the graded Hennings-Virelizier invariant. In Section
4
we show that Ohtsuki’s ribbon colored Hopf algebra associated to
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
leads to an example of the invariants defined in this paper.

2.
Virelizier invariant of
G
-manifolds: the abelian case

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an integral domain.

2.1.
Ribbon Hopf
G
-coalgebra over abelian group

The notion of a Ribbon Hopf
G
-coalgebra is a tool used to produce homotopy R-matrices and examples of non commutative HQFTs.
In this section we give a simplified version of a Hopf
G
-coalgebra in
the quasi-triangular case which coincide with Ohtsuki’s notion of
colored Hopf algebra
[
11
]
. In particular the group
G
is
commutative and we use additive notation.

A Hopf
G
-coalgebra is a family
{\mathbf{H}}=\{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\}_{a\in G}
of

\mathbb{K}
-algebras (with a product
m_{a}:{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
and a unit
\eta_{a}:\mathbb{K}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
for each
a\in G
) endowed with a
comultiplication

\Delta=\{\Delta_{a,b}:{\mathbf{H}}_{a+b}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{b}\}_{a,b\in G}
, a counit
\varepsilon:{\mathbf{H}}_{0}\to\mathbb{K}
and an
antipode
S=\{S_{a}:{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{-a}\}_{a\in G}
which satisfy
(see
[
11
,
12
,
14
]
):

(1)

\Delta
is
coassociative: for any
a,b,c\in G
,

\left(\Delta_{a,b}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{c}}\right)\circ\Delta_{a+b,c}={\left(\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\otimes\Delta_{b,c}\right)}\circ\Delta_{a,b+c},

(2)

\varepsilon
is a counit:

{\left(\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\otimes\varepsilon\right)}\circ\Delta_{a,0}={\left(\varepsilon\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\right)}\circ\Delta_{0,a}=\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}
,

(3)

S
is a family of
bijective antipode:
for any
a\in G
,

m_{a}\circ{\left(S_{-a}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\right)}\circ\Delta_{-a,a}=\eta_{a}\circ\varepsilon=m_{a}\circ{\left(\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\otimes S_{-a}\right)}\circ\Delta_{a,-a},

(4)

\Delta
and
\varepsilon
are algebra maps (for the standard algebra
structure of
{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{b}
and
\mathbb{K}
).

Let us now recall some definitions from
[
14
,
8
]
.

Definition 2.1

(pivotal structure)
.

Let
{\mathbf{H}}=\{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\}_{a\in G}
be a Hopf
G
-coalgebra.

(1)

A
G
-grouplike
element is a family

\{x_{a}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\}_{a\in G}
such that

\Delta_{a,b}(x_{a+b})=x_{a}\otimes x_{b}
, for all

a,b\in G
and
\varepsilon(x_{0})=1
.

(2)

A
pivot
for
{\mathbf{H}}
is a
G
-grouplike element

\{g_{a}\}_{a\in G}
such that,
for all
a\in G
for any
x\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
,

S_{-a}S_{a}(x)=g_{a}xg_{a}^{-1}
. If

{\mathbf{H}}
has a pivot we say it is a
pivotal
Hopf

G
-coalgebra.

Definition 2.2

(quasitriangular structure)
.

Let
\tau:{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{b}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{b}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
be the linear map
defined by
x\otimes y\mapsto y\otimes x
. A Hopf
G
-coalgebra

{\mathbf{H}}=\{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\}_{a\in G}
is quasitriangular if it has an R-matrix,
which is a family of invertible elements

{\mathcal{R}}=\{{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{b}\}_{a,b\in G}
satisfying for
any
a,b,c\in G

(1)

{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}.\Delta_{a,b}(x)=\tau{\left(\Delta_{b,a}(x)\right)}.{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}

for any
x\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a+b}
,

(2)

{\left(\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}\otimes\Delta_{b,c}\right)}{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b+c}={\left({\mathcal{R}}_{a,c}\right)}_{1b3}{\left({\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}\right)}_{12c}
,

(3)

{\left(\Delta_{a,b}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{c}}\right)}{\mathcal{R}}_{a+b,c}={\left({\mathcal{R}}_{a,c}\right)}_{1b3}{\left({\mathcal{R}}_{b,c}\right)}_{a23}

where

{\left({\mathcal{R}}_{a,c}\right)}_{1b3}=r_{a}\otimes 1_{b}\otimes s_{c},\ {\left({\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}\right)}_{12c}=r_{a}\otimes s_{b}\otimes 1_{c}
and
{\left({\mathcal{R}}_{b,c}\right)}_{a23}=1_{a}\otimes r_{b}\otimes s_{c}
uses the notation
r_{a}\otimes s_{b}
(with an implied summation) for the element
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{b}
.

In
[
14
]
, Virelizier defines a ribbon Hopf
G
-coalgebra using a ribbon element (a similar style of definition is given in
[
11
]
). Here we give an equivalent definition in terms of the pivotal structure and a twist element.

Definition 2.3

(ribbon structure)
.

A pivotal quasitriangular Hopf
G
-coalgebra is
ribbon
if for any

a\in G
the element

\theta_{a}=m_{a}(\tau((g_{a}\otimes 1_{a})R_{a,a}))

satisfies

(1)

\theta_{a}=m_{a}{\left((1_{a}\otimes g_{a}^{-1})R_{a,a}\right)}.

Definition 2.4

(symmetrized integral)
.

A
symmetrized integral
for a pivotal Hopf
G
-coalgebra is a family
of linear forms
{\mu}=\{{\mu}_{a}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}^{*}\}_{a\in G}
such that

(2)

\displaystyle{\left({\mu}_{a}\otimes g_{b}\right)}\Delta_{a,b}(x)

\displaystyle={\mu}_{a+b}(x)1_{b}\text{ for }x\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a+b},

(3)

\displaystyle{\mu}_{a}(xy)

\displaystyle={\mu}_{a}(yx)\ \text{for}\ x,y\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a},

(4)

\displaystyle{\mu}_{-a}{\left(S_{a}(x)\right)}

\displaystyle={\mu}_{a}(x)\ \text{for}\ x\in{\mathbf{H}}_{a}.

A symmetrized integral is
twist non
degenerate
if the scalar

\mu_{0}(g_{0}\theta_{0})\mu_{0}(g_{0}^{-1}\theta_{0}^{-1})

is non zero.

It is known (see
[
14
]
) that if
{\mathbf{H}}
is ribbon and finite type
(i.e.
\dim({\mathbf{H}}_{a})<\infty
for all
a\in G
), then
{\mathbf{H}}
has a unique up to
a scalar symmetrized integral closely related to the right integral.
However, the finiteness is not a necessary
conditions as we shall see in Section
4
.

2.3.
Invariant of
G
-manifolds

From now on we assume
\mathbb{K}
is an algebraic closed field.
A
G
-manifold
is a pair
(M,\omega)
where
M
is an oriented closed
3
-manifold and
\omega\in H^{1}(M,G)
.
A surgery presentation of a
G
-manifold
M
is a
G
-link
L
in

S^{3}
such that
S^{3}_{L}
is homeomorphic to
M
. Note, we put an ordering on the components of
L
. Such a link is
G
-colored where the color of the
i^{th}

component of
L
is defined by the value of
\omega
on its oriented
meridian in
S^{3}\setminus L\subset M
and denoted by

.

In
[
13
]
, Virelizier proves the following theorem, we give a sketch of the proof because similar ideas will be used below when we re-normalize this invariant.
Let
{\mathbf{H}}
be a ribbon Hopf
G
-coalgebra with a twist non-degenerate symmetrized integral (we assume
\mu_{0}(g_{0}^{-1}\theta_{0}^{-1})^{-1}=\mu_{0}(g_{0}\theta_{0})=\delta
for some non zero element
\delta\in\mathbb{K}
, which is always possible, up to rescaling, since
\mathbb{K}
has square roots).

Theorem 2.9
.

For each
G
-manifold

(M,\omega)
the assignment

{\mathrm{HV}}(M,\omega)=\delta^{-s}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{a_{i}}{\left(J(L,\omega)\right)}\in\mathbb{K},

is an invariant of
G
-manifolds
where
s\in\mathbb{Z}
is the signature of the linking matrix of
L
.

Proof.

Any two presentations of
M
are
related by isotopy, orientation reversal of components of
L
and
colored Kirby I and II moves which are a stabilization with
\pm 1
framed
0
-colored unknot and sliding along a component of
L
, respectively. The colored Kirby II move is represented by

\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-3.69885pt\raisebox{-4.0pt}{\psfig{figure,height}}\end{array}\put(-38.0,4.0){\mbox{\tiny$a+b$}}\put(0.0,0.0){\mbox{\tiny$b$}}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-3.69885pt\raisebox{-4.0pt}{\psfig{figure,height}}\end{array}\put(-22.0,4.0){\mbox{\tiny$a$}}\put(2.0,-20.0){\mbox{\tiny$b$}}\put(-13.0,-18.0){\mbox{\tiny$g_{b}$}}\;\;\;.

To prove the theorem we will show
{\mathrm{HV}}
is unchanged under these operations.

Since

{\mu}\circ S={\mu}
then Proposition
2.7
implies
{\mathrm{HV}}
is invariant by orientation reversal of
a component of
L
. A Kirby I move increases the signature of
L
by
\pm 1
and also multiplies the invariant by
\mu_{0}((g_{0}\theta_{0})^{\pm 1})=\delta^{\pm 1}
. Thus,
{\mathrm{HV}}
is

Finally, invariance by a colored Kirby II move
follow from Proposition
2.8
and the first property of
the symmetrized integral. Remark that the invariance under this move only uses that the
components colored with

and
a

3.1.
Ambidexterity of the symmetrized integral

Let
{\mathbf{H}}
be a finite type pivotal Hopf
G
-coalgebra with a
symmetrized integral
{\mu}
.

For a linear endomorphism

of a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector

we denote by

the right
partial trace of

for any
basis

. We define similarly
the left partial trace of

denoted

. Remark that denoting by

the usual linear trace, we have

.

Let

be the left multiplication by

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

and

be a
morphism of

-module (i.e.

(11)

{\mu}_{a}(\operatorname{ptr}^{\mathbb{K}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{b}}{\left((\operatorname{Id}\otimes L_{g_{b}})f\right)}(1_{a}))={\mu}_{b}(\operatorname{ptr}^{\mathbb{K}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{a}}{\left((L_{g_{a}^{-1}}\otimes\operatorname{Id})f\right)}(1_{b}))

Proof.

The proof is based on the theory of modified trace: The guiding
principle is that by
[
2
,
8
]
the symmetrized integral is the
modified trace and we transpose the partial trace property of
modified trace to a property for the symmetrized integral.

In this proof we use the notation and result of

[
8
]
. Let
{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}
be the pivotal category of left
{\mathbf{H}}
-modules
(an object of
{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
equipped with a structure of left
{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
-module for some

).
For
a\in G
, the action of left multiplication on
{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
makes
{\mathbf{H}}_{a}
into a projective object of
{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}
.
A
cyclic trace
on
the subcategory of projective object

of
{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}
is a family
of linear function

and

and Definition

there is a unique cyclic trace

such that for any

.
Definition

(1) implies that

is a right symmetrized

G

,
\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}
is a right

{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}

{\operatorname{\mathscr{C}}}

{\mu}
is
also a left symmetrized
G
-integral (i.e.

({\mathbf{H}},g)

and

A surgery presentation of
M
is given by an unknot colored by

vanishes on it. Let now

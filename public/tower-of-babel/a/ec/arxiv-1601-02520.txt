Wigner Functions for the Pair Angle and Orbital Angular Momentum
H.A. Kastrup

Abstract
The problem of constructing physically and mathematically well-defined Wigner functions for the canonical pair angle θ \theta and angular momentum p p is solved. While a key element for the construction of Wigner functions for the planar phase space { ( q , p ) ∈ ℝ 2 } \{(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\} is the Heisenberg-Weyl group, the corresponding group for the cylindrical phase space { ( θ , p ) ∈ S 1 × ℝ } \{(\theta,p)\in S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\} is the Euclidean group E ⁡ ( 2 ) E(2) of the plane and its unitary representations. Here the angle θ \theta is replaced by the pair ( cos ⁡ θ , sin ⁡ θ ) (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) which corresponds uniquely to the points on the unit circle. The main structural properties of the Wigner functions for the planar and the cylindrical phase spaces are strikingly similar. A crucial role plays the sinc \sinc function which provides the interpolation for the discontinuous quantized angular momenta in terms of the continuous classical ones, in accordance with the famous Whittaker cardinal function well-known from interpolation and sampling theories. The quantum mechanical marginal distributions for angle (continuous) and angular momentum (discontinuous) are as usual uniquely obtained by appropriate integrations of the ( θ , p ) (\theta,p) Wigner function. Among the examples discussed is an elementary system of simple ”cat” states.

\mathbf{1}
the unit operator in

(or

As already mentioned in the Introduction the function

Notice the following difference as to integrations over angles above: whereas

the corresponding measure for the phase space angle variable

For expansions

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{\displaystyle-ip\vartheta}\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\theta-\vartheta/2)\,\psi_{1}(\theta+\vartheta/2)\,,

\displaystyle=(\psi_{2},V(\theta,p)\psi_{1})\,,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,V_{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}(\theta,p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\theta)\,\psi_{1}(\theta)\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}(\theta,p)=(\psi_{2},\psi_{1})\,,

(52)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V^{\ast}_{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}(\theta,p)\,V_{\phi_{2}\phi_{1}}(\theta,p)=

(53)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\psi_{1},\phi_{1})\,(\psi_{2},\phi_{2})^{\ast}\,.

III.2
Phase space functions associated with

Hilbert space
operators

If
\psi_{1}(\varphi)=A\psi(\varphi)
, where

(e.g.

\psi_{2}=\psi

For

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,a_{\psi_{2}\psi}(\theta,p)\,,

\displaystyle a_{\psi_{2}\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2}c^{(2)\ast}_{m}[V(\theta,p)\cdot A+A\cdot V(\theta,p)]_{mn}c_{n}\,,

The (Weyl) symmetrization of

For

\displaystyle A_{mn}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(e_{m},Ae_{n})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,a_{mn}(\theta,p)\,,

\displaystyle a_{mn}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[V(\theta,p)\cdot A+A\cdot V(\theta,p)]_{mn}\,,

which can formally be written as

A=(A_{mn})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{1}{2}[V(\theta,p)\cdot A+A\cdot V(\theta,p)]\,.

(59)

¿From the diagonal elements

(60)

we obtain

\displaystyle\tr A=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,a_{m}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,\tr[A\cdot V(\theta,p)]\,.

If

is a diagonal density matrix

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,\tr[\rho\cdot V(\theta,p)]=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{m}=1\,.

(64)

Using the relations (
54

\lambda_{m}

\lambda_{m}=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,\sinc\pi(p-m)\,\tr[\rho\cdot V(\theta,p)]\,.

(65)

As

where

is a density

As

the right-hand side of Eq. (

The phase space representation of the trace

\displaystyle\tr[A\cdot V(\theta,p)]=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{mn}V_{nm}(\theta,p)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{mn}e^{\displaystyle i(m-n)\theta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{\displaystyle i[(m+n)/2-p]\vartheta}\,,

with the corresponding expression for

III.3
Time evolution

Inserting the expansions (
48
) into their Schrödinger equations

i\partial_{t}\psi_{j}(t,\varphi)=H\psi_{j}(t,\varphi)

(71)

implies

i\,\dot{c}^{(j)}_{m}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}^{(j)}(t)(e_{m},He_{n})\,,

(72)

which leads to the Schrödinger time evolution

\partial_{t}V_{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}(\theta,p;t)=i(\psi_{2}(t),[H,V]\psi_{1}(t))\,.

(73)

That is, the time evolution of
V_{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}
is determined by the operator (matrix)

K(\theta,p)=i[H,V(\theta,p)],

(74)

which is Hermitian if
H
and
V
are Hermitian and for which
\tr K=0
.

A simple example is

H=\varepsilon L^{2},~~~~(e_{m},He_{n})=\varepsilon\,n^{2}\delta_{mn},

(75)

which implies the matrix

(K_{mn}(\theta,p)),~K_{mn}=(e_{m},Ke_{n})=i\varepsilon(m^{2}-n^{2})V_{mn}(\theta,p).

(76)

The evolution (
73
) holds, of course, also for the Wigner function proper

V_{\psi}(\theta,p;t)
for which
\psi_{2}=\psi_{1}=\psi
in Eq. (
49
) and which is discussed in the next section.

There it will also be discussed that the Wigner function is given by

V_{\rho}(\theta,p;t)=\tr[\rho(t)\cdot V(\theta,p)],

(77)

if a state is not characterized by a single wave function
\psi(t)
but by
a density matrix
\rho(t)
which obeys the von Neumann equation
(we are still in the Schrödinger picture !)

\partial_{t}\rho(t)=-i[H,\rho(t)]\,.

(78)

Inserting this into

\partial_{t}V_{\rho}(\theta,p;t)=\tr[\partial_{t}\rho(t)\cdot V(\theta,p)]

(79)

yields

\partial_{t}V_{\rho}(\theta,p;t)=\tr[\rho\cdot K(\theta,p)]\,,

(80)

where
K(\theta,p)
is defined in Eq. (
74
).

IV.1
General properties

If
\psi_{2}=\psi_{1}=\psi
in Eq. (

\displaystyle V_{\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{\displaystyle-ip\vartheta}\psi^{\ast}(\theta-\vartheta/2)\,\psi(\theta+\vartheta/2)

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}^{\ast}V_{mn}(\theta,p)c_{n}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\psi,V(\theta,p)\psi)

) - (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,V_{\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}|\psi(\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}|^{2}\sinc\pi(p-n)

\displaystyle\omega_{\psi}(p)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,\omega_{\psi}(p)\,\sinc\pi(p-m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c_{m}|^{2}\,,

(84)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}|^{2}=1\,,

(85)

|V_{\psi}(\theta,p)|\leq 1/\pi\,

holds. It follows from Schwarz’s inequality as follows: the expression (

\displaystyle V_{\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})\,,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\theta,p;\vartheta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-ip\vartheta/2}\psi(\theta+\vartheta/2)\,,

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\theta,p;\vartheta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{ip\vartheta/2}\psi(\theta-\vartheta/2)\,.

Because

|(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})|^{2}\leq(\chi_{2},\chi_{2})\,(\chi_{1},\chi_{1})

(89)

and

\displaystyle(\chi_{1},\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\vartheta}{2\pi}|\psi(\vartheta/2+\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\beta}{2\pi}|\psi(\beta)|^{2}

\displaystyle 2(\psi,\psi)=2,

with the same for
(\chi_{2},\chi_{2})
,
the inequality (

Eq. (

\psi

\rho

First we rewrite
the scalar product
(
81

P_{\psi}

\psi

As

is a special density matrix the generalization of
V_{\psi}
to a mixed

V_{\rho}(\theta,p)=\tr[\rho\cdot V(\theta,p)]\,,

a quantity we know already from the last Section.

Again using the relations (
36

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,V_{\rho}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{\rho}(\theta,p)

(95)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{\rho}(\theta,p)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{mm}=\tr(\rho)=1\,,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{\rho_{2}}(\theta,p)\,V_{\rho_{1}}(\theta,p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\tr(\rho_{2}\cdot\rho_{1})\,.

(98)

IV.2
Marginal densities

For the Wigner function
W_{\psi}(q,p)
on the
classical
phase space
\{(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}

one has the marginal
quantum mechanical
distributions

\displaystyle\int dp\,W_{\psi}(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\psi(q)|^{2},

(100)

\displaystyle\int dq\,W_{\psi}(q,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\hat{\psi}(p)|^{2}

(101)

for
q
and
p
separately. The properties (
100
) and (
101
) constitute one
of the main requirements the Wigner function
W_{\psi}(q,p)
should fulfil
Hillery et al. 1984
;
Leonhardt 1997
;
Schleich 2001
;
Römer 2009
;
Agarwal 2013
;
Folland 1989
;
de Gosson 2006
.

At first sight those properties do hold in our case only for the
marginal density
|\psi(\theta)|^{2}
in Eq. (
82
). However, the situation here is only slightly
more complicated, but in principle as straightforward as in the
(q,p)
case:
The quantum mechanical marginal probabilities for the quantized angular momentum are the discontinuous
numbers

b_{m}=|c_{m}|^{2}\,,~m\in\mathbb{Z},

(102)

whereas the
\theta
-integration in Eq. (
83
) yields ”only” the function
\omega_{\psi}(p)
.

But Eq. (
84
) shows how the marginal probabilities (
102
) can be extracted uniquely from
\omega_{\psi}(p)
.

The density
\omega_{\psi}(p)
of Eq. (
83
) is an example of Whittaker’s famous
cardinal
function

C(f,h)(x)
associated with a function
f(x)
the values of which are known only for a discrete subset of the otherwise continuous arguments
x

Whittaker 1915
,
an important case well-known
from interpolation, sampling and signal processing theories
(see the reviews
McNamee et al. 1971
;
Stenger 1981
;
Butzer 1983
;
Higgins 1985
;
Stenger 1993
;
Stenger 2011
;
Vetterli et al. 2014
;
Eldar 2015
):

C(f,h)(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n\,h)\,\sinc[\pi(x-nh)/h]\,,

(103)

where
h
is the step size.

In our case we have

\displaystyle C(g,h=1)(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}[g(p=m)=b_{m}]\sinc\pi(m-p)

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\psi}(p)

for a possible function
g(p),p\in\mathbb{R},
which now interpolates the different discrete values
|c_{m}|^{2}
,
i.e.
g(p=m)=|c_{m}|^{2}
.
The interval
h
between adjacent supporting points here has the value
h=1
.
If the Fourier transform
\hat{f}(u)
of
f(x)
is “band-limited”, i.e.
\hat{f}(u)
vanishes outside the interval
u\in[-\pi/h,\pi/h]
(Palais-Wiener case)
then one even has
C(f,h)(x)=f(x)
(Whittaker-Shannon sampling theorem, mathematically proved by Hardy
Hardy 1941
).

The functions
\sinc\pi(p-m),m\in\mathbb{Z}
, of Eq. (
54
) form a complete orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space of such functions
g(p)
on
the real line
\mathbb{R}
(see also Ref.
Christensen 2008
).

Here then is the complete set of marginal densities from above (Eqs. (
82
), (
84
), (
94
) and (
99
)),
for a wave function
\psi(\varphi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\,e_{n}(\varphi)
or a density matrix

\rho=(\rho_{mn})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}|\psi(\theta)|^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,V_{\psi}(\theta,p),

(105)

\displaystyle|c_{n}|^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,\sinc\pi(p-n)V_{\psi}(\theta,p),

(106)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{mn}e_{m}(\theta)e_{n}^{\ast}(\theta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,V_{\rho}(\theta,p),

(107)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\rho_{mn}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\,V_{mn}(\theta,p)\,V_{\rho}(\theta,p).

(108)

\hbar\,\partial_{t}
.

remarks

VI.1
Dirac notation

Throughout the whole text above I have avoided the use of the widespread Dirac
notion of ”bra”, ”ket” etc. for describing quantum mechanical states and
operators. The reason being (see, e.g. Ref.
Kastrup 2006
) that
there is no mathematically well-defined angle
operator
\hat{\varphi}
with eigenfunctions
|\varphi\rangle
such that

\hat{\varphi}|\varphi\rangle=\varphi\,|\varphi\rangle\,.

(185)

The use of such mathematically non-existent objects might be helpful heuristically
if one is appropriately careful: Here the formal objects
|\varphi\rangle
only make
sense in the combination

\langle\varphi|n\rangle=e^{\displaystyle in\varphi}=e_{n}(\varphi)\,,~~L|n\rangle=n|n\rangle\,,

(186)

where
L
is the well-defined angular momentum operator.
Armed with this mental reservation one may write for the operator
V
of Eq. (
34
)

\tilde{V}(\theta,p)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{\displaystyle-ip\vartheta}|\theta-\vartheta/2\rangle\langle\theta+\vartheta/2|\,,

(187)

the matrix elements
\tilde{V}_{mn}=\langle m|\tilde{V}|n\rangle
of which are the same as those in Eq. (
34
).

The Wigner function in Eq. (
81
) may be written as

V_{\psi}(\theta,p)=\tilde{V}_{\psi}(\theta,p)=\langle\psi|\tilde{V}(\theta,p)|\psi\rangle\,,

(188)

where
|\psi\rangle
is to be considered as an expansion in terms of the
eigenstates
|n\rangle
.

Another interesting example is (see Section III.B.)

\displaystyle\tr[V(\theta,p)\cdot A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr[\tilde{V}(\theta,p)\cdot A]

(189)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{\displaystyle-ip\vartheta}\langle\theta+\vartheta/2|A|\theta-\vartheta/2\rangle\,.

Inserting the completeness relations
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|m\rangle\langle m|=\mathbf{1}
and

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n\rangle\langle n|=\mathbf{1}

before and after the operator
A
in the last expression leads back to the
results of Section III.B.

VI.2
A possible generalisation and some related work

The approach discussed above for phase spaces of the topological type
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
can
be generalized, e.g. to a free rigid body in 3 dimensions with one point fixed. Its configuration space
can be identified with the group
SO(3)

chi
;
Marsden and Ratiu 2003
. Its 2-fold covering
SU(2)
has the topology of
S^{3}
.
The associated phase space
S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be quantized in terms of unitary representations
of the Euclidean group
E(4)

ish
.

At a late stage of the present investigations I became aware of the work by Leaf
Leaf 1968a
;
Leaf 1968b
in which the operator

\Delta(q,p)
plays a role with respect to the
(q,p)
Wigner function which corresponds to that of the operator/matrix

V(\theta,p)
in the discussions above. Leaf’s approach was used by de Groot and Suttorp in their textbook
de Groot and Suttorp 1972
.

A Wigner-Moyal function on the circle very similar in structure to the one in Eq. (
49
) above can be found
in Refs.
Alonso et al. 2003
;
Mukunda et al. 2005
. There, however, the classical angular momentum is treated as discontinuous, contrary to its properties
in its classical phase space and its treatment in the present paper!

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit disk and
\mathbf{T}
the unit circle in the complex plane.
For
p>1
, we define the Hardy class
\mathbf{h}^{p}
as the class of harmonic mappings
f=g+\bar{h}
, where
g
and
h
are holomorphic mappings defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D},
so that

\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{\mathbf{h}^{p}}=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(f,r)<\infty,

where

M_{p}(f,r)=\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}d\sigma(\zeta)\right)^{1/p}.

Here
d\sigma(\zeta)=\frac{dt}{2\pi},
if
\zeta=e^{it}\in\mathbf{T}
. The subclass of holomorphic mappings that belongs to the class
\mathbf{h}^{p}
is denoted by
H^{p}
.

If
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
, then it is well-known that there exists

f(e^{it})=\lim_{r\to 1}f(re^{it}),a.e.

and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T}).

Then there hold

(1.1)

\|f\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{h}^{p}}}=\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Similarly, we define the
Hardy space

\mathbf{h}^{p}
of harmonic functions in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathbb{S}=\partial\mathbb{B}
. Then we say a harmonic function
u
in
\mathbb{B}
belongs to the Hardy space
\mathbf{h}^{p}
if

\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{\mathbf{h}^{p}}=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(f,r)<\infty,

where

M_{p}(f,r)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}d\sigma(\zeta)\right)^{1/p}.

Here
d\sigma
is the surface
n-1
dimensional
measure of the Euclidean sphere which satisfies the condition:

\sigma(\mathbb{S})=1
. The Hardy space of harmonic function in the space has similar properties as in the plane (see e.g.
[
1
]
).

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\overline{p}=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}
. Verbitsky in
[
21
]
proved the following results. If
f=u+iv\in H^{p}
and
v(0)=0
, then

(1.2)

\sec(\pi/(2\overline{p}))\|v\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p},

and

(1.3)

\|f\|_{p}\leq\csc(\pi/(2\overline{p}))\|u\|_{p},

and both estimates are sharp. Those results improve the sharp inequality

(1.4)

\|v\|_{p}\leq\cot(\pi/(2\overline{p}))\|u\|_{p}

found by S. Pichorides (
[
20
]
). For some related results see
[
5
,
7
,
11
,
9
]
.

Then those results have been extended by the author in
[
12
]
. As a byproduct, the author proved a Hollenbeck-Verbitsky conjecture for the case
s=2
.

Further, those results have been extended by Marković and Melentijević in
[
16
]
and Melentijević in
[
14
]
. Melentijević proved a Hollenbeck-Verbitsky conjecture for the case
s<\sec^{2}(\pi/(2p))
,
p\leq 4/3
or
p\geqslant 2
.

Recently Liu and Zhu in
[
15
]
generalized Riesz conjugate functions theorem for
planar harmonic
K
-quasiregular mappings (when
1<p\leq 2
) provided that the real part does not vanish at the unit disk. Their result is asymptotically sharp when
K\to 1
. Moreover, they extended this result for invariant harmonic quasiconformal mappings in the unit ball also assuming that the first coordinate is non-vanishing.

In this paper, we will remove the assumption that the real part of the mapping does not vanish. Moreover, our approach works for harmonic quasiregular mappings in the space.

1.1.
Quasiregular and harmonic mappings

A continuous and nonconstant mapping
f:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
, in the local Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{loc}(G,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is

K
-quasiregular,
K\geqslant 1
, if

|Df(x)|\leq K\ell(f^{\prime}(x))

for almost
every
x\in G
, where
G
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
Df(x)
is the formal differential matrix and

|Df(x)|=\sup_{|h|=1}|f^{\prime}(x)h|,\ \ \ell(Df(x))=\inf_{|h|=1}|f^{\prime}(x)h|.

Let
(Df(x))^{t}
be the transpose of the matrix
Df(x)
. Then we define the Hilbert norm of
Df(x)

by the formula

\|Df(x)\|=\sqrt{\mathrm{trace}\left(Df^{t}Df\right)}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}},

where
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{n}
are eigenvalues of the matrix
Df^{t}Df
.

Observe that

|Df^{t}(x)|=|Df(x)|,\ \ \ell(Df(x))=\ell(Df^{t}(x)).

Further if
f
is
K
-quasiregular, then

\nabla|f(x)|=\nabla\sqrt{\left<f(x),f(x)\right>}=Df^{t}(x)\frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|}.

Thus

(1.5)

|\nabla|f(x)||\geqslant\ell(Df(x))\geqslant\frac{|Df(x)|}{K}.

If
n=2
, then we use the notation
k-
quasiconformal mapping for
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
.

A smooth mapping
w:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called harmonic if it satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta u=0
.
The solution of the equation
\Delta w=g
(in the sense of
distributions see
[
10
]
) in the ball
B_{R}=R\cdot\mathbb{B}
, satisfying the
boundary condition
w|_{S_{R}}=f\in L^{1}(S_{R})
, where
S_{R}=R\cdot\mathbb{S}
is given by

(1.6)

w(x)=\int_{S_{R}}P(x,\eta)f(\eta)d\sigma(\eta)-\int_{B_{R}}G(x,y)g(y)dV(y),\,|x|<1.

Here

(1.7)

P(x,\eta)=\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{R|x-\eta|^{n}}

is the Poisson kernel and
d\sigma
is the surface
n-1
dimensional
measure of the Euclidean sphere which satisfies the condition:

\int_{\mathbb{S}}d\sigma(\eta)\equiv 1
. The first integral
in (
1.6
) is called the Poisson integral and is usually
denoted by
P[f](x)
. It is a harmonic mapping. The function

(1.8)

G(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{R|x-y|}{|R^{2}-x\overline{y}|},&\hbox{for $n=2$;}\\
c_{n}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{(R^{2}+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}/R^{2}-2\left<x,y\right>)^{(n-2)/2}}\right),&\hbox{for $n\geqslant 3$,}\end{array}\right.

where

(1.9)

c_{n}=\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}

and

\omega_{n-1}
is the measure of
\mathbb{S}
, is the Green function of
the ball
B_{R}
. The Poisson kernel and the Green function are
harmonic in
x
.

2.
Main results

The main result of this paper are following theorems

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

c_{n}^{p}(K,p)=\frac{(1+(n-1)K^{2})(1+\frac{(p-2)}{nK^{2}})}{(p-1)}.

a)

Assume that
f
is a
K-
quasiregular harmonic mapping in the unit disk so that
\Im f(0)=0
. Assume that
\Re f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
for some
p\in(1,2]
. Then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
and we have the inequality

\|f\|_{p}\leq c_{2}(K,p)\|\Re f\|_{p}.

b)

Assume that
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
is a
K-
quasiregular harmonic mapping of the unit ball into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
f_{1}\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
for some
p\in(1,2]
. Then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
and we have the inequality

\|f\|^{p}_{p}\leq|f(0)|^{p}+c_{n}(K,p)(\|f_{1}\|^{p}_{p}-|f_{1}(0)|^{p}).

The constant
C_{k}(K,p)
is asymptotically sharp when
p\to 2
.

The following theorem is an improvement of the main result of Liu and Zhu
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

c(p,K)=\left(\frac{\tan^{p-1}\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\cot(\frac{\pi}{2p})}+(K^{2}-1)\frac{\sin^{p-1}\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2p}}\right)^{1/p},

and

d(p,K)=\left(\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right]^{-p}+(K^{2}-1)\tan\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right]\right)^{1/p}.

a)

Assume that
f
is a
K-
quasiregular harmonic mapping in the unit disk so that
\arg(f(0))\in(-\pi/(2p),\pi/(2p))
. Assume that
\Re f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
for some
p\in(1,2]
and that
f(\mathbb{D})\cap(-\infty,0)=\emptyset
. Then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
and we have the inequality

\|\Im f\|_{p}\leq c(K,p)\|\Re f\|_{p}.

The constant
c(K,p)
is asymptotically sharp.

b)

Under condition of a) we have the asymptotically sharp inequality

\|f\|_{p}\leq d(K,p)\|\Re f\|_{p}.

We also have the following Kolmogorov type theorem

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
f=u+iv
is harmonic
K-
quasiregular mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
so that
u>0
and let
0<p,r<1
and that
v(0)=0
. Then
u\in h^{1}
and
v\in h^{p}
and

(2.1)

M^{p}_{p}(r,v)\leq\sec\frac{\pi p}{2}(K^{2}M^{p}_{1}(r,u)-(K^{2}-1)M^{p}_{p}(r,u))

and

(2.2)

(2-K^{2})M^{p}_{1}(r,u)\leq(2-K^{2})M^{p}_{p}(r,u)+\cos(p\pi/2)M^{p}_{p}(r,v).

The constants in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are asymptotically sharp when
K\to 1
.

Remark 2.4
.

Astala and Koskela
proved in
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
that if
f
is quasiconformal in
\mathbb{B}
with one of its coordinate
functions belongs to
\mathbf{h}^{p}
, then
f\in h^{q}
for all
q<p
. Furthermore, they showed that
q
can not reach
p
by giving a concrete example. Here we have a better outcome, but under essentially stronger conditions. The proofs of those theorems are similar to the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
, and this method has been also used by Liu and Zhu in
[
15
]
. Theorem
2.2
improves the main result in
[
15
]
, because we do not assume that
u
is positive and get the same outcome.

Theorem
2.3
is Kolmogorov theorem for quasiregular harmonic mappings. We want to point out that it seems that inequality (
2.2
) is new also for holomorphic functions.

Proof of main results

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We will assume that the mapping
f
has a smooth extension to the boundary. If not, then we take the dilatation
F(x)=f(rx)
, with
r<1
and let
r\to 1
.

The case
n=2
.
Since

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(e^{it})|^{p}dt,

by using (
1.6
) to the function
w(z)=|f(z)|^{p}
we get

|f(0)|^{p}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\frac{1}{|w|}\Delta|f(w)|^{p}dudv.

Let
u=\Re f
. Let
dA(z)=dxdy
. Then by
[
19
, Theorem 1.B]

(3.1)

\|u\|_{p}^{p}=|u(0)|^{p}+\frac{p(p-1)}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|u|^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|z|}dA(z).

Let
v(0)=0.

We first have

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=|f(0)|^{p}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta|f(z)|^{p}\log\frac{1}{|z|}dA(z)

Since

\Delta|f|^{p}=\frac{p(p-2)}{4}|f|^{p-4}\|\nabla|f|^{2}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}|f|^{p-2}\Delta|f|^{2}

and

\frac{p}{2}|f|^{p-2}\Delta|f|^{2}=2p|f|^{p-2}(|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}+|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}),

and because
f
is
K-
quasiregular, by (
1.5
) we have

|\nabla|f|^{2}|^{2}=4|f|^{2}|\nabla|f||^{2}\geqslant 4|f|^{2}\|Df\|^{2}K^{-2}.

So

\begin{split}\Delta|f|^{p}&\leq\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)|f|^{p-2}(|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}+|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2})\\
&\leq\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)|u|^{p-2}(|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}+|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2})\\
&\leq\frac{1+K^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)|u|^{p-2}|g^{\prime}(z)+h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}.\end{split}

For
p\in(1,2)
and
v(0)=0
since
|\nabla u|^{2}=|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|^{2}
, by the previous estimate we obtain that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{p}^{p}&\leq|f(0)|^{p}+\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{p-2}(|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}+|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2})\log\frac{1}{|z|}\frac{dA(z)}{2\pi}\\
&\leq|f(0)|^{p}+\frac{1+K^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|u|^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|z|}\frac{dA(z)}{2\pi}\\
&=|f(0)|^{p}+\frac{1+K^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{p(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2p\right)\frac{1}{p(p-1)}(\|u\|_{p}^{p}-|u(0)|^{p})\\
&\leq\left(\frac{(p-2)}{K^{2}}+2\right)\frac{1+K^{2}}{2(p-1)}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.\end{split}

The case
n>2
.
This proof is similar to the proof of the case
n=2
. The only difference appears due to the different Green functions.
Assume that
u>0
and assume that
\omega_{n}
is the
n-1
dimensional area of
\mathbb{S}
and let
c_{n}=1/(\omega_{n}(n-2))
.
Then by (
1.6
) we have

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=|f(0)|^{p}+c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\Delta|f(x)|^{p}\left(|x|^{2-n}-1\right)dV(x).

Further, we have (see e.g.
[
13
]
)

\begin{split}\Delta|f|^{p}&=\frac{p(p-2)}{4}|f|^{p-4}\|\nabla|f|^{2}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}|f|^{p-2}\Delta|f|^{2}\\
&=\frac{p(p-2)}{4}|f|^{p-4}\|\nabla|f|^{2}\|^{2}+p|f|^{p-2}\|Df\|^{2}.\end{split}

Since

|Df|^{2}=\max\{{\lambda_{k}},k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{n}\|Df\|^{2},

where
\lambda_{k}
are the eigenvalues of
(Df)^{\ast}Df
, in view of (
1.5
), we get that

\|\nabla|f|^{2}\|=4|f|^{2}\|\nabla|f|\|^{2}\geqslant 4|f|^{2}\frac{|Df|^{2}}{K^{2}}\geqslant 4|f|^{2}\frac{\|Df\|^{2}}{nK^{2}}.

Thus

\Delta|f|^{p}\leq(p+\frac{p(p-2)}{nK^{2}})|f|^{p-2}\|Df\|^{2}.

On the other let
u=f_{1}
. Then we have

\Delta|u|^{p}=p(p-1)|\nabla u|^{2}|u|^{p-2}

Thus

\Delta|f|^{p}\leq\frac{(1+(n-1)K^{2})(1+\frac{(p-2)}{nK^{2}})}{(p-1)}\Delta u^{p}.

Now we use
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
which states the following

\|u\|_{p}^{p}=|u(0)|^{p}+\frac{p(p-1)}{n(n-2)}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|u(x)|^{p-2}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}(|x|^{2-n}-1)dV(x).

Then for

C(K,p)=\frac{(1+(n-1)K^{2})(1+\frac{(p-2)}{nK^{2}})}{(p-1)}

\begin{split}\|f\|_{p}^{p}&\leq|f(0)|^{p}+c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\Delta|f|^{p}\left(|x|^{2-n}-1\right)dV(x)\\
&\leq|f(0)|^{p}+C(K,p)c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta|u|^{p}\left(|x|^{2-n}-1\right)dV(x)\\
&\leq|f(0)|^{p}+C(K,p)\left(\|u\|_{p}^{p}-|u(0)|^{p}\right).\end{split}

To prove the last statement of the theorem, choose
I(x)=x
and consider first the case
K=1
. Then the equality holds in
\|I\|_{2}=C_{2}(1,2)\|I_{1}\|_{2}
, where
I_{1}(x)=x_{1}
. Here
C_{2}(1,2)=\sqrt{n}
.
In order to see this observe that

\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}x_{1}^{2}d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}x_{2}^{2}d\sigma(x)=\dots=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}x_{n}^{2}d\sigma(x).

So summing altogether we get

\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}x_{1}^{2}d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{n}.

In a similar way we prove that
f(x)=(x_{1},Kx_{2},\dots,Kx_{n})
is
K-
quasiconformal harmonic and we have

\frac{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}{\|f_{1}\|_{2}^{2}}=C(K,2)=(1+(n-1)K^{2}).

3.1.
Power of a quasiregular function

Observe that as in Remark
2.4
,
\Re(f^{p}(z))=|f(z)|^{p}\cos(p\arg(f(z)))
is smooth in
\mathbb{D}
, because
\arg:\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]\to(-\pi,\pi)
is smooth. Then after straightforward calculation, we get

\Delta(f^{p})=p(p-1)f^{p-2}(f_{x}^{2}+f_{y}^{2}).

Then

\Delta\Re(f^{p})=p(p-1)\Re(f^{p-2}(f_{x}^{2}+f_{y}^{2})).

Now we have

f_{x}=g^{\prime}+\overline{h^{\prime}}

and

f_{y}=i(g^{\prime}-\overline{h^{\prime}}).

So

f_{x}^{2}+f_{y}^{2}=4g^{\prime}\overline{h^{\prime}}.

Thus for
R=|f|
,

|\Delta\Re(f^{p})|\leq p(p-1)R^{p-2}(|f_{x}^{2}+f_{y}^{2}|=4p(p-1)R^{p-2}|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|.

Therefore

(3.2)

|\Delta\Re(f^{p})|\leq 4p(p-1)R^{p-2}|g^{\prime}\cdot h^{\prime}|\leq 4kp(p-1)R^{p-2}|g^{\prime}|^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

As in Theorem
2.1
, we will assume that the mapping
f
has a smooth extension to the boundary. If not, then we take the dilatation
F(z)=f(rz)
, with
r<1
and let
r\to 1
.

a)
For
p\in[1,2]
by
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have for
p\in[1,2]
and
|x|\leq\pi
,

(3.3)

|\sin x|^{p}\leq A(p)|\cos x|^{p}-B(p)\cos(px),

where

A(p)=\frac{\tan^{p-1}\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\cot(\frac{\pi}{2p})},\ \ B(p)=\frac{\sin^{p-1}\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2p}}.

Notice that the corresponding inequality in
[
20
]
is formulated only for
|x|\leq\pi/2
, but the inequality (

) continues to hold for
|x|\leq\pi
. Indeed, just observe that if

, then

and (

) hold for

instead of

. But we also have

for

. This proves the claimed inequality.

Thus

|v(re^{it})|^{p}\leq A(p)|u(re^{it})|^{p}-B(p)\Re(f^{p}(re^{it})).

In particular

(3.4)

\|v\|_{p}^{p}\leq A(p)\|u\|_{p}^{p}-B(p)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}(e^{it}))\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Further, because
\Re f^{p}
is smooth,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}((e^{it})))\frac{dt}{2\pi}=\Re(f^{p}(0))+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta\Re f^{p}(w)\log\frac{1}{|w|}dudv,

and

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|u(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}=|u(0)|^{p}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta|u^{p}(w)|\log\frac{1}{|w|}dudv.

Further, because
u=\Re(g+h)
, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}\Delta|u|^{p}&=p(p-1)|\nabla u|^{2}|u|^{p-2}\\
&=p(p-1)|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|^{2}|u|^{p-2}\\
&\geqslant p(p-1)(1-k)^{2}|g^{\prime}|^{2}|u|^{p-2}.\end{split}

Then (
3.7
) and (

) imply that

|\Delta\Re(f^{p})|\leq\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\Delta|u|^{p}.

Thus

\begin{split}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}(e^{it}))\frac{dt}{2\pi}&=\Re(f^{p}(0))+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta\Re f^{p}(w)\log\frac{1}{|w|}dudv,\\
&\geqslant\Re(f^{p}(0))-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\Delta\Re f^{p}(w)|\log\frac{1}{|w|}dudv\\
\ &\geqslant\Re(f^{p}(0))-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Delta|u^{p}(w)|\log\frac{1}{|w|}dudv\\
&=\Re(f^{p}(0))-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(-|u(0)|^{p}+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|u(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)\\
&=\Re(f^{p}(0))+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}|u(0)|^{p}-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|u(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}.\end{split}

Now if
\theta=\arg(f(0))\in(-\pi/(2p),\pi/(2p)
then

\Re(f^{p}(0))+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}|u(0)|^{p}\geqslant|f(0)|^{p}\cos(\theta p)\geqslant 0.

Thus

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}((e^{it})))\frac{dt}{2\pi}\geqslant-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|u(e^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Hence

\begin{split}\|v\|_{p}^{p}&\leq A(p)\|u\|_{p}^{p}-B(p)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}((e^{it})))\frac{dt}{2\pi}\\
&\leq A(p)\|u\|_{p}^{p}+B(p)\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\\
&=\left(A(p)+(K^{2}-1)B(p)\right)\|u\|_{p}^{p}.\end{split}

b) In this case we use the following inequality (
[
21
]
): for every
t\in[-\pi,\pi]
we have

-1+\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right]^{-p}|\cos t|^{p}-\cos(pt)\tan\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right]\geqslant 0.

Then the proof is very similar to the previous proof, but instead of (
3.4
)
we use the inequality

(3.6)

\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq C(p)\|u\|_{p}^{p}-D(p)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Re(f^{p}((e^{it})))\frac{dt}{2\pi},

where

C(p)=\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right]^{-p}\text{ and }D(p)=\tan\left[\frac{\pi}{2p}\right].

∎

Remark 3.1
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
also works under more general condition

\cos(p\theta)+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}|\cos\theta|^{p}\geqslant 0,

where
\theta=\arg(f(0))\in(-\pi,\pi]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Notice that in this case (for positive
u
) the means

M_{p}(r,u)=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)^{1/p}

are decreasing (
[
17
, Sec. 3.4]
) and thus
\|u\|_{p}=u(0)
for every
0<p<1
.
As in proof of Theorem
2.2
we have

(3.7)

|\Delta\Re(f^{p})|\leq 4p(1-p)r^{p-2}|g^{\prime}\cdot h^{\prime}|\leq 4kp(1-p)r^{p-2}|g^{\prime}|^{2}.

(3.8)

\begin{split}\Delta|u|^{p}&=p(p-1)|\nabla u|^{2}|u|^{p-2}\\
&=p(1-p)|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|^{2}|u|^{p-2}.\end{split}

Hence

-\Delta|u|^{p}\geqslant p(1-p)(1-k)^{2}|g^{\prime}|^{2}|u|^{p-2}.

Thus

|\Delta\Re(f^{p})|\leq-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\Delta|u|^{p}.

Now by (
1.6
)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}=u^{p}(0)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta u^{p}\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy,

where
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{rz,|z|<1\}
.
By using again (
1.6
)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}=\Re f^{p}(0)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta\Re f^{p}\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy.

Thus

(3.9)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}&\geqslant\Re f^{p}(0)+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta|u|^{p}\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy\\
&=\Re f^{p}(0)+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}-u^{p}(0)\right)\\
&=u^{p}(0)+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}-u^{p}(0)\right)\end{split}

and

(3.10)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}&\leq\Re f^{p}(0)-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta|u|^{p}\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy\\
&=\Re f^{p}(0)-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}-u^{p}(0)\right)\\
&=(u(0))^{p}-\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}-u^{p}(0)\right),\end{split}

because
v(0)=0
.
Since
u
is harmonic, by mean value property we have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)^{p}+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(u^{p}(0)-\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right).

Further by
[
20
, Eq. 22]
,

\cos px\geqslant\cos(p/2\pi)|\sin x|^{p}

and thus for
f(re^{it})=Re^{ix}
,

\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\geqslant\cos\frac{\pi p}{2}|\Im f(re^{it})|^{p}.

Hence by (
3.10
),

M^{p}_{p}(r,v)\leq\sec\frac{\pi p}{2}(K^{2}M_{1}^{p}(r,u)-(K^{2}-1)M_{p}^{p}(r,u)).

Moreover, by using again
[
20
, Eq. 22]

\cos(px)\leq|\cos x|^{p}+\cos(p\pi/2)|\sin x|^{p},

for every
|x|\leq\pi
. Thus by using the previous inequality to
f(re^{it})=Re^{ix}
, we have

\Re f^{p}(re^{it})\leq|u(re^{it})|^{p}+\cos(p\pi/2)|v(re^{it})|^{p}.

Hence, in view or (
3.9
),

u^{p}(0)+\frac{4k}{(1-k)^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u^{p}(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}-u^{p}(0)\right)\leq M^{p}_{p}(r,u)+\cos(p\pi/2)M^{p}_{p}(r,v).

And therefore

(2-K^{2})M^{p}_{1}(r,u)\leq(2-K^{2})M^{p}_{p}(r,u)+\cos(p\pi/2)M^{p}_{p}(r,v),

because
u(0)=M_{1}(r,u)
for every

.

Observe that for

). Similarly the constant in (

and

Remark 3.2
.

We expect the positiveness of real part
u
of
f
in Theorem
2.3
is redundant. We want to mention that it is an open question whether the constant
\sec\frac{\pi p}{2}
is sharp in (
2.1
), for the class of holomorphic mappings without positiveness condition on the real part (
[
20
]
). On the other hand, the validity of Kolmogorov theorem for quasiregular harmonic mappings for a certain constant which is not as good as in Theorem
2.3
can be proved. We use the so called (Littlewood-Paley)
g
-function to prove a more general result. The idea of using
g-
function for this purpose but for
p>1
appears in a recent preprint by Chen and Huang
[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f=g+\bar{h}
be a
K-
quasiregular harmonic mapping, and assume that
0<p<1
and that
\Im f(0)=0
. Assume also that
\re f\in h^{1}
. Then
f\in h^{p}
and there is a constant
C_{0}(K,p)
so that

(3.11)

\|f\|_{p}\leq C_{0}(K,p)\|\Re f\|_{1}.

Proof.

Note first that if
H
is an analytic function defined in the unit disk with
H(0)=0
, then we have the double inequality

\|H\|_{p}\leq c_{1}(p)\|G[H]\|_{p}\leq c_{2}(p)\|H\|_{p},

where

(3.12)

G[H](z)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|H^{\prime}(rz)|^{2}(1-r)dr\right)^{1/2}.

This hold due to Calderon type theorem of Pavlović
[
17
, Theorem 10.6]
.
Assume first that
g(0)=h(0)=0
.
Let
F=g+h
. Then
\Re F=\Re f
.

Since
f
is quasiregular we have

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1+K}{2}|F^{\prime}(z)|,\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then by using (
3.12
), and classical Kolmogorov theorem, we have

\begin{split}\|g\|_{p}&\leq c_{1}(p)\|G[g]\|_{p}\\
&\leq\frac{1+K}{2}c_{1}(p)\|G[F]\|_{p}\\
&\leq\frac{1+K}{2}c_{1}(p)\cdot c_{2}(p)\|F\|_{p}\\
&\leq\frac{1+K}{2}c_{3}(p)\|\Re F\|_{1}=C_{1}(K,p)\|\Re F\|_{1}.\end{split}

Similarly we obtain

\|h\|_{p}\leq C_{1}(K,p)\|\Re F\|_{1}.

Since
\|f\|^{p}_{p}\leq\|g\|^{p}_{p}+\|h\|^{p}_{p}
, we obtain (
3.11
) with
C_{0}(K,p)=C(K,p):=2^{1/p}C_{1}(K,p)
.
If
g(0)\neq 0
or
h(0)\neq 0
, then we use the inequalities

\begin{split}\|f\|^{p}_{p}&\leq\|f-f(0)\|^{p}_{p}+\|f(0)\|^{p}_{p}\\
&=\|f-f(0)\|^{p}_{p}+|\Re f(0)|^{p}\\
&\leq C^{p}(K,p)\|\Re(f-f(0))\|^{p}_{1}+|\Re f(0)|^{p}\leq(2C^{p}(K,p)+1)\|\Re f\|^{p}_{1},\end{split}

because
|\Re f(0)|\leq\|\Re f\|_{1}
and
f(0)=\Re f(0)
. In this case
C_{0}(K,p)=(2C^{p}(K,p)+1)^{1/p}
.
∎

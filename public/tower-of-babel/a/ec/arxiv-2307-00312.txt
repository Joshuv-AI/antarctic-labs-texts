Upper bounds for the number of isolated critical points via Thom-Milnor theorem
Vladimir Zolotov

Abstract
. We apply the Thom-Milnor theorem to obtain the upper bounds on the amount of isolated (1) critical points of a potential generated by several fixed point charges(Maxwell’s problem on point charges), (2) critical points of SINR, (3) critical points of a potential generated by several fixed Newtonian point masses augmented with a quadratic term, (4) central configurations in the n n -body problem. In particular, we get an exponential bound for Maxwell’s problem and the polynomial bound for the case of an ”even dimensional” potential in Maxwell’s problem.

31B05

1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we use the Thom-Milnor theorem to
give upper bounds for the number of isolated

(1)

critical points of a potential of generated by several fixed point
charges (Maxwell’s problem on point charges),

(2)

critical points of
\operatorname{SINR}
,

(3)

critical points of a potential generated by several fixed
Newtonian point masses augmented with a quadratic term,

(4)

central configurations in the
n
-body problem.

Surprisingly the direct application of the Thom-Milnor theorem
allows to obtain tighter or more general bounds
than specialized methods. In particular, we get an exponential
bound for (
1
)
which were previously
only known for the
2
-dimensional case, see
[
13
]
. Additionally for the case of
”even dimensional” potential in (1) we get a polynomial bound.

1.1.
Thom-Milnor theorem

Our main tool is the following theorem by R. Thom
[
26
]

and J. Milnor
[
18
, Theorem 2]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
m,k,p=1,2,3,\dots
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{p}
be a real
polynomials in
m
variables. Let
V
be the
zero set of the system

(1.1)

f_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\dots=f_{p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=0.

Suppose that each
f_{i}
has degree
\leq k,
then
the sum of the Betti numbers of
V
is
\leq k(2k-1)^{m-1}
.

In the above formulation by
q
th Betti numbers of
V

we mean the rank of Čech cohomology group
H^{q}(V)
,
using coefficients in some fixed field
F
. All we need
from Betty numbers are two following properties:

•

they all are non-negative since they are ranks
of some groups,

•

0
th Betti number
b_{0}
is the number of the connected components of
V
.

The above two properties directly imply the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Under assumptions of Proposition
1
,
the number of connected components of
V

does not exceed

k(2k-1)^{m-1},

and in particular the number of isolated zeroes
of the system (
1.1
) does not exceed

k(2k-1)^{m-1}.

2.
Maxwell’s problem

2.1.
The statement

Fix
d\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
.
Let
||\cdot||
denotes the the standard Euclidean
norm.
Fix
m\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
Suppose that we have points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
And numbers
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}

which symbolize point charges located in those points.
Consider a function

V=V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},q_{n})}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by

V(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}}{||p-x_{i}||^{m}},\text{for $m\neq 0$,}

V(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{q_{i}}\log{||p-x_{i}||},\text{for $m=0$.}

Maxwell’s problem asks for an upper bound on the number of critical points of
V
.

If
m=d-2
then
V
is the potential of electrostatic field
created by point charges
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
(maybe up to a constant)
as it is considered in mathematical physics. If in addition

d=3
then
V
is the ”real life” potential of
electrostatic field from physics.

J.C. Maxwell
[
17
]
argued that in the case
d=3,m=1

the upper bound
on the number of isolated critical points of
V

is
(n-1)^{2}
, but
his proof contains an unproven claim,
and thus is considered to be incomplete
(see
[
6
, Section 4]
).

2.2.
Rough summary of our results

If
m
is even then the critical
points of
V
coincide with solutions
of a system of polynomial equations with
d
independent
variables. Combining this with the
Thom-Milnor estimate on the number of connected
components of a set of solutions of a polynomial system
we get that the number of isolated critical points
of
V
is bounded from above by

(1+(n-1)(m+2))(1+2(n-1)(m+2))^{d-1},

see Theorem
2
(
2
).

For the general case
critical points of
V

correspond to zeroes of a polynomial system with
n+d
independent
variables. Once again by the use of the Thom-Milnor estimate we get
that the number of isolated critical points
of
V
can not exceed

(m+4)(2m+7)^{d+n},

see Theorem
2
(
4
).

2.3.
Previous results

2.3.1.
The only previously known result for
d=3
and
m=1
and arbitrary
n
.

The work
[
6
]
by A. Gabrielov, D. Novikov, and B. Shapiro
is the only one which deals with arbitrary
d,m
and
n
and even
the only one which addresses the case
d=3
and
m=1
for arbitrary
n
.
Authors of
[
6
]
represent the set
of critical points of
V
as a set of solutions of
a system of quasi-polynomial equations.
From that by application of Khovanskii’s theory of fewnomials
[
11
]

they deduce that
for any
m\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
any
d\in\{1,2,\dots\}
if all the critical points of
V
are non-degenerate then
their total number
does not exceed
4^{n^{2}}(3n)^{2n}
.

In general, there exist configuration where critical points are not isolated:
consider a square with point charges
1,-1,1,-1
in its
vertices. Then every point on the line through the center of the square
and orthogonal to the plane of the square will be critical. It is
unknown if non-isolated critical points could exist if all
the charges have the same sign, see
[
24
, Conjecture 2]
.

2.3.2.
Other results which work for arbitrary
n
.

In
[
13
]
K. Killian considers the case
d=2,m=1
and
shows that if all critical points of
V
are isolated then their total number
does not exceed
2^{2n-2}(3n-2)^{2}
.

For the case
d=2,m=1
it is unknown if non-isolated critical points are possible.
T. Erdélyi, J. Rosenblatt and R. Rosenblatt
[
5
]

show that there are no isolated critical points in the case if
all point charges are on the same line.

The study a of a case
d=2,m=0
goes back to K. F. Gauss,
see
[
16
, Chapter I.3]
.
In this case using the identification
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
we can write

(\nabla V(z))^{*}=c\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}}{(z-x_{i})},

where
*
denotes the complex conjugation and
c\neq 0
is an absolute constant.
Thus the zeroes of
\nabla V
coincide with zeroes of a complex polynomial of
a degree at most
(n-1)
and
V
has at most
(n-1)
critical points.

2.3.3.
Modeling of
m=m^{\prime}
inside
m=m^{\prime}+1
.

Another phenomena mentioned in
[
16
, Chapter I.3]
is that
one can generate

V=V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},q_{n})}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

using
V_{m+1}
by adding additional dimension and substituting point charges by charged lines.
More precisely consider

\bar{V}=\bar{V}_{m+1}^{(l_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(l_{n},q_{n})}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\{0\}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},

where
l_{i}
is a line
(x_{i},*)
and
\bar{V}
is given by

\bar{V}(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{x\in l_{i}}\frac{q_{i}dx}{||p-x||^{m+1}}.

Then
V=c(m)\bar{V}
, where
c(m)>0
is a constant depending only on
m
.

Theorem
2
gives a polynomial upper bound for
the number of isolated critical points for the case of even
m
.
Thus, if one could model
m=m^{\prime}
inside
m=m^{\prime}+1
using say
100
point charges
instead of each charged line then he would likely be able to get a polynomial upper bound for
the case of odd
m
too. But the author fails to figure
out how to do a reduction of this nature.

2.3.4.
Results for a specific
n
.

T.-L. Lee and Y.-L. Tsai
[
15
]
give an example with
9
equilibrium
points for
d=2,m=1
, and
n=4
which is the claimed upper bound of the Maxwell conjecture.

2.4.
Our results for Maxwell’s problem

The following theorem formalizes the informal summary given in Subsection
2.2
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
x_{1}=(x_{11},\dots,x_{1d}),\dots,x_{n}=(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nd})

be distinct points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
,

m\in\{0,1,2,3,\dots\}
,

q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}

and
V=V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},q_{n})}
. Then,

(1)

Point
p
is a critical point of
V
iff

(2.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(q_{i}(p-x_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}(p_{k}-x_{jk})^{2})^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\Big)=0.

(2)

If
m
is even then
V
has at most

(1+(n-1)(m+2))(1+2(n-1)(m+2))^{d-1}

isolated critical points.

(3)

Point
p
is a critical point of
V
iff there exist
{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{n}>0

satisfying

{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}(p_{k}-x_{jk})^{2}=1\text{, for every $1\leq j\leq n$ and}

(2.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(q_{i}(p-x_{i}){\sigma}_{i}^{m+2}\Big)=0.

(4)

V
has at most

(m+4)(2m+7)^{d+n}

isolated critical points.

Proof of Theorem
2
(
1
).

We remind that

V(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}}{||p-x_{i}||^{m}},\text{for $m\neq 0$,}

V(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{q_{i}}\log{||p-x_{i}||},\text{for $m=0$.}

We differentiate
V
:

V^{\prime}_{p_{k}}=c(m)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}(p_{k}-x_{ik})}{||p-x_{i}||^{m+2}},

where
c(m)\neq 0

is a constant depending only on
m
,

\nabla V=c(m)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_{i}(p-x_{i})}{||p-x_{i}||^{m+2}},

which is (maybe up to a constant) the force given by Coulomb’s law.
Thus,
\nabla V(p)=0
is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(q_{i}(p-x_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}||p-x_{j}||^{m+2}\Big)=0.

Which is equivalent to (
2.1
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
(
2
).

When
m
is even (
2.1
) is a polynomial system in
d

variables:
p_{1},\dots,p_{d}
. Each polynomial of the system
has degree
\leq(1+(n-1)(m+2))
. Thus, by Thom-Milnor
theorem (see Corollary
1
) we have the number of
isolated critical points of
V
does not exceed

(1+(n-1)(m+2))(1+2(n-1)(m+2))^{d-1}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2
(
2.2
).

Theorem
2
(
2.2
) is
just a reformulation of
Theorem
2
(
1
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
(
4
).

Denote

N=(m+4)(2m+7)^{d+n}.

We will argue by contradiction.
Suppose that
V
has at least
N+1
isolated critical points.
Then by Theorem
2
(
2.2
)
the zero set of the system (
2.2
) (considered as a polynomial
system in
d+n
variables
p_{1},\dots,p_{k},{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{n}
)
has at least
N+1
connected components.

The degree of every polynomial in this system is

\leq\max\{4,m+3\}\leq m+4
.
Thus,
by Thom-Milnor theorem (see Corollary
1
) we
have that the total number of the connected components of the zero set
does not exceed

(m+4)(2m+7)^{d+n}=N,

so we have a contradiction.
∎

2.5.
Existence of non-isolated critical points

As we already mentioned in subsubsections
2.3.1
and
2.3.2

non-isolated critical points do exist but it is unknown if they exist
in dimension
2
or for potentials generated by charges
of the same sign.

It is also known that in almost all configurations of
charges there are no non-isolated critical points.
More precisely M. Morse and S. Cains

[
21
, Theorem 6.2]
give the following theorem.

Proposition 2
.

Let
d\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
and
m=\{0,1,2,3,\dots\}
.
Let
x_{1}=(x_{11},\dots,x_{1d}),\dots,x_{n-1}=(x_{(n-1)1},\dots,x_{(n-1)d})

be distinct points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and

q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, for almost all
x_{n}=(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nd})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}\}
the potential

V=V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},q_{n})}
has no degenerate critical points.

The above proposition is a corollary of the following
theorem
[
21
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
d,{\delta}\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
. Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+{\delta}}
be a Euclidean space
with Cartesian coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{d},a_{1},\dots,a_{\delta}
.
Let
W
be an open non-empty subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+{\delta}}
and

U:W\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a
C^{2}
-mapping such that for every point
p\in W

satisfying

U^{\prime}_{x_{i}}(p)=0\text{, for every $1\leq i\leq d$},

we have

\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix}U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}x_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}x_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}x_{d}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{1}a_{\delta}}\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}x_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}x_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}x_{d}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{2}a_{\delta}}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}x_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}x_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}x_{d}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{d}a_{\delta}}\end{bmatrix}=d.

Then for almost all
a\in\{{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\delta}|\exists x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:(x,{\alpha})\in W\}
the map

u^{a}:\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}|(x,a)\in W\}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

given by

u^{a}(x)=U(x,a)

does not have degenerate critical points (i.e., if the gradient of
u^{a}

is
0
at some point then Hessian of
u^{a}
has the maximal rank at this point).

We will not give the proof of Proposition
3
since
it can be found in
[
21
]
. But we will
give the proof of Proposition
2
because

[
21
]
only states Proposition
2

for
d=3,m=1
and only sketches the proof.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

We take
W=\{(p,a)|p,a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},p\neq a,p\neq x_{1},\dots,p\neq x_{n-1}\}

and we define

U:W\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

by

U(p,a)=V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(x_{n-1},q_{n-1}),(a,q_{n})}(p).

Consider a matrix
d\times d
matrix
M
given by

M=\begin{bmatrix}U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}a_{d}}\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}a_{d}}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}a_{1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}a_{2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}a_{d}}\end{bmatrix}

and we are going to show that
\operatorname{rank}(M)=d
.
For
1\leq k\leq d
have

U^{\prime}_{a_{k}}(p)=c(m)\frac{q_{n}(p_{k}-a_{k})}{||p-a||^{m+2}},

where
c(m)=m
, for
m\neq 0
and
c(0)=-1
.
And thus for
1\leq j\neq k\leq d
we have

U^{\prime\prime}_{a_{k}p_{j}}(p)=-c(m)(m+2)\frac{q_{n}(p_{k}-a_{k})(p_{j}-a_{j})}{||p-a||^{m+4}},

and

U^{\prime\prime}_{a_{k}p_{k}}(p)=-c(m)(m+2)\frac{q_{n}(p_{k}-a_{k})^{2}}{||p-a||^{m+4}}+c(m)\frac{q_{n}}{||p-a||^{m+2}}.

Thus

(2.3)

M=(c(m)\frac{q_{n}}{||p-a||^{m+2}})(I-(m+2)vv^{T}),

where

v=(p-a)/||p-a||.

Suppose that
\operatorname{rank}(M)<d
then there exists
w\neq 0
such that

Mw=0,

from (
2.3
) we see that
w={\lambda}v
for some
{\lambda}\neq 0
.
But then

Mw=M{\lambda}v=(c(m)\frac{q_{n}}{||p-a||^{m+2}})({\lambda}v-{\lambda}v(m+2))\neq 0.

So we have a contradiction and thus
\operatorname{rank}(M)=d
.
Now we can apply Proposition
3
and get that
for almost all
a
the potential
V_{m}^{(x_{1},q_{1}),\dots,(a,q_{n})}

does not have degenerate critical points.
∎

3.
Critical points of
\operatorname{SINR}

3.1.
The statement

In wireless communications the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (
\operatorname{SINR}
)
is used as a way to measure the quality of wireless connection (see
[
4
,
8
]
).
Given
d\in\{2,3\}
, points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}>0
,
\alpha>0
and
N\geq 0

a function
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

(3.1)

\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},p)=\frac{\psi_{i}||x_{i}-p||^{-\alpha}}{\sum_{j\neq i}(\psi_{j}||x_{j}-p||^{-\alpha})+N}.

In this model a receiver at a point
p
successfully receives
a message from sender
x_{i}
, if and only if
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},p)\geq\beta,

where
\beta
is a constant
\geq 1
.
Numbers
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}
represent transmitting powers of concurrently transmitting stations
at points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
N
represents the environmental noise.
The pass-loss parameter
\alpha
is typically taken from the interval
[2,4]
, with
{\alpha}=2
being the
most common. The reception threshold
{\beta}
is commonly taken to be
{\beta}\approx 6
.

Interest in the critical points of
\operatorname{SINR}
is motivated by the point location problem, see
[
8
]
.
Here is the description of the problem from
[
8
]
:
”Given a query point
p
, it is required to identify which of the
n
transmitting stations is heard at
p
,
if any, under interference from all other
n-1
transmitting stations and background noise
N
.
Obviously, one can directly compute
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},p)
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
in time
\Theta(n)

and answer the above question accordingly. Yet, this computation may be too expensive,
if the query is asked for many different points
p
.”

3.2.
Rough summary of our results for
\operatorname{SINR}

For the case when
{\alpha}
is an even integer the critical
points of
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
coincide with solutions
of a system of polynomial equations with
d
independent
variables. From the
Thom-Milnor Theorem (see Corollary
1
) we get that the number of isolated critical points
is bounded from above by

({\alpha}(2n-1)-1)(2{\alpha}(2n-1)-3)^{d-1},

see Theorem
3
(
2
).

3.3.
Our results for
\operatorname{SINR}

Now we give a more detailed version of the above statement.

Theorem 3
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
,

{\alpha}\in\{2,4,6,8\dots\}
,

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}>0
,
N\geq 0
,

i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

and
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
be a function defined as in (
3.1
). Then,

(1)

Point
p
is a critical point of
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
iff for every
m={1,\dots,d}
we have

(3.2)

f^{\prime}_{m}(p)g(p)-f(p)g^{\prime}_{m}(p)=0,

where
f,g
are real polynomials in variables
(\bar{p}_{1},\dots,\bar{p}_{d}):=\bar{p}
given by

f=\psi_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}||x_{i}-\bar{p}||^{\alpha},

g=\sum_{j\neq i}(\psi_{j}\prod_{k\neq j}||x_{k}-\bar{p}||^{\alpha})+N\prod_{1\leq k\leq n}||x_{k}-\bar{p}||^{\alpha}.

(2)

\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
has at most

({\alpha}(2n-1)-1)(2{\alpha}(2n-1)-3)^{d-1}

isolated critical points.

Proof of Theorem
3
(
1
).

Note that

\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\bar{p})=\frac{f(\bar{p})}{g(\bar{p})}.

Thus,

\operatorname{SINR}^{\prime}_{m}(x_{i},p)=\frac{f^{\prime}_{m}(p)g(p)-f(p)g^{\prime}_{m}(p)}{g^{2}(p)}

and the claim follows. (Note that since
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}>0
the denominator
is always positive and thus can not create any problems.)
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
(
2
).

Since
{\alpha}
is even natural number, (
3.2
) is a polynomial system in
d

variables:
p_{1},\dots,p_{d}
. Each polynomial of the system
has degree
\leq({\alpha}(2n-1)-1)
. Thus, by Thom-Milnor
theorem (see Corollary
1
) we have the number of
isolated critical points of
V
does not exceed

({\alpha}(2n-1)-1)(2{\alpha}(2n-1)-3)^{d-1}.

∎

3.4.
Existence of non-isolated critical points

It is unclear whether non-isolated critical points of
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)

could exist in the general case.
But for almost all of the selections of locations of transmitters
there are no non-isolated critical points.
The following proposition has the same nature as Proposition
2

but the proof is a bit more messy.

Proposition 4
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
,
n\in\{d+2,d+3,d+4,\dots\}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
let
x_{i}
be a point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

{\alpha}\in\{2,4,6,8,\dots\}
,

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}>0
,
N\geq 0
.
Then, for almost all

(x_{1}=(x_{11},\dots,x_{1d}),\dots,x_{i-1}=(x_{(i-1)1},\dots,x_{(i-1)d}),x_{i+1}=(x_{(i+1)1},\dots,x_{(i+1)d}),\dots,x_{n}=(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nd}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d(n-1)}

the function
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
has no degenerate critical points.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Instead of working of working with
\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)
we prefer to work
with

\frac{1}{\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)}=\frac{\sum_{j\neq i}(\psi_{j}||x_{j}-p||^{-\alpha})+N}{\psi_{i}||x_{i}-p||^{-\alpha}}.

By computing the gradient and the
Hessian one can see that for any open
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and any

C^{2}
-function
f:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)

(1)

a point
p\in\Omega
is a critical point of
f
iff
p
is a critical point of
1/f
,

(2)

a point
p\in\Omega
is a degenerate critical point of
f
iff
p
is a degenerate critical point of
1/f
.

Thus, it suffices to show that
for almost all

(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})

a function
\frac{1}{\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},\cdot)}
has no degenerate critical points.

We take

W=\{(p,(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}))|

|p,x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}\text{ are distinct points in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{i}\},

\text{ and }x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}\text{ do not lie on all on the same line}\}

and we define

U:W\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

by

U(p,(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}))=\frac{1}{\operatorname{SINR}(x_{i},p)}.

For
h\in\{1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,n\}
consider a matrix
d\times d
matrix
M_{h}

given by

M_{h}=\begin{bmatrix}U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}x_{h1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}x_{h2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{1}x_{hd}}\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}x_{h1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}x_{h2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{2}x_{hd}}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}x_{h1}}&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}x_{h2}}&\dots&U^{\prime\prime}_{p_{d}x_{hd}}\end{bmatrix}

By Proposition
3
it suffices to show that the matrix

[M_{1},\dots,M_{i-1},M_{i+1},\dots,M_{n}]

has rank
d
everywhere in
W
.
Suppose that’s not true. Then for some

(p,(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}))\in W

there exists
{\omega}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(3.3)

w^{T}M_{h}=0,

for every
h\in{1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,n}
.

Next, we need to explain what happens to a row vector when it gets multiplied by
M_{h}
from the right.
For every
h\in\{1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,n\}
and
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}

U^{\prime}_{x_{hk}}(p)={\alpha}\frac{\psi_{h}||x_{h}-p||^{-(\alpha+2)}}{\psi_{i}||x_{i}-p||^{-\alpha}}(p_{k}-x_{hk}).

We denote

Q_{h}:={\alpha}\frac{\psi_{h}||x_{h}-p||^{-(\alpha+2)}}{\psi_{i}||x_{i}-p||^{-\alpha}},

so we have

U^{\prime}_{x_{hk}}(p)=(p_{k}-x_{hk})Q_{h}.

For every
h\in\{1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,n\}
and
k,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we have

U^{\prime\prime}_{x_{hk}p_{j}}(p)=P^{h,k,j}_{1}+P^{h,k,j}_{2}+P^{h,k,j}_{3},

where

P^{h,k,j}_{1}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }j\neq k\\
Q_{h}&\text{if }j=k\end{cases},

P^{h,k,j}_{2}=-({\alpha}+2)(p_{k}-x_{hk})(p_{j}-x_{hj})||x_{h}-p||^{-2}Q_{h},

P^{h,k,j}_{3}={\alpha}(p_{k}-x_{hk})(p_{j}-x_{ij})||x_{i}-p||^{-2}Q_{h}.

Thus
M_{h}
can be presented as

M_{h}=M_{h1}+M_{h2}+M_{h3},

such that for every
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we have

v^{T}M_{h1}=v^{T}Q_{h},

v^{T}M_{h2}=-({\alpha}+2)||x_{h}-p||^{-2}v^{T}(p-x_{h})(p-x_{h})^{T}Q_{h},

v^{T}M_{h3}={\alpha}||x_{i}-p||^{-2}v^{T}(p-x_{i})(p-x_{h})^{T}Q_{h}.

Note that for every
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we have that

v^{T}M_{h2}={\lambda}_{2}(v)(p-x_{h})^{T},

v^{T}M_{h3}={\lambda}_{3}(v)(p-x_{h})^{T},

for some
{\lambda}_{2}(v),{\lambda}_{3}(v)\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus from
{\omega}^{T}M_{h}=0
we have that

{\omega}^{T}M_{h1}=-({\lambda}_{2}(w)+{\lambda}_{3}(w))(p-x_{h})^{T},

and we can conclude that for every
h\in{1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,n}

there exists
c(h)\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
such that

{\omega}=c(h)(p-x_{h}).

This implies that all
x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}
all lie on the same line.
But that’s the opposite of what is stated in the definition of
W
.
∎

4.
Newtonian Point Masses with a Central Force

This model is similar to Maxwell’s problem for the case
m=1

with all the charges having the same sign except there is an
additional quadratic term. More precisely,
let
d\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
.
Suppose that we have points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
And numbers
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}>0

which symbolize point masses located in those points.
Consider a function

F=F^{(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(4.1)

F(p)=\frac{1}{2}||p||^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}}{||p-x_{i}||}.

Once again we are interested in an upper bound on the number
of critical points of
F
.

This model arises in the study of the restricted
(n+1)
-body
problem. We refer the reader to
[
3
]

for details and the relevant bibliography.

4.1.
Rough summary of our results for Newtonian Point Masses with a Central Force

We give an exponential upper bound for the number of isolated
critical points for any dimension
d
. More precisely the number
of isolated critical points of
F
does not exceed

(1+3n)(1+6n)^{d+n}.

4.2.
Previous results

Arustamyan et al.
[
3
]
claim an exponential upper bound
for the number of isolated
critical points for the planar case of this problem.

In astrophysics, there is a related open problem
of finding upper bounds on the
number of possible images in gravitational microlensing,
see
[
9
,
23
]
.
S. Perry
[
22
]
gives the only
known upper bound for the general case.

4.3.
Our results for Newtonian Point Masses with a Central Force

The following theorem formalizes the informal summary given in Subsection
4.1
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
d\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
.
Let
x_{1}=(x_{11},\dots,x_{1d}),\dots,x_{n}=(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nd})

be points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
,

m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}

and let
F=F^{(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})}
be the function given by (
4.1
). Then,

(1)

Point
p
is a critical point of
F
iff there exist
{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{n}>0

satisfying

{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}(p_{k}-x_{jk})^{2}=1\text{, for every $1\leq j\leq n$, and}

(4.2)

p-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(m_{i}(p-x_{i}){\sigma}_{i}^{3}\Big)=0.

(2)

F
has at most

4\cdot 7^{d+n}

isolated critical points.

Proof of Theorem
4
(
4.2
).

We remind that

F(p)=\frac{1}{2}||p||^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}}{||p-x_{i}||}.

We differentiate
V
:

F^{\prime}_{p_{k}}=p_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(p_{k}-x_{ik})}{||p-x_{i}||^{3}},

\nabla F=p-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(p-x_{i})}{||p-x_{i}||^{3}},

Thus,
\nabla F(p)=0
is equivalent to
(
4.2
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
(
2
).

Denote

N=4\cdot 7^{d+n}.

We will argue by contradiction.
Suppose that
F
has at least
N+1
isolated critical points.
Then by Theorem
4
(
4.2
)
the zero set of the system (
4.2
) (considered as a polynomial
system in
d+n
variables
p_{1},\dots,p_{k},{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{n}
)
has at least
N+1
connected components.

The degree of every polynomial in this system is
\leq 4
.
Thus,
by Thom-Milnor theorem (see Corollary
1
) we
have that the total number of the connected components of the zero set
does not exceed

4\cdot 7^{d+n}=N,

so we have a contradiction.
∎

4.4.
Existence of non-isolated critical points

There is only one known example of a configuration with non-isolated
critical points: we take
n=1
and put the only one point mass
we have into the origin.

It is also known that for almost all configurations
there is no degenerate critical points, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]

(authors of
[
3
]
state their result only for

5.
Central configurations

Let
d,n\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}>0
and
let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be distinct points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We say that a system of point masses
(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})

is a central preconfiguration if there exists
{\lambda}>0

such that for every
i=1,\dots,n
,

{\lambda}x_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(m_{i}||x_{i}-x_{j}||^{-3}(x_{i}-x_{j})).

We say that a central preconfiguration is normalized if
{\lambda}
in the above system
is equal to
1
. (Every central preconfiguration can be normalized by scaling.)

We say that normalized central preconfigurations

(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})
and
(x^{\prime}_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x^{\prime}_{n},m_{n})

are equivalent if there
exists an orientation and origin-preserving isometry

F:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

such that

F(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
.
Central configuration is an equivalence class of normalized central preconfigurations.

We say that a c
central configurations
K
is isolated
iff there exists
{\epsilon}>0
such that for every normalized central preconfiguration

(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})
from
K
and every normalized central preconfiguration

(y_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(y_{n},m_{n})
which does not belong to
K
we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}||x_{i}-y_{i}||\geq{\epsilon}.

5.1.
Our results for central configurations

The following theorem is very similar to the result of R. Kuzmuna
[
14
, Theorem 3]
.
R. Moekel’s estimates
[
19
,
20
]
for the number of central
configurations also utilize Thom-Milnor theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d,n\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}>0

There are at most

4\cdot 7^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nd-1}

isolated central configurations with
those parameters.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}=(x_{11},\dots,x_{1d}),\dots,x_{n}=(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nd})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
A system of point masses
(x_{1},m_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},m_{n})
is
a normalized central preconfiguration iff there exist

\{{\sigma}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}

such that
{\sigma}_{ij}={\sigma}_{ji}>0
and
satisfying

{\sigma}_{ij}^{2}\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}(x_{ik}-x_{jk})^{2}=1\text{, for every $1\leq i<j\leq n$ and}

(5.1)

x_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(m_{i}{\sigma}_{ij}^{3}(x_{i}-x_{j})).

Denote

N=4\cdot 7^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nd-1}.

We will argue by contradiction.
Suppose that there are at least
N+1
isolated central configurations.
Then
the zero set of the system (
5.1
) (considered as a polynomial
system in
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nd
variables

\{{\sigma}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq n}
,

\{x_{ij}\}_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq d}

)
has at least
N+1
connected components.
The degree of every polynomial in this system is
\leq 4
.
Thus,
by Thom-Milnor theorem (see Corollary
1
) we
have that the total number of the connected components of the zero set
does not exceed

4\cdot 7^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+nd-1}=N,

so we have a contradiction.
∎

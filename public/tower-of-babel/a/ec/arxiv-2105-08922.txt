The Cuboidal Lattices and their Lattice sums
Antony Burrows, Shaun Cooper, Peter Schwerdtfeger

Abstract
. Lattice sums of cuboidal lattices, which connect the face-centered with the mean-centered and the body-centered cubic lattices through parameter dependent lattice vectors, are evaluated by decomposing them into two separate lattice sums related to a scaled cubic lattice and a scaled Madelung constant. Using theta functions we were able to derive fast converging series in terms of Bessel functions. Analytical continuations of these lattice sums are discussed in detail.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11E45. Secondary 33E20, 40A25, 65B10

1.
Introduction

Lattice sums have a long history in solid-state physics and discrete mathematics
[
5
]
. They connect lattices to observables
such as the equation of state for a bulk system using interacting potentials between the lattice points (atoms or molecules)
in three-dimensional space
[
12
,
13
,
18
,
20
]
. Most notable cases for such interactions are the
Lennard-Jones
[
14
]
and the Coulomb potential, leading in the latter case, for example, to the famous Madelung
constant derived as early as in 1918
[
17
]
. For such potentials the corresponding lattice sums become functions of
quadratic forms
\boldsymbol{i}^{\top}G\boldsymbol{i}
with
\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}

[
6
]
, i.e. the expression
\boldsymbol{i}^{\top}G\boldsymbol{i}

is the quadratic form associated with the lattice
\mathcal{L}
(or simply, the associated quadratic form).

In the general case of a
n
-dimensional lattice (
\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
),
G
is a positive definite,
real and symmetric
(n\times n)
matrix called the Gram matrix of the lattice
\mathcal{L}
, defined by its basis (or lattice)
vectors
\{\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\}
through
G=BB^{\top}
.
B=(\boldsymbol{b}_{1},...,\boldsymbol{b}_{n})^{\top}
is called
the generator matrix (
B
not necessarily positive definite). Lattice sums represent often conditionally
convergent series
[
4
]
, and the theory of converting them into fast converging
series has become an intense research field over the past 50 years
[
5
]
.

Concerning the Gram matrix
G
or generator matrix we introduce a few important definitions required in this work
[
9
]
. Two generator matrices
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are equivalent if
B_{2}=cUB_{1}\mathcal{O}
,
c
a non-zero real number,
\mathcal{O}
a real orthogonal matrix (
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}^{\top}=1
) with
\textrm{det}(\mathcal{O})=\pm 1
describing rotation, reflection or rotoreflection of the lattice, and
U
a matrix containing integers with
\textrm{det}U=1
describing for example permutations of the basis vectors. Given two equivalent generator matrices
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, the corresponding Gram matrices are related by

G_{2}=B_{2}B_{2}^{\top}=cUB_{1}\mathcal{O}\left(cUB_{1}\mathcal{O}\right)^{\top}=c^{2}UB_{1}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}^{\top}B_{1}^{\top}U^{\top}=c^{2}UG_{1}U^{\top}.

The minimum distance
d_{\textrm{min}}
in a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

d_{\textrm{min}}(\mathcal{L})=\textrm{min}\{d(\boldsymbol{v}_{1};\boldsymbol{v}_{2})|\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2}\in\mathcal{L};\boldsymbol{v}_{1}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{2}\}

where
d(\boldsymbol{v}_{1};\boldsymbol{v}_{2})=|\boldsymbol{v}_{1}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2}|
is the Euclidean distance.
In terms of the Gram matrix this is equivalent to

d_{\textrm{min}}=\textrm{min}\{+\sqrt{\boldsymbol{i}^{\top}G\boldsymbol{i}}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \boldsymbol{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash(0,0,0)^{\top}\}.

The minimal norm is related to the minimum distance by
\mu=d_{\textrm{min}}^{2}
. Dividing
G
by
\mu
assures that
d_{\textrm{min}}=1
used in most lattice sum applications
[
6
]
. For dense sphere packings the radius of a sphere
\rho
is simply
\rho=d_{\textrm{min}}/2
. The packing density
\Delta_{L}
and the center density
\delta_{L}
of a three-dimensional lattice are given by

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\rho^{3}}{\textrm{vol}(\mathcal{L})}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\rho^{3}}{|\textrm{det}(B)|}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\rho^{3}}{\sqrt{\textrm{det}(G)}}.

The kissing number for dense sphere packings is defined by

\textrm{kiss}(\mathcal{L})=\#\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{L}\penalty\ |\penalty\ |\boldsymbol{v}|=d_{\textrm{min}}(\mathcal{L})\}.

In this work we discuss cuboidal lattices and their lattice sums. We first present the characteristics of cuboidal lattices
\mathcal{L}(A)
dependent on a single parameter
A
. In what follows we decompose the corresponding lattice sum into two lattice sums, where one is related to a scaled cubic lattice and the other to a scaled Madelung constant. We evaluate these lattice sums in two ways using theta functions. We discuss these lattice sums including their analytical continuations and provide a more complete analysis for the lattice sum difference between f.c.c. (face centred cubic) and h.c.p. (hexagonal close packing).

2.
The cuboidal lattices

Following Conway and Sloane
[
7
, Sec. 3]
we consider the lattice generated by the vectors

(\pm u,\pm v,0)^{\top}\quad\text{and}\quad(0,\pm v,\pm v)^{\top}
, where
u
and
v
are non-zero real numbers.
To make it specific, take the basis vectors

(2.1)

\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(u,v,0)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=(u,0,v)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=(0,v,v)^{\top}.

Then the generator matrix
B
is given by

B=\begin{pmatrix}u&v&0\\
u&0&v\\
0&v&v\end{pmatrix}

which has determinant
-2uv^{2}
.
The Gram matrix is

(2.2)

G=B\,B^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}u^{2}+v^{2}&u^{2}&v^{2}\\
u^{2}&u^{2}+v^{2}&v^{2}\\
v^{2}&v^{2}&2v^{2}\end{pmatrix}=v^{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+A&A&1\\
A&1+A&1\\
1&1&2\end{pmatrix}

where
A=u^{2}/v^{2}
and
G
is positive definite for
A>0
. Conway and Sloane
[
7
]
use
\sigma=u/v
, so
A=\sigma^{2}
.
The most important cases, in decreasing numerical order, are:

(1)

A=1
: the face-centred cubic (f.c.c.) lattice;

(2)

A=1/\sqrt{2}
: the mean centred-cuboidal (m.c.c.) lattice;

(3)

A=1/2
: the body-centred cubic (b.c.c.) lattice;

(4)

A=1/3
: the axial centred-cuboidal (a.c.c.) lattice.

The f.c.c. and b.c.c. lattices are well known. The corresponding Gram matrices for the f.c.c. and b.c.c lattices
are identical to the ones shown in our previous paper on lattice sums
[
6
]
. The m.c.c. and a.c.c. lattices
occur in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. The m.c.c. lattice is the densest isodual lattice in three-dimensional space.

The quadratic form associated with the lattice is

\displaystyle g(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=(i,j,k)\,G(i,j,k)^{\top}

\displaystyle=(u^{2}+v^{2})i^{2}+(u^{2}+v^{2})j^{2}+2v^{2}k^{2}+2u^{2}ij+2v^{2}ik+2v^{2}jk

\displaystyle=u^{2}(i^{2}+j^{2})+v^{2}(j+k)^{2}+v^{2}(i+k)^{2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=v^{2}\left(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\right).

It is easy to check that

(2.4)

\min_{i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\atop(i,j,k)\neq(0,0,0)}\{A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\}=\begin{cases}4A&\text{if $0<A<1/3$,}\\
A+1&\text{if $1/3\leq A\leq 1$,}\\
2&\text{if $A>1$.}\end{cases}

It follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that the minimum distance is given by

(2.5)

d_{\textrm{min}}=\min_{i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\atop(i,j,k)\neq(0,0,0)}\,\sqrt{g(i,j,k)}=\begin{cases}2v\sqrt{A}&\text{if $0<A<1/3$,}\\
v\sqrt{A+1}&\text{if $1/3\leq A\leq 1$,}\\
v\sqrt{2}&\text{if $A>1$.}\end{cases}

We rescale to make the minimum distance
1
by defining

\displaystyle g(A;i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{g(i,j,k)}{\left(d_{\textrm{min}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4A}\left(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\right)}&\text{if $0<A<1/3$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{A+1}\left(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\right)}&\text{if $1/3\leq A\leq 1$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\left(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\right)}&\text{if $A>1$.}\end{cases}

The examples we are interested are in (f.c.c., m.c.c., b.c.c., a.c.c.) all satisfy
1/3\leq A\leq 1
.
Because of its practical interest, this is the only case we will study, and from here on (unless otherwise mentioned)
we will always assume
1/3\leq A\leq 1

in which case we have

(2.6)

g(A;i,j,k)=\frac{1}{A+1}\left(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}\right),

corresponding to the rescaled Gram matrix

(2.7)

G(A):=\frac{1}{\left(d_{\textrm{min}}\right)^{2}}\;G=\frac{1}{A+1}\begin{pmatrix}1+A&A&1\\
A&1+A&1\\
1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.

The packing density is calculated as

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\rho^{3}}{\sqrt{\textrm{det}(G)}}

where
\rho=d_{\textrm{min}}/2
and

\det(G)=\left(\det(B)\right)^{2}=4u^{2}v^{4}=4Av^{6}.

It follows that

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{\pi}{12\,\sqrt{A}}\left(\frac{d_{\textrm{min}}}{v}\right)^{3}.

On using the formula for
d_{\textrm{min}}
in (
2.5
) we obtain the formula for the packing density, given by

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{2\pi A}{3}}&\text{if $0<A<1/3$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{\frac{(A+1)^{3}}{A}}}&\text{if $1/3\leq A\leq 1$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{\frac{2}{A}}}&\text{if $A>1$.}\end{cases}

Figure 1 shows a graph of the packing density as a function of the parameter
A
.
Further information is recorded in Table

In the main region of interest
1/3\leq A\leq 1
, we have

(2.8)

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{\pi}{12}\sqrt{\frac{(A+1)^{3}}{A}}

and so

(2.9)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}}{\mathrm{d}A}=\frac{\pi}{12}\left(A-\frac{1}{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{A+1}{A^{3}}}.

It follows that on the interval
1/3\leq A\leq 1
, the packing density has a maximum of
\pi\sqrt{2}/6\approx 0.74048
at
A=1

corresponding to f.c.c., and a minimum of
\pi\sqrt{3}/8\approx 0.68017
at
A=1/2
corresponding to b.c.c.

It is also interesting to note that as
A\rightarrow\infty
the limiting case of the lattice is the two-dimensional square close
packing with minimal distance
1
and kissing number
4
; while in the other extreme case the limit as
A\rightarrow 0

gives the one-dimensional lattice with minimal distance
1
and kissing number
2
. These cases are briefly analysed in Appendix B.

Given a positive definite quadratic form
g(i,j,k)
, the corresponding theta series is defined for
|q|<1
by

\theta_{g}(q)=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{g(i,j,k)}.

For the quadratic form in (
2.6
) the theta series is

\theta(A;q)=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2})/(A+1)}\quad\text{ where $1/3\leq A\leq 1$.}

\displaystyle\theta(1;q)

\displaystyle=1+12q+6q^{2}+24q^{3}+12q^{4}+24q^{5}+8q^{6}+48q^{7}+6q^{8}+36q^{9}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\theta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}};q)

\displaystyle=1+8q+4q^{4-2\sqrt{2}}+2q^{4\sqrt{2}-4}+4q^{8-4\sqrt{2}}+8q^{2\sqrt{2}}+16q^{-4\sqrt{2}+9}

\displaystyle\quad+8q^{4}+8q^{8\sqrt{2}-7}+4q^{16-8\sqrt{2}}+8q^{-8\sqrt{2}+17}+8q^{20-10\sqrt{2}}+8q^{-4\sqrt{2}+12}

\displaystyle\quad+8q^{-8+12\sqrt{2}}+8q^{9}+\cdots,

We introduce the following lattice sum important in solid state theory
[
5
]
,

(2.10)

L(A;s)={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{g(A;i,j,k)}\right)^{s}={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{A+1}{A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}}\right)^{s}

where
1/3\leq A\leq 1
.
Here and throughout this work, a prime on the summation symbol will denote
that the sum ranges over all integer values except for the term when all of the summation indices are simultaneously zero.
Thus, the sums in (
2.10
) are over all integer values
of
i
,
j
and
k
except for the term
(i,j,k)=(0,0,0)
which is omitted.
This lattice sum smoothly connects four different well known lattices, i.e., when
A=1
,
1/\sqrt{2}
,
1/2
or
1/3
we obtain the expressions for the
lattices f.c.c, m.c.c., b.c.c. and a.c.c. respectively (face-centred cubic, mean centred-cuboidal, body-centred cubic, and axial centred cuboidal).
In these cases, we also write

\displaystyle L_{3}^{\text{FCC}}(s)

\displaystyle=L(1;s),

\displaystyle L_{3}^{\text{MCC}}(s)

\displaystyle=L(1/\sqrt{2};s),

\displaystyle L_{3}^{\text{BCC}}(s)

\displaystyle=L(1/2;s),

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad L_{3}^{\text{ACC}}(s)

\displaystyle=L(1/3;s).

We conclude this section by reconciling the Gram matrix
G
in (
2.2
) with two
matrices given by Conway and Sloane
[

]
. Let

U_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
-1&0&1\\

and consider the equivalent matrices
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
defined by

(2.11)

G_{1}=U_{1}\,G\,U_{1}^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}u^{2}+v^{2}&-u^{2}&-u^{2}\\
-u^{2}&u^{2}+v^{2}&u^{2}-v^{2}\\
-u^{2}&u^{2}-v^{2}&u^{2}+v^{2}\end{pmatrix}

and

(2.12)

G_{2}=U_{2}\,G\,U_{2}^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}4u^{2}&2u^{2}&2u^{2}\\
2u^{2}&u^{2}+v^{2}&u^{2}\\
2u^{2}&u^{2}&u^{2}+v^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

When
u=1/\sqrt{2}
and
v=1/\sqrt[4]{2}
, the matrix
G_{1}
in (
2.11
) is the Gram matrix
for the m.c.c lattice given by Conway and Sloane
[
7
, (10)]
.
Moreover, when
u=\sqrt{1/3}
and

, the matrix
G_{1}
leads to another well-known
quadratic form for the b.c.c. lattice, e.g., see
[

, (8b)]
.
When
u=1
,
v=\sqrt{3}
, the matrix
G_{2}
in (
2.12
) is the Gram matrix for the a.c.c. lattice given
in
[
7
, p. 378]
.
Since
\det U_{1}=\det U_{2}=1
it follows that

\det G_{1}=\det G_{2}=\det G=(\det B)^{2}=4u^{2}v^{4}=4v^{6}A.

The corresponding quadratic forms
g_{1}
and

are defined by

\displaystyle g_{1}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=(i,j,k)\,G_{1}(i,j,k)^{\top}

\displaystyle=(u^{2}+v^{2})i^{2}+(u^{2}+v^{2})j^{2}+(u^{2}+v^{2})k^{2}-2u^{2}ij-2u^{2}ik+2(u^{2}-v^{2})jk

and

\displaystyle g_{2}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=(i,j,k)\,G_{2}(i,j,k)^{\top}

They are related to the quadratic form

in (

) by

g_{1}(i,j,k)=g\left((i,j,k)U_{1}\right)=g(i-j,-k,j)

and

3.
A minimum property of the lattice sum
L(A;s)

In the previous section — see (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) — it was noted that on the interval
1/3\leq A\leq 1
, the packing density function
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}

has a minimum value when
A=1/2
. The next result shows that provided
s>3/2
, the corresponding lattice sum
L(A;s)

also attains a minimum at the same value
A=1/2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
L(A;s)
be the lattice defined by (
2.10
), that is,

L(A;s)={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{g(A;i,j,k)}\right)^{s}={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{A+1}{A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}}\right)^{s}

where
s>3/2
and
1/3\leq A\leq 1
.
Then

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial A^{2}}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}>0.

Proof.

By definition we have

L(A;s)={\sum_{I,J,K}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{g(A;I,J,K)}\right)^{s}

where

g(A;I,J,K)=\frac{1}{A+1}\left(A(I+J)^{2}+(J+K)^{2}+(I+K)^{2}\right).

Now make the change of variables given by (
2.13
), namely

(I,J,K)=(i-j,-k,j).

This is a bijection since

(i,j,k)=(I+K,K,-J),

and it follows that

\displaystyle L(A;s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{g(A;i-j,-k,j)}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{1}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-2(ij+ik)\left(\frac{A}{A+1}\right)+2jk\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)\right)^{s}}.

By direct calculation, the derivative is given by

(3.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)=\frac{2s}{(A+1)^{2}}{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{ij+ik-2jk}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-2(ij+ik)\left(\frac{A}{A+1}\right)+2jk\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)\right)^{s+1}}.

Setting
A=1/2
gives

(3.2)

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}=\frac{8s}{9}\;{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{ij+ik-2jk}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-\frac{2}{3}(ij+ik+jk)\right)^{s+1}}.

Switching
i
and
j
gives

(3.3)

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}=\frac{8s}{9}\;{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{ij+jk-2ik}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-\frac{2}{3}(ij+ik+jk)\right)^{s+1}},

while switching
i
and
k
in (
3.2
) gives

(3.4)

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}=\frac{8s}{9}\;{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{jk+ik-2ij}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-\frac{2}{3}(ij+ik+jk)\right)^{s+1}}.

On adding (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) and noting that

(ij+ik-2jk)+(ij+jk-2ik)+(jk+ik-2ij)=0

it follows that

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial A}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}=0.

Next, taking the derivative of (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial A^{2}}L(A;s)

\displaystyle=\frac{-4s}{(A+1)^{3}}{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{ij+ik-2jk}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-2(ij+ik)\left(\frac{A}{A+1}\right)+2jk\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)\right)^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4s(s+1)}{(A+1)^{4}}{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{(ij+ik-2jk)^{2}}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-2(ij+ik)\left(\frac{A}{A+1}\right)+2jk\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)\right)^{s+2}}.

When
A=1/2
the first sum is zero by the calculations in the first part of the proof. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial A^{2}}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{64s(s+1)}{81}{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\frac{(jk+ik-2ij)^{2}}{\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}-\frac{2}{3}(ij+ik+jk)\right)^{s+2}}.

The term
(jk+ik-2ij)^{2}
in the numerator is non-negative and not always zero. The
denominator is always positive because the quadratic form is positive definite. It follows that

\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial A^{2}}L(A;s)\right|_{A=1/2}>0

as required.

The calculations above are valid provided term-by-term differentiation of the series is allowed. All of the series encountered above
converge absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of the region Re
(s)>3/2
. On restricting
s
to real values, the conclusion
about positivity is valid for
s>3/2
.
∎

A consequence of Theorem
3.1
is that for any fixed value
s>3/2
, the lattice
sum
L(A;s)
attains a minimum when
A=1/2
.

Graphs of
y=L(A;s)

In the limiting case
s\rightarrow\infty
we have

L(A;\infty)=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}L(A;s)=\textrm{kiss}(\mathcal{L})=\begin{cases}10&\text{if $A=1/3$,}\\
8&\text{if $1/3<A<1$,}\\
12&\text{if $A=1$.}\end{cases}

This graph is also shown in Fig. 2.

4.
Evaluation of the sum
L(A;s)

We now turn to the evaluation of
L(A;s)
. Our objectives are to find formulas that are both simple
and computationally efficient. The formulas we obtain can be used to show that
L(A;s)
can be analytically continued
to complex values of
s
, with a simple pole at
s=3/2
and no other singularities.

One method of evaluating the sum
L(A;s)
is to use the Terras decomposition. This was done for
f.c.c. and b.c.c. in
[
6
]
and can in principle also be used for
L(A;s)
.
Here we use an easier method that also works the whole parameter range
1/3\leq A\leq 1

and hence gives the lattice sum for all four lattices f.c.c., m.c.c., b.c.c. and a.c.c. In fact, the advantage here is that
we obtain two formulas which not only can be used as checks, but also contain different information about their analytic continuation.

We begin by writing the lattice sum in the form

\displaystyle L(A;s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{A+1}{A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2}}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle={\sum_{I,J,K\atop I+J+K\,\text{even}}}^{\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\prime}\left(\frac{A+1}{AI^{2}+J^{2}+K^{2}}\right)^{s}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(A+1)^{s}}{2}{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1+(-1)^{i+j+k}}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

Therefore, we evaluate the sums

(4.2)

T_{1}(A;A;s):={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

and

(4.3)

T_{2}(A;s):={\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{(-1)^{i+j+k}}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

By (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), the required lattice sum is given by

(4.4)

L(A;s)=\frac{(A+1)^{s}}{2}\left(T_{1}(A;s)+T_{2}(A;s)\right).

4.1.
The sum
T_{1}(A;s)

We shall consider two ways for handling the sum in (
4.2
). The first is to separate the terms according to whether
i=0
or
i\neq 0
,
which gives rise to

(4.5)

T_{1}(A;s)=f(s)+2F(s)

where

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{j,k}}^{\,\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1}{(j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

and

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

This is the starting point of the approach taken by Selberg and Chowla
[
19
, Section 7]
.
Another way is to separate the terms
according to whether
(j,k)=(0,0)
or
(j,k)\neq(0,0)
and write

(4.6)

T_{1}(A;s)=2g(s)+G(s)

where

\displaystyle g(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(Ai^{2})^{s}}

and

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{j,k}}^{\,\prime}\;\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

The series
F(s)
,
g(s)
and
G(s)
also depend on
A
.
For simplicity we omit the parameter
A
from the notation and just write
F(s)
,
g(s)
and
G(s)
in place of
F(A;s)
,
g(A;s)
and
G(A;s)
, respectively.

We will now analyse (
4.5
); the corresponding analysis for (
4.6
) will be given in Section
4.2
.
By the well-known result (
A.20
) we have

f(s)={\sum_{j,k}}^{\,\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1}{(j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}=4\zeta(s)L_{-4}(s)

where

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}

is the Riemann zeta function, and

L_{-4}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{1^{s}}-\frac{1}{3^{s}}+\frac{1}{5^{s}}-\frac{1}{7^{s}}+\cdots.

is the Dirichlet beta series. It remains to analyse
F(s)
. By the integral formula for the gamma function (
A.2
) we have

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi x(j^{2}+k^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi xj^{2}}\right)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Now apply the modular transformation for theta functions (
A.12
) to obtain

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi j^{2}/x}\right)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)e^{-\pi N/x}\ \;\mathrm{d}x

where
r_{2}(N)
is the number of representations of
N
as a sum of two squares, e.g., see (
A.13
). Now separate out the
N=0
term and
evaluate the resulting integrals. We find that

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}-\pi N/x}\ \;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(s-1)\zeta(2s-2)}{A^{s-1}\pi^{s-1}}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{N}{Ai^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{AN}\right)

where we have used the formula (
A.3
) for the
K
-Bessel function.
On using all of the above back in (
4.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}=4\zeta(s)L_{-4}(s)+\frac{2\pi}{(s-1)}\frac{\zeta(2s-2)}{A^{s-1}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\,A^{(1-s)/2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{N}{i^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{AN}\right).

This is essentially Selberg and Chowla’s formula
[
19
, (45)]
.
They write it in a slightly different form in terms of a sum over the divisors of
N
to minimise the number of
Bessel function evaluations. We will leave it as it is for simplicity.

4.2.
A second formula for the sum
T_{1}(A;s)

This time we split the terms according as in (
4.6
) and start with

(4.8)

T_{1}(A;s)=2g(s)+G(s)

where

\displaystyle g(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(Ai^{2})^{s}}=A^{-s}\,\zeta(2s)

and

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{j,k}}^{\,\prime}\;\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

Now apply the integral formula for the gamma
function (
A.2
) and then the modular transformation for the theta function (
A.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)G(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi i^{2}Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\,{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi i^{2}/Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Separate the
i=0
term, to get

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)G(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\,{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\,{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi i^{2}/Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The first integral can be evaluated in terms of the gamma function by (
A.2
), while the second integral
can be expressed in terms of the modified Bessel function by (
A.3
). The result is

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)G(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{A}\,\pi^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\;{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}\;\frac{1}{(j^{2}+k^{2})^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{A^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\,{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{j^{2}+k^{2}}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{\frac{j^{2}+k^{2}}{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sqrt{A}}\,{\pi^{-(s-\frac{1}{2})}}\,\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\zeta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,L_{-4}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{A^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\right).

On using all of the above back in (
4.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{1}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

\displaystyle=2A^{-s}\zeta(2s)+4\,\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{A}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s)}\,\zeta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,L_{-4}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{A^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\,\frac{\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\,\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\right).

The terms in (
4.7
) involve
K_{s-1}
Bessel functions whereas
K_{s-1/2}
Bessel functions occur in (
4.9
).
That is because each application of the theta function transformation formula lowers the subscript in the resulting
Bessel function by
1/2
, due to the creation of a
x^{-1/2}
factor in the integral.
The theta function transformation formula is used twice (i.e., the formula is squared) in the derivation of (
4.7
) and only once in the derivation of (
4.9
).

Each of (
4.7
) and (
4.9
) turns out to have its own advantages, as will be seen in Sections
6.4
and
6.3
.

4.3.
The alternating sum
T_{2}(A;s)

The analysis in the previous sections can be modified to handle the alternating series (
4.3
) which has
the term
(-1)^{i+j+k}
in the numerator, as follows.
Separating the terms according to whether
i=0
or
i\neq 0
gives

(4.10)

\displaystyle T_{2}(A;s)

\displaystyle=h(s)+2H(S)

where

\displaystyle h(s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{(-1)^{j+k}}{(j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

and

\displaystyle H(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i+j+k}}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}.

By a known result (
A.21
), we have

h(s)=-4(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)L_{-4}(s).

Next, by the integral formula for the gamma function (
A.2
) we have

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)H(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j+k}e^{-\pi x(j^{2}+k^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}e^{-\pi xj^{2}}\right)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Now apply the modular transformation for theta functions to obtain

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)H(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi(j+\frac{1}{2})^{2}/x}\right)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

By formula (
A.14
) this can be expressed as

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)H(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}r_{2}(4N+1)e^{-\pi(4N+1)/2x}\ \;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}r_{2}(4N+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}e^{-\pi Axi^{2}-\pi(4N+1)/2x}\ \;\mathrm{d}x.

The integral can be expressed in terms of Bessel functions by (
A.3
) to give

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)H(s)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}r_{2}(4N+1)\left(\frac{2N+\frac{1}{2}}{Ai^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{A(2N+\frac{1}{2})}\right).

On using all of the above back in (
4.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{(-1)^{i+j+k}}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

\displaystyle=-4(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)L_{-4}(s)

(4.11)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\,A^{(1-s)/2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}r_{2}(4N+1)\left(\frac{2N+\frac{1}{2}}{i^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(2\pi i\sqrt{A(2N+\frac{1}{2})}\right).

4.4.
A second formula for the alternating sum
T_{2}(A;s)

This time we separate the terms
according to whether
(j,k)=(0,0)
or
(j,k)\neq(0,0)
and write

(4.12)

T_{2}(A;s)=2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(Ai^{2})^{s}}+J(s)

where

By (
A.30

It remains to analyse the sum for

. By the integral formula for the gamma function (

) we have

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)J(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}(-1)^{j+j}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}e^{-\pi i^{2}Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now apply the modular transformation (
A.11
) to obtain

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)J(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\,{\sum_{j,k}}^{\;\prime}(-1)^{j+k}e^{-\pi(j^{2}+k^{2})x}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi(i+\frac{1}{2})^{2}/Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now put

to deduce

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)J(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{A}}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{N}r_{2}(N)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\,e^{-\pi Nx-\pi(i-\frac{1}{2})^{2}/Ax}\,\mathrm{d}x.

) to give

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)J(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{A^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\,\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{N}\,r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{i-\frac{1}{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi(i-\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j,k}}^{\prime}\;\;\;\frac{(-1)^{i+j+k}}{(Ai^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2})^{s}}

\displaystyle=-2A^{-s}(1-2^{1-{2s}})\zeta(2s)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{A^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\,\frac{\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\,\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{N}\,r_{2}(N)\left(\frac{i-\frac{1}{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi(i-\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\right).

5.

Because of its importance in solid state chemistry and physics, we give a similar analysis of the lattice sum for the hexagonal close packed structure given by

,

.
We shall analyse

5.1.
The sum
S_{1}(s)

Break the sum for
S_{1}(s)
into two, according to whether

or
k\neq 0
. This gives

(5.1)

S_{1}(s)=f(s)+2F(s)

and

By (
A.33
) we have

f(s)=6\zeta(s)\,L_{-3}(s)

where

L_{-3}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sin(2k\pi/3)}{\sin(2\pi/3)}\right)\frac{1}{k^{s}}=\frac{1}{1^{s}}-\frac{1}{2^{s}}+\frac{1}{4^{s}}-\frac{1}{5^{s}}+\cdots.

It remains to calculate

.
Applying the gamma function integral (
A.2
) followed by the theta function transformation formula (

),
we obtain

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi k^{2}x/3}\sum_{i,j}e^{-2\pi(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi k^{2}x/3}\sum_{i,j}e^{-2\pi(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})/3x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now separate out the
i=j=0

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}e^{-16\pi k^{2}x/3}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\;{\sum_{i,j}}^{\;\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}e^{-16\pi k^{2}x/3-2\pi(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})/3x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{3}{16\pi}\right)^{s-1}\Gamma(s-1)\zeta(2s-2)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\sum_{i,j}}^{\;\prime}\left(\frac{i^{2}+ij+j^{2}}{8k^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(\frac{8\pi k}{3}\sqrt{2(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle S_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=6\zeta(s)\,L_{-3}(s)+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{s-1}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)\zeta(2s-2)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}\,\frac{\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\sum_{i,j}}^{\;\prime}\left(\frac{i^{2}+ij+j^{2}}{2k^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(\frac{8\pi k}{3}\sqrt{2(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=6\zeta(s)\,L_{-3}(s)+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{s-1}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)\zeta(2s-2)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}\,\frac{\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}u_{2}(N)\left(\frac{N}{2k^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(\frac{8\pi k}{3}\sqrt{2N}\right)

where

is the number of representations of

by the form

\displaystyle\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)G(s)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\,\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{(2s+1)/4}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}u_{2}(N)\,\left(\frac{k^{2}}{N}\right)^{(2s-1)/4}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\pi k\sqrt{\frac{3N}{2}}\right).

5.3.
The sum

The analysis in this case is a little simpler because

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi(k+\frac{1}{2})^{2}x/3}\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi((i+\frac{1}{3})^{2}+(i+\frac{1}{3})(j+\frac{1}{3})+(j+\frac{1}{3})^{2})x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now make use of the transformation formula (

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\left(2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi(k+\frac{1}{2})^{2}x/3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{x\sqrt{3}}\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{i-j}e^{-2\pi(i^{2}+ij+j^{2})/3x}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi(k+\frac{1}{2})^{2}x/3}\;\;\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\omega^{N}\,u_{2}(N)\,e^{-2\pi N/3x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle S_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}\,\frac{\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\omega^{N}\,u_{2}(N)\,\left(\frac{N}{2(k+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\right)^{(s-1)/2}K_{s-1}\left(\frac{8\pi\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{3}\sqrt{2N}\right).

5.4.
A second formula for the sum

We introduce the abbreviation

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi Y_{i,j}x}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-16\pi(k+\frac{1}{2})^{2}x/3}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This time we apply the transformation formula (

\displaystyle S_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-3/2}\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi Y_{i,j}x}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,e^{-3\pi k^{2}/16x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now separate the terms according to whether

or

and evaluate the resulting integrals by (

) and (

\displaystyle S_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{8}}\,\frac{\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}\,\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{Y_{ij}^{s-1/2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{(2s+1)/4}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{Y_{ij}}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\pi k\sqrt{3Y_{i,j}/2}\right).

\displaystyle S_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{27\pi}{8}}\;\frac{\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}\;(3^{s-1/2}-1)\;\zeta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{-3}\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{N+\frac{1}{3}}}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\pi k\sqrt{\frac{3N+1}{2}}\right).

]
.

6.1.
Behaviour of the lattice sums at
s=3/2

We start by showing that
L(A;s)
has a simple pole at
s=3/2
and determine the residue.
In the formula (
4.14
), all of the terms are analytic at
s=3/2
except for the term involving
\zeta(2s-2)
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 3/2}(s-3/2)L(A;s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\rightarrow 3/2}(s-3/2)\frac{\pi A}{s-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)^{s}\zeta(2s-2)

\displaystyle=2\pi A\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)^{3/2}\lim_{s\rightarrow 3/2}(s-3/2)\zeta(2s-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\times\frac{1}{2}\;\lim_{u\rightarrow 1}(u-1)\zeta(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}

where (
A.23
) was used in the last step of the calculation.
It follows further that
L(A;s)
has a simple pole at
s=3/2
and the residue is given by

\text{Res}(L(A;s),3/2)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}.

By (
2.8
) this is just 12 times the packing density, i.e.,

\text{Res}(L(A;s),3/2)=12\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}.

For example, taking
A=1
gives

(6.1)

\text{Res}(L_{3}^{\text{FCC}}(s),3/2)=2\sqrt{2}\,\pi

while taking
A=1/2
gives

\text{Res}(L_{3}^{\text{BCC}}(s),3/2)=3\sqrt{3}\,\pi/2.

Laurent’s theorem implies there is an expansion of the form

(6.2)

L(A;s)=\frac{c_{-1}}{s-3/2}+c_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}(s-3/2)^{n}

where

c_{-1}=\text{Res}(L(A;s),3/2)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}

and the coefficients
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2},\ldots
depend on
A
but not on
s
.
To calculate
c_{0}
, start with the fact that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 3/2}\left(\frac{\pi A}{s-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)^{s}\zeta(2s-2)-\frac{c_{-1}}{s-3/2}\right)

=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\left(2\gamma-2+\log\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)\right)

where
\gamma=0.57721\,56649\,01532\,86060\,\cdots
is Euler’s constant. Then use (
4.14
) and (
A.5
) to deduce

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\rightarrow 3/2}\left(L(A;s)-\frac{c_{-1}}{s-3/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\zeta\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)L_{-4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\left(2\gamma-2+\log\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,r_{2}(N)\exp\left(-2\pi k\sqrt{AN}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\,r_{2}(4N+1)\,\exp\left(-2\pi k\sqrt{A\left(2N+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right).

Interchanging the order of summation and evaluating the sum over
k
gives

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\zeta\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)L_{-4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\left(2\gamma-2+\log\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}r_{2}(N)\log\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{AN}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A}}\left(A+1\right)^{3/2}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}r_{2}(4N+1)\,\log\left(1+e^{-\pi\sqrt{2A\left(4N+1\right)}}\right).

Numerical evaluation in the case
A=1
gives

(6.3)

\left.c_{0}\right|_{A=1}=6.98405\,25503\,22247\,93406\cdots.

A similar analysis can be given for
L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s)
using (
5.7
). We omit the details of the calculations as they are similar to the above.
The end result is a Laurent expansion of the form

(6.4)

L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s)=\frac{d_{-1}}{s-3/2}+d_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}(s-3/2)^{n}

where

(6.5)

d_{-1}=\text{Res}(L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s),3/2)=2\sqrt{2}\pi

and

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle=6\zeta\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)L_{-3}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\;+\;2\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi\left(2\gamma-2+\log\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{2}\pi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,u_{2}(N)\exp\left(-\frac{8}{3}\pi k\sqrt{2N}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{2}\pi\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos\left(\frac{2\pi N}{3}\right)}{(k+\frac{1}{2})}\,u_{2}(N)\,\exp\left(-\frac{8}{3}\pi\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\sqrt{2N}\right)

\displaystyle=6\zeta\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)L_{-3}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\;+\;2\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi\left(2\gamma-2+\log\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2\sqrt{2}\pi\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}u_{2}(N)\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{8}{3}\pi\sqrt{2N}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{2}\pi\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi N}{3}\right)\,u_{2}(N)\,\log\left(\frac{1+e^{-\frac{4}{3}\pi\sqrt{2N}}}{1-e^{-\frac{4}{3}\pi\sqrt{2N}}}\right)

(6.6)

\displaystyle=6.98462\;37414\;38416\;61307\;\cdots.

In particular, the pole of
L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s)
at
s=3/2
is simple.
By (
6.1
) and (
6.5
) we have

\text{Res}(L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s),3/2)=\text{Res}(L_{3}^{\text{FCC}}(s),3/2).

It follows that the difference

has a removable singularity at

and from the Laurent expansions we deduce that

\lim_{s\rightarrow\frac{3}{2}}\left(L_{3}^{\text{FCC}}(s)-L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s)\right)=\left.c_{0}\right|_{A=1}-d_{0}.

Using the numerical values from (
6.3
) and (
6.6
) we obtain

\lim_{s\rightarrow\frac{3}{2}}\left(L_{3}^{\text{FCC}}(s)-L_{3}^{\text{HCP}}(s)\right)=-0.00057\;11911\;16168\;67901\cdots.

This gives the value at the left hand end of the graph in
[

, Fig. 3]
. The value
s=3/2
used here corresponds to taking
s=3
in
[

]

because of the different way the exponents are used in the definitions.

\frac{\pi A}{s-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)^{s}\zeta(2s-2).

The function in (

due to the pole of
\zeta(s)
, as the function

and the exponential function are both entire.
The function in (

) is
analytic except at

and

. We studied the singularity at

in the previous section, so this
leaves only the point

. Using (

) and the values of

and

in (

) and (

) we find that

and

\frac{\pi A}{s-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{A}\right)^{s}\zeta(2s-2)=-\frac{(A+1)\pi}{2(s-1)}+O(1)\quad\text{as $s\rightarrow 1$}.

It follows that the sum of the functions in (

) and (
6.8
) has a removable singularity at

is also analytic at
s=1

In conclusion, it has been shown that

has an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function of

By the theory of complex variables, the analytic continuation, if one exists, is unique, e.g., see

Therefore analytic continuation formulas can be used to assign values to divergent series.

A.1.
The gamma function

The gamma function may be defined for

(A.1)

\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\,e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t.

By the change of variable

(A.2)

\frac{1}{w^{s}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\,e^{-wx}\,\mathrm{d}x.

See
[

A.2.
The modified Bessel function

The following integral may be evaluated in terms of the modified Bessel function:

(A.3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-ax-b/x}\mathrm{d}x=2\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{s/2}K_{s}(2\sqrt{ab}).

By the change of variable

it can be shown that

(A.4)

K_{s}(z)=K_{-s}(z).

When

the modified Bessel function reduces to an elementary function:

(A.5)

K_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,e^{-z}.

The asymptotic formula holds:

(A.6)

K_{s}(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,e^{-z}\quad\text{as}\quad z\rightarrow\infty,\quad(\,|\text{arg}\,z|<3\pi/2).

For all of these properties, see

, pp. 223, 237]

Appendix B
Behaviour as
A\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
A\rightarrow+\infty

We briefly consider the behaviour of the lattices in the limiting cases
A\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
A\rightarrow+\infty
.
Some of the basis vectors become infinite in the limit, leaving a sublattice of lower dimension. We discuss each case

A\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
A\rightarrow+\infty
both in terms of theta functions
and then in terms of the basis vectors.

First, consider the limit
A\rightarrow 0^{+}
. In the interval
0<A<1/3
the theta function is

\displaystyle\theta(A;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{g(A;i,j,k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2})/4A}.

As
A\rightarrow 0^{+}
we have

q^{(j+k)^{2}/4A}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad q^{(i+k)^{2}/4A}\rightarrow 0

unless
j=-k
and
i=-k
, respectively. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{A\rightarrow 0^{+}}\theta(A;q)

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=-k}\sum_{j=-k}q^{(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2})/4A}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{A\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{A(-k-k)^{2}/4A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{k^{2}}.

This corresponds to the one-dimensional lattice with minimal distance
1
. The kissing number is 2, which is in agreement with the other lattices
in the range
0<A<1/3
, as indicated in Table 1.
In terms of the basis vectors, from (
2.1
) we have

\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}},0\right)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}}\right)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=\left(0,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}},\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}}\right)^{\top}.

The only linear combinations
\boldsymbol{v}=i\boldsymbol{b}_{1}+j\boldsymbol{b}_{2}+k\boldsymbol{b}_{3}
(for
i,j,k\in\mathbf{Z}
) that remain finite in the limit
A\rightarrow 0^{+}
occur when
i=-k
,
j=-k
in which case we
obtain

\boldsymbol{v}=-k\boldsymbol{b}_{1}-k\boldsymbol{b}_{2}+k\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=-k(1,0,0)^{\top}.

That is, the limiting lattice is just the one-dimensional lattice consisting of integer multiples of
(1,0,0)^{\top}
.

Now consider the limit
A\rightarrow+\infty
. For
A>1
the theta function is

\displaystyle\theta(A;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{g(A;i,j,k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2})/2}.

Since
q^{A(i+j)^{2}/2}\rightarrow 0
as
A\rightarrow+\infty
unless
i=-j
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{A\rightarrow+\infty}\theta(A;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=-j}q^{(A(i+j)^{2}+(j+k)^{2}+(i+k)^{2})/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{((j+k)^{2}+(-j+k)^{2})/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{j^{2}+k^{2}}.

This is the theta series for the two-dimensional square close packing lattice with minimal distance
1
. The kissing number is
4
, in agreement
with other values in the range
A>1
given by Table 1.
In terms of the basis vectors, from (
2.1
) we have

\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sqrt{A},1,0)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sqrt{A},0,1)^{\top},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,1)^{\top}.

The only linear combinations
\boldsymbol{v}=i\boldsymbol{b}_{1}+j\boldsymbol{b}_{2}+k\boldsymbol{b}_{3}
(for
i,j,k\in\mathbf{Z}
) that remain finite in the limit
A\rightarrow+\infty

occur when
i=-j
, in which case we obtain

\boldsymbol{v}=-j\boldsymbol{b}_{1}+j\boldsymbol{b}_{2}+k\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[j(0,-1,1)^{\top}+k(0,1,1)^{\top}\right].

This is isomorphic to the two-dimensional square close packing lattice with minimal distance
1
, rotated from the coordinate axes by 45 degrees.

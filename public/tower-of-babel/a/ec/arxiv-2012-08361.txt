Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to extend the work of Voevodsky and Morel on the homotopy t t -structure on the category of motivic complexes to the context of motives for logarithmic schemes. To do so, we prove an analogue of Morel’s connectivity theorem and show a purity statement for ( 𝐏 1 , ∞ ) (\mathbf{P}^{1},\infty) -local complexes of sheaves with log transfers. The homotopy t t -structure on 𝐥𝐨𝐠𝐃𝐌 eff ⁡ ( k ) \operatorname{\mathbf{logDM}^{eff}}(k) is proved to be compatible with Voevodsky’s t t -structure i.e. we show that the comparison functor R □ ¯ ​ ω ∗ : 𝐃𝐌 eff ⁡ ( k ) → 𝐥𝐨𝐠𝐃𝐌 eff ⁡ ( k ) R^{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{*}\colon\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}^{eff}}(k)\to\operatorname{\mathbf{logDM}^{eff}}(k) is t t -exact. The heart of the homotopy t t -structure on 𝐥𝐨𝐠𝐃𝐌 eff ⁡ ( k ) \operatorname{\mathbf{logDM}^{eff}}(k) is the Grothendieck abelian category of strictly cube-invariant sheaves with log transfers: we use it to build a new version of the category of reciprocity sheaves in the style of Kahn–Saito–Yamazaki and Rülling.



1.
Introduction

Voevodsky’s category of motivic complexes over a perfect field
k
is based on a simple idea: most cohomology theories for smooth
k
-schemes are insensitive to the affine line, i.e. they satisfy
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-homotopy invariance. This observation led Voevodsky to introduce as a building block of his theory of motives the category of homotopy invariant sheaves with transfers
\operatorname{\mathbf{HI}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(k)
, that is, sheaves
F
for the Nisnevich topology defined on the category of finite correspondences over
k
such that
F(X\times\mathbf{A}^{1})\xrightarrow{\simeq}F(X)
for every smooth
k
-scheme
X
. These sheaves enjoy many nice properties: the category
\operatorname{\mathbf{HI}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(k)
is a Grothendieck abelian subcategory of the category
\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k)
of Nisnevich sheaves with transfers, closed under extensions and equipped with a (closed) symmetric monoidal structure
\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathbf{HI}}}
. Moreover, a celebrated theorem of Voevodsky shows that the cohomology presheaves
H^{n}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(-,F)
of a homotopy invariant sheaf with transfers
F
are still
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-homotopy invariant. In fact,
\operatorname{\mathbf{HI}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(k)
can be identified with the heart of a certain
t
-structure on the triangulated category
\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}^{eff}}(k)
, induced by the standard
t
-structure on the derived category
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k))
and called by Voevodsky the
homotopy
t
-structure
. The
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-invariance of the cohomology of homotopy invariant sheaves can be rephrased by saying that a sheaf
F\in\operatorname{\mathbf{HI}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(k)
, seen as object of
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k))
, is
local
with respect to the Bousfield localization of
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k))
over the complexes
(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{X})[n]\to X[n]
for
X\in\mathbf{Sm}(k)
.

Much work has been done around the homotopy
t
-structure, including Déglise’s extension to the non-effective version of
\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}^{eff}}(k)
and the identification of its heart with the category of Rost’s cycle modules
[
Dég11
]
, and Morel’s work on the stable homotopy category
\mathbf{SH}(k)

[
Mor05
]
. In informal terms, we can interpret the existence of the homotopy
t
-structure as a manifestation of the interplay between the Postnikov truncation functors
\tau_{\leq n}
and the
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-localization functor on the derived category
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k))
. This interplay is precisely expressed by Morel’s connectivity theorem.

Voevodsky’s category of motives over a field has been recently extended to the setting of logarithmic algebraic geometry in
[
BPØ
]
. The basic objects in this context are no longer smooth
k
-schemes but rather fine and saturated log schemes, log smooth over a base considered with trivial log structure (typically, the base is a perfect field). The Nisnevich topology on the underlying schemes defines naturally a topology, called the
strict Nisnevich topology
,
{\operatorname{sNis}}
for short. This topology is not enough to guarantee that the resulting category of motives satisfies a number of nice properties, and needs to be replaced with a subtle variant, the
dividing Nisnevich topology
,
{\operatorname{dNis}}
for short, with additional covers given by certain blow-ups with center in the support of the log structure. The affine line
\mathbf{A}^{1}
is replaced by its compactified avatar, i.e. the log scheme
{\overline{\square}}=(\mathbf{P}^{1},\infty)
obtained by considering the compactifying log structure along the embedding
\mathbf{A}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. The category of log motives
\operatorname{\mathbf{logDM}^{eff}}(k,\Lambda)
(with transfers) is then defined as the homotopy category of the
({\operatorname{dNis}},{\overline{\square}})
-local model structure on the category of (unbounded) chain complexes of presheaves with logarithmic transfers,
\mathbf{C}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}^{\rm ltr}(k,\Lambda))
for
\Lambda
a ring of coefficients. See
[
BPØ
, 4-5]
and Section
2
below for more details. The variant without transfers will be denoted
\operatorname{\mathbf{logDA}^{eff}}(k,\Lambda)
, and it is obtained as Bousfield localization of the category of (unbounded) chain complexes of presheaves
\mathbf{C}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}^{\rm log}(k,\Lambda))
.

The goal of this paper is to develop in the logarithmic context the analogue of Voevodsky’s homotopy
t
-structure, and to derive some consequences from this. As discussed above, the homotopy
t
-structure on (usual) motives is induced by the standard
t
-structure on the derived category of sheaves. In order to restrict this
t
-structure to the subcategory of local objects, one needs to understand how much connectivity (with respect to the homology sheaves) is lost after taking a fibrant replacement for the
(\mathbf{A}^{1},{\operatorname{Nis}})
-local model structure. This is the content of Morel’s connectivity theorem
[
Mor05
, Thm. 6.1.8]
.

A modulus pair

Given two modulus pairs

is also abelian, where

Definition 2.1

Proposition 2.2
.

Remark 2.3

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{X}}-D_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{Y}}-D_{Y}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(X)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(Y^{+})}

2.2.
log correspondences

Following
[
BPØ
]
, we denote by
\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
the category of finite log correspondences over
k
. It is a variant of the Suslin–Voevodsky category of finite correspondences
\operatorname{\mathbf{Cor}}(k)
introduced in
[
Voe00
]
, see
[
MVW06
]
. It has the same objects as
\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(k)
, and morphisms are given by the free abelian subgroup

\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(X,Y)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbf{Cor}}(X-\partial X,Y-\partial Y)

generated by elementary correspondences
V^{o}\subset(X-\partial X)\times(Y-\partial Y)
such that the closure
V\subset{\underline{X}}\times{\underline{Y}}
is finite and surjective over (a component of)
{\underline{X}}
and such that there exists a morphism of log schemes
V^{N}\to Y
, where
V^{N}
is the fs log scheme whose underlying scheme is the normalization of
V
and whose log structure is given by the inverse image log structure along the composition
\underline{V^{N}}\to{\underline{X}}\times{\underline{Y}}\to{\underline{X}}
. See
[
BPØ
, 2.1]
for more details, and for the proof that this definition gives indeed a category.

Additive presheaves (of
\Lambda
-modules) on the category
\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
will be called
presheaves (of
\Lambda
-modules) with log transfers
. Write
\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{ltr}}(k,\Lambda)
for the resulting category. We have a natural adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{log}}(k,\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{ltr}}(k,\Lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{*}}

where by convention
\gamma_{\sharp}
is left adjoint to
\gamma^{*}
, which is left adjoint to
\gamma_{*}
. Here
\gamma\colon\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(k)\to\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
is the graph functor. For a topology
\tau
on
\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(k)
, a presheaf with log transfers
F
is a
\tau
-sheaf if
\gamma^{*}F
is a
\tau
-sheaf. We denote by
\mathbf{Shv}_{\tau}^{\rm ltr}(k,\Lambda)\subset\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{ltr}}(k,\Lambda)
the subcategory of
\tau
-sheaves. By
[
BPØ
, Prop. 4.5.4]
and
[
BPØ
, Thm. 4.5.7]
, the strict Nisnevich and the dividing Nisnevich topology on
\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(k)
are compatible with log transfers: this means in particular that the inclusion
\mathbf{Shv}_{\tau}^{\rm ltr}(k,\Lambda)\subset\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{ltr}}(k,\Lambda)
admits an exact left adjoint
a_{\tau}
(see
[
BPØ
, Prop. 4.2.10]
), and that the category
\mathbf{Shv}_{\tau}^{\rm ltr}(k,\Lambda)
is a Grothendieck Abelian category (
[
BPØ
, Prop. 4.2.12]
).

2.3.
Effective log motives

We fix again a Noetherian fs log scheme
S
and a field
k
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be either
\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(S)
or
\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
. We start by recalling some standard facts.
The category
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
of unbounded complexes of presheaves is equipped with the usual global (projective) model structure
(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Fib})
, where the weak equivalences are the quasi-isomorphisms and the fibrations are the degreewise surjective maps (see, for example, the remark after
[
HPS97
, Thm. 9.3.1]
or
[
Ayo07
, Proposition 4.4.16]
).

Let
\tau
be a topology on
\mathcal{C}
(and we require that
\tau
is compatible with transfers when
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
). Recall that a morphism of complexes of presheaves
F\to G
in
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
is called a
\tau
-local equivalence if it induces isomorphisms
a_{\tau}H_{i}(F)\simeq a_{\tau}H_{i}(G)
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
H_{i}(F)
denotes the
i
-th homology presheaf of
F
.

The left Bousfield localization of the global model structure on
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
with respect to the class of
\tau
-local equivalences exists and the resulting model structure
(\mathbf{W}_{\tau},\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Fib}_{\tau})
is called the
\tau
-
local
model structure (see, for example,
[
Ayo07
, Prop. 4.4.31]
). The maps in
\mathbf{W}_{\tau}
are precisely the
\tau
-local equivalences. It is well known that the homotopy category of
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
with respect to the local model structure, denoted
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}_{\tau}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
, is equivalent to the unbounded derived category
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
of the Grothendieck abelian category of
\tau
-sheaves
\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda)
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{C}
, we write

R\Gamma_{\tau}(X,-)\colon{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}_{\tau}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))\to{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}(\Lambda)

for the right derived functor of the global section functor
\Gamma(X,-)
. The
\tau
-(hyper) cohomology of
X
with values in a complex of presheaves
C
is then computed as

\mathbf{H}^{*}_{\tau}(X,a_{\tau}(C))=\mathbf{H}^{*}(R\Gamma_{\tau}(X,a_{\tau}C)).

Finally, let
{\overline{\square}}_{S}:=(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{S},\infty_{S})\in\mathcal{C}
, with
S=\operatorname{Spec}(k)
if
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{\mathbf{lCor}}(k)
.

Definition 2.7
.

The
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local model structure on
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda))
is the (left) Bousfield localization of the
\tau
-local model structure with respect to the class of maps

\Lambda({\overline{\square}}_{S}\times_{S}X)[n]\to\Lambda(X)[n]

for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

General properties of the Bousfield localization (see e.g.
[

\textstyle{A\otimes\Lambda(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.11

Let

We end this section with a computation of the localization functor

An explicit description of the localization functor has been worked out by Ayoub in
[

, Section 2]
for the

-localization. We spell out the construction for presheaves without transfers and for

Construction 2.13
.

(see
[
Ayo21
, Construction 2.6]
)
We fix an endofunctor
(-)_{\tau}
which gives a
\tau
-fibrant replacement
. Let
\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S})
be the kernel of the map
\Lambda({\overline{\square}}_{S})\to\Lambda
. For a complex

we put

\Phi(C):=\textrm{Cone}\bigl\{\delta:\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S})\otimes_{\Lambda}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S}(\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S}),C_{\tau})\to C_{\tau}\bigl\}

where
\delta
is the counit of the adjuntion
\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S})\otimes_{\Lambda}\_\dashv\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S}(\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S}),\_)
.

We obtain an endofunctor
\Phi
equipped with a natural transformation
\varphi\colon id\to\Phi
, and we define the endofunctor
\Phi^{\infty}
by taking the colimit of the following sequence:

C\xrightarrow{\varphi_{C}}\Phi(C)\xrightarrow{\varphi_{\Phi(C)}}\Phi^{\circ 2}(L)\xrightarrow{\varphi_{\Phi^{\circ 2}(C)}}\ldots\to\Phi^{\circ n}(C)\to\ldots

By construction, the functor
\Phi^{\infty}
comes equipped with a natural transformation
\varphi^{\infty}\colon id\to\Phi^{\infty}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

(see
[
Ayo21
, Théorème 2.7]
)
Let
C\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{log}}(S,\Lambda))
. Then
\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-fibrant and
\varphi^{\infty}
is a
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local equivalence. In other words, the pair
(\Phi^{\infty},\varphi^{\infty})
is naturally isomorphic to the
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-localization
(L,\lambda)
.

Proof.

We follow the same pattern of the proof in
[
Ayo21
]
, and we divide the proof in two steps. First, we need to show that for any complex of presheaves
C
, the morphism
C\to\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is a
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local equivalence. After that, we have to prove that
\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is fibrant for the
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local model structure.

We begin by observing that for all
F\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Cpx}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{log}}(S,\Lambda))
, the tensor product
\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}})\otimes_{\Lambda}F
is
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-locally acyclic (see Def.
2.8
). Indeed, the subcategory of
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-locally acyclic complexes is a triangulated subcategory of
{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}_{\tau}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}^{log}}(S,\Lambda))
which is stable by direct sums, and by construction it contains all the objects of the form
\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S})\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda(X)
for any
X\in\operatorname{\mathbf{lSm}}(S)
.

Next, note that since the homotopy fiber of
\varphi_{C}
is given by

\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S})\otimes_{\Lambda}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S}(\Lambda({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S}),C_{\tau}),

which is then
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-locally acyclic in virtue of what we just observed,
\varphi_{C}
is a
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local equivalence for all complexes
C
.
Since filtered colimits preserve
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local equivalences, we conclude that the map
C\to\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is a
(\tau,{\overline{\square}}_{S})
-local equivalence.

We move to the second part of the proof. By construction, the map

factors through
\Phi^{\circ n}(C)_{\tau}
, which are by construction
\tau
-fibrant. Hence
\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is a filtered colimit of
\tau
-fibrant objects.

By Lemma
2.15
below, filtered colimits preserve
\tau
-fibrant objects, hence
\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is
{\operatorname{dNis}}

Finally, we need to show that
\Phi^{\infty}(C)
is
{\overline{\square}}_{S}
-local, which is equivalent to show that
\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S}({\overline{\square}}^{\textrm{red}}_{S},\Phi^{\infty}(C))
is acyclic. The argument in the proof of part (B) of
[
Ayo21
, Thm. 2.7]
goes through without changes. We leave the verification to the reader.
∎

Proposition 5.6

Theorem 5.7
.

Consider the inclusions

(5.7.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{logDA}^{eff}}(k,\Lambda)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathbf{D}}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{PSh}}^{\rm log}(k,\Lambda))

(5.7.2)

{\overline{\square}}
-local complexes. Then the standard

(resp.

Proof.

The first assertion follows directly from Theorem
5.1
(resp. Corollary
5.2
), the second from Corollary
5.5
and Proposition

. The fact that the heart of a

]

Next, note that the homotopy

[

, Definition 1.4.4.12]

Moreover, filtered colimits commute with cohomology, hence if

fibrant objects, then

In particular, as observed in

Proposition 5.8
.

The inclusions
i\colon\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\rm log}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}_{dNis}^{log}}(k,\Lambda)

We prove the assertion for
\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\rm log}

\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\rm ltr}

By the Special Adjoint Functor Theorem (see

Let

and

denote the
{\operatorname{dNis}}
-fibrant replacements. Since

and

are strictly
{\overline{\square}}
-local,
F_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}
and
G_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}
are
({\operatorname{dNis}},{\overline{\square}})
-fibrant, hence
\textrm{Cofib}(F_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}\to G_{{\operatorname{dNis}}})

({\operatorname{dNis}},{\overline{\square}})
-fibrant.

In particular,

\textrm{coker}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}}(F\to G)\simeq a_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}H_{0}(\textrm{Cofib}(F_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}\to G_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}))\\
\simeq a_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}H_{0}(L^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\textrm{Cofib}(F_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}\to G_{{\operatorname{dNis}}})))\overset{(*)}{\simeq}\textrm{coker}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}^{\mathrm{log}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}}(F\to G),

5.6

∎

6.

In this section, we discuss some applications to the theory of reciprocity sheaves. As above, for
X\in\operatorname{\mathbf{SmlSm}}(k)

be the strict normal crossing divisor supporting the log structure of

\operatorname{\mathbf{MCor}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{SmlSm}}(k)

Here,

is the functor defined in
[
KSY
, Prop. 2.3.7]
(see also
[
KSY
, Thm. 2.4.1-2.4.2]
and
6.3
below), and
\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}
is the subcategory of
{\overline{\square}}
-invariant Nisnevich sheaves on
\operatorname{\mathbf{MCor}}
, defined in
[
KMSY21a
]
(not to be confused with
\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\rm ltr}
introduced in the present paper). By
[
Sai20b
, Thm. 0.2]
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{L}\textit{og}}
is fully faithful and exact.

Proposition 6.1
.

The essential image of

is a subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}
.

Proof.

By
[

, Theorem 4.1]
we have that for

then

{\overline{\square}}
-invariant.
∎

One can wonder if the two categories agree, i.e. if

is essentially surjective onto
\operatorname{\mathbf{CI}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}
. This is not the case, as the following example indicates.

Example 6.2
.

See
[
Ayo21
, Proposition 3.5]

Let

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}\textit{og}}(\mathbf{G}_{a})(X)=\Gamma(\underline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}})

By e.g.
[

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}\textit{og}}(\mathbf{G}_{a})\to I^{\bullet}

-sheaves. Thus, for all

\prod_{A}\operatorname{\mathcal{L}\textit{og}}(\mathbf{G}_{a})\to\prod_{A}I^{\bullet}

\prod_{A}I^{\bullet}
is an injective resolution of

We conclude that

{\overline{\square}}

On the other hand, by

, Remark 6.1.2]
, if

is infinite

does not belong to
\operatorname{\mathbf{RSC}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}
.

6.3

We recall some further constructions from the theory of modulus (pre)sheaves with transfers. For

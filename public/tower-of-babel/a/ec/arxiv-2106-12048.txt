The Dirichlet problem for a family of totally degenerate differential operators Thanks: ⋆ Corresponding author.
Maria Manfredini a , Mirco Piccinini b,⋆ , Sergio Polidoro c

Abstract
. In the framework of the Potential Theory we prove existence and uniqueness for the Perron-Wiener-Brelot solution to the Dirichlet problem related to a family of totally degenerate, in the sense of Bony, differential operators. We also state and prove a Wiener-type criterium and an exterior cone condition for boundary regularity. Our results apply to a wide family of strongly degenerate operators that includes the following example ℒ = t 2 Δ x + ⟨ x , ∇ y ⟩ − ∂ t {\mathscr{L}}=t^{2}\Delta_{x}+\langle x,\nabla_{y}\rangle-\partial_{t} , for ( x , y , t ) ∈ ℝ N × ℝ N × ℝ (x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}} .



Keywords:

Degenerate Kolmogorov equations; Hypoelliptic equations; Boundary value problem, Perron-Wiener-Brelot solution, Boundary regularity.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
35K70; 35B65; 35K20; 31B20; 31B25; 31D05.

Declarations of interest:
none.

1.
Introduction

We consider second-order partial differential operators of the form

(1.1)

{\mathscr{L}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=t^{2\vartheta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}b_{ij}x_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{t},

where
z=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
m,N
and
\vartheta
are non-negative integers with
1\leq m\leq N
. Moreover,
b_{ij}
is a real constant for every
i,j=1,\dots,N
. The standing assumption of this article is:

[H.1]

The matrix
B\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(b_{ij})_{i,j=1,\ldots,N}
has the form

(1.2)

B=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}&\ldots&{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}\\
B_{1}&{\mathbb{O}}&\ldots&{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}\\
{\mathbb{O}}&B_{2}&\ldots&{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}&\ldots&B_{\kappa}&{\mathbb{O}}\end{pmatrix}

where every block
B_{j}
is a
m_{j}\times m_{j-1}
matrix of rank
m_{j}
with
j=1,2,\ldots,\kappa
. Moreover, every
m_{j}
is a positive integer such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle m_{0}\geq m_{1}\geq\ldots\geq m_{\kappa}\geq 1,\quad\textrm{and}\quad m_{0}+m_{1}+\ldots+m_{\kappa}=N.

We agree to let
m_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=m
to have a consistent notation, moreover every
{\mathbb{O}}
denotes a block matrix whose entries are zeros.

As we will see in the following Proposition
2.1
, the condition
[H.1]
implies that the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
is
hypoelliptic
. This means that, for every open set
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, every function
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(U)
, which solves the equation
{\mathscr{L}}u=f
in the distributional sense, belongs to
C^{\infty}(U)
whenever
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
. In particular,
u
is a classical solution to
{\mathscr{L}}u=f
.

In the framework of the Potential Theory, we address the boundary value problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}{\mathscr{L}}u=0\quad\text{in}\ U,\\[2.15277pt]
u_{|\partial U}={\varphi},\end{cases}

where
U
is any open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and
{\varphi}\in{C}_{c}(\partial U)
1
1

1

We indicate with
\textup{C}_{c}(\partial U)
the family of continuous functions on
\partial U
with compact support
. The Perron-Weiner-Brelot method provides us with a function
H^{U}_{\varphi}
which is a classical solution to
{\mathscr{L}}H^{U}_{\varphi}=0
in
U
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Every open set
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is resolutive, i.e. for every
{\varphi}\in{C}_{c}(\partial U)
it is defined the Perron-Weiner-Brelot solution
H^{U}_{\varphi}
to the problem (
1.4
). Moreover
H^{U}_{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(U)
is a classical solution to
{\mathscr{L}}H^{U}_{\varphi}=0
.

Concerning the boundary value datum, it is well known that solution
H^{U}_{\varphi}
to (
1.4
) does not attain the prescribed boundary datum at every point of
\partial U
. We say that a point
z_{0}\in\partial U
is
{\mathscr{L}}
-regular
if
H^{U}_{\varphi}(z)\to{\varphi}(z_{0})
as
z\to z_{0}
, for every
{\varphi}\in{C}_{c}(\partial U)
.
The second main result of this article is a Wiener criterium for the regularity of a boundary point
z_{0}\in\partial U
. Its statement requires some notation. For any fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the following set

(1.5)

U^{c}_{n}(z_{0})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\setminus U:\biggl(\frac{1}{\lambda}\biggr)^{n\log n}\!\!\!\!\leq\Gamma(z;z_{0})\leq\biggl(\frac{1}{\lambda}\biggr)^{(n+1)\log(n+1)}\right\}\cup\{z_{0}\}.

Here
\Gamma
is the fundamental solution of
{\mathscr{L}}
, whose explicit expression is given in (
2.19
). Moreover,
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}_{n}(z_{0})}
denotes the
balayage
of the constant function
1

U^{c}_{n}(z_{0})
(see Definition
3.21
). With this notation we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
be an open set and let
z_{0}\in\partial U
. Then
z_{0}
is a
{\mathscr{L}}
-regular point, if and only if

(1.6)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}_{n}(z_{0})}(z_{0})=+\,\infty.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the explicit expression of the fundamental solution
\Gamma
of
{\mathscr{L}}
and follows the lines of the work
[
12
]
of Kogoj, Lanconelli and Tralli, where the regularity of Kolmogorov operator (
1.1
) with
\vartheta=0
is studied. In particular the article
[
12
]
extends to degenerate Kolmogorov equations the Evans Gariepy’s Wiener test for the heat equation
[
9
]
, and a regularity criterion proved by Landis in
[
15
]
, which again holds for the heat equation.

Finally we give a Zaremba type criterion for the regularity of boundary points
z_{0}=(x_{0},0)
. It is a sufficient geometric condition for the regularity of a boundary point which relies on the definition of
cone

C_{z_{0}}
with vertex at
z_{0}

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
U
be an open set of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and let
z_{0}=(x_{0},0)\in\partial U
. If there exists an exterior cone
C_{z_{0}}
with vertex at

, then

is
{\mathscr{L}}
-regular.

Note that Proposition

extends the analogous result proved by Manfredini
[
18
]
for the case
\vartheta=0
. The requirement that the time coordinate of

is

is needed because the definition of the cone

requires a dilation-invariance property of

which, in the case

, is granted only for

[

applies to every point
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})

.

Let us briefly discuss Proposition
1.3
in the simplest case of
{\it m_{0}}=N
,
\vartheta=1
and
B=\mathbb{O}
, that is

(1.7)

{\mathscr{L}}=t^{2}\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t}.

In this setting the group of dilations is defined as

\delta(r)(x,t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(r^{3}x,r^{2}t),\qquad\mbox{for any}\,(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\mbox{and any}\penalty\ r>0,

and following Definition
5.5
the cone of vertex
z_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(x_{0},0)
, height
T>0
and base
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is given by

C_{z_{0}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{(x_{0}+r^{3}x,-r^{2}T):x\in K,0\leq r\leq 1\right\}.

Note that (
1.7
) can be reduced to the heat equation by the change of the time-scale
u(x,t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=v(x,t^{3}/3)
. The classical parabolic cone

\widetilde{C}_{z_{0}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{(x_{0}+rx,-r^{2}\tilde{T}):x\in\tilde{K},0\leq r\leq 1\right\},

introduced in
[
8
]
by Effros and Kazdan, guarantees the regularity of boundary point
z_{0}
for the solution
v
to problem (
1.4
) relevant to the heat operator. Inverting the time-scale change of variables defined above
\widetilde{C}_{z_{0}}
does coincide with
C_{z_{0}}
. However, this simple argument does not apply to ultraparabolic operators of the type (
1.1
). Hence, the result stated in Proposition
1.3
can not be proved trivially with a time-scale change of variables in the general setting we are dealing with.

We next give some comments about our main results. The first one concerns the uniqueness of the solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
). A first simple answer to the uniqueness problem plainly follows from the maximum principle (see Corollary
3.16
below). In particular, it implies that if
u
and
v
belong to
C(\overline{U})
, for some bounded open set
U
,
u
and
v
are both classical solutions to (
1.4
), and attain the same values on
\partial U
, then necessarily agree. This result is however unsatisfactory. Indeed, it is well known that, if we consider the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for the heat equation in a cylinder, then the solution is uniquely defined by the boundary condition on the
parabolic boundary
of the cylinder. For this reason, we would expect that only the regular boundary points need to be considered in order to have the uniqueness of the solution to (
1.4
). Unfortunately, this fact is not true even in the case of the heat equation. Indeed, Lukeš proves in Example 3.2 (D) of
[
16
]
that there exist bounded open sets that admit different solutions that agree at every regular boundary point.

The classical Perron method for the Laplace equation relies on the Poisson kernel, which provides us with the solution to the Dirichlet problem on any ball of the Euclidean space. In the more general setting of the abstract Potential Theory the Euclidean balls are replaced by the
resolutive
sets, that are sets such that the Perron-Wiener-Brelot solution is defined. Specifically, it is assumed that
there exists a family of resolutive open sets
\left\{U_{\imath}\right\}_{\imath\in\mathcal{I}}
, such that
\left\{U_{\imath}\right\}_{\imath\in\mathcal{I}}
is a basis for the topology of the space
. Note that, unlike in the case of the Laplace equation, it is not required that all the boundary points of a resolutive set are regular.

The development of the Potential Theory is simpler in the case of the existence of a basis of
regular sets
, which are resolutive sets whose all the boundary points are regular. For this reason, even in the case of the heat operator, an effort has be done in order to build a basis of regular sets for the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.
In particular, Bauer first pointed out in
[
3
]
that the cones defined for
(x_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, and
r>0
as

K_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})=\big\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\mid|x-x_{0}|<t_{0}-t<r\big\},

have this property. Later Effros and Kazdan introduce in
[
8
]
regular sets that are build as follows. Every set is the union of the cylinder

\widetilde{Q}_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})=\big\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\mid|x-x_{0}|<r,t_{0}-2r<t\leq t_{0}-r\big\},

and the cone
K_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
.
The regularity of the boundary points for the above families of sets is proved by a simple barrier argument, which relies on the fact that, for every point of the lateral boundary of the cone, the spatial component
\nu_{x}
of the outer normal
\nu=\left(\nu_{x},\nu_{t}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is non zero.

Bony considers in
[
4
]
the boundary value problem (
1.4
) for degenerate operators in the form

(1.8)

{\mathscr{L}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+Y,

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
and
Y
are vector fields defined in
\Omega
, with smooth coefficients, satisfying the Hörmander’s condition
[
10
]

(1.9)

{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y)(z)={\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},\qquad\text{for every}\quad z\in\Omega.

We recall that
{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y)
is the Lie algebra generated by the vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y
, that is the vector space generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y
and their commutators. The commutator of two given vector fields
W
and
Z
is the vector field defined as:

[W,Z]\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=W\,Z-Z\,W.

In his work, Bony restricts his study to
non totally degenerate
operators. This means that, for every
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, at least one of the vector fields
X_{1}(z),\ldots,X_{m}(z)
is non zero. The
non total degeneracy
of the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
allows Bony to build a family of bounded open regular sets by a general method that relies on a barrier argument. Note that for
\vartheta=0
the Bony’s theory applies to the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
. We refer the reader to
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
18
]
for the study of the relevant Dirichlet problem.

We remark that the non total degeneracy of the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
is a mild requirement. Indeed, from the very definition of commutator it follows that

W(z)=0\ \text{and}\ Z(z)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad[W,Z](z)=0,

thus

X_{1}(z)=0,\ldots,X_{m}(z)=0,Y(z)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y)(z)=\big\{0\big\}.

In particular, if
{\mathscr{L}}
satisfies the Hörmander’s condition, then at least one of the vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y
is different from zero. Concerning the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
, it can be written in the form (
1.8
) with

(1.10)

X_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=t^{\vartheta}\partial_{x_{j}},\quad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad Y\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\langle Bx,D\rangle-\partial_{t},

and, as we say in Proposition
2.1
, the assumption
[H.1]
is equivalent to the Hörmander’s condition, even though
{\mathscr{L}}
is
totally degenerate
at
t=0
, for
\vartheta\geq 1
.

In this work we rely on the construction of the Perron-Weiner-Brelot solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
) based on the existence of a family of
resolutive sets
, as explained in the monograph
[
7
]
by Constantinescu and Cornea. We recall that a family of resolutive sets for operators in the form (
1.1
) satisfying the assumption
[H.1]
has been built by Montanari in
[
19
]
. We point out that in the particular case of the heat operator, these resolutive sets agree with the standard cylinders.

Further developments
. We aim to extend the theory developed in the present article to more general totally degenerate, according to Bony, differential operators for which neither an explicitly expression of the fundamental solution nor an Harnack-type inequality is available.

)

2.
Notation and preliminaries

In this section we specify the notation adopted throughout the rest of the article and provide some known result about the family of operators we are going to study. We also give a detailed proof of the hypoellipticity of
{\mathscr{L}}
and of the existence of its fundamental solution.
Here and in the following of this note we write the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
in the Hörmander’s form

{\mathscr{L}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(t^{\vartheta}\partial_{x_{j}}\big)^{2}+\langle Bx,D\rangle-\partial_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+Y,

with

(2.1)

X_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=t^{\vartheta}\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad Y\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\langle Bx,D\rangle-\partial_{t},

for
j=1,\dots,m
. As usual in the theory of the Hörmander’s operators, we identify any vector field
X
with the vector valued function whose entries are the coefficients of
X
, specifically

(2.2)

X=\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}(x,t)\partial_{x_{j}}+c_{0}(x,t)\partial_{t}\simeq(c_{1}(x,t),\dots,c_{N}(x,t),c_{0}(x,t)).

We denote by
A
and
E(s)
the
N\times N
matrices defined as

(2.3)

A\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m}&{\mathbb{O}}\\
{\mathbb{O}}&{\mathbb{O}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad E(s)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\exp(-sB).

where
\mathbb{I}_{m}
is the
m\times m
identity matrix and
s
is any real number. For every
t,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
we eventually define the matrix

(2.4)

C(\tau,t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\int_{\tau}^{t}s^{2\vartheta}E(s)AE^{T}(s)ds.

We recall some known facts about the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
in the case
\vartheta=0
, which will be useful for the study of the case
\vartheta\geq 1
.

If
\vartheta=0
, specifically when

(2.5)

{\mathscr{L}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+\langle Bx,D\rangle-\partial_{t},

the following statements are equivalent to the condition
[H.1]
:

i)

(Hörmander’s condition):

{\rm rank\,Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y)(x,t)=N+1
for every
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
;

ii)

{\rm Ker}(A)
does not contain non-trivial subspaces which are invariant for
B^{T}
;

iii)

C(\tau,t)>0
for every
t>\tau
;

iv)

(Kalman’s rank condition):

{\rm rank}\,\left(A,BA,\ldots,B^{N-1}A\right)=N
.

The equivalence between
i)
and
ii)
was first proved by Hörmander in
[
10
]
. A detailed proof of the equivalence between
i)
,
ii)
,
iii)
and
[H.1]
can be found in
[
13
]
(see Proposition A.1, and Proposition 2.1). The equivalence between
iii)
and
iv)
was pointed out by Lunardi in
[
17
]
. We next prove that the above result also holds in the case
\vartheta\geq 1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following statements are equivalent to the condition
[H.1]
:

i)

(Hörmander’s condition):

{\rm rank\,Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y)(x,t)=N+1
for every
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
;

ii)

{\rm Ker}(A)
does not contain non-trivial subspaces which are invariant for
B^{T}
;

iii)

C(\tau,t)>0
for every
t>\tau
;

iv)

(Kalman’s rank condition):

{\rm rank}\,\left(A,BA,\ldots,B^{N-1}A\right)=N
.

Proof.

As said above, the assertion is known to be true in the case
\vartheta=0
. Moreover, the constant
\vartheta
doesn’t appear in
[H.1]
,
ii)
and
iv)
, hence the equivalence between
[H.1]
,
ii)
and
iv)
trivially follows from the case
\vartheta=0
.

We next prove that
[H.1]
is equivalent to
ii)
for every
\vartheta\geq 1
. With this aim, we compare condition
ii)
with
\vartheta=0
and condition
ii)
with
\vartheta\geq 1
. In order to distinguish the two cases we set, for
j=1,\dots,m
,

(2.6)

\widetilde{X}^{0}_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad\widetilde{X}^{k}_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left[\widetilde{X}^{k-1}_{j},Y\right],\qquad k=1,\dots,\kappa.

Moreover, we let

(2.7)

V_{k}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{span}\left\{\widetilde{X}^{k}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{m}\right\},\qquad k=0,\dots,\kappa.

and we set, for
j=1,\dots,m
,

(2.8)

X^{0}_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=t^{\vartheta}\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad X^{k}_{j}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left[X^{k-1}_{j},Y\right],\qquad k=1,\dots,\kappa.

A direct computation shows that

(2.9)

\left[t^{\vartheta}\partial_{x_{j}},Y\right]=t^{\vartheta}\left[\partial_{x_{j}},Y\right]+\vartheta t^{\vartheta-1}\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,m,

that can be written as follows

(2.10)

X_{j}^{1}=t^{\vartheta}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{1}+\vartheta t^{\vartheta-1}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{0},\qquad j=1,\dots,m.

By iterating the same argument, we find

(2.11)

X_{j}^{2}=t^{\vartheta}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{2}+2\vartheta t^{\vartheta-1}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{1}+\vartheta(\vartheta-1)t^{\vartheta-2}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{0},\qquad j=1,\dots,m.

and, for
k=3,\dots,\kappa
, and
j=1,\dots,m
,

(2.12)

X_{j}^{k}=t^{\vartheta}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{k}+k\vartheta t^{\vartheta-1}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{k-1}+\dots+\vartheta(\vartheta-1)\dots(\vartheta-k+1)t^{\vartheta-k}\widetilde{X}_{j}^{0}.

Note that the last coefficient vanishes whenever
k>\vartheta
.

We are now ready to show that the Hörmander’s condition
ii)
is satisfied by the system of vector fields
\left\{X^{k}_{1},\dots,X^{k}_{m},Y\right\}
in the set
\big\{t\neq 0\big\}
. Indeed, we easily see that, in this case,

(2.13)

\text{span}\left\{\widetilde{X}^{0}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{X}^{0}_{m}\right\}=\text{span}\left\{X^{0}_{1},\dots,X^{0}_{m}\right\}.

Moreover (
2.11
) implies that

(2.14)

\text{span}\left\{\widetilde{X}^{0}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{X}^{0}_{m},\widetilde{X}^{1}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{X}^{1}_{m_{1}}\right\}=\text{span}\left\{X^{1}_{1},\dots,X^{1}_{m_{1}}\right\},

for every
t\neq 0
. By the same reason, using the above assertions and (
2.12
), we conclude that

agrees with

We are left with the set

. In this case we use (

for every

. This means that

belongs to

. Hence,

contains

and the Hörmander’s condition (

. This concludes the proof of the equivalence between

and
ii)
.

We next prove that
ii)
is equivalent to
iii)
. We follow the Hörmander’s argument. We first note that the matrix
E(s)AE^{T}(s)
is non negative, for every

. Then
C(\tau,t)\geq 0
whenever

. Moreover, the function

is non-decreasing. We claim that the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a

such that
\langle C(\tau,t_{0})\xi,\xi\rangle=0
;

(2)

\langle C(\tau,t)\xi,\xi\rangle=0
fo every
t>\tau
;

(3)

A(B^{T})^{k}\xi=0
, for every non-negative integer
k
.

We first prove that
1.
implies

Assume that there exists a
t_{0}>\tau
and a vector
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
such that
\langle C(\tau,t_{0})\xi,\xi\rangle=0
. Then
\langle C(\tau,t)\xi,\xi\rangle=0
for every
t\in[\tau,t_{0}]
. From the definition of

(
2.4
), it follows that

(2.16)

s^{2\vartheta}\langle AE^{T}(s)\xi,E^{T}(s)\xi\rangle=0,\quad\text{for every}\quad s\in[\tau,t_{0}],

then

(2.17)

\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}s^{k+2\vartheta}A(B^{T})^{k}\bigg)\xi=0,\quad\text{for every}\quad s\in[\tau,t_{0}],

which implies the assertion
3.
The implications

are trivial and are omitted.

The proof of the equivalence between
ii)
and

is a direct consequence of the fact that

(2.18)

V\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid A(B^{T})^{k}\xi=0\ \text{fo every non-negative integer}\ k\right\}

is the greatest subspace of
{\rm Ker}(A)
which is
B^{T}
–invariant.
This completes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We emphasize that the condition

of Proposition

3.
Recalls of potential theory

We begin recalling some definitions and results from Potential Theory. We adopt the notation of the monograph
[
7

by Constantinescu and Cornea. Let us indicate with

) a metric space, locally connected and locally compact. Moreover, denoting with

on

Definition 3.1

(
Sheaf of functions).

Suppose we are given, for every open set

, a family
{\mathscr{H}}(U)
of extended real valued functions
u:U\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
. We say that the map

{\mathscr{H}}:U\longmapsto{\mathscr{H}}(U),

is a
sheaf of functions
on

if the following properties hold:

(i)

if
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathfrak{T}\mbox{ with }U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}\mbox{ and }u\in{\mathscr{H}}(U_{2})\mbox{ then }u_{|U_{1}}\in{\mathscr{H}}(U_{1})
;

(ii)

if
(U_{\imath})_{\imath\in\mathcal{I}}\in\mathfrak{T}\mbox{ and }u:\bigcup\limits_{\imath\in\mathcal{I}}U_{\imath}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]\mbox{ is such that}\penalty\ u_{|U_{\imath}}\in{\mathscr{H}}(U_{\imath})\,\forall\imath\in\mathcal{I},\mbox{ then}\penalty\ u\in{\mathscr{H}}(\bigcup\limits_{\imath\in\mathcal{I}}U_{\imath}).

A sheaf of functions
{\mathscr{H}}
on
E
will be called
harmonic
if,
for every
U\in\mathfrak{T}
,
{\mathscr{H}}(U)
is a linear subspace of
\textup{C}(U,{\mathbb{R}})
.
A sheaf of functions
{\mathscr{U}}
on
E
will be said

if, for any

, the family
{\mathscr{U}}(U)
is a convex cone of lower semi-continuous, lower finite functions.

Note that if

is a hyperharmonic sheaf on

, then the map

{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}:U\longmapsto{\mathscr{U}}(U)\cap(-{\mathscr{U}}(U)),\qquad\forall U\in\mathfrak{T},

is a harmonic sheaf on

.

Throughout the sequel we indicate with

(resp.
{\mathscr{U}}
) a harmonic (resp. hyperharmonic) sheaf on

and

(resp.

is bounded from below on

. Hence the set on the righthand side in (

(

Let

\displaystyle\mu^{V}_{\varphi}\colon V

\displaystyle\to(-\infty,+\infty],

\displaystyle x

3.1.
Resolutive sets

Throughout the rest of this section
{\mathscr{U}}
will denote a given hyperharmonic sheaf on the space
E
. Let us give the following definition.

Definition 3.5

(

-set).

An open set
U\subseteq E
will be called a
minimum principle set
, in short a MP
-set
, if every
{\mathscr{U}}
-function

which is non-negative outside the intersection with

and

\liminf_{x\rightarrow y}u(x)\geq 0,\qquad\forall y\in\partial U,

.

Remark 3.6
.

We point out that, if in the previous definition we are considering an open set
U
with compact closure, we drop the condition that a
{\mathscr{U}}
-function
u
is non-negative outside the intersection with
U
of a compact set
K\subseteq E
.

Let
{\mathscr{U}}
be the hyperharmonic sheaf on
E
,
U\subseteq E
be a
MP
-set and let
{\varphi}:\partial U\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty]
. Let us consider the set

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{\varphi}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\biggl\{u\in{\mathscr{U}}(U):\overline{\{u<0\}}&\mbox{ is a compact, possibly empty, subset of $U$}\\
&\liminf\limits_{U\ni x\rightarrow y}u(x)\geq{\varphi}(y)\,\forall y\in\partial U\biggr\}.\end{array}\right.

The sets
\overline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{\varphi}
and
\underline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{\varphi}=-\overline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{-{\varphi}}
will be called respectively the set of
upper-functions
and the set of
lower-function
. We will call
upper-solution
and

the functions:

\overline{H}^{U}_{\varphi}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\inf\overline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{\varphi},\qquad\underline{H}^{U}_{\varphi}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup\underline{{\mathscr{U}}}^{U}_{\varphi}.

The next proposition is a straightforward consequence of the definition of upper and lower solution.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
{\varphi}_{1},{\varphi}_{2}:\partial U\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\alpha>0
. Then:

(i)

{\varphi}_{1}\leq{\varphi}_{2}\Rightarrow\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}}\leq\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{2}},\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}}\leq\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{2}},

(ii)

\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}+{\varphi}_{2}}\leq\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}}+\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{2}},\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}+{\varphi}_{2}}\geq\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}}+\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{2}}
, whenever the sums are defined.

(iii)

\underline{H}^{U}_{\alpha{\varphi}_{1}}=\alpha\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}},\overline{H}^{U}_{\alpha{\varphi}_{1}}=\alpha\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}},\overline{H}^{U}_{-\alpha{\varphi}_{1}}=-\alpha\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{2}}
,

(iv)

{\varphi}_{1}\geq 0\Rightarrow\overline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}},\underline{H}^{U}_{{\varphi}_{1}}\geq 0
.

Let us given now a crucial definition.

Definition 3.8

(
Resolutive set and PWB solution).

A function
{\varphi}:\partial U\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
is called

if the functions
\overline{H}^{U}_{\varphi},\underline{H}^{U}_{\varphi}
are
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-functions and coincide. In this case we set

is the

An open set

of

(with respect to
{\mathscr{U}}
) if every

If

Harmonic spaces and

Let us begin defining a harmonic space.

Definition 3.9

(
Harmonic space).

The couple
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
, where
{\mathscr{U}}
is a hyperharmonic sheaf on
E
, is called a
harmonic space
if the following axioms are satisfied:

(A1)(Positivity):

For every
x\in E

{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-function, defined in a neighbourhood of
x
, that does not vanish at
x
.

(A2)(Bauer convergence property):

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a monotone increasing sequence of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-functions on an open set
U
of

. Then

u\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}u_{n},

is a
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-function whenever it is locally bounded.

(A3)(Resolutivity):

The resolutive sets (with respect to
{\mathscr{U}}
) form a basis for the topology
\mathfrak{T}
on
E
.

(A4)(Completeness):

A lower semi-continuous, lower finite function
u
on an open set
U
of
E
belongs to
{\mathscr{U}}(U)
if, for any relatively compact with non-empty boundary resolutive set
V
(with respect to
{\mathscr{U}}
) such that
\overline{V}\subset U
, we have
\mu^{V}_{u}\leq u
on
V
, that is

u(x)\geq\int_{\partial V}u\,{\rm d}\mu^{V}_{x},\qquad\forall x\in V,

where
\mu^{V}
is given by the sweeping constructed with the basis of resolutive sets.

Remark 3.10
.

In the particular case the hyperharmonic sheaf
{\mathscr{U}}
is generated by a sweeping system
\Omega
(see Definition

), the axiom
(A4)
of Completeness, is trivially satisfied.

In our setting, by using the Harnack inequality for the non-negative solutions to

given in Theorem

(A2)’(Doob convergence property):

If
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a monotone increasing sequence of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-functions on an open set
U\subset E
such that the set

\biggl\{x\in U|\,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}u_{n}(x)<\infty\biggl\},

is dense in
U
, then

u\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n},

is a
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-function on
U
.

Throughout the sequel we indicate with
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
a harmonic space.

Definition 3.11

(
Superharmonic function).

A hyperharmonic function
u
on a harmonic space
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
is called
superharmonic
if, for any relatively compact resolutive set
V
, the function
\mu^{V}_{u}
is harmonic. A hypoharmonic function
u
will be said
subharmonic
if
-u
is superharmonic.

Remark 3.12
.

Every superharmonic function
u
is finite on a dense subset of its domain. Moreover, if the harmonic sheaf
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
has the Doob convergence property
(A2)’
, then hyperharmonic functions, which are finite on a dense set, are superharmonic.

Definition 3.13

(
Potential).

A non-negative superharmonic function
p
for which any non-negative harmonic minorant vanishes identically is called a
potential
.

We refer to
[
7
]
for some properties of superharmonic functions and potentials. Let us give the following definition.

Definition 3.14

(
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space).

A harmonic space
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
will be called
\mathscr{B}
-
harmonic space
if for any
x\in E
there exists a potential
p
on
E
such that
p(x)>0
.

The following result holds (see
[
7
, Proposition 2.3.2 ]
).

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a harmonic space. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

E
is a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space;

(ii)

the set
\mathscr{P}_{c}
of finite continuous potentials on
E
such that any
p\in\mathscr{P}_{c}
is harmonic outside a compact set, separates the points of
E
;

(iii)

the set of non-negative superharmonic functions on
E
separates the points of
E
;

(iv)

for any relatively compact, resolutive set
V
and for any
x\in V
, there exists a non-negative, finite, continuous superharmonic function
u
on
E
such that

\int_{\partial V}u\,{\rm d}\mu^{V}_{x}<u(x).

As a consequence of the Proposition above we have the following corollary
[
7
, Corollary 2.3.3]
.

Corollary 3.16
.

Every open set of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space is an MP-set, according to Definition
3.5
.

\mathscr{B}
-harmonic spaces are really important since the following result holds true
[
7
, Theorem 2.4.2]
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Any open set of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space with non-empty boundary is resolutive.

The consequence of Theorem
3.17
is that given an open set
U
of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space the
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-Dirichlet problem

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}(U),\\
u_{|\partial U}={\varphi},\qquad\forall{\varphi}\in\textup{C}_{c}(\partial U,{\mathbb{R}}),\end{array}\right.

admits a solution
H^{U}_{\varphi}
in the sense of Perron-Wiener-Brelot.

In general, we cannot expect a good behaviour of
H^{U}_{\varphi}
at the boundary points of

. In the following section we describe the conditions under which the boundary datum
{\varphi}
in (

) is attained by the generalized solution
H^{U}_{\varphi}
.

3.3.
Boundary regularity

Let us give the following definitions.

Definition 3.18

(
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-regular point).

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space and let
U
be an open subset of
E
with non-empty boundary. A point
x_{0}\in\partial U
is said
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-regular
if

\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}H^{U}_{\varphi}(x)={\varphi}(x_{0}),\qquad\forall{\varphi}\in\textup{C}_{c}(\partial U,{\mathbb{R}}).

A point
x_{0}\in\partial U
which is not regular is called
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-irregular.

Definition 3.19

(
Barrier function).

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space and let
U
be an open set of
E
with non-empty boundary,
x_{0}\in\partial U
and let
V
be an open neighbourhood of
x_{0}
. We say that a function
\omega\in{\mathscr{U}}(V\cap U)
is a
barrier at
x_{0}
if:

(i)

\omega>0
on
U\cap V
;

(ii)

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\omega(x)=0.

The first condition for a boundary point to be regular is having a barrier function
[
7
, Proposition 2.4.7]
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
U
be a resolutive subset of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
. Then, any boundary point
x_{0}
which possesses a barrier is regular.

In order to state some geometrical characterization of regular point we need some further notation. The following notion was introduced by Brelot
[
5
]
.

Definition 3.21

(
Reduit and balayage function).

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space. For any non-negative function
u
on
E
and any subset
A
of
E
we call the
reduit
of

on

the following function

(3.5)

{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\inf\biggl\{v\in{\mathscr{U}}(E):v\geq 0\mbox{ on }E\mbox{ and }v\geq u\mbox{ on }A\biggr\}.

We call
balayage
of

on

the lower semi-continuous regularization of the reduit function of

on

, that is

(3.6)

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A}(x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\liminf_{y\rightarrow x}{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}(y),\qquad\forall x\in E.

We list also some further properties of balayage and reduit function which will turn useful in the following parts of the article.

Proposition 3.22
.

For any subsets
A
and
B
of
E
and for every non-negative function
u
and
v
on

the following properties hold

(i)

{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}=u
on

;

(ii)

if

and

{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}\leq{\mathbf{R}}^{v}_{B}

(iii)

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A}={\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}

(iv)

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A\cup B}+{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A\cap B}\leq{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A}+{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{B}
, and
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A\cup B}+{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A\cap B}\leq{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}+{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{B}
.

For a proof of the last property in the proposition above we refer to
[

, Theorem 4.2.2]
.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space and let

be a non-negative superharmonic function on

and

. Then,
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{A}
is harmonic on

and

and

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space. The balayage of any non-negative superharmonic function on

, on any compact subset of

, is a potential.

For a proof of the previous propositions we refer to
[
7
, Proposition 5.3.1 and Proposition 5.3.5]
.

Definition 3.25

(
Polar set).

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space and let
U
be an open set of
E
. A set
P
is said
polar set
in
U
if there exists a non-negative superharmonic function
p
on
U
which is equal
+\infty
at least on
U\cap P
. In this case, we say that the function
p
is
associated to

P
.

Even though most of the results stated below are proved in
[
5
]
, we give here their proofs, since our axiomatic setting is slightly different than the one adopted by the author of
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space,
P
a polar set in
E
and
u
a non-negative function on
E
. Then, the reduit
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{P}
is zero on a dense subset of
E
. Moreover,
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{P}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
P
is a polar set and consider its associated function
p
. Then,
p=+\infty
on
P
. Choose any point
x_{0}
where
p(x_{0})<+\infty
. We have that
\lambda p\geq u
on
P
, for every
\lambda>0
, moreover
p\geq 0
outside
P
. Then,

(3.7)

\lambda p\geq{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{P},\qquad\forall\lambda>0.

Since (
3.7
) holds also in
x_{0}
and
p(x_{0})
is finite, taking the infimum on
\lambda>0
we get that
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{P}(x_{0})=0
. By the previous argument we have that
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{P}
is zero on every point in which
p
is finite. Since
p
is a superharmonic function, it is finite on a dense subset of
E
(see Remark
3.12
). Then
{\mathbf{R}}^{u}_{P}=0
on a dense subset of
E
. Form this fact it follows that
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{P}\equiv 0
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.26
and
3.22
we have

Corollary 3.27
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space. If
P
is a polar set of
E
, then
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A\cup P}={\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{u}_{A}
, for any subset
A
of
E
and for any non-negative function
u
on
E
.

The following definition will be used to give a further characterization of regular points.

Definition 3.28

(
Thinnes).

Let
A
be a subset of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
and let us consider a point
x_{0}\not\in A
. We say that
A
is
thin
at
x_{0}
if either
x_{0}\not\in\overline{A}
or
x_{0}\in\overline{A}
and there exists a non-negative superharmonic function
u
on
E
such that

(3.8)

u(x_{0})<\liminf_{A\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}u(x).

Let us consider a point
x_{0}\in A
. We say that
A
is
thin
at its point
x_{0}
if
\{x_{0}\}
is a polar set in
E
(according to Definition
3.25
) and
A\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}
is thin at
x_{0}
.

The following Proposition holds.

Proposition 3.29
.

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space, let
A
be any subset of
E
,
x_{0}\not\in A
and let
w
be a positive function on
E
, finite and continuous at
x_{0}
. Then,
A
is thin in
x_{0}
if and only if there exists an open neighbourhood
V
of
x_{0}
such that

(3.9)

{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x_{0})<w(x_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x_{0})<w(x_{0}).

Proof.

Let us begin noticing that the second inequality in (
3.9
) is a straightforward consequence of the first one. Hence, it is enough to prove the first condition in (
3.9
).

If
A
is thin in
x_{0}
and
x_{0}\not\in\overline{A}
the inequalities trivially follow. Indeed, chosen an open neighbourhood such that
A\cap V=\emptyset
we have that
{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}\equiv 0
. Since
w(x_{0})>0
inequalities (
3.9
) follow.

Let us suppose that
x_{0}\in\overline{A}
. By Definition
3.28
there exists a non-negative superharmonic function
u
such that (
3.8
) holds, that is

u(x_{0})<\liminf_{A\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}u(x).

Since
w(x_{0})>0
let us choose
\lambda>0
such that,

u(x_{0})<\lambda w(x_{0})<\liminf_{A\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}u(x).

Since
w
is continuous in
x_{0}
we have that

\liminf_{A\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}(u(x)-\lambda w(x))>0.

Then, there exists a neighbourhood
V
of
x_{0}
such that
u(x)>\lambda w(x)
for every
x\in A\cap V
. Hence,
u\geq{\mathbf{R}}^{\lambda w}_{A\cap V}
. In particular for
x=x_{0}
, we have

{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x_{0})\leq\frac{u(x_{0})}{\lambda}<w(x_{0}),

which gives the first inequality in (
3.9
).
Let us prove the vice versa. Since
{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}\geq{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{w}_{A\cap V}
, it is enough to show that the second condition in (
3.9
) implies that
A
is thin in
x_{0}
. Since the balayage function
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{w}_{A\cap V}
is the lower semi-continuous regularization of
{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}
, we have that

\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x)<w(x_{0}).

Hence, choosing
{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x)+{\varepsilon}<w(x_{0})
, we have, by definition of
\liminf
and the particular choice of
{\varepsilon}
, we have that

(3.10)

\forall W\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}\,\exists\bar{x}\in W\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}:\,{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(\bar{x})<\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}{\mathbf{R}}^{w}_{A\cap V}(x)+{\varepsilon}<w(x_{0}).

Therefore, by definition of reduit function, there exists a non-negative superharmonic function
u
such that
u\geq w
on
A\cap V
and

\forall W\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}\,\exists\bar{x}\in W\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}:u(\bar{x})<w(x_{0}).

Thus, for any
W\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}
, we have that

\inf_{x\in W\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}}u(x)<w(x_{0}),

which yields

\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}u(x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup_{W\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}}\biggl(\inf_{x\in W\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}}u(x)\biggr)<w(x_{0}).

By the inequality above and the lower semi-continuity of
u
we have that
u(x_{0})<w(x_{0})
. Then, by the continuity of
w
in
x_{0}
and the fact that
u\geq w
on
A\cap V
we get that

u(x_{0})<\lim_{A\cap V\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}w(x)\leq\liminf_{A\cap V\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}u(x).

The thesis follows.
∎

It is important noticing that thanks to Proposition
3.29
our definition of thinnes (Definition
3.28
) turns out to be equivalent to the one presented in
[
7
, pp. 149]
. Hence, the following Theorem holds true
[
7
, Theorem 6.3.3]
.

Theorem 3.30
.

Let
U
be an open subset of a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
and
x_{0}\in\partial U
. Hence,
x_{0}
is
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-regular if and only if
U^{c}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=E\smallsetminus U
is not thin at
x_{0}
.

We conclude this chapter proving the following Theorem, which gives a characterization of boundary regularity in the abstract setting of Potential Theory similar to the one due by Negrini and Scornazzani
[
20
, Theorem 14]
for
non-totally degenerate
Kolmogorov operators.

Theorem 3.31

(
Characterization of regular points).

Let
(E,{\mathscr{U}})
be a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space,
U
be an open subset of
E
and
x_{0}\in\partial U
such that
\{x_{0}\}
is a polar set in
E
, according to Definition
3.25
. Then,
x_{0}
is a
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-irregular point if and only if

(3.11)

\inf_{K\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}}{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K}(x_{0})=0,

where the infimum is taken on the family of compact neighbourhoods
K
of
x_{0}
ordered by inclusion and
{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K}
is the balayage of the constant function
1
on
U^{c}\cap K
according to Definition
3.21
.

Proof.

Suppose (
3.11
) holds. Then, for any
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, there exists a compact neighbourhood
K_{\varepsilon}
of
x_{0}
such that

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K_{\varepsilon}}(x_{0})\leq{\varepsilon}.

Since
\{x_{0}\}
is a polar set in
E
it follows from Corollary
3.27
that

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K_{\varepsilon}\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}}(x_{0})={\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K_{\varepsilon}}(x_{0})\leq{\varepsilon}<1.

Thus, by Proposition
3.29
, it follows that
U^{c}\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}
is thin in
\{x_{0}\}
. Then, since
\{x_{0}\}
is a polar set in
E
and
U^{c}\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}
is thin in
\{x_{0}\}
, it follows that
U^{c}
is thin in
x_{0}\in U^{c}
according to Definition
3.28
. By Theorem
3.30
it follows that
x_{0}
is a
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-irregular point. Conversely assume
x_{0}
is
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-irregular. As a consequence of Theorem
3.30
we get that
U^{c}
is thin in
x_{0}
according to Definition
3.28
, that is
U^{c}\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}
is thin in
\{x_{0}\}
and
\{x_{0}\}
is a polar set in

. Then, for any compact neighbourhood
K
of
x_{0}
, by Corollary
3.27
, we get that

0\leq{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K}(x_{0})={\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{U^{c}\cap K\smallsetminus\{x_{0}\}}(x_{0}).

Taking the infimum for
K\in\mathfrak{U}_{x_{0}}
we obtain the desired equality (
3.11
).
∎

4.
The Perron-Wiener-Brelot solution for
{\mathscr{L}}

We consider the Dirichlet problem

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}{\mathscr{L}}u=0\mbox{ in }U,\\
u_{|\partial U}={\varphi},\end{array}\right.

where
U
is an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
{\varphi}\in\textup{C}_{c}(\partial U,{\mathbb{R}})
and
{\mathscr{L}}
is the operator defined in (
1.1
) satisfying hypothesis
[H.1]
. As we are interested in classical solution to
{\mathscr{L}}u=0
, throughout the sequel of this article we denote with
{\mathscr{H}}
the harmonic sheaf defined as

(4.2)

U\longmapsto{\mathscr{H}}(U)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big\{u\in\textup{C}^{\infty}(U,{\mathbb{R}}):{\mathscr{L}}u=0\mbox{ in }U\big\},

for every open set
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and we say that a function
u
is harmonic in an open set
U
if
u\in{\mathscr{H}}(U)
.

We next discuss the main steps of the procedure that provides us with the unique solution
u
to the boundary value problem (
4.1
).

4.1.
Definition of the sweeping system

We construct the Perron-Wiener-Brelot solution to problem (
4.1
). With this aim we consider, for any
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},T>t_{0}
and
r>0
, the cylinder
{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})
defined in (
2.26
), and the relevant Dirichlet problem (
2.27
). Note that, in the simplest case of the heat operator
{\mathscr{L}}=\Delta-\partial_{t}
, we are considering the usual Cauchy-Dirichlet problem on the parabolic cylinder

(4.3)

{\mathit{Q}}_{r}(z_{0})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0},T).

As already recalled in Chapter 2, there exists a unique classical solution
u\in\textup{C}^{\infty}({\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0}))
to the Dirichlet problem (
2.27
), which attains the boundary data on
\partial_{P}{\mathit{Q}}_{r}(z_{0})
. By Riesz’s Theorem we have that there exists a Radon measure
\mu^{{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}_{z}
, supported on
\partial_{P}{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})
, such that

(4.4)

u(z)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\int_{\partial_{P}{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}{\varphi}(\zeta){\rm d}\mu^{{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}_{z}(\zeta),\qquad\forall z\in{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0}).

Thus, the family

(4.5)

\Omega\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\biggl\{\mu^{{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{\mu^{{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}_{z}\}_{z\in{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0})}:z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},T>t_{0}\biggl\},

is a sweeping system on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.

4.2.
The hyperharmonic sheaf
{\mathscr{U}}
and the
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space
({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},{\mathscr{U}})

We now consider the hyperharmonic sheaf
{\mathscr{U}}
generated by
\Omega
in accordance with the Definition
3.4
, and we prove that
({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},{\mathscr{U}})
is a
\mathscr{B}
-harmonic space, according to Definition
3.14
.

We first prove that
({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},{\mathscr{U}})
is a harmonic space in the sense of Definition
3.9
. We postpone the proof of axiom
(A2)
, since it is the most involved. The axiom
(A1)
holds because the constant functions are
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-functions. The validity of the axiom
(A3)
is a direct consequence of the fact that

\mathscr{C}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\biggl\{{\mathit{Q}}_{r,T}(z_{0}):z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},T>t_{0},r>0\biggr\},

is a basis of resolutive sets for the euclidean topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
. The axiom
(A4)
follows from the fact that
{\mathscr{U}}
is the hyperharmonic sheaf generated by
\Omega
.

Let us focus our attention to the proof of axiom
(A2)
. We show that
({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},{\mathscr{U}})
has the Doob convergence property (axiom
(A2)’
). With this aim, we consider a monotone increasing sequence of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{U}}
-functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in an open set

W_{q,p}(z)\leq W_{q,p}(z_{0})+\sum_{n=q}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{n}}<2+W_{q,p}(z_{0}),\qquad\mbox{on}\penalty\ O.

Define the non-negative function

v_{q,p}(z)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{W_{q,p}(z)}{2+W_{q,p}(z_{0})},\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}.

Since
v_{q,p}\leq 1
on
O
we have that from Proposition
3.22
it follows that
{\mathbf{R}}^{1}_{O}\geq{\mathbf{R}}^{v_{q,p}}_{O}
. In particular, by definition of
v_{q,p}
, Proposition
3.22
and the fact that
O\subset B_{r}
, we eventually arrive at

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{B_{r}}(z_{0})\geq{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{O}(z_{0})={\mathbf{R}}^{1}_{O}(z_{0})\geq{\mathbf{R}}^{v_{q,p}}_{O}(z_{0})\geq v_{q,p}(z_{0})=\frac{W_{q,p}(z_{0})}{2+W_{q,p}(z_{0})}.

Note that the function
s\mapsto\frac{s}{2+s}
is increasing. Thus, since
W_{q,p}(z_{0})>2
, we obtain that

{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}}^{1}_{B_{r}}(z_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2},

which gives the desired inequality (
5.13
). Hence, the sufficient condition (
5.12
) follows.
∎

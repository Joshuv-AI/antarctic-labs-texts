Chance Constrained Economic Dispatch Considering the Capability of Network Flexibility Against Renewable Uncertainties
Yue Song, Tao Liu, and David J. Hill

Abstract
This paper incorporates a continuous-type network flexibility into chance constrained economic dispatch (CCED). In the proposed model, both power generations and line susceptances are continuous variables to minimize the expected generation cost and guarantee a low probability of constraint violation in terms of generations and line flows under renewable uncertainties. From the analytical form of CCED, we figure out the mechanism of network flexibility against uncertainties—while renewable uncertainties shrink the usable line capacities and aggravate transmission congestion, network flexibility mitigates congestion by re-routing the base-case line flows and reducing the line capacity shrinkage caused by uncertainties. Further, we propose an alternate iteration solver for this problem. By duality theory, we set up a master problem in the form of second-order cone programming to optimize generation dispatch scheme and a subproblem in the form of linear programming to optimize line susceptances. A satisfactory solution can be obtained efficiently by alternately solving these two problems. The proposed method applies to both Gaussian uncertainty and non-Gaussian uncertainty by means of Gaussian mixture model. The case studies on the IEEE 14-bus system and IEEE 118-bus system suggest that network flexibility can significantly improve operational economy while ensuring security under uncertainties.



I
Introduction

Economic dispatch (ED) is a representative class of optimal power flow problems, which aims to find the most economical generation scheme that meets load consumption and satisfies operational constraints regarding generations and line flows.
The normal ED problem, which does not consider uncertain power injections and takes a deterministic formulation, has been extensively studied and leads to many classic results
[
1
]
.
For instance, when the generation and line flow limits are ignored, the optimal dispatch scheme is determined by the so-called equal incremental cost criterion.
When the generation and line flow limits are included, transmission congestion may occur and the optimal dispatch scheme induces the locational marginal price at each bus.
These results are of fundamental importance in power system operation.

With the growing penetration of renewable energy, nowadays system operators are asking for more from ED to tackle the challenge posed by the uncertain nature of renewables.
Under this background, the chance constrained economic dispatch (CCED) is a notable extension that receives popularity.
The system states become random under renewable uncertainties.
The CCED replaces the deterministic constraints by chance constraints to guarantee a low probability of constraint violation in case that the renewable generations follow a certain probability distribution
[
2
]
.
The CCED solution is less conservative than the solution given by robust optimization and achieves a sufficiently low risk of insecurity.
The DC power flow model is commonly adopted in CCED. The problem formulation can be transformed into a second-order cone program (SOCP) under Gaussian uncertainty
[
2
,
3
]
, while non-Gaussian uncertainty is usually tackled by approximation techniques such as the Gaussian mixture model (GMM)
[
4
]
and kernel density representation
[
5
,
6
]
.
The AC power flow-based CCED formulations are emerging and hard to solve due to their high nonlinearity and non-convexity.
So far the mainstream solution methods include convex relaxation
[
7
]
, sequential linearization
[
8
]
and polynomial chaos expansion
[
9
]
.

Traditionally, ED problems rely on the flexibility in generation outputs.
Nowadays the remote controlled line switches and series flexible AC transmission system (FACTS) devices have become a part of the transmission network, e.g., a number of thyristor controlled series compensators (TCSCs) are already in practical operation in the US, China, India and Sweden
[

The growing flexibility in network topology, in the form of discrete or continuous adjustments of line susceptances, adds a new dimension of flexibility to system dispatch

The ED problem with discrete-type network flexibility (i.e., line switching) is usually known as the optimal transmission switching (OTS) problem

The continuous-type network flexibility enabled by power electronics provides a softer way of line susceptance adjustment which attracts attention recently.

Moreover, the existing works mainly investigate the role of network flexibility in a numerical way, e.g., line susceptances are treated as extra decision variables in the optimization. The obtained solution does improve the system performance, but it does not explain why and how the improvement is made.
It remains to explore a deeper understanding of the mechanism of how the network topologies affect system operation, which could also facilitate the design of solution method to efficiently handle the complexity of continuous-type network flexibility.

This paper formulates the CCED problem with continuously adjustable line susceptances, which finds the optimal generation dispatch and line susceptance scheme to achieve the minimal expected generation cost and satisfy the generation and line flow chance constraints. The main contributions are twofold.

First, we reveal the mechanism of network flexibility in addressing the uncertainty-induced congestion. Assuming the renewable uncertainties follow Gaussian distributions, we derive an analytical form of the CCED problem that reveals the role of network flexibility in handling uncertainties. It turns out that renewable uncertainties take up some line capacities and shrink the feasible region for line flows, while network flexibility tunes the base-case line flows and reduces the line capacities taken up by uncertainties. With the help of network flexibility, transmission congestion is greatly mitigated so that the cost-effective generations can be better utilized.

Second, based on the mechanism of network flexibility in congestion mitigation, an efficient alternate iteration solver is designed without discretizing the continuous variables of line susceptances.
The CCED problem is decomposed into a master problem and a subproblem.
The master problem optimizes generation dispatch scheme by treating line susceptances as a parameter, which is an SOCP problem. The linear subproblem is formulated using duality theory, which optimizes line susceptances to provide a better parameter for the master problem. A satisfactory solution can be obtained by alternately solving the master problem and subproblem.
Further, we extend the proposed method to non-Gaussian uncertainty via GMM technique.

Remark 1

The flexible line susceptance has several types of physical realizations.
The most common realization is to install a TCSC in the line, which has been put into practice in some countries as mentioned in the introduction.
This function can also be achieved by more recently developed devices such as power electronic transformers
[
29
,
30
]
.
Note that the complex power transfer across a line takes the expression
S_{ij}=-\textup{j}b_{ij}V_{i}(V_{i}^{*}-V_{j}^{*})
, where
V_{i}
denotes the complex voltage.
Now consider a pair of power electronic transformers installed at the two terminals of line
(i,j)
that induce the secondary voltages
V_{i,s}=kV_{i}
and
V_{j,s}=kV_{j}
for power transfer, where
k
denotes the flexible tap ratio of the two transformers (see Fig.
1
).
In this case, the power transfer across line
(i,j)
becomes
S_{ij}=-\textup{j}b_{ij}V_{i,s}(V_{i,s}^{*}-V_{j,s}^{*})=-\textup{j}k^{2}b_{ij}V_{i}(V_{i}^{*}-V_{j}^{*})
, which implies that the effective line susceptance is changed to
k^{2}b_{ij}
.
Therefore, it is realistic to consider flexible line susceptances in modern power systems with series controllers.

Remark 2

The classical DC power flow model is adopted in this paper to capture the behavior of line flows under uncertainties, which is of major concern in transmission system security.
In the recent years, there emerge a family of advanced linear power flow models that can approximately describe the network loss and voltage-reactive power relationship, e.g., see
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
In fact, the proposed method just requires system states to be linearly dependent on power injections. So it is not limited to DC power flow and applies to the family of linear power flow models.
An extended study of CCED based on a general linear power flow model will be a future direction.

Remark 3

The single-sided chance constraints is adopted in (
4
), which is a more tractable modeling choice that helps us to focus on the mechanism of network flexibility in high-renewable system operation.
In case that two-sided joint chance constraints are adopted, they can still be transformed into single-sided constraints via, e.g., Bonferroni approximation
[
34
,
35
]
. So choosing the single-sided constraints does not lose generality in the problem formulation.

(13b)

\displaystyle b_{ij}^{\min}\leq b_{ij}\leq b_{ij}^{\max},\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}

(13c)

\displaystyle\bm{1}_{n}^{T}\bm{\alpha}_{g}=1,\penalty\ \bm{\alpha}_{g}\geq\bm{0}

(13d)

\displaystyle\alpha_{gi}=0,\penalty\ \forall i\notin\mathcal{V}_{g}

(13e)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}=0,\penalty\ \forall i\notin\mathcal{V}_{g}

(13f)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}\leq P_{gi}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}),\penalty\ \forall i\in\mathcal{V}_{g}

(13g)

\displaystyle-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}\leq-P_{gi}^{\textup{emin}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}),\penalty\ \forall i\in\mathcal{V}_{g}

(13h)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{b})\leq f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g},\bm{b}),\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}

(13i)

\displaystyle-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{b})\leq f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g},\bm{b}),\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}

(13j)

where

\begin{split}P_{gi}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g})&=P_{gi}^{\max}-\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon_{i})s_{\Sigma}\alpha_{gi}\\
P_{gi}^{\textup{emin}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g})&=P_{gi}^{\min}+\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon_{i})s_{\Sigma}\alpha_{gi}\\
f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g},\bm{b})&=f_{ij}^{\max}-\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon_{ij})\big\|\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}\bm{T}_{g}\bm{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}\end{split}

(14)

can be regarded as the effective generation limits and line capacities under uncertainty with
\big\|\cdot\big\|_{2}
denoting the 2-norm.
Note that (
13b
) is equivalent to (
2a
) since the amount of renewable fluctuation is fully balanced by the dispatchable generators.

II-C

Role of decision variables in transmission congestion

Transmission congestion occurs when some line flow constraints are binding.
In this case, the capacities of those congested lines, rather than generation dispatchability, become the major bottleneck for further utilizing the cost-effective generators, which may drastically impact operational economy
[
39
]
.
This undesirable event is more prone to occur in the CCED since
f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}
is equal to the physical capacity reduced by an uncertainty-related margin, i.e., the actual usable line capacity shrinks under uncertainty.
On the other hand, the expression in (
14
) reveals how the decision variables contribute to congestion mitigation:

1) The base-case generation
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}
appears in the left-hand-side of (
13i
)-(
13j
) to regulate the base-case line flows. It does not contribute to congestion mitigation.

2) The participation factor
\bm{\alpha}_{g}
appears in the right-hand-side of (
13i
)-(
13j
).
It helps to save the line capacity by adjusting
\bm{T}_{g}
, but we note that the main function of
\bm{\alpha}_{g}
is to achieve power balancing under uncertainties.
It will be seen in the case study that
\bm{b}
has a much more significant effect on saving line capacity than
\bm{\alpha}_{g}
.

3) The line susceptance
\bm{b}
appears in both sides of (
13i
)-(
13j
) and has a composite contribution to congestion mitigation.
It saves the line capacity by tuning
\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}
, and meanwhile re-routes power injections to improve the base-case line flows
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}
to better utilize the saved line capacity.
The later case study will show that a proper adjustment of line susceptances leads to a both highly economic and secure operating condition under uncertainties.

III
Solution methodology

Problem (
13
) is hard to solve due to the non-convexity of (
13i
) and (
13j
).
On the other hand, the CCED without network flexibility (i.e.,
\bm{b}
is fixed) is an SOCP problem (constraints (
13g
)-(
13h
) are second-order cones if
\bm{b}
is fixed), where convex solvers apply.
In addition, it is shown in Section
II-C
that
\bm{b}
plays a different role from
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}
and
\bm{\alpha}_{g}
in the problem.
Therefore, we separate the decision variables into two groups, say
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g})
and
\bm{b}
, which correspond to generation dispatchability and network flexibility, respectively.
An alternate iteration framework is then developed to iteratively solve the master problem and subproblem with respect to the two groups of decision variables.
The master problem optimizes
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g})
while treating
\bm{b}
as a given parameter.
The subproblem optimizes
\bm{b}
to provide a better parameter for the master problem.
The formulations of the master problem and subproblem are detailed below.

III-A

Master problem to optimize generation dispatch

Let
\bm{b}^{*}=[b_{ij}^{*}]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
denote the current profile of line susceptances.
Then, the master problem is set up below to optimize generation dispatch

\displaystyle\min_{\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g}}\penalty\

\displaystyle h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g})

(15a)

\displaystyle s.t.\penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\bm{1}_{n}^{T}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}+\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{d})=0

(15b)

\displaystyle\bm{1}_{n}^{T}\bm{\alpha}_{g}=1,\penalty\ \bm{\alpha}_{g}\geq\bm{0}

(15c)

\displaystyle\alpha_{gi}=0,\penalty\ \forall i\notin\mathcal{V}_{g}

(15d)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}=0,\penalty\ \forall i\notin\mathcal{V}_{g}

(15e)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}\leq P_{gi}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}),\penalty\ \forall i\in\mathcal{V}_{g}

(15f)

\displaystyle-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}\leq-P_{gi}^{\textup{emin}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}),\penalty\ \forall i\in\mathcal{V}_{g}

(15g)

\displaystyle\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{b}^{*})\leq f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g},\bm{b}^{*}),\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}

(15h)

\displaystyle-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{b}^{*})\leq f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}(\bm{\alpha}_{g},\bm{b}^{*}),\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}

(15i)

where line susceptances are fixed to
\bm{b}^{*}
as a given parameter.
As mentioned before, (
15
) is an SOCP problem that can be efficiently solved by commercial convex solvers such as CVX.

Let
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
denote the optimal solution of (
15
).
If there is no transmission congestion at
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, it means that the current network topology
\bm{b}^{*}
is satisfactory and changing line susceptances will not further reduce the generation cost.
This can also be seen from the next subsection showing that the objective function has a zero sensitivity to line susceptances in case of no congestion. In this case,
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*},\bm{b}^{*})
provides an optimal solution for CCED.
If some lines are congested at
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, it means that the current network topology
\bm{b}^{*}
is inadequate and needs an adjustment, which will be addressed in the following subproblem.

III-B

Subproblem to optimize line susceptances

Based on the obtained generation dispatch scheme
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, we formulate the subproblem to provide better line susceptances for the master problem for congestion mitigation and cost reduction.
Since the line flow constraints (
15h
)-(
15i
) are highly nonlinear with respect to line susceptances, let us consider a rather small adjustment of line susceptances in the subproblem so that the sensitivity analysis is applicable to the problem formulation.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{+},\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{-}
respectively denote the set of lines with binding constraints (
15h
) and binding constraints (
15i
) (i.e., the congested lines consist of
\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{+},\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{-}
), which are of interest here.
Then, we can derive the sensitivity of the optimal objective value of master problem (
15
) to
b_{km}
by duality theory.
Let
\lambda_{ij}^{+},\lambda_{ij}^{-}
,
\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
be the optimal dual variables associated with line flow constraints (
15h
) and (
15i
), respectively.
These dual variables are a byproduct of solving (
15
), which are obtained without additional computation.
The KKT condition of (
15
) gives
[
40
]

\begin{split}&\lambda_{ij}^{+}\geq 0,\penalty\ \lambda_{ij}^{-}=0,\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{+}\\
&\lambda_{ij}^{+}=0,\penalty\ \lambda_{ij}^{-}\geq 0,\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{-}\\
&\lambda_{ij}^{+}=0,\penalty\ \lambda_{ij}^{-}=0,\penalty\ \forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\backslash(\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{+}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{-}).\end{split}

(16)

By Theorem 8.2 in
[
41
]
, the sensitivity of the optimal objective value to
b_{km}
is give by

\begin{split}\frac{\partial h}{\partial b_{km}}=&\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{ij}^{+}(\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}-\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}})\\
&-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{ij}^{-}(\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}+\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}})\end{split}

(17)

which includes the influence of
\bm{b}
in both the base-case line flows and effective line capacities.
Further, (
17
) can be simplified into the following form since a majority of dual variables are zero (see (
16
))

\begin{split}\frac{\partial h}{\partial b_{km}}=&\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{+}}\lambda_{ij}^{+}(\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}-\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}})\\
&-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{con}}^{-}}\lambda_{ij}^{-}(\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}+\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}})\end{split}

(18)

where the expressions of
\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}}
and
\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}
are derived below.

For the term
\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}}
, it follows from (
14
) that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}}=-\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon_{ij})\frac{\partial\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}}{\partial b_{km}}\frac{\bm{T}_{g}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{T}_{g}^{T}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}^{T}(\bm{b}^{*})}{\big\|\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}(\bm{b}^{*})\bm{T}_{g}(\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})\bm{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}}.\end{split}

(19)

According to (
10
), the partial derivative in (
19
) is given by

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}}{\partial b_{km}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{ij}\bm{E}_{ij}^{T}\frac{\partial\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}}{\partial b_{km}}+\bm{E}_{ij}^{T}\bm{B}^{{\dagger}},\penalty\ (k,m)=(i,j)\\
b_{ij}\bm{E}_{ij}^{T}\frac{\partial\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}}{\partial b_{km}},\penalty\ \textup{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(20)

where it follows from
[
42
]
that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}}{\partial b_{km}}=-\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}\frac{\partial\bm{B}}{\partial b_{km}}\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}=-\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}(\bm{b}^{*})\bm{E}_{km}\bm{E}_{km}^{T}\bm{B}^{{\dagger}}(\bm{b}^{*}).\end{split}

(21)

Thus, the formula for
\frac{\partial f_{ij}^{\textup{emax}}}{\partial b_{km}}
are obtained by substituting (
20
)-(
21
) into (
19
).

As for the term
\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}
, it follows from (
9
) that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}=\frac{\partial\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}}{\partial b_{km}}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*}+\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{d})\end{split}

(22)

and substituting (
20
) into (
22
) gives the formula for
\frac{\partial\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muf\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{ij}}{\partial b_{km}}
.
Finally, we obtain the sensitivities
\frac{\partial h}{\partial b_{km}}
by substituting (
19
)-(
22
) into (
18
).

With the obtained sensitivities
\frac{\partial h}{\partial b_{km}}
, we propose the linear subproblem below that aims to reduce generation cost by adjusting line susceptances

\displaystyle\min_{\Delta b_{km}}\penalty\

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{(k,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial b_{km}}\Delta b_{km}

(23a)

\displaystyle s.t.\penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle b_{km}^{\min}\leq b_{km}^{*}+\Delta b_{km}\leq b_{km}^{\max},\penalty\ \forall(k,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}

(23b)

\displaystyle-\Delta b_{km}^{\max}\leq\Delta b_{km}\leq\Delta b_{km}^{\max},\penalty\ \forall(k,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}

(23c)

where the partial derivative terms have been given in (
19
)-(
22
);

\Delta b_{km}^{\max}
is a predefined small positive number and (
23c
) is a trust-region constraint that enforces the line susceptance adjustment to be small so that the sensitivity analysis is valid.

Let
\Delta\bm{b}=[\Delta b_{km}]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
be the susceptance adjustment, where
\Delta b_{km}=0
,
\forall(k,m)\notin\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}
and
\Delta b_{km}
,
\forall(k,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}
are given by the solution of (
23
).
When solving subproblem (
15
) again with the updated line susceptances
\bm{b}^{*}+\Delta\bm{b}
, the generation dispatch scheme is expected to further exploit the line capacity saved by
\Delta\bm{b}
and achieve a lower-cost solution.

III-C

Alternate iteration algorithm

Based on the proposed master problem (
15
) and subproblem (
23
), we design an alternate iteration algorithm for the CCED problem.
The solution procedure is presented below. The algorithm flow chart is also depicted in Fig.
2
.

Step 1:

Parameter setting. Set input parameters
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{d},\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w},\bm{\Sigma}
;
a_{1,i},a_{2,i},\varepsilon_{i},P_{gi}^{\max},P_{gi}^{\min}
for bus
i\in\mathcal{V}_{g}
;
b_{ij}^{\max},b_{ij}^{\min}
for line
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}
;
b_{ij}
for line
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}
and
\varepsilon_{ij},f_{ij}^{\max}
for line
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
.
Set algorithm parameters
\delta>0
(convergence criterion),
\Delta b_{ij}^{\max,0}>0
(benchmark for trust-region size),
0<\beta<1
(reduction factor for trust region).
Let
\Delta b_{ij}^{\max}=\Delta b_{ij}^{\max,0}
be the initial trust-region size, and
\bm{b}^{*}
be the initial line susceptances.

Step 2:

Initial solution. Solve master problem (
15
) under the fixed line susceptances
\bm{b}^{*}
, and obtain the solution
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
.
If all the constraints (
15h
)-(
15i
) are not binding at
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, stop the algorithm and output
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*},\bm{b}^{*})
as the optimal solution.

Step 3:

Solve subproblem (
23
) under
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
and obtain the line susceptance adjustment
\Delta\bm{b}
.

Step 4:

Solve master problem (
15
) under the fixed line susceptances
\bm{b}^{*}+\Delta\bm{b}
, and obtain the tentative solution, say
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})
.

Step 5:

If all the constraints (
15h
)-(
15i
) are not binding at
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})
, update the solution
\bm{b}^{*}\leftarrow\bm{b}^{*}+\Delta\bm{b}
,
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*}\leftarrow\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime}
,
\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*}\leftarrow\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime}
, stop the algorithm and output
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*},\bm{b}^{*})
as the optimal solution.

Step 6:

If
h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})>h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, the tentative solution is aborted, reduce the trust-region size by
\Delta b_{ij}^{\max}\leftarrow\beta\Delta b_{ij}^{\max}
. Go back to Step 3.

If
h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})\leq h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
, the susceptance adjustment
\Delta\bm{b}
is accepted, update the solution
\bm{b}^{*}\leftarrow\bm{b}^{*}+\Delta\bm{b}
,
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*}\leftarrow\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime}
,
\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*}\leftarrow\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime}
and recover the trust-region size
\Delta b_{ij}^{\max}\leftarrow\Delta b_{ij}^{\max,0}
.
Further, if
|\Delta b_{ij}|<\delta
,
\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}
, stop the algorithm and output
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*},\bm{b}^{*})
as the optimal solution; otherwise go back to Step 3.

We further explain the manipulations in Step 6.
The case of
h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})>h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
implies that subproblem (
23
) does not provide a proper susceptance adjustment, which is due to that the trust-region size is too large to guarantee the validity of sensitivity analysis. In this case, we need to solve subproblem (
23
) again with a reduced trust-region size.
The case of
h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\prime})\leq h(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*})
implies that the generation cost is reduced as expected after applying the susceptance adjustment
\Delta\bm{b}
, and hence
\bm{b}^{*}
should be updated to
\bm{b}^{*}+\Delta\bm{b}
.

According to the stop criteria in the algorithm, the final output of this algorithm, say
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{b}^{\textup{opt}})
, has two types of physical meaning.
If the algorithm is stopped in Step 2 or Step 5, then
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{b}^{\textup{opt}})
fully eliminates transmission congestion, which is desirable.
If the algorithm is stopped in Step 6, then
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\textup{opt}})
and
\bm{b}^{\textup{opt}}
reach such a matching state although transmission congestion is not fully eliminated:
1) under the power network topology
\bm{b}^{\textup{opt}}
, any change on generation dispatch scheme
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\textup{opt}})
cannot decrease the generation cost;
2) under the generation dispatch scheme
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{\textup{opt}},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{\textup{opt}})
, transmission congestion cannot be further mitigated by applying any change on
\bm{b}^{\textup{opt}}
.
In this case, both the generation dispatchability and network flexibility have been fully exploited.

Remark 4

Since problem (
13
) is a general nonlinear and non-convex program, it is theoretically hard to guarantee the convergence and optimality of the proposed algorithm.
However, the proposed algorithm has a salient merit that every accepted solution
(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}^{*},\bm{\alpha}_{g}^{*},\bm{b}^{*})
generated during the iteration is a feasible solution to (
13
) and has a lower generation cost than the last accepted solution.
Thus, even when the iteration has not yet converged, we can still implement the latest accepted solution to improve system operation.
This feature enables our algorithm to periodically output a satisfactory solution (i.e., an operating point with better economy and security), which is friendly to the real-time dispatch.

III-D

Extension to non-Gaussian uncertainty

The proposed method can be extended to the case where
\bm{P}_{w}
follows a general non-Gaussian distribution via GMM.
Note that any smooth probability density function can be approximated with any specific, non-zero amount of error by a GMM with enough components
[
43
]
.
Thus, when
\bm{P}_{w}
follows a non-Gaussian distribution, its characteristics can be captured by the following GMM

\begin{split}\bm{P}_{w}\sim\sum\nolimits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{GMM}}}\pi^{m}\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m},\bm{\Sigma}^{m})\end{split}

(24)

where
\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m},\bm{\Sigma}^{m})
denotes the
m
-th Gaussian distribution component consisting of the mean value
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m}
and covariance matrix
\bm{\Sigma}^{m}
;
\mathcal{I}_{\textup{GMM}}
denotes a finite index set of Gaussian distribution components; and
\pi^{m}
denotes the weight of
\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m},\bm{\Sigma}^{m})
with
0\leq\pi^{k}\leq 1
,
\sum\nolimits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{GMM}}}\pi^{m}=1
.
With this GMM, the mean value of
\bm{P}_{w}
is given by

\begin{split}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}=\sum\nolimits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{GMM}}}\pi^{m}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m}.\end{split}

(25)

Now consider the CCED problem (
4
) under GMM uncertainties (
24
).
Note that GMM has linear additivity in terms of probability
[
44
]
, i.e., in case of (
24
) it follows that

\begin{split}\Pr\{\bm{c}^{T}\bm{P}_{w}\leq\bm{d}\}=\sum\nolimits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\textup{GMM}}}\pi^{m}\Pr\{\bm{c}^{T}\bm{P}_{w}^{m}\leq\bm{d}\}\end{split}

(26)

with
\bm{P}_{w}^{m}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m},\bm{\Sigma}^{m})
.
This property enables us to apply the analytical reformulation to each Gaussian component.
Similar to the idea in Section
II
, the generations and line flows under the Gaussian component
\bm{P}_{w}^{m}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m},\bm{\Sigma}^{m})
are expressed as

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{g}

\displaystyle=\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}-\bm{\alpha}_{g}\bm{1}_{n}^{T}(\bm{P}_{w}^{m}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w})

(27a)

\displaystyle\bm{f}

\displaystyle=\bm{T}_{f}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{d}+\bm{\alpha}_{g}\bm{1}_{n}^{T}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w})+\bm{T}_{f}\bm{T}_{g}\bm{P}_{w}^{m}

(27b)

and hence their mean values and standard deviations take the following forms

\begin{split}\textup{mean}\{P_{gi}\}&=\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5muP\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{gi}-\alpha_{gi}\bm{1}_{n}^{T}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w})\\
\textup{std}\{P_{gi}\}&=\alpha_{gi}s_{\Sigma}\\
\textup{mean}\{f_{ij}\}&=\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g}-\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{d}+\bm{\alpha}_{g}\bm{1}_{n}^{T}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}+\bm{T}_{g}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{m})\\
\textup{std}\{f_{ij}\}&=\sqrt{\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}\bm{T}_{g}\bm{\Sigma}^{m}\bm{T}_{g}^{T}\bm{T}_{f_{ij}}^{T}}\end{split}

(28)

Thus, the CCED problem under the non-Gaussian uncertainty is equivalent to

The CCED model under GMM description can also be solved by setting the master problem and subproblem below:

1) The master problem is set to be (
29
) with a fixed
\bm{b}
. This master problem formulation is non-convex due to the presence of auxiliary variables
y_{Gi}^{m},z_{Gi}^{m},y_{ij}^{m},z_{ij}^{m}
, which can be handled by the iterative risk allocation (IRA).
The IRA adopts a two-step strategy.
First, it allocates the overall probability to each component
y_{Gi}^{m},z_{Gi}^{m},y_{ij}^{m},z_{ij}^{m}
based on the property of the current solution of
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g}
.
Second, it updates
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{g},\bm{\alpha}_{g}
by solving the SOCP problem (
29
) with
y_{Gi}^{m},z_{Gi}^{m},y_{ij}^{m},z_{ij}^{m},\bm{b}
fixed to their current values.
These two steps are executed iteratively until convergence. We refer to
[
45
,
46
]
for more details of IRA.
Using the IRA, the master problem is solved by solving a sequence of SOCP problems, and the optimal dual variables associated with constraints (
29k
)-(
29n
) can be obtained simultaneously.

2) The subproblem can be constructed in terms of those optimal dual variables obtained by the master problem, following the similar manipulations in Section
III-B
. The solution of the subproblem finds a proper susceptance adjustment
\Delta\bm{b}
.

Therefore, the proposed algorithm still works for the CCED under non-Gaussian uncertainty by applying the IRA to solve the master problem.
A comparison between the CCED solutions under Gaussian and non-Gaussian uncertainty will be given in Section
IV-C
.

IV-C

Further discussion on CCED parameters

Taking IEEE 118-bus system as an example, this subsection further studies the influence of some important input parameters on CCED solutions.

IV-C
1
Degree of network flexibility

The degree of flexibility
d_{ij}
plays a role in the generation cost reduction and we have fixed
d_{ij}
to 0.7 so far.
Fig.
6
shows how the generation cost of S1 (CCED with network flexibility) of IEEE 118-bus system changes with different degrees of network flexibility.
We observe that the cost decreases nearly linearly with
d_{ij}
.
For this particular system,
d_{ij}
should be greater than 0.5 in order to achieve more than 2% cost reduction.

IV-C
2
Location of flexible susceptance lines

Note that the location of the nine flexible susceptance lines in the previous test is determined by trail-and-error.
Now let us arbitrarily choose the location of more flexible susceptance lines, e.g., assume these twelve lines
\mathcal{E}_{\textup{flex}}=\{(1,2),(1,3),(4,5),(3,5),(5,6),(6,7),(8,9),(8,5),(9,10),

(4,11),(5,11),(11,12)\}
have flexible susceptances.
In this case, the generation cost of S1 (CCED with network flexibility) becomes 321065.5, which has little reduction comparing to 321571.6 (i.e., the cost of CCED solution without network flexibility).
It shows that the location of flexible susceptance lines is crucial to the solution quality and should be carefully chosen.
The optimal placement of flexible susceptance lines is beyond the scope of this paper and will be a future direction.

IV-C
3
Continuous network flexibility v.s. discrete network flexibility

Let us set up the following experiment to compare the continuous network flexibility with the discrete version
[
23
,
24
]
.
Suppose each transmission line in IEEE 118-bus system adopts the double-circuit line structure.
Let
b_{ij}^{(S1)}
denote the optimal line susceptance obtained in our previous test in Section
IV-B
, then we set the following line susceptance profile

\begin{split}b_{ij}^{dis}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}},&b_{ij}^{(S1)}>b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}}\\
0.5b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}},&b_{ij}^{(S1)}<b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

where
b_{ij}^{dis}=2b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}}
simulates the double-circuit mode of line
(i,j)
and
b_{ij}^{dis}=0.5b_{ij}^{\textup{rated}}
simulates the single-circuit mode of line
(i,j)
.
Solving the CCED problem under this line susceptance profile, the consequent generation cost is 311879.6, which is significantly greater than 310210.0 (i.e., the cost of S1).
It shows the merit of continuously adjustable line susceptance over its discrete counterpart.

IV-C
4
Covariance between renewable outputs

So far we have neglected the covariance between the renewable outputs.
In IEEE 118-bus system, assume every pair of renewable generators have the same covariance
\sigma^{2}_{cov}
.
Fig.
7
shows how the the generation costs of S1 (CCED with network flexibility) and S2 (CCED without network flexibility) change with differemt values of
\sigma^{2}_{cov}
.
It can be seen that the generation costs slowly decrease with
\sigma^{2}_{cov}
.
A stronger covariance actually implies a more similar behavior between renewable outputs and hence reduces the probability of some extreme cases, e.g., one renewable generation goes very large and another renewable generation goes very small.
Therefore, the existence of renewable covariance slightly reduces the risk of constraint violations so that a lower-cost solution can be obtained.

IV-C
5
Gaussian uncertainty v.s. non-Gaussian uncertainty

This test generalizes the Gaussian uncertainty to non-Gaussian case.
Let
\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w},\bm{\Sigma})
denote the Gaussian uncertainty adopted in our previous test on IEEE 118-bus system in Section
IV-B
.
In the new test, suppose the renewable outputs
\bm{P}_{w}
follow the non-Gaussian distribution described by the GMM below

\begin{split}\bm{P}_{w}\sim\pi^{1}\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{1},\bm{\Sigma}^{1})+\pi^{2}\mathcal{N}(\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{2},\bm{\Sigma}^{2})\end{split}

where
\pi^{1}=0.9
,
\pi^{2}=0.1
,
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{1}=0.778\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}
,
\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{2}=3\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}
,
\bm{\Sigma}^{1}=\bm{\Sigma}^{2}=\bm{\Sigma}
.
This non-Gaussian distribution function is illustrated in Fig.
8
.
Note that the mean value of
\bm{P}_{w}
in this case is still equal to
\pi^{1}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{1}+\pi^{2}\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}^{2}=\mkern 3.5mu\overline{\mkern-3.5mu\bm{P}\mkern 0.0mu}\mkern 0.0mu_{w}
, so that this test scenario is comparable to the previous one in Section
IV-B
.

Under this GMM model, we solve the following three types of CCED problems and obtain the corresponding solutions:

•

S1-GMM: CCED under non-Gaussian uncertainty with network flexibility. The optimal generation cost is 310568.5.

•

S2-GMM: CCED under non-Gaussian uncertainty without network flexibility. The optimal generation cost is 322843.3.

•

S3-GMM: CCED under non-Gaussian uncertainty with network flexibility but fixed participation factors
\alpha_{gi}=1/|\mathcal{V}_{g}|
. The optimal generation cost is 312208.5.

It turns out that S1-GMM, S2-GMM and S3-GMM all have slightly higher generation costs than their counterparts under Gaussian uncertainty (see S1, S2 and S3 in Section
IV-B
).
This higher cost is caused by that a more conservative dispatch scheme has to be adopted to hedge against the extra risk of constraint violations brought by the GMM long tail.

V
Conclusion

Continuous-type network flexibility has been incorporated into the CCED problem to cope with the impact caused by renewable uncertainties.
From the analytical form of the CCED problem, we have discovered that the flexible line susceptances tune the base-case line flows and reduce the line capacities shrunk by uncertainties. Thus, network flexibility greatly contributes to congestion mitigation and generation cost saving.
Furthermore, we have proposed an efficient solver for the CCED problem with network flexibility.
Using duality theory, we have established an SOCP master problem to optimize generation dispatch and a linear subproblem to optimize line susceptances.
Alternately solving these two subproblems gives the solution to the CCED.
The extension of the proposed method from Gaussian uncertainty to non-Gaussian uncertainty has also been made.
Case studies have shown that the operational economy under uncertainties is much improved with the help of network flexibility.

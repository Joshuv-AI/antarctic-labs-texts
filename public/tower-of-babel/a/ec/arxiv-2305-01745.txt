We provide precise answers not only to this question, but also to similar questions for more general intertwining and copositive polynomial approximation. 1
Introduction and Main Results

In this paper, we are interested in shape preserving approximation (see
e.g.
[
klps2011
]
for a brief introduction to this area) with an additional restriction that approximating polynomials interpolate the function that is being approximated at a given set of points.

Without any shape restrictions, we can easily do this and also preserve the rate of best approximation in the uniform norm (it is rather evident that this is no longer possible in the integral norms). Indeed, it is sufficient to correct the polynomial of best approximation using an appropriate Lagrange polynomial obtaining a near best polynomial approximant satisfying the needed interpolation conditions (the constant will necessarily depend on the minimum distance among all interpolation points).

Hence, the following question is rather natural:
If
f
has a certain shape that we wish to preserve while approximating it by polynomials, is interpolation at a given set of points possible and, if so, what can be said about errors of approximation of
f
by such polynomials?
Our goal is to provide answers to this question.

In order to discuss this further, we now recall some standard notations and definitions (see
e.g.
[
klps2011
]
).
We say that a function
f
is
q
-monotone on an interval
J
and write
f\in\Delta^{(q)}(J)
) if, for all collections of
q+1
distinct points
t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{q}
in
J
, the divided differences
[t_{0},\dots,t_{q};f]
are nonnegative. In particular,
\Delta^{(0)}(J)
,
\Delta^{(1)}(J)
and
\Delta^{(2)}(J)
are the sets of all nonnegative, nondecreasing and convex functions on
J
, respectively. If
q\geq 3
, then functions
f
from
\Delta^{(q)}(J)
are
(q-2)
-times differentiable in
\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits J
(the interior of
J
) and
f^{(q-2)}\in\Delta^{(2)}(\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits J)
.

Let
\mathbb{Y}_{s}
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
, be the set of all collections
Y_{s}:=\big\{y_{i}\big\}_{i=1}^{s}
of points
y_{i}
, such that
-1<y_{s}<\dots<y_{1}<1
, and define
y_{0}:=1
and
y_{s+1}:=-1
.
We denote by
\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})
the set of functions which change their
q
-monotonicity at the points in
Y_{s}
. More precisely,

f\in\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})\quad\iff\quad(-1)^{i}f\in\Delta^{(q)}([y_{i+1},y_{i}]),\;0\leq i\leq s.

Also, if
s=0
, then
\mathbb{Y}_{0}:=\{\emptyset\}
and
\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{0}):=\Delta^{(q)}(I)
, where
I:=[-1,1]
.

Let
C^{r}:=C^{r}(I)
denote the space of all
r
-times continuously differentiable functions on
I
, and
let
\mathbb{A}_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, be the set of all collections

A_{p}:=\big\{\alpha_{i}\big\}_{i=1}^{p}
of points
\alpha_{i}
such that
-1\leq\alpha_{p}<\dots<\alpha_{1}\leq 1
. For a given
f\in C^{r}(I)
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we denote

{\mathcal{I}}^{(r)}(f,A_{p}):=\left\{g\in C^{r}(I)\;\;\big|\;\;g^{(i)}(\alpha)=f^{(i)}(\alpha),\;\text{for all $\alpha\in A_{p}$ and $0\leq i\leq r$}\right\}.

In particular, if
r=0
, we use the notation
{\mathcal{I}}(f,A_{p}):={\mathcal{I}}^{(0)}(f,A_{p})
. For convenience, we also denote
\mathbb{A}_{0}:=\{\emptyset\}
.
Obviously, if
f\in C^{r}(I)
,
r\geq 1
, then

{\mathcal{I}}^{(m+1)}(f,A_{p})\subset{\mathcal{I}}^{(m)}(f,A_{p})
, for all
0\leq m\leq r-1
.

As usual,

\Pi_{n}
is the set of all polynomials of degree
\leq n
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{J}
is the uniform norm on
J
,
{\omega}_{k}(f,\cdot;J)
is the
k
-th modulus of smoothness of
f
on
J
,

\rho_{n}(x):=n^{-1}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}+n^{-2}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
Throughout this paper,
c(\dots)
denote positive constants which depend only on the parameters in the parentheses. Also, the interval
I=[-1,1]
will be omitted from the notation,
i.e.,
\left\|\cdot\right\|:=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{I}
,
{\omega}_{k}(f,\cdot):={\omega}_{k}(f,\cdot;I)
, etc.

Throughout this paper, if sets
Y_{s}
and
A_{p}
both appear in a statement, it is assumed that they have no points in common,
i.e., the restriction
Y_{s}\cap A_{p}=\emptyset
is always assumed but is not explicitly stated.

Note that this has no influence on the generality of the obtained results and
is done for convenience only.

1.1
If
q\geq 1
, then
q
-monotone approximation with general interpolatory constraints is impossible

We start with the following observation that implies that
q
-monotone polynomial approximation with interpolatory constraints is, in general, not possible if
q\geq 1
.

Lemma 1.1
.

For any
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
Y_{s}\in\mathbb{Y}_{s}
, there exists
f\in\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})
and a set
A_{p}\in\mathbb{A}_{p}
,
p\geq q+2
, such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(f,A_{p})\cap\Pi_{n}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Given
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
Y_{s}\in\mathbb{Y}_{s}
, we set
\xi:=(1+y_{1})/2
and pick
A_{p}
,
p\geq q+2
, so that
y_{1}<\alpha_{q+2}<\dots<\alpha_{2}<\xi<\alpha_{1}
. Then,

f:=(\cdot-\xi)_{+}^{q}
is clearly in
\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})
.
We now recall the following property of
q
-monotone functions (see
[
b
]
*Theorem 5):

Let
f\in\Delta^{(q)}(J)
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
l_{q-1}:=l_{q-1}(z_{q},\dots,z_{1})\in\Pi_{q-1}
be the Lagrange polynomial interpolating
f
at
q
distinct points in
J
:

z_{q}<z_{q-1}<\dots<z_{1}
.
Then,
(-1)^{i}\left(f(x)-l_{q-1}(x)\right)\geq 0
, for
x\in[z_{i+1},z_{i}]
,
1\leq i\leq q
, and
f(x)-l_{q-1}(x)\geq 0
, for
x\geq z_{1}
. In other words,

(1.1)

f-l_{q-1}\in\Delta^{(0)}\left(\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}\right).

Note that this result is a generalization of the property that a convex function always lies below its chord.

Suppose now that
p_{n}\in\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(f,A_{p})\cap\Pi_{n}
. Then,
p_{n}
is
q
-monotone on
[y_{1},1]
,
p_{n}(\alpha_{i})=0
,
2\leq i\leq q+2
and
p_{n}(\alpha_{1})=f(\alpha_{1})>0
.
Now, polynomials
\ell_{1}:=l_{q-1}(\alpha_{q+2},\dots,\alpha_{3})\equiv 0
and

\ell_{2}:=l_{q-1}(\alpha_{q+2},\dots,\alpha_{4},\alpha_{2})\equiv 0
(in the case
q=1
,
\ell_{2}:=l_{0}(\alpha_{2})\equiv 0
)
interpolate
p_{n}
at
q
points in
[y_{1},1]
, and so it follows from (
1.1
) that
p_{n}(x)=p_{n}(x)-\ell_{1}(x)\geq 0
, for
x\geq\alpha_{3}
, and

-p_{n}(x)=(-1)\left(p_{n}(x)-\ell_{2}(x)\right)\geq 0
, for
\alpha_{4}\leq x\leq\alpha_{2}
. Therefore,
p_{n}(x)=0
,
\alpha_{3}\leq x\leq\alpha_{2}
, and so
p_{n}\equiv 0
. Hence,
p_{n}(\alpha_{1})>0
does not hold.
This contradiction implies that such a polynomial
p_{n}
does not exist.
∎

We remark that, if the number of points
p
in
A_{p}\in\mathbb{A}_{p}
is sufficiently small, then
q
-monotone approximation with interpolatory constraints may be possible, but it may or may not be as good as
q
-monotone approximation without interpolation at the points in
A_{p}
. For example, if
q\geq 1
, then
denoting

E_{n}^{(q)}(f,Y_{s};A_{p}):=\inf\left\{\left\|f-p_{n}\right\|\;\;\big|\;\;p_{n}\in\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(f,A_{p})\cap\Pi_{n}\right\},

we have

E_{n}^{(q)}(f,Y_{s};A_{1})\leq 2E_{n}^{(q)}(f,Y_{s};\emptyset),\quad\text{for any $A_{1}=\{\alpha\}\subset I$,}

and

E_{n}^{(q)}(f,Y_{s};A_{q+1})=1,\quad\text{if $Y_{s}\subset[-1,0)$, $f(x)=x_{+}^{q}\in\Delta^{(q)}(Y_{s})$ and $A_{q+1}\subset(y_{1},0)$.}

1.2
Positive, copositive, onesided and intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints: definitions and a motivational example

Note that the case for
q=0
(copositive approximation) is not covered by Lemma
1.1
. In fact, as shown below, (co)positive polynomial approximation requiring interpolation on general sets
A_{p}
is always
possible. However, its rate of approximation is often worse than the rate of (co)positive approximation without these additional interpolatory constraints.

Another type of shape preserving approximation that is related to (co)positive approximation is the so-called onesided and, more generally,
intertwining
approximation.
We say (see
[
hky
]
; as far as we know, this is where intertwining approximation was first introduced and discussed) that
\{P,Q\}
is an
intertwining pair
of functions (polynomials, splines, etc.)
for
f
with respect to

Y_{s}\in\mathbb{Y}_{s}
if

P-f,f-Q\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s}).

In particular, in the case
s=0
, we have
Q\leq f\leq P
on
I
, and approximation of
f
by
P
and
Q
is usually referred to as
onesided
approximation.

The error of intertwining/onesided polynomial approximation is usually defined as the infimum of
\left\|P-Q\right\|
taken over all intertwining pairs of polynomials for
f
. In this paper, we also work with an equivalent quantity (and equivalent corresponding pointwise estimates)

\widetilde{E}_{n}(f,Y_{s}):=\inf\left\{\|f-P\|\;\;\big|\;\;P\in\Pi_{n}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}(f,Y_{s})\right\},

where

\widetilde{\Delta}(f,Y_{s}):=\left\{P:I\mapsto\mathbb{R}\;\;\big|\;\;P-f\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})\right\}.

The error of onesided approximation is denoted by
\widetilde{E}_{n}(f):=\widetilde{E}_{n}(f,Y_{0})
.

Estimates for intertwining/onesided approximation immediately imply those for copositive/positive approximation since, if
f\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
and
P\in\widetilde{\Delta}(f,Y_{s})
, then

P=(P-f)+f\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
.
However, as we show below, there are many cases when copositive (polynomial) approximation (with or without interpolatory constraints) is possible while intertwining approximation is not.

It is well known that the ‘correct’ pointwise estimates for polynomial approximation need to involve the quantity
\rho_{n}(x)
since, without it, matching inverse results would not be possible.
Namely, the classical Timan-Dzyadyk-Freud-Brudnyi direct theorem for the approximation by algebraic polynomials can be stated as follows (see
e.g.
[
kls21
]
for discussions):

if
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f\in C^{r}
, then for each
n\geq k+r-1
there is a polynomial
p_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying

(1.2)

|f(x)-p_{n}(x)|\leq c(k,r)\rho_{n}^{r}(x){\omega}_{k}(f^{(r)},\rho_{n}(x)),\quad x\in I.

Hence, estimates of type (
1.2
) is what we are interested in while discussing approximation with interpolatory constraints (see also Section
7
for discussions of how interpolation yields further improvement of
these estimates).

Illustrative example:

Suppose that
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is fixed, and two functions
f,g\in C^{r}
are such that
g\in\Delta^{(0)}(I)
and
f\in\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})

(

g\geq 0
on
[-1,1]
, and
f\leq 0
on
[-1,0]
and
f\geq 0
on
[0,1]
), and recall that, for
0\leq m\leq r
,
{\mathcal{I}}^{(m)}(f,\{\alpha\})

for all

.

We are interested in

2.1
Onesided and intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints

For convenience, we introduce the following notation:

\Upsilon_{\text{intertwining}}:=\bigcup_{r\geq 1}\Upsilon_{r},

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{r}:=\left\{(r,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\;\;\big|\;\;\right.

\displaystyle m_{1}=r-1,\;m_{2}=r-2,\;m_{3}=r-1\;\text{if $r$ is even; }

\displaystyle\left.m_{1}=r-2,\;m_{2}=r-1,\;m_{3}=r-1\;\text{if $r$ is odd}\right\}.

Hence,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\text{intertwining}}=

\displaystyle\left\{(2j,2j-1,2j-2,2j-1)\;\;\big|\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(2j+1,2j-1,2j,2j)\;\;\big|\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

Note that, when
r=1
,

\Upsilon_{1}:=\left\{(1,-1,0,0)\right\},

which is the only case when any of
m_{i}
’s can become negative.

The following is our main result (or rather a collection of results combined in one statement) for intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints.

Theorem 2.1

(intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints)
.

Let

,

,

, and

(
2.1
)
.

, there exists

such that

|f(x)-P_{n}(x)|\leq c(k,r,s,p)\rho_{n}^{r}(x){\omega}_{k}(f^{(r)},\rho_{n}(x)),\qquad x\in I,

where

, the set of all real valued functions on

, i.e., the restriction

is vacuous, and so

interpolate

at the points in

if

.

to be

\displaystyle\left\{(2j,2j-1,2j-1)\;\;\big|\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\cup\left\{(2j+1,2j-1,2j)\;\;\big|\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

(onesided approximation with interpolatory constraints)
.

3
Negative results

Recall that, for functions
g:I\mapsto\mathbb{R}
with absolutely continuous
(r-1)
st derivatives on
I
, the following (Taylor’s formula) holds:

g(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{1}{i!}g^{(i)}(a)(x-a)^{i}+\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{a}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}g^{(r)}(t)dt,\quad x\in I.

We often use this elementary identity without explicitly mentioning it.

3.1
Onesided and intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints

The purpose of the following lemmas is to establish general negative results for onesided and intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints.

\lambda

I
(i.e.,

),

such that

on
[-1,\lambda]
, and

{\mathcal{I}}^{({\tilde{r}})}(f,\{\lambda\})\cap\left\{g\in C^{r+1}\;\;\big|\;\;\text{$g\geq f$ on $(\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)$, for some $\varepsilon>0$}\right\}=\emptyset,

where

{\tilde{r}}:=2\lfloor r/2\rfloor=\begin{cases}r-1\,,&\text{if $r$ is odd,}\\
r\,,&\text{if $r$ is even.}\end{cases}

(ii)

if
\lambda
is an endpoint of
I
(i.e.,
\lambda=\pm 1
), then there exists
f\in C^{r}
such that

{\mathcal{I}}^{(r)}(f,\{\lambda\})\cap\left\{g\in C^{r+1}\;\;\big|\;\;\text{$g\geq f$ on $(\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)\cap I$, for some $\varepsilon>0$}\right\}=\emptyset.

Proof.

If
\lambda
is an interior point of
I
then, without loss of generality, we assume that
\lambda=0
and
define

f(x)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}f^{(r)}(t)dt,\quad\text{where}\quad f^{(r)}(x):=\begin{cases}-x\ln x,&\text{if $0<x\leq 1$,}\\
0,&\text{if $-1\leq x\leq 0$.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
f\in C^{r}
and
f\equiv 0
on
[-1,0]
. Now, suppose that, for some
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
g\in C^{r+1}
satisfying
g(x)\geq f(x)
,
x\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, and such that
g^{(i)}(0)=f^{(i)}(0)=0
,
0\leq i\leq{\tilde{r}}
.
Then, since
{\tilde{r}}\geq r-1
,

\displaystyle g(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}\left[g^{(r)}(t)-f^{(r)}(t)\right]dt.

If
r
is even, then
{\tilde{r}}=r
, and so
g^{(r)}(0)=0
, and we will now show that this equality also holds if
r
is odd.
Indeed, for
-\varepsilon<x<0
,

0\leq g(x)-f(x)=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{(r-1)!}\int_{x}^{0}(t-x)^{r-1}g^{(r)}(t)dt,

and so, by continuity of
g^{(r)}
, we must have
(-1)^{r}g^{(r)}(0)\geq 0
. Similarly, because
g\geq f
on
(0,\varepsilon)
, we should have
g^{(r)}(0)\geq f^{(r)}(0)=0
. Hence, if
r
is odd, this implies that
g^{(r)}(0)=0
.

Thus, for
x>0
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle g(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}\left[g^{(r)}(t)+t\ln t\right]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}\int_{0}^{t}\left(g^{(r+1)}(u)+\ln u+1\right)\,du\,dt.

Since
g\in C^{r+1}
and
\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}\ln u=-\infty
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
g^{(r+1)}(u)+\ln u+1<0
, for all
0<u<\delta
. Hence,

g(x)-f(x)<0
, for
0<x<\delta
,
which is a contradiction.

If
\lambda
is an endpoint of
I
, then the same proof as above works with no changes (for example, one can consider
[0,1]
instead of
I
,
\lambda=0
, and then arrive at a contradiction using (
3.1
)).
∎

The same proof can be used to verify validity of the following lemma that is used for negative results involving Hermite interpolation at the points in
Y_{s}
.

Lemma 3.2

(local intertwining approximation with interpolation at
Y_{s}
: negative result for
f\in C^{r}
with
r\geq 1
)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}

and suppose that
\lambda\in(-1,1)
.
Then there exists a function
f\in C^{r}
such that
f\equiv 0
on
[-1,\lambda]
, and

{\mathcal{I}}^{({\hat{r}})}(f,\{\lambda\})\cap\left\{g\in C^{r+1}\;\;\big|\;\;\text{$g\leq f$ on $(\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda]$, and $g\geq f$ on $[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon)$, for some $\varepsilon>0$}\right\}=\emptyset,

where

{\hat{r}}:=2\lceil r/2\rceil-1=\begin{cases}r\,,&\text{if $r$ is odd,}\\
r-1\,,&\text{if $r$ is even.}\end{cases}

We remark that in all cases that are not covered by the statements of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
onesided/intertwining polynomial approximation with interpolatory constraints is possible (see Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
).

Note that the only reason for including the property that
f\equiv 0
on
[-1,\lambda]
in the statements of
Lemma
3.1
(if
\lambda
is an interior point of
I
) and Lemma
3.2

is to indicate that these lemmas are still valid if
f
is assumed to be from the class
\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
(with
y_{1}\leq\lambda
). In particular, this is important for the illustrative example in Section
1.2
.

3.2
Positive and copositive approximation with interpolatory constraints

The following is the first lemma in this paper that provides a negative result for
polynomial
approximation with interpolatory constraints. Even though, in some sense, it is less general than Lemma
3.4
that follows it, its proof is rather simple and illustrates the main ideas that are used in the proofs of more complicated negative results below but without the technical details required there.

Lemma 3.3

(positive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C
)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
A>0
and
\lambda\in I
, there exists
f\in C
which is nonnegative on
I
and such that, for any nonnegative on
I
polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, we have

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A{\omega}_{3}(f,1).

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A>0
be fixed, and without loss of generality suppose that
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
We now let
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda/2)
, and pick
f(x):=\max\left\{(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^{2},0\right\}
and suppose that
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
is nonnegative on
I
, satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
(this necessarily implies that
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq 0
, and actually
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
if
\lambda\neq 1
) and such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|\leq A{\omega}_{3}(f,1)
.
If
Q(x):=(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^{2}
, then

\left\|f-Q\right\|=\left\|Q\right\|_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda]}=1/4

and

{\omega}_{3}(f,1)={\omega}_{3}(f-Q,1)\leq 8\left\|f-Q\right\|=2.

Hence,

\left\|P_{n}-Q\right\|\leq\left\|P_{n}-f\right\|+\left\|f-Q\right\|\leq 2A+1/4,

and so, by Markov’s inequality,

1/\varepsilon=Q^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq Q^{\prime}(\lambda)-P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\left\|Q^{\prime}-P_{n}^{\prime}\right\|\leq(2A+1/4)(\max\{n,2\})^{2}.

We now get a contradiction by letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(copositive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C
)
.

For any
A>1
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any positive sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
0
, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq n_{0}
, and
f=f_{n}\in C
such that

f\geq 0
on
I
,

f\equiv 0
on
[-1,-1/2]
,
and for any polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
which is nonnegative in some nonempty neighborhood of
\lambda

and satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, we have

\left\|\frac{f-P_{n}}{{\omega}_{3}(f,\rho_{n})}\right\|>A\,\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}.

Proof.

Recall that the classical “glue function” is

{\widetilde{G}}(x):={\widetilde{G}}(x;[a,b]):=\frac{G(x;[a,b])}{G(1;[a,b])},\quad\text{where}\quad G(x;[a,b]):=\int_{-1}^{x}g(t;(a+b)/2,(b-a)/2)\,dt,

g(x;x_{0},d):=\begin{cases}\exp(d^{2}/((x-x_{0})^{2}-d^{2})),&x\in(x_{0}-d,x_{0}+d),\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and
[a,b]\subset[-1,1]
. Then,
{\widetilde{G}}\in C^{\infty}
,
{\widetilde{G}}(x)=0
if
x\leq a
,
{\widetilde{G}}(x)=1
if
x\geq b
, and
0\leq{\widetilde{G}}(x)\leq 1
if
a\leq x\leq b
.

Now, let
\varepsilon:=\rho_{n}^{3/2}(\lambda)
,
Q(x):=(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda+\varepsilon)
, and define

f(x):=\begin{cases}0,&x\in[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda],\\
Q(x),&x\in[-1/4,1]\setminus[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda],\\
Q(x)\,{\widetilde{G}}(x;[-1/2,-1/4]),&x\in(-1/2,-1/4),\\
0,&x\in[-1,-1/2].\end{cases}

(Here, we assume that
n
is sufficiently large so that everything is well defined.)

Suppose now that

P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}

is nonnegative near
\lambda
, satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
(this necessarily implies that
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq 0
, and actually
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
if
\lambda\neq 1
) and such that

|f(x)-P_{n}(x)|\leq A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}\,{\omega}_{3}(f,\rho_{n}(x))
,
x\in I
.

Clearly,
f\in C^{\infty}[-1,-1/8]
, and it is not difficult to see that
\left\|f^{(3)}\right\|_{[-1,-1/8]}\leq c_{0}
, for some absolute constant
c_{0}
(it is convenient to assume that
c_{0}\geq 2
). Hence,

{\omega}_{3}(f,t;[-1,-1/8])\leq t^{3}\left\|f^{(3)}\right\|_{[-1,-1/8]}\leq c_{0}t^{3},\quad t>0.

Since

\left\|f-Q\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}=\left\|Q\right\|_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda]}=\varepsilon^{2}/4,

we also have

{\omega}_{3}(f,t;[-1/4,1])={\omega}_{3}(f-Q,t;[-1/4,1])\leq 8\left\|f-Q\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}=2\varepsilon^{2}.

Hence,

{\omega}_{3}(f,\rho_{n}(x))\leq c_{0}\max\{\rho_{n}^{3}(x),\rho_{n}^{3}(\lambda)\},\quad x\in I.

For
x\in[-1/4,1]
, we now have

(3.2)

|P_{n}(x)-Q(x)|\leq|P_{n}(x)-f(x)|+|f(x)-Q(x)|\leq(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}+1)\max\{\rho_{n}^{3}(x),\rho_{n}^{3}(\lambda)\}.

We now consider two cases.

Case 1:

\lambda<1

Since
\rho_{n}(x)\leq 2n^{-1}
,
x\in I
,
using (
3.2
) and Bernstein’s inequality on
[-1/4,1]
, we have

\displaystyle(1-\lambda^{2})^{3/4}n^{-3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon=Q^{\prime}(\lambda)=|Q^{\prime}(\lambda)-P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)|\leq(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}(\lambda+1/4)^{-1/2}n\left\|Q-P_{n}\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}

\displaystyle\leq 16(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}n^{-2}\left(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}+1\right),

and we arrive at a contradiction by taking
n
to be sufficiently large.

Case 2:

\lambda=1

We need the following Dzyadyk inequality (see
e.g.
[
dzya
]
*p. 386 or
[
kls21
]
*Lemma 2.5):

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{0}\in I
,
R_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
, and

|R_{n}(x)|\leq(|x-x_{0}|+\rho_{n}(x))^{m},\quad x\in I,

then

|R_{n}^{\prime}(x_{0})|\leq c(m)\rho_{n}^{m-1}(x_{0}).

We use this result with
m=3
,
x_{0}=\lambda=1
and
R_{n}=B^{-1}(P_{n}-Q)
, where
B:=c_{0}A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}+1
.
Suppose that we verified that

(3.3)

|P_{n}(x)-Q(x)|\leq B(|x-1|+\rho_{n}(x))^{3},\quad x\in I.

Then, by Dzyadyk’s inequality,

n^{-3}=\varepsilon=Q^{\prime}(1)\leq Q^{\prime}(1)-P_{n}^{\prime}(1)\leq cB\rho_{n}^{2}(1)=c(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}+1)n^{-4},

and we arrive at a contradiction by taking
n
to be sufficiently large.

It remains to verify (
3.3
). Note that (
3.3
) immediately follows from (
3.2
) for
x\in[-1/4,1]
and, for
x\in[-1,-1/4]
, we have

|Q(x)|\leq x(x-1)\leq(1-x)^{3},

and so

\displaystyle|P_{n}(x)-Q(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|P_{n}(x)-f(x)|+|f(x)-Q(x)|\leq|P_{n}(x)-f(x)|+|Q(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}A\alpha_{n}\sqrt{n}\rho_{n}^{3}(x)+|x-1|^{3}\leq B(|x-1|+\rho_{n}(x))^{3}.

The proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(copositive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C^{1}
and
\lambda\neq 1
)
.

For any
A>1
,
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any positive sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
0
, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq n_{0}
, and
f=f_{n}\in C^{1}
such that

f\geq 0
on
I
,

f\equiv 0
on
[-1,-1/2]
,
and for any polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
which is nonnegative on
(\lambda-1/n,\lambda+1/n)

and satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, we have

(3.4)

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A\,\alpha_{n}n^{-2/3}{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},n^{-1}).

We remark that inequality (
3.4
) implies that

\left\|\frac{f-P_{n}}{\rho_{n}{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},\rho_{n})}\right\|>cA\,\alpha_{n}n^{1/3},

where
c>0
is some absolute constant. Also, note that the statement of this lemma is no longer valid if
\lambda=1
,
i.e.,
if interpolation takes place at an endpoint of
I

(see Theorem
2.3
with
(r,k,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(1,k,-1,0,0)
).

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Suppose that
n\geq 25(1-\lambda)^{-1}
, let
\varepsilon:=n^{-4/3}
and define

g(x):=\begin{cases}Q(x),&\text{if $x<\lambda$,}\\
0,&\text{if $\lambda\leq x\leq\lambda+\varepsilon$,}\\
Q(x)+\varepsilon^{4},&\text{if $x\geq\lambda+\varepsilon$.}\end{cases}

where
Q(x):=3(x-\lambda)^{4}-4\varepsilon(x-\lambda)^{3}
.
Note that
g\in C^{1}
and

g^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}Q^{\prime}(x),&\text{if $x<\lambda$ or $x>\lambda+\varepsilon$,}\\
0,&\text{if $\lambda\leq x\leq\lambda+\varepsilon$,}\end{cases}

with
Q^{\prime}(x)=12(x-\lambda)^{2}(x-\lambda-\varepsilon)
.

Now, define

f(x):=\begin{cases}g(x),&x\in[-1/4,1],\\
g(x)\,{\widetilde{G}}(x;[-1/2,-1/4]),&x\in(-1/2,-1/4),\\
0,&x\in[-1,-1/2],\end{cases}

where
{\widetilde{G}}
is the glue function from the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Suppose that
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
is nonnegative on
(\lambda-1/n,\lambda+1/n)
, satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
(this necessarily implies that
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
) and such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|\leq A\alpha_{n}n^{-2/3}\,{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},1/n)
.

Clearly,
f\in C^{\infty}[-1,-1/8]
, and it is not difficult to see that
\left\|f^{(5)}\right\|_{[-1,-1/8]}\leq c_{0}
, for some absolute constant
c_{0}\geq 32
. Hence,

{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},n^{-1};[-1,-1/8])\leq n^{-4}\left\|f^{(5)}\right\|_{[-1,-1/8]}\leq c_{0}n^{-4}.

Since

\left\|f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}=\left\|Q^{\prime}\right\|_{[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon]}\leq 2\varepsilon^{3},

we also have

{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},n^{-1};[-1/4,1])={\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime},n^{-1};[-1/4,1])\leq 16\left\|f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}\leq 32\varepsilon^{3}.

Hence,

{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},n^{-1})\leq\max\{c_{0}n^{-4},32\varepsilon^{3}\}=c_{0}n^{-4},

and so

\displaystyle\left\|P_{n}-Q\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{n}-f\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}+\left\|g-Q\right\|_{[-1/4,1]}

\displaystyle\leq A\alpha_{n}n^{-2/3}\,{\omega}_{4}(f^{\prime},1/n)+\max\left\{\varepsilon^{4},\left\|Q\right\|_{[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon]}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-14/3}\left(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}+n^{-2/3}\right).

By the Markov-Bernstein inequality on
[-1/4,1]
,

\left\|P_{n}^{\prime\prime}-Q^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon]}\leq c(1-\lambda)^{-1}n^{-8/3}\left(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}+n^{-2/3}\right),

and taking into account that
P_{n}(\lambda+\varepsilon)\geq 0
and
P_{n}^{(i)}(\lambda)=Q^{(i)}(\lambda)=0
,
i=0,1
, we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-4}\left[P_{n}(\lambda+\varepsilon)-Q(\lambda+\varepsilon)\right]=\varepsilon^{-4}\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\varepsilon}(\lambda+\varepsilon-t)\left[P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)-Q^{\prime\prime}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\lambda)^{-1}n^{-8/3}\left(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}+n^{-2/3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c(1-\lambda)^{-1}\left(c_{0}A\alpha_{n}+n^{-2/3}\right),

and we arrive at a contradiction by taking
n
to be sufficiently large.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(copositive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C^{r}
with even
r\geq 2
)
.

If
r\geq 2
is
even
, then for any
n\geq r
,
A>0
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, there exists
f\in C^{r}
which is nonnegative on
I
, is identically
0
on
[-1,-1/2]
,
and such that, for any nonnegative on
(\lambda-1/n,\lambda)
polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying
P_{n}^{(i)}(\lambda)=f^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
0\leq i\leq r
, we have

(3.5)

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|.

Proof.

Let
n\geq r
and
A\geq 1
be fixed, and define
f\in C^{r}
as

f(x)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{\lambda-\varepsilon}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}f^{(r)}(t)dt,

where

f^{(r)}(x):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $x\leq\lambda-\varepsilon$ or $x\geq\lambda$,}\\
-(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^{2},&\text{if $\lambda-\varepsilon<x<\lambda$,}\end{cases}

and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/(2n))
. Then,
f\equiv 0
on
[-1,\lambda-\varepsilon]
,
f\uparrow
on
[\lambda-\varepsilon,1]

(and so
f
is nonnegative on
I
),
\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|=1/4
and, for
x\geq\lambda
,

(3.6)

f(x)=Q(x):=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\lambda-\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(x-t)^{r-1}(\lambda-t)(t-\lambda+\varepsilon)dt.

Note that
Q
is a polynomial of degree
\leq r-1
and

Q(\lambda-\varepsilon)=\frac{(-1)^{r-1}}{(r-1)!}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\lambda-\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(t-\lambda+\varepsilon)^{r}(\lambda-t)dt=(-1)^{r-1}\frac{r}{(r+2)!}\varepsilon^{r}.

Now, suppose that
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
is such that
P_{n}\geq 0
on
(\lambda-1/n,\lambda)
,
P_{n}^{(i)}(\lambda)=f^{(i)}(\lambda)=Q^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
0\leq i\leq r
, and

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|\leq A\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|=A/4
.
Hence,

\left\|P_{n}\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}\leq\left\|P_{n}-f\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}+\left\|f\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}\leq A\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|+\left\|f\right\|_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda]}\leq(A+\varepsilon^{r}/r!)\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|\leq A.

Now, by Markov’s inequality,

\left\|P_{n}^{(r+1)}\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}\leq\left(\frac{2n^{2}}{\lambda+1}\right)^{r+1}\left\|P_{n}\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}\leq(2n^{2})^{r+1}A,

and so, since
Q^{(r+1)}\equiv 0
and
r
is even,

\displaystyle\frac{r}{(r+2)!}\varepsilon^{r}

\displaystyle=-Q(\lambda-\varepsilon)\leq P_{n}(\lambda-\varepsilon)-Q(\lambda-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda-\varepsilon}(\lambda-\varepsilon-t)^{r}\left(P_{n}^{(r+1)}(t)-Q^{(r+1)}(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(r+1)!}\varepsilon^{r+1}\left\|P_{n}^{(r+1)}\right\|_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda]}\leq\frac{(2n^{2})^{r+1}A}{(r+1)!}\varepsilon^{r+1},

and we get a contradiction by letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
∎

.

Lemma 3.8

(copositive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C^{r}
with odd
r\geq 3
)
.

If
r\geq 3
is
odd
, then for
any
n\geq r-1
and
A>0
, there exists
f\in C^{r}

which is identically
0
on
[-1,-1/2]
, is nonnegative on
I
and such that, for any nonnegative on
[-1/2,1/2]
polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying
P_{n}^{(i)}(0)=f^{(i)}(0)
,
0\leq i\leq r-1
, we have

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A\left\|f^{(r)}\right\|.

We note that an analog of this lemma is not valid if we require that
P_{n}
be nonnegative on
[-1/2,1]
and
P_{n}^{(i)}(1)=f^{(i)}(1)
,
0\leq i\leq r-1
(
i.e.,
this negative result does not hold if
A_{p}=\{\pm 1\}
),
see
e.g.
Theorem
2.3
with
(r,k,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(2j+1,k,2j-1,2j,2j)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq r-1
and
A>0
be fixed, and define
f\in C^{r}
as

f(x)=\frac{1}{(r-2)!}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{x}(x-t)^{r-2}f^{(r-1)}(t)dt,

where

f^{(r-1)}(x):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $x<-\varepsilon$ or $x>0$,}\\

,

on

, and

is clearly nonnegative on

Now, for

Note that

,

is odd.

which is nonnegative on

and

\displaystyle\leq Q(x)+a_{r}x^{r}+|x|^{r+1}\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}|a_{i}||x|^{i-r-1}

\displaystyle\leq Q(x)+a_{r}x^{r}+Bx^{r+1},

. We also note that

\displaystyle 0\leq P_{n}(-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq Q(-\varepsilon)+D\varepsilon^{2/(r+1)}\varepsilon^{r}+B\varepsilon^{r+1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{r}\left(-c_{1}+O(\varepsilon^{2/(r+1)})\right)<0\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$},

which is a contradiction.
∎

4
Spline approximation with interpolatory constraints

Given a collection of
q
points
Y=\{\upsilon_{j}\}_{j=0}^{q-1}
with possible repetitions,
\upsilon_{0}\leq\upsilon_{1}\leq\dots\leq\upsilon_{q-1}
, we recall that, for each
j
, the multiplicity
m_{j}
of
\upsilon_{j}
is the number of
\upsilon_{i}
such that
\upsilon_{i}=\upsilon_{j}
. Also, we let
l_{j}
be the number of
\upsilon_{i}=\upsilon_{j}
with
i\leq j
. Suppose that a function
f
is defined at all points in
Y
and, moreover, for each
\upsilon_{j}\in Y
,
f^{(l_{j}-1)}(\upsilon_{j})
is defined as well. In other words,
f
has
m_{j}-1
derivatives at each point that has multiplicity
m_{j}
. Then, there is a unique Lagrange-Hermite polynomial
L_{q-1}(\cdot;f,Y)
of degree
\leq q-1
that satisfies

L_{q-1}^{(l_{j}-1)}(\upsilon_{j};f,Y)=f^{(l_{j}-1)}(\upsilon_{j}),\quad\text{for all }\;0\leq j\leq q-1.

The following lemma is a generalization of the classical Whitney’s theorem. It is used for construction of local polynomial pieces near interpolation points in Lemmas
4.5
and
4.7
.

Lemma 4.1

(see
[
kls-umzh
]
*Theorem 5.2 and Lemma 3.1 or
[

Lemma 4.2

(onesided and intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints:
f\in C^{r}
,
r\geq 1
)
.

Suppose that
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in C^{r}[a,b]
and
\lambda\in[a,b]
. Then, there exists
p:=p(\cdot;f,k,r,\lambda)\in\Pi_{k+r-1}
such that
p^{(i)}(\lambda)=f^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
0\leq i\leq r-1
,

(4.1)

\left\|f-p\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c(b-a)^{r}{\omega}_{k}(f^{(r)},b-a;[a,b]),\quad\text{where $c=c(k,r)$,}

and

(i)

if
\lambda\in(a,b)
, then
p-f\geq 0
on
[\lambda,b]
, and
(-1)^{r}\left(p-f\right)\geq 0
on
[a,\lambda]
,

(ii)

if
\lambda=a
, then
p-f\geq 0
on
[a,b]
,

(iii)

if
\lambda=b
, then
(-1)^{r}\left(p-f\right)\geq 0
on
[a,b]
.

Proof.

Since
f^{(r)}\in C
, there exists a polynomial
q\in\Pi_{k-1}
such that

\left\|f^{(r)}-q\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c{\omega}_{k}(f^{(r)},b-a;[a,b])\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad q(x)\geq f^{(r)}(x),\;x\in[a,b].

We now define

p(x):=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{\lambda}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}q(t)dt+\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{1}{i!}f^{(i)}(\lambda)(x-\lambda)^{i}

and note that
p^{(i)}(\lambda)=f^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
0\leq i\leq r-1
, and

p(x)-f(x)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{\lambda}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}\left(q(t)-f^{(r)}(t)\right)dt,\quad x\in[a,b].

This immediately implies (
4.1
), and all other properties of
p
are easily verified.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(positive approximation with interpolatory constraints:
f\in C
)
.

Suppose that
f\in C[a,b]
is nonnegative on
[a,b]
, and
\lambda\in[a,b]
is such that either
\lambda
is one of the endpoints of
[a,b]
, or
\min\{|a-\lambda|,|b-\lambda|\}\geq\gamma|b-a|
,
\gamma>0
. Then, there exists
l\in\Pi_{1}
such that
l
is nonnegative on
[a,b]
,
l(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
and

(4.2)

\left\|f-l\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a),

where
c
is an absolute constant if
\lambda
is an endpoint of
[a,b]
, or
c=c(\gamma)
if
\lambda\in(a,b)
.

Proof.

Let
l_{1},l_{2}\in\Pi_{1}
be linear polynomials interpolating
f
at
a
and
\lambda
, and at
\lambda
and
b
, respectively. Then, by Whitney’s inequality,
\left\|f-l_{i}\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a)
,
i=1,2
, and if at least one of them is nonnegative on
[a,b]
, then we let
l
to be that nonnegative linear polynomial (this is obviously the case if
\lambda
is an endpoint of
[a,b]
). Otherwise,
l_{1}(b)<0
and
l_{2}(a)<0
, and so

0\leq f(a)<f(a)-l_{2}(a)\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a)\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad 0\leq f(b)<f(b)-l_{1}(b)\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a).

Also, since in this case
f(\lambda)<\min\{f(a),f(b)\}
, we have
0\leq f(\lambda)\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a)
. This implies that
\left\|l_{i}\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a)
,
i=1,2
, and so
\left\|f\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f,b-a)
. Hence, we can set
l\equiv f(\lambda)
on
[a,b]
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(positive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C
and
\lambda
near the endpoints)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A>0
, there exist
f\in C
which is nonnegative on
I
and
\lambda\in(-1,1)
such that, for any nonnegative on
I
polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, we have

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A{\omega}_{2}(f,1).

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A>0
be fixed, pick
f(x):=\max\left\{(x-\lambda)/(\lambda+1),0\right\}
and suppose that
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
is nonnegative on
I
, satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
(this necessarily implies that
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
) and such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|\leq A{\omega}_{2}(f,1)
.
If
Q(x):=(x-\lambda)/(\lambda+1)
, then

\left\|f-Q\right\|=\left\|Q\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}=1

and

{\omega}_{2}(f,1)={\omega}_{2}(f-Q,1)\leq 4\left\|f-Q\right\|=4.

Hence,

\left\|P_{n}-Q\right\|\leq\left\|P_{n}-f\right\|+\left\|f-Q\right\|\leq 4A+1,

and so, by Markov’s inequality,

1/(\lambda+1)=Q^{\prime}(\lambda)=Q^{\prime}(\lambda)-P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\left\|Q^{\prime}-P_{n}^{\prime}\right\|\leq n^{2}\left(4A+1\right).

We now get a contradiction by letting
\lambda\to-1^{+}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(positive approximation with interpolatory constraints:
f\in C^{1}
)
.

Suppose that
f\in C^{1}[a,b]
is nonnegative on
[a,b]
, and
\lambda\in[a,b]
is such that
\min\{|a-\lambda|,|b-\lambda|\}\geq\gamma|b-a|
,
\gamma>0
. Then, there exists
p\in\Pi_{3}
such that
p\geq 0
on
[a,b]
,
p(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
and

(4.3)

\left\|f-p\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c(b-a){\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},b-a;[a,b]),\quad\text{where $c=c(\gamma)$.}

Proof.

Assume, for convenience, that
[a,b]=I
and
\lambda=0
.

Let
L_{0}(x)
be the Lagrange-Hermite polynomial of degree
\leq 3
such that
L_{0}^{(i)}(0)=f^{(i)}(0)
,
i=0,1
and
L_{0}(\pm 1)=f(\pm 1)
:

L_{0}(x)=f(0)+f^{\prime}(0)\,x+\left[\frac{f(-1)+f(1)}{2}-f(0)\right]\,x^{2}+\left[\frac{f(1)-f(-1)}{2}-f^{\prime}(0)\right]\,x^{3}.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
with
q=4
and
r=1
that

\left\|f-L_{0}\right\|\leq c{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1).

This also implies that

(4.4)

\left\|f^{\prime}-L_{0}^{\prime}\right\|\leq A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1),

where
A
is some positive constant that we now consider fixed.

Consider the following two cases:

Case 1:

|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq 2A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)

We define

p(x):=f(0)+\left[\frac{f(-1)+f(1)}{2}-f(0)\right]\,x^{2}+\left[\frac{f(1)-f(-1)}{2}\right]\,x^{3}=:f(0)+ax^{2}+bx^{3}

and note that
\left\|L_{0}-p\right\|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|
which implies that
\left\|f-p\right\|\leq c{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)
. Clearly,
p(0)=f(0)
, and so it remains to show that
p
is nonnegative on
I
.

Note that
p(1)=f(1)
and
p(-1)=f(-1)
. If
b=0
, then
p
is clearly nonnegative on
I
, and so suppose that
b\neq 0
and, in fact,
without loss of generality, we can assume that
b>0
.

Clearly,
p^{\prime}(x)=0
iff
x=0
or
x=-2a/(3b)=:x_{0}
.
Now, if
a\geq 0
, then
x_{0}\leq 0
, and
p\downarrow
on
[x_{0},0]
and
p\uparrow
, otherwise.
This implies that
p\geq 0
on
I
.

If
a<0
, then
x_{0}>0
, and
p\downarrow
on
[0,x_{0}]
and
p\uparrow
, otherwise. If
x_{0}\geq 1
, then
p
is clearly nonnegative on
I
.
Hence, it remains to show that
p(x_{0})\geq 0
in the case
0<x_{0}<1
(
i.e.,
if
0<-2a<3b
). Indeed, we have

p(x_{0})=f(0)+\frac{4}{27}\cdot\frac{a^{3}}{b^{2}}>f(0)-\frac{b}{2}>\frac{f(-1)+f(1)}{2}-\frac{f(1)-f(-1)}{4}=\frac{3f(-1)+f(1)}{4}\geq 0.

Case 2:

|f^{\prime}(0)|>2A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)

Without loss of generality, we assume that
f^{\prime}(0)<0
.
Define

p(x):=L_{0}(x)+A(x+x^{2}){\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)

and note that
\left\|f-p\right\|\leq c{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)
and
p(0)=f(0)
, and so we only need to show that
p
is nonnegative on
I
.
It follows from (
4.4
) that, for
x\in[0,1]
,

p^{\prime}(x)=L_{0}^{\prime}(x)+A(1+2x){\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)\geq L_{0}^{\prime}(x)+A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)\geq f^{\prime}(x),

and so

p(x)-f(x)=\int_{0}^{x}[p^{\prime}(u)-f^{\prime}(u)]\,du\geq 0,\quad 0\leq x\leq 1.

For convenience, we denote

p(x)=f(0)+ax+bx^{2}+cx^{3},

and note that

a=p^{\prime}(0)=L_{0}^{\prime}(0)+A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)=f^{\prime}(0)+A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)<-A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)<0.

If
c\leq 0
, then, for any
x\in[-1,0)
, we have

p(x)=p(-x)+2ax+2cx^{3}\geq p(-x)\geq 0.

Hence, it remains to consider the case
c>0
.

If
p^{\prime}\leq 0
on
[-1,0]
, then
p\downarrow
on
[-1,0]
and so is nonnegative there. Hence, we can assume that
p^{\prime}
is positive somewhere in
[-1,0]
.
Since
c>0
, this implies that there exists
\alpha_{0}\in(-1,0)
such that
p^{\prime}>0
on
[-1,\alpha_{0})
, and
p^{\prime}<0
on
(\alpha_{0},0]
. Hence,
p\uparrow
on
[-1,\alpha_{0}]
and
p\downarrow
on
[\alpha_{0},0]
.
This implies that we only need to verify that
p(-1)\geq 0
in order to conclude that
p
is nonnegative on the whole interval
I
.
We have

p(-1)=L_{0}(-1)=f(-1)\geq 0,

and the proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(positive approximation with interpolatory constraints: negative result for
f\in C^{1}
and
\lambda
near the endpoints)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A>0
, there exist
f\in C^{1}
which is nonnegative on
I
and
\lambda\in(-1,1)
such that, for any nonnegative on
I
polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfying
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, we have

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|>A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1).

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A>0
be fixed, pick
f(x):=\max\left\{(x-\lambda)^{3}/(\lambda+1)^{2},0\right\}
and suppose that
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
is nonnegative on
I
, satisfies
P_{n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)=0
(this necessarily implies that
P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
) and such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|\leq A{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)
.
If
Q(x):=(x-\lambda)^{3}/(\lambda+1)^{2}
, then

\left\|f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime}\right\|=\left\|Q^{\prime}\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}=3,\quad\left\|f-Q\right\|=\left\|Q\right\|_{[-1,\lambda]}=\lambda+1,

and

{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime},1)={\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime},1)\leq 8\left\|f^{\prime}-Q^{\prime}\right\|=24.

Hence,

\left\|P_{n}-Q\right\|\leq\left\|P_{n}-f\right\|+\left\|f-Q\right\|\leq 24A+2,

and so, by Markov’s inequality,

\left\|P_{n}^{\prime\prime}-Q^{\prime\prime}\right\|\leq n^{4}(24A+2)=:K,

and so, since
P_{n}^{(i)}(\lambda)=Q^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
i=0,1
,

\lambda+1=-Q(-1)\leq P_{n}(-1)-Q(-1)=\int_{\lambda}^{-1}(-1-t)\left[P_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)-Q^{\prime\prime}(t)\right]\,dt\leq K(\lambda+1)^{2}/2,

and we get a contradiction by letting
\lambda\to-1^{+}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7

(copositive approximation with interpolation at
Y_{s}
:
f\in C^{1}
)
.

Suppose that
f\in C^{1}[a,b]
is such that
(x-\lambda)f(x)\geq 0
,
x\in[a,b]
,
where
\lambda\in[a,b]
is such that
\min\{|a-\lambda|,|b-\lambda|\}\geq\gamma|b-a|
,
\gamma>0
. Then, there exists
p\in\Pi_{2}
such that

(x-\lambda)p(x)\geq 0
,
x\in[a,b]
,

p^{(i)}(\lambda)=f^{(i)}(\lambda)
,
i=0,1
, and

(4.5)

\left\|f-p\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c(b-a){\omega}_{2}(f^{\prime},b-a;[a,b]),\quad\text{where $c=c(\gamma)$.}

Proof.

Assume, for convenience, that
[a,b]=I
and
\lambda=0
. Then,
f\in\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})
, and we need to construct
p\in\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})\cap\Pi_{2}
such that
p^{(i)}(0)=f^{(i)}(0)
,
i=0,1
, and

\left\|f-p\right\|\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f^{\prime},1)
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be the quadratic Lagrange-Hermite polynomials such that
L_{1}^{(i)}(0)=L_{2}^{(i)}(0)=f^{(i)}(0)
,
i=0,1
,
L_{1}(-1)=f(-1)
and
L_{2}(1)=f(1)
. Note that
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
. Hence,

L_{1}(x)=f^{\prime}(0)\,x+\left[f(-1)+f^{\prime}(0)\right]\,x^{2}

and

L_{2}(x)=f^{\prime}(0)\,x+\left[f(1)-f^{\prime}(0)\right]\,x^{2}.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
with
q=3
and
r=1
that

\left\|f-L_{j}\right\|\leq c{\omega}_{2}(f^{\prime},1),\quad j=1,2.

Now,
note that
L_{1}\in\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})
iff
L_{1}(1)\geq 0
, and
L_{2}\in\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})
iff
L_{2}(-1)\leq 0
. So, if

L_{1}(1)\geq 0

p:=L_{1}

L_{2}(-1)\leq 0

Lemma 4.8

(copositive approximation with interpolation at

(x-\lambda)f(x)\geq 0
,

,

\left\|f-P\right\|_{[a,b]}\leq c(b-a)^{2}{\omega}_{3}(f^{\prime\prime},b-a;[a,b]),\quad\text{where $c=c(\gamma)$.}

and
\lambda=0

P\in\Pi_{4}\cap\Delta^{(0)}(\{0\})

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}=\left\{y\in Y_{s}\;\;\big|\;\;y<x_{j}\right\}\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\{b_{1},\dots,b_{l}\}=\left\{y\in Y_{s}\;\;\big|\;\;y>x_{j}\right\}.

For even
m
, the same construction also works if points
a_{i}
’s and
b_{i}
’s are allowed to coalesce, which implies Hermite interpolation of
g_{m,\lambda}
by
R_{i}
’s at these points.
Hence, it is sufficient to set

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}:=\bigcup_{y\in Y_{s},\;y<x_{j}}\big\{\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{r^{\prime}}\big\}\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A_{p},\;\alpha<x_{j}}\big\{\underbrace{\alpha,\dots,\alpha}_{r^{\prime\prime}}\big\}

and

\{b_{1},\dots,b_{l}\}:=\bigcup_{y\in Y_{s},\;y>x_{j}}\big\{\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{r^{\prime}}\big\}\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A_{p},\;\alpha>x_{j}}\big\{\underbrace{\alpha,\dots,\alpha}_{r^{\prime\prime}}\big\},

where

r^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime\prime}
are, respectively, odd and even integers which are not less than
r+1
(for example, we can set
r^{\prime}:=2r+1
and
r^{\prime\prime}:=2(r+1)
),
so that
R_{i}-g_{m,\lambda}
,
i=1,2
, changes its sign at the points in
Y_{s}
and does not change it at the points in
A_{p}
.

A byproduct of the construction in
[
hky
]
*Lemma 19 was the fact that
R_{i}(\lambda)=0
,
i=1,2
in the case
m>0
. This makes it impossible for the same construction to work for odd
m
. For example, if
m=1
, then it is clear that there does not exist a polynomial
R\in\Pi_{n}
such that
R(\lambda)=0
and, at the same time,
R\geq g_{1,\lambda}
on
(\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)
.

Case 2: odd
m
.
Suppose that
m
is odd, and let
n^{\prime}:=2n
,
j^{\prime}:=2j
and

Y_{s}^{\prime}:=Y_{s}\cup\{y^{\prime}\}
, where
y^{\prime}:=x_{j^{\prime}+1,n^{\prime}}
.

Note that

x_{j^{\prime},n^{\prime}}=x_{j},\quad I_{j^{\prime}+1,n^{\prime}}=[x_{j^{\prime}+1,n^{\prime}},x_{j^{\prime},n^{\prime}}]\subset I_{j}\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\dist\{y^{\prime},\{x_{j},x_{j+1}\}\geq|I_{j+1}|/4.

It now follows from the statement of the lemma for even
m
(Case 1 above) that there exist polynomials
R_{1}^{\prime}
and
R_{2}^{\prime}
such that

(5.4)

R_{1}^{\prime},R_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{I}}^{(r)}(g_{m-1,\lambda},A_{p}\cup Y_{s}^{\prime}),

(5.5)

\left\{R_{1}^{\prime}-g_{m-1,\lambda},g_{m-1,\lambda}-R_{2}^{\prime}\right\}\subset\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s}^{\prime}),

and

(5.6)

\left|R_{i}^{\prime}(x)-g_{m-1,\lambda}(x)\right|\leq c(\mu)\psi_{j}(x)^{\mu-m+1}|I_{j}|^{m-1},\quad\text{for all $x\in I$ and $i=1,2$.}

We define

R_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x):=(x-\lambda)R_{i}^{\prime}(x),\quad i=1,2,

and note that
R_{1}^{\prime\prime},R_{2}^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{I}}^{(r)}(g_{m,\lambda},A_{p}\cup Y_{s})
and, for all
x\in I
and
i=1,2
,

(5.7)

\left|R_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x)-g_{m,\lambda}(x)\right|\leq c(\mu)|x-\lambda|\psi_{j}(x)^{\mu-m+1}|I_{j}|^{m-1}\leq c(\mu)\psi_{j}(x)^{\mu-m}|I_{j}|^{m},

since

|x-\lambda|\leq|x-x_{j}|+|I_{j}|=\psi_{j}(x)^{-1}|I_{j}|.

Let
J
be the index such that
y_{i}\in[x_{j-2},1]
for
1\leq i\leq J
, and
y_{i}\in[-1,x_{j+1}]
for
J+1\leq i\leq s
, and note that, if
J=0
or
J=s
, then there are no
y_{i}
’s in the intervals

[x_{j-2},1]
or
[-1,x_{j+1}]
, respectively.

Now, letting
h
be either
R_{1}^{\prime}-g_{m-1,\lambda}
or
g_{m-1,\lambda}-R_{2}^{\prime}
, we
note that

h\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s}^{\prime})\quad\iff\quad\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}h\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{i+1},y_{i}]$, for $0\leq i\leq J-1$,}\\
(-1)^{J}h\geq 0&\text{on $[y^{\prime},y_{J}]$,}\\
(-1)^{J+1}h\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{J+1},y^{\prime}]$,}\\
(-1)^{i+1}h\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{i+1},y_{i}]$, for $J+1\leq i\leq s+1$,}\end{cases}

and so, for
h^{\prime}:=(\cdot-\lambda)h
, we have

\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}h^{\prime}\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{i+1},y_{i}]$, for $0\leq i\leq J-1$,}\\
(-1)^{J}h^{\prime}\geq 0&\text{on $[\lambda,y_{J}]$,}\\
(-1)^{J+1}h^{\prime}\geq 0&\text{on $[y^{\prime},\lambda]$,}\\
(-1)^{J}h^{\prime}\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{J+1},y^{\prime}]$,}\\
(-1)^{i}h^{\prime}\geq 0&\text{on $[y_{i+1},y_{i}]$, for $J+1\leq i\leq s+1$.}\end{cases}

In other words, all the right inequalities for
h^{\prime}\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
are satisfied except that the restriction
(-1)^{J}h^{\prime}\geq 0
that we need on
[y_{J+1},y_{J}]
fails on its small subinterval
[y^{\prime},\lambda]
.
Hence, the polynomials
R_{i}^{\prime\prime}
,
i=1,2
, need to be corrected on
[y^{\prime},\lambda]

in order to guarantee that

\widetilde{R}_{1}^{\prime\prime}-g_{m,\lambda},g_{m,\lambda}-\widetilde{R}_{2}^{\prime\prime}\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
, where
\widetilde{R}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
,
i=1,2
, are these corrected polynomials.

We note that it follows from (
5.7
) that

\left\|h^{\prime}\right\|\leq c(\mu)\left\|\psi_{j}^{\mu-m}\right\|\cdot|I_{j}|^{m}\leq c(\mu)|I_{j}|^{m}:=c_{1}|I_{j}|^{m}.

We now recall (see
e.g.
[
dzya
]
) that the algebraic polynomial of degree
\leq 4n-2
,

t_{j}(x):=(x-x_{j}^{0})^{-2}\cos^{2}2n\arccos x+(x-\bar{x}_{j})^{-2}\sin^{2}2n\arccos x,

where

\bar{x}_{j}:=\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad x_{j}^{0}:=\begin{cases}\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{n}-\frac{\pi}{4n}\right),&\text{if $j<n/2$,}\\
\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{n}-\frac{3\pi}{4n}\right),&\text{if $j\geq n/2$,}\\
\end{cases}

satisfies

|I_{j}|^{-2}\psi_{j}(x)^{2}\leq t_{j}(x)\leq 4000|I_{j}|^{-2}\psi_{j}(x)^{2},\quad\text{$x\in I$ and $1\leq j\leq n-1$.}

We now define

H(x):=t_{j}(x)^{\mu^{\prime}}|I_{j}|^{2\mu^{\prime}}\prod_{y\in Y_{s}}\frac{(x-y)^{2r+1}}{|x_{j}-y|^{2r+1}}\prod_{\alpha\in A_{p}}\frac{(x-\alpha)^{2r+2}}{|x_{j}-\alpha|^{2r+2}}

with
\mu^{\prime}:=\mu+(2r+2)(s+p)
, for example,
and note that

H\in{\mathcal{I}}^{(r)}(0,A_{p}\cup Y_{s})\cap\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})

(in particular,
(-1)^{J}H\geq 0
on
[y_{J+1},y_{J}]
),

\displaystyle|H(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c\psi_{j}(x)^{2\mu^{\prime}}\prod_{y\in Y_{s}}\frac{|x-y|^{2r+1}}{|x_{j}-y|^{2r+1}}\prod_{\alpha\in A_{p}}\frac{|x-\alpha|^{2r+2}}{|x_{j}-\alpha|^{2r+2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\psi_{j}(x)^{2\mu^{\prime}-(2r+2)(s+p)}\leq c\psi_{j}(x)^{\mu},\quad x\in I,

since, for
\beta\in Y_{s}\cup A_{p}
,

\displaystyle\frac{|x-\beta|}{|x_{j}-\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{|x-x_{j}|}{|x_{j}-\beta|}\leq 1+\frac{|x-x_{j}|+|I_{j}|}{|I_{j}|}\cdot\frac{|I_{j}|}{|x_{j}-\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\psi_{j}(x)^{-1}\cdot\frac{|I_{j}|}{\min\{|I_{j-1}|,|I_{j+1}|\}}\leq 1+3\psi_{j}(x)^{-1}\leq 4\psi_{j}(x)^{-1}.

Also, for
x\in[y^{\prime},\lambda]
, we have

t_{j}(x)\geq(|x-x_{j}|+|I_{j}|)^{-2}\geq\left(\max\{|y^{\prime}-x_{j}|+|I_{j}|,|\lambda-x_{j}|+|I_{j}|\}\right)^{-2}\geq 16^{-1}|I_{j}|^{-2}

and

\frac{|x-\beta|}{|x_{j}-\beta|}\geq\min\left\{\frac{|x_{j+1}-y^{\prime}|}{|x_{j+1}-x_{j}|},\frac{|x_{j-2}-\lambda|}{|x_{j-2}-x_{j}|}\right\}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Hence,

(-1)^{J}H(x)=|H(x)|\geq 4^{-2\mu^{\prime}-(2r+1)s-(2r+2)p}:=c_{2},\quad x\in[y^{\prime},\lambda].

This implies that, for the function

h^{\prime\prime}:=h^{\prime}+\widetilde{H},\quad\text{where $\widetilde{H}(x):=(c_{1}/c_{2})H(x)|I_{j}|^{m}$,}

we have

(-1)^{J}h^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq-\left\|h^{\prime}\right\|+(c_{1}/c_{2})(-1)^{J}H(x)|I_{j}|^{m}\geq 0,\quad x\in[y^{\prime},\lambda],

and so
h^{\prime\prime}\in\Delta^{(0)}(Y_{s})
.

It remains to notice that polynomials

\widetilde{R}_{1}^{\prime\prime}:=R_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\widetilde{H}\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\widetilde{R}_{2}^{\prime\prime}:=R_{2}^{\prime\prime}-\widetilde{H}

satisfy (
5.1
)-(
5.3
), and the proof is now complete.
∎

5.2
Approximation of general splines

If

we denote by

the subset
of

that do not have any knots “too close” to the

More precisely, if

,

is in

if and only if
S\in\Sigma_{k}(\mathbf{Z}_{n})
and, for every
1\leq i\leq m

to

(see

]

Appendix: Exact pointwise estimates

It is clear that if a polynomial
P_{n}
not only approximates
f
but also interpolates it (and perhaps its derivatives) then the rate of approximation (
1.2
)
can be improved near all interpolation points.

The following theorem follows from the results obtained in
[
kls21
]
.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
kls21
]
*Theorems 1.5, 1.6 and 1.7)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{0}\in I
,
r,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
f\in C^{r}
, and suppose that
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is such that
m\leq r
. If

is such that

(7.1)

|f(x)-P_{n}(x)|\leq A\rho_{n}^{r}(x){\omega}_{k}(f^{(r)},\rho_{n}(x)),\quad x\in I,

and

(7.2)

P_{n}^{(j)}(x_{0})=f^{(j)}(x_{0}),\quad\text{for }\;0\leq j\leq m,

then, for all

1\leq\ell\leq k
and
x\in I
, we have

(7.3)

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle f(x)-P_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c(k,r)A\begin{cases}|x-x_{0}|^{m+1}\rho_{n}^{r-m-1}(x)\omega_{\ell}(f^{(r)},\rho_{n}(x)),&\text{if }\;m\leq r-1,\\
|x-x_{0}|^{r}\omega_{\ell}(f^{(r)},|x-x_{0}|^{1/\ell}\rho_{n}^{1-1/\ell}(x)),&\text{if }\;m=r.\end{cases}

Moreover, estimates in
(
7.3
)
cannot be improved in the sense that none of the powers of

can be increased.

The following result immediately follows from Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
and
7.1
observing that, for any
(r,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\in\Upsilon_{\text{intertwining}}
, we have

,

, and so the case

in the statement of Theorem

does not hold.
Note also that we are incorporating the onesided case into a general result for intertwining approximation by allowing

to be

, in which case the restriction

becomes vacuous (recall that the restriction

Corollary 7.2

(intertwining approximation with interpolatory constraints)
.

Let
k,p\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
Y_{s}\in\mathbb{Y}_{s}
,
A_{p}\in\mathbb{A}_{p}
, and

)
.

Abstract
. Exponentially-localized Wannier functions are a basis of the Fermi projection of a Hamiltonian consisting of functions which decay exponentially fast in space. In two and three spatial dimensions, it is well understood for periodic insulators that exponentially-localized Wannier functions exist if and only if there exists an orthonormal basis for the Fermi projection with finite second moment (i.e. all basis elements satisfy ∫ | 𝒙 | 2 ​ | w ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) | 2 ​ d ​ 𝒙 < ∞ \int|\bm{x}|^{2}|w(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}<\infty ). In this work, we establish a similar result for non-periodic insulators in two spatial dimensions. In particular, we prove that if there exists an orthonormal basis for the Fermi projection which satisfies ∫ | 𝒙 | 5 + ϵ ​ | w ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) | 2 ​ d ​ 𝒙 < ∞ \int|\bm{x}|^{5+\epsilon}|w(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}<\infty for some ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 then there also exists an orthonormal basis for the Fermi projection which decays exponentially fast in space. This result lends support to the Localization Dichotomy Conjecture for non-periodic systems recently proposed by Marcelli, Monaco, Moscolari, and Panati in [ 12 , 13 ] .



1.
Introduction

In electronic structure theory, we are often interested in localized representations of the occupied space. For band-insulating materials (i.e., materials where the Fermi energy lies the in the band gap) these local representations can be studied by constructing a well localized basis for a subspace known as the Fermi projection. Over the past few decades, exponentially localized Wannier functions (ELWFs) have emerged as the basis of choice for the Fermi projection in periodic insulators. Among the many applications, Wannier functions have been instrumental in constructing effective tight binding models and in the development of the modern theory of polarization. We refer the readers to the review article
[
14
]
and references therein for more detailed discussions on the Wannier functions from a physical point of view.

Given the importance of ELWFs, there have been much research devoted to understanding when ELWFs exist. As a result of these efforts, it is now well understood that for periodic insulators in dimension one, two, and three the existence of ELWFs is tied to the vanishing of certain topological invariants. 1.2.
Connection with Previous Work and Discussions

As discussed in the beginning of this section, for a two dimensional gapped, periodic Schödinger operator, it is now well understood that a collection of isolated bands admit a well localized basis if and only if the Chern number associated with those bands vanish. Given the nature of topological invariants, it is natural to expect that the localization dichotomy should also be true under small perturbations which do not close the gap of the Hamiltonian. The main result of this paper shows a weaker form of the localization dichotomy for
all
gapped systems, not just those which are close to periodic.

Since we make no symmetry assumptions on our underlying system, the usual method of applying the Bloch-Floquet transform to construct Wannier functions cannot be applied. Instead, we use an alternate technique for constructing (generalized) Wannier functions first proposed by Kivelson and was later expanded by Nenciu-Nenciu
1
1

1

We will only highlight the key ideas of the Kivelson and Nenciu-Nenciu return to discuss these works in more detail in Section
3
.
. In these works, the authors proposed defining the Wannier functions of a one dimensional system to be the eigenfunctions of the operator
PXP
. For periodic systems, this definition agrees with the usual definition of Wannier functions and are exponentially localized
[
17
]
.

While the eigenfunctions of
PXP
are a simple way of constructing ELWFs in one dimension, extending this idea to two dimensions is not straightforward due to the existence of topological obstructions. Following our previous work
[
10
]
, we construct ELWFs by assuming it is possible to decompose the projector
P
into a family of mutually orthogonal projectors
\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
so that each
P_{j}

X

X

P_{j}
is effectively one dimensional. Therefore, by extending the results of Kivelson and Nenciu-Nenciu, it can be shown that for each

are exponentially localized in both
X

Y

In periodic systems, the ability to decompose

\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is both necessary and sufficient for the system to be topologically trivial as a consequence of the localization dichotomy. To see why this is the case, suppose that the set
\{\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)},\cdots,\psi^{(N)}\}

N

\{T_{(j,k)}:(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}

inherits the group structure of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in the obvious way. In this case, the projectors
\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
can be constructed by the formula:

(16)

P_{j}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T_{(j,k)}|\psi^{(n)}\rangle\langle\psi^{(n)}|T_{(j,k)}^{\dagger}.

If we additionally assume that
\psi^{(n)}

P_{j}
admits an exponentially localized kernel and is localized in
X

\{P_{j}\}
can be constructed in both periodic and non-periodic systems

assuming the existence of an exponentially localized basis; fast enough algebraic decay suffices.

Given a point

Projected Position Operators and Exponential Localization

In this section, we will review our strategy for proving the existence of an exponentially localized basis of
\range{(P)}

3.1.
Projected Position Operators in One Dimension

The idea of relating projected position operators can be traced back to a proposition by Kivelson for defining Wannier functions in non-periodic system. In
[
7
]
, Kivelson proposed defining generalized Wannier functions to be the eigenfunctions of the projected position operator
PXP
. To support this proposal, Kivelson showed in centrosymmetric crystals the exponentially localized Wannier functions found by Kohn in

8

are in fact eigenfunctions of
PXP
. After the work by Kivelson, Niu
[
20
]
argued heuristically that in one dimension the eigenfunctions of
PXP
should decay faster than any polynomial. Later, Nenciu-Nenciu
[
17
]
showed rigorously under very general assumptions in one dimension the operator
PXP
has discrete spectrum and its eigenfunctions are exponentially localized.

While Nenciu-Nenciu’s original result was for spectral projectors of Hamiltonians of a particular form, using techniques developed in
[
10
]
, one can show that in fact the eigenfunctions of
PXP
exist and are exponentially localized only making use of the fact that
P
admits an exponentially localized kernel. In particular, one can show the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projector on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
P
admits an exponentially localized kernel then the following is true

(1)

The operator
PXP
is essentially self-adjoint.

(2)

The operator
PXP
has discrete spectrum.

(3)

There exists finite, positive constants
(C,\tilde{\gamma})
such that if
\psi\in\range{(P)}
and
PXP\psi=\eta\psi
, then

(24)

\int e^{2\tilde{\gamma}\sqrt{1+(x-\eta)^{2}}}|\psi(x)|^{2}\,\text{\emph{d}}x\leq C.

Therefore by the spectral theorem, the collection of all eigenfunctions of the operator
PXP
form an exponentially localized, orthogonal basis for
\range{(P)}
.

Unfortunately, in two dimensions the operator
PXP
does not generically have compact resolvent and therefore
PXP
may not have any

-eigenvectors.

for
PXP
via the spectral theorem as follows:

Definition 5

(Band Projectors for
PXP
)
.

Suppose that
PXP
is essentially self-adjoint and satisfies the uniform spectral gaps assumption for a collection of sets
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
. We define the
band projectors
for
PXP
as the collection of orthogonal projectors
\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
where
P_{j}
is the spectral projection associated with
\sigma_{j}
.

Intuitively speaking, since
P_{j}
is a spectral projection for
PXP
associated with the bounded set
\sigma_{j}
, we can expect that functions from
\range{(P_{j})}
will be concentrated in the strip
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\in\sigma_{j}\}
. Additionally, since there is some separation between the “bands” of
PXP
, using techniques from Combes-Thomas-Agmon theory, it can be shown that the projectors
P_{j}
admit each admit an exponentially localized kernel with a rate which is
\Omega(d^{-1})
. Hence, by Theorem
3
, if
PXP
has uniform spectral gaps, then
\range{(P)}
admits a basis of exponentially localized generalized Wannier functions.

While
PXP
having uniform spectral gaps is a sufficient condition to prove the existence of exponentially localized generalized Wannier functions it is not a necessary condition. In
[
24
]
, we show that in time reversal symmetric systems with non-zero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-invariant,
PXP
cannot have uniform spectral gaps. Despite this difficulty, the main techniques for proving Theorem
3
do not strongly rely on the specific properties of the position operator
X
. In fact, we can replace
X
with any essentially self-adjoint operator which is not “too far from
X
”. More formally, we have the following result:

Lemma 3.1

(adapted from
[
10
, Lemma A.4]
)
.

Suppose that
\widehat{X}
is a symmetric operator and there exist constants
(C,C^{\prime},\gamma^{*})
so that for all
\gamma\leq\gamma^{*}
:

(1)

\|\widehat{X}-X\|\leq C
.

(2)

\|\widehat{X}_{\gamma}-\widehat{X}\|\leq C^{\prime}\gamma

(3)

P\widehat{X}P
has uniform spectral gaps.

If
\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
are the band projectors of
P\widehat{X}P
, then the family
\{P_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3
.

The main result of this paper is to show that if there exists a generalized Wannier basis for
\range{(P)}
which is
s
-localized for
s>5/2
, then it is possible to construct an operator
\widehat{X}
satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
and hence
\range{(P)}
admits an exponentially localized generalized Wannier basis by Theorem
3
. Henceforth, we will assume that we have fixed an
s
-localized basis
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}
with centers points
\{\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\}
where
s>5/2
.

3.3.
Paper Organization

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
4
, we show that as a consequence of the exponential localization of
P
, the center points of any generalized Wannier basis cannot be too strongly clustered; a property we term as having “bounded density”. Using the notion of bounded density, we then construct our new position operator
\widehat{X}
in Section
5
. In the following sections, we prove the necessary properties of
\widehat{X}
. In particular, in Section
6
we show that
\widehat{X}
is close to
X
in spectral norm, in Section
7
we show that
\widehat{X}
is exponentially localized, and finally in Section
8
we show that
P\widehat{X}P
has uniform spectral gaps.

4.
On the center points of generalized Wannier bases

A key part of our construction of
\widehat{X}
revolves around the center points of the generalized Wannier basis
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}
. Unlike in the periodic case, where the set of center points is closed under lattice translations, for non-periodic systems there is a large amount of freedom in the choice of center points. For sake of argument, let us fix some element of the generalized Wannier basis
\psi_{\alpha}
with center point
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}
. By definition there exists a constant
C
so that

(31)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\rangle^{2s}|\psi_{\alpha}(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}\leq C.

Now, let us fix some
\bm{v}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If we perform the mapping
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\mapsto\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}+\bm{v}_{\alpha}
, by triangle inequality and the fact that
|a+b|^{p}\leq 2^{p}(|a|^{p}+|b|^{p})
one easily checks that

(32)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\bm{x}-(\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}+\bm{v}_{\alpha})\rangle^{2s}|\psi_{\alpha}(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}\leq 2^{2s}(C+|\bm{v}_{\alpha}|^{2s}).

Hence, so long as
|\bm{v}_{\alpha}|
is bounded, at the price of making the constant
C
slightly worse, we could have equally well taken the center point of
\psi_{\alpha}
to be
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}+\bm{v}_{\alpha}
instead of
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}
.

Despite this apparent freedom in the choice of center points, the center points of a localized generalized Wannier basis is not arbitrary; the fact that
P
has an exponentially localized kernel puts a restriction on how densely the center points may cluster. In particular, as shown in
[
11
]
, we have the following result:

Definition 6
.

We say that a collection of points
\{\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
has
bounded density
if there exists a constant
M<\infty
such that for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

(33)

\#\{\alpha:|\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}-\bm{x}|\leq 1\}\leq M

Lemma 4.1

(
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projector which admits an exponentially localized kernel. If
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
is an
s
-localized generalized Wannier basis for
P
for some
s>0
, then the center points for
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
have bounded density.

Since we are free to move each center point by a constant amount and all localized basis for
\range{(P)}
have center points with bounded density, we can assume without loss of generality that these center points lie on the integer lattice:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
is a
s
-localized basis with center points
\{\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
. If we additionally assume that the center points have bounded density, then we may find a positive integer
M
so that we can relabel the basis as
\{\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\}
where
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
j\in\{1,\cdots,M\}
. Furthermore, the center point of
\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}
can be taken to be
\bm{m}
without loss of generality.

Proof Sketch.

For each
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
let us define the unit square centered at
\bm{m}
as follows

(34)

S_{\bm{m}}:=\bigg[m_{1}-\frac{1}{2},m_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\times\bigg[m_{2}-\frac{1}{2},m_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg).

Since the basis
\{\psi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
has center points with bounded density, we know that there are at most
M
center points contained in the square
S_{\bm{m}}
(as it is contained in the Euclidean ball of radius 1 centered at
\bm{m}
). Because of this, we can relabel this basis as
\{\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\}
where
\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}
has its center in
S_{\bm{m}}
and
j
is a degeneracy index which runs from
\{1,\cdots,M\}
. If
S_{\bm{m}}
has fewer than
M
center points, say it has
j^{*}
, then we define
\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\equiv 0
for all
j>j^{*}
. Strictly speaking this enlarged set is no longer a basis, but it does not matter as we will be interested in controlling various spectral norms which are unchanged by this enlargement.
∎

By using the relabeling from Lemma
4.2
, we can now construct the operator
\widehat{X}
.

5.
Construction of
\widehat{X}

In the previous section, we have relabeled our
s
-localized basis as
\{\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\}
and discretized its center points to lie on the integer lattice. We will now use this discretization of the center points to define a new position operator
\tilde{X}
as follows:

(35)

\tilde{X}:=\sum_{\bm{m},j}m_{1}|\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\rangle\langle\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}|+QXQ.

Since
PQ=QP=0
it’s clear that
\sigma(P\tilde{X}P)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and therefore
P\tilde{X}P
has uniform spectral gaps. Furthermore, it can be shown that if
\{\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\}
is
s
-localized with
s>2
then
\|\tilde{X}-X\|=O(1)
. Hence, it can be shown that
\tilde{X}
satisfies Assumptions (1) and (3) of Lemma
3.1
. Unfortunately, it doesn’t seem possible to prove that
\tilde{X}
satisfies Assumption (2) since the basis
\{\psi_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}\}
only decays algebraically quickly.

To address this issue, we will define a new position operator
\widehat{X}
which modifies
\tilde{X}
so that it satisfies Assumption (2) while still preserving Assumptions (1) and (3). Following the approach used by Hastings in
[
5
]
, we define the operator
\widehat{X}
as follows:

(36)

\widehat{X}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(t_{1})f(t_{2})e^{i(Xt_{1}+Yt_{2})/\Delta}\tilde{X}e^{-i(Xt_{1}+Yt_{2})/\Delta}\,\textrm{d}{t_{1}}\,\textrm{d}{t_{2}}

Here
\Delta
is a finite parameter to be chosen as part of our proofs and
f(t)
is a filter function defined in terms of its Fourier transform as follows:

(37)

\hat{f}(\xi)=\begin{cases}(1-|\xi|^{2})^{3}&|\xi|\leq 1\\
0&|\xi|\geq 1\\
\end{cases}

Note that since
\hat{f}(\xi)
is
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
tf(t)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Also, note that
\int f(t)\,\textrm{d}{t}=\hat{f}(0)=1
and
\hat{f}
is an even function so
f
is real valued.

The effect of this modification is best understood in the special case where
\tilde{X}
and
\widehat{X}
are both matrices and
X
and
Y
are integer valued. In this case, we can find a simultaneous eigenbasis for
X
and
Y
,
\{e_{\bm{\lambda}}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
so that for each
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we have that:

(38)

Xe_{\bm{\lambda}}=\lambda_{1}e_{\bm{\lambda}}\quad\text{and}\quad Ye_{\bm{\lambda}}=\lambda_{2}e_{\bm{\lambda}}.

One then easily checks that

(39)

\langle e_{\bm{\lambda}},\widehat{X}e_{\bm{\mu}}\rangle=\langle e_{\bm{\lambda}},\tilde{X}e_{\bm{\mu}}\rangle\hat{f}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}}{\Delta}\right)\hat{f}\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}}{\Delta}\right).

Since
\hat{f}(0)=1
and
\hat{f}
is compactly supported, this calculation shows that the entries of
\widehat{X}
are roughly equal to the entries of
\widehat{X}
near the diagonal and entries which are distance
\gtrsim\Delta
away from the diagonal are set to zero.

Now that we’ve defined
\widehat{X}
, to prove the main theorem it remains to show that
\widehat{X}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
. We prove
\widehat{X}
that satisfies Assumption (1) in Section
6
, Assumption (2) in Section
7
, and Assumption (3) in Section
8
.

Using reverse triangle inequality and elementary calculus one can check that

Due to Proposition

. Hence there exists a constant

which is what we wanted to show.

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose that

is an orthogonal projector which admits an exponentially localized kernel (Definition

) and

. Then there exists a finite positive constants

&\|P_{\gamma}XQ_{\gamma}-PXQ\|\leq C^{\prime}\gamma.\end{split}

Proof.

We will only prove the first bound, the second bound follows by analogous calculations. Since

we have that

(88)

\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}XP_{\gamma}-QXP

\displaystyle=Q_{\gamma}[X,P_{\gamma}]-Q[X,P]

\displaystyle=(Q_{\gamma}-Q+Q)[X,P_{\gamma}]-Q[X,P]

\displaystyle=(Q_{\gamma}-Q)[X,P_{\gamma}]-Q[X,P_{\gamma}-P]

Using the fact that

&\|[X,P_{\gamma}-P]\|\leq C_{3}\gamma\end{split}

Hence,

and the result is proved.
∎

Hence applying Lemma

While it is possible to prove the bound in Equation (

Appendix A

We collect two technical lemmas here which will be used in other parts of the proof.

A.1.
Decay Lemma

For the statement of our technical lemmas, for each
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we introduce special notation for characteristic function of the unit box centered at
\bm{k}
:

(132)

\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})=\begin{cases}1&\bm{x}\in\left[k_{1}-\frac{1}{2},k_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\times\left[k_{2}-\frac{1}{2},k_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Using this notation, we now state the following result:

Lemma A.1
.

For any
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
, any
\bm{m},\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and any
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

(133)

\|\chi_{\bm{k}}v\|\leq\frac{2^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\|\chi_{\bm{k}}(|X-m_{1}|+1)^{s_{1}}(|Y-m_{2}|+1)^{s_{2}}v\|}{\langle m_{1}-k_{1}\rangle^{s_{1}}\langle m_{2}-k_{2}\rangle^{s_{2}}}

where
\langle x\rangle
is the Japanese bracket
\langle x\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.

Proof.

Instead of proving Equation (
133
) directly we will instead prove that:

(134)

\|\chi_{\bm{k}}v\|\leq\frac{\|\chi_{\bm{k}}(|X-m_{1}|+1)^{s_{1}}(|Y-m_{2}|+1)^{s_{2}}v\|}{(|m_{1}-k_{1}|+1/2)^{s_{1}}(|m_{2}-k_{2}|+1/2)^{s_{2}}}

Proving Equation (
134
) is sufficient since for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
one can check that

(135)

\sqrt{1+a^{2}}\leq 2||a|+1/2|.

Therefore, for any
\bm{m},\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and any
s>0
we have that for
i=1,2
:

(136)

\displaystyle(|m_{i}-k_{i}|+1/2)^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{s}\langle m_{i}-k_{i}\rangle^{-s}

Hence, the proving Equation (
134
) implies Equation (
133
).

We will now prove Equation (
134
) in the case where
m_{1}\neq k_{1}
and
m_{2}\neq k_{2}
; the other cases follow easily using similar arguments. Our main tool for proving Equation (
134
) will be to use “strip” characteristic functions in
X
and
Y
:

(137)

\displaystyle\chi\{|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq d\}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq d\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(138)

\displaystyle\chi\{|x_{2}-m_{2}|\leq d\}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&|x_{2}-m_{2}|\leq d\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The key observation is that characteristic functions

(139)

\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\chi\{|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq|m_{1}-k_{1}|-1/2\}(\bm{x})

have disjoint supports (up to a set of measure zero). Therefore,

(140)

\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})=\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})(1-\chi\{|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq|m_{1}-k|-1/2\}(\bm{x})).

Using Equation (
140
) for any function
v
we have that:

(141)

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\chi_{\bm{k}}v\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\bm{k}}|v(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}

(142)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})(1-\chi\{|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq|m_{1}-k_{1}|-1/2\}(\bm{x}))|v(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}

(143)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})(1-\chi\{|x_{1}-m_{1}|\leq|m_{1}-k_{1}|-1/2\})(\bm{x})\frac{(1+|x_{1}-m_{1}|)^{2s_{1}}}{(1+|x_{1}-m_{1}|)^{2s_{1}}}|v(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\textrm{d}{\bm{x}}

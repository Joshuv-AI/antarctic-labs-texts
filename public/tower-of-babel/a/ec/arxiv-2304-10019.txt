Black holes in a new gravitational theory with trace anomalies Preprint: WUCG-23-05
Shinji Tsujikawa

Abstract
In a new gravitational theory with the trace anomaly recently proposed by Gabadadze, we study the existence of hairy black hole solutions on a static and spherically symmetric background. In this theory, the effective 4-dimensional action contains a kinetic term of the conformal scalar field related to a new scale M ¯ \bar{M} much below the Planck mass. This property can overcome a strong coupling problem known to be present in general relativity supplemented by the trace anomaly as well as in 4-dimensional Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity. We find a new hairy black hole solution arising from the Gauss-Bonnet trace anomaly, which satisfies regular boundary conditions of the conformal scalar and metric on the horizon. Unlike unstable exact black hole solutions with a divergent derivative of the scalar on the horizon derived for some related theories in the literature, we show that our hairy black hole solution can be consistent with all the linear stability conditions of odd- and even-parity perturbations.

Introduction

It is well known that the quantum field theory of gravitation gives rise to
a trace anomaly which is absent at classical level.
Quantum corrections to the graviton propagator arising from

\left(M^{2}e^{2\phi}-\bar{M}^{2}\right)\bar{G}_{\mu\nu}=-M^{2}e^{2\phi}\left[2\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi-2\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi+2\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\left(\bar{\square}\phi-\bar{X}\right)\right]-\alpha\bar{{\cal H}}_{\mu\nu}-2\beta\phi\left(2\bar{\nabla}^{\rho}\bar{\nabla}^{\sigma}+\bar{R}^{\rho\sigma}\right)\bar{C}_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}\,,

where

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal H}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-4\bar{X}\bar{G}_{\mu\nu}+4\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}\phi\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi-\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi\right)\left(\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi-\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi\right)+4\left(\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\phi-\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi\right)\bar{\square}\phi

(20)

\displaystyle+\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\left[2(\bar{\square}\phi)^{2}-4\bar{X}^{2}+2\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}\phi\left(2\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi\bar{\nabla}^{\beta}\phi-\bar{\nabla}^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi\right)\right]+4\bar{{\cal P}}_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\left(\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi\bar{\nabla}^{\beta}\phi-\bar{\nabla}^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi\right)\,,

with

\bar{{\cal P}}_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\equiv\bar{R}_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}+\bar{g}_{\mu\beta}\bar{R}_{\alpha\nu}-\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{R}_{\alpha\beta}+\bar{g}_{\alpha\nu}\bar{R}_{\mu\beta}-\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}-\bar{g}_{\mu\beta}\bar{g}_{\alpha\nu}\right)\bar{R}\,.

(21)

We take the trace of Eq. (
19
) by exerting
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}

and exploit the relation
12\bar{X}-6\bar{\square}\phi=e^{2\phi}R-\bar{R}
.
On using the property
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{\cal P}_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}=-\bar{G}_{\alpha\beta}
,
it follows that
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{{\cal H}}_{\mu\nu}=(e^{4\phi}{\cal G}-\bar{\cal G})/2
. We also note that the divergence of the Weyl tensor
vanishes, such that
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{C}_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}=0

Then, the trace of Eq. (
19

2\bar{M}^{2}\bar{R}-\alpha\bar{\cal G}=e^{4\phi}\left(2M^{2}R-\alpha{\cal G}\right)\,.

(22)

Varying the action
{\cal S}_{R\bar{R}}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\left[M^{2}e^{2\phi}(\bar{R}-12\bar{X}\right)-\bar{M}^{2}\bar{R}]
with respect to
\phi

\frac{\delta{\cal S}_{R\bar{R}}}{\delta\phi}=2\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,M^{2}e^{2\phi}\left(\bar{R}+12\bar{X}-6\bar{\square}\phi\right)=2\sqrt{-g}M^{2}R\,.

(23)

From Eq. (
10
), the variation of the trace anomaly action is
given by
\delta{\cal S}_{A}/\delta\phi=\sqrt{-g}\left(-\alpha{\cal G}+\beta W^{2}\right)
.
Then, varying the action
{\cal S}={\cal S}_{R\bar{R}}+{\cal S}_{A}

with respect to
\phi
leads to

2M^{2}R-\alpha{\cal G}+\beta W^{2}=0\,,

(24)

which is the scalar field equation in the frame with the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.
From Eqs. (
22
) and (
24

2\bar{M}^{2}\bar{R}-\alpha\bar{\cal G}+\beta\bar{W}^{2}=0\,.

(25)

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}

The Weyl curvature term in Eq. (

equations of motion higher than second order.
If the Weyl trace anomaly is absent, i.e.,

\beta=0\,,

then the action (

\displaystyle=

IV
Linear stability of hairy black hole

Finally, we study the linear stability of hairy BHs derived
in Sec.
III

(
45
)-(
47
) obtained under the expansion
of a small coupling constant
\alpha
in the frame with
the metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}

The perturbations on the static and spherically symmetric background
can be decomposed into odd- and even-parity
modes
Regge and Wheeler 1957
;
Zerilli 1970

In Refs.
Kobayashi et al. 2012

Kobayashi et al. 2014

conditions of BHs against odd- and even-parity linear

even-parity modes.
In Ref.

with the background density
\rho

all the linear stability conditions applicable to NSs
as well (see also Ref.
Kase et al. 2020

We will exploit those results in the following discussion.

In Horndeski theories given by the action (
29
), the
odd-parity sector has a gravitational wave mode
\chi

arising from the metric perturbation.
For this perturbation

, there are neither ghost nor Laplacian
instabilities under the following conditions

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal G}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2\bar{G}_{4}+2\bar{h}\phi^{\prime 2}\bar{G}_{4,\bar{X}}-\bar{h}\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\bar{G}_{5,\phi}+{\frac{\bar{f}^{\prime}\bar{h}\phi^{\prime}\bar{G}_{5,\bar{X}}}{2\bar{f}}}\right)=2(M^{2}e^{2\phi}-\bar{M}^{2})+4\alpha\frac{\bar{h}}{\bar{f}}\phi^{\prime}(\bar{f}\phi^{\prime}+\bar{f}^{\prime})>0\,,

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2\bar{G}_{4}+\bar{h}\phi^{\prime 2}\bar{G}_{5,\phi}-\bar{h}\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{h}^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+\bar{h}\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)\bar{G}_{5,\bar{X}}=2(M^{2}e^{2\phi}-\bar{M}^{2})+4\alpha(2\bar{h}\phi^{\prime\prime}+\bar{h}^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-\bar{h}\phi^{\prime 2})>0\,,

All the propagation speeds discussed above are close to 1

V
Conclusions

In a new gravitational theory with the trace anomaly
recently proposed by Gabadadze,
we studied the existence of hairy BH solutions
on the static and spherically symmetric background.
Introducing the action
-\bar{M}^{2}\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{R}

besides the Einstein-Hilbert term allows a possibility for avoiding the
strong coupling problem present in GR supplemented by the trace anomaly.
In terms of the metric tensor
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=e^{-2\phi}g_{\mu\nu}

invariant under Weyl transformations

g_{\mu\nu}\to e^{2\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}
and

\phi\to\phi+\sigma
, the total action

{\cal S}={\cal S}_{R\bar{R}}+{\cal S}_{A}
is expressed
in the form (
17
).
This is the EFT valid below the mass scale
\bar{M}
,
in which regime nonlinear derivatives of the conformal
scalar field are weakly coupled.
Thanks to the presence of the action

-\bar{M}^{2}\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{R}
,
the scalar field acquires a kinetic term without the ghost.
We note that 4DEGB gravity is plagued by the strong
coupling problem because of the absence of
such a healthy kinetic term of the radion field.

In Eq. (
19
), we derived the covariant gravitational
field equations by varying the action (
17
)
with respect to
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
.
The trace of this equation can be expressed in the simple form
(
22
). Varying the action with respect to
\phi

leads to the scalar field equation
2M^{2}R-\alpha{\cal G}+\beta W^{2}=0

and hence the combination with Eq. (
22
) gives

2\bar{M}^{2}\bar{R}-\alpha\bar{\cal G}+\beta\bar{W}^{2}=0
.
Thus, the GB and Weyl terms explicitly affect the spacetime
geometry in both frames with the metrics

g_{\mu\nu}
and
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
.
So long as the Weyl trace anomaly is absent, i.e.,
\beta=0
,
the resulting theory is equivalent to a subclass of Horndeski theories
given by the action (
27
) with the coupling functions (
28
).
In this paper, we studied whether the GB trace anomaly induces
a hairy BH solution without instabilities.

In Sec.
III
, we first showed that the linearly stable
BH solution in theories without the GB trace anomaly (
\alpha=0
)
is restricted to a no-hair Schwarzschild solution given by Eq. (
37
).
In this case there exists the other hairy BH solution (
36
) originally
found by BBMB for a conformally invariant scalar, but this solution is known
to be unstable (see Appendix
A
).
For
\alpha\neq 0
, we derived the solutions to
\bar{f}(\bar{r})
,

\bar{h}(\bar{r})
, and
\phi(\bar{r})
expanded with respect to
a small coupling constant
\alpha
in the frame with
the metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
.
The BH solution expanded up to second order in
\alpha^{i}
,
which is consistent with regular boundary conditions on the horizon
and at spatial infinity, is given by Eqs. (
45
)-(
47
),
with the BH scalar charge (
50
).

The two metric components
\bar{f}(\bar{r})
and
\bar{h}(\bar{r})

are not proportional to each other for our BH solution,
so it is different from the exact solution derived
by imposing the condition

\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})

(see Appendix
B
).
Indeed, the latter is not consistent with all the
field equations of motion and regular boundary conditions
on the horizon. As we observe in Fig.
1
, our
analytic hairy BH solution (
45
)-(
47
), which is
valid for
\hat{\alpha}\ll 1
, exhibits good agreement
with the numerical results.
We also derived the metric components
f(r)
and
h(r)

in the frame with the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
as
(
51
)-(
52
) and computed the fifth force
exerted on a test particle away from the horizon.

In Sec.
IV
, we studied the stability of the hairy BH
solution (
45
)-(
47
) against linear perturbations
on the background metric (
30
).
In the odd-parity sector, the stability conditions are given
by Eqs. (
56
)-(
58
).
Provided that
M^{2}e^{2\phi}-\bar{M}^{2}>0
, they can be
satisfied for the small coupling constant in the range
\hat{\alpha}\ll 1
.
Under this inequality, the no-ghost condition for even-parity
perturbations is also consistently satisfied without the
strong coupling problem.
The radial and angular propagation speeds of three dynamical
perturbations in the odd- and even-parity sectors are close to 1
with small corrections induced by the trace anomaly.
Thus, there are no Laplacian instabilities for our hairy BH solution
under the conditions
\hat{\alpha}\ll 1
and
\bar{M}\ll M
.
These properties are different from those for exact BH solutions
known in 4DEGB gravity, which are plagued by the strong coupling
as well as the linear instability problems
Tsujikawa 2022
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2023
.

Since the BH solution obtained in this paper possesses the scalar charge
(
50
), it may be possible to probe its signature from
gravitational waves emitted from the binary system
containing BHs (see e.g., Refs.
Will 1994
;
Yunes and Pretorius 2009
;
Higashino and Tsujikawa 2023
;
Quartin et al. 2023
).
While we have neglected effects of the Weyl trace anomaly
on the BH solutions and its stabilities, it may also give rise to an
additional scalar hair for BHs and possibly for NSs.
Since the Weyl term gives rise to the field equations of motion
containing derivatives higher than second order, the BH
stability conditions derived for Horndeski theories cannot be
literally applied to Weyl gravity theories.
In the EFT scheme, there should be some way of properly
dealing with such higher-order derivative terms.
It will be certainly of interest to study whether
the linearly stable BH solutions exist or not
in the presence of full trace anomaly terms.
The detailed studies of such issues are left for future works.

g_{\mu\nu}

;

In the frame with

), which we will derive from now on.
We will also show that it is unstable against linear
perturbations for some ranges of
\bar{r}
outside the horizon.

The BBMB solution has the metric components where

\bar{f}(\bar{r})
is proportional to

\bar{h}(\bar{r})
, i.e.,
\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})
,
where
c_{0}
is a constant.
In this case, Eq. (
35
) is integrated to give

\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}+\frac{C_{1}}{\bar{r}}+\frac{C_{2}}{\bar{r}^{2}}\,,

(69)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are integration constants.
Since we are considering the case
\bar{h}^{\prime}/\bar{h}=\bar{f}^{\prime}/\bar{f}
,
the scalar field satisfies the relation
\phi^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
from Eq. (
34
).
Then, we obtain the following integrated solution

\phi^{\prime}(\bar{r})=-\ln\left(C_{3}\bar{r}+C_{4}\right)\,,

(70)

where
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
are constants.
The other equations of motion are satisfied for

C_{2}=\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{4c_{0}}\,,\qquad C_{4}=\frac{C_{1}C_{3}}{2c_{0}}\,,\qquad C_{1}=\pm\frac{2Mc_{0}}{C_{3}\bar{M}}\,.

(71)

Setting
C_{3}=M/(\bar{M}m_{0})
, the resulting BH solution is
given by Eq. (
36
).

For the exact BH solution (
36
), the quantities
(
56
)-(
58
) and (
63
)
reduce, respectively, to

\bar{{\cal G}}=\bar{{\cal F}}=\bar{{\cal H}}=\frac{2\bar{M}^{2}\bar{r}(2m_{0}-\bar{r})}{(\bar{r}-m_{0})^{2}}\,,\qquad\bar{{\cal K}}=\frac{6\bar{M}^{2}m_{0}^{2}\bar{r}(2m_{0}-\bar{r})}{(\bar{r}^{2}-3m_{0}\bar{r}+3m_{0}^{2})^{2}}\,,

(72)

which are all negative for
\bar{r}>2m_{0}
.
Hence the exact BH solution is unstable at the
distance
\bar{r}>2m_{0}
.

On using the transformation properties (
32
), the metrics and
scalar field reduce to the forms

f(r)=\frac{c_{0}\bar{M}^{2}}{M^{2}}\left(1-\frac{m}{r}\right)^{2}\,,\qquad h(r)=\left(1-\frac{m}{r}\right)^{2}\,,\qquad\varphi=\bar{M}e^{-\phi}=\pm\frac{Mm}{r-m}\,,

(73)

where

r=m\frac{\bar{r}}{\bar{r}-m_{0}}\,,\qquad m=\frac{\bar{M}}{M}m_{0}\,.

(74)

One can choose
c_{0}=M^{2}/\bar{M}^{2}
by using the time
reparametrization freedom of
f
, in which case

f(r)=h(r)=(1-m/r)^{2}
.
On using the first relation of Eq. (
74
),
it follows that the BH instability region
\bar{r}>2m_{0}

translates to
r<2m
. Indeed, this result agrees with
the stability analysis performed in the frame with the metric

g_{\mu\nu}

Kobayashi et al. 2014
.

Appendix B
Exact solution with
\bar{f}
proportional to
\bar{h}

For
\alpha\neq 0
, we derive an exact BH solution by imposing
the condition
\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})
,
where
c_{0}
is a constant.
Then, from Eq. (
35
), we obtain the simple differential
equation

\bar{h}^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{2[\bar{M}^{2}(2\bar{r}\bar{h}^{\prime}+\bar{h}-1)+\alpha\bar{h}^{\prime 2}]}{\bar{M}^{2}\bar{r}^{2}+2\alpha(\bar{h}-1)}\,.

(75)

This is integrated to give

\bar{h}(\bar{r})=1-\frac{\bar{M}^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{2\alpha}\left[1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4\alpha(2m\bar{r}-q)}{\bar{M^{2}}\bar{r}^{4}}}\right]\,,

(76)

where
m
and
q
are integration constants.
Note that we have chosen the asymptotically flat branch where

\bar{h}(\bar{r})
approaches 1 in the limit

\bar{r}\to\infty
, such that

\bar{h}(\bar{r})=1-2m/\bar{r}+q/\bar{r}^{2}+{\cal O}(1/\bar{r}^{4})
.
This solution is analogous to the one derived in Ref.
Fernandes 2021

in gravitational theories with a conformal scalar field.
Since we are now imposing the condition
\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})
,
the right hand side of Eq. (
34
) vanishes.
This gives a constraint on the scalar field equation of motion.
For example, there is a possibility for
\phi
satisfying
the relation
\phi^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
.
However, we recall that the hairy BH solution (
45
)-(
46
) derived
by using regular boundary conditions for both
the metric and scalar field on the horizon does not satisfy the relation

\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})
.
This means that imposing the condition

\bar{f}(\bar{r})=c_{0}\bar{h}(\bar{r})
is not consistent
with all the field equations of motion and
regular boundary conditions at
\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{h}
,
so Eq. (
76
) is not actually a desirable solution to the system.

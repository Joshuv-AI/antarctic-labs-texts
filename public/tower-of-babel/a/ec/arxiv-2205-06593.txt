Urysohn and Hammerstein operators on Hölder spaces
Christian Pötzsche

Abstract
We present an application-oriented approach to Urysohn and Hammerstein integral operators acting between spaces of Hölder continuous functions over compact metric spaces. These nonlinear mappings are formulated by means of an abstract measure theoretical integral involving a finite measure. This flexible setting creates a common framework to tackle both such operators based on the Lebesgue integral like frequently met in applications, as well as e.g. their spatial discretization using stable quadrature/cubature rules (Nyström methods). Under suitable Carathéodory conditions on the kernel functions, properties like well-definedness, boundedness, (complete) continuity and continuous differentiability are established. Furthermore, the special case of Hammerstein operators is understood as composition of Fredholm and Nemytskii operators. While our differentiability results for Urysohn operators appear to be new, the section on Nemytskii operators has a survey character. Finally, an appendix provides a rather comprehensive account summarizing the required preliminaries for Hölder continuous functions defined on metric spaces.

(2.13)

is well-defined and continuous.

The well-definedness of
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}
is shown verbatim to the step (I) of the proof for Prop.
2.2
and we hence focus on continuity. Given
u\in U_{\alpha}
let
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
U_{\alpha}
with
\lim_{l\to\infty}\left\|u_{l}-u\right\|_{0}=0
. Choose
r>0
so large that
u,u_{l}\in B_{r}(0,C_{n}^{0}(\Omega))
for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using
(U_{0}^{k})
this leads to

\lim_{l\to\infty}D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u_{l}(y))=D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega_{1}\text{ and almost all }y\in\Omega.

For
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that subsets
\tilde{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega
satisfying
\mu(\tilde{\Omega})\leq\delta
fulfill
\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}b_{r}^{k}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4}
and Egoroff’s theorem
[
15
, p. 87, Prop. 3.1.3]
yields a
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
with
\mu(\Omega^{\prime})\leq\delta
and an
L_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\left|D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u_{l}(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))\right|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2\mu(\Omega)}
for all
x\in\Omega_{1}
,
y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
,
l\geq L_{1}
. From this, for any
x\in\Omega_{1}
and integers
l\geq L_{1}
we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)]v_{1}\cdots v_{k}(x)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ukdef}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}\left|D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u_{l}(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u_{l}(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2\mu(\Omega)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)+2\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}b_{r}^{k}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}+2\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4}=\varepsilon.

Passing first to the supremum over
x\in\Omega_{1}
therefore implies

\left\|[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)]v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\right\|_{0}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }l\geq L_{1},\,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\bar{B}_{1}(0,C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega))

(2.15)

and second over the vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
yields
\bigl\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)\bigr\|_{L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq\varepsilon
for all
l\geq L_{1}
. Hence, since
u
was arbitrary,
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}
is continuous.
∎

Proposition 2.10

(continuous differentiability of
{\mathscr{U}}
)
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
(U_{0}^{k},U_{1}^{k},U_{2}^{k})
hold for all
0\leq k\leq m
on a convex set
Z\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then an Urysohn operator
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1})
is
m
-times continuously differentiable with
D^{k}{\mathscr{U}}={\mathscr{U}}^{k}
for every
1\leq k\leq m
.

(I) Thanks to Lemma
2.9
the mappings
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}:U_{\alpha}\to L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
are well-defined and continuous for
0\leq k\leq m
. Let
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
h\in C_{d}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
such that
u+h\in U_{\alpha}
. Due to the convexity of
Z
, the inclusion
u(y)+\theta h(y)\in Z
holds for all
y\in\Omega
and
\theta\in[0,1]
. Then the remainder functions

r_{k}(h):=\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\left\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u+\theta h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)\right\|_{L_{k+1}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))}

satisfy
\lim_{h\to 0}r_{k}(h)=0
for all
0\leq k<m
. Now we obtain from
[
35
, p. 341, Thm. 4.2]
that

\displaystyle[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h](x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ukdef}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y)+h(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y))h(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\left[D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y)+\theta h(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y))\right]h(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ukdef}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[({\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u+\theta h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))h\right](x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta

by Fubini’s theorem
[
17
, p. 155, Thm. 14.1]
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h](x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u+\theta h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)\right\|_{L_{k+1}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))}\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta\left\|h\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq r_{k+1}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega_{1}

and after passing to the least upper bound over
x\in\Omega_{1}
it results

\left\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h\right\|_{L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq r_{k}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}.

(2.16)

This establishes that
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}:U_{\alpha}\to L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
is differentiable in
u
with the derivative
{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)
.

(II) Applying step (I) in case
k=0
shows that
{\mathscr{U}}
is differentiable on
U_{\alpha}
with the derivative
{\mathscr{U}}^{1}
. Given this, mathematical induction yields that
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1})
is actually
m
-times differentiable with the derivatives
D^{k}{\mathscr{U}}={\mathscr{U}}^{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq m
, which in turn are continuous due to Lemma
2.9
.
∎

We proceed to Urysohn operators having values in a Hölder space.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
(U_{0}^{k},\bar{U}_{1}^{k},U_{2}^{k})
hold for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}:U_{\alpha}\to L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))
given by (
2.13
) is well-defined. If additionally
(\bar{U}_{3}^{k})
holds, then
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}
is continuous.

The well-definedness of
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}
follows as in step (I) from the proof of Thm.
2.6
. Let
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
denote a sequence in
U_{\alpha}
fulfilling the limit relation
\lim_{l\to\infty}\left\|u_{l}-u\right\|_{0}=0
. In addition, choose
r>0
sufficiently large so that the inclusion
u,u_{l}\in\bar{B}_{r}(0,C_{n}^{0}(\Omega))
for each
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
holds. Therefore,

\displaystyle[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}](x)-[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}](\bar{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ukdef}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u_{l}(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(x,y,u(y))-[D_{3}^{k}f(\bar{x},y,u_{l}(y))-D_{3}^{k}f(\bar{x},y,u(y))]\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot v_{1}(y)\cdots v_{k}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

and after passing to the norm our assumption
(\bar{U}_{3}^{k})
results in

\displaystyle\left|[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}](x)-[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}](\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ub03s}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}c_{r}^{k}(\left|u_{l}(y)-u(y)\right|,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cmod}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Omega}\bar{c}_{r}^{k}(\left\|u_{l}-u\right\|_{0},y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega_{1}.

Consequently, for
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\bar{B}_{1}(0,C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega))
we derive the estimate

[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}]_{\beta}\leq\int_{\Omega}\bar{c}_{r}^{k}(\left\|u_{l}-u\right\|_{0},y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y).

(2.17)

In particular, given
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
L_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}]_{\beta}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }l\geq L_{2},\,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\bar{B}_{1}(0,C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega)).

In conclusion, with (
2.15
) this implies

\displaystyle\left\|({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\right\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left\|({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\right\|_{0},\left[({\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\right]_{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\bar{B}_{1}(0,C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega))

and thus
\bigl\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u_{l})-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)\bigr\|_{L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq\varepsilon
for all
l\geq\max\left\{L_{1},L_{2}\right\}
. Therefore, because the function
u
was arbitrarily chosen,
{\mathscr{U}}^{k}
is continuous on
U_{\alpha}
.
∎

In contrast to Prop.
2.10
, establishing continuous differentiability now requires to invest one additional order of differentiability on the kernel function:

Theorem 2.12

(continuous differentiability of
{\mathscr{U}}
)
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
(U_{0}^{k},\bar{U}_{1}^{k},U_{2}^{k},\bar{U}_{3}^{k})
hold for all
0\leq k\leq m
on a convex set
Z\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then an Urysohn operator
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1})
is
m
-times continuously differentiable with
D^{k}{\mathscr{U}}={\mathscr{U}}^{k}
for every
1\leq k\leq m
.

We establish the assertion for
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1})
first.
Let
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
h\in C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
such that
u+h\in U_{\alpha}
. Moreover, choose
r>0
so large that
u,u+h\in\bar{B}_{r}(0,C_{n}^{0}(\Omega))
holds.

(I) Let
0\leq k<m
. Above all, with the function
\bar{c}_{r}^{k}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega_{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
in (
2.11
) we observe that

\rho_{k}(h):=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\bar{c}_{r}^{k+1}(\theta\left\|h\right\|_{0},y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta

satisfies
\lim_{h\to 0}\rho_{k}(h)=0
. Given arbitrary
x,\bar{x}\in\Omega_{1}
, again
[
35
, p. 341, Thm. 4.2]
yields

\displaystyle[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)](x)-[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)](\bar{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ukdef}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y)+\theta h(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y))

\displaystyle\quad-(D_{3}^{k+1}f(\bar{x},y,u(y)+\theta h(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f\bigl(\bar{x},y,u(y))\bigr)\bigr]\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta h(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\bigl[D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y)+\theta h(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f(x,y,u(y))

\displaystyle\quad-(D_{3}^{k+1}f\bigl(\bar{x},y,u(y)+\theta h(y))-D_{3}^{k+1}f(\bar{x},y,u(y))\bigr)\bigr]h(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta

due to Fubini’s theorem
[
17
, p. 155, Thm. 14.1]
and using the assumption
(\bar{U}_{3}^{k+1})
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h](x)-[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h](\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ub03s}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}c_{r}^{k+1}(\theta\left|h(y)\right|,y)\left|h(y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cmod}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\bar{c}_{r}^{k+1}(\theta\left\|h\right\|_{0},y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha},

which in turn implies

\left[{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h\right]_{\beta}\leq\rho_{k}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}.

(2.18)

If we combine this with the inequalities (
2.16
), then
\|{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u+h)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k}(u)-{\mathscr{U}}^{k+1}(u)h\|_{\beta}\leq R_{k}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}
with the remainder term
R_{k}(h):=\max\left\{r_{k}(h),\rho_{k}(h)\right\}
satisfying the desired limit relation
\lim_{h\to 0}R_{k}(h)=0
.

(II) Applying step (I) in case
k=0
shows that
{\mathscr{U}}
is differentiable on
U_{\alpha}
with the derivative
{\mathscr{U}}^{1}
. Given this, mathematical induction yields that
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1})
is actually
m
-times differentiable with the derivatives
D^{k}{\mathscr{U}}={\mathscr{U}}^{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq m
. Their continuity is guaranteed by Lemma
2.11
.
∎

We close our general analysis of Urysohn operators with several remarks:

Remark 2.3

(boundedness and continuity of
{\mathscr{U}}
)
.

(1) The boundedness of Urysohn operators
{\mathscr{U}}
stated in Prop.
2.2
and Thm.
2.6
actually means that the
{\mathscr{U}}
-images of merely
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
-bounded subsets
B\subset U_{\alpha}
are bounded. This means that the functions in
B
need not to have uniformly bounded Hölder constants.

(2) The continuity statements for the Urysohn operator
{\mathscr{U}}
in Prop.
2.2
and Thm.
2.6
, as well as for its derivatives
D^{k}{\mathscr{U}}
in Prop.
2.10
and Thm.
2.12
are to be understood in the following strong form: Already convergence in the domain
U_{\alpha}
w.r.t. the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
is sufficient for convergence of the
{\mathscr{U}}
-values in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
resp.
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\beta}
. A corresponding statement applies to both Cor.
2.5
and
2.8
.

Remark 2.4

(Urysohn operators
{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{\gamma}(\Omega_{1})
)
.

The above statements extend to Urysohn operators mapping into the
\gamma
-Hölder functions over bounded metric spaces
\Omega_{1}
. This is due to the corresponding representation
\mathscr{I}_{\beta}^{\gamma}{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{d}^{\gamma}(\Omega_{1})
, where the embedding
\mathscr{I}_{\beta}^{\gamma}
from Thm.
A.13
is continuous.

Remark 2.5

(Nyström methods)
.

Let
\Omega^{(l)}
be a discrete subset of a compact set
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and suppose
w_{\eta}\geq 0
are nonnegative reals,
\eta\in\Omega^{(l)}
. In Ex.
2.1
resp.
2.2
we pointed out that both Urysohn operators

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}),

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}_{l}:U_{\alpha}^{(l)}

\displaystyle\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}),

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(\cdot,y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}_{l}(u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\eta\in\Omega^{(l)}}w_{\eta}f(\cdot,\eta,u(\eta))

fit well into our abstract setting, where we abbreviated
U_{\alpha}^{(l)}:=\left\{u:\Omega^{(l)}\to Z\mid u\in C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{(l)})\right\}
. However, when dealing with Nyström methods or for iterating integral operators
{\mathscr{U}}_{l}
it is desirable to work with Urysohn operators defined on
U_{\alpha}
rather than
U_{\alpha}^{(l)}
. For this purpose, let us introduce the linear operator
E_{l}:C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega_{1})\to C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{(l)})
given by
E_{l}u:=u|_{\Omega^{(l)}}
. It satisfies
E_{l}U_{\alpha}\subseteq U_{\alpha}^{(l)}
and is easily seen to be bounded with
\left\|E_{l}\right\|_{C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega_{1}),C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{(l)})}\leq 1
. Hence, rather than
{\mathscr{U}}_{l}
we consider the composition

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}_{l}^{\prime}:U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}),

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}_{l}^{\prime}(u):={\mathscr{U}}_{l}(E_{l}u)=\sum_{\eta\in\Omega^{(l)}}w_{\eta}f(\cdot,\eta,u(\eta)),

which, under appropriate assumptions on the kernel function
f
, inherits its properties from
{\mathscr{U}}_{l}
.

2.3
Convolutive operators

In our above analysis the Hölder continuity of an image
{\mathscr{U}}(u):\Omega_{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of a general Urysohn operator (
2.1
) was guaranteed and prescribed by the exponent of the kernel function
f
in its first variable from assumption
(\bar{U}_{1}^{0})
. A higher degree of smoothness cannot be expected, as simple examples like the kernel function
f(x,y,z):=f_{1}(x)
illustrate, where
{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)=\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)=\mu(\Omega)f_{1}(x)
inherits its smoothness from
f_{1}
. This situation changes for kernel functions of convolution type. Here the smoothness (Hölder continuity, differentiability) of the arguments
u
transfers to the images
{\mathscr{U}}(u)
, i.e. such integral operators possess a smoothing property we are about to analyze over the course of this section. Our results generalize those of
[
26
, pp. 52ff, Sect. 3.4.2]
obtained for linear operators.

To be more precise, let us restrict to a compact interval
\Omega=\Omega_{1}=[a,b]
,
a<b
, equipped with the
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mu=\lambda_{1}
in (
2.1
). Moreover, the kernel function is of the form

f(x,y,z)=\tilde{f}(x-y,z)

with a function
\tilde{f}:[a-b,b-a]\times Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. This yields a
convolutive Urysohn operator

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{U}}:U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to F([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x):=\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y\quad\text{for all }x\in[a,b].

(2.19)

Hypothesis
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\tilde{\Omega}:=[a-b,b-a]
. With
0\leq k\leq m
one assumes:

(C_{0}^{k})

The partial derivative
D_{2}^{k}\tilde{f}(y,\cdot):Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
exists and is continuous for Lebesgue-almost all
y\in\tilde{\Omega}
,

(C_{1}^{k})

D_{2}^{k}\tilde{f}(\cdot,z):\tilde{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is measurable for all
z\in Z
and for every
r>0
there exists an integrable function
\tilde{b}_{r}^{k}:\tilde{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
so that for Lebesgue-almost all
y\in\tilde{\Omega}
the following holds:

\left|D_{2}^{k}\tilde{f}(y,z)\right|\leq\tilde{b}_{r}^{k}(y)\quad\text{for all }z\in Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0),

(2.20)

(C_{2})

for all
r>0
there exists an integrable function
\tilde{l}_{r}:\tilde{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, so that for Lebesgue-almost all
y\in\tilde{\Omega}
the following holds:

\left|\tilde{f}(y,z)-\tilde{f}(y,\bar{z})\right|\leq\tilde{l}_{r}(y)\left|z-\bar{z}\right|\quad\text{for all }z,\bar{z}\in Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0).

(2.21)

Note that the Lipschitz condition
(C_{2})
implies
(C_{0}^{0})
, but also (
2.20
) for
k=1
with
\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}=\tilde{l}_{r}
.

Under these assumptions the Hölder continuity of
u\in U_{\alpha}
carries over to the values
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
:

Theorem 2.13

(Hölder continuity of
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
)
.

Assume that
(C_{1}^{0},C_{2})
hold and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
If
u\in U_{\alpha}
,
r>\left\|u\right\|_{0}
and there exists a real
C\geq 0
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\bar{x}}\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq C(\bar{x}-x)^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }a-b\leq x\leq\bar{x}\leq b-a,

(2.22)

then the image satisfies
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)\in C_{d}^{\alpha}[a,b]
.

Let
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
r>\left\|u\right\|_{0}
.

(I) Let
x\in[a,b]
be given. Above all,
(C_{2})
implies that
\tilde{f}(y,\cdot):Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is continuous for Lebesgue-almost all
y\in\tilde{\Omega}
(i.e. the assumption
(C_{0}^{0})
holds). Combined with the measurability assumed in
(C_{1}^{0})
we conclude from
[
48
, p. 62, Lemma 5.1]
that
y\mapsto\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))
is measurable. Moreover, due to (
2.20
) one has the estimate
\left|\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\right|\leq\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(x-y)
, where

\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(x-y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\leq\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(\eta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

for all
x\in[a,b]
. Consequently the function
y\mapsto\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))
is integrable. Hence
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u):[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is well-defined.

(II) For
x,\bar{x}\in[a,b]
with
\bar{x}=x+\Delta>x
it results

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{deftU}}}{{=}}\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(\bar{x}-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x+\Delta-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\int_{a-\Delta}^{a}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta+\Delta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta+\int_{a}^{b-\Delta}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta+\Delta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

via the substitution
\eta:=y-\Delta
and analogously

\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{deftU}}}{{=}}\int_{a}^{b-\Delta}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta+\int_{b-\Delta}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta.

Whence, the difference
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(\bar{x})-\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}
can be written as sum of the terms

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle:=\int_{a}^{b-\Delta}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta+\Delta))-\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{a-\Delta}^{a}\tilde{f}(\eta-\Delta,u(\eta+\Delta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{b-\Delta}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta=\int_{x-b}^{x-b+\Delta}\tilde{f}(y,u(x-y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

which can be estimated separately as

\displaystyle\left|I_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b-\Delta}\left|\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta+\Delta))-\tilde{f}(x-\eta,u(\eta))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{tildelip}}}{{\leq}}\int_{a}^{b-\Delta}\tilde{l}_{r}(x-\eta)\left|u(\eta+\Delta)-u(\eta)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{\alpha}\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{l}_{r}(x-\eta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta\Delta^{\alpha}\leq[u]_{\alpha}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}\tilde{l}_{r}(\eta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta\Delta^{\alpha}

and (
2.22
) imply that

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a-\Delta}^{a}\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(x-\eta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta\leq C\Delta^{\alpha},

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x-b}^{x-b+\Delta}\tilde{b}_{r}^{0}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\ y\leq C\Delta^{\alpha}.

Hence, with
I_{0},I_{1}
and
I_{2}
also their sum is
\alpha
-Hölder due to Thm.
A.8
and thus
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)\in C^{\alpha}[a,b]
.
∎

Theorem 2.14

(Hölder continuity of
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
)
.

Assume that
(C_{1}^{0},C_{2})
hold and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, the kernel function
\tilde{f}
and the partial derivative
D_{1}\tilde{f}
exist as a continuous functions on both sets
[a-b,0)\times Z
and
(0,b-a]\times Z
.
If
u\in U_{\alpha}
,
r>\left\|u\right\|_{0}
and there exists a
h_{0}>0
such that

\int_{-h_{0}}^{h_{0}}\tilde{l}_{r}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y<\infty,

then
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is
\alpha
-Hölder on every subinterval compact in
[a,b]
.

Let
u\in U_{\alpha}
. As in step (I) of the proof to Thm.
2.13
one shows that
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is well-defined. Now suppose that
I\subseteq(a,b)
is a compact subinterval. For each

we choose

and

holds. This allows us to represent

\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)+I_{2}(x)

(2.23)

with the functions
I_{0},I_{1},I_{2}:I\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
given by

\displaystyle I_{0}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{x-h}^{x+h}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\int_{-h}^{h}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta,

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{a}^{x-h}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

\displaystyle I_{2}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{x+h}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

where we applied the substitution
\eta=x-y
in order to rewrite
I_{0}(x)
. First, we investigate the parameter integral

. Thereto, using

\displaystyle\left|I_{0}(x)-I_{0}(\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-h}^{h}\left|\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x-\eta))-\tilde{f}(\eta,u(\bar{x}-\eta))\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{tildelip}}}{{\leq}}\int_{-h}^{h}\tilde{l}_{r}(y)\left|u(x-\eta)-u(\bar{x}-\eta)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{\alpha}\int_{-h}^{h}\tilde{l}_{r}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\left|x-\bar{x}\right|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in I

and consequently also

is
\alpha
-Hölder on the compact subinterval
I
. Second, the integration variable
y
in the parameter integrals

and
I_{2}(x)
satisfies
0<h\leq x-y

x-y\leq-h<0
. Thus, the functions

are differentiable with the derivatives

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{x-h}D_{1}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\tilde{f}(h,u(x-h)),

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{x+h}^{b}D_{1}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\tilde{f}(-h,u(x+h))

for all

-Hölder on

A.8
yields that the sum (

-Hölder.
∎

Theorem 2.15

(continuous differentiability of
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
)
.

Assume that
(C_{0}^{1},C_{1}^{1})
hold on a convex set
Z\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\tilde{f}:\tilde{\Omega}\times Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
D_{2}\tilde{f}(\cdot,z)
are continuous for all
z\in Z
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. If
u:[a,b]\to Z
is continuously differentiable, then also the image
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u):[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is continuously differentiable with the derivative

\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)^{\prime}(x)=\tilde{f}(x-a,u(a))-\tilde{f}(x-b,u(b))-\int_{a}^{b}D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\quad\text{for all }x\in[a,b].

(2.24)

Since the derivative
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)^{\prime}
is bounded as a continuous function over the compact interval
[a,b]
, it results from Ex.
A.2
that
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is
\alpha
-Hölder,
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Let
u:[a,b]\to Z
be continuously differentiable and choose
r>\left\|u\right\|_{0}
. Since
\tilde{f}
is continuous, the assumptions
(C_{0}^{0},C_{1}^{0})
are satisfied and consequently as shown in step (I) of the proof to Thm.
2.13
the expression
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is well-defined. Now fix some
x\in[a,b]
.

(I) Claim:
One has the limit relation

\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\int_{a}^{b}D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y.

(2.25)

We define the function

F(h,y):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\right),&h\neq 0,\\
D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y),&h=0\end{cases}

having the following properties: First,
F(h,\cdot):[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is integrable for every fixed
h
. In order to see this, let us distinguish two cases:

h\neq 0
: The continuity of
\tilde{f}
and
u
yield that
F(h,\cdot)
is integrable.

h=0
: Due to
(C_{1}^{1})
the derivative
D_{2}\tilde{f}(\cdot,z)
is measurable for all
z\in Z
and the continuity of
u
and
u^{\prime}
guarantee that
F(0,\cdot)
is measurable due to
[
48
, p. 62, Lemma 5.1]
. Moreover, for Lebesgue-almost all
y\in[a,b]
one has
\left|F(0,y)\right|=\left|D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)\right|\leq\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}(x-y)\left\|u^{\prime}\right\|_{0}
from (
2.20
) and due to

\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}(x-y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\leq\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}b_{r}^{1}(\eta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

(2.26)

also the function
F(0,\cdot)
is integrable.

Second,
F(\cdot,y)
is continuous in
0
, which readily results from the chain rule
[
35
, p. 337]
. Third, applying the Mean Value Theorem
[
35
, p. 341, Thm. 4.2]
twice leads to

\displaystyle F(h,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}D_{2}\tilde{f}\bigl(x-y,u(y)+\theta(u(y+h)-u(y))\bigr)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(y+\theta h)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta

and hence

\left|F(h,y)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{no16}}}{{\leq}}\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}(x-y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta\left\|u^{\prime}\right\|_{0}\leq\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}(x-y)\left\|u^{\prime}\right\|_{0}

for all
h
. Thanks to (
2.26
) the right-hand side of this inequality is bounded above by an integrable function independent of
h
(cf.
(C_{1}^{1})
). Combining these three aspects, it is a consequence of the dominated convergence theorem
[
17
, p. 149, Thm. 10.1]
that taking the limit and integration in (
2.25
) can be exchanged, which yields the claim.

(II) Using the substitution
\eta=x-y
we obtain the representation

\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{deftU}}}{{=}}\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\int_{x-b}^{x-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta,

which in turn yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x+h)-\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)=\int_{x+h-b}^{x+h-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta-\int_{x-b}^{x-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{x-b}^{x+h-b}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta+\int_{x-b}^{x-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))-\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta+\int_{x-a}^{x+h-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{x-b}^{x+h-b}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta-\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\int_{x-a}^{x+h-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

for all
h\in[a-x,b-x]
; note that we re-substituted
\eta=x-y
in the center term of the above sum. The Mean Value Theorem from the integral calculus applies to each continuous component function
\tilde{f}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d
, in the first and third term in the above sum. Hence, there exist reals

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{i}(h)

\displaystyle\in\left(\min\left\{x-b,x+h-b\right\},\max\left\{x-b,x+h-b\right\}\right),

\displaystyle\xi_{2}^{i}(h)

\displaystyle\in\left(\min\left\{x-a,x+h-a\right\},\max\left\{x-a,x+h-a\right\}\right),

such that the identities

\displaystyle-\int_{x-b}^{x+h-b}\tilde{f}_{i}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

\displaystyle=-h\tilde{f}_{i}\bigl(\xi_{1}^{i}(h),u(x+h-\xi_{1}^{i}(h))\bigr),

\displaystyle\int_{x-a}^{x+h-a}\tilde{f}_{i}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta

\displaystyle=h\tilde{f}_{i}\bigl(\xi_{2}^{i}(h),u(x+h-\xi_{2}^{i}(h))\bigr)

hold, which allow us to conclude

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)_{i}(x+h)-\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)_{i}(x)\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\left(\int_{x-a}^{x+h-a}\tilde{f}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta-\int_{x-b}^{x+h-b}\tilde{f}_{i}(\eta,u(x+h-\eta))\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}_{i}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}_{i}(x-y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}\bigl(\xi_{2}^{i}(h),u(x+h-\xi_{2}^{i}(h))\bigr)-\tilde{f}_{i}\bigl(\xi_{1}^{i}(h),u(x+h-\xi_{1}^{i}(h))\bigr)

\displaystyle-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{f}_{i}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}_{i}(x-y,u(y))\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq d.

Thanks to the limit relations
\lim_{h\to 0}\xi_{1}^{i}(h)=x-b
,
\lim_{h\to 0}\xi_{2}^{i}(h)=x-a
for all
1\leq i\leq d
and returning to vector notation we consequently arrive at

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\tfrac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x+h)-\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x-a,u(a))-\tilde{f}(x-b,u(b))-\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{h}\left(\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y+h))-\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{limconv}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x-a,u(a))-\tilde{f}(x-b,u(b))-\int_{a}^{b}D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y.

This establishes that the image
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is differentiable in
x\in[a,b]
with the derivative (
2.24
). Hence, in order to show that
{\mathscr{U}}(u)^{\prime}
is continuous, it suffices to establish the continuity of the parameter integral
x\mapsto\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{F}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y
with
\tilde{F}(x,y):=D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)
. By assumption follows that
\tilde{F}(\cdot,y)
is continuous on
[a,b]
. From
[
48
, p. 62, Lemma 5.1]
we conclude that
F(x,\cdot)
is measurable and because of

\left|\tilde{F}(x,y)\right|=\left|D_{2}\tilde{f}(x-y,u(y))u^{\prime}(y)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{no16}}}{{\leq}}\tilde{b}_{r}^{1}(x-y)\left\|u^{\prime}\right\|_{0}\quad\text{for all }x\in[a,b]

integrable (uniformly in
x
, cf. (
2.26
)). Then the dominated convergence theorem
[
17
, p. 149, Thm. 10.1]
shows that
x\mapsto\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{F}(x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y
is continuous, and thus
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}(u)
is continuously differentiable.
∎

3
Hammerstein integral operators

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
Hammerstein operators
are of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}:U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to F(\Omega_{1},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}k(\cdot,y)g(y,u(y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

(3.1)

and represent a relevant special case of the Urysohn operators studied in Sect.
2
having the kernel function
f(x,y,z):=k(x,y)g(y,z)
. Nevertheless, we investigate them as composition of Fredholm and Nemytskii operators. For this reason, let us study these operator classes independently first.

3.1
Fredholm integral operators

A
Fredholm operator
is a linear integral operator of the form

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}}:C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to F(\Omega_{1},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}}u

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}k(\cdot,y)u(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

(3.2)

determined by a matrix-valued
kernel

k:\Omega_{1}\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times p}
.

Fredholm operators apparently fit in the set-up of Sect.
2
with kernel function
f(x,y,z)=k(x,y)z
. Nevertheless, we take the opportunity for formulate our assumptions in terms of integrals over the kernels, rather than over the kernels. Then the corresponding counterparts to
(U_{1}^{0})
and
(U_{2}^{0})
read as:

Hypothesis
.

(K_{1})

\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)-k(x_{0},y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)=0
for all
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}
,

(K_{2})

k(x,\cdot)
is measurable for all
x\in\Omega_{1}
and

\sup_{\xi\in\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(\xi,y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)<\infty.

Proposition 3.1

(well-definedness of
{\mathscr{K}}
)
.

Assume that
(K_{1},K_{2})
hold. Then a Fredholm operator
{\mathscr{K}}
satisfies
{\mathscr{K}}\in L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
and

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}\right\|_{L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\}\sup_{\xi\in\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(\xi,y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y).

Let us abbreviate
M:=\sup_{\xi\in\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(\xi,y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)
and write
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}:C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)\to C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1})
instead of
{\mathscr{K}}
. The inclusion
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}\in L(C_{n}^{0}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
with
\left\|{\mathscr{K}}_{0}\right\|\leq M
is shown in
[
21
, p. 244, Satz 1]
. In case
\alpha\in(0,1]
we consider the composition
{\mathscr{K}}={\mathscr{K}}_{0}\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
, where the embedding operator
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
from (
A.3
) satisfies the estimate
\left\|\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}\right\|\leq\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\}
by Thm.
A.13
. This implies the remaining assertions.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(compactness of
{\mathscr{K}}
)
.

A Fredholm operator
{\mathscr{K}}\in L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
is compact, provided one of the following holds:

(i)

\alpha\in(0,1]
,

(ii)

\Omega_{1}
is compact,
\alpha=0
and
(K_{1})
holds uniformly in
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}
.

For compactness of
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}\in L(C_{n}^{0}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
we refer to
[
21
, p. 247, Satz 4]
. In case
\alpha\in(0,1]
we consider the composition
{\mathscr{K}}={\mathscr{K}}_{0}\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
of the continuous
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}
and the embedding operator
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
introduced in (
A.3
), which is compact due to Thm.
A.15
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

If
\Omega_{1}
is compact and
k:\Omega_{1}\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times p}
is continuous, then
(K_{1},K_{2})
are fulfilled. Hence, Prop.
3.1
and Cor.
3.2
guarantee that
{\mathscr{K}}\in L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{0}(\Omega_{1}))
is compact.

In order to handle Fredholm operators which map into the Hölder continuous functions a refinement of assumption
(\bar{U}_{1}^{0})
is due:

Hypothesis
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
.

(\bar{K}_{1})

There exists a continuous function
\tilde{h}:\Omega_{1}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)-k(x_{0},y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\leq\tilde{h}(x,x_{0})d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }x,x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}.

(3.3)

Obviously,
(\bar{K}_{1})
implies
(K_{1})
.

Theorem 3.3

(well-definedness of
{\mathscr{K}}
)
.

Assume that
(\bar{K}_{1},K_{2})
hold. Then a Fredholm operator
{\mathscr{K}}
satisfies
{\mathscr{K}}\in L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))
and

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}\right\|_{L(C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq\max\left\{\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\}\sup_{\xi\in\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(\xi,y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y),\sup_{x,x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}}\tilde{h}(x,x_{0})\right\}.

We abbreviate
N:=\sup_{x,x_{0}\in\Omega_{1}}\tilde{h}(x,x_{0})
. First,

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}u\right\|_{0}\leq M\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\}\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}

holds due to Prop.
3.1
(
M\geq 0
is defined in its proof). Second, the inequality

\displaystyle\left|{\mathscr{K}}u(x)-{\mathscr{K}}u(\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{kdef}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)-k(\bar{x},y)\right|\left|u(y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{khyp2}}}{{\leq}}Nd(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega_{1}

consequently implies that

[{\mathscr{K}}u]_{\beta}\leq N\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}

holds. A combination of these two estimates finally guarantees that

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}u\right\|_{\beta}=\max\left\{\left\|{\mathscr{K}}u\right\|_{0},[{\mathscr{K}}u]_{\beta}\right\}\leq\max\left\{M\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\},N\right\}\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}

and thus

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}\right\|_{L(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))}\leq\max\left\{M\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}\right\},N\right\}

holds.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(compactness of
{\mathscr{K}}
)
.

,

(ii)

and

,

(iv)

\Omega_{1}
is compact and
\gamma\in[0,\beta)
.

We write
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}:C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1})
and
{\mathscr{K}}_{\alpha}^{\gamma}:C_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to C_{d}^{\gamma}(\Omega_{1})
instead of
{\mathscr{K}}
.

(I) Claim:
If (iii) holds, then
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}^{\beta}\in L(C_{n}^{0}(\Omega),C_{d}^{\beta}(\Omega_{1}))
is compact.

Given the unit ball

we apply the compactness criterion from Thm.
A.14
in order to show that

is relatively compact. First,
{\mathscr{K}}_{0}^{\beta}B
is bounded due to the above Thm.

. Second, given

we obtain by assumption that there exists a
\delta>0
such that

yields
\tilde{h}(x,\bar{x})<\varepsilon
for all

(\bar{K}_{1})
implies that

\left|({\mathscr{K}}u)(x)-({\mathscr{K}}u)(\bar{x})\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{kdef}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)-k(\bar{x},y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{khyp2}}}{{\leq}}\varepsilon d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega_{1}

and all

.

(II) Under (i) the operator

is a composition of the continuous

(see Prop.

(see Thm.

). Under (ii) one has

3.2
Nemytskii operators

A
Nemytskii operator
3
3

3

also denoted as
composition
or
superposition operator
. Further transcriptions are Nemytskij, Nemyzki, Nemytsky, Nemyckij, Nemyckii, Nemitski, Nemitskii, Nemitsky, Nemickij, Nemickii or Niemytzki
is a mapping of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}:U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to F(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{p}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(u)(x)

\displaystyle:=g(x,u(x))\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,

(3.4)

which is generated by a function
g:\Omega\times Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
. Our terminology using the letter ’g’ comes from
growth function
met in applications
[
31
,
36
]
.

Hypothesis
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. With
0\leq k\leq m
one assumes:

(N_{0}^{k})

The partial derivative
D_{2}^{k}g:\Omega\times Z\to L_{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{p})
exists and can be continuously extended to
\Omega\times\overline{Z}
.

Proposition 3.5

(well-definedness of
\mathscr{G}
)
.

Assume that
(N_{0}^{0})
holds. Then a Nemytskii operator
\mathscr{G}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
is well-defined, bounded and continuous. Moreover,
\mathscr{G}
is completely continuous, provided
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.2
.

For
\alpha=0
and compact intervals
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
a Nemytskii operator
\mathscr{G}:C^{0}(\Omega)\to C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
is well-defined if and only if
g:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is continuous
[
6
, Thm. 3.1]
. Indeed, due to
[
6
, Table 1]
one has the equivalences:

g
is continuous

\Updownarrow

\mathscr{G}
is bounded

\Leftrightarrow

\mathscr{G}
is well-defined

\Leftrightarrow

\mathscr{G}
is continuous

We denote
\mathscr{G}
defined on
U_{0}
as
\mathscr{G}_{0}
. Since every
u\in U_{\alpha}
is continuous, also
\mathscr{G}(u):\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is continuous and bounded. As a result,
\mathscr{G}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
is well-defined. Let
u_{0}\in U_{\alpha}
and choose
r:=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{0}+1
. Now
g
can be extended continuously to
\Omega\times\bar{Z}
by assumption
(N_{0}^{0})
and
g
is uniformly continuous on
\Omega\times(Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0))
. Given
\varepsilon>0
this means that there exists a
\delta>0
such that

\begin{cases}d(x,\bar{x})<\delta,\\
\left|z-\bar{z}\right|<\delta\end{cases}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left|g(x,z)-g(\bar{x},\bar{z})\right|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega,\,z,\bar{z}\in Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0).

If
u\in U_{\alpha}\cap B_{\delta}(u_{0},C_{n}^{0}(\Omega))
and
\delta<1
, then
\left|u(x)\right|\leq\left|u_{0}(x)\right|+\left|u(x)-u_{0}(x)\right|\leq r
and consequently

\left|[\mathscr{G}(u)-\mathscr{G}(u_{0})](x)\right|=\left|g(x,u(x))-g(x,u_{0}(x))\right|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega.

Passing to the supremum over
x\in\Omega
yields
\left\|\mathscr{G}(u)-\mathscr{G}(u_{0})\right\|_{0}\leq\varepsilon
, i.e.
\mathscr{G}
is continuous. The boundedness of
\mathscr{G}
results from the uniform continuity of
g
on
\Omega\times\overline{Z}
, as well as properties of the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
. In conclusion,
\mathscr{G}
is well-defined, bounded and continuous.

Thanks to Thm.
A.15
the embedding
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
is compact and therefore
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}_{0}\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{0}
is even completely continuous for
\alpha\in(0,1]
(see
[
48
, pp. 25–26, Thm. 2.1(2)]
).
∎

Hypothesis
.

Let
\vartheta\in(0,1]
.

(N_{0}^{\prime})

For every
r>0

such that

\left|g(x,z)-g(x,\bar{z})\right|\leq l_{r}^{\prime}\left|z-\bar{z}\right|^{\vartheta}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,z,\bar{z}\in Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0).

(3.5)

.

Assume that

and
(N_{0}^{\prime})

The same argument in case

.

Remark 3.3
.

Note for

and a compact interval

does in the second variable. In case

As a consequence of Thm.

\left|[\mathscr{G}(u)-\mathscr{G}(\bar{u})](x)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gdef}}}{{=}}\left|g(x,u(x))-g(x,\bar{u}(x))\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{nem02}}}{{\leq}}l_{r}^{\prime}\left|u(x)-\bar{u}(x)\right|^{\vartheta}\leq l_{r}^{\prime}\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{\alpha}^{\vartheta}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega

∎

We next show that the derivative of a Nemytskii operator is a multiplication operator:

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
(N_{0}^{k})
holds for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
\mathscr{G}^{k}:U_{\alpha}\to L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))
given by

(\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)v_{1}\cdots v_{k})(x):=D_{2}^{k}g(x,u(x))v_{1}(x)\cdots v_{k}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

(3.6)

is well-defined and continuous.

Let

be given.
With
u\in U_{\alpha}
also

is continuous and consequently
\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in C_{d}^{0}(\Omega)
holds, i.e.
\mathscr{G}^{k}
is well-defined. Let
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in

with
\lim_{l\to\infty}\left\|u_{l}-u\right\|_{0}=0
. Choose
r>0
so large that

for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
D_{2}^{k}g
is uniformly continuous on
\Omega\times(Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0))
and given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
with

\left|z-\bar{z}\right|<\delta\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left|D_{2}^{k}g(x,z)-D_{2}g^{k}(x,\bar{z})\right|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,z,\bar{z}\in Z\cap\bar{B}_{r}(0).

Moreover, there exists a

such that
\left|u_{l}(x)-u(x)\right|\leq\delta
for all

and consequently

\displaystyle\left|((\mathscr{G}^{k}(u_{l})-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u))v_{1}\cdots v_{k})(x)\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gkdef}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left|\bigl(D_{2}^{k}g(x,u_{l}(x))-D_{2}^{k}g(x,u(x))\bigr)v_{1}(x)\cdots v_{k}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|D_{2}^{k}g(x,u_{l}(x))-D_{2}^{k}g(x,u(x))\right|\left|v_{1}(x)\right|\cdots\left|v_{k}(x)\right|\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega.

Passing to the supremum over
x\in\Omega
yields

\left\|[\mathscr{G}^{k}(u_{l})-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)]v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\right\|_{0}\leq\varepsilon

for

and, in turn,
\bigl\|\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u_{0})\bigr\|_{L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))}\leq\varepsilon
for all

. Since

was arbitrary,
\mathscr{G}^{k}
is continuous.
∎

Proposition 3.8

(continuous differentiability of
\mathscr{G}
)
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
(N_{0}^{k})
hold for all
0\leq k\leq m
on a convex set
Z\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then a Nemytskii operator
\mathscr{G}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
is
m
-times continuously differentiable with
D^{k}\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}^{k}
for every
1\leq k\leq m
.

(I) Let
0\leq k<m
. Thanks to Lemma
3.7
the mappings
\mathscr{G}^{k}:U_{\alpha}\to L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))
are well-defined and continuous. Thus, given
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
h\in C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
with
u+h\in U_{\alpha}
the remainders

r_{k}(h):=\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\left\|\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u+\theta h)-\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)\right\|_{L_{k+1}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))}

satisfy
\lim_{h\to 0}r_{k}(h)=0
. Now we obtain from
[
35
, p. 341, Thm. 4.2]
that

\displaystyle[\mathscr{G}^{k}(u+h)-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)-\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)h](x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gkdef}}}{{=}}D_{2}^{k}g(x,u(x)+h(x))-D_{2}^{k}g(x,u(x))-D_{2}^{k+1}g(x,u(x))h(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left[D_{2}^{k+1}g(x,u(x)+\theta h(x))-D_{2}^{k+1}g(x,u(x))\right]h(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta,

consequently

\displaystyle\left|[\mathscr{G}^{k}(u+h)-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)-\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)h](x)\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gkdef}}}{{\leq}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u+\theta h)-\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)\right\|_{L_{k+1}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))}\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta\left\|h\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq r_{k}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}

and after passing to the least upper bound over
x\in\Omega
it results

\left\|\mathscr{G}^{k}(u+h)-\mathscr{G}^{k}(u)-\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)h\right\|_{L_{k}(C_{n}^{\alpha}(\Omega),C_{p}^{0}(\Omega))}\leq r_{k}(h)\left\|h\right\|_{\alpha}.

This establishes that the mapping
\mathscr{G}^{k}
is differentiable in
u
with the derivative
\mathscr{G}^{k+1}(u)
and, in turn,
\mathscr{G}^{k+1}
is continuous due to Lemma
3.7
.

(II) Applying step (I) in case
k=0
shows that
\mathscr{G}
is continuously differentiable on
U_{\alpha}
with the derivative
\mathscr{G}^{1}
. Given this, mathematical induction yields that
\mathscr{G}:U_{\alpha}\to C_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
is actually
m
-times continuously differentiable with the derivatives
D^{k}\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}^{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq m
.
∎

Appendix A
Hölder continuous functions

The definition of continuity for a function
u
in e.g. a point
x_{0}
is not quantitative in the sense that it provides no information on how fast its values
u(x)
approach
u(x_{0})
as
x\to x_{0}
. In consequence, the modulus of continuity
\omega:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
of a continuous function
u
satisfying the estimate
\left\|u(x)-u(x_{0})\right\|\leq\omega(d(x,x_{0}))
may decrease arbitrarily slowly. For this reason, the space of continuous functions is often not suitable for a quantitative analysis in fields such as numerical analysis or partial differential equations. A straightforward and feasible way to strengthen the notion of continuity of
u
is to assume that its modulus of continuity is proportional to a power of
d(x,x_{0})^{\alpha}
for some exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Such functions are denoted as Hölder continuous and in the focus of this appendix.

Our overall setting is as follows. Let
(\Omega,d)
be a metric space and
(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be a normed space over
{\mathbb{K}}
, which stands for the real or complex field.

A function
u:\Omega\to Y
is said to be
\alpha
-Hölder
(with
Hölder exponent

\alpha\in(0,1]
), if it satisfies

[u]_{\alpha}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,\bar{x}\in\Omega\\
x\neq\bar{x}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}<\infty;

the finite quantity
[u]_{\alpha}\geq 0
is called
Hölder constant
of
u
. One speaks of a
Hölder continuous
function
u
, if it is
\alpha
-Hölder for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and in case
\alpha=1
one denotes
u
as
Lipschitz continuous
with
Lipschitz constant

[u]_{1}
— a comprehensive approach to this class of functions is given in
[
14
]
. For the set of all such
\alpha
-Hölder functions
u:\Omega\to Y
we write
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
, supplemented by
C^{0}(\Omega,Y)
for the linear space of continuous functions. It is convenient to denote a continuous function as
0
-Hölder, and unless indicated otherwise, let us assume
\alpha\in[0,1]
throughout.

Remark A.1
.

(1) A function
u:\Omega\to Y
is constant, if and only if its Hölder constant vanishes.

(2) For
\alpha\in(0,1]
there is an evident relation between Hölder functions and Lipschitz functions: Indeed,
u:(\Omega,d)\to Y
is
\alpha
-Hölder, if and only if
u:(\Omega,d_{\alpha})\to Y
is Lipschitz with the metric
d_{\alpha}:\Omega\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
given by
d_{\alpha}(x,\bar{x}):=d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}
. Of course the metrics
d
and
d_{\alpha}
on
\Omega
are not equivalent.

(3) One does restrict to exponents
\alpha\in(0,1]
for the following reason. Suppose that a function
u:\Omega\to Y
on an open subset
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
satisfies
[u]_{\alpha}<\infty
for an exponent
\alpha>1
. Then

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})-0(x-\bar{x})\right\|=\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq[u]_{\alpha}\left|x-\bar{x}\right|^{\alpha-1}\left|x-\bar{x}\right|\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

yields that
u
is differentiable on
\Omega
with derivative
0
and thus constant on the components of
\Omega
.

(4) Suppose that
\Omega
has a finite, positive diameter and that
\phi:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a function satisfying
\phi(0)=0
,
\phi(1)=1
such that
t\mapsto\phi(t)
and
t\mapsto\tfrac{t}{\phi(t)}
are positive and increasing on
(0,1)
. Then
u:\Omega\to Y
is called
generalized Hölder
, if

\sup_{x,\bar{x}\in\Omega,x\neq\bar{x}}\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\phi\bigl(\tfrac{d(x,\bar{x})}{\diam\Omega}\bigr)^{-1}<\infty

(see
[
8
, Ch. 7]
and
[
11
]
). In case
\phi(t):=t^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
this reduces to the situation studied here.

(5) Differentiable functions on
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
whose
m
th derivative satisfies an
\alpha
-Hölder condition, and associated function spaces
C^{m,\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
, are addressed in
[
22
, pp. 30ff, Sect. 1.5]
or
[
24
, pp. 51ff, Sect. 4.1]
.

(6) The inner structure of Hölder spaces from an abstract Banach spaces perspective is studied in
[
29
]
.

Example A.1
.

Let
\Omega=Y={\mathbb{R}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The function
u_{\alpha}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
u_{\alpha}(x):=\left|x\right|^{\alpha}
is not differentiable in
0
(see Fig.
1
), but
\alpha
-Hölder. Indeed, given
x,\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}
the case
0<|x|\leq|\bar{x}|
leads to the inequality
1-\tfrac{|x|^{\alpha}}{|\bar{x}|^{\alpha}}\leq 1-\tfrac{|x|}{|\bar{x}|}\leq\Bigl(1-\tfrac{|x|}{|\bar{x}|}\Bigr)^{\alpha}
and therefore
|u_{\alpha}(\bar{x})-u_{\alpha}(x)|=u_{\alpha}(\bar{x})-u_{\alpha}(x)\leq\left|\bar{x}-x\right|^{\alpha}
. A similar argument in case
0<|\bar{x}|\leq|x|
yields the assertion with Hölder constant
[u_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}\leq 1
.

Theorem A.1

(local Hölder continuity)
.

Let
\Omega
be compact and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. If
u:\Omega\to Y
is
locally
\alpha
-Hölder
, i.e. every
x\in\Omega
has a neighborhood
U\subseteq\Omega
such that
[u|_{U}]_{\alpha}<\infty
holds, then
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder.

We proceed indirectly and suppose that for each
c\geq 0
there exist
x,\bar{x}\in\Omega
yielding the inequality
\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|>cd(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}
. In particular, there are sequences
(x_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(\bar{x}_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\Omega
so that

\left\|u(x_{l})-u(\bar{x}_{l})\right\|>ld(x_{l},\bar{x}_{l})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }l\in{\mathbb{N}}

(A.1)

holds. Since
\Omega
is compact, w.l.o.g. we can assume that these sequences converge to
x^{\ast},\bar{x}^{\ast}\in\Omega
, respectively. Passing to the limit
l\to\infty
in (
A.1
) therefore enforces
\lim_{l\to\infty}d(x_{l},\bar{x}_{l})^{\alpha}=0
, i.e. one has
x^{\ast}=\bar{x}^{\ast}
. Because the function
u
is assumed to be locally Hölder, there exists a neighborhood
U\subseteq\Omega
of
x^{\ast}
and a real
C\geq 0
with
\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq Cd(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}
for all
x,\bar{x}\in U
. Thanks to the inclusion
x_{l},\bar{x}_{l}\in U
for sufficiently large
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
this implies
\left\|u(x_{l})-u(\bar{x}_{l})\right\|\leq Cd(x_{l},\bar{x}_{l})^{\alpha}
contradicting (
A.1
) for
l>C
.
∎

The relationship between differentiable and Hölder continuous functions is addressed in

Example A.2
.

(1) Differentiable functions
u:\Omega\to Y
on open, bounded and convex sets

-Hölder for each
\alpha\in(0,1]
. This follows from the Mean Value Inequality
[
38

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq\sup_{\xi\in\Omega}\left\|Du(\xi)\right\|\left|x-\bar{x}\right|\leq(\diam\Omega)^{1-\alpha}\sup_{\xi\in\Omega}\left\|Du(\xi)\right\|\left|x-\bar{x}\right|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

and thus
[u]_{\alpha}\leq(\diam\Omega)^{1-\alpha}\sup_{\xi\in\Omega}\left\|Du(\xi)\right\|
. A version of this result on not necessarily convex sets
\Omega
can be found in
[
22
, p. 11, Prop. 1.1.13]
. In the Lipschitz case
\alpha=1
boundedness of
\Omega
is not required.

(2) Rademacher’s theorem
[
17

guarantees that Lipschitz functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
on open sets
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
are differentiable Lebesgue-almost everywhere in
\Omega
. This situation changes for exponents
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
[
27
]
shows that the
Weierstraß function

u:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given as Fourier series

u(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a^{k}\cos(b^{k}\pi x)\quad\text{with $a\in(0,1)$ and integers }b>1

satisfying
ab>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with exponent
\alpha=-\log_{b}a
, but nowhere differentiable.

Theorem A.2
.

Hölder continuous functions are uniformly continuous.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
(w.l.o.g.
u
is not constant). Setting
\delta:=\bigl(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{[u]_{\alpha}}\bigr)^{1/\alpha}
guarantees

and thus

is uniformly continuous.
∎The converse to Thm.

Example A.3
.

Let
\Omega=[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]

\displaystyle u:[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}-\tfrac{1}{\ln|x|},&x\in[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]\setminus\left\{0\right\},\\

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
Y
be a Banach space. Every
\alpha
-Hölder function
u:U\to Y
on an open set
U\subseteq\Omega
allows an
\alpha
-Hölder extension to the closure
\overline{U}
with the same Hölder constant.

Let
u:U\subseteq\Omega\to Y
be
\alpha
-Hölder. By Thm.
A.2
it follows that
u:U\to Y
is uniformly continuous. Hence, because
Y
is complete, there exists a continuous extension
\bar{u}:\bar{U}\subseteq\Omega\to Y
of
u
to the boundary. We next show that
\bar{u}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with
[\bar{u}]_{\alpha}=[u]_{\alpha}
. Thereto, given arbitrary
y,\bar{y}\in\bar{U}
and the estimate
\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq[u]_{\alpha}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}
for all
x,\bar{x}\in U
, we first pass to the limit
x\to y
, then to
\bar{x}\to\bar{y}
, and it results from the extension property that
\left\|\bar{u}(y)-\bar{u}(\bar{y})\right\|\leq[u]_{\alpha}d(y,\bar{y})^{\alpha}
for all
y,\bar{y}\in\bar{U}
. This guarantees that
\bar{u}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with
[\bar{u}]_{\alpha}\leq[u]_{\alpha}
. Moreover, it evidently holds

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|=\left\|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(\bar{x})\right\|\leq[\bar{u}]_{\alpha}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in U

and
[u]_{\alpha}\leq[\bar{u}]_{\alpha}
. Thus, the minimum Hölder coefficients of
u
and
\bar{u}
are equal yielding the claim.
∎

Theorem A.4
.

Hölder continuous functions are bounded (on bounded sets).

Let
u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
,
B\subseteq\Omega
be bounded and choose a fixed
x_{0}\in B
. Then

\left\|u(x)\right\|\leq\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|+\left\|u(x)-u(x_{0})\right\|\leq\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|+[u]_{\alpha}d(x,x_{0})^{\alpha}\leq\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|+[u]_{\alpha}(\diam B)^{\alpha}

(A.2)

for all
x\in\Omega
holds and therefore the image
u(B)\subseteq Y
is bounded.
∎

For globally bounded
\alpha
-Hölder functions
u:\Omega\to Y

\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left\|u(x)\right\|,&\alpha=0,\\
\max\left\{\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left\|u(x)\right\|,[u]_{\alpha}\right\},&\alpha\in(0,1].\end{cases}

On the product
Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
of two normed spaces

\left\|(y_{1},y_{2})\right\|=\max\left\{\left\|y_{1}\right\|,\left\|y_{2}\right\|\right\}\quad\text{for all }y_{1}\in Y_{1},\,y_{2}\in Y_{2}.

Theorem A.5
.

A function
u=(u_{1},u_{2}):\Omega\to Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
is
\alpha
-Hölder, if and only if both component functions
u_{j}:\Omega\to Y_{j}
are
\alpha
-Hölder for
j=1,2
. In this case and for
\alpha\in(0,1]
one has

[u_{j}]_{\alpha}\leq[u]_{\alpha}\leq\max\left\{[u_{1}]_{\alpha},[u_{2}]_{\alpha}\right\}

and for globally bounded functions
u
results

\|u_{j}\|_{\alpha}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\max\left\{\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\alpha},\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}\right\}

for all
j=1,2
.

We restrict to the case
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

(\Rightarrow)
If
u:\Omega\to Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
is
\alpha
-Hölder, then also the components
u_{1},u_{2}
are
\alpha
-Hölder due to

\left\|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(\bar{x})\right\|\leq\max_{i=1}^{2}\left\|u_{i}(x)-u_{i}(\bar{x})\right\|=\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq[u]_{\alpha}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega,\,j=1,2.

(\Leftarrow)
Conversely, if the component functions
u_{1},u_{2}
are
\alpha
-Hölder, then also
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder because of

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|=\max_{i=1}^{2}\left\|u_{i}(x)-u_{i}(\bar{x})\right\|\leq\max_{i=1}^{2}[u_{i}]_{\alpha}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega.

These inequalities also imply the claimed estimates for the Hölder constants. Combining them with

\left\|u_{j}(x)\right\|\leq\left\|u(x)\right\|=\max_{i=1}^{2}\left\|u_{i}(x)\right\|\leq\max_{i=1}^{2}\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,j=1,2

yields the estimates for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha}
after passing to the supremum over
x\in\Omega
.

The product of two metric spaces

.

(\Rightarrow)
Let

.

.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. If a function

Let

and

. If

, then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u(x_{l}^{1},x_{l}^{2})-u(x_{1}^{\ast},x_{2}^{\ast})\right\|\leq\left\|u(x_{l}^{1},x_{l}^{2})-u(x_{l}^{1},x_{2}^{\ast})\right\|+\left\|u(x_{l}^{1},x_{2}^{\ast})-u(x_{1}^{\ast},x_{2}^{\ast})\right\|

Theorem A.8

(sum rule)
.

With functions
u_{1},u_{2}:\Omega\to Y
also
\lambda_{1}u_{1}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}
is

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}

u_{1},u_{2}
holds

\left\|\lambda_{1}u_{1}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\left|\lambda_{1}\right|\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\alpha}+\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}.

The straightforward proof is left to the reader.
∎

A mapping
\cdot:Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Y
is called a
product
, if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle y_{1}\cdot(y_{2}+\bar{y}_{2})

\displaystyle=y_{1}\cdot y_{2}+y_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2},

\displaystyle(y_{1}+\bar{y}_{1})\cdot y_{2}

\displaystyle=y_{1}\cdot y_{2}+\bar{y}_{1}\cdot y_{2},

\displaystyle\left\|y_{1}\cdot y_{2}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|y_{1}\right\|\left\|y_{2}\right\|

for all
y_{1},\bar{y}_{1}\in Y_{1}
,
y_{2},\bar{y}_{2}\in Y_{2}
.

Theorem A.9

(product rule)
.

If the functions
u_{1}:\Omega\to Y_{1}
and
u_{2}:\Omega\to Y_{2}
are globally bounded and
\alpha
-Hölder, then also their product
u_{1}\cdot u_{2}:\Omega\to Y
is
\alpha
-Hölder. In case
\alpha\in(0,1]
one has the estimates

[u_{1}\cdot u_{2}]_{\alpha}\leq C\bigl(\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{0}[u_{1}]_{\alpha}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{0}[u_{2}]_{\alpha}\bigr)

and

\left\|u_{1}\cdot u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}\leq C\bigl(\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{0}\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\alpha}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{0}\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}\bigr)
.

We restrict to
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Using properties of a product, we obtain from the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left\|(u_{1}\cdot u_{2})(x)-(u_{1}\cdot u_{2})(\bar{x})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(\bar{x}))\cdot u_{2}(x)\right\|+\left\|u_{1}(\bar{x})\cdot(u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(\bar{x}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(\bar{x})\right\|\left\|u_{2}(x)\right\|+C\left\|u_{1}(\bar{x})\right\|\left\|u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(\bar{x})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{0}[u_{1}]_{\alpha}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{0}[u_{2}]_{\alpha}\bigr)d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

and this implies that
u_{1}\cdot u_{2}
is
\alpha
-Hölder. Then the estimate for
\left\|u_{1}\cdot u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha}
follows easily.
∎

Theorem A.10

(chain rule)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,1]
. If functions
u_{1}:\Omega\to Y_{1}
is
\alpha_{1}
-Hölder and
u_{2}:u_{1}(\Omega)\to Y
is
\alpha_{2}
-Hölder, then the composition
u_{2}\circ u_{1}:\Omega\to Y
is
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
-Hölder. In case
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,1]
one has the estimate

[u_{2}\circ u_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\leq[u_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}[u_{2}]_{\beta}

and for globally bounded
u_{2}
results

\left\|u_{2}\circ u_{1}\right\|_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\leq\max\left\{1,[u_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}\right\}\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\alpha_{2}}.

We focus on the situation
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,1]
. The following holds

\displaystyle\left\|u_{2}\circ u_{1}(x)-u_{2}\circ u_{1}(\bar{x})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq[u_{2}]_{\alpha_{2}}\left|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(\bar{x})\right|^{\alpha_{2}}\leq[u_{1}]_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}[u_{2}]_{\alpha_{2}}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

and so the composition
u_{2}\circ u_{1}
is
\alpha
-Hölder. The remaining norm estimate is readily derived.
∎

Let
B(\Omega,Y)
abbreviate the space of globally bounded functions and we define the space of globally bounded
\alpha
-Hölder functions by

C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y):=\left\{u\in B(\Omega,Y):\,u\text{ is $\alpha$-H\"{o}lder}\right\}.

Due to Thm.
A.4
the characterization
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)=\left\{u:\Omega\to Y\mid u\text{ is $\alpha$-H\"{o}lder}\right\}
holds on bounded spaces
\Omega
. By Lemma
A.3
it is
C^{\alpha}(U,Y)=C^{\alpha}(\overline{U},Y)
for subsets
U\subseteq\Omega
and Banach spaces
Y
.

Theorem A.11
.

The set
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is a normed space over
{\mathbb{K}}
w.r.t. the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, with
Y
also
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is a Banach space.

We merely show the completeness of
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
w.r.t. the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Thereto, let
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
. Since
C^{0}(\Omega,Y)
is complete in the
\sup
-norm,
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to a continuous function
u:\Omega\to Y
. It remains to show that
\lim_{l\to\infty}[u_{l}-u]_{\alpha}=0
and that
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder. Thereto, for
\varepsilon>0
first choose
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\left\|u_{l}-u_{m}\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}
for all
l,m\geq L
, and given
x,\bar{x}\in\Omega
,
x\neq\bar{x}
, choose a fixed
\bar{l}\geq L
such that

\left\|u_{\bar{l}}(x)-u(x)\right\|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}
and

\left\|u_{\bar{l}}(\bar{x})-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}
. Now this results in

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|(u_{l}-u)(x)-(u_{l}-u)(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|(u_{l}-u_{\bar{l}})(x)-(u_{l}-u_{\bar{l}})(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}+\frac{\left\|u_{\bar{l}}(x)-u(x)\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}+\frac{\left\|u_{\bar{l}}(\bar{x})-u(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq[u_{l}-u_{\bar{n}}]_{\alpha}+\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{3}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }l\geq L

and therefore
[u_{l}-u]_{\alpha}\leq\varepsilon
. If we set
\varepsilon=1
in the above inequality and note that
([u_{l}]_{\alpha})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded, then the generalized triangle inequality guarantees

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}\leq 1+\frac{\left\|u_{l}(x)-u_{l}(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}\leq 1+[u_{l}]_{\alpha}\leq 1+\sup_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}[u_{l}]_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

and consequently
u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
.
∎

Remark A.2
.

(1) The positive homogeneity
[\lambda u]_{\alpha}=\left|\lambda\right|[u]_{\alpha}
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
and Thm.
A.8
guarantee that
[\cdot]_{\alpha}
defines a semi-norm on
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
. It is not a norm, since
[\cdot]_{\alpha}
vanishes on the constant functions. However, if
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
is fixed, then

\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=\max\left\{\left|u(x_{0})\right|,[u]_{\alpha}\right\}

defines an equivalent norm on
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
. Indeed, any globally bounded
\alpha
-Hölder function
u:\Omega\to Y
satisfies (
A.2
) for all
x\in\Omega
, which implies the inequality
\left\|u\right\|_{0}\leq\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|+[u]_{\alpha}(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha}
and consequently

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\max\left\{\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|+[u]_{\alpha}(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha},[u]_{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left\|u(x_{0})\right\|,[u]_{\alpha}\right\}+\max\left\{(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha},1\right\}[u]_{\alpha}\leq(1+\max\left\{(\diam\Omega)^{\alpha},1\right\})\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}^{\prime}

guarantees that both norms are equivalent.

(2) Let
\Omega
be compact. Then
C^{\alpha}(\Omega)_{+}:=\left\{u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}):\,0\leq u(x)\text{ for all }x\in\Omega\right\}
is an order cone in
C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
with nonempty interior. However, if
\Omega
has at least one accumulation point, then
C^{\alpha}(\Omega)_{+}
is not normal (see
[
3
]
).

The following result establishes that
\alpha
-Hölder functions on bounded sets form a decreasing scale of spaces between the Lipschitz continuous and the uniformly continuous functions (cf. Thm.
A.2
).

Lemma A.12
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded. If
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
, then
\beta
-Hölder functions are
\alpha
-Hölder and satisfy

[u]_{\alpha}\leq(\diam\Omega)^{\beta-\alpha}[u]_{\beta}

.

Given
u\in C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
one has

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq d(x,\bar{x})^{\beta-\alpha}[u]_{\beta}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\leq(\diam\Omega)^{\beta-\alpha}[u]_{\beta}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,\bar{x}\in\Omega

yielding the assertion.
∎

Theorem A.13

(continuous embeddings)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded. If
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
, then
C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is a continuous embedding with

\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\beta-\alpha}\right\}\left\|u\right\|_{\beta}

for all
u\in C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
.

In order words, one has a bounded embedding operator

\mathscr{I}_{\beta}^{\alpha}:C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)\to C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)\quad\text{for all }0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1.

(A.3)

If
u\in C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
, then Lemma
A.12
implies
[u]_{\alpha}<\infty
and thus

\max\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{0},[u]_{\alpha}\right\}\leq\max\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{0},(\diam\Omega)^{\beta-\alpha}[u]_{\beta}\right\}\leq\max\left\{1,(\diam\Omega)^{\beta-\alpha}\right\}\left\|u\right\|_{\beta}

yields that
u
is also
\alpha
-Hölder and satisfies the claimed estimate.
∎

Remark A.3

(differentiable and Sobolev functions)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
be open and bounded.

(1) If
\Omega
is convex and
\bar{C}^{1}(\Omega,Y)
denotes the (canonically normed) space of continuously differentiable functions allowing a continuous extension to
\overline{\Omega}
, then one has the continuous embedding
[
1
, pp. 11–12, 1.34 Thm.]

\bar{C}^{1}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega},Y)\quad\text{for all }0\leq\alpha\leq 1.

(A.4)

(2) Let
\dim Y<\infty
. If
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\geq 1
satisfy
(k-\alpha)p\geq\kappa
, then the Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\Omega,Y)
satisfies the following continuous embedding
[
1
]

W^{k,p}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega},Y)\quad\text{for all }0<\alpha\leq 1.

(A.5)

For Hölder exponents
0<\alpha<\beta\leq 1
the inclusion from Thm.
A.15
will typically be strict.

Example A.4
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1]
and
Y={\mathbb{R}}
. In case
\alpha\in(0,1)
the function
u:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
u(x):=x^{\alpha}
is contained in
C^{\alpha}[0,1]
. Now if
u
would be
\beta
-Hölder with exponent
\beta>\alpha
, then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that
x^{\alpha}=\left|u(x)-u(0)\right|\leq C\left|x-0\right|^{\beta}=Cx^{\beta}
for
x\in(0,1]
yielding the contradiction
C\geq x^{\alpha-\beta}\xrightarrow[x\searrow 0]{}\infty
. Concerning the case
\alpha=0
we refer to Ex.
A.3
for a continuous function not being Hölder.

On the space
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
exist several measures of noncompactness, which even are not necessarily equivalent (cf.
[
37
, Sect. 5]
). Among them, and for finite-dimensional spaces
Y
, we use

\chi(B):=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\sup_{u\in B}\left\{\tfrac{\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|}{d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}}:\,0<d(x,\bar{x})\leq\varepsilon\right\}

(see
[
10
,
11
]
and
[
50
]
) and obtain a sufficient compactness criterion:

Theorem A.14

(compactness in
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
, cf.
[
10
, Thm. 4]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be compact and
\dim Y<\infty
. A subset
B\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is relatively compact, provided the following two conditions hold:

(i)

B
is bounded,

(ii)

for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that for all
x,\bar{x}\in\Omega
one has the implication

d(x,\bar{x})\leq\delta\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq\varepsilon d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }u\in B.

For
\alpha=0
this is essentially the sufficiency part of the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem
[
38
, p. 31, Thm. 3.2]
, which establishes that (i) and (ii) characterize the relatively compact subsets of
C^{0}(\Omega,Y)
.

Theorem A.15

(compact embeddings)
.

Let
\Omega
be compact and
\dim Y<\infty
. If
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 1
, then
C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is a compact embedding. Moreover, the embedding
C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{0}(\Omega,Y)
is even dense, provided one has
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

This means that bounded subsets of
C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
are relatively compact in
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
. In case
\alpha\in(0,1)
the embedding (
A.4
) is compact. Similarly, for
(k-\alpha)p>\kappa
also (
A.5
) is compact (see
[
1
, pp. 11–12, 1.34 Thm.]
).

(I) Let
B\subseteq C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
be bounded, that is, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that
\left\|u\right\|_{\beta}\leq C
for all
u\in B
. This implies
\left\|u(x)\right\|\leq C
and

\left\|u(x)-u(\bar{x})\right\|\leq Cd(x,\bar{x})^{\beta}\leq Cd(x,\bar{x})^{\beta-\alpha}d(x,\bar{x})^{\alpha}

for all
x,\bar{x}\in\Omega
and
u\in B
, which guarantees that
B\subseteq C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)
satisfies the assumptions of Thm.
A.14
. Consequently,
B
is a relatively compact subset of
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
.

(II) Referring to the Stone-Weierstraß theorem
[
17
, p. 218, Thm. 16.1]
the polynomials over a compact
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
form a set of
\beta
-Hölder functions being dense in the continuous functions.
∎However, note that the embedding
C^{\beta}(\Omega,Y)\subseteq C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is not dense for
0<\alpha<\beta\leq 1
.

Example A.5
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1]
,
Y={\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
be given by
u(x):=x^{\alpha}
. Choose
v\in C^{\beta}[0,1]
and consider the difference
u-v\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
satisfying

\frac{\left|(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(0)\right|}{\left|x-0\right|^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{\left|u(x)-u(0)\right|}{\left|x-0\right|^{\alpha}}-\frac{\left|v(x)-v(0)\right|}{\left|x-0\right|^{\alpha}}=1-\frac{\left|x\right|^{\beta}}{\left|x\right|^{\alpha}}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(0)\right|}{\left|x-0\right|^{\beta}}\xrightarrow[x\searrow 0]{}1.

This implies that any function
v\in C^{\beta}[0,1]
has
\alpha
-norm greater or equal to
1
from
u
.

The final example demonstrates that
C^{\alpha}(\Omega,Y)
is not separable.

Example A.6
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1]
,
Y={\mathbb{R}}
and for reals
c\in(0,1)
define the
\alpha
-Hölder functions

\displaystyle u_{c}:[0,1]

\displaystyle\to{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle u_{c}(x)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}0,&0\leq x\leq c,\\
(x-c)^{\alpha},&a<x\leq 1,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
. This implies the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|u_{a}-u_{b}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq[u_{a}-u_{b}]_{\alpha}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,\bar{x}\in[0,1]\\
x\neq\bar{x}\end{subarray}}\frac{(u_{a}-u_{b})(x)-(u_{a}-u_{b})(\bar{x})}{\left|x-\bar{x}\right|^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{\left|(u_{a}-u_{b})(b)-(u_{a}-u_{b})(a)\right|}{\left|b-a\right|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b-a)^{\alpha}}{\left|b-a\right|^{\alpha}}=1\quad\text{for all }a,b\in(0,1)

with the uncountable family
\left\{u_{c}\right\}_{c\in(0,1)}\subseteq C^{\alpha}[0,1]
.
